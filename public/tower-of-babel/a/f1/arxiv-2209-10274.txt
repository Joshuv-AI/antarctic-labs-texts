Abstract
Send to AADM, Mathematica Slovoca (Patricks article), etc… For positive integers k , l ≥ 2 k,l\geq 2 , the set of k k -regular partitions in which parts appear at most l l times has attracted a lot of interest in that a composition of Glaisher’s mapping can be used to prove the associated partition identities in certain cases. We consider special cases and derive some arithmetic properties. Of particular focus is the set of partitions in which parts are odd and distinct ( k = 2 k=2 , l = 2 l=2 ). Sylvester proved that, for fixed weight, this set of partitions is equinumerous with the set of self-conjugate partitions. We introduce a new class of partitions that generalizes self-conjugate partitions and as a result, we extend Sylvester’s theorem. Furthermore, using this class of partitions, we give new combinatorial interpretation of some Rogers-Ramanujan identities which were previously considered by A. K. Agarwal.



Some consequences of Glaisher’s map and a generalization of Sylvester’s theorem

Key words
: partition; bijection; generating function; conjugate.

Introduction

A partition of
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
is sequence
\lambda=\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
of positive integers such that
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\ell}\lambda_{i}=n
and
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}
. The summand
\lambda_{i}
is called a part of partition and the sum of summands is the weight. The weight of
\lambda
is denoted by
|\lambda|
. There is so much study of restricted partition functions, for example, see
[
2
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. In
[
3
]
, G. Andrews recalls a theorem of Schur, and also reports a hint from K. Alladi. With this hint, the following partition identity is established.

Theorem 1.1
.

The number of partitions of
n
into distinct parts not divisible by 3 is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into odd parts occuring not more than twice.

Actually, it turns out that there is a more general theorem beyond Theorem
1.1
that can be viewed as a consequence of Glaisher’s theorem
[
5
]
. More precisely, for integers
p,k\geq 2
, let
b(n,p,k)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts not divisible by
p
and occurring at most
k-1
times and let
c(n,k,p)
denote the number of partitions of
n
into parts not divisible by
k
and occurring at most
p-1
times. It follows that

b(n,p,k)=c(n,k,p).

(1.1)

This observation was stated in
[
7
]
and attempts to provide a bijective proof that is purely a composition of Glaisher mappings have been made. For the case where
\gcd(k,p)=1

[

-partitions onto

-partitions. In Section 2, we study a certain class of partitions involving

-subpartitions and the special case
b(n,3,2)

b(n,2,2)
and introduce a new class of partitions, which we call symmetric partitions. We generalize Sylvester’s theorem for
b(n,2,2)
via symmetric partitions and consequently, present new interpretations of some Rodgers-Ramanujan identities.

Certain partitions involving
b(n,pt,p)
-subpartitions

Consider partitions in
B(n,pt,p)
. We investigate the case where we allow parts divisible by
p

Let
t\geq 1
and
p\geq 2
be integers and denote by
F(n,p,t)
the set of partitions of
n

occur with multiplicities

p

Proposition 2.1
.

For all

f(n,p,t)=r(n,p).

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f(n,p,t)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{tpn}+q^{2tpn}+q^{3tpn}+\cdots+q^{(p-1)tpn})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\prod\limits_{r=1}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{pn-r}+q^{2(pn-r)}+\cdots+q^{(pt-1)(pn-r)})

\displaystyle=\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{p^{2}tn}}{1-q^{ptn}}\prod\limits_{r=1}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{pt(pn-r)}}{1-q^{pn-r}}

\displaystyle=\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{p^{2}tn})}{(1-q^{ptn})}\cdot\frac{(1-q^{pn})}{(1-q^{n})}\cdot\frac{(1-q^{ptn})}{(1-q^{p^{2}tn})}

\displaystyle=\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{pn}}{1-q^{n}}

\displaystyle=\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{n}+q^{2n}+q^{3n}+\cdots+q^{(p-1)n}).

∎

We exhibit a bijection from between the sets

In reference to Proposition

(

into distinct parts. Let

Corollary 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

f_{o}(n,2,t)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{n}{2t}}\rfloor}(-1)^{j+1}d(n-2tj^{2}),

(2.3)

where

is the number of partitions of

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(f_{e}(n,2,t)-f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{2tn})\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}+\cdots+q^{(2t-1)(2n-1)})

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{2tn}}{1+q^{2tn}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{2tn})(1+q^{2n-1}+q^{2(2n-1)}+\cdots+q^{(2t-1)(2n-1)})

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{2tn}}{1+q^{2tn}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{2tn^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}\,\,\,\text{(by \eqref{cauchy})}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(f_{e}(n,2,t)+f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{2tn^{2}}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}+2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{2tn^{2}}\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}+2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n},

i.e.

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d(n)q^{n}+2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}d(n-k)q^{n}+2\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n}

where

a_{k}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{j},&k=2tj^{2},j\geq 1;\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence,

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{o}(n,2,t))q^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\sqrt{\frac{n}{2t}}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}d(n-2tj^{2})\right)q^{n}.

By comparing the coefficients of
q^{n}
on both sides, we obtain the result.

3
Extending Sylvester’s theorem

In this section we introduce a new class of partitions called symetric partitions and how they relate to certain types of partitions in the literature. However, we shall require a statistic called the order of a partition (see
[
11
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

For a partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}\geq\cdots
, we define the order of
\lambda
to be the integer
\max\{i:\lambda_{i}\geq i\}
.

Our new class of partitions which we shall call (
\mu,\gamma
)-symmetric partitions is defined as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mu\geq 2
and
\gamma\geq 0
be integers. A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)
is said to be
(\mu,\gamma)
-symmetric if the tail
(\lambda_{s+1},\lambda_{s+2},\ldots)
is equal to

\begin{cases}(s^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{s}-s)+\gamma},(s-1)^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{s-1}-\lambda_{s}+1)-1},\ldots,1^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1)-1}),&\text{if $s>1$};\\
s^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{s}-s)+\gamma},&\text{if $s=1$}.\end{cases}

where
s
is the order of
\lambda
.

We denote the number of
(\mu,\gamma)
-symmetric partitions of
n
by
g(n,\mu,\gamma)
.

Example 3.1
.

The
(2,1)
-symmetric partitions of 10 are
(5,1^{5})
,
(4,2^{2},1^{2})
and
(3^{2},2^{2})
.

We declare the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geq 0
,
g(n,\mu,\gamma)
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into distinct parts congruent to
1+\gamma\pmod{\mu}
and the smallest part is at least
\gamma+1
.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n,\mu,\gamma)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq 0,}\sum_{\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{s}\geq s}q^{\mu\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}-\mu\frac{s(s+1)}{2}+s\gamma+s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\geq 0,}\sum_{n_{s}\geq 0,}\sum_{n_{s-1\geq 0}}\cdots\sum_{n_{1}\geq 0}\left(q^{\mu s^{2}}\cdot(q^{\mu s})^{n_{s}}\cdot(q^{\mu(s-1)})^{n_{s-1}}\cdots(q^{2\mu})^{n_{2}}\cdot(q^{\mu})^{n_{1}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad\times q^{-\frac{\mu s(s+1)}{2}+s\gamma+s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\mu s^{2}-\frac{\mu s(s+1)}{2}+s\gamma+s}}{(1-q^{\mu s})(1-q^{\mu(s-1)})\ldots(1-q^{2\mu})(1-q^{\mu})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{\mu s(s-1)}{2}+s(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{s}},

i.e.

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n,\mu,\gamma)q^{n}\quad=\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{\mu n(n-1)}{2}+n(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{n}}.

(3.1)

Replacing

by

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{\mu n(n-1)}{2}+n(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{n}}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1+q^{\mu n+(\gamma+1)}).

\geq\gamma+1
.
Firstly, observe that the parts of
\beta
are distinct since

\beta_{i}-\beta_{i+1}=\mu(\lambda_{i}-i)+1+\gamma-(\mu(\lambda_{i+1}-i-1)+1+\gamma)=\mu(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i+1})+\mu\geq\mu>1

so that
\beta_{i}>\beta_{i+1}
for all
i
.

Secondly, we claim that the map preserves weight since

\displaystyle|\beta|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\left(\mu(\lambda_{i}-i)+\gamma+1\right)

\displaystyle=\mu\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}(\lambda_{i}-i)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}(\gamma+1)

\displaystyle=\mu\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}\,\,-\mu\frac{s(s+1)}{2}+s\gamma+s

\displaystyle=\mu\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}\,\,-\,\,(\mu-1)s^{2}+s\gamma+\mu\frac{s(s-1)}{2}-s(s-1)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}+(\mu-1)\left(s\lambda_{s}-s^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\lambda_{j}-s\lambda_{s}\right)+s\gamma+(\mu-2)\frac{s(s-1)}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}+(\mu-1)(s\lambda_{s}-s^{2})+s\gamma+(\mu-1)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}j(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1})\right)+(\mu-2)\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}j

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s}\lambda_{i}+s((\mu-1)(\lambda_{s}-s)+\gamma)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{s-1}j((\mu-1)(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{j+1}+1)-1)

\displaystyle=|\lambda|.

Remark 3.1
.

The explicit bijection given above generalises the explicit bijection given for Theorem
2.2
.

The inverse of the map above is described as follows. Let
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots,\beta_{\ell})
be a partition of
n
into distinct parts congruent to
\gamma+1
modulo
\mu
and each part is at least
\gamma+1
. For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, let

\lambda_{i}=i+\frac{\beta_{i}-1-\gamma}{\mu}.

Then the partition

(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell},\lambda_{\ell+1},\lambda_{\ell+2},\ldots)
where

(\lambda_{\ell+1},\lambda_{\ell+2},\ldots)
defined as:

\begin{cases}(\ell^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{\ell}-\ell)+\gamma},(\ell-1)^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{\ell-1}-\lambda_{\ell}+1)-1},\ldots,1^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}+1)-1}),&\text{ if $\ell>1$};\\
\ell^{(\mu-1)(\lambda_{\ell}-\ell)+\gamma},&\text{if $\ell=1$}\end{cases}

is a
(\mu,\gamma)
-symmetric partition of
n
.

3.2
New combinatorial interpretation of some Rogers-Ramanujan Identities

In this section, we interpret the following Rogers-Ramanujan identities via symmetric partitions:

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n^{2}}}{(q;q)_{2n}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{8n-1})(1-q^{8n-7})(1-q^{16n-10})(1-q^{16n-6})(1-q^{8n})}{1-q^{n}},

(3.3)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n(n+1)}}{(q;q)_{2n+1}}=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{8n-3})(1-q^{8n-5})(1-q^{16n-14})(1-q^{16n-2})(1-q^{8n})}{1-q^{n}}.

(3.4)

The two identities are part of Slater’s compendium (see
[
12
]
) and were given an interpretation in terms of
n
-color partitions by A. K. Agarwal (see
[
1
]
).

However, before we proceed, it is worth mentioning that the number of
(\mu,\gamma)
-symmetric partitions
g(n,\mu,\gamma)
is actually the sum of two functions:

g(n,\mu,\gamma)=g_{e}(n,\mu,\gamma)\,\,+\,\,g_{o}(n,\mu,\gamma)

where
g_{e}(n,\mu,\gamma)
(resp.
g_{o}(n,\mu,\gamma)
) denotes the number of
g(n,\mu,\gamma)
-partitions with an even (resp. odd) order. Hence, it is clear from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{e}(n,\mu,\gamma)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{\mu 2j(2j-1)}{2}+2j(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{2j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\mu j(2j-1)+2j(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{2j}}

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(n,\mu,\gamma)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{\mu(2j+1)(2j)}{2}+(2j+1)(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\mu(2j+1)j+(2j+1)(\gamma+1)}}{(q^{\mu},q^{\mu})_{2j+1}}.

(3.6)

The following theorems give new partition-theoretic interpretation of (
3.3
) and (
3.4
), respectively.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}
be a positive integer. Then
g_{e}(\alpha n,\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts not congruent to
0,\pm 1,\pm 6,\pm 7,8\pmod{16}
.

Proof.

Replacing
\mu
by
\alpha
,
\gamma
by
\frac{\alpha}{2}-1
in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{e}(n,\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{{\alpha j(2j-1)}+j\alpha}}{(q^{\alpha};q^{\alpha})_{2j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2\alpha j^{2}}}{(q^{\alpha};q^{\alpha})_{2j}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{\alpha(8n-1)})(1-q^{\alpha(8n-7)})(1-q^{\alpha(16n-10)})(1-q^{\alpha(16n-6)})(1-q^{8\alpha n})}{(1-q^{\alpha n})}

Setting
q:=q^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
yields

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{e}(\alpha n,\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)q^{n}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{8n-1})(1-q^{8n-7})(1-q^{16n-10})(1-q^{16n-6})(1-q^{8n})}{(1-q^{n})}.

∎

Example 3.3
.

Consider
\alpha=2
and
n=6
.

The
(2,0)
-symmetric partitions of 12 with an even order are

(6,2,1,1,1,1),(5,3,2,1,1),(4,4,2,2)

and partitions of 6 whose parts are not congruent to
0,\pm 1,\pm 6,\pm 7,8\pmod{16}
are

(4,2),(3,3),(2,2,2).

In either case, the number is the same.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}
be a positive integer. Then
g_{o}(\alpha n+\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)
is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts not congruent to
0,\pm 2,\pm 3,\pm 5,8\pmod{16}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3.2
. Set
\mu:=\alpha
,
\gamma:=\frac{\alpha}{2}-1
in (
3.2
) so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(n,\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\alpha(2j+1)j+(2j+1)(\frac{\alpha}{2})}}{(q^{\alpha},q^{\alpha})_{2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2\alpha j(j+1)+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{(q^{\alpha},q^{\alpha})_{2j+1}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(n+\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2\alpha j(j+1)}}{(q^{\alpha},q^{\alpha})_{2j+1}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{\alpha(8n-3)})(1-q^{\alpha(8n-5)})(1-q^{\alpha(16n-14)})(1-q^{\alpha(16n-2)})(1-q^{8\alpha n})}{1-q^{\alpha n}}

which yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(\alpha n+\frac{\alpha}{2},\alpha,\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{8n-3})(1-q^{8n-5})(1-q^{16n-14})(1-q^{16n-2})(1-q^{8n})}{1-q^{n}}.

∎

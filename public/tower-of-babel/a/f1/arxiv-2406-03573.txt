1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
L
be a finite-dimensional Lie superalgebra, and let
L\cong F/R
, where
F
is a free Lie superalgebra. The Schur multiplier
\mathcal{M}(L)
of
L
is isomorphic to
R\cap F^{2}/[R,F]
as shown in
[
3
]
. Classifying Lie superalgebras of arbitrary dimensions without any constraints is a challenging task. In fact, the classification of all finite-dimensional Lie superalgebras remains an open problem. A common approach is to classify nilpotent Lie superalgebras based on a fixed invariant. Some researchers focus on the structure of
L
by examining the dimension of the Schur multiplier. This invariant aids in classifying certain nilpotent Lie superalgebras. For example, the classification of nilpotent Lie superalgebras based on the dimension of the Schur multiplier is discussed in
[
4
]
. According to
[
3
]
, the dimension of the Schur multiplier of an
(m\mid n)
-dimensional nilpotent Lie superalgebra
L
with the derived supersubalgebra of maximum dimension
m+n-2
is bounded by
m+2n-2
. Thus, there exists a non-negative integer
\gamma(L)
such that
\gamma(L)=m+2n-2-\dim\mathcal{M}(L)
.

Throughout this paper, we assume that
L
is a Lie superalgebra over a field of characteristic not equal to 2 or 3. We classify all finite-dimensional nilpotent Lie superalgebras
L
when
\gamma(L)\in\{0,1,2\}
. Additionally, we demonstrate that all these Lie superalgebras are capable. Recall from
[
7
]
that a Lie superalgebra
L
is said to be capable if there exists a Lie superalgebra
M
such that
L\cong M/Z(M)
. Also,
Z^{*}(L)
is the epicenter of a Lie superalgebra
L
, defined as the smallest ideal in
L
such that
L/Z^{*}(L)
is capable. Clearly, the ideal
Z^{*}(L)=0
if and only if
L
is capable. For more details on capable Lie superalgebras, see
[
7
]
.

The following theorem is a useful tool for identifying capable Lie superalgebras.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 4.9]

Let
N
be a central ideal of the Lie superalgebra
L
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{M}(L)\cong\mathcal{M}(L/N)/N\cap L^{2},

(ii)

N\subseteq Z^{*}(L),

(iii)

the natural map
\mathcal{M}(L)\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}(L/N)
is a monomorphism.

The lower central series is defined by
C^{0}(L)=L
and inductively by
C^{i+1}(L)=[L,C^{i}(L)]
. A Lie superalgebra
L
is nilpotent if there exists an integer
n
such that
C^{n}(L)=0
. Let
L=L_{\overline{0}}\oplus L_{\overline{1}}
. Similarly, we may define the following sequences for
L
as defined in
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle C^{0}(L_{\overline{0}})=L_{\overline{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad C^{k}(L_{\overline{0}})=[L_{\overline{0}},C^{k-1}(L_{\overline{0}})]\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 1,

\displaystyle C^{0}(L_{\overline{1}})=L_{\overline{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad C^{k}(L_{\overline{1}})=[L_{\overline{0}},C^{k-1}(L_{\overline{1}})]=ad_{L_{\overline{0}}}(C^{k-1}(L_{\overline{1}}))\quad\text{for all}\quad k\geq 1.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
2
, Theorem 2.1]

The Lie superalgebra
L=L_{\overline{0}}\oplus L_{\overline{1}}
is nilpotent if and only if there exist
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
such that
C^{p-1}(L_{\overline{0}})\neq 0
,
C^{q-1}(L_{\overline{1}})\neq 0
, and
C^{p}(L_{\overline{0}})=C^{q}(L_{\overline{1}})=0
.

For convenience, we list some results regarding the Schur multiplier of a Lie superalgebra.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.4]

Let
L
be an
(m|n)
-dimensional Lie superalgebra. Then

\dim\mathcal{M}(L)=\frac{1}{2}\big((m+n)^{2}+(n-m)\big)

if and only if
L
is abelian.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
3
, Theorem 3.7]

Let
L=L_{\overline{0}}\oplus L_{\overline{1}}
be a Lie superalgebra with
\dim L=(m|n)
and let
K\subseteq Z(L)
be a graded ideal. If
H\cong L/K
is the quotient Lie superalgebra, then

\displaystyle(i)\quad\dim\mathcal{M}(L)+\dim(L^{2}\cap K)\leq\dim\mathcal{M}(H)+\dim\mathcal{M}(K)+\dim(H/H^{2}\otimes K/K^{2}),

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\dim\mathcal{M}(L)+\dim(L^{2}\cap K)\leq\frac{1}{2}\big((m+n)^{2}+(n-m)\big).

Theorem 1.5
.

[
3
, Theorem 5.4]

Let
L
be a non-abelian nilpotent Lie superalgebra with
\dim L=(m|n)
, where
m+n\geq 3
and
\dim L^{2}=m+n-2
.

(i)

If
m+n=3
, then
\dim\mathcal{M}(L)
is equal to 1 or 2.

(ii)

If
m+n\geq 4
and
n\geq 1
, then
\dim\mathcal{M}(L)\leq m+2n-2
.

Metrized Quantum Vector Bundles over Quantum Tori Built from Riemannian Metrics and Rosenberg’s Levi-Civita Connections
Leonard HUANG

Abstract
We build metrized quantum vector bundles, over a generically transcendental quantum torus, from Riemannian metrics, using Rosenberg’s Levi-Civita connections for these metrics. We also prove that two metrized quantum vector bundles, corresponding to positive scalar multiples of a Riemannian metric, have distance zero between them with respect to the modular Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity.

Introduction

This paper was inspired by an apparent connection between Jonathan Rosenberg’s work on Riemannian metrics on a generically transcendental quantum torus and Levi-Civita connections for these metrics
[
14
]
, and Frédéric Latrémolière’s work on metrized quantum vector bundles and the modular Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity
[
6
]
.

The subject of the Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity has its origin in Marc Rieffel’s observation
[
12
]
that in certain papers on theoretical physics, statements can be found regarding the convergence of a sequence of operator algebras to an operator algebra. He deduced that the bookkeeping device used by the authors of these papers to prove convergence can be described as a metric structure on unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras. Seeing that the Gromov–Hausdorff distance for compact metric spaces enables us to discuss the convergence of a sequence of compact metric spaces to a compact metric space, he defined for the class of order-unit spaces endowed with a special metric structure (called the
compact quantum metric spaces
) an analogous distance called the
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance
. The relation between order-unit spaces and
C^{\ast}
-algebras is made clear when one knows that the space of self-adjoint elements of a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra is an order-unit space.

The quantum Gromov–Hausdorff distance suffers from some deficiencies. Designed for order-unit spaces, it does not incorporate the multiplicative structure of a
C^{\ast}
-algebra. Also, it was unknown if distance zero between two
C^{\ast}
-algebras necessarily means that they are
\ast
-isomorphic. These problems were settled when Latrémolière defined, in
[
4
]
, the
quantum Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity
for the class of unital
C^{\ast}
-algebras endowed with a special metric structure (called the
quantum compact metric spaces
).

Recently, Latrémolière was able to generalize the Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity to Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-modules over a quantum compact metric space endowed with a special metric structure
[
6
]
. He calls these objects
metrized quantum vector bundles
, regarding them to be a noncommutative generalization of vector bundles over a compact Riemannian manifold endowed with a metric. The
modular Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity
then allows us to formalize the concept of convergence for metrized quantum vector bundles. Distance zero between metrized quantum vector bundles is equivalent to the existence of an isomorphism between them, in terms of their Hilbert-
C^{\ast}
-module structures and their metric structures.

In Section
3
, we will define Riemannian metrics on generically transcendental quantum tori and provide a brief overview of Rosenberg’s work on Levi-Civita connections for these metrics.

In Section
4
, we will define Latrémolière’s quantum compact metric spaces and metrized quantum vector bundles. We will then show how to build a metrized quantum vector bundle from a Riemannian metric on a generically transcendental quantum torus and the Levi-Civita connection for the metric.

In Section
5
, we will prove that two metrized quantum vector bundles, corresponding to positive scalar multiples of a Riemannian metric, have distance zero between them with respect to the modular Gromov–Hausdorff propinquity.

In Section
6
, we will pose some open questions that serve as the basis for future work in this area.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric, and let
r,s\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}.

Proof.

One can verify that
\nabla^{r\cdot g}
is also a Levi-Civita connection for
g
, so
\nabla^{r\cdot g}=\nabla^{g}
by Theorem
3.6
and

\displaystyle\forall\,\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta},~\forall\,X,Y\in\chi_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{\infty}}\colon\quad\left\langle\left.\nabla^{r\cdot g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}=\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}=r\cdot\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{g},

which yields

\displaystyle\forall\,\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta},~\forall\,X,Y\in\chi_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{\infty}}\colon\quad\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{r\cdot g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}=\sqrt{r}\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|\sqrt{r}\cdot Y\right\rangle_{g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}.

Hence, we have for all
\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}
and
X\in\chi_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{\infty}}
that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla^{r\cdot g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right\|_{r\cdot g}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\chi_{\Theta}\\
\left\|Y\right\|_{r\cdot g}=1\end{subarray}}\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{r\cdot g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\chi_{\Theta}\\
\left\|Y\right\|_{r\cdot g}=1\end{subarray}}\sqrt{r}\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|\sqrt{r}\cdot Y\right\rangle_{g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\chi_{\Theta}\\
\left\|\sqrt{r}\cdot Y\right\|_{g}=1\end{subarray}}\sqrt{r}\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|\sqrt{r}\cdot Y\right\rangle_{g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}\quad\big(\text{as $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}=\sqrt{r}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}$}\big)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\chi_{\Theta}\\
\left\|Y\right\|_{g}=1\end{subarray}}\sqrt{r}\big\|\left\langle\left.\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right|Y\right\rangle_{g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}=\sqrt{r}\left\|\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right\|_{g}.

It follows readily that

\displaystyle\forall\,X\in\chi_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{\infty}}\colon\quad\left\VERT X\right\VERT_{r\cdot g}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}\\
\left\|\delta\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\|\nabla^{r\cdot g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right\|_{r\cdot g}=\sqrt{r}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}\\
\left\|\delta\right\|_{\mathcal{D}_{\Theta}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left\|\nabla^{g}_{\delta}\left(X\right)\right\|_{g}=\sqrt{r}\left\VERT X\right\VERT_{g}.

We thus obtain
\left\VERT\cdot\right\VERT_{r\cdot g}=\sqrt{r}\left\VERT\cdot\right\VERT_{g}
, so

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}=\max\big(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g},\left\VERT\cdot\right\VERT_{r\cdot g}\big)=\max\big(\sqrt{r}\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g},\sqrt{r}\left\VERT\cdot\right\VERT_{g}\big)=\sqrt{r}\max\big(\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g},\left\VERT\cdot\right\VERT_{g}\big)=\sqrt{r}\mathsf{D}_{g}.

Consequently,
\mathsf{D}_{g}^{1}=\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{\sqrt{r}}=\sqrt{r}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}
.

Now, we have by Definition
4.12
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\left(\left\{{X\in\chi_{\Theta}}\,\Big|\,{\text{there exists a}~t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}~\text{such that}~X\in t\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\left(\left\{{X\in\chi_{\Theta}}\,\Big|\,{\text{there exists a}~t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}~\text{such that}~X\in t\cdot\overline{\sqrt{r}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\left(\left\{{X\in\chi_{\Theta}}\,\Big|\,{\text{there exists a}~t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}~\text{such that}~X\in t\sqrt{r}\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\left(\left\{{X\in\chi_{\Theta}}\,\Big|\,{\text{there exists a}~t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}~\text{such that}~X\in t\sqrt{r}\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{=}{}\ \big(\text{as $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}$ and $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}$ are equivalent}\big)

\displaystyle=\sup\left(\left\{{X\in\chi_{\Theta}}\,\Big|\,{\text{there exists a}~t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}~\text{such that}~X\in t\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right).

Next, observe for all
X\in\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}\right)=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)
that

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}\left(X\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\left(\left\{{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\Big|\,{X\in t\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\left(\left\{{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\Big|\,{X\in t\cdot\overline{\sqrt{r}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\left(\left\{{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\Big|\,{X\in t\sqrt{r}\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\inf\left(\left\{{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\Big|\,{X\in t\sqrt{r}\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}}}\right\}\right)\quad\big(\text{as $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{g}$ and $\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}$ are equivalent}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\inf\left(\left\{{t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}}\,\Big|\,{X\in t\cdot\overline{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g}^{1}}^{\left\|\cdot\right\|_{r\cdot g}}}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\left(X\right).

As
X\in\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}\right)=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)
is arbitrary, we obtain
\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}=\sqrt{r}\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}
.

Similarly,
\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}\right)=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)
and
\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}=\sqrt{s}\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}
, so

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)=\operatorname{Dom}\left(\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{s}}\mathsf{D}_{s\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}

as required.
∎

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}-\left\langle\left.\sqrt{\frac{r}{s}}\cdot X\right|\sqrt{\frac{r}{s}}\cdot Y\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\right\|_{A_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g}-\frac{r}{s}\cdot\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\right\|_{A_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\left\|r\cdot\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{g}-\frac{r}{s}\cdot\big(s\cdot\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{g}\big)\right\|_{A_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\big\|r\cdot\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{g}-r\cdot\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{g}\big\|_{A_{\Theta}}=0.

As the argument is symmetric in
X
and
Y
, we also have for all
X,Y\in\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}
that

\displaystyle\mathsf{bn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\left\langle\left.Y\right|X\right\rangle_{r\cdot g},\left\langle\left.\beta_{r,s}\left(Y\right)\right|\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\big)=0,

so

\displaystyle\mathsf{dn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\to\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}}\left(X\right),\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\big)=\mathsf{dn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\left(X,\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup\left(\left\{\left.\begin{matrix}\mathsf{bn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\big\langle X\,|\,\operatorname{Id}_{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\to\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}}\left(Y\right)\big\rangle_{r\cdot g},\left\langle\left.\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right|\beta_{r,s}\left(Y\right)\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\big)\\
\mathsf{bn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\big\langle\operatorname{Id}_{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\to\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}}\left(Y\right)\,|\,X\big\rangle_{r\cdot g},\left\langle\left.\beta_{r,s}\left(Y\right)\right|\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\big)\end{matrix}\right|Y\in\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sup\left(\left\{\left.\begin{matrix}\mathsf{bn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\left\langle\left.X\right|Y\right\rangle_{r\cdot g},\left\langle\left.\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right|\beta_{r,s}\left(Y\right)\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\big)\\
\mathsf{bn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\left\langle\left.Y\right|X\right\rangle_{r\cdot g},\left\langle\left.\beta_{r,s}\left(Y\right)\right|\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\right\rangle_{s\cdot g}\big)\end{matrix}\right|Y\in\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\right\}\right)=0

for all
X\in\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathsf{mod}}_{F,G,H}\left(\Omega_{r},\Omega_{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\left(\gamma_{r,s}\right)=\rho^{\sharp}\left(\gamma_{r,s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sup\big(\big\{\mathsf{dn}_{\gamma_{r,s}}\big(\operatorname{Id}_{\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}\to\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}}\left(X\right),\beta_{r,s}\left(X\right)\big)\,|\,{X\in\mathsf{D}_{r\cdot g,\mathsf{M}}^{1}}\big\}\big)=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion

The only property of Levi-Civita connections used in this paper is metric compatibility. We could have allowed our
\mathsf{D}
norms to depend not only on the choice of a Riemannian metric, but also on the choice of a connection that is not necessarily torsion-free. However, this would introduce an extra degree of variability that could unnecessarily complicate the study of convergence questions in modular propinquity.

Many unanswered questions remain. The four most important ones (in our opinion) are the following:

•

Is
\mathsf{D}_{g}
lower-semicontinuous on
\chi_{\Theta}^{\operatorname{\infty}}
for any Riemannian metric
g
? If this is the case, then (4) of Lemma
4.13
says that
\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}
extends
\mathsf{D}_{g}
, which would render
\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}}
more manageable to deal with.

•

Can we find a topology on
M_{n}\left(A_{\Theta}\right)
so that if
h\to g
in the space of Riemannian metrics with respect to this topology, then

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\mathsf{mod}}_{F,G,H}\left(\big(\chi_{\Theta},\left\langle\left.\cdot\right|\cdot\right\rangle_{g},\mathsf{D}_{g,\mathsf{M}},A_{\Theta},\mathsf{L}\big),\big(\chi_{\Theta},\left\langle\left.\cdot\right|\cdot\right\rangle_{h},\mathsf{D}_{h,\mathsf{M}},A_{\Theta},\mathsf{L}\big)\right)\to 0?

This question can be posed so straightforwardly because we consider only Levi-Civita connections.

•

Can we incorporate the Riemannian curvature operator
[
14
, Definition 3.1]
into the current work?

•

Although Rosenberg considers only free modules in
[
14
]
, can we extend our results to non-free modules? In
[
7
]
, Latrémolière establishes a technical propinquity result for metrized quantum vector bundles whose underlying modules are non-free Heisenberg modules over quantum
2
-tori, but it is not clear at this time how his result may be extended to non-free Hilbert modules over quantum tori of arbitrary dimension.

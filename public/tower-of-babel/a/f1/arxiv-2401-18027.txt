On affine Toda field theories related to 𝐃 r {\bf D}_{r} algebras and their real Hamiltonian forms


Abstract
The paper deals with affine 2-dimensional Toda field theories related to simple Lie algebras of the classical series 𝐃 r {\bf D}_{r} . We demonstrate that the complexification procedure followed by a restriction to a specified real Hamiltonian form commutes with the external automorphisms of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . This is illustrated on the examples 𝐃 r + 1 ( 1 ) → 𝐁 r ( 1 ) {\bf D}_{r+1}^{(1)}\to{\bf B}_{r}^{(1)} and 𝐃 4 ( 1 ) → 𝐆 2 ( 1 ) {\bf D}_{4}^{(1)}\to{\bf G}_{2}^{(1)} using external automorphisms of the corresponding extended Dynkin diagrams.

Introduction

It is well known that with each simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of rank
r
one can relate a 2-dimensional affine Toda field theory (ATFT)
[
43
,
8
,
9
,
45
,
46
,
3
,
4
]
. Their Lagrangian densities are given by:

{\cal L}[{\bf q}]={1\over 2}\left((\partial_{x_{0}}{\bf q}\cdot\partial^{x_{0}}{\bf q})-(\partial_{x_{1}}{\bf q}\cdot\partial^{x_{1}}{\bf q}\right)-\sum_{k=0}^{n}n_{k}\left({\rm e}^{-2(\alpha_{k}\cdot{\bf q})}-1\right),

(1)

where the field
{\bf q}(x,t)
is an
r
-dimensional vector. The equations of motion are therefore:

{\partial^{2}{\bf q}\over\partial x\partial t}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}n_{j}\alpha_{j}e^{-2(\alpha_{j},{\bf q}(x,t))},

(2)

where
t=\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}-x_{0})
and
x=\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{0})
.
The important results that mattered for the derivation of ATFT were: 1) the work of A. Zamolodchikov
[
51
]
on deformation of conformal field theories preserving
integrability and 2) the discovery of Mikhailov’s reduction group which allowed one to derive their Lax representation
[
42
,
43
]
.
Here
r
is the rank of
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\alpha_{k}
’s (
k=1,\dots,n
) are the simple roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\alpha_{0}
is the minimal root.

The notion of real Hamiltonian forms was introduced in
[
28
]
and used to study reductions of ATFTs in
[
23
,
24
,
27
]
.
Real Hamiltonian forms (RHF) are another type of “reductions” of Hamiltonian systems. First one complexifies the ATFT and then applies
an involution
\mathcal{C}
which is compatible with the Poisson structure of the initial system. This is similar to the obtaining a real forms of
a semi-simple Lie algebra. that is why the RHF have indefinite kinetic energy quadratic form.

The structure of the paper is as follows. Section 2 contains some facts about the ATFT and simple Lie algebras.
Section 3 describes the complexification procedure and Section 4 specifies the RHF of ATFT for which the
involution is the external automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus we demonstrate that the complexification procedure commutes with the external
automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
. This is illustrated on the examples
{\bf D}_{r+1}^{(1)}\to{\bf B}_{r}^{(1)}
and
{\bf D}_{4}^{(1)}\to{\bf G}_{2}^{(1)}
.

3
Complexification of ATFT

The starting point in the construction of real Hamiltonian forms (RHF) is the complexification of the field
functions
{\bf q}(x,t)
and
{\bf p}(x,t)
involved in the Hamiltonian (
7
):

{\bf q}^{\mathbb{C}}={\bf q}^{0}+i{\bf q}^{1},\qquad{\bf p}^{\mathbb{C}}={\bf p}^{0}+i{\bf p}^{1}.

Next we introduce an involution
\mathcal{C}
acting on the
phase space
\mathcal{M}\equiv\{q_{k}(x),p_{k}(x)\}_{k=1}^{n}
as
follows:

\displaystyle\mbox{1)}\qquad\mathcal{C}(F(p_{k},q_{k}))=F(\mathcal{C}(p_{k}),\mathcal{C}(q_{k})),

\displaystyle\mbox{2)}\qquad\mathcal{C}\left(\{F(p_{k},q_{k}),G(p_{k},q_{k})\}\right)=\left\{\mathcal{C}(F),\mathcal{C}(G)\right\},

(11)

\displaystyle\mbox{3)}\qquad\mathcal{C}(H(p_{k},q_{k}))=H(p_{k},q_{k}).

Here
F(p_{k},q_{k})
,
G(p_{k},q_{k})
and the Hamiltonian
H(p_{k},q_{k})
are
functionals on
\mathcal{M}
depending analytically on the fields

q_{k}(x,t)
and
p_{k}(x,t)
.

The complexification of the ATFT is rather straightforward. The resulting
complex ATFT (CATFT) can be written down as standard Hamiltonian system with
twice as many fields
{\bf q}^{a}(x,t)
,
{\bf p}^{a}(x,t)
,
a=0,1
:

{\bf p}^{\mathbb{C}}(x,t)={\bf p}^{0}(x,t)+i{\bf p}^{1}(x,t),\qquad{\bf q}^{\mathbb{C}}(x,t)={\bf q}^{0}(x,t)+i{\bf q}^{1}(x,t),

(12)

\{{q}_{k}^{0}(x,t),{p}_{j}^{0}(y,t)\}=-\{{q}_{k}^{1}(x,t),{p}_{j}^{1}(y,t)\}=\delta_{kj}\delta(x-y).

(13)

The densities of the corresponding Hamiltonian and symplectic form equal

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm ATFT}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re\,}\mathcal{H}_{\rm ATFT}({\bf p}^{0}+i{\bf p}^{1},{\bf q}^{0}+i{\bf q}^{1})

(14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 2}({\bf p}^{0},{\bf p}^{0})-{1\over 2}({\bf p}^{1},{\bf p}^{1})+\sum_{k=1}^{r}n_{k}e^{-({\bf q}^{0},\alpha_{k})}\cos(({\bf q}^{1},\alpha_{k})),

\displaystyle\omega^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d{\bf p}^{0}\wedge id{\bf q}^{0})-(d{\bf p}^{1}\wedge d{\bf q}^{1}).

(15)

The family of RHF then are obtained from the CATFT by imposing an
invariance condition with respect to the involution

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}\equiv\mathcal{C}\circ\ast
where by

\ast
we denote the complex conjugation. The involution

\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
splits the phase space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{C}}
into a direct sum
\mathcal{M}^{\mathbb{C}}\equiv{\cal M}_{+}^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{-}^{\mathbb{C}}
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{+}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{0}\oplus i\mathcal{M}_{1},

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{M}_{-}^{\mathbb{C}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{0}\oplus-i\mathcal{M}_{1},

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}({\boldsymbol{q}}^{+}+i{\boldsymbol{q}}^{-})

\displaystyle=({\boldsymbol{q}}^{+}-i{\boldsymbol{q}}^{-}),

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{C}({\boldsymbol{p}}^{+}+i{\boldsymbol{p}}^{-})

\displaystyle=({\boldsymbol{p}}^{+}-i{\boldsymbol{p}}^{-}).

Each involution
\mathcal{C}
on
{\cal M}
induces an involution
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Thus to each involution
\mathcal{C}
one can relate a RHF of the ATFT. Due to Property 3),
\mathcal{C}^{\#}

preserves the system of admissible roots of
\mathfrak{g}
(and thus the
extended Dynkin diagrams of
\mathfrak{g}
).

Indeed, the condition 3) above requires that:

(\mathcal{C}({\boldsymbol{q}}),\alpha)=({\boldsymbol{q}},\mathcal{C}^{\#}(\alpha)),\qquad\alpha\in\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},

(17)

and therefore we must have
\mathcal{C}(\pi_{\mathfrak{g}})=\pi_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

The relation (
17
) defines uniquely the relation between

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
. Using
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
we can
split the root space
{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
into direct sum
{\mathbb{E}}^{n}={\mathbb{E}}_{+}\oplus{\mathbb{E}}_{-}
of two eigensubspaces of
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
. Taking the average
of the roots
\alpha_{j}
with respect to
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
we get:

\beta_{j}={1\over 2}(\alpha_{j}+\mathcal{C}^{\#}(\alpha_{j})),\qquad j=0,\dots,n_{+}=\dim{\mathbb{E}}_{+}.

(18)

By construction the set
\{\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n_{+}}\}
will be a set of admissible roots for some Kac-Moody algebra
with rank
n_{+}
. Graphically each set of admissible roots can be
represented by an extended Dynkin diagrams. Therefore one can
relate an automorphism
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
to each
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}

symmetry of the extended Dynkin diagram.

The splitting of
{\mathbb{E}}^{n}
naturally leads to the splittings of the
fields:

\displaystyle{\bf p}={\bf p}^{+}+{\bf p}^{-},\qquad{\bf q}={\bf q}^{+}+{\bf q}^{-},

(19)

where
{\bf p}^{+},{\bf q}^{+}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{+}
and
{\bf p}^{-},{\bf q}^{-}\in{\mathbb{E}}_{-}
. If
we also introduce:

\gamma_{j}={1\over 2}(\alpha_{j}-\mathcal{C}^{\#}(\alpha_{j})),\qquad j=0,\dots,n_{-}=\dim{\mathbb{E}}_{-}.

(20)

The Hamiltonian along with the terms related to the simple roots, contains also the minimal root
\alpha_{0}
given in (
6
).
The RHF of ATFT are more general integrable systems than the models
described in
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
40
,
41
]
which involve only the fields
{\bf q}^{+}
,
{\bf p}^{+}
invariant with respect to
\mathcal{C}
.

\begin{split}{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{1})&=\frac{1}{2}(e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}-e_{4}),\quad{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{2})=\frac{1}{2}(e_{1}+e_{2}-e_{3}+e_{4}),\\
{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{3})&=\frac{1}{2}(e_{1}-e_{2}+e_{3}+e_{4}),\quad{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{4})=\frac{1}{2}(e_{1}-e_{2}-e_{3}-e_{4}),\end{split}

(29)

i.e.

\begin{split}{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{1}+e_{2})=e_{1}+e_{2},\qquad{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{2}-e_{3})=e_{2}-e_{3},\qquad{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{1}-e_{2})=e_{3}-e_{4},\\
{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{3}-e_{4})=e_{3}+e_{4},\qquad{\cal W}^{\#}(e_{3}+e_{4})=e_{1}-e_{2},\end{split}

(30)

and

\displaystyle\beta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(e_{1}+e_{2}),\qquad\beta_{2}=(e_{2}-e_{3}),

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 3}\left[\alpha_{1}+{\cal W}^{\#}(\alpha_{1})+{\cal W}^{\#}{}^{2}(\alpha_{1})\right]={1\over 3}(e_{1}-e_{2}+2e_{3}).

This is the extended root system of
{\bf G}_{2}^{(1)}
(see Figure
2
(b)). This results to a RHF related to
{\bf G}_{2}^{(1)}
with Hamiltonian given by

H_{{\bf G}_{2}^{(1)}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}x\,\left({1\over 2}\left(p_{1}^{+}\right)^{2}+{1\over 2}\left(p_{2}^{+}\right)^{2}+{1\over 2}\left(p_{3}^{+}\right)^{2}+{\rm e}^{-2(\beta_{0},{\bf q})}+2{\rm e}^{-2(\beta_{1},{\bf q})}+3{\rm e}^{-2(\beta_{2},{\bf q})}\right),

where the three component vectors
{\bf q}
and
{\bf p}
are restricted by
q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}=0
and
p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3}=0
.

The above results establish the following

Proposition 1
.

Let us consider the RHF of ATFT using the external automorphisms of the algebras
{\bf D}^{(1)}_{r+1}
.
If we use the second order external automorphisms, then

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{R}}_{{\bf D}^{(1)}_{r+1}}=\mathcal{H}_{{\bf B}^{(1)}_{r}},\qquad\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{R}}_{{\bf D}^{(1)}_{4}}=\mathcal{H}_{{\bf B}^{(1)}_{3}}.\end{split}

(31)

If we use the third order external automorphism of
D_{4}
, then we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}^{\mathbb{R}}_{{\bf D}^{(1)}_{4}}=\mathcal{H}_{{\bf G}^{(1)}_{2}}.\end{split}

(32)

The next step would be to analyze the more general situation when the RHF is generated by
\mathcal{C}_{0}\circ\mathcal{C}^{\#}

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a generic suitable automorphism and
\mathcal{C}^{\#}
is an external automorphism.
Our hypothesis is that the above Proposition will hold true also for this more general case.

We presented here real Hamiltonian forms of affine Toda field theories related to the untwisted complex Kac-Moody algebra

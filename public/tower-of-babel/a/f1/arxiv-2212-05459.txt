Abstract
. We use a suitable transform related to Sobolev inequality to investigate the sharp constants and optimizers for some Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg-type inequalities which are related to the weighted p p -Laplace equations. Moreover, we give the classification to the linearized problem related to the radial extremals. As an application, we investigate the gradient type remainder term of related inequality by using spectral estimate combined with a compactness argument which extends the work of Figalli and Zhang (Duke Math. J. 2022) at least for radial case.



1.
Introduction and statement the main results

In this paper, we consider the classical radial solutions of the following weighted equation:

(1.1)

-{\rm div}(|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|x|^{\beta}u^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}-1},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

(1.2)

1<p<N,\quad p-N<\alpha<p+\beta,\quad p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{p(N+\beta)}{N-p+\alpha}.

This problem, for
\alpha\neq 0
or
\beta\neq 0
, generalizes the well-known equation which involves the critical Sobolev exponent

(1.3)

-{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=u^{p^{*}-1},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
. Caffarelli et al.
[
13
]
proved that all the solutions are indeed the only ones of Aubin-Talenti functions
[
34
]
when
p=2
. Recently, Vétois
[
35
]
obtained the same conclusion for
1<p<2
and Sciunzi
[
32
]
for
2<p<N
however in

\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):|\nabla u|\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\},

and it is not clear whether Aubin-Talenti functions have Moser index equal to one so far. Catino et al.
[
14
]
and Ou
[
28
]
removed the restricted condition
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some special cases.
We summarize those results as follows: let
1<p<N
and
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a solution to (
1.3
), then there exist
\lambda>0
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

u(x)=V_{\lambda,z}(x):=\frac{\gamma_{N,p}\lambda^{\frac{N-p}{p}}}{(1+\lambda^{\frac{p}{p-1}}|x-z|^{\frac{p}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p}{p}}},\quad\gamma_{N,p}=\left[N\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\frac{N-p}{p^{2}}},

and they are only extremal functions (up to scalar multiplications) for the well-known Sobolev inequality

(1.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla v|^{p}\geq S\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v|^{p^{*}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}}},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

for some constant
S>0
. Pistoia and Vaira
[
30
]
proved that the solution
V\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of equation (
1.3
) is non-degenerate in the sense that all solutions of equation

(1.5)

\begin{split}&-{\rm div}(|\nabla V|^{p-2}\nabla\varphi)-(p-2){\rm div}(|\nabla V|^{p-4}(\nabla V\cdot\nabla\varphi)\nabla V)=\left(p^{*}-1\right)V^{p^{*}-2}\varphi\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\end{split}

in the space
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, are linear combination of the functions

(1.6)

Z_{0}(x)=\frac{N-p}{p}V+x\cdot\nabla V,\quad Z_{i}(x)=\frac{\partial V(x)}{\partial x_{i}},\quad i=1,\ldots,N.

Here
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the weighted Sobolev space which is defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla V|^{p-2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Pistoia and Vaira proved that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
continuously, where
L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the set of measurable functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
whose norm

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V^{p^{*}-2}\varphi^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, Figalli and Neumayer
[
20
]
proved that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
compactly when
2\leq p<N
then they showed the solutions of (
1.5
) in
L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
are linear combination of the functions

Z_{0}
and
Z_{i}

(i=1,\ldots,N)
. Figalli and Zhang
[
21
]
proved that
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{0,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
compactly for all
1<p<N
and the non-degenerate conclusion of
[
20
]
also holds.

If
p-N<\alpha=p+\beta
, (
1.1
) is related to the well known weighted Hardy inequality:

(1.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq\left(\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p}\right)^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha-p}|u|^{p}dx,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
[(N-p+\alpha)/p]^{p}
is sharp and not attained which implies that (
1.1
) does not exist solutions in this case, see
[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
. If
\alpha=0
,
p=N
and
\beta>-N
, the problem

-{\rm div}(|\nabla u|^{N-2}\nabla u)=|x|^{\beta}e^{u}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}e^{u}<\infty,

has its own interest, see
[
19
,
29
]
.

Let
\alpha=-pa
,
\beta=-bh
, then for
1<p<N
, (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(1.8)

-{\rm div}(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=|x|^{-bh}u^{h-1},\quad u>0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},

where

-\infty<a<\frac{N-p}{p},\quad a-\frac{N-p}{p}<b<a+1,\quad h=\frac{Np}{N-p(1+a-b)},

which is related the classical Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (we write (CKN) for short) inequality
[
12
]
:

(1.9)

\||x|^{-a}\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\geq C\||x|^{-b}u\|_{L^{h}(\mathbb{R}^{N})},\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For equation (
1.8
) with
p=2
, Dolbeault et al.
[
17
]
proved an optimal rigidity result in the range
2<h<\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Namely, assuming the integrability condition
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-bh}u^{h}dx<\infty
, they showed this equation has a unique (therefore radial) nonnegative solution either for

0\leq a<\frac{N-2}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad a\leq b<a+1,

or for

a<0\quad\mbox{and}\quad b^{(1)}_{FS}(a)\leq b<a+1,

where

b^{(k)}_{FS}(a):=\frac{N}{2}\left[1+\frac{4k(N-2+k)}{(N-2-2a)^{2}}\right]^{-1/2}-\frac{N-2-2a}{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

When
a<0
and
a\leq b<b^{(1)}_{FS}(a)
, Felli and Schneider
[
22
]
have previously shown that the best constant in (
1.9
) with
p=2
is achieved by non-radial functions only and, as a byproduct, (
1.8
) with
p=2
has non-radial nonnegative solutions and uniqueness is broken. Moreover, if
b=b^{(k)}_{FS}(a)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, non-radial bifurcation occurs, see
[
5
,
15
,
23
]
. It is worth to mention that for
p\neq 2
, Ciraolo and Corso
[
8
]
proved that the positive solutions to (
1.8
) are radial symmetric for some special case which extends the result of Dolbeault et al.
[
17
]
to a general
p
.
For more recent results about the (CKN) inequality, see
[
3
,
2
,
9
,
16
,
24
]
and the references therein.

Coming back to (
1.1
), in present paper, we are mainly concerned the linearized problem related to the radial solutions of (
1.1
).
Define
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the norm

(1.10)

\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla\phi|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Then we quote the following result by
[
12
]
, the so-called Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality, which, in the radial case, partly extends the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
1<p<N
and
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
. Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a radial function, then we have

(1.11)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\geq S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}|u|^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}},

for some positive constant
S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
. The explicit form of the best constant in (
1.11
) is

(1.12)

S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p}\right)^{\frac{pN-p+(p-1)\beta+\alpha}{N+\beta}}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{N+\beta}}C_{p}\left(\frac{p(N+\beta)}{p+\beta-\alpha}\right),

where

C_{p}(K)=K\left(\frac{K-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(K/p)\Gamma(1+K-K/p)}{\Gamma(K)\Gamma(1+K/2)}\frac{\Gamma(K/2)}{2}\right)^{p/K}.

Moreover the extremal radial functions which achieve
S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
in (
1.11
) are given by

(1.13)

W_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{A\lambda^{\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p}}}{(1+\lambda^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}}|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p+\beta-\alpha}}},

for any
A\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
\lambda>0
.

It is well known that the extremal functions of
S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
in (
1.11
) are the ground state solutions of the weighted
p
-Laplace equation (
1.1
). Thus, as the direct consequence of Theorem
1.1
, we obtain

Corollary 1.2
.

Assume
1<p<N
and
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
. Then problem (
1.1
) in the space
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

has a radial solution of the form

(1.14)

U_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}(x)=\frac{C_{N,\alpha,\beta}\lambda^{\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p}}}{(1+\lambda^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}}|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p+\beta-\alpha}}},

with
\lambda>0
, where

C_{N,\alpha,\beta}=\left[(N+\beta)\left(\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\right]^{\frac{N-p+\alpha}{p(p+\beta-\alpha)}}.

Our second result concerns the study of the linearized problem related to (
1.1
) at the function
U_{1,\alpha,\beta}
. This leads to study the problem:

\displaystyle-{\rm div}(|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla U_{1,\alpha,\beta}|^{p-2}\nabla v)-(p-2){\rm div}(|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla U_{1,\alpha,\beta}|^{p-4}(\nabla U_{1,\alpha,\beta}\cdot\nabla v)\nabla U_{1,\alpha,\beta})

(1.15)

\displaystyle=(p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}-1)|x|^{\beta}U_{1,\alpha,\beta}^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}-2}v\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad v\in L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Here, the weighted Sobolev space
L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is defined as the completion of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with respect to the norm

(1.16)

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}_{\beta,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}U_{1,\alpha,\beta}^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}-2}\varphi^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the classification of solutions for above equation, see
[
1
,
31
]
with
\alpha=\beta=0
and
p=2
,
[
23
]
with
\alpha=0
and
p=2
,
[
5
,
15
]
with
p=2
, and
[
20
,
21
,
30
]
with
\alpha=\beta=0
. Next theorem characterizes all the solutions to (
1
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
1<p<N
and
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
.
If

(1.17)

\left(\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p}\right)^{2}\left[\frac{p(N+\beta)}{p+\beta-\alpha}-1\right]=k(N-2+k),\quad\mbox{for some}\quad k\in\mathbb{N}^{+},

then the space of solutions of (
1
) has dimension
1+\frac{(N+2k-2)(N+k-3)!}{(N-2)!k!}
and is spanned by

(1.18)

W_{0}(x)=\frac{(p-1)-|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}}}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N+\beta}{p+\beta-\alpha}}},\quad W_{k,i}(x)=\frac{|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p(p-1)}}\Psi_{k,i}(x)}{(1+|x|^{\frac{p+\beta-\alpha}{p-1}})^{\frac{N+\beta}{p+\beta-\alpha}}},

where
\{\Psi_{k,i}\}
,
i=1,\ldots,\frac{(N+2k-2)(N+k-3)!}{(N-2)!k!}
, form a basis of
\mathbb{Y}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the space of all homogeneous harmonic polynomials of degree
k
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Otherwise the space of solutions of (
1
) has dimension one and is spanned by
W_{0}(x)
and in this case we say the solution
U_{1,\alpha,\beta}
of equation (
1.1
) is non-degenerate.

Remark 1.4
.

The key step of the proofs of the above three conclusions is the change of variables
r\mapsto r^{\frac{p}{p+\beta-\alpha}}
, which are mainly inspired by
[
11
]
, see also
[
23
, Theorem A.1]
. Recently, after completing this work, we find a paper of Su and Wang
[
33
]
which gives analogous results as Theorem
1.1
by using same methods. For the completeness of this paper, we keep this result and our main conclusion is Theorem
1.3
.

Note that in the case
\alpha=\beta=0
we get
k=1
and one gets back the well known result for the equation involving the critical Sobolev exponent, see
[
30
]
. Furthermore, for all
\alpha\neq 0
or
\beta\neq 0
the solutions of problem (
1.1
) are invariant for dilations but not for translations, and Theorem
1.3
highlights the new phenomenon that if (
1.17
) holds then there exist new solutions to (
1
) that “replace” the ones due to the translations invariance. Indeed, nonradial solutions might appear. When
p=2
,
\alpha=0
,
\beta=2
and
N\geq 4
is even, Gladiali, Grossi and Neves
[
23
]
constructed the nonradial solutions to equation (
1.1
), that is, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the functions

u(x)=u(|x^{\prime}|,|x^{\prime\prime}|)=C_{N,0,2}(1+|x|^{4}-2a(|x^{\prime}|^{2}-|x^{\prime\prime}|^{2})+a^{2})^{-\frac{N-2}{4}},

form a branch of solutions to (
1.1
) bifurcating from
U_{1,0,2}
, where
(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}=\mathbb{R}^{\frac{N}{2}}\times\mathbb{R}^{\frac{N}{2}}
.

As mentioned above, we will extend the work of Figalli and Zhang
[

]
at least in radial space.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume
2\leq p<N
and
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
. Then there exists constant
B_{1}=B_{1}(N,p,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p}dx-S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}|u|^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}}\geq B_{1}{\rm dist}(u,\mathcal{M})^{p}.

where
\mathcal{M}=\{cU_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}:c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0\}
is a two-dimensional manifold (see
U_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}
as in (
1.14
)), and
{\rm dist}(u,\mathcal{M}):=\inf_{c\in\mathbb{R},\lambda>0}\|u-cU_{\lambda,\alpha,\beta}\|_{\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Assume
1<p<2\leq N
and
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
. Then there exists constant
B_{2}=B_{2}(N,p,\alpha,\beta)>0
such that for every
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p}dx-S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}|u|^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}}\geq B_{2}{\rm dist}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}.

Remark 1.7
.

In this paper, to handle the general case
1<p<N
and obtain the remainder terms, as stated in
[
21
]
, we need to consider three cases
1<p\leq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}
,
\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}<p<2
, and
2\leq p<N
by using different arguments.
In fact, we note that
p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{p(N+\beta)}{N-p+\alpha}\leq 2
implies
p\leq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}
,
p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}>2
implies
p>\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}
, respectively. Moreover,
\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}<2
is equivalent to
\alpha<2+\beta
. Therefore, in order to prove Theorem
1.6
, we will split our problem into two cases:

(i)

1<p\leq\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}
;

(ii)

\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}<p<2
,

due to
p-N<\alpha<p+\beta
, then
1<p<2
implies
\alpha<2+\beta
and also
\frac{2(N+\alpha)}{N+2+\beta}<2
.
The reason why we consider the above two cases is that, it needs some appropriate algebraic inequalities which requires to compare
p
and
p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}
with
2
, see Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
. However,
2\leq p<N
implies
p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}>2
, and the
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm is stronger than any weighted
\mathcal{D}^{1,2}_{\alpha,*}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm (see (
4.7
)), so that we can deal with this case directly.

Furthermore,
by using perturbation methods as in
[
21
]
, it is easy to verify that the exponents
p
in Theorem
1.5
and
2
in Theorem
1.6
are sharp.

2.
Sharp constant and optimizers

Let
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the radial space of
\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as in (
1.19
).
Then the best constant
S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
in (
1.11
) can be defined as

(2.1)

S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta):=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{p}dx}{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{\beta}|u|^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}}}.

We will use a suitable transform that is changing the variable
r\mapsto r^{\frac{p}{p+\beta-\alpha}}
, related to Sobolev inequality to investigate the sharp constant
S^{rad}_{p}(\alpha,\beta)
and optimizers.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We follow the arguments in the proof of
[
23
, Theorem A.1]
. Let
u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,p}_{\alpha,r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Making the changes that
v(s)=u(r)
and
r=s^{t}
where
t>0
will be given later, then we have that

\begin{split}&\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{\alpha}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{p}r^{N-1}dr=t^{1-p}\int^{\infty}_{0}|v^{\prime}(s)|^{p}s^{(N-p+\alpha)t+p-1}ds.\end{split}

Set

(2.2)

K:=(N-p+\alpha)t+p>p,

which implies

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{\alpha}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{p}r^{N-1}dr=t^{1-p}\int^{\infty}_{0}|v^{\prime}(s)|^{p}s^{K-1}ds.\end{split}

When
K
is an integer, we use the classical Sobolev inequality (see
[
34
]
) and we get

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}|v^{\prime}(s)|^{p}s^{K-1}ds\geq&C_{p}(K)\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|v(s)|^{\frac{pK}{K-p}}s^{K-1}ds\right)^{\frac{K-p}{K}}\\
=&t^{-\frac{K-p}{K}}C_{p}(K)\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}|u(r)|^{\frac{pK}{K-p}}r^{\frac{K}{t}-1}dr\right)^{\frac{K-p}{K}},\end{split}

where

see

. Moreover, even

In order to get (

So we get

\begin{split}\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{\alpha}|u^{\prime}(r)|^{p}r^{N-1}dr\geq t^{-p+\frac{p}{K}}C_{p}(K)\left(\int^{\infty}_{0}r^{\beta}|u(r)|^{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}r^{N-1}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{\alpha,\beta}}},\end{split}

Moreover, from the previous inequalities, we also get that the extremal functions are obtained as

It is well known that

[

]
directly. Setting

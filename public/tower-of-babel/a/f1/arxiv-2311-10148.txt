Ultra-light dark matter explanation of NANOGrav observations
Debtosh Chowdhury, Arpan Hait, Subhendra Mohanty, Suraj Prakash

Abstract
\vskip2.84544pt The angular correlation of pulsar residuals observed by NANOGrav NANOGrav:2023gor and other pulsar timing array (PTA) collaborations show evidence in support of the Hellings-Downs correlation HD expected from stochastic gravitational wave background (SGWB). In this paper, we offer a non-gravitational wave explanation of the observed pulsar timing correlations as caused by an ultra-light L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} gauge boson dark matter (ULDM). ULDM can affect the pulsar correlations in two ways. The gravitational potential of vector ULDM gives rise to a Shapiro time delay of the pulsar signals and a non-trivial angular correlation (as compared to the scalar ULDM case). In addition, if the pulsars have a non-zero charge of the dark matter gauge group, then the electric field of the local dark matter causes an oscillation of the pulsar and a corresponding Doppler shift of the pulsar signal. We point out that pulsars carry a significant charge of muons, and thus the L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} vector dark matter contributes to both the Doppler oscillations and the time delay of the pulsar signals. The synergy between these two effects provides a better fit to the shape of the angular correlation function, as observed by the NANOGrav collaboration, compared to the standard SGWB explanation or the SGWB combined with time delay explanations. Our analysis shows that in addition to the SGWB signal, there may potentially be excess timing residuals attributable to the L μ − L τ L_{\mu}-L_{\tau} ULDM.

Proca dark matter

The Lagrangian for a massive vector field is given by

\displaystyle{\cal L}=-\cfrac{1}{4}\,Z^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}\,Z^{\prime\mu\nu}+\cfrac{1}{2}\,m^{2}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,Z^{\prime\mu}+g\,J_{\mu}\,Z^{\prime\mu}\,.

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}+m^{2}\right)Z^{\prime\mu}=0\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\,Z^{\prime t}-\nabla\cdot\vec{Z}^{\prime}=0\,.

From Eq. (

Conclusions

In this paper, we proposed a non-gravitational explanation of NANOGrav pulsar timing observations
NANOGrav:2023gor
;
NANOGrav:2023hvm
. We proposed that
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
gauge bosons as the ultralight dark matter, which can have two effects on pulsar timings observed on Earth. Firstly, pulsars carry about
\sim 10^{55}
muons, therefore they will oscillate with a frequency

due to the
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
‘electric field’ of the dark matter. This will give rise to an angular correlation between pulsar residuals proportional to

as compared to pulsar oscillations due to stochastic gravitational wave background which give the well-known Hellings-Downs angular correlations
HD

Appendix A
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
charge of pulsars

In the neutron stars, electrons are degenerate and there is a beta-equilibrium in the processes
n\rightarrow p+e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}
,
n\rightarrow p+\mu^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{\mu}
and
\mu^{-}\rightarrow e^{-}+\bar{\nu}_{e}+\nu_{\mu}
. The charge neutrality condition constrains the number densities of
p,\,e^{-}
and
\mu^{-}
to obey the relation
n_{p}=n_{e}+n_{\mu}
.
In neutron stars electrons are degenerate and have a Fermi momentum
k_{f}=(3\pi^{2}n_{e})^{1/3}
greater than the mass of the muon
m_{\mu}\sim 106\,{\rm MeV}
. There is a beta blocking of the outgoing electrons in the muon decay and muons are stable inside neutron stars. The equilibrium of the beta-decay processes in the neutron stars gives the relations between the chemical potential of the different species, i.e.,
\mu_{n}=\mu_{p}+\mu_{e}=\mu_{p}+\mu_{\mu}
which implies that
\mu_{e}=\mu_{\mu}
. The chemical potential of the degenerate electrons is the Fermi-energy given by

\displaystyle\mu_{e}={\epsilon_{e}}_{f}={k_{e}}_{f}=\left(3\pi^{2}n_{e}\right)^{1/3}\,.

(35)

The muon-chemical potential written in terms of muon number density is

\displaystyle\mu_{\mu}={\epsilon_{\mu}}_{f}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(m_{\mu}^{2}+{{k_{\mu}}_{f}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(m_{\mu}^{2}+\left(3\pi^{2}n_{\mu}\right)^{2/3}\right)^{1/2}\,.

Equating Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
) we can solve for
\nu_{\mu}
in terms of
\nu_{e}
to obtain

\displaystyle n_{\mu}=\cfrac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\left[\left(3\pi^{2}n_{e}\right)^{2/3}-m_{\mu}^{2}\right]^{3/2}.

(37)

Model calculations give the electron fraction
Y_{e}=n_{e}/n=0.052
and the nucleon density in neutron stars
n=0.238{\,\rm fm^{-3}}

Pearson:2018tkr
. This gives the number density of electrons in neutron stars
n_{e}=Y_{e}\,n=0.01237\,{\rm fm^{-3}}=1.237\times 10^{37}\,{\rm cm^{-3}}
. Using this in Eq. (
37
), we obtain the number density of muons in neutron stars to be
n_{\mu}=3.63\times 10^{36}{\,\rm cm^{-3}}
which implies that a typical neutron star with of
R=10{\,\rm km}
radius will carry a total muon number

\displaystyle N_{\mu}=n_{\mu}\left(\cfrac{4\pi}{3}R^{3}\right)=1.55\times 10^{55}\,.

(38)

The
L_{\mu}-L_{\tau}
charge of a neutron star is then the coupling constant times the number of muons,
Q=gN_{\mu}

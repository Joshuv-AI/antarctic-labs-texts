Vacuum stability of the type II seesaw leptogenesis from inflation
Chengcheng Han 1, Sihui Huang 1, Zhanhong Lei 1

Abstract
Recently it has been found that introducing a triplet Higgs to the standard model could provide a feasible leptogenesis to generate the baryon asymmetry of our universe, providing that the inflation is driven by the mixing state of the triplet Higgs and SM Higgs. In this work, we survey the viable parameter space satisfying the vacuum stability and perturbativity in this model. We find that the introduction of the triplet Higgs would also ameliorate the problem of the Higgs vacuum instability. We present two representative parameter regions where the origin of neutrino masses, baryon asymmetry of the universe as well as inflation can be explained while keeping consistent with the condition of vacuum stability and perturbativity.



1
Introduction

The Standard Model(SM), a theory for strong and electroweak interactions, is regarded as a landmark of modern particle physics. However, there remain several unsolved problems in particle physics and cosmology, such as the origin of the cosmic inflation
Brout:1977ix
;
Sato:1980yn
;
Guth:1980zm
;
Linde:1981mu
;
Albrecht:1982wi
, neutrino masses, and baryon asymmetry of our universe. Numerous models have been promoted to solve one or two of the above problems but rarely solve all of them at once. Interestingly, recently the authors found that the type II seesaw model
Magg:1980ut
;
Cheng:1980qt
;
Lazarides:1980nt
;
Mohapatra:1980yp
could provide a simple framework to address all three problems simultaneously
Barrie:2021mwi
;
Barrie:2022cub
. The idea is that the neutrino masses are generated by the traditional type II seesaw mechanism where the neutrino masses are provided by the vacuum value of the neutral part of the triplet Higgs, and the inflation is driven by the mixing state of the triplet Higgs and SM Higgs doublet in the early universes, and the baryon asymmetry is generated by the Affleck-Dine mechanism
Affleck:1984fy
;
Dine:1995kz
during the inflation.

However, since the model contains many additional scalars, it is important to examine whether such a model satisfies various theoretical constraints, particularly, the stability of the vacuum and the perturbativity of the parameters. Actually, the standard model Higgs itself already suffers from the instability problem due to the second minimum of the potential at the high energy scale
Buttazzo:2013uya
;
Han:2018yrk
. It would be interesting to study whether this problem can be addressed by the introduction of the triplet Higgs. Although there is already a lot of work concerning the vacuum stability and perturbativity in the framework of type II seesaw
Arhrib:2011uy
;
Chun:2012jw
;
BhupalDev:2013xol
;
Bonilla:2015eha
;
Haba:2016zbu
;
Moultaka:2020dmb
, many of them focus on these problems around the electroweak scale. However, in the model of type II seesaw triplet leptogenesis
Barrie:2021mwi
;
Barrie:2022cub
, the triplet Higgs also plays a role of inflaton. If a second vacuum develops at a higher energy scale, we could possibly either worry about the transition of our vacuum into the second vacuum or that we keep staying in the second vacuum from the beginning. The reason for the latter statement is that after the inflation, there exists an oscillation stage of the triplet Higgs and SM Higgs doublet
Barrie:2021mwi
;
Barrie:2022cub
, and then there is a high chance that the oscillation would end at the second vacuum. One may wonder whether the thermal effect during the radiation stage following reheating could save this situation. However, it is believed that the reheating temperature could not be much higher than
\sim 10^{14}
GeV
Sfakianakis:2018lzf
;
Barrie:2021mwi
. If the second minimum develops at a higher scale, we would still keep staying in the second vacuum. In this paper, to simplify our analysis we require the electroweak vacuum to be absolutely stable and no second vacuum develops at any intermediate scale between the electroweak scale and Planck scale.

In addition, to generate the baryon asymmetry, the inflation should be driven by a mixing state of the triplet Higgs and the SM Higgs doublet, providing additional conditions on the viable parameter space. Therefore it would be intriguing to give a full analysis of the parameter space satisfying the vacuum stability and perturbativity, keeping the above three problems being resolved.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.
2
we overview the idea of the leptogenesis from Higgs inflation.
In Sec.
3
we summarize the theoretical requirement of vacuum stability as well as the necessary condition for Higgs inflation with successful leptogenesis. We show our numerical result in Sec.
4
and we draw the conclusion in Sec.
6
.

2
Leptogenesis from Higgs inflation

In this section, we briefly overview how leptogenesis could occur if the inflation is provided by a mixture of triplet Higgs and SM Higgs doublet. We will first show the condition of such mixture to drive the inflation and then present the Affleck-Dine mechanism to generate the baryon asymmetry during the inflation stage.

2.1
Inflation from Higgs

The Type-II seesaw mechanism introduces a triplet scalar
\Delta
to SM, which transforms as
(1,3,2)
under the
SU(3)_{c}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}
gauge group and carries a lepton number
l_{\Delta}=-2
. Under tensor representation of
SU(2)_{L}
, the triplet
\Delta
and SM Higgs field can be written as:

\displaystyle\Delta=\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta^{+}/\sqrt{2}&\Delta^{++}\\
\Delta^{0}&-\Delta^{+}/\sqrt{2}\end{matrix}\right)~~~H=\left(\begin{matrix}h^{+}\\
h\end{matrix}\right)~.

(1)

The potential of the Higgs fields is given by:

\displaystyle V(H,\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m^{2}_{H}H^{\dagger}H+m_{\Delta}^{2}\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)+\lambda_{H}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\lambda_{1}(H^{\dagger}H)\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)

(2)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}(\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta))^{2}+\lambda_{3}\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)^{2}+\lambda_{4}H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H+[\mu(H^{T}i\sigma^{2}\Delta^{\dagger}H)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{5}}{M_{p}}(H^{T}i\sigma^{2}\Delta^{\dagger}H)(H^{\dagger}H)+\frac{\lambda_{5}^{\prime}}{M_{p}}(H^{T}i\sigma^{2}\Delta^{\dagger}H)(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)+h.c.]~.

where the terms in the bracket break the
U(1)_{L}
symmetry and we also include the Planck suppressed operators. Even if the high dimension operators have no effect on the low energy physics, they could dominate the
U(1)_{L}
breaking terms during inflation.
The Yukawa term becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{Yukawa}=\mathcal{L}^{SM}_{Yukawa}-\frac{1}{2}Y_{\nu jk}\bar{L}^{c}_{j}i\sigma^{2}\Delta L_{k}+h.c.~.

(3)

However, the recent CMB observation already exclude the possibility of the potential being a simple polynomial function. The reason is that the limit of tensor to scalar ratio around 0.056 indicates that the potential of the inflaton should be flat enough at which inflation happens. Fortunately, it is known that in Higgs inflation a non-minimal coupling of Higgs field to gravity can flatten the potential and result in a Starobinsky type model. Therefore we extend the model with both H and
\Delta
taking the non-minimal coupling to gravity, and then the Lagrangian is as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g}}=&-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{P}R-\xi_{H}H^{\dagger}HR-\xi_{\Delta}\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)R\\
&-g^{\mu\nu}(D_{\mu}H)^{\dagger}(D_{\nu}H)-g^{\mu\nu}\text{Tr}(D_{\mu}\Delta)^{\dagger}(D_{\nu}\Delta)-V(H,\Delta)+\mathcal{L}_{Yukawa}~.\end{aligned}

(4)

To simplify our analysis, we only focus on the neutral components of
\Delta
and
H
. The relevant lagrangian becomes:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g}}=&-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}_{P}R-\xi_{H}|h|^{2}R-\xi_{\Delta}|\Delta^{0}|^{2}R-(\partial_{\mu}h)^{2}-(\partial_{\mu}\Delta^{0})^{2}\\
&-V(h,\Delta^{0})-[\frac{1}{2}Y_{\nu ij}\bar{\nu}^{c}_{i}\nu_{j}\Delta^{0}+h.c.]+\cdots~,\end{aligned}

(5)

where
V(h,\Delta^{0})
is defined as

\displaystyle V(h,\Delta^{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m_{H}^{2}|h|^{2}+m_{\Delta}^{2}|\Delta^{0}|^{2}+\lambda_{H}|h|^{4}+\lambda_{\Delta}|\Delta^{0}|^{4}+\lambda_{H\Delta}|h|^{2}|\Delta^{0}|^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle-\left(\mu h^{2}{\Delta^{0}}^{*}+\frac{\lambda_{5}}{M_{p}}|h|^{2}h^{2}{\Delta^{0}}^{*}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}_{5}}{M_{p}}|\Delta^{0}|^{2}h^{2}{\Delta^{0}}^{*}+h.c.\right)+...~,

where
\lambda_{\Delta}=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3},~\lambda_{H\Delta}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}
. The Yukawa term of lepton and
\Delta^{0}
provides a Majorana mass term for neutrino once
\Delta
obtains a non-vanishing vacuum expectation value.

We parameterize the
h
and
\Delta^{0}
by the polar coordinates:
h\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\rho_{H}e^{i\eta}
and
\Delta^{0}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\rho_{\Delta}e^{i\theta}
. Then the Lagrangian can be written as:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g}}=-\frac{1}{2}(M_{P}^{2}+\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2})R-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\rho_{H})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\rho_{\Delta})^{2}-V(h,\Delta^{0})+\cdots~.

(7)

Considering
\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2}\gg 1
during inflation, we can perform a weyl transformation:

\displaystyle g_{E\mu\nu}=\Omega^{2}g_{\mu\nu},~~\Omega^{2}=1+\frac{\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}+\frac{\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2}}{M_{P}^{2}}~.

(8)

Then Lagrangian is translated from Jordan frame into Einstein frame:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g_{E}}}=-\frac{1}{2}M_{P}^{2}R_{E}-\frac{1}{2\Omega^{2}}\left((\partial_{\mu}\rho_{H})^{2}+(\partial_{\mu}\rho_{\Delta})^{2}\right)-3M_{P}^{2}(\partial_{\mu}\text{log}\Omega)^{2}-\frac{V(h,\Delta^{0})}{\Omega^{4}}+\cdots~.

(9)

Subsequent derivations in this section mainly follows
Lebedev:2011aq
. Here we use the natural unit
M_{p}=1
. Then from Eq.
8
and Eq.
9
we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{kin}=-\frac{3}{4}\left(\partial_{\mu}\text{log}(\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2})\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2(\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2})}\left((\partial_{\mu}\rho_{H})^{2}+(\partial_{\mu}\rho_{\Delta})^{2}\right)~.

(10)

We can redefine the fields:

\displaystyle\chi=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\text{log}(\xi_{H}\rho_{H}^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}\rho_{\Delta}^{2}),~~\kappa=\frac{\rho_{H}}{\rho_{\Delta}}~.

(11)

Plugging Eq.
11
into Eq.
10
and requiring a large non-minimal coupling
\xi_{H},\xi_{\Delta}\gg 1
, we get:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{kin}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\chi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\xi_{H}^{2}\kappa^{2}+\xi_{\Delta}^{2}}{(\xi_{H}\kappa^{2}+\xi_{\Delta})^{3}}(\partial_{\mu}\kappa)^{2}~.

(12)

For large field value, the quartic terms dominate the potential, thus the
\mu
term in
V(h,\Delta^{0})
can be ignored. Considering a large
\chi
, the potential of the scalar fields can be written as

\displaystyle U(\rho_{H},\rho_{\Delta})=\frac{V}{\Omega^{4}}=\frac{\lambda_{H}\kappa^{4}+\lambda_{H\Delta}\kappa^{2}+\lambda_{\Delta}}{4(\xi_{H}\kappa^{2}+\xi_{\Delta})^{2}}~,

(13)

with the following minima:

\displaystyle(1)~2\lambda_{H}\xi_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{H}>0,~2\lambda_{\Delta}\xi_{H}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{\Delta}>0,~\kappa=\sqrt{\frac{2\lambda_{\Delta}\xi_{H}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{\Delta}}{2\lambda_{H}\xi_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{H}}}~,

(14)

\displaystyle(2)~2\lambda_{H}\xi_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{H}>0,~2\lambda_{\Delta}\xi_{H}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{\Delta}<0,~\kappa=0~,

(15)

\displaystyle(3)~2\lambda_{H}\xi_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{H}<0,~2\lambda_{\Delta}\xi_{H}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{\Delta}>0,~\kappa=\infty~,

(16)

\displaystyle(4)~2\lambda_{H}\xi_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{H}<0,~2\lambda_{\Delta}\xi_{H}-\lambda_{H\Delta}\xi_{\Delta}<0,~\kappa=0,\infty~.

(17)

In scenario 1, the inflation is dominated by
\Delta^{0}
and
H
, which is just the case we are interested in. Thus we get our first constraint on the parameters to ensure the inflation is driven by a mixing state of the triplet Higgs and SM doublet Higgs, i.e,

\displaystyle x=\frac{\xi_{H}}{\xi_{\Delta}},~2\lambda_{H}-x\lambda_{H\Delta}>0,~2x\lambda_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}>0~.

(18)

Note that a necessary condition for vacuum stability is that
\lambda_{\Delta}>0,\lambda_{H}>0
. However,
\lambda_{H\Delta}
could be either positive or negative. Obviously, if
\lambda_{H\Delta}<0
, the above condition can be automatically satisfied, while for the case of
\lambda_{H\Delta}>0
, a dedicated study should be preformed and we leave the details of discussion in Sec.
4
.

2.2
Leptogensis from Higgs inflation

we can simplify our analysis by defining the inflaton as
\varphi
, through the following relations to the polar coordinate fields,

\displaystyle\rho_{H}=\varphi\sin\alpha,~\rho_{\Delta}=\varphi\cos\alpha~,

\displaystyle\xi\equiv\xi_{H}\sin^{2}\alpha+\xi_{\Delta}\cos^{2}\alpha~.

(19)

The Lagrangian in Eq. (
7
) is now given by,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g}}=-\frac{M_{p}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{\xi}{2}\varphi^{2}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}\cos^{2}\alpha~g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\theta\partial_{\nu}\theta-V(\varphi,\theta)~,

(20)

where

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636ptV(\varphi,\theta)=\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{4}\varphi^{4}+2\varphi^{3}\left(\tilde{\mu}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{5}}{M_{p}}\varphi^{2}\right)\cos\theta~,

(21)

and

\displaystyle m^{2}=m^{2}_{\Delta}\cos^{2}\alpha-m_{H}^{2}\sin^{2}\alpha~,

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda_{H}\sin^{4}\alpha+\lambda_{H\Delta}\sin^{2}\alpha\cos^{2}\alpha+\lambda_{\Delta}\cos^{4}\alpha~,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\mu\sin^{2}\alpha\cos\alpha~,

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{5}=-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}(\lambda_{5}\sin^{4}\alpha\cos\alpha+\lambda^{\prime}_{5}\sin^{2}\alpha\cos^{3}\alpha)~.

(22)

To fit the current CMB observation, we need roughly
\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\approx 5\times 10^{4}
. The Affleck-Dine mechanism will be realised in our scenario through the motion of the dynamical field
\theta
. The size of the generated lepton asymmetry will be determined by the size of the non-trivial motion induced in
\theta
sourced by inflation. During inflation,
m\ll\varphi
, which means that the quartic term in the Jordan frame potential dominates the inflationary dynamics.
The lepton number asymmetry generated by the motion of the triplet Higgs phase
\theta
at the end of inflation,

\displaystyle{n_{L}}_{\textrm{end}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{L}\varphi^{2}_{\textrm{end}}\dot{\theta}_{\textrm{end}}\cos^{2}\alpha

(23)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-Q_{L}\tilde{\lambda}_{5}\varphi^{3}_{\textrm{end}}\sin\theta_{\rm end}/\sqrt{3\lambda}~.

After inflation, the lepton number density is roughly red-shifted by
a^{3}
. To generate the correct baryon asymmetry of our univsere, we need
{n_{L}}_{\textrm{end}}\sim 10^{-16}
.
For
\lambda\sim\mathcal{O}(0.1)
,
\varphi_{\textrm{end}}\sim 1
in Planck unit, we just need
\tilde{\lambda}_{5}\sim 10^{-15}
.

3
Vacuum stability from type II seesaw

In spite of the intriguing motivation, this mechanism suffers from constraints of theoretical consistency including vacuum stability, perturbativity, and the condition of inflation. The vacuum stability condition requires that the potential of Eq.
2
does not develop a second vacuum between TeV scale and the Planck scale, otherwise our electroweak vacuum would be meta-stable and may transit to another vacuum. In the large field limit, we only need focus on the quartic term of the potential. These conditions can be summarized as Eq.
24
, with detailed derivation in appendix
A
.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}>0~\text{and}~[C_{6}>0~\text{or}~C^{\prime}_{6}>0]~.\end{aligned}

(24)

where

\displaystyle C_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{H}~,

(25)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}~,

(26)

\displaystyle C_{3}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{3}~,

(27)

\displaystyle C_{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}~,

(28)

\displaystyle C_{5}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}~,

(29)

\displaystyle C_{6}

\displaystyle=|\lambda_{4}|\sqrt{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}-2\lambda_{3}\sqrt{\lambda_{H}}~,

(30)

\displaystyle C_{6}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=2\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}+\sqrt{(8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2})(2{\lambda_{2}}/{\lambda_{3}}+1)}~.

(31)

Note that the above conditions are should be satisfied at any energy scale between TeV and
M_{p}
. The perturbativity condition requires that all couplings should be not much beyond
O(1)
. Here we require all the couplings smaller than
\sqrt{4\pi}
.

Since inflation in our context requires a flat direction along the mixing of
h
and
\Delta^{0}
, the condition in scenario 1 of Eq.
14
should also be satisfied, which can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle C_{7}>0,~C_{8}>0~,

(32)

where

\displaystyle~C_{7}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{H}-x\lambda_{H\Delta}~,

(33)

\displaystyle~C_{8}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2x\lambda_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}~,

(34)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{H}}{\xi_{\Delta}}~.

(35)

We stress that the conditions of
C_{7}>0
and
C_{8}>0
should be satisfied only at around Planck scale.

In our calculation we utilize SARAH
Staub:2013tta
to derive the 2-loop renormalization group equations for the relevant couplings. The 1-loop result is extracted in appendix
C
and we find it consistent with the result from
Bonilla:2015eha
.

4
Numerical results

In our calculation, the free parameters include three gauge coupling parameters
g_{1},~g_{2},~g_{3}
, five quartic coupling parameters
\lambda_{H},~\lambda_{1},~\lambda_{2},~\lambda_{3},~\lambda_{4}
and yukawa coupling of top quark and leptons with the triplet Higgs. The threshold effect of
\mu

Barrie:2021mwi
;
Barrie:2022cub
. Here we set the triplet Higgs mass to be 1 TeV, then all the SM parameters firstly have to run from the top mass scale to TeV scale, then all the couplings run into Planck scale. During the intermediate scale, the vacuum stability condition has been checked and the inflation conditions
C_{7}
and
C_{8}
are only imposed at Planck scale. Since all the couplings, particularly the Higgs self-coupling is sensitive to the top mass, here we set the top mass to be 172 GeV and Higgs mass to be 125 GeV. All the SM model couplings at the top mass scale are derived following the formulas from
Buttazzo:2013uya
.

Before going to the numerical results, we give some analysis on the preference of parameter space. First of all, to realize the mixing of the triplet Higgs and SM Higgs doublet, we have the following relation,

x=\frac{\xi_{H}}{\xi_{\Delta}},~2\lambda_{H}-x\lambda_{H\Delta}>0,~2x\lambda_{\Delta}-\lambda_{H\Delta}>0~.

\lambda_{H}>0,\lambda_{\Delta}>0

6
Conclusions

In this work we investigate the parameter space of type II seesaw model consistent with the vacuum stability and perturbativity, while solving the problems of neutrino masses, baryon asymmetry of the universe and inflation. A successful leptogenesis requires that the inflation is driven by the mixing state of the SM Higgs doublet and the triplet Higgs, providing additional constraints on the parameter space. After combining these conditions, we give two typical viable parameter regions where the SM Higgs self-coupling could be large
\mathcal{O}(1)
or small
\mathcal{O}(0.01)
.

Appendix A A
Derivation of the Vacuum Stability Conditions

In this section, we calculate the vacuum stability conditions for a potential including the SM Higgs doublet and triplet Higgs. All the results can be found in
Bonilla:2015eha
;
Moultaka:2020dmb
and here we just add some details. Here we consider the potential from Eq.
2
where high dimension operators are neglected,

\displaystyle V(H,\Delta)=

\displaystyle-m^{2}_{H}H^{\dagger}H+m_{\Delta}^{2}\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)+[\mu(H^{T}i\sigma^{2}\Delta^{\dagger}H)+h.c.]

(41)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{H}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\lambda_{1}(H^{\dagger}H)\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)+\lambda_{2}(\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta))^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda_{3}\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)^{2}+\lambda_{4}H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H+\cdots~.

It can be seen that in the large field limit the potential
V
mainly depends on the following four items:
H^{\dagger}H
,
(\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta))^{2}
,
\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)
and
H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H
. Take
\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)\neq 0
and define
r
,
\zeta
and
\xi
as the following non-negative dimensionless quantities,

H^{\dagger}H\equiv r\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)~,

(42)

\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)^{2}\equiv\zeta[\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)]^{2}~,

(43)

H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H\equiv\xi\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)(H^{\dagger}H)~.

(44)

For all scalar masses are positive, the potential does not classically drop to another minimum, but this still leaves the possibility of tunneling to a deeper minimum. In order for this not to happen, we must ensure that
V
is bounded, which is equivalent to requiring that the quartic part of the potential in Eq.
41
,
V^{(4)}
, must be positive for all non-zero field values.

\frac{V^{(4)}}{[\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)]^{2}}=\lambda_{H}r^{2}+\lambda_{1}r+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta+\lambda_{4}\xi r~.

(45)

For
V^{(4)}>0
and
[\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)]^{2}>0
, we have

\frac{V^{(4)}}{[\text{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)]^{2}}>0~,

(46)

so we can rewrite Eq.
45
as follow,

f(r)\equiv ar^{2}+br+c>0~,

(47)

where
a=\lambda_{H},b=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi,c=\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta
. Obviously, Eq.
47
is regarded as a one-dimensional quadratic inequality.

(I)
b>0

For
f(r)>0
and
r\in(0,\infty)
, the parabola opens up,
a>0
, which is

\lambda_{H}>0~.

(48)

When
r\rightarrow 0
, we have
c>0
and

\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta>0~.

(49)

In this case, Eq.
46
is apparently satisfied.

(II)
b<0

Likewise,
a>0
and
c>0
still hold. At this time, due to
f(r)>0
, the minimum must also satisfy this condition. For
f^{\prime}(r)=0
, it can be obtained,

2\lambda_{H}r+\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi=0~,~r=-\frac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi}{2\lambda_{H}}~.

(50)

Substitute the minimum point back to Eq.
45
,

-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi)^{2}+4\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)>0~.

(51)

Simplified available

-2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)}<\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi<2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)}~.

(52)

Since the case where
b
is less than zero is considered here, it is only necessary to consider that

-2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)}<\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi~,

(53)

which is

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)}>0~.

(54)

To sum up, we can make a summary and redefine Eq.
49
and Eq.
54
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{H}>0~,

(55)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta\equiv F_{1}(\zeta)>0~,

(56)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}\xi+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}\zeta)}\equiv F_{2}(\xi,\zeta)>0~.

(57)

Since the value range of
\zeta
is
2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1\leqslant\zeta\leqslant 1

B

is monotonic about
\zeta
and

(58)

Since
\xi\in[0,1]
and
F(\xi)>0
, there are
F(0)>0
and
F(1)>0
, i.e.

F(0)=\lambda_{1}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}>0~,

(59)

F(1)=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}>0~.

(60)

If

F(\xi)

F^{\prime}(\xi)=\lambda_{4}+\frac{2\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}(2\xi-1)}{\sqrt{\lambda_{H}[\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}(2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1)]}}~,

(61)

whose second derivative is

F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=\frac{2\lambda_{H}^{2}\lambda_{3}(2\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}{{\lambda_{H}[\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}(2\xi^{2}-2\xi+1)]}^{3/2}}~.

(62)

It can be seen from the above formula that the sign of
F^{\prime\prime}(\xi)
is the same as that of
\lambda_{3}
. If
\lambda_{3}>0
and
F^{\prime}(0)<0,~F^{\prime}(1)>0

(0,1)
. Otherwise, the minimum value is
F(0)
or
F(1)
, and Eq.
59
with Eq.
60

F(\xi)>0
. For
F^{\prime}(0)<0
and
F^{\prime}(1)>0
, we have

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(0)=\lambda_{4}-\frac{2\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}}<0~,

(63)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(1)=\lambda_{4}+\frac{2\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}}>0~.

(64)

Combining Eq.
63
and Eq.
64

2\lambda_{3}\sqrt{\lambda_{H}}>|\lambda_{4}|\sqrt{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}~.

(65)

That is to say, if and only if
2\lambda_{3}\sqrt{\lambda_{H}}>|\lambda_{4}|\sqrt{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}
, we should calculate the minimum of
F(\xi)
in
(0,1)
. Taking zero for Eq.
61
and solving the equation, the extreme point can be obtained as

\xi_{0}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\lambda_{4}}{2\lambda_{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{3}(2\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}{8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2}}}~.

(66)

Substituting
\xi_{0}
back into Eq.
58

\displaystyle F(\xi_{0})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{4}+4\lambda_{H}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{3}(2\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}{8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2}}}-\frac{\lambda_{4}^{2}}{2\lambda_{3}}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{3}(2\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}{8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2}}}

(67)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{4}+\frac{1}{2}(8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2})\sqrt{\frac{2\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}+1}{8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2})(2\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}+1)}>0~.

In summary, the vacuum stability conditions are obtained as follow

\lambda_{H},\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3},2\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3},\lambda_{1}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})},\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4}+2\sqrt{\lambda_{H}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})}>0~,

(68)

and

2\lambda_{3}\sqrt{\lambda_{H}}\leqslant\lvert\lambda_{4}\rvert\sqrt{\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}}~\text{or}~\lambda_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(8\lambda_{H}\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{4}^{2})(2\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{3}}+1)}>0~.

B.1
0\leq\xi\leq 1

We already know

\Delta=\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta^{+}/\sqrt{2}&\Delta^{++}\\
\Delta^{0}&-\Delta^{+}/\sqrt{2}\end{matrix}\right),~\Delta^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{matrix}\Delta^{-}/\sqrt{2}&\Delta^{0}\\
\Delta^{--}&-\Delta^{-}/\sqrt{2}\end{matrix}\right)~.

(70)

From the above formula, we can easily find

\text{Tr}\Delta=0,~\text{Tr}\Delta^{\dagger}=0~.

(71)

For any
2\times 2
matrices
M
and
N
, we have

MN+NM=\bm{1}(\text{Tr}MN-\text{Tr}M\text{Tr}N)+M\text{Tr}N+N\text{Tr}M~,

(72)

where
\bm{1}
is identity matrix, from which follows immediately

\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}+\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta=\bm{1}\times\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}~.

(73)

Then multiply this by
H^{\dagger}
on the left and right by
H
to get

H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H+H^{\dagger}\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta H=H^{\dagger}H\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}~.

(74)

Due to
H^{\dagger}\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta H\geq 0
, we can obtain

H^{\dagger}H\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}\geq H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H~.

(75)

According to the definition of
\xi
, it is easy to know

\xi\equiv\frac{H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H}{H^{\dagger}H\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}}\leq 1~.

(76)

Since
H^{\dagger}H\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}
and
H^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}H
are positive,
\xi
must be trivially greater than zero. Finally the two boundary values 0 and 1 are effectively reached respectively when
H^{\dagger}\Delta=0
and
\Delta H=0
. Thus

0\leq\xi\leq 1~.

(77)

B.2
\frac{1}{2}\leq\zeta\leq 1

Using Eq.
70
and considering their product

\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}=\left(\begin{matrix}{\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2}&\frac{\Delta^{+}\Delta^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\Delta^{++}\Delta^{-}}{\sqrt{2}}\\
\frac{\Delta^{-}\Delta^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\Delta^{--}\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}&{\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{0}\rvert}^{2}\end{matrix}\right)~.

(78)

First, trace Eq.
78
and then square it, we have

(\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}=({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})^{2}+({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})^{2}+2({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{0}\rvert}^{2})~.

(79)

Furthermore, square Eq.
78
and then find the trace to get

\text{Tr}(\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}=({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})^{2}+({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})^{2}+2[\frac{\Delta^{+}\Delta^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{\Delta^{++}\Delta^{-}}{\sqrt{2}}][\frac{\Delta^{-}\Delta^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{\Delta^{--}\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}]~.

(80)

Finally, subtract Eq.
79
and Eq.
80

\begin{aligned} (\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}-\text{Tr}(\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}&=2({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{++}\rvert}^{2})({\lvert\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\rvert}^{2}+{\lvert\Delta^{0}\rvert}^{2})\\

(81)

Using
\text{Det}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}\equiv|\text{Det}\Delta|^{2}\geq 0
and Eq.
81
, it is easily to note that
(\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}\geq\text{Tr}(\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}
. According to the definition of
\zeta
, we have

\zeta\equiv\frac{\text{Tr}(\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}}{(\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}}\leq 1~,

(82)

and
\zeta
must be positive because
(\text{Tr}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}
and
\text{Tr}(\Delta\Delta^{\dagger})^{2}

\zeta

M_{2}^{2}

(83)

and then divide it up and down by

\zeta(x)=(1+x^{2})/(1+x)^{2}

\zeta=1/2
when
x=1

\frac{1}{2}\leq\zeta\leq 1~.

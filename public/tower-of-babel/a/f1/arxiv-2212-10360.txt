Wrapped M5-branes and AdS 5 Black Holes
Nikolay Bobev, Vasil Dimitrov, and Annelien Vekemans

Abstract
We use consistent truncations in supergravity to show that the backreaction of N N rotating M5-branes wrapped on a Riemann surface leads to asymptotically AdS 5 black hole solutions of 11d supergravity. We discuss the thermodynamic properties of these black holes focusing on their supersymmetric limit. The Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the supersymmetric black holes scales as N 3 N^{3} and can be reproduced by the superconformal index of the holographically dual 4d 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 SCFTs of class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} .

Introduction

String and M-theory provide a consistent framework to describe the quantum physics of black holes in terms of microscopic ingredients such as strings and branes. The AdS/CFT correspondence gives an additional vantage point on this venerable research endeavor. Asymptotically AdS black holes can be viewed through the prism of holography and their properties can in principle be delineated using the quantum dynamics of a dual CFT. This is best understood when the AdS black hole solution is constructed in a consistent supergravity theory that arises as a low-energy limit of string or M-theory. This allows to understand the black hole solution in terms of its microscopic ingredients, identify its dual CFT, and then harness the power of holography to study the black hole itself. Supersymmetry can be a very useful crutch in this enterprise. It is often technically easier to construct explicit supersymmetric AdS black hole solutions in supergravity and explore their properties. Moreover, the recent advances in supersymmetric localization allow for the explicit calculation of the path integral of holographic SCFTs, which in turn provides a host of information about the physics of the black hole. Indeed, recently there has been a flurry of activity using these methods to study the thermodynamic and microscopic properties of supersymmetric AdS black holes and their dual QFT description, see
Zaffaroni:2019dhb
and references therein for a review.

Our goal in this work is to contribute to these developments by studying supersymmetric AdS
5
black holes in M-theory that arise from the backreaction of M5-branes wrapped on a compact Riemann surface,
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
, of genus
\mathfrak{g}
. The supergravity solutions describing this system of branes can be constructed in a two-step procedure that involves supergravity consistent truncations. As shown in
Nastase:1999cb

S^{4}
leads to the maximal 7d
SO(5)
gauged supergravity of
Pernici:1984xx

5

and then generalized in
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg

N
, these AdS
5

\mathfrak{g}

(\mathfrak{g},z)

\mathcal{N}=2

It is well-known that 5d

Gutowski:2004ez

We build on these results and show how the entropy, on-shell action, and charges of the black hole solution can be expressed in terms of the microscopic parameters
(N,\mathfrak{g},z)
that define the underlying M5-brane system. The black hole solutions admit a somewhat subtle analytic continuation to Euclidean signature which allows for rigorous calculations of their properties using the tools of holographic renormalization which we discuss in some detail. S^{1}\times S^{3}

4.1
CCLP solution

The 5d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauged supergravity with bosonic action (
13
) admits a general non-supersymmetric black hole solution found in
Chong:2005hr
which we will refer to as CCLP. The solution is specified by four real parameters
(a,b,q,m)
which determine its two angular momentum, electric charge and mass. For the Lorentzian black hole solutions these parameters are further constrained as

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle<ag<1\,,\quad-1<bg<1\,,\quad 0\leq q\leq\frac{m}{1+ag+bg}\,,

(20)

where
g=1/L
is the inverse of the AdS scale. The first two conditions arise from requiring that the black hole is not over-rotating and the last condition constitutes the BPS bound. The solution is specified by the following metric and gauge field

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \differential{s}^{2}_{\text{CCLP}}&=-\frac{\Delta_{\eta}[(1+g^{2}r^{2})\rho^{2}\differential{t}+2q\nu]\differential{t}}{\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}\rho^{2}}+\frac{2q\nu\omega}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{f}{\rho^{4}}\quantity(\frac{\Delta_\eta\dd{t}}{\Xi_a \Xi_b} - \omega)^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{\rho^{2}\differential{r}^{2}}{\Delta_{r}}+\frac{\rho^{2}\differential{\eta}^{2}}{\Delta_{\eta}}+\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Xi_{a}}\sin^{2}\eta\differential{\xi_1}^{2}+\frac{r^{2}+b^{2}}{\Xi_{b}}\cos^{2}\eta\differential{\xi_2}^{2}\,,\\
A_{\text{CCLP}}&=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,q}{\mathpzc{x}\rho^{2}}\quantity(\frac{\Delta_\eta\dd{t}}{\Xi_a \Xi_b} - \omega)+\alpha\differential{t}\,,\end{aligned}

(21)

where we have defined the one-forms

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=b\,\sin^{2}\eta\differential{\xi_1}+a\,\cos^{2}\eta\differential{\xi_2}\,,\quad\omega=\frac{a\,\sin^{2}\eta}{\Xi_{a}}\differential{\xi_1}+\frac{b\,\cos^{2}\eta}{\Xi_{b}}\differential{\xi_2}\,,

(22)

the functions

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta_{r}(r)&=\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})(r^{2}+b^{2})(1+g^{2}r^{2})+q^{2}+2abq}{r^{2}}-2m\,,\\
\Delta_{\eta}(\eta)&=1-a^{2}g^{2}\cos^{2}\eta-b^{2}g^{2}\sin^{2}\eta\,,\\
\rho^{2}(r,\eta)&=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\eta+b^{2}\sin^{2}\eta\,,\\
f(r,\eta)&=2\quantity(m + abqg^2)\rho^{2}(r,\eta)-q^{2}\,,\end{aligned}

(23)

and the constants

\displaystyle\Xi_{a}

\displaystyle=1-a^{2}g^{2}\,,\quad\Xi_{b}=1-b^{2}g^{2}\,.

(24)

We have also included a pure gauge term,
\alpha\differential{t}
, in
A_{\text{CCLP}}
, which is important to guarantee regularity of
A^{\mu}A_{\mu}
on the horizon. These coordinates cover the region outside of the black hole and have the following ranges and identifications

\displaystyle-\infty<t<\infty\,,\quad r_{+}<r<\infty\,,\quad 0<\eta<\frac{\pi}{2}\,,\quad\xi_{1}\sim\xi_{1}+2\pi\,,\quad\xi_{2}\sim\xi_{2}+2\pi\,,

(25)

where
r_{+}
is the largest positive root of
\Delta_{r}(r)
. The surface
r=r_{+}
is the event horizon of the Lorentzian black hole, see Appendix
B.4
for further details. Sometimes, it will be convenient to trade the parameter
m
for
r_{+}
. The explicit relation, which is obtained by solving
\Delta_{r}(r_{+})=0
, reads

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\frac{(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(r_{+}^{2}+b^{2})(1+g^{2}r_{+}^{2})+q^{2}+2abq}{2r_{+}^{2}}\,.

(26)

As emphasized in
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
the Lorentzian CCLP black hole can be analytically continued to a Euclidean “black saddle”, which solves the equations of motion of the Euclidean theory (
126
)
6
6

6

The term “black saddle” was coined in
Bobev:2020pjk
and refers to the fact that, in general, there exist Euclidean saddle points of the supergravity path integral that do not admit analytic continuation to sensible Lorentzian field configurations. The CCLP black saddle is an example of an Euclidean solution that does admit continuation to the Lorentzian black hole of
Chong:2005hr
for some values of the parameters. We refer to this black saddle also as CCLP and the meaning is to be inferred from the context.
. The Euclidean metric and gauge field are given by

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}_{\text{CCLP},\mathpzc{E}}=\evaluated{\dd{s}^2_{\text{CCLP}}}_{t \to-\iu\, \tau}\,,\quad A_{\text{CCLP},\mathpzc{E}}=\evaluated{A_{\text{CCLP}}}_{t \to-\iu\, \tau}\,.

(27)

Note that these metric and gauge field are both complex. One might want to work with a real Euclidean section by, in addition to Wick rotation, also analytically continuing the rotation parameters as:
a\to\mathrm{i}\,a,b\to\mathrm{i}\,b
. Once this is done the Euclidean metric is purely real and the Euclidean gauge field is purely imaginary. In the context of evaluating the on-shell action of this solution it is useful to work with this real Euclidean section. To interpret the result holographically as a Lorentzian partition function the rotation parameters have to be analytically continued back to their original values:
a\to-\mathrm{i}\,a,b\to-\mathrm{i}\,b
. It turns out that this procedure gives the same final answer as simply working with the complex Wick rotated metric and gauge field (
27
), without continuing the rotation parameters back and forth.

In the holographic context it will be useful also to work with the conformally rescaled boundary metric

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}_{\text{CCLP},\mathpzc{B}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{r\to\infty}g^{-2}r^{-2}\differential{s}^{2}_{\text{CCLP}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\Delta_{\eta}}{\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}}\differential{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{g^{2}\Delta_{\eta}}\differential{\eta}^{2}+\frac{\sin^{2}\eta}{g^{2}\Xi_{a}}\differential{\xi_1}^{2}+\frac{\cos^{2}\eta}{g^{2}\Xi_{b}}\differential{\xi_2}^{2}\,.

(28)

This metric, with certain identifications of the coordinates that are discussed in detail in
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
, determines the background on which the 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
dual SCFT is placed.

4.2
Thermodynamic properties CCLP

In Appendix
B.4
we show that to the CCLP black hole we can associate the following thermodynamic potentials:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \beta&=\frac{2\pi r_{+}\quantity[(r_+^2 + a^2)(r_+^2 + b^2) + abq]}{r_{+}^{4}\quantity[1 + g^2(2r_+^2 + a^2 + b^2)]-(ab+q)^{2}}\,,\\
\Omega_{1}&=\frac{a(r_{+}^{2}+b^{2})(1+g^{2}r_{+}^{2})+bq}{(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(r_{+}^{2}+b^{2})+abq}\,,\quad\Omega_{2}=\frac{b(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(1+g^{2}r_{+}^{2})+aq}{(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(r_{+}^{2}+b^{2})+abq}\,,\\
\Phi&=\frac{\sqrt{3}qr_{+}^{2}}{\mathpzc{x}\quantity[(r_+^2 + a^2)(r_+^2 + b^2) + abq]}\,,\end{aligned}

(29)

physically corresponding to: inverse temperature, angular velocities measured in a non-rotating frame at infinity, and electrostatic potential. To obtain the entropy, via the Bekenstein-Hawking area formula
S=\text{Area}/4G_{5}
, one has to integrate the square root determinant,
\sqrt{g_{\mathcal{H}}}
, of the induced metric on the horizon, which has topology of
S^{3}
and metric given by

\displaystyle\differential{s}^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\evaluated{\dd{s}^2_{\text{CCLP}}}_{\dd{t} \to 0, \, \dd{r} \to 0, \, r \to r_+}\,.

(30)

Performing this integral we obtain

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\quantity[(r_+^2 + a^2)(r_+^2 + b^2) + abq]}{2\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}r_{+}G_{5}}\,.

(31)

In Appendices
B.5
and
B.7
we calculate the following charges of the CCLP black hole

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E&=\quantity(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}')+\frac{m\pi(2\Xi_{a}+2\Xi_{b}-\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b})+2abq^{2}\pi(\Xi_{a}+\Xi_{b})}{4\Xi_{a}^{2}\Xi_{b}^{2}G_{5}}\,,\\
J_{1}&=\frac{\pi\quantity[2am + bq(1 + a^2g^2)]}{4\Xi_{a}^{2}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}\,,\quad J_{2}=\frac{\pi\quantity[2bm + aq(1 + b^2g^2)]}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}^{2}G_{5}}\,,\\
Q&=\frac{\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\pi q}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}\,,\end{aligned}

(32)

physically corresponding to: energy, angular momenta and electric charge. Above we have defined the constants

\displaystyle E_{\text{AdS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3\pi}{32g^{2}G_{5}}\quantity(1 + \frac{(\Xi_a - \Xi_b)^2}{9\Xi_a \Xi_b})\,,\quad\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=\frac{9g\pi\beta\quantity(\Xi_a^2 - \Xi_a \Xi_b + \Xi_b^2)}{\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}\zeta^{\prime}\,,

(33)

where
\zeta^{\prime}
is the coefficient of a certain finite counterterm that we introduce to the boundary action in the process of holographic renormalization, see Appendix
B.6
. In a similar calculation
Papadimitriou:2005ii
obtains the energy of the Kerr-
\text{AdS}_{5}
black hole — the special case
q=0
, in a renormalization scheme where
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=0
. For the quantity
E_{\text{AdS}}
, which physically corresponds to the Casimir energy of empty
\text{AdS}_{5}
, we find agreement with
Papadimitriou:2005ii
. The Euclidean on-shell action is obtained by evaluating (
125
), on-shell, on a surface
r=R_{0}
, where
R_{0}\gg r_{+}
. Naively, one obtains divergences, scaling as
R_{0}^{4}
and
R_{0}^{2}
. They can be cancelled by subtracting the on-shell action of
\text{AdS}_{5}
, written in suitable coordinates, in a background subtraction procedure. The final answer reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{\text{bs}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\beta}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}\quantity[m - g^2(a^2 + r_+^2)(b^2 + r_+^2) - \frac{q^2 r_+^2}{(a^2 + r_+^2)(b^2 + r_+^2) + abq}]\,.

(34)

To obtain this answer, crucially, one has to include the gauge parameter
\alpha
in (
21
) and then set it to
-\Phi
to ensure that the gauge field is regular on the horizon. This important aspect of the calculation was not explicitly stated in the original derivation presented in
Chen:2005zj
. Alternatively, in Appendix
B.6
we employ holographic renormalization to obtain the following Euclidean on-shell action

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\beta\quantity(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}')+\mathcal{I}_{\text{bs}}\,,

(35)

where precisely the constants (
33
) appear. We can interpret this discrepancy between the two on-shell action results as follows. In a non-supersymmetric setting there is no preferred mechanism to fix the coefficient
\zeta^{\prime}
of the finite counterterm, i.e. the value of the constant
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}
defines a choice of renormalization scheme. In particular, the “background subtraction renormalization scheme” corresponds to setting
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=E_{\text{AdS}}
. In a different scheme the energy acquires an additive constant factor and the on-shell action acquires the same additive factor times
\beta
.

Having collected the potentials, charges, entropy and on-shell action of CCLP we have verified explicitly that, in any renormalization scheme, the quantum statistical relation holds

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\beta E-S-\beta\Omega_{1}J_{1}-\beta\Omega_{2}J_{2}-\beta\Phi Q\,.

(36)

Naively, the first law of thermodynamics

\displaystyle\differential{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\differential{S}+\Omega_{1}\differential{J_1}+\Omega_{2}\differential{J_2}+\Phi\differential{Q}\,,

(37)

where the variations are taken with respect to the “bare” black hole parameters
(a,\,b,\,r_{+},\,q)
, is only satisfied in the renormalization scheme
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=E_{\text{AdS}}
. Insisting on using the “bare” parameters, it was argued in
Papadimitriou:2005ii
that in a renormalization scheme where
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}\neq E_{\text{AdS}}
one should use the following generalized first law

\displaystyle\differential{E}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\sigma}E+\frac{1}{\beta}\differential{S}+\Omega_{1}\differential{J_1}+\Omega_{2}\differential{J_2}+\Phi\differential{Q}\,,

(38)

where
\delta_{\sigma}E
is the variation of the energy with respect to a Weyl transformation that keeps representative of the conformal class of boundary metrics fixed. They go further to prove that the variation of the on-shell action under this Weyl transformation is
7
7

7

Technically, they only prove this in the renormalization scheme
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=0
, but their result trivially generalizes.

\displaystyle\delta_{\sigma}\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\beta\differential{\qty(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}')}=\beta\,\delta_{\sigma}E\,.

(39)

Using this result, we see that the generalized first law takes the form

\displaystyle\differential{\qty(E - \qty(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}'))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\differential{S}+\Omega_{1}\differential{J_1}+\Omega_{2}\differential{J_2}+\Phi\differential{Q}\,,

(40)

which is now trivially satisfied in any renormalization scheme, when the variation is taken with respect to the parameters
(a,b,r_{+},q)
.

The Euclidean on-shell action can be though of as minus the logarithm of the grand canonical partition function

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\beta,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Phi)

\displaystyle=-\log\mathcal{Z}(\beta,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Phi)\,,

(41)

where the grand canonical ensemble is the one in which the thermodynamic potentials
(\beta,\,\Omega_{1},\,\Omega_{2},\,\Phi)
are held fixed, while the charges are allowed to fluctuate. In this setup each state is assigned a probability

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}e^{-\beta\quantity(E_i - \Omega_1 J_{1,i} - \Omega_2 J_{2,i} - \Phi Q_i)}\,,\quad\mathcal{Z}=\sum_{i}e^{-\beta\quantity(E_i - \Omega_1 J_{1,i} - \Omega_2 J_{2,i} - \Phi Q_i)}\,.

(42)

Then the average charges are given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} E&=\sum_{i}P_{i}E_{i}=\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\beta}+\Omega_{1}J_{1}+\Omega_{2}J_{2}+\Phi Q\,,\\
J_{1}&=\sum_{i}P_{i}J_{1,i}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_1}\,,\quad J_{2}=\sum_{i}P_{i}J_{2,i}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_2}\,,\\
Q&=\sum_{i}P_{i}Q_{i}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Phi}\,.\end{aligned}

(43)

The above relations can be readily seen from the quantum statistical relation (
36
) and indicate that the potentials
(\beta,\,\Omega_{1},\,\Omega_{2},\,\Phi)
are conjugate to the charges
(E,\,J_{1},\,J_{2},\,Q)
, respectively. We can verify these relations on the CCLP solution as follows. First, we express the Euclidean on-shell action and the potentials solely in terms of the black hole parameters
(a,\,b,\,r_{+},\,q)
. Then we solve the following system of four equations

\displaystyle\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{(a,b,r_+,q)}

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\beta}\partialderivative{\beta}{(a,b,r_+,q)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_1}\partialderivative{\Omega_1}{(a,b,r_+,q)}+\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_2}\partialderivative{\Omega_2}{(a,b,r_+,q)}+\partialderivative{\mathcal{I}}{\Phi}\partialderivative{\Phi}{(a,b,r_+,q)}

(44)

for the four unknowns
\quantity(\pdv{\mathcal{I}}{\beta}, \, \pdv{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_1}, \, \pdv{\mathcal{I}}{\Omega_2}, \, \pdv{\mathcal{I}}{\Phi})
. Plugging the results in (
43
) we get perfect agreement with the charges obtained in Appendices
B.5
and
B.7
via holographic renormalization and Komar integrals. Finally, one can view the entropy as a function of the charges
(E,\,J_{1},\,J_{2},\,Q)
and write the quantum statistical relation as

\displaystyle S(E,J_{1},J_{2},Q)

\displaystyle=\beta E-\beta\Omega_{1}J_{1}-\beta\Omega_{2}J_{2}-\beta\Phi Q-\mathcal{I}(\beta,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Phi)\,,

(45)

where the following relations should hold

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{S}{E}\,,\quad\Omega_{1}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{S}{J_1}\,,\quad\Omega_{2}=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{S}{J_2}\,,\quad\Phi=-\frac{1}{\beta}\partialderivative{S}{Q}\,.

(46)

Again, we establish their validity on the CCLP solution by solving the following system of four equations

\displaystyle\partialderivative{S}{(a,b,r_+,q)}

\displaystyle=\partialderivative{S}{E}\partialderivative{E}{(a,b,r_+,q)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\partialderivative{S}{J_1}\partialderivative{J_1}{(a,b,r_+,q)}+\partialderivative{S}{J_2}\partialderivative{J_2}{(a,b,r_+,q)}+\partialderivative{S}{Q}\partialderivative{Q}{(a,b,r_+,q)}\,,

(47)

for the four unknowns
\quantity(\pdv{S}{E}, \, \pdv{S}{J_1}, \, \pdv{S}{J_2}, \, \pdv{S}{Q})
and plugging the results in (
46
). This analysis shows that the entropy is the Legendre transform of the Euclidean on-shell action, in which the charges
(E,\,J_{1},\,J_{2},\,Q)
replace the potentials
(\beta,\,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Phi)
as independent variables.

4.3
Thermodynamics in the supersymmetric and BPS limits of CCLP

One can make the CCLP solution supersymmetric by saturating the BPS bound

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{1+ag+bg}\,.

(48)

In what follows it is convenient to also exchange the parameter
m
for

\displaystyle\widetilde{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{mg}{(a+b)(1+ag)(1+bg)(1+ag+bg)}-1\,.

(49)

We show that the resulting three-parameter family of solutions, i.e.
(a,\,b,\,\widetilde{m})
, is supersymmetric in Appendix
B.8
by explicitly solving the Killing spinor equations. This three parameter family of Euclidean supersymmetric solutions can be analytically continued to Lorentzian signature resulting in a family of supersymmetric black holes. These black hole solutions are however not regular and causal outside their horizon which is manifested by the generically complex roots of
\Delta_{r}(r)=0
. Setting
\widetilde{m}=0
one obtains a two-parameter family, i.e.
(a,\,b)
, of supersymmetric and extremal black holes that do not suffer from such problems and have vanishing temperature, i.e.
\beta\to\infty
. This was called the BPS limit in
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
and one can show that in this BPS limit
\Delta_{r}(r)=0
has a real double root given by

\displaystyle r_{*}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{g}(a+b+abg)\,,

(50)

and the surface
r=r_{*}
constitutes the event horizon of the BPS black hole.

To have better control over the IR divergence associated with the fact that
\beta\to\infty
in the BPS limit, we provide an alternative description of the supersymmetric black hole. Using (
49
) and (
26
), the supersymmetry condition (
48
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-ab+r_{+}^{2}(1+ag+bg)\pm\sqrt{-r_{+}^{2}(r_{*}^{2}-r_{+}^{2})}\,.

(51)

Insisting that
q
is real, we see that we are forced to take the extremal limit
r_{+}\to r_{*}
alongside the supersymmetric limit. In general however
q
is complex and leads to the following (complex) supersymmetric thermodynamic quantities

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\pi(a-\mathrm{i}r_{+})(b-\mathrm{i}r_{+})(gr_{*}^{2}+\mathrm{i}r_{+})}{g(r_{*}^{2}-r_{+}^{2})\quantity[2r_+(1 + ag + bg) + \iu g(r_*^2 - 3r_+^2)]}\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{g(r_{*}^{2}+\mathrm{i}ar_{+})(1-\mathrm{i}gr_{+})}{(gr_{*}^{2}+\mathrm{i}r_{+})(a-\mathrm{i}r_{+})}\,,\quad\Omega_{2}=\evaluated{\Omega_1}_{a \leftrightarrow b}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,\mathrm{i}r_{+}(1-\mathrm{i}gr_{+})}{\mathpzc{x}(gr_{*}^{2}+\mathrm{i}r_{+})}\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\quantity[3 - g^2\qty(a^2(1 + bg) + b^2(1 + ag) - ab)]\quantity[-ab + r_+^2\qty(1 + ag + bg) + \iu g r_+\qty(r_*^2 - r_+^2)]}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}(1-ag)(1-bg)G_{5}}\,,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\quantity[2a + b(1 + ag)]\quantity[-ab + r_+^2\qty(1 + ag + bg) + \iu g r_+\qty(r_*^2 - r_+^2)]}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}(1-ag)G_{5}}\,,\quad J_{2}=\evaluated{J_1}_{a \leftrightarrow b}\,,

(52)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\,\pi\quantity[-ab + r_+^2\qty(1 + ag + bg) + \iu g r_+\qty(r_*^2 - r_+^2)]}{4\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}\,,

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}\quantity[r_+\qty(r_+^2 + a^2(1 + bg) + b^2(1 + ag) + ab) + \iu abg(r_*^2 - r_+^2)]}{2\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}G_{5}}

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}(a-\mathrm{i}r_{+})^{2}(b-\mathrm{i}r_{+})^{2}(1+g^{2}r_{*}^{2})}{2\Xi_{a}\Xi_{b}\quantity[2r_+(1 + ag + bg) + \iu g(r_* - 3r_+^2)]G_{5}}\,,

where we have defined
\widetilde{E}=E-\quantity(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}')
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}=\mathcal{I}-\beta\quantity(E_{\text{AdS}} - \widetilde{\zeta}')
. As expected, the quantum statistical relation (
36
) and the generalized first law (
40
) continue to hold in the supersymmetric limit. We also find that the charges obey the following linear relation

\displaystyle E-gJ_{1}-gJ_{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{\mathpzc{x}}Q=E_{\text{AdS}}-\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}\,.

(53)

If the right hand side of this equation vanishes it will look like the familiar BPS constraint of the charges obeyed by supersymmetric black holes. As explained in Appendix
B
the parameter
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}
is the coefficient of a finite counterterm in the holographic renormalization procedure used to compute the gravitational charges. We can therefore make the right hand side of (
53
) vanish if we choose a scheme in which
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=E_{\text{AdS}}
. This choice is implemented by the background subtraction scheme discussed above (
34
). We note that since there is no preferred holographic renormalization scheme for 5d minimal gauged supergravity that is compatible with covariance, gauge invariance, and supersymmetry, see for example
BenettiGenolini:2016tsn
, one is in principle free to choose any other value of the finite counterterm coefficient
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}
. From now on we will work in the
\widetilde{\zeta}^{\prime}=E_{\text{AdS}}
scheme which implies that
E=\widetilde{E}
and
\mathcal{I}=\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}
.

In addition to the linear relation in (
53
), supersymmetry implies a linear relation between the corresponding potentials that reads

\displaystyle\beta\quantity(g + \Omega_1 + \Omega_2 - g \mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\, \Phi)

\displaystyle=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,.

(54)

In Appendix
B.8
we show how this constraint arises from a periodicity condition on the Killing spinor on the horizon. It is useful to define new chemical potentials which are convenient for taking the
\beta\to\infty
supersymmetric black hole limit

\displaystyle\omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\beta\quantity(\Omega_1 - g)\,,\quad\omega_{2}=\beta\quantity(\Omega_2 - g)\,,\quad\varphi=\beta\quantity(\Phi- \frac{\sqrt{3}}{\mathpzc{x}})\,.

(55)

In terms of them the supersymmetric Euclidean on-shell action takes the remarkably simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\mathpzc{x}^{3}}{12\sqrt{3}\,G_{5}}\frac{\varphi^{3}}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,.

(56)

The quantum statistical relation (
36
) and the linear constraint (
54
) can also be written in a manifestly
\beta
-independent way

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=-S-\omega_{1}J_{1}-\omega_{2}J_{2}-\varphi Q\,;\quad\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-g\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\,\varphi

\displaystyle=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,,

(57)

where the energy has been eliminated using (
53
).

This presentation of the supersymmetric CCLP black hole is adapted to taking the limit to extremality:
r_{+}\to r_{*}
. Even though
\beta
diverges in this limit, the newly defined chemical potentials assume the following finite (and complex) BPS values

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\pi(1-ag)(b-\mathrm{i}\,r_{*})}{r_{*}\quantity[1 + g(a + b - \iu\, r_*)]}\,,\quad\bm{\omega}_{2}=\evaluated{\omega_1}_{a \leftrightarrow b}\,,\quad\bm{\varphi}=\frac{\sqrt{3}\,\pi(a-\mathrm{i}\,r_{*})(b-\mathrm{i}\,r_{*})}{\mathpzc{x}\,r_{*}\quantity[1 + g(a + b - \iu\, r_*)]}\,,

(58)

where we adopted the convention to denote BPS quantities in the
\beta\to\infty
limit with bold letters. The BPS charges and the entropy become real in this limit and read

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \bm{E}&=\frac{\pi(a+b)\quantity[3 - g^2\qty(a^2(1 + bg) + b^2(1 + ag) - ab)]}{4g(1-ag)^{2}(1-bg)^{2}G_{5}}\,,\,\,\,\bm{Q}=\frac{\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\,\pi(a+b)}{4(1-ag)(1-bg)G_{5}}\,,\\
\bm{J}_{1}&=\frac{\pi(a+b)\quantity[2a + b(1 + ag)]}{4g(1-ag)^{2}(1-bg)G_{5}}\,,\quad\bm{J}_{2}=\evaluated{\bm{J}_1}_{a \leftrightarrow b}\,,\quad\bm{S}=\frac{\pi^{2}(a+b)r_{*}}{2g(1-ag)(1-bg)G_{5}}\,,\\
\end{aligned}

(59)

while the BPS Euclidean on-shell action remains complex and is naturally expressed in terms of the new BPS potentials

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\mu\frac{\bm{\varphi}^{3}}{\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{\omega}_{2}}\,,

(60)

where we have extracted the constant pre-factor
\mu=\pi\mathpzc{x}^{3}/(12\sqrt{3}G_{5})
. The quantum statistical relation and linear constraint between the potentials take precisely the same form as in the supersymmetric case

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{I}}=-\bm{S}-\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{J}_{1}-\bm{\omega}_{2}\bm{J}_{2}-\bm{\varphi}\bm{Q}\,;\quad\bm{\omega}_{1}+\bm{\omega}_{2}-\nu\bm{\varphi}

\displaystyle=2\pi\mathrm{i}\,,

(61)

where we have defined
\nu=g\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}
. We again work in the grand canonical ensemble where the potentials are held fixed and the charges allowed to fluctuate and write the entropy as

\displaystyle\bm{S}(\bm{J}_{1},\bm{J}_{2},\bm{Q})

\displaystyle=-\bm{\mathcal{I}}(\bm{\omega}_{1},\bm{\omega}_{2},\bm{\varphi})-\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{J}_{1}-\bm{\omega}_{2}\bm{J}_{2}-\bm{\varphi}\bm{Q}-\lambda\quantity(\bm{\omega}_1 + \bm{\omega}_2 - \nu\bm{\varphi} - 2 \pi\iu)\,,

(62)

where we have included a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda
multiplying the linear constraint. Taking partial derivatives of the above with respect to the potentials one obtains three extremization equations

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_1}&=\bm{J}_{1}+\lambda\,,&-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_2}&=\bm{J}_{2}+\lambda\,,&-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}}&=\bm{Q}-\nu\lambda\\
&&&\Downarrow\\
\mu\frac{\bm{\varphi}^{3}}{\bm{\omega}_{1}^{2}\bm{\omega}_{2}}&=\bm{J}_{1}+\lambda\,,&\mu\frac{\bm{\varphi}^{3}}{\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{\omega}_{2}^{2}}&=\bm{J}_{2}+\lambda\,,&-\mu\frac{3\bm{\varphi}^{2}}{\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{\omega}_{2}^{2}}&=\bm{Q}-\nu\lambda\,.\\
\end{aligned}

(63)

In principle, to perform the Legendre transform one has to invert the bottom line above (together with the linear constraint) to express
(\bm{\omega}_{1},\bm{\omega}_{2},\bm{\varphi},\lambda)
in terms of the charges
(\bm{J}_{1},\bm{J}_{2},\bm{Q})
and then plug the results in (
62
) to obtain the entropy. However, due to
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
being a homogeneous function of degree
1
, from Euler’s theorem one has
\bm{\mathcal{I}}=\bm{\omega}_{1}\partial_{\bm{\omega}_{1}}\bm{\mathcal{I}}+\bm{\omega}_{2}\partial_{\bm{\omega}_{2}}\bm{\mathcal{I}}+\bm{\varphi}\partial_{\bm{\varphi}}\bm{\mathcal{I}}
, and the expression for the entropy simplifies to

\displaystyle\bm{S}

\displaystyle=2\pi\mathrm{i}\lambda\,.

(64)

This provides a shortcut to obtaining the entropy: by inspection we see that

\displaystyle 0=\frac{1}{\nu^{3}}\quantity[ \qty(\pdv{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}})^3 - 27 \mu\pdv{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_1} \pdv{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_2} ]\implies 0=p_{0}+p_{1}\lambda+p_{2}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{3}\,,

(65)

where after the implies sign we have used the extremization equations and extracted the coefficients in front of the
\lambda
terms

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\nu^{3}}\bm{Q}^{3}-\frac{27\mu}{\nu^{3}}\bm{J}_{1}\bm{J}_{2}\,,\quad p_{1}=\frac{3}{\nu^{2}}\bm{Q}^{2}-\frac{27\mu}{\nu^{3}}(\bm{J}_{1}+\bm{J}_{2})\,,\quad p_{2}=-\frac{3}{\nu}\bm{Q}-\frac{27\mu}{\nu^{3}}\,.

(66)

We seek a purely real entropy in the BPS limit, so we should have a purely imaginary root of the cubic (
65
). This is possible if
p_{0}=p_{1}p_{2}
, which translates to the following non-linear relation between the BPS charges:

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{2}{g \mathpzc{x} \sqrt{3}}\bm{Q})^{3}+\frac{2\pi}{g^{3}G_{5}}\bm{J}_{1}\bm{J}_{2}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\frac{2 \sqrt{3}}{g \mathpzc{x}} \bm{Q} + \frac{\pi}{2 g^3 G_5 })\quantity[\qty(\frac{2}{g \mathpzc{x}} \bm{Q})^2 - \frac{\pi}{g^3 G_5}\qty(\bm{J}_1 + \bm{J}_2)]\,.

(67)

Then the purely imaginary roots are
\lambda=\pm\mathrm{i}\,\sqrt{p_{1}}
. Picking the minus sign in order to have positive entropy we obtain

\displaystyle\bm{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{g}\sqrt{\frac{4}{\mathpzc{x}^{2}}\bm{Q}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{gG_{5}}\quantity(\bm{J}_1 + \bm{J}_2)}\,.

(68)

While we have shown how to obtain the supersymmetric black hole entropy as a Legendre transformation of the on-shell action it is also possible to verify the validity of (
68
) and (
67
) using the explicit expression of the BPS charges and entropy in terms of the black hole parameters given in (
59
).

5
Holography and the superconformal index

The results for the on-shell action and entropy of the supersymmetric CCLP solution presented above can be understood from a holographic viewpoint in terms of an appropriate supersymmetric partition function on
S^{1}\times S^{3}
called the superconformal index
\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}
, see
Rastelli:2016tbz
for a review. Here we discuss how this can be done in some detail.

5.1
Anomalies and the index on the second sheet

The superconformal index of class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs has been studied for low values of
N
in
Beem:2012yn
;
Gadde:2009kb
;
Gadde:2011ik
;
Rastelli:2014jja
. We are not aware of an extension of these results valid in the large
N
limit relevant to our holographic setup. Recently, building on previous results in
Hosseini:2017mds
;
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
, it was shown in
Cassani:2021fyv
, see also
GonzalezLezcano:2020yeb
, that the superconformal index of general 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFTs is given by the following compact expression

\begin{split}\log\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}&=\frac{\varphi_{R}^{3}}{48\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}k_{RRR}-\frac{\varphi_{R}(\omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}-4\pi^{2})}{48\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}k_{R}+\log|G|\,,\end{split}

(69)

where,
\omega_{1,2}
are the fugacities for the two angular momenta on
S^{3}
,
\varphi_{R}
is the
U(1)
R-symmetry fugacity,
G
is the order of the Abelian 1-form symmetry in the theory (if any), and
(k_{RRR},k_{R})
are the cubic and linear ’t Hooft anomalies. Supersymmetry imposes the following linear relation between the fugacities

\varphi_{R}=(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+2\pi{\rm i}n_{0})\,,

(70)

and the expression in (
69
) is valid for
n_{0}=\pm 1
, i.e. on the “second sheet” of the complexified fugacities. This result for the index includes the contribution from the supersymmetric Casimir energy
Bobev:2015kza
;
Assel:2015nca
and is valid up to exponentially suppressed terms of the form
e^{-\ell_{3}/\beta}
where
\ell^{3}
is the radius of the
S^{3}
and
\beta
is the circumference of the
S^{1}
. It is not immediately clear that this Cardy-like limit of the index is useful for studying its large
N
properties. However, it was shown in
Hosseini:2017mds
;
Cabo-Bizet:2018ehj
;
Bobev:2022bjm
;
Cassani:2022lrk
that the
k_{RRR}
and
k_{R}
terms in (
69
) are in perfect agreement with the regularized on-shell action of the supersymmetric CCLP solution in 5d
\mathcal{N}=2
minimal gauged supergravity and its four-derivative extension. This in turn implies that the on-shell action and entropy of the wrapped M5-brane black holes we constructed above can be reproduced by using the index in (
69
). This is indeed possible as we show below.

To use the expression in (
69
) we need the ’t Hooft anomalies for the R-symmetry of the SCFT at hand. The 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs of interest here have a
U(1)_{F}
flavor symmetry in addition to the
U(1)_{R}
R-symmetry. The ’t Hooft anomalies for these global symmetries can be computed as in
Gaiotto:2009gz
;
Benini:2009mz
;
Alday:2009qq
;
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg
either with field theory methods or by integrating the anomaly polynomial of the 6d
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)
SCFT over
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
. For concreteness here we focus on the type
A_{N-1}
class
\mathcal{S}
theories and take
\mathfrak{g}>1
8
8

8

The results below have a straightforward generalization to arbitrary genus and to the type
D_{N}
theories.
for which the ’t Hooft anomalies read

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} k_{RRR}&=\frac{2(\mathfrak{g}-1)}{27z^{2}}\left[9z^{2}-1+(3z^{2}+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right]N^{3}\,,&k_{RRF}&=\frac{(\mathfrak{g}-1)}{9}\,z\,N\,,\\
k_{RFF}&=-\frac{(\mathfrak{g}-1)}{3}\sqrt{3z^{2}+1}N^{3}\,,&k_{FFF}&=(\mathfrak{g}-1)\,z\,N^{3}\,,\\
k_{R}&=\frac{\mathfrak{g}-1}{3}\left[4-\sqrt{3z^{2}+1}\right]N\,,&k_{F}&=(\mathfrak{g}-1)\,z\,N\,.\end{aligned}

(71)

We have presented the results for the leading term in the large
N
expansion of the anomalies which is all we need for our holographic discussion. The exact finite
N
values can be easily found using the results in
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg
. To obtain these results for the ’t Hooft anomalies, as explained in
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg
, one has to employ
a
-maximization
Intriligator:2003jj
. As expected from
a
-maximization we find that
k_{RFF}
is negative and that the superconformal Ward identity
k_{F}=9k_{RRF}
is obeyed. In addition, one can use superconformal Ward identities to find the following conformal anomalies

\begin{split}a&=\frac{1}{32}(9k_{RRR}-3k_{R})=(\mathfrak{g}-1)\frac{\left(9z^{2}-1+(1+3z^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)}{48z^{2}}N^{3}\,,\\
c&=\frac{1}{32}(9k_{RRR}-5k_{R})=(\mathfrak{g}-1)\frac{\left(9z^{2}-1+(1+3z^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)}{48z^{2}}N^{3}\,.\end{split}

(72)

Again we have presented the conformal anomalies only to leading order in the large
N
limit and as expected for a holographic SCFT we find that
a=c
to this order.

Plugging the results for
k_{RRR}
and
k_{R}
presented above in the formula for the superconformal index (
69
) we obtain an explicit expression for
\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}
valid in the Cardy-like limit and to leading order in the large
N
limit. To compare
\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}
with the regularized on-shell action of the supersymmetric CCLP solution we need to employ the holographic dictionary. As shown in
Bobev:2021qxx
;
Bobev:2022bjm
the cubic ’t Hooft anomaly is related to the 5d AdS radius and Newton constant as

k_{RRR}=\frac{4\pi L^{3}}{9G_{5}}\,,

(73)

while
k_{R}
is related to the coefficients of the four-derivative corrections to the 5d supergravity theory. To relate this 5d supergravity expression for
k_{RRR}
to the microscopic parameters
(N,\mathfrak{g},z)
one should use the relation between the 5d and 7d Newton constants in (
14
), the expressions that define the AdS
5
supersymmetric vacua in (
11
), as well as the uplift of this solution to 11d using the formulae in Section
2
. As shown in
Bobev:2021qxx
;
Bobev:2022bjm
, and can be checked explicitly using the formulae above, this supergravity calculation leads to exactly the same result for
k_{RRR}
as the one in (
71
).

Another entry from the holographic dictionary we need is the relation between the fugacities specifying the index and the gravitational potentials of the CCLP solution. This was also discussed in some detail recently in
Bobev:2022bjm
where it was shown that the supergravity BPS potentials in (
58
) can be related to the field theory fugacities as follows

\omega_{1,2}\longleftrightarrow\bm{\omega}_{1,2}\,,\qquad\qquad\varphi_{R}\longleftrightarrow\frac{\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}}{L}\bm{\varphi}\,.

(74)

Comparing the supergravity BPS relation in (
61
) with the field theory one in (
70
) we see that we must set
n_{0}=-1
in order to find agreement. This choice for the sign of
n_{0}
is related to a conventional choice in the sign of the electric field in the CCLP solution.

Combining all these ingredients together we find that regularized supergravity on-shell action
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
in (
60
) precisely agrees with the
k_{RRR}
term in the logarithm of the superconformal index
\log\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}
in (
69
). If one would like to obtain the entropy of the supersymmetric CCLP black hole in (
68
) one can simply take
\log\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}
and repeat the Legendre transformation procedure discussed above (
68
). These results constitute a precision test of holography for the wrapped M5-brane black holes we constructed above and a microscopic understanding of their Bekenstein-Hawking entropy in terms of the superconformal index of the dual 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs.

5.2
More general black holes?

The 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFTs in
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg
have a
U(1)_{F}
flavor symmetry in addition to the
U(1)_{R}
R-symmetry. This means that the superconformal index can be additionally refined with a flavor fugacity
\varphi_{F}
. The explicit form of this refined index in the Cardy-like or large
N
limit has not been studied in the literature. From the supergravity point of view the
U(1)_{F}\times U(1)_{R}
global symmetry is realized by the two Killing vectors in the 11d metric in (
2
) or alternatively by the two Abelian gauge fields in the 7d gauged supergravity (
2
). The asymptotically AdS
5
black hole solutions we studied above have only one electric charge associated with the
U(1)_{R}
gauge field. Both the QFT and supergravity setups therefore suggest that there may be more general black hole solutions with two electric charges and two angular momenta with entropy and on-shell action that can be accounted for by the large
N
limit of the refined superconformal index. While we have not calculated the refined index or constructed these supergravity solutions we present some speculations and educated guesses about their properties.

We begin with the following proposal for the generalized version of the “second sheet” formula for the index of a general 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFT with a
U(1)
flavor symmetry

\begin{split}\log\mathcal{I}_{\rm QFT}&=\frac{k_{RRR}\,\varphi_{R}^{3}+k_{RRF}\,\varphi_{R}^{2}\varphi_{F}+k_{RFF}\,\varphi_{R}\varphi_{F}^{2}+k_{FFF}\,\varphi_{F}^{3}}{48\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\,,\end{split}

(75)

where
\varphi_{R}
obeys the linear constraint (
70
) and we have only presented terms that involve the cubic ’t Hooft anomalies since these should be the leading contributions to the large
N
limit of the index of a holographic SCFT. This proposed formula for the index is based on the form of the supersymmetric Casimir energy for general 4d
\mathcal{N}=1
SCFTs in
Bobev:2015kza
as well as the holographic results for the index of
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM in
Hosseini:2017mds
.

Then, we propose the following identification between the field theory fugacities and the supergravity BPS potentials

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}

\displaystyle\longleftrightarrow\bm{\omega}_{1,2}\,,\quad\varphi_{R}\longleftrightarrow\nu\bm{\varphi}_{R}\,,\quad\varphi_{F}\longleftrightarrow\rho\bm{\varphi}_{F}\,,

(76)

where, the constants
\nu
and
\rho
should be determined by using the holographic dictionary using the explicit putative black hole solutions.To be consistent with the form of the on-shell action in (
60
) we expect that
\nu=\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}\,g
, such that
\bm{\varphi}\equiv\bm{\varphi}_{R}
.

In the dual supergravity there should be a supersymmetric Euclidean solution with two angular momenta and two electric charges that has the following on-shell action

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}\,\bm{\varphi}_{R}^{3}+\mu_{2}\,\bm{\varphi}_{R}^{2}\bm{\varphi}_{F}+\mu_{3}\,\bm{\varphi}_{R}\bm{\varphi}_{F}^{2}+\mu_{4}\,\bm{\varphi}_{F}^{3}}{\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{\omega}_{2}}\,,

(77)

where the real parameters
\mu_{1,2,3,4}
should be determined by the coupling constants of the particular supergravity theory hosting the proposed new black hole. One should then derive, via the holographic dictionary, explicitly how they are related to the ’t Hooft anomalies
k_{RRR},k_{RRF},k_{RFF}
and
k_{FFF}
. In order to have a holographic match between
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
and
\log\mathcal{I}_{\text{QFT}}
we need the following relations to hold

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{RRR}\nu^{3}}{48}\,,\quad\mu_{2}=\frac{k_{RRF}\nu^{2}\rho}{48}\,,\quad\mu_{3}=\frac{k_{RFF}\nu\rho^{2}}{48}\,,\quad\mu_{4}=\frac{k_{FFF}\rho^{3}}{48}\,,

(78)

Establishing this equivalence is of course non-trivial and hinges on the explicit construction of the black hole solution with two electric charges, the details of the holographic map, and the validity of the formula for the index in (
75
).

If all our educated guesses (
75
)-(
78
) are correct and such a non-trivial agreement can indeed be established one can try to proceed and perform a Legendre transform of the on-shell action, or equivalently the large
N
index in order to derive the entropy of the black hole. This proves to be a non-trivial calculation as we now outline. First let us rewrite the on-shell action as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{24\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{\omega}_{2}}\kappa_{IJK}\bm{\varphi}^{I}\bm{\varphi}^{J}\bm{\varphi}^{K}\,,

(79)

where
\kappa_{IJK}
is totally symmetric with its independent components given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{111}

\displaystyle=\frac{24\mu_{1}}{\pi}\,,\quad\kappa_{112}=\frac{8\mu_{2}}{\pi}\,,\quad\kappa_{122}=\frac{8\mu_{3}}{\pi}\,,\quad\kappa_{222}=\frac{24\mu_{4}}{\pi}\,,

(80)

and
\bm{\varphi}^{I}=(\bm{\varphi}_{R},\bm{\varphi}_{F})
. This rewriting of the on-shell action is inspired by the on-shell action of supersymmetric black holes in 5d gauged supergravity coupled to two vector multiplets studied in
Cassani:2019mms
. The only difference here is that we use a basis of supergravity BPS potentials that explicitly singles out the superconformal
R
-symmetry. To find the entropy we now follow the familiar procedure of extremizing

\displaystyle\bm{S}

\displaystyle=-\bm{\mathcal{I}}-\bm{\varphi}^{I}\bm{Q}_{I}-\bm{\omega}_{1}\bm{J}_{1}-\bm{\omega}_{2}\bm{J}_{2}-\lambda(\bm{\omega}_{1}+\bm{\omega}_{2}-\nu\bm{\varphi}_{R}-2\pi\mathrm{i})\,,

(81)

where
\bm{Q}_{I}=(\bm{Q}_{R},\bm{Q}_{F})
and, crucially, only
\bm{\varphi}_{R}
is on the “second sheet”. The extremization equations read

\displaystyle-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_1}

\displaystyle=\bm{J}_{1}+\lambda\,,\quad-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_2}=\bm{J}_{2}+\lambda\,,\quad-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}_R}=\bm{Q}_{R}-\nu\lambda\,,\quad-\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}_F}=\bm{Q}_{F}\,.

(82)

Together with the linear constraint
\bm{\omega}_{1}+\bm{\omega}_{2}-\nu\bm{\varphi}_{R}=2\pi\mathrm{i}
, this amounts to a system of
5
non-linear equations that need to be solved for
(\bm{\omega}_{1},\bm{\omega}_{2},\bm{\varphi}_{R},\bm{\varphi}_{F},\lambda)
in terms of the charges
(\bm{J}_{1},\bm{J}_{2},\bm{Q}_{R},\bm{Q}_{F})
. We have not been able to solve this system of equations analytically and have only found numerical solutions. To proceed further we resort to the shortcut outlined in Section
4.3
. We note that
\bm{\mathcal{I}}
is again homogeneous of degree
1
and the extremized entropy is therefore given by
\bm{S}=2\pi\mathrm{i}\lambda
.

We note that the relation

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\kappa^{IJK}\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}^I}\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}^J}\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\varphi}^K}-\frac{\pi}{4}\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_1}\partialderivative{\bm{\mathcal{I}}}{\bm{\omega}_2}\,,

(83)

holds provided that
\kappa_{IJK}
obeys

\displaystyle\kappa_{IJK}\kappa_{J^{\prime}(LM}\kappa_{PQ)K^{\prime}}g^{JJ^{\prime}}g^{KK^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}g_{I(L}\kappa_{MPQ)}\,,

(84)

where the metric and the inverse metric read

\displaystyle g_{IJ}

\displaystyle=6\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2 \mu_1})^{-2/3}\delta_{IJ}\,,\quad g^{IJ}=\frac{1}{6}\quantity(\frac{\pi}{2 \mu_1})^{2/3}\delta^{IJ}\,,

(85)

and consequently, the non-zero components of
\kappa^{IJK}
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa^{111}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{18\mu_{1}}\,,\quad\kappa^{112}=\frac{\pi\mu_{2}}{54\mu_{1}^{2}}\,,\quad\kappa^{122}=\frac{\pi\mu_{3}}{54\mu_{1}^{2}}\,,\quad\kappa^{222}=\frac{\pi\mu_{4}}{18\mu_{1}^{2}}\,.

(86)

In the symmetric fugacity basis used in the supergravity analysis in
Cassani:2019mms
the relation (
84
) holds since it ensures that the scalars residing in the two vector multiplets parametrize a symmetric coset space. If we take this requirement imposed by gauged supergravity as a given and use the extremization equations (
82
) together with (
83
) we find that
\lambda
has to obey the following cubic equation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=p_{0}+p_{1}\lambda+p_{2}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{3}\,,\\
p_{0}&=-\frac{1}{\nu^{3}}\bm{Q}_{R}^{3}-\frac{27\mu_{1}}{\nu^{3}}\bm{J}_{1}\bm{J}_{2}-\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\nu^{3}}\bm{Q}_{R}^{2}\bm{Q}_{F}-\frac{\mu_{4}}{\mu_{1}\nu^{3}}\bm{Q}_{F}^{3}\,,\\
p_{1}&=-\frac{27\mu_{1}}{\nu^{3}}(\bm{J}_{1}+\bm{J}_{2})+\frac{3}{\nu^{2}}\bm{Q}_{R}^{2}+\frac{2\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\nu^{2}}\bm{Q}_{R}\bm{Q}_{F}+\frac{\mu_{3}}{\mu_{1}\nu^{2}}\bm{Q}_{F}^{2}\,,\\
p_{2}&=-\frac{27\mu_{1}}{\nu^{3}}-\frac{3}{\nu}\bm{Q}_{R}-\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\nu}\bm{Q}_{F}\,.\end{aligned}

(87)

Then the non-linear relation between the charges and the entropy read

\displaystyle p_{0}

\displaystyle=p_{1}p_{2}\,,\quad\bm{S}=2\pi\sqrt{p_{1}}\,.

(88)

Note that these expression nicely reduce to our results in Section
4.3
when
\bm{Q}_{F}=0
and

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{k_{RRR}\nu^{3}}{48}=\frac{1}{48}\frac{4\pi L^{3}}{9G_{5}}\quantity(\frac{\mathpzc{x}\sqrt{3}}{L})^{3}=\frac{\pi\mathpzc{x}^{3}}{12\sqrt{3}G_{5}}=\mu\,,

(89)

where we have used our educated guess for the relation between the supergravity constant
\mu_{1}
and the SCFT anomaly
k_{RRR}
.
More importantly, this expression for the entropy and the non-linear constraint between the black hole charges are only valid when (
84
) is satisfied. From the point of view of the dual SCFT the origin of this relation is far from clear. We assume that
\mu_{2}
is subleading in large
N
, which is indeed realized by the concrete example of the class
\mathcal{S}
theories studied above, see (
78
) and (
71
) where
\rho
is assumed not to scale with
N
. Using this we find that the only way to satisfy the constraint in (
84
) is if the following relations hold

\displaystyle\mu_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}\mu_{1}\,,\quad\mu_{4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\mu_{1}\,.

(90)

We are not aware of any QFT reason why such a relation should be obeyed by the supergravity constants
\mu_{1},\mu_{3}
and
\mu_{4}
. Indeed, using our educated guess (
78
), in terms of the QFT anomalies these relations read

\displaystyle k_{RFF}\rho^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{2}k_{RRR}\nu^{2}\,,\quad k_{FFF}\rho^{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}k_{RRR}\nu^{3}

(91)

Clearly, these relations cannot hold for the explicit anomalies (
71
) for any value of
\rho
, even if we allow it to be
z
-dependent.

Based on the discussion above we conclude that for general holographic SCFTs with a
U(1)_{F}
flavor symmetry, including the particular example of the
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs discussed above, one cannot find a simple compact expression for the black hole entropy in terms of the two electric charges and two angular momenta. This points to the fact that perhaps the sought more general wrapped M5-brane black holes with an additional flavor electric charge cannot be constructed using a 5d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauged supergravity theory coupled to a finite number of vector multiplets. Indeed, based on the consistent truncation classification results in
Cassani:2020cod
;
Josse:2021put
there are no candidates for such a gauged supergravity theory.

The situation is different for
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM. One can show that for this theory the ’t Hooft anomalies for the
U(1)^{3}
Cartan subalgebra of the
SO(6)
global symmetry obey the constraints in (
90
) and indeed, as shown in
Kim:2006he
, there is a simple compact expression for the entropy of the supersymmetric black hole with two angular momenta and three electric charges constructed in
Kunduri:2006ek
using the STU model of 5d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauged supergravity.

6
Discussion

The results presented above leave several open questions and interesting possibilities for generalizations. Here we discuss some of them.

•

As discussed in Section
5.2
it is very likely that there are more general supersymmetric black holes with an additional electric charge corresponding to the
U(1)_{F}
flavor symmetry of the class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs. It will be very interesting to construct these solutions, either by using an appropriate 5d matter-coupled supergravity theory arising as a consistent truncation of 11d supergravity, or by a judicious Ansatz for the fields of the 7d maximal gauged supergravity. Of course it should in principle be possible to construct such solutions directly in 11d supergravity but we expect that to be prohibitively difficult since the corresponding BPS equations should reduce to a system of coupled nonlinear PDEs.

•

There are two other generalizations of the black hole solutions discussed above that one can contemplate. For concreteness here we have focused the discussion on the class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs associated to the 6d
\mathcal{N}=(2,0)
SCFT of type
A_{N-1}
compactified on a smooth
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}
with
\mathfrak{g}>1
. Using the results in
Bah:2011vv
;
Bah:2012dg
it is straightforward to generalize this setup to the
D_{N}
type class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs and to
\mathfrak{g}=0,1
. On the supergravity side this corresponds to modifying the 11d supergravity solutions discussed above to have an internal
\mathbb{RP}^{4}
space instead of the
S^{4}
used in (
2
) and to modify the metric in (
10
) to that of the torus or the two-sphere. More non-trivially, one can attempt to find a larger class of black hole solutions arising from wrapped branes on punctured Riemann surfaces. While it is known how to construct such AdS
5
vacua with
\mathcal{N}=2
and
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry, see
Gaiotto:2009gz
;
Bah:2013qya
;
Bah:2015fwa
, it is not entirely clear how to generalize these supergravity solutions to include a black hole. Perhaps the 7d gauged supergravity method developed in
Bobev:2019ore
to treat some special punctures on the Riemann surface will prove useful in this regard.

•

As already emphasized in Section
5.1
it is not entirely clear to us why the Cardy-like limit of the index used to derive the “second sheet” formula for the index in
Cassani:2021fyv
has a larger regime of validity that captures the behavior of the large
N
limit of the index relevant for holography. It will be most interesting to understand this better and also to generalize the “second sheet” formula for the index to include fugacities associated with flavor symmetries. We have proposed such a generalization in (
75
) and it will be interesting to check our educated guess more rigorously. Finally, it is important to study
\log N
corrections to the large
N
class
\mathcal{S}
superconformal index which should correspond to the 1-loop contributions to the gravitational path integral of the KK modes of 11d supergravity around the AdS
5
black hole solutions. Establishing this relation rigorously will provide a stringent precision test of holography. We also note in passing that subleading terms in the large
N
power law expansion of the superconformal index are also captured by “second sheet” formula. These correspond to higher-derivative corrections to the CCLP solution in gauged supergravity. This was studied recently in
Bobev:2022bjm
;
Cassani:2022lrk
where a precise agreement between the holographic and QFT results was established. In particular a discussion on higher-derivative corrections to AdS
5
black holes arising from wrapped branes was presented in
Bobev:2022bjm
based on the assumptions that such solutions indeed exist. Our results here indeed show that this is true.

•

The superconformal index for the class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs discussed above was studied in
Beem:2012yn
for low values of
N
. It will be most interesting to understand how to extend the results of this work to larger values of
N
. For gauge theories coupled to matter a useful approach to studying the large
N
limit of the index is to employ the Bethe Ansatz re-formulation, see
Benini:2018mlo
;
Benini:2018ywd
. If such a formulation is possible for class
\mathcal{S}
SCFTs it will provide a concrete calculational tool to gain valuable insights into the quantum gravity corrections to the properties of the black holes constructed in this work.

Here we show that the three parameter, i.e.

Introduction

Estimating how many copies of an unknown quantum state are needed to identify the quantum state is a fundamental question in physics experiments. Quantum property testing provides a primary tool for achieving reliable control of quantum devices. Various quantum property testing problems occur during the preparation of an experimental setup
[
MdW16
]
, such as quantum state certification–whether a state is close to a target state, purity–whether a state is pure, and entanglement verification–whether a state is entangled.

Quantum property testing is the natural extension of the classical distribution property testing problem, which is a fundamental and active research topic in statistics. The study of quantum property testing has attracted much attention recently
[
MdW16
,
ACH+18
,
AR19
,
Aar20
,
BO21a
,
Yu20a
,
BO21b
,
CHL+22
]
.
Among many scenarios of quantum property testing, quantum state tomography is a remarkable and central problem.
Quantum state tomography aims to reconstruct the quantum states of physical systems. For an unknown
d
-dimensional mixed quantum state
\rho
with a rank less than
r
, how many copies of
\rho
are necessary and sufficient to generate an output of a good approximation of
{\rho}
with high probability? Provided general joint measurements are allowed, i.e., measurements on
\rho^{\otimes n}
for any
n
, the sample complexity of state tomography is
\Theta(\frac{d^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}})
for trace distance
\epsilon
, or
\tilde{\Theta}(\frac{dr}{\delta})
for infidelity
\delta
(see
[
HHJ+16
,
OW16
,
OW17
]
).

Quantum state certification, or general quantum identity testing, has also been actively studied
[
FL11
,
dSLCP11
,
AGKE15
,
BCL20
,
Yu21a
,
FSG21
,
Yu20b
,
OW15
]
. This problem asks whether an unknown state is identical to the other state, known or unknown.
[
BOW19
]
showed the sample complexity of the quantum identity testing (and state certification problem) is
\Theta(\frac{d}{\epsilon^{2}})
.

For many-body quantum systems, the state space dimension grows exponentially concerning the number of parties. Such growth is a fundamental challenge in performing quantum property testing. On the other hand, the quantum states of physical interest usually have special properties or are within a specific structure. Among all the quantum states of physical interest, quantum Markov chains are an important class. A quantum state
\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain if it can be reconstructed from its marginal
\rho_{AB}
by a quantum operation from
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
. Due to the importance of quantum Markov Chains, several equivalence conditions have been characterized.

•

\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov chain, i.e., there exists a quantum operation
\mathcal{N}_{B\mapsto BC}
from the
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
to
the
{\mathcal{H}}_{B\otimes C}
system such that

\rho_{ABC}=\mathcal{N}_{B\mapsto BC}(\rho_{AB})
.

•

\rho_{ABC}
can be reconstructed via the Petz map
[
Pet96
]
, i.e.,

\rho_{ABC}=\rho_{BC}^{1/2}(\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\otimes id_{C})\rho_{BC}^{1/2}\ .

•

The quantum conditional mutual information of
\rho_{ABC}
is 0, i.e.
[
Pet96
]
,

I(A:C|B):=S(AB)_{\rho}+S(BC)_{\rho}-S(B)_{\rho}-S(ABC)_{\rho}=0\ .

Here,
S(\rho)=-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho\log_{2}\rho)
is the von Neumann entropy.

•

\rho_{ABC}=\oplus_{k}p_{k}\rho_{AB_{L,k}}\otimes\rho_{CB_{R,k}}

for a probability distribution
\{p_{k}\}
and states
\rho_{AB_{L,k}}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{L,k}}
and
\rho_{CB_{R,k}}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{C}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{L,k}}
where
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}=\oplus_{k}({\mathcal{H}}_{B_{L,k}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{R,k}})
(with orthogonal subspaces

{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{L,k}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B_{R,k}}
)
[
HJPW04
]
.

The non-negativity of quantum conditional mutual information
I(A:C|B)\geq 0
, also known as strong subadditivity of the von Neumann entropy, is a highly
non-trivial theorem

[
LR73
]
. It plays a central role in quantum information theory.
Recently, applications of the quantum conditional mutual information have been found in
new areas of computer science and physics, including communication and information complexity
(see
[
Tou15
,
BGK+18
,
ATYY17
]
and references therein), de Finetti type theorems
[
BH13a
,
BH13b
]
and also the study of quantum many-body
systems
[

]
.

It is natural to study the robustness of the quantum Markov Chains. Many efforts have been devoted to exploring the robustness of quantum Markov Chain in terms of quantum conditional mutual information
I(A:C|B)
. Unlike the classical probability distribution, there is a tripartite quantum state far from the quantum Markov Chain while having small conditional mutual information
[
ILW07
]

A recent breakthrough from Fawzi and Renner
shows small conditional mutual information implies high fidelity recovery from bipartite reductions

[
FR15
]
. On the other hand, a large conditional mutual information does not imply bad recovery
[

]
. The relation between quantum conditional mutual information and fidelity of recovery has received lots of attention
[

]
.

1.1
Our results

One of the fundamental questions regarding quantum Markov Chain is then the following:

For a quantum Markov Chain
\rho_{ABC}
of system
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
, how many copies
n
of
\rho_{ABC}
are necessary and sufficient to output an estimate
\hat{\rho}
with
expected trace distance
\epsilon
(or infidelity
\delta
) to the true state
\rho_{ABC}
?

Consider a simple quantum Markov Chain
\rho_{ABC}=\rho_{AB}\otimes I_{C}/d_{C}
. To obtain a good estimation of
\rho_{ABC}
, one must have a good estimation of
\rho_{AB}
. Similarly, one must get a good estimation of
\rho_{BC}
by considering quantum Markov Chains of the form
\rho_{ABC}=I_{A}/d_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC}
. Intuitively, a quantum Markov Chain of system
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
is completely determined by its marginal of subsystems
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
and system
{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
via the Petz map

\displaystyle\rho_{BC}^{1/2}(\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\otimes id_{C})\rho_{BC}^{1/2}.

(1)

(One can switch the role of the system
AB
and
BC
here.)
If we can learn
\rho_{AB}
and
\rho_{BC}

exactly
, we can
fully
recover
\rho_{ABC}
.
It is reasonable to conjecture that
\Theta(d_{A}^{2}d_{B}^{2}+d_{B}^{2}d_{C}^{2})
copies are necessary and sufficient.

However, with finite copies of
\rho
, one can not learn
\rho
exactly, but only up to some precision. On the other hand, the Petz map involves
\rho^{-1/2}
, which is unbounded and not Lipschitz continuous for
\rho
in general. This obstacle motivates us to study the robustness of the Petz map concerning
\rho_{AB}
and
\rho_{BC}
.

The accuracy is usually measured in the following two forms.
The fidelity of two quantum states
\rho,\sigma
is

F(\rho,\sigma):=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho}~\sigma\sqrt{\rho}}
, then
the “infidelity” is
1-F
, represented by
\delta
, and
their trace distance is
\frac{1}{2}\|\rho-\sigma\|_{1}
, represented by
\epsilon
.
These are related by
[
NC11
]
.

\displaystyle\delta\leq\epsilon\leq\sqrt{1-(1-\delta)^{2}}=\sqrt{2\delta-\delta^{2}}.

(2)

Our results on the robustness of the Petz map enable us to confirm the correctness of the above intuition. More precisely, we show

Theorem 1
.

The sample complexity of quantum Markov Chain tomography of system
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
is

\tilde{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}}{\delta})
in term of infidelity
\delta
, and
\tilde{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}})
in term of trace distance
\epsilon
.

By employing the robustness results, we can settle the sample complexity of the quantum Markov Chain certification problem.

We have two quantum Markov Chains
\sigma
and
\rho
at
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
where
\sigma
is known. The quantum Markov Chain certification problem asks how many copies are sufficient and necessary to distinguish the case of
\rho=\sigma
and the case where the infidelity (or trace distance) between
\rho
and
\sigma
is at least
\delta
(or
\epsilon
)?

More precisely, we show

Theorem 2
.

The sample complexity of quantum Markov Chain certification is
{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\delta})
for indfidelity
\delta
, and
{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\epsilon^{2}})
for trace distance
\epsilon
.

Another application of the robustness results for the Petz map is the quantum Markov Chain certification problem.

For tripartite quantum state
\rho_{ABC}
of system
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
, how many copies are sufficient and necessary to distinguish the following two cases:
\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov Chain, and
\rho_{ABC}
is
\epsilon
-far from its Petz recovery state
\rho_{BC}^{1/2}(\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-1/2}\otimes I_{C})\rho_{BC}^{1/2}
.

Here, we choose the Petz recovery state but not general quantum Markov Chains because of the complicated structure of quantum Markov Chains. According to the literature, the closeness to a recovery state is more suitable than the distance to quantum Markov Chains, and the Petz map provides the most natural recovery state. We choose the closeness to a recovery state because bad recovery implies large conditional mutual information, but the converse is not valid in general
[
Sut18
]
.

In the classical distribution version, the Petz recovery state is always a Markov Chain, i.e. distribution with
0
conditional mutual information.
[
CDKS18
]
gives the first conditional independence tester with
sublinear
sample complexity. The method is used in
[
Yu21a
]
to provide a subquadratic tester for the quantum Markov Chain when
B
is classical.

The following result provides a tester for general quantum Markov Chains.

Theorem 3
.

\tilde{O}(\frac{\min\{d_{A}d_{B}^{3}d_{C}^{3},d_{A}^{3}d_{B}^{3}d_{C}\}}{\epsilon^{2}})
copies are sufficient to certify whether

\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov Chain or
\epsilon
-far from its Petz recovery state for trace distance
\epsilon
.

For quantum systems with
d_{A}\gg d_{B}d_{C}
or
d_{C}\gg d_{A}d_{B}
, this is better than the standard full state tomography which uses
\tilde{\Theta}(\frac{d_{A}^{2}d_{B}^{2}d_{C}^{2}}{\epsilon^{2}})
copies.

We generalized the tomography results into multipartite quantum Markov Chain of the system
\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
. Here
\rho
is called a quantum Markov Chain in
\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
if

\displaystyle\rho=\mathcal{N}_{n-1}\circ\cdots\mathcal{N}_{2}(\rho_{1,2})

(3)

where
\mathcal{N}_{i}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{i})\mapsto{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{i+1})
with form
\mathcal{N}_{i}(X)=\rho_{i,i+1}^{1/2}(\rho_{i}^{-1/2}X\rho_{i}^{-1/2}\otimes id_{i+1})\rho_{i,i+1}^{1/2}
, the Petz map defined of system
{\mathcal{H}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{i+1}
with
\rho_{s}
denotes the reduced density matrix of system
s\subset\{1,2,\cdots,n\}
.

Theorem 4
.

\tilde{O}(\frac{n^{2}\max_{i}\{d_{i}^{2}d_{i+1}^{2}\}}{\delta})
copies are sufficient to tomography a quantum Markov Chain in term of infidelity
\delta
with
d_{i}
be the dimension of the
i
-th subsystem.

This could be exponentially better than standard tomography method of quantum system
\otimes_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}
which cost
\tilde{O}(\frac{n^{2}\max_{i}\{\Pi_{i}d_{i}^{2}\}}{\delta})
copies.

Preliminaries

2.1
Basic quantum mechanics

An isolated physical system is associated with a
Hilbert space, called the
state space
. A
pure state
of a
quantum system is a normalized vector in its state space, denoted by the Dirac notation
\left|\varphi\right\rangle
. A

mixed state
is represented by a density operator on the state
space. Here, a density operator
\rho
on
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is a
semi-definite positive linear operator such that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho)=1
.

The state space of a composed quantum system is the tensor product of the state spaces of its component systems.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
be a Hilbert space with dimension
d_{k}
. One can define a Hilbert space
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
as the tensor product of Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{k}
.
The quantum state on the multipartite system
\bigotimes_{k=1}^{n}{\mathcal{H}}_{k}
is a semi-definite positive linear operator such that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho)=1
.

2.2
Quantum measurement

A positive-operator valued measure (POVM) is
described by a collection of matrices
\{M_{i}\}
with
M_{i}\geq 0
and

\displaystyle\sum_{i}M_{i}=I_{d}.

If the state of a quantum system was
\rho

immediately before measurement
\{M_{i}\}
was performed,
the probability of that result
i
recurring is

\displaystyle p(i)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(M_{i}\rho).

2.3
Quantum operation

Quantum operations describe a broad class of transformations a quantum mechanical system can undergo. A quantum operation
{\Phi}
can be represented in an elegant form known as the operator-sum
representation

\displaystyle{\Phi}(\rho)=\sum_{i}A_{i}\rho A_{i}^{{\dagger}}

with
\sum_{i}A_{i}^{{\dagger}}A_{i}=I
. The adjoint map
\Phi^{*}
of
\Phi
is defined as

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(X)=\sum_{i}A_{i}^{{\dagger}}XA_{i}.

\Phi^{*}
is a quantum operation iff
\sum_{i}A_{i}^{{\dagger}}A_{i}=\sum_{i}A_{i}A_{i}^{{\dagger}}=I
.

Any quantum operation
\Phi
applied on system
{\mathcal{H}}
has a Stinespring Dilation, i.e., there exists an isometry
V:{\mathcal{H}}\mapsto{\mathcal{H}}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{E}
such that

\displaystyle\Phi(\rho)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{E}(V\rho V^{{\dagger}}),

where
V
is an isometry if
V^{{\dagger}}V=I_{{\mathcal{H}}}
.

2.4
Norms and fidelity

Recall that the Schatten
p
-norm of a general operator
A
is defined as

\displaystyle||A||_{p}=(\mathrm{Tr}|A|^{p})^{1/p}\ ,\ 1<p<\infty,

where
|A|\equiv\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}
is the positive square root of
A^{\dagger}A
. We denote
S_{p}({\mathcal{H}})
for the Schatten
p
-class operator on the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. For
p=1
,
\parallel\!\cdot\!\parallel_{1}
is the trace class norm and the case

is Hilbert-Schmidt norm. We will also denote
\parallel\!A\!\parallel_{\infty}
as the operator norm of

.

The
\ell_{1}
distance (also called trace distance) between quantum states
\rho
and
\sigma
is defined as

\displaystyle||\rho-\sigma||_{1}\equiv\mathrm{Tr}|\rho-\sigma|

and their

distance is defined as

\displaystyle||\rho-\sigma||_{2}=\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}(\rho-\sigma)^{2}}.

For
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{D}({\mathcal{C}}^{d})
, we have the following relation between

and
\ell_{2}
distances,

\displaystyle||\rho-\sigma||_{2}\leq||\rho-\sigma||_{1}\leq\sqrt{d}||\rho-\sigma||_{2}.

We will also use fidelity

to measure the "closeness" of two quantum states:

\displaystyle F(\rho,\sigma)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}|.

It is known that the following relation between fidelity and
\ell_{1}
distance hold.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
ANSV08
,
OW17
]

For density matrices
\rho
and
\sigma

\displaystyle F(\rho,\sigma)+\frac{\parallel\!\rho-\sigma\!\parallel_{1}}{2}\geq 1,

\displaystyle F^{2}(\rho,\sigma)+\frac{\parallel\!\rho-\sigma\!\parallel_{1}^{2}}{4}\geq 1,

\displaystyle F^{2}(\rho,\sigma)\leq\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}})\leq F(\rho,\sigma).

2.6
Petz map for general quantum operation

The relative entropy between quantum state
\rho
and
\sigma
is

\displaystyle S(\rho||\sigma)=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho\log\rho)-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho\log\sigma)

if the support of
\rho
is contained in the support of
\sigma
, and

\infty
otherwise.

The strong subadditivity is equivalent to the monotonicity of relative entropy
under a partial trace operation:

\displaystyle S(\rho_{ABC}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC})-S(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=S(AB)_{\rho}+S(BC)_{\rho}-S(B)_{\rho}-S(ABC)_{\rho}=I(A:C|B).

Petz considered the general Petz map for quantum operation
\mathcal{N}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Petz
[
Pet96
]
)
.

For states
\rho
and
\sigma
and quantum operation
\Phi
,

S(\rho\|\sigma)=S(\Phi(\rho)\|\Phi(\sigma))

iff there exists a quantum operation
\widehat{\Phi}

such that

\widehat{\Phi}(\Phi(\rho))=\rho,\quad\widehat{\Phi}(\Phi(\sigma))=\sigma.

Furthermore, on the support of
\Phi(\sigma)
,

\widehat{\Phi}(\alpha)=\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi^{*}\left(\Phi(\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\alpha\Phi(\sigma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4)

Here
\Phi^{*}
denotes the adjoint map of
\Phi
.

For tripartite quantum system
{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}
, we choose
\Phi:{\mathcal{H}}_{BC}\mapsto{\mathcal{H}}_{B}
to be the partial trace operator, i.e., for any
\sigma_{BC}

\displaystyle\Phi(\sigma_{BC})=\sigma_{BC}.

Then

I(A:C|B)_{\rho}=0
iff

\displaystyle S(\rho_{ABC}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{BC})=S(\rho_{AB}||\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

Lemma
2.5
implies

\displaystyle\rho_{ABC}=\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{AB}\otimes id_{C})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hskip 2.84544pt.

(5)

One can verify that

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
\rho_{BC}
, the
Petz map
\mathcal{N}_{B\mapsto BC}(X)=\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(X\otimes id_{C})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}
is a quantum operation.

Proof.

Let
M_{i}=\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\otimes\left|i\right\rangle_{C}
, one can verify that

\displaystyle\sum_{i}M_{i}^{{\dagger}}XM_{i}=\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(X\otimes\sum_{i}|i\rangle\langle i|)\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(X\otimes id_{C})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\mathcal{N}_{B\mapsto BC}(X).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}M_{i}^{{\dagger}}M_{i}=\sum_{i}\rangle i|\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|i\right\rangle=\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{i}\rangle i|\rho_{BC}\left|i\right\rangle\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}=I_{{\mathcal{H}}_{B}}.

∎

3
Continuity of the Petz map

In this section, we study the continuity of the Petz map.

3.1
Useful Lemmata

We start with the key estimate in our results.

Lemma 3.1
.

For density matrices,
\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB}
,
\rho_{BC},\sigma_{BC}
and
\rho_{B},\sigma_{B}
, with

\displaystyle\rho_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}\rho_{AB},

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{C}\sigma_{BC},

\displaystyle\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}+\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}+\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}

\displaystyle\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\parallel\!\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{ABC}(\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})+\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{ABC}(\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{ABC}(\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{ABC}(\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\parallel\!\rho_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}^{2}

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\parallel\!(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}^{1/2}\!\parallel_{4}

\displaystyle\parallel\!(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}\parallel\!(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}\parallel\!(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{4}

Here the first inequality uses Lemma
3.6
. The second inequality follows from the following fact: For any
X_{AR}
,

||\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}X_{AR}||_{2}\leq d_{A}^{1/2}||X_{AR}||_{2}\ .

Indeed, we write

\displaystyle X_{AR}=\sum_{i,j}|i\rangle\langle j|\otimes X_{i,j}

Then

\displaystyle||\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}X_{AR}||_{2}^{2}=||\sum_{i}X_{i,i}||_{2}^{2}\leq(\sum_{i}||X_{i,i}||_{2})^{2}\leq d_{A}(\sum_{i}||X_{i,i}||_{2}^{2})\leq d_{A}(\sum_{i,j}||X_{i,j}||_{2}^{2})=d_{A}||X_{AR}||_{2}^{2}.

The third term is similar,

\displaystyle\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{AB}^{1/2}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})\!\parallel_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\parallel\!(\sigma_{AB}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{AB}^{1/2}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d_{C})^{1/4}\parallel\!(\sigma_{AB}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{AB}^{1/2}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d_{C})^{1/4}\parallel\!(\sigma_{AB}^{1/2}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2})^{2}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(d_{C})^{1/4}\parallel\!\sigma_{AB}^{1/2}-\rho_{AB}^{1/2}\!\parallel_{4}^{1/2}

For the second term,

\displaystyle\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{AB}^{1/2}\!\parallel_{4}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{AB}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}\parallel\!\rho_{B}^{1/2}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{BC}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}^{1/2}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}d_{C}^{1/4}\parallel\!\rho_{B}^{1/2}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{B}(\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\rho_{B}^{1/2}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}d_{C}^{1/4}\parallel\!(\sigma_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\!\parallel_{2}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{A}^{1/4}d_{C}^{1/4}\parallel\!\sigma_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{4}

∎

Remark 3.13

The above lemma also applies to Schatten

For

\displaystyle\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ 2d_{A}^{1/2p^{\prime}}\parallel\!\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2p}+2d_{A}^{1/2p^{\prime}}d_{C}^{1/2p^{\prime}}\parallel\!\sigma_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2p}+2d_{C}^{1/2p^{\prime}}\parallel\!\omega_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{AB}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2p}.

Quantum Markov Chain tomography

In this section, we study the quantum Markov Chain tomography problem:

4.1
Tripartite quantum Markov Chain

We settle the complexity of this problem for a tripartite quantum system.

Theorem

1

{\mathcal{H}}_{A}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{B}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{C}

n

for trace distance
\varepsilon

Proof.

Suppose the given state is
\rho_{ABC}
. The upper bound for infidelity
\delta

•

2.2
to tomography
\rho_{AB}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{C}\rho_{ABC}
and
\rho_{BC}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}\rho_{ABC}
with infidelity
0.01\delta
, and the estimations are
\hat{\rho}_{AB}
and
\hat{\rho}_{BC}

•

Output

\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{AB}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}

where
\hat{\rho}_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{C}\rho_{BC}

By the monotonicity of fidelity under partial trace, we have

F(\rho_{B},\hat{\rho}_{B})\geq F(\rho_{BC},\hat{\rho}_{BC})\geq 1-0.01\delta,

According to Theorem
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle F(\rho_{ABC},\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{AB}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\geq 1-0.18\delta.

where we use the fact that for quantum Markov Chain

In the first step, we use

\frac{100d_{A}^{2}d_{B}^{2}\ln(d_{A}d_{B}/\delta)}{\delta}+\frac{100d_{B}^{2}d_{C}^{2}\ln(d_{B}d_{C}/\delta)}{\delta}=O(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}\ln(d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}/\delta)}{\delta}).

samples of
\rho_{ABC}
.

To prove the upper for distance

\epsilon

\delta

\frac{1}{2}||\rho_{ABC}-\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{AB}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{1}\leq\epsilon.

The used number of samples is

O(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}\ln(d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}/\epsilon)}{\epsilon^{2}}).

If we use Lemma
2.3
with parameter

instead of Lemma
2.2
with parameter
0.01\delta
.
Theorem
3.9

\frac{1}{2}||\rho_{ABC}-\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{AB}\hat{\rho}_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{1}\leq 0.6\epsilon.

O(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}}{\epsilon^{4}})

samples.
The bound for
\delta

\delta

O(\frac{(d_{A}^{2}+d_{C}^{2})d_{B}^{2}}{\delta^{4}})

The lower bound follows from the following fact:

4.2
Multipartite quantum Markov Chain

In this subsection, we generalized the tomography results into multipartite quantum Markov Chain of the system
\otimes_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}

Theorem

4
. For quantum system
\otimes_{i=1}^{m}{\mathcal{H}}_{i}

i

\tilde{O}(\frac{m^{2}\max_{i}\{d_{i}^{2}d_{i+1}^{2}\}}{\delta})

\delta

Proof.

We use the following algorithm.

•

For each even number
2\leq i\leq m-1
, we use Lemma
2.2
to tomography
\rho_{i,i+1}
in parallel, with infidelity
\delta_{i}
and a successful probability
1-\frac{1}{100m}
.

•

For each odd number
2\leq i\leq m-1
, we use Lemma
2.2
to tomography
\rho_{i,i+1}
in parallel, with infidelity
\delta_{i}
and a succesful probability
1-\frac{1}{100m}
.

•

Let the estimations be
\hat{\rho}_{i,i+1}
and
\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{i}:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{i})\mapsto{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}_{i}\otimes{\mathcal{H}}_{i+1})

\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{i}(X)=\hat{\rho}_{i,i+1}^{1/2}(\hat{\rho}_{i}^{-1/2}X\hat{\rho}_{i}^{-1/2}\otimes id_{i+1})\hat{\rho}_{i,i+1}^{1/2}.

•

Output

\hat{\rho}=\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{m-1}\circ\cdots\circ\hat{\mathcal{N}}_{2}(\hat{\rho}_{1,2}).

The parameters
\delta_{i}
will be chosen later.

By union bound, we know that with probability at least
1-\frac{m}{100m}=0.99

1-F(\rho_{i,i+1},\hat{\rho}_{i,i+1})\leq\delta_{i}\ \ \ \ \forall\ \ \ 1\leq i\leq m-1.

We first show the following,

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\mathcal{N}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{N}_{m-2}((\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2})}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+\sqrt{\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\mathcal{N}_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\mathcal{N}_{m-3}((\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{2})}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +||\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\rho_{m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle||\rho_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1,m}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\rho_{m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\rho}_{m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}+||\hat{\rho}_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\hat{\rho}_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\rho_{m-2,m-1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdots\rho_{1,2}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}\delta_{i}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The parallel processing in the first two steps allows us to use the same copies to accomplish the tomography of

5
Certification of quantum Markov Chain

In this section, we study the quantum Markov Chain certification problem:

For a quantum Markov Chain
\rho_{ABC}
and a known quantum Markov Chain
\sigma_{ABC}
, how many copies of
\rho_{ABC}
are needed to distinguish between two cases:

•

\rho_{ABC}=\sigma_{ABC}
;

•

F(\rho_{ABC},\sigma_{ABC})<1-\delta
(or,
||\rho_{ABC}-\sigma_{ABC}||_{1}>\epsilon
)?

Theorem

2
. The sample complexity of quantum Markov Chain certification is
{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\delta})
for indfidelity
\delta
, and
{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\epsilon^{2}})
for trace distance
\epsilon
.

Proof.

The upper bound for infidelity
\delta
is proved as follows:

•

Use Lemma
2.4
certify whether
\rho_{AB}=\sigma_{AB}
and
\rho_{BC}=\sigma_{BC}
with infidelity
0.01\delta
and success probability
0.99
. and the estimations are
\hat{\rho}_{AB}
and
\hat{\rho}_{BC}
, respectively.

•

If any of the certifications returns that the infidelity is at least
0.01\delta
, then we output “They are at least
\delta
-far". Otherwise, return
\rho_{ABC}=\sigma_{ABC}
.

If one of the certification returns the infidelity at least
0.01\delta
, says
F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})<1-0.01\delta
, then
\rho_{ABC}\neq\sigma_{ABC}
, which implies
F(\rho_{ABC},\sigma_{ABC})<1-\delta
.

If both
F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})>1-0.01\delta
and
F(\rho_{BC},\sigma_{BC})>1-0.01\delta
.
By the monotonicity of fidelity under partial trace, we have

F(\rho_{B},\hat{\rho}_{B})\geq F(\rho_{BC},\hat{\rho}_{BC})\geq 1-0.01\delta,

Corrolary
3.8
leads to

F(\rho_{ABC},\sigma_{ABC})>1-0.18\delta.

This implies
\rho_{ABC}=\sigma_{ABC}
.

In this process, we use

{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\delta})

samples of
\rho_{ABC}
.

To prove the upper for distance
\epsilon
, we only need to choose
\delta=\epsilon^{2}/2
and use

{\Theta}(\frac{(d_{A}+d_{C})d_{B}}{\epsilon^{2}})

samples of
\rho_{ABC}
.

The lower bound follows from the following fact:
\rho_{AB}\otimes\frac{I_{C}}{d_{C}}
and
\frac{I_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\rho_{BC}
and are both quantum Markov Chains for any
\rho_{AB}
and
\rho_{BC}
. We let
\sigma=\frac{I_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\frac{I_{B}}{d_{B}}\otimes\frac{I_{C}}{d_{C}}
.
If we know
\rho_{ABC}=\rho_{AB}\otimes\frac{I_{C}}{d_{C}}
, then

||\rho_{ABC}-\sigma_{ABC}||_{1}=||\rho_{AB}-\frac{I_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\frac{I_{B}}{d_{B}}||_{1}.

According to Lemma
2.4
,
\Omega(\frac{d_{A}d_{B}}{\epsilon^{2}})
copies of
\rho_{AB}
is needed to distinguish

•

\rho_{AB}=\sigma_{AB}
;

•

||\rho_{AB}-\sigma_{AB}||_{1}>\epsilon
.

By the relation between
\epsilon
and
\delta
, we know that

\Omega(\frac{d_{A}d_{B}}{\delta})
copies of
\rho_{AB}
is needed to distinguish

•

\rho_{AB}=\sigma_{AB}
;

•

F(\rho_{AB},\sigma_{AB})<1-\delta
.

By choosing
\rho_{ABC}=\frac{id_{A}}{d_{A}}\otimes\rho_{BC}
, similar arguments lead to lower bound

\Omega(\frac{d_{B}d_{C}}{\epsilon^{2}})
and
\Omega(\frac{d_{B}d_{C}}{\delta})
. This completes the proof.
∎

6
Testing quantum Markov Chain

In this section, we study the problem of testing quantum Markov Chain, i.e., distinguishing between two cases for
\rho_{ABC}
:

•

\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov Chain;

•

\parallel\!\rho_{ABC}-\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{1}\geq\epsilon
.

We use the following observation to study the quantum Markov Chain testing problem.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
\rho_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}\rho_{AB}
, and
\sigma_{B}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{C}\sigma_{BC}
with

F(\rho_{BC},\sigma_{BC})\geq 1-\delta,

then

\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}\leq 8\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

We first observe

F(\rho_{B},\sigma_{B})\geq F(\rho_{BC},\sigma_{BC})\geq 1-\delta.

Theorem
3.7
implies

F(\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}},\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}})\geq 1-8\delta.

According to the relation between
\ell_{2}
norm,
\ell_{1}
-norm and infidelity, we have

\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{2}\leq\parallel\!\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{1}\leq 8\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

Full-state tomography is not always necessary for the quantum Markov Chain testing problem.

Theorem

3
.
\tilde{O}(\frac{\min\{d_{A}d_{B}^{3}d_{C}^{3},d_{A}^{3}d_{B}^{3}d_{C}\}}{\epsilon^{2}})
copies are sufficient to certify whether

\rho_{ABC}
is a quantum Markov Chain or
\epsilon
-far from its Petz recovery state for trace distance
\epsilon
.

Proof.

We use the following tester to show the upper bound
\tilde{O}(\frac{d_{A}d_{B}^{3}d_{C}^{3}}{\epsilon^{2}})
.

•

Use Lemma
2.2
to tomography
\rho_{BC}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits_{A}\rho_{ABC}
with infidelity
\delta:=\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{400d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}
, and the estimation is
\sigma_{BC}
.

•

Apply Petz map
\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}
on
\rho_{AB}
to obtain
\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}

•

Use Lemma
2.4
to distinguish
\rho_{ABC}
and

\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}
in
\ell_{2}
norm.
Return “
\rho_{ABC}

||\rho_{ABC}-\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\sigma_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}||_{2}<0.4\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{d_{A}d_{B}d_{C}}}

\parallel\!\rho_{ABC}-\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{AB}\rho_{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{BC}^{\frac{1}{2}}\!\parallel_{1}\geq\varepsilon

Corollary
6.1
implies

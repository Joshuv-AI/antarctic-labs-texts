Negative pressure in the quantum vacuum
Zhentao Zhang

Abstract
It is well known that a constant energy density of the vacuum produces a negative pressure. We show that, in the presence of special boundaries for the photon field, the virtual photons in the quantum vacuum may induce a negative pressure of this type. In this context, we find in particular that an anomalous quantum vacuum radiation is responsible for the appearance of this negative pressure in a system, which generates the tangential Casimir force experienced by parallel conducting plates.



The cosmological constant in Einstein’s equations is equivalent to the existence of a constant vacuum energy density
\rho_{vac}

Carroll
. Inevitable quantum fluctuations imply that the vacuum energy may originate from the zero-point energy in the vacuum, and this perspective is encouraged by the overall positive sign for the reality of the quantum vacuum
Milonni
. Aside from its profound cosmological implications,
\rho_{vac}(>0)
itself has a peculiar property that it produces a negative pressure. The construction of the energy-momentum tensor of the vacuum could provide an argument for this property
Peebles
. And essentially, the negative pressure is an effect required by the conservation of energy. However, it is of interest to understand the appearance of negative pressure from substructure of the quantum vacuum; be that as it may, it is hard to imagine that such an understanding can be fully achieved at this stage, since we do not yet understand the huge discrepancy between the observational data of the dark energy
Planck
and the prediction of zero-point energy. Therefore, in this work we focus on a simpler but well-defined variant of this negative pressure problem: We shall show that an observable negative pressure in the vacuum could occur in confined spaces for the photon field and that its appearance is a result of the quantum vacuum radiation, which may offer some insights into the quantum origin of the negative pressure.

Before we end this study, a technical note may be given here. In our discussions, the plane wave is assumed to be reflected by the single cross section at the edge, and then possible disturbance to the propagation of the incident field near the overlapping edge is not considered. Is this simplified treatment well-defined? The answer is affirmative. There are two ways to understand this conclusion: 1) From the conventional viewpoint of zero-point energy, Ref.
Zhang
pointed out that the tangential force experienced by the plates in Fig.
1
is irrelevant to the details of the quantum vacuum in the edge regions. This fact suggests that it will not be necessary to complicate our discussion on this point; 2) But a concise proof may also be outlined as follows. Notice that one can always assume an arbitrary curved surface outside (or inside) the space between the plates, which can form a closed surface with the cross section at the edge (including formally the “ends” of the infinite edge along
x
-direction, which is actually an insignificant detail), and near this imaginary surface the propagation of the virtual photons is not disturbed by the edge. Each element of the surface should experience a net, normal momentum flux of the incident field. Then, the incoming flux of momentum through the curved surface is equal to the undisturbed flux through the edge cross section, and it would be simple to realize that this fact should eventually approve our treatment.

Anomaly-induced vacuum effective action with torsion: covariant solution and ambiguities


Abstract
The conformal anomaly in curved spacetime with antisymmetric torsion is reconsidered, taking into account new important details. We formulate, for the first time, the covariant solution of the anomaly-induced effective action. The covariant effective action includes local terms corresponding to total derivatives in the conformal anomaly. The contribution of massless fermions to these terms is characterized by multiplicative anomaly, coming from two different choices of doubling for the spinor operator. On the other hand, the nonlocal part of anomaly-induced action is free of ambiguities and admits a low-energy limit, similar to the effective potential in the metric-scalar theory. Keywords: Effective action, conformal anomaly, torsion, multiplicative anomaly, local terms, ambiguities

,

,
4
,

]
and anomaly-induced

[

]
and four (

)

[

,

]
spacetime dimensions. The reason for this special
interest is related to important applications to black hole physics

[
9
,
10
]
, cosmology
[

,
12
,
13
]
(see
also
[
5
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
for review and further
references), effective

).

From a more general perspective, the anomaly is technically simple
and elegant way for describing loop corrections in the semiclassical
approach. Usually, the trace anomaly is associated with the UV
limit because classical conformal symmetry is typical only in a
massless theory. In other words, the anomaly-induced action is a
direct generalization of the renormalization group improved classical
action based on the Minimal Subtraction scheme of renormalization.
The generalization, in this case, means that the constant rescaling of
the metric (curved-space equivalent of rescaling the momenta

[
23
,
24
,
25
]
) is replaces by the local conformal
transformation with the conformal factor depending on the
coordinates. On the other hand, the anomaly-induced action can be
adapted to describe the low-energy (IR) limit of a massless theory.
The first examples of this sort can be found in
[
26
]
and

[
27
]
, where the anomaly has been used to obtain the
one-loop effective potential of scalar fields in curved spacetime.

Anomaly is directly related to the logarithmic divergences in the
conformal massless theory
[
5
,
15
]
. This is true for a
purely metric background, but also for the theory with extra
background fields, such as scalars, vector fields, and torsion.
One can prove that the divergences satisfy the conformal Noether
identity
[
24
]
and, for this reason, the anomaly is composed
by the following three types of terms
[
4
,
28
]
:

i)
Topological term, such as the Gauss-Bonnet term in

4D
, or its analogs in higher even dimensions. The conformal
transformation of this term greatly simplifies if supplemented by
a specially chosen surface terms. This fundamental feature is the
main basis of integrating anomaly, which was verified in
2D

[
6
]
, in
4D

[
7
,
8
]
and in
6D

[
29
]
.
The general proof for higher dimensions is not known and remains
a conjecture
[
30
]
. Assuming this is correct, the topological
term is the main source of the nonlocal structures in the
anomaly-induced action.

ii)
Legitimate conformal terms (we will call them

C
-terms), such as the square of the Weyl tensor in
4D
; three
possible conformal structures in
6D

[
31
]
, etc. The
integration of these terms is relatively simple assuming the
aforementioned conjecture. The result for the covariant induced
action is nonlocal.

iii)
The total derivative terms in the divergences provide
the same terms in the anomaly. These terms are known to be
ambiguous
[
5
]
and this ambiguity equivalent to adding
a finite local covariant nonconformal term to the classical action,
as discussed in
[
32
,
33
]
for dimensional
and Pauli-Villars regularizations.
Strictly speaking,
i)
- and
iii)
-type terms are not
conformal invariant. However, those are surface terms that satisfy
the conformal Noether identity. For this reason, we shall call them

N
-terms.

Verifying the generality of the described classification may go
in two different ways, namely either
increasing the dimension or trying to enrich space-time geometry.
The first approach is extremely difficult for practical realization
(see, e.g.,
[
29
]
). At the same time, the second possibility
may be related to introducing torsion or nonmetricity of the
spacetime. The present work reports on the verification of the
scheme described above for the theory with torsion. Namely,
we explore the uniqueness of the topological term, the
possibility to construct covariant forms for anomaly-induced
action with torsion, and the ambiguities in the local covariant
terms, responsible for the total derivatives in the anomaly.

The case of the anomaly with torsion has been explored in several
works
[
34
,
35
]
, including for the different
realizations of conformal symmetry
[
36
,
37
]
. However,
the covariant (nonlocal and local) form of the anomaly-induced
effective action of gravity with torsion was never formulated and
the first purpose of the present work is to fill this gap. We shall
see that the integration of all terms of an anomaly in the theory
with torsion can be done in a very standard way, with one
important addition. As we are working with fermions, there is
always a possibility to have an ambiguity related to different
ways of doubling the Dirac operator. Previously, it was shown
that, in case of massive fermions, this ambiguity leads to the
nonlocal multiplicative anomaly
[
38
]
,
something one cannot consistently achieve
[
39
,
40
]

using
\zeta
-regularization
[
41
]
owing to the presence of
renormalization
\mu
-dependence in the local terms in the
effective action. In what follows we show that, in the theory with
torsion, the multiplicative anomaly is possible even in the

massless
case, i.e., in the local terms coming from the
integration of total derivative terms in the trace anomaly. Since
these terms are not renormalized, this new version of multiplicative
anomaly avoids the
\mu
-dependence and the arguments of

[
39
,
40
]
.

2
Conformal fields with torsion

In what follows we shall give only a brief list of necessary formulas
about gravity with torsion and conformal matter fields. The path
integrals over these fields require renormalization of vacuum
action and produce trace (conformal) anomaly. A more detailed
review can be found, e.g., in
[
42
,
37
]
. In the last
reference, the notations are the same as here.

The affine connection without torsion is the Christoffel symbol
(Levi-Civita connection)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\,\beta\gamma}=\big\{^{\,\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\big\}=\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\alpha\lambda}\,\big(\partial_{\beta}\,g_{\lambda\gamma}+\partial_{\gamma}\,g_{\lambda\beta}-\partial_{\lambda}\,g_{\beta\gamma}\big).

(1)

The corresponding covariant derivative satisfies the metricity
condition
\nabla_{\lambda}g_{\alpha\beta}=0
and is free of torsion, i.e.,

\Gamma^{\tau}_{\,\alpha\beta}=\Gamma^{\tau}_{\,\beta\alpha}
. In what follows, we do
not consider the theories with nonmetricity, but include nonzero
torsion, that is making geometry more extensive and, in particular,
links it to the spin of matter fields
[
42
]
.

Torsion tensor is defined as the difference between the two affine
connections which are not assumed symmetric,

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{\,\cdot\,\beta\gamma}=\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\,\beta\gamma}-\tilde{\Gamma}^{\alpha}_{\,\gamma\beta}.

(2)

It is useful to present torsion tensor as a sum of the irreducible
components
[
43
]
(see also
[
37
]
),

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta\mu}=\frac{1}{3}\left(T_{\beta}\,g_{\alpha\mu}-T_{\mu}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\right)-\frac{1}{6}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\,S^{\nu}+q_{\alpha\beta\mu}\,,

(3)

namely the vector
T_{\beta}=T^{\alpha}_{\,\cdot\,\beta\alpha}
, axial vector

S^{\nu}=\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}T_{\alpha\beta\mu}
, and the remaining tensor

\;q^{\alpha}_{\,\cdot\,\beta\gamma}
,
satisfying the conditions
q^{\alpha}_{\;\cdot\,\beta\alpha}=0

and
\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}q_{\alpha\beta\mu}=0
.

One can derive the generalizations of the Riemann tensor, Ricci
tensor and scalar curvature for the connection with torsion, e.g.,

\displaystyle{\tilde{R}}\,=\,R-2\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}-\frac{4}{3}T_{\alpha}T^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}q^{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\frac{1}{24}S^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}\,.

(4)

Since our purpose is to consider the quantum theory of matter
fields, regarding both metric and torsion as external fields, the
parametrization of these background fields is mostly irrelevant.
Thus, in what follows, we shall use the Riemannian version of
the curvature tensors.

Since the interaction of torsion with the gauge fields is forbidden
by the gauge invariance
[
42
]
, we shall assume that gauge
vectors decouple from torsion at the classical level. The same
symmetry protects the theory from these interactions at the
quantum level
[
44
,
37
]
. Thus, we need to consider only
scalar and fermion fields, as described below.

2.1
Scalar field

The action of the real nonminimal scalar field
\phi
in curved
spacetime with torsion has the form

\displaystyle S_{0}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\big\{g^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\beta}\phi+\xi_{i}P_{i}\phi^{2}-m^{2}\phi^{2}\big\},

(5)

where
the nonminimal parameters
\xi_{i}
correspond to the
structures

\displaystyle P_{1}=R,\quad P_{2}=\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha},\quad P_{3}=T_{\alpha}T^{\alpha},\quad P_{4}=q_{\alpha\beta\tau}q^{\alpha\beta\tau},\quad P_{5}=S_{\alpha}S^{\alpha},

(6)

repeating the ones of (
4
) but with arbitrary coefficients

\xi_{i}
.
It is known
[
44
,
43
,
37
]
that in the curved-space
theory with fermions, scalars and Yukawa coupling, arbitrary
nonminimal parameters
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{5}
, of the interaction
of scalar field(s) with
R
and
S_{\alpha}
, are needed to provide
renormalizable semiclassical theory, as will be explained below.
In this article, we will mainly restrict the consideration
by a purely antisymmetric
torsion. The reasons are that
i)
this is the most
relevant part of the matter-torsion interaction, in particular
linking spin to geometry
[
42
]
;

ii)
more general cases are not expected to bring new
details, concerning the aforementioned aspects of the anomaly
and also compared to the previous analysis in
[
37
]
.
Thus, we shall assume that
T_{\mu}=0
and
q_{\alpha\beta\tau}=0
,
such that only the axial vector component in (
3
) is present.

Action (
5
) is invariant under general coordinate
transformations. On top of that, the massless model with
\xi=1/6

is invariant under the transformation called local conformal
symmetry,

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\sigma}\,{\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu},\qquad S_{\alpha}={\bar{S}}_{\alpha},\qquad\phi=e^{-\sigma}\,{\bar{\phi}},

(7)

where
\sigma=\sigma(x)
. Note that the value of

\xi_{5}
does not affect conformal invariance. Also, if we include

T_{\mu}
component, there are three types of the conformal
transformations with torsion. This subject was discussed in detail
in
[
44
,
36
]
and we will not repeat it here.

2.2
Massless Dirac field

The interaction of Dirac spinor field with torsion is described
by the parity-preserving action

\displaystyle S\,=\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,\bar{\psi}\Big\{i\gamma^{\mu}\big(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma^{5}S_{\mu}-i\eta_{2}T_{\mu}\big)-m\Big\}\psi

(8)

with the nonminimal parameters
\eta
and
\eta_{2}
. It is important
that the minimal coupling of fermion with torsion correspond to

\eta=1/8
and
\eta_{2}=0

[
42
]
. This feature explains the
difference between interaction of torsion with
S_{\mu}

and
T_{\mu}
. Starting from the minimal actions, there is no
T_{\mu}

and
q_{\alpha\beta\mu}
, and the corresponding interactions never
emerge in the divergences. On the contrary,
S_{\mu}
is always present
in both classical theory and in the divergences. As a result, one has
to renormalize the parameters
\eta
and also
\xi_{5}
, in case the
theory includes Yukawa interactions between scalars and fermions.
Thus, one cannot have a renormalizable theory based on the minimal
coupling to the external torsion.

In the massless case, the theory (
8
) possesses three different
symmetries. One of those is the usual Abelian gauge symmetry related
to
T_{\mu}
. In fact, one can trade
\eta_{2}T_{\mu}
by
eA_{\mu}
and,
in the part of the gauge symmetry, reduce the problem to the usual
gauge field. Since we are interested in the
T_{\mu}=0
case, the
corresponding transformation will not be considered. Another
symmetry is

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\,\longrightarrow\,\bar{\psi}e^{i\eta\alpha\gamma^{5}},\qquad\psi\,\longrightarrow\,e^{i\eta\alpha\gamma^{5}}\psi,\qquad S_{\mu}\,\longrightarrow\,S_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\alpha,

(9)

where
\alpha=\alpha(x)
is a scalar transformation parameter.
Finally, there is a conformal transformation of the spinor field,
supplementing the one of (
7
),

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\sigma}\,{\bar{g}}_{\mu\nu},\qquad S_{\alpha}={\bar{S}}_{\alpha},\qquad\bar{\psi}=\bar{\psi}e^{-\frac{3}{2}\,\sigma},\qquad\psi=\psi e^{-\frac{3}{2}\,\sigma}.

(10)

Let us mention that the values of torsion nonminimal parameters,

\eta
and
\xi_{5}
, do not affect the conformal invariance.

The rest of this work is devoted to the anomaly in the local
conformal symmetry (
7
), (
10
) in the
vacuum part of the one-loop effective action
\Gamma^{(1)}(g,S)
.

According to the general proof
[
24
]
, if the classical
actions of quantum matter fields have local conformal symmetry
(
7
) and (
10
), the divergent part

\Gamma_{div}^{(1)}(g,S)
satisfies the corresponding Noether identity,

\displaystyle-\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{\delta\Gamma_{div}^{(1)}(g,S)}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\,=\,\Phi(g,S).

(11)

Here
\Phi(g,S)
is a covariant finite expression, that is also local
owing to the Weinberg’s theorem. We note that Eq. (
11
)
does not include the variational derivative with respect to
S_{\mu}

because, according to (
7
), its conformal weight is zero.

The possible vacuum divergences obey the mentioned
symmetries. The Riemannian terms include the square of the
Weyl tensor in
4D
,

\displaystyle C^{2}\,=\,R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}R^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}-2R_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}+\frac{1}{3}R^{2},

(12)

the integrand of the Gauss-Bonnet topological term

\displaystyle E_{4}\,=\,R_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}R^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}-4R_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}+R^{2},

(13)

and the surface term
\cx R
. The important difference between
these terms is that, in
4D
, the integral of
C^{2}
is invariant,

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\,C^{2}\,=\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\bar{C}^{2}.

(14)

We shall call the actions satisfying this condition

\mathcal{C}
-terms. On
the contrary, the integrals
\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}E_{4}
and

\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\cx R
do not possess this property and, strictly
speaking, are not conformal invariant. At the same time, both terms
satisfy the conformal Noether identity. All such actions, that are not
really conformal, but obey the rule

\displaystyle-\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,g_{\alpha\beta}\,\frac{\delta S(g,\,S}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\,=\,0,

(15)

will be called
\mathcal{N}
-terms.

Besides the mentioned metric-dependent integrals, there are
torsion-dependent
\mathcal{C}
- and
\mathcal{N}
-terms. In the first group, there
are two new candidates
[
57
]

\displaystyle S^{4}\,=\,(S^{2})^{2}\,=\,\big(S_{\mu}S^{\mu}\big)^{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,=\,g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}S_{\mu\nu}S_{\alpha\beta},

(16)

where
\,S_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}S_{\mu}
.
One can ask the following questions:
i)
Whether there are
torsion-dependent analogs of (or alternatives to) the Riemannian
topological term
E_{4}
?
ii)
Are there torsion-dependent

\mathcal{N}
-terms, including total derivatives, similar to
\cx R
, and
iii)
Whether the renormalization of these torsion-dependent

\mathcal{N}
-terms has ambiguities (see
[
5
]
and

[
32
]
) which are present in the cases of

\cx R
and, also, for a non-zero background scalar field, in the

\cx\phi^{2}
-term
[
33
,
27
]
. We shall address these
questions by making direct calculations of divergences, anomaly
and anomaly-induced action of gravity with torsion.

3
Derivation of one-loop divergences

For the sake of generality, we perform calculations for massive
versions of scalar and spinor fields and for an arbitrary
\xi_{1}
.
We can set masses to zero and
\xi_{1}=1/6
at the end.

Let us start by quoting the known result for the scalar field

[
37
]

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{div,\,scal}\,=\,-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\,\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[\frac{1}{120}C^{2}-\frac{1}{360}E_{4}+\frac{1}{180}\cx R+\frac{1}{6}\cx P+\frac{1}{2}\,P^{2}\Big],\qquad\qquad

(17)

where

\displaystyle P=\Big(\frac{1}{6}-\xi_{1}\Big)R-\xi_{5}S^{2}+m^{2}.

(18)

In the massless limit and assuming
\xi_{1}=1/6
, we meet the first

\mathcal{C}
-term from (
16
) and the
\mathcal{N}
-terms
i.e.,
E_{4}
,
\cx R
, and
\cx S^{2}
.

Consider the fermion contribution.
In this part, we go into the detail of the calculation,
regardless they can be partially found in
[
37
]
. Our
purpose is to evaluate
-i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}
, where

\displaystyle{\hat{H}}\,=\,i\gamma^{\mu}\big(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma^{5}S_{\mu}\big)-m.

(19)

In order to perform this calculation, we have to multiply (
19
)
by a conjugate operator, such that the product belongs to the
standard class of minimal operators

{\hat{F}}={\hat{\cx}}+2{\hat{h}}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}+{\hat{\Pi}}
, admitting
application of the Schwinger-DeWitt technique
[
46
,
47
]
.
It seems that there should be many possible choices for such a
conjugate operator, but there are two constraints. First of all,
the contribution of the conjugate operator should be calculable.
And, on the other hand, we have to respect the chiral symmetry
(
9
), as otherwise the result may be wrong. The last means
the structure
\gamma^{\mu}\big(\nabla_{\mu}-i\eta\gamma^{5}S_{\mu}\big)
has to
be part of the conjugate operator or, alternatively, the conjugate
operator must be
S_{\mu}
-independent. In what follows, we consider
both these options and explore the difference.

3.1
First calculation of fermion contributions

As a first option, consider the conjugate operator of the
form

\displaystyle{\hat{H}}_{1}\,=\,i\gamma^{\nu}\big(\nabla_{\nu}-i\eta\gamma^{5}S_{\nu}\big)+m.

(20)

It is known that the change of the sign of the mass does not
change the result (see, e.g.,
[
48
]
), such that

\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}=\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}_{1}
and we can use
the relations

\displaystyle-i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}\,=\,-\frac{i}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\big({\hat{H}}{\hat{H}}_{1}\big)\,=\,-\frac{i}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\big({\hat{\cx}}+2{\hat{h}}_{1}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}+{\hat{\Pi}}_{1}\big).

(21)

After some algebra, we get

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{1}^{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{i}{2}\,\gamma^{5}\big(\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\alpha}-\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\lambda}\big)S_{\lambda}\,,

\displaystyle{\hat{\Pi}}_{1}\,=\,m^{2}-\frac{1}{4}R+S^{2}-i\gamma^{5}(\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha})-\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}S_{\alpha\beta}.

(22)

In these and subsequent formulas we made a rescaling of the external
torsion field
\eta S_{\mu}\to S_{\mu}
, making formulas more compact.

The elements of the Schwinger-DeWitt technique are

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{1}\,=\,{\hat{\Pi}}_{1}+\frac{{\hat{1}}}{6}R-\nabla_{\alpha}{\hat{h}}_{1}^{\alpha}-{\hat{h}}_{1\alpha}{\hat{h}}_{1}^{\alpha}\,=\,m^{2}-\frac{1}{12}R-2S^{2}-i\gamma^{5}(\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha})

(23)

and

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{1,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,\big[\nabla_{\beta},\,\nabla_{\alpha}\big]+\nabla_{\beta}{\hat{h}}_{1\alpha}-\nabla_{\alpha}{\hat{h}}_{1\beta}+{\hat{h}}_{1\beta}{\hat{h}}_{1\alpha}-{\hat{h}}_{1\alpha}{\hat{h}}_{1\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{4}R_{\alpha\beta\lambda\tau}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\tau}-S^{2}\big(\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}-\gamma_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha}\big)-2S^{\lambda}\big(S_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}-S_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha}\big)\gamma_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{5}\Big[\big(\nabla_{\beta}S^{\lambda}\big)\big(\gamma_{\lambda}\gamma_{\alpha}-\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\lambda}\big)-\big(\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\lambda}\big)\big(\gamma_{\lambda}\gamma_{\beta}-\gamma_{\beta}\gamma_{\lambda}\big)\Big].

(24)

The general expression for the one-loop divergences is

[
46
]

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{div}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\,\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}\,\,\mbox{tr}\,\bigg[\frac{{\hat{1}}}{180}\big(R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}-R_{\alpha\beta}^{2}+\cx R\big)

(25)

\displaystyle\quad+\,\,\frac{1}{2}{\hat{P}}^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\,\cx{\hat{P}}\bigg],\qquad\qquad

where the trace and sign correspond to bosonic fields and
for the fermions the sign should be inverted. In the present case,

{\hat{P}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\mu\nu}
are defined by
(
23
) and (
24
). The calculation is pretty much
standard, but we quote simple relation for (
16
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,=\,(\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu})^{2}-\big(\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu}\big)^{2}+R_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}+2\nabla_{\nu}\big(S^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu}-S^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}S^{\nu}\big),

(26)

which proves useful, also, for integrating anomaly. Here

(\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu})^{2}=\big(\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu}\big)\big(\nabla^{\mu}S^{\nu}\big)
.

Finally, for the divergences we obtain the expression (with
recovered
\eta
)

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{div,\,fer,\,1}\,=\,-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\,\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[\frac{m^{2}}{3}R+8m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}-2m^{4}+\frac{1}{20}C^{2}-\frac{11}{360}E_{4}+\frac{1}{30}\cx R

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\,\frac{2}{3}\eta^{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}+\frac{4}{3}\eta^{2}\cx S^{2}-\frac{4}{3}\eta^{2}\nabla_{\beta}\big(S^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\beta}-S^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}\big)-\frac{1}{3}\eta\,\nabla_{\beta}B^{\beta}\Big].\qquad\qquad

(27)

There are several remarkable aspects in this formula. In the
massless theory and in the limit
n\to 4
, the integrand is
conformal invariant, that is, composed by the
\mathcal{C}
-type and

\mathcal{N}
-type invariants
[
37
]
. In the expression for
divergences, one can identify three torsion-dependent total
derivatives. In our opinion, one of them is especially interesting,
albeit it turns out trivial (hence it was ignored in
[
37
]
).
The last term in (
27
) depends on the vector field

\displaystyle B^{\nu}=R^{\nu}_{\,\cdot\,\mu\tau\lambda}\varepsilon^{\tau\lambda\alpha\mu}S_{\alpha}=C^{\nu}_{\,\cdot\,\mu\tau\lambda}\varepsilon^{\tau\lambda\alpha\mu}S_{\alpha}.

(28)

Under the conformal transformation (
7
), this vector
has a non-conventional transformation rule

B^{\nu}={\bar{B}}^{\nu}e^{-4\sigma}
and, as a result,
\nabla_{\nu}B^{\nu}

is a curious example of the
\mathcal{C}
-type total derivative
invariant. Moreover, anticipating this part, the integration of the
corresponding total derivative term of the anomaly surprisingly
produces a nonlocal term in the effective action.
Unfortunately, the bright career of this term ends early, because

B^{\nu}
can be shown to vanish as a result of cyclic identity for a
Riemann (or Weyl) tensor. Anyway, it is remarkable that the general
rule of having nonlocal term in the action for a
\mathcal{C}
-type
invariant in the anomaly holds in this case.

3.2
Second calculation of fermion contribution

The second scheme of doubling the fermion operator (
19
) uses
the torsion-independent conjugate operator

\displaystyle{\hat{H}}_{2}\,=\,i\gamma^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}+m.

(29)

In this case, one has to use the formula (
21
) for the
torsion-independent terms, which are certainly the same as in
(
27
). However, for the
S_{\mu}
-dependent terms,

\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}_{2}
is irrelevant and we have to use the modified
rule

\displaystyle-i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,{\hat{H}}\,=\,-i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\big({\hat{H}}{\hat{H}}_{2}\big)\,=\,-i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\big({\hat{\cx}}+2{\hat{h}}_{2}^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}+{\hat{\Pi}}_{2}\big).

(30)

The elements of the operator, in this case, are

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{2}^{\alpha}\,=\,\frac{i}{2}\,\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\alpha}S_{\lambda},

\displaystyle{\hat{\Pi}}_{2}\,=\,m^{2}-\frac{1}{4}R+m\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\lambda}S_{\lambda}.

(31)

The elements of Schwinger-DeWitt technique are
also different,

\displaystyle{\hat{P}}_{2}\,=\,m^{2}-\frac{1}{12}R-\frac{1}{2}S^{2}+m\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\alpha}S_{\alpha}-\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{5}(\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha})+\frac{i}{4}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\alpha}\gamma^{\beta}S_{\alpha\beta},

(32)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{2,\,\alpha\beta}\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{4}R_{\alpha\beta\lambda\tau}\gamma^{\lambda}\gamma^{\tau}-\frac{1}{4}S^{2}\big(\gamma_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}-\gamma_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha}\big)-\frac{1}{2}S^{\lambda}\gamma_{\lambda}\big(S_{\beta}\gamma_{\alpha}-S_{\alpha}\gamma_{\beta}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\frac{i}{2}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\lambda}\Big[\gamma_{\alpha}\big(\nabla_{\beta}S_{\lambda}\big)-\gamma_{\beta}\big(\nabla_{\alpha}S_{\lambda}\big)\Big].

(33)

Let us write only the
S_{\mu}
-dependent divergences, which are
obtained via (
30
) and (
25
),

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{div,\,fer,\,2}\,=\,-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\,\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}\,\Big[8m^{2}\eta^{2}S^{2}\,-\,\frac{2}{3}\eta^{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+\,\frac{2}{3}\eta^{2}\cx S^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\eta^{2}\nabla_{\beta}\big(S^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}-S^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\beta}\big)-\frac{1}{3}\eta\,\nabla_{\beta}B^{\beta}\Big].\qquad\qquad

(34)

Compared to (
27
), the non-surface terms are the same.
However, the total derivative,
\mathcal{N}
-terms, have different
coefficients. This result represents the new kind of multiplicative
anomaly, being qualitatively different from the previously known
examples (starting from
[
38
]
) concerning the
nonlocal part of the one-loop effective action. In these examples
the multiplicative anomaly shows up only for the massive fields and,
on the other hand, it cannot be compensated by the change of
renormalization condition because the last concerns only the
local terms. In the present case, the difference cannot be
compensated by the change of renormalization conditions for
the irrelevant surface integrals because such change given only
finite differences. As we will see in the next section, the finite
difference shows up in the local terms which are
not

total derivatives.

3.3
Action of torsion and UV logarithmic corrections

One of the important outputs of the one-loop calculations for
scalars and fermions is that, in the semiclassical conformal theory
with antisymmetric torsion, the classical action of torsion has the
form
[
57
,
37
]

\displaystyle S_{tors}\,=\,\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big\{\,-a_{1}S^{4}-\,\frac{a_{2}}{4}\,S_{\mu\nu}^{2}+b_{1}\nabla_{\beta}\big(S^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\beta}-S^{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}S^{\alpha}\big)+b_{2}\cx S^{2}\Big\},

(35)

where
a_{1,2}>0
and
b_{1,2}
are arbitrary parameters. The
positiveness of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
provides the tree-level potential
of
S_{\mu}
bounded from below (as will be discussed in the
next sections) and the positiveness of energy for propagating torsion

[
56
]
(see also
[
37
]
).

It may look natural to set

a_{2}=1

[
56
]
, that can be provided by rescaling

S_{\mu}
and
\eta
. However, it is sometimes useful to keep

a_{2}
arbitrary, as we shall see in what follows. From the
viewpoint of conformal symmetry,
a_{1,2}
-structures
represent
\mathcal{C}
-terms and the values of those parameters can
be defined only from the measurements, which in the case of
a_{2}

can be traded to the measurement of
\eta S_{\mu}
. At the same
time, the coefficients of the
\mathcal{N}
-terms
b_{1,2}
do not
affect equations of motion and are artificial parameters that cannot
be measured. Still, these terms are necessary for renormalizability
of a semiclassical theory.

Let us evaluate loop corrections to the vacuum action
(
35
). As a first step in this direction, we can
recover the leading one-loop logarithms in the most relevant

\mathcal{C}
-terms. Using the standard considerations
[
47
]

(see also
[
15
]
for more details), we arrive at the one-loop
corrected torsion sector of the theory

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}_{\,tors}\,=\,-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg\{S^{2}\Big[a_{1}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{\cx\,}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]S^{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{4}\,S_{\mu\nu}\Big[a_{2}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{\cx\,}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]\,S^{\mu\nu}\Big\},

(36)

From (
17
) and (
27
) [see also
subsequent Eq. (
34
)], we can easily get

\displaystyle\beta_{1}\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}\xi^{2}_{5,\,i},

\displaystyle\beta_{2}\,=\,\frac{8}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\eta^{2}_{k}.

(37)

Here
\xi_{5,\,i}
and
\eta_{k}
are nonminimal parameters for
different species of scalar and spinor fields. According to the
analysis of renormalization in interacting theories
[
44
]
,
these parameters may be different for different fields. Independent
on this, the signs of the beta functions show that the sign of

\beta_{1}
indicated the asymptotic freedom in the parameter
a_{1}

and the sign of
\beta_{2}
is positive, as it is typical for the
Abelian vector models. It is worth mentioning that these signs
correspond to the fermion and scalar contributions only, while
the contribution of the proper field
S_{\mu}
was not taken into
account.

The integration of anomaly is, to a great extent, an elegant and
useful way to work with formula (
36
) by constructing
a local version of renormalization group.
After deriving the covariant form of anomaly-induced action, we
use the duality of the UV and IR limits in the massless theory
and construct the low-energy alternative to (
36
).

4
Integration of anomaly with torsion

The one-loop

The beta functions

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}^{(1)}\,=\,-\frac{4}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\eta^{2}_{k},\qquad\gamma_{1}^{(2)}\,=\,\frac{1}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\eta^{2}_{k},

(46)

\displaystyle\gamma_{2}^{(1)}\,=\,\frac{4}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N_{f}}\eta^{2}_{k}\,-\,\frac{1}{6(4\pi)^{2}}\,\sum_{i=1}^{N_{s}}\xi_{5,\,k},

(47)

Let us note that the scalar contributions to
\gamma_{2}
in
(

), coming from (

of the one described in
[

]

[

In the rest of this section, we describe the solution of the equation

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{\delta\Gamma_{ind}}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\,=\,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{-\bar{g}}}\,e^{-4\sigma}\,\frac{\delta\Gamma_{ind}}{\delta\sigma}\bigg|\,\,=\,\,\langle T^{\mu}_{\,\,\mu}\rangle.

(48)

The first equation here is an identity which uses
\sigma
, i.e., the
conformal factor of the metric defined in (
7
).
Also,
\big|
means the procedure of replacing

\big(\bar{g}_{\mu\nu},\,\bar{S_{\mu}}\big)\rightarrow\big(g_{\mu\nu},\,S_{\mu}\big)
and
\sigma\rightarrow 0
.

The
4D
solution for a purely gravitational case was found in

[
7
,
8
]
. The generalization for a theory with torsion has been
found
[
35
,
36
]
, but only in the noncovariant
formulation as a functional of
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
,
\bar{S_{\mu}}

and
\sigma
. In what follows, we shall construct the most informative,

working for an arbitrary even dimension
[
29
]
. Thus, we need
just to give a practical realization of this scheme for the theory
with torsion.

The conformal invariants in (
38
) can be denoted in
a common way as

\displaystyle Y\,=\,Y(g,S)\,=\,wC^{2}-\beta_{1}S^{4}-\frac{1}{4}\beta_{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}.

(49)

The unique topological term
E_{4}
has the remarkable conformal
property

\displaystyle\sqrt{-g}\,\Big(E_{4}-\frac{2}{3}\,{\cx}R\Big)\,=\,\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\,\Big({\bar{E}_{4}}-\frac{2}{3}\,{\bar{\cx}}{\bar{R}}+4{\bar{\Delta}_{4}}\sigma\Big),

(50)

where
\Delta_{4}=\cx^{2}+2R^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-\dfrac{2}{3}R\square+\dfrac{1}{3}(\nabla^{\mu}R)\nabla_{\mu}
, which obeys

\sqrt{-g}\Delta_{4}=\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{\Delta}_{4}

[
49
,
50
]
.

These notations and features do not depend on the presence of torsion
and, therefore, we can directly write down the nonlocal part of the
solution of (
48
),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind,\,nonloc}\,=\,\frac{b}{8}\int_{x}\int_{y}\Big(E_{4}-\frac{2}{3}\square R\Big)_{\hskip-2.84526ptx}G(x,y)\Big(E_{4}-\frac{2}{3}\square R\Big)_{\hskip-2.84526pty}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}\int_{y}Y(x)\,G(x,y)\Big(E_{4}-\frac{2}{3}\square R\Big)_{\hskip-2.84526pty},

(51)

where we used the notation
\int_{x}\equiv\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g(x)}

and the Green function of the Paneitz operator

\displaystyle(\sqrt{-g}\Delta_{4})_{x}G(x,y)\,=\,\delta(x,y).

(52)

Let us find a solution for the
total derivative terms. For the
\cx R
the result is well-known,

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\int_{x}R^{2}\,=\,12\square R.

(53)

and for the
\cx S^{2}
the answer can be easily found by direct
calculation,

\displaystyle-\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\int_{x}RS^{2}\,=\,6\square S^{2}.

(54)

Thus, the remaining problem is to integrate the
\gamma_{1}
term in
(
38
). Let us use the hypothesis that, as in all previously
known cases, the solution for the total derivative should be a local
covariant action. Then we have the following candidate terms:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind,\,local}\,=\,\int_{x}\big\{\alpha_{1}(\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu})^{2}\,+\,\alpha_{2}(\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu})^{2}\,+\,\alpha_{3}RS^{2}\big\},

(55)

where the last one is already worked out in (
54
). We can
rewrite the

of this formula using

\cx S^{2}=2\nabla_{\nu}(S_{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}S^{\mu})

26

functional
\int_{x}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\int_{x}(\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu})^{2}\,=\,4\nabla_{\nu}(S^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu}),

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}g_{\mu\nu}\frac{\delta}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\int_{x}(\nabla_{\nu}S_{\mu})^{2}\,=\,2\nabla_{\nu}\big[S^{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}S^{\mu}-S_{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}S^{\mu}-S_{\mu}\nabla^{\mu}S^{\nu}\big].

(56)

Using (
56
) together with the modified version of
(
54
), replacing the result into the linear combination of
(

the solution for
\alpha_{1,2,3}

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=0,\qquad\alpha_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\,\gamma_{1},\qquad\alpha_{3}=\frac{1}{12}(\gamma_{1}-2\gamma_{2}).

(57)

Taking into account relations (
53
) and (

the local part of the covariant induced action has the form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind,\,loc}\,=\,-\,\frac{3c+2b}{36}\int_{x}R^{2}\,\,+\,\int_{x}\Big\{\frac{\gamma_{1}}{2}(\nabla_{\mu}S_{\nu})^{2}\,+\,\frac{\gamma_{1}-2\gamma_{2}}{12}\,RS^{2}\Big\}.

(58)

Just to complete the story, we mention that this expression may
be modified by using the relations (
12
) and (
13
)
in the purely metric part and (
26
) in the torsion-metric
part. This means, one can use the replacement

or

(

It is worth mentioning another detail concerning fermion

All in all, the general covariant solution for the anomaly-induced

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind}\,=\,S_{c}(g,S)\,+\,\Gamma_{ind,\,nonloc}\,+\,\Gamma_{ind,\,loc}\,,

(59)

where

\varphi

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ind}\,=\,S_{c}(g,S)\,+\,\Gamma_{ind,\,loc}\,+\,\int_{x}\bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\varphi\Delta_{4}\varphi-\frac{1}{2}\psi\Delta_{4}\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\,\,\frac{\sqrt{-b}}{2}\varphi\Big(E_{4}-\frac{2}{3}\square R+\frac{1}{b}Y\Big)\,+\,\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-b}}\,\psi Y\bigg\},

5
Anomaly-induced effective action in the IR

In the recent works
[
53
]
and
[
54
]
it was shown that
dynamical torsion may be used to construct phenomenologically
successful models of dark matter (DM). On the other hand, there is
a general statement that the
consistency of quantum field theory of the propagating torsion
requires a large torsion mass
[
56
,
55
]
, something that
can be in contradiction to the DM applications. In this respect, it
looks interesting to explore the possibility of dynamical symmetry
breaking in the torsion sector. In scalar field theory, this is one
of the ways have a large mass in the IR and, at the same time,
leave some space for the applications in the high energy physics,
including to early Universe.

In the scalar case, the analysis of symmetry breaking in initially
massless theory requires the effective potential
[
58
]
,
that can be also derived in curved spacetime
[
57
,
15
]
.
We shall follow
[
27
]
, where the scalar potential was
obtained in the IR limit of the anomaly-induced action,
i.e., the scalar analog of (
59
). We shall concentrate
only on the nonlocal part of this action because the local part is
ambiguous.

Let us define the meaning of the low-energy (IR) limit in the
massless conformal theory, with
\xi_{1}=1/6
. The main assumption
is that torsion terms in (
49
) dominate over the square of the
Weyl tensor. This may be a reasonable approximation in the early
Universe because Weyl tensor vanishes for the homogeneous and
isotropic metric and shows up only because of the metric
perturbations. On the other hand, one can assume that torsion plays
an important role in the formation of DM and hence should be a
strong field
[
54
]
. Thus,

\displaystyle\big|S^{4}\big|\gg\big|C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}\big|\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\big|S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\big|\gg\big|C_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}^{2}\big|.

(61)

As usual in general relativity, the IR limit implies a weak
gravitational field. The weak gravity can be described by a small
metric perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}
, that
means, e.g.,
|\cx R|\gg|R^{2}_{\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp\ldotp}|
for all
curvature tensors (e.g., Weyl, Ricci tensors, and
R
).

In this approximation, the Green function (
52
)
reduces to

\displaystyle G\,=\,\Delta^{-1}_{4}\,=\,\Big(\square^{2}+2R^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}-\dfrac{2}{3}R\square+\dfrac{1}{3}R^{;\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\Big)^{-1}\,\approx\,\frac{1}{\square^{2}}.

(62)

Thus, the nonlocal, torsion-dependent part of
the effective action (
51
) boils down to

\displaystyle\Gamma^{IR}_{ind,\,nonloc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\int_{x}\int_{y}\Big(\beta_{1}S^{4}+\frac{1}{4}\beta_{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\Big)_{x}\Big(\frac{1}{\cx^{2}}\Big)_{x,y}\,\Big(\cx R\Big)_{\hskip-2.84526pty}

(63)

\displaystyle=\,\,\frac{1}{6}\int_{x}\int_{y}\Big(\beta_{1}S^{4}+\frac{1}{4}\beta_{2}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\Big)_{x}\Big(\frac{1}{\cx}\Big)_{x,y}\,R(y).

On top of this expression, the IR limit of the induced effective
action includes
\mathcal{O}(R^{2}_{...})
-terms, but those were
discussed in
[
27
]
and we can refer the interested reader
to this work.

In the presence of torsion, the terms
S^{4}\cx^{-1}R
and

S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\cx^{-1}R
have the same
global

scaling as the respective classical terms
S^{4}
and

S_{\mu\nu}^{2}
, i.e., they are invariant
under the transformation (
7
) with
\sigma\to\lambda=const
.
Indeed, this is the usual feature of the nonlocal induced action,
independent on extra fields and even spacetime dimension

[
29
]
, but it is quite remarkable that this feature holds in the
IR limit, just as in the scalar case
[
27
]
.

The next step is to derive the low-energy effective action of
torsion from (
63
). To this end, we separate the
conformal factor of the metric and use the analogy with the
renormalization group - based derivation of effective action

[
59
,
57
]
. At one loop, it is sufficient to account
only for the linear in
\sigma
terms. Thus, we consider

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}e^{2\sigma},\qquad\sqrt{-g}S^{4}=\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{S}^{4},\qquad\sqrt{-g}S_{\mu\nu}^{2}=\sqrt{-\bar{g}}\bar{S}_{\mu\nu}^{2},

\displaystyle{\cx}^{-1}\,=\,e^{2\sigma}\bar{\cx}^{-1},\qquad R\,=\,e^{-2\sigma}\big[\bar{R}-6\bar{\cx}\sigma\big],

(64)

where
\bar{\cx}=\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}
.
In this framework, (
63
) becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma^{IR}_{ind,\,nonloc}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x}\Big(\beta_{1}\bar{S}^{4}-\frac{1}{4}\beta_{2}\bar{S}_{\mu\nu}^{2}\Big)_{x}\sigma(x).

(65)

This result demonstrates, as we expected, that the anomaly-induced
action is a local version of the renormalization group corrected
classical action (
35
), that means the substitution

\displaystyle a_{1}\longrightarrow a_{1}+\beta_{1}\sigma(x),\qquad a_{2}\longrightarrow a_{2}+\beta_{2}\sigma(x).

(66)

Compared to the usual curved-space renormalization group

[
24
,
15
]
, the constant scaling parameter
\lambda

is traded for the coordinate-dependent conformal factor of the
metric
\sigma
, i.e., we arrive at the local form of renormalization
group in curved space
[
14
]
.

At this point, one can use (
66
) to recover the
low-energy effective action. This requires identification of the
scale parameter
\sigma
and we have several choices because of
the scaling rules

\displaystyle S^{2}=\bar{S}^{2}e^{-2\sigma},\qquad S^{4}=\bar{S}^{4}e^{-4\sigma},\qquad S_{\mu\nu}^{2}=\bar{S}_{\mu\nu}^{2}e^{-4\sigma}.

(67)

E.g., choosing the first option, we arrive at the identification

\sigma\to\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\frac{S^{2}}{\mu^{2}}
. Then, the
improvement (
66
) of the action (
35
)
gives, in the torsion-dependent sector,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{tors}\,=\,-\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\bigg\{\Big[a_{1}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{S^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]S^{4}\,+\,\frac{1}{4}\Big[a_{2}+\frac{\beta_{2}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{S^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]S_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,+\,...\bigg\},

(68)

where we omitted surface terms.
An obvious observation here is that (
68
) is not just an
integral of the effective potential since there is a kinetic term

S_{\mu\nu}^{2}
. Thus, the result can be seen as a form of the
one-loop effective action in the IR limit.

The effective potential part of (
68
) has the form

\displaystyle V_{eff}\,=\,\Big[a_{1}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{2}\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big(\frac{S^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\Big)\Big]S^{4},

(69)

together with the negative
\beta_{1}
-function (
37
) shows
that the one-loop potential always becomes unstable for large
values of
S^{2}
, where the quantum corrections start to dominate
over the classical coefficient
a_{1}
. The coefficients
\eta
for
fermions are experimentally bounded by very small values, at least
for electrons (one can use
[
37
]
as a starting point for further
references on the subject). Thus, according to (
37
), the
strong effect of the negative
\beta_{1}
may be expected only for
extremely large values of
S^{2}
. Anyway, at the one-loop level
the effective potential is unbounded from below.

This feature does not mean that the theory, in general, is badly
defined at the quantum level because the second and higher loop
contributions may restore the positive definiteness of the potential.
On the other hand, assuming the change of sign of
\beta_{1}
at
higher loops, we can rewrite the effective potential part of
(
68
) in terms of the dimensionless parameter

z=S^{2}/\mu^{2}

\displaystyle V_{eff}\,=\,\mu^{4}v(z)\,=\,a_{1}\mu^{4}\big[z^{2}\big(1+\tilde{\beta}\,\mbox{ln}\,z\big)\big],\qquad\tilde{\beta}=\frac{\beta_{1}}{2a_{1}}\,.

(70)

v(z)
for
\beta_{1}>0
is
shown in Fig.

. However, since the real sign of the
beta function is negative, the implementation of the dynamical
symmetry breaking in this theory requires further investigation
and, especially, higher loops contributions to the potential.

6
Conclusions and discussions

We calculated the vacuum divergences and formulated, for the
first time, the covariant version of the anomaly-induced effective
action in curved spacetime with torsion. The output can be presented
in the covariant nonlocal form (
59
) or in the local form
with auxiliary scalars (
60
). The main novelty is
the detection of the multiplicative anomaly in the total derivative
part of the divergences, i.e., (
27
) vs (
34
)
and the corresponding ambiguity
in the local part of the induced effective action. The ambiguity
cannot be removed by the change of renormalization condition and
represents a new feature of massless fermionic determinants that
does not take place without torsion.

Multiplicative anomaly appears in the finite local part of induced
effective action, as shown in expression (
58
). All the terms
in this action are ambiguous. The coefficient
c
may be modified
by adding the finite
\int_{x}R^{2}
term to the classical action or,
equivalently, by the choice of the divergent Weyl-squared
counterterm
[
32
]
. On the
other hand, the ambiguity in the torsion-dependent terms can be
compensated by adding the local nonconformal terms similar to
those in (
58
), to the classical action (
35
).

Another new result of our work is the covariant expression for
the low-energy limit of the anomaly-induced effective action
(
68
). This part may be eventually useful for describing
dynamical symmetry breaking in torsion theories but, independent
on that, we have an interesting analogy with the scalar effective
potential in the axial vector model. On the other hand,
the low-energy effective action (
68
) by itself may
serve as an evidence of breaking local conformal symmetry
by quantum corrections. In this sense, it is an analog of the
effective potential of a scalar field
\phi
in the conformal theory,
where the
\phi^{4}\,\mbox{ln}\,(\phi/\mu)
-term breaks the symmetry of the
classical
\phi^{4}
-type potential. It looks remarkable that we can
obtain this low-energy breaking with torsion from the
anomaly-induced effective action (
59
).

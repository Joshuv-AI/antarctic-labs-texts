One-element Extensions of Hyperplane Arrangements
Hang Cai, Houshan Fu, Suijie Wang

Abstract
We classify one-element extensions of a hyperplane arrangement by the induced adjoint arrangement. Based on the classification, several kinds of combinatorial invariants including Whitney polynomials, characteristic polynomials, Whitney numbers and face numbers, are constants on those strata associated with the induced adjoint arrangement, and also order-preserving with respect to the intersection lattice of the induced adjoint arrangement. As a byproduct, we obtain a convolution formula on the characteristic polynomials χ ⁡ ( 𝒜 + H 𝜶 , a , t ) \chi(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a},t) when 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is defined over a finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} or a rational arrangement. Keywords: Hyperplane arrangement; Whitney polynomial; Whitney number; No broken circuit MSC classes: 52C35.

Introduction

1.1. Main purposes and background.

The starting point of this paper is coming from Fu-Wang’s work in
[
7
]
, which has characterized the classification of the linear one-element extensions of a linear arrangement by the adjoint arrangement. We shall extend their work to general hyperplane arrangement. Our main goal is to classify intersection semi-lattices of one-element extensions of a hyperplane arrangement, and the related combinatorial invariants involving in Whitney polynomials, characteristic polynomials, Whitney numbers and face numbers. According to the classification, we further obtain that these combinatorial invariants are order-preserving with respect to the intersection lattice of the induced adjoint arrangement, and a convolution formula on the characteristic polynomials.

For this purpose, we shall fix our notation and introduce the definition of one-element extension of hyperplane arrangement. Throughout this paper, we use
[m]
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{F}
to denote the set of
\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, the set of integers, the finite field with
q
elements, the field of rational numbers, the field of real numbers and a general field respectively. For any vectors
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{F}^{d},(\bm{\alpha},a)\in\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
, the notations
H_{\bm{\alpha}}
and
H_{\bm{\alpha},a}
always refer to the linear hyperplane
H_{\bm{\alpha}}:\bm{\alpha}\cdot\bm{x}=0
and the affine hyperplane
H_{\bm{\alpha},a}:\bm{\alpha}\cdot\bm{x}=a
in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
respectively. In particular,
H_{\bm{\alpha}}=H_{\bm{\alpha},0}
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement in the
d
-dimensional vector space
\mathbb{F}^{d}
. The hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a}:=\mathcal{A}\cup\{H_{\bm{\alpha},a}\}

in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
is called a

of

. In particular, the hyperplane arrangement

is indeed an

in 1965, which gives a characterization of all single-element extensions by the linear subclasses of a matroid.

2
Preliminaries

2.1. Basic concepts.

Our terminology and notations on hyperplane arrangement refer to
[
10
]
. A
hyperplane arrangement

\mathcal{A}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is called a
linear arrangement
if all hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
pass through the origin. The
intersection semi-lattice

L(\mathcal{A})
consists of all nonempty intersections of some hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by the reverse inclusion and including
V=\bigcap_{H\in\emptyset}H
as the minimal element. For
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
, the
localization

\mathcal{A}_{X}
of
\mathcal{A}
at
X
is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\{H\in\mathcal{A}\mid X\subseteq H\},

and the
restriction

\mathcal{A}/X
is a hyperplane arrangement in
X
given by

\mathcal{A}/X:=\{H\cap X\neq\emptyset\mid H\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{X}\}.

The
complement
of
\mathcal{A}
is
M(\mathcal{A})=V\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal{A}}H
. It is clear that the whole space
V
has the set partition

V=\bigsqcup_{X\in L(\mathcal{A})}M(\mathcal{A}/X).

(1)

Namely, for each point
{\bm{x}}\in V
, there is a unique element
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
satisfying
{\bm{x}}\in M(\mathcal{A}/X)
, denoted by
X_{\bm{x}}
. Indeed,
X_{\bm{x}}
is the inclusion-minimal element of
L(\mathcal{A})
containing
{\bm{x}}
.

The
characteristic polynomial

\chi(\mathcal{A},t)
of
\mathcal{A}
is

\chi(\mathcal{A},t):=\sum\limits_{X\in L(\mathcal{A})}\mu(V,X)t^{{\rm dim}(X)}=w_{0}^{+}(\mathcal{A})t^{d}-w_{1}^{+}(\mathcal{A})t^{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}w_{d}^{+}(\mathcal{A}),

where
\mu
is the Möbius function of
L(\mathcal{A})
. In particular, we assume
\chi(\mathcal{A},t)=0
if the ambient space
V
is a member of
\mathcal{A}
. Each unsigned coefficient
w_{k}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
is called the
k
-th unsigned Whitney number of the first kind
. The number
W_{k}(\mathcal{A})
of members in
L(\mathcal{A})
of codimension
k
is called the
k
-th Whitney number of the second kind
. As a generalization of characteristic polynomial, Zaslavsky
[
12
]
introduced the
Whitney polynomial

w(\mathcal{A};s,t)
of
\mathcal{A}
as

w(\mathcal{A};s,t):=\sum\limits_{X\leq Y{\rm\,in\,}L(\mathcal{A})}\mu(X,Y)s^{d-{\rm dim}(X)}t^{{\rm dim}(Y)}=\sum_{0\leq j\leq i\leq d}(-1)^{j}c_{ij}(\mathcal{A})s^{d-i}t^{i-j}.

(2)

Likewise, we assume
w(\mathcal{A};s,t)=0
if
V\in\mathcal{A}
. Clearly it can be written as

w(\mathcal{A};s,t)=\sum_{X\in L(\mathcal{A})}\chi(\mathcal{A}/X,t)\cdot s^{d-{\rm dim}(X)}.

(3)

Indeed,
\chi(\mathcal{A},t)=w(\mathcal{A};0,t)
. The
doubly indexed Whitney number of the first kind
was proposed by Zaslavsky
[
13
]
and defined as

w_{ij}(\mathcal{A}):=\sum_{X\leq Y{\rm\,in\,}L(\mathcal{A})}\{\mu(X,Y):{\rm codim}(X)=i,\;{\rm codim}(Y)=j\}.

More information about it, see
[
8
,
14
]
. Actually, each
c_{ij}(\mathcal{A})
equals
(-1)^{j}w_{d-i,d+j-i}(\mathcal{A})
.

When
V
is a Euclidean space,
M(\mathcal{A})
consists of finitely many connected components, called
regions
of
\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
r(\mathcal{A})
the number of regions of
\mathcal{A}
. The celebrated Zaslavsky’s formula
[
12
]
states
r(\mathcal{A})=(-1)^{d}\chi(\mathcal{A},-1)
. Let
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
be an affine subspace of dimension
k
. We call each region of
\mathcal{A}/X
a
k
-dimensional face of
\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
f_{k}(\mathcal{A})
the number of
k
-dimensional faces of
\mathcal{A}
. Then Zaslavsky’s formula directly yields the consequence

f_{k}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{X\in L(\mathcal{A}),\,\dim(X)=k}(-1)^{k}\chi(\mathcal{A}/X,-1).

In particular,
f_{d}(\mathcal{A})=r(\mathcal{A})
.

\mathcal{A}
as the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}:=\big\{H_{\bm{\alpha}_{i},a_{i}}\mid i\in[m]\big\}

\mathbb{F}^{d}
later. The

of

\mathcal{A}^{o}
in
\mathbb{F}^{d}

\mathcal{A}^{o}:=\big\{H_{\bm{\alpha}_{i}}\mid i\in[m]\big\}.

When
\mathcal{A}
is essential, i.e., the whole space
\mathbb{F}^{d}
is exactly spanned by the normal vectors
{\bm{\alpha}}_{1},{\bm{\alpha}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{\alpha}}_{m}
, then
\mathcal{A}^{o}
is also essential. In this case, we always assume that

\displaystyle L_{0}(\mathcal{A}):=\{{\bm{v}}_{1},{\bm{v}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{v}}_{n_{0}}\}\quad\mbox{\rm~and~}\quad L_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{o}):=\{\mathbb{F}{\bm{u}}_{1},\mathbb{F}{\bm{u}}_{2},\ldots,\mathbb{F}{\bm{u}}_{n_{1}}\}

consist of
0
-dimensional members of
L(\mathcal{A})
and
1
-dimensional members of
L(\mathcal{A}^{o})
later respectively (unless otherwise stated), where each
\mathbb{F}{\bm{u}}_{i}
denotes the subspace spanned by
{\bm{u}}_{i}
. The
adjoint arrangement

\sigma\mathcal{A}^{o}
of
\mathcal{A}^{o}
is a linear arrangement in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
defined as

\sigma\mathcal{A}^{o}:=\big\{H_{\bm{u}_{i}}:\bm{u}_{i}\cdot\bm{x}=0\mid i\in[n_{1}]\big\},

(4)

which was first constructed by Bixby-Coullard
[
2
]
for vector matroids in 1988. Associated with
L_{0}(\mathcal{A})
and
L_{1}(\mathcal{A}^{o})
, the
induced adjoint arrangement

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
in

is defined as

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{H_{\bm{\tilde{}}{\bm{v}}_{i}}:\tilde{\bm{v}}_{i}\cdot{\bm{x}}=0\mid i\in[n_{0}]\big\}\cup\big\{H_{\tilde{\bm{u}}_{i}}:\tilde{\bm{u}}_{i}\cdot{\bm{x}}=0\mid i\in[n_{1}]\big\},

(5)

where
\tilde{{\bm{v}}}_{i}=({\bm{v}}_{i},-1)

.
For convenience, let

(6)

2.3. Main results.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is essential. For any
(\bm{\alpha},a)\in\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
, the one-element extension
\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a}
is obtained from
\mathcal{A}
by adding a hyperplane
H_{\bm{\alpha},a}
to
\mathcal{A}
. Note from (
1
) that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
makes a partition
\mathbb{F}^{d+1}=\bigsqcup_{X\in L(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})}M(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}/X)
, which will classify the intersection semi-lattices
L(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})
for all
(\bm{\alpha},a)\in\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
. In particular,
L\big(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{0},a}\big)=L(\mathcal{A})
for
a\in\mathbb{F}\setminus\{0\}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is essential. Given
X\in L(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
. For any vectors
({\bm{\alpha}},a),({\bm{\alpha}}^{\prime},a^{\prime})\in M(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}/X)
, we have

L\big(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a}\big)\cong L\big(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}}\big).

In fact, when
\mathcal{A}
is not essential, the classification of the one-element extensions can be reduced to classifying
\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a}
for
(\bm{\alpha},a)\in O\times\mathbb{F}
(
O
is the space spanned by the normal vectors
\bm{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\bm{\alpha}_{m}
of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
), which will be explained at the end of Subsection 3.1.

According to
Theorem 2.1
, each invariant (unsigned Whitney number of the first kind, Whitney number of the second kind, face number, etc.) of every one-element extension of a hyperplane arrangement can be regarded as a function on the intersection lattice of the induced adjoint arrangement. Next we will further establish the order-preserving relations of these combinatorial invariants with respect to
L(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
, including the unsigned coefficients
c_{ij}(\mathcal{A})
of the Whitney polynomial, the unsigned Whitney numbers
w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
of the first kind, the Whitney numbers
W_{i}(\mathcal{A})
of the second kind, the face numbers
f_{i}(\mathcal{A})
and the region number
r(\mathcal{A})
. For convenience, we use an united notation
{\rm INV}(\mathcal{A})
to denote any invariant among
c_{ij}(\mathcal{A}),w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A}),W_{i}(\mathcal{A}),f_{i}(\mathcal{A})
,
r(\mathcal{A})
for all
0\leq j\leq i\leq d
. For example, if
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
are two hyperplane arrangements in
\mathbb{F}^{d}
, the notation
{\rm INV}(\mathcal{A})\leq{\rm INV}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
means
c_{ij}(\mathcal{A})\leq c_{ij}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}),w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A})\leq w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}),W_{i}(\mathcal{A})\leq W_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}),f_{i}(\mathcal{A})\leq f_{i}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
,
r(\mathcal{A})\leq r(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
for all
j\leq i
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is essential. Given
(\bm{\alpha},a),(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{F}^{d+1}
. If
X_{(\bm{\alpha},a)}\subseteq X_{(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime})}
for
X_{(\bm{\alpha},a)},X_{(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime})}\in L(\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
, then

{\rm INV}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})\leq{\rm INV}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}}).

Theorem 2.1
says that given

,
\chi(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a},t)
are the same polynomial for any

, denoted by
\chi(X,t)
. When
\mathbb{F}
is defined over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or a rational arrangement, we have the following decomposition formula.

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}
is essential. If (a)
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}

3.1. Proof of
Theorem 2.1
.

For any
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
, the rank
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)
of
X
is defined as

\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)=d-{\rm dim}(X).

Given a subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
, define
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})
to be

\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\mbox{\rm rank}\big\{{\bm{\alpha}}_{i}\mid H_{\bm{\alpha}_{i},a_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}\big\}.

(7)

Obviously
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)=\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{A}_{X})
. Generally, if
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and
X=\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal{B}}H\in L(\mathcal{A})
, then

\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{B})=\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(X)=d-{\rm dim}(X).

(8)

Below is a key property to verifying

Given

So we have

\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J)
(with respect to
\mathcal{A}
) to be

\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J):=\mbox{\rm max}\big\{\#I\mid I\subseteq J\mbox{\rm~and~}I\mbox{ is affinely independent with respect to }\mathcal{A}\big\}.

In particular, if
\bigcap_{j\in J}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{j},a_{j}}\neq\emptyset
, the maximal affinely independent subset
I
of
J
is called a
basis
of
J
, which means
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J)=\#I
and
\bigcap_{j\in I}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{j},a_{j}}=\bigcap_{j\in J}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{j},a_{j}}
. Clearly,
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J)\leq\#J
. If
\bigcap_{j\in J}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{j},a_{j}}\neq\emptyset
, then
J
is affinely independent (affinely dependent resp.) if and only if
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J)=\#J
(
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(J)<\#J
resp.) with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. Additionally, a minimal affinely dependent subset
I
of
[m]
is said to be an
affine circuit
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,
\bigcap_{i\in I}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{i},a_{i}}\neq\emptyset
and
\mbox{\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}(I)={\rm rank}_{\mathcal{A}}\big(I\setminus\{i\}\big)=\#I-1
for all
i\in I
. Given a total order
\prec
on
[m]
, an
affine broken circuit
is a subset of
[m]
obtained from an affine circuit by deleting the minimal element. An
affine NBC set
is a subset of
[m]
containing no affine broken circuits. It follows that every affine NBC set is affinely independent. Denote by
{\rm NBC}_{k}(\mathcal{A})
the collection of affine NBC
k
-subsets of
[m]
. Here the affine NBC sets are exactly the
\chi
-independent sets defined in
[
9
, p. 72]
, which gives a counting interpretation of the unsigned Whitney number
w_{k}^{+}(\mathcal{A})
of the first kind. Generally, a hyperplane arrangement can be regarded as a semimatroid. For more information about affine broken circuits of hyperplane arrangements can refer to
[
6
]
.

Theorem 4.1

(Affine NBC Theorem
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hyperplane arrangement in a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
. Then

w_{k}^{+}(\mathcal{A})=\#{\rm NBC}_{k}(\mathcal{A}),\quad k=0,1,\ldots,d.

Here we may consider that given
X\in L(\mathcal{A})
,
\mathcal{A}/X
is a multi-arrangement in
X
consisting of hyperplane
H\cap X\neq\emptyset
for all
H\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}_{X}
. In addition, the label of each hyperplane in
\mathcal{A}/X
is inherited from
\mathcal{A}
, and these labels of hyperplanes with respect to
\mathcal{A}/X
inherit the total order on
[m]
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
. Applying
Theorem 4.1
to equation (
3
), together with (
2
), immediately we obtain

c_{ij}(\mathcal{A})=\sum_{X\in L(\mathcal{A}),\,\dim(X)=i}\#{\rm NBC}_{j}(\mathcal{A}/X).

(10)

Proof of
Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(\bm{\alpha}_{m+1},a_{m+1})=(\bm{\alpha},a)
and
(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime}_{m+1},a^{\prime}_{m+1})=(\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime})
.

implies that there is a rank-preserving injection

\phi:L(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})\to L(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}}),\quad Y\mapsto Y^{\prime},

where
Y^{\prime}=\bigcap_{j\in I_{Y}}H_{\bm{\alpha}_{i}^{\prime},a_{i}^{\prime}}\in L(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})
for some basis
I_{Y}
of
Y
. Recall from the definition of the Whitney number of the second kind that the injectivity of
\phi
implies directly
W_{i}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})\leq W_{i}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})
. Moreover, together with
Section 4
and equation (

c_{ij}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})\leq c_{ij}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})
. From
\chi(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a},t)=w(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a};0,t)
, we obtain
w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})=c_{di}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})
. Likewise, we have
w_{i}^{+}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})=c_{di}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})
. So we get

f_{i}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha},a})\leq f_{i}(\mathcal{A}+H_{\bm{\alpha}^{\prime},a^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\#\Omega

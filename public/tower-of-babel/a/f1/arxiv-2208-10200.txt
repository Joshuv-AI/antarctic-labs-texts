Active Spherical Model Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Harukuni Ikeda

Abstract
The spherical model is a popular solvable model and has been applied to describe several critical phenomena such as the ferromagnetic transition, Bose-Einstein condensation, spin-glass transition, glass transition, jamming transition, and so on. Motivated by recent developments of active matter, here we consider the spherical model driven by the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck type self-propulsion force with persistent time τ p \tau_{p} . We show that the model exhibits the Ising universality for finite τ p \tau_{p} . On the contrary, the model exhibits the random field Ising universality in the limit τ p → ∞ \tau_{p}\to\infty .



I
Introduction

In this work, we extend the spherical model to describe non-equilibrium
critical phenomena in the steady state. In particular, we have in mind
the so-called Motility-Induced Phase Separation (MIPS) of active matter:
the self-propelled particles spontaneously aggregate on increasing the
motility, eventually leading to a phase
separation
Cates and Tailleur 2015
. One of the fundamental questions is
to which universality class MIPS belongs. Extensive numerical
simulations showed that the MIPS has the Ising universality
class
Partridge and Lee 2019
;
Maggi et al. 2021
, while a different
universality is also reported
Siebert et al. 2018
. The field theoretical
studies support the Ising
universality
Caballero et al. 2018
;
Cates 2019
;
Maggi et al. 2022
,
where the self-propulsion force on the
\phi^{4}
field theory gives rise
to only irrelevant terms in the case of the bulk phase separation, while
a different universality appears in the case of the micro phase
separation
Caballero et al. 2018
. Here we study the effect of the
self-propulsion force on the Ising universality in a different way by
considering an exactly solvable model.

There are several solvable models to describe non-equilibrium critical
phenomena in the steady state, such as the celebrated Kuramoto
model
Acebrón et al. 2005
, asymmetric simple exclusion
process
Derrida 1998
, zero-range
process
Evans and Hanney 2005
, and so
on
Tailleur and Cates 2008
;
Arnoulx de Pirey et al. 2019
. However, most of those results are
obtained in the mean-field limit or one dimension, and it is not obvious
how to generalize the results to arbitrary spatial dimensions. The
spherical model is one of the few models that can be solved in any
dimension
Berlin and Kac 1952
and several
settings
Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Kosterlitz et al. 1976
;
Crisanti and Sommers 1992
;
Vojta 1996
;
Franz and Parisi 2016
;
Franz et al. 2017
;
Casasola et al. 2021
,
even out of
equilibrium
Cugliandolo and Kurchan 1993
;
Cugliandolo and Dean 1995
;
Henkel et al. 2008
;
Berthier and Kurchan 2013
;
Barbier et al. 2022
.
Therefore, the spherical model would be the most promising candidate to
describe the non-equilibrium critical phenomena in steady states, such
as MIPS.

Here we consider the spherical model driven by a self-propulsion force
with persistent time
\tau_{p}

ten Hagen et al. 2011
;
Szamel 2014
. We
show that for finite
\tau_{p}
, the model indeed has the Ising
universality, while in the limit
\tau_{p}\to\infty
, the model exhibits
the random field Ising universality.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Sec.
II
,
we review some known results of the spherical model through the analysis
of the spherical Debye model (SDM), which is a simplified version of the
spherical model of a ferromagnet initially introduced by Berlin and
Kac
Berlin and Kac 1952
. In particular, we argue that the
condensation transition of the model can be identified with the
underlying ferromagnetic transition
Gunton and Buckingham 1968
. In
Sec.
III
, we consider the SDM driven by an active noise
with the persistent time
\tau_{p}
. We investigate the model for

\tau_{p}<\infty
. In Sec.
IV
, we investigate the model
in the limit
\tau_{p}\to\infty
. In Sec.
V
, we briefly
discuss the effect of correlated noise. Finally, in
Sec.
VI
, we conclude the work.

II
Spherical Debye model

Here we first review some known results for the spherical model through
the analysis of a simplified version of the spherical model originally
proposed by Berlin and Kac
Berlin and Kac 1952
. We show that the
model exhibits the condensation transition at a critical temperature

T_{c}
, and the transition has the same universality as the ferromagnetic
transition
Berlin and Kac 1952
;
Gunton and Buckingham 1968
.

Let we consider the following quadratic interaction potential:

\displaystyle V_{N}=\frac{\bm{x}\cdot W\cdot\bm{x}}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}(\bm{x}\cdot\bm{x}-N),

(1)

where
\bm{x}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\}
denotes the state vector, and
W
is a

N\times N
matrix representing the nearest neighbor interaction in a

d
dimensional lattice.
\mu
denotes the Lagrange multiplier to impose
the following constraint:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle x_{i}^{2}\right\rangle=N,

(2)

where the braket denotes the thermal average by the Maxwell-Boltzmann
distribution at temperature
T
. To analyze the model, one
can expand
V_{N}
by the normal modes:

\displaystyle V_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu}{2}u_{i}^{2}-\frac{N\mu}{2},

(3)

where
\omega_{i}
denotes the frequency of the
i
-th mode. We will order

\omega_{i}
as

\displaystyle\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}<\cdots<\omega_{N}.

(4)

Since, an orthogonal transformation does not change the inner product,
the spherical constraint is written as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle=N.

(5)

The precise value of
\omega_{i}
depends on the details of
W
, but the
vibrational properties of a
d
dimensional lattice would eventually be
dominated by the phonon modes on a large enough scale. So we assume that
the distribution of
\omega_{i}
,

D(\omega)=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta(\omega-\omega_{i})
, is given by the
Debye density of states
Kittel and McEuen 2018
:

\displaystyle D(\omega)=\begin{cases}d\omega_{D}^{-d}\omega^{d-1}&\omega\in[0,\omega_{D}],\\
0&{\rm otherwise},\end{cases}

(6)

where
\omega_{D}
denotes the Debye frequency.
D(\omega)
is normalized
so that
\int_{0}^{\omega_{D}}D(\omega)d\omega=1
. To simplify the notation,
hereafter we set
\omega_{D}=1
. We call the model defined by
Eqs. (
3
), (
5
), and
(
6
) as the spherical Debye model (SDM).

In this section, we study the model in equilibrium at temperature

T
. From the equipartition theorem
Greiner et al. 2012
, we
get

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{k_{B}T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu},

(7)

where
k_{B}
denotes the Boltzmann constant. To simplify the notation,
hereafter we set
k_{B}=1
. Since
\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle\leq N
,
\mu
should
satisfy the following condition

\displaystyle\mu\geq-\min_{i}\omega_{i}^{2}=0.

(8)

The value of
\mu
is to be determined by the spherical constraint

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu},

(9)

In the limit
N\to\infty
, we expect that the summation can be replaced
with an integral:

\displaystyle 1=TF(\mu),

\displaystyle F(\mu)=\int d\omega\frac{D(\omega)}{\omega^{2}+\mu}.

(10)

F(\mu)
is a decreasing function of
\mu

and is maximal in the limit
\mu\to+0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to+0}F(\mu)=\begin{cases}+\infty&(d\leq 2)\\
1/T_{c}&(d>2).\end{cases},

(11)

where

\displaystyle T_{c}=\frac{d-2}{2}.

(12)

When
d>2
and
T<T_{c}
,

\displaystyle TF(\mu)\leq TF(0)<1,

(13)

implying that
TF(\mu)=1
has no solution. This is the signature of the
condensation to the lowest eigenmode

\omega_{1}

Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Crisanti et al. 2019
. Below
T_{c}
, we
should separate the first and other terms in Eq. (
9
)
to replace the summation with an integral, as in the case of the
Bose-Einstein
condensation
Greiner et al. 2012
;
Crisanti et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle}{N}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=2}^{N}\frac{T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left\langle u_{1}\right\rangle^{2}}{N}+TF(\mu).

(14)

Substituting it back into Eq. (
9
), we get for
T<T_{c}

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle u_{1}\right\rangle^{2}}{N}=1-TF(0)=1-\frac{T}{T_{c}}.

(15)

In the case of the spherical model of a
ferromagnet
Berlin and Kac 1952
, the condensation transition is
identified with the ferromagnetic phase transition, and
Eq. (
15
) corresponds to the square of the
magnetization
\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle/N\sim m^{2}
, which leads to the well-known
scaling behavior
{m\sim(1-T/T_{c})^{1/2}}

Berlin and Kac 1952
;
Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Crisanti et al. 2019
.

The detailed analysis of Eq. (
10
) reveals that on
approaching
T_{c}
from above,
\mu
behaves as follows (see
Appendix.
A
):

\displaystyle\mu\sim\begin{cases}(T-T_{c})^{\frac{2}{d-2}}&d\in(2,4),\\
(T-T_{c})^{1}&d>4.\end{cases}

(16)

Below
T_{c}
,
\mu
is written as

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{1}{\left\langle u_{1}\right\rangle^{2}}=\frac{1}{N}\frac{T_{c}}{T_{c}-T},

(17)

which vanishes in the thermodynamic limit
N\to\infty
. The Lagrange
multiplier
\mu
of the spherical model plays a similar role as the
chemical potential of the ideal Bose gas, in that it fixes the mean
value of an extensive quantity. Indeed, both quantities have the same
critical
exponent
Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Greiner et al. 2012
;
Crisanti et al. 2019
.
Since
\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle=1/\mu
, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle\sim\begin{cases}(T-T_{c})^{-\frac{2}{d-2}}&d\in(2,4),\\
(T-T_{c})^{-1}&d>4.\end{cases},

(18)

which can be identified with the susceptibility
\chi\sim m^{2}
of the
spherical ferromagnetic model for

T>T_{c}

Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Crisanti et al. 2019
.
Using the
equipartition theorem, the mean-value of the interaction potential
Eq. (
3
) is calculated as follows

\displaystyle u=\frac{\left\langle V_{N}\right\rangle}{N}=T-\mu=\begin{cases}T-\mu&(T>T_{c})\\
T&(T\leq T_{c})\end{cases}.

(19)

The specific heat for
d\in(2,4)
is

\displaystyle C=\frac{du}{dT}=\begin{cases}(T-T_{c})^{\frac{4-d}{d-2}}&(T>T_{c})\\
{\rm const}&(T<T_{c})\end{cases},

(20)

and for
d>4
, the critical exponent is zero, i.e.,
C
changes
discontinuously at
T=T_{c}
. The result is again consistent with the
spherical ferromagnetic model
Berlin and Kac 1952
. One can also
argue the scaling of the correlation length by assuming the linear
relation
\omega_{i}=cq_{i}
between the frequency
\omega_{i}
and wave
number
q_{i}

Gunton and Buckingham 1968
;
Nishimori and Ortiz 2010
.

In summary, the SDM, which consists of
N
non-interacting oscillators
Eq. (
3
), has the same universality as the spherical
model of a ferromagnet. It is worth mentioning that the phase behavior
and the critical exponent of
\mu
can be deduced from only the
information of the second moment of
u_{i}
in the steady state

\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle
. This allows us to draw the phase diagram even for a
non-equilibrium version of the model where the steady-state distribution
is in general not known. Furthermore, the critical exponent of the
Lagrange multiplier
\mu
, which controls the other critical exponents,
is also calculated from
\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle
. This allows us to discuss the
lower and upper critical dimensions, and the universality class.

III
Active spherical model

Now we consider a non-equilibrium model. We consider the following
equation
ten Hagen et al. 2011
:

\displaystyle\frac{du_{i}(t)}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial V_{N}}{\partial u_{i}(t)}+f_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-(\mu+\omega_{i}^{2})u_{i}(t)+f_{i}(t),

(21)

where
f_{i}(t)
denotes the self-propulsion force.
The time evolution of
f_{i}(t)
is given by the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
Szamel 2014
:

\displaystyle\tau_{p}\dot{f}_{i}(t)=-f_{i}(t)+\sqrt{2T}\eta_{i}(t),

(22)

where
\tau_{p}
denotes the persistent time,

T
denotes the strength of the noise,
and
\eta_{i}(t)
denotes the white noise
satisfying the following condition

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta_{i}(t)\right\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta_{i}(t)\eta_{j}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(23)

Eq. (
22
) can be directly integrated as
follows:

\displaystyle f_{i}(t)=\frac{\sqrt{2T}}{\tau_{p}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{-(t-t^{\prime})/\tau_{p}}\xi_{i}(t^{\prime}).

(24)

Then, we get

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{i}(t)f_{j}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{2T}{\tau_{p}^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt_{1}\int_{-\infty}^{t^{\prime}}dt_{2}e^{-(2t-t_{1}-t_{2})/\tau_{p}}

\displaystyle\times\delta_{ij}\delta(t_{1}-t_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta_{ij}\frac{T}{\tau_{p}}e^{-\left|t-t^{\prime}\right|/\tau_{p}}.

(25)

In the limit
\tau_{p}\to 0
,
\left\langle f_{i}(t)f_{j}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle\to 2T\delta_{ij}\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, implying that
f_{i}(t)
reduces to the
thermal white noise at temperature

T

Zwanzig 2001
. Now we assume that the system is
in the steady state at
t=-\infty
so that the Lagrange multiplier
\mu

does not depend on time. Then, one can easily integrate
Eq. (
21
) as follows:

\displaystyle u_{i}(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{-(\mu+\omega_{i}^{2})(t-t^{\prime})}f_{i}(t^{\prime}).

(26)

With a similar calculation as Eq. (
25
), the second
moment of
u_{i}(t)
in the steady state is calculated as
follows
Szamel 2014
:

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}(t)^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{T}{(\mu+\omega_{i}^{2})\left[1+\tau_{p}(\mu+\omega_{i}^{2})\right]}.

(27)

This implies that the equipartition theorem Eq. (
7
)
does not hold for
\tau_{p}>0
, as a consequence of the violation of the
detailed balance
Szamel 2014
. Some readers may wonder how
f_{i}(t)

behaves in real space. In fact, the properties of
f_{i}(t)
do not depend
on the choice of the coordinate system, since a transformation by an
orthogonal matrix
O
preserves the second moment of
f_{i}(t)
:

\displaystyle\left\langle(Of(t))_{i}(Of(t^{\prime}))_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{nm}O_{in}O_{jm}\left\langle f_{n}(t)f_{m}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{\tau_{p}}\delta_{ij}e^{-\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|/\tau_{p}}.

(28)

The value of
\mu
is to be determined by the spherical constraint:
1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle
. For
T>T_{c}
, the summation can be
replaced with an integral:

\displaystyle 1=TF(\mu),

\displaystyle F(\mu)=\int d\omega D(\omega)\frac{1}{(\mu+\omega^{2})\left[1+\tau_{p}(\mu+\omega^{2})\right]}.

(29)

For finite
\tau_{p}
and for
d>2
,
F(\mu)
converges to a finite value,
implying that the condensation transition occurs at the critical
temperature

\displaystyle T_{c}=\frac{1}{F(0)}.

(30)

As shown in Fig.
1
,
T_{c}
increases on increasing

\tau_{p}
, suggesting that the motility facilitates the phase transition,
as in the case of the MIPS
Cates and Tailleur 2015
.

Figure 1:
Phase diagram of the
active spherical model. The vertical axis is rescaled by the critical
temperature in equilibrium
T_{c}^{\rm eq}\equiv T_{c}(\tau_{p}=0)
. The
solid line denotes the transition line.

Below
T_{c}
, the condensation to the first mode occurs as in the case of
the equilibrium model, see Eq. (
15
). The critical
behavior for
\mu\ll 1
is governed by the small
\omega
behavior of the integrand
in Eq. (
29
):

\displaystyle\frac{D(\omega)}{(\mu+\omega^{2})\left[1+\tau_{p}(\mu+\omega^{2})\right]}\sim\frac{D(\omega)}{(\mu+\omega^{2})},

(31)

which is the same as that of the original model
Eq. (
10
). Therefore, the critical exponent is unchanged
from the original one.

IV
Extreme active matter

Recently, the extreme active matter, which
corresponds to the limit
\tau_{p}\to\infty
, has attracted much
attention
Mandal et al. 2020
;
Caprini et al. 2020
;
Morse et al. 2021
. So we
here consider the corresponding limit. For this purpose, we introduce
a scaled variable:

\displaystyle\tilde{T}\equiv\frac{T}{\tau_{p}}.

(32)

Then, in the limit
\tau_{p}\to\infty
, Eq. (
27
)
reduces to

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle\to\frac{\tilde{T}}{(\mu+\omega_{i})^{2}}.

(33)

Repeating the same arguments as in the previous sections, we obtain
the self-consistent equation for
\mu
:

\displaystyle 1=\tilde{T}F(\mu),

\displaystyle F(\mu)=\int d\omega\frac{D(\omega)}{(\omega^{2}+\mu)^{2}}.

(34)

In the limit
\mu\to 0
, we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to 0}F(\mu)=\begin{cases}\infty&(d\leq 4)\\
d/(d-4)&(d>4)\end{cases},

(35)

implying that the lower critical dimension is
d_{u}=4
. Below
T<T_{c}
,
the condensation to the first mode occurs:

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle}{N}=1-\frac{T}{T_{c}},

(36)

where

\displaystyle T_{c}=F(0)^{-1}=\frac{d-4}{d}.

(37)

The detailed analysis of Eq. (
34
) leads to

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(T-T_{c})^{\frac{2}{d-4}}&d\in(4,6)\\
(T-T_{c})^{1}&d>6,\end{cases},

(38)

\displaystyle\left\langle u_{1}^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(T-T_{c})^{-\frac{4}{d-4}}&d\in(4,6)\\
(T-T_{c})^{-2}&d>6,\end{cases}

(39)

implying that the upper critical dimension is
d_{u}=6
, see
Appendix.
A
. The above results are consistent with
the spherical model with random field
Vojta and Schreiber 1996
. This would
be a reasonable result because in the large
\tau_{p}
limit, the
self-propulsion force is permanently frozen and may be identified with a
random field, see also Appendix
B
.

V
Correlated noise

The analysis in the previous sections revealed
that the phase transition does not occur for
d\leq d_{u}=2
when

\tau_{p}<\infty
and for
d\leq d_{u}=4
when
\tau_{p}=\infty
. However,
there is some numerical evidence of the phase transitions in

d=2

Siebert et al. 2018
;
Partridge and Lee 2019
. A possible ingredient of the
phase transition in low
d
is a long-range correlation of
f_{i}
, which
originates from the hydrodynamic interaction
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
,
elastic interaction
Landau et al. 1986
, or something
else
Szamel and Flenner 2021
;
Kuroda et al. 2022
. So, here we briefly
discuss the effects of the correlated noise. For this purpose, we modify
Eq. (
25
) as follows:

\displaystyle\left\langle f_{i}(t)f_{j}(t^{\prime})\right\rangle=\delta_{ij}\frac{T_{i}}{\tau_{p}}e^{-\left|t-t^{\prime}\right|/\tau_{p}},

(40)

where
T_{i}
depends on the frequency
\omega_{i}
(or equivalently wave
number
q_{i}=c^{-1}\omega_{i}
). The long-range correlation would suppress
the fluctuation of
f_{i}
on a large scale,
i.e.
, small

q_{i}=c^{-1}\omega_{i}
. To express this effect, we assume a power-law
function:

\displaystyle T_{i}=T\omega_{i}^{p}.

(41)

Now the self-consistent equation for
\mu
Eq. (
29
) is
modified as

\displaystyle 1=TF(\mu),

\displaystyle F(\mu)=\int_{0}^{1}d\omega\frac{\omega^{d+p-1}}{(\mu+\omega^{2})\left[1+\tau_{p}(\mu+\omega^{2})\right]}.

(42)

From the above expression, one can see that the correlated noise
Eq. (
41
) effectively increases the spatial dimension
from
d
to
d+p
.
Therefore, the upper and lower critical dimensions are

\displaystyle d_{l}=\begin{cases}2-p&(\tau_{p}<\infty)\\
4-p&(\tau_{p}=\infty)\end{cases},

(43)

\displaystyle d_{u}=\begin{cases}4-p&(\tau_{p}<\infty)\\
6-p&(\tau_{p}=\infty)\end{cases}.

(44)

Therefore, the
\omega_{i}
(or
q_{i}
) dependence of the noise
Eq. (
41
) indeed can cause the phase transition below
the lower critical dimension. However in reality, the noise should be
determined self-consistently as a consequence of the complex interaction
between the system and environment
Sagués et al. 2007
. Further studies for
this problem would be beneficial.

VI
Summary and discussions

In summary, we investigated the effects of the active noise on the
spherical model. For this purpose, we first introduced and analyzed the
spherical Debye model, which is a simplified version of the spherical
model for a ferromagnet. The model is so simple that one can discuss the
phase behavior solely from the information of the second-order moment at
the steady state, even without complete knowledge of the steady state
distribution. The simplicity also allows us to introduce an active
noise: the noise produced by the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with the
persistent time
\tau_{p}
. We found that for a finite value of
\tau_{p}
,
the model has the same universality as the original model, while
in the limit
\tau_{p}\to\infty
, the universality reduces to that of
the random field Ising model.

In this work, we only investigated the static properties of the model.
Of course, what is more interesting is the dynamic behaviors such as
aging dynamics
Cugliandolo and Dean 1995
;
Henkel 2022
, effective
temperature
Cugliandolo and Kurchan 1993
;
Berthier and Kurchan 2013
, entropy
production
Caballero and Cates 2020
;
Paoluzzi 2022
, and so on. The current
model may allow us to derive a full dynamical solution as done for

p
-spin spherical
models
Cugliandolo and Kurchan 1993
;
Cugliandolo and Dean 1995
. Further
studies on this problem would be beneficial.

Appendix A
Critical exponent

To determine
\mu
, one should solve the following self-consistent equation:

\displaystyle 1=TF(\mu)\equiv TA\int_{0}^{1}d\omega\frac{\omega^{d-1}}{(\omega^{2}+\mu)^{n}},

(45)

where
A
is a constant,
n=1
for
\tau_{p}<\infty
and
n=2
for

\tau_{p}=\infty
.
If
d-2n>0
,
F(0)
is finite, implying that there exists the condensation
transition. So, the lower critical dimension is

\displaystyle d_{u}=2n.

(46)

When
d>2n+2
,
F(\mu)
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}

\displaystyle=F(0)+\mu F^{\prime}(0)+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{c}}+\mu F^{\prime}(0)+\cdots,

(47)

leading to

\displaystyle\mu\sim(T-T_{c})^{1}.

(48)

On the contrary, if
d\in(2n,2n+2)
,
F^{\prime}(\mu)
for small
\mu

behaves as

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\mu)\sim\mu^{\frac{d-(2n+2)}{2}},

(49)

implying

\displaystyle F(\mu)-F(0)=\int_{0}^{\mu}d\mu^{\prime}F^{\prime}(\mu^{\prime})\sim\mu^{\frac{d-2n}{2}},

(50)

leading to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}=F(\mu)=\frac{1}{T_{c}}-B\mu^{\frac{d-2n}{2}},

(51)

where
B
is a constant. Therefore, the scaling of
\mu
for
\mu\ll 1
is

\displaystyle\mu\sim(T-T_{c})^{\frac{2}{d-2n}}.

(52)

A logaithmic correction may appear when

d=2n+2

Nishimori and Ortiz 2010
. The above results imply that the
upper critical dimension is

\displaystyle d_{u}=2n+2.

(53)

Appendix B
Spherical model with random field

We here consider the spherical model with a random
field
Vojta and Schreiber 1996

Ikeda 2022
:

\displaystyle V_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{i}+\mu}{2}u_{i}^{2}-\frac{N\mu}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}u_{i},

(54)

where
h_{i}

In equilibrium at temperature
T

Ikeda 2022

\displaystyle\overline{\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle}=\frac{T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu}+\frac{\Delta}{(\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu)^{2}},

where the overline denotes the average for
h_{i}

\Delta=\overline{h_{i}^{2}}

\mu

determined by the spherical constraint:

\displaystyle 1=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left[\frac{T}{\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu}+\frac{\Delta}{(\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu)^{2}}\right].

(56)

As mentioned in the main text, the scaling for

\overline{\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle}

second term in Eq. (
55

\displaystyle\overline{\left\langle u_{i}^{2}\right\rangle}\approx\frac{\Delta}{(\omega_{i}^{2}+\mu)^{2}}.

(57)

This agrees with the active spherical model with

,

33

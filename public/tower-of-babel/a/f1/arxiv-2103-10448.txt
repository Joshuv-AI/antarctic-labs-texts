Non-autonomous scalar linear-dissipative and purely dissipative parabolic PDEs over a compact base flow Thanks: Both authors were partly supported by FEDER Ministerio de Economía y Competitividad grants MTM2015-66330-P and RTI2018-096523-B-I00 and by Universidad de Valladolid under project PIP-TCESC-2020
Rafael Obaya, Ana M. Sanz

Abstract
. In this paper a family of non-autonomous scalar parabolic PDEs over a general compact and connected flow is considered. The existence or not of a neighbourhood of zero where the problems are linear has an influence on the methods used and on the dynamics of the induced skew-product semiflow. That is why two cases are distinguished: linear-dissipative and purely dissipative problems. In both cases, the structure of the global and pullback attractors is studied using principal spectral theory. Besides, in the purely dissipative setting, a simple condition is given, involving both the underlying linear dynamics and some properties of the nonlinear term, to determine the nontrivial sections of the attractor.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we investigate a family of time-dependent scalar linear-dissipative parabolic PDEs over a continuous flow on a compact metric space
(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
, with Neumann, Robin or Dirichlet boundary conditions, of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+h(p{\cdot}t,x)\,y+g(p{\cdot}t,x,y)\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\;\,\text{for each}\;p\in P,\\[5.69046pt]
By:=\bar{\alpha}(x)\,y+\kappa\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

where
p{\cdot}t=\sigma_{t}(p)
,
h
denotes the coefficient of the linear part of the problems, which is continuous, and
g
is a smooth dissipative term with
g(p,x,0)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(p,x,0)=0
for every
p\in P
and
x\in{\bar{U}}
. We assume that
g
is dominant with respect to the linear term for
y
large enough and that there is a constant
r_{0}\geq 0
such that
g(p,x,y)=0
if and only if
|y|\leq r_{0}
, which determines the structure near zero. The long-term behaviour of the solutions is studied taking into account the internal dynamics of
P
. This dynamical formulation is similar to the one in Mierczyński and Shen
[
20
]
to investigate deterministic time-dependent parabolic equations, with applications to Kolmogorov models.

We consider standard regularity assumptions which provide existence, uniqueness, existence in the large and continuous dependence of mild solutions with respect to initial data. Then, mild solutions of the abstract Cauchy problems (ACPs for short) associated to the linear-dissipative equations generate a global skew-product semiflow
\tau
on
P\times X
, where
X=C(\bar{U})
for Neumann and Robin boundary conditions and
X=C_{0}(\bar{U})
in the Dirichlet case.

Although the term
linear-dissipative
is in general applied to the complete family of the previous parabolic equations for all the values of
r_{0}\geq 0
, in this paper and for the sake of distinction, we will refer to the
linear-dissipative
case provided that
r_{0}>0
, which implies that there is a zone around
0
where the problems are in fact linear and a zone away from
0
where the dissipative term comes into play, whereas the term
purely dissipative
will be employed whenever
r_{0}=0
, in order to emphasize the fact that the dissipative term is always active and the problems are strictly nonlinear in every neighbourhood of zero.

The global dynamics of the semiflow
\tau
is dissipative, which implies the existence of a global attractor
\mathbb{A}
.
Due to the monotonicity of
\tau
,
\mathbb{A}
has lower and upper boundaries which can be identified with the graphs of two semicontinuous functions
a
and
b
, respectively. Assuming for simplicity that
g(p,x,y)
is odd with respect to
y
,
a=-b
and the global attractor can be represented as
\mathbb{A}=\cup_{p\in P}\{p\}\times A(p)
, with sections
A(p)\subseteq[-b(p),b(p)]
for
p\in P
. It turns out that for each
p\in P
the parametrized set
\{A(p{\cdot}t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is the pullback attractor of the process
S_{p}(\cdot,\cdot)
generated by the solutions of the non-autonomous parabolic equation obtained by the evaluation of the coefficients along the trajectory of
p
. The parametric family of compact sets
\{A(p)\}_{p\in P}
takes the name of cocycle (or pullback) attractor of the semiflow. Some nice references for attractors in non-autonomous dynamics are Carvalho et al.
[
5
]
and Kloeden and Rasmussen
[
16
]
.

Since the underlying linear dynamics is decisive in the description of the attractor, we also consider the global linear skew-product semiflow
\tau_{L}
induced by the solutions of the linear part of the parabolic problems. A crucial fact in this theory is that
\tau_{L}
is strongly positive in
P\times X^{\gamma}
, where
X^{\gamma}=C({\bar{U}})
for Neumann and Robin boundary conditions and
X^{\gamma}=X^{\alpha}
, a fractional power space, in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions. In consequence,
\tau_{L}
admits a continuous separation
X^{\gamma}=X_{1}(p)\oplus X_{2}(p)
for
p\in P
, in the terms stated in Poláčik and Tereščák
[
27
]
and Shen and Yi
[
32
]
. The restriction of
\tau_{L}
to the principal bundle
\cup_{p\in P}\{p\}\times X_{1}(p)
defines a continuous one-dimensional (1-dim for short) linear flow determined by a 1-dim linear cocycle
c(t,p)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in P
, and its continuous spectrum is called the
principal spectrum
. The 1-dim cocycle
c(t,p)
determines the asymptotic behaviour of the positive solutions of
\tau_{L}
and plays an important role in the description of both topological and ergodic properties of
\tau
on the global attractor.

We investigate the structure and internal dynamics of the global and cocycle attractors of
\tau
in terms of the principal spectrum of
\tau_{L}
.
These questions were initiated in Cardoso et al.
[
3
]
, Caraballo et al.
[
2
]
and Langa et al.
[
17
]
when the flow in
P
is minimal and uniquely ergodic, and the principal spectrum is thus a singleton. Although purely dissipative problems are also considered, these references extensively cover the linear-dissipative case when the flow on
P
is aperiodic, showing ingredients of high complexity or chaotic dynamics that cannot occur when the equations are autonomous or periodic.
In the present paper, for both the linear-dissipative and the purely dissipative settings we give a version of the theory valid for a general continuous flow on a compact base
P
. In particular we describe the dynamical complexity when several ergodic measures or minimal subsets coexist in
P
. Besides, for a very general class of purely dissipative problems, we determine integrability conditions on the cocycle
c(t,p)
on
(-\infty,0]
for a fixed
p\in P
which imply that the section of the attractor
A(p)
is nontrivial. Similar conditions are necessary for
A(p)\not=\{0\}
with Neumann or Robin boundary conditions, under some spectral restrictions. The conclusions in the purely dissipative case are compatible with the presence of ergodic measures with null Lyapunov exponent and are new also when the flow on the base
P
is minimal or even almost periodic.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
compiles some basic notions in the field of monotone non-autonomous dynamical systems. In Section
3
the family of PDEs problems under study is detailed, and known results on the existence of a global attractor for the induced skew-product semiflows are reviewed. Also, some general results are given for the boundary map
b
and its null set. Some aspects of the linear semiflow
\tau_{L}
are treated in Section
3.1
. In particular, the principal spectrum and the 1-dim linear cocycle
c(t,p)
are introduced, which have leading roles in the paper. In order to have that the principal spectrum is a possibly degenerate compact interval
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=[\alpha_{P},\lambda_{P}]
on the real line, in the rest of the paper we assume that the compact base
P
is also connected.

In Section
4
we consider linear-dissipative problems, that is,
r_{0}>0
. One advantage in this case is that
b(p)\gg 0
is characterized by the boundedness of
c(t,p)
for negative times. A description of the map
b
is given depending on the location of the principal spectrum
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}
on the real line. The only chance for a trivial attractor happens for
\lambda_{P}<0
. If
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=[\alpha_{P},0]
with
\alpha_{P}\leq 0
, there is some
p\in P
with
b(p)\gg 0
. The set of these points can even have measure
1
for some ergodic ergodic measure with null Lyapunov exponent, giving rise to a big subset of the phase space with chaotic dynamics. If
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=[0,\lambda_{P}]
with
\lambda_{P}\geq 0
, the previous chaotic dynamics can also appear
and in addition every minimal set
M
in
P
has some point
p\in M
with
b(p)\gg 0
. Besides, if
\lambda_{P}>0
,
b
is almost always strongly positive with respect to an ergodic measure and, if
\alpha_{P}>0
,
b
is uniformly strongly positive.
Last but not least, when the nonlinear term
g
is strictly sublinear in the zone
y>r_{0}
, a more detailed dynamical description is obtained and, with
\alpha_{P}>0
,
b
is continuous.

The purely dissipative case, with
r_{0}=0
, is treated in Section
5
. The internal dynamics of the attractor is described once more according to the location of
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}
. Also now further information is obtained under a strictly sublinear condition: we note that some of the results had been proved by Mierczyński and Shen
[
19
,
20
]
for Kolmogorov models. But in fact the most important result in the paper is Theorem
5.5
, determining a sufficient (and many times necessary) integrability condition on the 1-dim cocycle
c(t,p)
for
t\leq 0
to guarantee that
b(p)\gg 0
, eventually applicable to a very general class of problems in Theorem
5.9
. One can find in the literature classical examples by Poincaré
[
26
]
, Conley and Miller
[
8
]
, Zhikov and Levitan
[
35
]
or Johnson and Moser
[
14
]
of almost periodic maps satisfying the cited integrability condition. Finally, Section
6
offers a collection of simple examples to illustrate different dynamical behaviours in the attractor.

2.
Basic notions

In this section we include some preliminaries about
topological dynamics for non-autonomous dynamical systems.

Let
(P,d)
be a compact
metric space. A real
continuous flow

(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
is
defined by a continuous map
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\times P\to P,\;(t,p)\mapsto\sigma(t,p)=\sigma_{t}(p)
satisfying

(i)

\sigma_{0}=\text{Id},

(ii)

\sigma_{t+s}=\sigma_{t}\circ\sigma_{s}
for each
s
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The set
\{\sigma_{t}(p)\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is called the
orbit

of the point
p
. A point
p
is called
topologically transitive
if its orbit is dense in
P
. We say that a subset
P_{1}\subset P
is
invariant
if
\sigma_{t}(P_{1})=P_{1}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
The flow
(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
is called
minimal
if it does not contain properly any other
compact invariant set, or equivalently, if every
orbit is dense.
The flow is
distal
if the orbits of any two distinct
points
p_{1},\,p_{2}\in P
keep at a positive distance,
that is,
\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}d(\sigma(t,p_{1}),\sigma(t,p_{2}))>0
; and it
is
almost periodic
if the family of maps
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}:P\to P
is uniformly equicontinuous. An almost periodic flow is always distal.

A finite regular measure defined on the Borel sets of
P
is called
a Borel measure on
P
. Given
\mu
a normalized Borel measure on

P
, it is
invariant
for the flow
\sigma
if
\mu(\sigma_{t}(P_{1}))=\mu(P_{1})
for every Borel subset

P_{1}\subset P
and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
. It is
ergodic
if, in
addition,
\mu(P_{1})=0
or
\mu(P_{1})=1
for every
invariant Borel subset
P_{1}\subset P
. An invariant Borel set
P_{1}\subseteq P
is
of complete measure
if
\mu(P_{1})=1
for every ergodic measure
\mu
. If that is the case, then
\mu(P_{1})=1
for every invariant measure.

(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
is
uniquely ergodic
if it has a
unique normalized invariant measure, which is then necessarily
ergodic. A minimal and almost periodic flow
(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
is uniquely ergodic. For simplicity, we will denote the flow by
\sigma_{t}(p)=p{\cdot}t
for
p\in P
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

A standard method to, roughly speaking, get rid of the time variation in a non-autonomous equation and build a non-autonomous dynamical system, is the so-called
hull
construction. More precisely, a function
f\in C(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
is said to be
admissible
if for any
compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
f
is bounded and uniformly continuous
on
\mathbb{R}\times K
. Provided that
f
is admissible, its
hull

P
is the closure for the compact-open topology of the set of
t
-translates of
f
,
\{f_{t}\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
with
f_{t}(s,x)=f(t+s,x)

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. The translation map
\mathbb{R}\times P\to P
,

(t,p)\mapsto p{\cdot}t
given by
p{\cdot}t(s,x)=p(s+t,x)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, defines a
continuous flow on the compact metric space
P
. This flow is minimal as far as the map
f
has certain recurrent behaviour in time, such as periodicity, almost periodicity, or other weaker properties of recurrence. If the map
f(t,x)
is uniformly almost periodic (that is, it is admissible and almost periodic in
t
for any fixed
x
), then the flow on the hull is minimal and almost periodic, and thus uniquely ergodic. It is aperiodic whenever
f
is not time-periodic.

Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=\{t\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,t\geq 0\}
. Given a continuous compact flow
(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
and a
complete metric space
(X,\textsf{d})
, a continuous
skew-product semiflow

(P\times X,\tau,\,\mathbb{R}_{+})
on the product space
P\times X
is determined by a continuous map

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\tau\colon&\mathbb{R}_{+}\times P\times X&\longrightarrow&P\times X\\
&(t,p,x)&\mapsto&(p{\cdot}t,u(t,p,x))\end{array}

which preserves the flow on
P
, called the
base flow
.
The semiflow property means:

(i)

\tau_{0}=\text{Id},

(ii)

\tau_{t+s}=\tau_{t}\circ\tau_{s}
for
t,s\geq 0,

where again
\tau_{t}(p,x)=\tau(t,p,x)
for each
(p,x)\in P\times X
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
This leads to the so-called (nonlinear) semicocycle property,

u(t+s,p,x)=u(t,p{\cdot}s,u(s,p,x))\quad\mbox{for $t,s\geq 0$ and $(p,x)\in P\times X$}.

The set
\{\tau(t,p,x)\mid t\geq 0\}
is the
semiorbit
of
the point
(p,x)
. A subset
K
of
P\times X
is
positively
invariant
if
\tau_{t}(K)\subseteq K

for all
t\geq 0
and it is
\tau
-
invariant
if
\tau_{t}(K)=K

for all
t\geq 0
. A compact
\tau
-invariant set
K
for the
semiflow is
minimal
if it does not contain any nonempty
compact
\tau
-invariant set other than itself.

A compact
\tau
-invariant set
K\subset P\times X
is called a
pinched
set if there exists a residual set
P_{0}\subsetneq P
such that for every
p\in P_{0}
there is a unique element in
K
with
p
in the first component, whereas there are more than one if
p\notin P_{0}
.

The reader can find in Ellis
[
10
]
, Sacker and
Sell
[
30
]
, Shen and Yi
[
32
]
and references therein, a
more in-depth survey on topological dynamics.

We now state the definitions of global and cocycle (or pullback) attractors for skew-product semiflows. The books by Carvalho et al.
[
5
]
and Kloeden and Rasmussen
[
16
]
are good references for this topic.
We say that the skew-product semiflow
\tau
has a
global attractor
if there exists an invariant compact set attracting bounded sets forwards in time, i.e., there is a compact set
\mathbb{A}\subset P\times X
with
\tau_{t}(\mathbb{A})=\mathbb{A}
for any
t\geq 0
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}{\rm dist}_{P\times X}(\tau_{t}(\mathbb{B}),\mathbb{A})=0
for any bounded set
\mathbb{B}\subset P\times X
, for the Hausdorff semidistance
{\rm dist}
on
P\times X
.

Besides, provided that
P
is compact, if
\mathbb{A}
is the global attractor, the non-autonomous set
\{A(p)\}_{p\in P}
, with
A(p)=\{z\in X\mid(p,z)\in\mathbb{A}\}
for each
p\in P
, is a
cocycle attractor
(or a
pullback attractor
). This means that
\{A(p)\}_{p\in P}
is compact, invariant and it pullback attracts all bounded subsets
B\subset X
, that is,
\lim_{t\to\infty}{\rm dist}_{X}(u(t,p{\cdot}(-t),B),A(p))=0
for any
p\in P
(see
[
16
]
).

To finish, we include some basic notions on monotone skew-product semiflows. When the state space
X
is a strongly ordered Banach space, that is, there is a closed convex cone of nonnegative vectors
X_{+}
with a nonempty interior, then, a (partial)
strong order relation
on
X
is
defined by

\begin{split}x\leq y\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad y-x\in X_{+}\,;\\
x<y\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad y-x\in X_{+}\;\text{ and }\;x\neq y\,;\\
x\ll y\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad y-x\in\Int X_{+}\,.\qquad\quad\quad~\end{split}

(2.1)

In this situation, the skew-product semiflow
\tau

is
monotone
if
u(t,p,x)\leq u(t,p,y)
for
t\geq 0
,
p\in P
and
x,y\in X
with
x\leq y
; and it is
strongly monotone
if besides,
u(t,p,x)\ll u(t,p,y)
for
t>0
and
p\in P
, provided that
x<y
.

A Borel map
a:P\to X
such that
u(t,p,a(p))
exists for any
t\geq 0
is said to be

(a)

an
equilibrium
if
a(p{\cdot}t)=u(t,p,a(p))
for any
p\in P
and
t\geq 0
;

(b)

a
sub-equilibrium
if
a(p{\cdot}t)\leq u(t,p,a(p))
for any
p\in P
and
t\geq 0
;

(c)

a
super-equilibrium
if
a(p{\cdot}t)\geq u(t,p,a(p))
for any
p\in P
and
t\geq 0
.

The study of semicontinuity properties of these maps and other related issues can be found in Novo et al.
[
21
]
.

3.
The setting of the problem and some general results

This section contains the description of the parabolic problems under study. Some known results for them are reviewed and some new general results are given.

In this paper we consider a family of time-dependent scalar linear-dissipative parabolic PDEs over a continuous flow on a compact metric space
(P,\sigma,\mathbb{R})
, with Neumann, Robin or Dirichlet boundary conditions, given for each
p\in P
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+h(p{\cdot}t,x)\,y+g(p{\cdot}t,x,y)\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\\[5.69046pt]
By:=\bar{\alpha}(x)\,y+\kappa\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where
p{\cdot}t
denotes the flow on
P
;
U
, the spatial domain, is a bounded, open and
connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
m\geq 1
) with a sufficiently smooth boundary

\partial U
;
\Delta
is the Laplacian operator on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
; the linear coefficient
h:P\times\bar{U}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous
and the nonlinear term
g:P\times\bar{U}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and of class
C^{1}
with respect to
y
and satisfies the following conditions which in particular render the equations dissipative:

(c1)

g(p,x,0)=\displaystyle\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(p,x,0)=0
for any
p\in P
and
x\in\bar{U}
;

(c2)

y\,g(p,x,y)\leq 0
for any
p\in P
,
x\in\bar{U}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
;

(c3)

\displaystyle\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\frac{g(p,x,y)}{y}=-\infty
uniformly on
P\times\bar{U}
;

(c4)

g(p,x,-y)=-g(p,x,y)
for any
p\in P
,
x\in\bar{U}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
;

(c5)

there exists an
r_{0}\geq 0
such that
g(p,x,y)=0
if and only if
|y|\leq r_{0}
.

The problem has Dirichlet boundary conditions if
\kappa=0
and
\bar{\alpha}(x)\equiv 1
; Neumann boundary conditions if
\kappa=1
and
\bar{\alpha}(x)\equiv 0
; and Robin boundary conditions if
\kappa=1
and
\bar{\alpha}:\partial U\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative sufficiently regular map. As usual,

\partial/\partial n
denotes the
outward normal
derivative at the boundary.
As it has been indicated in the introduction, we make a distinction between what we call linear-dissipative problems (
r_{0}>0
) and purely dissipative problems (
r_{0}=0
) in terms of (c5).

Even if no further mention is made, the former regularity conditions on
h
and
g
are always presumed. Nonetheless, sometimes we will assume additional regularity conditions on
g
with respect to the
y
variable. Namely, given a
\delta>0
, an integer
n\geq 1
and a
\beta\in(0,1^{-}]
we write
C^{0,0,n+\beta}(P\times\bar{U}\times[0,\delta])
for the space of real continuous maps
g
on
P\times\bar{U}\times[0,\delta]
which are
n
times continuously differentiable with respect to
y
and the
n^{\rm{th}}
-order partial derivative satisfies that:

\sup\Big\{\frac{|\frac{\partial^{n}g}{\partial y^{n}}(p,x,y_{2})-\frac{\partial^{n}g}{\partial y^{n}}(p,x,y_{1})|}{(y_{2}-y_{1})^{\beta}}\,\Big|\;p\in P,\,x\in\bar{U},\,0\leq y_{1}<y_{2}\leq\delta\Big\}<\infty\,.

Note that
\frac{\partial^{n}g}{\partial y^{n}}
is
\beta
-Hölder continuous uniformly for
p\in P
and
x\in\bar{U}
if
\beta\in(0,1)
, whereas it is Lipschitz continuous if
\beta=1^{-}
. The superscript in
1^{-}
is just used to distinguish the Lipschitz condition from a
C^{1}
character.

Also, on some occasions we will be able to give more complete information by adding a strict sublinearity condition on
g
:

(c6)

g(p,x,\lambda\,y)<\lambda\,g(p,x,y)
for
p\in P
,

x\in\bar{U}
,
y>r_{0}
and
\lambda>1
.

Note that the sublinear case includes the non-autonomous versions of some classical problems, such as the Chafee-Infante equation (see Chafee and Infante
[
6
]
and Carvalho et al.
[
4
]
), which in principle is purely dissipative, but we can allow
r_{0}>0
to get new linear-dissipative versions of this classical problem. Also some Kolmogorov and Fisher models (see Shen and Yi
[
33
]
) provide a field of application of our results, both in the linear-dissipative and purely dissipative settings.

Assuming only conditions (c1), (c2) and (c3) on the nonlinear term
g
in the parabolic PDEs (
3.1
) with Neumann or Robin boundary conditions, plus a minimality condition on the base flow
P
, Cardoso et al.
[
3
]
prove the existence of a global attractor
\mathbb{A}
for the induced skew-product semiflow

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\tau:&\mathbb{R}_{+}\times P\times X&\longrightarrow&\hskip 8.5359ptP\times X\\
&(t,p,z)&\mapsto&(p{\cdot}t,u(t,p,z))\end{array}

defined by the mild solutions of the associated ACPs in
X=C(\bar{U})
(see also
[
2
]
for all the details). The semiflow
\tau
is globally defined because of the boundedness of the solutions, it is strongly monotone, and the section semiflow
\tau_{t}
is compact for every
t>0
. All these results remain valid without the minimality condition on
P
, that is, just assuming that
P
is compact.

The case of Dirichlet boundary conditions has recently been treated in Langa et al.
[
17
]
. The reader is referred to this paper for all the details. The appropriate state space to build the skew-product semiflow
\tau
induced by mild solutions is now
X=C_{0}(\bar{U})
, the set of continuous maps on
\bar{U}
vanishing on the boundary
\partial U
. Once more, under assumptions (c1), (c2) and (c3) there exists a global attractor
\mathbb{A}
for
\tau
. This is a consequence of Proposition 3.4 in
[
17
]
, whose proof works under no further conditions on
P
rather than compactness.

An additional state space is introduced in the Dirichlet case in order to deal with strong monotonicity issues, which is
X^{\alpha}
, a fractional power space associated with the realization of the Laplacian in an
L^{p}(U)
space, and in particular satisfies
X^{\alpha}\hookrightarrow C^{1}(\bar{U})\cap C_{0}(\bar{U})
under some restrictions, namely,
m<p<\infty
and
\alpha\in(1/2+m/(2p),1)
. The (partial) strong order in the Banach space
X^{\alpha}
is defined as in (
2.1
) in association with the cone of positive maps
X^{\alpha}_{+}=\{z\in X^{\alpha}\,\big|\;z(x)\geq 0\;\text{for}\;x\in\bar{U}\}
, which has a nonempty interior:

\Int X^{\alpha}_{+}=\Big\{z\in X^{\alpha}_{+}\,\big|\;z(x)>0\;\text{for}\;x\in U\;\text{and}\;\frac{\partial z}{\partial n}(x)<0\;\text{for}\;x\in\partial U\Big\}\,.

As it is,
u(t,p,z)\in X^{\alpha}
for
t>0
,
p\in P
and
z\in X
, the skew-product semiflow
\tau
is strongly monotone and for each
t>0
the section semiflow
\tau_{t}:P\times X\to P\times X^{\alpha}
is compact. The global attractor
\mathbb{A}\subset P\times X^{\alpha}
and the topologies of
P\times X
and
P\times X^{\alpha}
coincide over
\mathbb{A}
.

Remark 3.1
.

In order to unify the writing for any kind of boundary conditions, we will consider the Banach spaces
(X,\|\,{\cdot}\,\|)
and
(X^{\gamma},\|\,{\cdot}\,\|_{\gamma})
:

- With Neumann or Robin boundary conditions,
X=X^{\gamma}=C(\bar{U})
, always with the sup-norm.

- With Dirichlet boundary conditions,
X=C_{0}(\bar{U})
with the sup-norm, whereas
X^{\gamma}=X^{\alpha}
with the standard
\alpha-
norm
\|\,{\cdot}\,\|_{\alpha}
.

One crucial dynamical element is the upper boundary map of the global attractor, that is, the map
b(p):=\sup A(p)
, for the sections
A(p)
of the attractor
\mathbb{A}
defined in the natural way,
A(p):=\{z\in X^{\gamma}\mid(p,z)\in\mathbb{A}\}
for each
p\in P
. Note that with condition (c4), the lower boundary map
a(p):=\inf A(p)
,
p\in P
satisfies
a(p)=-b(p)
, and
\mathbb{A}\subseteq\bigcup_{p\in P}\{p\}\times[-b(p),b(p)]
. That is why the study is centered on
b
. With any kind of boundary conditions, the map
b:P\to X^{\gamma}
defines a semicontinuous equilibrium for
\tau
, which is thus continuous over the points in an invariant and residual set of
P
. For convenience, we explain this fact further. The main ideas involved can be found in the work by Chueshov
[
7
]
in a random setting, and in Novo et al.
[
21
]
in a deterministic framework.
Let
e_{0}\gg 0
be the eigenfunction associated to the first eigenvalue of the boundary value problem (BVP for short)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta\,u+\lambda\,u=0\,,\quad x\in U,\\
Bu=0\,,\quad x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

with
\|e_{0}\|=1
.
In the case of Neumann or Robin boundary conditions,
b(p)
is obtained as the decreasing limit of a family of continuous super-equilibria (see Proposition 3 in Cardoso et al.
[
3
]
):

b(p)=\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t,p{\cdot}(-t),re_{0})\quad\text{for}\;\,p\in P,

(3.3)

where
r>0
is big enough; whereas with Dirichlet boundary conditions the construction is a bit more delicate and
b
is obtained as the decreasing limit of a family of continuous super-equilibria for an associated discrete semiflow:

b(p):=\lim_{n\to\infty}u(nt_{0},p{\cdot}(-nt_{0}),re_{0})\quad\text{for}\;\,p\in P,

(3.4)

for
r>0
and
t_{0}>0
both big enough. This is done in Proposition 3.5 in Langa et al.
[
17
]
. In fact, it can be derived from its proof that the limit in (
3.3
) exists, so that formula (
3.3
) is also applicable with Dirichlet boundary conditions.

In order to study the upper boundary map
b
, the semiflow can be restricted to the positive cone. Also, in models of applied sciences often only nonnegative solutions make sense. For these reasons, we state the following result. Here, the strong monotonicity of
\tau
is crucial.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c3)
on
g
. The restriction of the semiflow
\tau
to the positive cone, that is, to
P\times X_{+}
, admits a global attractor
\mathbb{A}_{+}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
which is contained in
(P\times\{0\})\cup(P\times\Int X^{\gamma}_{+})
. Besides,
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}_{+}
share the upper boundary map
b
.

Proof.

Take the compact absorbing set
C_{1}\subset P\times X
for
\tau
given in Proposition 2 in
[
3
]
or in Proposition 3.4 in
[
17
]
, respectively for Neumann or Robin, and Dirichlet boundary conditions. It is obvious that
C_{1}\cap(P\times X_{+})
is a compact absorbing set for the restriction of the semiflow
\tau
to the closed and positively invariant set
P\times X_{+}
. Therefore, there exists a global attractor for the restricted semiflow, call it
\mathbb{A}_{+}\subset P\times X_{+}
. Clearly
\mathbb{A}_{+}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
and the trivial null orbit lies inside
\mathbb{A}_{+}
. By the strong monotonicity, if
z>0
, then
u(t,p,z)\gg 0
for any
t>0
and
p\in P
. Now, take a pair
(p,z)\in\mathbb{A}_{+}
with
z\not=0
. Since we have full orbits inside
\mathbb{A}_{+}
, we can write
z=u(t,p{\cdot}(-t),u(-t,p,z))
for
(p{\cdot}(-t),u(-t,p,z))\in\mathbb{A}_{+}
for any
t>0
. It must be
u(-t,p,z)\not=0
since otherwise
z=0
, so that
u(-t,p,z)>0
and then
z\gg 0
, as we wanted to prove. The fact that
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{A}_{+}
share the upper boundary follows from formula (
3.3
) for
b(p)
. The proof is finished.
∎

As a consequence, we get some basic properties for the upper boundary of
\mathbb{A}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c3)
on
g
. Then, for any
p\in P
, either
b(p)=0
or
b(p)\gg 0
. Besides, the sets
P_{0}:=\{p\in P\mid b(p)=0\}

and
P_{+}:=\{p\in P\mid b(p)\gg 0\}
are invariant and, if
R\subseteq P
is the invariant and residual set of continuity points for
b
, then
P_{0}\subseteq R
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, in particular
b(p)=0
or
b(p)\gg 0
for
p\in P
.
Now, if
b(p)=0
for some
p\in P
, for
t>0
,
b(p{\cdot}t)=u(t,p,0)=0
; and also
b(p{\cdot}(-t))=0
, for if not, we would get a contradiction moving forwards. Thus, the set
P_{0}=\{p\in P\mid b(p)=0\}
is invariant and so is its complementary set
P_{+}
.

Finally, if
p_{0}
is such that
b(p_{0})=0
, then
p_{0}
is a point of continuity for
b
due to the semicontinuity of
b
, which in this case means that
\{(p,z)\mid z\leq b(p)\}
is a closed set, and the fact that
b
is always nonnegative.
∎

The size of the sets
P_{0}
and
P_{+}
will be studied in Sections
4
and
5
, at least from an ergodic point of view, depending on the associated principal spectrum. Maintaining the previous notation, we state the next result.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c3)
on
g
. Then:

(i)

P
decomposes as the disjoint union
P=V\cup F
of an open invariant set
V\subseteq P_{+}
and a compact invariant set
F
(both possibly empty) in such a way that, if
F\not=\emptyset
, then
P_{0}=R\cap F
is the residual subset on
F
of continuity points for the restriction map
b_{|F}
.

(ii)

V
is not void if and only if
R\cap P_{+}
is not void.

(iii)

If
p_{0}\in P
is topologically transitive and
b(p_{0})=0
, then
P_{0}=R
and
P=F
.

(iv)

If
P
is minimal, then either
V=\emptyset
or
F=\emptyset
.

(v)

If there is an ergodic measure
\mu
with support
\supp(\mu)=P
and
V\not=\emptyset
, then
\mu(V)=1
and
V
is dense.

Proof.

(i) Let us take the open set
V:=\Int P_{+}
and the compact set
F:=\overline{P_{0}}
. If there exists some
p\in\Int P_{+}
, then there is an open neighbourhood of
p
contained in
P_{+}
, so that it cannot be
p\in\overline{P_{0}}
. On the other hand, if
p\notin\Int P_{+}
, in any open neighbourhood of
p
we can find a point which is not in
P_{+}
and is thus in
P_{0}
, so that
p\in\overline{P_{0}}
. From these facts, we conclude that
V\cap F=\emptyset
and
P=V\cup F
. Since
P_{0}
is known to be invariant, its closure
F
is invariant too, and so is its complementary set
V
. Finally, if
F\not=\emptyset
, it is obvious that
R\cap F
is the residual subset on
F
of continuity points for the restriction map
b_{|F}
and
P_{0}\subseteq R\cap F
by Proposition
3.3
. Conversely, if
p\in R\cap F
, it is immediate to check that
b(p)=0
and
p\in P_{0}
.

(ii) If
V=\Int P_{+}\not=\emptyset
, there is a ball in
P_{+}
, and since the residual set
R
is dense, it must be
R\cap P_{+}\not=\emptyset
. Conversely, if
R\cap P_{+}\not=\emptyset
, by continuity there is a ball inside
P_{+}
, so that
V\not=\emptyset
.

(iii) First of all,
b(p_{0}{\cdot}t)=0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
P_{0}\subseteq R
, by Proposition
3.3
. Argue by contradiction and assume that
P_{0}\subsetneq R
. Then,
R\cap P_{+}\not=\emptyset
or equivalently, by (ii), the open set
V\not=\emptyset
. Since
p_{0}
is topologically transitive,
p_{0}{\cdot}t\in V
for some
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is absurd. In consequence,
P_{0}=R
,
V=\emptyset
and
P=F
.

(iv) If
P_{0}=\emptyset
, then
F=\emptyset
. Else
P=F
and
V=\emptyset
by (iii), since any point in
P
is topologically transitive when
P
is minimal.

(v) If
P=V
, we are done. Else,
\emptyset\not=F\subsetneq P
is a closed set and since
\supp(\mu)=P
,
\mu(V)=\mu(P\setminus F)>0
. By the invariance of
V
and the ergodicity of
\mu
, necessarily
\mu(V)=1
and
\mu(F)=0
. Finally, if
V
were not dense, for some
p\in F
there would be a ball
B(p,r)\subset F
(

), and this cannot happen if
\supp(\mu)=P
.
The proof is finished.
∎

In the description of the attractor
\mathbb{A}
when
P
is minimal and uniquely ergodic and the upper Lyapunov exponent is null (see
[
2
]
), only in the linear-dissipative case it can happen that
b(p)\gg 0
for any
p\in P
, and then
b
is continuous on
P
and thus, uniformly strongly positive. But note that in the general case the upper boundary map
b
is only known to be semicontinuous, and it can happen that
b(p)\gg 0
for any
p\in P
, and
b
is not uniformly strongly positive: see Example
6.1
(ii), where this behaviour is shown for a linear-dissipative family.
In the next result we give a sufficient condition in order to have
b
uniformly strongly above
0
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c3)
on
g
, assume that
b(p)\gg 0
for any
p\in P
and let
R
be the set of continuity points for
b
. Then, if
R\cap M\not=\emptyset
for any minimal set
M\subset P
, there exists a
z_{0}\gg 0
such that
b(p)\geq z_{0}
for any
p\in P
.

Proof.

Let
(M_{i})_{i\in I}
denote the family of minimal sets for the flow in
P
, for an appropriate index set
I
. Assuming the axiom of choice, we can take a
p_{i}\in M_{i}
for each
i\in I
. Now, for each family
(\delta_{i})_{i\in I}
of positive numbers
\delta_{i}>0
, we affirm that

P=\cup_{i\in I}\cup_{t\geq 0}B(p_{i},\delta_{i}){\cdot}t
, where
B(p_{i},\delta_{i})
denotes the open ball centered at
p_{i}
with radius
\delta_{i}
. The argument is simple: if
p\in P
, we consider its
\alpha
-limit set
F_{p}
, which is a compact invariant set and thus contains a minimal set
M_{i}
for some
i\in I
. Then, for the element
p_{i}\in M_{i}\subset F_{p}
, there exists a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\downarrow-\infty
such that
p{\cdot}t_{n}\to p_{i}
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, there exists an integer
n_{0}
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
,
p{\cdot}t_{n}\in B(p_{i},\delta_{i})
or equivalently,
p\in B(p_{i},\delta_{i}){\cdot}(-t_{n})
with
-t_{n}>0
.

At this point, fix a
z\gg 0
and note that we can choose the points
p_{i}\in R\cap M_{i}
,
i\in I
so that they are points of continuity for
b
. Then, for each
i\in I
, since
b(p_{i})\gg 0
, we can take
\delta_{i}>0
and
\varepsilon_{i}>0
such that
b(p)\geq\varepsilon_{i}z
for any
p\in B(p_{i},\delta_{i})
. Since
P=\cup_{i\in I}\cup_{t\geq 0}B(p_{i},\delta_{i}){\cdot}t
, the sets
B(p_{i},\delta_{i}){\cdot}t
are open because the maps
\sigma_{t}:P\to P
,
p\mapsto p{\cdot}t
are homeomorphisms, and
P
is compact, we can extract a finite covering which we can write for simplicity by
P=\cup_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\cup_{j=1}^{n_{2}}B(p_{{i_{k}}},\delta_{{i_{k}}}){\cdot}t_{j}
. Now, since the semiflow is strongly monotone, for any
p\in B(p_{{i_{k}}},\delta_{{i_{k}}})
,
b(p{\cdot}t_{j})=u(t_{j},p,b(p))\geq u(t_{j},p,\varepsilon_{{i_{k}}}z)\geq\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{{i_{k}}j}z
for a certain
\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{{i_{k}}j}>0
. To conclude the proof, it suffices to take
z_{0}=\varepsilon z\gg 0
for
\varepsilon=\min\{\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{{i_{k}}j}\mid 1\leq k\leq n_{1},\,1\leq j\leq n_{2}\}>0
.
∎

3.1.
The linear equations: continuous separation, principal spectrum and Lyapunov exponents

Here we collect some fundamental facts related to the associated linear equations. Recall that
P
is a compact metric space with a continuous flow on it. Let us denote by
\tau_{L}
the linear skew-product semiflow

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\tau_{L}:&\mathbb{R}_{+}\times P\times X&\longrightarrow&\hskip 8.5359ptP\times X\\
&(t,p,z)&\mapsto&(p{\cdot}t,\phi(t,p)\,z)\end{array}

induced by the mild solutions
\phi(t,p)\,z
of the linear ACPs associated to the family of linearized problems along the null solution of (
3.1
), which with condition (c1) is exactly given by the linear part of the problems:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+h(p{\cdot}t,x)\,y\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\;\,\text{for each}\;p\in P,\\[5.69046pt]
By:=\bar{\alpha}(x)\,y+\kappa\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U.\end{array}\right.

(3.5)

In particular
\phi(t,p)
are bounded operators on
X
which are compact for
t>0
and satisfy the linear semicocycle property
\phi(t+s,p)=\phi(t,p{\cdot}s)\,\phi(s,p)
,
p\in P
,
t,s\geq 0
.

In the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, as mentioned before, we can also build a linear skew-product semiflow
\tau_{L}
on the space
P\times X^{\alpha}
. Doing this, we gain the strong positivity of the operators
\phi(t,p)
for
t>0
and
p\in P
. Together with the compact character of the linear operators, this implies that the linear skew-product semiflow
\tau_{L}
admits a continuous separation
X^{\gamma}=X_{1}(p)\oplus X_{2}(p)
for
p\in P
, in the terms stated in Poláčik and Tereščák
[
27
]
and Shen and Yi
[
32
]
. Namely, there are two families of subspaces
\{X_{1}(p)\}_{p\in P}
and
\{X_{2}(p)\}_{p\in P}
of
X^{\gamma}
which satisfy:

(1)

X^{\gamma}=X_{1}(p)\oplus X_{2}(p)
and
X_{1}(p)
,
X_{2}(p)
vary
continuously in
P
;

(2)

X_{1}(p)=\langle e(p)\rangle
, with
e(p)\gg 0
and

\|e(p)\|=1
for any
p\in P
;

(3)

X_{2}(p)\cap X^{\gamma}_{+}=\{0\}
for any
p\in P
;

(4)

for any
t>0
,
p\in P
,

\displaystyle\phi(t,p)\,X_{1}(p)

\displaystyle=X_{1}(p{\cdot}t)\,,

\displaystyle\phi(t,p)\,X_{2}(p)

\displaystyle\subset X_{2}(p{\cdot}t)\,;

(5)

there are
M_{0}>0
,
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
p\in P
,
z\in X_{2}(p)
with
\|z\|_{\gamma}=1
and
t>0
,
\|\phi(t,p)\,z\|_{\gamma}\leq M_{0}\,e^{-\delta_{0}t}\|\phi(t,p)\,e(p)\|_{\gamma}
.

In this situation, the 1-dim invariant subbundle
\bigcup_{p\in P}\{p\}\times X_{1}(p)
is called the
principal bundle
. Note that in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions the vectors
e(p)\in\Int X^{\alpha}_{+}
generating the principal bundle are normalized with respect to the sup-norm. The Sacker-Sell spectrum or continuous spectrum of the restriction of
\tau_{L}
to this invariant subbundle is called the
principal spectrum
of
\tau_{L}
, and is denoted by
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}
(see Mierczyński and Shen
[
18
]
). The classical theory of Sacker and Sell
[
29
]
for finite-dimensional linear skew-product flows says that, provided that
P
is connected, the principal spectrum
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}
is a possibly degenerate compact interval of the real line, that is,
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=[\alpha_{P},\lambda_{P}]
with
\alpha_{P}\leq\lambda_{P}
. The upper end
\lambda_{P}
is the upper Lyapunov exponent of
\tau_{L}
.
It is well-known that if
P
is uniquely ergodic, then
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=\{\lambda_{P}\}
. In what follows, we assume that
P
is compact and connected.

Besides, the continuous separation permits to associate to
h
the 1-dim continuous linear cocycle
c(t,p)
driving the linear dynamics inside the principal bundle, that is, given for
t\geq 0
by the positive number
c(t,p)
such that

\phi(t,p)\,e(p)=c(t,p)\,e(p{\cdot}t)\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,\;p\in P

(3.6)

and by the relation
c(-t,p)=1/c(t,p{\cdot}(-t))
for any
t>0
and
p\in P
. This 1-dim linear cocycle has certainly proved to be a fundamental tool in the dynamical study of the infinite dimensional problems we are dealing with (see
[
2
]
and
[
17
]
).

In what refers to Lyapunov characteristic exponents for the linear dynamics of
\tau_{L}
, we recall the standard definitions for
p\in P
and
z\in X^{\gamma}
,
z\not=0
:

\lambda_{s}^{+}(p,z):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\|\phi(t,p)\,z\|_{\gamma}}{t}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\lambda_{i}^{+}(p,z):=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\|\phi(t,p)\,z\|_{\gamma}}{t}\,.

The subscripts
s
and
i
stand for superior or inferior limits, respectively, and the superscript
+
means that
t\to\infty
.
In the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions, it is important to know that one can argue as in Proposition 3 in Obaya and Sanz
[
25
]
to see that the previous exponents can be equally computed using the sup-norm instead of the norm in
X^{\alpha}
.
Besides, whenever it makes sense, that is, if we are dealing with a linear flow instead of a semiflow, one also defines the exponents
\lambda_{s}^{-}(p,z)
and
\lambda_{i}^{-}(p,z)
by taking limits as
t\to-\infty
. For instance, it makes sense to consider the four Lyapunov characteristic exponents linked to the 1-dim linear cocycle
c(t,p)
, which in this scalar case are independent of
z\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\not=0
:

\lambda_{s}^{\pm}(p):=\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}\quad\text{ and }\quad\lambda_{i}^{\pm}(p):=\liminf_{t\to\pm\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}\,.

In particular, the Lyapunov exponents
\lambda_{s}^{\pm}(p)
and
\lambda_{i}^{\pm}(p)
for
p\in P
lie in
[\alpha_{P},\lambda_{P}]
(see
[
29
]
). Besides, by Theorem 2.3 in Johnson et al.
[
15
]
there are ergodic measures
\nu
and
\mu
for the flow on
P
(they may coincide) such that

\begin{split}\alpha_{P}=&\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}=\lim_{t\to-\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}\quad\text{$\nu$-a.e.},\\
\lambda_{P}=&\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}=\lim_{t\to-\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}\quad\text{$\mu$-a.e.}.\end{split}

(3.7)

On the other hand, the pullback Lyapunov characteristic exponents are defined as follows for linear semiflows over a base flow, denoted with a superscript
′
, and for simplicity we write the definition directly for the sup-norm:

\lambda_{s}^{\prime}(p,z):=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z\|}{t}\,,\quad\lambda_{i}^{\prime}(p,z):=\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z\|}{t}\,.

Due to the existence of a continuous separation, we can calculate the forwards and pullback characteristic exponents of pairs
(p,z)
with
z\gg 0
in terms of the characteristic exponents of the 1-dim cocycle
c(t,p)
as
t\to\infty
or
t\to-\infty
, respectively. The result is stated for the exponents
\lambda_{s}^{+}(p,z)
and
\lambda_{s}^{\prime}(p,z)
, but it can immediately be rephrased for
\lambda_{i}^{+}(p,z)
and
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}(p,z)
just by taking inferior limits instead.

Proposition 3.6
.

For any
p\in P
and any
z\gg 0
,

\lambda_{s}^{+}(p,z)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}=:\lambda_{s}^{+}(p)\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\lambda_{s}^{\prime}(p,z)=\limsup_{t\to-\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p)}{t}=:\lambda_{s}^{-}(p)\,.

Proof.

Fix a
z\in X^{\gamma}
,
z\gg 0
. By the properties of the continuous separation and the compact character of
P
, we can find constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}z\leq e(p)\leq c_{2}z
for any
p\in P
. Applying monotonicity of both
\tau_{L}
and the norm, on the one hand we have that
c_{1}\|\phi(t,p)\,z\|\leq c(t,p)\leq c_{2}\|\phi(t,p)\,z\|
, from where the formula for
\lambda_{s}^{+}(p,z)
follows. On the other hand, we obtain that
c_{1}\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z\|\leq c(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\leq c_{2}\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z\|
, so that

\lambda_{s}^{\prime}(p,z)=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z\|}{t}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln c(t,p{\cdot}(-t))}{t}=\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ln c(-t,p)}{-t}\,,

by the cocycle property of
c(t,p)
. The proof is finished.
∎

4.
Linear-dissipative problems over a compact base flow

In this section we concentrate on the linear-dissipative case, that is, for the family of problems (
3.1
) over a compact and connected base flow
P
we assume the existence of a zone around
0
where the dissipative term is negligible and the problems are linear. In this case the dynamical behaviour of the linear part has strong implications in the general nonlinear dynamics. We keep the notation and terminology introduced in the previous section.

First of all, the 1-dim continuous linear cocycle
c(t,p)
in (
3.6
) permits to characterize the nontrivial sections
A(p)
of the attractor, that is, when
b(p)\gg 0
. Another characterization of a pullback nature is offered, also in terms of the linear dynamics.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c5)
on
g
with
r_{0}>0
in
(c5)
, and let us fix a
z_{0}\in X^{\gamma}
,
z_{0}\gg 0
. Then, given a
p\in P
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

b(p)\gg 0
;

(ii)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq 0}c(t,p)<\infty
;

(iii)

\displaystyle\inf_{t\geq 0}\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}\|>0
.

Proof.

The proof of (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) can be extracted from that of Proposition 5.3 in Caraballo et al.
[
2
]
. To prove (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii), as done in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, we take constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that
c_{1}z_{0}\leq e(p)\leq c_{2}z_{0}
for any
p\in P
. Then, applying the monotonicity of
\tau_{L}
, for any
t\geq 0
,

c_{1}\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}\leq\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,e(p{\cdot}(-t))\leq c_{2}\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}
, and then

c_{1}\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}\leq c(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,e(p)\leq c_{2}\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}
by (
3.6
). Now, since the sup-norm is monotone, for any
t\geq 0
,

c_{1}\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}\|\leq c(t,p{\cdot}(-t))=\frac{1}{c(-t,p)}\leq c_{2}\|\phi(t,p{\cdot}(-t))\,z_{0}\|\,,

from where the equivalence follows immediately. The proof is finished.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

It is important to note that
\sup_{t\leq 0}c(t,p)<\infty
is a necessary condition for

independently of whether
r_{0}>0
or
r_{0}=0
in condition (c5) (once more, see the proof of Proposition 5.3 in
[

]
). However, it is not a sufficient condition in the purely dissipative case. We will return to this issue in Section

.

Next we study some structural properties of the attractor in terms of the location of the principal spectrum
\Sigma_{\text{pr}}=[\alpha_{P},\lambda_{P}]
in the real line. The situations considered correspond to the idea of the principal spectrum being pulled from left to right in the real line. More precisely, for
\alpha_{P}<0
we distinguish the three possible situations (s1) with
\lambda_{P}<0
, (s2) with
\lambda_{P}=0
and (s3) with

\alpha_{P}=0

\alpha_{P}>0
in situation (s5). Note that the case

fits in (s4). The exponential convergence to

when

and the uniform persistence when

are standard results in more general settings: see Mierczyński and Shen
[

and Novo et al.
[

Ergodic measures are always meant with respect to the flow on

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume conditions
(c1)
-
(c5)
on

with
r_{0}>0
in

(s1)

If

(s3)

(s4)

(s5)

Proof.

(s1) The uniform exponential stability has been proved in Proposition 5 in Cardoso et al.
[

]
. Just note that if

lies strictly on the negative real semiaxes. By (

(s2) By (

\nu

for almost every

On the other hand, we now pay attention to the value

. If for some

on

determined by the linear 1-dim cocycle

Then, for any minimal set

Since the same happens for any minimal set, one can apply Lemma 12 and Theorem 2 in Sacker and Sell

(s3) The measure
\nu
is provided by

as in (s2). Now, for
\lambda_{P}>0
we can take an ergodic measure

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\ln c(t,p)/t=\lambda_{P}>0

to affirm that there exists a sequence

for almost every

with respect to

(s4) Maintaining the notation in the proof of (s2), fixed a minimal set

the continuous spectrum of

over

. It might be

by Proposition

. Otherwise

over

and once more by Proposition

b(p)\gg 0
for all such

. Finally, we can argue as in (s3) for
\lambda_{P}>0
.

(s5) We now assume that the principal spectrum is contained in the set of positive real numbers.
(i) First of all, since

lies in the resolvent set of the linear skew-product semiflow

on

for any

Second, by taking

and

for any

\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|b(p{\cdot}t)\|\geq r_{0}

Moreover, taking

r_{1}c(t,p)\,e(p{\cdot}t)(x)\leq r_{0}
for any

and

, and the nontrivial segment

for any

(ii) Let us fix a

\phi(t,p)\,z\geq\frac{1}{c_{2}}\,\phi(t,p)\,e(p)=\frac{1}{c_{2}}\,c(t,p)\,e(p{\cdot}t)\geq\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\,c(t,p)\,z\geq\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}\,c_{0}\,e^{\beta t}\,z\,,

(taking the compact invariant set

∎

Remarks 4.4
.

1. Note that some of the arguments used in the proof identically work in the purely dissipative case, that is, when

), often showing a chaotic behaviour in a precise sense (see Theorem 5.9 in
[
2
]
).

3. Related to the last comment, assume that
\alpha_{P}\leq 0\leq\lambda_{P}
. Assume also that there exists an ergodic measure
\nu
with associated Lyapunov exponent
\lambda=0
, that is,
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\ln c(t,p)/t=0
for almost every

with respect to

. This happens for sure if
\alpha_{P}=0
or if
\lambda_{P}=0
: see (

). In line with a classical result by Shneiberg
[
34

, it can be deduced that for almost every

with respect to

, which depends on

for

(

is said to be recurrent at

, which contains the points

such that

is recurrent at

for every

, is invariant and satisfies that
\nu(P_{\rm{r}})=1
. For all the details, see Proposition 5.5 in
[
2
]
. Arguing as in the proof of Theorem 4.17 (a) in
[
17
]
, if there is a
p\in P_{\rm{r}}
with

, then the trajectory of
(p,b(p))
inside the attractor remains in the zone where the problems are linear, that is,
0\ll b(p{\cdot}t)\leq r_{0}

, meaning that

and

[

]
and
[

]

On the other hand, if

. To see it, take a

as well. Recalling the properties of the continuous separation, we can decompose

Besides, if

) over

with linear coefficient

p\in P

b:P\to X^{\gamma}
is continuous.

Proof.

For (i) just see Theorem
4.3
(s3). As for (ii), if
\alpha_{P}>0
, Theorem
4.3
(s5.i) justifies the application of Theorem
4.5
for any
p\in P
. With this dynamics, if
P
is minimal, the general theory in Núñez et al.
[
24
]
for abstract monotone and sublinear skew-product semiflows on
P\times X^{\gamma}
over a minimal base flow
P
implies that
\{(p,b(p))\mid p\in P\}
is the only strongly positive minimal set and
b
is continuous.

It remains to complete the proof if
P
is not minimal. Recall that
b
is known to be upper semicontinuous, because it is the nonincreasing limit of a family of continuous super-equilibria: see (
3.3
) for Neumann or Robin boundary conditions and (
3.4
) for Dirichlet boundary conditions. So, it suffices to prove that
b
can also be obtained as the nondecreasing limit of a family of continuous sub-equilibria.

With this purpose, let
z_{0}\gg 0
be the vector given in Theorem
4.3
(s5.ii) related to the uniform persistence. For
z_{0}\gg 0
itself there exists a
t_{0}>0
such that
u(t,p,z_{0})\geq z_{0}
for any
t\geq t_{0}
and
p\in P
. In particular
u(nt_{0},p,z_{0})\geq z_{0}
for
p\in P
and
n\geq 1
. This means that the constant map
a_{0}:P\to X^{\gamma}
,
p\mapsto a_{0}(p)=z_{0}
is a continuous sub-equilibrium for the discrete semiflow generated by the iteration of
\tau_{t_{0}}
.
Then, we use the method described in the proof of Theorem 3.6 in Novo et al.
[
21
]
to build, starting from
a_{0}
, a nondecreasing family of continuous sub-equilibria
(a_{n})_{n}:P\to X^{\gamma}
given by
a_{n}(p)=u(nt_{0},p{\cdot}(-nt_{0}),z_{0})
,
p\in P
and there exists
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}(p)=\sup_{n\geq 1}a_{n}(p)=:s(p)
. In particular,
z_{0}\leq s(p)\in A(p)
for
p\in P
, by the pullback attraction of
\mathbb{A}
. The map
s(p)
constructed in this way is lower semicontinuous.

We affirm that for any
p\in P
,
s(p)=\min\{z\in A(p)\mid u(t,p,z)\geq z_{0}\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Let us first check that
u(t,p,s(p))\geq z_{0}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Write
t_{n}=nt_{0}
for
n\geq 1
. If
t\geq 0
, for any
n\geq 1
,

z_{0}\leq u(t+t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})=u(t,p,u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0}))\to u(t,p,s(p))
by the continuity of
u(t,p,\,{\cdot}\,)
for
t\geq 0
, so that
z_{0}\leq u(t,p,s(p))
for
t\geq 0
. Also for
t<0
, for
n
big enough
t+t_{n}\geq t_{0}
and
z_{0}\leq u(t+t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})
. Recall that
(p,s(p))
has a full orbit and let us see that
u(t+t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})\to u(t,p,s(p))
, so that
z_{0}\leq u(t,p,s(p))
. Otherwise, taking a subsequence if necessary,
u(t+t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})\to z_{1}\not=u(t,p,s(p))
, but moving forwards and applying the semicocycle identity and the continuity of
u(-t,p{\cdot}t,\,{\cdot}\,)
,
u(-t,p{\cdot}t,u(t+t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0}))=u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})\to u(-t,p{\cdot}t,z_{1})\not=u(-t,p{\cdot}t,u(t,p,s(p)))=s(p)
, which is absurd. It remains to check that
s(p)\leq z
for any
z\in A(p)
such that
u(t,p,z)\geq z_{0}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since the orbits in
\mathbb{A}
are full, for each
n\geq 1
we can take
(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{n})\in\mathbb{A}
such that
u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{n})=z
. As
z_{0}\leq z_{n}
for
n\geq 1
, by monotonicity
u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{0})\leq u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),z_{n})=z
and taking limits
s(p)\leq z
. As a consequence, note that the sequence
(nt_{0})_{n}
used above can be substituted by any other sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\uparrow\infty
.

Now, it is easy to check that
s
defines an equilibrium for
\tau
: just take
p\in P
,
t\geq 0
and a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that there exist
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{n}-t,p{\cdot}(t-t_{n}),z_{0})=s(p)
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(t-t_{n}),z_{0})=s(p{\cdot}t)
. By the semicocycle property and the continuity of
u(t,p,\,{\cdot}\,)
,
u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(t-t_{n}),z_{0})=u(t,p,u(t_{n}-t,p{\cdot}(t-t_{n}),z_{0}))\to u(t,p,s(p))
as
n\to\infty
and thus,
s(p{\cdot}t)=u(t,p,s(p))
, as wanted.

It only remains to check that
s(p)=b(p)
for any
p\in P
. Have in mind that, whenever we reduce attention to a minimal set
M\subset P
, due to Theorem
4.5
, the general theory in
[
24
]
applied to the semiflow
\tau
restricted to
M\times X^{\gamma}
is only compatible with the fact that
s(p)=b(p)
.

So, let us fix a
p\in P
and consider its
\alpha
-limit set which is a compact invariant set and thus contains a minimal set
M
of
P
. Take a
p_{1}\in M
, recall that
s(p_{1})=b(p_{1})
and choose a sequence of real numbers
(t_{n})_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that
p{\cdot}(-t_{n})\to p_{1}
. By taking a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that
(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),s(p{\cdot}(-t_{n})))_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{A}
and
(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),b(p{\cdot}(-t_{n})))_{n\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{A}
converge, respectively to
(p_{1},z_{1})
and
(p_{1},z_{2})
. Since
s(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}))\leq b(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}))
for
n\geq 1
, with the respective semicontinuity properties of
s
and
b
, we get that
s(p_{1})\leq z_{1}\leq z_{2}\leq b(p_{1})
, so that they all coincide. Then, fixed an
\varepsilon>0
as small as wanted, for sufficiently big
n
,

(1-\varepsilon)\,b(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}))\leq s(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}))\leq b(p{\cdot}(-t_{n}))\,.

Taking
u(t_{n},p{\cdot}(-t_{n}),\,{\cdot}\,)
, the sublinearity and monotonicity of the semiflow and the fact that both
s
and
b
are equilibria for
\tau
lead us to

(1-\varepsilon)\,b(p)\leq s(p)\leq b(p)
, and since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small, we can conclude that
s(p)=b(p)
and the proof is finished.
∎

5.
Purely dissipative problems over a compact base flow

In this section we keep the basic notation and terminology from Section
3
and we consider a family (
3.1
) of purely dissipative parabolic problems over a compact and connected base flow
P
, that is, we assume that
r_{0}=0
in condition (c5) so that the dissipative term
g
is always active. As in the linear-dissipative case, a first goal is to describe the structure of the attractor taking into account the topological and ergodic properties of
P
. This is done once more through principal spectral theory. Recall that
\Sigma_{\rm{pr}}=[\alpha_{P},\lambda_{P}]
is related only to the linear part of the problems.

When
P
is minimal and uniquely ergodic, and thus
\Sigma_{\rm{pr}}=\{\lambda_{P}\}
, the structure of the attractor
\mathbb{A}
has been described, with Neumann or Robin boundary conditions, in Cardoso et al.
[
3
]
for
\lambda_{P}<0
(see Proposition 5) and
\lambda_{P}>0
(see Theorem 11), and in Caraballo et al.
[
2
]
for
\lambda_{P}=0
(see Proposition 5.12). When
\lambda_{P}=0
, the upper boundary map

of

is either identically null so that
\mathbb{A}=P\times\{0\}
, or else it is strongly positive over a set of null measure and of first category of Baire, that is, a small set in both measure and topology senses.
In particular, when the associated 1-dim linear cocycle

satisfies that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\ln c(t,p)|<\infty
for any

[
2
]
that

. This makes it clear that the condition

is no longer sufficient to guarantee that

in the purely dissipative setting. Stronger conditions are needed in this case and to determine them is the second objective in this section.

Some arguments used in the description of
\mathbb{A}
in Section
4
also apply in the present setting, so that we get some parts of the following theorem for free.

Theorem 5.1

(ii) Note that if

and the proof is finished just as before.

(ii) We maintain all the former notation in the proof. This time we only write the proof assuming enough regularity on the coefficients so that mild solutions are classical solutions, but note that in the general case it is necessary to return to the approximation arguments explained in (i).

Let

For further insight into condition (
5.10
), we prove that it presumes an asymptotic behaviour of the 1-dim cocycle as

.

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume that
c(t,p)
is the 1-dim linear cocycle associated to a linear family (
3.5
) over a compact base flow
P
. If for some
\beta>0
and for some
p_{0}\in P
,
c(t,p_{0})^{\beta}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
, then
p_{0}
is an asymptotic point at
-\infty
, i.e.,
\lim_{t\to-\infty}c(t,p_{0})=0
, and
c(t,p_{0})^{\beta^{\prime}}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
for any
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
.

Proof.

As it has already been mentioned in the proof of Theorem
5.5
, every continuous linear cocycle is cohomologous to a smooth one. By the relation between them, it is enough to prove the result assuming that
c(t,p)
is a smooth cocycle, that is,
c(t,p)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}a(p{\cdot}s)ds}
,
p\in P
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
for some map
a\in C(P)
. We argue by contradiction and assume that
c(t,p_{0})
does not tend to
0
as
t\to-\infty
. Then, there exists an
0<\varepsilon_{0}<1
and a sequence
(t_{n})_{n}\downarrow-\infty
such that
c(t_{n},p_{0})\geq\varepsilon_{0}
,
n\geq 1
, so that
\ln c(t_{n},p_{0})\geq\ln\varepsilon_{0}
. Now, the map
\ln c(t,p_{0})=\int_{0}^{t}a(p_{0}{\cdot}s)\,ds
is uniformly continuous on
t\in\mathbb{R}
due to its bounded derivative, and then, we can take a
\delta>0
such that
\ln c(t,p_{0})\geq 2\ln\varepsilon_{0}
for
t\in[t_{n},t_{n}+\delta]
for any
n\geq 1
. That is,
c(t,p_{0})^{\beta}\geq\varepsilon_{0}^{2\beta}
for
t\in[t_{n},t_{n}+\delta]
for any
n\geq 1
. But this is absurd, since then
\int_{-\infty}^{0}c(t,p_{0})^{\beta}\,dt\geq\sum_{n\geq 1}\delta\,\varepsilon_{0}^{2\beta}=\infty
. Consequently,
\lim_{t\to-\infty}c(t,p_{0})=0
.
Finally, if
\beta^{\prime}>\beta
, fixed an
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
t_{0}<0
such that for
t\leq t_{0}
we can write
c(t,p_{0})^{\beta^{\prime}}=c(t,p_{0})^{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}\,c(t,p_{0})^{\beta}\leq\varepsilon^{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}\,c(t,p_{0})^{\beta}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
. The proof is finished.
∎

Remarks 5.7
.

1. We fall into the framework of Theorem
5.5
whenever we start with a single non-autonomous parabolic problem given for
y\geq 0
by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+h_{0}(t,x)\,y-\rho\,y^{\theta}\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\\[5.69046pt]

with

and
\theta>1
. Then, one can build the hull

of

and define

as the shift flow on

, which is compact and connected, and consider the family of problems over the hull (
5.9
), where

is defined by

for

,

, in such a way that we recover the initial problem by taking the element

.

In particular, almost periodic problems provide us with examples of different dynamical behaviours. Let
h_{0}(t)
be an almost periodic function with zero mean value and let

be its hull. If

we can assure that the family (
5.9
) with linear coefficient

(

is the first eigenvalue of the BVP (
3.2
)) has a nontrivial attractor. Note that

is the associated 1-dim cocycle and, by Proposition
5.6
, it satisfies
\lim_{t\to-\infty}c(t,h_{0})=0
. On the other extreme,

might be such that every point

is recurrent at
\pm\infty
: see Example 4.20 in
[

]
for more details. Since the recurrence property is incompatible with the asymptotic character of the cocycle at

that

in those cases.

2. If for some

the pullback Lyapunov exponent

for some

(equivalently, for any

), then (

) holds. To see it, apply Proposition

to get that

. Therefore, given

, there exists a

(

) such that

, so that the integrability condition in (

) is fulfilled for any

. The advantage is that this exponent might be numerically computed, since it depends on the behaviour of the positive solutions of the associated linear problems. But note that

, whereas (

) is compatible with

.

Our next goal is to extend the application of (i) and (ii) in Theorem

to a class as big as possible of families (

states to what extent this can be done. Summing up, the sufficient condition (

Proposition 5.8
.

1.
Assume that

(5.16)

(t_{n})_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that
p{\cdot}t_{n}\in E_{0}
for every
n\geq 1
. That is,
\|b(p{\cdot}t_{n})\|\geq\eta_{0}
for
n\geq 1
and
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\|b(p{\cdot}t)\|>0
, so that whenever condition (c6) is assumed, Theorem
5.2
(ii) applies.
The proof is finished.
∎

6.
Some illustrative examples

Note that the disadvantage of condition (
5.10
) is that normally we do not know the explicit expression for the 1-dim linear cocycle
c(t,p)
.
In this last section we include some easy examples in the linear-dissipative and the purely dissipative contexts, in which we can calculate everything and draw nice conclusions.

Example 6.1
.

Consider the odd, bounded and uniformly continuous map
p_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

p_{0}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\leq-1\\[7.11317pt]
-2\,(t-1)-2\,,&\text{if}\;\,-1\leq t\leq 1\\[2.84544pt]
-\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\geq 1\end{array}\right.

which satisfies that the continuous spectrum for
v^{\prime}=p_{0}(t)\,v
is
\Sigma=\{0\}
, and also
e^{\int_{0}^{t}\!p_{0}}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
. Besides, the integral
\int_{0}^{t}-p_{0}=1+2\,\ln t
grows slower than any
t^{\beta}
(
0<\beta<1
) as
t\to\infty
(this comment comes in relation with Remark
5.4
.3). Also note that the hull of
p_{0}
is the compact and connected set
P
formed by all the translated maps of
p_{0}
, which we denote as usual by
p_{0}{\cdot}t
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
plus the identically null map. Thus, the shift flow on
P
is not a minimal flow, the only minimal set in it is
\{0\}
and
\delta_{\{0\}}
, the Dirac measure at
p=0
, is the only ergodic measure.

(i)
A purely dissipative example with
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=\{0\}
and a homoclinic orbit
. For
\rho>0
,
\theta>1
and
a:P\to\mathbb{R}
,
p\mapsto a(p)=p(0)
, let
\tau(t,p,z)=(p{\cdot}t,u(t,p,z))
be the skew-product semiflow induced by the solutions of the family of purely dissipative problems for
p\in P
, given in the positive cone by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+(\gamma_{0}+a(p{\cdot}t))\,y-\rho\,y^{\theta}\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\\[5.69046pt]
By:=\bar{\alpha}(x)\,y+\kappa\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

(6.1)

where
\gamma_{0}
is the first eigenvalue of the BVP (
3.2
) with associated normalized eigenfunction
e_{0}\gg 0
. It is easy to check that the principal bundle is given by
X_{1}(p)=\langle e_{0}\rangle
for all
p\in P
and
c(t,p)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}a(p{\cdot}s)ds}
is the 1-dim linear cocycle, so that the principal spectrum is the degenerate interval
\{0\}
, i.e.,
\alpha_{P}=\lambda_{P}=0
and Theorem
5.10
(s4) applies. Thus, there exists an ergodic measure
\nu
in
P
such that
b(p)=0
for almost all
p
with respect to
\nu
. Since necessarily
\nu=\delta_{\{0\}}
, it follows that
b(0)=0
. By Proposition
3.3
,
p=0
is a point of continuity for
b
. Since
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}p_{0}{\cdot}t=0
, then
\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}b(p_{0}{\cdot}t)=0
. Thus, looking at
(0,b(0))=(0,0)
as an equilibrium point for the skew-product semiflow,
\{(p_{0}{\cdot}t,b(p_{0}{\cdot}t))\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a homoclinic orbit.

Now, for the initial map
p_{0}\in P
we can calculate the values
\theta>1
for which
c(t,p_{0})^{\theta-1}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
, in order to guarantee that
b(p_{0})\gg 0
by Theorem
5.5
(i). Some routine calculations show that the previous condition holds if and only if
\theta>3/2
. Therefore, for any
\theta>3/2
,
b(p_{0})\gg 0
, and thus also
b(p_{0}{\cdot}t)\gg 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and the homoclinic orbit is nontrivial. However, for
1<\theta\leq 3/2
, at least with Neumann or Robin boundary conditions, by Theorem
5.5
(ii) we know that
b(p_{0})=0
and the attractor is the trivial one,
\mathbb{A}=P\times\{0\}
.

(ii)
A linear-dissipative example with
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=\{0\}
and strongly positive upper boundary map, not bounded away from
0
and discontinuous
. Based on the previous purely dissipative example, we build a linear-dissipative family of problems for
p\in P
sharing the linear term, and thus the associated 1-dim cocycle
c(t,p)
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+(\gamma_{0}+a(p{\cdot}t))\,y+g(y)\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\\[5.69046pt]
By:=\bar{\alpha}(x)\,y+\kappa\,\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

for a map
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{1}
which satisfies (c1)-(c5) with
r_{0}>0
in (c5) and such that
g(y)\geq-\rho\,y^{\theta}
for
y\geq 0
, for
\rho>0
and
\theta>3/2
. Then, denoting by
\widetilde{\tau}(t,p,z)=(p{\cdot}t,\widetilde{u}(t,p,z))
the induced skew-product semiflow and by
\widetilde{b}(p)
the upper boundary map of the attractor, since
\widetilde{u}(t,p,z)\geq u(t,p,z)
for
t\geq 0
,
p\in P
and
z\geq 0
, then
\widetilde{b}(p)\geq b(p)
for
p\in P
(once more, see (
3.3
)), so that in particular
\widetilde{b}(p_{0}{\cdot}t)\gg 0
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This time also
\widetilde{b}(0)\gg 0
, since for
p=0
,
c(t,0)\equiv 1
, and Proposition
4.1
applies. Besides, taking
\beta>0
such that
\widetilde{b}(p_{0})\leq\beta\,e_{0}
and comparing the solutions of the nonlinear problem and the linear one,
\widetilde{b}(p_{0}{\cdot}t)=\widetilde{u}(t,p,\widetilde{b}(p_{0}))\leq\phi(t,p)\,\beta\,e_{0}=\beta\,c(t,p_{0})\,e_{0}=\beta\,e^{-1-2\ln t}e_{0}\to 0
as
t\to\infty
. In all, we have an example in which
\widetilde{b}(p)\gg 0
for any
p\in P
, but
\widetilde{b}
is not uniformly strongly above
0
and it is discontinuous at
p=0
.

Next, inspired in an example in Mierczyński and Shen
[
18
]
(p. 203-204), we modify the map
p_{0}(t)
in the previous example in order to obtain a heteroclinic orbit in the global attractor. For convenience, we restrict ourselves to the case of Chafee-Infante equations with Neumann boundary conditions.

Example 6.2
.

Consider the bounded and uniformly continuous map
p_{1}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

p_{1}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\displaystyle\frac{2}{t}\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\leq-1\\[7.11317pt]
2\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\geq-1\,.\end{array}\right.

This time, the hull of
p_{1}
is the compact and connected set
P
formed by all the translated maps of
p_{1}
, which we denote by
p_{1}{\cdot}t
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, plus the identically null map and the identically equal to
2
map. More precisely,
p_{1}{\cdot}t\to 0
as
t\to-\infty
and
p_{1}{\cdot}t\to 2
as
t\to\infty
. Again, the shift flow on
P
is not minimal and the only minimal sets are
\{0\}
and
\{2\}
, as well as the only ergodic measures are the Dirac measures
\delta_{\{0\}}
and
\delta_{\{2\}}
. Besides, for
a:P\to\mathbb{R}
,
p\mapsto a(p)=p(0)
, the continuous spectrum or Sacker-Sell spectrum of the family of linear equations over the hull,
v^{\prime}=a(p{\cdot}t)\,v
,
p\in P
is the interval
[0,2]
.

(i)
A purely dissipative family with
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[0,1]
and a heteroclinic orbit
.
Let us consider the family for
p\in P
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\Delta\,y+\frac{1}{2}\,a(p{\cdot}t)\,y-\frac{1}{2}\,y^{3}\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in U,\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial y}{\partial n}=0\,,\quad t>0\,,\;\,x\in\partial U,\end{array}\right.

and denote by
\tau
the induced skew-product semiflow. Note that in the Neumann case
\gamma_{0}=0
and the associated 1-dim cocycle is
c(t,p)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\,a(p{\cdot}s)ds}
with principal spectrum
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[0,1]
. Besides, for
p=0
and
p=2
we have two equilibrium points, namely
(0,0)
and
(2,\sqrt{2})
, for the skew-product semiflow. In particular this implies that
b(2)\gg 0
. In what respects to
p_{1}
, since it coincides with the map
p_{0}
in Example
6.1
for times
t\leq-1
,
e^{\int_{0}^{t}\!p_{1}}\in L^{1}((-\infty,0])
. Then, as
c(t,p_{1})^{2}=e^{\int_{0}^{t}\!p_{1}}
, Theorem
5.5
(i) with
\theta=3
implies that
b(p_{1})\gg 0
, so that
b(p_{1}{\cdot}t)\gg 0
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Besides, Theorem
5.10
(s4) applies and, with the former description, it must be
\nu=\delta_{\{0\}}
and
b(0)=0
. In particular
p=0
is a continuity point for
b
and
\lim_{t\to-\infty}(p_{1}{\cdot}t,b(p_{1}{\cdot}t))=(0,0)
. Finally,
\lim_{t\to\infty}(p_{1}{\cdot}t,b(p_{1}{\cdot}t))=(2,\sqrt{2})
, so that the orbit
\{(p_{1}{\cdot}t,b(p_{1}{\cdot}t))\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
connects both equilibria. To see this, we turn to the explicit expression of
b(p_{1}{\cdot}t)
which can be extracted from the proof of Lemma
5.3
and we do some routine calculations.

(ii)
A linear-dissipative example with
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[0,1]
and strongly positive upper boundary map
. As in Example
6.1
(ii), we now build a linear-dissipative family for
p\in P
, majorating the previous purely dissipative family in the positive cone and sharing its linear part, so that in particular
\Sigma_{\rm{pr}}=[0,1]
. Then, if
\widetilde{b}(p)
denotes the upper boundary map of the attractor,
\widetilde{b}(p)\gg 0
for any
p\in P
: the only doubt might be for
\widetilde{b}(0)
, but the cocycle for
p=0
is
c(t,0)\equiv 1
, and Proposition
4.1
applies in the linear-dissipative setting.

To finish, we present a final example to show that in the purely dissipative case, with principal spectrum
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[\alpha_{P},0]
with
\alpha_{P}<0
, the attractor might be the trivial one
\mathbb{A}=P\times\{0\}
: see Theorem
5.10
(s2) and compare with Theorem
4.3
(s2), saying that this cannot happen in the linear-dissipative context.

Example 6.3
.

A purely dissipative family with
\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[-1,0]
and a trivial attractor
.
Consider the bounded and uniformly continuous map
p_{2}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

p_{2}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\leq-1\\[7.11317pt]
-1\,,&\text{if}\;\,t\geq-1\,.\end{array}\right.

This time, the hull of
p_{2}
is the compact and connected set
P
formed by all the translated maps of
p_{2}
,
\{p_{2}{\cdot}t\mid t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, plus the identically null map and the identically equal to
-1
map. Moreover,
p_{2}{\cdot}t\to 0
as
t\to-\infty
and
p_{2}{\cdot}t\to-1
as
t\to\infty
. Again, the shift flow on
P
is not minimal and the only minimal sets are
\{0\}
and
\{-1\}
, as well as the only ergodic measures are the Dirac measures
\delta_{\{0\}}
and
\delta_{\{-1\}}
. Besides, for
a:P\to\mathbb{R}
,
p\mapsto a(p)=p(0)
, the Sacker-Sell spectrum of the family of linear equations over the hull,
v^{\prime}=a(p{\cdot}t)\,v
,
p\in P
is the interval
[-1,0]
.

For
\rho>0
and
\theta>1
, let
\tau
be the skew-product semiflow induced by the solutions of the family of purely dissipative problems for
p\in P
, given in the positive cone by (
6.1
),
where
\gamma_{0}
is the first eigenvalue of the BVP (
3.2
). Recall that then
c(t,p)=e^{\int_{0}^{t}a(p{\cdot}s)ds}
is the associated 1-dim linear cocycle, so that the principal spectrum is

\Sigma_{{\rm pr}}=[-1,0]
. Let us see that
b(p)=0
for all
p\in P
. First of all note that for
p=0
, the restricted family over the minimal set
M=\{0\}
which is uniquely ergodic, has trivial bounded cocycle
c(t,0)=1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
and null Lyapunov exponent. Thus, by Proposition 5.12 in
[
2
]
we know that
b(0)=0
.

Finally, it is straightforward that both
c(t,-1)
and
c(t,p_{2})
tend to
\infty
as
t\to-\infty
. Then, by Remark
4.2
,

b(-1)=b(p_{2})=0
, and then also
b(p_{2}{\cdot}t)=0
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and we are done.

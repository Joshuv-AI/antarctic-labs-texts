A Note on Systems of Realizations on Shimura Varieties
Ziquan Yang

Abstract
Let ( G , Ω ) (G,\Omega) be a Shimura datum of abelian type. It is well known that the corresponding Shimura variety Sh ⁡ ( G , Ω ) \mathrm{Sh}(G,\Omega) should be a moduli space of abelian motives equipped with some additional structures. In this half-expository note, we give under some simplifying assumptions a moduli interpretation of Sh ⁡ ( G , Ω ) \mathrm{Sh}(G,\Omega) over the reflex field purely in terms of systems of realizations. The main purpose is to introduce some convenient formalism that can be used to avoid the technicalities about dealing with various notions of families of motives.

1
Introduction

It is well known that Shimura varieties of abelian type are supposed to be moduli spaces of “abelian motives equipped with centain additional structures”. The purpose of this note is to provide a reference for a precise statement, under some simplifying assumptions. Let
(G,\Omega)
be a Shimura datum of abelian type with reflex field
E
, and for each compact open subgroup
\mathsf{K}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, denote the Shimura variety
G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\Omega\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/\mathsf{K}
by
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)_{\mathbb{C}}
and its canonical model over
E
by
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)
. Let
\mathrm{Sh}(G,\Omega)
be the limit
\varprojlim_{\mathsf{K}}\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)
as
\mathsf{K}
runs through compact open subgroups.

Before giving a moduli interpretation, we first show that:

Theorem A
.

(see also
(4.2.5)
)

Assume that
(G,\Omega)
has
\mathbb{Q}
-rational weight, the center of
G
has no anisotropic torus split over
\mathbb{R}
, and
(G,\Omega)
has a nice Hodge-type cover (see
(4.2.1)
). Then there is a natural functor from the category
\mathrm{Rep}(G)
of finite-dimensional
G
-representations over
\mathbb{Q}
to the category of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
-equivariant weakly abelian-motivic systems of realizations over
\mathrm{Sh}(G,\Omega)
.

Roughly speaking, a system of realizations is a cohomological avatar of a “genuine” family of motives. For a subfield
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and a smooth
k
-variety
S
, a system of realizations is given by a variation of Hodge structures (VHS) over
S_{\mathbb{C}}
whose de rham and étale components descend to
S
(see
(3.1.1)
). The advantage of working with systems of realizations, as opposed to “genuine” families of motives, is that the former is defined purely in terms of sheaves so that its pullback and descent properties are well understood (see also
(3.1.5)
). The only price to pay is that we need to explicitly keep track of a compatability condition between the descent data of the étale and de Rham realizations of the VHS in a system. This is why we introduce the notion of
weakly abelian-motivic
systems of realizations (see
(3.2.3)
), which are those whose descent data are controlled by abelian motives. It is a pointwise definition and may look a little odd at first glance, but we hope the reader will find it actually very flexible to work with once one gets used to it (see
(3.2.5)
).

Theorem B
.

Let
(G,\Omega)
be as in Theorem A and
\mathsf{K}
be a neat compact open subgroup. Then for every faithful
G
-representation
V
and smooth
E
-variety
S
, there is a natural bijection between
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)(S)
and isomorphism classes of pairs
(\mathsf{W},[\xi])
such that
\mathsf{W}
is a weakly abelian-motivic system of realizations over
S
, and
[\xi]
is a
\mathsf{K}
-level structure which is
\mathsf{W}
-rational and is of type
\Omega
.

A more precise version is stated in
(4.3.1)
. The meaning of “
\mathsf{W}
-rational” and “of type
\Omega
” are explained in §
3.3
, and the motivation for these definitions is explained in
(4.1.4)
. The moduli interpretation of the complex Shimura variety
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)_{\mathbb{C}}
in terms of VHS equipped with additional structures (
[
10
, Prop. 3.10]
) follows relatively easily from the complex uniformization
G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\Omega\times G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/\mathsf{K}
and standard results due to Baily and Borel. This note is mainly focused on the canonical model
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G,\Omega)
over the reflex field. Morally of course Theorem B should follow from
[
10
, Thm 3.31]
. Unfortunately, some proofs in
loc. cit.
seem to be only sketched, and there are some technical subtleties about the notion of a family of motives used there that should be further explained (see also
(3.1.5)
).

The author hopes that the notion of weakly abelian-motivic systems of realizations set up in this note gives a convenient alternative formalism for one to fill in the small gaps in literature. Our general approach here is inspired by
[
4
, §2.2]
and
[
8
, §5.24]
. We expect that the simplifying assumption of the existence of a “nice Hodge-type cover”
(4.2.1)
can be removed by suitably adapting some constructions in
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
, and
[
9
]
, if needed.

Preliminaries

2.1 Generalities on Galois Descent

We will make heavy use of Galois descent arguments, so we first spell out some tautology and set up some notations to facilitate discussion. We do not intend to state the results in utmost generality, but only the simple cases we need.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
and
L
is an algebraically closed field over
k
. We allow
L
to have infinite transcendence degree over
k
, e.g.,
L=\mathbb{C}
and
k
is a number field. Let
X_{0}
be a smooth
k
-variety. Let
X
be the base change
X_{0}{\otimes}_{k}L
. Note that there is a canonical identification
X(L)=X_{0}(L)
. Given a
k
-linear morphism
s:\mathrm{Spec\,}(L)\to X_{0}
(i.e., an element of
X_{0}(L)
) and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(L/k)
, let
\sigma(s)
be the composition
\mathrm{Spec\,}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{Spec\,}(\sigma)}}{{\to}}\mathrm{Spec\,}(L)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s}}{{\to}}X_{0}
. Then
s\mapsto\sigma(s)
defines the
\mathrm{Aut}(L/k)
-action on
X_{0}(L)
, and thus on
X(L)
via the identificaton
X(L)=X_{0}(L)
above.

(2.1.1)

Suppose now that
\mathcal{F}\in\mathsf{Loc}(X)
. A descent of
\mathcal{F}
to
X_{0}
is a pair
(\mathcal{F}_{0}\in\mathsf{Loc}(X_{0}),\alpha:\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{X}\,{\cong}\,\mathcal{F})
. For every
x\in X(L)
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(L/k)
, the descent
(\mathcal{F}_{0},\alpha)
gives us an isomorphism of stalks
\mathcal{F}_{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{F}_{\sigma(x)}
, which we label as
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}_{0},x}
. Indeed, when we write
\mathcal{F}_{x}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma(x)}
, we are viewing
x
as an element of
X(L)
, but via the identification
X(L)=X_{0}(L)
we also have identifications
\mathcal{F}_{x}=\mathcal{F}_{0,x}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\sigma(x)}=\mathcal{F}_{0,\sigma(x)}
. By applying
[
14
, 04FN]
to
(X_{0},x,\sigma(x))
, we obtain a canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{F}_{0,x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{F}_{0,\sigma(x)}
. This gives
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}_{0},x}
. If
x_{0}\in X_{0}
is the image of the geometric point
x
, then
x=\sigma(x)
for
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(k(x)/k(x_{0}))=\mathrm{Aut}(L/k(x_{0}))
. The collection
\{\sigma_{\mathcal{F}_{0},x}\}_{\mathrm{Aut}(L/k(x_{0}))}
gives precisely the
\mathrm{Aut}(L/k(x_{0}))
-action on the stalk
\mathcal{F}_{0,x}
(
[
14
, 03QW]
).

Similarly, for
\mathcal{V}\in\mathsf{Vect}(X)
, a descent of
\mathcal{V}
to
X_{0}
is a pair
(\mathcal{V}_{0}\in\mathsf{Vect}(X_{0}),\beta:\mathcal{V}_{0}|_{X}\,{\cong}\,\mathcal{V})
, which gives at each
x\in X(L)
a
\sigma
-linear isomorphism
\sigma_{\mathcal{V}_{0},x}:\mathcal{V}_{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(x)}
. A global section
\mathbf{s}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{F})
(resp.
\mathbf{t}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{V})
) descends to
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{F}_{0})
(resp.
\mathrm{H}^{0}(X_{0},\mathcal{V}_{0})
) if and only if for every
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(L/k)
and
x\in X(L)
,
\sigma_{\mathcal{F}_{0},x}(\mathbf{s}_{x})=\mathbf{s}_{\sigma(x)}
(resp.
\sigma_{\mathcal{V}_{0},x}(\mathbf{t}_{x})=\mathbf{t}_{\sigma(x)}
).

We often omit the identification
\alpha
from the notation, and simply say that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a descent of
\mathcal{F}
. However, we emphasize that as an object of
\mathsf{Loc}(X_{0})
,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
may certainly have nontrivial automorphisms, but there is no nontrivial automorphism, say
g\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{F}_{0})
, such that
\alpha\circ(g|_{X}:\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{X})=\alpha
. That is, as a descent of
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}_{0}
cannot have nontrivial automorphisms. A similar remark applies to descent of vector bundles. Therefore, if we say that two descents of a fixed object in
\mathsf{Loc}(X)
or
\mathsf{Vect}(X)
to
X_{0}
are isomorphic, then they are
uniquely isomorphic
.

(2.1.2)

Let
X,X_{0},\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{0},\alpha:\mathcal{F}_{0}|_{X}\,{\cong}\,\mathcal{F})
be as above. Suppose that

is a finite étale cover of another smooth

-variety

with

admits a descent

. A

of

to

is a triple
(\mathcal{G}_{0}\in\mathsf{Loc}(Y_{0}),\xi:\mathcal{G}_{0}|_{X_{0}}\,{\cong}\,\mathcal{F}_{0},\zeta:\mathcal{G}_{0}|_{Y}\,{\cong}\,\mathcal{G})
such that the diagram on the right below commutes:

2.2 Motives and Absolute Hodge Cycles

Let
k
be a field of characteristic
0
. We denote by
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)
the neutral
\mathbb{Q}
-linear Tannakian category of motives over
k
for absolute Hodge cycles (cf.
[
13

\mathbf{1}(n)
for every

in this category. For any object

\mathbf{1}\to M

Following
[
7
, §1]

\omega_{\ell}
the
\ell
-adic realization functor which sends
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)
to the category of finite dimensional
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
-vector spaces with an action of
\mathrm{Gal}_{k}:=\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)

\bar{k}
is some chosen algebraic closure of
k
. Putting
\omega_{\ell}

\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
, which takes values in the category of finite free
\mathbb{A}_{f}
-modules with a
\mathrm{Gal}_{k}
-action. Let
\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}
denote the de Rham realization functor, which takes values in the category of filtered
k
-vector spaces. If
k=\mathbb{C}
, we additionally consider the Betti realization
\omega_{B}
(resp. the Hodge realization
\omega_{\mathrm{Hdg}}
) which takes values in the category of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces (resp. Hodge structures).

Since the operations of taking duals, tensor products, and symmetric and exterior powers make sense for the Tannakian category
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)
and are compatible with the cohomological realizations, it makes sense to say whether a tensor
s\in(\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M)\times\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M))^{\otimes}
(resp.

) is absolute Hodge for
M\in\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)
(resp.
M\in\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(\mathbb{C})
, when

).

Let
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(k)\subseteq\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)
be the full Tannakian sub-category generated by the Artin motives and the motives attached to abelian varieties.
As in
loc. cit.
, we will repeatedly make use of the following fact:

Theorem (2.2.1)
.

(
[
2
, Ch 1]
)

The functor
\omega_{\mathrm{Hdg}}
is fully faithful when restricted to
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(\mathbb{C})
. In particular, for every
M\in\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(\mathbb{C})
, every element
s\in\omega_{B}(M)\cap\mathrm{Fil}^{0}\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)
is given by an absolute Hodge cycle.

We often refer to objects in
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathsf{Ab}}(k)
as abelian motives.

(2.2.2)

If
L
is an extension of
k
, then there is a natural faithful functor of Tannakian categories
-{\otimes}_{k}L:\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(k)\to\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(L)
compatible with fiber functors. A construction we shall often consider is the following: Let
M
be an object of
\mathsf{Mot}_{\mathrm{AH}}(\mathbb{C})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
. We denote

by

. Base change properties of étale and de Rham cohomology give us canonical isomorphisms
\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M)\,{\cong}\,_{\mathrm{bc}}\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M^{\sigma})
and
\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\,{\cong}\,_{\mathrm{bc}}\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M^{\sigma})
. Note that the latter is
\sigma
-linear. Here the supscript “bc” stands for “base change”, and we add this supscript when we need to distinguish these isomorphisms from other potentially different isomorhisms (e.g., in most Galois descent arguments). If

is absolute Hodge, then we write

for the (necessarily unique) element in
\omega_{B}(M^{\sigma})^{\otimes}
such that
s
and
s^{\sigma}
have the same étale and de Rham realizations via the isomorphisms
\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M)\,{\cong}\,_{\mathrm{bc}}\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M^{\sigma})
and
\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)\,{\cong}\,_{\mathrm{bc}}\omega_{\mathrm{dR}}(M^{\sigma})
.

3
Systems of Realizations

3.1 Basic Definitions

Definition (3.1.1)
.

Let
k
be a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
and
S
be a smooth
k
-variety. By a system of realizations we mean a tuple
\mathsf{V}=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}},i_{\mathrm{dR}},i_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
where

•

\mathsf{V}_{B}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-local system over
S_{\mathbb{C}}:=S{\otimes}_{k}\mathbb{C}
;

•

\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}
is a filtered flat vector bundle over
S
;

•

\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
is an étale local system of
\mathbb{A}_{f}
-coefficients over
S
;

•

i_{\mathrm{dR}}
is an isomorphism of flat vector bundles
(\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S_{\mathbb{C}}},\mathrm{id}{\otimes}d)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}})^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}})
forms a pure
polarizable
VHS;

•

i_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
is an isomorphism
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
.

We may often omit
(i_{\mathrm{dR}},i_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
in the notation and simply write
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}=\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}^{\mathrm{an}}=\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
. There are some changes of topology here: Technically,
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
is defined for the analytic topology, but it has a canonically associated étale local system, which we also denote by
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
. Similarly, we often do not distinguish
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
with its analytification
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{an}}_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
, because over a smooth complex variety there is a natural equivalence of categories between flat algebraic vector bundles with regular singularieties and flat holomorphic vector bundles (
[
1
, Thm II.5.9]
). In particular, a flat global section of
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathcal{O}_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}^{\mathrm{an}}
is also a flat global section of
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
.

(3.1.2)

If
k^{\prime}
is another subfield of
\mathbb{C}
which contains
k
,
S^{\prime}
is a
k^{\prime}
-variety, and there is a
k
-linear morphism of schemes
S^{\prime}\to S
, then we can naturally form the restriction
\mathsf{V}|_{S^{\prime}}
(or pullback) of
\mathsf{V}\in\mathsf{R}(S)
to
S^{\prime}
. Indeed,
S^{\prime}{\otimes}_{k^{\prime}}\mathbb{C}
is the same as
S{\otimes}_{k}\mathbb{C}
, so the Betti component
\mathsf{V}_{B}
stays the same. Then we simply restrict
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}
and
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
to
S^{\prime}
. If
k=\mathbb{C}
, we do not distinguish
\mathsf{R}(S)
with the category of polarizable VHS, as these two categories the naturally equivalent.

(3.1.3)

Our definition looks slightly different from
[
3
, Def. 6.1]
(see also
[
10
, §3.41]
). However, if
K
is a number field (which is no longer equipped with a chosen embedding into
\mathbb{C}
) and
S
is a smooth
K
-variety, then a system of realizations over
S
as defined in
[
3
, Def. 6.1]
is equivalent to a system of realizations over
S_{(\mathbb{Q})}
in our definition, where
S_{(\mathbb{Q})}
is the
\mathbb{Q}
-variety obtained by composing the structural morphism of
S
with
\mathrm{Spec\,}(K)\to\mathrm{Spec\,}(\mathbb{Q})
.

(3.1.4)

With the obvious definitions of morphisms, tensor products and duals, systems of realizations over a smooth variety
S
over
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
form a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear Tannakian category
\mathsf{R}(S)
. We have Tate objects
\mathbf{1}(n)
and denote
\mathsf{V}(n)
by
\mathsf{V}{\otimes}\mathbf{1}(n)
(cf. the discussion below
loc. cit.
). We write
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathsf{V})
for the
\mathbb{Q}
-module
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbf{1},\mathsf{V})
. Concretely, an element in
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathsf{V})
is given by a triple
(\mathbf{s}_{B},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
where
\mathbf{s}_{B}\in\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathsf{V}_{B})
is a global section which is everywhere of Hodge type
(0,0)
, and
\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{dR}}
and
\mathbf{s}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
are respectively global sections in
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}})
and
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
whose restrictions over
S_{\mathbb{C}}
are the de Rham and étale realizations of
\mathbf{s}_{B}
.

Remark (3.1.5)
.

We introduce systems of realizations mainly to circumvent the problem of not having a definition of a family of motives in literature that has been documented to have satisfactory pullback and descent properties. For example, if one uses
[
10
, Def. 2.37]
, then it is not a priori clear how to perform pullback along non-dominant morphisms, though when we restrict to considering abelian motives over smooth
\mathbb{C}
-varieties this definition has nice descent properties (e.g., combine Lem. 2.33 and Prop. 2.42 in
loc. cit.
). On the other hand,
[
12
, Def. 4.3.3]
is amenable to defining pullback, but its descent properties have not been discussed.

3.2 Characterizing “abelian-motivic” systems

Definition (3.2.1)
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. We say that a system of realizations
\mathsf{V}
over
S
is
strongly abelian motivic (strongly AM)
if there exists another system
\mathsf{H}
which is given by the first cohomology of an abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}/S
, a realization
\mathsf{H}^{\natural}
that is a direct sum of those constructed out of
\mathsf{H}
by taking Tate twists, duals, tensor products, exterior and symmetric powers, as well as an idempotent projector
\boldsymbol{\pi}=(\boldsymbol{\pi}_{B},\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\mathrm{dR}},\boldsymbol{\pi}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}):\mathsf{H}^{\natural}\to\mathsf{H}^{\natural}
such that
\mathsf{V}
is isomorphic to the image of
\boldsymbol{\pi}
. We write the full subcategory of
\mathsf{R}(S)
consisting of such objects as
\mathsf{R}^{\circ}_{\mathrm{am}}(S)
.

We say that
\mathsf{V}
is
étale-locally
strongly AM, if for some finite étale cover
S^{\prime}\to S
of
k
-varieties, the restriction
\mathsf{V}|_{S^{\prime}}
is strongly AM. We write the full subcategory of
\mathsf{R}(S)
consisting of such objects as
\mathsf{R}_{\mathrm{am}}(S)
.

Remark (3.2.2)
.

In
[
10
]
, there is also a similar notion of an abelian motivic VHS (Def. 2.30), and a notion of an abelian motive on a smooth connected variety over a field of characteristic

(Def. 2.37). To distinguish with Milne’s definitions, we added “strongly” to indicate that we ask the relevant abelian scheme

to be defined over the entire

, as opposed to just an open dense subset of

. One reason for doing so is that being strongly AM is clearly stable under base change along any morphisms between smooth
k
-varieties, rather than just the dominant ones (see also

The following definition is an extension of

.

[\eta]
on
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
is said to be
rigid
on
\mathsf{V}
if for any

, there is no nontrivial automorphism of
\mathsf{V}_{B,s}
which preserves the Hodge filtration on
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},s}
and the level structure
[\eta]_{s}
.

3.4 Galois Descent Lemmas

Lemma (3.4.1)
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over a field
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and take
\mathsf{V},\mathsf{W}\in\mathsf{R}_{\mathrm{am}}^{*}(S)
. Let
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
be a morphism
\mathsf{V}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\to\mathsf{W}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
. Then
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
descends to a morphism
\mathsf{V}\to\mathsf{W}
if and only if either the étale or the de Rham component of
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
descends to
S
.

Proof.

Considering
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
as a global section of
(\mathsf{V}^{\vee}{\otimes}\mathsf{W})|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
, we know that either its étale or de Rham component descent to
S
. By
(3.2.6)
this implies that
\varphi_{\mathbb{C}}
itself descends to
S
.
∎

Corollary (3.4.2)
.

Let
S
be a smooth variety over a field
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that
(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathbb{C}})
is a VHS over
S_{\mathbb{C}}
equipped with a descent
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
of
\mathsf{V}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
to
S
. If there exists a descent
(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\varphi:\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\,{\cong}\,\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathbb{C}})
of
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathbb{C}}
as a filtered flat vector bundle over
S
such that
\mathsf{V}:=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})\in\mathsf{R}(S)
belongs to the subcategory
\mathsf{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{am}}(S)
, then the descent
(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\varphi)
is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Recall that an automorphism of
(\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\varphi)
is an automorphism of
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}
which fixes
\varphi
, so it has to be the identity. Therefore, it suffices to prove uniqueness up to isomorphism.

Proof.

Assume that we have
\mathsf{V}=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}),\mathsf{V}^{\prime}=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})\in\mathsf{R}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{am}}(S)
such that there exist isomorphisms
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\cong}}\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},\mathbb{C}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi^{\prime}}}{{\cong}}\mathsf{V}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
. By Lemma
(3.4.1)
the isomorphism
(\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{V}_{B}},\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi,\mathrm{id}_{\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}})\colon\mathsf{V}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\to\mathsf{V}^{\prime}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
descends to
S
since its étale component does, proving the claim.∎

Let

Lemma (3.4.4)
.

(Rigidity Lemma)

Let
S
be a smooth variety over a field
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and
(G,V,\mathsf{K})
be as in
(2.3.1)
. Let
\mathsf{V}=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
and
\mathsf{W}=(\mathsf{W}_{B},\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})
be objects of
\mathsf{R}^{*}_{\mathrm{am}}(S)
equipped with rigid
\mathsf{K}
-level structures
[\eta]
and
[\xi]
respectively. Then every isomorphism
\lambda:(\mathsf{V},[\eta])|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{W},[\xi])|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
over
S_{\mathbb{C}}
descends to an isomorphism
(\mathsf{V},[\eta])\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{W},[\xi])
over
S
.

Proof.

The conclusion amounts to saying that the isomorphisms
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
and
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
over
S_{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\lambda
descend to isomorphisms
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR}}
and
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
over
S
respectively, and the latter moreover sends
[\eta]
to
[\xi]
. In fact, it suffices to show that
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
descends. Indeed, it is then automatic that
[\eta]
is sent to
[\xi]
(cf.
(2.3.2)
), and one applies
(3.4.1)
to conclude that
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR}}|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
descends as well.

We are given that
\lambda
sends
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]:=[\eta]_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]:=[\xi]_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
. Write
\lambda_{x}
for the fiber of
\lambda
at a point
x\in S(\mathbb{C})
and
\lambda_{B,x}
for its Betti component. Note that
\lambda_{B,x}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
sends
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]_{x}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{x}
.

Fix an arbitrary
s\in S(\mathbb{C})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}/k)
. Let
(M,\gamma)
be a test object at
s
for
\mathsf{V}
to be weakly AM and remember the resulting
\gamma^{\sigma}:\omega_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(M^{\sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{V}_{B,\sigma(s)},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR},\sigma(s)})
(see
(3.2.3)
). Set
\nu=\lambda_{s}\circ\gamma
. Then
(M,\nu)
becomes a test object at
s
for
\mathsf{W}
, and take the resulting
\nu^{\sigma}:\omega_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(M^{\sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{W}_{B,\sigma(s)},\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR},\sigma(s)})
such that the diagrams in
(3.2.3)
commute with
\gamma^{\sigma}
replaced by
\nu^{\sigma}
. Let
[\eta_{M}]
be the level structure on
\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M)
given by pulling back
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]_{s}
along
\gamma_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
. Then since
\lambda_{B,s}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
sends
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]_{s}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{s}
,
[\eta_{M}]
is also the pullback of
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{s}
along
\nu_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
. By
(3.2.6)
,
\gamma^{\sigma}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
sends
[\eta_{M}]^{\sigma}
to
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
and
\nu^{\sigma}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
sends
[\eta_{M}]^{\sigma}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
.

Here comes the key observation: Since
\lambda_{B,\sigma(s)}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
sends
[\eta^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
,
\lambda_{\sigma(s)}\circ\gamma^{\sigma}
is another isomorphism
\omega_{\mathrm{Hdg}}(M^{\sigma})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(\mathsf{W}_{B,\sigma(s)},\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR},\sigma(s)})
whose étale realization sends
[\eta_{M}]^{\sigma}
to
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
. By the rigidity assumption, there is no nontrivial automorphism of the Hodge structure
(\mathsf{W}_{B,\sigma(s)},\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{dR},\sigma(s)})
whose étale realization fixes
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]_{\sigma(s)}
. Therefore, we must have
\nu^{\sigma}=\lambda_{\sigma(s)}\circ\gamma^{\sigma}
.

Now one readily checks that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{V}_{B,s}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{W}_{B,s}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\omega_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}(M^{\sigma})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{V}_{B,\sigma(s)}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{W}_{B,\sigma(s)}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{B,s}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}},s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{B,\sigma(s)}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}},s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma^{\sigma}_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nu_{B}^{\sigma}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\,{\cong}\,_{\mathrm{bc}}}

Indeed, by the construction of
\gamma^{\sigma}
and
\nu^{\sigma}
, the two squares which contain

commute. The upper triangle commutes by definition of

, and the lower triangle commutes by the preceeding paragraph. Therefore, the square at the front has to commute as well. As

and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}/k)
were arbitrary, this implies that
\lambda_{B}{\otimes}\mathbb{A}_{f}
descends to an isomorphism
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
as desired.
∎

Remark (3.4.5)
.

The above rigidity lemma roughly says that Galois descent of isomorphisms between weakly AM systems of realizations is forced upon us by the existence of certain rigid level structures. The following well known example can be seen as a special case of the above lemma, in which the role played by the rigid level structure is made more explicit: Suppose for
i=1,2
and
N\geq 3
,
(E_{i},\alpha_{i})
is an elliptic curve over a smooth variety
S
over
k\subseteq\mathbb{C}
with a (full)
N
-level structure
\alpha_{i}:\underline{(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})}_{S}^{\oplus 2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(E_{i}/S)[N]
. Set
\mathsf{K}:=\ker(\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})\to\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}))
. Then the first cohomology of
E_{i}
defines an object
\mathsf{H}_{i}\in\mathsf{R}_{\mathrm{am}}^{\circ}(S)
and
\alpha_{i}
defines a
\mathsf{K}
-level structure on
\mathsf{H}_{i}
.

If
(E_{1},\alpha_{1})|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\,{\cong}\,(E_{2},\alpha_{2})|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
(or equivalently
(\mathsf{H}_{1},\alpha_{1})|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}\,{\cong}\,(\mathsf{H}_{2},\alpha_{2})|_{S_{\mathbb{C}}}
), then in fact
(E_{1},\alpha_{1})\,{\cong}\,(E_{2},\alpha_{2})
over
S
already (and hence
(\mathsf{H}_{1},\alpha_{1})\,{\cong}\,(\mathsf{H}_{2},\alpha_{2})
). This follows a fortiori from the existence of a fine moduli space of elliptic curves with level
N
-structure over
k
. Indeed, two
S
-valued points on this moduli space must be equal if they are equal after tensoring with
\mathbb{C}
. But without using the moduli theory this can be seen as follows: Without loss of generality, suppose that
S
is connected and
K
is the fraction field of
S
. Then
K
-forms of
(E_{i},\alpha_{i})|_{K}
are classified by
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathrm{Gal}_{K},\mathrm{Aut}((E_{i},\alpha_{i})|_{\bar{K}}))
, which is trivial as
\mathrm{Aut}((E_{i},\alpha_{i})|_{\bar{K}})
is trivial. The resulting isomorphism
(E_{1},\alpha_{1})|_{K}\,{\cong}\,(E_{2},\alpha_{2})|_{K}
(which has to be unique) extends over
S
.

Let

\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)

and let

goes to

\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)

[

and

is the

on
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)
associated to

.

We use a superscript

(e.g.,

.

admits a

-level structure

Remark (4.1.3)
.

We remark that the assumption (
5
) together with the neatness of
\mathsf{K}
implies that for every
(\mathbb{W},[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}])\in\mathcal{M}_{V}(T)
as above, the
\mathsf{K}
-level structure
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]
is rigid, as defined in
(3.3.3)
(cf.
[
15
, §2.1]
,
[
10
, Rmk 3.11]
). In particular, the groupoid
\mathcal{M}_{V}(T)
is in fact a set. The above theorem implies that
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)_{\mathbb{C}}
represents the functor
\mathcal{M}_{V}(-)
from the category of smooth
\mathbb{C}
-varieties to sets.

Remark (4.1.4)
.

Unlike the set up in
[
10
, Prop. 3.10]
, we do not keep a collection of global Hodge tensors (
\mathfrak{s}
in Milne’s notation, which plays the role of
\{\mathbf{t}_{\alpha,B}\}
in ours) of
\mathbb{W}
as part of the data for objects parametrized by
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)_{\mathbb{C}}
. Instead, we impose the condition that the
\mathsf{K}
-level structure
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]
needs to be
\mathbb{W}
-rational. Doing so does not change the mathematical content because
\mathbf{t}_{\alpha,B}
’s are uniquely determined by the level structure
[\xi^{\mathrm{an}}]
provided that they exist.

4.2 Constructing Systems of Realizations

We shall work under the following assumption:

Definition (4.2.1)
.

We say that a Shimura datum
(G,\Omega)
of abelian type which satisfies (
5
) has a nice Hodge-type cover if there exists a morphism of Shimura data
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})\to(G,\Omega)
such that

(i)

(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
is of Hodge type and also satisfies assumption (
5
);

(ii)

\widetilde{G}\to G
is surjective and the kernel lies in the center of
\widetilde{G}
;

(iii)

the induced embedding on reflex fields
E(G,\Omega)\subseteq E(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
is an equality.

In the above, by “cover” we just mean that
\widetilde{G}
can be viewed as a cover of
G
. The map between Hermitian symmetric domains
\widetilde{\Omega}\to\Omega
is usually not surjective, but only a cover over some connected components of
\Omega
. In practice,
\mathrm{Sh}(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
does function as an “étale cover” of
\mathrm{Sh}(G,\Omega)
up to taking Hecke translates (e.g., in the proof of
(4.2.2)
below).

In general, a Shimura datum
(G,\Omega)
is said to be of abelian type if there exists a Shimura datum
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
of Hodge type and a central isogeny between derived subgroups
\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{der}}\to G^{\mathrm{der}}
which induces an isomorphism between the adjoint Shimura data
(\widetilde{G}^{\mathrm{ad}},\widetilde{\Omega}^{\mathrm{ad}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(G^{\mathrm{ad}},\Omega^{\mathrm{ad}})
, so that the relationship between
(G,\Omega)
and
(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
is more indirect than the one in
(4.2.1)
; in particular,
E(G,\Omega)
could be strictly smaller than
E(\widetilde{G},\widetilde{\Omega})
.

Theorem (4.2.2)
.

Assume that
(G,\Omega)
is a Shimura datum of abelian type which satisfies (
5
) and has a nice Hodge-type cover. Let
\mathsf{K}\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be a neat compact open subgroup. Let

be a representation and

(resp.
\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}

\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)_{\mathbb{C}}
(resp. automorphic étale local system on
\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)

Then there exists a unique descent

\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G)

\mathsf{V}:=(\mathsf{V}_{B},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{dR}},\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}})

\mathsf{R}_{\mathrm{am}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{\mathsf{K}}(G))

We call

as above the automorphic system of realizations associated to

The truth of to

Suppose that

4.3 Moduli Interpretations over Reflex Fields

We are now ready to give the moduli interpretations of the Shimura varieties over the reflex fields, under our simplifying assumptions.

\mathsf{K}

\mathsf{W}

(\mathsf{W},[\xi])
and
\rho^{*}(\mathsf{V},[\eta_{V}])
when restricted to
T_{\mathbb{C}}
. We conclude by the rigidity lemma
(3.4.4)
that in fact we must have
(\mathsf{W},[\xi])\,{\cong}\,\rho^{*}(\mathsf{V},[\eta_{V}])
.
∎

Remark (4.3.2)
.

The above proof is certainly inspired by that of
[
7
, Cor. 5.4]
, which deals with the special case when
(G,\Omega)
is an orthogonal type Shimura datum,
V
is the standard representation of
G
,
T
is the moduli space of quasi-polarized K3 surfaces of a certain degree, and
(\mathsf{W},[\xi])
is given by the primitive cohomology of the universal family. We remark though that after Madapusi-Pera proved the rationality of the peirod map, he proved in
[
7
, Prop. 5.6]
that
\rho^{*}\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}\,{\cong}\,\mathsf{W}_{\mathrm{{\acute{e}}t}}
by resorting to Deligne’s big monodromy trick. It is in fact unnecessary if we only work with neat level structures, by the rigidity lemma
(3.4.4)
.

Introduction

Braid groups were introduced by E. Artin as a tool for working with classical knots and links. The
famous Alexander theorem says that every classical link is equivalent to the closure of some braid,
and the well-known Markov theorem describes braids which have equivalent closures.

In 1999 L. Kauffman introduced the virtual knot theory which generalizes the classical knot theory
[
33
]
. Virtual braid groups were introduced in the same paper as a tool for working with virtual knots and links.
The analogues of the Alexander and the Markov theorems for virtual braids and links were formulated and proved by S. Kamada in
[
31
]
. Another form of the Markov theorem for virtual braids
and links was introduced by Kauffman and Lambropoulou in
[
35
]
.

Welded braid groups, unrestricted virtual braid groups, flat virtual braid groups, flat welded braid groups, Gauss virtual braid groups are quotients of virtual braid groups. These groups have various applications in algebra and topology, in particular, each of these groups corresponds to some knot theory, which has a geometric interpretation that is often very useful when studying applications. Welded and unrestricted virtual braid groups are used for working with welded links
[
25
]
and fused links
[
2
,
41
]
, respectively. Flat virtual braid groups were introduced in
[
34
]
for working with flat virtual knots and links, which were introduced in
[
33
]
, and which model curves on surfaces. The notion of flat virtual links is the same to the notion of virtual strings introduced by V. Turaev in
[
48
]
. In the papers
[
30
,
36
]
invariants for flat virtual knots and links were used for constructing invariants for virtual knots and links. Gauss virtual braid groups are used for studying Gauss virtual links, which are called “homotopy classes of
Gauss words” by Turaev in
[
49
]
, and called “free knots” by Manturov in
[
38
]
. All together classical braid groups, virtual braid groups, welded braid groups, unrestricted virtual braid groups, flat virtual braid groups, flat welded braid groups and Gauss virtual braid groups are called braid-like groups. Universal braid groups were introduced in
[
4
]
. The name “universal” in this paper is motivated by the fact that universal braid groups contain classical braid groups, and moreover these groups have singular braid groups and all braid-like groups as quotients. Hence the structure of these groups play an important role in the study of all braid-like groups.

One way of studying braid-like groups is to study representations of these groups by automorphisms of different algebraic systems. Using the representations one can prove that various algorithmic problems (the word problem, the conjugacy problem etc) can be solved in these groups. Also using representations of braid-like groups one can construct invariants for the corresponding knot theories
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

One of the first representations of braid-like groups is the Artin’s representation
\varphi:B_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n})
of the braid group
B_{n}
by automorphisms of the free group
F_{n}
with
n
generators. The Artin’s representation is faithful. During the years a lot of representations of braid-like
groups were investigated. For example, there are the Silver-Williams representation
\varphi_{SW}:VB_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n}*\mathbb{Z}^{n+1})
(see
[
46
]
), the Boden-Dies-Gaudreau-Gerlings-Harper-Nicas representation
\varphi_{BD}:VB_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n}*\mathbb{Z}^{2})
(see
[
21
]
), the Kamada representation
\varphi_{K}:VB_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n}*\mathbb{Z}^{2n})
(see
[
15
]
), the representations
\varphi_{M}:VB_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n}*\mathbb{Z}^{2n+1})
,
\tilde{\varphi}_{M}:VB_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(F_{n}*\mathbb{Z}^{n})
of Bardakov-Mikhalchishina-Neshchadim (see
[
10
,
11
]
). In the paper
[
8
]
there are representations of the flat virtual braid groups by automorphisms of the free group, which do not preserve forbidden relations.

The study of linear representations of braid groups was started when Burau constructed his famous representation
\varphi_{B}:B_{n}\to{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}])
(see, for example,
[
20
, Section 3]
). For
n=2,3
the Burau representation is known to be faithful, for
n\geq 5
the Burau representation is known to have non-trivial kernel
[
18
]
, for
n=4
the question on faithfulness of the Burau representation is still open (see some positive results, for example, in
[
17
]
). The Burau representation was generalized in 1961 by Gassner
[
27
]
to a related representation of the pure braid group
\varphi_{G}:P_{n}\to{\rm GL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\dots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}])
. It is not known whether the Gassner representation is faithful for
n>3
. Some new results about representations of virtual braid groups can be found, for example, in
[
12
,
14
]
. Certain linear representations of flat virtual braid groups and Gauss virtual braid groups are studied in
[
8
]
.

Another way to study braid-like groups is to explore normal forms of elements in braid-like groups which usually (but not always) arise when a braid-like group can be written as a product (for example, direct product, semiderict product, free product etc). There are several normal forms of elements in classical braid groups which can be found, for example, in
[
32
]
. Attempts to construct a normal form for elements of the virtual braid groups were made in the paper
[
4
]
. Paper
[
7
]
contains results about the normal form of elements in unrestricted virtual braid groups and flat welded braid groups.

In the present paper we study structural aspects of certain quotients of braid groups and virtual braid groups using both representations and decompositions. In particular, we construct and study linear representations

\displaystyle B_{n}\to{\rm GL}_{n(n-1)/2}\left(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]\right),

\displaystyle VB_{n}\to{\rm GL}_{n(n-1)/2}\left(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n-1}^{\pm 1}]\right)

which are connected with the famous Lawrence-Bigelow-Krammer representation. It turns out that these representations are faithful representations of crystallographic groups
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
,
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
, respectively. Using these representations we study certain properties of the groups
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
,
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
. Moreover, we construct new representations and decompositioms of universal braid groups.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
1
we recall definitions and basic properties of braid groups, virtual braid groups, welded braid groups and pure subgroups of these groups. In Section

we construct a faithful linear representation of the crystallographic group

that is the quotient of the braid group
B_{n}
by the commutator subgroup

of the pure braid group
P_{n}
(Theorem
2.1
). The torsion in this group is studied in
[
28
]
. Using the constructed representation we describe all elements of order three in
B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
(Proposition
2.1
). Note that this group has only three-torsion.
In Section
3
we construct a faithful linear representation of another crystallographic group
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
which contains
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
(Theorem
3.1
). In Proposition
3.2
we demonstrate that
VB_{3}/VP_{3}^{\prime}
contains infinitely many elements of order two. Note that
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
has no two-torsion. In Section
4
we show that
VP_{3}
can be decomposed into the free product of infinite cyclic group and group that is a semi-direct product of free group of rank three and free group of rank two (Proposition
4.1

is linear. In Section

introduced in

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we give necessary preliminaries. We use classical notations. If
G
is a group, and
a,b\in G
, then we denote by
a^{b}=b^{-1}ab
the conjugate of
a
by
b
, and by
[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
the commutator of the elements
a,b
. If
X
is a set, then we denote by
{\rm Sym}(X)
the set of all bijections from
X
to
X
. All actions are supposed to be right, i. e. if
f,g\in{\rm Sym}(X)
, then for
x\in X
we denote
fg(x)=g(f(x))
.

1.1.
Braid groups and conjugating automorphism groups

For
n\geq 2
the braid group
B_{n}
on
n
strands is defined as
the group generated by
(n-1)
elements

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}

with defining relations

(1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2,

(2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 2.

Relations (
1
) and (
2
) are called
braid relations
. There is a homomorphism
\varphi
from
B_{n}
to the symmetric group
S_{n}
on
n
symbols
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, which maps the generator
\sigma_{i}
to the transposition
(i,i+1)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
. The kernel
\operatorname{Ker}(\varphi)
is called the
pure braid group
on
n
strands and is denoted by
P_{n}
. From the definition it is clear that we have the following short exact sequence:

1\to P_{n}\to B_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\to}}S_{n}\to 1.

It is known that the pure braid group
P_{n}
can be generated by the elements
a_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
, where

\displaystyle a_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}^{2},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\dots,n-1,

\displaystyle a_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j-1}\,\sigma_{j-2}\ldots\sigma_{i+1}\,\sigma_{i}^{2}\,\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\ldots\sigma_{j-2}^{-1}\,\sigma_{j-1}^{-1},

\displaystyle i+1<j\leq n.

Also it is know that the pure braid group
P_{n}
can be decomposed into a semi-direct product
P_{n}=U_{n}\rtimes P_{n-1}
, where
U_{n}
is the free group with free generators
a_{1,n},a_{2,n},\ldots,a_{n-1,n}
, and
P_{n-1}
is the pure braid group on
(n-1)
strands. Here
U_{n}
is a normal subgroup of
P_{n}
. Similar,
P_{n-1}
is a semi-direct product of the free group
U_{n-1}
with the free generators
a_{1,n-1},a_{2,n-1}\ldots,a_{n-2,n-1}
and
P_{n-2}
, and so on. Therefore
P_{n}
has the following decomposition into the iterated semi-direct product (see
[
39
]
):

P_{n}=U_{n}\rtimes(U_{n-1}\rtimes(\ldots\rtimes(U_{3}\rtimes U_{2}))\ldots),

where
U_{i}\simeq F_{i-1}
is the free group on
(i-1)
generators for
i=2,3,\ldots,n
.

The group
B_{n}
has a faithful representation by automorphisms of the free group
F_{n}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
, where the generator
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
, defines the automorphism (which we denote by the same symbol
\sigma_{i}\in\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
) which acts on the generators
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
of
F_{n}
by the following rule

\sigma_{i}:\begin{cases}x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}x_{i+1}x_{i}^{-1},&\\
x_{i+1}\mapsto x_{i},&\\
x_{l}\mapsto x_{l},&l\neq i,i+1.\end{cases}

By the Artin’s theorem
[
20
, Theorem 1.9]
an automorphism
\beta
from
\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
belongs to
B_{n}<\operatorname{Aut}(F_{n})
if and only if
\beta
satisfies the following two conditions:

1)

\beta(x_{i})=a_{i}^{-1}\,x_{\pi(i)}\,a_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,

2)

\beta(x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n})=x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n}
,

where
\pi
is a permutation from
S_{n}
and
a_{i}\in F_{n}
for
i=1,2,\dots,n
.

The automorphism
\beta
of the free group
F_{n}
is called a
conjugating automorphism
(or permutation-conjugating by terminology from
[
25
]
) if it satisfies condition 1) from the list above. It is clear that every automorphism from
B_{n}<{\rm Aut}(F_{n})
is a conjugating automorphism. The group of all conjugating automorphisms of
F_{n}
is denoted by
C_{n}
. It is known that this group is generated by the automorphisms
\sigma_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
and automorphisms
\alpha_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,

n-1
, where
\alpha_{i}
acts on the generators
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
of
F_{n}
by the following rule

\alpha_{i}:\begin{cases}x_{i}\mapsto x_{i+1},&\\
x_{i+1}\mapsto x_{i},&\\
x_{l}\mapsto x_{l},&l\neq i,i+1.\end{cases}

Generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1}
of
C_{n}
satisfy the braid relations (
1
) and (
2
) of
B_{n}
. Also it is easy to see that the automorphisms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
of
C_{n}
generate the symmetric group
S_{n}
in
C_{n}
which has the following defining relations

(3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+1},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2,

(4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\alpha_{j}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j}\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 2,

(5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-1.

Moreover, the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
of
C_{n}
satisfy the following mixed relations (see
[
25
,
44
]
):

(6)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 2,

(7)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}\sigma_{i+1},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2,

(8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\alpha_{i+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2.

Summarizing, the group
C_{n}
has generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{n-1},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}
with the defining relations (
1
)-(
8
).

For
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
denote by
\varepsilon_{i,j}
the automorphism of
F_{n}
which acts on the generators of
F_{n}
by the rule

\varepsilon_{i,j}:\begin{cases}x_{i}\mapsto x_{j}^{-1}x_{i}x_{j},&i\neq j,\\
x_{l}\mapsto x_{l},&l\neq i.\end{cases}

The subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(F_{n})
generated by
\varepsilon_{i,j}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
is called the
group of basis-conjugating automorphisms
and is denoted by
Cb_{n}
. It is clear that every generator
\varepsilon_{i,j}
belongs to
C_{n}
, therefore the group
Cb_{n}
of basis-conjugating automorphisms is a subgroup of the group
C_{n}
of conjugating automorphisms. In
[
40
]
it is proved that
Cb_{n}
has the following defining relations:

(9)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i,j}\varepsilon_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{k,l}\varepsilon_{i,j},

(10)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i,j}\varepsilon_{k,j}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{k,j}\varepsilon_{i,j},

(11)

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{i,j}\varepsilon_{k,j})\varepsilon_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i,k}(\varepsilon_{i,j}\varepsilon_{k,j}),

where different letters denote different indices.

It is easy to see that the group
C_{n}
of conjugating automorphisms can be decomposed into a semi-direct product
C_{n}=Cb_{n}\rtimes S_{n}
, where the permutation group
S_{n}
is generated by the automorphisms
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}
. In
[
44
]
it is prove that the following relations hold:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{i}\sigma^{-1}_{i},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i+1,i}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{-1}_{i}\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j-1}\alpha_{j-2}\ldots\alpha_{i+1}\varepsilon_{i,i+1}\alpha_{i+1}\ldots\alpha_{j-2}\alpha_{j-1},

\displaystyle\text{if}~i<j,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{j-1}\alpha_{j-2}\ldots\alpha_{i+1}\alpha_{i}\varepsilon_{i,i+1}\alpha_{i}\alpha_{i+1}\ldots\alpha_{j-2}\alpha_{j-1},

\displaystyle\text{if}~i<j.

The structure of
Cb_{n}
was studied in
[
4
,
5
]
, where it was shown that
Cb_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, has a decomposition into an iterated semi-direct product

Cb_{n}=D_{n-1}\rtimes(D_{n-2}\rtimes(\ldots.\rtimes(D_{2}\rtimes D_{1}))\ldots),

where
D_{i}
denotes a subgroup of
Cb_{n}
generated by the elements

\{\varepsilon_{i+1,1},\varepsilon_{i+1,2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{i+1,i},\varepsilon_{1,i+1},\varepsilon_{2,i+1},\ldots\varepsilon_{i,i+1}\},

where
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1,

The elements
\{\varepsilon_{i+1,1},\varepsilon_{i+1,2},\ldots,\varepsilon_{i+1,i}\}
generate the free group of rank
i
in
D_{i}
, while the elements
\{\varepsilon_{1,i+1},\varepsilon_{2,i+1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{i,i+1}\}
generate the free abelian group of rank
i
in
D_{i}
.

The pure braid group
P_{n}
is a subgroup of
Cb_{n}
. The generators
a_{i,j}
(for
1\leq i<j\leq n
) of
P_{n}
can be written in
Cb_{n}
in the following form

\displaystyle a_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{i,i+1}^{-1}\varepsilon_{i+1,i}^{-1}

for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
, and

\displaystyle a_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{j-1,i}\varepsilon_{j-2,i}\ldots\varepsilon_{i+1,i}(\varepsilon_{i,j}^{-1}\varepsilon_{j,i}^{-1})\varepsilon_{i+1,i}^{-1}\ldots\varepsilon_{j-2,i}^{-1}\varepsilon_{j-1,i}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{j-1,j}^{-1}\varepsilon_{j-2,j}^{-1}\ldots\varepsilon_{i+1,j}^{-1}(\varepsilon_{i,j}^{-1}\varepsilon_{j,i}^{-1})\varepsilon_{i+1,j}\ldots\varepsilon_{j-2,j}\varepsilon_{j-1,j},

for
2\leq i+1<j\leq n
.

1.3.
The virtual braid group and the welded braid group

The
virtual braid group

VB_{n}
on
n
strands, introduced in
[
33
]
, is the group with the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n-1}
, which is defined by the relations (
1
)-(
2
) and additional relations

(18)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}

\displaystyle=\rho_{i+1}\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2,

(19)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\rho_{j}

\displaystyle=\rho_{j}\rho_{i},

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 2,

(20)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-1,

(21)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\rho_{j}

\displaystyle=\rho_{j}\sigma_{i},

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 1,

(22)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1}\sigma_{i}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i+1}\rho_{i}\rho_{i+1},

\displaystyle i=1,2,\ldots,n-2.

The elements
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
generate the braid group

with the braid relations (

)-(

) in

. Relations (

)-(

, where the elements
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n-1}
generate the permutation group
S_{n}
in

and the permutation group

. Relations (

for

. It was shown in
[
29
]
that the relations

(23)

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\,\sigma_{i+1}\,\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\,\sigma_{i}\,\rho_{i+1},

\displaystyle\rho_{i+1}\,\sigma_{i}\,\sigma_{i+1}=\rho_{i}\,\sigma_{i+1}\,\sigma_{i}.

donot hold in
VB_{n}
. Relations (
23
) are usually called
forbidden relations
.

The virtual pure braid group
introduced in
[
4
]
is the kernel

of the homomorphism

from the virtual braid group

onto the symmetric group

given on generators

\nu(\sigma_{i})=\nu(\rho_{i})=\rho_{i},\qquad\text{for}~i=1,2,\ldots,n-1.

From the definition it is clear that the following short exact sequence holds:

1\to VP_{n}\to VB_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\nu}}{{\to}}S_{n}\to 1.

Following
[
4
]
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
let us definte the elements
\lambda_{i,j}
by the following formulas

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,i+1}

\displaystyle=\rho_{i}\,\sigma_{i}^{-1},

\displaystyle\lambda_{i+1,i}

\displaystyle=\rho_{i}\,\lambda_{i,i+1}\,\rho_{i}=\sigma_{i}^{-1}\,\rho_{i}

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,j}

\displaystyle\lambda_{j,i}

\displaystyle=\rho_{j-1}\,\rho_{j-2}\ldots\rho_{i+1}\,\lambda_{i+1,i}\,\rho_{i+1}\ldots\rho_{j-2}\,\rho_{j-1}

.

Theorem 1.1
.

The group

admits a presentation with the generators
\lambda_{k,l}
for

and the defining relations

(24)

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,j}\,\lambda_{k,\,l}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k,\,l}\,\lambda_{i,j},

(25)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k,i}\,\lambda_{k,j}\,\lambda_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{i,j}\,\lambda_{k,j}\,\lambda_{k,i},

where different letters denote different indices.

The
welded braid group

on
n
strands introduced in

is the group with the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{n-1}

on

(27)

\displaystyle\sigma_{i}\,\alpha_{i+1}\,\alpha_{i}

Recall that a group

into the general linear group

1.4.
Braid groups and crystallographic groups

An interesting connection between braid groups and crystallographic groups was observed in
[
28
]
. Recall that a discrete and uniform subgroup
G
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rtimes O_{n}(\mathbb{R})\subseteq\operatorname{Aff}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is said to be a
crystallographic group
of dimension
n
. If in addition
G
is torsion free, then
G
is called a
Bieberbach group
of dimension
n
. The correspondence between Bieberbach groups and fundamental groups of closed flat Riemannian manifold is presented in
[
51
]
.

The following characterization of crystallographic groups was used in
[
28
, Lemma 8]
in order to demonstrate the relation between braid groups and crystallographic groups.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
G
be a group. Then
G
is a crystallographic group if and only if there exist an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to G\to\Phi\to 1

such that the following conditions hold:

(a)

\Phi
is a finite group,

(b)

the integral representation
\theta:\Phi\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
, induced by conjugation on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
defined by
\theta(\varphi)(x)=\pi x\pi^{-1}
, where
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
\varphi\in\Phi
and
\pi\in G
is such that
\zeta(\pi)=\varphi
,
is faithful.

If
G
is a crystallographic group, then the integer
n
from Lemma
1.1
is called the dimension of
G
, the finite group
\Phi
is called the holonomy group of
G
, and the integral representation
\theta:\Phi\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is called the holonomy representation of
G
.

Let
P^{\prime}_{n}=[P_{n},P_{n}]
be the commutator subgroup of the pure braid group
P_{n}
. It was shown in
[
28
]
that the quotient
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
is a crystallographic group for all
n\geq 3
. Moreover, it was proved that
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
possesses torsion, and there is
a one-to-one correspondence between the conjugacy classes of the finite-order elements of
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
and the conjugacy classes of the elements of odd order of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

The groups
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
also arise in the study of pseudo-symmetric braided categories
[
42
]
. In this paper the authors consider the quotient, denoted by
PS_{n}
, of
B_{n}
by the normal subgroup generated by the relations

s_{i}s^{-1}_{i+1}s_{i}=s_{i+1}s^{-1}_{i}s_{i+1},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n-2,

and show that
PS_{n}
is isomorphic to
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
.

2.
Linear representation of
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}

Let
R=\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1},t^{\pm 1}]
be the ring of Laurent polynomials with two variables
q,t
, and
V
be the free
R
-module with the basis
e_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
The Lawrence-Bigelow-Krammer representation (shortly LBKR) is the linear representation of the braid group
B_{n}
by the automorphisms of
V

\psi=\psi_{q,t}:B_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(V)

which is given on the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
of
B_{n}
by the following rule

(29)

\psi(\sigma_{k})(e_{i,j})=\begin{cases}e_{i,j},&k<i-1\text{ or }k>j,\\
e_{i-1,j}+(1-q)e_{i,j},&k=i-1,\\
tq(q-1)e_{i,i+1}+qe_{i+1,j},&k=i<j-1,\\
tq^{2}e_{i,j},&k=i=j-1,\\
e_{i,j}+tq^{k-i}(q-1)^{2}e_{k,k+1},&i<k<j-1,\\
e_{i,j-1}+tq^{j-i}(q-1)e_{j-1,i},&i<k=j-1,\\
(1-q)e_{i,j}+qe_{i,j+1},&k=j.\\
\end{cases}

Example 2.1
.

If
n=3
, then the braid group
B_{3}
is generated by
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
, and the module
V
has the basis
\{e_{1,2},e_{1,3},e_{2,3}\}
. From formula (
29
) it follows that the linear maps
\psi(\sigma_{1})
,
\psi(\sigma_{2})
,
\psi(\sigma_{1})^{-1}
,
\psi(\sigma_{2})^{-1}
can be presented by the following matrices, which are written in the basis
e_{1,2},e_{1,3},e_{2,3}
of
V

\displaystyle[\psi(\sigma_{1})]

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}tq^{2}&0&0\\
tq(q-1)&0&q\\
0&1&1-q\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle[\psi(\sigma_{2})]

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1-q&q&0\\
1&0&tq^{2}(q-1)\\
0&0&tq^{2}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle[\psi(\sigma_{1})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}t^{-1}q^{-2}&0&0\\
-1+2q^{-1}-q^{-2}&1-q^{-1}&1\\
-q^{-1}+q^{-2}&q^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle[\psi(\sigma_{2})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&-q+1\\
q^{-1}&1-q^{-1}&-q+2-q^{-1}\\
0&0&t^{-1}q^{-2}\end{pmatrix}.

For two complex numbers
q_{0},t_{0}\in\mathbb{C}
denote by
V_{q_{0},t_{0}}
the free module over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[q_{0},t_{0}]
with the same basis as
V
, and denote by
\psi_{q_{0},t_{0}}
the specialization of
\psi_{q,t}
obtained by putting
q=q_{0}
,
t=t_{0}
. The above specialization gives the linear representation
\psi_{q_{0},t_{0}}:B_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(V_{q_{0},t_{0}})
. In particular, the map
\psi_{1,t}:B_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(V_{1,t})
is defined on the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
of
B_{n}
by the following rules

\psi_{1,t}(\sigma_{k}):\begin{cases}e_{k,k+1}\mapsto te_{k,k+1},&\\
e_{i,j}\mapsto e_{s_{k}(i),s_{k}(j)},&\text{if}~(i,j)\not=(k,k+1)~\text{and}~1\leq i<j\leq n,\\
\end{cases}

where
s_{k}=(k,k+1)
is the transposition from
S_{n}
. In this formula we assume that
e_{j,i}=e_{i,j}
if
i<j
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
q=1
and
t=\pm 1
, then the image of
B_{n}
under the specialization
\psi_{1,\pm 1}
of LBKR is isomorphic
to the symmetric group
S_{n}
.

Proof.

It is enough to observe that
\left(\psi_{1,\pm 1}(\sigma_{i})\right)^{2}
is the identity automorphism. This property immidiately follows from the definition of
\psi_{1,\pm 1}
.
∎

From the expressions of the generators
a_{i,j}~(1\leq i<j\leq n)
of
P_{n}
introduced in Section
1.1
and direct calculations we have the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

The representation
\psi_{1,t}:B_{n}\to{\rm Aut}(V_{1,t})
acts on the generators
a_{i,j}~(1\leq i<j\leq n)
of
P_{n}
by the following rules

\psi_{1,t}(a_{ij}):\begin{cases}e_{i,j}\mapsto t^{2}e_{i,j},&\\
e_{k,l}\mapsto e_{k,l},&~\text{for}~(i,j)\not=(k,l).\\
\end{cases}

The following theorem is the main results of the present section.

Theorem 2.1
.

The image of
B_{n}
under
\psi_{1,t}
is isomorphic to
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

In Section
1.1
we have noticed that the following short exact sequence holds

1\to P_{n}\to B_{n}\to S_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}1.

From this short exact sequence and the isomorphism

(B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime})/(P_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime})=B_{n}/P_{n}

we have the following short exact sequence

1\to P_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}\to B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}S_{n}\to 1.

From this short exact sequence it follows that every element
b\in B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
can be expressend in the form
b=p\lambda
, where
p\in P_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
and
\lambda
is a coset representative of
P_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
in
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
. In order to prove the statement of the theorem we have to prove that if
b
is non-trivial, then
\psi_{1,t}(b)
in non-identity automorphism.

Let
b=p\lambda\neq 1
. If
\lambda\not=1
, then
\pi(b)\not=1
, and hence the composition of
\psi_{1,t}
and specialization
t=1
send
b
to non-trivial automorphism. Therefore,
\psi_{1,t}(b)
is non-trivial. If
\lambda=1
and
b\not=1
, then
b
can be presented in the form

b=a_{1,2}^{\alpha_{1,2}}\left(a_{1,3}^{\alpha_{1,3}}a_{2,3}^{\alpha_{2,3}}\right)\ldots\left(a_{1,n}^{\alpha_{1,n}}\ldots a_{n-1,n}^{\alpha_{n-1,n}}\right)P_{n}^{\prime}

for some integers
\alpha_{i,j}\in\mathbb{Z}
. From Lemma
2.2
it follows that the matrix of the automorphism
\psi_{1,t}(b)
is the following diagonal matrix

[\psi_{1,t}(b)]=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2\alpha_{1,2}},t^{2\alpha_{1,3}},t^{2\alpha_{2,3}}\ldots t^{2\alpha_{1,n}}\ldots t^{2\alpha_{n-1,n}}),

which is not the identity matrix. Hence, we have proved that if
b
is non-trivial, then its image
\psi_{1,t}
is non-trivial.
∎

It was shown in
[
28
]
that the group
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
possesses torsion, and there
is a one-to-one correspondence between the conjugacy classes of the finite-order elements of

B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
and the conjugacy classes of the elements of odd order of the symmetric group

S_{n}
. The group
B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
was considered separately in the paper
[
28
]
. Using the linear representation
\psi_{1,t}
we obtain the following statement.

Proposition 2.1
.

The group
B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
has only
3
-torsion. Every element which has one of the following forms

\displaystyle a_{1,2}^{\alpha}a_{1,3}^{\beta}a_{2,3}^{\gamma}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},

\displaystyle a_{1,2}^{\alpha}a_{1,3}^{\beta}a_{2,3}^{\gamma}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1},

where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
are such that
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=-1
, has order
3
. Any other non-trivial element has infinite order.

Proof.

Since
\psi_{1,t}(B_{n})\cong B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
, we can present elements of
B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
by matrices in
\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}])
. For
n=3
any element
b\in B_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
can be presented in the form
b=p\lambda
, where
p\in P_{3}/P_{3}^{\prime}
has the following form

p=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2\alpha},t^{2\beta},t^{2\gamma}),\qquad~\text{for}~\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{Z},

and
\lambda
is one of the following matrices

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}t&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&t\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&t&0\\
0&0&t\\
1&0&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\lambda_{4}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
t&0&0\\
0&t&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\lambda_{5}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&t\\
0&t&0\\
t&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let us consider each
\lambda_{i}
separately. First, it is obvious that the element
p\lambda_{0}
has infinite order. If the element
p\lambda_{1}
has a finite order, then from the matrix form of
\lambda_{1}
it is clear that this order has to be even. Since for an arbitrary integer
k
we have

(p\lambda_{1})^{2k}=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2k(2\alpha+1)},t^{2k(\beta+\gamma)},t^{2k(\beta+\gamma)}),

the element
p\lambda_{1}
has infinite order.
Further if the element
p\lambda_{2}
has a finite order, then from the matrix form of
\lambda_{2}
we see that this order is even. Since for an arbitrary integer
k
we have

(p\lambda_{2})^{2k}=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2k(\alpha+\beta)},t^{2k(\alpha+\beta)},t^{2k(2\gamma+1)}),

the element
p\lambda_{2}
has infinite order. From the matrix representations it is clear that the order of the element
p\lambda_{3}
is divisible by
3
. For an arbitrary
k
we have the equality

(p\lambda_{3})^{3k}=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)},t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)},t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)}),

i. e. if
\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1=0
, then
p\lambda_{3}
has order 3, otherwise
p\lambda_{3}
has infinite order. Using the same argument, the order of the element
p\lambda_{4}
is divisible by
3
, and for an arbitrary
k
we have

(p\lambda_{4})^{3k}=\operatorname{diag}(t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)},t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)},t^{2k(\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1)}),

i. e. if
\alpha+\beta+\gamma+1=0
, then
p\lambda_{4}
has order 3, otherwise
p\lambda_{4}
has infinite order. It is easy to check that elements of the form
p\lambda_{5}
have infinite order
∎

In general case it is not difficult to prove the following statement.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
b
is a non-trivial element in
B_{n}/P_{n}^{\prime}
such that the permutation
\pi(b)
is non-identity but fixes some points, then
b
has infinite order.

3.
Linear representation of
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}

In Section
1.3
we have noticed that the following short exact sequence holds

1\to VP_{n}\to VB_{n}\to S_{n}\to 1.

From this short exact sequence and the isomorphism

(VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime})/(VP_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime})=VB_{n}/VP_{n}

we have the following short exact sequence

1\to VP_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}\to VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}\to S_{n}\to 1,

where
VP_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}^{n(n-1)}
(since
VP_{n}
is generated by the elements
\lambda_{i,j}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
),
S_{n}=\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}\rangle
, and the group
S_{n}
acts on the generators of
VP_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
by the permutation of indices. From the equality
VB_{n}=VP_{n}\rtimes S_{n}
it follows that
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}=(VP_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime})\rtimes S_{n}
, and hence,
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
is a crystallographic group.

In this section we construct an extension of the representation
\psi_{1,t}
from the braid group
B_{n}
to the virtual braid group
VB_{n}
. Let
U
be the free module with the basis
e_{i,j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq n
over the ring
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n-1}^{\pm 1}]
of Laurent polinomials with
n
variables
t,t_{1},\dots,t_{n-1}
. Let us define the map
\Psi:VB_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(U)
on the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\dots,\rho_{n-1}
of
VB_{n}
by the following rules:

(30)

\displaystyle\Psi(\sigma_{i})

\displaystyle=\psi_{1,t}(\sigma_{i}),

(31)

\displaystyle\Psi(\rho_{i})

\displaystyle:\begin{cases}e_{i,i+1}\mapsto e_{i,i+1},&\\
e_{k,l}\mapsto e_{k,l},&k\not=i,i+1,l\not=i,i+1,\\
e_{i,j}\mapsto t_{i}e_{i+1,j},&i<j-1,\\
e_{i+1,j}\mapsto t_{i}^{-1}e_{i,j},&\\
e_{k,i}\mapsto t_{i}e_{k,i+1},&\\
e_{k,i+1}\mapsto t_{i}^{-1}e_{k,i}.&\end{cases}

The following property can be easy verified using direct calculations.

Proposition 3.1
.

The map
\Psi:VB_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(U)
, defined by (
30
) and (
31
) is a representation. The images
\Psi(\sigma_{i})
and
\Psi(\rho_{i})
satisfy both forbidden relations (
23
) for
VB_{n}
.

The following statement describes the image of
\Psi
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The image
\Psi(VB_{n})
is isomorphic to
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Using direct calculations it is easy to see that the image
\Psi(b)
of every element
b\in VP_{n}^{\prime}
is the trivial automorphism. Hence, in order to prove the theorem we have to prove that the image of any elements
b\in VB_{n}\setminus VP_{n}^{\prime}
is non-trivial. If
b
does not belong to
VP_{n}
, then the image of
b
under
\Psi
is clearly non-trivial automorphism of
U
. If
b
is a non-trivial element of
VP_{n}
, then looking to the representative of
b
in
bVP_{n}^{\prime}
it is easy to see that the matrix of
\Psi(b)
is a non-identity diagonal matrix.
∎

The following questions arise naturally from the paper
[
28
]
where the same questions are studied for classical linear groups.

Question 3.1
.

What are the possible finite orders of elements from
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
which do not belong to
S_{n}
?

Question 3.2
.

Which Bieberbach subgroups belong to
VB_{n}/VP_{n}^{\prime}
?

Let us consider the particular case
n=3
.
The virtual braid group
VB_{3}
is generated by four elements
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
and it has the following defining relations

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}\rho_{1}\rho_{2},

\displaystyle\rho_{1}^{2}=\rho_{2}^{2}=1,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}\rho_{1}\rho_{2}.

The images of the generators
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
of
VB_{n}
under the representation
\Psi:VB_{3}\to\operatorname{GL}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1}]
are the following matrices

\displaystyle\Psi(\sigma_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}t&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\Psi(\sigma_{2})=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&t\end{pmatrix},

i=1,2,\dots,m
. Since
\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s})=x_{s}
and (from the definition of
\phi_{k}
) the element

\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}})\right)\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m-1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}})\right)\dots\left(\phi_{k}(u_{2})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{2}})\right)\left(\phi_{k}(u_{1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}})\right)

can be written in terms of
x_{s+1},x_{s+2},\dots,x_{n}
, from equality (
45
) we conclude the equality

(46)

\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}})\right)\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m-1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}})\right)\dots\left(\phi_{k}(u_{2})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{2}})\right)\left(\phi_{k}(u_{1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}})\right)=1.

From the definition of
\phi_{k}
it is easy to see that for
i=1,2,\dots,m
we have the equality

(47)

\left(\phi_{k}(u_{i})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{i}})\right)=\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{i}}\right)^{v_{i}},

where
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{m}
are elements in
F_{n}
which can be written in terms of
x_{s+2},x_{s+3},\dots,x_{n}
. From the equality
u_{m}=1
we have

\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}})\right)=x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}}

and taking into account equality (
47
) we can rewrite equality (
46
) in the following form

(48)

x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}}\right)^{v_{m-1}}\dots\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{2}}\right)^{v_{2}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}}\right)^{v_{1}}=1.

Due to the fact that for
i=1,2,\dots,m
the elements
v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{m}\in F_{n}
can be written in terms of
x_{s+2},x_{s+3},\dots,x_{n}
, in order for the element
x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}}
in equality (
48
) to be reduced with something, there must be a number
t
such that
\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t}}\right)^{v_{t}}=x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t}}
and

(49)

x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}}\right)^{v_{m-1}}\dots\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t+1}}\right)^{v_{t+1}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t}}\right)^{v_{t}}=1.

From equality (
49
) it follows that

\displaystyle x_{s}

\displaystyle=x_{s}^{x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}}\right)^{v_{m-1}}\dots\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t+1}}\right)^{v_{t+1}}\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t}}\right)^{v_{t}}}

\displaystyle=x_{s}^{\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m}})\right)\left(\phi_{k}(u_{m-1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{m-1}})\right)\dots\left(\phi_{k}(u_{t})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{t}})\right)}

(50)

\displaystyle=\phi_{k}(\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{t}}w_{t+1}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{t+1}}w_{t+2}\dots\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m-1}}w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}})(x_{s})

If
t\neq 1
, then

|\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{t}}w_{t+1}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{t+1}}w_{t+2}\dots\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m-1}}w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}}|<|\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}}|=|w|

and equality (
50
) contradicts the fact that
w
is a counter example to the lemma with the minimal value
|w|
, hence we have
t=1
, what means that

\phi_{k}(u_{1})(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}})=\left(x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}}\right)^{v_{1}}=x_{s+1}^{\beta_{1}}.

This equality together with the definition of
\phi_{k}
implies that the eqiality
\phi_{k}(u_{1})(x_{s+1})=x_{s+1}
holds.

Let us calculate
\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s+1})
. From the definition of
\phi_{k}
we have the following equality

\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s+1})=\phi_{k}(\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}})(x_{s+1})=\\
=\phi_{k}(w_{2}w_{3}\dots w_{m})(x_{s+1})=\phi_{k}(u_{1})(x_{s+1})=x_{s+1}.

So, now we have the following equalities

(51)

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}},

(52)

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s})

\displaystyle=x_{s},

(53)

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s+1})

\displaystyle=x_{s+1}.

Note that in equality (
51
) the number
m
can not be equal to
1
, since if
m=1
, then
w=\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}
, and it is clear that
\phi_{k}(\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}})(x_{s})\neq x_{s}
for
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
. Therefore
m>1
.

Consider the element

\widetilde{w}=\tau_{s}^{-\alpha_{1}}w\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}=w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}}.

From equalities (
52
), (
53
) we have the equality

\phi_{k}(\widetilde{w})(x_{s})=x_{s}.

Since the element
w_{2}
can be written in terms of generators
\tau_{s+1},\tau_{s+2},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
from the definition of the representation
\phi_{k}
it is clear that

x_{s}=\phi_{k}(w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}})(x_{s})=\phi_{k}(\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}})(x_{s})

and since
|\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}}|<|w_{2}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}}|
we must have that the element
\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{2}}w_{3}\dots w_{m}\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{m}+\alpha_{1}}
doesn’t depent on
\tau_{s}
. It means that
m=2
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{m}=0
, and hence

w=\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}\tau_{s}^{-\alpha_{1}},

where the word
w_{2}
can be written in terms of generators
\tau_{s+1},\tau_{s+2},\dots,\tau_{n}
and
\phi_{k}(w_{2})(x_{s+1})=x_{s+1}
. By the unduction conjecture the word
w
can be written in terms of
\tau_{s+2},\tau_{s+3},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
, therefore from the definint relations of
T_{n}
we have
w=\tau_{s}^{\alpha_{1}}w_{2}\tau_{s}^{-\alpha_{1}}=w_{2}
, i. e.
w
can be written without using
\tau_{s}
, what contradicts conditions of the lemma.
∎

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.1
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

If
k\neq 0
, then the representation
\phi_{k}:T_{n}\to Cb_{n}
is faithful.

(2)

If
k\neq 0
, then the representation
\psi_{k,k}:T_{n}\to Cb_{n}
is faithful.

(3)

The representation
\xi:T_{n}\to\operatorname{Aut}(F_{3n-2})
is faithful for

and has non-trivial kernel for

.

Proof.

(1) Let
w
be an element from
{\rm Ker}(\phi_{k})
. Since for all
s\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
we have
\phi_{k}(w)(x_{s})=x_{s}
, from Lemma
5.1
it follows that in the representation of
w
in terms of
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
there are no generators
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
, hence,
w=1
.

(2) From result of Collins
[
24
]
follows that the subgroup
\langle\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}^{2}\rangle
of
B_{n}
has the presentation

\langle\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}^{2}~|~\sigma_{i}^{2}\sigma_{j}^{2}=\sigma_{j}^{2}\sigma_{i}^{2}\text{ for }|i-j|\geqslant 2\rangle,

hence the map
\tau_{i}\to\sigma_{i}^{2}
for

T_{n}\to\langle\sigma_{1}^{2},\sigma_{2}^{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}^{2}\rangle<P_{n}.

From the main result of the paper
[
45
]
it follows that if
k\neq 0

\sigma_{i}^{2}\mapsto\varepsilon_{i,i+1}^{k}\varepsilon_{i+1,i}^{k}=\psi_{k,k}(\tau_{i})

to

. Since

(3) For

.
∎

5.2.
Linear representations

As a corollary from the previous section, in the present section we construct a linear representations of the groups
T_{n}
. In order to do it we use the Magnus approach for construction of linear representations from representations by automorphisms of free groups. This approach is described, for example, in
[
8
, Section 4.1]
. In the present section we will just introduce the final representation we got.

Let
M
be the free module with the basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n-1}\}
over the ring
R=\mathbb{Z}[t_{1}^{\pm 1},t_{2}^{\pm 1},\ldots,t_{n}^{\pm 1}]
of Laurent polynomials with variables
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}
. Define the map
\Theta:T_{n}\to\mathrm{Aut}(M)
which acts on the generators
\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\dots,\tau_{n-1}
by the following rule

\Theta(\tau_{i}):\begin{cases}e_{i}\longmapsto t_{i+1}^{-1}e_{i}+t_{i+1}^{-1}(t_{i}-1)e_{i+1},&\\
e_{j}\longmapsto e_{j},&j\not=i.\end{cases}

The following statement obviously follows from the fact that the matrices of the elements
\Theta(\tau_{i})
in the basis
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}
are triangular.

Proposition 5.3
.

The image
\operatorname{Im}\Theta
is a solvable group. Hence, for
n>2
the representation
\Theta
has a non-trivial kernel.

5.3.
Universal pure braid group

In this section we study universal pure braid groups on small amount of strands. Consider the homomorphism
\pi:UB_{n}\to S_{n}
given on generators of
UB_{n}
by the rule
\pi(\sigma_{i})=\pi(\tau_{i})=(i,i+1)
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n-1
.
The kernel
\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
of this homomorphism is known as the
universal pure braid group
, denoted by
U{P_{n}}
. The following short exact sequence holds:

1\to UP_{n}\to UB_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\to}}S_{n}\to 1.

It is obvious that
P_{n}
is a subgroup of
U{P_{n}}
. Hence, the last group contains all generators and relations of
P_{n}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following properties hold:

1)
UB_{2}=B_{2}*T_{2}\cong F_{2}
,
UB_{3}=B_{3}*T_{3}=B_{3}*F_{2}

2)
U{P_{2}}=F_{3}
,

Proof.

1) Follows from the presentations of
UB_{2}
and
UB_{3}
.

2)
We will find the presentation of
UP_{3}=\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
by the Reidemeister-Shreier method using the Shreier set
\{1,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2},\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\}
. Since the restriction of homomorphism
\pi
on

has
P_{3}
as a kernel, it is enough to consider only generators
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
of

. Hence we get

\displaystyle S_{1,\tau_{1}}=\tau_{1}\cdot(\overline{\tau_{1}})^{-1}=\tau_{1}\cdot\sigma_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle S_{1,\tau_{2}}=\tau_{2}\cdot(\overline{\tau_{2}})^{-1}=\tau_{2}\cdot\sigma_{2}^{-1},

\displaystyle S_{\sigma_{1},\tau_{1}}=\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}\cdot(\overline{\sigma_{1}\tau_{1}})^{-1}=\sigma_{1}\tau_{1},

\displaystyle S_{\sigma_{1},\tau_{2}}=\sigma_{1}\tau_{2}\cdot(\overline{\sigma_{1}\tau_{2}})^{-1}=\sigma_{1}\tau_{2}\sigma_{2}^{-1}\sigma_{1}^{-1},

\displaystyle S_{\sigma_{2},\tau_{1}}=\sigma_{2}\tau_{1}\cdot(\overline{\sigma_{2}\sigma_{1}})^{-1}=\sigma_{2}\tau_{1}\sigma_{1}^{-1}\sigma_{2}^{-1},

\displaystyle S_{\sigma_{2},\tau_{2}}=\sigma_{2}\tau_{2},

T_{3}
is free, all of its subgroups are also free. Hence, we have the decomposition for

.

The following properties hold:

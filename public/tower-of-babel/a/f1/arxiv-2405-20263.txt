Abstract
. For a set ℱ \mathcal{F} of finite tournaments, the ℱ \mathcal{F} -free orientation problem is the problem of orienting a given finite undirected graph in such a way that the resulting oriented graph does not contain any member of ℱ \mathcal{F} . Using the theory of smooth approximations, we give a new shorter proof of the complexity dichotomy for such problems obtained recently by Bodirsky and Guzmán-Pro. In fact, our approach yields a complexity dichotomy for a considerably larger class of computational problems where one is given an undirected graph along with additional local constraints on the allowed orientations. Moreover, the border between tractable and hard problems is described by a decidable algebraic condition.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The graph orientation problem

For a set
\mathcal{F}
of oriented graphs, the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem
is the following problem: given a finite undirected graph
G
, can the edges of
G
be oriented in such a way that the resulting oriented graph does not contain any member of
\mathcal{F}
?
Two natural questions arise in this context. On the one hand, one might aim for a structural classification of those graphs admitting an
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation.
Early results in this direction are due to Skrien
[
38
]
, who provides a characterisation of the
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs with
\mathcal{F}
ranging over all possible combinations of oriented paths on three vertices.
Another classical result, the Gallai–Hasse–Roy–Vitaver Theorem
[
23
,
25
,
36
,
40
]
, implies that for every
k\geq 1
there is a set of oriented graphs
\mathcal{F}_{k}
such that a graph is
k
-colourable if and only if it admits an
\mathcal{F}_{k}
-free orientation. On the other hand, one can investigate the computational complexity of the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem. In the case where
\mathcal{F}
consists of oriented paths on three vertices only, the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem admits a polynomial-time reduction to 2-SAT
[
35
]
, and hence is tractable. In the general case, however, the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem might be equal to an NP-complete vertex coloring problem, as mentioned above.
Recently, Bodirsky and Guzmán-Pro have shown that if
\mathcal{F}
consists of tournaments, then the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem is either solvable in polynomial time or NP-complete
[
9
]
, thus establishing a complexity dichotomy.

The present paper provides a new shorter proof of this complexity dichotomy using the novel algebraic theory of smooth approximations from
[
33
]
(see also
[
31
]
). The main advantage of this approach is that the new proof avoids most ad-hoc arguments and lines up smoothly with proofs of other complexity dichotomies using the same theory, thus making it amenable to further generalizations.

Such generalizations include the following.

•

Problems where we are not necessarily given a graph, but more generally a list of constraints on vertices given by quantifier-free formulas over the language of graphs, in the spirit of Graph-SAT problems classified in
[
13
]
. This is achieved in the present paper for problems where we are given a graph enhanced with additional local constraints which prescribe some labelled cliques of the given graph to belong to a fixed set of allowed tournaments after orientation.

•

The
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem for sets
\mathcal{F}
of oriented graphs which are not necessarily tournaments. Using a theorem of Cherlin, Shelah, and Shi
[
22
]
,
and its refinement by Hubička and Nešetřil
[
27
]
, this problem can be modelled as the constraint satisfaction problem of a template enjoying similar properties to that modelling the situation where
\mathcal{F}
consists of tournaments, provided that
\mathcal{F}
is closed under homomorphic images (more precisely, the template then is a
first-order reduct of a finitely bounded Ramsey structure
, to be introduced below).

•

More general edge-coloring problems captured by the logic GMSNP
[
5
]
, whose complexity classification belongs to the most prominent open problems in infinite-domain constraint satisfaction.

1.2.
Constraint Satisfaction Problems (CSPs)

For a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
in some finite relational signature
\tau
, the
Constraint Satisfaction Problem of
\mathbb{A}
, denoted by
\operatorname{CSP}(\mathbb{A})
, is the membership problem for the class of all finite
\tau
-structures which have a homomorphism into
\mathbb{A}
; the structure
\mathbb{A}
is called a
template
of the CSP.

For certain
\mathcal{F}
(including all sets of tournaments), the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem can be viewed as the CSP of an (in general necessarily infinite) undirected graph with strong model-theoretic properties. We elaborate on this claim below. This perspective provides access to the rich literature on infinite-domain constraint satisfaction.

It is easy to see that the class
\mathcal{K}
of finite
\mathcal{F}
-free oriented graphs has the
hereditary property
, i.e. is closed under (induced) substructures.
If all elements of
\mathcal{F}
are connected, then
\mathcal{K}
is closed under taking disjoint unions. Consequently, there exists an infinite oriented graph
D
whose finite substructures are precisely the elements of
\mathcal{K}
up to isomorphism. This oriented graph can be obtained simply by taking the disjoint union of all structures in
\mathcal{K}
. Let
H
be the undirected graph reduct of
D
. For any finite undirected graph
G
we then have that
G
embeds into
H
if and only if it is a positive instance of the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem. If
\mathcal{F}
consists of tournaments (or more generally, is closed under homomorphic images), this is the case if and only if
G
has a homomorphism into
H
. We thus have that the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem is the same as
\operatorname{CSP}(H)
.
Finally, if
\mathcal{F}
consists of tournaments, whose underlying undirected graph is complete, then the class
\mathcal{K}
is even closed under taking unions. Equivalently, in model-theoretic jargon, the class
\mathcal{K}
has the free amalgamation property. Consequently, the infinite oriented graph
D
can be chosen to be
homogeneous
: any finite isomorphism between finite induced substructures of
D
extends to an automorphism of
D
(see Fraïssé’s theorem in
[
26
]
).
In fact, there is only one such structure up to isomorphism which we denote by
D_{\mathcal{F}}
. Accordingly, we denote the undirected graph reduct of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
by
H_{\mathcal{F}}
; thus the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem is equal to
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
.

The structure
H_{\mathcal{F}}
has the advantage that it lies within the scope of the infinite-domain CSP dichotomy conjecture
[
17
]
, and thus the methods developed for that scope for more than a decade apply. Indeed, one of the goals of the present paper is to apply the full power of the available techniques, thus aligning the proof of the complexity classification for
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
, and in fact generalizations thereof, with the bigger picture both conceptually and technically.

1.3.
The results

We start by stating the result for
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
; however, before stating it in full detail (Theorem
1
), we first provide an informal description. Our complexity dichotomy distinguishes the following two cases. In the first case,
H_{\mathcal{F}}
can
pp-construct
, i.e. construct in a certain precise way, any finite structure
[
3
]
; it then follows immediately that
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
is NP-complete. In the second case, there is a ternary function
f
acting on the two-element set
\{\leftarrow,\to\}
whose values are invariant under cyclic permutations of its arguments, and such that whenever
f
is applied componentwise to the arcs of three tuples enumerating
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs (returning no arc whenever one of the three tuples has no arc), then the resulting tuple also enumerates an
\mathcal{F}
-free graph. This “symmetry” of the solution space of
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
implies that the latter is in P. If
\mathcal{F}
is finite, then the border between the two cases can be checked algorithmically. In full detail, using the technical notions found in the preliminaries, the following dichotomy for the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation problem holds.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite set of forbidden tournaments. Then precisely one of the following holds:

•

The polymorphism clone
\operatorname{Pol}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
has a uniformly continuous minion homomorphism to
\mathscr{P}
. In this case,
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
is NP-complete by
[
14
]
.

•

The polymorphism clone
\operatorname{Pol}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
contains a ternary operation that is pseudo-cyclic modulo
\overline{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})}
. In this case,
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
is in
P
, as follows from
[
12
]
.

Our proof however yields a much more general result, thanks to the strength and flexibility of the new proof method. Note that the edge relation of the undirected graph
H_{\mathcal{F}}
can be first-order defined in
D_{\mathcal{F}}
by the formula
(x\to y)\vee(y\to x)
, where
\to
is the edge relation of the oriented graph
D_{\mathcal{F}}
; we say that
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is a
first-order reduct
of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
. More generally, let
k\geq 2
and consider a first-order definable
k
-ary relation
R
on
D_{\mathcal{F}}
all of whose tuples induce cliques in
H_{\mathcal{F}}
. Then using the homogeneity of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, it is not hard to see that
R
is defined by a disjunction
\phi_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\phi_{\ell}
, where each
\phi_{i}
is a conjunction describing a tournament on a
k
-tuple; in other words,
R

consists of all
k
-tuples inducing in
D_{\mathcal{F}}
one of a finite number of allowed tournaments. Let
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
are relations of this form, and let
(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
be the expansion of the graph
H_{\mathcal{F}}
by these relations. Then
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
is the problem where we are given an undirected graph
G
, together with constraints for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
requiring that certain tuples of vertices of this graph must belong to
R_{i}
(i.e., belong to the set of tournaments allowed by
R_{i}
); the question is whether the graph can be oriented to become an
\mathcal{F}
-free oriented graph in such a way that all the constraints are satisfied.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite set of forbidden tournaments, and let
R_{1},\ldots,R_{m}
be relations first-order definable in
D_{\mathcal{F}}
consisting of tuples inducing tournaments. Then precisely one of the following holds:

•

The polymorphism clone
\operatorname{Pol}(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
has a uniformly continuous minion homomorphism to
\mathscr{P}
. In this case,
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
is NP-complete by
[
14
]
.

•

The polymorphism clone
\operatorname{Pol}(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
contains a ternary operation that is pseudo-cyclic modulo
\overline{\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})}
. In this case,
\operatorname{CSP}(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
is in
P
, as follows from f
[
12
]
.

As an example, consider the
\mathcal{F}
-free orientation completion problem
, where some edges of the input graph might already be oriented, and the task is to orient the remaining edges as to obtain an
\mathcal{F}
-free oriented graph. Bodirsky and Guzmán-Pro in
[
9
]
showed that each such problem is equivalent to the CSP with a 2-element template, thus obtaining a P/NP-complete complexity dichotomy. We obtain this result as a direct consequence of Theorem
2
by considering the structure
(H_{\mathcal{F}},\{\to\})
.

1.4.
Related work

Constraint satisfaction problems with a finite template are known to exhibit a P/NP-complete complexity dichotomy by the recent theorem of Bulatov and Zhuk
[
20
,
41
]
. If the template is infinite, however, then there is provably no complexity dichotomy, even under the assumption of
\omega
-categoricity
[
8
,
24
]
, a model-theoretic notion that can be viewed as an approximation of finiteness (see Section
2.1
for a precise definition). It is however conjectured that the CSPs of certain infinite
\omega
-categorical structures, more specifically of
first-order reducts of finitely bounded homogeneous structures
, do in fact exhibit a P/NP-complete complexity dichotomy.
For a modern formulation of the conjecture, originally posed by Bodirsky and Pinsker in 2012
[
17
, Conjecture 1.2]
, see
[
3
, Conjecture 1.9]
. Here we only provide the necessary information about the scope of the conjecture.
A homogeneous relational structure
\mathbb{B}
with finite relational signature
\tau
is said to be
finitely bounded
if there is some natural number
b_{\mathbb{B}}
such that a finite
\tau
-structure
\mathbb{F}
embeds into
\mathbb{B}
(i.e. is isomorphic to some substructure of
\mathbb{B}
) if and only if every substructure
\mathbb{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{F}
of size at most
b_{\mathbb{B}}
embeds into
\mathbb{B}
. By taking
b_{D_{\mathcal{F}}}\geq 2
bigger than the size of any tournament in
\mathcal{F}
, it follows that
D_{\mathcal{F}}
is a finitely bounded homogeneous structure, so the CSP of its first-order reduct
H_{\mathcal{F}}
falls into the scope of the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture.

The conjecture has been verified for various classes of structures such as the first-order reducts of
(\mathbb{Q},<)

[
10
]
, of any homogeneous graph
[
11
]
including the random graph
[
13
]
, the universal homogeneous poset
[
29
]
, the universal homogeneous tournament
[
31
]
, certain homogeneous hypergraphs including the universal one
[
30
]
, and many more. The recent theory of smooth approximations
[
31
,
33
]
conceptualizes and generalizes the methods that have been repeatedly used in the proofs of the above dichotomies; it has been used to obtain for the first time the last two results mentioned above, and to reprove the previous ones in a streamlined manner. Our complexity dichotomy constitutes progress on the Bodirsky-Pinsker conjecture for first-order reducts of finitely bounded homogeneous oriented graphs, while our proof refines the smooth approximations approach along the way. The reason for the latter is that the automorphism group of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
(except in trivial cases) acts on pairs of distinct vertices with three orbits (a pair
(x,y)
can be non-adjacent, or an arc, or an inverted arc). In contrast to this, the previous classifications above had at most two such orbits; on a technical level, this step is reminiscent of the challenge of passing from a two-element domain to a three-element domain in the realm of finite-domain CSPs
[
37
,
19
]
.

1.5.
Outline of the article

In Section
2
we introduce the necessary notions from the universal-algebraic approach to infinite-domain CSPs and review a description of the automorphism group of
H_{\mathcal{F}}
from
[
1
]
. Section
3
is devoted to establishing that the structure under consideration can be assumed to be a
model-complete core
, i.e. in a sense the smallest template of its CSP.
We continue with several preparatory observations about the
polymorphism clones
, i.e., “higher-ary symmetries” of the CSP templates at hand in Section
4
.
Finally, Theorem
2
is proved by a direct application of the theory of smooth approximations in Section
5
.

2.
Preliminary facts

All structures appearing in this article are tacitly assumed to be (at most) countable, and their signatures are assumed to be finite.

2.1.
Model-theoretic notions

A relational structure
\mathbb{B}
is
homogeneous
if every isomorphism between finite induced substructures of
\mathbb{B}
extends to an automorphism of
\mathbb{B}
.
If
\mathcal{F}
is a set of tournaments, then there exists a homogeneous oriented graph whose (induced) subgraphs are, up to isomorphism, precisely all
\mathcal{F}
-free oriented graphs (see e.g.
[
26
]
); this oriented graph is unique up to isomorphism and shall be denoted by
D_{\mathcal{F}}
. It is
\omega
-categorical
, i.e. its automorphism group
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
acts on its
k
-tuples (componentwise) with only finitely many orbits, for all
k\geq 1
. We denote the underlying set of vertices of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
by
V
and its edge relation by
\to
; the flipped edge relation shall be denoted by
\leftarrow
.

The edge relation
E
of the undirected graph
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is defined in
D_{\mathcal{F}}
by
\{(x,y)\;|\;(x\to y)\vee(x\leftarrow y)\}
. This graph is not necessarily homogeneous, but
\omega
-categorical since
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})\supseteq\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
. In fact, any structure with a first-order definition in
D_{\mathcal{F}}
is
\omega
-categorical, for the same reason; such structures are called
first-order reducts
of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
.
We denote by
N\subseteq V^{2}
the
non-edges
of
H_{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. injective pairs of vertices which are not adjacent in
H_{\mathcal{F}}
. We say that two tuples
x,y
of elements of
V
of the same length have the same
edge-type
if the map sending the former onto the latter is a partial isomorphism on
H_{\mathcal{F}}
. Only if
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is homogeneous this is equivalent to
x,y
belonging to the same
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
-orbit.

The class
\operatorname{Age}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
,
the age of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, consisting of finite substructures of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
up to isomorphism, has
free amalgamation
, i.e. it is closed under not necessarily disjoint unions. This implies (see e.g.
[
16
]
) that there exists a linear order
<
on
V
such that
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<):=(V;\to,<)
is homogeneous and has precisely the class of linearly ordered
\mathcal{F}
-free graphs as its substructures; it is also
\omega
-categorical. The order
<
is isomorphic to the order of the rationals and the structure
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
is unique up to isomorphism and called the
free superposition
of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
with the order of the rationals. It enjoys the
Ramsey property

[
27
]
, a combinatorial property whose definition itself will not be needed; we shall make a single reference to a consequence of it in Section
2.3
.

Another consequence of
\operatorname{Age}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
being a free amalgamation class is that
D_{\mathcal{F}}
has
no algebraicity
(see e.g.
[
21
]
), i.e. for all finite
F\subseteq V
, every orbit of the pointwise stabilizer of
F
within
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
, acting on
V\setminus F
, is infinite.
As
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})\supseteq\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
, also
H_{\mathcal{F}}
has no algebraicity and more generally the same holds true for any first-order reduct of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
.

2.2.
Universal-algebraic notions

A
polymorphism

f
of a relational structure
\mathbb{A}
is a homomorphism from a finite power
\mathbb{A}^{n}
of
\mathbb{A}
to
\mathbb{A}
; here
n
is said to be the
arity
of
f
.
Polymorphisms are closed under composition and every coordinate projection
\mathbb{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}
for arbitrary
n
is a polymorphism of
\mathbb{A}
. This implies that the set
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{A})
consisting of all polymorphisms of
\mathbb{A}
, called the
polymorphism clone
of
\mathbb{A}
, is a
function clone
.

In general, a function clone
\mathscr{C}
on a fixed set
C
, its
domain
, is a set of finitary operations on
C
containing all projections and which is closed under arbitrary composition. For
n\geq 1
, the set of
n
-ary functions of
\mathscr{C}
will be denoted by
\mathscr{C}^{(n)}
. We also say that the clone
\mathscr{C}

acts on

C
. But
\mathscr{C}
has various other actions: on any power
C^{I}
of
C
, by componentwise evaluation, on any subset
S
of
C
that is invariant under the action of
\mathscr{C}
, by restriction, and on
S/\sim
, for an equivalence relation
\sim
on
S
that is invariant under the action of
\mathscr{C}
, by evaluating operations on representatives of the
\sim
-classes. We write
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright C^{I}
,
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright S
and
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright S/\sim
for these actions, and call
(S,\sim)
a
subfactor
of
\mathscr{C}
.
A subfactor
(S,\sim)
of
\mathscr{C}
such that
S/\sim
contains at least two elements and such that
\mathscr{C}
acts on
S/\sim
by projections is called a
naked set
.

For
n\geq 1
, an
n
-ary operation
c
is called
cyclic
if it satisfies
c(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})=c(a_{2},\ldots,a_{n},a_{1})
for all elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
of its domain; we indicate this by writing
c(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\approx c(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},x_{1})
. It is called
pseudo-cyclic modulo
U
, where
U
is a set of unary functions on its domain, if there exist
u,v\in U
such that
u\circ c(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\approx v\circ c(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},x_{1})
.
A 6-ary operation
s
is called
Siggers
if
s(x,y,x,z,y,z)\approx s(y,x,z,x,z,y)
.
Pseudo-Siggers operations modulo
U
are defined accordingly.
A ternary operation
m
is called a
majority
if
m(x,x,y)\approx m(x,y,x)\approx m(y,x,x)
. An operation
f
is
idempotent
if
f(x,\ldots,x)\approx x
; a function clone is idempotent if all of its operations are.

A mapping
\xi\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
between two function clones is a
clone homomorphism
if it preserves arities, maps the
i
th coordinate projection to the
i
th coordinate projection (of the same arity) and respects the composition of functions. Spelled out this means, for all
n
and all
n
-ary functions
f\in\mathscr{C}
and all
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
of some arity
k
, it holds
\xi(f\circ(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}))=\xi(f)\circ(\xi(g_{1}),\dots,\xi(g_{n}))
. Observe that for a subfactor
(S,\sim)
of
\mathscr{C}
, the natural mapping
\xi\colon\mathscr{C}\to(\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright S/\sim)
is a clone homomorphism. A clone
\mathscr{C}
is said to be
equationally trivial
if it homomorphically maps into the clone of projections on a two element domain, which we denote by
\mathscr{P}
. Otherwise it is called
equationally non-trivial
.

A mapping
\xi\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{D}
between two function clones is a
minion homomorphism
if it preserves arities and respects
minors
, i.e.
\xi(f(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)}))=\xi(f)(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)})
for all
f\in\mathscr{C}
and all functions
\sigma\colon\{1,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

2.3.
Topology and canonicity

For all
n\geq 1
, the set
V^{V^{n}}
of all
n
-ary functions on
V
is naturally equipped with the
topology of pointwise convergence
, where for
f\in V^{V^{n}}
and
S\subseteq V^{V^{n}}
we have that
f
is contained in the closure
\overline{S}
of
S
if for all finite
F\subseteq V^{n}
there exists
g_{F}\in S
which agrees with
f
on
F
. If
\mathbb{A}
is any structure, then
\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{A})^{(n)}
is closed. If
\mathbb{A}
is any
\omega
-categorical structure, then
V^{V^{n}}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
, defined as the quotient space where
f,g\in V^{V^{n}}
are equivalent if
f\in\overline{\{\alpha g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\;|\;\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})\}}
, is compact (see e.g.
[
15
]
).

A function
f\in V^{V^{n}}
is
canonical
with respect to
\mathbb{A}
if
f
and
f(\beta_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\beta_{n}(x_{n}))
are equivalent in above factor space for all
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
; this means that
f
preserves the equivalence relations given by the partitions of
k
-tuples into
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
-orbits, and hence
f
acts on these orbits, for all
k\geq 1
. It is called
diagonally canonical
if this is the case whenever
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{n}
. For
f,g\in V^{V^{n}}
, we say that
g
locally interpolates
f
modulo
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
if
f\in\overline{\{\alpha g(\beta_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,\beta_{n}(x_{n}))\;|\;\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})\}}\;.

Diagonal interpolation
is defined similarly, again by requiring
\beta_{1}=\cdots=\beta_{n}
. Since
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
has the Ramsey property, every function
g\in V^{V^{n}}
locally (diagonally) interpolates modulo
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)

2.4.
Smooth approximations

In
[
31
]
different notions of

are introduced, which we shall briefly recall here.
Let
A
be a set,
n\geq 1
,
S
and
X
subsets of
A^{n}
with
S\subseteq X
and
{\sim}
some equivalence relation on
S
. An equivalence relation
\eta
on
X

approximates

{\sim}
if the restriction of
\eta
to
X
is a (possibly non-proper) refinement of
{\sim}
. Let
\mathscr{G}
be a permutation group acting on

leaving the
\sim

. We say that the approximation

is

•

presmooth (with respect to
\mathscr{G}
)
if for every equivalence class
C
of
\sim
there is some equivalence class

of

such that

contains two disjoint tuples in the same

-orbit;

•

very smooth (with respect to
\mathscr{G}
)
if
\mathscr{G}
-orbit equivalence on
X
is a (possibly non-proper) refinement of
\eta
on

.

We now exhibit some sufficient conditions under which a presmooth approximation can be upgraded to a very smooth approximation. The following result is a variant of Lemma 8 in
[
31
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a homogeneous relational structure whose age has free amalgamation and
\mathbb{A}
a first-order reduct of
\mathbb{F}
. Let
\mathscr{C}\subseteq\operatorname{Pol}(\mathbb{A})
be some clone above
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
, preserving
{\not=}
and acting on
X\subseteq A^{k}
for some
k\geq 1
, such that
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright X
preserves
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit equivalence. If
(S,\sim)
is some naked set of
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright X
with
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-invariant
\sim
-classes and
\eta
some
\mathscr{C}
-invariant equivalence relation on
X
which presmoothly approximates
\sim
with respect to
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
, then there is a naked set
(S^{\prime},\sim)
of
\mathscr{C}\curvearrowright X
that is very smoothly approximated by
\eta
with respect to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
.

Proof.

Let
T
be the set consisting of
a\in X
such that there is disjoint
b\in X
in the same
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit with
a\eta b
. The set
T
is
\mathscr{C}
-invariant as
{\not=}
,
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit equivalence on
X
and
\eta
are. Since the approximation

is presmooth with respect to
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
,

intersects every
\sim
-class non-trivially. Setting

and letting

be the restriction of
\sim
to

it follows that
(S^{\prime},\sim^{\prime})
is a naked set of
\mathscr{C}
with
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-invariant equivalence classes. Moreover
\eta
presmoothly approximates
\sim^{\prime}
with respect to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
.

We are left showing that the approximation is very smooth with respect to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
. To do so take
a\in S
and denote its
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit by
O
. By construction of

there is
b\in O
, disjoint from
a
with
a\eta b
. Since
\operatorname{Age}(\mathbb{F})
has free amalgamation, there is

such that no relations hold between

and

and moreover
(a,b)
and
(c,b)
belong to the same
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit. The invariance of

implies

, hence

. For arbitrary

we choose

such that no relations hold between

and

and between

and

, which is again possible by free amalgamation. Homogeneity of

implies that
(a,c)
,
(r,s)
and
(r,t)
all lie in the same
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{F})
-orbit, whence

and

. In summary,

is contained in a single

-class, and since

was chosen arbitrarily the claim follows.
∎

2.5.
The automorphism group of
H_{\mathcal{F}}

In
[
1
]
Agarwal and Kompatscher give a description of the automorphism group of first-order reducts of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
. This description turns out to be useful for our purposes (it is crucially used in Lemma
10
and Lemma
13
), so we detail the relevant results.
For a set
F\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(V)
, we write
\langle F\rangle
to denote the closed subgroup of
\operatorname{Sym}(V)
generated by
F
.

A
flip of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
is any function
fl\in\operatorname{Sym}(V)
preserving
E
and
N
and flipping every arc of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. every pair in
\to
is sent to a pair in
\leftarrow
. It is easy to see that whenever

is a flip of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, then the group
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})\cup\{fl\}\rangle
contains all flips of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, and that the inverse of any flip is again a flip of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
.

A
simple switch of

is any
sw\in\operatorname{Sym}(V)
preserving
E
and
N
such that there exists some
a\in V
such that
sw
inverts an arc of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
if and only if it is incident to
a
. Again, if
sw
is some simple switch of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, then the closed subgroup
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})\cup\{sw\}\rangle
contains all simple switches of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
, and the inverse of any simple switch is again a simple switch. As a generalization of a simple switch we define for any finite set
A\subseteq V
an
A
-switch of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
to be any function
sw_{A}\in\operatorname{Sym}(V)
that preserves
E
and
N
and that flips an arc of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
precisely if the arc is incident to a unique vertex in
A
. If we do not specify
A
, we call any such function a
switch
.

Theorem 4
.

[
1
, Theorem 2.2]

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a first-order reduct of
D_{\mathcal{F}}
with
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
such that one of the following cases applies:

•

the graph
H_{\mathcal{F}}
is not homogeneous;

•

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
is a proper subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(H_{\mathcal{F}})
.

Then
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
is among the following groups:
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})
,
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})\cup\{fl\}\rangle
,
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})\cup\{sw\}\rangle
,
\langle\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}})\cup\{fl,sw\}\rangle
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be as in Theorem
4
. Then for every
\alpha\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{A})
and for every finite set
F\subseteq V
there exists
a switch
\beta

and a flip
\gamma
such that
\alpha
agrees with
\beta
or with
\gamma\circ\beta
on
F
.

Proof.

Note that every automorphism of

is a
\emptyset
-switch. Hence, by Theorem
4
, we have that
\alpha
agrees with some permutation
\beta_{1}\circ\cdots\circ\beta_{n}
on

, where each

is either a switch or a flip. Since the composition of two switches is a switch, and the composition of two flips is an automorphism of

, we may assume that there are no two consecutive switches or flips in

. Moreover, the composition

of a switch

with a flip

is equal to the composition

∎

3.
Model-complete cores

Henceforth, we let
\mathbb{A}=(H_{\mathcal{F}},R_{1},\ldots,R_{m})
be as in Theorem
2
. In a first step, we observe that we can assume
\mathbb{A}
to be a model-complete core. A structure
\mathbb{B}
is a
model-complete core
if for every endomorphism
g
of
\mathbb{B}
and every finite subset
F
of its domain there is an automorphism of
\mathbb{B}
which agrees with
g
on
F
. For every
\omega
-categorical structure
\mathbb{B}
there exists a model-complete core
\mathbb{C}
which has the same CSP as
\mathbb{B}
; this structure is unique up to isomorphism
[
6
]
. Any model-complete core
\mathbb{B}
has the property that all orbits of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B})
on tuples are
pp-definable
in
\mathbb{B}
, the latter meaning that they are first-order definable using only conjunction, existential quantification, and equality.

Lemma 6
.

\mathbb{A}
is a model-complete core, or its model-complete core is either a one-element structure, or a first-reduct of a homogeneous graph or of the universal homogeneous tournament (i.e. the unique homogeneous tournament whose age equals all finite tournaments).

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{C}
be the model-complete core of
\mathbb{A}
. If
E=\emptyset
, then all relations of
\mathbb{A}
are empty, and
\mathbb{C}
is a one-element structure. Assume henceforth that
E\neq\emptyset
. By the main theorem of
[
32
]
,
\mathbb{C}
can be assumed to be a first-order reduct of a homogeneous substructure
\mathbb{B}
of
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
. More specifically each relation of
\mathbb{C}
is defined by a quantifier-free formula defining the corresponding relation of
\mathbb{A}
, so
\mathbb{C}
is the structure which
\mathbb{A}
induces on the domain of
\mathbb{B}
. Moreover there exists an endomorphism
g
of
\mathbb{A}
with the following properties:

(1)

the range of
g
is contained in
\mathbb{C}
, and the age of the structure induced by the range of
g
in
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
equals
\operatorname{Age}(\mathbb{B})
;

(2)

g
acts on the orbits of
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
;

(3)

g
is
range-rigid
, i.e. it acts as the identity function on those orbits of
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
that intersect the range of
g
.

We will first establish that
g
is monotonous with respect to
<
. Let
O
be the orbit of
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
that is the image under

of the
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
-orbit

consisting of increasing (henceforth to be understood with respect to
<
) non-edges. Then

consists of increasing pairs: if

is any
\operatorname{Aut}(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
-orbit in the range of

consisting of increasing pairs, then there exist
x,y,z\in V
with
(x,y),(y,z)\in W
and
(x,z)\in U
; but then
(g(x),g(y)),(g(y),g(z))\in W
by range-rigidity of

, so the pair
(g(x),g(z))\in O
is increasing by transitivity.
For arbitrary

with

, there is

with

, so

imply

.

Assume first that the arcs in the range of

are increasing only, or decreasing only. Let
\mathbb{H}
be the graph induced by

on the domain of
\mathbb{C}
, and note that
\mathbb{H}
is a first-order reduct of
\mathbb{C}
. The following argument shows that
\mathbb{H}
is homogeneous and that

is contained in

is a first-order reduct of
\mathbb{H}
.
Let

be isomorphic tuples in
\mathbb{H}
. There are automorphisms

of

such that

are both increasing. We then have that

are isomorphic even in

, by the fact that

is monotonous. Since

and

act as endomorphisms on
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbb{C}
is a model-complete core, there are automorphisms of
\mathbb{C}
which agree with the two functions on

, respectively. Since

are isomorphic in
(D_{\mathcal{F}},<)
, the homogeneity of
\mathbb{B}
then implies that they belong to the same orbit of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{B})
and hence also of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
. Putting this together,

belong to the same orbit of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{C})
, so

is homogeneous as every automorphism of
\mathbb{C}
is an automorphism of

. Moreover the argument shows that

is contained in

We now assume that the range of

contains both increasing and decreasing arcs. Assume first that

intersects the range of

is a model-complete core. Finally, assume that

is the universal homogeneous ordered tournament

is a first-order reduct of

[

, Theorem 4.4.4]
.
∎

We remark that the notion of

for

considered by Bodirsky and Guzmán-Pro in

]

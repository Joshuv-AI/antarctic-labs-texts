Statistical Error of Numerical Integrators for Underdamped Langevin Dynamics with Deterministic And Stochastic Gradients
Xuda Ye, Zhennan Zhou

Abstract
We propose a novel discrete Poisson equation approach to estimate the statistical error of a broad class of numerical integrators for the underdamped Langevin dynamics. The statistical error refers to the mean square error of the estimator to the exact ensemble average with a finite number of iterations. With the proposed error analysis framework, we show that when the potential function U ⁡ ( x ) U(x) is strongly convex in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} and the numerical integrator has strong order p p , the statistical error is 𝒪 ⁡ ( h 2 ​ p + 1 N ​ h ) \mathcal{O}(h^{2p}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh}) , where h h is the time step and N N is the number of iterations. Besides, this approach can be adopted to analyze integrators with stochastic gradients, and quantitative estimates can be derived as well. Our approach only requires the geometric ergodicity of the continuous-time underdamped Langevin dynamics, and relaxes the constraint on the time step.



Keywords
underdamped Langevin dynamics, geometric ergodicity, discrete Poisson equation, statistical error

Introduction

The computation of the ensemble average from a target Boltzmann distribution stands as a cornerstone challenge in both molecular dynamics
[
34
,
24
,
25
]
and data science
[
43
,
42
]
. We examine the Hamiltonian system

H(x,v)=\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+U(x),

where

represent the particle’s position and velocity in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively, and
U(x)
denotes the potential function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Our primary focus is the error analysis of numerical methods employed for sampling from the target Boltzmann distribution

\pi(x,v)=\frac{1}{Z}e^{-H(x,v)},~~~~Z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{-H(x,v)}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v.

In the realm of molecular dynamics, an arsenal of numerical methods has been proposed with the aim of effectively sampling from
\pi(x,v)
. These methods encompass Langevin dynamics
[
34
,
24
,
33
]
, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC)
[
1
,
19
]
, Andersen dynamics
[
41
,
2
]
, and Nosé–Hoover dynamics
[
17

. In this paper, we focus on the time discretization of the underdamped Langevin dynamics

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{t}&=v_{t},\\
\dot{v}_{t}&=-\nabla U(x_{t})-\gamma v_{t}+\sqrt{2\gamma}\dot{B}_{t},\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
x_{t}
and
v_{t}
denote the position and velocity variables in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively. Here,
\gamma>0
represents the damping rate, and
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
signifies the Brownian motion in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By fixing the test function
f(x,v)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the ergodic theory dictates that the ensemble average
\pi(f)
can be obtained through the limit of the time average

\pi(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}f(x,v)\pi(x,v)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v=\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(x_{t},v_{t})\mathrm{d}t.

The time discretization of the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1

\pi(f)
attainable in practice. Suppose a numerical integrator with time step
h
yields the numerical solution
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
; in that case, the error in computing
\pi(f)
can be formulated as

e(N,h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(X_{n},V_{n})-\pi(f),

(2)

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
denotes the number of iterations.
As
N
tends to infinity and
h
decreases to 0, the error
e(N,h)
is expected to converge to 0, as the consistency of a numerical integrator requires. Therefore,
the accuracy of the numerical integrator can be characterized by:

•

the
bias

\mathbb{E}[e(N,h)]
, delineating the deviation of the numerical invariant distribution;

•

the
statistical error

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
, emanating from the bias and the random fluctuation of the numerical solutions
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
, also referred to as the
mean square error
.

While the bias indicates the average error of an ensemble of stochastic numerical solutions, the statistical error encapsulates the accuracy of the a single trajectory in a more holistic manner.
The objective of this paper is to estimate the statistical error of various numerical integrators for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
).

We study a large class of prevailing numerical integrators such as the Euler–Maruyama (
EM
) integrator and the UBU (

) integrator

Before we illustrate the main strategy to prove these results, we review the prevailing approaches to estimate the statistical error. In general, the bias of the numerical method can be estimated via the geometric ergodicity of the numerical method
[
28
,
44
,
22
]
, namely, the convergence rate of the distribution law of the numerical solution.
In comparison, the statistical error usually encompasses more information than the geometric ergodicity, and the analysis based on the functional inequalities
[
26
,
39
,
27
,
6
]
can be inappropriate for estimating the statistical error.
Currently, there are two major approaches to analyze the statistical error for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
).

The first one is to prove the global contractivity of the numerical integrator.
The utilization of the probabilistic coupling approach facilitates the establishment of global contractivity for a broad spectrum of numerical integrators, including both HMC
[
32
,
4
,
8
,
3
]
and Langevin discretization
[
37
,
31
,
11
]
. Subsequently, leveraging these contractivity results allows for a direct and explicit estimation of the statistical error.
However, the application of these results necessitates stringent conditions on the parameter: the time step
h
should be sufficiently small to ensure the global contractivity in a single step. When the numerical integrator is driven by stochastic gradients, it’s important to note that these contractivity results not always guarantee the optimal order in the time step
[
32
,
8
]
.

The second one is to utilize the Poisson equation.
The Poisson equation approach has gained prominence in the analysis of overdamped Langevin dynamics since the seminal work
[
29
]
. Subsequently, this approach has been utilized to scrutinize the statistical error of the Euler–Maruyama integrator
[
5
,
20
]
and the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD)
[
43
,
16
,
40
,
12
,
13
]
. Notably, the Poisson equation approach shares a close connection with Stein’s method
[
29
,
20
]
, and serves as a viable strategy to numerically augment the sampling efficiency of underdamped Langevin dynamics
[
14
,
9
,
35
]
.
It’s noteworthy that the efficacy of the Poisson equation approach primarily hinges upon the ability to pointwise estimate the solution to the Poisson equation
[
20
]
. In the overdamped scenario, such estimates can be achieved via
L^{p}
-estimates, owing to the fact that the generator
\mathcal{L}
is a uniformly elliptic operator. However, in the underdamped case, such estimates can pose significant challenges due to the hypoellipticity of the generator
\mathcal{L}
, thereby rendering the estimation of statistical error via the Poisson equation approach inherently difficult.

In this paper, we estimate the statistical error of numerical integrators for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) utilizing the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
. This solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
can be perceived as a time-discretized version of the original Poisson solution
\phi(x,v)
(see Section
5
). The utilization of
\phi_{h}(x,v)
enables the explicit representation of the time average in (
5
), and thus circumvents the need for high-order Taylor expansions on
\phi(x,v)
. Consequently, the statistical error of a wide class of numerical integrators can be examined using the same discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
.
Moreover, the constraint on the time step
h
is solely dependent on the uniform-in-time moment estimate in Lemmas
2
, which is typically less stringent than the requirement in the proof of global contractivity.
When the potential function
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we introduce a novel estimation approach for the second derivative
\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(x,v)
based on the tangent process
[
9
]
. This estimate of
\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(x,v)
facilitates the examination of the statistical error of stochastic gradient numerical integrators, via the explicit representation of the time average in (
48
).

We present a brief comparison of our results and those of related works. In the case of deterministic gradients, we provide the estimate of the statistical error for a broad class of numerical integrators in Theorems
2
and
3
. When
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the error order in the time step coincides with the strong order of the numerical integrator. Compared to those works based on the global contractivity of the numerical integrators
[
7
,
37
,
32
,
31
]
, our analysis only requires the geometric ergodicity of the continuous-time dynamics (
1
), and the numerical stability is simply guaranteed by the uniform-in-time moments of the numerical solution.
In this way, we relax the constraint of the upper bound on the time step
h
. However, there are some limitations in our results for numerical integrators with deterministic gradients. First, we are unable to obtain the optimal statistical error
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
when
U(x)
is not globally convex. As a comparison, it is proved in
[
4
,
11
]
that the numerical invariant distribution of the generalized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (gHMC) has an
\mathcal{O}(h^{2})
deviation from the true invariant distribution
\pi
, and thus the statistical error is
\mathcal{O}(h^{4}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
.
Also, the dependence on the parameters in Theorems
2
and
3
are not clear, especially on the spatial dimension
d
.
Finally, since the error order in the time step
h
is limited by the strong order, our results are suboptimal for the integrators whose weak order is larger than the strong order, such as the popular BAOAB integrator
[
24
,
46
,
23
]
.

For numerical integrators with stochastic gradients, the proposed approach leads to improved results which are not justified before. In particular, Theorem
4
showcases the optimal statistical error
\mathcal{O}(h^{2}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
when
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for both (
SG-EM
) and (
SG-UBU
). As a comparison, although the global contractivity of the stochastic gradient generalized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (SGgHMC) has been well established in
[
32
,
8
]
, these contractivity results do not provide an optimal error bound in terms of the stochastic gradients.
For instance, it is shown in
[
32
,
8
]
that the deviation of the numerical invariant distribution of SGgHMC from the true invariant distribution
\pi
is
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{h})
, resulting the statistical error to be only
\mathcal{O}(h\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
.
Theorem
4
also shows that there is no gain in the error order by using a second-order scheme for numerical integration, which aligns with the discourse presented in Proposition 18 of
[
32
]
.

2.3
Main idea of the statistical error estimate

Here’s a concise procedure outlining how to estimate the statistical error
\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
for various numerical integrators.

1.

Given the test function
f(x,v)
, construct the Kolmogorov solution as

u(x,v,t)=(e^{t\mathcal{L}}f)(x,v)-\pi(f)=\mathbb{E}^{x,v}[f(x_{t},v_{t})]-\pi(f),

where
\mathbb{E}^{x,v}
means the dynamics
(x_{t},v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
starts with the initial state
(x,v)
.

Then we employ the coupling contractivity of the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) prove
|\nabla u(x,v,t)|
have exponential decay in time (see Lemma
4
).

2.

Utilize the function
u(x,v,t)
to construct the discrete Poisson solution:

\phi_{h}(x,v)=h\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u(x,v,nh),

where
h>0
represents the time step of the numerical method. Formally,
\phi_{h}(x,v)
serves as the solution to the discrete Poisson equation:

\frac{1-e^{h\mathcal{L}}}{h}\phi_{h}(x,v)=f(x,v)-\pi(f),

where
\mathcal{L}
denotes the generator of (
1
). With the estimates of

|u(x,v,t)|
and
|\nabla u(x,v,t)|
,
|\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)|
can be globally bounded across the variables
x,v
.

3.

Consider the numerical solution
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
with time step
h
, and let
r\geqslant 1
. Utilizing the Lyapunov function
\mathcal{H}(x,v)
in (
12
), the uniform-in-time moment estimate

\sup_{n\geqslant 0}\big(|X_{n}|+|V_{n}|+1\big)^{2r}\leqslant C\big(|X_{0}|+|V_{0}|+1\big)^{2r}

holds true for some constant
C
independent of the time step
h
.

4.

For the numerical solution
Z_{n}=(X_{n},V_{n})
, the difference
\phi_{h}(Z_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(Z_{n})
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(Z_{n})}{h}+f(Z_{n})-\pi(f)=

(4)

\displaystyle\underbrace{\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(Z_{n}(h))}{h}}_{S_{n}:\text{ local error}}+\underbrace{\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{n}(h))-\phi_{h}(Z_{n})}{h}+f(Z_{n})-\pi(f)}_{T_{n}:\text{ mean-zero}}.

Here, the term
S_{n}
corresponds to the local error of the numerical integrator, and the term
T_{n}
is a mean-zero random variable, because

\mathbb{E}\big[T_{n}|Z_{n}\big]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\frac{(e^{h\mathcal{L}}\phi_{h})(Z_{n})-\phi_{h}(Z_{n})}{h}\bigg]+f(Z_{n})-\pi(f)=0.

As a consequence, the random variables
\{T_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
are mutually independent, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[T_{n}T_{m}]=0
for distinct
n,m\geqslant 0
.

5.

The summation of (
4
) over
n
gives

e(N,h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(Z_{n})-\pi(f)=\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{0})-\phi_{h}(Z_{N})}{Nh}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(S_{n}+T_{n}).

(5)

Then using Cauchy’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]

\displaystyle\leqslant 3\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{0})-\phi_{h}(Z_{N})}{Nh}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]+3\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]+3\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3}{N^{2}h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|\phi_{h}(Z_{0})-\phi_{h}(Z_{N})|^{2}\big]+\frac{3}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{2}]+\frac{3}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}],

(6)

yielding the quantitative estimate of the statistical error
\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
.

When the evolution time
Nh\gg 1
,
\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
primarily stems from the time discretization error in
\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{2}]
, which necessitates the estimation of the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
. When an estimate of
|\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)|
is available, an
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p-1})
error, as stated in Theorem
2
, can be achieved. When an estimate of
|\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(x,v)|
is attainable, an
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p})
error, as stated in Theorem
3
, can be attained.

Two significant advancements characterize our numerical analysis methodology. Firstly, we establish the boundedness of the second derivative
\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)
when the potential function
U(x)
exhibits global convexity (refer to Lemma
7
). This property stems from a thorough analysis of the contractivity in the tangent process, as delineated in Lemmas
5
and
6
. Secondly, in the error analysis framework, we employ the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
rather than the infinite integral
\phi(x,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(x,v,t)\mathrm{d}t
as commonly used in prior works
[
29
,
5
]
, to assess the statistical error. Notably, the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
ensures that
T_{n}
is a mean-zero random variable, thereby rendering
\{T_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
are mutually independent random variables.

3
Numerical integrator and stability

We introduce the definition of the strong order of a numerical integrator for underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
).
For convenience, in Sections
3
to
5
, we exclusively investigate numerical integrators driven by the deterministic gradient
\nabla U(x)
. The scenario involving the stochastic gradient
b(x,\omega)
will be addressed comprehensively in Section
6
.

The strong order of a numerical integrator is defined as follows.

Definition 1
.

Suppose
p>0
and
q\geqslant 1
. Given the intial state
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})
in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, the numerical integrator

Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})\mapsto Z_{1}=(X_{1},V_{1})

has
strong order
(p,q)
if

•

For some constant
C>0
, the local error can be decomposed as

Z_{1}-Z_{0}(h)=M_{h}+N_{h},

(7)

and the random variables
M_{h}
and
N_{h}
satisfy

\mathbb{E}[M_{h}]=0,~~~\mathbb{E}\big[|M_{h}|^{4}\big]\leqslant Ch^{4p+2}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q},~~~\mathbb{E}\big[|N_{h}|^{4}\big]\leqslant Ch^{4p+4}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q}.

(8)

•

For some constant
C>0
, the local error is bounded by

\mathbb{E}|Z_{1}-Z_{0}(h)|^{4}\leqslant Ch^{6}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4}.

(9)

•

For any
r\geqslant 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
r
such that

\mathbb{E}|Z_{1}|^{r}\leqslant C(|Z_{0}|+1)^{r}.

(10)

Here,
p>0
is the usual strong order of the numerical integrator, and
q\geqslant 1
signifies the order of moments required on the initial state
Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0})
.
A numerical integrator has
strong order
p
if there exists
q\geqslant 1
such that this integrator has strong order
(p,q)
.

Remark 1
.

Remarks on the definition of the strong order:

1.

In the local error
Z_{1}-Z_{0}(h)=M_{h}+N_{h}
,
M_{h}
represents the mean-zero variable, while
N_{h}
denotes the high-order term. Usually,
M_{h}
constitutes a stochastic integral with respect to the Brownian motion
B_{t}
(see the proof of Lemma
1
in Appendix), consequently manifesting as a normally distributed random variable.

2.

The condition (
8
) is a stronger requirement compared to the conventional definition of strong order, as elucidated in Theorem 1.1 of
[
30
]
and Assumption 22 of
[
36
]
. This heightened rigor arises from our requirement of the fourth moment constraint on the local error, as opposed to the second moment typically required. Indeed, the second moment bound

\mathbb{E}\big[|M_{h}|^{2}\big]\leqslant Ch^{2p+1}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q},~~~~\mathbb{E}\big[|N_{h}|^{2}\big]\leqslant Ch^{2p+2}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q},

as delineated in Equation (22) of
[
36
]
, can be can be readily derived from (
8
).

3.

The conditions (
9
) and (
10
) ensure the consistency and the linear stability of the numerical integrator (see Condition 7.1 of
[
28
]
). Moreover, when
p=q=1
, (
8
) directly implies (
9
).

For the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
), various numerical integrators have been developed both for practical simulation and theoretical analysis. These include the Euler–Maruyama integrator, the left-point integrator
[
21
,
26
,
18
]
, the family of UBU integrators
[
45
,
36
,
7
]
, and the family of BAOAB integrators
[
24
,
46
,
23
]
.
In this paper, we demonstrate the application of the error analysis framework by the Euler–Maruyama integrator (
EM
) and the UBU integrator (
UBU
), which necessitate only a single evaluation of the gradient at each iteration.

Definition 2
.

Consider the following numerical integrators for (
1
).

1.

Euler–Maruyama integrator
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} X_{n+1}&=X_{n}+hV_{n},\\
V_{n+1}&=V_{n}-h\nabla U(X_{n})-\gamma hV_{n}+\sqrt{2\gamma}\xi_{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(EM)

where the random variable
\xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
is sampled independently at each time step.

2.

UBU integrator
. We decompose (
1
) into two dynamics:

\mathcal{U}:\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{t}&=v_{t},\\
\dot{v}_{t}&=-\gamma v_{t}+\sqrt{2\gamma}\dot{B}_{t},\end{aligned}\right.~~~~\mathcal{B}:\dot{v}_{t}=-\nabla U(x_{t}),

where both the
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{B}
dynamics can be explicitly solved (refer to Equations (2.2)-(2.4) of
[
7
]
). The update from
(X_{n},V_{n})
to
(X_{n+1},V_{n+1})
is given by

(X_{n},V_{n})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{U}\mathrm{\,for\,}\frac{h}{2}}(Y_{n},V_{n}^{*})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{B}\mathrm{\,for\,}h}(Y_{n},V_{n}^{**})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{U}\mathrm{\,for\,}\frac{h}{2}}(X_{n+1},V_{n+1}).

(UBU)

If we couple (
EM
) and the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) synchronously, the random variable
\xi_{n}=(B_{(n+1)h}-B_{nh})/\sqrt{h}
, where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is the Brownian motion.

The strong order of the numerical integrators (

) and (

) is stated as follows.

Lemma 1
.

Assume
A1
. In the sense of Definition
1
,

1.

The Euler–Maruyama integrator (

) has strong order
(1,1)
.

2.

The UBU integrator (

) has strong order
(2,2)
.

The proof of Lemma

is provided in Appendix, wherein the demonstration of the strong order of (

[

]
.

In addition to the strong order of the local error, the uniform-in-time moment estimate also plays a crucial role in proving the convergence of the numerical integrators.

Lemma 2
.

Assume
A1
,
A2
. Suppose the numerical integrator has strong order
(p,q)
, and let
r\geqslant 1
be an arbitrary constant. For some constants
\gamma_{0},h_{0}>0
, whenever

(11)

Lemma

guarantees that the numerical solution

\mathcal{H}(x,v)=\begin{pmatrix}x^{\mathrm{T}}&v^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\

(12)

which serves as a Lyapunov function for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (

).
The construction of
\mathcal{H}(x,v)
draws inspiration from Theorem 3.5 of
[

]
and Theorem 1 of
[

]

is provided in Appendix.

Remark 2

necessitates the damping rate

as proposed in

A corollary of Lemma

Lemma 3
.

Assume

,

4
Estimate of the Kolmogorov equation

In this section, we delve into the pointwise estimate of the solution
u(x,v,t)
to the Kolmogorov equation, which holds paramount importance in constructing the discrete Poisson solution outlined in Section
5
. Leveraging the Wasserstein-1 contractivity of (
1
), we bound
|u(x,v,t)|
and
|\nabla u(x,v,t)|
when
U(x)
is strongly convex outside a ball. Furthermore, we utilize synchronous coupling techniques to estimate
|\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)|
when
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

4.1
Estimate under strong convexity outside a ball

We introduce the Wasserstein-1 contractivity property of the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
), which holds pivotal significance in estimating the Kolmogorov equation. We denote the Markov semigroup of (
1
) by
(p_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, wherein for any initial distribution
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the resultant distribution
\nu p_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
signifies the distribution law of
(x_{t},v_{t})
provided
(x_{0},v_{0})\sim\nu
. Moreover, the Wasserstein-1 distance in
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
is

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|\big)\gamma(\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathrm{d}v^{\prime}),

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is set of the transport plans between the distributions
\mu
and
\nu
.

Theorem 1
(contractivity).

Assume
A1
,
A2
. For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever the damping rate
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
, there exist constants
C,\lambda>0
depending on
\gamma
such that

\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu p_{t},\nu p_{t})\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda t}\mathcal{W}_{1}(\mu,\nu),~~~~t\geqslant 0,

(14)

for any probability distributions
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
.

The proof of Theorem
1
using the reflection coupling can be found in Theorem 5 of
[
37
]
.

Remark 3
.

The first reflection coupling result regarding the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) is provided in Theorem 2.3 of
[
15
]
, albeit solely in the context of a semimetric. In comparison to the Wasserstein-1 distance, contractivity in a semimetric poses challenges in establishing estimates for the Kolmogorov equation.

Next, we present the estimation of the Kolmogorov equation when the potential function
U(x)
is strongly convex outside a ball. The generator of the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) is expressed as

\mathcal{L}=v\cdot\nabla_{x}-(\nabla U(x)+\gamma v)\cdot\nabla_{v}+\gamma\Delta_{v}.

We define
u(x,v,t)
to be the solution of the following Kolmogorov equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}(x,v,t)&=(\mathcal{L}u)(x,v,t),&&t\geqslant 0,\\
u(x,v,0)&=f(x,v)-\pi(f),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\pi(f)
represents the average value of
f(x,v)
. Then,
u(x,v,t)
can be expressed as

u(x,v,t)=(e^{t\mathcal{L}}f)(x,v)-\pi(f)=\mathbb{E}^{x,v}[f(x_{t},v_{t})]-\pi(f),

(15)

where the superscript
x,v
denotes that the initial state of
(x_{t},v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

The Wasserstein-1 contractivity outlined in Theorem
1
offers a straightforward estimation of
u(x,v,t)
and its derivatives. This approach, known as Stein’s method, provides a means of estimating
u(x,v,t)
utilizing contractivity properties. For further insights, refer to Theorem 5 of
[
20
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Assume
A1
,
A2
,
A3
. Let
u(x,v,t)
be the Kolmogorov solution defined in (
15
). For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
, there exist constants
C,\lambda>0
depending on
\gamma
such that for any
x,v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|u(x,v,t)|\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda t}(|x|+|v|+1),~~~~|\nabla u(x,v,t)|\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda t}.

(16)

The proof of Lemma
4
is provided in Appendix. Although
u(x,v,t)
and
\nabla u(x,v,t)
can be readily bounded using the Wasserstein-1 contractivity, estimating the second derivative
\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)
remains challenging without the global convexity of the potential
U(x)
.

Remark 4
.

The availability of the estimate for the second derivative
\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)
is the distinguishing factor leading to the difference in the order of the time step
h
between Theorems
2
and
3
. It is remarkable that the second derivative estimate in the overdamped Langevin dynamics can be readily accomplished from the
L^{p}
-estimate for elliptic operators, as elaborated in Lemma 17 of
[
5
]
and Theorem 5 of
[
20
]
.

4.2
Estimate under strong convexity in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

We proceed to provide an estimate of
\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)
under the assumption that the potential function
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Our approach relies on the tangent process introduced in Section 3.2 of
[
9
]
to characterize the derivative
\nabla u(x,v,t)
. The tangent process, also referred to as the first variation process
[
38
,
47
,
10
]
, is instrumental in our analysis.

Definition 3
.

Given the initial state
(x,v)
and the Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, let
(x_{t},v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
be the strong solution to (
1
) with initial state
(x,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. The tangent processes

D_{x}x_{t},D_{x}v_{t},D_{v}x_{t},D_{v}v_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

are defined as the partial derivatives of
(x_{t},v_{t})
with respect to
(x,v)
.

By differentiating (
1
) with respect to
(x,v)
, we derive that
(D_{x}x_{t},D_{x}v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
satisfies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{pmatrix}D_{x}x_{t}\\
D_{x}v_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{d}&I_{d}\\
-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&-\gamma I_{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D_{x}x_{t}\\
D_{x}v_{t}\end{pmatrix},~~~~\begin{pmatrix}D_{x}x_{0}\\
D_{x}v_{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{d}\\
O_{d}\end{pmatrix},

(17)

and
(D_{v}x_{t},D_{v}v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
satisfies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\begin{pmatrix}D_{v}x_{t}\\
D_{v}v_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{d}&I_{d}\\
-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&-\gamma I_{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D_{v}x_{t}\\
D_{v}v_{t}\end{pmatrix},~~~~\begin{pmatrix}D_{v}x_{0}\\
D_{v}v_{0}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{d}\\
I_{d}\end{pmatrix}.

(18)

In a unified framework, the matrix-valued process
(Q_{t},P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
defined by

\begin{pmatrix}\dot{Q}_{t}\\
\dot{P}_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{d}&I_{d}\\
-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&-\gamma I_{d}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Q_{t}\\
P_{t}\end{pmatrix}

(19)

encompasses both tangent processes (
17
) and (
18
). It’s noteworthy that
(Q_{t},P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
solely depends on the initial state
(x,v)
, the Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, and the initial value
(Q_{0},P_{0})
. We can thus represent a tangent process using the following mapping:

(Q_{t},P_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}=\mathrm{TangentProcess}\big[(x,v),(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},(Q_{0},P_{0})\big].

(20)

The tangent process mapping (
20
) exhibits linearity in
(Q_{0},P_{0})
, yet it relies nonlinearly on the initial state
(x,v)
. This nonlinearity arises from the fact that the dynamics (
19
) directly depend on the trajectory
(x_{t},v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
.

The tangent process provides a straightforward representation of
\nabla u(x,v,t)
with the Kolmogorov solution
u(x,v,t)
defined in (
15
). By taking the derivative with respect to the initial state
(x,v)
in (
15
), we obtain the following expressions:

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}u(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{x,v}\Big[\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot D_{x}x_{t}+\nabla_{v}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot D_{x}v_{t}\Big],

(21a)

\displaystyle\nabla_{v}u(x,v,t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}^{x,v}\Big[\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot D_{v}x_{t}+\nabla_{v}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot D_{v}v_{t}\Big].

(21b)

The expressions (
21
) reveal that the exponential decay of
\nabla u(x,v,t)
can be inferred from the exponential decay of the tangent processes
D_{x}x_{t},D_{x}v_{t},D_{v}x_{t},D_{v}v_{t}
.

Lemma 5
.

Assume
A1
,
A2’
,
A3
. For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}

(22)

Inspired from the proof of Lemma

\mathcal{H}(Q,P):=\Tr[\begin{pmatrix}Q^{\mathrm{T}}&P^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma I_{d}&I_{d}\\

The exponential decay of the tangent process

.

A1
,

A2’
,
A3

5
Analysis of statistical error with deterministic gradients

In this section, we analyze the statistical error of the numerical integrators outlined in Section
3
, focusing on scenarios where the drift force is governed by the deterministic gradient
\nabla U(x)
. Our approach begins with leveraging the Kolmogorov solution
u(x,v,t)
discussed in Section
4
to construct the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v,t)
. Subsequently, we derive representations for the time average of the numerical solution
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
.

5.1
Construction of the discrete Poisson solution

The discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
is defined by the expression:

\phi_{h}(x,v)=h\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}u(x,v,nh),

(25)

where
u(x,v,t)
represents the Kolmogorov solution in (
15
). Given that
u(x,v,t)=(e^{t\mathcal{L}}f)(x,v)-\pi(f)
, the function
\phi_{h}(x,v)
can be formally expressed as

\phi_{h}(x,v)=h\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{nh\mathcal{L}}\big(f(x,v)-\pi(f)\big)=h\big(1-e^{h\mathcal{L}}\big)^{-1}f,

leading to the derivation of the discrete Poisson equation,

\frac{1-e^{h\mathcal{L}}}{h}\phi_{h}(x,v)=f(x,v)-\pi(f).

(26)

The term
discrete
is employed here to underscore the distinction from the integral

\phi(x,v)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u(x,v,t),

which represents the solution to the Poisson equation

-\mathcal{L}\phi(x,v)=f(x,v)-\pi(f).

Evidently, as the time step
h\rightarrow 0
,
\phi(x,v)
emerges as the formal limit of
\phi_{h}(x,v)
.

The Poisson solution
\phi(x,v)
has found widespread application in scrutinizing the statistical error across various explicit numerical integrators
[
29
,
5

5.2
Statistical error of numerical integrators

We examine the numerical solution
(X_{n},V_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}
initialized with

(p,q)
. Under

as delineated in (

Theorem 2
.

Assume
A1
,
A2
,
A3
. Suppose the numerical integrator for (
1
) has strong order
(p,q)
. For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
, there exist constants
C,h_{0}>0
depending on
\gamma
such that for any
0<h\leqslant h_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]\leqslant C\bigg(h^{2p-1}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{0}|+|V_{0}|+1\big)^{2q}.

(29)

Proof of Theorem
2
. Statistical error of the numerical integrator.

Define the quantities
S_{n}
and
T_{n}
as in (
4
), namely,

S_{n}=\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(Z_{n}(h))}{h},~~~~T_{n}=\frac{\phi_{h}(Z_{n}(h))-\phi_{h}(Z_{n})}{h}+f(Z_{n})-\pi(f).

Next we estimate the error terms
\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{2}]
and
\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]
respectively.

Using the boundedness of
|\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)|
in Lemma
4
,
S_{n}
is bounded by

|S_{n}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|.

Since the numerical integrator has strong order
(p,q)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{h^{2}}\mathbb{E}|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|^{2}\leqslant Ch^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{2q}

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

(30)

In the last inequality, we have used the uniform-in-time
2q
-th moment estimate in Lemma
2
.

Again using the boundedness of
|\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)|
,
T_{n}
is bounded by

|T_{n}|\leqslant\frac{C|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|}{h}+|f(Z_{n})|+C.

When the time step
h\leqslant h_{0}
, from Itô’s calculus we have the inequality

\mathbb{E}|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|^{2}\leqslant Ch\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{2}\leqslant Ch\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2},

hence we obtain

\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{C}{h}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2}.

(31)

From Lemma
3
, there holds diffusion estimate of the numerical solution

\mathbb{E}|Z_{N}-Z_{0}|^{2}\leqslant CNh\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

(32)

In the representation of the statistical error (
6
), combining (
30
), (
31
) and (
32
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{3C}{N^{2}h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{0}-Z_{N}|^{2}\big]+\frac{3}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{n}^{2}]+\frac{3}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{Nh}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}+Ch^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}+\frac{C}{Nh}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(h^{2p-1}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q},

thereby concluding the proof of Theorem
2
.
\blacksquare

In the proof of Theorem
2
, the estimate of
S_{n}
is suboptimal due to the availability of only the first derivative
\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)
, yielding an error order of
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p-1})
. To achieve a discretization error of
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p})
, the global convexity of

is required to estimate the second derivative
\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(x,v)

Theorem 3
.

Assume
A1
,
A2’
,
A3
. Suppose the numerical integrator for (
1
) has strong order
(p,q)
. For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
, there exist constants
C,h_{0}>0
depending on
\gamma
such that for any
0<h\leqslant h_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]\leqslant C\bigg(h^{2p}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\big(|X_{0}|+|V_{0}|+1\big)^{4q}.

(33)

Proof of Theorem
3
. Statistical error of the numerical integrator under global convexity.

The proof closely resembles that of Theorem
2
; however, a more accurate estimation of the summation
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}
is required. We express
S_{n}
for each
n\geqslant 0
as follows,

S_{n}=\frac{1}{h}\big(Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)\big)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\phi_{h}\big(\theta Z_{n+1}+(1-\theta)Z_{n}(h)\big)\mathrm{d}\theta.

(34)

According to Definition
1
, the local error
Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)=M_{n,h}+N_{n,h}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}\big[M_{n,h}\big|Z_{n}\big]=0,~~\mathbb{E}\big[|M_{n,h}|^{4}\big|Z_{n}\big]\leqslant Ch^{4p+2}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{4q},~~\mathbb{E}\big[|N_{n,h}|^{4}\big|Z_{n}\big]\leqslant Ch^{4p+4}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{4q}.

Here,
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|Z_{n}]
means the conditional expectation for given
Z_{n}=(X_{n},V_{n})
.
As a consequence,
S_{n}
in (
34
) can be decomposed into
S_{n,1}+S_{n,2}+S_{n,3}
, where

\displaystyle S_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}M_{n,h}\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}(Z_{n}),

\displaystyle S_{n,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}N_{n,h}\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}(Z_{n}),

\displaystyle S_{n,3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h))\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\nabla\phi_{h}\big(\theta Z_{n+1}+(1-\theta)Z_{n}(h)\big)-\nabla\phi_{h}(Z_{n})\Big)\mathrm{d}\theta.

Next we estimate the mean square error of
\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n,i}
for
i=1,2,3
, respectively.

1.

Estiamte for
S_{n,1}
.

Since
\nabla\phi_{h}(Z_{n})
is globally bounded by Lemma
4
, we obtain
|S_{n,1}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|M_{n,h}|
.
Then uniform-in-time moment estimate from Lemma
2
yields

\mathbb{E}[S_{n,1}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{C}{h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|M_{n,h}|^{2}\big]\leqslant Ch^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{2q}\leqslant Ch^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

Given that
\{S_{n,1}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
are unbiased and mutually independent, we arrive at

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n,1}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{n,1}^{2}]\leqslant CNh^{2p-1}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

(35)

2.

Estimate for
S_{n,2}
.

Again, exploiting the boundedness of
\nabla\phi_{h}(Z_{n})
, we have
|S_{n,2}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|N_{n,h}|
and

\mathbb{E}[S_{n,2}^{2}]\leqslant Ch^{2p}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{2q}\leqslant Ch^{2p}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

Utilizing Cauchy’s inequality, we deduce

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n,2}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\leqslant N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{n,2}^{2}]\leqslant CN^{2}h^{2p}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}.

(36)

3.

Estimate for
S_{n,3}
.

Utilizing the boundedness of
\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(z)
from Lemma
7
, we have

|S_{n,3}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|\big(|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}|+|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|\big)

Applying Cauchy’s inequality, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[S_{n,3}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{C}{h^{2}}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|^{4}\big]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}|^{4}+|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|^{4}\big]}

(37)

Utilizing the linear stability condition (
9
) in Definition
1
, we deduce

\displaystyle~~~~\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}|^{4}+|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|^{4}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|^{4}\big]+C\,\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n}(h)-Z_{n}|^{4}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant Ch^{6}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{4}+Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{4}\leqslant Ch^{2}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4}.

Moreover, the local error bound (
8
) implies

\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{n+1}-Z_{n}(h)|^{4}\big]\leqslant Ch^{4p+2}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{n}|+1)^{4q}\leqslant Ch^{4p+2}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q}.

Therefore, from (
37
) and Cauchy’s inequality, we conclude

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n,3}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\leqslant N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[S_{n,3}^{2}]\leqslant CN^{2}h^{2p}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q}.

(38)

From the estimates (
35
), (
36
) and (
38
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\leqslant 3\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n,i}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\leqslant C(N^{2}h^{2p}+Nh^{2p-1})\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q}.

Recalling the expression of the time average error
e(N,h)
in (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{(Nh)^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|Z_{N}-Z_{0}|^{2}\big]+\frac{C}{N^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]+\frac{C}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{Nh}\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{2q}+C\bigg(h^{2p}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(h^{2p}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|Z_{0}|+1)^{4q},

(39)

which completes the proof of Theorem
3
.
\blacksquare

6
Analysis of statistical error with stochastic gradients

In this section, we investigate the statistical error of numerical integrators with stochastic gradients, where the potential function
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The stochastic gradient
b(x,\omega)
involves a random variable
\omega\in\Omega
, which is independently sampled at each time step.
For example, when
U(x)
is the average of a set of potential functions
\{U_{j}(x)\}_{j=1}^{M}
, namely
U(x)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}U_{j}(x)
,
a typical choice of the stochastic gradient
b(x,\omega)
is the mini-batch approximation

b(x,\omega)=\frac{1}{\mathcal{B}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}}\nabla U_{j}(x),

(40)

where
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{N}
is the batch size, and
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}(\omega)
is a random subset of
\{1,\cdots,M\}
of size
\mathcal{B}
. In this paper we adopt the original notation
b(x,\omega)
rather than the mini-batch approximation (
40
) to represent the stochastic gradient.

6.1
Stochastic gradient numerical integrators

For any numerical integrator for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
), its stochastic version can be constructed by replacing the exact gradient
\nabla U(x)
by
b(x,\omega_{n})
, where the random variable
\omega_{n}
is chosen independently at each iteration.
In particular, the stochastic gradient versions of (
EM
) and (
UBU
) are constructed as follows.

Definition 4
.

Consider the following stochastic gradient numerical integrators.

1.

Euler–Maruyama integrator.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \tilde{X}_{n+1}&=\tilde{X}_{n}+h\tilde{V}_{n},\\
\tilde{V}_{n+1}&=\tilde{V}_{n}-hb(\tilde{X}_{n},\omega_{n})-\gamma h\tilde{V}_{n}+\sqrt{2\gamma h}\xi_{n},\end{aligned}\right.

(SG-EM)

where
\xi_{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
and
\omega_{n}\in\Omega
are independently sampled at each time step.

2.

UBU integrator
. Consider two separate dynamics

\mathcal{U}:\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{x}_{t}&=v_{t},\\
\dot{v}_{t}&=-\gamma v_{t}+\sqrt{2\gamma}\dot{B}_{t},\end{aligned}\right.~~~~\tilde{\mathcal{B}}:\dot{v}_{t}=-b(x_{t},\omega_{n}),

where both the
\mathcal{U}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{B}}
dynamics can be solved explicitly.
The update from
(\tilde{X}_{n},\tilde{V}_{n})
to
(\tilde{X}_{n+1},\tilde{V}_{n+1})
is given by

(\tilde{X}_{n},\tilde{V}_{n})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{U}\mathrm{\,for\,}\frac{h}{2}}(\tilde{Y}_{n},\tilde{V}_{n}^{*})\xrightarrow{\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\mathrm{\,for\,}h}(\tilde{Y}_{n},\tilde{V}_{n}^{**})\xrightarrow{\mathcal{U}\mathrm{\,for\,}\frac{h}{2}}(\tilde{X}_{n+1},\tilde{V}_{n+1}),

(SG-UBU)

and
\omega_{n}\in\Omega
is sampled independently at each time step.

The strong order of a stochastic gradient numerical integrator can be defined as follows.

Definition 5
.

Suppose
p>0
and
q\geqslant 1
. The stochastic gradient numerical integrator

\tilde{Z}_{0}=(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{V}_{0})\mapsto\tilde{Z}_{1}=(\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{V}_{1})

has strong order
(p,q)
if its deterministic gradient version

\tilde{Z}_{0}=(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{V}_{0})\mapsto\tilde{Z}_{0,1}=(\tilde{X}_{0,1},\tilde{V}_{0,1})

has strong order
(p,q)
in the sense of Definition
1
, and

•

The one-step update
\tilde{Z}_{1}
is an unbiased approximation to
\tilde{Z}_{0,1}
with respect to the random variable
\omega
, namely

\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{Z}_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{0,1}\big|\tilde{Z}_{0,1}\big]=0.

(41)

•

For some constant
C>0
, the difference
\tilde{Z}_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{0,1}
is bounded by

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{Z}_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{0,1}|^{4}\leqslant Ch^{4}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4}.

(42)

•

For some constant
C>0
, the local error is bounded by

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{Z}_{1}-\tilde{Z}_{0}\{h\}|^{4}\leqslant Ch^{6}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4}.

(43)

•

For any
r\geqslant 1
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
r
such that

\mathbb{E}|\tilde{Z}_{1}|^{r}\leqslant C(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{r}.

(44)

A stochastic gradient numerical integrator has
strong order
p
if there exists
q\geqslant 1
such that this integrator has
strong order
(p,q)
.

Remark 5
.

Remarks on the definition of the strong order:

1.

The unbiased condition (

) characterizes the unbiased property of the numerical solution
\tilde{Z}_{1}

6.2
Analysis of the statistical error

To obtain the statistical error of the stochastic gradient numerical integrator, we need to evaluate the time average error
e(N,h)
, which is given by

e(N,h)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(\tilde{X}_{n},\tilde{V}_{n})-\pi(f).

The randomness of
e(N,h)
comes from the initial state
(\tilde{X}_{0},\tilde{V}_{0})
, the Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
, and the random variables
(\omega_{n})_{n=0}^{N-1}
.
The statistical error is given by
\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
.

Theorem 4
.

Assume
A1
,
A2’
,
A3
,
A4
.
Suppose the stochastic gradient numerical integrator has strong order
(p,q)
. For some constant
\gamma_{0}>0
, whenever
\gamma\geqslant\gamma_{0}
, there exist constants
C,h_{0}>0
depending on
\gamma
such that for any
0<h\leqslant h_{0}
,

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]\leqslant C\bigg(h^{\min\{2p,2\}}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}\big(|\tilde{X}_{0}|+|\tilde{V}_{0}|+1\big)^{4q}.

In particular, the statistical error of (
SG-EM
) and (
SG-UBU
) is both
\mathcal{O}\big(h^{2}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh}\big)
.

Proof of Theorem
4
. Statistical error of the stochastic gradient numerical integrators.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2
, for integer each
n\geqslant 0
we define the random variables

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n,1})}{h},~~~~S_{n}=\frac{\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n,1})-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n}(h))}{h},

\displaystyle T_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n}(h))-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n})}{h}+f(\tilde{Z}_{n})-\pi(f),

where the definitions of
\tilde{Z}_{n},\tilde{Z}_{n,1},\tilde{Z}_{n}(h)
can be found in Section
2
. Then, we have

R_{n}+S_{n}+T_{n}=\frac{\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n+1})-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n})}{h}+f(\tilde{Z}_{n})-\pi(f).

(47)

Summation of
R_{n}+S_{n}+T_{n}
over
n
in (
47
) yields a similar expression with (
5
):

e(N,T)=\frac{\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{0})-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{N})}{Nh}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(R_{n}+S_{n}+T_{n}),

(48)

and the only difference is the additional term
R_{n}
due to stochastic gradients.
Similar to the proof of Theorem
3
, the quantities
S_{n}
and
T_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant CN^{2}\bigg(h^{2p}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4q},

(49)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[T_{n}^{2}]\leqslant\frac{CN}{h}\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{2}.

(50)

Here we recall that the numerical integrator has strong order
(p,q)
, and the random variables
\{T_{n}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
are unbiased and mutually independent.

Now, we focus on estimating
R_{n}
. Notably,
R_{n}
can be expressed as

R_{n}=\frac{1}{h}\big(\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1}\big)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\phi_{h}\big(\theta\tilde{Z}_{n+1}+(1-\theta)\tilde{Z}_{n,1}\big)\mathrm{d}\theta,

which can be decomposed into
R_{n,1}+R_{n,2}
, where

\displaystyle R_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1})\cdot\nabla\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n,1}),

\displaystyle R_{n,2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1})\cdot\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\nabla\phi_{h}\big(\theta\tilde{Z}_{n+1}+(1-\theta)\tilde{Z}_{n,1}\big)-\nabla\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{n,1})\Big)\mathrm{d}\theta.

From the condition (
8
), the random variables
\{R_{n,1}\}_{n=0}^{N-1}
are unbiased and thus mutually independent. Utilizing the boundedness of
\nabla\phi_{h}(x,v)
and
\nabla^{2}\phi_{h}(x,v)
as stated in Lemmas
8
and
9
, we derive

|R_{n,1}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1}|,~~~~|R_{n,2}|\leqslant\frac{C}{h}|\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1}|^{2}.

Thus, by the condition (
42
) and Lemma
11
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{n,1}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[R_{n,1}^{2}\big]\leqslant\frac{C}{h^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[|\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1}|^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{n}|+1)^{2}\leqslant CN\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{2}.

(51)

By applying Cauchy’s inequality and the condition (
42
), we further derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{n,2}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[R_{n,2}^{2}\big]\leqslant\frac{CN}{h^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\big[|\tilde{Z}_{n+1}-\tilde{Z}_{n,1}|^{4}\big]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{CN}{h^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}h^{4}\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{n}|+1)^{4}\leqslant CN^{2}h^{2}\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4}.

(52)

Combining (
51
) and (
52
), we conclude

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\leqslant CN^{2}\bigg(h^{2}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4}.

(53)

Finally, from the expression of
e(N,h)
in (
48
), the diffusion estimate (
46
) in Lemma
12
, and the quantitative estimates of
R_{n},S_{n},T_{n}
in (
49
)(
50
)(
53
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{2}{N^{2}h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{0})-\phi_{h}(\tilde{Z}_{N})|^{2}\big]+\frac{2}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}(R_{n}+S_{n}+T_{n})\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{C}{N^{2}h^{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[|\tilde{Z}_{0}-\tilde{Z}_{N}|^{2}\big]+\frac{6}{N^{2}}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}R_{n}\bigg|^{2}+\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}S_{n}\bigg|^{2}+\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}T_{n}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(h^{2p}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4q}+C\bigg(h^{2}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4q}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\bigg(h^{\min\{2p,2\}}+\frac{1}{Nh}\bigg)\mathbb{E}(|\tilde{Z}_{0}|+1)^{4q},

which completes the proof of Theorem
4
.
\blacksquare

to

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]

\mathbb{E}[e^{2}(N,h)]
for the numerical integrators (

), (

) and their stochastic gradient versions (

8
Conclusion

In this paper we employ the discrete Poisson solution
\phi_{h}(x,v)
to study the statistical error of the numerical integrators for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
). When the potential function
U(x)
is strongly convex outside a ball, we prove that the statistical error is
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p-1}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
, where
p>0
is the strong order of the numerical integrator. When
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the estimate is enhanced to be
\mathcal{O}(h^{2p}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
. For the stochastic gradient numerical integrator, when
U(x)
is strongly convex in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we show that the statistical error is
\mathcal{O}(h^{2}\hskip-1.0pt+\hskip-1.0pt\frac{1}{Nh})
. Our results provide the estimates of the statistical error for a broad class of numerical integrators for the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
), and relaxes the constraint on the time step
h
.

There are several potential avenues for future research.
Firstly, for stochastic gradient numerical integrators, it would be intriguing to derive an explicit bound of the statistical error with clear dependence on the batch size
\mathcal{B}
and the spatial dimension
d
.
Secondly, investigating the stochastic variance reduced gradient (SVRG) dynamics
[
43
,
7
]
could be worthwhile.

Proof of Lemma
2
. Uniform-in-time moment estimates of the numerical solution.

We employ the same procedure with Theorem 7.2 of

to prove this result. The generator of the underdamped Langevin dynamics (

To proceed, we prove that
\mathcal{H}(x,v)

\displaystyle\leqslant-2\Big((\gamma-1)|v|^{2}+m|x|^{2}-C_{1}|v|(|x|+1)-C_{0}\Big)+2\gamma

z=(x,v),~~~~Z_{0}=(X_{0},V_{0}),~~~~Z_{0}(t)=(X_{0}(t),V_{0}(t)),~~~~Z_{1}=(X_{1},V_{1}).

. Estimate

t\geqslant 0
, there holds the inequality

\displaystyle SA_{t}+A_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}S

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}2\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})\\
\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&2(\gamma-1)I_{d}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\succcurlyeq\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})&O_{d}\\
O_{d}&2(\gamma-1)I_{d}-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})\end{pmatrix}\succcurlyeq\begin{pmatrix}mI_{d}&O_{d}\\
O_{d}&\big(2(\gamma-1)-C_{1}\big)I_{d}\end{pmatrix}.

When the damping rate
\gamma\geqslant C_{1}+1
, there exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that

SA_{t}+A_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}S\succcurlyeq\begin{pmatrix}mI_{d}&O_{d}\\
O_{d}&C_{1}I_{d}\end{pmatrix}\succcurlyeq\lambda S,

(66)

and thus the Lyapunov function
\mathcal{H}(W_{t})
satisfies

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}(W_{t})=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Tr[W_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}SW_{t}]=\Tr[W_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}(SA_{t}+A_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}S)W_{t}]\leqslant-\lambda\Tr[W_{t}^{\mathrm{T}}SW_{t}]=-\lambda\mathcal{H}(W_{t}).

As a consequence,
\mathcal{H}(W_{t})
has exponential decay, and

\mathcal{H}(W_{t})\leqslant e^{-\lambda t}\mathcal{H}(W_{0})\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda t}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)^{2},

which completes the proof.

\blacksquare

Proof of Lemma
6
. Coupling error the tangent processes
(Q_{t},P_{t})
and
(Q_{t}^{\prime},P_{t}^{\prime})
.

Similar to the proof of Lemma

, denote

W_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{t}\\

P_{t}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},~~~~A_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}O_{d}&-I_{d}\\

where the matrices

Let

be the deviation in the initial states.

The strong solutions
(x_{t},v_{t})
and

[
37
]
, there exists a constant

(x_{t},v_{t})
and

Write
B_{t}=\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Since
\nabla^{3}U(x)
is bounded by
A1
, we have

|B_{t}|\leqslant|\nabla^{2}U(x_{t})-\nabla^{2}U(x_{t}^{\prime})|\leqslant C|x_{t}-x_{t}^{\prime}|\leqslant C\delta,~~~~\forall t\geqslant 0.

The time derivative of the Lyapunov function
\mathcal{H}_{t}:=\mathcal{H}(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Tr[(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}S(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=-2\Tr[(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})S(A_{t}W_{t}-A_{t}^{\prime}W_{t}^{\prime})]

\displaystyle=-2\Tr[(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}SA_{t}^{\prime}(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})]-2\Tr[(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}S(A_{t}-A_{t}^{\prime})W_{t}].

According to (
66
), for some constant
\lambda_{1}>0
there is
SA_{t}^{\prime}+(A_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}S\succcurlyeq\lambda_{1}S
,
and thus

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{t}\leqslant-\lambda_{1}\mathcal{H}_{t}-2\Tr[(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}SB_{t}W_{t}].

(67)

From Lemma
5
, there exists a constant
\lambda_{2}>0
such that
|W_{t}|\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda_{2}t}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)
, then

\big|(W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}SB_{t}W_{t}\big|\leqslant C|B_{t}||W_{t}-W_{t}^{\prime}||W_{t}|\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda_{2}t}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}_{t}}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)\delta.

(68)

We derive from (
67
) and (
68
) that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{t}\leqslant-\lambda_{1}\mathcal{H}_{t}+Ce^{-\lambda_{2}t}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}_{t}}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)\delta.

(69)

Let
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\}>0
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}=e^{\lambda t}\mathcal{H}_{t}
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{0}=0
,
\mathcal{G}_{t}\geqslant 0
and
\mathcal{G}_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{G}_{t}

\displaystyle=\lambda e^{\lambda t}\mathcal{F}_{t}+e^{\lambda t}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{H}_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lambda e^{\lambda t}\mathcal{H}_{t}+e^{\lambda t}\Big(-\lambda_{2}\mathcal{H}_{t}+Ce^{-\lambda_{3}t}\sqrt{\mathcal{H}_{t}}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)\delta\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\lambda e^{\lambda t}\mathcal{H}_{t}+C\sqrt{\mathcal{H}_{t}}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)\delta

\displaystyle\leqslant-\lambda\mathcal{G}_{t}+C\sqrt{\mathcal{G}_{t}}(|Q_{0}|+|P_{0}|)\delta.

(70)

, the inequality (

As a consequence,

\blacksquare

Proof of Lemma
7
. Estimate of the second derivative
\nabla^{2}u(x,v,t)
.

According to (
21
),
\nabla_{x}u(x,v,t)
and
\nabla_{v}u(x,v,t)
can be unified in the form

(71)

where the initial value
(Q_{0},P_{0})=(I_{d},O_{d})
as in (
17
) or
(Q_{0},P_{0})=(O_{d},I_{d})
as in (
18
).

For another initial state
(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})
, let

be the strong solution to the underdamped Langevin dynamics (
1
) driven by the same Brownian motion
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
. In other words, the stochastic processes
(x_{t},v_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
and
(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})_{t\geqslant 0}
are in the synchronous coupling. Also, the tangent process
(Q_{t}^{\prime},P_{t}^{\prime})_{t\geqslant 0}
is defined by

(Q_{t}^{\prime},P_{t}^{\prime})_{t\geqslant 0}=\mathrm{TangentProcess}\big[(x^{\prime},v^{\prime}),(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0},(Q_{0},P_{0})\big],

then the function

can be represented as

g(x^{\prime},v^{\prime},t)=\mathbb{E}^{x^{\prime},v^{\prime}}\Big[\nabla_{x}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})\cdot Q_{t}^{\prime}+\nabla_{v}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})\cdot P_{t}^{\prime}\Big].

(72)

Using (
71
) and (
72
), the difference
g(x^{\prime},v^{\prime},t)-g(x,v,t)
can be bounded by

\displaystyle|g(x^{\prime},v^{\prime},t)-g(x,v,t)|\leqslant\,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|\nabla_{x}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})\cdot Q_{t}^{\prime}-\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot Q_{t}\big|

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big|\nabla_{v}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})\cdot P_{t}^{\prime}-\nabla_{v}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot P_{t}\big|.

(73)

Using the boundedness of
\nabla f(x,v)
and
\nabla^{2}f(x_{t},v_{t})
in

, we have

\displaystyle~~~~\mathbb{E}\big|\nabla_{x}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})\cdot Q_{t}^{\prime}-\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\cdot Q_{t}\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\big|\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\big||Q_{t}^{\prime}-Q_{t}|+\mathbb{E}\big|\nabla_{x}f(x_{t}^{\prime},v_{t}^{\prime})-\nabla_{x}f(x_{t},v_{t})\big||Q_{t}^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\,\mathbb{E}\Big(|Q_{t}^{\prime}-Q_{t}|+|x_{t}^{\prime}-x_{t}|+|v_{t}^{\prime}-v_{t}|\Big).

(74)

By Lemma
6
,
|Q_{t}^{\prime}-Q_{t}|

|Q_{t}^{\prime}-Q_{t}|\leqslant Ce^{-\lambda_{1}t}(|x^{\prime}-x|+|v^{\prime}-v|).

(75)

From the proof of Theorem 1 of
[
37
]
,

THE SIXTH MOMENT OF AUTOMORPHIC L L -FUNCTIONS Joshua Stucky


Abstract
We investigate the sixth moment of the family of L L -functions associated to holomorphic modular forms on G ​ L 2 GL_{2} with respect to a congruence subgroup Γ 1 ​ ( q ) \Gamma_{1}(q) . We improve on previous work and obtain an unconditional upper bound of the correct order of magnitude.



1. Introduction

Moments of
L
-functions are among the central objects of study in modern analytic number theory, and there is a vast literature on the subject. In this paper, we shall be concerned with a family of
L
-functions attached to automorphic forms on
GL_{2}
. Specifically, we consider the sixth moment of
L
-functions associated to the family of holomorphic modular forms with respect to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(q)
(see
[
6
]
for definitions). Our work is motivated by the work of Djanković
[
3
]
and Chandee and Li
[
2
]
on this family. For a detailed introduction to this family of
L
-functions, see the introductions of the above two papers.

Let
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(q))
denote the space of holomorphic cusp forms on
\Gamma_{1}(q)
. We assume
k\geq 3
is an odd integer and
q
is prime (these assumptions are made mostly to eliminate oldforms). Then
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(q))
is a Hilbert space with the Petersson’s inner product

\langle f,g\rangle=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Gamma_{1}(q)\backslash\mathbb{H}\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}f(z)\overline{g}(z)y^{k-2}dx\ dy,

and

S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(q))=\bigoplus_{\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)}S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(q),\chi).

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
be an orthogonal basis for
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(q),\chi)
consisting of Hecke cusp forms, normalized so that the first Fourier coefficient is 1. For each
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
, we let
L(f,s)
be the
L
-function associated to
f
, defined for
\mathrm{Re}\phantom{.}s>1
as

L(f,s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{f}(p)}{p^{s}}+\frac{\chi(p)}{p^{2s}}\right)^{-1},

where
\left\{\lambda_{f}(n)\right\}
are the Hecke eigenvalues of
f
. In general, these satisfy the Hecke relation

\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{d\mid(m,n)}\chi(d)\lambda_{f}\left(\frac{mn}{d^{2}}\right).

(1.1)

We define the completed
L
-function as

\Lambda\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+s\right)=\left(\frac{q}{4\pi^{2}}\right)^{\frac{s}{2}}\Gamma\left(s+\frac{k}{2}\right)L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+s\right).

(1.2)

This satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+s\right)=i^{k}\overline{\eta_{f}}\Lambda\left(\overline{f},{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}-s\right)

where
\left|\eta_{f}\right|=1
when
f
is a newform. We define the harmonic average over
\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
by

\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\alpha_{f}=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\frac{\alpha_{f}}{\left|\left|f\right|\right|^{2}},

where
\left|\left|f\right|\right|
represents the norm given by Petersson’s inner product. We are interested in the sixth moment

\mathcal{M}(q)=\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\left|L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{6}.

The conjectured asymptotic (see
[
2
]
) is

\mathcal{M}(q)=\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\left|L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right|^{6}\sim\mathcal{C}_{q}(\log q)^{9}

where
\mathcal{C}_{q}\asymp 1
is an explicit constant (see (1.4) of
[
2
]
for a precise description). Using the asymptotic large sieve for the Fourier coefficients of cusp forms developed by Iwaniec and Xiaoqing Li
[
7
]
, Djanković
[
3
]
has shown

\mathcal{M}(q)\ll q^{\varepsilon}

for any
\varepsilon>0
, whereas Chandee and Xiannan Li
[
2
]
have obtained the following asymptotic formula for the smoothed sixth moment:

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\Lambda\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}+it\right)\right|^{6}dt\sim\mathcal{C}_{q}(\log q)^{9}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\Gamma({\textstyle\frac{k}{2}}+it)\right|^{6}dt.

Note that the integral in
t
is quite short due to the presence of the gamma function. Building on these results, we prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q
be prime and
k\geq 3
. Then, as
q\to\infty
, we have

\mathcal{M}(q)\ll(\log q)^{9}.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
adheres closely to the work of Chandee and Li
[
2
]
and may be seen as an application of their ideas that avoids many of the technical details of their proof. Although we sacrifice an asymptotic, our result has the benefit of having no integral in
t
and being of the correct order of magnitude.

\boldsymbol{a}

a_{1},a_{2}

f(\boldsymbol{a})

f

\boldsymbol{n}

, respectively. We write
n\asymp N

. The use of the notation

indicates that the is sum over residue classes
x
which are coprime to the modulus of the sum, in this case
c
. In such a sum, we denote by
\overline{x}

x

. All other notation should be clear from context.

2. Outline of the Paper

To help orient the reader, we provide an outline for the proof. First, after applying the approximate functional equation for
L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{3}
, the main object we need to understand is roughly of the form

\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\sum_{m,n\asymp q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{\tau_{3}(m)\tau_{3}(n)\lambda_{f}(m)\lambda_{f}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}.

We apply the functional equation for
L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{3}
rather than for
L\left(f,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{6}
to avoid unbalanced sums in
m
and
n
(i.e.
m,n\asymp q^{3/2}
rather than the weaker condition
mn\leq q^{3}
). We note that the
t
integral used
[
2
]
is included for precisely the same reason. It is also worth noting that the application of Cauchy’s inequality in (
3.2
) immediately precludes any hope of obtaining an asymptotic formula by our method, as we completely ignore the arithmetic of the root numbers
\eta_{f}
. Applying Petersson’s formula to the average over
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}
leads to diagonal terms
m=n
and off-diagonal terms. The diagonal terms are evaluated fairly easily in Section
4
. The off-diagonal terms involve sums of the form

\sum_{m,n\asymp q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{\tau_{3}(m)\tau_{3}(n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\sum_{c}S_{\chi}(m,n;cq)J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{cq}\right),

where
S_{\chi}(m,n;cq)
is the Kloosterman sum defined in (
4.1
) and
J_{k-1}
is the usual Bessel function of order
k-1
.

The most important range for
c
is in the transition region for the Bessel function, i.e.
c\asymp q^{\frac{1}{2}}
. To focus on this region, we truncate the sum in
c
using the Weil estimate for Kloosterman sums. The details of this truncation are given in Section
5
. The conductor of the Kloosterman sum is then essentially of size
cq\asymp q^{\frac{3}{2}}
. To understand the correlations between the Kloosterman sums and the Bessel functions, we apply harmonic analysis in the form of the Voronoi formula of Ivić
[
5
]
. Before doing so, we reduce the conductor in the Kloosterman sums by taking advantage of the average over
\chi
. The conductor lowering trick of
[
2
]
(see Lemma
6.1
) produces new Kloosterman sums of the form

e\left(\frac{m+n}{cq}\right)\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{x(c)}e\left(\frac{\overline{q}(x-1)m+\overline{q}(\overline{x}-1)n}{c}\right),

where the conductor is now reduced to
c\asymp q^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the exponential in front may be treated as a smooth function with small derivatives. Applying the Voronoi formula then produces a single main term and eight error terms. The details of these transformations are given in Section
6
. The main term is estimated in Section
7
, and it is here that we require a more delicate analysis of the Laurent series coefficients
D_{-i}
of the third-order Estermann zeta function
E_{3}\left(s,\frac{\lambda}{\eta}\right)
(see (
6.8
), (
6.9
), and (
6.10
)). This is accomplished via Lemma
7.4
, in which we improve the trivial estimate
D_{-i}(\eta)\ll\eta^{-1+\varepsilon}
to

D_{-i}(\eta)\ll\frac{\tau_{2}(\eta)(\log\eta)^{3-i}}{\eta}.

In order to apply this estimate without losing too much from the triangle inequality, we need to suitably transform the sum. This is accomplished by an application of Poisson summation (in the form of Lemma
7.2
), along with some identities involving integrals of Bessel functions. The details of these transformations are given in Subsection
7.2
.

After the above transformations, we would like to extract the contribution from the residue of
\zeta(s)
at
s=1
. However, it turns out that the Laurent coefficients
D_{-i}
are not multiplicative for
i=1,2
. Consequently, we require a somewhat delicate analysis of certain complex-valued arithmetic functions (see Proposition
7.5
), in contrast to the simple contour shifting argument used in
[
2
]
. We then estimate the remaining sum by elementary means to obtain a final estimate of
(\log q)^{9}
for the main term. These computations are given in Subsection
7.3
.

Finally, the eight error terms (i.e. the dual sums arising from Voronoi summation) are estimated in Section
8
. The main aspect of the calculations in this section is that the dual sums are short. This is precisely the reason for reducing the conductor in the Kloosterman sums using Lemma
6.1
. The details of these calculations are standard but technical and follow closely the arguments of
[
2
]
.

3. Approximate Functional Equation

As is standard in such problems, we begin with an approximation functional equation for
L(f,1/2)^{3}
. The derivation of this is standard. For our purposes, it suffices to cite equation (2.5) of
[
3
]
, which is

\displaystyle L(f,1/2)^{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\geq 1}\sum_{b\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mu(a)\chi(b)\tau_{3}(b)\lambda_{f}(an)\tau_{3}(n)}{(a^{3}b^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a^{3}b^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+(i^{k}\overline{\eta_{f}})^{3}\sum_{a\geq 1}\sum_{b\geq 1}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mu(a)\overline{\chi}(b)\tau_{3}(b)\overline{\lambda_{f}}(an)\tau_{3}(n)}{(a^{3}b^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a^{3}b^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right),

where

U(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(2)}y^{-s}\gamma^{3}(s)\left(e^{s^{2}}\right)^{3}\frac{ds}{s},\qquad\gamma(s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\frac{\Gamma({\textstyle\frac{k}{2}}+s)}{\Gamma({\textstyle\frac{k}{2}})}.

Here we have chosen the specific function
e^{s^{2}}
to ensure that
U(y)
is real when
y
is real. This is mainly for notational simplicity. The function
U
satisfies

\displaystyle U(y)

\displaystyle\ll(1+y)^{-A},

(3.1)

\displaystyle U(y)

\displaystyle=1+O(y^{A})\quad\text{as}\ y\to 0

for any
A>1
. Applying Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\mathcal{M}(q)\ll\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\bigg|\sum_{a,b,n\geq 1}\frac{\mu(a)\tau_{3}(b)\tau_{3}(n)\chi(ab)\lambda_{f}(an)}{(a^{3}b^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a^{3}b^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)\bigg|^{2}.

(3.2)

Expanding the square and rearranging, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(q)\ll

\displaystyle\operatorname*{\sum...\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},b_{1},n\geq 1\\
a_{2},b_{2},m\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a_{1})\mu(a_{2})\tau_{3}(b_{1})\tau_{3}(n)\tau_{3}(b_{2})\tau_{3}(m)}{(a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)U\left(\frac{a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\chi(a_{1}b_{1})\overline{\chi}(a_{2}b_{2})\bigg)\bigg(\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\lambda_{f}(a_{1}n)\overline{\lambda_{f}}(a_{2}m)\bigg).

Note that by (
3.1
), the terms with
a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n,a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m\gg q^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}
give a contribution of
q^{-2022}
.

4. Orthogonality and the Diagonal Contribution

We now apply the orthogonality relations for
\chi
and
\lambda_{f}
given by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

The orthogonality relation for Dirichlet characters is

\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(q)\\
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{k}\end{subarray}}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $m\equiv n(q)$, $(mn,q)=1$},\\
(-1)^{k}&\text{if $m\equiv-n(q)$, $(mn,q)=1$},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Petersson’s formula gives

\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\textit{h}}}_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{\chi}}\lambda_{f}(n)\overline{\lambda}_{f}(m)=\delta_{m=n}+\sigma_{\chi}(m,n),

where

\sigma_{\chi}(m,n)=2\pi i^{-k}\sum_{c=1}^{\infty}(cq)^{-1}S_{\chi}(m,n;cq)J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi}{cq}\sqrt{mn}\right)

and
S_{\chi}
is the Kloosterman sum defined by

S_{\chi}(m,n,cq)=\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{a(cq)}\chi(a)e\left(\frac{am+\overline{a}n}{cq}\right).

(4.1)

Here
\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}
denotes a sum over residues
a
with
(a,cq)=1
and
\overline{a}
satisfies
a\overline{a}\equiv 1(cq)
.

Lemma
4.1
gives

\mathcal{M}(q)\ll\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{O}\mathcal{D},

where
\mathcal{O}\mathcal{D}
is given by (
5.1
) and
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{+}+\mathcal{D}_{-}
with

\mathcal{D}_{+}=\operatorname*{\sum...\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},b_{1},n,a_{2},b_{2},m\geq 1\\
a_{1}b_{1}\equiv a_{2}b_{2}(q)\\
a_{1}n=a_{2}m\\
(a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a_{1})\mu(a_{2})\tau_{3}(b_{1})\tau_{3}(n)\tau_{3}(b_{2})\tau_{3}(m)}{(a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)U\left(\frac{a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

and
\mathcal{D}_{-}
is the same sum but multiplied by
(-1)^{k}
with the condition
a_{1}b_{1}\equiv a_{2}b_{2}\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)
replaced by
a_{1}b_{1}\equiv-a_{2}b_{2}\ (\textnormal{mod}\ q)
. The only relevant case is when
a_{1}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{2}
in

. Thus

\mathcal{D}=\operatorname*{\sum...\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},b_{1},n,a_{2},b_{2},m\geq 1\\
a_{1}b_{1}=a_{2}b_{2}\\
a_{1}n=a_{2}m\\
(a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2},q)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a_{1})\mu(a_{2})\tau_{3}(b_{1})\tau_{3}(n)\tau_{3}(b_{2})\tau_{3}(m)}{(a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)U\left(\frac{a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)+O(q^{-2022}).

(4.2)

Neglecting the error term, we open the factors of
U
and write

\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{2}}\int\limits_{(2)}\int\limits_{(2)}q^{\frac{3}{2}(s_{1}+s_{2})}\mathscr{D}(1+s_{1}+s_{2})\gamma^{3}(s_{1})\gamma^{3}(s_{2})e^{3(s_{1}^{2}+s_{2}^{2})}\frac{ds_{1}}{s_{1}}\ \frac{ds_{2}}{s_{2}}.

(4.3)

where

We write

\mathscr{D}(s)=\prod_{p}\mathscr{D}_{p}(s),

\mathscr{D}_{p}(s)=\operatorname*{\sum...\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},b_{1},n,a_{2},b_{2},m\geq 0\\

\mathcal{D}=R_{1}+R_{2}+E_{1}+E_{2},

where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\underset{u=0}{\text{Res\ }}\left[q^{\frac{3}{2}u}\zeta^{9}(1+u)H(1+u)\gamma^{3}(u)e^{3u^{2}}u^{-1}\right],

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(-1)}\gamma^{3}(s)\underset{u=0}{\text{Res\ }}\left[q^{\frac{3}{2}u}\zeta^{9}(1+u)H(1+u)\gamma^{3}(u-s)e^{3u^{2}-2us}\frac{1}{u-s}\right]\frac{ds}{s},

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int\limits_{(-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon)}q^{\frac{3}{2}u}\zeta^{9}(1+u)H(1+u)\gamma^{3}(u)e^{3u^{2}}\frac{du}{u},

5. Truncation of the The Off-Diagonal Terms

The off-diagonal contribution is

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{\sum...\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},b_{1},n\geq 1\\
a_{2},b_{2},m\geq 1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a_{1})\mu(a_{2})\tau_{3}(b_{1})\tau_{3}(n)\tau_{3}(b_{2})\tau_{3}(m)}{(a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{2}}(a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m)^{\frac{1}{2}}}U\left(\frac{a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}n}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)U\left(\frac{a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}m}{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\bigg(\frac{2}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi(q)\\

so we are left to consider

\displaystyle(\log q)^{5}\operatorname*{\sum\sum\sum\sum}_{a_{1},b_{1},a_{2},b_{2}\geq 1}\frac{(a_{1}b_{1}a_{2}b_{2})^{\varepsilon}(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2})^{2}(a_{1},u_{2})(a_{2},u_{1})}{(a_{1}a_{2})^{3}(b_{1}b_{2})^{2}}\sum_{c\leq q}\frac{(a_{1},c)(a_{2},c)\tau_{2}(c)^{2}}{c}.

,
a_{2}=\delta\lambda_{2}
with

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid\delta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\leq q\\

\displaystyle=\varepsilon(a_{1}+b_{1}+a_{2}+b_{2})+2\min(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})+\min(a_{1},u_{2})+\min(a_{2},u_{1})

Case 1.3:

. In this case
Ay_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\asymp B
, and so the range of
m
that should be considered is of size
(A\eta_{2})^{3}M^{\frac{1}{2}}
by the same arguments above, and the contribution from this range is also bounded by
a_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}
.

8.2.  The Sums
\mathcal{T}_{6},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{9}

Each of the four sums
\mathcal{T}_{6},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{9}
has essentially the same form and behavior, so we deal only with
\mathcal{T}_{6}
. The treatment these sums is very similar to that of
\mathcal{T}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{T}_{5}
, so we shall be somewhat brief. Recall that

\displaystyle E_{6}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq C}\frac{1}{\delta}\sum_{c\leq C/\delta}\frac{1}{c}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{*}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}x(c\delta)\\
(u_{2}x-u_{1},c\delta)=\delta\end{subarray}}\frac{\pi^{3}}{\eta_{1}^{3}\eta_{2}^{3}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\geq 1}A_{3}^{+}\left(n,\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\eta_{1}}\right)A_{3}^{+}\left(m,\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\eta_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{1}(y_{1})F_{2}(y_{2})U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}ny_{1}}{\eta_{1}^{3}}\right)U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}my_{2}}{\eta_{2}^{3}}\right)J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi}{c\delta q}\sqrt{a_{1}y_{1}a_{2}y_{2}}\right)dy_{1}\ dy_{2},

where

\eta_{1}=\frac{u_{2}c}{(a_{1},u_{2}c)},\qquad\eta_{2}=\frac{u_{1}c}{(a_{2},u_{1}c)}.

As before, we use (
8.1
) and estimate the sum over
x
trivially by
c\delta
to see that

E_{6}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{N})\ll\sum_{\delta\leq C}\sum_{c\leq C/\delta}\frac{1}{(\eta_{1}\eta_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{m\geq 1}(nm)^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}\left|I(n,m)\right|,

where

I(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{1}(y_{1})F_{2}(y_{2})U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}ny_{1}}{\eta_{1}^{3}}\right)U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}my_{2}}{\eta_{2}^{3}}\right)J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi}{c\delta q}\sqrt{a_{1}y_{1}a_{2}y_{2}}\right)dy_{1}\ dy_{2}.

Recall that it suffices to show that
E_{6}\ll(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}
. Following our previous analysis, we write

E_{6}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}E_{6,i},

where
E_{6,i}
is the contribution to
E_{6}
from case
i
below.

(1)

n\ll\frac{\eta_{1}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{N}
and
m\ll\frac{\eta_{2}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{M}
;

(2)

n\gg\frac{\eta_{1}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{N}
and
m\ll\frac{\eta_{2}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{M}
;

(3)

n\ll\frac{\eta_{1}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{N}
and
m\gg\frac{\eta_{2}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{M}
;

(4)

n\gg\frac{\eta_{1}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{N}
and
m\gg\frac{\eta_{2}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{M}
.

By symmetry, the treatment of cases (2) and (3) is the same, so we treat only
the second case.

8.2.1
The Contribution of
E_{6,1}

For this case, we use the estimate

U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}n_{i}y_{i}}{\eta_{i}^{3}}\right)\ll q^{\varepsilon}

along with (
5.5
) to see that

I(n,m)\ll q^{\varepsilon}(NM)^{\frac{1}{2}}\min\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}}{c\delta q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}}{c\delta q}\right)^{k-1}\right),

and so

\displaystyle E_{6,1}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{N})

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon}(NM)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\delta\leq C}\sum_{c\leq C/\delta}\frac{1}{(\eta_{1}\eta_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\min\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}}{c\delta q}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}}{c\delta q}\right)^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{n\ll\frac{\eta_{1}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{N}}\sum_{m\ll\frac{\eta_{2}^{3}q^{\varepsilon}}{M}}(nm)^{\frac{1}{4}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ll q^{\varepsilon}\frac{(u_{1}u_{2})^{\frac{9}{4}}}{(NM)^{-\frac{3}{4}}}\sum_{\delta\leq C}\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}}{\delta q}\right)^{\frac{13}{4}}

\displaystyle\ll q^{-\frac{13}{4}+\varepsilon}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{31}{8}}(b_{1}b_{2})^{\frac{9}{4}}(NM)^{\frac{7}{8}}

\displaystyle\ll q^{-\frac{13}{4}+\varepsilon}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(a_{1}^{3}b_{1}^{2}N)^{\frac{9}{8}}(a_{2}^{3}b_{2}^{2}M)^{\frac{9}{8}}

\displaystyle\ll(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{8}+\varepsilon}.

8.2.2
The Contribution of
E_{6,2}

We write

I(n,m)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{2}(y_{2})U_{3}\left(\frac{\pi^{3}my_{2}}{\eta_{2}^{3}}\right)I_{1}(n,y_{2})dy_{1}\ dy_{2}.

The integration in
y_{2}
can be bounded trivially and the sum over
m
can be treated as in the previous subsection. The integral
I_{1}(n,y_{2})
can be handled in the same way as cases 1 and 2 in the Section
8.1.2
, and we obtain
E_{6,2}\ll a_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}
.

8.2.3
The Contribution of
E_{6,4}

As in Section
8.1.2
, we let
C_{2}=8\pi(q\delta)^{-1}\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}NM}
and divide into two cases depending as
c\leq C_{2}
and
c>C_{2}
.

Case 1:
c>C_{2}
. We again use (
5.4
) and (
8.3
) and consider integrals of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2})e\left(\frac{a_{1}^{2}b_{1}y_{1}}{c\delta qa_{2}b_{2}}+\frac{a_{2}^{2}b_{2}y_{2}}{c\delta qa_{1}b_{1}}\pm\frac{3n^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\eta_{1}}\pm\frac{3m^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{2}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\eta_{2}}\right)dy_{1}\ dy_{2},

where
\frac{\partial^{j}\partial^{k}\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2})}{\partial y_{1}^{j}\partial y_{2}^{k}}\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2})\ll N^{-j}M^{-k}
,
\mathcal{H}(y_{1},y_{2})\ll 1
, and is supported on
[N,2N]\times[M,2M]
. Thus the range of integration is
O(NM)
.

By the same arguments as in Case 1 of Section
8.1.2
, it suffices to consider the case when
c_{1}(B_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{2}\ll n\ll c_{2}(B_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{2}
and
c_{1}(B_{2}\eta_{2})^{3}M^{2}\ll m\ll c_{2}(B_{2}\eta_{2})^{3}M^{2}
, where
c_{1},c_{2}
are some constants,

B_{1}=\frac{a_{1}^{2}b_{1}}{c\delta qa_{2}b_{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{2}=\frac{a_{2}^{2}b_{2}}{c\delta qa_{1}b_{1}}.

The terms outside these ranges give negligible contribution from integration by parts many times. As before, we have

(B_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{2}\ll\frac{(a_{1}^{2}b_{1})^{3}N^{2}}{\delta^{3}q^{3}}\ll\frac{q^{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{3}},\quad(B_{2}\eta_{2})^{3}M^{2}\ll\frac{(a_{2}^{2}b_{2})^{3}M^{2}}{\delta^{3}q^{3}}\ll\frac{q^{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{3}}.

There are no terms of this form unless

N\gg\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(a_{1}^{2}b_{1})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\qquad M\gg\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(a_{2}^{2}b_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\qquad\delta\ll q^{\varepsilon}.

The contribution to
E_{6,4}
of the terms with
c>C_{2}
is bounded by

\frac{q^{\varepsilon}(NM)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(u_{1}u_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\sum_{\delta\ll q^{\varepsilon}}\sum_{c>C_{2}}\frac{((a_{1},u_{2}c)(a_{2},u_{1}c))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{c^{3}}.

(8.7)

To estimate the sum over
c
, let

\displaystyle g_{1}=(a_{1},u_{2}),

\displaystyle a_{1}=\lambda_{1}g_{1},

\displaystyle u_{2}=\gamma_{1}g_{1},

\displaystyle g_{2}=(a_{2},u_{1}),

\displaystyle a_{2}=\lambda_{2}g_{2},

\displaystyle u_{1}=\gamma_{2}g_{2},

and

d=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}),\quad\lambda_{1}=\alpha_{1}d,\quad\lambda_{2}=\alpha_{2}d,

where
(\lambda_{1},\gamma_{1})=(\lambda_{2},\gamma_{2})=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=1
. Then the sum over
c
is

\displaystyle(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{c>C_{2}}\frac{((\lambda_{1},c)(\lambda_{2},c))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{c^{3}}=(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell\mid d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c>C_{2}\\
(c,d)=\ell\end{subarray}}\frac{((\alpha_{1}d,c)(\alpha_{2}d,c))^{\frac{3}{2}}}{c^{3}}

\displaystyle=(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell\mid d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c>\frac{C_{2}}{\ell}\\
(c,\frac{d}{\ell})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},c)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{c^{3}}=(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell\mid d}\sum_{k\mid\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}k^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c>\frac{C_{2}}{\ell}\\
(c,\frac{d}{\ell})=1\\
(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},c)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{3}}

\displaystyle=(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell\mid d}\sum_{k\mid\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}k^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c>\frac{C_{2}}{\ell k}\\
(c,\frac{d}{\ell})=1\\
(\frac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{k},c)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{c^{3}}\ll\frac{(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{C_{2}^{2}}\sum_{\ell\mid d}\ell^{2}\sum_{k\mid\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}k^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\delta^{2}q^{2+\varepsilon}\frac{(g_{1}g_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{a_{1}a_{2}NM}d^{2}(\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll\delta^{2}q^{2+\varepsilon}\frac{(a_{1},u_{2})(a_{2},u_{1})(a_{1},a_{2})}{NM(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Thus the total contribution is bounded by

\displaystyle q^{2+\varepsilon}\frac{(a_{1},u_{2})(a_{2},u_{1})(a_{1},a_{2})}{(u_{1}u_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(NM)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{(a_{1},u_{2})(a_{2},u_{1})(a_{1},a_{2})}{(u_{1}u_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}(a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}a_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\ll q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2})}{(u_{1}u_{2}a_{1}a_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}^{\frac{3}{4}}a_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{2}^{\frac{3}{4}}\right)=q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(a_{1},a_{2})(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2})(b_{1}b_{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\ll q^{\frac{7}{8}+\varepsilon}(a_{1},a_{2})(a_{1}b_{1},a_{2}b_{2}).

Summing this over
a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2}
produces several factors of
\log q
, and thus the contribution from these terms is sufficiently small.

Case 2:

c\leq C_{2}
. We proceed as in the last case, except that we use (
5.3
) in place of (
5.4
). The integrals we consider have the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}G(y_{1},y_{2})e\left(\varphi(m,n,y_{1},y_{2})\right)dy_{1}\ dy_{2},

where

\varphi(m,n,y_{1},y_{2})=\frac{a_{1}^{2}b_{1}y_{1}}{c\delta qa_{2}b_{2}}+\frac{a_{2}^{2}b_{2}y_{2}}{c\delta qa_{1}b_{1}}\pm\frac{3n^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{1}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\eta_{1}}\pm\frac{3m^{\frac{1}{3}}y_{2}^{\frac{1}{3}}}{\eta_{2}}\pm\frac{2\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}y_{1}y_{2}}}{c\delta q}

\frac{\partial^{j}\partial^{k}G(y_{1},y_{2})}{\partial y_{1}^{j}\partial y_{2}^{k}}G(y_{1},y_{2})\ll N^{-j}M^{-k}
,
G(y_{1},y_{2})\ll 1
. As before, the range of integration is
O(NM)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi(m,n,y_{1},y_{2})}{\partial y_{1}}

\displaystyle=B_{1}\pm\frac{n^{\frac{1}{3}}}{y_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}\eta_{1}}\pm\frac{A_{1}}{y_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi(m,n,y_{1},y_{2})}{\partial y_{2}}

\displaystyle=B_{2}\pm\frac{m^{\frac{1}{3}}}{y_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}\eta_{2}}\pm\frac{A_{2}}{y_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where
B_{1},B_{2}
are as above and
A_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}y_{2}}}{c\delta q}
and
A_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}a_{2}y_{1}}}{c\delta q}
. We now follow closely the analysis for Case 2 of Section
8.1.2
, dividing into several subcases.

Case 2.1
:
a_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{2}M^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 4a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For this case, we have
\left|\frac{A_{1}}{y_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\pm B_{1}\right|\asymp\frac{A_{1}}{y_{1}}
and
\left|\frac{A_{2}}{y_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\pm B_{2}\right|\asymp B_{2}
. By similar arguments to Case 2 of Section
8.1.2
, we consider the ranges
n\asymp(A_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
m\asymp(B_{2}\eta_{2})^{3}M^{2}
and note that

(A_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll\left(\frac{\sqrt{a_{1}}}{\delta q^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)^{3}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\ll\frac{q^{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{3}},\qquad(B_{2}\eta_{2})^{3}M^{2}\ll\left(\frac{(a_{2}^{2}b_{2})^{3}M^{2}}{\delta^{3}q^{3}}\right)\ll\frac{q^{\varepsilon}}{\delta^{3}},

and thus there are no terms of this form unless
N\gg\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}{a_{1}^{3}}
,
M\gg\frac{q^{\frac{3}{2}}}{(a_{2}^{2}b_{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}
and
\delta\ll q^{\varepsilon}
. The contribution from these terms to
E_{6,4}
is
O(a_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon})
.

Case 2.2:

a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 4a_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{2}N^{2}
. The calculation as in Case 2.1 gives and error of
O(a_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon})
.

Case 2.3:

\frac{1}{4}a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}<a_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{2}M^{\frac{1}{2}}<4a_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}b_{1}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. For this case, we have
\frac{A_{1}}{y_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\asymp B_{1}
and
\frac{A}{y_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\asymp B_{2}
. By similar arguments to Cases 1.2 and 1.3, we need only consider the ranges
n\asymp(A_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
m\asymp(A_{1}\eta_{1})^{3}N^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The contribution from these terms is also
O\left(a_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}q^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\right)
.

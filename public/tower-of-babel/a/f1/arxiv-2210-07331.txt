Relativistic Hydrodynamics a brief review of classical and quantum fluids in relativistic astrophysics
R. F. Santos, A. C. Amaro Faria Jr, L. G. Almeida

Abstract
The objective of this work is to revisit fundamental aspects of relativistic hydrodynamics, aiming at the construction of a first course in relativistic hydrodynamics and its applications to astrophysics at the level of end of undergraduate course and beginning of graduate course. We aim to introduce more basic concepts of basic hydrodynamics, going through models analogous to gravity to the theory of superfluids, applying mainly to astrophysics and the cosmology of the dark universe. We review the classical hydrodynamics, Galileo symmetry and its extension to Lorentz Symmetry applied to fluids, enabling the analogy of fluids with space-time. We study the conservation of the momentum-energy tensor and the energy conditions of Hawking-Ellis. In the next sections we investigate quantum effects, in particular linked to superfluids, and we also sketch an application to dark matter. In this study, we conclude that superfluidity is one of the possible ways to quantize gravity.

II
Non-relativistic fluid

II.1
Continuity Equation

We will now demonstrate the most important equation of hydrodynamics, the continuity equation.
The mass of a fluid can be defined as follows

m=\int\rho{dV}

(1)

where
\rho
is the volumetric density of mass and
V
the volume occupied by the fluid, considering the mass of a given fluid that flows through an element of area
ds
per unit of time, we have:

\frac{dm}{dt}=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int\rho{dV}\right).

(2)

We can define the current

\vec{J}=\rho\vec{v}

(3)

where
\vec{v}=\vec{v}(x,y,z,t)
is the velocity of the fluid. If we calculate the fluid current flow through the same area element as the integral of the dot product between the current and the normal vector to the area element

\Phi=\int_{\mathcal{D}s}\vec{J}\cdot{d}\vec{s}.

(4)

By the theorem of Gauss, we relate the integral in the area with the volume integral of the current source, represented by the divergence,

\int_{\mathcal{D}s}\vec{J}\cdot{d}\vec{s}=\int_{\mathcal{D}V}\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}dV,

(5)

where the operator
\vec{\nabla}
is given by

\vec{\nabla}=e_{x}\partial_{x}+e_{y}\partial_{y}+e_{z}\partial_{z}.

(6)

The triplet
(e_{x},e_{y},e_{z})
is the set of unitary vectors in the respective coordinate directions, and the operation

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}=\partial_{x}J_{x}+\partial_{y}J_{y}+\partial_{z}J_{z}

(7)

is a dot product, representing the sources and drains of current.
Substituting
3
in
4
we get

\Phi=\int_{\mathcal{D}s}\rho\vec{v}\cdot{d}\vec{s}.

(8)

Considering now the rate of mass that passes through a given unit of area per unit of time,
2
and taking
\vec{v}
as a constant, we write

\frac{d\Phi}{dt}=\int_{\mathcal{D}s}\frac{d\rho}{dt}\vec{v}\cdot{d}\vec{s}.

(9)

Analyzing the right side of
5
, we have

\int\frac{d\rho}{dt}\vec{v}\cdot\vec{ds}=\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int\vec{J}\cdot\vec{ds}\right].

(10)

Calculating the scalar product

\vec{v}\cdot{d}\vec{s}=\frac{dxds}{dt},

(11)

we find

\frac{dxds}{dt}=\frac{dV}{dt}.

(12)

We now consider the action of the operator
{d}/{dt}
on the right side of
5
,

\frac{d}{dt}\left[\int\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}dV\right]=\int\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}\frac{dV}{dt},

(13)

which is equivalent to saying that the font
\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}
is a time-independent function. Therefore, we can write

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}.

(14)

This is the so-called
continuity equation
, which implies that fluid is a conserved quantity, so the same amount of fluid that enters the pipe is the amount that leaves, as the sources and sinks are computed in the temporal variation of the fluid density.

II.2
Conservation of Momentum – The Navier Stokes equation

We are going to calculate the pressure under a fluid element. We can consider an infinitesimal element of volume of cubic form and calculate the force on walls that have a normal in the
x
direction

dF_{x}=p(x,y,z)dydz-p(x+dx,y+dy,z+dz)dydz,

(15)

expanding
p(x+dx,y+dy,z+dz)
in Taylor series and rewriting
15
, we can approximate

dF(x)=p(x,y,z)dydz-p(x,y,z)dydz-\frac{\partial{p}}{\partial{x}}dxdydz.

(16)

Considering then the force on the mass element
dm
and remembering that

\displaystyle\rho=\frac{dm}{dV}

\displaystyle dV=dxdydz,

we can write

(dm)\vec{a}=d\vec{F}_{x}=-\vec{\nabla}pdV,

(17)

where

\vec{\nabla}{p}=e_{x}\partial_{x}p+e_{y}\partial_{y}p+e_{z}\partial_{z}p

(18)

is the pressure gradient. We know that

\displaystyle m\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}=\vec{F}

\displaystyle\frac{dm}{dV}\frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}=\frac{d\vec{F}}{dV}=-\vec{\nabla}p,

where

\vec{v}=\vec{v}(t,\vec{r}),

(19)

we then write:

\frac{d\vec{v}(t,\vec{r})}{dt}=\frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial{t}}dt+\frac{\partial\vec{v}(t,\vec{r})}{\partial{\vec{r}}}d\vec{r},

(20)

where

\frac{\partial\vec{v}(t,\vec{r})}{\partial{r}}=\frac{\partial\vec{r}}{\partial{t}}\cdot\frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial\vec{r}}\vec{v}=\frac{\partial\vec{v}(t,\vec{r})}{\partial{t}}+(\vec{v\cdot\vec{\nabla}})\vec{v}.

(21)

Returning the result above in
20
, we get

\frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial{t}}+(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\nabla})\vec{v}=-\frac{1}{\rho}\vec{\nabla}p.

(22)

The equation
22
is the
Navier Stokes equation
, which corresponds to Newton’s second law for the fluid mass element
dm
.

II.3
Barotropic fluids

A fluid is defined as
barotropic
if the energy density is a function only of the pressure, or
\rho\equiv\rho(p)
, as seen in
Visser and Molina-París 2010
. Therefore, the inverse function shall be
p=p(\rho)
and we can define

\mathcal{P}=\int\frac{dp}{\rho}\Rightarrow d\mathcal{P}=\frac{dp}{\rho},

(23)

thus

d\vec{x}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\mathcal{P}=\frac{dp}{\rho},

(24)

and we can write

\frac{dp}{\rho}=d\vec{x}\cdot\frac{\vec{\nabla}p}{\rho},

(25)

so

d\vec{x}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\mathcal{P}=d\vec{x}\cdot\frac{\vec{\nabla}p}{\rho}

(26)

then

d\vec{x}\cdot\left(\vec{\nabla}\mathcal{P}-\frac{\vec{\nabla}p}{\rho}\right)=0

(27)

indicating then that

\vec{\nabla}\mathcal{P}=\frac{\vec{\nabla}p}{\rho}.

(28)

We then write the acceleration as the sum of the gravitational potential gradient and the gradient of
\mathcal{P}
,

\vec{a}=\vec{\nabla}G-\vec{\nabla}\mathcal{P}=\vec{\nabla}\left(G-\int\frac{dp}{\rho}\right).

(29)

Returning the equation
22
, we can see that

\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\vec{v}=\frac{1}{2}\vec{\nabla}\left(\vec{v}\cdot\vec{v}\right)-\vec{v}\times\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{v},

(30)

we then define the vorticity of a fluid as

\vec{w}=\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{v}.

(31)

Replacing
31
in
22
, we have

\frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial{t}}-\vec{v}\times\vec{w}=\vec{\nabla}\left[G-\int\frac{dp}{\rho}-\frac{|\vec{v}|^{2}}{2}\right].

(32)

If we do
\vec{w}=0
in
31
we have an irrotational fluid. If we have, in addition to an irrotational liquid, a fluid with steady flow
{\partial\vec{v}}/{\partial{t}}=0
, then we have:

\vec{\nabla}\left[G-\int\frac{dp}{\rho}-\frac{|\vec{v}|^{2}}{2}\right]=0,

(33)

so we have the
Bernouli equation

\left[G-\int\frac{dp}{\rho}-\frac{|\vec{v}|^{2}}{2}\right]=constant.

(34)

Considering, therefore, a steady state

f(t)=G-\int\frac{dp}{\rho}-\frac{|\vec{v}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{t}},

(35)

and doing
f=-g,G=-gz,p=constant
, we find

G-\frac{p}{\rho}-\frac{|\vec{v}|^{2}}{2}-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial{t}}=constant.

(36)

II.4
Potential flow

A potential flow is defined according to the following relation of fluid velocity with a scalar potential

\vec{v}=\vec{\nabla}\phi,

(37)

if we extract the divergence of
37

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{v}=\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\phi=\nabla^{2}\phi=0,

(38)

we find the
Laplace equation

\nabla^{2}\phi=0.

(39)

This type of flow is said to be incompressible. Which implies that
{d\rho}/{dt}=0
, by the continuity equation and considering the definition of the current
3
, we have that

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}=\vec{\nabla}\cdot\rho\vec{v},

(40)

using the derivative properties, we get

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}=\vec{\nabla}\rho\cdot\vec{v}+\rho\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\phi,

(41)

in this case, by the equation
39
the second term vanishes, what makes the fluid incompressible is the density gradient
\vec{\nabla}\rho=0
. In this case, the
Euler equation
comes from
22

\frac{\partial\vec{v}}{\partial{t}}=-\frac{1}{\rho}\vec{\nabla}P.

(42)

So the pressure generates acceleration of the fluid element in the flow direction.

III
Electrodynamics as a theory of fluid

Electromagnetism conceived current as a fluid of charge carriers, which propagated through conductors. The very idea of fields also had at its core the concept of fluid, in the case of the electric field being a fluid, the flux was calculated by Gauss’ law

\vec{\nabla}\cdot{\vec{E}}=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_{0}}

(43)

and charge

q=\int_{V}\rho{dV}.

(44)

The vortices associated with this fluid are described by Faraday’s law

\vec{\nabla}\times{\vec{E}}=-\partial_{t}\vec{B}

(45)

where the magnetic field is associated with the fluid circulation property.
Furthermore, the divergence of the magnetic field is zero

\vec{\nabla}\cdot{\vec{B}}=0,

(46)

that shows the inexistence of magnetic sources (monopoles). Completing the symmetry between the fields,
we have the Ampere-Maxwell law

\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{B}=\mu_{0}\vec{J}+\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\partial_{t}\vec{E},

(47)

where
\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\partial_{t}\vec{E}
is the displacement current discovered by Maxwell.
Here
\vec{J}=q\vec{v}
is the charge carrier current. Applying the divergence to
47
, we have

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{B}=\mu_{0}\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}+\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\partial_{t}\left(\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}\right).

(48)

Substituting
43
and considering the properties of the divergence, we get

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{J}+\partial_{t}\rho=0,

(49)

which is the
continuity equation
.

In the absence of sources
q=0,\vec{j}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\cdot{\vec{E}}=0,

(50)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\times{\vec{E}}=-\partial_{t}\vec{B},

(51)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\cdot{\vec{B}}=0,

(52)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{B}=\mu_{0}\epsilon_{0}\partial_{t}\vec{E}.

(53)

Therefore, the vortex of an electric field implies the temporal variation of the magnetic field,
just as the magnetic vortex implies the temporal variation of the electric field.
Applying the curl (
53
), we have:

\nabla^{2}\vec{B}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial^{2}_{t}\vec{B}=0,

(54)

so we have the wave equation for the magnetic field. We can likewise deduce for the electric field, by applying the curl to the equation (
51
)

\nabla^{2}\vec{E}-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial^{2}_{t}\vec{E}=0.

(55)

Although the fields are similar to fluids, the electromagnetic wave does not propagate in any medium,
this was one of the greatest revolutions in science, for it put an end to the idea of the luminiferous ether, which the Mickelson-Morley experiment later showed did not exist.

IV
Covariance of the Wave Equation

We now are going to check whether the Galileo transformations keep the wave equation invariant.

\nabla^{2}\phi-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\partial_{t}\phi=0,

(56)

\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial{t^{\prime}}}-V\frac{\partial}{\partial{x^{\prime}}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial{t^{\prime}}}-\vec{V}\cdot{\vec{\nabla^{\prime}}}.

The second time derivative

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{t}^{2}}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{t^{\prime}}}-\vec{V}\cdot{\vec{\nabla^{\prime}}}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{t^{\prime}}}-\vec{V}\cdot{\vec{\nabla^{\prime}}}\right).

Substituting

(64)

We can see that the wave equation is not covariant against Galileo transformations, in the case of sound waves, this non-covariance is associated with the presence of a medium, so the extra term represents
motion in relation to the medium, however electromagnetic waves do not depend on a medium to propagate, as demonstrated by the Michelson-Morlei experiment.

Space-time interval

In relativity the spatial intervals are replaced by space-temporal intervals, because time becomes an axis, a coordinate as well as space. Therefore to describe an event in space-time, we need coordinates. To describe a point in space, thinking in two dimensions, we can build a space-time interval, which is an invariant quantity of relativity

\delta{s}^{2}=(s_{1}-s_{0})^{2}=(x_{1}-x_{0})^{2}-c^{2}(t_{1}-t_{0})^{2},

(103)

being the same in any inertial frame of reference
being

\delta{s}^{2}<0,

(104)

wich we call time-type interval. This interval is related to events which are connected by a causal relationship, the signals that link these events are slower than light. We call it time interval.
If we have

\delta{s^{2}}=-c^{2}\delta\tau^{2}.

(105)

We may have a light-type interval,

\delta{s}^{2}=0,

(106)

where the signal connecting the two events is a light signal.
this is the boundary of the causal region.

Finally, if we have the interval

\delta{s}^{2}>0,

(107)

this type of interval is no longer a causal interval, so the events placed by these signals cannot be
related as cause and effect, space-like region.

These properties define what we call the cone of light (see the Fig.

XIX
Conclusions

The study of the momentum-energy tensor is a fundamental topic in relativity, the review of classical conditions of
energy is a necessity given its relevance to several lines of research in astrophysics and cosmology. The emergence of dark and exotic relativistic fluids, brings the need to review their energy conditions especially in terms of causality.
The anisotropy of the momentum-energy tensor is a topic that still raises many doubts among students and the
cosmological constant is increasingly being associated with ultra-dense objects. We review these themes by calculating the conservation
of the momentum-energy tensor, we seek to contribute to the discussion about energy conditions,
both in the isotropic and anisotropic case, identifying exactly which energy condition is associated with vacuum energy.

We built the superfluid structure via Landau formalism, introduced Galilean and Lorentzian symmetries and some of its aspects applied to superfluids were discussed.

In the future, we intend to extend this study to a review of the SVT theory, also to study relativistic fluids associated with nuclear astrophysics such as Bjorken and Gupsen fluids and review properties of relativistic fluids with viscosity, in addition to investigating further the causal structure, especially in dark and tachionic fluids. Exploring fluid-gravity correspondence is also one of our goals.

Hess-Schrader-Uhlenbrock inequality for the heat semigroup on differential forms over Dirichlet spaces tamed by distributional curvature lower bounds
Kazuhiro Kuwae

Abstract
The notion of tamed Dirichlet space was proposed by Erbar, Rigoni, Sturm and Tamanini [ 27 ] as a Dirichlet space having a weak form of Bakry-Émery curvature lower bounds in distribution sense. After their work, Braun [ 14 ] established a vector calculus for it, in particular, the space of L 2 L^{2} -normed L ∞ L^{\infty} -module describing vector fields, 1 1 -forms, Hessian in L 2 L^{2} -sense. In this framework, we establish the Hess-Schrader-Uhlenbrock inequality for 1 1 -forms as an element of L 2 L^{2} -cotangent module (an L 2 L^{2} -normed L ∞ L^{\infty} -module), which extends the Hess-Schrader-Uhlenbrock inequality by Braun [ 14 ] under an additional condition.

\displaystyle|R_{\alpha}^{\rm HK}\omega|\leq R_{\alpha}^{\kappa}|\omega|\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}

(1.7)

(ii)

For every

Smooth measures of extended Kato class

In this section, we summarize results on smooth measures of extended Kato class.
Denote by
S({\bf X})
the family of (non-negative) smooth measures on
(M,\mathscr{B}(M))
(see
[
30
, p. 83]
for the precise
definition of smooth measures). Then there exists a PCAF
A^{\nu}
admitting exceptional set satisfying

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{h\mathfrak{m}}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}f(X_{t})\mathrm{d}A_{t}^{\nu}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{f\nu}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}f(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t\right],\quad f,h\in\mathscr{B}_{+}(M)

(see
[
30
, (5.14)]
).
A measure
\nu\in S({\bf X})
is said to be of
Dynkin class
(resp.
Kato class
) if
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}<\infty
for some/all
t>0
(resp.
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}=0
).
A measure
\nu\in S({\bf X})
is said to be of
extended Kato class
if
\lim_{t\to 0}\|\mathbb{E}_{x}[A_{t}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}<1
. Denote by
S_{D}({\bf X})
(resp.
S_{K}({\bf X})
,
S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
) the family of
(non-negative) smooth measures of Dynkin class (resp. Kato class, extended Kato class). For
\nu\in S_{D}({\bf X})
,

x\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{x}[A_{t}^{\nu}]
is
\mathscr{E}
-quasi-continuous, hence
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}
actually coincides with a quasi-essentially supremum:

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}=q\text{-}\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}_{x}[A_{t}^{\nu}]:=\inf_{N:\mathscr{E}\text{-exceptional}}\sup_{x\in N^{c}}\mathbb{E}_{x}[A_{t}^{\nu}].

For
\nu\in S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
, there exist positive constants
C=C(\nu)
and
C_{\nu}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[e^{A_{t}^{\nu}}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}\leq Ce^{C_{\nu}t}.

(2.1)

Indeed, we can choose
C(\nu):=(1-\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})})^{-1}
and
C_{\nu}:=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\log C(\nu)
.
for some sufficiently small
t_{0}\in]0,1[
satisfying
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}<1
(see
[
62
, Proof of Theorem 2.2]
). Denote by
P_{t}^{-\nu}f(x)
the Feynman-Kac semigroup defined by

P_{t}^{-\nu}f(x):=\mathbb{E}_{x}[e^{A_{t}^{\nu}}f(X_{t})]
,
f\in\mathscr{B}_{+}(M)
. (
2.1
) is nothing but

\|P_{t}^{-\nu}\|_{\infty,\infty}\leq Ce^{C_{\nu}t}
. Moreover, for the conjugate exponent

q:=p/(p-1)\in[1,+\infty]
we can deduce

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{-\nu}\|_{p,p}\leq C(q\nu)e^{\frac{C_{q\nu}}{q}t}\quad\text{ for }\quad p\in]1,+\infty].

(2.2)

Here
\|P_{t}^{-\nu}\|_{p,p}:=\sup\{\|P_{t}^{-\nu}f\|_{L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})}\mid f\in L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m}),\|f\|_{L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})}=1\}
stands for the operator norm from

L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})
to
L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})
.
For each
\beta\geq 1
satisfying
\beta\nu\in S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
, we can choose

\displaystyle C_{\beta\nu}\in]0,C_{\nu}]\quad\text{ and }\quad C(\nu)=C(\beta\nu).

(2.3)

Indeed
C_{\nu}
and
C(\nu)
can be given by

\displaystyle C_{\nu}=\frac{1}{t_{0}}\log\left(1-\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{\infty}\right)^{-1},\quad C(\nu)=\left(1-\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{\infty}\right)^{-1}

(2.4)

for some small
t_{0}\in]0,1]
satisfying
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{\infty}<1
(see
[
62
, Proof of Theorem 2.2]
).
If we choose
s_{0}\in]0,t_{0}]

satisfying
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{s_{0}}^{\nu}]\|_{\infty}=\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}[A_{t_{0}}^{\beta\nu}]\|_{\infty}<1
, then we see
C_{\beta\nu}\leq C_{\nu}
and
C(\nu)=C(\beta\nu)
for such selection of
s_{0}
(resp.
t_{0}
) to
C_{\nu}
(resp.
C_{\beta\kappa}
).
If
\beta\nu\in S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
for
\beta\geq 1
, then we can deduce from (
2.2
)
and (
2.3
) that
for
p\in[\beta/(\beta-1),+\infty]

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{-\nu}\|_{p,p}\leq C(\nu)e^{C_{\nu}t}.

(2.5)

In particular, if
\nu\in S_{K}({\bf X})
, then (
2.5
) holds for any
p\in[1,+\infty]

(the proof for
p=1
can be directly deduced).
When
\nu=-R\mathfrak{m}
for a constant
R\in\mathbb{R}
,
C_{\nu}
(resp.
C(\nu)
) can be taken to be
R\lor 0
(resp.
1
).

It is known that
\nu\in S_{D}({\bf X})
(resp.
\nu\in S_{K}({\bf X})
) if and only if
\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\mathrm{d}A_{t}^{\nu}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}<\infty
for some/all
\alpha>0
(resp.
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\mathrm{d}A_{t}^{\nu}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}=0
), and
\nu\in S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
if and only if

\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\alpha t}\mathrm{d}A_{t}^{\nu}\right]\|_{L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})}<1
.

Under Assumption

.

Let

The following lemmas hold.

Lemma 3.2

(

In particular, for

(

Under

(

.

Under Assumption

Lemma 3.5
.

{\rm Test}(M)\cap L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})
is dense in
L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})\cap L^{2}(M;\mathfrak{m})
both in

-norm

Vector space calculus for tamed space

4.1

L^{\infty}
-module

We need the notion of
L^{p}
-normed
L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})
-modules.

Definition 4.1

(
L^{\infty}
-module)
.

Given
p\in[1,+\infty]
, a real Banach space
(\mathscr{M},\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{M}})
, or simply,
\mathscr{M}
is called an
L^{p}
-normed
L^{\infty}
-module
(over
(M,\mathfrak{m})
) if it satisfies

(a)

a bilinear map
\cdot
:
L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})\times\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{M}
satisfying

\displaystyle(fg)\cdot v

\displaystyle=f\cdot(gv),

\displaystyle{\bf 1}_{M}\cdot v

\displaystyle=v,

(b)

a nonnegatively valued map
|\cdot|_{\mathfrak{m}}:\mathscr{M}\to L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})
such that

\displaystyle|f\cdot v|_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle=|f||v|_{\mathfrak{m}}\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathscr{M}}

\displaystyle=\||v|_{\mathfrak{m}}\|_{L^{p}(M;\mathfrak{m})},

for every
f,g\in L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})
and
v\in\mathscr{M}
. If only (a) is satisfied, we call

(\mathscr{M},\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{M}})
or simply
\mathscr{M}
an
L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})
-module.

Throughout this paper, we always assume that for every
v\in\mathscr{M}
its point-wise norm
|v|_{\mathfrak{m}}
is Borel measurable.
We call
\mathscr{M}
an
L^{\infty}
-module
if it is
L^{p}
-normed
for some
p\in[1,+\infty]
.
\mathscr{M}
is called
separable

if it is a separable Banach space. We call
v\in\mathscr{M}
is
(
\mathfrak{m}
-essentially) bounded if
|v|_{\mathfrak{m}}\in L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})
.
\mathscr{M}
is called
Hilbert module
if is an
L^{2}
-normed
L^{\infty}
-module, in this case, the point-wise norm
|\cdot|_{\mathfrak{m}}
satisfies a point-wise
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. parallelogram identity, hence it induces a
point-wise scalar product

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}:\mathscr{M}\times\mathscr{M}\to L^{1}(M;\mathfrak{m})
which is
L^{\infty}
-bilinear,
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. nonnegative definite, local in both components, satisfies the point-wise
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and reproduces the Hilbertian scalar product on
\mathscr{M}
by integration with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
.

Definition 4.2

(Dual module)
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}
be
L^{p}
-normed
L^{\infty}
-module. Denote both point-wise norms by
|\cdot|_{\mathfrak{m}}
. A map
T:\mathscr{M}\to\mathscr{N}
is called
module morphism

if it is a bounded linear map in the sense of functional analysis and

\displaystyle T(f\,v)=f\,T(v)

(4.1)

for every
v\in\mathscr{M}
and every
f\in L^{\infty}(M;\mathfrak{m})
. The set of all module morphisms
is written
{\rm Hom}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N})
and equipped with the usual operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N}}
. We call
\mathscr{M}
and
\mathscr{N}

isomorphic
(as
L^{\infty}
-module) if there exists
T\in{\rm Hom}(\mathscr{M},\mathscr{N})
and
S\in{\rm Hom}(\mathscr{N},\mathscr{M})
such that
T\circ S={\rm Id}_{\mathscr{N}}
and
S\circ T={\rm Id}_{\mathscr{M}}
.
Any such
T
is called
module isomorphism
. If in addition, such a
T
is a norm isometry, it is called
module isometric isomorphism
. In fact, by (
4.1
) every module isometric isomorphism

preserves pointwise norms
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e., i.e. for every
v\in\mathscr{M}
,

\displaystyle|T(v)|_{\mathfrak{m}}=|v|_{\mathfrak{m}}\quad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}

The dual module
\mathscr{M}^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{*}:={\rm Hom}(\mathscr{M},L^{1}(M;\mathfrak{m}))

and will be endowed with the usual operator norm. The point-wise paring between

v\in\mathscr{M}
and
L\in\mathscr{M}^{*}
is denoted by
L(v)\in L^{1}(M;\mathfrak{m})
. If
\mathscr{M}
is
L^{p}
-normed, then
\mathscr{M}^{*}
is an
L^{q}
-normed
L^{\infty}
-module, where
p,q\in[1,+\infty]

1/p+1/q=1
(see
[
32
, Proposition 1.2.14]

\displaystyle|L|_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\mathfrak{m}\text{\rm-esssup}\{|L(v)|\mid v\in\mathscr{M},|v|_{\mathfrak{m}}\leq 1\;\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}\}.

(4.2)

By
[
32

, if
p<\infty
,

\displaystyle|v|_{\mathfrak{m}}=\mathfrak{m}\text{\rm-esssup}\{|L(v)|\mid L\in\mathscr{M},|L|_{\mathfrak{m}}\leq 1\;\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.}\}

for every
v\in\mathscr{M}
. Moreover, under

\mathscr{M}^{*}
and the dual Banach space
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}
of
\mathscr{M}
are isometrically isomorphic

. In this case, the natural point-wise paring map

Definition 4.3

(

Tensor products

Definition 4.5

(Tensor products)
.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{1}
and
\mathscr{M}_{2}

By a slight abuse of notation,
we denote both point-wise scalar products by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We consider the
L^{0}
-module
\mathscr{M}_{i}^{0}
induced from
\mathscr{M}_{i}
,

Let
\mathscr{M}_{1}^{0}\odot\mathscr{M}_{2}^{0}
be the “algebraic tensor product”
consisting of all finite linear combinations of formal elements

,
v\in\mathscr{M}_{1}^{0}
and

, obtained by factorizing appropriate vector spaces (see
[

, Section 1.5]
). It naturally comes with a multiplications
\cdot\,:L^{0}(M;\mathfrak{m})\times\mathscr{M}_{1}^{0}\odot\mathscr{M}_{2}^{0}\to\mathscr{M}_{1}^{0}\odot\mathscr{M}_{2}^{0}

L^{0}(T^{*}\!M)
:

\displaystyle{\sf d}_{L^{0}(T^{*}\!M)}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{2^{-i}}{\mathfrak{m}(B_{i})}\int_{B_{i}}\min\{|\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}|,1\}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},\quad\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in L^{0}(T^{*}\!M).

This distance does not depend on the choice of such Borel partition
(B_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
(see
[
14
, 1.4.3]
).
Since
P_{t}^{\kappa}{\bf 1}_{B_{i}}\in L^{2}(M;\mathfrak{m})
under
2\kappa^{-}\in S_{E\!K}({\bf X})
, we see from Theorem
1.2
that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{i}}|P_{t}^{\rm HK}(\omega_{n}-\omega_{m})|\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}P_{t}^{\kappa}{\bf 1}_{B_{i}}|\omega_{n}-\omega_{m}|\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)}\int_{M_{m}\setminus M_{n}}P_{t}^{\kappa}{\bf 1}_{B_{i}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n,m\to\infty.

This means that
(P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
forms a
{\sf d}_{L^{0}(T^{*}\!M)}
-Cauchy sequence.
Denote its limit by
P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega\in L^{0}(T^{*}\!M)
. Then we see
P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega\in L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
and (
9.2
) holds for
\omega
.

Next we suppose
\kappa^{-}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
and
p\in[1,+\infty[
. Then
(
2.5
) with
\nu=\kappa^{-}
yields

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\kappa}\|_{p,p}\leq C(\kappa)e^{C_{\kappa}t}.

(9.3)

Thus Theorem
1.2
and (
9.3
) yield
the estimate (
9.2
) for
\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)\cap L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)
, consequently
(
9.2
) holds for
\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)
as proved above.

Finally we prove the strong continuity of
(P_{t}^{\rm HK})_{t\geq 0}
under
\kappa^{-}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
and

p\in[1,+\infty[
.
Suppose first
\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)\cap H^{1,2}(T^{*}\!M)
. We then know

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{H^{1,2}(T^{*}\!M)}=0

(9.4)

by
[
14
, Theorem 8.31(v)]
.
In particular,

|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega|\to 0
in
\mathfrak{m}
-measure as
t\to 0
.
Take any subsequence
\{t_{n}\}
tending to
0
decreasingly as
n\to\infty
such that

|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega|\to|\omega|

n\to\infty

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. Applying Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\omega|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{M}|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}.

(9.5)

On the other hand, we already know that (
9.2
) holds for
\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)
.
This implies

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}\leq C(\kappa)\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}.

Since the coefficient
C(\kappa)
has the following expression for some sufficiently small
t_{0}>0

\displaystyle C(\kappa)=\frac{1}{1-\left\|\mathbb{E}_{\cdot}\left[A_{t_{0}}^{\kappa^{-}}\right]\right\|_{\infty}}

by (
2.4
)
(see
[
62
, Proof of Theorem 2.2]
), it can be taken to be arbitrarily close to
1
in view of the fact that

\kappa^{-}
is a smooth measure of Kato class. Then, we have

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}.

Combining this and (
9.5
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}=\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}
.
Applying
[
55
, Theorem 16.6]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\|P_{t_{n}}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}=0.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}=0.

Now we consider general
\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)
.
By Corollary
9.2
, there exists
\{\omega_{n}\}\subset L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)\cap H^{1,2}(T^{*}\!M)
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\omega_{n}-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}=0
.
By use of triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}\leq\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}(\omega-\omega_{n})\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}+\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega_{n}-\omega_{n}\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}+\|\omega_{n}-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}.

By (
9.2
) and the conclusion for
\omega_{n}\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)\cap L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)
,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{t\to 0}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}\leq\left(C(\kappa)+1\right)\|\omega_{n}-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{p}(T^{*}\!M)}=0\quad\text{ for }\quad\omega\in L^{p}(T^{*}\!M).

Finally, we prove the weak* continuity of
(P_{t}^{\rm HK})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
under
\kappa^{-}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
.
Take
X\in L^{1}(TM)
. First we assume
\omega\in L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)\cap L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
.
Consider
X_{n}:={\bf 1}_{\{|X|\leq n\}\cap M_{n}}X
as defined above. Then we see

\displaystyle|\langle X_{n},P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle|\leq\|X_{n}\|_{L^{1}(TM)}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad t\to 0.

From this,

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{t\to 0}|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\varlimsup_{t\to 0}\|X-X_{n}\|_{L^{1}(TM)}\|P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)}+\varlimsup_{t\to 0}|\langle X_{n},P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|X-X_{n}\|_{L^{1}(TM)}(1+C(\kappa)))\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty.

Then, we have
\lim_{t\to 0}\langle X_{n},P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle=0
for
\omega\in L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)\cap L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
. Next we take general
\omega\in L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
. Consider
\omega_{n}:={\bf 1}_{M_{n}}\omega
as above. Then we know
P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega\in L^{2}(T^{*}\!M)\cap L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\langle X,\omega_{n}-\omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\langle X,{\bf 1}_{M_{n}^{c}}\omega\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}|\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)}\int_{M_{n}^{c}}|X|_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty,

\displaystyle|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}(\omega_{n}-\omega)\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}{\bf 1}_{M_{n}^{c}}\omega\rangle_{\mathfrak{m}}|\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

\displaystyle\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)}\int_{M_{n}^{c}}P_{t}^{\kappa}|X|_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty,

where we use the
\mathfrak{m}
-symmetry of
P_{t}^{\kappa}
and
P_{t}^{\kappa}|X|\in L^{1}(M;\mathfrak{m})
under
\kappa^{-}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
. Combining this inequality and

\displaystyle|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}(\omega-\omega_{n})\rangle|+|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega_{n}-\omega_{n})\rangle|+|\langle X,\omega_{n}-\omega)\rangle|,

we obtain

\displaystyle\varlimsup_{t\to 0}|\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle|\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(TM)}\int_{M_{n}^{c}}(|X|_{\mathfrak{m}}+P_{t}^{\kappa}|X|_{\mathfrak{m}})\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore,
\lim_{t\to 0}\langle X,P_{t}^{\rm HK}\omega-\omega\rangle=0
for
X\in L^{1}(TM)
and
\omega\in L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)
. This implies the weak* continuity of
(P_{t}^{\rm HK})_{t\geq 0}
on
L^{\infty}(T^{*}\!M)=L^{1}(TM)^{*}
.
∎

10

Examples

Example 10.1

(Riemannian manifolds with boundary)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
.
Denote by
\mathfrak{v}:={\rm vol}_{g}
the Riemannian volume measure induced by
g
, and by

\mathfrak{s}
the surface measure on
\partial M

(see
[
14
, §1.2]
).
If
\partial M\neq\emptyset
, then
\partial M
is a smooth co-dimension
1
submanifold of
M
and it becomes Riemannian
when endowed with the pulback metric

\displaystyle\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{j}:=j^{*}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle,\qquad\langle u,v:=g(u,v)\quad\text{ for }\quad u,v\in TM.

under the natural inclusion
j:\partial M\to M
. The map
j
induces a natural inclusion
d_{j}:T\partial M\to TM|_{\partial M}
which is not surjective. In particular, the vector bundles
T\partial M
and
TM|_{\partial M}
do not coincide.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a Borel measure on
M
which is locally equivalent to
\mathfrak{v}
.
Let
D(\mathscr{E}):=W^{1,2}(M^{\circ})
be the Sobolev space with respect to

\mathfrak{m}
defined in the usual sense on
M^{\circ}:=M\setminus\partial M
. Define
\mathscr{E}:W^{1,2}(M^{\circ})\to[0,+\infty[

by

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(f):=\int_{M^{\circ}}|\nabla f|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}

and the quantity
\mathscr{E}(f,g)
,
f,g\in W^{1,2}(M^{\circ})
, by polarization. Then
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
becomes a strongly local regular Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(M;\mathfrak{m})
, since
C_{c}^{\infty}(M)
is a dense set of
D(\mathscr{E})
.
Let
k:M^{\circ}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\ell:\partial M\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous functions providing lower bounds on the Ricci curvature and the second fundamental form of
\partial M
, respectively.
Suppose that
M
is compact and
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{v}
. Then
(M,\mathscr{E},\mathfrak{m})
is tamed by

\displaystyle\kappa:=k\mathfrak{v}+\ell\mathfrak{s},

because
\mathfrak{v},\mathfrak{s}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
(see
[
27
, Theorem 2.36]
and
[
40
, Theorem 5.1]
). Then one can apply Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
to
(M,\mathscr{E},\mathfrak{m})
.

More generally, if
M
is regularly exhaustible, i.e., there exists an increasing sequence
(M_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of domains
M_{n}\subset M^{\circ}
with smooth boundary
\partial M_{n}
such that
g
is smooth on
M_{n}
and the following properties hold:

(1)

The closed sets
(\overline{M}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
constitute an
\mathscr{E}
-nest for
(\mathscr{E},W^{1,2}(M))
.

(2)

For all compact sets
K\subset M^{\circ}
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such tht
K\subset M_{n}
for all
n\geq N
.

(3)

There are lower bounds
\ell_{n}:\partial M_{n}\to\mathbb{R}
for the curvature of
\partial M_{n}
with
\ell_{n}=\ell
on
\partial M\cap\partial M_{n}
such that the distributions
\kappa_{n}=k\mathfrak{v}_{n}+\ell_{n}\mathfrak{s}_{n}
are uniformly
2
-moderate in
the sense that

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\sup_{x\in M_{n}}\mathbb{E}^{(n)}\left[e^{-2A_{t}^{\kappa_{n}}}\right]<\infty,

(10.1)

where
\mathfrak{v}_{n}
is the volume measure of
M_{n}
and
\mathfrak{s}_{n}
is the surface measure of
\partial M_{n}
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{v}
.
Then
(M,\mathscr{E},\mathfrak{m})
is tamed by
\kappa=k\mathfrak{v}+\ell\mathfrak{s}

(see
[
27
, Theorem 4.5]
). In this case,
Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
hold for
(M,\mathscr{E},\mathfrak{m})
provided

\kappa^{+}\in S_{D}({\bf X})
,
\kappa^{-}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
.

Let
Y
be the domain defined by

\displaystyle Y:=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\mid z>\phi(\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}})\},

where
\phi:[0,+\infty[\to[0,+\infty[
is
C^{2}
on
]0,+\infty[
with
\phi(r):=r-r^{2-\alpha}
,
\alpha\in]0,1[
for

r\in[0,1]
,
\phi
is a constant for
r\geq 2
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)\leq 0
for
r\in[0,+\infty[
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}_{Y}
be the
3
-dimensional Lebesgue measure restricted to
Y
and

\sigma_{\partial Y}
the
2
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial Y
. Denote by

\mathscr{E}_{Y}
the Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(Y;\mathfrak{m})
with Neumann boundary conditions.
The smallest eigenvalue of the second fundamental form of
\partial Y
can be given by

\displaystyle\ell(r,\phi)=\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(r)}{(1+|\phi^{\prime}(r)|^{2})^{3/2}}(\leq 0),

for
r\leq 1
and
\ell=0
for
r\geq 2
. It is proved in
[
27
, Theorem 4.6]
that
the Dirichlet space
(Y,\mathscr{E}_{Y},\mathfrak{m}_{Y})
is tamed by

\displaystyle\kappa=\ell\sigma_{\partial Y}.

In this case, the measure-valued Ricci lower bound
\kappa
satisfies

|\kappa|=|\ell|\sigma_{\partial Y}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
(see
[
27
, Lemma 2.34, Theorem 2.36, Proof of Theorem 4.6]
). Then
Theorem
1.2
and Corollay
1.3
hold for
(Y,\mathscr{E}_{Y},\mathfrak{m}_{Y})
.

Example 10.2

(Configuration space over metric measure spaces)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a complete smooth Riemannian manifold without boundary.
The configuration space
\Upsilon
over
M
is the space of all locally finite point measures, that is,

\displaystyle\Upsilon:=\{\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(M)\mid\gamma(K)\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\quad\text{ for all compact sets}\quad K\subset M\}.

In the seminal paper Albeverio-Kondrachev-Röckner
[
1
]
identified a natural geometry on
\Upsilon
by lifting the geometry of
M
to
\Upsilon
. In particular, there exists a natural gradient
\nabla^{\Upsilon}
, divergence
{\rm div}^{\Upsilon}
and
Laplace operator
\Delta^{\Upsilon}
on
\Upsilon
. It is shown in
[
1
]
that the Poisson point measure
\pi
on

\Upsilon
is the unique (up to intensity) measure on
\Upsilon
under which the gradient and divergence become
dual operator in
L^{2}(\Upsilon;\pi)
. Hence, the Poisson measure
\pi
is the natural volume measure on
\Upsilon

and
\Upsilon
can be seen as an infinite dimensional Riemannian manifold. The canonical Dirichlet form

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(F)=\int_{\Upsilon}|\nabla^{\Upsilon}F|_{\gamma}^{2}\pi(\mathrm{d}\gamma)

constructed in
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
is quasi-regular and strongly local
and it induces the heat semigroup
T_{t}^{\Upsilon}
and a Brownian motion
{\bf X}^{\Upsilon}
on
\Upsilon
which can be identified with the independent infinite particle process. If
{\rm Ric}_{g}\geq K
on
M
with
K\in\mathbb{R}
, then
(\Upsilon,\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon},\pi)
is tamed by

\kappa:=K\pi
with
|\kappa|\in S_{K}({\bf X}^{\Upsilon})
(see
[
26
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
).
Then one can apply Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
for
(\Upsilon,\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon},\pi)
.

More generally, in Dello Schiavo-Suzuki
[
21
]
, configuration space
\Upsilon
over proper complete and separable metric space
(M,{\sf d})
is considered. The configuration space
\Upsilon
is endowed with the
vage topology

\tau_{V}
, induced by duality with continuous compactly supported functions on
M
, and with a reference Borel probability measure
\mu
satisfying
[
21
, Assumption 2.17]
, commonly understood as the law of a proper point process on
M
. In
[
21
]
,
they constructed the strongly local Dirichlet form
\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon}
defined to be the
L^{2}(\Upsilon;\mu)
-closure of a certain pre-Dirichlet form on a class of certain cylinder functions and prove its quasi-regularity for a wide class of measures
\mu
and base spaces (see
[
21
, Proposition 3.9 and Theorem 3.45]
). Moreover, in
Dello Schiavo-Suzuki
[
22
]
, for any fixed
K\in\mathbb{R}
they prove that a Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E},D(\mathscr{E}))
with its
carré-du-champ
\Gamma
satisfies
{\sf BE}_{2}(K,\infty)
if and only if the Dirichlet form
(\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon},D(\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon}))
on
L^{2}(\Upsilon;\mu)
with its carré-du-champ
\Gamma^{\Upsilon}
satisfies
{\sf BE}_{2}(K,\infty)
.
Hence, if
(M,\mathscr{E},\mathfrak{m})
is tamed by
K\mathfrak{m}
with
|K|\mathfrak{m}\in S_{K}({\bf X})
, then
(\Upsilon,\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon},\mu)
is tamed by
\kappa:=K\mu
with
|\kappa|\in S_{K}({\bf X}^{\Upsilon})
.
Then Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.3
hold for
(\Upsilon,\mathscr{E}^{\Upsilon},\mu)
under the suitable class of measures
\mu
defined in
[
21
, Assumption 2.17]
.

Prime Hasse principles via Diophantine second moments
Victor Y. Wang

Abstract
. We show that almost all primes p ≢ ± 4 mod 9 p\not\equiv\pm 4\bmod{9} are sums of three cubes, assuming a conjecture due to Hooley, Manin, et al. on cubic fourfolds. This conjecture is approachable under standard statistical hypotheses on geometric families of L L -functions.

\operatorname{Var}(X,K;d)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{a\in d\mathbb{Z}}[N_{a,\nu}(X)-s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]^{2}.

Both

\displaystyle\nu^{\otimes 2}(\bm{x})=\nu^{\otimes 2}(\bm{y},\bm{z})

\displaystyle\lVert\nu\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\bm{y}\,\nu(\bm{y})^{2}.

2.1.
Non-archimedean work

For integers

, let

S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{n\geq 1:\,\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m}\,\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})}e_{n}(uF(\bm{x})).

Then
S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)=0
unless

.
For every positive integer

, we have by (

)

\begin{split}\rho(q;d)&=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}}q^{-6}\sum_{\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})}e_{q}(aF(\bm{x}))\\

since
n\mid q\Leftrightarrow\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)\mid q
when
q\in d\mathbb{Z}
.
Reducing
\bm{x}
modulo
\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)
, we get
(for
q\in d\mathbb{Z}
)

(2.9)

\begin{split}\rho(q;d)&=\sum_{n\geq 1:\,\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)\mid q}\,\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)^{-6}\sum_{\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})}e_{n}(uF(\bm{x}))\\
&=\sum_{m\mid q}m^{-6}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d);\end{split}

this is well known when

*Lemma 2.12,
but perhaps less so for

.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2},d_{1},d_{2}\geq 1
be integers.
If
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
, then
T_{a}(m_{1}m_{2})=T_{a}(m_{1})T_{a}(m_{2})
.
If
\gcd(m_{1}d_{1},m_{2}d_{2})=1
, then
S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m_{1}m_{2};d_{1}d_{2})=S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m_{1};d_{1})S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m_{2};d_{2})
.

Proof.

It is well known that
T_{a}(m)
and
S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1)
are multiplicative in
m
;
see
[
4
]
*Lemma 2.13 for a general treatment.
A similar proof should show that
S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)
is multiplicative in pairs
(m,d)
, but we instead use double Dirichlet series.
By (
2.9
),

\sum_{q,d\geq 1:\,d\mid q}\rho(q;d)q^{-r}d^{-s}=\sum_{d,m\geq 1}m^{-6}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)d^{-s}\sum_{q\geq 1:\,m\mid q}q^{-r}=\zeta(r)\sum_{d,m\geq 1}m^{-6-r}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)d^{-s}.

The left-hand side has an Euler product (by (
1.6
) and the Chinese remainder theorem).
Multiplying by
\zeta(r)^{-1}=\prod_{p}(1-p^{-r})
gives the desired multiplicativity of
S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
m,d\geq 1
be integers.
Suppose
v_{p}(m)>v_{p}(d)
for all primes
p\mid m
.
Then
\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1)\rvert
.

Proof.

Here
\{n\geq 1:\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m\}=\{m\}
.
So by (
2.8
), the quantity
d^{2}\cdot S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)
equals

\begin{split}&d^{2}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}_{1}),F_{0}(\bm{y}_{2})}e_{m}(uF_{0}(\bm{y}_{1})-uF_{0}(\bm{y}_{2}))\\
&=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{y}_{1},\bm{y}_{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}e_{m}(uF_{0}(\bm{y}_{1})-uF_{0}(\bm{y}_{2}))\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}}e_{d}(v_{1}F_{0}(\bm{y}_{1})+v_{2}F_{0}(\bm{y}_{2}))\\
&=\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\prod_{1\leq i\leq 2}\,\biggl(\,\sum_{\bm{y}_{i}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}e_{m}((-1)^{i-1}uF_{0}(\bm{y}_{i}))e_{d}(v_{i}F_{0}(\bm{y}_{i}))\biggr).\end{split}

But for any
\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}
and
v\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\,\Bigl\lvert{\sum_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}e_{m}(\epsilon uF_{0}(\bm{y}))e_{d}(vF_{0}(\bm{y}))}\Bigr\rvert^{2}=S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1),

since the formula
u\mapsto\epsilon u+(m/d)v
defines a bijection on
(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
So Cauchy over
u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}

implies
d^{2}\cdot\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z}}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1)=d^{2}\cdot S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1)
.
This suffices.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
N,d\geq 1
be integers.
Then the following holds:

\sum_{m\in[N,2N)}m^{-6}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq d\sum_{ab\in[N,2N):\,a\mid d}b^{-6}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(b;1)\rvert\ll_{\epsilon}d^{5/3}N^{-2/3+\epsilon}.

Proof.

Write
m=ef
, where
p\mid e\Rightarrow v_{p}(e)\leq v_{p}(d)
and
p\mid f\Rightarrow v_{p}(f)>v_{p}(d)
.
Trivially,
\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(e;\gcd(d,e^{\infty}))\rvert\leq\sum_{n\mid e}\phi(n)e^{6}=e^{7}
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(f;\gcd(d,f^{\infty}))\rvert\leq\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(f;1)\rvert
.
Therefore,
\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq e^{7}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(f;1)\rvert
by Proposition
2.2
.
Since
e\mid d
, we get

\sum_{m\in[N,2N)}m^{-6}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq\sum_{e\mid d}e\sum_{f\in[N/e,2N/e)}f^{-6}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(f;1)\rvert\leq d\sum_{e\mid d}\sum_{f\in[N/e,2N/e)}f^{-6}\,\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(f;1)\rvert.

By
[
31
]
*
(t,k,\lambda)=(4,3,0)
case of Lemma 4.9 and
[
31
]
*Theorem 4.2,
the inner sum over
f
is
\ll_{\epsilon}(N/e)^{\epsilon-2/3}
.
The lemma then follows from the bound
\sum_{e\mid d}e^{2/3-\epsilon}\ll d^{2/3}
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
d,m\geq 1
be integers with
d\mid m
.
Then

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1:\,\operatorname{lcm}(n_{1},d)=m,\;\operatorname{lcm}(n_{2},d)=m}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)}{m^{6}},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1:\,\operatorname{lcm}(n_{1},d)=m,\;\operatorname{lcm}(n_{2},d)=m}\,\biggl\lvert{\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}}\biggr\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\tau(m)\sum_{rn=m:\,r\mid d}\frac{\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(n;1)\rvert}{n^{6}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to prove the lemma when

is a prime power.
So suppose
(d,m)=(p^{e},p^{f})

Case 1:
e<f
.

Then
\{n\geq 1:\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m\}=\{m\}
.
In particular,

(2.13)

\begin{split}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)&=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})}e_{m}(uF(\bm{x}))\\
&=p^{e}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{f-e}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})}e_{m}(uF(\bm{x}))\end{split}

(since

depends only on

And if
b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, then

(2.14)

\begin{split}T_{b}(m)=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}e_{m}(uF_{b}(\bm{y}))&=\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y})}e_{m}(uF_{b}(\bm{y}))\\
&=p^{e}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{f-e}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,d\mid F_{0}(\bm{y})}e_{m}(uF_{b}(\bm{y}))\end{split}

(since

whence

But
\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}e_{m}(-ub-vb)=p^{f-e}\cdot\bm{1}_{p^{f-e}\mid u+v}
.
So summing the previous display over
b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
, and then using (
2.13
), we get
\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}T_{b}(m)^{2}=p^{f}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)
.
This suffices for both (
2.11
), (
2.12
).
(For (
2.12
), note that
\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)\rvert\leq\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;1)\rvert
by Lemma
2.3
).

Case 2:
e=f
.

Then
d=m
and
\{n\geq 1:\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m\}=\{n\geq 1:n\mid m\}
.
So

(2.15)

S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)=\sum_{n\mid m}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\\
m\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})\end{subarray}}e_{n}(uF(\bm{x}))=m\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}:\\
m\mid F_{0}(\bm{y}),F_{0}(\bm{z})\end{subarray}}1.

But
d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}=0
(in (
2.11
), (
2.12
)), and

(2.16)

\begin{split}\sum_{n\mid m}\frac{T_{0}(n)}{n^{3}}&=\sum_{n\mid m}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{3}}[e_{n}(uF_{0}(\bm{y}))]\\
&=\sum_{n\mid m}\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}[e_{n}(uF_{0}(\bm{y}))]\\
&=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{E}_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}[e_{m}(uF_{0}(\bm{y}))]=m^{-2}\sum_{\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,m\mid F_{0}(\bm{y})}1.\end{split}

Upon squaring (

), dividing by
m
, and using (

), we get (
2.11
).
For (

), note that by Cauchy,

) (since

Lemma 2.7

\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}d\mathbb{Z}}T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})=0.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
d,K\geq 1
be integers.
Then

(2.21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq K}\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}d}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}d\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}(d)+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}),

(2.22)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq K}\frac{1}{(nd)^{3}}\sum_{\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/nd\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{e})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}\frac{T_{F_{0}(\bm{e})}(n)}{n^{3}}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}(d)+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}).

Proof.

If
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
are integers with
\operatorname{lcm}(n_{1},d)=\operatorname{lcm}(n_{2},d)=m
, say, then
d\mid m
and

\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}d}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}d\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}.

By Lemmas
2.7

2.5
, it follows that the left-hand side of (

\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1:\,d\mid m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq K:\\

&=\sum_{m\leq K:\,d\mid m}\frac{S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)}{m^{6}}+\sum_{m>K:\,d\mid m}\tau(m)\sum_{rn=m:\,r\mid d}\frac{O(\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(n;1)\rvert)}{n^{6}}\\
&=\mathfrak{S}(d)+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}),\end{split}

where in the final step we write
\sum_{m\leq K:\,d\mid m}=\sum_{m\geq 1:\,d\mid m}-\sum_{m>K:\,d\mid m}
, use (
2.10
) to write
\sum_{m\geq 1:\,d\mid m}S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)/m^{6}=\mathfrak{S}(d)
, and use Lemma
2.4
to bound the
m>K
contributions.

The second part, (
2.22
), is similar, but simpler.
If
n\geq 1
and
\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m
, say, then

\frac{1}{(nd)^{3}}\sum_{\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/nd\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{e})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}\frac{T_{F_{0}(\bm{e})}(n)}{n^{3}}=\frac{1}{m^{3}}\sum_{\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{e})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}\frac{T_{F_{0}(\bm{e})}(n)}{n^{3}}.

By Lemma
2.6
, the left-hand side of (
2.22
) thus equals

\begin{split}&\sum_{m\geq 1:\,d\mid m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq K:\\
\operatorname{lcm}(n,d)=m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m^{3}}\sum_{\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{e})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}\frac{T_{F_{0}(\bm{e})}(n)}{n^{3}}\\
&=\sum_{m\leq K:\,d\mid m}\frac{S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(m;d)}{m^{6}}+\sum_{m>K:\,d\mid m}d\cdot\tau(m)\sum_{rn=m:\,r\mid d}\frac{O(\lvert S^{+}_{\bm{0}}(n;1)\rvert)}{n^{6}}\\
&=\mathfrak{S}(d)+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}),\end{split}

where in the last step we again use (
2.10
) and Lemma
2.4
.
This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Archimedean work

Let
\nu\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and suppose
\bm{0}\notin\operatorname{Supp}\nu
.
Given
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
(\bm{y},a)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
,
write
\tilde{\bm{y}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bm{y}/X
and
\tilde{a}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}a/X^{3}
.
By (
2.2
) (after replacing
\epsilon
with
X^{3}\epsilon
),

(2.23)

\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{(2\epsilon)^{-1}\int_{\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\,\lvert F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})-\tilde{a}\rvert\leq\epsilon}d\tilde{\bm{y}}\,\nu(\tilde{\bm{y}})=\sigma_{\infty,\tilde{a},\nu}(1)}.

For convenience
(when working with
\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)
),
we now observe the following:

Observation 2.9
.

Suppose
\lvert y_{1}\rvert\geq\delta>0
for all
\bm{y}\in\operatorname{Supp}\nu
.
Let
(a,X)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
Then a change of variables in (
2.23
) from
\tilde{y}_{1}
to

F_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})
gives (by
[
7
]
*§5.4, par. 4)

(2.24)

\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,\nu(\tilde{\bm{y}})\cdot\lvert\partial F_{0}/\partial\tilde{y}_{1}\rvert^{-1},

where
F_{0},\tilde{y}_{1}
are constrained by the equation
F_{0}=\tilde{a}
(and thus determined by
\tilde{y}_{1},\tilde{y}_{2}
),
and where
\partial F_{0}/\partial\tilde{y}_{1}=3\tilde{y}_{1}^{2}\geq 3\delta^{2}>0
over the support of the integrand.

At least in the absence of better surface coordinates,
the definition (
2.2
) (via “
\epsilon
-thickening”) still provides greater intuition,
while the surface integral allows for effortless rigor.
The area form
d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,\lvert\partial F_{0}/\partial\tilde{y}_{1}\rvert^{-1}
in (
2.24
) is often called a
Leray form
, as in
[
28
]
.

We now prove three results on real densities.
For technical convenience, we assume for the rest of §
2
that
\nu
is clean (see (
1.11
)).
Let
B(\nu)
and
\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}
be as in §
1.1
.

Proposition 2.10
.

For integers
k\geq 0
,
we have
\partial_{\tilde{a}}^{k}[\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]\ll_{k}\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}B(\nu)^{A_{0}+A_{1}k}
(for some constants
A_{0},A_{1}\in[1,10]
), uniformly over
(a,X)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

Proof.

The integrand on the right in (
2.24
) vanishes unless
\tilde{a}\ll B(\nu)^{3}
and
\tilde{y}_{1}^{-1},\tilde{y}_{2},\tilde{y}_{3}\ll B(\nu)
.
Fix
\tilde{y}_{2},\tilde{y}_{3}
,
and let
\tilde{y}_{1}
vary with
\tilde{a}
according to
F_{0}=\tilde{a}
.
Then
\partial_{\tilde{a}}[\tilde{y}_{1}]=(3\tilde{y}_{1}^{2})^{-1}\ll B(\nu)^{2}
.
Now repeatedly apply
\partial_{\tilde{a}}
to the integrand
(using Leibniz and the chain rule).
This gives
\partial_{\tilde{a}}^{k}[\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]\ll_{k}\int_{\tilde{y}_{2},\tilde{y}_{3}\ll B(\nu)}d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}B(\nu)^{2k}/\lvert\tilde{y}_{1}\rvert^{2+k}\ll_{k}\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}B(\nu)^{4+3k}
.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.
The “pure
L^{2}
moment”

\int_{a\in\mathbb{R}}d\tilde{a}\,\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}

and the “mixed
L^{1}
moment”

\int_{\tilde{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\tilde{\bm{z}}\,\nu(\tilde{\bm{z}})\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)

both equal
\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}
(defined via (
1.7
)).

Proof.

First,

\int_{a\in\mathbb{R}}d\tilde{a}\,\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}
expands (via (
2.24
) and the relation
\tilde{a}=F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})
) to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,d\tilde{z}_{2}\,d\tilde{z}_{3}\int_{\tilde{y}_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}dF_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})\,\frac{\nu(\tilde{\bm{y}})\nu(\tilde{\bm{z}})}{\partial_{\tilde{y}_{1}}F_{0}|_{\tilde{\bm{y}}}}(\partial_{\tilde{z}_{1}}F_{0}|_{\tilde{\bm{z}}})^{-1}|_{F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})=F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{z}})},

which simplifies to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{5}}d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,d\tilde{z}_{2}\,d\tilde{z}_{3}\,d\tilde{y}_{1}\,\nu^{\otimes 2}(\tilde{\bm{x}})\cdot(\partial_{\tilde{z}_{1}}F_{0}|_{\tilde{\bm{z}}})^{-1}|_{F(\tilde{\bm{x}})=0}
(by (
2.6
)),
which equals
\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}

by
[
7
]
*§5.4, par. 4.
Second,

F_{0}(\bm{z})/X^{3}=F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{z}})
,
so by (
2.24
),

\int_{\tilde{\bm{z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\tilde{\bm{z}}\,\nu(\tilde{\bm{z}})\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}d\tilde{\bm{z}}\,d\tilde{y}_{2}\,d\tilde{y}_{3}\,\nu(\tilde{\bm{y}})\nu(\tilde{\bm{z}})\cdot(\partial_{\tilde{y}_{1}}F_{0}|_{\tilde{\bm{y}}})^{-1}|_{F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{y}})=F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{z}})},

which again simplifies to
\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X,N\geq 1
be integers.
Let
b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
and
\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{3}
.
Then for integers
j\geq 4
, we have (for some constants
A_{2},\dots,A_{5}\in[1,30]
)

(2.25)

\displaystyle\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}:\,a\equiv b\bmod{N}}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{3}\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}}{N}+\frac{O_{j}(\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{2}+A_{3}j})}{(X^{3}/N)^{j-1}},

(2.26)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\,\bm{z}\equiv\bm{e}\bmod{N}}\nu(\bm{z}/X)\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{3}\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}}{N^{3}}+\frac{O_{j}(\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{4}+A_{5}j})}{(X/N)^{j-3}},

(2.27)

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\,\bm{z}\equiv\bm{e}\bmod{N}}\nu(\bm{z}/X)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{X^{3}\lVert\nu\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}}{N^{3}}+\frac{O_{j}(\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{3})}{(X/N)^{j-3}}.

Proof.

We want to replace certain sums with integrals.
This is loosely analogous to
[
11
]
*proof of Lemma 3.1,
but we can get better error terms using the smoothness of
\nu
.
The error will depend polynomially on the size and support of
\nu
.
We do not optimize the exponents of
B(\nu)
above, since they are ultimately not so important.

The method is standard.
Poisson summation,
together with Proposition
2.11
,
gives

\sum_{a\equiv b\bmod{N}}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}=\frac{X^{3}\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}}{N}+\sum_{c\neq 0}\frac{O(1)}{N}\left\lvert{\int_{a\in\mathbb{R}}da\,\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}e(-c\cdot a/N)}\right\rvert.

We plug in
a=X^{3}\tilde{a}
,
integrate by parts
j\geq 2
times in
\tilde{a}
,
and invoke Proposition
2.10
,
to get

\frac{1}{N}\left\lvert{\int_{a\in\mathbb{R}}da\,\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}e(-c\cdot a/N)}\right\rvert\ll_{j}\frac{\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{2}+A_{3}j}}{\lvert c\rvert^{j}(X^{3}/N)^{j-1}}\quad(A_{2}=3+2A_{0},\;A_{3}=A_{1}).

(Note that
\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)=0
unless
\tilde{a}\ll B(\nu)^{3}
.)
The estimate (
2.25
) follows.

The estimate (
2.26
) similar.
Let
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
Choose
i\in\{1,2,3\}
with
\lvert c_{i}\rvert=\lVert\bm{c}\rVert
.
Integrating by parts
j\geq 4
times in
\tilde{z}_{i}=z_{i}/X
gives
(via Proposition
2.10
with
a=F_{0}(\bm{z})
)

\frac{1}{N^{3}}\left\lvert{\int_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\bm{z}\,\nu(\bm{z}/X)\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)e(-\bm{c}\cdot\bm{z}/N)}\right\rvert\ll_{j}\frac{\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{4}+A_{5}j}}{\lVert\bm{c}\rVert^{j}(X/N)^{j-3}},

with
A_{4}=3+A_{0}
,
A_{5}=A_{1}+2
; this is because a factor of
\partial_{\tilde{z}_{i}}F_{0}(\tilde{\bm{z}})=3\tilde{z}_{i}^{2}\ll B(\nu)^{2}
appears each time we differentiate
\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)
, by the chain rule.
(Note that
\nu(\bm{z}/X)=0
unless
\tilde{\bm{z}}\ll B(\nu)
.)
Poisson summation and Proposition
2.11
then give (
2.26
).

Finally, (
2.27
) is similar to (
2.26
), but easier; integrating by parts in
\tilde{z}_{i}=z_{i}/X
gives

\frac{1}{N^{3}}\left\lvert{\int_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}d\bm{z}\,\nu(\bm{z}/X)^{2}e(-\bm{c}\cdot\bm{z}/N)}\right\rvert\ll_{j}\frac{\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{3}}{\lVert\bm{c}\rVert^{j}(X/N)^{j-3}},

for
j\geq 4
.
Poisson summation and (
2.7
) then give (
2.27
).
∎

2.3.
Final calculations

We are finally ready to establish the main results of §
2
, which concerns the variance defined in (
2.5
).

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
\nu\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfy (
1.11
).
Let
X,K,d\geq 1
be integers with
Kd\leq X^{9/10}
.
Then

\operatorname{Var}(X,K;d)=[N_{\nu^{\otimes 2}}(X;d)-\mathfrak{S}(d)\cdot\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}X^{3}]+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}X^{3}\lVert\nu\rVert_{100,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{5000}).

Proof.

Squaring out (
2.5
) yields
\operatorname{Var}(X,K;d)=\Sigma_{1}-2\Sigma_{2}+\Sigma_{3}
, where

\Sigma_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{a\in d\mathbb{Z}}N_{a,\nu}(X)^{2},\quad\Sigma_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{a\in d\mathbb{Z}}N_{a,\nu}(X)s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X),\quad\Sigma_{3}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{a\in d\mathbb{Z}}[s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]^{2}.

Plugging in (
1.12
) gives
\Sigma_{1}=N_{\nu^{\otimes 2}}(X;d)
(by (
1.3
), (
1.5
), and (
2.6
)).
Next, write

\Sigma_{3}=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq K}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}d\mathbb{Z}}(n_{1}n_{2})^{-3}T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}:\,a\equiv b\bmod{n_{1}n_{2}d}}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)^{2}

by plugging in (
2.4
) and then grouping terms by

and write

\begin{split}\Sigma_{2}&=\sum_{\bm{z}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{z})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}\nu(\bm{z}/X)s_{F_{0}(\bm{z})}(K)\sigma_{\infty,F_{0}(\bm{z}),\nu}(X)\\

by expanding
N_{a,\nu}(X)
, plugging in (
2.4
), and grouping terms by
\bm{z}\bmod{nd}
.
Then by Proposition
2.12
and the trivial bound
\lvert T_{b}(n)\rvert\leq n^{4}
, we have (for
j\geq 4
)

\Sigma_{3}-\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq K}\sum_{b\in d\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}d\mathbb{Z}}(n_{1}n_{2})^{-3}T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})\cdot\frac{X^{3}\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}}{n_{1}n_{2}d}\ll K^{6}\cdot\frac{O_{j}(\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{2}+A_{3}j})}{(X^{3}/K^{2}d)^{j-1}}

by (
2.25
) (with
N=n_{1}n_{2}d
), and

\Sigma_{2}-\sum_{n\leq K}\sum_{\bm{e}\in(\mathbb{Z}/nd\mathbb{Z})^{3}:\,F_{0}(\bm{e})\equiv 0\bmod{d}}n^{-3}T_{F_{0}(\bm{e})}(n)\cdot\frac{X^{3}\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}}{(nd)^{3}}\ll K^{2}(Kd)^{3}\cdot\frac{O_{j}(\lVert\nu\rVert_{j,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{A_{4}+A_{5}j})}{(X/Kd)^{j-3}}

by (
2.26
) (with
N=nd
), where
A_{2},\dots,A_{5}\leq 30
.
Upon taking
j=100
above, and plugging in (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) from Proposition
2.8
, we find that
\operatorname{Var}(X,K;d)
equals

[N_{\nu^{\otimes 2}}(X;d)-\mathfrak{S}(d)\cdot\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}X^{3}]+O_{\epsilon}(d^{5/3}K^{-2/3+\epsilon}\cdot\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}X^{3})+O(\lVert\nu\rVert_{100,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{5000}),

since
X,K,d,B(\nu)\geq 1
and
Kd\leq X^{9/10}
(and thus

,
K^{2}d
,

are at most
X^{6}
,
X^{2}
,

, respectively).
To finish, note that
\sigma_{\infty,\nu^{\otimes 2}}\ll B(\nu)^{3}\cdot\lVert\nu\rVert_{0,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{2A_{0}}
by Propositions
2.11
and
2.10
,
and that

∎

Call a function
\nu\colon\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}

very clean
if

(2.28)

(\operatorname{Supp}{\nu})\cap\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}(y_{1}+y_{2})(y_{1}+y_{3})(y_{2}+y_{3})=0\}=\emptyset,

Theorem 2.14

Let
\xi\in\{0,1\}

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}:\,\gcd(a,P)=1}[N_{a,\nu}(X)-s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]^{2}\ll\frac{X^{3}\lVert\nu\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}}{(\log{X})^{\xi}}+\frac{X^{3}\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{k}}{\min(X^{\delta/11},K^{2/3}X^{-3\delta})}.

.

\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}:\,\gcd(a,P)=1}[N_{a,\nu}(X)-s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(X)]^{2}\leq\frac{3!X^{3}\lVert\nu\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}}{H^{\xi}}+R(P),

where

\begin{split}H&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{d<X^{\hbar}}\mu(d)^{2}\prod_{p\mid d}\frac{g(p)}{1-g(p)}\geq 1,\\
R(P)&\ll_{\epsilon}\sum_{d\leq X^{2\xi\hbar}}d^{\epsilon}\lvert r_{d}\rvert\ll X^{\epsilon}(X^{3-\delta/10}+X^{3+2.9\delta}K^{-2/3})\cdot\lVert\nu\rVert_{k,\infty}^{2}B(\nu)^{k}.\end{split}

(We use (
2.33
) to bound
r_{d}
.)
The Hasse bound for elliptic curves gives
g(p)=p^{-1}+O(p^{-3/2})
;
so
\sum_{p\leq x}g(p)\log{p}=\log{x}+O(1)
(for
x\geq 1
) and
\sum_{p>1}g(p)^{2}\log{p}<\infty
.
By
[
24
]
*§6.6, derivation of (6.85) using Theorem 1.1, then,
H\asymp\log(X^{\hbar})
.
Theorem
2.14
follows.
∎

3.
Statistics of truncated singular series

For integers
K\geq 1
, recall the definition of
s_{a}(K)
from (

).
In this section, we will prove that
s_{a}(K)
is typically sizable (see Theorem
3.9
below).
The proof makes use of an “approximate inverse” of
s_{a}(K)=\sum_{n\leq K}n^{-3}T_{a}(n)
.
There is some flexibility here; we let

(3.1)

M_{a}(K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{n\leq K:\,\gcd(n,30)=1}\mu(n)n^{-3}T_{a}(n).

The strategy for Theorem
3.9
is to show that
s_{a}(K)M_{a}(K)
is typically sizable, and that
M_{a}(K)
is typically bounded.
For analytic convenience, let
T^{\natural}_{a}(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}n^{-2}T_{a}(n)
.

For integers
a
and primes
p\nmid 3a
, Propositions
3.2
and
3.3
below imply
T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\ll 1
and
T_{a}(p^{\geq 2})=0
.
Indeed, if
a\neq 0
, then
s_{a}(K)
resembles the value of an
L
-function at
1
,
and in this sense Theorem
3.9
is related to work such as
[
15
]
.
But there are also significant issues with this comparison, because
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(n)\rvert
can be as large as
n
or so.
(Trivially
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(n)\rvert\leq n^{2}
.)
The most serious issues arise when

has a large cube divisor.

For an integer
n\geq 1
,
let
\operatorname{sq}(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p^{2}\mid n}p^{v_{p}(n)}
denote the

of

,
and
\operatorname{cub}(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\prod_{p^{3}\mid n}p^{v_{p}(n)}
the
cube-full part
of
n
.
Let
\mathcal{S}(D)
be the set of nonzero integers
a
with
\operatorname{sq}(\lvert a\rvert)\leq D
.
Let
\mathcal{C}(D)
be the set of nonzero integers
a
with
\operatorname{cub}(\lvert a\rvert)\leq D
.
We call an integer
n\geq 1

square-full
if
n=\operatorname{sq}(n)
,
and
cube-full
if

.

We now collect some basic facts about
T_{a}(n)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
n\geq 1
.
Let
n_{3}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{cub}(n)
.
Then
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(n)\rvert\leq O(1)^{\omega(n)}(n_{3}n)^{1/2}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to prove the lemma when
n=p^{l}
for some prime
p
and integer
l\geq 1
.
But by (
2.3
) and
[
31
]
*(4.24) and Lemma 4.7, we have

p^{3\lfloor(l-1)/3\rfloor}\cdot p^{-3l}T_{a}(p^{l})\ll p^{l-3/2}\bm{1}_{3\mid l-1}+p^{l-3}\bm{1}_{3\nmid l-1}.

Thus
T^{\natural}_{a}(p^{l})\ll p^{l-1/2}\bm{1}_{3\mid l-1}+p^{l-1}\bm{1}_{3\mid l-2}+p^{l}\bm{1}_{3\mid l}\ll p^{l/2}\cdot\bm{1}_{l\in\{1,2\}}+p^{l}\cdot\bm{1}_{l\geq 3}
.
(There should be more robust proofs, but since
F_{0}
is diagonal, it is convenient to call on
[
31
]
.)
∎

For the proofs of the next two results, let
N_{a}(m)
denote the number of solutions
\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}
to
F_{0}(\bm{y})\equiv a\bmod{m}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let
p
be a prime.
Then the following hold:

(1)

If
p\nmid 3a
, then
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 6+2p^{-1/2}
.

(2)

If
p\mid 3a
, then
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 6p^{1/2}
.

(3)

If
p\geq 7
, then
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert<0.99p
.

Proof.

By (
2.3
), we have

(3.2)

T_{a}(p)=pN_{a}(p)-p^{3}.

Case 1:
p\nmid 3a
.

Then the equation
F_{0}(\bm{y})=aw^{3}
cuts out a smooth cubic hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
The “curve at infinity” (cut out by
w=0
), namely
F_{0}(\bm{y})=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, is also smooth.
So by the Weil conjectures,
N_{a}(p)=(p^{2}+p+1+\lambda_{1})-(p+1-\lambda_{2})
, where
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(a;p)
and
\lambda_{2}=\lambda_{2}(p)
are integers with
\lvert\lambda_{1}\rvert\leq 6p
and
\lvert\lambda_{2}\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{p}
.
Thus
\lvert T_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 6p^{2}+2p^{3/2}
, and
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 6+2p^{-1/2}
.
So (1)–(3) hold (with (2) being vacuous).

Case 2:
p=3
.

Then
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert\leq p^{2}
trivially.
(In fact,
T_{a}(p)=0
, but we do not need this special fact.)
So (1)–(3) hold (with (1) and (3) being vacuous).

Case 2:
p\mid 3a
and
p\neq 3
.

Then
p\mid a
.
So
T_{a}(p)=T_{0}(p)
.
But
F_{0}(\bm{y})=0
in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
is an affine cone over a curve.
This curve,
F_{0}(\bm{y})=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, is smooth.
So by the Weil conjectures,
N_{0}(p)=1+(p-1)(p+1-\lambda_{2}(p))
, where
\lvert\lambda_{2}(p)\rvert\leq 2\sqrt{p}
.
Thus
\lvert T_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 2p(p-1)\sqrt{p}
, and
\lvert T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\rvert\leq 2(p^{1/2}-p^{-1/2})
.
So (1)–(3) hold (with (1) being vacuous).
∎

We now strengthen the vanishing result in

]
*Lemma 4.7.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be a prime.
Let
a,l\in\mathbb{Z}
with

.
If
p^{l-1}\nmid 3a
, then
T_{a}(p^{l})=0
.

Proof when
p\neq 3
.

By (

), we have

(3.3)

T_{a}(p^{l})=p^{l}N_{a}(p^{l})-p^{l-1}p^{3}N_{a}(p^{l-1}).

Let
N^{0}_{a}(p^{v})
(for
v\geq 1
) denote the number of solutions
\bm{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{v}\mathbb{Z})^{3}
to

.
(Here

Then by (
3.3
) and Hensel’s lemma,

(3.4)

T_{a}(p^{l})=p^{l}N^{0}_{a}(p^{l})-p^{l-1}p^{3}N^{0}_{a}(p^{l-1}).

Case 1:

Suppose

.
Then

).

Case 2:

.

(3.5)

Case 1:

Suppose

.

Case 2:

Suppose

But

Noting that

Now recall (

For every

(3.6)

s_{a}(K)M_{a}(K)=\sum_{n\leq K}c_{a}(n)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}\leq K:\\

By Proposition

,

is multiplicative in

.
For any prime

c_{a}(p^{l})=p^{-3l}[T_{a}(p^{l})-T_{a}(p^{l-1})T_{a}(p)\bm{1}_{p\geq 7}]=p^{-l}[T^{\natural}_{a}(p^{l})-T^{\natural}_{a}(p^{l-1})T^{\natural}_{a}(p)\bm{1}_{p\geq 7}].

We now bound
c_{a}(n)
using our work above (for convenience;

[

]
*Lemma 4.7, a “stratification” result for

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
a,n\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a\neq 0
and

.
Let
a_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{sq}(\lvert a\rvert)

Then

is a square, and

\lvert c_{a}(n)\rvert\leq O(1)^{\omega(n)}n_{1}^{-2}\cdot n_{2}^{-1}\gcd(n_{2}^{1/2},3a)\cdot n_{3}^{-1/2}\prod_{p\mid n_{3}:\,v_{p}(n_{3})\leq 2v_{p}(9a_{2})}p^{v_{p}(n_{3})/2}.

Since

.
As for (

Case 1:

In general, the series

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

with
a\neq 0
and

.
Suppose

.

(1)

For

\gamma_{p}(a)>0
.

(2)

If

, then
\gamma_{p}(a)\geq(1-p^{-2})^{O(1)}
.

p\mid 3a
, then
\gamma_{p}(a)\geq(1-p^{-1})^{O(1)}
.

(3)

\prod_{\textnormal{$p$ prime}}\gamma_{p}(a)
converges absolutely to a real number

.

(4)

If

, then
\sum_{n\leq K}c_{a}(n)=\gamma(a)+O_{\epsilon}(\lvert a\rvert^{\epsilon}K^{\epsilon-1/6}D)

Proof.

By (

), Proposition
3.3
, and (
2.1
), the sum
\sum_{j\geq 0}c_{a}(p^{j})
converges
absolutely
to

(3.9)

\gamma_{p}(a)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sigma_{p,a}\cdot(1-p^{-3}T_{a}(p)\bm{1}_{p\geq 7}),

.

by (

).

Now we bound
\gamma_{p}(a)
from below.
First suppose

.
By
[
4
]

\sigma_{p,a}\geq p^{-6}\gg 1
.
So

) and (

Let

\sum_{n>K}\lvert c_{a}(n)\rvert\ll_{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},m_{2},n_{3}\geq 1:\\

5.
Nonnegative cubes

Let
A\geq 2
.
By the Selberg sieve,
\sum_{p\leq A}r_{3}(p)\ll A/\log{A}
.
Assuming something like (
1.10
) (ideally for arbitrarily small
\delta>0
), can one prove
\sum_{p\leq A}r_{3}(p)\gg A/\log{A}
?
Something like (
1.10
) might let one handle certain “Type II” sums.
The main difficulty might instead lie in “Type I
j
” estimates (roughly corresponding to counting solutions to
dn_{1}n_{2}\cdots n_{j}=x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}
for
j
large, where
d
is fixed).
One may be able to handle “Type I
j
” sums for
j\leq 2
using the methods of
[
18
]
, but it would be nice to treat larger
j
, even conditionally.

Or, assuming precise asymptotic second moments for
r_{3}(a)
over
\{a\leq A:a\equiv 0\bmod{d}\}
for
d\leq A^{\delta}
,
can one show that
\sum_{p\leq A}r_{3}(p)^{2}\ll A/\log{A}
?
Note the lack of exact multiplicative structure in
d
in the expected main term over
\{a\leq A:a\equiv 0\bmod{d}\}
.
(For
d=1
, see
[
19
]
*Conjecture 2.)
This may or may not be a serious obstacle.

Finally, in the conditional sense above, can one show that a positive proportion of primes
p
have
r_{3}(p)\neq 0
(i.e. are sums of three nonnegative cubes)?
Another direction, suggested by discussion with Christian Bernert and Damaris Schindler, would be to find a sequence of arithmetic progressions
P_{i}
along which
\{a\in P_{i}:r_{3}(a)\neq 0\}
has relative density
\to 1
.

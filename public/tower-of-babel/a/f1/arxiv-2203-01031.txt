Residual categories of quadric surface bundles
Fei Xie

Abstract
. We show that the residual categories of quadric surface bundles are equivalent to the (twisted) derived categories of some scheme under the following hypotheses. Case 1: The quadric surface bundle has a smooth section. Case 2: The total space of the quadric surface bundle is smooth and the base is a smooth surface. We provide two proofs in Case 1 describing the scheme as the hyperbolic reduction and as a subscheme of the relative Hilbert scheme of lines, respectively. In Case 2, the twisted scheme is obtained by performing birational transformations to the relative Hilbert scheme of lines. Finally, we apply the results to certain complete intersections of quadrics.

1.
Introduction

For a flat family
f\colon X\to S
of Fano varieties of index
n
, there is a semiorthogonal decomposition (SOD)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},f^{*}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),\dots,f^{*}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(n)\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is called the
residual category
of
X
. In other words, the residual category
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is the non-trivial component in the derived category. We can view
\mathcal{A}_{X}
as a refined invariant of
X
. For example, the refined derived Torelli problem asks if
X
is determined by
\mathcal{A}_{X}
. It is interesting to see when
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is (twisted) geometric, i.e., equivalent to the (twisted) derived category of some scheme. In this paper, we study the problem when
f
is a flat quadric surface bundle allowing fibers of corank
2
and prove that their residual categories are (twisted) geometric in two cases.

Let
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
be a flat quadric surface bundle where
S
is an integral noetherian scheme over a field
\Bbbk
with
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)\neq 2
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
be the residual category of
p
. We start with Case 1. Assume that
p
has a smooth section as in Definition
2.1
and the second degeneration
S_{2}\subset S
is different from
S
(The
k
-th degeneration
S_{k}
is the locus in
S
where fibers of
p
have corank at least
k
.). In this case,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is geometric.

Theorem
4.2
.

In the hypotheses of Case 1,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\bar{\mathcal{Q}})
where
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
is the hyperbolic reduction of
\mathcal{Q}
with respect to the smooth section in Definition
2.2
.

The hyperbolic reduction
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
is isomorphic to a subscheme
Z
of the relative Hilbert scheme of lines
M
over
S
parametrizing lines in the fibers of
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
that intersect the smooth section. Let
\mathbb{P}_{Z}(\mathcal{R}_{Z})\subset\mathcal{Q}\times_{S}Z
be the universal family of lines that
Z
parametrizes. Using this identification, the embedding functor
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{Q})
can be described explicitly as below.

Theorem
4.4
.

In the hypotheses of Case 1,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Z)

where
Z
is introduced above. The embedding functors
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Z)\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{Q})
are of Fourier-Mukai type with kernels
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{R}_{Z}},n\in\mathbb{Z}
where
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{R}_{Z}}
is the
n
-th spinor sheaf with respect to the isotropic subbundle
\mathcal{R}_{Z}
in Definition
2.8
.

In Case 2,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is twisted geometric.

Theorem
5.10
.

Assume
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)=0
. Let
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
be a flat quadric surface bundle where
\mathcal{Q}
is smooth and
S
is a smooth surface over
\Bbbk
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S^{+},\mathcal{A}^{+})
where
S^{+}
is the resolution of the double cover
\widetilde{S}
over
S
ramified along the (first) degeneration locus
S_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is an Azumaya algebra on
S^{+}
. In addition, the Brauer class
[\mathcal{A}^{+}]\in\operatorname{Br}(S^{+})
is trivial if and only if
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has a rational section.

When the quadric surface bundle
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has
simple degeneration
, i.e., fibers of
p
have corank at most
1
, or equivalently
S_{2}=\emptyset
, it is well-known that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is equivalent to a twisted derived category of the double cover
\widetilde{S}
over
S
ramified along
S_{1}
. Furthermore, the twist on
\widetilde{S}
is closely related to the relative Hilbert scheme of lines
M
. More precisely,
\rho\colon M\to S
factors as a smooth conic bundle
\tau\colon M\to\widetilde{S}
followed by the double cover
\alpha\colon\widetilde{S}\to S
. The twist on
\widetilde{S}
is given by the Azumaya algebra corresponding to
\tau
. This correspondence is a relative version of that between central simple algebras and Severi-Brauer varieties (Theorem 2.4.3, 5.2.1 in
[
10
]
).

When
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has fibers of corank
2
,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
becomes more complicated and this paper focuses on this case. Having fibers of corank
2
gives two challenges. On one hand, the singular locus of a quadric surface
Q
of corank
2
over an algebraically closed field is not isolated (isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
). This means that
\mathcal{A}_{Q}
would be “
1
-dimensional” because it
absorbs the singularity
of
Q
(Its semiorthogonal complement in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Q)
is an exceltional collection.). On the other hand, the Hilbert scheme of lines on
Q
is the union of two
\mathbb{P}^{2}
’s intersecting at a point, which is reducible and has higher dimension than cases for quadric surfaces of corank at most
1
.

Conjecture 1
.

If a quadric surface bundle
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has simple degeneration generically and each fiber has corank at most
2
, i.e.,
S_{2}\neq S
and
S_{3}=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y,\alpha_{Y})
where
(Y,\alpha_{Y})
is some twisted scheme.

We expect the conjecture to be true because étale locally
p
has a smooth section (Case 1). More specifically, we expect that
Y
is a double cover over
S\backslash S_{1}
and a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
S_{2}
. This is supported by Theorem
4.2
,
4.4
and Theorem
5.10
in the paper. So far we do not have a natural way to construct such
Y
in general. But in the two cases considered in the paper, we can make such constructions. In the remaining part of the introduction, we will describe
Y
more explicitly and give an overview of the techniques used in the paper.

In Case 1 where
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
is assumed to have a smooth section,
Y
can be constructed as the hyperbolic reduction
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
with respect to the smooth section. The relative linear projection of
\mathcal{Q}
from the smooth section identifies the blow-up of
\mathcal{Q}
along the smooth section with a blow-up along
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
; see the diagram (
4.5
). The condition
S_{2}\neq S
ensures that the blow-up center
\bar{Q}
is a regular embedding of codimension
2
, and then we can apply the blow-up formula
[
12
, Theorem 6.11]
. Theorem
4.2
follows from performing mutations under this identification. This proof is straightforward, but it has the disadvantage that the information on the embedding functor
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{Q})
is lost under mutations. This is why we provide a second proof in Theorem
4.4
and focus on working with
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
by itself.

In the second proof of Case 1, we make use of the isomorphism from
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}
to the scheme
Z
over
S
parametrizing lines in the fibers of
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
that intersect the smooth section. It has been shown in
[
14
,
3
]
that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S,\mathcal{B}_{0})
, the derived category of coherent sheaves on
S
with right
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module structures, where
\mathcal{B}_{0}
is the even Clifford algebra of
p
. We prove in Proposition
4.3
that
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S,\mathcal{B}_{0})\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Z,\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptnd(\mathcal{I}_{0}^{\mathcal{R}_{Z}}))
where
\mathcal{I}_{0}^{\mathcal{R}_{Z}}
is certain Clifford ideal introduced in Section
2.2
. Finally, we use Morita equivalence to deduce
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Z)
. The embedding functors
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Z)\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{Q})
can be described explicitly because each functor involved can be described so. This approach relies on the study of derived categories of non-commutative schemes and we provide the necessary foundations in Appendix
A
.

In Case 2 where we assume
\mathcal{Q}
is smooth and
S
is a smooth surface,
Y
can be constructed as the resolution
S^{+}
of the double cover
\widetilde{S}
over
S
. The idea is to make use of the relation between the relative Hilbert scheme of lines
M
and the residual category
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
when
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has simple degeneration. Although this relation fails for quadric surfaces of corank
2
, which means that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
described in terms of the map
\tau\colon M\to\widetilde{S}
is no longer a twisted derived category, we can fix this by making a modification to
\tau
. Namely, we will construct a smooth conic bundle
\tau_{+}\colon M^{+}\to S^{+}
from
\tau
such that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S^{+},\mathcal{A}^{+})
where
\mathcal{A}^{+}
is Brauer equivalent to the Azumaya algebra corresponding to
\tau_{+}
. This construction is motivated by the work
[
17
]
, in which the base
S
is a smooth
3
-fold. We will not need the additional assumptions of
[
17
]
on the degeneration loci
S_{k}
.

Now we give a more detailed description of the modification. We will assume
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed and
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)=0
so that we can perform birational transformations to
M
. In this case, there are only a finite number of fibers with corank
2
by Lemma
5.1
. As pointed out before, the fiber
M_{s},s\in S_{2}
is a union of two
\mathbb{P}^{2}
’s. For each
M_{s}
, we will blow up one of the
\mathbb{P}^{2}
’s and contract the exceptional locus onto
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In this process,
M_{s}
becomes a Hirzebruch surface
M^{+}_{s}
, and
M^{+}\to\widetilde{S}
factor as a smooth conic bundle
\tau_{+}\colon M^{+}\to S^{+}
followed by the resolution
S^{+}\to\widetilde{S}
. Details of this process are given in Proposition
5.5
. It should be pointed out that this geometric construction relies on being able to choose one of the two
\mathbb{P}^{2}
’s for each
M_{s},s\in S_{2}
. Hence, it works well when
S_{2}
is a finite set of points, but it would not work in general.

The main results of the paper can be used to reprove results on semiorthogonal decompositions for the nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds (Example
6.1
) given in
[
24
]
and cubic
4
-folds containing a plane in non-generic cases (Example
6.2
). Since the residual category of a Fano complete intersection of quadrics is equivalent to that of the associated net of quadrics by the Homological Projective Duality theory, we can produce new examples of Fano complete intersections of quadrics whose residual categories are twisted geometric. For example, residual categories of smooth complete intersections of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{2m+3}
for
m\leqslant 5
are twisted geometric; see Proposition
6.5
.

2.
Quadric bundles, Clifford algebras and ideals

In this section, we recall some notions of quadric bundles, review definitions of Clifford algebras and introduce Clifford ideals.

2.1.
Some basic notions of quadric bundles

Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle and
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
S
. We say
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is a
(line-bundle valued) quadratic form
on
S
if
q
is an
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-homogeneous morphism of degree
2
such that the associated morphism
b_{q}\colon\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
defined by
b_{q}(v,w)=q(v+w)-q(v)-q(w)
is a symmetric bilinear form. The
rank
of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is the rank of
\mathcal{E}
.

Assume
q
is non-zero. Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E})\to S
be the projection map. Then
q
corresponds to a non-zero section

(2.1)

s_{q}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E})/S}(2)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathcal{L})\cong\Gamma(S,\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\otimes\mathcal{L}),

where
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
is the dual of
\mathcal{E}
and
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}
is the second symmetric product. Let
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E})
be the zero locus of the section
s_{q}
. We write
\mathcal{Q}=\{q=0\}
and call
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
the associated
quadric bundle
. We say that
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is
primitive
if
q
is non-zero over the residue field of every point on
S
. This is equivalent to
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
being a flat quadric bundle. In this paper, when we consider quadric bundles, we only require that
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E})
has codimension
1
and thus
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
may not be flat.

Denote the
k
-th degeneration locus
of
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
by
S_{k}\subset S
, which is the closed subscheme of
S
defined by the sheaf of ideal

\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda^{n+1-k}\mathcal{E}\otimes\Lambda^{n+1-k}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{n+1-k}\xrightarrow{\Lambda^{n+1-k}b_{q}}\mathcal{O}_{S}),

where
n=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})
. This means that
S_{k}
is the locus where fibers of
p
have corank at least
k
. The
corank
of a quadric is the corank of its associated symmetric bilinear form. In particular,
S_{1}\cong\{\det(b_{q})=0\}
is the locus of singular fibers. We say that
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
has
simple degeneration
if
S_{2}=\emptyset
.

A subbundle
\mathcal{W}
of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is isotropic if
q|_{\mathcal{W}}=0
. This is equivalent to
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W})\subset\mathcal{Q}
.

Definition 2.1
.

An isotropic subbundle
\mathcal{W}
of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is called
regular
if for each geometric point
x\in S
, the fiber
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W})_{x}
over
x
is contained in the smooth locus of the fiber
\mathcal{Q}_{x}
. We call
\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{W})
a
smooth
r
-section
of
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
if
\mathcal{W}
is a regular isotropic subbundle of
q
of rank
r+1
. A smooth
0
-section is simply called a
smooth section
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be a regular isotropic subbundle of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
. Then there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{W}^{\perp}\to\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{b_{q}|_{\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{W}}}\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom(\mathcal{W},\mathcal{L})\to 0,

where
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is the kernel of
b_{q}|_{\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{W}}
. Since
\mathcal{W}
is isotropic, we have
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{W}
is contained in the kernel of
q|_{\mathcal{W}^{\perp}}\colon\mathcal{W}^{\perp}\to\mathcal{L}
. It induces a new quadratic form
\bar{q}\colon\mathcal{W}^{\perp}/\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Denote
\bar{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{W}^{\perp}/\mathcal{W}
. The induced quadratic from
\bar{q}\colon\bar{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{L}
is called the
hyperbolic reduction
of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
with respect to the regular isotropic subbundle
\mathcal{W}
. Alternatively,
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}=\{\bar{q}=0\}
is called the
hyperbolic reduction
of
\mathcal{Q}=\{q=0\}
with respect to the smooth
r
-section
\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{W})
, where
r=\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})-1
.

The quadric bundles
\mathcal{Q}\to S
and
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}\to S
related by the hyperbolic reduction share many features. For example, they have the same degeneration loci
S_{k}
.

2.2.
Clifford algebras and ideals

Let
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
be a non-zero quadratic form and let
p\colon\mathcal{Q}=\{q=0\}\to S
be the associated quadric bundle. There are several equivalent definitions for even Clifford algebras and Clifford bimodules (also called the odd part of Clifford algebras) of
q
; see
[
7
, §3]
,
[
3
, §1.5]
,
[
14
, §3.3]
. We will recall the construction in
[
7
]
.

Set the degrees of elements of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{L}
to be
1
and
2
, respectively. The
generalized Clifford algebra
is defined by

(2.2)

\mathcal{B}=T(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{L}^{n})/\langle(v\otimes v)\otimes 1-1\otimes q(v)\rangle_{v\in\mathcal{E}},

where
T(\mathcal{E})
is the tensor algebra of
\mathcal{E}
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be the subgroups of
\mathcal{B}
consisting of elements of degree
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\mathcal{B}\cong\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{B}_{n}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra over
\mathcal{B}_{0}
. The
even Clifford algebra
and the
Clifford bimodule
are defined to be
\mathcal{B}_{0}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}
, respectively.

Write
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=2m
or
2m+1
. Then there are
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-filtrations

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{O}_{S}=F_{0}\subset F_{2}\subset\dots\subset F_{2m}=\mathcal{B}_{0},\\
&\mathcal{E}=F_{1}\subset F_{3}\subset\dots\subset F_{2m+1}=\mathcal{B}_{1}\\
\end{split}

such that
F_{2i}/F_{2i-2}\cong\Lambda^{2i}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{i}
and
F_{2i+1}/F_{2i-1}\cong\Lambda^{2i+1}\mathcal{E}\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{i}
. Moreover, we have

(2.4)

\mathcal{B}_{n}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{B}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k},&n=2k\\
\mathcal{B}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{k},&n=2k+1\\
\end{array}\right..

When
q
is primitive, the Clifford multiplications

(2.5)

\mathcal{B}_{n}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{B}_{m}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{B}_{n+m}

are isomorphisms for
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is an invertible
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-bimodule.

Note that if
\mathcal{W}
is an isotropic subbundle, then
\bigoplus_{n}\Lambda^{n}\mathcal{W}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}
. We define the associated Clifford ideals below.

Definition 2.3
.

The
n
-th (left) Clifford ideal

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}
of
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
with respect to an isotropic subbundle
\mathcal{W}
is the degree
n
part of the left principal ideal of
\mathcal{B}
generated by
\det\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})}
, i.e.,

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}=\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{B}_{n-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})}\otimes\det\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}),

where the map is given by Clifford multiplications. Similarly, we can define the
n
-th right Clifford ideal

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}=\operatorname{Im}(\det\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{n-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}).

We have
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\cong\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}
as vector bundles on
S
because tensor is commutative and
\mathcal{I}_{n+2}^{\mathcal{W}}\cong\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathcal{L}
by relations (
2.4
).

The Clifford ideals play an important role in the study of quadric bundles. They have been studied for a quadric hypersurface and were used to define spinor sheaves for an isotropic subspace in
[
1
, §2]
. In this paper, we will provide a relative version.

Firstly, we provide two lemmas about Clifford ideals that will be used later. Next, we give the relation between Clifford ideals of different isotropic subbundles and describe the dual of Clifford ideals. As with the quadric hypersurface case, we can define spinor sheaves for an isotropic subbundle, and we will discuss properties of spinor sheaves that follow from those of Clifford ideals. We primarily work with left Clifford ideals and properties proved for them also apply to right Clifford ideals.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
is primitive and
\mathcal{W}
is an isotropic subbundle. Then for all
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(1) the Clifford multiplication induces a left
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module isomorphism

\sigma_{m,n}\colon\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}};

(2) there are isomorphisms of sheaves of algebras
\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptnd(\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}})\cong\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptnd(\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}})
.

A similar result is true for right Clifford ideals.

Proof.

(1) We have the map
\sigma_{m,n}
because the image of the Clifford multiplication
\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{B}_{m+n}
is
\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}}
and it factors through
\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}
. Applying
\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}-
to the map

\sigma_{-m,m+n}\colon\mathcal{B}_{-m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}

and using the isomorphism (
2.5
), we obtain the map
\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}
. This is the inverse map of
\sigma_{m,n}
.

(2) is a consequence of (1) where the morphism
\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptnd(\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}})\to\mathscr{E}\kern-1.0ptnd(\mathcal{I}_{m+n}^{\mathcal{W}})
is induced by
\mathcal{B}_{m}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}-
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an isotropic subbundle of rank
r
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there are exact sequences

\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{I}_{n+r}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\to 0

of left
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-modules, where the first map is given by the Clifford multiplication
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}
. A similar result is true for right Clifford ideals.

Proof.

There is a left
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module surjection
\mathcal{B}_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{n+r}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})
induced by the multiplication
\mathcal{B}_{n}\otimes\det(\mathcal{W})\subset\mathcal{B}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{r}\to\mathcal{B}_{n+r}
. By construction, the kernel of the surjection is given by the image of
\mathcal{B}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{W}
be isotropic subbundles and assume
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})-\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime})=1
. Let
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
. Then for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there are short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}^{\vee}\to 0

of left
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-modules. Similarly, if
\mathcal{W}
is an isotropic sub line bundle, then for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
there are short exact sequences

0\to\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\to\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\vee}\to 0.

A similar result is true for right Clifford ideals.

Proof.

Consider the map given by the Clifford multiplication

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{B}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\mathcal{B}_{n+1}.

The image is
\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{W}}
and it factors through
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}\otimes(\mathcal{W}/\mathcal{W}^{\prime})
. Furthermore, the induced map

\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{n+1}^{\mathcal{W}}

has kernel
\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{1}
. The proof when
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})=1
is similar.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an isotropic subbundle of rank
r
.

(1) If
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=2m
, then for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
there are right
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module isomorphisms

(\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\mathcal{W}})^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{I}_{r-k}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}\otimes\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\otimes\det(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{m}.

(2) If
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=2m+1
, then for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
there are right
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module isomorphisms

(\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\mathcal{W}})^{\vee}\cong\mathcal{I}_{r+1-k}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}\otimes\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\otimes\det(\mathcal{E}^{\vee})\otimes\mathcal{L}^{m}.

Proof.

(1) Let
\operatorname{tr}\colon\mathcal{B}_{0}\to\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m}
be the map
F_{2m}\to F_{2m}/F_{2m-2}
induced by the filtration (
2.3
). For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, there is a pairing

\begin{array}[]{rcccl}\mathcal{B}_{-k}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{k}&\to&\mathcal{B}_{0}&\xrightarrow{\operatorname{tr}}&\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m},\\
\xi\otimes\eta&\mapsto&\xi\eta&\mapsto&\operatorname{tr}(\xi\eta).\end{array}

When the pairing is restricted to
\mathcal{B}_{-k}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\mathcal{W}}
, it induces

f\colon\mathcal{B}_{-k}\to\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom(\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\mathcal{W}},\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m}).

It is clear from the construction that
f
is a homomorphism of right
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-modules. On the other hand, by Lemma
2.5
, we get a right
\mathcal{B}_{0}
-module surjection

g\colon\mathcal{B}_{-k}\twoheadrightarrow\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{r-k}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}.

Since
f,g
have the same kernel, we have an injection

\bar{f}\colon\det(\mathcal{W}^{\vee})\otimes\mathcal{I}_{r-k}^{\circ\mathcal{W}}\to\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom(\mathcal{I}_{k}^{\mathcal{W}},\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m}).

Note both the vector bundles above have rank

. Then
\bar{f}
is an isomorphism because it is so over the residue field of every point on
S
.

(2) The proof when
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})
is odd is similar. The only differences are that we should use
\operatorname{tr}\colon\mathcal{B}_{1}\to\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m}
and the pairing
\mathcal{B}_{1-k}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{k}\to\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes(\mathcal{L}^{\vee})^{m}
instead.
∎

Now we define spinor sheaves on quadric bundles by means of Clifford ideals. Let

be the projection map. Regard

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{S}(\mathcal{E})/S}(-1)\otimes\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}_{n-1}^{\mathcal{W}}&\xrightarrow{\phi_{n}}&\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}},\\

Then

. Taking into account that

is integral and

and

.

We can also construct

.

Let
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{W}
be isotropic subbundles and assume

Proof.

By Lemma

, there are short exact sequences

Then the result follows because these short exact sequences are compatible with the map

(

∎

Corollary 2.11
.

The spinor sheaf
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}}
is reflexive on
\mathcal{Q}
. Let
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{W})=r
. Recall

is the quadric bundle.

(1) If

(2) If

The reflexivity of

follows from the observation that taking double dual of (

(1) Taking the dual of the second sequence in (

∎

2.3.
Non-primitive quadratic forms of rank two

Given a quadric surface bundle with a smooth section, the hyperbolic reduction with respect to the smooth section as in Definition
2.2
gives a quadratic form of rank
2
. If

, then the new quadratic form is non-primitive. As a preparation for Section

, we will study possibly non-primitive quadratic forms of rank

in this section.

Let
q\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{L}
be a non-zero quadratic form of rank
2
, i.e.,
\operatorname{rank}(\mathcal{E})=2
, and let

be the associated quadric bundle. Then

, and

over

and is not flat if

requires a non-trivial argument.

We observe that

is an isotropic line bundle of

. The Clifford multiplication gives

p^{*}\mathcal{B}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{m}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}/S}(-1)}\to\mathcal{I}_{n+m}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}/S}(-1)}.

Since

is locally free, the map above induces

(2.8)

Lp^{*}\mathcal{B}_{n}\cong p^{*}\mathcal{B}_{n}\to\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}}}(\mathcal{I}_{m}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}/S}(-1)},\mathcal{I}_{n+m}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Q}/S}(-1)}).

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

be the quadric bundle associated with a non-zero quadratic form

of rank

be the projection map and let

(1) There is a short exact sequence

) is an isomorphism for all

.

Proof.

(1) Since
S
is integral,

and

, we have
\phi_{n}
is injective and an isomorphism outside of

. Moreover,

is supported on

schematically and we can write it as

and

and all

to the sequence in (1), we get the claim holds for

Now we prove for

3.
Relative Hilbert schemes of lines

In this section, we focus on describing the relative Hilbert schemes of lines of flat quadric surface bundles.

In the rest of the paper, we will use the following notations.

•

Let
p\colon\mathcal{Q}\to S
be a flat quadric surface bundle with the associated quadratic form

4.

Firstly, we provide a type of mutations for derived categories of not necessarily smooth schemes. Recall that a morphism is
perfect
if it is pseudo-coherent and has finite Tor-dimension. A proper
local complete intersection
morphism (locally factors as a Koszul-regular closed immersion followed by a smooth morphism) is perfect and it has

relative dualizing complex (a degree shift of a line bundle); see Example 3.2 in

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a proper and perfect morphism of noetherian schemes. Denote by
\omega_{f}=f^{!}(\mathcal{O}_{S})
the relative dualizing complex. Assume
\omega_{f}
is invertible, e.g., when

is a Gorenstein or a proper local complete intersection morphism. Denote by
S_{X/S}=-\otimes\omega_{f}
the equivalence functor on
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
(This is the relative Serre functor on
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
). Then
f^{!}\cong S_{X/S}\circ Lf^{*}
on
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
.

(1) Assume there is an
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

(4.1)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}\rangle

where
S
-linear means that
\mathcal{F}\otimes f^{*}\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(S)
, the derived category of perfect complexes, and
i=1,2
. Assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1}
or
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is equivalent to
Lf^{*}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)\otimes T
where
T\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
is relative exceptional, i.e.,
Rf_{*}R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(T,T)\cong\mathcal{O}_{S}
. Then both
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are admissible, i.e., inclusion functors have both left and right adjoints, and there is an
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

(4.2)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{2}\otimes\omega_{f},\mathcal{A}_{1}\rangle.

(2) More generally, assume that there is an
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle.

Assume that there exists some
i_{0}
such that for all
i\neq i_{0}
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{i}\cong Lf^{*}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)\otimes T_{i}
for relative exceptional
T_{i}\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
. Then each
\mathcal{A}_{i},1\leqslant i\leqslant n
is admissible and there is an
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

(4.3)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{f},\mathcal{A}_{1}\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\rangle.

Proof.

The claim that
f^{!}\cong S_{X/S}\circ Lf^{*}
is given in Theorem 3.1 (2) of
[
12
]
.

(1) We will make use of Lemma 2.7 in
[
16
]
, which states that
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
is semiorthogonal, i.e.,
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{1})=0
for
all

\mathcal{F}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
i=1,2
if and only if
Rf_{*}R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{F}_{2},\mathcal{F}_{1})=0
for
all

\mathcal{F}_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
i=1,2
.

We assume that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cong Lf^{*}\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)\otimes T
for
T\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
relative exceptional. The other case is similar. Recall that for a full subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
, the
right orthogonal
to
\mathcal{A}
is

\mathcal{A}^{\perp}=\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)\,|\,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F})=0,\forall\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{A}\}

and the

to
\mathcal{A}
is

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)\,|\,\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{K})=0,\forall\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{A}\}.

From the SOD (
4.1
), we get
\mathcal{A}_{2}\cong{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}\cong\mathcal{A}_{2}^{\perp}
. The existence of the SOD (

Firstly, we show

. Let
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
,
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S)

\begin{split}Rf_{*}R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(Lf^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes T,S_{X/S}(\mathcal{F}))&\cong Rf_{*}R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(Lf^{*}\mathcal{G},T^{\vee}\otimes S_{X/S}(\mathcal{F}))\\
&\cong R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(\mathcal{G},Rf_{*}(T^{\vee}\otimes S_{X/S}(\mathcal{F}))).\end{split}

Note that

is a perfect complex. The local Grothendieck-Serre duality gives

&\cong R\mathscr{H}\kern-2.0ptom_{\mathcal{O}_{S}}(Rf_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes T^{\vee}),\mathcal{G}).\end{split}

Since

p^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{k-1}
.

For smooth complete intersections of three quadrics, we have better results.

Proposition 6.5
.

Assume
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed with
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)=0
. Let
Y^{2m}
be the smooth complete intersection of three quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{2m+3}
. Assume that
Y^{2m}
contains a
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
for
m\geqslant 6
, which is automatically satisfied for
1\leqslant m\leqslant 5
. Then we have a semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y^{2m})=\langle\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S^{2m},\mathcal{A}^{2m}),\mathcal{O}_{Y^{2m}}(1),\mathcal{O}_{Y^{2m}}(2),\dots,\mathcal{O}_{Y^{2m}}(2m-2)\rangle

where
S^{2m}
is the resolution of the double cover over
\mathbb{P}^{2}
ramified along a nodal curve of degree
2m+4
and
\mathcal{A}^{2m}
is an Azumaya algebra on
S^{2m}
. Moreover,
Y^{2m}
for
m\geqslant 3
is rational and
Y^{4}
is rational when
[\mathcal{A}^{4}]\in\operatorname{Br}(S^{4})
is trivial.

Proof.

Firstly, we claim that
Y^{2m}
contains a
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
for
1\leqslant m\leqslant 5
. Let
F_{m-1}
be the Hilbert scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
’s on a quadric of dimension
2m+2
. It is the zero locus of a section in

\Gamma(\operatorname{Gr}(m,2m+4),\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\mathcal{R}_{m})

where
\mathcal{R}_{m}
is the universal subbundle on
\operatorname{Gr}(m,2m+4)
. Thus,
F_{m-1}\subset\operatorname{Gr}(m,2m+4)
has codimension at most
m(m+1)/2
. Since
3m(m+1)/2\leqslant\dim\operatorname{Gr}(m,2m+4)
when
1\leqslant m\leqslant 5
,
Y^{2m}
contains a
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
in these cases.

In previous notations,
Y^{2m}=X^{2m+2,3}
. Let
p^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{k-1}
be the hyperbolic reduction of
p^{2m+2,3}\colon\mathcal{Q}^{2m+2,3}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
with respect to the smooth
(m-1)
-section
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}\subset\mathcal{Q}^{n,k}
. Then
p^{\prime}
is a flat quadric surface bundle. Proposition 1.1 (3) in
[
19
]
deduces the Morita equivalence

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}^{2m+2,3})\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\prime})

where
\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\prime}
is the even Clifford algebra of
p^{\prime}
. From the smoothness of
Y^{2m}
, we get that
\mathcal{Q}^{n,k}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
are also smooth. In addition, the (first) degeneration locus of
q^{2m+2,3}\colon\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{2m+4}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(1)
is a curve of degree
2m+4
and the degeneration locus is preserved under hyperbolic reduction. By Lemma
5.1
, the curve is nodal along the locus where fibers of
p^{\prime}
have corank
2
. From Theorem
5.10
, we get that

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\prime})\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S^{2m},\mathcal{A}^{2m})

and
[\mathcal{A}^{2m}]\in\operatorname{Br}(S^{2m})
is trivial if and only if
p^{\prime}
has a rational section. We get the semiorthogonal decomposition from the SOD in (
6.1
).

Lastly, by Example 1.4.4 in
[
6
]
, we have that
Y^{2m},m\geqslant 2
is birational to the hyperbolic reduction
\mathcal{Q}_{l}
of
p^{2m+2,3}
with respect to a smooth
1
-section
l\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
where
l\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a line on
Y^{2m}
. It can be computed similarly that the Hilbert scheme of planes on a quadric of dimension
2m+2
has codimension at most
6
in
\operatorname{Gr}(3,2m+4)
. We get that
\mathcal{Q}_{l}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
has a section for
m\geqslant 3
. Hence,
Y^{2m}
is rational for
m\geqslant 3
. Moreover, if
m=2
and
[\mathcal{A}^{4}]=0
, then
\mathcal{Q}_{l}\cong\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
has a rational section and thus
Y^{4}
is rational.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

We expect that Conjecture
1
holds for every flat quadric bundle of relative even dimension under the same hypotheses. If it is true, then we do not need to take hyperbolic reduction so that the results of quadric surface bundles can be applied. In this case, the assumption that
Y^{2m}
contains a
\mathbb{P}^{m-1}
can be removed for getting the semiorthogonal decomposition.

Appendix A
Non-commutative schemes

In the appendix, we give an overview of derived categories of non-commutative schemes as in Definition
A.1
generalizing Appendix D in
[
21
]
and
[
24
, §2.2]
. We will discuss the relations among
\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{QCoh}},\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh})
,
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Coh})
and prove the projection formula (Proposition
A.6
).

Definition A.1
.

A pair
(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
is a
non-commutative scheme
if
X
is a noetherian scheme,
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras and a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. A morphism

H=(h,h_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})

of non-commutative schemes consists of a morphism
h\colon X\to Y
of schemes and a homomorphism
h_{\mathcal{A}}\colon h^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras.

Denote by

•

\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}}
the opposite algebra of
\mathcal{A}_{X}
,

•

\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
the category of right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules,

•

\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
the category of quasi-coherent sheaves on
X
with right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module structures,

•

\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
the category of coherent sheaves on
X
with right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module structures.

Further denote by

•

\mathbf{D},\mathbf{D}^{-},\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}
the unbounded, bounded above and bounded derived categories,

•

\mathbf{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
the derived category
\mathbf{D}^{*}(\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))
for
*=\emptyset,-,\textrm{b}
,

•

\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{QCoh}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
(resp.,
\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
) the unbounded derived category of right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules with quasi-coherent (resp., coherent) cohomologies.

There are pairs of adjoint functors

(A.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{Q}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Mod}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{M}}

where
j_{Q},j_{M}
are forgetful functors, and
-\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}
is left adjoint to
j_{Q}
and
j_{M}
. When
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a coherent sheaf, there is an additional pair of adjoint functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

where
j\colon\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\operatorname{Coh}(X)
is the forgetful functor.

Recall that the
coherator
of
X
is the functor
Q_{X}
right adjoint to the inclusion
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(X)
. For example, if
X
is affine, then
Q_{X}(\mathcal{F})
for
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}(X)
is the quasi-coherent sheaf
\widetilde{\Gamma(X,\mathcal{F})}
associated with
\Gamma(X,\mathcal{F})
. Note that
\widetilde{\Gamma(X,\mathcal{F})}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
if
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
. Thus,
Q_{X}
induces a coherator functor

(A.2)

Q_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\colon\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}),

which is right adjoint to the inclusion
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
.

Let
T
be an abelian category and let
K(T)
be its homotopy category. Recall that a complex
I^{\bullet}
of objects in
T
is called
K-injective
if
\operatorname{Hom}_{K(T)}(M^{\bullet},I^{\bullet})=0
for every acyclic complex
M^{\bullet}
. In particular, a bounded below complex of injectives is K-injective.

For a scheme
X
and a complex
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules,
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
is called
K-flat
if the complex

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\mathcal{K}^{\bullet})

is acyclic for every acyclic complex
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. In particular, a bounded above complex of flat
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is K-flat. We can define a similar notion for a non-commutative scheme
(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
. We say that a right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{K}
is
right flat
if
\mathcal{K}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}-
is an exact functor on
\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}})
and a complex
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
of right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules is
right K-flat
if the complex

\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})

is acyclic for every acyclic complex
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
of left
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules. As with before, a bounded above complex of right flat
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules is right K-flat. Replacing
\mathcal{A}_{X}
by
\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}}
, we get notions for left flat and left K-flat.

Lemma A.2
.

(1)
\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
,
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
are Grothendieck abelian categories and every complex in
\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
or
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
has a K-injective resolution.

(2) For every complex
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
of right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules, there exists a right K-flat complex
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
whose terms are right flat
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules and a quasi-isomorphism
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}\to\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
which is termwise surjective. The same is true for complexes of left
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules.

Proof.

(1)
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
is a Grothendieck abelian category. The abelian category structure, direct sums and exact filtered colimits on
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
carry over to
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
. By the adjointness (
A.1
),
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
has a generator
U\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}
where
U
is a generator of
\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
. Hence,
\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
is a Grothendieck abelian category. The existence of K-injective complexes follows from
[
23
, Tag 079P]
. The proof for
\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
is similar.

(2) We only need to prove for complexes of right
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules. The proof is a modification of
[
23
, Tag 06YF]
and it suffices to show that
\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
has enough right flat objects. We know that
\operatorname{Mod}(X)
has enough flat objects. For
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
, there is a surjection
\mathcal{F}\twoheadrightarrow j_{M}(\mathcal{G})
from a flat
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
. Then its adjoint map
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}\to\mathcal{G}
is also a surjection and
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a right flat
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-module. Now
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
can be constructed in the same way as
loc. cit.
, which is the filtered colimit of a nice sequence of bounded above complexes of right flat
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-modules.
∎

Thanks to the lemma above, it makes sense to talk about the right adjoint functor
RQ_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}
.

Lemma A.3
.

The natural functor
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{QCoh}}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
is an equivalence with quasi-inverse given by
RQ_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}
.

Proof.

Since the coherator functors
Q_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}\colon\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
and
Q_{X}\colon\operatorname{Mod}(X)\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X)
commute with forgetful functors, the claim is a consequence of
[
23
, Tag 09T4]
.
∎

Lemma A.4
.

When
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is coherent, the natural functors

\mathbf{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}^{*}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}^{*}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})

for
*=-,\textrm{b}
are equivalences.

Proof.

The equivalence of the second functor follows from Lemma
A.3
. For the first functor, we will modify the proof in
[
23
, Tag 0FDA]
. We claim that if there is a surjection
\mathcal{G}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}
for
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
and
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
, then there is some
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
that surjects onto
\mathcal{F}
. Consequently, the first functor is an equivalence by
[
23
, Tag 0FCL]
. We know
j_{Q}(\mathcal{G})
is a filtered union of coherent submodules
\mathcal{G}_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{\prime}=\operatorname{Im}(\mathcal{G}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X}\to\mathcal{G})
be the image of the map adjoint to the inclusion
\mathcal{G}_{i}\hookrightarrow j_{Q}(\mathcal{G})
. Then
\mathcal{G}
is the filtered union of coherent
\mathcal{A}_{X}
-submodules
\mathcal{G}_{i}^{\prime}
and one of them will be
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.
∎

Given a morphism
H=(h,h_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
, a
push-forward
functor is defined by

H_{*}\colon\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})

where as a quasi-coherent sheaf,
H_{*}\mathcal{F}
is given by
h_{*}\mathcal{F}
for
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
, and a right
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-module structure on
h_{*}\mathcal{F}
is induced by

(h_{*}\mathcal{F})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{Y}\cong h_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes h^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y})\xrightarrow{h_{\mathcal{A}}}h_{*}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{X})\to h_{*}\mathcal{F}.

A
pull-back
functor is defined by

H^{*}\colon\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})\to\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})

where for
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
,

H^{*}\mathcal{G}:=(h^{*}\mathcal{G})\otimes_{h^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong(h^{-1}\mathcal{G})\otimes_{h^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}\mathcal{A}_{X}.

In the lemma below, we keep the same notations
RH_{*}
,
LH^{*}
for the derived push-forward and pull-back functors induced from the original ones, respectively.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
H=(h,h_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
be a morphism between non-commutative schemes.

(1) There exists a right derived functor

RH_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})).

When
h
is proper and
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
is coherent, it induces

RH_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\mathbf{D}^{*}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})

for
*=-,\textrm{b}
.

(2) There exists a left derived functor

LH^{*}\colon\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})).

When
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is coherent, it induces

LH^{*}\colon\mathbf{D}^{-}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})\to\mathbf{D}^{-}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}).

(3)
H^{*}\dashv H_{*}
and
LH^{*}\dashv RH_{*}
are adjoint functors.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
A.2
(1), the K-injective resolutions exist for
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))
and thus the right derived functor
RH_{*}
can be defined. When
h
is proper, we have an induced functor

RH_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})).

When
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
is coherent, from Lemma
A.4
, we get the right derived functor

RH_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}^{*}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}^{*}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{Coh}}^{*}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))\cong\mathbf{D}^{*}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}).

for
*=-,\textrm{b}
.

(2) Given
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
, we define

LH^{*}\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}:=RQ_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}(H^{*}\mathcal{K}^{\bullet})\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))

where
\mathcal{K}^{\bullet}
is the right K-flat resolution of
\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}
constructed in Lemma
A.2
(2) and

is the derived coherator in Lemma
A.3
. Standard arguments show that this is well-defined.

Given
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{Coh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
, we claim that
H^{*}\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{Coh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
when
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is coherent. This is a local question. Assume that

,
(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})\cong(\operatorname{Spec}B,\widetilde{R_{B}})
and
\mathcal{G}\cong\widetilde{M}
. There is a surjection

for some

Then

is a finitely generated

is such.

Given

, we have

where the equivalence is given by Lemma
A.4
.

(3) It suffices to prove the adjointness for

. For
\mathcal{F}\in\operatorname{Mod}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
and
\mathcal{G}\in\operatorname{Mod}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}(H^{*}\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}(h^{*}\mathcal{G}\otimes_{h^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{F})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{h^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}(h^{*}\mathcal{G},\mathcal{F})

∎

Proposition A.6

(Projection formula)
.

Let
H=(h,h_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
be a morphism of non-commutative schemes in Definition
A.1
.

(1) Given
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}}))
and
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
, there is a natural map

(A.3)

\mathcal{G}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{Y}}^{\mathbb{L}}RH_{*}(\mathcal{F})\to RH_{*}(LH^{*}(\mathcal{G})\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}\mathcal{F}),

and it is an isomorphism in
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y))
.

(2) Assume that
h:X\to Y
is proper and
\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{A}_{Y}
are coherent. Given
\mathcal{F}\in\mathbf{D}^{-}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}})
and
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{-}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
, the natural map (
A.3
) is an isomorphism in
\mathbf{D}^{-}(Y)
.

Proof.

The derived functors involved are defined in Lemma
A.5
and the natural map (
A.3
) is induced by the adjointness
LH^{*}\dashv RH_{*}
. The proof of the proposition is the same as the proof of Lemma 2.5 in
[
24
]
.
∎

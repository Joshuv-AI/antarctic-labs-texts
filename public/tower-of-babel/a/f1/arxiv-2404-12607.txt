The Chow ring of the universal Picard stack over the hyperelliptic locus
Hannah Larson

Abstract
. Let 𝒥 g d → ℳ g \mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}\to\mathscr{M}_{g} be the universal Picard stack parametrizing degree d d line bundles on genus g g curves, and let 𝒥 2 , g d \mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g} be its restriction to locus of hyperelliptic curves ℋ 2 , g ⊂ ℳ g \mathscr{H}_{2,g}\subset\mathscr{M}_{g} . We determine the rational Chow ring of 𝒥 2 , g d \mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g} for all d d and g g . In particular, we prove it is generated by restrictions of tautological classes on 𝒥 g d \mathscr{J}^{d}_{g} and we determine all relations among the restrictions of such classes. We also compute the integral Picard group of 𝒥 2 , g d \mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g} , completing (and extending to the PGL 2 \PGL_{2} -equivariant case) prior work of Erman and Wood. As a corollary, we prove that 𝒥 2 , g d \mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g} is either a trivial 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} -gerbe over its rigidification, or has Brauer class of order 2 2 , depending on the parity of d − g d-g .



1.
Introduction

Understanding the line bundles on curves is essential to understanding the curves themselves. As such, the universal Picard stack
plays a prominent role in the intersection theory of moduli of curves, recently for example in connection with the double ramification cycle
[
2
]
.
Like the moduli space of curves, the universal Picard stack
has a distinguished collection of classes in its Chow ring called
tautological classes
.
We briefly review their construction, which generalizes that of kappa classes on the moduli space of curves.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of genus
g
curves, and let
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}\to\mathscr{M}_{g}
be the universal Picard stack parameterizing degree
d
line bundles on genus
g
curves.
We write

f:\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
for the pullback of the universal curve from
\mathscr{M}_{g}
. Then there are two natural line bundles on
\mathscr{C}
: the relative dualizing sheaf
\omega_{f}
, and the universal degree
d
line bundle, which we call
\mathscr{L}
. From these we obtain the
twisted kappa classes

\kappa_{i,j}:=f_{*}(c_{1}(\omega_{f})^{i+1}\cdot c_{1}(\mathscr{L})^{j})\in A^{i+j}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g})\qquad\text{for $i\geq-1,j\geq 0$}.

Note that
\kappa_{i,0}
is the pullback of
\kappa_{i}
from
\mathscr{M}_{g}
.

In analogy with the Madsen–Weiss theorem on the stable cohomology of
\mathscr{M}_{g}

[
22
]
, it is known that the
the stable cohomology of
\mathscr{J}^{0}_{g}
as
g
tends to infinity is freely generated by the twisted kappa class
[
10
,
12
]
. The relations among twisted kappa classes for fixed
g
were studied in
[
3
]
, which discovered a family of “Faber–Zagier type” relations.
However, despite much interest in
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
and its tautological subring, there are no cases (beyond genus
0
or
1
) where either the tautological subring or the full Chow ring of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
is known.

Over the past several years, the Chow ring of
\mathscr{M}_{g}
has been determined for
g\leq 9

[
15
,
16
,
18
,
26
,
7
]
. Each of these works sets up the problem considering the stratification of
\mathscr{M}_{g}
by gonality. The first step of such calculations is to understand the smallest stratum, namely the hyperelliptic locus
\mathscr{H}_{2,g}\subset\mathscr{M}_{g}
. For
\mathscr{M}_{g}
, this smallest stratum is particularly nice:
The fundamental class of
\mathscr{H}_{2,g}
generates the socle in the tautological ring of
\mathscr{M}_{g}
, and, with rational coefficients, there are no other classes supported on it.

One might hope to similarly approach the intersection theory of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
via the gonality stratification. The first step here is to study the restriction of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}\to\mathscr{M}_{g}
to the hyperelliptic locus, which we call
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathscr{H}_{2,g}
. Our main theorem determines the rational Chow ring of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
for all
d
and
g
.
In contrast with
\mathscr{H}_{2,g}
, we find that
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
already supports many interesting classes.
As a special case, this provides the first non-trivial complete calculation of any
A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g})
in the case
g=2
.
Below, by slight abuse of notation, we write
\kappa_{i,j}
for the restriction of
\kappa_{i,j}
along the inclusion
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}\subset\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given an integer
g\geq 2
and any integer
d
, the rational Chow ring of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is

A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})=\frac{\mathbb{Q}[\kappa_{0,1},\kappa_{-1,2}]}{\langle(d\kappa_{0,1}-(g-1)\kappa_{-1,2})^{g+1}\rangle}.

We remark that
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over its rigidification
J^{d}_{2,g}
. This explains why its Chow ring possesses
non-trivial classes in arbitrarily high codimension.
As we shall explain in Section
6.4
, the Chow ring of
J^{d}_{2,g}
is a subring living in finitely many degrees:

A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})=\mathbb{Q}[u]/(u^{g+1})\hookrightarrow A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})\qquad\text{given by}\qquad u\mapsto d\kappa_{0,1}-(g-1)\kappa_{-1,2}.

This also gives us a distinguished description of the codimension
1
class whose
(g+1)
st power vanishes.
When
d=g-1
, this distinguished class is a multiple of the class of the theta divisor (see Section
6.3
).

As a corollary, we determine all relations among the restrictions of tautological classes to the hyperelliptic locus. The shape of these relations may be of independent interest since, for example, the ideal they generate must contain the ideal of tautological relations on
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
g\geq 2
and let
d
be any integer. The relations among the restrictions of tautological classes to
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
are generated by the following:

\displaystyle\kappa_{i,j}

\displaystyle=0\qquad\text{for all $i\geq 1,j\geq 0$}

\displaystyle\kappa_{0,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2g-2)^{j-1}}(\kappa_{0,1})^{j}

\displaystyle\kappa_{-1,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2g-2)^{j-1}}\cdot(\kappa_{0,1})^{j-2}\cdot\left((g-1)(j^{2}-j)\cdot\kappa_{-1,2}-d(j^{2}-2j)\cdot\kappa_{0,1}\right)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(d\kappa_{0,1}-(g-1)\kappa_{-1,2})^{g+1}.

Perhaps most interesting is the codimension
g+1
relation among
the codimension
1
generators, which
also appears in the theorem statement.
Our proof of this relation depends on the parity of
d
, but somewhat magically yields the same formula for all
d
.
The geometric explanation of this relation — and indeed the core of our approach more broadly — relies on
the relationship between line bundles on hyperelliptic curves and rank
2
vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Namely,
given a line bundle
L
on a hyperelliptic curve
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
its pushforward
\alpha_{*}L
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
By the Grothendieck–Birkhoff theorem, this vector bundle splits as a direct sum of line bundles
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{1})\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e_{2})
with
e_{1}\leq e_{2}
. We call the pair
(e_{1},e_{2})
the
splitting type of
L
.
Using Riemann–Roch, one sees that

(1.1)

e_{1}+e_{2}+2=\chi(\alpha_{*}L)=\chi(L)=d-g+1\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad e_{1}+e_{2}=d-g-1.

In addition, the Brill–Noether theory of hyperelliptic curves tells us that
e_{2}\leq\frac{d}{2}
, and equality holds if and only if
L=\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\frac{d}{2})
.

When
d
is odd, there are no line bundles of splitting type
(\lceil d/2\rceil,\lfloor d/2\rfloor-(g+1))
. The formula for the class of this splitting locus gives rise to the codimension
g+1
relation. Meanwhile, when
d
is even, the splitting type
(d/2,d/2-(g+1))
plays a special role. The line bundles with this splitting type are exactly those of the form
\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(\frac{d}{2})
.
Our approach to studying
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
involves excising a discriminant locus from a universal linear system of curves on Hirzebruch surfaces.
It turns out that the map from the universal singular point to the universal Hirzebruch surface jumps fiber dimension precisely along the directrices within Hirzebruch surfaces of this most unbalanced splitting type
(d/2,d/2-(g+1))
. This gives rise to a codimension
g+1
relation obtained as the pushforward of a certain codimension
1
class living inside this codimension
g
splitting locus.

1.1.
The integral Picard group

The Picard group of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
was found in
[
23
,
12
]
.
Here, we study the Picard group of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
, completing an analysis begun by Erman and Wood in
[
14
]
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{2,g}^{\dagger}
be the Hurwitz space of
degree
2
covers of a fixed
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The map

\mathcal{H}_{2,g}^{\dagger}\to\mathscr{H}_{2,g}
is a
\PGL_{2}
bundle.
Erman and Wood considered the universal Picard stack
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
over
\mathcal{H}_{2,g}^{\dagger}
and
computed its integral Picard group when
d-g
is even
[
14
, Theorem 7.2]
.
Erman and Wood make use of the stratification of
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
by the splitting type of line bundles.
Specifically, they find the Picard group of the largest piece in this stratification.
When
d-g
is even, the complement of the largest piece in the stratification has codimension
2
, so its Picard group agrees with that of
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
.
However, when
d-g
is odd, the complement of the largest piece has codimension
1
, so that studying piece alone is not enough to determine the Picard group.

The novel contribution in this paper is to leverage the Chow ring of the moduli stack of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to “put together” the intersection theory of all the pieces in this stratification. In addition to the main theorem,
this allows us to complete the missing case from
[
14
]
when
d-g
is odd. The answer is the same as when
d-g
is even.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
g\geq 2
and any
d
, we have

\Pic(\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g})=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/(8g+4).

We also extend the calculation of the integral Picard group to the
\SL_{2}
and
\PGL_{2}
equivariant settings. Let us write
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}=[\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}/\SL_{2}]
and
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}=[\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}/\PGL_{2}]
for the respective quotient stacks. We note that
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe. Thus, their rational Chow rings are isomorphic, and in fact, we shall work with
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}
for the rational computations. However, their integral Picard groups are not always the same.
We will see that
\Pic(\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g})=\Pic(\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g})
, but when working
\PGL_{2}
equivariantly, the answer turns out to depend on the parity of
g
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
g\geq 2
and any
d
, we have

\Pic(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/(8g+4)&\text{if $g$ is odd}\\
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/(4g+2)&\text{if $g$ is even.}\end{cases}

The integral Picard group of
\mathscr{H}_{2,g}
was computed in
[
1
, Theorem 5.1]
and it also depends on the parity of
g
.
Combining that result with our proof of Theorem
1.4
, it follows that the pullback map
\Pic(\mathscr{H}_{2,g})\to\Pic(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})
is exactly the inclusion of the torsion subgroup. Explicit line bundles on
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
generating the Picard group are given in Section
5.3
.

It was shown in
[
23
, Theorem 6.4]
that the Brauer class of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}\to J^{d}_{g}
has order
\gcd(d-g+1,2g-2)
. Using Theorem
1.4
above, we obtain the following result for its restriction to the hyperelliptic locus.

Corollary 1.5
.

The Brauer class of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to J^{d}_{2,g}
has order
\gcd(d-g+1,2)
.

Tensoring with the
g^{1}_{2}
shows that all
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
with
d
of the same parity are isomorphic. The above result shows that those of opposite parity cannot be isomorphic as
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-stacks.

Remark 1.6
.

(1) Corollary
1.5
provides a new proof of
[
24
, Corollary 3.1]
, which showed that a Poincaré line bundle exists on
J^{d}_{2,g}
if and only if
d
and
g
have the same parity. Conversely, using that result and arguing as in
[
23
, Proposition 6.6]
gives rise to an alternative proof of Corollary
1.5
.

(2) The Picard stack of a curve over a non-algebraically closed field is typically a non-trivial gerbe over its Picard scheme.
For genus
1
curves, this Brauer class was studied and related to the period-index problem in
[
17
]
.

1.2.
Overview of Techniques

They key to understanding
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is its relationship with a moduli stack
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
of certain pairs of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. In particular,
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is an open substack of a vector bundle
\mathscr{X}
over
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. Thus, the Chow ring of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is a quotient of the Chow ring of
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
.
Section
2
is devoted to defining

\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
and determining its Chow ring. This builds off of our previous calculation of the Chow ring of the moduli stack of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
[
19
]
; however, it is not an immediate consequence of earlier work, as we must restrict to an open substack where the splitting type is bounded, which introduces additional relations in the Chow ring. The relations all occur in codimension
g+1
and higher, and proving that our claimed relations on
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
are complete involves a careful analysis with universal splitting loci.

The next step is to study the closed complement of
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
inside
\mathscr{X}
. The complement can be described as the discriminant locus of a linear series of curves on the universal Hirzebruch surface over
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. We control the relations that arise from excising this discriminant locus by using relative bundles of principal parts. We briefly review the construction and basic properties of relative bundles of principal parts in Section
3
. We then use them in Section
4
to construct the universal singular point, whose Chow groups surject onto the Chow groups of the discriminant locus. Even though the arguments in this section depend significantly on the parity of
d
, the final result for the rational Chow ring is the same. We also show that our codimension
1
calculations hold integrally, proving Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
in Section
5
.

Finally, in Section
6
, we take advantage of an embedding of the universal curve over
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
inside a Hirzebruch surface to compute the twisted kappa classes in terms of our generators that were pulled back from
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. We can then translate the presentation of
A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})
given in Section
4
into the form presented in Theorem
1.1
. To express the higher codimension kappa classes in terms of our codimension
1
generators, we set up a system of recursive relations. Solving this recursion proves Corollary
1.2
.

Remark 1.7
.

Let us also mention some recent related work.
When we stratify
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
according to the splitting type of line bundles, each stratum is the moduli space of hyperelliptic curves on some fixed Hirzebruch surface.
The stable cohomology of each of these strata was found in
[
5
]
.

2.
Construction of the moduli stack

In
[
14
]
, Erman and Wood relate
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
to a moduli stack of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. While our presentation is slightly different, the key ideas that we present below and in Sections
2.1
and
2.5
are due to them. The important original contributions of this section are in Sections
2.3
and
2.4
, where we determine the Chow ring of the stack
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
defined below.

Suppose we are given a hyperelliptic curve
\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a degree
d
line bundle
L
on
C
. There are two naturally associated vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. First, there is the rank
2
vector bundle
E:=\alpha_{*}L
, which by (
1.1
) has degree
d-g-1
. If we denote the splitting type of
E
by
(e_{1},e_{2})
with
e_{1}\leq e_{2}
, then

h^{0}(C,L\otimes\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e_{2}))=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},E(-e_{2}))\geq 1.

Hence, the degree of
L\otimes\alpha^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e_{2})
must be nonnegative. This implies

d-2e_{2}\geq 0
, or equivalently,
e_{1}\geq\frac{d}{2}-g-1
.

In the special case that
L=\mathcal{O}_{C}
, the pushforward
\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}
necessarily has an
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
summand. The dual of the quotient is called the
Tschernhausen bundle
of the cover, which we’ll denote
F:=(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}/\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}})^{\vee}
. By (
1.1
), the line bundle
F
has degree
g+1
.
As we will explain below, the association of
(\alpha:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1},L)
with the pair of vector bundles
(E,F)
defines a morphism of
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
to a moduli stack
\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
parameterizing pairs of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We would also like to allow the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
target to vary in families, in other words take the quotient of both of these stacks by
\SL_{2}
or
\PGL_{2}
. Below, we use caligraphic font for the
\SL_{2}
quotient and script font for the
\PGL_{2}
quotient. The caligraphic version is always a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe over the script version.
The distinction between these will only matter for the computation of the integral Picard group.

2.1.
The morphism
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}

An object of the stack
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
over a scheme
S
is the data of a pair
(\alpha:C\to S\times\mathbb{P}^{1},L)

where

(1)

\alpha:C\to S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a degree
2
cover such that the composition
C\to S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to S
is a family of smooth genus
g
curves;

(2)

L
is a line bundle on
C
having degree
d
on fibers of
C\to S
.

Given
\alpha
and
L
as above, the pushforward
E:=\alpha_{*}L
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
having degree
d-g-1
on fibers over
S
. In addition,
F:=(\alpha_{*}\mathcal{O}_{C}/\mathcal{O}_{P})^{\vee}
is a line bundle of relative degree
g+1
on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let us define
\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
to be the stack whose objects over a scheme
S
are pairs
(E,F)
where

(1)

E
is a rank
2
, relative degree
d-g-1
vector bundle on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose restrictions to fibers of
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to S
have splitting type
(e_{1},e_{2})
with
e_{1}\geq\frac{d}{2}-g-1
;

(2)

F
is a relative degree
g+1
line bundle on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Sending
(\alpha:C\to S\times\mathbb{P}^{1})
to
(E,F)
defines a morphism of stacks
\mathcal{J}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}
.
We define
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}=[\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}/\SL_{2}]
and
\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}=[\mathcal{B}^{d,\dagger}_{2,g}/\PGL_{2}]
.
Taking the
\SL_{2}
or
\PGL_{2}
quotient of our morphism above, we obtain morphisms
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
and
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. These are defined in the same way, where
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
is replaced with a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\mathbb{P}V
where
V
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
S
with trivial determinant in the
\SL_{2}
case, or it is replaced with a family
P\to S
of smooth genus
0
curves in the
\PGL_{2}
case.

An essential fact, which we shall show in Section
2.5
, is that the morphism
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
factors as an open inclusion inside of a vector bundle over
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
, and similarly with script letters.
Consequently, the Chow ring of
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}
is generated by the pullbacks of classes from
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
.
The main focus of this section is to understand the intersection theory of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
.

2.2.
Stacks of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

Let
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}^{\dagger}
denote the moduli stack of rank
r
, degree
d
vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. An object of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}^{\dagger}
over a scheme
S
is the data of a rank
r
, relative degree
d
vector bundle
E
on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
A morphism between objects
(S,E)
and
(S^{\prime},E^{\prime})
is a cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}\times S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi}

together with an isomorphism
\phi:\xi^{*}E\to E^{\prime}
.

We also require the quotient stacks
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}=[\mathcal{V}_{r,d}^{\dagger}/\SL_{2}]
and
\mathscr{V}_{r,d}=[\mathcal{V}_{r,d}^{\dagger}/\PGL_{2}]
. Explicitly, the objects of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
over a scheme
S
are triples
(S,V,E)
where
V
is a rank
2
vector bundle on
S
with trivial determinant and
E
is a rank
r
, relative degree
d
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}V
. Meanwhile, the objects of
\mathscr{V}_{r,d}
over a scheme
S
are

(S,P,E)
where
P\to S
is a family of smooth genus
0
curves and
E
is a rank
r
, relative degree
d
vector bundle on
P
. (The difference between the objects of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
and
\mathscr{V}_{r,d}
is that
P\to S
need not have a relative degree
1
line bundle.) The natural map
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\to\mathscr{V}_{r,d}
is a
\mu_{2}
-gerbe. For more background on the moduli stack of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we refer the reader to
[
19
]
.

We define

(2.1)

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}:=\mathcal{V}_{2,d-g-1}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{1,g+1}

and

(2.2)

\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{d}_{2,g}:=\mathscr{V}_{2,d-g-1}\times_{\BPGL_{2}}\mathscr{V}_{1,g+1}.

These stacks parameterize pairs of vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles, respectively familes of genus
0
curves.
Note that, by construction,
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
is the open substack of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
where the restriction of the universal rank
2
vector bundle to fibers of the universal genus
0
curve has splitting type
(e_{1},e_{2})
with
e_{1}\geq\frac{d}{2}-g-1
, and similarly for script letters.

Remark 2.1
.

We note that
\mathcal{V}_{1,e}^{\dagger}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
. Suppose
E
is a line bundle of degree
e
on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Then
E(-e)
is a line bundle of relative degree
0
. Writing
\pi:S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\to S
for the projection, cohomology and base change shows there is an isomorphism
E(-e)\cong\pi^{*}\pi_{*}E(-e)
. Thus the equivalence
\mathcal{V}_{1,e}^{\dagger}\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is given by sending
(S,E)
to the line bundle
\pi_{*}E(-e)
. The inverse map is defined by sending a line bundle
L
on
S
to the line bundle
\pi^{*}L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e)
on
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. A similar argument shows that
\mathcal{V}_{1,e}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\BSL_{2}
. (Replace
S\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\mathbb{P}V
and replace
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(1)
with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}V}(1)
.)

However, the
\PGL_{2}
quotient is sensitive to the parity of
e
. Suppose
E
is a line bundle of degree
e
on a family of genus
0
curves
P\to S
. If
e
is even, then
E\otimes\omega_{P/S}^{\otimes e/2}\cong\pi^{*}\pi_{*}(E\otimes\omega_{P/S}^{\otimes e/2})
and a similar argument applies to show
\mathscr{V}_{1,e}
is equivalent to
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\BPGL_{2}
. However, if
e
is odd, it turns out
\mathscr{V}_{1,e}
is equivalent to
\BGL_{2}
. Given
E
on
\pi:P\to S
, we produce a rank
2
vector bundle on
S
by
V:=\pi_{*}(E\otimes\omega_{P/S}^{\otimes\lfloor e/2\rfloor})
. Using cohomology and base change, we find that
P\cong\mathbb{P}V
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}V}(1)\cong E\otimes\omega_{P/S}^{\otimes\lfloor e/2\rfloor}
, so
E
can be recovered as
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}V}(1)\otimes\omega_{P/S}^{-\otimes\lfloor e/2\rfloor}
.

We determined the integral Picard groups of stacks of the form in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) in
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
. To state the result, we need some more notation. We shall follow the notation in
[
8
]
with one difference: We have not imposed the condition that our vector bundles
E
and
F
on
P\to S
are globally generated on fibers. A “boundedness condition” does come into play when we restrict to the open substack
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
, but we first need to understand
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
.

Let us write
\pi:\mathcal{P}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
for the universal
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle, pulled back from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}\to\BSL_{2}
.
It comes equipped with a relative degree
1
line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1)
. Let
z=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1))\in A^{1}(\mathcal{P})
. In addition, let
c_{2}=c_{2}(\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(1))
be the pullback of the universal second Chern class from
\BSL_{2}
. By the projective bundle formula, we have
z^{2}=-\pi^{*}c_{2}\in A^{2}(\mathcal{P})
.

Next, let
\mathcal{E}
be the universal rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathcal{P}
and let
\mathcal{F}
be the universal rank
1
vector bundle on
\mathcal{P}
.
Since
\mathcal{P}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle, there exist
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime}\in A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})
such that

c_{i}(\mathcal{E})=\pi^{*}a_{i}+\pi^{*}a_{i}^{\prime}z\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{i}(\mathcal{F})=\pi^{*}b_{i}+\pi^{*}b_{i}^{\prime}z.

Note that
a_{1}^{\prime}=d-g-1
and
b_{1}^{\prime}=g+1
are the relative degrees of
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}
respectively. Since
\pi^{*}:A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\mathcal{P})
is an inclusion, in what follows, we shall often omit
\pi^{*}
’s from notation simply identifying
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime}
and
c_{2}
with their images under it.

By the sentence before
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
,
\Pic(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})
is freely generated by
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}
and
b_{1}
. The following is a consequence of
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
. (Note that
b_{2}^{\prime}=0
since our
\mathcal{F}
has rank
1
.)

Lemma 2.2
.

The natural map
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}\to\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
induces an inclusion

\Pic(\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{d}_{2,g})\hookrightarrow\Pic(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})=\mathbb{Z}a_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Z}a_{2}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{Z}b_{1}

whose image is the subgroup generated by

\begin{cases}a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1}&\text{if $g$ is odd and $d$ is even}\\
2a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1}&\text{if $g$ and $d$ are both odd}\\
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},2b_{1}&\text{if $g$ is even and $d$ is odd}\\
a_{1}-b_{1},2a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime}&\text{if $g$ and $d$ are both even.}\end{cases}

Next we show that the rational Chow ring of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
is freely generated by
a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},b_{1},
and
c_{2}
. This is a special case of the following more general fact regarding such fiber products.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
r,d,s,e
, the rational Chow ring of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{s,e}
is free on the
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime}
and
c_{2}
. In particular,

A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})=\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime},b_{1},c_{2}].

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{gg}}_{r,d}\subset\mathcal{V}_{r,d}
denote the open substack parameterizing globally generated vector bundles.
In
[
9
, Theorem 4.4]
, we proved that the Chow ring of any product of the form
\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{gg}}_{r,d}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{gg}}_{s,e}
is generated by
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime},
and
c_{2}
, and all relations among these classes lie in codimension at least
\min(d,e)+1
.

Note that
twisting both of the universal vector bundles by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(m)
gives an isomorphism of
\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{s,e}

with

\mathcal{V}_{r,d+mr}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{s,e+ms}
.
As we increase
m
, the degrees of the vector bundles increase, and the codimension of the complement of

\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{gg}}_{r,d+mr}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}^{\mathrm{gg}}_{s,e+ms}\subset\mathcal{V}_{r,d+mr}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{s,e+ms}

tends to infinity as
m
tends to infinity.
The relationship between the
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime},
and
c_{2}
and the new versions in higher degree is given by an invertible change of variables. By choosing
m
large enough, it follows that, for each fixed codimension
j
, we have
A^{j}(\mathcal{V}_{r,d}\times_{\BSL_{2}}\mathcal{V}_{s,e})
is generated by the
a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},b_{i},b_{i}^{\prime},
and
c_{2}
and there are no relations among them.
∎

We now have a good understanding of the intersection theory of

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
. However, our morphsim
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
factors through the open substack
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
where the splitting type of
\mathcal{E}
is not too unbalanced. Our next task is therefore to determine the relations on
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
that occur from bounding the splitting type of
\mathcal{E}
.

2.3.
Universal splitting loci

Given integers
i,j
with
i+j=e
,
we define the
universal splitting locus

\Sigma_{i,j}\subset\mathcal{V}_{2,e}
as follows. It is the substack whose points over a scheme
S
are pairs
(P,E)
where
P\to S
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle and
E
is a rank
2
vector bundle such that
E
has locally constant splitting type
(i,j)
, see
[
7
, Section 3]
. As explained there, we have an equivalence of stacks

\Sigma_{i,j}\cong\mathrm{B}\mathrm{Aut}(\mathcal{O}(i)\oplus\mathcal{O}(j))\rtimes\SL_{2}.

As such,
\Sigma_{i,j}
has codimension
j-1-1
inside
\mathcal{V}_{2,e}
. The closure of
\Sigma_{i,j}
is the union of the
\Sigma_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}
with
i^{\prime}\leq i
.

These splitting loci are significant for us because

\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}\smallsetminus\overline{\Sigma}_{\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor-g-2}.

Notice that
\overline{\Sigma}_{\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor-g-2}
has codimension
g+1
if
d
is odd and codimension
g+2
when
d
is even. In either case, since we assume throughout that
g\geq 2
, it follows that

(2.3)

\Pic(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})=\Pic(\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Pic(\widehat{\mathscr{B}}^{d}_{2,g})=\Pic(\mathscr{B}^{d}_{2,g}).

However, we still need to understand the relations that are introduced in higher codimension.

The goal of this section is to prove the following closed formula for the fundamental class of the closure
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]\in A^{j-i-1}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
in terms of our generators
a_{1},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime}
and
c_{2}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
e
is odd, then
j-i-1
is even, and
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]\in A^{j-i-1}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
is a non-zero multiple of

\prod_{i<k<\frac{e}{2}}(ka_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})((e-k)a_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})+(e-2k)^{2}(a_{2}+k(e-k)c_{2}).

If
e
is even, then
j-i-1
is odd, and
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]\in A^{j-i-1}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
is a non-zero multiple of

(ea_{1}-2a_{2}^{\prime})\cdot\prod_{i<k<\frac{e}{2}}(ka_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})((e-k)a_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})+(e-2k)^{2}(a_{2}+k(e-k)c_{2}).

Remark 2.5
.

A formula for
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]
in terms of Chern classes of pushforward sheaves
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)
is given in
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
. In principle, one could use Grothendeick–Riemann–Roch to find the Chern classes of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{E}(m)
in terms of our desired generators and plug that in. The generating function in
[
19
, Lemma 5.4]

provides the conversion modulo the ideal generated by
c_{2}
. Following this route, one can derive formulas that agree with those in Lemma
2.4
modulo
c_{2}
. (In the eventual context where we apply this formula, it actually only matters what the answer is modulo
c_{2}
.)
However, the argument given below has the benefit of providing a conceptual reason for why the formulas factor and giving the more general result when
c_{2}\neq 0
, which may be of use elsewhere.

Proof.

Our basic strategy is to induct on the difference
j-i
.
The key step is to find the fundamental class of
\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}
, viewed as a codimension
2
class on
\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}
(or codimension
1
class in the case
i=j
).
We will prove that
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}]
is equivalent to the pullback of a class in
A^{2}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
. Using the push-pull formula and applying induction then gives rise to the factoring formula in the statement.

To set up the argument, consider the restriction diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime*}\mathcal{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma_{i,j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{V}_{2,e}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

First suppose
i\neq j
. As explained in
[
7
, Section 3]
, the stack
\Sigma_{i,j}
comes equipped with two line bundles
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
(called the “HN bundles”) such that
\iota^{\prime*}\mathcal{E}
sits in an exact sequence

(2.4)

0\rightarrow(p^{*}\mathcal{N}_{2})(j)\rightarrow\iota^{\prime*}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow(p^{*}\mathcal{N}_{1})(i)\rightarrow 0.

By
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have
A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j})=\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},n_{2},c_{2}]
where
n_{k}=c_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{k})
.
Using (
2.4
), we find

\displaystyle\iota^{\prime*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=(p^{*}n_{1}+iz)+(p^{*}n_{2}+jz)=p^{*}(n_{1}+n_{2})+ez

\displaystyle\iota^{\prime*}c_{2}(\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle=(p^{*}n_{1}+iz)(p^{*}n_{2}+jz)=p^{*}(n_{1}n_{2}-ijc_{2})+p^{*}(in_{2}+jn_{1})z.

It follows that

(2.5)

\iota^{*}a_{1}=n_{1}+n_{2},\qquad\iota^{*}a_{2}=n_{1}n_{2}-ijc_{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\iota^{*}a_{2}^{\prime}=in_{2}+jn_{1}.

Since
A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
is a domain
[
19
]
, the fundamental class
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]
is a non-zero divisor. By the push-pull formula, it follows that the Gysin pullback
\overline{\iota}^{*}:A^{*}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})\to A^{*}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})
is injective.
Now,
A^{2}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})
has dimension
5
with basis
a_{1}^{2},a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},c_{2}
. Thus,
\dim A^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})\geq 5
.
Meanwhile,
by excision, we have

(2.6)

A^{0}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1})\rightarrow A^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})\rightarrow A^{2}(\Sigma_{i,j})\rightarrow 0.

By the discussion above, we know that
A^{2}(\Sigma_{i,j})
has dimension
4
with basis
n_{1}^{2},n_{1}n_{2},n_{2}^{2},c_{2}
. It follows that
\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}\in A^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})
is non-zero and

(2.7)

A^{2}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})=\langle a_{1}^{2},a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},c_{2}\rangle\xrightarrow{\overline{\iota}^{*}}A^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})=\langle[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}],n_{1}^{2},n_{1}n_{2},n_{2}^{2},c_{2}\rangle

is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces.
In particular,
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}]
lies in the image of
\overline{\iota}^{*}
. Now let
\alpha_{i-1,j+1}
be a generator for the kernel of

A^{2}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})=\langle a_{1}^{2},a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},c_{2}\rangle\xrightarrow{\iota}A^{2}(\Sigma_{i,j})=\langle n_{1}^{2},n_{1}n_{2},n_{2}^{2},c_{2}\rangle.

By (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we see that
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}]\in A^{2}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j})
is a non-zero multiple of
\overline{\iota}^{*}\alpha_{i-1,j+1}
.

It remains to determine
\alpha_{i-1,j+1}
. From (
2.5
) we see that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(ia_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(i-j)n_{2}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(ja_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(j-i)n_{1}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(a_{2}+ijc_{2})

\displaystyle=n_{1}n_{2}.

This helps us spot a generator of the kernel, namely any non-zero multiple of

\alpha_{i-1,j+1}=(ia_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})(ja_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime})+(i-j)^{2}(a_{2}+ijc_{2}).

By the push-pull formula,
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,j+1}]
is a non-zero multiple of
\alpha_{i-1,j+1}[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]
.
Inducting on the difference
j-i
completes the proof in the case
e
is odd.

If
e
is even, we need a small variation on this argument to find the class of
\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,i+1}
inside
\overline{\Sigma}_{i,i}=\mathcal{V}_{2,e}
, since it is codimension
1
. Here, there is an exact sequence

A^{0}(\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,i+1})\rightarrow A^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})\to A^{1}(\Sigma_{i,i})\rightarrow 0.

In this case, there is a rank
2
HN bundle
\mathcal{N}
with the property that
\iota^{\prime*}\mathcal{E}\cong(p^{*}\mathcal{N})(i)
. However, if we think of
\mathcal{N}_{1}
and
\mathcal{N}_{2}
as the Chern roots of
\mathcal{N}
and
A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,i})
as the subring of symmetric polynomials in
n_{1},n_{2}
, then
the calculations in (
2.5
) are still valid. In particular, we see that the kernel of
A^{1}(\mathcal{V}_{2,e})\to A^{1}(\Sigma_{i,i})
is generated by
ia_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime}
. Hence,
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i-1,i+1}]
is a non-zero multiple of
ia_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime}=\frac{e}{2}a_{1}-a_{2}^{\prime}
.
∎

We make note of a specialization of these formulas that will be relevant in later sections. Although the universal formula on
\mathcal{V}_{2,e}
is not a multiple of a perfect power, the specialization we wind up using is. The following is easy to check directly.

Corollary 2.6
.

If we substitute
c_{2}=0
and
a_{2}=\frac{1}{4}a_{1}^{2}
, then the formula for
[\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}]
becomes a non-zero multiple of
(ea_{1}-2a_{2}^{\prime})^{j-i-1}
.

2.4.
The Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}

We are now ready to find the rational Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
.
Let us define
\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
to be the preimage of
\Sigma_{i,j}
along the projection
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{V}_{2,d-g-1}
.
We first note an important corollary of the proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Corollary 2.7
.

(1) For any
i,j
with
i+j=d-g-1
,
the pullback map

A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E}))

is surjective.

(2) If
\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
, then

A^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E}))

is also surjective.

Proof.

If
i=j
, then
\Sigma_{i,i}(\mathcal{E})
is open inside
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
, so (1) is immediate. If
i\neq j
, then
A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j})
is generated by
n_{1},n_{2},c_{2}
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})\to\Sigma_{i,j}
is a trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe (as
\mathcal{V}_{1,g+1}
is a copy of
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\BSL_{2}
by Remark
2.1
).
Hence,
A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E}))
is generated by
n_{1},n_{2},c_{2},b_{1}
. It is easy to see that
c_{2}
and
b_{1}
are pulled back from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
.
By (
2.9
) and (
2.8
), the classes
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
also lie in the image of the pullback map.

(2) follows from (1): If
\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
, then the pullback map
A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E}))
factors through
A^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g})
. Thus, in order for (1) to be surjective, (2) must be surjective.
∎

With this, we can now completely determine the Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
, which is the key takeaway from this section.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
S^{d}_{g}
be the fundamental class of the closure of the

(\lceil\frac{d+1}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{d+1}{2}\rfloor-g-2)

splitting locus for
\mathcal{E}
. Note that
S^{d}_{g}\in A^{g+1}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})
when
d
is odd and

S^{d}_{g}\in A^{g+2}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})
when
d
is even.
In either case,

A^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g})=\frac{A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g})}{\langle S^{d}_{g}\rangle}=\frac{\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},b_{1},c_{2}]}{\langle S^{d}_{g}\rangle}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
, we know that
A^{*}(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{2,g}^{d})=\mathbb{Q}[a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime},a_{2},b_{1},c_{2}^{\prime}]
.
The complement of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{B}}^{d}_{2,g}
is the union of splitting loci
\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})
with
j-i\geq g+2
. Let
m=g+1
if
d
is odd and
g+2
if
d
is even, so it is the codimension of the complement.
Because the pullback map in Corollary
2.7
(1) is surjective, repeated use of excision and the push-pull formula shows that the image of

A^{*-m}\left(\bigcup_{j-i\geq g+2}\Sigma_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})\right)\rightarrow A^{*}(\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g})

is the ideal generated by the fundamental classes of the
\overline{\Sigma}_{i,j}(\mathcal{E})
with
j-i\geq g+2
. Considering the formulae in Lemma
2.4
, we see that each of these classes is a multiple of
S^{d}_{g}
.
∎

2.5.
Factoring the morphism

We now have a good understanding of the Chow ring of
\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. In order to use that to understand the Chow ring of
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}
, we need to understand the fibers of the morphism
\mathcal{J}^{d}_{2,g}\to\mathcal{B}^{d}_{2,g}
. The results of this section follow from
[
14
]
by taking the
\SL_{2}

3.
Principal parts

Here we define and state some basic properties of relative bundles of principal parts that we shall need in the next section. We shall only need first order principal parts. For more on bundles of principal parts, we refer the reader to
[
13
, Sections 7 and 11]
; or, for further variations, see
[
6
, Section 3]
.

Let
b:Y\to Z
be a smooth proper surjective morphism.
Let
\Delta_{Y/Z}\subset Y\times_{Z}Y
be the relative diagonal and let
p_{1},p_{2}:Y\times_{Z}Y\to Y
be the two projection maps.
Let
\mathcal{W}
be a vector bundle on
Y
and let
\mathcal{I}_{\Delta_{Y/Z}}
denote the ideal sheaf of the diagonal in
Y\times_{Z}Y
. The bundle of relative first order principal parts
P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})
is defined as

P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})=p_{2*}(p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y\times_{Z}Y}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta_{Y/Z}}^{2}).

The fiber of
P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})
at a point
y\in Y
corresponds to the restriction of
\mathcal{W}
to a first order neighborhood of
y
.
We shall need the following properties of
P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})
:

Proposition 3.1

(Theorem 11.2 of
[
13
]
)
.

With notation as above,

(1)

There is an isomorphism
b^{*}b_{*}\mathcal{W}\xrightarrow{\sim}p_{2*}p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{W}
.

(2)

The quotient map
p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y\times_{Z}Y}/\mathcal{I}_{\Delta_{Y/Z}}^{2}
pushes forward to a map

b^{*}b_{*}\mathcal{W}\cong p_{2*}p_{1}^{*}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W}),

which, fiber by fiber, associates to a global section
f
of
\mathcal{W}
a section
f^{\prime}
whose value at
z\in Z
is the restriction of
f
to a first order neighborhood of
z
in the fiber
b^{-1}b(z)
.

(3)

The order of vanishing of sections gives rise to a filtration

0\rightarrow\mathcal{W}\otimes\Omega_{Y/Z}\rightarrow P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})\rightarrow\mathcal{W}\rightarrow 0.

Below are some criteria to help us determine when the evaluation map from part (2) is surjective. Proofs of the following lemmas can be found in
[
6
, Section 3]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 3.3 of
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{W}
is a relatively very ample line bundle on
Y
over
Z
. Then the evaluation map

b^{*}b_{*}\mathcal{W}\rightarrow P^{1}_{Y/Z}(\mathcal{W})

is surjective.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 3.5 of
[
6
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle on a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi:\mathcal{P}\to B
and let
\gamma:\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}\to\mathcal{P}
be the projectivization. Suppose
\mathcal{W}=(\gamma^{*}A)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}E^{\vee}}(m)
for some
m\geq 1
and vector bundle
A
on
\mathcal{P}
.
If
R^{1}\pi_{*}[\gamma_{*}\mathcal{W}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{P}}(-2)]=0
, then the evaluation map

(\pi\circ\gamma)^{*}(\pi\circ\gamma)_{*}\mathcal{W}\to P^{1}_{\mathbb{P}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}/B}(\mathcal{W})

is surjective.

Finally, suppose we have a tower
X\xrightarrow{a}Y\xrightarrow{b}Z
of smooth proper surjective morphisms. We have the relative cotangent sequence

0\rightarrow a^{*}\Omega_{Y/Z}\rightarrow\Omega_{X/Z}\rightarrow\Omega_{X/Y}\rightarrow 0.

Tensoring the left map above with

and combining with Proposition

\mathcal{W}\otimes a^{*}\Omega_{Y/Z}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}\otimes\Omega_{X/Z}\hookrightarrow P^{1}_{X/Z}(\mathcal{W}).

The quotient gives rise to a surjection

5.4.
Proof of Corollary

Since

is a

-gerbe, their Picard groups are related by the following exact sequence (see
[

, Equation 6.1]

0\rightarrow\Pic(J^{d}_{2,g})\rightarrow\Pic(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{res}}\Pic(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\cong\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{obs}}\mathrm{Br}(J^{d}_{2,g}).

Above, the map
\mathrm{res}
sends a line bundle to the character of its restriction to a fiber of the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe, and
\mathrm{obs}

\nu_{d}
in the Brauer group of

. In particular, the order of
\nu_{d}
in the Brauer group of

is the index of the image of

Recall that
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on the fibers of
\mathscr{L}
by scaling. Thus, using the definitions in the previous subsection, we find

(5.6)

\mathrm{res}(\mathscr{A})=\begin{cases}4&\text{if $d-g-1$ is odd}\\
2&\text{if $d-g-1$ is even}\end{cases}\qquad\mathrm{res}(\mathscr{B})=0\qquad\mathrm{res}(\mathscr{N})=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $d$ is even}\\

Moreover, by
[

\mathrm{res}

\mathrm{res}

Lemma 6.2

We have

\kappa_{0,j}=\frac{1}{2^{j-1}}(g-1)a_{1}^{j}=\frac{1}{(2g-2)^{j-1}}(\kappa_{0,1})^{j}.

Lemma 6.3

For all

6.3.
The theta divisor and splitting loci

Recall that when
d=g-1
, there is a canonical divisor called the
theta divisor
on
\mathscr{J}^{g-1}_{g}
corresponding to those line bundles that have a non-zero global section. By Riemann–Roch, since
d=g-1
, having a global section is the same as having non-zero
H^{1}
. Thus, by cohomology and base change, we can describe the theta divisor as the support of the sheaf
R^{1}f_{*}\mathscr{L}
.
A standard calculation with the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch formula expresses the class of the theta divisor in terms of the twisted kappa classes.

Lemma 6.4
.

We have
\Theta=\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{0,1}-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{-1,2}-\frac{1}{12}\kappa_{1,0}\in A^{1}(\mathscr{J}^{g-1}_{g})
.

Proof.

When
d=g-1
, a general line bundle has no global sections, so
f_{*}\mathscr{L}=0
. The theta divisor is the support of
R^{1}f_{*}\mathscr{L}
, so has fundamental class
c_{1}(R^{1}f_{*}\mathscr{L})
.
By Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch, we have

\displaystyle-c_{1}(R^{1}f_{*}\mathscr{L})

\displaystyle=\left[f_{*}(\mathrm{Ch}(\mathscr{L})\mathrm{Td}(\omega_{f}^{\vee}))\right]_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[f_{*}\left(1+c_{1}(\mathscr{L})+\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{L})^{2}}{2}+\ldots\right)\left(1-\frac{c_{1}(\omega_{f})}{2}+\frac{c_{1}(\omega_{f})^{2}}{12}+\ldots\right)\right]_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{*}\left(\frac{c_{1}(\omega_{f})^{2}}{12}-\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{L})c_{1}(\omega_{f})}{2}+\frac{c_{1}(\mathscr{L})^{2}}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{12}\kappa_{1,0}-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{0,1}+\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{-1,2},

from which the result follows.
∎

The formula given in Lemma
6.4
holds on the whole universal Picard stack. When we restrict to the hyperelliptic locus
\mathscr{J}^{g-1}_{2,g}\subset\mathscr{J}^{g-1}_{g}
we have
\kappa_{1,0}=0
and so
\Theta
is a multiple of
\kappa_{0,1}-\kappa_{-1,2}
. This is the class whose
(g+1)
st power generates the relations in Theorem
1.1
.

We also give a direct conceptual argument in terms of splitting loci.
When
d=g-1
, the general line bundle has splitting type
(-1,-1)
, so the theta divisor is the same thing as the splitting locus for splitting type
(-2,0)
. We saw in Corollary
2.6
that, once we set
c_{2}=0
and
a_{2}=\frac{1}{4}a_{1}^{2}
,
the universal formulas for splitting loci become perfect powers of the class of the codimension
1
splitting locus.
Thus, the class of the codimension
g+1
splitting locus must be a multiple of the
(g+1)
st power of the class of theta.

6.4.
The Chow ring of the rigidification

In this last section, we study the relationship between the Chow ring of the stack
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g}
and of its rigidification
J^{d}_{2,g}
.
The former is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over the latter.

We first recall some general facts about
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes. Suppose
\mathscr{X}\to X
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.
There is a restriction map
\mathrm{res}:\Pic(\mathscr{X})\to\Pic(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})=\mathbb{Z}
that restricts a line bundle to a fiber of
\mathscr{X}\to X
(as in (
5.5
)). A line bundle
\mathscr{M}
on
\mathscr{X}
is called

\emph{$n$-twisted}
if
\mathrm{res}(\mathscr{M})=n
(see e.g.
[
25
, Chapter 12.3]
).
We claim that if
\mathscr{M}
is an
n
-twisted line bundle, then the total space of
\mathscr{M}
minus the zero section is a
\mu_{n}
-gerbe over
X
. Indeed, the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
stabilizer at a point on
\mathscr{X}
acts transitively on the fiber of
\mathscr{M}\smallsetminus 0
with stabilizer
\mu_{n}
. Alternatively, let
\mathscr{X}_{n}\to X
represent
n
times the class of
\mathscr{X}\to X
. There is a natural map
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}_{n}
. Fiberwise over
X
this map looks like the map
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
induced by
t\mapsto t^{n}
. Hence,
\mathscr{X}\to\mathscr{X}_{n}
is a
\mu_{n}
-gerbe and pullback along it induces an equivalence of
1
-twisted line bundles on
\mathscr{X}_{n}
with
n
-twisted line bundles on
\mathscr{X}
.
By
[
21
, Lemma 3.1.1.8]
,
\mathscr{X}
posesses an
n
-twisted line bundle if and only if
\mathscr{X}_{n}
is trivial. In this case, we have the following diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}\smallsetminus 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\mathrm{pt}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pt}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{X}_{n}\cong X\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

where the squares are fibered squares. This shows that
\mathscr{M}\smallsetminus 0\to X
is a
\mu_{n}
-gerbe.

Lemma 6.5
.

We have
A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})\cong\mathbb{Q}[u]/(u^{g+1})
, and the pullback map
A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})

u\mapsto d\kappa_{0,1}-(g-1)\kappa_{-1,2}

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be the line bundle defined in Section

. By (

), we have that
\mathscr{A}
is an
n
-twisted line bundle for

or

\mathscr{A}\smallsetminus 0\to J^{d}_{2,g}
is a

-gerbe, so
we have an isomorphism of rational Chow rings
A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})\cong A^{*}(\mathscr{A}\smallsetminus 0)

A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})/\langle c_{1}(\mathscr{A})\rangle=A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})/\langle a_{1}\rangle
.
The first claim now follows from Lemmas
4.3
and
4.8
.

For the second claim, note that the composition

A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\mathscr{A}\smallsetminus 0)
is an isomorphism on rational Chow rings. Hence, the first map must be an injection. Up to scale, there is a unique codimension

(g+1)

A^{*}(J^{d}_{2,g})\to A^{*}(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{2,g})

.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

When
d=0
, an argument using the Beauville–Deninger–Murre weight decomposition for Chow groups of abelian schemes shows that
(\kappa_{-1,2})^{g+1}=0\in A^{*}(J^{0}_{g})
.
In this decomposition, the weight
w

n
map
J^{0}_{g}\to J^{0}_{g}
acts by
n^{w}
for all integers
n
. One computes that

\kappa_{i,j}
has weight
j
and weights are multiplicative. By
[

, Theorem 2.19]
, all classes of weight larger than

vanish. See also
[
4
, Proposition 4.2]
.

The codimension

also arises in the results
[

]
on the Picard groups of

and
\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g}
over all of
\mathscr{M}_{g}
. Let

\mathscr{M}_{g}
.
There, it was shown that
\Pic(J^{d}_{g})\to\Pic(\mathscr{J}^{d}_{g})
is the subgroup generated by
\lambda

. (We note that
[

]
defines

using powers of the relative tangent bundle instead of the relative cotangent bundle, so their

differs from ours by

There is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{d}_{2,g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J^{d}_{g}}

where the horizontal arrows are closed embeddings and the vertical arrows are

-gerbes. It follows that the pullback on rigidifications

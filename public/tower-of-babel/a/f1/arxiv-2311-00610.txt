Regularized Shannon sampling formulas related to the special affine Fourier transform
Frank Filbir, Manfred Tasche, Anna Veselovska

Abstract
In this paper, we present new regularized Shannon sampling formulas related to the special affine Fourier transform (SAFT). These sampling formulas use localized sampling with special compactly supported window functions, namely B-spline, sinh \sinh -type, and continuous Kaiser–Bessel window functions. In contrast to the Shannon sampling series for SAFT, the regularized Shannon sampling formulas for SAFT possesses an exponential decay of the approximation error and are numerically robust in the presence of noise, if certain oversampling condition is fulfilled. Several numerical experiments illustrate the theoretical results. Key words : special affine Fourier transform, SAFT, Shannon sampling theorem, compactly supported window function, regularized Shannon sampling formula related to SAFT, error estimates, numerical robustness. AMS Subject Classifications : 94A20, 42A38, 65T50.



1
Introduction

The
special affine Fourier transform
(SAFT) was introduced by S. Abe and J.T. Sheridan
[
1
]
for the study of certain operations on optical
wave functions. For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, it is an integral transform of the form

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\phi_{A}(t,\omega)\,\mathrm{d}t\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(1.1)

with the kernel

\phi_{A}(t,\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,|b|}}\exp\Big[\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2b}\,\big(at^{2}+2pt-2\omega t+d\omega^{2}+2(bq-dp)\omega\Big]\,,\quad t,\,\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

depending on a vector
A=(a,b,c,d,p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
which satisfy the conditions
ad-cb=1
and
b\not=0
. The name “special affine Fourier transform” comes from
the fact that the transform (
1.1
) is related to a special affine transform of the time-frequency coordinates

\begin{pmatrix}t^{\prime}\\
\omega^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}t\\
\omega\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}\,,

where the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
is an element of the special linear group
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
, i.e., its determinant is equal to 1. We will not go into a detailed discussion of the
origin and relations of the SAFT to various fields in physics, but we take (
1.1
) merely as a signal transform. The SAFT includes a number of
well-known signal transforms as special cases. Among them are the following:

(1)

For
A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
we get back the classical
Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}
, and the set
A=(0,-1,-1,0,0,0)
gives the
inverse Fourier
transform

\mathscr{F}^{-1}
such that

\mathscr{F}f(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\textnormal{d}t\,,\quad\mathscr{F}^{-1}f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\omega)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\textnormal{d}\omega\,.

For
A=(0,1,-1,0,p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
we obtain the
offset Fourier transform
.

(2)

For
A=(a,b,c,d,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
with
ad-cb=1
and
abd\not=0
, we get the
quadratic Fourier transform

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,|b|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2b}(at^{2}-2\omega t+d\omega^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

which is known in quantum physics as the
canonical linear transform
. Special cases of a linear canonical transform are given by

A=(1,\lambda,0,1,0,0)
with
\lambda\not=0

(
Fresnel transform
), by
A=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta),-\sin(\theta),\cos(\theta),0,0)
with
\theta\notin\pi\,\mathbb{Z}
(
fractional Fourier transform
), and by
A=(\cosh(\theta),\sinh(\theta),\sinh(\theta),\cosh(\theta),0,0)
with
\theta\not=0
(
hyperbolic transform
).
For
A=(\cos(\theta),\sin(\theta),-\sin(\theta),\cos(\theta),p,q)\in\mathbb{R}^{6}
with
\theta\notin\pi\,\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain the
offset fractional Fourier transform
.

As a matter of fact the ordinary translation operator
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
does not interact nicely with the kernel of the SAFT (
1.1
), i.e., the function

T_{x}\phi_{A}(t,\omega)=\phi_{A}(t-x,\omega)
is in general different from
\phi_{A}(t,\omega)\,\phi_{A}(-x,\omega)
except if
A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
. As a consequence, working
with the ordinary translation operator a number of facts known from ordinary Fourier analysis are no longer valid. This holds in particular for the important Shannon sampling theorem, which allows the reconstruction of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with Fourier transform
\mathscr{F}f
supported in
[-\pi,\pi]

from its samples at integer points using ordinary translates of the cardinal sine function, viz.

f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\ \mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(1.2)

In case that the SAFT of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is supported on a compact interval, formula (
1.2
) does in general no longer hold. In order to get an analogue
for (
1.2
) in case of the SAFT it is necessary to work with a different concept of translations.
In
[
11
]
a generalized translation operator
T^{A}_{x}
was introduced which suits the SAFT and its various consequences for the related harmonic
analysis were studied. This operator reads as

T^{A}_{x}f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{b}x(t-x)}\,f(t-x)\,,\quad t,\,x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

It obviously reduces to the ordinary translation in case that
A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
.
Moreover, it was demonstrated in
[
11
]
that crucial facts from Fourier analysis, as for example the convolution theorem, hold for the SAFT
if we use with the operator
T^{A}_{x}
instead of
T_{x}
. In
[
8
]
A. Bhandari and A.I. Zayed also studied the SAFT in some detail and worked out a number of aspects of this transform. In
[
4
]
the SAFT and related modulation spaces were studied in detail. Furthermore, in
[
8
]
and later in
[
11
]
shift invariant spaces related to
T^{A}_{x}
were
studied and, in particular, an analogue of the Shannon sampling theorem was derived. It states that a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with

\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)\subseteq[-|b|\,\pi+p,|b|\,\pi+p]
can be reconstructed from its samples on integer points as

f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\,f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(1.3)

Although this representation is quite satisfactory from a theoretical point of view it suffers in the same manner as the ordinary Shannon formula
(
1.2
) from some computational shortcomings. Apart from the obvious problem of using infinitely many samples, the slow decay of the
cardinal sine function prevents a good approximation of
f
by truncation of the series. In order to mitigate these flaws, the cardinal sine function is
multiplied by a suitable window function
\varphi
. Hence instead of
\mathrm{sinc}
, the function
\varphi\cdot\mathrm{sinc}
is used for the reconstruction of a function

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)\subseteq[-|b|\,\delta+p,|b|\,\delta+p]
, where
0<\delta\leq\pi
. In the classical situation, i.e. where

A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
, localization by a suitable window function and oversampling was used as regularization strategies for the problem. There is a huge stack
of papers dealing with the problem of a stable and robust computation of the sampling series, see
[
10
,
28
,
29
,
21
,
14
,
15
]
and references therein. The Gaussian
function was frequently used as a window. However, it turned out that certain compactly supported window function lead to much better approximation
results
[
14
]
. Motivated by this observation we concentrate our studies in this paper on a suitable class of compactly supported window functions
as well. We consider in particular three types of window functions in detail an analyse there approximation behaviour.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we recall all necessary properties of the special affine Fourier transform. Section 3 provides the results on
sampling for the special affine Fourier transform. Regularization of the Shannon sampling formula will be considered in Section 4. A detailed study of
regularization by specific window functions will be presented in Section 5. We will complete the paper by Section 6 which is dedicated to numerical
experiments.

2
Special affine Fourier transform

In this section we shall define the special affine Fourier transform (SAFT) more precisely and we will discuss the relevant properties of this transform.
Before doing so, let us briefly recall the definition of function spaces which are relevant for us in the sequel. By
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
we denote the Banach space of
continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
which vanish at infinity equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|
, and
C_{c}(\mathbb{R})
denotes the subspace of
continuous functions with compact support. The spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
are defined as usual with their respective norm

\|f\|_{p}=\begin{cases}\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|^{p}\,\,\textnormal{d}t\Big)^{1/p}&1\leq p<\infty,\\
ess\,\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|f(t)|&p=\infty.\end{cases}

The inner product for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.

The special affine Fourier transform (SAFT) of a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,|b|}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2b}\,\big(at^{2}+2pt-2\omega t+d\omega^{2}+2(bq-dp)\omega\big)}\,\mathrm{d}t,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R},

(2.1)

where
A=(a,b,c,d,p,q)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6}
is a fixed vector with
ad-bc=1
and
b\not=0
. We exclude the limit case
b=0
, as this would make it
necessary to define the transform in a distributional sense, a setup which we will not consider. For comprehensive studies of the properties of the
SAFT we refer to
[
8
]
,
[
4
]
, and
[
11
]
.

The SAFT can be written in a more convenient form using the auxiliary functions

\eta_{A}(\omega)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2b}(d\omega^{2}+2(bq-dp)\omega)}\,,\quad\rho_{A}(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2b}(at^{2}+2pt)}\,,\quad\omega,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(2.2)

The SAFT now reads as

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)=\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|b|}}\ \mathscr{F}(f\,\rho_{A})\big(\frac{\omega}{b}\big)\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(2.3)

where
\mathscr{F}
stands for the ordinary Fourier transform, given by (
2.1
) with
A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
.
As
\eta_{A}
and
\rho_{A}
are
unimodular functions, i.e.,
|\eta_{A}(\omega)|=1=|\rho_{A}(t)|
for all
t,\,\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, we immediately get from (
2.3
) that
\mathscr{F}_{A}f
belongs to

C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|\mathscr{F}_{A}f\|_{\infty}\leq(2\pi|b|)^{-1/2}\,\|f\|_{1}
. Moreover, (
2.3
) also shows that
\mathscr{F}_{A}
can be extended to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
defines a unitary operator on that space, viz.

\langle\mathscr{F}_{A}f,\mathscr{F}_{A}g\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle,\quad f,\,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.4)

In particular,
\|\mathscr{F}_{A}f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The inverse of
\mathscr{F}_{A}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
can readily be obtained from (
2.3
). It is given as

\mathscr{F}_{A}^{-1}f(t)=\frac{\bar{\rho}_{A}(t)}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\omega)\,\bar{\eta}_{A}(\omega)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t/b}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

(2.5)

In
[
11
]
, the authors introduced a generalized translation operator related to the SAFT which was called
A
-
translation
.
For a function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
it reads as

T^{A}_{x}f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{b}x(t-x)}\,f(t-x),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.6)

Obviously,
T^{A}_{x}
is norm preserving on all spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),\ 1\leq p\leq\infty
, and it reduces to the ordinary translation
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
if

A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
. However, note that
T^{A}_{x}(f\cdot g)
is in general different from
(T^{A}_{x}f)\cdot(T^{A}_{x}g)
. The following statements regarding the
generalized translation operator
T^{A}_{x}
can be found in
[
11
]
. The proofs of these statements are rather straightforward and we will omit them
here.

Proposition 2.1
.

For the
A
-translation
T^{A}_{x}
and any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the following properties hold

(i)

T^{A}_{x}\,T^{A}_{y}=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{b}xy}\,T^{A}_{x+y},\quad x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(ii)

\mathscr{F}_{A}(T^{A}_{x}f)(\omega)=\rho_{A}(x)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega x/b}\,\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega),\quad x,\,\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,.

With help of the
A
-translation we define the
A
-
convolution
of two functions
f,\,g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
as

(f\star_{A}g)(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,T^{A}_{x}g(t)\ \mathrm{d}x\,.

Then we have
f\star_{A}g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\|f\star_{A}g\|_{1}\leq(2\pi|b|)^{-1/2}\|f\|_{1}\|g\|_{1}
. Moreover,

\mathscr{F}_{A}(f\star_{A}g)(\omega)=\bar{\eta}_{A}(\omega)\,\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)\,\mathscr{F}_{A}g(\omega)\,.

Again, the latter two equations reduce to the classical expression in case
A=(0,1,-1,0,0,0)
, i.e.,

(f\star g)(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\,g(t-x)\ \mathrm{d}x\,,\quad\mathscr{F}(f\star g)(\omega)=\mathscr{F}f(\omega)\,\mathscr{F}g(\omega).

The proofs of these facts can be found in
[
11
]
.

It is convenient to define to introduce the
chirp modulation operator

C_{s}f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}st^{2}}f(t)\,,\quad s,\,t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(2.7)

The A-translation operator and the chirp modulation operator do obviously not commute. We have

T_{x}^{A}C_{s}f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}(t-x)[(2\frac{a}{b}+s)x-s\,t]}f(t-x),

(2.8)

and if
s=-\frac{a}{b}
we get

T^{A}_{x}\,C_{-\frac{a}{b}}\,f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-x^{2})}\,f(t-x)\,.

(2.9)

3
Shannon sampling series for SAFT

In
[
11
]
, an analogue of the Shannon sampling theorem for the SAFT was obtained. In order to formulate the sampling theorem, let us introduce some
notation. For
0<\delta\leq\pi
let
I_{\delta}=[-\delta|b|,\,\delta|b|]
and define the subspace
B^{A}_{p,\delta}
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as

B^{A}_{p,\delta}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)\subseteq p+I_{\delta}\}\,.

This space is a generalization of the space of ordinary
\delta
-bandlimited functions
B_{\delta}=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F}f)\subseteq[-\delta,\,\delta]\}
to the case
of the SAFT. Between the spaces
B_{\delta}
and
B_{p,\delta}^{A}
, the following close relations hold:

Proposition 3.1
.

If
g\in B_{\delta}
, then
f=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g
belongs to
B^{A}_{p,\delta}
.

If
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
, then it holds
g=C_{\frac{a}{b}}f\in B_{\delta}
.

Proof.
Assume that
g\in B_{\delta}
. Using (
2.2
), (
2.3
), and (
2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{A}(C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g)(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|b|}}\,\mathscr{F}\big((C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g)\,\rho_{A}\big)\big(\frac{\omega}{b}\big)

(3.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{2\pi\,|b|}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{\omega-p}{b}\,t}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|b|}}\,(\mathscr{F}g)(\textstyle{\frac{\omega-p}{b}})\,.

From
g\in B_{\delta}
it follows that
(\mathscr{F}g)(\textstyle{\frac{\omega-p}{b}})=0
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
with
|\omega-p|>\delta\,|b|
, i.e.,
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}\setminus(p+I_{\delta})
. Hence for
f=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g
equation (
3.1
) gives

\mathrm{supp}\,(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)\subseteq p+I_{\delta}\,,

that means
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
.

Now we consider
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
. Applying (
2.2
) and (
2.7
), we conclude

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(f\,\rho_{A})\big(\frac{\omega}{b}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\,\rho_{A}(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{\omega}{b}\,t}\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}}(C_{\frac{a}{b}}f)(t)\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{\omega-p}{b}\,t}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}(C_{\frac{a}{b}}f)\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)\,.

Then from (
2.3
) it follows that

(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)(\omega)=\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|b|}}\,\mathscr{F}(f\,\rho_{A})\big(\frac{\omega}{b}\big)=\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{\sqrt{|b|}}\,\mathscr{F}(C_{\frac{a}{b}}f)\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)\,.

(3.2)

By assumption it holds

\mathrm{supp}\,(\mathscr{F}_{A}f)\subseteq p+I_{\delta}\,.

Hence by (
3.2
) and
|\eta_{A}(\omega)|=1
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\mathrm{supp}\,\mathscr{F}(C_{\frac{a}{b}}f)\big(\frac{\cdot-p}{b}\big)\subseteq p+I_{\delta}\,,

that means

\mathrm{supp}\,\mathscr{F}(C_{\frac{a}{b}}f)\subseteq[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

Thus
g=C_{\frac{a}{b}}f
belongs to
B_{\delta}
.

Therefore the mapping
\mathscr{I}:\,B_{\delta}\to B_{p,\delta}^{A}
with
\mathscr{I}g=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g
for each
g\in B_{\delta}

is a linear isometry, since we have

\|\mathscr{I}g\|_{2}=\|C_{-\frac{a}{b}}g\|_{2}=\|g\|_{2}

for all
g\in B_{\delta}
.

Note that the equivalence class of functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathscr{F}(C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f))\subseteq[-\delta,\delta]
always contains a smooth function.
Indeed, for any
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the function
(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{r}\,\mathscr{F}(C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f)(\omega)
is in
L^{1}([-\delta,\delta])
which implies that

(C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f)^{(r)}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\mathscr{F}(C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f)(\omega)\,(\mathrm{i}\omega)^{r}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\textnormal{d}\omega

belongs to
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
. Clearly, with
C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f
also
f
is smooth. In the sequel we will always pick the smooth representative from the equivalence
class
C_{-\frac{a}{b}}f
.

If
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
0<\delta<\pi
, we will speak about
oversampling
of
f
by
\{f(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
The cardinal sine function is defined as usual by

\mathrm{sinc}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{\sin(\pi t)}{\pi t}\,,&t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}\,,\\
1\,,&t=0\,.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Define
\psi(t)=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}\mathrm{sinc}\,(t)
. As
\mathrm{sinc}\in B_{\pi}
we have
\psi\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
in view of Proposition
3.1
. The following
Shannon sampling theorem for the SAFT was proved in
[
8
]
and in
[
11
]
.
For some special SAFT’s, corresponding sampling theorems
were studied mainly in the signal processing literature. In particular sampling theorems for the Fresnel transform
[
12
]
, fractional Fourier transform

[
35
,
7
,
9
]
, and the linear canonical transform
[
34
,
31
,
26
]
were studied before. However, they are all straightforward
consequences of following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
, then it holds

f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,T^{A}_{n}\,\psi(t)

(3.4)

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Using the relation (
2.9
), the sampling series (
3.4
) can be rewritten as

f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n).

(3.5)

In order to obtain the convergence of the truncated sampling series

S_{N}f(t)=\sum_{n=-N}^{N}f(n)\,T^{A}_{n}\psi(t)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

to the function
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
for
N\to\infty
, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\pi]
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
be given. Then we have

\|f\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{2}.

(3.6)

Proof.
Since

\langle T^{A}_{n}\psi,T^{A}_{m}\psi\rangle=\langle T_{n}\,\mathrm{sinc},T_{m}\,\mathrm{sinc}\rangle=\delta_{n,m}

for all
n,\,m\in\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\{T^{A}_{n}\psi:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
we get from Theorem
3.2

f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,T^{A}_{n}\psi(t)\,.

This leads to

\langle f,f\rangle=\Big\langle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,T^{A}_{n}\psi,\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}f(m)\,T^{A}_{m}\psi\Big\rangle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|^{2}.

We are now prepared to prove the desired convergence result for the truncated sampling series.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\pi]
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
be given. Then we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\,\|f-S_{N}f\|_{\infty}=0\,.

(3.7)

Moreover, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
the sampling series (
3.4
) converges absolutely and we have

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|f(n)|\,|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)|\leq\|f\|_{2}\,.

(3.8)

Proof.
Using Theorem
3.2
we get

f(t)-S_{N}f(t)=\sum_{|n|>N}f(n)\,T^{A}_{n}\psi(t)\,.

The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\displaystyle\big|f(t)-S_{N}f(t)\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{|n|>N}|f(n)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{|n|>N}\big|T_{n}^{A}\psi(t)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{|n|>N}|f(n)|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{|n|>N}\big|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

Since

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)|^{2}=1\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(3.10)

the second factor of (
3.9
) converges to zero uniformly with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}
as
N\to\infty
. In view of
(
3.6
) the first factor converges to zero for
N\to\infty
. This implies (
3.7
).

The statement about the absolute convergence of the sampling series (
3.4
) resp. (
3.5
) and the estimate (
3.8
) follow again
by applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
3.6
), and (
3.10
).

Although, the reconstruction of
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
from samples
f(n),n\in\mathbb{Z}
, is possible according to Theorem
3.2
, it is not a stable process.
In order to demonstrate this consider
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,\pi]
. In view of Proposition
3.1
we may without lost of generality assume that
f

is smooth. For a sufficiently large
N\in\mathbb{N}
define erroneous samples of
f(n)
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, by setting

\tilde{f}(n)=\begin{cases}f(n)+\varepsilon_{n}&-N\leq n\leq N\,,\\
f(n)&\mbox{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

with error terms
\varepsilon_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
. We obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{f}(t)&=\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{f}(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\\[12.91663pt]
&=f(t)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\varepsilon_{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.\end{array}

Thus an upper estimate is given by

\|\tilde{f}-f\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\,\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}|\sin(t-n)|

By
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
we have

\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{n=-N}^{N}|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)|<\frac{2}{\pi}[\,\ln(N)+2\ln(2)+\gamma\,]+\frac{N+2}{\pi N(N+1)}\,,

with Euler’s constant
[
33
]

\gamma:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{n}-\ln(N)\Big)=0.57721566\dots\,.

We shall now give a lower bound for the approximation of
f
utilizing perturbed sample values. The result shows in particular that the reconstruction of

f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
by the Shannon sampling series (
3.4
) resp. (
3.5
) is numerically unstable.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\pi]
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
be given. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
define

\varepsilon_{n}=\varepsilon\ \mathrm{sign}\big(\mathrm{sinc}({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}-n)\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{8b}(4n^{2}-1)}=\varepsilon\,(-1)^{n+1}\mathrm{sign}(2n-1)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{8b}(4n^{2}-1)}

for
-N\leq n\leq N
and
\varepsilon_{n}=0
for
|n|>N
. Then it holds

\|\tilde{f}-f\|_{\infty}\geq\varepsilon\,\big(\frac{2}{\pi}\ln(N)+\frac{4}{\pi}\,\ln(2)+\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\big)>\varepsilon\big(\frac{2}{\pi}\ln(N)+\frac{5}{4}\big)\,.

Proof.
The special choice of the error term leads to

\tilde{f}(t)-f(t)=\varepsilon\,\sum_{n=-N}^{N}\mathrm{sign}\big(\mathrm{sinc}({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}-n)\big)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{8b}(4n^{2}-1)}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(n^{2}-t^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,.

For
t=\frac{1}{2}
we obtain

\|\tilde{f}-f\|_{\infty}\geq|\tilde{f}({\textstyle\frac{1}{2})-f(\frac{1}{2})}|=\varepsilon\,\sum_{k=-N}^{N}|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)|=\frac{2\varepsilon}{(2N+1)\pi}+\frac{4\varepsilon}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2n-1}\,.

From
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
we get

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{2n-1}>\frac{1}{2}\ln(N)+\ln(2)+\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{1}{4N(2N+1)},

which yields

\|\tilde{f}-f\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon\,\big(\frac{2}{\pi}\ln(N)+\frac{4}{\pi}\ln(2)+\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\big)+\varepsilon\,\frac{2N+1}{\pi\,N(2N+1)}>\varepsilon\,\big(\frac{2}{\pi}\ln(N)+\frac{4}{\pi}\ln(2)+\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}\big).

Since
\frac{4}{\pi}\ln(2)+\frac{2\gamma}{\pi}=1.2500093\dots>\frac{5}{4}
, we get the final estimate.

4
Regularized Shannon sampling formulas for SAFT

The practical use of the Shannon sampling series (
3.5
) for SAFT is rather limited due to the need of infinitely many samples. A second limitation of its use in
practice originates in the fact that the cardinal sine function decays very slowly, which results in poor convergence of the sampling series. Furthermore, in case
of noisy samples
f(n)
the convergence of the Shannon sampling series can even break down completely (see
[
10
,
15
]
). In order to overcome these limitations, regularization
techniques were considered. For the classical Fourier transform, the concept of regularized Shannon sampling formulas with localized sampling and oversampling has been studied
by several authors
[
22
,
Q04
,
24
,
25
,
30
,
16
,
14
,
15
]
. Often a Gaussian window function supported on whole
\mathbb{R}
was used in these studies.
In
[
14
]
, it was shown that the compactly supported
\sinh
-type window function (
4.1
) produces smaller approximation errors than the Gaussian window function.
Therefore we focus on compactly supported window functions in the following.

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
let
\Phi_{m}
be the class of even continuous functions
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
with the following properties:

(i)

\varphi
is supported on
[-m,m]
,

(ii)

\varphi
is monotonically decreasing on
[0,m]
with
\varphi(0)=1
,

(iii)

the Fourier transform of
\varphi
is explicitly known.

A function from the class
\Phi_{m}
will henceforth be called
window function
. The following window functions will be considered in the remaining part
of the paper.

Example 4.1
.

(a)

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
denote by
M_{m}
the centered cardinal
\mathrm{B}
-spline of order
m
which is defined recursively by

M_{m}(t)=\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}M_{m-1}(t-\tau)\,\textnormal{d}\tau,\ m\geq 1,\quad M_{1}(t)=\mathds{1}_{[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]}.

Note that
M_{m}
is supported on
[-\frac{m}{2},\frac{m}{2}]
. For even order
2s\in 2\mathbb{N}
we define the
\mathrm{B}
-spline window function as

\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}(t)=\frac{1}{M_{2s}(0)}\,M_{2s}\Big(\frac{s\,t}{2\pi m}\Big)\,.

(4.1)

(b)

The
\sinh
-type window function is defined for a positive parameter
\beta
as

\varphi_{\sinh}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sinh\beta}\sinh\big(\beta\sqrt{1-(\frac{t}{2\pi m})^{2}}\big)\,,&t\in[-2\pi\,m,2\pi\,m]\,,\\
0\,,&\mbox{elsewhere}\,.\end{cases}

(4.2)

(c)

Let again
\beta
be a positive number. The continuous Kaiser-Bessel window function is defined as

\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}(t)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\Big(I_{0}\big(\beta\sqrt{1-(\frac{t}{2\pi m})^{2}}\big)-1\Big)\,,&t\in[-2\pi\,m,2\pi\,m]\,,\\
0\,,&\mbox{elsewhere}\,,\end{cases}

(4.3)

where
I_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the first kind, given by
[
32
]

I_{0}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k)!!)^{2}}\,x^{2k}\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Particular cases of the above-indicated window functions and a regularized version of the sinc function are depicted in Figure
4.1
.

:
(a) Window functions from Example
4.1
for the window size
m=2
.

We will consider the following regularization strategies for Shannon sampling series of SAFT:

1.

The sampling series (
3.5
) will be regularized by a window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
, viz., we consider instead of (
3.5
) the
regularized Shannon sampling formula for the
SAFT
\mathscr{F}_{A}
given by

R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(4.4)

Since
\varphi
is compactly supported, the computation of
R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(t)
for fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
needs only a finite number of samples of
f
.

2.

Another established technique is to oversample the function
f
, for which we need the stronger condition
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with some
\delta\in(0,\,\pi)
.

Note that since

{\mathrm{e}}^{-{\mathrm{i}}\,\frac{a}{2b}\,(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi(t-n)\big|_{t=k}=\delta_{n,k}\,,\quad n,\,k\in\mathbb{Z}\,.

we have the following interpolation property

R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(k)=f(k)\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

for
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
.

Furthermore, note that the use of a window function
\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
implies that the computation of
R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(t)
for

t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
requires only
2m
samples
f(n)
, where
n\in\mathbb{Z}
fulfills
|n-t|<m
. Hence the function
f
can be recovered on the interval
[0,\,1]
by

f(t)=\begin{cases}f(0)\,,&t=0\,,\\
f(1)\,,&t=1\,,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1-m}^{m}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi(t-n)\,,&t\in(0,\,1)\,.\end{cases}

Thus the reconstruction of
f
on the interval
[-N,N]

with
N\in\mathbb{Z}
needs only
2N+2m-1
samples
f(n)
with
n=1-N-m,\,\dots,\,N+m-1
.

Now we give an estimate for the uniform approximation error
\|f-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f\|_{\infty}
for the approximation of
f
by
R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,\pi]
be given. Further let
\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
. Then we have

\|f-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f\|_{\infty}\leq E(m,\delta)\ \|f\|_{2}\,,

with

E(m,\delta)=\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\ \max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\Big|1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Big|.

(4.5)

Proof.
We shall compute the SAFT of the error function

e(t)=f(t)-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(t)\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Applying
\mathscr{F}_{A}
to the representation (
3.4
) and using Proposition
2.1
(ii) leads to

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\rho_{A}(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\omega/b}\,\mathscr{F}_{A}\psi(\omega)

with
\psi(t)=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t)
.
A straightforward computation shows

\mathscr{F}_{A}\psi(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\,\eta_{A}(\omega)\,\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)

with the indicator function
\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
of the interval
[-\pi,\,\pi]
. Hence it holds

\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\,\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)\,\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\rho_{A}(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\omega/b}.

For the computation of the SAFT of
R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f
, let

\chi(t)=C_{-\frac{a}{b}}\big(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc}\big)(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}}\,\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{sinc}(t)\,.

The SAFT of the function
\chi
can be computed using (
3.1
),
we obtain

\mathscr{F}_{A}\chi(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,|b|}}\,\eta_{A}(\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{sinc}(t)\,\varphi(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}(\omega-p)t/b}\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|b|}}\,\eta_{A}(\omega)\ \mathscr{F}(\varphi\,\mathrm{sinc})\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big).

The convolution property of
\mathscr{F}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
14
, Lemma 2.2]
) gives
(\mathscr{F}\varphi)\ast(\mathscr{F}\mathrm{sinc})=\mathscr{F}(\varphi\cdot\mathrm{sinc})
,
and since
\mathscr{F}\mathrm{sinc}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}
and
\mathscr{F}\varphi=\hat{\varphi}
, we get

\mathscr{F}(\varphi\cdot\mathrm{sinc})(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,\big(\hat{\varphi}\ast\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}\big)(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Hence we arrive at

\mathscr{F}_{A}(R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f)(\omega)=\frac{\eta_{A}(\omega)}{2\pi\sqrt{|b|}}\int_{-\pi+(\omega-p)/b}^{\pi+(\omega-p)/b}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\ \sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\rho_{A}(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}n\omega/b}\,.

Thus, with the auxiliary function

\Delta(\omega)=\mathds{1}_{[-\pi,\pi]}(\omega)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\pi+\omega}^{\pi+\omega}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(4.6)

the SAFT of the error function
e
can be written as

\mathscr{F}_{A}e(\omega)=\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)\,\Delta\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)\,.

Taking its inverse SAFT we finally obtain the following estimate

\begin{array}[]{ll}|e(t)|&=\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\bar{\rho}_{A}(t)\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathscr{F}_{A}e(\omega)\,\bar{\eta}_{A}(\omega)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t/b}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\Big|\\[17.22217pt]
&\leq\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)|\,\big|\Delta\big(\frac{\omega-p}{b}\big)\big|\,\mathrm{d}\omega.\\[17.22217pt]
\end{array}

Taking into account that
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
we arrive at

|e(t)|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi|b|}}\int_{p-|b|\,\delta}^{p+|b|\,\delta}\mathscr{|}\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)|\,\mathrm{d}\omega\ \max_{\omega^{\prime}\in[-\delta,\delta]}\Big|1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\pi+\omega^{\prime}}^{\pi+\omega^{\prime}}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Big|

(4.7)

with
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega-p)/b\in[-\delta,\delta]
. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in combination with the Parseval equality (
2.4
) gives

\int_{p-|b|\,\delta}^{p+|b|\,\delta}\mathscr{|}\mathscr{F}_{A}f(\omega)|\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq\sqrt{2\delta\,|b|}\,\|\mathscr{F}_{A}f\|_{2}=\sqrt{2\delta\,|b|}\,\|f\|_{2}\,.

With this inequality (
4.7
) finally yields

\|e\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\ \|f\|_{2}\,\max_{\omega^{\prime}\in[-\delta,\delta]}\Big|1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\pi+\omega^{\prime}}^{\pi+\omega^{\prime}}\hat{\varphi}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\Big|\,.

This completes the proof.

In the previous section we have demonstrated that the Shannon sampling series does not behave stably with respect to perturbation of the samples of the
function. Now we are considering this problem for the regularized Shannon sampling formula related to SAFT. It turns out that in contrast to the sampling series (
3.4
) resp.
(
3.5
) the regularized Shannon sampling formula (
4.2
) for SAFT is numerically robust, i.e., the uniform error

\|R^{A}_{\varphi,m}\tilde{f}-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f\|_{\infty}
is small for small perturbations of the samples of the function
f
. More precisely we have the
following statement.

Theorem 4.3
.

For
\delta\in(0,\pi]
let
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
, and let
\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
be a window function. Furthermore, let
\tilde{f}(n)=f(n)+\varepsilon_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, be noisy samples,
where the complex sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Then the following estimates
hold

\displaystyle\|R^{A}_{\varphi,m}\tilde{f}-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon\,\big(2+\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}(0)\big)\,,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|f-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f\|_{\infty}+\varepsilon\,\big(2+\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}(0)\big)\,.

(4.9)

Proof.
We consider the perturbation error

\,\tilde{e}(t)=R^{A}_{\varphi,m}\,\tilde{f}(t)-R^{A}_{\varphi,m}f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\varepsilon_{n}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(n^{2}-t^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi(t-n).

Note that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
the sum contains only finitely many non-vanishing terms. We first consider the case
t\in(0,\,1)
. Due to the properties of

\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
and the fact
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
, we obtain

|\,\tilde{e}(t)|\leq\sum_{n=1-m}^{m}|\varepsilon_{n}|\,|\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)|\,\varphi(t-n)\leq\varepsilon\,\sum_{n=1-m}^{m}\varphi(t-n)\,.

Since
\varphi\in\Phi_{m}
is monotonically decreasing on
[0,m]
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1-m}^{m}\varphi(t-n)&=\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{n=1-m}^{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big)\varphi(t-n)=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\varphi(t+n)+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\varphi(t-n)\\[17.22217pt]
&\leq\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\varphi(n)+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\varphi(1-n)=2\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\varphi(n)\,.\end{array}

The latter sum can be estimated further by applying once more the monotonicity of
\varphi

\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\varphi(n)<\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{m-1}\varphi(t)\,\textnormal{d}t\leq\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{m}\varphi(t)\,\textnormal{d}t\,.

Since
\hat{\varphi}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{0}^{m}\varphi(t)\,\textnormal{d}t
we eventually have

|\,\tilde{e}(t)|\leq 2\,\varepsilon\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\varphi(n)\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2\,\varepsilon\,\varphi(0)+\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}(0)\big)=\varepsilon\,\big(2+\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}(0)\big)

for every
t\in(0,\,1)
. Note that
|\,\tilde{e}(0)|=|\varepsilon_{0}|\leq\varepsilon
and
|\,\tilde{e}(1)|=|\varepsilon_{1}|\leq\varepsilon
, which leads to

\max_{t\in[0,1]}|\,\tilde{e}(t)|\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2+\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}(0)\big)\,.

(4.10)

The same technique can obviously be applied to get the estimate (
4.10
) for every interval
[k,k+1],\,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and thus (
4.9
) follows.

The inequality (
4.9
) can easily be derived by applying the triangular inequality and (
4.8
).

5
Specific regularized Shannon sampling formulas for SAFT

In this section we will study regularizations of the Shannon sampling series by specific window functions which were introduced in Example
4.1
.

5.1
Regularization with B-spline window function

First we consider the B-spline window function as defined in (
4.1
) with
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
s=\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.
Assume that a function
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
is given. Then the
B-
spline regularized Shannon sampling formula for

\mathscr{F}_{A}

and

f
reads as follows

R^{A}_{{\mathrm{B}},m}f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}(t-n)\,.

(5.1)

We will now show that the approximation error
\|f-R^{A}_{{\mathrm{B}},m}f\|_{\infty}
decays exponentially with respect to
m
. More precisely the following statement
holds.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}\in\Phi_{m}
be the
{\mathrm{B}}
-spline window function (
4.1
) with
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and

s=\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.
Let
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
be given.

Then it holds

\|f-R^{A}_{{\mathrm{B}},m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\,\Big(\frac{2s}{m\,(\pi-\delta)}\Big)^{2s-1}\,\|f\|_{2}\,.

(5.2)

Proof.
According to Theorem
4.2
we have

\|f-R^{A}_{{\mathrm{B}},m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\ \|f\|_{2}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}\Big|1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(\tau)\,\textnormal{d}\tau\Big|\,.

By
[
19
, p. 452]
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}M_{2s}(t)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\textnormal{d}t=\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{\omega}{2}\big)^{2s}\,,

since the cardinal sine function is defined by (
3.3
).
Hence the B-spline window function (
4.1
) has the Fourier transform

\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(\omega)=\frac{m}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,s\,M_{2s}(0)}\,\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\omega}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,,

(5.3)

which results in

1=\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(\tau)\,\textnormal{d}\tau=\frac{m}{2\pi\,s\,M_{2s}(0)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\,.

Then for
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
the auxiliary function (
4.6
) takes the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta(\omega)&=\displaystyle\frac{m}{2\pi\,s\,M_{2s}(0)}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau-\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\Big]\\[17.22217pt]
&=\displaystyle\frac{m}{2\pi\,s\,M_{2s}(0)}\Big[\int_{\pi-\omega}^{\infty}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau+\int_{\omega+\pi}^{\infty}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\Big].\end{array}

For the single integral terms we have the following estimates

\int_{\pi\pm\omega}^{\infty}\big(\mathrm{sinc}\,\frac{m\,\tau}{2s}\big)^{2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau\leq\frac{(2s)^{2s}}{m^{2s}}\int_{\pi\pm\omega}^{\infty}\tau^{-2s}\,\textnormal{d}\tau=\frac{(2s)^{2s}}{(2s-1)\,m^{2s}(\pi\pm\omega)^{2s-1}}

which gives

\Delta(\omega)\leq\frac{(2s)^{2s-1}}{(2s-1)\,m^{2s-1}\,\pi\,M_{2s}(0)}\Big[\frac{1}{(\pi-\omega)^{2s-1}}+\frac{1}{(\pi+\omega)^{2s-1}}\Big]

for all
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
. Since
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
and
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
we have
\pi-\omega,\,\pi+\omega\in[\pi-\delta,\,\pi+\delta]
. Taking into
account that the function
x^{1-2s}
is decreasing for
x>0
, we conclude

\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]}|\Delta(\omega)|\leq\frac{2\,(2s)^{2s}}{(2s-1)\,m^{2s-1}\,\pi\,M_{2s}(0)\,(\pi-\delta)^{2s-1}}\,.

Applying
[
14
, formula (5.3)]
gives

\frac{4}{3}\leq\sqrt{2s}\,M_{2s}(0)<\sqrt{\frac{6}{\pi}}\,,

(5.4)

and thus we obtain for the error term (
4.5
) the estimate

E(m,\delta)\leq\frac{3}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\ \Big(\frac{2s}{m(\pi-\delta)}\Big)^{2s-1}\,\frac{\sqrt{2s}}{2s-1}\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\ \Big(\frac{2s}{m(\pi-\delta)}\Big)^{2s-1}\,.

The assumption
s=\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil\geq 2
implies
2s-2\leq m\leq 2s-1
. By the oversampling condition
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
it holds
0<\frac{2s}{m\,(\pi-\delta)}<1
.
This completes the proof.

Now we show that for the B-spline regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.1
) for
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
, the uniform perturbation error

\|R_{{\mathrm{B}},m}^{A}f-R_{{\mathrm{B}},m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}
only grows as
{\mathcal{O}}(\sqrt{m})
, if the oversampling condition
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
is fulfilled.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\varphi_{\mathrm{B}}\in\Phi_{m}
be the
{\mathrm{B}}
-spline window function (
4.1
) with
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and

s=\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
.
Let
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
be given. Furthermore, let
\tilde{f}(n)=f(n)+\varepsilon_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, be noisy samples,
where the complex sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Then the
\mathrm{B}
-spline regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.1
) for
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
is numerically robust and it holds

\|R^{A}_{{\mathrm{B}},m}f-R_{{\mathrm{B}},m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2+\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt{m}\big)\,.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.3
we only have to determine the value
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(0)
for the B-spline window function (
4.1
). From (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) it follows that

\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(0)=\frac{m}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,s\,M_{2s}(0)}\leq\frac{3\,m}{4\,\sqrt{s\,\pi}}\,.

Due to
s=\lceil\frac{m+1}{2}\rceil
it holds
\sqrt{s}\geq\sqrt{\frac{m}{2}}
and hence

\sqrt{2\pi}\,\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{B}}(0)\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt{m}\,.

By the oversampling condition
0<\delta<\pi-\frac{2s}{m}
, the error
\|f-R_{{\mathrm{B}},m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}
is small too by Theorem
5.1
and the triangle inequality.

5.2
Regularization with sinh-type window function

Now we consider the regularization of the Shannon sampling series for the SAFT
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,\pi)
using the
\sinh
-type window function
(
4.2
) with parameter
\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
. The
\sinh
-
type regularized Shannon sampling formula for

\mathscr{F}_{A}

and

f
takes the form

R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi_{\sinh}(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(5.5)

We will demonstrate that the uniform error of approximating
f
by
R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f
decays exponentially with respect to
m
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\pi)
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
be given. Further let
\varphi_{\sinh}
be the
\sinh
-type window function as defined in (
4.2
) with parameter

\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
. Then it holds

\|f-R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-m(\pi-\delta)}\,\|f\|_{2}\,.

Proof.
According to Theorem
4.2
we have

\|f-R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\,\|f\|_{2}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}|\Delta(\omega)|

with

\Delta(\omega)=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}(\tau)\,\textnormal{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

Following
[
18
, p. 38, 7.58]
we have

\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}(\tau)=\frac{m\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{2}\sinh\beta}\cdot\begin{cases}(1-\nu^{2})^{-1/2}\,I_{1}(\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}})\,,&|\nu|<1\,,\\[8.61108pt]
(\nu^{2}-1)^{-1/2}\,J_{1}(\beta\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})\,,&|\nu|>1\end{cases}

(5.6)

with
\nu=\frac{m}{\beta}\tau
. With this change of variables the function
\Delta
now reads as

\Delta(\omega)=1-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu

with the linear increasing function
\nu_{1}(\omega)=\frac{m}{\beta}(\omega+\pi),\ \omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
. Since
\delta\in(0,\pi)
and
\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
,
we have
\nu_{1}(-\delta)=1
and
\nu_{1}(\omega)\geq 1
for all
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
.

Now let

\Delta(\omega)=\Delta_{1}(\omega)-\Delta_{2}(\omega)
with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{1}(\omega)&=1-\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\int_{-1}^{1}\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,,\\[17.22217pt]
\Delta_{2}(\omega)&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\Big(\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{-1}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\\[17.22217pt]
&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\Big(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,.\end{array}

In view of (
5.6
) these functions take the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{1}(\omega)&=1-\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2\sinh\beta}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{I_{1}(\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}})}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,,\\[17.22217pt]
\Delta_{2}(\omega)&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{2\sinh\beta}\Big(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\frac{J_{1}(\beta\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,.\end{array}

Using
[
13
, 6.681–3]
and
[
2
, 10.2.13]
, we get

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{I_{1}(\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}})}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu=\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}I_{1}(\beta\cos\,s)\,\textnormal{d}s=\pi\Big(I_{1/2}\big(\textstyle\frac{\beta}{2}\big)\Big)^{2}=\frac{4}{\beta}\Big(\sinh\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)^{2}

and hence

\Delta_{1}(\omega)=1-\frac{2\,(\sinh\frac{\beta}{2})^{2}}{\sinh\beta}=\frac{2\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{1+\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}.

By
[
13
, 6.645–1]
we have

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{1}(\beta\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,\textnormal{d}\nu=I_{1/2}\big({\textstyle\frac{\beta}{2}}\big)\,K_{1/2}\big({\textstyle\frac{\beta}{2}}\big)=\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\beta}}{\mathrm{e}^{\beta}}\,,

where
I_{1/2}
and
K_{1/2}
are modified Bessel functions of half order (see
[
2
, 10.2.13, 10.2.14, and 10.2.17]
). Numerical experiments have shown that

0<\int_{1}^{T}\frac{J_{1}\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu\leq\frac{3\,\big(1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\big)}{2\,\beta}\,.

for all
T>1
.
Thus we obtain

0\leq\Delta_{2}(\omega)\leq\frac{\beta}{2\,\sinh\beta}\,\frac{3\,\big(1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\big)}{\beta}=\frac{3\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{1+{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}\,.

Altogether the estimate

|\Delta(\omega)\big|\leq\big|\Delta_{1}(\omega)-\Delta_{2}(\omega)\big|\leq\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{1+{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}<{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}

holds for all
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
. This implies

E(m,\delta)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)},

which completes the proof.

Now we show that for the
\sinh
-type regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.5
) with respect to
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
, the uniform perturbation
error
\|R_{\sinh,m}^{A}f-R_{\sinh,m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}
only grows as
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
, if the oversampling condition
0<\delta\leq\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}
is fulfilled.

Theorem 5.4
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
\delta\in\big(0,\,\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}\big]
, let
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
be given.
Let
\varphi_{\sinh}\in\Phi_{m}
be the
\sinh
-type window function (
4.2
) with
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
.
Furthermore, let
\tilde{f}(n)=f(n)+\varepsilon_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, be noisy samples,
where the complex sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Then the
\sinh
-type regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.5
) for
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
is numerically robust and it holds

\|R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f-R_{\sinh,m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2+\frac{3}{2}\,m\big)\,.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.3
we only have to determine the value
\hat{\varphi}_{\sinh}(0)
for the
\sinh
-type window function (
4.2
). From (
5.6
) it follows that

{\hat{\varphi}}_{\sinh}(0)=\frac{m\,\sqrt{\pi}\,I_{1}(\beta)}{\sqrt{2}\,\sinh\beta}\,.

Applying the inequality
\sqrt{2\pi\beta}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\,I_{1}(\beta)<1
(see
[
21
, Lemma 7]
), we find that

{\hat{\varphi}}_{\sinh}(0)<\frac{m\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\beta}}{2\,\sqrt{\beta}\,\sinh\beta}=\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{\pi-\delta}\,\big(1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\beta}\big)}\,.

Under the oversampling condition
0<\delta\leq\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}
with
m\geq 2
it holds
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)\geq\pi
and hence

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi-\delta}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{m}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,.

Therefore by Theorem
4.3
we can estimate

\|R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f-R_{\sinh,m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\beta}}\,m\big)\,.

From
\beta\geq\pi
it follows that

\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\beta}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi}}<\frac{3}{2}\,.

This completes the proof.

5.3
Regularization with continuous Kaiser-Bessel window function

Finally we consider the regularization of the Shannon sampling series for the SAFT
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,\pi)
using the continuous Kaiser-Bessel window function
(
4.3
) with parameter
\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
. The
continuous Kaiser-Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula for

\mathscr{F}_{A}

and

f
takes the form

R^{A}_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f(t)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f(n)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}(t^{2}-n^{2})}\,\mathrm{sinc}(t-n)\,\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}(t-n)\,,\quad t\in\mathbb{R}\,.

(5.7)

Now we show that the uniform approximation error
\|f-R^{A}_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\|_{\infty}
decays exponentially with respect to
m
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\pi)
and
f\in B^{A}_{p,\delta}
be given. Further let
\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}
be the continuous Kaiser-Bessel window function
(
4.3
) with parameter
\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
. Then

\|f-R^{A}_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\,\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\|f\|_{2}.

Proof.
From Theorem
4.2
it follows that

\|f-R^{A}_{\mathrm{cKB},m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{\pi}}\,\|f\|_{2}\,\max_{\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]}|\Delta(\omega)|

with

\Delta(\omega)=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\omega-\pi}^{\omega+\pi}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\tau)\,\textnormal{d}\tau\,,\quad\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]\,.

According to
[
18
, p. 3, 1.1, and p. 95, 18.31]
, the Fourier transform of (
4.3
) has the form

\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(\tau)=\frac{\sqrt{2}\,m}{\sqrt{\pi}\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\cdot\begin{cases}\Big(\frac{\sinh(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}})}{\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\Big)\,,&0<|\nu|<1\,,\\[17.22217pt]
\Big(\frac{\sin(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\Big)\,,&|\nu|>1,\end{cases}

(5.8)

with
\nu=\frac{m}{\beta}\tau
. With this substitution the function
\Delta
now reads as

\Delta(\omega)=1-\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu

with the linear increasing function
\nu_{1}(\omega)=\frac{m}{\beta}(\omega+\pi)
for
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
. Since
\delta\in(0,\pi)
and
\beta=m(\pi-\delta)
,
we have
\nu_{1}(-\delta)=1
and
\nu_{1}(\omega)\geq 1
for all
\omega\in[-\delta,\delta]
.

Now we split

\Delta(\omega)=\Delta_{1}(\omega)-\Delta_{2}(\omega)
with

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{1}(\omega)&=1-\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\int_{-1}^{1}\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,,\\[17.22217pt]
\Delta_{2}(\omega)&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\Big(\int_{-\nu_{1}(-\omega)}^{-1}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\\[17.22217pt]
&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,m}\Big(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}\big(\frac{\beta}{m}\nu\big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,.\end{array}

Using (
5.8
), these functions take the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{1}(\omega)&=1-\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\pi\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\int_{-1}^{1}\Big(\frac{\sinh(\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}})}{\beta\,\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\Big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,,\\[17.22217pt]
\Delta_{2}(\omega)&=\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\pi\,(I_{0}(\beta)-1)}\Big(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\Big)\Big(\frac{\sin(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1})}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\Big)\,\textnormal{d}\nu\,.\end{array}

By
[
13
, 3.997–1]
we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sinh\big(\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\,\big)}{\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\beta}\,\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh\big(\beta\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}\,\big)}{\sqrt{1-\nu^{2}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\beta}\,\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sinh(\beta\cos\sigma)\,{\mathrm{d}}\sigma=\frac{\pi}{\beta}\,{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)\,,

where
{\textbf{L}}_{0}
denotes the
modified Struve function
given by (see
[
2
, 12.2.1]
)

{\textbf{L}}_{0}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{2k+1}}{\big(\Gamma\big(k+\tfrac{3}{2}\big)\big)^{2}}=\frac{2x}{\pi}\,\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2k}}{\big((2k+1)!!\big)^{2}}\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,.

Note that the function
I_{0}(x)-{\textbf{L}}_{0}(x)
is completely monotonic on
[0,\,\infty)
(see
[
5
, Theorem 1]
) and tends to zero as
x\to\infty
.
Applying the
sine integral function

\mathrm{Si}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sin t}{t}\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\,,

implies

\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu=2\,\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu=\frac{2}{\beta}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\,.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(\omega)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg({\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\bigg)=\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg(I_{0}(\beta)-{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)\bigg)\,.

By numerical test it can be shown that for
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
it holds

0<I_{0}(\beta)-{\textbf{L}}_{0}(\beta)-1+\frac{2}{\pi}\,\mathrm{Si}(\beta)<1\,.

Further it is known that
I_{0}(x)\geq 1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
0<\Delta_{1}(\omega)<\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}
for
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
.

Now we estimate
\Delta_{2}(\omega)
for
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
by the triangle inequality as

|\Delta_{2}(\omega)|\leq\frac{\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\bigg(\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(-\omega)}+\int_{1}^{\nu_{1}(\omega)}\bigg)\Big|\frac{\sin\big(\beta\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\,\nu}\Big|\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu\,.

By
[
21
, Lemma 4]
, we have for
\nu\geq 1
that

\Big|\frac{\sin\big(\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}\big)}{\beta\,\sqrt{\nu^{2}-1}}-\frac{\sin(\beta\nu)}{\beta\nu}\Big|\leq\frac{2}{\nu^{2}}\,.

Thus we conclude that

|\Delta_{2}(\nu)|\leq\frac{4\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\nu^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\nu=\frac{4\beta}{\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,.

Therefore we obtain for
\omega\in[-\delta,\,\delta]
that

|\Delta(\omega)|\leq\Delta_{1}(\omega)+|\Delta_{2}(\omega)|\leq\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}\,\bigg(1+\frac{4\beta}{\pi}\bigg)\,.

Since the function
{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,I_{0}(x)
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\,\infty)
and tends to zero as
x\to\infty
(see
[
3
]
), we have

\frac{1}{I_{0}(\beta)-1}=\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\,I_{0}(\beta)-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}=\frac{1}{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\,I_{0}(\beta)-{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-m\,(\pi-\delta)}\,.

Thus, the approximation error of the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon formula (
5.7
) with the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
4.3
) decreases exponentially with respect to
m
.

Finally we show that for the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.7
) related to
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
, the uniform perturbation error

\|R_{\mathrm{cKB},m}^{A}f-R_{\mathrm{cKB},m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}
only grows as
{\mathcal{O}}(m)
, if
\delta
fulfills the oversampling condition
0<\delta\leq\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}
.

Theorem 5.6
.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}
and
\delta\in\big(0,\,\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}\big]
, let
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
be given.
Let
\varphi_{\mathrm{cKB}}\in\Phi_{m}
be the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
4.3
) with
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)
.
Furthermore, let
\tilde{f}(n)=f(n)+\varepsilon_{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, be noisy samples,
where the complex sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies
|\varepsilon_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Then the continuous Kaiser–Bessel regularized Shannon sampling formula (
5.7
) for
\mathscr{F}_{A}
and
f\in B_{p,\delta}^{A}
is numerically robust
and

\|R^{A}_{\sinh,m}f-R_{\sinh,m}^{A}\tilde{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon\,\big(2+\frac{7}{4}\,m\big)\,.

Proof.
By Theorem
4.3
we only have to estimate the value
\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(0)
for the continuous Kaiser–Bessel window function (
4.3
). From (
5.8
) it follows that

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{\mathrm{cKB}}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}\,m}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\Big(\frac{\sinh\beta}{\beta}-1\Big)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\beta\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,\big(1-{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\beta}-2\beta\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-\beta}\big)

(5.10)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{m\,{\mathrm{e}}^{\beta}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\beta\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\,.

(5.11)

By
[
2
, p. 377, 9.7.1]
one knows that

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt{2\pi x}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,I_{0}(x)=1

and hence also

\lim_{x\to\infty}\sqrt{2\pi x}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\big(I_{0}(x)-1\big)=1\,.

Numerical calculation shows that for all
x\geq\pi
it holds

\sqrt{2\pi x}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-x}\,\big(I_{0}(x)-1\big)\geq\sqrt{2}\,\pi\,\big(I_{0}(\pi)-1\big)\,.

By the oversampling condition
\delta\in\big(0,\,\pi-\frac{\pi}{m}\big)
we obtain
\beta=m\,(\pi-\delta)\geq\pi
.
Therefore we can estimate

\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{\beta}}{\sqrt{2\beta}\,\big(I_{0}(\beta)-1\big)}\leq\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{\pi}}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\big(I_{0}(\pi)-1\big)}=1.163167956\,.

Since
1.163167956\times\sqrt{2}<\frac{7}{4}
, the above estimate of the perturbation error is shown.

\int_{\mathbb{R}}S_{k,h}(t)S_{\ell,h}(t)\mathrm{d}t=h\delta_{k,\ell},\quad k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

We choose as an auxiliary function, the function
g
defined as the following sum

g(t)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{5h}}\big(S_{0,h}(t)+\frac{1}{2}S_{1,h}(t)\big),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

It is easy to check that
g
is normalized, that is
\int_{\mathbb{R}}g^{2}(t)\mathrm{d}t=1
. Using
g
, the test function
f
of our choice will be defined by the following relation

f(t)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{a}{2b}t^{2}}g(t),

(6.1)

which according to Proposition
3.1
belongs to the space
B_{p,\delta}^{A}
and has the unit
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-norm.
The real and imaginary parts of
f
are depicted in Figure

for different parameter choices.

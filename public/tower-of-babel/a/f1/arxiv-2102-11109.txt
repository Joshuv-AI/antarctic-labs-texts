Abstract
. In this paper we investigate the asymptotic behavior and decay of the solution of the discrete in time N N -dimensional heat equation. We give a convergence rate with which the solution tends to the discrete fundamental solution, and the asymptotic decay, both in L p ​ ( ℝ N ) . L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}). Furthermore we prove optimal L 2 L^{2} -decay of solutions. Since the technique of energy methods is not applicable, we follow the approach of estimates based on the discrete fundamental solution which is given by an original integral representation and also by MacDonald’s special functions. As a consequence, the analysis is different to the continuous in time heat equation and the calculations are rather involved.

1.
Introduction

The linear heat equation
u_{t}=\Delta u
is one of the most studied problems in the theory of partial differential equations. It was introduced by J. Fourier (see
[
19
]
) to model several diffusion phenomena. Since then, it has been applied in the study of different processes in many mathematical areas such as PDEs, functional analysis, harmonic analysis, probability, among others. The nature of this problem is well known and we will not further explain it.

One of the aspects of interest, see
[
14
,
27
,
29
]
, is the large time behavior of solutions of the heat problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}=\Delta u,\quad t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N},\\
u(0)=f(x).\end{array}\right.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),
the solution of (
1.1
) on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
is
u(t)=G_{t}*f,
where
*
denotes the classical convolution on
\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
and

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N},

is the heat kernel. It is known that integrating over all of
\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
we get that the total mass of solutions is conserved for all time, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}u(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}u(0,x)\,dx.

This fact leads us to think that the total mass of solutions should have importance in the asymptotic behavior of solutions. Indeed, it is well known that if
M=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}u(0,x)\,dx
then

(1.2)

t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}\|u(t)-MG_{t}\|_{p}\to 0,\quad\text{as }t\to\infty,

for
1\leq p\leq\infty.
The previous estimate shows that the difference on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
between the solution
u(t)
and

MG_{t}
decays to zero like
o(\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}})
as
t
goes to infinity.

Also, it is known that the
p
-norms of the solution vanish as
t\to\infty
for
p>1.
This fact is known as that the
p
-energy is not conservative. More precisely

\|u(t)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{q}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}},\quad\|\nabla u(t)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{q}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)+1/2}},\quad\|\Delta u(t)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{q}}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)+1}},

for
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty.

One can consider the first moment as the vector quantity
\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{n}}x\,u(t,x)\,dx.
It can be seen that such moment is also conserved in time for the solution of (
1.1
) whenever
(1+|x|)f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}).
Moreover, under such assumption we are able to improve the convergence (
1.2
), that is,

t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+1/2}\|u(t)-MG_{t}\|_{p}\to 0,\quad\text{as }t\to\infty.

However, the second moment

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{n}}|x|^{2}\,u(t,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{n}}|x|^{2}\,f(x)\,dx+2Nt

is not conservative. In fact, it is known that only integral quantities conserved by the solutions of (
1.1
) are the mass and the first moment.

This type of large-time asymptotic results have been also studied for several diffusion problems. For example in
[
9
,
12
,
16
,
20
,
26
]
the authors studied large-time behaviour and other asymptotic estimates for the solutions of different diffusion problems in
\mathbb{{R}}^{N},
and similar aspects are studied for open bounded domains in
[
8
,
16
]
. Estimates for heat kernels on manifolds have been also studied in
[
18
,
21
,
7
]
. In
[
25
]
, the author obtains gaussian upper estimates for the heat kernel associated to the sub-laplacian on a Lie group, and also for its first-order time and space derivatives.

On the other hand, finite differences, sometimes also called discrete derivatives, were introduced some centuries ago, and they have been used along the literature in different mathematical problems, mainly in approximation of derivatives for the numerical solution of differential equations and partial differential equations. The most knowing ones are the forward, backward and central differences (the forward and backward differences are associated to the Euler, explicit and implicit, numerical methods). We denote them in the following way; let
h>0,
for a function
u
defined on the mesh
\mathbb{{Z}}_{h}:=\{nh\,:\,n\in\mathbb{{Z}}\}
we write

\delta_{\text{right}}u(nh):=\frac{u((n+1)h)-u(nh)}{h},\quad\delta_{\text{left}}u(nh):=\frac{u(nh)-u((n-1)h)}{h},

and

\delta_{c}u(nh):=\biggl(\frac{\delta_{\text{right}}+\delta_{\text{left}}}{2}\biggr)u(nh)=\frac{u((n+1)h)-u((n-1)h)}{2h}.

In the last years, and taking as a guide the paper
[
4
]
, several authors have been working in the context of partial difference-differential equations (
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
22
,
23
]
) from an specific point of view; in that papers the approach has been focused in mathematical analysis, more precisely, harmonic analysis, functional analysis and fractional differences. Particularly in
[
1
]
it is shown that the operators
\delta_{\text{right}}
and
\delta_{\text{left}}
generate markovian
C_{0}
-semigroups on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{{Z}}).
Also, in
[
5
]
, the authors study harmonic properties of the solution of the heat problem on one-dimensional graphs (the mesh
\mathbb{{Z}}_{h}
), and the wave equation on graphs is studied in
[
23
]
. An abstract approach for discrete in time Cauchy problems is given in
[
22
]
. Also, non-local problems in the discrete framework appear in
[
2
,
6
]
.

The previous comments motivate the main aim of this paper; let
h>0,
we consider the first order Cauchy problem for the heat equation in discrete time

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta_{\text{left}}u(nh,x)=\Delta u(nh,x)+g(nh,x),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\,x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N},\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{array}\right.

where
\Delta
is the classical laplacian on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
,
u
is defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}\times\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
, with
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}:=\{nh:n\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0}\}
,
f
is defined on
\mathbb{{R}}
and
g
is defined on
\mathbb{{N}}^{h}\times\mathbb{{R}}^{N},
with
\mathbb{{N}}^{h}:=\{nh:n\in\mathbb{{N}}\}.

Along the paper we study asymptotic behavior and decay of the solution of (
1.3
). For that purpose, we need to know properties of the fundamental solution of the homogeneous problem associated to (
1.3
) (when
g=0
). In fact, one of the key points to get such asymptotic properties is an integral representation of the fundamental solution for the associated homogeneous equation. Furthermore, we describe explicitly this solution in terms of MacDonald’s functions which arise naturally from the integral representation of the solution. This representation is quite original and allows to study the decay of solutions for the problem (
1.3
) when the initial datum belongs to
p
-integrable Lebesgue spaces. Moreover, both the integral representation and the explicit expression via MacDonald’s functions allow to give a quantitative rate at which the solution converges to M times the fundamental solution, where
M
will denote, as in the continuous case, the initial mass of solution. The techniques used to obtain our results differs to the continuous case because we have to deal with the integral representation and
asymptotic properties of MacDonald’s special functions. We also note to the reader that doing the relation
t=nh,
the asymptotics of
G_{t}
will be similar to
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
as
t\to\infty
or equivalently
n\to\infty,
where
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
will denote the fundamental solution of the homogeneous problem associated to (
1.3
).

In this paper we are not interested in the study of the convergence as
h\to 0
of solutions of the problems (
1.3
) (depending on
h
) to the classical heat problem. However, it can be seen as a natural problem studied in semigroup theory via Yosida approximants (see Remark
2.1
). Also, one can think about the possibility to consider similar problems to (
1.3
) but considering the discrete derivatives
\delta_{\text{right}}
or
\delta_{c}.
However, as we explain in Remark
2.2
, the fundamental solutions to that problem does not have good properties.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is focused in the fundamental solution of the homogeneous problem associated to (
1.3
). We introduce an integral representation and the explicit expression via MacDonald’s functions. We deduce basic properties, we calculate its gradient and laplacian, and we see that the mass and the first moment of solutions of the homogeneous problem are conservative in discrete time
nh,
and not the second moment. Also some pictures of the continuous and discrete gaussian kernels, with their corresponding comments, are stated. In Section
3
we give pointwise and
L^{p}
asymptotic upper bounds for the fundamental solution
\mathcal{G}_{n,h},
and we use such estimates to prove in Section
4
that the
p
-energies of solutions of (
1.3
) are dissipative. Section
5
is the main part of the paper; we prove the asymptotic behaviour for the discrete in time heat problem (Theorem
5.1
). In Section 6 we success in proving optimal
L^{2}
-decay estimates for the solution of the homogeneous problem associated to (
1.3
). The proof is based on Fourier analysis techniques. Finally we include an Appendix where we show some basic properties of Gamma and MacDonald’s functions, and a technical result about integrability.

2.
The discrete gaussian fundamental solution

In this section we study the fundamental solution for the homogeneous discrete in time heat initial value problem on the Lebesgue
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
spaces.
Let
h>0,
we consider

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta_{\text{left}}u(nh,x)=\Delta u(nh,x),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{array}\right.

where
u
and
f
are functions defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}\times\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
and
\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
, respectively. Formally, one can write the solution in the following way

u(nh,x)=\frac{1}{h^{n}}(1/h-\Delta)^{-n}f(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

whenever the resolvent operator
(1/h-\Delta)^{-1}
has sense. It is well known that
the laplacian operator
\Delta
associated to the standard heat equation in continuous time on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
generates the gaussian semigroup with convolution kernel

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}},\quad t>0,\,x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

From semigroup theory (see
[
11
, Corollary 1.11]
) we obtain

u(nh,\cdot)=\frac{1}{h^{n}}(1/h-\Delta)^{-n}f(\cdot)=\frac{1}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/h}t^{n-1}(G_{t}*f)(\cdot)dt:=(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}*f)(\cdot),\,\,f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),

where
*
denotes the classical convolution on
\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
and

(2.2)

\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)=\frac{1}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t/h}t^{n-1}G_{t}(x)\,dt,\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that fixed a positive number
t>0,
the Yosida approximants
(1-\frac{t}{n}\Delta)^{-n}
(see
[
11
, Theorem 3.5]
) allow to approximate the gaussian
C_{0}
-semigroup
G_{t}
as
n\to\infty.
Writing
h=t/n,
the previous convergence shows that the gaussian semigroup can be approximated by the solutions of the discrete in time problems (
2.1
) as the mesh
h\to 0.

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to see that if we consider the forward difference
\delta_{\text{right}}
on (
2.1
), then formally the solution of the problem would be
u(nh,\cdot)=\frac{1}{h^{n}}(1/h+\Delta)^{n}f(\cdot),
which is not defined (bounded) on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}).

Also, for the central difference
\delta_{\text{c}}
, the fundamental solution would be given by

\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{n}(t/h)G_{t}(x)\,dt,

where
J_{n}
are the Bessel functions of first kind. In this case is not difficult to prove that the solution is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),
however it does not have as good properties as
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
satisfies, for example the contractivity on
L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}).

These are the main reasons because of we consider the discrete in time heat problem with the backward difference
\delta_{\text{left}}.

Now we will see the explicit expression of the fundamental solution
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
in terms of special functions. By
[
17
, p.363 (9)]
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)(4\pi)^{N/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(t/h+|x|^{2}/4t)}t^{n-N/2-1}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\biggr)^{n-N/2}K_{n-N/2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\setminus\{0\}.

Here, the functions
K_{\nu}
denote the Bessel functions of imaginary argument, also called MacDonald’s functions or modified cylinder functions (see Section
7
). Observe that the identity has not pointwise sense for
x=0
if
N/2-n\geq 0.
In fact, for that values
N/2-n\geq 0
, taking
|x|\to 0
in (
2.3
) and using (P4) and (P6) of Appendix one gets
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\to\infty.
However, as we will see, good properties on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
hold. For the case
N/2-n<0,
by (P4) we have
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\to\frac{\Gamma(n-N/2)}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}
as
|x|\to 0.

Remark 2.3
.

The gaussian kernel satisfies the semigroup property on time,
G_{t}\ast G_{s}=G_{t+s}
. Since
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
is given by natural powers of the resolvent operator of the laplacian, it satisfies the discrete semigroup property. Indeed, we also can prove that property using the expression (
2.2
) as follows,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast\mathcal{G}_{m,h})(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n+m}\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t+s}{h}}t^{n-1}s^{m-1}G_{t}(x-y)G_{s}(y)\,ds\,dt\right)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n+m}\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{t+s}{h}}t^{n-1}s^{m-1}G_{t+s}(y)\,ds\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n+m}\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{h}}t^{n-1}(\sigma-t)^{m-1}G_{\sigma}(x)\,d\sigma\right)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n+m}\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{h}}G_{\sigma}(x)\left(\int_{0}^{\sigma}t^{n-1}(\sigma-t)^{m-1}\,dt\right)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h^{n+m}\Gamma(n)\Gamma(m)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{h}}G_{\sigma}(x)\sigma^{m+n-1}B(n,m)\,d\sigma=\mathcal{G}_{n+m,h}(x).

Here
B(n,m)
is the Beta function.

In the following we denote

p_{n,h}(t)=:\frac{1}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)}e^{-t/h}t^{n-1},\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Then we can write

(2.4)

\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n,h}(t)G_{t}(x)dt,\quad x\neq 0.

The above integral representation is a discretization formula for the gaussian semigroup. The case
h=1
was treated in
[
22
]
for a general
C_{0}
-semigroup on an abstract context.

Next, we refer to the function
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
as the
fundamental solution
for the problem (
2.1
). The following proposition states some basic properties of it.

Proposition 2.4
.

The function
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
satisfies:

(i)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)>0,\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},x\neq 0.

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\ dx=1
.

(iii)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}({\mathcal{G}}_{n,h})(\xi)=\frac{1}{(1+h|\xi|^{2})^{n}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

(iv)

\dfrac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}=\Delta\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x),\quad n\geq 2,x\neq 0.

(v)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\,dx=2Nnh.

Proof.

(i) It is clear by (
2.4
). (ii) Note that
\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n,h}(t)dt=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}G_{t}(x)dx=1,
then the result follows from the Fubini’s theorem. (iii) It is known that
\mathcal{F}({G}_{t})(\xi)=e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}
, for
\xi\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N},
then by (
2.4
) one gets

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{n,h})(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n,h}(t)\mathcal{F}({G}_{t})(\xi)\,dt=\frac{1}{(1+h|\xi|^{2})^{n}}.

(iv) First of all, observe that
\frac{d}{dt}p_{n,h}(t)=-\frac{1}{h}(p_{n,h}(t)-p_{n-1,h}(t))
for
n\geq 2.
Then integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n,h}(t)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{t}(x)\ dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}p_{n,h}(t)\Delta G_{t}(x)\ dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x),\quad x\neq 0,

where we have used that
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}
and
\lim_{t\to\infty}
of
p_{n}(t)G_{t}(x)
vanishes. (v) It follows easily by the second moment of
G_{t}(x)
and the representation (
2.2
).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Observe that one can prove the above properties via the expression (
2.3
) given by the MacDonald’s function. For example, from (P1) of Appendix we get the positivity of the fundamental solution. Furthermore, by
[
17
, p. 668 (16)]
it follows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\biggr)^{n-N/2}K_{n-N/2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1+\frac{N}{2}-n}}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{1/2}h^{\frac{n}{2}-\frac{N}{4}}}\left(\frac{N\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n-N/2}K_{n-N/2}\biggl(\frac{r}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)\,dr\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{N}{2}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})h^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{4}}}{h^{\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{4}}\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}=1.

Also note that by
\frac{\partial|x|}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{x_{j}}{|x|}
and (P2) of Appendix, we obtain

(2.5)

\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)=\frac{-2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}\sqrt{h}}\frac{x_{j}}{|x|}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\biggr)^{n-N/2}K_{n-N/2-1}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{2h\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}\left(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\right)^{n-\frac{N}{2}-2}K_{n-\frac{N}{2}}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\right)

\displaystyle+N\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{4h\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}\left(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\right)^{n-\frac{N}{2}-3}K_{n-\frac{N}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\right)

\displaystyle-2(n-1)\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{h}\frac{1}{4h\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}\left(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\right)^{n-\frac{N}{2}-3}K_{n-\frac{N}{2}-1}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\right).

Now, since
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{h}=\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\right)^{2}
, we get

\Delta\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)=\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}.

Finally observe that the mean square displacement can be also calculated in the following way; using (
2.3
), a change of variables and

17
, p.668 (16)]
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{2}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}(2\sqrt{h})^{N-1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|x|^{n-N/2+2}K_{n-N/2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}(2\sqrt{h})^{N-1/2}}\left(\frac{N\pi^{N/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{n+\frac{N}{2}+1}K_{n-N/2}\biggl(\frac{r}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)\,dr\right)

\displaystyle=2Nnh.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that by Proposition
2.4
(i) we have that the total mass of solution of (
2.1
) is conservative in the discrete time
nh,
that is,

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}u(nh,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}f(x)\,dx.

Moreover, the first moment is also conservative; if
(1+|x|)f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
one gets

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}x(u(nh,x)-u((n-1)h,x))\,dx=h\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}x\Delta u(nh,x)\,dx=0,

and so
\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}xu(nh,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}xf(x)\,dx.
However, as in the continuous case holds, by Proposition
2.4
(v) it follows that the second moment is

\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{n}}|x|^{2}\,u(nh,x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{n}}|x|^{2}\,f(x)\,dx+2Nnh.

3.
Estimates for the fundamental solution

In this section we present pointwise and
p
-norm estimates for the fundamental solution of (
2.1
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R:=\frac{|x|^{2}}{nh}.

(i)

If
R\leq 1,
then

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\sim\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2}},\,\text{ for }n-N/2>0,

and if
R\geq 1
, then

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{nh}{|x|^{N+2}}.

(ii)

If
R\leq 1,
then

|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{|x|}{(nh)^{N/2+1}},\,\text{ for }n-N/2>1,

and if
R\geq 1
, then

|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{nh}{|x|^{N+3}}.

(iii)

If
R\leq 1,
then

|\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}|\lesssim\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2+1}},\,\text{ for }n-N/2>1,

and if
R\geq 1
, then

|\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{N+2}}.

Proof.

(i) Let
R\leq 1.
By (
2.3
), (P4) of Appendix and (
7.1
) we have

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\sim\frac{\Gamma(n-N/2)}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}}\sim\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2}}.

Now let
R\geq 1.
Along the proof we will use that

(3.1)

e^{-\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}}\leq\frac{k!h^{k/2}}{|x|^{k}},\quad k\in\mathbb{{N}}_{0}.

By (P5) of Appendix and (
3.1
) for
k=n+N+1
one gets

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{n^{3N/4+5/4}}{2^{n}}\frac{nh}{|x|^{N+2}}\lesssim\frac{nh}{|x|^{N+2}}.

(ii) Let
R\leq 1.
Equation (
2.5
) implies that

(3.2)

|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|=\frac{2}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi h)^{N/2}\sqrt{h}}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{2\sqrt{h}}\biggr)^{n-N/2}K_{n-N/2-1}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr).

From (P4) of Appendix and (
7.1
) we have

|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\sim\frac{\Gamma(n-N/2-1)|x|}{\Gamma(n)h^{N/2+1}}\lesssim\frac{|x|}{(nh)^{N/2+1}}.

For
R\geq 1,
by (
3.2
), (P5) of Appendix and (
3.1
) for
k=n+N+2
we obtain

|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{hn(n+1)(n+2)(n+3)}{2^{n}|x|^{4}}\lesssim\frac{nh}{|x|^{4}}.

(iii) Applying (P3) and (P2) of Appendix in turn, it follows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-2|x|^{n-N/2-1}}{\Gamma(n)(4\pi)^{N/2}h^{N/2+1/2}(2\sqrt{h})^{n-N/2}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\biggl(NK_{n-N/2-1}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)-\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}K_{n-N/2-2}\biggl(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{h}}\biggr)\biggr)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(I)+(II).

Let
R\leq 1.
By (P4) of Appendix and (
7.1
), that

|(I)|\lesssim\frac{\Gamma(n-N/2-1)}{\Gamma(n)h^{N/2+1}}\sim\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2+1}},

and

|(II)|\lesssim\frac{|x|^{2}}{(nh)^{N/2+2}}\leq\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2+1}}.

Therefore we conclude the result for
R\leq 1.

Now let
R\geq 1.
Note that from the part (i), we have

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|,|\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)|\lesssim\frac{hn^{3N/4+9/4}}{2^{n}|x|^{N+2}}.

Then

|\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}(x)}{h}|\lesssim\frac{1}{|x|^{N+2}}.

∎

Now we present the asymptotic decay of the fundamental solution
\mathcal{G}_{n,h}
in Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty,
then

(i)

\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}},\quad n-\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)>0.

(ii)

\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+1/2}},\quad n-\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)>1/2.

(iii)

\|\frac{\mathcal{G}_{n,h}-\mathcal{G}_{n-1,h}}{h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+1}},\quad n-\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)>1.

Here,
C_{p}
is a constant independent of
h
and
n
.

Proof.

(i) It is well known (see
[
17
, p.334 (3.326)]
) that there exist
C_{p}
(independent of
t
) such that
||G_{t}||_{p}=C_{p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}},

\|\nabla G_{t}\|_{p}=C_{p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})+1/2}}
and
\|\frac{\partial}{\partial t}G_{t}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\frac{1}{t^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})+1}}
for
t>0
. Then by (
2.4
) one gets

\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{h^{n}\Gamma(n)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-t}{h}}t^{n-\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})-1}\,dt=C_{p}\frac{\Gamma(n-\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p}))}{h^{\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\Gamma(n)}\leq\frac{C_{p}}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}},

where we have applied (
7.1
).
The cases (ii) and (iii) follow in a similar way than (i).

∎

4.
Asymptotic
L^{p}-L^{q}
decay

Let
h>0,
and the time mesh
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}
. Now we consider the non-homogenous problem

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\delta_{\text{left}}u(nh,x)=\Delta u(nh,x)+g(nh,x),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\end{array}\right.

where
u,f,g
are functions defined on
\mathbb{{N}}_{0}^{h}\times\mathbb{{R}}^{N},

\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
and
\mathbb{{N}}^{h}\times\mathbb{{R}}^{N}
respectively.

Formally, from (
4.1
), one gets

(4.2)

u(nh,x)=(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}*f)(x)+h\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{G}_{j,h}*g((n-j+1)h,\cdot))(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{{N}},x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}.

The expression (
4.2
) gives a classical solution of (
4.1
) on
L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
(
1\leq p\leq\infty
) whenever
f,g(nh,\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),
for
n\in\mathbb{{N}}.
For convenience, we write the classical solution as
u(nh,x)=u_{c}(nh,x)+u_{p}(nh,x),
where

u_{c}(nh,x)=(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}*f)(x)

and

u_{p}(nh,x)=h\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}*g(jh,\cdot))(x).

Next, let us present a result about the
L^{p}-L^{q}
asymptotic decay for
u_{c}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty.
If
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
, then the solution
u
of (
4.1
) satisfies

(i)

\|u_{c}(nh)\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\|f\|_{q}.

(ii)

\|\nabla u_{c}(nh)\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)+1/2}}\|f\|_{q}.

(iii)

\|\delta_{\text{left}}u_{c}(nh)\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)+1}}\|f\|_{q}.

Here,
C_{p}
is a constant independent of
h
and
n
.

Proof.

Take
r\geq 1
such that
1+1/p=1/q+1/r,
and applying Young’s inequality we get

\|u_{c}(nh)\|_{p}=\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}*f\|_{p}\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{r}\|f\|_{q}.

From Theorem
3.2
(i) follows the case (i). The other cases are similar.
∎

Now, assuming certain conditions on the function
g
we get an asymptotic decay for
u_{p}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty,
and
g(nh,\cdot)\in L^{q}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
with

\|g(nh,\cdot)\|_{q}\lesssim\frac{1}{(nh)^{\gamma}},

for
\gamma>0.

(i)

If
\gamma,\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)\neq 1
, then the solution
u
of (
4.1
) satisfies

\|u_{p}(nh)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{(nh)^{1-\min\{1,\gamma\}}}{(nh)^{\min\{1,\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)\}}}.

(ii)

If
\gamma=1
and
\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)\neq 1
, then the solution
u
of (
4.1
) satisfies

\|u_{p}(nh)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\log nh}{(nh)^{\min\{1,\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)\}}}.

(iii)

If
\gamma\neq 1
and
\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)=1
, then the solution
u
of (
4.1
) satisfies

\|u_{p}(nh)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\log nh}{(nh)^{\min\{1,\gamma\}}}.

(iv)

If
\gamma,\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)=1
, then the solution
u
of (
4.1
) satisfies

\|u_{p}(nh)\|_{p}\lesssim\frac{\log nh}{nh}.

Proof.

Let
r\geq 1
such that
1+1/p=1/q+1/r.
By Young’s inequality and Theorem
3.2
(i) one gets

\displaystyle\|u_{p}(nh)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}\|_{r}\|g(jh,\cdot))\|_{q}\lesssim h\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{((n-j+1)h)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{[n/2]}+\sum_{j=[n/2]+1}^{n}\biggr)\frac{1}{((n-j+1)h)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\gamma}}=I_{1}+I_{2}.

On one hand, for
1\leq j\leq[n/2]
we have
n/2\leq n-j+1,
which in turn implies that

I_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\sum_{j=1}^{[n/2]}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\gamma}}\lesssim\frac{(nh)^{1-\min\{1,\gamma\}}}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}},\quad\gamma\neq 1,

and
I_{1}\lesssim\frac{\log nh}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}
when
\gamma=1.

On the other hand,

I_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{(nh)^{\gamma}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)}}\lesssim\frac{(nh)^{1-\min\{1,\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)\}}}{(nh)^{\gamma}},\quad\gamma\neq 1,

and
I_{2}\lesssim\frac{\log nh}{(nh)^{\gamma}}
when
\frac{N}{2}(1/q-1/p)=1.

∎

5.
Large-time behaviour of solutions

In the following we study the asymptotic behavior of solution of (
4.1
), more precisely we will prove as the solution
u_{c}+u_{p}
converges asymptotically to a lineal combination of the mass of the initial data
f
and the mass of the non-homogeneity
g.
Moreover, we will able to state the rate of the convergence. Along the section we will assume the following:

(a)

f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
.

(b)

There exists
\gamma>\max\{1,\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)\}
such that

\|g(jh,\cdot)\|_{1}\lesssim\frac{1}{j^{\gamma}},\quad j\in\mathbb{{N}}.

Set also

M_{c}=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}f(x)\,dx,\quad M_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}g(jh,x)\,dx.

Taking into account the previous notation, we present the next theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty.
Assume the conditions
(a)
-
(b),
and suppose that
u
is the classical solution of (
4.1
).

(i)

Then

(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty,

and

(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|u_{p}(nh)-hM_{p}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(ii)

Suppose in addition that
|x|f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}),
then

(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\lesssim(nh)^{-1/2}.

Proof.

We start proving the assertion
(ii)
. Since
f,|x|f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}),

by Decomposition Lemma
7.1
there exists
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

f=M_{c}\delta_{0}+\mbox{div}\,\phi

in the distributional sense, and

\|\phi\|_{1}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x||f(x)|\,dx<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle u_{c}(nh,x)

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast(M_{c}\delta_{0}+\mbox{div}\,\phi(\cdot)))(x)

\displaystyle=M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)+(\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast\phi)(x),

which implies

\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C\|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\||x|f(x)\|_{1}\leq K_{p,f}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+1/2}},

where we have used part
(ii)
of Theorem
3.2
.
This implies

(5.1)

\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast f-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq K_{p,f}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+\frac{1}{2}}}.

To prove the first part of assertion
(i)
, we choose a sequence
(\eta_{j})\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\eta_{j}(x)\,dx=M_{c}
for all
j,
and
\eta_{j}\to f
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. For each
j
, by Theorem
3.2
and (
5.1
) we get

\displaystyle\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{G}_{nh}\ast f-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast(f-\eta_{j})\|_{p}+\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast\eta_{j}-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}+\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\ast\eta_{j}-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}+K_{p,\eta_{j}}\dfrac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)+\frac{1}{2}}}.

It follows that

(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}+K_{p,\eta_{j}}\frac{1}{(nh)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

which implies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}\|u_{c}(nh)-M_{c}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f-\eta_{j}\|_{1}.

The assertion follows by letting
j\to\infty
.

Next, let us prove the second part of
(i)
. We can write

M_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}g(jh,x)+\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}g(jh,x)\,dx.

It follows from Theorem
3.2
that

\displaystyle(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}g(jh,x)\,dx\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\|_{p}\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|g(jh,x)|\,dx\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

Therefore it is enough to show the following

(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}\left\|h\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}\ast g(jh,\cdot))(\cdot)-h\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}g(jh,y)\,dy\right\|_{p}\to 0,\,\,\,n\to\infty.

Ir order to prove the assertion, we fix

0<\delta<\frac{1}{10}.

In particular, this implies that
0<\delta<\dfrac{1}{5}<\dfrac{1}{2}
and

\frac{\delta}{1-\delta}<\frac{1}{4}.

Next, we decompose the set
\{1,2,3,..,n\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
into two parts

\Omega_{1}:=\{1,2,...,\lceil n\delta\rceil\}\times\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:|y|\leq(\delta nh)^{1/2}\},

\Omega_{2}:=\{1,2,...,n\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega_{1}.

Let us start with the set
\Omega_{1}
. By the integral form of the Minkowski inequality we get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}}\int(h\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)-h\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot))g(jh,y)dy\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}}\int\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy.

Note that in this set the following inequalities hold

(5.2)

n\geq n-j+1\geq n(1-\delta)>\frac{n}{2},

where the second inequality follows from
\delta n-\lceil\delta n\rceil\geq-1.
Now, when
(j,y)\in\Omega_{1},
we consider the following subsets over
\mathbb{{R}}^{N}

A=\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\,:\,|x-y|\leq 2(\delta nh)^{1/2}\},\qquad B:=\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{n}\,:\,|x-y|>2(\delta nh)^{1/2}\},

and we write the
p
-norm over
\Omega_{1}
in the following way

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{A}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\biggl(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}.

Let us estimate on
\Omega_{1}
the part
A
of the
p
-norm. First we write

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{A}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{A}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\biggl(\int_{A}|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

For
(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}
and
x\in A
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{(n-j+1)h}\leq\frac{4\delta}{(1-\delta)}<1.

Since we want to estimate the solution for large values of
n
, we can assume that
n>N
. Thus, (
5.2
) implies that
n-j+1>n/2>N/2.

It follows from Theorem
3.1
(i)
that

|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)|\sim\frac{1}{((n-j+1)h)^{N/2}}.

Then

\displaystyle(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}hI_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}h}{((n-j+1)h)^{N/2}}\biggl(\int_{A}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{p}(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}h(\delta nh)^{N/2p}}{((n-j+1)h)^{N/2}}\leq C_{p}h\delta^{N/2p},

where in the last inequality we have used (
5.2
). Analogously, for
(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}
and
x\in A
we have

\frac{|x|^{2}}{nh}\leq\frac{(|x-y|+|y|)^{2}}{nh}\leq 9\delta<1,

which implies that

|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|\sim\frac{1}{(nh)^{N/2}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle(nh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})}hI_{2}\lesssim C_{p}h\delta^{N/2p}.

Since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|g(jh,\cdot)\|<\infty,
we get

(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}}\int\biggl(\int_{A}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}|g(jh,y)|\,dy\leq C_{p}h\delta^{N/2p}\to 0,\quad\delta\to 0.

Now we consider on
\Omega_{1}
the part
B
of the
p
-norm. We write

\displaystyle\biggl(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\biggl(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle+\biggl(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=:I_{3}+I_{4}.

First, let us estimate
I_{3}
. By mean value theorem there exists
\tilde{x}
between
x-y
and
x
(
x
denote the integration variable) such that

I_{3}=|y|\biggl(\int_{B}|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\tilde{x})|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}.

Since
|y|\leq(\delta nh)^{1/2}<\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
then

(5.3)

|\tilde{x}|\geq|x-y|-|\tilde{x}-(x-y)|\geq|x-y|-|y|\geq\frac{|x-y|}{2},

and

(5.4)

|\tilde{x}|\leq|x-y|+|y|\leq|x-y|+\frac{|x-y|}{2}=\frac{3}{2}|x-y|.

Equations (

) and (

) show that
|\tilde{x}|
and
|x-y|
are comparable. Also, by (

) and (

(5.5)

\frac{|\tilde{x}|}{((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\geq\frac{|x-y|}{2((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}>\delta^{1/2}.

Now we will use the asymptotics of
|\nabla\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\tilde{x})|,
so we divide
I_{3}
in two parts,
I_{31}
and
I_{32}
depending on whether
\frac{|\tilde{x}|}{((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}
is less or greater than 1 respectively (we are assuming
\delta
enough small).

In
I_{32}
, when
\frac{|\tilde{x}|}{((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\geq 1
, by (
5.4
) one gets

((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}\leq|\tilde{x}|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x-y|.

For this reason, the integration region in
I_{32}
is contained in
\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\,:\,\frac{2}{3}((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}\leq|x-y|\}
. From Theorem
3.1
(ii), the fact that
|y|\leq(\delta nh)^{1/2},
(
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle I_{32}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta nh)^{1/2}\left(\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{2}{3}((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{(n-j+1)h}{|\tilde{x}|^{N+3}}\right)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta nh)^{1/2}\left(\int_{|x-y|\geq\frac{2}{3}((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\left(\frac{nh}{|x-y|^{N+3}}\right)^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=C_{p}\delta^{1/2}(nh)^{3/2}((n-j+1)h)^{N/(2p)-(N+3)/2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\delta^{1/2}(nh)^{-N/2(1-1/p)}.

Consequently,

(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}hI_{32}\leq C_{p}h\delta^{1/2}.

For
I_{31},
by (
5.5
) note that the set of integration is contained in
\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\,:\,1\geq\frac{|x-y|}{2((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}>\delta^{1/2}\}.
Then from Theorem
3.1
(ii) it follows

\displaystyle I_{31}

\displaystyle\leq C(\delta nh)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\delta^{1/2}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\leq 1}\left(\frac{|\tilde{x}|}{((n-j+1)h)^{N/2+1}}\right)^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\delta^{1/2}(nh)^{1/2}}{(nh)^{N/2+1}}\left(\int_{\delta^{1/2}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{((n-j+1)h)^{1/2}}\leq 1}|x-y|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\delta^{1/2}(nh)^{-N/2(1-1/p)}(1-\delta^{(N+p)/2})^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\delta^{1/2}(nh)^{-N/2(1-1/p)},

which is equivalent to

(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}hI_{31}\leq C_{p}h\delta^{1/2}.

Next, let us estimate
I_{4}
. From discrete mean value theorem (see
[
3
, Corollary 2]
), there exist
\tilde{n}\in\{n-j+2,...,n\}
(whenever
j\geq 2
) and
C>0
such that

(5.6)

I_{4}\leq C(j-1)h\left(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{n},h}(x)-\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{n}-1,h}(x)|\,dx\right)^{p}=C(j-1)h\left(\int_{B}|\Delta\mathcal{G}_{\tilde{n},h}(x)|\,dx\right)^{p}.

Recall that in
\Omega_{1}
we have
n-j+1\leq\tilde{n}\leq n,
which implies by (
5.2
) that
nh(1-\delta)\leq\tilde{n}h\leq nh.
Also, in
\Omega_{1}
and
B
we have

|x|=|x+y-y|\geq|x-y|-|y|\geq(\delta nh)^{1/2},

so

\tilde{z}:=\frac{|x|}{(\tilde{n}h)^{1/2}}\geq\frac{|x|}{(nh)^{1/2}}\geq\frac{(\delta nh)^{1/2}}{(nh)^{1/2}}=\delta^{1/2},

and we have again two cases. We denote by
I_{41}
and
I_{42}
depending on whether
\tilde{z}\leq 1
or
\tilde{z}\geq 1
on the right side of (
5.6
).

For
I_{41},
since
\tilde{z}\leq 1
and
|x|\geq(\delta nh)^{1/2}
the set of integration is contained in
\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\,:\,(\delta nh)^{1/2}\leq|x|\leq(nh)^{1/2}\}.
Then, from Theorem
3.1
(iii) and the fact that we are in
\Omega_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{41}

\displaystyle\leq C(j-1)h\left(\int_{(\delta nh)^{1/2}\leq|x|\leq(nh)^{1/2}}\frac{1}{(\tilde{n}h)^{(N/2+1)p}}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\delta nh}{(nh(1-\delta))^{N/2+1}}\left(\int_{(\delta nh)^{1/2}\leq|x|\leq(nh)^{1/2}}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{p}\delta(1-\delta^{N/2})^{1/p}(nh)^{-N/2(1-1/p)}}{(1-\delta)^{N/2+1}}.

Consequently,

(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}hI_{41}\leq\frac{C_{p}h\delta}{(1-\delta)^{N/2+1}}.

For
I_{42}
we have

1\leq\tilde{z}=\frac{|x|}{(\tilde{n}h)^{1/2}}\leq\frac{|x|}{(nh(1-\delta))^{1/2}},

which implies that the set of integration is contained in
\{x\in\mathbb{{R}}^{N}\,:\,|x|\geq(nh(1-\delta))^{1/2}\}.
Then

\displaystyle I_{42}

\displaystyle\leq C(j-1)h\left(\int_{|x|\geq((1-\delta)nh)^{1/2}}\frac{1}{|x|^{(N+2)p}}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{p}\delta(nh)^{-N/2(1-1/p)}}{(1-\delta)^{N/2(1-1/p)+1}}.

Consequently,

(nh)^{1/2(1-1/p)}hI_{42}\leq\frac{C_{p}h\delta}{(1-\delta)^{N/2(1-1/p)+1}}.

Collecting all above terms over
B
we get

\displaystyle(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{1}}\int\biggl(\int_{B}|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(x-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(x)|^{p}\,dx\biggr)^{1/p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\delta^{\eta}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(jh,y)|dy

for some positive number
\eta
. The upper bound tends to zero as
\delta\to 0
uniformly in
nh
.

Now, we consider the set
\Omega_{2}
. Then

\displaystyle(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{2}}\int\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)-\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{2}}\int\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle+(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{2}}\int\|\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\cdot)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle=:I_{5}+I_{6}.

By Theorem
3.2
(i) one gets

I_{6}\leq C_{p}\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{2}}\int|g(jh,y)|dy.

As
n\to\infty
,
\Omega_{1}\to\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. This implies that
\Omega_{2}
has measure zero, and since
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|g(jh,y)|dy<\infty,
then
\sum_{(j,y)\in\Omega_{2}}\int|g(jh,y)|dy\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. It follows that
I_{6}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

For
I_{5}
we have two possibilities: either
j\leq\lceil\delta n\rceil
or
j>\lceil\delta n\rceil
. Thus, we divide

\Omega_{2}=\{1,...,\lceil\delta n\rceil\}\times\{y\in\mathbb{{R}}:|y|>(\delta nh)^{1/2}\}\cup\{\lceil\delta n\rceil+1,...,n\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle\leq(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\delta n\rceil}\int_{|y|>(\delta nh)^{1/2}}\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle+(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{j=\lceil\delta n\rceil+1}^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\|\mathcal{G}_{n-j+1,h}(\cdot-y)\|_{p}|g(jh,y)|dy

\displaystyle=:I_{51}+I_{52}.

Let us start with
I_{51}
. Recall that for
j\in\{1,...,\lceil\delta n\rceil\}
the expression (
5.2
) holds. So, by Theorem
3.2
(i) we have that

\displaystyle I_{51}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}h\sum_{j=1}^{\lceil\delta n\rceil}\int_{|y|>(\delta nh)^{1/2}}|g(jh,y)|dy\to 0

as
n\to\infty.

Next, for
I_{52},
again by Theorem
3.2
(i), (
5.2
) and the fact that
\gamma>1
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{52}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h\sum_{j=\lceil\delta n\rceil+1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{((n-j+1)h)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}}\frac{1}{j^{\gamma}}.

Thus, if
\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)\in[0,1)
and
\gamma>1,
then

I_{52}\leq\frac{C_{p}(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h}{(\lceil\delta n\rceil+1)^{\gamma}h^{\gamma}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-\lceil\delta n\rceil}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}}\leq\frac{C_{p}h(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}(nh-\lceil\delta n\rceil h)^{1-N/2(1-1/p)}}{(\delta nh)^{\gamma}}\to 0,

as
n\to\infty.
Also, if
\gamma>\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)>1
, then

I_{52}\leq\frac{C_{p}(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}h}{(\lceil\delta n\rceil+1)^{\gamma}h^{\gamma}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-\lceil\delta n\rceil}\frac{1}{(jh)^{\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)}}\leq\frac{C_{p}h(nh)^{N/2(1-1/p)}}{(\delta nh)^{\gamma}}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

The case
\gamma>\frac{N}{2}(1-1/p)=1
implies, similarly to the previous one, that

I_{52}\leq\frac{C_{p}h(nh)\log(n)}{(\delta nh)^{\gamma}}\to 0,\quad n\to\infty.

∎

6.
Optimal
L^{2}
-decay for solutions

In this section we prove that the decay rate of the solution
u_{c}
of (
2.1
) given in Theorem
4.1
(i) is optimal.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
u_{c}
be the solution of (
2.1
). Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
and
\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}f(x)dx\neq 0
. Then

\|u_{c}(nh,\cdot)\|_{2}\sim\frac{C}{(nh)^{N/4}},\quad nh\geq 1.

Proof.

Let
\rho>0,
we have by Proposition
2.4
(iii) that

\displaystyle\|u_{c}(nh,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{F}u_{c}(n,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}|\mathcal{F}\mathcal{G}_{n,h}(\xi)|^{2}\,\,|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)|^{2}\,d\,\xi

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{B}(0,\rho)}\frac{1}{(1+h|\xi|^{2})^{2n}}|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)|^{2}d\,\xi

(6.1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(1+h|\rho|^{2})^{2n}}\int_{\mathcal{B}(0,\rho)}|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)|^{2}d\,\xi.

By Plancherel Theorem and the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma we have that
\mathcal{F}f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, we may choose
\rho_{0}
small enough such that

\frac{1}{\rho^{N}}\int_{\mathcal{B}(0,\rho)}|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)|^{2}d\,\xi\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\mathcal{F}(0)\right|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}].

Substituting the previous inequality in (
6
) we have that for all
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]

\|u_{c}(nh,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{\rho^{N}}{2(1+h|\rho|^{2})^{2n}}\left|\mathcal{F}(0)\right|^{2}.

We choose
\rho:=\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{(nh)^{1/2}}
. For
n
enough large,
nh\geq 1
then
\rho
belongs to
(0,\rho_{0})
. Hence

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{N}}{(1+h|\rho|^{2})^{2n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{0}^{N}}{(nh)^{N/2}\left(1+\dfrac{\rho_{0}^{2}}{n}\right)^{2n}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\rho_{0}^{N}}{(nh)^{N/2}e^{2\rho_{0}^{2}}}=\frac{C}{(nh)^{N/2}},\quad nh\geq 1,

and then we get the first assertion of the result.

Next, let us prove the upper bound. By Plancherel’s Theorem and the Riemann-Lebesgue Lemma we have

\displaystyle\|u_{c}(nh,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+h|\xi|^{2})^{2n}}|\mathcal{F}f(\xi)|^{2}d\,\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{F}f\|_{\infty}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+h|\xi|^{2})^{2n}}d\,\xi\leq\|f\|_{1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+nh|\xi|^{2})^{2}}d\,\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{C\|f\|_{1}^{2}}{(nh)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}^{N}}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2}}d\,\xi=\frac{C}{(nh)^{N/2}}.

∎

7.
Appendix

Here, we present some useful facts which are needed in order to obtain our results.

First, we recall the following asymptotic behavior of the Gamma function. Let
\alpha,z\in\mathbb{{C}}
, then

(7.1)

\frac{\Gamma(z+\alpha)}{\Gamma(z)}=z^{\alpha}\biggl(1+\frac{\alpha(\alpha+1)}{2z}+O(|z|^{-2})\biggr),\quad|z|\to\infty,

whenever
z\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots,
and
z\neq-\alpha,-\alpha-1,\ldots,
see
[
13
]
.

Next, we recall the definition of Bessel functions and some basic results which are used in this work. See
[
17
,
24
,
28
]
for more information about this topic.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{{R}}
. The
Modified Bessel functions of the first kind
are defined by

I_{\nu}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)n!}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2n+\nu}.

Such functions allow to define, for
\nu\in\mathbb{{R}}
a non entire number, the
Modified Bessel functions of second kind
or
MacDonald’s functions
as follows

K_{\nu}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{\nu}(x)-I_{-\nu}(x)}{\sin(\nu x)}.

For the case
\mu\in\mathbb{{Z}}
they are defined by

K_{\mu}(x)=\lim_{\nu\to\mu}K_{\nu}(x)=\lim_{\nu\to\mu}\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{I_{\nu}(x)-I_{-\nu}(x)}{\sin(\nu x)}.

These functions arise as the solutions for the ODE

\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}u(z)=\left(1+\frac{\nu^{2}}{z^{2}}\right)u(z)-\frac{1}{z}\frac{d}{dz}u(z).

Some properties of the MacDonald’s functions used along the paper are the following ones:

(P1)

K_{\nu}(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\cosh t}\cosh(\nu t)\ dt,\quad|arg(z)|<\frac{\pi}{2}\ \mbox{or}\ \Re z=0\ \mbox{and }\nu=0
.

(P2)

z\frac{d}{dz}K_{\nu}(z)+\nu K_{\nu}(z)=-zK_{\nu-1}(z)
.

(P3)

z\frac{d}{dz}K_{\nu}(z)-\nu K_{\nu}(z)=-zK_{\nu+1}(z)
.

(P4)

When
0<z\ll\sqrt{\nu+1}
, we have

K_{\nu}(z)\sim\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{2}\left(\frac{2}{z}\right)^{\nu},\quad\mbox{if}\ \nu\neq 0.

(P5)

K_{\nu}(z)=\biggl(\dfrac{\pi}{2z}\biggr)^{1/2}e^{-z}\biggl(1+O(1/z)\biggr),\quad z\to\infty.

(P6)

K_{\nu}=K_{-\nu}.

(P7)

zK_{\nu-1}(z)-zK_{\nu+1}(z)=-2\nu K{\nu}(z).

We also need in this paper the following decomposition lemma (see
[
10
]
).

Lemma 7.1
.

Suppose
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x||f(x)|dx<\infty.
Then there exists
F\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

f=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)dx\right)\delta_{0}+\mbox{div}\,F

in the distributional sense and

\|F\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x||f(x)|dx.

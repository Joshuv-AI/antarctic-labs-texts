Temporal Graphs from Encounters in a Corridor


Abstract
Temporal graphs arise when edges of a graph appear at certain points in time. Many practical graphs are indeed temporal, think of co-authorship or contact tracing for example. We are motivated by characterizing the temporal graphs that can result from mobility models where some agents move in a space and a temporal edge is created each time two agents meet. We propose a rather natural one-dimensional model and provide a characterization in terms of forbidden patterns. If we consider that each pair of agents meet exactly once, we get a temporal clique where each edge has exactly one time value. By ordering the edges according to meeting times, we get a subset of the temporal cliques. We provide, thanks to classical combinatorial results, the number of possible cliques for a given number of agents.

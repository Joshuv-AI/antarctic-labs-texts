Lie groups of Poisson diffeomorphisms
Wilmer Smilde

Abstract
By considering suitable Poisson groupoids, we develop an approach to obtain Lie group structures on (subgroups of) the Poisson diffeomorphism groups of various classes of Poisson manifolds. As applications, we show that the Poisson diffeomorphism groups of (normal-crossing) log-symplectic, elliptic symplectic, scattering-symplectic and cosymplectic manifolds are regular infinite-dimensional Lie groups.



Introduction

A Poisson structure on a manifold
M
is a Lie bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
on the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}(M)
subject to the Leibniz rule
\{fg,h\}=f\{g,h\}+g\{f,h\}
for
f,g,h\in C^{\infty}(M)
. Alternatively, the Poisson structure can be described by a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
for which the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
[\pi,\pi]
vanishes. It induces the Lie bracket on
C^{\infty}(M)
via
\{f,g\}=\pi(df,dg)
.

A
Poisson map

f:(M,\pi_{M})\to(N,\pi_{N})
between Poisson manifolds
(M,\pi_{M})
and
(N,\pi_{N})
is a smooth map
f
for which
\pi_{M}
is
f
-related to
\pi_{N}
, meaning that
\wedge^{2}T_{x}f(\pi_{M,x})=\pi_{N,f(x)}
for all
x\in M
.

A Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
comes with a symplectic foliation, spanned by the
Hamiltonian vector fields

X_{f}=\{f,\cdot\},\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for $f\in C^{\infty}(M)$}.

A symplectic leaf
S
comes with a symplectic stucture determined by
\omega_{S}(X_{f},X_{g})=-\{f,g\}|_{S}
for
f,g\in C^{\infty}(M)
.

A
Poisson diffeomorphism
of
(M,\pi)
is a diffeomorphism of
M
that is also a Poisson map. This defines a subgroup
\Diff(M,\pi)
of
\Diff(M)
, called the Poisson diffeomorphism group. Note that a diffeomorphism is Poisson if and only if it sends each symplectic leaf symplectomorphically onto a (possibly different) symplectic leaf.

The Poisson diffeomorphism group

The group of symplectomorphisms
\Diff(M,\omega)
of a symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is well-understood as an infinite-dimensional Lie group
[
6
,
11
]
. Much less is known about
\Diff(M,\pi)
for general Poisson manifolds.

For an arbitrary Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
, the group of Poisson diffeomorphisms
\Diff(M,\pi)
can have many interesting subgroups:

•

Foliated Poisson diffeomorphisms

\Fol(M,\pi)
are those that send each leaf to itself. Correspondingly, one has the
foliated Poisson vector fields

\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)
, which are the Poisson vector fields that are tangent to the symplectic foliation.

•

The group of
Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms

\Ham(M,\pi)
consists of time-1 flows of Hamiltonian vector fields generated by a time-dependent Hamiltonian function.

•

The
locally Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms

\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
are the of time-1 flows of locally Hamiltonian vector fields generated by a time-dependent closed one-form.

Example 0.1

(Symplectic manifolds)
.

For a compact symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
, the group of locally Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms coincides with the identity component of the symplectomorphism group. This follows from the fact that every symplectic vector field is locally Hamiltonian.
∎

Example 0.2

(Zero Poisson manifolds)
.

For the zero Poisson manifold
(M,0)
, all of the above subgroups are trivial, while
\Diff(M,\pi)=\Diff(M)
.
∎

Example 0.3

(Poisson manifolds of constant rank)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold for which the bivector
\pi
has constant rank, with underlying symplectic foliation
(\mathcal{F},\omega)
. In general,
\Diff(M,\pi)
is larger than
\Fol(M,\pi)
, and
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
is smaller than (the identity-component of)
\Fol(M,\pi)
. The latter can be seen, if they were Lie groups, on the level of Lie algebras. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)
is isomorphic via
\omega^{\flat}
to the space
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{F})
of closed
foliated
one-forms, while
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
is isomorphic to the space of foliated one-forms that admit a closed extension in
\Omega^{1}(M)
.
∎

In this paper, we investigate the existence of Lie group structures on the group of Poisson diffeomorphisms and on its subgroups for several classes of Poisson manifolds.

Coisotropic bisections of Poisson groupoids

When
(M,\omega)
is a symplectic manifold, a manifold structure on
\Diff(M,\omega)
can be obtained in the following way. First, symplectomorphisms on
(M,\omega)
correspond to Lagrangian submanifolds of
(M\times M,\overrightarrow{\omega}-\overleftarrow{\omega})
. Second, by means of Weinstein’s Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem, the symplectic form
\overrightarrow{\omega}-\overleftarrow{\omega}
can be linearized around the graph of a symplectomorphism. Finally, the linearization of
\omega
around the graph also linearizes the deformation space of the Lagrangian submanifold: the Lagrangian submanifolds near the graph of a symplectomorphism form a closed subspace of the sections of the normal bundle. This ‘models’ the symplectomorphism group on an infinite-dimensional locally convex space, namely
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl},c}(M)
, the space of compactly supported closed one-forms on
M
(see
[
11
]
, section 43, for more details).

For a general Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
, things become much more complicated, partly because of the intransitive nature on both the global and local level of the Poisson diffeomorphism groups. For example, points on non-symplectomorphic leaves can not be permuted, as any Poisson diffeomorphism sends each symplectic leaf symplectomorphically onto another leaf.

A useful framework to capture intransitive symmetries in differential geometry is provided by Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids. The group of bisections
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
of a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
naturally acts on
M
. Moreover, it is well-known that
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
comes with a natural Lie group structure
[
22
,
23
]
. We will see in many examples that the bisection group of an appropriate Lie groupoid corresponds to an intransitive subgroup of
\Diff(M)
.

When
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
is a Poisson groupoid
[
26
]
, the group of coisotropic bisections
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
acts by Poisson diffeomorphisms on
(M,\pi)
. This puts the object
\Diff(M,\pi)
in a more general perspective: coisotropic bisections of the
pair Poisson groupoid

(M\times M,\overleftarrow{\pi}-\overrightarrow{\pi})\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
are in one-to-one correspondence with Poisson diffeomorphisms of
(M,\pi)
.

In
[
15
]
, Mărcu

t

,

constructs an example of a compact Poisson manifold of constant rank for which
\Diff(M,\pi)
is not locally path-connected in
\Diff(M)
. This is on itself not surprising or any problem –it only tells that
\Diff(M,\pi)
can not be embedded in
\Diff(M)
– but it does indicate that the pair Poisson groupoid might not be the right object to investigate, and we must broaden our perspective to involve other Poisson groupoids. However, in general there is no known or obvious Lie group structure on the group of coisotropic bisections.

Example 0.4

(Lagrangian bisections)
.

A particularly important class of Poisson groupoids over a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
are, when integrable, the symplectic groupoids. Let
(\mathcal{G},\Omega)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a symplectic groupoid. Its coisotropic bisection are better known as Lagrangian bisections, studied in
[
21
,
27
]
. As explained in more detail in Section
2.3
, the group of Lagrangian bisections
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Omega)
has interesting but complicated interactions with the Poisson diffeomorphism group
\Diff(M,\pi)
. For instance, the identity path component
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G},\Omega)
acts on
(M,\pi)
by locally Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms! In fact, any locally Hamiltonian diffeomorphism comes from a Lagrangian bisection. The map
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G},\Omega)\to\Diff(M,\pi)
has a non-trivial kernel in general.
∎

The Lie group of coisotropic bisections

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid. In order to build charts for a manifolds structure on
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
, it is necessary to describe the coisotropic deformations of the unit manifold
M\subset(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. It turns out
M
is always
Lagrangian
in
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
(Proposition
2.2
), in which case it makes sense to ask whether the Poisson structure
\Pi
is linearizable around it. That is: does there exists a local Poisson diffeomorphism
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\dashrightarrow(NM,\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
, that restricts to the identity on
M
? Here, the Poisson structure
\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}
is the linearization of
\Pi
on the normal bundle
NM
of
M
in
\mathcal{G}
, via any tubular neighbourhood (see section
1.1.1
). If the answer to this question is positive, we say that the Poisson groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is
linearizable
.

Under the additional assumption of linearizability of a Poisson groupoid, we show that its coisotropic bisection group is a Lie group. The used framework for infinite-dimensional manifolds is the "convenient setting" by Kriegl and Michor
[
11
]
. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 0.5

(Theorem
2.12
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid with Lie bialgebroid
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
. Assume that
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is linearizable around
M
. Then
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is a regular embedded Lie subgroup of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
with Lie algebra
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})=\{v\in\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}):d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}v=0\}
, where
d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}
is the differential associated to the Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}^{*}\Rightarrow M
.

This breaks up the search of Lie group structures on Poisson diffeomorphism groups into two steps, which are both very interesting on their own.

Step 1.

Find out which Poisson groupoids are linearizable.

Step 2.

Search for Poisson groupoids whose coisotropic bisections ‘compute’ relevant groups of Poisson diffeomorphisms on the base.

In fact, to apply Theorem
0.5
, one is often forced to look for Poisson groupoids beyond the product, because of the following result from
[
24
]
.

Theorem 0.6

(
[
24
]
, Theorem 3.26)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. If
(M\times M,\overleftarrow{\pi}-\overrightarrow{\pi})
is linearizable around the diagonal, then
\pi
has constant rank.

The linearization problem of Poisson groupoids has been investigated in
[
24
]
, where it is shown that integrations of triangular Lie algebroids of so-called cosymplectic type, as well as dual integrations of arbitrary triangular Lie bialgebroids are always linearizable. We recall these results in more detail in Section
2.4
.

Building on this work, we show in this paper that several classes of Poisson manifolds come with relevant Poisson groupoids that turn out to be linearizable. This way, we obtain Lie group structures on their groups of Poisson diffeomorphisms. The results are collected in the list of examples at the end of the introduction.

Recall from example

Theorem 0.9

(Theorem

Let

Example 0.11

(Cosymplectic manifolds)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold of

(Log-symplectic manifolds)
.

1
Poisson geometry: Lagrangians and diffeomorphisms

1.1  Lagrangian submanifolds

First, we recall the definition of a coisotropic submanifold.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. A submanifold
C\subset M
is
coisotropic
when
\pi(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=0
for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(TC)^{\circ}
, where
(TC)^{\circ}\subset T^{*}M
is the annihilator of
TC
. Equivalently,
(TC)^{\bot_{\pi}}:=\pi^{\sharp}\left((TC)^{\circ}\right)\subset TC
.
∎

Coisotropic submanifolds play a crucial role in this paper, because of the following proposition, which is folklore.

Proposition 1.2

(
[
26
]
, Corollary 2.2.3)
.

Let
(M,\pi_{N})
and
(N,\pi_{N})
be Poisson manifolds. A map
f:M\to N
is a Poisson map if and only if the graph of
f
is coisotropic in
(N\times M,\pi_{N}\times(-\pi_{M}))
.

Example 1.3
.

The graph of a Poisson diffeomorphism
\varphi:M\to M
is a coisotropic submanifold in
(M\times M,\pi\times(-\pi))
. There is more to it: the intersection of the graph of
\varphi
with the symplectic leaves of
(M\times M,\pi\times(-\pi))
is either empty or a Lagrangian submanifold of the leaf. This hints to a more restrictive class of coisotropic submanifolds, suitable for the study of Poisson diffeomorphisms.
∎

Definition 1.4

(
[
25
]
, Remark 7.8)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold. A submanifold
L\subset M
is
Lagrangian
when

(TL)^{\bot_{\pi}}:=\pi^{\sharp}\left((TL)^{\circ}\right)=TL\cap\operatorname{im}\pi^{\sharp}.\qed

The following lemma provides practical characterizations of Lagrangian submanifolds.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold and
L\subset M
a submanifold. The following are equivalent:

(i).

The submanifold
L
is a Lagrangian submanifold of
M
.

(ii).

For every symplectic leaf
(S,\omega_{S})
, the intersection
L\cap S
is a Lagrangian submanifold of
(S,\omega_{S})
satisfying
T(L\cap S)=TL\cap TS
.

(iii).

The submanifold
L
is coisotropic and
\pi(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=0
for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(\pi^{\sharp})^{-1}(TL)
.

Proof.

(i).
\Leftrightarrow
(ii).
Clearly,
(ii).
implies
(i).
For the converse, recall that, because
L
is coisotropic, the annihilator
(TL)^{\circ}
becomes a subalgebroid of the cotangent algebroid
T^{*}M
, that we call the conormal algebroid. We equip the path-components of
L\cap S
with the structure of a manifold by identifying them with the leaves of the algebroid
(TL)^{\circ}\Rightarrow L
. As
(TL)^{\circ}\Rightarrow
is a subalgebroid of
T^{*}M\Rightarrow M
, every leaf
\tilde{L}
of
(TL)^{\circ}\Rightarrow L
is contained in a symplectic leaf
S
. Conversely, if
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to L\cap S
is a path, it must be tangent to
L
and
S
simultaneously, i.e.
\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}L\cap T_{\gamma(t)}S=T_{\gamma(t)}L\cap\Im\pi^{\sharp}_{\gamma(t)}
for all
t
. Since
\pi^{\sharp}((TL)^{\circ})=TL\cap\Im\pi^{\sharp}
, it follows that
\gamma
is tangent to a leaf
\tilde{L}
of the conormal algebroid
(TL)^{\circ}\Rightarrow L
.

(i).
\Rightarrow
(iii).
A Lagrangian submanifold
L
is clearly coisotropic. Moreover, for
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(\pi^{\sharp})^{-1}(TL)
there exists
\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2}\in(TL)^{\circ}
with
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{1})=\pi^{\sharp}(\tilde{\alpha}_{1})
and
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{2})=\pi^{\sharp}(\tilde{\alpha}_{2})
. In particular,
\pi(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\pi(\tilde{\alpha}_{1},\tilde{\alpha}_{2})=0
.

(iii).
\Rightarrow
(i).
To prove the converse, we have to find for
\alpha\in(\pi^{\sharp})^{-1}(TL)
an element
\tilde{\alpha}\in(TL)^{\circ}
such that
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha)=\pi^{\sharp}(\tilde{\alpha})
. Since
\alpha|_{TL\cap\operatorname{im}\pi^{\sharp}}=0
, we can construct
\tilde{\alpha}
by extending
\alpha|_{\operatorname{im}\pi^{\sharp}}
by 0 over
TL
.
∎

Remark 1.6
.

An analogue for
(ii).
in Lemma
1.5
does not exist for arbitrary coisotropic submanifolds. A counterexample is the following. Consider
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with coordinates
(x,y,z)
, foliated by the planes of constant
z
with a foliated symplectic form
dx\wedge dy
. The graph
\Gamma
of
f(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
is coisotropic in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with respect to this Poisson structure, but the intersection is not clean at the origin. Indeed, the intersection of
\Gamma
with the
z=0
plane is just a point, while the intersection of the tangent spaces is two-dimensional. This is also the only point where the Lagrangian condition fails.

1.1.1 Linear Poisson structures and the linearization problem for Lagrangian submanifolds

Let
E\to L
be a vector bundle, with scalar multiplication
m_{t}:E\to E
. A Poisson structure
\pi

1.2  Poisson Lie algebroids

A Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
over
M
consists of a vector bundle
\mathcal{A}\to M
together with an anchor
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to TM
and a Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathcal{A}}
on
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
subject to the Leibniz rule:
[v,fw]_{\mathcal{A}}=f[v,w]_{\mathcal{A}}+\mathcal{L}_{\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(v)}(f)w
. Due to the presence of the bracket, a Lie algebroid behaves much like the tangent bundle of a manifold
M
, and therefore it is often the case that geometry on
TM
can by ‘lifted’ to a Lie algebroid. In this section we introduce Poisson Lie algebroids as the ‘lifts’ of Poisson structures (see also
[
10
]
).

Recall that the bracket of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
can be extended to a Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket on
\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathcal{A})
.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid. A
Poisson structure on
\mathcal{A}
, or an
\mathcal{A}
-Poisson structure
, is a section
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}\mathcal{A})
satisfying
[\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\pi_{\mathcal{A}}]_{\mathcal{A}}=0
. The pair
(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})\Rightarrow M
is referred to as a
Poisson Lie algebroid.

∎

Remark 1.10
.

A Lie bialgebroid
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
consists of a vector bundle
\mathcal{A}
with Lie algebroid structures on
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{*}
that are compatible in the following sense:

d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}[v,w]_{\mathcal{A}}=\left[d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}v,w\right]_{\mathcal{A}}+\left[v,d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}w\right]_{\mathcal{A}}

for all
v,w\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
. Given a Poisson Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})\Rightarrow M
, the
\mathcal{A}
-Poisson structure
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
anchors
\mathcal{A}^{*}
to
\mathcal{A}
via the sharp map determined by
\beta(\pi^{\sharp}_{\mathcal{A}}(\alpha))=\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(\alpha,\beta)
for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}^{*}
. Also, it induces a bracket on
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}^{*})
via the formula

[\alpha,\beta]_{\pi_{\mathcal{A}}}=\mathcal{L}_{\pi_{\mathcal{A}}^{\sharp}(\alpha)}(\beta)-\mathcal{L}_{\pi_{\mathcal{A}}^{\sharp}(\beta)}(\alpha)-d_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\pi_{\mathcal{A}}(\alpha,\beta)\right).

Together with the anchor map
\rho_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}\circ\pi_{\mathcal{A}}^{\sharp}
, the vector bundle
\mathcal{A}^{*}
becomes a Lie algebroid whose differential on
\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}\mathcal{A})
corresponds to
[\pi_{\mathcal{A}},\cdot]
, from which easily follows that the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
is a Lie bialgebroid. The data
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
constitutes a triangular Lie bialgebroid, introduced in
[
14
]
. In the context of Lie bialgebras, the
\mathcal{A}
-Poisson structure
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
is called the

-matrix.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})\Rightarrow M
be a Poisson Lie algebroid. It induces a Poisson structure
\pi
on
TM
via the following diagram.

Let

1.3  Algebraic aspects of the Poisson diffeomorphisms group

Throughout this section, we fix a Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
. A
Poisson diffeomorphism
of
(M,\pi)
is a diffeomorphism
\varphi\in\Diff(M)
that is also a Poisson map, i.e.
\varphi_{*}(\pi)=\pi
. They form the group
\Diff(M,\pi)
of Poisson diffeomorphisms. A vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is
Poisson
when
\mathcal{L}_{X}(\pi)=0
. They form a subalgebra
\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)
of
\mathfrak{X}(M)
under the Lie bracket.

The Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
has an underlying symplectic foliation, leading to the group of
foliated Poisson diffeomorphisms

\Fol(M,\pi)
, consisting of the maps that send each symplectic leaf symplectomorphically to itself. Infinitesimally, the
foliated Poisson vector fields
, constituting a subalgebra
\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)
of
\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)
, are those Poisson vector fields that are tangent to the symplectic foliation.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)
has two more interesting subalgebras. Let
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)
and
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{ex}}(M)
be the closed and exact one-forms on
M
, respectively. We introduce the Lie algebras of Hamiltonian and locally Hamiltonian vector fields, respectively, as

\mathfrak{ham}(M,\pi)=\pi^{\sharp}\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{ex}}(M)\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)=\pi^{\sharp}\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(M)\right).

A diffeomorphism is
(locally) Hamiltonian
if is the time-1 flow of a (locally) Hamiltonian vector field generated by a time-dependent (closed) exact one-form. These give rise to the group
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{(loc)}}(M,\pi)
of (locally) Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms. Clearly, there are inclusions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{ham}(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi),}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ham(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Fol(M,\pi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Diff(M,\pi).}

Proposition 1.16
.

The groups
\Fol(M,\pi)
,
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
and
\Ham(M,\pi)
are normal subgroups of
\Diff(M,\pi)
.

Proof.

Establishing that
\Ham(M,\pi)
and
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
are actually subgroups goes via the usual argument: if
\varphi_{t}
and
\psi_{t}
are isotopies generated by (locally) Hamiltonian vector fields
\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{t})
and
\pi^{\sharp}(\beta_{t})
, respectively, then
\varphi_{t}\circ\psi_{t}
is generated by

\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{t})+(\varphi_{t})_{*}\pi^{\sharp}(\beta_{t})=\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{t}+(\varphi_{t})_{*}\beta_{t}),

which is again (locally) Hamiltonian.

The foliated diffeomorphisms clearly form a normal subgroup of
\Diff(M,\pi)
. Normality of
\Ham(M,\pi)
and
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)
also follows from the identity
\varphi_{*}(\pi^{\sharp}(\alpha))=\pi^{\sharp}(\varphi_{*}(\alpha))
, which holds for general
\varphi\in\Diff(M,\pi)
and
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.
∎

Lemma 1.17
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
be an isotopy on
M
generated by
X_{t}
. Then
\varphi_{t}
is a path in
\Diff(M,\pi)
(resp. in
\Fol(M,\pi)
) if and only if
X_{t}
is in
\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)
(resp. in
\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)
).

Remark 1.18
.

A similar result for the (locally) Hamiltonian groups is hard. For symplectic manifolds, the argument relies on a flux homomorphism, which is not available for general Poisson manifolds. It is one of the interesting open questions regarding the Poisson diffeomorphism group.

There are other open questions that will not be addressed in this paper. For instance, if a time-dependent vector field
X_{t}
is Hamiltonian for all
t
, is it generated by a smooth time-dependent function?

Remark 1.19
.

The infinitesimal algebraic structure of
\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)
is encoded in the first Poisson cohomology group. For example,

\frac{\mathfrak{X}(M,\pi)}{\mathfrak{ham}(M,\pi)}=H^{1}_{\pi}(M),\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)}{\mathfrak{ham}(M,\pi)}=\operatorname{im}\left(\pi^{\sharp}:H^{1}(M)\to H^{1}_{\pi}(M)\right).

When
\pi
has constant rank, and induced by the symplectic foliation
(\mathcal{F},\omega)
, then

\frac{\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)}{\mathfrak{ham}(M,\pi)}=H^{1}(\mathcal{F}),\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)}{\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(M,\pi)}=\frac{H^{1}(\mathcal{F})}{\operatorname{im}\left(H^{1}(M)\to H^{1}(\mathcal{F})\right)}.

Remark 1.20
.

There are many more interesting subgroups of
\Diff(M,\pi)
. For instance, it is very natural to study
leafwise Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms
, which are foliated Poisson diffeomorphisms that restrict to a Hamiltonian diffeomorphism on each leaf. All these different subgroups illustrate that the algebraic structure of the Poisson diffeomorphism group is more intricate than the symplectomorphism group of a symplectic manifold: the latter is determined by the Hamiltonian diffeomorphism and (a quotient of)
H^{1}(M)
(via a flux homomorphism, see
[
16
]
, Chapter 10).

Many of the concepts introduced in this section can be defined on an arbitrary Poisson Lie algebroid
(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})\Rightarrow M
, as we will now briefly discuss. A
Poisson section
is a section
v\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathcal{L}_{v}(\pi_{\mathcal{A}})=[v,\pi_{\mathcal{A}}]=0
, defining a subalgebra
\Gamma(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
. The closed
\mathcal{A}
-forms,
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{A})
form a Lie subalgebra of
\Omega^{1}(\mathcal{A})
under the bracket on
\mathcal{A}^{*}
(Remark
1.10
). Therefore, we obtain a subalgebra of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
, called the
\mathcal{A}
-locally Hamiltonians, by

\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})=\pi_{\mathcal{A}}^{\sharp}\left(\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{cl}}(\mathcal{A})\right).

An automorphism of
\mathcal{A}
is
\mathcal{A}
-locally Hamiltonian when it is the time-1 flow of a
\mathcal{A}
-locally Hamiltonian vector field generated by a time-dependent closed
\mathcal{A}
-form, forming the group
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
regarded as a subgroup of the group
\InnAut(\mathcal{A})
of inner automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
.

1.4  Poisson structure of divisor type

To provide context to several central examples considered in this paper, we give a brief summary of the Poisson structures of divisor type developed by Klaasse in
[
10
]
.

A (real)
divisor
on a smooth manifold
M
is a pair
(L,\sigma)
consisting of a (real) line bundle
L\to M
and a section
\sigma\in\Gamma(L)
whose zero locus
Z_{\sigma}
is nowhere dense.

The section
\sigma
of a divisor
(L,\sigma)
defines an evaluation map
\sigma:\Gamma(L^{*})\to C^{\infty}(M)
, and the ideal of
C^{\infty}(M)
associated to
(L,\sigma)
is its image
I_{\sigma}=\sigma(\Gamma(L^{*}))
. Note that the
vanishing set
Z_{I_{\sigma}}
of
I_{\sigma}
is exactly
Z_{\sigma}
. The vanishing ideal
I_{Z_{\sigma}}
of
Z_{\sigma}
is generally larger than
I_{\sigma}
. An ideal
I\subset C^{\infty}(M)
that comes from a divisor is a
divisor ideal.

Every divisor ideal is locally principal, as it is generated by
\langle\alpha,\sigma\rangle
, where
\alpha
is a local trivialization of
L^{*}
.

Definition 1.21

(
[
10
]
, Definition 3.6)
.

A Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is
of divisor type
when

\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{A})=\left(\det(\mathcal{A}^{*})\otimes\det(TM),\det(\rho_{\mathcal{A}})\right)

is a divisor, or, equivalently, when the isomorphism locus of
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
is dense in
M
. The divisor ideal associated to
\operatorname{div}(\mathcal{A})
is denoted
I_{\mathcal{A}}
.
∎

Remark 1.22
.

A Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is of divisor type if and only if the isomorphism locus of the anchor
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, which is the set on which
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
is an isomorphism, is dense in
M
.

Let
I\subset C^{\infty}(M)
be an ideal and
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
a Lie algebroid. Following
[
10
]
, we set

\Gamma(\mathcal{A})_{I}=\left\{v\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}):\mathcal{L}_{v}(I)\subset I\right\},

the set of sections of
\mathcal{A}
that preserve
I
. This is a subalgebra of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
(
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.41).

When
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})_{I}
is locally finitely generated and projective, the Serre-Swan theorem gives a vector bundle
\mathcal{A}_{I}\to M
such that
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{I})=\Gamma(\mathcal{A})_{I}
. The inclusion of sections determines a bundle map
\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{I}}^{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}_{I}\to\mathcal{A}
. Together with the Lie bracket on
\Gamma(\mathcal{A}_{I})
inherited from
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
, and with anchor
\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{I}}=\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{I}}^{\mathcal{A}}\circ\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, it becomes a Lie algebroid anchored to
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 1.23

(
[
10
]
, Definition 3.42)

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid and

\mathcal{A}_{I}\Rightarrow M

associated to

.
∎

This is often applied in the case that

A divisor ideal

By the Serre-Swan theorem, a divisor ideal

(

A Poisson structure

One of the main results in

Before we discuss some examples, we want to highlight the following proposition, which explains the significance of the Lie algebroid

from the perspective of Poisson diffeomorphisms: because

contains all the Poisson vector fields, an integration of

Proposition 1.26
.

Let

be a Poisson manifold of divisor type whose divisor ideal

is projective. Then any Poisson vector field lifts to a section of

.

Proof.

Let

. Then there exists a top-form

such that

. If

is a Poisson vector field, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}(f)

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{X}(\omega),\wedge^{n}\pi\rangle+\langle\omega,\mathcal{L}_{X}(\wedge^{n}\pi)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{L}_{X}(\omega),\wedge^{n}\pi\rangle\in I_{\pi}.

It follows that

is a section of

.
∎

, Lemma 3.4).
∎

1.5  Scattering-symplectic manifolds

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid. A subalgebroid of
\mathcal{A}
is a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow Z
together with an injective Lie algebroid homomorphism
(\iota,i):\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
that makes
i:Z\hookrightarrow M
into a submanifold of
M
. Usually, we identify a subalgebroid
\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow Z
with its image in
\mathcal{A}
.

Given a subalgebroid
\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow Z
of
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
, we consider the module of sections of
\mathcal{A}
that are "tangent" to
\mathcal{B}
:

\Gamma(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\left\{v\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}):v|_{Z}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{B})\right\}.

If
Z\subset M
is a closed hypersurface, this is a locally finitely generated subalgebra of
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
and therefore by the Serre-Swan theorem corresponds to a vector bundle
[\mathcal{A};\mathcal{B}]\rightarrow M
that inherits a Lie algebroid structure from
\mathcal{A}

[
7
,
12
]
. The Lie algebroid
[\mathcal{A};\mathcal{B}]\Rightarrow M
is the
rescaling
of
\mathcal{A}
by
\mathcal{B}
.

Example 1.31
.

Let
(M,Z)
be a smooth log-manifold. Then
TZ\Rightarrow Z
is a subalgebroid of
TM
, and the rescaling
[TM;TZ]
is exactly the log-tangent bundle
T_{Z}M
from Example
1.27
.
∎

Example 1.32

(Zero-rescalings)
.

The zero algebroid
0_{Z}\Rightarrow Z
is a subalgebroid of any Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
. Then
[\mathcal{A};0_{Z}]
is called the
zero-rescaling
of
\mathcal{A}
over
Z
. In the case that
\mathcal{A}=TM
, the resulting algebroid is called the
0-tangent bundle

{}^{0}T_{Z}M
. It’s sections correspond to vector fields on
M
that vanish over
Z
.
∎

Example 1.33

(Scattering manifolds)
.

More interesting is the zero-rescaling of
T_{Z}M
over
Z
. The resulting Lie algebroid
{}^{sc}T_{Z}M=[T_{Z}M;0_{Z}]
is the
scattering-tangent
bundle. A Poisson structure
\pi
in
M
is scattering-symplectic when it lifts to a symplectic structure
\omega=\pi^{-1}
on
{}^{sc}T_{Z}M
. We refer to
(M,Z,\omega)
as a scattering-symplectic manifold.

Scattering-symplectic structures are ubiquitous. For example, every even dimensional sphere admists a scattering-symplectic structure, whose hypersurface is the equator
[
12
]
.
∎

The following proposition is a consequence of the Serre-Swan theorem.

Proposition 1.34
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid and
\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow Z
a subalgebroid over a hypersurface
Z\subset M
. Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
be a bundle map that restricts to a bundle map
\varphi|_{\mathcal{B}}=\psi:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
. Then
\varphi
lifts to a (unique) map
[\varphi;\psi]:[\mathcal{A};\mathcal{B}]\to[\mathcal{A};\mathcal{B}]
. If
\varphi
and
\psi
are Lie algebroid morphisms, then so is
[\varphi;\psi]
.

As a consequence, every morphism of the log-tangent bundle
T_{Z}f:T_{Z}M\to T_{Z}M
induces a morphisms of the scattering-tangent bundle
{}^{sc}T_{Z}f:{}^{sc}T_{Z}M\to{}^{sc}T_{Z}M
.

2
Coisotropic bisections

In this section, we establish general properties of coisotropic bisections of Poisson groupoids and their connection to Poisson diffeomorphisms. After that, we show that they form a Lie group whenever the Poisson groupoid is linearizable.

2.1  Coisotropic bisections of a Poisson groupoid

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be a groupoid. A bisection
\sigma\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is a section of the source map
s:\mathcal{G}\to M
with the property that
l_{\sigma}:=t\circ\sigma:M\to M
is a diffeomorphism. Any bisection corresponds to and is determined by a left translation map

L_{\sigma}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G},\hskip 10.00002ptg\mapsto m(\sigma(t(g)),g).

If
X\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
is a section of the Lie algebroid of
\mathcal{A}
, then the flow
\varphi_{t}
of its right-invariant vector field
\overrightarrow{X}
gives rise to a family of bisections
\sigma_{t}=\varphi_{t}|_{M}
, and in fact, it holds that
\varphi_{t}=L_{\sigma_{t}}
.

Bisections of a Lie groupoid form a group, and can be multiplied: if
\sigma,\tau\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, we define
\sigma\cdot\tau
as

\sigma\cdot\tau(x)=m(\sigma(t\circ\tau(x)),\tau(x)),\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{ for $x\in M$,}

or, equivalently, as
L_{\sigma\cdot\tau}=L_{\sigma}\circ L_{\tau}
. Several authors already described
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
as a Lie group, e.g. in
[
22
,
23
]
.

A Poisson groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
is a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
with a Poisson structure
\Pi
on the space of arrows that is multiplicative: the graph of the multiplication map is a coisotropic submanifold of
(\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G},(-\Pi)\times\Pi\times\Pi)

[
26
]
. For each multiplicative Poisson structure
\Pi
on a groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
, there is a unique Poisson structure
\pi
on
M
for which the target map
t:(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
is a Poisson map. This allows us to talk about Poisson groupoids
over
a fixed Poisson manifold
(M,\pi)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid. A bisection
\sigma\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is
coisotropic
when
\operatorname{im}\sigma
is a coisotropic submanifold of
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. The collection of coisotropic bisections is denoted by
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
.
∎

We establish some properties of coisotropic bisections.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid.

(i).

The coisotropic bisections form a subgroup
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.

(ii).

A bisection
\sigma\in\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is coisotropic if and only if
L_{\sigma}
is a Poisson map.

(iii).

For any
\sigma\in\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
, the induced diffeomorphism on the base
l_{\sigma}:M\to M
is a Poisson map.

(iv).

The image of a coisotropic bisection is a Lagrangian submanifold.

Remark 2.3
.

Although every coisotropic bisection is Lagrangian for any Poisson groupoid, we reserve the term "Lagrangian bisection" (a bisection with Lagrangian image) for symplectic groupoids only.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from the second, while
(ii)
and
(iii)
can be proved by means of the coisotropic calculus developed in
[
26
]
. Let us briefly sketch how. The central idea in
[
26
]
is to relax the notion of a Poisson map from
(M,\pi_{M})
to
(N,\pi_{N})
to that of a
Poisson relation

r:(M,\pi_{M})\dashrightarrow(N,\pi_{N})
, which is a coisotropic submanifold of
(N\times M,\pi_{N}\times(-\pi_{M}))
. By Proposition
1.2
, every Poisson map defines a Poisson relation via its graph. Weinstein proved in
[
26
]
that, under a "very clean" condition (loc. cit., Definition 1.3.7), Poisson relations compose to Poisson relations (loc. cit., Theorem 1.3.9).

To prove
(ii)
and
(iii)
, we only have to write the maps as a composition of coisotropic relations, and check that they satisfy the "very clean" condition. For instance, if
\sigma
is a coisotropic bisection, then its image can be regarded as a relation
\sigma:\{*\}\dashrightarrow(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. We can write
L_{\sigma}
as a composition of the Poisson relations

\sigma\times\id=\{(k,h,g)\in\mathcal{G}^{3}:g=h,k\in\Im\sigma\}:\mathcal{G}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}^{2}

and the multiplication map
m:\mathcal{G}^{2}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}
. It is slightly tedious but straightforward to show that this composition is "very clean", so that it composes to a Poisson relation, which is exactly the map
L_{\sigma}
. Conversely, if
L_{\sigma}
is a Poisson map, then
\Im\sigma=L_{\sigma}(M)
is coisotropic because
M
is. Statement
(iii)
can be proved in a similar fashion, using that the target map
t:(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\to(M,\pi)
is Poisson.

As for
(iv)
, we only have to show that the unit section
M\subset\mathcal{G}
is a Lagrangian, because of
(ii)
. To do so, we apply Lemma
1.5
. For
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in(\Pi^{\sharp})^{-1}(TM)
we can write

\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}+s^{*}\gamma_{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\alpha_{2}=\beta_{2}+t^{*}\gamma_{2},

for
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in(TM)^{\circ}
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in T^{*}M
. It follows that

\Pi(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})=\Pi(s^{*}\gamma_{1},t^{*}\gamma_{2})+\Pi(\alpha_{1},\beta_{2})+\Pi(\beta_{1},\alpha_{2})-\Pi(\beta_{1},\beta_{2}).

The first term vanishes because
(\ker Ts)^{\circ}
and
(\ker Tt)^{\circ}
are Poisson orthogonal for Poisson groupoids (
[
26
]
, Proposition 4.2.6), the second and third therm are zero because
\beta_{i}\in(TM)^{\circ}
and
\Pi^{\sharp}(\alpha_{j})\in TM
, and the last term vanishes because
M
is coisotropic in
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. Thus,
M
is Lagrangian.
∎

Proposition 2.4

(Curves of coisotropic bisections)
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows M
be a Poisson groupoid with Lie bialgebroid
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
. The following Lie algebras are isomorphic.

(i).

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}^{R}(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
of right-invariant Poisson vector fields, equipped with the Lie bracket.

(ii).

The Lie algebra
\Gamma(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})
of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
-closed sections of
\mathcal{A}
, equipped with the bracket on
\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
.

The isomorphism is given by restricting
\overrightarrow{v}\in\mathfrak{X}^{R}(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
along
M
.
Moreover, if an isotopy
\sigma_{t}
of bisections on
\mathcal{G}
is generated by
v_{t}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
, then
\sigma_{t}\in\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
for all
t
if and only if
v_{t}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})
.

Proof.

The differential
d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}
is determined by
\overrightarrow{d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}v}=[\Pi,\overrightarrow{v}]
for
v\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A})
(
[
13
]
, Corollary 11.4.9), which proves that the correspondence is an isomorphism. The second statement now follows directly from combining Proposition
2.2
,
(ii)
with Lemma
1.17
.
∎

Example 2.5

(Linear Poisson structures)
.

It is well-known that there is a duality between linear Poisson structures and Lie algebroids (
[
13
]
, Chapter 9). Therefore, we always think of a vector bundle
\mathcal{A}^{*}\to M
with a linear Poisson structure
\pi_{\mathrm{lin}}
on its total space as the dual of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
. Since
(\mathcal{A}^{*},\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
can be interpreted as a Poisson groupoid, a section
\alpha:M\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
with coisotropic image is always a Lagrangian submanifold by Proposition
2.2
. The coisotropic bisections of
(\mathcal{A}^{*},\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
form a vector space by Proposition
2.6
below, and will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
2.12
.
∎

Proposition 2.6

(
[
24
]
, Corollary 2.10)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be a Lie algebroid. The image of a section
\alpha\in\Gamma(\mathcal{A}^{*})
is coisotropic in
(\mathcal{A}^{*},\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
if and only if
d_{\mathcal{A}}\alpha=0
, in which case it is a Lagrangian submanifold.

2.2  The Lie group of (coisotropic) bisections

The convenient setting of Kriegl and Michor
[
11
]
is a framework for infinite-dimensional manifolds that is based on smooth curves. At it’s core lies Boman’s theorem, which states that a map
f:M\to N
between finite dimensional manifolds
M
and
N
is smooth if for every smooth curve
c
in
M
, the curve
f\circ c
is smooth in
N
. Likewise, smooth maps between convenient manifolds are characterized by the same property, making smoothness easy to check.

A (convenient) Lie group
G
is a convenient manifold together with a group structure for which the multiplication
m:G\times G\to G
and inversion
\iota:G\to G
are smooth. The tangent space
\mathfrak{g}=T_{e}G
at the identity inherits a Lie bracket from
G
.

In the following, we set
C^{\infty}((\mathbb{R},0),(G,e))
to be the space of smooth curves
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G
with
\gamma(0)=e
. The
(right-)logarithmic derivative

\delta^{r}:C^{\infty}((\mathbb{R},0),(G,e))\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{g})
is defined on a smooth curve
\gamma
by

(\delta^{r}\gamma)(t)=\frac{d}{d\epsilon}\big|_{\epsilon=0}\gamma(t+\epsilon)\gamma(t)^{-1}.

A Lie group is
regular
when the inverse of
\delta^{r}
,

\operatorname{Evol}^{r}_{G}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{g})\to C^{\infty}((\mathbb{R},0),(G,e))

exists and is bijective, and the time-1 evolution map

\operatorname{evol}^{r}_{G}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R},\mathfrak{g})\to G,\hskip 10.00002pt\operatorname{evol}^{r}_{G}(\gamma)=\operatorname{Evol}^{r}_{G}(\gamma)(1)

is smooth. When restricted to constant curves in
\mathfrak{g}
, the evolution map coincides with the usual exponential map for Lie groups. All the Lie groups in this paper are regular. In fact, to the author’s knowledge, up until now all known convenient Lie groups are regular.

Let
G
be a Lie group. An
initial
Lie subgroup is a Lie group
H
together with an injective smooth group homomorphism
i:H\to G
which is an initial map, meaning that a curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to H
is smooth if and only if
i\circ\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G
is. Note that this implies that the tangent map
T_{h}i:T_{h}H\to T_{h}G
is injective. We do not consider immersed submanifolds in the context of infinite dimensional manifolds.

An initial Lie subgroup
i:H\to G
is
embedded
when
H
is an embedded submanifold of
G
, that is,
i:H\to G
is a topological embedding and
G
admits charts
(\mathcal{V},\Phi)
modelled on a convenient vector space
V
covering
H
for which
\Phi(H\cap\mathcal{V})=W\cap\Phi(\mathcal{V})
, where
W\subset V
is a closed subspace.

The following lemma will be of use later.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
11
]
, 38.7)
.

Let
G
be a regular Lie group and
i:K\hookrightarrow G
an initial subgroup with the following property: there exists an open neighbourhood
U
of
e
in
G
and a smooth mapping
p:U\to E
into a convenient vector space
E
such that
p^{-1}(0)=K\cap U
and
p
is constant on the left cosets
Kg\cap U
. Then
K
is regular.

2.2.1 The Lie group of bisections

Interestingly, the group of bisections of a Lie groupoid can always be given the structure of a Lie group.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
22
]
, Theorem 2.2)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be a (not necessarily Hausdorff) Lie groupoid with Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
over a Hausdorff manifold
M
. Then
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is a regular (Hausdorff) Lie group integrating the Lie algebra
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})
of compactly supported sections of
\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 2.9
.

A more recent paper on the group of bisections of a Lie groupoid is the work by Schmeding and Wockel
[
23
]
. While the current paper is set up in in the convenient setting, they are using the so-called Bastiani calculus to describe their infinite-dimensional manifolds. An fairly recent overview of Lie theory in infinite dimensions, using the Bastiani calculus, can be found in
[
18
]
. Although the Bastiani calculus seems to be more popular in the literature (to the author’s awareness), we chose to work in the convenient setting for two reasons: first, it requires a less technical setup, and second, the paper by Rybicki
[
22
]
, using the convenient setting, proves Theorem
2.8
also for non-Hausdorff Lie groupoids.

For the spaces considered in this paper, the two settings aren’t too far off: they even agree up to the level of Fréchet spaces. Thus, one can expect the results in this paper to be valid in the MB-setting.

To proceed to the group of coisotropic bisections, a description of the smooth structure on
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is necessary. In this paper, the arrow space of a groupoid is assumed to be finite-dimensional.

Charts

A chart of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
around a bisection
\sigma
can be obtained from any tubular neighbourhood as follows. Identify
N\operatorname{im}\sigma\cong\sigma^{*}\ker Ts=:\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
. Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\supset U\to V\subset\mathcal{G}
be a tubular neighbourhood around
\operatorname{im}\sigma
. Define the
C^{1}
-opens

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}):\begin{subarray}{c}\mbox{ $\operatorname{im}v\subset U$}\\
\mbox{ $s\circ\varphi\circ v$ and $t\circ\varphi\circ v$ are diffeo's}\end{subarray}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=\left\{\tau\in\Bis_{c}(\mathcal{G}):\begin{subarray}{c}\mbox{$\operatorname{im}\tau\subset V$}\\
\mbox{ $p_{\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}}\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ\tau$ is a diffeo}\end{subarray}\right\},

and the map
\Phi:\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{U}
by

\Phi(\tau)=\varphi^{-1}\circ\tau\circ\left(p_{\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}}\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ\tau\right)^{-1}\in\mathcal{U}.

Then
(\mathcal{V},\Phi)
is a chart modelled on
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma})
around the identity. The crux is that any tubular neighbourhood around
\operatorname{im}\sigma
defines a chart of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
around
\sigma
this way.

Topological and smooth structure

Let
E\to M
be a vector bundle. The space of smooth sections
\Gamma(E)
, together with the topology of uniform convergence on compact subsets of each derivative separately, is a topological vector space (which is Fréchet when
M
is second countable) (
[
11
]
, 30.3). A curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\Gamma(E)
is smooth if and only if the associated map
\gamma^{\wedge}:\mathbb{R}\times M\to E
is smooth (
[
11
]
, 27.17).

Consider a compact set
K\subset M
, and denote by
\Gamma_{K}(E)
the space of sections of
E
with support in
K
. This is a closed subspace of
\Gamma(E)
. The space of compactly supported sections
\Gamma_{c}(E)
is endowed with the direct limit locally convex topology inherited from
\Gamma_{K}(E)
, ranging over compact subsets
K
of
M
(
[
11
]
, 30.4).

A curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\Gamma_{c}(E)
is smooth if and only if the associated map
\gamma^{\wedge}:\mathbb{R}\times M\to E
is smooth and is
timely proper
in the following sense: for every bounded interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists a compact
K\subset M
for which
\gamma(t)
is constant on
M\setminus K
for all
t\in[a,b]
(
[
11
]
, 42.5).

As the group of bisections of a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is modelled on
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})
, we have the following characterization of smooth curves in
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 2.10

(
[
21
]
, Lemma 3.3)
.

A curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\Bis(\mathcal{G})
is smooth if and only its associated map
\gamma^{\wedge}:\mathbb{R}\times M\to\mathcal{G}
is smooth and
\gamma
is timely proper: for every bounded interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
there exists a compact
K\subset M
such that
\gamma(t)
is constant on
M\setminus K
for all
t\in[a,b]
.

Remark 2.11
.

Convenient manifolds are assumed to be
smoothly Hausdorff
, which means that the space of smooth functions separates points in the manifold. The group of bisections of a Lie groupoid is smoothly Hausdorff, because it is topologically Hausdorff and is modeled on a convenient vector space that admits partitions of unity (
[
11
]
, 16.10).

2.2.2 The Lie group of coisotropic bisections

Under the assumption of linearizability of a Poisson groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
around
M
, the coisotropic bisections form a Lie group.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid with bialgebroid
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
. Assume that
(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is linearizable around
M
. Then
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is a regular embedded Lie subgroup of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, whose Lie algebra corresponds to
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})=\{v\in\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}):d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}v=0\}
, the space of closed compactly supported one-forms of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

We will construct charts of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
adapted to
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. Choose a linearization
\varphi:(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})\supset U\to V\subset(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
around
M
. Let
\sigma\in\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. As
L_{\sigma}
is a Poisson map,
L_{\sigma}\circ\varphi
linearizes
\Pi
around
\operatorname{im}\sigma
. Additionally, let
(\mathcal{V},\Phi)
be the chart obtained from
\varphi
as described above. Then, according to Proposition
2.6
,
\tau\in\mathcal{V}
is coisotropic if and only if
\Phi(\tau)
is coisotropic in
\mathcal{A}
, which happens if and only if
d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\Phi(\tau)=0
. It follows that
\Phi(\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)\cap\mathcal{V})=\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})\cap\Phi(\mathcal{V})
. Hence,
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is an embedded submanifold of
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, modelled on
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})
, that is closed with respect to the compact-open
C^{1}
-topology (
[
11
]
, 41.9). This shows that
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is an embedded Lie subgroup.

By Proposition
2.4
, the Lie algebra of
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
with this Lie group structure is exactly
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A},d_{\mathcal{A}^{*}})
.

For regularity, we use Lemma
2.7
. Consider the map
p:\Bis(\mathcal{G})\to\mathfrak{X}^{2}(\mathcal{G})
given by
p(\sigma)=(L_{\sigma})^{*}(\Pi)
. This map is smooth: if
\sigma_{t}
is a smooth curve in
\Bis(\mathcal{G})
, then the map
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{G}\ni(t,g)\mapsto(L_{\sigma_{t}})^{*}(\Pi)_{g}\in\wedge^{2}T\mathcal{G}
is smooth, so by the discussion in section
2.2
,
L_{\sigma_{t}}(\Pi)
is a smooth curve in
\mathfrak{X}^{2}(\mathcal{G})
. From Proposition
2.2

(ii)
it follows that
p^{-1}(0)=\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. Also,
p
is constant on the left cosets of
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
. Hence
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Pi)
is regular by Lemma
2.7
.
∎

2.3  Lagrangian bisections of symplectic groupoids

When
(\mathcal{G},\Omega)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
is a symplectic groupoid, the Poisson structure is always linearizable around
M
by Weinstein’s Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem. As a special case of Theorem
2.12
, we recover the result by Rybicki
[
21
]
: the group of Lagrangian bisections
\Bis(\mathcal{G},\Omega)
of a symplectic groupoid is a regular Lie group, whose Lie algebra is given by closed, compactly supported one-forms. Already this group has many interesting connections with the Poisson diffeomorphism group on the base.

Analogous to Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms on symplectic manifolds, one has the notion of (compactly supported)
exact
Lagrangian bisections
\Bis_{\mathrm{ex},c}(\mathcal{G},\Omega)
. These are the time-one flows of (compactly supported) exact time-dependent one-forms
\Omega^{1}_{\mathrm{ex},c}(M)
. By means of a flux homomorphism for Lagrangian bisections
[
21
,
27
]
, the exact sequence of Lie algebras

3.1  Almost injective Lie algebroids

Many interesting Lie algebroids are determined by the image of the anchor on sections.

Definition 3.1
.

A Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is
almost injective
if the anchor is injective on sections.
∎

Isomorphisms of almost injective Lie algebroids are uniquely determined by the base map.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be an almost injective Lie algebroid and
f\in\Diff(M)
. Then
Tf
extends to a (necessarily unique) Lie algebroid isomorphism
(\varphi,f):\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
if and only if
f_{*}(\rho(\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})))=\rho(\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}))
.

Proof.

One implication is obvious. For the reverse, we first recall that for a vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, the push-forward by
f
is defined by
(f_{*}X)_{x}=T_{f^{-1}(x)}f(X_{f^{-1}(x)})
. Since
\rho
is injective on the level of sections, there is a unique map
f_{*}:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})
determined by
f_{*}\rho(v)=\rho(f_{*}v)
. This factors through a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear map
\varphi:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\Gamma_{c}(f^{*}\mathcal{A})
, defined by
\varphi(v)=(f_{*}v)\circ f
. Thus, by the Serre-Swan theorem, we obtain bundle maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

The composite, that we will denote by
(\varphi,f)
, clearly induces
f_{*}
on the level of sections. It is invertible because the same construction with
f^{-1}
gives the inverse. Finally, it is easily checked that this map is a Lie algebroid morphism.
∎

Almost injective Lie algebroids are always integrable. Moreover, the holonomy groupoid (the final object in the category of integrations of
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
) can be recovered from any integration (cf.
[
5
]
).

Proposition 3.3

(
[
1
]
, Proposition 1.9)
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be any integration of an almost-injective Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
. Then, as a set,
\Hol(\mathcal{A})\rightrightarrows M
is given by
\left(\mathcal{G}/\sim\right)\rightrightarrows M
, where

g\sim h\iff\begin{subarray}{c}\mbox{there exists local bisections $\sigma_{g}$ and $\sigma_{h}$}\\
\mbox{ through $g$ and $h$ such that $t\circ\sigma_{g}=t\circ\sigma_{h}$.}\end{subarray}

The holonomy groupoid
\Hol(\mathcal{A})\rightrightarrows M
carries the unique structure of a Lie groupoid for which the map
\mathcal{G}\to\Hol(\mathcal{A})
is a local diffeomorphism.

It follows that the groupoid anchor
\chi:\Hol(\mathcal{A})\to M\times M
induces an injective Lie group homomorphism
\chi_{*}:\Bis(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))\to\Bis(M\times M)\cong\Diff(M)
which differentiates at the identity to the injective map
\rho:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
.

Recall that the inner automorphisms
\InnAut(\mathcal{A})
of a Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
are given by the time-1 flows of (time-dependent) Lie algebroid sections. If
M
is not compact, we also want to consider
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
, the inner automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}
with compact support. For an almost injective Lie algebroid, an automorphism
(\varphi,f):\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
is uniquely determined by
f
, and can thus be regarded as an element of
\Diff(M)
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be an almost injective Lie algebroid. Then the group
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
is a regular Lie group.

Proof.

The group
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds to
\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))
, the identity component of the group of compactly supported bisections of
\Hol(\mathcal{A})
, which is a regular Lie group.
∎

We expect that the inclusion
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})\hookrightarrow\Diff_{c}(M)
is an initial map. However, we could only prove it under additional assumptions, as we will now explain.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be an almost injective Lie algebroid. We call
\mathcal{A}

isotopically closed
when
\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})
generates all of its isotopies of inner automorphisms. That is, if an isotopy
\varphi_{t}
in
\Diff(M)
generated by
X_{t}
is such that
\varphi_{t}\in\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
for all
t
, then
X_{t}\in\rho(\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}))
for all
t
.
∎

Example 3.6
.

A regular foliation
\mathcal{F}\subset TM
is always isotopically closed. More generally, if
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is an almost injective Lie algebroid such that
X\in\rho(\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}))
if and only if
X
is tangent to the leaves of
\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is isotopically closed. In particular, the log-tangent bundle of a log-manifold is isotopically closed.
∎

Example 3.7
.

If
I
be a projective divisor ideal (Definition
1.24
), then
T_{I}M
is isotopically closed. Indeed, if
\varphi
is an inner automorphism, then
\varphi
is the time-1 flow of a (time-dependent) vector field that preserves
I
, and so
\varphi^{*}I=I
. If
\varphi_{t}
is an isotopy of inner automorphisms, smooth in
\Diff(M)
and generated by
X_{t}
, then

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi^{*}_{t}(I)=\varphi_{t}^{*}\left(\mathcal{L}_{X_{t}}(I)\right)\subset I,

hence
X_{t}
is in
\rho(\Gamma(T_{I}M))
.

In particular, the (normal-crossing) log-tangent bundle (Example
1.27
) and the elliptic tangent bundle (Example
1.29
) are isotopically closed.
∎

Example 3.8
.

If
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is an isotopically closed almost injective Lie algebroid, and
\mathcal{B}\Rightarrow Z
an (almost injective) isotopically closed subalgebroid over a hypersurface
Z\subset M
, then the rescaling
[\mathcal{A};\mathcal{B}]
is also isotopically closed. This applies to the scattering-tangent bundle (Example
1.33
).
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In all of the above examples, the anchor also provides an embedding
\rho:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
of topological vector spaces, and thus it is an initial map. In general, when
\rho(\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A}))
is closed in
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
, the map
\rho
is an embedding by the open mapping theorem (
[
9
]
, Theorem 5.5.2) for bornological webbed spaces to inductive limits of Baire topological vector spaces (both of which include inductive limits of Fréchet spaces, which is our case) .

We expect that every almost injective Lie algebroid is isotopically closed, at least for those for which the anchor
\rho:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
is an embedding. However, we were not able to prove it in this paper. Therefore, it is added as a separate assumption in the proposition below.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
be an
isotopically closed
almost injective Lie algebroid for which
\rho:\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
is an embedding of topological vector spaces. Then
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
is a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff_{c,0}(M)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi_{\epsilon}
be an isotopy in
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
that is smooth in
\Diff_{c}(M)
, and generated by
X_{\epsilon}\in\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is isotopically closed, it follows that
X_{\epsilon}=\rho(v_{\epsilon})
for some
v_{\epsilon}\in\Gamma_{c}(\mathcal{A})
. Because
\rho
is an embedding, it is initial, and therefore
v_{\epsilon}
is a smooth curve in
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})=\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))
. By regularity of
\Bis(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))
,
v_{\epsilon}
integrates to a smooth curve
\sigma_{\epsilon}
in
\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))
. Now, since
\mathcal{A}
is almost-injective, the curve
t\circ\sigma_{\epsilon}
is generated by
X_{\epsilon}
, and therefore must agree with
\varphi_{\epsilon}
. It follows that
\varphi_{\epsilon}
is smooth in
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be any integration of an almost injective Lie algebroid
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
. Since
\mathcal{G}\to\Hol(\mathcal{A})
is an isomorphism in a neighbourhood of
M
, the map
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})\to\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))
is surjective with discrete kernel

K=\ker\left(\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})\to\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A})\right)=\ker\left((\chi_{G})_{*}:\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})\to\Diff_{c,0}(M)\right)

Although topological discreteness is not enough to prove that
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})/K
is a Lie group (see
[
11
]
, 38.5), there is still an isomorphism of groups

\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})/K\to\Bis_{c,0}(\Hol(\mathcal{A}))\to\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A})

and therefore
\Bis(\mathcal{G})/K
admits the structure of a convenient Lie group this way.

Example 3.12
.

If the anchor of
\mathcal{F}\Rightarrow M
is injective already on the level of the vector bundles, it corresponds to a regular (i.e. non-singular) foliation. In this case, there is an identification
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{F})=\Fol_{c,0}(\mathcal{F})
, where
\Fol(\mathcal{F})
is the group of diffeomorphisms sending each leaf to itself, that equips
\Fol(\mathcal{F})
with the structure of a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
. It coincides with the one described in
[
20
]
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Even for a (non-singular) foliation
\mathcal{F}\Rightarrow M
, the subgroup
\InnAut(\mathcal{F})=\Fol(\mathcal{F})
may not be embedded in
\Diff(M)
. Consider for instance a foliation on the torus
T^{2}
with coordinates
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
whose leaves are integral curves of a constant vector field
X=\partial_{\theta_{1}}+a\partial_{\theta_{2}}
, where
a\in\mathbb{R}
is irrational. For each
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
\varphi_{t}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=(\theta_{1}+t,\theta_{2})
preserves the foliation, and for a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}
, the maps
\varphi_{t}
are foliated. Hence, there is a sequence
\varphi_{t_{n}}\in\Fol(\mathcal{F})
such that
\varphi_{t_{n}}
converges to the identity in
\Diff(M)
, but does not converge in
\Fol(\mathcal{F})
.

Another interesting application arises when we consider almost everywhere non-degenerate Poisson structures, combined with the Lagrangian bisections of its integrating symplectic groupoid.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold whose non-degeneracy locus is open and dense. Then the group
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
is a regular Lie group, which is an initial subgroup of
\Diff(M,\pi)
whenever the cotangent algebroid
T^{*}M
is isotopically closed and
\pi^{\sharp}:\Gamma_{c}(T^{*}M)\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
is an embedding.

More generally, if
(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})\Rightarrow M
is an almost injective Poisson Lie algebroid and
\pi_{\mathcal{A}}
is generically non-degenerate, then
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
is a regular Lie group and and initial subgroup of
\Diff(M,\pi)
whenever
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is isotopically closed and
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}\circ\pi_{\mathcal{A}}^{\sharp}:\Omega^{1}_{c}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M)
is an embedding.

Proof.

We prove the general case. First, the dual
\mathcal{A}^{*}
becomes an almost-injective Lie algebroid as in Remark
1.10
. Let
(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)\rightrightarrows(M,\pi)
be a Poisson groupoid integrating the bialgebroid
(\mathcal{A}^{*},\mathcal{A})
. As in Remark
3.11
, we set

K=\ker\left((\chi_{\mathcal{G}^{*}})_{*}:\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*})\to\Diff(M)\right),

which is a discrete subgroup of
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*})
. Since this is the kernel of a smooth covering map of Lie groups, we can find a neighborhood
\mathcal{W}
of the identity in
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*})
satisfying

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{W}\mathcal{W}\cap k\mathcal{W}\mathcal{W}=\emptyset\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $k\in K$}.

By Theorem
2.13
, the Poisson groupoid
(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)
is linearizable, and therefore the identity component
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)
of the group of coisotropic bisections is an embedded submanifold of
\Bis(\mathcal{G}^{*})
(Theorem
2.12
), it follows that

K_{\Pi}=K\cap\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)

is discrete in
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)
. Moreover, shrinking
\mathcal{W}
if necessary, we have, by setting
\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}=\mathcal{W}\cap\Bis(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)
, an open neighborhood of the identity satisfying

\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}=\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}^{-1},\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}\cap k\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}\mathcal{W}_{\Pi}=\emptyset\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all $k\in K_{\Pi}$}.

By
[
11
]
, 38.5, the quotient
\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G}^{*},\Pi)/K_{\Pi}\cong\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(\mathcal{A},\pi_{\mathcal{A}})
is then a regular Lie group.

Under the additional assumptions, it becomes an initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff_{c}(M)
, because
\InnAut_{c}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
is.
∎

3.2  Poisson structures of divisor type

Our approach is particularly effective for a class of generically non-degenerate Poisson structures, namely those of divisor type considered
[
10
]
(see Section
1.4
).
Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson structure of divisor type with projective divisor ideal
I_{\pi}
. The algebroid
T_{I_{\pi}}M
is almost injective, and by Proposition
1.26
, every Poisson vector field is a section of
T_{I_{\pi}}M
. When
T_{I_{\pi}}^{*}M
locally admits bases of closed sections, the Poisson structure
\pi
lifts to a Poisson structure
\pi_{I}
on
T_{I_{\pi}}M
by Theorem 4.35 in
[
10
]
. If in addition
I_{\pi}
is a standard ideal, the lift
\pi_{I}
is non-degenerate.

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson structure of standard divisor type. Suppose that
T_{I_{\pi}}M
locally admits bases of closed sections. Then
\Diff(M,\pi)
is a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
.

Proof.

We will equip the path component
\Diff_{c,0}(M,\pi)
of
\Diff(M,\pi)
with the structure of a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff_{c,0}(M)
. By Proposition
1.26
, every Poisson diffeomorphism in
\Diff_{c,0}(M,\pi)
is an inner automorphism of
T_{I_{\pi}}M
, to which
\pi
lifts to a non-degenerate Poisson structure
\pi_{I}
. By Example
3.7
, the Lie algebroid
T_{I_{\pi}}M
is isotopically closed, and therefore
\InnAut_{c}(T_{I_{\pi}}M,\pi_{I})
is a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff_{c,0}(M)
by Theorem
3.25
. Now, the whole group
\Diff(M,\pi)
becomes a Lie group by declaring
\Diff_{c}(M,\pi)
to be open
\Diff(M,\pi)
and using group multiplication to translate the manifold structure to the entire group. This way,
\Diff(M,\pi)
is becomes an initial subgroup of
\Diff(M)
.
∎

3.2.1 Log-symplectic structures

Let
(M,Z)
be a (normal-crossing) log-manifold (Example
1.27
). Analogous to the boundary of a manifold with corners, the hypersurface
Z
comes with a natural stratification
Z=\cup_{j=0}^{2n-1}Z_{j}
(the
Z_{j}
’s are (unions of) the
j
-dimensional orbits of
T_{Z}M
). Note that by the local form, each
Z_{j}
is embedded (but not closed!). Because
(M\setminus\left(\cup_{i=1}^{2n-2}Z_{i}\right),Z_{2n-1})
is a smooth log-manifold, the log-symplectic structure induces a cosymplectic structure on
i:Z_{2n-1}\hookrightarrow M
, whose one-form is given by
\alpha=i^{*}(\iota_{\mathcal{E}}\omega)
, where
\mathcal{E}
is the Euler vector field obtained via any tubular neighbourhood of
Z_{2n-1}
(
[
8
]
, Proposition 10).

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a log-symplectic manifold with underlying Poisson structure
\pi
. A vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is tangent to the symplectic foliation of
\pi
if and only if there is a one-form
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
such that
\pi^{\sharp}(\eta)=X
.

Proof.

Any vector field of the form
\pi^{\sharp}(\eta)
it tangent to the symplectic foliation. Conversely, if
X
is tangent to the symplectic foliation, it is certainly tangent to
Z
and thus can be regarded as a section of
T_{Z}M
, and thus we can set
\eta=\omega^{\flat}(X)
. Since
X
is tangent to the symplectic foliation, it must be in the kernel of
\alpha=i^{*}(\iota_{\mathcal{E}}\omega)
when restricted to
Z_{2n-1}
. In local coordinates
(x_{i})
adapted to
Z
, we have
Z_{2n-1}=\{\prod_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}=0:\mbox{no two $x_{i}$'s are zero}\}
. Therefore, on
Z_{2n-1}\cap\{x_{i}=0\}
, the Euler vector field is given by
x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}
. We find that

0=\iota_{X}\alpha=\omega\left(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}},X\right)\big|_{Z_{2n-1}\cap\{x_{i}=0\}}=-\eta\left(x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial{x_{i}}}\right)\big|_{Z_{2n-1}\cap\{x_{i}=0\}}.

By continuity,
\iota_{x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}}\eta=0
along
\{x_{i}=0\}
. Since this is true for all
i=1,\dots,k
, it follows that
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.
∎

Consequently, for log-symplectic manifolds, the locally Hamiltonian and foliated vector fields coincide! This implies that
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
is the identity component of
\Fol_{c}(M,\pi)
.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
(M,Z)
be a log-manifold. Then

\Diff(M,Z)=\{\varphi\in\Diff(M):\varphi(Z)=Z\}

is a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M,Z)
.

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a log-symplectic manifold with underlying Poisson structure
\pi
. Then
\Diff(M,\pi)
is a regular embedded Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M,Z)
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M,\pi)
.

Finally,
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
coincides with the identity component of
\Fol(M,\pi)
, and comes with the structure of a regular Lie group for which it is initial in
\Diff(M,\pi)
, with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc},c}(M,\pi)=\mathfrak{fol}_{c}(M,\pi)
.

Remark 3.18
.

If
Z
is a smooth hypersurface, Ebin and Marsden proved in
[
6
]
that
\Diff(M,Z)
is actually embedded in
\Diff(M)
by means of a metric for which
Z
is geodesically closed. This is not immediate from our approach.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be the holonomy groupoid of
T_{Z}M
. The identity path-component of
\Diff(M,Z)
identifies with the (compactly supported) inner automorphisms of
T_{Z}M
, which is an initial subgroup of
\Diff(M)
by Proposition
3.10
after identifying
\Diff_{c,0}(M,Z)=\Bis_{c,0}(\mathcal{G})
. The second statement is a special case of Theorem
3.15
. Finally, due to Lemma
3.16
, the identity component of
\Fol(M,\pi)
coincides with
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
. The cotangent algebroid
T^{*}M
is almost injective an isotopically closed by Lemma
1.17
, and therefore
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
obtains the structure of a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
via Theorem
3.14
.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

When
Z\subset M
is a smooth hypersurface, explicit integrations of the log-tangent bundle
T_{Z}M
have been constructed in
[
7
]
. One of its integrations, that we call the
log-pair
groupoid
\Pair_{Z}(M)
, can be obtained by blowing up
Z\times Z
in
M\times M
. After removing a certain subset (namely the arrows that point into and out of
Z
),
\Pair_{Z}(M)\rightrightarrows M
becomes a groupoid integrating
T_{Z}M
. If
\omega
is a log-symplectic structure on
T_{Z}M
, then
\Pi^{-1}=s^{*}\omega-t^{*}\omega
is a log-symplectic structure on

(see Proposition

). For the resulting Poisson groupoid

3.2.2 Elliptic symplectic structures

Let
|D|
be an elliptic divisor on
M
. If
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a vector field with the property that
[X,Y]
is elliptic for all
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M,|D|)
, then
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M,|D|)
. This implies that if
\varphi_{t}
is an isotopy generated by
X_{t}
consisting of automorphisms of
T_{|D|}M
, then
X_{t}
is an elliptic vector field. It follows that
\Diff_{c,0}(M,|D|):=\operatorname{Aut}_{c,0}(T_{|D|}M)\cong\InnAut_{c}(T_{|D|}M)
.

The symplectic foliation of an elliptic symplectic manifold
(M,\pi)
depends on the elliptic residue of the elliptic two-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(T_{|D|}M)
, defined as follows. Near the degeneracy locus
i:D\hookrightarrow M
, we can write

\omega=\lambda d\log r\wedge d\theta+d\log r\wedge\alpha_{1}+d\theta\wedge\alpha_{2}+\beta

where
\lambda\in\Omega^{0}(D;\mathfrak{k}^{*})
, with
\mathfrak{k}=\det\left(\ker\rho_{T_{|D|}M}|_{D}\right)
, is locally constant. Then
\lambda
is the
elliptic residue
of
\omega
.

Definition 3.20
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be an elliptic symplectic manifold with
\lambda
defined as above. If
\lambda\neq 0
, we say that
(M,\pi)
has
non-zero elliptic residue
. If
\lambda=0
, then we say that
(M,\pi)
has
zero
or
vanishing elliptic residue
.
∎

If
(M,\pi)
has non-zero elliptic residue, then the symplectic foliation of
(M,\pi)
consists of (the connected components of)
M\setminus D
and
D
. Therefore, every Poisson vector field is automatically foliated.

When the elliptic residue vanishes, the symplectic foliation is given by connected components of
M\setminus D
and the foliation induced by the kernels of the closed one-forms
i^{*}\alpha_{1},i^{*}\alpha_{2}\in\Omega^{1}(D)
(these are the radial and
\theta
-residues, respectively).

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be an elliptic symplectic manifold,
|D|
its elliptic divisor and
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(T_{|D|}M)
its elliptic symplectic form. Assume
\omega
has zero elliptic residue. Then every foliated Poisson vector field is locally Hamiltonian.

Proof.

This proof is inspired by the proof of Lemma 1.10 in
[
4
]
. Locally, in polar coordinates
(r,\theta)
around
D
, we can write

\omega=d\log r\wedge\alpha_{1}+d\theta\wedge\alpha_{2}+\beta.

An elliptic vector field
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M,|D|)
is Poisson if and only if
\iota_{X}\omega\in\Omega^{1}(T_{|D|}M)
is closed and it is foliated if and only if
\iota_{X}\alpha_{1}
and
\iota_{X}\alpha_{2}
vanish over
D
. If
X\in\mathfrak{fol}(M,\pi)
, we have to show that
\iota_{X}\omega
comes from a de Rham form on
M
. It is enough to show this in the locally.

Let
\eta=\iota_{X}\omega\in\Omega^{1}(T_{|D|}M)
be the corresponding closed elliptic form. The first step is to show that
\eta
is cohomologous to a smooth de Rham form. As in the proof of Lemma 1.10 in
[
4
]
, we denote by
\rho_{t}:U\to U
for
t\in S^{1}
the
S^{1}
-action in a tubular neighbourhood
U
of
D
, generated by
\partial_{\theta}
. Since this is an elliptic vector field,
\rho^{*}_{t}
acts trivially on the level of the cohomology of
T_{|D|}M
.

Claim
.

The form
\rho^{*}_{t}\eta
defines a foliated Poisson vector field for all
t\in S^{1}
.

Proof of claim.
This is a direct computation. The form
\rho^{*}_{t}\eta
is certainly closed. It is foliated when the associated vector field
(\omega^{\flat})^{-1}(\rho^{*}_{t}\eta)
paired with
\alpha_{i}
vanishes over
D
. For
\alpha_{1}
, this can be shown as follows. First, we observe that

\rho^{*}_{t}\omega=d\log r\wedge\rho^{*}_{t}\alpha_{1}+d\theta\wedge\rho^{*}_{t}\alpha_{2}+\rho^{*}_{t}\beta.

Then

\displaystyle\langle\alpha_{1},(\omega^{\flat})^{-1}(\rho^{*}_{t}\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle(\omega^{\flat})^{-1}(\alpha_{1}),\rho^{*}_{t}\eta\rangle=-\langle r\partial_{r},\rho^{*}_{t}(\iota_{X}\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle r\partial_{r},\iota_{\rho^{*}_{t}X}(\rho^{*}_{t}\omega)\rangle=(\rho^{*}_{t}\omega)(r\partial_{r},\rho^{*}_{t}X)

\displaystyle=\langle\rho^{*}_{t}\alpha_{1},\rho^{*}_{t}X\rangle=\rho^{*}_{t}\langle\alpha_{1},X\rangle,

which vanishes over
D
because
\rho_{t}
leaves
D
invariant. The computation with
\alpha_{2}
is the same. This proves the claim.

By averaging the forms
\rho^{*}_{t}\eta
over
S^{1}
, we obtain an
\theta
-invariant form
\overline{\eta}
for which
\overline{X}=(\omega^{\flat})^{-1}(\overline{\eta})
is foliated. Writing

\overline{\eta}=f_{1}d\log r+f_{2}d\theta+\zeta

for smooth functions
f_{0},f_{1}
on
U
and
\zeta\in\Omega^{1}(U)
, we have that

•

f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are
S^{1}
-invariant, and

•

f_{i}=\iota_{\overline{X}}\alpha_{i}
, so it vanishes over
D
.

Therefore,
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are divisible by
r^{2}
and thus
\overline{\eta}
is a smooth one-form on
U
.

Finally, since
\overline{\eta}
is cohomologuous to
\eta
, we can write
\eta=\overline{\eta}+df
. Since every exact elliptic one-form comes from a smooth one-form on
M
, it follows that
\eta
is indeed a smooth one-form on
U
(in general, if
\mathcal{A}\Rightarrow M
is a Lie algebroid, with differential
d_{\mathcal{A}}
, then
d_{\mathcal{A}}f=\rho_{\mathcal{A}}^{*}(df)
for any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
). This completes the proof.
∎

With this lemma out of the way, we have a complete picture of the Poisson diffeomorphism group of elliptic symplectic manifolds.

Theorem 3.22
.

Let
|D|
be an elliptic divisor. Then
\Diff(M,|D|)
is a regular initial Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{X}(M,|D|)
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be an elliptic symplectic manifold, with elliptic divisor

\Diff(M,\pi)
,
\Fol(M,\pi)
and
\operatorname{Ham}_{\mathrm{loc,c}}(M,\pi)
are regular initial Lie subgroups of
\Diff(M)
with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{X}_{c}(M,\pi)
,
\mathfrak{fol}_{c}(M,\pi)
and
\mathfrak{ham}_{\mathrm{loc},c}(M,\pi)

If
(M,\pi)
has non-zero elliptic residue, then
\Fol(M,\pi)
is an open subgroup of
\Diff(M,\pi)
. If
(M,\pi)

Proof.

Since

locally admits bases of closed sections, the following is a special case of Proposition
3.10
and Theorem
3.15
.
The group

Let

.

The holonomy groupoid

\mathcal{A}

on

.

If

Under the additional assumptions, it becomes initial by Proposition

3.5  Poisson structures of cosymplectic type

In this section we consider the symmetry group of Poisson stuctures of cosymplectic type on the tangent bundle of a manifold.

Lemma 3.26
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold of cosymplectic type, with underlying foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
has trivial holonomy.

Proof.

Choose a cosymplectic structure
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},\omega)
inducing
\pi
, The Reeb vector fields
R_{1},\dots,R_{k}
are pairwise commuting and transverse to
\mathcal{F}
, while also preserving
\mathcal{F}
. Let
L
be a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
and
x\in L
and
\gamma:[0,1]\to L
a loop based at
x
. The flows
\varphi^{i}_{\epsilon}
of
R_{i}
induce an embedding
(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{k})\mapsto\varphi^{1}_{\epsilon_{1}}\circ\dots\circ\varphi^{k}_{\epsilon_{k}}(x)
of a small disc through
x
transverse to
\mathcal{F}
. Since the image of
\gamma
is compact, for
\epsilon_{i}
small enough,
\varphi_{\epsilon_{1}}^{1}\circ\dots\circ\varphi^{k}_{\epsilon_{k}}\circ\gamma
is a loop tangent to
\mathcal{F}
starting at
\varphi_{\epsilon_{1}}^{1}\circ\dots\circ\varphi^{k}_{\epsilon_{k}}(x)
. Clearly, at
t=1
, it has returned to its starting point.
∎

It follows that
\Hol(\mathcal{F})
coincides with the relation groupoid

\operatorname{Rel}(\mathcal{F})=\{(x,y)\in M\times M:\mbox{$x$ and $y$ belong to the same leaf}\}.

We aim to strengthen Theorem
3.25
in the case that
\mathcal{A}=TM
.

Theorem 3.27
.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold of cosymplectic type with underlying foliation
\mathcal{F}
. Then
\Diff(M,\pi)
is an embedded regular Lie subgroup of
\Diff(M)
. Furthermore,
\Fol(M,\pi)

\Diff(M,\pi)
.

If the relation groupoid
\operatorname{Rel}(\mathcal{F})
is embedded in
M\times M
, then
\Fol(M,\pi)
is embedded in
\Diff(M,\pi)
.

Note that the relation groupoid
\operatorname{Rel}(\mathcal{F})
is embedded in
M\times M
when every symplectic leaf of
(M,\pi)
is embedded.

Let
(M,\pi)
be a Poisson manifold of cosymplectic type, and choose a cosymplectic structure
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},\omega)
inducing
\pi
. To obtain charts of
\Diff(M,\pi)
adapted to
\Fol(M,\pi)
, it is crucial to keep track of the one-forms that are part of the cosymplectic structure in the linearization of the pair Poisson groupoid
(M\times M,\overleftarrow{\pi}-\overrightarrow{\pi})
(this information is lost in the proof of Theorem
2.14
), because of the following observation.

Lemma 3.28
.

Suppose that
M
is connected. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
be everywhere independent closed one-forms on
M
and set
\mathcal{F}=\cap_{i}\ker\alpha_{i}
. The relation groupoid
\operatorname{Rel}(\mathcal{F})
is the leaf of the foliation
\cap_{i}\ker(\overrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}-\overleftarrow{\alpha_{i}})
on
M\times M
through the diagonal.

To keep track of the cosymplectic structure, we identify
N\Delta=\{(-v,v):v\in TM\}\subset TM\times TM
and choose a tubular neighbourhood
\psi:TM\to U\subset M\times M
of
\Delta
that respects the splittings along
M
, meaning that
T\psi
sends
(v,w)\in T_{x}(TM)\cong T_{x}M\oplus T_{x}M
to
(v-w,v+w)\in T_{(x,x)}(M\times M)
. We let
p:T^{*}M\to M
be the bundle projection, and denote by
\ell_{R_{i}}:T^{*}M\to\mathbb{R}
the linear function associated to the Reeb vector field
R_{i}
:
\ell_{R_{i}}(\alpha)=\alpha(R_{i}(x))
for
\alpha\in T^{*}_{x}M
.

Lemma 3.29
.

Let
\flat:TM\to T^{*}M
be the flat map induced by the cosymplectic structure
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k},\omega)
, with inverse
\sharp=\flat^{-1}
. Then the following equalities hold along
M
:

p^{*}\alpha_{i}|_{M}=\sharp^{*}\psi^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}+\overleftarrow{\alpha_{i}}\right)\right)\big|_{M},\hskip 10.00002ptd\ell_{R_{i}}\big|_{M}=\sharp^{*}\psi^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}-\overleftarrow{\alpha_{i}}\right)\right)\big|_{M},

\left(\omega_{\mathrm{can}}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d(\ell_{R_{i}}p^{*}\alpha_{i})\right)\big|_{M}=\sharp^{*}\psi^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\omega}-\overleftarrow{\omega}\right)\right)\big|_{M}.

Proof.

Since the map
\sharp
is a vector bundle map, its derivative along the zero section is given in terms of the natural splittings by
T\sharp(v,w)=(v,\sharp(w))
.

For the first two equalities, we have on one hand, for
(v,\beta)\in T_{x}(T^{*}M)
:

p^{*}\alpha_{i}(v,\beta)=\alpha_{i}(v),\hskip 10.00002ptd\ell_{R_{i}}(v,\beta)=\beta(R_{i}),

while the right side evaluates to:

\displaystyle\sharp^{*}\psi^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}+\overleftarrow{\alpha_{i}}\right)\right)(v,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\alpha_{i}}+\overleftarrow{\alpha_{i}}\right)(v-\sharp(\beta),v+\sharp(\beta))=\alpha_{i}(v),

and the second equality goes similar. For the third one, let
(v,\beta),(w,\gamma)\in T_{x}(T^{*}M)
. Then,

\displaystyle\sharp^{*}\psi^{*}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\omega}-\overleftarrow{\omega}\right)\right)\left((v,\beta),(w,\gamma)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\overrightarrow{\omega}-\overleftarrow{\omega}\right)\left(T\psi(v,\sharp(\beta)),T\psi(w,\sharp(\gamma))\right)

\displaystyle=\omega(\sharp(\beta),w)+\omega(v,\sharp(\gamma))

\displaystyle=\beta(w)-\gamma(v)-\left(\sum_{i}\beta(R_{i})\alpha_{i}(w)-\gamma(R_{i})\alpha_{i}(v)\right),

where we used that
\iota_{\sharp(\beta)}\omega=\beta-\sum_{i}\beta(R_{i})\alpha_{i}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\omega_{\mathrm{can}}((v,\beta),(w,\gamma))

\displaystyle=\beta(w)-\gamma(v)

\displaystyle d\ell_{R_{i}}\wedge p^{*}\alpha_{i}\left((v,\beta),(w,\gamma)\right)

\displaystyle=\beta(R_{i})\alpha_{i}(w)-\gamma(R_{i})\alpha_{i}(v).

The third equality now follows easily.
∎

Proof of Theorem

Appendix A
Linearization of Poisson groupoids over scattering-symplectic manifolds

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a scattering-symplectic manifold, with underling Poisson structure
\pi
. If
\mathcal{G}_{sc}\rightrightarrows M
is an integration of the scattering-tangent bundle
{}^{sc}T_{Z}M

Denote by

the Poisson structure on the log-tangent bundle

induced by
\omega

The section
\pi_{Z}
degenerates over

, so it is not symplectic anymore, and thus Theorem
2.13
does not apply to integrations of the bialgebroid
(T_{Z}M,T^{*}_{Z}M)
. Yet, those integrations are linearizable, as we will show in this section.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a scattering-symplectic manifold, with underlying
T_{Z}M
-Poisson structure
\pi_{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
be an integration of the log-tangent bundle
T_{Z}M
. Then the Poisson groupoid
(\mathcal{G},\overleftarrow{\pi_{Z}}-\overrightarrow{\pi_{Z}})\rightrightarrows M
is linearizable.

A.1  Linearization of scattering-symplectic structures

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a scattering-symplectic manifold, with underlying Poisson structure
\pi
, and
i:L\hookrightarrow Z
a Lagrangian submanifold transverse to
Z
. Certainly, the Poisson structure is linearizable around
L\setminus Z
in
M\setminus Z
, by Weinstein’s Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem. Moreover, the scattering symplectic structure induces a contact structure on
Z

[
12
]
which is linearizable around
\tilde{Z}=Z\cap L
(which is Legendrian in
Z
) by the Legendrian neighbourhood theorem in contact geometry. This gives an intuition to why the Poisson structure should be linearizable around
L
.

The first step in the linearization is a Moser lemma adapted to scattering-symplectic manifolds.

Lemma A.2

(
sc
-Moser lemma)
.

Let
i:L\hookrightarrow(M,Z)
be a submanifold transverse to
Z
. Let
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}
be two scattering symplectic forms such that
\omega_{1}|_{L}=\omega_{0}|_{L}
. Then there exists neighbourhoods
U_{0},U_{1}
of
L
in
M
and a diffeomorphism
\varphi:(U_{0},U_{0}\cap Z)\to(U_{1},U_{1}\cap Z)
such that
\varphi^{*}\omega_{1}=\omega_{0}
on
U_{0}
and
\varphi|_{L}=\id_{L}
.

Remark A.3
.

For the scattering-symplectic forms
\omega_{0},\omega_{1}
, the condition
\omega_{1}|_{L}=\omega_{1}|_{L}
does
not
imply that the cohomology classes in the scattering-cohomology agree. In fact, often they do not (see
[
12
]
). The scattering-morphism changes the cohomology class of the symplectic form.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
.

By the splitting theorem for Lie algebroids (

2

, Theorem 4.1), applied to the log-tangent bundle, we can assume that
M=E
is a vector bundle over
L
and the hypersurface
Z
is the bundle
E|_{\tilde{Z}}
over the hypersurface
\tilde{Z}=Z\cap L
in
L
. We set
\omega_{t}=\omega_{0}+t(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})
.

On
M\setminus Z
, we can write
\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}=d\psi
with

\psi=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}m^{*}_{t}(\iota_{\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}))dt.

Here,
\mathcal{E}
is the Euler vector field of
E
, and
m_{t}
is scalar multiplication by
t
. On
Z
, this is just a formal expression. We will show that there is a family of log-vector fields
X_{t}\in\mathfrak{X}(M,Z)
such that

\iota_{X_{t}}\omega_{t}=-\psi\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{on $M\setminus Z$}.

(
*
)

By non-degeneracy of
\omega_{t}
away from
Z
, the family of vector fields
X_{t}
is uniquely determined on
M\setminus Z
, and satisfies
X_{t}|_{L\setminus\tilde{Z}}=0
.

To show that this extends to a log-vector field over
Z
, we need to zoom in on a neighbourhood of
Z
. Choose a defining function
\tilde{x}
of
\tilde{Z}
in
L
. Then
x=p^{*}\tilde{x}
is a defining function for
Z
, with the nice property that
m^{*}_{t}(x)=x
(and thus
\iota_{\mathcal{E}}dx=0
). The key observation is that if
Y
is a scattering vector field, then
Y/x
is a log-vector field.

Claim
.

The form
x\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})
is a scattering one-form.

Proof of the claim.

The Euler-vector field
\mathcal{E}
is a log-vector field, and thus
x\mathcal{E}
is a scattering-vector field. Therefore,
x\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})=\iota_{x\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})
is a scattering one-form.
∎

From the claim, it follows that

x\cdot\psi=x\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}m^{*}_{t}(\iota_{\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}))dt=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{t}m^{*}_{t}(x\cdot\iota_{\mathcal{E}}(\omega_{1}-\omega_{0}))dt

is a scattering form (
m_{t}
is a log-map and thus a scattering algebroid morphism by Proposition
1.34
). Hence, we can find scattering vector fields
\tilde{X}_{t}
near
Z
such that
\iota_{\tilde{X}_{t}}\omega_{t}=-x\cdot\psi
in a neighbourhood of
Z
. The log-vector fields
X_{t}=\tilde{X}_{t}/x
extend the solution to (

A.1
) over
Z
.

Let
\varphi
be the time-1 flow of
X_{t}
, which is defined in a neighbourhood

. Then
\varphi
is a

-map that satisfies
\varphi^{*}\omega_{1}=\omega_{0}
outside

by the standard Moser argument. But since both are scattering forms, they must agree also on

.

A.1.1 Scattering Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem

Let
i:(L,\tilde{Z})\hookrightarrow(M,Z,\omega)
be a Lagrangian submanifold transverse to
Z
. As in the proof of Lemma
A.2
, we can assume that
(M,Z)=(NL,NL|_{\tilde{Z}})
. We can already linearize
\omega
on the scattering algebroid level.

Lemma A.4
.

The limit

\omega_{\mathrm{lin}}=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{m^{*}_{t}\omega}{t}

defines a linear scattering 2-form on
(NL,NL|_{\tilde{Z}})
that satisfies
\omega_{\mathrm{lin}}|_{L}=\omega|_{L}
.

Proof.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
m_{t}:NL\to NL
is a log-morphism and thus a scattering morphism by Proposition
1.34
. Therefore,
m^{*}_{t}\omega
is scattering 2-form for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Because
L
is Lagrangian,
m_{0}^{*}\omega=0
. Since
m_{t}^{*}\omega
is smooth in
t
, the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}m^{*}_{t}\omega/t
exists and the resulting form is again smooth. Clearly,
m^{*}_{\lambda}\omega_{\mathrm{lin}}=\lambda\omega_{\mathrm{lin}}
, so the resulting form is indeed linear.
∎

Theorem A.5

(
sc
-Lagrangian neighbourhood theorem)
.

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a scattering symplectic manifold with Poisson structure
\pi
and
i:L\hookrightarrow(M,Z)
a Lagrangian submanifold transverse to
Z
. Then there are neighbourhoods
U
of
L
in
M
and
V
of
L
in
NL
and a Poisson diffeomorphism on
\varphi:(U,\pi)\to(V,\pi_{\mathrm{lin}})
that restricts to the identity on
L
.

Proof.

The theorem follows from Lemma
A.2
and Lemma
A.4
.
∎

A.2  Poisson groupoids over scattering-symplectic manifolds

Let
(M,Z)
be a smooth log-manifold, with
p_{Z}:T_{Z}M\to M
and
p_{sc}:{}^{sc}T_{Z}M\to M
the bundle projections. The scattering Lie algebroid
{}^{sc}T_{Z}M
is naturally anchored to the log-tangent bundle
T_{Z}M
, and therefore, according to
[
24
]
, if
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is an integration of
T_{Z}M
, the following squares are a double Lie algebroid and an LA-groupoid, respectively.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{sc}^{!}T_{Z}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{Z}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{sc}T_{Z}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M,}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{sc}^{!}\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{}^{sc}T_{Z}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M.}

Proposition A.6
.

The pair
(T_{Z}M,T_{Z}M|_{Z})
is a log-manifold. Furthermore:

(i).

the prolongation algebroid
p^{!}_{Z}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{Z}M
is isomorphic to the log-tangent bundle
T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
,

(ii).

the prolongation algebroid
p^{!}_{sc}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{Z}M
is isomorphic to the scattering algebroid
{}^{sc}T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
.

If
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
is an integration of
T_{Z}M
, then, with
\mathcal{H}=s^{-1}(Z)=t^{-1}(Z)
as a critical hypersurface, the following hold:

(iii).

the Lie algebroid
p^{!}_{Z}\mathcal{G}\Rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to the log-tangent bundle
T_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}
.

(iv).

the Lie algebroid
p^{!}_{sc}\mathcal{G}\Rightarrow\mathcal{G}
is isomorphic to the scattering-tangent bundle
{}^{sc}T_{\mathcal{H}}\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

We follow the notation in
[
24
]
, Section 2.

Recall that
p^{!}_{Z}(T_{Z}M)
has side bundles and core isomorphic to
T_{Z}M
. The same is true for
T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
.

The sections of
p^{!}_{Z}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{Z}M
are generated by the complete lifts
\widetilde{v}
and vertical lifts
v^{\uparrow}
for
v\in\Gamma(T_{Z}M)
. Since the
\varphi_{t}
of a section
v\in\Gamma(T_{Z}M)
preserves
T_{Z}M|_{Z}
, it follows that
\widetilde{v}
is tangent to
T_{Z}M|_{Z}
, and thus a section of
T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
. This shows that the anchor of
p^{!}_{Z}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{Z}M
factors through
T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
. The map
p^{!}_{Z}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
is a map of double vector bundles, because it sends linear sections to linear sections and core sections to core sections. Now it follows that the map is an isomorphism as it is the identity on the side bundles and the core.

For the scattering bundles, the argument is similar. The sections of
p_{sc}^{!}(T_{Z}M)\to T_{Z}M
are generated by
T_{Z}v
and the core lifts
\widehat{v}
for
v\in\Gamma({}^{sc}T_{Z}M)
. The anchor sends
T_{Z}v
to the vector field
\widetilde{v}
, which vanishes (as a log-vector field) over
T_{Z}M|_{Z}
(indeed, the flow of scattering vector fields induce the identity on
T_{Z}M|_{Z}
), and is therefore a scattering-vector field. The anchor sends
\widehat{v}
to
v^{\uparrow}
, which too vanishes as a log-vector field over
T_{Z}M|_{Z}
. It follows that there is an induced map of double vector bundles
p_{sc}^{!}(T_{Z}M)\to{}^{sc}T_{(T_{Z}M|_{Z})}(T_{Z}M)
that is an isomorphism because it is the identity on the side bundles and the core.

The proofs of statements
(iii)
and
(iv)
are similar. One replaces
T_{Z}v
by the star sections
(t^{*}v+s^{*}v,v)
and the core sections by the right-invariant core sections
t^{*}v
(whose anchor is the right-invariant vector field
\overrightarrow{v}
on
\mathcal{G}
) as in the proof of Theorem 2.16 in
[
24
]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
A.1
.

Let
(M,Z,\omega)
be a scattering-symplectic manifold and
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
an integration of the log-tangent bundle

. In the notation of Proposition

, let

be the source- and target map. Then

is a scattering-symplectic manifold with underlying Poisson structure

on

is transverse to the hypersurface

. This completes the proof.

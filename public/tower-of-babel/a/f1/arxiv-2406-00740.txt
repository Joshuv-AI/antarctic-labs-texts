Maximum Erdős-Ko-Rado sets of chambers and their antidesigns in vector-spaces of even dimension
Philipp Heering 1, Jesse Lansdown 2, Klaus Metsch 1

Abstract
. A chamber of the vector space 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} is a set { S 1 , … , S n − 1 } \{S_{1},\dots,S_{n-1}\} of subspaces of 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} where S 1 ⊂ S 2 ⊂ … ⊂ S n − 1 S_{1}\subset S_{2}\subset\ldots\subset S_{n-1} and dim ( S i ) = i \dim(S_{i})=i for i = 1 , … , n − 1 i=1,\dots,n-1 . By Γ n ​ ( q ) \Gamma_{n}(q) we denote the graph whose vertices are the chambers of 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} with two chambers C 1 = { S 1 , … , S n − 1 } C_{1}=\{S_{1},\dots,S_{n-1}\} and C 2 = { T 1 , … , T n − 1 } C_{2}=\{T_{1},\dots,T_{n-1}\} adjacent in Γ n ​ ( q ) \Gamma_{n}(q) , if S i ∩ T n − i = { 0 } S_{i}\cap T_{n-i}=\{0\} for i = 1 , … , n − 1 i=1,\dots,n-1 . The Erdős-Ko-Rado problem on chambers is equivalent to determining the structure of independent sets of Γ n ​ ( q ) \Gamma_{n}(q) . The independence number of this graph was determined in [ 7 ] for n n even and given a subspace P P of dimension one, the set of all chambers whose subspaces of dimension n 2 \frac{n}{2} contain P P attains the bound. The dual example of course also attains the bound. It remained open in [ 7 ] whether or not these are all maximum independent sets. Using a description from [ 6 ] of the eigenspace for the smallest eigenvalue of this graph, we prove an Erdős-Ko-Rado theorem on chambers of 𝔽 q n \mathbb{F}_{q}^{n} for sufficiently large q q , giving an affirmative answer for n even.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by Erdős, Ko, and Rado’s fundamental result on maximum intersecting sets
[
10
]
, an entire subfield of combinatorics has developed which seeks to answer the following questions in different settings:
Given a suitable notion of “intersection”, how large can a set of intersecting objects be? What is their structure?
Results of this type are referred to as
Erdős-Ko-Rado
theorems (or simply
EKR
theorems) and have been considered for
blocks in designs
[
2
,
19
]
,
multisets
[
18
]
,
integer sequences
[
12
]
,
geometries
[
8
]
,
and even permutations
[
3
,
5
]
.
For an overview of the subject, see the recent survey by Ellis
[
9
]
or the book by Godsil and Meagher
[
13
]
.

One of the most natural and significant ways to generalize results on sets is to instead consider vector spaces over finite fields, where
k
-subsets become subspaces of dimension
k
.
Indeed, the properties of sets often behave like the limit case of the vector space analogue.
An EKR theorem for vector spaces was proved by Frankl and Wilson
[
11
]
.
More recently, interest has developed around EKR theorems for chambers of subspaces in finite vector spaces. Here the term
chamber
is borrowed from the theory of buildings and refers to chains of contained subspaces.

More formally, let
V
be a vector space of finite dimension
d
, then a
flag
of
V
is a set
F
of subspacesof
V
(other than
\{0\}
and
V
) such that
U\leqslant W
or
W\leqslant U
for all
U,W\in F
and
\{\dim(U)\mid U\in F\}
is the
type
of
F
. A
chamber

C
of
V
is a flag of type
\{1,\dots,d-1\}
and we denote its
i
-subspace by
C_{i}
. Two chambers
C
and
D
of
V
are
opposite
if
C_{i}\cap D_{d-i}=\{0\}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant d-1
. An
Erdős-Ko-Rado set
(or
EKR-set
) is a set of chambers for which no two are opposite. The following constructions in Example
1.1
yield large EKR-sets, which we shall refer to as
classical
.

Example 1.1
.

Let
P
be any
1
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, then an EKR-set is formed by the set of chambers whose
n
-dimensional subspace contains
P
. Dually, let
S
be any
(2n-1)
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, then an EKR-set is formed by the set of chambers whose
n
-dimensional subspace is contained in
S
.

By taking the chambers as the vertices of a graph and adjacency as oppositeness, we obtain the
Kneser graph on chambers
. Clearly an EKR-set is then a coclique in this graph, mirroring the original EKR problem in which an EKR-set is a coclique of the
Kneser graph.
By computing the eigenvalues of the Kneser graph on chambers and applying the Delsarte-Hoffman bound, an upper bound on the size of an EKR-set was obtained in
[
7
]
.
For odd-dimensional vector spaces no examples meeting the bound are known, however for even-dimension the classical examples meet the bound. Thus, we have the following important theorem on the size of a maximum EKR-set of chambers in a vector space of even dimension.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an EKR-set of chambers of maximum size in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, then

|\mathcal{F}|=\frac{(q^{2n}-1)(q^{2n-1}-1)\ldots(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}{(q-1)^{2n}(q^{n}+1)}.

For
n=2
an EKR theorem is given in
[
14
]

where maximum EKR-sets of chambers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
are determined to be precisely the classical examples for
q\geqslant 4
. Note that Theorem
1.2
resolves the question of maximum size of an EKR-set of chambers for finite vector spaces of even dimension, but it does not resolve the question of their structure. Indeed, determining their structure is a difficult question in general. In this paper we provide machinery for approaching this problem in the form of
antidesigns
which are (weighted) sets of chambers that intersect a maximum EKR-set in a constant, determined only by the size of the EKR-set and the antidesign itself. We borrow the terminology of “antidesigns” from Delsarte theory in association schemes, since they are determined by the eigenspaces of the Kneser graph on chambers.
However, for dimension at least
3
the association scheme induced by the Kneser graph on chambers is neither symmetric nor commutative and so Delsarte theory is not directly applicable.
We provide key families of antidesigns in Section
3
and by combining them with geometric arguments involving weights we are able to prove that for sufficiently large
q
(relative to
n
), all maximum EKR-sets of chambers are classical for even dimensional finite vector spaces.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every integer
n\geqslant 3
, there exists an integer
m_{n}
such that every maximum
Erdős-Ko-Rado set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
with
q\geqslant m_{n}
is of classical type.

Explicit values for
m
can be derived from the proof and are given for small
n
at the end of Section
4
.
Using a spread based antidesign, we resolve the case
n=2
for all
q
, thus completing the EKR theorem for
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Every maximum Erdős-Ko-Rado set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
is classical.

2.
Preliminaries

Notation 2.1
.

For integers
q,a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
q\geqslant 2
we define the Gaussian coefficient

\displaystyle{b\brack a}_{q}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{a}\frac{q^{b-a+i}-1}{q^{i}-1}&\text{if $0\leqslant a\leqslant b$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We omit the index
q
if it is clear from the context.

For a prime power
q
we denote the finite field with
q
elements by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For integers
c\geqslant 0
, we denote by
z_{c}(q)
the number of chambers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
, but we write
z_{c}
if
q
is clear from the context. For integers
c\geqslant 0
we define
[c]_{q}:=\prod_{i=1}^{c}(q^{i}-1)
and omit the index
q
if it is clear from the context.

Remarks 2.2
.

1.

We have
z_{0}(q)=z_{1}(q)=1
,
[0]_{q}=1
and
[1]_{q}=q-1
.

2.

For every prime power
q
and all
0\leqslant a\leqslant b
we have
{b\brack a}_{q}=\frac{[b]_{q}}{[a]_{q}[b-a]_{q}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
c\geqslant s\geqslant 0
be integers. Then

(a)

The number of
s
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
is
{c\brack s}_{q}
.

(b)

For
c\geqslant 0
the number of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
is
z_{c}(q)=(q-1)^{-c}[c]_{q}
.

(c)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
z_{2n}(q)={2n\brack n}_{q}\cdot z_{n}(q)^{2}
.

(d)

Every chamber of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
is opposite to exactly
q^{c\choose 2}
chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
.

(e)

If
C
is a chamber of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
and
S
a subspace of dimension
s
with
S\cap C_{c-s}=\{0\}
, then the number of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
that contain
S
and are opposite to
C
is
q^{{s\choose 2}+{c-s\choose 2}}
.

Proof.

(a)

See Section 3.1 in
[
15
]

(b)

We use induction on
c
. For
c=0
and
c=1
the only chamber is the empty set. If
c\geqslant 2
, there exist
{c\brack 1}
subspaces
U
of dimension one. The induction hypothesis applied to the factor space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}/U
shows that each such subspace lies in
(q-1)^{-c+1}[c-1]
chambers. Hence the total number of chambers is
(q-1)^{-c+1}[c-1]{c\brack 1}=(q-1)^{-c}[c]
.

(c)

This follows from (b) but can also be seen geometrically, since
{2n\brack n}
is the number of
n
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and each
n
-subspace is a member of
z_{n}(q)^{2}
chambers.

(d)

We prove this by induction on
c
, the cases
c=0,1
being obvious. Now let
c\geqslant 2
and
C
be a chamber of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}
. If
D
is a chamber opposite to
C
, then its
1
-subspace
D_{1}
is not contained in
C_{c-1}
. From (a) we see that there are
{c\brack 1}-{c-1\brack 1}=q^{c-1}
subspaces
U
of dimension one not contained in
C_{c-1}
. In the quotient space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}/U
, we see that each such subspace
U
can be extended in exactly
q^{c-1\choose 2}
chambers that are opposite to
C
. Hence
C
is opposite to
q^{c-1}q^{c-1\choose 2}=q^{c\choose 2}
chambers.

(e)

The set
\underline{C}:=\{S\cap C_{i}\mid c-s<i<c\}
is a chamber of
S
. Part (c) of Lemma
2.3
shows that
S
has exactly
q^{s\choose 2}
chambers
D
opposite to
\underline{C}
. The set
\bar{C}:=\{(S+C_{i})/S\mid 0<i<c-s\}
is a chamber in the factor space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}/S
and Lemma
2.3
shows that
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{c}/S
has exactly
q^{c-s\choose 2}
chambers opposite to
\bar{C}
. Such a chamber has the form
\{U_{i}/S\mid s<i<c\}
where
U_{i}
is a subspace of dimension
s+i
containing
S
. For such a chamber, the union of
\{U_{i}\mid s<i<c\}
with any of the above chambers

is a chamber opposite to
C
and all chambers opposite to
C
have this form.

∎

For integers

.

Lemma 2.5
.

Consider in

a non-empty flag

and let

with

Let

3.
Antidesigns

For an integer
d\geqslant 1
and the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
we define the Kneser graph on chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
to be the graph whose vertices are the chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{d}
where two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding chambers are opposite. In this paper we denote this graph by
\Gamma_{d}(q)
. For positive integers
n\geqslant 1
it was shown in
[
7
]
that the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
is
\lambda:=-q^{2n(n-1)}
and that the corresponding eigenspace has dimension
n\cdot\frac{q^{2n}-q}{q-1}
. Applying the Hoffmann bound, the authors of
[
7
]
showed that the independence number of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
is (see also Theorem
1.2
)

\displaystyle\alpha(\Gamma_{2n}(q))=\frac{z_{2n}(q)}{q^{n}+1}.

If
d
is the number of chambers of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
, then we index the entries of a vector of
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
by the chambers of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
. We also view a vector
v
of
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
as a map from the set of all chambers to
\mathbb{Q}
; then
v(C)
is the entry of
v
in the position corresponding to
C
for all chambers
C
. For a subset
X
of chambers we define its
characteristic vector

\mathds{1}_{X}
by
\mathds{1}_{X}(C)=1
, if
C\in X
, and
\mathds{1}_{X}(C)=0
, if
C
is a chamber that is not in
X
.
We shall denote the all ones vector of
\mathbb{Q}^{d}
by
\mathds{1}
, which is the characteristic vector of the set of all chambers.

For every independent set
\mathcal{F}
of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
satisfying
|\mathcal{F}|=\alpha(\Gamma_{2n}(q))
we have
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\in\langle\mathds{1}\rangle+E
where
E
is the eigenspace of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
for its smallest eigenvalue
(see Theorem 2.4.1 in
[
13
]
).
An explicit description of
E
is thus a helpful tool for classifying all largest independent sets. One possibility is to use such a description to prove that certain vectors are antidesigns, where we use the following notation in this paper.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E
be the eigenspace for the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
. An
antidesign
of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
is a function
v:V(\Gamma_{2n}(q))\to\mathbb{Q}
such that
e^{\top}v=0
for all
e\in E
.

The reason why we are interested in antidesigns is the following well-known fact, which will enable us to derive geometric insight about largest independent sets. Though its proof is well-known, we give the short argument.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a largest independent set of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
and if
v
is an antidesign, then

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\top}v=\frac{\mathds{1}^{\top}v}{q^{n}+1}.

Proof.

If
E
is the eigenspace of the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
, then
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}\in\langle\mathds{1}\rangle+E
, that is
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}=k\mathds{1}+e
for some
k\in\mathbb{Q}
and
e\in E
. We write
v=\ell\mathds{1}+w
with
\mathds{1}^{\top}w=0
. Since
e^{\top}v=0
,
\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}=k\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}
and
\mathds{1}^{\top}v=\ell\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}
, then

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\top}v=(k\mathds{1}+e)^{\top}(\ell\mathds{1}+w)=k\ell\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}=\frac{(\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}})(\mathds{1}^{\top}v)}{\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}}.

The assertion follows from the fact that
\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}
is the number of vertices, which is
z_{2n}
and
\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}
is the size of
\mathcal{F}
which is
\frac{z_{2n}}{q^{n}+1}
by
Theorem
1.2
and Lemma
2.3
.
∎

In order to prove that certain functions are antidesigns, we need the following explicit description of the eigenspace
E
, which was proved in
[
6
]
.

Notation 3.3
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
\CC
be the set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. For each subspace
P
of dimension one and each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, define the map
\chi^{i}_{P}:\CC\to\mathbb{Q}
where

\displaystyle\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}q^{i}&\text{if $P\leqslant C_{n}$ and $P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}$}\\
-1&\text{if $P\leqslant C_{n+i}$ and $P\not\leqslant C_{n}$}\\
0&\text{if $P\leqslant C_{n-i}$ or $P\not\leqslant C_{n+i}$}\end{array}\right.

for all
C\in\CC
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The eigenspace for the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
,
n\geqslant 1
, is spanned by the vectors
\chi^{i}_{P}
where
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
P
is a subspace of dimension one of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
.

This section is devoted to the construction of antidesigns. In our first family the antidesigns are based on a spread.
Recall that the vertices of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
are the chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
.

3.1.
Spread based antidesigns

An
n
-spread
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
is a set
S
of subspaces of rank
n
such that every
1
-subspace is a subspace of a unique member of
S
. It follows that
|S|=q^{n}+1
. Spreads exist for all
n
, e.g. consider the set
S_{0}
of all
q^{n}+1
subspaces of dimension one of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
. If we understand
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace, then it has dimension
2n
and
S_{0}
is a spread of
n
-subspaces of it. A
t
-fold
n
-spread
is a set
S
of subspaces of rank
n
such that every
1
-subspace is a subspace of exactly
t
subspaces of
S
. It follows that
|S|=t(q^{n}+1)
. Of course, the disjoint union of
t
spreads is a
t
-fold
n
-spread but there exist others.

Notation 3.5
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
S
be a
t
-fold
n
-spread of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Let
\CC
be the set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and let
v_{S}
be the map from
\CC
to
\mathbb{Q}
with

\displaystyle v_{S}(C)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\text{if $C_{n}\in S$,}\\
0&\text{if $C_{n}\notin S$,}\end{array}\right.

for all chambers
C
.

Theorem 3.6
.

For every t-fold n-spread
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, the map
v_{S}
is an antidesign with
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{S}=t(q^{n}+1)z_{n}(q)^{2}
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.4
we have to show
v_{S}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
. If
\mathcal{C}
is the set of all chambers we have
v_{S}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}v_{f}(C)\chi^{i}_{P}(C)
.
Consider
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and a
1
-subspace
P
.
Then
P
is a subspace of exactly
t
members
U_{1},\dots,U_{t}
of
S
. By Lemma
2.4
each of these contains
{n-1\brack n-i}q^{i}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n-i
with
P\not\leqslant V
. Given such spaces
U
and
V
with
U\leqslant V
, Lemma
2.5
shows that there exist
z_{n-i}z_{i}z_{n}
chambers that contain
V
und
U_{i}
. Hence, the number of chambers
C
satisfying
v_{S}(C)=1
and
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=q^{i}
, that is
C_{n}\in\{U_{1},\dots,U_{t}\}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
is

\displaystyle A:=t\cdot{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}z_{n}.

A subspace
U\in S\setminus\{U_{1},\dots,U_{t}\}
does not contain
P
and hence it is contained in
{n-1\brack i-1}
subspaces
W
of dimension
n+i
with
P\leqslant W
. Lemma
2.5
implies that for each pair
(U,W)
there exist
z_{n-i}z_{i}z_{n}
chambers that contain
U
und
W
. Hence the number of chambers satisfying
v_{S}(C)=1
and
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=-1
, that is
C_{n}\in S\setminus\{U_{1},\dots,U_{t}\}
and
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
is

\displaystyle B:=(|S|-t){n-1\brack i-1}z_{i}z_{n-i}z_{n}=t{n-1\brack i-1}q^{n}z_{i}z_{n-i}z_{n}.

Since
v_{S}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=Aq^{i}-B
, it follows that
v_{S}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
.
Since this holds for all
i
and
P
, Theorem
3.4
implies that
v_{S}
is an antidesign. Since every
n
-subspace lies in
z_{n}(q)^{2}
chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, it follows that we have
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{S}=|S|\cdot z_{n}(q)^{2}=t(q^{n}+1)z_{n}(q)^{2}
.
∎

t
-fold

If

3.2.
Antidesigns from alternating forms

Let
f
be a non-degenerate alternating form on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. The maximal totally isotropic subspaces have dimension
n
, see Section 6.5 in
[
4
]
. Let
\perp

We call a chamber

of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
symplectic with respect to
f
, if
C_{n}
is totally isotropic (and hence
C_{i}
is for all

) and if
C_{2n-i}=C_{i}^{\perp}
for

.

Notation 3.8
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
v_{f}
be the map from the set of all chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
to
\mathbb{Q}
with
v_{f}(C)=1
, if the chamber
C
is symplectic, and
v_{f}(C)=0
otherwise.

Theorem 3.9
.

For a non-degenerate alternating form
f
on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, the map
v_{f}
is an antidesign with

\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{f}=z_{n}(q)\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{i}+1)
.

Proof.

From Theorem 6.14 in
[
4
]
we see that the number of totally isotropic
n
-subspaces is
g_{n}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(q^{i}+1)
and that every
1
-subspace is contained in
g_{n-1}:=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(q^{i}+1)
of these.

In view of Theorem
3.4
we have to show that
v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and for every
1
-subspace
P
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Given
P
and
i
, we first count the number
A
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=q^{i}
and
v_{f}(C)=1
, that is the number of symplectic chambers satisfying
P\leqslant C_{n}
,
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.
There are
g_{n-1}
totally isotropic
n
-subspaces
U
containing
P
and each such
U
contains
{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n-i
that do not contain
P
. Lemma
2.5
implies that for every
U
and
V
with
V\leqslant U
there are
z_{i}z_{n-i}
symplectic chambers containing
U
and
V
. This gives

\displaystyle A=g_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}.

Next we count the number
B
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=-1
and
v_{f}(C)=1
, that is the number of symplectic chambers satisfying
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
, which is equivalent to
C_{n-i}\leqslant P^{\perp}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
. There are
g_{n}-g_{n-1}=q^{n}g_{n-1}
totally isotropic
n
-subspaces

that do not contain

. For such a subspace

has dimension

and hence

contains
{n-1\brack n-i}
subspaces

of dimension

with

. Lemma
2.5
shows that each of the resulting flags

is contained in

symplectic chambers. This gives

\displaystyle B=q^{n}g_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}.

Since

, it follows that
v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
.
This completes the proof that

is an antidesign.
Finally, we have that
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{f}
is the number of symplectic chambers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. It follows that
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{f}=z_{n}(q)\cdot g_{n}
.
∎

The following corollary follows immediately from Theorem
3.6
and Proposition
3.2
.

Corollary 3.10
.

If

is a non-degenerate alternating form on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and

, then

.

Remark 3.11
.

Note that the set of symplectic chambers is in one-to-one correspondence with the chambers of the polar space and moreover the definition of oppositeness agrees
[
7
]
. Hence an EKR-set of chambers in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
induces an EKR-set of chambers in the polar space, and if the EKR-set in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
is a maximum one, then the size of the induced EKR is given by Corollary
3.10
which is maximal in the polar space
[
7
]
.

3.3.
Antidesigns from unitary forms

In this subsection we assume that the order
q
of the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is a square, and we let
f
be a non-degenerate unitary form on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Let
\perp
denote the related polarity. Subspaces
U
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
with
f(u,v)=0
for all
u,v\in U
are called
totally isotropic
; this is equivalent to
U\leqslant U^{\perp}
. The maximal totally isotropic subspaces will be called
generators
, by Lemma 23.3.1 of
[
16
]
they have dimension
n
.
By
t_{n}(q)
we denote the number of generators of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
with respect to a non-degenerate unitary form. The following lemma is well-known.

Lemma 3.12
.

We have

\displaystyle t_{n}(q)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\sqrt{q}\cdot q^{j-1}+1).

If
f
is a non-degenerate unitary form on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, then each totally isotropic subspace of dimension one is contained in
t_{n-1}(q)
generators.

Proof.

The formula for
t_{n}(q)
can be found in Lemma 23.3.2 of
[
16
]
. Let
P
be a subspace of dimension one. If
P
is totally isotropic, then
f
induces a non-degenerate unitary form in the factor space
P^{\perp}/P\simeq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n-2}
, which implies that
P
lies on
t_{n-1}(q)
generators.
∎

We call a chamber of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}

hermitian
with respect to
f
, if
C_{2n-i}=C_{i}^{\perp}
for
i=1,\dots,n
, which implies that
C_{1},\dots,C_{n}
are totally isotropic. These are in one-to-one correspondence with the chambers of the polar space and moreover the definition of oppositeness agrees
[
7
]
.

Recall that
z_{n}(q)
is the number of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.

Notation 3.13
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
f
be a non-degenerate unitary form on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Put
k:=z_{n}(q)-1
. Let
\CC
be the set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and let
v_{f}
be the map from
\CC
to
\mathbb{Q}
with

\displaystyle v_{f}(C)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-k&\text{if $C_{n+i}=C_{n-i}^{\perp}$ for $i=0,\dots,n-1$,}\\
1&\text{if $C_{n}=C_{n}^{\perp}$ but $C_{n+i}\not=C_{n-i}^{\perp}$ for some $i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}$,}\\
0&\text{if $C_{n}\not=C_{n}^{\perp}$,}\end{array}\right.

for all chambers
C
.

Theorem 3.14
.

For a non-degenerate unitary form
f
on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
,
q
a square, the map
v_{f}
is an antidesign satisfying
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{f}=0
.

Proof.

We write
t_{i}
for
t_{i}(q)
.
The number of chambers
C
with
C_{n}=C_{n}^{\perp}
, that is for which
C_{n}
is totally isotropic, is
t_{n}\cdot z_{n}(q)^{2}
. Exactly
t_{n}z_{n}(q)
of these satisfy
C_{n+i}=C_{i}^{\perp}
for
i=0,\dots,n-1
, and the remaining
t_{n}z_{n}(q)(z_{n}(q)-1)
do not. Since
k=z_{n}(q)-1
, it follows that
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{f}=0
.

We now prove that
v_{f}
is an antidesign.
In view of Theorem
3.4
this requires
us
to show that
v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all
i=1,\dots,n
and all
1
-subspace
P
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Recall that
v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}v_{f}(C)\chi^{i}_{P}(C)
where the sum is over all chambers. For the calculation of this sum, we distinguish two cases. Recall that
\chi^{i}_{P}\in\{0,-1,q^{i}\}
.

Case 1.

P
is not totally isotropic.

Consider a chamber
C
with
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)\not=0
and
v_{f}(C)\not=0
. Then
C_{n}
is totally isotropic, so
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
, hence
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=-1
, that is
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

The number of these chambers
C
is
A:=t_{n}{n-1\brack i-1}z_{n}z_{i}z_{n-i}
for the following reasons. There are
t_{n}
generators
U
and each generator is contained in
{n-1\brack i-1}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n+i
that also contain
P
. Furthermore, Lemma
2.5
implies that each flag
\{U,V\}
extends in
z_{n}z_{i}z_{n-i}
ways to a chamber. Let
B
be the number of these chambers that are hermitian.

We calculate
B
. If
C
is a hermitian chamber with
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
, then
P\leqslant C_{n-i}^{\perp}
, that is
C_{n-i}\leqslant P^{\perp}
. Given a generator
U
, then
P\not\leqslant U=U^{\perp}
, so
P^{\perp}\cap U
has dimension
n-1
and contains
{n-1\brack n-i}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n-i
. For these
P\leqslant V^{\perp}
.
Lemma
2.5
states that for every
U
and
V
with
V\leqslant U
and a generator
U
, there exists
z_{i}z_{n-i}
hermitian chambers containing
U
and
V
. Hence
B:=t_{n}{n-1\brack n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}
. It follows that

\displaystyle v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}

\displaystyle=B\cdot k+(A-B)\cdot(-1)=-A+B(k+1)

\displaystyle=t_{n}{n-1\brack i-1}z_{i}z_{n-i}(k+1-z_{n})

and this is zero, since
k=z_{n}-1
.

Case 2.

P
is totally isotropic.

1.

We count the number
A_{11}
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=q^{i}
and
v_{f}(C)=-k
, that is the number of hermitian chambers satisfying
P\leqslant C_{n}
,
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.

There are
t_{n-1}
generators
U
containing
P
and each such
U
contains
{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n-i
that do not contain
P
. For every
U
and
V
with
V\leqslant U
there are
z_{i}z_{n-i}
hermitian chambers containing
U
and
V
. This gives

\displaystyle A_{11}=t_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}.

2.

We count the number
A_{12}
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=q^{i}
and
v_{f}(C)=1
, that is the number of chambers that are not hermitian and satisfy
P\leqslant C_{n}
,
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.

The number of all chambers
C
with
P\leqslant C_{n}
,
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
and for which
C_{n}
is a generator is
t_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}z_{n}
, since there are
t_{n-1}
choices for the generator and each contains
{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}
subspaces of rank
n-i
that do not contain
P
. Since
A_{11}
of these are hermitian, it follows that

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle=t_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}z_{n}-A_{11}

\displaystyle=t_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}(z_{n}-1).

3.

We count the number
A_{21}
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=-1
and
v_{f}(C)=-k
, that is the number of hermitian chambers satisfying
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
, which is equivalent to
C_{n-i}\leqslant P^{\perp}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

There are
t_{n}-t_{n-1}=\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}
generators
U
that do not contain
P
. For such a generator
U
the subspace
P^{\perp}\cap U
has dimension
n-1
and hence
U
contains
{n-1\brack n-i}
subspaces
V
of dimension
n-i
with
V\leqslant P^{\perp}
. Each of the resulting flags
\{V,U\}
is contained in
z_{i}z_{n-i}
hermitian chambers. This gives

\displaystyle A_{21}=\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}{n-1\brack n-i}z_{i}z_{n-i}.

4.

We count the number
A_{22}
of chambers
C
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
that satisfy
\chi^{i}_{P}(C)=-1
and
v_{f}(C)=1
, that is the number of chambers
C
that are not hermitian such that
C_{n}
is a generator,
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

The number of all chambers
C
such that
C_{n}
is a generator,
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
is equal to
\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}{n-1\brack i-1}z_{i}z_{n-1}z_{n}
, since there are
t_{n}-t_{n-1}=\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}
generators
U
that do not contain
P
and each is contained
{n-1\brack i-1}
subspaces of dimension
n+i
that contain
P
. Since
A_{21}
of these are hermitian, it follows that

\displaystyle A_{22}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}{n-1\brack i-1}z_{i}z_{n-1}z_{n}-A_{21}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{q}q^{n-1}t_{n-1}{n-1\brack i-1}z_{i}z_{n-i}(z_{n}-1)

Finally, we have

\displaystyle v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}

\displaystyle=A_{11}\cdot(-kq^{i})+A_{12}\cdot q^{i}+A_{21}\cdot k+A_{22}\cdot(-1)

and this is zero, since
A_{11}=kA_{12}
and
A_{21}=kA_{22}
.

Hence,
v_{f}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
in Case 1 as well as in Case 2. Therefore
v_{f}
is an antidesign.
∎

Corollary 3.15
.

If
f
is a non-degenerate unitary form on the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
,
q
square, and
\mathcal{F}
is a maximum EKR-set of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
, then
v_{f}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}=0
.

3.4.
Subspace based antidesigns

Notation 3.16
.

We say that two subspaces meet if they intersect non-trivially. On the other hand, we say that two subspaces are skew if they intersect trivially.

In this subsection we introduce the antidesigns that will play a prominent role in the characterizations of the largest cocliques of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
.

Notation 3.17
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
be an integer and let
\CC
be the set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. For
s\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and each subspace
S
of dimension
s
, we define
v_{S}:\CC\to\mathbb{Q}
such that

\displaystyle v_{S}(C)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q^{s(2n-s)-n}&\text{if $S=C_{s}$,}\\
1&\text{if $S\cap C_{2n-s}=\{0\}$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

In order to show that this is an antidesign, we will show that
v_{Q}^{\top}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all
i
and
P
. We distinguish two cases, which will be handled separately in the next two lemmata.

Lemma 3.18
.

For each
1
-subspace
P
and each subspace
S
with
1\leqslant s:=\dim(S)\leqslant n
and
P\leqslant S
, we have
v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Proof.

1.

We count the number of chambers
C
with
C_{s}=S
,
P\leqslant C_{n}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.

Since
P\leqslant S
, this number is zero when
s\leqslant n-i
. Now consider the situation when
s\geqslant n-i+1
.

Then
S
has
{s-1\brack n-i}\cdot q^{n-i}
subspaces of dimension
n-i
that do not contain
P
. For each such subspace
X
, the flag
\{X,S\}
can be extended in
z_{n-i}z_{s+i-n}z_{2n-s}
ways to a chamber fulfilling the requirements. Hence the number of chambers in question is

\displaystyle A:={s-1\brack n-i}\cdot q^{n-i}\cdot z_{n-i}\cdot z_{s+i-n}\cdot z_{2n-s}

2.

We count the number of chambers
C
with
C_{s}=S
,
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

Since
P\leqslant S
and each chamber
C
satisfies
C_{s}\leqslant C_{n}
, this number is zero.

3.

We count the number of chambers
C
such that
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
,
P\leqslant C_{n}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.

Since
P\leqslant S
and each chamber
C
satisfies
C_{n}\leqslant C_{2n-s}
, this number is zero.

4.

We count the number of chambers
C
such that
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
and
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

If
n+i\leqslant 2n-s
, then each chamber
C
satisfies
C_{n+i}\leqslant C_{s}
and hence no chamber has the desired properties, so its number is zero.

Now consider the situation when
2n-s\leqslant n+i-1
, that is
s\geqslant n-i+1
. Then there exist
q^{s(2n-s)}
subspaces
U
of dimension
2n-s
with
U\cap S=\{0\}
. For each such subspace
U
there are
{2n-(2n-s+1)\brack n+i-(2n-s+1)}={s-1\brack s-n+i-1}

of dimension
n+i
containing
U
and
P
. Finally, each such flag
\{U,V\}
can be extended to
z_{2n-s}z_{s+i-n}z_{n-i}
distinct chambers, which all fulfill the requirements. Hence the number in question is

\displaystyle B:=q^{s(2n-s)}\cdot{s-1\brack s-n+i-1}\cdot z_{2n-s}\cdot z_{s+i-n}\cdot z_{n-i}.

We have
v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=q^{s(2n-s)-n}A-B
. Since the Gaussian coefficients occurring in

and
B
are equal, it follows that
v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.19
.

For each point
P
and each subspace
S
with
1\leqslant s:=\dim(S)\leqslant n
and

, we have
v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all

. For each point

and each subspace

of dimension at most

with

, we have
v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=0
for all

.

Proof.

1.

We count the number of chambers
C
with
C_{s}=S
,
P\leqslant C_{n}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n-i}
.

There are
{2n-s-1\brack n-s-1}

of dimension

. Notice that this is zero if

If

If

, then for each subspace

as above, there we see in the quotient space on

, and each flag

Using the symmetry of the Gaussian coefficients we find

\displaystyle A_{11}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{2n-s-1\brack n-s-1}{n-s-1\brack n-i-s}q^{n-i-s}z_{s}z_{n-i-s}z_{i}z_{n}&\mbox{if $s+i\leqslant n$,}\\
\\
\displaystyle{2n-s-1\brack n-s-1}z_{s}z_{n-s}z_{n}&\mbox{if $s+i\geqslant n$}\end{cases}

.

2.

We count the number of chambers
C
with
C_{s}=S
,

and

.

There exist
{2n-s-1\brack n-s}q^{n-s}

with

\displaystyle A_{12}={2n-s-1\brack n-s}q^{n-s}\cdot{n-1\brack i-1}\cdot z_{s}\cdot z_{n-s}\cdot z_{i}\cdot z_{n-i}.

3.

We count the number
A_{21}
of chambers
C
with
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
,

and

{2n-s-1\brack n-1}

. Hence

\displaystyle A_{21}=q^{s(2n-1-s)}\cdot{2n-s-1\brack n-1}\cdot{n-1\brack n-i}q^{n-i}\cdot z_{n-i}\cdot z_{i}\cdot z_{n-s}\cdot z_{s}.

4.

We count the number
A_{22}
of chambers
C
with
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
and
P\leqslant C_{n+i}
and
P\not\leqslant C_{n}
.

Case 1.

s+i\leqslant n
.

Then
n+i\leqslant 2n-s
and hence each chamber
C
with
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
also satisfies
C_{n+i}\cap S=\{0\}
. In the quotient space on
P
, we see that there are

\displaystyle{2n-s-1\brack n+i-1}q^{s(n+i-1)}

subspaces
U_{n+i}
satisfying
P\leqslant U_{n+i}
and
U_{n+i}\cap S=\{0\}
. Each such subspace
U_{n+i}
contains

\displaystyle{n+i-1\brack n}q^{n}

subspaces
U_{n}
with
P\not\leqslant U_{n}
, and each subspace
U_{n+i}
is contained in

\displaystyle q^{s(2n-s-(n+i))}

subspaces
U_{2n-s}
with
U_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
. Hence for
s\leqslant n-i
we have

\displaystyle A_{22}={2n-s-1\brack n+i-1}{n+i-1\brack n}\cdot q^{s(2n-s-1)+n}\cdot z_{n}\cdot z_{i}\cdot z_{2n-s-n-i}\cdot z_{s}.

Case 2.

s+i\geqslant n
.

Then
n+i\geqslant 2n-s
. There exist
q^{s(2n-s)}
subspaces
U_{2n-s}
that intersect
S
trivially, and of these
q^{s(2n-s-1)}
contain
P
.

If
U_{2n-s}
is a subspace skew to
S
and if
P\in U_{2n-s}
, then
U_{2n-s}
lies in
{2n-(2n-s)\brack n+i-(2n-s)}
subspaces
U_{n+i}
of dimension
n+i
and all these contain
P
, and
U_{2n-s}
contains
{2n-s-1\brack n}q^{n}
subspaces of dimension
n
that all do not contain
P
.

If however
U_{2n-s}
is a subspace skew to
S
and if
P\notin U_{2n-s}
, then
U_{2n-s}
lies in
{2n-(2n-s+1)\brack n+i-(2n-s+1)}
subspaces
U_{n+i}
of dimension
n+i
that contain
P
, and
U_{2n-s}
contains
{2n-s\brack n}
subspaces of dimension
n
and none of these contains
P
.

Hence, the number of flags
(U_{n},U_{2n-s},U_{n+i})
that can be extended to a chamber satisfying the requirements in question is

\displaystyle(q^{s(2n-s)}-q^{s(2n-s-1)}){2n-(2n-s+1)\brack n+i-(2n-s+1)}{2n-s\brack n}

\displaystyle+q^{s(2n-s-1)}{2n-(2n-s)\brack n+i-(2n-s)}{2n-s-1\brack n}q^{n}.

Since each such flag can be extended in exactly
z_{n}z_{n-s}z_{n+i-(2n-s)}z_{n-i}
ways to a chamber, which all fulfill the requirements, we find after some simplifications of the above term that the total number of chambers in question is equal to

\displaystyle A_{22}=q^{s(2n-s-1)}

\displaystyle\left((q^{s}-1){s-1\brack s+i-n-1}{2n-s\brack n}+{s\brack s+i-n}{2n-s-1\brack n}q^{n}\right)z_{n}z_{n-s}z_{i+s-n}z_{n-i}.

We have

(1)

\displaystyle v_{S}\chi^{i}_{P}=q^{s(2n-s)-n+i}A_{11}-q^{s(2n-s)-n}A_{12}+q^{i}A_{21}-A_{22}.

To see that this is zero, we first consider the case that
i+s\leqslant n
. Using Lemma
2.2
we find

\displaystyle\frac{A_{11}q^{i}}{A_{12}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{2n-s-1\brack n-s-1}}{{2n-s-1\brack n-s}}\cdot\frac{{n-s-1\brack i-1}}{{n-1\brack i-1}}\cdot\frac{z_{n-i-s}\cdot z_{n}}{z_{n-s}\cdot z_{n-i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1]\cdot[n-s]\cdot[n-1]}{[n-s-1]\cdot[n]\cdot[2n-s-1]}\cdot\frac{[n-s-1]\cdot[i-1]\cdot[n-i]}{[i-1]\cdot[n-s-i]\cdot[n-1]}\cdot\frac{[n-i-s]\cdot[n]}{[n-s]\cdot[n-i]}=1

and

\displaystyle\frac{A_{21}q^{i}}{A_{22}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{2n-s-1\brack n-1}\cdot{n-1\brack n-i}\cdot z_{n-i}\cdot z_{n-s}}{{2n-s-1\brack n+i-1}{n+i-1\brack n}\cdot z_{n}\cdot z_{n-s-i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1]\cdot[n+i-1]\cdot[n-s-i]\cdot[n-1]\cdot[n]\cdot[i-1]\cdot[n-i]\cdot[n-s]}{[n-1]\cdot[n-s]\cdot[2n-s-1]\cdot[n-i]\cdot[i-1]\cdot[n+i-1]\cdot[n]\cdot[n-s-i]}=1.

It follows that the right-hand side of (
1
) is zero. Now consider the case when
i+s\geqslant n
.
Then

\displaystyle\hskip-8.61108pt\frac{(q-1)^{n+s}}{z_{n-s}q^{s(2n-1-s)}}\cdot\left(q^{s(2n-s)-n+i}A_{11}+A_{21}q^{i}\right)

\displaystyle={2n-s-1\brack n-s-1}\cdot q^{i+s-n}z_{n}z_{s}+q^{n}\cdot{2n-s-1\brack n-1}\cdot{n-1\brack n-i}\cdot z_{n-i}\cdot z_{i}\cdot z_{s}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1]\cdot q^{i+s-n}[n][s]}{[n-s-1]\cdot[n]}+\frac{q^{n}\cdot[2n-s-1][n-1]\cdot[n-i][i][s]}{[n-1][n-s][n-i][i-1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1]\cdot q^{i+s-n}[s]}{[n-s-1]}+\frac{q^{n}\cdot[2n-s-1][i][s]}{[n-s][i-1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1][s]}{[n-s]}\left(q^{i+s-n}(q^{n-s}-1)+q^{n}(q^{i}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1][s]}{[n-s]}\left(q^{i}-q^{i+s-n}+q^{n+i}-q^{n}\right)

and

\displaystyle\hskip-8.61108pt\frac{(q-1)^{n+s}}{z_{n-s}q^{s(2n-1-s)}}q^{s(2n-s)-n}A_{12}+A_{22}

\displaystyle={2n-s-1\brack n-s}\cdot{n-1\brack i-1}\cdot[s]\cdot[i]\cdot[n-i]

\displaystyle+\left((q^{s}-1){s-1\brack s+i-n-1}{2n-s\brack n}+{s\brack s+i-n}{2n-s-1\brack n}q^{n}\right)[n]\cdot[i+s-n][n-i]

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1]\cdot[s]\cdot[i]}{[n-s][i-1]}+(q^{s}-1)\frac{[s-1][2n-s]\cdot[i+s-n]}{[s+i-n-1][n-s]}+\frac{[s][2n-s-1]q^{n}}{[n-s-1]}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1][s]}{[n-s]}\left(\frac{[i]}{[i-1]}+\frac{(q^{2n-s}-1)\cdot[i+s-n]}{[s+i-n-1]}+\frac{q^{n}(q^{n-s}-1)}{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1][s]}{[n-s]}\left((q^{i}-1)+(q^{2n-s}-1)(q^{s+i-n}-1)+q^{n}(q^{n-s}-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-s-1][s]}{[n-s]}\left(q^{i}+q^{n+i}-q^{s+i-n}-q^{n}\right)

and hence also in this case the right-hand side of (
1
) is zero.
∎

Theorem 3.20
.

For every nontrivial subspace
S
of dimension at most
n
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, the map
v_{S}
defined in
3.17
is an antidesign. If
s=\dim(S)
, then
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{S}=q^{s(2n-s)-n}(q^{n}+1)z_{s}z_{2n-s}

Proof.

Since the eigenspace for the smallest eigenvalue of
\Gamma_{2n}(q)
is spanned by the vectors
\chi^{i}_{P}
, Lemma
3.18
and Lemma
3.19
show that
v_{S}
is an antidesign.

The formula for
\mathds{1}^{\top}v_{S}
follows from the definition of
v_{S}
and the observation that the number of chambers
C
satisfying
C_{s}=S
is
z_{s}z_{2n-s}
and that the number of chambers satisfying
S\cap C_{2n-s}=\{0\}
is
q^{s(2n-s)}z_{s}z_{2n-s}
.
∎

Corollary 3.21
.

If
S
is a subspace of dimension
s\leqslant n
and if
\mathcal{F}
is a maximum EKR-set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
, then
v_{S}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}=q^{s(2n-s)-n}z_{s}z_{2n-s}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathds{1}^{\top}\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}={2n-1\brack n}z_{n}^{2}
and since the total number of chambers is

, Theorem
3.20
shows that

\displaystyle\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\top}v_{S}

\displaystyle=\frac{{2n-1\brack n}z_{n}^{2}\cdot q^{s(2n-s)-n}(q^{n}+1)}{z_{2n}}\cdot z_{s}z_{2n-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{{2n-1\brack n}[n]^{2}\cdot q^{s(2n-s)-n}(q^{n}+1)}{[2n]}\cdot z_{s}z_{2n-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{[2n-1][n]\cdot q^{s(2n-s)-n}(q^{n}+1)}{[2n][n-1]}\cdot z_{s}z_{2n-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{[n]\cdot q^{s(2n-s)-n}(q^{n}+1)}{(q^{2n}-1)[n-1]}\cdot z_{s}z_{2n-s}

\displaystyle=q^{s(2n-s)-n}z_{s}z_{2n-s}.

∎

4.
Classifying maximum EKR-sets of chambers

In this section we acquire all results needed to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Notation 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an EKR-set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
.

1.

For every subspace
S
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
the weight of
S
(with respect to
\mathcal{F}
) is the number of chambers of
\mathcal{F}
that contain
S
. We call
S

heavy
(with respect to
\mathcal{F}
) if all chambers that contain
S
belong to
\mathcal{F}
; otherwise, we call
S

light
.

2.

A chamber is called
light
if all its subspaces are light, and otherwise it is called
heavy
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<s<2n
, let
S
be a subspace of dimension
s
, and
\mathcal{F}
a maximum EKR-set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
. Consider the chambers
C\in\mathcal{F}
, let
x
be the number of these with
C_{s}=S
, let
y
the number of these with
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
, and let
z
the number of these with
C_{s}\neq S
and
C_{2n-s}\cap S\neq\{0\}
.

(a)

We have
y=q^{s(2n-s)-n}z_{s}z_{2n-s}-xq^{s(2n-s)-n}
.

(b)

If
S
is heavy, then
x=z_{s}z_{2n-s}
and
z=\frac{z_{2n}}{1+q^{n}}-z_{s}z_{2n-2}
.

(c)

If
S
is light, then
x\leqslant z_{s}z_{2n-s}-q^{n^{2}-n+(n-s)^{2}}
and
z\leqslant\frac{z_{2n}}{1+q^{n}}-q^{2n^{2}-2n}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this for
s\leqslant n
, since the remaining cases are dual to these ones.
Therefore, we assume
s\leqslant n
. We have
x+y+z=|\mathcal{F}|=\frac{z_{2n}}{1+q^{n}}
. From the definition of
v_{S}
(see Notation
3.17
) we see that
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{F}}^{\top}v_{S}=xq^{s(2n-s)-n}+y
so Corollary
3.21
proves the formula for
y
. It follows that
x\leqslant z_{s}z_{2n-s}
. If equality holds, then
y=0
and
z=|\mathcal{F}|-x
and we are in situation (b).

Now consider the situation when
x<z_{s}z_{2n-s}
. Then
y
is positive, so there exists a chamber
C\in\mathcal{F}
such that
C_{2n-s}\cap S=\{0\}
. Lemma
2.3
shows that the number of chambers that contain
S
and are opposite to
C
is
q^{{s\choose 2}+{2n-s\choose 2}}=q^{2n^{2}-2ns-n+s^{2}}
. Since the total numbers of chambers containing
S
is
z_{s}z_{2n-s}
, it follows that
x\leqslant z_{s}z_{2n-s}-q^{2n^{2}-2ns-n+s^{2}}
. Using the formula for
y
and the equation
x+y+z=\frac{z_{2n}}{1+q^{n}}
, it follows that we are in situation (c).
∎

First, we consider the case in which every chamber in
\mathcal{F}
is heavy.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a maximum EKR-set of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2n}
and let
1\leqslant s\leqslant 2n-1
.

(a)

A subspace
S
of dimension
s
is heavy if and only if
for every chamber
C
in
\mathcal{F}
we have
C_{2n-s}\cap S\neq\{0\}
.

(b)

If
s\leqslant n
, the heavy subspaces of dimension
s
mutually meet non-trivially.

(c)

For
s\leqslant n
the number of heavy
s
-subspaces is at most
{\begin{bmatrix}2n-1\\
s-1\end{bmatrix}_{q}}
and the number of heavy
(2n-s)
-subspaces is at most
{\begin{bmatrix}2n-1\\
s-1\end{bmatrix}_{q}}
.

(d)

A heavy
n
-subspace meets the
n
-subspace of every chamber of
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

(a)

The statement follows directly from Lemma
4.2
.

(b)

Assume that two heavy subspaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of dimension
s\leqslant n
meet trivially. As
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
meet trivially, there exists a chamber
C
with
C_{s}=S_{1}
and
C_{2n-s}\cap S_{2}=\{0\}
. As
S_{1}
is heavy, then

. But this contradicts (a) applied to

.

(c)

For
s\leqslant n
, the heavy
s
-subspaces mutually intersect non-trivially, so the EKR-theorem for vector spaces (
[
11
]
) shows that there exists at most
{\begin{bmatrix}2n-1\\
s-1\end{bmatrix}_{q}}
heavy
s
-subspaces. The second statement follows by duality.

(d)

Consider two skew
n
-subspaces
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
and assume that there is a chamber in
\mathcal{F}
that contains
S_{1}
and there is a chamber
C
in
\mathcal{F}
that contains
S_{2}
. If
S_{1}
is heavy, all chambers that have
S_{1}
as their
n
-subspace are in
\mathcal{F}
. One of these chambers is opposite to

, contradiction.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

(a)

For

we have
\deg({\begin{bmatrix}n\\

.

(b)

For

we have
\deg(z_{s})=\frac{s(s-1)}{2}

Part (a) follows directly from the definition of the Gaussian coefficient. For part (b) we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}z_{s}={\begin{bmatrix}s\\

with (a) and Gauss summation we obtain

∎

Proposition 4.8

Let

and

is not classical

be the number of heavy

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n-1}\alpha_{s}\cdot z_{s}z_{2n-s}=\alpha_{n}z_{n}^{2}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}(\alpha_{s}+\alpha_{2n-s})z_{s}z_{2n-s}.

For

and for the other

(2)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|<3{\begin{bmatrix}n\\

implies

\displaystyle\deg({\begin{bmatrix}2n-1\\

\displaystyle\deg(3{\begin{bmatrix}n\\

. The result follows.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a maximum Erdős-Ko-Rado set of chambers of

. Assume that
\mathcal{F}

such that

Let

be a light chamber in
\mathcal{F}
.
Every chamber

in

has to satisfy

for some

. Let

be the number of chambers

and let

be the number of chambers

with
B_{s}\neq C_{s}
and

. Then

(3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|\leqslant\sum\limits_{s=1}^{2n-1}(x^{s}+z^{s}).

Lemma

implies

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{s}&\leqslant z_{s}z_{2n-s}-q^{2n^{2}-2ns-n+s^{2}},\\

\displaystyle\deg(z_{s}z_{2n-s}-q^{2n^{2}-2ns-n+s^{2}})\leqslant 2n^{2}-2ns-n+s^{2}-1\leqslant 2n^{2}-3n+1.

\displaystyle\deg(\frac{z_{2n}}{1+q^{n}}-q^{2n^{2}-2n})\leqslant 2n^{2}-2n-1.

Using (

5.
The case
n=2

We have shown for
n\geqslant 3
that every maximum EKR-set of chambers is of classical type for sufficiently large
q
. In this section we prove the same result for
n=2
and all
q
. In order to show this, we need to closely examine the geometric properties of chambers of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
. We use observations made in
[
14
]
. Since
[
14
]
utilizes the language of projective spaces, we switch to projective notation for this section. A chamber of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
is now a chamber of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
that consist of a (projective) point, a (projective) line and a (projective) plane that are mutually incident.
We adopt some notation from
[
14
]
.

Notation 5.1
.

1.

Chambers of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
consisting of a point
P
, a line
\ell
and a plane
\pi
are written as triples
(P,\ell,\pi)
.

2.

The chambers of an EKR-set
\mathcal{F}
are also called
\mathcal{F}
-chambers
.

3.

If
\ell
is a line, then the number of chambers of an EKR-set
\mathcal{F}
that contain
\ell
is called the
weight
of
\ell
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a maximum independent set of chambers of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
and let
\ell
be a line.
Then
\ell
has weight
0
,
1
,
2
,
q+1
,
2q+1
or
(q+1)^{2}
. Moreover, the following hold.

(a)

The line
\ell
has weight
(q+1)^{2}
if and only if it meets the line of every
\mathcal{F}
-chamber.

(b)

If
\ell
has weight
2q+1
, then
\ell
is incident with a point
P
and a plane
\pi
such that the
\mathcal{F}
-chambers that contain
\ell
are the chambers that contain
\ell
and
P
or
\pi
. Furthermore every
\mathcal{F}
-chamber
(Q,h,\tau)
with
h\cap\ell=\emptyset
satisfies
Q\in\pi
and
P\in\tau
. In particular, every line
h
of a chamber in
\mathcal{F}
with
h\cap\ell=\emptyset
has weight
1
.

(c)

If
\ell
has weight
q+1
, then one of the following two cases occurs.

(i)

There exists a plane
\pi
on
\ell
, such that the
\mathcal{F}
-chambers of
\ell
are the
q+1
chambers that contain
\pi
and
\ell
. In this case every
\mathcal{F}
-chamber
(Q,h,\tau)
satisfies
h\cap\ell\not=\emptyset
or
Q\in\pi
.

(ii)

There exists a point
P
on
\ell
, such that the
\mathcal{F}
-chambers of
\ell
are the
q+1
chambers that contain
P
and
\ell
. In this case every
\mathcal{F}
-chamber
(Q,h,\tau)
satisfies
h\cap\ell\not=\emptyset
or
P\in\tau
.

Proof.

This is Proposition 2.3 and Notation 2.8 in
[
14
]
.
∎

Notation 5.3
.

Let
\ell
be a line of weight
<(q+1)^{2}
. If there is a plane
\tau
such that all the
q+1
chambers on
\ell
and
\tau
are in
\mathcal{F}
, we call
\ell
a
\pi
-line.
If there is a point
Q
such that all the
q+1
chambers on
\ell
and
Q
are in
\mathcal{F}
, we call
\ell
a
P
-line.

Lemma 5.4
.

(a)

A line of weight
2q+1
is a
\pi
-line and a
P
-line.

(b)

No two
\pi
-lines are skew.

(c)

No two
P
-lines are skew.

(d)

There are at most two pairwise skew lines of weight
2
.

(e)

A line of weight
2
meets every line of weight
>2
.

Proof.

Part (a) follows directly from Lemma
5.2
. Part (b) and (c) are dual, and (b) follows from Lemma 2.9 in
[
14
]
.
Lemma 5.3 in
[
14
]
implies (c)
and Lemma 5.6 in
[
14
]
is part (d).
∎

We restate Theorem
1.4
in projective language.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a maximum EKR-set of chambers of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
. Then for any two chambers
(P_{1},\ell_{1},\pi_{1})
and
(P_{2},\ell_{2},\pi_{2})
in
\mathcal{F}
, we have that
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
are not skew.

Proof.

Assume that two lines of chambers of
\mathcal{F}
are skew. Extend these two skew lines to a line-spread
S=\{\ell_{0},\ldots,\ell_{q^{2}}\}
of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
and
let
L
be the set of all chambers of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
whose line is in
S
.
Let
v_{S}
be a map from the set of chambers of
{\rm PG}(3,q)
to
\mathbb{Q}
, a chamber gets mapped to
1
if it is in
L
and to
0
if it is not in
L
.
Theorem
3.6
yields that
v_{S}
is an antidesign.
Corollary
3.7
yields
|\mathcal{F}\cap L|=(q+1)^{2}
.

Any line
\ell_{i}\in S
is skew to at least one line of a chamber in
\mathcal{F}
.
Therefore Lemma
5.2
implies that
\ell_{i}
has weight
0
,
1
,
2
,
q+1
, or
2q+1
. We denote the weight of
\ell_{i}
as
w(\ell_{i})
.
We have

(4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{2}}w(\ell_{i})=|\mathcal{F}\cap L|=(q+1)^{2}.

Assume that there is no
\pi
-line in
S
. Then we have two options. There is a
P
-line in
S
, or there is no
P
-line in
S
.

If there is a
P
-line in
S
, Lemma
5.4
implies that there is at most one
P
-line
in
S
and no line of weight
2
in
S
. Note that in this case the
P
-line has to have weight
q+1
, since there is no
\pi
-line in
S
. This implies

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{2}}w(\ell_{i})\leqslant(q+1)+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{2}-1}1<(q+1)^{2}

which is a contradiction to (
4
).

If there is no
P
-line in
S
, Lemma
5.4
implies that all lines in
S
have weight
1
, except at most two lines, which can have weight
2
. This implies

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{2}}w(\ell_{i})\leqslant 2+2+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{q^{2}-2}1<(q+1)^{2}

which also stands in contradiction to (
4
).

We have shown that there is a
\pi
-line in
S
. Using duality and Lemma
5.4
we get that there is exactly one
\pi
-line in
S
and exactly one
P
-line in
S
, note that the
\pi
-line and the
P
-line might be the same line. It follows from (
4
) that all the lines in
S
that are neither a
P
-line, nor a
\pi
-line have to have weight
1
.

From Lemma
5.2
we know that for every
\pi
-line, there is a special plane, such that all chambers of
\mathcal{F}
, whose line is skew to the
\pi
-line, have their point in this special plane. For the
P
-line we get the dual statement. Let
\pi
be the special plane associated with the
\pi
-line in
S
and let
P
be the special point associated with the
P
-line in
S
. Lemma
5.2
implies
P\in\pi
.

There are at least
q^{2}-1
lines
\ell_{0},\ldots,\ell_{q^{2}-2}
in
S
that have weight one and that are skew to the
\pi
-line and the
P
-line. Let
(Q_{i},\ell_{i},\tau_{i})
be the chambers of
\mathcal{F}
associated with
\ell_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,q^{2}-2
. Since the lines of
S
are pairwise skew, Lemma
5.2
implies that
Q_{i}\in\pi
and
P\in\tau_{i}
for all
i=0,\ldots,q^{2}-2
.
Let
h
be a line incident with
P
and
\pi
. There are
q
points
\neq P
on
h
. Since
q^{2}-1>q
for all
q\geqslant 2
, there are two points (w.l.o.g.)
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
in
\pi
, such that
P
,
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
are not collinear. But since
P\in\tau_{0}
, we have
\tau_{0}\cap\pi=PQ_{0}
and hence
Q_{1}\notin\tau_{0}
. Similarly, we get
Q_{0}\notin\tau_{1}
. Since
\ell_{0}
and
\ell_{1}
are skew, we get that the chambers
(Q_{0},\ell_{0},\tau_{0})
and
(Q_{1},\ell_{1},\tau_{1})
are opposite, which is a contradiction.
∎

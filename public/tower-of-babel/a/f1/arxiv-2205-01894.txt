Results on bar-core partitions, core shifted Young diagrams, and doubled distinct cores
Hyunsoo Cho, JiSun Huh, Hayan Nam, Jaebum Sohn

Abstract
. Simultaneous bar-cores, core shifted Young diagrams (or CSYDs), and doubled distinct cores have been studied since Morris and Yaseen introduced the concept of bar-cores. In this paper, our goal is to give a formula for the number of these core partitions on ( s , t ) (s,t) -cores and ( s , s + d , s + 2 ​ d ) (s,s+d,s+2d) -cores for the remaining cases that are not covered yet. In order to achieve this goal, we observe a characterization of s ¯ \overline{s} -core partitions to obtain characterizations of doubled distinct s s -core partitions and s s -CSYDs. By using them, we construct N ​ E NE lattice path interpretations of these core partitions on ( s , t ) (s,t) -cores. Also, we give free Motzkin path interpretations of these core partitions on ( s , s + d , s + 2 ​ d ) (s,s+d,s+2d) -cores.

2.
Properties and generating functions

We begin this section by showing a property which follows straightly from the definition of the bar lengths and the shifted hook lengths.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
be a strict partition. The set of bar lengths in the
i
th row of
\lambda
is equal to the set of the shifted hook lengths in the
i
th row of
S(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\coloneqq(\lambda_{1}-\ell+1,\lambda_{2}-\ell+2,\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
.
By the definition of the shifted hook lengths, we have

h_{i,j}^{*}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j+1}&\text{ if }\penalty\ i\leq j\leq\ell-1,\\
h_{i,j-\ell+1}(\mu)&\text{ if }\penalty\ \ell\leq j\leq\lambda_{i}.\end{cases}

We show that the statement is true for the first row.
Assume, on the contrary, that
h_{1,j}^{*}(\lambda)=h_{1,j-\ell+1}(\mu)=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{k}=h_{1,1}(\mu)-h_{k,1}(\mu)
for some
k
. Then, by the definition of hook lengths,

\mu_{1}+\mu_{j-\ell+1}^{\prime}-(j-\ell+1)=(\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}^{\prime}-1)-(\mu_{k}+\mu_{1}^{\prime}-k),

which implies that
\mu_{k}+\mu_{j-\ell+1}^{\prime}-(k+j-\ell)=h_{k,j-\ell+1}(\mu)=0
. Since the hook lengths are always nonzero, we get a contradiction. Similarly, this argument works for the
i
th row in general.
∎

2.1.
Characterizations

In the theory of core partitions, a partition
\lambda
is an
s
-core if
s\notin\mathcal{H}(\lambda)
or, equivalently, if
ms\notin\mathcal{H}(\lambda)
for all
m
. In
[
9
, p. 31]
, Morris and Yaseen gave a corollary that
\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core if and only if none of the bar lengths in the rows of
\lambda
are divisible by
s
. However, Olsson
[
10
, p. 27]
pointed out that this corollary is not true when
s
is even. In Figure
2
, one can see that
\lambda=(7,6,3,2)
is a
\overline{4}
-core partition, but
h^{*}_{2,3}(\lambda)=8
. Later, Wang and Yang
[
11
]
gave a characterization of
\overline{s}
-core partitions.

Proposition 2.2
.

[
11
]

For a strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
,
\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core if and only if all the following hold:

(a)

s\notin\lambda
.

(b)

If
\lambda_{i}\in\lambda
with
\lambda_{i}>s
, then
\lambda_{i}-s\in\lambda
.

(c)

If
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\in\lambda
, then
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}\not\equiv 0\pmod{s}
except when
s
is even and
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\equiv s/2\pmod{s}
.

We extend this characterization to doubled distinct
s
-core partitions and
s
-CSYDs.

Proposition 2.3
.

For a strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
,
\lambda\lambda
is a doubled distinct
s
-core partition if and only if all the following hold:

(a)

\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core.

(b)

s/2\notin\lambda
for even
s
.

Proof.

It is known by Lemma
2.1
and the definition of
\lambda\lambda
that

\mathcal{H}(\lambda\lambda)=\mathcal{H}^{*}(\lambda)\cup\{h_{i,i}(\lambda\lambda)=2\lambda_{i}\mid i=1,2,\dots,\ell\}.

Therefore, for an
\overline{s}
-core partition
\lambda
and even
s
,
s/2\in\lambda
if and only if
s\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda\lambda)
, meaning that
\lambda\lambda
is not a doubled distinct
s
-core.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

For a strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
,
S(\lambda)
is an
s
-CSYD if and only if all the following hold:

(a)

\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core.

(b)

3s/2\notin\lambda
for even
s
.

Proof.

Assume first that
S(\lambda)
is an
s
-CSYD. By Lemma
2.1
,
\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core. If
3s/2\in\lambda
, then
s/2\in\lambda
by Proposition
2.2
(b). This implies that there is a bar length of
2s
in
\lambda
, which means that
S(\lambda)
is not an
s
-CSYD.

Conversely, suppose that two conditions (a) and (b) hold. If
\lambda
is an
\overline{s}
-core but
S(\lambda)
is not an
s
-CSYD, then there is a box
(i,j)
in
S(\lambda)
such that
h^{*}_{i,j}(\lambda)=sk
for some
k\geq 2
. It follows from the definition of the bar lengths that there exist
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\in\lambda
satisfying
\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}=sk
. Also, by Proposition
2.2
(c), we deduce that
s
is even and
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\equiv s/2\pmod{s}
. Hence, when
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{j}
, we can write
\lambda_{i}=(2m+1)s/2
for some

, and therefore
3s/2\in\lambda
by Proposition
2.2
(b). It leads to a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

From the characterizations we observe that,
for coprime odd integers

\mathcal{BC}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{p})}=\mathcal{CS}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{p})}=\mathcal{DD}_{(s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{p})}.

A path
P
is called a
free Motzkin path of type
(s,t)
if it is a path from
(0,0)
to
(s,t)
which consists of steps
U=(1,1)
,
F=(1,0)
, and
D=(1,-1)
. Let
\mathcal{F}(s,t)
be the set of free Motzkin paths of type
(s,t)
.
For given sets
A,B
of sequences of steps, we denote

\mathcal{F}(s,t\,;\,A,B)
the set of free Motzkin paths
P
of type
(s,t)
, where
P
does not start with the sequences in the set
A
and does not end with the sequences in the set
B
.

Recently, Cho and Huh
[
4
, Theorem 8]
and Yan, Yan, and Zhou
[
13
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
found a free Motzkin path interpretation of self-conjugate
(s,s+d,s+2d)
-core partitions and enumerated them independently.

Theorem 4.1
.

[
4
,
13
]

For coprime positive integers
s
and
d
, there is a bijection between the sets
\mathcal{SC}_{(s,s+d,s+2d)}
and

(a)

\mathcal{F}\left((s+d-1)/2,-d/2\right)
if
s
is odd and
d
is even;

(b)

\mathcal{F}\left((s+d)/2,-(d+1)/2\,;\,\emptyset,\{U\}\right)
if
s
is odd and
d
is odd;

(c)

\mathcal{F}\left((s+d+1)/2,-(d+1)/2\,;\,\emptyset,\{U\}\right)
if
s
is even and
d
is odd.

In addition, the number of self-conjugate
(s,s+d,s+2d)
-core partitions is

\displaystyle|\mathcal{SC}_{(s,s+d,s+2d)}|=\begin{cases}&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor s/4\rfloor}\binom{(s+d-1)/2}{i,d/2+i,(s-1)/2-2i}\qquad\text{if $d$ is even,}\\
&\\
&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor s/2\rfloor}\binom{\lfloor(s+d-1)/2\rfloor}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor,\lfloor(d+i)/2\rfloor,\lfloor s/2\rfloor-i}\quad\text{if $d$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Similar to the construction in
[
4
]
, we give an abacus construction and a path interpretation for each set of
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partitions, doubled distinct
(s,s+d,s+2d)
-core partitions, and
(s,s+d,s+2d)
-CSYDs.

4.1.
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partitions

For coprime positive integers
s
and
d
, let the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram
be a diagram with infinitely many rows labeled by
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\left\lfloor(s+d+2)/2\right\rfloor
columns labeled by
j\in\{0,1,\dots,\left\lfloor(s+d)/2\right\rfloor\}
from bottom to top and left to right whose position
(i,j)
is labeled by
(s+d)i+dj
.

The following proposition guarantees that, for each positive integer
h
, there is at least one position on the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram labeled by either
h
or
-h
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
s
and
d
be coprime positive integers and
h
be a positive integer. For a given
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram, we get the following properties.

(a)

If
h\not\equiv 0,(s+d)/2\pmod{s+d}
, then there exists a unique position labeled by
h
or
-h
.

(b)

If
h\equiv 0\pmod{s+d}
, then there are two positions labeled by
h
and
-h
, respectively, in the first column.

(c)

If
s+d
is even and
h\equiv(s+d)/2\pmod{s+d}
, then there are two positions labeled by
h
and
-h
, respectively, in the last column.

Proof.

In the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram, the absolute values of the labels in column
j
are
congruent to
dj
or
-dj
modulo
s+d
.
We claim that
dj
and
-dj
for
j\in\{0,1,\dots,\left\lfloor(s+d)/2\right\rfloor
} are all incongruent modulo
s+d
except
j=0
or
(s+d)/2
.
For
0\leq j_{1}<j_{2}\leq\left\lfloor(s+d)/2\right\rfloor
, it is clear that
dj_{1}
and
dj_{2}
are incongruent modulo
s+d
.
Suppose
dj_{1}\equiv-dj_{2}\pmod{s+d}
for some
0\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq\left\lfloor(s+d)/2\right\rfloor
, it follows that
d(j_{1}+j_{2})
is a multiple of
s+d
. Since
s
and
d
are coprime,
d(j_{1}+j_{2})
is not a multiple of
s+d
except for
j_{1}=j_{2}=0
or
j_{1}=j_{2}=(s+d)/2
, where both
s
and
d
are odd. This completes the proof of the claim.
The claim implies that, for every positive integer
h
, there exists
j\in\{0,1,\dots,\left\lfloor(s+d)/2\right\rfloor\}
such that
h
is congruent to
dj
or
-dj
modulo
s+d
. In addition, if
h\not\equiv 0,(s+d)/2\pmod{s+d}
, then there exists a unique position labeled by
h
or
-h
in the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram, which shows the statement (a). The statements (b) and (c) follows immediately.
∎

For a strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
, the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus of
\lambda
is obtained from the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus diagram by placing a bead on position labeled by
\lambda_{i}
if exists. Otherwise, we place a bead on position labeled by
-\lambda_{i}
. A position without bead is called a

. See Figure

We use this
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus when we deal with
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partitions.
For the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus of an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partition
\lambda
, let
r(j)
denote the row number such that position
(r(j),j)
is labeled by a positive integer while position
(r(j)-1,j)
is labeled by a non-positive integer. The arrangement of beads on the diagram can be determined by the following rules.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\lambda
be a strict partition. For coprime positive integers
s
and
d
, if
\lambda
is an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core, then the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus of
\lambda
satisfies the following.

(a)

If a bead is placed on position
(i,j)
such that
i>r(j)
, then a bead is also placed on each of positions
(i-1,j),(i-2,j),\dots,(r(j),j)
.

(b)

If a bead is placed on position
(i,j)
such that
i<r(j)-1
, then a bead is also placed on each of positions
(i+1,j),(i+2,j),\dots,(r(j)-1,j)
.

(c)

For each
j
, at most one bead is placed on positions
(r(j),j)
or
(r(j)-1,j)
.

Proof.

(a)

The fact that a bead is placed on position
(i,j)
with
i>r(j)
implies that
(s+d)i+dj
is a part in
\lambda
. Since
\lambda
is an
(\overline{s+d})
-core, it follows from Proposition
2.2
(b) that
(s+d)(i-1)+dj
is a part in
\lambda
. In a similar way, we also have
(s+d)(i-2)+dj,\dots,(s+d)r(j)+dj\in\lambda
so that a bead is placed on each of positions
(i-1,j),(i-2,j),\dots,(r(j),j)
.

(b)

If a bead is placed on position
(i,j)
with
i<r(j)-1
, then
-(s+d)i-dj
is a part in
\lambda
. Again, it follows from Proposition
2.2
(b) that
-(s+d)(i+1)-dj
is a part in
\lambda
and so are
-(s+d)(i+2)-dj,\dots,-(s+d)(r(j)-1)-dj\in\lambda
. Thus, we place a bead on positions
(i+1,j),(i+2,j),\dots,(r(j)-1,j)
.

(c)

Suppose that beads are placed on both positions
(r(j),j)
and
(r(j)-1,j)
labeled by
(s+d)r(j)+dj
and
(s+d)(r(j)-1)+dj
, respectively.
One can notice that
(s+d)(r(j)-1)+dj
is a non-positive integer and the sum of the absolute values of
(s+d)r(j)+dj
and
(s+d)(r(j)-1)+dj
is
s+d
, which contradicts to Proposition
2.2
(c). In particular, if one of them is labeled by
(s+d)/2
, then the other must be labeled by
-(s+d)/2
, which is also a contradiction to the definition of the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus.

∎

For an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partition

more simply, we define the

-abacus function of

f:\{0,1,\dots,\lfloor(s+d)/2\rfloor\}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

as follows:
For each

,
if there is a bead labeled by a positive integer in column

, let
f(j)
be the largest row number in column

f(j)
be the largest row number in column
j
, where position
(f(j),j)
is a spacer with a non-positive labeled number.

The following propositions give some basic properties of the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus function of an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
s
and
d
be coprime positive integers. If
\lambda
is an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partition, then the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus function
f
of
\lambda
satisfies the following.

(a)

f(0)=0
and
f(1)=0
or
-1
.

(b)

f(j-1)
is equal to one of the three values

,
f(j)
, and
f(j)+1
for

.

Proof.

We consider the
(\overline{s+d},d)
-abacus of
\lambda
.

(a)

Since positions
(0,0)
and
(1,0)
are
labeled by
0
and
s+d
, respectively, there is no bead in column
0
. Hence,
f(0)=0
. Similarly, since positions
(-1,1)
,
(0,1)
, and
(1,1)
are labeled by
-s
,

, and
s+2d
respectively, there is at most one bead on position
(0,1)
in column

. Hence,
f(1)=0
or
-1
.

(b)

For a fixed
j
, let
f(j)=i
.
Suppose that a bead is placed on position
(i,j)
which is labeled by a positive integer. If position
(i-1,j-1)
is labeled by a positive integer, then a bead is placed on this position by Proposition
2.2
(b). Otherwise, position
(i-1,j-1)
is a spacer by Proposition

(c). In any case, it follows from the definition of

that

. Additionally, since position

is a spacer, position

.

Next, suppose that position
(i,j)
is a spacer which is labeled by a negative integer. Since position
(i-1,j-1)
is labeled by a negative integer, it is a spacer, so

. We now assume that
f(j-1)\geq i+2
. If position
(i+2,j-1)
is labeled by a positive integer, then a bead is placed on this position by Lemma

either has with a bead labeled by a positive integer or is a spacer labeled by a negative integer by Proposition

(b) and (c), which contradicts to

. Otherwise, if position
(i+2,j-1)
is labeled by a negative integer, then it is a spacer. Therefore, position
(i+1,j)
is a spacer by Proposition

(b), which also contradicts to
f(j)=i
. Hence,
f(j-1)\leq f(j)+1
.

∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
s
and

be coprime integers. For an
(\mskip 0.0mu\overline{\mskip-1.5mu{s\phantom{d}}\mskip-7.5mu}\mskip 0.0mu,\overline{s+d},\overline{s+2d})
-core partition
\lambda

-abacus function

(a)

.

(b)

If

. In addition,
f(\frac{s+d-2}{2})=-\frac{d+1}{2}
when
f(\frac{s+d}{2})=-\frac{d-1}{2}
.

(c)

If
s

d

f(\frac{s+d-1}{2})\in\{-\frac{d+3}{2},-\frac{d+1}{2},-\frac{d-1}{2}\}

Proof.

Let position
(a,b)
denote position
(-\lfloor d/2\rfloor,\lfloor(s+d)/2\rfloor)
.

(a)

Positions
(a-1,b),(a,b)
, and
(a+1,b)
are labeled by
-s-3d/2,-d/2
, and

, respectively.
First we show that

and

are not parts of

. If

, then

by Proposition

is odd. Until the second last step of the corresponding free Motzkin paths, the paths should be in one of the following sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left((s+d-1)/2,-(d-1)/2\,;\,\{U\},\{D\}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left((s+d-1)/2,-(d+1)/2\,;\,\{U\},\emptyset\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left((s+d-1)/2,-(d+3)/2\,;\,\{U\},\{U\}\right).

By adding the end point of the free Motzkin path, we get the statements.

∎

4.4.
Enumerating
(s,s+d,s+2d)
-core partitions

In this subsection we give a proof of Theorem
1.2
. We begin with a useful lemma.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
a
and
b
be positive integers.

(a)

The total number of free Motzkin paths of type
(a+b,-b)
for which starts with either a down or a flat step is given by

|\mathcal{F}(a+b,-b\,;\,\{U\},\emptyset)|=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-1}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor,b+\lfloor(i-1)/2\rfloor,a-i}.

(b)

The total number of free Motzkin paths of type
(a+b,-b)
for which starts with either a down or a flat step and ends with either a up or a flat step is

|\mathcal{F}(a+b,-b\,;\,\{U\},\{D\})|=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\binom{a+b-2}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}\binom{a+b-1-\lfloor i/2\rfloor}{a-i-1}.

(c)

The total number of free Motzkin paths of type
(a+b,-b)
for which starts with either a down or a flat step and ends with either a down or a flat step is

|\mathcal{F}(a+b,-b\,;\,\{U\},\{U\})|=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\binom{a+b-2}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}\binom{a+b-1-\lfloor i/2\rfloor}{a-i}.

Proof.

(a)

The number of free Motzkin paths of type
(a+b,-b)
having
k
up steps (so that it has
b+k
down steps and
a-2k
flat steps) for which starts with a down (resp. flat) step is
\binom{a+b-1}{k,b+k-1,a-2k}
(resp.
\binom{a+b-1}{k,b+k,a-(2k+1)}
). Hence, the total number of free Motzkin paths of type
(a+b,-b)
for which starts with either a down or a flat step is

\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}\binom{a+b-1}{k,b+k-1,a-2k}+\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor(a-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{a+b-1}{k,b+k,a-(2k+1)},

which can be written as in the statement.

(b)

Note that

|\mathcal{F}(a+b,-b\,;\,\{U\},\{D\})|
is equal to the sum of the two values, which are given by (a),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(a+b-1,-b\,;\,\{U\},\emptyset)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\binom{a+b-2}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor,b+\lfloor(i-1)/2\rfloor,a-i-1},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}(a+b-1,-b-1\,;\,\{U\},\emptyset)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{a-2}\binom{a+b-2}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor,b+\lfloor(i+1)/2\rfloor,a-i-2}.

Hence,
|\mathcal{F}(a+b,-b\,;\,\{U\},\{D\})|
is equal to

\sum_{i=0}^{a-1}\binom{a+b-2}{\lfloor i/2\rfloor}\left(\binom{a+b-2-\lfloor i/2\rfloor}{a-i-1}+\binom{a+b-2-\lfloor i/2\rfloor}{a-i-2}\right),

which can be written as in the statement.

(c)

Similar to (b), the formula follows.

∎

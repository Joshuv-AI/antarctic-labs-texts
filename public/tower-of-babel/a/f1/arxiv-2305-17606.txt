Nonhermitian adiabatic perturbation theory of topological quantization of the average velocity of a magnetic skyrmion under thermal fluctuations
Shan-Chang Tang, Yu Shi

Abstract
We study the two-dimensional motion of a magnetic skyrmion driven by a ratchetlike polarized electric current that is periodic in both space and time. Some general cases are considered, in each of which, in the low temperature and adiabatic limit, regardless of the details of the driving current, the time and statistical average velocity along any direction is topologically quantized as a Chern number, multiplied by a basic unit. We make two approaches, one based on identifying the drift direction, and the other based on the nonhermitian adiabatic perturbation theory developed for the Fokker-Planck operator. Both approach applies in the case of periodicity along the direction of the driving current and homogeneity in the transverse direction, for which the analytical result is confirmed by our numerical simulation on the constituent spins, and a convenient experiment is proposed.

I
introduction

Magnetic skyrmion is a kind of noncollinear spin texture with topological stability, and has attracted a lot of interest ever since it had been theoretically proposed

and experimentally observed
MuhlbauerBinz-78
;
YuOnose-161
;
YuKanazawa-83
;
Heinzevon-246
;
NagaosaTokura-893
. On a large scale, a magnetic skyrmion behaves as a pointlike object moving on a two-dimensional space. The presence of both driving force and thermal fluctuations motivates us to consider using magnetic skyrmion to implement the thermal ratchet model, which is an important topic with a wide range of interests
reimann
. We proposed to use a magnetic skymion adiabatically driven by a ratchet-like spin-polarized electric currents to implement the adiabatic thermal ratchet
ShiNiu-1596
, in addition to the stochastic force representing thermal fluctuations
TangShi-2020
, which was the first realization of a thermal ratchet in terms of skyrmions in a uniform temperature. Other realizations of ratchet motions of skyrmions include unidirectional rotation driven by thermal fluctuations in presence of a temperature gradient
MochizukiYu-44
, Magnus-induced ratchet effects for skyrmions
interacting with asymmetric substrates
ReichhardtRay-1386
;
MaReichhardt-2017
, ratchet motion induced by a biharmonic in-plane magnetic field
ChenLiuJiZheng-2019
.

For the adiabatially driven skyrmion thermal ratchet
TangShi-2020
, the thermal fluctuations are represented as a stochastic force, and the dynamics is described by Langevin equation, which was treated by using Fokker-Planck equation. It was shown that if the driving electric current is periodic along a specific direction, which is different from that of the current itself, and is determined by a quantity which is a function of several parameters of the system, in other words, the periods along and orthogonal to the direction of the driving current are locked in a specific way, then the time and statistical average velocity of the skyrmion is proportional to a closed integral of a curvature of an eigenfunction of an hermitian operator, which is a similarity transformation of the Fokker-Planck operator. Hence the average velocity is topologically quantized as a Chern number multiplied by a basic unit. The result implemented a generalization of a one-dimensional adiabatic thermal rathet model
ShiNiu-1596
, and provides a novel method of manipulating magnetic skyrmions. Interesting as it is, this result was under the special condition concerning the direction of the periodicity, which needs very careful arrangement in the experiment.

In this paper, we make two new approaches and consider several extensions of this problem. First, we make an approach based on identifying the drift direction, along which the component of the velocity depends on the driving electric current while independent along the direction perpendicular to it. This approach can apply to the case studied in the previous work
TangShi-2020
, which assumes the periodicity in the drift direction, and is here generalized to a more general form of locking between periods in the direction of the driving current and the orthogonal direction, so that there is periodicity along the drift direction.

The second approach is based on a perturbation theory for the nonhermitian operator which is a similarity transformation of the Fokker-Planck operator. This approach applies to the case that the periods along the direction of the driving electric current and along the orthogonal direction are independent.

These two approaches both apply to a special case, which is also most practical, that the electric current is periodic along or perpendicular to the direction of the current itself while homogeneous in the direction orthogonal to it. This is because the homogeneity can be regarded as the periodicity with period
0
. Except for this special case, the first approach cannot apply to the case that the periods in the longitudinal and transverse directions are independent, as the driving current is then not periodic along the drift direction.

In each of these two approaches, we find that the time and statistical average velocity is topologically quantized. For the special case in which the driving electric current is periodic along its own direction while homogeneous on the orthogonal direction, we also perform a numerical simulation in terms of the constituent spins, using the stochastic Landau-Lifschitz-Gilbert equation, confirming the topological quantization. We also propose a convenient experimental setup for this special case.

On the theoretical aspect, we find a Chern number in a nonhermitian system. From the two previous papers
ShiNiu-1596
;
TangShi-2020
, to the present paper, this line of research has been inspired by the analogy with the adiabatic transport of quantum particle in a periodic potential
Thouless
;
NiuThouless-1984
, known as Thouless pump. Indeed, the theoretical framework based on the perturbation theory has been inspired by that for the Thouless pump
Thouless
;
NiuThouless-1984
. But the systems considered in our line of research are classical stochastic systems with thermal fluctuations, rather than quantum fluctuations, as in Thouless pump. It has been noted that the skyrmions can manifest quantum behavior at low temperatures
Ochoa
. It is interesting, as the future work, to consider the coexistence of quantum and thermal fluctuations and combine elements of Thouless pump and thermal ratchet.

The rest of the paper is organized as the following. In Section
II
, we introduce the stochastic motion of the magnetic skyrmion, its description in terms of Langevin and Fokker-Planck equations, and the similarity transformation of the Fokker-Planck operator. In Section

Especially, we discuss the case with independent periods along and orthogonal to the direction of the driving current, as well as the special case that the driving electric current is periodic along its own direction while homogeneous on the orthogonal direction, or vice versa. For the first special case, we also make the numerical simulation by using the stochastic Landau-Lifschitz-Gilbert equation, and propose an experiment. A summary is made in Section

IV.3
Discussion

Without loss of generality, suppose that the electric current is along
x
direction.
We now derive a constraint on the relation between the two components of the average velocity.
According to the Langevin equation (
1

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\dot{q}_{x}\rangle}=\frac{\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\alpha_{d}^{2}+\alpha_{m}^{2}}{\alpha_{d}^{2}+\alpha_{m}^{2}}\overline{\langle v_{sx}\rangle},

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\dot{q}_{y}\rangle}=\frac{\left(-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}+1\right)\alpha_{d}\alpha_{m}}{\alpha_{d}^{2}+\alpha_{m}^{2}}\overline{\langle v_{sx}\rangle}.

In the following, we consider three subcases. In the first subcase, the electric current is periodic in

In the third case, the electric current is periodic in both

V
Summary

We have studied in details the two-dimensional stochastic motion of a magnetic skyrmion driven by a generic spin-polarized electric current which is periodic
in time while periodic and asymmetric in the direction of the electric current or in the transverse direction, or in both directions. In any case, the average velocities along the two directions are shown to be proportional, with the proportional factor given by the drift direction.

We have considered some general cases significantly beyond the special case considered in our previous work, in which the periods in the longitudinal and transverse directions are locked in a special way such that the superposed periodicity is along the drift direction, which is determined by the parameters of the system.

We have made an approach based on identifying the drift direction, which applies to a more general case of period-locking, of which the case treated in our previous work is a special one. If the adiabatic condition is satisfied, the time and probabilistic average of the velocity component along the drift direction is the basic unit, which is the ratio between the space period along this direction and the time period, multiplied by a Chern number. The average velocity along the longitudinal and transverse directions can be obtained as components. Consequently, the average velocity along any direction, as a projection of that along the drift direction, is quantized.

We have also made a second approach and developed a formalism based on the eigenfunctions of the nonhermitian similarity transformation of the Fokker-Planck operator, and it is assumed that the periods along the longitudinal and transverse direction are independent.

In case the driving current is periodic along one of these two direction while homogeneous along the other, the average velocity along this direction is the basic unit multiplied by a Chern number. Multiplying it by the proportional factor mentioned above gives the average velocity along the orthogonal direction. This result can be obtained using either of the two approaches. For the first approach to be applicable, the periods along the longitudinal and transverse directions should be in a way that lead to periodicity along the drift direction. This requirement may not be satisfied if the periods along those two directions are independent and both nonzero.

For the case that the driving current is periodic along its own direction while homogeneous in the transverse direction, we have also performed a numerical simulation which confirms our theoretical prediction, and have proposed the experimental setup to realize this case, which is more convenient than that in our previous work
TangShi-2020
, in which the electric current must be in the form of
f(x-\kappa y)
, where
x
and
y
are the spatial coordinates,
\kappa
is the proportional factor.

The topological quantization provides a method to robustly manipulate the magnetic skyrmions at a low temperature, which may be useful in memory storage and communication.

(101)

Therefore, the eigenvectors of

P_{21}\\

P_{22}\\

Appendix B
Insensitivity of

to
\bm{k}

The following Hermitian and antihermitian operators can be obtained from the transformed Fokker-Planck operator (
24
)

\displaystyle L_{H}=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\dagger}}{2}=-\nabla^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\bm{M})+U,

(113)

\displaystyle L_{A}=

\displaystyle\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}-\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\dagger}}{2}=-\bm{M}\cdot\nabla-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\cdot\bm{M}),

(114)

where
\bm{M}\equiv\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)
. Then we can construct an operator from the above two operators
Risken-1585

\mathcal{H}=L_{H}-i\eta L_{A}=-\nabla^{2}+i\eta\bm{M}\cdot\nabla+U^{\prime},

(115)

where
U^{\prime}\equiv U+\frac{1+i\eta}{2}(\nabla\cdot\bm{M})
.

It can be seen that when
\eta=i
,
\mathcal{H}=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
. When
\eta
is real,
\mathcal{H}
is Hermitian.

For the time being, we assume
\eta
is real.
The eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{H}
are
\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}
, satisfying

\mathcal{H}(\eta)\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta).

They are of course Bloch wave functions and their periodic parts are
w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}
’s, which satisfy

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\eta)w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta),

where

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\equiv-(\nabla+i\bm{k})^{2}+i\eta\bm{M}\cdot(\nabla+i\bm{k})+U^{\prime}.

(116)

Then we obtain the probability current

\displaystyle\bm{J}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}(\eta)=

\displaystyle-i(\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle-\langle\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle)=-2(\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime h}+\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime h*}),

(117)

where

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime h}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle.

(118)

Following a method of Niu and Thouless for the quantized adiabatic charge transport
NiuThouless-1984
, we first write
\bm{J}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}
in the form of Green functions and then prove its insensitivity to
\bm{k}
in the following.

From (
118
),

\begin{split}\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime h}(\eta)=&\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle\\
=&\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle\\
=&-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle.\end{split}

(119)

Then calculate the derivatives of both sides of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\eta)w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(\eta)
with respect to
\bm{k}
,

\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}+\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\frac{\partial w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}=\frac{\partial E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}+E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\frac{\partial w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}.

(120)

The inner product of both sides of the above equation with
w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}
, for
n\neq 0
, leads to

\begin{split}&\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle,\end{split}

(121)

therefore,

\begin{split}\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\rangle=-\frac{1}{E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}-E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle.\end{split}

(122)

which is substituted into Eq. (
119
) to obtain

\begin{split}\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{\prime h}(\eta)=&\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}|w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle}{E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}-E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}\\
=&\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|(-2i)(\nabla+i\bm{k}-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M})|w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle w^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle}{E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}-E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}\\

(123)

where we have used

and

\begin{split}&\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)(\mathcal{H}\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}})=\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)(E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}),\end{split}

\begin{split}&\left[\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M},\mathcal{H}\right]\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}+\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}},\end{split}

(125)

Therefore

\begin{split}&\langle\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\left[\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M},\mathcal{H}\right]|\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+\langle\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\mathcal{H}\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)|\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle=E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\langle\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}|\left(\nabla-\frac{i\eta}{2}\bm{M}\right)|\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\rangle,\end{split}

(127)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}&\dot{\mathcal{H}}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}+\mathcal{H}\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}=\dot{E}^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}+E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}.\end{split}

therefore

\begin{split}&\langle\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\dot{\mathcal{H}}|\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle+E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}\langle\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle=E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\langle\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle,\end{split}

\begin{split}&\langle\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle=\frac{\langle\psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}|\dot{\mathcal{H}}|\psi^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle}{E^{\prime}_{0\bm{k}}-E^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}}.\end{split}

\begin{split}g_{\alpha}(\bm{x},\bm{x^{\prime}})=&\langle\bm{x}|\frac{1}{z-\mathcal{H}}|\bm{x^{\prime}}\rangle\\

(140)

\begin{split}g_{\alpha}(\bm{x},\bm{x^{\prime}})\rightarrow&\iint d^{2}\bm{r}\frac{\psi_{\bm{r},\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x})\psi^{*}_{\bm{r},\bm{\alpha}}(\bm{x}^{\prime})}{z-U^{\prime}(\bm{r})}\\

\begin{split}g_{\alpha}(\bm{x},\bm{x^{\prime}})\rightarrow&\frac{1}{z-U(\bm{x}^{\prime})}\sum_{m,n,m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}e^{ik_{x}(m-m^{\prime})L_{x}+ik_{y}(n-n^{\prime})L_{y}}\Gamma_{x}(x-x^{\prime}-(m-m^{\prime})L_{x})\Gamma_{y}(y-y^{\prime}-(n-n^{\prime})L_{y})\\

(142)

where
\mathcal{N}
is the number of different values of
(m,n)
.

It is clear that in the deterministic or low-temperature limit, the Green functions peak at points with

and

, and decay rapidly away from the the peaks.

On the other hand,
\bm{j}(0)
in

-dependent terms, only appearing as the exponents tend to vanish, consequently the Green functions and thus
\bm{J}^{\prime}_{\bm{k}}(\eta)
are insensitive to
\bm{k}
in the deterministic or low-temperature limit.

\eta
to

On the other hand, our simulation results confirm topological quantization, hence indirectly confirm the the insensitivity of

Appendix C
Simplification of
\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}

After the introduction of the Bloch periodic function,
\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}
can be transformed

\displaystyle\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}v_{0\bm{k}}|\left[\nabla+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)\right]|e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}v_{n\bm{k}}|e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\dot{w}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle}{E_{0\bm{k}}-E_{n\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|\left[\nabla+i\bm{k}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)\right]|w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle\langle v_{n\bm{k}}|\dot{w}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle}{E_{0\bm{k}}-E_{n\bm{k}}}.

In the Hilbert space of
w_{n\bm{k}}
, the transformed Fokker-Planck operator must be transformed to

\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}=-(\nabla+i\bm{k})^{2}-\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)\cdot(\nabla+i\bm{k})+U,

(143)

in order that
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}w_{n\bm{k}}=E_{n\bm{k}}w_{n\bm{k}}
. Then calculate the derivative of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
versus
\bm{k}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}=-2i(\nabla+i\bm{k})-i\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\nabla+i\bm{k}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{-1}(\hat{\bm{e}}_{x}\partial_{y}A-\hat{\bm{e}}_{y}\partial_{x}A)=\frac{i}{2}\frac{\partial\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}.

Consequently,
\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}
can be simplified,

\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}|w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle}{E_{0\bm{k}}-E_{n\bm{k}}}\langle v_{n\bm{k}}|\dot{w}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle.

(144)

After that, calculate the derivative of
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}w_{n\bm{k}}=E_{n\bm{k}}w_{n\bm{k}}
versus
\bm{k}
,

\frac{\partial\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}w_{n\bm{k}}+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\frac{\partial w_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}=\frac{\partial E_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}w_{n\bm{k}}+E_{n\bm{k}}\frac{\partial w_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}.

Then take the inner product of
v_{0\bm{k}}
and the above equation,

\left\langle v_{0\bm{k}}\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right|w_{n\bm{k}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime{\dagger}}v_{0\bm{k}}\left|\frac{\partial w_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right.\right\rangle=\frac{\partial E_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle+E_{n\bm{k}}\left\langle v_{0\bm{k}}\left|\frac{\partial w_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right.\right\rangle.

It is straightforward to obtain
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime{\dagger}}v_{0\bm{k}}=E_{0\bm{k}}^{*}v_{0\bm{k}}
and
\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle=0
for
n\neq 0
, which lead to

\frac{\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|\frac{\partial{\tilde{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}}}{\partial\bm{k}}|w_{n\bm{k}}\rangle}{E_{0\bm{k}}-E_{n\bm{k}}}=-\left\langle v_{0\bm{k}}\left|\frac{\partial w_{n\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right.\right\rangle=\left\langle\left.\frac{\partial v_{0\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right|w_{n\bm{k}}\right\rangle.

By substituting the above equation into (
144
), one obtains

\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n\neq 0}\left\langle\left.\frac{\partial v_{0\bm{k}}}{\partial\bm{k}}\right|w_{n\bm{k}}\right\rangle\langle v_{n\bm{k}}|\dot{w}_{0\bm{k}}\rangle.

(145)

One has a completeness identity
\mathbf{1}=\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|=\sum_{n}|w_{n}\rangle\langle v_{n}|
, where the index
\bm{k}
is omitted for simplicity. Its validity can be justified by the calculation of the matrix elements,

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{i}|\left[\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|\right]|\psi_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}T_{in}\delta_{nj}=T_{ij}=\langle\psi_{i}|\psi_{j}\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{i}|\left[\sum_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|\right]|\psi_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\delta_{in}\delta_{nj}=\delta_{ij}=\langle\phi_{i}|\psi_{j}\rangle.

Therefore, half of the probability current can be simplified further

\begin{split}\bm{J}_{\bm{k}}^{h}=&\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}v_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w_{0\bm{k}}\rangle-\frac{i}{2}\langle\partial_{\bm{k}}v_{0\bm{k}}|w_{0\bm{k}}\rangle\langle v_{0\bm{k}}|\partial_{t}w_{0\bm{k}}\rangle.\end{split}

Abstract
. 1.
Introduction

While studying inequalities of Kantorovich type, Dragomir
[
9
]
defined the transform
C_{M,m}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

C_{M,m}(A)=(MI-A^{*})(A-mI),

where
M>m>0
are predefined real numbers,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the
C^{*}-
algebra of all bounded linear operators on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
I
is the identity operator in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
A^{*}
is the adjoint of
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

Basic properties and applications of
C_{M,m}
were presented in
[
9
]
. Later, Niezgoda
[
21
]
used this transform to obtain certain Cassel- type inequalities.

Our primary goal in this work is to use the transform
C_{M,m}
to obtain new operator inequalities that involve relations among
|A|,|A^{*}|,\mathfrak{R}A
and
\mathfrak{I}A
, where
|A|=(A^{*}A)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathfrak{R}A
and
\mathfrak{I}A
refer respectively to the real and imaginary parts of the operator
A
. Then new forms of numerical radius inequalities are found using this transform.

To this end, we need to remind the reader of some terminologies. Recall that an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be positive semi-definite if
\left<Ax,x\right>\geq 0
for all vectors
x\in\mathcal{H}
and we write
A\geq 0
, while it is said to be positive (or positive definite) if
\left<Ax,x\right>>0
for all nonzero
x\in\mathcal{H},
and we write
A>0
. The real and imaginary parts of the operator
A
are defined respectively by
\mathfrak{R}A=\frac{A+A^{*}}{2}
and
\mathfrak{I}A=\frac{A-A^{*}}{2\textup{i}}.
Further,
A
is said to be accretive (dissipative) if
\mathfrak{R}A\geq 0
(
\mathfrak{I}A\geq 0
). If
\mathfrak{R}A,\mathfrak{I}A\geq 0
, then
A
is said to be accretive-dissipative. Accretive, dissipative, and accretive-dissipative operators have received considerable attention in the literature due to their applicability in operator theory and its inequalities. We refer the reader to
[
1
,
2
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
19
,
22
]
as a list of references dealing with such operators.

Our approach here will assume accretivity or dissipativity of the transform
C_{M,m}.
For this, we begin by presenting simple properties of this transform for this context.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
m<M
be given real numbers and let
C_{M,m}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the transform
C_{M,m}(A)=(MI-A^{*})(A-mI)
. Then

(1)

{C_{M,m}}\left({{A}^{*}}\right)=C_{M,m}^{*}\left(A\right)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A\text{ is normal}.

(2)

{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)=C_{M,m}^{*}\left(A\right)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A\text{ is self-adjoint}.

(3)

{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A\right|\right)\text{ is accretive }\Leftrightarrow m{I}\leq\left|A\right|\leq M{I}.

(4)

\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right),\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)\leq{{\left(\frac{M-m}{2}\right)}^{2}}{I}.

(5)

\mathfrak{I}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)\geq 0\;if\;and\;only\;if\;\mathfrak{I}A\geq 0.

(6)

If\;\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)\geq 0,\;then\;\mathfrak{R}A\geq 0.

(7)

If\;{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)\;is\;accretive-dissipative,\;then\;A\;is\;accretive-dissipative.

Proof.

Statement (1) follows, noting that

C_{M,m}^{*}\left(A\right)-{C_{M,m}}\left({{A}^{*}}\right)={{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}-{{\left|A\right|}^{2}},

while (2) follows immediately from

{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)-C_{M,m}^{*}\left(A\right)=\left(M-m\right)\left(A-{{A}^{*}}\right).

The third statement follows from the following fact

\left(M{I}-\left|A\right|\right)\left(\left|A\right|-m{I}\right)\geq 0\Leftrightarrow m{I}\leq\left|A\right|\leq M{I}.

It is not hard to check that

(1.1)

\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)+{{\left|A-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right|}^{2}}={{\left(\frac{M-m}{2}\right)}^{2}}{I},

and

\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)+{{\left|\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right|}^{2}}={{\left(\frac{M-m}{2}\right)}^{2}}{I},

which together imply (4). On the other hand, direct calculations show that

\mathfrak{I}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)=\left(M-m\right)\mathfrak{I}A

which implies (5), and (6) follows from the definition of
C_{M,m}
likewise. The last statement (7) follows from both (5) and (6).

∎

Having established these fundamental properties, we proceed to our main results in the coming sections, where operator inequalities are discussed first, then numerical radius inequalities are presented.

2.
Operator Inequalities

In this section, we present several operator inequalities using properties of the transform
C_{M,m}
. In particular, this section’s results will focus on relations among
|A|,|A^{*}|,\mathfrak{R}A
, and
\mathfrak{I}A
. We should remark that, in general, such relations do not exist. However, by imposing an extra condition on
C_{M,m}
, we obtain such ties.

We notice the appearance of the constant
\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}
, which is the ratio between the arithmetic and geometric means of
M
and
m
. This constant is, in fact, the square root of the well-known Kantorovich constant. In the sequel,
m
and
M
are positive numbers.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

(2.1)

\left|A\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\mathfrak{R}A.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
is accretive, then

\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\mathfrak{I}A.

(iii)

If
A
is invertible and
{C_{M,m}}\left({{A}^{-1}}\right)
is accretive, then

\left|{{A}^{-1}}\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\mathfrak{R}{{A}^{-1}}.

Proof.

For the first statement, the assumption implies that
\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)\geq 0
. This is equivalent to saying

\frac{\left(MA-Mm{I}-{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}+m{{A}^{*}}\right)+{{\left(MA-Mm{I}-{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}+m{{A}^{*}}\right)}^{*}}}{2}\geq 0.

Namely,

(2.2)

\left(M+m\right)\mathfrak{R}A\geq Mm{I}+{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}.

Applying the operator arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we infer that

Mm{I}+{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\geq 2\sqrt{Mm}\left|A\right|.

Combining the last two inequalities, we get (
2.1
).

To prove parts (ii) and (iii), we replace, in part (i),
A
by
\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}
, and
A
by
{{A}^{-1}}
, respectively.
∎

We note that the inequality (
2.1
) in Theorem
2.1
has been given in
[
21
, Proposition 2.4]
, using a different method.

Theorem
2.1
entails the following reverse of the triangle inequality.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
S,T\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left[\begin{matrix}0&S\\
{{T}^{*}}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right)
is accretive, then

\left\|S\right\|+\left\|T\right\|+\left|\;\left\|S\right\|-\left\|T\right\|\;\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|S+T\right\|.

Proof.

Let
A=\left[\begin{matrix}0&S\\
{{T}^{*}}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right]\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{H})
. If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

\displaystyle\left\|S+T\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|A+{{A}^{*}}\right\|

\displaystyle=2\|\mathfrak{R}A\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|\quad\text{(by \eqref{0},\;since\;$\|A\|=\|\;|A|\;\|$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}0&S\\
{{T}^{*}}&0\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{4\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\max\left(\left\|S\right\|,\left\|T\right\|\right)\quad\text{(since $\|X\|=\|\;|X^{*}|\;\|$)}.

Noting the identity
\max\{a,b\}=\frac{a+b+|a-b|}{2}
, the desired inequality follows.
∎

An upper bound of the difference
|A|-\mathfrak{R}A
is given next.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

0\leq\left|A\right|-\mathfrak{R}A\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|{I}.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
is accretive, then

0\leq\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|-\mathfrak{I}A\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|{I}.

(iii)

If
A
is invertible and
{C_{M,m}}\left({{A}^{-1}}\right)
is accretive, then

0\leq\left|A^{-1}\right|-\mathfrak{R}A^{-1}\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A^{-1}\right\|{I}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.1
,

\displaystyle\left|A\right|-\mathfrak{R}A

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)\left|A\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left|A\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|\;\left|A\right|\;\right\|{I}

\displaystyle=\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|{I}.

This completes the proof of part (i).

The other two parts can be proven similarly.
∎

In the following theorem, we use the fact that the function
f(x)=x^{2}
is an operator convex function on
\mathbb{R}
. This means that when
A
and
B
are self adjoint operators in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, we have
((1-t)A+tB)^{2}\leq(1-t)A^{2}+tB^{2}
for
0\leq t\leq 1.
We refer the reader to
[
3
, Chapter V]
for
further information on operator convex functions.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.
If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
and
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
are accretive, then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\left(1-t\right)\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|+t\left|A\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left(\left(1-t\right)\mathfrak{I}A+t\mathfrak{R}A\right).

Proof.

By (i) and (ii) of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\left(1-t\right)Mm{I}+\left(1-t\right){{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\leq\left(1-t\right)\left(M+m\right)\mathfrak{I}A

and

tMm{I}+t{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\leq t\left(M+m\right)\mathfrak{R}A.

Adding these inequalities, we obtain

Mm{I}+\left(1-t\right){{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}+t{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\leq\left(M+m\right)\left(\left(1-t\right)\mathfrak{I}A+t\mathfrak{R}A\right).

Now, using the operator convexity of the function
f\left(t\right)={{t}^{2}}
on
\left(0,\infty\right)
and the operator arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we get

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{Mm}\left(\left(1-t\right)\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|+t\left|A\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq Mm{I}+{{\left(\left(1-t\right)\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|+t\left|A\right|\right)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq Mm{I}+\left(1-t\right){{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}+t{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Squaring operator inequalities are more complex than squaring real inequalities. In other words, if
a,b
are positive numbers such that
a\leq b,
then
a^{2}\leq b^{2}
. Now, if
A\leq B
, where
A,B
are positive operators, we cannot conclude
A^{2}\leq B^{2}
since the function
f(x)=x^{2}
is not operator monotone. We refer the reader to
[
3
, Chapter V]
to get more insight about this. The following result shows that the inequalities in Theorem
2.1
can be squared.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

(2.3)

{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{2}}.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
is accretive, then

\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|^{2}\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{\left(\mathfrak{I}A\right)}^{2}}.

(iii)

If
A
is invertible and
{C_{M,m}}\left(A^{-1}\right)
is accretive, then

{{\left|A^{-1}\right|}^{2}}\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A^{-1}\right)}^{2}}.

Proof.

By the inequality (
2.2
),

{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\leq\left(M+m\right)\mathfrak{R}A-Mm{I}.

So, to prove the inequality (
2.3
), it is enough to show that

\left(M+m\right)\mathfrak{R}A-Mm{I}\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{2}},

holds. For
m\leq t\leq M
, define

f\left(t\right)={{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{t}^{2}}-\left(M+m\right)t+Mm.

Then

f^{\prime}\left(t\right)=\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{2Mm}t-\left(M+m\right),

and

f^{\prime\prime}\left(t\right)=\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{2Mm}>0.

Namely,
f
is convex. On the other hand, if we put
f^{\prime}\left(t\right)=0
, then we get
t={2Mm}/{\left(M+m\right)}\;
, and
f\left({2Mm}/{\left(M+m\right)}\;\right)=0
. So
f\left(t\right)
is positive, i.e.,

\left(M+m\right)t-Mm\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{t}^{2}}.

We get the desired result by applying functional calculus for the positive operator
\mathfrak{R}A
.

The other parts can be established similarly, so we omit the details.
∎

In fact, Theorem
2.1
is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.4
, since
f\left(t\right)={{t}^{{1}/{2}\;}}
is operator monotone on
\left(0,\infty\right)
,
[
4
, Theorem 1.5.9]
.

Our next target is to investigate commutators of
|A|
and
\mathfrak{R}A
. To this end, the following lemma will be needed.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
20
, Lemma 2.1]
Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be positive operators and let
\alpha>0
. Then

A\leq\alpha B\Leftrightarrow\left\|{{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{B}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\leq\sqrt{\alpha}.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
be such that both
\mathfrak{R}A
and
\mathfrak{I}A
are invertible.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

\left|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I},

and

\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I}.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
is accretive, then

\left|\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{I}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{I}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I},

and

\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{I}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{I}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, the inequality (
2.3
) is equivalent to

\left\|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\right\|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}.

By
[
17
, Lemma 3.5.12]
, we get

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}{I}&\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\\
{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|&\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}{I}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0\text{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}{I}&{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|\\
\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}&\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}{I}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0.

Adding these two operator matrices, we have

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I}&\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|\\
{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|+\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}&\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}{I}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

To show how Corollary
2.2
improves Theorem
2.4
, we recall the following inequality
[
5
, (1.9)]
, for
A,B\geq 0
:

(2.4)

||AB||\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(A+B)\|^{2}.

Now, we notice that

\displaystyle 4\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-2}}\left|A\right|

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-2}}\left|A\right|\right\|{I}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left\|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|\right\|}^{2}}{I}\quad\text{(by \eqref{ned_bhakit})}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{I},

which is equivalent to saying that

\displaystyle{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-2}}\left|A\right|\right\|{{\left|A\right|}^{-2}}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left\|\frac{\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|}{2}\right\|}^{2}}{{\left|A\right|}^{-2}}

\displaystyle\leq{{\left(\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\right)}^{2}}{{\left|A\right|}^{-2}}.

Now, by taking the inverse, we get

\displaystyle{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq{{\left\|\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-2}}\left|A\right|\right\|}^{-1}}{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq{{\left\|\frac{\left|A\right|{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}+{{\left(\mathfrak{R}A\right)}^{-1}}\left|A\right|}{2}\right\|}^{-2}}{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq{{\left(\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\right)}^{2}}{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}.

For an arbitrary
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, the inequality

(2.5)

\Phi^{\frac{1}{2}}(|A|^{2})\geq\Phi(|A|)

is well known for the unital positive linear map
\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
[
7
,
8
]
. In this context, recall that such a map is a map that satisfies
\Phi(A)\geq 0
whenever
A\geq 0
and
\Phi(I)=I.
In what follows, a reversed version is presented via the transform
C_{M,m}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
and let
\Phi
be a unital positive linear map on
\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A\right|\right)
is accretive, then

{{\Phi}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left({{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\Phi\left(\left|A\right|\right).

Proof.

Since
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A\right|\right)
is accretive, we have by
[
14
, Theorem 1.32 (iii)]
,

\Phi\left({{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{4Mm}{{\Phi}^{2}}\left(\left|A\right|\right).

The result follows by taking into account that the function
f\left(t\right)={{t}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
is operator monotone on
\left(0,\infty\right)
.
∎

In the next theorem, reverses of the inequalities of Theorem
2.1
are presented.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A\right|\right)
is accretive, then

\mathfrak{R}A\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left|A\right|.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|\textup{i}A^{*}\right|\right)
is accretive, then

\mathfrak{I}A\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|.

(iii)

If
A
is invertible and
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A^{-1}\right|\right)
is accretive, then

\mathfrak{R}A^{-1}\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left|A^{-1}\right|.

Proof.

We prove part (i) since the other parts are easy to prove. By the inequality (
2.5
), we have for any unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
,

\left|\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right|\leq{{\left\langle{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}x,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Applying Lemma
2.2
for
\Phi\left(T\right)=\left\langle Tx,x\right\rangle{I}\left(x\in\mathcal{H},\left\|x\right\|=1\right)
, implies

{{\left\langle{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}x,x\right\rangle}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\langle\left|A\right|x,x\right\rangle.

Hence,

\left|\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\langle\left|A\right|x,x\right\rangle.

Now, by combining this inequality with the fact that
\left\langle\mathfrak{R}Ax,x\right\rangle\leq\left|\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right|
, we reach the desired result.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|A\right|\right)
is accretive, then

\mathfrak{R}A-\left|A\right|\leq\frac{{{\left(M-m\right)}^{2}}}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|A\right\|{I}.

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\left|\textup{i}A^{*}\right|\right)
is accretive, then

\mathfrak{I}A-\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|\leq\frac{{{\left(M-m\right)}^{2}}}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|A\right\|{I}.

3.
Numerical Radius Inequalities

This section uses the properties of the transform
C_{M,m}
and its consequences to obtain some new numerical radius inequalities. In this context, we recall that the numerical range of
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined as

W(A)=\{\left<Ax,x\right>:x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|=1\}.

Then, we define the numerical radius of
A
as

\omega(A)=\sup\{|z|:z\in W(A)\}.

The numerical radius has a notable recognition in the literature due to its impact on understanding the geometry of the numerical range of the operator. Among the most basic inequalities of the numerical radius, we have

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2}\|A\|\leq\omega(A)\leq\|A\|

and

(3.2)

\|\mathfrak{R}A\|\leq\omega(A)\;{\text{and}}\;\|\mathfrak{I}A\|\leq\omega(A).

We refer the reader to
[
6
,
15
,
23
]
for further information on the numerical radius, its properties, and recent advances in its inequalities.

We begin with the following observation.

Remark 3.1
.

From Theorem
2.1
, we have

\left\|A\right\|\leq\left\|\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\mathfrak{R}A\right\|=\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|.

That is

\left\|A\right\|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|A\right\|-\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|.

Noting that for any operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\omega(A)\leq\|A\|
and
\omega(A)\geq\|\mathfrak{R}A\|
, we deduce that when
C_{M,m}(A)
is accretive, one has

(3.3)

\omega\left(A\right)\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega\left(A\right)-\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\omega\left(A\right),

and

(3.4)

\left\|A\right\|\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\omega\left(A\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|A\right\|-\omega\left(A\right)\leq\frac{{{\left(\sqrt{M}-\sqrt{m}\right)}^{2}}}{M+m}\left\|A\right\|.

The last two inequalities in (
3.4
) have been proved in
[
10
, Remark 35]
. Before proceeding, it is worth mentioning the importance of the above inequalities. We know that for any
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
,
\|\mathfrak{R}A\|\leq\omega(A)
. Thus, the first inequality in (
3.3
) provides a reversed version of this known inequality. Of course, this is valid when
C_{M,m}
is accretive. Further, under this condition, the first inequality in (
3.4
) provides a refinement of the well-known inequality
\|A\|\leq 2\omega(A)
in case we have
\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}<2.
Notice that this latter ratio is always not less than one. Here we give a numerical example to show that for the given matrix, the lower bound
\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\|A\|
is larger than
\frac{\|A\|}{2};
as lower bounds of
\omega(A).
For this, let

A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}5-4\textup{i}&2\textup{i}\\
1+\textup{i}&6\end{array}\right],m=4,M=50.

Then it can be easily seen that

C_{M,m}(A)=\left[\begin{matrix}27-184\textup{i}&6+92\textup{i}\\
52+46\textup{i}&84\end{matrix}\right]\;and\;\mathfrak{R}C_{M,m}(A)=\left[\begin{matrix}54&54+46\textup{i}\\
58-46\textup{i}&168\end{matrix}\right].

Since
\mathfrak{R}C_{M,m}(A)>0
, it follows that
C_{M,m}(A)
is accretive. Now direct calculations show that

\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\|A\|=3.56083\;and\;\frac{\|A\|}{2}=3.3991.

Thus, in this example, we have

\omega(A)\geq\frac{2\sqrt{Mm}}{M+m}\|A\|>\frac{\|A\|}{2},

explaining the significance of the first inequality in (
3.4
).

In the following, we present an inequality that relates the numerical radius of
A
with the norms of its real and imaginary parts as a reversed type of (
3.2
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.
If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
and
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}^{*}}\right)
are accretive, then

\omega\left(A\right)\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\sqrt{\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\left\|\mathfrak{I}A\right\|}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector. Then by the mixed Schwarz inequality
[
16
, pp. 75-76]
, and Theorem
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle Ax,x\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left\langle\left|A\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|x,x\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\sqrt{\left\langle\mathfrak{R}Ax,x\right\rangle\left\langle\mathfrak{I}Ax,x\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\sqrt{\left\|\mathfrak{R}Ax\right\|\left\|\mathfrak{I}Ax\right\|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M+m}{2\sqrt{Mm}}\sqrt{\left\|\mathfrak{R}A\right\|\left\|\mathfrak{I}A\right\|}.

Now, we get the desired result by taking supremum over all unit vectors
x\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

In the following, we present a lower bound of the numerical radius in terms of
\|\;|A|^{2}+|A^{*}|^{2}\|.
The significance of this result is explained in Remark
3.2
below.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

\frac{2Mm}{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}\left\|{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}+{{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\right\|\leq{{\omega}^{2}}\left(A\right).

Proof.

We know that

{{\left\|A\right\|}^{2}}={{\left\|\,\left|A\right|\,\right\|}^{2}}=\left\|{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}\right\|={{\left\|\,\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|\,\right\|}^{2}}=\left\|{{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\right\|.

This, together with Theorem
2.1
, implies that

\left\|{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}+{{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\right\|\leq\frac{{{\left(M+m\right)}^{2}}}{2Mm}{{\omega}^{2}}\left(A\right),

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If

Mm\geq\frac{1}{4}{{\left(M-m\right)}^{2}},

then, Theorem
3.2
improves (see
[
18
, Theorem 1]
)

\frac{1}{4}\left\|{{\left|A\right|}^{2}}+{{\left|{{A}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\right\|\leq{{\omega}^{2}}\left(A\right).

If we let
f(x)=x-\frac{(x-1)2}{4},x\geq 1,
we can see that
f
is increasing on
[1,3]
and is decreasing afterwards. Calculating, we find that
f(x)=0
when
x=3+2\sqrt{2}
, and that
f\geq 0
on
[1,3+2\sqrt{2}],
while it is negative on
[3+2\sqrt{2},\infty).
Letting
x=\frac{M}{m},
this means that the condition
Mm\geq\frac{1}{4}{{\left(M-m\right)}^{2}}
holds when
1<\frac{M}{m}\leq 3+2\sqrt{2}.

On the other hand, a submultiplicative inequality for the numerical radius may be shown as follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
. If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
and
{{C}_{N,n}}\left(B\right)
are accretive, then

\omega\left(AB\right)\leq\frac{\left(M+m\right)\left(N+n\right)}{4\sqrt{MNmn}}\omega\left(A\right)\omega\left(B\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\omega\left(AB\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|AB\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A\right\|\left\|B\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left(M+m\right)\left(N+n\right)}{4\sqrt{MNmn}}\omega\left(A\right)\omega\left(B\right),

where we have used (
3.4
) to obtain the last inequality. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

If

{{\left(\sqrt{MN}-\sqrt{mn}\right)}^{2}}+{{\left(\sqrt{Mn}-\sqrt{Nm}\right)}^{2}}\leq 12\sqrt{MNmn},

then, Corollary
3.1
refines (see
[
15
, Theorem 2.5-2]
)

\omega\left(AB\right)\leq 4\omega\left(A\right)\omega\left(B\right).

It is well known that when
A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
then
\omega(AB-BA^{*})\leq 2\|A\|\omega(B),

[
13
]
. In the following, we present a refinement of this inequality when
C_{M,m}
is accretive.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.

(i)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
is accretive, then

\omega\left(AB-B{{A}^{*}}\right)\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right).

(ii)

If
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}}\right)
is accretive, then

\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right)\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right).

Proof.

By the inequality (see
[
13
]
)

\omega\left(AB-B{{A}^{*}}\right)\leq 2\left\|A\right\|\omega\left(B\right),

and the relation (
1.1
), we can write

\displaystyle\omega\left(AB-B{{A}^{*}}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega\left(\left(A-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right)B-B\left({{A}^{*}}-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|{{A}^{*}}-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right\|\omega\left(B\right)

\displaystyle=2\left\|A-\frac{M+m}{2}{I}\right\|\omega\left(B\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right),

as desired.

The inequality in part (ii) can be shown similarly, so we omit the details.
∎

In the following, we give an example to show how Theorem
3.3
improves the inequality
\omega(AB-BA^{*})\leq 2\|A\|\omega(B).

Example 3.1
.

Let
A=\left[\begin{matrix}2&0\\
-1&4\\
\end{matrix}\right]
,
M=8
, and
m=0.01
. A simple calculation shows that

\mathfrak{R}{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)=\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{551}{50}&-\frac{1601}{200}\\
-\frac{1601}{200}&\frac{399}{25}\\
\end{matrix}\right]>0.

In this case

2\left\|A\right\|\approx 8.31

while

M-m=7.99.

These values imply that Theorem
3.3
improves the inequality

\omega\left(AB-B{{A}^{*}}\right)\leq 2\left\|A\right\|\omega\left(B\right).

We conclude with the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{B}\left(\mathcal{H}\right)
.
If
{C_{M,m}}\left(A\right)
and
{C_{M,m}}\left(\textup{i}{{A}}\right)
are accretive, then

\omega\left(AB\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left|\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right)-\omega\left(AB-{{B}^{*}}A\right)\right|\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right).

Proof.

If we replace
A
by
\textup{i}A
, in Theorem
3.3
, we get

\omega\left(AB\pm{{B}^{*}}A\right)\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right).

This implies,

\displaystyle\omega\left(AB\right)+\frac{1}{2}\left|\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right)-\omega\left(AB-{{B}^{*}}A\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right)+\omega\left(AB-{{B}^{*}}A\right)+\left|\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right)-\omega\left(AB-{{B}^{*}}A\right)\right|\right)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\omega\left(AB+{{B}^{*}}A\right),\omega\left(AB-{{B}^{*}}A\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\left(M-m\right)\omega\left(B\right),

as desired.
∎

The

The

This graph was first introduced and investigated in
[

The

is a

Theorem 29

.

Let
R
be a ring and
A_{I}(R)\neq\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
.
Suppose that
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is a star graph.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

R
is indecomposable.

(2)

A_{I}(R)
is a complete graph.

Proof.

(1) First we note that
R
is a non-reduced ring.
If
R
is reduced, then
[
2
, Corollary 26]
implies that
R\cong F\times D
, where
F
is a field and
D
is an integral domain. By Theorem
19
,

A_{I}(R)=\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
,
a contradiction. Suppose that
R\cong R_{1}\times R_{2}
, where
R_{i}
is a
ring, for
1\leq i\leq 2
. Since
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
has no cycle,
we deduce that
R\cong F\times S
, where
F
is a field and
S
is a ring with a unique
non-trivial ideal by
[
3
, Lemma 1]
. Thus
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)\cong P_{4}
and this contradicts the assumption.

(2) Suppose that
I
is the center of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
.
It is not hard to check that
\mathrm{Ann}(J)=I
, for every
J(\neq I)\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}
. Assume that
J-\hskip-5.69046pt-K

is an edge of
A_{I}(R)
that is not an edge of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
, for some
J,K\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}\setminus\{I\}
.
If
|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|=3
, then there is nothing to prove.
So let
|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|>3
.
Obviously,
\mathrm{Ann}(JK)\neq\mathrm{Ann}(J)\cup\mathrm{Ann}(K)
and
\mathrm{Ann}(J)=\mathrm{Ann}(K)=I
. Hence
\mathrm{Ann}(JK)\neq I
and so
JK=I
.
The equalities
JKK=JK^{2}=(0)
imply that
K^{2}=I
.
Since
JKJ=J^{2}K=(0)
,
J^{2}=I
. Let
H,H^{\prime}\neq I

be two arbitrary annihilating ideals. We prove that the following claims:

Claim 1.
The equality
JH=I
holds. Assume to the contrary,
JH\neq I
.
Since
JJH=J^{2}H
and
J^{2}=I
, we deduce that
JJH=(0)
and hence
JH=I
, a contradiction.

Claim 2.
The equality
HH^{\prime}=I
holds. For if not,
HH^{\prime}\neq I
.
Since
JH^{2}=IH=(0)
, we deduced that
JH^{2}=(0)
and so

H^{2}=I
.
Thus
H^{2}H^{\prime}=IH^{\prime}=(0)
and hence
HH^{\prime}=I
,
a contradiction.

Now, it is easy to see that
\mathrm{Ann}(HH^{\prime})\neq\mathrm{Ann}(H)\cup\mathrm{Ann}(H^{\prime})
.
Therefore
H-\hskip-5.69046pt-H^{\prime}
is an edge of
A_{I}(R)
.
This completes the proof.

\Box

Theorem 30

.

Let
R
be an Artinian ring and
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
be a star graph.
Then
A_{I}(R)
is a complete graph.

Proof.

By
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
, either
R\cong F_{1}\times F_{2}
,
where
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
are fields, or
R
is a local ring with non-zero
maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and one of the following cases holds:

(i)
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=(0)
and
\mathfrak{m}
is the only non-zero proper ideal of
R
.

(ii)
\mathfrak{m}^{3}=(0)
,
\mathfrak{m}^{2}
is the only minimal ideal of
R
and
for every distinct proper ideals
I_{1},I_{2}
of
R
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\neq I_{i}
(
i=1,2
),
I_{1}I_{2}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.

(iii)
\mathfrak{m}^{4}=(0)
,
\mathfrak{m}^{3}\neq(0)
and

\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}=\{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}^{2},\mathfrak{m}^{3}\}
.

If
R\cong F_{1}\times F_{2}
, then
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)=A_{I}(R)=K_{2}
.
Suppose that
R
is a local ring with non-zero maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.
If case (i) holds, then there is nothing to prove.
If case (ii) holds, then for every pair of distinct proper ideals
I_{1},I_{2}
such that

\mathfrak{m}^{2}\neq I_{i}
(
i=1,2
), we have
I_{1}I_{2}=\mathfrak{m}^{2}
and so

\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(I_{1}I_{2})\neq\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(I_{1})\cup\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(I_{2})
.
Thus
A_{I}(R)
is a complete graph.
If case (iii) holds, then
\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}=\{\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}^{2},\mathfrak{m}^{3}\}

and
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is the path
\mathfrak{m}-\hskip-5.69046pt-\mathfrak{m}^{3}-\hskip-5.69046pt-\mathfrak{m}^{2}
.
Obviously,
\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(\mathfrak{m}^{3})\neq\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(\mathfrak{m})\cup\mathrm{Ann}_{R}(\mathfrak{m}^{2})
and so
A_{I}(R)\cong K_{3}
.

\Box

Remark 31

.

Let
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
be a star graph and
I
be the center of

\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
. Then it is easily seen that
I
is a minimal ideal of
R
. Also, it is worthy to mention that
if
R
is a non-reduced ring, then
I^{2}=(0)
(As
R
is indecomposable, by part 1 of Theorem
29
).

Finally, we may characterize star annihilator-ideal graphs of non-reduced rings.

Theorem 32

.

Let
R
be a non-reduced ring with
|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|\geq 2
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

A_{I}(R)
is a star graph;

(2)

\mathrm{gr}(A_{I}(R))=\infty
;

(3)

\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)=A_{I}(R)
and
\mathrm{gr}(\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R))=\infty
;

(4)

\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is a prime ideal and either
Z(R)=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
and

|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|=2
or

Z(R)\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
and
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is a minimal ideal of
R
;

(5)
Either
A_{I}(R)\cong K_{1,1}
or

A_{I}(R)\cong K_{1,\infty}
;

(6)
Either
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)\cong K_{1,1}
or

\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)\cong K_{1,\infty}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
It is clear.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
It follows from Corollary
9
.

(3)\Rightarrow(4)
By Theorems
22
,
28
and

[
4
, Theorem 3.4]
,
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)=A_{I}(R)
is a star graph and

R
has at most two non-zero nilpotent ideals.
Suppose that
R
has exactly one nilpotent ideal.
It is not hard to see that, in this case,
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is the only
nilpotent ideal of
R
. By Remark
31
,
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is a minimal ideal of
R
and so
Z(R)\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
. We need only to show that
\mathrm{Nil}(R)

is a prime ideal of
R
.
Assume that
I,J\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}\setminus\{\mathrm{Nil}(R)\}
.
Since
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is a star graph, we conclude that
IJ\neq(0)
.
We show that
xy\neq 0
, for every
x\in I,~y\in J

where
x,y\notin\mathrm{Nil}(R)^{\ast}
. Assume to the contrary,

xy=0
. Thus
(Rx)(Ry)=(0)
. If
Rx\neq Ry
, then

\mathrm{Nil}(R)-\hskip-5.69046pt-Rx-\hskip-5.69046pt-Ry-\hskip-5.69046pt-\mathrm{Nil}(R)

is a cycle in
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
, a contradiction.
Thus
Rx=Ry
, and hence
(Rx)^{2}=(0)
which means that
Rx\subseteq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is
a nilpotent ideal of
R
, a contradiction. Since
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is the center
of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
,
\mathrm{Nil}(R)Z(R)=(0)
(We note that by
[
8
, Theorem 2.2]
,
Z(R)
is a vertex) and
xy\neq 0

for every
x,y\in Z(R)^{\ast}\setminus(\mathrm{Nil}(R))^{\ast}
, as desired.

Now, assume that
R
has exactly two nilpotent ideals, say
I
and
J
where
I

is the center of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
. If
J
is a minimal
ideal, then

I-\hskip-5.69046pt-I+J-\hskip-5.69046pt-J-\hskip-5.69046pt-I
is a cycle
in
\Gamma^{\prime}_{\mathrm{Ann}}(R)
by Theorem
22
, a contradiction.
If
J
is not a minimal ideal and
I\nsubseteq J
, then there exists
J^{\prime}\subseteq J

and so
J^{\prime}
is a nilpotent ideal of
R
. Therefore

I-\hskip-5.69046pt-J-\hskip-5.69046pt-J^{\prime}-\hskip-5.69046pt-I

is a cycle of length three in
A_{I}(R)
by Theorem
22
,
a contradiction. Hence
I\subsetneqq J
.
If
x\in\mathrm{Nil}(R)
, then
Rx\subseteq J
and so

J=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
. We claim that
J^{2}\neq(0)
. If
J^{2}=(0)
, then
J\subseteq\mathrm{Ann}(J)
.
By the proof of part 2 of Theorem
29
,

\mathrm{Ann}(J)=I
. Therefore
J\subseteq I
, a contradiction and so the claim is proved.
Since
J^{2}
is a nilpotent ideal of
R
, either

J^{2}=I
or
J^{2}=J
. If
J^{2}=J
, then
by Nakayama
,
s Lemma,
J=(0)
, which is impossible. Consequently,
J^{2}=I
.
Now we show that
\mathrm{Nil}(R)=Z(R)
.
Suppose that
x\in Z(R)\setminus\mathrm{Nil}(R)
. Thus
Rx
is an annihilating
ideal of
R
, and hence
Rx\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}
. If
J(Rx)=(0)
,
then
I-\hskip-5.69046pt-J-\hskip-5.69046pt-Rx-\hskip-5.69046pt-I
is a cycle of length three
in
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
, a contradiction. Thus, we may assume that
J(Rx)\neq(0)
.
Since
J(Rx)
is a nilpotent ideal of
R
and
R
has exactly two
nilpotent ideals, either
J(Rx)=I
or

J(Rx)=J
. If
JR(x)=J
, then
J^{2}(Rx)^{2}=J^{2}=I
.
Since
J^{2}(Rx)^{2}=I(Rx)^{2}=(0)
and
I\neq(0)
, we conclude that
J(Rx)=I
. Set
K=I+Rx
. The equality
IK=I^{2}+I(Rx)=(0)
implies that

K
is a vertex of
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
. Clearly,
K\neq I,J
.
Thus
JK=J(I+Rx)=IJ+J(Rx)=I
and hence
\mathrm{Ann}(JK)=\mathrm{Ann}(I)=Z(R)
.
In addition, by the proof of part 2 of Theorem
29
,

\mathrm{Ann}(J)=\mathrm{Ann}(K)=I
. These facts mean that

\mathrm{Ann}(JK)\neq\mathrm{Ann}(J)\cup\mathrm{Ann}(K)
. Therefore,

I-\hskip-5.69046pt-J-\hskip-5.69046pt-K-\hskip-5.69046pt-I
is a cycle of length three
in
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
, a contradiction. So
Z(R)=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
and since
I,J=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
are
nilpotent ideals of
R
, we deduce that
|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|=2
.

(4)\Rightarrow(5)
Suppose that
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is a prime ideal.
Let
Z(R)=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
and

|\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}|=2
.
Then
A_{I}(R)\cong K_{1,1}
.
Now, let
Z(R)\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
and
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
be a minimal ideal of
R
. Since

\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is prime, if
xy=0
where
x,y\neq 0
, then

x\in(\mathrm{Nil}(R))^{\ast}
or
y\in(\mathrm{Nil}(R))^{\ast}
.
Therefore if
I,J\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}\setminus\mathrm{Nil}(R)
, then
IJ\neq(0)
. Thus

\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is a star graph with
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
as the center. It is clear
\mathrm{Ann}(I)=\mathrm{Ann}(J)=\mathrm{Nil}(R)
, for every

I,J\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
.
Hence
\mathrm{Ann}(IJ)=\mathrm{Ann}(I)\cup\mathrm{Ann}(J)
, for every
I,J\in\mathbb{A}(R)^{\ast}

where
I,J\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
, i.e.,
A_{I}(R)

is also star graph. If
A_{I}(R)\neq K_{1,\infty}
,
then
R
has only finitely many ideals, by
[
8
, theorem 1.4]
.
This implies that
R
is an Artinian ring. Since
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is a star graph and
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is
a minimal prime ideal, we get a contradiction (See
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
).
Therefore,
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)\cong K_{1,\infty}
and so the proof is complete.

(5)\Rightarrow(6)
It is obvious.

(6)\Rightarrow(1)
Since
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
is a star graph
and
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)\ncong K_{1,n}
for some
1<n<\infty
,
A_{I}(R)=\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)
by part 2 of Theorem
29
.
Thus
A_{I}(R)
is a star graph.

\Box

The last example of this paper provides a ring
R
such that
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)=A_{I}(R)=K_{1,\infty}
.

Example 33

.
Let
R=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[X,Y]/(X^{2},XY)
and let
x=X+(X^{2}+XY)
,

y=Y+(X^{2}+XY)\in R
. Then
Z(R)=(x,y)R
,
\mathrm{Nil}(R)=(x)R=\{0,x\}
,
and
Z(R)\neq\mathrm{Nil}(R)
. It is clear that
\mathrm{Nil}(R)
is a minimal prime ideal
of
R
and
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{G}(R)=A_{I}(R)=K_{1,\infty}
.

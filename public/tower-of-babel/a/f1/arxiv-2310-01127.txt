Real-analytic modular forms for Γ 0 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{0}(N) and their L L -series
Joshua Drewitt, Joshua Pimm

Abstract
We extend the definition of real-analytic modular forms from S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℤ ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}) to congruence subgroups of the type Γ 0 ​ ( N ) \Gamma_{0}(N) . We examine their properties and discuss examples, such as real-analytic Eisenstein series and modular iterated integrals. We also associate an L L -series to these forms and prove its functional equation. For the L L -series of a special class of forms, which includes length-one modular iterated integrals, we establish a converse theorem.



Key Words:
Real-analytic modular forms, non-holomorphic modular forms, congruence subgroups,
L
-series, converse theorem, real-analytic Eisenstein series, modular iterated integrals.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we set up the basic groundwork needed in order to extend the definition of real-analytic modular forms from
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
to certain congruence subgroups. We begin by introducing such subgroups:

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer, we are interested in (level
N
) congruence subgroups that are of the form

\Gamma_{0}(N)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\,{\rm mod}\,N\right\}.

(2.1)

For a cusp
u
of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, the stabiliser of
u
is given by the group

\Gamma_{u}\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\gamma u=u\}.

There exists a
\sigma_{u}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, called a scaling matrix of
u
, such that
\sigma_{u}(\infty)=u
and

\sigma_{u}^{-1}\Gamma_{u}\sigma_{u}=\Gamma_{\infty}=\left\{\pm\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}:b\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

(2.2)

We can see that
\gamma_{u}\coloneqq\sigma_{u}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\sigma_{u}
and
-\gamma_{u}
generate the stabiliser
\Gamma_{u}
. To make computations simpler throughout, we will always take

\sigma_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(2.3)

Let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character modulo
N
, then, for
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we define
\chi(\gamma)=\chi(d)
(and note that
\overline{\chi(d)}=\chi(a)
). A cusp
u
is said to be singular for
\chi
if
\chi
is trivial on
\Gamma_{u}
.
We note that a Dirichlet character
\chi
is always trivial on
\Gamma_{\infty}
and, therefore, the cusp
\infty
is singular for any
\chi
.

We set
r,s
to be integers throughout and, for
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R})=\{\gamma\in GL(2,\mathbb{R}):\det(\gamma)>0\}
, we define
j(\gamma,z)\coloneqq(cz+d)
. For a function
f\colon\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
, the double slash operator
f||_{r,s}
is then defined by

(f||_{r,s}\gamma)(z)\coloneqq\det(\gamma)^{\frac{r+s}{2}}j(\gamma,z)^{-r}j(\gamma,\bar{z})^{-s}f(\gamma z),\quad\ \forall\ z\in\mathbb{H}.

This is a group action, and therefore, we have

f||_{r,s}(\gamma\mu)=(f||_{r,s}\gamma)||_{r,s}\,\mu\,,\quad\forall\ \gamma,\mu\in GL^{+}(2,\mathbb{R}).

3
Properties of real-analytic modular forms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)

3.1
Key definitions

We are now able to study the space of real-analytic modular forms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. We start by giving the definition of such forms, and then spend the rest of the section studying their fundamental properties and characteristics.
Unless stated otherwise, we will always assume
\chi
is a Dirichlet character modulo
N
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\chi
be a character. We say a real-analytic function
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a real-analytic modular form of weights
(r,s)
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with character
\chi
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

\displaystyle(i)\quad\

\displaystyle F(\gamma z)=\chi(d)j(\gamma,z)^{r}j(\gamma,\bar{z})^{s}F(z),\quad\ \forall\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{H}.

(3.1)

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\

At the cusp
\infty
we have an expansion of the form

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912ptF(z)=\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}y^{j}\left(\sum_{m,n\geq-N_{0}^{\prime}}a_{m,n}^{(j)}\,q^{m}\overline{q}^{n}\right)\!,

(3.2)

where
N_{0},N_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{m,n}^{(j)}\in\mathbb{C}
, and
q\coloneqq e^{2\pi iz}
.

\displaystyle(iii)\quad\

For all
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
there is a
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt(F||_{r,s}\gamma)(x+iy)=O(e^{Cy})

(3.3)

\displaystyle\text{as $y\rightarrow\infty$, uniformly in $x$. We will refer to this as the growth of $F$ \emph{at the cusps}}.

We denote the space of such real-analytic modular forms by
\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and set
\mathcal{M}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\!=\!\bigoplus_{r,s}\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
. The notation
\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
and
\mathcal{M}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
will be used throughout to refer to those spaces where
\chi
is trivial. Since
\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
, we will assume that
\chi(-1)=(-1)^{r+s}
.

If we restrict to the condition that the
a^{(j)}_{m,n}
vanish if either
m
or
n
are negative, then we have a new (sub)space denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.

Let
A
be a complete set of inequivalent cusps for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, if the expansion of
F(z)\in\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
at any singular cusp
u\in A
can be given by

F(\sigma_{u}z)j(\sigma_{u},z)^{-r}j(\sigma_{u},\bar{z})^{-s}=\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}y^{j}\left(\sum_{m,n\geq-N_{0}^{\prime}}a_{m,n}^{u,j}\,q^{m}\overline{q}^{n}\right)\!,

(3.4)

for some
N_{0},N_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{m,n}^{u,j}\in\mathbb{C}
, then
condition
(iii)
of Definition
3.1
is implied. The above expansion reduces to (
3.2
) when taking
\sigma_{u}=\sigma_{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}
and, as we can assume that
A
includes the cusp at
\infty
, condition
(ii)
is also implied. We will refer to these expansions as being ‘at the cusps’.

As with the classical case of
\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
, we equip the space
\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
with a pair of operators given by

\partial_{r}=2i\,\text{Im}(z)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}+r\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{\partial}_{s}=-2i\,\text{Im}(z)\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}+s.

(3.5)

We have the following two lemmas from
[
1
]
(and an additional corollary):

Lemma 3.2

(Lemma 2.5 of
[
1
]
)
.

For all
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
and real analytic
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left(j(\gamma,z)^{-r}F(\gamma z)\right)=\,j(\gamma,z)^{-r-1}j(\gamma,\bar{z})(\partial_{r}F)(\gamma z),

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{s}\left(j(\gamma,\bar{z})^{-s}F(\gamma z)\right)=\,j(\gamma,z)j(\gamma,\bar{z})^{-s-1}(\overline{\partial}_{s}F)(\gamma z).

Corollary 3.3
.

For all
\gamma\in SL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
z\in\mathbb{H}
and real analytic
F:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}((F||_{r,s}\gamma)(z))=((\partial_{r}F)||_{r+1,s-1}\gamma)(z),

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{s}((F||_{r,s}\gamma)(z))=((\overline{\partial}_{s}F)||_{r-1,s+1}\gamma)(z).

Lemma 3.4

(Lemma 2.6 of
[
1
]
)
.

The operators
\partial_{r},\overline{\partial}_{s}
preserve the expansion appearing in the RHS of equation (
3.2
). Their action is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(y^{j}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n})=(-4\pi my+r+j)y^{j}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n},

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{s}(y^{j}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n})=(-4\pi ny+s+j)y^{j}q^{m}\bar{q}^{n}.

Therefore,
\partial_{r}
and
\overline{\partial}_{s}
induce bigraded operators
\partial,\overline{\partial}
on real-analytic functions
F:\mathbb{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of weight
(r,s)
of bidegree
(1,-1)
and
(-1,1)
, respectively. If
F
has weight
(r,s)
, then
\partial
acts on
F
via
\partial_{r}
and similarly
\overline{\partial}
acts via
\overline{\partial}_{s}
. Unfortunately, condition
(iii)
of Definition
3.1
is not in general preserved by the
\partial,\overline{\partial}
operators.

We give the following proposition relating to the kernel of the
\partial
operator, which is an extension of Proposition 3.2 of
[
1
]
:

Proposition 3.5
.

If
F\in\mathcal{M}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
such that
\partial_{r}F=0
, then

y^{r}F\in\overline{M}_{s-r}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi),

where
\overline{M}_{s-r}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
denotes the space of anti-holomorphic forms of weight
s-r
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with character
\chi
. If
r>s
then
F
vanishes, and if
r=s
then
F\in\mathbb{C}y^{-r}
.

Proof.

The same proof used for Proposition 3.2 of
[
1
]
holds here since there are no non-zero holomorphic forms of negative weight for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and character
\chi
. The final part is due to the fact that that
M_{0}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\mathbb{C}
.
∎

This proposition means that the solution to
\partial F=G
, for

and unique up to an addition of

We can use a combination of the
\partial_{r}
and
\overline{\partial}_{s}
operators to construct the Laplacian
\Delta_{r,s}
. This is defined for all integers

by

\Delta_{r,s}=-\overline{\partial}_{s-1}\partial_{r}+r(s-1)=-\partial_{r-1}\overline{\partial}_{s}+s(r-1).

(3.6)

which induces a bigraded operator
\Delta
of bidegree
(0,0)
on real-analytic functions of weight
(r,s)
.

Now that we have introduced real-analytic modular forms for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and the associated operators
\partial

\overline{\partial}
and
\Delta
, we are in a good position to study the real-analytic Eisenstein Series.

3.2
Real-analytic Eisenstein series

The classical holomorphic Eisenstein series for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, defined for all even
r\geq 4
by

\mathbb{G}(z,r):=\dfrac{(r-1)!}{2(2\pi i)^{r}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(m,n)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}(mz+n)^{-r}=\dfrac{\zeta(r)(r-1)!}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}j(\gamma,z)^{-r},

naturally gives rise to a more general definition when studying the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Indeed, we can define the holomorphic Eisenstein series associated to a singular cusp
u
of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and a character
\chi
by

\mathbb{G}_{u}(z,r,\chi)\coloneqq\dfrac{\zeta(r)(r-1)!}{(2\pi i)^{r}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\dfrac{\overline{\chi}(\gamma)}{j(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma,z)^{r}}.

(3.7)

This series is defined for any integer
r\geq 3
provided that
j(\sigma_{u}\gamma\sigma^{-1}_{u},z)^{r}=1
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}
, when
r
is even this is guaranteed to hold.

We also have the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series associated to a singular cusp
u
and character
\chi
, defined for all
\text{Re}(r)>1
by

E_{u}(z,r,\chi)\coloneqq\zeta(2r)\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\overline{\chi}(\gamma)(\text{Im}(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma z))^{r}.

(3.8)

Both of these objects have already undergone intense study in previous literature and have a variety of applications (an interested reader could see

\Gamma_{0}(N)
, which we will define below.
Indeed, as in the classical case above, we wish to generalise the real-analytic Eisenstein series for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, defined by Brown in
[

and given by

\mathcal{E}(z,r,s)\coloneqq\dfrac{w!}{(2\pi i)^{w+1}}\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\\
(m,n)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\dfrac{i\,\text{Im}(z)}{(mz+n)^{r+1}(m\bar{z}+n)^{s+1}},

(3.9)

for

and

and even, to the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. As a way of unifying all of the above Eisenstein series, we will also show how our function relates to (

Definition 3.6

(3.10)

Proposition 3.7
.

Fix a congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, a character

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,w,0,\chi)=-2\pi\emph{Im}(z)\mathbb{G}_{u}(z,w+2,\chi)

(3.11)

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r,s,\chi)-(r+1)\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r+1,s-1,\chi)=0

\displaystyle,\ \hskip 28.45274pt\text{for }s\geq 1.

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,0,w,\chi)=-2\pi\emph{Im}(z)\overline{\mathbb{G}}_{u}(z,w+2,\chi)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r,s,\chi)-(s+1)\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r-1,s+1,\chi)=0

\displaystyle,\ \hskip 28.45274pt\text{for }r\geq 1.

We have

\displaystyle E_{u}(z,r+1,\chi)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2r+2)\!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\!\overline{\chi}(\gamma)(\text{Im}(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma z))^{r+1}

\displaystyle=\zeta(2r+2)\!\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\dfrac{\overline{\chi}(\gamma)\text{Im}(z)^{r+1}}{j(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma,z)^{r+1}j(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma,\bar{z})^{r+1}}

and hence

\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r,r,\chi)=\dfrac{i}{y^{r}}\dfrac{(2r)!}{(2\pi i)^{2r+1}}E_{u}(z,r+1,\chi).

The expansion of the right-hand side is known to lie in
\mathbb{C}[[q,\bar{q}]][y^{\pm}]

\mathbb{C}[[q,\bar{q}]][y^{\pm}]

The two above points prove that

Equation (

-2\pi\text{Im}(z)\mathbb{G}_{u}(z,w+2,\chi)=\dfrac{\zeta(w+2)(w+1)!}{(2\pi i)^{w+1}}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{u}\backslash\Gamma_{0}(N)}\dfrac{\overline{\chi}(\gamma)\>\!i\>\!\text{Im}(z)}{j(\sigma^{-1}_{u}\gamma,z)^{w+2}}

3.3
Eigenfunctions of the Laplacian

For

denote the space of eigenfunctions of

Lemma 3.9

(Lemma 5.2 of
[
1
]
)

Let

be of weights
(r,s)

\mathcal{HM}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{HM}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi;n).

(3.15)

\displaystyle F^{a}(z)\coloneqq\sum^{-r}_{j=k^{\prime}_{0}}y^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq-N^{\prime}\\
m\neq 0\end{subarray}}b_{m}^{(j)}\bar{q}^{m},

(3.16)

\displaystyle F^{0}(z)\coloneqq ay^{k^{\prime}_{0}}+by^{1-r-s-k^{\prime}_{0}},

for some

and

.
If

Finally

,
F^{a}
,

and

are eigenfunctions of

with eigenvalue

.

Proof.

This proof is essentially identical to the proof of the

[
1
]
), which also holds for

(see Lemma 4.3 of
[
3
]
and the remarks following it). The main difference being that in the classical case

is always distinct from

, whereas here

may equal

for

(see Lemma 5.2 of
[
1
]
for more details). This difference leads to the addition of the second decomposition.
∎

3.4
Modular iterated integrals

We fix a congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and a character
\chi
, and then define
\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
to be the space of all real-analytic Eisenstein series associated to
\chi
and any singular cusp of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. By Proposition
3.7
, this space is almost, but not quite, closed under the action of the operators
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
:

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\subset\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)+\mathbb{G}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)[y],

(3.18)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\subset\mathcal{E}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)+\overline{\mathbb{G}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)}[y],

where
\mathbb{G}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
is the space of all holomorphic Eisenstein series associated to
\chi
and any singular cusp of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.

We can generalise this idea to define a space of functions in
\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
, called the space of modular iterated integrals, which will be closed under the operators
\partial
and
\overline{\partial}
,
up to the addition of an extra component (and its complex conjugation). This extra component will include the space
M^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
of weakly holomorphic modular forms, which, in turn, includes
\mathbb{G}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
. We first recall the definition of
M^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and then define the space of modular iterated integrals.

A variation of the transformation equation used to define real-analytic modular forms (
3.1
) is given by

f(\gamma z)=\chi(d)j(\gamma,z)^{r}f(z),\quad\ \forall\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)\text{ and }z\in\mathbb{H}.

A holomorphic function on
\mathbb{H}
that satisfies this equation and is meromorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is a weakly holomorphic modular form of weight
r
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with character
\chi
. The space of such forms is denoted by
M^{!}_{r}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and we set
M^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\bigoplus_{r}M^{!}_{r}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.

Definition 3.11
.

We let
\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{-1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=0
. For any integer
k\geq 0
we define the space of modular iterated integrals of length
k
,
\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
, to be the largest subspace of
\bigoplus_{r,s\geq 0}\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
which satisfies

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k}(\Gamma,\chi)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k}(\Gamma,\chi)+M^{!}(\Gamma,\chi)[y]\otimes\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k-1}(\Gamma,\chi),

(3.19)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k}(\Gamma,\chi)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k}(\Gamma,\chi)+\overline{M^{!}(\Gamma,\chi)}[y]\otimes\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{k-1}(\Gamma,\chi),

where we have set
\Gamma\coloneqq\Gamma_{0}(N)
to keep the above expressions more succinct.

This space is closed under complex conjugation and is an extension of the space given by Brown in Definition 5.1 of
[
3
]
, we also note that our definition reduces to Brown’s one when
\Gamma_{0}(N)=SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
and
\chi
is trivial.

The simplest example we can look at, for non-negative
k
, is when
k=0
. We begin by introducing the following two lemmas (these lemmas are extensions of Lemma 3.1 and Corollary 3.5 from
[
3
]
, their proofs work in a very similar manner and so they are not included for the sake of brevity):

Lemma 3.12
.

For all weights
r,s
we have the following:

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\cap\emph{ker}(\partial_{r})\subset y^{-r}\mathbb{C}[[\overline{q}]],

\displaystyle\hskip 8.53581pt\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\cap\emph{ker}(\overline{\partial}_{s})\subset y^{-s}\mathbb{C}[[q]],

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)\cap\emph{ker}(\partial)\cap\emph{ker}(\overline{\partial})\subset\mathbb{C}[y^{\pm}].

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
with
h:=r-s\geq 0
, and suppose that

\partial F=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\overline{\partial}^{h+1}F=0.

Then
F
vanishes unless
h=0
, in which case
F\in\mathbb{C}y^{-r}
.

Using the above two lemmas we can now give a characterisation of
\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{0}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
.

Proposition 3.14
.

Fix a congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and a character
\chi
, then the associated space of length-zero modular iterated integrals is given by

\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{0}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)=\mathbb{C}[y^{-1}].

(3.20)

Proof.

The proof for Proposition 5.2 of
[
3
]
also works in this case when it is combined with Lemmas
3.12
and
3.13
above.
∎

Using this proposition, we can then view
\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
as the largest subspace of
\bigoplus_{r,s\geq 0}

\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
which satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\partial\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)+M^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)[y^{\pm}],

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)+\overline{M^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)}[y^{\pm}],

which is more reminiscent of (
3.18
). We can deduce from the above that the real-analytic Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}_{u}(z,r,s,\chi)
is a length-one modular iterated integral.
We are also able to extend Proposition 5.8 of
[
3
]
to our more general class of modular iterated integrals, this allows us to decompose length-one modular iterated integrals as sums of eigenfunctions of the Laplaican.

Proposition 3.15
.

Any length-one modular iterated integral
F\in\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
of weights
(r,s)
can be uniquely decomposed as a linear combination

F=\sum_{0\leq k\leq\rm{min}\{r,s\}}F_{k},

where the
F_{k}
also exist in
\mathcal{MI}^{!}_{1}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and have modular weights
(r,s)
. Furthermore, they are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian with eigenvalue
(k-1)(r+s-k)
:

\left(\Delta-(k-1)(r+s-k)\right)F_{k}=0.

4
L-series for real-analytic modular forms

Fix
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}
. We start by defining the matrix

W_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-N^{-1/2}\\
N^{1/2}&0\end{pmatrix}\!,

which will be useful throughout this section, along with the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
F\in\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
and set
G\coloneqq F||_{r,s}W_{N}
, then

G||_{r,s}\gamma=\overline{\chi}(d)G,\qquad\forall\ \gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N)

Proof.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
is of the form
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, then
c/N\in\mathbb{Z}
and

W_{N}\gamma W_{N}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-c/N\\
-bN&a\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(N).

Therefore, we see that

G||_{r,s}\gamma=F||_{r,s}W_{N}||_{r,s}\gamma=F||_{r,s}W_{N}\gamma W_{N}^{-1}||_{r,s}W_{N}=\overline{\chi}(d)F||_{r,s}W_{N}=\overline{\chi}(d)G,

where we used the fact that, since
N|c
,
\overline{\chi}(d)=\chi(a)
.
∎

We also introduce the single slash operator, this is defined for a function
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R_{+}}\to\mathbb{C}
and an integer
a
by

(\varphi|_{a}W_{N})(x)\coloneqq(Nx)^{-a}\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{Nx}\right),\qquad\forall\ x\in\mathbb{R_{+}}.

Now, for each smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the Laplace transform by

(\mathcal{L}\varphi)(s)=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-st}\varphi(t)dt,

for
s\in\mathbb{C}
such that the integral converges absolutely. We also define

\varphi_{s}(x)\coloneqq\varphi(x)x^{s-1}

where, for example,
\varphi_{1}(x)=\varphi(x)
. We denote the space of piece-wise smooth complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}
by
C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})
. Now let
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and suppose
F
is a function on
\mathbb{H}
given by the series

F(z)=\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}y^{k}a_{n}^{(k)}q^{n/M}+\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}y^{k}b_{n}^{(k)}\bar{q}^{n/M}

(4.1)

where
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
is a finite subset. We then let
\mathcal{F}_{F}
be the space of functions
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C})

\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}|a_{n}^{(k)}|\mathcal{L}|\varphi_{k+1}|\left(\frac{2\pi n}{M}\right)+\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}|b_{n}^{(k)}|\mathcal{L}|\varphi_{k+1}|\left(\frac{2\pi n}{M}\right)<\infty.

(4.2)

Definition 4.2
.

Let
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and suppose

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle G\coloneqq F||_{r,s}W_{N}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{F,G}\coloneqq\bigcap_{\chi\mod D}\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{F_{\chi}}:\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N}\in\mathcal{F}_{G_{\bar{\chi}}}\right\}.

\displaystyle L_{F_{\chi}}(\varphi)=\frac{i^{r-s}\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}L_{G_{\bar{\chi}}}(\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N}).

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}|\varphi_{k+1}|\left(\frac{2\pi n}{D}\right)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{\frac{-2\pi ny}{D}}|\varphi_{k+1}(y)|dy

Given the condition (

converges for all

. And we deduce that

.

Recalling the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle F_{\chi}||_{r,s}W_{N}

\displaystyle=D^{(r+s)/2}\sum_{\mu\,\rm{mod}\,D}\overline{\chi(\mu)}\left(F\Big|\Big|_{r,s}\!\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\mu\,D^{-1/2}\\
0&D^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}W_{N}\right)

\displaystyle=D^{(r+s)/2}\sum_{\mu\,\rm{mod}\,D}\overline{\chi(\mu)}\left(F\Big|\Big|_{r,s}\!W_{N}\,\Big|\Big|_{r,s}W_{N}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\mu\,D^{-1/2}\\

W_{N}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\mu\,D^{-1/2}\\
0&D^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}W_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}D&-\nu\\
-N\mu&(1+N\mu\nu)D^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\nu D^{-1/2}\\
0&D^{1/2}\end{pmatrix},

for integers
\mu
and

, the above becomes

\displaystyle F_{\chi}||_{r,s}W_{N}

\displaystyle=D^{(r+s)/2}\sum_{\mu\,\rm{mod}\,D}\overline{\chi(\mu)}\left(G\Big|\Big|_{r,s}\begin{pmatrix}D&-\nu\\
-N\mu&(1+N\mu\nu)D^{-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\nu D^{-1/2}\\
0&D^{1/2}\end{pmatrix}\!\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(-N)\psi(D)D^{(r+s)/2}\sum_{\nu\,\rm{mod}\,D}\chi(\nu)\left(G\Big|\Big|_{r,s}\begin{pmatrix}D^{-1/2}&\nu D^{-1/2}\\

\overline{\chi}(\mu)=\chi(-N)\chi(\nu)
. This allows us to deduce the equality

F_{\chi}||_{r,s}W_{N}=X(-N)\psi(D)G_{\bar{\chi}}.

(4.8)

Making the change of variable

gives

\displaystyle L_{F_{\chi}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N}\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{\chi}\left(\frac{i}{Ny}\right)\,\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{Ny}\right)y^{-2}dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N}\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{\chi}\left(\frac{-1}{Niy}\right)\,\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{Ny}\right)y^{-2}dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{i^{r-s}}{N^{\frac{-r-s+2}{2}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}(F_{\chi}||_{r,s}W_{N})(iy)\,\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{Ny}\right)y^{r+s-2}dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{i^{r-s}X(-N)\psi(D)}{N^{\frac{-r-s+2}{2}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}G_{\bar{\chi}}(iy)\,\varphi\left(\dfrac{1}{Ny}\right)y^{r+s-2}dy,

where we used the equation (

Finally, using

\displaystyle L_{F_{\chi}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{i^{r-s}X(-N)\psi(D)}{N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}\int^{\infty}_{0}G_{\bar{\chi}}\left(iy\right)\,\big(\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N}\big)\!\big(y\big)\,dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{i^{r-s}X(-N)\psi(D)}{N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}\,L_{G_{\bar{\chi}}}(\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N}),

4.1
Converse

To prove our converse theorem, we use the following lemma, in analogy to
[
19
]
(Lemma 1.9.2):

Lemma 4.6
.

Suppose
F
has an expansion of the form

F(z)=\sum_{k\in S}y^{k}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}a_{n}^{(k)}q^{n/M}+\sum_{k\in S}y^{k}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}b_{n}^{(k)}\bar{q}^{n/M},

(4.9)

for
S\subset\mathbb{Z}
a finite subset and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and with the
a_{n}^{(k)},b_{n}^{(k)}\ll e^{Cn^{1/t}}
as in
(
4.6
)
. Further suppose that

F(iy)=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(iy)=0,

(4.10)

for all
y>0
. Then
F(z)=0
for all
z\in\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

We have that

F(iy)=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}P_{n}(y)e^{-2\pi ny/M}

(4.11)

for some ‘polynomials’ with negative powers

P_{n}(y)=\sum_{k\in S}(a_{n}^{(k)}+b_{n}^{(k)})y^{k}.

(4.12)

Similarly, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}F(iy)=\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}Q_{n}(y)e^{-2\pi ny/M},

(4.13)

where

Q_{n}(y)=\frac{2\pi in}{M}\sum_{k\in S}(a_{n}^{(k)}-b_{n}^{(k)})y^{k}.

(4.14)

Now we consider

F(iy)=\sum_{n<0}P_{n}(y)e^{-2\pi ny/M}+\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{n}(y)e^{-2\pi ny/M}.

(4.15)

If nonzero, the right left-hand term grows exponentially in
y
and the right-hand term is
O(y^{k^{\prime}})
for
k^{\prime}
the largest element of
S
, by the condition on the
a_{n}^{(k)},b_{n}^{(k)}
. This is a contradiction, and therefore we at least have the left-hand term being uniformly zero. That is,

F(iy)=\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{n}(y)e^{-2\pi ny/M}=0.

(4.16)

But then we can successively multiply equation (
4.16
) by
e^{2\pi ny/M}
to gain exponentially growing terms like those on the left side of (
4.15
), which by the same reasoning must vanish. Inductively, then,
P_{n}(y)=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. In a similar way, we can get
Q_{n}(y)=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Comparing with (
4.12
) and (
4.14
) gives us a pair of simultaneous equations with the unique solution
a_{n}^{(k)}=b_{n}^{(k)}=0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. That is to say,
F\equiv 0.

∎

is of the form (

) with

a character modulo

and

\displaystyle L_{\partial_{r}(F_{\chi})}(\varphi)=\int^{\infty}_{0}\partial_{r}(F_{\chi})(iy)\varphi(y)dy,

(4.17)

for

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
N,r,s
be integers with
N\geq 1
and
\psi
be a Dirichlet character modulo

. Suppose
a^{(k)}_{n},b^{(k)}_{n},A^{(k)}_{n},B^{(k)}_{n}

(4.19)

for some

F_{1}=\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}y^{k}a_{n}^{(k)}q^{m}+\sum_{k\in S}\sum_{n\geq n_{0}}y^{k}b_{n}^{(k)}\bar{q}^{n}

(4.20)

(4.21)

Finally, assume that for any

,

\displaystyle L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi)=\frac{\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{i^{s-r}N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N}),

(F_{1_{\chi}}||_{r,s}W_{N})(z)=\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}(z)

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in S_{c}(\mathbb{R_{+}})

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}|\varphi_{s_{k+1}}|\left(\frac{2\pi m}{M}\right)&=\int_{0}^{\infty}|y^{s+k-1}||\varphi(y)|e^{-2\pi ym/M}dy\\

Now an application of the Weierstrass

\displaystyle F_{j_{\chi}}(iy)\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{j_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t})y^{-r-s+t}dt,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(F_{j_{\chi}})(iy)\varphi(y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{\partial_{r}F_{j_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t})y^{-r-s+t}dt,

Combining equations (

(4.31)

Following the same procedure as above we also obtain the following equations for the operators

and

\displaystyle\partial_{r}(F_{1_{\chi}})(iy)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{i^{s-r}N^{\frac{r+s}{2}}}y^{-r-s}\partial_{r}(F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}})(i/Ny),

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{s}(F_{1_{\chi}})(iy)\,

, be sequences of complex numbers with condition

.
We can define the smooth functions
F_{1}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)=\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}y^{j}\left(\sum_{m,n\geq-N_{0}^{\prime}}a_{m,n}^{(j)}\,e^{2\pi imz}e^{-2\pi in\bar{z}}\right)

and
F_{2}

\displaystyle F_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}y^{j}\left(\sum_{m,n\geq-N_{0}^{\prime}}b_{m,n}^{(j)}\,e^{2\pi imz}e^{-2\pi in\bar{z}}\right).

(4.39)

Now, for all
D\in\{1,2,...,N^{2}-1\}
with gcd
(D,N)=1
, let
\chi
be a Dirichlet character mod
D
. If, for any
\varphi\in S_{c}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
,
D
and
\chi
, the following equation holds:

L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi;u)=\frac{(i-u)^{r}(-i-u)^{s}\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{(u^{2}+1)^{(r+s)/2}N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u),

(4.40)

for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, then
(F_{1_{\chi}}||_{r,s}W_{N})(z)=\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}(z)
and
F_{1}\in\mathcal{M}^{!}_{r,s}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\psi)
.

Proof sketch.

We start with the equation

\displaystyle L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{t};u)

\displaystyle=\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{1_{\chi}}((i+u)y)\varphi(y\sqrt{u^{2}+1})(y\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{t-1}dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{(i+u)y}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\varphi(y)y^{t-1}dy.

Mellin inversion gives

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{(i+u)y}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\varphi(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{t};u)y^{-t}dt.

Setting
y=1/(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})
gives

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Ny(i-u)}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{t};u)(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{t}dt.

Moving the line of integration from
(\sigma)
to
(r+s-\sigma)
and changing variables
t\to r+s-t
, we get

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Ny(i-u)}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{1_{\chi}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t};u)(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{r+s-t}dt.

Using equation (
4.40
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Ny(i-u)}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)

(4.41)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(i-u)^{r}(-i-u)^{s}\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{(u^{2}+1)^{(r+s)/2}N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u)(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{r+s-t}dt.

Now expand
L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u)=\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y)\,(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N})(y\sqrt{u^{2}+1})dy
:

\displaystyle(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N})(y\sqrt{u^{2}+1})

\displaystyle=(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{r+s-2}\varphi_{r+s-t}\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{r+s-2}(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{t+1-r-s}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{t-1}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right),

and therefore, we have

\displaystyle L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u)=\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y)(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{t-1}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)dy

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\int^{\infty}_{0}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}\left(\frac{(i-u)y}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)y^{t-1}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)dy.

Mellin inversion gives:

\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u)y^{-t}dt=\dfrac{1}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}\left(\frac{(i-u)y}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{y}\right)

and then setting
y=Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}
gives

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\sigma)}L_{F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}}(\varphi_{r+s-t}|_{2-r-s}W_{N};-u)

\displaystyle(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{-t}dt

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\!.

Now, equation (
4.41
) becomes

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Ny(i-u)}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\ \,\ =\frac{(i-u)^{r}(-i-u)^{s}\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{(u^{2}+1)^{(r+s)/2}N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}(Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1})^{r+s}\dfrac{1}{N\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y)\varphi\left(\frac{1}{Ny\sqrt{u^{2}+1}}\right)\!.

Hence

\displaystyle F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Ny(i-u)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(i-u)^{r}(-i-u)^{s}\chi(-N)\psi(D)}{N^{\frac{r+s-2}{2}}}N^{r+s-1}y^{r+s}F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y)

\displaystyle=((i-u)y)^{r}((-i-u)y)^{s}N^{\frac{r+s}{2}}\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}((i-u)y).

Since this holds for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
, this holds for
u=-x/y
. Using this substitution, the above equation becomes

F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{N(x+iy)}\right)=(x+iy)^{r}(x-iy)^{s}N^{\frac{r+s}{2}}\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}(x+iy)

and hence

F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Nz}\right)=z^{r}\bar{z}^{s}N^{\frac{r+s}{2}}\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}(z).

We then conclude that

\displaystyle\chi(-N)\psi(D)F_{2_{\bar{\chi}}}(z)

\displaystyle=z^{-r}\bar{z}^{-s}N^{\frac{-r-s}{2}}F_{1_{\chi}}\left(\frac{-1}{Nz}\right)

\displaystyle=(F_{1_{\chi}}||_{r,s}W_{N})(z),

as required. We then conclude in the same way as in Theorem
4.8
that
F_{1}
satisfies
(i)
of Definition
3.1
with weights
(r,s)
. We can use a result similar to Lemma
4.9
to verify that
F_{1}
has sufficient growth at the cusps for condition
(iii)
.
∎

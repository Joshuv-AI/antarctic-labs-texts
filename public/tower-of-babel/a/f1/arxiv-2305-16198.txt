Kondo screening and coherence in kagome local-moment metals: Energy scales of heavy fermions in the presence of flat bands
Christos Kourris, Matthias Vojta

Abstract
The formation of a heavy Fermi liquid in metals with local moments is characterized by multiple energy and temperature scales, most prominently the Kondo temperature and the coherence temperature, characterizing the onset of Kondo screening and the emergence of Fermi-liquid coherence, respectively. In the standard setting of a wide conduction band, both scales depend exponentially on the Kondo coupling. Here we discuss how the presence of flat, i.e., dispersionless, conduction bands modifies this situation. Focussing on the case of the kagome Kondo-lattice model, we utilize a parton mean-field approach to determine the Kondo temperature and the coherence temperature as function of the conduction-band filling n c n_{c} , both numerically and analytically. For n c n_{c} values corresponding to the flat conduction band located at the Fermi level, we show that the exponential is replaced by a linear dependence for the Kondo temperature and a quadratic dependence for the coherence temperature, while a cubic law emerges for the coherence temperature at n c n_{c} corresponding to the band edge between the flat and dispersive bands. We discuss implications of our results for pertinent experimental data.

Introduction

II
Model and general considerations

II.1
Kagome Kondo-lattice model

As noted above, we are interested in the modifications to standard Kondo phenomenology brought about by flat conduction bands. Many strongly frustrated lattices display flat tight-binding bands as a result of destructive interference. Motivated by its experimental abundance, we choose the kagome lattice, Fig.
1
, where we consider a Kondo-lattice model of SU(2) spins
1/2
(also dubbed
f

J_{\rm K}
. The Hamiltonian reads

\mathcal{H}=t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\left(c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+\rm h.c.\right)+\frac{J_{\rm K}}{2}\sum_{i}\vec{S}_{i}\cdot c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\vec{\tau}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}c_{i\sigma^{\prime}}

(1)

in standard notation

, with
\langle ij\rangle
referring to pairs of nearest-neighbor sites, and

being the vector of Pauli matrices.

The kagome lattice is a non-Bravais lattice with a unit cell of three sites. The conduction-electron part of Eq. (
1

\displaystyle\dutchcal{h}(\vec{k})

\displaystyle=t\begin{pmatrix}0&1+e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{1}}&1+e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{3}}\\
1+e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{1}}&0&1+e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{2}}\\
1+e^{-i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{3}}&1+e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{\beta}_{2}}&0\end{pmatrix}

(2)

where
\vec{k}
is a momentum from the Brillouin zone of the triangular Bravais lattice, the matrix structure refers to sublattice space, and

\vec{\beta}_{1}=(2,0)

\vec{\beta}_{2}=(-1,\sqrt{3})

\vec{\beta}_{3}=(-1,-\sqrt{3})

\displaystyle\epsilon_{fb}=-2t,~\epsilon_{\pm}=t\pm t\sqrt{3+2g_{\vec{k}}}

(3)

II.2
Competing phases

As with any Kondo-lattice model, the physics of Eq. (
1
) is governed by the competition between Kondo screening and inter-moment exchange, mediated by RKKY interactions
doniach77
. In case the latter dominate, magnetically ordered states can arise: a thorough discussion of those in the limit of classical spins (where Kondo screening is suppressed) has been given in Refs.
batista14
;
ghosh16
; they include coplanar
\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{3}
as well as various noncoplanar orders, including multi-
Q
states. For quantum spins
1/2
, it is conceivable that fractionalized Fermi-liquid states (FL
∗
) occur, where the
f
moments do not order, but realize a fractionalized spin liquid
senthil03
;
senthil04
.

In this work, we will focus on the opposite case when Kondo screening dominates. For this, we ignore the effect of inter-site interactions and treat the local moments as quantum spins. With the local moments being screened, a paramagnetic Fermi-liquid phase emerges whose characteristic energy scales –
T_{\rm K}
and
T_{\rm coh}

hewson
– will, however, be strongly influenced by the presence of flat bands. We note that the heavy Fermi liquid is potentially unstable to superconductivity (with the tendency being enhanced for a flat heavy band
kopnin11
;
huber18
); we will ignore such an instability.

We note that Kondo screening is inevitably realized in the strong-coupling limit
J_{\rm K}\gg t
. Here, the ground state is a product of local
c
-
f
singlets, with small perturbative corrections, at
n_{c}=1
. Departing from
n_{c}=1
, unpaired carriers will move in this singlet background forming a standard (heavy) Fermi liquid.

II.3
Renormalized band structure

It is instructive to discuss the heavy Fermi liquid in a picture of renormalized bands: we recall that the relevant low-energy and low-temperature physics is captured by hybridized
c
and
f
bands
hewson
. Assuming non-dispersive bare
f
electrons as in Eq. (
1
), the hybridized band structure will consist of two triplets of bands, separated by a hybridization gap, with both triplets featuring one flat and two dispersive bands. Moreover, the heavy Fermi liquid will obey Luttinger’s theorem, i.e., its Fermi volume equals
(V_{\rm BZ}/2)[3(n_{c}+1)\,{\rm mod}\,2]
where
V_{\rm BZ}
is the momentum-space volume of the first Brillouin zone, and the factors of two and three account for spin and the number of sites per cell, respectively. In contrast, the behavior at elevated temperatures above
T_{\rm K}
is governed by the unhybridized conduction electrons which are weakly scattered off the local moments.

These considerations enable us to map the special band fillings of the problem at hand, as shown in Fig.
2
. The primary focus of this paper will be on fillings
0<n_{c}\lesssim 2/3
where the bare conduction band is flat, but the hybridized band structure features dispersive bands at the Fermi level, such that coherent transport can be expected. A detailed study of the regime
1<n_{c}\leq 5/3
, with a flat heavy-fermion band at the Fermi level (i.e. with ultra-heavy fermions), is left for future work. This latter regime is apparently prone to further flat-band-driven ordering tendencies, driven by a combination of RKKY interactions and effects of quantum geometry
mera22
;
bernevig22
.

III
Parton theory

To describe the heavy Fermi liquid, we utilize the standard Abrikosov fermion representation for the local moments,
\vec{S}_{i}=f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}\vec{\tau}_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}f_{i\sigma^{\prime}}/2
, together with a slave-boson approximation of the Kondo interaction. This approach has been used in numerous earlier works
coleman83
;
coleman84
;
coleman85
;
burdin00
;
hewson
;
senthil03
;
senthil04
, and we quickly summarize its main ingredients.

III.1
Mean-field approximation

Inserting the above spin representation into
\mathcal{H}
yields a quartic Kondo term which is decoupled in the singlet particle–hole channel using a bosonic field
r_{i}
at lattice site
i
. The key approximation is to then assume that
r_{i}
is static, corresponding to a saddle-point approximation which becomes exact in the large-
N
limit where the spin symmetry is generalized to SU(
N
) for both local moments and bath electrons. Consistent with this, the Hilbert-space constraint for the
f
fermions,
\sum_{\sigma}f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}=1
, is enforced only on average via Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{i}
. Assuming furthermore a homogeneous saddle-point solution,
r_{i}\to r
and
\lambda_{i}\to\lambda
, and introducing a chemical potential
\mu
for the conduction electrons, the Hamiltonian takes the form (up to constants)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\rm MF}=

\displaystyle-t\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}\left(c^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{j\sigma}+\rm h.c.\right)-r\sum_{i}\left(f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}+\rm h.c.\right)

\displaystyle-\lambda\sum_{i}f_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}f_{i\sigma}-\mu\sum_{i}c_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}c_{i\sigma},

(4)

together with the self-consistency condition

\displaystyle r=\frac{J_{\rm K}}{2\mathcal{N}}\sum_{i\sigma}\langle f^{\dagger}_{i\sigma}c_{i\sigma}\rangle.

(5)

The mean-field parameter
r
encodes the emergent hybridization between
c
and
f
bands and is thus a measure of Kondo screening. At high temperatures, the mean-field equation is solved by
r=0
only, whereas a finite-
r
solution emerges at low
T
. All solutions preserve SU(2) spin symmetry such that all bands and expectation values are spin-degenerate; therefore we will drop the spin index from now on.
The mean-field problem can be solved in Fourier space. Using the spinor

\psi_{\vec{k}}=(c^{A}_{\vec{k}},c^{B}_{\vec{k}},c^{C}_{\vec{k}},f^{A}_{\vec{k}},f^{B}_{\vec{k}},f^{C}_{\vec{k}})

(6)

we re-write the Hamiltonian per spin flavor as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\sum_{\vec{k}}\psi^{\dagger}_{\vec{k}}\dutchcal{H}(\vec{k})\psi_{\vec{k}}+\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{r^{2}}{J_{\rm K}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{\mu n_{c}}{2}\right)

(7)

where the
6\times 6
Bloch matrix
\dutchcal{H}(\vec{k})
is given by

\displaystyle\dutchcal{H}(\vec{k})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\dutchcal{h}(\vec{k})-\mu\mathbb{I}&-r\mathbb{I}\\
\hline\cr-r\mathbb{I}&-\lambda\mathbb{I}\end{array}\right).

The resulting band structure consists of six hybridized bands, obtained by diagonalizing
\dutchcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\bar{E}_{\text{p}}^{q}

\displaystyle=-\mu+\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon_{p}-\lambda^{\prime}+q\sqrt{(\epsilon_{p}+\lambda^{\prime})^{2}+4r^{2}}\right)

(10)

where
q=\pm
denotes the upper and lower triplet of bands,
p\in\{fb,+,-\}
, and
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda-\mu
. As noted above, the band structure is split into two triplet of bands, separated by a gap. Both triplets support a flat band, a Dirac point, located at
\bar{E}^{+}_{-}=\bar{E}^{+}_{+}
and
\bar{E}^{-}_{-}=\bar{E}^{-}_{+}
respectively, and two vHs each.

The mean-field free-energy density per spin flavor can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(T)=&-T\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\ \rho_{0}(\epsilon)\sum_{p=\pm}\ln\left(1+e^{-\beta\bar{E}^{p}(\epsilon)}\right)\\
&+\left(\frac{r^{2}}{J_{\rm K}}+\frac{\lambda}{2}+\frac{\mu n_{c}}{2}\right)\end{split}

(11)

where
\rho_{0}(\epsilon)
is the DOS of the (free) conduction electrons, and
\bar{E}^{\pm}=-\mu+\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon-\lambda^{\prime}\pm\sqrt{(\epsilon+\lambda^{\prime})^{2}+4r^{2}}\right)
. Minimizing
\mathcal{F}(T)
with respect to the three parameters
r,\mu,\lambda
leads to the mean-field equations

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{J_{\rm K}}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\epsilon}{\epsilon+\lambda}\ \rho_{0}\left(\epsilon+\mu-\frac{r^{2}}{\epsilon+\lambda}\right)n_{F}(\epsilon),

(12)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\epsilon\,r^{2}}{(\epsilon+\lambda)^{2}}\ \rho_{0}\left(\epsilon+\mu-\frac{r^{2}}{\epsilon+\lambda}\right)n_{F}(\epsilon),

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{n_{c}}{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ d\epsilon\ \rho_{0}\left(\epsilon+\mu-\frac{r^{2}}{\epsilon+\lambda}\right)n_{F}(\epsilon)

(14)

with
n_{F}(\epsilon)
the Fermi function. In the remainder of the paper, we will solve these equations numerically for arbitrary
J_{\rm K}
as well as analytically in the small-
J_{\rm K}
limit.

III.2
Kondo temperature

The Kondo temperature
T_{\rm K}
is a crossover temperature which is usually associated with Kondo screening becoming substantial upon cooling. Different quantitative definitions are routinely employed, ranging from elevated-temperature fits of the spin susceptibility to
\chi(T)=C/(T+\sqrt{2}T_{\rm K})

wilson75
to the renormalized dimensionless Kondo coupling becoming unity within a renormalization-group framework. The present parton mean-field theory displays a continuous phase transition as function of
T
where
r
becomes non-zero indicating the onset of screening; the temperature of this transition is taken as the Kondo temperature
T_{\rm K}
. It is known that this transition is an artifact of the mean-field approximation and turns into a crossover once fluctuations of the compact U(1) gauge field are taken into account
compact
.

Solving the MF equation (
12
) for the onset of a finite
r
yields the equation for
T_{\rm K}
:

\displaystyle\frac{2}{J_{\rm K}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\epsilon\ \frac{\rho_{0}(\epsilon+\mu_{0})}{\epsilon}\tanh\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2T_{\rm K}}\right).

(15)

with
\mu_{0}
the bare conduction-electron chemical potential corresponding to
n_{c}
. We note that, within the mean-field approximation,
T_{\rm K}
for the Kondo lattice is identical to that of the single-impurity problem on the same lattice, as only local properties of the conduction band enter. For a flat conduction band with DOS
\rho
, the mean-field theory delivers
T_{\rm K}\propto e^{-1/(\rho J_{\rm K})}
; this exponential agrees with the exact solution of the single-impurity problem
hewson
;
tknote
.

III.3
Coherence temperature

The coherence temperature
T_{\rm coh}
marks the crossover temperature below which the heavy-electron state behaves as a coherent Fermi liquid. Experimentally, it is often defined as the temperature location of the maximum in the resistivity. An alternative definition, more suitable for the present mean-field theory, is via the effective bandwidth of the heavy quasiparticles which can be efficiently obtained from DOS of the hybridized bands at the Fermi energy,
T_{\rm coh}=1/\rho(\omega=0)
, evaluated at
T=0
. For
r\ll D
this can be approximated by
T_{\rm coh}=r^{2}/D
, for
D
the conduction-electron bandwidth and assuming a featureless hybridized DOS. We will employ this expression for
T_{\rm coh}
throughout the paper, note its limitations when appropriate, and also offer an a-posteriori justification by a direct comparison with the mean-field results for
1/\rho(\omega=0)
in Appendix
C
.

Determining
T_{\rm coh}=r^{2}/D
requires solving the MF equations (
12
)-(
14
) in the
T\to 0
limit where we can then replace

when using the integration limits
(\epsilon_{L},0)
, where
\epsilon_{L}=-\mu-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{2}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{2}\right)^{2}+r^{2}}
, and
\mu
corresponds to the

.
We recall that

IV.2
Weak-coupling expansion

To access the asymptotic low-energy behavior, we analyze the mean-field solutions at weak Kondo coupling,
J_{\rm K}\ll t

The behavior of the mean-field solutions is determined by the conduction-electron DOS (per spin). Its exact form can be expressed as

\displaystyle\rho_{0}(\epsilon)=\frac{1}{3}\delta({\epsilon})+\frac{2}{3}|\epsilon-3|\rho_{\Delta}[(\epsilon-3)^{2}-3]

g(n_{c})=\frac{1}{2}n_{c}(\frac{2}{3}-n_{c})
, with the prefactor again symmetric around
n_{c}=\frac{1}{3}
. The numerical data shown in Fig.

J_{\rm K}
except near

. We refer the reader to Appendix
C

IV.4
n_{c}=2/3
: Filled flat conduction band

IV.4.1
Kondo temperature

The divergence of the linear prefactor of the chemical potential, Eq. (
19
), signifies a qualitative change once the flat band is filled. As is sketched in Appendix
A
, the chemical potential is now
\mu(T)=T\ln\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T}\right)
with
T_{0}\simeq 3.126t
. The Kondo MF equation reduces to

\displaystyle J_{\rm K}=6T_{\rm K}\ln\left(\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\rm K}}\right)\cdot\left(1-3a\tilde{\rho}T_{\rm K}\ln\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\rm K}}\right)\,.

(22)

The leading part of this equation can be inverted using the Lambert
W
function:

T_{\rm K}=-\frac{J_{\rm K}}{6W_{-1}(-J_{\rm K}/(6T_{0}))}\,.

(23)

Using the asymptotic expansion of
W_{-1}
yields

T_{\rm K}\approx\frac{J_{\rm K}}{6\ln(6T_{0}/J_{\rm K})}\,.

(24)

IV.4.2
Coherence temperature

At
n_{c}=2/3
the coherence behavior is also qualitatively different, as indicated by the singularity in Eq. (
21
). The chemical potential
\lambda^{\prime}
, which for fillings
1/3<n_{c}<2/3
is such that
\lambda^{\prime}<0
, is now related to the mean-field parameter
r
through
r\approx\frac{3^{1/4}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}|\lambda^{\prime}|^{3/2}
. Correspondingly, the mean-field parameter
r
attains a power-law dependence,
r\propto J_{\rm K}^{3/2}
, leading to

\displaystyle T_{\rm coh}=\frac{1}{3^{3/2}\pi}\frac{J_{\rm K}^{3}}{D^{2}}

(25)

using
D=6t
, in agreement with the numerical data in Fig.
5
.

The analysis makes clear that
n_{c}=2/3
is special because of the touching of a flat and a quadratically dispersing conduction band. This band touching is symmetry-protected so that, in the absence of an additional spin-orbit coupling, the two bands will always touch
bergman08
;
kang20
. In the opposite case of a gap between a flat and a dispersing band, the asymptotic behavior of both
T_{\rm K}
and
T_{\rm coh}
would be perfectly symmetric with respect to the half-filled flat band, without a special power law at the edge.

IV.5
2/3<n_{c}<2
: Dispersive conduction band

IV.5.1
Kondo temperature

For fillings above the flat band, and away from the fillings which correspond to the Dirac point and the two vHs, the system is a normal metal with a finite DOS. This case has been considered fully in Ref.
burdin00
where full expressions with exact prefactors for both the Kondo and coherence temperature are provided. For the fillings considered here,
\mu(T\to 0)
is a filling-dependent constant, for which the Kondo MF equation reproduces the conventional behavior,

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=F_{K}(n_{c})\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}J_{\rm K}}\right);

(26)

the prefactor
F_{K}
is a smooth function of
n_{c}
and has been calculated for a flat conduction band in Ref.
burdin00
. Such exponential behavior is consistent with our numerical data, where
\tilde{\rho}
needs to be replaced with the actual value of the conduction-electron DOS at
n_{c}
.

For the special fillings corresponding to the vHs and the Dirac point, the featureless-DOS approximation is invalid. Previous work on the Kondo effect on the square lattice found that the logarithmic vHs yields a Kondo temperature scaling as
T_{\rm K}^{\rm VH}\sim\exp(-1/\sqrt{\nu J_{\rm K}})
, for
\nu
proportional to the inverse bandwidth
gogolin93
;
irkhin11
;
zhuravlev2018
. The data for the Kondo temperature at the vHs, depicted in Fig.
6
, is in agreement with this scaling.

For the Dirac point, the absence of states at the Fermi level causes the Kondo temperature to vanish exactly at small

.

IV.5.2
Coherence temperature

For the flat DOS that corresponds to the fillings above the flat band, our calculation reduces to a simplified version of the calculation in
burdin00
, with a flat DOS. We find that the exponential dependence of the coherence temperature is recovered,

T_{\rm coh}=F_{\rm coh}(n_{c})\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}J_{\rm K}}\right).

(27)

The prefactor is again a smooth function of
n_{c}

burdin00

\tilde{\rho}

As above, the vHs and the Dirac point are beyond this approximation. The data corresponding to the coherence temperature at the vHs are depicted in Fig.

IV.6
Thermodynamics

An essential outcome of the parton theory is that all the physical properties are determined by the structure of the hybridized bands. In the following, we briefly discuss the thermodynamics for band fillings corresponding to the flat conduction band.

As illustrated in Fig.
2
, fillings
n_{c}\lesssim 2/3
yield dispersive (instead of flat) heavy fermion bands. Hence, the asymptotic low-
T
properties are non-singular, i.e., that of a standard heavy Fermi liquid, with heat capacity
C\sim\gamma T
etc. This behavior can be expected for
T\ll T_{\rm coh}
whereas the specific features of the kagome-lattice band structure will only enter at elevated temperatures.

We have used the parton theory to compute the entropy density per spin,
S(T)
, and the resulting heat capacity,
C=TdS/dT
. A sample result for
J_{\rm K}=0.97t
and
n_{c}=0.03
is shown in Fig.
8
. At this filling, the
T=0
Fermi level is below the Dirac point of the heavy bands. At
T\ll T_{\rm coh}
both
S
and
C
depend linearly on
T
, with a strongly enhanced specific-heat coefficient
C/T
. Upon heating up to
T_{\rm coh}
,
C/T
significantly decreases due to the proximity to the heavy-electron Dirac point. For
T>T_{\rm K}
the mean-field theory delivers
r=0
, such that
S
consists of a small and weakly
T
-dependent
c
-electron contribution and that from a localized
f
level at the Fermi energy, resulting in a value slightly larger than
\ln 2
. The dominant release of entropy upon cooling happens immediately below
T_{\rm K}
where the originally flat
c
band is removed from the Fermi level.

V
Summary and outlook

Using a parton mean-field theory, we have studied the energy/temperature scales characterizing a heavy Fermi liquid in the kagome Kondo lattice model, with focus on electronic fillings corresponding to the flat conduction band and its vicinity. In accordance with the enhanced density of states, we have found a strong enhancement of both the Kondo and coherence temperatures which display power-law instead of exponential dependencies on the Kondo coupling. Such behavior is expected to be generic for flat-band Kondo-lattice systems and to also apply in three space dimensions, e.g., for pyrochlore-lattice systems with flat conduction bands.
We note that the Lieb lattice treated in Refs.
tran18
;
tran19
features inequivalent sites and hence displays multiple Kondo scales.

Beyond the single-site approximation employed here, various instabilities to symmetry-broken as well as topological phases may occur, driven by RKKY-type interactions
doniach77
;
pines08
. On the kagome lattice, these will be influenced in a non-trivial fashion by sublattice interference
thomale
;
batista14
;
ghosh16
and effects of quantum geometry
mera22
;
bernevig22
. The present work establishes the screening scales against which other phases need to compete; detailed studies of this competition are left for future work.

Flat electronic bands are a recurring theme in many experimental contexts, one being magic-angle Moire systems
macdonald20
;
song22
;
kumar22
;
shi22
;
sarma23
. In most cases, the bands are not exactly but only approximately flat. This also applies to many of the heavy-fermion compounds mentioned in the introduction whose kagome structure is distorted. Whether or not Kondo enhancement by flat conduction bands is relevant there is unclear at present; density functional studies of the band structure assuming localized
f
electrons might be helpful. Adapting the analysis of the present paper to almost flat bands is the subject on ongoing work. Our results also call for numerical studies beyond the static mean-field approximation to understand the dynamic properties in the flat-band-dominated regime.

The approximate form of the non-interacting DOS, Eq. (

\displaystyle n_{c}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{1+e^{-\mu_{0}/T}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}\pi}\frac{T}{t}\ln{\left(\frac{e^{\mu_{0}/T}+1}{e^{-(\tilde{D}-\mu_{0})/T}+1}\right)}

A.1.1
0<n_{c}<2/3

In the case of a partially filled flat band, the asymptotic behavior is well captured by the first term of Eq. (

\displaystyle\mu_{0}(T)=T\ln\left(\frac{3n_{c}}{2-3n_{c}}\right)

A.1.2
n_{c}=\frac{2}{3}

The case of a filled flat band is special and needs to be treated separately. The linear term diverges meaning that a qualitatively different solution holds as

A.1.3
\frac{2}{3}<n_{c}<2

\displaystyle\rho(\epsilon)=\left(1+\frac{r^{2}}{(\epsilon+\mu+\lambda^{\prime})^{2}}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\epsilon+\mu-\frac{r^{2}}{\epsilon+\mu+\lambda^{\prime}}\right)

where

is the non-interacting DOS with bandwidth

To obtain the analytic expressions for the mean-field parameter

and

\displaystyle\frac{n_{c}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=\int_{\epsilon_{L}}^{0}d\epsilon\ \rho(\epsilon),

c-

and
f-
electrons shift the chemical potential exactly by
\Delta\mu=\mu_{0}(n_{c}+1)-\mu=-r^{2}/(\mu+\lambda^{\prime})

Appendix B
Kondo temperature

As explained above, the solution for
T_{\rm K}
follows from the single-impurity mean-field equations which lead to Eq. (
15
). Under the approximation taken for the DOS, the flat-band contribution is isolated from the dispersive bands contribution. The equation can then be manipulated to give,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{J_{\rm K}}\approx\frac{1}{3}\frac{\tanh\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm{}_{K}}}{2T_{\rm K}}\right)}{\mu_{\rm{}_{K}}}+\tilde{\rho}\ln\left(\frac{\mu_{\rm{}_{K}}(\tilde{D}-\mu_{\rm{}_{K}})}{T_{\rm K}^{2}}\right)+\kappa\ \tilde{\rho}

(35)

where
\kappa=0.25127
is a constant contribution to the integral of Eq. (
15
) and
\mu_{\rm{}_{K}}\equiv\mu_{0}(T_{\rm K})
is the chemical potential at the Kondo temperature. The last two terms are the asymptotic limit of the integral when
T_{\rm K}\ll D
, so the equation is strictly valid only in the weak-coupling limit.

As explained in the main text, to numerically extract the Kondo temperature we use the fact that the order-parameter critical exponent of mean-field is
\beta=1/2
, i.e., we expect
r\sim\sqrt{T-T_{K}}
. We hence fit
r(T)
to such a dependence to obtain
T_{\rm K}
, with an example displayed in Fig.
11
.

B.1
0<n_{c}<\frac{2}{3}

In the case of a partially fillled flat band, the chemical potential from Eq. (
29
) simplifies the equation but it must still be inverted for
T_{\rm K}
. The latter is achieved by making the ansatz
T_{\rm K}=f_{0}J_{\rm K}+f_{n}J_{\rm K}^{2}\ln(J_{\rm K})
, and expanding perturbatively to obtain,

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=\frac{1}{2}m_{n_{c}}J_{\rm K}-\frac{2}{3}m_{n_{c}}\frac{J_{\rm K}^{2}}{4\tilde{D}}\ln(J_{\rm K}/2\tilde{D})

(36)

with
m_{n_{c}}=\frac{1}{3}\frac{\tanh(\alpha(n_{c})/2)}{\alpha(n_{c})}
. The comparison to numerical data is in Fig.
12
, with excellent agreement.

B.2
n_{c}=\frac{2}{3}

For a fully filled flat band, the form of the chemical potential is different and should thus be treated by its own. Both terms in Eq. (
35
) may be expanded perturbatively for small
T_{\rm K}
, so that the equation to be solved is,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{J_{\rm K}}\approx\frac{1}{3}\frac{1}{T_{\rm K}\ln(T_{0}/T_{\rm K})}\left(1-2\frac{T_{\rm K}}{T_{0}}\right)+\tilde{\rho}\ln\left(\frac{\ln(T_{\rm K})}{T_{\rm K}}\right).

(37)

A truncation to leading order in
T_{\rm K}
allows us to solve for
T_{\rm K}
explicitly,

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=-\frac{J_{\rm K}}{6}\frac{1}{W_{-1}(-J_{\rm K}/6T_{0})}

(38)

where
W_{-1}(x)
is the lower real branch of the Lambert
W
function. The asymptotic form of
W_{-1}(x)\approx\ln(-x)-\ln(-\ln(x))
allows us to approximate
T_{\rm K}
as

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=\frac{J_{\rm K}}{6}\frac{1}{\ln(6T_{0}/J_{\rm K})}\left(1-\frac{\ln\ln(6T_{0}/J_{\rm K})}{\ln(6T_{0}/J_{\rm K})}\right)\,.

(39)

B.3
0<n_{c}<\frac{2}{3}

For fillings in the dispersive band, we should expect conventional Kondo effect behavior. This is indeed the case, since from Eq. (
31
), it follows that the first term in Eq. (
35
) is a constant, so that

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=F_{K}(n_{c})\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}J_{\rm K}}\right)

(40)

up to a filling-dependent prefactor. Note that
\tilde{\rho}
, a constant, replaces the conventional
\rho_{0}(n_{c})
as a result of the approximation to the DOS.

Appendix D
Kondo temperature at large
J_{\rm K}

As an isolated flat band has vanishing kinetic energy, it is instructive to consider a Kondo lattice in the (somewhat artificial) limit of
J_{\rm K}\gg t
. Here we expect that also
T_{K}\gg t
. We can think of a system of dimers, each formed by one
c
and one
f

t
. At half-filling, i.e.
n_{c}=1
, each dimer hosts two electrons forming a singlet, and we expect
T_{\rm K}\propto J_{\rm K}
.

Indeed, we can solve Eq. (
18
) for the case of a single narrow band in the limit
T\gg t
, with the result
\mu(T)=\beta(n_{c})T
with,
\beta(n_{c})=\ln\left(\frac{n_{c}}{2-n_{c}}\right)
. Then, the large-
J_{\rm K}
solution of the Kondo equation is

\displaystyle T_{\rm K}=\frac{1}{2\beta(n_{c})}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta(n_{c})}{2}\right)J_{\rm K}.

(45)

being symmetric about
n_{c}=1
, as expected from a picture of isolated dimers.

We note that Fermi-liquid coherence in a Kondo lattice with
J_{\rm K}\gg t
is limited by
t
(not by
J_{\rm K}
), as the effective carriers display a hopping rate
t
. As a result, we will have
T_{\rm coh}\propto t
. Similarly, any effects of inter-moment interaction are limited by
t
, such that Kondo screening is realized in the limit
J_{\rm K}\gg t
also beyond mean-field theory. This does not exclude that the (heavy-fermion) metal emerging for
n_{c}\neq 1
is unstable against symmetry-breaking order.

Integration in semirings
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
The concept of integral as an inverse to that of derivation was already introduced for rings and recently also for lattices. Since semirings generalize both rings and bounded distributive lattices, it is natural to investigate integration in semirings. This is our aim in the present paper. We show properties of such integrals from the point of view of semiring operations. Examples of semirings with derivation where integrals are introduced are presented in the paper. These illuminate rather specific properties of such integrals. We show when the set of all integrals on a given semiring forms a semiring again.

The formula describing the derivation of a product of two elements can be easily extended to a formula describing the derivation of a finite product of elements.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a semiring,

We use induction on

\displaystyle d(a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n+1})

\displaystyle=d\big((a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}\big)=d(a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n})\cdot a_{n+1}+a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n}\cdot d(a_{n+1})=

\displaystyle=\big(d(a_{1})\cdot a_{2}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n}+\cdots+a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n-1}\cdot d(a_{n})\big)\cdot a_{n+1}+

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n}\cdot d(a_{n+1})=

\displaystyle=d(a_{1})\cdot a_{2}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n+1}+\cdots+a_{1}\cdot\cdots\cdot a_{n}\cdot d(a_{n+1}),

Let us recall some elementary, but useful properties of derivations on semirings.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a semiring and
d_{1},d_{2}\in\Der\mathbf{S}
. Then

\displaystyle(d_{1}+d_{2})(x+y)

\displaystyle=d_{1}(x+y)+d_{2}(x+y)=\big(d_{1}(x)+d_{1}(y)\big)+\big(d_{2}(x)+d_{2}(y)\big)=

\displaystyle=\big(d_{1}(x)+d_{2}(x)\big)+\big(d_{1}(y)+d_{2}(y)\big)=(d_{1}+d_{2})(x)+(d_{1}+d_{2})(y),

\displaystyle(d_{1}+d_{2})(x\cdot y)

\displaystyle=d_{1}(x\cdot y)+d_{2}(x\cdot y)=

\displaystyle=\big(d_{1}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot d_{1}(y)\big)+\big(d_{2}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot d_{2}(y)\big)=

\displaystyle=\big(d_{1}(x)\cdot y+d_{2}(x)\cdot y\big)+\big(x\cdot d_{1}(y)+x\cdot d_{2}(y)\big)=

\displaystyle=\big(d_{1}(x)+d_{2}(x)\big)\cdot y+x\cdot\big(d_{1}(y)+d_{2}(y)\big)=

\displaystyle=(d_{1}+d_{2})(x)\cdot y+x\cdot(d_{1}+d_{2})(y).

Hence
d_{1}+d_{2}\in\Der\mathbf{S}
. Trivially the mapping
d_{0}:S\rightarrow S
defined by
d_{0}(x):=0
for all
x\in S
is a derivation on
\mathbf{S}
. Thus
(\Der\mathbf{S},+,d_{0})
forms a monoid.

Remark 6

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a semiring and

is surjective if and only if

is

Lemma 7

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a semiring,
d\in\Der\mathbf{S}
,

(i)

(ii)

If

(ii)

∎

5
,
I_{d}(a)+I_{d}(b)\subseteq I_{d}(a+b)
. Now we prove the converse inclusion. If
f\in I_{d}(a+b)
and
g:=(-e)+f
then

\displaystyle d(g)

\displaystyle=d\big((-e)+f\big)=d(-e)+d(f)=-d(e)+d(f)=(-a)+(a+b)=

\displaystyle=\big((-a)+a\big)+b=0+b=b

and hence
g\in I_{d}(b)
and

e+g=e+\big((-e)+f\big)=\big(e+(-e)\big)+f=0+f=f.

∎

We are going to show that the set of all
d
-integrals on a given semiring
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
(together with the empty set in the case when
\Int_{d}(S)=S
) can be equipped with two binary operations such that the resulting structure forms a semiring again. A condition for this construction is that the set of all
d
-integrable elements of
S
is closed under multiplication.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\mathbf{S}=(S,+,\cdot,0,1)
be a semiring,
d\in\Der\mathbf{S}
and

I_{d}(S):=\{\emptyset\}\cup\{I_{d}(x)\mid x\in S\},

assume

\Int_{d}(S)\cdot\Int_{d}(S)\subseteq\Int_{d}(S)

(1)

and define binary operations
+
and
\cdot
on
I_{d}(S)
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}I_{d}(x)\oplus I_{d}(y)&:=I_{d}(x+y)&\text{if }I_{d}(x),I_{d}(y)\neq\emptyset,\\
z\oplus\emptyset=\emptyset\oplus z&:=z,&\\
I_{d}(x)\odot I_{d}(y)&:=I_{d}(x\cdot y)&\text{if }I_{d}(x),I_{d}(y)\neq\emptyset,\\
z\odot\emptyset=\emptyset\odot z&:=\emptyset&\end{array}

(
x,y\in S
;
z\in I_{d}(S)
)
. Then
(I_{d}(S),\oplus,\odot,\emptyset)
is a
(
not necessarily unitary
)
semiring.

Proof.

Let
a,b,c\in S
and
e,f\in I_{d}(S)
. It is easy to see that
\oplus
and
\odot
are well-defined. Now (i) of Proposition
5
implies
\Int_{d}(S)+\Int_{d}(S)\subseteq\Int_{d}(S)
. If
a,b\in\Int_{d}(S)
then

I_{d}(a)\oplus I_{d}(b)=I_{d}(a+b)=I_{d}(b+a)=I_{d}(b)\oplus I_{d}(a).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\emptyset\oplus e

\displaystyle=e=e\oplus\emptyset,

\displaystyle e\oplus\emptyset

\displaystyle=e=\emptyset\oplus e.

This shows that
\oplus
is commutative. If
a,b,c\in\Int_{d}(S)
then

\displaystyle\big(I_{d}(a)\oplus I_{d}(b)\big)\oplus I_{d}(c)

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a+b)\oplus I_{d}(c)=I_{d}\big((a+b)+c\big)=I_{d}\big(a+(b+c)\big)=

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a)\oplus I_{d}(b+c)=I_{d}(a)\oplus\big(I_{d}(b)\oplus I_{d}(c)\big).

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\emptyset\oplus e)\oplus f

\displaystyle=e\oplus f=\emptyset\oplus(e\oplus f),

\displaystyle(e\oplus\emptyset)\oplus f

\displaystyle=e\oplus f=e\oplus(\emptyset\oplus f),

\displaystyle(e\oplus f)\oplus\emptyset

\displaystyle=e\oplus f=e\oplus(f\oplus\emptyset).

This shows that
\oplus
is associative. Because of

e\oplus\emptyset=\emptyset\oplus e=e,

\emptyset
is the neutral element with respect to
\oplus
. In order to be able to prove associativity of
\odot
we need assumption (
1
). If
a,b,c\in\Int_{d}(S)
then

\displaystyle\big(I_{d}(a)\odot I_{d}(b)\big)\odot I_{d}(c)

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a\cdot b)\odot I_{d}(c)=I_{d}\big((a\cdot b)\cdot c\big)=I_{d}\big(a\cdot(b\cdot c)\big)=

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a)\odot I_{d}(b\cdot c)=I_{d}(a)\odot\big(I_{d}(b)\odot I_{d}(c)\big).

We have

\displaystyle(\emptyset\odot e)\odot f

\displaystyle=\emptyset\odot f=\emptyset=\emptyset\odot(e\odot f),

\displaystyle(e\odot\emptyset)\odot f

\displaystyle=\emptyset\odot f=\emptyset=e\odot\emptyset=e\odot(\emptyset\odot f),

\displaystyle(e\odot f)\odot\emptyset

\displaystyle=\emptyset=e\odot\emptyset=e\odot(f\odot\emptyset).

This shows that
\odot
is associative. If
a,b,c\in\Int_{d}(S)
then

\displaystyle\big(I_{d}(a)\oplus I_{d}(b)\big)\odot I_{d}(c)

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a+b)\odot I_{d}(c)=I_{d}\big((a+b)\cdot c\big)=I_{d}(a\cdot c+b\cdot c)=

\displaystyle=I_{d}(a\cdot c)\oplus I_{d}(b\cdot c)=\big(I_{d}(a)\odot I_{d}(c)\big)\oplus\big(I_{d}(b)\odot I_{d}(c)\big).

Moreover,

\displaystyle(\emptyset\oplus e)\odot f

\displaystyle=e\odot f=\emptyset\oplus(e\odot f)=(\emptyset\odot f)\oplus(e\odot f),

\displaystyle(e\oplus\emptyset)\odot f

\displaystyle=e\odot f=(e\odot f)\oplus\emptyset=(e\odot f)\oplus(\emptyset\odot f),

\displaystyle(e\oplus f)\odot\emptyset

\displaystyle=\emptyset=\emptyset\oplus\emptyset=(e\odot\emptyset)\oplus(f\odot\emptyset).

This proves one distributive law. The other one can be shown analogously. Finally,

e\odot\emptyset=\emptyset\odot e=\emptyset.

The proof is complete.
∎

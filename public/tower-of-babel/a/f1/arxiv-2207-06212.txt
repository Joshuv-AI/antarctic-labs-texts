A new combinatorial formula for alternating descent polynomials Thanks: August 10, 2026
Qiongqiong Pan

Abstract
. We prove a combinatorial formula for the alternating descent polynomials of type A and B. Combining with Josuat-Vergès’ combinatorial interpretation for Hoffman’s derivative polynomials for tangent and secant functions, we obtain a unified combinatorial proof of Ma-Yeh’s two formulae linking these two families of polynomials.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
denote the symmetric group of all permutations of
[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
. For a permutation
\sigma=\sigma(1)\sigma(2)\ldots\sigma(n)\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
,
an index
i\in[n-1]
is a
descent
if
\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
. The generating function of
descent statistic is the classical Eulerian polynomials.
In 2008 Chebikin
[
2
]
studied new statistics on permutations that are variations of the descent statistic. We say that
\sigma=\sigma(1)\sigma(2)\cdots\sigma(n)
has an
alternating descent
at position
i
if either
\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
and
i
is odd, or else if
\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
and
i
is even.

Let
\hat{D}(\sigma)
be the set of
alternating descent positions

of
\sigma
, and set
\hat{d}(\sigma)=|\hat{D}(\sigma)|
. We define
the alternating Eulerian polynomials
\hat{A}(x)

by

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{n}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}x^{\hat{d}(\sigma)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\hat{A}(n,k)x^{k}.

(1)

Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{n}
the collection of permutations
\sigma
of the set

[\pm n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\cup\{-1,-2,\ldots,-n\}
such that
\sigma(-i)=\sigma(i)
for all
i\in[n]
. Obviously, the word
|\sigma|:=|\sigma(1)|\cdots|\sigma(n)|
is a permutation
in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
. An element of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is called a permutation of type
B
.
As usual, we always assume that type B permutations are prepended by
0
.
That is, we identify an element
\sigma=\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(n)
in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
with the word
\sigma(0)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(n)
, where
\sigma(0)=0
.
Following Ma and Yeh
[
9
]

we say that
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
has an alternating descent at position
i\in\{0\}\cup[n-1]

if either
\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
and
i
is even, or else if
\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
and
i
is odd.

Let
\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)
be the set of positions at which
\sigma
has an alternating descent, and set
\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)=|\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)|
.
Similarly, we define the type B alternating Eulerian polynomials
\hat{B}_{n}(x)
by

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{n}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{B}(n,k)x^{k}.

(2)

In what follows, to any
\sigma=\sigma(1)\ldots\sigma(n)\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
we append

\sigma(n+1)
and prepend
\sigma(0)
in two different ways:

(1)

\sigma(0)=n+1
and
\sigma(n+1)=n+1
;

(2)

\sigma(0)=0
and
\sigma(n+1)=n+1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
be the set of permutations in
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
with the first convention and

{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}
the second convention, respectively.
For
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}\cup{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}
, an integer
i\in[n]
is

•

a double ascent of
\sigma
if
\sigma(i-1)<\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
,

•

a double descent of
\sigma
if
\sigma(i-1)>\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
,

•

a valley of
\sigma
if
\sigma(i-1)>\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)
,

•

a peak of
\sigma
if
\sigma(i-1)<\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)
.

Let
\rm{da}(\sigma)
,
\mathrm{dd}(\sigma)
,
\rm{val}(\sigma)
and
\rm{pk}(\sigma)
denote the number of double ascents, double descents,
valleys and peaks of
\sigma
, respectively. And let
\mathrm{DA}(\sigma)
,
\mathrm{DD}(\sigma)
,
\mathrm{VAL}(\sigma)
and
\mathrm{PEAK}(\sigma)
denote the set of double ascents, double descents,
valleys and peaks of
\sigma
, respectively. It is easy to verify the
following facts:

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n},\quad\rm{val}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\rm{pk}(\sigma)+1,\quad 2\,\rm{val}(\sigma)+\rm{dda}(\sigma)=n+1,

(3)

\displaystyle\forall\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0},\quad\rm{val}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\rm{pk}(\sigma),\quad 2\,\rm{val}(\sigma)+\rm{dda}(\sigma)=n,

(4)

where
\rm{dda}(\sigma)=\rm{da}(\sigma)+\rm{dd}(\sigma)
.
Our starting point is the following result.

Theorem 1
.

We have

\displaystyle 2^{n}\hat{A}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}2^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{pk}(\sigma)},

(5)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}2^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{pk}(\sigma)}.

(6)

Hoffman
[
5
]
considered the derivative
polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
appearing in the successive derivatives of
\tan x
and
\sec x
:

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{d}^{n}}{\textrm{d}x^{n}}\tan(x)=P_{n}(\tan(x)),\quad\frac{\textrm{d}^{n}}{\textrm{d}x^{n}}\sec(x)=Q_{n}(\tan(x))\sec(x).

Clearly these polynomials satisfy the differential equations

\displaystyle P_{n+1}(x)=(1+x^{2})P_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(x),\quad Q_{n+1}(x)=xQ_{n}(x)+(1+x^{2})Q_{n}^{{}^{\prime}}(x).

(7)

Hoffman proved the following generating function formulas:

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}P_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin(z)+xcos(z)}{\cos(z)-x\sin(z)},

(8)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}Q_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\cos(z)-x\sin(z)}.

(9)

For
n\geq 1
, the following combinatorial interpretation are due to Josuat-Vergès
[
6
]
.

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}x^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{val}(\sigma)},

(10)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}}x^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}.

(11)

By (
3
) and (
4
), we derive from the above identities that

\displaystyle(1-x)^{n+1}P_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}(2+2x^{2})^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=(1+x^{2})\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}2^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{pk}(\sigma)},

(12)

and

\displaystyle(1-x)^{n}Q_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}(2+2x^{2})^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}^{0}}(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}2^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{pk}(\sigma)}.

(13)

Combining Theorem
1
with (
12
) and (
13
) we derive the following relation linking the derivative polynomials and descent polynomials.

Theorem 2
.

We have

\displaystyle 2^{n}(1+x^{2})\hat{A}_{n}(x)=

\displaystyle(1-x)^{n+1}P_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{n}(x)=

\displaystyle(1-x)^{n}Q_{n}\left(\frac{1+x}{1-x}\right).

(15)

Now, invoking the generating functions (
8
) and (
9
), Theorem
2
implies immediately
the generating functions of the descent polynomials.

Theorem 3
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n>0}\hat{A}_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sec(1-x)z+\tan(1-x)z-1}{1-x(\sec(1-x)z+\tan(1-x)z)},

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\hat{B}_{n}(x)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{x-1}{(x-1)\cos(z(1-x))+(x+1)\sin(z(1-x))}.

(17)

Ma and Yeh
[
9
]
proved that (
14
) and (
16
) are equivalent and conjectured (
15
). Formula (
16
) is due to
Chebikin
[
2
]

and further generalized by Remmel
[
10
]
and Gessel and Zhuang
[
4
]
.
Recently, several
classical approaches
to
(
16
) and (
17
) were given by Ma-Fang-Mansour-Yeh
[
8
]
,
Lin-Ma-Wang-Wang
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
and Ding-Zhu
[
3
]
. Here, by
classical approach
we mean that the proof consists of first establishes a linear recurrence relation of the polynomials, then converting the latter into a differential equation of the generating function, and finally solving the differential equation.

We shall prove Theorem
1

in section 2 and give another proof of (
17
) in Section 3, which is inspired by
Chebikin’s proof of (
16
) in
[
2
]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

For a
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, we can obtain
2^{n}
of type B permutations by signing
\sigma(i)
with
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
, for
i\in[n]
.
Since sign
\epsilon_{i}
’s can be seen as involutions and that they commute, hence the group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
acts on
{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
via the functions
\prod_{i\in S}\epsilon_{i}
,
S\subset[n]
. For
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, let
\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)=\{g(\sigma):g\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\}
be the orbit of
\sigma
under the signed action. For example, the orbit of
123
in
\mathcal{B}_{3}
has the following elements:

\mathrm{Orb}(123)=\{123,\;\bar{1}23,\;1\bar{2}3,\;\;12\bar{3},\;\bar{1}\bar{2}3,\;1\bar{2}\bar{3},\;\bar{1}2\bar{3},\;\bar{1}\bar{2}\bar{3}\}.

For convention, we still use
\hat{d}(\pi)
to denote the number of alternating descent of
\pi\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, the only difference of
\hat{d}(\pi)
from
\hat{d}_{B}(\pi)
is that we do not consider the 0 position in
\hat{d}(\pi)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\pi)=

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{B}(\pi)-1,

if
\pi(1)>0

\displaystyle\hat{d}(\pi)=

\displaystyle\hat{d}_{B}(\pi),

if
\pi(1)<0
.

Let
E=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a subtset of
\mathbb{Z}
. A permutation (or list)

\pi
of elements of
E
is order isomorphic to
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
if
\sigma
is the permutation obtained from
\pi
by replacing its
i
-th smallest entry with
i
. For any
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, let
\mathrm{Iso}(\sigma)
be the set of
permutations isomorphic to

\sigma
in
\mathcal{B}_{n}
. For example, the permutations isormorphic to
123
in
\mathcal{B}_{3}
are

\mathrm{Iso}(123)=\{123,\;\bar{1}23,\;\bar{3}\bar{2}\bar{1},\;\bar{3}\bar{2}1,\;\bar{2}13,\;\bar{2}\bar{1}3,\;\bar{3}12,\;\bar{3}\bar{1}2\}.

Clearly we have

2^{n}\hat{A}_{n}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Iso}(\sigma)}x^{\hat{d}(\pi)}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}x^{\hat{d}(\pi)}.

(19)

Lemma 1
.

For
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and
\pi=\epsilon_{1}\sigma(1)\epsilon_{2}\sigma(2)\cdots\epsilon_{n}\sigma(n)\in\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)
.

(i)

If
i
is a double ascent in
\sigma
, then whether
i-1
is an alternating descent position in
\pi
or not, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i)
.

(ii)

If
i
is a double descent in
\sigma
, then whether
i
is an alternating descent position in
\pi
or not, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i)
.

(iii)

If
i
is a peak, then whether both
i-1
and
i
are alternating descents positions or neither
i-1
nor
i
is alternating descent position, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i)
.

(iv)

If
i
is a valley, then whether
i
is an alternating descent or not, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i+1)
.

Proof.

For
\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
, let
\pi=\epsilon_{1}\sigma(1)\epsilon_{2}\sigma(2)\cdots\epsilon_{n}\sigma(n)\in\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)
, if
i\in[n-1]
and
\sigma(i-1)<\sigma(i)
, then we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i-1}\sigma(i-1)<\epsilon_{i}\sigma(i),

if
\epsilon_{i}=1

\displaystyle\epsilon_{i-1}\sigma(i-1)>\epsilon_{i}\sigma(i),

if
\epsilon_{i}=-1
.

So, whether
i-1
is an alternating descent position in
\pi
or not, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i)
.
Similarly, if
\sigma(i-1)>\sigma(i)
, whether
i-1
is an alternating descent position in
\pi
or not, it is only determined by the sign of
\pi(i-1)
.

The properties (i)-(iv) can be verified easily
by the above oberservations.
∎

Then we prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 2
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)}x^{\hat{d}(\pi)}=(1+x)^{\rm{dda}(\sigma)}2^{\rm{val}(\sigma)}(1+x^{2})^{\rm{pk}(\sigma)}.

(21)

Proof.

In Lemma
1
, for the case (iv), if
i
is a valley of
\sigma
, then
i+1
must be a peak or a double ascent in
\sigma
, so for position
i
, whether it can be an alternating descent or not should be considered in (i) or (iii) case, which means for
\pi\in\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)
, the number of alternating descent of
\pi
is only determined by the signs of peaks, double ascents and double descents of
\sigma
. That is, if sign a peak of
\sigma
with
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
, then it will contribute either 0 or 2 alternating descents, i.e.,
(1+x^{2})
. If sign a double descent or a double ascent with
\epsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
, then it will contribute either 0 or 1 alternating descent, i.e.,
(1+x)
. For valleys of
\sigma
, each valley has two possibilities to sign it. Then we have (
21
).
∎

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{B}_{n}}x^{\hat{d}(\pi)}=\sum_{\sigma\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Orb}(\sigma)}x^{\hat{d}(\pi)},

(22)

by Lemma
2
we have (
5
).
Similarly, we prove (
6
).

Another proof of Theorem (
17
)

Let
\sigma
be a permutation of type A or B.
We say that
\sigma
is
an
(down-up) alternating permutation

with length
n
if
\sigma(1)>\sigma(2)<\sigma(3)>\sigma(4)\cdots
. It is well known that the numbers of
alternating permutations of type A are
the Euler numbers
E_{n}
, which have the exponential generating function

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n>0}E_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\tan x+\sec x.

(23)

Let
\mathcal{DU}_{n}^{(B)}
be the set of (down-up) alternating permutations of type B with length
n
. Let
DU_{n}^{(B)}
be the cardinality of
\mathcal{DU}_{n}^{(B)}
, clearly, we have
DU_{n}^{(B)}=2^{n}E_{n}
. Similarly we define
the
up-down alternating permutations of type
A or B.
An alternating permutation of type B starting with a positive entry is called a
snake
(of type B). Let
\mathcal{S}_{n}
be the set of snakes of
length
n
. Arnol’d
[
1
]
showed that
the numbers of snakes
S_{n}:=|\mathcal{S}_{n}|
have the generating function
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n>0}S_{n}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{\cos(x)-\sin(x)}.

(24)

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}|\sigma(1)<0\}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{+}=\{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}|\sigma(1)>0\}
, and denote

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{n}^{-}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)},\quad\hat{B}_{n}^{+}(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{+}}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)},

then
\mathcal{B}_{n}=\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}\cup\mathcal{B}_{n}^{+}
and
\hat{B}_{n}(x)=\hat{B}_{n}^{-}(x)+\hat{B}_{n}^{+}(x)
.

We begin by deducing a formula for the number of permutations in
\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}
with a given alternating descent set. For
\bar{S}\subseteq[n-1]
, let
\hat{\beta}_{n}^{-}(\bar{S})
be the number of permutations
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}
with
\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)=\bar{S}
, and let
\hat{\alpha}_{n}^{-}(\bar{S})=\sum_{T\subseteq\bar{S}}\hat{\beta}_{n}^{-}(T)
be the number of permutations
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}
with
\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)\subseteq\bar{S}
. For
\bar{S}=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\}\subseteq[n-1]
, let
co(\bar{S})
be the composition
(s_{1},s_{2}-s_{1},s_{3}-s_{2},\ldots,s_{k}-s_{k-1},n-s_{k})
of
n
, and for a composition
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{l})
of
n
, let
\bar{S}_{\gamma}
be the subset
\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\,\ldots,\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\gamma_{l-1}\}
of
[n-1]
. Also, define

\displaystyle{n\choose\gamma}:={n\choose\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{l}}=\frac{n!}{\gamma_{1}!\cdots\gamma_{l}!}.

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, let
\sigma^{-}=\sigma^{-}(1)\ldots\sigma^{-}(n)\in\mathcal{B}_{n}
, where
\sigma^{-}(i)=-\sigma(i)
. Obviously,
\sigma\longmapsto\sigma^{-}
is a bijection on
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.
Now, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

We have

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{n}^{-}(\bar{S})={n\choose s_{1},s_{2}-s_{1},\cdots,n-s_{k}}\cdot S_{s_{1}}\cdot DU_{s_{2}-s_{1}}^{(B)}\cdots DU_{n-s_{k}}^{(B)}.

(25)

Proof.

Let
\bar{S}=\{s_{1}<s_{2}<\cdots<s_{k}\}\subseteq[n-1]
. Set
s_{0}=0
and
s_{k+1}=n
for convenience. The alternating descent set of a permutation
\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}
is contained in
\bar{S}
if and only if for all
1<i\leq k+1
, the subword
\tau_{i}=\sigma(s_{i-1}+1)\sigma(s_{i-1}+2)\cdots\sigma(s_{i})
forms either an up-down(if
s_{i-1}
is even) or a down-up(if
s_{i-1}
is odd) alternating permutations of type B. And for the subword
\tau_{1}=\sigma(s_{0}+1)\sigma(s_{0}+2)\cdots\sigma(s_{1})
,
\tau^{-}_{1}
forms a snake of type B. Thus to construct a permutation
\sigma
with
\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)\subseteq\bar{S}
, one must choose one of the
{n\choose co(\bar{S})}
ways to distribute the elements of
[n]
among the subwords
|\tau_{1}|,\ldots,|\tau_{k+1}|
, then for each
1<i\leq k
choose one of the
\mathrm{DU}_{s_{i}-s_{i-1}}^{(B)}
ways of ordering the signed elements within the subword
\tau_{i}
and for
i=1
choose one of the
\mathrm{S}_{s_{1}}
ways of ordering the signed elements within the subword
\tau^{-}_{1}
. Then the equation (
25
) follows.
∎

Now, consider the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{\bar{S}\subseteq[n-1]}\hat{\alpha}_{n}^{-}(\bar{S})\cdot x^{|\bar{S}|}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}^{-}_{n}}(\sum_{\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)\subseteq T}x^{|T|}).

(26)

The right hand side of (
26
) is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}}\sum_{T\supseteq\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)+|T-\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1-\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)}{n-1-\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)\choose i}x^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}}x^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)}(1+x)^{n-1-\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)},

(27)

as there are
{n-1-\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)\choose i}
subsets with
i
elements of
[n-1]\setminus\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)
containing
\hat{D}_{B}(\sigma)
.
Continuing with the right hand side of (
27
), we get

\displaystyle(1+x)^{n-1}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{B}_{n}^{-}}\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)^{\hat{d}_{B}(\sigma)}=(1+x)^{n-1}\hat{B}_{n}^{-}\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right).

(28)

Combining equations (
26
)-(
28
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{S\subseteq[n-1]}\hat{\alpha}_{n}(S)\cdot x^{|S|}\right)\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{1+x}\sum_{n\geq 1}\hat{B}_{n}^{-}\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)\cdot\frac{z^{n}(1+x)^{n}}{n!},

(29)

since
\bar{S}\longmapsto co(\bar{S})
is a bijection between
[n-1]
and the set of compositions of
n
, the left hand side of (
29
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{\gamma}\frac{S_{s_{1}}}{\gamma_{1}!}\cdot\frac{DU_{\gamma_{2}}^{(B)}}{\gamma_{2}!}\cdots\frac{DU_{\gamma_{l}}^{(B)}}{\gamma_{l}!}\cdot x^{l-1}\right)\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\geq 1}x^{l-1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{DU_{i}^{(B)}z^{i}}{i!}\right)^{l-1}\left(\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{S_{i}z^{i}}{i!}\right).

As
DU_{n}^{(B)}=2^{n}E_{n}
, by (
23
)
and (
24
), the last sum is equal to

\displaystyle\sum_{l\geq 1}x^{l-1}(\tan(2z)+\sec(2z)-1)^{l-1}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\cos(z)-\sin(z)}-1\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{\cos(z)-\sin(z)}-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{1-x(\tan(2z)+\sec(2z)-1)}\right).

thus, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\hat{B}_{n}^{-}\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)\frac{[z(1+x)]^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1+x}{1-x(\tan(2z)+\sec(2z)-1)}\left(\frac{1}{\cos(z)-\sin(z)}-1\right).

Replacing
z
by
\frac{z}{1+x}
,
x
by
\frac{x}{1-x}

Abstract
. We study the variance of a linear statistic of the Laplace eigenvalues on a hyperbolic surface, when the surface varies over the moduli space of all surfaces of fixed genus, sampled at random according to the Weil-Petersson measure. The ensemble variance of the linear statistic was recently shown to coincide with that of the corresponding statistic in the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE) of random matrix theory, in the double limit of first taking large genus and then shrinking size of the energy window. In this note we show that in this same limit, the energy variance for a typical surface is close to the GOE result, a feature called “ergodicity” in the random matrix theory literature.

Background on point processes

Let
\mathcal{N}

\mu

{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

\mu(0)=0

\mu(t)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}

A measure

has a representation

(3.1)

\mu=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\delta_{\xi_{j}},

where

, and
0<\xi_{1}\leq\xi_{2}\leq\ldots
, and sometimes it is convenient to regard

(3.2)

\mu=\{\xi_{j}\}_{j\geq 1}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}

Given a sequence

, we say that

vaguely converges

is continuous at

(3.3)

\mu^{n}(t)\rightarrow\mu(t).

Equivalently, for every non-negative continuous test function of compact support

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\mu^{n}(dx)\rightarrow\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}f(x)\mu(dx).

A point process (in a somewhat restrictive sense, sufficient for our purposes)

(w.r.t. the vague topology on
\mathcal{N}
, generating the Borel

We say that a point process

A sequence of point processes

(written

(\mathbf{N}_{n}(B_{1}),\dots,\mathbf{N}_{n}(B_{k}))
converges in distribution to the random vector
(\mathbf{N}(B_{1}),\dots,\mathbf{N}(B_{k}))
for all
k\geq 1
and Borel sets

with boundaries satisfying
\mathbf{N}(\partial B_{i})=0
almost surely for all
i
. The convergence of
\mathbf{N}_{n}
to
\mathbf{N}
implies

[
3

that the random element
\mathbf{N}_{n}
converges, in distribution, to
\mathbf{N}
.

For a measure

Next, for a point process

and the rest and use

(4.4)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\left|\operatorname{Diag}-\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)\right|\right]\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\left|\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)-\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)\right|\right]\\
+\frac{4}{L^{2}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\sum_{x}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3}\frac{|H_{L}(k_{1}x)H_{L}(k_{2}x)|}{k_{1}k_{2}}|U_{T}\left(k_{1}x,k_{2}x\right)|\right].

4.2.1.
The sum
k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3

We set

D(k_{1},k_{2}):=\frac{4}{L^{2}}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\sum_{x}\frac{|H_{L}(k_{1}x)H_{L}(k_{2}x)|}{k_{1}k_{2}}|U_{T}\left(k_{1}x,k_{2}x\right)|\right]

and want to show that

\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3}D(k_{1},k_{2})\ll\frac{\log L}{L^{2}}.

We apply Campbell’s formula ((
3.4
) with
m=1
)

(4.5)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\sum_{x}h(x)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)d\nu_{MP}(x)

with
h(x)=\frac{|H_{L}(k_{1}x)H_{L}(k_{2}x)|}{k_{1}k_{2}}|U_{T}\left(k_{1}x,k_{2}x\right)|

to find

\begin{split}D(k_{1},k_{2})&=\frac{4}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|H_{L}(k_{1}x)H_{L}(k_{2}x)|}{k_{1}k_{2}}|U_{T}\left(k_{1}x,k_{2}x\right)|d\nu_{MP}(x)\\
&\ll\int_{0}^{C_{0}L}\frac{\sinh^{2}(Lx/2)}{\sinh(Lk_{1}x/2)\sinh(Lk_{2}x/2)}xdx\end{split}

on using boundedness of
U_{T}
and
\widehat{f}
being supported in
[-C_{0},C_{0}]
. It is easy to show
(see the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 5.2]
)
that this last integral is bounded by

\ll\min\left(\frac{1}{L^{2}(k_{1}+k_{2}-2)^{2}},\frac{1}{k_{1}k_{2}^{3}}\right)

(assuming
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
) and from this to show that

\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\geq 3}D(k_{1},k_{2})\ll\frac{\log L}{L^{2}}

which is our claim.

4.2.2.
The term
k_{1}=k_{2}=1

We are left with showing
that the first term on the RHS of (
4.4
) tends to zero,
and using Cauchy-Schwartz it suffices to bound the second moment

\lim_{L\to\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\left|\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)-\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)\right|^{2}\right]=0

for
T\geq 1
. Squaring out, we want to show

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\left(\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)\right)^{2}\right]\\
-2\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)\right]\to(\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f))^{2}.

We have

(4.6)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)\right]=\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)+O\left(\frac{1}{TL}\right).

Indeed, by Campbell’s formula (
4.5
),

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)\right]=\frac{4}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)d\nu_{MP}(x)\\
=\frac{4}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{x}{L}\right)}{\sinh^{2}(x/2)}\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\widehat{w}(2Tx)-2\widehat{w}(Tx)^{2}\right)2\frac{\sinh^{2}(x/2)}{x}dx\\
=4\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(y)^{2}ydy+4\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(y)^{2}y\left(\widehat{w}(2TLy)-2\widehat{w}(TLy)^{2}\right)dy,

using

U_{T}(x,x)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\widehat{w}(2Tx)-\widehat{w}(Tx)^{2}.

The first term is
2\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(y)^{2}|y|dy=\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)
. The second term is treated by observing that since
\operatorname{Supp}\widehat{w}\subseteq[-1,1]
, the integral is bounded by

\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(y)^{2}y\left(|\widehat{w}(2TLy)|+2|\widehat{w}(TLy)^{2}|\right)dy\ll\int_{0}^{1/2TL}\widehat{f}(y)^{2}ydy\ll\frac{1}{TL},

hence we obtain (
4.6
)

Therefore it suffices to show

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\left(\frac{4}{L^{2}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)\right)^{2}\right]\to(\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f))^{2}.

Again square out, obtain a diagonal sum and an off-diagonal sum.

We bound the diagonal sum using Campbell’s formula (
4.5
)

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\frac{16}{L^{4}}\sum_{x}H_{L}(x)^{4}U_{T}(x,x)^{2}\right]=\frac{16}{L^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{L}(x)^{4}U_{T}(x,x)^{2}d\nu_{MP}(x)\\
=\frac{16}{L^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{4}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{x}{L}\right)^{4}}{\sinh^{4}(x/2)}\frac{2\sinh^{2}(x/2)}{x}U_{T}(x,x)^{2}dx\\
\ll\frac{1}{L^{4}}\int_{0}^{cL}\frac{x^{3}}{\sinh^{2}(x/2)}dx\ll\frac{1}{L^{4}}.

We evaluate the off-diagonal term using the correlation formula (
3.4
) with
m=2
for the Poisson process

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}_{\operatorname{Pois}}\left[\frac{16}{L^{4}}\sum_{x\neq y}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)H_{L}(y)^{2}U_{T}(y,y)2\right]\\
=\frac{16}{L^{4}}\iint H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)H_{L}(y)^{2}U_{T}(y,y)d\nu_{MP}(x)d\nu_{MP}(y)\\
=\left(\frac{4}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}H_{L}(x)^{2}U_{T}(x,x)d\nu_{MP}(x)\right)^{2}=\left(\Sigma^{2}_{\rm GOE}(f)\right)^{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{TL}\right)

by (
4.6
), so we are done.

5.
Almost sure GOE fluctuations: Concluding the proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

Let
\epsilon>0
be given, and let us consider for a moment the random variables
\mathcal{V}_{T,L}=\mathcal{V}_{T,L}(X)
and
\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}
for sufficiently large, but fixed
T,L
within the allowed range
L=o(\log{T})
. It would follow that, as
g\rightarrow\infty
,

(5.1)

\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon\right),

provided that
\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\pm\epsilon
are not atoms of the distribution of
\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}
. Otherwise, apply (
5.1
) with

\epsilon/2<\epsilon^{\prime}=\epsilon^{\prime}(T,L)<\epsilon

so that, for the given
T,L
, the distribution of
\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}
does not have an atom at
\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\pm\epsilon^{\prime}
, and use that, for sufficiently large admissible
T,L
, the probability of

\left\{\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right\}

is arbitrarily small, by (
2.9
), a corollary from Theorem
2.3
.
Finally, the double limit
statement (
1.4
) follows, as

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon\right)\leq\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\\
&=\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\\
&+\left(\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\right)\\
&\leq\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\frac{\epsilon}{2}\right)\\
&+\left(\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)-\operatorname{Prob}\left(\left|\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}-\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\right|>\epsilon^{\prime}\right)\right),\end{split}

with the first term small by (
2.9
), and the difference tends to zero as
g\to\infty
since
\Sigma_{GOE}^{2}(f)\pm\epsilon^{\prime}
is not an atom of
\mathcal{V}_{T,L}^{\infty}
.
∎

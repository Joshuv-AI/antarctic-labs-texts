Types, transversals and definable compactness in o-minimal structures
Pablo Andújar Guerrero

Abstract
Through careful analysis of types inspired by [ AGTW21 ] we characterize a notion of definable compactness for definable topologies in general o-minimal structures, generalizing results from [ PP07 ] about closed and bounded definable sets in o-minimal expansions of ordered groups. Along the way we prove a parameter version for o-minimal theories of the connection between dividing and definable types known in the more general dp-minimal context [ SS14 ] , through an elementary proof that avoids the use of existing forking and VC literature. In particular we show that, if an A A -definable family of sets has the ( p , q ) (p,q) -property, for some p ≥ q p\geq q with q q large enough, then the family admits a partition into finitely many subfamilies, each of which extends to an A A -definable type.

Introduction

We study the relationship between (complete) types, intersection properties, and the topological notion of definable compactness in general o-minimal structures.

In

They derived this from the work of Dolich
[
Dol04
]
on forking in o-minimal theories --in particular Dolich’s proof that, in an o-minimal expansion of a field

In the present paper we study types and intersecting definable families of sets in connection with a notion of definable compactness that applies to definable topological spaces. A definable topological space in a structure
\mathcal{M}
is a topological space
(X,\tau)
, with
X\subseteq M^{n}
, such that there exists a basis for
\tau
that is (uniformly) definable. The study of tame definable topological spaces is connected to the development of o-minimality and dates back to Pillay
[
Pil87
]
. A prominent example in o-minimal structures, beyond the canonical o-minimal euclidean topology, is given by definable groups
[
Pil88
]
, which admit a definable manifold structure in the sense of van den Dries
[
vdD98
, Chapter 10]
making them topological groups. Further examples within o-minimality are given by definable families of functions with definable norms
[
Tho12
]
, definable metric spaces
[
Wal15
]
[
vdD05
]
, and some classical non-metrizable spaces in topology
[

, Appendix A]
. The first notion of definable compactness in o-minimal structures was introduced for definable manifold spaces, albeit the definition generalizes to any o-minimal definable topology, by Peterzil and Steinhorn
[
PS99
]
, and proved to be equivalent in the euclidean topology to being closed and bounded. This is the property that every definable curve converges, which we refer to in this paper as
curve-compactness
. A more recent approach with wider applicability (including outside linearly ordered structures) to the notion, and the one we use in this paper, is as follows: every downward directed definable family of nonempty closed sets has nonempty intersection (a family is downward directed if for any two sets in the family there is a third one that is a subset of their intersection). This property has been studied in recent years in a general model theoretic context by Fornasiero
[
For
]
. In the context of definable topologies in o-minimal structures, it has been approached by Johnson
[
Joh18
]
, and by Thomas, Walsberg and the author
[
AGTW21
]
. Johnson
[
Joh18
]
noted that, within o-minimality, it is equivalent to curve-compactness for euclidean sets. In
[
AGTW21
]
, we proved this equivalence for all definable topologies in o-minimal expansions of ordered groups. Additionally we proved, in the case of an o-minimal expansion of an ordered field, that definable compactness is equivalent to
type-compactness:
for every definable type that inhabits the topological space there is a point contained in every closed set in the type. From this we derived the equivalence with classical topological compactness in o-minimal expansions of the field of reals.

We seek to characterize definable compactness in the general o-minimal setting, in particular in terms of intersecting definable families of closed sets, and also by showing the equivalence of the various compactness notions. Our study of o-minimal types allows us to set many of our questions and results outside a topological context, and later apply these to reach the desired characterization (similarly to how the work from Dolich
[
Dol04
]
on forking was used in
[
PP07
]
). From this perspective we improve, within o-minimality, all the results mentioned above, in a number of ways. Our focus throughout is on definable families of sets, and our proofs are mostly elementary, in that they rely solely on o-minimal cell decomposition.

A pair

Let

2
Types

This section contains results about types in o-minimal structures that will be used later on. Our main result is Theorem
2.12
, which shows that
types with a uniform basis
are dense in a rather strong sense among all types. The content of this section was motivated by results on types and downward directed families of sets in o-minimal expansions of ordered groups and fields in
[
AGTW21
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A family of sets
\mathcal{S}
is
downward directed
if, for every pair
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
, there exists
S^{\prime\prime}
such that
S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq S\cap S^{\prime}
. For convenience we also ask that
\mathcal{S}
does not contain the empty set. Equivalently
\mathcal{S}
is a
filter basis
.

We are interested in the interface between types and uniform downward directed families of sets.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p
be an
n
-type. A
type basis
for
p
is a partial type
q\subseteq p
that is downward directed and generates
p
, i.e.
p=\{X\in Def(M^{n}):Y\subseteq X\text{ for some }Y\in q\}
.

A
uniform type basis
for
p
is a type basis given by a uniform family of sets, e.g.
q=\{\varphi(u,M^{n}):u\in\Omega\}
.

In general, we only consider type bases that are uniform. In particular we refer to bases as “definable” in the sense of “uniform definability” (not just “type definability”). Note that a type with a uniform basis is
A
-definable if and only if it has an
A
-definable basis.

In o-minimal and weakly o-minimal structures every
1
-type
p
has a uniform basis. When
p
is not realized this basis is given by all the open intervals in
p
. A natural question to ask is whether all types have a uniform basis. We observe that this already fails among definable types in any o-minimal expansion of
(\mathbb{R},+,<)
.

The dimension of a type is the lowest dimension of a set in it. In
[
AGTW21
, Corollary 32]
we proved the following two statements. If
\mathcal{M}
expands an ordered group, then every definable type with a uniform basis is of dimension at most
2
. And if
\mathcal{M}
expands an ordered field then one such type is of dimension at most
1
. The Marker-Steinhorn theorem (Theorem
A.1
) implies that every type in an o-minimal expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<)
is definable. So, in any o-minimal expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
or
(\mathbb{R},<,+,\cdot)
, any type of sufficiently large dimension lacks a uniform basis. Finally recall that, for any
n
, any expansion of the partial type
\{M^{n}\setminus X:X\in Def(M^{n}),\,\dim X<n\}
is
n
-dimensional.

Nevertheless, we end this section by arguing, using the next two lemmas, that types with
a uniform basis are dense among all types in the context of a topology strictly stronger than the usual Stone type topology, and an analogous density result
among definable types.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S},\,\mathcal{F}\subseteq Def(M^{n})
be consistent families of sets. We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
finer than
\mathcal{F}
if, for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, there is
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that
S\subseteq F
. That is, the filter generated by
\mathcal{S}
is finer than the one generated by
\mathcal{F}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq Def(M^{n})
be downward directed and
\mathcal{X}\subseteq Def(M^{n})
. We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
complete for
\mathcal{X}
if, for every
X\in\mathcal{X}
, there is
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that either
S\subseteq X
or
S\cap X=\emptyset
. We say that
\mathcal{S}
is
complete
if it is complete for

. In other words, if
\mathcal{S}
is a type basis for some type.

1\leq i\leq n
, the family
\mathcal{S}_{i}
extends to an
A
-definable type.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq Def(M^{m})
be an
A
-definable family of sets having a finite tame transversal of size

2.10
there exists
n

\varphi_{1}(x,y_{1},a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{n}(x,y_{n},a_{n})

By Proposition

\langle\mathbf{b_{1}},\ldots,\mathbf{b_{n}}\rangle
a realization of

-definable. By Proposition

, let

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M^{k})
be a definable family of nonempty sets. Let
n>\dim\cup\mathcal{S}
. The following are equivalent.

(1)

There exists some
m\geq n
such that
\mathcal{S}
has the
(m,n)
-property.

(2)

\mathcal{S}
has a finite tame transversal.

We precede the proof of Theorem
3.5
with two lemmas and a short proposition. First however we must introduce some notation on cuts.

Given a definable totally preordered set
(X,\preccurlyeq)
, a cut
(P,Q)
of
(X,\preccurlyeq)
is a partition of
X
into two sets
P
and
Q
such that
x\prec y
for every
x\in P
and
y\in Q
. Such a cut
(P,Q)
is definable if
P
(equivalently
Q
) is definable. All the cuts we consider satisfy that either
P
does not have a maximum or
Q
does not have a minimum with respect to
\preccurlyeq
. That is, for every
x\in P
and
y\in Q
the interval
(x,y)_{\preccurlyeq}
is nonempty. Consequently we may always associate to a cut
(P,Q)
the partial type
\{(x,y)_{\preccurlyeq}:x\in P,y\in Q\}
, and by means of this association we often refer to cuts as types. In particular we say that
z\in X(\mathcal{U})
realizes
(P,Q)
if
(x,y)_{\preccurlyeq}\in\operatorname{tp}(z/M)
for every
x\in P
and
y\in Q
.

Without context a cut is a cut in
(M,\leq)
. In this setting non-definable and definable cuts are denoted, respectively, irrational and rational cuts in
[
PS87
]
, and simply cuts and noncuts in other sources such as
[
MS94
]
and
[
Dol04
]
.
By o-minimality, any non-isolated
1
-type over
M
is uniquely characterised by the unique cut it realizes in
(M,\leq)
.

It is worth noting that, in the statement of the Lemma
3.6
below, the existence of the uniform bound
l
is redundant, since it follows from o-minimality.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(X,\preccurlyeq)
,
X\subseteq M^{k}
, be a definable totally preordered set, and
l<\omega
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a definable family of nonempty subsets of
X
, all of which are finite union of at most
l
intervals with respect to
\preccurlyeq
(including degenerate intervals of the form
[x,x]_{\preccurlyeq}
) with endpoints in
X\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
(where
-\infty
and
+\infty
have the natural interpretation with respect to
\preccurlyeq
). Then exactly one holds:

(1)

\mathcal{S}
has a finite tame transversal.

(2)

There exists
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
an infinite subfamily (not necessarily definable) of pairwise disjoint sets.

Lemma
3.6
stands on its own right as a powerful observation regarding all definable total preorders in an o-minimal structure. Note that, by letting
(X,\preccurlyeq)=(M,\leq)
, it implies a strengthening of the (base) case
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M)
of Theorem
3.5
, since it shows that, if
\mathcal{S}
is a definable family of nonempty sets with the
(\omega,2)
-property, then it has a finite tame transversal. We do not know whether or not this strengthening holds in higher dimensions (see Question
3.17
).

The fact that Lemma
3.6
applies to any definable totally preordered set
(X,\preccurlyeq)
, and in particular to preorders like those described in Subsection
1.3
, will be useful later on in constructing an inductive proof in Lemma
3.11
.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Clearly (
1
) and (
2
) are mutually exclusive. We assume the negation of (
1
) and prove (
2
). Throughout the proof let interval refer to an interval in
(X,\preccurlyeq)
with endpoints in
X\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
.

Hence let us assume that
\mathcal{S}
does not admit a finite tame transversal. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\varphi(u,M^{k}):u\in\Omega\}
. Then by first order logic compactness there exists
c\in\Omega(\mathcal{U})
,
\mathbb{S}=\varphi(c,U^{k})
, with the property that, for every definable
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega
, if

extends to a definable type, then

. In particular this holds for
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega_{x}=\{u\in\Omega:\mathcal{M}\models\varphi(u,x)\}
for every

, and so
\mathbb{S}\cap X=\emptyset
. Moreover note that, if
\sim
denotes the equivalence relation induced by the preorder
\preccurlyeq
on

, then by definition every
S\in\mathcal{S}
is compatible with the equivalence classes of

, i.e. for every

if

and

then

too. This must also be satisfied by
\mathbb{S}
with respect to the interpretation of
\preccurlyeq
on
X(\mathcal{U})
(which we also denote
\preccurlyeq
). So each equivalence class in

which includes points from
X
must be disjoint from
\mathbb{S}
. Hence each point
x\in\mathbb{S}
induces a cut
(P,Q)
in

by

and

.

Let
(P,Q)
and
(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
be different cuts in

, with, say,
P\subsetneq P^{\prime}
, and let

. Since

we have that

and so, if

and

are points in
\mathbb{S}
that realize

and
(P^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
respectively, then these points do not belong in the same subinterval of
\mathbb{S}
. In other words, each subinterval of
\mathbb{S}
contains only realizations from a single cut in
(X,\preccurlyeq)
. Since every set in
\mathbb{S}
is union of at most
l
intervals, there are at most
l
many distinct cuts
(P_{i},Q_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq m
, such that every point in
\mathbb{S}
realizes one of these cuts.

For simplicity we assume that that all the cuts
(P_{i},Q_{i})
are such that

and

. The proof readily adapts to the other case. Let the indexing be such that
(P_{i},Q_{i})
, for
0\leq i\leq n
, are the cuts that are non-definable, and
(P_{i},Q_{i})
, for
n<i\leq m
, are definable.

For every
0\leq i\leq m
let us fix
a_{i}\in P_{i}
and
b_{i}\in Q_{i}
such that
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}\subseteq P_{j}
or
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}\subseteq Q_{j}
for every

. We show that there exists
S\in\mathcal{S}
with

such that, for every
0\leq i\leq m
, there are
a^{\prime}_{i}\in P_{i}
,

with

(in particular

). We may then apply again the result with parameters

,

in place of

and

and reach a second set

that will be disjoint from

. Repeating this process yields a countably infinite pairwise disjoint subfamily of

as desired.

Let
(P,Q)
be a cut. We say that a definable set

is disjoint from

if there exists any

and

such that

that is disjoint from every cut

,

.

We focus first on the definable cuts.

.

Let

, then

For all choice of

Finally we require a claim regarding non-definable cuts.

Claim 3.9

For any

and

,

. We define by reverse recursion a family of definable sets

(\text{I}_{i})

(\text{II}_{i})

Lemma

can be generalized to higher dimensions as follows.

Proposition 3.10

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M^{n})
be a definable family of nonempty sets. At least one of the following holds.

(1)

There exists an infinite subfamily of
\mathcal{S}
of pairwise disjoint sets.

(2)

There exists a tame transversal

.

Proof.

) and prove (

). We work by induction on

. The case

3.6
. Suppose that

. Let
\pi_{1}

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S})=\{\pi_{1}(S):S\in\mathcal{S}\}
. If
\mathcal{S}
does not contain an infinite subfamily of pairwise disjoint sets then the same holds for
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S})
. By the case
n=1
there exists a finite set
\{\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}
that is a tame transversal for
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S})
. Let
\mathcal{N}=(N,<,\ldots)
be a tame extension of
\mathcal{M}
that contains
\{\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{m}\}
. Then
\cup_{0\leq i\leq m}\{\xi_{i}\}\times N^{n-1}
is a tame tranversal for
\mathcal{S}
of dimension
n-1
.
∎

In order to prove Theorem
3.5
we first show that any consistent definable family has a finite tame transversal. We in fact prove the following more precise statement. Recall that a family of sets
\mathcal{S}
is
n
-consistent if any subfamily of at most
n
sets has nonempty intersection.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M^{n})
be a definable family of sets. If
\mathcal{S}
is
2^{n}
-consistent then it has a finite tame transversal.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The case
n=1
is given by Lemma
3.6
. Suppose that
n>1
. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{\varphi(u,M^{n}):u\in\Omega\}
.

Consider the family
\{\pi(S\cap S^{\prime}):S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}\}
. This family is definable and
2^{n-1}
-consistent. Hence by induction hypothesis there exists a finite collection of definable
(n-1)
-types
p_{0},\ldots,p_{m}
such that, for every
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
,
\pi(S\cap S^{\prime})
belongs in one of them. We construct a finite tame transversal for
\mathcal{S}
given by types whose projection is one of
p_{0},\ldots p_{m}
. The approach is to use cell decomposition to witness the “fiber over the
p_{i}
” of each set in
\mathcal{S}
as a finite union of intervals on some definable preordered set, and then apply Lemma
3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a uniform cell decomposition of
\mathcal{S}
, i.e. a cell decomposition of
\varphi(\Omega,M^{n})
. For any
D\in\mathcal{D}
and
u\in\Omega
with
D_{u}\neq\emptyset
let
f_{D_{u}}
and
g_{D_{u}}
denote the functions such that
D_{u}=(f_{D_{u}},g_{D_{u}})
or
D_{u}=graph(f_{D_{u}})=graph(g_{D_{u}})
.
Let
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\{f_{D_{u}},g_{D_{u}}:D\in\mathcal{D},u\in\Omega,\pi(D_{u})\in p_{i}\}
. We briefly observe that this family is definable.

For every
i
and
D\in\mathcal{D}
let
\Omega(i,D)=\{u\in\Omega:\pi(D_{u})\in p_{i}\}
. By definability of
p_{i}
these sets are definable. In particular, the families
\{f_{D_{u}},g_{D_{u}}:u\in\Omega(i,D)\}
are definable. Note that
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\{f_{D_{u}},g_{D_{u}}:D\in\mathcal{D},u\in\Omega(i,D)\}
. By re-indexing
\mathcal{H}_{i}
in terms of a finite disjoint union
X_{i}
of sets
\Omega(i,D)
for
D
ranging in
\mathcal{D}
, we may take
\mathcal{H}_{i}
to be a definable family
\{h_{x}:x\in X_{i}\}
.

Let
\preccurlyeq_{i}
be the total preorder induced by
p_{i}
, in particular on
\mathcal{H}_{i}
, described in Subsection
1.3
. As usual we abuse notation and also let
\preccurlyeq_{i}
denote the induced definable preorder on the index set
X_{i}
. Let
(X,\preccurlyeq)
be the definable totally preordered set that extends the disjoint union of
(X_{i},\preccurlyeq_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq m
, by letting
x\prec y
for any
x\in X_{i}
,
y\in X_{j}
with
i<j
.

For every
u\in\Omega
, let
B_{u}
denote the union in
(X,\preccurlyeq)
of intervals
[y,z]_{\preccurlyeq}
where
y,z\in X_{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq m
and satisfy that there exists
D\in\mathcal{D}
with
D_{u}=(h_{y},h_{z})
or
D_{u}=graph(h_{y})=graph(h_{z})
. So every set
B_{u}
is the union of at most
(m+1)|\mathcal{D}|
closed intervals in
(X,\preccurlyeq)
. By definability of
\mathcal{S}
,
\preccurlyeq
and each
X_{i}
, the family
\mathcal{B}_{S}=\{B_{u}:u\in\Omega\}
is definable. Onwards for clarity we write
B_{S}
in place of
B_{u}
where
S=\varphi(u,M^{n})
. This is valid because
B_{u}=B_{v}
whenever
S=\varphi(u,M^{n})=\varphi(v,M^{n})
. However this is done only for clarity, since the proof can also be completed in terms of the subscript
u
.

Since every two sets
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
satisfy that
\pi(S\cap S^{\prime})\in p_{i}
for some
0\leq i\leq m
, the family
\mathcal{B}_{\mathcal{S}}
is
2
-consistent. Consequently, by Lemma
3.6
, there exists finitely many definable types
q_{0},\ldots,q_{l}
such that, for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
, the set
B_{S}
belongs in one of them. We complete the proof by fixing an arbitrary
0\leq j\leq l
and showing that the subfamily
\mathcal{S}_{j}=\{S\in\mathcal{S}:B_{S}\in q_{j}\}
admits a finite tame transversal. We will use the construction of types appearing in Definition
2.15
.

Let
i
be such that
X_{i}\in q_{j}
. We define
Q=\{x\in X_{i}:(-\infty,x]_{\preccurlyeq}\in q_{j}\}
. Note that, by definability of
q_{j}
, this set is definable. We complete the proof by considering two cases. First we assume that
Q
has a minimum
\hat{x}
with respect to
\preccurlyeq
, and show that the family
\mathcal{S}_{j}
has a tame transversal of size at most three, given by the types
h_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}
,
h^{+}_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}
and
h^{-}_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}
. Then we assume that
Q
does not have a minimum, and show that
\mathcal{S}_{j}
extends to a definable type (i.e. it has a tame transversal of size one).
We make use of the following short claim.

Claim 3.12
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{j}
. Then there exists
y,z\in X_{i}
with
[y,z]_{\preccurlyeq}\in q_{i}
, where
h_{y}
and
h_{z}
define a cell inside
S
, i.e.
dom(h_{y})=dom(h_{z})
and
(h_{y},h_{z})\subseteq S
or
graph(h_{y})=graph(h_{z})\subseteq S
. Moreover
y\preccurlyeq Q
and
z\in Q
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{S}_{j}
. Recall that, definition of
\mathcal{S}_{j}
, we have that
B_{S}\in q_{j}
. By definition of
B_{S}
there must exists
y,z\in X_{i}
with
[y,z]_{\preccurlyeq}\in q_{i}
such that
h_{y}
and
h_{z}
define a cell inside
S
. Clearly
z\in Q
. Moreover, for every
x\in Q
, we have that
[y,z]_{\preccurlyeq}\cap(-\infty,x]_{\preccurlyeq}\in q_{i}
, and so
y\preccurlyeq x
. Hence
y\preccurlyeq Q
. This completes the prove of the claim. We proceed with the proof by cases.

Case
1
:
The set
Q
has a minimum
\hat{x}
. Let
y,z\in X_{i}
be as in Claim
3.12
. Note that
y\preccurlyeq\hat{x}\preccurlyeq z
. By definition of
\preccurlyeq
we have that

\{a:h_{y}(a)\leq h_{\hat{x}}(a)\leq h_{z}(a)\}\in p_{i}.

It follows that at least one of the following three sets must belong in
p_{i}

\displaystyle\{a:h_{y}(a)=h_{\hat{x}}(a)=h_{z}(a)\}

\displaystyle\{a:h_{y}(a)\leq h_{\hat{x}}(a)<h_{z}(a)\}

\displaystyle\{a:h_{y}(a)<h_{\hat{x}}(a)\leq h_{z}(a)\}.

Since
h_{y}
and
h_{z}
define a cell inside
S
, we conclude that
S\in h_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}\cup h^{+}_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}\cup h^{-}_{\hat{x}}|_{p_{i}}
.

Case
2
:

Q
does not have a minimum. Let
q
be the type of all sets
S\in Def(M^{n})
such that
S\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}
for all
x
in some subset of
Q
that is unbounded from below in
Q
with respect to
\preccurlyeq
. We show that this type is consistent and definable, and that
\mathcal{S}_{j}\subseteq q
. This follows the arguments in Case
2
in the proof of Proposition
2.17
, and so we are concise in the presentation.

To prove that
\mathcal{S}_{j}\subseteq q
let us fix
S\in\mathcal{S}_{j}
. Let
y,z\in X_{i}
be as in Claim
3.12
. Since
Q
does not have a minimum we have that
y\prec Q
and moreover there exists
x\in Q
with
x\prec z
. For every
x\in Q
with
x\prec z
we have that
y\prec x\prec z
. By definition of
\preccurlyeq
, from the inequality
y\prec x\prec z
it follows that
S\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}
. We conclude that
\mathcal{S}_{j}\subseteq q
.

Note that, by definability of
p_{i}
,
\mathcal{H}_{i}
and
Q
, the type
q
is definable. It remains to show consistency of
q
. By cell decomposition it suffices to do it for cells.
Let
C
be a cell in
q
. Since
Q
does not have a minimum there must exist
x,x^{\prime}\in Q
with
x\prec x^{\prime}
such that
C\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}\cap h_{x^{\prime}}|_{p_{i}}
. But then we have that

\{a:h_{x}(a)<h_{x^{\prime}}(a),\,\langle a,h_{x}(a)\rangle\in C,\,\langle a,h_{x^{\prime}}(a)\rangle\in C\}\in p_{i}

In particular we derive that
C
must be a cell of the form
(f,g)
for two functions
f
and
g
with domain in
p_{i}
. Moreover observe that, for any
x\in Q
, it holds that
C\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}
if and only if

\{a:\langle a,h_{x}(a)\rangle\in C\}=\{a:f(a)<h_{x}(a)<g(a)\}\in p_{i}.

In other words,
C\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}
if and only if
f\prec_{i}h_{x}\prec_{i}g
. Now let
C_{1}=(f_{1},g_{1}),C_{2}=(f_{2},g_{2})
be two cells in
q
. For any
k\in\{1,2\}
observe that, since
C_{k}\in q
, then it must be that
f_{k}\prec_{i}\{h_{x}:x\in Q\}
and
h_{x_{k}}\prec_{i}g_{k}
for some
x_{k}\in Q
. Let
x^{\prime}=\min_{\preccurlyeq}\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
.
For every
x\in Q
with
x\preccurlyeq x^{\prime}
and any
k\in\{1,2\}
we have that
f_{k}\prec_{i}h_{x}\prec_{i}g_{k}
, and so
C_{k}\in h_{x}|_{p_{i}}
. It follows that
C_{1}\cap C_{2}\in q
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We may now prove Theorem
3.5
. Recall that a family of sets is
n
-inconsistent if every subfamily of size
n
has empty intersection. Recall also that, for a definable set
X
, we denote its boundary (in the o-minimal euclidean topology) by
bd(X)
. If
X
is contained in a set
Y
then let
bd_{Y}(X)=bd(X)\cap Y
.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

To prove that (
2
) implies (
1
) note that, if
\mathcal{S}
admits a tame transversal of cardinality
l
, then it has a covering of
l
consistent subfamilies (those given by the sets containing a given element in the transversal). It follows that, for every
n>0
,
\mathcal{S}
has the
(nl+1,n+1)
-property. We assume the negation of (
2
) and derive the negation of (
1
). Note that, if
\mathcal{S}
does not have a finite tame transversal, then
\mathcal{S}
must be infinite (otherwise it suffices to take a point from every set in
\mathcal{S}
to build a finite transversal in
M^{k}
). It follows that we may assume throughout that
\dim\cup\mathcal{S}\geq 1
, and in particular that
n>1
. Observe that the negation of (
1
) is the statement that, for every
m\geq n
, there exists a subfamily of
\mathcal{S}
of cardinality
m
that is
n
-inconsistent. Instead of this we will show the following apparently stronger statement.

Let
X_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{n}
be a collection of nested definable subsets of
M^{k}
satisfying that
\dim X_{i}<i
for every
1\leq i<n
and
\cup\mathcal{S}\subseteq X_{n}
. For every
m\geq n
, there exists
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
with
|\mathcal{F}|=m
such that, for every
1\leq i\leq n
and subfamily
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
with
|\mathcal{F}^{\prime}|=i
, it holds that
\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap X_{i}=\emptyset
. That is, for every
1\leq i\leq n
, the family
\mathcal{F}\cap X_{i}
is
i
-inconsistent. In particular, since
\cup\mathcal{S}\subseteq X_{n}
, the family
\mathcal{F}
is
n
-inconsistent.

Let
Prop(m,n)
denote the statement that the above holds for some fixed
m\geq n>1
and any given definable family
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M^{k})
of nonempty sets with
n>\dim\cup\mathcal{S}
and without a finite tame transversal. We prove
Prop(m,n)
by induction. We let
Prop(2,2)
be the base case. Suppose that
m>2
. If
m>n
then we use
Prop(m-1,n)
to derive
Prop(m,n)
. Otherwise we use
Prop(m-1,n-1)
to derive
Prop(m,n)
. We prove all cases simultaneously. We divide the argument into two parts.

Let us fix
m\geq n>1
and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M^{k})
a definable family of nonempty sets with
n>\dim\cup\mathcal{S}
that does not admit a finite tame transversal; and
X_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X_{n}
a family of definable nested subsets of
M^{k}
with
\dim X_{i}<i
for
1\leq i<n
and
\cup\mathcal{S}\subseteq X_{n}
. The first part of the argument consists of the following claim.

Claim 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be a finite subfamily and, for every
1\leq i<n
, let
Y_{i}=\cup_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}bd_{X_{i+1}}(S\cap X_{i+1})
. There exists a subfamily
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
of cardinality
m-1
that is
n
-inconsistent and such that
\mathcal{F}\cap(X_{i}\cup Y_{i})
is
i
-inconsistent for
1\leq i<n
.

Note that, by o-minimality,
\dim Y_{i}<i
for every
i
. So
\dim(X_{i}\cup Y_{i})<i
for every
1\leq i<n
. Note that, if
m=n
, then any subfamily
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
of cardinality
m-1=n-1
is
n
-inconsistent vacuously. It follows that, if
m=n=2
, the claim follows easily from the fact that
\mathcal{S}
does not admit a finite transversal in
M^{k}
.

Suppose that
m>2
. If
m>n
then the claim is given by
Prop(m-1,n)
applied to
\mathcal{S}
and the collection of nested sets
\{X_{1}\cup Y_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\cup Y_{n-1},X_{n}\}
. Suppose that
m=n>2
. We may apply
Prop(m-1,n-1)
to the definable family
\mathcal{S}\cap(X_{n-1}\cup Y_{n-1})
and collection of nested sets
\{X_{1}\cup Y_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\cup Y_{n-1}\}
. It follows that there exists
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
with cardinality
m-1=n-1
such that
\mathcal{F}\cap(X_{n-1}\cup Y_{n-1})\cap(X_{i}\cup Y_{i})=\mathcal{F}\cap(X_{i}\cup Y_{i})
is
i
-inconsistent for
1\leq i<n
. Since
|\mathcal{F}|=n-1
the family
\mathcal{F}
is
n
-inconsistent vacuously. This completes the proof of the claim.

We continue with the second part of the proof. For any
S\in\mathcal{S}
consider the family of all ordered tuples
\langle S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\rangle\in\prod_{1\leq i<m}\mathcal{S}
such that
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}
is
n
-inconsistent and
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}\cap(X_{i}\cup bd_{X_{i+1}}(S\cap X_{i+1}))
is
i
-inconsistent for every
1\leq i<n
. We denote this family
\mathcal{S}^{m}(S)
.
Note that, by Claim
3.13
, the family
\{\mathcal{S}^{m}(S):S\in\mathcal{S}\}
is consistent.

We now explicitly identify the family
\{\mathcal{S}^{m}(S):S\in\mathcal{S}\}
with a family of definable sets as follows. Let
\varphi(u,v)
be such that
\mathcal{S}=\{\varphi(u,M^{k}):u\in\Omega\}
. For any
u\in\Omega
,
S=\varphi(u,M^{k})
, let
\Omega^{m}(u)
denote the set of all index tuples
\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{m-1}\rangle
such that
\langle\varphi(u_{1},M^{k}),\ldots,\varphi(u_{m-1},M^{k})\rangle\in\mathcal{S}^{m}(S)
. Clearly the family
\{\Omega^{m}(u):u\in\Omega\}
is definable. Like
\{\mathcal{S}^{m}(S):S\in\mathcal{S}\}
, it is consistent.

By Lemma
3.11
it follows that
\{\Omega^{m}(u):u\in\Omega\}
admits a finite tame transversal. That is, we may partition
\Omega
into finitely many definable subfamilies
\Omega_{1},\ldots,\Omega_{s}
satisfying that, for each
1\leq i\leq s
, the family
\{\Omega^{m}(u):u\in\Omega_{i}\}
extends to a definable type. Note that, since by assumption
\mathcal{S}
does not have a finite tame transversal, there must be some
1\leq i\leq s
such that
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{\varphi(u,M^{k}):u\in\Omega_{i}\}
does not have a finite tame transversal either. Hence, by passing if necessary to a subfamily
\mathcal{S}_{i}
of
\mathcal{S}
, we may assume that
\{\Omega^{m}(u):u\in\Omega\}
extends to a definable type
p
.

Let
\mathcal{N}=(N,\ldots)
be a tame extension of
\mathcal{M}
that realizes
p
. Such an extension exists by one direction of the Marker-Steinhorn theorem
[
MS94
, Theorem 2.1]
. Onwards let
S^{*}=S(\mathcal{N})
for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
and
X_{i}^{*}=X_{i}(\mathcal{N})
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. That is, we use an asterisk to denote the interpretation in
\mathcal{N}
of a definable set in
\mathcal{M}
. Let
c=\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{m-1}\rangle
be a realization in
\mathcal{N}
of
p
and, for
1\leq i<m
, let
S_{i}=\varphi(c_{i},N^{k})
. Then we have that
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}
is
n
-inconsistent and
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}\cap(X^{*}_{i}\cup bd_{X^{*}_{i+1}}(S^{*}\cap X^{*}_{i+1}))
is
i
-inconsistent for every
S\in\mathcal{S}
and
1\leq i<n
. We prove that there exists
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that the family
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1},S^{*}\}
witnesses
Prop(m,n)
in
\mathcal{N}
, by satisfying that
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1},S^{*}\}\cap X^{*}_{i}
is
i
-inconsistent for every
1\leq i\leq n
. Since
\mathcal{N}
is an elementary extension of
\mathcal{M}
, then an analogous family witnessing
Prop(m,n)
in
\mathcal{M}
must exist too.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a cell decomposition of
N^{k}
compatible with each intersection of sets in
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1},X^{*}_{1},\ldots,X^{*}_{n}\}
. For each cell
C\in\mathcal{C}
let us choose a point
\xi_{C}\in C
. Since
\mathcal{S}
does not have a finite tame transversal, there exists
S\in\mathcal{S}
such that
S^{*}\cap\{\xi_{C}:C\in\mathcal{C}\}=\emptyset
. Since
X_{1}
is finite (because
\dim X_{1}<1
), we also choose
S
so that
S\cap X_{1}=\emptyset
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1},S^{*}\}
. We show that
\mathcal{F}\cap X^{*}_{i}
is
i
-inconsistent for every
1\leq i\leq n
.

Towards a contradiction suppose that there exists some
1\leq i\leq n
and a subset
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
of cardinality
i
such that
\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap X^{*}_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Note that, by construction of
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}
, it must be that
S^{*}\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
. If
i=1
then
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=\{S^{*}\}
and
S^{*}\cap X^{*}_{1}\neq\emptyset
contradicts that
S\cap X_{1}=\emptyset
. Suppose that
i>1
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\setminus\{S^{*}\}
and let us fix a cell
C\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C\cap(\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\cap X^{*}_{i}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\mathcal{C}
is compatible with
\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\cap X^{*}_{i}
it must be that
C\subseteq\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}\cap X^{*}_{i}
. Moreover recall that, by construction of
\{S_{1},\ldots,S_{m-1}\}
, the intersection
\cap\mathcal{F}^{\prime\prime}
(and in particular
C
) is disjoint from
bd_{X^{*}_{i}}(S^{*}\cap X^{*}_{i})
. By definable connectedness from
C\cap S^{*}\neq\emptyset
it follows that
C\subseteq S^{*}
. This however contradicts that
\xi_{C}\notin S^{*}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Note that, if we add to the statement of Theorem
3.5
the condition that all the sets in
\mathcal{S}
are finite, then the transversal can always be assumed to be in
M^{k}
.

With the use of the Alon-Kleitman-Matoušek
(p,q)
-theorem (Theorem
1.2
), Lemma
3.11
can be used to prove a different version of Theorem
3.5
, where the
n
in the statement is substituted by any interger greater than the
VC
-codensity of
\mathcal{S}
.

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a definable family of sets with the
(m,n)
-property, where
m\geq n
and
n
is greater than the
VC
-codensity of
\mathcal{S}
. Then
\mathcal{S}
admits a finite tame transversal.

Proof.

For simplicity we assume that
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(M)
, the proof otherwise being analogous. Applying Theorem
1.2
, there exists a natural number
l>0
such that every finite
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
has a transversal of cardinality at most
l
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=\{F(S)\subseteq M^{l}:S\in\mathcal{S}\}
be the definable family of sets given by
F(S)=(S\times M^{l-1})\cup(M\times S\times M^{l-2})\cup\cdots\cup(M^{l-1}\times S)
. We observe that
\mathcal{F}

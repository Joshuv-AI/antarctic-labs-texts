Dynamical torsion gravity backgrounds
Ph. Spindel

Abstract
We write the field equations of torsion gravity theories and the Noether identity they obey directly in terms of metric and contorsion tensor components expressed with respect to natural coordinates, i.e. without using vierbien but Lagrange multipliers. Then we obtain explicit solutions of these equations, under specific ansätze for the contorsion field, by assuming the metric to be respectively of the Bertotti-Robinson, pp-wave, Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker or static spherically symmetric type. Among these various solutions we obtain some of them have their contorsion tensor depending on arbitrary functions that did not influence their geometry. This raises questions about the predictability of the theory.

Introduction

Recently there has been renewed attention to modified gravity theories. The main motivations
for these endeavors are on the one hand purely theoretical (the quest of an unification of all forms
of interactions and matter and of a deeper understanding of the peculiarities of Einstein gravity
theory) and on the other hand dictated by the desire to offer alternative explanations for the recent cosmological
observations that have led to the introduction of hypothetical dark matter and energy. There are in fact several versions

Of the many these theories, torsion gravity looks particularly interesting. It is a geometrical theory, based on a dynamical metric and a dynamical independent metric preserving connection, thus generalising general relativity. In their simplest expression they are obtained by adding to the metric and connection scalar curvature terms quadratic in the torsion and curvature tensor that for appropriate values of the coupling constants of these extra-terms provides physically sane models i.e. without ghost and tachyon ( see Refs

Compared to the usual Einstein-Hilbert gravity, torsion gravity theories extend the usual gravity
framework by modifying the infrared sector of the theory via the introduction, in addition to the massless
gauge spin 2 excitations, of positive and negative parity massive spin 2, 1 and zero modes. Such a theory was first
considered by Cartan

As a consequence the torsion degrees of freedom did not propagate but were determined by an
algebraic equation coupling them to the matter spin content. Accordingly the torsion variables
could be eliminated by the introduction of spin-spin matter interactions.

To obtain a propagating torsion, we have to introduce non-linear terms in the torsion tensor.
Of course this open the Pandora’s box of ghosts and tachyonic modes. Remarkably, in

without ghosts and tachyons once expanded around the empty space configuration) that describe in addition to the usual massless spin 2 graviton,
massive spin 2, spin 1 and spin 0 excitations. The main difference between these two classes is the parity of their fluctuation field :

Usually these theories are formulated in terms of a coframe (vierbein) and the curvature tensor
built on a metric preserving affine connection. It is the latter that introduces the torsion. This

triangular hierarchy of the field equations. Nevertheless at Grassmann degree zero the fermionic

2
Torsion gravity field equations

2.1
Metric & connection formalism

The models are expressed in terms of two sets of variables. The metric components
g^{\mu\nu}
and the affine connection components
A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}
, defining a covariant derivative, denoted by
{\overline{\nabla}\!}
, that is assumed to be metric preserving :

\displaystyle{\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\mu}\,g^{\rho\sigma}:=\partial_{\mu}\,g^{\rho\sigma}+g^{\lambda\sigma}A^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\lambda\mu}+g^{\rho\lambda}A^{\sigma}_{{\,.\,}\lambda\mu}=0\qquad.

(2.1)

In what follows we will distinguished between the Levi-Civita connection (denoted as usual, with respect to a natural basis/coordinate system by
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}
),
\nabla
denoting the covariant differential associated to it and
R^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\,\Gamma^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\nu}+\dots
its curvature tensor. The latter will be called Riemann-tensor in order to be distinguished from the curvature tensor obtained from the affine connection whose components in a natural basis reads :

\displaystyle F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\,A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\nu}+A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\mu}A^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\beta\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\,A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}+A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\nu}A^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}\qquad,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\phantom{F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu\nu}}=R^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu\nu}+{\nabla\!}_{\mu}K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\nu}-{\nabla\!}_{\nu}K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}+K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\mu}K^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\beta\nu}-K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\nu}K^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}\qquad.

(2.3)

To be complete, we recall the definitions of the contorsion and torsion tensors components in natural coordinates. The first is obtained as the difference between the affine connection and the Levi-Civita connection :

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}:=A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}\qquad.

(2.4)

The second is related to the antisymmetric part of the affine connection :

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}=A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\gamma\beta}-A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}=K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\gamma\beta}-K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}\qquad.

(2.5)

These two objects being obtained from differences of connections are tensors. The flip of the indices in the torsion with respect to those of the connection is a reminiscence of the natural formalism to discuss these objects : Cartan’s exterior differential calculus.

The Lagrangian we shall consider consists of two pieces. The usual Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian

c_{R}
:

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{E-H}:=\sqrt{-g}(c_{R}\,R-2\,\Lambda)\qquad,

(2.6)

and the “connection matter Lagrangian” :

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{F}(g^{\mu\nu},F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta})=\sqrt{-g}\,L_{F}(g^{\mu\nu},F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta})

(2.7)

that we assume only to depend on the metric (but not on its derivative) and on the curvature tensor. The introduction of a cosmological constant is disputable. One primary aim of torsion gravity models is to provide a dynamical origin of the acceleration of the Universe. Moreover we also have to remind the reader that some conditions leading to the absence of tachyons or ghosts have to be reconsidered on curved backgrounds, in particular on an anti-deSitter background

. Nevertheless the consistency of the models around these backgrounds has been established in Ref.

Thus the total Lagrangian is given by the sum
\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{E-H}+\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{F}
. The field equations are obtained by varying this Lagrangian with respect to
g^{\mu\nu}
and
A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}
, taking into account the metric preserving assumption Eq. (
2.1

This condition is implemented with the help of Lagrange multipliers :
\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}=\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}(\beta\gamma)}

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{P}=\sqrt{-g}(L_{E-H}+L_{F})+\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}{\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\alpha}g^{\beta\gamma}\qquad.

(2.8)

Let us define auxiliary quantities :

\displaystyle{\Delta}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}:=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}g_{\mu\nu}L_{F}+\left.\frac{\partial L_{F}}{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}\right|_{F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}}={\Delta}\mathstrut_{(\mu\nu)}\qquad,

(2.9)

\displaystyle Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}:=\left.\frac{\partial L_{F}}{\partial F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}}\right|_{g^{\mu\nu}}=Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta[\gamma\delta]}\qquad,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{-g}}}\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut_{F}}{\delta A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}}=({\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\delta}Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\rho\sigma}\,T_{{\,.\,}\rho\sigma}^{\gamma}-Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\rho}\,T_{\rho})

(2.11)

\displaystyle\phantom{\Delta_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2\,\sqrt{-g}}}\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut_{F}}{\delta A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}}}={\nabla\!}_{\delta}Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}-K^{\rho}_{{\,.\,}\alpha\delta}Z_{\rho}^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma\delta}+K^{\beta}_{{\,.\,}\rho\delta}Z_{\alpha}^{{\,.\,}\rho\gamma\delta}\qquad.

(2.12)

The variational derivative (taking into account the Lagrangian multipliers) are :

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{P}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}=+\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}g_{\mu\nu}\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}{\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\alpha}g^{\beta\gamma}-{\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\alpha}\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\mu\nu}+T_{\alpha}\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\mu\nu}\qquad,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut_{P}}{\delta A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}}=2\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut\mathstrut^{{\,.\,}\beta\gamma}_{\alpha}+\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut\mathstrut^{\gamma{\,.\,}\beta}_{{\,.\,}\alpha}+\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\lambda}\mathstrut\mathstrut\mathstrut^{\gamma\beta}_{{\,.\,}{\,.\,}\alpha}\qquad,

(2.14)

The Lagrange multiplier Euler equations imply the antisymmetry of the contorsion tensor
K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}
:

\displaystyle A^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}=\Gamma^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}+K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}\qquad\text{with}\qquad K_{\alpha\beta\mu}:=g_{\alpha\gamma}K^{\gamma}_{{\,.\,}\beta\mu}=K_{[\alpha\beta]\mu}

(2.15)

while the connection Euler equation fix the Lagrange multiplier expression
\lambda\mathstrut^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle\lambda^{\gamma\alpha\beta}=\Delta^{(\alpha\beta)\gamma}\qquad.

(2.16)

The remaining field equations are :

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{{\mathcal{S}}}\mathstrut_{\,\gamma}^{{\,.\,}\alpha\beta}\equiv(\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut\mathstrut_{\gamma}^{{\,.\,}\alpha\beta}-\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut\mathstrut_{{\,.\,}\gamma{\,.\,}}^{\alpha{\,.\,}\beta})=0\qquad,

(2.17)

\displaystyle(\text{i.e. }\quad\Delta^{[\alpha\beta]\gamma}={\nabla\!}_{\delta}Z^{[\alpha\beta]\gamma\delta}-K^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\delta}Z^{[\beta\rho]\gamma\delta}+K^{\beta}_{{\,.\,}\rho\delta}Z^{[\alpha\rho]\gamma\delta}=0)\qquad,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{E}}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}\equiv\sqrt{-g}{\big(}c_{R}(R_{\mu\nu}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,g_{\mu\nu}\,R)+\Lambda\,g_{\mu\nu}\big)-\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{T}}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}=0\qquad.

(2.19)

with :

\displaystyle\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{T}}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}=-\Big(\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut_{\mu\nu}+({\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\alpha}\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut^{{\,.\,}{\,.\,}\alpha}_{(\mu\nu)}-T_{\alpha}\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\Delta}\mathstrut^{{\,.\,}{\,.\,}\alpha}_{(\mu\nu)})\Big)\qquad.

(2.20)

Let us notice that on-shell :

\displaystyle{\overline{\nabla}\!}_{\rho}{\Delta}^{\alpha\beta\rho}-T_{\rho}\,{\Delta}^{\alpha\beta\rho}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\big(Z^{\alpha\rho\mu\nu}F^{\beta}_{{\,.\,}\rho\mu\nu}+Z^{\rho\beta\mu\nu}F^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\rho\mu\nu}\big)\qquad.

(2.21)

Accordingly, defining the symmetric part

\displaystyle S^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}:=Z^{(\alpha\beta)\mu\nu}\qquad,

(2.22)

we obtain the symmetric contorsion energy-momentum tensor :

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\alpha\beta}=-(\Delta_{\alpha\beta}+F_{(\alpha}^{\,{\,.\,}\mu\nu\rho}\,S_{\beta)\mu\nu\rho})\qquad.

(2.23)

This last expression shows that the Einstein equations involve only polynomials of the curvature, but no derivatives of it ( contrary to the connection equations that involve first derivatives of the curvature tensor). Thus in general the field equations will involves at most third order derivatives of the metric components and second order derivatives of the contorsion components.

2.2
Noether identities ( A reminder )

Let us briefly recall the essence of Noether identity applied to an invariant Lagrangian density
\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut
. There are two relevant such identities. We restrict ourselves to Lagrangians like those here considered, i.e. such that
\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{\mathcal{L}}\mathstrut
depends at most on the second derivative of fields
Q_{\omega}
whose variations are tensors and whose Lie derivatives involve at most the second derivative of the generator
\xi^{\alpha}
of the infinitesimal coordinate change :

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}Q_{\omega}=:\xi^{\lambda}\partial_{\lambda}Q_{\omega}+c^{\lambda_{1}}_{\omega|\mu}\partial_{\lambda_{1}}\xi^{\mu}+c^{(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})}_{\omega|\mu}\partial^{2}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\xi^{\mu}\qquad.

(2.24)

Let us define :

\displaystyle P^{\omega}:=\frac{\partial\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\partial Q_{\omega}}\qquad,\qquad P^{\omega|\alpha}:=\frac{\partial\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\partial Q_{\omega,\alpha}}\qquad,\qquad P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}:=\frac{\partial\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\partial Q_{\omega,\alpha\beta}}\qquad.

(2.25)

The fundamental Noether identity
[
42
,
43
]
reads :

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}Q_{\omega}+\partial_{\alpha}\big((P^{\omega|\alpha}-\partial_{\beta}P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}){\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}Q_{\omega}+P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\partial_{\beta}({\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}Q_{\omega})-\xi^{\alpha}\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut\big)\equiv 0\qquad.

(2.26)

Expanding it with respect to the arbitrary field
\xi^{\mu}
and its derivatives we obtain from the invariance of the Lagrangian :

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}+\partial_{\alpha}(P^{\omega|\alpha}\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}-\delta_{\mu}^{\alpha}\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut)+P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\partial^{3}_{\alpha\beta\mu}Q_{\omega}-\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}\equiv 0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\,c^{\lambda_{1}}_{\omega|\mu}+\partial_{\alpha}(P^{\omega|\alpha}\,c^{\lambda_{1}}_{\omega|\mu})+P^{\omega|{\lambda_{1}}}\,\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}-\delta^{\lambda_{1}}_{\mu}\,\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut+2\,P^{\omega|\alpha\lambda_{1}}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\mu}Q_{\omega}+P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{1}}-\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\,c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{1}}\equiv 0

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\,c^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}_{\omega|\mu}+\partial_{\alpha}(P^{\omega|\alpha}\,c^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}_{\omega|\mu})+P^{\omega|(\lambda_{1}}\,c^{\lambda_{2})}_{\omega|\mu}+2\,P^{\omega|\alpha(\lambda_{1}}\partial_{\alpha}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{2})}+P^{\omega|\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}-\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\,c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle+P^{\omega|\alpha\beta}\partial^{2}_{\alpha\beta}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\equiv 0

\displaystyle 2\,P^{\omega|\alpha(\lambda_{1}}\partial_{\alpha}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3})}+P^{\omega|(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{3})}+P^{\omega|(\lambda_{1}}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3})}\equiv 0

\displaystyle P^{\omega|(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}c_{\omega|\mu}^{\lambda_{2}\lambda_{4})}\equiv 0

Let us notice that the first identity says that
\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut
cannot depends explicitly on the coordinates. The second exhibits the link between the symmetric and the canonical energy-momentum tensors. The next ones express symmetry properties.

By combining all these identities we obtain Nœther’s famous second theorem :

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\partial_{\mu}Q_{\omega}-\partial_{\lambda_{1}}{\big(}\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\,c^{\lambda_{1}}_{\omega|\mu}\Big)+\partial^{2}_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}\Big(\frac{\delta\underaccent{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}{L}\mathstrut}{\delta Q_{\omega}}\,c^{(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})}_{\omega|\mu}\Big)\equiv 0\qquad.

(2.27)

To apply Noether theorem in the framework of this work we have to make use of the expression of the Lie derivative of the metric :

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}g^{\alpha\beta}=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}g^{\alpha\beta}-g^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\alpha}-g^{\alpha\mu}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\beta}=-({\nabla\!}^{\alpha}\xi^{\beta}+{\nabla\!}^{\beta}\xi^{\alpha})\qquad.

(2.28)

and of the connection

]
:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}A^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}A^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}+A^{\alpha}_{\beta\mu}\partial_{\gamma}\xi^{\mu}-A^{\mu}_{\beta\gamma}\partial_{\mu}\xi^{\alpha}+A^{\alpha}_{\mu\gamma}\partial_{\beta}\xi^{\mu}+\partial^{2}_{\beta\gamma}\xi^{\alpha}\qquad.

(2.29)

Note that this Lie derivative (
2.29

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}A^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}={\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}+{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}K^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}\qquad,\qquad{\mathfrak{L}}_{\xi}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}={\nabla\!}_{\beta}{\nabla\!}_{\gamma}\xi^{\alpha}+R^{\alpha}_{{\,.\,}\gamma\sigma\beta}\xi^{\sigma}

Usually, authors prefer to use Cartan formalism to discuss torsion gravity models. Their starting point is a coframe

4.2
Torsionfull solutions

In this section we will integrate the field equations under appropriate simplifying ansatzes. We start from a prescribed form of the metric which solves the field in absence of torsion and deform it by introducing a minimal contorsion that maintains the equations tractable. Each of them constitutes a solution of the field equation in the absence of torsion. A similar property also appears for the black hole configuration.

the metric tensor reads :

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})^{2}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}\,d\theta^{2})+\frac{1}{(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})^{2}}(d\xi^{2}-\xi^{2}\,d\tau^{2})\qquad.

(4.23)

It is an Einstein space when
Q_{1}=Q_{2}
, a conformally flat space when
Q_{1}=-Q_{2}
.

To pursue we made the following ansatz for the expression of the contorsion tensor components :

\displaystyle K_{r\theta r}=-K_{\theta rr}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}}k_{\theta}(r)\quad,\quad K_{\theta r\theta}:={\textstyle\frac{r^{2}}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}}k_{r}(r)=-K_{r\theta\theta}\qquad,\qquad

\displaystyle K_{\xi\tau\xi}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})}}k_{\xi}(\xi)=-K_{\tau\xi\xi}\qquad,\qquad K_{\tau\xi\tau}:=-{\textstyle\frac{\xi^{2}}{(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})}}k_{\tau}(\xi)=-K_{\xi\tau\tau}\qquad,

(4.24)

all the other components being assumed to be zero.

The ten non-vanishing contorsion field equations depend only on two terms :

\displaystyle{\kappa_{E}}(r):=(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})^{2}\Big(k^{\prime}_{r}(r)+\frac{1}{r}\,k_{r}(r)\Big)\qquad,\qquad{\kappa_{L}}(\xi)=(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})^{2}\Big(k^{\prime}_{\xi}(\xi)+\frac{1}{\xi}\,k_{\xi}(\xi)\Big)\qquad.

(4.25)

They read as :

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\tau}_{{\,.\,}\theta\tau}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}\theta\xi}=k_{\theta}(r){\big(}c_{F}+2{\,\bar{f}\,}(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}({\kappa_{E}}(r)+{\kappa_{L}}(\xi))\big)=0\quad,

(4.26)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\theta}_{{\,.\,}\tau\theta}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{r}_{{\,.\,}\tau r}=k_{\tau}(\xi){\big(}c_{F}+2{\,\bar{f}\,}(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}({\kappa_{E}}(r)+{\kappa_{L}}(\xi))=0\quad,

(4.27)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\theta}_{{\,.\,}r\theta}=-{\big(}{\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}\,\bar{d}\,+{\,\bar{f}\,}\big){\kappa_{E}}^{\prime}(r)=0\quad,

(4.28)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}\tau\tau}=-{\big(}{\textstyle\frac{2}{3}}\,\bar{d}\,+{\,\bar{f}\,}\big){\kappa_{L}}^{\prime}(\xi)=0\quad,

(4.29)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\tau}_{{\,.\,}r\tau}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}r\xi}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}{\kappa_{E}}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle\phantom{{{\mathcal{S}}}^{\tau}_{{\,.\,}r\tau}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}r\xi}=}-k_{r}(r){\big(}c_{F}+2{\,\bar{f}\,}(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}({\kappa_{E}}(r)+{\kappa_{L}}(\xi))\big)=0\quad,

(4.30)

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{S}}}^{r}_{{\,.\,}\xi r}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{\theta}_{{\,.\,}\xi\theta}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}{\kappa_{L}}^{\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle\phantom{{{\mathcal{S}}}^{r}_{{\,.\,}\xi r}={{\mathcal{S}}}^{\theta}_{{\,.\,}\xi\theta}=}-k_{\xi}(\xi){\big(}c_{F}+2{\,\bar{f}\,}(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,}({\kappa_{E}}(r)+{\kappa_{L}}(\xi))\big)=0\quad.

(4.31)

Their solutions are given by :

\displaystyle{\kappa_{E}}(r)=2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})+\frac{c_{F}}{{\,\bar{f}\,}}+\beta\qquad,\qquad{\kappa_{L}}(\xi)=2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})+\frac{c_{F}}{{\,\bar{f}\,}}-\beta

(4.32)

where
\beta
is a constant.
From them we obtain :

\displaystyle k_{r}(r)=\frac{\alpha_{r}}{r}-\frac{c_{F}/{\,\bar{f}\,}+2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})+\beta}{2\,Q_{1}\,r\,(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}\quad,

(4.33)

\displaystyle k_{\xi}(\xi)=\frac{\alpha_{\xi}}{\xi}-\frac{c_{F}/{\,\bar{f}\,}+2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-\beta}{2\,Q_{2}\,\xi\,(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})}

(4.34)

with
\alpha_{r}
and
\alpha_{\xi}
two integration constants.

Using these expression of
k_{r}(r)
and
k_{\xi}(\xi)
, the four non trivial remaining Einstein equations reduce to two algebraic equations :

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{r}_{r}=\mathcal{E}^{\theta}_{\theta}=\Lambda-4\,c_{R}\,Q_{2}-\frac{4\,c_{F}^{2}\,{\bar{d}}}{3{\,\bar{f}\,}^{2}}+\frac{4\,c_{F}(\beta-4\,Q_{1}){\big(}\bar{d}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,}\big)}{3{\,\bar{f}\,}}=0\qquad,

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\xi}_{\xi}=\mathcal{E}^{\tau}_{\tau}=\Lambda-4\,c_{R}\,Q_{1}+\frac{4\,c_{F}^{2}\,({\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,})}{3{\,\bar{f}\,}^{2}}-\frac{4\,c_{F}(\beta-4\,Q_{1}){\big(}\bar{d}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,}\big)}{3{\,\bar{f}\,}}=0\qquad,

(4.36)

that fix the value of the curvatures of the two factors of the product geometry :

\displaystyle Q_{1}=\frac{4\,c_{F}({\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,}){\big(}c_{F}^{2}+{\,\bar{f}\,}(\beta\,c_{R}+\Lambda)\big)-3\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}{\big(}c_{F}^{2}+{\,\bar{f}\,}\Lambda\big)}{16\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}(c_{F}({\,\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,})-{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}}

(4.37)

\displaystyle Q_{2}=\frac{4\,c_{F}({\,\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,}){\big(}c_{F}^{2}-{\,\bar{f}\,}(\beta\,c_{R}-\Lambda)\big)-3\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}{\big(}c_{F}^{2}+{\,\bar{f}\,}\Lambda\big)}{16\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}(c_{F}({\,\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}{\,\bar{f}\,})-{\textstyle\frac{3}{4}}\,c_{R}{\,\bar{f}\,}}

(4.38)

The solution here above involves two arbitrary functions of one variable :
k_{\theta}(r),\ k_{\tau}(\xi)
and three integration constants :
\alpha_{r}
,
\alpha_{\xi}
and
\beta
.

The previous solutions could be slightly generalised. The coordinate
\theta
used to write the metric Eq. [
4.23
] is an angle. Accordingly functions depending on it have to be periodic. But the time coordinate
\tau
varies from
-\infty
to
+\infty
and functions depending on it have no
a priori
restriction. This suggests to consider the arbitrary functions appearing in the
K_{r\theta r}
and
K_{\theta r\theta}
components of the contorsion to depend also on
\tau
.

Written as :

\displaystyle K_{r\theta r}=-K_{\theta rr}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}}\tilde{k}_{\theta}(r,\tau)\quad,\quad K_{\xi\tau\xi}=-K_{\tau\xi\xi}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})}}\tilde{k}_{\xi}(\xi,\tau)\qquad,

(4.39)

the
(\xi,\ \theta,\ \tau)
contorsion equation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}\theta\tau}=\frac{f_{1}(Q_{2}^{2}\,\xi^{4}-1)}{2\,\xi}\partial_{\tau}\tilde{k}_{\theta}(r,\tau)=0

(4.40)

implies, assuming to be in a generic case, that
K_{r\theta r}
cannot depends on
\tau
, but is an arbitrary function of
r
:

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{\theta}(r,\tau)=k_{\theta}(r)\qquad.

(4.41)

On the other hand the
(\xi,\ \tau,\ \xi)
equation

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{\xi}_{{\,.\,}\tau\xi}=-\frac{({\,\bar{d}}+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}{\,\bar{f}\,})(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})^{6}}{3\,\xi^{4}}\,\partial^{2}_{\tau\tau}\tilde{k}_{\tau}(\xi,\tau)=0

(4.42)

implies that
k_{\tau}(\xi,\tau)
is linear in the
\tau
coordinate. Under these assumptions we obtain a solution with
k_{r}(r)
still given by Eq. [
4.33
], depending on two integration constants but with
k_{\xi}(\xi)
an arbitrary function and
k_{\tau}(\xi,\tau)
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{k}_{\tau}(\xi,\tau)=k_{\tau}(\xi)+\Big(\xi\,k_{\xi}(\xi)+\xi^{2}\,{\big(}k_{\xi}^{\prime}(\xi)-\frac{(2\,c_{F}/{\,\bar{f}\,}+2\,(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-\beta)}{(1+Q_{2}\,\xi^{2})^{2}}\big)\Big)\tau

(4.43)

where
k_{\tau}(\xi)
is another arbitrary function.

More involved solution may be obtained by performing a coordinate transformation from polar coordinates to planar coordinates. For illustrative purpose let us suppose
Q_{2}=4\,L^{2}>0
and the metric
given by :

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{1}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})^{2}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}\,d\theta^{2})+\frac{1}{4\,Q_{2}\,t^{2}}(dz^{2}-dt^{2})

(4.44)

The new planar coordinates
(t,\,z)
are related to the polar coordinates by :

\displaystyle t=\frac{L(4\,L^{2}+\xi^{2})}{4\,L^{2}-\xi^{2}-4\,L\,\sinh(\tau)}\qquad,\qquad z=\frac{4\,L^{2}\,\xi\,\cosh(\tau)}{4\,L^{2}-\xi^{2}-4\,L\,\sinh(\tau)}\qquad.

(4.45)

On this new coordinate patch the non-vanishing contorsion components are, in accordance with the ansatz [
4.24
] :

\displaystyle K_{r\theta r}=-K_{\theta rr}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}}k_{\theta}(r)\qquad,\qquad K_{\theta r\theta}=-K_{r\theta\theta}:={\textstyle\frac{r^{2}}{(1+Q_{1}\,r^{2})}}k_{r}(r)\qquad,

\displaystyle K_{ztz}=-K_{tzz}:={\textstyle\frac{1}{(4\,Q_{2}\,t^{2})}}k_{t}(t,z)\qquad,\qquad K_{tzt}=-K_{ztt}:=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{(4\,Q_{2}\,t^{2})}}k_{z}(t,z)\qquad.

(4.46)

Again the contorsion equations depend only on two terms :
\kappa_{E}(r)
already defined in Eq. [
4.25
] and

\displaystyle\tilde{\kappa}_{L}(t,z):=4\,Q_{2}\,t^{2}\big(\partial_{z}k_{z}(t,z)-\partial_{t}k_{t}(t,z)\big)\qquad.

(4.47)

The structure of the system of equations remains the same. The equations that
\kappa_{E}(r)
and
\tilde{\kappa}_{L}(t,z)
have to satisfy are similar to Eqs [
4.32
] :

\displaystyle{\kappa_{E}}(r)=2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})+\frac{c_{F}}{{\,\bar{f}\,}}+\beta\qquad,\qquad{\tilde{\kappa}_{L}}(t,z)=2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})+\frac{c_{F}}{{\,\bar{f}\,}}-\beta\qquad.

(4.48)

Accordingly, the function
k_{\theta}(r)
remains an arbitrary function of one variable,
k_{r}(r)
is still given by Eq.[
4.33
] but :

\displaystyle k_{z}(t,z)=A_{z}(t,z)\qquad k_{t}(t,z)=\int\partial_{z}A_{z}(t,z)\,dt+b_{t}(z)+\frac{c_{F}/{\,\bar{f}\,}+2(Q_{1}+Q_{2})-\beta}{4\,Q_{2}\,t^{2}}\qquad.

(4.49)

Using the Jacobian defined by the coordinate transformation (
k_{z},\ k_{t}
transforms as a covector components), we easily obtain a solution in polar coordinate that generalise the previous one.

To take into account the conditions that ensure the health of the theory around flat space we rewrite the solution using the parametrisation (Eqs[
3.5
–
3.9
]) :

\displaystyle Q_{1}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{c_{R}}+\frac{3{\,c_{F}}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}\,}\beta}{3{\,c_{F}}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}+2{\,c_{R}}{\,\sigma_{0^{+}}}+c_{R}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}}-\frac{3{\,c_{F}}}{\kappa_{N}\,(2\,{\sigma_{0^{+}}}+\sigma_{2^{+}})}\right)\qquad,

(4.50)

\displaystyle Q_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{\Lambda}{c_{R}}-\frac{3{\,c_{F}}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}\,}\beta}{3{\,c_{F}}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}+2{\,c_{R}}{\,\sigma_{0^{+}}}+c_{R}{\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}}-\frac{3{\,c_{F}}}{\kappa_{N}\,(2\,{\sigma_{0^{+}}}+\sigma_{2^{+}})}\right)\qquad.

(4.51)

Notice that

\displaystyle\bar{f}=-\frac{\kappa_{N}}{6}\,\frac{c_{F}}{c_{R}}\,\big(\sigma_{2^{+}}+2\,\sigma_{0^{+}}\big)\qquad.

(4.52)

Thus the Bertotti-Robinson geometry, for the contorsion ansatz here assumed, is only compatible, in the framework of a class
I
model with a
0^{+}
field, and for a class
I\!I
model with a
2^{+}
field. It is easy to check that if
\sigma_{0^{+}}
and
\sigma_{2^{+}}
vanish no torsionful solutions are possible in the framework here considered. Notice that, on the contrary to what is done in Ref.
[
26
]
we did not assume the metric to be conformally flat and the contorsion field we obtain as solution of the field equations depends on arbitrary functions instead of arbitrary constants.

4.2.2
Plane-fronted wave spacetimes

This family of spaces is known since a long time
[
37
]
and has been extensively studied, in particular their physical interest being put into evidence in numerous works (See for instance Refs
[
55
,
56
,
57
,
58
]
). Among them there also is a subset of symmetric Lorentzian spaces
[
36
]
, making them exact torsionless solutions of the torsion gravity equations.

The general expression of their metric is :

\displaystyle ds^{2}=2\,du\,dv+H(u,x,y)\,du^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}

(4.53)

In absence of contorsion it solves the field equations (both for classes I and II models) if and only if
H(u,x,y)
is an harmonic function with respect to the
x
and
y
coordinates and the bare cosmological constant vanishes :

\displaystyle\Lambda=0\qquad,\qquad\Delta\,H(u,x,y)=0\qquad.

(4.54)

Here
\Delta
denotes the two dimensionnal Laplacian (
\Delta:={\partial_{x}^{2}}+{\partial_{y}^{2}}
).

Extension of this solution to torsionful configurations has been discussed with great generality in Ref.
[
20
]
. To display some explicit solutions we assume as
a priori
non-vanishing components of the contorsion :

\displaystyle K_{xuu}=-K_{uxu}=:X(u,x,y)\qquad,\qquad K_{yuu}=-K_{uyu}=:Y(u,x,y)\qquad.

(4.55)

We will now briefly discuss the resolution of the field equations under this ansatz :

•

Class
I
models : Einstein equations continue to impose to put
\Lambda=0
. Nevertheless let us mention that solutions representing pp-wave propagating on (anti-) de Sitter spaces are described in Ref.
[
22
]
).Two independents contorsion equations (
\mathcal{S}^{x}_{{\,.\,}vv}=0
,
\mathcal{S}^{y}_{{\,.\,}vv}=0
) and two independent Einstein equations (
\mathcal{E}^{u}_{v}=0
,
\mathcal{E}^{u}_{u}=0
) remain to be solved.
We obtain from the contorsion equations :

\displaystyle c_{F}\big({\partial_{x}^{2}}Y(u,x,y)-{\partial^{2}_{xy}}X(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}Y(u,x,y)\big)=0

(4.56)

\displaystyle c_{F}\big({\partial_{y}^{2}}X(u,x,y)-{\partial^{2}_{xy}}Y(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}X(u,x,y)\big)=0

(4.57)

from which we deduce that (let us recall that we assume
c_{F}\neq 0
) :

\displaystyle\partial_{x}X(u,x,y)+\partial_{y}Y(u,x,y)=0

(4.58)

i.e. the contorsion components
K_{xuu}
and
K_{yuu}
have to verify a two dimensional Helmholtz equation :

\displaystyle\Delta\,X(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}\,X(u,x,y)=0=\Delta\,Y(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}\,Y(u,x,y)\qquad.

(4.59)

Accordingly
X(u,x,y)
(
resp
.
Y(u,x,y)
) may be written as a superposition of exponential modes
\xi_{I}(u,x,y)
(
resp
.
\eta_{I}(u,x,y)
) :

\displaystyle\xi_{I}(u,x,y)=\pm\sqrt{\Big(\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}-p^{2}(u)\Big)}\,k(u)\,e^{-p(u)\,x\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}-p^{2}(u)}\,y}\qquad,

(4.60)

\displaystyle\eta_{I}(u,x,y)=p(u)\,k(u)\,e^{-p(u)\,x\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}\pm p^{2}(u)}\,y}\qquad,

(4.61)

p(u)
and
k(u)
being arbitrary functions of
u
, only limited by the condition that the contorsion components have to be real.

The diagonal Einstein equations impose that the bare cosmological constant
\Lambda
vanishes. Then, taking this into account and the relation Eq.(
4.58
), we obtain from the remaining equation :

\displaystyle\Delta H(u,x,y)=0

(4.62)

i.e. the geometry is still Ricci flat. Notice that as
x
and
y
varies from
-\infty
to
+\infty
we are confronted by an exponential blowup of the contorsion (the metric blowing up only polynomially).

•

Class
I\!I
models : In this case the equations apparently look a little bit more complicated but lead to a similar solution. We have still to impose
\Lambda=0
. The contorsion equations read

\displaystyle c_{F}\Big({\partial_{x}^{2}}X(u,x,y)+{\partial^{2}_{xy}}Y(u,x,y)-\frac{2\,c_{R}}{\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}X(u,x,y)-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{x}\Delta H(u,x,y)\Big)=0\qquad,

(4.63)

\displaystyle c_{F}\Big({\partial_{y}^{2}}Y(u,x,y)+{\partial^{2}_{xy}}X(u,x,y)-\frac{2\,c_{R}}{\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}Y(u,x,y)-\frac{1}{2}\,\partial_{y}\Delta H(u,x,y)\Big)=0\qquad,

(4.64)

from which we obtain :

\displaystyle\partial_{y}X(u,x,y)=\partial_{x}Y(u,x,y)\qquad.

(4.65)

The diagonal Einstein equations still imply the vanishing of the bare cosmological constant. The remaining non trivial equation is :

\displaystyle\Delta H(u,x,y)=2\frac{c_{F}}{\kappa_{N}}\big(\partial_{y}Y(u,x,y)+\partial_{x}X(u,v,y)\big)

(4.66)

which inserted in Eqs [
4.63
,
4.64
] leads again to Helmholtz equations :

\displaystyle\Delta\,X(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{+}}}\,X(u,x,y)=0=\Delta\,Y(u,x,y)-\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{+}}}\,Y(u,x,y)\qquad.

(4.67)

From these last two we deduce that the solution of Eq. [
4.66
] is given by :

\displaystyle H(u,x,y)=H_{0}(u,x,y)+\frac{c_{F}\,\sigma_{2^{+}}}{\kappa_{N}}\big(\partial_{y}Y(u,x,y)+\partial_{x}X(u,x,y)\big)

(4.68)

H_{0}(u,x,y)
being an harmonic function and the contorsion components
X(u,x,y)
and
Y(u,x,y)
by superpositions of modes :

\displaystyle\xi_{II}(u,x,y)=p(u)\,k(u)\,e^{-p(u)\,x\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}-p^{2}(u)}\,y}\qquad,

(4.69)

\displaystyle\eta_{II}(u,x,y)=\mp\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}-p^{2}(u)}\,k(u)\,e^{-p(u)\,x\pm\sqrt{\frac{2}{\sigma_{2^{-}}}-p^{2}(u)}\,y}\qquad,

(4.70)

p(u)
and
k(u)
being arbitrary functions of
u
, satisfying the same conditions as those encountered for class
I
pp-wave solutions. Let us emphasise that the function
H(u,x,y)
being no more an harmonic function (in
x
and
y
), on the contrary to the metric obtain in the framework of class
I
pp-wave, the metric no longer constitutes a solution of a vacuum Einstein space.

4.2.3
Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker spatially flat geometry

The simplest cosmological geometry is :

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+e^{2\,A(t)}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

(4.71)

describing a spatially flat homogeneous and isotropic space.
In this section we discuss some solutions of torsion gravity theories under the assumptions that the metric is such one and that the contorsion tensor is of the special form :

\displaystyle K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=\eta_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}a^{\delta}+\frac{1}{3}\,(g_{\alpha\gamma}\,k_{\beta}-g_{\beta\gamma}\,k_{\alpha})

(4.72)

with

\displaystyle a^{\alpha}=(0,\,0,\,0,\,-g(t))\qquad,\qquad k_{\alpha}=(0,\,0,\,0,\,-3\,f(t))\qquad.

(4.73)

This assumption results from the requirement that the contorsion tensor is invariant with respect to the isometry (sub)group of the metric :
\mathbf{R}^{3}\rtimes SO(3)
. It extends the framework considered in Ref.
[
59
]
by taking into account parity breaking terms.

Using this ansatz, there remains only four algebraically distinct field equations. One of them is a consequence of the other three, as expected from the Bianchi identities Eq.[
2.32
]. The three relevant equations (see Ref.
[
25
]
)
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}_{{\,.\,}01}=0
,
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}_{{\,.\,}23}=0
,
\mathcal{E}^{0}_{0}=0
lead to :

\displaystyle c_{R}\,g(t)+\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{0^{+}}\,g(t)\,\Big(g(t)^{2}-f(t)^{2}-\ddot{A}(t)-2\,\dot{A}(t)^{2}+3\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)+\dot{f}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle+(c_{R}\,\sigma_{2^{-}}-\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{2^{+}})\,g(t)\,\Big(\ddot{A}(t)+\dot{A}(t)^{2}-f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)-\dot{f}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle+c_{R}\,\sigma_{0^{-}}\Big(g(t)\big({\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\ddot{A}(t)+3\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)-\dot{f}(t)-2\,f^{2}(t)\big)+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\,\dot{A}(t)\,\dot{g}(t)+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\ddot{g}(t)\Big)=0

(4.74)

\displaystyle c_{R}\,f(t)+c_{R}\,\sigma_{0^{-}}\,g(t)\,\Big(g(t)\big(2\,f(t)-3\,\dot{A}(t)\big)-\dot{g}(t)\Big)+\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{2^{+}}\,g(t)\big(g(t)\,\dot{A}(t)+\dot{g}(t)\bigg)

\displaystyle+\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{0^{+}}\Bigg(\bigg(f(t)\Big((g^{2}(t)-f^{2}(t))+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\ddot{A}(t)-2\,\dot{A}^{2}(t)+3\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)\Big)+g(t)\,\dot{g}(t)\bigg)+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\,\dot{f}(t)\,\dot{A}(t)-2\,\dot{A}(t)\,\ddot{A}(t)+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\ddot{f}(t)-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\overset{\,\dots}{A}(t)\Bigg)

\displaystyle-c_{R}\,\sigma_{2^{-}}\,g(t)\big(g(t)\,\dot{A}(t)+\dot{g}(t)\big)=0

(4.75)

\displaystyle\Lambda+3\,c_{F}\big(g^{2}(t)-f^{2}(t)+2\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)\big)-3\,\kappa_{N}\,\dot{A}^{2}(t)+c_{F}\,\sigma_{0^{-}}\Big({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}(3\,g(t)\,\dot{A}(t)-\dot{g}(t))^{2}-2\,\dot{g}^{2}(t)+6\,f(t)\,g^{2}(t)(f(t)-2\,\dot{A}(t))\Big)

\displaystyle-{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\kappa_{N}\,{\textstyle\frac{c_{F}}{c_{R}}}\bigg(f^{2}(t)\Big(f^{2}(t)-4\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)+5\,\dot{A}^{2}-2\,g^{2}(t)\Big)+2\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)\Big(\ddot{A}(t)-\dot{A}^{2}(t)-\dot{f}(t)+2\,g^{2}(t)\Big)

\displaystyle\phantom{-{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\kappa_{N}\,{\textstyle\frac{c_{F}}{c_{R}}}\Big(}-\Big(\ddot{A}(t)+2\,\dot{A}^{2}(t)-\dot{f}(t)-g^{2}(t)\Big)\Big(\ddot{A}(t)-\dot{f}(t)+g^{2}(t)\Big)\bigg)=0

(4.76)

We emphasise that the trace of the Einstein equations leads to a remarkably simple equation, independent of the
\sigma_{J^{\pm}}
parameters :

\displaystyle\Lambda+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\,c_{F}(g^{2}(t)-f^{2}(t))+{\textstyle\frac{9}{2}}\,c_{F}\,f(t)\,\dot{A}(t)+{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\,c_{F}\,\dot{f}(t)-3\,\kappa\,\dot{A}^{2}(t)-{\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}\kappa\,\ddot{A}(t)=0\qquad.

(4.77)

In Ref.
[
25
]
cosmological solutions of class
I\!I
torsion gravity equations, build on de Sitter geometry

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+e^{2\,\lambda\,t}(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2})

(4.78)

were studied, under the assumption that the contorsion tensor was of the special form Eqs [
4.73
].
It was demonstrated that this ansatz implies that the functions
f(t)
and
g(t)
are constants. The same proof remains valid when a non-zero bare cosmological constant is included. This was analysed in Ref.
[
24
]
. which provides torsionful solutions.

For class
I
Lagrangian, the consequences differ. Let us briefly summarise how the field equations are solved in this framework. The equation
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}_{{\,.\,}23}=0
provides (assuming that
\kappa_{N}\,\sigma_{0^{+}}\neq c_{r}\,\sigma_{2^{-}}
) the expression of
\dot{f}(t)
in terms of
f(t)

5
Conclusion

We studied pure torsion gravity theories without matter sources. This simplification has allowed us to write the field equations in terms
of the metric and contorsion components expressed with respect to a natural (coordinate) frame instead of vielbiens and spin coefficients. Of course both approaches are equivalent, but the former is simpler than the latter (and well adapted for symbolic calculations on the computer). We also wrote a general expression of the Noether identities resulting from the diffeomorphism invariance of the theory. These identities, established, in a general framework, look more useful in this context than the Bianchi ones.

To obtain specific solution of the theory we restricted ourself to quadratic models. Two classes of physically acceptable such models are known. They mainly differ by the parity of their respective spin zero and spin two massive fields. We have obtained analytical solutions of the field equations in various contexts. Some of them present an unexpected aspect that make the theory questionable. The contorsion field (which is not a gauge field) sometime involves arbitrary functions, that may even be time dependent, see Eq. [
4.49
] but dont play any rôle in the expression of the spacetime metric.
The occurrence of arbitrary functions in the expression of some of the solutions presented here above raises the question of the predictability of the theory and thus the possibility of a possible confrontation of the models with observations. This point has already been raised by various authors. For instance, predictability is discussed in reference
[
63
]
where sufficient conditions of uniqueness of the solution of the Cauchy problem are established
3
3

3

As noticed in Ref.
[
64
]
, models of class
I
and
I\!I
do not verify them
, but some Lagrangians with well-posed initial value problems, that escape these sufficient conditions are described in Ref.
[
64
]
.
Moreover, various authors have also obtained solutions containing arbitrary functions : in the framework of an analysis of asymptotic solutions
[
65
]
or in the ones of solutions built using the double duality ansatz
[
66
,
32
]
. In particular, this last work proposes an interesting conjecture. The appearance of arbitrary functions would reflect a hidden gauge symmetry that could be revealed by a Hamiltonian analysis, as the symmetry could emerge through a bifurcation phenomenon of the constraint algebra for certain configurations. However, none of the arbitrary functions encountered (that are not reflecting the diffeomorphism invariance) are unrestricted. They do not depend on all the coordinates, which makes the previous interpretation unlikely. In any case, the question of their physical and mathematical meaning remains open and requires a further (certainly difficult) work to be elucidated.

We also met obstruction to the existence of the contorsion field despite the fact that the metric remains perfectly regular (see the solution Eq. [
4.86b
], in section [
4.2.3
]). The specific solutions we obtain also emphasise differences between the two classes of quadratic gravity theories. For instance we obtain, under specific assumption, a black hole solution for the class
I
theory that cannot exits in the framework of the class
I\!I
.

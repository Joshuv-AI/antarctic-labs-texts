An advance in the arithmetic of the Lie groups as an alternative to the forms of the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff-Dynkin theorem
Sunghyun Kim, Zhichen Liu, Richard A. Klemm

Abstract
The exponential of an operator or matrix is widely used in quantum theory, but it sometimes can be a challenge to evaluate. For non-commutative operators 𝐗 {\bf X} and 𝐘 {\bf Y} , according to the Campbell-Baker-Hausdorff-Dynkin theorem, e 𝐗 + 𝐘 {\rm e}^{{\bf X}+{\bf Y}} is not equivalent to e 𝐗 ​ e 𝐘 {\rm e}^{\bf X}{\rm e}^{\bf Y} , but is instead given by the well-known infinite series formula. For a Lie algebra of a basis of three operators { 𝐗 , 𝐘 , 𝐙 } \{{\bf X,Y,Z}\} , such that [ 𝐗 , 𝐘 ] = κ ​ 𝐙 [{\bf X},{\bf Y}]=\kappa{\bf Z} for scalar κ \kappa and cyclic permutations, here it is proven that e a ​ 𝐗 + b ​ 𝐘 {\rm e}^{a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}} is equivalent to e p ​ 𝐙 ​ e q ​ 𝐗 ​ e − p ​ 𝐙 {\rm e}^{p{\bf Z}}{\rm e}^{q{\bf X}}{\rm e}^{-p{\bf Z}} for scalar p p and q q . Extensions for e a ​ 𝐗 + b ​ 𝐘 + c ​ 𝐙 {\rm e}^{a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}+c{\bf Z}} are also provided. This method is useful for the dynamics of atomic and molecular nuclear and electronic spins in constant and oscillatory transverse magnetic and electric fields.



I
Introduction

In 1954, Rabi, Ramsey and Schwinger reviewed magnetic resonance problems in the rotating coordinates of a nuclear spin
rabi
. The reviewed literature focused on the spin in a weak rotating magnetic field normal to a strong constant magnetic field and the transition probability from a singly occupied state to another singly occupied state, rather than upon the quantum spin wave function. A brief derivation of the spin wave function that satisfies the Schrödinger equation at the time

\displaystyle\Psi(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\omega t{\bm{J}}_{z}}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}[({\omega-\Omega}){\bm{J}}_{z}-\lambda\Omega{\bm{J}}_{x}]t}\Psi(0)

(

. As Ramsey noted,
(

the second exponential factor in (1) drives the transitions from one quantum state to another, but is complicated by the non-commutivity of the operators.

From a mathematical viewpoint, this exponential operator in (

{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}[({\omega-\Omega}){\bm{J}}_{z}-\lambda\Omega{\bm{J}}_{x}]t}\rightarrow{\rm e}^{a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}}

(2)

II

According to the CBHD theorem

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{{\bf X}}{\rm e}^{{\bf Y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{H({\bf X},{\bf Y})},

(3)

\displaystyle H({\bf X},{\bf Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log{({\rm e}^{{\bf X}}{\rm e}^{{\bf Y}})}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{1}+n_{1}>0}\cdots\sum_{m_{k}+n_{k}>0}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\frac{{\bf X}^{m_{1}}{\bf Y}^{n_{1}}\cdots{\bf X}^{m_{k}}{\bf Y}^{n_{k}}}{m_{1}!n_{1}!\cdots m_{k}!n_{k}!}

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf X}+{\bf Y}+\frac{1}{2}[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]+\frac{1}{12}[{\bf X},[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]]-\frac{1}{12}[{\bf Y},[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]]+\cdots

(6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m_{1}+n_{1}>0}\cdots\sum_{m_{k}+n_{k}>0}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k\sum_{i=1}^{k}(m_{i}+n_{i})}\frac{1}{m_{1}!n_{1}!\cdots m_{k}!n_{k}!}

(8)

\displaystyle\overbrace{[{\bf X},[\cdots,{\bf X}}^{m_{1}},\overbrace{[{\bf Y},[\cdots,[{\bf Y}}^{n_{1}},[\cdots\overbrace{[{\bf X},[\cdots,[{\bf X}}^{m_{k}},\overbrace{[{\bf Y},[\cdots,[{\bf Y},[\cdots]}^{n_{k}}\cdots],

where

{\bf X}

In this view, the exponential in (

\displaystyle a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(a{\bf{X}},b{\bf{Y}})-\frac{1}{2}[a{\bf{X}},b{\bf{Y}}]-\frac{1}{12}[a{\bf{X}},[a{\bf{X}},b{\bf{Y}}]]+\cdots,

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}}

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{a{\bf X}}e^{b{\bf Y}}

(10)

The operator in (
2

III
The result

We desire to obtain a more useful form of the exponential
e^{a{\bf X}+b{\bf Y}}
to apply for operators representing physical systems. Our approach is not from the CBHD theorem, but instead from a transformation analogous to a physical rotation for the relevant subgroup of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. We confine our Lie algebra to physical systems with three basis operators, and define the form of the unitary transformation needed in order to obtain our results.

Definition 1

A set of operators
\{{\bf O}_{\mu}\}=\{{\bf X},{\bf Y},{\bf Z}\}
is in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
if the following 3-cyclic relation is satisfied

\displaystyle[{\bf O}_{\mu},{\bf O}_{\nu}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}{\bf O}_{\lambda},

(11)

where
\epsilon_{\mu\nu\lambda}
is the Levi-Civita symbol and summation over like Greek subscripts is implied.

Then, our Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
maintains the Jacobi identity by setting
[{\bf O}_{\mu},{\bf O}_{\mu}]=0
, or explicitly

\displaystyle[{\bf X},[{\bf Y},{\bf Z}]]+[{\bf Y},[{\bf Z},{\bf X}]]+[{\bf Z},[{\bf X},{\bf Y}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(12)

and the spin matrices
{\bm{J}}_{x},{\bm{J}}_{y},{\bm{J}}_{z}
satisfy Definition
1
with
\kappa={\rm i}
in units of
\hbar
.

Definition 2

The transformation of
{\bf O}_{\nu}
by
{\bf O}_{\mu}
is defined as

{\rm e}^{-p{{\bf O}_{\mu}}}{\bf O}_{\nu}{\rm e}^{p{\bf O}_{\mu}},

(13)

where
p
is a scalar.

The transformation is analogous to a physical rotation, but we are seeking to apply it to the exponential of two operators. With the above two definitions, the following theorem demonstrates the existence of a simple product form of the exponentials
{\rm e}^{\bf X},{\rm e}^{\bf Y},{\rm e}^{\bf Z}

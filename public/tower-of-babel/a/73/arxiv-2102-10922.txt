The envelope of holomorphy of a classical truncated tube domain
Marek Jarnicki, Peter Pflug

Abstract
. We present the envelope of holomorphy of a classical truncated tube domain.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
32D10, 32D15, 32D25

S. Bochner showed that the envelope of holomorphy of a tube domain
G=D+i\mathbb{R}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is given by the convex hull of
G
. Discussions of so called generalized tube domains
G=D_{1}+iD_{2}
(also called truncated tube domains)
have shown that Bochner’s result becomes false, i.e. the envelope of holomorphy of
G
is, in general, a strict subdomain of
\operatorname{conv}(D_{1})+i\operatorname{conv}(D_{2})
(see
[
Jar-Pfl 2020
]
). The simplest known example of this type is given by the following domain

G:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:R_{1}^{\prime}<\|x\|<R_{1}^{\prime\prime},\;\|y\|<R_{2}\},\;n\geq 2,

where
\|\;\|
stands for the Euclidean norm in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0\leq R_{1}^{\prime}<R_{1}^{\prime\prime}
,
0<R_{2}
, see
[
Jar-Pfl 2020
]
.

Recently, discussing the tube theorem, (see
[
Nog 2020
]
) J. Noguchi asked for the envelope of holomorphy
\mathcal{E}(G)
of
G
. Here we present the answer.

Theorem 1
.

\mathcal{E}(G)=\{x+iy\in\widehat{G}:\|y\|^{2}<\|x\|^{2}-(R_{1}^{\prime 2}-R_{2}^{2})\}
, where
\widehat{G}
is the convex hull of
G
. In particular, the envelope is univalent.

Remark 2
.

This result shows that the envelope of our truncated tube domain is never the convex hull of
G
except when
R_{1}^{\prime}=0
or
R_{2}=\infty
(see the paper of J. Kajiwara
[
Kaj 1963
]
); the last case is just Bochner’s tube theorem while the other case can be also handled using
[
Shi 1968
]
if
n>2
and
[
Pfl 1980
]
,
[
Kar 1991
]
if
n=2
. Moreover, it is univalent; but we don’t know whether the envelope of every generalized tube domain is univalent.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
R_{1}^{\prime\prime}=1
; then we set
R_{1}:=R_{1}^{\prime}
to simplify the notation, i.e. the domain we discuss is

G:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:R_{1}<\|x\|<1,\;\|y\|<R_{2}\}.

The proof is done via induction. Let
n=2
. Then it is based on the work of S.M. Ivashkovich (
[
Iva 1982
]
), in particular on Lemma 8 in this work.

Let
f\in\mathcal{O}(G)
.

Note that
G\subset\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\operatorname{Re}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})-(R_{1}^{2}-R_{2}^{2})>0\}
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,\min\{R_{2}^{2},1-R_{1}^{2}\})
,
\varepsilon\neq R_{2}^{2}-R_{1}^{2}
, put

\displaystyle\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z):

\displaystyle=z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}-(R_{1}^{2}-R_{2}^{2})-\varepsilon,\;z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2},

\displaystyle\Pi_{\varepsilon}:

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\operatorname{Re}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)=0\},\text{and finally }

\displaystyle\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{+}:

\displaystyle=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\operatorname{Re}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z)>0\}.

Put
R_{1,\varepsilon}:=\sqrt{R_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon}
, and fix
R_{1,\varepsilon}^{\ast}\in(R_{1},R_{1,\varepsilon})
.

Finally, define
D_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:\|x\|<R_{1,\varepsilon}^{\ast},\|y\|^{2}<\|x\|^{2}-(R_{1}^{2}-R_{2}^{2})-\varepsilon\}\subset\Pi_{\varepsilon}^{+}
.

Then
S_{\varepsilon}:=\overline{\partial D_{\varepsilon}\setminus(\overline{D_{\varepsilon}}\cap\Pi_{\varepsilon})}
is connected and
S_{\varepsilon}\subset G
. Note that
f
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
S_{\varepsilon}
. Then, using Lemma 8 of
[
Iva 1982
]
,
f
extends to a holomorphic function
f_{\varepsilon}
on
D_{\varepsilon}
which is identically equal to
f
near
S_{\varepsilon}
. Using the identity theorem and
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
we end up with a holomorphic function on
\mathcal{E}(G)
.

Observe that
\mathcal{E}(G)
is the intersection of a convex domain and a Levi-flat pseudoconvex domain and so it is pseudoconvex. Therefore we have got the envelope of holomorphy of
G
.

Now let
n\geq 3
and assume that the theorem is true in dimension
n-1
. Take an
f\in\mathcal{O}(G)
and fix a point
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}
with
|x_{n}|<1
,
|y_{n}|<R_{2}
. Then
G
intersects the hyperplane
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}\times\{z_{n}\}

along

G(z_{n}):=\{\widetilde{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}:R_{1}^{2}-|x_{n}|^{2}<\|\widetilde{x}\|^{2}<1-|x_{n}|^{2},\;\|\widetilde{y}\|^{2}<R_{2}^{2}-|y_{n}|^{2}\}.

If
|x_{n}|>R_{1}
, then
G(z_{n})
is convex and therefore, pseudoconvex; in particular,
\mathcal{E}(G(z_{n}))=G(z_{n})
. On
G(z_{n})
put
\widetilde{f}(\cdot,z_{n}):=f(\cdot,z_{n})
.

If
|x_{n}|\leq R_{1}
, then
G(z_{n})
is our domain, now in
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
.
Therefore, by induction, there exists an
\widetilde{f}(\cdot,z_{n})\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{E}(G(z_{n}))
extending
f(\cdot,z_{n})
.

Summarizing, we end up with a function
\widetilde{f}
on
\mathcal{E}(G)=\bigcup_{|x_{n}|<1,|y_{n}|<R_{2}}\mathcal{E}(G(z_{n}))
.
Recall that for all
z_{n}=x_{n}+iy_{n}
with
|x_{n}|<1
and
|y_{n}|<R_{2}
, the function
\widetilde{f}(\cdot,z_{n})
is holomorphic. Moreover,
\widetilde{f}=f
on
G
, i.e.
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic on
G
.

It remains to show that
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{E}(G)
. Fix a point
a+ib\in\mathcal{E}(G)
. If
\|a\|>R_{1}
, then
a+ib\in G
and so
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
a+ib
.

Now assume that
\|a\|\leq R_{1}
. Observe that it suffices to find open connected sets
\varnothing\neq U^{\prime}_{1}\subset U_{1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
,
U_{2}\subset\mathbb{C}
with
a+ib\in U_{1}\times U_{2}\subset\mathcal{E}(G)
and
U^{\prime}_{1}\times U_{2}\subset G
. Then Hartogs Theorem on separate analyticity (see Theorem 1.1.10(a) from
[
Jar-Pfl 2011
]
) will show that
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic on
U_{1}\times U_{2}
, and therefore, in a neighborhood of
a+ib
.

Take a positive real number
\varepsilon
such that
R_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon<1
and

\|b\|^{2}+2\varepsilon<\|a\|^{2}-(R_{1}^{2}-R_{2}^{2})
. Put

\displaystyle U_{1}:

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{x}+i\widetilde{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}:\|\widetilde{a}\|^{2}-\varepsilon/4<\|\widetilde{x}\|^{2}<1-|a_{n}|^{2}-\varepsilon/2,\|\widetilde{y}\|^{2}<\|\widetilde{b}\|^{2}+\varepsilon/2\},

\displaystyle U_{2}:

\displaystyle=\{x_{n}+iy_{n}\in\mathbb{C}:|a_{n}|^{2}-\varepsilon/4<|x_{n}|^{2}<|a_{n}|^{2}+\varepsilon/2,|y_{n}|^{2}<|b_{n}|^{2}+\varepsilon/2\},

\displaystyle U_{1}^{\prime}:

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{z}=\widetilde{x}+i\widetilde{y}\in U_{1}:1-|a_{n}|^{2}-3\varepsilon/2<\|\widetilde{x}\|^{2}<1-|a_{n}|^{2}-\varepsilon/2\}.

Then
a+ib\in U_{1}\times U_{2}\subset\mathcal{E}(G)
and
\varnothing\neq U_{1}^{\prime}\times U_{2}\subset G
. Therefore, using Hartogs theorem,
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic in a neighborhood of
a+ib
.

Hence
\widetilde{f}
is holomorphic on
\mathcal{E}(G)
and we have shown that
\mathcal{E}(G)
is a holomorphic extension of
G
. On the other hand, as above, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{E}(G)
is pseudoconvex. Therefore, it is the envelope of holomorphy of
G
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Observe that the generalized tube domain
G=D_{1}+iD_{2}
discussed above has a special symmetry; namely, whenever
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\|x\|=\|x^{\prime}\|
, and
x\in D_{1}
, then
x^{\prime}\in D_{1}
(resp. whenever
y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\|y\|=\|y^{\prime}\|
, and
y\in D_{2}
, then
y^{\prime}\in D_{2})
. Moreover
D_{2}
is convex. So one may ask whether any generalized tube domain with this kind of an additional geometric property (e.g. substitute in the definition of
G
the norm
\|\cdot\|
by another
\mathbb{R}
-norm) has a univalent envelope of holomorphy and what is its description.

Let us end this short note with emphasizing some open general problems (see
[
Nog 2020
]
):

Remark 4
.

a) Does every generalized tube domain have a univalent envelope of holomorphy ?

b) In case of univalence what is the description of the envelope ?

c) What is the shape of the envelope of holomorphy in general ?

Complex Intuitionistic fuzzy bracket product


Abstract
. A complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie superalgebra is a generalization of intuitionistic fuzzy Lie superalgebra whose membership function takes values in the unit disk in the complex plane. In [ 5 ] , we introduced and studied the concepts of complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebras and complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals of Lie superalgebras. Moreover, we also, in [ 5 ] , defined the image and preimage of complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebra under Lie superalgebra anti-homomorphism, and he investigated the properties of anti-complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie sub-superalgebras and anti-complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals under anti-homomorphisms of Lie superalgebras. In this research, we use the concepts of complex intuitionistic fuzzy Lie superalgebras to introduce the complex intuitionistic fuzzy bracket products. Finally, we use the definitions of the image and preimage of complex intuitionistic fuzzy ideals under Lie superalgebra anti-homomorphisms, to study the characterizations of the image and preimage of the complex intuitionistic fuzzy bracket products under Lie superalgebra anti-homomorphisms. 2.
Complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets

Let
X\not=\phi
. A complex intuitionistic fuzzy set on
X
is an object having the form
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in X\}
, where the complex functions
\lambda_{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\rho_{A}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the degree of membership (namely
\lambda_{A}(x)
) and the degree of non-membership (namely
\rho_{A}(x)
) of each element
x\in X
to the set
A
, respectively, that assign to any element
x\in X
complex numbers
\lambda_{A}(x)
,
\rho_{A}(x)
lie within the unit circle with the property
|\lambda_{A}(x)|+|\rho_{A}(x)|\leq 1
.
For the sake of simplicity, we shall use the symbol
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
for the complex intuitionistic fuzzy set
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in X\}
.

We shall assume
\lambda_{A}(x)
,
\rho_{A}(x)
will be represented by
r_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(x)}
and
\hat{r}_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)}
, respectively, where
i=\sqrt{-1}
,
r_{A}(x),\hat{r}_{A}(x),\omega_{A}(x),\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\in[0,1]
. Thus the property of
|\lambda_{A}(x)|+|\rho_{A}(x)|\leq 1
implies
r_{A}(x)+\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq 1
. Note that the intuitionistic fuzzy set is a special case of complex intuitionistic fuzzy set with
\omega_{A}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)=0.

Also, if
\rho_{A}(x)=(1-r_{A}(x))e^{i2\pi(1-\omega_{A}(x))}
, then we obtain a complex fuzzy set.
Let
\alpha e^{i2\pi\beta}
and
\gamma e^{i2\pi\delta}
be two complex numbers, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in[0,1]
. By
\alpha e^{i2\pi\beta}\leq\gamma e^{i2\pi\delta}
we mean
\alpha\leq\gamma
and
\beta\leq\delta
.
In this paper,we use the symbols
a\wedge b=min\{a,b\}
and
a\vee b=max\{a,b\}
.
Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be a complex intuitionistic fuzzy set on
X
with the degree of membership
\lambda_{A}(x)=r_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(x)}
and the degree of non-membership
\rho_{A}(x)=\hat{r}_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)}
. Then
A
is said to be a homogeneous complex intuitionistic fuzzy set if the following two conditions hold
\forall x,y\in X

(1)
r_{A}(x)\leq r_{A}(y)
if and only if
\omega_{A}(x)\leq\omega_{A}(y)
,

(2)
\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{r}_{A}(y)
if and only if
\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{\omega}_{A}(y)
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be two complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets on the same set
X
, we say that
A
is homogeneous with
B
if the following conditions hold
\forall x,y\in X

(1)
r_{A}(x)\leq r_{B}(y)
if and only if
\omega_{A}(x)\leq\omega_{B}(y)
,

(2)
\hat{r}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y)
if and only if
\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\leq\hat{\omega}_{B}(y)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be any field, and let
\mathbf{V}
be a
K
-vector space. A complex intuitionistic fuzzy (CIF for short) set on
\mathbf{V}
defined as an object having the form
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in\mathbf{V}\}
, where the complex functions
\lambda_{A}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
\rho_{A}:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
denote the degree of membership (namely
\lambda_{A}(x)
) and the degree of non-membership (namely
\rho_{A}(x)
) of each element
x\in\mathbf{V}
to the set
A
, respectively, that assign to any element
x\in\mathbf{V}
complex numbers
\lambda_{A}(x)
,
\rho_{A}(x)
lie within the unit circle with the property
|\lambda_{A}(x)|+|\rho_{A}(x)|\leq 1
.

We shall use the symbol
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
for the CIF set
A=\{(x,\lambda_{A}(x),\rho_{A}(x))\ |x\in\mathbf{V}\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF sets of a vector subspace
\mathbf{V}
. Then
A\subseteq B
if
\lambda_{A}(x)\leq\lambda_{B}(x)
and
\rho_{A}(x)\geq\rho_{B}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbf{V}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{V}
be a
K
-vector space. A CIF set
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
of a vector space
\mathbf{V}
is called a CIF vector space of
\mathbf{V}
, if it satisfies the following conditions

for any
x,y\in\mathbf{V}
,
\alpha\in K

(1)
\lambda_{A}(x+y)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)\wedge\lambda_{A}(y)
, and
\rho_{A}(x+y)\leq\rho_{A}(x)\vee\rho_{A}(y)

(2)
\lambda_{A}(\alpha x)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)
, and
\rho_{A}(\alpha x)\leq\rho_{A}(x)
.

From this definition, we know that for any
x\in\mathbf{V}
,
\lambda_{A}(0)\geq\lambda_{A}(x)
and
\rho_{A}(0)\leq\rho_{A}(x)
. In this paper, we always assume that
\lambda_{A}(0)=1e^{i2\pi}=1
and
\rho_{A}(0)=0e^{i2(\pi)0}=0
.

Definition 2.4
.

[
5
]

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF vector subspaces of a vector subspace
\mathbf{V}
, where
\lambda_{A}=r_{A}e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}},\lambda_{B}=r_{B}e^{i2\pi\omega_{B}}
and
\rho_{A}=\hat{r}_{A}e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}},\rho_{B}=\hat{r}_{B}e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{B}}
. If
A
is homogenous with
B
. Then the complex intuitionistic sum of
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
is defined to the CIF set
A+B=(\lambda_{A+B},\rho_{A+B})
of
\mathbf{V}
given by

\lambda_{A+B}(x)=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{x=a+b}\{(r_{A}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b))\}e^{i2\pi\sup\limits_{x=a+b}\{(\omega_{A}(a)\wedge\omega_{B}(b))\}}&:\quad{\rm if}\ x=a+b\\
0&:\quad otherwise,\end{cases}

\rho_{A+B}(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{x=a+b}\{(\hat{r}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b))\}e^{i2\pi\inf\limits_{x=a+b}\{(\hat{\omega}_{A}(a)\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(b))\}}&:\quad{\rm if}\ x=a+b\\
1&:\quad otherwise,\end{cases}

Further, if
A\cap B=(\lambda_{A\cap B},\rho_{A\cap B})
, where

\lambda_{A\cap B}(x)=\begin{cases}0&:\quad x\not=0\\
1&:\quad x=0,\end{cases}\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \rho_{A\cap B}(x)=\begin{cases}1&:\quad x\not=0\\
0&:\quad x=0,\end{cases}.

Then
A+B
is said to be the direct sum and denoted by
A\oplus B
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
5
]

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF vector subspaces of a vector space
\mathbf{V}
such that
A
is homogenous with
B
. Then
A+B=(\lambda_{A+B},\rho_{A+B})
is also a CIF vector subspaces of
\mathbf{V}
.

Definition 2.6
.

[
5
]

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be a CIF vector subspace of a
K
-vector space
\mathbf{V}
. For
\alpha\in K
and
x\in\mathbf{V}
, define
\alpha A=(\lambda_{\alpha A},\rho_{\alpha A})
, where

\lambda_{\alpha A}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)=r_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)}&:\quad\alpha\not=0\\
1&:\quad\alpha=0,x=0\\
0&:\quad\alpha=0,x\not=0\end{cases}

and

\rho_{\alpha A}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)=\hat{r}_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(\alpha^{-1}x)}&:\quad\alpha\not=0\\
0&:\quad\alpha=0,x=0\\
1&:\quad\alpha=0,x\not=0\end{cases}

3.
Complex intuitionistic fuzzy brackets

In this section we assume that
\mathbf{V}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space over a field
K
. Also we assume that
A
is homogeneous with
B
for any two complex intuitionistic fuzzy sets
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
,
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
of
\mathbf{V}
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
5
]

Let
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}_{0}+\mathbf{V}_{1}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space. Suppose that
A_{0}=(\lambda_{A_{0}},\rho_{A_{0}})
and
A_{1}=(\lambda_{A_{1}},\rho_{A_{1}})
are CIF vector subspaces of
\mathbf{V}_{0}
and
\mathbf{V}_{1}
, respectively. Define
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=(\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}},\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}})

where

\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{A_{0}}(x)&:\quad x\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\\
0&:\quad x\not\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\end{cases}\ \ and\ \ \rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho_{A_{0}}(x)&:\quad x\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\\
1&:\quad x\not\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\end{cases}

and define
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=(\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}},\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}})

where

\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{A_{1}}(x)&:\quad x\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\\
0&:\quad x\not\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\end{cases}\ \ and\ \ \rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}\rho_{A_{1}}(x)&:\quad x\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\\
1&:\quad x\not\in\mathbf{V}_{1}\end{cases}

Then
\mathfrak{a}_{0}=(\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}},\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}})
and
\mathfrak{a}_{1}=(\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}},\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}})
are the CIF vector subspaces of
\mathbf{V}
. Moreover, we have
\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{1}=(\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{1}},\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{1}})
, where

\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}(x)\wedge\lambda_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}1&:\quad x=0\\
0&:\quad x\not=0,\end{cases}

and

\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{0}}(x)\vee\rho_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}0&:\quad x=0\\
1&:\quad x\not=0.\end{cases}

So
\mathfrak{a}_{0}+\mathfrak{a}_{1}
is the direct sum and is denoted by
A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
. If
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is a CIF vector subspace of
\mathbf{V}
and
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}
, then
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is called a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded CIF vector subspaces of
\mathbf{V}
.

Definition 3.2
.

[
5
]

Let
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}_{0}+\mathbf{V}_{1}
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded vector space, and let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be CIF set of
\mathbf{V}
. Then
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is called a CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
, if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded CIF vector subspace of
\mathbf{V}
,

(2)
\lambda_{A}([x,y])\geq\lambda_{A}(x)\vee\lambda_{A}(y)
and
\rho_{A}([x,y])\leq\rho_{A}(x)\wedge\rho_{A}(y)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
5
]

If
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
are CIF ideals of
\mathbf{V}=\mathbf{V}_{0}+\mathbf{V}_{1}
, then so is
A+B=(\lambda_{A+B},\rho_{A+B})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF sets of
\mathbf{V}
such that
A
is homogenous with
B
. We define the complex intuitionistic fuzzy bracket product
[A,B]=(\lambda_{[A,B]},\rho_{[A,B]})
where

\lambda_{[A,B]}(x)=\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{r_{A}(x_{i})\wedge r_{B}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\omega_{A}(x_{i})\wedge\omega_{B}(y_{i})\}}\}&:\quad{\rm where}\ \alpha_{i}\in K,x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbf{V}\\
0&:\quad{\rm if}\ x\not=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}],\end{cases}

and

\rho_{[A,B]}(x)=\begin{cases}\inf\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i})\vee\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{\omega}_{A}(x_{i})\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(y_{i})\}}\}&:\quad{\rm where}\ \alpha_{i}\in K,x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbf{V}\\
1&:\quad{\rm if}\ x\not=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]\end{cases}.

We have to remark that if
x,y\in\mathbf{V}
, then

(1)
\lambda_{[A,B]}([x,y])=r_{[A,B]}([x,y])e^{i2\pi\omega_{[A,B]}([x,y])}
and
r_{[A,B]}([x,y])\geq r_{A}(x)\wedge r_{B}(y)
,
\omega_{[A,B]}([x,y])\geq\omega_{A}(x)\wedge\omega_{B}(y)

(2)
\rho_{[A,B]}([x,y])=\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}([x,y])}
and
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])\leq r_{A}(x)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(y)
,
\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}([x,y])\leq\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(y)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A_{1}=(\lambda_{A_{1}},\rho_{A_{1}})
,
A_{2}=(\lambda_{A_{2}},\rho_{A_{2}})
and
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF sets of
\mathbf{V}
such that
A_{1}\subseteq B
,
A_{2}\subseteq B
, then
A_{1}+A_{2}\subseteq B
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{V}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=r_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x=a+b}\{(r_{A_{1}}(a)\wedge r_{A_{2}}(b))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{A_{1}}(a)\wedge\omega_{A_{2}}(b))}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x=a+b}\{(r_{B}(a)\wedge r_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\omega_{B}(a)\wedge\omega_{B}(b))}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x=a+b}\{r_{B}(a+b)e^{i2\pi\omega_{B}(a+b)}\}

\displaystyle=r_{B}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{B}(x)}=\lambda_{B}(x)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x)}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{x=a+b}\{(\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{A_{2}}(b))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{A_{1}}(a)\vee\hat{\omega}_{A_{2}}(b))}\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{x=a+b}\{(\hat{r}_{B}(a)\vee\hat{r}_{B}(b))e^{i2\pi(\hat{\omega}_{B}(a)\vee\hat{\omega}_{B}(b))}\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{x=a+b}\{\hat{r}_{B}(a+b)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{B}(a+b)}\}

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{B}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{B}(x)}=\rho_{B}(x).

Hence
A_{1}+A_{2}\subseteq B
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A_{1}=(\lambda_{A_{1}},\rho_{A_{1}})
,
A_{2}=(\lambda_{A_{2}},\rho_{A_{2}})
and
B_{1}=(\lambda_{B_{1}},\rho_{B_{1}})
,
B_{2}=(\lambda_{B_{2}},\rho_{B_{2}})
be CIF sets of
\mathbf{V}
such that
A_{1}\subseteq A_{2}
,
B_{1}\subseteq B_{2}
. Then
[A_{1},B_{1}]\subseteq[A_{2},B_{2}]
. In particular, if
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
,
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
are CIF sets of
\mathbf{V}
, then
[A_{1},B]\subseteq[A_{2},B]
and
[A,B_{1}]\subseteq[A,B_{2}]
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{V}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{[A_{1},B_{1}]}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{r_{A_{1}}(x_{i})\wedge r_{B_{1}}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\omega_{A_{1}}(x_{i})\wedge\omega_{B_{1}}(y_{i})\}}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{r_{A_{2}}(x_{i})\wedge r_{B_{2}}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\omega_{A_{2}}(x_{i})\wedge\omega_{B_{2}}(y_{i})\}}\}=\lambda_{[A_{2},B_{2}]}(x)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{[A_{1},B_{1}]}(x)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(x_{i})\vee\hat{r}_{B_{1}}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{\omega}_{A_{1}}(x_{i})\vee\hat{\omega}_{B_{1}}(y_{i})\}}\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{r}_{A_{2}}(x_{i})\vee\hat{r}_{B_{2}}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\hat{\omega}_{A_{2}}(x_{i})\vee\hat{\omega}_{B_{2}}(y_{i})\}}\}=\rho_{[A_{2},B_{2}]}(x)

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
A_{1}=(\lambda_{A_{1}},\rho_{A_{1}})
,
A_{2}=(\lambda_{A_{2}},\rho_{A_{2}})
and
B_{1}=(\lambda_{B_{1}},\rho_{B_{1}})
,
B_{2}=(\lambda_{B_{2}},\rho_{B_{2}})
and
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
,
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be CIF sets of
\mathbf{V}
. Then

[A_{1}+A_{2},B]=[A_{1},B]+[A_{2},B]
and
[A,B_{1}+B_{2}]=[A,B_{1}]+[A,B_{2}]
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbf{V}
. If we define
\overline{\sup}
by
\overline{\sup}=\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{[A_{1}+A_{2},B]}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{r_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x_{i})\wedge r_{B}(y_{i})\}e^{i2\pi\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\omega_{A_{1}+A_{2}}(x_{i})\wedge\omega_{B}(y_{i})\}}\}

which implies that
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])>\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(x)
or
\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}([x,y])>\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}(x)
. Let
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])>\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(x)
. Then
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])>t>\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(x)
for some
t\in[0,1]
. So there exist
x_{i},y_{i}\in\mathbf{V}
and
\alpha_{i}\in K
such that
x=\sum_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]
and for all
i\in N
,
\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i})<t
,
\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i})<t
. Moreover,
\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i})=\hat{r}_{A_{0}+A_{1}}(x_{i_{0}}+x_{i_{1}})=\hat{r}_{A_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})\vee\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(x_{i_{1}})<t
, and
\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i})=\hat{r}_{B_{0}+B_{1}}(y_{i_{0}}+y_{i_{1}})=\hat{r}_{B_{0}}(y_{i_{0}})\vee\hat{r}_{B_{1}}(y_{i_{1}})<t
, then we have
\hat{r}_{A_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})<t
,
\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(x_{i_{1}})<t
and
\hat{r}_{B_{0}}(y_{i_{0}})<t
,
\hat{r}_{B_{1}}(y_{i_{1}})<t
.
We get

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(\sum_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[[x_{i},y_{i}],y])\leq\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([[x_{i},y_{i}],y])\leq\max\{\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x_{i},[y_{i},y]]),

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{0}},y]]),\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{1}},y]]),\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{0}},y]]),\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])\}

if
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x_{i},[y_{i},y]])
is the maximum, then we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x_{i},[y_{i},y]])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i})\vee\hat{r}_{B}([y_{i},y])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i})\vee(\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i})\wedge\hat{r}_{B}(y))<t;

if
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])
is the maximum, then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])=\hat{r}_{[B,A]}([y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{0}})\vee\hat{r}_{A}([x_{i_{0}},y])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{0}})\vee(r_{A}(x_{i_{0}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{B_{0}}(y_{i_{0}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))<t;

if
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])
is the maximum, then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[B,A]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{1}})\vee\hat{r}_{A}([x_{i_{1}},y])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{1}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i_{1}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{B_{1}}(y_{i_{1}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(x_{i_{1}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))<t;

if
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])
is the maximum, then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{0}},y]]

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[B,A]}([y_{i_{1}},[x_{i_{0}},y]])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{1}})\vee\hat{r}_{A}([x_{i_{0}},y])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{1}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i_{0}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{B_{1}}(y_{i_{1}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A_{0}}(x_{i_{0}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))<t;

if
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])
is the maximum, then, by Lemma
3.12
, we have

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{[A,B]}(-[y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{1}},y]]

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{[B,A]}([y_{i_{0}},[x_{i_{1}},y]])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{0}})\vee\hat{r}_{A}([x_{i_{1}},y])

\displaystyle\leq\hat{r}_{B}(y_{i_{0}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A}(x_{i_{1}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))

\displaystyle=\hat{r}_{B_{0}}(y_{i_{0}})\vee(\hat{r}_{A_{1}}(x_{i_{1}})\wedge\hat{r}_{A}(y))<t.

So we have
\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])<t<\hat{r}_{[A,B]}([x,y])
, this is a contradiction, similarly we can prove the case of
\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}([x,y])>\hat{\omega}_{[A,B]}(x)
. We use the similar method of
\rho_{[A,B]}([x,y])>\rho_{[A,B]}(y)
. Hence
[A,B]
is CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
.
∎

4.
Anti-homomorphisms of complex intuitionistic fuzzy brackets

Definition 4.1
.

[
5
]

If
\phi:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
is a linear map between lie superalgebras
\mathbf{V},\ \mathbf{V}^{\prime}
which satisfies:

(1)

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{V}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{\prime}_{\alpha},\ (\alpha=0,1),

(2)

\displaystyle\phi([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-[\phi(x),\phi(y)]

Then
\phi
is called an anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras.

Definition 4.2
.

[
5
]

Let
\mathbf{V}
,
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
be
K
-vector spaces and let
f:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
be any map. If
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
,
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
are CIF vector subspaces of
\mathbf{V}
and
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
, respectively, then the preimage of
B
under
f
is defined to be a CIF set
f^{-1}(B)=(\lambda_{f^{-1}(B)},\rho_{f^{-1}(B)})
, where
\lambda_{f^{-1}(B)}(x)=\lambda_{B}(f(x))
and
\rho_{f^{-1}(B)}(x)=\rho_{B}(f(x))
for any
x\in\mathbf{V}
and the image of
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
under
f
is defined to be the CIF set
f(A)=(\lambda_{f(A)},\rho_{f(A)})
where

\lambda_{f(A)}(y)=\begin{cases}\underset{x\in f^{-1}(y)}{\sup}\{\lambda_{A}(x)\}=\underset{x\in f^{-1}(y)}{\sup}\{r_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\omega_{A}(x)}\}&:\quad y\in f(\mathbf{V})\\
0&:\quad y\not\in f(\mathbf{V})\end{cases}

and

\rho_{f(A)}(y)=\begin{cases}\underset{x\in f^{-1}(y)}{\inf}\{\rho_{A}(x)\}=\underset{x\in f^{-1}(y)}{\inf}\{\hat{r}_{A}(x)e^{i2\pi\hat{\omega}_{A}(x)}\}&:\quad y\in f(\mathbf{V})\\
1&:\quad y\not\in f(\mathbf{V})\end{cases}

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
,
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be any two CIF ideals of
\mathbf{V}
, and
let
\phi:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
be a surjective anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras. Then the CIF set
\phi([A,B])
of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
and
\phi([A,B])\subseteq[\phi(A),\phi(B)]
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.13
,
[A,B]
is a CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
and by
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
,
\phi([A,B])
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
. To complete the proof we need to show that
[\phi(A),\phi(B)]\subseteq\phi([A,B])
. Let
y\in\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi([A,B])}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{y=\phi(x)}\{\lambda_{[A,B]}(x)\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{y=\phi(x)}\{\sup\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\lambda_{A}(x_{i})\wedge\lambda_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[\phi(x_{i}),\phi(y_{i})]}\{\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\lambda_{A}(x_{i})\wedge\lambda_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\},

for each
i\in N
, let
m_{i}=\phi(x_{i})
and
n_{i}=\phi(y_{i})
. Then
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(m_{i})=\sup_{m_{i}=\phi(x)}\{\lambda_{A}(x)\}\geq\lambda_{A}(x_{i})
, similarly,
\lambda_{\phi(A)}(n_{i})\geq\lambda_{A}(y_{i})
. So

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi([A,B])}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[\phi(x_{i}),\phi(y_{i})]}\{\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\lambda_{A}(x_{i})\wedge\lambda_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[m_{i},n_{i}]}\{\min\limits_{i\in N}\{\lambda_{\phi(A)}(m_{i})\wedge\lambda_{\phi(B)}(n_{i})\}\}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{[\phi(A),\phi(B)]}(y).

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi([A,B])}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y=\phi(x)}\{\rho_{[A,B]}(x)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y=\phi(x)}\{\inf\limits_{x=\sum\limits_{i\in N}\alpha_{i}[x_{i},y_{i}]}\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\rho_{A}(x_{i})\vee\rho_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[\phi(x_{i}),\phi(y_{i})]}\{\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\rho_{A}(x_{i})\vee\rho_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\},

for each
i\in N
, let
m_{i}=\phi(x_{i})
and
n_{i}=\phi(y_{i})
. Then
\rho_{\phi(A)}(m_{i})=\inf_{m_{i}=\phi(x)}\{\rho_{A}(x)\}\leq\rho_{A}(x_{i})
, similarly,
\rho_{\phi(A)}(n_{i})\leq\rho_{A}(y_{i})
. So

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi([A,B])}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[\phi(x_{i}),\phi(y_{i})]}\{\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\rho_{A}(x_{i})\vee\rho_{B}(y_{i})\}\}\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\limits_{y=\sum\limits_{i\in N}(-\alpha_{i})[m_{i},n_{i}]}\{\max\limits_{i\in N}\{\rho_{\phi(A)}(m_{i})\vee\rho_{\phi(B)}(n_{i})\}\}

\displaystyle=\rho_{[\phi(A),\phi(B)]}(y).

Hence
\phi([A,B])\subseteq[\phi(A),\phi(B)]
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\phi:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
be a surjective anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras, and Let
B=(\lambda_{B},\rho_{B})
be any CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
. Then, for any
\alpha\in K
, the CIF set
\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)=\alpha\phi^{-1}(B)
of
V
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
.

Proof.

We already proved in
[
5
, Proposition 4.3]
that
\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
. Therefore the only thing we need to prove is that
\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)=\alpha\phi^{-1}(B)
. Let
x\in\mathbf{V}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)}(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\alpha B}(\phi(x))=\lambda_{B}(\alpha^{-1}\phi(x))

\displaystyle=\lambda_{B}(\phi(\alpha^{-1}x))=\lambda_{\phi^{-1}(B)}(\alpha^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\alpha\phi^{-1}(B)}(x)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)}(x)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha B}(\phi(x))=\rho_{B}(\alpha^{-1}\phi(x))

\displaystyle=\rho_{B}(\phi(\alpha^{-1}x))=\rho_{\phi^{-1}(B)}(\alpha^{-1}x)

\displaystyle=\rho_{\alpha\phi^{-1}(B)}(x).

Hence,
\phi^{-1}(\alpha B)=\alpha\phi^{-1}(B)
.
∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\phi:\mathbf{V}\rightarrow\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
be a surjective anti-homomorphism of lie-superalgebras, and Let
A=(\lambda_{A},\rho_{A})
be any CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}
. Then, for any
\alpha\in K
, the CIF set
\phi(\alpha A)=\alpha\phi(A)
of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We already proved in
[
5
, Proposition 4.4]
that
\phi(\alpha A)
is an anti-CIF ideal of
\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
. Therefore the only thing we need to prove is that
\phi(\alpha A)=\alpha\phi(A)
. Let
y\in\mathbf{V}^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\phi(\alpha A)}(y)

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{x\in\phi^{-1}(y)}\{\lambda_{\alpha A}(x)\}=\sup\limits_{x\in\phi^{-1}(y)}\{\lambda_{A}(\alpha^{-1}(x))\}

\displaystyle=\sup\limits_{\alpha^{-1}x\in\phi^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}y)}\{\lambda_{A}(\alpha^{-1}(x))\}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{\phi(A)}(\alpha^{-1}y)=\lambda_{\alpha\phi(A)}(y)

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi(\alpha A)}(y)

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{x\in\phi^{-1}(y)}\{\rho_{\alpha A}(x)\}=\inf\limits_{x\in\phi^{-1}(y)}\{\rho_{A}(\alpha^{-1}(x))\}

\displaystyle=\inf\limits_{\alpha^{-1}x\in\phi^{-1}(\alpha^{-1}y)}\{\rho_{A}(\alpha^{-1}(x))\}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\phi(A)}(\alpha^{-1}y)=\rho_{\alpha\phi(A)}(y).

Hence,
\phi(\alpha A)=\alpha\phi(A)
.
∎

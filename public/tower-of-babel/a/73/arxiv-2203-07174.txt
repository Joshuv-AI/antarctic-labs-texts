Cohomological supports of tensor products of modules over commutative rings
Srikanth B. Iyengar, Josh Pollitz, William T. Sanders

Abstract
. This works concerns cohomological support varieties of modules over commutative local rings. The main result is that the support of a derived tensor product of a pair of differential graded modules over a Koszul complex is the join of the supports of the modules. This generalizes, and gives another proof of, a result of Dao and the third author dealing with Tor-independent modules over complete intersection rings. The result for Koszul complexes has a broader applicability, including to exterior algebras over local rings.

Introduction

Throughout we fix a Koszul complex
E
over a (commutative noetherian) local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
on a list of elements
\bm{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
in
\mathfrak{m}.
As explained in
[
25
]
studying the homological properties of differential graded (abbreviated to dg)
E
-modules allows one to unify and extend the results about quotients
R\to R/(\bm{f})
when
\bm{f}
is an
R
-regular sequence as well as those about exterior algebras over
R
. The dg
E
-modules perfect when regarded as
R
-complexes—in the sense that they are quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite rank free
R
-modules—are the ones that exhibit especially structured homological phenomena; see, for example,
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
16
,
20
,
26
]
. The homological properties of such a dg
E
-module
M
are often encoded in its cohomological support, denoted
\V_{E}(M)
, which is a naturally associated Zariski closed subset of
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{c-1};
cf.
5.2
.

The main result of this article is the following.

Theorem
.

For dg
E
-modules
M,N
that are perfect over
R
there is an equality

\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N))\,.

The join of closed subsets
U,V
of
\mathbb{P}_{k}^{c-1}
, denoted
\operatorname{Join}(U,V)
, is the closure of the union of lines connecting a point from
U
to a point from
V
; see
1.1
for details. Specializing the theorem above to the case where
R
is a regular ring,
\bm{f}
is an
R
-regular sequence, and
M,N
are finitely generated
R/(\bm{f})
-modules satisfying
\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 1
recovers
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
. The proof in
loc. cit.
involves a series reductions and ad hoc geometric arguments. Besides generalizing this result, a main point of this article is to offer a simpler proof by a passage to an exterior algebra, as briefly described below.

The theorem above is proved in Section
6
. As a corollary, we deduce that when R is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N)
is perfect as an
R
-complex, there is an equality

\V_{E}(\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N))=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N))\,.

This is Corollary
6.4
and it generalizes
[
15
, Theorem 4.7]
. Theorem
6.6
relates the support of the dg module
M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N
to that of its homology modules, namely,
\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N)
, thereby providing a positive answer to
[
15
, Question 2]
.

The key ingredient in our work is a functor, denoted
\mathsf{t}
, from the derived category of dg
E
-modules
{\mathsf{D}}(E)
to the derived category of dg
\Lambda
-modules
{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)
where
\Lambda
is an exterior algebra on
{\sf\Sigma}k^{c}
; see
Section

5
. The relevance of this functor arises from
Lemma

5.3
which identifies
\V_{E}(M)
with
\V_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{t}M)
, and that as dg
\Lambda
-modules

\mathsf{t}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)\simeq\mathsf{t}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{t}N\,.

The expression for
\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)
in the theorem above is a consequence of
Proposition

4.4
that asserts if
X,Y
are dg
\Lambda
-modules with finite dimensional homology, then

\V_{\Lambda}(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}Y)=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{\Lambda}(X),\V_{\Lambda}(Y))\,.

This equality is in turn deduced using a contravariant version, from
[
2
]
, of the Bernstein-Gelfand-Gelfand correspondence functor:

\mathsf{d}\colon{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)\to{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{S})\,,

where
\mathcal{S}
is the symmetric algebra on
{\sf\Sigma}^{-2}k^{c}
. The main calculation in the proof of (
\dagger
) is the interaction between tensor products and the functor
\mathsf{d}
, namely: Given dg
\Lambda
-modules
X,Y
with homology finite dimensional over
k
, there is an isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

\mathsf{d}(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}Y)\simeq\mathsf{d}X\otimes_{k}\mathsf{d}Y\,,

where the right-hand side is regarded as a dg
\mathcal{S}
-module through a natural map of
k
-algebras
\Delta\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
, which makes
\mathcal{S}
into a Hopf algebra; see
Eq.

1.1.1
. Given this result, (
\dagger
) follows by a standard argument concerning supports of modules over polynomial rings, discussed in
Section

1
; see especially
Lemma

1.4
. The isomorphism above, which is folklore, is contained in
Proposition

4.4
.

1.
Joins and supports

Let
k
be a field. In what follows we encounter graded
k
-vector spaces
W
whose natural grading is lower and also those whose natural grading is upper. It is convenient to adopt the convention that
W
has both an upper and a lower grading, with
W^{i}=W_{-i}
for each integer
i
. We indicate the primary grading when necessary.

Fix a finite dimensional graded
k
-space
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{W^{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
concentrated in positive even degrees. Let
\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Sym}_{k}W
be the symmetric algebra (over
k
) on
W
and
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
the set of homogeneous prime ideals of
\mathcal{S}
not containing the irrelevant maximal ideal
\mathcal{S}^{>0}
of
\mathcal{S}
, equipped with the Zariski topology. In this section we recall some basics on joins of closed subsets of
\operatorname{Proj}{\mathcal{S}}
and of supports of graded
\mathcal{S}
-modules. Our standard references are
[
18
, Section 1.3]
, for joins, and
[
19
]
, for supports.

1.1
.

The map
W\to W\oplus W
given by
w\mapsto(w,0)+(0,w)
induces a map

(1.1.1)

\Delta\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}

of graded
k
-algebras and makes
\mathcal{S}
into a graded Hopf algebra over
k
. It also defines a rational map

\delta\colon\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})\dashrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}

that is defined (and regular) off of the anti-diagonal
D
in
\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
; here
D
is the image of the embedding
\operatorname{Proj}{\mathcal{S}}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
determined by the map
W\oplus W\to W
given by
(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto w_{1}+w_{2}
.

Given closed subsets
U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{I})
and
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{J})
of
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
, consider

J(U,V)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{I}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{J})

viewed as a closed subset of
\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
. The
join
of
U
and
V
, denoted
\operatorname{Join}(U,V)
, is the closure in
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
of the set

\delta\left(J(U,V)\smallsetminus D\right)\,.

When
k
is algebraically closed, the Nullstellensatz identifies
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
with projective space
\mathbb{P}^{d-1}_{k}
where
d=\dim_{k}W.
Under this identification, the join of
U
and
V
is the closure of the union of lines in
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
containing a point
u
in
U
and a point
v
in
V
.

Remark 1.2
.

The join can also be defined as follows: Consider the rational map

\delta^{\prime}\colon\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})\dashrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\,,

that is regular of the diagonal in
\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
, induced by the
k
-algebra map
\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}

determined by
w\mapsto w\otimes 1-1\otimes w.
The linear automorphism
\alpha
of
\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
determined by

w\otimes 1\mapsto w\otimes 1\quad\text{and}\quad 1\otimes w\mapsto-1\otimes w

fixes
J(U,V)
for any pair of closed subsets
U,V
of
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{\mathcal{}}S
, maps
D
bijectively to
D^{\prime}
and
\delta=\delta^{\prime}\alpha
.
Hence

\delta(J(U,V)\smallsetminus D)=\delta^{\prime}(J(U,V)\smallsetminus D^{\prime})

where the right-hand side is the definition of the join used in
[
18
, Section 1.3]
. That is the definitions of joins from
loc. cit.
and
1.1
coincide. We opt for the latter as the isomorphism in
Proposition

4.4
respects
\Delta
.

1.3
.

Let
X
be a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module. The
support of
X
over
\mathcal{S}
is the subset

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\mid X_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}\,,

where
X_{\mathfrak{p}}
denotes the homogeneous localization of
X
at
\mathfrak{p}
. Following Foxby
[
19
]
, the
small support of
X
is

\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\mid X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0\}\,,

where
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is the graded field
\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Consider the closed subset

\mathcal{V}(\ann_{\mathcal{S}}X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\mid\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\ann_{\mathcal{S}}X\}

of
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
. In general there are inclusions

(1.3.1)

\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\ann_{\mathcal{S}}X)\,.

Moreover,
\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X
is the specialization closure of
\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X;
see
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
.
Equalities hold when the
\mathcal{S}
-module
X
is finitely generated.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
X,Y
be finitely generated graded
\mathcal{S}
-modules. There is an equality

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)=\operatorname{Join}(\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X,\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}Y)

where
X\otimes_{k}Y
is regarded as a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module via
Eq.

1.1.1
.

Proof.

As a matter of notation, we write
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}
for
\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
and use
\overline{(-)}
for closure in the Zariski topology.
For any finite generated
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}
-module
N
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
one has

N\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\simeq N\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\,.

This leads to the following equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}N

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{supp}_{{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}}(N)\cap\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}({\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{supp}_{{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}}(N)\cap(\delta^{-1}(\mathfrak{p})\smallsetminus D)\neq\emptyset

\displaystyle\iff\mathfrak{p}\in\delta(\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}(N)\smallsetminus D)\,.

Applying this observation to
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}X\otimes_{k}Y
justifies the last equality below:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Join}(\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}X,\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}Y)

\displaystyle=\overline{\delta(\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)\smallsetminus D)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\delta(\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)\smallsetminus D)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)}\,.

The first equality holds as
X,Y
are finitely generated over
\mathcal{S}
, whilst the second equality holds because
X\otimes_{k}Y
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}
. Thus, for the desired statement, it suffices to verify that

\overline{\operatorname{supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)}=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)\,.

To that end, given
1.3
, it suffices to verify that
\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)
is closed in
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
. As an
\mathcal{S}
-module
X\otimes_{k}Y
need not be finitely generated, but it is finitely generated over
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}
, and that suffices.

Indeed, let
G
be a finite generating set for
X\otimes_{k}Y
over
\mathcal{S}^{\mathsf{e}}
and
T
the
\mathcal{S}
-submodule of
X\otimes_{k}Y
generated by
G
; here
\mathcal{S}
is acts via the diagonal map
Eq.

1.1.1
.
Since
T
is finitely generated over
\mathcal{S}
, one gets the first equality below:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(\ann_{\mathcal{S}}T)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}T

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\ann_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}(\ann_{\mathcal{S}}T)\,.

The containments are from
1.3
; the last equality holds as
\ann_{\mathcal{S}}(X\otimes_{k}Y)=\ann_{\mathcal{S}}T
. Thus the inclusions above are equalities, as desired.
∎

2.
Dg modules over graded algebras

Let
A=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a strictly graded-commutative dg algebra. Its homology algebra,
\operatorname{H}(A)
, is thus also strictly graded-commutative.

2.1
.

A dg
A
-module
F
is
semifree
provided it admits an exhaustive filtration

0=F(-1)\subseteq F(0)\subseteq F(1)\subseteq\ldots\subseteq F

where each subquotient
F(i)/F(i-1)
is a coproduct of suspensions of
A
. A
semifree
resolution of a dg
A
-module
M
is a surjective quasi-isomorphism of dg A-modules
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
where
F
is a semifree dg A-module. Such resolutions of
M
exist and any two are unique up to homotopy equivalence; see, for example,
[
17
, 6.6]
.

2.2
.

Let
M
be a dg
A
-module and fix
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
a semifree resolution over
A
. By
[
17
, ]
, the functors
F\otimes_{A}-
and
\Hom_{A}(F,-)
preserve (surjective) quasi-isomorphisms. Hence by replacing objects with their semifree resolutions we obtain bi-functors
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}-
and
\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(-,-)
on
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
; that is to say,

M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}-\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}F\otimes_{A}-\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(M,-)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{A}(F,-)\,.

As usual, we set

\Tor^{A}_{*}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{H}_{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{A}N)\quad\text{ and }\quad\Ext_{A}^{*}(M,N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{H}^{*}(\operatorname{RHom}_{A}(M,N))\,.

As
A
is graded-commutative these are graded
\operatorname{H}(A)
-modules.

2.3
.

The derived category of dg
A
-modules is denoted
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
, and it is regarded as a triangulated category in the standard way; see, for example,
[
5
, Section 2]
. The suspension functor associates to each dg
A
-module
M
the dg module
{\sf\Sigma}M
with

({\sf\Sigma}M)_{i}=M_{i-1},\quad\partial^{{\sf\Sigma}M}=-\partial^{M}\quad\text{ and }\quad a\cdot{\mathtt{\Sigma}}m=(-1)^{|a|}am\,

where
|a|
denotes the degree
a
. A
thick
subcategory of a triangulated category is a triangulated subcategory that is closed under retracts.

2.4
.

Let
A
be a dg algebra over a field
k
.
We define several thick subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
that will be of interest in what follows.

Let
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(A)
denote the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
consisting of dg
A
-modules
M
with each
\operatorname{H}_{i}(M)
finite dimensional and
\operatorname{H}_{i}(M)=0
for all
i\ll 0;
define
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(A)
analogously where the second condition is replaced with
\operatorname{H}_{i}(M)=0
for all
i\gg 0
. We let
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(A)
denote
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(A)\cap{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(A).
That is,
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(A)
consists exactly of those dg
A
-modules whose homology is finite dimensional over
k
. We write
\mathsf{Perf}(A)
for the thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(A)
generated by
A
; see
[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
for an alternative characterization.

3.
Exterior algebras

In this section
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{V_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a finite graded
k
-space concentrated in positive odd degrees. Set
(-)^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Hom_{k}(-,k)
, the graded dual, and
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\sf\Sigma}^{-1}(V^{\vee})
.
Let

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigwedge_{k}V\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Sym}_{k}W\,;

the former being the exterior algebra, over
k
, on
V
. Set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\mathcal{S}}^{\vee}
with the standard
\mathcal{S}
-module structure: For
\alpha\in\Gamma
and
\chi\in\mathcal{S}
, one has

\chi\cdot\alpha\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\alpha(\chi\cdot-)\,.

We view
\Lambda
as a graded Hopf algebra, with coproduct
\Lambda\to\Lambda\otimes_{k}\Lambda
induced by the map of
k
-spaces
v\mapsto(v,1)+(1,v)
, for
v\in V
. Hence for any left dg
\Lambda
-module the antipode defines a dg
\Lambda
-module structure on
M^{\vee}
. Also, for a pair of dg
\Lambda
-modules
M,N
, their tensor product
M\otimes_{k}N
is regarded as a dg
\Lambda
-module through the coproduct. See, for example,
[
7
, Remark 5.2]
. We also view
\mathcal{S}
as a graded Hopf algebra over
k
, with coproduct defined in
1.1
.

Notation 3.1
.

Fix a basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{c}
for
V
, and let
\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{c}
be the dual basis for
W
; thus
\chi_{i}
has lower degree
-|e_{i}|-1
. These determine isomorphisms

\Lambda\cong\bigwedge\left(ke_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus ke_{c}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{S}\cong k[\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{c}]\,.

3.2
.

For a dg
\Lambda
-module
M
, its
universal resolution

\mathsf{u}M
is the dg
(\Lambda\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
-module with underlying graded
(\Lambda\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
-module
\Lambda\otimes_{k}\Gamma\otimes_{k}M
, with
\Lambda\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
acting by left multiplication on the two left factors, and differential

1\otimes 1\otimes\partial^{M}+\sum_{i=1}^{c}(1\otimes\chi_{i}\otimes e_{i}-e_{i}\otimes\chi_{i}\otimes 1)\,.

The canonical projection
\mathsf{u}M\to M
is a semifree resolution of
M
over
\Lambda
; see
[
3
, Proposition 2.6]
or
[
5
, Section 7]
. Moreover, since
\mathsf{u}M
is a dg module over
\Lambda\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
, the graded
k
-space
\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,-)
retains a dg
\mathcal{S}
-module structure and so

\Ext_{\Lambda}^{*}(M,-)=\operatorname{H}^{*}(\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,-))

is a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module.

3.3
.

Let
M
be dg
\Lambda
-module with
M_{i}
degreewise finite dimensional over
k
for each
i
and
0
for
i\ll 0
; up to quasi-isomorphism any object in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)
has this form. There is an isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

(3.3.1)

\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,k)\cong\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}M^{\vee}

where the right-hand term has differential
1\otimes\partial^{M^{\vee}}+\sum_{i=1}^{c}\chi_{i}\otimes e_{i};
we denote the dg
\mathcal{S}
-module on the right by
\mathcal{S}_{M}
. From this isomorphism and
[
25
, Proposition 1.2.8]
,
\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,k)
is a semifree dg
\mathcal{S}
-module.

The contravariant functor
\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}(-),k)
induces the exact functor

\mathsf{d}\colon{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)^{\textsf{op}}\to{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{S})\,.

By
[
2
]
, this restricts to an exact equivalence

\mathsf{d}\colon{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)^{\textsf{op}}\xrightarrow{\equiv}{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(\mathcal{S})\,,

that further restricts to equivalences

{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)^{\textsf{op}}\xrightarrow{\equiv}\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{S})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{Perf}(\Lambda)^{\textsf{op}}\xrightarrow{\equiv}{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\mathcal{S})\,.

One has also the functor

that induces an exact functor

\mathsf{b}\colon{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)\to{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{S})\,

which restricts to equivalences

{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\equiv}\mathsf{Perf}(\mathcal{S})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathsf{Perf}(\Lambda)\xrightarrow{\equiv}{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\mathcal{S})\,.

cf.
[
5
]
. There is the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)^{\textsf{op}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(\mathcal{S})\,.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(\Lambda)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{d}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{b}}

3.4
.

The functors

defined above determine two notions of cohomological support for dg
\Lambda
-modules. Namely, for a dg
\Lambda
-module

, consider subsets of
\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(M)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{b}M)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\Ext_{\Lambda}(k,M)

\displaystyle\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\Ext_{\Lambda}(M,k)\,.

In
[
14
]
, the supports
\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(-)
are used to classify the thick subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
.
Our focus will be on
\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(-)
but it is worth recording their relationship.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
M
be in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)
. There is an equality
\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(M^{\vee})
.
Moreover if

{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
, then
\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(M)

Proof.

The first equality is immediate from
\mathsf{b}((-)^{\vee})=\mathsf{d}
; see

. The second equality follows from the first. Indeed, it is easy to check that if
N
is in the thick subcategory generated by

then

\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(N)\subseteq\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{b}}(N^{\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N)\subseteq\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N^{\prime})\,.

When
M
is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
, the dg
\Lambda
-modules
M
and

generate the same thick subcategory—see
[
23
, Section 4]
—so the second equality follows from the first.
∎

4.
Support for tensor products, I

As in the previous section,
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{V_{i}\}_{i>0}
is a finite graded
k
-space concentrated in positive even degrees, and

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigwedge V\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Sym}_{k}W\,,

where
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\sf\Sigma}^{-1}(V^{\vee})
. In this section we analyze the interaction between the functor
\mathsf{d}\colon{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)^{\text{op}}\to{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{-}(\mathcal{S})
, from
3.3
, and the tensor products
\otimes_{k}
and
\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}
. The main results,
Proposition

4.2
and
Proposition

4.4
, are folklore but we could not find adequate references, so we give complete proofs; see also
Remark

4.3
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
{\mathcal{S}}
-modules
X,Y
with finitely generated homology,

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{S}Y)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(X)\cap\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(Y)\,.

Proof.

Since
X,Y
have finitely generated homology, there are equalities

\displaystyle\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(X)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\mid X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\not\simeq 0\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(Y)

\displaystyle=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}\mid Y\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\not\simeq 0\}\,.

See, for instance,
[
14
, Theorem 2.4]
. Since
\mathcal{S}
has finite global dimension, the
\mathcal{S}
-module
\operatorname{H}(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}Y)
is also finitely generated and so

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}Y)=\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Proj}{\mathcal{S}}\mid X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}Y\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\not\simeq 0\}\,.

The desired equality follows from the ones above and the isomorphism

X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}Y\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\simeq(X\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(Y\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\,.\qed

The result below records the relationship between
\mathsf{d}
and tensor products.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
M,N
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)
, there is an isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

\mathsf{d}(M\otimes_{k}N)\simeq\mathsf{d}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{d}N\,.

Furthermore if
M,N
are in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
, then

\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M\otimes_{k}N)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M)\cap\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N)\,.

Proof.

Replacing
M
and
N
with semifree resolutions over
\Lambda
, we may assume both
M
and
N
are bounded below and degreewise finite dimensional over
k
, as in
3.3
. Let
\Phi
denote the composition of the isomorphisms of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}M^{\vee})\otimes_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}N^{\vee})\longrightarrow(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{S})\otimes_{k}M^{\vee}\otimes_{k}N^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}M^{\vee}\otimes_{k}N^{\vee}

where the first one is the twist isomorphism given by

(s\otimes\alpha)\otimes(s^{\prime}\otimes\beta)\mapsto(s\otimes s^{\prime})\otimes(\alpha\otimes\beta)

and the second map is the multiplication isomorphism. It is straightforward to see

\Phi\circ\sum_{i=1}^{c}(\chi_{i}\otimes e_{i})\otimes 1+1\otimes(\chi_{i}\otimes e_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{c}\chi_{i}\otimes(e_{i}\otimes 1+1\otimes e_{i})\circ\Phi\,.

As
M,N
are degreewise finite dimensional and bounded below, there is a natural isomorphism of dg
\Lambda
-modules

(M\otimes_{k}N)^{\vee}\cong M^{\vee}\otimes_{k}N^{\vee}\,.

Hence
\Phi
yields an isomorphism

{\mathcal{S}}_{M}\otimes_{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\xrightarrow{\cong}{\mathcal{S}}_{M\otimes_{k}N}\,.

As a consequence,
Eq.

3.3.1
establishes the isomorphisms in
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{S})
:

(4.2.1)

\mathsf{d}(M\otimes_{k}N)\simeq\mathsf{d}M\otimes_{\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{d}N\simeq\mathsf{d}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{d}N\,.

As for the statement regarding supports, consider the following equalities

\displaystyle\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M\otimes_{k}N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}(M\otimes_{k}N))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{d}N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M)\cap\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}^{*}(\mathsf{d}N)

\displaystyle=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M)\cap\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N)\,.

The second equality is from
Eq.

4.2.1
, while the third is
Lemma

4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Buchweitz proved that if
M,N
are graded
\Lambda
-modules that are bounded below and are degreewise finite rank over
k
, then

(4.3.1)

\mathsf{b}(M\otimes_{k}N)\simeq\mathsf{b}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\mathcal{S}}\mathsf{b}N\,;

see
[
13
, (9.4.10)]
. It is easy to see that this isomorphism holds for all pairs of objects in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)
. From the equality
\mathsf{b}((-)^{\vee})=\mathsf{d}
the isomorphisms in
Eq.

4.3.1
can also be deduced from (and imply) the ones in
Proposition

4.2
.

Proposition 4.4
.

For
M,N
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{+}(\Lambda)
there is an isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

\mathsf{d}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N)\simeq\mathsf{d}M\otimes_{k}\mathsf{d}N

where the right hand side is a dg
\mathcal{S}
-module through the diagonal
\Delta\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
described in
Eq.

1.1.1
. Furthermore if
M,N
are in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
, then

\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N)=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M),\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N))\,.

Proof.

Replacing
M
and
N
with suitable resolutions we can assume
M
and
N
are bounded above, degreewise finite dimensional over
k
, and semifree. As in
3.2
we consider
\Gamma={\mathcal{S}}^{\vee}
, regarded as a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module. Forgetting differentials one has a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{u}M\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathsf{u}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathsf{u}(M\otimes_{\Lambda}N)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda\otimes_{k}\Gamma^{\otimes 2}\otimes_{k}M\otimes_{k}N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda\otimes_{k}\Gamma\otimes_{k}(M\otimes_{\Lambda}N)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\otimes\mu\otimes\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

of graded
\mathcal{S}
-modules, where the map on the bottom is defined using the multiplication
\mu\colon\Gamma\otimes_{k}\Gamma\to\Gamma
map which is dual to the diagonal
\Delta\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S}
in
Eq.

1.1.1
, and
\pi\colon M\otimes_{k}N\to M\otimes_{\Lambda}N
is the canonical projection. It is straightforward to check
\Phi
is a
\Lambda
-linear morphism of complexes that is compatible with the canonical augmentations to
M\otimes_{\Lambda}N.
Thus
\Phi
is a comparison map between semifree resolutions of
M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N
over
\Lambda
, and so it is a homotopy equivalence.

Applying
\Hom_{\Lambda}(-,k)
to
\Phi
yields the top map in the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}(M\otimes_{\Lambda}N),k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathsf{u}N,k)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{S}}_{M\otimes_{\Lambda}N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{S}}_{M}\otimes_{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta\otimes\pi^{*}}

of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules, where
{\mathcal{S}}_{M}\otimes_{k}{\mathcal{S}}_{N}
is viewed as a dg
\mathcal{S}
-module through
\Delta
. The vertical parallel maps are isomorphisms by
Eq.

3.3.1
; the one on the right also uses the standard isomorphisms

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M\otimes_{\Lambda}\mathsf{u}N,k)

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}N,k))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}M,k)\otimes_{k}\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{u}N,k)\,;

This is where the assumption that both
M
and
N
are degreewise finite rank and bounded below is needed. As
\Phi
is a homotopy equivalence, from the commutativity of the diagram above it follows that
\Delta\otimes\pi^{*}
is a homotopy equivalence of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules justifying the first assertion; cf.
3.3
.

With this in hand we have

\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N))\cong\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M\otimes_{k}\mathsf{d}N)\cong\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}N)

where the second map is the Künneth isomorphism. This gives the second of the following equalities

\displaystyle\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}N))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\left(\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M)\otimes_{k}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}N)\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}M),\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}N))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(M),\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(N))\,;

The third equality is
Lemma

1.4
.
∎

5.
Passage to the exterior algebra

Throughout this section and the next
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a commutative noetherian local ring. Fix a list of elements
\bm{f}=f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
in
\mathfrak{m}
and set

E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}R\langle e_{1},\ldots,e_{c}\mid\partial e_{i}=f_{i}\rangle\,,

the Koszul complex on
\bm{f}
over
R
, regarded as a local dg
R
-algebra in the standard way. One could take
R
to be a local dg algebra where
\bm{f}
is a list of cycles in even degrees, contained in the maximal ideal of
R
; we stick to the situation above for ease of exposition. Two special cases are worth mention.

Remark 5.1
.

When
\bm{f}
forms an
R
-regular sequence the augmentation
E\xrightarrow{\simeq}R/(\bm{f})
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg algebras and the map
R\to R/(\bm{f})
is complete intersection. When
\bm{f}
is the zero sequence,
E
is the exterior algebra over
R
on
c
generators of degree one.

Set
\Lambda\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k\otimes_{R}E
and
V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\Lambda_{1}
. We identify
e_{1},\dots,e_{c}
with their images in
\Lambda
; they are a basis for the
k
-space
V
. Set
W\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}{\sf\Sigma}^{-1}(V^{\vee})
, and

\mathcal{S}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Sym}_{k}W\,.

Let
\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{c}
be the basis of
W
dual to
e_{1},\dots,e_{c}
.

5.2
.

Let
M
be a dg
E
-module whose homology is finitely generated over
R
. Let
F
be a dg
E
-module that is semifree as a dg
R
-module, and
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
an
E
-linear quasi-isomorphism. By
[
25
, Proposition 4.2.8]
,
\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,k)
can be equipped with a dg
\mathcal{S}
-module structure through the isomorphism

\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,k)\simeq\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\Hom_{R}(F,k)

where the differential of the complex on the right is

1\otimes\partial^{\Hom_{E}(F,k)}+\sum_{i=1}^{c}\chi_{i}\otimes\Hom(e_{i},k)\,;

we let
\mathcal{C}_{F}
denote this dg
\mathcal{S}
-module. Following
[
24
, Definition 3.3.1]
, the
cohomological support of M over E
is

\V_{E}(M)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\Ext_{E}^{*}(M,k)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}^{*}(\mathcal{C}_{F})\,.

A bridge to exterior algebras has been used effectively to acquire cohomological information on these support varieties when
R
is regular and
\bm{f}
is an
R
-regular sequence; see, for instance,
[
7
,
14
,
23
]
. This path is still sensible at this generality and can be used to establish results over
E
, as we do now.

Consider the functor
\mathsf{t}\colon{\mathsf{D}}(E)\to{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)
given by
k\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{R}-
. In the statement below, the construction of the dg
\mathcal{S}
-module
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathsf{t}F}
is given in
3.3

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M
be a dg
E
-module with finitely generated homology over
R
and fix
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}M
a quasi-isomorphism of dg
E
-modules where
F
is semifree when regarded as dg
R
-module. There is the following isomorphism of dg
\mathcal{S}
-modules

\mathcal{C}_{F}\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathsf{t}F}\,.

In particular
\V_{E}(M)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}M).

Proof.

For the isomorphism, since
\mathfrak{m}\Hom_{R}(F,k)=0
the
E
-action on
\Hom_{R}(F,k)
factors through
\Lambda
. It is immediate to check the adjunction isomorphism

\alpha:\Hom_{R}(F,k)\xrightarrow{\cong}\Hom_{k}(\mathsf{t}F,k)

is one of
\Lambda
-modules. Therefore from the definitions of
\mathcal{C}_{F}
and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathsf{t}F}

is an isomorphism of dg

\displaystyle\V_{E}(M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{C}_{F})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathsf{t}F})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}({\mathcal{S}}_{\mathsf{t}M})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{H}(\mathsf{d}\mathsf{t}M)

\displaystyle=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}M)\,;

the second equality holds by the established isomorphism above and the others are clear from the various definitions.
∎

.

Suppose
\bm{f}
is an

Let

The equivalence of

, and

6.
Support for tensor products, II

The notation in this section is as in the previous one. The result below is the theorem announced in the introduction.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose
E
is a Koszul complex over a local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
on a finite list of elements in
\mathfrak{m}.
For
M,N
in
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
,

\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N))\,.

Proof.

We need only pass to the exterior algebra:

\displaystyle\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)

\displaystyle=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N))

\displaystyle=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{t}N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}M),\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}N))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N))\,.

The first and fourth equalities hold by
Lemma

5.3
; the second one follows from the isomorphism

\mathsf{t}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)\simeq\mathsf{t}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{\Lambda}\mathsf{t}N\,.

By
Proposition

5.6
, the dg
\Lambda
-modules
\mathsf{t}M,\mathsf{t}N
are in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\sf{f}}_{\sf{b}}(\Lambda)
and so the third equality holds by
Proposition

4.4
.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

There is an alternative proof of
Theorem

6.1
, using the Hopf algebra structure on
\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)
. The key point is that for any dg
E
-modules the maps

(6.2.1)

\Ext_{E}^{*}(M,k)\otimes_{k}\Ext_{E}^{*}(N,k)\to\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}k)\to\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)

are
\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)
-linear. The second map is induced by multiplication,
k\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}k\to k.
In
Eq.

6.2.1
the graded Ext-module on the left is given an
\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)
-module structure through the diagonal

\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)\to\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)\otimes_{k}\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)\,.

This is a straightforward calculation. However this approach requires a bit of background on dg algebras with divided powers and suitably adapting classical material to this more general setting; cf.
[
21
]
. The main point is that
\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)
is generated, as a
k
-algebra, by primitives induced by derivations that respect divided powers on the minimal semifree resolution of
k
over
E
.

One can identify
\mathcal{S}
as a Hopf subalgebra of
\Ext_{E}^{*}(k,k)
so the maps in
Eq.

6.2.1
are also
\mathcal{S}
-linear. When
M,N
are in
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
, the
\mathcal{S}
-modules
\Ext_{E}^{*}(M,k),\Ext_{E}^{*}(N,k)
are finite over
\mathcal{S}
, see
Proposition

5.6
, so the assertion of
Theorem

6.1
follows directly from
Lemma

1.4
once noting the composition in
Eq.

6.2.1
is an isomorphism.

Consider the equivalence

(-)^{\dagger}\colon{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})^{\textsf{op}}\longrightarrow{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})

where
M^{\dagger}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,E)
for each
M
.

Lemma 6.3
.

If
M
is in
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
, then
\V_{E}(M)=\V_{E}(M^{\dagger}).

Proof.

As
M
is perfect over
R
there is an isomorphism of dg
\Lambda
-modules

\mathsf{t}(M^{\dagger})\simeq\operatorname{RHom}_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{t}M,\Lambda)\,.

By
[
23
, Theorem 4.1]
,
\mathsf{t}M
and
\operatorname{RHom}_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{t}M,\Lambda)
generate the same thick subcategory in
{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda).
Thus the second equality below holds:

\V_{E}(M)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}M)=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\operatorname{RHom}_{\Lambda}(\mathsf{t}M,\Lambda))=\V_{\Lambda}^{\mathsf{d}}(\mathsf{t}(M^{\dagger}))=\V_{E}(M^{\dagger})\,.\qed

Corollary 6.4
.

If
R
is Gorenstein and
\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N)
belongs to
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
, then

\V_{E}(\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N))=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N)).

Proof.

As
R
is Gorenstein and the
R
-modules
\operatorname{H}(M),\operatorname{H}(N)
are finitely generated, there is an isomorphism

\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N)^{\dagger}\simeq M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N^{\dagger}\,.

Thus the second equality below holds

\displaystyle\V_{E}(\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N))

\displaystyle=\V_{E}(\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N)^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N^{\dagger}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Join}(\V_{E}(M),\V_{E}(N))\,;

the first and fourth equalities are by
Lemma

6.3
and the third is by
Theorem

6.1
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

In light of
Theorem

6.1
, it would be interesting to determine whether
Corollary

6.4
holds without the assumption that
\operatorname{RHom}_{E}(M,N)
is in
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
.

The result below relates the cohomological support of
M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N
to those of its homology modules. Specializing to the case
R
is regular and
\bm{f}
is an
R
-regular sequence, yields a positive answer to
[
15
, Question 2]
. The containment in the statement of the theorem can be strict; see
[
15
, Example 5.3]
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
M,N
be in
{\mathsf{D_{b}}}({E}/{R})
and suppose the
\mathcal{S}
-modules
\Ext_{E}^{*}(M,k)
and
\Ext_{E}^{*}(N,k)
are generated in cohomological degrees at most
s
and
t
, respectively. There is a containment of closed subsets

\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)\subseteq\bigcup_{i\leq s+t}\V_{E}(\Tor_{i}^{E}(M,N))\,.

Proof.

By
Proposition

4.4
and
Lemma

5.3
one may identify
\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)
with

\Ext_{E}^{*}(M,k)\otimes_{k}\Ext_{E}^{*}(N,k)

viewed as a graded
\mathcal{S}
-module via restriction along the diagonal map
Eq.

1.1.1
.
Let
T
denote its graded
\mathcal{S}
-submodule of
\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)
generated by

\bigoplus_{i+j\leqslant u}\Ext_{E}^{i}(M,k)\otimes_{k}\Ext_{E}^{j}(N,k)

where
u=s+t
. The
(\mathcal{S}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{S})
-module generated by
T
is
\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)
, so arguing as in the proof of
Lemma

1.4
, one gets an equality

(6.6.1)

\V_{E}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N)=\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}T\,.

Fix a semifree resolution
F\xrightarrow{\simeq}M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N
over
E
, and let
F^{\prime}
be the soft truncation of
F
in lower degrees at most
u
. Thus there is morphism of dg
E
-modules
\tau\colon F\to F^{\prime}
with the property that

\tau_{\leqslant u}\colon F_{\leqslant u}\to F^{\prime}_{\leqslant u}

is the identity map. Hence

\Ext(\tau,k)\colon\Ext_{E}^{*}(F^{\prime},k)\to\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)

is an isomorphism in upper degrees at most
u
. In particular, under the identification discussed above

T\subseteq\Image\left(\Ext_{E}^{*}(F^{\prime},k)\xrightarrow{\Ext(\tau,k)}\Ext_{E}^{*}(M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N,k)\right)

and hence one has an inclusion

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}T\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\Ext_{E}^{*}(F^{\prime},k)\,.

Since
F^{\prime}
has bounded homology, it is in the thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(E)
generated by
\operatorname{H}(F^{\prime})
regarded as dg
E
-module via the augmentation
E\to\operatorname{H}_{0}(E)
. Thus

\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}T\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathcal{S}}\Ext_{E}\left(\oplus_{i\leqslant u}\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N),k\right)=\bigcup_{i\leqslant u}\V_{E}(\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N))

where for the first containment we are also using the equality

\operatorname{H}(F^{\prime})=\bigoplus_{i\leqslant u}\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N)\,.

Combining this with
Eq.

6.6.1
finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 6.7
.

Theorem

6.6
implies that when
R

M\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{E}N

1

\Tor^{E}_{i}(M,N)
for
i\leq s+t

s
and

6.6
.

s ​ u ​ ( d ) su(d) -squeezing and many-body entanglement geometry in finite-dimensional systems
Giuseppe Vitagliano, Otfried Gühne, Géza Tóth

Abstract
Generalizing the well-known spin-squeezing inequalities, we study the relation between squeezing of collective N N -particle s ​ u ​ ( d ) su(d) operators and many-body entanglement geometry in multi-particle systems. For that aim, we define the set of pseudo-separable states, which are mixtures of products of single-particle states that lie in the ( d 2 − 1 ) (d^{2}-1) -dimensional Bloch sphere but are not necessarily positive semidefinite. We obtain a set of necessary conditions for states of N N qudits to be of the above form. Any state that violates these conditions is entangled. We also define a corresponding s ​ u ​ ( d ) su(d) -squeezing parameter that can be used to detect entanglement in large particle ensembles. Geometrically, this set of conditions defines a convex set of points in the space of first and second moments of the collective N N -particle s ​ u ​ ( d ) su(d) operators. We prove that, in the limit N ≫ 1 N\gg 1 , such set is filled by pseudo-separable states, while any state corresponding to a point outside of this set is necessarily entangled. We also study states that are detected by these inequalities: We show that states with a bosonic symmetry are detected if and only if the two-body reduced state violates the positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion. On the other hand, highly mixed states states close to the s ​ u ​ ( d ) su(d) singlet are detected which have a separable two-body reduced state and are also PPT with respect to all possible bipartitions. We also provide numerical examples of thermal equilibrium states that are detected by our set of inequalities, comparing the spin-squeezing inequalities with the s ​ u ​ ( 3 ) su(3) -squeezing inequalities.

It has been shown in Refs.

(i)

It defines a polytope in the space of

(iii)

After applying orthogonal transformations

3

-squeezing entanglement criteria

We derive here a closed set of inequalities, called the

-squeezing inequalities, which are necessarily satisfied by all pseudo-separable states.

Observation 1
.

For every given
N
-particle
su(d)
generators
\{G_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}
, all inequalities in the following set must hold for every
N
-qudit pseudo-separable state
\varrho

\displaystyle\Tr(\gamma_\varrho)-\sum_{k\in I}(\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho})_{kk}-2N(d-1)\geq 0,

(56)

where
\gamma_{\varrho}
and
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
are defined by
Eqs.

12b
and
15
, and

I
is a subset of
I_{d}:=\{1,2,...,d^{2}-1\},
including the case when
I
is an empty set.

Proof.—
Let us consider a pure product of non-physical states of the type given in Eq. (
42
). For such states, based on Eqs. (
44
) and (
47
), and based on the uncertainty relation given in Eq. (
6
), it follows that

\Tr(\gamma_\varrho)=\sum_{k}(\Delta G_{k})_{\varrho}^{2}=\sum_{k}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Delta g_{k}^{(n)})_{\varrho}^{2}\geq 2N(d-1)

(57)

holds. On the other hand, the diagonal elements of
\mathfrak{U}
are such that

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho})_{kk}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\Delta g_{k}^{(n)})_{\varrho}^{2}+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle(g_{k}^{(n)})^{2}\rangle_{\varrho}+\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{n\neq m}^{N}\langle g_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{\varrho}\langle g_{k}^{(m)}\rangle_{\varrho}-\frac{N}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle(g_{k}^{(n)})^{2}\rangle_{\varrho}

(58)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle g_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{\varrho}^{2}+\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m<n}\langle g_{k}^{(n)}\rangle_{\varrho}\langle g_{k}^{(m)}\rangle_{\varrho}\leq 0,

where the inequality comes from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Thus, the diagonal elements of
\mathfrak{U}
must be non-positive for product states,
hence, all such states must satisfy the inequalities

\displaystyle\Tr(\gamma_\varrho)-\sum_{k\in I}(\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho})_{kk}\geq 2N(d-1)

(59)

for all the possible choices of

To extend the result to mixtures of product states we use the fact that the left-hand side of Eq. (
59
) is a concave function of the state.
In order to see this, based on Eq. (
58

59

\displaystyle\Tr(\gamma_\varrho)-\sum_{k\in I}(\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho})_{kk}=\sum_{k\in I_{d}\backslash I}(\Delta G_{k})_{\varrho}^{2}-\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{k\in I_{d}}\langle{G_{k}^{2}}\rangle_{\varrho}+\frac{N}{N-1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle(g_{k}^{(n)})^{2}\rangle_{\varrho}.

∎

4.4
Relation with Spin-Squeezing polytope

Let us see here how the geometric picture provided above generalizes the spin-squeezing polytope discussed in Refs.
[
46
,
48
]
and reviewed in Sec.
2.2
. Let us start then, by considering the case
d=2
, where the
su(d)
operators reduce to the three spin components. First of all, as we recalled in Sec.
2.2
, the spin-squeezing polytope is defined in the space of
(\langle\tilde{J}_{x}^{2}\rangle,\langle\tilde{J}_{y}^{2}\rangle,\langle\tilde{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle,\langle J_{x}\rangle,\langle J_{y}\rangle,\langle J_{z}\rangle)
. More precisely, the polytope can be better visualized in the space of

\langle\boldsymbol{\tilde{K}}\rangle:=(\langle\tilde{J}_{x}^{2}\rangle,\langle\tilde{J}_{y}^{2}\rangle,\langle\tilde{J}_{z}^{2}\rangle)

(90)

for each fixed value of the vector

\langle\boldsymbol{J}\rangle=(\langle J_{x}\rangle,\langle J_{y}\rangle,\langle J_{z}\rangle).

(91)

Then, the completeness property of the set means, loosely speaking, that the spin-squeezing polytope detects all possible entangled states, given information about
\langle\boldsymbol{\tilde{K}}\rangle
and
\langle\boldsymbol{J}\rangle
.

When employing our
su(d)
-squeezing approach to the
d=2
case, we again fix the value of the vector
\langle\boldsymbol{J}\rangle
, but instead of the second moments we consider as coordinates the following quantities

x_{k}\propto\mathfrak{U}_{kk}=\langle\tilde{J}_{k}^{2}\rangle-\langle J_{k}\rangle^{2}=\langle\tilde{J}_{k}^{2}\rangle-{\rm const.},

(92)

and

x_{d^{2}}\propto\left[\left(N+d\right)\Lambda_{\max}-\tfrac{1}{N}{\rm Tr}(C)\right]=\tfrac{2}{N}\left[\tfrac{N(N+2)}{4}-|\langle\boldsymbol{K}\rangle|^{2}\right],

(93)

where
\langle\boldsymbol{K}\rangle:=(\langle J_{x}^{2}\rangle,\langle J_{y}^{2}\rangle,\langle J_{z}^{2}\rangle),

and we simplified the expressions by not writing the normalization factors explicitly.
Note also that for
d=2
the modified second moments can be written as
\langle\boldsymbol{\tilde{K}}\rangle=\langle\boldsymbol{K}\rangle-N/2
.

Thus, here, we extend the geometrical picture in the
d=2
case from a
3
-dimensional to a
4
-dimensional space for each fixed value of
\langle\boldsymbol{J}\rangle
. However, these
4
coordinates obey the relation

\Lambda=Nx_{4}+x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},

(94)

and thus we still restrict our problem to a
3
-dimensional subspace. Furthermore, the vertices of our polytope in the
d=2
case coincide exactly with the vertices of the spin-squeezing polytope, as in Eq. (
35
).

For higher dimensions, analogously, we fix the value of
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
and consider the space of

\langle\boldsymbol{\tilde{K}}\rangle=(\langle\tilde{G}^{2}_{1}\rangle,\langle\tilde{G}^{2}_{2}\rangle,\dots,\langle\tilde{G}^{2}_{d^{2}-1}\rangle)

(95)

and the additional coordinate
x_{d^{2}}
, together with the constraint given in Eq. (
68
).
The advantage of this is that all our coordinates appear linearly in our parameter, which in turn can be interpreted as a signed distance, as we discussed.

Still, the vertices of our polytope are a direct generalization of Eq. (
35
), where, however, the single particle
matrices are just restricted to lie inside a
(d^{2}-1)
-dimensional Bloch sphere, and thus not necessarily positive.
In fact, in practice most of the vertices correspond to non-physical matrices. For example, in the case
\boldsymbol{G}=(0,\dots,0)
the single-particle components are given by (see
Appendix

D
)

\varrho_{\pm,k}:=\tfrac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}\pm\sqrt{\Lambda_{\max}}\ g_{k},

(96)

and have a negative eigenvalue for many
su(d)
generators
g_{k}
.

5
su(d)
-squeezing and two-body entanglement

Here we show that average two-body correlations provide all information that is needed to compute
\xi_{su(d)}
.
Afterwards, we analyze whether states detected with
\xi_{su(d)}
have also entangled two-body marginals. Analogously as in the case of the spin-squeezing inequalities,
we show that this is the case for permutationally symmetric states, but not in the generic case.

Observation 6
.

The parameter
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)
in Eq. (
61
) can be expressed with average two-body correlations as

\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)=N^{2}\left(\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\lambda_{k}(\gamma_{\rm av2})+\tfrac{2}{N}(1-\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2})\right),

(97)

where
the summation is over all the negative eigenvalues of
\gamma_{\rm av2}.

Proof.—
We start from the equation

\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\left[\left(N+d\right)\Lambda_{\max}-\frac{1}{N}\tr(C_\varrho)\right]=\frac{2}{N}(1-\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2}),

(98)

which relates the correlation matrix of collective quantities to the average two-particle density matrix.
Equation (
98
) can be verified based on
Eq.

29
, which provides a relation between
C_{\varrho}
and
C_{\rm av2}
, and
Eq.

27
, giving a relation between
\Tr(C_{\rm av2})
and
\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2}
. Beside
Eq.

98
, we also need the expression in
Eq.

30
, which gives a connection between
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
and
\gamma_{\rm av2},
to show that the right-hand sides of
Eq.

64
and
Eq.

97
are equal to each other. ∎

Thus, our spin squeezing parameter
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)
depends only on the average two-body density matrix
\varrho_{\rm av2}.
We then ask the question, whether it detects only states that have an entangled
\varrho_{\rm av2}.
We show that this is not the case, and present a family of states that are detected by our criterion, while their average two-body density matrix is separable and can even be very close to the completely mixed state.

Let us now look at the definition of our parameter given in
Eq.

97
. Our entanglement criterion states that

\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)\geq 0

(99)

holds for all
N
-particle separable states. The criterion given in Eq. (
99
) can be rewritten as

\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2}\leq 1+\frac{N}{2}\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\lambda_{k}(\gamma_{\rm av2}),

(100)

where on the right-hand side there is a summation over all the negative eigenvalues of
\gamma_{\rm av2}.

Observation 7
.

For permutationally symmetric states,
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)<0
holds if and only if
\varrho_{\rm av2}
is not PPT.

Proof.—
Let us consider the parameter given in
Eq.

97
. For symmetric multi-qudit states, all two-body reduced states are also symmetric. Thus,
\varrho_{\rm av2}
is also symmetric and hence

\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2}=1.

(101)

For symmetric states
\varrho_{\rm sym}
, the entanglement criterion given in Eq. (
100
) implies that whenever a negative eigenvalue of
\gamma_{\rm av2}
exists, then the state must be entangled. In particular, in this case the value of the
su(d)
-squeezing parameter is proportional to the sum of the negative eigenvalues of
\gamma_{\rm av2}
, i. e.:

\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho_{\rm sym})=N^{2}\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\lambda_{k}(\gamma_{\rm av2}).

(102)

Thus,
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho_{\rm sym})
is negative if an only if
\gamma_{\rm av2}
has some negative eigenvalues.
On the other hand, Ref.
[
61
]
shows that the correlation matrix of a bipartite symmetric state has negative eigenvalues if and only if the state has a negative partial transpose, i. e., it violates the criterion defined in
Eq.

38
and it also violates the CCNR criterion defined in
Eq.

37
. ∎

If a quantum state is not symmetric (i. e., not bosonic), then

\langle F\rangle_{\rm av2}<1

(103)

holds, while
\varrho_{\rm av2}
is still permutationally invariant due to its definition in Eq. (
17
). Then, the criterion given in Eq. (
100
) does also detect only states for which
\gamma_{\rm av2}
has some negative eigenvalues, however, it does not detect all such states. Moreover, the existence of negative eigenvalues of
\gamma_{\rm av2}
does not imply that
\varrho_{\rm av2}
violates the PPT criterion for states that are not symmetric
[
61
]
. Thus, as we will see in a concrete example, the criterion can detect entangled states for which
\varrho_{\rm av2}

More precisely, permutationally invariant states of

\tr(|\varrho- \sigma|)

O(k/N)
. In other words, every

-body reduced state of an

in trace distance.
As we have explained before, our

in trace distance. Nevertheless, even if the reduced state is very close to separable, or even separable, it can be incompatible with a globally separable (and permutationally-invariant) state. We have shown that it is possible to prove such a property in some cases simply from our

-squeezing parameter. In particular, we have seen in the exemplary class of Werner states such as the

,
96

.
Furthermore, our conditions do not require the state to be permutationally invariant, but it just uses information from its permutationally invariant part.
Therefore, generic states can also be detected even when its average two-body reduced state is separable.

7
Conclusions and outlook

We have derived a set of
su(d)
-squeezing inequalities and defined a corresponding
su(d)
-squeezing parameter that detects entangled multi-qudit states that cannot be written in a certain pseudo-separable form. Our conditions are formulated with the first and second moments of collective operators that are the sums of single-particle
su(d)
generators, however, they do not depend on the particular choice of the
su(d)
generators, which can be chosen optimally for a given known quantum state in a straightforward way, i.e., as the principal directions in which our collective correlation matrix
\mathfrak{U}
is diagonal. We have also provided a geometric interpretation of the parameter as a signed distance from the set of pseudo-separable states, which forms a convex polytope in the space of collective
su(d)
averages and correlations.
We studied how the parameter detects thermal states of spin-models and
su(d)
-models with a permutation symmetry and obtained several interesting results. (i) States close to
su(d)
singlets are detected as entangled, some of which are also PPT with respect to all possible bipartitions. (ii) Permutationally symmetric states are detected if and only if their average two-body reduced state is not PPT. (iii) The comparison with spin-squeezing inequalities seems to be nontrivial. One can find example states that are detected by either set of inequalities and not by the other, at least for small values of
d
and
N
. The question of what happens in the large
N
limit is still open and, also in the light of our results, might be nontrivial.

Furthermore, in the light of our results it might be interesting to look for a similar approach involving different sets of operators, and understand, for example,
what the minimal set of inequalities is that characterizes all possible separable states (i.e., not just pseudo-separable as in our work) that can be described in terms of just first and second moments of collective operators. Likely such a minimal set of conditions will contain
su(d)
generators as well as smaller sets of observables, like
su(2)
generators in the
d
-dimensional representation.

Our results suggest a possible application of the framework to a deeper study of entanglement in
many-body
su(d)
models, especially mean-field models
[
67
]
and a deeper exploration of potential connections with the seminal results in that framework, e. g., de Finetti theorems
[
91
,
92
]
. Experimentally, being based on just bipartite correlations these criteria could find applications to detect entanglement in a wide range of systems, especially in many-body scenarios such as atomic clouds and condensed matter systems
[
3
,
5
,
7
]
. It could be used to to detect entanglement of the spin-1 singlet created in atomic ensembles
[
15
]
, or to detect the entanglement of spin-1 Dicke states created in BEC’s
[
79
]
, or detecting entanglement in experiments with large spins on an optical lattice
[
97
]
.
In the future it would be interesting to compare these sets of criteria with other approaches based on two-body correlations and uncertainty relations
[
37
]
, such as the well-known covariance matrix criterion
[
42
,
43
,
44
]
. Such criteria have been also recently employed to quantify entanglement via so-called entanglement monotones
[
34
,
35
]
.
In the light of our results as compared to
[
48
]
, and also in the light of recent studies with a similar approach
[
53
]

it would be particularly interesting to study what is the relation between the specific observables considered for entanglement detection and the symmetries of the states detected with the various criteria.
Furthermore, one could extend the framework to distinguish different levels of multipartite entanglement, such as the so-called depth of entanglement
[
22
,
23
,
30
]
, and to include higher order correlations of the observables. The details of the numerical calculations
are discussed in

also hold for every
N
-qudit pseudo-separable state
\varrho
.
Note that the bound
2N(d-1)
comes once again directly from the purity bound, that in this case simply reads

\tr(\varrho^2)=\sum_{k=0}^{d^{2}-1}\langle\tilde{g}_{k}\rangle^{2}_{\varrho}\leq 2,

(134)

and leads to the same bound for the sum of the variances as in
Eq.

6
:

\sum_{k}(\Delta\tilde{g}_{k})^{2}_{\varrho}\geq 2(d-1).

(135)

Below, we prove that such an extended formulation does not bring essentially any advantage for detecting entanglement via our method.

Lemma 2
.

For every state
\varrho
there exists always a basis
\tilde{g}_{k}
with the following characteristics. First,
\tilde{g}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{d}}\mathbbm{1}.
Second,
\tilde{g}_{k}
for
k=1,2,...,d^{2}-1
are the
su(d)
basis operators,
such that
\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho}
is diagonal using that basis.

Proof.—
Let us consider a general state
\varrho
and express
\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho}
in
N
-particle basis of the type mentioned above, with

\tilde{G}_{0}=N\sqrt{\frac{2}{d}}\mathbbm{1}_{N},

(136)

which is the collective operator corresponding to
\tilde{g}_{0}.
The key point is to observe that for any state
\varrho
we have
(\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho})_{0,k}=0
for all
k=0,\dots,d^{2}-1
. In fact, we have

(\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho})_{0,k}=N\left(\left\langle\tilde{G}_{0}\cdot\tilde{G}_{k}\right\rangle_{\varrho}-\sum_{n}\left\langle\tilde{g}_{0}^{(n)}\cdot\tilde{g}_{k}^{(n)}\right\rangle_{\varrho}\right)-(N-1)\left\langle\tilde{G}_{0}\right\rangle_{\varrho}\left\langle\tilde{G}_{k}\right\rangle_{\varrho}.

(137)

That is, we have

(\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho})_{0,k}=N\sum_{n\neq m}\left\langle\tilde{g}_{0}^{(n)}\otimes\tilde{g}_{k}^{(m)}\right\rangle_{\varrho}-(N-1)\left\langle\sum_{n}\tilde{g}_{0}^{(n)}\right\rangle_{\varrho}\left\langle\sum_{n}\tilde{g}_{k}^{(n)}\right\rangle_{\varrho}=0

(138)

independently from
\varrho
. Thus, in such basis
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
is of the form

\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}\end{array}\right)\ .

(139)

The final diagonal form is obtained diagonalizing
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
, i. e., finding the optimal
su(d)
basis. ∎

Thus, the eigenvalues of
\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho}
will be the same as the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{U}_{\varrho}
apart from an additional zero eigenvalue.
Moreover, the additional term in
\tr(\tilde\gamma)
is also equal to zero in that basis. Thus,
since the entanglement criterion in
Observation

1
is based on the sum of the negative eigenvalues, we would not detect more entangled states with the extended matrix.

As a final comment, we note that one could also think to consider an overcomplete observable basis, in which the operators are not necessarily orthogonal and still use our approach to derive entanglement conditions, which are essentially based on the bound on the single-particle purity.
Let us consider such a set of operators
\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime}
and first its covariance matrix on a single-particle state, namely the matrix

[\gamma]_{kl}=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime}\tilde{g}_{l}^{\prime}+\tilde{g}_{l}^{\prime}\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime}\rangle-\langle\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime}\rangle\langle\tilde{g}_{l}^{\prime}\rangle,

(140)

calculated on single-particle states.
Note that
\gamma
is still a symmetric matrix, but now has a dimension potentially larger than
d^{2}
.
Nevertheless, it can be diagonalized by an orthogonal transformation, i.e.,
\gamma^{\prime}=O\gamma O^{T}
that effectively maps the observable basis
\{\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime}\}
into an orthonormal one
\{\tilde{g}_{k}\}
in a

d^{2}
-dimensional subset.
Because of that the diagonalized covariance matrix will have the form
\gamma^{\prime}=\gamma_{su(d)}\oplus 0
, i.e., it will have a larger kernel, but the same number of nonzero diagonal elements, that are still obtained as variances of

su(d)
generators. Thus we still obtain

\Tr(\gamma)=\sum_{k}(\Delta\tilde{g}_{k}^{\prime})^{2}=\sum_{k}(\Delta\tilde{g}_{k})^{2}\geq d-1.

(141)

Thus, our method to derive the inequalities as in
Observation

1
still applies equally, and one would just get a larger set of inequalities.
However, following the same argument as above we can see that the eigenvalues of the corresponding matrix
\tilde{\mathfrak{U}}_{\varrho}
(which is still a symmetric matrix) are still obtained in an orthonormal basis of the form
as in
Lemma

2
, i. e., composed by the identity matrix plus an
su(d)
basis.
Thus, the form of our criteria as in
Observation

1
is in that sense still optimal.

Lemma 3
.

For every
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
, the set
\Omega
has vertices

and

as given in Eq. (

Proof.—
Let us consider the points
A_{k}
and

as in Eq. (

\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle

) defines a linear half space, the boundary being a hyperplane. Then, at first we can observe that the points
A_{k}
and

\displaystyle x_{d^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r=1}^{d^{2}-1}x_{r},

(142a)

\displaystyle x_{d^{2}}

\displaystyle=0.

(142b)

Let us then consider the subspace
\mathcal{W}

68
).
The intersection of Eqs. (
142b
) and (
142a

for every

,

. Conversely, the points

correspond to the intersections of all such hyperplanes with

. Thus, we have
d^{2}-1
points corresponding to the intersection of all inequalities but Eq. (

. And also, there are

On the other hand, the boundary of Eq. (

Appendix C
\xi_{su(d)}
as a signed distance in
\mathcal{W}

We will relate
\xi_{su(d)}
to the signed distance of the point corresponding to the quantum state from the polytope described by
\Omega.

As a signed distance between two points
X
and
Y
we consider

S_{D}(X,Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{d^{2}}(x_{i}-y_{i}),

(143)

which is obtained from
\|X-Y\|=\sum_{i}|x_{i}-y_{i}|
by removing the absolute value
|\cdot|.

Let us now assume that
x_{i}
are the coordinates of a general point
X
corresponding to a given quantum state and for that state we have the expectation values of the
N
-particle operators
G_{k}
given in Eq. (
8
). Then, let us assume that
A_{k}
are the coordinates of a point
saturating one of the inequalities given in Eq. (
70
) for some
I,
where
I
is a certain subset of the coordinates with
1\leq k\leq d^{2}-1.
Hence

y_{d^{2}}+\sum_{l\in I}y_{l}=0

(144)

holds for a given set
I.
The equation in Eq. (
144
) corresponds to hyperplane that contains a face of the polytope
\Omega
corresponding to a given
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle.
Then, for the point closest to
X
on that hyperplane

A_{k}=B_{k}

(145)

holds for all
k\notin I.
Thus, the signed distance of
X
from the hyperplane corresponding to the set
I
is

S^{I}_{D}(X)=x_{d^{2}}+\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}.

(146)

Then, our parameter
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)
gives the minimal signed distance from the point
X,
corresponding to
\varrho,
to the boundary of
\Omega
, namely
\xi_{su(d)}(\varrho)/N^{2}=\min_{I}S^{I}_{D}(X)
,
where the minimization is over all possible
I
corresponding to all the different hyperplanes.

Appendix D
Quantum states at the vertices of the polytope

Lemma 4
.

Let us consider a fixed value of
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
and the corresponding
\Omega
. In the limit
N\gg 1
, there exist pseudo-separable matrices
\varrho_{A_{k}}
and
\varrho_{B_{k}}
corresponding to the vertices
A_{k}
and
B_{k}
, respectively, and this holds for every given
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
.

Proof.—
Let us consider an
su(d)
basis
\{g_{r}\}
and the following matrices

\displaystyle\varrho_{A_{k}}

\displaystyle=p\varrho^{\otimes N}_{+,k}+(1-p)\varrho^{\otimes N}_{-,k},

(147)

\displaystyle\varrho_{B_{k}}

\displaystyle=\varrho^{\otimes N_{+}}_{+,k}\otimes\varrho^{\otimes(N-N_{+})}_{-,k},

where
\varrho_{\pm,k}:=\tfrac{\mathbbm{1}}{d}\pm\tfrac{1}{2}c_{k}g_{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{r\neq k}(\langle G_{r}\rangle/N)g_{r}
, with
c_{k}=\sqrt{\Lambda_{\max}-\sum_{r\neq k}\langle G_{r}\rangle^{2}/N^{2}}
for a certain direction
k
and certain numbers
N_{+}\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}
, which later will be related by

p=N_{+}/N

(148)

whenever possible. (When this will not be possible there will be a small difference and
p=N_{+}/N+\delta
with
\delta\ll 1
).

First of all, we have that, by construction,
\sum_{r=1}^{d^{2}-1}{\rm Tr}(g_{r}\varrho_{\pm,k})^{2}=\Lambda_{\max}
holds, and thus the
\varrho_{\pm,k}
satisfy Eq. (
6
). Therefore, the above are pseudo-separable according to
Definition

1
.
Furthermore, the matrices
\mathfrak{U}
associated to those states are diagonal. The values of the diagonal entries are

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}_{rr})_{A_{k}}

\displaystyle=N^{2}4p(1-p)\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}^{2}=N^{2}(x_{r})_{A_{k}},

(149a)

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{U}_{rr})_{B_{k}}

\displaystyle=-4N_{+}(N-N_{+})\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}^{2}\tfrac{1}{N-1}=N^{2}(x_{r})_{B_{k}},

(149b)

for
1\leq k\leq d^{2}-1,
where
\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}:={\rm Tr}(g_{r}\varrho_{\pm,k})
. Furthermore, we have that
\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}=\langle G_{r}\rangle/N
for all
r\neq k
, while we have

\langle g_{k}\rangle_{\pm,k}=c_{k}=\sqrt{\Lambda+\langle G_{k}\rangle^{2}/N^{2}}\quad\text{with}\quad\Lambda=\Lambda_{\max}-\sum_{r=1}^{d^{2}-1}\langle G_{r}\rangle^{2}/N^{2}.

(150)

Let us also recall the definition of the last coordinate given in
Eq.

66
,
and let us calculate it for those states. For the states
\varrho_{B_{k}}
we have

\displaystyle(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}

\displaystyle=-4\tfrac{N_{+}(N-N_{+})}{N^{2}}\tfrac{1}{N-1}\Lambda_{\max}=\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\Tr(\mathfrak{U}_{B_k}),

(151)

where the last equality follows by calculating the sum of the values
(\mathfrak{U}_{rr})_{B_{k}}
in
Eq.

149b
.

For the states
\varrho_{A_{k}}
instead, we find

\tfrac{1}{N}\tr(C_\varrho)=\left(N+d\right)\Lambda_{\max}\quad\Rightarrow\quad(x_{d^{2}})_{A_{k}}=0,

(152)

which is consistent with the fact that the state is permutationally symmetric (bosonic).

By straightforward algebra we also find the following relation

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\Tr(\mathfrak{U}_{A_k})

\displaystyle=4p(1-p)\Lambda_{\max}.

(153)

Let us now verify the relation (
68
) for the states
\varrho_{B_{k}}
. For the right-hand side we have

\displaystyle N(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{d^{2}-1}(x_{k})_{B_{k}}

\displaystyle=N(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}+\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\Tr(\mathfrak{U}_{B_k})=\frac{N_{+}(N-N_{+})}{N^{2}}\Lambda_{\max},

(154)

which follows from
Eq.

151
.
The left-hand side instead, is simply the definition of
\Lambda
that is

\Lambda=\Lambda_{\max}-|\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle|^{2}/N^{2}.

(155)

Thus, in order to verify
Eq.

68
for the states
\varrho_{B_{k}}
we have to impose

4N_{+}(N-N_{+})/N^{2}=\Lambda/\Lambda_{\max}:=\kappa,

(156)

where in the latter equality we defined the quantity
\kappa,
for which
0\leq\kappa\leq 1
holds.

Similarly, for the states
\varrho_{A_{k}}
we have that the right-hand side of
Eq.

68
is given by

N(x_{d^{2}})_{A_{k}}+\tfrac{1}{N^{2}}\Tr(\mathfrak{U}_{A_k})=4p(1-p)\Lambda_{\max},

(157)

due to
Eqs.

152
and
153
.
Thus, in this case in order to verify
Eq.

68
we have to impose

4p(1-p)=\Lambda/\Lambda_{\max}=\kappa.

(158)

In summary, to have a correspondence
(\varrho_{A_{k}},\varrho_{B_{k}})\rightarrow(A_{k},B_{k})
for a fixed value of
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
we have to impose the following condition

\displaystyle 4N_{+}(N-N_{+})/N^{2}=4p(1-p)

\displaystyle=\Lambda/\Lambda_{\max}:=\kappa,

(159)

that can be satisfied
if and only if

Np=N_{+}
is an
integer number
. In particular its value will be related to
\Lambda
as

N_{+}=\frac{N}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\Lambda/\Lambda_{\max}}\right).

(160)

When
Np
is not integer we consider

(\varrho_{A_{k}},\varrho_{B^{\prime}_{k}})
such that
Np=N^{\prime}_{+}
and

N^{\prime}_{+}=\bigg\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\Lambda/\Lambda_{\max}}\right)\bigg\rfloor:=N_{+}-\epsilon,

(161)

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the integer part of
x
and we defined

\epsilon:=N_{+}-N^{\prime}_{+}\quad\text{with}\quad 0\leq\epsilon\leq 1.

(162)

Then again
\varrho_{A_{k}}
will correspond to
A_{k}
and

\varrho_{B_{k}^{\prime}}
will correspond to a point
B_{k}^{\prime}
with coordinates

\displaystyle(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-4\frac{N^{\prime}_{+}(N-N^{\prime}_{+})}{N^{2}(N-1)}\Lambda_{\max}=(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}+4\frac{\epsilon(N+\epsilon)}{N^{2}(N-1)}\Lambda_{\max}=(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}+O(1/N^{2}),

(163)

\displaystyle(x_{r})_{B_{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-4\frac{N^{\prime}_{+}(N-N^{\prime}_{+})}{N^{2}(N-1)}\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}^{2}=(x_{d^{2}})_{B_{k}}+4\frac{\epsilon(N+\epsilon)}{N^{2}(N-1)}\langle g_{r}\rangle_{\pm,k}^{2}=(x_{r})_{B_{k}}+O(1/N^{2}),

where
O(\cdot)
is the usual big-
O
notation that describes the asymptotic behavior for large
N
in this case.

From this, we can observe that the signed distance between the two points
B_{k}
and
B_{k}^{\prime}
, is given by

\|B_{k}-B_{k}^{\prime}\|=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}|(x_{r})_{B_{k}^{\prime}}-(x_{r})_{B_{k}}|=O(1/N^{2}).

(164)

Now, one might ask whether such a scaling is negligible or not in the large
N
limit, as compared to the size of the polytope.
For that, we can consider as a length scale the smallest distance between two points
X_{k}
and
X_{l}
which would correspond to the smallest edge. Note in fact that, for instance the distance between a point
B_{k}
and a point
A_{k}
is much longer. See
Fig.

2
.
From
Eq.

80
we find that

\|B_{k}-B_{l}\|=\sum_{i=1}^{d^{2}}|(x_{r})_{B_{k}}-(x_{r})_{\rm B_{l}}|=\frac{\kappa}{N-1}(|c_{k}^{2}-\langle G_{k}\rangle^{2}/N^{2}|+|c_{l}^{2}-\langle G_{l}\rangle^{2}/N^{2}|)=2\frac{\kappa}{N-1}\Lambda,

(165)

which is of order
O(1/N)
.
Here, we consider this specific distance because it is the one directly related to our entanglement parameter.
Note however, that a similar observation can be made for other distances.

Thus in the limit
N\gg 1
the two points
B_{k}
and
B_{k}^{\prime}
coincide. This means that in the limit

N\gg 1
there are pseudo-separable matrices corresponding to all vertices of
\Omega
for all
\langle\boldsymbol{G}\rangle
. ∎

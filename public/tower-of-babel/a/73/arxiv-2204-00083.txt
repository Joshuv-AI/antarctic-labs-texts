Irreducibility of Chebyshev-Lissajous polynomials
Hanxiong Zhang

Abstract
. We study certain kind of polynomials associated with Lissajous curves, called Chebyshev-Lissajous polynomials. We investigate their irreducibilities over the real numbers and complex numbers, thus comfirming two conjectures proposed by Merino [ 2 ] .



1.
Introduction

Suppose
m
and
n
are two coprime positive integers,
a
and
b
are two real numbers. The plane curve given by

x=\cos(mt+a),\quad y=\cos(nt+b)

is called a Lissajous curve, or Lissajous figure. It is a beautiful pattern formed when two harmonic vibrations along perpendicular lines are superimposed.
After eliminating the parameter
t
, we can get a polynomial equation

f(x,y)=0
. Such polynomial
f(x,y)
is called a Lissajous polynomial.

For any natural number
n
, the
n^{th}
Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind
T_{n}(x)
, is determined by

T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos n\theta,\quad\forall\theta.

For example,

T_{0}(x)=1,T_{1}(x)=x,T_{2}(x)=2x^{2}-1,T_{3}(x)=4x^{3}-3x.

By the Sum-to-Product formula

\cos(n+1)\theta+\cos(n-1)\theta=2\cos\theta\cos n\theta,

we can get the recursive relation for Chebyshev polynomials:

T_{n+1}(x)=2xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x),\quad\forall n\geq 1.

By induction, it is not hard to see that
T_{n}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n
with integer coefficients, and the coefficient of the highest term is
2^{n-1}
.
When
n
is odd,
T_{n}(x)
is an odd function. When
n
is even,
T_{n}(x)
is an even function.
Chebyshev polynomials also satisfy the following nested property:

T_{m}(T_{n}(x))=T_{mn}(x),\quad\forall m,n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}.

How are Lissajous curves and Chebyshev polynomials related to each other? Here is a simple example: Suppose
m,n
are two coprime positive integers and
m
is odd. Consider the Lissajous curve
given by

x=\cos mt,\quad y=\sin nt=\cos\big(nt-\frac{\pi}{2}\big).

Using Chebyshev polynomials, we have

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)+T^{2}_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=T^{2}_{n}(\cos mt)+T^{2}_{m}\Big(\cos\big(nt-\frac{\pi}{2}\big)\Big)=\cos^{2}mnt+\cos^{2}\Big(mnt-\frac{m\pi}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\cos^{2}mnt+\sin^{2}mnt=1.

Hence,
T^{2}_{n}(x)+T^{2}_{m}(y)-1
is a Lissajous polynomial. Since it is given by Chebyshev polynomials, we call it a Chebyshev-Lissajous polynomial.
For example,
T^{2}_{1}(x)+T^{2}_{1}(y)-1=x^{2}+y^{2}-1
, and the associated Lissajous curve is just the unit circle.
Another example,
T^{2}_{2}(x)+T^{2}_{3}(y)-1=(2x^{2}-1)^{2}+(4y^{3}-3y)^{2}-1
, and the associated Lissajous curve is given in Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
Curve
T^{2}_{2}(x)+T^{2}_{3}(y)-1=0

Merino
[
2
]
introduced the general procedure of using Chebyshev polynomials to get the equations of Lissajous curves
(we have slightly modified the description):
Suppose
m
and
n
are two coprime positive integers,
a
and
b
are two real numbers, and
\delta=mb-na
. Consider the Lissajous
curve given by
x=\cos(mt+a),y=\cos(nt+b)
, then

T_{n}(x)=\cos(mnt+na),\quad T_{m}(y)=\cos(mnt+mb)=\cos(mnt+na+\delta).

If
\sin\delta=0
, i.e.,
\delta
is an integral multiple of
\pi
, then

T_{m}(y)=\pm\cos(mnt+na)=\pm T_{n}(x).

This is a degenerate Lissajous curve. If
\sin\delta\neq 0
, by eliminating the parameter
t
, we can get

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=0.

This is a non-degenerate Lissajous curve. Thus, a Lissajous curve is degenerate or not, depends on whether
\delta
is an integral multiple of
\pi
.

Figure 2.
Curve
T^{2}_{3}(x)+T^{2}_{3}(y)-1=0

Obviously, the above two equations are well-defined for any positive integers
m
and
n
. For example, the curve defined by
T^{2}_{3}(x)+T^{2}_{3}(y)-1=0

is the union of the unit circle and two ellipses, see Figure
2
. In the end of
[
2
]
, Merino proposed two conjectures:

Conjecture 1
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two positive integers,
\delta
is a real number with
\sin\delta\neq 0
, then the curve defined by

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=0

is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.

Conjecture 2
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two positive integers,
\delta
is a real number with
\sin\delta\neq 0
, then
the polynomial

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
m
and
n
are coprime.

From the physical point of view, these two conjectures are quite obvious. The main purpose of this article is to prove these two conjectures, mathematically.
To achieve this, we need some theorems.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose
n
is a positive integer and
\delta
is a real number, then

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{n}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{n}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

This theorem tells us: when
\sin\delta\neq 0
, the curve defined by

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{n}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{n}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=0

is the union of
n
ellipses (including circles).

When
\delta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, we get an important special case.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
n
is a positve integer, then

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)+T^{2}_{n}(y)-1=4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{(4k+1)\pi}{2n}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{(4k+1)\pi}{2n}\Big).

Theorem 2
.

Suppose
n
is a positve integer, then

\displaystyle T_{2n+1}(x)-T_{2n+1}(y)=4^{n}(x-y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big).

2.
Preliminary

First, let us introduce a factorization of Chebyshev polynomials. For more factorizations, see
[
3
]
.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
n
is a positve integer and
\delta
is a real number, then

T_{n}(x)-\cos\delta=2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

Proof.

Since
T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos n\theta,
we have

T_{n}\Big(\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)=\cos(\delta+2k\pi)=\cos\delta,\quad k=1,2,\cdots,n.

When
\sin\delta\neq 0
, i.e.,
\delta
is not an integral multiple of
\pi
,

\displaystyle\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n},\quad k=1,2,\cdots,n

are mutually different.
Thus
\displaystyle\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}(k=1,2,\cdots,n)
are different roots of
T_{n}(x)-\cos\delta
.
Since
T_{n}(x)-\cos\delta
is a polynomial of degree
n
, with highest coefficient
2^{n-1}
, hence

T_{n}(x)-\cos\delta=2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

When
\sin\delta=0
, by a suitable limit process, it is easy to see that the above equality still holds for any real number
x
.
Hence the two sides are equal as polynomials of
x
.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Suppose
n
is a positve integer and
\delta
is a real number, then

\cos n\theta-\cos\delta=2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\cos\theta-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

Lemma 2
.

Suppose
f(x,y)
is a polynomial of two variables with real coefficients. If there exists
r>0
, such that

f(x,y)=0,\quad\forall x,y\in(-r,r),

then
f=0
.

Proof.

Suppose
\displaystyle f(x,y)=\sum_{i,j}a_{i,j}x^{i}y^{j}
, which is a finite sum.
Let
x=y=0
, we get
a_{0,0}=0
. Take partial derivative with respect to
x
and let
x=y=0
, we get
a_{1,0}=0
.
By similar operations, we can see that
a_{i,j}=0
for all
i
and
j
. Hence
f=0
.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Suppose
f(x,y)
and
g(x,y)
are polynomials of two variables with real coefficients. If there exists
r>0
, such that

f(x,y)=g(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in(-r,r),

then
f=g
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

Proof.

When
x,y\in[-1,1]
, let
x=\cos\alpha,y=\cos\beta
, then

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{n}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{n}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos^{2}n\alpha-2\cos n\alpha\cos n\beta\cos\delta+\cos^{2}n\beta-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos 2n\alpha}{2}-\big[\cos n(\alpha+\beta)+\cos n(\alpha-\beta)\big]\cos\delta+\frac{1+\cos 2n\beta}{2}-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos n(\alpha+\beta)\cos n(\alpha-\beta)-\big[\cos n(\alpha+\beta)+\cos n(\alpha-\beta)\big]\cos\delta+\cos^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[\cos n(\alpha+\beta)-\cos\delta\big]\cdot\big[\cos n(\alpha-\beta)-\cos\delta\big].

By Corollary
2
, the above is equal to

\displaystyle 2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\cos(\alpha+\beta)-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)\cdot 2^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\cos(\alpha-\beta)-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

Since
\cos(\alpha\pm\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta\mp\sin\alpha\sin\beta
, the above is equal to

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)\Big(\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big[\Big(\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\sin\alpha\sin\beta\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big[\Big(xy-\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big)^{2}-(1-x^{2})(1-y^{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

Hence, when
x,y\in[-1,1]
, we have

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{n}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{n}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{n}\Big).

By Corollary
3
, the two sides are equal as polynomials. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
2

Proof.

When
y\in[-1,1]
, let
y=\cos\beta
. By Lemma
1
,

\displaystyle T_{2n+1}(x)-T_{2n+1}(y)=T_{2n+1}(x)-\cos(2n+1)\beta=2^{2n}\prod_{k=1}^{2n+1}\Big(x-\cos(\beta+\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n}(x-\cos\beta)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x-\cos(\beta+\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1})\Big)\Big(x-\cos(\beta-\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n}(x-\cos\beta)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big[\Big(x-\cos\beta\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\sin\beta\sin\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n}(x-y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big[\Big(x-y\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big)^{2}-(1-y^{2})\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{n}(x-y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big).

Hence, for any
x,y\in[-1,1]
, we have

\displaystyle T_{2n+1}(x)-T_{2n+1}(y)=4^{n}(x-y)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\Big(x^{2}-2\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}xy+y^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2n+1}\Big).

By Corollary
3
, the two sides are equal as polynomials. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

5.
Proof of the sufficient part of Conjecture
2

We rewrite the the sufficient part of Conjecture
2
as a theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two coprime positive integers, and
\delta
is a real number with
\sin\delta\neq 0
, then

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
.

Tverberg
[
1
]
provided a simple proof of the following result: Suppose
f(x)
and
g(y)
are two nonconstant polynomials with complex coefficients.
If the degrees of
f,g
are coprime, then
f(x)+g(y)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
.

The first half of the following proof is essentially the same as the proof in
[
1
]
, while the second half is new.

Proof.

Suppose
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=P(x,y)Q(x,y)
,
where
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
, and
P,Q
are both nonconstant, then

T^{2}_{n}(x^{m})-2T_{n}(x^{m})T_{m}(y^{n})\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y^{n})-\sin^{2}\delta=P(x^{m},y^{n})Q(x^{m},y^{n}).

Denote
P_{1},Q_{1}
the homogeneous parts of
P(x^{m},y^{n}),Q(x^{m},y^{n})
with highest degrees, then

4^{n-1}x^{2mn}-2^{m+n-1}\cos\delta\cdot x^{mn}y^{mn}+4^{m-1}y^{2mn}=P_{1}Q_{1}.

Let
w=\cos\delta+i\sin\delta
, then the left hand side equals

(2^{n-1}x^{mn}-w2^{m-1}y^{mn})(2^{n-1}x^{mn}-\bar{w}2^{m-1}y^{mn}),

which has no monomial factors. Hence,
P_{1}
has at least two terms, i.e.,

P_{1}=px^{km}y^{ln}+p^{\prime}x^{k^{\prime}m}y^{l^{\prime}n}+\cdots.

As
P_{1}
is homogeneous,
km+ln=k^{\prime}m+l^{\prime}n
, hence
(k-k^{\prime})m=(l^{\prime}-l)n
. Since
m,n
are coprime, we have
n|k-k^{\prime},m|l^{\prime}-l
.
Suppose
k>k^{\prime}
, then
k\geq n+k^{\prime}\geq n
,
l^{\prime}>l
, and
l^{\prime}\geq m+l\geq m
. So
\deg P_{1}=km+ln\geq mn,

with equality if and only if
k=n,l^{\prime}=m,l=k^{\prime}=0
.

Similarly,
Q_{1}
has at least two terms, i.e.,

Q_{1}=qx^{sm}y^{tn}+q^{\prime}x^{s^{\prime}m}y^{t^{\prime}n}+\cdots.

As
Q_{1}
is homogeneous,
sm+tn=s^{\prime}m+t^{\prime}n
,
(s-s^{\prime})m=(t^{\prime}-t)n
.
Since
m,n
are coprime, we have
n|s-s^{\prime},m|t^{\prime}-t
. Suppose
s>s^{\prime}
, then
s\geq n+s^{\prime}\geq n
,
t^{\prime}>t
, and
t^{\prime}\geq m+t\geq m
. So
\deg Q_{1}=sm+tn\geq mn,

with equality if and only if
s=n,t^{\prime}=m,t=s^{\prime}=0
.

Since the degree of
4^{n-1}x^{2mn}-2^{m+n-1}\cos\delta\cdot x^{mn}y^{mn}+4^{m-1}y^{2mn}
is
2mn
,
the above inequalities are all equalities, and
P_{1},Q_{1}
both have two terms, i.e.,
P_{1}=px^{mn}+p^{\prime}y^{mn},Q_{1}=qx^{mn}+q^{\prime}y^{mn}
.
Compare the coefficients of the two sides of

4^{n-1}x^{2mn}-2^{m+n-1}\cos\delta\cdot x^{mn}y^{mn}+4^{m-1}y^{2mn}=(px^{mn}+p^{\prime}y^{mn})(qx^{mn}+q^{\prime}y^{mn}),

we have

pq=4^{n-1},p^{\prime}q^{\prime}=4^{m-1},pq^{\prime}+p^{\prime}q=-2^{m+n-1}\cos\delta.

Hence,

-2^{m+n-1}\cos\delta=p\cdot\frac{4^{m-1}}{p^{\prime}}+p^{\prime}\cdot\frac{4^{n-1}}{p}.

Since
\displaystyle\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}
and
\displaystyle\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}
are both positive or both negative, we can take absolute values and get

2^{m+n-1}|\cos\delta|=\Big|\frac{4^{m-1}p}{p^{\prime}}\Big|+\Big|\frac{4^{n-1}p^{\prime}}{p}\Big|\geq 2\sqrt{\Big|\frac{4^{m-1}p}{p^{\prime}}\Big|\cdot\Big|\frac{4^{n-1}p^{\prime}}{p}\Big|}=2^{m+n-1}.

This implies
|\cos\delta|=1
, which contradicts with
\sin\delta\neq 0
.∎

We can strengthen the conclusion a little further.

Theorem 4
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two coprime positive integers, and
\delta
is a real number with
\sin\delta\neq 0
, then

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Suppose
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=f_{1}f_{2}\cdots f_{l}
,
where
l\geq 2,f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{l}
are all nonconstant irredubible polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
.
Take conjugation, we get
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=\bar{f}_{1}\bar{f}_{2}\cdots\bar{f}_{l}
.
Since the highest term (in
x
) of the left hand side is
4^{n-1}x^{2n}
, we can assume that each of
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{l}
has highest coefficient
\sqrt[l]{4^{n-1}}
.

As
\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is a UFD,

\bar{f}_{1},\bar{f}_{2},\cdots,\bar{f}_{l}
must be a rearrangement of
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{l}
.
By Theorem
3
,
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta
is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
,
we have
l=2
and
f_{2}=\bar{f}_{1}
. Hence,

T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=f_{1}(x,y)\bar{f}_{1}(x,y).

Take
x=\frac{\pi}{2n}
and
y=\frac{\pi}{2m}
, the left hand side is equal to
-\sin^{2}\delta<0
, while the right hand side is nonnegative, contradiction!
∎

When
\delta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, we get an important special case.

Corollary 4
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two coprime positive integers, then
T^{2}_{n}(x)+T^{2}_{m}(y)-1
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
.

6.
Proof of the necessary part of Conjecture
2

Denote
t_{n}(x,y)=T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{n}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{n}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta
, then Theorem
1
tells us: when
n>1
,
t_{n}(x,y)
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.
Now we rewrite the necessary part of Conjecture
2
as a theorem.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two positive integers, and
\delta
is a real number with
\sin\delta\neq 0
.
If
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta
is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
, then
m,n
are coprime.

Proof.

We use contradiction. Suppose
d=\gcd(m,n)>1
, let
m=m^{\prime}d,n=n^{\prime}d
, then

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))^{2}-2T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))\cos\delta+T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))^{2}-\sin^{2}\delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x),T_{m^{\prime}}(y))

is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

7.
Proof of Conjecture
1

Proof.

Let
d=\gcd(m,n)
, and
m=m^{\prime}d,n=n^{\prime}d
. Using the notations introduced in the last section and Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=t_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x),T_{m^{\prime}}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{d-1}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\Big(T^{2}_{n^{\prime}}(x)-2\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{d}T_{n^{\prime}}(x)T_{m^{\prime}}(y)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{d}\Big).

As
m^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
are coprime, each factor

T^{2}_{n^{\prime}}(x)-2\cos\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{d}T_{n^{\prime}}(x)T_{m^{\prime}}(y)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{\delta+2k\pi}{d}

is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
, corresponding to a Lissajous curve. Hence, the curve defined by
T^{2}_{n}(x)-2T_{n}(x)T_{m}(y)\cos\delta+T^{2}_{m}(y)-\sin^{2}\delta=0

is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.
∎

8.
The degenerate case of Conjecture
2

The two conjectures proposed in
[
2
]
, also include the degenerate case, i.e., when the Chebyshev-Lissajous polynomial is
T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)
.
In this section, we consider the degenerate case of Conjecture
2
, i.e., when is the polynomial

T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)
irreducible? The answer is a little bit subtle.

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two positive integers,
d=\gcd(m,n)
, then

T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
d=1
, and
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
if and only if
d\leq 2
.

If we replace
\mathbb{R}
by
\mathbb{C}
, the conclusion is also correct.

Proof.

When
d=1
, by the conclusion of
[
1
]
,
T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
, hence irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
.

When
d>1
, let
m=m^{\prime}d,n=n^{\prime}d
, then
m^{\prime},n^{\prime}
are coprime.

1. The case of
T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)
is simple. Since
x-y|T_{d}(x)-T_{d}(y)
,
T_{d}(x)-T_{d}(y)
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
, so

T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))-T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.

2. The case of
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)
is a little complicated.

(1)When
d
is odd, since
x+y|T_{d}(x)+T_{d}(y)
,
T_{d}(x)+T_{d}(y)
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
, hence

T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))+T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.

(2)When
d=2
,
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=T_{2n^{\prime}}(x)+T_{2m^{\prime}}(y)=2\big(T_{n^{\prime}}^{2}(x)+T_{m^{\prime}}^{2}(y)-1\big)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
.
The simplest example is
T_{2}(x)+T_{2}(y)=2(x^{2}+y^{2}-1)
.

(3)When
d
is an even integer bigger than
2
, let
d=2e
, then
e>1
. By Corollary
1
,
T_{d}(x)+T_{d}(y)=2\big(T_{e}^{2}(x)+T_{e}^{2}(y)-1\big)
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))+T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))
is reducible over
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

9.
The degenerate case of Conjecture
1

In this section, we consider the degenerate case of Conjecture
1
, i.e., whether the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)=0

is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves? The answer is yes.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
m
and
n
are two positive integers, then the curve defined by

T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)=0
is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.

Proof.

If
m
and
n
are coprime, then
T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)
is irreducible over
\mathbb{C}
, so the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)\pm T_{m}(y)=0
is a Lissajous curve.

If
m
and
n
are not coprime, then
d=\gcd(m,n)>1
. Let
m=m^{\prime}d,n=n^{\prime}d
.

(1)If
d
is odd, suppose
d=2e+1
. By Theorem
2
,

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))-T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{e}\big(T_{n^{\prime}}(x)-T_{m^{\prime}}(y)\big)\prod_{k=1}^{e}\Big(T_{n^{\prime}}(x)^{2}-2\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2e+1}T_{n^{\prime}}(x)T_{m^{\prime}}(y)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2e+1}\Big).

As
m^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
are coprime, each factor on the right hand side is irreducible, corresponding to a Lissajous curve.
Hence, the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)=0
is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.

When
d
is odd,
T_{d}(x)
is an odd function, so

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))+T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))-T_{d}(-T_{m^{\prime}}(y))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4^{e}\big(T_{n^{\prime}}(x)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)\big)\prod_{k=1}^{e}\Big(T_{n^{\prime}}(x)^{2}+2\cos\frac{2k\pi}{2e+1}T_{n^{\prime}}(x)T_{m^{\prime}}(y)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{2k\pi}{2e+1}\Big).

Hence, the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=0
is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.

(2)When
d
is even, suppose
d=2e
. By
T_{d}(x)+T_{d}(y)=2\big(T_{e}^{2}(x)+T_{e}^{2}(y)-1\big)
, we have

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=T_{d}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))+T_{d}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))=2\big(T_{e}^{2}(T_{n^{\prime}}(x))+T_{e}^{2}(T_{m^{\prime}}(y))-1\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\cdot 4^{e-1}\prod_{k=1}^{e}\Big(T^{2}_{n^{\prime}}(x)-2\cos\frac{(4k+1)\pi}{2e}T_{n^{\prime}}(x)T_{m^{\prime}}(y)+T_{m^{\prime}}(y)^{2}-\sin^{2}\frac{(4k+1)\pi}{2e}\Big).

Hence, the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)+T_{m}(y)=0
is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.

When
d
is even, both
m
and
n
are even. Using the identity

T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)=2\Big(T_{\frac{n}{2}}(x)-T_{\frac{m}{2}}(y)\Big)\Big(T_{\frac{n}{2}}(x)+T_{\frac{m}{2}}(y)\Big)

and induction, we can see that the curve defined by
T_{n}(x)-T_{m}(y)=0
is the union of a finite number of Lissajous curves.
∎

Existence of weak solutions and long-time asymptotics for hydrodynamic model of swarming
Nilasis Chaudhuri, Young-Pil Choi, Oliver Tse, Ewelina Zatorska

Abstract
. We consider a one-dimensional hydrodynamic model featuring nonlocal attraction-repulsion interactions and singular velocity alignment. We introduce a two-velocity reformulation and the corresponding energy-type inequality, in the spirit of the Bresch-Desjardins estimate. We identify a dependence between the communication weight and interaction kernel and between the pressure and viscosity term allowing for this inequality to be uniform in time. It is then used to study long-time asymptotics of solutions.



1.
Introduction

The purpose of this manuscript is to study the existence of solutions and the long-time behavior of the one-dimensional hydrodynamic model of collective motion with unknown density
\rho
and velocity
u
satisfying the conservation of mass and momentum balance equations

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad t>0,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})=-\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)+\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u)+\rho[\phi*,u]\rho-\tau\rho u,

henceforth, simply called the Navier-Stokes system. In the model above,
\rho\mapsto\mathcal{F}(\rho)
is the driving functional with contribution due to the internal energy and the nonlocal interaction parts, i.e.

\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\varphi(r)\,{\rm d}r\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x)W*\rho(x)\,{\rm{d}}x,

and
\delta\mathcal{F}
is its
L^{2}
-variational derivative given by

(1.2)

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)=\varphi(\rho)+W*\rho.\end{split}

Here, we consider the explicit form of
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}\coloneq[0,\infty)
, given by

(1.3)

\begin{split}\varphi(\rho)=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1},\quad\gamma>1,\end{split}

which corresponds to the barotropic pressure law
\rho^{\gamma}
. The interaction potential
W:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be bounded from below—the precise assumptions on its regularity and growth will be given in Assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{W})
in the next section.

The second term on the right-hand side of (
1.1b
) is the viscosity dependent stress tensor with the viscosity coefficient
\mu=\mu(\rho)
. In our paper, we consider
\mu
and
\varphi
linked by the algebraic relation

(1.4)

\begin{split}\mu(\rho)\coloneq\rho^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)=\gamma\rho^{\gamma}.\end{split}

In particular,
\mu(\rho)=0
when
\rho=0
. The third term is the nonlinear and nonlocal velocity alignment force with communication weight
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here
[\cdot,\cdot]
stands for the commutator operator that can be equivalently expressed as

\rho[\phi*,u]\rho\coloneq\rho(\phi*(u\rho)-u\phi*\rho)=\rho(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x-y)(u(y)-u(x))\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

Typically,
\phi
is chosen as a radially decreasing function so that closer particles have a stronger tendency to be aligned than particles further away. Finally, the last term in the momentum equation (
1.1b
) is the linear velocity damping with a parameter
\tau\geq 0
.

Simply put, the construction of weak solutions (cf. Definition
2.2
) to the system (
1.1
) is obtained as an accumulation point of a sequence of solutions
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
to an approximate system with parameter
\varepsilon>0
(cf. Section
4
), which hinges on providing relevant a-priori estimates. In this work, this is achieved by establishing a functional inequality for an appropriate linear combination of several energy functionals with
one of them being the
total energy

\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho).

Along sufficiently regular solutions of (
1.1
), the total energy is known to dissipate in time, i.e.

-\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,{\rm{d}}x+\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u),

where the right-hand side is nonnegative and

\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\phi(x-y)|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x.

However, the control provided by the total energy alone does not provide sufficient control to characterize the accumulation points of the approximating sequence
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
. For this reason, we provide additional control of the so-called
modulated energy

\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho),

which is a Bresch-Dejardins (BD) type energy
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
that incorporates modulation of the nonlocal forces. Here, the quantity
w=u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)
can be seen as an augmented velocity.

In Section
3
, we formally show that for sufficiently smooth solutions to the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
), the functional

\displaystyle-\infty<\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\coloneq\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)+\tau\mathcal{F}(\rho)+\lambda\mathcal{E}(\rho,u),

for sufficiently large
\lambda\gg 1
,
satisfies the BD-type differential inequality

(1.5)

\displaystyle-\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\geq\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u),

where the dissipation—the right-hand side—is bounded from below by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u),

with appropriate constants
-\infty<\underline{\ell}\leq\ell_{\tau,\lambda}
and
c_{\lambda}>0
.

As it turns out, solutions to the approximate system satisfy a similar differential inequality (cf. Section
4
), allowing us to deduce
\varepsilon
-independent bounds in the form

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\left\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}t\right\}<+\infty.

Together with the Mellet-Vasseur type estimate
[
39
]
derived in Section
5
, the uniform-in-
\varepsilon
bound above provides sufficient control to (a) deduce the integrated version of (
1.5
), i.e.

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho(t),u(t))+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\,{\rm{d}}t\leq\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho_{0},u_{0}),

for the accumulation points
(\rho,u)
of
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
, and (b) characterize these accumulation points as weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
) according to Definition
2.2
below (cf. Theorem
2.3
).

In the scenario when either
\tau>0
or
\phi
is uniformly bounded from below, i.e.
\phi\geq\underline{\phi}
for some
\underline{\phi}>0
, the constant
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}
can be chosen to be stricly positive. In particular, the dissipation
\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}\geq 0
is non-negative, and the BD-type integral inequality (
1.6
) can be used to deduce the long-time behaviour of the accumulation points
(\rho,u)
. Explicitly, we show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho))^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to\infty$}.

If
W
is additionally bounded and Lipschitz continuous, we further prove the existence of some
\rho_{\infty}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(cf. Theorem
2.5
) such that for some (not relabelled) subsequence

\rho(t)\to\rho_{\infty}\quad{\rm strongly\ in}\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),\ p\in[1,\infty),

where the limit point
\rho_{\infty}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{\infty}(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\infty}))^{2}\,{\rm{d}}x=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho_{\infty})+\partial_{x}W\ast\rho_{\infty}=0\quad\text{on $\text{supp}(\rho_{\infty})$}.

In particular, this implies that the limit profile
\rho_{\infty}
is a minimizer of the driving functional
\mathcal{F}
.

Relation to previous work

The pressureless Euler system, incorporating nonlocal interactions modelled by the interaction potential
W
and a linear damping term (corresponding to constant alignment
\phi=1
), has been previously examined in
[
11
]
. In that work, the authors established the global existence of classical solutions over time and analyzed their long-time behaviour, contingent upon initial data falling below a specific threshold.
When the interaction potential is given as the Riesz one, the rigorous derivation of the pressureless Euler system from its microscopic description was investigated in
[
46
]
. We also refer to
[
35
]
for the study of the long-time asymptotics of solutions to the Euler system with pressure using relative entropy methods and to
[
10
]
using second-order Wasserstein calculus.

In the case
W,\mu\equiv 0
, the system (
1.1
) becomes the so-called
Euler-alignment system
, also known as a macroscopic description of the celebrated Cucker-Smale model
[
26
,
27
]
. The global existence of regular solutions and their long-time asymptotics were well studied in
[
22
,
30
,
43
,
44
,
45
]
under the smallness and smoothness assumptions on the initial data. The critical threshold phenomena in the pressureless Euler-alignment model were analysed in
[
47
,
9
]
and more recently in
[
2
]
. The rigorous derivation from its kinetic formulation was discussed in
[
7
,
29
]
and
[
21
,
23
]
in the case of regular and singular communication weights, respectively. In a recent work
[
6
]
, the rigorous derivation of the pressureless Euler-alignment model from the Cucker-Smale model by means of mean-field limits was established. We refer to
[
8
,
20
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
and references therein for a general survey and recent development of swarming models.

An intriguing question arising from these studies concerns the existence and behaviour of solutions beyond the blow-up time. For the Euler-alignment model, the existence and uniqueness of these solutions have been explored through the sticky particles approximation in
[
36
]
. On the other hand, for the pressureless Euler with nonlocal interaction forces, the equivalence between Lagrangian and entropy solutions existing globally in time was shown in the recent work of Carrillo and Galtung
[
12
]
.
The motivation for the present work was similar. However, instead of delving into the different notions of measure-valued solutions, we focus on the global-in-time existence of weak solutions and their long-time asymptotics for a certain approximation of the original system from
[
11
]
. The discussion of the approximation with viscosity and pressure, for instance, can be found in
[
14
]
, where a relative entropy inequality was derived akin to the earlier work of Haspot
[
31
]
.

Despite the imposition of the relation between the viscosity coefficient and adiabatic exponent of pressure as in (
1.4
), the authors of
[
14
]
were unable to derive global estimates over time. The equation (
1.4
) proved crucial for conducting the pressureless and inviscid limit simultaneously, and to arrive at the Euler system from
[
11
]
. Performing these limit passages independently requires using compensated compactness techniques of Chen and Perepelitsa
[
17
,
18
]
and is investigated in the forthcoming work
[
16
]
.

The key observation of our paper is that a suitable global in-time estimate holds provided pressure and viscosity are related by (
1.4
) and that a similar relation is imposed between their non-local counterparts: the attraction-repulsion potential
W
and the communication weight
\phi
. In a sense, we further explore the two-velocity structure of hydrodynamic models, inspired by Bresch and Desjardins
[
3
]
, but our augmented velocity
w
also encompasses the gradient of the nonlocal function of density, i.e.
w=u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)
.
The analogy between nonlocal alignment and viscous dissipation was recognized and discussed earlier, as detailed in
[
40
, Chapter 5]
. We also draw attention to the works of Do et al.
[
28
]
and the series by Shvydkoy and Tadmor
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
, with various results on fractional Euler alignment system in the 1D torus, exploiting the extra conservation law for the so-called
active potential
, see also
[
24
,
25
]
. A similar approach involving the utilization of an additional conservation law for artificial momentum was applied by Haspot and Zatorska in
[
32
]
to perform the pressureless limit of the Navier-Stokes system, and by Constantin et.al.
[
24
,
25
]
to prove the existence of solutions for a general class of density-degenerate viscous models.

It is important to note that all the results mentioned above pertain to the one-dimensional spatial domain. For insights into the long-term behaviour of solutions in a general multi-dimensional hydrodynamic model of collective motion (with constant viscosity), we refer to
[
13
]
.
Additionally, Carrillo and Shu recently investigated the pressureless Euler-Poisson system with quadratic confinement in a spherically symmetric multi-dimensional context
[
15
]
; we refer to this paper for an up-to-date overview of results on that system in the context of continuous collective behaviour models.

The technique of proving the existence of solutions for our system is hugely inspired by the papers
[
32
,
33
,
37
]
, which in turn rely on the a-priori estimates derived in the earlier works of Bresch and Desjardins
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
and of Mellet and Vasseur
[
39
]
. The existence of solutions to the multi-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosities satisfying these a-priori estimates was proven in the seminal work of Vasseur and Yu
[
48
]
, see also
[
38
]
. The construction of the approximate solution in a one-dimensional setting is considerably less complex: we adapt the iterative scheme developed by Constantin et al.
[
25
]
, along with the construction of approximate initial data by Chen et al.
[
19
]
to the nonlocal case.

Outline of the paper

Section

is devoted to stating our main results. We then present the main a-priori estimates for regular solutions of system (

2.
Main results

We supplement system (
1.1
) with the initial data

(2.1)

\begin{split}(\rho(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot))|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\end{split}

for which we assume that

(2.2)

\begin{gathered}\rho_{0}\geq 0,\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\partial_{x}(\rho_{0}^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho_{0}u_{0}^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\\
|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad 0<\kappa\leq\min\left\{2\gamma-1,\frac{2}{\gamma}\right\}.\end{gathered}

Since the mass of
\rho_{0}
will be preserved in time, we can consider w.l.o.g. that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{0}(x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=1
.

Throughout this manuscript, we assume that the interaction potential
W
and the communication weight
\phi
satisfy the following conditions
(\mathcal{A})
:

(\mathcal{A}_{W})

W
is bounded from below by some constant, and further it has a form of

W(x)=-|x|+{\widetilde{W}}(x),

where
{\widetilde{W}}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is symmetric and satisfies

(i)

{\widetilde{W}}\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
,

(ii)

|\partial_{x}{\widetilde{W}}(x)|\leq l_{W}(1+|x|)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
for some constant
l_{W}>0
, and

(iii)

there exists some positive constants
c_{\phi,W}
and
c_{W}
such that

(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{-}\leq c_{\phi,W}\phi\quad\mbox{for }x\neq 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{+}\leq c_{W}\quad\mbox{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},

where
(f)^{\pm}=\max\{0,\pm f\}
denote the positive and negative parts of a given function
f
.

(\mathcal{A}_{\phi})

\phi\in C(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
and for any
R>0

\phi\mathbf{1}_{B(0,R)}\in L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}(\mathbb{R})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\phi\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}\backslash B(0,R)}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).

Remark 2.1
.

(a)

The interaction potential
W
given as a sum of the Coulomb potential and quadratic confinement, i.e.

\begin{split}W(x)=-|x|+\frac{|x|^{2}}{2},\end{split}

clear satisfies assumption
(\mathcal{A}_{W})
.

(b)

If the communication weight
\phi
and the interaction potential
W
are given as

\phi(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}\quad\mbox{with}\;\;a<\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma},\quad W=-|x|-|x|^{2-a},

then they satisfy conditions
(\mathcal{A})
. Indeed, in this case,

(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{-}=(2-a)(1-a)|x|^{-a}=(2-a)(1-a)\phi(x),\qquad(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{+}=0.

(c)

We deduce from Assumption (
\mathcal{A}2
) the following lower bound on the interaction energy

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,W\ast\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x\geq c_{0}-c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{|x|^{2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x,

for some constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
, depending only on
{\widetilde{W}}(0)
and
l_{W}
.

Let us now define the notion of solutions to the system (
1.1
)

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T>0
be fixed but arbitrary.
The pair of functions
(\rho,\sqrt{\rho}u)
is called a global-in-time weak solution to system (
1.1
) with initial data (
2.1
) satisfying (
2.2
) if

\begin{gathered}\rho\in C_{w}([0,T];L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})),\qquad\rho\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})),\\
\sqrt{\rho}u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),\qquad\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R})).\end{gathered}

Moreover, the following weak formulations are satisfied.

(i)

The continuity equation holds

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\psi(t_{2})\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho\psi(t_{1})\,{\rm{d}}x=\int^{t_{2}}_{t_{1}}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho\partial_{t}\psi+\rho u\partial_{x}\psi)\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t

for any
0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq T
and any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
.

(ii)

The momentum equation holds

(2.4)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}u_{0}\psi(0)\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\left(\rho u\partial_{t}\psi+\rho u^{2}\partial_{x}\psi\right)}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t{-\langle\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}\psi\rangle}\\
&\hskip 60.00009pt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)\psi}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[\phi*,u]\rho\psi}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+\tau\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\psi}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t,\end{split}

for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
, where the diffusion term is defined as follows:

\begin{split}&\langle\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u,\partial_{x}\psi\rangle\\
&\hskip 30.00005pt=-\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\frac{\mu(\rho)}{\sqrt{\rho}}\sqrt{\rho}u\,{\partial_{xx}}\psi}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t-\frac{2\gamma}{2\gamma-1}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}\left(\frac{\mu(\rho)}{\sqrt{\rho}}\right)\sqrt{\rho}u\,{\partial_{x}\psi}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t.\end{split}

Before stating our main results, let us recall the definition of the total energy
\mathcal{E}
and the modulated energy
\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}
for sufficiently smooth pairs
(\rho,u)
given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho).

We further recall the functionals

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)+\tau\mathcal{F}(\rho)+\lambda\mathcal{E}(\rho,u),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

Our first result pertains to the existence of weak solutions to the nonlocal Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\tau\geq 0
and the conditions
(\mathcal{A})
hold. Then there exist a weak solution to system (
1.1
) in the sense of Definition
2.2
, and
\lambda>0
such that the modulated energy inequality

(2.5)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)(t)+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\,{\rm{d}}t\leq\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho_{0},u_{0})\end{split}

holds with constants
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{\lambda}>0
.

If either
\tau>0
or
\phi
is uniformly bounded from below, i.e.
\phi\geq\underline{\phi}
for some constant
\underline{\phi}>0
, then
\lambda>0
can be chosen such that
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}>0
. In these cases,
\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}\geq 0
, and the modulated energy inequality (
2.5
) is satisfied uniformly w.r.t.
T
.

Remark 2.4
.

The hypothesis (
2.2
) implies

\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0},u_{0})\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{0}u_{0}^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})<\infty,

\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho_{0},u_{0})\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{0}|u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})<\infty.

Once the existence of solutions is established we turn our attention to their long-time asymptotic. Our second main result reads.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(\rho,\sqrt{\rho}u)
be the global-in-time weak solution to the Navier-Stokes system (
1.1
) provided by Theorem
2.3
, and let either
\tau>0
or
\phi
uniformly bounded from below. Then, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\to 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho))^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to\infty$}.

Moreover, if
W
is bounded and Lipschitz continuous, then there exists
\rho_{\infty}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\begin{gathered}\rho(t)\to\rho_{\infty}\quad strongly\ in\ L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),\ p\in[1,\infty),\\
\sqrt{\rho(t)}\,u(t)\to 0\quad strongly\ in\ L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).\end{gathered}

up to a subsequence, and
\rho_{\infty}
is characterized by

\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho_{\infty})+\partial_{x}W*\rho_{\infty}=0\quad\mbox{on }{\rm supp}(\rho_{\infty}).

Remark 2.6
.

The boundedness and Lipschitz continuity conditions on the interaction potential
W
can be relaxed to requiring

(2.6)

\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|W*\rho|}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}W*\rho|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\right)<\infty.

Clearly, if the interaction potential
W
is Lipschitz and bounded, then (
2.6
) holds.

3.
A priori energy and modulated energy estimate

This section is dedicated to the derivation of the modulated energy inequality
that allows us to deduce the following a-priori estimates:

(3.1)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho)\right)\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+C(\tau)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u)\,{\rm{d}}t\leq C(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\\
&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho))^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\leq C(\rho_{0},u_{0}),\end{split}

for some constant
C(\tau)>0
.

We have the following result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\rho,u)
be a regular solution to system (
1.1
) for
\tau\geq 0
on the interval
[0,T]
, and let Assumption
(\mathcal{A})
be satisfied. Then there exists
\lambda>0
such that

(3.2)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\leq-\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho,u)\end{split}

with constants
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
depending on
\tau
and
\lambda
,
c_{\lambda}>0
depending only on
\lambda
.

Before proving this lemma let us make a few observations.

Remark 3.2
.

(a)

If
\tau>0
, then
\lambda
can be chosen sufficiently large such that
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}>0
. In particular, the right-hand side of (
3.2
) is nonpositive, and therefore the estimates in (
3.1
) are uniform in time. The same conclusion holds true if instead of
\tau>0
, we assume that
\phi
is uniformly bounded from below, i.e.
\phi\geq\underline{\phi}
for some constant
\underline{\phi}>0
.

(b)

If
\tau=0
and
\phi
is allowed to vanish, then
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}=-c_{W}^{2}<0
, and applying Gröwnwall’s lemma to (
3.2
) provides the upper bound estimates in (
3.1
) depending on
T>0
, which can be arbitrary large but finite.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

We only prove the case
\tau>0
as the case when
\phi\geq\underline{\phi}
can be done analogously.

Straightforward computation gives that the mass of
\rho
is preserved in time, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{0}(x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=1,

and that

(3.3)

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}\,{\rm{d}}x-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

Since
W
is bounded from below, we deduce from the above that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\gamma}}<+\infty.

In particular, this implies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi*\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\leq 1}*\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\geq 1}*\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\leq 1}\|_{L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\gamma}}+\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\phi\mathbf{1}_{|\cdot|\geq 1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{1}}<+\infty.

For the modulated energy, we estimate

(3.4)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)&=\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

We begin by rewriting
I_{2}
using the form of
\delta\mathcal{F}
in (
1.2
) and the continuity equation (
1.1a
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)\,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\hskip 40.00006pt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)\,\partial_{x}\left((\partial_{x}\varphi)u+\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}W*(\rho u)\right)}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\hskip 40.00006pt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)\left((\partial_{xx}\varphi)u+(\partial_{x}\varphi)\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}u)+\partial_{xx}W*(\rho u)\right)}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\hskip 40.00006pt=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u)|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}-\int\rho[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\hskip 40.00006pt\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\left(\partial_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}u)+(\partial_{x}u)(\partial_{x}\varphi)\right)\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x.\end{aligned}

Since
\mu(\rho)=\rho^{2}\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)
, we can express the integrand in the last term on the right-hand side as

\rho\left(\partial_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}u)+(\partial_{x}u)(\partial_{x}\varphi)\right)=\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u),

and thus

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)\,\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u)(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho))^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\quad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x.\end{split}

This yields

\displaystyle I_{2}=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x.

For
I_{3}
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}I_{3}&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[\phi*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x.\end{split}

Similarly, as before, we can find

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\partial_{t}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x

(3.6)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\left((\partial_{x}u)(\partial_{x}\varphi)+\partial_{x}(\varphi^{\prime}(\rho)\rho\partial_{x}u)\right)}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

and integrating by parts in the last term of (
3.5
), and using the continuity equation, we obtain

\begin{split}-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x&=\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\rho u)\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x=-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{t}\rho\,\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x=-\tau\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}(\rho).\end{split}

This gives

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[\phi*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\tau\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}(\rho)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}(\mu(\rho)\partial_{x}u)\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Putting
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
together, we arrive at

\begin{split}I_{2}+I_{3}=&-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[(\phi-\partial_{xx}W)*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\qquad-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u[\partial_{xx}W*,u]\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\tau\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{F}(\rho).\end{split}

We then combine the above with (
3.3
) to express (
3.4
) as:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)+\tau\mathcal{F}(\rho)\right)&=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[(\phi-\partial_{xx}W)*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\quad-\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{(\phi+\partial_{xx}W)(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&\quad-\tau\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\cr&=:J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4}.\end{aligned}

Note that

J_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho[(\phi-\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})*,u]\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)}\,{\rm{d}}x,

and

J_{3}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{(\phi+\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})(x-y)(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x.

Since

|\phi-\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}}|\leq(1+c_{\phi,W})\phi+c_{W},

we obtain

\displaystyle|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq c_{W}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{|u(x)-u(y)||\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)(x)|\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+(1+c_{\phi,W})\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\phi(x-y)|u(x)-u(y)||\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)(x)|\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x=:J_{2,1}+J_{2,2}.

We then estimate the term
J_{2,1}
as

\displaystyle J_{2,1}

\displaystyle\leq c_{W}^{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{W}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Next, we estimate
J_{2,2}
to have

\displaystyle\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\phi(x-y)|u(x)-u(y)||\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)(x)|\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\qquad=\int\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}{\sqrt{\phi(x-y)}|u(x)-u(y)|\sqrt{\rho(x)\rho(y)}\sqrt{\phi(x-y)\rho(y)}\sqrt{\rho(x)}|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)(x)|}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\|\phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Together, this gives

J_{2}\leq 2c_{W}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+(1+c_{\phi,W})^{2}\|\phi\ast\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

For the term
J_{3}
, we observe that the part corresponding to
\varphi+(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{+}
is nonpositive. Hence, we only need to control the contribution due to
(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}})^{-}
;
we use

-c_{\phi,W}\phi\leq-\bigl(\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}}\bigr)^{-}\leq\phi+\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}}

to obtain

J_{3}\leq\frac{1}{2}c_{\phi,W}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

Combining the estimate above yields

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}(\rho,u)+\tau\mathcal{F}(\rho)\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-(\tau-2c_{W}^{2})\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt+\left(\frac{1}{2}c_{\phi,W}+(1+c_{\phi,W})^{2}\|\phi*\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}+\tau\mathcal{F}(\rho)+\lambda\mathcal{E}\right)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mu(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-c_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho,u).

We finally choose
\lambda>0
large enough so that

\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\coloneq(1+\lambda)\tau-2c_{W}^{2}>0,\qquad c_{\lambda}\coloneq\frac{\lambda}{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2}c_{\phi,W}+(1+c_{\phi,W})^{2}\|\phi*\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)>0,

to conclude the desired result.
∎

4.
Existence of regular solutions to the approximate system

The goal of this section is to discuss the existence and uniqueness of an approximate system and to deduce the functional inequalities seen in Section
3
for the solution of the approximate system.

4.1.
The approximate system

We approximate our system on the whole space
\mathbb{R}
by a family of problems on a bounded domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\coloneq(-R_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})\subset\mathbb{R}
,
\varepsilon>0
,
R_{\varepsilon}>0
and look for regular solutions
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
to the approximate system, where the dependence of
R_{\varepsilon}>0
on
\varepsilon>0
will be clarified later.

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})

\displaystyle=-\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)+\partial_{x}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)\partial_{x}u)+\rho[\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon},u]\rho-\tau\rho u,

where

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\rho)\coloneq\varphi(\rho)+\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}\alpha}{\alpha-1}\rho^{\alpha-1},\quad\alpha\in(0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}),\quad\beta=\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}+1,\quad\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)\coloneq\rho^{2}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\rho)=\mu(\rho)+\varepsilon^{\beta}\alpha\rho^{\alpha},\\
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)\coloneq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\int_{0}^{\rho(x)}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(r)\,\mathrm{d}r}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho(x)W*^{\varepsilon}\rho(x)}\,{\rm{d}}x,\qquad\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\rho)+W*^{\varepsilon}\rho,\\
W*^{\varepsilon}\rho\coloneq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}W(\cdot-y)\rho(y)\,{\rm{d}}y,\qquad[\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon},u]\rho\coloneq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}(\cdot-y)(u(y)-u(\cdot))\rho(y)\,{\rm{d}}y.\end{gathered}

Here,
\phi_{\varepsilon}
is a regularized symmetric communication weight satisfying

\phi_{\varepsilon}\to\phi\quad\text{in\;\;$L^{\gamma^{\prime}}$},\quad\gamma^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}.

To simplify notation, we drop the upper-indexes
\varepsilon
in all places corresponding to the sequence and only keep it to denote the truncation of the convolution terms. Note that both
\rho
and
u
are only defined on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
while the interaction potential
W
and communication weight
\phi_{\varepsilon}
are defined on
\mathbb{R}
.

We further associate the following functionals to the regularized system:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}_{\varepsilon}(\rho,u)+\tau\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)+\lambda\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho,u),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\lambda\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c_{\lambda}\mathcal{K}_{\phi^{\varepsilon}}(\rho,u),

with the driving functional

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\gamma}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho\,W*^{\varepsilon}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x,

and the total and modulated energies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho,u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho),\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\text{mod}}_{\varepsilon}(\rho,u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho).

4.2.
The approximate initial data

Here, we discuss the approximation of the initial data and show the convergences of the approximated energy functionals.

We first recall our main assumptions on the initial data:

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\rho_{0}\geq 0,\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\partial_{x}(\rho_{0}^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho_{0}u_{0}^{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\\
|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad 0<\kappa\leq\min\left\{2\gamma-1,\frac{2}{\gamma}\right\}.\end{gathered}

Motivated by
[
19
]
, we approximate our initial density
\rho_{0}
by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\frac{1}{Z_{\varepsilon}}\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\left(\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}+\varepsilon e^{-x^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}},

where
Z_{\varepsilon}
is the normalizing constant, i.e.

Z_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=(-R_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
with
0<R_{\varepsilon}\nearrow+\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
which will be determined appropriately later. Note that
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}>0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

For the approximation of the momentum
m_{0}=\rho_{0}u_{0}
, we introduce

w_{0}\coloneq\sqrt{\rho_{0}}u_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{w}_{0}\coloneq\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}.

Note that

w_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\tilde{w}_{0}\in L^{2+\kappa}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\text{and}\quad m_{0}\in L^{\frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}}(\mathbb{R})

due to (
4.2
). We next consider an approximation sequence
\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

(4.5)

\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\tilde{w}_{0}\text{ in }L^{2+\kappa}(\mathbb{R}).

Using this newly defined approximation
\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we define our approximated initial velocity by

(4.6)

{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\frac{\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}}{{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}}\xi_{\varepsilon},

with
\xi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

{\bf 1}_{(\frac{-R_{\varepsilon}}{4},\frac{R_{\varepsilon}}{4})}\leq\xi_{\varepsilon}\leq{\bf 1}_{(\frac{-R_{\varepsilon}}{2},\frac{R_{\varepsilon}}{2})}.

Note that
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Thus, the approximated initial velocity satisfies the following homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition:

(4.7)

\begin{split}u=0\quad\text{on\;\;$\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}$}.\end{split}

Naturally, the approximation of
w_{0}
is given by

\displaystyle w_{0}^{\varepsilon}\coloneq{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\xi_{\varepsilon}=\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}}u_{0}^{\varepsilon}.

For the sequence of approximations
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
constructed above, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
R_{\varepsilon}>0
be given by

(4.8)

\displaystyle R_{\varepsilon}=a|\log\varepsilon|^{\theta}\text{ with }0<\theta<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{4(\gamma-1)\widetilde{\kappa}}\right\}\;\text{ and }a=\sqrt{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{2(\gamma-\alpha)}}>0,

for
\varepsilon<e^{-1}
, where
\widetilde{\kappa}\coloneq(2+\kappa)\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}+1
. Then we have

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{-R_{\varepsilon}}^{R_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}|^{2+\kappa}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{0}|{u}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0})\to\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda}(\rho_{0},u_{0})\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
is rather technical and lengthy, thus for smoothness of reading, we postpone the details of proof to Appendix
B
.

4.3.
Formulation of the main result

The goal of the rest of this section is to discuss the existence of a global-in-time regular solution to the approximate system (
4.1
) for fixed
\varepsilon>0
.
We will frequently make use of the following notation for function spaces:

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{k}(T)\coloneq\{f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{k}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})):\;\partial_{t}f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{k-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{k}(T)\coloneq\{g\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{k+1}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{k}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}):\partial_{t}g\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{k-1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\}.

The main result in this section is summarized in the following statement.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed and
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
be given by (
4.4
) and (
4.6
) with

(4.10)

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}=\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon}>0.

Then, for any
T>0
, there exists a unique global-in-time regular solution
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
to the system (
4.1
) with initial data
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
and boundary condition (
4.7
) such that

\displaystyle(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{X}_{2}(T)\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T)\quad\text{with}\quad\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,x)\geq c\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon}>0\quad\text{for}\;\;(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon},

where
c>0
is an
\varepsilon
-independent constant.
Moreover, there exists
\lambda>0
such that the solution satisfies the estimate

(4.11)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})\leq-\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})+a_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\right),\end{split}

with
a_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

In the case when either
\tau>0
or
\phi
is uniformly bounded from below, we find
\lambda>0
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
:

(4.12)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})\leq-(1-\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon})\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon}),\end{split}

with
\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}\geq 0
for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\tilde{a}_{\varepsilon}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem. Although local existence theory for regular solutions to system (
4.1
) without the non-local terms is by now classical, it does not directly apply to our case. For this reason, we outline the proof of the following result in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
be given by (
4.4
), (
4.6
) and satisfy (
4.10
). Moreover, set

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}\coloneq\|\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{3}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}<+\infty.

Then, there exist some
T_{0}=T_{0}(\varepsilon,A_{\varepsilon},\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon})>0
and a unique regular solution
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
to (
4.1
) on the interval
[0,T_{0})
.

Let us now denote the maximal time of existence of the regular solution by
T_{\max}
. Our next step is to derive the estimates that are independent of
T_{\max}
and in consequence allow us to extend the solution to arbitrary large time interval
[0,T)
. The crucial estimate here is the positive bound from below for the density, which is a consequence of the
\varepsilon
-dependent approximation.

4.4.
Total energy and modulated energy esimates

We begin by estimating the total energy
\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}
along solutions of the approximate system. Due to the regularity of
(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})
, we can rigorously compute the temporal derivative of the total energy to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\tau\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}_{\phi_{\varepsilon}}(\rho,u)\leq 0.

In particular, the energy is nonincreasing with

(4.13)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\varepsilon}(t))\leq\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon}),\end{split}

which provides uniform estimates for the kinetic energy and internal energy since the interaction potential
W
is assumed to be bounded from below. Hence,

\mathcal{F}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))=\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))+\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}}{1-\alpha}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\alpha}(t)}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})+\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}}{1-\alpha}(2R_{\varepsilon})^{1-\alpha}.

Choosing
\beta
and
R_{\varepsilon}
appropriately, one obtains
\varepsilon^{\beta}R_{\varepsilon}^{1-\alpha}\to 0
, which consequently implies

c_{\rho}\coloneq\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\gamma}}<+\infty.

To obtain the functional inequality (
4.11
), we perform similar computations as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
to arrive at the differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq-\mathcal{D}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}+\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon},

with the error term
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\geq 0
, that can be estimated from above by

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon}\leq 3c_{\rho}\|{\phi_{\varepsilon}}-\phi\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)|u^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\right).

Since
{\phi_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
\phi
in
L^{\gamma}
, (
4.11
) holds with
a_{\varepsilon}\coloneq 3c_{\rho}\|{\phi_{\varepsilon}}-\phi\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}}
.

Summarizing the above estimates, we obtain the following a-priori estimates

(4.14)

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)|u^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\mathcal{F}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\right)\\
&\qquad+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+\tau\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}t\leq C,\end{split}

for some
\varepsilon
-independent constant
C>0
.

4.5.
Uniform estimates for the density

To deduce a-priori estimates for the density from the lemmas above, we need to analyze the structure of the term
\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
. To do so, we first estimate the total mass and the higher-order moments of the density.

Lemma 4.4
.

For regular solutions
(\rho,u)
on time interval
[0,T_{\rm max})
, and for
\kappa>0
, we have

(4.15a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=1\qquad\text{for all $t\in[0,T_{\max})$},

(4.15b)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\rm max})}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{2}\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq C(T_{\rm max},data),

(4.15c)

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq C_{1}(\kappa)\int_{\mathbb{R}}{|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x+C_{2}(\kappa)\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho(t,x)|u(t,x)|^{2+\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Proof.

Integrating the continuity equation (
4.1a
) w.r.t. the spatial variable, and using the zero Dirichlet boundary condition, we obtain

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=0,

and so, integrating w.r.t. time, we see that (
4.15a
) holds.

Next, multiplying continuity equation (
4.1a
) by
x^{2}
and integrating by parts, we obtain

(4.16)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{x^{2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x=2\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{x\rho u}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{x^{2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x,\end{split}

and we conclude the proof of (
4.15b
) by the Gronwall argument using estimates from (
4.14
) .

At last, we multiply the continuity equation by
|x|^{2+\kappa}
to check that

\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{x^{2}|x|^{\kappa}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x=(2+\kappa)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{x|x|^{\kappa}\rho u}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Applying the Young inequality to the r.h.s., we directly obtain

(4.17)

\begin{split}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x&\leq C_{1}(\kappa)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{\kappa+2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+C_{2}(\kappa)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{2+\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x.\qed\end{split}

Corollary 4.5
.

For regular solutions, we have that

(4.18)

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}\Biggl\{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{(\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,x)\right|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}\alpha^{2}}{(\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})^{2}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,x)\right|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\Biggr\}\leq C,\end{split}

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon>0
.

In particular, there exists a constant
c_{\infty}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon>0
such that

(4.19)

\begin{split}\|\rho\|_{C([0,T_{\max}]\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon})}\leq c_{\infty}.\end{split}

Moreover, for some
c_{\varepsilon}>0
depending on
\varepsilon>0
, we have

(4.20)

\begin{split}\|\rho^{-1}\|_{C([0,T_{\max}]\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon})}\leq c_{\varepsilon},\qquad\text{with\; $c_{\varepsilon}\to+\infty$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.}\end{split}

Proof.

From Lemma
4.15
and the fact that

|\partial_{x}W*\rho(x)|\leq C\left(1+|x|+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|\,\rho(x)\,{\rm{d}}x\right)\leq C(1+|x|),

we conclude from (
4.15b
) that

(4.21)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho(x)|\partial_{x}W*\rho(x)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq C\left(1+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{2}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x\right)\leq C.\end{split}

Therefore, we may now deduce from (
4.14
) that

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|\partial_{x}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x=\sup_{t\in[0,T_{\max})}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\biggl|\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}\alpha}{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\biggr|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq C.\end{split}

Note that since

\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}\alpha}{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}=\varepsilon^{\beta}\gamma\alpha\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\partial_{x}\rho|^{2}\geq 0,

this estimate directly implies (
4.18
).

Using this estimate, we first see how the density is bounded from below. From (
4.15a
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho(t,x)}\,{\rm{d}}x=1\quad\text{for all $t\in[0,T_{\max})$}.

Since
\rho
is a continuous function, there exist some
x_{\varepsilon}(t)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}
for each
t\in[0,T_{\max})
such that

\begin{split}\rho(t,x_{\varepsilon}(t))=\frac{1}{2R_{\varepsilon}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, for every
(t,x)\in[0,T_{\max})\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\big(\rho(t,x)\big)^{\alpha-1/2}-\big(\rho(t,\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(t))\big)^{\alpha-1/2}

\displaystyle=\int_{x_{\varepsilon}(t)}^{x}\partial_{y}\rho^{\alpha-1/2}(t,y)\,{\rm{d}}y

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{|x-\bar{x}_{\varepsilon}(t)|}\|\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})},

which then implies

\displaystyle\big(\rho(t,x)\big)^{\alpha-1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2R_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\alpha-1/2}+\sqrt{2R_{\varepsilon}}\,\|\partial_{x}\rho^{\alpha-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

Finally, owing to the second part of (
4.18
), we obtain

\rho^{-1}(t,x)\leq c_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all $(t,x)\in[0,T_{\max})\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}$},

for some constant
c_{\varepsilon}
with
c_{\varepsilon}\to+\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, thus proving (
4.20
).

Proving the estimate (
4.19
) is very similar, and uses the first part of the estimate (
4.18
). Indeed, as before, we have that

\begin{split}\rho^{\gamma}(t,x)&=\rho^{\gamma}(t,x_{\varepsilon}(t))+\int_{x_{\varepsilon}(t)}^{x}\partial_{y}\rho^{\gamma}(t,y)\,{\rm{d}}y\\
&=\rho^{\gamma}(t,x_{\varepsilon}(t))+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\int_{x_{\varepsilon}(t)}^{x}\sqrt{\rho(t,y)}\,\partial_{y}\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\leq\left(\frac{1}{2R_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.\end{split}

The last term is again controlled thanks to (
4.18
), and so the proof of (
4.19
) is complete. Observe that the previous estimate is uniform w.r.t.
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

4.6.
Conclusion of the proof of Theorem
4.2

With these estimates at hand, we may extend our local-in-time solution to a global in-time regular solution, and the proof for the first part of Theorem
4.2
is complete, and the estimates above are valid for any
T>0
instead of
T_{\rm max}
.

As for the second part when either
\tau>0
or
\phi
is uniformly bounded from below, then
\lambda>0
can be chosen, independently of
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\ell_{\tau,\lambda}>0
. We then choose
\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
small such that

a_{\varepsilon}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\ell_{\tau,\lambda}\right\}\qquad\text{for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})$},

which can be done since

as

. Since the term on the right-hand side of (

) is contained in

5.
Compactness results for approximate solutions

The estimates obtained in Section
4
(cf. (
4.14
)) are insufficient to pass to the limit in the convective term in (
4.1
), which, at this point, is only known to be bounded in
L^{1}
due to kinetic energy estimate (

5.1.
Derivation of the Mellet-Vasseur inequality

We start with the better velocity estimate in the spirit of the Mellet-Vasseur estimate
[
39
]
, but on a bounded domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=(-R_{\varepsilon},R_{\varepsilon})
. Note that due to zero Dirichlet boundary condition for
u^{\varepsilon}
, this is indeed possible, and we have the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\kappa\in(0,2\alpha]
, let

\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}(\rho,u)\coloneq\frac{1}{2+\kappa}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{2+\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Then, for regular approximate solutions, we have

\begin{split}&\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathcal{E}_{\kappa}(t)+(1+\kappa)\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|u|^{\kappa}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t+\tau\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{k+2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c(\rho_{0},u_{0},T).\end{split}

Proof.

Multiplying the momentum equation (
4.1b
) by
u|u|^{\kappa}
, multiplying the continuity equation (
4.1a
) by
|u|^{2+\kappa}/(2+\kappa)
, and adding up the obtained expressions integrated over space, we obtain

(5.1)

\begin{split}&\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\frac{\rho|u|^{2+\kappa}}{2+\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x+(\kappa+1)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)|u|^{\kappa}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\tau\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{k+2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho\partial_{x}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\rho)u|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho\partial_{x}W*^{\varepsilon}\rho u|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho u|u|^{\kappa}[\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon},u]\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.\end{split}

Thanks to zero Dirichlet boundary condition for
u
,
I_{1}
can be integrated by parts and estimated as

(5.2)

\begin{split}I_{1}&=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma}+\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha})u|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x=(\kappa+1)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\gamma}+\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha})|u|^{\kappa}\partial_{x}u}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\leq\frac{(\kappa+1)}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(\gamma\rho^{\gamma}+\varepsilon\alpha\rho^{\alpha})|u|^{\kappa}|\partial_{x}u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c(\kappa,\alpha,\gamma)\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(\rho^{\gamma}+\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha})|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x.\end{split}

The first term can be absorbed into the left-hand side of (
5.1
), while to control the second term, we use Young’s inequality to write for
\kappa\leq 2\alpha
,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\gamma}|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x&=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left(\rho^{\gamma-\frac{\kappa}{2}}+\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha-\frac{\kappa}{2}}\right)\rho^{\frac{\kappa}{2}}|u|^{\kappa}}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho u^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\frac{2\gamma-\kappa}{2-\kappa}}}\,{\rm{d}}x+c\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{2-\kappa}}\rho^{\frac{2\alpha-\kappa}{2-\kappa}}}\,{\rm{d}}x.\end{split}

In the inequality above, the first term can be controlled by the energy estimate (
4.14
), the second one is bounded by the
L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}
norm of
\rho
, provided that
\kappa\leq 2\alpha
, which is bounded on the account of (
4.15a
) and (
4.19
) (independently of
\varepsilon
), and the third one disappears when
\varepsilon\to 0
also on account of (
4.19
).

For term
I_{3}
, we observe that

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{u(x)|u(x)|^{\kappa}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(u(y)-u(x))\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=-\int\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{u(y)|u(y)|^{\kappa}\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(u(y)-u(x))\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x,

and so we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int\!\!\!\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left(u(x)|u(x)|^{\kappa}-u(y)|u(y)|^{\kappa}\right)(u(y)-u(x))\rho(x)\rho(y)}\,{\rm{d}}y\,{\rm{d}}x\leq 0,

due to the monotonicity of
r\mapsto r|r|^{\kappa}
.

The last difficulty is to deal with the term
I_{2}
, and in particular with the attractive part of the potential
W
, for which
|\partial_{x}W*\rho|\leq c(1+|x|)
. The constant
c
depends on the initial data and
T
via the estimate of
\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x
which follows from (
4.15a
) and (
4.15b
). Therefore, we have

\begin{split}I_{2}\leq c\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(1+|x|)\rho|u|^{\kappa+1}}\,{\rm{d}}x&=c\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left((1+|x|)^{2+\kappa}\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}\left(\rho^{\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}|u|\right)^{\kappa+1}}\,{\rm{d}}x\\
&\leq c\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(1+|x|)^{2+\kappa}\rho}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho|u|^{\kappa+2}}\,{\rm{d}}x,\end{split}

where we used Young’s inequality with
p=\kappa+2
,
p^{\prime}=\frac{\kappa+2}{\kappa+1}
.
The second term can be estimated by the l.h.s. of (
5.1
) and the Gronwall argument. To estimate the first one, we use the higher order moment estimate for
\rho
, (
4.15c
), and then we combine it with the Gronwall argument.
∎

The rest of this section is devoted to proving the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Here,
\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbbm{1}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\rho^{\varepsilon}
is the extension of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
by zero outside of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and
\varepsilon
and
R_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following hypthesis:

(5.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\,\varepsilon^{\beta}R_{\varepsilon}^{1-\alpha}<+\infty.

5.2.
Convergence of the density

5.2.1.
Strong convergence of
(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}

From the energy estimate (
4.13
), we have that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t,x)}\varphi(r)\,\mathrm{d}r\,{\rm{d}}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}}{1-\alpha}(2R_{\varepsilon})^{1-\alpha}+c,

where
c>0
is a constant uniform in
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, if
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\,\varepsilon^{\beta}R_{\varepsilon}^{1-\alpha}<+\infty
, then

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|(\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\|_{L^{1}}<+\infty.

Furthermore, from the bound on the derivative of the pressure, we obtain for all
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))^{\gamma}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))^{\gamma}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip-40.00006pt=-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))^{\gamma}\,{\rm{d}}x+\psi(R_{\varepsilon})(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,R_{\varepsilon}))^{\gamma}-\psi(-R_{\varepsilon})(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,-R_{\varepsilon}))^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\hskip-40.00006pt=-\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}(t)}\,\partial_{x}(\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\psi(R_{\varepsilon})(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,R_{\varepsilon}))^{\gamma}-\psi(-R_{\varepsilon})(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,-R_{\varepsilon}))^{\gamma}

\displaystyle\hskip-40.00006pt\leq c\|\psi\|_{\sup}\left(\|\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}(t))^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}^{2}\right)<+\infty.

Taking the supremum over all
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|\psi\|_{\sup}\leq 1
yields

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|D(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\|_{TV}<+\infty,

which together with the previous bound implies that

\bigl((\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\bigr)_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{R}))\quad\text{is bounded}.

We now prove the equi-continuity property of the family of curves
\{t\mapsto(\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
. Owing to the regularity of the curve
t\mapsto\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon}
, we can take the temporal derivative to deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\gamma\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-1}\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\Bigl(\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})}\|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}}^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu(\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{\varepsilon}))}\Bigr),

which, due to the energy estimate (
4.13
) and (
4.18
), implies that the sequence

\bigl(\partial_{t}(\tilde{\rho}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\bigr)_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\quad\text{is bounded}.

Since the embedding
BV(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the Aubin–Lions lemma then yields the relative compactness of the sequence
((\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma})_{\varepsilon>0}
in
C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
, i.e. there exists a curve
\chi\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{R}))
and a (not relabelled) subsequence, such that

(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))^{\gamma}\to\chi(t)\quad\text{strongly in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ and pointwise-a.e.\ for every $t\in[0,T]$}.

5.2.2.
Strong convergence result

Setting
\rho(t)\coloneq\chi^{1/\gamma}(t)
for every
t\in[0,T]
, we notice that also
\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)\to\rho(t)
pointwise. Since
(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))_{\varepsilon>0}
is tight and the uniform
L^{\gamma}
-integrability gives

\int_{B}\rho^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq|B|^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{\gamma}}}\|\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}}\qquad\text{for every Borel set $B\subset\mathbb{R}$},

this implies that
(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t))_{\varepsilon}
is equi-absolutely continuous for every
t\in[0,T]
, and we conclude with the Vitali convergence theorem that

\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)\to\rho(t)\quad\text{strongly in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ and pointwise-a.e.\ in $\mathbb{R}$ for every $t\in[0,T]$}.

In particular, we obtain the strong convergence in every
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p\geq 1
.

5.3.
Convergence of the momentum

5.3.1.
Weak convergence result

We begin with a weak convergence result for the sequence of approximate momentum
(m^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
, relying only on energy estimates obtained in (
4.4
) and Lemma
5.1
. From the energy estimate (
4.13
), Lemma
5.1
, and (
4.19
) we easily find that

(m^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}\cap L^{1+\kappa}(\mathbb{R}))\quad\text{is a bounded sequence.}

Moreover, using the momentum equation, we have for all
\psi\in\text{Lip}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\psi\,m^{\varepsilon}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}\psi\|_{\sup}\Bigl(\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}^{2}+\sqrt{\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}}}\|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}))}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\hskip 40.00006pt+\|\psi\|_{\sup}\Bigl(\sqrt{\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon})}+\|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}+\tau\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}\Bigr),

thereby implying that the sequence

(\partial_{t}m^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;\text{Lip}_{b}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime})\quad\text{is bounded}.

Consequently, we obtain from an Arzela-Ascoli-type argument, the existence of a (not relabelled) subsequence and a curve
m\in C([0,T];L^{1}\cap L^{1+\kappa}(\mathbb{R}))
such that

m^{\varepsilon}(t)\rightharpoonup m(t)\quad\text{weakly in $L^{1}\cap L^{1+\kappa}(\mathbb{R})$ for every $t\in[0,T]$.}

Owing to the weak-
*
lower semicontinuity of the functional (cf. Proposition
6.1
below)

(\rho,m)\mapsto\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\frac{|m|^{2}}{\rho}}\,{\rm{d}}x&\text{if\; $m\,{\rm{d}}x\ll\rho\,{\rm{d}}x$},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\frac{|m(t)|^{2}}{\rho(t)}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\frac{|m^{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}}{\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)}}\,{\rm{d}}x<+\infty\qquad\text{for every $t\in[0,T]$}.

Therefore, we can define

\begin{split}u(t,x)\coloneq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{m}{\rho}&\text{for\; }\rho(t,x)>0,\\
0&\text{for\; }\rho(t,x)=0.\end{cases}\qquad\text{for every $t\in[0,T]$}.\end{split}

5.3.2.
Strong convergence result for an auxiliary sequence

To prove the strong convergence of the momentum, we first prove the convergence of the sequence
((\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
.

For almost every
t\in[0,T]
, we easily deduce

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\,{\rm{d}}t\leq c_{\infty}^{(2\gamma-1)}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}^{2}\,{\rm{d}}t<+\infty.

Furthermore, we obtain, for almost every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}{\gamma}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\,{\rm{d}}x-\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\psi\,\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\,\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\gamma}\sqrt{c_{\infty}}\|\psi\|_{\sup}\Bigl(\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}\|\partial_{x}(\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}+\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}}^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}))}\Bigr),

for some constant
c_{\gamma}>0
depending only on
\gamma\geq 1
. Therefore, taking the supremum over all
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|\psi\|_{\sup}\leq 1
, squaring and then integrating over time yields

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{0}^{T}\|(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\|_{TV}^{2}\,{\rm{d}}t<+\infty,

which, together with the previous estimate, implies that

\bigl((\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\bigr)_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;BV(\mathbb{R}))\quad\text{is bounded}.

As in the prove for the convergence of the density, we need to show the equi-continuity property of the curves
\{t\mapsto(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
in an appropriate function space. Recall that
u^{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}+u^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}=-\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon})+[\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon}]\rho^{\varepsilon}

for
\rho^{\varepsilon}
-almost every
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. For any
\psi\in\text{Lip}_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, we then compute to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{{\rm{d}}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,u^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{t}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip-80.00012pt=-(\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,u^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}m^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,u^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip-80.00012pt-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}[\phi\ast,u^{\varepsilon}]\rho^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip-80.00012pt=-(\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,u^{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\hskip-80.00012pt-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x-\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}[\phi\ast,u^{\varepsilon}]\rho^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x,

\displaystyle\hskip-80.00012pt=-(\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}})I_{1}+I_{2}-I_{3}-I_{4}-I_{5}+I_{6}.

where each of the terms above can be estimated by

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\psi\,\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c_{\infty}^{\gamma}\|\psi\|_{\sup}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}\|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}))}

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c_{\infty}^{\gamma}\|\partial_{x}\psi\|_{\sup}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma}\sqrt{\rho^{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c_{\infty}^{\gamma}\|\psi\|_{\sup}\|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\rho^{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{x}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c_{\infty}^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\partial_{x}\psi\|_{\sup}\sqrt{\|\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}}}\|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu(\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon}))}

\displaystyle I_{5}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})\,\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq\gamma c_{\infty}^{\gamma}\|\psi\|_{\sup}\|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\|\partial_{x}u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{t}^{\varepsilon}))}

\displaystyle I_{6}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\psi\,(\rho^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}[\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon}]\rho^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x\leq c_{\infty}^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\psi\|_{\sup}\sqrt{\|\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\mathcal{K}_{\phi}(\rho^{\varepsilon},u_{t}^{\varepsilon})}.

Since all the terms on the right-hand side are 2-integrable in time, we deduce that

\bigl(\partial_{t}[(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}]\bigr)_{\varepsilon>0}\subset L^{2}(0,T;\text{Lip}_{b}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}).

With the compact embedding
BV(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and the continuity of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\text{Lip}_{b}(\mathbb{R})^{\prime}
, we again apply the Aubin-Lions lemma to obtain the existence of a (not relabelled) subsequence and a curve
\eta\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))\cap L^{2}([0,T];BV(\mathbb{R}))
, such that

(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\to\eta\quad\text{strongly in $L^{2}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))$ and pointwise-a.e.\ in $[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}$}.

To identify the limit
\eta
, we simply notice that for every
\psi\in C_{c}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\psi\,\eta}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\psi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\psi\,\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,m}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t,

where the second equality follows from the strong convergence of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
in any
L^{p}
-space and the weak convergence of
m^{\varepsilon}
in
L^{2+\kappa}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})
. By density, we then conclude that

(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\to\rho^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,m\quad\text{strongly in $L^{2}([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}))$ }

and

(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}\to\rho^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,u\quad\text{a.e.\ in $[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}$}.

5.3.3.
Strong convergence of
m^{\varepsilon}/\sqrt{\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}}

An important consequence of the strong convergence of the sequence
(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}
and of the uniform (in
\varepsilon>0
) Mellet-Vasseur estimate from Lemma
5.1
is the convergence

(5.9)

\displaystyle g^{\varepsilon}\coloneq\sqrt{\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}}u^{\varepsilon}\to\sqrt{\rho}u=:g\quad\text{strongly in $L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})$},

which we now prove, resulting in the convergence of the convective term.

Consider, as before, the set
A_{\delta}\coloneq\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}:\rho_{t}(x)>\delta\}
,
\delta>0
. Then,

g^{\varepsilon}(t,x)=\frac{(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t,x))^{\gamma-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}m^{\varepsilon}(t,x)}{(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t,x))^{\gamma}}\to\frac{(\rho(t,x))^{\gamma+\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\,u(t,x)}{(\rho(t,x))^{\gamma}}=g(t,x)\quad\text{for a.e.\ $(t,x)\in A_{\delta}$.}

We then use Lemma
5.1
and the second moment estimate to obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{[0,T]\times B_{n}^{c}}|g^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\delta}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq n^{-\frac{2\kappa}{2+\kappa}}\left(\iint_{[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}|x|^{2}\,\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\right)^{\frac{\kappa}{2+\kappa}}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2+\kappa}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})}^{2},

which implies the existence of some constant
c_{2}>0
, independent of
n\geq 1
, such that

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\iint_{[0,T]\times B_{n}^{c}}|g^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\delta}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\leq c_{2}n^{-\frac{2\kappa}{2+\kappa}}\qquad\forall\,n\geq 1,

i.e.,
(g^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
is uniformly integrable in
L^{2}(A_{\delta})
. Using again Lemma
5.1
together with the uniform
L^{\gamma}
-bound of
(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
, we obtain for every Borel set
B\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
,

(5.10)

\displaystyle\iint_{B}|g^{\varepsilon}|^{2}\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\delta}}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t\leq|B|^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\frac{\kappa}{2+\kappa}}\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\gamma}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{\kappa}{2+\kappa}}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2+\kappa}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R},\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon})}^{2},

which gives the equi-absolute continuity of
(g^{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon>0}
in
L^{2}(A_{\delta})
, and therewith the convergence

\displaystyle\|g^{\varepsilon}-g\|_{L^{2}(A_{\delta})}\longrightarrow 0\qquad\text{by Vitali's convergence theorem.}

On the set when the density is close to zero, Lemma
5.1
and estimate similar to (
5.10
) imply that

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\to 0}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\iint_{A_{\delta}^{c}}|g^{\varepsilon}-g|^{2}\,{\rm{d}}x\,{\rm{d}}t=0,

which concludes the proof of (
5.9
).

5.4.
Convergence of
\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}

We first notice that

(5.11)

\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{t}-\rho_{t}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\to 0,\qquad\|f\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}}\coloneq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|^{\alpha}f\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

Indeed, for any
\eta\geq 1
, we observe

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}_{2}(B(0,\eta))}+\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\backslash B(0,\eta))}

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{2}\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}(B(0,\eta))}+\frac{1}{\eta^{\kappa}}\left(\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}_{2+\kappa}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{1}_{2+\kappa}}\right).

Thus, taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, and then sending
\eta\to\infty
give (
5.11
).

Based on the assumptions on
W
, we have that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}W(x-y)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq c(1+|x|^{2})\|f\|_{L^{1}}+c\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\qquad\forall\,f\in L^{1}_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Then, we estimate

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}W*\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho W*\rho\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(W*(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho))\|_{L^{1}}+\|(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)(W*\rho)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\right)

for some
c>0
independent of
\varepsilon>0
. Using (
5.11
), we then obtain

\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\to\rho\,W\ast\rho\quad\text{strongly in $L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$ for almost every $t\geq 0$}.

5.5.
Convergence of
\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}

For any
\varphi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}))
and compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{K}\varphi(t,x)\left(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho\right){\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{K}\varphi\,(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)\,\partial_{x}W*\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,{\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}t+\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{K}\varphi\,\rho\,\partial_{x}W*(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)\,{\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}t=:I_{\varepsilon}+II_{\varepsilon}.

For
I_{\varepsilon}
, we have

|I_{\varepsilon}|\leq C(c_{W},K,T)\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{1}(K))}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}}+\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{1}})\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. As for
II_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|II_{\varepsilon}|

\displaystyle\leq C(c_{W},K,T)\|\rho\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\|(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)(t)\|_{L^{1}}+\|(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)(t)\|_{L^{1}_{1}})\to 0

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Together, this yields the convergence

\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\,\partial_{x}W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\rho\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho\quad\text{weakly in $L^{1}(0,T;L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}))$}.

5.6.
Convergence of
\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}

Similarly, we consider a test function
\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}))
and a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let us choose
\eta\geq 1
such that
|K|\leq\eta
. Then,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{K}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t,x)\phi(x-y)(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)(t,y)\,{\rm{d}}y{\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{T}\!\!\int_{K}\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta+1}+\int_{|y|\geq\eta+1}\right)\varphi(t,x)\phi(x-y)(\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho)(t,y)\,{\rm{d}}y{\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}t\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(-\eta-1,\eta+1))}\|\phi 1_{|\cdot|\leq 2\eta+1}\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times K)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(\eta+1)^{2}}\|\phi 1_{|\cdot|\geq 1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}((0,T)\times K)}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Bigl(\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}_{2}}+\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{1}_{2}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}-\rho\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times(-\eta-1,\eta+1))}+\frac{C}{(\eta+1)^{2}},

where
C>0
is independent of
\varepsilon
and
\eta
. Thus, by sending
\varepsilon\to 0
first and then
\eta\to\infty
, we conclude the desired convergence, i.e.

\phi\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\phi\ast\rho\quad\text{weakly-$*$ in $L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}))$}.

6.
Recovery of the original system: existence of weak solutions

To recover weak solutions to (
1.1
), we make use of the convergences provided in Section
5
to first prove that the functional inequality obtain in Section
4
(cf. (
4.14
)) is retained when
\varepsilon\to 0
(cf. Theorem
6.4
). We then conclude in the same result that the limit pair
(\rho,\sqrt{\rho}u)
obtained in Section
5
satisfies the weak formulation (
2.3
) and (
2.4
). The main difficulty is that, while extension of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
to the whole space is possible, for the gradient of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
it is not, due to the discontinuity at the boundary of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore, we reformulate our functional inequalities in terms of measures that allow us to integrate over the whole space and easily pass to the limit in all the energy-type estimates.
We will use the following lower semicontinuity result w.r.t. distributional convergence of measures for integral functionals with lower semicontinuous and convex integrands.

A sequence of measures
(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
on Borel set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to converge distributionally to
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
if

\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)\,\nu_{n}({\rm{d}}x)\longrightarrow\int_{\Omega}\psi(x)\,\nu({\rm{d}}x)\qquad\forall\,\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

In this case, we simply write
\nu_{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}\nu
in
\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega)
.

The following is a corollary of a classical result in the Calculus of Variations (see eg.
[
1
]
).

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}\to[0,+\infty]
be a superlinear, convex, and lower semicontinuous function. Then, the functional

\mathcal{M}^{+}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R})\ni(\nu,\eta)\mapsto\mathcal{G}(\nu,\eta)\coloneq\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}g\biggl(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\eta}{\,\mathrm{d}\nu}\biggr)\,\mathrm{d}\nu&\displaystyle\text{if $\eta\ll\nu$},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

t
and
\varepsilon
, where we used the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|[\partial_{xx}{\widetilde{W}}*^{\varepsilon},u^{\varepsilon}]\rho^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c(1+\|\phi*^{\varepsilon}\rho^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}})^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Hence, we have also that
I_{2}+I_{3}\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
. Combining all of the above estimates shows (

). On the other hand, by (

) and (

), we obtain

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\in L^{1}(0,\infty),

and subsequently, this, together with (
7.3
) and (
7.4
), yields

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x,\ \int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\in W^{1,1}(0,\infty).

This shows

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0,\qquad\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0,

due to
[
34
]
, and consequently also

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}))^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0\qquad\text{as $t\to\infty$}.

Here, we would like to remark that the above estimates in
W^{1,1}(0,\infty)
are independent of
\varepsilon>0
.

On the other hand, by using almost the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
6.2
, we observe

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho\,(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho))^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}))^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho|u|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x

where the right-hand sides tend to zero as
t\to\infty
.

To get more information about the limits, we further assume that the interaction potential
W
satisfies (
2.6
). Then, by invoking the estimates in the proof of Corollary
4.5
, we deduce

\|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C

for some

independent of
t
. Moreover, we find

\|\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

Thus, we obtain

\sup\nolimits_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|\rho^{\gamma}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq C

from which we also find that there is a sequence
\{t_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
with
t_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
such that

(7.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \rho^{\gamma}(t_{n})&\to\nu\quad\mbox{strongly in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$ and a.e.,}\cr\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma})(t_{n})&\rightharpoonup\eta\quad\mbox{weakly in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R})$}\end{aligned}

for some
\nu\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
\eta\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Now we define
\rho_{\infty}
by

\rho_{\infty}\coloneq\nu^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}.

We first easily observe
\eta=\partial_{x}\nu
a.e. Indeed, for any
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\varphi\,\eta}\,{\rm{d}}x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\varphi\,\partial_{x}(\rho^{\gamma})(t_{n})}\,{\rm{d}}x=-\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}\varphi\,\rho^{\gamma}(t_{n})}\,{\rm{d}}x=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial_{x}\,\varphi\nu}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Now we claim that
\rho_{\infty}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rho_{\infty}(x)\left(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho_{\infty}(x))\right)^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x=0.

For this, similarly as in the proof of Lemma
6.3
, it suffices to show

\bigl[\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma}(t_{n})+\rho(t_{n})\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho(t_{n})\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x=j_{t_{n}}\rightharpoonup^{*}j_{\infty}({\rm{d}}x)\coloneq\bigl[\partial_{x}\rho_{\infty}^{\gamma}+\rho_{\infty}\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho_{\infty}\bigr]\,\mathrm{d}x.

Here due to (
7.5
), we readily see that

\partial_{x}\rho^{\gamma}(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\rightharpoonup^{*}\partial_{x}\rho_{\infty}^{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\gamma>1
, the convergence of
\rho^{\gamma}
in (

) implies
\rho(t_{n})\to\rho_{\infty}
in

. More precisely, for any bounded set

,

\|\rho(t_{n})-\rho_{\infty}\|_{L^{1}(A)}=\|(\rho(t_{n})^{\gamma})^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}-\nu^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\|_{L^{1}(A)}\leq C(|A|,\gamma)(\|\rho^{\gamma}(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}(A)}+\|\nu\|_{L^{2}(A)}).

Together with the almost everywhere convergence
\rho(t_{n})\to\rho_{\infty}
, we can then conclude the claim by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem. This yields

\rho(t_{n})\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho(t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\rightharpoonup^{*}\rho_{\infty}\,\partial_{x}W\ast\rho_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This completes the proof of Theorem
2.5
.

Remark 7.1
.

We notice from (

Appendix A
Local existence and uniqueness of solutions to the approximate system

In this appendix, we present the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to the approximate system. Let us first recall our approximate system:

(A.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{x}(\rho u)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(A.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\partial_{x}(\rho u^{2})

\displaystyle=-\rho\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho)+\partial_{x}(\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho)\partial_{x}u)+\rho[\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon},u]\rho-\tau\rho u,

with the initial data

(\rho,u)(0,x)=(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x),\quad x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},

and the boundary condition

u=0,\quad x\in\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

For simplicity, we omit the
\varepsilon
-dependence in the solutions
(\rho,u)
in (
A.1
).

Since the local existence theory for compressible Navier–Stokes system in the absence of vacuum is by now well established, we briefly sketch the idea of the proof for the local well-posedness theory for the system (
A.1
). We would like to remark that our strategy is similar to that of
[
24
]
, where the existence theory for one-dimensional compressible fluid models with periodic boundary conditions is investigated.

Step I

We first construct a sequence of approximate solutions
\{(\rho_{n},u_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
to the following system:

(A.2a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho_{n}+u_{n-1}\partial_{x}\rho_{n}=-\rho_{n-1}\partial_{x}u_{n-1},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(A.2b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{n}-\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{n}=G(\rho_{n-1},u_{n-1})

subject to the initial data, first iteration step, and boundary condition:

(\rho_{n}(0,x),u_{n}(0,x))=(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x),\quad n\in\mathbb{N},\quad x\in\Omega_{\varepsilon},

(\rho_{0}(t,x),u_{0}(t,x))=(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega_{\varepsilon},

and

u_{n}(t,x)=0,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Here
G(\rho_{n-1},u_{n-1})
is given by

G(\rho_{n-1},u_{n-1})=u_{n-1}\partial_{x}u_{n-1}-\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n-1})+\frac{\partial_{x}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n-1})}{\rho_{n-1}}\partial_{x}u_{n-1}+[\phi_{\varepsilon}*^{\varepsilon},u_{n-1}]\rho_{n-1}.

We first solve (
A.2a
) with
n=1
to get a solution
\rho_{1}
, and then solve (
A.2b
) with the obtained solution
\rho_{1}
. Due to the smoothness of initial data
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
together with the lower bound condition on
\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we have the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions
\{(\rho_{n},u_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
to the system (
A.2
) by the classical existence theory for transport and parabolic equations.

Step II

Next, we provide uniform-in-
n
bound estimates of the approximate solutions in our desired solution space. Precisely, let us assume that
(\rho_{n},u_{n})\in\mathcal{X}_{2}(T)\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T)
as given in (
4.9
) for some
T>0
with

\|(\rho_{n},u_{n})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{2}(T)\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T)}\leq M,

where
M>0
depends only on the initial data
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})
,
\varepsilon
,
T
, and
\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon}
. Then by employing the characteristic method, we deduce

\min_{(t,x)\in[0,T_{1}]\times\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{n+1}(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon}

for some small
T_{1}>0
, which depends only on
M
and
\underline{\rho}^{\varepsilon}
, and using this lower bound estimate, we can obtain

\|(\rho_{n+1},u_{n+1})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{2}(T_{0})\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T_{0})}\leq M.

for sufficiently small
T_{0}\in(0,T_{1}]
. Since
T_{1}>0
is independent of
n
, we conclude by an inductive argument that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|(\rho_{n},u_{n})\|_{\mathcal{X}_{2}(T_{0})\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T_{0})}\leq M.

Here, we would like to point out that in
[
24
]
, the uniform-in-
n
estimates are only obtained for
H^{k}
,
k\geq 2
, norms of approximate solutions. However, in our case, the homogeneous boundary condition on the velocity imposes us to estimate the time derivatives of solutions as well.

Step III

In order to pass
n\to\infty
, we show that the sequence
\{(\rho_{n},u_{n})\}_{n\geq 0}
forms a Cauchy sequence in
{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\times{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}
. Indeed, if we define

\Delta\rho_{n}\coloneq\rho_{n+1}-\rho_{n},\qquad\Delta u_{n}\coloneq u_{n+1}-u_{n},

then
\Delta\rho_{n}
and
\Delta u_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Delta\rho_{n}+u_{n-1}\partial_{x}\Delta\rho_{n}=-\Delta u_{n-1}\partial_{x}\rho_{n}-\Delta\rho_{n-1}\partial_{x}u_{n}+\rho_{n-1}\partial_{x}\Delta u_{n-1},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\Delta u_{n}-\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}}\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta u_{n}=-\left(\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n})}{\rho_{n}}-\frac{\mu_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{n-1})}{\rho_{n-1}}\right)\partial_{x}^{2}u_{n-1}+G(\rho_{n},u_{n})-G(\rho_{n-1},u_{n-1})

with

(\Delta\rho_{n},\Delta u_{n})(0,x)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta u_{n}=0\ (t,x)\in(0,T_{0})\times\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Then, we use the uniform bound estimates obtained in (Step II) to deduce

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\|\Delta\rho_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\left(\|\Delta\rho_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta\rho_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Delta u_{n-1}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

for
t\in(0,T_{0})
, where
C>0
is independent of
n
, from which we conclude the existence of a pair
(\rho,u)\in{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\times{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}
such that

(\rho_{n},u_{n})\to(\rho,u)\quad\mbox{strongly in }{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}\times{L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))}.

Moreover, our uniform-in-n upper bound estimates imply

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}\rho\text{ weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})),\quad\partial_{t}\rho_{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}\partial_{t}\rho\text{ weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})),\\
\rho_{n}\rightarrow\rho\text{ strongly in }L^{2}((0,T_{0})\times\Omega_{\varepsilon}),\quad u_{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\text{ weakly-$*$ in }L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})),\\
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})),\quad\partial_{t}u_{n}\rightharpoonup\partial_{t}u\text{ weakly in }L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})),\quad\mbox{and}\\
u_{n}\rightarrow u\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})).\end{gathered}

Thus, the limit pair
(\rho,u)
belongs to
\mathcal{X}_{2}(T_{0})\times\mathcal{Y}_{2}(T_{0})
, concluding the proof of the existence part.

Step IV

We finally adapt the Cauchy estimates in (Step III) to obtain the uniqueness of the constructed solutions.

implies that for any
p\geq\gamma-\frac{1}{2}

\|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{p}}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

In particular, we get

\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\rho_{0}\text{ in }L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R}).

Next, we claim that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|(\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0})\mathbf{1}_{{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}}\|_{L^{1}}=0.

For this, we use (
B.11
) to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}|}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\left(\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}+\varepsilon e^{-x^{2}}\right)-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|^{{\frac{1}{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{1}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{c}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\left(\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}+\varepsilon e^{-x^{2}}\right)-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}R_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}+\frac{c}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}\|\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\delta>0
will be determined later. On the other hand, we observe

\displaystyle\|\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\|\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{H^{1}},

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}|}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}R_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}+\frac{c}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}\varepsilon^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}\leq 2\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}R_{\varepsilon}+\frac{c\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}

due to
\varepsilon<1
.
Then, we choose
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}>0
to get from (
B.1
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}|}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4(\gamma-1)}}R_{\varepsilon}+c\varepsilon^{1/2}\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4(\gamma-1)}}R_{\varepsilon}^{\tilde{\kappa}}+c\varepsilon^{1/2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

On the other hand, due to the monotone convergence theorem, we find

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\rightarrow\rho_{0}\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

Hence, the triangle inequality gives
\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\mathbf{1}_{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\rightarrow\rho_{0}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. In particular, this shows
Z_{\varepsilon}\to 1
and subsequently, we have
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}\to\rho_{0}
in
L^{1}\cap L^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

For the convergence (
B.5
), similarly as before, by monotone convergence theorem, we first observe

\displaystyle|x|^{2+\kappa}\rho_{0}\mathbf{1}_{{\overline{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}}\rightarrow|x|^{2+\kappa}\rho_{0}\text{ in }L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0.

Then, for any
\delta>0
, we again use (
B.11
) to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{2+\kappa}|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}|}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{2+\kappa}\left|\left(\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}+\varepsilon e^{-x^{2}}\right)-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|^{{\frac{1}{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{(2+\kappa)\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{c}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\left|\left(\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}+\varepsilon e^{-x^{2}}\right)-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\right|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}R_{\varepsilon}^{(2+\kappa)\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}+1}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}+\frac{c}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}\|\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\ast\eta_{\varepsilon^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}-\rho_{0}^{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq c\delta^{\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}}R_{\varepsilon}^{(2+\kappa)\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}+1}+\frac{c\varepsilon^{2}}{\delta^{2({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}.

By choosing
\delta=\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4({\gamma-\frac{1}{2}})}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{|x|^{2+\kappa}|\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\rho_{0}|}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{4(\gamma-1)}}R_{\varepsilon}^{\widetilde{\kappa}}+c\varepsilon^{1/2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

where
\widetilde{\kappa}=(2+\kappa)\frac{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}{\gamma-1}+1
due to (
B.1
). Then again by using
Z_{\varepsilon}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we conclude the convergence (
B.5
).
∎

Approximation of initial velocity

In the following lemma, we show the convergence of kinetic energy and uniform-in-
\varepsilon
bound estimate on the velocity moments.

Lemma B.2
.

The following convergence and bound estimate hold:

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\mbox{and}\quad\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

By using the triangle inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and (
B.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|w_{0}^{\varepsilon}-w_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}\left(\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{w}_{0}\right)\|_{L^{2}}+\|({(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2+\kappa}}-{(\rho_{0})}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2+\kappa}})\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}}\|\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2+k}}+\|{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})}^{\frac{1}{p}}-{(\rho_{0})}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|_{L^{p}}\|\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2+\kappa}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\tilde{w}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2+k}}+\|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}-\rho_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|\tilde{w}_{0}\|_{L^{2+\kappa}}\

where
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2+\kappa}
. This together with Lemma
B.1
and (
4.5
) yields the convergence of initial kinetic energy.

For the uniform bound estimate on the velocity moments, by definition of
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}
, we easily find

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\tilde{w}^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Thus, we get from (
4.5
) that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x.

This completes the proof.
∎

We are now in a position to give the details of proof for Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

As a direct consequence of Lemma
B.2
, we get

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2+\kappa}\,\mathrm{d}x.

For the convergence of
\mathcal{J}_{\tau,\lambda,\varepsilon}
, by definition of that, it suffices to show that

(B.12)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0})\to\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0}),

and

(B.13)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x\to\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}|u_{0}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}(\rho_{0})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Note that the convergences (
B.12
) can be directly obtained from the results of Lemmas
B.1
and
B.2
. For (
B.13
), we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\left(\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})\right)}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x,

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x+\frac{\varepsilon^{\beta}\alpha}{\alpha-1}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\partial_{x}\varphi(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}))(\partial_{x}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\alpha-1})}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2\beta}\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\right)^{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\alpha-1}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x.

Here we use (
B.3
) to estimate the third term on the right-hand side of the above equality as

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2\beta}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|\partial_{x}(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\alpha-1}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=c_{\alpha}Z_{\varepsilon}^{1-2\gamma}\varepsilon^{2\beta}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}{(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{2(\alpha-\gamma)}|\partial_{x}(\widetilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}}\,{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}\varepsilon^{2\beta}\left(\varepsilon\exp{(-R_{\varepsilon}^{2})}\right)^{\frac{2(\alpha-\gamma)}{{\gamma-\frac{1}{2}}}}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad\varepsilon\to 0,

due to (
B.2
) and
Z_{\varepsilon}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This together with the convergence results of Lemmas
B.1
and
B.2
concludes the desired result.
∎

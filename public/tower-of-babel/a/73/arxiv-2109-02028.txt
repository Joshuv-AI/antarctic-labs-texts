A high-order and fast scheme with variable time steps for the time-fractional Black-Scholes equation
Kerui Song, Pin Lyu

Abstract
In this paper, a high-order and fast numerical method is investigated for the time-fractional Black-Scholes equation. In order to deal with the typical weak initial singularities of the solution, we construct a finite difference scheme with variable time steps, where the fractional derivative is approximated by the nonuniform Alikhanov formula and the sum-of-exponentials (SOE) technique. In the spatial direction, an average approximation with fourth-order accuracy is employed. The stability and the convergence with second-order in time and fourth-order in space of the proposed scheme are religiously derived by the energy method. Numerical examples are given to demonstrate the theoretical statement.



Key words:
time-fractional Black-Scholes equation; high-order method; variable time steps, fast algorithm

1
Introduction

In recent years, the option theory has been widely used in financial and economic fields, so the study of option pricing becomes more important in both theoretical significance and practical application. The Black-Scholes model, a second-order parabolic partial differential equation related to stock price and time, is used for pricing European or American put and call options on stock
[
1
]
.

With the proposals of the fractional partial differential equation about stochastic model and financial theory, a growing number of scholars began to study fractional option pricing model and made great progress. Wyss
[
27
]
considered the pricing of option derivatives under a time-fractional Black-Scholes equation preliminarily by replacing the time first-order derivative by a fractional derivative of order
\alpha~(0<\alpha\leq 1)
, and derived a closed-form solution for European vanilla options. Cartea and del-Castillo-Negrete
[
2
]
displayed that some particular Lévy processes satisfy a fractional partial differential equation, and employed numerical methods to solve the related fractional models in order to price exotic options, in particular barrier options. Jumarie
[
8
,
9
]
applied the fractional Taylor formula to remove the effects of the non-zero initial value of the function. Under the Itô lemma of fractional order illustrated in the special case of a fractional growth with white noise, they derived the time and space fractional Black-Scholes equations. By assuming that the stock price dynamics follows a fractional Itô process, Liang et al.
[
12
,
13
]
proposed a bi-fractional Black-Merton-Scholes model of option pricing. A slightly simplified version based on Liang et al.’s model
[
13
]
was then discussed in Chen et al.
[
5
]
, they assumed that the underlying asset price is consistent with the classical Brownian motion, and the spatial-fractional derivative in the governing equation disappears, but the time-fractional derivative remains.

Since the analytical solution of the fractional differential equations are always hard to find, it is necessary to study efficient numerical methods for the related problems. In this paper, we will discuss a high-order finite difference method for the time-factional Black-Scholes equation
[
5
]
:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle{\frac{\partial^{\alpha}C}{\partial\zeta^{\alpha}}}+\frac{1}{2}{\varrho^{2}}S^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}C}{\partial S^{2}}+(r-D)S\frac{\partial C}{\partial S}-rC=0,\quad(S,\tau)\in\tilde{\Omega}\times[0,T),\\
C(S,T)=R(S),\qquad S_{l}<S<S_{r},\\
C(S_{l},\zeta)=P(\zeta),~C(S_{r},\zeta)=Q(\zeta),\qquad{\zeta}\in[0,T),\end{array}

(1.1)

where
C(S,\tau)
is the time-
\tau
price of a European-style double barrier option with the underlying
S
,
\tilde{\Omega}=(S_{l},S_{r})\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\zeta
is the current time,
T
is the expiry,
r
is the risk-free interest rate,
D
is the dividend yield and
{\varrho}
is the volatility of the returns. The functions
P
and
Q
are the rebates paid when the corresponding barrier is hit, and
R
is the payoff of the option. The time derivative in (
1.1
) is defined as

\frac{\partial^{\alpha}C}{\partial\zeta^{\alpha}}=\int_{\zeta}^{T}{\omega_{1-\alpha}}(\eta-\zeta){\partial}_{\eta}C(S,\eta)d\eta\quad 0<{\alpha}<1,

where the kernel
{\omega_{\beta}}(t):={t^{\beta-1}}/{\Gamma(\beta)}
,
t>0
.

We notice that (see also
[
24
]
) by taking the auxiliary variables:
x=\ln S
,
t=T-\zeta
and the function
w(x,t)=C(e^{x},T-t)
, one has

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{\alpha}C}{\partial\zeta^{\alpha}}=\int_{0}^{t}{\omega_{1-\alpha}}(t-s){\partial}_{s}w(x,s)ds=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}w,

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}
represents the Caputo derivative of order
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, the problem (
1.1
) can be transformed to the following equations with constant coefficients:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}w-a\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}-b\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}+cw=0,\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
w(x,0)=r(x),\qquad x\in\Omega,\\
w(x_{l},t)=p(t),~w(x_{r},t)=q(t),\qquad t\in(0,T],\end{array}

(1.3)

where
a=\frac{1}{2}\varrho^{2}
,
b=r-a-D
,
c=r
and
\Omega=(x_{l},x_{r})
.

Moreover, denote
u(x,t):=w(x,t)-z(x,t)
, where

\displaystyle z(x,t):=\frac{q(t)-p(t)}{x_{r}-x_{l}}(x-x_{l})+p(t).

It is easy to see that the problem (
1.3
) is equivalent to the next equations with homogeneous boundary conditions:

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u=a\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+b\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}-cu+f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T],\\
u(x,0)=\varphi(x),\qquad x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,\qquad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times\in(0,T],\end{array}

(1.4)

where

\displaystyle f(x,t)=b\frac{q(t)-p(t)}{x_{r}-x_{l}}-cz-\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}z,

\displaystyle\varphi(x)=r(x)-\frac{q(0)-p(0)}{x_{r}-x_{l}}(x-x_{l})-p(0).

In recent years, several numerical methods for solving fractional Black-Scholes model have been developed. In
[
3
]
, a difference scheme on nonuniform time grids is proposed for an equivalent integral-differential equation of the problem (
1.1
), but it is only first-order convergent in time. For the problem (
1.4
), Zhang et al.
[
29
]
discussed a discrete implicit numerical scheme which has the temporal
(2-\alpha)
-order and spatial second-order convergence. Roul
[
23
]
studied a finite difference method with the convergence of (
2-\alpha
)-order in time and fourth-order in space. De Staelen et al.
[
24
]
investigated an implicit numerical scheme with a temporal accuracy of (
2-\alpha
)-order and spatial accuracy of fourth-order by using the Fourier analysis method. It should be noticed that all of the above numerical methods are based on the analytical solution is smooth enough in the time direction.
However, the solution of time-fractional differential equations generally exhibits weak singularities near the initial time, which such that most of the classical numerical methods based on smooth assumptions are difficult to achieve the high-order convergence in the general situations, one may refer to
[
25
,
7
]
for the discussion on the regularity of the solution of time-fractional diffusion equations and the restrictions of some classical approximations based on sufficient smooth solutions.

To deal with the weak singularities of the solutions, a natural and efficient way is implementing numerical methods with variable step sizes (the mesh will be nonuniform), that is concentrating more mesh points around the (weak) singular points to catch the rapid variation of the solution and use large steps while the solution changes slowly. Numerical methods with variable time step sizes are found to be very efficient and fairly popular in recent years to solve the weak initial singularities of the time-fractional partial differential equations
[
4
,
10
,
14
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
21
,
22
,
25
,
26
]
.
In view of the practical advantage of the nonuniform mesh technique, we will discuss the Alikhanov formula with variable steps to develop an efficient finite difference scheme with second-order temporal accuracy for the time-fractional Black-Scholes equation with weak singular solutions, and the sum-of-exponentials (SOE) technique
[
6
]
will also be utilized at the same time to the discrete Caputo derivative to save the computation costs. Moreover, a high-order average approximation will be employed to approximate the space derivatives to such that the proposed fully discrete scheme is fourth-order accuracy in the spatial direction.
The stability of the proposed scheme will be established according to the analysis framework developed in
[
15
,
16
]
and some matrix analysis techniques. Based on the following regularity assumptions on the exact solution
u
(for
0<t\leq T
):

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{k+l}u}{\partial t^{k}\partial x^{l}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t^{\sigma-l}),\quad\mbox{for}\quad k=0,1,2,3,\quad l=0,1,2;

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\partial^{m}u}{\partial x^{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,\quad\mbox{for}\quad m=3,4,5,6,

(1.6)

where
\sigma\in(0,1)\cup(1,2)
is a regularity parameter, and under weak mesh restrictions,
we can show that the proposed nonuniform scheme is unconditionally convergent with second-order accuracy in time and fourth-order accuracy in space.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce a spatial fourth-order approximation for the governing problem, and show the fast nonuniform Alikhanov formula and derive some necessary properties of the discrete coefficients. In Section
3
, based on the fast nonuniform Alikhanov formula and the spatial forth-order approximation, we construct an efficient nonuniform finite difference scheme for the time-fractional Black-Scholes equation. The unconditional stability and the convergence of second-order in time and fourth-order in space for the proposed scheme are well displayed by energy method. Numerical examples are provided in Section
4
to demonstrate the theoretical statement. A brief conclusion is followed in Section
5
.

2
The high-order and nonuniform approximations

2.1
Spatial high-order approximation

Some notations are needed. For a positive integer
M
, the spatial step size
h=(x_{r}-x_{l})/M
, the discrete grid
{\Omega}_{h}:=\{x_{l}+ih~|~1\leq i\leq M-1\}
and
\bar{\Omega}_{h}:={\Omega}_{h}\cup\partial\Omega
. Denote the space of grid functions
\mathcal{V}_{h}:=\{v_{i}~|~v_{i}~\mbox{vanishes on}~\partial\Omega_{h},~0\leq i\leq M\}
. For two grid functions
v_{i},w_{i}\in\mathcal{V}_{h}
, the inner product is denoted as
\displaystyle\langle v,w\rangle:=h\sum_{i=1}^{M-1}v_{i}w_{i}
, and the discrete
L^{2}
norm is
\|v\|:=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}
. Define spatial central difference operators
\delta_{x}^{2}v_{i}:=(v_{i+1}-2v_{i}+v_{i-1})/h^{2}
and
\delta_{\hat{x}}v_{i}:=(v_{i+1}-v_{i-1})/(2h)
.

In order to obtain a spatial high accuracy numerical scheme, we will utilize a fourth-order approximation which is derived in
[
24
]
to discretize the space derivatives of the time-fractional Black-Scholes equations (
1.4
), we review it briefly in the following.

Applying the Taylor formula at the grid points
x_{i}~(1\leq i\leq M-1)
, and based on the assumption (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x}=\delta_{\hat{x}}u(x_{i},t)-\frac{h^{2}}{6}\frac{\partial^{3}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{2}}=\delta_{x}^{2}u(x_{i},t)-\frac{h^{2}}{12}\frac{\partial^{4}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{4}}+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

(2.2)

Denote
g(x,t):=\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x,t)+cu(x,t)-f(x,t)
.
It follows from the first equation in (
1.4
), and (
2.1
)–(
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\displaystyle a\delta_{x}^{2}{u(x_{i},t)}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}u(x_{i},t)-\frac{h^{2}}{12}\left(a\frac{\partial^{4}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{4}}+2b\frac{\partial^{3}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{3}}\right)+\mathcal{O}(h^{4})=g(x_{i},t),

(2.3)

On the other hand, suppose
g(x,\cdot)\in{\cal C}^{2}(\Omega)
, the Taylor formula shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{3}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\left(\delta_{\hat{x}}g(x_{i},t)-b\delta_{x}^{2}{u(x_{i},t)}\right)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{4}u(x_{i},t)}{\partial x^{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a}\left(\delta_{x}^{2}{g(x_{i},t)}-\frac{b}{a}\left(\delta_{\hat{x}}g(x_{i},t)-b\delta_{x}^{2}{u(x_{i},t)}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}(h^{2}).

(2.5)

Substituting (
2.4
)–(
2.5
) in (
2.3
), one has

\displaystyle\frac{h^{2}}{12}\left(\delta_{x}^{2}{g(x_{i},t)}+\frac{b}{a}\delta_{\hat{x}}g(x_{i},t)\right)+g(x_{i},t)=\left(a+\frac{h^{2}b^{2}}{12a}\right)\delta_{x}^{2}{u(x_{i},t)}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}u(x_{i},t)+\mathcal{O}(h^{4}).

(2.6)

Thus, from (
2.6
), we have a high-order operator
{\cal H}:=\displaystyle\frac{h^{2}}{12}\left(\delta_{x}^{2}+\displaystyle\frac{b}{a}\delta_{\hat{x}}\right)+1
to implement a spatial fourth-order accurate approximation.

2.2
Fast nonuniform Alikhanov formula

Our numerical method will be implemented on possible nonuniform time partitions:
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<{\cdots}<t_{N}=T
, where
N
is a positive integer. Denote a fractional time level
t_{n-{\theta}}:={\theta}t_{n-1}+(1-\theta)t_{n}
for an off-set parameter
{\theta}={\alpha}/{2}
, and take
{\tau}_{k}:=t_{k}-t_{k-1}
(
1\leq k\leq N
) as the
k
th time-step size, and
{\tau}:=\mathop{\mbox{max}}\limits_{1\leq k\leq N-1}{\tau}_{k}
being the maximum step size. Besides, the local step-size ratios are defined as

{\rho}_{k}:=\frac{\tau_{k}}{\tau_{k+1}}~\mbox{ for }~1\leq k\leq N-1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rho}:=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N-1}{\rho}_{k}.

The numerical analysis of our proposed scheme will be based on the following weak assumptions on the temporal mesh:

M1.

The maximum time-step ratio is
{\rho}=7/4
.

M2.

There is a constant
C_{\gamma}>0
such that
\tau_{k}\leq C_{\gamma}\tau\mbox{min}\{1,t_{k}^{1-1/\gamma}\}
for
1\leq k\leq N
, with
t_{k}\leq C_{\gamma}t_{k-1}
and
\tau_{k}/t_{k}\leq C_{\gamma}\tau_{k-1}/t_{k-1}
for
2\leq k\leq N
.

We next introduce the time approximation for the Caputo derivative. For any time sequence
(v^{k})_{k=0}^{N}
, define the backward difference
{\nabla}_{\tau}v^{k}:=v^{k}-v^{k-1}
and the interpolated value
v^{n-\theta}:={\theta}v^{n-1}+(1-\theta)v^{n}
. Denoting
\Pi_{1,n}v
the linear interpolation of a function
v
with respect to the nodes
t_{k-1}
and
t_{k}
, and
\Pi_{2,n}v
the quadratic interpolation of a function
v
with respect to the nodes
t_{k-1}
,
t_{k}
and
t_{k+1}
.
To obtain a second-order scheme, we apply the Alikhanov formula on possible nonuniform meshes
[
16
]
to approximate the Caputo derivative. Meanwhile, the SOE technique is employed to result a nonuniform and fast Alikhanov formula in order to reduce the computational costs.

First of all, we review the SOE approximation (see also
[
6
, Theorem 2.5]
or
[
17
, Lemma 5.1]
) which is designed for the kernel function
\omega_{1-\alpha}(t)
on the interval
[\Delta t,T]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

For the given
\alpha\in(0,1)
, an absolute tolerance error
\epsilon\ll 1
, a cut-off time
\Delta t>0
and a finial time
T
, there exists a
positive integer
N_{q}
, positive quadrature nodes
s^{l}
and corresponding positive weights
\varpi^{l}(1\leq l\leq N_{q})
such that

\displaystyle\left|\omega_{1-\alpha}(t)-\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}e^{-s^{l}t}\right|\leq\epsilon,\quad\forall t\in[\Delta t,T].

Next, the Caputo fractional derivative at the time point
t_{n-\theta}
will be divided into two parts: an integral over
[0,t_{n-1}]
(the historical part) and an integral over
[t_{n-1},t_{n-\theta}]
(the local part). The local part will be approximated directly via a linear interpolation and the historical part will be evaluated by the SOE approximation given in Lemma
2.1
, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(t_{n-\theta})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\theta}}\varpi^{\prime}_{n}(s)(\Pi_{1,n}u)^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t_{n-1}}{\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}u^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{(n)}\nabla_{\tau}u^{n}+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{n-1}),\quad n\geq 1,

(2.7)

where (
1\leq k\leq n
)

\displaystyle a_{n-k}^{(n)}:=\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{\min\{t_{k},t_{n-\theta}\}}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{n-\theta}-s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{0}):=0,\qquad\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{k}):=\int_{0}^{t_{k}}{e^{-s^{l}(t_{k+1-\theta}-s)}u^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}.

The quantity
\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{k})
can be approximated by using the quadratic interpolation and a recursive formula, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{k})

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{t_{k-1}}{e^{-s^{l}(t_{k+1-\theta}-s)}u^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}+\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{e^{-s^{l}(t_{k+1-\theta}-s)}(\Pi_{2,k}u)^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle=e^{-s^{l}(\theta\tau_{k}+(1-\theta)\tau_{k+1})}\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{k-1})+a^{(k,l)}\nabla_{\tau}u^{k}+b^{(k,l)}(\rho_{k}\nabla_{\tau}u^{k+1}-\nabla_{\tau}u^{k}),

(2.9)

in which the positive coefficients
a^{(k,l)}
and
b^{(k,l)}
are respectively determined by

a^{(k,l)}:=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{k+1-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad b^{(k,l)}:=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{k+1-\theta}-s)}\frac{2(s-t_{k-1/2})}{\tau_{k}(\tau_{k}+\tau_{k+1})}\,\mathrm{d}s.

Thus, from (
2.7
)–(
2.9
), the fast Alikhanov formula is presented as

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}u)^{n-\theta}=a_{0}^{(n)}\nabla_{\tau}u^{n}+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{l}(t_{n-1}),\quad n\geq 1.

(2.10)

It can be observed that the average storage of the approximation (
2.10
) is
{\cal O}(N_{\exp})
instead of
{\cal O}(N)
, where the later one is generated from classical Alikhanov approximation, while computing the discrete Caputo derivative at the terminal point
t_{N}
. Thus the total computational cost of the corresponding numerical scheme with the SOE approximation will be far less than that of the standard schemes with classical Alikhanov approximation while
N
is large.

One may notice that the discrete formula (
2.10
) has the following alternative form

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}u)^{n-\theta}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n-\theta}}\varpi^{\prime}_{n}(s)(\Pi_{1,n}u)^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}(\Pi_{2,k}u)^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}}

\displaystyle=a_{0}^{(n)}\nabla_{\tau}u^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}\left(c^{(k,l)}\nabla_{\tau}u^{k}+d^{(k,l)}(\rho_{k}\nabla_{\tau}u^{k+1}-\nabla_{\tau}u^{k})\right),

(2.11)

where the discrete coefficients
c^{(k,l)}
and
d^{(k,l)}
are defined by

\displaystyle c^{(k,l)}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s,

(2.12)

\displaystyle d^{(k,l)}

\displaystyle:=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\frac{2(s-t_{k-1/2})}{\tau_{k}(\tau_{k}+\tau_{k+1})}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.13)

Rearranging the terms in (
2.11
), we obtain the compact form of (
2.11
):

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}u)^{n-\theta}={\sum_{k=1}^{n}}A_{n-k}^{(n)}{\nabla}_{\tau}u^{k},\quad n\geq 1,

where the discrete convolution kernel
{A}_{n-k}^{(n)}
are defined as follows:
{A}_{0}^{(1)}:=a_{0}^{(1)}
if n = 1 and, for
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{A}_{n-k}^{(n)}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle a_{0}^{(n)}+\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}\rho_{n-1}d^{(n-1,l)},&k=n,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}\left(\rho_{k-1}d^{(k-1,l)}+c^{(k,l)}-d^{(k,l)}\right),&2\leq k\leq n-1,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N_{q}}\varpi^{l}(c^{(1,l)}-d^{(1,l)}),&k=1.\end{array}\right.

To analyze the proposed numerical scheme later, we need to show that above discrete convolution kernel
A_{n-k}^{(n)}
fulfill two basic properties
[
15
]
, i.e.,

\mathbf{A1.}
There is a constant
\pi_{A}>0
such that

A_{n-k}^{(n)}\geq\frac{1}{\pi_{A}\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{n}-s)ds\quad~\mbox{ for }~1\leq k\leq n\leq N;

\mathbf{A2.}
The discrete kernels are positive and monotone, that is,

A_{0}^{(n)}\geq A_{1}^{(n)}\geq A_{2}^{(n)}\geq\cdots\geq A_{n-1}^{(n)}>0\quad~\mbox{ for }~1\leq k\leq n\leq N.

We first derive some properties of the discrete coefficients
c^{(k,l)}
and
d^{(k,l)}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
16
, Lemma 2.1]

For any function
q\in C^{2}([t_{k-1},t_{k}])
,

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{(s-t_{k-1/2})q^{\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle=-\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{(\widetilde{\Pi_{1,k}}q)(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{(s-t_{k-1})(t_{k}-s)q^{\prime\prime}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s}.

Applying Lemma
2.2
, the definition (
2.13
) of
d^{(k,l)}
gives

\displaystyle d^{(k,l)}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\frac{2(s-t_{k-1/2})}{\tau_{k}(\tau_{k}+\tau_{k+1})}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=s^{l}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\frac{(s-t_{k-1})(t_{k}-s)}{\tau_{k}(\tau_{k}+\tau_{k+1})}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(2.17)

Since
0<(s-t_{k-1})(t_{k}-s)<\tau_{k}^{2}/4
for
t_{k-1}<s<t_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle d^{(k,l)}

\displaystyle\leq s^{l}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\frac{\tau_{k}^{2}}{4\tau_{k}(\tau_{k}+\tau_{k+1})}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\frac{s^{l}\rho_{k}}{4(1+\rho_{k})}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(2.18)

For simplicity of presentation, we let

\displaystyle I^{(k,l)}:=s^{l}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{\frac{t_{k}-s}{\tau_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s},~J^{(k,l)}:=s^{l}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{\frac{s-t_{k-1}}{\tau_{k}}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s},\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1.

(2.19)

Lemma 2.3
.

For
1\leq k\leq n-1
, the positive coefficients
d^{(k,l)}
in (
2.13
) satisfy

(i)
I^{(k,l)}\geq\displaystyle\frac{1+\rho_{k}}{\rho_{k}}d^{(k,l)};
(ii)
J^{(k,l)}\geq\displaystyle\frac{2(1+\rho_{k})}{\rho_{k}}d^{(k,l)};
(iii)
J^{(k+1,l)}\geq\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}J^{(k,l)}.

Proof.

The alternative definition (
2.17
) of
d^{(k,l)}
gives the result (i) directly since
0<s-t_{k-1}<\tau_{k}
for
s\in(t_{k-1},t_{k})
. Since
e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}>0
for
0<s<t_{n-\theta}
, we apply Lemma
2.2
to find

s^{l}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{\left(\frac{s-t_{k-1}}{\tau_{k}}-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\right)e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s}=\displaystyle\frac{(s^{l})^{2}}{2\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}(s-t_{k-1})(t_{k}-s)e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s>0,

and then
J^{(k,l)}>\frac{s^{l}}{2}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}{e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s}
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. So the inequality (ii) follows immediately from (
2.18
). We now introduce an auxiliary function

G_{k}(z):=\displaystyle\frac{s^{l}}{\tau_{k}}\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k-1}+z\tau_{k}}(s-t_{k-1})e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1,\quad z\in[0,1],

with its first-order derivative
G_{k}^{\prime}(z)=zs^{l}\tau_{k}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-(t_{k-1}+z\tau_{k}))}
for
1\leq k\leq n-1
. By using the Cauchy differential mean-value theorem, there exist
\xi\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{J^{(k+1,l)}}{J^{(k,l)}}=\frac{G_{k+1}(1)}{G_{k}(1)}=\frac{G_{k+1}(1)-G_{k+1}(0)}{G_{k}(1)-G_{k}(0)}=\frac{G_{k+1}^{\prime}(\xi)}{G_{k}^{\prime}(\xi)}=\frac{\tau_{k+1}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-(t_{k}+\xi\tau_{k+1}))}}{\tau_{k}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-(t_{k-1}+\xi\tau_{k}))}}\geq\frac{1}{\rho_{k}},

which yields the inequality (iii).
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The positive coefficients
c^{(k,l)}
in (
2.12
) satisfy

c^{(k+1,l)}-c^{(k,l)}=I^{(k+1,l)}+J^{(k,l)},\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1~(2\leq n\leq N).

Proof.

For fixed
n~(2\leq n\leq N)
, from the definition (
2.12
), we exchange the order of integration to find (for
1\leq k\leq n-1
)

\displaystyle c^{(k+1,l)}-e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-t_{k})}=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\frac{e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}-e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-t_{k})}}{\tau_{k+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\int_{t_{k}}^{s}\frac{s^{l}e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-y)}}{\tau_{k+1}}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}s=I^{(k+1,l)}.

(2.20)

Similarly, for
1\leq k\leq n-1~(2\leq n\leq N)
,

\displaystyle c^{(k,l)}-e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-t_{k})}=\int_{t_{k-1}}^{t_{k}}\frac{e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-s)}-e^{-s^{l}(t_{n-\theta}-t_{k})}}{\tau_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}s=-J^{(k,l)}.

(2.21)

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

If
\mathbf{M1}
holds, for
1\leq k\leq n-1~(2\leq n\leq N)
, the positive coefficients

in (

\displaystyle c^{(k+1,l)}-c^{(k,l)}>\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d^{(2,l)},&k=1,\\
d^{(k+1,l)}+\rho_{k-1}d^{(k-1,l)}&2\leq k\leq n-1,\end{array}\right.

Proof.

By using Lemma
2.3
(ii) and (iii),

\frac{\rho_{k-1}^{3}}{2(1+\rho_{k-1})}J^{(k,l)}\geq\frac{\rho_{k-1}^{2}}{2(1+\rho_{k-1})}J^{(k-1,l)}\geq\rho_{k-1}d^{(k-1,l)},\quad~2\leq k\leq n-1.

The proof is complete.

The fast and nonuniform high-order scheme

3.1
The numerical scheme

Let
u_{i}^{n}
be the discrete approximation of solution
u(x_{i},t_{n})
for

,
0\leq n\leq N
. Considering the first equation in (
1.4
) at the grid points
(x_{i},t_{n-\theta})
, utilizing the fast nonuniform Alikhanov formula (
2.10
) and the spatial high-order approximation (
2.6
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\cal H}g_{i}^{n-\theta}=\left(a+\frac{h^{2}b^{2}}{12a}\right)\delta_{x}^{2}{u_{i}^{n-\theta}}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}u_{i}^{n-\theta}+R_{i}^{n-\theta}\quad\mbox{for}~~x_{i}\in\Omega_{h},~1\leq n\leq N,

(3.1)

where

g_{i}^{n-\theta}:=({\cal D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}u_{i})^{n-\theta}+cu_{i}^{n-\theta}-f(x_{i},t_{n-\theta}),

and

R_{i}^{n-\theta}=\mathcal{H}(R_{t1})_{i}^{n-\theta}+\mathcal{H}(R_{t2})_{i}^{n-\theta}+(R_{t3})_{i}^{n-\theta}+(R_{s})_{i}^{n-\theta}
,
in which
(R_{s})_{i}^{n-\theta}=\mathcal{O}(h^{4})
(according to (
2.6
)), and

\displaystyle(R_{t1})_{i}^{n-\theta}=

\displaystyle(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}u_{i})^{n-\theta}-\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u(x_{i},t_{n-\theta}),

\displaystyle(R_{t2})_{i}^{n-\theta}=

\displaystyle-c(R_{u})_{i}^{n-\theta},

\displaystyle(R_{t3})_{i}^{n-\theta}=

\displaystyle\left(a+\frac{h^{2}b^{2}}{12a}\right)\delta_{x}^{2}(R_{u})_{i}^{n-\theta}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}(R_{u})_{i}^{n-\theta}.

with the error of the weighted time approximation at
t_{n-\theta}
is given as

(R_{u})_{i}^{n-\theta}=u(x_{i},t_{n-\theta})-[\theta u(x_{i},t_{n-1})+(1-\theta)u(x_{i},t_{n})].

Based on the regularity assumption (
1.5
) and the mesh condition
\mathbf{M1}
, and referring to
[

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{n-k}^{(n)}|(R_{t1})_{i}^{k-\theta}|\leq C\left(\tau_{1}^{\sigma}/\sigma+t_{1}^{\sigma-3}\tau_{2}^{3}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\mathop{\mbox{max}}\limits_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k}^{\alpha}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-3}\tau_{k}^{3}/\tau_{k-1}^{\alpha}\right),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{n-k}^{(n)}|(R_{u})_{i}^{k-\theta}|\leq C\left(\tau_{1}^{\sigma+\alpha}/\sigma+t_{n}^{\alpha}\mathop{\mbox{max}}\limits_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-2}\tau_{k}^{2}\right),

(3.3)

where

is called the discrete complementary convolution kernels which satisfying the basic rule:

\sum_{j=k}^{n}P_{n-j}^{(n)}A_{j-k}^{(j)}\equiv 1
for

.
Moreover, the complementary kernels are nonnegative and satisfy (
[
15

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}P_{n-j}^{(n)}\omega_{1+(m-1)\alpha}(t_{j})\leq\frac{11}{4}\omega_{1+m\alpha}(t_{n})\quad\mbox{ for }~m=0,1,~\mbox{and}~1\leq n\leq N.

(3.4)

One may refer to
[
15
,
16
]
for more details about the

which is a crucial tool in the numerical analysis.
With (

) (for

), it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}|(R_{s})_{i}^{j-\theta}|\leq Ch^{4}.

(3.5)

Thus, combining (

)–(

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{n-k}^{(n)}\|R^{k-\theta}\|\leq C\left(\tau_{1}^{\sigma}/\sigma+\max_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k}^{\alpha}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-3}\tau_{k}^{3}/\tau_{k-1}^{\alpha}+t_{n}^{\alpha}\max_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-2}\tau_{k}^{2}+h^{4}\right).

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}u_{i}^{n-\theta}+cu_{i}^{n-\theta}-f_{i}^{n-\theta})=\left(a+\frac{h^{2}b^{2}}{12a}\right)\delta_{x}^{2}{u_{i}^{n-\theta}}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}u_{i}^{n-\theta},\quad x_{i}\in\Omega_{h},~1\leq n\leq N,

Lemma 3.5
.

The matrices
H^{T}A+AH
and
\displaystyle\frac{a}{h^{2}}(H^{T}A+AH)+\displaystyle\frac{b}{2h}(H^{T}S+S^{T}H)
are negative semi-definite.

Proof.

Straightforward computations show that

\displaystyle{H}^{T}A+AH=

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}B+\displaystyle\frac{b}{24a}hS^{T}\right)A+A\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{12}B+\displaystyle\frac{b}{24a}hS\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}-19-\displaystyle\frac{b}{2a}h&8&1&&&\\
8&-18&8&1&&\\
1&8&-18&8&1&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&8&-18&8\\
&&&1&8&-19+\displaystyle\frac{b}{2a}h\\
\end{array}\right].

So
{H}^{T}A+AH
is diagonally dominant with small
h
. By similar arguments to the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we can check that
\lambda_{\max}({H}^{T}A+AH)\leq 0
which leads to first part of the desired result.

For the second part, we notice that

\displaystyle\frac{a}{h^{2}}(H^{T}A+AH)+\frac{b}{2h}(H^{T}S+S^{T}H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6h^{2}}\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccc}c_{4}&8a&a-ac_{1}h^{2}&&&\\
8a&-18a+2ac_{1}h^{2}&8a&a-ac_{1}h^{2}&&\\
a-ac_{1}h^{2}&8a&-18a+2ac_{1}h^{2}&8a&a-ac_{1}h^{2}&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&a-ac_{1}h^{2}&8a&-18a+2ac_{1}h^{2}&8a\\
&&&a-ac_{1}h^{2}&8a&c_{5}\\
\end{array}\right].

where
c_{4}=-19a-bh+ac_{1}h^{2},c_{5}=-19a+bh+ac_{1}h^{2}
. One may easy to find that the above matrix is also diagonally dominant. Similarly, it is negative semi-definite.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

(Stability)
If the assumptions
\mathbf{A1}
-
{\bf A2}
hold, then the numerical scheme (
3.7
) is stable and satisfies

\displaystyle\|u^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{12}{5}}\left(\|u^{0}\|+2\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{12}{5}}\left(\|u^{0}\|+\displaystyle\frac{11}{2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\{t_{j}^{\alpha}(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|)\}\right),\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

Proof.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.19
) with
h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{l}\vskip 5.69054pt\displaystyle h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}H\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}{\bf u}^{n-\theta}+ch({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}H{\bf u}^{n-\theta}-h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}H{\bf f}^{n-\theta}-h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta}\\
=\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{12a}\right)h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}A{\bf u}^{n-\theta}+\frac{b}{2h}h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}S{\bf u}^{n-\theta}.\end{array}

(3.45)

According to Lemmas
3.3
and
3.5
, the right-hand side of (
3.45
) satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a}{h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{12a}\right)h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}A{\bf u}^{n-\theta}+\frac{b}{2h}h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}S{\bf u}^{n-\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{a}{h^{2}}+\frac{b^{2}}{12a}\right)({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}(H^{T}A+AH){\bf u}^{n-\theta}+\frac{b}{4}({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}(H^{T}S+S^{T}H){\bf u}^{n-\theta}\leq 0,

(3.46)

where the identity
{\bf u}^{T}W{\bf u}=\frac{1}{2}{\bf u}^{T}(W+W^{T}){\bf u}
has been applied.

From (
3.45
)–(
3.46
), and utilizing Lemma
3.4
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}H\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}{\bf u}^{n-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}H{\bf f}^{n-\theta}+h({\bf u}^{n-\theta})^{T}H^{T}\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}(H{\bf f}^{n-\theta})+h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}({\bf v}^{n-\theta})}\sqrt{h(H{\bf f}^{n-\theta})^{T}(H{\bf f}^{n-\theta})}+\sqrt{h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}({\bf v}^{n-\theta})}\sqrt{h(\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta})^{T}(\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}({\bf v}^{n-\theta})}\sqrt{h({\bf f}^{n-\theta})^{T}({\bf f}^{n-\theta})}+\sqrt{h({\bf v}^{n-\theta})^{T}({\bf v}^{n-\theta})}\sqrt{h(\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta})^{T}(\widehat{\bf f}^{n-\theta})},

where
{\bf v}^{n-\theta}=H{\bf u}^{n-\theta}
, and it leads to (by taking
v_{i}^{n}={\cal H}u_{i}^{n}
)

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{D}_{\tau}^{\alpha}v)^{n-\theta},v^{n-\theta}\rangle\leq\|v^{n-\theta}\|(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|)\leq\left(\theta\|v^{n-1}\|+(1-\theta)\|v^{n}\|\right)(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|).

(3.47)

Hence, it follows from Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, and (
3.47
) that

\displaystyle\|v^{n}\|\leq

\displaystyle\|v^{0}\|+2\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v^{0}\|+\displaystyle\frac{11}{2}\Gamma(1-\alpha)\max\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\{t_{j}^{\alpha}(\|f^{n-\theta}\|+\|\widehat{f}^{n-\theta}\|)\},\quad\mbox{ for }~1\leq n\leq N.

Theorem 3.7
.

(Convergence)
Let
e_{i}^{n}=u(x_{i},t_{n})-u_{i}^{n}
, if
\mathbf{M1}
,
\mathbf{M2}
and the regularity assumptions (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) hold, then the proposed scheme (
3.7
) is convergent with

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|\leq C(\tau^{\min\{\gamma\sigma,2\}}+h^{4}),\quad 1\leq n\leq N.

Proof.

We have the following error equation

\displaystyle{\cal H}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{\alpha}e_{i}^{n-\theta}=\left(a+\frac{h^{2}b^{2}}{12a}\right)\delta_{x}^{2}{e_{i}^{n-\theta}}+b\delta_{\hat{x}}e_{i}^{n-\theta}-c{\cal H}e_{i}^{n-\theta}+R_{i}^{n-\theta},\quad x_{i}\in\Omega_{h},~1\leq n\leq N.

(3.48)

By using analogous derivations to the proof of Theorem
3.6
, and notice (
3.6
), we can get

\displaystyle\|e^{n}\|\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\displaystyle\frac{12}{5}}\left(\|e^{0}\|+2\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}\|R^{j-\theta}\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left({\tau_{1}^{\sigma}}+\mathop{\max}\limits_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k}^{\alpha}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-3}\frac{\tau_{k}^{3}}{\tau_{k-1}^{\alpha}}+\mathop{\max}\limits_{2\leq k\leq n}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-2}\tau_{k}^{2}+h^{4}\right).

\mathbf{M2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle t_{k}^{\alpha}t_{k-1}^{\sigma-3}\tau_{k}^{3}/\tau_{k-1}^{\alpha}\leq Ct_{k}^{\max\{0,\sigma-(3-\alpha)/\gamma\}}\tau^{\min\{2,\gamma\sigma\}},

see also

5
Conclusion

We proposed a high-order and nonuniform finite difference method for solving the time-fractional Black-Scholes equation. The numerical method is constructed by combining the fast nonuniform Alikhanov formula and a spatial fourth-order average approximation. The unconditional stability and convergence of second-order in time and fourth-order in space are rigorously derived by energy method. Numerical examples are included and the results indicated that the proposed numerical method works very accurately.

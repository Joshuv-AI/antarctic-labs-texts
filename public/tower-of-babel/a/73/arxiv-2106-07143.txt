On Absence of Threshold Resonances for Schrödinger and Dirac Operators Thanks: Appeared in Discrete Cont. Dyn. Syst, Ser. S , 13 , 3427–3460 (2020).
Fritz Gesztesy, Roger Nichols

Abstract
. Using a unified approach employing a homogeneous Lippmann-Schwinger-type equation satisfied by resonance functions and basic facts on Riesz potentials, we discuss the absence of threshold resonances for Dirac and Schrödinger operators with sufficiently short-range interactions in general space dimensions. More specifically, assuming a sufficient power law decay of potentials, we derive the absence of zero-energy resonances for massless Dirac operators in space dimensions n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 , the absence of resonances at ± m \pm m for massive Dirac operators (with mass m > 0 m>0 ) in dimensions n ⩾ 5 n\geqslant 5 , and recall the well-known case of absence of zero-energy resonances for Schrödinger operators in dimension n ⩾ 5 n\geqslant 5 .

and hence
V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}\in[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}
. Therefore,
H(m)\Psi_{\pm m}=\pm m\Psi_{\pm m}
yields

\displaystyle i\alpha\cdot\nabla\Psi_{\pm m}

\displaystyle=(m\beta\mp mI_{N})\Psi_{\pm m}+V\Psi_{\pm m}

\displaystyle=(m\beta\mp mI_{N})\Psi_{\pm m}+V_{1}^{*}V_{2}\Psi_{\pm m}

\displaystyle=(m\beta\mp mI_{N})\Psi_{\pm m}+V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}.

(3.166)

Hence,

\displaystyle(-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta\mp mI_{N})\big[\Psi_{\pm m}+(H_{0}(m)-(\mp m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}\big](x)

\displaystyle\quad=[(-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta\mp mI_{N})\Psi_{\pm m}](x)

\displaystyle\qquad+(-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta\mp mI_{N})[R_{0,\pm m}\ast(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})](x)

\displaystyle\quad=-(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})(x)+[((-i\alpha\cdot\nabla+m\beta\mp mI_{N})R_{0,\pm m})\ast(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})](x)

\displaystyle\quad=-(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})(x)+(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})(x)=0,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(3.167)

In addition,
\Psi_{\pm m}\in[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}
, and the same arguments as those in (
3.126
)–(
3.127
) and (
3.138
)–(
3.158
) (which now extend to include
n\in\{3,4\}
, due to the assumption that
\Psi_{\pm m}\in[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}
) imply
[R_{0,\pm m}\ast(V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m})]\in[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}
, so that

\big[\Psi_{\pm m}+(H_{0}(m)-(\mp m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}\big]\in[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}.

(3.168)

It follows that

\widetilde{\Psi}_{\pm m}:=\big[\Psi_{\pm m}+(H_{0}(m)-(\mp m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}\big]=0;

(3.169)

otherwise,
\pm m
is an eigenvalue of
H_{0}(m)
and
\widetilde{\Psi}_{\pm m}
is a corresponding eigenfunction. However, this contradicts the fact that the spectrum of
H_{0}(m)
is purely absolutely continuous. Hence,

\Psi_{\pm m}=-(H_{0}(m)-(\mp m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}.

(3.170)

Thus,
\Phi_{\pm m}\neq 0
, and

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=V_{2}\Psi_{\pm m}+V_{2}(H_{0}(m)-(\pm m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}\Phi_{\pm m}

\displaystyle=\big[I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}}+\overline{V_{2}(H_{0}(m)-(\pm m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}}\big]\Phi_{\pm m},

(3.171)

that is,

0\neq\Phi_{\pm m}\in\ker\big(\big[I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}}+\overline{V_{2}(H_{0}(m)-(\pm m+i0)I_{[L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})]^{N}})^{-1}V_{1}^{*}}\big]\big).

(3.172)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Some Remarks on
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-Properties

of Threshold Eigenfunctions

In this appendix we collect some negative results on
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
-properties of threshold
eigenfunctions. We hope to return to this issue at a later date.

We start with the case of Schrödinger operators:

Remark A.1
.

We briefly indicate why the line of reasoning used in the proof of
\psi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for
3\leqslant n\leqslant 7

in Theorem
3.3
is bound to fail for
n\geqslant 8
.

Suppose
n\geqslant 8
. Then (
3.53
) continues to hold and by (
3.47
),
\psi\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
for all

q\in(2n/(n+4),2n/(n-4)).

(A.1)

One notes that
q
satisfies (
A.1
) if and only if its conjugate exponent
q^{\prime}:=q/(q-1)
satisfies

q^{\prime}\in(2n/(n+4),2n/(n-4)).

(A.2)

Applying Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents
q^{\prime}
and
q
on the right-hand side in (
3.53
) yields

\displaystyle|\psi(x)|\leqslant\widetilde{d}_{n}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{(2-n)q^{\prime}}\langle y\rangle^{-4q^{\prime}}\bigg)^{1/q^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|\psi(y)|^{q}\bigg)^{1/q},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(A.3)

The second integral on the right-hand side in (
A.3
) is finite for any choice of
q^{\prime}
which satisfies (
A.2
). When applying Lemma
2.5
to estimate the first integral on the right-hand side in (
A.3
), one must choose
k=(n-2)q^{\prime}
. The hypotheses in Lemma
2.5
require
k\in[0,n)
. Hence, one must choose
q^{\prime}
so that
(n-2)q^{\prime}<n
, that is, one must choose
q^{\prime}<n/(n-2)
. However, there is no
q^{\prime}
which simultaneously satisfies (
A.2
) and
q^{\prime}<n/(n-2)
since

\frac{2n}{n+4}-\frac{n}{n-2}=\frac{n(n-8)}{(n-2)(n+4)}>0\,\text{ implies }\,\frac{n}{n-2}<\frac{2n}{n+4}.

(A.4)

Thus,
q^{\prime}<n/(n-2)
yields
q^{\prime}<2n/(n+4)
, so that
q^{\prime}
does not satisfy (
A.2
).
\diamond

Next, we illustrate the case of massless Dirac operators:

Remark A.2
.

We briefly indicate why the line of reasoning used in the proof of
\Psi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{N}
for
n=2,3

in Theorem
3.8
is bound to fail for
n\geqslant 4
.

We start with the case
n=4
. Then the inequality in (
3.81
) implies, when combined with (
3.80
),

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{0}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}\leqslant\widetilde{d}_{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}d^{4}y\,|x-y|^{-3}\langle y\rangle^{-2}\|\Psi_{0}(y)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}.

(A.5)

In order to invoke the property
\Psi_{0}\in[L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{4})]^{4}
,
q\in(4/3,4)
, one applies Hölder’s inequality with conjugate indices
q^{\prime}
and
q
on the right-hand side in (
A.5
) to obtain

\|\Psi_{0}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}\leqslant\widetilde{d}_{4}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}d^{4}y\,|x-y|^{-3q^{\prime}}\langle y\rangle^{-2q^{\prime}}\bigg)^{1/q^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}d^{4}y\,\|\Psi_{0}(y)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4}.

(A.6)

The second integral on the right-hand side in (
A.6
) is finite for any
q\in(4/3,4)
. The requirement
q\in(4/3,4)
implies

q^{\prime}\in(4/3,4).

(A.7)

In order to apply Lemma
2.5
to the first integral on the right-hand side in (
A.6
), one would choose
k=3q^{\prime}
and
\ell=0
. The hypotheses (viz.,
k\in[0,n)
) of Lemma
2.5
require

k=3q^{\prime}<4,

(A.8)

which is satisfied if and only if
q^{\prime}<4/3
. However, the condition
q^{\prime}<4/3
is incompatible with (
A.7
).

An analogous problem is also encountered for all other dimensions
n\geqslant 5
: Indeed, for
n\geqslant 5
, (
3.80
) and (
3.81
) combine to yield

\begin{split}\|\Psi_{0}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}\leqslant\widetilde{d}_{n}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,|x-y|^{(1-n)q^{\prime}}\langle y\rangle^{-2q^{\prime}}\bigg)^{1/q^{\prime}}\bigg(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}y\,\|\Psi_{0}(y)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{N}}^{q}\bigg)^{1/q},&\\
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},&\end{split}

(A.9)

for conjugate exponents
q^{\prime}
and
q
. The second integral on the right-hand side in (
A.9
) is finite for all
q\in(2n/(n+2),2n/(n-2))
. For
q\in(2n/(n+2),2n/(n-2))
, one infers

q^{\prime}\in(2n/(n+2),2n/(n-2)).

(A.10)

To apply Lemma
2.5
to the first integral on the right-hand side in (
A.9
), one must choose
k=(n-1)q^{\prime}
. The condition
k\in[0,n)
in Lemma
2.5
requires
(n-1)q^{\prime}<n
, which is equivalent to

q^{\prime}<\frac{n}{n-1}.

(A.11)

However, the condition in (
A.11
) is incompatible with (
A.10
) in the sense that there is no

which satisfies both (
A.10
) and (

\diamond

Finally, we also illustrate the case of massive Dirac operators:

Remark A.3
.

We briefly indicate why the line of reasoning used in the proof of

3.12

For simplicity we focus again on the case

Applying (
3.123

for some

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{\pm m}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}d^{4}y\,|x-y|^{-3}\langle y\rangle^{-4}\|\Psi_{\pm m}(x)\|_{{\mathbb{C}}^{4}}\bigg\},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{4},

(A.12)

where

Applying Hölder’s inequality (with conjugate exponents

Applying Hölder’s inequality (with conjugate indices

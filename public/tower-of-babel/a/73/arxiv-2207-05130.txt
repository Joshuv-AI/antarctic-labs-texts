Cohomology of moduli spaces via a result of Chenevier and Lannes
Jonas Bergström, Carel Faber

Abstract




Abstract.
We use a classification result of Chenevier and Lannes for algebraic automorphic representations together with a conjectural correspondence with
\ell
-adic absolute Galois representations to determine the Euler characteristics (with values in the Grothendieck group of such representations) of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
and
\mathcal{M}_{3,n}
for
n\leq 14
and of local systems
\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{A}_{3}
for
|\lambda|\leq 16
.

Keywords.
Automorphic representations, Euler characteristic, Galois representations, moduli of curves, moduli of abelian varieties, Siegel modular forms, Lefschetz trace formula, Hecke operators

1. Introduction

The central role in this paper is played by a classification result of Chenevier and Lannes
[
CL19
]
. For
n\geq 1
, they classify the level
1
algebraic cuspidal automorphic representations of
\PGL_{n}
of motivic weight
w\leq 22
. There are
11
of them, given in Theorem
2.1
.

As a part of the web of conjectures in the Langlands program, there is the so-called Langlands correspondence, which says, loosely speaking, that there should be a bijection between algebraic automorphic representations and irreducible geometric Galois representations. The assumption that such a bijection exists, formulated explicitly for weight at most
22
in Conjecture
3.2
, has very concrete geometric consequences, as exemplified by Theorem
3.5
. Namely, the cohomology groups of Deligne–Mumford stacks that are smooth and proper over
\mathbb{Z}
of relative dimension at most
22
would all consist of Galois representations corresponding to the automorphic representations given by Chenevier and Lannes.

We stress the strong implications this has for several moduli spaces of central interest in algebraic geometry. For example, assuming Conjecture
3.2
, we determine the cohomology groups of the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
of stable
n
-pointed curves of genus
3
for all
n\leq 14
, as Galois representations endowed with an
\mathbb S_{n}
-action (up to semisimplification). Similarly, we determine the
\mathbb S_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{M}_{3,n}
for all
n\leq 14
as an element of the Grothendieck group of Galois representations, and we also determine the Euler characteristics of all standard symplectic local systems of weight at most 16 on the moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{3}
of principally polarized abelian threefolds. For this, we use the fact that the integer-valued Euler characteristics are known, and we also use computer counts over small finite fields (with at most 25 elements) of all smooth curves of genus 3 with their zeta functions. The results are available at
https://github.com/jonasbergstroem/M3A3interp
. This adds to a series of recent results about the cohomology of moduli spaces of pointed curves; see for instance
[
BFP22
,
CL22
,
CLP23
]
.

2. A result of Chenevier and Lannes

Let
G
be a semisimple group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
. Recall that the
space
\mathcal{A}^{2}(G)
of square integrable automorphic forms for
G

may be seen as the space of square integrable functions
on
G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/G(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})

for a natural Radon measure.

Denote by
\mathcal{A}_{\disc}(G)
the discrete part of
\mathcal{A}^{2}(G)
, the closure of the sum of the closed and topologically irreducible
G(\mathbb{R})
-subrepresentations of
\mathcal{A}^{2}(G)
. Let
H(G)
be the Hecke ring of
G
(
cf.

[
CL19
, Section V.2.5]
). One denotes by
\Pi_{\disc}(G)
the set of (isomorphism classes of) closed and topologically irreducible subrepresentations of
\mathcal{A}_{\disc}(G)
, for the natural actions of
G(\mathbb{R})
and
H(G)
(
cf.

[
CL19
, Section IV.3]
).
The elements of
\Pi_{\disc}(G)
are called the
discrete automorphic representations
of
G
. The set
\Pi_{\disc}(G)
is in general countably infinite.

In addition, one has the subspace
\mathcal{A}_{\cusp}(G)\subset\mathcal{A}^{2}(G)
of cuspidal automorphic forms; it is known to be contained in
\mathcal{A}_{\disc}(G)
. Correspondingly, one has the subset
\Pi_{\cusp}(G)\subset\Pi_{\disc}(G)
of
cuspidal automorphic representations
of
G
.

Let
\pi=\pi_{\infty}\otimes\pi_{f}\in\Pi_{\cusp}(G)
. Denote by
c_{\infty}(\pi)
the infinitesimal character of
\pi_{\infty}
(
cf.

[
CL19
, Section VI.3.4]
). By the Harish–Chandra isomorphism, it may be seen as a semisimple conjugacy class of the Lie algebra
\hat{\mathfrak g}
of the Langlands dual group
\hat{G}
of
G_{\mathbb{C}}
(
cf.

[
CL19
, Sections VI.1.2 and VI.3.1]
).

Now take
G=\PGL_{n}
. Then
\hat{G}(\mathbb{C})\cong\SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. For
\pi\in\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{n})
, the
weights
of
\pi
are the eigenvalues of the semisimple conjugacy class
c_{\infty}(\pi)\subset M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. One says that
\pi
is
algebraic
if the weights of
\pi
are contained in
\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
and if all the differences
w-w^{\prime}
between the weights of
\pi
are integers (
cf.

[
CL19
, Section VIII.2.6]
). The
motivic weight
of an algebraic
\pi\in\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{n})
is by definition twice the largest weight of
\pi
.

The classification result of Chenevier and Lannes
[
CL19
, Théorème F]
reads as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n\geq 1
, and let
\pi\in\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{n})
be an algebraic cuspidal automorphic representation of motivic weight at most
22
. Then
\pi
is one of the following
11
representations:

1,\hphantom{,}\Delta_{11},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{15},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{17},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{19},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{19,7},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{21},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{21,5},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{21,9},\hphantom{,}\Delta_{21,13},\hphantom{,}\Sym^{2}\Delta_{11}.

Here, the weights of
\Delta_{w}
are
\pm\tfrac{w}{2}
, and those of
\Delta_{w,v}
are
\pm\tfrac{w}{2},\pm\tfrac{v}{2}
. More precisely, for
w\in\{11,15,17,19,21\}
, the representation
\Delta_{w}
is the element of
\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{2})
generated by the
1
-dimensional space
S_{w+1}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
of cusp forms of weight
w+1
for
\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
; the representations
\Delta_{19,7}
,
\Delta_{21,5}
,
\Delta_{21,9}
, and
\Delta_{21,13}
in
\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{4})
correspond, respectively, to the
1
-dimensional spaces of Siegel cusp forms for
\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z})
of types
\Sym^{6}\det^{8}
,
\Sym^{4}\det^{10}
,
\Sym^{8}\det^{8}
, and
\Sym^{12}\det^{6}
; and
\Sym^{2}\Delta_{11}\in\Pi_{\cusp}(\PGL_{3})
is the symmetric square of
\Delta_{11}
, with weights
-11,0,11
;
cf.

[
CL19
, Section IX.1.3]
.

Further strong results along these lines, as well as a simplification of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, have been obtained by Chenevier and Taïbi
[
CT20
]
. See also the earlier work by Chenevier and Renard
[
CR15
]
.

3. Galois representations and cohomology

Fix a prime
\ell
. By a Galois representation, we will mean an
\ell
-adic representation of
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. A Galois representation will be called geometric if it is unramified for
all
primes
p\neq\ell
and crystalline at
p=\ell
. The cyclotomic character will be denoted by
\mathbb L
. It is of dimension
1
, Hodge–Tate weight
1
, and motivic weight
2
. For an irreducible geometric Galois representation
V
of dimension
N
, we have that
\wedge^{N}V=\mathbb L^{i}
for some
i
, and we define the motivic weight
w(V)
to be
2i/N
. For a semisimple
V
, we let
w(V)
denote the maximum of the weights of its irreducible pieces. Let
F_{p}
denote a Frobenius element at
p
.

For a Hecke eigenform
f
in
S_{k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
(or in
S_{j,k}(\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z}))
), let
a_{p}(f)
denote the eigenvalue of the Hecke operator
T_{p}
. By
[
Del71
]
(respectively,
[
Lau05
]
and
[
Wei05
]
),
there is a
2
-dimensional (respectively,
4
-dimensional) semisimple
geometric Galois representation
V_{f}
such that

\Tr\left(F_{p},V_{f}\right)=a_{p}(f),

and with Hodge–Tate weights
0,w(V)=k-1
(respectively,
0,k-2,j+k-1,w(V)=j+2k-3
). It is irreducible, except when
j=0
and
f
is a Saito–Kurokawa lift. Also,
\Sym^{2}V_{f}
, for an eigenform
f
in
S_{k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
, will be geometric and irreducible; see for instance
[
Ram09
, Lemma 4.7]
. Hence, to each of the
11
automorphic representations given in Theorem
2.1
, there is naturally associated a Galois representation
\phi_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq 11
. Note that all these representations are self-dual in the sense that
\phi_{i}\cong\phi_{i}^{\vee}\otimes\det\,\phi_{i}
.

Notation 3.1
.

If
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
is any basis of eigenforms of
S_{k}(\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))
(or of
S_{j,k}(\Sp_{4}(\mathbb{Z}))
), let
S[k]
(respectively,
S[j,k]
) denote the direct sum of the Galois representations
V_{f_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq N
.

This means in particular that

\displaystyle\phi_{2}=S[12],\,\phi_{3}=S[16],\,\phi_{4}=S[18],\,\phi_{5}=S[20],\,\phi_{6}=S[6,8],\,\phi_{7}=S[22],

\displaystyle\phi_{8}=S[4,10],\,\phi_{9}=S[8,8],\,\phi_{10}=S[12,6],\,\phi_{11}=\Sym^{2}S[12],

which aligns with the notation used in
[
BFvdG14
]

(note that
N=1
in all these cases).

As a part of the web of conjectures in the Langlands program, there is the so-called Langlands correspondence, which says, loosely speaking, that there should be a bijection between algebraic automorphic representations and irreducible geometric Galois representations. In our case the Galois representations corresponding to the automorphic representations have been constructed. Moreover, a semisimple Galois representation is determined by the traces of Frobenius for almost all primes. Hence, what we need is something similar to
[
Tay04
, Conjecture 3.5]
, saying that to an irreducible geometric Galois representation there corresponds an algebraic automorphic representation such that the traces of Frobenius equal the Hecke eigenvalues for all primes. On the basis of such a conjecture, we are in turn led to conjecture the following. Note that this conjecture is in no way original.

Conjecture 3.2
.

Every semisimple geometric Galois representation of motivic weight at most
22
, and with non-negative Hodge–Tate weights, is a direct sum of representations
\phi_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq 11
tensored with
\mathbb L^{k}
for some
k\geq 0
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separated Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{Z}
of relative dimension
d
together with an action of the symmetric group
\mathbb S_{n}
. For a field
k
, let
H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k})
denote the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
compactly supported
\ell
-adic étale cohomology group. It is an
\ell
-adic representation of
\Gal(\bar{k}/k)\times\mathbb S_{n}
. We denote the category of such by
\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{k}
and the corresponding Grothendieck group by
K_{0}(\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{k})
. Denote by
\Gal_{k}
and
K_{0}(\Gal_{k})
the versions without the
\mathbb S_{n}
-action. We identify an irreducible representation of
\mathbb S_{n}
with a partition
\mu
of
n
in the usual way, and we denote the corresponding Schur polynomial by
s_{\mu}
. So we have a decomposition

H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k})=\bigoplusop\displaylimits_{\mu\vdash n}\left(W_{i,\mu}\boxtimes s_{\mu}\right)\in\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{k}

for certain
W_{i,\mu}
in
\Gal_{k}
; we put
H^{i}_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k}):=W_{i,\mu}
. Then we define

e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k})=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{2d}(-1)^{i}\left[H^{i}_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k}\right)\right]\in K_{0}(\Gal_{k})

and

e_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k})=\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\vdash n}e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k})\,s_{\mu}\in K_{0}\left(\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{k}\right).

Note that for any closed substack
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
, defined over
k
and invariant under
\mathbb S_{n}
, it holds that

(3.1)

e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k}\right)=e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{Z}\otimes\bar{k}\right)+e_{c,\mu}\left(\left(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Z}\right)\otimes\bar{k}\right).

The analogous statements hold for the integer-valued Euler characteristics
E_{c}
and
E_{c,\mu}
.

When
\mathcal{X}
is smooth, the semisimplification of the Galois representation
H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
will be of motivic weight between
0
and
i
. If
\mathcal{X}
is also proper, then it will be geometric and pure of weight
i
(meaning that all of its irreducible pieces will have weight
i
). It follows that
e_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
as an element of
K_{0}(\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
determines
H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
as an element of
K_{0}(\Gal^{\mathbb S_{n}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
for all
i
. Moreover, by Poincaré duality, we then have that

(3.2)

\mathbb L^{d}\,H^{i}_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)^{\vee}\cong H^{2d-i}_{c}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)

for all
0\leq i\leq d
.

For a finite field
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
q=p^{r}
and
\ell\neq p
, we define the (geometric) Frobenius
F_{q}\in\Gal(\bar{k}/k)
to be the inverse of
x\mapsto x^{q}
. We choose a Frobenius element (by abuse of notation, we will use the same letter)
F_{q}\in\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, using an element of the Galois group of the
p
-adic completion of
\mathbb{Q}
that is mapped to the Frobenius element
F_{q}\in\Gal(\bar{k}/k)
. If
\mathcal{X}
is smooth and proper, then for all
q
such that
p\neq\ell
, these Frobenii satisfy

(3.3)

\Tr\left(F_{q},e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)\right)=\Tr\left(F_{q},e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\bar{k}\right)\right)

(by
[
vdBE05
, Proposition 3.1]
) and these traces will be elements of
\mathbb{Z}
. Note that the traces of
F_{q}
for (almost) all primes
q
will determine
e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
as an element of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
, by a Chebotarov density argument.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}
denote the submonoid of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by

\mathbb L^{k}\,\phi_{j}
for all
k\geq 0
and
1\leq j\leq 11
such that
2k+w(\phi_{j})=i
.

Example 3.4
.

The following
34
Galois representations generate
\cup_{i=0}^{22}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}\,
:

\displaystyle 1,\,\mathbb L,\,\mathbb L^{2},\,\mathbb L^{3},\,\mathbb L^{4},\,\mathbb L^{5},\,\mathbb L^{6},\,\mathbb L^{7},\,\mathbb L^{8},\,\mathbb L^{9},\,\mathbb L^{10},\,\mathbb L^{11},\,S[12],\,\mathbb L\,\,S[12],\,\mathbb L^{2}\,\,S[12],\,\mathbb L^{3}\,\,S[12],

\displaystyle\mathbb L^{4}\,S[12],\,\mathbb L^{5}\,S[12],\,S[16],\,\mathbb L\,S[16],\,\mathbb L^{2}\,S[16],\,\mathbb L^{3}\,S[16],\,S[18],\,\mathbb L\,S[18],\,\mathbb L^{2}\,S[18],

\displaystyle S[20],\,\mathbb L\,S[20],\,S[22],\,S[6,8],\,\mathbb L\,S[6,8],\,S[4,10],\,S[8,8],\,S[12,6],\,\Sym^{2}S[12].

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that Conjecture
3.2
holds. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth and proper Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{Z}
of relative dimension
d
at most
22
with a given action of the symmetric group
\mathbb S_{n}
. Then the semisimplification of
H_{c,\mu}^{i}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is an element of
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}
for any
0\leq i\leq d
and partition
\mu
of
n
.

Proof.

For every
i\geq 0
, the cohomology group
H_{c,\mu}^{i}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
with
\mathcal{X}
a smooth and proper Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{Z}
is geometric and pure of weight
i
; see
[
Tay04
, Section 1, p. 79]
and also
[
vdBE05
]
for the generalization to Deligne–Mumford stacks. The result for
0\leq i\leq d
now follows from Conjecture
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

By (
3.2
) it follows that the
\mathbb L^{k}\phi_{j}
that occur in
H_{c,\mu}^{i}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for
i>d
are also in
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}
with the additional constraint

k+w(\phi_{j})\leq d
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\mathbf{\Psi}_{i}
denote the submonoid of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
\mathbb L^{k}\,\phi_{j}
for all
k\geq 0
and
1\leq j\leq 11
such that
k+w(\phi_{j})\leq i
.

Remark 3.8
.

Fix any
i\geq 0
, and assume that
H_{c}^{2i}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, as an element of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
, is a direct sum of representations
\mathbb L^{i}
. By
[

4. Moduli spaces

4.1. Moduli spaces of curves

For any
g,n
, with
n\geq 3
if
g=0
and
n\geq 1
if
g=1
, let
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
denote the moduli spaces of smooth, respectively stable,
n
-pointed curves of genus
g
. These are smooth Deligne–Mumford stacks over
\mathbb{Z}
of dimension
3g-3+n
, and
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is also proper over
\mathbb{Z}
. They come with a natural action of the symmetric group
\mathbb S_{n}
, permuting the
n
marked points.

The complement of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, denoted by
\partial\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
, can be written as a disjoint union of locally closed subsets

(4.1)

\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}:=\left(\prodop\displaylimits_{v\in\Gamma}\mathcal{M}_{g(v),n(v)}\right)\,/{\Aut\,\Gamma},

one for each non-trivial stable graph
\Gamma
of genus
g
and with
n
markings (see,
e.g.
,
[
ACG11
, Proposition XII.10.11]
).

Note that

(4.2)

\dim\mathcal{M}_{\Gamma}=\sumop\displaylimits_{v\in\Gamma}\left(3g(v)-3+n(v)\right)<3g-3+n.

We will analyze which elements of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
can appear in
e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
using a formula by Getzler and Kapranov, namely
[
GK98
, Theorem 8.13]
. We will follow the notation of
[
GK98
]
, but with a slight generalization. Put
G=\Gal_{k}
for some field
k
. We will consider a stable
\mathbb S
-module
\mathcal{V}=\{\mathcal{V}((g,n)):g,n\geq 0\}
, see
[
GK98
, Section 2.1]
, but where the graded pieces
\mathcal{V}((g,n))_{i}
will be finite-dimensional
\ell
-adic
G\times\mathbb S_{n}
-representations (instead of just
\mathbb S_{n}
-representations). These pieces will then be direct sums of
G\times\mathbb S_{n}
-representations of the form
W\boxtimes V
.
Let
[W]
denote the element of the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(G)

of finite-dimensional
G
-representations
corresponding to a
G
-representation
W
.
For an element
W^{\prime}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j}a_{j}[W_{j}]\in K_{0}(G)
, with
a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
G
-representations
W_{j}
with characters
\chi_{W_{j}}
, define the character
\chi_{W^{\prime}}=\sumop\displaylimits_{j}a_{j}\chi_{W_{j}}
. Note that this gives a bijection between elements of
K_{0}(G)
and their characters.

Let
\Lambda
be the ring of symmetric power series; see
[
GK98
, Section 7.1]
. We define the character
\ch_{n}(W\boxtimes V)
to be
\chi_{W}\cdot\ch_{n}(V)
, compare with
[
GK98
, Section 7]
, which is an element of
\Lambda
with coefficients in
K_{0}(G)
. With this definition,
\mathbb{C}\mathrm{h}(\mathcal{V})
, see
[
GK98
, Section 8.2]
, will be an element of
\Lambda((h))
with coefficients in
K_{0}(G)
. We then define
\mathbb{M}\mathcal{V}
as in
[
GK98
, Section 2.17]
.

Let
p_{i}
denote the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
power sum in
\Lambda
. For a
G
-representation
W
, we then put
p_{n}\circ(\chi_{W}\cdot p_{k})=\chi_{p_{n}\circ W}\cdot p_{kn}
, where
\chi_{p_{n}\circ W}(g)=\chi_{W}(g^{n})
. Theorem 8.13 of
[
GK98
]
then follows, with the same proof, and so

(4.3)

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{h}(\mathbb{M}\mathcal{V})=\Log(\Exp(\Delta)\,\Exp(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{h}(\mathcal{V}))).

Putting
\mathcal{V}((g,n))_{i}=H^{i}_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
, which are finite-dimensional
\ell
-adic
G\times\mathbb S_{n}
-representations, we get a stable
\mathbb S
-module
\mathcal{V}
. For a partition
\mu\vdash n
, we let
s_{\mu}
denote the corresponding Schur polynomial. We now have, see
[
GK98
, Section 6.2]
,

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{h}(\mathcal{V})=\sumop\displaylimits_{2(g-1)+n>0}h^{g-1}\,\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\vdash n}e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)\,s_{\mu}

and

\mathbb{C}\mathrm{h}(\mathbb{M}\mathcal{V})=\sumop\displaylimits_{2(g-1)+n>0}h^{g-1}\,\sumop\displaylimits_{\mu\vdash n}e_{c,\mu}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)\,s_{\mu}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that Conjecture
3.2
holds.
For any
g,n
such that
3g-3+n\leq 22
and partition
\mu\vdash n
,

is in

Proof.

, and assume that

such that

is in
\mathbf{\Psi}_{d-1}
, noting that no non-trivial tensor product of representations

with

can appear, for dimension reasons.

Applying Theorem
3.5
to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, we see that if

appears in

for

and be of the form

4.3. The Torelli map

The moduli spaces we are considering are related via the Torelli map
t_{g}\colon\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
for
g\geq 2
and
t_{1}\colon\mathcal{M}_{1,1}\to\mathcal{A}_{1}
. This morphism between stacks has degree
1
if
g\leq 2
(it is an isomorphism in genus
1
) and degree
2
if
g\geq 3
, ramified along the hyperelliptic locus
\mathcal{H}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
. If
|\lambda|
is even, then

(4.5)

e_{c}\left(t_{g}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right),\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\right)=e_{c}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\right)\in K_{0}\left(\Gal_{k}\right),

where these Euler characteristics (and
E_{c}
) are defined as in Section
3
. If
|\lambda|
is odd, then

e_{c}\left(t_{g}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right),\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\right)=0

due to the presence of the automorphism
-1
on all Jacobians. But for
g\geq 3
, there is no reason for
e_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
to be zero. The first non-zero example is

e_{c}\left(\mathcal{M}_{3}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},t_{3}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{2,1,0}\right)=\mathbb L^{6}-\mathbb L^{2};

see
[
Ber08
]
.

Remark 4.6
.

In fact,
e_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{g}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
will contain Galois representations that are not present in
any
e_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{h}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},{\mathbb{V}_{\lambda^{\prime}}})

with
h=g
and
|\lambda^{\prime}|\leq|\lambda|
, or
h<g
.
These could be called
Teichmüller motives
.
Conjecturally, the first two such instances occur
for
g=3
and
\lambda=(11,3,3)
or
(7,7,3)

and correspond to two of the seven automorphic representations of weight
23
in
[
CT20
, Theorem 3]
, as described in
[
CFvdG20
]
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For each partition
\mu
of
n
, there are elements
a_{\mu,\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb L]
such that

e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\right)=\sumop\displaylimits_{|\lambda|\leq n}a_{\mu,\lambda}\,e_{c}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g},t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\right).

Conversely, for each
\lambda
with
|\lambda|=n
, there are elements
b_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb L]
such that

e_{c}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g},t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}\right)=\sumop\displaylimits_{|\mu|\leq n}b_{\lambda,\mu}\,e_{c,\mu}\left(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\right).

Proof sketch.

This is well known.
The Leray spectral sequence associated to the forgetful morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{M}_{g,n}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
gives a relation between the cohomology of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}
and the cohomology of the higher direct images
R^{i}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
. The latter sheaves can be expressed in terms of local systems
t_{g}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda}
for
\lambda
with
|\lambda|\leq n
. Taking Euler characteristics, this connection becomes a representation-theoretic question of relating
\mathbb S_{n}
-representations to
\GSp_{2g}
-representations. This can be formulated in symmetric polynomials as finding an expression of the (usual) Schur polynomial
s_{\mu}
in terms of symplectic Schur polynomials
s_{<\lambda>}
(see the definition in
[
FH91
, Appendix A.45]
, which should be homogenized using an extra variable
q
). This can always be done, and there are elements
a^{\prime}_{\mu,\lambda}\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
such that

s_{\mu}=\sumop\displaylimits_{|\lambda|\leq n}a^{\prime}_{\mu,\lambda}\,s_{<\lambda>},

and replacing
q
with
\mathbb L
in
a^{\prime}_{\mu,\lambda}
gives
a_{\mu,\lambda}
.
There are also
b^{\prime}_{\lambda,\mu}\in\mathbb{Z}[q]
such that

s_{<\lambda>}=\sumop\displaylimits_{|\mu|\leq n}b^{\prime}_{\lambda,\mu}\,s_{\mu},

and replacing
q
with
\mathbb L
in
b^{\prime}_{\lambda,\mu}
, we get
b_{\lambda,\mu}
.
∎

s_{n(\lambda)}=1

∎

7. The cohomology of
\boldsymbol{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}}
for
\boldsymbol{n\leq 14}

Compared to the situation for local systems on
\mathcal{A}_{3}
, we have less information about the Hodge–Tate weights of the cohomology groups of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth and proper Deligne–Mumford stack over
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
of dimension
d
with unirational coarse moduli space. For any
m\neq 0,2d
, the
\ell
-adic
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
-representation

H_{c}^{m}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
is crystalline and cannot contain the Hodge–Tate weight
m
.

Proof.

We follow the notation of
[
vdB08
]
and let
D_{dR}
denote the de Rham Fontaine-functor. By
[
Fal89
,
Tsu02
]
, generalized in
[
vdB08
, Corollary 8.12]
to Deligne–Mumford stacks, we have an isomorphism of filtered vector spaces

D_{dR}\left(H^{m}\left(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\right)\right)\cong H^{m}_{dR}\left(\mathcal{X}/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}\right).

This identifies the part of
H^{m}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
of Hodge–Tate weight
m
with
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell},{}^{m})
. Since
H^{0}(\mathcal{X}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell},{}^{m})
is a birational invariant, and since it vanishes for any projective space, the result follows.
∎

Definition 7.2
.

Let
\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{i}
denote the submonoid of
K_{0}(\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}})
generated by
\mathbb L^{k}\,\phi_{j}
for all
k>0
and
1\leq j\leq 11
such that
2k+w(\phi_{j})=i
. Put
\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{\leq i}:=\mathbf{\Phi}_{0}\cup_{l=1}^{i}\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{l}
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Assume that Conjecture
3.2
is true. For all partitions
\mu
of
n\leq 14
,
e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{M}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
and
e_{c,\mu}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
can be determined.

Proof.

It is easy to see that the coarse moduli space of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
is rational for
n=14
; hence it is unirational for
n<14
.
Using Theorem
7.1
and Conjecture
3.2
, we conclude that
H_{c}^{m}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
is in
\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{m}
for any
0<m\leq 6+n
.

Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is irreducible, we know that

H^{0}_{c}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right)

is
1
-dimensional, and it will furthermore have a trivial action by
\Gal(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. In
[
AC98
, Theorem 2.2]
there is a formula for the dimension (using duality) of

H^{2}_{c}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}}\right),

and it is shown that this cohomology group is tautological, so it will consist of a direct sum of Galois representations
\mathbb L
.

For any
n\leq 13
,
\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{\leq 6+n}
has rank
r_{n}\leq 17
. Fix any partition
\mu
of
n
. Since we know the coefficients of
1
and
\mathbb L
, we have
r_{n}-2
unknowns
c^{\mu}_{k,j}
. In Section
5
we found
15
linear relations for the
r_{n}
unknowns
c^{\mu}_{k,j}
, which turn out to be linearly independent. These therefore determine
e_{c,\mu}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
. Since we can compute
e_{c,\mu}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for any
n
, see Section
4.5
, we can also determine
e_{c,\mu}({\mathcal{M}}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for
n\leq 13
.

Since
\mathbf{\Phi}^{\prime}_{\leq 20}
has rank
18
, we are missing one relation for
n=14
. But by Theorem
6.2
we know
e_{c}(\mathcal{A}_{3},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
for any
\lambda
such that
|\lambda|=14
. Using the stratification (
4.8
) together with the information about genus
1
and
2
, we can compute
e_{c}(t_{3}(\mathcal{M}_{3})\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
for any
\lambda
such that
|\lambda|=14
. Using Lemma
4.7
and the above, we can compute
e_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{3}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}},t_{3}^{*}\mathbb{V}_{\lambda})
for any
\lambda

|\lambda|\leq 14
. Using Lemma
4.7
again, we can compute
e_{c,\mu}(\mathcal{M}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for all
n\leq 14
and partitions
\mu
. By Section
4.5
we can also compute
e_{c,\mu}(\partial\mathcal{M}_{3,n}\otimes\overline{\mathbb{Q}})
for all
n\leq 14
and partitions
\mu
.
∎

Imaginarity-free quantum multiparameter estimation
Jisho Miyazaki, Keiji Matsumoto

Abstract
Multiparameter quantum estimation is made difficult by the following three obstacles. First, incompatibility among different physical quantities poses a limit on the attainable precision. Second, the ultimate precision is not saturated until you discover the optimal measurement. Third, the optimal measurement may generally depend on the target values of parameters, and thus may be impossible to perform for unknown target states. We present a method to circumvent these three obstacles. A class of quantum statistical models, which utilizes antiunitary symmetries or, equivalently, real density matrices, offers compatible multiparameter estimations. The symmetries accompany the target-independent optimal measurements for pure-state models. Based on this finding, we propose methods to implement antiunitary symmetries for quantum metrology schemes. We further introduce a function which measures antiunitary asymmetry of quantum statistical models as a potential tool to characterize quantumness of phase transitions.

Preliminaries

2.1
Quantum multi-parameter estimation theory

Let us consider an estimation of parameters that determines density operators of finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The density operator
\rho_{\bm{x}}
is parametrized by
{\bm{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}\in X\subset\Re^{n}
.
The set
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is called a
quantum statistical model
or simply a
model
.
The task is to estimate parameters
{\bm{x}}
from results of quantum measurements performed on multiple copies
\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}\times N_{C}}
.
The identical measurement on
\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}
is assumed to be performed independently
N_{C}
times.
Each measurement on
\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}
can be entangled over the
N_{Q}
copies.
An estimation process is described by the pair of a positive operator-valued measures (POVMs)
\Pi=\{\Pi_{\omega}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N_{Q}})\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
and an estimator
\check{\bm{x}}:\Omega^{N_{C}}\rightarrow X
, where
\Omega
is the set of measurement results (
\mathcal{B}
stands of the space of bounded linear operators).

Precision of estimation is characterized by the following mean square error matrix:

\displaystyle{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{x}}(\Pi,\check{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}\in\Omega^{N_{C}}}p({\bm{\omega}}|{\bm{x}})\left(\check{\bm{x}}({\bm{\omega}})-{\bm{x}}\right)\left(\check{\bm{x}}({\bm{\omega}})-{\bm{x}}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},

(1)

where
p({\bm{\omega}}|{\bm{x}})=\prod_{k=1}^{N_{C}}\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi_{\omega_{k}}\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}]
for
{\bm{\omega}}=(\omega_{1},...,\omega_{N_{C}})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
For estimators satisfying

\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}\in\Omega^{N_{C}}}p({\bm{\omega}}|{\bm{x}})\check{\bm{x}}({\bm{\omega}})={\bm{x}},

(2)

the mean square error matrix is a covariance matrix around the target-point (true value)
{\bm{x}}
.
We consider locally unbiased estimators and consistent estimators in this article.
The estimator is called locally unbiased at
{\bm{x}}
when (
2
) and

\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}\in\Omega^{N_{C}}}\check{x}_{i}({\bm{\omega}})\partial_{j}p({\bm{\omega}}|{\bm{x}})=\delta_{ij},

(3)

holds at the point
{\bm{x}}
(
\partial_{j}
stands for the partial derivative with respect to
x_{j}
).
The estimator is called consistent if (
2
) is satisfied at any point
{\bm{x}}
in the asymptotic limit, namely, if

\lim_{N_{C}\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{{\bm{\omega}}\in\Omega^{N_{C}}}p({\bm{\omega}}|{\bm{x}})\check{\bm{x}}({\bm{\omega}})={\bm{x}},\penalty\ (\forall{\bm{x}}\in X)

(4)

holds (to be more precise, consistency is defined on a sequence of estimators
\check{\bm{x}}_{N_{C}}:\Omega^{N_{C}}\rightarrow X
for
N_{C}\in\mathbb{N}
).
The maximum likelihood estimator

\check{\bm{x}}=\mathrm{argmax}\left\{\sum_{l=1}^{N_{C}}\ln p(\omega_{l}|{\bm{x}})\middle|{\bm{x}}\in X\right\},

(5)

is consistent.
While consistent estimators assume sufficiently many rounds of measurements, the unbiasedness condition (
4
) holds globally over the parameter space
X
.

One of the goals of quantum multiparameter estimation is to find the optimal estimator
\check{\bm{x}}
and measurement
\Pi
to minimize the covariance matrix
{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{x}}(\Pi,\check{\bm{x}})
.
Classical and quantum Cramér-Rao bounds deal with the minimization over estimators and measurements, respectively.
Define the
n\times n
classical Fisher information matrix (CFIM) for measurement
\Pi=\{\Pi_{\omega}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\otimes N_{Q}})\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
by

\displaystyle[\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})]_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}\frac{\partial_{i}\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi_{\omega}\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}]\partial_{j}\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi_{\omega}\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}]}{\mathrm{Tr}[\Pi_{\omega}\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\otimes N_{Q}}]},

(6)

where
\partial_{i}
represents the partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
.
CFIM provides the classical Cramér-Rao bound of the covariance matrix

N_{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{x}}(\Pi,\check{\bm{x}})\geq\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})^{-1}.

(7)

At any point
{\bm{x}}\in X
, there exists an
{\bm{x}}
-dependent locally unbiased estimator saturating the inequality for any
N_{C}
(see e.g. Chapter 2 of
[
63
]
for an explicit construction of the estimator).
Note that the locally unbiased estimator at
{\bm{x}}
is generally impossible to construct if the target-point
{\bm{x}}
is unknown.
The maximum likelihood estimator saturates the same inequality globally in the asymptotic limit
N_{C}\rightarrow\infty
.

The remaining optimization is about the measurement.
For general model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
, symmetric logarithmic derivatives (SLDs)
L_{{\bm{x}},i}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
(
i=1,...,n
) are Hermitian operators defined indirectly by

2\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}=L_{{\bm{x}},i}\rho_{\bm{x}}+\rho_{\bm{x}}L_{{\bm{x}},i}.

(8)

The solution of Eq. (
8
) is not unique but one is given by

L_{{\bm{x}},i}=2\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\rho_{\bm{x}}}\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}e^{-t\rho_{\bm{x}}}\mathrm{d}t.

(9)

SLDs define
n\times n
quantum Fisher information matrix (QFIM) via
[
9
,
10
]

[\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})]_{ij}:=\mathrm{Re}\left[\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\bm{x}}L_{{\bm{x}},i}L_{{\bm{x}},j}]\right],

(10)

and provides the quantum Cramér-Rao bound (QCRB) of the CFIM

\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})\leq N_{Q}\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}}),

(11)

which holds for any measurement
\Pi

[
22
,
23
]
.
While SLDs are not necessarily determined uniquely by Eq. (
8
), the QFIM is independent of the choice of SLDs.

For multiparameter estimation
(n\geq 2)
, the QCRB is not necessarily saturated by any measurement, unlike for single-parameter estimations.
This limitation is caused by the incompatibility between estimations of different parameters.
A necessary and sufficient condition for the saturation
\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})=N_{Q}\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})
in the limit
N_{Q}\rightarrow\infty
is the
weak commutativity

[
25
,
28
]
:

[\mathcal{U}({\bm{x}})]_{ij}:=\frac{\mathrm{Im}\left[\mathrm{Tr}[\rho_{\bm{x}}L_{{\bm{x}},i}L_{{\bm{x}},j}]\right]}{2}=0.\penalty\ (\forall i,j)

(12)

The matrix
\mathcal{U}
is called as the mean Uhlmann curvature
[
55
]
, and is independent of the choice of SLDs.
In this article, compatible models refer to weakly commutative models.

If the saturation
\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})=N_{Q}\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})
occurs for
N_{Q}=1
, then the multiple product of the same measurement on the copied model saturates it for any
N_{Q}
, since the CFIM is additive with respect to the tensor product and separable measurements.
For
N_{Q}=1
, a necessary condition for the saturation
\mathcal{F}_{C}(\Pi,{\bm{x}})=\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})
is the partial commutativity:

\Pi_{\bm{x}}[L_{{\bm{x}},i},L_{{\bm{x}},j}]\Pi_{\bm{x}}=0,\penalty\ (\forall i,j)

(13)

where
\Pi_{\bm{x}}
is the projector onto the support of
\rho_{\bm{x}}

[
30
]
.
A sufficient condition for the saturation of QCRB with
N_{Q}=1
is quasi-classicality represented by

[L_{{\bm{x}},i},L_{{\bm{x}},j}]=0,\penalty\ (\forall i,j),

(14)

at the point
{\bm{x}}\in X
in question.
Quasi-classical models do not demand collective measurements for saturating the QCRBs.

All pure weakly commutative models are quasi-classical, and have measurements to saturate QCRB (
11
) with
N_{Q}=1

[
25
]
.
For a pure-state model
\rho_{\bm{x}}=|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|
, a necessary and sufficient condition for a POVM
\mathcal{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
to saturate QCRB (
11
) at
{\bm{x}}\in X
is
[

]
,

\displaystyle{\rm If}\penalty\ \langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|E|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle=0\penalty\ {\rm then}\penalty\ \exists\eta_{i,j}\in\Re,

\displaystyle\penalty\ EL_{{\bm{x}},i}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle=\eta_{i,j}EL_{{\bm{x}},j}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle,\penalty\ \penalty\ (\forall i,\penalty\ j),

(15)

and

\displaystyle{\rm If}\penalty\ \langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|E|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\neq 0\penalty\ {\rm then}\penalty\ \exists\xi_{i}\in\Re,

\displaystyle EL_{{\bm{x}},i}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle=\xi_{i}E|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle,\penalty\ \penalty\ (\forall i),

(16)

for any element
E
of
\mathcal{E}
.
Note that condition (
15
) should be interpreted so that when the vector
EL_{{\bm{x}},j}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
on the right-hand-side of the equation is zero, condition (
15
) still holds with
\eta_{i,j}
a real infinity
[
64
]
.
More precisely, condition (
15
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle{\rm If}\penalty\ \langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|E|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle=0\penalty\ {\rm then}\penalty\ \exists|\psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H},\penalty\ \eta_{i}\in\Re,

\displaystyle\eta_{i}|\psi\rangle=EL_{{\bm{x}},i}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle,\penalty\ \penalty\ (\forall i).

(17)

While this interpretation of condition (
15
) is not obvious in the first look at the statement of
[
30
]
Theorem 2, it can be deduced from its proof.

As in the case for single-parameter estimation, the optimal measurement
\Pi
generally depend on the target point
{\bm{x}}\in X
to be estimated.
Explicit methods to construct the optimal measurements proposed so far typically generates parameter-dependent POVMs
[
16
,
30
]
.
To perform such a measurement, one needs a prerequisite knowledge on the point
{\bm{x}}\in X
, or must start from adaptive measurements to find
{\bm{x}}\in X
in advance.

2.2
Antiunitary operators

We review basic properties of antiunitary operators on finite dimensional Hilbert spaces.
Differences between general antiunitary operators and conjugations are relevant in this article.
More advanced review on the topic can be found in
[
65
]
.

An operator
\Theta
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be antilinear if

\Theta(c_{1}|\psi_{1}\rangle+c_{2}|\psi_{2}\rangle)=c_{1}^{\ast}\Theta|\psi_{1}\rangle+c_{2}^{\ast}\Theta|\psi_{2}\rangle,

(18)

holds for any complex coefficients
c_{1},\penalty\ c_{2}
and vectors
|\psi_{1}\rangle,\penalty\ |\psi_{2}\rangle
.
The Hermitian adjoint
\Theta^{\dagger}
of an antilinear operator is defined differently to linear ones by

\langle\psi_{1}|(\Theta^{\dagger}|\psi_{2}\rangle)=\langle\psi_{2}|(\Theta|\psi_{1}\rangle),

(19)

from which
(\Theta^{\dagger})^{\dagger}=\Theta
,
(\Theta_{1}\Theta_{2})^{\dagger}=\Theta_{2}^{\dagger}\Theta_{1}^{\dagger}
,
(A\Theta)^{\dagger}=\Theta^{\dagger}A^{\dagger}
and
(\Theta A)^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}\Theta^{\dagger}
follow for antilinear operators
\Theta_{1},\penalty\ \Theta_{2}
and linear operator
A
.
Hermitian adjoints of antilinear operators are again antilinear.
Antilinear operator
\Theta
is said to be Hermitian and unitary when
\Theta^{\dagger}=\Theta
and
\Theta^{\dagger}=\Theta^{-1}
hold, respectively.
Unitary antilinear operators are called antiunitaries.
Hermitian antiunitary operators are called conjugations.
Equivalently, conjugation
\theta
is an antiunitary satisfying
\theta^{2}=\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{H}}
.
In this article, antiunitary operators are represented by
\Theta
, and conjugations are by
\theta
.

A conjugation is a complex conjugation in its reference-bases.
To see this, choose a basis
\{|\psi_{i}\rangle\}_{i=1,...,d}
of a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Define a matrix representation
[\Theta]
of antilinear operator
\Theta
in this basis by

\Theta|\psi_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}[\Theta]_{ij}|\psi_{i}\rangle,

(20)

from which

\Theta\sum_{j}c_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle=\sum_{ij}c_{j}^{\ast}[\Theta]_{ij}|\psi_{i}\rangle,

(21)

follows.
The matrix
[\Theta]
is unitary and symmetric when
\Theta
is unitary and Hermitian, respectively.
A conjugation
\theta
has a symmetric unitary matrix representation
[\theta]
, and thus decomposed as

[\theta]=VV^{\mathrm{T}},

(22)

with a unitary matrix
V
.
This is a direct consequence from Autonne
-
Takagi factorization.
Inserting Eq. (
22
) to Eq. (
21
), we have

\displaystyle\theta\sum_{j}c_{j}|\psi_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{ijk}c_{j}^{\ast}V_{ik}V^{\mathrm{T}}_{kj}|\psi_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j}c_{j}V^{\dagger}_{kj}\right)^{\ast}\left(\sum_{i}V_{ik}|\psi_{i}\rangle\right).

(23)

The action of
\theta
is thus a complex conjugation in the
V
-rotated basis
|\psi_{k}^{\prime}\rangle:=\sum_{i}V_{ik}|\psi_{i}\rangle
.

The reference-basis of a conjugation is not unique.
If a basis
\{|\psi_{j}\rangle\}_{j=1,...,\dim\mathcal{H}}
is a reference-basis of
\theta
, then another basis
\{|\psi^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\}_{j=1,...,\dim\mathcal{H}}
defined by

|\psi^{\prime}_{j}\rangle:=R|\psi_{j}\rangle,\penalty\ (j=1,...,\dim\mathcal{H}),

(24)

with a real orthogonal matrix
R
is also a reference-basis of
\theta
.

A general antiunitary operator
\Theta
has a decomposition

\Theta=U\theta,

(25)

into a (linear) unitary operator
U
and a conjugation
\theta
.

\Theta
is a conjugation if
U
is symmetric with respect to the reference basis that makes
\theta
a complex conjugation.
Otherwise
\Theta
is an antiunitary that is not a conjugation.

Spin-flip is an example of antiunitary operator that is not a conjugation.
Consider a 2-dimensional space
\mathcal{H}
with a basis
\{|\psi^{+}\rangle,\penalty\ |\psi^{-}\rangle\}
.
Spin-flip
\Theta_{f}
is defined by

\Theta_{f}=\sigma_{Y}\theta,

(26)

where
\sigma_{Y}
is the Pauli-
Y
operator and
\theta
is the complex conjugation in the same basis.
Obviously
\sigma_{Y}
is not symmetric, and
\Theta_{f}^{2}=-\mathbb{I}_{2}\neq\mathbb{I}_{2}
.

In most part of this article antiunitary operators act in adjunction

\Theta A\Theta^{\dagger},

(27)

on (linear) Hermitian operators
A
such as quantum states.
The above operator is equal to

U\theta A\theta U^{\dagger}=UA^{\ast}U^{\dagger},

(28)

from Eq. (
25
), where
A^{\ast}
is the complex conjugation of
A
in the reference basis of
\theta
.
The adjoint action of conjugation on a Hermitian operator is equivalent to transposition since
A^{\ast}=(A^{\dagger})^{\ast}=A^{\mathrm{T}}
.
When
A
is a quantum state, namely, a density operator, the transformation
A\mapsto\Theta A\Theta^{\dagger}
cannot be a completely positive map.

Direct sums and tensor products of antiunitary operators are well-defined, and both result in antiunitary operators again.
The tensor product of antiunitary operators
\Theta_{A}
on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
and
\Theta_{B}
on
\mathcal{H}_{B}
is defined By

\displaystyle\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\sum_{j}

\displaystyle c_{j}|\psi^{A}_{j}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{B}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j}c_{j}^{\ast}|\Theta_{A}\psi^{A}_{j}\rangle\otimes|\Theta_{B}\psi^{B}_{j}\rangle,

(29)

for vector
\sum_{j}c_{j}|\psi^{A}_{j}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{B}_{j}\rangle
in the composite system
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
.
For arbitrary two vectors in
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}
decomposed by

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}c_{j}|\psi^{A}_{j}\rangle\otimes|\psi^{B}_{j}\rangle,

(30)

\displaystyle|\xi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}d_{k}|\xi^{A}_{k}\rangle\otimes|\xi^{B}_{k}\rangle,

(31)

the inner product
\langle\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\xi|\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\psi\rangle
is calculated as

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\xi|\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}d_{k}c^{\ast}_{j}\langle\Theta_{A}\xi^{A}_{k}|\Theta_{A}\psi^{A}_{j}\rangle\langle\Theta_{B}\xi^{B}_{k}|\Theta_{B}\psi^{B}_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k}d_{k}c^{\ast}_{j}\langle\psi^{A}_{k}|\xi^{A}_{j}\rangle\langle\psi^{B}_{k}|\xi^{B}_{j}\rangle=\langle\psi|\xi\rangle,

(32)

by using Eq. (
29
).
Equation
\langle\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\xi|\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}\psi\rangle=\langle\psi|\xi\rangle
reveals that
\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}
is well-defined and is antiunitary.
We also have

(U_{A}\Theta_{A})\otimes(U_{B}\Theta_{B})=(U_{A}\otimes U_{B})(\Theta_{A}\otimes\Theta_{B}),

(33)

where
U_{A}
and
U_{B}
are linear operators, and the tensor product in the middle is of linear operators.
Tensor products of pairs of an antiunitary operator and a linear operator is not well-defined.
There is no “partial conjugation,” in contrast to the well-defined partial transposition.

3
Global antiunitary symmetry

This section presents a direct link between antiunitary symmetry and weak commutativity.
Theorem
2
states that quantum statistical models with “global” antiunitary symmetries are weakly commutative at all points in the parameter spaces.
Theorem
3
ensures the target-independent optimal measurements for pure-state models with global antiunitary symmetries.
These theorems underlie our methods to find globally optimal measurements.

3.1
Global antiunitary symmetry and conjugation

Let us define models with global antiunitary symmetry, and clarify the distinction between general antiunitaries and conjugations with respect to the definition.

Definition 1

A quantum statistical model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is said to have a
global antiunitary symmetry
(GAS) when there is an antiunitary operator
\Theta
such that

\Theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=\rho_{\bm{x}},

(34)

holds for any
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
Models with GASs are said to be
imaginarity-free
.

The antiunitary operator
\Theta
in the definition does not depend on the parameter
{\bm{x}}
.
This is why we call the antiunitary symmetry in the sense of Def.
1
as being global.

As a trivial example, any classical model, whose states are diagonal in a fixed basis for all
{\bm{x}}\in X
, i.e., when all the eigenvectors are independent of
{\bm{x}}
, is imaginarity-free.
The antiunitary operator is the complex conjugation in the diagonal basis.

There also are various non-classical imaginarity-free models.
This article presents GASs of N00N states
[
51
,
52
,
2
]
, super-dense coding

3.1.1
Antiunitary symmetry defined on horizontal lift

A lift of a quantum statistical model is its orbit in the associated fiber bundle.
For pure-state models, the defined GAS is equivalent to the antiunitary symmetry on the so-called horizontal lift of the model.
We briefly explain this equivalence without any prerequisite knowledge on geometry here.
The content of this section is independent on other parts of this article.
See
[
73
,
74
]
for complete expositions.

For a pure-state model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
, a lift is identified with a parameterized unit vector
|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
in
\mathcal{H}
such that

|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|=\rho_{\bm{x}},

(49)

for any
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
The lift is said to be horizontal, if it satisfies

\mathrm{Im}\left[\langle\psi_{\bm{x}}|\partial_{i}\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\right]=0,

(50)

for any
i
and
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
The horizontal lift exists if and only if the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is quasi-classical.

The global antiunitary symmetry (GAS) of a horizontal lift is defined as the antiunitary operator
\Theta
such that

\Theta|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle=|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle,

(51)

holds for any
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
Now it is readily seen that
\Theta
becomes a GAS of the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
.
Conversely, if a model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
has a GAS
\Theta
, then there is a lift satisfying (
51
).
This lift is horizontal since
|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
and
|\partial_{i}\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
are in the same real subspace of
\mathcal{H}
invariant by the antiunitary
\Theta
.

The model has a GAS if its horizontal lift does, and they share the same antiunitary operator.

Quantum mechanics in real Hilbert spaces lacks an uncertainty principle between observables of a specific form

Nevertheless there is an uncertainty principles of a different form

We are not sure how Thm.

The link between uncertainty principles and incompatibility of parameters should be clarified in more detail.

Antiunitary symmetries are sometimes easier to check than the zero of mean Uhlmann curvature.

Here we assume that the temperature is contained in the Hamiltonian as a parameter.

One might wonder if QFIMs of imaginarity-free models have a particular form.

3.3
Optimal target-independent measurements

Any imaginarity-free pure-state model is accompanied by a set of globally optimal POVMs.
This means that the QCRB is saturated by the corresponding measurement at any target-point.

In this section, we set the number
N_{Q}
of copies in joint measurements to

A set of optimal POVMs is known for antiparallel spins

These POVM operators are first proposed in

[

The POVMs must be independent from the target point

in the parameter space, in order to be optimal for the fidelity measure.
Later the same set of operators is shown to saturate QCRB
[
54

The target-independent POVMs are fascinating because they are optimal all over the parameter space.
In general, around different regions in the parameter space, we require different POVMs to achieve the best precision limit in the region.
This is the case even for single-parameter estimations.
If the optimal POVMs are independent of the target point, the precise measurement can be carried out without any prerequisite knowledge on the source.

The optimality of antiunitary-invariant POVM is independent not only on the target point, but also on how the parameters are encoded in the state.
The optimal POVMs depend only on the GAS, which in turn depends only on the states
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
as a set.
Thus re-parametrization change neither the GAS nor the optimal POVMs.
One does not have to use different POVMs according to different parametrization.

The condition on POVMs indicated by Thm.
3
is so loose that continuously many such POVMs exist.
To see this, let us consider a pure-state model whose GAS is a conjugation
\theta
.
The conjugation defines a
\dim\mathcal{H}
-dimensional real Hilbert subspace of
\mathcal{H}
spanned by vectors satisfying
\theta|\psi\rangle=|\psi\rangle
.
You can take any vectors from this
\theta
-invariant real subspace to construct the optimal POVM by
E=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
.
For example, the reference basis for representing a conjugation
\theta
as a complex conjugation is not unique.
In Appx.
A.1.3
, we find two different sets of optimal POVMs for antiparallel spins from its GAS.
One of them is given in
[
53
]
and the other one is new.
As another example, rank-1 projectors distributed randomly over the real subspace also saturates the QCRB
[
74
]
.

While any antiunitary-invariant measurements work equally well, there should be experimentally preferable ones, such as those requiring less entanglement.
Consider, for example, an estimation of parameters
{\bm{x}}
encoded as a unitary evolution
e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}
.
A typical method of quantum metrology utilizes multiple copies of the same system driven by
e^{-i\sum_{n=1}^{N}H^{(n)}_{\bm{x}}}=\otimes_{n=1}^{N}e^{-iH^{(n)}_{\bm{x}}}
.
If the
1
-particle Hamiltonian
H_{\bm{x}}
has an antiunitary skew-symmetry
\Theta H_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=-H_{\bm{x}}
, the overall Hamiltonian has an antiunitary skew-symmetry under
\Theta^{\otimes N}
.
We show in Sec.
4.1
how to construct imaginarity-free models based on Hamiltonians’ antiunitary skew-symmetries.
If you construct an imaginarity-free model based on the GAS
\Theta^{\otimes N}
, the POVM must be invariant under
\Theta^{\otimes N}
.
You would prefer the measurement in the product POVM, each being invariant under
\Theta
.

We apply the above reasoning to find the optimal measurement basis for 3D magnetometry
[
15
]
in Sec.
3.4.3
.
There the GAS provides a parameter-independent optimal measurement basis.
The entanglement required for the measurement is reduced from
N
-partite to
2
-partite.
The search of reasonable measurements is possible thanks to the freedom of the optimal measurement for imaginarity-free models.

We close this section by extending the globally optimal POVMs to globally optimal estimation strategies.
Even if a target-independent measurement saturates the QCRB everywhere, the optimal locally unbiased estimator could still depend on the target-point.

Maximum likelihood estimators are independent on the target-point to be estimated.
The only condition for the maximum likelihood estimator to work globally is the identifiability of the parameter.
More precisely, the probability distribution
p(\Omega|{\bm{x}})
generated by the measurement must be in one-to-one correspondence with the point
{\bm{x}}
.
Projectors onto a single reference-basis for
\theta
will not produce sufficiently informatic probability distributions to identify the parameters in general.

Fortunately, the condition on the optimal POVMs described in Thm.
3
is loose enough to admit the POVMs to identify the parameters.
Under the restrictions (
54
) and (
55
), it is possible to construct a POVM
\{E_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
such that the map
\rho\mapsto(\mathrm{Tr}[E_{\omega}\rho])_{\omega\in\Omega}
from
\theta
-invariant density operators
\{\rho\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\penalty\ |\penalty\ \rho\geq 0,\penalty\ \mathrm{Tr}[\rho]=1,\penalty\ \theta\rho\theta=\rho\}
to
\Re^{\Omega}
is one-to-one.
An example of such
real
-informationally-complete POVMs associated to
\theta
is the rank-1 projectors distributed randomly over the real subspace defined by
\theta

[
74
]
.
Because the space of real density operators associated to
\theta
contains
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
if
\theta
is its GAS, the probability distribution generated by
\{E_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
identifies the parameter.
In this way we obtain the following corollary:

Corollary 1

Let
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
be an imaginarity-free pure-state model.
Suppose that the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is identifiable in that the map
{\bm{x}}\mapsto\rho_{\bm{x}}
is invertible.
Then there is a POVM
\Pi=\{\Pi_{k}\}_{k\in\Omega}
on
\mathcal{H}
with which the maximum likelihood estimator
\check{\bm{x}}:\Omega^{N_{C}}\rightarrow X
satisfies

\lim_{N_{C}\rightarrow\infty}N_{C}{\bm{\Sigma}}_{\bm{x}}(\Pi,\check{\bm{x}})=\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})^{-1},

(61)

at any point
{\bm{x}}\in X
.

We can globally saturate QCRB by a fixed measurement and a maximum likelihood estimator without any prior knowledge on the target-point, if the pure-state model has a GAS.
In practice, there is no need to stick to the real-informationally-complete POVMs.
One can employ POVMs that identify the state from the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
only.

4
Methods to implement global antiunitary symmetries

In this section we consider methods to construct imaginarity-free models.
The benefit of implementing the GASs is twofold.
First, the weak commutativity of the model is ensured at any point (Thm.
2
).
Second, the models have parameter-independent optimal POVMs (Thm.
3
and Cor.
1
), when the models are pure.

We focus on quantum metrology, and thus assume some ability to modify the physical systems to adjust the models.
If the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
cannot be adjusted, the attainable precision does not improve even if we implement antiunitary symmetry on
\rho_{\bm{x}}
by pre-measurement operations.
The assumed ability is to replace the initial states before interactions.

Even if you can adjust the models, you may still wonder if the restriction to GASs is worth taking.
GASs inevitably restrict available models.
There may be a model without GAS but still offers better precisions for all the parameters.
In short, you may wonder if compatible models offer maximum precisions.
Unfortunately, previous methods to design compatible models for quantum metrology did not consider optimizations on the attainable precision
[
25
,
28
,
33
]
.

This question has almost been ignored since it was difficult to calculate the optimal precision bounds for incompatible models.
To propose a metrology scheme, you have to evaluate the precisions attained by the scheme.
If the model is compatible, it suffices to calculate the QCRB.
Otherwise you have to calculate the so-called Holevo Cramér-Rao bound
[
34
,
11
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
]
, whose closed-form expression is missing.
This has been one of the reasons for researchers to seek for compatible models.
However, methods to calculate the Holevo Cramér-Rao bound has been developed
[
27
,
25
,
40
,
41
,
42
,
44
]
, and a semi-definite programming is available now
[
14
]
.
The complexity is further reduced when you require only a lower bound of the Holevo Cramér-Rao bound
[
45
]
.
The final blow on the QCRB is its modest advantage: the Holevo Cramér-Rao bound secures at least half of the QCRB even in the worst case
[
43
]
.
This encourages the pursuit for better precisions in the realm of incompatible models.

Our answer to the question on the simultaneous realizability of best precisions and GASs is twofold.

In Sec.
4.1
, we consider a particular generator of unitary transformation.
The optimal initial state for the phase estimation inevitably leads to imaginarity-free models.
The GAS coexists with the optimal initial state, and provides optimal global measurements at the same time.

In Sec.
4.2
, we propose a different method to implement antiunitary symmetry by generalizing antiparallel spins.
The method implements GASs without deteriorating the precision limit of a given model, under some symmetry conditions.

4.1
Antiunitary skew-symmetric Hamiltonians

The first method is inspired by 3D magnetometry.
Consider a model whose state given by

e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}\rho_{\rm ini}e^{iH_{\bm{x}}},

(90)

where
H_{\bm{x}}
is a parametrized Hamiltonian.
The Hamiltonian is assumed to have a skew-symmetry

\Theta H_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=-H_{\bm{x}},

(91)

under an antiunitary
\Theta
.
If there is a state
|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle
satisfying

\Theta|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle=\epsilon|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle,

(92)

with some unimodular
\epsilon
,
\Theta
becomes a GAS of the model (
90
) by setting the initial state to
\rho_{\rm ini}=|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle\langle\psi_{\rm ini}|
.
The existence of antiunitary skew-symmetry (
91
) is the key for implementing the GAS.

Now we question the optimality of the antiunitary-invariant initial state (
92
) for estimating
{\bm{x}}
.
The N00N state reviewed in Sec.
3.4.1
is antiunitary-invariant and, at the same time, the best initial state for the phase estimation on interferometry.
We show in the following that the simultaneous realization of the optimal initial state and the GAS is not a mere coincidence.

Optimal initial states are known for particular single-parameter estimations.
Consider a quantum system driven by a traceless generator
H
.
Now we are given a task to estimate the single parameter
t
from the state

e^{-iHt}\rho_{\rm ini}e^{iHt},

(93)

where the initial state
\rho_{ini}
is of our choice.
The best initial state that maximizes the quantum Fisher information for
t
of the state (
93
) is given by
[
82
]

\rho_{\rm ini}=|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle\langle\psi_{\rm ini}|,\penalty\ \penalty\ |\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle=\frac{|\lambda_{+}\rangle+e^{i\phi}|\lambda_{-}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

(94)

where
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
is arbitrary, and
|\lambda_{+}\rangle
and
|\lambda_{-}\rangle
are eigenstates of
H
with maximum and minimum eigenvalues
\lambda_{+}
and
\lambda_{-}
, respectively.
The optimal state maximizes the quantum Fisher information by maximizing the uncertainty in expecting
H

[
83
,
84
]
.

We can readily see that the resulting model

e^{-iHt}|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle=\frac{e^{-i\lambda_{+}t}|\lambda_{+}\rangle+e^{i(\phi-\lambda_{-})t}|\lambda_{-}\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

(95)

is imaginarity-free.
The state lies on a great circle of the Bloch sphere of the qubit system spanned by
|\lambda_{+}\rangle
and
|\lambda_{-}\rangle
.
Therefore the state is represented with real coefficients in a suitable basis.

When the Hamiltonian has a specific form, a GAS of the model (
95
) is derived from the antiunitary skew-symmetry of the Hamiltonian.
Suppose that the spectrum of the generator
H
distributes symmetrically about the origin.
We further assume that the eigensubspaces
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-\lambda}
belonging to eigenvalues
\lambda
and
-\lambda
, respectively, have the same dimension.
Then an operator
\Theta
on
\mathcal{H}
defined by

\displaystyle\Theta\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{d=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}}c_{\lambda,d}|\lambda,d\rangle=\sum_{\lambda}\sum_{d=1}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}}c_{\lambda,d}^{\ast}|-\lambda,d\rangle,

(96)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\left(|\lambda_{+},1\rangle:=|\lambda_{+}\rangle,\penalty\ |\lambda_{-},1\rangle:=|\lambda_{-}\rangle\right)

is an antiunitary.
This antiunitary is a symmetry of the initial state.
We have
\Theta|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle=e^{-i\phi}|\psi_{\rm ini}\rangle
and hence

\Theta\rho_{\rm ini}\Theta^{\dagger}=\rho_{\rm ini},

(97)

by definition.
Moreover, the skew-symmetry

\Theta H\Theta^{\dagger}=-H,

(98)

of the generator under the action of
\Theta
follows from the definition.

Combining Eqs. (
97
) and (
98
), we arrive at the equality

\Theta e^{-iHt}\rho_{\rm ini}e^{iHt}\Theta^{\dagger}=e^{-iHt}\rho_{\rm ini}e^{iHt},

(99)

meaning that
\Theta
is a GAS of the model (
93
) for any optimal initial state.
Its optimal global measurements are lead by Thm.
3
and Cor.
1
.

The optimization of initial state presented above underlies single-parameter metrology schemes including the N00N state reviewed in Sec.
3.4.1
.

In summary, if the spectrum and the eigensubspaces of generator
H
distribute symmetrically about the origin, the model (
93
) with any optimal initial state (
94
) is imaginarity-free.
The resulting model with optimal initial state provides the target-independent optimal measurement to attain the best QCRB.
For a special generator of this kind, the optimization of initial state naturally leads to imaginarity-free models.

This observation dispels a doubt that GASs spoil advantages of quantum statistical models.
Imaginarity-free models look close to classical ones in that the freedom of imaginary numbers disappears, that all the parameters are compatible, and that global optimal measurements exist.
The classical behavior can, however, coexist with the most effective metrological schemes offered by quantum models.
In this sense, the imaginarity-free estimations benefits from both of classical and quantum estimations.

4.2
Antiparallel model

Our second method requires the state
\rho_{\bm{x}}
of the original model and its conjugation
\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\ast}
.
This is a non-trivial requirement on the physical system since there is no completely-positive map to transform given state

The antiparallel models additionally require the conjugated states

\theta e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}\theta=e^{i\theta H_{\bm{x}}\theta}

In the following subsections we introduce two major instances where the conjugate state evolution is available.

4.2.1
Hamiltonians’ skew-symmetry

Let us assume that a Hamiltonian
H_{\bm{x}}
flips its sign

\Theta H_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=-H_{\bm{x}},

(105)

under an antiunitary
\Theta=U\theta
.
In this case, the conjugated state evolution
e^{i\theta H_{\bm{x}}\theta}
can be decomposed to

e^{i\theta H_{\bm{x}}\theta}=U^{\dagger}e^{i\Theta H_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}}U=U^{\dagger}e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}U.

(106)

Namely, the conjugate state evolution is unitarily equivalent to the original evolution
e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}
.
Since the original evolution is available, the conjugated state

U^{\dagger}e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}U|\theta\psi\rangle,

(107)

can be produced by implementing unitary transformations
U
and
U^{\dagger}
before and after the evolution.

Note that if there is a state
|\psi\rangle
such that
\Theta|\psi\rangle=\epsilon|\psi\rangle
with some unimodular
\epsilon

e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}|\psi\rangle
itself obtains the GAS
\Theta
because
\Theta e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}|\psi\rangle=\epsilon e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}|\psi\rangle
holds (see Sec.
4.1
for details).
This is how 3D magnetometry and models in Sec.
4.1
acquire their GASs.

4.2.2
Inverse transformation

Suppose that the inverse transformation
e^{iH_{\bm{x}}}
is available in addition to
e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}
.
Let
\theta
be any conjugation that leaves
H_{\bm{x}}
invariant.
For example, one may take the eigenbasis of
H_{\bm{x}}
as the reference basis for conjugation.
Then for any initial state
|\psi\rangle
,

e^{iH_{\bm{x}}}|\theta\psi\rangle,

(108)

is the conjugate state of
e^{-iH_{\bm{x}}}|\psi\rangle
.

5
Discussions

5.1
Antiunitary invariant POVMs on mixed states

The antiunitary-invariant POVMs do not necessarily saturate QCRBs for mixed models.
This is partly because multipartite measurements (
N_{Q}\geq 2
) are necessary for saturating QCRBs in general.
Any single-copy measurement cannot saturate the QCRB for such a model.
Theorem
3
does not generalizes to models with mixed states.

Now you may wonder what if the imaginarity-free mixed-state model is quasi-classical.
Any quasi-classical model is accompanied by a (potentially target-dependent) single-copy measurement to saturate the QCRB.
Does our antiunitary-invariant POVM saturate QCRBs for quasi-classical mixed models?

The answer is “no.”
Even if a quasi-classical mixed state model has a GAS, the antiunitary-invariant POVM does not necessarily form the optimal POVM.
A counterexample is given in Appx.
A.1.4
by the off-equator estimation on depolarized antiparallel spins.
Our antiunitary-invariant POVM is not optimal to this quasi-classical mixed model.

The antiunitary-invariant POVMs do not seem to have any special property in the mixed state regime.
In Appx.
A.1.4
, we demonstrate that different antiunitary-invariant POVMs give different CFIMs for the off-equator phase estimation on depolarized antiparallel spins.
This implies that the antiunitary-invariant POVMs do not share anything in terms of the measurement precisions at the mixed state regime.
Their coincidence is only at the pure state limit.

5.2
Local antiunitary symmetry

We have seen that the converse of Thm.
2
is not true. There are weakly commutative models without any GAS.
To refine Thm.
2
, we compare weak commutativity and antiunitary symmetry at each point in the parameter spaces.

We are motivated from the following geometric perspective.
Consider application of Thm.
2
to subspaces of the whole parameter space.
If
X_{1}
is a subspace of the parameter space
X
and if there is a GAS
\Theta_{1}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta_{1}^{\dagger}=\rho_{\bm{x}}
inside
X_{1}
, then the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is weakly commutative at all points in
X_{1}
.
If there are such “small” GASs
\Theta_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta_{i}^{\dagger}=\rho_{\bm{x}}
for covering subspaces
X_{i}

(i\in I,\penalty\ X=\cup_{i\in I}X_{i})
, then the model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is weakly commutative at all points in
X
.
Note that
\Theta_{i}
s do not necessarily coincide.

Now a question is, whether any weakly commutative models have such covering subspaces endowed with antiunitary symmetries.
While global weak commutativity does not imply GAS, it may imply patchworks of the small antiunitary symmetries.
If so, weak commutativity and the existence of antiunitary symmetries should be equivalent at sufficiently small regions.
In the light of Thm.
3
, such a “local” antiunitary symmetry might be useful for constructing target-
dependent
optimal POVMs.

The answer to the question is positive for pure-state models and negative in general.
We show that all weakly commutative pure-state models have local antiunitary symmetries.
However, this result does not extend to mixed-state models.
The weakly commutativity does not imply local antiunitary symmetries.

We define the local antiunitary symmetry (LAS) as a straightforward limitation of the GAS to neighborhoods of a point.

Definition 3

A model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
is said to have a LAS at
{\bm{x}}\in X
, if there is an antiunitary operator
\Theta
such that
\Theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=\rho_{\bm{x}}
and
\Theta\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}
hold for all
i
.

To see how LAS relates to the GAS, consider the first-order approximation of state
\rho_{{\bm{x}}+\Delta{\bm{x}}}
around the point
{\bm{x}}\in X
:

\rho_{{\bm{x}}+\Delta{\bm{x}}}\approx\rho_{\bm{x}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta x_{i}\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}},

(109)

where
\Delta x_{i}
are small real numbers.
Then the LAS
\Theta
satisfies

\Theta\rho_{{\bm{x}}+\Delta{\bm{x}}}\Theta^{\dagger}\approx\rho_{{\bm{x}}+\Delta{\bm{x}}},

(110)

in the first-order approximation.
Thus if there is a LAS at
{\bm{x}}\in X
, the states in a small neighborhood around
{\bm{x}}
share an approximately good GAS.

LAS does imply weak commutativity at each point.
Furthermore, these two are equivalent for pure-state models.

Theorem 4

If a model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
has a LAS at
{\bm{x}}\in X
, it is weakly commutative at
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
Pure models have LASs at points where the models are weakly commutative.

The proof of Thm.
4
is delegated to Appx.
B.1
.
The off-equator phase estimation on spin, for example, have LASs at all points, despite having no GAS.
This implies that the piecewise antiunitary symmetries do not necessarily joint together to form a single antiunitary symmetry.

The LAS accompanies the optimal POVMs to saturate QCRB for pure-state models (see Appx.
B.2
for details).
Similarly to the case of GAS (Thm.
3
), they are the rank-
1
POVM invariant under the LAS.
Since the LAS may depend on the target point, the optimal POVMs are also target-dependent in general.

Regarding mixed states, there are weakly commutative models without any LAS.
We can further say that there are quasi-classical models without any LAS when
\dim\mathcal{H}\geq 3
, but not when
\dim\mathcal{H}=2
.
Examples are presented in Appx.
B.3
.
The structure of compatibility is more involved than to be explained by antiunitary symmetries.

5.3
Quantifying antiunitary asymmetry

In this section, we try quantifying antiunitary asymmetry of quantum statistical models.
Theorems
2
and
4
both states that incompatibility, a unique property of quantum mechanics, is absent for models with antiunitary symmetry.
Conversely, a model behaves quantum mechanically when it does not have antiunitary symmetry.

There will be many functions measuring antiunitary asymmetry of a quantum statistical model.
For a model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
at a point
{\bm{x}}\in X
define

\displaystyle M(\rho_{\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Theta\in\mathcal{U}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})\\
\Theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger}=\rho_{\bm{x}}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})^{2}\right],

(111)

where
\mathcal{U}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the set of antiunitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
This measure is faithful in that it takes zero if and only if there is a
local
antiunitary symmetry at
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
It is also invariant under isometric coordinate transformations
{\bm{x}}\mapsto R{\bm{x}}
(
R
: isometric matrix, see Appx.
5.3
for details).

We are inspired by imaginarity measures formulated in the resource theory to define the asymmetry measure (
111
).
Originally, the imaginarity of a single quantum state is measured with respect to a fixed basis
\{|k\rangle\}_{k=1,...,d}

[
60
,
61
,
62
]
, or equivalently to a fixed conjugation
\theta
.
The formalisms resemble resource theories of coherence
[
92
,
93
,
94
]
, in that they both require a fixed reference basis.

In contrast, the weak commutativity is implied by any conjugation.
Such a basis-independent definition of imaginarity has not been studied yet.
However, there is a basis-independent definition of coherence termed “set coherence”
[
95
]
.
In Appx.
C
we define “set imaginarity” by following the procedure for set coherence, and extend it to asymmetry measures for quantum statistical models.

The faithful measure of antiunitary asymmetry characterizes quantumness of the model, similarly to an incompatibility measure.
Carollo
et. al.

[
43
]
introduced a faithful measure of incompatibility.
As being faithful, it takes zero only if the mean Uhlmann curvature is zero.
The incompatibility measure is used to detect quantumness of phase transitions through the incompatibility of parametrized thermal states
[
43
]
.
Belliardo and Giovannetti
[
33
]
introduced another faithful measure of incompatibility as a ratio between attainable precision and the precision based on SLD QFIMs.

Faithful asymmetry measures could be more sensitive to systems’ quantumness than the incompatibility is.
As is presented in Appx.
B.3
, there are models with zero incompatibility but with positive antiunitary asymmetry.
Furthermore, imaginarity can be defined on single-parameter models, while the incompatibility requires more than
2
parameters.
Thus measures of antiunitary asymmetry could be more useful than the incompatibility measure for characterizing quantum phase transitions.
We hope to study this in more detail on a different occasion.

6
Conclusion

We introduced a class of quantum statistical models endowed with symmetries under antiunitary operators.
A model of this class consists only of real density matrices.
The null phase of density matrices implies the null mean Uhlmann curvature, enabling the compatible multiparameter estimation.
A variety of existing models have antiunitary symmetries.

If a pure-state model has an antiunitary symmetry, any rank-
1
antiunitary-invariant POVM saturates the QCRB.
Once you find the antiunitary symmetry, you discover an optimal measurement at the same time.
The antiunitary-invariant measurement is optimal independently of the target point to be estimated.
To perform such a measurement, you require neither prerequisite knowledge on the target, nor the adaptive measurement to guess the target.
By utilizing the freedom of the optimal measurements, we derived an antiunitary-invariant measurement for 3D magnetometry, which requires only bipartite entanglement irrespective to the number of spins.
The antiunitary-invariant measurement is, however, not necessarily optimal for mixed-state models.

We proposed methods to implement antiunitary symmetries in several settings of quantum metrology.

For phase estimations of particular unitary evolutions, all the optimal initial states lead to imaginarity-free models.
Antiunitary symmetries can coexist with optimal metrological schemes in this setting.

Mutually conjugate state pair
\rho_{\bm{x}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\ast}
has better precision bounds than
\rho_{\bm{x}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{x}}
does, independently of the model
\rho_{\bm{x}}
.
The conjugate pair has an antiunitary symmetry independent of the original model
\rho_{\bm{x}}
.
We consider how to produce the conjugated state
\rho_{\bm{x}}^{\ast}
when a parametrized unitary evolution generates
\rho_{\bm{x}}
.
This is possible especially if the Hamiltonian has an antiunitary skew-symmetry, or if the inverse state evolution is also available.

Finally, we studied antiunitary symmetries varying along the target points of a model.
A pure-state model is compatible if and only if it has the target-dependent antiunitary symmetry.
Based on the study, we introduced a function to measure antiunitary asymmetry of quantum statistical models at given target points.
The measure is faithful and invariant under isometric coordinate transformations on the parameter space.

Our results have room for extension.
Despite no mention here, infinite dimensional Hilbert spaces have unique uncertainty relations.
Target-independent optimal measurements are, including their existence, still unknown for mixed state models.
Extending our results in these untouched regions will help develop various metrology schemes such as superresolution
[
47
,
96
,
97
,
48
,
98
,
31
]
.

We are currently relying on physicists’ intuition to discover the antiunitary symmetry of quantum statistical models.
While intuition sometimes works perfectly on physics-born models, a general algorithm to find the antiunitary symmetry (if it exists) would be much more effective.
Such an algorithm will be useful not only for quantum metrology, but also for understanding quantum phase transitions via asymmetry measures.

We first show that the depolarized antiparallel spins is not quasi-classical.
Then we present the deviation
\Delta\phi^{2}
of single-parameter
\phi

The latter corresponds to the precision of the off-equator phase estimation on the depolarized antiparallel spins.

The state of depolarized antiparallel spins is defined by Eq. (

A pair of SLDs for depolarized antiparallel spins is given by

for

\displaystyle L_{\phi}

(1-\delta)^{2}\left([L_{\eta},L_{\phi}]\otimes\mathbb{I}_{2}+\mathbb{I}_{2}\otimes\Theta_{f}[L_{\eta},L_{\phi}]\Theta_{f}\right).

This never becomes zero since

\mathbb{I}_{2}

It is not partially commutative as being of full-rank.

\phi

A.2
Qubit inside a disc

[

gave an weakly commutative model of a qubit

\displaystyle\left\{\rho_{\bm{x}}=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{2}+{\mathbf{n}}_{\bm{x}}\cdot{\bm{\sigma}}}{2}\middle|\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathbf{n}}_{\bm{x}}=f_{1}({\bm{x}}){\mathbf{n}}_{1}+f_{2}({\bm{x}}){\mathbf{n}}_{2},\\
{\bm{x}}\in X\end{array}\right\},

where

and

are arbitrary real functions of the parameter
{\bm{x}}\in X
such that
|{\mathbf{n}}_{\bm{x}}|\leq 1
, and
{\mathbf{n}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{n}}_{2}
are arbitrary (not necessarily orthogonal) unit vectors on

{\mathbf{n}}_{1}
and
{\mathbf{n}}_{2}

(0,0,0)
and cuts the sphere into halves.

[
27
]

The qubit inside a disc (
A.2
) is imaginarity-free.

A.3
Embedding quantum simulators

Here we introduce a class of model inspired by embedding quantum simulators
[
67
,
68
,
69
,
70

EQS model
for pure state model

(151)

(152)

with some conjugation
\theta

\mathcal{H}

\mathcal{H}_{2}
is a two dimensional ancillary system with an orthonormal basis
\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}
.
The transformation

is not realized as a completely positive map.
Extra resources are required to produce the state
|\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{EQS}}\rangle
.

The global phase subtlety matters when modifying to EQS models.
The modifications to EQS models result in different models from
|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
and from
|e^{i\phi({\bm{x}})}\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
where
\phi({\bm{x}})
is a parameter-dependent global phase.

Embedding quantum simulators typically utilize the encoding of state
|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle
in the form

\frac{|\mathrm{Re}\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\otimes|0\rangle+|\mathrm{Im}\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\otimes|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}

(153)

instead of
|\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{EQS}}\rangle

67
]
.
With (
153
) we arrive at the same precision bound since it is unitarily equivalent to
|\Psi_{\bm{x}}^{\mathrm{EQS}}\rangle
.

A GAS of EQS model (
151

(\mathbb{I}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\sigma_{X})(\theta\otimes\theta_{2}),

(154)

where
\theta\otimes\theta_{2}
is the conjugation in the product basis
|i\rangle\otimes|j\rangle
such that
\theta|i\rangle=|i\rangle
(
i=1,...,\dim\mathcal{H},\penalty\ j=0,1
).
A reference basis to represent (
154

\left\{|i\rangle\otimes\frac{|0\rangle+|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},\penalty\ |i\rangle\otimes\frac{|0\rangle-|1\rangle}{\sqrt{2}i}\middle|\penalty\ i=1,...,\dim\mathcal{H}\right\}.

(155)

\mathcal{F}_{Q}^{\mathrm{EQS}}({\bm{x}})=\mathcal{F}_{Q}({\bm{x}})+{\bm{X}}{\bm{X}}^{\mathrm{T}},

(156)

{\bm{X}}^{\mathrm{T}}=2i\left(\langle\partial_{1}\psi_{\bm{x}}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\penalty\ ...\penalty\ \langle\partial_{n}\psi_{\bm{x}}|\psi_{\bm{x}}\rangle\right),

(157)

It is futile to modify EQS models again to their EQS models to obtain even better precision bound.
The second modifications are equivalent to mere unitary transformations.

Appendix C
Set imaginarity, set antiunitary asymmetry, and asymmetry measures for quantum statistical models

In this section, we consider measures of imaginarity and antiunitary asymmetry for a set of quantum states.
The purpose is to later introduce corresponding measures for quantum statistical models.
We employ measures of imaginarity from a resource theoretic framework reviewed below.
Then the measures are extended to fit to our purpose.

C.1
Resource theory of imaginarity

Resource theories of imaginarity
[
60
,
61
,
62
]
start from specifying a reference basis
\{|k\rangle\}_{k=1,...,d}
.
Then imaginarity-free states are determined as real density matrices in the basis.
To fit to our discussion, we represent the set of imaginarity-free states as

F_{\theta}=\left\{\rho\penalty\ \mid\penalty\ \theta\rho\theta=\rho\right\}.

(192)

where
\theta
is the complex conjugation in the reference basis
\{|k\rangle\}_{k=1,...,d}
.
Measures of imaginarity quantify how much a given state differs from imaginarity-free states.
The measures must satisfy monotonicity under imaginarity-free operations (operations that does not affect imaginarity-free states
[
60
]
), and return zero for imaginarity-free states.
Hickey and Gour proposed the distance measures

M^{C}_{\theta}(\rho):=\min_{\tau\in F_{\theta}}C(\rho,\tau),\penalty\ (C:{\rm contractive\penalty\ metric})

(193)

and the robustness of imaginarity
[
60
]
.
If we choose the trace distance, the distance measure is expressed in a closed-form

M^{1}_{\theta}(\rho):=\min_{\tau\in F_{\theta}}||\rho-\tau||_{1}=\frac{||\rho-\theta\rho\theta||_{1}}{2},

(194)

and is faithful, meaning that they are strictly positive outside the imaginarity-free states.
The robustness later turned out to be equal to
M^{1}_{\theta}

[
61
,
62
]
.

C.2
Set imaginarity

The imaginarity thus defined is measured in terms of a fixed basis
\{|k\rangle\}_{k=1,...,d}
, or equivalently a fixed conjugation
\theta
.
In contrast, the weak commutativity is implied by any conjugation.
In our sense, a model
\{\rho_{\bm{x}}|{\bm{x}}\in X\}
should be called “imaginarity-free” if the states are simultaneously real for some reference basis which is not fixed in advance.
We are looking at a basis-independent definition of imaginarity.

Such a definition of imaginarity has not been studied yet.
However, there is a basis-independent definition of coherence
[
92
,
93
,
94
]
termed “set coherence”
[
95
]
.
We follow the procedure to define “set imaginarity,” and “set antiunitary asymmetry.”

A set of
n
quantum states is presented by
\rho^{(n)}=\{\rho_{1},...,\rho_{n}\}
.
The free set is given by

F^{(n)}=\left\{\rho^{(n)}\middle|\exists\theta,\rho^{(n)}\subset F_{\theta}\right\},

(195)

for each
n
.
It is a union
F^{(n)}=\cup_{\theta}F^{(n)}_{\theta}
of free sets for fixed conjugations defined by

F^{(n)}_{\theta}=\left\{\rho^{(n)}\middle|\rho^{(n)}\subset F_{\theta}\right\}.

(196)

Completely analogously to set coherence
[
95
]
, we may define max-distance

M^{C}_{\max}(\rho^{(n)}):=\min_{\theta}\max_{j}M^{C}_{\theta}(\rho_{j}),

(197)

and mean-distance

M^{C}_{\rm mean}(\rho^{(n)})=\min_{\theta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}M^{C}_{\theta}(\rho_{j}),

(198)

as measures of set imaginarity.
These measures are faithful when we choose the trace-distance because
M^{1}_{\theta}
is faithful for the basis-dependent imaginarity.
We do not touch operational meanings of the measures thus defined.
A measure of set coherence inherits an operational meaning from basis-dependent coherence
[
95
]
.

C.3
Antiunitary asymmetry

The procedure to define (basis-dependent) imaginarity generalizes straightforwardly to antiunitary asymmetry.
The representation (
192
) of imaginarity-free states extends the states with general antiunitary symmetry

F_{\Theta}=\left\{\rho\penalty\ \middle|\penalty\ \Theta\rho\Theta^{\dagger}=\rho\right\},

(199)

by replacing the conjugation
\theta
to an antiunitary operator
\Theta
.
The distant measures are generalized accordingly.

The set imaginarity and “set antiunitary asymmetry” are equivalent when defined by distance measures.
Theorem
1
implies that for any antiunitary operator
\Theta
, there is a conjugation
\theta
such that

F_{\Theta}\subset F_{\theta},

(200)

and hence

M^{C}_{\theta}(\rho)\leq M^{C}_{\Theta}(\rho),\penalty\ (\forall\rho)

(201)

hold for any distance measure.
The set of free states for the set antiunitary asymmetry

\left\{\rho^{(n)}\middle|\exists\Theta,\rho^{(n)}\subset F_{\Theta}\right\},

(202)

is equal to the free set for imaginarity
F^{(n)}
by Eq. (
200
).
The max- and mean-distances

\min_{\Theta}\max_{j}M^{C}_{\Theta}(\rho_{j}),\penalty\ \min_{\Theta}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}M^{C}_{\Theta}(\rho_{j}),

(203)

are equals respectively to
M^{C}_{\max}(\rho^{(n)})
and
M^{C}_{\rm mean}(\rho^{(n)})
by Eq. (
201
).
The difference between imaginarity and antiunitary asymmetry only appears at the basis-dependent definitions for distance measures.

C.4
Antiunitary asymmetry of quantum statistical models

There are many ways to define antiunitary asymmetry of a quantum statistical model
\rho_{\bm{x}}
at each point
{\bm{x}}\in X
.
We require the measure to be faithful: the measure must be zero only if there is a LAS at the point.
The max-distance (
197
) inspires us to define

M^{1}_{\max}({\bm{x}}):=\min_{\theta}\lim_{\Delta x\rightarrow 0}\frac{\max_{i}M^{1}_{\theta}(\rho_{\bm{x}}+\Delta x\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}})}{\Delta x},

(204)

and call it max-imaginarity.
Since the limit diverges unless
\theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta=\rho_{\bm{x}}
, we have

M^{1}_{\max}({\bm{x}})=\min_{\theta,\theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta=\rho_{\bm{x}}}\max_{i}\frac{||\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\theta\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta||_{1}}{2}.

(205)

In a similar way we define mean-imaginarity as

\displaystyle M^{1}_{\rm mean}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle:=\min_{\theta}\lim_{\Delta x\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{M^{1}_{\theta}(\rho_{\bm{x}}+\Delta x\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}})}{\Delta x}

(206)

\displaystyle=\min_{\theta,\theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta=\rho_{\bm{x}}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{||\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\theta\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta||_{1}}{2},

inspired by the mean-distance (
198

The imaginarity and the antiunitary asymmetry remains equivalent for these definitions.

Our asymmetry measure (
111

206

We are not sure if the measure is equivalent to

\displaystyle\min_{\theta,\penalty\ \theta\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta=\rho_{\bm{x}}}\frac{1}{n}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\theta\partial_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\theta)^{2}\right],

(207)

Our asymmetry measure(

Since the Jacobian of an isometric coordinate transformation is an orthogonal matrix

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\partial^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial^{\prime}_{i}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}R_{ij}(\partial_{j}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial_{j}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})

\displaystyle\hskip 71.13188pt\times R_{ik}(\partial_{k}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial_{k}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})

\displaystyle\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}\delta_{jk}(\partial_{j}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial_{j}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})(\partial_{k}\rho_{\bm{x}}-\Theta\partial_{k}\rho_{\bm{x}}\Theta^{\dagger})

I am not sure if there is a faithful asymmetry measure that is invariant under general normal coordinate transformations.
The LASs, if they exist, are preserved by these transformations.
Namely, a model

has a LAS at

\left\{\rho_{f^{-1}({\bm{y}})}\mid{\bm{y}}\in f(X)\right\},

(209)

Methods to extend this invariance of antiunitary symmetry to asymmetry measures remain open here.

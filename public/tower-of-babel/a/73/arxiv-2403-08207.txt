BG-HGNN : Toward Scalable and Efficient Heterogeneous Graph Neural Network
Junwei Su, Lingjun Mao, Chuan Wu

Abstract
Many computer vision and machine learning problems are modelled as learning tasks on heterogeneous graphs, featuring a wide array of relations from diverse types of nodes and edges. Heterogeneous graph neural networks (HGNNs) stand out as a promising neural model class designed for heterogeneous graphs. Built on traditional GNNs, existing HGNNs employ different parameter spaces to model the varied relationships. However, the practical effectiveness of existing HGNNs is often limited to simple heterogeneous graphs with few relation types. This paper first highlights and demonstrates that the standard approach employed by existing HGNNs inevitably leads to parameter explosion and relation collapse , making HGNNs less effective or impractical for complex heterogeneous graphs with numerous relation types. To overcome this issue, we introduce a novel framework, B lend& G rind-HGNN ( BG-HGNN ), which effectively tackles the challenges by carefully integrating different relations into a unified feature space manageable by a single set of parameters. This results in a refined HGNN method that is more efficient and effective in learning from heterogeneous graphs, especially when the number of relations grows. Our empirical studies illustrate that BG-HGNN significantly surpasses existing HGNNs in terms of parameter efficiency (up to 28.96 × \times ), training throughput (up to 8.12 × \times ), and accuracy (up to 1.07 × \times ). † † footnotetext: *: equal contribution

Conversely, subgraph-based HGNNs resemble GNNs but adapt different parameter spaces for modelling various relations

Despite their potential, we find that
existing HGNNs become both inefficient and ineffective at learning from complex heterogeneous graphs as the number of relation types grows
. We pinpoint and attribute this shortcoming to the issues of
parameter explosion
and
relation collapse
, as illustrated in Fig.
1(b)
. Specifically, existing HGNNs rely on distinct aggregation functions (parameter spaces) to model varied relations, causing the parameter space for each HGNN layer to expand with the increase in relation types—a situation exacerbated by using multiple layers. Moreover, as different relations might exhibit distinct distribution modes, combining these varied relations can trigger information loss, diminishing the expressiveness of HGNN and its ability to capture complex interactions. Therefore, addressing parameter explosion and relation collapse is crucial for enhancing the practicality and effectiveness of HGNNs. This motivates the central query of this study:

How can we develop a unified mechanism to mitigate parameter explosion and relation collapse in HGNNs?

In this paper, we conduct a thorough examination of parameter explosion and relation collapse in HGNNs and propose a novel, straightforward, yet effective HGNN framework,
B
lend&
G
rind-HGNN
(

), to tackle these challenges. The contributions of this paper are highlighted as follows.

1.

To the best of our knowledge, we are the first to explicitly identify and detail the dual challenges of parameter explosion and relation collapse in HGNNs. Through theoretical and empirical analysis, we demonstrate how an increase in relation types can drastically affect the efficiency and performance of HGNNs. Our findings offer insights into the underlying mechanisms of these limitations, providing valuable guidance for enhancing HGNN scalability in the design of this work and future research.

2.

To overcome these challenges, we introduce
BG-HGNN
, an innovative framework designed to scale HGNNs for complex heterogeneous graphs with numerous relations.
BG-HGNN
effectively integrates heterogeneous information into a unified feature space via a series of fusion and encoding processes (“blend and grind”), allowing the use of a unified layer (parameter space) for learning. This approach not only makes

3.

To validate our analysis and the effectiveness of our proposed method, we conduct an extensive experimental study with

Preliminary and Related Works

A heterogeneous graph can be defined as,

\mathcal{G}=(V,E,\mathcal{T}_{V},\mathcal{T}_{E},\phi,\psi),

where
V
denotes the set of nodes,
E\subseteq V\times V
represents the set of edges,
\mathcal{T}_{V}=\{\tau_{v,1},\dots,\tau_{v,m}\}
and
\mathcal{T}_{E}=\{\tau_{e,1},\dots,\tau_{e,n}\}
are the sets of node types and edge types respectively.
\phi:V\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{V}
is a function mapping each node to its type, and
\psi:E\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{E}
is a function mapping each edge to its type. Each node
v\in V
is associated with feature vectors
\mathbf{x}_{v}
, to represent their attributes. Notably, feature vectors for nodes of different types might possess varying dimensions, corresponding to distinct feature channels. For instance, in a citation network, author nodes might feature channels related to their field of study, affiliated institutions, and venues, whereas paper nodes might include channels related to the paper’s keywords.

As mentioned, existing HGNNs are categorized into meta-path-based
[
6
,
25
,
21
,
31
,
2
]
and subgraph-based approaches
[
39
,
40
,
20
,
42
,
7
]
. A meta-path is a sequence of relations that connects different types of entities in a heterogeneous graph, effectively encapsulating the complex structural and semantic relationships among these entities. It serves as a schema-guided pathway that reveals how different entities are interconnected through specific types of relationships, providing a high-level abstraction for analyzing and extracting meaningful patterns from heterogeneous graphs. Meta-path-based HGNNs leverage these meta-paths to guide the information aggregation process, selectively gathering contextual information from neighbouring nodes along specified meta-paths. However, the effectiveness of meta-path-based HGNNs is heavily contingent on the selection of appropriate meta-paths, which requires substantial domain knowledge and manual design effort. This reliance on pre-defined meta-paths limits the transferability of meta-path-based HGNNs across different datasets and domains. Since the relevance and availability of specific types of entities and relations can vary significantly between graphs, a meta-path that is informative and effective in one domain may not be applicable or even present in another.

Conversely, subgraph-based HGNNs focus on leveraging the entire subgraphs or specific substructures within a heterogeneous graph to compute node representations, rather than relying on predefined meta-paths. This approach utilizes a message-passing scheme
[
20
]
, where information is aggregated and updated from a node’s local neighbourhood based on the various types of relationships present, allowing for the capture of more nuanced and detailed structural patterns within the graph. Unlike meta-path-based HGNNs, which require domain-specific knowledge to define relevant paths, subgraph-based HGNNs automatically explore and utilize the rich connectivity inherent in the graph’s substructures. This makes them more flexible and adaptable across different datasets and domains, as they do not necessitate manual selection and design of meta-paths, thereby offering a more generalized solution for learning from heterogeneous graphs. The inherent ability of subgraph-based HGNNs to preserve and exploit local graph structures without the need for domain-specific customization enhance their utility and scalability, making them favourably suited for a wide range of applications. The computation in HGNNs to generate the node representation
\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_{v}
at the
l
-th layer is defined as (the difference from GNN is highlighted in blue):

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_{v,r}=\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{AGGREGATE}^{(l)}_{r}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\left\{\mathbf{h}^{(l-1)}_{u},u\in N_{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r}}(v)\right\}\right),\\
\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_{v}=\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\text{COMBINE}^{(l)}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\left(\mathbf{h}^{(l-1)}_{v},\left\{\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_{v,r},\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r\in\mathcal{R}\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\right\}\right).\end{gathered}

(2.1)

Here,
\text{AGGREGATE}_{r}^{(l)}
is a relation-specific aggregation function,
N_{r}(v)
denotes the set of nodes under relation
r
within the neighbourhood of
v
,
\mathbf{h}^{(l)}_{v,r}
represents the intermediate representation for relation
r
, and
\text{COMBINE}^{(l)}
is the cross-relation combine function that blends the representations from different relations. The AGGREGATE function is often chosen as a GNN layer such as GAT and GCN, while COMBINE is typically a reduction function like summation.

In this paper, we focus on the subgraph-based HGNNs for their generality. In particular, we study and investigate the limitations of HGNN, parameter explosion and relation collapse. We propose a novel framework,
BG-HGNN
, that can effectively address these two limitations further advancing the practicality of HGNN in more complex heterogeneous graphs.

Figure 2
:
Overview of the
BG-HGNN
workflow.
BG-HGNN
commences by extracting node types and relation information from the heterogeneous graph and subsequently transforming them into feature vectors. The Kronecker product is then employed for feature fusion. A subsequent linear layer refines these features into their final representation. Then, these representations can be used as features as the ones in a homogeneous GNN setting.

3
Methodology

This section outlines the
BG-HGNN
framework, which consists of: 1) attribute space transformation for fusing heterogeneous attributes into a shared space; 2) encoding and projection of node and edge types; 3) fusion of attribute and type spaces to encapsulate interconnected information. Fig.
7
provides a graphical illustration of the overall procedure. The pseudo-code of the overall procedure can be found in the supplementary material.

3.1
Attributes Space Fusion

The first step of our framework is to merge different feature channels from various node types. Attribute space fusion aims to consolidate feature vectors from distinct types into a common space for efficient representation. This involves extracting a minimal feature space
\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}}
that encompasses all feature channels across node types, then projecting each node’s feature vector into this unified space. The projection retains the original feature vector values for existing channels, using a designated value (e.g.,
-\infty
) for absent channels. Fig.
7
visually depicts this approach. Formally, this process is defined as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{V}}\mathcal{X}_{\tau}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{v}^{\prime}=\text{proj}(\mathbf{x}_{v},\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}x_{v,i}&\text{if feature }i\text{ exists in }\mathbf{x}_{v}\\
-\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(3.2)

This process fosters a unified attribute space across diverse node types, enhancing weight sharing among different types of nodes
for efficient information learning. Moreover, it makes the absence of specific features becomes informative during the learning process.

3.2
Type Encoding through Dense Random Projection

To effectively encode the type information of nodes and edges, we transition from traditional one-hot encoding to dense random projection. One-hot encoding, despite its common use, often leads to sparse representations that inadequately capture the intricate interactions across different types of relations. Therefore, we propose employing dense random projection to encode type information. This approach ensures that the encoded type information is dense and unbiased, aiding in nuanced integration into subsequent learning phases without prior bias. This methodology is detailed as follows:

node Type Encoding.
We denote,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}}=\{\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{v,1}},\dots,\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{v,m}}\}

(3.3)

the set of encoding for the node type after uniform random projections. Each
\mathbf{o}_{v,i}
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and each component
o_{j}
of
\mathbf{o}_{v,i}
is drawn independently from
\mathcal{U}(a,b)
where
\mathcal{U}(a,b)
is a uniform distribution over the interval
[a,b]
.

Edge Type Encoding.
Analogous to node types, edge types are encoded using random vectors. We denote,

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{T}_{E}}=\{\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{e,1}},\dots,\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{e,n}}\}

(3.4)

the set of encoding for the edge type after uniform random projections. Each
\mathbf{o}_{e,i}
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and each component
o_{j}
(the
j
-th entry) of
\mathbf{o}_{e,i}
is drawn independently from
\mathcal{U}(a,b)
where
\mathcal{U}(a,b)
is a uniform distribution over the interval
[a,b]
. Further, for each node, the type information of its neighbours is averaged. Then, we denote the set of vectors after aggregating the type information from the neighbour as,

\displaystyle\Omega=\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m}\}.

(3.5)

Each
\omega_{v}\in\Omega
is given by,

\displaystyle\omega_{v}=\frac{1}{|N(v)|}\sum_{u\in N(v)}\mathbf{o}_{\psi((u,v))},

(3.6)

where
N(v)
represent the set of neighboring node of
v
and
(u,v)
is the corresponding edge.

3.3
Information Fusion and Projection

At this stage, information from the heterogeneous graph has been consolidated into a tuple,

(\mathcal{X}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{T}_{V}},\Omega).

The final component of our framework is to further fuse this information into a common space. The common approach would simply concatenate all this information into one vector. However, simply concatenating all the feature vectors renders these feature vectors embedded in orthogonal feature space and is ineffective in capturing the interactions across these feature vectors.

Therefore, in
BG-HGNN
, we draw inspiration from multi-modality learning
[
37
,
11
,
18
,
10
,
29
,
32
]
and the recent empirical success of low-rank adaptation (LoRa)
[
11
]
to devise an improved approach. We propose encapsulating the above information and exploring interactions through the Kronecker product
[
37
]
and using a learnable linear layer to distil them into a low-dimensional space. Specifically, for each node
v
of type
\tau_{v,i}
, along with its associated attribute
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}^{\prime}
, type vector
\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{v,i}}
, and
\omega_{v}
, we define,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{v}=\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{v,i}}\otimes\omega_{v},

(3.7)

yielding a tensor that captures all possible interactions among diverse information. Then, following the idea of LoRa, we use a learnable linear projection to distil this fused space into a low-rank weighted decomposition

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{W}_{i}\mathbf{H}_{v}.

(3.8)

To further enhance the memory and computation efficiency of our method, we use the derivation from
[
11
]
and arrive at the following expression,

\displaystyle\mathbf{h}_{v}=(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{w}^{(\mathbf{x})}_{i}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{v})\otimes(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{w}^{(\mathbf{o})}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{o}_{\tau_{v,i}})\otimes(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{w}^{(\omega)}_{i}\cdot\omega_{v}),

(3.9)

where
\mathbf{w}^{(\mathbf{x})}_{i},\mathbf{w}^{(\mathbf{o})}_{i},\mathbf{w}^{(\omega)}_{i}
are the learnable weights. In addition, our empirical results (Fig.
5(a)
) find that
r=4/5
is enough to achieve the best performance in most scenarios. After the above series of encoding and transformation, we arrived at a feature vector
\mathbf{h}_{v}
for each node that has the encoded information from the heterogeneity of the data. Then, a simple GNN-like base model can be used to further integrate the structural information of the graph. In our experiment, we use GAT
[
28
]
as the base model to further capture the graph structure.

3.4
Theoretical Discussion

We delve into a theoretical examination of
BG-HGNN
, demonstrating its efficiency and enhanced expressive power compared to traditional HGNNs. Our analysis covers both the parameter complexity and expressiveness of the model, showing that
BG-HGNN
maintains a low parameter count while effectively capturing the complexities of heterogeneous graphs.

Complexity Analysis.

Our analysis centres on the parameter complexity of
BG-HGNN
, which serves as an indicator of computational efficiency. Given that hidden dimensions are typically adjusted during training, we assume the hidden dimension is fixed and focus the analysis on the number of independent parameter spaces required.

For a heterogeneous graph with a relation count of
|\mathcal{R}|>0
and an HGNN comprising
L
layers, standard HGNN models, as described by Eq. (
2.1
), exhibit a parameter complexity of
O(|\mathcal{R}|^{L})
. This indicates an exponential growth in parameter complexity proportional to the number of HGNN layers. Conversely,
BG-HGNN
amalgamates all heterogeneous information into a singular vector, rendering its parameter complexity independent of the graph’s relation count and thus
O(rL)
, where
r
is the rank used in decomposition Eq. (
3.9
). This shows that the parameter complexity of
BG-HGNN
remains stable, even as the number of relations increases within the graph. Empirical evidence suggests that a small rank (
r=4/5
) is enough to achieve effective performance, further underscoring the efficiency of
BG-HGNN
.

Expressiveness.

The expressiveness of a graph neural network, or its capacity to distinguish between different subgraphs, is a vital measure of its performance. According to the established framework
[
19
]
in graph neural network literature:

Definition 1

We say a method
f_{1}
is at least as expressive as method
f_{2}
if, for two given subgraphs
\mathcal{G}_{1},\mathcal{G}_{2}
that can be differentiated by
f_{1}(f_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\neq f_{1}(\mathcal{G}_{2}))
, the two subgraphs can also be differentiated by
f_{2}(f_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{1})\neq f_{2}(\mathcal{G}_{2}))
.

Then, we have the following results for the expressive power between
BG-HGNN
and existing HGNN.

Proposition 1

BG-HGNN
is at least as expressive as standard HGNN given by Eq.
2.1
.

The detailed proof of Proposition
1
can be found in the supplementary material. Proposition
1
asserts that the increased (parameter) efficiency of
BG-HGNN
does not come at the cost of expressive power. This shows that our method is more efficient in learning heterogeneous graphs.

Furthermore, the different parameter spaces of standard HGNN squash information about different relations (potentially from different feature channels) into a fixed subspace. This can result in relation collapse. To formally illustrate this, we consider the following example.

Example 1

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a heterogeneous graph with three relations
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}
whose representations are characterzied by normal distributions
\mathbb{N}(0,1),\mathbb{N}(-1,1),
and
\mathbb{N}(1,1)
respectively.

It becomes evident that with a standard set of aggregation functions, existing HGNNs lacks the capability to discern the existence of all three relations for any given node within this graph. For instance, aggregating the data pertaining to
r_{2}
and
r_{3}
using an average might inadvertently suggest the distribution characteristic of
r_{1}
. In simpler terms, existing HGNNs might falsely recognize the existence of
r_{1}
over
r_{2}
or
r_{3}
. Conversely,
BG-HGNN
encodes relation information into distinct channels for
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3})
, ensuring that the distributions for different relations do not overlap or interfere. Therefore,
BG-HGNN
is more expressive (powerful) than existing HGNN in the example above. Combining Example.
1
and Proposition
1
, we can immediately obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1

BG-HGNN
is more expressive than standard HGNN.

Corollary
3.1
asserts the superiority of
BG-HGNN
in terms of expressiveness, particularly in complex heterogeneous graphs where diverse relational data is prevalent. This increased expressiveness, combined with parameter efficiency, positions
BG-HGNN
as a highly effective framework for learning from heterogeneous graphs.

5
Conclusion and Discussion

In this work, we study and investigate heterogeneous graph learning, which is fundamental to many computer vision and machine learning tasks. We presented
BG-HGNN
, a framework innovatively addressing parameter explosion and relation collapse in HGNNs. Through theoretical and empirical analyses,
BG-HGNN
showcased superior efficiency and expressiveness compared to conventional HGNNs by integrating attribute space transformation, dense random projection, and a novel information fusion mechanism. This enables
BG-HGNN
to adeptly handle the complexities of heterogeneous graphs with fewer parameters and enhanced learning capabilities.

Discussion.
In the development of
BG-HGNN
, we observed that the sparsity inherent in type encoding complicates the learning of interactions across various relations. To mitigate this, we employed dense projection techniques using a uniform distribution. The selection of this distribution can play a role in performance, suggesting that exploring the effects of different distributions on performance is a promising avenue for future research, albeit outside the scope of this paper. Moreover,
BG-HGNN
lays the groundwork for a unified graph framework that transcends dataset and domain boundaries, paving the way towards establishing foundational models for graph data. This opens up possibilities for broader applications and further advancements in graph-based learning.

This is achieved by amalgamating the attributes of all nodes to create a comprehensive common space, which encompasses all potential features. This approach presents two significant benefits: firstly, it enables the sharing of weights across different node types, enhancing the efficiency of the learning process. Departing from standard feature aggregation methods like vector concatenation, our methodology adopts the Kronecker product. Crucially, we append a ’1’ to each feature vector beforehand, a step that serves several purposes. It not only captures the interaction between different modalities, but also preserves the distinct features of each one. Here, we take a two-dimensional example as an illustration; the detailed principle can be seen in Fig

Upon completing the initial processing, the three distinct features undergo a Kronecker product operation, subsequently passed through a linear layer, culminating in a singular one-dimensional feature vector. A crucial concern arising from the Kronecker product of three vectors is the potential exponential escalation in the number of parameters. To strategically address this predicament, we draw inspiration from the Low-rank Multimodal Fusion (LMF) approach, which is shown in Fig
8
. The essence of LMF lies in its ability to parallelly decompose tensors and weights. This decomposition leverages modality-specific low-rank factors to carry out the multimodal fusion. By eschewing the computation of high-dimensional tensors, this method not only curtails memory overhead but also adeptly mitigates the time complexity, transforming it from exponential to linear. To gain a deeper understanding of the computational costs associated with our method, we extend our analysis beyond the comparison of time required per epoch for different models in the main text. This extension involves considering the preprocessing time, encompassing essential tasks such as encoding node types and edge types, calculating node degrees, deriving node type features and relation features for each node, and mapping diverse node features into a common space. In this experiment, we adopt the execution time for a single epoch of the BG-HGNN model on the current dataset as the reference baseline, then we quantify the preprocessing time equivalently in terms of the number of epochs of BG-HGNN. For the sake of providing a more intuitive comparison, we chose to contrast the preprocessing time with the duration of a single epoch in the Heterogeneous Graph Transformer (HGT) model.

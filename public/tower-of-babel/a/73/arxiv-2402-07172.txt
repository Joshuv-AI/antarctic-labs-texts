Abstract

This paper reviews the results of existence and uniqueness of the solutions of these equations: the Korteweg-de Vries equation, the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, the generalized Korteweg-de Vries-Kuramoto-Sivashinski equation and the non homogeneous boundary value problem for KdV-KS equation in quarter plane.

. 35G25, 35K55.

Keywords
. Existence, Uniqueness, Stability, The modified generalized Korteweg-de Vries-Kuramto-Sivashinsky equation, non linear initial value problems, Cauchy problem.

Remark 1.7

\rhd_{1}
The Sobolev spaces
[[35]]
were introduced mostly for the use of the theory of partial differential equations. Differential operators are often closable in such spaces. Of course, Sobolev spaces being examples of Banach or, sometimes, Hilbert spaces are interesting object for themselves. But their importance is connected with the fact that the theory of partial differential equations can be, and even most easily, developed just in such a spaces. The reason is because partial differential operators are very well situated in Sobolev spaces.. The spaces of continuous (or of class
C^{k}
) functions is not very suitable for the studies of partial differential equations.

Why spaces of (only) continuous functions are not very suitable? The answer is connected with the following observation
[[29]]

Namely, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
the Laplace operator

\displaystyle{\Delta;C^{k+2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow C^{k}(\Omega);u=0\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega}

(1.10)

is continuous, but its image is not closed in
C^{k}(\Omega)
. In particular, for continuous right hand side
f\in C^{k}(\Omega)
the solution of the equation:

\displaystyle{\Delta u=f,\quad u=0\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega}

(1.11)

in general must not be a
C^{2}(\Omega)
function. Similar situation we face for other elliptic operators.

\rhd_{2}
Consider now a partial differential operator with constant coefficients
a_{\alpha}
:

\displaystyle{P(D)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}}

(1.12)

It can be considered as an operator from
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
into itself, or as an operator from
C^{k}(\Omega)
into
C^{k-m}(\Omega)
, for any
m\leq k
. But we prefer to define it as:

\displaystyle{P(Du)(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}D^{\alpha}u(x)}

(1.13)

on the linear subspace
\mathfrak{C}^{m}(\Omega)
consisting of all the functions in
C^{m}(\Omega)
that have a finite norm:

\displaystyle{||u||_{W^{m,p}(\Omega)}=(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}|D^{\alpha}u|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}}

(1.14)

It is often desirable to extend
P(D)
to a closed linear operator in
L^{p}(\Omega),(1\leq p<\infty)
. It is well known that this is possible:

Theorem 1.8
.

The operator
P(D)
from
L^{p}(\Omega)\,\,\,p\in[1;\infty)
into itself with a domain
\mathfrak{C}^{m}(\Omega)
has a closure.

Proof

The following is a well known equivalent condition for an operator
T
to have a closure:

\rhd
Let
T
be a linear operator from a linear subspace
D(T)
of a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
into a Banach space
\mathbb{Y}
.
T
has a closure
\tilde{T}
if and only if the following condition is satisfied:

\displaystyle{u_{n}\in D(T),u_{n}\longrightarrow 0,Tu_{n}\longrightarrow v\,\,\text{ imply}\,\,v=0}

(1.15)

\rhd
Now, in view of the necessary condition above it suffices to show that if
\displaystyle{u_{n}\in C^{m}(\Omega);||u_{n}||_{W^{0,p}}\longrightarrow 0,||P(D)u_{n}-v||_{W^{0,p}}\longrightarrow 0}
then
v=0
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Integration by parts gives:

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}P(D)u_{n}\varphi dx={\int_{\Omega}u_{n}P(-D)}\varphi dx}

(1.16)

As
n\longrightarrow\infty
, the integrals on the right converge to zero whereas the integrals on the left converge to
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}v\varphi dx}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}v\varphi dx=0\,\,\text{for all}\,\,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)}

(1.17)

Thus, by density, we conclude that
v=0
and this result show that the Sobolev spaces are natural for the studies of differential operators.

fractional Sobolev spaces

\displaystyle{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
. In the literature, fractional Sobolev-type spaces are also called Aronszajn, Gagliardo or Slobodeckij spaces, by the name of
the ones who introduced them, almost simultaneously (see
[[4]], [[11]] , [[34]]
).

We start by fixing the fractional exponent
s
in
]0,1[
. For any
p\in[1,+\infty[
, we define
\displaystyle{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}
as follows

\displaystyle{W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega);\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{\frac{n}{p}+s}}dxdy\in L^{p}(\Omega\times\Omega)}

(1.18)

i.e., an intermediary Banach space between
L^{p}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
endowed with the natural norm :

\displaystyle{||u||_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy)^{\frac{1}{p}}}

(1.19)

where the term

\displaystyle{[u]_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}:=(\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+ps}}dxdy)^{\frac{1}{p}}}

is the so-called
Gagliardo semi-norm
of
u
.

Proposition 1.9
.

.Let
p\in[1,+\infty[
and
0<s\leq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}}<1
. Let
\Omega
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. Then

\displaystyle{||u||_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq||u||_{W^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}},p}(\Omega)}}

(1.20)

for some suitable positive constant
C=C(n,p,s)\geq 1
. In particular

\displaystyle{W^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}},p}(\Omega)\subset W^{s,p}(\Omega)}

Proof

Since
n+sp>n
then the kernel
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{|z|^{n+ps}}}
is integrable. It follows that

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\leq\int_{\Omega}(\int_{|z|\geq 1}\frac{1}{|z|^{n+ps}}dz)|u(x)|^{p}dx}

\displaystyle{\leq C(n,p,s)||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}
.

Taking into account the above estimate, it follows

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\leq 2^{p-1}\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\{|x-y|\geq 1\}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}+|u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy}

\displaystyle{\leq 2^{p}C(n,p,s)||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}}

(1.21)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\{|x-y|<1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\leq\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega\cap\{|x-y|<1\}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}}p}}dxdy}

(1.22)

Thus, combining the two last inequalities, we get

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}dxdy\leq 2^{p}C(n,p,s)||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}}p}}dxdy}

and so

\displaystyle{||u||_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq(C(n,p,s)+1)||u||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}^{p}+\int_{\Omega}\,\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}}p}}dxdy}

i.e.

\displaystyle{||u||_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}(n,p,s)||u||_{W^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}s^{{}^{\prime}}},p}(\Omega)}}

which gives the desired estimate.

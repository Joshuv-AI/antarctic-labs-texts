A restricted model for the bounded derived category of gentle algebras
Esha Gupta

Abstract
. We present a restricted model for the bounded derived category of gentle algebras that encodes the indecomposable objects and positive extensions between them. The model is then used to count the number of d d -term silting objects for linearly oriented A n A_{n} , recovering the result that they are counted by the Pfaff-Fuss-Catalan numbers.



1.
Introduction

In recent years, (graded) gentle algebras have come to gather a lot of attention because of their ubiquity in several areas of mathematics. These include cluster theory, where they appear as cluster-tilted algebras and Jacobian algebras associated to triangulations of marked surfaces
[
3
,
9
]
, as well as geometry, where they appear as endomorphism rings of formal generators of some partially wrapped Fukaya categories
[
6
]
. A geometric model for the module categories of all gentle algebras was provided in
[
4
]
, where the authors realised them as tiling algebras associated to partial triangulations of unpunctured surfaces with marked points on the boundary. A geometric model for the
m
-cluster category of type
A_{n-1}
was provided in
[
5
]
using diagonals of a regular polygon. In
[
10
]
, the authors provided a complete model for the bounded derived category of a gentle algebra, which encoded information such as the indecomposable objects, morphisms, mapping cones, Auslander-Reiten translation for perfect objects, and Auslander–Reiten triangles. A model for studying support
\tau
-tilting modules, or equivalently
2
-term silting complexes, of all locally gentle algebras was provided in
[
11
]
. For an acyclic quiver
Q
, it was shown in
[
8
, Theorem 5.14]
that the hearts of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{mod}kQ)
lying between the canonical heart
\mathcal{H}_{Q}=\operatorname{mod}kQ
and
\mathcal{H}_{Q}[d-1]
are in bijection with basic
d
-term silting complexes as well as
d
-cluster tilting sets.

In this work, we modify the model of
[
10
]
to give a model for the bounded derived category of a gentle algebra that encodes the indecomposable objects and their positive extensions as (ungraded) arcs on a surface and their intersections (Proposition
3.11
). We can then restrict this model to study the truncated homotopy categories
\mathrm{K}^{[-d+1,0]}(\operatorname{proj}\Lambda)
of
d
-term complexes of projectives for a gentle algebra
\Lambda
. Since the model encodes positive extensions, it is particularly suited for the study of
d
-term silting complexes generalizing the case of
2
-term silting complexes studied in
[
11
]
(Corollary
3.15
, Remark
3.17
). Applying the model to the case of linearly oriented
A_{n}
quiver, we recover the result of
[
12
]
that the number of
d
-term silting complexes in
\mathrm{K}^{b}(\operatorname{proj}kA_{n})
is given by the Pfaff-Fuss-Catalan number
C^{d}_{n+1}
(§
4
).

Marked surfaces and admissible dissections

Throughout this work,

Definition 2.1

[
2

A

is a triple
(S,M,P)
, where

(1)

S
is an oriented closed smooth surface with non-empty boundary
\partial S
;

(2)

\partial S
. The elements of
M_{\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ}
and

\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
and
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet

\partial S

(3)

is a finite set of marked points in the interior of
S
, called punctures. The elements of
P_{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet}
will also be represented by
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet

Definition 2.2

[
2
, Definition 1.8]

A
\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
-arc
(or
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet
-arc

\gamma

[0,1]
to

such that

\gamma(0)

We will usually consider arcs up to homotopy and inverses. Two arcs are said to

if any choice of homotopic representatives intersect.

Definition 2.3

[
2
, Definition 1.9]

A collection of pairwise non-intersecting and pairwise different
\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
-arcs
\{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{r}\}

\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
-dissection

The notion of admissible
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet
-dissection is defined similarly.

To any admissible
\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
-dissection, we can associate a dual
\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\bullet
-dissection in the following sense.

Proposition 2.4

[
2

Let
(S,M,P)
be a marked surface, and let

be an admissible
\color[rgb]{0,0.5,0.5}\circ
-dissection. There exists a unique admissible

Definition 2.5

[

The dissections

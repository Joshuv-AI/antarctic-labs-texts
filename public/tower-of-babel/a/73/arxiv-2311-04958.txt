Moiré Fractional Chern Insulators I: First-principles calculations and Continuum Models of Twisted Bilayer MoTe 2
Yujin Jia, Jiabin Yu, Jiaxuan Liu, Jonah Herzog-Arbeitman, Ziyue Qi, Nicolas Regnault, Hongming Weng, B. Andrei Bernevig, Quansheng Wu

Abstract
Recent experiments observed fractional Chern insulators (FCI) in twisted bilayer MoTe 2 at zero magnetic field, yet even the single-particle model of this material is controversial, leading to unreliable predictions of the experimental phase diagram as discussed in [Yu et al., 2023]. In this light, we revisit the single-particle model of twisted bilayer MoTe 2 . Utilizing large-scale density functional theory, we calculate the band structure of twisted AA-stacked bilayer MoTe 2 at various twist angles relevant to experiment. We find that a band inversion occurs near 4.41 ∘ 4.41^{\circ} between the second and third bands. Our ab initio band structure is in qualitative agreement with [Wang et al., 2023], but shows important differences in the remote bands and in the Γ \Gamma valley. We incorporate two higher harmonic terms into the continuum model to capture the highest 3 valence bands per valley. We confirm that the two highest valence bands per valley have opposite Chern numbers with | C | = 1 |C|=1 for small angles, and also use our model to predict a variety of Chern states in the remote bands accessible by displacement field. Finally, we perform DFT calculations and build models for the AB stacking configuration. Our work serves as a foundation for accurate determination of the correlated phases in twisted bilayer MoTe 2 .

IV
Conclusions

Our extensive DFT study, accelerated by machine learning, has confirmed that, and our continuum model analysis show that the lowest two bands in the
K
valley have Chern numbers
+1
and
-1
respectively, at a twist angle of
3.89^{\circ}
for the experimentally relevant AA stacking. These Chern numbers mod 3 are accessible directly from the DFT data, and we compute the exact value from the continuum model fit to the DFT bands. The Chern numbers are consistent with Ref.
Wang et al. 2023
but do not agree with those computed by Ref.
Reddy et al. 2023
at larger twist angles and extrapolated to
3.89^{\circ}
.

Unlike the fitting in Ref.
Reddy et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2023
which captures the top two valence bands per valley, we succeeded in matching the top three valence bands (as well as much of the fourth band) per valley by adding only two higher harmonic terms to the moiré Hamiltonian (see Eq. (
2
)). This Hamiltonian still preserves the effective intra-valley inversion symmetry, and will serve as a faithful model of the dispersion and topology for a wide range of electron fillings. Excitingly, our model predicts a rich topological phase diagram accessible through displacement fields in the remote bands. Our forthcoming work will study the many-body physics of this model, contributing to the study of correlations and topology

A.2

Since the relaxation will greatly affect the band structure, it is necessary to perform relaxation on

2

We decided to construct a Machine Learning Force Field (MLFF) to get relaxed structures in an efficient way. MLFF is a machine learning algorithm that will “learn” energies and forces of atoms from ab-initio calculation and can be applied to predict forces and energies for similar systems.
We note that MLFF is not a relaxed structure—it is a function that maps structures to forces/energies; it can be used to efficiently generate the relaxed structures.
We firstly constructed a MLFF and applied it to get an MLFF-relaxed structure. Then, we performed DFT relaxation on the MLFF-relaxed structures. It only took around 20 DFT steps in relaxing the largest moire structure to converge in this strategy. In comparison, the relaxation from rigid 3.89

A.2.1

ab-initio
data needed for training a high-precision MLFF.
The VASP MLFF module itself is a way to generate MLFF and accelerate the Molecular Dynamics (MD) simulation.
It firstly runs several
ab initio
MD steps, collects all the energies, forces and structure data into a dataset, then trains a MLFF, and finally estimates the error by doing MLFF-based MD calculations.
Here the MLFF-based MD calculation is based on Bayesian linear regression, and the error is directly estimated by the spread of the Gaussian distribution.
If the estimated error of MLFF is large, an additional
ab initio
MD step will be performed to enlarge dataset, the MLFF is retrained with the new dataset, and estimate the error again.
The procedure will be repeated until the total number of MD steps exceeds the preset.

However, the MLFF algorithm in VASP is lightweight. It helps accelerate the MD simulation, but the MLFF generated by the algorithm is not accurate enough.
Therefore, we will not directly use the generated MLFF; instead, we just run the VASP MLFF module for tens of thousands of steps to generate a set of
ab-initio
MD data.

With the set of
ab-initio
MD data generated from the VASP MLFF module, we use the NequIP software to train an accurate MLFF. NequIP is based on an E(3) equivariant neural network, meaning that the input and output of each neural network layer are equivariant (in other words, covariant) under the rotation, reflection and translation in 3D space. The NequIP software is reported to outperform several other MLFF algorithms in both data efficiency and accuracy
Batzner et al. 2022
.

The training process is summarized as the follows.
We started from small supercells of untwisted AA and AB bilayer MoTe
2
with different in-plane shift between top and bottom layer. We ran MD simulation using VASP MLFF module on those structures and collected the data from all ab-initio MD steps. We merged all the collected data and trained a NequIP MLFF, which is then used in Atomic Simulation Environment
Larsen et al. 2017
to relax the
t
MoTe
2
at various angles.

(39)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})=\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m_{\Gamma}^{*}}s_{0}+V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})\end{split}

(40)

since a linear Rashba-like kinetic term is forbidden by
C_{3}
,
\mathcal{T}
, and
M_{z}
symmetry in the monolayer. The effective mass
m_{\Gamma}^{*}
is the same in both layers because of the
C_{2y}
symmetry.
We note that the value of
m_{\Gamma}^{*}
in Eq. (
40
) might not be equal to that for monolayer
MoTe
2
due to the inter-layer coupling renormalizing the bands, due to the fact that the bands near
\Gamma
in the monolayer is quite flat as shown in Fig.
11
(a). The potentials are periodic and can be expanded as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{G}}V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{G}\cdot\bm{r}}\\
&t_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{G}}t_{\Gamma,\bm{G}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{G}\cdot\bm{r}}\end{split}

(41)

whose components are restricted by symmetry to obey

\displaystyle\begin{split}&e^{-\mathrm{i}s_{z}\frac{\pi}{3}}V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}e^{\mathrm{i}s_{z}\frac{\pi}{3}}=V_{\Gamma,l,C_{3}\bm{G}}\ ,\ e^{-\mathrm{i}s_{z}\frac{\pi}{3}}t_{\Gamma,\bm{G}}e^{\mathrm{i}s_{z}\frac{\pi}{3}}=t_{\Gamma,C_{3}\bm{G}}\\
&V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}^{*}=V_{\Gamma,\bar{l},-C_{2y}\bm{G}}\ ,\ t_{\Gamma,\bm{G}}^{*}=t^{\dagger}_{\Gamma,-C_{2y}\bm{G}}\\
&s_{y}V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}^{*}s_{y}=V_{\Gamma,l,-\bm{G}}\ ,\ s_{y}t_{\Gamma,\bm{G}}^{*}s_{y}=t_{\Gamma,-\bm{G}}\\
&V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}^{\dagger}=V_{\Gamma,l,-\bm{G}}\ .\end{split}

(42)

To capture the dominant contribution, we only include the first harmonics in
V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})
, and the zeroth harmonic in the
t_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})
,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})=\sum_{\bm{G}\in\{\pm\bm{g}_{i}|i=1,2,3\}}V_{\Gamma,l,\bm{G}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{G}\cdot\bm{r}}\\
&t_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})=t_{\Gamma,0}\ .\end{split}

(43)

We neglect the zeroth harmonic in
V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})
since it is just a total shift of energy (although it must be included to compare the energies of the in-active
\Gamma
valley and active
\pm\text{K}
valleys).
According to Eq. (
42
), the first harmonic terms in
V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})
can be written

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V_{\Gamma,b,\pm\bm{g}_{i}}=V_{\Gamma}e^{\pm\mathrm{i}\psi_{\Gamma}}s_{0}\\
&V_{\Gamma,t,\pm\bm{g}_{i}}=V_{\Gamma}e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\psi_{\Gamma}}s_{0}\ ,\end{split}

(44)

and similarly,
t_{\Gamma,0}
has the form

t_{\Gamma,0}=w_{\Gamma}e^{\mathrm{i}s_{z}\phi_{\Gamma}}\ .

(45)

Here
V_{\Gamma}
,
\psi_{\Gamma}
,
w_{\Gamma}
and
\phi_{\Gamma}
are real. We set
\phi_{\Gamma}=0
as a gauge choice of the relative phase between the layers. As a result, Eq. (
43
) is further simplified to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{3}V_{\Gamma}e^{\mathrm{i}(-)^{l}\psi_{\Gamma}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}V_{\Gamma}e^{-\mathrm{i}(-)^{l}\psi_{\Gamma}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}\\
&t_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})=w_{\Gamma}s_{0}\ .\end{split}

(46)

With the simplification in Eq. (
40
) and Eq. (
46
), the
\Gamma
-valley continuum model in Eq. (
36
) has effective inversion symmetry,
i.e.
,

\mathcal{I}\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},l}\mathcal{I}^{-1}=\psi^{\dagger}_{-\bm{r},\bar{l}}\ .

(47)

Similar to the
\pm\text{K}
-valley case, adding displacement breaks the effective inversion symmetry, as well as the
C_{2y}
symmetry, where the displacement field term reads

H_{\Gamma,\varepsilon}=\int d^{2}r\sum_{l}\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},l}\psi_{\bm{r},l}(-)^{l}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ .

(48)

Including the first harmonics in
t_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})
and the higher harmonics in
V_{\Gamma,l}(\bm{r})
may also break the effective inversion symmetry.

Single-Particle Continuum Model and Fitting: AB-Stacking (2H-Stacking)

Replacing
C_{2y}
by
C_{2x}
in the generators of the symmetry group of

t
MoTe

t
MoTe
2

t
MoTe
2
by another
180^{\circ}

C_{2x}
is local to the monolayer
K
point (unlike
C_{2y}
), it preserves the valley quantum in the moiré model.

Different from the
AA-
t
MoTe
2

AB-
t
MoTe
2
come from both the
\pm\text{K}
valleys and the
\Gamma
valleys.
Therefore, in the following, we will review the continuum model for the
\pm\text{K}
valleys proposed in Ref.
Wu et al. 2019
, and will also discuss the continuum model for the
\Gamma
valley.

C.1
AB-Stacking:
\pm\text{K}
Valleys

In this part, we review the continuum model of
AB-
t
MoTe
2
for the
\pm\text{K}
valleys proposed in Ref.
Wu et al. 2019
.
The basis of the continuum model is still labelled by
c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}
with
\eta=\pm
labeling the
\pm\text{K}
valleys (or equivalently spins),
l=t,b
labels the layer, and
\bm{r}
labels the position.
Since
AB-
t
MoTe
2
is given by rotating the top layer of
AA-
t
MoTe
2
by an extra
180^{\circ}
, the two layers in one valley now have opposite spin.
Then, the symmetry reps furnished by
c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}
now read

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{3}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}C_{3}^{-1}=c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,C_{3}\bm{r}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta(-)^{l}\frac{\pi}{3}}\\
&C_{2x}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}C_{2x}^{-1}=c^{\dagger}_{\eta,\bar{l},C_{2x}\bm{r}}(-\mathrm{i})\\
&\mathcal{T}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}\mathcal{T}^{-1}=c^{\dagger}_{-\eta,l,\bm{r}}(-\eta)\\
&T_{\bm{R}_{M}}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}T_{\bm{R}_{M}}^{-1}=c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}+\bm{R}_{M}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta\bm{R}_{M}\cdot\text{K}_{l}}\ ,\end{split}

(49)

where
\bar{l}=b,t
for
l=t,b
respectively,
\text{K}_{l}
is the rotated
K
point of the
l
th layer, and
(-)^{t}=-(-)^{b}=1
.

The general form of the single-particle Hamiltonian in the
\eta
valley reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{\eta,0}^{AB}&=\sum_{l}\int d^{2}rc^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}\left[\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m^{*}}+V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})\right]c_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}\\
&\ +\left[\int d^{2}rc^{\dagger}_{\eta,b,\bm{r}}t_{\eta}(\bm{r})c_{\eta,t,\bm{r}}+h.c.\right]\ ,\end{split}

(50)

where
V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})
is real, and we have used the fact that the dispersion around
\pm\text{K}
in the monolayer MoTe
2
is quadratic and the fact that
m^{*}
is the same for two valleys and two layers owing to
C_{2x}
and
\mathcal{T}
symmetries.
The symmetry requirements on
V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})
and
t_{\eta}(\bm{r})
read

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C_{3}:\ V_{\eta,l}(C_{3}\bm{r})=V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})\ ,\ t_{\eta}(C_{3}\bm{r})=t_{\eta}(\bm{r})e^{-\mathrm{i}\eta\frac{2\pi}{3}}\\
&C_{2x}:\ V_{\eta,l}(C_{2x}\bm{r})=V_{\eta,\bar{l}}(\bm{r})\ ,\ t_{\eta}^{\dagger}(C_{2x}\bm{r})=t_{\eta}(\bm{r})\\
&\mathcal{T}:\ V_{-\eta,l}(\bm{r})=V_{\eta,l}^{*}(\bm{r})\ ,\ t_{-\eta}(\bm{r})=t_{\eta}^{*}(\bm{r})\\
&T_{\bm{R}_{M}}:V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r}+\bm{R}_{M})=V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})\\
&\qquad\ \ t_{\eta}(\bm{r}+\bm{R}_{M})=t_{\eta}(\bm{r})e^{\mathrm{i}\eta\bm{q}_{1}\cdot\bm{R}_{M}}\end{split}

(51)

By keeping the first harmonics, the symmetry requirements lead to

\displaystyle

\displaystyle V_{\eta,l}(\bm{r})=Ve^{-\mathrm{i}\psi}\sum_{i=1,2,3}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}+Ve^{\mathrm{i}\psi}\sum_{i=1,2,3}e^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}

(52)

\displaystyle t_{\eta}(\bm{r})=w\sum_{i=1,2,3}e^{\eta\mathrm{i}(i-1)\frac{2\pi}{3}}e^{-\eta\mathrm{i}\bm{q}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}\ .

As can be checked from the expressions above, this lowest-harmonics model has effective anti-unitary symmetry

\mathcal{K}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}\mathcal{K}^{-1}=c^{\dagger}_{\eta,\bar{l},\bm{r}}

(53)

which flips the layers and acts like time-reversal. This symmetry can be anticipated since the opposite layers have opposite spins in the twisted AB stacking.
Adding displacement field would induce an energy difference between the two layers,
i.e.
,

H_{\eta,\varepsilon}=\int d^{2}r\sum_{l}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}c_{\eta,l,\bm{r}}(-)^{l}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ ,

(54)

which breaks the effective TR symmetry, as well as the
C_{2x}
symmetry.
Including higher harmonics for the intralayer and interlatyer terms may break the effective TR symmetry.

Similar to the discussion in Appendix.
B.1
, we can express the Hamiltonian in the momentum space based on the Fourier transformation in Eq. (
30
), leading to

H_{\eta,0}^{AB}=\sum_{\bm{k}}\sum_{\bm{Q},\bm{Q}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}}c^{\dagger}_{\eta,\bm{k},\bm{Q}}h_{\eta,\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\prime}}^{AB}(\bm{k})c_{\eta,\bm{k},\bm{Q}^{\prime}}\ ,

(55)

where
c^{\dagger}_{\eta,\bm{k},\bm{Q}}
is defined in Eq. (
33
), and
\mathcal{Q}
is defined right below Eq. (
32
).
Specifically,
h_{\eta,\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\prime}}^{AB}(\bm{k})
reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\eta,\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\prime}}^{AB}(\bm{k})&=\delta_{\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\prime}}\left(\frac{-\hbar^{2}\left(\bm{k}-\bm{Q}\right)^{2}}{2m^{*}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\eta(-)^{\bm{Q}}\right)\\
&\quad+V\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{\alpha=\pm}\left[e^{-\alpha\mathrm{i}\psi}\delta_{\bm{Q}+\alpha\bm{g}_{i},\bm{Q}^{\prime}}\right]\\
&\quad+w\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{\alpha=\pm}\left[e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha\frac{2\pi(i-1)}{3}}\delta_{\bm{Q},\bm{Q}^{\prime}+\alpha\bm{q}_{i}}\right]\ ,\end{split}

(56)

where we have included the displacement field, and
w
is chosen to be real by picking the relative phase between the two layers, and
(-)^{\bm{Q}}=\pm 1
for
\bm{Q}\in\{\bm{G}_{M}\pm\bm{q}_{1}\}
.
We can see that without the displacement field,
h_{\eta,\bm{Q}\bm{Q}^{\prime}}^{AB}(\bm{k})
is the same for two valleys due to the effective time-reversal symmetry.

\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},l}=(\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},l,\uparrow},\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},l,\downarrow})\ ,

(60)

t
and
b

With the simplification in Eq. (

valley has the same bands as the
+\text{K}
valley, leading to at least double degeneracy of each band from the
\pm\text{K}
-valley continuum model.
Along the
\Gamma_{M}-M_{M}
line, the bands from the
\pm\text{K}
-valley continuum model have 4-fold degeneracy because we set the interlayer coupling to be zero (
w=0
), which is justified by the nearly 4-fold degeneracy for the top 4 DFT valence bands along
\Gamma_{M}-M_{M}
, as shown in Fig.
4
.
The small
w=0
can be understood as the follows.
The two layers in one valley now have opposite spin; owing to the spin U(1) symmetry for the low-energy states near
\pm\text{K}
valleys in monolayer
MoTe
2
, we expect the spin
U(1)
symmetry is approximately preserved in
t
MoTe
2
, which means the interlayer coupling is very small for the AB-stacking.
The zero interlayer coupling makes the eigenstates have well-defined valley and layer/spin.
As a result, the two states with the same spin are degenerate at
\text{K}_{M}
, since the combination of the effective TR symmetry and the TR symmetry leaves the spin invariant.
This is consistent with the DFT result in Fig.
19
.
In Fig.
22

C_{3}
eigenvalues of the bands from the
\pm\text{K}
-valley model, which is consistent with Fig.

atomic limit, representing an atomic

C.3.2
\Gamma
-valley

In our fitting, we choose the values of
m_{\Gamma}^{*}
to be the effective mass of the top valence band for the untwisted AB-stacking bilayer structure around
\Gamma
in Fig.
11
. (There is some anisotropy, so in practice we round the effective mass to
10m_{e}
. The resulting bands show little dependence on the precise value of the mass.)
Although the value of effective mass around
K
is similar for the monolayer
MoTe
2
and the untwisted AB-stacking bilayer
MoTe
2
, it differs a lot around
\Gamma
as shown in Fig.
11
.
The change of the
\Gamma
effective mass from the monolayer
MoTe
2
to the untwisted AB-stacking bilayer
MoTe
2
comes from the interlayer coupling between the monolayer conduction and valence bands.
This effect can only be taken into account in the
\Gamma
-valley continuum model by changing the value of
m^{*}
, since the the monolayer conduction bands are not explicitly included in the
\Gamma
-valley continuum model.
Therefore, we do not take the values of
m_{\Gamma}^{*}
from the monolayer; instead, we round it to the
\Gamma
effective mass in the untwisted AB-stacking bilayer
MoTe
2
.
Furthermore, we determine the interlayer coupling
w_{\Gamma}
for the
\Gamma
-valley model based on the interlayer coupling at
\Gamma
for the untwisted AB-stacking bilayer structure, as shown in Fig.
11
.

The
\Gamma
-valley continuum model in Eq. (
58
) preserves spin
\mathrm{U}(1)
.
Owing to the effective TR symmetry Eq. (
53
) and the TR symmetry, the spin-down bands are the same as the spin-up bands, leading to at least double degeneracy of each band from the
\Gamma
-valley continuum model.
As shown in Fig.

-valley model are consistent with the DFT calculation shown in Fig.

The
\Gamma
-valley bands are extremely flat as shown in Fig.
4
.
To understand it, first we note that the interlayer coupling
w_{\Gamma}
is very large (shown in Tab.
2
), allowing us to project the model into
\psi^{\dagger}_{\bm{r},s}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\psi^{\dagger}_{t,\bm{r},s}+\psi^{\dagger}_{b,\bm{r},s})
basis.
(Note that we have rotated the relative phase between
\psi^{\dagger}_{t,\bm{r},s}
and
\psi^{\dagger}_{b,\bm{r},s}
to make
w_{\Gamma}
positive.)
Then, the
\Gamma
-valley continuum model in Eq. (
59
) with zero displacement field becomes

\tilde{h}^{AB}(\bm{r})=\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m_{\Gamma}^{*}}+w_{\Gamma}+E_{\Gamma}+V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})\ ,

(67)

where the spin degeneracy is implicit, and

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})&=\sum_{i=1}^{3}V_{\Gamma}e^{\mathrm{i}\psi_{\Gamma}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}V_{\Gamma}e^{-\mathrm{i}\psi_{\Gamma}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}}\\
&=2V_{\Gamma}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\cos(\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r}+\psi_{\Gamma})\ .\end{split}

(68)

For convenience of the discussion, let us consider the hole Hamiltonian

-\tilde{h}^{AB}(\bm{r})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m_{\Gamma}^{*}}-w_{\Gamma}-E_{\Gamma}-V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})\ ;

(69)

the highest bands of
\tilde{h}^{AB}(\bm{r})
are lowest bands of
-\tilde{h}^{AB}(\bm{r})
.
With
\psi=0
and
V_{\Gamma}>0
in Tab.
2
, the minimum of the potential
-V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})
is at the moiré lattice points
\bm{r}=\bm{R}_{M}
, which has value
-V_{\Gamma}(\bm{R}_{M})=-6V_{\Gamma}
.
To go from one moiré lattice point
\bm{R}_{M}
to its nearest neighbors
\bm{R}_{M}
to
\bm{R}_{M}+\bm{a}_{M,1}
, the minimum energy barrier is
8V_{\Gamma}=576
meV.
The energy barrier is much larger than the kinetic energy term estimated for
m^{*}_{\Gamma}=10m_{e}
:
\frac{\hbar^{2}|\bm{q}_{1}|^{2}}{2m^{*}_{\Gamma}}=2.49
meV.
As a result, the lowest-energy states of
-\tilde{h}^{AB}(\bm{r})
are bounded around
\bm{R}_{M}
; then we can expand
-V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})
around
\bm{R}_{M}
to the second order of
\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{M}
(the first order of
\bm{r}-\bm{R}_{M}
is forbidden by the
C_{3}
symmetry):

\displaystyle\begin{split}-V_{\Gamma}(\bm{r})&=V_{\Gamma}\sum_{i}(\bm{g}_{i}\cdot\bm{r})^{2}+O(|\bm{r}|^{4})\\
&=\frac{3}{2}V_{\Gamma}|b_{M,1}|^{2}\left|\bm{r}\right|^{2}+O(|\bm{r}|^{4})\ .\end{split}

(70)

Then, the effective Hamiltonian for those lowest-energy states can be approximated by an array of decoupled harmonic oscillators centered around the
\bm{R}_{M}
.
Harmonic oscillators centered around different
\bm{R}_{M}
’s are related by the moiré lattice translations, and thus have the same spectrum.
As a result, we have a set of flat bands with energies given by solving

h_{\Gamma,harmonic}^{AB}(\bm{r})=-\frac{\hbar^{2}\nabla^{2}}{2m_{\Gamma}^{*}}+w_{\Gamma}-E_{\Gamma}+\frac{3}{2}V_{\Gamma}|b_{M,1}|^{2}\left|\bm{r}\right|^{2}

(71)

which yields

E_{\Gamma,harmonic}^{AB}(n_{x},n_{y})=w_{\Gamma}E_{\Gamma}-\hbar\omega-(n_{x}+n_{y})\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\ ,

(72)

where
n_{x},n_{y}=0,1,2,3,...
and

\hbar\omega=\hbar\sqrt{\frac{3V_{\Gamma}|b_{M,1}|^{2}}{m_{\Gamma}^{*}}}=56.76\text{meV}\ .

(73)

This spectrum tells us (i) after including spin, the highest valence band
(n_{x},n_{y})=(0,0)
is doubly degenerate and the second highest valence band
(n_{x},n_{y})=(1,0),(0,1)
is 4-fold degenerate, (ii) the highest and second valence bands have spinless
C_{3}
eigenvalues
1
and
e^{\pm\mathrm{i}2\pi/3}
, respectively, and (iii) the energy difference between the highest valence band and the second highest valence is
\hbar\omega=56.76\text{meV}
.
All of the features are consistent with the DFT calculation.
In Fig.
21
(b), the flat bands from the
\Gamma
valley around
-30
meV and
-86
meV have near 2-fold degeneracy (spinless
C_{3}
eigenvalue
1
) and near 4-fold degeneracy (spinless
C_{3}
eigenvalue
e^{\pm\mathrm{i}2\pi/3}
), and the energy difference between them is

At last, we discuss the effect of the displacement field.
As shown in Fig.

, the effect of the displacement field on the
\pm K

2

C.4
AB-Stacking Electron Bands

For completeness, we also briefly discuss the conduction bands although they are not the focus of this work. The Fermi surface of the electron bands in the untwisted structure is more complex than the valence bands (see Fig.
11
), exhibiting an electron-like pocket along the
\Gamma\text{K}
line which is below the
\pm\text{K}
-valley states in both bilayer configurations. Let us denote the center of the pocket by
s\mathbf{K}
,
s\sim.7
, along with its
C_{3}
- and
\mathcal{T}
-related partners. Thus in total there are 6 pockets in the untwisted BZ. Fig.
11
shows that there are only two low-energy states (spin
\uparrow
and spin
\downarrow
) at
s\mathbf{K}
, unlike the four total states (spin
\uparrow
and spin
\downarrow
in both layers) at
\mathbf{K}
. This can be understood from the simple model
\frac{\mathbf{k}^{2}}{2m_{p}}\tau_{0}-w_{p}\tau_{1}
where
\tau
is a layer Pauli matrix,
\mathbf{k}
is the momentum measured from the Fermi pockets with mass
m_{p}
, and
w_{p}
is the effective inter-layer coupling, which splits the two layers into bonding/anti-bonding states with energy
\mathbf{k}^{2}/2m_{p}\mp w_{p}
.

Comparing the monolayer and bilayer band structures in Fig.
11
, we estimate
w_{p}\sim 200
meV. Since this is a large hybridization that removes the anti-bonding state from the low-energy bands, an effective moiré model on the bonding state is

\displaystyle H_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-i\nabla+\mathbf{p}_{i})^{2}}{2m_{p}}+V_{p}(\mathbf{r})

(74)

where
V_{p}(\mathbf{r})
is a moiré-periodic potential and
\mathbf{p}_{j}=R(\frac{2\pi j}{6})s\mathbf{K}
is the center of the pocket. Note that the momentum-space origin of
H_{\mathbf{p}_{i}}
,
\mathbf{p}_{i}
mod
\mathbf{G}_{M}
, depends sensitively on the value of
s
and
\theta
and is generically off the moiré high-symmetry points. The resulting shifting and folding of the
\mathbf{p}_{i}
pocket onto the moiré BZ explains the non-uniform evolution of the conduction bands in Fig.
13
and
18
as a function of twist angle. In comparison, the valence bands which originate at the K points converge smoothly.

We show band structures at
\theta=4.41^{\circ},3.89^{\circ}
in Fig.
26
using
s=.68
as an example to illustrate the strong effect of
\mathbf{p}_{i}
. Here we chose similar parameters to the
K
-valley model in Tab.
2
, namely
m_{p}=.62m_{e}
,
V=26.5
meV (half the
K
valley value), and
\psi=-52^{\circ}
. We see that Fig.
26
qualitatively matches the lowest bands in Fig.
18
. Since the only symmetry preserved at
\mathbf{p}_{i}
is
C_{2x}
, symmetry does not strongly constrain the form of Eq.
74
. Nevertheless, the minimal model here successfully reproduces many features of the bands.

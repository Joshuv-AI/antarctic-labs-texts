Introduction

This paper concerns polynomial representations of the general linear group
GL_{n}
over the integers. For a partition
\lambda

\Delta(\lambda)
denote the Weyl module of
GL_{n}
of highest weight
\lambda

yields the

space between the corresponding modular Weyl modules of

, where

is an algebraically closed field of characteristic

, and the dimensions of the higher modular extensions may be obtained through torsion and restriction of integral extensions. Jantzen’s sum formula can be viewed and proved via integral extension groups
[
4
]
.

There are not many cases where explicit computations of integral extension groups between Weyl modules have been carried out. In
[
2
]
the

case was treated and in
[
5
]
the

case when
\lambda
and
\mu
differ by a multiple of a single root, both for
i=1
. In
[
1
]
the case
\lambda=(1^{a}),\mu=(a)
was studied and in
[
15
]
the situation where
\lambda,\mu
are hooks differing by a single root was considered. In
[
14
]
the case where
\lambda,\mu
are any partitions differing by a single root was settled. As the modular extension groups are intimately related to the integral ones, we mention the result on neighboring Weyl modules
[
11
]
Part II Section 7, and the
SL_{2}
result in
[
16
]
for all
i
generalizing
[
9
]
and
[
7
]
. More can be found in
[
8
]
.

Let
\mathrm{h},\mathrm{h}(k)
be hooks,
\mathrm{h}=(a,1^{b}),\mathrm{h}(k)=(a+k,1^{b-k})
, where

is an integer such that
1\leq k\leq b
. It follows that
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(\mathrm{h})),\Delta(\mathrm{h}(k)))=0
if

. In this paper we determine

2.1
Notation

Let

F
yields an identification of general linear groups
GL(F)=GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

of degree

S_{\mathbb{Z}}(n,r)

[
10
]
, Section 2.4. We will write

in place of
S_{\mathbb{Z}}(n,r)

By
DF=\sum_{i\geq 0}D_{i}F
and
\wedge F=\sum_{i\geq 0}\wedge^{i}F

we denote the divided power algebra of
F
and the exterior algebra of
F
respectively. We will usually omit

and write

and
\wedge^{i}

From
[
10
]
or
[
2
]
, Proposition 2.1, we recall that for each sequence

of non negative integers

that sum to

-module
D_{a_{1}}\otimes...\otimes D_{a_{n}}
is projective. Throughout this paper all tensor products are over the integers.

For a partition
\lambda
of

with at most
n
parts, we denote by

the corresponding Weyl module for
S(n,r)
. If
\lambda=(a)
is a partition with one part, then
\Delta(\lambda)=D_{a}
, and if
\lambda=(1^{b})
, then

. A hook

is a partition of the form

. The following complex of

-modules (which is the dual of the usual Koszul complex) is exact

where

is the composition

\ D_{a+1}\otimes\wedge^{b-1}\xrightarrow{\triangle\otimes 1}D_{a}\otimes D_{1}\otimes\wedge^{b-1}\xrightarrow{1\otimes m}D_{a}\otimes\wedge^{b},

where
\triangle
(respectively,

) is the indicated component of the comultiplication (resp., multiplication) map of the Hopf algebra
DF
(resp.,
\wedge F
). It is well known that if
\mathrm{h}=(a,1^{b})
is a hook,
b\geq 1
, then

\Delta(\mathrm{h})\simeq cok(\theta_{a})\simeq ker(\theta_{a-1}),

so that we have the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Delta(\mathrm{h}(1))\xrightarrow{i}D_{a}\otimes\wedge^{b}\xrightarrow{\pi_{0}}\Delta(\mathrm{h})\rightarrow 0,

(2.1)

where
\mathrm{h}(1)=(a+1,1^{b-1})
, the map
i
is induced by
\theta_{a}
on generators and the map
\pi_{0}
is induced by the identity map on generators

Notation
: Throughout this paper we use the notation
\mathrm{h}=(a,1^{b}),\mathrm{h}(k)=(a+k,1^{b-k}),1\leq k\leq b,r=a+b.

2.4
The extensions
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),D_{a+k}\otimes\wedge^{b-k})

We will use the following lemma several times. Let

r_{k}=gcd\left(\tbinom{k+1}{1},\dots,\tbinom{k+1}{k}\right)

and note that
r_{k}=1
unless
k+1=p^{e}
,
p
prime, in which case
r_{k}=p
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
n\geq{b+1}
and
1\leq k<b
. Then

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),D_{a+k}\otimes\wedge^{b-k})=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\wedge^{k+1},D_{k+1}).

In particular,
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),D_{a+k}\otimes\wedge^{b-k})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and

\mathrm{Ext}^{k}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),D_{a+k}\otimes\wedge^{b-k})=\mathbb{Z}_{r_{k}}.

Proof.

A special case of the main result, Theorem 2, of
[
13
]
yields

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(a,1^{b}),D_{a+k}\otimes\wedge^{b-k})=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(D_{a-1}\otimes\wedge^{k+1},D_{a+k}).

Applying contravariant duality
[
11
]
, p. 209, and
[
2
]
, Theorem 7.7, we have

\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(D_{a-1}\otimes\wedge^{k+1},D_{a+k})=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\wedge^{a+k},D_{k+1}\otimes\wedge^{a-1}).

Again by
[
13
]
,
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\wedge^{a+k},D_{k+1}\otimes\wedge^{a-1})=\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\wedge^{k+1},D_{k+1})
and the first equality of the lemma follows.

We have
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}(\wedge^{k+1},D_{k+1})=\mathbb{Z}_{2},
by
[
1
]
, Section 4, and
\mathrm{Ext}^{k}(\wedge^{k+1},D_{k+1})=\mathbb{Z}_{r_{k}},
according to
[
15
]
, eqn. (6) p. 2207.

Remark
.

Let
n\geq b+1
. If
i>k
, then
\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),\Delta(\mathrm{h}(k)))=0.

Proof.

We use induction on
k
. For

the result follows from the general fact that no Weyl module has non trivial self extension, see [10], B.4. Remark. Applying
\mathrm{Hom}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),-)
to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\Delta(\mathrm{h}(k+1))\rightarrow\Delta(a+k)\otimes\Lambda(b-k)\rightarrow\Delta(\mathrm{h}(k))\rightarrow 0

we obtain the exact sequence

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{i}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),\Delta(\mathrm{h}(k)))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{i+1}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),\Delta(h(k+1)))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathrm{Ext}^{i+1}(\Delta(\mathrm{h}),\Delta(a+k)\otimes\Lambda^{b-k}).

The term on the left is zero by induction. By Lemma 2.3, the term on the right is

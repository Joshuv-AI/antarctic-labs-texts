A deterministic counterexample for high dimensional L 2 ​ L ∞ L^{2}L^{\infty} Strichartz estimates for the Wave equation
Cristian Gavrus

Abstract
. In this note we discuss the question of homogeneous L 2 ​ L ∞ L^{2}L^{\infty} Strichartz estimates for the Wave equation in dimensions n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 raised by Fang and Wang and recently shown to fail by Guo, Li, Nakanishi and Yan using probability theory. We record a deterministic example for disproving this estimate.

Introduction

The question of determining the validity of the following statement

(W)

\|e^{it\left|D\right|}u\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\quad\text{holds for all }u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\text{ when }n\geq 4.

The function

(-\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta)v=0

In fact

a=2
corresponds to the Schrödinger equation.

In this note we record a proof of the failure of (
W
) using basic Fourier analysis. This argument is specific to the wave equation and does not apply to the Schrödinger case.

Remark 1.2
.

When

1.1.
Notation

In what follows
\hat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
. Operators
m(D)
, such as
e^{it\left|D\right|},e^{it\Delta}
, are defined by
\widehat{m(D)f}(\xi)=m(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)
. Here
\Delta=-\left|D\right|^{2}
. Homogeneous Sobolev spaces are defined by
\dot{H}^{s}=\left|D\right|^{-s}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
s<\frac{n}{2}
. Space-time norms are defined by

\|F\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}^{q}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\|F(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Littlewood-Paley projections
P_{\leq k}
are defined by
\widehat{P_{\leq k}h}(\xi)=\chi(\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2^{k}})\hat{h}(\xi)
, where
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\chi\geq 0
and
\chi(\eta)=1
for
\eta
near
0
.
For example,
\widehat{P_{\leq k}\delta_{0}}(\xi)=\chi(\frac{\left|\xi\right|}{2^{k}})
. Here
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta measure.

S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denotes the unit sphere.
The notation
X\lesssim Y
means
X\leq CY
for an absolute constant
0<C<\infty
. When
Y\lesssim X\lesssim Y
we denote
X\simeq Y
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Assume that (
W
) is true. Then, by duality, one also has

(W’)

\left\lVert\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\left|D\right|}\frac{1}{\left|D\right|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}F(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\|F\|_{L^{2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}

for all
F\in L^{2}_{t}L^{1}_{x}\cap L^{1}_{t}\dot{H}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}_{x}
.

Take any
f\in L^{1}_{t}\cap L^{2}_{t}
with
\|f\|_{L^{2}}=1
such that
\int_{0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\neq 0
. Consider also the function
h\in L^{1}_{x}\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}_{x}
with
\|h\|_{L^{1}_{x}}=1
which remains to be defined. Fix a direction
\omega\in S^{n-1}
and a constant
c>0
.
Applying (
W’
) with

F(t,x)\vcentcolon=f(t)h(x-ct\omega)

and using Plancherel’s theorem one obtains

\left\lVert\frac{1}{\left|\xi\right|^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\hat{h}(\xi)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\left|\xi\right|}e^{-ict\omega\cdot\xi}f(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\lesssim 1

which implies

\left\lVert{\left|\xi\right|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}}\hat{h}(\xi)\hat{f}(\left|\xi\right|+c\omega\cdot\xi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\xi}}\lesssim 1.

Consider
h=\delta_{0},\ \hat{h}\equiv 1
, or rather, since
\delta_{0}\notin L^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, take an approximation to the identity sequence
h_{k}=P_{\leq k}\delta_{0}
and pass to the limit. Here
\delta_{0}
denotes the Dirac delta measure. By the Monotone Convergence theorem one obtains

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{\left|\xi\right|^{n-1}}|\hat{f}(\left|\xi\right|+c\omega\cdot\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\lesssim 1.

Write this integral in spherical coordinates, where

\left|\xi\right|=\lambda,\quad\omega\cdot\xi=\lambda\cos\theta,\quad\theta\in[0,\pi].

This implies

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\pi}|\hat{f}(\lambda(1+c\cos\theta))|^{2}(\sin\theta)^{n-2}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\lambda\lesssim 1.

Remark 2.1
.

For
n=3
one may take
c=1
and change coordinates to
u=1+\cos\theta
to obtain

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2}|\hat{f}(\lambda u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}u\,\mathrm{d}\lambda=\int_{0}^{2}\frac{1}{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\int_{0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\lesssim 1

which gives a contradiction. One may view this as a Fourier version of the physical space construction in Remark
1.2
above, due to
[
9
]
.
\Delta

For
n\geq 4
, choosing
c=1
does not work. Instead, take
c=2
and restrict the integral to
\theta\in[\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{2\pi}{3}]
. On this interval
\sin\theta\simeq 1
. As before, one obtains

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\hat{f}(\eta)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\frac{(\sin\theta)^{n-2}}{1+2\cos\theta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta\lesssim 1.

The last integral is logarithmically divergent, being
\simeq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y
, thus obtaining a contradiction again, which concludes the proof.

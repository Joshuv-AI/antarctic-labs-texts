Abstract
A general quantum electrodynamic method for the derivation of nuclear recoil corrections in hydrogenic systems, which are exact in the nuclear charge parameter Z ​ α Z\,\alpha , is introduced. The exemplary derivation is presented for the O ⁡ ( m / M ) O(m/M) nuclear pure recoil correction to the hyperfine splitting. The obtained result is verified by comparison to the known ( Z ​ α ) 5 (Z\,\alpha)^{5} contribution.

I
Introduction

Consider a two-body system with an arbitrary nucleus and a point light fermion, such as an electron or muon.
If the nucleus can be treated as a static source of an electric potential, then
its energy levels can be obtained from the Dirac equation

\displaystyle H_{D}\,\phi=E_{D}\,\phi\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle H_{D}=\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{p}+\beta m+V_{C}

(2)

and where
V_{C}
is a Coulomb potential including the nuclear charge distribution
\rho_{C}(r)
,

\displaystyle V_{C}(r)=

\displaystyle\ -\int d^{3}r^{\prime}\,\frac{Z\,\alpha}{|\vec{r}-\vec{r}\,^{\prime}|}\,\rho_{C}(r^{\prime})\,.

(3)

The Dirac equation is valid only in the limit of the infinite nuclear mass
M
, and
there is no corresponding equation for the finite nuclear mass case.
This means that we are not able to treat exactly two-body systems with arbitrary masses
in the relativistic theory, in contrast to nonrelativistic quantum mechanics.

There are in principle two perturbative approaches that are employed for two-body systems.
The first relies on expansion in powers of
Z\,\alpha
while keeping an arbitrary mass ratio

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{m}{M},Z\,\alpha\Big)=

\displaystyle\ m+M+E^{(2)}+E^{(4)}+E^{(5)}+E^{(6)}+\ldots

(4)

where
E^{(n)}
is of the order
(Z\,\alpha)^{n}
and may sometimes contain finite powers of
\ln(Z\,\alpha)
.
In this expansion, the coefficient
E^{(2)}
is the nonrelativistic energy,

E^{(4)}
is the relativistic correction, and
E^{(n>4)}
are higher-order quantum electrodynamics (QED) and relativistic corrections.
These corrections for point arbitrary mass particles have already been obtained up to
E^{(6)}

adkins_2023
;
twobody
;
ptwobody

using the so-called nonrelativistic QED (NRQED) approach.
For the finite nuclear size case, they are also known up to
E^{(6)}
for an arbitrary mass ratio
twobody
;
ptwobody
,
with the exception of
S
-states which are known only in the nonrecoil limit
threephoton
.

In the second approach, one performs an expansion in the mass ratio
m/M
,
while keeping the parameter
Z\,\alpha
arbitrary,

\displaystyle E\Big(\frac{m}{M},Z\,\alpha\Big)=E_{D}(Z\,\alpha)+E_{\mathrm{rec}}(Z\,\alpha)+\ldots\,,

(5)

where the Dirac energy
E_{D}
is in the infinite nuclear mass limit,
E_{\mathrm{rec}}

is the first order in the mass ratio
\sim m/M
correction, while the higher-order terms are merely unknown.
This first order in mass ratio correction (for the point nucleus) was first derived Shabaev in Refs.
shabaev:85
;
shabaev:88
.
Next, it was independently rederived in Ref.
pachucki:95
,
together with the calculations of the, unknown at that time,
(Z\,\alpha)^{6}\,m^{2}/M

The compact form for this first-order recoil correction for a point nucleus was obtained in Ref.
yelkhovsky:96
.
Soon after, the direct numerical calculations were performed in Refs.
artemyev:95:pra
;
artemyev:95:jpb
.
The generalization for the finite size nucleus was achieved only recently in Ref.
fsrec
, and the formulas are
the following,

\displaystyle E_{\rm rec}=\frac{i}{M}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\ \frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]\,

\displaystyle\times G(E_{D}+\omega)\,\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]|\phi\rangle\,,

(6)

where
\phi
is an eigenstate of the Dirac-Coulomb Hamiltonian in Eq. (
2
),

\displaystyle G(E)=[E-H_{D}(1-i\epsilon)]^{-1}

(7)

is the Dirac-Coulomb Green’s function,
D_{C}^{j}(\omega)
is an operator which in coordinate representation
is given by the function

\displaystyle D_{C}^{j}(\omega,\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ -4\pi Z\alpha\,\alpha^{i}\,G_{C}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{r})\,,

(8)

where

\displaystyle G_{C}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{1}{k^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}}\biggr)\,e^{i\,\vec{k}\,\vec{r}}

(9)

is the transverse photon propagator for a point nucleus, and

\displaystyle G_{C}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,\biggl[\frac{\rho_{C}(-k^{2})}{k^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\ -\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\,\frac{\rho_{C}(\vec{k}^{2})}{\vec{k}^{2}}\biggr]\,e^{i\,\vec{k}\,\vec{r}}

(10)

for the finite-size nucleus
fsrec
, with

and

.
Equation (

II
Recoil correction in the temporal gauge

We first transform Eq. (
6
) to a different
{\cal F}_{2}
form
and introduce a notation
\int_{s}
for a symmetric integration around a pole at
\omega=0
,

\displaystyle E_{\rm rec}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{M}\int_{s}\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|\big[p^{j}(V_{C})-\omega\,D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]

\displaystyle\times G(E_{D}+\omega)\,\big[p^{j}(V_{C})+\omega\,D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]|\phi\rangle\,\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\,,

(11)

where
p^{j}(V_{C})=[p^{j}\,,\,V_{C}]
.
Let us introduce a photon propagator in the temporal gauge including the finite nuclear size
ulj
,

\displaystyle G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{k})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\rho_{C}(-k^{2})}{k^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)\,,

(12)

and analogously

\displaystyle D_{T}^{j}(\omega,\vec{r})=

\displaystyle\ -4\pi Z\alpha\,\alpha^{i}\,G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{r})\,.

(13)

The temporal gauge is a particular case of the axial gauge and is defined by the condition

G^{0\mu}_{T}=0
. The relation to the propagator in the Coulomb gauge is

\displaystyle D_{C}^{j}(\omega)=

\displaystyle\ D_{T}^{j}(\omega)+\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}\,\big[\omega+E_{D}-H_{D}\,,\,p^{j}(V_{C})\big].

(14)

Using
D_{T}
, the recoil correction
E_{\mathrm{rec}}
takes the form

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rec}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\int_{s}\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|D_{T}^{j}(\omega)\,G(E_{D}+\omega)\,D_{T}^{j}(\omega)|\phi\rangle\,.

(15)

The simplest form, called here the
{\cal F}_{3}
form, for the recoil correction is achieved when using the temporal gauge for the photon propagator.
We will use this observation when presenting the recoil correction to the hyperfine splitting.

III
Derivation of recoil correction to the binding energy

The original derivation
shabaev:85
;
shabaev:88
;
pachucki:95
of the nonperturbative formula for the recoil correction was quite complicated.
Here, we present a very much simplified derivation, which later will be used for the hyperfine splitting.

Consider the nonrelativistic kinetic energy of the nucleus

\displaystyle H_{N}=\frac{1}{2\,M}\,\big[\vec{P}-q\,\vec{A}(\vec{R})\big]^{2}\,,

(16)

where
\vec{P}=-i\vec{\nabla}_{R}
,
q=-Z\,e
, and
e
is the electron charge.
The leading recoil correction can formally be written as the expectation value

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rec}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|(\vec{P}-q\,\vec{A})^{2}|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}

(17)

on a hydrogenic state
|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}
(which is centered at the position of nucleus
\vec{R}
) in quantum electrodynamic (QED) theory.
The meaning of this expectation value is not obvious and is explained as follows.
The matrix element of an arbitrary operator
Q
on a state
\Psi
is

\displaystyle\langle\Psi|Q|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}=\frac{\langle\Psi|{\mathrm{T}}Q\,\exp[-i\int d^{4}y\,H_{I}(y)]|\Psi\rangle}{\langle\Psi|{\mathrm{T}}\exp[-i\int d^{4}y\,H_{I}(y)]|\Psi\rangle}\,,

(18)

where T denotes chronological ordering with an assumption that the time coordinate of
Q
is
t=0
,
the interaction Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{I}(y)=e\,j_{\mu}(y)A^{\mu}(y)\,,

(19)

and
|\Psi\rangle
is the bare hydrogenic state in the second quantized theory.
We keep in mind that in Eq. (
18
), for the purpose of this work,
all the electron self-energy and vacuum polarizations are being neglected.
The crucial point is the interpretation of
\vec{P}
and its action on
|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}
.
Namely, consider the representation of the fermion field
in terms of creation and annihilation operators of one-particle hydrogenic states
\phi_{s}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{+}a_{s}\phi_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{-i\,E_{s}t}+\sum_{s}^{-}b_{s}\phi_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{-i\,E_{s}t}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{+}a^{+}_{s}\phi^{+}_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{i\,E_{s}t}+\sum_{s}^{-}b^{+}_{s}\phi^{+}_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{i\,E_{s}t}\,;

(20)

for details, see Appendix B. The differentiation
\vec{\nabla}_{R}
acts on functions
\phi_{s}
and
operators
a_{s},b_{s}
, and this can be represented as

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}_{R}=

\displaystyle\ \int d^{3}r\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{r})\,\vec{\partial}_{R}\,\hat{\psi}(\vec{r})+\vec{\partial}_{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\int d^{3}r\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{r})\,\vec{\partial}_{r}\,\hat{\psi}(\vec{r})+\vec{\partial}_{R}

(21)

where
\hat{\psi}(\vec{r})\equiv\hat{\psi}(0,\vec{r})
, and
\vec{\partial}_{R}
is understood in the following sense.
The hydrogenic state
\phi_{s}
is a function of
\phi_{s}(\vec{r}-\vec{R})
of the difference in electron and nucleus position vectors,
therefore
\vec{\partial}_{R}\,\phi_{s}=-\vec{\partial}_{r}\,\phi_{s}
, and
\hat{a}_{s},\hat{b}_{s}
remain intact.
As a test, for
t=0
,

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}_{R}\hat{\psi}(0,\vec{x})=

\displaystyle\ -\int d^{3}r\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{r})\,\vec{\partial}_{r}\,\hat{\psi}(\vec{r})\;\hat{\psi}(0,\vec{x})-\vec{\partial}_{x}\hat{\psi}(0,\vec{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ 0\,,

(22)

as it should. Moreover, for an arbitrary Fock state
|\Psi\rangle
,

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}_{R}\,|\Psi\rangle=-\int d^{3}r\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{r})\,\vec{\partial}_{r}\,\hat{\psi}(\vec{r})|\Psi\rangle\,,

(23)

and this holds in particular for the vacuum state
|0\rangle
.

We can now return to the expectation value of the nuclear kinetic energy, and we split it into three parts,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rec}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|(\mathchoice{\mkern 3.0mu\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-3.0mu\displaystyle\nabla\mkern 3.0mu$}}$}\mkern-3.0mu}{\mkern 3.0mu\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-3.0mu\textstyle\nabla\mkern 3.0mu$}}$}\mkern-3.0mu}{\mkern 2.0mu\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-2.0mu\scriptstyle\nabla\mkern 2.0mu$}}$}\mkern-2.0mu}{\mkern 2.0mu\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-2.0mu\scriptscriptstyle\nabla\mkern 2.0mu$}}$}\mkern-2.0mu}_{R}+i\,q\,\vec{A})(\vec{\nabla}_{R}-i\,q\,\vec{A})|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ E_{C}+E_{T}+E_{S}\,,

(24)

where the quadratic derivative is understood in the symmetric form, namely

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}_{R}^{2}=-\mathchoice{\mkern 3.0mu\reflectbox{$\displaystyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-3.0mu\displaystyle\nabla\mkern 3.0mu$}}$}\mkern-3.0mu}{\mkern 3.0mu\reflectbox{$\textstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-3.0mu\textstyle\nabla\mkern 3.0mu$}}$}\mkern-3.0mu}{\mkern 2.0mu\reflectbox{$\scriptstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-2.0mu\scriptstyle\nabla\mkern 2.0mu$}}$}\mkern-2.0mu}{\mkern 2.0mu\reflectbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\vec{\reflectbox{$\mkern-2.0mu\scriptscriptstyle\nabla\mkern 2.0mu$}}$}\mkern-2.0mu}_{R}\,\vec{\nabla}_{R}\,.

(25)

Let us start the derivation from the Coulomb part
E_{C}
, where
|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}
can be replaced by
|\Psi\rangle

because we neglect all the radiative corrections.
The hydrogenic state
|\Psi\rangle=\hat{a}^{+}_{\phi}|0\rangle
, and

\displaystyle E_{C}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\langle\Psi|\vec{P}^{\,2}|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\,d^{3}x\,\langle 0|\hat{a}_{\phi}\,(\hat{\psi}^{+}\,\vec{p}\,\hat{\psi})_{x^{\prime}}\,(\hat{\psi}^{+}\,\vec{p}\,\hat{\psi})_{x}\,\hat{a}^{+}_{\phi}|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\,d^{3}x\,\phi^{*}(x^{\prime})\,\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,\bigl[\langle 0|\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)|0\rangle

\displaystyle\ -\langle 0|\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)\,\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})|0\rangle\bigr]\,\vec{p}\,\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{M}\int d^{3}x^{\prime}\,d^{3}x\,\phi^{*}(x^{\prime})\,\vec{p}\,^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{T}}\langle 0|\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)|0\rangle\,\vec{p}\,\phi(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\langle\phi|\,\vec{p}\,(P_{+}-P_{-})\,\vec{p}\,|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|\,p^{j}\,G(\omega+E_{0})\,p^{j}\,|\phi\rangle\,.

(26)

We note that the leading recoil correction is obtained from the above by replacing
P_{+}=I-P_{-}

and subsequent neglect of
P_{-}
, thus

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{rec}}\approx\langle\phi|\frac{\vec{p}\,^{2}}{2\,M}|\phi\rangle.

(27)

The second part is the single transverse photon exchange,

\displaystyle E_{T}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|\{\vec{P}\,,\,Z\,e\,\vec{A}(\vec{R})\}|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i\,Z\,e}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|A^{i}(\vec{R})\,\nabla^{i}_{R}|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}+\mathrm{h.c.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Z\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\int d^{4}y\,\langle\Psi|{\mathrm{T}}\big[A^{i}(\vec{R})\,\nabla^{i}_{R}\,j^{j}(y)\,A^{j}(y)\big]|\Psi\rangle+\mathrm{h.c.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i\,Z\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\int d^{4}y\,\langle\Psi|{\mathrm{T}}\big[\nabla^{i}_{R}\,j^{j}(y)\big]|\Psi\rangle\,G_{C}^{ij}(y-R)+\mathrm{h.c.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ E_{T1}+E_{T2}\,,

(28)

where the
\vec{\nabla}_{R}
operator is assumed at
t=0
in the chronological ordering.

E_{T1}
is due to the first term in Eq. (
21
), so

\displaystyle E_{T1}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i\,Z\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\int d^{4}y\,G_{C}^{ij}(y-R)\int d^{3}x\,

\displaystyle\times\langle 0|\hat{a}_{\phi}\,{\mathrm{T}}[(\hat{\psi}^{+}\partial^{i}\,\hat{\psi})_{x}\,(\hat{\psi}^{+}\alpha^{j}\,\psi)_{y}]\,\hat{a}^{+}_{\phi}|0\rangle+\mathrm{h.c.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{M}\,\int\frac{d\,\omega}{2\,\pi}\Bigl[\langle\phi|p^{j}\,G(\omega+E_{0})\,D_{C}^{j}(\omega)|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\ +\langle\phi|D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\,G(\omega+E_{0})\,p^{j}|\phi\rangle\Big]\,.

(29)

E_{T2}
, due to the second term in Eq. (
21
)

\displaystyle E_{T2}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i\,Z\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\int d^{4}y\,\Theta(-y_{0})\,\langle\Psi|j^{j}(y)|\Psi\rangle\,

\displaystyle\times\partial^{i}_{y}G^{ij}(y-R)+\mathrm{h.c.}=0\,,

(30)

vanishes due to the current conservation
\langle\Psi|\partial^{i}_{y}\,j^{i}(y)|\Psi\rangle=0
.

The third part, the double transverse (seagull) contribution
E_{S}
, is

\displaystyle E_{S}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Z^{2}\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|\vec{A}^{\,2}(\vec{R})|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{Z^{2}\,e^{2}}{2\,M}\,\frac{(i\,e)^{2}}{2}\,\int d^{4}x\int d^{4}y

\displaystyle\times\langle\Psi|{\mathrm{T}}\big[\vec{A}^{\,2}(\vec{R})\,\vec{j}(x)\cdot\vec{A}(x)\;\vec{j}(y)\cdot\vec{A}(y)\big]|\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\,\int\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\,G(\omega+E_{0})\,D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\,\alpha^{j}|\phi\rangle\,.

(31)

The sum
E_{C}+E_{T}+E_{S}
gives
E_{\mathrm{rec}}
in the
{\cal F}_{1}
form in Eq. (
6
),
which is next transformed to the
{\cal F}_{3}
form in Eq. (
15
).
The same approach will be used for the derivation of the recoil correction to the hyperfine splitting.

IV
Nonperturbative recoil correction to HFS

In the relativistic formalism the hyperfine splitting for the point and infinitely heavy nucleus is obtained from the expectation value of

\displaystyle V_{\rm hfs}=-e\,\vec{\alpha}\cdot\vec{A}_{I}\,,

(32)

where

\displaystyle e\,\vec{A}_{I}(\vec{r})=\frac{e}{4\,\pi}\,\vec{\mu}\times\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\,,

(33)

on a state
\phi

\displaystyle E_{\rm hfs}=\langle\phi|V_{\rm hfs}|\phi\rangle\,.

(34)

For the finite size nucleus, the Coulomb interaction becomes

\displaystyle\biggl[\frac{1}{r}\biggr]_{\mathrm{fs}}=\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,4\,\pi\,\frac{\rho_{C}(\vec{q}^{\,2})}{\vec{q}^{\,2}}\,e^{i\,\vec{q}\,\vec{r}}

(35)

[cf. Eq. (
3
)], and the magnetic one becomes

\displaystyle\biggl[\frac{\vec{r}}{r^{3}}\biggr]_{\mathrm{fs}}=-\vec{\nabla}\int\frac{d^{3}q}{(2\,\pi)^{3}}\,4\,\pi\,\frac{\rho_{M}(\vec{q}^{\,2})}{\vec{q}^{\,2}}\,e^{i\,\vec{q}\,\vec{r}}\,.

(36)

In the following we will assume
\rho_{C}=\rho_{M}=\rho
to keep the notation short, and the final formulas
will later be generalized to
\rho_{C}\neq\rho_{M}
.

The recoil correction to the hyperfine splitting is obtained using the following effective Hamiltonian for a particle
with an arbitrary spin
I
and charge
q
, which includes all spin-dependent terms up to
1/M^{2}
,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{nuc}}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\vec{\Pi}^{2}}{2\,M}+q\,A^{0}-\frac{q}{2\,M}\,g\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{B}

\displaystyle\ -\frac{q}{4\,M^{2}}\,(g-1)\,\vec{I}\cdot[\vec{E}\times\vec{\Pi}-\vec{\Pi}\times\vec{E}]\,,

(37)

where
\vec{\Pi}=\vec{P}-q\,\vec{A}
, and where we introduced the nuclear
g
factor,

\displaystyle\vec{\mu}=\frac{q}{2\,M}\,g\,\vec{I}

(38)

We will use this Hamiltonian for the nucleus, where we assume that the charge of the nucleus is
q=-Z\,e
with
e
being the electron charge.
Electromagnetic form factors are neglected in the above, because they depend on
q_{0}
through
q^{2}=q_{0}^{2}-\vec{q}^{\,2}
and thus
cannot be included on the Hamiltonian level.
Because every photon exchange between the nucleus and the electron involves the photon propagator multiplied by the
nuclear form factors, we can move these form factors to the redefined photon propagators [see Eqs. (
10
) and (
12
)].
The neglect of
q_{0}
in nuclear form factors is a common mistakes in relativistic atomic structure calculations.

Using Eq. (
37
) the recoil correction to hfs is split into three parts,

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{hfsrec}}=E_{\mathrm{kin}}+E_{\mathrm{so}}+E_{\mathrm{sec}}\,,

(39)

which are calculated one by one in the following.

IV.1
Kinetic energy contribution

The kinetic energy contribution is

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{kin}}=\frac{1}{2\,M}\,\langle\Psi|\vec{\Pi}^{2}|\Psi\rangle_{\mathrm{QED}}\,.

(40)

For its derivation we use Eq. (
18
) with
H_{I}
including the nucleus magnetic interaction

\displaystyle H_{I}(y)=e\,j_{\mu}(y)A^{\mu}(y)-\vec{\mu}\cdot\vec{B}(y)\,\delta^{3}(\vec{y}-\vec{R})\,,

(41)

and split it into two parts

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{kin}}=E_{\mathrm{kin1}}+E_{\mathrm{kin2}}\,.

(42)

E_{\rm kin1}
due to the first term in Eq. (
21
) is obtained from the previous result for the recoil correction to energy,

\displaystyle E_{\rm kin1}=

\displaystyle\ E_{C1}+E_{T1}+E_{S1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \delta_{\mathrm{hfs}}\,\frac{i}{M}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\,\langle\phi|\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]

\displaystyle\times G(E_{D}+\omega)\,\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]|\phi\rangle\,,

(43)

where the state
\phi
and the propagator

are corrected by the hyperfine interaction
V_{\mathrm{hfs}}
including the finite nuclear size,
namely

\displaystyle E_{\rm kin1}=

\displaystyle\ \frac{i}{M}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\Bigl[\langle\phi|\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]G(E_{D}+\omega)

\displaystyle\times\big(V_{\mathrm{hfs}}-\langle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}\rangle\big)G(E_{D}+\omega)\,\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]|\phi\rangle

\displaystyle\ +2\,\langle\phi|V_{\mathrm{hfs}}\,G^{\prime}(E_{D})\,\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]G(E_{D}+\omega)

\displaystyle\times\big[p^{j}-D_{C}^{j}(\omega)\big]|\phi\rangle\Bigr]\,.

(44)

E_{\rm kin2}
is due to the second term in Eq. (
21

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{kin2}}=E_{C3}+E_{C2}+E_{T2}\,,

(45)

which are calculated as follows. The first part is

\displaystyle E_{C3}=

Zemach

VI
Summary

We have introduced a general quantum electrodynamic method for the derivation of nuclear recoil corrections in hydrogenic systems,
and we present an exemplary derivation of the nuclear recoil correction to the hyperfine splitting.
The exact formulas in
Z\,\alpha
are shown in Eqs. (
67
) - (
69
). They can be used for the direct numerical calculation of the nuclear
recoil effects, or for an analytic derivation of
Z\,\alpha
expansion coefficients, in particular of the
O(Z\,\alpha)^{2}\,E_{F}
contribution, which was
originally derived by Bodwin and Yennie in Ref.
Bodwin:88
, but has not been confirmed.

This general method can be applied for the derivation of all the other nuclear recoil effects of an arbitrary order in the mass ratio,
including radiative recoil. It would be worthwhile, however, to simplify the derivation by direct use of the temporal gauge, because
formulas are very much simplified in this gauge.

Appendix A
Photon propagator

The photon propagator in the Feynman gauge is

\displaystyle i\,G^{\mu\nu}(x^{\prime}-x)=

\displaystyle\ \langle 0|{\mathrm{T}}A^{\mu}(x^{\prime})\,A^{\nu}(x)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -i\,g^{\mu\nu}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\,\pi)^{4}}\frac{e^{-i\,k(x^{\prime}-x)}}{k^{2}+i\,\epsilon}\,,

(87)

\displaystyle G^{\mu\nu}(k)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{g^{\mu\nu}}{k^{2}+i\,\epsilon}\,,

(88)

while in a Coulomb gauge with finite size,

\displaystyle G_{C}^{00}=

\displaystyle\ \rho(\vec{k}^{2})/\vec{k}^{2},

(89)

\displaystyle G_{C}^{ij}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\rho(-k^{2})}{k^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{(k^{0})^{2}}\biggr)-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{(k^{0})^{2}}\,\frac{\rho(\vec{k}^{2})}{\vec{k}^{2}}\,.

(90)

The transverse part is not orthogonal to
k^{i}

\displaystyle k^{i}\,G_{C}^{ij}(k)=

\displaystyle\ \bigl[\rho(-k^{2})-\rho(\vec{k}^{2})\bigr]\,\frac{k^{j}}{(k^{0})^{2}}\,,

(91)

in contrast to the propagator in the regular Coulomb gauge.
The photon propagator in the temporal gauge with finite size is

\displaystyle G_{T}^{ij}(\omega,\vec{k})=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\rho(-k^{2})}{k^{2}}\,\biggl(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}\,k^{j}}{\omega^{2}}\biggr)\,.

(92)

The auxiliary propagators are

\displaystyle\langle 0|{\mathrm{T}}A^{i}(x)\,B^{j}(y)|0\rangle=

\displaystyle\ -i\,\epsilon^{ijk}\,\nabla^{k}\,{D}(x-y)\,,

(93)

\displaystyle D(x-y)=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\,e^{-i\,k\,r}\,\frac{\rho(-k^{2})}{k^{2}}\,,

(94)

\displaystyle D(\omega,r)=

\displaystyle\ \int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,e^{i\vec{k}\cdot\vec{r}}\,\frac{\rho({\vec{k}}^{2}-\omega^{2})}{\omega^{2}-{\vec{k}}^{2}}\,,

(95)

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{hfs}}(\omega,r)=

\displaystyle\ \epsilon^{ijl}\,e\,\mu^{i}\,\alpha^{j}\,\partial^{l}\,D(\omega,r)

(96)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{4\,\pi\,Z\,\alpha}{2\,M}\,g\,\vec{I}\cdot\vec{\alpha}\times\vec{\nabla}D(\omega,r).

Appendix B
Spinor field

The representation of the spinor field in terms of solutions of the Dirac equation are

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{+}a_{s}\phi_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{-i\,E_{s}t}+\sum_{s}^{-}b_{s}\phi_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{-i\,E_{s}t}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{+}a^{+}_{s}\phi^{+}_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{i\,E_{s}t}+\sum_{s}^{-}b^{+}_{s}\phi^{+}_{s}(\vec{x})\,e^{i\,E_{s}t}\,,

(97)

which form a complete basis

\displaystyle\sum_{s}^{+}\phi_{s}(x^{\prime})\,\phi^{+}_{s}(x)+\sum_{s}^{-}\phi_{s}(x^{\prime})\,\phi^{+}_{s}(x)=

\displaystyle\ \delta^{3}(\vec{x}^{\prime}-\vec{x})\,.

(98)

The fermionic anticommutation relations are

\displaystyle\{a_{r}\,,\,a^{+}_{s}\}=\{b_{r}\,,\,b^{+}_{s}\}=\delta_{r,s}\,,

(99)

and

\displaystyle\{\hat{\psi}(\vec{x}\,^{\prime},0)\,,\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{x},0)\}=

\displaystyle\ \delta^{3}(\vec{x}^{\prime}-\vec{x})\,.

(100)

The fefinition of the vacuum state is

\displaystyle a_{r}|0\rangle=b^{+}_{r}|0\rangle=0\,.

(101)

The projection operators into positive and negative energy subspace are

\displaystyle P^{+}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{+}\phi_{s}(x^{\prime})\,\phi^{+}_{s}(x)=\langle 0|\hat{\psi}(\vec{x}\,^{\prime},0)\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{x},0)|0\rangle\,,

\displaystyle P^{-}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{s}^{-}\phi_{s}(x^{\prime})\,\phi^{+}_{s}(x)=\langle 0|\hat{\psi}^{+}(\vec{x},0)\,\hat{\psi}(\vec{x}\,^{\prime},0)|0\rangle\,,

(102)

with

\displaystyle P^{+}+P^{-}=

\displaystyle\ I\,.

(103)

The fermion propagator is

\displaystyle i\,G(x^{\prime},x)=

\displaystyle\ \langle 0|{\mathrm{T}}\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ i\,\int\frac{d\omega}{2\,\pi}\sum_{s}\frac{\psi_{s}(\vec{x}\,^{\prime})\,\psi^{+}_{s}(\vec{x})}{\omega-E_{s}(1-i\,\epsilon)}\,e^{-i\,\omega\,(x^{\prime 0}-x^{0})}\,.

(104)

The equal time propagator can be written as

\displaystyle i\,G(x^{\prime},x)|_{x^{\prime 0}=x^{0}}=

\displaystyle\ \langle 0|{\mathrm{T}}\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)|0\rangle|_{x^{\prime 0}=x^{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\,\langle 0|\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})\,\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)-\hat{\psi}^{+}(x)\,\hat{\psi}(x^{\prime})|0\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\,(P^{+}-P^{-})\,.

(105)

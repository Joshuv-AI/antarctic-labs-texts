Functional law of large numbers and central limit theorem for slow-fast McKean-Vlasov equations Thanks: This work is supported by NNSF of China (No. 12090011, 12071186, 11931004).
Yun Li and Longjie Xie

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the asymptotic behavior of a fully-coupled slow-fast McKean-Vlasov stochastic system. Using the non-linear Poisson equation on Wasserstein space, we first establish the strong convergence in the averaging principle of the functional law of large numbers type. In particular, the diffusion coefficient of the slow process can depend on the distribution of the fast motion. Then we consider the stochastic fluctuations of the original system around its average, and prove that the normalized difference will converge weakly to a linear McKean-Vlasov Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type process, which can be viewed as a functional central limit theorem. Extra drift and diffusion coefficients involving the expectation are characterized explicitly. Furthermore, the optimal rates of the convergence are also obtained. AMS 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 60H10, 60F05, 70K70. Keywords: Averaging principle; central limit theorem; McKean-Vlasov equation; multi-scale system.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we state the main results. In Section 3, we prepare some auxiliary results about the non-linear Poisson equation on the whole space and some a priori estimates. Section 4 is devoted to the proof of the strong convergence in the averaging principle. In Section 5, we establish a fluctuation lemma and then give the proof of the functional central limit type theorem. Finally, an Itô’s formula is provided in the Appendix for the sake of complicity.

Proof.

Following the idea of the proof of Itô’s formula in
[

, we assume without loss of generality that the derivatives of

f(t),b(t),g_{i}(t),\sigma_{i}(t)(i=1,2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}(|f(t)|^{2}+\|g_{1}(t)\|^{4}+\|g_{2}(t)\|^{4}+|b(t)|^{2}+\|\sigma_{1}(t)\|^{4}+\|\sigma_{2}(t)\|^{4})\Big]<\infty.

For every

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X^{k}_{t}=f^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+g_{1}^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t}+g_{2}^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B^{k}_{t},~~X^{k}_{0}=\xi^{k},

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y^{k}_{t}=b^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma_{1}^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W^{k}_{t}+\sigma_{2}^{k}(t){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B^{k}_{t},~~Y^{k}_{0}=\eta^{k}.

Denote by

and
\nu_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{Y^{k}_{t}}
the empirical measures of
(X^{k}_{t})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant N}
and
(Y^{k}_{t})_{1\leqslant k\leqslant N}
, respectively. Then we define

\displaystyle u^{N}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N},y_{1},\cdots,y_{N})=u\bigg(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{x_{k}},\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_{y_{k}}\bigg).

Using the classical Itô’s formula, we derive

\displaystyle u(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})=u^{N}(X^{1}_{t},\cdots,X^{N}_{t},Y^{1}_{t},\cdots,Y^{N}_{t})

\displaystyle=u^{N}(\xi^{1},\cdots,\xi^{N},\eta^{1},\cdots,\eta^{N})+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(X^{k}_{s})\cdot f^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(X^{k}_{s})\cdot g_{1}^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}^{k}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(X^{k}_{s})\cdot g_{2}^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\partial_{\nu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(Y^{k}_{s})\cdot b^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\nu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(Y^{k}_{s})\cdot\sigma_{1}^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}^{k}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\nu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(Y^{k}_{s})\cdot\sigma_{2}^{k}(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}B_{s}^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2N}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathord{{\rm Tr}}\big([\sigma_{1}^{k}\sigma_{1}^{k,*}+\sigma_{2}^{k}\sigma_{2}^{k,*}](s)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}u(\mu_{s}^{N},\nu_{s}^{N})(Y^{k}_{s})\big){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}u(\mu_{t}^{N},\nu_{t}^{N})

\displaystyle u(\mu_{t},\nu_{t})=u({\mathcal{L}}_{\xi},{\mathcal{L}}_{\eta})+\int_{0}^{t}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\partial_{\mu}u(\mu_{s},\nu_{s})(X_{s})\cdot f(s)\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{t+h}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\mathord{{\rm Tr}}\big([\sigma_{1}\sigma_{1}^{*}+\sigma_{2}\sigma_{2}^{*}](s)\cdot\partial_{\tilde{y}}\partial_{\nu}u(x,\mu_{s},y,\nu_{s})(Y_{s})\big)\big]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s.

Upper bounds on the one-arm exponent for dependent percolation models
Vivek Dewan 1, Stephen Muirhead 2

Abstract
. We prove upper bounds on the one-arm exponent η 1 \eta_{1} for a class of dependent percolation models which generalise Bernoulli percolation; while our main interest is level set percolation of Gaussian fields, the arguments apply to other models in the Bernoulli percolation universality class, including Poisson-Voronoi and Poisson-Boolean percolation. More precisely, in dimension d = 2 d=2 we prove that η 1 ≤ 1 / 3 \eta_{1}\leq 1/3 for continuous Gaussian fields with rapid correlation decay (e.g. the Bargmann-Fock field), and in d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 we prove η 1 ≤ d / 3 \eta_{1}\leq d/3 for finite-range fields, both discrete and continuous, and η 1 ≤ d − 2 \eta_{1}\leq d-2 for fields with rapid correlation decay. Although these results are classical for Bernoulli percolation (indeed they are best-known in general), existing proofs do not extend to dependent percolation models, and we develop a new approach based on exploration and relative entropy arguments. The proof also makes use of a new Russo-type inequality for Gaussian fields, which we apply to prove the sharpness of the phase transition and the mean-field bound for finite-range fields.

1.
Introduction

The critical phase of percolation models is believed to be described by a set of
critical exponents
which govern the power-law behaviour of macroscopic observables at, or near, criticality. These exponents have been thoroughly investigated for Bernoulli percolation (see, e.g.,
[
28
, Chapter 9]
) and there is a general expectation that, by
universality
, they are identical within the class of dependent percolation models with sufficient decay of correlations. However not much is rigorously known about critical exponents for dependent percolation models.

In this paper we study the
one-arm exponent
governing the distribution of critical clusters with large diameter. Using a new approach we establish upper bounds on the exponent for a wide class of dependent percolation models induced by the excursion sets of Gaussian fields (‘Gaussian percolation’), which contains Bernoulli site percolation as a special case; roughly-speaking our results extend the state-of-the-art for Bernoulli percolation to this general class.

Although we focus on Gaussian percolation, our approach could be adapted to other models in the same universality class, including Poisson-Voronoi and Poisson-Boolean percolation.

1.1.
Gaussian percolation and the one-arm exponent

We consider the following class of dependent percolation models defined on either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. Let
f
be either (i) a continuous stationary-ergodic centred Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or (ii) a stationary-ergodic centred Gaussian field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We write
\mathbb{X}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}\}
depending on the domain
\mathbb{X}^{d}
of the field.

For
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
write
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\cdot]
to denote the law of
f+\ell
(abbreviating
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{0}
). Then the excursion sets
\{f+\ell\geq 0\}:=\{x\in\mathbb{X}^{d}:f(x)+\ell\geq 0\}
induce a stationary-ergodic percolation model on
\mathbb{X}^{d}
via the connectivity relation

\{A\longleftrightarrow B\}:=\{\text{there exists a path in $\mathbb{X}^{d}$ in $\{f\geq 0\}$ that intersects $A$ and $B$}\}

for closed sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{X}^{d}
. When
f
is an i.i.d. field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, this model is equivalent to Bernoulli
site
percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. When
f
is a continuous field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2
, the model is
self-dual
at
\ell=0
, and in that sense is similar to
bond
percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

Let
\Lambda_{R}:=[-R,R]^{d}\cap\mathbb{X}^{d}
. By monotonicity we may define the
critical level

\ell_{c}=\ell_{c}(f)\in[-\infty,\infty]
of the model to be the unique level satisfying

\theta(\ell):=\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\infty]:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\ell<\ell_{c},\\
>0&\text{if }\ell>\ell_{c}.\end{cases}

By self-duality one expects that
\ell_{c}=0
if
f
is continuous and
d=2
, whereas in general one expects
\ell_{c}\in(-\infty,\infty)
(see
[

,
43
,
26

42
]

[
40
,
41
]
respectively for sufficient conditions, which are implied by Assumption
1.1

\theta(\ell_{c})=0
, although so far this is only known if
d=2
and
f
is positively-correlated
[
32
,
24
,
4
]
, or if
f
is i.i.d. and
d\geq 11

[
23
]
.

For Bernoulli percolation
[
12
,
2
]
, as well as certain other (non-Gaussian) dependent percolation models
[
19
,
18
,
20
]
, it is known that
\theta(\ell)
satisfies the
mean-field bound

(MFB)

\theta(\ell)\geq c(\ell-\ell_{c})

for a constant

and
\ell>\ell_{c}
sufficiently close to
\ell_{c}
; this bound is expected to be tight for dimensions

in which critical exponents take their mean-field value. We expect (

) to be true in general for Gaussian percolation but this has not yet been established; in this paper we prove it for Gaussian models with
finite-range dependence
.

At criticality
\ell=\ell_{c}
it is believed that connection probabilities obey a power law, in the sense that there exists a
one-arm exponent

\eta_{1}>0
such that, as
R\to\infty
and for
r=o(R)
,

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[\Lambda_{r}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]=(r/R)^{\eta_{1}+o(1)}.

While the existence of
\eta_{1}
is not known (except in the i.i.d. case in high dimension), since we are interested in upper bounds we define

(1.2)

\eta_{1}:=\liminf_{R\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]}{\log R}.

Clearly upper bounds on (
1.2
) imply upper bounds on the exponent in (
1.1
) assuming its existence. Note however that the choice of
\liminf
, rather than
\limsup
, in the definition of
\eta_{1}
is deliberate and yields an a priori weaker bound (c.f. Remark
1.5
).

For Bernoulli percolation it is expected that

\eta_{1}=\begin{cases}&\!\!\!\!5/48\qquad\ \ \hskip 1.42271ptd=2,\qquad\qquad 0.48\ldots\qquad d=3,\qquad\qquad 0.95\ldots\qquad d=4,\\
&\!\!\!\!1.5\ldots\qquad d=5,\qquad\qquad 2\qquad\qquad\hskip 8.5359ptd\geq d_{c}=6.\end{cases}

According to the
Harris criterion
(see
[
57
]
, or
[
8
]

\eta_{1}
should be the same for all Gaussian models whose correlations decay faster than

of Bernoulli percolation (see Section

may be different
[

,

]

1.2.
Upper bounds on the one-arm exponent

Recall that
f
is a stationary-ergodic centred Gaussian field on the state space
\mathbb{X}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
, where
\mathbb{X}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}\}
. We refer to
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
and
d=2
as the
self-dual case
.

We will always assume that
f
has a
spatial moving average
representation
f=q\star W
, where
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{X}^{d})\neq 0
is Hermitian (i.e.
q(x)=q(-x)
),
W
is the white noise on
\mathbb{X}^{d}
(interpreted as a collection of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
if
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}
), and
\star
denotes convolution. Such a representation always exists if the covariance kernel
K(\cdot):=\mathbb{E}[f(0)f(\cdot)]=(q\star q)(\cdot)
is absolutely integrable, since then we may define
q:=\mathcal{F}[\sqrt{\rho}]
, where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform and
\rho=\mathcal{F}[K]
is the
spectral density
of the field. Bernoulli site percolation corresponds to the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}
and
q(x)=\mathds{1}_{x=0}
.

We will further assume that
q
satisfies the following basic properties:

Assumption 1.1

(Basic assumptions)
.

(a)

(Decay of correlations, with parameter
\beta>d
) There exists a
c>0
such that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(1.3)

|q(x)|\leq c|x|^{-\beta}.

(b)

(Symmetry)
q
is symmetric under negation and permutation of the coordinate axes.

(c)

(Regularity, only if
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
)
q\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, all derivatives up to third-order are in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and (
1.3
) holds with
|\nabla q(x)|
in place of
|q(x)|
.

Let us make some remarks on Assumption
1.1
. The decay condition ensures that
K=q\star q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X}^{d})
, and so correlations decay polynomially with exponent
\beta>d
. This also ensures that the spectral density
\rho=\mathcal{F}[K]
is continuous. The symmetry assumption is crucial for our results in self-dual case (for instance to prove RSW estimates), but it also simplifies some aspects of the proof in general. In the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}
, the regularity condition ensures that
f
is
C^{2}
-smooth almost surely. Along with the continuity of the spectral measure, this also implies that
(f,\nabla f,\nabla^{2}f)
is non-degenerate (i.e. its evaluation on a finite number of distinct points is a non-degenerate Gaussian vector, see
[
6
, Lemma A.2]
). Finally, as we mentioned above, Assumption
1.1
is sufficient to prove that
\ell_{c}\in(-\infty,\infty)

[
40
,
41
]
, and that
\ell_{c}=0
in the self-dual case (see
[
42
, Theorem 1.3]
and Remark 1.9 therein).

For most of our results we also assume that

(POS)

\int q:=\int_{\mathbb{X}^{d}}q(x)\,dx>0,

where for a function
g:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
we interpret
\int_{D}g(x)\,dx
as
\sum_{x\in D\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g(x)
. This is equivalent to the spectral density
\rho
being strictly positive at the origin, and is a natural assumption when studying how properties of a Gaussian field change with the level; see e.g.
[
43
,
7
]
.

For some of our results we further assume that
f
is
positively correlated
:

(PA)

K(x)=(q\star q)(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{X}^{d}
.

This is equivalent to the
FKG inequality
holding for the field (i.e. the field is
positively associated
), so that events increasing with respect to the field are positively correlated
[
46
]
.
1
1

1

Although in
[
46
]
this is proven only for finite Gaussian
vectors
, one can deduce positive associations for all increasing events considered in this paper via standard approximation arguments, see
[
48
, Lemma A.12]
.
Note that (
PA
) is stronger than (
POS
), since the former implies that
\rho=\mathcal{F}[K]
is positive definite, and so strictly positive at the origin.

We are now ready to state our results. Our first result concerns Gaussian percolation with finite-range of dependence:

Theorem 1.2

(Finite-range models)
.

Suppose
f
satisfies Assumption
1.1
, (
POS
), and
q
has bounded support. Then
\eta_{1}\leq d/3
. In the self-dual case (
f
is continuous and
d=2
), if additionally (
PA
) holds, then there exists
c>0
such that, for all
R\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]\geq cR^{-1/3},

and in particular
\eta_{1}\leq 1/3
.

For Bernoulli percolation on

Remark 1.4
.

We emphasise that the bound

Remark 1.5

Unlike in the self-dual case, our proof that

Remark 1.6

In the self-dual case we also obtain a bound on the ‘one-arm exponent’ for

Remark 1.7
.

Clearly if

has bounded support then

is finite-range dependent, but we do not know whether every finite-range

. It is also known

Our next result extends Theorem
1.2
to a class of models with unbounded range of dependence. This depends on the decay exponent

Theorem 1.8

(Models with polynomial correlation decay)
.

Suppose

satisfies Assumption
1.1
with parameter
\beta>d
, and (

).

(1)

If

\beta>4d-4

\eta_{1}\leq\min\{\frac{d-2+\alpha(3d-1)}{1+2\alpha},d-1\}

(2)

If

\beta>2d-1
, and (

\eta_{1}\leq\min\{\max\{\frac{d}{3}+\frac{\alpha(d-1)}{3},\frac{\alpha(2d-1)}{3}\},d-1\}

.

(3)

If

is continuous and

\beta>\frac{8}{3}

\eta_{1}\leq\min\{\frac{1}{3}+\frac{5}{6(\beta-1)},\frac{1}{2}\}

(1)

If

.

(2)

(3)

Take

1.3.
Relations to other critical exponents

Our methods also give bounds on
\eta_{1}
in terms of other critical exponents, some of which appear to be new even for Bernoulli percolation.

For simplicity we state these results only in the case of Bernoulli site percolation, although similar results to Theorems
1.2
and
1.8
could be proven under more general assumptions. The proofs also go through unchanged for Bernoulli
bond
percolation. For the remainder of the subsection we assume that
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}
and
q(x)=\mathds{1}_{x=0}
.

Let us introduce the relevant exponents. Recall the mean-field bound (
MFB
) on
\theta(\ell)
. It is expected that
\theta(\ell)\to 0
as a power law as
\ell\downarrow\ell_{c}
; although this is not known rigorously (except in high dimension), we will assume that the corresponding exponent exists

\beta=\lim_{\ell\downarrow\ell_{c}}\frac{\log\theta(\ell)}{\log|\ell_{c}-\ell|}\in(0,\infty).

Below criticality
\ell<\ell_{c}
, it is known that connection probabilities decay exponentially
[
39
,
2
,
21
]
and that the limit

\frac{1}{\xi(\ell)}:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]}{R}\in(0,\infty)

exists
[
28
, Theorem 6.10]
. The
correlation length

\xi(\ell)
is expected to diverge as a power law as
\ell\uparrow\ell_{c}
, and we will again assume that the corresponding exponent exists

\nu=\lim_{\ell\uparrow\ell_{c}}\frac{-\log\xi(\ell)}{\log|\ell_{c}-\ell|}\in(0,\infty).

Similarly, as
\ell\uparrow\ell_{c}
the
susceptibility

\chi(\ell):=\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[0\longleftrightarrow v]<\infty
is expected to diverge as a power law, and we will assume the existence of

\gamma=\lim_{\ell\uparrow\ell_{c}}\frac{-\log\chi(\ell)}{\log|\ell_{c}-\ell|}\in(0,\infty).

Finally we also assume that the critical
two-point function
decays as a power law with exponent

d-2+\eta:=\lim_{|v|_{\infty}\to\infty}\frac{-\log\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[0\longleftrightarrow v]}{\log|v|_{\infty}}\in(0,\infty),

where
|\cdot|_{\infty}
denotes the sup-norm. We remark that
\beta\leq 1
because of (
MFB
), and it is well known that
\nu\geq 2/d

[
11
]
,
\gamma\geq 1

[
3
]
, and
\eta\leq 1

[
29
]
.

Theorem 1.12
.

For Bernoulli percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, assuming the existence of
\beta,\nu,\gamma
and
\eta
,

(1.5)

\frac{2-\gamma}{\nu}\leq\eta_{1}\leq\bar{\eta}_{1}\leq\min\Big\{d-\frac{2}{\nu},\frac{2-\eta}{2/\beta-1}\Big\}\leq 2-\eta,

where
\bar{\eta}_{1}
is defined as in (

) with limsup replacing liminf. Moreover
\eta_{1}\leq\frac{d}{2/\beta+1}
.

39
]
), this argument also yields the
sharpness
of the phase transition.

Theorem 1.16

(Sharpness of the phase transition for finite-range models)
.

Suppose
f
satisfies Assumption
1.1
and
q
has bounded support. Then the mean-field bound (
MFB
) holds. Moreover, for every
\ell<\ell_{c}
there exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, for
R\geq 1
,

(1.6)

\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\Lambda_{1}\longleftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{R}]\leq c_{1}e^{-c_{2}R}.

Remark 1.17
.

For Gaussian percolation, the exponential decay in (
1.6
) was only known so far in two cases: (i) in the self-dual case assuming positive correlations
[
43
]
, and (ii) for certain positively-correlated Gaussian fields on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
16
,
27
]
. The mean-field bound (
MFB
) was not yet known for any Gaussian models.

We emphasise that in Theorem
1.16
we do not assume positive correlations, so this theorem holds for a class of models lacking positive associations. The proof also does not require the symmetry condition in Assumption
1.1
.

1.5.
Elements of the proof: the ‘entropic’ bound, and a new Russo-type inequality

The existing upper bounds on
\eta_{1}
for Bernoulli percolation rely heavily on specific properties of this model (such as the BK inequality, or for the sharper results on specific lattices, also on properties of parafermionic observables) and do not extend easily to dependent percolation models. We develop a new approach based on exploration and relative entropy arguments.

There are two main ingredients in our approach, giving upper and lower bounds respectively on the change in probability of events as
\ell
varies:

(1)

A non-differential ‘entropic’ upper bound, with control in terms of revealment probabilities of explorations.

(2)

A ‘Russo-type inequality’ giving a lower bound on the derivative for increasing events, which when combined with the OSSS inequality also gives control in terms of revealment probabilities of explorations.

In the self-dual case we compare these inequalities when applied to square-crossing events at criticality; by a judicious choice of algorithm the revealment probabilities are controlled by one-arm probabilities for level lines (c.f. Remark
1.6
). In the general case we first use the Russo-type inequality to establish the mean-field bound (
MFB
), and then compare this to the entropic bound applied to arm events in the critical and slightly supercritical regimes.

Since we believe these ingredients to be of independent interest, we describe them in detail:

1.5.1.
The entropic bound

For context, let us first state a well-known bound for Bernoulli percolation. Recall the concept of a
randomised algorithm
(or ‘decision tree’ or ‘exploration’ depending on the context):

Definition 1.18

(Randomised algorithms)
.

Let
X=(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable set of random variables taking values in arbitrary measurable spaces, and let
Z=(Z_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an auxiliary sequence of independent uniform random variables. A
(randomised) algorithm

\mathcal{A}
on
X
is a random sequence
X^{\mathcal{A}}=(X_{\varphi(1)},..,X_{\varphi(i)},..)
such that, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varphi(i)\in\mathbb{N}
is distinct from all
\varphi(j)
,
j<i
, and depends only on (i)
(X_{\varphi(1)},..,X_{\varphi(i-1)})
, and (ii) the auxiliary randomness
Z_{i}
. The sequence
X^{\mathcal{A}}
can be either infinite or finite; in the latter case we say that
\mathcal{A}

terminates
, and a real number is returned which is a measurable function of
X^{\mathcal{A}}
. We say that
\mathcal{A}

determines an event

A
if it almost surely terminates and returns the value
\mathds{1}_{A}
. The
revealment probability

\textrm{Rev}(i)
is the probability that
\mathcal{A}

reveals

X_{i}
, i.e. that
X^{\mathcal{A}}
contains the coordinate
X_{i}
.

The following bound is essentially contained in
[
45
]
(see also
[
52
, Appendix B]
and
[
56
]
for similar bounds):

Proposition 1.19
.

Let
Y=(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}
be a finite set of independent Bernoulli random variables, and let
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\cdot]
denote its law under parameter
p\in[0,1]
. Let
A
be an event, and let
\mathcal{A}
be an algorithm on
Y
determining
A
. Then for all
p\in(0,1)
,

\Big|\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]\Big|\leq\frac{1}{p(1-p)}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]\mathbb{E}_{p}[N]},

where
N
is the number of coordinates that are revealed by
\mathcal{A}
.

We extend Proposition
1.19
in two ways:

•

We give a non-differential version bounding
|\mathbb{P}_{q}[A]-\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]|
,
q>p
, in terms of the expected number of revealed coordinates
\mathbb{E}_{p}[N]
when the algorithm
is run at parameter
p
. This is a sharper bound whenever the algorithm is more efficient when run at parameter
p
compared to parameter
q
.

•

We give a new proof based on properties of the relative entropy, which applies in a variety of settings. See Remark
2.6
for a comparison with the approach in
[
45
,
52
,
56
]
.

To state this extension we return to the Gaussian setting. Fix a constant
s>0
and partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into boxes
\mathcal{S}_{s}
which are translations of
[0,s)^{d}
by the lattice
s\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}
, let
W|_{S}=W\mathds{1}_{S}
denote the restriction of the white noise
W
to
S
, and consider the decomposition

(1.7)

f=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}f_{S}\ ,\quad f_{S}=q\star W|_{S},

where each
f_{S}
is an independent centred Gaussian field (in the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we also assume that each
f_{S}
is simultaneously continuous almost surely, which is possible by countability). Let
\mathcal{A}_{s}
denote the collection of randomised algorithms on
(f_{S})_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}
. We say that a box
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}
is
revealed
by an algorithm
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
if
f_{S}
is revealed, with
\text{Rev}(S)
the corresponding probability.

Proposition 1.20

(Entropic bound)
.

Suppose
f=q\star W
is either a Gaussian field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or a continuous Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and assume that
q
has bounded support and
\int q>0
. Then for every
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, compactly supported event
A
,
s>0
, algorithm
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
that determines
A
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

\big|\mathbb{P}_{\ell+\varepsilon}\big[A\big]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon s^{d/2}}{\int q}\!\!\sqrt{\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A],\mathbb{P}_{\ell+\varepsilon}[A]\}\mathbb{E}_{\ell}[N]},

where
N
is the number of boxes in
\mathcal{S}_{s}
that are revealed by
\mathcal{A}
.

Notice that Proposition
1.20
compares the field
f+\ell
and its perturbation by
\varepsilon>0
. In fact we use an extension of this result (see Proposition
2.1
) which allows for more general perturbations. For the reader interested primarily in Bernoulli percolation, we record the extended form of the entropic bound in that setting here:

Proposition 1.21

(Entropic bound for Bernoulli percolation)
.

Let
Y
,
A
and
\mathcal{A}
be as in Proposition
1.19
. Let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
be a subset of coordinates, let
p,q\in(0,1)
, and let
\mathbb{P}_{p;q;I}[\cdot]
denote the law of
Y
where the Bernoulli parameter of coordinate
Y_{i}
is
q
if
i\in I
and
p
if
i\notin I
. Then

|\mathbb{P}_{p;q;I}[A]-\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]|\leq\max\!\Big\{\!\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{q(1-q)}}\!\Big\}|p-q|\sqrt{\max\{\mathbb{P}_{p}[A],\mathbb{P}_{p;q;I}[A]\}\mathbb{E}_{p}[N_{I}]},

where
N_{I}
is the number of coordinates in
I
that are revealed by
\mathcal{A}
.

Setting
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and taking
q\downarrow p
recovers Proposition
1.19
(with improved constant
1/\sqrt{p(1-p)}
in place of
1/(p(1-p))
).

1.5.2.
The Russo-type inequality

Recall the classical
Russo formula
for Bernoulli percolation: for
p\in(0,1)
and an increasing event
A
,

(1.8)

\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]=\frac{2}{p(1-p)}\sum_{i}\textrm{Infl}_{p;A}(i),

where
Y=(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}
and
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\cdot]
are as in Proposition
1.19
, and

\textrm{Infl}_{p;A}(i):=\mathbb{P}_{p}\big[\mathds{1}_{Y\in A}\neq\mathds{1}_{Y^{(i)}\in A}\big]

denotes the
resampling influence
, where
Y^{(i)}
denotes a copy of
Y
with the coordinate
Y_{i}
resampled independently (for increasing events, the resampling influence coincides with the classical ‘pivotal probability’ up to a constant). By combining (
1.8
) with the OSSS inequality
\textrm{Var}_{p}[\mathds{1}_{A}]\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}\textrm{Rev}(i)\textrm{Infl}_{p;A}(i)

[
44
]
, one obtains the bound

\frac{d}{dp}\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]\geq\frac{4}{p(1-p)}\frac{\textrm{Var}_{p}[\mathds{1}_{A}]}{\max_{i}\textrm{Rev}(i)},

where the revealment probabilties are under
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\cdot]
.

In order to apply similar arguments in Gaussian percolation, one wishes to have a Gaussian extension of (
1.8
); in fact one only needs a
lower bound
in place of the equality.

In
[
43
]
such an extension was given for discrete Gaussian fields with finite-range dependence (i.e. the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}
and
q
with bounded support). As in the previous subsection, fix
s>0
and recall the orthogonal decomposition
f=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}f_{S}
in (
1.7
). For an event
A
and
S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}
, we define the
resampling influence

(1.9)

\textrm{Infl}_{\ell;A}(S):=\mathbb{P}_{\ell}\big[\mathds{1}_{\{f\in A\}}\neq\mathds{1}_{\{f^{(S)}\in A\}}\big],

where
f^{(S)}
denotes the field
f=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}f_{S}
with
f_{S}
resampled independently. The result in
[
43
]
concerned the case
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{Z}
with
s=1
, so that resampling
f_{S}
is equivalent to resampling the one-dimensional Gaussian
W|_{S}
. We state a version of this result:

Proposition 1.22

(Russo-type inequality, discrete
L^{1}
-version;
[
43
, Proposition 5.3]
)
.

Suppose
f=q\star W
is a Gaussian field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
q\geq 0
has bounded support. Then there exists a constant
c>0
depending only on
d
such that, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
and increasing compactly supported event
A
,

\frac{d}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]\geq\frac{c}{\|q\|_{1}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\textrm{Infl}_{\ell;A}(S).

Observe that setting
q(x)=\mathds{1}_{x=0}
in Proposition
1.22
recovers (
1.8
) as a lower bound, up to a constant.

Although this result concerns discrete Gaussian fields, in
[
43
]
it was applied to continuous fields by first
discretising
the white noise
W
at scale
\varepsilon\ll 1
. However the presence of the ‘
L^{1}
-type’ factor
\|q\|_{1}
makes the bound degenerate in the
\varepsilon\to 0
limit. Although this issue was circumvented in the specific application in
[
43
]
, it prevented an adaptation of
[
19
]
to prove Theorem
1.16
for continuous fields, even finite-range dependent.

In this paper we establish an improvement on Proposition
1.22
which does not suffer from this degeneracy, and applies directly to discrete and continuous fields. We also remove the requirement that
q\geq 0
. To achieve this we analyse the effect of resampling
large regions of white noise simultaneously
. Interpreting the Russo-type inequality as a consequence of the Gaussian isoperimetric inequality, we then exploit the fact that Gaussian isoperimetry is dimension-free.

We say that an event
A
is a
continuity event
if
\ell\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f+g|_{\mathbb{X}^{d}}\in A]
is continuous for every smooth function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
. Recall the
Dini derivatives
, defined for
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(1.10)

\frac{d^{+}}{dx}g(x)=\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{g(x+\varepsilon)-g(x)}{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{d^{-}}{dx}g(x)=\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\frac{g(x+\varepsilon)-g(x)}{\varepsilon}.

Proposition 1.23

(Russo-type inequality,
L^{2}
-version)
.

Suppose
f=q\star W
is either a Gaussian field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or a continuous Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
r>0
be such that
q
is supported on
\Lambda_{r}
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
depending only on
d
such that, for every
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
,
s>0
, and increasing compactly supported continuity event
A
,

\frac{d^{-}}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]\geq\frac{c\min\{1,(s/r)^{d}\}}{\|q\|_{2}}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}\textrm{Infl}_{\ell;A}(S).

Remark 1.24
.

In the literature there have been other approaches to obtaining Russo-type inequalities for Gaussian percolation using alternative measures of influence, see e.g.
[
50
, Proposition 3.2]
and
[
49
, Theorem 2.19]
. However these also suffer from degeneracy issues when passing to the continuum, and do not combine easily with the OSSS inequality.

1.5.3.
A general lower bound for revealment probabilities

Recall that in the self-dual case we obtain bounds on
\eta_{1}
by comparing the entropic bound Proposition
1.19
with the Russo-type inequality in Proposition
1.23
(and the OSSS inequality) for a well-chosen algorithm.

A similar approach appeared previously in the general setting of increasing Boolean functions
[
9
]
, where it was used to obtain the following result:

Proposition 1.25

(
[
9
, Theorem 2 (part 2)]
)
.

Let
Y=(Y_{i})_{i\leq n}
,
A
and
\mathcal{A}
be as in Proposition
1.19
, and suppose that
A
is increasing. Then

\max_{i\leq n}\textrm{Rev}(i)\geq\frac{\textrm{Var}_{1/2}[\mathds{1}_{A}]^{2/3}}{n^{1/3}},

where the revealment probabilties are under
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\cdot]
.

As far as we know one cannot recover the bound
\eta_{1}\leq 1/3
in the self-dual case, even for Bernoulli percolation, simply by applying the result in Proposition
1.25
to a well-chosen algorithm. Instead one needs a ‘conditional’ version that bounds the maximum revealment probabilities within a subset of coordinates. Since we believe it to be of independent interest, we state this bound in the Bernoulli setting. See Remark
3.9
for comments on the Gaussian version.

Proposition 1.26
.

Let
Y
,
A
and
\mathcal{A}
be as in Proposition
1.25
, let
I\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
be a subset of coordinates, and let
\mathcal{F}_{I}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(Y_{i})_{i\in I}
. Then

\max_{i\in I}\textrm{Rev}(i)\geq\frac{\big(4\textrm{Var}_{p}\big[\,\mathbb{P}_{p}[A\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{I}]\big]\big)^{2/3}}{(p(1-p)\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]|I|)^{1/3}},

where the revealment probabilties are under
\mathbb{P}_{p}[\cdot]
.

Setting
p=1/2
and
I=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
recovers Proposition
1.26
with an improved constant.

Proof.

We use the conditional version of the OSSS inequality (see the proof of Proposition
3.8
for how to derive this from the standard version)

\textrm{Var}_{p}\big[\mathbb{P}_{p}[A\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{I}]\big]\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in I}\textrm{Rev}(i)\textrm{Infl}_{p;A}(i).

Combining with Russo’s formula (
1.8
) (applied to the subset
I
) gives

(1.11)

\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]=\frac{2}{p(1-p)}\sum_{i\in I}\textrm{Infl}_{p;A}(i)\geq\frac{4}{p(1-p)}\frac{\textrm{Var}_{p}\big[\,\mathbb{P}_{p}[A\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{I}]\big]}{\max_{i\in I}\textrm{Rev}(i)},

where
\partial/\partial p_{i}
denotes the derivative with respect to changing the Bernoulli parameter at coordinate
i
. On the other hand, Proposition
1.19
(with
q\to p
) gives

(1.12)

\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]\mathbb{E}_{p}|N_{I}|}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{p(1-p)}}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{p}[A]|I|\max_{i\in I}\textrm{Rev}(i)}.

Combining (
1.11
)–(
1.12
) and rearranging proves the result.
∎

Remark 1.27
.

If
p=1/2
, the quantity
\textrm{Var}_{p}[\mathbb{P}_{p}[A|\mathcal{F}_{I}]]
has an interpretation as the square-sum of the Fourier coefficients of
\mathds{1}_{A}
supported on non-empty subsets of
I
(see, e.g.,
[
25
]
).

1.5.4.
Other models

Other than Bernoulli and Gaussian percolation, our approach adapts naturally to many other models in the Bernoulli percolation universality class. For instance, both Poisson-Voronoi and Poisson-Boolean percolation could be treated in a similar way (although in the latter case the bounds on
\eta_{1}
may depend on the decay of the radius distribution, and note that only the former model is self-dual in dimension
d=2
). For brevity we do not discuss details here, although see
[
14
]
for a recent application of the entropic bound to the Poisson-Boolean model.

2.
The entropic bound

In this section we prove the entropic bound in Proposition
1.20
, or rather a slight extension of this bound. Suppose
f=q\star W
is either a Gaussian field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
or a continuous Gaussian field on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and assume that
q
has bounded support.

Proposition 2.1

(Entropic bound; extended version)
.

For every
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, compactly supported event
A
,
s>0
, algorithm
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{A}_{s}
that determines
A
, subset
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{s}
, and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

\Big|\mathbb{P}_{\ell}\Big[f+\varepsilon\!\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}q\star\mathds{1}_{S}\in A\Big]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f\in A]\Big|\leq\varepsilon s^{d/2}\!\!\sqrt{\max\Big\{\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A],\mathbb{P}_{\ell}\big[f+\varepsilon\!\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}q\star\mathds{1}_{S}\in A\big]\Big\}\mathbb{E}_{\ell}[N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}]},

where
N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
is the number of boxes in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
that are revealed by
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof of Proposition
1.20
assuming Proposition
2.1
.

Since
\!\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{s}}q\star\mathds{1}_{S}=\int q
, setting
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{s}
in Proposition
2.1
and replacing
\varepsilon\mapsto\varepsilon/\int q
gives Proposition
1.20
.
∎

2.1.
The relative entropy

Our proof of Proposition
2.1
relies on some properties of the relative entropy which we recall here. For
P
and
Q
probability measures on a common measurable space, the
relative entropy (or Kullback-Leibler divergence) from
P
to
Q
is defined as

D_{KL}(P||Q):=\int\log\Big(\frac{dP}{dQ}\Big)\,dP

if
P
is absolutely continuous with respect to
Q
, and
D_{KL}(P||Q):=\infty
otherwise;
D_{KL}(P||Q)
is non-negative by Jensen’s inequality. If
X
and
Y
are random variables taking values in a common measurable space, with respective laws
P
and
Q
, we also write
D_{KL}(X||Y)
for
D_{KL}(P||Q)
. We shall need two basic properties of the relative entropy (see
[
37
, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 3.2]
):

(1)

(Chain rule) Let
X=(X_{1},X_{2})
and
Y=(Y_{1},Y_{2})
be
\mathbb{R}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
-valued mutually absolutely continuous random variables which have densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Then

(2.1)

D_{KL}(X||Y)=D_{KL}(X_{1}||Y_{1})+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim X_{1}}\big[D_{KL}((X_{2}|X_{1}=x)||(Y_{2}|Y_{1}=x))\big].

This expression is well-defined since
G(x):=D_{KL}((X_{2}|X_{1}=x)||(Y_{2}|Y_{1}=x))
is well-defined for an
X_{1}
-almost sure set of values of
x
.

(2)

(Contraction) Let
X
and
Y
be random variables taking values in a common measurable space and let
F
be a measurable map from that space. Then

(2.2)

D_{KL}(X||Y)\geq D_{KL}(F(X)||F(Y)).

We shall also need a simple formula for the relative entropy of stopped sequences of i.i.d. random variables. A
stopping time
for a real-valued sequence
X=(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
is a positive integer
\tau=\tau(X)
such that
\{\tau\geq n+1\}
is determined by
(X_{i})_{i\leq n}
. We define the corresponding
stopped sequence

X^{\tau}=(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\geq 1}
as
X^{\tau}_{i}=X_{i}
for
i\leq\tau
, and
X^{\tau}_{i}=\dagger
for
i>\tau
, where
\dagger
is an arbitrary symbol.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
X=(X_{i})^{n}_{i\geq 1}
and
Y=(Y_{i})^{n}_{i\geq 1}
be finite real-valued sequences of i.i.d. random variables whose respective univariate laws
\mu
and
\nu
are mutually absolutely continuous and have densities with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Let
\tau\leq n
be a stopping time, and let
X^{\tau}
and
Y^{\tau}
be the corresponding stopped sequences. Then

D_{KL}\big(X^{\tau}\big\|Y^{\tau}\big)=\mathbb{E}[\tau(X)]\,D_{KL}(\mu\|\nu).

Proof.

Define
X^{k\wedge\tau}=(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}
and analogously for
Y
. By the chain rule (
2.1
), for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

\displaystyle D_{KL}\big(X^{(k+1)\wedge\tau}\big\|Y^{(k+1)\wedge\tau}\big)

\displaystyle\quad=D_{KL}\big(X^{k\wedge\tau}\big\|Y^{k\wedge\tau}\big)\!+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}}\!\big[D_{KL}\!\left(X^{\tau}_{k+1}\big|(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}\!=\!x\big\|Y^{\tau}_{k+1}\big|(Y^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}\!=\!x\right)\!\big]

\displaystyle\quad=D_{KL}\big(X^{k\wedge\tau}\big\|Y^{k\wedge\tau}\big)\!+\mathbb{E}_{x\sim(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}}\!\big[\mathds{1}_{\tau(X)\geq k+1}D_{KL}\!\left(X_{k+1}\big|(X^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}\!=\!x\big\|Y_{k+1}\big|(Y^{\tau}_{i})_{i\leq k}\!=\!x\right)\!\big]

\displaystyle\quad=D_{KL}\big(X^{k\wedge\tau}\big\|Y^{k\wedge\tau}\big)\!+\mathbb{P}[\tau(X)\geq k+1]D_{KL}(\mu\|\nu)

where in the last step we used that
\tau
is a stopping time. Hence, by induction,

D_{KL}\big(X^{\tau}\big\|Y^{\tau}\big)=\sum_{1\leq k\leq n-1}\mathbb{P}[\tau(X)\geq k+1]D_{KL}(\mu\|\nu)=\mathbb{E}[\tau(X)]D_{KL}(\mu\|\nu).\qed

Finally we need a variant of Pinsker’s inequality:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
P
and
Q
be probability measures on a common measurable space and let
A
be an event. Then

|P(A)-Q(A)|\leq\sqrt{2\max\{P(A),Q(A)\}D_{KL}(P\|Q)}.

Proof.

We use a standard reduction to the binary case. Let
\textrm{Ber}(p)
and
\textrm{Ber}(q)
be Bernoulli random variables with respective parameters
p:=P(A)
and
q:=Q(A)
. By the contraction property (
2.2
)
D_{KL}(P\|Q)\geq D_{KL}(\textrm{Ber}(p)\|\textrm{Ber}(y))
, so it suffices to prove that

(2.3)

(p-q)^{2}\leq 2\max\{p,q\}D_{KL}(\textrm{Ber}(p)\|\textrm{Ber}(q)).

If
p\in\{0,1\}
or
q\in\{0,1\}
then (
2.3
) is trivial since either the right-hand side is at least
1
(if
p\neq q
) or both sides are zero (if
p=q
). On the other hand, if
p,q\in(0,1)
then

(2.4)

\displaystyle D_{KL}(\textrm{Ber}(p)\|\textrm{Ber}(q))

\displaystyle:=p\log\frac{p}{q}+(1-p)\log\frac{1-p}{1-q}=\int_{q}^{p}\frac{p-s}{s(1-s)}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\max\{p,q\}}\int_{q}^{p}(p-s)ds=\frac{1}{2\max\{p,q\}}(p-q)^{2}

where we used that
\sup_{s\in[a,b]}s(1-s)\leq\max\{a,b\}
for
0\leq a\leq b\leq 1
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In the proof we could replace
\max\{p,q\}
with
\min\{\max\{p,q\},1/4\}
, which recovers the classical Pinsker’s inequality
d_{TV}(P,Q):=\sup_{A}|P(A)-Q(A)|\leq\sqrt{D_{KL}(P\|Q)/2}
.

2.2.
Proof of Proposition

Consider

in other words, the change in the law of

|\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[A]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]|\leq\sqrt{2\max\{\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A],\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}[A]\}D_{KL}((X,V)||(Y,V))]}

where

(resp.

Remark 2.5

To prove the Bernoulli version in Proposition

Remark 2.6
.

For comparison we sketch an alternative approach which is similar to arguments in
[

52
,
56
]
; this recovers the differential form of Proposition

(2.7)

\Big|\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}\Big[f+\varepsilon\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}q\star\mathds{1}_{S}\in A\Big]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\Big|\leq s^{d/2}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]\mathbb{E}_{\ell}|N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}|},

Recall the representation

f_{S}(\cdot)+\ell+\varepsilon(q\star\mathds{1}_{S})(\cdot)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\frac{(Z_{S}+\varepsilon s^{d/2})(q\star\mathds{1}_{S})(\cdot)}{s^{d/2}}+\ell+g_{S}(\cdot).

Then the Cameron-Martin formula applied to

gives that

\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}\Big[f+\varepsilon\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}q\star\mathds{1}_{S}\in A\Big]\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}=s^{d/2}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}\textrm{Cov}_{\ell}[\mathds{1}_{A},Z_{S}].

Decompose

and so the second sum vanishes. Hence (

and

where the first equality used that off-diagonal terms are zero by independence, and the second equality again used independence and that

).

4.
Extension to models with unbounded range of dependence

In this section we use approximation arguments to extend our results to models with unbounded range of dependence. We shall assume that
f
satisfies Assumption
1.1
with parameter
\beta>d
, and also that (
POS
) holds.

As well as the intermediate propositions
3.1
–
3.3
, in this section we shall additionally rely on a slight variant of Proposition
3.3
which applies outside the self-dual case:

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose
r>0
is such that
q
is supported on
\Lambda_{r}
. Then for
k\geq 1
there exists
c=c(k)>0
such that, for
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
and
R\geq 8r>0
,

\frac{d^{-}}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\textrm{Cross}_{k}(R)]\geq\frac{c}{\|q\|_{2}}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\textrm{Cross}_{k}(R)]\big(1-\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[\textrm{Cross}_{k}(R)]\big)}{\frac{r}{R}\sum_{i=2}^{R/r}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A_{1}(2r,ir)]}.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

The proof is very similar to that of Proposition
3.3
. Similarly to in Lemma
3.6
, for every
k\geq 1
,
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
, and
R\geq 4r>0
, there is an algorithm in
\mathcal{A}_{r}
determining
\textrm{Cross}_{k}(R)
such that, under
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}
,

\max_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{r}}\textrm{Rev}(S)\leq\frac{4r}{R}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{R/r}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A_{1}(2r,ir)].

Indeed we can define an algorithm which initially reveals a line
L
of the form
L=\{ir\}\times[-kR,kR]^{d-1}
for some uniform random
i
in the given range. Then the algorithm reveals sequentially the set which is connected to that line through primal paths until the occurrence of the crossing event is determined. Then we deduce the result by applying Proposition
3.8
(in the case
s=r
,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{s}
, and
A=\textrm{Cross}_{k}(R)
) to this algorithm.
∎

We shall also need an approximation lemma that allows us to compare a general
f=q\star W
to a field with finite-range dependence. Fix a smooth symmetric cutoff function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
such that
\varphi(x)=1
for
\|x\|_{\infty}\leq 1/2
,
\varphi(x)=0
for
\|x\|_{\infty}\geq 1
. For
r>0
define

(4.1)

f_{r}:=q_{r}\star W

where
q_{r}(x):=q(x)\varphi(|x|/r)
. Note that
q_{r}
is supported on
\Lambda_{r}
, and also, since
q\in L^{1}(\mathbb{X}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{X}^{d})
, as
r\to\infty
,

(4.2)

\|q_{r}\|_{2}\to\|q\|_{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int q_{r}\to\int q.

Using the fact that Assumption
1.1
and (
POS
) hold for
f
, we can verify that these hold also for
f_{r}
for sufficiently large
r
(on the other hand, deducing that (
PA
) holds for
f_{r}
if it holds for
f
seems difficult but we do not need it). In particular, in the self-dual case
\ell_{c}(f_{r})=\ell_{c}(f)=0
.

The following lemma, essentially taken from
[
43
]
, allows us to compare
f
and
f_{r}
. We give details on the proof at the end of the section.

Lemma 4.2
.

There exist
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, for
r,R\geq 2
, increasing event
A
measurable with respect to
f|_{B(R)}
, and
\ell\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left|\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f\in A]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f_{r}\in A]\right|\leq c_{1}\big(R^{d/2}(\log R)r^{-(\beta-d/2)}+e^{-c_{2}(\log R)^{2}}\big).

The same conclusion holds if
A
is the intersection of one increasing and one decreasing event which are both measurable with respect to
f|_{B(R)}
.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.8
:

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

In the proof
c>0
are constants that depend only on
f
(and the choice of the cutoff function
\varphi
in (
4.1
)) and may change from line to line.

The bound
\eta_{1}\leq d-1
, and also
\eta_{1}\leq 1/2
if
d=2
and (
PA
) holds, are rather classical; in fact they are true for any
\beta>d
. For the former, combining
\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]\geq\delta
(the first statement of Lemma
3.4
) with the union bound applied along the hyperplane
\{0\}\times[-kR,kR]
gives
\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(R)]\geq cR^{-(d-1)}
. For the latter, by combining the RSW estimates (the second statement of Lemma
3.4
) with Lemma
4.2
one can deduce (see
[
5
,
43
]
for similar arguments)

\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}\big[\{-R\}\times[-R,R]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle B_{1}(R)}}{{\doublepath}}\{R\}\times[-R,R]\big]\geq c\big(1-R^{1-(\beta-1)}(\log R)\big)\geq c/2

for sufficiently large
R
. By the union bound applied along
\{0\}\times[-R,R]
this implies
\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{2}(R)]\geq cR^{-1}
, and given (
3.1
) we see that
\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(R)]\geq cR^{-1/2}
.

We now prove the remaining bounds, beginning with the first statement. Fix
1>\alpha>\frac{d/2}{\beta-d/2}
and
\eta_{1}>\eta^{\ast}>0
(if
\eta_{1}=0
there is nothing to prove). Then by monotonicity in
\ell
, the union bound, and the definition of
\eta_{1}
,

\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A_{1}(r,R)]\leq\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(r,R)]\leq cr^{d-1}\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(R-r)]\leq r^{d-1}R^{-\eta^{\ast}}

for all
\ell\leq\ell_{c}
,
R
sufficiently large, and
r\in[1,R/2]
. Set
r=R^{\alpha}
. Then by an integral comparison,

\displaystyle\frac{r}{R}\sum_{i=2}^{R/r}\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(2r,ir)]

\displaystyle\leq cr^{-\eta^{\ast}+(d-1)}\times\frac{r}{R}\sum_{i=2}^{R/r}i^{-\eta^{\ast}}

\displaystyle\leq cr^{-\eta^{\ast}+(d-1)}(R/r)^{-\min\{\eta^{\ast},1\}}(\log(R/r))

\displaystyle\leq c(\log R)\big(R^{\alpha(d-1-\eta^{\ast})-(1-\alpha)\min\{\eta^{\ast},1\}}\big)

for
\ell\leq\ell_{c}
and large
R
. Consider the field
f_{r}
defined in (
4.1
). By Lemma
4.2
,

\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(r^{\prime},R)]\leq\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A_{1}(r^{\prime},R)]+cR^{d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)}(\log R)+ce^{-c(\log R)^{2}}

for
\ell\leq\ell_{c}
and
2\leq r^{\prime}\leq R
, and hence

\frac{r}{R}\sum_{i=2}^{R/r}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(2r,ir)]\leq c(\log R)\big(R^{\alpha(d-1-\eta^{\ast})-(1-\alpha)\min\{\eta^{\ast},1\}}+R^{d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)}\big)

for
\ell\leq\ell_{c}
and large
R
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.4
there are
\delta>0
and
\ell^{\prime}=\ell^{\prime}(R)\leq\ell_{c}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]=\delta
. Hence, again by Lemma
4.2
,

\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[f_{r}\in\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]\big(1-\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[f_{r}\in\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]\big)\geq\delta(1-\delta)-cR^{d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)}(\log R)\geq\delta(1-\delta)/2

for large
R
, where we used that
\alpha>\frac{d/2}{\beta-d/2}
.

We now apply Propositions
3.2
and
4.1
to the field
f_{r}
at the sequence of levels
\ell^{\prime}(R)\leq\ell_{c}
. First, by Proposition
3.2
(recalling (
4.2
))

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f_{r}\in\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]\Big|_{\ell=\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c\delta(1-\delta)}{\|q_{r}\|_{2}}\Big(\frac{r}{R}\sum_{i=2}^{R/r}\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(2r,ir)]\Big)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq c(\log R)^{-1}\big(R^{\alpha(d-1-\eta^{\ast})-(1-\alpha)\min\{\eta^{\ast},1\}}+R^{d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)}\big)^{-1}

for large
R
. Similarly, by Proposition
3.1
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[f_{r}\in\textrm{Cross}_{5}(R)]\Big|_{\ell=\ell^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{d/2}\Big(\mathbb{P}_{l^{\prime}}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(2r,R)]\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq cR^{d/2}\big(R^{-\eta^{\ast}+\alpha(d-1)}+R^{d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)}(\log R)\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq c\sqrt{\log R}\big(R^{d/2-\eta^{\ast}/2+\alpha(d-1)/2}+R^{3d/4-\alpha(\beta-d/2)/2}\big)

for large
R
, where we used that
\sqrt{a+b}\leq\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}
for

. Comparing (

) and expanding the brackets we deduce that at least one of the exponents

\displaystyle E_{1}:=\big(3d/4-\alpha(\beta-d/2)/2\big)+\big(d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)\big)

\displaystyle E_{2}:=\big(d/2-\eta^{\ast}/2+\alpha(d-1)/2\big)+\big(d/2-\alpha(\beta-d/2)\big)

\displaystyle E_{3}:=\big(d/2-\eta^{\ast}/2+\alpha(d-1)/2\big)+\big(\alpha(d-1-\eta^{\ast})-(1-\alpha)\min\{\eta^{\ast},1\}\big)

. The second implies that

. For the second statement, fix

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(r^{\prime},R^{\prime})]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(r^{\prime},R^{\prime})]|

for large

, for large

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(R)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[A_{1}(R)]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{1}(R)]\leq 2(\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(R)]-\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[f_{r}\in A_{1}(R)])

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2r^{d/2}(\ell^{\prime}-\ell_{c})}{\int q}\sqrt{\mathbb{P}_{\ell^{\prime}}[A_{1}(R)]\!\!\sum_{v\in r\mathbb{Z}^{d}\cap\Lambda_{R+2r}}\!\!\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[\Lambda_{2r}\longleftrightarrow v+\Lambda_{2r}]}

and

. By the RSW estimates (the second statement of Lemma

) and Lemma

\mathbb{P}_{\ell_{c}}[A_{2}(R)]\geq c_{2}R^{-2/3-5/(3(\beta-1))-\varepsilon}.

) (recall that here

5.
The Russo-type inequality for Gaussian fields and applications

In this section we prove the Russo-type inequality in Proposition
1.23
, and deduce Theorem
1.16
as an application. We emphasise that in this section (
POS
)–(
PA
) play no role.

The main idea in the proof of Proposition
1.23
, which distinguishes it from the approach in
[
43
]
(see Proposition
1.22
), is to use an orthonormal decomposition of each
f_{S}
to interpret
\frac{d^{-}}{d\ell}\mathbb{P}_{\ell}[A]
and the resampling influences
\textrm{Infl}_{\ell;A}(S)
as measuring, respectively, the ‘boundary’ and ‘volume’ of certain sets in Gaussian space. Then we apply Gaussian isoperimetry to deduce the result. For a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote

E^{+\varepsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{ there exists }y\in E\text{ s.t. }\|x-y\|_{2}\leq\varepsilon\}

to be the
\varepsilon
-thickening of

Proposition 5.1

(Gaussian isoperimetry)
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that, for every measurable
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}[X\in E^{+\varepsilon}\setminus E]\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\mathbb{P}[X\in E](1-\mathbb{P}[X\in E])\varepsilon-c\varepsilon^{2}

where
X
is an
n
-dimensional standard Gaussian vector.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(x)
and
\Phi(x)
denote the standard normal pdf and cdf respectively. The classical Gaussian isoperimetric inequality states that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{-1}\mathbb{P}[X\in E^{+\varepsilon}\setminus E]\geq\varphi(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbb{P}[X\in E])).

A simple consequence (see, e.g.,
[
38
, Eq. (3)]
) is that, for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

(5.1)

\mathbb{P}[X\in E^{+\varepsilon}]\geq\Phi(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbb{P}[X\in E])+\varepsilon).

By Taylor expanding
\Phi
on the right-hand side of (
5.1
) we have

\mathbb{P}[X\in E^{+\varepsilon}\setminus E]\geq\varepsilon\varphi(\Phi^{-1}(\mathbb{P}[X\in E]))-\frac{1}{2}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\varepsilon^{2},

and the result follows since, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\varphi(x)\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\,\Phi(x)(1-\Phi(x))
(as can be seen from the fact that the Mill’s ratio
(1-\Phi(x))/\varphi(x)
is decreasing on
x\geq 0
), and since
|\varphi^{\prime}(x)|
is bounded over
\mathbb{R}
.
∎

We use the following orthogonal decomposition of

(see Proposition

in the appendix). Let

be a sequence of i.i.d. standard normal random variables and let

be an orthonormal basis of

(5.2)

f^{n}_{S}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}^{n}Z_{i}(q\star\varphi_{i})\Rightarrow f_{S},

in law with respect to the

-topology.

1.23
.

(Orthogonal decomposition of

)

u(x):=\mathbb{E}[g(x)^{2}]
. Then

\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\int_{\Omega}g\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}g_{n}\,d\mu\Big|\Big]\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\big(g(x)-g_{n}(x)\big)^{2}\big]\Big)^{1/2}\,|\mu|(dx)\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big(u(x)-u_{n}(x)\Big)^{1/2}|\mu|(dx).

Since
u_{n}\to u
monotonically, by Dini’s theorem the convergence is uniform on
\Omega
, so we have that
\mathbb{E}[|\int_{\Omega}g\,d\mu-\int_{\Omega}g_{n}\,d\mu|]\to 0
as required.
∎

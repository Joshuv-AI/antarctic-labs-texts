Abstract
We propose reaching steps towards the real-time strain control of multiphysics, multiscale continuum soft robots. To study this problem fundamentally, we ground ourselves in a model-based control setting enabled by mathematically precise dynamics of a soft robot prototype. Poised to integrate, rather than reject, inherent mechanical nonlinearity for embodied compliance, we first separate the original robot dynamics into two separate subdynamics — aided by a perturbing time-scale separation parameter. Second, we prescribe a set of stabilizing nonlinear backstepping controllers for regulating the resulting subsystems’ strain dynamics. Third, we study the interconnected singularly perturbed system by analyzing and establishing its stability. Fourth, our theories are backed up by fast numerical results on a single arm of the Octopus robot arm. We demonstrate strain regulation to equilibrium, in a significantly reduced time, of the whole-body reduced-order dynamics of infinite degrees-of-freedom soft robots. This paper communicates our thinking within the backdrop of embodied intelligence: it informs our conceptualization, formulation, computational setup, and yields improved control performance for the nonlinear control of infinite degrees-of-freedom soft robots.

Introduction

Soft manipulators, inspired by the functional role of living organisms’ soft tissues, provide better compliance and configurability compared to their rigid counterparts. In proof-of-concept studies and in certain real-world cases, they have found applications in delicate 6D dexterous bending and whole-arm manipulation tasks

Despite their attractiveness, rigid robots are still the go-to mechanism in many automation tasks today. How can we bridge this divide for soft robot adoption in everyday automation? In this paper, we argue that a sustained research effort for developing real-time computational tools for interaction modeling and control will be the key to wide adoption.

Soft robots are multiphysics systems that generate physically heterogeneous interactions from muscle activation to contact and adhesion with the environment in an embodied intelligence fashion
[

Embodied intelligence stipulates that rather than reject external mechanical processes that impede performance, a robot should leverage its shape, geometry of components, along with constraints in the external environment to achieve its desired configuration. Given that the nonlinear deformation of soft robots occur at multiple scales: from millimeters (in their continuum deformation characterization) to meters (in their overarching compliance strategy), we are poised with the fast and precise control of soft robots. To systematically dissect the problem, we focus on model-based control methods. This is attractive since the long time scales required to computationally resolve models and control has been a drawback for their ubiquitous adoption.

We take a holistic approach that includes modeling, applied mathematics and control, and fast scientific computing schemes to solve the multiscale problem constrained by the robot’s multiphysics. Being a continuum phenomenon, the default machinery for soft robot analyses are nonlinear partial differential equations (PDEs). However, nonlinear PDE theory is tedious and computationally intensive for realizing computationally fast and compliant behavior in soft robots. {\bm{s}}_{\text{slow}}-\epsilon\bar{\bm{M}}_{21}^{p}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\bm{s}_{\text{fast}}\end{bmatrix}+

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}\\
\bm{u}_{\text{slow}}-\epsilon\bar{\bm{M}}_{21}^{p}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}\end{bmatrix}

(19)

which is in the standard singularly perturbed form (
7
).

III-A
1
Fast subsystem dynamics extraction

Consider the fast time scale
T=t/\epsilon
, with
dT/dt=1/\epsilon
. The dynamics on this time scale is
\dot{\bm{z}}_{\text{fast}}=\dfrac{d\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}}{dt}\equiv\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}\dfrac{d\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}}{dT}\triangleq\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime}
and
\epsilon\dot{\bm{z}}_{\text{slow}}=\bm{z}_{\text{slow}}^{\prime}
. Rewriting (
19
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}&\epsilon\bar{\bm{M}}^{p}_{12}\\
\bm{0}&\bar{\bm{M}}_{22}^{p}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{z}^{\prime}_{\text{fast}}\\
\bm{z}^{\prime}_{\text{slow}}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon{\bm{s}}_{\text{fast}}\\
{\bm{s}}_{\text{slow}}-\epsilon\bar{\bm{M}}_{21}^{p}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\bm{s}_{\text{fast}}\end{bmatrix}+

\displaystyle\quad\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}\\
\bm{u}_{\text{slow}}-\epsilon\bar{\bm{M}}_{21}^{p}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}\end{bmatrix},

(20)

or,

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\prime}_{\text{fast}}=\epsilon\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}({\bm{s}}_{\text{fast}}+\bm{u}_{\text{fast}})-\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\bm{\mathcal{H}}^{\text{fast}}_{\text{slow}}\bm{z}^{\prime}_{\text{slow}}

(21a)

\displaystyle\bm{z}^{\prime}_{\text{slow}}=\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{slow}}^{-1}({\bm{s}}_{\text{slow}}-\bm{u}_{\text{slow}})-\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}(\bm{s}_{\text{fast}}-\bm{u}_{\text{fast}})

(21b)

where the slow variables are frozen on this fast time scale.

III-A
2
Slow sub-dynamics

To extract the slow subdynamics, we let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
in (
19
), so that what is left, i.e,

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{z}}_{\text{slow}}=\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{slow}}^{-1}({\bm{s}}_{\text{slow}}+\bm{u}_{\text{slow}})

(22)

constitutes the system’s slow dynamics, where the fast components are frozen on this slow time scale.

We seek a
multi-rate feedback backstepping controller

[\bm{q}(t)^{\top},\dot{\bm{q}}(t)^{\top}]^{\top}

[\bm{q}^{d\top},\dot{\bm{q}}^{d\top}]^{\top}

1

Let us first consider the velocity component of the fast subdynamics in (

t_{f}\triangleq T\triangleq
.
Consider the transformation
[\bm{\theta}^{\top},\bm{\phi}^{\top}]^{\top}=[\bm{q}^{\top}_{\text{fast}},\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}^{\top}]^{\top}
where
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}=\epsilon\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}

\bm{\nu}

\bm{\theta}^{\prime}=\bm{\nu}
and let
\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{d}=[\bm{\xi}_{1}^{d},\ldots,\bm{\xi}_{n_{\xi}}^{d}]_{\text{fast}}^{\top}

Theorem 1

The control law

\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{d}(t_{f})-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}(t_{f})+\bm{q}^{\prime d}_{\text{fast}}(t_{f})

is sufficient to guarantee an exponential stability of the origin of
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}=\bm{\nu}
such that for all

,
\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}(t_{f})\in S
for a compact set
S\subset\mathbb{R}^{6N}
. That is,
\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}(t_{f})
remains bounded as
t_{f}\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof:

Define the tracking error and corresponding error dynamics as

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}-\bm{q}^{d}_{\text{fast}},\,\implies\bm{e}_{1}^{\prime}=\bm{\theta}^{\prime}-\bm{q}^{\prime^{d}}_{\text{fast}}\triangleq\bm{\nu}-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime^{d}}.

(23a)

Consider the following candidate Lyapunov function,

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{1}(\bm{e}_{1})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}

(24)

where
\bm{K}_{p}
is a diagonal matrix of positive damping (gains).
Ignoring the templated arguments for ease of readability, for a constant
\bm{q}^{d}_{\text{fast}}
, we must have

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}{\bm{e}}^{\prime}_{1}=\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}(\bm{\nu}-{\bm{q}}^{\prime d}_{\text{fast}}).

(25)

Set
\bm{\nu}={\bm{q}}^{\prime d}_{\text{fast}}-\bm{e}_{1}
, then

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{1}^{\prime}=-\bm{e}_{1}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}\leq 2\bm{V}_{1}.

(26)

That is for,
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bm{e}_{1}(t)=0
the control law
{\bm{q}}^{\prime d}_{\text{fast}}-\bm{e}_{1}\triangleq\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{d}-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}+\bm{q}^{\prime d}
implies an exponentially stable origin of the subsystem hence satisfying Assumption
1
. ∎

Stability analysis of the strain twist subdynamics

Theorem 2

Under the tracking error
\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{\phi}-\bm{\nu}
and matrices

\displaystyle\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}[\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime\prime d}+\bm{e}_{1}-2\bm{e}_{2}-\bm{K}_{q}^{\top}(\bm{K}_{q}\bm{K}_{q}^{\top})^{-1}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}]

Proof:

First recall that

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{\theta}^{\prime}-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime d}\triangleq\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime d}+(\bm{\nu}-\bm{\nu})

(28b)

Now, consider the whole nonlinear fast subsystem (

\displaystyle\bm{e}_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bm{\phi}^{\prime}-\bm{\nu}^{\prime}=\bm{z}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime}+\bm{e}_{1}^{\prime}-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime\prime d}

(29)

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{fast}}^{-1}\left[\epsilon\bm{u}_{\text{fast}}+\epsilon\bm{s}_{\text{fast}}-\bm{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{slow}}^{\text{fast}}\bm{z}_{\text{slow}}^{\prime}\right]+(\bm{e}_{2}-\bm{e}_{1})-\bm{q}_{\text{fast}}^{\prime\prime d}.

Suppose that we choose the Lyapunov candidate function

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{2}(\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2})=\bm{V}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\bm{e}_{2}^{\top}\bm{K}_{q}\bm{e}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}[\bm{e}_{1}\,\,\bm{e}_{2}]\begin{bmatrix}\bm{K}_{p}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}&\bm{K}_{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{e}_{1}\\
\bm{e}_{2}\end{bmatrix},

it can be verified that

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle(\bm{e}_{1},\bm{e}_{2})=\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}^{\prime}+\bm{e}_{2}^{\top}\bm{K}_{q}\bm{e}_{2}^{\prime}

(30a)

(30b)

Substituting the value of

\displaystyle=\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}(\bm{e}_{2}-\bm{e}_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad-\bm{e}_{2}^{\top}\bm{K}_{q}\left(\bm{e}_{2}-\bm{K}_{q}^{\top}(\bm{K}_{q}\bm{K}_{q}^{\top})^{-1}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\bm{e}_{1}^{\top}\bm{K}_{p}\bm{e}_{1}-\bm{e}_{2}^{\top}\bm{K}_{q}\bm{e}_{2}\triangleq-2\bm{V}_{2}\leq 0.

VI
Conclusion

In the quest towards the adoption of soft robots in everyday automation processes, we identified that the long processing times for computing models and controllers/policies is a significant drawback. To circumvent this, we introduced a singularly perturbed technique for decomposing system dynamics to a fast and slower subdynamics, respectively. Stabilizing nonlinear backstepping controllers were introduced to the respective subdynamics to further improve computation times. The fast part was controlled at a finer resolution while the slower part was controlled at a more coarse resolution, with the overall scheme executed in a decentralized fashion. We found that our results do not merely regulate particulate strain states but also achieve desired equilibrium faster and better compared to other reported schemes. Our approach takes a further step towards replicating embodied intelligence
[
8
]
in soft robots that mimic the behavior of living matter by engrossing hierarchy layers in soft robots’ dynamics and control computational schemes.

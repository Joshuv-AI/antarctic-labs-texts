Bounding the Frobenius norm of a q q -deformed commutator
Dariusz Chruściński, Gen Kimura, Hiromichi Ohno, Tanmay Singal

Abstract
For two n × n n\times n complex matrices A A and B B , we define the q q -deformed commutator as [ A , B ] q ≔ A ​ B − q ​ B ​ A [\ A,B\ ]_{q}\ \coloneqq\ AB-qBA for a real parameter q q . In this paper, we investigate a generalization of the Böttcher-Wenzel inequality which gives the sharp upper bound of the (Frobenius) norm of the commutator. In our generalisation, we investigate sharp upper bounds on the q q -deformed commutator. This generalization can be studied in two different scenarios: firstly bounds for general matrices, and secondly for traceless matrices. For both scenarios, partial answers and conjectures are given for positive and negative q q . In particular, denoting the Frobenius norm by | | . | | F \left|\left|\ .\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}} , when either A A or B B is normal, we prove the following inequality to be true and sharp: | | [ A , B ] q | | F 2 ≤ ( 1 + q 2 ) ​ | | A | | F 2 ​ | | B | | F 2 \left|\left|\ [\ A,B\ ]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\leq\left(1+q^{2}\right)\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{{2}}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{{2}} for positive q q . Also, we conjecture that the same bound is true for positive q q when either A A or B B is traceless. For negative q q , we conjecture other sharp upper bounds to be true for the generic scenarios and the scenario when either of A A or B B is traceless. All conjectures are supported with numerics and proved for n = 2 n=2 .

[Case 1-1] For negative

\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\leq\left(1-q\right)^{2}\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}.

\left|\left|\ AB+BA\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\leq 4\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}.

[Case 1-2] For

We find that imposing tracelessness on either

[Case 2-1] For

[Case 2-2] For

Proposition 1

Let

{\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}}\ \leq\ \left(1+q^{2}\right)\ \left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{(2),2}^{2}\ \left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2},

Proof.

Let
A

is a diagonal matrix

. Let

\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=\sum_{ij}|b_{ij}|^{2}\left(|a_{i}|^{2}+q^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}-2q\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\overline{a_{j}}a_{i}\right).

Since

-2q\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\overline{a_{j}}a_{i}\ \leq\ \left(1-\delta_{ij}\right)\left(|a_{j}|^{2}+q^{2}|a_{i}|^{2}\right),

and hence

\displaystyle|a_{i}|^{2}+q^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}-2q\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\overline{a_{j}}a_{i}\ \leq\ \left(1+q^{2}\right)\left(|a_{i}|^{2}+\left(1-\delta_{ij}\right)|a_{j}|^{2}\right)\ \leq\ \left(1+q^{2}\right)\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{(2),2}^{2},

which proves the result as
\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=\sum_{ij}|b_{ij}|^{2}
. The bound is attained e.g. by matrices

with

∎

{\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}}\ \leq\ \left(1+q^{2}\right)\ \left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\ \left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{(2),2}^{2}\

Conjecture 1

For any

Proposition 3

We give two different proofs of Proposition

Proof.

Note that, as is easily shown, a traceless

]
). Thus, we can assume that

a_{21}&0\end{pmatrix}}
. Let

b_{21}&b_{22}\end{pmatrix}}

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(1+q^{2})\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}-\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+q^{2})|a_{12}|^{2}|b_{12}|^{2}+(1+q^{2})|a_{21}|^{2}|b_{21}|^{2}

\displaystyle{}+|a_{12}|^{2}(|b_{11}|^{2}+q^{2}|b_{22}|^{2})+|a_{21}|^{2}(|b_{22}|^{2}+q^{2}|b_{11}|^{2})

\displaystyle{}+q(|a_{12}|^{2}+|a_{21}|^{2})(b_{11}\overline{b_{22}}+\overline{b_{11}}b_{22})+2q(a_{12}\overline{a_{21}}b_{21}\overline{b_{12}}+\overline{a_{12}}a_{21}\overline{b_{21}}b_{12})

\displaystyle(1\mp q)^{2}(|a_{12}|^{2}|b_{12}|^{2}+|a_{21}|^{2}|b_{21}|^{2})+|a_{12}|^{2}|b_{11}+qb_{22}|^{2}

\Delta_{i}=\left(\mathrm{tr}{M_{i}}\right)^{2}-4\ \mathrm{det}M_{i}
. If
\left|\ 4\ \mathrm{det}M_{i}\ \right|\leq\left(\mathrm{tr}{M_{i}}\right)^{2}
, then both eigenvalues of
M_{i}
are smaller than
\mathrm{tr}{M_{i}}
. For both
i=1,2
, we see that
\mathrm{tr}{M_{i}}=\left(1+q^{2}\right)\left(|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}\right)
, and
\mathrm{det}M_{i}=(1-q^{2})^{2}|ab|^{2}
. Since
2|ab|\leq|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}
, we have that
\left|\ 4\ \mathrm{det}M_{i}\ \right|\leq\left(\mathrm{tr}{M_{i}}\right)^{2}
. Noting that
\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=|a|^{2}+|b|^{2}
, the inequality is proved.
∎

Remark 1
.

Note that if both
A
and
B
are traceless, then (
7
) follows from the properties of the Lie algebra
su(2)
. Indeed, for
A=\bm{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma}
and
B=\bm{b}\cdot\bm{\sigma}
with
\bm{a},\bm{b}\in\mathop{\mathbb{C}}\nolimits^{3}
one easily computes

[A,B]_{q}=(1-q)\bm{a}{\cdot}\bm{b}\,\mathop{\mathbb{I}}\nolimits+i(1+q)(\bm{a}\times\bm{b})\cdot\bm{\sigma},

(20)

and hence

\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=2\Big((1-q)^{2}|\bm{a}{\cdot}\bm{b}|^{2}+(1+q)^{2}|\bm{a}\times\bm{b}|^{2}\Big),

(21)

where we have used

{\rm Tr}[(\bm{a}\cdot\bm{\sigma})(\bm{b}\cdot\bm{\sigma})]=2\bm{a}{\cdot}\bm{b}.

Hence using

|\bm{a}{\cdot}\bm{b}|^{2}\leq|\bm{a}|^{2}|\bm{b}|^{2}\ ,\ \ \ |\bm{a}\times\bm{b}|^{2}\leq|\bm{a}|^{2}|\bm{b}|^{2},

one obtains

\displaystyle\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\leq 4(1+q^{2})|\bm{a}|^{2}|\bm{b}|^{2}=(1+q^{2})\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}.

(22)

Remark 2
.

Note that in the special case when
\mathrm{tr}{A}=0
and
\mathrm{rank}A=1
, then the conjecture is satisfied. Indeed, let
\left\{|e_{i}\rangle\right\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the standard basis for
\mathop{\mathbb{C}}\nolimits^{n}
. When
\mathrm{tr}{A}=0
and
\mathrm{rank}A=1
, we can employ a unitary similarity transformation, so that its singular value decomposition is then given in the following form:
A=a|e_{1}\rangle\langle e_{2}|
, where
a\geq 0
. Hence
A
is now a traceless matrix, and its non-zero matrix elements are limited to the upper left block of size
2\times 2
. By following the same steps using the proof of Proposition
3
, the result is proved.

1

When

, it seems that the bound will depend on

. For

Let
A
and
B
be
n\times n
trace-less diagonal matrices defined by

A=B={\rm diag}[n-1,-1,\cdots,-1].

Then,

\|A\|^{2}=\|B\|^{2}=(n-1)^{2}+(-1)^{2}(n-1)=n(n-1).

Also note that

AB={\rm diag}[(n-1)^{2},1,\cdots,1],

and therefore,

\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=(1-q)^{2}\left|\left|\ AB\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}=\left(1-q\right)^{2}((n-1)^{4}+(n-1))=\left(1-q\right)^{2}n(n-1)(n^{2}-3n+3).

This implies

\frac{\left|\left|\ [A,B]_{q}\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}}{\left|\left|\ A\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}\left|\left|\ B\ \right|\right|_{\mathbf{\mathrm{F}}}^{2}}=g(n)\left(1-q\right)^{2},

(23)

where

g(n)=\frac{n^{2}-3n+3}{n(n-1)}.

(24)

For
n=2,3

. Since
\frac{\left(1-q\right)^{2}}{2}\leq\left(1+q^{2}\right)

\displaystyle q_{\min}(n)

(25a)

\displaystyle q_{\max}(n)

Then, for any

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the generalization of BW inequality with
q
-deformed commutator in two scenarios.
In the first scenario, the bound for general matrices is investigated: for
q<0
, the sharp bound is trivially given by (
5
).
For positive
q
, the sharp bound with normal matrices is given by (
7
).
However, it seems to be far from trivial to give an upper bound for the general case.
In the second scenario, we study the bound under the constraint that one of the matrices is traceless, and this constraint is founded on the idea that the trace of the participating matrices is irrelevant in the BW-inequality. For both positive and negative
q
, we give conjectures
1
and
2
which are numerically supported and are proved for
n=2
.

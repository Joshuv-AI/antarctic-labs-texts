Long time behaviour and turnpike solutions in mildly non-monotone mean field games
Marco Cirant, and, Alessio Porretta

Abstract
We consider mean field game systems in time-horizon ( 0 , T ) (0,T) , where the individual cost functional depends locally on the density distribution of the agents, and the Hamiltonian is locally uniformly convex. We show that, even if the coupling cost functions are mildly non-monotone, then the system is still well posed due to the effect of individual noise. The rate of anti-monotonicity (i.e. the aggregation rate of the cost functions) which can be afforded depends on the intensity of the diffusion and on global bounds of solutions. We give applications to either the case of globally Lipschitz Hamiltonians or the case of quadratic Hamiltonians and couplings having mild growth. Under similar conditions, we investigate the long time behavior of solutions and we give a complete description of the ergodic and long term properties of the system. In particular we prove: (i) the turnpike property of solutions in the finite (long) horizon ( 0 , T ) (0,T) , (ii) the convergence of the system from ( 0 , T ) (0,T) towards ( 0 , ∞ ) (0,\infty) , (iii) the vanishing discount limit of the infinite horizon problem and the long time convergence towards the ergodic stationary solution. This way we extend previous results which were known only for the case of monotone and smoothing couplings; our approach is self-contained and does not need the use of the linearized system or of the master equation.

Introduction

The theory of mean field games was initiated by J.-M. Lasry and P.-L. Lions since 2006 (
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
) in order to describe Nash equilibrium configurations in multi-agents strategic interactions. Similar ideas appeared in
[
21
]
, at the same time. Mostly peculiar to the approach suggested by Lasry and Lions is to describe the equilibria through solutions of a forward-backward system of PDEs involving a Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation for the value function of a single agent and a Kolmogorov-Fokker-Planck equation for the distribution law of the whole population. The simplest form of this kind of system is the following

\begin{cases}-u_{t}-\kappa\Delta u+H(x,Du)=F(x,m(t)),&t\in(0,T)\\
m_{t}-\kappa\Delta m-{\rm div}(mH_{p}(x,Du))=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
m(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\qquad u(x,T)=G(x,m(T))\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u(t,x)
represents the optimal value for a player at time
t
in state
x
, while
m(t,x)
represents the density of the distribution law of the controlled dynamical state.
The typical interpretation of the system is that any single player controls his/her dynamical state (in a standard probability space where a given
d
-dimensional Brownian motion is defined)

\begin{cases}dX_{\tau}=\alpha_{\tau}\,d\tau+\kappa\,dB_{\tau}&\\
X_{t}=x&\end{cases}\rightsquigarrow\quad u(t,x)=\inf_{\{\alpha_{\tau}\}}{\mathbb{E}}\left\{{\small\int_{t}^{T}[L(X_{\tau},\alpha_{\tau})+F(X_{\tau},m_{\tau})]+G(X_{T},m_{T})}\right\}

where
\kappa>0
and
L,F,G
represent different costs depending on the control process
\{\alpha_{\tau}\}
as well as on the measures
\{m_{\tau}\}
. Here
m
enters as an exogeneous
datum and stays fixed in the agents’optimization; so defining the Hamiltonian
H(x,p):=\sup_{q}[-q\cdot p-L(x,q)]
, under suitable convexity and smoothness conditions the value function satisfy the first equation in (
1.1
) and
\alpha_{t}=-H_{p}(X_{t},Du(t,X_{t}))
is the optimal feedback control. Assuming consistency with the agents’ rational anticipations, at equilibrium it happens that the distribution law of the optimal controlled process coincides with the family of measures
m_{t}
used in the individual optimization. Thus
m
satisfies the second equation as the law of the optimal process, where
\alpha
corresponds to the optimal feedback strategy.
We refer to
[
6
]
for an extended introduction to mean field games systems.

In all the above presentation, the state space could be differently chosen, together with possibly boundary effects (reflection or absorption effects at the boundary, for instance)
but we will assume here the simplest, yet instructive case of periodic setting. This means that
x
belongs to the flat torus
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/{{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
.

This is a typical setting to investigate ergodic properties and long time behavior of the controlled dynamics.
This kind of question is very natural in control theory and quite popular in applications. Many economic models, for instance, expect that if the time horizon is long then the optimal strategies are nearly stationary for most of the time. This is referred to as the
turnpike property
of optimal control problems, according to a terminology
introduced by P.A. Samuelson in 1949 (see
[
15
]
). Even if the
turnpike theory
is a longstanding topic in optimal control, it has attracted an increasing renovated interest in the last years from both theoretical and applicative viewpoint: it is impossible here to mention all contributions in this direction, we refer e.g. to
[
14
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
33
]
and references therein.

When coming at mean field game systems as in (
1.1
), the long time behavior was investigated in several papers under the assumption that the cost functions
F,G
are nondecreasing in
m
. It is well established in the theory that this monotonicity condition gives uniqueness and stability of solutions; under the same condition the turnpike property and the convergence of solutions towards the stationary ergodic state have been first proved
in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
for quadratic Hamiltonian (i.e.
H(x,p)=|p|^{2}
). Milder statements (time average convergence) or stronger statements (exponential pointwise decay estimates) were obtained according to different sets of assumptions.
The case of discrete time, finite states system was analyzed in
[
17
]
. Later on, the long time behavior was completely described in
[
5
]
in case of smoothing couplings and uniformly convex Hamiltonian, and in
[
29
]
for the case of local couplings and globally Lipschitz Hamiltonian. We also point out that the turnpike pattern is clearly shown in many numerical simulations, see e.g.
[
1
]
.

The purpose of this paper is twofold. On one hand we wish to show that the monotonicity of the couplings
F,G
can be relaxed to some extent, using the diffusive character of the equations. Otherwise said, we show that
the Brownian noise in the individual dynamics can compensate, to some extent, the lack of monotonicity
so that mildly aggregative cost functions
F,G
may not affect the uniqueness of solutions and their stability, even in long time.
This was already suggested by P.-L. Lions in the early stages of the theory (
[
27
]
) although we could not find any further development of this issue in the literature.
On another hand, still in the context of monotone or mildly non-monotone couplings, we revisit both the long time behavior of solutions and the ergodic limits in order to clarify the full picture: turnpike estimates for solutions in
(0,T)
, pointwise limit of the system as
T\to\infty
, vanishing discount limit in the infinite horizon problem.

The above issues are somehow related. In fact, the complete characterization of the long time behavior, namely the convergence of
u^{T}(t)-\lambda(T-t)
and
m^{T}(t)
at any time
t
, was previously obtained in
[
5
]
as a byproduct of the long time convergence of the master equation. The master equation is an infinite dimensional equation
(defined on time, space and the Wasserstein space of probability measures) which gives the value function
u
as a feedback of the measure
m
. This equation is not easy
to handle and plays a similar role as the Riccati equation for the feedback operator in control systems. Establishing the long time behavior of the master equation allows one to
completely describe the behavior of the system but is actually a hard result which requires much stronger conditions, starting from the smoothness of the functions
F,G
.
In addition, what is more relevant here, when the couplings are not monotone, the master equation can not be properly used, since no satisfactory notion of solution has been developed so far outside the monotone case. Therefore, motivated by a setting of mildly nonmonotone cost functions
F,G
, we refine and develop some of the arguments introduced in
[
5
]
in order to study the long time convergence - as well as the vanishing discount limit -
without any use of the master equation
. The approach that we propose here is actually simpler and only relying on the PDE system; this seems to be much more flexible and it is actually promising for many other situations where the master equation still looks untractable (e.g. the case of state constraint problems, congestion models, etc…).

Let us mention that other nonmonotone mean field games have been considered in several works previously: for example, second-order problems have been analyzed in
[
9
,
12
,
13
]
, while
[
7
,
18
]
deal with first-order systems. The works by Ambrose (see
[
2
]
and references therein) show the existence of solutions to second-order systems under smallness conditions on the coupling, though these conditions seem to be depending on the time horizon
T
. Tran considers in
[
31
]
a setting which is nonmonotone in a broader sense: the coupling in the system is increasing, but the Hamiltonian is not convex. Though he does not address the long-time analysis, his uniqueness results are closer to ours, as he obtains uniqueness under smallness conditions on
H
, which are independent of the time horizon.

In Section 3, we first show that, for given

A similar result is also proved for the stationary ergodic problem

\begin{cases}\bar{\lambda}-\kappa\Delta\bar{u}+H(x,D\bar{u})=F(x,\bar{m}),\\
-\kappa\Delta\bar{m}-{\rm div}(\bar{m}H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}))=0,\\
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{m}=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\bar{u}=0.\end{cases}

(1.2)

We suggest two sets of assumptions under which this kind of result can be applied, assuming for simplicity that the final cost is a given function
u_{T}(x)
:

(A)

the case of globally Lipschitz (and locally uniformly convex) Hamiltonian.

This corresponds to typical control problems with smooth, uniformly convex, Lagrangian cost and controls in a compact set. Thanks to the global Lipschitz bound of

, in this case the growth of

can be arbitrary, for instance one can take

(B)

the case of superlinear, uniformly convex, Hamiltonian, with quadratic-like growth, and cost function

which satisfies
0\geq F(x,m)\geq-\gamma\,m^{\alpha}
with
\alpha<\frac{2}{d}
.

The threshold
\frac{2}{d}
for the growth of the coupling is not new in the context of mean field game systems with quadratic Hamiltonian (see e.g.
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
19
]
), and we comment this issue in Remark
3.10
.

Let us stress that, in the aforementioned results, the admissible threshold

of anti-monotonicity depends on the diffusivity constant
\kappa
and on the initial datum (through
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
). This latter fact explains very well why the master equation is hardly usable, in this context; indeed, there is no general feedback policy of

in the space of probability measures (unless we reduce to the case of monotone couplings).

In Section 4 we show that, in the same context given above, the solutions
(u^{T},m^{T})
of (
1.1
) satisfy an exponential turnpike property. More precisely,
if
\|Du^{T}\|_{\infty}
is bounded in
(1,T-1)
independently of

, then there exists
\omega>0
and

(independent of

) such that

\|m^{T}(t)-\bar{m}\|_{\infty}+\|Du^{T}(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{\infty}\leq M(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in(1,T-1)\,,

where
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
is a stationary state. See Theorem
4.1
and Corollary

.

In particular, this result applies to the examples (A), (B) mentioned above. It is to be noted that this result is independent of initial and terminal conditions, as is customary in turnpike theory.

•

In Section 5, we describe the convergence of
u^{T}(t),m^{T}(t)

at any time scale

. This is now influenced by both initial and terminal conditions (which we assume to be fixed). Namely, we prove that, given
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}),u(T)\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and, for instance, a globally Lipschitz, locally uniformly convex, Hamiltonian, there exists

is nondecreasing, then

locally uniformly in
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
, where
(v,\mu)
is one particular solution of the infinite horizon problem

\begin{cases}-v_{t}+\bar{\lambda}-\kappa\Delta v+H(x,Dv)=F(x,\mu)\,,&\hbox{$t\in(0,\infty)$}\\
\mu_{t}-\kappa\Delta\mu-{\rm div}(\mu\,H_{p}(x,Dv))=0\,,&\hbox{$t\in(0,\infty)$}\\
\mu(0)=m_{0}\,,\quad v\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})\,,\,\,Dv\in D\bar{u}+L^{2}((0,\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))\,.\quad&\end{cases}

(1.3)

Notice that the convergence is not just for subsequences, but for the whole sequence
(u^{T},m^{T})
. See Theorem
5.3
.

•

In Section 6 and 7, we describe the vanishing discount limit for the (discounted) infinite horizon problem

\begin{cases}-u_{t}+\delta u-\kappa\Delta u+H(x,Du)=F(x,m)&\\
m_{t}-\kappa\Delta m-{\rm div}(m\,H_{p}(x,Du))=0&\\
m(0)=m_{0}\,,\qquad u\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})\,.&\end{cases}

Under similar conditions as before, we prove that the solution
(u_{\delta},m_{\delta})
satisfies

u_{\delta}(t,x)-\frac{\bar{\lambda}}{\delta}\mathop{\to}^{\delta\to 0}v(t,x)\,\,\,;\qquad\quad m_{\delta}(t,x)\mathop{\to}^{\delta\to 0}\mu(t,x)

locally uniformly in
[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
, where
(v,\mu)
is again one particular solution of (

).

Once more, the convergence occurs for the whole sequence; we prove that actually the particular solution selected in the limit satisfies

v(t,x)\mathop{\to}^{t\to\infty}\bar{u}(x)+\theta\,\,\,,\,\,\,

where
\theta
is itself a specific ergodic constant of a linearized problem. See Proposition

and Theorem

.

In the end, the results in Sections 6-7 establish the commutation property between the limit as

and the limit as

Let us point out that
\bar{m}
is the unique invariant measure of problem (

); indeed, in this problem

is uniquely determined (while

is unique up to addition of constants) and satisfies

\mu(t,x)\mathop{\to}^{t\to\infty}\bar{m}(x)\qquad\hbox{uniformly in ${\mathbb{T}^{d}}$.}

To conclude, we recall that the results described in the above items and contained in Sections 4-7 were previously proved in
[

]
for the case of smoothing and monotone couplings

Let us point out that even the case of

2
Standing assumptions

Let
x
belong to the flat torus
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
the space of probability measures on
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
.
Throughout the whole paper, we suppose that

H(x,\cdot)
is locally Lipschitz continuous and locally uniformly convex on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Namely,
p\mapsto H(x,p)
is a
C^{2}
function which satisfies

\forall K>0\,,\,\,\exists\,L_{K}>0\,:\quad|H_{p}(x,p)|\leq L_{K}\qquad\forall(x,p)\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|p|\leq K

(2.1)

and

\forall K>0\,,\,\,\exists\,\alpha_{K},\beta_{K}>0\,:\quad\alpha_{K}\,I\leq H_{pp}(x,p)\leq\beta_{K}I\qquad\forall(x,p)\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:\,|p|\leq K\,.

(2.2)

The couplings
F,G
are real valued functions defined on
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times[0,\infty)
; we suppose, as a standing condition, that they are locally bounded and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
m
:

\forall\,K>0\,,\,\,\exists\,\,c_{K}\,,\,\ell_{K}>0\,:\,\begin{cases}|F(x,m)|\leq c_{K}\,,&\qquad\forall x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,,m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}:\\
|F(x,m)-F(x,m^{\prime})|\leq\ell_{K}|m-m^{\prime}|&\qquad|m|,|m^{\prime}|\leq K\end{cases}

(2.3)

and similarly for
G(x,\cdot)
:

\forall\,K>0\,,\,\,\exists\,\,\hat{c}_{K}\,,\,\hat{\ell}_{K}>0\,:\,\begin{cases}|G(x,m)|\leq\hat{c}_{K}\,,&\qquad\forall x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,,m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}:\\
|G(x,m)-G(x,m^{\prime})|\leq\hat{\ell}_{K}|m-m^{\prime}|&\qquad|m|,|m^{\prime}|\leq K\end{cases}

(2.4)

The dependence of
H,F,G
with respect to
x
is only assumed to be measurable; as it is commonly said, they are Carathéodory functions (measurable in
x
for any
p
, or
m
, accordingly), and the above conditions (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) are meant to hold almost everywhere for
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. We stress that
H,F
could depend on
t
as well (in a measurable way) without additional difficulty, unless for results in which the long time behavior is concerned, where this dependence could change drastically the picture, of course.

3
Uniqueness for the MFG system

In a first result, we show that the MFG system admits a unique solution if the rate of anti-monotonicity does not exceed some threshold, depending on the global
L^{\infty}
-bounds of
m,Du
.

To this purpose, we consider the set of (classical) solutions
(u,m)
to (
1.1
) which satisfy

\sup_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m\leq\mathcal{M},\qquad\sup_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Du|\leq\mathcal{U}

(3.1)

for some
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}>0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let us set

X:=\{(u,m)\in L^{\infty}((0,T);W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))\times L^{\infty}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})\,\,\hbox{satisfying \eqref{MUT}}\}.

There exists
\gamma>0
, only depending on
\kappa,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
(in particular through the constants
L_{\mathcal{U}},\alpha_{\mathcal{U}},\beta_{\mathcal{U}}
in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
)), such that if

F(x,m)+\gamma m
,
G(x,m)+\gamma m
are nondecreasing, for
m\in[0,\mathcal{M}],x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
,

then (
1.1
) admits at most one solution
(u,m)
in
X
.

Proof.

Let
(u_{1},m_{1})
and
(u_{2},m_{2})
be two solutions to (
1.1
) which belong to the set
X
. Convexity of
H
and the usual duality identity give

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(u_{1}-u_{2})(m_{1}-m_{2})\geq\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}\{H(x,Du_{1})-H(x,Du_{2})-H_{p}(x,Du_{2})D(u_{1}-u_{2})\}\\
+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{1}\{H(x,Du_{2})-H(x,Du_{1})-H_{p}(x,Du_{1})D(u_{2}-u_{1})\}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(x,m_{1})-F(x,m_{2})][m_{1}-m_{2}].

Integrating on
(0,T)
, using (
2.2
) and the monotonicity assumption on
F(m),G(m)
, we have

\alpha_{\mathcal{U}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{1}+m_{2})|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}\leq\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{1}-m_{2})^{2}+\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{1}(T)-m_{2}(T))^{2}.

(3.2)

The equation for
\rho:=m_{1}-m_{2}
reads

\rho_{t}-\kappa\Delta\rho-{\rm div}(\rho H_{p}(x,Du_{1}))={\rm div}\Big(m_{2}\big(H_{p}(x,Du_{1})-H_{p}(x,Du_{2})\big)\Big),\quad t\in(0,T)

with
\rho(0)=0
. Thus Lemma
A.2
applies and yields, by Lipschitz regularity of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{1}-m_{2})^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\,\beta_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{2})^{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\,\beta_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\,\mathcal{M}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}dt

for some constant
C
only depending on
\kappa
and
L_{\mathcal{U}}
given by (
2.1
).

Similarly, using (
A.3
) in Lemma
A.2
we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(m_{1}(T)-m_{2}(T))^{2}\leq C\,\beta_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\,\mathcal{M}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}dt

Plugging those informations into (

) we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}\leq 2\gamma\,C\,\beta_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\,\alpha_{\mathcal{U}}^{-1}\mathcal{M}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}dt,

and therefore
Du_{1}=Du_{2}
whenever
\gamma<\alpha_{\mathcal{U}}(2C\,\beta_{\mathcal{U}}^{2}\mathcal{M})^{-1}
(note that

∎

Remark 3.2
.

The constant
\gamma
does not depend directly on the time horizon
T
, but only on
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
. In particular, if those bounds are independent of
T
, then so is
\gamma
as well.
Note also that
\gamma
depends on the diffusion coefficient
\kappa
, and it must vanish as
\kappa

[
9
]
, it is possible to prove the existence of multiple solutions (having comparable Lipschitz bounds) for large

and arbitrarily small anti-monotonicity degree, i.e.

. Therefore, the constant

in the previous proof must explode as
\kappa\to 0
.

Remark 3.3
.

Of course the constant
\gamma
depends on the

-norm of the initial datum, because

.

A similar result can be proved to hold for the stationary ergodic problem (

The proof follows the lines of Theorem

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(x,m_{1})-F(x,m_{2})][m_{1}-m_{2}]\leq 0

\alpha_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{2}|Du_{1}-Du_{2}|^{2}\leq\gamma_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|m_{1}-m_{2}|^{2}

\kappa,L_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}}
,

\|m_{1}-m_{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}\leq C\|m_{2}(H_{p}(x,Du_{1})-H_{p}(x,Du_{2}))\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}\leq C\,\bar{\mathcal{M}}\,\beta_{\bar{U}}^{2}\|\sqrt{m_{2}}(Du_{1}-Du_{2})\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}

Plugging this information into (
3.3
) gives that
Du_{1}-Du_{2}=0
if
\gamma_{0}
is sufficiently small, only depending on
\kappa,L_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}},\alpha_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}},\beta_{\bar{\mathcal{U}}}
. Since
Du_{1}=Du_{2}
it follows that
m_{1}=m_{2}
(from the second equation) and
u_{1}=u_{2}
from the prescribed normalization condition. Finally, the first equation gives
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}
.
∎

We now give two examples of applications of the previous results, namely two settings where the global bounds (

) are proved to hold.

(A) Globally Lipschitz Hamiltonians

The simplest case where a global bound in ensured for
m,Du
is when the Hamiltonian is globally Lipschitz. This is for instance the case when the set of controls of the individual agents lies in a compact set. For simplicity, we assume here that the final datum
G
is
m
-independent, i.e.

G(x,m(T))=u_{T}(x)\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}).

(3.4)

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume conditions (
2.2
)-(
2.3
), and in addition suppose that
H(x,p)
satisfies

\exists\,\,L>0\,\,:\,\quad|H_{p}(x,p)|\leq L\qquad\forall(x,p)\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(3.5)

Assume also that
G
satisfies (
3.4
).

For any
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, there exist a constant
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, only depending on
\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
and another constant
\gamma_{0}
, depending on
\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\|u_{T}(x)\|_{W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
and on the functions
F,H
(through the constants in (
2.2
)-(
2.3
) for a value of
K
depending on
\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\|u_{T}(x)\|_{W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
) such that if

is nondecreasing, for
m\in[0,\mathcal{M}_{0}],x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
,

(3.6)

then the MFG system (
1.1
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

Since
\|H_{p}(x,Du)\|_{\infty}\leq L
due to (
3.5
), by standard parabolic regularity we know that there exists
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, only depending on
\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
such that

\|m\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{M}_{0}\,.

Using (
3.4
)
and parabolic regularity, there exists a constant
\mathcal{U}_{0}
, depending on
\kappa,L
,
\|u_{T}\|_{W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
and on the regularity of
F(x,m)
for
|m|\leq\mathcal{M}_{0}
, such that

Since the bounds above are true for all solutions, by Theorem
3.1
there exists
\gamma_{0}
, depending on
\kappa,\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathcal{U}_{0}
, such that if (

) holds then
there is a unique solution of the MFG system. This concludes the proof of the statement, because

only depend on

and on the local behavior of

on related compact sets.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

It is possible to quantify a bit more precisely the dependence of
\gamma_{0}
on the functions
F,H
. Assume that
H
satisfies (
3.5
) and

c_{0}\,(1+|p|)^{-1}I_{d}\leq H_{pp}\leq c_{0}^{-1}I_{d}\qquad\forall(x,p)\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,

for some
c_{0}>0
. Let for simplicity
G=0
, and suppose that
F(x,m)\simeq-\gamma\,m^{p}
, with
|F_{m}(x,m)|\simeq\gamma\,m^{p-1}
for some
\gamma>0
.

If
L
is given by (
3.5
), then one has

\|m\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{M}=C(\kappa,L)\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}

for a constant
C
only depending on
\kappa,L
(and the dimension
d
). Therefore
\|F(x,m)\|_{\infty}\lesssim\gamma\,\mathcal{M}^{p}
, hence

\|Du\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{U}=C(\kappa,L)\gamma\,\mathcal{M}^{p}

for a possibly different constant
C
still depending only on
\kappa,L,d
. Coming back to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, with
\alpha_{U}\simeq\frac{c_{0}}{\mathcal{U}},\beta_{U}=c_{0}^{-1}
we need to require

\gamma\lesssim C\,\frac{1}{\mathcal{U}\,\mathcal{M}}\lesssim C\,\frac{1}{\gamma\,\,\mathcal{M}^{p+1}}\,.

Hence we can estimate

\gamma_{0}\lesssim C(\kappa,L)\,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}^{-(p+1)/2}

for some
C
only depending on
\kappa,L,d
. It is also easy to check that
C\to 0
as
\kappa\to 0
, so we have

\gamma_{0}\to 0\qquad\hbox{as $\kappa\to 0$ or $\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}\to\infty$.}

One may guess at this point that
\gamma_{0}
vanishes as the diffusion
\kappa
vanishes because norms of solutions are uncontrolled. In fact this seems related to subtler issues, involving the deterioration of the exponential decay of the heat semigroup as
\kappa\to 0
. Indeed, as we already observed in Remark
3.2
, one can find multiple solutions with controlled norms whenever
F^{\prime}\approx-\kappa
. Hence, even though one assumes bounds
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{M}
on solutions, it is mandatory to require smaller
\gamma_{0}
as
\kappa
becomes smaller. Note also that bifurcation methods allow to construct solutions that are periodic in time; therefore, not only uniqueness fails, but also the turnpike property that will be addressed in the next section, at least for selected families of solutions. See also
[
18
]
for the failure of long time stabilization (due to existence of traveling waves) in deterministic mean field games with anti-monotone couplings.

In a similar way, using the elliptic regularity, there is existence and uniqueness of solutions for the stationary problem, provided the rate of anti-monotonicity of
F
is not too large. We skip the proof which follows the same lines as in Corollary
3.5
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Assume that
p\mapsto H(x,p)
is a
C^{2}
function which satisfies (
2.2
) and (
3.5
), and that
F(x,m)
satisfies (

).

Then there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
, only depending on

and on the functions
F,H
(through (

), (
2.2
) for some

only depending on

), such that if

is nondecreasing, then the stationary ergodic problem (

) admits a unique solution
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{u},\bar{m})
.

(B) Quadratic Hamiltonians and couplings with mild growth

Our second example includes the case of superlinear Hamiltonians, having quadratic-like growth in the gradient. Namely, we assume that
H\in C^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is nonnegative and satisfies, for some
c_{0}>0
:

\begin{gathered}c_{0}^{-1}I_{d}\leq H_{pp}(x,p)\leq c_{0}\,I_{d},\\
c_{0}^{-2}|p|^{2}\leq H_{p}(x,p)\cdot p-H(x,p),\\
c_{0}^{-1}|H_{p}(x,p)|^{2}\leq H_{p}(x,p)\cdot p-H(x,p),\\
|H(x,p)-H(y,p)|\leq c_{0}\,(1+|p|),\end{gathered}

(3.7)

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
,
p,q\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

In order to have global bounds, we need here to restrict the growth of the coupling term. Thus, we suppose that
F
satisfies, for some
\alpha<\frac{2}{d}
and
c_{F}>0
,

-c_{F}m^{\alpha}\leq F(x,m)\leq 0\qquad\forall m\geq 0.

(3.8)

Note that it is sufficient that
F
be bounded from above. Then, one can assume that it is nonpositive by adding a term
Ct
, for suitable
C
, to

u
.
We also assume here that the final datum
G
is more regular, i.e.

G(x,m(T))=u_{T}(x)\in C^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}).

(3.9)

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume that (
3.7
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) are in force. Then, there exist
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
depending on
\kappa,\alpha,c_{0},c_{F},\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
such that (
3.1
) holds for all classical solutions to (
1.1
). Moreover, assuming in addition the local Lipschitz assumption (
2.3
), there exists another constant
\gamma_{0}
(depending also on
\ell_{\mathcal{M}}
in (
2.3
)) such that if

m\,\,\mapsto\,\,F(x,m)+\gamma_{0}m\,\,
is nondecreasing, for

\,\,m\in[0,\mathcal{M}],x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}},

then the MFG system (
1.1
) has a unique solution.

We divide the proof of the estimate (
3.1
) in several steps. The uniqueness statement is then a straightforward consequence of Theorem
3.1
.

Step 1. Estimates on the oscillation of
u(t)
.
Denote, as usual,
{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(t)=\max_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(x,t)-\min_{x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(x,t)
. We claim that there exists
C_{0}>0
depending on
c_{0},c_{F},d,\alpha
such that

{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-n)\leq{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u_{T}+2C_{0}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.10)

The estimate will be a consequence of the following “oscillation decay”
inequality

{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-k)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-k+1)+C_{0}\qquad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.11)

Indeed, given (
3.11
), by induction

{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-n)\leq\frac{1}{2^{n}}{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u_{T}+C_{0}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\leq{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u_{T}+2C_{0},

which is (
3.10
). We now turn to (
3.11
). It will be sufficient to prove it for
T-k=0
, being the case
T-k\neq 0
identical; it suffices indeed to perform a time-shift, which is allowed by the following
crucial observation: since
\int m(t)=1
for all
t
, by (
3.8
)

\|F(\cdot,m(\cdot,t))\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq c_{F},\qquad p=\frac{1}{\alpha}>\frac{d}{2}.

(3.12)

To obtain the oscillation estimate, we will argue by duality. For a review of the so-called adjoint method and its application to mean field games, see
[
19
]
and the recent developments in
[
11
]
.

For a smooth probability density
\rho_{0}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
let
\rho
be the classical solution to the Fokker-Planck equation

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}-\Delta\rho-{\rm div}(\rho H_{p}(x,Du))=0,&t\in(0,1)\\
\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(3.13)

Then, as
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}<\frac{d}{d-2}
, by means of Lemma
A.3
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}
depending on
\varepsilon,d,\alpha
(but independent of
\rho_{0}
) such that

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|H_{p}(x,Du)|^{2}\rho+C_{\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon c_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot Du-H(x,Du)]\rho+C_{\varepsilon}.

(3.14)

for
\varepsilon>0
that will be chosen below; the second inequality is just a
consequence of the third assumption (
3.7
) on
H
.

We may now add a constant to
u
so that
\max_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x,1)=0
. Note that by the maximum principle,
u(x,t)\leq 0
for all
t\leq 1
,
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. Using the duality between the equations of
u
and
\rho
, and estimate (
3.14
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Du)Du-H(x,Du)]\rho=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(0)\rho_{0}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(1)\rho(1)-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)\rho\\
\leq{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(1)+\|F(x,m)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\\
\leq{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(1)+c_{0}c_{F}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot Du-H(x,Du)]\rho+c_{F}C_{\varepsilon}.

(3.15)

Thus, choosing
\varepsilon=(2c_{0}c_{F})^{-1}
, we get

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot Du-H(x,Du)]\rho\leq 2\,{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(1)+C_{2}.

(3.16)

Pick now
x_{0},y_{0}\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
such that
{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(0)=u(y_{0},0)-u(x_{0},0)
and let
z=y_{0}-x_{0}
. Setting
\hat{\rho}(x,t)=\rho(x-(1-t)z,t)
,
\hat{\rho}
solves

\begin{cases}\hat{\rho}_{t}-\Delta\hat{\rho}-{\rm div}\big(\hat{\rho}H_{p}(x-(1-t)z,Du(x-(1-t)z,t))\big)-{\rm div}(\hat{\rho}z)=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x-z).\end{cases}

Testing the equation of
\hat{\rho}
by
u
and the equation of
u
by
\hat{\rho}
and integrating by parts we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[-H_{p}(x-(1-t)z,Du(x-(1-t)z,t))\cdot Du(x,t)-z\cdot Du(x,t)+H(x,Du(x,t))]\hat{\rho}(x,t)\\
=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(x,0)\rho_{0}(x-z)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}u(1)\rho(1)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)\hat{\rho}.

Denote for simplicity
y=x+(1-t)z
. After the change of variables
x-(1-t)z\mapsto x
, add (
3.15
) to get

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[-H_{p}(x,Du(x,t))\cdot(Du(y,t)-Du(x,t))+H(y,Du(y,t))-H(x,Du(x,t))]\rho(x,t)\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}z\cdot Du(y,t)\rho(x,t)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}[u(x,0)-u(x+z,0)]\rho_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)(\hat{\rho}-\rho).

Applying now the first and the fourth assumption in (
3.7
) to the left-hand side of this resulting inequality yields

\frac{c_{0}^{-1}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Du(y,t)-Du(x,t)|^{2}\rho(x,t)+\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}[u(x+z,0)-u(x,0)]\rho_{0}(x)\\
\leq c_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(1+|Du(y,t)|)\rho(x,t)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}z\cdot Du(y,t)\rho(x,t)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)(\hat{\rho}-\rho).

Using now Young’s and Hölder’s inequalities, there exists
c_{1}
(only depending on
c_{0}
) such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}[u(x+z,0)-u(x,0)]\rho_{0}(x)\\
\leq\frac{1}{8c_{0}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Du|^{2}\rho+2\|F(x,m)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}+c_{1}\leq\\
\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Du)\cdot Du-H(x,Du)]\rho+2\|F(x,m)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,1);L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,1);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}+c_{1},

as a consequence of the second assumption in (
3.7
). Finally, we plug in (
3.14
) and (
3.16
), and by an appropriate choice
of
\varepsilon
small we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}[u(x+z,0)-u(x,0)]\rho_{0}(x)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(1)+C_{3},

for some
C_{3}
depending on
c_{0},c_{F},\alpha,d
. Choosing now a sequence of
\rho_{0}
converging (weak-*) to
\delta_{x_{0}}
we obtain the desired
estimate

{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(0)=u(y_{0},0)-u(x_{0},0)\leq\frac{1}{2}{\rm osc}_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(1)+C_{3}.

Step 2. Estimates on a
C^{\beta}
-norm of
u
.
We claim that there exists
\beta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
depending on
c,c_{F},\alpha,d
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|u(\cdot,t)-\max_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-n)\|_{C^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq C\qquad\text{for all $t\in[T-(n+2),T-(n+1)]$,}

(3.17)

and the inequality can be extended up to
t=T
when
n=0
.

First,
L^{\infty}
-bounds on
z(x,t):=u(x,t)-\max_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-n)
can be obtained by duality as in Step 1 (and the argument is even simpler). Note that
\|z(T-n)\|_{\infty}\leq C
by (
3.10
), independently on
T,n
. We then proceed assuming without loss of generality that
[T-(n+2),T-n]=[-1,1]
. Since
z(1)\leq 0
and
F\leq 0
, we have
z\leq 0
on
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times(-\infty,1]
by the maximum principle.
Let now
\rho
be as in (
3.13
). Arguing as before by duality (see equations (
3.14
)-(
3.16
)), there exists a constant
\overline{C}
depending on
c_{0},c_{F},\|z(1)\|_{\infty}
(but not depending on
\rho_{0}
) such that

\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)\rho\right|\leq\overline{C},

and therefore

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|z(0)|\rho_{0}=-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}z(0)\rho_{0}=\\
-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,Dz)Dz-H(x,Dz)]\rho-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}z(1)\rho(1)-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,m)\rho\leq\|z(1)\|_{\infty}+\overline{C}.

Varying
\rho_{0}
yields a bound on
\|z(\tau)\|_{\infty}
for
\tau=0
.
Then, varying
\tau\in[-1,1)
in the initial condition
\rho(x,\tau)=\rho_{0}(x)
for
\rho
allows to extend such
L^{\infty}
-bounds for
z
to the whole cylinder
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\times[-1,1]
.

Once sup-bounds on
u(\cdot,t)-\max_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(T-n)
are established, using the uniform integrability of
F(m)
in (
3.12
), a control on a Hölder semi-norm follows by standard results for quasi-linear parabolic equations with quadratic growth in the gradient, see e.g.
[
22
, Theorem V.1.1]
.

Step 3. Estimates on the
L^{q}(L^{p})
-norm of
|Du|^{2}
.
Let
q>1
.
We claim that there exists
C>0
depending on
c_{0},c_{F},\alpha,d,q
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|Du\|_{L^{2q}(\,(T-(n+1),T-n)\,;L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq C.

(3.18)

We prove the inequality in the case
(T-(n+1),T-n)=(0,1)
and
T\geq 2
; constants below will not depend on
T
nor
n
, so the validity of (
3.18
) for
t\in[0,T-1]
will be a straightforward consequence. Some comments regarding the interval

, that is for
t
close to the time-horizon will be made below.

First,
\tilde{u}(x,t)=(2-t)[u(x,t)-\max_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u(x,2)]
solves
\tilde{u}(x,2)=0
and

-\tilde{u}_{t}-\Delta\tilde{u}=-(2-t)H\left(x,\frac{D\tilde{u}}{2-t}\right)+(2-t)F(x,m(t))

Therefore, for any
q>1
, by maximal
L^{q}-L^{p}
regularity for linear parabolic equations (see e.g.
[
20
]
), there exists
C_{q}>0
depending
on
q,p,d
such that

\int_{0}^{2}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|^{q}_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq C_{q}\int_{0}^{2}\Big\|(2-t)H\left(x,\frac{D\tilde{u}}{2-t}\right)\Big\|^{q}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}+\|F\|^{q}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}dt

Since
H(x,p)
has quadratic growth in the

-variable, we may adjust
C_{q}
, and use (
3.12
) to obtain

\int_{0}^{2}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|^{q}_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq C^{\prime}_{q}\left(\int_{0}^{2}\|D\tilde{u}(t)\|^{2q}_{L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\frac{dt}{(2-t)^{q}}+2c_{F}^{q}\right).

(3.19)

We now recall the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg type inequality

\|D\tilde{u}(t)\|_{L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\leq C\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{\theta}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{C^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{1-\theta},

(3.20)

which holds for
\theta\in\left[\frac{1-\beta}{2-\beta},1\right)
and

\frac{1}{2p}=\frac{1}{d}+\theta\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{d}\right)-(1-\theta)\frac{\beta}{d}.

Then we pick
\beta>0
as in the previous Step 2. Note that since
p>\frac{d}{2}
, we have
\theta<1/2
, and by Step 2

\frac{\|D\tilde{u}(t)\|^{2q}_{L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{(2-t)^{q}}\leq\frac{C\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2q\theta}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{C^{\beta}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{q(2-2\theta)}}{(2-t)^{q}}\leq C^{\prime}(2-t)^{q(1-2\theta)}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2q\theta}.

Thus, plugging the previous inequality into (
3.19
) yields

\int_{0}^{2}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|^{q}_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}dt\leq C_{4}\left(\int_{0}^{2}\|\tilde{u}(t)\|_{W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2q\theta}dt+1\right)

for all
t\in(0,2)
. Since
2q\theta<q
, an estimate on
L^{q}((0,2);W^{2,p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
for
\tilde{u}
follows. In turn, back to (
3.20
), this gives bounds in
L^{2q}((0,2);L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
for
D\tilde{u}
. Finally, claimed bounds in
L^{2q}((0,1);L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
for
Du
are straightforward.

In the interval
[T-1,T]
there is no need to localize in time with the term
(2-t)
and normalize the sup-norm, i.e. it is sufficient to perform the very same argument with
\tilde{u}(x,t)=u(x,t)
(and use that
u(T)
is
C^{2}
).

Step 4. Estimates on the sup-norm of
Du
and
m
.
By the assumptions on
H_{p}
, which has linear growth in
|p|
, the previous estimate (
3.18
) reads

\|H_{p}(x,Du)\|_{L^{2q}(\,(T-(n+1),T-n)\,;L^{2p}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq C.

for any

and for some

. Hence,

solves a linear equation in divergence form with drift
H_{p}(x,Du)
, that in turn satisfy the previous integrability condition. Since
\|m(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}=1
for all

, it is standard the existence of
\mathcal{M}
(independent of
T
) such that

\max_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m\leq\mathcal{M},

see e.g.
[
22
, Theorem III.7.1]
. Hence, now we have
\max_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|F(x,m)|\leq c_{F}\mathcal{M}^{\alpha}
. Then, reasoning as in Step
2 in any interval

[T-(n+2),T-(n+1)]
, where we use that
u(t)-u(T-n)
is bounded
uniformly (see (
3.17
)), we can apply
[
22
, Theorem V.3.1]
in
order to get a bound for

at time

. Since this bound is independent of

, and thanks to (

), we conclude that a uniform bound holds up to

\max_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Du|\leq\mathcal{U}\,.

Hence, (
3.1
) is proved.
∎

A similar result also holds for the stationary ergodic problem, as well.

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that
H
satisfies (

) and

satisfies (

). Then there exists a solution

) such that

,
\bar{u}\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
.

Moreover, there exist
\bar{\mathcal{M}},\bar{\mathcal{U}}
(only depending on
\kappa,\alpha
and the constants

) such that any solution of (

) satisfies

Finally, there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
(only depending on
\kappa,\alpha
and the constants
c_{0},c_{F}
) such that, if
F(x,s)+\gamma_{0}s
is nondecreasing, then the solution

(\bar{\lambda},\bar{m},\bar{u})
is unique.

Proof.

We first prove the second assertion, namely the a priori estimate.
Let
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{m},\bar{u})
be any solution of (
1.2
).
We start with bounds on
\bar{\lambda}
(that are somehow related to oscillation estimates in the previous part). First,
\bar{\lambda}\leq-\max_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}H(\cdot,0)
(it just suffices to evaluate the equation for
\bar{u}
at a
maximum point of
\bar{u}
). Then, we use an estimate in
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
: since
\alpha<\frac{2}{d}
, there exists

and

(depending on

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\bar{m}^{\alpha+1}\leq C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})|^{2}\bar{m}+1\right)^{\sigma}

Hence, testing the equation of
\bar{m}
by
\bar{u}
and the equation of

\bar{u}
by
\bar{m}
and integrating by parts we obtain

\bar{\lambda}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})D\bar{u}-H(x,D\bar{u})]\bar{m}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}F(x,\bar{m})\bar{m}\\
\geq c_{0}^{-1}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})|^{2}\bar{m}-c_{F}C\left(\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})|^{2}\bar{m}+1\right)^{\sigma},

which is clearly bounded from below by a positive constant depending on

. Therefore,
\bar{\lambda}
is bounded only in terms of

.

Moreover, since

Thus, we obtain bounds on

{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

is then classical (e.g.
[

[

\max_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|D\bar{u}|\leq\bar{\mathcal{U}}
for some
\bar{\mathcal{U}}

\bar{\mathcal{M}}
and
\bar{\lambda}
. This concludes the a priori estimate.

Note that the above procedure holds also if one replaces

. Therefore, one may consider a classical solution of the system

\begin{cases}\bar{\lambda}-\Delta u+H(x,Du)=\overline{F}(x,m)&\qquad{\rm in}\;{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\\
-\Delta m-{\rm div}\left(m\,H_{p}(x,Du)\right)=0&\qquad{\rm in}\;{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\\
{\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m=1\;,\;\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}u=0}&\end{cases}

(3.21)

which exists, e.g, by results in
[

]
(

, then

In view of the a priori bounds found above, the uniqueness statement is
a direct consequence of Theorem

.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

On the growth assumption
\alpha<\frac{2}{d}
.
We have seen in this section that a condition of mild growth of
F(x,\cdot)
guarantees the existence of solutions to the MFG systems (both the ergodic and the evolutive one). It is worth noting that the existence of a triple
(\bar{u},\bar{m},\bar{\lambda})
to the ergodic MFG system
has been established under the weaker growth assumption
\alpha<\frac{2}{d-2}
and additional
smallness
constraints on
c_{F}
, while non-existence of solutions might even arise in the regime
\alpha>\frac{2}{d-2}
, see
[
8
]
. Concerning the evolutive MFG system (
1.1
), existence of solutions for arbitrarily large time horizon
T
may fail already when
\alpha>\frac{2}{d}
in general
[
12
]
(so the study of the
long time behavior becomes much more delicate, being the MFG system even more sensitive to the data). Still, smallness assumptions on
c_{F}
are sufficient to recover existence for all
T>0
, as described in
[
12
]
. These assumptions may then guarantee uniqueness also, and the turnpike property, but such an analysis is a bit beyond the scopes of this work.

4
The exponential turnpike estimate

In this section we prove that, if the anti-monotonicity of the coupling
F(x,m)
is sufficiently small, then we can prove the existence of solutions of
(
1.1
) satisfying the turnpike property.
The strategy we adopt follows
[
6
, Section 1.3.6]
, through the construction of a solution via a fixed point in a suitable weighted space.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
m_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. Assume that
F(x,m),G(x,m)
satisfy
(
2.3
), (
2.4
), and that
H(x,p)
satisfies (
2.2
) and (
3.5
).

Then there exists
\gamma>0
only depending on
L,\kappa
(and on the functions
F,H
), such that if
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing then
any solution
(u^{T},m^{T})
of problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\|m^{T}(t)-\bar{m}\|_{\infty}+\|Du^{T}(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{\infty}\leq M(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in(1,T-1)\,,

(4.1)

for some
\omega,M>0
(independent of
T
), where
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{u},\bar{m})
is given by Proposition
3.7
.

Remark 4.2
.

According to the Theorem, there is a threshold of anti-monotonicity, namely there exists some
\hat{\gamma}>0
such that if
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing with
\gamma<\hat{\gamma}
, then any solution enjoys the turnpike estimate. Of course, we have
\hat{\gamma}\leq\gamma_{0}
given by Proposition
3.7
(indeed, a unique stationary state is used here). This value
\hat{\gamma}
depends on
F,H
through the constant
L
in (
3.5
) and through assumptions (
2.3
), (
2.2
), in the sense that it depends on the constants
c_{K},\ell_{K},\alpha_{K}
for a value of
K
only depending on

. No special effort is devoted, in the proof below, to catch a refined estimate of
\hat{\gamma}
; as it will be clear, several arguments will need
\gamma
to be smaller than generic constants appearing in the global (in time) estimates of the solutions.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
will rely on the application of Schaefer’s fixed point theorem (
[
16
, Thm 11.3]
). The key-point is given in the following lemma which contains an a priori estimate on a sort of linearization of the mean field game system. We recall that, for any
v\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, we denote
\langle v\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v
and
\tilde{v}=v-\langle v\rangle
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
h(x,p)
be differentiable with respect to
p
, and assume that
h(x,p),h_{p}(x,p)
,
f(x,s)
and
B(x,p)
are all Carathéodory functions which satisfy the following growth conditions for some constants
\ell_{0},C_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
and for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
|p|\leq K
:

h(x,0)=0\,,\quad|h_{p}(x,p)|\leq\ell_{0}\,,

(4.2)

f(x,s)s\geq-\gamma\,s^{2}\,,\quad|f(x,s)|\leq C_{0}\,,\quad|f(x,s)|\leq C_{1}\,|s|

(4.3)

B(x,p)\cdot p\geq C_{2}^{-1}|p|^{2}\,,\qquad|B(x,p)|\leq C_{2}\,|p|\,.

(4.4)

For
\sigma\in[0,1]
,
\mu_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, with
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{0}=0
, and
v_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, let
(\mu,v)
be a solution of the system

\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v-\Delta v+h(x,Dv)=f(x,\mu)&t\in(0,T),\\
v(T)=v_{T}&\\
\partial_{t}\mu-\Delta\mu-{\rm div}(\mu\,h_{p}(x,Dv))=\sigma\,{\rm div}(B(x,Dv))&\,t\in(0,T),\\
\mu(0)=\sigma\,\mu_{0}&\end{cases}

(4.5)

where we assume that, for any
(t,x)\in Q_{T}
, we have
|Dv(t,x)|\leq K
and

\begin{split}\sigma B(x,p)\cdot p-\mu(t,x)(h(x,p)-h_{p}(x,p)\cdot p)\geq\sigma\,c_{0}\,|p|^{2}\qquad\forall(t,x)\in Q_{T},\forall p\,:|p|\leq K\,,\end{split}

(4.6)

for some
c_{0},K>0
.

Then there exist constants
\gamma_{0},\omega,c>0
(independent of
\sigma,v,\mu
) such that, if
\gamma\leq\gamma_{0}
(
\gamma
is in (
4.3
)),
then
(\mu,v)
satisfies

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}]\left(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in(0,T)\,,

(4.7)

where
\tilde{v}(t)=v(t)-\langle v(t)\rangle
. The constants
\gamma_{0},\omega,c
only depend on
\kappa,\ell_{0},C_{1},C_{2},c_{0}
.

Proof.

For
T>0
,
\sigma\in[0,1]
,
\mu_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
with
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{0}=0
, and
v_{T}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, let
(\mu,v)
be the solution of system (
4.5
).
We first prove that there exists a constant
c
, independent of
\sigma,T,\mu_{0},v_{T}
, such that

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}\leq c(\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2})\,.

(4.8)

To start with, we observe that, due to (
4.6
) and (
4.3
),
(\mu,v)
satisfies

\begin{split}-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu(t)v(t)&=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}f(x,\mu)\mu+\sigma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}B(x,Dv)Dv-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu(h(x,Dv)-h_{p}(x,Dv)\cdot Dv)\\
&\geq\sigma\,c_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dv|^{2}-\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|\mu|^{2}\,.\end{split}

(4.9)

Integrating and using Lemma
A.2
we get, for a constant
C
only depending on
\kappa,\ell_{0}
(given by (
4.2
)):

\displaystyle\sigma\,c_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|Dv|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(v(0),\mu_{0})-(v_{T},\mu(T))+\gamma\int_{0}^{T}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq(v(0),\mu_{0})-(v_{T},\mu(T))+C\gamma\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\,\gamma\sigma^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|B(x,Dv)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(v(0),\mu_{0})-(v_{T},\mu(T))+C\gamma\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+C\,C_{2}^{2}\gamma\sigma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|Dv|^{2}

where we used (
4.4
) and
\sigma\leq 1
.
If
\gamma C\,C_{2}^{2}<c_{0}
we deduce the bound

\sigma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|Dv|^{2}\leq c\,\left\{\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}\big[\|\tilde{v}(0)\|_{2}+\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}\big]+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}\|\mu(T)\|_{2}\right\}.

(4.10)

Hereafter, we denote by
c
possibly different constants, depending on
\kappa,\ell_{0},C_{1},C_{2},c_{0}
, which may vary from line to line. Those constants are independent of
\sigma,T,\mu_{0},v_{T}
.

We deduce from (
4.10
), using again Lemma
A.2
and (
4.4
),

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\,\,\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma^{2}C_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|Dv|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\left\{\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}[\|\tilde{v}(0)\|_{2}+\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}]+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}\|\mu(T)\|_{2}\right\}

which implies

\sup_{[0,T]}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}^{2}]+c\,\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}\|\tilde{v}(0)\|_{2}\,.

(4.11)

Since the Hamilton-Jacobi equation implies (using Lemma
A.1
and (
4.3
))

\displaystyle\|\tilde{v}(0)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\,e^{-\nu T}\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}+C\,C_{1}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-\nu s}\|\mu(s)\|_{2}ds\leq C\,\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}+\frac{CC_{1}}{\nu}\,\sup_{[0,T]}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}

coming back to (
4.11
) we deduce (for possibly different
c
)

\sup_{[0,T]}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}^{2}]\,.

A similar estimate follows for
\sup_{[0,T]}\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}
,
using again Lemma
A.1
. This allows us to conclude estimate (
4.8
).
In addition, the inequalities above also show that

\sigma\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|Dv|^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}|\mu|^{2}dt\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}^{2}]\,.

(4.12)

We claim now that this implies the existence of
\tau
such that, for every
T>2\tau
we have

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}]\qquad\forall t\in[\tau,T-\tau]\,.

(4.13)

In fact, using (
4.12
), we know that there exist points
\xi_{\tau}\in[0,\tau/2]
and
\eta_{\tau}\in[T-\tau/2,T]
such that

\|\mu(\xi_{\tau})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2c}{\tau}M^{2}\,,\quad\|\mu(\eta_{\tau})\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{2c}{\tau}M^{2}\,,\qquad\hbox{where $M^{2}=\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}^{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}^{2}$.}

(4.14)

Estimating once more
\mu
through Lemma
A.2
, and then using (
4.9
) (integrated in the interval
(\xi_{\tau},\eta_{\tau})
), thanks to (
4.14
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mu(\xi_{\tau})\|_{2}^{2}+C\,C_{2}^{2}\sigma^{2}\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dv|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2c\,C}{\tau}M^{2}+\frac{C\,C_{2}^{2}}{c_{0}}\left\{\gamma\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu(\xi_{\tau})v(\xi_{\tau})-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu(\eta_{\tau})v(\eta_{\tau})\right\}

Using the global bound for
\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}
and (
4.14
) we estimate last two terms. In the end, choosing
\gamma
sufficiently small we deduce

\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}dt\leq M^{2}(\frac{c}{\tau}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau}})

(4.15)

and in turn what we estimate in between gives (as we said before, for possibly different
c
)

\sigma^{2}\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dv|^{2}\leq M^{2}(\frac{c}{\tau}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau}})\,.

(4.16)

Using once more Lemma
A.2
we have, for all
t\in(\xi_{\tau},\eta_{\tau})

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\left(\|\mu(\xi_{\tau})\|_{2}^{2}+\sigma^{2}\,C_{2}^{2}\int_{\xi_{\tau}}^{\eta_{\tau}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dv|^{2}\right)

and so (
4.14
) and (
4.16
) yield

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq M^{2}(\frac{c}{\tau}+\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau}})\qquad\forall t\in(\xi_{\tau},\eta_{\tau})\,.

(4.17)

Similarly we estimate
\tilde{v}
, using Lemma
A.1
and (
4.3
). For
t\in(\xi_{\tau},\eta_{\tau})
we have

\begin{split}\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}&\leq C\,e^{-\nu(\eta_{\tau}-t)}\|\tilde{v}(\eta_{\tau})\|_{2}+C\,C_{1}\int_{t}^{\eta_{\tau}}e^{-\nu(s-t)}\|\mu(s)\|_{2}ds\\
&\leq c\,e^{-\nu(\eta_{\tau}-t)}M+M(\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{c}{\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}})\,.\end{split}

(4.18)

where we used (
4.8
) and (
4.17
). For
t\in[\tau,T-\tau]
we have
\eta_{\tau}-t\geq\frac{\tau}{2}
, hence we get

\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq c\,e^{-\nu\tau/2}M+M(\frac{c}{\sqrt{\tau}}+\frac{c}{\tau^{\frac{1}{4}}})\,.

This inequality, together with (
4.17
), imply (
4.13
) for a convenient choice of
\tau
.

Finally, by iteration of (
4.13
), we deduce that there exist
\omega>0
and

such that

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}_{T}\|_{2}]\left(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in(0,T)\,,

which is (
4.7
).
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Step 1
(definition of the fixed point mapping) We will first prove the result assuming
m_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\cap C^{0,\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. We set

, where
L^{2}_{0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
denotes the subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
made of functions with zero average. Then we introduce the following norm in
X
:

|||u|||_{X}:=\sup_{[0,T]}\left(\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}}{e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)}}\right)

where
\omega>0
will be chosen later. We have that
(X,|||u|||_{X})
is a Banach space and
|||\cdot|||
is equivalent to the standard norm
\|u\|=\sup_{[0,T]}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
.

By Proposition
3.7
, there exists some
\gamma_{0}
such that, if
F(x,s)+\gamma_{0}s
is nondecreasing, then there exists a unique
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{m},\bar{u})
solution of the stationary ergodic problem (
1.2
).

We define the operator
\Phi
on
X
as follows: given
\mu\in X
, let
(v,\rho)
be the solution to the system

\begin{cases}-v_{t}-\kappa\Delta v+H(x,D\bar{u}+Dv)-H(x,D\bar{u})=F(x,\bar{m}+\mu)-F(x,\bar{m})&\\
v(T)=G(x,\bar{m}+\mu(T))-\bar{u}&\\
\rho_{t}-\kappa\Delta\rho-{\rm div}(\rho\,H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}+Dv))=-{\rm div}(\bar{m}\left[H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}+Dv)-H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})\right])&\\
\rho(0)=m_{0}-\bar{m}\,.&\end{cases}

(4.19)

Then we set
\rho=\Phi\mu
. We observe that the existence and uniqueness of
(v,\rho)
is well known because
H
satisfies (
3.5
). We point out that
\mu
is a fixed point of
\Phi
if and only if

and

yield a solution of (
1.1
).

Step 2
. (a priori estimates and existence of a fixed point) We first observe that, due to (

), there exists
\mathcal{R}
, only depending on
L,\kappa
and
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\|\bar{m}\|_{\infty}
, such that

\|\rho\|_{\infty}\leq{\mathcal{R}}\qquad\forall\rho\in{\rm Range}(T)\,.

(4.20)

Therefore, up to replacing
\mu
with
\min(\mu,\mathcal{R})
in the first equation of (
4.19
), we can assume that
F(x,\cdot)
is globally bounded and Lipschitz, thanks to (
2.3
) used with
K=\|\bar{m}\|_{\infty}+\mathcal{R}
. Using again (
3.5
) and the global bound of the right-hand side, we deduce (e.g. by Lemma
A.1
) that a global bound holds for
\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}
. Hence, by (local) regularizing effect of parabolic equations, there exists a constant
K>0
(only depending on
L,\kappa
and
{\mathcal{R}}
) such that

\|Dv(t)\|_{\infty}\leq K\qquad\forall t\leq T-1\,,\qquad\forall\mu\in X\,.

(4.21)

The continuity of the operator
\Phi
in
C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
(hence in
X
) is a routine stability argument for parabolic equations, due to the Lipschitz character of
H
and the boundedness of
F
. In addition, since
\bar{m},m_{0}\in C^{0,\alpha}
, by standard regularity results (see
[
22
, Chapter V, Thms 1.1 and 2.1]
) we have that the range of
\Phi
is bounded in
C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}(Q_{T})
, in particular its closure is compact in
X
.
Thus,
\Phi
is a compact and continuous operator. In order to apply Schaefer ’s fixed point theorem, we
are left to prove the following claim: there exists a constant
M>0
such that

|||\mu|||\leq M\quad\hbox{ for every $\mu\in X$ and every $\sigma\in[0,1]$ such that $\mu=\sigma\Phi(\mu)$.}

(4.22)

Now we observe that the estimate (
4.22
) follows from Lemma
4.3
(which will be applied in
(0,T-1)
due to (
4.21
)). Indeed, if
\mu=\sigma\Phi(\mu)
, then
(\mu,v)
is a solution to
(
4.5
) with
\mu_{0}=m_{0}-\bar{m}
,
v_{T-1}=v(T-1,x)
, and where
h(x,p),f(x,s),B(x,p)
are defined by

\displaystyle h(x,p):=H(x,D\bar{u}(x)+p)-H(x,D\bar{u}(x))

\displaystyle f(x,\mu):=F(x,\bar{m}(x)+\mu)-F(x,\bar{m}(x))

\displaystyle B(x,p):=\bar{m}(x)\left[H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}(x)+p)-H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}(x))\right]\,.

Using (
4.20
) and (
4.21
), together with (
2.3
) and (
2.2
), the functions
h(x,p),f(x,s),B(x,p)
satisfy the conditions (
4.2
)-(
4.4
),
where we also used that
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing.

In addition, since
\mu=\sigma\Phi(\mu)
implies
\mu(t,x)\geq-\sigma\bar{m}(x)
, we also have, for some constant
c_{0}
,

\displaystyle\sigma B(x,p)\cdot p-

\displaystyle\mu(t,x)(h(x,p)-h_{p}(x,p)\cdot p)\geq\sigma B(x,p)\cdot p-\sigma\bar{m}(x)(h_{p}(x,p)\cdot p-h(x,p))

\displaystyle=\sigma\bar{m}(x)\left[-H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}(x))\cdot p+H(x,D\bar{u}(x)+p)-H(x,D\bar{u}(x))\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sigma\,c_{0}\,|p|^{2}\qquad\forall(t,x)\in Q_{T},\forall p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,:|p|\leq K\,,

where we used the local uniform convexity of
H
and that
\bar{m}>0
. Therefore, condition (
4.6
) holds too.
Applying Lemma
4.3
we deduce that there exists a constant
c
(independent of
\sigma,T
) such that

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(t)\|_{2}\leq c\,[\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}+\|\tilde{v}(T-1)\|_{2}]\left(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in(0,T-1)\,.

Since
\|\tilde{v}\|_{2}
is uniformly bounded, this yields

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}\leq M\left(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)}\right)\qquad\forall t\in(0,T-1)\,.

The estimate extends to
(0,T)
because
\mu
is also uniformly bounded, thus we proved that
|||\mu|||\leq M
for some
M
independent of
\sigma,T
.
This proves (
4.22
) and concludes the fixed point argument. Eventually, one can upgrade the estimate in
(1,T-1)
to the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
\mu(t)
and
Dv(t)
by the regularizing effect in the two equations. This latter argument is already developed e.g. in
[
29
]
.

Step 3.
(conclusion of the general case) Assume now that
m_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and
(u,m)
is any solution of (
1.1
). Due to (
3.5
), by standard regularizing effect we have that
m(t)\in C^{0,\alpha}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and that
\|m(t)\|_{\infty}\leq c\,(t-t_{0})^{-{d/2}}
, for every
t\in(t_{0},t_{0}+1)
and every
t_{0}>0
. Therefore,
m
is uniformly bounded (globally in time) in the interval
(1,T)
. Similarly, again by (
3.5
) and parabolic regularity, we have
\|Du(t)\|_{\infty}\leq C
,
t\in(0,T-1]
, for some
C
only depending on
L
(the Lipschitz bound of
H
) and the global bounds of
m
,
\tilde{u}
. Therefore,
(u,m)
satisfies (
3.1
) in the interval
(1,T-1)
, for some
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{U}
only depending on
L
. By Theorem
3.1
, there exists
\gamma_{L}
such that problem (
1.1
) admits at most one solution in
(1,T-1)
if
\gamma\leq\gamma_{L}
. This means that
(u,m)
coincides with the solution built in Step 1 in the interval
(1,T-1)
, with initial-terminal conditions given by
m(1)
and
u(T-1)
. Hence
(u,m)
satisfies the exponential estimate (
4.1
).
∎

We now observe that, as a consequence of the previous result, any globally bounded solution has a stationary attractor, provided the anti-monotonicity constant is sufficiently small.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume that
(u^{T},m^{T})
is a solution of (
1.1
) which satisfies

\sup_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Du^{T}|\leq\mathcal{U}

for some
\mathcal{U}
independent of
T
. Assume that
F,G,H
satisfy conditions (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) respectively.

Then, there exists some
\gamma
, only depending on
\kappa,\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
, and on
F,H
(through the local bounds induced by
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
) such that
if
s\mapsto F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing we have

\|m^{T}(t)-\bar{m}\|_{\infty}+\|Du^{T}(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{\infty}\leq M(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in(1,T-1)\,,

(4.23)

for some
\omega,M>0
(independent of
T
) and some
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
solution of the ergodic problem (
1.2
).

Proof.

We first build a globally Lipschitz extension of the Hamiltonian function
H(x,p)
. Namely, we consider a function
\tilde{H}(x,p)
which still satisfies (
2.2
) and such that

\tilde{H}(x,p)\equiv H(x,p)\quad\hbox{for $|p|\leq\mathcal{U}$}\,,\qquad|H_{p}(x,p)|\leq C_{\mathcal{U}}\quad\forall(x,p)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

for some number
C_{\mathcal{U}}
only depending on
\mathcal{U}
(eventually through some constants in (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) depending on
\mathcal{U}
).

An example of a similar extension can be built as follows. First of all we take a cut-off function
\zeta\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\zeta\equiv 1
for
|p|\leq 2
and
\zeta\equiv 0
for
|p|>3
; then we take a
C^{2}
real function
\varphi(r)
such that
\varphi(r)\equiv 0
if
|r|\leq\frac{3}{2}
,
\varphi
is increasing, locally uniformly convex for
r\in(\frac{3}{2},+\infty)
and globally Lipschitz continuous. Then the function

\tilde{H}(x,p):=H(x,p)\zeta\left(\frac{p}{\mathcal{U}}\right)+C\varphi\left(\frac{|p|}{\mathcal{U}}\right)

satisfies the required properties for a convenient choice of
C
(which will only depend on
\mathcal{U}
).

Now, we can apply Theorem
4.1
to the MFG system

\begin{cases}-u_{t}-\kappa\Delta u+\tilde{H}(x,Du)=F(x,m(t)),&t\in(0,T)\\
m_{t}-\kappa\Delta m-{\rm div}(m\tilde{H}_{p}(x,Du))=0,&t\in(0,T)\\
m(x,0)=m_{0}(x),\qquad u(x,T)=G(x,m(T))\end{cases}

(4.24)

so there exists some
\gamma>0
such that if
s\mapsto F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing, then the ergodic stationary problem (corresponding to
\tilde{H}(x,p)
) has a unique solution
(\bar{m},\bar{u})

and any solution of (
4.24
) satisfies (
4.1
). This value of
\gamma
depends on
\tilde{H}
, so it actually depends on
\mathcal{U}
and on the functions
F,H
. Now, since
\|Du^{T}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{U}
, we have
\tilde{H}(x,Du^{T})=H(x,Du^{T})
so the result applies to the given solution of the original problem (
1.1
). Therefore,
(u^{T},m^{T})
satisfies (
4.23
). It remains only to show that
(\bar{m},\bar{u})
is solution with
H(x,p)
rather than with
\tilde{H}(x,p)
. But this must necessarily be true, because (
4.23
) implies, e.g., that
Du^{T}\left(x,\frac{T}{2}\right)\to D\bar{u}(x)
, so
\|D\bar{u}\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{U}
. This also proves that
(\bar{m},\bar{u})
does not depend on the extension

\tilde{H}(x,p)
which was constructed.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The previous result is new even in case that
F(x,\cdot)
is nondecreasing. It means that any solution
(u^{T},m^{T})
such that
Du^{T}
is uniformly bounded
exhibits a turnpike behavior in long horizon and approximates (for most of time) a stationary solution
(\bar{m},\bar{u})
. So far this shows that a global (in time) gradient bound for
u^{T}
is sufficient for the turnpike property to hold.

Corollary
4.4
may be applied, for instance, to the case of superlinear Hamiltonian with quadratic growth when the cost function
F
has a moderate growth in the
m
variable, i.e. the model case (B) addressed in the previous Section. The corollary below is in particular a consequence of Corollary
4.4
and Theorem
3.8
.

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume that
H
and
F
satisfy the conditions (
3.7
) and (
2.3
), (
3.8
), respectively. For
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and
G
satisfying (
3.9
), let
(u^{T},m^{T})
be solution of (
1.1
).

Then, there exists some
\gamma
, only depending on
\kappa
,
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
, and on
F,H
(through constants appearing in the assumptions above, and the local bounds induced by
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{U}
) such that
if
s\mapsto F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing we have

\|m^{T}(t)-\bar{m}\|_{\infty}+\|Du^{T}(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{\infty}\leq M(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in(1,T-1)\,,

for some
\omega,M>0
(independent of
T
) and some
(\bar{u},\bar{m})
solution of the ergodic problem (
1.2
).

5
The stationary feedback and the convergence of
u^{T}

In this section we are going to see how the exponential turnpike estimate established so far implies the convergence of
u^{T}(t)-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)
, as well as of
m^{T}(t)
, for any fixed
t>0
.

From now on, and throughout the rest of the paper, we will consider only the case (A) discussed above, which involves globally Lipschitz and locally uniformly convex Hamiltonians. Similar results can be proven for case (B) involving uniformly convex Hamiltonians and costs with mild growth, in view of the estimates obtained in Section 3, but they will not be stated explicitly for brevity.

We are now interested in a convergence for any
t>0
, so we are going to require that (
4.1
) holds in the whole interval
[0,T]
(this is true in Theorem
4.1
if
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and
u_{T}\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
):

\|m^{T}(t)-\bar{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|Du^{T}(t)-D\bar{u}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in[0,T]\,.

(5.1)

We start by deducing the following global bound as a corollary of (
5.1
).

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
H,F,G
satisfy (
2.1
), (
2.3
), (
2.4
) respectively. Assume that
(u^{T},m^{T})
is a solution of (
1.1
) which satisfies (
5.1
). Then there exists a constant
K
, independent of
T
, such that

\|u^{T}-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)\|_{\infty}\leq K\qquad\forall t\in[0,T]\,.

(5.2)

Proof.

Due to estimate (
5.1
), and the local Lipschitz character of
F
, we have that

|F(x,m^{T})-F(x,\bar{m})|\leq c\,(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega(T-t)})\qquad\forall t\in[0,T]\,.

Moreover, using (
2.4
), we have that
G(x,m^{T}(T))
is uniformly bounded.
Hence, if we denote by
\varphi(t):=M(1+\int_{t}^{T}(e^{-\omega s}+e^{-\omega(T-s)})ds)
, we deduce that, for sufficiently large
M
,
the functions
\bar{u}+\bar{\lambda}(T-t)+\varphi(t)
and
\bar{u}+\bar{\lambda}(T-t)-\varphi(t)
are, respectively, super and sub solution to the equation satisfied by
u^{T}
. The comparison principle readily yields

\bar{u}-\varphi(t)\leq u^{T}-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)\leq\bar{u}+\varphi(t)\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

which implies (
5.2
).
∎

Now we establish a uniqueness result for the limiting problem of the MFG system as
T\to\infty
; namely, we consider the problem

\begin{cases}-v_{t}+\bar{\lambda}-\kappa\Delta v+H(x,Dv)=F(x,\mu(t))\,,&\hbox{$t\in(0,\infty)$}\\
\mu_{t}-\kappa\Delta\mu-{\rm div}(\mu\,H_{p}(x,Dv))=0\,,&\hbox{$t\in(0,\infty)$}\\
\mu(x,0)=m_{0}(x)\,,\\
v\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})\,,\,\,Dv\in D\bar{u}+L^{2}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})\,,\,\end{cases}

(5.3)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
,
m_{0}\geq 0
. Assume that
H
satisfies (
2.2
) and (
3.5
), and
F
satisfies (
2.3
). There exists
\gamma>0
, only depending on
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\kappa,L
(appearing in (
3.5
)) and on
H,F
(through (
2.2
), (
2.3
) for some
K
only depending on
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\kappa,L
)
such that, if
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is nondecreasing, then there are at most one
\mu
and one
v
(up to addition of a constant) which solve problem (
5.3
).

Proof.

We first observe that
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m_{0}
for every
t
, so
\|\mu(t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}
is uniformly bounded; since
H_{p}
is uniformly bounded by
L
, this implies that
\mu\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and its norm is universally bounded by a constant only depending on
d,\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
. This provides with a uniform bound for
F(x,\mu)
and in turn, as a consequence of Lemma
A.1
, this implies that
\tilde{v}(t)
and
Dv(t)
are bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, uniformly in time.
By local parabolic regularity we deduce that
Dv\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
as well and its norm is bounded uniformly in time, for all solutions.
We will therefore use the upper and lower bounds of
H_{pp}
for
|p|
varying in a compact set
which is the same for all solutions
(\mu,v)
.

On account of the above ingredients, we follow a standard argument for the proof of uniqueness.
Let
(\mu_{1},v_{1}),(\mu_{2},v_{2})
be two solutions of (
5.3
).
Consider a function
\xi_{R}(t):=\xi(t/R)
, where
\xi
is a
C^{1}
function such that
\xi\equiv 1
in
(0,1)
and
with support in
(-1,2)
. Using
\psi=(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})\xi_{R}
as test function in the equation of
v_{1}-v_{2}
, we
get as usual:

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dx\right)=-\xi_{R}^{\prime}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dx

\displaystyle+\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(\mu_{1})-F(\mu_{2})][\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}]\,dx

\displaystyle+\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{1}\left[H(x,Dv_{2})-H(x,Dv_{1})-H_{p}(x,Dv_{1})D(v_{2}-v_{1})\right]dx

\displaystyle+\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}\left[H(x,Dv_{1})-H(x,Dv_{2})-H_{p}(x,Dv_{2})D(v_{1}-v_{2})\right]dx

which implies, using the uniform convexity of
H
:

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dx\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\xi_{R}^{\prime}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dx

\displaystyle+\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(\mu_{1})-F(\mu_{2})][\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}]\,dx

\displaystyle+c\,\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2})|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dx\,,

for some constant
c>0
.
Integrating we get

\displaystyle c\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}\,|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}^{\prime}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dxdt

\displaystyle+\gamma\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}|^{2}\,dxdt

where we used that
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is monotone. Notice that
Dv_{1}-Dv_{2}\in L^{2}((0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
by assumption. Then, by Lemma
A.2
(see (
A.4
) with
\delta=0
), we deduce

\int_{0}^{\infty}\|(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int\mu_{2}^{2}\,|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\int\mu_{2}\,|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt

for some constant
C
depending on
\|H_{p}\|_{\infty}
, on the
L^{\infty}
bound of
\mu_{2}
and on the local upper bound of
H_{pp}
(all being estimated only in terms of
\kappa,L,\|m_{0}\|_{\infty}
). Hence we deduce

\displaystyle(c-\gamma\,C)\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}(t)|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}^{\prime}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})(v_{1}-v_{2})dxdt

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\xi_{R}(t))\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}(t)|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\,.

Using Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality and the fact that
\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
, due to the properties of
\xi_{R}(t)
we get

\displaystyle(c-\gamma\,C)\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi_{R}(t)\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}(t)|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\leq c\,\left(\frac{1}{R}\int_{R}^{2R}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+c\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\xi_{R}(t))\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{2}(t)|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt

and letting
R\to\infty
we conclude

\int_{0}^{\infty}\int\mu_{2}(t)|D(v_{1}-v_{2})|^{2}dxdt=0\,

for a sufficiently small
\gamma
, only depending on
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\kappa,L
(eventually trough
F,H
).

Hence
Dv_{1}=Dv_{2}
and, from the Fokker-Planck equation, this yields
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
. Finally, this implies
(v_{1}-v_{2})_{t}=0
, hence
v_{1}(t,x)=v_{2}(t,x)+K
for some constant
K\in\mathbb{R}
, and for every
(t,x)
.
∎

We now establish the convergence of
u^{T}-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
. Assume that
F(x,m)
satisfies
(
2.3
),
H(x,p)
satisfies (
2.2
) and (
3.5
), and the final cost
G
satisfies (
3.4
). Let
(u^{T},m^{T})
be a solution of problem (
1.1
).

There exists
\gamma>0
, only depending on
\|m_{0}\|_{\infty},\kappa,L
(and on the functions
F,H
), such that if
F(x,s)+\gamma s

is nondecreasing, then
there exists a solution
(v,\mu)
of (
5.3
) such that

\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}u^{T}(x,t)-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)=v(x,t)\,\qquad;\qquad\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}m^{T}(x,t)=\mu(x,t)\qquad\forall t>0,x\in{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

and the convergence is uniform in
[a,b]\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
, for any
[a,b]\subset[0,\infty)
.

In particular, there exists a constant
\bar{c}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
(v,\mu)
is the unique solution of (
5.3
) such that
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\int v(t)=\bar{c}
.

Proof.

We set
v^{T}:=u^{T}(x,t)-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)
. Since
m_{0}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})
and
u(T)\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, we know that the exponential estimate (
4.1
) holds for
t\in[0,T]
. Hence, from Corollary
5.1
, we have that
\|v^{T}\|_{\infty}
and
\|Dv^{T}\|_{\infty}
are bounded
uniformly with respect to
T
; and by parabolic regularity, this readily implies that
v^{T}
is locally (in time) relatively compact
in the uniform topology. Similarly,
m^{T}
is locally bounded in Hölder (time-space) norms, and it is relatively compact in the uniform topology (locally in time). Then, it is straightforward to see that
(v^{T},m^{T})
converges, up to subsequences, to a solution
(v,\mu)
of (
5.3
). We only need to prove that the limit function
v
is the same for all subsequences.

To this purpose, we develop an idea from
[
5
]
: for
T,T^{\prime}
we consider
v^{T},v^{T^{\prime}}
and we define the shifted functions

(\hat{v}^{T},\hat{\mu}^{T}):=(v^{T}(t+T),m^{T}(t+T))\,,\,\,\,\quad(\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}},\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}):=(v^{T^{\prime}}(t+T^{\prime}),m^{T^{\prime}}(t+T^{\prime}))\,.

Both
(\hat{v}^{T},\hat{\mu}^{T})
and
(\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}},\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})
are solutions of MFG systems in the interval
(-\tau,0)
; the usual
estimate gives

-\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})dx\geq-\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})^{2}\,dx+c\,\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}+\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})|D(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})|^{2}dx\,,

where we used that
F(x,s)+\gamma s
is monotone and the uniform bound from below of
H_{pp}
on compact subsets.
Integrating and using the final condition,
we have

\displaystyle c\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}+\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})|D(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})|^{2}dx\leq\gamma\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}(-\tau)-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}(-\tau))(\hat{v}^{T}(-\tau)-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}}(-\tau))dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\gamma\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})^{2}\,dx+\|m^{T}(T-\tau)-m^{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime}-\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|Du^{T}(T-\tau)-Du^{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime}-\tau)\|_{L^{2}}

which yields, thanks to (
5.1
),

\displaystyle\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}+\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})|D(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})|^{2}dx\leq c\,\gamma\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+c(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})^{2}

where, as usual, we denote generically by

possibly different constants (which may vary from line to line) independent of
T,T^{\prime}
.
Applying Lemma
A.2
to the equation of
\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int(\hat{\mu}^{T}-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\,\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int(\hat{\mu}^{T})^{2}|D(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|m^{T}(T-\tau)-m^{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime}-\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,.

Thus for

sufficiently small we conclude that

\int_{-\tau}^{0}\int(\hat{\mu}^{T}+\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})|D(\hat{v}^{T}-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}})|^{2}dx\leq c\,(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})^{2}\,.

A similar estimate is then deduced for
\sup_{t\in[-\tau,0]}\|\hat{\mu}^{T}(t)-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}(t)\|_{2}
, from the above estimates.

With a bootstrap argument, using the global

bounds for

and for

, the previous

bound can be updated into a

\|\hat{\mu}^{T}(t)-\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq c\,(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})\qquad\forall t\in[-\tau,0]\,.

In particular, those bounds are inherited by
F(x,\hat{\mu}^{T})-F(x,\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}})
as well as by
G(x,\hat{\mu}^{T}(0))-G(x,\hat{\mu}^{T^{\prime}}(0))
.
Therefore, the maximum principle implies that

\|\hat{v}^{T}(t)-\hat{v}^{T^{\prime}}(t)\|_{\infty}\leq c\,\tau(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})\qquad\forall t\in[-\tau,0]\,.

In particular, we have proved that

\|v^{T}(T-\tau)-v^{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime}-\tau)\|_{\infty}\leq c\tau(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})\,.

(5.4)

Now we consider the equation of
v^{T}-\bar{u}
in the interval
(t,T-\tau)
; integrating we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T}(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T}(T-\tau)+\int_{t}^{T-\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H(Du^{T})-H(D\bar{u})]dxds=\int_{t}^{T-\tau}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(m^{T})-F(\bar{m})]dxds

which yields, using (
2.3
), (

) and (

),

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T}(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T}(T-\tau)\right|\leq c\int_{t}^{T-\tau}(e^{-\omega s}+e^{-\omega(T-s)})ds\leq c\,(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega\tau})\,.

Similarly we integrate the equation of
v^{T^{\prime}}-\bar{u}
in the interval
(t,T^{\prime}-\tau)
and we get

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T^{\prime}}(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T^{\prime}}(T^{\prime}-\tau)\right|\leq c(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega\tau})\,.

Putting the latter two inequalities together with (

\begin{split}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T}(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v^{T^{\prime}}(t)\right|&\leq c\,(e^{-\omega t}+e^{-\omega\tau})+c\,\tau(e^{-\omega\tau}+e^{-\omega(T-\tau)}+e^{-\omega(T^{\prime}-\tau)})\,.\end{split}

(5.5)

Finally, we consider two possible limits

obtained with different subsequences
v^{T_{n}}
and

is a constant. However, passing to the limit as

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v_{1}(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v_{2}(t)\right|\leq c\,e^{-\omega t}\,.

Letting

can only be the null constant. This proves that the limit of

is independent of the subsequence, hence the whole sequence

converges. The convergence of

.

Finally, by integrating the equation of

, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v(t^{\prime})+\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[H(Dv)-H(D\bar{u})]dxds=\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[F(\mu)-F(\bar{m})]dxds\,.

Since

satisfy the estimate (a consequence of (

we deduce that

\left|\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v(t)-\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}v(t^{\prime})\right|\leq K\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}e^{-\omega s}ds\quad\mathop{\to}^{t,t^{\prime}\to\infty}\,\,\,0

hence

is a Cauchy sequence and admits a limit as

, fully characterizes the function

due to Lemma

∎

Remark 5.4
.

In the above result, we have assumed that the final cost

is independent of

. The reason is that if

, then we are not able to show a bound for

We stress, in particular, that the convergence result of Theorem

remains true for smoothing couplings at final time, say for instance if

is a Lipschitz continuous mapping from

(endowed with the Wasserstein distance

) such that

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(G(x,m_{1})-G(x,m_{2})d(m_{1}-m_{2})\geq-\gamma\,\|m_{1}-m_{2}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}^{2}\qquad\forall m_{1},m_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})

Remark 5.5
.

It would be possible to characterize the limit of
u^{T}-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)
in terms of a stationary feedback operator defined on the current measure
\mu(t)
, which is the unique solution of (
5.3
) (see Lemma
5.2
). Namely, one can define an operator
\hat{E}
such that

\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}u^{T}(x,t)-\bar{\lambda}(T-t)=\hat{E}(\mu(t))\qquad\hbox{for any $t>0$.}

In the present setting,

could only be defined as an unbounded operator in
(L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))_{+}
, with a domain which includes the set of bounded functions. Nevertheless,

could still be characterized thanks to the well-posedness of the MFG system. We stress that this kind of feedback (which plays a similar role as the Riccati stationary operator in other control problems) would coincide with a solution of the stationary ergodic master equation introduced in
[
5
]
for monotone and smooth mean field game systems. This may suggest alternative ways to look at the master equation of mean field games whenever it cannot be defined as a smooth function on the space of probability measures.

is a parameter used in a first step for compactness issues.
We notice that

if and only if

).

In fact, if

, then

solves the evolution equation

Since

is bounded by

, and since

,
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}m(t)=1
for every

such that

for every

. The same applies to
\bar{m}_{\delta}
. We deduce that the range of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is contained in a uniform ball in

. Hence, there is no loss of generality in restricting the domain of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
to functions which are uniformly bounded (it would be enough to replace
\mu
with a suitable truncation in the definition of the operator); in particular, due to (

), the function

can be treated as uniformly Lipschitz in the
\mu

We also observe that the uniform bound of

implies a uniform bound for

. Indeed, one can first proceed as in (

) to deduce that

is uniformly bounded, then by Lemma

and the regularizing effect of the equation it follows that

\|Dv(t)\|_{\infty}\leq K\qquad\forall t>0

(6.19)

for some constant

.
We now show the following properties of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
:

(i)
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
is continuous and compact.

To show this fact, let

be bounded in

. By decay properties of the equation of

, and by Lipschitz continuity of

,
we have

e^{-\delta t}\|Dv_{n}(t)\|_{2}\leq c\int_{t}^{\infty}e^{-\nu(s-t)}e^{-\delta s}\|\mu_{n}(s)e^{-\varepsilon s}\|_{2}ds

which implies

\|Dv_{n}(t)\|_{2}\leq c\,e^{-\varepsilon t}\,.

Since

\|\rho(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq ce^{-2\nu(t-t_{0})}\|\rho(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}+c\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|Dv(s)\|^{2}ds

by choosing

\|\rho_{n}(t)\|_{2}\leq ce^{-\nu\frac{t}{2}}+c\,e^{-\varepsilon\frac{t}{2}}\,.

In particular,

is uniformly small in

for

large. Since

is (locally in time) relatively compact for the uniform topology, we deduce that it is compact in
L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{2}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
.
The continuity is easily proved in a similar way.

(ii) There exists
M>0
such that, for any
\sigma\in[0,1]
, any solution of
\mu=\sigma\Phi_{\varepsilon}\mu
satisfies the estimate

\|\mu(t)\|_{2}\leq M\,e^{-\omega t}\|\mu_{0}\|_{2}\quad\forall t>0\,.

In particular,

After (i) and (ii), we can apply Schaefer’s fixed point theorem (

Finally, we show that there is a unique solution to (

Proof of Proposition
6.4
.

Let
(\bar{u}_{\delta},\bar{m}_{\delta})
be solutions of (
6.2
). We set

w_{\delta}:=\frac{\bar{u}_{\delta}-\frac{\lambda}{\delta}-\bar{u}}{\delta}\,,\qquad\mu_{\delta}=\frac{\bar{m}_{\delta}-\bar{m}}{\delta}

and we verify that
(w_{\delta},\mu_{\delta})
solves the problem

\begin{cases}\delta w_{\delta}+\bar{u}-\kappa\Delta w_{\delta}+\frac{H(x,D\bar{u}+\delta Dw_{\delta})-H(x,D\bar{u})}{\delta}=\frac{F(x,\bar{m}+\delta\mu_{\delta})-F(x,\bar{m})}{\delta}&\\
-\kappa\Delta\mu_{\delta}-{\rm div}(\mu_{\delta}\,H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}+\delta Dw_{\delta}))={\rm div}\left(\bar{m}\frac{[H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}+\delta Dw_{\delta})-H(x,D\bar{u})]}{\delta}\right)&\\
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{\delta}=0\,.&\end{cases}

We rephrase this problem as the system

\begin{cases}\delta w_{\delta}+\bar{u}-\kappa\Delta w_{\delta}+h^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta})=f^{\delta}(x,\mu_{\delta})&\\
-\kappa\Delta\mu_{\delta}-{\rm div}(\mu_{\delta}\,h_{p}^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta}))={\rm div}(B^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta}))&\\
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu=0\,.&\end{cases}

(6.26)

where we have

\displaystyle h^{\delta}(x,p):=\frac{H(x,D\bar{u}+\delta p)-H(x,D\bar{u})}{\delta}\to H_{p}(x,D\bar{u})\cdot p\,,

\displaystyle f^{\delta}(x,\mu):=\frac{F(x,\bar{m}+\delta\mu)-F(x,\bar{m})}{\delta}\to F_{m}(x,\bar{m})\mu

\displaystyle B^{\delta}(x,p):=\bar{m}\frac{[H_{p}(x,D\bar{u}+\delta p)-H(x,D\bar{u})]}{\delta}\to\bar{m}\,H_{pp}(x,D\bar{u})\,p

It is easy to see (due to (

, and then
\|D\bar{u}_{\delta}\|_{\infty}
, are bounded independently of
\delta
. Then we can use the local conditions
(

), (
2.3
) and we deduce that

,

|f^{\delta}(x,\mu_{\delta})|\leq C_{1}\,|\mu_{\delta}|

and

|B^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta})|\leq C_{2}\,|Dw_{\delta}|\,,\qquad B^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta})Dw_{\delta}\geq c_{0}\,|Dw_{\delta}|^{2}\,.

(6.28)

Therefore, using the convexity of

and assumption (
6.1
), we estimate

\displaystyle\delta\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}w_{\delta}\mu_{\delta}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\bar{u}\,\mu_{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}f^{\delta}(x,\mu_{\delta})\mu_{\delta}+\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}B^{\delta}(x,Dw_{\delta})Dw_{\delta}

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dw_{\delta}|^{2}-\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{\delta}^{2}\,.

From the second equation in (

[

) that, for a constant

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\mu_{\delta}^{2}\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|B(x,Dw_{\delta}|^{2}\leq C\,C_{2}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dw_{\delta}|^{2}\,.

Using again (

\gamma_{0}>0

\gamma<\gamma_{0}
, and
\delta

From the bound of

w
such that, up to subsequences,
w_{\delta}-\langle w_{\delta}\rangle
converges to

uniformly and in

, for any

.
Since
\delta\|w_{\delta}\|_{\infty}
is bounded, overall we conclude that, for some constant
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and some subsequence (not relabeled), we have

\delta w_{\delta}\to\theta
,
w_{\delta}-\langle w_{\delta}\rangle\to w
,
\mu_{\delta}\to\mu
, uniformly in
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
,

(6.30)

where
(\theta,w,\mu)
is a solution of (
6.22

6.22
) we observe that
F_{m}(x,\bar{m})\geq-\gamma
due to (
6.1
) and that

c_{0}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\bar{m}\,|Dw-D\hat{w}|^{2}\leq\gamma\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}(\mu-\hat{\mu})^{2}\leq\gamma\,C\,C_{0}^{2}\,\|\bar{m}\|_{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\bar{m}\,|Dw-D\hat{w}|^{2}\\

Lemma A.1
.

For given

and

be
the solution of

Then there exist constants

(only depending on

\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|Dv|^{2}\leq C\,\|\tilde{v}(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}+C\int_{t}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}[|f|^{2}+|\tilde{v}|^{2}]\,.

∎

Now we turn to the Fokker-Planck equation.

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that

V\in L^{\infty}((t_{0},T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})
,

\rho\in L^{2}((t_{0},T);L^{2}_{0}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))

\nu>0
and a constant
C
(only depending on
\kappa,d

\|V\|_{\infty}
) such that

\|\rho(t)\|_{2}^{2}\leq C\,\left\{e^{-\nu(t-t_{0})}\,\|\rho(t_{0})\|_{2}^{2}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|F|^{2}\right\}

(A.3)

Moreover, if

is such that

,
then we have, for every

\int_{t_{1}}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\|\rho(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq C\left\{e^{-\delta t_{1}}\|\rho(t_{0})\|^{2}_{2}e^{-2\nu(t_{1}-t_{0})}+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\|F(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\right\}

(A.4)

for some
C>0
depending on

and independent of
\delta

Proof.

Estimate (

) is already proved in the Appendix of
[
4
]

For a fixed

\begin{cases}-v_{t}-\kappa\Delta v+Dv\cdot V=0&\text{in $(t_{0},t)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}$,}\\

\|\tilde{v}(s)\|_{2}\leq Ce^{-\nu(t-s)}\qquad\forall s\leq t

.
Therefore we get

\|\rho(t)\|^{2}_{2}\leq C\left\{\|\rho(t_{0})\|^{2}_{2}e^{-2\nu(t-t_{0})}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t-1}e^{-\nu(t-s)}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}ds+\int^{t}_{t-1}e^{\delta(t-s)}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}ds\right\}.

(A.10)

Now we multiply (
A.10
) by
e^{-\delta t}
and we integrate in
(t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1,T)
, obtaining

\int_{t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\|\rho(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq C\left\{\|\rho(t_{0})\|^{2}_{2}\int_{t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1}^{T}e^{-2\nu(t-t_{0})-\delta t}dt\right.\\
\left.+\int_{t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\int_{t_{0}}^{t-1}e^{-\nu(t-s)}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}dsdt+\int_{t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1}^{T}\int^{t}_{t-1}e^{-\delta s}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}dsdt\right\},

and by exchanging the order of integration we easily get

\int_{t_{1}\vee t_{0}+1}^{T}e^{-\delta t}\|\rho(t)\|^{2}_{2}dt\leq C\left\{e^{-\delta t_{1}}\|\rho(t_{0})\|^{2}_{2}e^{-2\nu(t_{1}-t_{0})}+\int_{t_{0}}^{T-1}e^{-\delta s}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}ds+\int_{t_{0}}^{T}e^{-\delta s}\|F(s)\|^{2}_{2}ds\right\}

for a possibly different constant
C
, depending on
\nu
but independent of
\delta
. Going back to (
A.8
), we get the remaining bound (if needed) on
\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{0}+1}\|\rho(t)\|^{2}_{2}
by a straightforward application of (
A.6
) and (
A.7
).
∎

Finally, we include in this Appendix a regularity lemma on the Fokker-Planck equation.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\rho
be a non-negative classical solution to

\rho_{t}-\kappa\Delta\rho-{\rm div}(\rho V)=0\qquad\text{in $(0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}}$}.

Then, for all
\varepsilon>0
and
p^{\prime}<\frac{d}{d-2}

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq\varepsilon\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|V|^{2}\rho+C\left(1+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}.

for some
C>0
depending on
\kappa,p^{\prime},T,d
.

Proof.

Let
G(x,t)
be the kernel of
\partial_{t}(\cdot)-\kappa\Delta(\cdot)
(on
{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
), and denote by
\star
and
\star\star
the space and space-time convolution respectively.
Then, the Duhamel representation formula yields

\rho=\rho(0)\star G(t)+(\rho V)\star\star\,D_{x}G.

For
q>1
and
\gamma\in(1,2)
to be chosen below, Young’s inequality for convolutions yields

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\|G\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\\
+C\|\rho V\|_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}+\gamma-1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}\|D_{x}G\|_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}\gamma-p^{\prime}+1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}.

(A.11)

By Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\|\rho V\|_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}+\gamma-1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}V\|_{L^{\gamma}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}}\|_{L^{\gamma^{\prime}}\big((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|V|^{\gamma}\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\|\rho\|^{\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}}_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}.

Moreover,
G
and
D_{x}G
are bounded in
L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))
and
L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}\gamma-p^{\prime}+1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)
respectively, provided that

\frac{2}{dp^{\prime}}-\frac{d}{2}+1>0,\qquad\frac{d}{2}\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma+p^{\prime}-1}{p^{\prime}\gamma}-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{2}>0,

that is equivalent to

p^{\prime}<\frac{d}{d-2},\qquad p^{\prime}<\frac{d}{d-\gamma}.

The first inequality is true by the standing assumptions. Pick then
\gamma<2
so that
p^{\prime}<\frac{d}{d-\gamma}<\frac{d}{d-2}
. Then,

\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}\|G\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}+C\|\rho V\|_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}+\gamma-1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}\|D_{x}G\|_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{\frac{p^{\prime}\gamma}{p^{\prime}\gamma+p^{\prime}-1}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}\\
\leq C_{1}\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}+C_{1}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|V|^{\gamma}\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\|\rho\|^{\frac{1}{\gamma^{\prime}}}_{L^{1}\big((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})\big)}.

Using Young’s inequality, and plugging back into (
A.11
), we obtain

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq C_{2}\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}+C_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|V|^{\gamma}\rho\right).

A further application of Young’s inequality yields, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\|\rho\|_{L^{1}((0,T);L^{p^{\prime}}({\mathbb{T}^{d}}))}\leq C_{2}\|\rho(0)\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{d}})}+\varepsilon\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}|V|^{2}\rho\right)+\frac{C_{2}^{\frac{2}{2-\gamma}}}{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\rho\right),

and since
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\rho(t)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{d}}}\rho(0)
for all
t
, we conclude.
∎

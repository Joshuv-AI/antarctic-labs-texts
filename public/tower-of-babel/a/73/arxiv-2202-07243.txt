Introduction

The theory of projective representations of finite groups was first studied by Schur in a series of papers
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.
A projective representation of a group
G
is a homomorphism from
G
to projective general linear group
\mathrm{PGL}(V)
, where
V
is a complex vector space. So
\rho:G\to\mathrm{GL}(V)
is a map such that
\rho(1)=id_{V}
and there is a
2
-cocycle
\alpha:G\times G\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
satisfying

\rho(xy)=\alpha(x,y)\rho(x)\rho(y).

Then, we say
\rho
is an
\alpha
-representation.

For cyclic groups the irreducible projective representations are same as the ordinary representations (up to equivalence) and hence one dimensional. However, this is not true in general for abelian groups. For abelian groups, these have been studied by several authors in
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
.

Recently, the first named and third named authors studied the projective representations of the discrete Heisenberg group over cyclic rings in
[
6
]
. This paper may be considered as a continuation of
[
6
]
, where we study the projective representations of discrete Heisenberg groups over the unital rings
R
of order
p^{2},
where
p
is an odd prime. The discrete Heisenberg group of rank one over
R
, denoted by
\mathbb{H}_{2n+1}(R)
, is the set
R\times R^{n}\times R^{n}
with the multiplication given by

\begin{array}[]{l}(c_{1},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})(c^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{n},a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{n})\\
=(c_{1}+c^{\prime}_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}*b_{i}^{\prime},a_{1}+b^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,b_{n}+b^{\prime}_{n},b_{1}+a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a_{n}+a^{\prime}_{n}),\end{array}

where

denotes the multiplication in
R
.

Now onwards, we consider
R
to be a ring of order
p^{2}
with unity. It is easy to prove that any such ring

is commutative and is isomorphic to one of the following:

•

\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z},

•

\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},

•

\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2}),

•

\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}.

We remark that the results in
[
6
]
take care of the case

for

Indeed, one of the main results in
[
6
]
is that, for

every irreducible projective representation of

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we fix the notation and recall a few results which will be used in the upcoming sections. For a group
G
, we use
G^{\prime}
to denote its commutator subgroup and
Z(G)
denotes the center of
G
. The first result, due to Schur, describes the Schur multiplier of a direct product in terms of the Schur multipliers of its components, see
[
11
, Theorem 2.2.10]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For finite groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
,

{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G_{1}\times G_{2},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\cong{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G_{1},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\times{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G_{2},\mathbb{C}^{\times})\times{\mathrm{Hom}}(\frac{G_{1}}{G_{1}^{\prime}}\otimes\frac{G_{2}}{G_{2}^{\prime}},\mathbb{C}^{\times}).

A central extension,

(2.0.1)

1\to A\to G\to G/A\to 1

is called a
stem
extension, if
A\subseteq Z(G)\cap G^{\prime}
. For a central extension (
2.0.1
), the map
{\text{tra}}:{\mathrm{Hom}}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
given by
f\mapsto[{\text{tra}}(f)]
,

{\text{tra}}(f)(\overline{x},\overline{y})=f(\mu(\overline{x})\mu(\bar{y})\mu(\bar{xy})^{-1}),\,\,\overline{x},\overline{y}\in G/A,

for a section
\mu:G/A\rightarrow G
is a group homorphism and is called the transgression homomorphism. The map
\inf:{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\to{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
given by
[\alpha]\mapsto[\inf(\alpha)]
, where
\inf(\alpha)(x,y)=\alpha(xA,yA)
, is a group homomorphism and is called the inflation homomorphism, The spectral sequence
for cohomology of groups yields the following exact sequence.

(2.0.2)

1\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\xrightarrow{{\text{tra}}}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G/A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\xrightarrow{\inf}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\xrightarrow{({\text{res}},\chi)}{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\oplus{\mathrm{Hom}}(G/G^{\prime}\otimes A,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}),

where the map
\chi
, defined by Iwahori and Matsumoto
[
9
]
, is given by
\chi([\alpha])(gG^{\prime}\otimes a)=\alpha(g,a)\alpha(a,g)^{-1}
for
g\in G,a\in A
.

Lemma 2.2

(Hall–Witt identity)
.

Let
G
be a finite group of nilpotency class
3
. For
x,y,z\in G
, we have

[x,y^{-1},z][y,z^{-1},x][z,x^{-1},y]=1.

2.1.
Projective representations of a finite group

In this section we include the results that we require regarding the projective representations of a finite group. We will use these in Section
6
.

Let
G
be a finite group. We use
Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
to denote the set of all
2
-cocycles of
G
. For
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, we use
\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)
to denote the set of equivalence classes of irreducible
\alpha
-representations of
G
. For
\alpha=1
, we use
\mathrm{Irr}(G)
instead of
\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)
and call these to be the set of ordinary irreducible representations of
G
. Let
G^{\star}
be a representation group of
G
with
A\cong{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
such that

(2.1.1)

\displaystyle 1\rightarrow A\rightarrow G^{\star}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1

is a stem extension. The following well known result relates the projective representations of
G
and the ordinary ones of
G^{\star}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\alpha
be a
2
-cocycle of
G
. Let
\chi\in{\mathrm{Hom}}(A,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
be such that
\mathrm{tra}(\chi)=[\alpha]
.
There is a bijective correspondence between

\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Irr}{(G^{\star}\mid\chi)}

obtained via inflation. In particular, we obtain the following bijection via inflation.

\bigcup_{[\alpha]\in{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(G,{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})}\mathrm{Irr}^{\alpha}(G)\leftrightarrow\mathrm{Irr}{(G^{\star})}

A proof of this follows from the proof of
[
12
, Chapter 3, Section 3]
, see also
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
. Therefore, to determine the projective representations of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2})\big)
and
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}\big)
, it suffices to determine the ordinary representations of their representation groups. We first discuss a general method that will work in our situation.

Let
G
be a finite group with an abelian normal subgroup
N
such that
G/N
is abelian. Let
\chi:N\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a one dimensional irreducible representation of
N
and let
I_{G}(\chi)=\{g\in G\mid\chi^{g}=\chi\}
be the inertia group of
\chi
in
G
. Let
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi)
denote the set of inequivalent irreducible representations of
G
lying above
\chi
, that is
\rho\in\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi)
if and only if
\mathrm{Hom}_{N}(\rho|_{N},\chi)
is non-trivial.

By Clifford theory, the map
\rho\mapsto{\text{Ind}}_{I_{G}(\chi)}^{G}(\rho)
gives a bijection between
\mathrm{Irr}(I_{G}(\chi)\mid\chi)
and
\mathrm{Irr}(G\mid\chi)
. Therefore it suffices to determine
\mathrm{Irr}(I_{G}(\chi)\mid\chi)
for every
\chi
. Below we mention a method that helps us in this direction.

Let
K
be a finite group with an abelian normal subgroup
N
such that
K/N
is abelian. Let
\chi:N\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a one dimensional irreducible representation of
N
such that
\chi^{k}=\chi
for all
k\in K
, that is
I_{K}(\chi)=K
. Let
T
be a fixed set of left coset representatives of
N
in
K
. Define a map
\chi^{\prime}:K\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by
\chi^{\prime}(kn)=\chi(n)
for all
k\in T
and
n\in N
. Following
[
8
, Chapter 11]
, let
\alpha\in Z^{2}(K,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
be a 2-cocycle of
K
associated to
\chi^{\prime}
and
\beta\in Z^{2}(K/N,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
be defined by
\beta(gN,hN)=\alpha(g,h)
for
g,h\in K
.

Lemma 2.4
.

The following are equivalent.

(1)

The character
\chi
extends to
K
.

(2)

[K,K]\subseteq\mathrm{Ker}(\chi)
.

(3)

[\beta]=1
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1) and (2) follows from the fact that
[K,K]\subseteq N
and

is an abelian group. The equivalence of (1) and (3) follows from
[

, Theorem 11.7]
.
∎

With the above notations, the following result can be obtained from
[

.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a dimension preserving bijection between the sets
\mathrm{Irr}(K\mid\chi)
and
\mathrm{Irr}^{\beta^{-1}}(K/N)
.

Hence it boils down to understand the projective representations of the quotient group

. For our case, this quotient group will turn out to be an abelian group. The projective representations of abelian groups are well studied, and we use some of these results. In particular, we will use the following lemma without further reference.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be a finite abelian group. Then the following hold:

(1)

For any fixed
\alpha\in Z^{2}(G,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, all irreducible representations in

(2)

For

(3)

For
|G|=p^{4}
and

.

Here (1) follows from

and

divides the order of

. ∎

At this point, we mention the ways to identify the non-degenerate cocycles of a group

The following conditions are equivalent.

(2)

(3)

(4)

\chi\big([x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}z_{1}^{c_{1}}z_{2}^{c_{2}},x_{1}^{a_{1}^{\prime}}x_{2}^{a_{2}^{\prime}}y_{1}^{b_{1}^{\prime}}y_{2}^{b_{2}^{\prime}}z_{1}^{c_{1}^{\prime}}z_{2}^{c_{2}^{\prime}}]\big)=1.

By using (
6.1
)
and substituting the values
\chi(w_{i}),\chi(v_{j})
for
1\leq i\leq 4,1\leq j\leq 2
in terms of
\chi(u_{2})
we get,

\chi(u_{2})^{(a_{1}^{\prime}a_{2}-a_{1}a_{2}^{\prime})\big(r_{1}-r_{2}r_{8}-r_{3}(r_{7}r_{8}-r_{5})+r_{4}r_{8}^{2}-\frac{r_{8}}{2}+\frac{r_{8}^{2}}{2}\big)}=1.

Since
a_{1},a_{1}^{\prime},a_{2},a_{2}^{\prime}
are arbitrary, we must have

(6.1.3)

\displaystyle r_{1}-r_{2}r_{8}-r_{3}(r_{7}r_{8}-r_{5})+r_{4}r_{8}^{2}+{r_{8}\choose 2}=0.

As earlier, this implies that
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p^{2}
dimensional irreducible representations if (
6.1.3
) holds and of dimension
p^{3}
otherwise. This completes our discussion regarding the irreducible representations of
G^{\star}
.

6.2.
Non-degenerate 2-cocycles of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2})\big)

In this section we describe the degenerate and non-degenerate cocycles of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2})\big)
. Recall that upto cohomologous every
2
-cocycle
\alpha
of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2})\big)
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha\big(x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}z_{1}^{c_{1}}z_{2}^{c_{2}},x_{1}^{a_{1}^{\prime}}x_{2}^{a_{2}^{\prime}}y_{1}^{b_{1}^{\prime}}y_{2}^{b_{2}^{\prime}}z_{1}^{c_{1}^{\prime}}z_{2}^{c_{2}^{\prime}}\big)=\mu_{1}^{a_{2}a_{1}^{\prime}}\mu_{2}^{b_{2}a_{1}^{\prime}}\mu_{3}^{b_{2}a_{2}^{\prime}}\mu_{4}^{b_{2}b_{1}^{\prime}}\mu_{5}^{b_{1}{a_{1}^{\prime}\choose 2}+a_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}}

(6.2.1)

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\mu_{6}^{b_{2}{a_{1}^{\prime}\choose 2}+a_{1}^{\prime}c_{2}+a_{2}^{\prime}c_{1}}\mu_{7}^{a_{1}^{\prime}{b_{1}\choose 2}-b_{1}c_{1}^{\prime}}\mu_{8}^{a_{2}^{\prime}{b_{1}\choose 2}-b_{2}c_{1}^{\prime}-b_{1}c_{2}^{\prime}},

where
\mu_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
such that
\mu_{i}^{p}=1
for all
i
. Following the notations of Section
6.1
, if
\mu_{6}\neq 1
, we assume that
\mu_{i}=\mu_{6}^{r_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 8
with
r_{6}=1

and, if
\mu_{8}\neq 1
we assume that
\mu_{i}=\mu_{8}^{t_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq 7
with
t_{8}=1
. Let
G^{\star},N,\chi
be as given in the Section
6.1
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The cocycle
\alpha
as given in (
6.2
) is non-degenerate if and only if one of the following holds.

(i)

\mu_{6}=1,

\mu_{8}\neq 1
and
t_{4}\neq t_{3}t_{7}
.

(ii)

\mu_{8}=1
,
\mu_{6}\neq 1
and
r_{1}\neq-r_{3}r_{5}
.

(iii)

\mu_{8}\neq 1,\mu_{6}\neq 1
and
r_{1}-r_{2}r_{8}-r_{3}(r_{7}r_{8}-r_{5})+r_{4}r_{8}^{2}+{r_{8}\choose 2}\neq 0
.

In particular, the number of non-degenerate cocycles of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{F}_{p}[t]/(t^{2})\big)
is
p^{5}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)
.

Proof.

Let
A=\langle u_{i},v_{i},w_{j},i=1,2,1\leq j\leq 4\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{8}
be a subgroup of
G^{\star}
. Then
A
is a central subgroup of
G^{\star}
such that
1\rightarrow A\rightarrow G^{\star}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
is a stem extension. For this case, we note that
{\text{tra}}(\chi|_{A})=[\alpha]
. This fact, Lemma
2.7
and Section
6.1
give the result.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We remark that the existence of
p^{5}
number of non-degenerate cocycles out of the above
p^{5}(p-1)^{2}(p+1)
has already appeared in
[
4
, Theorem A]
.

6.3.
Projective representations of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}\big)

In this section we first give a construction of projective representations of
G=\mathbb{H}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
. As mentioned earlier, it is enough to give a construction of all ordinary irreducible representations of a representation group of
G
.
From
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
,

G^{\star}=\langle x,y\mid[y,x]=z,[z,x]=z_{1},[z,y]=z_{2},x^{p^{2}}=y^{p^{2}}=z^{p^{2}}=1\rangle

is a representation group of
G
. Note that,
x^{a}y^{b}z^{c},x^{a^{\prime}}y^{b^{\prime}}z^{c^{\prime}}\in G^{\star}
satisfy

[x^{a}y^{b}z^{c},x^{a^{\prime}}y^{b^{\prime}}z^{c^{\prime}}]=z^{a^{\prime}b-ab^{\prime}}z_{1}^{b{a^{\prime}\choose 2}-b^{\prime}{a\choose 2}+(a^{\prime}c-ac^{\prime})}z_{2}^{a^{\prime}{b\choose 2}-a{b^{\prime}\choose 2}+(b^{\prime}c-bc^{\prime})}.

Consider
N=\langle z,z_{1},z_{2}\rangle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})^{3}
, a normal subgroup of
G^{\star}
.
Any character
\chi:N\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is given by
\chi(z_{i})=\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2
and
\chi(z)=\lambda
, where
\lambda_{i}^{p^{2}}=1
for
i=1,2
and
\lambda^{p^{2}}=1
. The inertia group
S_{\chi}=I_{G^{\star}}(\chi)
of
\chi
consists of the elements
x^{a}y^{b}n,n\in N
such that
\chi([x^{a}y^{b},z])=1
, that is
\chi(z_{1})^{a}\chi(z_{2})^{b}=1
.
We consider the various cases of
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
s.

(i) Assume
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=1
. In this case
S_{\chi}=G^{\star}
. We note that
|S_{\chi}/N|=|G^{\star}|/|N|=p^{4}
,
S_{\chi}/N
is abelian. Hence by Clifford theory, depending on
\chi(z)
,
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of representations of either
1
,
p
or
p^{2}
dimension.

(ii) Assume
\lambda_{1}=1,\lambda_{2}\neq 1
such that
\lambda_{2}^{p}=1
. Then
S_{\chi}=\langle N,x,y^{p}\rangle
. Therefore
|S_{\chi}/N|=p^{3}
and
S_{\chi}/N
is abelian. Hence
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p
-dimensional representations if
\chi
extends to
S_{\chi}
and if
\chi
does not extend to
S_{\chi}
then
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists to
p^{2}
dimensional representations. We use Lemma
2.4
and
\chi(z_{2})^{pt}=\chi(z_{2})^{pt_{1}}=1
to observe that
\chi
extends to
S_{\chi}
if and only if

\chi([x^{a}y^{pt}z^{c},x^{a^{\prime}}y^{pt_{1}}z^{c^{\prime}}])=\chi(z)^{p(ta^{\prime}-t_{1}a)}=1.

Therefore
\chi
extends if and only if
\lambda^{p}=1
.

(iii) Assume
\lambda_{1}=1
and order of
\lambda_{2}
is
p^{2}
. In this case, we obtain
S_{\chi}=\langle N,x\rangle
. Since
S_{\chi}/N
is cyclic of order
p^{2}
. By Clifford theory,
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p^{2}
dimensional irreducible representations.

(iv) For
\lambda_{2}=1
and
\lambda_{1}\neq 1
, we proceed as above.

(iv) Suppose
\lambda_{1}\neq 1,\lambda_{2}\neq 1
and
\lambda_{1}^{p}=\lambda_{2}^{p}=1
. Assume that
\chi(z_{1})=\chi(z_{2})^{r}
for some
1\leq r<p-1
. For the inertia group computations,
a
and
b
are such that
\chi(z_{1})^{a}\chi(z_{2})^{b}=1
, that is
\chi(z_{2})^{b+ra}=1
. This implies

b\in\{-ra+pt\mid 0\leq t\leq p-1\}
. Therefore
S_{\chi}=\langle N,x^{a}y^{-ra+pt}\rangle
. So
|S_{\chi}/N|=p^{3}
and
S_{\chi}/N
is abelian. As in (ii),
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p
-dimensional representations if
\chi
extends to
S_{\chi}
and if
\chi
does not extend to
S_{\chi}
then
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists to
p^{2}
dimensional representations. So it remains to determine when the conditions for which
\chi
extend to
S_{\chi}
.
By Lemma
2.4
,
\chi
extends to
S_{\chi}
if and only if
\chi([x^{a}y^{-ra+pt}z^{c},x^{a^{\prime}}y^{-ra^{\prime}+pt^{\prime}}z^{c^{\prime}}])=1
. This is equivalent to

\chi(z)^{p(a^{\prime}t-at^{\prime})}\chi(z_{2})^{r^{2}aa^{\prime}(a-a^{\prime})}=1,

where
a,a^{\prime},t,t^{\prime}
are arbitrary. Therefore, we must have
\lambda_{2}^{r^{2}}=1
and
\chi(z)^{p}=1
. This implies that
r
is a multiple of
p
. This in turn gives that
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}^{r}=1
, a contradiction. Hence
\chi
does not extend to
S_{\chi}
in this case. Therefore
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists to
p^{2}
dimensional representations.

(v) Assume that
\lambda_{1}\neq 1
and
\lambda_{2}
is of order
p^{2}
. Assume that
\lambda_{1}=(\lambda_{2})^{r}
. It is easy to see that
S_{\chi}=\langle N,x^{a}y^{-ra}\rangle
, and therefore
S_{\chi}/N
is an abelian group of order
p^{2}
. As in (iv), we obtain that
\chi
extends to
S_{\chi}
if and only if
\lambda_{1}^{p}=1
. Hence in this case
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p^{2}
-dimensional representations. If both
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are of order
p^{2}
, then
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p^{3}
-dimensional representations.

(vi) Assume that order of
\lambda_{1}
is
p^{2}
and that of
\lambda_{2}
is
p
. This case can be done parallel to (iv). Here we obtain that
\mathrm{Irr}(G^{\star}\mid\chi)
consists of
p^{2}
dimensional representations.

6.4.
Non-degenerate 2-cocycles of
\mathbb{H}_{3}\big(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}\big)

In this section we describe the non-degenerate cocycles of the group
\mathbb{H}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
.

Theorem 6.3
.

The cocycle
\alpha
as given in (
6.4.1
) is non-degenerate if and only if
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are of order
p^{2}
. In particular, the number of non-degenerate cocycles is
(p-1)^{2}p^{2}
.

Proof.

Recall that every
2
-cocycle of
G=\mathbb{H}_{3}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z})
, upto cohomologous, is given by

(6.4.1)

\displaystyle\alpha\big(x^{a_{1}}y^{b_{1}}z^{c_{1}},x^{a_{1}^{\prime}}y^{b_{1}^{\prime}}z^{c_{1}^{\prime}}\big)=\lambda_{1}^{c_{1}^{\prime}a_{1}+b_{1}{a_{1}^{\prime}\choose 2}+a_{1}b_{1}a_{1}^{\prime}}\lambda_{2}^{c_{1}^{\prime}b_{1}+a_{1}^{\prime}{b_{1}\choose 2}},\lambda_{1}^{p^{2}}=\lambda_{2}^{p^{2}}=1.

Let
Z
be a subgroup of
G^{\star}
generated by
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
. Then
Z\subseteq N
and
1\rightarrow Z\rightarrow G^{\star}\rightarrow G\rightarrow 1
is a stem extension. For any character
\chi
of
N
as given above, let
\chi|_{Z}
denote its restriction to the group
Z
. We note that
{\text{tra}}(\chi|_{Z})=[\alpha]
. The result now follows from above description of ordinary irreducible representations of
G^{\star}
, Theorem
2.3
and Lemma
2.7
.
∎

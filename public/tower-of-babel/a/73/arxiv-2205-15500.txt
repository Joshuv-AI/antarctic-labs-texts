Brane Dynamics of Holographic BCFTs
Keisuke Izumi

Abstract
In this paper we study various dynamical aspects of the AdS/BCFT correspondence in higher dimensions. We study properties of holographic stress energy tensor by analyzing the metric perturbation in the gravity dual. We also calculate the stress energy tensor for a locally excited state on a half plane in a free scalar CFT. Both of them satisfy a reflective boundary condition that is expected for any BCFTs. We also study the behavior of the scalar field perturbation in the AdS/BCFT setup and show that they also show complete reflections. Moreover, we find that the entanglement entropy of a BCFT computed from the AdS/BCFT matched with that calculated from the Island formula, which supports the Island/BCFT correspondence in higher dimensions. Finally we show how we can calculate one point functions in a BCFT in our gravity dual.

Stress energy tensors in BCFTs

We consider a
d

d

d-1

In this section, as a preparation of our later arguments, we would like to explain what we expect as the behavior of the stress energy tenor in BCFTs. We will also describe the gravity results for BCFT
2

\displaystyle I\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int d^{d}x\left[\partial_{a}\phi\partial^{a}\phi\,+\,\frac{d-2}{4(d-1)}\,R_{0}\phi^{2}\right]\,,

-dimensional bulk is

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{AdS_{d+1}}=d\rho^{2}+\left(\mathfrak{a}^{2}(\rho)\gamma_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}\right)dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu},

(3.7)

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}(\rho):=\ell\cosh\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\right)

(3.8)

and
\gamma_{\mu\nu}
is the
d
-dimensional AdS metric with the unit curvature.
The Riemann curvature of the background metric (i.e. for the case with
h_{\mu\nu}=0
) becomes

\displaystyle R^{(0)}_{MNAB}=-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\left(g_{MA}g_{NB}-g_{MB}g_{NA}\right).

(3.9)

When the Einstein equation with the cosmological constant
\Lambda
gives this solution,

\Lambda
should be

\displaystyle\Lambda=-\frac{d(d-1)}{2\ell^{2}}.

(3.10)

For the perturbation
h_{\mu\nu}
, we use the following gauge condition:

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{\mu}h_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad h^{\mu}{}_{\mu}=0.

(3.11)

Note that it gives

\displaystyle\nabla_{M}h^{M}{}_{N}=0.

(3.12)

This gauge condition is called transverse-traceless (TT) gauge,
which is often used for the analysis of gravitational waves in the vacuum
and of gravitational perturbations in
braneworld model

100

.

3.2
Perturbed equation in the bulk

The perturbation of the bulk Ricci tensor is calculated as

\displaystyle R_{MN}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{(0)}_{MN}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla_{A}\nabla_{M}h^{A}{}_{N}+\nabla_{A}\nabla_{N}h^{A}{}_{M}-\Box h_{MN}-\nabla_{M}\nabla_{N}h\right)+{\cal O}\left(h_{MN}^{2}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{\ell^{2}}\left(g^{(0)}_{MN}+h_{MN}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\Box h_{MN}-\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}h_{MN}+{\cal O}\left(h_{MN}^{2}\right).

Then vacuum equation gives the perturbation equation in the bulk:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{MN}-\frac{1}{2}Rg_{MN}+\Lambda g_{MN}\qquad\left(=:G_{MN}+\Lambda g_{MN}\right)

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{d}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d(d+1)}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d(d-1)}{\ell^{2}}\right)\left(g^{(0)}_{MN}+h_{MN}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Box+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}\right)h_{MN}+{\cal O}\left(h_{MN}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\Box+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}\right)h_{MN}+{\cal O}\left(h_{MN}^{2}\right),

where
\delta\left(\cdots\right)
means the first order terms of
\left(\cdots\right)
with respect to
h_{MN}
.
Therefore, the bulk equation of the metric perturbation becomes

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Box+\frac{2}{\ell^{2}}\right)h_{\mu\nu}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}+(d-4)\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-2(d-2)\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)^{2}h_{\mu\nu}-2\left(\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\right)^{\prime}h_{\mu\nu}+\mathfrak{a}^{-2}\left(\tilde{\Box}+2\right)h_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{2}\left(H_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}+d\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}H_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}\right)+\left(\tilde{\Box}+2\right)H_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}^{2}\left(H_{\mu\nu}^{\prime\prime}+dl\tanh^{2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\right)H_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}+\cosh^{-2}\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\right)\left(\tilde{\Box}+2\right)H_{\mu\nu}\right),

where

\displaystyle h_{MN}=:\mathfrak{a}^{2}H_{MN}

(3.16)

and

\displaystyle\Box:=g^{(0)MN}\nabla_{M}\nabla_{N},\qquad\tilde{\Box}:=\gamma^{\mu\nu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}.

(3.17)

Note that
\Box
includes covariant derivatives, and thus, the operation to
h_{\mu\nu}
is different from that to scalar field.
Note that “
2
” in
\left(\tilde{\Box}+2\right)
is required for spin-2 fields, which is not mass.
Due to the curvature of AdS, this “
2
” is generated from the kinetic term of spin-2 action.
Equation (
3.15
) can be solved with the separation of the variables.

Hereinafter, we set
\ell=1
.
Solutions could be written in separable form
H_{\mu\nu}=\sum_{i}{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}(\rho)Y^{(i)}_{\mu\nu}(x^{\mu})
.
With this separable form of

, Eq. (

) becomes the following two different

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}_{,\rho\rho}+d\,\tanh\rho{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}_{,\rho}=-\frac{\lambda^{(i)}_{\rho}}{\cosh^{2}\rho}{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\Box}+2-\lambda^{(i)}_{\rho}\right)Y^{(i)}_{\mu\nu}=0.

(3.19)

This decomposition is interpreted as a Kaluza-Klein reduction;
the equation for extra-dimension Eq. (
3.18
) fixes the Kaluza-Klein (KK) mass as

, while Eq. (
3.19
) is
d
-dimensional equation for spin-2 field with (KK) mass

d
-dimensional AdS spacetime.
Hereinafter, we omit the superscript

for the simple notation, when the difference of

Let us solve Eq. (

Equation (

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{R}}=\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}}\lambda_{\rho}{\mathcal{R}},

(3.20)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mathcal{R}}:=-\frac{d}{d\zeta}\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\frac{d}{d\zeta},

(3.21)

\displaystyle\zeta:=\tanh\rho.

(3.22)

Solutions of Eq. (

) are expressed by Ferrers’ associated Legendre functions

3

Note that it is different from Hobson’s associated Legendre functions.

and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}=\left[c_{1}P^{\mu}_{l}(\zeta)+c_{2}Q^{\mu}_{l}(\zeta)\right](1-\zeta^{2})^{\frac{d}{4}},

(3.23)

where

\displaystyle l=\nu-\frac{1}{2},\qquad\mu=\frac{d}{2},\qquad\nu=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(d-1)^{2}+4\lambda_{\rho}}.

Since, near the AdS boundary

(3.25)

\displaystyle 2\sin(\pi\mu)Q^{\mu}_{l}(\zeta)\,\simeq\,\pi\cos(\pi\mu)P^{\mu}_{l}(\zeta),

(3.26)

the Dirichlet boundary condition on the AdS boundary gives the relation between

and

\displaystyle 2\sin(\pi\mu)c_{1}=-\pi\cos\left(\pi\mu\right)c_{2}.

(3.27)

Note that, because of

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}=\begin{cases}P^{\mu}_{l}(\zeta)(1-\zeta^{2})^{\frac{d}{4}}&\mbox{(if $d$ is even)}\\

(3.28)

However, with the form of Eq. (
3.23
),
both cases can be analysed simultaneously
and if the bulk field has mass, which we will face on in Sec.
4
,
the mode function

has a generic form of Eq. (

Therefore, we use Eq. (

For

To avoid this situation, we use

Mode function

For

Therefore,

is a unified expression which can be used for any

.
Hence, we write the bulk mode function as

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad{\mathcal{R}}\,=\,B_{0}P^{-\mu}_{l}(\zeta)(1-\zeta^{2})^{\frac{d}{4}}\,,\qquad\mbox{(for\, any\, $d$)}

(3.29)

where

Another boundary, namely junction condition on the brane, will be discussed in the next section,

3.3
Junction condition

The boundary condition on the brane, that is, the junction condition (
2.17
) or equally

\displaystyle K^{\mu}{}_{\nu}-K\delta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,\hat{=}\,-\frac{1}{2}\sigma\delta^{\mu}{}_{\nu}

(3.30)

is required locally.
In the derivation of the junction condition for the metric perturbations,
it is better to use the local coordinate based on the brane called Gaussian normal coordinate.
Note that the brane position can be perturbed, that is, it does not generically exist on
\rho=\rho_{*}
surface in the TT gauge, where
\rho_{*}
is a constant.
By the coordinate transformation
\bar{\rho}=\rho+\varphi(x^{\mu})
from the TT gauge,
we can set the brane to be on
\bar{\rho}=
const. surface.
This coordinate transformation generates the non-zero
(\bar{\rho},x^{\mu})
-components of metric perturbations.
Considering the coordinate transformation

\displaystyle\bar{x}^{\nu}=x^{\nu}+\xi^{\nu},

(3.31)

we can remove the cross components.
Then, the Gaussian normal coordinate
(\bar{\rho},\bar{x}^{\mu})
is achieved
[
101
]
.
The relation between the metric perturbations
\bar{h}_{MN}
in the Gaussian normal coordinate and
h_{MN}
in TT gauge
is

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{MN}=h_{MN}+\nabla_{M}\xi_{N}+\nabla_{M}\xi_{N},

(3.32)

where
\xi^{\rho}=\varphi
.
The Gaussian normal coordinate is chosen such that the
(\rho\rho)
and
(\rho\mu)
components of the metric perturbation are zero, as with the TT gauge condition.
The former

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\rho\rho}=h_{\rho\rho}(=0)

(3.33)

gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}\xi_{\rho}=0.

(3.34)

Therefore,
\xi_{\rho}
depends only on
x^{\mu}
, which is consistent with

\displaystyle\xi^{\rho}=\xi_{\rho}=\varphi(x^{\mu}).

(3.35)

The latter one, namely vanishing of
(\rho\mu)
component, means

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\rho\mu}=h_{\rho\mu}(=0).

(3.36)

This gives

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{\rho}\xi_{\mu}+\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\rho}=\partial_{\rho}\xi_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\xi_{\rho}-2\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\xi_{\mu}.

(3.37)

The solution for
\xi^{\mu}(=g^{(0)\mu M}\xi_{M})
is

\displaystyle\xi^{\mu}=-\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\varphi+\chi^{\mu}(x^{\alpha})

(3.38)

where
\chi^{\mu}
is an integration constant.Then, the relation in the
(\mu\nu)
component is

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}+\nabla_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}+\nabla_{\nu}\xi_{\mu}

(3.39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}+2\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\left(\gamma_{\mu\nu}\varphi-\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\varphi\right)+2\mathfrak{a}^{2}\tilde{\nabla}_{(\mu}\left(\gamma_{\nu)\alpha}\chi^{\alpha}\right).

The junction condition (
3.30
) for the leading order gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\,\hat{=}\,-\frac{\sigma}{2(d-1)},

(3.40)

where
\sigma
is the brane tension.
This fixes the background position of brane, which is consistent with Eq. (
3.5
).

The perturbed junction condition in the Gaussian normal coordinate is written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}\,\hat{=}\,-\frac{\sigma}{2(d-1)}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}.

(3.41)

Substituting the background equation (
3.40
) into the above, we have

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-2\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}\,\hat{=}\,0.

(3.42)

From the relation between the metric perturbations in the Gaussian normal coordinate and TT gauge (
3.39
),
the perturbed junction condition for the TT gauge is obtained as

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-2\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}h_{\mu\nu}\,\hat{=}\,\frac{2}{l^{2}}\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\varphi-\gamma_{\mu\nu}\varphi\right).

(3.43)

The trace of this equation gives

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\Box}-d\right)\varphi\,\hat{=}\,0

(3.44)

and the traceless part becomes

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-2\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}h_{\mu\nu}\,\hat{=}\,2\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\varphi.

(3.45)

This boundary condition says that the brane bending
\varphi
excites a metric perturbation in the bulk.
This mode is called a brane bending mode.

Let us analyze the brane bending mode first.
Note that divergence of the right hand side in Eq. (
3.45
) becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{\mu}\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d-1}{d}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\tilde{\Box}+\tilde{R}_{\nu}{}^{\alpha}\tilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\right)\varphi

(3.46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{d}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}\left(\tilde{\Box}-d\right)\varphi.

With Eq. (
3.44
), Eq. (
3.46
) shows that the divergence of the right hand side of Eq. (
3.45
) becomes zero.
Moreover, the right hand side of Eq. (
3.45
) is traceless.
Hence, it is a transverse-traceless tensor:

\displaystyle Y^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\nu}=\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\varphi

(3.47)

satisfies

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}^{\mu}Y^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\nu}=0,\qquad Y^{(\varphi)}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}=0.

(3.48)

This means that a scalar mode with
m^{2}=d
can generate spin-2
(transverse-traceless 2-tensor)
4
4

4

Then, helicity-0 mode of spin-2 can be expressed by scalar as Eq. (
3.48
).
This property is used in partially massless gravity discussed in cosmology
[
102
]
.
The corresponding mass of spin-2 in
4
-dimensional de Sitter is “2”, which coincides with Higuchi mass.

[
103
]
.
The corresponding mass in spin-2 field can be understood from the following calculation;

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\left(\tilde{\Box}-d\right)\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\Box}+2+(d-2)\right)\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\varphi

(3.49)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{\Box}+2+(d-2)\right)Y^{(\varphi)}_{\mu\nu},

that is, the corresponding mass for spin-2 is given by

\displaystyle m_{2}^{2}=\lambda_{\rho}=-(d-2).

(3.50)

Therefore, for the brane bending mode, we have
l=\mu-2
.
Since the
d
-dimensional eigenfunction
Y_{\mu\nu}
of the brane bending mode is fixed by

,
the mode decomposition corresponding to the

\displaystyle h^{\varphi}_{\mu\nu}=\mathfrak{a}^{2}{\mathcal{R}}^{(\varphi)}Y_{\mu\nu}^{(\varphi)}=\mathfrak{a}^{2}{\mathcal{R}}^{(\varphi)}\left(\tilde{\nabla}_{\mu}\tilde{\nabla}_{\nu}-\frac{1}{d}\tilde{\Box}\gamma_{\mu\nu}\right)\varphi.

(3.51)

Then, the junction condition (
3.45
) gives

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{R}}^{(\varphi)}}^{\prime}\,\hat{=}\,2.

(3.52)

We will see that there always exists a mode satisfying this junction condition.

Since the perturbative analysis here is linear, any solution is written with superposition of homogeneous solutions and non-homogeneous solutions.
The source generating non-homogeneous solutions is only the brane bending.
Let us next derive the homogeneous solutions.
With the brane position fixed (
\varphi=0
), Eq. (
3.43
) becomes

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}^{\prime}-2\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{a}}h_{\mu\nu}\,\hat{=}\,0.

(3.53)

The above equation (
3.53

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\,\hat{=}\,0,

(3.54)

that is, the Neumann boundary condition is imposed for
{\mathcal{R}}
on the brane.
With the form of Eq. (
3.29
) and an identity for the associated Legendre function, Eq. (
3.54
) is written as

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\hat{=}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\zeta}\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{4}}P^{-\mu}_{l}(\zeta)

(3.55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{2}\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{4}-1}\zeta P^{-\mu}_{l}(\zeta)+\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{\frac{d}{4}}\frac{d}{d\zeta}P^{-\mu}_{l}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{4}}P^{-\mu+1}_{l}(\zeta).

We suppose that the brane exists at
\zeta=\zeta_{*}
and then, the Neumann boundary condition (

)

\displaystyle 0=P^{-\mu+1}_{l}(\zeta_{*})=\frac{\sqrt{2}\left(\sin\theta\right)^{-(\mu-1)}}{\sqrt{\pi}{\Gamma\left({\mu-\frac{1}{2}}\right)}}\int_{0}^{\theta}\frac{\cos\left(\left(l+\frac{1}{2}\right)\psi\right)}{\left(\cos\psi-\cos\theta\right)^{\frac{3}{2}-\mu}}d\psi,

We will analyse the case with

Let us go back to the junction condition for the brane bending mode (

\displaystyle 2\,\hat{=}\,{{\mathcal{R}}^{(\varphi)}}^{\prime}=-B_{1}\left(1-\zeta^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{4}}P^{-\mu+1}_{\mu-2}(\zeta).

Therefore, if

Another important aspect of the AdS/BCFT correspondence is that it has the third description where the
d
dimensional CFT on a half plane
w>0
is coupled to
d
dimensional gravity on AdS
d
which extends in
w<0
. This description is qualitatively expected from the brane-world holography
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
8
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
and has been beautifully applied to the black hole information problem in the light of the Island formula
[
10
,
11
,
9
]
.
This triality includes a purely
d
dimensional statement that a
d
dimensional BCFT is equivalent to a
d
dimensional CFT on a half plane, coupled to
d
dimensional gravity on AdS
d
, which is called Island/BCFT correspondence
[
40
]
. Even though this duality can be formally obtained by combining the AdS/BCFT and brane-world holography, it is fair to say that the precise and quantitative justification of the Island/BCFT correspondence is still poorly understood. In the recent paper
[
40
]
, we examined this problem and presented quantitative evidences in the lowest dimension
d=2
. Below we would like to test the Island/BCFT correspondence via computations of entanglement entropy in higher dimensions
d>2
. Since we do not have powerful controls of entanglement entropy, as opposed to the
d=2
case studied in
[
40
]
, we will focus on the area law term contributions.

5.1
Entanglement entropy from island formula

Consider a
d
dimensional BCFT on a half plane
w>0
in the flat Lorentzian spacetime

\displaystyle ds_{d}^{2}\,=\,-dt^{2}+dw^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dx_{i}^{2}\,,

(5.1)

and take the subregion
B
as a half ball

\displaystyle w^{2}\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}x_{i}^{2}\,\leq\,l^{2}\,,\qquad w>0\,.

(5.2)

We call its complement the subsystem
A
for which we consider the entanglement entropy, denoted by
S_{A}
.
We couple this BCFT
d
to the AdS
d
gravity at
w=0
, so that we have AdS
d
in
w<0
and BCFT
d
in
w>0
.
Then the corresponding metric of AdS
d
is given by

\displaystyle ds_{d}^{2}\,=\,\frac{\delta^{2}}{(\delta-w)^{2}}\left(-dt^{2}+dw^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dx_{i}^{2}\right)\,,\qquad(w<0),

(5.3)

where in this AdS
d
spacetime,
\delta
can be understood as an UV cutoff.
By the following coordinate transformation

\displaystyle\delta-w\,

\displaystyle=\,r\cos\theta\,,

(5.4)

\displaystyle x_{i}\,

\displaystyle=\,r\sin\theta\cos\phi_{i}\prod_{n=1}^{i-1}\sin\phi_{n}\,,\qquad(i=1,\cdots,d-3)

(5.5)

\displaystyle x_{d-2}\,

\displaystyle=\,r\sin\theta\prod_{n=1}^{d-3}\sin\phi_{n}\,,

(5.6)

we can also bring the metric into the spherical coordinates

\displaystyle ds_{d}^{2}\,=\,\frac{\delta^{2}}{r^{2}\cos\theta^{2}}\left(-dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}\right)\,.

(5.7)

In such a system of a CFT coupled to gravity, the entanglement entropy
S_{A}
can be computed by applying the Island prescription
[
10
,
11
,
9
]
. Namely, the entanglement entropy is obtained by the formula (refer to Fig.
6
)

\displaystyle S_{A}=\mbox{Ext}_{Is}\left[S^{(G)}_{\partial Is}+S^{(Q)}_{A\cup Is}\right].

(5.8)

Here
Is
denotes the Island which is a region in the AdS
d
which is originally taken to be arbitrary under the condition that it connects with the region
A
at the boundary
w=0
as in Fig.
6
. The entropy
S^{(G)}
denotes the classical gravity contribution, which is the area of the boundary of Island
\partial Is
divided by
4G_{N}
in Einstein gravity. In our case, we expect that the brane-world gravity is purely induced from quantum corrections of matter fields and thus we simply set
S^{(G)}=0
.
In other words, we can set
\frac{1}{G^{(d)}_{N}}=0
before we integrate over the matter fields, where
G^{(d)}_{N}
is the
d
dimensional Newton constant.
On the other hand,
S^{(Q)}
describes all contributions from the quantum fields to entanglement entropy, where the subsystem is taken to be an union of
A
and the Island. Finally we extremize the total entropy by allowing to change the shape of the Island. This is the Island prescription in our setup. In our higher dimensional case
d>2
, we expect that
S^{(Q)}
follows the area law
[
104
,
105
]
and thus the leading divergent contribution looks like

\displaystyle S^{(Q)}_{A\cup Is}\simeq\mbox{Ext}_{Is}\left[\gamma\cdot\frac{\mbox{Area}(\partial Is)}{\epsilon^{d-2}}\right]+S_{0}+O(\epsilon^{-(d-4)}),

(5.9)

where
S_{0}
represents the area law contribution from the subsystem
A
, being proportional to
\frac{l^{d-2}}{\epsilon^{d-2}}
. The extremization procedure is equivalent to finding an extremal surface as
\partial Is
. For static backgrounds, which we focus in this section, this becomes a minimal surface on a constant time slice.

Island setup in BCFT

We parameterize a co-dimension two extremal surface on a constant time slice in the AdS
d
, we parameterize the surface by

\displaystyle r\,=\,r(\theta,\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{d-3})\,,

(5.10)

with a boundary condition
r=l
at
w=0
.
Then, the induced metric on the surface is given by

\displaystyle ds_{d-2}^{2}=\frac{\delta^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta}\left[\left(\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\theta}d\theta+\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{i}}d\phi_{i}\right)^{2}\,+\,d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}\right]\,.

(5.11)

For the above induced metric, we denote the contribution from
d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}
by
(h_{d-2}^{(0)})_{\mu\nu}
and the contribution from
dr^{2}
by
(\tilde{h}_{d-2})_{\mu\nu}
as

\displaystyle\big(h_{d-2}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\frac{\delta^{2}}{\cos^{2}\theta}\left[\big(h_{d-2}^{(0)}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,+\,\big(\tilde{h}_{d-2}\big)_{\mu\nu}\right]\,,

(5.12)

where

\displaystyle\big(h_{d-2}^{(0)}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,

\displaystyle=\,\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\sin^{2}\theta&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&\sin^{2}\theta\prod_{i=1}^{d-4}(\sin\phi_{i})^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\big(\tilde{h}_{d-2}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,

\displaystyle=\,v_{\mu}\,(v^{T})_{\nu}\,,

(5.14)

\displaystyle v^{T}_{\mu}\,

\displaystyle=\,\left(\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\theta}\,,\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{1}}\,,\,\cdots\,,\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{d-3}}\right)\,.

(5.15)

The areal law term of the entanglement entropy for
A\cup Is
reads

\displaystyle S^{(Q)}_{A\cup Is}\,\approx\,\frac{\gamma}{\epsilon^{d-2}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta^{\prime}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}d\theta\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}d\phi_{i}\sqrt{\det(h_{d-2})}\,+\,S_{0}\,,

(5.16)

where the regularization parameter
\delta^{\prime}
is defined by
l\cos\delta^{\prime}=\delta
.

Using the decomposition of the determinant, the determinant of the induced metric is given by

\displaystyle\det(h_{d-2})\,

\displaystyle=\,\left(\frac{\delta}{\cos\theta}\right)^{2(d-2)}\,\det\Big(h_{d-2}^{(0)}+vv^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle=\,\left(\frac{\delta}{\cos\theta}\right)^{2(d-2)}\,\det\big(h_{d-2}^{(0)}\big)\det\big(I_{d-2}+v^{T}(h_{d-2}^{(0)})^{-1}v\big)\,,

(5.17)

where
I_{d-2}
is the
d-2
dimensional identity matrix and we used Sylvester’s determinant theorem (or the matrix determinant lemma) for the second line.
Since the first determinant
\det\big(h_{d-2}^{(0)}\big)
does not depend on the surface profile
r=r(\theta,\phi_{i})
, we need to consider to minimize the second determinant
\det\big(I_{d-2}+v^{T}(h_{d-2}^{(0)})^{-1}v\big)
.
This is archived by the vanishing vector
v=0
, so that
\det\big(I_{d-2}+v^{T}(h_{d-2}^{(0)})^{-1}v\big)=1
.
Therefore, the minimal surface is semi-circle with the constant radius
r=l
.
Hence in this case, the entanglement entropy is given by

\displaystyle S_{A}=S^{(Q)}_{A\cup Is}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\gamma\delta^{d-2}}{\epsilon^{d-2}}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\delta^{\prime}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\delta^{\prime}}d\theta\,\frac{(\sin\theta)^{d-3}}{(\cos\theta)^{d-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}d\phi_{i}\big(\sin\phi_{i}\big)^{d-3-i}\,+\,S_{0}

\displaystyle\approx\,\frac{\gamma\delta S_{d-3}l^{d-3}}{(d-3)\epsilon^{d-2}}\,+\,S_{0}\,,

(5.18)

where
S_{d-3}=2\pi^{(d-2)/2}/\Gamma((d-2)/2)
.
The above result of the
\theta
integral assumes
d>3
and the case of
d=3
must be treated separately.

For
d=3
, we actually obtain

\displaystyle S_{A}\simeq 2\gamma\cdot\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}\log\frac{2l}{\delta}+S_{0},

(5.19)

where we have
S_{0}=\gamma\frac{\pi l}{\epsilon}
.

We also would like to note an important fact. We initially assumed the subsystem
A
to be the outside of a round half ball
B
specified by (
5.2
). However, the above calculation based on the Island formula only uses the location of the boundary
\partial A
at the interface between the CFT and the gravity i.e. we have
x=\pm l
at

. Thus we obtain the same result (
5.19
) for any shape of the subsystem

as long as it ends on
|x|=\pm l
at
w=0
.

Next we would like to compare the previous result based on the Island formula with the result obtained from a holographic BCFT, using the AdS/BCFT.

\displaystyle S_{A}=\mbox{Ext}_{\Gamma_{A}}\left[\frac{\mbox{Area}(\Gamma_{A})}{4G_{N}}\right].

with an extra rule

that
the surface

will be identified with the boundary of the Island i.e.

becomes a minimal surface on a constant time slice.

We choose the coordinates of the AdS

to be

\displaystyle ds_{d+1}^{2}\,=\,d\rho^{2}+\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{y^{2}}\left(-dt^{2}+dy^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dx_{i}^{2}\right)\,.

(5.21)

When

(5.22)

In the previous sections, we placed the EOW brane at

\rho_{*}<0

\displaystyle S_{A}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\int_{-\rho_{*}}^{\rho_{\infty}}d\rho\,(\cosh\rho)^{d-2}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{\epsilon}{l}\cosh\rho}^{\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\epsilon}{l}\cosh\rho}d\theta\,\frac{(\sin\theta)^{d-3}}{(\cos\theta)^{d-2}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=1}^{d-3}d\phi_{i}\big(\sin\phi_{i}\big)^{d-3-i}

\displaystyle\approx\,S^{half}_{A}\,+\,\frac{S_{d-3}l^{d-3}L^{d-1}}{4(d-3)G_{N}\epsilon^{d-3}}\,\sinh\rho_{*}\,.

\displaystyle S_{A}

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}}{2G^{(4)}_{N}}\sinh\rho_{*}\log\frac{2l}{\epsilon}+S_{0}.

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma\delta}{\epsilon}\,=\,\frac{L^{d-1}}{4G_{N}^{(d+1)}}\,\sinh\rho_{*}\,.

(5.25)

Note that since we expect

\displaystyle\frac{w}{z}\left(=\frac{\delta}{\epsilon}\right)=\sinh\rho_{*},

(5.26)

Notice that this analysis of entanglement entropy in a holographic BCFT is correct for any value of tension

However, when the tension takes a generic value, we need to take into account the subleading terms other than the area law term in the Island calculation of the dual system, via the Island/BCFT correspondence. When we deform the shape of the subsystem

7
Conclusions and Discussions

In this paper, we have studied several dynamical aspects of holographic boundary conformal field theories (BCFTs), by employing a gravity dual construction known as the AdS/BCFT correspondence. A key feature of AdS/BCFT is the presence of end of the world-brane (EOW brane), which stretches from the boundary of the BCFT towards the AdS bulk. When we excite a holographic BCFT, the EOW brane starts to vibrate in addition to bulk metric excitations in the gravity dual. The EOW brane is characterized by the value of its tension
\sigma
. In this way, we can analyze the dynamical property of holographic BCFT via the gravity dual in a way similar to the one employed in earlier studies of brane-world models.

First we noted that the gravity dual of a two dimensional BCFT, i.e. AdS
{}_{3}/
BCFT
2
is special in that there are no propagating modes in the bulk of the pure AdS
3
gravity. Owing to this property, we can find analytical solutions to the gravity duals of an arbitrary excited states in a holographic BCFT, whose stress energy tensor satisfies the boundary condition of complete reflection. However, the main aim of this paper is to investigate dynamical properties of AdS/BCFT in higher dimensions.

In higher dimensional AdS
{}_{d+1}/
BCFT
d
(
d\geq 3
), the bulk graviton mode is propagating and its dynamics becomes much richer. In this paper we performed a complete analysis of metric perturbations in the presence of EOW brane. We found that they are organized into the helicity 0, 1 and 2 mode. In particular, the lightest mode of helicity 0 can be identified with the brane bending mode. We can calculate the holographic stress energy tensor from these solutions to the metric perturbations. The
brane bending mode provides a diagonal component of stress energy tensor which shows an universal behavior such that it approaches a constant value at the boundary of BCFT, being independent from the value of brane tension. On the other hand, all other modes of helicity 0, 1 and 2 are sensitive to the value of tension and the corresponding stress energy tensors are proportional to characteristic powers of the distance
w
between the point and the boundary. These powers depend on the tension and interestingly, they become integer valued when the tension vanishes. We confirmed that the boundary condition of stress energy tensor
T_{iw}|_{w=0}=0
, which is expected for any BCFTs, is indeed satisfied for any values of tension. It will be an important future problem to understand the above behaviors of stress energy tensor, predicted by the gravity dual analysis, from field theoretic calculations in BCFTs.

We also examined solutions of the scalar field perturbations in the AdS/BCFT setup. This is dual to the scalar operator excitation in a holographic BCFT.
This analysis again confirms that the bulk excitation propagates to the EOW brane and is completely reflected back, as expected from the dual BCFT. We construct an explicit numerical solution which shows this behavior. It would be an interesting future problem to study both scalar field and metric perturbations at the same time and construct a gravity dual of realistic black hole evaporation in higher dimensions, where we expect that the dynamics of brane world plays a crucial role.

Then we analyzed another important aspect of AdS/BCFT, namely this has the third description in terms of a CFT coupled to gravity along the boundary. This is expected from the brane-world holography, so called double holography. If we focus on the duality in lower dimensional (i.e.
d
dimensional) descriptions, this leads to the equivalence that a
d
dimensional BCFT is equivalent to a
d
dimensional CFT on a half plane, coupled to
d
dimensional gravity on AdS
d
, which is called Island/BCFT correspondence. In this paper, we provided an evidence which supports this duality by calculating the entanglement entropy for a semi-disk subsystem. The upshot is that the entanglement entropy in a
d
dimensional holographic BCFT agrees with that in the theory defined by a
d
dimensional CFT coupled to a
d
dimensional gravity, where the latter gravitational theory is assumed to be an induced gravity. It will be intriguing to generalize this analysis to more general subsystems.

Finally, we present a gravity dual computation of one point functions in holographic BCFTs, where the scalar field solution was given numerically in the presence of a non-trivial boundary condition on EOW brane. This reproduces the expected form of one-point functions in BCFTs. An interesting future problem is to consider a string theory embedding of the AdS/BCFT and calculate the one point functions both from string theory and the dual BCFT.

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\ P^{-3/2}_{1/2}(\tanh\rho)\propto\cosh^{2}\rho\left[2\arctan\left(\tanh\frac{\rho}{2}\right)+\frac{\sinh\rho}{\cosh^{2}\rho}-\frac{\pi}{2}\right],\ \to(Q_{1},Q_{2}),(R_{1},R_{2})

\displaystyle(iii)\ \lambda_{\rho}=3\ (a=2):

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\ P^{3/2}_{3/2}(\tanh\rho)\propto\tanh\rho(\sinh^{2}\rho+3),\ \to A_{1},V_{1}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\cosh\rho}\ P^{-3/2}_{3/2}(\tanh\rho)\propto\frac{1}{\cosh\rho}.\ \to A_{2},V_{2}.

(A.17)

In the above, physical modes are
(U_{1},U_{2})
,
A_{2}
and
V_{2}
.
Other modes are either non-normalizable or violating the boundary condition. The mode (ii)
\lambda_{\rho}=0

is not allowed due to the condition (
3.60
).
Note also that
B
mode does not satisfy the traceless gauge condition
h^{\mu}_{\mu}=0
. These perfectly match with the physical modes in the TT gauge, discussed in subsection
3.6
.

Appendix B
Minimal surface in AdS
d+1

In this appendix, we study the condimension-2 minimal surface in AdS
d+1
with the metric (
5.21
)

\displaystyle ds_{d+1}^{2}\,=\,d\rho^{2}+\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{y^{2}}\left(-dt^{2}+dy^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}dx_{i}^{2}\right)\,,

(B.1)

with a condition that the boundary of the surface in the
\rho\to\infty
limit is given by

\displaystyle y^{2}\,+\,\sum_{i=1}^{d-2}x_{i}^{2}\,=\,l^{2}\,,\qquad y\,\geq\,0\,.

(B.2)

The determination of the minimal surface in this case is very much similar to the one we discussed in section
5.1
for AdS
d
space.

First, we again move to the spherical coordinates by

\displaystyle y\,

\displaystyle=\,r\cos\theta\,,

(B.3)

\displaystyle x_{i}\,

\displaystyle=\,r\sin\theta\cos\phi_{i}\prod_{n=1}^{i-1}\sin\phi_{n}\,,\qquad(i=1,\cdots,d-3)

(B.4)

\displaystyle x_{d-2}\,

\displaystyle=\,r\sin\theta\prod_{n=1}^{d-3}\sin\phi_{n}\,,

(B.5)

which brings the metric into

\displaystyle ds_{d+1}^{2}\,=\,d\rho^{2}+\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\big(-dt^{2}+dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}\big)\,.

(B.6)

We specify the surface by

\displaystyle r\,=\,r(\rho,\theta,\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{d-3})\,,

(B.7)

so that the induced metric is found as

\displaystyle ds_{d-1}^{2}\,

\displaystyle=\,d\rho^{2}+\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{\cos^{2}\theta}\,d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\,+\,\frac{\cosh^{2}\rho}{\cos^{2}\theta}\left(\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\rho}d\rho+\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\theta}d\theta+\sum_{i=1}^{d-3}\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{i}}d\phi_{i}\right)^{2}\,.

(B.8)

As before, we decompose this induced metric into two contributions as one from the first line and the other from the second line of (
B.8
)

\displaystyle\big(h_{d-1}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,=\,\big(h_{d-1}^{(0)}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,+\,\big(\tilde{h}_{d-1}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,.

(B.9)

Again the
\big(\tilde{h}_{d-1}\big)_{\mu\nu}
metric is given by a tensor product of a vector

\displaystyle\big(\tilde{h}_{d-1}\big)_{\mu\nu}\,=\,v_{\mu}\,(v^{T})_{\nu}\,,

(B.10)

\displaystyle v^{T}_{\mu}\,=\,\frac{\cosh\rho}{\cos\theta}\left(\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\rho}\,,\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\theta}\,,\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{1}}\,,\,\cdots\,,\,\frac{\partial\log r}{\partial\phi_{d-3}}\right)\,,

(B.11)

so that the determinant is decomposed as

\displaystyle\det(h_{d-1})\,

\displaystyle=\,\det\Big(h_{d-1}^{(0)}+vv^{T}\Big)

\displaystyle=\,\det\big(h_{d-1}^{(0)}\big)\det\big(I_{d-1}+v^{T}(h_{d-1}^{(0)})^{-1}v\big)\,,

(B.12)

Minimization of this determinant requires
v=0
, therefore, the minimal surface is given by
y=l
.

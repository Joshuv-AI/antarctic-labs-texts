Introduction

Let
\lambda
be a positive constant and
\Delta_{\lambda}
be the Bessel operator which is defined by setting, for all suitable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
,

\Delta_{\lambda}f(x):=-{d^{2}\over dx^{2}}f(x)-{2\lambda\over x}{d\over dx}f(x).

An early work concerning the Bessel operator goes back to Muckenhoupt and Stein
[
20
]
. They developed a theory associated to which is parallel to the classical one associated to the Laplace operator. Since then, a lot of work concerning the Bessel operators was carried out; see, for example
[
1
,
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
19
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
. In particular, Betancor et al. in
[
4
]

established the characterizations of the atomic Hardy space
H^{1}((0,\infty),dm_{\lambda})
associated to
\Delta_{\lambda}
in terms of the Riesz transform and the radial maximal function related to a class of functions including the Poisson semigroup
\{{\mathcal{P}}_{t}\}_{t>0}
and the heat semigroup
\{{\mathcal{W}}_{t}\}_{t>0}
as special cases, where
dm_{\lambda}:=x^{2\lambda}dx
and
dx
is the Lebesgue measure. The Poisson semigroup operators are defined by

{\mathcal{P}}_{t}f(x):=e^{-t\sqrt{\Delta_{\lambda}}}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,y)f(y)dm_{\lambda}(y),

where
J_{v}
is the Bessel function of the first kind of order
v
with
v\in(-{1\over 2},\infty)
and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tz}(xz)^{-\lambda+1/2}J_{\lambda-1/2}(xz)(yz)^{-\lambda+1/2}J_{\lambda-1/2}(yz)dm_{\lambda}(z)

\displaystyle={2\lambda t\over\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}{(\sin\theta)^{2\lambda-1}\over(x^{2}+y^{2}+t^{2}-2xy\cos\theta)^{\lambda+1}}d\theta,\quad t,x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

For good enough functions
f
, the families

\{{\mathcal{P}}_{t}f\}_{t>0},\{{\mathcal{W}}_{t}f\}_{t>0}
converge to
f
when
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
. The type of convergence (pointwise, norm, measure,
\dots
) depends on the class of functions
f
in consideration.
One way to enter more deeply into this kind of convergence is to analyze the behavior of the following type sums

(1.1)

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{j}({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f(x)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f(x))\quad\hbox{and}\quad\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}v_{j}({\mathcal{W}}_{a_{j+1}}f(x)-{\mathcal{W}}_{a_{j}}f(x)),

where
\{v_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a bounded sequence of real numbers and
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is an increasing sequence of positive numbers. Observe that in the case
v_{j}\equiv 1
, the above series is telescopy, and their behavior coincide with the one of
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}f(x)
and
{\mathcal{W}}_{t}f(x)
. This way of analyzing convergence of sequences was considered by Jones and Rosemblatt for ergodic averages(see
[
17
]
), and latter by Bernardis et al. for differential transforms(see
[
8
]
).

To better understand the behavior of the sums in (
1.1
), we shall analyze its “partial sums” defined as follows(we only consider the case for
{\mathcal{P}}_{t}
in this paper).
For each
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2},~N=(N_{1},N_{2})
with
N_{1}<N_{2},
we define

(1.2)

T_{N}f(x)=\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f(x)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f(x)),\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We shall also consider the maximal operators

(1.3)

T^{*}f(x)=\sup_{N}\left|T_{N}f(x)\right|,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

where the supremum are taken over all
N=(N_{1},N_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
with
N_{1}<N_{2}
.
In
[
9
,
10
]
, the authors proved the boundedness of the above operators related with the one-sided fractional Poisson type operator sequence and the classical laplacian separately.

Some of our results will be valid only when the sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
is lacunary. It means that there exists a
\rho>1
such that
\displaystyle\frac{a_{j+1}}{a_{j}}\geq\rho,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular, we shall prove the boundedness of the operators
T^{*}
in the weighted spaces

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\omega dm_{\lambda})
and
BMO(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})
space, where

is the usual Muckenhoupt weight on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. We refer the reader to the book by J. Duoandikoetxea
[

classes. We say that, a function

\left\|f\right\|_{BMO({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}:=\sup_{x,r\in(0,\infty)}\Big\{\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I(x,r))}\int_{I(x,r)}\Big|f(y)-f_{I(x,r)}\Big|\ dm_{\lambda}(y)\Big\}<\infty,

where

f_{I(x,r)}:={1\over m_{\lambda}(I(x,r))}\int_{I(x,r)}f(y)\ dm_{\lambda}(y).

And we have the following results.

.

Assume that the sequence

Uniform boundedness of the operatrs
T_{N}

In this section, we give the proofs of Theorem
1.1
. Vector-valued Calderón-Zygmund theory will be a fundamental tool in proving the
L^{p}
boundedness of the operators
T_{N}
. For this theory, the reader can see the paper
[
21
]
. Nowadays it is well known that the fundamental ingredients in the theory are the
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
boundedness for some
1<p_{0}<\infty
and the smoothness of the kernel of the operator. Even more, the constants that appear in the results only depend on the boundedness constant in
L^{p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the constants related with the size and smoothness of the kernel.

Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty).
For
y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},
consider the functions

\varphi_{y}(x)=(xy)^{-\lambda+1/2}J_{\lambda-1/2}(xy),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

where
J_{v}
denotes the Bessel function of the first kind and order
v
, see
[
22
]
. It is well known that for each
y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, the function
\varphi_{y}
is an eigenfunction of the Bessel operator
\Delta_{\lambda},
and the corresponding eigenvalue is
|y|^{2}
,

\Delta_{\lambda}\varphi_{y}=|y|^{2}\varphi_{y},\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

The set
\{\varphi_{y}(x)\}_{y\in(0,\infty)}
of eigenfunctions of
\Delta_{\lambda}
, does not span a dense subset of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda}).
Thus, we cannot use the usual spectral techniques to define the classical operators associated with
\Delta_{\lambda}.
The Hankel transform
H_{\lambda}
defined by

H_{\lambda}f(y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\varphi_{y}(x)f(x)dm_{\lambda}(x),\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},

plays in the Bessel context a similar role as the Fourier transform in the Euclidean setting. It is well known that
H_{\lambda}
is an isometry in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})
and it coincides there with its inverse,
H_{\lambda}^{-1}=H_{\lambda}.
Moreover, for sufficiently regular functions
f
, say
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),
we have

H_{\lambda}(\Delta_{\lambda}f)(y)=|y|^{2}H_{\lambda}(f)(y),\quad y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

We consider the nonnegative self-adjoint extension of
\Delta_{\lambda}
(still denoted by the same symbol) defined by

\Delta_{\lambda}f=H_{\lambda}(|y|^{2}H_{\lambda}f),\quad f\in\text{Dom}(\Delta_{\lambda}),

on the domain

\text{Dom}(\Delta_{\lambda})=\{f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda}):|y|^{2}H_{\lambda}f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})\}.

Then the spectral decomposition of
\Delta_{\lambda}
is given via the Hankel transform.

In the following theorem, we present and prove the
L^{2}
boundedness of the operators
T_{N}
by Hankel transform.

Proposition 2.1
.

There is a constant
C
, depending on
\lambda,\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}
(not on
N
), such that

\|T_{N}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\text{Dom}(\Delta_{\lambda})
. Using the Plancherel theorem for Hankel transform, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T_{N}f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f\right)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f(x)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f(x)\right)\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(H_{\lambda}{\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f(\cdot)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f(\cdot)\right)\Big\}}(y)\right)^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}H_{\lambda}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{t}f\right)(y)dt\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\Big|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}H_{\lambda}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{t}f\right)(y)dt\Big|\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle=C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\Big|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|y\right|e^{-t\left|y\right|}H_{\lambda}(f)(y)dt\Big|\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\left|y\right|e^{-t\left|y\right|}dt|H_{\lambda}(f)(y)|\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\Big\{\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|y\right|e^{-t\left|y\right|}dt\Big||H_{\lambda}(f)(y)|\Big\}^{2}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,\lambda}\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}^{2}.

Since
\text{Dom}(\Delta_{\lambda})
is dense in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda}),

T_{N}
extends uniquely to a bounded linear operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda}).

Then the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(I_{m-1})}\int_{I_{m-1}}\int_{I_{m}^{c}}\left|K_{(-M,m-1)}(z,y)f(y)\right|dm_{\lambda}(y)dm_{\lambda}(z).

\displaystyle\int_{I_{m}^{c}}\left|K_{(-M,m-1)}(z,y)f(y)\right|dm_{\lambda}(y)

A_{4}\leq C{\mathcal{M}}f(x_{0}).

Combining the estimates above for
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
and
A_{4}
, we get

II\leq{\mathcal{M}}(T_{(-M,M)}f)(x_{0})+C{\mathcal{M}}_{q}f(x_{0}).

As the constants

\rho
and
\lambda

Theorem 3.4
.

(a)

Proof.

First, we shall see that if
\varphi
is a test function, then
T_{N}\varphi(x)
converges for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. In order to prove this, it is enough to see that for any

with

converge to zero, when
L,M\rightarrow+\infty
.
By the mean value theorem and the
\rho
-lacunarity of the sequence
\{a_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we have

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{j=L}^{M}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right||\varphi(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,\rho,\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{j=L}^{M}\frac{C}{a^{2\lambda+1}_{j}}|\varphi(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)\leq C_{v,\rho,\lambda}\left({1\over a_{L}^{2\lambda+1}}\sum_{j=L}^{M}\frac{a_{L}^{2\lambda+1}}{a_{j}^{2\lambda+1}}\right)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}|\varphi(y)|dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,\rho,\lambda}{\rho^{{2\lambda+1}}\over{\rho^{{2\lambda+1}}-1}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}{1\over a_{L}^{{2\lambda+1}}}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

On the other hand, as the integral of the kernels are zero, we can write

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right)(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int^{+\infty}_{\sqrt{a}_{j}}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right)(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))dm_{\lambda}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle=:B_{1}+B_{2}.

And, then proceeding as in the case
A
, and by using the fact that
\varphi

\displaystyle|B_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho}\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{a}_{j}}{a_{j}\over(|x-y|^{2}+a_{j}^{2})^{\lambda+1}}\left|\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\right|dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho}\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{a}_{j}}{a_{j}|x-y|\over(|x-y|^{2}+a_{j}^{2})^{\lambda+1}}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho,\varphi}{\sqrt{\rho}\over\sqrt{\rho}-1}a_{-L}^{1/2}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle|B_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho}\left\|v\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{\sqrt{a}_{j}}^{+\infty}{a_{j}\over(|x-y|^{2}+a_{j}^{2})^{\lambda+1}}\left|\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\right|dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}\int_{\sqrt{a}_{j}}^{+\infty}{a_{j}\over(|x-y|^{2}+a_{j}^{2})^{\lambda+1}}dm_{\lambda}(y)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho,\varphi}a_{-L}^{1/2}\sum_{j=-M}^{-L}{a_{j}^{1/2}\over a_{-L}^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda,\rho,\varphi}{\sqrt{\rho}\over\sqrt{\rho}-1}a_{-L}^{1/2}\longrightarrow 0,\quad\hbox{as}\ {L,M\to+\infty}.

As the set of test functions is dense in

1.1
we get the

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

We will prove it only in the case
1<p<\infty
. For the cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
, the proof follows by introducing the obvious changes. Since
2r<1,
we know that
\tilde{I}\backslash\tilde{I}_{2r}\neq\emptyset.
Let
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, where
f_{1}=f\chi_{\tilde{I}_{2r}}
and
f_{2}=f(x)\chi_{\tilde{I}\backslash\tilde{I}_{2r}}
. Then

\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|\leq\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|+\left|T^{*}f_{2}(x)\right|.

By Theorem
1.1
, we have

\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|dm_{\lambda}(x)\leq\left(\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{1}(x)\right|^{2}dm_{\lambda}(x)\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq C\left(\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|f_{1}(x)\right|^{2}dm_{\lambda}(x)\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

We should note that, for any
j\in\mathbb{Z},
by Lemma
2.2
,

(4.1)

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|~dm_{\lambda}(y)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|\int_{a_{j}}^{a_{j+1}}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{P}}_{t}(x,y)dt\right|~dm_{\lambda}(y)\\
\leq C_{\lambda}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}{a_{j+1}-a_{j}\over{(|x-y|^{2}+a_{j}^{2})^{\lambda+1}}}dm_{\lambda}(y)=C_{\lambda,\rho}.

And for
1<p<\infty
and any
N=(N_{1},N_{2})
, by Hölder’s inequality, (
4.1
), Fubini’s Theorem and Lemma
2.2
we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}v_{j}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}f_{2}(x)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}f_{2}(x)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left|v_{j}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left({\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right)f_{2}(y)~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{l^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}\left(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|\left|f_{2}(y)\right|~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v}\Big(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\Big\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\times\Big\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|~dm_{\lambda}(y)\Big\}^{p^{\prime}/p}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,p,\lambda,\rho}\left(\sum_{j=N_{1}}^{N_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,p,\lambda,\rho}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{+\infty}\left|{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j+1}}(x,y)-{\mathcal{P}}_{a_{j}}(x,y)\right|\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,p,\lambda,\rho}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\lambda+1}}\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}.

For
y\in\tilde{I}\backslash\tilde{I}_{2r}
and
x\in\tilde{I}_{r}
, we have
r\leq|x-y|\leq 2
. Then, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left|T^{*}f_{2}(x)\right|dm_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,p,\lambda,\rho}\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n}}\left|f_{2}(y)\right|^{p^{\prime}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}dm_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{v,p,\lambda,\rho}\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left(\int_{r\leq|x-y|\leq 2}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2\lambda+1}}~dm_{\lambda}(y)\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}dm_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle\sim\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

Hence,

\frac{1}{m_{\lambda}(\tilde{I}_{r})}\int_{\tilde{I}_{r}}\left|T^{*}f(x)\right|dm_{\lambda}\leq C\left(1+\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\right)\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}\leq C\Big(\log\frac{2}{r}\Big)^{1/p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},dm_{\lambda})}.

∎

Abstract
A set S S of vertices in a graph G G is a dominating set if every vertex of V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ S V(G)\setminus S is adjacent to a vertex in S S . A coalition in G G consists of two disjoint sets of vertices X X and Y Y of G G , neither of which is a dominating set but whose union X ∪ Y X\cup Y is a dominating set of G G . Such sets X X and Y Y form a coalition in G G . A coalition partition, abbreviated c c -partition, in G G is a partition 𝒳 = { X 1 , … , X k } \mathcal{X}=\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\} of the vertex set V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) of G G such that for all i ∈ [ k ] i\in[k] , each set X i ∈ 𝒳 X_{i}\in\mathcal{X} satisfies one of the following two conditions: (1) X i X_{i} is a dominating set of G G with a single vertex, or (2) X i X_{i} forms a coalition with some other set X j ∈ 𝒳 X_{j}\in\mathcal{X} . Let 𝒜 = { A 1 , … , A r } {\cal A}=\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{r}\} and ℬ = { B 1 , … , B s } {\cal B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}\} be two partitions of V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) . Partition ℬ {\cal B} is a refinement of partition 𝒜 {\cal A} if every set B i ∈ ℬ B_{i}\in{\cal B} is either equal to, or a proper subset of, some set A j ∈ 𝒜 A_{j}\in{\cal A} . Further if 𝒜 ≠ ℬ {\cal A}\neq{\cal B} , then ℬ {\cal B} is a proper refinement of 𝒜 {\cal A} . Partition 𝒜 {\cal A} is a minimal c c -partition if it is not a proper refinement of another c c -partition. Haynes et al. [AKCE Int. J. Graphs Combin. 17 (2020), no. 2, 653–659] defined the minmin coalition number c min ​ ( G ) c_{\min}(G) of G G to equal the minimum order of a minimal c c -partition of G G . We show that 2 ≤ c min ​ ( G ) ≤ n 2\leq c_{\min}(G)\leq n , and we characterize graphs G G of order n n satisfying c min ​ ( G ) = n c_{\min}(G)=n . A polynomial-time algorithm is given to determine if c min ​ ( G ) = 2 c_{\min}(G)=2 for a given graph G G . A necessary and sufficient condition for a graph G G to satisfy c min ​ ( G ) ≥ 3 c_{\min}(G)\geq 3 is given, and a characterization of graphs G G with minimum degree 2 2 and c min ​ ( G ) = 4 c_{\min}(G)=4 is provided.

Bounds on the minmin coalition number

In this section, we present lower and upper bounds on the minmin coalition number. We prove firstly that the minmin coalition number is bounded above by the cardinality of an arbitrary
c
-partition of
G
.

Proposition 1

If
G
is a graph and
{\cal X}
is a
c
-partition of
G
, then
c_{\min}(G)\leq|{\cal X}|
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal X}
be an arbitrary
c
-partition of
G
. If
{\cal X}
is a minimal
c
-partition of
G
, then
c_{\min}(G)\leq|{\cal X}|
. Now, assume that
{\cal X}
is not a minimal
c
-partition. By repeated applications of Observation
1
, there exists a minimal
c
-partition
{\cal P}
of
G
with
{\cal P}<{\cal X}
. Hence,
c_{\min}(G)\leq|{\cal P}|<|{\cal X}|
.
\Box

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
1
, we have the following upper bound on the minmin coalition number.

Corollary 1

If
G
is a graph, then
c_{\min}(G)\leq C(G)
.

We determine next a relationship between the minmin coalition number of a graph and the graph obtained from it by removing the universal vertices.

Proposition 2

If
G
is a graph that is not a complete graph and contains
k
universal vertices, then
c_{\min}(G)=c_{\min}(G^{\prime})+k
, where
G^{\prime}
is obtained from

by removing the universal vertices.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph that is not a complete graph and contains
k
universal vertices, and let
{\cal X}
be a
c_{\min}
-partition of
G
. Thus,
{\cal X}
is a minimal
c
-partition of
G
and
|{\cal X}|=c_{\min}(G)
. If
u
is a universal vertex, then every
c
-partition of
G
contains the set
\{u\}
. In particular,
\{u\}\in{\cal X}
. Let
{\cal X}^{\prime}
be obtained from
{\cal X}
by removing all sets
\{u\}
, where
u
is a universal vertex of
G
. The resulting partition
{\cal X}^{\prime}
is a
c
-partition of
G^{\prime}
. Thus by Proposition
1
we infer that
c_{\min}(G^{\prime})\leq|{\cal X}^{\prime}|=|{\cal X}|-k=c_{\min}(G)-k
. To prove the reverse inequality, let

be a

-partition of

. Thus,
{\cal Y}^{\prime}
is a minimal

-partition of
G^{\prime}

We next establish lower and upper bounds on the minmin coalition number of a graph.

Theorem 1

If
G
is a graph of order

, then
2\leq c_{\min}(G)\leq n
, and these bounds are sharp.

Proof.

Let

. By Corollary

. Since

, the upper bound

, let

-partition of

contains a set of cardinality

. If every set in

To prove the sharpness of lower bound, consider, for example, when

\Box

G=\overline{K}_{2}+G^{\prime}
where
G^{\prime}=G-\{v,v^{\prime}\}
. Let
G^{\prime}
have order
n^{\prime}
, and so
n=n^{\prime}-2
. If
G^{\prime}
is a complete graph
K_{n-2}
, then
G^{\prime}\in{\cal M}
, implying that
G^{\prime}\in{\cal M}
.

Hence we may assume that
G^{\prime}
contains at least two vertices that are not universal vertices in
G^{\prime}
. Let
x
and
y
be two distinct vertices that are not universal vertices of
G^{\prime}
. Since
x
and
y
are not universal vertices of
G
, by our earlier observations that
\{x,y\}
is a dominating set of
G
. This in turn implies that
\{x,y\}
is a dominating set of
G^{\prime}
. This is true for every two distinct vertices that are not universal vertices of
G^{\prime}
. Hence,
c_{\min}(G^{\prime})=n^{\prime}
. If
n^{\prime}=2
, then either
G^{\prime}=K_{2}
, in which case
G=K_{4}-e
, or
G^{\prime}=\overline{K}_{2}
, in which case
G=C_{4}
. In both cases,
G\in{\cal M}
. Hence, we may assume that
n^{\prime}\geq 3
, for otherwise the desired result follows. Thus by our earlier properties of the graph
G^{\prime}
, we infer that
G^{\prime}
is a connected graph. As observed earlier,
c_{\min}(G^{\prime})=n^{\prime}
. Applying the inductive hypothesis to
G^{\prime}
, we infer that
G^{\prime}\in{\cal M}
. This in turn implies that
G=\overline{K}_{2}+G^{\prime}\in{\cal M}
.
\Box

4
Graphs with small minmin coalition number

In this section, we study graphs with small minmin coalition number. We characterize graphs
G
with no universal vertex satisfying
c_{\min}(G)=2
. We present necessary and sufficient condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
, and necessary and sufficient condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
. We first prove a necessary condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
c_{\min}(G)=2
.

Lemma 1

If
G
is an isolate-free graph with
\delta(G)=1
that does not contain a universal vertex, then
c_{\min}(G)=2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an isolate-free graph with
\delta(G)=1
that does not contain a universal vertex. Suppose, to the contrary, that
c_{\min}(G)\neq 2
. Hence by Theorem
1
,
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
. In particular, this implies that
G
has order at least
3
. Let
x
be a vertex of degree
1
in
G
, and let
y
be its only neighbor. We now consider the partition
\Psi=(V_{1},V_{2})
of
V(G)
into sets
V_{1}=\{x,y\}
and
V_{2}=V(G)\setminus V_{1}
. Since the vertex
x
is not dominated by
V_{2}
, the set
V_{2}
is not a dominating set of
G
. By supposition the graph
G
does not contain a universal vertex. In particular, the vertex
y
is not a universal vertex of
G
, implying that the set
V_{1}
is not a dominating set of
G
. Hence, the sets
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
form a coalition in
G
. Thus,
\Psi
is a
c
-partition of
G
, implying by Proposition
1
that
c_{\min}(G)\leq|\Psi|=2
, a contradiction.
\Box

As a consequence of Lemma
1
, we can determine the minmin coalition number of a tree.

Corollary 2

If
T
is a nontrivial tree, then
c_{\min}(T)=2
, unless
T
is a star
T\cong K_{1,r}
where
r\geq 2
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a nontrivial tree. Thus,
T
has order
n\geq 2
. If
n=2
, then
c_{\min}(T)=2
. Hence we may assume that
n\geq 3
, for otherwise the desired result is immediate. If
T
does not contain a universal vertex, then we immediately infer from Lemma
1
that
c_{\min}(G)=2
. Hence we may further assume that
T
contains a universal vertex. Thus,
T
is a star
K_{1,n-1}
. By our earlier assumptions,
n\geq 3
. If
\Psi
is an arbitrary
c
-partition of
T
, then the universal vertex,
v
say, of
T
form a singleton set
\{v\}
in
\Psi
. Since
n\geq 3
, no leaf of
T
is a dominating set of
T
. Since the set of leaves in
T
forms a dominating set of
T
, the set of leaves cannot be a set in the
c
-partition
\Psi
, implying that
\Psi
contains at least two sets different from the singleton set
\{v\}
. Therefore,
|\Psi|\geq 3
. Since this is true for every
c
-partition of
T
, we infer that
c_{\min}(T)\geq 3
. On the other hand, if
(V_{1},V_{2})
is an arbitrary partition of the set of leaves of
T
, then the partition
{\cal X}=(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3})
where
V_{3}=\{v\}
is a
c
-partition of
T
, and so by Proposition
1

c_{\min}(G)\leq|{\cal X}|=3
. Consequently,
c_{\min}(T)=3
.
\Box

We present next a necessary and sufficient condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
.

Theorem 3

If
G
is a graph that does not contain a universal vertex, then
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
if and only if for every vertex
v\in V
, the set
N[v]
is a dominating set of
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph that does not contain a universal vertex. Suppose firstly that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
. Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
, and let
S=N_{G}[v]
. Since the vertex
v
is not adjacent to any vertex in
V\setminus S
, the set
V\setminus S
is not a dominating set of
G
. If
S
is not a dominating set of
G
, then
\{S,V\setminus S\}
is a coalition partition of
G
, and so by Proposition
1

c_{\min}(G)\leq 2
, a contradiction. Hence,
S
is a dominating set of
G
.

Conversely, suppose that the set
N[v]
is a dominating set of
G
for every vertex
v\in V
. We show that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
c_{\min}(G)<3
. By Theorem
1
, this implies that
c_{\min}(G)=2
. Let
{\cal X}=\{A,B\}
be a
c_{\min}
-partition of
G
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, neither set
A
nor
B
is a dominating set of
G
. Let
u
be a vertex of
G
not dominated by the set
A
, and so
A\cap N[u]=\emptyset
. Since
B=V\setminus A
, we have
N[u]\subseteq B
. By supposition the set
N[u]
is a dominating set of
G
. Since the property of being a dominating set is superhereditary, the set
B
is therefore a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
, as desired.
\Box

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
, we have the following characterization of graphs with no universal vertex satisfying
c_{\min}(G)=2
.

Corollary 3

If
G
is a graph that does not contain a universal vertex, then
c_{\min}(G)=2
if and only if there exists a vertex
v\in V
such that
N[v]
is not a dominating set of
G
.

As an application of Corollary
3
, consider the Heawood graph and the Petersen graph illustrated in Figure
2
(a) and (b), respectively. Since neither graph has a vertex whose closed neighborhood is a dominating set, we infer from Corollary
3
that the minmin coalition number is equal to
2
for both the Heawood graph and the Petersen graph.

As a further application of Corollary
3
, we can determine the minmin coalition number of a cycle.

Corollary 4

The minmin coalition number of a cycle
C_{n}
is given by following closed formula.

c_{\min}(C_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2&\text{\rm if~}n\geq 6\\
3&\text{\rm if~}n=3\text{~\rm or~}n=5.\\
4&\text{\rm if~}n=4.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

If
n\geq 6
, then by Corollary
3
we have
c_{\min}(C_{n})=2
. For
n\leq 5
, Theorem
3
implies that
c_{\min}(C_{n})\geq 3
. When
n=3
, the only
c
-partition of
C_{3}
consists of three singleton sets which implies that
c_{\min}(C_{3})=3
. When
n=4
, we note that every subset of vertices of
C_{4}
with at least two vertices is a dominating set, implying that the only
c
-partition of
C_{4}
consists of four singleton sets, and so
c_{\min}(C_{4})=4
. Suppose that
n=5
and consider the
5
-cycle
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{4}v_{5}v_{1}
. The partition
\{\{c_{1}\},\{c_{2},c_{3}\},\{c_{4},c_{5}\}\}
, for example, is a minimal
c
-partition of
C_{5}
, implying that
c_{\min}(C_{5})=3
.
\Box

We remark that Theorem
3
yields a polynomial time algorithm to determine whether a graph
G
with no universal vertex satisfies
c_{\min}(G)=2
or
c_{\min}(G)\neq 2
. We recursively examine all the vertices of
G
one by one. If there exists a vertex
v\in V
such that the set
N[v]
is not a dominating set of
G
, then we infer by Theorem
3
that
c_{\min}(G)=2
. If no such vertex exists, then we infer that
c_{\min}(G)\neq 2
. Given a set of vertices of
G
, one can easily check in polynomial time whether the set is a dominating set of
G
or not. Therefore, the total time required to determine whether
c_{\min}(G)=2
is polynomial. This yields the following result.

Theorem 4

There exists a polynomial time algorithm to determine whether for a given graph
G
with no universal vertices the equation
c_{\min}(G)=2
holds or not.

We present next a necessary and sufficient condition for a graph
G
to satisfy
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
.

Theorem 5

If
G
is a graph that does not contain a universal vertex, then
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
if and only if for every vertex
v\in V
and every partition
\{P,Q\}
of
N[v]
, at least one of
P
or
Q
is a dominating set of
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph that does not contain a universal vertex. Suppose firstly that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
. Let
v
be an arbitrary vertex of
G
, and let
\{P,Q\}
be an arbitrary partition of
N[v]
. Thus,
P\neq\emptyset
,
Q\neq\emptyset
and
P\cup Q=N[v]
. Suppose that
P=\{v\}
, and so
Q=N(v)
. By Theorem
3
,
N[v]
is a dominating set of
G
, implying that
Q
is a dominating set, yielding the desired result. Analogously, if
Q=\{v\}
, then the desired result follows. Hence we may assume that
P\neq\{v\}
and
Q\neq\{v\}
. Renaming the sets
P
and
Q
if necessary, we may assume that
v\in P
. Let
R=V\setminus(P\cup Q)
. Since
R
contains no neighbors of
v
, it is not a dominating set of
G
. We now consider the partition
{\cal X}=\{P,Q,R\}
of
V
. Suppose that neither
P
nor
Q
is a dominating set of
G
. Since
c_{\min}(G)\neq 2
, by Theorem
3
the set
P\cup Q
is a dominating set. Additionally,
P\cup R
is a dominating set of
G
noting that the vertex
v
dominates all vertices in
Q
. Therefore,
\cal X
is a
c
-partition of
G
, which implies that
c_{\min}(G)\leq 3
, a contradiction. Hence, at least one of
P
or
Q
is a dominating set.

Conversely, suppose that for every vertex
v\in V
and every partition
\{P,Q\}
of
N[v]
, at least one of
P
or
Q
is a dominating set of
G
. We show that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
c_{\min}(G)<4
. Hence by Theorem
1
,
c_{\min}(G)
can only be equal to
2
or
3
. If
c_{\min}(G)=2
, then by Corollary
3
there exists a vertex
v\in V
such that
N[v]
is not a dominating set of
G
. However in this case for every possible partition
\{P,Q\}
of
N[v]
, neither
P
nor
Q
is a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Therefore,
c_{\min}(G)=3
. Let
{\cal X}=\{A,B,C\}
be a
c_{\min}
-partition of
G
. Thus,
{\cal X}
is a minimal
c
-partition of
G
and
|{\cal X}|=3
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, we note that none of the sets
A
,
B
and
C
is a dominating set of
G
. Let
u
be a vertex of
G
not dominated by the set
A
, and so
A\cap N[u]=\emptyset
. Since
B\cup C=V\setminus A
, we therefore have
N[u]\subseteq B\cup C
. Suppose that
B\cap N[u]=\emptyset
, implying that
N[u]\subseteq C
. By Theorem
3
and our assumption that
c_{\min}(G)\neq 2
, the set
N[u]
is a dominating set. Since the property of being a dominating set is superhereditary, the set
C
is therefore a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
B\cap N[u]\neq\emptyset
. Analogously,
C\cap N[u]\neq\emptyset
. Thus letting
P=B\cap N[u]
and
Q=C\cap N[u]
, we have that
\{P,Q\}
is a partition of
N[u]
. Hence by supposition, at least one of
P
or
Q
is a dominating set of
G
. This in turn implies that at least one of
B
or
C
is a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
.
\Box

{\cal F}
be the family of graphs
G
with vertex set
V=\{v,x,y\}\cup U
, where
\deg(v)=2
and
N(v)=\{x,y\}
, and where the vertices
x
and
y
are not adjacent but are both adjacent to all vertices of
U
as illustrated in Figure

. Furthermore, the subgraph
G[U]
induced by
U
contains any number of edges, including the possibility of no edges. As an application of Theorem
5
, we have the following result.

Theorem 6

If
G
is a graph with
\delta(G)=2
that does not contain a universal vertex, then
c_{\min}(G)=4
if and only if
G\in{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with
\delta(G)=2
that does not contain a universal vertex. Let
v
be a vertex of degree
2
in
G
, and let
N(v)=\{x,y\}
. Further, let
U=V\setminus N[v]
. Suppose firstly that
c_{\min}(G)=4
. Let
P=\{v,x\}
and
Q=\{y\}
. By Theorem
5
, at least one of
P
and
Q
is a dominating set of
G
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, the set
Q
is not a dominating set of
G
, implying that
P
is necessarily a dominating set of
G
. This in turn implies that
x
is adjacent to all vertices of
U
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, the vertex
x
is therefore not adjacent to the vertex
y
. Interchanging the roles of
x
and
y
, identical arguments show that the vertex
y
is adjacent to all vertices of
U
. Hence,
G\in{\cal F}
.

Conversely, suppose that
G\in{\cal F}
. We adopt the notation used in the definition of the family
{\cal F}
, and so
V=\{v,x,y\}\cup U
, where
N_{G}(v)=\{x,y\}
. We show firstly that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
c_{\min}(G)=2
. Let
{\cal X}=\{A,B\}
be a
c_{\min}
-partition of
G
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, neither set
A
nor
B
is a dominating set of
G
. If
\{x,y\}\subseteq A
or
\{x,y\}\subseteq B
, then since
\{x,y\}
is a dominating set of
G
, at least one of
A
or
B
is a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
|A\cap\{x,y\}|=1
and
|B\cap\{x,y\}|=1
. Renaming the sets
A
and
B
if necessary, we may assume that
x\in A
and
y\in B
, and that
|A|\geq 2
. Let
w
be a vertex in
A
different from
x
. Thus either
w=v
or
w\in U
. In both cases, the set
A
is a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
c_{\min}(G)\geq 3
.

We show next that
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
. Suppose, to the contrary, that
c_{\min}(G)=3
. Let
{\cal X}=\{A,B,C\}
be a
c_{\min}
-partition of
G
. Since
G
has no universal vertex, none of the sets
A
,
B
or
C
is a dominating set of
G
. We therefore infer that the set
\{x\}
forms a singleton set in
{\cal X}
, as does the set
\{y\}
. Renaming the sets
A
,
B
and
C
we may assume that
A=\{x\}
and
B=\{y\}
, and so
C=V\setminus\{x,y\}
. However, such a set
C
is a dominating set of
G
, a contradiction. Hence,
c_{\min}(G)\geq 4
. The partition
\Psi=(\{v\},\{x\},\{y\},U)
is a
c
-partition of
G
, implying by Proposition
1
that
c_{\min}(G)\leq 4
. Consequently,
c_{\min}(G)=4
.
\Box

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper we address an open problem posed by Haynes, Hedetniemi, Hedetniemi, McRae, and Mohan
[
2
]
to study the minmin coalition number
c_{\min}(G)
of a graph
G
. We show that if
G
is a graph of order
n
, then
2\leq c_{\min}(G)\leq n
. Among other results, we characterized graphs
G
of order
n
satisfying
c_{\min}(G)=n
and we provided polynomial time algorithm to determine if
c_{\min}(G)=2
. It would be intriguing to investigate the open question of whether a polynomial time algorithm exists to determine larger values of
c_{\min}(G)
.

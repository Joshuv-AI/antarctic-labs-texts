More is Less: Inducing Sparsity via Overparameterization
Hung-Hsu Chou, Johannes Maly, Holger Rauhut

Abstract
In deep learning it is common to overparameterize neural networks, that is, to use more parameters than training samples. Quite surprisingly training the neural network via (stochastic) gradient descent leads to models that generalize very well, while classical statistics would suggest overfitting. In order to gain understanding of this implicit bias phenomenon we study the special case of sparse recovery (compressed sensing) which is of interest on its own. More precisely, in order to reconstruct a vector from underdetermined linear measurements, we introduce a corresponding overparameterized square loss functional, where the vector to be reconstructed is deeply factorized into several vectors. We show that, if there exists an exact solution, vanilla gradient flow for the overparameterized loss functional converges to a good approximation of the solution of minimal ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm. The latter is well-known to promote sparse solutions. As a by-product, our results significantly improve the sample complexity for compressed sensing via gradient flow/descent on overparameterized models derived in previous works. The theory accurately predicts the recovery rate in numerical experiments. Our proof relies on analyzing a certain Bregman divergence of the flow. This bypasses the obstacles caused by non-convexity and should be of independent interest.

Introduction

Overparameterization is highly successful in learning with deep neural networks. While this empirical finding was likely observed by countless practitioners, it was systematically studied in numerical experiments in
[
20
,
21
,
33
]
. Increasing the number of parameters far beyond the number of training samples leads to better generalization properties of the learned networks. This is in stark contrast to classical statistics, which would rather suggest overfitting in this scenario.
The loss function typically has infinitely many global minimizers in this setting (there are usually infinitely many networks fitting the training samples exactly in the overparameterized regime
[
33
]
), so that the employed optimization algorithm has a significant influence on the computed solution. The commonly used (stochastic) gradient descent and its variants seem to have an implicit bias towards “nice” networks with good generalization properties. It is conjectured that in fact, (stochastic) gradient descent applied to learning deep networks favors solutions of low complexity.
Of course, the right notion of “low complexity” needs to be identified and may depend on the precise scenario, i.e., network architecture. In the simplified setting of linear networks, i.e., matrix factorizations, and the problem of matrix recovery or more specifically matrix completion, several works

identified the right notion of low complexity to be low rank of the factorized matrix (or tensor, in some these works). Nevertheless, despite initial theoretical results, a fully convincing theory is not yet available even for this simplified setting.

As a contribution to this line of research, this article studies the performance of overparameterized models in the context of the classical compressed sensing problem, where the goal is to recover an unknown high-dimensional signal
{\bf x}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
from few linear measurements of the form

\displaystyle{\bf y}={\bf A}{\bf x}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{M},

(1)

where
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
models the measurement process. Whereas this is in general not possible for
M<N
, the seminal works
[
6
,
7
,
9
]
showed that unique reconstruction of
{\bf x}^{\star}
from
{\bf A}
and
{\bf y}
becomes feasible if
{\bf x}^{\star}
is
s
-sparse,
{\bf A}
satisfies certain conditions (e.g. restricted isometry property), and
M
scales like
s\log(N/s)
. While it is well-known that

{\bf x}^{\star}
can be recovered via
\ell_{1}
-minimization, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\bf x}^{\star}=\argmin_{{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf y}}\|{\bf z}\|_{1},

(2)

also known as basis pursuit, surprisingly enough, we observe similar recovery with gradient descent on overparameterized models, which originate from a very different background. (Here in the following,
\|\cdot\|_{p}
denotes the standard
\ell_{p}
-norm for
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
{\bf x}_{0}>0
means all entries of
{\bf x}_{0}
are strictly positive.) Our goal is to understand this connection via gradient flow – a continuous version of gradient descent – in this context.

In order to gain a first understanding of the power of overparameterization, we compare the quadratic loss (without overparameterization)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}}({\bf x}):=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}

(3)

and its overparameterized versions

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}\big({\bf x}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(L)}\big)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\Big\|{\bf A}\big({\bf x}^{(1)}\odot\cdots\odot{\bf x}^{(L)}\big)-{\bf y}\Big\|_{2}^{2},

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}\big({\bf u}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf u}^{(L)},{\bf v}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf v}^{(L)}\big)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\Big\|{\bf A}\Big(\bigodot_{k=1}^{L}{\bf u}^{(k)}-\bigodot_{k=1}^{L}{\bf v}^{(k)}\Big)-{\bf y}\Big\|_{2}^{2},

(5)

where
\odot
is the Hadamard (or entry-wise) product. It turns out that the results from minimizing different loss functions via gradient flow/descent are quite different. Being initialized with
{\bf x}(0)=\bm{0}
, on the quadratic loss
\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}}
gradient flow and descent converge to the least-squares solution

\displaystyle{\bf x}_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}{\bf x}(t)=\argmin_{{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf y}}\|{\bf z}\|_{2}\;,

which is unrelated to the sparse ground-truth
{\bf x}^{\star}
in general. On the other hand, gradient flow and descent applied to
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
often lead to sparse results, despite the absence of explicit regularization. This can be viewed as a particular instance of implicit bias/regularization. In this work, we restrict ourselves to gradient flow under identical initialization — all factors
{\bf x}^{(k)}
resp.
{\bf u}^{(k)}
and
{\bf v}^{(k)}
are initialized with the the same vector — and formally define the gradient flow for
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
as

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{d}{dt}}{\bf x}^{(k)}(t)=-\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}({\bf x}^{(1)}(t),\ldots,{\bf x}^{(L)}(t)),\qquad{\bf x}^{(k)}(0)={\bf x}_{0}>0,\quad k=1,\ldots,L.

(The gradient flows
{\bf u}^{(k)}(t)
and
{\bf v}^{(k)}(t)
for
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
are defined similarly.) Due to the identical initialization, the factors
{\bf x}^{(k)}

{\bf u}^{(k)}
and
{\bf v}^{(k)}

[

,
23

\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
and
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
to the

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}({\bf x})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}

(6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\pm}({\bf u},{\bf v})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}({\bf u}^{\odot L}-{\bf v}^{\odot L})-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2},

(7)

where
{\bf z}^{\odot L}={\bf z}\odot\cdots\odot{\bf z}
denotes the

-th Hadamard power of a vector. Let us mention that this common simplification already appeared in

Our results show that, under the above initialization, the product

(and

-minimization problem (

. We refer the interested reader to the monograph
[
10
]

Our main result improves on previous work: For

, it was shown in

defined in (

1.1
Main results

As discussed above, we consider vanilla gradient flow on the reduced factorized models (
6
) and (
7
). We show that if the solution space is non-empty, gradient flow converges to a solution of (
1
) whose
\ell_{1}
-norm is close to the minimum among all solutions. (Here
{\bf x}_{0}>0
means all entries of
{\bf x}_{0}
are strictly positive.) We now state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
L\geq 2
,
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
, and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
. For the reduced loss function
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
defined in (
7
)
let
{\bf u}(t)
and
{\bf v}(t)
follow the flow

\displaystyle{\bf u}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla_{{\bf u}}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\big({\bf u},{\bf v}\big),\qquad{\bf u}(0)={\bf u}_{0}>0,

\displaystyle{\bf v}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla_{{\bf v}}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\big({\bf u},{\bf v}\big),\qquad{\bf v}(0)={\bf v}_{0}>0.

Let
\tilde{{\bf u}}={\bf u}^{\odot L}
,
\tilde{{\bf v}}={\bf v}^{\odot L}
. Suppose the solution set
S=\{{\bf z}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf y}\}
is non-empty. Then

\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\tilde{{\bf u}}(t)-\tilde{{\bf v}}(t)\right)

(8)

exists and is contained in
S
.

Moreover, denote the
signed weighted

\ell_{1}
-norm of
{\bf z}
with weights
({\bf w}_{+},{\bf w}_{-})
by

\|{\bf z}\|_{({\bf w}_{+},{\bf w}_{-}),1}=\|{\bf w}_{+}\odot{\bf z}_{+}+{\bf w}_{-}\odot{\bf z}_{-}\|_{1}

where
{\bf z}_{+},{\bf z}_{-}
are the positive and the negative part of
{\bf z}
, respectively. Let
\gamma=1-\frac{2}{L}\in[0,1)
. Consider

\displaystyle{\bf w}_{+}=\tilde{{\bf u}}(0)^{\odot-\gamma}

\displaystyle,\quad{\bf w}_{-}=\tilde{{\bf v}}(0)^{\odot-\gamma},

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\|\tilde{{\bf u}}(0)\|_{{\bf w}_{+},1}+\|\tilde{{\bf v}}(0)\|_{{\bf w}_{-},1}

\displaystyle,\quad\beta_{\min}=\min_{n\in[N]}w_{n}\min(\tilde{u}_{n}(0),\tilde{v}_{n}(0)).

Let
Q:=\min_{{\bf z}\in S}\|{\bf z}\|_{({\bf w}_{+},{\bf w}_{-}),1}
. Suppose
Q>\beta_{1}
. Then
\|\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}\|_{({\bf w}_{+},{\bf w}_{-}),1}-Q\leq\varepsilon Q
, where
\varepsilon
is defined as

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=\begin{cases}\frac{\log(\beta_{1}/\beta_{\min})}{\log(Q/\beta_{1})}&\text{if }L=2,\\[6.0pt]
\frac{L(\beta_{1}^{\gamma}-\beta_{\min}^{\gamma})}{2(Q^{\gamma}-\beta_{1}^{\gamma})}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

(9)

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section
3
.

Remark 1.2
.

One may interpret Theorem
1.1
from the following perspectives.

•

Effect of depth.

Consider the case
L>2
. According to (
9
), when
\beta_{\min}\ll\beta_{1}\ll Q
are fixed, the error bound
\varepsilon
scales like
\beta_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{L}}
. This indicates that the shrinkage effect of
\varepsilon
with respect to
L
increases as
L
increases.

•

Effect of weight.

For
L=2
, we simply get
\ell_{1}
-minimization, while for
L>2
we get weighted
\ell_{1}
-minimization, where the weights purely depend on the initialization. More importantly, we can put different weights for the positive and negative component with respect to the same entry. If
\tilde{{\bf u}}(0)=\tilde{{\bf v}}(0)
, then we have regular weighted minimization. If furthermore
\tilde{{\bf u}}(0)=\alpha{\bf 1}
for some
\alpha
, then we have un-weighted minimization.

•

Effect of
\beta_{\min}
.

Note that when
\beta_{\min}\ll\beta_{1}
(which holds for large
N
), the effect of
\beta_{\min}
in (
9
) becomes insignificant for
L>2
. Hence without much loss we can upper bound
\varepsilon
by

\frac{L}{2}\cdot\frac{\beta_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{L}}}{Q^{1-\frac{2}{L}}-\beta_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{L}}},

which no longer depends on
\beta_{\min}
. On the other hand, the effect of
\beta_{\min}
remains significant for
L=2
and cannot be removed easily. This indicates that when there is randomness in initialization, the error for
L>2
might be easier to bound than for the case where
L=2
.

•

Generality.

As previously discussed in Section
1
, Theorem
1.1
applies to the general loss function
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
in (
5
) as well as long as all factors
{\bf u}^{(k)}
and
{\bf v}^{(k)}
are initialized identically with
{\bf u}_{0}
and
{\bf v}_{0}
.

•

Scale invariance.

Equation (
9
) provides an explicit non-asymptotic bound that is scale invariant in the following sense. Let
\lambda>0
be a scaling factor. If we replace
{\bf u}_{0}
,
{\bf v}_{0}
, and
{\bf y}
with
\lambda{\bf u}_{0}
,
\lambda{\bf v}_{0}
, and
\lambda^{L}{\bf y}
, then
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{\infty}
, and
Q
will each be scaled by
\lambda^{L}
and the right-hand side of (
9
) remains unaffected under the scaling.

•

Convergence rate.

While decreasing the initialization scale leads to decreasing approximation error, empirically it makes the gradient descent converge slower. Hence there is a trade-off between accuracy and computational complexity.

1.2
Application to compressed sensing

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, accurately reconstructing (approximately) sparse vectors from incomplete linear measurements (compressed sensing) can provably be achieved via gradient flow on
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
, using the minimal amount of measurements. This suggests that gradient descent on overparametrized least-square functionals can be viewed as an alternative algorithm for compressed sensing. To be concrete let us provide example results in this direction. A matrix
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is said to satisfy the stable null space property of order
s
and constant
\rho\in(0,1)
if for all vector
{\bf v}\in\ker({\bf A})\setminus\{0\}
and all index sets
S\subset\{1,\ldots,N\}
of cardinality at most
s
it holds

\|{\bf v}_{S}\|_{1}\leq\rho\|{\bf v}_{S^{c}}\|_{1},

where
{\bf v}_{S}
denotes the restriction of
{\bf v}
to the entries in
S
and
S^{c}
is the complement of
S
. It is well-known that various types of random matrices satisfy the stable null space property
in an (almost) optimal parameter regime with high probability,
see for instance
[
5
,
10
,
19
]
and references therein. For instance, for a Gaussian random matrix
{\bf A}
this is true provided that

M\gtrsim\rho^{-2}s\log(eN/s).

(10)

Moreover, let us also introduce the error of best
s
-term approximation in
\ell_{1}
of a vector
x
as

\sigma_{s}({\bf x})_{1}=\min_{{\bf z}:\|{\bf z}\|_{0}\leq s}\|{\bf x}-{\bf z}\|_{1},

where
\|{\bf z}\|_{0}=\#\{\ell:z_{\ell}\neq 0\}
denotes the sparsity of
{\bf z}
. Clearly,
\sigma_{s}({\bf x})_{1}=0
if
{\bf x}
is
s
-sparse.

The following result is a straightforward implication of
[
10
, Theorem 4.14]
together with Theorem
1.1
.
We also refer to Figure

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
{\bf x}_{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
{\bf y}={\bf A}{\bf x}_{*}
, where
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
satisfies the stable null space property of order
s
with constant
\rho\in(0,1)
. Let
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}
be the limit vector of gradient flow as in (
8
) with initialization
{\bf u}_{0}={\bf v}_{0}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\|\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}-{\bf x}_{*}\|_{1}\leq\frac{1+\rho}{1-\rho}\left(\varepsilon\|{\bf x}_{*}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf x}_{*})_{1}\right),

where
\varepsilon
is defined in (
9
).

We can also show stability with respect to noise on the measurements assuming a certain quotient property of the measurement matrix
[
10
]
in addition to the null space property. To be precise, we say that
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
satisfies the
\ell_{1}
-quotient property with constant
d
relative to the
\ell_{2}
-norm if for all
{\bf e}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}

{\bf u}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
with

such that

\|{\bf u}\|_{1}\leq d\sqrt{s_{*}}\|{\bf e}\|_{2}\quad\mbox{ with }s_{*}=M/(\log(eN/M).

Gaussian random matrices satisfy this property for an absolute constant
d
with high probability, see
[
10
, Chapter 11]
for details.

Moreover, we require the following strengthened version of the null space property. A matrix
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
satisfies the
\ell_{2}
-robust null space property with constants
0<\rho<1
and
\tau>0
of order
s
(with respect to
\ell_{2}
) if, for any subset
S\subset[N]
of cardinality
s
,

\|{\bf v}_{S}\|_{2}\leq\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{s}}\|{\bf v}_{S^{c}}\|_{1}+\tau\|{\bf A}{\bf v}\|_{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }{\bf v}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(11)

Again, Gaussian random matrices satisfy this property with appropriate absolute constants
\tau
and
\rho
with high probability under (
10
).

The following theorem, which combines Theorem
1.1
with
[

via gradient flow on the overparameterized functional

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
c>1
be fixed. Let
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
be a matrix satisfying the
\ell_{2}
-robust null space property with constants

and

of order

,

,

,

,

Most of the above mentioned works consider the case of

]

,
16

,

]
, implicitly concentrate on the case

by only considering two specific Bregman divergences (one for the symmetric and one for the asymmetric case, which need to be distinguished in the matrix setting). In contrast, our work provides suitable Bregman divergences for all

and as such allows to analyze gradient descent on overparamterized loss functions with arbitrary depth. Let us emphasize that the deeper case

has not been covered by any existing work to the best of our knowledge.

Due to the strong coupling of weights in factorized matrix sensing,
several existing works restrict themselves to the vector case. In
[

]
the authors derive robust reconstruction guarantees for gradient descent and the compressed sensing model (

). Whereas in
[

]

]
extends the results to

. In
[

builds upon those ideas to perform sparse recovery with gradient descent by assuming a restricted isometry property of

Instead of specific initialization, in
[

the authors examine the limits of gradient flow when initialized by

1.5
Notation

For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
[N]=\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. Boldface lower-case letters like
{\bf x}
represent vectors with entries
x_{n}
, while boldface upper-case letters like
{\bf A}
represent matrices with entries
A_{mn}
. For
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
{\bf x}\geq{\bf y}
means that
x_{n}\geq y_{n}
, for all
n\in[N]
. We use
\odot
to denote the Hadamard product, i.e., the vectors
{\bf x}\odot{\bf y}
and
{\bf x}^{\odot p}
have entries
({\bf x}\odot{\bf y})_{n}=x_{n}y_{n}
and
({\bf x}^{\odot p})_{n}=x_{n}^{p}
, respectively. We abbreviate
\tilde{\bf x}:=\bigodot_{k\in[L]}{\bf x}^{(k)}={\bf x}^{(1)}\odot\cdots\odot{\bf x}^{(L)}
. The logarithm is applied entry-wise to positive vectors, i.e.,
\log({\bf x})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
\log({\bf x})_{n}=\log(x_{n})
. For convenience we denote
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}=\{{\bf x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{n}\geq 0\;\,\forall n\in[N]\}
.

2
Positive Case

To show Theorem
1.1
, we are going to prove in this section the following simplified version, Theorem
2.1
, that treats the model in (
6
) and is restricted to the positive orthant (recall that gradient flow applied to (
6

Since analyzing
\mathcal{L}
instead of
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
comes with less notational load, this approach facilitates to digest the core argument. The proof of Theorem

is then a straight-forward adaption that only requires technical fine tuning and will be discussed in Section

. Let us define the set of non-negative solutions

\displaystyle S_{+}=\{{\bf z}\geq 0:{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf y}\}

(12)

and note that Theorem
2.1
can be easily adapted to other orthants by changing the signs of corresponding entries of the initialization vector.

Theorem 2.1

(Equivalence to
\ell_{1}
-minimization, positive case)
.

Let
L\geq 2
,
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
and
{\bf y}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and assume that
S_{+}
defined in (
12
) is non-empty. For the reduced loss function
\mathcal{L}
in (
6
), define
{\bf x}(t)
via

{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla_{{\bf x}}\mathcal{L}({\bf x}(t)),\qquad{\bf x}(0)={\bf x}_{0}>0.

(13)

Let
\tilde{\bf x}={\bf x}^{\odot L}
.Then the limit
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{\bf x}(t)

exists and
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}\in S_{+}
.

Moreover, denote the weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm of
{\bf z}
with weight
{\bf w}
by
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}:=\|{\bf w}\odot{\bf z}\|_{1}
and consider

\displaystyle{\bf w}=\tilde{\bf x}(0)^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1},\quad Q:=\min_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1},\quad\beta_{1}=\|\tilde{\bf x}(0)\|_{{\bf w},1},\quad\beta_{\min}=\min_{n\in[N]}w_{n}\tilde{x}_{n}(0).

Suppose

, then
\|\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}\|_{{\bf w},1}-Q\leq\varepsilon Q
, where

) in Theorem

.

.

2.1
Bregman divergence

As a crucial ingredient for Theorem
2.1
, we show convergence of
{\bf x}(t)^{\odot L}
and characterize its limit. In order to state the corresponding result, we introduce the function

g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z})=\begin{cases}\langle{\bf z},\log({\bf z})-{\bf 1}-\log({\bf x})\rangle&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle{\bf z},{\bf x}^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf z}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

(14)

Theorem 2.2
.

For
L\geq 2
, let
\tilde{\bf x}(t)={\bf x}(t)^{\odot L}
and

\displaystyle{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bf x}(t))=-L\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}(t)-{\bf y})\right]\odot{\bf x}^{\odot L-1}(t)

(15)

with
{\bf x}(0)\geq 0
. Assume that
S_{+}
in (
12
) is non-empty. Then
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{\bf x}(t)
exists and

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}g_{\tilde{\bf x}(0)}({\bf z}).

(16)

The proof of this theorem is based on the Bregman divergence defined as follows.

Definition 2.3

(Bregman Divergence)
.

Let
F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuously-differentiable, strictly convex function defined on a closed convex set
\Omega
. The Bregman divergence associated with
F
for points
p,q\in\Omega
is defined as

D_{F}(p,q)=F(p)-F(q)-\langle\nabla F(q),p-q\rangle.

(17)

By strict convexity of
F
it is straight-forward to verify the following.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
4
]
)
.

The Bregman divergence
D_{F}
is non-negative and, for any
q\in\Omega
, the function
p\mapsto D_{F}(p,q)
is strictly convex.

To prove Theorem
2.2
, we use the Bregman divergence with respect to
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with

F({\bf x})=\left.\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\langle{\bf x}\odot\log({\bf x})-{\bf x},\bm{1}\rangle&\text{if }L=2\\
\frac{L}{2(2-L)}\langle{\bf x}^{\odot\frac{2}{L}},\bm{1}\rangle&\text{if }L>2\end{cases}\right\}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}\log(x_{n})-x_{n}&\text{if }L=2\\
\frac{L}{2(2-L)}\sum_{n=1}^{N}x_{n}^{\frac{2}{L}}&\text{if }L>2,\end{cases}

(18)

with the understanding that
z\log(z)=0
for
z=0
.
Note that
F
is strictly convex because its Hessian
\mathbf{H}_{F}({\bf x})
is diagonal with positive diagonal entries
\frac{1}{L}x_{n}^{-2+\frac{2}{L}}
, for
{\bf x}
in the interior of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
, i.e., if
x_{n}>0
for all
n
. Thus the Bregman divergence
D_{F}
is well-defined. It has the following property.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F
be the function defined in (
18
) and
\tilde{\bf x}(t)\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
be a continuous function with
\tilde{\bf x}(0)>\bm{0}
. Let
{\bf z}\geq\bm{0}
be fixed. If
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
is bounded, then
\|\tilde{\bf x}(t)\|_{2}
is bounded.

Proof.

We will prove the statement by contraposition. Let
\|\tilde{\bf x}(t)\|_{2}
be unbounded. Then there exists a sequence
0<t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots
such that
\|\tilde{\bf x}(t_{k})\|_{2}\to\infty
. Hence there exists some
n\in[N]
and a subsequence
0<t_{n_{1}}\leq t_{n_{2}}\leq\dots
such that
\tilde{x}_{n}(t_{n_{k}})\to\infty
.
Note that

\displaystyle D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\big(\langle\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf z}\odot\log(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),\bm{1}\rangle+\langle{\bf z}\odot\log({\bf z})-{\bf z},\bm{1}\rangle\big)&\mbox{if }L=2,\\
\frac{1}{2(L-2)}\big(\langle(L-2)\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{2}{L}}+2{\bf z}\odot\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot(\frac{2}{L}-1)},\bm{1}\rangle-L\langle{\bf z}^{\odot\frac{2}{L}},\bm{1}\rangle\big)&\mbox{if }L>2.\end{cases}

For
z\geq 0
, both the function
x\mapsto x-z\log(x)

(for
L=2
) and the function
x\mapsto(L-2)x^{\frac{2}{L}}+2zx^{\frac{2}{L}-1}
(for
L>2
) are bounded from below and diverge to infinity as
x
goes to infinity. Thus
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t_{n_{k}}))\to\infty
and consequently
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
is unbounded.
∎

Furthermore, we will use the following observation.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
{\bf x}
follows the dynamics (
13
) in Theorem
2.1
. Let
\tilde{\bf x}={\bf x}^{\odot L}
. Then for any
{\bf z}\in S_{+}
,

\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))=-2L\cdot\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}}).

(19)

Proof.

Note that due to continuity, for all
t\geq 0
,
{\bf x}(t)
remains non-negative (if any entry
x(t)_{i}
vanishes at
t_{*}>0
, then
x(t)_{i}=0
for all
t\geq t_{*}
by the shape of (
15
)). Suppose
{\bf z}\in S_{+}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\left[F({\bf z})-F(\tilde{\bf x}(t))-\langle\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),{\bf z}-\tilde{\bf x}(t)\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=0-\langle\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)\rangle-\langle\partial_{t}\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),{\bf z}-\tilde{\bf x}(t)\rangle+\langle\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\partial_{t}\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t)),{\bf z}-\tilde{\bf x}(t)\rangle.

By the chain rule and the diagonal shape of
\mathbf{H}_{F}
, we know that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(t))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{H}_{F}(\tilde{\bf x}(t))\cdot\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{L}\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot(-2+\frac{2}{L})}\odot\tilde{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{L}{\bf x}(t)^{\odot(-2L+2)}\odot\big(L{\bf x}(t)^{\odot(L-1)}\odot{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)\big)

\displaystyle={\bf x}(t)^{\odot(-2L+2)}\odot{\bf x}(t)^{\odot(L-1)}\odot\big(-L\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}(t)-{\bf y})\right]\odot{\bf x}^{\odot L-1}(t)\big)

\displaystyle=-L\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf y})\right].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))

\displaystyle=L\langle{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf y}),{\bf z}-\tilde{\bf x}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-L\langle{\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf y},{\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf A}{\bf z}\rangle

\displaystyle=-L\|{\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}=-2L\cdot\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}}).

This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let us begin with a brief outline of the proof. We will first use Lemma
2.6
to show that the loss function converges to zero. Then we employ Lemma
2.5
to deduce the convergence of
\tilde{\bf x}(t)
. Finally, we conclude with the unique characterization of the limit
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}
induced by the Bregman divergence.

We first show that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})=0
. Since
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))\geq 0
and
\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))\leq 0
by Lemma
2.6
,
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
must converge for any fixed
{\bf z}\in S_{+}
. Since
\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})
is non-negative and non-increasing in
t
, for any
t>0
we have
\min_{\tau\leq t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(\tau)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})=\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})
. Thus by Lemma
2.6

\displaystyle D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))-D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))=\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(\tau))d\tau=-2L\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(\tau)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})d\tau\leq-2Lt\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}}).

Rearranging terms we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})\leq\frac{D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))-D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))}{2Lt}\leq\frac{D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))}{2Lt}\longrightarrow 0

as
t\to\infty
. We conclude that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\bf x}(t)^{\odot\frac{1}{L}})=0
and thus
\lim_{t\to\infty}{\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}(t)={\bf y}
.

We now deduce that
\tilde{\bf x}(t)
converges to some
\bar{z}\in S_{+}
. By Lemma
2.6
,
D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
is bounded. By Lemma
2.5
,
\|\tilde{\bf x}(t)\|_{2}
is bounded. According to the Bolzano-Weierstrass Theorem, there exists a sequence
\{t_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\tilde{\bf x}(t_{k})
converges to some
\bar{{\bf z}}\in S_{+}
since the loss converges to zero. Consequently,
D_{F}(\bar{{\bf z}},\tilde{\bf x}(t_{k}))
converges to zero. Because
D_{F}(\bar{{\bf z}},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
is non-increasing, this implies that
D_{F}(\bar{{\bf z}},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
converges to zero as well. By strict convexity of
D_{F}
in its first component and the fact that
D_{F}({\bf w},{\bf w})=0
,
\tilde{\bf x}
must converge to
\bar{z}\in S_{+}
.

Because
\partial_{t}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(t))
is identical for all
{\bf z}\in S_{+}
, the difference

\displaystyle\Delta_{{\bf z}}=D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))-D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty})

(20)

is constant in
{\bf z}\in S_{+}
. By non-negativity of
D_{F}
,

\displaystyle D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))\geq\Delta_{{\bf z}}=\Delta_{\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}}=D_{F}(\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty},\tilde{\bf x}(0)).

(21)

Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}\in

\displaystyle\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}D_{F}({\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0))=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}F({\bf z})-F(\tilde{\bf x}(0))-\langle\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(0)),{\bf z}-\tilde{\bf x}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}F({\bf z})-\langle\nabla F(\tilde{\bf x}(0)),{\bf z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}\begin{cases}\sum_{n=1}^{N}z_{n}\log(z_{n})-z_{n}-\log(\tilde{x}_{n}(0))z_{n}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\sum_{n=1}^{N}-z_{n}^{\frac{2}{L}}+\frac{2}{L}\tilde{x}_{n}(0)^{\frac{2}{L}-1}z_{n}&\text{if }L>2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}\begin{cases}\langle{\bf z},\log({\bf z})-{\bf 1}-\log(\tilde{\bf x}(0))\rangle&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle{\bf z},\tilde{\bf x}(0)^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf z}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}&\text{if }L>2\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{{\bf z}\in S_{+}}g_{\tilde{\bf x}(0)}({\bf z}).

This completes the proof.
∎

According to Theorem
2.2
,

g_{\tilde{\bf x}(0)}(\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty})\leq g_{\tilde{\bf x}(0)}({\bf z})

(22)

for all
{\bf z}\in S_{+}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
. The proof of Theorem
2.1
now uses that
g_{\tilde{\bf x}(0)}({\bf z})\approx\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}
when
\tilde{\bf x}(0)
is small such that (
22
) implies that
\|{\bf x}\|_{{\bf w},1}\leq\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}+\varepsilon
, for some small
\varepsilon
. Before turning to the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we thus investigate the relation between
g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z})
and
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

Consider the function
g_{\bf x}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, which was defined in (
14
) and appeared in Theorem
2.2
, given by

g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z}):=\begin{cases}\langle{\bf z},\log({\bf z})-{\bf 1}-\log({\bf x})\rangle&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle{\bf z},{\bf x}^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle-\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf z}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

Suppose
{\bf x}>0
and
{\bf z}\geq 0
. Denote the
unsigned weighted

\ell_{1}
-norm of
{\bf z}
with weight
{\bf w}
by

\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf w}\odot{\bf z}\|_{1}.

Let
{\bf w}={\bf x}^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}
,
\beta_{1}=\|{\bf x}\|_{{\bf w},1}
, and
\beta_{\min}=\min_{n\in[N]}w_{n}x_{n}
. Then

\tilde{g}(\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1},\beta_{1})\leq g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z})\leq\tilde{g}(\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1},\beta_{\min}).

(23)

where
\tilde{g}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined as

\tilde{g}(a,b)=\begin{cases}a(\log a-1-\log b)&\text{if }L=2,\\
a-\frac{L}{2}a^{\frac{2}{L}}b^{1-\frac{2}{L}}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

(24)

Proof.

Note that the theorem holds trivially for
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}=0
. Hence we only consider the case
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}>0
. Since
{\bf z}\in\{\bm{\xi}\geq 0:\|\bm{\xi}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}\}
,

\displaystyle g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\bm{\xi}\geq 0,\,\|\bm{\xi}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}}g_{{\bf x}}(\bm{\xi}),

(25)

\displaystyle g_{{\bf x}}({\bf z})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\bm{\xi}\geq 0,\,\|\bm{\xi}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}}g_{{\bf x}}(\bm{\xi}).

(26)

Note that on such domain,
g_{{\bf x}}(\bm{\xi})
can be expressed as

\displaystyle g_{{\bf x}}(\bm{\xi})=\begin{cases}\langle\bm{\xi},\log(\bm{\xi})-\log({\bf x})\rangle-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-\frac{L}{2}\|\bm{\xi}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

Hence while taking the supremum/infimum, we only need to concentrates on the term
\langle\bm{\xi},\log(\bm{\xi})-\log({\bf x})\rangle
for
L=2
, and the term
-\frac{L}{2}\|\bm{\xi}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}
for
L>2
. Since those terms are convex (in
\bm{\xi}
) and the domain is a convex set, the supremum is attained at an extreme point of the form
\bm{\xi}=c{\bf e}_{n}
for some
c>0
where
{\bf e}_{n}
is a coordinate vector. To determine
c
, we use the relation
\|\bm{\xi}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}
to deduce that

c=\frac{\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}}{w_{n}}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}x_{n}^{1-\frac{2}{L}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sup_{\bm{\xi}\geq 0,\,\|\bm{\xi}\|_{{\bf w},1}=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}}g_{{\bf x}}(\xi)=\sup_{n\in[N]}g_{{\bf x}}(c{\bf e}_{n})

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in[N]}\begin{cases}c\log(c)-c\log(x_{n})-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-\frac{L}{2}c^{\frac{2}{L}}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\sup_{n\in[N]}\begin{cases}\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}(\log\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-1-\log(x_{n}))&\text{if }L=2,\\
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}^{\frac{2}{L}}x_{n}^{(1-\frac{2}{L})\frac{2}{L}}&\text{if }L>2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}(\log\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-1-\log(\min_{n\in[N]}x_{n}))&\text{if }L=2,\\
\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}-\frac{L}{2}\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}^{\frac{2}{L}}\min_{n\in[N]}x_{n}^{(1-\frac{2}{L})\frac{2}{L}}&\text{if }L>2,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}(\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1},\beta_{\min}).

Let us now consider the minimization problem (
26
).
The minimum is either attained in the interior of
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{N}
or on the boundary, where at least one coordinate of
\bm{\xi}
is zero. In order to treat both cases simultaneously we fix a set
I\subset[N]
of non-zero coordinates of
\bm{\xi}
(possibly
I=[N]
), set
\xi_{n}=0
for
n\in I
and restrict the minimization to the positive coordinates, i.e., to
\bm{\xi}^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
with
\xi_{n}>0
for all
n\in I
. With
{\bf x}^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I}
defined via
x^{I}_{n}=x_{n}
for
n\in I
, we set

g_{I,{\bf x}^{I}}(\bm{\xi}^{I}):=\begin{cases}\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},\log(\bm{\xi}^{I})-\log({\bf x}^{I})\rangle&\text{if }L=2,\\
-\frac{L}{2}\|\bm{\xi}^{I}\|_{2/L}^{2/L}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

If
\xi_{n}=0
for
n\notin I
and
\xi^{I}_{n}=\xi_{n}
for
n\in I^{c}
, then we have
g_{I,{\bf x}^{I}}(\bm{\xi}^{I})=g_{\bf x}(\bm{\xi})
, so that we consider now the minimization problem

\min_{\bm{\xi}^{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{I},\xi^{I}>0}g_{I,{\bf x}^{I}}(\bm{\xi}^{I})\quad\mbox{ subject to }\begin{cases}\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},{\bf 1}^{I}\rangle=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},({\bf x}^{I})^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle=\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

(27)

In order to solve this optimization problem, we consider the Lagrangian

h_{I}(\bm{\xi}^{I},\lambda)=g_{I,{\bf x}^{I}}(\bm{\xi}^{I})+\lambda\cdot\begin{cases}\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},{\bf 1}^{I}\rangle-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},({\bf x}^{I})^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

(28)

Its gradient is given, for
\bm{\xi}^{I}>0
, by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bm{\xi}^{I}}h_{I}(\bm{\xi}^{I},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}(\lambda+1)\cdot{\bf 1}^{I}+\log(\bm{\xi}^{I})-\log({\bf x}^{I})&\text{if }L=2,\\
\lambda({\bf x}^{I})^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}-(\bm{\xi}^{I})^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}&\text{if }L>2,\\
\end{cases}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\lambda}h(\bm{\xi}^{I},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},{\bf 1}^{I}\rangle-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L=2,\\
\langle\bm{\xi}^{I},({\bf x}^{I})^{\odot\frac{2}{L}-1}\rangle-\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1}&\text{if }L>2.\end{cases}

At a minimum
\bm{\xi}^{I}_{*}
for the problem (
27
) we necessarily have
\nabla h_{I}(\bm{\xi}^{I}_{*},\lambda_{*})=0
for some
\lambda_{*}\in\mathbb{R}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\xi^{I}_{*})_{n}=e^{-\lambda_{*}-1}x^{I}_{n}&\text{if }L=2,\\

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\xi}\geq 0\\

\displaystyle=\tilde{g}(\|{\bf z}\|_{{\bf w},1},\beta_{1}).

Let

Proof.

The proof follows the same steps as the proof of Theorem

.

\displaystyle D_{F^{\pm}}(({\bf z}_{+},\,{\bf z}_{-}),\,(\tilde{{\bf u}}(t),\,\tilde{{\bf v}}(t)))

\displaystyle=F({\bf z}_{+})+F({\bf z}_{-})-F(\tilde{{\bf u}}(t))-F(\tilde{{\bf v}}(t))

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\partial_{t}

5
Summary and Future Directions

In the present work, we considered overparametrized square losses for sparse vector recovery from linear measurements. We showed that in these settings vanilla gradient flow exhibits an implicit bias towards solutions that minimize the
\ell_{1}
-norm among all possible solutions. This lead to near-optimal sampling rates. Several intriguing research questions remain for the future.

First, our theory focuses on gradient flow for minimizing the factorized square loss. However, all conducted experiments use the corresponding gradient descent algorithm instead. A natural next step is thus to extend our results from gradient flow to gradient descent.

Second, we are also interested in low rank matrix/tensor recovery via overparameterization, which works well empirically but is not yet fully understood. Although sparsity and low-rankness are closely related, one of the significant differences between the Hadamard and the matrix product is that the later is non-commutative in general. In fact, many works
[
2
,
8
]
show that low rankness can be deduced if commutativity is assumed. We believe that extending our proof concepts to the non-commutative matrix case might lead to near-optimal matrix sensing results in this more challenging setting.

In this section, we formally show that the dynamics of gradient flow on

Lemma A.1
.

For
L\geq 2
, let
\tilde{\bf x}:=\bigodot_{\ell\in[L]}{\bf x}^{(\ell)}
and
\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}}:=\bigodot_{\ell\in[L]\backslash\{k\}}{\bf x}^{(\ell)}
for
k\in[L]
. Then

\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}\big({\bf x}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(L)}\big)=[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}-{\bf y})]\odot\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}}\;.

(38)

Proof.

By the chain rule we have, for any
n\in[N]
, that

\displaystyle\nabla_{x_{n}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}\big({\bf x}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(L)}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m\in[M]}\nabla_{x_{n}^{(k)}}\left({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}-{\bf y}\right)_{m}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in[M]}({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}-{\bf y})_{m}({\bf A})_{mn}(\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}})_{n}=[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}\tilde{\bf x}-{\bf y})]_{n}(\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}})_{n}\;.

This completes the proof.
∎

Using Lemma
A.1
,
we now show that the dynamics of the factors
{\bf x}^{(k)}
can be simplified if all factors are identically initialized.

Lemma A.2

(Identical Initialization)
.

Suppose
{\bf x}^{(k)}(t)
follows the negative gradient flow

\left({\bf x}^{(k)}\right)^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}\big({\bf x}^{(1)},\dots,{\bf x}^{(L)}\big)\;.

If all initialization vectors are identical, i.e.,
{\bf x}^{(k)}(0)={\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}(0)
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in[L]
, then
{\bf x}^{(k)}(t)={\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}(t)
for all
t\geq 0
and all
k,k^{\prime}\in[L]
. Moreover, with
{\bf x}(t):={\bf x}^{(1)}(t)=\dots={\bf x}^{(L)}(t)
and
\mathcal{L}({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}
the dynamics is given by

{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=-\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bf x})\;.

Proof.

It suffices to show that if
{\bf x}^{(k)}={\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in[L]
, then
\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}=\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
for all
k,k^{\prime}\in[L]
. By Lemma
A.1
, the only dependence of
\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
on
k
is through
\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}}
. Since
{\bf x}^{(k)}
and
{\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}
are identical,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}}

\displaystyle=\bigodot_{\ell\in[L]\backslash\{k\}}{\bf x}^{(\ell)}=\bigodot_{\ell\in[L]\backslash\{k^{\prime}\}}{\bf x}^{(\ell)}={\bf x}_{(k^{\prime})^{c}}

and hence
\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k)}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}=\nabla_{{\bf x}^{(k^{\prime})}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}
. Since by assumption all
{\bf x}^{(k)}
are identical, we can replace them with
{\bf x}
. Hence
\tilde{\bf x}={\bf x}^{\odot L}
and
\tilde{\bf x}_{k^{c}}={\bf x}^{\odot(L-1)}
. Plugging this into (
38
), we get that
{\bf x}^{\prime}(t)=-\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}-{\bf y})\right]\odot{\bf x}^{\odot(L-1)}
, which is exactly
\nabla\mathcal{L}({\bf x})
.
∎

At first sight, the reduction of the number of parameters in Lemma
A.2
may seem counter-intuitive to the idea of overparameterization. However, as opposed to the standard loss function

\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}}({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}
, we arrive at an alternative loss function
\mathcal{L}({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf A}{\bf x}^{\odot L}-{\bf y}\|_{2}^{2}
induced by the overparameterization and having a different optimization landscape than
\mathcal{L}_{\text{quad}}
.

Along similar lines it is straight-forward to check that the gradient flow of the general overparameterized loss function
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
in (
5
) is equivalent to the flow of the reduced factorized loss
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
in (
7
) if all
{\bf u}^{(k)}
and
{\bf v}^{(k)}
are initialized identically.
Note that the partial gradients of
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\bf u}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}({\bf u},{\bf v})

\displaystyle=L\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}({\bf u}^{\odot L}-{\bf v}^{\odot L})-{\bf y})\right]\odot{\bf u}^{\odot L-1}

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{\bf v}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}({\bf u},{\bf v})

\displaystyle=-L\left[{\bf A}^{\text{T}}({\bf A}({\bf u}^{\odot L}-{\bf v}^{\odot L})-{\bf y})\right]\odot{\bf v}^{\odot L-1}.

Noise robust compressed sensing

For completeness we provide a proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

The
\ell_{2}
-robust null space property of order
cs_{*}
with respect to
\ell_{2}
with constants
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\tau
as in (
11
) together with the
\ell_{1}
-quotient property implies the so-called simultaneous
(\ell_{2},\ell_{1})
-quotient property by
[
10
, Lemma 11.15]
, i.e., for any
{\bf e}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
there exists
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
such that
{\bf A}{\bf e}={\bf u}
and both

\|{\bf u}\|_{2}\leq d^{\prime}\|{\bf e}\|_{2}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|{\bf u}\|_{1}\leq d\sqrt{s_{*}}\|{\bf e}\|_{2},

(39)

where the constant
d^{\prime}
only depends on
d,c,\rho,\tau
. Let
\Delta:\mathbb{R}^{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
the reconstruction map represented by gradient flow, i.e.,
\Delta({\bf y}^{\prime})
is the limit of gradient flow (initialized with parameter
{\bf u}_{0},{\bf v}_{0}
) for the functional
\mathcal{L}_{\text{over}}^{\pm}
, the latter depending on
{\bf y}^{\prime}
.
We know from Theorem
1.1
that
{\bf A}(\Delta({\bf y}))={\bf y}
for any
{\bf y}
(the limit
\tilde{\bf x}_{\infty}
is contained in
S
).
By
[
10
, Theorem 4.25]
the
\ell_{2}
-robust null space property of
{\bf A}
implies
that

\|{\bf v}-{\bf w}\|_{2}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\|{\bf v}\|_{1}-\|{\bf w}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf w})_{1}\right)+D\|{\bf A}({\bf v}-{\bf w})\|_{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }{\bf v},{\bf w}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(40)

For the noise vector
{\bf e}
of the theorem, let
{\bf u}
be the vector such that
{\bf A}{\bf u}={\bf e}
and (
39
) holds. Note that
{\bf y}={\bf A}{\bf x}_{*}+{\bf e}={\bf A}({\bf x}_{+}+{\bf u})
. Further let,
\widehat{{\bf z}}
be a minimizer of
\min_{{\bf z}:{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf A}({\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u})}\|{\bf z}\|_{1}

1.1
we have

\|{\bf w}\|_{1}-\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}.

(41)

Applying inequality (
40
) for
{\bf v}={\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}
and
{\bf w}=\Delta({\bf A}({\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}))
below (noting that
{\bf A}{\bf v}={\bf A}{\bf w}
as argued above) yields

\displaystyle\|{\bf x}_{*}-\Delta({\bf y})\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|{\bf x}_{*}-\Delta({\bf A}({\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u})\|_{2}\leq\|{\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}-\Delta({\bf A}({\bf x}_{+}+{\bf u}))\|_{2}+\|{\bf u}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\|{\bf w}\|_{1}-\|{\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u})_{1}\right)+\|{\bf u}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\|{\bf w}\|_{1}-\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}+\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}-\|{\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf x}_{*})_{1}+\|{\bf u}\|_{1}\right)+\|{\bf u}\|_{2}.

Since
\widehat{{\bf z}}
minimizes the
\ell_{1}
-norm among all vectors satisfying
{\bf A}{\bf z}={\bf A}({\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u})

\|{\bf x}_{*}+{\bf u}\|_{1}\geq\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}
. Using (
39
) and (
41
)
and that
s=cs_{*}
gives

\displaystyle\|{\bf x}_{*}-\Delta({\bf y})\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\varepsilon\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf x}_{*})_{1}\right)+\frac{Cd\sqrt{s_{*}}}{\sqrt{s}}\|{\bf e}\|_{2}+d^{\prime}\|{\bf e}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{\sqrt{s}}\left(\varepsilon\|\widehat{{\bf z}}\|_{1}+2\sigma_{s}({\bf x}_{*})_{1}\right)+C^{\prime}\|{\bf e}\|_{2}

with
C^{\prime}=Cd\sqrt{s_{*}}/\sqrt{s}+d^{\prime}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

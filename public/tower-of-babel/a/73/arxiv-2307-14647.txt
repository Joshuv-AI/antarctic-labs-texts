Stiefel-Whitney Classes of representations of Dihedral Groups
Sujeet Bhalerao, Rohit Joshi, Neha Malik

Abstract
. We compute the Stiefel-Whitney Classes for representations of dihedral groups D m D_{m} in terms of character values of order two elements. We also provide criteria to identify representations V V which lift to the double covers of the orthogonal group O ⁡ ( V ) \Or(V) and those with non-trivial mod 2 2 Euler class.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group, and
\pi
be an orthogonal representation of
G
. To
\pi
, one can associate cohomology classes

w_{i}(\pi)
, living in
H^{i}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, called
Stiefel-Whitney Classes
(SWCs) of
\pi
. Their sum

w(\pi)=w_{0}(\pi)+w_{1}(\pi)+\ldots

is known as the
total SWC
of
\pi
. These classes are of importance in studying group cohomology through the representations of the group. We refer to
[
GKT89
]
,
[
MS23
, Section 2.3]
for more details.

The paper
[
GS20
]
of Ganguly and Spallone computed the second SWC to characterize spinorial representations of symmetric groups. This led to a program of calculating the total SWCs of representations in terms of character values for various groups. Joshi-Ganguly completed the case of
\GL(n,q)
for
q
odd in
[
GJ23
]
. The SWCs for
\SL(2,q)
were computed in
[
MS23
]
by Malik-Spallone. In this paper, we describe SWCs of representations of dihedral groups in terms of character values. This is the first step in the calculation of SWCs for
\Or(n,q)
, since
\Or(2,q)
is a dihedral group when
q
is odd. An important tool in our calculation is the cohomological
detection
of a group by its subgroups.

We write
H^{*}(G)
for
H^{*}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}).
The subgroups
K_{1},K_{2}
of
G
are said to detect the mod
2
cohomology of
G
if the restriction map

H^{*}(G)\to H^{*}(K_{1})\oplus H^{*}(K_{2})

is an injection. For instance, it is well known
[
AM04
, Chapter II, Corollary 5.2]
that a Sylow
2
-subgroup
K
detects the mod
2
cohomology of
G
, meaning the restriction
H^{*}(G)
to
H^{*}(K)
is injective.

Write
C_{n}
for the cyclic group of order
n
. Let
D_{m}
be the dihedral group of order
2m
with
`r
’ the rotation by angle
2\pi/m
and a reflection
`s
’ as its generators. All representations of
D_{m}
are orthogonal. The calculation of SWCs for abelian dihedral groups
D_{1}
,
D_{2}
is straightforward. We review them in Section
3
. When
m
is a multiple of
4
, from
[
Sna13
]
, the mod
2
cohomology of
D_{m}
is

H^{*}(D_{m},\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})=\frac{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[x,y,w]}{(y^{2}+xy)},

where
x,y
are certain elements of degree
1
and
w
is of degree
2
. In this case, the detection of
H^{*}(D_{m})
by two Klein-
4
groups is known (see
[
FP06
, Proposition 3.3]
for instance or Section
4.1
below). We use this detection to get:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
m
be a multiple of
4
, and
G=D_{m}
. Let
\pi
be a representation of
G
. Then,

w(\pi)=(1+y)^{a_{\pi}}(1+x+y)^{b_{\pi}}(1+x+w)^{c_{\pi}},

where

\displaystyle a_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\chi_{\pi}(1)-2\chi_{\pi}(rs)+\chi_{\pi}(r_{c})\big),

\displaystyle b_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\chi_{\pi}(1)-2\chi_{\pi}(s)+\chi_{\pi}(r_{c})\big),\text{ and }

\displaystyle c_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\big(\chi_{\pi}(1)-\chi_{\pi}(r_{c})\big).

Here
r_{c}=r^{m/2}
, and
\chi_{\pi}(g)
is the character value of the representation
\pi
at
g\in G
.

There are several corollaries of Theorem
1.1
. When
m
is a multiple of
4
, we first have:

Corollary 1.2
.

A representation
\pi
of
D_{m}
is trivial if and only if
w(\pi)=1
.

Let
W(G)
be the subgroup of
complete
cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(G)
, generated by the total SWCs of orthogonal representations of
G
(see
[
MS23
, Section 2.6]
). For
G=D_{m}
, we obtain:

Corollary 1.3
.

The group
W(D_{m})
is free abelian with generators
1+y,1+x+y,1+x+w.

For a representation
\pi
of degree
d
, we define the top SWC
w_{\too}(\pi):=w_{d}(\pi)
. When
\det\pi=1
, there is another relevant characteristic class
e(\pi)\in H^{d}(G,\mathbb{Z})
, called the
Euler class
of
\pi
. From
[
MS16
, Property 9.5]
,
w_{\too}(\pi)
is the reduction of
e(\pi)
mod
2
. Here, we describe
\pi
with
w_{\too}(\pi)\neq 0
for
m=0\pmod{4}
:

Corollary 1.4
.

The top SWC of
\pi
is non-zero iff either
\ell_{\pi}=0
or
m_{\pi}=0
where

\displaystyle\ell_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\pi}(1)+2\chi_{\pi}(s)+\chi_{\pi}(r_{c}),

\displaystyle m_{\pi}

\displaystyle=\chi_{\pi}(1)+2\chi_{\pi}(sr_{c})+\chi_{\pi}(r_{c}).

For a complex vector space
V
, there are four double covers of the orthogonal group
\Or(V)
, namely
\Or(V)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
\widetilde{\Or}(V)
,
\Pin^{+}(V)
and
\Pin^{-}(V)
(see
[
JS23
, Section 12]
for instance). We say an orthogonal representation
\pi
of a group
G
is
spinorial
provided it lifts to
\Pin^{-}(V)
, as in
[
JS21
]
. There is a cohomological criterion to see when
\pi
lifts to the extensions above. For instance,
\pi
always lifts to
\Or(V)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. We review this in Section
2.3
.

When
4
divides
m
, we obtain the following lifting criteria for the representations of
D_{m}
in terms of character values:

Corollary 1.5
.

A representation
(\pi,V)
of
D_{m}
lifts to:

(i)

\widetilde{\Or}(V)
iff
a_{\pi}=b_{\pi}=c_{\pi}\pmod{2},

(ii)

\Pin^{+}(V)
iff
c_{\pi}
is even and

\binom{a_{\pi}}{2}=\binom{b_{\pi}}{2}=\binom{c_{\pi}}{2}\pmod{2},

(iii)

\Pin^{-}(V)
iff
c_{\pi}
is even and

\binom{a_{\pi}+1}{2}=\binom{b_{\pi}+1}{2}=\binom{c_{\pi}+1}{2}\pmod{2}.

We also have such results for dihedral groups
D_{m}
when
m
is odd or
m=2\pmod{4}
, proved in later sections.

Let
G,G^{\prime}
be finite groups. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be orthogonal representations of
G
and
G^{\prime}
respectively with
\deg\pi=d
,
\deg\pi^{\prime}=d^{\prime}
. Then
\Pi=\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime}
, the external tensor product, is an orthogonal representation of
G\times G^{\prime}
. We have:

Theorem 1.6
.

The representation
\Pi
is spinorial if and only if both of the following hold:

(i)

The restriction of
\Pi
to each of
G\times 1
and
1\times G^{\prime}
is spinorial, and

(ii)

(dd^{\prime}+1)
is even or
\det\pi=1
or
\det\pi^{\prime}=1
.

When
G=D_{m}
and
G^{\prime}=D_{m^{\prime}}
, it leads to:

Corollary 1.7
.

Suppose
m,m^{\prime}
both are multiples of
4
. All non-trivial irreducible representations of
D_{m}\times D_{m^{\prime}}
are aspinorial.

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we set up notation and review the group cohomology of
D_{m}
. In section
3
, we review the calculation of SWCs for
C_{2}
and obtain SWCs for representations of
C_{2}\times C_{2}
. In section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.1
and its Corollaries
1.2
,
1.3
. Sections
5
,
6
are dedicated to proving Corollaries
1.4
,
1.5
along with similar results for
D_{m}
when
m
is odd or
m=2\pmod{4}
. In the last section, we give a general spinoriality result for the products of groups, leading to the proofs of Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.7
.

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

Let
D_{m}
be the dihedral group of order
2m
with presentation

D_{m}=\langle r,s\mid r^{m}=1,s^{2}=1,rs=sr^{-1}\rangle.

We now review the representation theory of
D_{m}
(see
[
Ser77
, Section 5.3]
for instance).

2.1.
Irreducible Representations of
D_{m}

Write
`\sgn
’ for the non-trivial linear character of
D_{1}
.

For
m
even, the group
D_{m}
has four linear characters, namely
1
,
\chi_{s}
,
\chi_{r}
,
\chi_{rs}
:

\displaystyle 1:(r,s)

\displaystyle\mapsto(1,1)

\displaystyle\chi_{s}:(r,s)

\displaystyle\mapsto(1,-1)

\displaystyle\chi_{r}:(r,s)

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1,1)

\displaystyle\chi_{rs}:(r,s)

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1,-1).

Set
\theta_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{m}
.
There are also irreducible
2
-dimensional representations enumerated by
k=1,2,\ldots,m/2-1
:

\sigma_{k}:D_{m}\to\GL(2,\mathbb{C})

is given by

\sigma_{k}(r)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta_{k}&&-\sin\theta_{k}\\[5.69054pt]
\sin\theta_{k}&&\cos\theta_{k}\end{pmatrix}\quad,\quad\sigma_{k}(s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&&1\\
1&&0\end{pmatrix}.

Write
\sigma=\sigma_{1}
; this is the standard
2
-dimensional representation of
D_{m}
.

When
m
is odd,
D_{m}
has two linear characters
1
, and
\chi_{s}
. Also, the representations
\sigma_{k}
for
k=1,\ldots,(m-1)/2
defined above are irreducible.

Note that all the above representations are orthogonal.

2.2.
Restriction to a Sylow
2
-Subgroup

Set
\chi_{0}=1
. In this section, we write
\chi_{i,m}
for the linear characters
\chi_{i}
and
\sigma_{k,m}
for the representations
\sigma_{k}
of the group
D_{m}
.

Assume
m=2^{n}l
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
l
odd.
D_{2^{n}}
, with usual inclusion, is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
D_{m}
. It is known
[
Sna13
, Chapter 1, Section 4]
that

(1)

H^{*}(D_{2^{n}})=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v]&n=0,\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},v_{2}]&n=1,\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[x,y,w]/(y^{2}+xy)&n\geq 2.\end{cases}

where
v=w_{1}(\chi_{s,1})
,
v_{1}=w_{1}(\chi_{r,2})
,
v_{2}=w_{1}(\chi_{s,2})
, and
x=w_{1}(\chi_{s,2^{n}}),

y=w_{1}(\chi_{r,2^{n}})
and
w=w_{2}(\sigma_{1,2^{n}})
for
n\geq 2
.

Consider the inclusion
\iota:D_{2^{n}}\to D_{m}
. Then, the restriction map

(2)

\iota^{*}:H^{*}(D_{m})\to H^{*}(D_{2^{n}})

is an isomorphism
[
Sna13
, Theorem 4.6]
. To understand
\iota^{*}
, the restrictions of representations of
D_{m}
to
D_{2^{n}}
are of interest. Below we define elements of
H^{*}(D_{m})
which map to the generators of
H^{*}(D_{2^{n}})
under
\iota^{*}
.
This is done in three cases.

For
m
odd, the linear character
\chi_{s,m}
restricted to
D_{1}
is the
\sgn
representation. Then, the functoriality of SWCs gives

\displaystyle\iota^{*}(w_{1}(\chi_{s,m}))

\displaystyle=w_{1}(\sgn)

\displaystyle=v.

Without ambiguity, we simply write

(3)

w_{1}(\chi_{s,m})=v.

Suppose now that
m=2\pmod{4}
. The restriction of
\chi_{i,m}
to
D_{2}
is
\chi_{i,2}
for each
i=0,r,s,rs
. Therefore,

\iota^{*}(w_{1}(\chi_{r,m}))=v_{1},
and

\iota^{*}(w_{1}(\chi_{s,m}))=v_{2}

and we write

(4)

\begin{split}w_{1}(\chi_{r,m})&=v_{1},\text{ and }\\
w_{1}(\chi_{s,m})&=v_{2}.\end{split}

Finally, suppose
m=0\pmod{4}
. In this case,
\chi_{i,m}
restricts to
\chi_{i,2^{n}}
and
\sigma_{1,m}
restricts to
\sigma_{1,2^{n}}
. Again, since
\iota^{*}
is an isomorphism, we write

(5)

\begin{split}w_{1}(\chi_{s,m})&=x\\
w_{1}(\chi_{r,m})&=y\\
w_{1}(\sigma_{1,m})&=w.\end{split}

With notation given by (
3
),(
4
) and (
5
), an equality
similar to (
1
) holds for
H^{*}(D_{m})
. Henceforth, we use the same set of notations for the generators of group cohomology of
D_{m}
and
D_{2^{n}}
.

2.3.
Extensions of
\Or(V)

Consider an orthogonal representation
\pi:G\to\Or(V)
. Here,
V
is a finite-dimensional (complex) vector space, with a nondegenerate quadratic form
Q
. Denote a classifying space of
\Or(V)
by BO
(V)
. An explicit one-to-one correspondence of the elements of
H^{2}(
BO
(V),\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
with the extensions of
\Or(V)
by
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is known (see, for instance,
[
JS23
]
). There are four mutually inequivalent such extensions of
\Or(V)
, namely
\Or(V)\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
,
\widetilde{\Or}(V)
,
\Pin^{+}(V)
and
\Pin^{-}(V)
.

The first one is easy to describe. For
\widetilde{\Or}(V)
, consider the determinant map from
\Or(V)
, which surjects onto
C_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
. Of course, squaring the cyclic group
C_{4}<\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, generated by the imaginary unit
i
, gives a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
-cover of
C_{2}
.
Write
\widetilde{\Or}(V)\to\Or(V)
for the pullback:

\textstyle{\widetilde{\Or}(V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{4}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{z\mapsto z^{2}}
\textstyle{\Or(V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\det}
\textstyle{C_{2}}

Thus
\widetilde{\Or}(V)
is the subgroup of pairs
(g,z)\in\Or(V)\times C_{4}
with
\det g=z^{2}
.

For a vector space
(V,Q)
, the Clifford algebra
C(V)
is the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)
by the two-sided ideal generated by the set

\{v\otimes v-Q(v):v\in V\}.

It contains
V
as a subspace. Write
C(V)^{\times}
for the group of invertible elements of
C(V)
. We say
u\in V
is a
unit vector
if
Q(u)=1
, and an
antiunit vector
, if
Q(u)=-1
.

Write
\Pin^{-}(V)
for the subgroup of
C(V)^{\times}
generated by the antiunit vectors in
V\subset C(V)
, and

\Pin^{+}(V)
for the subgroup generated by the unit vectors. This group
\Pin^{-}(V)
agrees with the one described in
[
FH91
, Chapter 20]
, and
\Pin^{+}(V)
with
[
Frö85
, Appendix 1]
.

Moreover, the following cohomological criterion is known (see
[
JS23
, Corollary 12.2.1]
for instance): The representation
(\pi,V)
lifts to:

(1)

\widetilde{\Or}(V)
iff
w_{1}(\pi)^{2}=0
,

(2)

\Pin^{+}(V)
iff
w_{2}(\pi)=0
, and

(3)

\Pin^{-}(V)
iff
w_{2}(\pi)+w_{1}(\pi)^{2}=0
.

3.
The case of
C_{2}
and
C_{2}\times C_{2}

Let
C_{2}=\{\pm 1\}
. Then,
D_{1}
is simply
C_{2}
and
D_{2}
is the Klein-
4
group
C_{2}\times C_{2}
. Below are the formulas for their SWCs.

3.1.
The Cyclic group
C_{2}

Let
\pi
be a representation of
C_{2}
. The total SWC of
\pi
is known (see, for instance, in
[
MS23
, Lemma 2.5]
). With
v=w_{1}(\sgn)
, we have

(6)

w(\pi)=(1+v)^{t_{\pi}},

where
t_{\pi}=\frac{1}{2}(\chi_{\pi}(1)-\chi_{\pi}(-1))
.

3.2.
The Klein-
4
Group

Consider the group
C_{2}\times C_{2}
and the projection maps
\pr_{i}:C_{2}\times C_{2}\to C_{2}
for
i=1,2
.
By Künneth, we have

H^{*}(C_{2}\times C_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},v_{2}],

where
v_{1}=\pr^{*}_{1}(v)=w_{1}(\sgn\boxtimes 1)
and
v_{2}=\pr^{*}_{2}(v)=w_{1}(1\boxtimes\sgn)
. Here
\boxtimes
denotes the external tensor product.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
C_{2}\times C_{2}
. The total SWC of
\pi
is

w(\pi)=(1+v_{1})^{b_{1}}(1+v_{2})^{b_{2}}(1+v_{1}+v_{2})^{b_{3}},

where

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(-1,1)+\chi_{\pi}(1,-1)-\chi_{\pi}(-1,-1)\right)

\displaystyle b_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\deg\pi+\chi_{\pi}(-1,1)-\chi_{\pi}(1,-1)-\chi_{\pi}(-1,-1)\right)

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\deg\pi-\chi_{\pi}(-1,1)-\chi_{\pi}(1,-1)+\chi_{\pi}(-1,-1)\right).

Proof.

Any representation
\pi
of
C_{2}\times C_{2}
has the form

\pi=b_{0}1\oplus b_{1}(\sgn\boxtimes 1)\oplus b_{2}(1\boxtimes\sgn)\oplus b_{3}(\sgn\boxtimes\sgn).

Note all representations of
G
are orthogonal.
By multiplicativity of SWCs, we have

w(\pi)=(1+v_{1})^{b_{1}}(1+v_{2})^{b_{2}}(1+v_{1}+v_{2})^{b_{3}}.

To express
b_{i}
in terms of character values, we use the following equations:

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(1,1)

\displaystyle=b_{0}+b_{1}+b_{2}+b_{3}

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(-1,1)

\displaystyle=b_{0}-b_{1}+b_{2}-b_{3}

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(1,-1)

\displaystyle=b_{0}+b_{1}-b_{2}-b_{3}

\displaystyle\chi_{\pi}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle=b_{0}-b_{1}-b_{2}+b_{3}.

Solving these for
b_{i}
completes the proof.

4.
Main Results

Recall
D_{2^{n}}
sits in

under the inclusion
\iota

and

The total SWC for

4.1.
Detection

In this section, suppose
m
is a multiple of
4
, and
G=D_{m}
.

We consider the following subgroups of
G
:

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=\{1,s,r_{c},sr_{c}\},

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=\{1,rs,r_{c},rsr_{c}\}.

Both
E_{1},E_{2}
are isomorphic to the Klein
4
-group. Let
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}
be the linear characters of
E_{1}
given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}:(s,r_{c})

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1,1)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}:(s,r_{c})

\displaystyle\mapsto(1,-1).

Then,
H^{*}(E_{1})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},v_{2}],
with
v_{1}=w_{1}(\alpha_{1}),

v_{2}=w_{1}(\beta_{1})
.
Similarly, we consider the linear characters
\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}
of
E_{2}
defined by,

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}:(rs,r_{c})

\displaystyle\mapsto(-1,1)

\displaystyle\beta_{2}:(rs,r_{c})

\displaystyle\mapsto(1,-1)

such that
H^{*}(E_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[u_{1},u_{2}]
with
u_{1}=w_{1}(\alpha_{2})
,
u_{2}=w_{1}(\beta_{2})
.

The following detection can be found in
[
FP06
, Proposition 3.3, Page 322]
. Although the result itself is accurate, an error is found within the accompanying proof provided in this book. We rectify the issue in the proof below.

Proposition 4.1
.

When
m
is a multiple of
4
, the subgroups
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
together detect the mod
2
cohomology of
D_{m}
.

Proving this requires a lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
`d
’ be a non-negative integer, and
\mathcal{P}_{d}=\{(i,j):i,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\;i+2j=d\}
. Then, the set
S_{d}=\{a^{i}b^{j}(a+b)^{j}:(i,j)\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\}
in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[a,b]
is linearly independent.

Proof.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{d}
can be enumerated as follows:

(d,0),(d-2,1),\ldots,(d-2k,k),\ldots,\begin{cases}(0,d/2),&\quad\text{ when }d\text{ is even}\\
(1,(d-1)/2),&\quad\text{ when }d\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

For a polynomial
p\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}[a,b]
, let
\deg_{a}(p)
be the highest degree of
`a
’ appearing in

.

(3)

The representation
2\chi_{s}=\chi_{s}\oplus\chi_{s}
lifts to
\widetilde{\Or}(V)

7.
Spinoriality for Products of Dihedral Groups

Let
G,G^{\prime}
be finite groups. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}
be representations of
G
and
G^{\prime}
respectively with
\deg\pi=d
,
\deg\pi^{\prime}=d^{\prime}
. Then
\Pi=\pi\boxtimes\pi^{\prime}
, the external tensor product of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
, is an orthogonal representation of
G\times G^{\prime}
. From
[
GS20
, Section 7.1]
, such a representation is spinorial if and only if the following elements in
H^{2}(G\times G^{\prime})
vanish:

(1)

d^{\prime}w_{2}(\pi)+\binom{d^{\prime}+1}{2}w_{1}(\pi)\cup w_{1}(\pi)
,

(2)

(dd^{\prime}+1)w_{1}(\pi)\otimes w_{1}(\pi^{\prime})
,

(3)

dw_{2}(\pi^{\prime})+\binom{d+1}{2}w_{1}(\pi^{\prime})\cup w_{1}(\pi^{\prime})
.

Lemma 7.1
.

The condition (
1
) above is equivalent to the restriction of
\Pi
to
G\times 1
being spinorial.

Proof.

We have the restriction
\Pi|_{G\times 1}=d^{\prime}\pi
, and its total SWC is

\displaystyle w(d^{\prime}\pi)

\displaystyle=w(\pi)^{d^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(1+w_{1}(\pi)+w_{2}(\pi)+\ldots)^{d^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=1+d^{\prime}w_{1}(\pi)+d^{\prime}w_{2}(\pi)+\binom{d^{\prime}}{2}w_{1}(\pi)\cup w_{1}(\pi)+\ldots

Now,
d^{\prime}\pi
is spinorial if and only if

d^{\prime}w_{2}(\pi)+\left(\binom{d^{\prime}}{2}+d^{\prime 2}\right)w_{1}(\pi)\cup w_{1}(\pi)\in H^{2}(G)

vanishes. Moreover,

\binom{d^{\prime}}{2}+d^{\prime 2}=_{2}\binom{d^{\prime}}{2}+d^{\prime}=_{2}\binom{d^{\prime}+1}{2},

completing the proof.

∎

Similarly, condition (
3
) is same as the spinoriality of
\Pi|_{1\times G^{\prime}}.
Thus, the conditions (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) along with Lemma
7.1
give Theorem
1.6
. A straightforward calculation with
G=D_{m}
and
G^{\prime}=D_{m^{\prime}}
yields a proof of Corollary
1.7
.

Example 7.2
.

Suppose
m,m^{\prime}
both are multiples of
4
.
Let
k
be even, and consider the representation of
D_{m}\times D_{m^{\prime}}
given by

\Pi=(\sigma_{k}\oplus\sigma_{k})\boxtimes(\sigma_{k}\oplus\sigma_{k}).

Applying Theorem
1.6
shows this representation is spinorial.

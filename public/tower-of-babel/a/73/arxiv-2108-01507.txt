Numerical analysis for a Cahn–Hilliard system modelling tumour growth with chemotaxis and active transport


Abstract
In this work, we consider a diffuse interface model for tumour growth in the presence of a nutrient which is consumed by the tumour. The system of equations consists of a Cahn–Hilliard equation with source terms for the tumour cells and a reaction-diffusion equation for the nutrient. We introduce a fully-discrete finite element approximation of the model and prove stability bounds for the discrete scheme. Moreover, we show that discrete solutions exist and depend continuously on the initial and boundary data. We then pass to the limit in the discretization parameters and prove convergence to a global-in-time weak solution to the model. Under additional assumptions, this weak solution is unique. Finally, we present some numerical results including numerical error investigation in one spatial dimension and some long time simulations in two and three spatial dimensions. Keywords: Cahn-Hilliard equation, diffuse interface model, tumour growth, chemotaxis, numerical analysis, finite element method, simulations. MSC Classification: 65M12, 65M22, 35Q92, 92B05

+1
in the tumour “phase” and
-1
in the healthy “phase”. These models go back to Cristini, Lowengrub and co-authors, see
[
18
,
29
,
55
]
and have been further developed by many authors, see, e.g.,
[
16
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
.

Here, the Cahn–Hilliard equation, which is a partial differential equation of fourth order, often plays an important role. The numerical approximation of Cahn–Hilliard systems is often made with finite element methods that are based on the works
[
23
,
24
]
and have found many applications in physics
[
8
,

]
.
In the past years, also numerical methods for tumour growth models have been proposed
[
17
,
21
]
but the numerical analysis such as stability or convergence analysis is often missing.

The biological effect of our main interest is the process of chemotaxis which describes the movement of tumour cells towards regions with a higher concentration of an extracellular chemical species, which can favour an instable tumour growth and lead to invasion
[
49
]
.
For chemotaxis models, often the Patlak–Keller–Segel system is proposed in the literature which is composed of a system of partial differential equations of second order, see
[
43
]
.
Here, numerical approximations are often based on finite volume or finite element methods which have been studied in, e.g.,
[

,
25
,

,
41
,
46
,
50
,

,
53
,

]
.
For a good overview about the most relavant works about Keller–Segel systems, we also refer the reader to the review
[

]
.

In this work, we study a phase-field model for tumour growth with chemotaxis and active transport from the numerical point of view.
In particular, we introduce and study a fully-discrete finite element approach, for which we establish stability, existence, continuous dependence and convergence results and present numerical examples to illustrate the practicability of the method.
The mathematical model of our interest was originally introduced in
[
37
]
and analyzed in
[
33
]
and consists of a Cahn–Hilliard
equation with source terms and a parabolic reaction-diffusion equation for a nutrient species, given by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}\big({m(\varphi)\nabla\mu}\big)+\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=A\psi^{\prime}(\varphi)-B\triangle\varphi-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\sigma

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}\big({n(\varphi)(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi)}\big)-\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

(1.1c)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\nabla\varphi\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}=\nabla\mu\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}

\displaystyle\text{ on }{\partial\Omega}\times(0,T),

(1.1d)

\displaystyle n(\varphi)\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}

\displaystyle=K(\sigma_{\infty}-\sigma)

\displaystyle\text{ on }{\partial\Omega}\times(0,T),

(1.1e)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, is a bounded domain with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
and outer unit normal
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}
.
Here, the phase field variable
\varphi\in[-1,1]
denotes the difference in volume fractions, with
\{\varphi=1\}
describing unmixed tumour tissue, and
\{\varphi=-1\}
representing the surrounding healthy tissue. By
\mu
, we denote the chemical potential for
\varphi
. Furthermore,
\sigma\geq 0
denotes the concentration of an unspecified chemical species (like oxygen or glucose) that serves as a nutrient for the tumour. Moreover,
\sigma_{\infty}\geq 0
and
K\geq 0
denote a given nutrient supply on the boundary and a permeability constant, respectively.
The source and sink term
\Gamma_{\varphi}
in the phase field equation (
1.1a
) may describe proliferation or apoptosis of the tumour cells, while
\Gamma_{\sigma}
in the nutrient equation (
1.1c
) models effects like nutrient consumption or a nutrient supply from an existing vasculature.
The positive parameter
\chi_{\sigma}>0
models the diffusivity of the nutrient and the non-negative parameter
\chi_{\varphi}\geq 0
refers to transport mechanisms such as chemotaxis and active uptake.
In the system (
1.1
),
m(\varphi)
and
n(\varphi)
denote positive mobilities for
\varphi
and
\sigma
, respectively.

\psi(\cdot)
is a non-negative potential with two equal minima at
\pm 1
.
The positive parameters
A
and
B
are constant with the typical choice
A=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}
,
B=\beta\epsilon
,
where
\beta>0
denotes the surface tension and
\epsilon>0
is a small parameter related to the interfacial thickness.

The above system is based on the well-known Ginzburg–Landau energy density

\displaystyle f(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)=A\psi(\varphi)+\frac{B}{2}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2},

(1.2)

which relates to interfacial energy and unmixing tendencies, and a nutrient energy density

\displaystyle N(\varphi,\sigma)=\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{2}\lvert\sigma\rvert^{2}+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma(1-\varphi),

(1.3)

where the second term describes an interaction between the nutrient and the cells
[
37
]
.
Analogously to
[
33
]
, the following formal energy identity is satisfied:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}\int_{\Omega}\Big(f(\varphi,\nabla\varphi)+N(\varphi,\sigma)\Big)\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}m(\varphi)\lvert\nabla\mu\rvert^{2}+n(\varphi)\lvert\nabla N_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)\rvert^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}N_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)-\mu\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}KN_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)(\sigma-\sigma_{\infty})\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=0,\end{split}

(1.4)

where
N_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)
denotes the partial derivative of
N(\varphi,\sigma)
with respect to
\sigma
.

The main obstacles, which the authors of
[
33
]
had to face in order to derive useful
a priori
estimates based on the energy identity (
1.4
), arise from the nutrient energy density, where the term
\sigma(1-\varphi)
may become negative, and the presence of source terms
N_{,\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)-\mu\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
, which may contain nonlinearities like, e.g., triple products.
However, under some appropriate assumptions, the authors of
[
33
]
established to prove well-posedness of weak solutions of the system (
1.1
).

In this work, we use the
a priori
estimates of
[
33
]
to analyze a discretization of the system (
1.1
).
The paper is organized as follows. At first, we discretize the system (
1.1
) with a practical fully-discrete finite element approximation, where all terms except of the nonlinear mobility functions in (
1.1a
) and (
1.1c
) are treated implicitly. Moreover, we make use of a convex-concave splitting of the double-well potential
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
with
\psi_{1}
convex and
\psi_{2}
concave, where
\psi_{1}^{\prime}
is treated implicitly and
\psi_{2}^{\prime}
explicitly.
After that, we derive stability estimates and prove existence of discrete solutions supposed that the time step size satisfies a minor constraint.
Moreover, the discrete solutions depend continuously on the initial and boundary data if the mobility functions are constant and if the time step size is small enough.
In comparison to the classical Cahn–Hilliard equation
[
5
,
23
]
where e.g. mass conservation of the order parameter
\varphi
is used, we have to use different techniques as we have to face the difficulties that arise from the non-positive nutrient energy, the additional source terms and the coupling to the reaction-diffusion equation.
Then, we establish higher order bounds for the discrete solutions before passing with the discretization parameters to zero.

In particular, we successfully prove that subsequences of the discrete solutions convergence to a weak solution of the system (
1.1
) which is unique under additional assumptions.
Finally, we present some numerical results including numerical error investigation in one spatial dimension and some long time simulations in two and three spatial dimensions which highlight the practicability of our discrete scheme.

Fully discrete finite element approximation

We split the time interval
[0,T)
into intervals
[t^{n-1},t^{n})

with
\Delta t_{n}=t^{n}-t^{n-1}
,

. For simplicity we assume that
\Delta t_{n}=\Delta t
for a
\Delta t>0
and all

.
Moreover, we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\partial\Omega
.
We require
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
to be a regular family of conform quasiuniform triangulations with mesh parameter

consists only of non-obtuse simplices.
For a given partitioning of meshes
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote the simplices by

with

and its vertices
\{P_{i}^{k}\}_{i=0}^{d}
.
The set of all the vertices of

is denoted by

.
For more details on finite elements, we refer to
[
9
]
.

In this work, we use the standard notation from, e.g.,
[

,
26
]
.
We denote the Euclidean norm by

. For a Banach space

, we denote the dual space by

.
For

and an integer

, we write
L^{p}\coloneqq L^{p}(\Omega)
,
W^{m,p}\coloneqq W^{m,p}(\Omega)
and
H^{m}\coloneqq H^{m}(\Omega)\coloneqq W^{m,2}(\Omega)
, where
W^{0,p}\coloneqq L^{p}
in the case

.
The norms and seminorms are denoted by
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{W^{m,p}}
and
\lvert\cdot\rvert_{W^{m,p}}
, respectively, and similarly for the spaces

and
H^{m}
.
We denote the inner product of the spaces

and

by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}
, respectively.
For

For a Banach space

We denote the finite element space of continuous and piecewise linear functions by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{q_{h}\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\ q_{h}|_{K_{k}}\in\mathcal{P}_{1},\ k=1,...,N_{k}\}

\displaystyle\subset H^{1}(\Omega).

Moreover, we denote the nodal interpolation operator by

\displaystyle(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})_{h}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\quad\quad

\displaystyle\lVert\cdot\rVert_{h}\coloneqq\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)_{h}},

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\eta_{3},\eta_{4})_{h,\partial\Omega}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\eta_{3}\eta_{4}\big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1},\quad\quad

\displaystyle\lVert\cdot\rVert_{h,\partial\Omega}\coloneqq\sqrt{(\cdot,\cdot)_{h,\partial\Omega}}.

Let the initial values fulfill

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega;[-1,1])\text{ with }\nabla\varphi_{0}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}\in H^{1}(\Omega),

where

denotes the outer unit normal on

We approximate the intial data by

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{h}\varphi_{0},\quad\sigma_{h}^{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}_{h}^{Cl}\sigma_{0}.

(2.11)

Hence, it follows from (

),

, that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\psi(\varphi_{h}^{0})\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{0}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{0}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{0}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}

where the discrete Neumann-Laplacian

is defined by

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\Delta_{h}q_{h}\zeta_{h}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\coloneqq-\int_{\Omega}\nabla q_{h}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad\forall\zeta_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{h},

where

.
We note for future reference, as

is a quasi-uniform family of partitionings and

\displaystyle\lvert q_{h}\rvert_{W^{1,s}}\leq C\lVert\Delta_{h}q_{h}\rVert_{L^{2}},

for

,

\displaystyle\sigma_{\infty,h}^{\Delta t,+}(t,\cdot)=\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}(\cdot)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t^{n-1}}^{t^{n}}\mathcal{I}_{h}^{Cl}\big[\sigma_{\infty}(t,\cdot)\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}t\ \in\mathcal{S}_{h},

Assumptions on the model functions and parameters

We make the following assumptions on the model parameters and functions.

(A1)

Let
\chi_{\varphi}\geq 0
and
\chi_{\sigma},A,B,K>0
be constant.

(A2)

The functions
\Gamma_{\varphi},\Gamma_{\sigma}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
only depend on
(\varphi,\sigma)
and they are continuous with linear growth, i.e.

\displaystyle\lvert\Gamma_{i}(\varphi,\sigma)\rvert\leq R_{0}(1+\lvert\varphi\rvert+\lvert\sigma\rvert),\quad i\in\{\varphi,\sigma\},

(2.17)

with a constant
R_{0}>0
.

(A3)

It holds
m,n\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
and there exist constants
m_{0},m_{1},n_{0},n_{1}>0
such that for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle m_{0}

\displaystyle\leq m(s)\leq m_{1},\quad n_{0}\leq n(s)\leq n_{1}.

(A4)

The potential
\psi
is nonnegative and belongs to
\psi\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
with

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle\geq R_{1}\lvert t\rvert^{2}-R_{2},

(2.18)

where
R_{1},R_{2}>0
. Additionally, the potential can be decomposed as
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
with
\psi_{1}\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
convex and
\psi_{2}\in C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R})
concave such that

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{i}^{\prime}(t)\rvert

\displaystyle\leq R_{3}(1+\lvert t\rvert),

(2.19)

where
i=1,2
and
R_{3}>0
. Moreover, we assume that

\displaystyle A>\frac{4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}R_{1}}.

(2.20)

As
A=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}
and
\epsilon
is small in applications, (
2.20
) is not a severe constraint.

Fully discrete system

Let us now introduce the numerical scheme approximating the system (
1.1
).

Let the discrete initial data
(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{2}
and, for
n=1,...,N_{T}
, let the discrete boundary values
\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
be given by (
2.11
) and (
2.15
), respectively.
Then, for
n=1,...,N_{T}
, find the discrete solution triplet
(\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
which satisfies for any test function triplet
(\zeta_{h},\varrho_{h},\xi_{h})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big)\zeta_{h}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}[m(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})]\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(2.21a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\mu_{h}^{n}-A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})-A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\varrho_{h}\Big]-B\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varrho_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(2.21b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\Big)\xi_{h}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}[n(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})]\big(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\cdot\nabla\xi_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\big(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big)\xi_{h}\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=0,

(2.21c)

where
\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\coloneqq\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})
and
\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\coloneqq\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})
.

In the scheme (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
), we make use of numerical integration by
mass lumping
which is often used for phase-field models because of computational reasons.
The main advantage is that
mass lumping
leads to simpler systems of equations as the
mass matrices
are diagonal whereas the precision of the numerical solutions is not affected.
In particular, the numerical errors resulting from
mass lumping
are based on the interpolation error estimate (
2.6
), and here we refer to, e.g.,
[
11
,
24
]
, where convergence rates for other phase-field systems have been studied.
Let us briefly remark that other quadrature rules can also be used as long as the quadrature weights are non-negative.

Moreover, the time discretization of the scheme is chosen such that the nonlinear mobility functions
m(\cdot),n(\cdot)
and the derivative of the concave part of the potential
\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\cdot)
are treated explicitly and all the other terms are treated implicitly.
However, also other time discretizations of the mobility functions
m(\cdot),n(\cdot)
and of the source terms
\Gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot),\Gamma_{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
are possible.
In (
2.21b
), the terms
\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\cdot),\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\cdot)
are discretized in time by a convex-concave splitting method which is often used in the context of phase-field systems, see e.g.
[
5
,
39
]
.
In particular, the convex-concave splitting allows the inequality (
3.4
) which is essential for the analysis of the scheme.

In the following two sections, we will discuss stability, existence and continuous dependence of solutions of the scheme (
2.21a
)-(
2.21
).

3
Stability of the discrete system

Let us introduce the discrete free energy
\mathcal{F}_{h}:\mathcal{S}_{h}\times\mathcal{S}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
of the system (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h},\sigma_{h})&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{B}{2}\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}\rvert^{2}+\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[A\psi(\varphi_{h})+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{2}\lvert\sigma_{h}\rvert^{2}+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}(1-\varphi_{h})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\end{split}

(3.1)

for all
\varphi_{h},\sigma_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
. We note that the last term in (
3.1
) can have a negative sign. This is one of the main obstacles we have to handle to derive useful
a priori
estimates.

We recall the following discrete version of Gronwall’s inequality. For the proof, we refer to, e.g.,
[
19
, pp. 401–402]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
e_{n},a_{n},b_{n}\geq 0
for all
n\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{n}&\leq a_{n}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i}e_{i}\quad\forall n\geq 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad e_{n}\leq a_{n}\cdot\exp\Big(\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}b_{i}\Big)\quad\forall n\geq 0.\end{split}

(3.2)

With the help of Lemma
3.1
, we can now derive stability estimates for the numerical scheme (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).

Lemma 3.2

(Stability)
.

Assume that
\Delta t<\Delta t_{*}
, where
\Delta t_{*}
is a constant that only depends on the model parameters. For the explicit form of
\Delta t_{*}
, see (
3.20
). Then, for
n=1,...,N_{T}
, solutions
\big(\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n}\big)\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
), if they exist, satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\max_{m=1,...,N_{T}}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\Big(\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq C\Big(T+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\rvert+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\cdot\exp(CT)\leq C.\end{split}

(3.3)

We now start the testing procedure.
In equation (
2.21a
), we set
\zeta_{h}=\mu_{h}^{n}
and use the lower bound of
m(\cdot)
to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big)\mu_{h}^{n}\Big]+m_{0}\big\lvert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\big\rvert^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq 0.

Testing (
2.21b
) with
\varrho_{h}=\frac{1}{\Delta t}(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})
gives

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big(-\mu_{h}^{n}+A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})+A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\Big]+\frac{B}{\Delta t}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\big(\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0.

As the potential can be decomposed into a convex and a concave part, i.e.
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
, we get the inequality

\displaystyle\Big(\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\Big)(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\geq\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n})-\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n-1}).

(3.4)

Using the elementary identity

\displaystyle 2x(x-y)=x^{2}-y^{2}+(x-y)^{2}\quad\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R},

(3.5)

we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[A\frac{\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n})-\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})}{\Delta t}-\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}(\mu_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{B}{2}\frac{\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}-\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{B}{2}\frac{\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert^{2}}{\Delta t}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq 0.

Next, we test (
2.21
) with
\xi_{h}=\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})
and use the lower bound of
n(\cdot)
. Then it holds on noting (
3.5
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{2}\frac{\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}-\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rvert^{2}}{\Delta t}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{2}\frac{\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rvert^{2}}{\Delta t}\Big]+n_{0}\big\lvert\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big\rvert^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\chi_{\sigma}\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+K\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})-K\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

So far we have that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{B}{2\Delta t}\Big(\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}-\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{A}{\Delta t}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n})-\psi(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{2\Delta t}\Big(\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+m_{0}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+n_{0}\big\lVert\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+K\chi_{\sigma}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)-\mu_{h}^{n}\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)-\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})-K\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
&\leq 0.\end{split}

(3.6)

Next, we derive an estimate for the chemical potential
\mu_{h}^{n}
.
On noting (
2.19
) and Young’s inequality, we receive by testing (
2.21b
) with
\varrho_{h}=\mu_{h}^{n}
that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\lvert\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})+A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\mu_{h}^{n}\Big]+B\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[AR_{3}(2+\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert+\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert\big)\lvert\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert+\chi_{\varphi}\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\lvert\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert\Big]+B\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert\lvert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\frac{1}{2}\lvert\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+2A^{2}R_{3}^{2}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+2A^{2}R_{3}^{2}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert^{2}+2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\frac{B^{2}}{m_{0}}\lvert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+\frac{m_{0}}{4}\lvert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+C(A,R_{3},\Omega),

which yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}&\leq 4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2B^{2}}{m_{0}}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{m_{0}}{2}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+C(A,R_{3},\Omega).\end{split}

(3.7)

With Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities and (
3.7
), we can estimate the source terms in (
3.6
) as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)-\mu_{h}^{n}\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}\lvert\Omega\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}\rVert_{h}^{2}+R_{0}^{2}\big\lVert(1+\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert)\big\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}\lvert\Omega\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\big(3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\big)\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\big(3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}\big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+C(R_{0},\chi_{\varphi},\Omega).\end{split}

(3.8)

For the terms in (
3.6
) involving the boundary integrals, we have by Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and the trace theorem, that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})-K\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big)\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
&\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\frac{3K\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+K\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert^{2}+C(K,\chi_{\varphi},\chi_{\sigma})\big(1+\lvert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rvert^{2}\big)\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\\
&\leq KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}K}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}+C(K,\chi_{\varphi},\chi_{\sigma})\big(\lvert{\partial\Omega}\rvert+\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big).\end{split}

(3.9)

Furthermore, we can calculate

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\chi_{\varphi}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})\big(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\big)-\chi_{\varphi}\big(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\big)\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n-1}(1-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})-\chi_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x,\end{split}

(3.10a)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big\lvert\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\chi_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\Big\rvert\leq\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.10b)

Combining (
3.6
)–(
3.10
), we obtain on noting (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})-\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\Big)+\frac{B}{2\Delta t}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4\Delta t}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{m_{0}}{2}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+n_{0}\big\lVert\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\frac{K\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&\leq C(A,R_{0},R_{3},K,\chi_{\varphi},\chi_{\sigma},\Omega,{\partial\Omega})\big(1+\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big)+\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}\Delta t}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\Big)\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Big(\frac{2B^{2}}{m_{0}}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)\Big)\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Big(4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}+3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)\Big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.11)

Next, applying the triangle inequality and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{\sigma}^{2}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\big\lVert\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}+\chi_{\varphi}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\big\lVert\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2},

so that (
3.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})-\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\Big)+\frac{B}{2\Delta t}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4\Delta t}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{m_{0}}{2}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{n_{0}\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}\big\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{K\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&\leq C(A,R_{0},R_{3},K,\chi_{\varphi},\chi_{\sigma},\Omega,{\partial\Omega})\big(1+\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big)+\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}\Delta t}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Big(3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\Big)\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Big(\frac{2B^{2}}{m_{0}}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)+n_{0}\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\Big)\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\quad+4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Big(4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}+3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)\Big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.12)

Now we define the constants

\displaystyle\begin{split}&c_{1}\coloneqq\frac{m_{0}}{2},\quad c_{2}\coloneqq\frac{n_{0}\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2},\quad c_{3}\coloneqq\frac{K\chi_{\sigma}}{4},\quad c_{4}\coloneqq 3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\sigma}^{2}}{2}+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2},\\
&c_{5}\coloneqq\frac{2B^{2}}{m_{0}}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big)+n_{0}\chi_{\varphi}^{2},\quad c_{6}\coloneqq 4A^{2}R_{3}^{2},\\
&c_{7}\coloneqq 4A^{2}R_{3}^{2}+3R_{0}^{2}+\frac{3\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2}+KC_{tr}^{2}\big(\frac{\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{2\chi_{\sigma}}+1\big).\end{split}

(3.13)

Then, (
3.12
) becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\Delta t}\Big(\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})-\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\Big)+\frac{B}{2\Delta t}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4\Delta t}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{2}\big\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{3}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&\leq C(A,R_{0},R_{3},K,\chi_{\varphi},\chi_{\sigma},\Omega,{\partial\Omega})\big(1+\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big)+\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}\Delta t}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+c_{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{5}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{6}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{7}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.14)

Multiplying both sides of (
3.14
) with
\Delta t
and summing from
n=1,...,m
, where
m=1,...,N_{T}
, yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{m},\sigma_{h}^{m})+\frac{B}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{2}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{3}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\rvert+C\Big(T+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(c_{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{5}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{6}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{7}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(3.15)

By Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\sigma_{h}^{m}(1-\varphi_{h}^{m})\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\leq\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+C(\chi_{\sigma},\chi_{\varphi},\Omega).

(3.16)

Moreover, we obtain from (
2.18
) that

\displaystyle R_{1}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\psi(\varphi_{h}^{m})\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x+R_{2}\lvert\Omega\rvert.

(3.17)

Hence, we can deduce from (
3.15
)–(
3.17
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(AR_{1}-\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\Big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{B}{2}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{B}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{2}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{3}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\rvert+C\Big(T+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(c_{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{5}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{6}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{7}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(3.18)

To apply a discrete Gronwall argument, i.e. Lemma
3.1
, we have to absorb all terms on the right-hand side with index
n=m
. Hence, we obtain from (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(AR_{1}-\frac{4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}-c_{7}\Delta t\Big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Big(\frac{B}{2}-c_{5}\Delta t\Big)\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big(\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}-c_{4}\Delta t\Big)\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{B}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{1}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{2}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{3}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\rvert+C\Big(T+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)+\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\Big(\frac{4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}+(c_{6}+c_{7})\Delta t\Big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\Big(c_{4}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+c_{5}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(3.19)

We need to make sure that all coefficients on the left-hand side are positive which can be achieved with the following restriction to the time step size
\Delta t
:

\displaystyle\Delta t

\displaystyle<\Delta t_{*}\coloneqq\min\bigg\{\frac{B}{2c_{5}},\ \frac{\chi_{\sigma}}{4c_{4}},\ \frac{AR_{1}-\frac{4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}}{c_{7}}\bigg\},

(3.20)

where the constants
c_{4},c_{5},c_{7}>0
are defined by (
3.13
). We remark that
AR_{1}-\frac{4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{\chi_{\sigma}}>0
by assumption (
2.20
).
Hence, we obtain from Lemma
3.1
, (
2.12
) and (
2.16
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}-\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq C\Big(T+\lvert\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\rvert+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\cdot\exp(CT)\leq C,\end{split}

(3.21)

for some constants
C>0
that are independent of
h
and
\Delta t
.
Taking the maximum over
m=1,...,N_{T}
on the left-hand side yields the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

We remark that the assumptions (
2.20
) and (
3.20
) are by no means optimal assumptions.
The assumption (
2.20
) is necessary because the term

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\sigma_{h}^{m}(1-\varphi_{h}^{m})\big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

in the discrete energy
\mathcal{F}_{h}(\varphi_{h}^{m},\sigma_{h}^{m})
on the left-hand side of (
3.15
) can be negative. Hence, we perform the steps (
3.16
)–(
3.18
) to absorb it. For more details, also see
[
33
, Remark 3.1]
. The condition (
3.20
) appears when applying a discrete Gronwall argument in (
3.19
).

4
Existence and continuous dependence of discrete solutions

We recall the following lemma from
[
26
, Chap. 9.1]
which is a direct consequence of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem.

Lemma 4.1

(Zeros of a vector field)
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, assume that the continuous function
\boldsymbol{v}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfies

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0\ \ \textit{ if }\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert=R,

for some
R>0
. Then there exists a point
\boldsymbol{x}\in B_{R}(0)
such that
\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})=0
.

Now we can establish the following existence result.

Theorem 4.2

(Existence)
.

Let
\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
and for
n=1,...,N_{T}
, let
\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
be given by (
2.11
) and (
2.15
), respectively. Furthermore, assume that
\Delta t<\Delta t_{*}
, where
\Delta t_{*}
is given by (
3.20
). Then, for all
n=1,...,N_{T}
, there exists a solution triplet
\big(\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n}\big)\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) which fulfills (
3.3
).

Let us define a vector field
\boldsymbol{v}:\mathbb{R}^{3N_{h}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3N_{h}}
that maps the coefficient vector
\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3N_{h}}
of

\displaystyle\Big(\mu_{h}^{n},\ -2\mu_{h}^{n}+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\varphi_{h}^{n},\ \chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\varphi_{h}^{n}\Big)\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3},

to the left-hand side of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).
Then, a zero of
\boldsymbol{v}
corresponds to a solution of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).

The aim is to show
\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}\geq c_{1}\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{2}-c_{2}
with
\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert=R>0
for some
R>0
and some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
that are independent of
\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n}
.
We obtain similarly to the proof of Lemma
3.2
that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big)\mu_{h}^{n}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}[m(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})]\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\mu_{h}^{n}-A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})-A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\big(2\mu_{h}^{n}-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}B\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\big(2\mu_{h}^{n}-\frac{1}{\Delta t}\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\Big)\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}[n(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})]\big(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\cdot\nabla\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\big(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big)\big(\chi_{\sigma}\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}

\displaystyle\geq C\Big(\frac{1}{\Delta t}-\frac{1}{\Delta t_{*}}\Big)\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+C\Big(\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)-C(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq C\big(\lvert\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\boldsymbol{\mu}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rvert^{2}\big)-C(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}),

for various constants
C>0
that are independent of
\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n}
, where
\Delta t_{*}
is given by (
3.20
) and the coefficient vectors of
\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
are denoted by
\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{h}}
.

Let us remark that

\displaystyle|||\cdot|||:\ (\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\sigma})\mapsto\Big(\lvert\boldsymbol{\mu}\rvert^{2}+\big\lvert-2\boldsymbol{\mu}+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\big\rvert^{2}+\big\lvert\chi_{\sigma}\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\chi_{\varphi}\boldsymbol{\varphi}\big\rvert^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

defines a norm on the finite dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{3N_{h}}
. The definiteness of
|||\cdot|||
can be shown as follows. Assuming
|||(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\sigma})|||=0
, it follows from the first term that
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{0}
. The second term yields
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\boldsymbol{0}
. Then, from the third term, we have
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=\boldsymbol{0}
.

Hence, on noting norm equivalence in finite dimensions,
we obtain with
R=\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert=|||(\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\sigma})|||
large enough that

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{v}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\geq C\big(\lvert\boldsymbol{\varphi}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\boldsymbol{\mu}\rvert^{2}+\lvert\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rvert^{2}\big)-C(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n})

\displaystyle\geq C\lvert\boldsymbol{x}\rvert^{2}-C(\varphi_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{h}^{n-1},\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n})

\displaystyle>0.

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that there exists a zero of
\boldsymbol{v}
which corresponds to a solution of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).
∎

In the next theorem, we assume that the source terms
\Gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\Gamma_{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
are Lipschitz continuous in both arguments and that the mobility functions
m(\cdot)
and
n(\cdot)
are constant. This makes it possible to show that solutions of (
2.21a
)-(
2.21
) depend continuously on the inital and boundary data if the time step size
\Delta t>0
is small enough. In particular, discrete solutions are unique.

Theorem 4.3

(Continuous dependence)
.

Let
\Gamma_{\varphi},\Gamma_{\sigma}\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with Lipschitz constants
L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}},L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}>0
. Moreover, suppose that
m(\cdot)=n(\cdot)=1
.
For
i=1,2
and
n=1,...,N_{T}
, let
(\varphi_{h,i}^{n},\mu_{h,i}^{n},\sigma_{h,i}^{n})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
be solutions of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) with corresponding initial data
\varphi_{h,i}^{0},\sigma_{h,i}^{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
and boundary data
\sigma_{\infty,h,i}^{n}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}
,
n=1,...,N_{T}
.
Let

\displaystyle\Delta t<\frac{B}{2A^{2}L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}+3B(L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}})}.

(4.1)

Then, there exist constants
C>0
that are independent of
h,\Delta t
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\max_{m=1,...,N_{T}}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h,1}^{m}-\varphi_{h,2}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h,1}^{m}-\sigma_{h,2}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\Big(\lVert(\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n})-(\varphi_{h,1}^{n-1}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n-1})\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert(\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n})-(\sigma_{h,1}^{n-1}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n-1})\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\Big(\lVert\mu_{h,1}^{n}-\mu_{h,2}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla(\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq C\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h,1}^{0}-\varphi_{h,2}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h,1}^{0}-\sigma_{h,2}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h,2}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\cdot\exp(CT).\end{split}

(4.2)

For
n=1,...,N_{T}
, suppose there are two solutions of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) denoted by
(\varphi_{h,i}^{n},\mu_{h,i}^{n},\sigma_{h,i}^{n})
,
i=1,2
, with corresponding initial data
\varphi_{h,i}^{0},\sigma_{h,i}^{0}
and boundary data
\sigma_{\infty,h,i}^{0}
. Let us denote the differences by

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}^{n}=\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\quad\mu_{h}^{n}=\mu_{h,1}^{n}-\mu_{h,2}^{n},\quad\sigma_{h}^{n}=\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n},\quad\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}=\sigma_{\infty,h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h,2}^{n}.

It holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\zeta_{h}-\Delta t\Big(\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\Big)\zeta_{h}\Big]+\Delta t\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(4.3a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\mu_{h}^{n}+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\varrho_{h}-A\Big(\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n})-\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n-1})-\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n-1})\Big)\varrho_{h}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}B\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varrho_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(4.3b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1})\xi_{h}+\Delta t\Big(\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\Big)\xi_{h}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Delta t\big(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\big)\cdot\nabla\xi_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\partial\Omega}\Delta tK\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n})\xi_{h}\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=0,

(4.3c)

for all
\zeta_{h},\varrho_{h},\xi_{h}\in S^{h}
.
Setting
\zeta_{h}=B\varphi_{h}^{n}
,
\varrho_{h}=\Delta t(\mu_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n})
,
\xi_{h}=B\sigma_{h}^{n}
in (
4.3a
)–(
4.3
) and adding the resulting equations yields, on noting (
3.5
), that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{B}{2}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\Delta t\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta tB\chi_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Delta tBK\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&=\Delta tA\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n})-\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n-1})-\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n-1})\Big)\big(\mu_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\big)\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\Delta tB\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\Big)\varphi_{h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad-\Delta tB\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\Big)\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\Delta tBK\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\sigma_{h}^{n}\big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}.\end{split}

(4.4)

Using the Lipschitz assumptions of
\psi_{1}^{\prime},\psi_{2}^{\prime},\Gamma_{\varphi},\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n})-\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert,

\displaystyle\lvert\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n-1})-\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n-1})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert,

\displaystyle\big\lvert\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}\big(\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\big),

\displaystyle\big\lvert\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,1}^{n},\sigma_{h,1}^{n})-\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h,2}^{n},\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}\big(\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\big).

On noting Young’s inequality, we obtain together with (
4.4
), that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{B}{2}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}-\Delta t\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta tB\chi_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Delta tBK\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\\
&\leq\Delta tA\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\big(L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+L_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}}\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rvert\big)\big(\lvert\mu_{h}^{n}\rvert+\chi_{\varphi}\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\big)\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\Delta tB\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}\big(\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\big)\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}\big(\lvert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\big)\lvert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rvert\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Delta tBK\big(\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta t\big(A^{2}L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+\chi_{\varphi}^{2}\big)\big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\big)+C(A,\chi_{\varphi},L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}},L_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}})\Delta t\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta tB\big(\frac{3}{2}L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+\frac{1}{2}L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}\big)\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta tB\big(\frac{1}{2}L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+\frac{3}{2}L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}}\big)\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{2}\Delta tBK\big(\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\big).\end{split}

(4.5)

Absorbing the terms on the right-hand side and summing from
n=1,...,m
, where
m=1,...,N_{T}
, leads to

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{B}{2}\big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\big)+\frac{B}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+B\chi_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}BK\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{B}{2}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}C(A,\chi_{\varphi},L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}},L_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}})\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}+A^{2}L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}B(L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}})\Big)\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(4.6)

In order to apply a discrete Gronwall argument, we absorb the terms on the right-hand side of (
4.6
) with index
n=m
. Therefore, we receive

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(\frac{B}{2}-\Delta t\big(2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}+A^{2}L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{3}{2}B(L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}})\big)\Big)\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\frac{B}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+B\chi_{\sigma}\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}BK\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq\frac{B}{2}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}C(A,\chi_{\varphi},L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}},L_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}},B,L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}},L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}})\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(4.7)

The terms on the left-hand side are nonnegative supposed that

\displaystyle\Delta t<\frac{B}{2A^{2}L_{\psi_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}+4\chi_{\varphi}^{2}+3B(L_{\Gamma_{\varphi}}+L_{\Gamma_{\sigma}})}.

(4.8)

Hence, we can deduce from Lemma
3.1
that there exist constants
C>0
that are independent of
h,\Delta t
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lVert\varphi_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{m}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\\
&\quad+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{m}\Big(\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\\
&\leq C\Big(\lVert\varphi_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{0}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\rVert_{h,{\partial\Omega}}^{2}\Big)\cdot\exp(CT).\end{split}

(4.9)

Taking the maximum over
m=1,...,N_{T}
on the left-hand side proves the result.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

1.

In practice, the constants
A
and
B
are usually defined as
A=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}
and
B=\beta\epsilon
, respectively, where
\epsilon
is a small constant. From Lemma
3.2
and Theorem
4.2
, we have the condition
\Delta t=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)
for the time step size in order to obtain stability and existence of solutions of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).
In contrast to this, and with additional assumptions on the mobility functions and source terms, we can deduce from Theorem
4.3
that the time step size must fulfill the condition
\Delta t=\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})
to obtain continuous dependence and, in particular, uniqueness of discrete solutions.

2.

Suppose that the source terms have the specific form

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi,\sigma)=\big(\lambda_{p}\sigma-\lambda_{a}\big)h(\varphi),\quad\quad\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi,\sigma)=\lambda_{c}\sigma h(\varphi),

(4.10)

for all
\varphi,\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\lambda_{p},\lambda_{a},\lambda_{c}
are nonnegative constants referring to proliferation, apoptosis and consumption rate. Moreover,
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a nonnegative, bounded and Lipschitz continuous function with
h(-1)=0
and
h(1)=1
. This specific choice of the source terms is motivated by linear kinetics and is a common choice for numerical simulations of tumour growth models
[
22
,
37
]
.

With the choice (
4.10
), continuous dependence of solutions of (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) on the initial and boundary data can be shown analogously to Theorem
4.3
if the time step size is small enough. The main difference is that

\displaystyle\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}h(\varphi_{h,1}^{n})-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}h(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}\rvert\lvert h(\varphi_{h,1}^{n})-h(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})\rvert+\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}\rvert\lvert h(\varphi_{h,2}^{n})\rvert

\displaystyle\leq L_{h}\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}\rvert\lvert\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n}\rvert+h_{\infty}\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}\rvert,

where
L_{h}
denotes the Lipschitz constant of
h(\cdot)
and
h_{\infty}=\lVert h(\cdot)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
. Following the proof, one then has to handle triple products of the form

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}\rvert\lvert\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n}\rvert^{2}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}\rvert\lvert\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n}\rvert\lvert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}\rvert\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

With further calculations, these terms can be bounded by

\displaystyle C_{1}\lVert\varphi_{h,1}^{n}-\varphi_{h,2}^{n}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+C_{2}\lVert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+C_{3}\lVert\nabla(\sigma_{h,1}^{n}-\sigma_{h,2}^{n})\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}

where the constant
C_{1}
depends on
\max\limits_{n=1,...,N_{T}}\lVert\sigma_{h,1}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}
, which can be bounded uniformly in
(h,\Delta t)
if the time step size is small enough, see Lemma
3.2
. The constants
C_{2},C_{3}
arise from Young’s inequality and only depend on the model parameters.
The third term can be absorbed whereas the first two terms can be handled with a discrete Gronwall argument, i.e. Lemma
3.1
, if the time step size satisfies an additional constraint.

In this section we prove higher order estimates for solutions of (

)–(

This is needed in order to show more compactness properties for

in space dimensions

At first, we introduce the projection operator

(5.1)

It holds, see, e.g.,
[

\displaystyle\lVert\eta-\hat{Q}_{h}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}}+h\lVert\nabla\eta-\nabla\hat{Q}_{h}\eta\rVert_{L^{2}}

(5.2)

with a constant

which is independent of

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
hold. Then it holds

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Delta t\sum_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\Big(\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}+\Big\lVert\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\rVert_{(H^{1})^{\prime}}^{2}+\Big\lVert\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\rVert_{(H^{1})^{\prime}}^{2}\Big)\leq C,\end{split}

and

\displaystyle\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{N_{T}-l}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n+l}-\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq Cl\Delta t,

(5.3b)

for any

, where the constants

are independent of
h,\Delta t
.

On noting the definition of the discrete Laplacian (
2.13
), we can rewrite (
2.21b
) as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(-\mu_{h}^{n}+A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})+A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}-B\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\Big)\varrho_{h}\Big]=0,\quad\quad\forall\zeta_{h}\in\mathcal{S}_{h}.

Choosing
\varrho_{h}=\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}
and dividing both sides by
B
yields

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\big(-\mu_{h}^{n}+A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})+A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\big)\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\Big].

Together with Young’s inequality and the growth assumptions on
\psi_{1}^{\prime},\psi_{2}^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2}{B^{2}}\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2A^{2}}{B^{2}}\lVert\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2A^{2}}{B^{2}}\lVert\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\rVert_{h}^{2}+\frac{2\chi_{\varphi}^{2}}{B^{2}}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+C\Big(1+\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big).

Absorbing
\frac{1}{2}\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}
to the left-hand side, noting (
3.3
), multiplying both sides with
\Delta t
and summing from

yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta t\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N_{T}}\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}&\leq\Delta t\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N_{T}}C\Big(1+\lVert\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n-1}\rVert_{h}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}^{2}\Big)\leq C.\end{split}

(5.4)

Applying (
2.3
) leads to the first bound in (
5.3a
).

Let
\zeta\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. On noting (
5.1
), (
5.2
), (
2.21a
), Hölder’s inequality, the linear growth of

and (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)\zeta\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\big]-m(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}\big)\lVert\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\rVert_{h}+m_{1}\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}\lVert\nabla\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\rVert_{L^{2}}

which gives

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big\rVert_{(H^{1})^{\prime}}\leq C\Big(1+\lVert\varphi_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{n}\rVert_{h}+\lVert\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\rVert_{L^{2}}\Big).

(5.5)

Similarly, we receive from (
5.1
), (
5.2
), (
2.21
), Hölder’s inequality, the linear growth of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and the trace theorem, that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)\zeta\,{\mathrm{d}}x=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}\Big)\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}-\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{n}\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\big]-n(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi)\cdot\nabla\hat{Q}_{h}\zeta\,{\mathrm{d}}x

) with

and

, to obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}-\Delta t\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{n}\Big)(\varphi_{h}^{m+l}-\varphi_{h}^{m})\Big]+\Delta t\mathcal{I}_{h}[m(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})]\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{h}^{m+l}-\varphi_{h}^{m})\,{\mathrm{d}}x.

\displaystyle 0

) leads to

6
Convergence to a weak solution

In this section, we will use compactness arguments and the bounds (

), (
5.3a
) to show that solutions of the discrete scheme (

)–(
2.21
) converge to a weak solution of (

)–(

) when we pass to the limit
(h,\Delta t)\to(0,0)
.

For future reference, we recall the following compactness results from
[
52
, Sect. 8, Cor. 4 and Thm. 5]
.
Let
X,Y,Z
be Banach spaces with a compact embedding
X\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow Y
and a continuous embedding
Y\hookrightarrow Z
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
r>1
. Then we have the following compact embeddings:

\displaystyle\{\eta\in L^{p}(0,T;X):\

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta\in L^{1}(0,T;Z)\}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow L^{p}(0,T;Y),

(6.1a)

\displaystyle\{\eta\in L^{\infty}(0,T;X):\

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta\in L^{r}(0,T;Z)\}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow C([0,T];Y).

(6.1b)

Moreover, let
F
be a bounded subset in
L^{p}(0,T;X)
with

\displaystyle\lim_{\theta\to 0}\lVert\eta(\cdot,\cdot+\theta)-\eta(\cdot,\cdot)\rVert_{L^{p}(0,T-\theta;Z)}=0\quad\text{ uniformly for }\eta\in F.

(6.1c)

Then
F
is relatively compact in
L^{p}(0,T;Y)
if
1\leq p<\infty
and in
C([0,T];Y)
if
p=\infty
, respectively.

Let us introduce the following notation for affine-linear and piecewise constant extensions of time-discrete functions
a^{n}(\cdot)
,
n=0,...,N_{T}
:

\displaystyle a^{\Delta t}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{t-t^{n-1}}{\Delta t}a^{n}(\cdot)+\frac{t^{n}-t}{\Delta t}a^{n-1}(\cdot)\quad\quad

\displaystyle t\in[t^{n-1},t^{n}],\ n\in\{1,...,N_{T}\},

(6.2)

\displaystyle a^{\Delta t,+}(\cdot,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq a^{n}(\cdot),\quad\quad a^{\Delta t,-}(\cdot,t)\coloneqq a^{n-1}(\cdot)\quad\quad

\displaystyle t\in(t^{n-1},t^{n}],\ n\in\{1,...,N_{T}\}.

(6.3)

Using this notation, we can reformulate the system (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) continuously in time. Multiplying each equation in (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) by
\Delta t
and summing from
n=1,...,N_{T}
, we obtain for all test functions
(\zeta_{h}
,

,

that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\big(\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t}-\Gamma_{\varphi,h}^{\Delta t,+}\big)\zeta_{h}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}[m(\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,-})]\nabla\mu_{h}^{\Delta t,+}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,

(6.4a)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(-\mu_{h}^{\Delta t,+}+A\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,+})+A\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,-})-\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t,+}\Big)\varrho_{h}\Big]\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}B\nabla\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,+}\cdot\nabla\varrho_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,

(6.4b)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\big(\partial_{t}\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t}+\Gamma_{\sigma,h}^{\Delta t,+}\big)\xi_{h}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}[n(\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,-})]\big(\chi_{\sigma}\nabla\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t,+}-\chi_{\varphi}\nabla\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,+}\big)\cdot\nabla\xi_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\,{\mathrm{d}}t\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\big(\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t,+}-\sigma_{\infty,h}^{\Delta t,+}\big)\xi_{h}\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\,{\mathrm{d}}t=0,

(6.4c)

subject to the initial conditions

,

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.2
, we can deduce from (

), (

), (

), (

), (
2.12
), (

) and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\lVert\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2}+\lVert\Delta_{h}\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{t}\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1})^{\prime})}^{2}\\
&\quad+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2}+\lVert\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}({\partial\Omega}))}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{t}\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;(H^{1})^{\prime})}^{2}\\
&\quad+\lVert\mu_{h}^{\Delta t,+}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2}+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\lVert\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t}-\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t,\pm}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{1})}^{2}+\frac{1}{\Delta t}\lVert\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t}-\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t,\pm}\rVert_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2}\\
&\leq C,\end{split}

(6.5a)

and for any

,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T-l\Delta t}\big\lVert\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}(t+l\Delta t)-\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}(t)\big\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}t

\displaystyle\leq Cl\Delta t,

(6.5b)

with constants

that are independent of

In the following step, we show that there exists a subsequence of

,

,

Lemma 6.1
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem

hold. Then there exist a subsequence of

has a further subsequence converging to the same limit

. We then already have that the whole sequence
\big(\varphi_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)},\mu_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)},\sigma_{h}^{\Delta t(,\pm)}\big)
converges to
(\varphi,\mu,\sigma)
.

7
Numerical results

In this section, we present numerical results for the model (
1.1
). In particular, we want to illustrate the practicability of the fully-discrete scheme (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) in the space dimensions
d\in\{1,2,3\}
. First, let us introduce the following scheme for which in (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
) specific choices of the parameters
A,B
and the nutrient mobility function
n(\cdot)
have been applied.

For given discrete initial data
(\varphi_{h}^{0},\sigma_{h}^{0})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{2}
and for
n=1,...,N_{T}
, find the discrete solution triplet
(\varphi_{h}^{n},\mu_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
which satisfies for any test function triplet
(\zeta_{h},\varrho_{h},\xi_{h})\in(\mathcal{S}_{h})^{3}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\varphi_{h}^{n}-\varphi_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}-\Gamma_{\varphi}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})\Big)\zeta_{h}\Big]+\mathcal{I}_{h}\big[m(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})\big]\nabla\mu_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\zeta_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(7.1a)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\mu_{h}^{n}-\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\psi_{1}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n})-\frac{\beta}{\epsilon}\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})+\chi_{\varphi}\sigma_{h}^{n}\Big)\varrho_{h}\Big]-\beta\epsilon\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varrho_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x=0,

(7.1b)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[\Big(\frac{\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{h}^{n-1}}{\Delta t}+\Gamma_{\sigma}(\varphi_{h}^{n},\sigma_{h}^{n})\Big)\xi_{h}\Big]+\Big(\nabla\sigma_{h}^{n}-\eta\nabla\varphi_{h}^{n}\Big)\cdot\nabla\xi_{h}\,{\mathrm{d}}x\quad\quad

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{I}_{h}\Big[K\big(\sigma_{h}^{n}-\sigma_{\infty,h}^{n}\big)\xi_{h}\Big]\ \mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}=0.

(7.1c)

Here we defined the parameters
A,B
as
A=\frac{\beta}{\epsilon},B=\beta\epsilon
, where
\epsilon>0
is proportional to the width of the diffuse interface, and
\beta>0
denotes the surface tension.
By the simplifying assumption that diffusion processes of the nutrient are not influenced by the type of tissue, we limit the numerical experiments as in
[
37
]
to the case of a constant nutrient mobility function, i.e.

n(\varphi_{h}^{n-1})=\chi_{\sigma}^{-1}.

Further, in the model (
1.1
), the effects of chemotaxis (movement of the tumour along the nutrient gradient) and active nutrient transport (active movement of nutrients towards the tumour) are both connected via the parameter
\chi_{\varphi}
. The choice of the nutrient mobility function
n(\cdot)
was introduced and motivated in
[
37
]
in order to decouple these two processes. In particular, the ratio between the parameters
\chi_{\varphi}
and
\chi_{\sigma}
, i.e.
\eta\coloneqq\frac{\chi_{\varphi}}{\chi_{\sigma}},

in (
7.1
) accounts for active nutrient transport while
\chi_{\varphi}
in (
7.1b
) controls the effects of chemotaxis.

Throughout all numerical experiments, we make the following choices for the source terms
\Gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot),\Gamma_{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
, the potential
\psi(\cdot)=\psi_{1}(\cdot)+\psi_{2}(\cdot)
and the mobility function
m(\cdot)
which are defined for all
r,s\in[-2,2]
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\varphi}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\lambda_{p}s-\lambda_{a}\big)(1+r),

(7.2)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\sigma}(r,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{c}s(1+r),

(7.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}r^{4}

\displaystyle\quad\text{with}\quad\psi_{1}^{\prime}(r)=r^{3},

(7.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\text{with}\quad\psi_{2}^{\prime}(r)=-r,

(7.5)

\displaystyle m(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{M}{2}(1+r)^{2}+m_{0}.

(7.6)

For
r,s\in\mathbb{R}\backslash[-2,2]
, we truncate the functions
\Gamma_{\varphi}(\cdot,\cdot),\Gamma_{\sigma}(\cdot,\cdot)
,
m(\cdot)
and restrict the potential
\psi(\cdot)=\psi_{1}(\cdot)+\psi_{2}(\cdot)
to quadratic growth such that the assumptions (
A2
)–(
A4
) hold true. We remind that the approach of the truncated functions is inevitable for the mathematical analysis of the discrete scheme (
2.21a
)–(
2.21
).
In practice, our numerical experiments indicate that the order parameter
\varphi
and the nutrient
\sigma
stay within the range
[-2,2]
. In particular, we observe
\varphi\in[-1-\delta,1+\delta]
and
\sigma\in[0,1+\delta]
for a relatively small constant
\delta>0
.

In the biological context, the source terms in (
7.2
)–(
7.3
) model the processes of proliferation, apoptosis and nutrient consumption with the corresponding rates
\lambda_{p},\lambda_{a},\lambda_{c}\geq 0
.
We assume these effects to occur only in presence of tumour cells and to vanish in the pure healthy phase where
\varphi=-1
.
Also, the potential
\psi
is chosen of polynomial type such that
\psi=\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}
with
\psi_{1}
convex and
\psi_{2}
concave.
Moreover, the choice of
m(\cdot)
in (
7.6
) allows for a constant mobility (
M=0
,
m_{0}>0
) and for a (nearly) one-sided degenerate mobility function (
M>0
,
m_{0}\approx 0
), where both choices were suggested in
[
22
]
.

Let us now explain some implementation aspects for the system (
7.1a
)–(
7.1

[
45
]
. In order to solve the nonlinear system (
7.1a
)–(
7.1

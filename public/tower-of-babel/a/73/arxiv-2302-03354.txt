Degenerate complex Hessian equations on compact Hermitian manifolds
Vincent Guedj & Chinh H. Lu

Abstract
. In this note we provide uniform a priori estimates for solutions to degenerate complex Hessian equations on compact hermitian manifolds. Our approach relies on the corresponding a priori estimates for Monge-Ampère equations; it provides an extension as well as a short alternative proof to results of Dinew-Kołodziej, Kołodziej-Nguyen and Guo-Phong-Tong.

Introduction

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold equipped with a hermitian form
\omega_{X}
.
We fix
1\leq k\leq n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
,
\omega
another hermitian form,
and
\mu_{X}
a volume form on
X
.
The complex Hessian equation is

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=c\mu_{X},

where
c>0
is a positive constant
and
\varphi
(the unknown) is a smooth
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic function,
i.e.
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-j}\geq 0
for all
1\leq j\leq k
.

When
k=1
this reduces to the Laplace equation. When
k=n
this yields the complex
Monge-Ampère equation which was famously solved by Yau
[
Yau78
]
when
\omega
is Kähler,
and by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
TW10
]
in the hermitian setting.

From now on we assume that
1<k<n
. A unique solution has been provided
by Dinew-Kołodziej
[
DK17
]
when
\omega=\omega_{X}
is Kähler; the hermitian setting has
been resolved independently by Székelyhidi
[
Szek18
]
and Zhang
[
Zh17
]
.

It is quite natural to consider such equations in degenerate settings,
allowing the measure
\mu_{X}=f\omega_{X}^{n}
to be merely absolutely continuous with respect
to the volume form
\omega_{X}^{n}
.
Such degenerate complex Hessian equations
have been intensively studied in the past fifteen years (see

[
Blo05
,
Lu13
,
DK14
,
DL15
,
LN15
,
Lu15
,
KN16
,
Char16
,
GN18
,
DDT19
,
BZ20
,
GP22
]
and the references therein).

In this note we go one step further by allowing the
form
\omega
to degenerate as well:
we no longer assume it is positive, we merely ask that it is
big
,
in analogy with the corresponding notion from algebraic geometry
[
BEGZ10
]
,
i.e. we assume there is an
\omega
-psh function
\rho

with analytic singularities such that

\omega+dd^{c}\rho
dominates a hermitian form.
We let
\Omega
denote the Zariski open set where
\rho
is smooth.

One can not expect any longer that
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions are smooth in
X
, but we
show in Section
1.2
how to define the Hessian operator

H_{k}(f)=(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}
for such functions

\varphi
which are
continuous
in
\Omega
and globally bounded on
X
.

In this context we obtain the following:

Theorem A
.

Let
\omega
be a semi-positive and big form
and
f\in L^{p}(\omega_{X}^{n})
with
p>n/k
and
\int_{X}f\omega_{X}^{n}>0
.
There exists
c>0
and
\varphi
a bounded
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic function, continuous in
\Omega
, such that

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=cf\omega_{X}^{n}.

This result extends the main result of
[
KN16
]
.
It applies in particular to the case when
\pi:X\rightarrow Y
is the desingularization
of a compact hermitian variety
Y
, and
\omega=\pi^{*}\omega_{Y}
is the pull-back
of a hermitian form
\omega_{Y}
on
Y
.

As is often the case in similar contexts, the main difficulty lies in establishing uniform a priori estimates.
This is the content of our second main result:

Theorem B
.

Let
\omega_{0},\omega
be semi-positive and big forms such that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega
.
Let
\varphi
be a smooth
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic function solution to

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=f\omega_{X}^{n},

where
f\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
,
p>n/k
.
Then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C,

where
C>0
depends on
\omega_{0},n,k,p
and an upper bound for

||f||_{L^{p}}
.

The approach of Kołodziej-Nguyen relies on a pluripotential theory for degenerate complex Hessian equations on hermitian manifolds (mimicking the corresponding results obtained by these authors
and Dinew for the Monge-Ampère equation
[
DK12
]
,
[
KN15
]
,
[
KN19
]
,
[
Ng16
]
).
An alternative PDE proof has been recently provided by Guo-Phong-Tong-Wang in
[
GPT21
,
GPTW21
]

when
X
is Kähler, and by Guo-Phong
[
GP22
]
when
X
is hermitian and
\omega
is a hermitian form.

We provide in this note a proof which relies directly on the corresponding results for the complex Monge-Ampère operator.
Given
\varphi
a solution of the
k
-Hessian equation with density
f
, we show that
the
\omega
-psh envelope of
\varphi
solves a Monge-Ampère equation whose density
is bounded from above by
f^{n/k}
.
A lower bound on
\varphi
thus follows from the
L^{\infty}
-a priori estimates for the Monge-Ampère
equation established in
[
GL21
]
.

Our method is new and also of interest when
X
is Kähler;
it applies to more general densities (see Remark
3.2
); in particular the
results of Theorem A and Theorem B
hold when
f
merely satisfies

\int_{X}f^{\frac{n}{k}}|\log f|^{n}(\log|\log f|)^{n+\delta}<+\infty,

for some
\delta>0
.
Radial examples show that this condition is almost optimal.

1.
Preliminaries

In the whole article we let
(X,\omega_{X})
denote a compact complex manifold
of complex dimension
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
equipped with a fixed hermitian form
\omega_{X}
. We use real differential operators
d=\partial+\bar{\partial}
,
d^{c}=i(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
, so that
dd^{c}=2i\partial\bar{\partial}
.

We also fix a semi-positive
(1,1)
-form
\omega
which is
big
:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
\omega
is big if there exists an
\omega
-psh function
\rho

such that
\omega+dd^{c}\rho
dominates a hermitian form.

It follows from Demailly’s regularization theorem
[
Dem94
]
that one can further assume that

\rho
has analytic singularities, in particular
\omega+dd^{c}\rho
is a
hermitian form in some Zariski open set
that we usually denote by
\Omega
in what follows.

An important source of examples of big forms is provided by the following construction:
if
V
is a compact complex space endowed with a hermitian form
\omega_{V}
, and

\pi:X\rightarrow V
is a resolution of singularities, then
\omega=\pi^{*}\omega_{V}
is big.

1.1.
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions

1.1.1.
Definition

Let
U\subset X
be an open subset and
\gamma
a hermitian form in
U
.
A function
h\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(U,\mathbb{R})
is called
\gamma
-harmonic if

dd^{c}h\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=0\;\text{pointwise in }\;U.

We let
H_{\gamma}(U)
denote the set of all
\gamma
-harmonic functions in
U
. A function
u:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\gamma
-subharmonic if it is upper semicontinuous and for every
D\subset U
and every
h\in H_{\gamma}(D)
the following implication holds

u\leq h\;\text{on}\;\partial D\Longrightarrow u\leq h\;\text{in}\;D.

As shown in
[
HL13
, Section 9]

the
\gamma
-subharmonicity can be equivalently defined using viscosity theory:

u
is
\gamma
-subharmonic in
U
if and only if the inequality
dd^{c}u\wedge\gamma^{n-1}
holds in the viscosity sense in
U
. The latter condition means that for every
x_{0}\in U
and
every
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function
\chi
defined in a neighborhood
V
of
x_{0}
,
the following implication holds:

\left[u(x)\leq\chi(x),\;\forall x\in V\;\text{and}\;u(x_{0})=\chi(x_{0})\right]\Longrightarrow dd^{c}\chi\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0\;\text{at}\;x_{0}.

When
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(U)
, it is
\gamma
-subharmonic if and only if
dd^{c}u\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0
pointwise.

Definition 1.2
.

We let
SH_{\gamma}(U)
denote the set of all
\gamma
-subharmonic functions in
U
that are locally integrable.

If
u\in SH_{\gamma}(U)
then, by
[
HH72
, Theorème 1, p.136]
,
dd^{c}u\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0
in the sense of distributions. Conversely, by
[
Lit83
, Theorem A]
, the latter condition implies that
u
coincides
a.e. with a unique function
\hat{u}\in SH_{\gamma}(U)
defined by

\hat{u}(x):={\rm ess}\limsup_{y\to x}u(y):=\lim_{r\searrow 0}{{\rm ess}\sup}_{B(x,r)}u,

where
{{\rm ess}\sup}_{B(x,r)}u
is the essential supremum of
u
in the ball
B(x,r)
.

Definition 1.3
.

A function
\varphi:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is quasi-
\gamma
-subharmonic if locally
\varphi=u+\rho
where
u
is
\gamma
-subharmonic and
\rho
is smooth.

We say that a function
\varphi
is
(\omega,\gamma)
-subharmonic if it is quasi-
\gamma
-subharmonic and
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0
in the sense of distributions on
X
.

We let
SH_{\gamma}(U,\omega)
denote the set of all
(\omega,\gamma)
-subharmonic functions in
U
.

If
u\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}(U,\mathbb{R})
then
u\in SH_{\gamma}(U,\omega)
if and only if
(\omega+dd^{c}u)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0
pointwise. In general this inequality holds in the viscosity sense:

Lemma 1.4
.

If
\varphi\in SH_{\gamma}(U,\omega)
and
\chi
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
function such that
\varphi-\chi
attains a local maximum at
x_{0}\in U
, then
(\omega+dd^{c}\chi)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0
at
x_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
a>1
. Let
\rho
be a quadratic function such that
dd^{c}\rho=a\omega(x_{0})
. By continuity of
\omega
, we have
dd^{c}\rho\geq\omega
in a small ball
B
around
x_{0}
. The function
\rho+\varphi
is then
\gamma
-subharmonic in
B
and the function
\rho+\varphi-(\rho+\chi)
attains a local maximum at
x_{0}
. It thus follows that, at
x_{0}
,

(a\omega+dd^{c}\chi)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}=dd^{c}(\rho+\chi)\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0.

The conclusion follows by letting
a\searrow 1
.
∎

Fix an integer
1\leq k\leq n
, and let
\Gamma_{k}(\omega_{X})
denote the set of all
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha
such that

\alpha^{j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-j}>0,\;j=1,...,k.

By Gårding’s inequality
[
Gar59
]
, if
\alpha,\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{k-1}\in\Gamma_{k}(\omega_{X})
then

\alpha\wedge\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}>0.

In particular,
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}
is a strictly positive
(n-1,n-1)
-form on
X
. By
[
Mic82
]
, one can write
\alpha_{1}\wedge...\wedge\alpha_{k-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=\gamma^{n-1}
, for some hermitian form
\gamma
. We let
F_{k}(\omega_{X})
denote the set of all such hermitian forms.

Definition 1.5
.

A function
\varphi:U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic if it is
(\omega,\gamma)
-subharmonic in
U
for all
\gamma\in F_{k}(\omega_{X})
.

We let
SH_{\omega_{X}}(U,\omega,k)
denote the set of all
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions on
U
which are locally integrable in
U
.

If
u
is of class
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
in
U
then, by
[
Gar59
]
,
u
is
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic if and only if
\omega+dd^{c}u\in\Gamma_{k}(\omega_{X})
.
We refer the reader to
[
Blo05
]
and
[
KN16
, Section 2.3]
for basic properties of
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions; they easily extend to our context.

Since
\omega_{X}
is fixed once and for all in this note, we
let
SH(X,\omega,k)
denote the set of
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions; they form a nested sequence

SH(X,\omega)\supset\cdots SH(X,\omega,k-1)\supset SH(X,\omega,k)\supset\cdots\supset PSH(X,\omega),

where
SH(X,\omega)=SH(X,\omega,1)
is the set of
\omega
-sh functions, while

PSH(X,\omega)

=SH(X,\omega,n)

is the set of
\omega
-psh functions, for which
\omega+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
is a positive
current of bidegree
(1,1)
(see
[
GZ17
, Chapter 8]
).

Observe that
SH(X,\omega,k)\subset SH(X,\omega^{\prime},k)
if
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
.
Since
\omega\leq A\omega_{X}
for some
A>0
, any
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh function is
also
(A\omega_{X},1)
-sh. We thus have the following compactness result
(see
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.3]
,
[
GN18
, Section 9]
).

Proposition 1.6
.

The set of normalized
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions is compact in the
L^{1}
-topology. In particular, there exists
C>0
such that for all
\varphi\in SH(X,\omega,k)
normalized by

\sup_{X}\varphi=0
,

\int_{X}(-\varphi)\omega_{X}^{n}\leq C.

1.1.2.
Smooth approximation

Theorem 1.7
.

If
\omega
is hermitian, any
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic function is the pointwise limit
of a decreasing sequence of smooth
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic functions.

This result is a slight extension of
[
KN16
, Lemma 3.20]
, which itself extends
[
LN15
, Theorem 1.2]
from the Kähler to the hermitian setting.
We propose below an alternative and more direct proof.

Proof.

Let
u
be a smooth function on
X
and let
f
be a smooth positive density such that

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq f\omega_{X}^{n}.

For
j\geq 1
, we solve the complex Hessian equation

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=e^{j(\varphi_{j}-u)}f\omega_{X}^{n}.

As shown in
[
KN16
]
, the estimates established in
[
Szek18
]

provide a smooth solution
\varphi_{j}
which is
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic on
X
.
We want to prove that
\varphi_{j}
converges uniformly to
P_{\omega,k}(u)
,
the largest
(\omega,k)
-subharmonic function lying below
u
.
The theorem follows from this fact, as
shown in
[
LN15
,
KN16
]
.

The classical maximum principle ensures that
\varphi_{j}\leq u
,
hence
\varphi_{j}\leq P_{\omega,k}(u)
.
Fix now
\varphi\in SH(X,\omega,k)
such that
\varphi\leq u
. Let
x_{0}\in X
be a point where the function
\varphi-(1+1/j)\varphi_{j}
attains its maximum on
X
. By definition
\varphi
belongs to
SH_{\gamma}(X,\omega)
, where
\gamma
is the hermitian form such that

\gamma^{n-1}=(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

From now on the computations are made at
x_{0}
.
It follows from Lemma
1.4
that

(\omega+(1+1/j)dd^{c}\varphi_{j})\wedge\gamma^{n-1}\geq 0.

From this and Gårding’s inequality
[
Gar59
]
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{j+1}{j}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{j}\omega\wedge(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{j}e^{\frac{j(k-1)}{k}(\varphi_{j}-u)}f^{(k-1)/k}g^{1/k}\omega_{X}^{n},

where
g\omega_{X}^{n}=\omega^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}
. Let
b\in(0,1)
be a positive constant such that
g\geq b^{k}f
on
X
. The above inequality yields

(1+j)e^{j(\varphi_{j}-u)/k}\geq b,

hence

\varphi_{j}-u\geq\frac{k(\log b-\log(1+j))}{j}.

Thus, for some uniform constant
C\geq 1
we have

\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)\varphi_{j}-u\geq\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)(\varphi_{j}-u)+\frac{u}{j}\geq-C\frac{\log j}{j}.

Since
\varphi\leq u
, we also obtain

\varphi-\left(1+\frac{1}{j}\right)\varphi_{j}\leq C\frac{\log j}{j}.

This inequality holds on
X
, as
\varphi-(1+1/j)\varphi_{j}
attains its global maximum at
x_{0}
.
Since this is true for all
\varphi\in SH(X,\omega,k)
lying below
u
, we obtain

P_{\omega,k}(u)-C^{\prime}\frac{\log j}{j}\leq\varphi_{j}\leq P_{\omega,k}(u),

for a uniform constant
C^{\prime}
,
proving that
\varphi_{j}
converges uniformly to
P_{\omega,k}(u)
.
∎

1.2.
The Hessian operator

1.2.1.
Hermitian forms

We assume in this subsection that
\omega
is a hermitian form.
For
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions
\varphi
of
class
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
, the complex Hessian operator is defined by

H_{k}(\varphi)=(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

By Theorem
1.7
, one can pointwise approximate from above any
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh function
\varphi
by a decreasing sequence of smooth
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions
\varphi_{j}
.

If
\varphi
is moreover continuous on
X
, then the convergence is uniform, and it was shown in
[
KN16
]
that the sequence of Hessian measures
H_{k}(\varphi_{j})
weakly converges to a unique positive Radon measure, independent of the approximants.
One sets

H_{k}(\varphi):=\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}H_{k}(\varphi_{j}):=(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

It is likely that one can define these operators for
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions
that are merely bounded. This is indeed the case when
\omega_{X}
is closed, as one can proceed by induction and use integration by parts following Bedford-Taylor’s construction
[
BT76
,
BT82
]
.
The situation is however delicate in the general hermitian setting, and more subtle than for the
Monge-Ampère operator.

Among the basic properties enjoyed by this operator are the following:

•

\varphi\mapsto H_{k}(\varphi)
is continuous for the uniform topology;

•

if
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}\in\mathcal{SH}(X,\omega,k)
are continuous, the mixed Hessian operators

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega+dd^{c}u_{k})\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}
are similarly defined and continuous;

•

if
u_{1},\ldots,u_{k}
are smooth
and
(\omega+dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=f_{j}dV_{X}
then

(1.1)

(\omega+dd^{c}u_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\omega+dd^{c}u_{k})\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq(f_{1}\cdots f_{k})^{1/k}dV_{X}.

These are a consequence of inequalities due to Gårding.

1.2.2.
Big forms

We now no longer assume that
\omega
is positive, but rather that it is semi-positive and big. Fix a quasi-plurisubharmonic function
\rho:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
with analytic singularities such that
\omega+dd^{c}\rho\geq\delta\omega_{X}
, for some constant
\delta>0
.
We let
\Omega
denote the Zariski open set where
\rho
is smooth.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{SH}(X,\omega,k)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
is continuous in
\Omega
,
it follows from
[
KN16
, Proposition 2.9]
that
for each smooth form
\gamma
, the mixed product

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\gamma^{l-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-l}

is a well-defined Radon measure in
\Omega
with finite mass.

Definition 1.8
.

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{SH}(X,\omega,k)\cap L^{\infty}(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(\Omega)
we set

H_{k}(\varphi):=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge(\omega-\omega_{X})^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

This operator is well-defined in
\Omega
with finite total mass,
so we consider its trivial extension to
X
.
The definition is motivated by the identity

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi+\omega-\omega_{X})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge(\omega-\omega_{X})^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

The operator
H_{k}
is continuous along sequences that converge locally uniformly in
\Omega

[
KN16
, Proposition 2.11]
. The following result follows.

Proposition 1.9
.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{SH}(X,\omega,k)\cap L^{\infty}(X)\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(\Omega)
,
then
H_{k}(\varphi)
is a positive Radon measure.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and approximate
\varphi

-using Theorem
1.7
- by a decreasing sequence
(\varphi_{j})
of
(\omega+\varepsilon\omega_{X})
-sh functions.
Since
(\omega+\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq 0
,
we infer from
[
KN16
, Proposition 2.11]
that

(\omega+\varepsilon\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq 0
.
The conclusion follows by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, as we then obtain that
(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq 0
.
∎

Lemma 1.10
.

Let
\varphi,\psi
be
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions which are continuous in
\Omega

and bounded on
X
. Then

H_{k}(\max(\varphi,\psi))\geq{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi>\psi\}}H_{k}(\varphi)+{\bf 1}_{\{\psi\geq\varphi\}}H_{k}(\psi).

In particular, if
\varphi\leq\psi
then

1_{\{\psi=\varphi\}}H_{k}(\varphi)\leq 1_{\{\psi=\varphi\}}H_{k}(\psi).

Proof.

Fix
t>0
.
Since
\{\varphi<\psi+t\}
and
\{\psi+t<\varphi\}
are open in
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle H_{k}(\max(\varphi,\psi+t))

\displaystyle\geq{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi>\psi+t\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}+{\bf 1}_{\{\psi+t>\varphi\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle\geq{\bf 1}_{\{\varphi>\psi+t\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}+{\bf 1}_{\{\psi\geq\varphi\}}(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

Letting
t\searrow 0
we obtain the first statement of the lemma.
The second one follows straightforwardly from the first.
∎

1.2.3.

-envelopes

We consider two types of envelopes in this note.

Definition 1.11
.

If

•

•

If

is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth and
\omega
is hermitian, then

P_{\omega}(h)
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth and

•

If

is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth and
\omega
is hermitian, then

P_{\omega,k}(h)
is
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
-smooth and

(\omega+dd^{c}P_{\omega,k}(h))^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}={\bf 1}_{{\mathcal{C}_{k}}}(\omega+dd^{c}h)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

]

1.3.
A priori estimates for the complex Monge-Ampère operator

We are going to use the following a priori estimates established
in
[
GL21
]
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
\omega,\omega_{0}
be semi-positive and big
(1,1)
-forms such that
\omega\geq\omega_{0}
.
Fix
\delta>0
and

g\in L^{1+\delta}(dV_{X})

a non-negative density.
If
\varphi\in PSH(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
satisfies

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}\leq gdV_{X}
, then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
that depends on

n,\delta,\omega_{0}
and an upper bound on
||g||_{L^{1+\delta}}
.

Since this result is not explicitly stated in
[
GL21
]
, we
briefly sketch its proof.

Proof.

We normalize
\varphi
so that
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
. It follows from
Skoda’s uniform integrability theorem

[
GZ17
, Theorem 8.11]
that for
0<\alpha
small enough,

\int_{X}e^{-\alpha\frac{(2+\delta)(1+\delta)}{\delta}\varphi}dV_{X}\leq C_{\alpha}

is bounded from above independently of
\varphi
.
Setting
\tilde{g}=e^{-\alpha\varphi}g
,
Hölder inequality then shows that

\tilde{g}\in L^{1+\delta/2}
with

||\tilde{g}||_{L^{1+\delta/2}}\leq C_{\alpha}^{1/q}||g||_{L^{1+\delta}}^{1/p},

where
p=\frac{1+\delta}{1+\delta/2}
and
q=2+2/\delta
.

[
GL21
, Lemma 3.3]
shows the existence of

c(\delta,\omega)>0
and
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
such that
-1\leq u\leq 0
and

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq c(\delta,\omega)\frac{\tilde{g}dV_{X}}{||\tilde{g}||_{L^{1+\delta/2}}}\geq e^{\alpha u}c(\delta,\omega)\frac{\tilde{g}dV_{X}}{||\tilde{g}||_{L^{1+\delta/2}}}.

The function
v:=u-\alpha^{-1}\log[c(\delta,\omega)/||\tilde{g}||_{L^{1+\delta/2}}]\in PSH(X,\omega)

thus satisfies

e^{-\alpha v}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{n}=e^{-\alpha v}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq\tilde{g}dV_{X}\geq e^{-\alpha\varphi}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}.

The comparison principle
[
GL21
, Corollary 1.14]
finally ensures that
v\leq\varphi
.

This provides a uniform lower bound on
\varphi
, which only depends on

\delta,\omega
and an upper bound on
||g||_{L^{1+\delta}}
.
∎

2.
Domination principle

Fix
\omega
a big form,
\rho
an
\omega
-plurisubharmonic function with analytic singularities such that
\omega+dd^{c}\rho\geq\delta\omega_{X}
,
\delta>0
,
and set
\Omega:=\{\rho>-\infty\}
.

We fix a constant
B_{1}>0
such that for all
x\in\Omega
,

-B_{1}\omega_{\rho}^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega_{\rho}\leq B_{1}\omega_{\rho}^{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;-B_{1}\omega_{\rho}^{3}\leq d\omega_{\rho}\wedge d^{c}\omega_{\rho}\leq B_{1}\omega_{\rho}^{3}.

The existence of
B_{1}
is clear since
d\omega=d\omega_{\rho}
,

d^{c}\omega=d^{c}\omega_{\rho}
and

-B\omega_{X}^{2}\leq dd^{c}\omega\leq B\omega_{X}^{2}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;-B\omega_{X}^{3}\leq d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\leq B\omega_{X}^{3}

for some
B>0
.
The following is an extension of
[
GL21
, Theorem 1.11]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume
\omega
is big. If
u
is a bounded

(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic function which is continuous in
\Omega
, then
\int_{X}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}>0
.

Proof.

Fix
\rho
and
B_{1}
as above. We set

m=\inf_{\Omega}(u-\rho)
. For
s>0
we set

\phi=\max(u,\rho+m+s)\;\;\text{ and }\;\;U:=\{u<\phi\}=\{u<\rho+m+s\}.

Observe that
U
is relatively compact in

\Omega
, and it is non empty for all
s>0
, by definition of
m
.
It follows from
[
KN16
, Corollary 2.4]
that, for any
0\leq j\leq k-1
,

dd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k})\leq C_{1}\sum_{m=k-2}^{k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{m}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{m-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-m},

where
C_{1}
is a uniform constant.
On the open set
U
since
\omega_{\phi}\geq\delta\omega_{X}
, we thus have

(2.1)

dd^{c}(\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j-1}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k})\leq C_{2}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

Since
u\leq\phi
, Lemma
1.10
ensures that

{\bf 1}_{\{u=\phi\}}\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq{\bf 1}_{\{u=\phi\}}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{n-k}.

Noting that
X\setminus U=\{u=\phi\}
we can write

\displaystyle\int_{U}(\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}-\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}(\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}-\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k})

\displaystyle=\int_{X}dd^{c}(\phi-u)\wedge\sum_{l=0}^{k-j}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}(\phi-u)dd^{c}\left[\sum_{l=0}^{k-j}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\right].

In the last line above, we have used Stokes theorem.
Since
0\leq\phi-u\leq s
, using (
2.1
) we can continue the above inequalities as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{U}(\omega_{u}^{j}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}-\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k})

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}s\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{U}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

Summing up the above inequalities for
j=0,...,k
, one gets

(1-C_{4}s)\sum_{l=0}^{k}\int_{U}\omega_{u}^{l}\wedge\omega_{\phi}^{k-l}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq(k+1)\int_{U}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

If
\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=0
then, for
s>0
small enough, the above inequality yields
\int_{U}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=0
, hence
U
has Lebesgue measure
0
, which is impossible.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume
\omega
is hermitian,
u,v
are continuous
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions
such that
\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=0
on
\{u<v\}
. Then
u\geq v
.

Proof.

By replacing
v
with
\max(u,v)
and noting that
\{u<v\}=\{u<\max(u,v)\}
, we can assume that
u\leq v
. Our plan is thus to show that
u=v
.

For
b\geq 1
, we set
\phi_{b}:=P_{\omega,k}(bu-(b-1)v)
. It is a negative
(\omega,k)
-sh function. Since
bu-(b-1)v
is continuous on
X
, so is
\phi_{b}
. Observe also that

b^{-1}\phi_{b}+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u

with equality on the contact set
\mathcal{C}:=\{\phi_{b}=bu-(b-1)v\}
.
Now by Lemma
1.10

{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(b^{-k}\omega_{\phi_{b}}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}+(1-b^{-1})^{k}\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\right)\leq{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k},

while
\int_{\mathcal{C}}H_{k}(\phi_{b})=\int_{X}H_{k}(\phi_{b})>0
by Theorem
2.1
.
Since
H_{k}(u)=0
on
\{u<v\}
, it follows that
\mathcal{C}\cap\{u=v\}
contains at least one point, say
x
. We then have

\phi_{b}(x)=bu(x)-(b-1)v(x)=v(x)\geq\inf_{X}v;

in particular
\sup_{X}\phi_{b}\geq\inf_{X}v
.

Note that
\phi_{b}
is decreasing in
b
since
u\leq v
. The limit
\phi:=\lim_{b\to+\infty}\phi_{b}
is a
(\omega,k)
-sh function which is not identically
-\infty
. For any
a>0
,

\phi_{b}\leq bu-(b-1)v\leq v-ba

on the open set
\{u<v-a\}
, thus
\phi=-\infty
on this set. Since
\phi
is integrable on
X
, the set
\{u<v-a\}
must be empty, hence letting
a\to 0^{+}
gives
u\geq v
.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume
\omega
is hermitian,
u
and
v
are continuous
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions such that
\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq c\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
on
\{u<v\}
for some
c\in[0,1)
. Then
u\geq v
.

Proof.

Take
b
so large that
(1-b^{-1})^{k}\geq c
, and consider
\phi:=P_{\omega,k}(bu-(b-1)v)
. Then
b^{-1}\phi+(1-b^{-1})v\leq u
with equality on the contact set

\mathcal{C}:=\{\phi=bu-(b-1)v\}.

By Lemma
1.10
we have

{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}\left(b^{-k}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}+(1-b^{-1})^{k}\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\right)\leq{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}.

Multiplying with
{\bf 1}_{\{u<v\}}
and using the assumption
\omega_{u}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}\leq c\omega_{v}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
on
\{u<v\}
and
(1-b^{-1})^{k}\geq c
, we then see that

{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}\cap\{u<v\}}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0.

Since
\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}
is supported on
\mathcal{C}
, we thus have

\int_{\{\phi<v\}}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{\{bu-(b-1)v<v\}}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=\int_{\{u<v\}}\omega_{\phi}^{k}\wedge\omega^{n-k}=0.

Invoking the domination principle (Theorem
2.2
) we then get
\phi\geq v
. It thus follows that
bu-(b-1)v\geq\phi\geq v
, hence
u\geq v
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Assume
\omega
is hermitian and
u
and
v
are continuous

(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions.
Then

e^{-v}(\omega+dd^{c}v)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{-u}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\Longrightarrow u\geq v.

Proof.

Fix
a>0
. On the set
\{u<v-a\}
we have
H_{k}(u)\leq e^{-a}H_{k}(v)
. By Corollary
2.3
,
u\geq v-a
. Since it holds for all

.
∎

Proof of the main theorems

3.1.
A priori estimates

In this section we prove Theorem B,
which is an extension of the main result of Kołodziej-Nguyen
[
KN16
]
:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\omega_{0},\omega
be semi-positive and big forms such that
\omega_{0}\leq\omega
.
Let
\varphi
be a smooth
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic function satisfying

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq fdV_{X},

where
f\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
,
p>n/k
.
Then

{\rm Osc}_{X}(\varphi)\leq C,

where
C>0
depends on
\omega_{0},n,k,p
and an upper bound for

||f||_{L^{p}}
.

Proof.

We can assume that

\sup_{X}\varphi=0
.
Let
u:=P_{\omega}(\varphi)
be the largest
\omega
-psh function lying below
\varphi
.
Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the contact set
\mathcal{C}:=\{u=\varphi\}
.
Then
u
is
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh with
u\leq\varphi

hence Lemma
1.10
yields

{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq{\bf 1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq fdV_{X}.

Observe that the smooth function
h:=\omega_{X}^{n}/dV_{X}\geq\delta_{0}
is positive on
X
.
Recall that the Monge-Ampère measure
(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}
of
u
is concentrated on the contact set

{\mathcal{C}}
. We set
g:=(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}/dV_{X}
.
It follows from Gårding’s inequalities (
1.1
) that

\delta_{0}^{1-k/n}g^{k/n}\leq h^{1-k/n}g^{k/n}\leq f,

hence
g\leq C_{0}f^{n/k}
. Since
f\in L^{p}
with
p>n/k
,
we obtain
g\in L^{1+\delta}
with
\delta>0
.
Theorem
1.12

therefore provides a uniform bound

{\rm Osc}_{X}(u)\leq M.

This provides a uniform bound on
u\leq 0
if we can bound
\sup_{X}u
from below.

Let
q^{\prime}
be the conjugate exponent to
p^{\prime}=pk/n>1
.
We obtain a uniform lower bound on
\sup_{X}u

by using
[
GL22
, Proposition 3.4]
and
Hölder inequality, via

\displaystyle v_{M}^{-}(\omega)(-\sup_{X}u)^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|u|^{1/q^{\prime}}(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|\varphi|\omega^{n}\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\left(\int_{X}f^{p^{\prime}}\omega^{n}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C,

as
\int_{X}|\varphi|\omega^{n}
is bounded from above independently of
\varphi
by Proposition
1.6
.
Together with the uniform bound for
{\rm Osc}_{X}(u)
, this yields a uniform bound
for
|u|
. This implies a uniform bound for
\varphi
since
u\leq\varphi
and
\sup_{X}\varphi=0
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We also have a uniform a priori bound
when
f
satisfies the weaker integrability condition

\int_{X}f^{n/k}|\log f|^{n}\left|h\circ\log\circ\log f\right|^{n}dt<+\infty

where
h
is an increasing continuous function such that
\int^{+\infty}dt/h(t)<+\infty
.
It follows indeed that
g
satisfies then Kołodziej’s optimal integrability condition

\int_{X}g|\log g|^{n}\left|h\circ\log\circ\log g\right|^{n}dt<+\infty,

which ensures a uniform bound on

by
[
Kol98

.
We actually need here the extension of Kołodziej’s result to

EGZ09
]
, as well as to the

[
DK12
,
GL21
]
.

The same proof thus applies with a minor modification: to obtain a lower bound for

3.2.
Stability estimates

We strengthen Theorem B by establishing the following stability estimate:

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
\omega
is hermitian and
\omega\geq\omega_{0}
for some semi-positive and big form
\omega_{0}
.
Assume
0\leq f,g\in L^{p}(dV_{X})
with
p>n/k
,
\varphi,\psi

are continuous
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-subharmonic functions such that

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{\varphi}f\omega_{X}^{n},\;\;\text{ and }\;\;(\omega+dd^{c}\psi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\leq e^{\psi}g\omega_{X}^{n}.

Then

(3.1)

\varphi\leq\psi+C\|f-g\|_{p}^{1/k}

for some
C>0
depending on
\omega_{0},n,k
and upper bounds
for
||f||_{p},\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
,
|\inf_{X}\psi|
.

In particular if
\varphi,\psi
are solutions of the corresponding equations, then

||\varphi-\psi||_{\infty}\leq C\|f-g\|_{p}^{1/k},

so that
\varphi-\psi
is uniformly small if
f
is close to
g
in
L^{p}
.

Proof.

Our proof uses a perturbation argument from
[
GLZ18
,
LPT21
]
, which goes back to
[
Kol96
]
.
We solve the complex Monge-Ampère equation

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}=bh\omega_{X}^{n},\;\;\text{ where }\;\;h=\frac{|f-g|^{n/k}}{\|f-g\|_{p}^{n/k}}+1,

\sup_{X}u=0
and
b>0
is a constant.
The existence of a continuous solution
u\in PSH(X,\omega)
follows from
[
KN15
,
KN19
]
.

We can assume without loss of generality that
\omega_{X}
is a Gauduchon metric
(i.e.
dd^{c}(\omega_{X}^{n-1})=0
) which dominates
\omega
.
Thus
u
is
\omega_{X}
-psh with

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{n}\geq b\omega_{X}^{n}

since
h\geq 1
.
The mixed Monge-Ampère inequalities (see
[
Ng16
]
) yield

(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}\geq b^{1/n}\omega_{X}^{n},

hence
b\leq 1
since
\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}u)\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}=\int_{X}\omega_{X}^{n}

by the Gauduchon condition.

Since
h\in L^{q}
with
q=pk/n>1
and
b\leq 1
,
Theorem
3.1
ensures that
\|u\|_{\infty}
is uniformly bounded.
It thus follows from
[
GL22
, Proposition 3.4]
that
b\geq\delta_{0}>0

is uniformly bounded from below.

If
\|f-g\|_{p}^{1/k}
is not small, the stability inequality (
3.1
) is trivially
obtained by adjusting the value of
C
. In the remainder of the proof, we can thus assume without
loss of generality that

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon:=b^{-1/n}\|f-g\|_{p}^{1/k}e^{\sup_{X}\varphi/k}\leq 1/2.

By the mixed Monge-Ampère inequalities (
1.1
) we have

(\omega+dd^{c}u)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq b^{k/n}\frac{|f-g|}{\|f-g\|_{p}}\omega_{X}^{n}.

We set
C_{1}=2k-\inf_{X}\varphi
and

\psi_{\varepsilon}:=(1-\varepsilon)\varphi+\varepsilon u-C_{1}\varepsilon,

The function
\psi_{\varepsilon}
belongs to
SH(X,\omega,k)\cap{\mathcal{C}}^{0}(X)
;
its Hessian measure satisfies

\displaystyle(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)^{k}e^{\varphi}f\omega_{X}^{n}+\varepsilon^{k}b^{k/n}\frac{|f-g|}{\|f-g\|_{p}}\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[(1-\varepsilon)^{k}e^{\varphi}f+e^{\sup_{X}\varphi}|f-g|\right]\omega_{X}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle e^{\varphi-2k\varepsilon}g\omega_{X}^{n},

using (
3.2
),
|f-g|+f\geq g
and
the inequality
\log(1-\varepsilon)\geq-2\varepsilon
valid on
[0,1/2]
.

Observe now that
\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq\varphi-\varepsilon\varphi-C_{1}\varepsilon\leq\varphi-2k\varepsilon

by the definition of
C_{1}
and
u\leq 0
. We infer

(\omega+dd^{c}\psi_{\varepsilon})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{\psi_{\varepsilon}}g\omega_{X}^{n},

hence
\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq\psi
by Corollary
2.4
.
This yields

\varphi\leq\psi+\varepsilon(\varphi-u)+C_{1}\varepsilon\leq\psi+C_{2}||f-g||_{p}^{1/k},

for some uniform constant
C_{2}
, as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem A

We approximate
f

For

\omega_{j}=\omega+2^{-j}\omega_{X}
.
It follows from

\varphi_{j}

such that

(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=c_{j}f_{j}\omega_{X}^{n},

and
\sup_{X}\varphi_{j}=0
.

We claim that the constants
c_{j}
stay uniformly bounded,
as
j
increases to
+\infty
. To prove the claim we use the solutions to the Monge-Ampère equations by Tosatti-Weinkove
[
TW10
]
:

(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}u_{j})^{n}=b_{j}f_{j}^{n/k}\omega_{X}^{n}.

Set
p^{\prime}=pk/n>1
. Since the
L^{p^{\prime}}
norm of
f_{j}^{n/k}
is uniformly bounded from above, by
[
GL21
, Lemma 3.3]
, there exists a constant
c>0
independent of
j
and a bounded
\omega
-psh function
u
such that
\omega_{u}^{n}\geq cf_{j}^{n/k}
. By
[
GL21
, Corollary 1.13]
,
b_{j}\geq c
. It then follows from Gårding’s inequality that

(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}u_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq c^{k/n}f_{j}\omega_{X}^{n}.

Corollary
2.3
thus ensures that
c_{j}\geq c^{k/n}
is uniformly bounded from below by a positive constant.

We next bound
c_{j}
from above. By Gårding’s inequality we have

(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-1}\geq c_{j}^{1/k}f_{j}^{1/k}\omega_{X}^{n}.

The functions
\varphi_{j}
belong to
SH(X,\omega_{1})
and are relatively compact in
L^{1}
, hence integrating over
X
and using that
\int_{X}f_{j}^{1/k}\omega_{X}^{n}\to\int_{X}f^{1/k}\omega_{X}^{n}>0
, we obtain a uniform upper bound for
c_{j}
.

Since
\omega\leq\omega_{j}
and
||c_{j}f_{j}||_{L^{p}}
is uniformly bounded from above,
it follows from Theorem
3.1
that the
\varphi_{j}
’s are uniformly bounded:

-C_{1}\leq\varphi_{j}\leq 0,

for a uniform constant
C_{1}
.
Extracting and using compactness in
L^{1}(X)
of normalized
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh functions,
we can thus assume that
c_{j}\rightarrow c>0
and
\varphi_{j}
converges in
L^{1}(X)
and almost everywhere
to a bounded function
\varphi
which is
(\omega,\omega_{X},k)
-sh, as
j\to+\infty
.

We next prove that the convergence
\varphi_{j}\to\varphi
is locally uniform in
\Omega
.
We set
g_{j}:=c_{j}f_{j}e^{-\varphi_{j}}
and
g=cfe^{-\varphi}
.
By Hölder inequality,
\|g_{j}-g\|_{q}\to 0
for some
q>n/k
.
Fix
j<l
large enough. Since
\omega_{j}\geq\omega_{l}
, we have

(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{l})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{\varphi_{l}}g_{l}\omega_{X}^{n}.

By Theorem
3.3
, we infer

(3.3)

\varphi_{l}\leq\varphi_{j}+C_{2}\|g_{j}-g_{l}\|_{q}^{1/k},

for a uniform constant
C_{2}
. We next consider

\psi_{j}:=(1-\varepsilon)\varphi_{j}+\varepsilon\rho-(2k+C_{1})\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon=\delta^{-1}2^{-j}<1/2
when
j
is large enough. Since
\omega+dd^{c}\rho\geq\delta\omega_{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\omega_{l}+dd^{c}\psi_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\omega+\varepsilon dd^{c}\rho+(1-\varepsilon)dd^{c}\varphi_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\delta\omega_{X}+(1-\varepsilon)(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j})-2^{-j}\omega_{X}

\displaystyle=(1-\varepsilon)(\omega_{j}+dd^{c}\varphi_{j}).

It follows that
\psi_{j}\in SH(X,\omega_{l},k)
and

(\omega_{l}+dd^{c}\psi_{j})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{\varphi_{j}+k\log(1-\varepsilon)}g_{j}\omega_{X}^{n}\geq e^{\varphi_{j}-2k\varepsilon}g_{j}\omega_{X}^{n}\geq e^{\psi_{j}}g_{j}\omega_{X}^{n}

holds in
\Omega
. The function
\psi_{j,l}:=\max(\psi_{j},\varphi_{l})
is
(\omega_{l},k)
-subharmonic and continuous on
X
. Lemma
1.10
yields

(\omega_{l}+dd^{c}\psi_{j,l})^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}\geq e^{\psi_{j,l}}\min(g_{j},g_{l})\omega_{X}^{n}.

Applying Theorem
3.3
we then get

\psi_{j,l}\leq\varphi_{l}+C_{3}\|\min(g_{j},g_{l})-g_{l}\|_{q}^{1/k}\leq\varphi_{l}+C_{3}\|g_{j}-g_{l}\|_{q}^{1/k},

for a uniform constant
C_{3}
. Together with (
3.3
), this implies

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}+\delta^{-1}2^{-j}\rho-(2k+C_{1})\delta^{-1}2^{-j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{l}+C_{3}\|g_{j}-g_{l}\|_{q}^{1/k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}+C_{1}\|g_{j}-g_{l}\|_{q}^{1/k}.

Therefore,
\varphi_{j}
converges locally uniformly in
\Omega
to
\varphi
.
Thus
\varphi
is continuous in
\Omega
where it solves the complex Hessian equation

(\omega+dd^{c}\varphi)^{k}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-k}=cf\omega_{X}^{n}.

Remark 3.4
.

When the form
\omega
is hermitian,
the solution
\varphi
that we construct is continuous on the whole of
X
.
Continuity of
\varphi
at points where
\omega
vanishes
is a delicate issue, even in the case of complex Monge-Ampère equations.
We refer the interested reader to
[
GGZ23
]
for a recent account.

The uniqueness of
\varphi
is also a subtle problem in the hermitian setting,
see
[
KN19
]
for a partial result in the case of the complex Monge-Ampère equation.

The asymptotic distribution of the remainder in a certain base- β \beta expansion
I. W. Herbst, J. Møller, A. M. Svane

Abstract
Let X = ∑ k = 1 ∞ X k ​ β − k X=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}X_{k}\beta^{-k} be the base- β \beta expansion of a continuous random variable X X on the unit interval where β \beta is the golden ratio. We study the asymptotic distribution and convergence rate of the scaled remainder ∑ k = n + 1 ∞ X k ​ β n − k \sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}X_{k}\beta^{n-k} when n n tends to infinity.

Introduction

Let
X
be a random variable so that
0\leq X<1
, and for
\beta>1
consider its
base-
\beta
expansion

X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\beta^{-n}

where its
n
-th digit
X_{n}
is defined as follows. For
x\in[0,1)
, let
T_{\beta}(x)=x\beta-\lfloor x\beta\rfloor
be the base-
\beta
transformation where
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
is the floor function, and let
T_{\beta}^{n}=T_{\beta}\circ\cdots\circ T_{\beta}
denote the composition of
T_{\beta}
with itself
n
times. Then

X_{n}=\lfloor\beta T_{\beta}^{n-1}(X)\rfloor
where
T_{\beta}^{0}(X)=X
. Furthermore, let

P_{n}
denote the law of
T_{\beta}^{n}(X)=\beta^{n}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}X_{k}\beta^{-k}
, where we interpret
\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}X_{k}\beta^{-k}
as ‘the
n
-th remainder if we keep the
n
first digits’. In
[
3
]
we studied the convergence properties of
P_{n}
when
\beta
is an integer
\beta=q\geq 2
:
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we proved that under mild conditions
P_{n}
converges in total variation distance to the uniform measure on
[0,1]
and under certain smoothness conditions it converges with rate
q^{-n}
. It is natural to ask if such results extend to the case where
\beta>1
is not an integer.
Base-
\beta

expansions where
\beta>1
is not an integer are of interest
in symbolic dynamics, see
[
1
,
5
]
.

In the present paper we consider the special case where
\beta=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is the golden ratio.
We show that when the distribution of
X
is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure,
P_{n}
converges in the total variation distance to the absolutely continuous probability measure
P_{\beta}
on
[0,1]
with probability density function (pdf)

f_{\beta}(x)=\begin{cases}(1+\beta)/\sqrt{5}&\text{if }0\leq x<\beta^{-1},\\
\beta/\sqrt{5}&\text{if }\beta^{-1}\leq x<1.\end{cases}

(1)

It is well-known that
P_{\beta}
is the unique absolutely continuous distribution which is
invariant under
T_{\beta}
, since
T_{\beta}
is ergodic with respect to Lebesgue measure, see e.g.
[
2
]
, where an equivalent expression for
f_{\beta}
is given. Hence this result is analogous to the ones in
[
3
]
.
Furthermore, we verify that when the pdf of
X
is differentiable with bounded derivative, the convergence rate is exponential, but with a less obvious exponent than in
[
3
]
. Also the proofs become more complicated when
\beta
is the golden ratio because the way that base-
\beta
expansions correspond to nested partitions of
[0,1)
is more complex, as detailed in Section
2
. The proof of the main result (Theorem
3.3
in Section
3
) exploits the nice properties of the golden ratio including its relation with the Fibonacci numbers.

We leave the following open
problems for future research. For cases where
\beta>1
is neither an integer nor the golden ratio it may be interesting to study the convergence properties of
P_{n}
, but partitions of
[0,1)
corresponding to the base-
\beta
expansion will be even more complicated than if
\beta
is the golden ratio or an integer greater than or equal to 2, see
[
4
]
. Another, possibly more important, case
of interest is obtained by defining

T(x)=1/x-\lfloor 1/x\rfloor
and considering the
n
-th remainder
T^{n}(X)=1/(X_{n}+1/(X_{n+1}+....))

obtained from
the continued fraction expansion

X=1/(X_{1}+1/(X_{2}+...))
where

X_{n}=\lfloor T^{n-1}(X)\beta\rfloor
.

2
Some background on
\beta
-expansions

For the remainder of this paper, let
\beta=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
. For any number
x\in[0,1)
,
T_{\beta}
defines a
\beta
-expansion
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}j_{n}\beta^{-n}
where the digits
j_{n}\in\{0,1\}
satisfy
j_{n}=\lfloor\beta T_{\beta}^{n-1}(x)\rfloor
,
n=1,2,\ldots
In fact there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\beta
-expansions of of this form and the set of digit sequences

\Omega=\{(j_{1},j_{2},...)\,|\,j_{n}\in\{0,1\}\mbox{ and }j_{n}j_{n+1}=0\mbox{ for }n=1,2,...\},

see
[
6
, Section 2.2]
. In this section, we introduce some more notation and prove two lemmas that will be needed for the main result in Section
3
.

For
n=1,2,...
, let

\Omega_{n}=\{(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\{0,1\}^{n}\,|\,j_{k}j_{k+1}=0\mbox{ for }1\leq k<n\}

which corresponds to the set of base-
\beta
fractions
\sum_{k=1}^{n}j_{k}\beta^{-k}
of order at most
n
and where
(j_{1},...,j_{n},0,0,...)\in\Omega
.
For each
J=(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
, define
L_{n,J}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}j_{k}\beta^{-k}
. Denote by
\prec
the lexicographic order on
\Omega_{n}
and let
\hat{J}=(\hat{j}_{1},...,\hat{j}_{n})
be the maximal element of
\Omega_{n}
, that is,
\hat{j}_{k}=1
if
k
is odd and
\hat{j}_{k}=0
if
k
is even.
Finally, define the intervals

I_{n,J}=I_{j_{1},...,j_{n}}=[L_{n,J},L_{n,J}+j_{n}\beta^{-n-1}+(1-j_{n})\beta^{-n}),\quad J=(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}.

The following lemma shows that the usual ordering of the numbers
L_{n,J}
with
J\in\Omega_{n}
agrees with that induced by
\prec
via the bijective mapping
J\mapsto L_{n,J}
. Moreover, the intervals
I_{n,J}
form a disjoint partition of
[0,1)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have

L_{n,\hat{J}}+\hat{j}_{n}\beta^{-n-1}+(1-\hat{j}_{n})\beta^{-n}=1.

(2)

Furthermore, for every
J=(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
with
J\prec\hat{J}
, if
J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}
is the smallest element with
J\prec J^{\prime}
then

L_{n,J^{\prime}}=L_{n,J}+j_{n}\beta^{-n-1}+(1-j_{n})\beta^{-n}<1.

(3)

Proof.

Since
\beta^{2}=\beta+1
, or equivalently
\beta(1-\beta^{-2})=1
,
L_{n,\hat{J}}
is equal to

\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n/2}\beta^{-2i+1}=[{1-\beta^{-n}}]/[{\beta(1-\beta^{-2})}]=1-\beta^{-n}&\text{if }n\text{ is even},\\
\sum_{i=1}^{(n+1)/2}\beta^{-2i+1}=[{1-\beta^{-n-1}}]/[{\beta(1-\beta^{-2})}]=1-\beta^{-n-1}&\text{if }n\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Thereby (
2
) follows.

Let
J=(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
with
J\prec\hat{J}
, and define
J^{\prime}=(j_{1}^{\prime},...,j_{n}^{\prime})
as in Lemma
2.1
. Let
j_{k}
be the first digit where
J
and
J^{\prime}
differ and
J_{k}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{k})
. Then we must have
j_{k-1}=j_{k}=0
and
j_{k}^{\prime}=1
. Since
J^{\prime}
is minimal with
J\prec J^{\prime}
, we must have that
j_{k-1}
and
j_{k}
is the last time two zeros appear in a row in
J
and that all digits following
j_{k}^{\prime}
in
J^{\prime}
are zero. Hence, if
n-k
is even, then
j_{k}=0
implies
j_{n}=0
and

\displaystyle L_{n,J}{}

\displaystyle=L_{k,J_{k}}+\sum_{i=0}^{(n-k-2)/2}\beta^{-(k+1)-2i}=L_{k,J_{k}}+\beta^{-k}-\beta^{-n}

\displaystyle L_{n,J^{\prime}}{}

\displaystyle=L_{k,J_{k}}+\beta^{-k}.

Thus, (
3
) holds when
n-k
is even.
If
n-k
is odd, then
j_{n}=1
and

\displaystyle L_{n,J}{}

\displaystyle=L_{k,J_{k}}+\sum_{i=0}^{(n-k-1)/2}\beta^{-(k+1)-2i}=L_{k,J_{k}}+\beta^{-k}-\beta^{-n-1}

\displaystyle L_{n,J^{\prime}}{}

\displaystyle=L_{k,J_{k}}+\beta^{-k}.

This shows (
3
) when
n-k
is odd.
∎

For
j=0,1
, define the set
\Omega_{n,j}=\{(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}\,|\,j_{n}=j\}
and its cardinality
N_{j}(n)=|\Omega_{n,j}|
,
so
N(n)=|\Omega_{n}|
is given by
N(n)=N_{0}(n)+N_{1}(n)
. Lemma
2.2
below determines these cardinalities
in terms of the Fibonacci
sequence given by
b_{0}=0
,
b_{1}=1
, and
b_{n}=b_{n-1}+b_{n-2}
for
n=2,3,...
Recall that

b_{n}=[{\beta^{n}-(-\beta)^{-n}}]/{\sqrt{5}},\quad n=0,1,2,...

(4)

Lemma 2.2
.

For
n=1,2,...
, we have

N_{1}(n)=b_{n},\quad N_{0}(n)=b_{n+1},\quad N(n)=b_{n+2}.

(5)

Proof.

We have
N_{0}(1)=N_{1}(1)=N_{1}(2)=1
and
N_{0}(2)=2
. Let
(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
with
n>1
.
If
j_{n-1}=1
then
j_{n}=0
and
I_{j_{1},...,j_{n-1}}=I_{j_{1},...,j_{n}}
is unchanged, whilst if
j_{n-1}=0
then
I_{j_{1},...,j_{n-1}}
splits into
I_{j_{1},...,j_{n-1},0}
and
I_{j_{1},...,j_{n-1},1}
. Therefore,
N_{1}(n)=N_{0}(n-1)
and
N_{0}(n)=N_{1}(n-1)+N_{0}(n-1)
whenever
n>1
.
Consequently,
N_{1}(n)=N_{1}(n-1)+N_{1}(n-2)
satisfies the recursion of the Fibonacci numbers for
n>1
, and since
N_{1}(1)=N_{1}(2)=1
, the first identity in (
5
) follows. This implies the second and third identities in (
5
), since
N_{0}(n)=N_{1}(n+1)
and
N(n)=N_{0}(n)+N_{1}(n)=b_{n}+b_{n+1}=b_{n+2}
for
n\geq 1
.
∎

3
Distribution of the remainder

Let
X=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}\beta^{-n}
be a random variable on
[0,1)
and let
F
denote the cumulative distribution function (CDF) of
X
. Let
F_{n}
be the CDF of
T_{\beta}^{n}(X)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that for every
J\in\Omega_{n}
,
F
has no jump at the endpoints of the interval
I_{n,J}
. Then for every
x\in[0,\beta^{-1})
we have

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n}}F\left(L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n}\right)-F\left(L_{n,J}\right),

(6)

and for every
x\in[\beta^{-1},1)
we have

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{J=(j_{1},...,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}}F\left(L_{n,J}+(1-j_{n})x\beta^{-n}+j_{n}\beta^{-n-1}\right)-F\left(L_{n,J}\right).

(7)

Proof.

By Lemma
2.1
, the collection of intervals

I_{n,J}
with
J\in\Omega_{n}

provides a subdivision of
[0,1)
, where
I_{n,J}
has length
\beta^{-n}
if
j_{n}=0
, and
\beta^{-n-1}
if
j_{n}=1
. Let
x\in[0,1)
and
J\in\Omega_{n}
.
If
x<\beta^{-1}
then
x\beta^{-n}\leq\beta^{-n-1}
, and if
x\geq\beta^{-1}
then
x\beta^{-n}\geq\beta^{-n-1}
. So if
x<\beta^{-1}
or if both
x\geq\beta^{-1}
and
J\in\Omega_{n,0}
, then

X\in I_{n,J}
and
T^{n}(X)\leq x
if and only if
L_{n,J}\leq X\leq L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n}
. However, if
x\geq\beta^{-1}
and
J\in\Omega_{n,1}
, then

X\in I_{n,J}
and
T^{n}(X)\leq x
if and only if
L_{n,J}\leq X\leq L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n-1}
. Now, using the assumption in the lemma, we immediately obtain (
6
) and (
7
).
∎

3.1
Main results

Henceforth, assume
X
has a pdf
f
on
[0,1)
. By Lemma
3.1
,
T_{\beta}^{n}(X)
is absolutely continuous with pdf given by

f_{n}(x)=\begin{cases}\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n}}\beta^{-n}f\left(L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n}\right)&\text{if }0\leq x<\beta^{-1},\\
\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n,0}}\beta^{-n}f\left(L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n}\right)&\text{if }\beta^{-1}\leq x<1.\end{cases}

(8)

Recall that
P_{n}
denotes the probability measure defined by
f_{n}
and that
f_{\beta}
denotes the pdf given by (
1
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume that for some integer
m\geq 1
,
f
is Lebesgue almost everywhere
equal to some constant
C_{J}\geq 0
on each interval
I_{m,J}
with
J\in\Omega_{m}
. In addition assume that

\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m,0}}C_{J}=\beta^{m}\frac{1+\beta}{\sqrt{5}},\quad\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m}}C_{J}=\beta^{m}\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{5}}.

(9)

Then
f_{n}=f_{\beta}
for
n=m,m+1,...
.

Proof.

First, let
y\in[0,\beta^{-1})
. Then

f_{m}(y)=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m}}\beta^{-m}f\left(L_{m,J}+x\beta^{-m}\right)=\beta^{-m}\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m}}C_{J}=f_{\beta}(y),

where (
8
) was used in the first equality, the second equality used the first assumption of the proposition together with the fact that
L_{m,J}+x\beta^{-m}\in I_{m,J}
, and the third equality used the assumption (
9
).

Second,
for
y\in[\beta^{-1},1)
, by similar arguments,

f_{m}(y)=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m,0}}\beta^{-m}f\left(L_{m,J}+x\beta^{-m}\right)=\beta^{-m}\sum_{J\in\Omega_{m,0}}C_{J}=f_{\beta}(y).

Thus, the proposition follows for
n=m
. For
n>m
, we just use that
f_{\beta}
is
T_{\beta}
-invariant.
∎

We need some notation for the following theorem. For a real function
g
defined on
[0,1]
, denote its
L_{1}
- and supremum-norm
by

\|g\|_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}|g(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t
and

\|g\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|g(x)|
, respectively, and denote the corresponding
L_{1}
-space by
L_{1}([0,1])=\{g\,|\,\|g\|_{1}<\infty\}
. Let
D([0,1])=\{g\geq 0\,|\,\|g\|_{1}=1\}
be the subspace of pdfs, and
CD([0,1])\subset D([0,1])
its subspace of functions
g
which are continuous on
[0,1]
and differentiable on the open interval
(0,1)
with
\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}<\infty
. For
g\in CD([0,1])
, define

g_{n}
as in (
8
) with
f
replaced by
g
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
denote the Borel subsets of
[0,1)
.
Define the total variation distance

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{n},P_{\beta})=\sup_{A\in\mathcal{B}}|P_{n}(A)-P_{\beta}(A)|=\frac{1}{2}\|f_{n}-f_{\beta}\|,

see e.g. Lemma 2.1 in
[
7
]
for the second equality.

Theorem 3.3
.

If
g\in CD([0,1])
then

\|f_{n}-f_{\beta}\|_{1}\leq\|f-g\|_{1}+O\left(\beta^{-2n/3}(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+1)\right).

(10)

In particular,

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{n},P_{\beta})=0.

(11)

Furthermore, if
f\in CD([0,1])
then
P_{n}
converges exponentially fast:

d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{n},P_{\beta})=O\left(\beta^{-2n/3}(\|f^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+1)\right).

(12)

Proof.

Let
g\in CD([0,1])
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta^{-1}}|f_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n}}\beta^{-n}\int_{0}^{\beta^{-1}}|f(L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n})-g(L_{n,J}+x\beta^{-n})|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n,0}}\int_{L_{n,J}}^{L_{n,J}+\beta^{-n-1}}|f(u)-g(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u+\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n,1}}\int_{I_{n,J}}|f(u)-g(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u,

(13)

while

\int_{\beta^{-1}}^{1}|f_{n}(x)-g_{n}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n,0}}\int_{L_{n,J}+\beta^{-n-1}}^{L_{n,J}+\beta^{-n}}|f(u)-g(u)|\,\mathrm{d}u.

(14)

Adding (
13
) and (
14
) and using Lemma
2.1
gives

\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{1}\leq\|f-g\|_{1}.

(15)

Set
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. For
J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}
define
\Omega_{n}(J)
to be the set of
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}
for which
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n_{1}})=J
. Note that
I_{n_{1},J}=\bigcup_{J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}(J)}I_{n,J^{\prime}}
. Similarly, let
\Omega_{n,0}(J)
and
\Omega_{n,1}(J)
denote the sets consisting of
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{n})\in\Omega_{n}(J)
with
j_{n}=0
and
j_{n}=1
, respectively. Then, for
x\in[0,\beta^{-1})
, (
8
) yields

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}(J)}\beta^{-n}g(L_{n_{1},J^{\prime}}+\beta^{-n}x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}(J)}\beta^{-n}|I_{n_{1},J}|^{-1}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+2})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

where in the second equality we used that by the mean value theorem

|g(L_{n,J^{\prime}}+\beta^{-n}x)-g(t)|\leq\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}|I_{n_{1},J}|\leq\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\beta^{-n_{1}},

for
J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}
,
J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n}(J)
and
t\in I_{n_{1},J}
, and the fact that by Lemma
2.2
,
N(k)\in O(\beta^{k+2})
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)=\bigg(\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1},0}}N(n_{2})\beta^{-n+n_{1}}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1},1}}N(n_{2}-1)\beta^{-n+n_{1}+1}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\mathrm{d}t\bigg)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+2})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}}\bigg(\frac{\beta+1}{\sqrt{5}}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\mathrm{d}t\bigg)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+2}\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty})+O(\beta^{-2n_{2}}),

(16)

where we used that
\beta^{-k}N(k)=\beta^{2}/\sqrt{5}+O(\beta^{-2k-2})=(1+\beta)/\sqrt{5}+O(\beta^{-2n_{2}-2})
by Lemma
2.2
and that
\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}|g(t)|\mathrm{d}t=1
.

For
x\in[\beta^{-1},1)
, a similar computation yields

\displaystyle g_{n}(x)=\bigg(\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1}}}\sum_{J^{\prime}\in\Omega_{n,0}(J)}\beta^{-n}|I_{n_{1},J}|^{-1}\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+1})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1},0}}\beta^{-n+n_{1}}N_{0}(n_{2})\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\sum_{J\in\Omega_{n_{1},1}}\beta^{-n+n_{1}+1}N_{0}(n_{2}-1)\int_{I_{n_{1},J}}g(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+1})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{5}}+O(\beta^{-2n_{2}+1})+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+1})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty},

(17)

where we used that
\beta^{-k}N_{0}(k)=\beta/\sqrt{5}+O(\beta^{-2k-1})
by Lemma
2.2
.

Combining (
16
) and (
17
) shows

g_{n}(x)=f_{\beta}(x)+O(\beta^{-n_{1}+2})\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+O(\beta^{-2n_{2}+1}).

(18)

Set
n_{2}=\lfloor n/3\rfloor
. Using (
15
) and (
18
), we get

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-f_{\beta}\|_{1}\leq\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{1}+O(\beta^{-2n/3+3})(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\|f-g\|_{1}+O(\beta^{-2n/3+3})(\|g^{\prime}\|_{\infty}+1),

which shows (
10
) and (
12
). We let
n\to\infty
and obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-f_{\beta}\|_{1}\leq\|f-g\|_{1}.

The right hand side can be made arbitrarily small, which shows (
11
).
∎

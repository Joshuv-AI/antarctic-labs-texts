A 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariance of localizing invariants
Vladimir Sosnilo

Abstract
. Weibel proved that p p -inverted K-theory is A 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant on F p \mathbb{F}_{p} -schemes and K-theory with Z / p \mathbb{Z}/p -coefficients is A 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} -invariant on Z ⁡ [ 1 p ] \mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{p}] -schemes. We extend this result to all finitary localizing invariants of small stable ∞ \infty -categories. Along the way we study the Frobenius and Verschiebung endofunctors defined by Tabuada and provide a categorical version of Stienstra’s projection formula.

In the very foundation of motivic homotopy theory lies the idea of enforcing the affine line to be
contractible,
hence imposing the
\mathbb{A}^{1}
-invariance property
1
1

1

Concretely, a cohomology theory
2
2

2

This is not as restrictive as it sounds at first, because many important cohomology theories satisfy

However, in general one is also interested in studying singular schemes.
In this context K-theory is known not to be

\mathbb{A}^{1}

As another example, the

0.6.
Outline of the proof

We follow the same steps as the original Weibel’s proof
[
Wei81
]
.
Given a
k
-localizing invariant define

NE(\mathcal{C}):=\operatorname{Fib}(E(\mathcal{C}\otimes_{k}\mathbf{Perf}_{k[t]})\to E(\mathcal{C})).

Similar to how it is done in
[
Tab17
]
, we establish an action of
the ring of rational
Witt vectors

\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
on the homotopy groups of
NE(\mathcal{C})
. The main ingredients going into
defining this action are Almkvist’s isomorphism
[
Alm74
]

\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)\simeq K_{0}(\mathcal{E}nd(\mathbf{Perf}_{k}))

and an explicit isomorphism

\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{C})\oplus\pi_{i+1}NE(\mathcal{C})\simeq\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})).

Here

\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})
is the stable
\infty
-category of endomorphisms of
\mathcal{C}

and
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
is the
subcategory of nilpotent endomorphisms.
Having these, the action is induced by the tensor product functor

\mathcal{E}nd(\mathbf{Perf}_{k})\times\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C}).

Our main results then follow from a thorough analysis of the action, granted by the following steps:

(1)

There are Frobenius and Verschiebung endofunctors

\mathcal{F}_{n},\mathcal{V}_{n}
on
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
as well as
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})

(see
[
BGT15
, Section 5]
). We provide a different way of defining them, which
allows to show that they are nicely compatible with the ring structure of
\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
and
satisfy the projection formula with respect to the action of
\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
on
NE(\mathcal{C})
(see Lemma
4.13
). For
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Perf}_{k}

they induce the classical Frobenius and Verschiebung operations on

K_{0}(\mathcal{E}nd(\mathbf{Perf}_{k}))\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))
.
This part allows us to represent
the action of
p\in\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
on
NE(\mathcal{C})
by an explicit endofunctor of
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
.

(2)

The action of
\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
on the (discrete) abelian group
\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C}))
is continuous.
For
E=K
this follows from the construction of K-theory. In general, we introduce a filtration

\cdots\to\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n+1}(\mathcal{C})\to\cdots

of
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
such that

\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits\limits_{n}\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})

and so that the action is continuous on the images of
all
\pi_{i}E(\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n}(\mathcal{C}))
by construction (see (
5.2
) and (
5.3.1
)).
This allows us to extend the action of
\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
to an action of the full ring of Witt vectors

\mathbb{W}(k)
and
deduce the main results from its properties.

The constructions in each of the steps are interesting on their own and appear to be completely new.

Reminder on noncommutative motives and localizing invariants

We say that a diagram in

Any

1.9.
Non-commutative motives

Tabuada, along with Blumberg, Cisinski and Gepner has developed a theory of non-commutative motives based
on localizing invariants.
The idea to define the
\infty
-category of motives of small idempotent complete stable
\infty
-categories
as a universal localizing invariant comes quite naturally after
one settles on the idea that localizing invariants are a good categorical analogue of cohomology theories.

One expects such an
\infty
-category of motives to be constructed as a localization of the
\infty
-category of presheaves on

\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}
and there should be a “motive” functor

M:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc}}((\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Spt})

such that

E(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{maps}(M(\mathcal{C}),E)

for any
E\in\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc}}((\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Spt})
.
However, this is not easy to achieve because of set-theoretic difficulties, so instead, one considers

finitary
localizing invariants.

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category. We say that a functor

E:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k}\to\mathcal{E}

is
finitary
if it preserves filtered colimits. For a cocomplete stable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
we denote the subcategory of
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{D})
consisting of finitary localizing invariants
by
\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k},\mathcal{D})
.

Example 1.11
.

K-theory, topological Hochschild homology, homotopy K-theory, Blanc’s topological K-theory are finitary localizing invariants.

We denote the
\infty
-category of finitary
k
-localizing presheaves by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},k}
.

Remark 1.12
.

The
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k}
is compactly generated by compact

k
-linear stable
\infty
-categories (see
[
HSS17
, Proposition 4.7]
and
[
BGT13
, Corollary 4.25]
). We denote this subcategory by
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\omega}_{\infty,k}
.
In particular, this implies that the restriction functor

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k},\mathcal{E})\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\omega}_{\infty,k},\mathcal{E})

induces an equivalence between finitary functors on
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k}
and all functors on
\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\omega}_{\infty,k}
.
Moreover, every
k
-localization sequence is a filtered colimit of
k
-localization sequences between compact

k
-linear stable
\infty
-categories (
[
HSS17
, Proposition 5.5]
). Therefore, we also have an equivalence

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},k}\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc}}((\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\omega}_{\infty,k})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Spt}).

Theorem 1.13

(
[
BGT13
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The embedding functor

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},k}\to\mathrm{Fun}((\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf},\omega}_{\infty,k})^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{Spt})

admits a left adjoint.
In particular, after precomposing with the Yoneda embedding one obtains a functor

\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{loc},k}:\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty,k}\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},k}.

This functor is a universal finitary localizing invariant in the sense that it induces an equivalence

\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{L}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc},k},\mathcal{D})\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{fin}}(\mathrm{Cat}^{\mathrm{perf}}_{\infty},\mathcal{D})

for any stably presentable
\infty
-category
\mathcal{D}
.
In this category one has

4.
Witt vectors

Assume
k
is a discrete ring. One can check that the map

\mathcal{E}nd(k)\to(1+tk[\![t]\!])^{\times}

given by sending a pair
(P,\phi)\in\mathcal{E}nd(k)
to its
characteristic polynomial

\mathrm{det}(1-t\phi)

is a group homomorphism. Here
\mathcal{E}nd(k)
denotes the exact category of endomorphisms on finitely
generated projective
k
-modules.
In fact, the codomain of the map can be endowed with a ring structure in such a way
that this map
is also a ring homomorphism. This ring is called the

ring of Witt vectors
and is denoted by
\mathbb{W}(k)
. A trivial endomorphism has
trivial characteristic polynomial, so we end up having a ring homomorphism

K_{0}(\mathcal{E}nd_{0}(k))\to\mathbb{W}(k).

The image of this map consists of fractions of the form

\frac{1+a_{1}t+\cdots a_{n}t^{n}}{1+b_{1}t+\cdots b_{l}t^{l}}.

We call the subring spanned by the image the
ring of rational Witt
vectors
and denote by
\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)
.

If
k
is an arbitrary connective ring spectrum and
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathbf{Perf}_{k})
is considered instead of

\mathcal{E}nd(k)
, the same story goes through, except that now the
characteristic polynomial lands in power series over
\pi_{0}(k)
.
The following is, in essence, a classical result of Almkvist
[
Alm74
]
. As stated, however, it
follows from
[
BGT15
, Theorem 1.12]
(see also
[
BGT15
, Theorem 1.9]
):

Theorem 4.1

(Almkvist)
.

The characteristic polynomial map

K_{0}(\mathcal{E}nd_{0}(\mathbf{Perf}_{k}))\to\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))

is an isomorphism onto its image
\mathbb{W}_{0}(\pi_{0}(k))
. Moreover, it becomes an isomorphism after completing with respect to the topology defined by preimages
of the ideals
(1+t^{N}\pi_{0}(k)[\![t]\!])
.

Example 4.2
.

\mathbb{W}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is isomorphic to the countable product of copies of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
(see

[
Hes08
, Proposition 10]
).

One of the key properties of the ring of Witt vectors is the following:

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
R
is a discrete
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{l}]
-algebra for
l\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then
l
is also invertible in
\mathbb{W}(R)
.

Proof.

See
[
Wei81
, Proposition 1.2]
.
∎

4.4.
Verschiebung and Frobenius

Any element
x
of the ring
\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))

can be represented as an infinite product

\prod\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(1-a_{i}t).

We define

V_{n}(1-at)=1-at^{n}

F_{n}(1-at)=1-a^{n}t.

By additivity and continuity this extends to natural additive Verschiebung and Frobenius operations

V_{n},F_{n}:\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))\to\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
(cf.
[
Gra78
]
).

These operations have been studied thoroughly over the past century and they have been shown to satisfy many
interesting properties (see
[
Hes08
]
,
[
Sti82
]
). These operations restrict to the subring
\mathbb{W}_{0}(\pi_{0}(k))
and we use the same notation for the
restricted operations.
For our purposes the most important property is the following:

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume
R
is a discrete
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra.
Then the equality

V_{p^{l}}F_{p^{l}}(x)=p^{l}x

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{W}(R)
.

Proof.

By continuity and additivity it suffices to show the equality for
x=(1-at)
.
In this case this is obvious from the definitions and the fact that taking the

p^{l}
-th power on
R
is additive.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

One has a nice description of the topology on
\mathbb{W}(R)
from Theorem
4.1

using the Verschiebung operations.
Recall that any element
x\in\mathbb{W}(R)
can be uniquely represented as a product

\prod\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}}(1-a_{i}t^{i})=\prod\limits_{i\in\mathbb{N}}V_{i}(1-a_{i}t).

If
x\in 1+t^{N}R[\![t]\!]
then
a_{i}=0
for
i<N
. Hence
x
is a (possibly infinite) sum of
V_{i}(x_{i})

for
x_{i}\in\mathbb{W}(R)
and
i\geqslant N
. Conversely, any such sum belongs to the ideal, so we have:

1+t^{N}R[\![t]\!]=\{\prod\limits_{i\geqslant N}V_{i}(x_{i})|x_{i}\in\mathbb{W}(R)\}.

l\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.13
(2) that there is a fiber sequence

K(X,Y)\to X\otimes\mathcal{V}_{p^{l}}(Y)\to\mathcal{V}_{p^{l}}(F_{p^{l}}(X)\otimes Y)

where

is a finite extension of functors that factor through
\iota:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})
functorial in
X\in\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})
and
Y\in\mathcal{E}nd(\mathbf{Perf}_{k})
.
Now we set
Y=(k,id)
. Since the

-class of
\mathcal{V}_{p^{l}}(k,id)
is
p^{l}
(this follows from
Proposition
4.11
and Lemma
4.5
), the endofunctor

X\mapsto X\otimes\mathcal{V}_{p^{l}}(k,id)
induces the multiplication by
p^{l}
on

\pi_{0}E
of both
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
or
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})
. On the other hand, by additivity

X\mapsto K(X,Y)
induces the zero map on
\pi_{0}E
of both
\mathcal{N}il_{0}(\mathcal{C})
or
\mathcal{E}nd_{0}(\mathcal{C})
.
Hence, applying additivity again we see that
X\mapsto\mathcal{V}_{p^{l}}(\mathcal{F}_{p^{l}}(X))
induces the multiplication by
p^{l}
on
\pi_{0}E
of either
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})
or
\mathcal{E}nd(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

5.
Torsion in Nil-theories

This section is dedicated to proving the main results
(Theorems
0.4
and
0.5
). So far we
never had to require our localizing invariants to be finitary.
The reason we need it is to show that the action of
\mathbb{W}_{0}(\pi_{0}(k))

constructed in Corollary
3.7
is continuous. This allows to extend the action
to the action of the ring of Witt vectors
\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))
. The ring
\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))

has many nice properties which the ring
\mathbb{W}_{0}(\pi_{0}(k))
does not (for example, an integer

l
is invertible in
\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))
whenever it is invertible in
k
by
Proposition
4.3
).
The
\mathbb{W}(\pi_{0}(k))
-modules

inherit these properties allowing to prove the main theorems.

5.1.

We define

as the pushout of

-

-algebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[t]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[t]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k[t]/(t^{n}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t\mapsto t^{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t\mapsto 0}

We have canonical morphisms

for

.

5.2.

Now we

\infty
-category
\mathcal{N}il(\mathcal{C})

We start from the case
\mathcal{C}=\mathbf{Perf}_{k}

Define

Restricting scalars gives rise to canonical functors

\tau_{n}:\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n}(\mathbf{Perf}_{k})\to\mathcal{N}il(\mathbf{Perf}_{k})\text{ for any }n.

Lemma 5.3
.

The canonical map

\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font colim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\rightarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits\limits_{n}\mathcal{N}il^{\leqslant n}(k)\to\mathcal{N}il(k)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
BCM20
, Proposition 2.15]
.
Note that
k[t]
is assumed to be a discrete ring there:
the assumption is needed to show that the map of pro-spectra

(5.3.a)

k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]}k[t]/(t^{n})\to\{k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]/(t^{m})}k[t]/(t^{n})\}_{m\geqslant n}.

is a pro-equivalence.
We show that it still holds in our setting.

First, using the Künneth spectral sequence, we
can compute the homotopy groups of each of the modules in the tower.
In particular, we see that the cofibers are free as
k
-modules, meaning that they are equivalent
to infinite direct sums of copies of suspensions of
k
.
Now the map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus\Sigma^{i_{l}}k\simeq\operatorname{Cofib}(k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]/(t^{m+n})}k[t]/(t^{n})\to k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]}k[t]/(t^{n}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Cofib}(k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]/(t^{m})}k[t]/(t^{n})\to k[t]/(t^{n})\otimes_{k[t]}k[t]/(t^{n}))}

is a zero map on the homotopy groups, and hence (by freeness) is a zero map. This shows the the
cofiber of (

) is a pro-null tower and that the map itself is a pro-equivalence.

∎

5.3.1.

For

Lemma 5.5

The functor

Proof.

The base change theorem tells us that for all

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Mod}_{k}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{Mod}_{k[t]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u_{l}^{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{0,*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a_{l,*}}

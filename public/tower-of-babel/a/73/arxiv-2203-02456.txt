Effect of magnetic field on fermions in graphene through time-oscillating potential
Rachid El Aitouni, Ahmed Jellal

Abstract
We study the effect of a magnetic field on Dirac fermions in graphene subject to a scalar potential oscillating in time. Using the Floquet theory and resonance approximation, we show that the energy spectrum exhibits extra subbands resulted from the oscillating potential in addition to quantized Landau levels. It is found that a current density can be generated in x x and y y -directions that is strongly dependent on the magnetic field and potential. Our numerical analysis show that the energy spectrum possesses a symmetry and the current density oscillates with different amplitudes under various conditions.

Because momentum

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}-\varepsilon&p_{x}-i(k_{y}-Bx)\\
p_{x}+i(k_{y}-Bx)&-\varepsilon\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\psi_{A}\\
\psi_{B}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\

(4)

To go further, it is convenient to apply the algebraic method based on the annihilation and creation operators. They can be mapped as

\displaystyle a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2B}}\left[p_{x}+i(k_{y}-Bx)\right]

\displaystyle a^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2B}}\left[p_{x}-i(k_{y}-Bx)\right]

(6)

which fulfill the commutation relation

associated to the quantized

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}=s\sqrt{2Bn},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}

where the dimensionless variable

and the magnetic length
l_{B}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{B}}
are introduced. Here
the sign function is
s=\pm 1

V
Conclusion

We have studied the effect of a magnetic field on graphene in the presence of a temporal potential. Solving the Dirac equation, we have derived the exact solutions of the energy spectrum using the Floquet theory together with algebraic method. The eigenvalues are found as Landau levels added to extra subbands originated from the oscillating potential in the first approximation of the frequency
\omega
. It is showed that the energy exhibits a symmetry under the change of energy signs in addition to crossing points between different bands. Our results are an agreement with precious published works on the subject
Sabeeh08
;
Mekkaoui2014
.

Subsequently, we have determined the current density in
x
and
y
-directions. It consists of two separate parts depending on the position dimensionless
X
and the oscillating potential. It was found that the current density exhibits extra terms that originated from oscillating potential, knowing that they could be interpreted as corrections to standard results. Our numerical analysis showed that the current density oscillates with different amplitudes strongly dependent on the magnetic field
B
. In fact, we have found three sets of
B
generating various amplitudes of oscillation. More precisely, the amplitude can decrease, increase, or remain constant according to the value taken by
B
. Furthermore, the wave vector
k_{y}
and space position
x
are discovered to be important in the density plot of the current density in terms of
B
and the argument
\omega t
. As a result, they act by decreasing the current density to a very low level.

Holographic entanglement entropy for L ​ i ​ f 4 ( 2 ) × S 1 × S 5 Lif_{4}^{(2)}\times{S}^{1}\times S^{5} spacetime with string excitations
Sabyasachi Maulik, Harvendra Singh

Abstract
The (F1,D2,D8) brane configuration with L ​ i ​ f 4 ( 2 ) × S 1 × S 5 Lif_{4}^{(2)}\times{S}^{1}\times S^{5} geometry is a known Lifshitz vacua supported by massive B μ ​ ν B_{\mu\nu} field in type IIA theory. This system allows exact IR excitations which couple to massless modes of the fundamental string. Due to these massless modes the solutions have a flow to a dilatonic L ​ i ​ f 4 ( 3 ) × S 1 × S 5 Lif_{4}^{(3)}\times S^{1}\times S^{5} vacua in IR. We study the entanglement entropy on the boundary of this spacetime for the strip and the disc subsystems. To our surprise net entropy density of the excitations at first order is found to be independent of the typical size of subsystems. We interpret our results in the light of first law of entanglement thermodynamics.

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}r_{y}}{q\ell^{2}}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\ln 2\right)\right)^{-2}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\right)^{-1}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{L^{2}r_{y}}{q\ell^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(2-2\ln 2\right)\right).

So we get

\displaystyle\mu_{E}^{(1)}\delta\Delta\rho\penalty\ =

\displaystyle\penalty\ \frac{2L^{3}r_{y}}{qG_{5}\ell^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(2-2\ln 2\right)\right)\delta\left(z_{I}^{-2}\right),

\displaystyle\penalty\ \frac{L^{2}}{\pi G_{4}\ell^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(2-2\ln 2\right)\right)\delta\left(z_{I}^{-2}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{L^{2}}{\pi G_{4}\ell^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(1-\ln 2\right)\right]}{\frac{L^{2}}{4G_{4}}\left[1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{z_{I}^{2}}\left(4\ln 2-\frac{5}{2}\right)\right]},

Entanglement entropy of narrow strip

We now consider a strip like subsystem with coordinate
width
-\ell/2\leq x_{1}\leq\ell/2

x_{2}\in[0,l_{2}]
, such that

l_{2}\gg\ell

is identified with the boundary of the RT surface in the bulk
at constant time.
The area functional of this static surface is

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{\gamma}=2L^{2}l_{2}\int^{z_{\ast}}_{\epsilon}dz{\sqrt{1+x_{1}^{\prime 2}}\over z^{2}}h^{1\over 2}\,.

(30)

For small width
\ell\ll z_{I}
, we
make a perturbative expansion of the integrand. The extremal surface satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle x_{1}^{\prime}={z^{2}\over z_{\ast}^{2}}{1\over\sqrt{{h\over h_{\ast}}-{z^{4}\over z_{\ast}^{4}}}}\,,

(31)

where
h_{\ast}\equiv h(z_{\ast})

We have specific boundary conditions such that
near the spacetime boundary

x_{1}|_{z=0}=\ell/2
and
the turning point is given by
x_{1}|_{z\sim z_{\ast}}=0
.
This leads to the first integral of the following type

\displaystyle\ell=2\int^{z_{\ast}}_{0}dz{z^{2}\over z_{\ast}^{2}}{1\over\sqrt{{h\over h_{\ast}}-{z^{4}\over z_{\ast}^{4}}}}\,,

(32)

which gives rise to a perturbative expansion in
{z_{\ast}\over z_{I}}

\displaystyle\ell=z_{\ast}\left(b_{0}+{z_{\ast}^{2}\over 2z_{I}^{2}}I_{1}+\cdots\right)\,,

The equation (

\displaystyle z_{\ast}=z_{\ast}^{(0)}\left(1-{z_{\ast}^{(0)2}\over z_{I}^{2}}{I_{1}\over 2b_{0}}+\cdots\right),

where

The leading area of strip can be evaluated using the tree level values

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{0}

\displaystyle=2L^{2}l_{2}\int^{z_{\ast}^{(0)}}_{\epsilon}dz{\sqrt{1+x_{1(0)}^{\prime 2}}\over z^{2}}\,,

(35)

\displaystyle={2L^{2}l_{2}\over z_{\ast}^{(0)}}\int^{1}_{\epsilon\over z_{\ast}^{(0)}}d\zeta{1\over\zeta^{2}\sqrt{1-\zeta^{4}}}\,,

\displaystyle=2L^{2}l_{2}\left({1\over\epsilon}-{2(b_{0})^{2}\over\ell}\right).

while the first order contribution is evaluated as

\displaystyle{\cal A}_{1}

\displaystyle=2L^{2}l_{2}\int^{z_{\ast}}_{0}dz{\sqrt{1+x_{1(0)}^{\prime 2}}\over 2z_{I}^{2}}\,,

(36)

\displaystyle=L^{2}l_{2}\left({a_{1}z_{\ast}^{(0)}\over z_{I}^{2}}\right).

where the coefficient
a_{1}={1\over 4}B\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle S_{E}^{strip}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{1}}{4G_{5}}={L^{2}l_{2}\over 2G_{4}}\left({1\over\epsilon}-{2b_{0}^{2}\over\ell}+{a_{1}\over 4b_{0}}\penalty\ \frac{\ell}{z_{I}^{2}}\right).

13

V
Conclusion

The Lifshitz background

Lif_{4}^{(2)}\times{S}^{1}\times S^{5}
of the massive type IIA theory
allows exact excitations which couple to massless modes of string in the IR. We calculated the entanglement entropy of the theory
at the boundary of these spacetimes, both for strip as well as disc shaped systems. At leading order, we found that the entropy density of the excitations remains fixed and does not grow with
\ell
, the subsystem size, so long as
\ell\ll z_{I}
. We find that this behaviour is consistent with the fact that energy density of the excitations itself behaves as
\bigtriangleup{\cal E}\propto 1/\ell^{2}
, which is in agreement with
\bigtriangleup{\cal E}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\mu_{E}\bigtriangleup\rho
. Note that the entanglement temperature itself goes as
T_{E}\propto{1\over\ell^{2}}
.

But this entanglement behaviour is quite different in comparison to the relativistic CFTs, where the entropy density of excitations grows linearly with the subsystem size, while the energy density of excitations remains fixed. Nevertheless we have found that the first law of entanglement thermodynamics

\delta s_{E}=\frac{1}{T_{E}}\left(\delta\Delta\mathcal{E}+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{E}\delta\Delta\rho\right),

(48)

holds good if we accept the hypothesis that the energy of a subsystem in the Lifshitz background (
II
) is given by

\bigtriangleup{E}\simeq\mu_{E}N\simeq{1\over 2}Nk_{E}T_{E}\,.

Our results appear to indicate an
equipartition nature of the entanglement thermodynamics for non-relativistic Lifshitz
system. But this is perhaps true only for
the high entanglement temperature regime (i.e. small
\ell\ll z_{I}
).

Further, we studied what happens to the first law of entanglement if we assume it to remain valid beyond the leading order. There is lack of consensus on this aspect, despite there being enough evidence for it to be a natural feature at first order. We discussed how the first law could be extended up to second order by making use of appropriately modified chemical potential and entanglement temperature. We think this is necessary because otherwise, we need to look for a new quantity at each higher order to account for the corrections; while the entanglement entropy, like its thermal counterpart should depend only on the energy and conserved charges of the theory. Such redefinition should work at all orders, thereby allowing the ‘first law of entanglement thermodynamics’ to be obeyed quite generally, irrespective of the degree of perturbation theory.

It would be interesting to obtain the HEE numerically for ball subsystems and compare with our perturbative results. This, however, involves solving boundary value problem and proves to be non-trivial. Another interesting problem is to consider shape dependence of holographic entanglement entropy in similar spirit to
Fonda et al. 2015
;
Cavini et al. 2020
. We hope to return to these problems in future.

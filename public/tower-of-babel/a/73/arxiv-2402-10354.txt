A Heterotic Hermitian–Yang–Mills Equivalence
Jock McOrist

Abstract




\proofmodefalse

We consider
N=1
,
d=4

{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}\ll 1

\mathcal{D}=D+\bar{D}

Introduction

We want to understand the parameter space of a heterotic string compactification to four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with minimal supersymmetry at large radius. Large radius means a limit in moduli space where all curvatures measured in some appropriate length scale are small, so that heterotic supergravity is a good approximation to the string theory. In this case, supergravity is really an effective field theory for string theory with
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
measuring how close the approximation is to string theory. We want
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}\ll 1
in order to ensure our supergravity theory is not wallowing in the swampland. In
[
1
,
2
]
, the requirements of realising
N=1
and
d=4
Minkowski space were phrased in terms of differential geometric equations on the geometric data which we summarise shortly and have collectively become known as the Hull-Strominger equations.
1

1

These papers worked to first order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
only. How these equations are modified to higher orders in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
, equivalently derivatives, is an open question. Partial progress has however been made, see e.g.
[
3
]
.

The advantage of this formulation is that supersymmetry is directly related to the differential geometry of the underlying geometry of the string compactification. The geometry is non–Kähler and yet has a Kähler parameter space, which by itself is mathematically surprising. Indeed, there is now significant mathematical interest in the geometry and parameter space of these compactifications.

In previous work
[
4
]
we described how to reformulate the conditions for the vacuum to be supersymmetric in terms of the kernel of a nilpotent operator
{\overline{D}}
and its adjoint
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
. The former corresponded to the system satisfying a set of ‘F-term’ equations as they also can be derived from a superpotential functional while the latter we called ‘D-term’ equations. Indeed, the D-terms look closely related to those derived from a moment map construction as is expected from general supersymmetry lore
[
5
,
6

. The terminology thence is chosen to mirror the corresponding constructions in
N=1

d=4
supersymmetric field theories and the
{\overline{D}}
–operator seems to play a role analogous to a BRST operator.

The
{\overline{D}}

{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections give it a non–trivial derivative structure. It is, nonetheless, a nilpotent operator on a bundle and our work here suggests it should be thought of as a generalisation of a holomorphic structure.

This paper starts with inspiration from the Hodge dual relation between
{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}
and
{\partial}
in
{\overline{\partial}}
–cohomology: we construct a
D

{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
computed in
[
4
]

\mathcal{D}={\overline{D}}+D
. This again is not a connection, but it does have a curvature
\mathcal{F}
which we compute. What we demonstrate is that this curvature is holomorphic and satisfies a Hermitian-Yang-Mills equation if and only if the supersymmetry and Bianchi identity are satisfied. This sets us on the path to reformulating the existence of heterotic vacua as not so much satisfying the Strominger-Hull equations, but finding holomorphic HYM operators on a certain vector bundle. In other words, our works suggests there might be a generalisation of the Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem for the existence of heterotic vacua at large radius.

The idea of reinterpreting solutions to the Strominger–Hull equations as a sort of instanton connection on an extension bundle

1.1
Hull–Strominger as a description of heterotic at large radius

We now summarise Hull-Strominger equations for supersymmetry. The spacetime geometry is taken to be of the form

\mathbbl{R}^{3,1}\times X~.

The manifold is complex, which means it admits an integrable complex structure
J=J_{m}{}^{n}{\text{d}}x^{m}\otimes{\partial}_{n}
, where
x^{m}
are real coordinates on
X
. The complex structure is integrable meaning
J^{2}=-1
with its Nijenhuis tensor vanishing. We often have need for wedges – in the interest of clutter reduction, we omit the wedge symbol, writing for example
{\text{d}}x^{mn}\cong{\text{d}}x^{m}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{n}
. Occasionally, there may be ambiguity in which we case we write the wedge explicitly.

The complex manifold has trivial canonical bundle and so admits a holomorphic volume form
\Omega
. It admits a Riemannian metric
{\text{d}}s^{2}=g_{mn}{\text{d}}x^{m}\otimes{\text{d}}x^{n}
, which is compatible with the complex structure. It follows that there exists a hermitian 2-form
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{mn}{\text{d}}x^{mn}=\text{i}g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{\text{d}}x^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}
, where we have evaluated the hermitian form in the complex coordinates that diagonalise a given
J
.

There is a vector bundle
E\to X
, with a connection
A
, and a structure group being a subgroup of
E_{8}\times E_{8}
or
{\rm Spin}(32)/\mathbbl{Z}_{2}
. The gauge connection is anti-hermitian meaning

A\;=\;\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}~,

where
\mathcal{A}
is the
(0,1)
component of
A
. The field strength for the connection is
F={\text{d}}A+A^{2}
and supersymmetry requires it be holomorphic
F^{(0,2)}=0
and solve a Hermitian Yang–Mills

g^{mn}F_{mn}\;=\;0~,

which on
X
can be alternatively written as
\omega^{2}F=0
.

There is a 3-form
H
which is defined on the one hand as the contorsion of
\omega

H\;=\;{\text{d}}^{c}\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{3!}J^{m}J^{n}J^{p}({\text{d}}\omega)_{mnp}~.

(1.1)

For fixed complex structure this is
H=\text{i}({\partial}-{\overline{\partial}})\omega
.
The hermitian form is conformally balanced

{\text{d}}\left(e^{-2\Phi}\omega^{2}\right)\;=\;0~,

where
\Phi=-{1/2}\log|\Omega|_{\omega}
. We remark that in the case of
H=\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,})
, which covers the vacua considered in this document, the dilaton
e^{-2\Phi}
turns out to be constant to this order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}

[
11
]
, so the metric is balanced in the sense of Michelsohn
[
12
]
, but we will not make direct use of this fact as the dilaton will not play a prominent role in our analysis.

On the other hand the Green–Scharwz anomaly together with coupling of supergravity to a gauge field forces

to be

H\;=\;{\text{d}}B-\frac{{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big({\text{CS}}[A]-{\text{CS}}[\Theta]\Big)~,

(1.2)

where
CS
is the Chern–Simons form,
B
is the B-field, and
\Theta^{\text{\tiny H}}
is the Hull connection:
\Theta^{\text{\tiny H}}=\Theta^{\text{\tiny LC}}-\frac{1}{2}H
.
H
satisfies a Bianchi identity

{\text{d}}H~=-\frac{{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace(F^{2})-\text{Tr}\thinspace(R^{{\text{\tiny H}}\,2})\,\Big)~.

(1.3)

Note there is no ambiguity in the Bianchi identity in evaluating the curvature for
R^{\text{\tiny H}}
if we want our equations to approximate the string theory moduli space. There are other systems of equations one can study in which
R
in this equation is evaluated with respect to another connection, for example Chern, but this is a different mathematical problem, not relevant to string theory at large radius in which
H=\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,})
. String theory at large radius already has lots of interesting mathematics to be understood such as the generalisation of mirror symmetry, and so is our focus.

The role of
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections, or quantum corrections, are important physically and mathematically. The Hull–Strominger equations describe the requirements of
N=1

d=4
supersymmetry correct to first order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
. With the Bianchi identity, these imply the equations of motion, summarised in §
A
, are satisfied, again to first order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}

[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. The converse is not true, nor is it obvious what the analogous equations are to higher orders in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
. At
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
for example, it is likely the manifold is no longer conformally balanced and so the role of
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections is important as far the differential geometry of these string theory vacua go. Furthermore, non–perturbative effects in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
play an important role. These are computed via the string world sheet as instantons and normally carry information about geometric invariants. For example, in the study of Calabi–Yau manifolds and associated
(2,2)
CFTs it was only after including the
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections did one discover mirror symmetry: that the moduli space of a CY manifold

and its mirror
\hat{X}
are the same after a change of coordinates. The
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{3}
correction to the Kähler potential carries information about the Riemann–Zeta function. This term can be understood as a consequence of mirror symmetry. The corrections also encode geometric invariants such as Gromov–Witten invariants, see e.g.
[

and

{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
in which we work, and understand that equations such as the conformally balanced equation no longer hold at order
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
. We will at points need to work order-by-order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}

[
20

1.2
Moduli of heterotic vacua

We are interested in deformations of solutions. Deformations of the fields can be related to coordinates of the moduli space via derivatives. In the ten-dimensional physical string theory, this corresponds to a choice of Kaluza-Klein anstaz.

The gauge field
A
is antihermitian and so we write it as
A=\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}
where
\mathcal{A}=A^{(0,1)}

\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}+\mathfrak{a}~,\qquad\mathfrak{a}\;=\;\delta y^{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}+\delta y^{a}\delta y^{b}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A}+\cdots~,

(1.4)

In this paper we restrict to first order deformations and so
\mathfrak{a}=\delta y^{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A
. The derivative is necessary as background gauge transformations on

[
22
]

\delta y^{a}
and the deformation
\mathfrak{a}
. To save clutter we write
\mathfrak{a}
understanding its a small deformation.

A deformation of complex structure can be phrased in many ways. One of the roads to Rome is in terms of a deformation of complex coordinates on
X

{\text{d}}x^{\mu}\to{\text{d}}x^{\mu}+\delta y^{a}\Delta_{a\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\,{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\nu}}~.

(1.5)

The index

\mathcal{M}

\Delta=\delta y^{a}\Delta_{a\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\nu}}\otimes{\partial}_{\mu}
. The holomorphic top form
\Omega
transforms correspondingly

\Omega\to\Omega+\Delta^{\mu}\,\Omega_{\mu}~.

(1.6)

Requiring the complex structure remain integral means
{\overline{\partial}}\Delta^{\mu}=0

The B-field transforms under

This can be compared to the calculations in

H\;=\;\text{i}({\partial}-{\overline{\partial}})\omega~.

Therefore the Bianchi identity in terms of the hermitian form is

2\text{i}{\partial}{\overline{\partial}}\omega\;=\;\frac{{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\left(\text{Tr}\thinspace F^{2}-\text{Tr}\thinspace R^{2}\,\right)~.

(A.2)

The equations of motion, correct up to and including second order in
{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}
in the action, are given by

\displaystyle R_{MN}+2\nabla_{M}\nabla_{N}\Phi-\frac{1}{4}H_{MAB}H_{N}{}^{AB}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\frac{{{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace F_{MP}F_{N}{}^{P}-R_{MPAB}(\Theta^{+})R_{N}{}^{PAB}(\Theta^{+})\Big)+\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{3})\;=\;0~,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\nabla^{M}(\text{e}^{-2\Phi}H_{MNP})+\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{3})\;=\;0~,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\mathpzc D^{{\text{\tiny B}}\,M}(\text{e}^{-2\Phi}F_{MN})+\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{2})\;=\;0~.

(A.5)

Here
\mathpzc D^{\text{\tiny B}}=\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}+[A,\cdot]
, with
\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}
computed with respect to the Bismut connection
\Theta^{\text{\tiny B}}=\Theta-\frac{1}{2}H
and
R_{MN}
is the Ricci tensor. Note that the equation of motion for the gauge sector is only accurate modulo
\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
corrections. This is due to the fact that the gauge fields couple to supergravity at
\mathcal{O}({{\alpha}^{\backprime}\,})
in the action (
A.1
).

Appendix B
Identities from complex geometry

This section mostly serves to establish notation and conventions, and we also derive some identities in complex geometry that are used in the main text. The equations derived here hold generally on a complex manifold and we do not use any identities from supersymmetry.

Let
X
be a complex manifold with hermitian metric
\omega=ig_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}dx^{\rho}\wedge d\bar{x}^{\beta}
. Our conventions for the components of differential forms in holomorphic coordinates is

\eta\;=\;\frac{1}{p!q!}\,\eta_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}\overline{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{j_{q}}}\,dx^{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}\wedge d\bar{x}^{\overline{j_{1}}\cdots\overline{j_{q}}},\quad\eta\in\Omega^{p,q}(X)~.

(B.1)

The 3-form field strength associated to
\omega
will be denoted
H=i(\partial-{\overline{\partial}})\omega
, so that in components

H\;=\;\frac{1}{2}H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\nu}d\bar{z}^{\rho}\wedge dz^{\mu}\wedge dz^{\nu}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\rho{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}}dz^{\rho}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\mu}\wedge d\bar{z}^{\nu}~,

(B.2)

then

H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\nu}\;=\;\!-(\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu\bar{\rho}}-\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu\bar{\rho}})~.

(B.3)

Since we do not assume that
g
is a Kähler metric, the Levi-Civita connection no longer preserves the holomorphic structure, and so by default we will use
\nabla
and
R
to denote the Chern connection of
g
on
T^{1,0}X
. Our conventions are

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}V^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}V^{\lambda}+\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\lambda}{}_{\sigma}V^{\sigma},\quad\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;\partial_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}g^{{\overline{\sigma}}\nu}

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\overline{\mu}}}V^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\partial_{{\overline{\mu}}}V^{\lambda}~,

(B.4)

for any section
V\in\Gamma(T^{1,0}X)
and

R_{\mu\bar{\lambda}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\bar{\lambda}}\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho},\quad[\nabla_{\mu},\nabla_{\bar{\lambda}}]V^{\rho}\;=\;R_{\mu\bar{\lambda}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\beta}V^{\beta}~.

(B.5)

Our conventions imply

[\nabla_{\lambda},\nabla_{{\overline{\mu}}}]V^{\bar{\rho}}\;=\;\!-R_{\lambda{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\bar{\rho}}V^{{\overline{\tau}}},\quad[\nabla_{\lambda},\nabla_{{\overline{\mu}}}]V_{\rho}\;=\;\!-R_{\lambda{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\beta}{}_{\rho}V_{\beta}~.

(B.6)

We also have the non-Kähler symmetries

R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;R_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}{\overline{\nu}}\rho}-\nabla_{\mu}H_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}{\overline{\nu}}}~,\quad R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;R_{\rho{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\mu}-\nabla_{{\overline{\nu}}}H_{{\overline{\tau}}\rho\mu}~,

(B.7)

\nabla_{\mu}R_{s\bar{\gamma}{\overline{\sigma}}\lambda}\;=\;\nabla_{s}R_{\mu\bar{\gamma}{\overline{\sigma}}\lambda}+H^{r}{}_{s\mu}R_{r\bar{\gamma}{\overline{\sigma}}\lambda},\quad\nabla_{{\overline{\kappa}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;\nabla_{{\overline{\nu}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\kappa}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}+H^{\bar{r}}{}_{{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\kappa}}}R_{\mu\bar{r}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}~.

(B.8)

\overline{R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\beta}}\;=\;R_{\nu{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\rho}~.

(B.9)

We will also use the Bismut connection
\hat{\nabla}
(also known as the Strominger-Bismut connection
[
36
,
2
]
). Compared to the Chern connection
\nabla
, the Bismut connection is given in holomorphic coordinates by

\hat{\nabla}_{\kappa}V^{\rho}\;=\;\nabla_{\kappa}V^{\rho}+H^{\rho}{}_{\kappa\lambda}V^{\lambda}~,\qquad\hat{\nabla}_{{\overline{\kappa}}}V^{\rho}\;=\;\partial_{{\overline{\kappa}}}V^{\rho}-H_{\lambda{\overline{\kappa}}}{}^{\rho}V^{\lambda}~.

(B.10)

This connection was noticed by Yano
[
38
]
to be the unique connection on
T^{1,0}X
such that
\hat{\nabla}g=0
and

\hat{T}^{\rho}{}_{\kappa\beta}\;=\;\hat{\Gamma}_{\kappa}{}^{\rho}{}_{\beta}-\hat{\Gamma}_{\beta}{}^{\rho}{}_{\kappa}=\ H^{\rho}{}_{\kappa\beta},\quad\hat{T}^{\rho}{}_{{\overline{\kappa}}\beta}\;=\;\hat{\Gamma}_{{\overline{\kappa}}}{}^{\rho}{}_{\beta}-\hat{\Gamma}_{\beta}{}^{\rho}{}_{{\overline{\kappa}}}\;=\;H^{\rho}{}_{{\overline{\kappa}}\beta}~.

(B.11)

The Bismut curvature satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}H_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{{\overline{\tau}}}-\partial_{{\overline{\tau}}}H_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}+R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}~,

(B.12)

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{\rho\beta}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}H_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\beta}-\partial_{\beta}H_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}~,

(B.13)

where
R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}
is the Chern-Ricci curvature.

We will encounter various traces of the curvature of the Chern connection in our calculations, and we note here the following identity:

g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;\!-g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}(i\partial{\overline{\partial}}\omega)_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}-\hat{R}_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}+g^{\sigma\bar{\rho}}g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}H_{\sigma{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}}H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\rho}~.

(B.14)

Our conventions are such that
g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}=0
is the condition for the Chern connection to be a Hermitian-Yang-Mills connection on
T^{1,0}X
. We now give the derivation of (
B.14
). Direct calculation gives

(i\partial{\overline{\partial}}\omega)_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}\rho{\overline{\tau}}}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{{\overline{\nu}}}g_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}+\partial_{\rho}\partial_{{\overline{\nu}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}+\partial_{\mu}\partial_{{\overline{\tau}}}g_{\rho{\overline{\nu}}}-\partial_{\rho}\partial_{{\overline{\tau}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}~,

(B.15)

and substituting the Chern curvature tensor leads to

(\text{i}\partial{\overline{\partial}}\omega)_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}\rho{\overline{\tau}}}\;=\;R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}-R_{\rho{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\mu}-R_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}{\overline{\nu}}\rho}+R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}{\overline{\nu}}\mu}-g^{\sigma\bar{\rho}}H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\rho}H_{\sigma{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}}~.

(B.16)

Tracing by
g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}
then gives

-g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}(i\partial{\overline{\partial}}\omega)_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}-R_{\rho}{}^{\mu}{}_{{\overline{\tau}}\mu}-R_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}+R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}g^{\sigma\bar{\rho}}H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\rho}H_{\sigma{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}}~.

(B.17)

Taking traces of (
B.7
) gives

R_{\rho}{}^{\mu}{}_{{\overline{\tau}}\mu}\;=\;R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-\nabla_{\rho}H_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu},\quad R_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-\nabla_{{\overline{\tau}}}H^{\mu}{}_{\rho\mu}~.

(B.18)

Therefore

-g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}(\text{i}\partial{\overline{\partial}}\omega)_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}\;=\;g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}R_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}\rho}+\nabla_{\rho}H^{\mu}{}_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}+\nabla_{{\overline{\tau}}}H_{\mu}{}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}-R_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}-g^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}g^{\sigma\bar{\rho}}H_{\bar{\rho}\mu\rho}H_{\sigma{\overline{\nu}}{\overline{\tau}}}~.

(B.19)

We obtain (
B.14
) by substituting the trace of the Bismut curvature.

Next, we note the following 3-form field divergence identity:

\nabla^{{\overline{\kappa}}}H_{\rho\beta{\overline{\kappa}}}\;=\;\!-\hat{R}_{\rho\beta}{}^{\mu}{}_{\mu}~,

(B.20)

where
\nabla
is the Chern connection and
\hat{R}
is the curvature tensor of the Bismut connection. To derive (
B.20
), we start by differentiating
H
with the Chern connection, which gives

\nabla_{\rho}H_{\mu\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}\;=\;\partial_{\rho}H_{\mu\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\mu}H_{\sigma\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\nu}H_{\mu\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}~,

(B.21)

with
\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\nu}-\Gamma_{\nu}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\mu}=H_{\mu}{}^{\kappa}{}_{\nu}
. Thus

\displaystyle\nabla_{\rho}H_{\mu\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\nabla_{\mu}H_{\rho\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle-\partial_{\rho}(\partial_{\mu}g_{\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\partial_{\nu}g_{\mu{\overline{\kappa}}})-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\mu}H_{\sigma\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\nu}H_{\mu\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}-(\mu\leftrightarrow\rho)~,

(B.22)

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}(\partial_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\partial_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\kappa}}})+H_{\rho\mu}{}^{\sigma}H_{\sigma\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}-\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\nu}H_{\mu\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}+\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\sigma}{}_{\nu}H_{\rho\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}~,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}H_{\mu\rho{\overline{\kappa}}}+H_{\rho\mu}{}^{\sigma}H_{\sigma\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}+H_{\rho\nu}{}^{\sigma}H_{\mu\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}-H_{\mu\nu}{}^{\sigma}H_{\rho\sigma{\overline{\kappa}}}~.

We now contract with
g^{\rho{\overline{\kappa}}}
and simplify to obtain

\nabla^{{\overline{\kappa}}}H_{\mu\nu{\overline{\kappa}}}\;=\;\!-\partial_{\mu}H_{\rho}{}^{\rho}{}_{\nu}+\partial_{\nu}H_{\rho}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu}~.

(B.23)

The expression for Bismut curvature gives (
B.20
).

We conclude this section by stating our conventions for vector bundles. Let
E\rightarrow X
be a holomorphic vector bundle. Let
A
be a connection on
E
with curvature
F\in\Omega^{2}({\rm End}\,E)
. We will write
F_{ij}
for the 2-form components of
F
and omit the
{\rm End}\,E
indices so that
F_{ij}
is matrix-valued. If
A
is the Chern connection of a metric on

, then in a holomorphic frame

and

. We note the identity

\nabla_{{\overline{\tau}}}F^{{\overline{\tau}}\mu}-\nabla^{\mu}(g^{\sigma\bar{\rho}}F_{\sigma\bar{\rho}})-F^{{\overline{\tau}}\rho}H_{\rho{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\mu}\;=\;0~,

(B.24)

where
\nabla
is the Chern connection. To see this, we start with the Bianchi identity
{\overline{\partial}}F=0
, which in components reads

\partial_{{\overline{\tau}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}-\partial_{{\overline{\nu}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}\;=\;0~.

(B.25)

Converting the Dolbeault derivative to the Chern connection introduces torsion.

\nabla_{{\overline{\tau}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}-\nabla_{{\overline{\nu}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}+(\Gamma_{{\overline{\tau}}}{}^{\bar{\rho}}{}_{{\overline{\nu}}}-\Gamma_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\bar{\rho}}{}_{{\overline{\tau}}})F_{\mu\bar{\rho}}\;=\;0~.

(B.26)

In terms of

\nabla_{{\overline{\tau}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}-\nabla_{{\overline{\nu}}}F_{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}-H_{{\overline{\tau}}{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\bar{\rho}}F_{\mu\bar{\rho}}\;=\;0~.

(B.27)

Contracting with
g^{\mu{\overline{\tau}}}
implies (
B.24
).

Abstract

The quasilocal angular momentum L ⁡ ( ξ ) L(\xi) has the following attractive properties; ( i ) it follows from the Einstein’s constraint equations, ( ii ) it satisfies the Poisson algebra { L ⁡ ( ξ ) , L ⁡ ( η ) } P . Introduction

Einstein’s theory of general relativity is based on the general covariance and the equivalence principle. This implies that local definitions of non-zero gravitational energy, linear and angular momentum are incompatible with the general covariance. However, as is well-known, there have been several attempts
to define local gravitational energy and momentum. Einstein himself introduced energy-momentum pseudotensor of gravitational fields, which was further developed by L. Landau and E. Lifshitz
[
1
]
. In these approaches, local definitions of gravitational energy-momentum and the corresponding conservation laws were found, but only at the cost of the general covariance.

It is well-known that the Einstein’s theory is a highly constrained system with four non-linear constraints enforcing the general covariance
[
2
]
. By analyzing these constraint equations, R. Arnowitt, S. Deser, and C.W. Misner (ADM) defined canonical
gravitational energy-momentum as symmetry generators of the global Poincaré group at spatial infinity
[
3
]
. The quasilocal generalizations of gravitational energy-momentum on a two-sphere at a finite distance on spacelike hypersurfaces were proposed by several authors
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
, but it is highly non-trivial to extract dynamical contents such as the in- and out-going fluxes of gravitational energy-momentum from these expressions
[
11
,
12
]
.

On the other hand, H. Bondi, A.W.K. Metzner, and R.K. Sachs (BMS) defined gravitational energy-momentum at null infinity, whose difference at two different retarded times naturally defines the out-going gravitational flux
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. The definition of gravitational angular momentum remains tricky, however, as there are no natural notions of the center of rotation as seen by an observer at null infinity
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

To our knowledge, the most successful definition of gravitational angular momentum at null infinity is given by A. Rizzi
[
19
]
. He defined the angular momentum of gravitational fields as a two-surface integral of the inner product of the
SO(3)
generators
and the commutator of the in- and out-going null vector fields at null infinity. This definition is geometrical and reproduces the standard value for the Kerr spacetime. Despite its successful features, Rizzi’s definition has several
drawbacks; it is gauge-dependent in that the null vectors that play crucial roles in his definition must be affinely parametrized
[
20
]
, and it is not expressed in canonical variables, and moreover, it is not at all clear whether it is compatible with the Einstein’s constraints.

A list of guidelines of a satisfactory quasilocal generalization of gravitational angular momentum would be the followings
[
4
,
10
,
19
]
;
(
i
) it must be a two-surface integral of some scalar density function on the gravitational phase space,
(
ii
) it must be independent of how the two-surface in a given spacetime is labeled,
(
iii
) it must incorporate the anti-symmetry intrinsic to the angular momentum,
(
iv
) it must be interpretable geometrically,
(
v
) the in- and/or out-going quasilocal fluxes of angular momentum must be given by the time-derivative of the quasilocal angular momentum,
(
vi
) it must reproduce the standard value of angular momentum for the Kerr spacetime in the asymptotic limit,
(
vii
) its algebra must reduce to the angular momentum algebra

[L_{i},L_{j}]=-{\epsilon_{ij}}^{k}L_{k}
in the asymptotic limit,
(
viii
) finally, though not necessary, it is certainly desirable that the quasilocal gravitational angular momentum be deducible from the Einstein’s constraints.

In this paper, we study in detail the quasilocal gravitational angular momentum that meets the above criteria, which was proposed years ago
[
21
]
. However, such quasilocal angular momentum depends on a divergence-free tangent vector field on a compact two-surface.
It is well-known that, by the Poincaré-Hopf theorem, any continuous tangent vector field has at least one zero for any compact regular even dimensional manifold with non-zero Euler characteristic.
Therefore, our quasilocal angular momentum fails to “measure” the rotation of gravitational fields at these singular points of the divergence-free vector field.
Thus, it is desirable to have an invariant notion of
quasilocal gravitational angular momentum, as a gravitational analog of
Casimir invariant
of ordinary angular momentum, which has the following properties;
(
i
) it must commute with quasilocal angular momentum under the Poisson bracket,
(
ii
) it must be independent of the divergence-free vector field,
(
iii
) it must reproduce
(ma)^{2}
at null infinity of the Kerr spacetime.
By the property (
ii
), the invariant quasilocal angular momentum will be insensitive to the singular nature of the vector field. We will propose a two-surface integral that defines an
invariant
quasilocal angular momentum with these properties.

\displaystyle\hskip 28.90755pt=-k^{2}(e^{-2\sigma}\rho^{ab}\pi_{b}+e^{-\sigma}\rho^{ab}\partial_{b}\ln f),

(46)

where we used the equation (
21
).
Conversely,
\pi_{a}
can be written as

\pi_{a}=-{1\over k^{2}}e^{2\sigma}\rho_{ab}[n,l]_{\rm L}^{b}-e^{\sigma}\partial_{a}\ln f.

(47)

If we use the normalization (
8
) and the identity

[n,l]_{{\rm L}a}=e^{\sigma}\rho_{ab}[n,l]_{\rm L}^{b}
,
\pi_{a}

can be written in an invariant form

\pi_{a}=e^{\sigma}{[n,l]_{La}\over<n,l>}-e^{\sigma}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\ln f,

(48)

where
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
is the covariant derivative on
N_{2}
.
Let
d\mu
be the invariant measure on
N_{2}
given by

d\mu=d^{2}y\ e^{\sigma}.

(49)

Then, one finds that

\displaystyle\oint d^{2}y\ \xi^{a}\pi_{a}=\oint d\mu\Big(\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}-\xi^{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\ln{f}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 57.81621pt=\oint d\mu\Big(\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}+\ln{f}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}\Big),

(50)

so that the equation (
44
) becomes

L(u,v;\xi)=\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint d\mu\Big(\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}+\ln{f}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}\Big)+L_{0}.

(51)

3
Gauge-independence of quasilocal angular momentum

A two-surface
N_{2}
in a 4-dimensional spacetime
E_{4}
can be described as a level surface

\{p\in E_{4}\,|\,\lambda(p)=\nu(p)=0\}
, where
\lambda
and
\nu
are scalar functions defined on
E_{4}
. Since the null vector fields
n
and
l
are normal to
N_{2}
, they can be written locally as

\displaystyle n_{A}=\nabla_{A}\lambda,\quad l_{A}=\nabla_{A}\nu,

(52)

where
A=(+,-,a)
. The functions
\lambda
and
\nu
, however, are not unique, and the quasilocal angular momentum must be independent of
labeling two-surfaces
N_{2}
in
E_{4}
. Let us consider two functions
\lambda^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
related to
\lambda
and
\nu
by

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=\alpha\lambda,\quad\nu^{\prime}=\beta\nu,

(53)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are arbitrary functions, so that
the level surface defined by
\{p\in E_{4}\,|\,\lambda^{\prime}(p)=\nu^{\prime}(p)=0\}
describes the same surface
N_{2}
as before. We will show that the necessary and sufficient condition that the quasilocal angular momentum (
51
) is independent of the choice of the arbitrary functions
\alpha
and
\beta
is

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=0,

(54)

where
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
is the covariant derivative on
N_{2}
.
The null vector fields normal to
N_{2}
can be also written as

\displaystyle n^{\prime}_{A}=\nabla_{A}\lambda^{\prime}|_{\lambda^{\prime}=0}=(\alpha\nabla_{A}\lambda+\lambda\nabla_{A}\alpha)|_{\lambda=0}=\alpha\nabla_{A}\lambda,

\displaystyle l^{\prime}_{A}=\nabla_{A}\nu^{\prime}|_{\nu^{\prime}=0}=(\beta\nabla_{A}\nu+\nu\nabla_{A}\beta)|_{\nu=0}=\beta\nabla_{A}\nu.

(55)

In order to preserve the normalization condition
<n^{\prime},l^{\prime}>=-k^{2}
,
\beta
must be related to
\alpha
by

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{1}{\alpha}.

(56)

The covariant derivative
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
on
N_{2}
is the projection of the covariant derivative
\nabla_{A}
on the whole spacetime
E_{4}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{a}={h_{a}}^{A}\nabla_{A},

(57)

where
{h_{a}}^{A}
is the projector from the tangent space of
E_{4}
to the tangent space of
N_{2}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\hskip-39.74872pt[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{La}=h_{aA}[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{\rm L}^{A}

\displaystyle=h_{aA}(n^{\prime B}\nabla_{B}l^{\prime A}-l^{\prime B}\nabla_{B}n^{\prime A})

\displaystyle=h_{aA}\{\nabla^{B}(\alpha\lambda)\nabla_{B}\nabla^{A}(\beta\nu)-\nabla^{B}(\beta\nu)\nabla_{B}\nabla^{A}(\alpha\lambda)\}|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

\displaystyle=h_{aA}\{\alpha(\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}(\beta\nu)-\beta(\nabla^{B}\nu)\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}(\alpha\lambda)\}|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

\displaystyle=h_{aA}\{\alpha(\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla^{A}(\beta\nabla_{B}\nu+\nu\nabla_{B}\beta)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt-\beta(\nabla^{B}\nu)\nabla^{A}(\alpha\nabla_{B}\lambda+\lambda\nabla_{B}\alpha)\}|_{\lambda=\nu=0}.

(58)

Notice that we have

\displaystyle\hskip-14.45377pth_{aA}(\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla^{A}(\nu\nabla_{B}\beta)=h_{aA}(\nabla^{B}\lambda)\{(\nabla^{A}\nu)\nabla_{B}\beta+\nu\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}\beta\}=0,

(59)

because
\nu=0
and
h_{aA}\nabla^{A}\nu=h_{aA}l^{A}=0
on
N_{2}
. Likewise, we have

\displaystyle h_{aA}(\nabla^{B}\nu)\nabla^{A}(\lambda\nabla_{B}\alpha)=0.

(60)

If we use (
59
) and (
60
), then (
58
) becomes

\displaystyle[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{La}

\displaystyle=h_{aA}\{\alpha(\nabla^{B}\lambda)(\nabla^{A}\beta\cdot\nabla_{B}\nu+\beta\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}\nu)|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

(61)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\beta(\nabla^{B}\nu)(\nabla^{A}\alpha\cdot\nabla_{B}\lambda+\alpha\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}\lambda)\}|_{\lambda=\nu=0}.

This equation becomes, by the relation (
56
),

\displaystyle[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{La}=h_{aA}(\nabla^{B}\lambda\cdot\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}\nu-\nabla^{B}\nu\cdot\nabla^{A}\nabla_{B}\lambda)|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

\displaystyle\hskip 57.81621pt-2h_{aA}(\nabla^{A}\ln{\alpha})(\nabla^{B}\lambda\cdot\nabla_{B}\nu)|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

\displaystyle\hskip 43.36243pt=h_{aA}\{\nabla^{A}(\nabla^{B}\lambda\cdot\nabla_{B}\nu)-2(\nabla^{A}\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla_{B}\nu\}|_{\lambda=\nu=0}

\displaystyle\hskip 57.81621pt-2h_{aA}(\nabla^{A}\ln{\alpha})(\nabla^{B}\lambda\cdot\nabla_{B}\nu)|_{\lambda=\nu=0}.

(62)

By the identity
\nabla^{B}\lambda\cdot\nabla_{B}\nu=n^{B}l_{B}=-k^{2}
, the above equation becomes

\displaystyle[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{La}=-2(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla_{B}\nu|_{\lambda=\nu=0}+2k^{2}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\ln\alpha.

(63)

When
\alpha=1
,
n^{\prime}
and
l^{\prime}
become
n
and
l
, respectively, and the equation (
63
) becomes

\displaystyle[n,l]_{La}=-2(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\nabla^{B}\lambda)\nabla_{B}\nu|_{\lambda=\nu=0}.

(64)

Thus, the equation (
63
) can be written as

\displaystyle[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{La}=[n,l]_{La}+2k^{2}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\ln\alpha,

(65)

so that we have

\displaystyle\oint d\mu\frac{<\xi,[n^{\prime},l^{\prime}]_{\rm L}>}{<n^{\prime},l^{\prime}>}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint d\mu\Big(\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}-2\xi^{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\ln\alpha\Big)

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint d\mu\Big(\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}+2\ln\alpha\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}\Big).

Then,
L^{\prime}(u,v;\xi)
becomes

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(u,v;\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{1\over 16\pi}\oint d\mu\Big\{\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}+\ln(f\alpha^{2})\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}\Big\}+L_{0}

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L(u,v;\xi)+{1\over 16\pi}\oint d\mu\ \ln(\alpha^{2})\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}.

Thus, we find that the quasilocal angular momentum
is insensitive to the ways of labeling a given two-surface in the spacetime, i.e.,

\displaystyle L^{\prime}(u,v;\xi)=L(u,v;\xi),

(68)

if and only if

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=0.

(69)

Therefore, the quasilocal angular momentum is given by

L(u,v;\xi)=\frac{1}{16\pi}\oint d\mu\frac{<\xi,[n,l]_{\rm L}>}{<n,l>}+L_{0}\quad(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=0),

(70)

where
L_{0}
is an undetermined integration constant.

It is worth mentioning that the condition (
69
) is equivalent to
the condition that ensures the gauge-independence of quasilocal angular momentum defined by C.M. Liu and S.T. Yau
[
28
]
.
Notice that the condition (
69
) is trivially satisfied when
\xi
is a Killing vector field on
N_{2}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{(a}\xi_{b)}=0.

(71)

Obviously, the converse is not true; the divergence-free vector field
\xi

need not be a Killing vector field of
N_{2}
. Thus, the quasilocal angular momentum
L(u,v;\xi)
is well-defined even when
N_{2}
does not admit any isometries, provided that
\xi
is divergence-free!

One might ask whether it is always possible to foliate the whole spacetime
E_{4}
by
a given 2-dimensional surface
N_{2}
and question the stability of such foliation.
Works related to these issues can be found in
[
29
,
30
,
31
]
, where a “rigid-sphere foliation” with a non-constant mean curvature in a
3-dimensional Riemannian space was discussed.
It is also possible to refine the quasilocal angular momentum
L(u,v;\xi)
by further restricting divergence-free vector field
\xi^{a}
on
N_{2}
, which can be represented as

\xi^{a}=\epsilon^{ab}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}f+\xi_{0}^{a}

(72)

where
\xi_{0}^{a}
is the harmonic vector field. By selecting a suitable function
f
and

\xi_{0}^{a}
on
N_{2}
,
one may reduce the quasilocal angular momentum
L(\xi)
to a proper subclass that has special properties
[
29
]
.

The quasilocal quantities defined in (

The equation (

\displaystyle\hskip-36.135ptU(u,v)=\frac{1}{8\pi}\oint d^{2}ye^{\sigma}(D_{+}\sigma-\frac{h}{f}D_{-}\sigma)+U_{0}

\mathcal{A}

and

must be chosen
appropriately depending on the reference spacetime.
There are several works on how to fix these constants when the background spacetime is the Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{R}^{3,1}

With the Minkowski spacetime as the background spacetime,

5
Poisson algebra of quasilocal angular momentum

As was discussed in the previous section, the two-surface integral
L(u,v;\xi)
depends on the divergence-free vector field
\xi
. Let us assume that the integration constant
L_{0}=0
. By the constraint equation (
41
),
the two-surface integral can be also written as a 3-dimensional integral of the associated flux, namely,

\displaystyle L(u,v;\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16\pi}\int du\oint d^{2}y(\pi^{ab}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\rho_{ab}+\pi_{\sigma}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\sigma

(83)

\displaystyle\hskip 36.135pt+\pi_{h}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}h+\pi_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}A_{+}^{a}+\pi_{a}\partial_{+}{\xi}^{a})\quad(\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=0).

We prove the following theorem.

Theorem.
Let
\xi
and
\eta
be two arbitrary vector fields
tangent to
N_{2}
.
The Poisson bracket of quasilocal angular momentums associated with
\xi
and
\eta
is
given by the quasilocal angular momentum associated with the Lie bracket of
\xi
and
\eta
up to a constant factor. Precisely, it is given by

\displaystyle\{L(\xi),L(\eta)\}_{\rm{P.B.}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}L([\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}),

(84)

where the Poisson bracket of any two functionals
A
and
B
on a

v=
constant hypersurface
\Sigma
is defined by

\displaystyle\{A,B\}_{\rm P.B.}=\int du\oint d^{2}y\left(\frac{\delta A}{\delta q^{I}}\frac{\delta B}{\delta p_{I}}-\frac{\delta B}{\delta q^{I}}\frac{\delta A}{\delta p_{I}}\right).

(85)

Proof.
Let us notice that, for any
diff
(N_{2})
-tensor density
T_{b\cdots}^{\ c\cdots}
with weight 1, the following identity

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}T_{b\cdots}^{\ c\cdots}=\xi^{a}\partial_{a}T_{b\cdots}^{\ c\cdots}+T_{b\cdots}^{\ c\cdots}\partial_{a}\xi^{a}=\partial_{a}(\xi^{a}T_{b\cdots}^{\ c\cdots})

(86)

holds. Using this identity, one finds, after straightforward calculations,

\displaystyle\hskip-43.36243pt\{L(\xi),L(\eta)\}_{\rm P.B.}

\displaystyle=-\Big(\frac{1}{16\pi}\Big)^{2}\int du\oint d^{2}y\Big\{(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi^{ab})(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\rho_{ab})+(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi_{\sigma})(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\sigma)

(87)

\displaystyle+(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi_{h})(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}h)+(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\pi_{a})(\mathcal{L}_{\eta}A_{+}^{a}+\partial_{+}\eta^{a})-(\xi\leftrightarrow\eta)\ \Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip-43.36243pt=\Big(\frac{1}{16\pi}\Big)^{2}\int du\oint d^{2}y\Big\{(\pi^{ab}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\rho_{ab}+\pi_{\sigma}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\sigma+\pi_{h}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}h

\displaystyle+\pi_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}A_{+}^{a}+\pi_{a}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\partial_{+}\eta^{a}-(\xi\leftrightarrow\eta)\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip-43.36243pt=\Big(\frac{1}{16\pi}\Big)^{2}\int du\oint d^{2}y\Big\{(\pi^{ab}\mathcal{L}_{[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}}\rho_{ab}+\pi_{\sigma}\mathcal{L}_{[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}}\sigma+\pi_{h}\mathcal{L}_{[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}}h

\displaystyle+\pi_{a}\mathcal{L}_{[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}}A_{+}^{a}+\pi_{a}\partial_{+}[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}^{a}\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip-43.36243pt=\frac{1}{16\pi}L([\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}).

In the second to the last line of the above equation, we used the following identity

\displaystyle\hskip-28.90755pt\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\partial_{+}\eta^{a}-\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\partial_{+}\xi^{a}

\displaystyle=\xi^{b}\partial_{b}\partial_{+}\eta^{a}-(\partial_{+}\eta^{b})\partial_{b}\xi^{a}-(\xi\leftrightarrow\eta)

(88)

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}(\xi^{b}\partial_{b}\eta^{a})-(\partial_{+}\xi^{b})(\partial_{b}\eta^{a})-(\partial_{+}\eta^{b})\partial_{b}\xi^{a}-(\xi\leftrightarrow\eta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}(\xi^{b}\partial_{b}\eta^{a}-\eta^{b}\partial_{b}\xi^{a})

\displaystyle=\partial_{+}[\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}^{a}.

Let us show that the Lie bracket of any two divergence-free
vector fields is also divergence-free,

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}[\xi,\eta]_{L}^{\ a}=0\ \ {\rm if}\ \ \tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\eta^{a}=0.

(89)

Notice that the covariant divergence of the Lie bracket

[\xi,\eta]_{L}^{\ a}
defined by

[\xi,\eta]_{L}^{\ a}=\xi^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\eta^{a}-\eta^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\xi^{a}

(90)

is given by

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}[\xi,\eta]_{L}^{\ a}=\xi^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\eta^{a}-\eta^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\xi^{a}.

(91)

Using the 2-dimensional curvature tensor
R^{(2)\ c}_{abd}
and the Ricci tensor

R^{(2)}_{ad}
defined as

\tilde{\nabla}_{[a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b]}\eta^{c}=R^{(2)\ c}_{abd}\eta^{d},\quad R^{(2)\ b}_{abd}=R^{(2)\ }_{ad},

(92)

respectively, one finds that

\displaystyle\xi^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\eta^{a}=\xi^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{[a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b]}\eta^{a}=-R_{ab}^{(2)}\ \xi^{a}\eta^{b},

\displaystyle\eta^{b}\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\tilde{\nabla}_{b}\xi^{a}=-R_{ab}^{(2)}\ \xi^{a}\eta^{b},

(93)

for divergence-free vector fields
\xi^{a}
and
\eta^{a}
.
Therefore, it follows that

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}[\xi,\eta]_{L}^{\ a}=0.

(94)

It follows that, given two gauge-independent quasilocal angular momenta
L(\xi)
and
L(\eta)
, their Poisson bracket
L([\xi,\eta]_{\rm L})
is also gauge-independent.

6
Invariant quasilocal angular momentum
L^{2}

The quasilocal angular momentum
L(\xi)
given by (
44
) or (
51
) depends on the divergence-free vector field
\xi^{a}
defined on
N_{2}
.
By the Poincaré-Hopf theorem, for any compact regular 2-dimensional manifold with non-zero Euler characteristic, any continuous tangent vector field has at least one zero.
Therefore,
L(\xi)
fails to “measure” the rotation of gravitational fields
at these singular points of
\xi^{a}
, as was discussed in Introduction.

In this section, we propose a definition of
invariant
quasilocal angular momentum
L^{2}
of gravitational fields, which is free from the singularity of the divergence-free tangent vector field
\xi^{a}
and commutes with
L(\xi)
,

\{L^{2},L(\xi)\}_{\rm P.B.}=0.

(95)

It is defined as an invariant integral
over
N_{2}
,

L^{2}(u,v):=\frac{1}{96\pi}\Big(\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4\pi}\Big)^{2}\oint d^{2}y\ e^{-2\sigma}\rho^{ab}\pi_{a}\pi_{b},

(96)

where the integrand is a scalar density with weight
1
, and
\mathcal{A}
is the area of
N_{2}
. The factor
({\mathcal{A}}/{4\pi})^{2}
makes
L^{2}
to have the correct dimension of angular momentum squared.

In order to show the equation (
95
) is true,
let us first notice that
L(\xi)
is the generating function of diffeomorphisms of
N_{2}

along
\xi^{a}
. This can be seen from the Poisson algebra (
84
), which can be written as

\displaystyle\{\oint d^{2}y^{\prime\prime}\ \xi^{a}(y^{\prime\prime})\pi_{a}(y^{\prime\prime}),\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\ \eta^{b}(y^{\prime})\pi_{b}(y^{\prime})\}_{\rm P.B.}=\oint d^{2}y\ [\xi,\eta]^{a}\pi_{a}.

(97)

Let us choose the test function
\xi^{a}(y^{\prime\prime})
in (
97
) as

\xi^{a}(y^{\prime\prime})=\delta^{(2)}(y^{\prime\prime}-y)\ \delta^{a}_{j}

(98)

for fixed
j
. Then, the equation (
97
) becomes

\displaystyle\{\pi_{j},\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\ \eta^{b}(y^{\prime})\pi_{b}(y^{\prime})\}_{\rm P.B.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\ [\xi(y^{\prime}),\eta(y^{\prime})]^{a}\pi_{a}(y^{\prime})

(99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\ \pi_{j}(y^{\prime})\mathcal{L}_{\eta(y^{\prime})}\delta^{(2)}(y^{\prime}-y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\pi_{j}.

Thus, we find that

\{\pi_{j},L(\eta)\}_{\rm P.B.}={1\over 16\pi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\pi_{j}.

(100)

Also notice that

\displaystyle\hskip-57.81621pt\{\rho^{ij}(y),\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\ \eta^{b}(y^{\prime})\pi_{b}(y^{\prime})\}_{\rm P.B.}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\rho^{ij}(y),\int du^{\prime}\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\pi^{ab}(y^{\prime})\mathcal{L}_{\eta(y^{\prime})}\rho_{ab}(y^{\prime})\}_{\rm P.B.}

(101)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int du^{\prime}\oint d^{2}y^{\prime}\{\rho^{ij}(y),\pi^{ab}(y^{\prime})\}_{\rm P.B.}\mathcal{L}_{\eta(y^{\prime})}\rho_{ab}(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\eta(y)}\rho^{ij}(y),

where we used the equation (
83
) in the first line.
Therefore, it follows that

\{\rho^{ij},L(\eta)\}_{\rm P.B.}={1\over 16\pi}\mathcal{L}_{\eta}\rho^{ij}.

(102)

In a similar way, we find that

9
Discussion

It is remarkable that two-surface integrals of the Einstein’s constraint equations in the (2+2) Hamiltonian formalism are split into terms that can be geometrically interpreted as quasilocal energy, linear momentum, angular momentum on the two-surface and
the corresponding fluxes crossing that surface. In particular, we have shown that the Poisson algebra of the quasilocal angular momentum associated with an arbitrary tangent vector field
\xi
on
N_{2}
is closed,

\displaystyle\{L(\xi),L(\eta)\}_{\rm P.B.}=\frac{1}{16\pi}L([\xi,\eta]_{\rm L}).

(151)

Furthermore, we showed that the necessary and sufficient condition that ensures the gauge-independence of
the quasilocal angular momentum is

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\xi^{a}=0,

(152)

where
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
is the covariant derivative on
N_{2}
. The divergence-free vector field
\xi

need not be a Killing vector field on
N_{2}
, and therefore the quasilocal angular momentum
L(\xi)
is well-defined even in the absence of any isometries.
However, as was discussed in Section
6
, any continuous tangent vector field has at least one zero for any 2-dimensional compact manifold with non-zero Euler characteristic, so that
L(\xi)
fails to measure the rotation of gravitational fields at the singular points of
\xi^{a}
on such
N_{2}
.
This observation led us to introduce
the invariant quasilocal angular momentum
L^{2}
of gravitational fields on a given

N_{2}
, which is free from the singularity of the divergence-free tangent vector field
\xi^{a}

and commutes with
L(\xi)
,

\{L^{2},L(\xi)\}_{\rm P.B.}=0,

(153)

and becomes

\lim_{v\to\infty}L^{2}=(ma)^{2}\

(154)

at the null infinity of the Kerr spacetime. Therefore, we conjectured the invariant quasilocal angular momentum
L^{2}
given by (
96
) as a gravitational analog of the Casimir invariant of ordinary angular momentum.

We also showed that the asymptotic limit of our quasilocal angular momentum is identical to the geometric angular momentum of A. Rizzi at null infinity, and that it satisfies the
SO(3)
commutation relations

\displaystyle\{\bar{L}_{\alpha},\bar{L}_{\beta}\}_{\rm P.B.}=-\frac{1}{16\pi}{\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}}^{\gamma}\bar{L}_{\gamma}.

(155)

In spite of the agreement of the two definitions in the asymptotic limit, there are differences that must be mentioned.
Firstly, Rizzi’s angular momentum requires the affine foliation
of spacetimes at null infinity, whereas our angular momentum does not even at a finite distance. Our quasilocal angular momentum is based on the (2+2) fibre bundle decomposition
of spacetimes, where in- and out-going null vector fields need not be affinely parametrized
at all.

Secondly, our quasilocal angular momentum, though obtained in a canonical formalism,
admits a natural geometrical interpretation, whereas Rizzi’s definition is purely geometrical that lacks a canonical interpretation. Moreover, by the Einstein’s constraint equation, our quasilocal angular momentum can be also represented as a 3-dimensional integral of flux, as is shown in (
83
). Using this
3-dimensional integral representation of quasilocal angular momentum,
we were able to show that the Poisson algebra of our quasilocal angular momentum
reduces to the
SO(3)
algebra at the null infinity.
It would be hard to obtain this algebra from Rizzi’s definition even at null infinity,
due to the lack of the canonical representation.

Thirdly, unlike Rizzi’s definition, our quasilocal angular momentum and its flux integrals follow directly from the Einstein’s constraints. However,
it must be also said that
the splitting of the Einstein’s constraint into angular momentum and its flux part is not unique;
one can always add an arbitrary quantity to each part without changing the constraint equation as a whole. By the criteria of a satisfactory definition of quasilocal angular momentum stated in Section
1
, however, one can reasonably eliminate such ambiguities. Thus, our expressions of quasilocal angular momentum and its flux are fairly unique.

Finally, one might try to find the Poisson algebra and its limit of quasilocal gravitational energy, linear and angular momentum, and see whether they form the Poincarè
algebra or its BMS generalization in the asymptotic limit. This problem is interesting in its own right, and will be reported in a forthcoming paper.

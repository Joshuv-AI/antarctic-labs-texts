Abstract
We used the B 138 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 138$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{B} a ( d , α ) (d,\alpha) reaction to carry out an in-depth study of states in Cs 136 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cs} , up to around 2.5 MeV. In this work, we place emphasis on hitherto unobserved states below the first 1 + 1^{+} level, which are important in the context of solar neutrino and fermionic dark matter (FDM) detection in large-scale xenon experiments. We identify for the first time candidate metastable states in Cs 136 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cs} , which would allow a real-time detection of solar neutrino and FDM events in xenon detectors, with high background suppression. Our results are also compared with shell-model calculations performed with three Hamiltonians that were previously used to evaluate the nuclear matrix element (NME) for X 136 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{X} e neutrinoless double beta decay. We find that one of these Hamiltonians, which also systematically underestimates the NME compared to the others, dramatically fails to describe the observed low-energy C 136 {}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{C} s spectrum, while the other two show reasonably good agreement.

In conclusion, we used
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 138$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Ba}
(d,\alpha)
angular distribution measurements, together with shell-model calculations to report the location of possible metastable states with
J\geq 1
in the odd-odd
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cs}
nucleus. The observed new states offer an opportunity for high background rejection, and open new possibilities for the detection of solar
\nu_{e}
events and/or FDM interactions in large xenon detectors.
We unambiguously identify the first excited state in
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cs}
, which is has spin-parity
3^{+}
and would decay to the
5^{+}
ground state via a slow
E2
transition. Our findings are supported by a recent independent study
Haselschwardt et al. 2023
that measured the lifetime of the
3_{1}^{+}
state to be
\tau=157(4)
ns. We also compare our experimental results with shell-model predictions made with three Hamiltonians that were previously used to evaluate the
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Xe}

0\nu 2\beta
NME. The comparison shows that one of the Hamiltonians (QX), which also systematically underestimates the NME compared to the others, fails to accurately describe the
{}^{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle 136$}}_{\makebox[11.95839pt][r]{$\scriptstyle$}}\mathrm{Cs}
spectrum. As this inadequacy may have been obscured when predictions were compared with experimental data on even-even nuclei, one might disfavor
0\nu 2\beta
results obtained with this
Hamiltonian.

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the heat equation with a strongly singular potential and show that it has a very weak solution. Our analysis is devoted to general hypoelliptic operators and is developed in the setting of graded Lie groups. The current work continues and extends the work [ ARST21c ] , where the classical heat equation on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} was considered.



1.
Introduction

Baras and Goldstein
[
BG84a
,
BG84b
]
studied the heat equation with the singular potential in bounded and unbounded domains of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that initiated a series of further works on the topic; see
[
ARST21c
]
for a more detailed exposition of them. Later on, Goldstein and Zhang in
[
GZ01
]
studied the heat equation with singular potentials of certain type on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{n}
, where the usual Laplace operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
was replaced by the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}_{n}
.

Our consideration here is more broad not only in the sense that we allow for the setting to be any graded Lie group and the differential operator to be any positive Rockland operator, but we also allow for the potential to be a of any sign distributional function. Specifically, the following Cauchy problem is considered:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}u_{t}(t,x)+\mathcal{R}u(t,x)+V(x)u(t,x)=0\,,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{G}\,,T>0\,,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\,,x\in{\mathbb{G}}\,,\end{cases}

where
{\mathbb{G}}
is a graded Lie group,
{\mathcal{R}}
is any positive Rockland operator on
{\mathbb{G}}
,
V
is a distributional function and
T>0
is any real number.

The potential
V
is allowed to have
\delta
-type singularities. To conquer the problem of multiplication of distributions
[
Sch54
]
we follow the theory of very weak solutions as introduced in
[
GR15
]
where the theory initially applied on wave equations with irregular coefficients. To give an overview of the topic of very weak solutions we refer to the works
[
RT17b
]
and
[
MRT19
]

on the analysis of the problems with singular, time-dependent coefficients on the Euclidean setting. The application of the concept of the very weak solutions in the setting of a graded Lie group, appears in
[
RY20
,
CRT21
,
CRT21b
]
.

In this work we show that the notion of the very weak solution is applicable to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), and, when the classical solution exists, it converges to very weak one. The present work can be served as an extension of its first part
[
ARST21c
]
addressed to the heat equation in the Euclidean setting; i.e., the consideration there expressed with the present terminology becomes
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{R}=-\Delta
being the positive fractional Laplacian on the Euclidean space. The novelty of this work, that extends the realisation of the problem already in the Euclidean setting, relies on two main considerations: we allow the potential
V
in (
1.1
) to be of any sign, and extend the functional space of
V
by making use of Sobolev embedding techniques. Finally, let us draw the reader’s attention to Remarks
4.13
and
5.5
which rectify or clarify the statements of the first consideration
[
ARST21c
]
on the notion of the uniqueness of the very weak solution, and on its recapture of the classical one, respectively.

Notions and terminology

Let us briefly recall some, useful for our purposes, notions in the setting of graded groups. What follows is a shorter version of the introduction given in the recent papers by the same authors; see
[
CRT21
,
CRT21b
]
for some other aspects of this material. Interested readers can consult the detailed exposition by Folland and Stein [Chapter 1 in
[
FS82
]
]. A more recent comprehensive approach on the topic can be found in the work of Fischer and the second author; see [Chapter 3 in
[

2.1.
Graded Lie groups

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a connected simply connected Lie group. We call
{\mathbb{G}}
a
graded
Lie group if the next condition is satisfied for its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
:

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}\,,

where all, but finitely many elements of the below vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
’s, are equal to
\{0\}
. Additionally, the actions of each
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
shall satisfy
[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
, where we have set
\mathfrak{g}_{0}:=\mathfrak{g}
.

Graded Lie groups are
homogeneous
and their natural relevant structure is the so-called “canonical” dilations which appears also, due to the diffeomorphism of the two, as the structure of the corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{n}
are the weights of the dilations, i.e., the powers appearing in the automorphism, denoted by
D_{r}
,
r>0
, that is expressed as

(2.1)

D_{r}(x)=rx=(r^{\nu_{1}}x_{1},\cdots,r^{\nu_{n}}x_{n})\,,x\in{\mathbb{G}}\,,

then the
homogeneous dimension

Q
of the group is computed via the expression

Q:=\textnormal{Tr}A=\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}\,.

We invite the reader to refer to the examples of the Heisenberg group [Chapter 6 in
[
FR16
]
] and of the Engel and Cartan groups
[
Cha21
]
as the most well-studied cases of homogeneous groups.

2.2.
Representations and Rockland operators

We denote by
\pi
the representation of the group
{\mathbb{G}}
on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
, and keep the same notation for the representation of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. The latter may as well be extended to the universal enveloping Lie algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
, and is exactly the mapping
\pi:\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\textnormal{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is the space of smooth vectors. Elements of
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
are precisely the left-invariant operators on
{\mathbb{G}}
, and as such, they can be written in a unique way as a finite sum of the form

(2.2)

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{c}}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\,,

where we use the notation
X^{\alpha}:=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
, for some multi-index
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
.
The element
\pi\in\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}
acts on homogeneous of order
\nu
left-invariant differential operator
{\mathcal{R}}
in the following natural way:

\pi({\mathcal{R}})=\sum_{[\alpha]=\nu}c_{\alpha}\pi(X)^{\alpha}\,,

where
[\alpha]=\nu_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{n}\alpha_{n}
stands for the homogeneous length of the multi-index
\alpha
, and we have used the notation

\pi(X)^{\alpha}=\pi(X^{\alpha})=\pi(X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}),

where
X_{j}
is of homogeneous of degree
{\nu}_{j}
arising from the dilation weights in (
2.1
).
Important operators that are elements of
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
are the so-called
Rockland operators
on
{\mathbb{G}}
as first appeared in
[
Roc78
]
. The most trivial example of a Rockland operator is the so-called
sub-Laplacian
on a stratified group, which is exactly the sum of squares of vector fields that generate the first stratum of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. In the trivial case
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
the latter boils down to the Laplace differential operator. In their general consideration, Rockland operators are left-invariant differential operators on
{\mathbb{G}}
that are densely defined on
\mathcal{D}({\mathbb{G}})\subset L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
, that are homogeneous of some positive degree and satisfy the so-called
Rockland condition
; that is when the operator
\pi(\mathcal{{\mathcal{R}}})
is injective on the space of smooth vectors
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
on which
\pi({\mathcal{R}})
is densely defined. We mention that for the groups that we consider here we have
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
; see [Corollary 4.1.2 in
[
CG90
]
]. For a detailed discussion on the Rockland operator, and in particular for alternative characterisations of the Rockland condition, one can consult
[
Bea77
,
HN79
]
. By the spectral theorem for unbounded self-adjoint operators, both
{\mathcal{R}}
and
\pi({\mathcal{R}})
have a spectral decomposition associated with the spectral measures of them; see e.g. Theorem VIII.6 in
[
RS85
]
. The spectral properties of the operator
\pi({\mathcal{R}})
were examined in
[
TR97
]
. In
[
HJL85
]
the authors proved that the spectrum of the operator
\pi({\mathcal{R}})
, for
\pi\in\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}\setminus\{1\}
, is purely discrete and positive. This in turn allows for an infinite matrix representation of the following form:

(2.3)

\pi({\mathcal{R}})=\begin{pmatrix}\pi_{1}^{2}&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\pi_{2}^{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\,.

Particularly, the spectrum of these operators in the setting of the Heisenberg and Engel group, has been studied using the representations of Taylor
[
Tay84
]
and of Dixmier [p. 333
[
Dix57
]
], respectively, are investigated in e.g. [Chapter 6 in
[
FR16
]
] and
[
CDR18
]
.

2.3.
Group Fourier transform

For
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{G}})
and for
\pi\in\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}
the group Fourier transform of
f
at
\pi
is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{G}}}f(\pi)\equiv\widehat{f}(\pi)\equiv\pi(f):=\int_{{\mathbb{G}}}f(x)\pi(x)^{*}\,dx\,,

where we integrate against the binvariant Haar measure on
{\mathbb{G}}
. This produces a linear endomorphism on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
. Thanks to e.g. Kirillov’s orbit method (e.g.
[
CG90
,
Kir04
]
) one can explicitly construct the Plancherel measure
\mu
on the dual
\widehat{{\mathbb{G}}}
. This brings the Fourier inversion formula at our disposal, and additionally allows for the extension of the Euclidean Plancherel formula to the setting of graded Lie groups; i.e., we have the isometry:

(2.4)

\int_{{\mathbb{G}}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\widehat{{{\mathbb{G}}}}}\|\pi(f)\|^{2}_{\textnormal{HS}}\,d\mu(\pi)\,,

since the operator
\pi(f)
is Hilbert-Schmidt; that is we have
\|\pi(f)\|^{2}_{\textnormal{HS}}:=\textnormal{Tr}(\pi(f)\pi(f)^{*})<\infty
. It makes sense to define the group Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{G}}}({\mathcal{R}}f)(\pi)
, and the functional calculus allows for the identity
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{G}}}({\mathcal{R}}f)(\pi)=\pi({\mathcal{R}})\pi(f)
. The last expression and the representation in (
2.3
) allow for a matrix representation of the operator
\mathcal{F}_{{\mathbb{G}}}({\mathcal{R}}f)(\pi)
of the form
\{\pi_{k}^{2}\hat{f}(\pi)_{k,l}\}_{k,l\in\mathbb{N}}
that shall be useful for our purposes.

2.4.
Sobolev spaces and embedding results

The (homogeneous)
{\mathcal{R}}
-Sobolev spaces
in the setting of a graded Lie groups are defined by the second author and Fischer in
[
FR17
]
: for a fixed positive homogeneous Rockland operator
{\mathcal{R}}
of some homogeneous degree
\nu
, and for
s>0
,
p>1
, they are defined as the completion of
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{G}})\cap\textnormal{Dom}({\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{s}{\nu}})
for the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{p}_{s}({\mathbb{G}})}:=\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}_{p}f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{G}})\cap\textnormal{Dom}({\mathcal{R}}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}_{p})\,,

where
{\mathcal{R}}_{p}
is the maximal restriction of
{\mathcal{R}}
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{G}})
.
1
1

1

When
p=2
, we will write
{\mathcal{R}}_{2}={\mathcal{R}}
for the self-adjoint extension of
{\mathcal{R}}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
.

Sobolev embeddings read as follows:
for
1<\tilde{q}_{0}<q_{0}<\infty
and for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying the condition

b-a=Q\left(\frac{1}{\tilde{q}_{0}}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}\right)\,,

we have the continuous inclusions
\dot{L}^{\tilde{q}_{0}}_{b}({\mathbb{G}})\subset\dot{L}^{q_{0}}_{a}({\mathbb{G}})
. The latter means that that, if

f\in\dot{L}^{\tilde{q}_{0}}_{b}({\mathbb{G}})
, then, there exists a constant
C=C(\tilde{q}_{0},q_{0},a,b)>0
independent of
f
such that

(2.5)

\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{q_{0}}_{a}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{\tilde{q}_{0}}_{b}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

In the sequel we will make use of the following notation:

Notation 2.1
.

•

When we write
a\lesssim b
, we mean that there exists some constant
c>0
(independent of any involved parameter) such that
a\leq cb
;

•

if
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is some multi-index, then we denote by

|\alpha|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\,,

the length of it;

•

for
s>0
and for suitable
f\in\mathcal{S}^{{}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{G}})
, we have introduced the following norm

(2.6)

\|f\|_{H^{s}({\mathbb{G}})}:=\|f\|_{\dot{L}^{2}_{s}({\mathbb{G}})}+\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,;

•

when regulisations of functions/distributions on
{\mathbb{G}}
are considered, they must be regarded as arising via convolution with Friedrichs-mollifiers; that is,
\psi
is a Friedrichs-mollifier, if it is a compactly supported smooth function with
\int_{{\mathbb{G}}}\psi\,dx=1
. Then the regularising net is defined as

(2.7)

\psi_{\epsilon}(x)=\omega(\epsilon)^{-Q}\psi(D_{\omega(\epsilon)^{-1}}(x))\,,\quad\epsilon\in(0,1]\,,

where
\omega(\epsilon)
is a positive function converging to zero as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and
Q
stands for the homogeneous dimension of
{\mathbb{G}}
.

3.
Estimates for the classical solution

The results of the current section, and the forthcoming ones, fall into two categories; those where the potential
V
as appears in (
1.1
) is assumed to be non-negative, and those where the potential can be of any sign. In the latter case the potential
V
can only belong to the space
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
, while in the positive case it can also belong to the space
L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
, provided that the condition
Q>\nu
is satisfied.

Proposition 3.1

(Classical solution, case I:
V\geq 0
)
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
, where
V\geq 0
, and suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
. Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{G}}))\cap C([0,T];H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}}))
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), that satisfies the estimate

(3.1)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

Multiplying the equation (
1.1
) by
u_{t}
and integrating over
\mathbb{G}
, we get

\Re(\langle u_{t}(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}+\langle\mathcal{R}u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\langle V(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),u_{t}(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})})=0\,,

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\Re
stands for the real part of the above quantity. The above equation can be rewritten as

\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\left[\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{V}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\right]=0\,.

We define the energy as

E(t):=\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}+\|\sqrt{V}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\,,

which variation in time satisfies
\partial_{t}E(t)\leq 0
, and consequently

(3.2)

E(t)\leq E(0)\,,\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in[0,T]\,.

Straightforward computations give

\|\sqrt{V}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\leq\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\,,

so that equation (
3.2
) can be rephrased as the uniform in time
t\in[0,T]
estimates

(3.3)

\|\sqrt{V}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\lesssim\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\,,

and

(3.4)

\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\lesssim\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\,.

Recalling the definition of the sum of norms
H^{s}({\mathbb{G}})
in (
2.6
) we can estimate (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) further by

(3.5)

\|\sqrt{V}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\left(1+\|V\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

and

(3.6)

\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\left(1+\|V\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

respectively.

To proceed we need to deal with the term
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}
; let us rephrase the problem (
1.1
) as follows:

(3.7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\mathcal{R}u(t,x)=f(t,x)\,,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{G},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\,\\
\end{cases}

where we have set as a source term
f(t,x):=-V(x)u(t,x)
. Applying Duhamel’s principle (see, e.g.
[
Ev98
]
), we deduce that the solution to (
3.7
) is given by

(3.8)

u(t,x)=(h_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)+\int_{0}^{t}h_{t-s}\ast f_{s}(x)\,ds\,,

where
f_{s}(\cdot)=f(s,\cdot)
, and
h_{t}(\cdot)=h(t,\cdot)
with
h
being the fundamental solution, or the heat kernel, associated to the Rockland operator
{\mathcal{R}}
. We recall (see, e.g. Theorem 4.2.7
[
FR16
]
) the following upper bound for the heat kernel norm

\|h_{t}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq 1\,.

We take the
L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
-norm of the expression on (
3.8
). Young’s inequality implies for the convolution there

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|h_{t}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|h_{t-s}\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{G}})}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{s}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|V(\cdot)u(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,ds\,.

Straightforward computations then give

(3.10)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}+\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

Putting together (
3.6
) and (
3.10
) we obtain

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim(1+\|V\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}\,,

as required, and the proof of Proposition
3.1
is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Classical solution, case II:
V\geq 0
)
.

Assume that
Q>\nu
, and let
V\in L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
,
V\geq 0
. If we suppose that
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
, then there exists a unique solution
u\in C^{1}([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{G}}))\cap C([0,T];H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}}))
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfying the estimate

(3.11)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\left\{\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\,,

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We apply Hölder’s inequality to the norm
\|\sqrt{V}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}
for
1<q,q^{{}^{\prime}}<\infty
, so that

(3.12)

\|\sqrt{V}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\leq\|V\|_{L^{q^{{}^{\prime}}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2q}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

where
(q,q^{{}^{\prime}})
are conjugate exponents. We use the embeddings (
2.5
) for
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
,
b=\frac{\nu}{2}
,
a=0
, and
q_{0}=\frac{2Q}{Q-\nu}
. This leads to the estimate

(3.13)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{q_{0}}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}<\infty\,.

Putting together (
3.12
) for
q=\frac{q_{0}}{2}
, so that
q^{\prime}=\frac{Q}{\nu}
, and (
3.13
) we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{V}u_{0}\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\,.

Using the energy bounds (
3.2
) from
Proposition
3.1
, we have

\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}+\|\sqrt{V}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\leq\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\,,

and the latter, if rephrased, leads to the estimate

(3.15)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{L}^{2}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

The estimate (
3.9
) for the
L^{2}
-norm of
u(t,\cdot)
combined with the estimate (
3.14
) with
V
and
u(t,\cdot)
in place of
\sqrt{V}
and
u_{0}
, respectively, and inequality (
3.15
) gives

(3.16)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\|V^{2}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\left(1+\|V\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

Putting together the estimate (
3.16
) with the one in (
3.15
) we get the requested estimate for the
H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}
-norm of
u(t,\cdot)
. The proof of Proposition
3.2
is complete.
∎

The following result applies to the case where the potential
V
is of any sign. In this general case, we can ease the assumption on the initial data
u_{0}
, and, as a result, we control only the
L^{2}
-norm of the classical solution. In particular we have:

Proposition 3.3

(Classical solution, case
V\in\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
and suppose that
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
is a real function/distribution. Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{G}}))
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) that satisfies the estimate

(3.17)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\exp(t\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})})\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

We multiply the heat equation (
1.1
) by
u
and integrating on the group
{\mathbb{G}}
and obtain the equality:

\Re(\langle u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}+\langle\mathcal{R}u(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\langle V(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})})=0\,,

for all
t\in[0,T]
. The last equality can be rewritten as

(3.18)

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}+\|\sqrt{V^{+}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}=\|\sqrt{V^{-}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
V^{+},V^{-}
are the positive and negative parts of
V
, respectively.
Straightforward computations yield

\|\sqrt{V^{-}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|\sqrt{V^{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|\sqrt{|V|}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

Thus, equation (
3.18
) implies for the time-derivative of
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}
:

(3.19)

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\leq\|\sqrt{|V|}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}^{2}\,,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Hence we can apply Grönwall’s inequality to (
3.19
), so that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\,ds\right)\,,

and we obtain the desired estimate (
3.17
). This completes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let us point out that, under the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
, the solution
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), belongs also to the Sobolev space of order
\frac{\nu}{2}
: Indeed, by (
3.18
) we get

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{L}^{2}_{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}=\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\sqrt{V^{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,,

with the right-hand side of the last inequality being, by the assumptions of Proposition
3.3
, finite.

4.
Very weak well-posedeness

In what follows we shall show that the notion of the very weak solution as introduced in
[
GR14
]
is applicable to the heat equation (
1.1
) we consider here. Once the existence of the very weak solution is proven, we then investigate how the latter depends on the
\omega(\epsilon)
-scale or the approximation of the associated, to the initial data
u_{0}
and to the coefficient
V
, nets. In other words, we analyse the stability, or formally the

, of the very weak solution under the aforesaid modifications.

Let us recall the definitions of moderateness for a net of functions/distributions in space (and in time) as in
[
CTR21
]
and
[

]
.

Definition 4.1

(Moderateness)
.

Let
X
be a normed space of functions/distributions and let
\omega(\epsilon)
be as in (
2.7
).

(1)

A net of functions/distributions
(f_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}\in X
is said to be

-moderate
if there exists

such that

\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{X}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{-N}\,,

for all
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.

(2)

A net of functions/distribitions
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}=(u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot))_{\epsilon}
in
C([0,T];X)
is said to be
C([0,T];X)
-moderate
if there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{X}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{-N}\,,

for all
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.

Remark 4.2
.

Trivially, for nets that arise as regularisations of a distribitional function in some normed space

via a mollifier the

-moderate assumption is satisfied. More generally, see Proposition

below, the global structure of compaclty supported distributions

on the group, gives rise to

-moderate regularised such nets. We note that following the arguments developed in the proof of Proposition

we can show that the regularised net
(v_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is
\dot{L}^{2}_{s}({\mathbb{G}})
, and so also
H^{s}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate for every

.

Let us recall the following result as in

[
CTR21
,
CTR21b
]
:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
v\in\mathcal{E}^{{}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{G}})
, and let

v_{\epsilon}=v*\psi_{\epsilon}
be obtained as
the convolution of
v
with a mollifier
\psi_{\epsilon}
as in (

).
Then the regularising net
(v_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is
L^{p}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate for any

.

Describing the uniqueness of the very weak solutions amounts to “measuring” the changes on involved associated nets:
negligibility
conditions for nets of functions/di-stributions read as follows:

Definition 4.4

(Negligibility)
.

Let
Y
be a normed space of functions on
{\mathbb{G}}
.
Let
(f_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
,
(\tilde{f}_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
be two nets. Then, the net
(f_{\epsilon}-\tilde{f}_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is called

-
negligible
, if the following condition is satisfied

(4.1)

\|f_{\epsilon}-\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}\|_{Y}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{k}\,,

for all

,
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. In the case where

is a net depending also on

, then the

\|f_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)-\tilde{f}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{Y}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{k}\,,\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\,,

uniformly in

. The constant in the inequality (
4.1
) can depend on

but not on

.

Definition
4.5
and Definition
4.6
below introduce the notion of the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (

). The two definitions differ depending on the sign of the potential

and shall be used accordingly. However, in principle, one could simply suggest to use only Definition

where

Let us clarify that in both definitions the approximating net

shall be regarded as the regularisation of

via convolution with a Fredrichs-mollifier

as in (

). Regarding the potential

, the approximating net

is an expansion of such idea, that encloses the cases where

or

. In the latter case we realise

as an approximating family or in the Colombeau sense, and define

as

. We refer to Remark

Before stating the below definitions let us formulate the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in its “
\epsilon
-parametrised” version:

(4.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\mathcal{R}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)+V_{\epsilon}(x)u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=0\,,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{G}\,,\\
u_{\epsilon}(0,x)=u_{0,\epsilon}(x),\,x\in{\mathbb{G}}\,,\end{cases}

Definition 4.5

(Very weak solution, case
V\geq 0
)
.

Let
V
in (
1.1
) be non-negative. If there exists

•

a
L^{\infty}
-moderate, or (provided that

) a
L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate approximation of
V
;

•

a
H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate approximation of
u_{0}
;

•

and the solution to the Cauchy problem (
4.2
)
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is
C([0,T];H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}}))
-moderate,

then,
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is said to be
a very weak solution
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the case of a non-negative potential
V
.

Definition 4.6

(Very weak solution, case
V\in\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
V
be any real function. If there exists

•

a
L^{\infty}
-moderate approximation of
V
;

•

a
H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate approximation of

;

•

and the solution to the Cauchy problem (
4.2
)
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is
C([0,T];L^{2}({\mathbb{G}}))
-moderate,

then,
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
is said to be
a very weak solution
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) in the case of a real potential
V
.

As mention earlier in Remark
4.2
, proving the existence of a very weak solution means to prove that there exist suitable approximations of the initial data
u_{0}
and of the coefficient
V
such that the approximated problem (
4.2
) has a suitable moderate solution. In the next two theorems we prove such existence under suitable assumptions depending on the sign of
V
.

Theorem 4.7

(Existence of the very weak solution, case
V\geq 0
)
.

Let
V
be non-negative. Let also
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
and
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
, or (provided that
Q>\nu
),
V\in L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
.
Then, the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) exists.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. We first treat the case where
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. If
V_{\epsilon},u_{o,\epsilon}
are regularised (via convolution with a Friedrichs’ mollifier) nets, then they satisfy the moderateness assumptions of Definition
4.5
; i.e., we have

\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})},\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\epsilon^{-n_{0}}\,,

where we have chosen
\omega(\epsilon)=\epsilon
, for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, using the estimate (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})})\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-n_{0}}\times\epsilon^{-n_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-2n_{0}}\,,

uniformly in
t
, and this shows that the net
u_{0,\epsilon}
is
C([0,T];H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}}))
-moderate, as required. Next we treat the case where
V\in L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
. Consequently, for the net
V_{\epsilon}
as before, the
L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderateness assumptions for
\omega(\epsilon)=\epsilon
and for some
n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
are guaranteed. Thus, using the estimate (
3.11
), we can write

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}\left\{\left(1+\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\left(1+\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-n_{0}}\times\epsilon^{-n_{1}}\times\epsilon^{-\frac{n_{1}}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-n}\,,

for some
n
, and the proof of Theorem
4.7
is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.8

(Existence of the very weak solution, case
V\in\mathbb{R}
)
.

Let
V
be a real function. Let also
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
and
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. Then, the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) exists.

Proof.

By the assumptions of the theorem, the
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderateness assumptions of the net
V_{\epsilon}
are satisfied; i.e., for some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{-n_{0}}\,.

Taking
\omega
as
\omega(\epsilon):=(\log\epsilon^{-n_{0}})^{-\frac{1}{n_{0}}}
, where
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that the moderateness assumptions can be expressed in the form

\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{-n_{0}}=\log(\epsilon^{-n_{0}})\,.

For the net
u_{0,\epsilon}
the
L^{2}
-moderateness can be simply regarded as

\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\epsilon^{-n_{1}}\,,\quad n_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\,.

Using the estimate (
3.17
) for the solution to (
1.1
) in the case of a real potential we deduce that

\displaystyle\|u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\exp(t\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})})\|u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-tn_{0}}\times\epsilon^{-n_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-Tn_{0}-n_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-n}\,,

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and this completes the proof of Theorem
4.8
.
∎

.

the very weak solution

to the Cauchy problem (

) is also

The proof is an immediate consequence of Theorem

.

.

As mentioned in the beginning of this section, proving the well-posedness to the Cauchy problem (

Remark 4.13
.

In the work

]
, Definitions 4,5 and 8 on the uniqueness of the very weak solution to the heat equation in the Euclidean setting considered there, are a less meticulous version of Definitions

Let us highlight Remark

.

•

The following technical lemma is useful for our purposes in the case of a non-negative
V
.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
V
be non-negative. Assume also that
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
and
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
, or
V\in L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
, provided that
Q>\nu
. Then, for the unique solution
u
to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) we have the energy estimate

(4.5)

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

for all
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof.

If we multiply equation (
1.1
) by
u
, and integrate over
{\mathbb{G}}
we derive

\Re(\langle u_{t}(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}+\langle\mathcal{R}u(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\langle V(\cdot)u(t,\cdot),u(t,\cdot)\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})})=0\,,

where the last inequality can be rewritten as

\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}=-\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}-\|\sqrt{V}(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|{{}^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}}\leq 0\,,

and the claim (
4.5
) follows.
∎

Theorem 4.16

(Uniqueness of the very weak solution, case I:
V\geq 0
)
.

Let
V
be non-negative. Let also
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. The following statements hold true:

•

If
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
, then the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique;

•

if
Q>\nu
and
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
, then the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique.

Proof.

We denote by
u_{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}
the families of solutions to the Cauchy problems (
4.4
) and (
4.3
), respectively. Setting
U_{\epsilon}
to be the difference of these nets
U_{\epsilon}:=u_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)-\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
, then
U_{\epsilon}
solves

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U_{\epsilon}(t,x)+{\mathcal{R}}U_{\epsilon}(t,x)+V_{\epsilon}(x)U_{\epsilon}(t,x)=f_{\epsilon}(t,x)\,,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times{\mathbb{G}}\,,\\
U_{\epsilon}(0,x)=(u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon})(x)\,,\quad x\in{\mathbb{G}}\,,\end{cases}

where we set
f_{\epsilon}(t,x):=(\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}(x)-V_{\epsilon}(x))\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,x)
for the mass term to the inhomogeneous Cauchy problem (
4.6
).

We aim to express the solution to (
4.6
) in terms of the solutions to the corresponding homogeneous problems: fix
\sigma\in[0,T]
, and let
W_{\epsilon}(t,x)
and
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t,x;\sigma)
being the solutions to following the Cauchy problems

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}W_{\epsilon}(t,x)+{\mathcal{R}}W_{\epsilon}(t,x)+V(x)_{\epsilon}(x)W_{\epsilon}(t,x)=0\,,\quad\text{in}\,[0,T]\times{\mathbb{G}},\\
W_{\epsilon}(t,x)=(u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon})(x)\,\quad\text{on}\,\{t=0\}\times{\mathbb{G}}\,,\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t,x;\sigma)+{\mathcal{R}}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t,x;\sigma)+V_{\epsilon}(x)\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t,x;\sigma)=0\,,\quad\text{in}\,(\sigma,T]\times{\mathbb{G}},\\
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t,x;\sigma)=f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,x)\,\quad\text{on}\,\{t=\sigma\}\times{\mathbb{G}}\,.\end{cases}

Thus, with the above considerations, we can write as an application of Duhamel’s principle:

U_{\epsilon}(t,x)=W_{\epsilon}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t-\sigma,x;\sigma)\,d\sigma\,.

Passing to the
L^{2}
-norm of the latter expression we get

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t-\sigma,\cdot;\sigma)\,d\sigma\right\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

An application of Minkowski’s integral inequality, combined with the energy upper bound (
4.5
) for both solutions
W_{\epsilon}
and
\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}
to the Cauchy problems as above, allow to estimate
\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}
further as given below:

(4.7)

\displaystyle\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t-\sigma,\cdot;\sigma)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma\,.

Since

\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

using (
4.7
) we obtain

\|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\int_{0}^{T}\|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma\,.

Finally, taking into account the negligibility of the nets
u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}
and
\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}
we get

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\omega_{1}(\epsilon)^{N}+\omega_{2}(\epsilon)^{\tilde{N}}\int_{0}^{T}\omega_{3}(\epsilon)^{-N_{1}}\,d\sigma\,,

for some
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, and for all
N,\tilde{N}\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}
is
H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate, and so also
L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
-moderate. The last estimate holds true uniformly in
t
, and this completes the proof of Theorem
4.16
.
∎

Still in the case where
V\geq 0
, alternative to Theorem
4.16
conditions on the negligibility of the net
V_{\epsilon}-\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}
that guarantee the very weak well-posedness of (
1.1
) are given in the next theorem.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
V
be non-negative. Let also
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. The following statements hold true:

•

If
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
, then the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique;

•

if
Q>\nu
and
V\in L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
, then the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique.

Proof.

We keep the notation used in the proof of Theorem
4.16
and adapt the reasoning there if necessary. Observe that the next inequality follows by Hölder’s inequality combined with the Sobolev embeddings (
2.5
):

\|(\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon})(\cdot)\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. Now, if in (
4.7
) we plug in the formula for
f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)
and the last estimate, then we get

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\|\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})}

Taking into account the negligiblity and moderateness assumptions we deduce that

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{N}\,,\quad\text{for all}\,N\in\mathbb{N}\,,

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
, and for some suitable
\omega(\epsilon)
and this finishes the proof of Theorem
4.17
.
∎

In the next theorem we prove the uniqueness of the very weak solutions to (
1.1
) in the general case of a real coefficient
V
.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
V\in\mathbb{R}
. Let also
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
and
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})\cup\mathcal{E}^{\prime}({\mathbb{G}})
. Then, the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique or
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique, where in the second case we assume that
2Q\geq\nu
.

Proof.

The arguments developed here follow the same line as in Theorems
4.16
and
4.17
. The notation used there is adopted here as well. First we recall the estimate for the solution
U_{\epsilon}
yo the Cauhcy problem (
4.6
):

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|\tilde{U}_{\epsilon}(t-\sigma,\cdot;\sigma)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma\,.

Now, the
L^{2}
-norms appearing on the right-hand side of the above inequality shall be estimated after applying twice the inequality (
3.17
) for the classical solution to (
1.1
) in the case of a real potential. In particular we obtain

(4.8)

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\exp\left(t\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)+\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\exp\left(t\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\right)\,.

It is necessary for our purposes to consider the
L^{\infty}
-moderateness of the net
V_{\epsilon}
under the choice of the function
\omega
to be given by
\omega(\epsilon):=\left(\log\epsilon^{-N_{0}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{N_{0}}}
, where
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
is such that the moderateness assumption can be expressed as:

\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\omega(\epsilon)^{-N_{0}}=\log(\epsilon^{-N_{0}})\,,

uniformly in
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.
Let us plug into the estimate (
4.8
) the moderateness assumption as above and get

(4.9)

\|U_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim e^{-TN_{0}}\left[\|u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\right]\,,

uniformly in
\epsilon\in(0,1]
. We next need to consider the negligibility of nets involved into (
4.9
). First consider the case where the net
\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}
is
L^{\infty}
-negligible. Then, using the estimate

\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}}\,,

and the
L^{2}
-moderateness of
\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}
as being the very weak solution to (
1.1
) we get

\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\omega(\epsilon)^{-N}\,,

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The latter combined with the
L^{2}
-negligibility assumption of the net
u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}
under the estimate (
4.9
) implies the
L^{2}
-negligiblity of the net
u_{\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}
and we have shown that the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}),L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}}))
-unique. Now, for the case where the net
\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}
is
L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}
-negligible, applying Hölder’s inequality and using the Sobolev embedding (
2.5
) we get

\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon})(\cdot)\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|\tilde{V}_{\epsilon}-V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

Now, since by Corollary
4.9
the very weak solution
\tilde{u}_{\epsilon}
is
H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}
-moderate, by the last estimate we deduce that the net
f_{\epsilon}
is
L^{2}
-negligible, and consequently also the difference of nets
u_{0,\epsilon}-\tilde{u}_{0,\epsilon}
. The last observations implies that the very weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) is
(L^{\infty},L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}})
-unique, and the proof of Theorem
4.18
complete.
∎

To summarize in the general case
V\in\mathbb{R}
, the very weak solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) can be
(X,Y)
-unique with
X=L^{\infty}
and
Y=L^{\infty}\cup L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}
, while in the case
V\geq 0
we allow
X=L^{\infty}\cup\{L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}\cap L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}\}
.

5.
Consistency result

We conclude this paper by proving the consistency result with the classical case. This means to show that when the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is well posed, then the very weak solution can be recaptured by the classical one in the
L^{2}
-sense.
To see this we need to assume that the approximating, to
V
, nets, as described in Definitions
4.5
and
4.6
above on the existence of the very weak solution, are regularisations of
V
via a Friedrichs mollifier. As in the previous sections, the cases of a non-negative and of a real potential will be treated separately.

Before we engage with the proof of our claim, we recall the following space of functions:

Definition 5.1

(Space
C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
)
.

We denote by
C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
the space of continuous functions that vanish at infinity; i.e., we write
f\in C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, if for every
\delta>0
there exists a compact set
K_{\delta}
such that

|f|<\delta\quad\text{on}\quad{\mathbb{G}}\setminus K_{\delta}\,.

We note that the space
(C_{0}({\mathbb{G}}),\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}})
is a Banach space. The following observation is essential for our purposes:

Remark 5.2
.

For a function
f\in C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, we know that for the corresponding regularised net
f_{\epsilon}
we have:

\|f_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq C<\infty\,,

uniformly in
\epsilon\in(0,1]
.

Theorem 5.3

(Consistency with the classical solution, case
V\geq 0
)
.

Let
V
be a non-negative potential, and let
V_{\epsilon}
be its regularisation. Assume also that
u_{0}\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
. If one of the two following conditions is satisfied, then the regularised net
u_{\epsilon}
converges, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, in
L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
, to the classical solution
u
given by Proposition
3.2
:

(1)

We have
Q>\nu
, and
V\in L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})\cap L^{\frac{Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})
;

(2)

V\in C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
.

Proof.

For
u
and for
u_{\epsilon}
as in the hypothesis, we introduce the auxiliary notation
W_{\epsilon}(t,x):=u(t,x)-u_{\epsilon}(t,x)
. Then, the net
W_{\epsilon}
is a solution to the Cauchy problem

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}W_{\epsilon}(t,x)+{\mathcal{R}}W_{\epsilon}(t,x)+V_{\epsilon}(x)W_{\epsilon}(t,x)=f_{\epsilon}(t,x),\\
W_{\epsilon}(0,x)=(u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon})(x)\,,\end{cases}

where we denote
f_{\epsilon}(t,x):=(V_{\epsilon}(x)-V(x))u(t,x)
.
Analogously to Theorem
4.16
, an application of Duhamel’s principle gives:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f_{\epsilon}(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}+\int_{0}^{T}\|(V_{\epsilon}-V)(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|\,d\sigma\,,

where we have applied Minkowski’s integral inequality.

Let us first consider the case (
1
): standard arguments from functional analysis imply that the below
L^{p}
-norms converge to
0
as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
:

\|u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\,.

The integrated quantity on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) is finite since

\|(V_{\epsilon}-V)(\cdot)u(t,\cdot)\|\lesssim\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,,

as follows by the Sobolev embeddings (
3.14
),
and consequently, since
u\in H^{\frac{\nu}{2}}({\mathbb{G}})
, we also have the convergence

\|(V_{\epsilon}-V)(\cdot)u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\lesssim\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\frac{2Q}{\nu}}({\mathbb{G}})}\|\sqrt{{\mathcal{R}}}u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\rightarrow 0\,.

Combining (
5.2
) with the below observations and using Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem we get

(5.3)

\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\rightarrow 0\,,

uniformly in
t\in[\sigma,T]
, where
\sigma
is taken to be fixed. The last conludes the proof of the theorem for the case (
1
).

To deal with the case (
2
), inequality on the right-hand side of (
5.2
) shall now be estimated as:

\|(V_{\epsilon}-V)(\cdot)u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\leq\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\,.

Using Lemmas 3.1.58, 3.1.59 in
[
FR16
]
for
V\in C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
we have the convergence:
\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0
, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, and the last implies in turn that convergence in (
5.3
) is satisfied under the alternative assumptions (
2
). Summarising the above, we have shown that the very weak solution converges to the classical one in
L^{2}
in both cases (
1
) and (
2
), and the proof of Theorem
5.3
is complete.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
V
be a real coefficient, and let
u_{0}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
. Assume also that
V\in C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
, and also that
V_{\epsilon}
is a regularisation of
V
. Then, the regularised net
(u_{\epsilon})_{\epsilon}
converges, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, in
L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})
, to the classical solution
u
given by Proposition
3.3
.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

We keep the same notation as in Theorem
5.3
. Using the estimate (
3.17
) and arguments similar to those developed in Theorem
5.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\|W_{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\exp(t\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|V_{\epsilon}-V\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{T}\exp(\sigma\|V_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}})\|u(\sigma,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{G}})}\,d\sigma\,,

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
. Now, by Remark
5.2
and since the convergence in
L^{2}
-norm and in
L^{\infty}
-norm of the differences of nets
u_{0}-u_{0,\epsilon}
and
V_{\epsilon}-V
, respectively, is as we showed in Theorem
5.3
, we see that
u_{\epsilon}\rightarrow u
in the
L^{2}
-sense, and the proof of Theorem
5.4
is complete.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

To be consistent with the previous work
[
ARST21c
]
on the heat equation on the real case, let us point out that the assumption on the functional space
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{G}})
of the coefficient
V
(denoted by
q
there) as appears in the consistency results (see Theorems 2.8 and 3.5 in
[
ARST21c
]
) should be restricted to its subspace
C_{0}({\mathbb{G}})
. Indeed, under such assumptions, the necessary
L^{\infty}
-convergence of the net
V_{\epsilon}-V
is granted, and Theorems 2.8 and 3.5 in
[
ARST21c
]
would then follow as a spacial case of Theorem
5.3
and
5.4
, respectively, in the particular case where the group
{\mathbb{G}}
is the trivial one and the Rockland operator is the usual Laplace operator; in symbols, when
{\mathbb{G}}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{R}=-\Delta
.

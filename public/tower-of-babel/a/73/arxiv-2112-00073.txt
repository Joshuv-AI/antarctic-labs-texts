On the discrete Dirac spectrum of a point electron in the zero-gravity Kerr-Newman spacetime
Michael K.-H. Kiessling, Eric Ling, and A. Shadi Tahvildar-Zadeh Department of Mathematics Rutgers, The State University of New Jersey 110 Frelinghuysen Rd., Piscataway, NJ 08854

Abstract
The discrete spectrum of the Dirac operator for a point electron in the maximal analytically extended Kerr–Newman spacetime is determined in the zero- G G limit (z G G KN), under some restrictions on the electrical coupling constant and on the radius of the ring-singularity of the z G G KN spacetime. The spectrum is characterized by a triplet of integers, associated with winding numbers of orbits of dynamical systems on cylinders. A dictionary is established that relates the spectrum with the known hydrogenic Dirac spectrum. Numerical illustrations are presented. Open problems are listed.

One way of looking at the Dirac equation for a point electron on z

Since numerically the empirical magnetic moment of the proton and the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron are quite close in magnitude,
the Dirac spectra for a z

Moreover, for hydrogen parameters we expect (see below for why) that the discrete spectrum will be a small perturbation of the union of a positive and a negative

This resolution should result in hyperfine-

This will require extensive and highly accurate numerical evaluations that we plan to carry out in the future.

The reason for why we expect a small perturbation of the union of a positive and a negative Sommerfeld hydrogen energy spectrum is that with

matched to either the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron or the magnetic moment of the proton, the ring radius is about five orders
of magnitude smaller than the expected distance of electron and proton in the hydrogen ground state.
Therefore the spectrum should not deviate much from its

It was pointed out already in

carries a positive charge and in the other a negative one (representing an anti-proton).
Therefore, in line with our result

the formal

We expect that this formal limit

As to the hydrogenic problem, the interpretation of the ring singularity as an electron-positron bi-particle has the simplifying advantage that
one can keep the ring radius

Ze

With increasing

However, it is also of interest to let

Of course, for large

of the hydrogenic z

to compare the spectra with their Minkowski space counterparts.

In
[
34

Z>0

By comparison, the Dirac hamiltonian of a point electron that interacts with a point nucleus in Minkowski spacetime is not essentially
self-adjoint for

Thus, when approaching

from the Sommerfeld spectrum in a more and more pronounced way, and indeed this is what we found numerically.

Also of interest in this hydrogenic setting is the comparison of the Dirac spectrum of a z

the hydrogenic Dirac spectrum computed with two interacting point charges in Minkowski spacetime, one of which is given an anomalous
magnetic moment, cf.
[

This Dirac operator on Minkowski spacetime is essentially self-adjoint for all

the spectrum of the Dirac operator on z

What we found numerically was completely unexpected.
The z

is increased beyond

any other Dirac operator so far — to the best of our knowledge.

It is very tempting to speculate whether these oscillations could indicate some real physical effects.
The best we could come up with is by resorting to Dirac’s interpretation of the negative energies.
Since the spectrum of the Dirac operator of a point particle on z

positive and negative spectrum is oscillating with

This could mean that in heavy ion collisions that temporarily create super-heavy nuclei, the amount of electron-positron pairs produced in such a
collision could perhaps vary in an oscillatory way with

This should be noticeable in coincidence detection of
\gamma

energy per photon that oscillates with

2.4
The Prüfer transformed system

Thus, following
[

we transform the equations (

Thus

\sin(\Theta(\theta)/2)e^{i\Omega(r)/2}\\

\sin(\Theta(\theta)/2)e^{-i\Omega(r)/2}\end{array}\right),

(2.34)

and we obtain the following equations for the new unknowns, first

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{mr}{\varpi}\cos\Omega+2\frac{\lambda}{\varpi}\sin\Omega+2\frac{a\kappa+\gamma r}{\varpi^{2}}-2E,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\ln R

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{mr}{\varpi}\sin\Omega-\frac{\lambda}{\varpi}\cos\Omega,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2ma\cos\theta\cos\Theta+2\left(aE\sin\theta-\frac{\kappa}{\sin\theta}\right)\sin\Theta+2\lambda,

(2.37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ma\cos\theta\sin\Theta-\left(aE\sin\theta-\frac{\kappa}{\sin\theta}\right)\cos\Theta.

(2.38)

Note that the

We can further simplify the analysis of these systems and reduce the number of parameters involved by noting that

2.5
Transformation onto a coupled dynamical system on cylinders

Equations (
2.37
), (
2.35
) exhibit the independent and the dependent variables explicitly.
It is more convenient to transform them to a parametrically coupled pair of autonomous two-dimensional dynamical systems, by introducing a new independent
variable
\tau
, as follows.

Equation (
2.37
) can be written as a smooth dynamical system in the
(\theta,\Theta)
plane by introducing

\tau
such that
\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}=\sin\theta
.
Then, with dot representing differentiation in
\tau
, we have,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{\theta}&=&\sin\theta\\
\dot{\Theta}&=&-2a\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos\Theta+2aE\sin^{2}\theta\sin\Theta-2\kappa\sin\Theta+2\lambda\sin\theta\end{array}\right.

Identifying the line
\Theta=\pi
with
\Theta=-\pi
, this becomes a dynamical system on a closed finite cylinder

\mathcal{C}_{1}=[0,\pi]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
The only equilibrium points of the flow are on the two circular boundaries:
Two on the left boundary:
S^{-}=(0,0)
,
N^{-}=(0,\pi)
; two on the right:

and
N^{+}=(\pi,0)
.

For

, the linearization of the flow at the equilibrium points reveals that

and
S^{+}
are hyperbolic saddle points (with

\{1,-2\kappa\}
and

)

is a sink node (with eigenvalues
-1
and
-2\kappa
). The situation with
\kappa<0
is entirely analogous, with the two

Similarly, the
\Omega

) can be rewritten as a smooth dynamical system on a cylinder, in this case by setting

as new independent variable, as well as introducing a new dependent variable

(2.40)

Then, with dot again representing differentiation in
\tau

2.35
) is equivalent to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{\xi}&=&\cos^{2}\xi\\
\dot{\Omega}&=&2a\sin\xi\cos\Omega+2\lambda\cos\xi\sin\Omega+2\gamma\sin\xi\cos\xi+2\kappa\cos^{2}\xi-2aE\end{array}\right.

Once again, identifying
\Omega=-\pi
with
\Omega=\pi
turns this into a smooth flow on the closed finite cylinder

\mathcal{C}_{2}:=[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

For
E\in(0,1)
there are two equilibria on each:
S^{-}_{E}=(-\frac{\pi}{2},-\pi+\cos^{-1}E)
and
N^{-}_{E}=(-\frac{\pi}{2},\pi-\cos^{-1}E)

and

]

In

such that each of the two dynamical systems above possesses a

Given a dynamical system on a cylinder, there corresponds an integer known as the

which describes how many times an orbit in the dynamical system winds around the cylinder before terminating at an equilibrium point.
For the
\Omega
system, saddles connectors with different winding numbers correspond to different energy values (with energy increasing as the winding number increases). In
[

]
we showed that a bound state

for the

for the

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}=[x_{-},x_{+}]\times\mathbb{R}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{x}\,=\,f(x)\\
\dot{y}\,=\,g_{\mu}(x,y).\end{array}\right.

We will use
\tau
to denote independent variable (e.g.
\dot{x}=\frac{dx}{d\tau}
).
Note that the dynamical system on
\mathcal{C}
induces a dynamical system on the universal cover
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}
. We make five assumptions for the dynamical system on
\mathcal{C}
labeled (a) - (e).

Assumptions:

(a)

f(x_{-})=f(x_{+})=0
and

for all

. Consequently, equilibrium points can only occur at the boundary of the cylinder (i.e. at

), and the boundaries

and

are images of orbits of the dynamical system. An orbit whose image is not completely contained in one of the boundaries

or

will be referred to as a

(b)

For all
\mu\in I

and exactly two distinct points
n^{+}_{\mu},s^{+}_{\mu}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

0\,=\,g_{\mu}(x_{-},n^{-})\,=\,g_{\mu}(x_{-},s^{-})\,=\,g_{\mu}(x_{+},n^{+})\,=\,g_{\mu}(x_{+},s^{+}).

\begin{pmatrix}f^{\prime}&0\\

\end{pmatrix}.

-

-

N^{+}_{\mu}

-

When

(c)

There is a fundamental domain

Here we have identified the points

with points in

. We will also make this identification with the equilibrium points so that we can think of

as being points in either
\mathcal{C}
or
\mathcal{C}_{*}
. In general we can make this identification for all points

.

(d)

For all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{C}
, we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}g_{\mu}(x,y)\leq 0
.

(e)

Every non-boundary orbit is a heteroclinic orbit. That is, the
\alpha
and
\omega
-limit sets of any non-boundary orbit are precisely one-point sets containing equilibrium points on the boundary

and

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma^{\pm}_{\mu}
given by
\big\{\sigma^{\pm}_{\mu}(\tau)=\big(x(\tau),y_{\mu}^{\pm}(\tau)\big)\mid\tau\in\mathbb{R}\big\}
be any lift of the orbits

\mu_{1}\,<\,\mu_{2}\>\>\>\>\Longrightarrow\>\>\>\>y^{-}_{\mu_{1}}(\tau)\,\geq\,y^{-}_{\mu_{2}}(\tau)\>\>\>\text{ and }\>\>\>y^{+}_{\mu_{1}}(\tau)\,\leq\,y^{+}_{\mu_{2}}(\tau)\>\>\text{ for all }\>\>\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We prove it for
\sigma^{-}_{\mu}

\sigma^{+}_{\mu}
is analogous. Define

It suffices to show

for all

solves

, we have

\dot{z}\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\big(g_{\mu}(x,y^{-}_{\mu})\big)\,=\,z\frac{\partial g_{\mu}}{\partial y}(x,y^{-}_{\mu})+\frac{\partial g_{\mu}}{\partial\mu}(x,y^{-}_{\mu}).

. Therefore the differential form of Grönwall’s inequality gives for any

z(\tau)\,\leq\,z(\tau_{0})e^{\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}P(s)ds}.

Since
\partial g_{\mu}/\partial y|_{S^{-}_{\mu}}<0

which follows by

Proposition 3.24
.

. Then the limit

Proof.

Recall we are writing

\tau

is bounded in

. Moreover
\Omega_{E}(\tau)

. This proves the first part of the proposition. To prove the second part, define
\Omega^{\tau}_{1}=\lim_{E\to 1}\Omega_{E}(\tau)

\lim_{E\to 1}\phi_{\tau}\big(0,\Omega_{E}(0),E\big)\,=\,\phi_{\tau}\big(0,\Omega_{1}^{0},1\big)

It suffices to show

\lim_{E\to 1}\phi_{\tau+s}\big(0,\Omega_{E}(0),E\big)\,=\,\phi_{\tau}\big(\xi(s),\Omega_{1}^{s},1\big).

\Omega^{0}_{1}(\tau)
be given by
\big(\xi(\tau),\Omega_{1}^{0}(\tau)\big)=\phi_{\tau}\big(0,\Omega_{1}^{0},1\big)

\lim_{E\to 1}\phi_{\tau+s}\big(0,\Omega_{E}(0),E\big)\,=\,\phi_{\tau}\big(\xi(s),\Omega_{1}^{s},1\big)\,=\,\phi_{\tau}\big(\xi(s),\Omega^{0}_{1}(s),1\big)\,=\,\phi_{\tau+s}\big(0,\Omega_{1}^{0},1\big).

Next we prove Proposition

implies that an orbit for the dynamical system when

is contained in a compact set and hence its

-limit set is nonempty by the Poincaré-Bendexison theorem. However, we will show that any orbit for the dynamical system when

has an empty
\omega
-limit set. Here we sketch how this latter part works with specific parameter values

,
\lambda=-1
,
\gamma=-\frac{1}{4}
, and
\kappa=\frac{1}{2}
. Shifting
\xi\mapsto\xi-\frac{\pi}{2}
so that the equilibrium at
\xi=\frac{\pi}{2}
occurs at the origin and keeping only second order terms, our dynamical system when

becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{\xi}\,=\,\xi^{2}\\
\dot{\Omega}\,=\,-\frac{1}{4}\Omega^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\xi^{2}+2\xi\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\xi.\end{array}\right.

Compare this with the system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dot{\xi}\,=\,\xi^{2}\\
\dot{z}\,=\,\frac{3}{4}\xi^{2}+2\xi z+\frac{1}{2}\xi.\end{array}\right.

Any orbit for the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{z})
-system is a barrier for the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{\Omega})
-system, i.e. the slope field for the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{\Omega})
-system is ‘more negative’ than the slope of any orbit for the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{z})
-system. The

-system has can be solved via an integrating factor. We have

Let

and
\big(\xi(\tau),z(\tau)\big)
be orbits for the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{\Omega})
-system and the
(\dot{\xi},\dot{z})
-system, respectively, with the same initial condition. The
\omega
-limit set of

is either the equilibrium point
(0,0)
or it is empty. Since
\big(\xi(\tau),z(\tau)\big)
is a barrier for
\big(\xi(\tau),\Omega(\tau)\big)
and

as

\Omega(\tau)<-\frac{1}{8}

\omega
-limit set of

is empty.

Proposition 3.26
.

Fix an integer

.

\bullet

Suppose

. For each

\lambda_{N}(E)
be given by Theorem

\bullet

Suppose

\lambda_{N}(E)
be given by Theorem
3.15
. Then
\lambda_{N}(E)
is a
C^{1}
function of
E
and
|\lambda^{\prime}_{N}(E)|<a
.

Proof.

We first show that
\lambda_{N}(E)
is a
C^{1}
function of
E
. For each
E
and
\lambda
, let
\widetilde{\mathcal{W}}_{\lambda,E}^{\pm}(\tau)=\big(\theta(\tau),\Theta^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)\big)
denote the orbits corresponding to the unstable and stable manifolds emanating from
S^{-}
and
S^{+}-2\pi N
, respectively, with initial condition determined by
\theta(0)=\frac{\pi}{2}
where
S^{+}-2\pi N:=(\pi,-\pi-2\pi N)
if
\kappa>0
and
S^{+}-2\pi N:=(\pi,-2\pi N)
if
\kappa<0
.

By smooth dependence of solutions on parameters, the function

\Phi(\lambda,E)\,=\,\Theta^{-}_{\lambda,E}(0)-\Theta^{+}_{\lambda,E}(0)

is smooth in
E
and
\lambda
. Since
\lambda_{N}(E)
corresponds to a saddles connector with winding number
N
, we have
\Phi\big(\lambda_{N}(E),E\big)=0
. By the implicit function theorem, if

\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\lambda}\big(\lambda_{N}(E),E\big)\,\neq\,0,

then it follows that
\lambda_{N}
is a
C^{1}
function of
E
. To show this, define
u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)\,=\,\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Theta^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)
. Then
u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}
satisfies the following differential equation

\frac{d}{d\tau}u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)\,=\,P^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)+Q(\tau)

where

\displaystyle P^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}\,

\displaystyle=\,2a\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin\Theta^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}+(2aE\sin^{2}\theta-2\kappa)\cos\Theta^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}

\displaystyle Q\,

\displaystyle=\,2\sin\theta.

Setting
U^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,\tau_{0})=\exp(\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}P^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(s)ds)
, the solution to this differential equation is

u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau)\,=\,U^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,\tau_{0})u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau_{0})\,+\,\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}U^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,s)Q(s)ds.

Taking the limit
\tau_{0}\to-\infty
, we find
P^{-}_{\lambda,E}(-\infty)=-1
and so
U^{-}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,-\infty)=0
. Similarly, taking the limit
\tau_{0}\to\infty
, we find
P^{+}_{\lambda,E}(\infty)=1
and so
U^{+}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,\infty)=0
. Therefore
U^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau,\tau_{0})u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\tau_{0})\to 0
as
\tau_{0}\to\pm\infty
(it’s also the case that
u^{\pm}_{\lambda,E}(\pm\infty)=0
, but this follows because the
\Theta
system has special equilibrium points which don’t depend on the parameter
\lambda
; this doesn’t happen in general). Therefore

\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\lambda}(\lambda,E)\,=\,u^{-}_{\lambda,E}(0)-u^{+}_{\lambda,E}(0)\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{0}U^{-}_{\lambda,E}(0,s)Q(s)ds+\int_{0}^{\infty}U^{+}_{\lambda,E}(0,s)Q(s)ds.

Since
\lambda_{N}(E)
corresponds to a saddles connector with winding number
N
, we have
\Theta^{-}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}(\tau)=\Theta^{+}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}(\tau)
for all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore
U^{-}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}=U^{+}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}
. Thus

\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\lambda}\big(\lambda_{N}(E),E\big)\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U^{-}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}(0,s)Q(s)ds\,>\,0.

Now we prove the second part of the theorem:
|\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)|<a
for all
E
. Write
\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}:=\Theta^{-}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}=\Theta^{+}_{\lambda_{N}(E),E}
. Define
v=\frac{\partial}{\partial E}\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}
. Then
v
satisfies the differential equation
\frac{d}{d\tau}v(\tau)=P(\tau)v(\tau)+R(\tau)
where

\displaystyle P\,

\displaystyle=\,2a\sin\theta\cos\theta\sin\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}+(2aE\sin^{2}\theta-2\kappa)\cos\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}

\displaystyle R\,

\displaystyle=\,2a\sin^{2}\theta\sin\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}+2\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)\sin\theta.

Setting
U(\tau,\tau_{0})=\exp(\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}P(s)ds)
, the solution to this differential equation is

v(\tau)\,=\,U(\tau,\tau_{0})v(\tau_{0})+\int_{\tau_{0}}^{\tau}U(\tau,s)R(s)ds.

Using the same analysis as above, again we find

as
\tau_{0}\to\pm\infty
. Therefore

v(0)\,=\,\int_{-\infty}^{0}U(0,s)R(s)ds\,=\,-\int_{0}^{\infty}U(0,s)R(s)ds.

Hence

\displaystyle 0\,

\displaystyle=\,\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U(0,s)R(s)ds

\displaystyle=\,2a\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U(0,s)\sin^{2}\theta(s)\sin\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}(s)ds+2\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U(0,s)\sin\theta(s)ds.

Solving for
\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)

\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)\,=\,a\left(\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U(0,s)\sin^{2}\theta(s)\sin\Theta_{\lambda_{N}(E)}(s)ds}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}U(0,s)\sin\theta(s)ds}\right).

Since the numerator in absolute value is strictly less than the denominator in absolute value, we have
|\lambda_{N}^{\prime}(E)|<a
.
∎

k>0
, it suffices to show that
\mu+1<0
and
\mu+\frac{M}{M-1}>0
since this implies that the signs for
Q_{M}(x)
go like
--+-+\dotsb
. Note this only applies for
M\geq 2
; the case
M=1
can be verified independently.

Using
k=\mbox{sgn}(k)\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\gamma^{2}}
and
\sqrt{1-E^{2}}=-\gamma/\sqrt{(M+\rho)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}
, we have

\mu\,=\,\frac{M}{k+\gamma/\sqrt{1-E^{2}}}\,=\,\frac{M}{\mbox{sgn}(k)\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\gamma^{2}}-\sqrt{(M+\rho)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}}.

From this expression, it follows that
-\mu<1
if
k<0
; this proves (1). Now we prove (2). Since
k>0
, rationalizing the denominator gives

-\mu\,=\,\frac{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+\gamma^{2}}+\sqrt{(M+\rho)^{2}+\gamma^{2}}}{M+2\rho}\,<\,\frac{\rho+|\gamma|+M+\rho+|\gamma|}{M+2\rho}\,=\,1+\frac{2|\gamma|}{M+2\rho}.

Therefore
\mu+1<0
. Lastly, since
\frac{M}{M-1}=1+\frac{1}{M-1}
, it suffices to show
\frac{2|\gamma|}{M+2\rho}<\frac{1}{M-1}
. Indeed

\frac{2|\gamma|}{M+2\rho}\,=\,\frac{2|\gamma|}{M+2\sqrt{k^{2}-\gamma^{2}}}\,<\,\frac{2|\gamma|}{M+2\sqrt{1-\gamma^{2}}}\,<\,\frac{1}{M-1}

where the last inequality holds whenever
|\gamma|<\frac{1}{2}
. ∎

Numerical results

The following numerical simulations are performed using MATLAB
[

]
. The algorithm for finding the energies of saddles connectors is as follows: The two ODEs (
2.37
) and (
2.35
) are numerically integrated (using Runge-Kutta 4-5) over a suitably large interval symmetric about zero, with initial data corresponding to the saddle nodes on the left boundary of the corresponding cylinders. The two desired winding numbers
N_{\Theta}
and
N_{\Omega}
are fixed, as well as the other parameters
\kappa
,
a
and
\gamma=-Z\alpha_{S}
. A shooting method is used iteratively on the parameters
\lambda
for the
\Theta
equation and
E
for the
\Omega
equation, to fine tune the values for these parameters via a binary search, until solutions with the desired winding numbers are found for both equations using the same (up to a specified tolerance) pair of
(\lambda,E)
values. MATLAB’s graphic routines are then used to generate the plots.

At a formal level, the limit

6
Summary and Outlook

In this paper we have supplied a complete characterization of the discrete spectrum of a Dirac electron in the zero-
G
Kerr–Newman
spacetime, though only under certain smallness conditions on the electric coupling constant and on the radius of the ring singularity.
This improves on our previous works
[
34
]
,
[
35
]
,
[
36
]
where it was shown that the discrete
spectrum is non-empty for more restrictive conditions on the parameters of the problem.
The discrete spectrum is associated with a triplet of integers.
One set of integers is the familiar enumeration of the azimuthal modes, associate with the axisymmetry.
The other two integer families are associated with topological winding numbers of a coupled system of dynamical equations
on finite cylinders.
We have provided a dictionary for how our characterization maps into the familiar hydrogenic Dirac spectra in the small radius
regime of the ring singularity.
We have also provided some numerical illustrations of the low-lying eigenvalues as functions of the coupling constant and of the
radius of the ring singularity, obtained by implementing the algorithm of our constructive proof.
Empirically, the algorithm converges outside of the estimated convergence regime, demonstrating that our estimates are far from sharp.
Interestingly, the numerically obtained results show some never-before-seen oscillatory behavior of the Dirac eigenvalues for large
coupling constants beyond the currently assessible regime of experimental data.

Future work should improve on our assumptions on the parameters of the problem and obtain sharp results.

Although our constructive proof relies on the iteration of two dynamical systems that determine the pairs
(\lambda,E)
jointly,
and which is instrumental in identifying the doubly integer labels for
(\lambda,E)
, from the computational point of view it is of
interest to also try to compute the energy eigenvalues
E
directly from the dynamical
\Omega
system, with the angular
eigenvalues
\lambda
expressed as analytical functions of
E
in the manner determined in
[
4
]
.
Whether this has computational advantages or not is an interesting question.

In the future we plan to rigorously investigate the
a\to 0
limit and hopefully show that the
a\to 0
limit of the z
G
KN spectrum converges to the Bohr-Sommerfeld spectrum as suggested in Figures
12
and
13
. In fact, due to the symmetry of the spectrum, we expect to get two copies of the Bohr-Sommerfeld spectrum. One copy corresponds to a negative point charge
-e
in the
r>0
sheet, while the other copy corresponds to a positive point charge
+e
in the
r<0
sheet. An interpretation of this negative spectrum is given in
[
36
]
.

The KN electromagnetic field can also be generalized to exhibit what elsewhere we have called a
KN-anomalous magnetic moment
,

\mathbf{A}_{\textsc{KN}}^{\mbox{\tiny{gen}}}=-\frac{r}{r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}\left(\textsc{q}dt-\frac{\textsc{i}\pi a^{2}}{c^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\,d\varphi\right),

(6.74)

with which one can decorate the zero-
G
Kerr spacetime of the same ring radius
a
; here,

i
is the electrical current which produces a magnetic dipole moment
\textsc{i}\pi a^{2}/c
when viewed from
spacelike infinity in the
r>0
sheet.
Although no spacetime with such a generalized KN field has yet been found for when
G>0
, it is an interesting open question
whether one exists.
In any event, in the zero-
G
limit this family of spacetimes does exist, and it is not yet known whether the Dirac operator is
essentially self-adjoint or not on its minimal domain.
Clearly there are plenty of intriguing questions that await a rigorous mathematical treatment.

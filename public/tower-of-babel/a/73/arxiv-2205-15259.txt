Passivity, Port-Hamiltonian Formulation and Solution Estimates for a Coupled Magneto-Quasistatic System


Abstract
. In this paper, we study a quasilinear coupled magneto-quasistatic model from a systems theoretic perspective. First, by taking the injected voltages as input and the associated currents as output, we prove that the magneto-quasistatic system is passive. Moreover, by defining suitable Dirac and resistive structures, we show that it admits a representation as a port-Hamiltonian system. Thereafter, we consider dependence on initial and input data. We show that the current and the magnetic vector potential can be estimated by means of the initial magnetic vector potential and the voltage. We also analyse the free dynamics of the system and study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions for t → ∞ t\to\infty .

1.
Introduction

A subject of this paper is the investigation of systems theoretic properties of a quasulinear coupled magneto-quasistatic (MQS) model

\displaystyle{\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}}\left(\sigma\bm{A}\right)+\nabla\times\left(\nu(\cdot,\|\nabla\times\bm{A}\|_{2})\nabla\times\bm{A}\right)=

\displaystyle\;\chi\,\bm{i}

in

\displaystyle\mathit{\Omega}\times(0,T),

(1a)

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\bm{A}\,{\rm d}\xi+R\,\bm{i}=

\displaystyle\;\bm{v}

on

\displaystyle(0,T),

(1b)

\displaystyle\bm{A}\times\bm{n}_{o}=

\displaystyle\;0

in

\displaystyle\partial\mathit{\Omega}\times(0,T),

(1c)

\displaystyle\sigma\bm{A}(\cdot,0)=

\displaystyle\;\sigma\bm{A}_{0}

in

\displaystyle\mathit{\Omega},

(1d)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\bm{A}(\cdot,0)\,{\rm d}\xi=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\bm{A}_{0}\,{\rm d}\xi,

(1e)

where
\mathit{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain with some further properties specified later on,
\bm{A}:\overline{\mathit{\Omega}}\times[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the magnetic vector potential,

\nu:\mathit{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the magnetic reluctivity,

\sigma:\mathit{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is the electric conductivity, and

\bm{v},\bm{i}:{[0,T]}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are, respectively, the voltage and the electrical current through the electromagnetic conductive contacts. Furthermore,

\chi:\mathit{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{3\times m}
is the winding function, which expresses the geometry of
m
windings,

R\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
is the resistance of the winding,

\bm{n}_{o}:\partial\mathit{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the outward unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial\mathit{\Omega}
,
and
\bm{A}_{0}:\mathit{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

is the initial value for the magnetic vector potential. The voltage
\bm{v}
is considered as the input, whereas the current
\bm{i}
is the output of the system.
The dynamics of the magnetic vector potential is expressed by (
1a
), which arises from a simplification of Maxwell’s equations
[
11
]
, and (
1c
)
means that the magnetic flux through the boundary
\partial\mathit{\Omega}

The term

stands for the external current induced by
m

\bm{v}

, and the term

Note that

, whereas it vanishes on the complement of
\mathit{\Omega}_{C}
. This implies that the coupled MQS system (
1

Existence, uniqueness and regularity properties of solutions to (
1

[
5
,
25
,
26
]
for some special cases, while
[
11
]
presents well-posedness and regularity results for more general setting.
The purpose of this paper is to investigate the dynamic behavior of the coupled MQS model (
1
) from a systems theoretic point of view. In doing so, we analyse passivity of this model by establishing the existence of a certain energy balance. Further, we show that the MQS system (
1
) fits
into the framework of port-Hamiltonian systems. The findings on passivity will be the basis for solution estimates by means of the initial value
\bm{A}_{0}
and the input voltage
\bm{v}

1

\bm{v}\equiv 0
) are bounded and that the
L^{2}
-norm of the curl of the magnetic vector potential decays exponentially, we present estimates for
the
L^{2}
-norm of the output and the magnetic vector potential in terms of the initial value, the input and the material parameters.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we collect our model assumptions and review some results from
[
11
]
on existence, uniqueness and regularity of solutions of the coupled MQS system (
1

This section closes with a brief introduction to the concept of magnetic energy. It plays an essential role in Section
3

1
) and prove that this system is passive. In Section
4

1
) belongs to this class. The energy balance from Section

5

to establish the solution estimates for (
1
). In Section
5.1
, we derive a bound for the

-norm of the output in terms of the

-norm of the input and the initial value. This gives, in particular, an estimate for the generalization of the

-norm

Next, we state the assumptions on the electric conductivity
\sigma
,

\nu

Assumption 2.2

(Material parameters)
.

​​

a)

The electric conductivity
\sigma:\mathit{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

b)

The magnetic reluctivity
\nu:\mathit{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
has the following properties:

(i)

\nu
is measurable;

(ii)

the function
\zeta\mapsto\nu(\xi,\zeta)\zeta
is strongly monotone with a monotonicity constant

independent of
\xi\in\mathit{\Omega}
. In other words, there exists

such that

\bigl(\nu(\xi,\zeta)\zeta-\nu(\xi,\varsigma)\varsigma\bigr)(\zeta-\varsigma)\geq m_{\nu}(\zeta-\varsigma)^{2}\enskip\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathit{\Omega},\enskip\zeta,\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};

(iii)

is Lipschitz continuous with a Lipschitz constant

\xi\in\mathit{\Omega}
. In other words, there exists

|\nu(\xi,\zeta)\zeta-\nu(\xi,\varsigma)\varsigma|\leq L_{\nu}|\zeta-\varsigma|\quad\text{ for all }\xi\in\mathit{\Omega},\enskip\zeta,\varsigma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

c)

The resistance matrix

is symmetric and positive definite.

As the space in which the solutions of the coupled MQS system (
1

X(\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})
of all square integrable functions which are
L^{2}
-orthogonal to all gradient fields of functions from

being constant on each interface component

and
\Gamma_{\rm ext}

The space

is a Hilbert space equipped with the standard inner product in
{L^{2}(\mathit{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.
We further consider the space

X_{0}(\curl,\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})=H_{0}(\curl,\mathit{\Omega})\cap X(\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C}),

(2)

which is again a Hilbert space, now provided with the inner product in
H_{0}(\curl,\mathit{\Omega})
.
For any
\bm{A}\in X(\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})
, one has
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathit{\Omega}_{C}}\bm{A}\in X(\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})
, see
[
11

Moreover,
[

states that

is dense in

The space
X(\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})
enables us to formulate the assumptions on the initial magnetic vector potential
\bm{A}_{0}
and the winding function
\chi

are square integrable, i.e.,

such a function can, loosely speaking, be canonically identified with a
m

L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3\times m})\cong L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})^{1\times m},

(3)

where elements of
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})^{1\times m}
are regarded as operators from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3})
. The corresponding operator norm is given by

\|\chi\|_{L^{2}(\mathit{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3\times m})}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}\left(\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\chi\,{\rm d}\xi\right)},

(4)

where
\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X})
stands for the largest eigenvalue of a symmetric matrix

is not a Hilbert space unless

Assumption 2.3

(Initial condition and winding function)

a)

The initial magnetic vector potential

b)

The winding function

3.
Passivity

In this section, we investigate passivity of the coupled MQS system (
1
).
Passive systems form a special class of dissipative dynamical systems which have extensively been studied in
[
14
,
30
]
. They are of particular interest in circuit simulation
[
3
]
and controller design
[
10
]
. Roughly speaking, a system is passive if it does not generate energy or, equivalently, the energy is dissipated. Mathematically, passivity can be defined in terms of a storage function. An important property of passive systems is that an interconnection of passive subsystems often provides a new passive system.

Consider a general abstract differential-algebraic control system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x(t))&=&\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{A}$}(x(t))+\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{B}$}(u(t)),\\
y(t)&=&\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{C}$}(x(t))\end{array}

(12)

with (possibly nonlinear) operators
\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}:X\to Z
,
\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{A}$}:D(\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{A}$})\subset X\to Z
,
\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{B}$}:U\to Z
and

\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{C}$}:X\to U^{\prime}
for some Hilbert spaces
X
,
Z
and
U
.
The input
u\in L^{2}([0,T];U)
is called
admissible with the initial condition

\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x(0))=\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x_{0})
, if (
12
) has a solution
x:{[0,T]}\to X
with
\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x(0))=\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x_{0})
and
y\in L^{2}([0,T];U^{\prime})
. Similarly to the finite-dimensional case
[
30
]
, we define passivity for the infinite-dimensional system (

) as follows.

Definition 3.1

(Passivity)

A function

of system (

\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x(0))=\scalebox{1.25}{$\mathpzc{E}$}(x_{0})

a)

is continuous as a function from

The Coupled MQS System in Port-Hamiltonian Formulation

Port-Hamiltonian systems have meanwhile become rather popular as a modelling tool especially for coupled (multi-)physical systems
[
17
]
. In the past few years, this theory has successfully been extended to (finite-dimensional) differential-algebraic systems
[
20
,
21
,
29
,
13
]
. The aim of this section is to show that the coupled MQS system (
1
) also fits into the framework of port-Hamiltonian systems.

We first introduce some basics of port-Hamiltonian systems which are heavily inspired by
[
17
]
. Since infinite-dimensional port-Hamiltonian systems have been so far treated mainly from a differential geometric rather than from a functional analytic perspective
[
23
,
24
,
19
]
, the authors of this paper have a certain sovereignty of definition. Note that, though a functional analytic approach to infinite-dimensional port-Hamiltonian systems has been discussed in
[
15
]
, the theory presented therein, however, restricts to the very limited class of coupled systems of linear transport equations.

An important concept is the Dirac structure which describes the power preserving energy-routing of the system. Dirac structures on Hilbert spaces have been considered in
[
8
]
, and their structure has been analysed by using the theory of Krein spaces.

Definition 4.1

(Dirac structure)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset X\times X^{\prime}
is called a
Dirac structure
, if for all
f\in X
,
e\in X^{\prime}
, it holds

(f,e)\in\mathcal{D}\;\Longleftrightarrow\;\left(\langle{f},{\hat{e}}\rangle+\langle{\hat{f}},{{e}}\rangle=0\,\text{ for all }\,(\hat{f},\hat{e})\in\mathcal{D}\right).

Hereby,
X
is called the space of
flows
, whereas
X^{\prime}
is called the space of
efforts
.

Another concept needed for port-Hamiltonian systems is the resistive relation which represents the internal energy dissipation of the system
[
17
, Section 2.4]
.

Definition 4.2

(Resistive relation)
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. A relation
\mathcal{R}\subset X\times X^{\prime}
is called
resistive
, if

\left\langle f,e\right\rangle\leq 0\,\text{ for all }\,(f,e)\in{\mathcal{R}}.

Having defined the Dirac structure and the resistive relation, we are now ready to introduce port-Hamiltonian systems. The subsequent definition uses the concepts of
Gâteaux differentiability
and
Gâteaux derivative
, for which we refer to
[
31
, Definition 4.5]
.

Definition 4.3

(Port-Hamiltonian system)
.

Let
X_{\mathcal{S}}
,
X_{\mathcal{R}}
and
X_{\mathcal{P}}
be Banach spaces.
A
port-Hamiltonian system
is a triple
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{R})
, where

\mathcal{D}\subset(X_{\mathcal{S}}\times X_{\mathcal{R}}\times X_{\mathcal{P}})\times(X_{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\times X_{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\times X_{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime})

is a Dirac structure,
\mathcal{H}:X_{\mathcal{S}}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous on bounded sets and Gâteaux differentiable, and
\mathcal{R}\subset X_{\mathcal{R}}\times X_{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}
is a resistive relation.
The
dynamics
of the port-Hamiltonian system on an interval
\mathbb{I}\subset\mathbb{R}
are specified by the differential inclusions

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\bigl(-\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}x(t),f_{\mathcal{R}}(t),f_{\mathcal{P}}(t),{\rm D}\mathcal{H}(x(t)),e_{\mathcal{R}}(t),e_{\mathcal{P}}(t))&\in&\mathcal{D},\\
(f_{\mathcal{R}}(t),e_{\mathcal{R}}(t)\bigr)&\in&\mathcal{R},\quad t\in\mathbb{I},\end{array}

(19)

where
{\rm D}\mathcal{H}:X_{\mathcal{S}}\to X_{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is the Gâteaux derivative of
\mathcal{H}
.
The function
\mathcal{H}
is called
Hamiltonian
, whereas, for some time
t
in which the system is driven,
x(t)
is called
state
. The spaces
X_{\mathcal{S}}\times X_{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
,
X_{\mathcal{R}}\times X_{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}
and
X_{\mathcal{P}}\times X_{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime}
are referred to as
energy storage port
,
resistive port
and
external port
, respectively.

Remark 4

a)

For instance, in a very general setting, a Dirac structure is defined

\displaystyle\,-{\rm D}\mathcal{H}_{\rm MQS}(\bm{A}(t))+\chi\,\bm{i}_{\rm w}(t),

\displaystyle-R\,\bm{i}_{\rm w}(t)=

\displaystyle\,-\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\bm{E}(t)\,{\rm d}\xi-\bm{v}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\bm{i}_{\rm w}(t),

and, thus,

\displaystyle\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\sigma\bm{A}(t)=

\displaystyle-{\rm D}\mathcal{H}_{\rm MQS}(\bm{A}(t))+\chi\,\bm{i}(t),

\displaystyle\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\bm{A}(t)\,{\rm d}\xi=

\displaystyle-R\,\bm{i}(t)+\bm{v}(t).

Since the first equation takes place in
X_{0}(\curl,\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})^{\prime}
and
{\rm D}\mathcal{H}_{\rm MQS}
reads as in (
22
), we obtain the weak formulation (
5
) of the coupled MQS system (
1
).
Moreover, the external port is formed by the voltage
\bm{v}
and the current
\bm{i}
at the conductive contacts, which are, respectively, the input and the output of (
1
).
The variable
\bm{J}_{\rm int}
in (
26
) stands for the current density in
\mathit{\Omega}
induced by the electromagnetic field, whereas
\bm{E}
is the electric field intensity. Further,
\bm{v}_{\rm w}
is the part of the voltage at the winding which is caused by the resistive effect, and
\bm{i}_{\rm w}
is the corresponding current.

Remark 5
.

Note that the port-Hamiltonian model (
26
) requires weak differentiability with respect to time of the magnetic vector potential
\bm{A}
, whereas the solution concept in Section
2.2
only requires
\sigma\bm{A}
to be weakly differentiable.

The port-Hamiltonian model (
26
) can at least formally be represented in a compact form

\displaystyle\tfrac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\begin{bmatrix}I\;&0\;&0\;\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix}\!\begin{bmatrix}\bm{A}(t)\\
\bm{E}(t)\\
\bm{i}(t)\end{bmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0\enskip\;&-I&\;0\\
I\enskip\;&-\sigma&-\chi\\
0\enskip\;&\int_{\mathit{\Omega}}\chi^{\top}\cdot\,{\rm d}\xi\enskip\;&-R\;\end{bmatrix}\!\begin{bmatrix}{\rm D}\mathcal{H}_{\rm MQS}(\bm{A}(t))\\
\bm{E}(t)\\
\bm{i}(t)\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
I\end{bmatrix}\bm{v}(t),

\displaystyle\bm{i}(t)=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}0\,&\,0\,&\,I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\rm D}\mathcal{H}_{\rm MQS}(\bm{A}(t))\\
\bm{E}(t)\\
\bm{i}(t)\end{bmatrix},

whose structure amazingly resembles the class of finite-dimensional port-Hamiltonian differential-algebraic systems studied in
[
22
,
7
]
.

5.
Solution Estimates

In this section, we study the quantitative properties of the coupled MQS system (
1
) by considering its input-to-output and input-to-state behavior. We
present estimates for the current, the magnetic vector potential and the magnetic energy upon the initial magnetic vector potential and the voltage. We further show that, under some additional assumption on the initial value, the free dynamics of the MQS system decay exponentially.
Note that our estimates also cover the case of infinite intervals. To this end, we refer to Remark
1
for the definition and existence of solutions on the whole positive real axis
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.

5.1.
Input-to-output behavior

First, we derive estimates for the current
\bm{i}
, which is considered as the output of the coupled MQS system (
1
),
by means of the initial value
\bm{A}_{0}
and the voltage
\bm{v}

Lemma 5.1

Assume that

\displaystyle E(\bm{A}(t_{1}))-E(\bm{A}(t_{0}))

As Theorem
3.2
states that (

0\leq t_{0}\leq t_{1}<T
, if, additionally,
\bm{A}_{0}\in X_{0}(\curl,\mathit{\Omega},\mathit{\Omega}_{C})

∎

6.
Conclusion

We have considered a quasilinear magneto-quasistatic approximation of Maxwell’s
equations, which is furthermore coupled with an integral equation. By employing
the magnetic energy, we have shown that this system is passive and admits a representation as
a port-Hamiltonian system.
Further, we have derived estimates of the state and output of the system by means of the initial value and the input. A special emphasis in the solution estimates is placed on the free system with the zero input voltage,
where we have shown that the magnetic energy decays exponentially.

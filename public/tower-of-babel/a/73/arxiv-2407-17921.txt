Equivariant cohomology of odd–dimensional complex quadrics from a combinatorial point of view
Shintarô Kuroki, Bidhan Paul

Abstract
. This paper aims to determine the ring structure of the torus-equivariant cohomology with integer coefficients of odd-dimensional complex quadrics by computing the graph equivariant cohomology of their corresponding GKM graphs. We show that this graph equivariant cohomology is generated by three types of subgraphs in the GKM graph, which satisfy five distinct types of relations. Furthermore, we investigate the relationship between the graph equivariant cohomology rings associated with odd- and even-dimensional complex quadrics.

1.
Introduction

A
complex quadric

Q_{N}
is defined by the quadratic equation in the complex projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{N+1}
:

\displaystyle Q_{N}:=\Big\{[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{N+2}]\in\mathbb{CP}^{N+1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{N+2}z_{i}^{2}=0\Big\}.

If
N
is odd (resp. even), then
Q_{N}
is called an
odd-dimensional
(resp.
even-dimensional
)
complex quadric
.
The standard
SO(N+2)
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{N+1}
preserves this quadratic equation, leading to a maximal torus
T
-action on
Q_{N}
.

The cohomology ring of
Q_{N}
has been studied.
Over the integer coefficients, it is isomorphic to one of the following rings for
n\geq 1
(see
[
4
, Excercise 68.3]
or
[
16
]
if
N
is even):

\displaystyle H^{*}(Q_{N})\simeq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathbb{Z}[c,x]/\langle c^{n}-2x,x^{2}\rangle&{\rm if}\ N=2n-1,&{\rm where}\ \deg c=2,\ \deg x=2n\\
\mathbb{Z}[c,x]/\langle c^{2n}-2cx,x^{2}\rangle&{\rm if}\ N=4n-2,&{\rm where}\ \deg c=2,\ \deg x=4n-2\\
\mathbb{Z}[c,x]/\langle c^{2n+1}-2cx,x^{2}-c^{2n}x\rangle&{\rm if}\ N=4n,&{\rm where}\ \deg c=2,\ \deg x=4n\end{array}\right.

In
[
15
]
, the first author computed the
T^{n+1}
-equivariant cohomology ring of the even-dimensional complex quadric
Q_{2n}
using GKM theory (also see
[
18
]
), where the
T^{n+1}:=(S^{1})^{n+1}
-action on
Q_{2n}
, which is diffeomorphic to the zero locus of a quadratic equation in
\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}
i.e.

\displaystyle Q_{2n}:=\Big\{[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2n+1}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{n+1}x_{1}x_{2n+3-i}=0\Big\},

is given by

(1.1)

[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\cdot(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1}):=[x_{1}t_{1}:\cdots:x_{n+1}t_{n+1}:t_{n+1}^{-1}x_{n+2}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-1}x_{2n+2}],

where
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n+1})\in T^{n+1}
and
[x_{1}:\cdots:x_{2n+2}]\in Q_{2n}
. The action above is non-effective; the effective action is obtained by quotienting out the kernel (see
[
15
, Remark 2.2]
).
This provides a unified formula for the cohomology rings
H^{*}(Q_{4n-2})
and
H^{*}(Q_{4n})
using GKM graphs.
The approach also explains why two distinct relations appear in these rings through combinatorial interpretations.
Thus, GKM theory proves to be a powerful tool for studying (equivariant) cohomology rings of
T
-spaces.

We briefly recall GKM theory here (see also the introduction of
[
15
]
).
A
GKM manifold
is an equivariantly formal manifold
M^{2k}
(i.e.,
H^{odd}(M)=0
) endowed with a compact torus
T:=(S^{1})^{n}
action.
Goresky, Kottwitz, and MacPherson introduced this concept in
[
8
]
, where
the action ensures that the set of
0
- and
1
-dimensional orbits forms a graph structure.
Motivated by their work, Guillemin and Zara introduced the concept of an
abstract GKM graph
in
[
10
]
, an abstract graph with labeled edges (see Section
2
for details).
An abstract GKM graphs, which extend beyond their geometric motivations,
have enriched the field known as
GKM theory
.
In this paper, we study the
T^{n}
-equivariant cohomology of the odd-dimensional complex quadric
Q_{2n-1}
from a GKM theoretical perspective.

It is well-known that the
Q_{2n-1}
is
T^{n}
-equivariantly diffeomorphic to the zero locus of a quadratic equation in
\mathbb{CP}^{2n}
(see e.g.
[
19
, Chapter V.1, 1.1 Theorem]
):

\displaystyle Q_{2n-1}:=\Big\{[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{2n+1}]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2n}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}z_{2n+1-i}+z_{2n+1}^{2}=0\Big\}.

In this paper, we consider
Q_{2n-1}
as this manifold.
From this definition, the natural
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-1}
is defined by:

(1.2)

[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{2n+1}]\cdot(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}):=[z_{1}t_{1}:\cdots:z_{n}t_{n}:t_{n}^{-1}z_{n+1}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-1}z_{2n}:z_{2n+1}],

where
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in T^{n}
.
Since
Q_{2n-1}
is diffeomorphic to the oriented Grassmannian
SO(2n+1)/(SO(2n-1)\times SO(2))
–the set of oriented
2
-planes through the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
– this action is equivariantly diffeomorphic to the maximal torus
T^{n}
-action induced by restricting the transitive
SO(2n+1)
-action.
Furthermore, as
SO(2n-1)\times SO(2)\subset SO(2n+1)
is a maximal rank subgroup of
SO(2n+1)
, the set of
0
- and
1
-dimensional orbits of
T^{n}
-action forms a graph structure (see
[
9
]
).
Therefore, the GKM graph of
Q_{2n-1}
with
T^{n}
-action (
1.2
) can be determined by labeling the edges with tangential representations.

By the formula for
H^{*}(Q_{2n-1})
as above, we conclude that
H^{odd}(Q_{2n-1})=0
; hence,
Q_{2n-1}
is an
equivariantly formal
GKM manifold.
Consequently, the equivariant cohomology
H_{T^{n}}^{*}(Q_{2n-1})
can be computed using the graph equivariant cohomology of its GKM graph, denoted by
\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1}
.
Let
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{M},\mathbf{D},Q]
be the polynomial ring generated by some cohomology classes in
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})
and let
\mathfrak{I}\lhd\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{M},\mathbf{D},Q]
be the ideal generated by five types of relations (cf. Section
5
) among the elements in
\mathbf{M}
,
\mathbf{D}
, and
Q
. We define

\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1}]:=\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{M},\mathbf{D},Q]/\mathfrak{I}.

The main theorem of this paper, precisely presented in Section
5
, is as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

There exist the following isomorphisms as a ring:

\displaystyle H^{*}_{T^{n}}(Q_{2n-1})\simeq H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1}],

where the first isomorphism comes from the fact that
Q_{2n-1}
is an equivariantly formal GKM manifold.

On the other hand, there is a non-effective
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-2}
, which is restricted from that on
Q_{2n-1}
.
In the second part of this paper (Section
7
), we study the homomorphism
H^{*}_{T}(Q_{2n-1})\to H_{T}^{*}(Q_{2n-2})
using GKM theory.
We also compute the equivariant cohomology of the non-effective
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-2}
, obtaining the following result by combining Lemma
7.2
and Theorem
7.8
(see Section
7
for details):

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist the following isomorphisms as a ring:

\displaystyle H^{*}_{T^{n}}(Q_{2n-2})\simeq H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-2})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{M},\mathcal{D},X]/\mathcal{I},

where
X
and the elements in
\mathcal{M}
are of degree 2, and
\Delta_{K}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
for
K\subset{\mathcal{V}}_{2n-2}
is of degree
4n-2|K|-2
.

Note that the generators
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{D}
in Theorem
1.2
also appear in the equivariant cohomology of
Q_{2n-2}
with the effective
T^{n}
-action as the equivariant Thom classes, as shown in
[
15
]
.
Theorem
1.2
reveals that in the non-effective case, we also need an additional generator

, which is not induced from the equivariant Thom classes.
Motivated by this phenomenon, in the final part of this paper (Section
8
), we further study the non-effective
T^{1}
-actions on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, and obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\varphi_{n}
be the
n
-times rotated

-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
for
n\geq 0
, and let
H_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
be its equivariant cohomology.
Then, there is the following ring isomorphism:

\displaystyle H_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle,

where

\tau_{p}
(resp.
\tau_{q}
) is the equivariant Thom class associated with the fixed points
p=[1:0]
(resp.
q=[0:1]
), and

\alpha
is the pull-back of the generator of

.

Comparing the effective (

) and the non-effective (

-actions on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

GKM graphs of odd-dimensional complex quadrics
Q_{2n-1}

We use the symbol
(Q_{2n-1},T^{n})
to denote the
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-1}
defined by (
1.2
).
We first describe the GKM graph of
(Q_{2n-1},T^{n})
.
For the basics of GKM manifolds and GKM graphs, see the paper
[

.
In this article, we identify the cohomology ring
H^{*}(BT^{n})
as the following polynomial ring generated by degree 2 generators
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
:

(2.3)

H^{*}(BT^{n})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}].

Here, one may consider the generator
\alpha_{j}
, for
j=1,\ldots,n
, as the
j
-th coordinate projection
pr_{j}:T^{n}\to S^{1}
. Namely, we often use the following identifications:

(2.4)

H^{2}(BT^{n})\simeq\mbox{Hom}(T^{n},S^{1})\simeq(\mathfrak{t}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
\mathfrak{t}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is the lattice of the Lie algebra of
T^{n}
and
(\mathfrak{t}^{n}_{\mathbb{Z}})^{*}
is its dual.

2.1.
The GKM graph of the
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-1}

We now compute the GKM graph of
(Q_{2n-1},T^{n})
. It is easy to check that the
T^{n}
-fixed points of
Q_{2n-1}
are

Q_{2n-1}^{T}=\{[e_{i}]\,:\,1\leq i\leq 2n\},

where
[e_{i}]=[0:\cdots:0:1:0:\cdots:0]\in\mathbb{CP}^{2n}
is the
i
-th coordinate with
1
and
0
otherwise.
Furthermore, the
T^{n}
-invariant
2
-spheres of
Q_{2n-1}
are of the following two types:

\begin{split}&[z_{i}:z_{j}]:=[0:\cdots:0:z_{i}:0:\cdots:0:z_{j}:0:\cdots:0]\in Q_{2n-1},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{if $i+j\neq 2n+1$};\\
&[z_{i}:z_{j}]:=[0:\cdots:0:z_{i}:0:\cdots:0:z_{j}:0:\cdots:z_{2n+1}]\in Q_{2n-1},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{if $i+j=2n+1$}.\end{split}

Note that the second type as above satisfies the equation
z_{i}z_{j}+z_{2n+1}^{2}=0
.
Therefore, every pair of
[e_{i}],[e_{j}]\in Q_{2n-1}^{T}
becomes the fixed points of a
T^{n}
-invariant
2
-sphere.
Hence, we associate the following graph
\Gamma_{2n-1}:=(\mathcal{V}_{2n-1},\penalty\ \mathcal{E}_{2n-1})
with the pair
(Q_{2n-1},T^{n})
:

Vertices:

The set of vertices
\mathcal{V}_{2n-1}
consists of
Q_{2n-1}^{T}
, where we may identify the vertices
Q_{2n-1}^{T}=\{[e_{i}]\ |\ 1\leq i\leq 2n\}
with the set of numbers
[2n]:=\{1,\ldots,2n\}
;

Edges:

The set of edges
\mathcal{E}_{2n-1}
consists of
ij
for every
i,j\in\mathcal{V}_{2n-1}
.

Combinatorially,
\Gamma_{2n-1}
is just the complete graph with
2n
vertices.

.

Here we consider the edges to be undirected. This means that for each undirected edge in the graph, there are two directed edges, one in each direction.

Remark 2.2
.

For simplicity, we often write
j

\overline{i}
when
i+j=2n+1

\Gamma_{2n-1}
can be rewritten as follows:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{2n-1}

\displaystyle:=\{1,\ldots,n,\overline{n},\overline{n-1},\ldots,\overline{1}\};

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{2n-1}

\displaystyle:=\{ij\,:\,i,j\in\mathcal{V}_{2n-1}\text{ such that }j\neq i\}.

We next define an
axial function

\alpha:{\mathcal{E}}_{2n-1}\to H^{2}(BT^{n})
on the edges by computing the tangential representations around the fixed points.
Since the computation for the other vertices follows a similar method, we will compute the tangential representation only around the fixed point
[e_{1}]=[1:0:\cdots:0]
.
Around
[e_{1}]\in Q_{2n-1}^{T}
, the tangential representation is given by

[1:z_{2}:\cdots:z_{2n}:z_{2n+1}]\mapsto[1:t_{1}^{-1}t_{2}z_{2}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-1}t_{n}z_{n}:t_{1}^{-1}t_{n}^{-1}z_{n+1}:\cdots:t_{1}^{-2}z_{2n}:t_{1}^{-1}z_{2n+1}].

We notice that the quadric equation for the
z_{2n}
coordinate satisfies the following relation:

\Big(z_{2n}=-\sum_{i=2}^{n}z_{i}z_{2n+1-i}-z_{2n+1}^{2}\Big)\mapsto\Big(t_{1}^{-2}z_{2n}=-\sum_{i=2}^{n}(t_{1}^{-1}t_{i})z_{i}(t_{1}^{-1}t_{i}^{-1})z_{2n+1-i}-(t_{1}^{-1}z_{2n+1})^{2}\Big).

This shows that the representation on the
z_{2n}
coordinate is automatically determined by the representations on the other corrdinates.
Therefore, the tangential representation around the fixed point
[e_{1}]
can be obtained from the representations on the coordinates except
z_{1}
and
z_{2n}
. More precisely, it splits into the following complex
1
-dimensional irreducible representations:

T_{[e_{1}]}Q_{2n-1}\simeq\bigoplus_{i=2}^{2n-1}V(-\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{i})\oplus V(-\alpha_{1}),

where
V(\beta)
is the complex
1
-dimensional representation defined by the homomorphism
\beta:T^{n}\to S^{1}
.
Recall that
\alpha_{i}\in\mbox{Hom}(T^{n},S^{1})
for
i\in[n]
is the representation corresponding to the
i
-th coordinate projection
pr_{i}:T^{n}\to S^{1}
(see (

For

(2.7)

\alpha_{i}:=-\alpha_{\overline{i}}\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for $i\in\{n+1,\ldots,2n\}$ and $\overline{i}=2n+1-i$.}

Similarly, each tangential representation around a fixed point decomposes into complex
1
-dimensional irreducible representations. Furthermore, each
1
-dimensional irreducible representation corresponds to the tangential representation on the fixed point of the invariant
2
-sphere.
Therefore, we can define the following axial function on the edges:

(2.8)

\alpha:{\mathcal{E}}_{2n-1}\to H^{2}(BT^{n})

which takes

\displaystyle\alpha(ij)=-\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}\text{ for $j\not=i,\overline{i}$},

\displaystyle\alpha(i\overline{i})=-\alpha_{i}.

More precisely, using the notation (
2.7
),
we have the following assignments:

•

\alpha(ij)=-\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
;

•

\alpha(i\overline{j})=-\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{\overline{j}}=-\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}
for
1\leq i\neq j\leq n
;

•

\alpha(\overline{i}j)=-\alpha_{\overline{i}}+\alpha_{j}=+\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{j}
for

;

•

\alpha(\overline{i}\overline{j})=-\alpha_{\overline{i}}+\alpha_{\overline{j}}=\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}
for

;

•

\alpha(i\overline{i})=-\alpha_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,

•

for

Note that the relation
\alpha(ij)=-\alpha(ji)
holds for all

GKM subgraphs

In this subsection, we recall the notion of GKM subgraphs of a GKM graph
(\Gamma,\alpha)
. Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
be a subgraph. We use the following notation:

•

{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma)
and
{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma)
(resp.
{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
and
{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
) denote the sets of vertices and edges of
\Gamma
(resp. of
\Gamma^{\prime}
), respectively.

•

For a vertex
v\in{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma^{\prime})\subset{\mathcal{V}}(\Gamma)
, let
{\mathcal{E}}_{v}(\Gamma)
(resp.
{\mathcal{E}}_{v}(\Gamma^{\prime})
) is the set of outgoing edges in
\Gamma
(resp. in
\Gamma^{\prime}
).

Let
e=vw\in{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma)
be an edge. We define a
connection along

e
to be a bijection

(2.9)

\nabla_{e}:{\mathcal{E}}_{v}(\Gamma)\to{\mathcal{E}}_{w}(\Gamma)

such that
\nabla_{e}(e)=\overline{e}
, where
\overline{e}
is the edge with direction opposite to

. Furthermore, we define a
connection
on graph
\Gamma
to be a collection
\nabla:=\{\nabla_{e}\}_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}(\Gamma)}
satisfying
\nabla_{e}^{-1}=\nabla_{\overline{e}}
for every

.

Definition 2.5

(GKM subgraph)
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a subgraph of
\Gamma
and
\alpha^{\prime}

3.
Generators of
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})

The
graph equivariant cohomology
of the GKM graph
\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1}
is defined as follows:

(3.10)

H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})=\{f:{\mathcal{V}}_{2n-1}\to H^{*}(BT^{n})\penalty\ :\penalty\ f(i)-f(j)\equiv 0\penalty\ \mbox{mod}\penalty\ \alpha(ij)\penalty\ \mbox{for}\penalty\ ij\in{\mathcal{E}}_{2n-1}\}.

The equation

in (

) is often referred to as a
congruence relation
.
Note that
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})
has a graded
H^{*}(BT^{n})
-algebra structure induced by the graded algebra structure of
\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\big(\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathcal{V}}_{2n-1}}H^{k}(BT^{n})\big)

This algebraic structure is also induced by the injective homomorphism

(3.11)

\vartheta:H^{*}(BT^{n})\to H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})

such that the image of
x\in H^{*}(BT^{n})
(i.e.,
\vartheta(x):{\mathcal{V}}_{2n-1}\to H^{*}(BT^{n})
) is defined by the function

\vartheta(x)(i)=x\text{ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ for all $i\in{\mathcal{V}}_{2n-1}$}.

Furthermore,
H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1})
also acquires an
H^{*}(BT^{n})
-module structure induced by the above injective homomorphism
\vartheta
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the
T^{n}
-action on
Q_{2n-1}
, we have the following graded
H^{*}(BT^{n})
-algebra isomorphism:

(3.12)

H^{*}_{T^{n}}(Q_{2n-1})\simeq H^{*}(\mathcal{GQ}_{2n-1}).

Proof.

It is straightforward to verify
that for
x=[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{2n+1}]\in Q_{2n-1}
, the isotropy subgroup is, for some
k\leq n
, given by:

(T^{n})_{x}\simeq\begin{cases}T^{k}&\text{if $z_{2n+1}\neq 0$},\\
T^{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}&\text{if $z_{2n+1}=0$}.\end{cases}

Furthermore, the odd-cohomologies of
Q_{2n-1}
vanish (see Section
6
). Therefore, the isomorphism holds by
[
7
]
, (See, Theorem
7.1
and also the proof of Lemma
7.2
).

∎

Therefore, to compute the

-equivariant cohomology ring

of

it sufficies to compute the graph equivariant cohomology

.
In this section, we introduce three types of elements of

Remark 5.7
.

In
[
9
]
, Guillemin, Holm and Zara study the equivariant cohomology ring of a homogeneous
space using a combinatorial approach.
They obtain the Betti numbers for a broad class of graph equivaraint cohomologies associated with homogeneous spaces, and establish an isomorphism between the Borel description and the graph equivariant cohomology.
In contrast, our work provides explicit generators and relations for the graph equivariant cohomology associated with the odd-dimensional complex quadric.
In addition, we identify the generators corresponding to the subgraphs of the GKM graph.

6.

\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}\simeq H^{2}(BT^{1})
.

In summary, we have the following GKM description for
\varphi_{n}
.

Theorem 8.3

(GKM description for non-effective torus action on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
)
.

For every non-trivial
T^{1}
-action on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
,
there is the following ring isomorphism:

\displaystyle H_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})\simeq\{f:\{p,q\}\to\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\ |\ f(p)-f(q)\equiv 0\mod n\alpha\},

where
\{p,q\}
denotes the set of fixed points in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and
n\geq 1
.

We can also get the ring structure as follows:

Theorem 8.4
.

For
n\geq 0
,
the ring structure of the equivariant cohomology
H_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle H_{\varphi_{n}}^{*}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle,

where

•

\tau_{p},\tau_{q}
are the equivariant Thom classes associated with the fixed points
p=[1:0]
and
q=[0:1]
, respectively (cf. Figure
11
).

•

\alpha
is the pull-back of the generator of
H^{*}(BT)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
.

Proof.

For
n=0
, the statement follows from (
8.60
).
Assuming
n\geq 1
, we need to show the isomorphism:

\displaystyle GKM_{\varphi_{n}}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})

\displaystyle:=\{f:\{p,q\}\to\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]\ |\ f(p)-f(q)\equiv 0\mod n\alpha\}

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle.

In
GKM_{\varphi_{n}}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
, the equivariant Thom classes are defined as follows:

•

\tau_{p}(p)=n\alpha
and
\tau_{p}(q)=0
;

•

\tau_{q}(q)=n\alpha
and
\tau_{q}(p)=0
;

•

\alpha
is constant, so
\alpha(p)=\alpha=\alpha(q)
.

Moreover, it is easy to check that the relations
\tau_{p}\tau_{q}=0
and
n\alpha=\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}
hold in
GKM_{\varphi_{n}}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
.
Thus, this defines the ring homomorphism

(8.61)

\Phi:\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle\to GKM_{\varphi_{n}}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})

by taking the equivariant Thom classes and
\alpha
defined as above.

To show that the map
\Phi
is an isomorphism, we first consider the module structure.
Since

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}(\tau_{p}-n\alpha)\rangle,

we have the following module isomorphism:

(8.62)

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}[\tau_{p},\tau_{q},\alpha]/\langle\tau_{p}\tau_{q},n\alpha-\tau_{p}+\tau_{q}\rangle\simeq\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}\alpha^{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Z}\tau_{p}\alpha^{i}.

On the other hand, by the congruence relations, every element in
GKM_{\varphi_{n}}(\mathbb{CP}^{1})
can be written in one of the following forms
f,g:\{p,q\}\to\mathbb{Z}[\alpha]
, such that

\displaystyle f(p)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}{\alpha^{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}nk_{i}\alpha^{i},\ f(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}\alpha^{i}

or

\displaystyle g(p)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}\alpha^{i},\ g(q)=\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}nk_{i}\alpha^{i},

where
r_{i},k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Namely, we can write using
\tau_{p},\tau_{q}=\tau_{p}-n\alpha
and
\alpha
as follows:

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}\alpha^{i}+\tau_{p}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i+1}\alpha^{i}\right)=f

or

\displaystyle\Phi\left(\sum_{i=0}^{l}r_{i}\alpha^{i}+(\tau_{p}-n\alpha)\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}k_{i+1}\alpha^{i}\right)=g.

Therefore,
\Phi
is surjective.
If
f=0
(or
g=0
), then
r_{i}=k_{i}=0
in the above expressions.
Therefore,
\Phi
is injective.
This establishes the proof.
∎

On a class of singular Hamiltonian Choquard-type elliptic systems with critical exponential growth
Shengbing Deng, Junwei Yu

Abstract




Abstract
: In this paper, we study the following Hamiltonian Choquard-type elliptic systems involving singular weights

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{G(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{g(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v+V(x)v=\Big(I_{\mu_{2}}\ast\frac{F(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}\Big)\frac{f(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in(0,2)
,
0<\alpha\leq\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}
,
0<\beta\leq\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}
,
V(x)
is a continuous positive potential,
I_{\mu_{1}}
and
I_{\mu_{2}}
denote the Riesz potential,
\ast
indicates the convolution operator,
F(s),G(s)
are the primitive of
f(s),g(s)
with
f(s),g(s)
have exponential growth in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Using the linking theorem and variational methods, we establish the existence of solutions to the above problem.

Keywords
: Hamiltonian system; Choquard nonlinearity; Critical exponential growth; Trudinger-Moser inequality; Singular weights.

1.
Introduction

This paper deals with the following class of Hamiltonian elliptic systems of the Choquard type involving singular weights

(1.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{G(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{g(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v+V(x)v=\Big(I_{\mu_{2}}\ast\frac{F(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}\Big)\frac{f(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{array}\right.

where
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in(0,2)
,
0<\alpha\leq\frac{\mu_{1}}{2}
,
0<\beta\leq\frac{\mu_{2}}{2}
,
I_{\mu_{1}}
and
I_{\mu_{2}}
denote the Riesz potential defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle I_{\mu_{i}}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2-\mu_{i}}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\mu_{i}}{2})2^{\mu_{i}}\pi|x|^{2-\mu_{i}}}:=\frac{A_{\mu_{i}}}{|x|^{2-\mu_{i}}},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\},\ i=1,2,\end{aligned}

where
\Gamma
represents the gamma function,
\ast
indicates the convolution operator,
F(t)=\int^{t}_{0}f(\tau)d\tau
and
G(t)=\int^{t}_{0}g(\tau)d\tau
. The potential
V
and the nonlinearities
f,g
satisfy some suitable conditions that will be specified later.

We say that a function
h(s)
has subcritical growth if for all
\gamma>0
we have

(1.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|s|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|h(s)|}{e^{\gamma s^{2}}}=0

and
h(s)
has critical exponential growth if there exists
\gamma_{0}>0
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{|s|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|h(s)|}{e^{\gamma s^{2}}}=0,\ \ \forall\ \gamma>\gamma_{0},\ \ and\ \ \lim\limits_{|s|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{|h(s)|}{e^{\gamma s^{2}}}=+\infty,\ \ \forall\ \gamma<\gamma_{0}.

This notion of criticality is motivated by Trudinger-Moser inequality.

In recent years, there are many works dedicated to study the following non-local elliptic equation

(1.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-\Delta u+u=(I_{\mu}\ast F(u))f(u)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

This nonlocal equation arises in many interesting physical situations in quantum theory and plays an important role in describing the finite-range many-body interactions. Equation
(\ref{b})
is well known in the literature as the Choquard equation and was first introduced in the pioneering work of Fr
\ddot{o}
hlich
[
12
]
for the modeling of quantum polaron. We mention that a great attention has been focused on the study of problem involving exponential growth nonlinearity. In
[
5
]
, Battaglia and Van Schaftingen proved the existence of a nontrivial ground state solution for equation
(\ref{b})
when
F
has exponential growth. Using the Moser type functions, Alves et al.
[
4
]
obtained the existence and concentration results for a class of nonlinear Choquard equations in the plane with exponential critical nonlinearity. Qin and Tang
[
18
]
improved and extended the related results to the strongly indefinite problems. For more classical results regarding Choquard equations, we refer to
[
17
]
for a good survey.

On the other hand, we mention that the study of our problem is based on some interesting results of the following Hamiltonian elliptic system:

(1.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=f_{1}(x,v)&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v+V(x)v=f_{2}(x,u)&\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bound domain. For
V=0
, de Figueiredo et al.
[
9
]
studied the existence of solution for
(\ref{c})
with Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition. Lam and Lu
[
13
]
considered the existence of nontrivial nonnegative solutions for
(\ref{c})
with subcritical and critical exponential growth without the
(AR)
condition. When
V=1
and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the existence of ground state solutions of
(\ref{c})
was introduced by de Figueiredo et al.
[
10
]
. For more classical results regarding Hamiltonian system, the reader may refer
[
7
]
to the good survey.

When
f_{1}(x,v)=\frac{g(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}
and
f_{2}(x,u)=\frac{f(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}
, system
(\ref{c})
becomes the singular Hamiltonian system:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\frac{g(v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\ \ v>0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v+V(x)v=\frac{f(u)}{|x|^{\beta}}\ \ u>0&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha,\beta\in[0,2)
,
V:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous, positive and
[V(x)]^{-1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Souza
[
21
]
established the existence of nontrivial solutions for the system
(\ref{d})
. More precisely, Souza showed the existence results of system
(\ref{d})
with subcritical nonlinearities or critical nonlinearities
(\alpha=\beta)
in the Trudinger-Moser sense. For other singular Hamiltonian systems, we can refer to
[
3
,
6
,
14
]
.

When the nonlinear term is Choquard type, Maia and Miyagaki
[
15
]
studied system
(\ref{c})
of the type

(1.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+V(x)u=\Big(I_{\mu}\ast G(v)\Big)g(v)&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\[5.69054pt]
-\Delta v+V(x)v=\Big(I_{\mu}\ast F(v)\Big)f(v)&\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\end{array}\right.

where
I_{\mu}=\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}
denotes the Riesz potential, the potential
V
and the nonlinearities satisfy:

(H_{0})

f,g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are continuous functions, and both have critical exponential growth;

(H_{1})

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(s)s^{-1}=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}g(s)s^{-1}=0
;

(H_{2})
there exists
\theta>2
, such that
0<\theta F(s)\leq sf(s)
and
0<\theta G(s)\leq sg(s)
for all
s>0
;

(H_{3})
there exist constants
s_{0},M_{0}>0
, such that
0<F(s)\leq M_{0}f(s)
and
0<G(s)\leq M_{0}g(s)
for all
s>s_{0}
;

(H_{4})

f
and
g
are locally bounded;

(H_{5})
there exist constants
p>2
and
C_{p}
such that for all
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle F(s),G(s)\geq 2(\frac{C_{p}}{p})^{\frac{1}{2}}s^{p},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle C_{p}>(\frac{\overline{C}\alpha_{0}(p-1)}{4\pi p})^{\frac{p-1}{p}}S_{p}^{2}\end{aligned}

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle S_{p}=\inf\limits_{u\in H^{1}_{V}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),u\neq 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)u^{2})}{\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big[\frac{1}{|x|^{\mu}}\ast u^{p}\Big]u^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\end{aligned}

where
\overline{C}>\frac{4}{4-\mu}
. By
(H_{5})
, Maia and Miyagaki
[
15
]
obtained an upper bound
for the minimax level. Using approximation methods and linking arguments, they showed the existence of solutions for
(\ref{e})
with critical exponential growth. Moreover, Maia and Miyagaki
[
16
]
investigated the existence and nonexistence results of Hamiltonian Choquard-type elliptic systems with lower critical growth.

Recently, Tang et al.
[
19
]
obtained the existence of ground states for
(\ref{c})
by the non-Nehari manifold method. They developed a direct method to deal with the difficulties aroused by the strongly indefinite features and the critical exponential growth.

The purpose of this work is to consider the existence of solutions for the singular Hamiltonian Choquard-type elliptic systems (
1.1
).
Assume
V
is a continuous function satisfying:

(V_{1})

V(x)\geq\inf_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}V(x):=V_{0}>0
;

(V_{2})
The function
[V(x)]^{-1}
belongs to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Moreover, we assume that
f(s)=g(s)=0
for
t\leq 0
, and the nonlinearities
f,g
satisfy the following conditions:

(H_{0}^{\prime})

f,g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
are continuous functions;

(H_{1}^{\prime})

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}f(s)s^{-1}=\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}g(s)s^{-1}=0
;

(H_{2}^{\prime})
there exists
\theta>2
such that for all
s>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle 0<\theta F(s)\leq sf(s)\ \ and\ \ 0<\theta G(s)\leq sg(s);\end{aligned}

(H_{3}^{\prime})
there exist constants
s_{0},M_{0}>0
such that for all
s>s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle 0<F(s)\leq M_{0}f(s)\ \ and\ \ 0<G(s)\leq M_{0}g(s);\end{aligned}

(H_{4}^{\prime})

\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{tF(t)}{e^{\gamma_{0}t^{2}}}\geq\kappa
and
\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{tG(t)}{e^{\gamma_{0}t^{2}}}\geq\kappa
with

, where
V_{\rho}:=\sup\limits_{|x|\leq\rho}V(x)
.

Our results for the Hamiltonian elliptic system
(\ref{a})
read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
g
has subcritical growth,
f
has subcritical or critical growth,
(V_{1}),(V_{2})
and
(H_{0}^{\prime})-(H_{3}^{\prime})
hold, then system
(\ref{a})
has a nontrivial positive weak solution.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha=\beta
,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
,
f,g
both have critical exponential growth,
(V_{1}),(V_{2})
and
(H_{0}^{\prime})-(H_{4}^{\prime})
hold, then system
(\ref{a})
has a nontrivial positive weak solution.

Remark 1.3
.

We notice that, there are some difficulties to study the existence of solutions for system
(\ref{a})
. The first one is that the energy functional associated with system
(\ref{a})
is strongly indefinite and there is a lack of the compactness for the whole space case. Hence, we use a Galerkin approximation procedure to overcome the lack of compactness. The second one is the nonlinearities with critical exponent and singular weights, which also lead to the lack of compactness. Different from
[
15
]
, we restore compactness by the properties of the exponential critical nonlinear term at infinity. Therefore, we introduce the following
(H_{4}^{\prime})
condition instead of
(H_{5})
.Then using Trudinger-Moser inequality inequality and Doubly Weighted Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, introduced in Section
2
, we address this issue.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, the variational setting and some preliminary results are presented. In Section
3
, we introduce the geometry structure and give some properties related to system
(\ref{a})
. In Section
4
, we give a Galerkin
approximation procedure of system
(\ref{a})
. In Section
5
, using an approaching argument and variational methods we complete the proof of the subcritical case. Section
6
is devoted to give an estimate for the minimax level of the critical case. Finally in Section
7
, we complete the proof of critical case.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\|u\|=\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2}]dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}

4.
Finite dimensional approximation

Since the functional
\Phi
is strongly indefinite and defined in an infinite dimensional space, standard linking theorems are not applicable. We therefore approximate system
(\ref{a})
with a sequence of finite dimensional problems (Galerkin approximation procedure).

Associated with the eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<\lambda_{j}\rightarrow+\infty
of
(-\Delta+V(x),H^{1}_{V})
, there exists an orthonormal basis
\{\phi_{1},\phi_{2},...\}
of corresponding eigenfunctions in
H^{1}_{V}
. We set,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&E_{n}^{+}=\mbox{span}\{(\phi_{i},\phi_{i})|i=1,...,n\}\\
&E_{n}^{-}=\mbox{span}\{(\phi_{i},\phi_{-i})|i=1,...,n\}\\
&E_{n}=E_{n}^{+}\oplus E_{n}^{-}.\end{aligned}

Let
y\in H^{1}_{V}
be a fixed nonnegative function and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle Q_{n,y}=\{r(y,y)+w:w\in E_{n}^{-},\|w\|\leq R_{0}\ and\ 0\leq r\leq R_{1}\},\end{aligned}

where
R_{0},R_{1}
are given in Lemma
3.5
. We recall that these constants depend of
y
only. We use the following notation:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle H_{n,y}=\mathbb{R}(y,y)\oplus E_{n},\ \ H_{n,y}^{+}=\mathbb{R}(y,y)\oplus E_{n}^{+},\ \ H_{n,y}^{-}=\mathbb{R}(y,y)\oplus E_{n}^{-}.\end{aligned}

Furthermore, define the class of mappings

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Gamma_{n,y}=\{h\in C(Q_{n,y},H_{n,y}):h(z)=z\ on\ \partial Q_{n,y}\}\end{aligned}

and set

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle c_{n,y}=\inf\limits_{h\in\Gamma_{n,y}}\max\limits_{z\in Q_{n,y}}\Phi(h(z)).\end{aligned}

Using an intersection theorem (see
[
20
, Proposition 5.9]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle h(Q_{n,y})\cap(\partial B_{\rho}\cap E^{+})\neq\emptyset,\ \forall h\in\Gamma_{n,y},\end{aligned}

which in combination with Lemma

implies that

. On the other hand, since the identity mapping

&\leq r^{2}\|y\|^{2}\leq R_{1}^{2}.\end{aligned}

Then we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle 0<\sigma\leq c_{n,y}\leq R_{1}^{2}.\end{aligned}

We remark that the upper bound does not depend of
n
, but it depends on
y
.

Let us denote by

the functional
\Phi

3.4

6.
The estimates for the critical level

In this section, we assume that
f
and
g
have critical growth with exponent critical
\gamma_{0}
,
\alpha=\beta
and
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
. Then together Trudinger-Moser inequality with
(H_{4}^{\prime})
, we obtain an upper bound for the minimax level. In order to do this, we prove the following result in which we combine the Morser type functions and an approximation argument inspired by Tang et al.
[
19
]
.

Let us introduce the following Moser type functions supported in
B_{\rho}:=B_{\rho}(0)
by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle w_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\log n}&0\leq|x|\leq\rho/n;\\[5.69054pt]
\frac{\log\frac{\rho}{|x|}}{\sqrt{\log n}}&\rho/n\leq|x|\leq\rho;\\[5.69054pt]
0,&|x|\geq\rho.\end{array}\right.\end{aligned}

One has that

\displaystyle\|\nabla w_{n}\|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}dx=1,\qquad\mbox{and}\ \ \ \|w_{n}\|^{2}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|w_{n}|^{2}dx=\delta_{n},

where

(6.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta_{n}=\rho^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{4\log n}-\frac{1}{4n^{2}\log n}-\frac{1}{2n^{2}}\Big)>0.

Thanks to
(H_{4}^{\prime})
we have for all
t\geq R_{\epsilon}
,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle tF(t)\geq(\kappa-\varepsilon)e^{\gamma_{0}t^{2}}\ \ and\ \ tG(t)\geq(\kappa-\varepsilon)e^{\gamma_{0}t^{2}}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
(V_{1}),(V_{2}),(H_{0}^{\prime}),(H_{1}^{\prime})
and
(H_{4}^{\prime})
hold, then there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
the corresponding Moser’s function
w_{n}
satisfies

(6.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\mathbb{R^{+}}(w_{n},w_{n})\bigoplus\mathbb{E^{-}}}\Phi<\frac{(2-2\alpha+\mu_{1})\pi}{\gamma_{0}}.

Proof.

First, we give the following estimate in
B_{\rho/n}(0)
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}\frac{1}{|y|^{\alpha}}dy\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}\frac{dx}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}}&\geq(\frac{\rho}{n})^{-2\alpha}\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}dx\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}\frac{dy}{|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}}\\
&\geq(\frac{\rho}{n})^{-2\alpha}\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}dx\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(x)}\frac{dz}{|z|^{2-\mu_{1}}}\\
&\geq(\frac{\rho}{n})^{-2\alpha}\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}dx\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)-|x|}\frac{dz}{|z|^{2-\mu_{1}}}\\
&=(\frac{\rho}{n})^{-2\alpha}\frac{2\pi}{\mu_{1}}\int\limits_{B_{\rho/n}(0)}(\frac{\rho}{n}-|x|)^{\mu_{1}}\\
&=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\mu_{1}(1+\mu_{1})(2+\mu_{1})}(\frac{\rho}{n})^{2-2\alpha+\mu_{1}}\\
&=C_{\mu_{1}}(\frac{\rho}{n})^{2-2\alpha+\mu_{1}},\end{aligned}

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle C_{\mu_{1}}=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{\mu_{1}(1+\mu_{1})(2+\mu_{1})}.\end{aligned}

By
(V_{1}),(V_{2})
we know that V is bounded locally. Thus we let
V_{\rho}=\max\limits_{{x\in[0,\rho]}}{V(x)}\leq C
. Then by
(H_{4}^{\prime})
, we may choose
\varepsilon>0
small such that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}>\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}Q}{4}-1.

And using
(\ref{ESTa})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)&=t^{2}\|w_{n}\|^{2}-\|v\|^{2}-\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\\
&\leq t^{2}(\|\nabla w_{n}\|^{2}_{2}+V_{\rho}\|w_{n}\|^{2}_{2})-\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\\
&\leq(1+V_{\rho}\delta_{n})t^{2}-\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v),\ \ \ \forall\ t\geq 0.\end{aligned}

where
\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{F(tw_{n}+v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{F(tw_{n}+v)}{|x|^{\beta}}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{G(tw_{n}-v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{G(tw_{n}-v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx.

Then let

, there are four cases to distinguish.

Case
i).

t\in[0,\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{2\gamma_{0}}}]
. Then by

, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\leq\frac{Q\pi}{2\gamma_{0}}+O(\frac{1}{\log n}),\end{aligned}

which implies that
(\ref{ESTc})
hold.

Case
ii).

t\in[\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{2\gamma_{0}}},\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}}]
. In this case,
tw_{n}\geq R_{\varepsilon}
for
x\in B_{\rho/n}(0)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
large. Then using
(3.1)
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle F(tw_{n}+v)\geq\frac{(\kappa-\varepsilon)e^{\gamma_{0}(tw_{n}+v)^{2}}}{tw_{n}+v}\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \ G(tw_{n}-v)\geq\frac{(\kappa-\varepsilon)e^{\gamma_{0}(tw_{n}-v)^{2}}}{tw_{n}-v},\end{aligned}

which, together with
(\ref{ESTb})
, yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{F(tw_{n}+v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{F(tw_{n}+v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(I_{\mu_{1}}\ast\frac{G(tw_{n}-v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}\Big)\frac{G(tw_{n}-v)}{|x|^{\alpha}}dx\\
\geq&\frac{A_{\mu_{1}}}{2}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\frac{F(tw_{n}+v(x))\cdot F(tw_{n}+v(y))}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy\\
&\quad+\frac{A_{\mu_{1}}}{2}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\frac{G(tw_{n}-v(x))\cdot G(tw_{n}-v(y))}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy.\end{aligned}

Next by the inequality
A(x)e^{x}+B(y)e^{y}\geq 2\sqrt{A(x)B(y)}e^{\frac{x+y}{2}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\geq&\frac{A_{\mu_{1}}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}}{t^{2}w_{n}^{2}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\frac{e^{2\gamma_{0}t^{2}w_{n}^{2}+v^{2}(x)+v^{2}(y)}}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy\\

Then we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}}{Qn^{Q}\log n}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}:=\varphi_{n}(t).\end{aligned}

Choosing

satisfying

, then we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle 1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n}=\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{Q\pi n^{Q}}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}.\end{aligned}

It follows that

(6.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle t_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}\Big[1+\frac{\log(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})-\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q}}{Q\log n}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})},

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\varphi_{n}(t)\leq\varphi_{n}(t_{n})=(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t_{n}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n}),\ \ \forall\ t\geq 0.\end{aligned}

Using
(\ref{ESTd})
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\varphi_{n}(t)&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t_{n}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\\
&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\Big[\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}\Big]-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n})\\
&\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}\pi Q}{4\gamma_{0}\log n}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n}).\end{aligned}

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\Big[\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}Q}{4}-1-\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}\Big]+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n}),\end{aligned}

which together with

hold.

Case
iii).

t\in\Big[\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}},\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}(1+\varepsilon)}\Big]
. In this case,
tw_{n}\geq R_{\varepsilon}
for

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)&\geq\frac{A_{\mu_{1}}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}}{t^{2}w_{n}^{2}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\int_{B_{\rho/n}}\frac{e^{2\gamma_{0}t^{2}w_{n}^{2}+v^{2}(x)+v^{2}(y)}}{|x|^{\alpha}|x-y|^{2-\mu_{1}}|y|^{\alpha}}dxdy\\
&\geq\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}}{(1+\varepsilon)Qn^{Q}\log n}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}.\end{aligned}

Then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}}{(1+\varepsilon)Qn^{Q}\log n}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}:=\psi_{n}(t).\end{aligned}

Choosing
\widetilde{t}_{n}>0
satisfying
\psi_{n}^{\prime}(\widetilde{t}_{n})=0
, then we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle 1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n}=\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon)\pi Qn^{Q}}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n},\end{aligned}

It follows that

(6.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widetilde{t}_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}\Big[1+\frac{\log(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})-\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon)\pi Q}}{Q\log n}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\log\frac{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\psi_{n}(t)\leq\psi_{n}(\widetilde{t}_{n})=(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\widetilde{t}_{n}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n}),\ \ \forall\ t\ \geq\ 0.\end{aligned}

Using
(\ref{ESTe})
, we get

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\psi_{n}(t)&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\widetilde{t}_{n}^{2}-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\\
&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\Big[\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\log\frac{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}\Big]-\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n})\\
&\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\Big[\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}Q}{4}-1-\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}\Big]+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n}).\end{aligned}

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\leq\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}+\frac{\pi}{\gamma_{0}\log n}\Big[\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}Q}{4}-1-\log\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}\gamma_{0}^{2}}{\pi Q(1+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon V_{\rho}\rho^{2})}\Big]+O(\frac{1}{\log^{2}n}),\end{aligned}

which together with
(\ref{import})
imply that
(\ref{ESTc})
hold.

Case
iv).

t\in\Big(\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}(1+\varepsilon)},+\infty\Big)
. In this case,
tw_{n}\geq R_{\varepsilon}
for
x\in B_{\rho/n}(0)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
large. Then using
(\ref{ESTb})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\Phi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\Psi(tw_{n}+v,tw_{n}-v)\\
&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\frac{2\pi A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}}{t^{2}n^{Q}\log n}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}\\
&\leq(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})\frac{Q\pi(1+\varepsilon)}{\gamma_{0}}-\frac{2A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}Q\log n}e^{Q\varepsilon\log n}\\
&<\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}},\end{aligned}

where we have used the fact that the function

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \displaystyle\phi(t)=(1+\frac{V_{\rho}\rho^{2}}{4\log n})t^{2}-\frac{2\pi A_{\mu_{1}}C_{\mu_{1}}\rho^{Q}(\kappa-\varepsilon)^{2}}{t^{2}n^{Q}\log n}e^{(\pi)^{-1}\gamma_{0}t^{2}\log n}\end{aligned}

is decreasing on
t\in\Big(\sqrt{\frac{Q\pi}{\gamma_{0}}(1+\varepsilon)},+\infty\Big)
. Thus
(\ref{ESTc})
holds for
n\geq n_{0}
.

Then the proof is complete.
∎

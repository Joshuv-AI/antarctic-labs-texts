(\gamma(p),\;p)
is said to be
Schrödinger admissible
if

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{2\leq p\leq\frac{2n}{n-2},\;n\geq 3,}\\
{2\leq p<\infty,\;n=2,}\\
{2\leq p\leq\infty,\;n=1,}\end{array}\right.

(2.5)

and

\frac{2}{\gamma(p)}=\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n}{p}.

(2.6)

For a given interval
I
, we also define the following
Strichartz norms
:

\left\|u\right\|_{S\left(I,\;\dot{H}^{s}\right)}={\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right)\in A}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)}\left(I,\;\dot{H}_{r}^{s}\right)},\;\left\|u\right\|_{S\left(I,\;H^{s}\right)}={\mathop{\sup}\limits_{\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right)\in A}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)}\left(I,\;H_{r}^{s}\right)},

(2.7)

and the
dual Strichartz norms
:

\left\|u\right\|_{S^{\prime}\left(I,\;\dot{H}^{s}\right)}={\mathop{\inf}\limits_{\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right)\in A}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,\;\dot{H}_{r^{\prime}}^{s}\right)},\;\left\|u\right\|_{S^{\prime}\left(I,\;H^{s}\right)}={\mathop{\inf}\limits_{\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right)\in A}}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(r\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(I,\;H_{r^{\prime}}^{s}\right)},

(2.8)

where
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
A=\left\{\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right);\;\left(\gamma\left(r\right),\;r\right)\;\textnormal{is admissible}\right\}
. We shall abbreviate
S\left(\mathbb{R},\;X\right)
and
S^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{R},\;X\right)
as
S\left(X\right)
and
S^{\prime}\left(X\right)
, respectively, where
X
is
\dot{H}^{s}
or
H^{s}
. We write
S\left(L^{2}\right)
and
S^{\prime}\left(L^{2}\right)
instead of
S(\dot{H}^{0})
and
S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{0})
, respectively.

We end this section with recalling the well-known Strichartz estimates which are foundational tool to obtain the well–posedness results. See
[
17
,
24
]
for instance.

Lemma 2.9

(Strichartz estimates)
.

Let
S(t)=e^{it\Delta}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

\left\|S(t)\phi\right\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s})}\lesssim\left\|\phi\right\|_{\dot{H}^{s}},

(2.9)

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s})}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{s})}.

(2.10)

3
Nonlinear estimates

First, we establish the following important nonlinear estimates in Sobolev spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
1<p,\;r<\infty
,
0\leq\delta\leq\min\left\{s_{0},\;s\right\}<\infty,\;\sigma>0
and
\left\lceil s-\delta\right\rceil\leq\sigma+1
. Assume that
f\in C^{\left\lceil s-\delta\right\rceil}
satisfies the following condition:

\left|f^{\left(k\right)}\left(z\right)\right|\lesssim\left|z\right|^{\sigma+1-k},

(3.1)

for any
0\leq k\leq\left\lceil s-\delta\right\rceil
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Suppose that

\frac{1}{p}=\sigma\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}}{n}\right)+\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\delta}{n},\;\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}}{n}>0.

(3.2)

Then we have

\left\|f\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{s-\delta}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.3)

Proof.

If
s-\delta\in\mathbb{N}
, the proof can be found in Lemma 2.1 of
[
23
]
. Thus it suffices to consider the case
s-\delta\notin\mathbb{N}
. We use the argument similar to that used in the proof of Lemma 2.1 of
[
23
]
.
First, we consider the case
s-\delta<1
. Putting

\frac{1}{p_{1}}:=\sigma\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}}{n}\right),\;\frac{1}{p_{2}}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{\delta}{n},

and using Lemma 2.5, we have
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset L^{\frac{p_{1}}{\sigma}}
,
\dot{H}_{r}^{s}\subset\dot{H}_{p_{2}}^{s-\delta}
. Hence, using Lemma 2.4 (fractional chain rule), (3.1), (3.2) and Hölder inequality , we have

\left\|f\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{s-\delta}}\lesssim\left\|f^{\prime}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{1}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{2}}^{s-\delta}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\frac{p_{1}}{\sigma}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{2}}^{s-\delta}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

Next, we consider the case
s-\delta>1
. By Lemma 2.1, we have

\left\|f\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{s-\delta}}\lesssim\sum_{\left|\alpha\right|=\left[s-\delta\right]}\left\|D^{\alpha}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{v}},

where
v=s-\delta-\left[s-\delta\right]
. Without loss of generality and for simplicity, we assume that
f
is a function of a real variable. It follows from the Leibniz rule of derivatives that

D^{\alpha}f\left(u\right)=\sum_{q=1}^{\left|\alpha\right|}\sum_{\Lambda_{\alpha}^{q}}C_{\alpha,\;q}f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u,

(3.4)

where
\Lambda_{\alpha}^{q}=\left(\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{q}=\alpha,\;\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\geq 1\right)
. Hence it suffices to show that

A\equiv\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}},

(3.5)

where
\left[s-\delta\right]\geq q\geq 1
,
\left|\alpha_{1}\right|+\cdots+\left|\alpha_{q}\right|=\left[s-\delta\right]
,
\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\geq 1
and
v=s-\delta-\left[s-\delta\right]
.

We divide the proof of (3.5) in two cases:
s\leq s_{0}+1
and
s>s_{0}+1
.

Case 1
: We consider the case
s\leq s_{0}+1
. Put

\frac{1}{a}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}}{n},\;\frac{1}{b}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}-v}{n},\;\frac{1}{\tilde{a}_{q}}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s-\left|\alpha_{q}\right|}{n},

(3.6)

\frac{1}{a_{i}}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}-\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}{n},\;\frac{1}{b_{i}}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s-\left|\alpha_{i}\right|-v}{n},\;i=1,\;\ldots,\;q.

(3.7)

We can see that
v=s-\delta-\left[s-\delta\right]\leq s-\delta-1\leq s_{0}
, which implies that
b>0
and
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset\dot{H}_{b}^{v}
. We can also see that
s-\left|\alpha_{i}\right|-v\geq s-\left[s-\delta\right]-v=\delta\geq 0
and
s-\left|\alpha_{i}\right|-v\leq s-1\leq s_{0}
, which imply that
b_{i}>0
and
\dot{H}_{r}^{s}\subset\dot{H}_{b_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|+v}
. We also have
\dot{H}_{r}^{s}\subset\dot{H}_{\tilde{a}_{q}}^{\left|\alpha_{q}\right|}
, since
s\geq\left|\alpha_{i}\right|
. If
q\geq 2
, then we can verify that
\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\leq\left[s-\delta\right]-1\leq s-1\leq s_{0}
, which implies that
a_{i}>0
and
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset\dot{H}_{a_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}
. For
1\leq k\leq q
, we have

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\sigma-q}{a}+\frac{1}{b}+\frac{1}{\tilde{a}_{q}}+\sum_{i\in I_{{}_{q}}}\frac{1}{a_{i}}=\frac{\sigma-q+1}{a}+\frac{1}{b_{k}}+\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\frac{1}{a_{i}},

(3.8)

where
I_{k}=\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\;1\leq i\leq q,\;i\neq k\right\}
. Using (3.8) and Lemma 2.2 (fractional product rule), we have

A\lesssim\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{1}}^{v}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{r_{1}}+\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{2}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r_{2}}^{v}}\equiv A_{1}+A_{2},

(3.9)

where

\frac{1}{p_{1}}:=\frac{\sigma-q}{a}+\frac{1}{b},\;\frac{1}{r_{1}}:=\frac{1}{\tilde{a}_{q}}+\sum_{i\in I_{{}_{q}}}\frac{1}{a_{i}},

(3.10)

\frac{1}{p_{2}}:=\frac{\sigma-q+1}{a},\;\frac{1}{r_{2}}:=\frac{1}{b_{k}}+\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\frac{1}{a_{i}}.

(3.11)

First, we estimate
A_{1}
. Since
q\leq\sigma,
it follows from Lemma 2.4 (fractional chain rule) and (3.1) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{1}}^{v}}&\lesssim\left\|f^{\left(q+1\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\frac{a}{\sigma-q}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{b}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|\left|u\right|^{\sigma-q}\right\|_{\frac{a}{\sigma-q}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{b}^{v}}\\
&=\left\|u\right\|_{a}^{\sigma-q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{b}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma-q+1},\end{split}

(3.12)

where we assume
\frac{a}{0}=\infty
and the last inequality follows from the embeddings:
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset L^{a},\;\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset\dot{H}_{b}^{v}
. Using Hölder inequality, we also have

\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{r_{1}}\leq\left\|D^{\alpha_{q}}u\right\|_{\tilde{a}_{q}}\prod_{i\in I_{q}}\left\|D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{a_{i}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{\tilde{a}_{q}}^{\left|\alpha_{q}\right|}}\prod_{i\in I_{q}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{a_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{q-1}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.13)

In view of (3.12) and (3.13), we have

A_{1}=\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{1}}^{v}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{r_{1}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.14)

Next, we estimate
A_{2}
. We can easily see that

\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{2}}\lesssim\left\|\left|u\right|^{\sigma+1-q}\right\|_{p_{2}}=\left\|u\right\|_{a}^{\sigma+1-q}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma+1-q}.

(3.15)

Using (3.11) and Corollary 2.3, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r_{2}}^{v}}&\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(\left\|D^{\alpha_{k}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{b_{k}}^{v}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|D^{\alpha_{i}}u_{i}\right\|_{a_{i}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{b_{k}}^{\left|\alpha_{k}\right|+v}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{a_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}}\right)\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{q-1}{\rm.}\end{split}

(3.16)

In view of (3.15) and (3.16), we immediately have

A_{2}=\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{2}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r_{2}}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.17)

Using (3.9), (3.14) and (3.17), we have (3.5).

Case 2
: We consider the case
s>s_{0}+1
. Put
\beta_{0}=v
. In
[
23
]
, it was proved that one can choose
\beta_{i}\;\left(i=1,\;\ldots,\;q\right)
satisfying the following conditions:

0\leq\beta_{i}\leq\left|\alpha_{i}\right|,\;s_{0}+\beta_{i}\geq\left|\alpha_{i}\right|,\;i=1,\;\ldots,\;q,

(3.18)

\sum_{i=0}^{q}\beta_{i}=s-s_{0}.

(3.19)

For details, see the proof of Lemma 2.1. Put

\frac{1}{c_{i}}:=\frac{1}{r}-\frac{s_{0}+\beta_{i}-\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}{n},\;i=1,\;\ldots,\;q.

(3.20)

Using (3.18) and (3.19), we can see that

c_{i}>0,\;s_{0}\leq s_{0}+\beta_{i}\leq s,\;i=0,\;\ldots,\;q.

(3.21)

It also follows from (3.2) that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\sigma-q+1}{a}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{c_{i}},

(3.22)

where
a
is given in (3.6). Using Lemma 2.2 (fractional product rule), we have

A\lesssim\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{3}}^{v}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{r_{3}}+\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{3}}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r_{3}}^{v}}\equiv A_{3}+A_{4},

(3.23)

where

\frac{1}{p_{3}}:=\frac{\sigma-q+1}{a},\;\frac{1}{r_{3}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{q}\frac{1}{c_{i}}.

(3.24)

First, we estimate
A_{3}
. Since
q\leq\sigma,
it follows from Lemma 2.4 (fractional chain rule) and (3.1) that

\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\dot{H}_{p_{3}}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|f^{\left(q+1\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{\frac{a}{\sigma-q}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{a}^{v}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma-q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{0}}},

(3.25)

where we assume
\frac{a}{0}=\infty
and the last inequality follows from the embeddings:
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+v}\subset\dot{H}_{a}^{v},\;\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}\subset L^{a}
. We also have

\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{r_{3}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{c_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}},

(3.26)

where the last inequality follows from the embedding
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}\subset\dot{H}_{c_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}
.
For
i=0,\;\ldots,\;q
, we can take
0\leq\theta_{i}\leq 1
satisfying
s_{0}+\beta_{i}=\theta_{i}s_{0}+\left(1-\theta_{i}\right)s
, since
s_{0}\leq s_{0}+\beta_{i}\leq s
.
Using (3.19), we can easily see that
\sum_{i=0}^{q}\theta_{i}=q
and
\sum_{i=0}^{q}\left(1-\theta_{i}\right)=1
. Hence, it follows from Lemma 2.7 (convexity Hölder inequality) that

\prod_{i=0}^{q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{q}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.27)

In view of (3.25)–(3.27), we have

A_{3}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.28)

Next, we estimate
A_{4}
. Using Hölder inequality, we immediately have

\left\|f^{\left(q\right)}\left(u\right)\right\|_{p_{3}}\lesssim\left\|\left|u\right|^{\sigma+1-q}\right\|_{p_{3}}=\left\|u\right\|_{a}^{\sigma+1-q}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma+1-q}.

(3.29)

Using Corollary 2.3, (3.20) and (3.24), we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\prod_{i=1}^{q}D^{\alpha_{i}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r_{3}}^{v}}&\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(\left\|D^{\alpha_{k}}u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{{}_{c_{k}}}^{v}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|D^{\alpha_{i}}u_{i}\right\|_{c_{i}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{c_{k}}^{\left|\alpha_{k}\right|+v}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{c_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}}\right)\\
&\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{k}+\beta_{0}}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}}\right){\rm,}\end{split}

(3.30)

where the last inequality follows from the embeddings:
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{k}+v}\subset\dot{H}_{c_{k}}^{\left|\alpha_{k}\right|+v}
and
\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}\subset\dot{H}_{c_{i}}^{\left|\alpha_{i}\right|}
.
In view of (3.19), we can also see that
s_{0}+\beta_{k}+\beta_{0}\leq s
for
k=1,\;\ldots,\;q
. Thus we can take
0\leq\tilde{\theta}_{k}\leq 1
satisfying
s_{0}+\beta_{k}+\beta_{0}=\tilde{\theta}_{k}s_{0}+(1-\tilde{\theta}_{k})s
, for
k=1,\;\ldots,\;q
. In view of (3.19), we can see that

\tilde{\theta}_{k}+\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\theta_{i}=q-1,\;(1-\tilde{\theta}_{k})+\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\left(1-\theta_{i}\right)=1.

Hence, using Lemma 2.7 (convexity Hölder inequality), we have

\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{k}+\beta_{0}}}\prod_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}+\beta_{i}}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{q-1}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.31)

(3.29), (3.30) and (3.31) yield that

A_{4}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s_{0}}}^{\sigma}\left\|u\right\|_{\dot{H}_{r}^{s}}.

(3.32)

Using (3.23), (3.28) and (3.32), we immediately have (3.5). This completes the proof.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
0<s<\frac{n}{2}+1
,
0<b<\min\left\{2,{\rm\;1}+\frac{n-2s}{2}\right\}
and
\sigma_{0}<\sigma<\sigma_{s}
. Assume that
f
is of class
X\left(\sigma,s,b\right)
. Then we have

\left\||x|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma}v\right\|_{S^{\prime}\left(L^{2}\right)}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{S\left(H^{s}\right)}^{\sigma}\left\|v\right\|_{S\left(L^{2}\right)},

(3.33)

\left\||x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{s})}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{S\left(H^{s}\right)}^{\sigma+1}.

(3.34)

Proof.

Putting

\bar{r}:=\frac{2n}{n-2},\;\frac{1}{r_{1}}:=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n\left(\sigma+1\right)},

(3.35)

we can easily see that
\left(\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right),\bar{r}\right)
and
\left(\gamma\left(r_{1}\right),r_{1}\right)
are admissible. Furthermore, we can see that

\frac{1}{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}=\frac{\sigma+1}{\gamma\left(r_{1}\right)}.

(3.36)

For
B=B\left(0,\;1\right)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\left|x\right|\leq 1\right\}
, we have

\left\||x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{s})}\leq\left\||x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;\dot{H}_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}^{s}\right)}\leq C_{1}+C_{2},

(3.37)

\left\||x|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma}v\right\|_{S^{\prime}\left(L^{2}\right)}\leq\left\||x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;L^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}\right)}\leq D_{1}+D_{2},

(3.38)

where

C_{1}=\left\|\chi_{B^{C}}|x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;\dot{H}_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}^{s}\right)},~C_{2}=\left\|\chi_{B}|x|^{-b}f\left(u\right)\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;\dot{H}_{\bar{r}^{\prime}}^{s}\right)},

(3.39)

D_{1}=\left\|\chi_{B^{C}}|x|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma}v\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;L^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}\right)},~D_{2}=\left\|\chi_{B}|x|^{-b}\left|u\right|^{\sigma}v\right\|_{L^{\gamma\left(\bar{r}\right)^{{{}^{\prime}}}}\left(\mathbb{R},\;L^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}\right)}.

(3.40)

First, we estimate
C_{1}
and
D_{1}
. We can take
s_{0}\left(>0\right)
satisfying the following system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{0<\frac{1}{\bar{r}^{\prime}}-\sigma\left(\frac{1}{r_{1}}-\frac{s_{0}}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{r_{1}}<\frac{b}{n},\;}\\
{0<s_{0}<s,}\\
{\frac{1}{r_{1}}>\frac{s_{0}}{n}.}\end{array}\right.

(3.41)

In fact, we can see that the first equation in (3.41) is equivalent to

\frac{n}{2}-\frac{2}{\sigma}<s_{0}<\frac{n}{2}-\frac{2-b}{\sigma},

(3.42)

and the third equation in (3.41) is equivalent to

, since

\frac{n}{2}-\frac{2}{\sigma}<s

s_{0}\left(>0\right)
satisfying (3.44). Putting

(3.46)

it follows from (3.41) that

. Lemma 2.2 (fractional product rule) yields

It also follows from Hölder inequality and (3.48) that

Using (3.51), (3.52), (3.36) and Hölder inequality, we have

Next, we estimate

and

{\frac{1}{r_{1}}>\frac{s_{1}}{n}.}\end{array}\right.

(3.55)

In fact, we can see that the first equation in (3.55) is equivalent to

(3.56)

(3.57)

Proof of Theorem 1.3.
.

The proof is similar to one of Theorem 1.4 of
[
15
]
and we only sketch the proof. We define

(4.2)

(4.3)

(4.4)

15
]

(4.5)

Abstract
The study of weak domains and quasicontinuous domains leads to the consideration of two types generalizations of domains. In the current paper, we define the weak way-below relation between two nonempty subsets of a poset and quasiexact posets. We prove some connections among quasiexact posets, quasicontinuous domains and weak domains. Furthermore, we introduce the weak way-below finitely determined topology and study its links to Scott topology and the weak way-below topology first considered by Mushburn. It is also proved that a dcpo is a domain if it is quasiexact and moderately meet continuous with the weak way-below relation weakly increasing.

1
Introduction

Scott
[
11
]
proposed a model for information systems using the Scott topology and a binary relation
\prec
in connection with the information models. For continuous lattices, the relation
\prec
coincides with the way-below relation. The class of continuous complete lattices was introduced by Scott
[
12
]
. However, for general complete lattices, the aforementioned two relations may be distinct.

One of the notable features of continuous lattices is that they admit a unique compact Hausdorff topology for which the meet operation is continuous. This topology, referred to as the CL-topology
[
4
]
, turns out to be ‘order intrinsic’ - it can be defined merely using the lattice structure. Gierz and Lawson
[
4
]
characterized those complete lattices for which the CL-topology is Hausdorff and called them generalized continuous lattices. Gierz et al.
[
5
]
introduced the quasicontinuous posets and showed that a complete lattice is generalized continuous if and only if it is quasicontinuous. The key result for establishing the major properties of quasicontinuous dcpos is the Rudin’s Lemma
[
10
]
.

Coecke and Martin
[
2
]
introduced two orders: the Bayesian order on classical states and the spectral order on quantum states. They revealed that the corresponding sets are dcpos with an intrinsic notion of approximation. The operational significance of the orders involved conclusively establishes that physical information has a natural domain-like theoretic structure. Mushburn
[
14
]
called the approximation in
[
2
]
the weak way-below relation, and defined two topologies on posets: the way-below topology and the weak way-below topology. These topologies coincide with the Scott topology for continuous posets, but are very different for non-continuous posets. Mushburn also showed that while domain representable spaces must be Baire, this is not the case with respect to the new topologies. Thus, Mushburn defined the weak domains and weak domain representable spaces and constructed an example to show that weak domain representable spaces need not be Baire
[
15
]
.

The class of meet continuous lattices was first introduced by Birkhoff
[
1
]
. Later, much investigations on meet continuity for lattices and semilattices sprang up. One can refer to Isbell
[
8
]
, Hofmann and Stralka
[
7
]
and
[
3
]
. Kou et al.
[
9
]
extended the notion of meet continuity to general dcpos and proved that a dcpo is continuous iff it is meet continuous and quasicontinuous. See
[
17
]
and
[
18
]
for the investigation of the more general meet-continuous posets and quasicontinuous posets.

As a generalization of the way-below relation between two subsets of posets and the weak way-below relation between elements, we define the weak way-below relation between two subsets of a poset and use this relation to define the quasiexact posets. We show some connections among quasiexact posets, quasicontinuous domains and weak domains. Furthermore, we introduce the weak way-below finitely determined topology (briefly, wf topology) and study its properties as well as its links to Scott topology and weak way-below topology in some special posets. In addition, we prove that a poset is a weak domain if it is moderately meet continuous and quasiexact dcpo with the relation
\ll_{w}
weakly increasing, a result similar to the characterization of domains in terms of meet continuity and quasicontinuity. Finally, employing a result by Shen et al.
[
16
]
, it is deduced that a dcpo is a domain if and only if it is quasiexact and moderately meet continuous with the weak way-below relation weakly increasing.

3
Quasiexact dcpos

We first introduce the following definition, as the generalization of both the way-below relation between two nonempty subsets and the weak way-below relation between two points.

Definition 3

For any poset
P
and
G,H\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
, we say that
G
is
weakly way below

H
and write
G\ll_{w}H
, if for every directed subset
D\subseteq P
,
\bigvee D\in H
implies
d\in\uparrow\penalty\!G
for some
d\in D
.

The following properties can be verified straightforwardly.

Lemma 4
.

For any poset
P
, let
G,G^{\prime},H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
and
x\in P
. Then

(1)

G\ll_{w}H
if and only if
G\ll_{w}x
for all
x\in H
;

(2)

G\ll_{w}H
if and only if
\uparrow\penalty\!G\ll_{w}H
;

(3)

G\ll_{w}H
and
G\subseteq G^{\prime}
imply
G^{\prime}\ll_{w}H
;

(4)

G\ll_{w}H
and
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
imply
G\ll_{w}H^{\prime}
.

Thereafter, we write
G\ll_{w}x
instead of
G\ll_{w}\{x\}
and
y\ll_{w}H
instead of
\{y\}\ll_{w}H
. By Lemma
4
,
y\ll_{w}x
is unambiguously defined and this fact is similar with the one for the way-below relation.

For any poset
P
and
x\in P
, write

\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)=\{F\subseteq P:F~\mbox{is finite},F\ll_{w}x\}.

Definition 5

A poset
P
is said to be
quasiexact
if for each
x\in P
, the family
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
is directed and
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}=\uparrow\penalty\!x
.

Remark 6
.

For any poset
P
, it is easy to verify that
F\ll_{w}x
implies
x\in\uparrow\penalty\!F
for any
F\subseteq P
and
x\in P
. Thus,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}\supseteq\uparrow\penalty\!x
holds for all
x\in P
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
. In particular,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}\supseteq\uparrow\penalty\!x
for any
x\in P
.

Sometimes it is difficult to characterize the weak way-below relation on a poset. To show a poset
P
is quasiexact, it is sufficient to know for any
x\in P
, there are ‘enough’ elements that are weakly way below
x
. In order to elaborate this fact, we first show the following lemmas.

The proofs of the following Lemmas
7
and
8
are straightforward.

Lemma 7
.

For any poset
P
and
F\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P),x\in P
,
F\ll_{w}x~\mbox{if and only if}~\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F\ll_{w}x.

Lemma 8
.

For any poset
P
, let
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
and
x\in P
. If
\mathcal{F}
is directed, then
\{F\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is directed provided that each
F\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x
is nonempty.

The following lemma given by Rudin

is crucial in proving a number of major properties of quasicontinuous domains, one can also refer to Lemma III-3.3 in

Lemma 9
.

(
Rudin’s Lemma
)

For any poset
P
, let
\mathcal{F}
be a directed family of nonempty finite subsets of
P
. There exists a directed set
D\subseteq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F
such that
D\cap F\neq\varnothing
for every
F\in\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 10
.

For any dcpo
P
, let
G\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
and
x\in P
with
G\ll_{w}x
. If
\mathcal{F}
is a directed family of nonempty finite subsets
F\subseteq\downarrow\penalty\!x
with
\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\uparrow\penalty\!F\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!x
, then there exists
F_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F_{0}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!G
.

Proof 3.1
.

Assume, on the contrary that
F\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G\neq\varnothing
for all
F\in\mathcal{F}
. For any
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
, choose
F_{3}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F_{3}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}
. Then
F_{3}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G\subseteq(\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2})\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G=(\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)\cap(\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
. It is easy to verify that
\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!(F_{1}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
and
\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!(F_{2}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
. Hence

F_{3}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!(F_{1}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)\cap\uparrow\penalty\!(F_{2}\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
.
Thus,
\{F\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G:F\in\mathcal{F}\}
is a directed family of nonempty finite subsets. By Rudin’s Lemma, there exists a directed set
D\subseteq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(F\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
such that
D\cap(F\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)\neq\varnothing
for any
F\in\mathcal{F}
. For every
F\in\mathcal{F}
, choose
d_{F}\in D
such that
d_{F}\in F\setminus\uparrow\penalty\!G
. Then
\bigvee D\in\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\uparrow\penalty\!d_{F}\subseteq\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\uparrow\penalty\!(F\setminus\uparrow\penalty\!G)\subseteq\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\uparrow\penalty\!F\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!x
. Thus,
\bigvee D\geq x
. Note that
D\subseteq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}F\subseteq\downarrow\penalty\!x
, so
\bigvee D\leq x
. It follows that
\bigvee D=x
. By
G\ll_{w}x
, there exists
d\in D
such that
d\in\uparrow\penalty\!G
, which contradicts
D\subseteq\bigcup_{F\in\mathcal{F}}(F\backslash\uparrow\penalty\!G)
.

\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!(\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F):F\in\mathcal{F}\}\subseteq\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\mathcal{F}\}=\uparrow\penalty\!x

\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F_{3}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!(\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F_{1})\cap\uparrow\penalty\!(\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F_{2})

\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap F_{3}\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)

We write
\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)=\{F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x),F\subseteq\downarrow\penalty\!x\}

Proposition 12

A dcpo
P
is quasiexact if and only if for any
x\in P

\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)
is directed and
\bigcap_{F\in\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)}\uparrow\penalty\!F=\uparrow\penalty\!x

.

Note that
\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
. The sufficiency is immediately from Proposition

is quasiexact. Then for each

\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
is directed and
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!G:G\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}=\uparrow\penalty\!x
. By Lemma

\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)
is also directed. Note also that
\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)=\{\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap G:G\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}

\bigcap_{F\in\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)}\uparrow\penalty\!F=\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!(\downarrow\penalty\!x\cap G):G\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}\subseteq\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!G:G\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}=\downarrow\penalty\!x

\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)

\bigcap_{F\in\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)}\uparrow\penalty\!F\supseteq\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!G:G\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}=\downarrow\penalty\!x
.
Hence,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{(bl)w}(x)\}=\uparrow\penalty\!x
.

Proof 3.4

Obviously,

Proposition 15

(2)

Proof 3.5

4
The wf topology and moderate meet continuity

In this section, we investigate the links between the quasiexactness and some topologies on posets.

In
[
14
]
, when the family
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in P\}
generates a topology on a poset
P
, then this topology is called the
weak way-below topology
(
wwb topology
, for short), denoted by
\tau_{wwb}(P)
. The topological space
(P,\tau_{wwb}(P))
is simply written as
(P,\tau_{wwb})
.

For the following result, we refer the reader to the Theorems 3.8 and 3.10 in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 18
.

[
14
]

Let
P
be an exact poset. Then
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in P\}
generates the wwb topology, which is finer than the Scott topology.

For any subset
F\subseteq P
, we write
\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F=\{a\in P:F\ll_{w}a\}
. Whenever
F=\{x\}
for some
x\in P
, we replace
\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}\{x\}
with
\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x
.

Lemma 19
.

If
P
is a quasiexact poset, then
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F:F\subseteq P,F~\emph{\mbox{is finite}}\}
generates a topology on
P
.

Proof 4.1
.

For any
a\in P
,
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(a)
is directed with
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(a)\}=\uparrow\penalty\!a
. Thus,
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(a)\neq\varnothing
. Choose arbitrarily
F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(a)
, then
a\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F
.

Let
F_{1},F_{2}
be finite sets in
P
with
b\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F_{1}\cap\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F_{2}
, i.e.,
F_{1}\ll_{w}b
and
F_{2}\ll_{w}b
. Note that
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(b)
is directed. There exists
F_{3}\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(b)
such that
F_{3}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}
. For any
e\in P
, if
F_{3}\ll_{w}e
, then
F_{1},F_{2}\ll_{w}e
, so
b\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F_{3}\subseteq\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F_{1}\cap\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F_{2}
.

Whenever the family
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F:F\subseteq P,F~\mbox{is finite}\}
generates a topology on
P
, we call it the
weak way-below finitely determined topology
(briefly,
wf topology
) on
P
, denoted by
\tau_{wf}(P)
. The topological space
(P,\tau_{wf}(P))
will be simply written as
(P,\tau_{wf})
.

Remark 20
.

(1)

For any quasiexact poset
P
, we have
\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F\subseteq\operatorname{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)
for every
F\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
.

(2)

If a poset
P
admits both the wwb topology and the wf topology, then
\tau_{wwb}(P)\subseteq\tau_{wf}(P)
.

By Lemma
18
and Remark
20
(2), we have the following result.

Lemma 21
.

If
P
is an exact poset, then
\sigma(P)\subseteq\tau_{wf}(P)
.

Note that every quasicontinuous dcpo admits the wf topology. However, the following example shows that quasicontinuous dcpos do not necessarily admit the wwb topology.

Example 22

Consider the set
P=(\mathbb{N}\times\{1,2\})\cup\{\top\}
with the order defined in Example
16
, which shows that
P
is quasicontinuous dcpo. It is trivial to check the following facts:

(i)

\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}(n,i)=\{(m,i):m\geq n,m\in\mathbb{N}\}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,2
;

(ii)

\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}\top=\varnothing
.

Note that the family
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in P\}
can not cover
\top
. Thus,
\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in P\}
can not generate the wwb topology.

Mushburn
[
14
]
constructed an example to show the wwb topology can be strictly finer than the Scott topology.

Proposition 23
.

A poset
P
is quasiexact if for any nonempty
H\subseteq P
and any
x\in P
,
H\ll_{w}x
implies there exists a finite
F\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!H
such that
F\ll_{w}x
.

Proof 4.2
.

It is trivial to verify that
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(\bot)
is directed with
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\ll_{w}\bot\}=\bot
whenever the bottom element
\bot
exists. Without loss of generality, assume that
x\neq\bot
. Note that
\uparrow\penalty\!x\subsetneqq P
. For any
y\notin\uparrow\penalty\!x
, we show
P\setminus\downarrow\penalty\!y\ll_{w}x
. Otherwise, there exists a directed set
D
with
\bigvee D=x
, but
D\cap(P\setminus\downarrow\penalty\!y)=\varnothing
since
P\setminus\downarrow\penalty\!y
is an upper set. Thus,
D\subseteq\downarrow\penalty\!y
. It follows that
x=\bigvee D\leq y
, a contradiction. By the hypothesis, there exists a finite set
F\subseteq P\setminus\downarrow\penalty\!y
such that
F\ll_{w}x
. This implies that
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\neq\varnothing
. Furthermore, note that
\uparrow\penalty\!F\subseteq P\setminus\downarrow\penalty\!y
. Then
\uparrow\penalty\!F\cap\downarrow\penalty\!y=\varnothing
. It follows that
y\notin\uparrow\penalty\!F
. Hence,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)\}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!x
. It remains to show that
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
is directed. For this, let
F_{i}\in\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
and
i=1,2
. We show that
\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}\ll_{w}x
. For any directed set
D
, if
\bigvee D=x
, then
D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{i}\neq\varnothing
, i.e, there exists
d_{i}\in D
and
e_{i}\in F_{i}
such that
d_{i}\geq e_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Choose
d_{3}\in D
such that
d_{3}\geq d_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
d_{3}\geq e_{i}
for
i=1,2
. It follows that
\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}\ll_{w}x
. Note that
\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}
is an upper set. By the hypothesis, there exists a finite set
F_{3}\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!F_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F_{2}
such that
F_{3}\ll_{w}x
. Therefore,
\operatorname{fin}_{w}(x)
is directed.

Proposition 24
.

Any poset that has no infinite antichain is quasiexact.

Proof 4.3
.

Assume that
P
is a poset with no infinite antichain. For any nonempty
H\subseteq P
and any
x\in P
, if
H\ll_{w}x
, then by Zorn’s lemma we can pick a maximal antichain
A
in
\uparrow\penalty\!H\setminus\uparrow\penalty\!x
. Note that
F=A\cup\{x\}
is finite, contained in
\uparrow\penalty\!H
. We show
F\ll_{w}x
. For this, let
D
be a directed set with
\bigvee D=x
. Since
H\ll_{w}x
, eventually,
D
is in
\uparrow\penalty\!H
. If
d=x
for some
d\in D
, then
d\in D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F
. Otherwise,
d\notin\uparrow\penalty\!x
for any
d\in D
. If there exists
d\in D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!H
such that
d\notin\uparrow\penalty\!A\cup\downarrow\penalty\!A
, then
d
is incomparable with each
a\in A
. Note that
d\notin\uparrow\penalty\!x
and that
A
is an antichain in
\uparrow\penalty\!H\setminus\uparrow\penalty\!x
. It follows that
A\cup\{d\}
is also an antichain in
\uparrow\penalty\!H\setminus\uparrow\penalty\!x
, contradicting the maximality of
A
. So
D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!H\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!A\cup\downarrow\penalty\!A
. It cannot be the case
D\subseteq\downarrow\penalty\!A
since
\bigvee D\geq x
. Note that every
d
large enough in
D
is in
\uparrow\penalty\!H
, hence in
\uparrow\penalty\!A\cup\downarrow\penalty\!A
, but not all of them in
\downarrow\penalty\!A
, and those that are not must be in
\uparrow\penalty\!A
. Thus,
D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!F\supseteq D\cap\uparrow\penalty\!A\neq\varnothing
. Applying Proposition
23
, we conclude that
P
is quasiexact.

Following the characterization of meet continuous posets by means of Scott topology, we define the following meet continuity.

Definition 25

A poset
P
admitting the wf topology is said to be
moderately meet continuous
if for any
x\in P
and directed subset
D
,
x\leq\bigvee D
implies that
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{wf}}(\downarrow\penalty\!D\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x)
.

Lemma 26
.

Let
P
be a moderately meet continuous poset. Then
\operatorname{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)\subseteq\bigcup\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in F\}
for any
F\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
.

Proof 4.4
.

For convenience, we write
\operatorname{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)=U
and let
F=\{x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}
. Assume
x\in U
, but
y\notin\bigcup\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in F\}
, that is,
x_{i}\ll_{w}y
does not hold for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. Thus, there exists a directed subset
D_{i}\subseteq P
such that
\bigvee D_{i}=y
, but
x_{i}\notin\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i}
. By the moderately meet continuity,
y\in\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{wf}}(\downarrow\penalty\!D_{1}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!y)
. Since
y\in U
and
U\in\tau_{wf}(P)
, then
U\cap(\downarrow\penalty\!D_{1}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!y)\neq\varnothing
. Pick
z_{1}\in U\cap\downarrow\penalty\!D_{1}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!y
. Note that
z_{1}\leq y=\bigvee D_{2}
. By the moderately meet continuity again, we have
z_{1}\in\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{wf}}(\downarrow\penalty\!D_{2}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!z_{1})
, whence
U\cap\downarrow\penalty\!D_{2}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!z_{1}\neq\varnothing
. Pick
z_{2}\in U\cap\downarrow\penalty\!D_{2}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!z_{1}
. The rest can be done in the same manner. In other words, we can pick
z_{i}\in U\cap\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i}\cap\downarrow\penalty\!z_{i-1}
for any
i
(
1\leq i\leq n
). Note that
z_{i}\leq z_{i-1}
and
z_{i-1}\in\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i-1}
for all
i
. It follows that
z_{n}\in\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i}
for all
i
. Thus,
z_{n}\in\bigcap^{n}_{i=1}\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i}
. Also note that
z_{n}\in U=\mbox{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!F
. Hence, there exists
i_{0}
(
1\leq i_{0}\leq n
) such that
x_{i_{0}}\leq z_{n}\in\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i_{0}}
, showing that
x_{i_{0}}\in\downarrow\penalty\!D_{i_{0}}
, a contradiction. Therefore,
\operatorname{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)\subseteq\bigcup\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in F\}
.

Applying Lemmas
4
(3),
26
and Remark
20
, we derive the following conclusion.

Corollary 27
.

Let
P
be a moderately meet continuous quasiexact poset. Then

\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}F=\bigcup\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in F\}=\operatorname{int}_{\tau_{wf}}(\uparrow\penalty\!F)

for any
F\in\mathcal{P}^{*}(P)
.

By Remark
20
(2) and Corollary
27
, we deduce the following result strengthening Mushburn’s result in
[
14
, Theorem 10]
.

Theorem 28
.

If
P
is a moderately meet continuous quasiexact poset, then
\sigma(P)\subseteq\tau_{wwb}(P)=\tau_{wf}(P)
.

Theorem 29
.

Every moderately meet continuous quasiexact poset is exact and meet continuous.

Proof 4.5
.

Let
P
be a moderately meet continuous quasiexact. To show that
P
is exact, we consider any
a\in P
.

Claim 1.

\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a
is directed.

Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a
. Then
\{y_{1}\},\{y_{2}\}\in\operatorname{fin}_{bl(w)}(a)
. By the hypothesis, there exists
F\in\operatorname{fin}_{bl(w)}(a)
such that
F\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!y_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!y_{2}
. By Corollary
27
, we have
a\in\bigcup\{\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadrightarrow$}}_{w}x:x\in F\}
. So there exists
x\in F
such that
x\ll_{w}a
. Note that
F\subseteq\uparrow\penalty\!y_{1}\cap\uparrow\penalty\!y_{2}
, then
x\geq y_{1}
and
x\geq y_{2}
.

Claim 2.

\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a\}=\uparrow\penalty\!a
.

Obviously,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a\}\supseteq\uparrow\penalty\!a
. For any
F\in\operatorname{fin}_{bl(w)}(a)
, by Corollary
27
, we can pick
a_{F}\in F
such that
a_{F}\ll_{w}a
. Then
\{\uparrow\penalty\!y_{F}:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{bl(w)}(x)\}\subseteq\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}x\}
. It follows that
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}x\}\subseteq\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y_{F}:F\in\operatorname{fin}_{bl(w)}(x)\}\subseteq\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!F:F\in\operatorname{fin}^{w}_{bl}(x)\}=\uparrow\penalty\!x
. Hence,
\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a\}=\uparrow\penalty\!a
. Note that
\uparrow\penalty\!(\bigvee\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a)=\bigcap\{\uparrow\penalty\!y:y\in\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a\}
. So
\bigvee\mathord{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\twoheadleftarrow$}}_{w}a=a
.

By Proposition
2
, we conclude that
P
is exact.

For any
x\in P
and directed set
D\subseteq P
, if
x\in\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{wf}}(\downarrow\penalty\!D\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x)
, then by Lemma
21
and the definition of closure, we have
\operatorname{cl}_{\tau_{wf}}(\downarrow\penalty\!D\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x)\subseteq\operatorname{cl}_{\sigma}(\downarrow\penalty\!D\cap\downarrow\penalty\!x)
. Therefore,
P
is meet continuous.

Shen et al.
[
16
]
showed that every meet continuous weak domain is a domain. By Proposition
29
, we conclude the following result.

Corollary 30
.

A poset
P
is a domain if
P
is a moderately meet continuous quasiexact dcpo with the relation
\ll_{w}
weakly increasing.

In this paper, we explored the quasiexact posets, parallel to the quasicontinuous posets. A new topology, the wf topology on posets is introduced and used to define the moderately meet continuous posets. Although several results on such structures have been obtained, we still have basic problems to solve as illustrated below.

Problem 1.
What is the property
p
such that a poset
P
is exact if and only if it is quasiexact and has property
p
?

It is known that a poset
P
is continuous if and only if it is quasicontinuous and meet continuous
[
17
]
. However, we still do not have a similar result for exact posets. It is only proved that every exact poset is quasiexact.

Problem 2.
Under what conditions, a quasiexact dcpo is quasicontinuous?

At the moment we just know that every quasicontinuous dcpo is quasiexact. It would be ideal if we could find a property
q
such that a dcpo is quasicontinuous if and only if it is quasiexact and has property
q
.

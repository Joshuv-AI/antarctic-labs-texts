1.
Introduction

The interplay between the existence of canonical metrics and stability conditions arising from algebraic geometry has become a central theme in modern complex geometry. The most well-known and classical such result is the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, proved by Donaldson and Uhlenbeck–Yau
[
Don85
,
UY86
]
, which states that a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold admits a Hermite–Einstein metric if and only if the bundle is slope polystable.

Inspired by the recent advent of weighted cscK metrics and weighted K-stability
[
Lah19
,
Ino22
,
Ino20
]
we introduce a new weighted analogue of the Hermite–Einstein equation. The new equation encompasses many known examples of canonical metrics on vector bundles, including the usual Hermite–Einstein metrics, Kähler–Ricci solitons, and transversally Hermite–Einstein metrics on vector bundles over Sasaki manifolds studied by Biswas–Schumacher
[
BS10
]
and Baraglia–Hekmati
[
BH22
]
. Following
[
AJL23
, Section 5]
, we also show that the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation with polynomial weights reduces to the Hermite–Einstein equation over a semi-simple fibration, distinct from the original manifold.

To briefly introduce the equation, let
(E,h)
be a holomorphic vector bundle with Hermitian metric over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
. We assume a compact real torus
\mathbb{T}
acts on this setup, so
\mathbb{T}
acts by holomorphic isometries on both
(E,h)
and
(X,\omega)
, and the map
E\to X
is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant. Under these conditions, a moment map
\Phi_{h}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))
for the curvature
F_{h}\in\Omega^{2}(\mathrm{End}(E))
of
(E,h)
exists. We further assume the
\mathbb{T}
-action on
(X,\omega)
is Hamiltonian, and let
\mu:X\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be a fixed moment map with moment polytope
P:=\mu(X)
. Given a smooth
weight function

v:P\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we define the
v
-weighted contraction
of
F_{h}+\Phi_{h}
to be

\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h}):=v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}+\langle\Phi_{h},dv(\mu)\rangle,

where
dv(p)
is considered as an element of
\mathfrak{t}^{**}\cong\mathfrak{t}
for
p\in P
, and
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the canonical pairing
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbb{R}
extended to
\Gamma(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))\otimes\mathfrak{t}\to\Gamma(\mathrm{End}(E))
. The
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein equation
is then

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E},

where
c_{v}
is a suitable equivariant cohomological constant. For more details, see Section
2
.

In this work, we give a thorough study of these metrics. Alongside the equation, we introduce notions of weighted slope (semi/poly)stability, and prove the following weighted form of the Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence:

Theorem 1.1
.

A vector bundle admits a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric if and only if it is weighted slope polystable.

The proof that weighted slope polystability implies existence follows the approach of Uhlenbeck–Yau
[
UY86
]
, namely a continuity method. Analytically there is little change in the proof. However, we highlight here the geometric importance of Lemma
5.5
, which states that one may compute the weighted slope of an equivariant subsheaf by differential geometric means, provided the singular locus of the sheaf has codimension at least 2. The proof of this lemma exemplifies the equivariant approach we take, and is an indispensable ingredient in the proof of the correspondence.

A key technical tool that we develop and use extensively throughout the paper is a new extension of Inoue’s equivariant intersection theory
[
Ino20
]
to arbitrary weight functions on the moment polytope
P
. These intersection numbers, defined via Fourier transforms, provide a powerful and convenient framework for expressing the complicated equivariant cohomological quantities that appear frequently in the weighted theory. The simplest examples of such intersection numbers are

(v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))=\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]},\quad\quad(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))=\int_{X}\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})\omega^{[n]},

where
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}:=[\omega+\mu]
denotes the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Kähler class, and
\omega^{[n]}:=\omega^{n}/n!
. Indeed, the second number here provides a suitable notion of
v
-weighted degree of
E
, allowing us to define weighted slope stability. While the notion of applying
v
to
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}
might seem perverse at first, the notation is perfectly natural from the viewpoint of our Fourier transform definition.

As a further application of the weighted intersection numbers, we state and prove a weighted version of the Kobayashi–Lübke inequality for vector bundles admitting weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics, providing an equivariant cohomological obstruction to the existence of such metrics.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
E
admits a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric, and that
v
satisfies the inequality

(1)

\mathrm{Hess}(v)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv\otimes dv}{v}\leq 0,

where
n:=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}X
. Then

(\mathrm{rk}(E)-1)(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))\leq 2\,\mathrm{rk}(E)(c_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})).

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
E
is projectively flat.

Note the unexpected emergence of inequality (
1
). While we show that (
1
) is satisfied for all weight functions encompassing the examples listed above, the condition remains somewhat mysterious to us.

Alongside these results, we show that the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation has a natural interpretation as a moment map on the space of connections, a result which in the unweighted setting goes back to Atiyah–Bott
[
AB83
]
. The approach taken is that of Dervan–Hallam
[
DH23
]
, namely by integrating equivariant forms over the fibres of a universal family. The argument requires an extension of Inoue’s equivariant intersection 2-forms
[
Ino20
]
to the case of arbitrary weight functions on the moment polytope.

We prove the following uniqueness result.

Theorem 1.3
.

A weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on a simple bundle is unique up to scaling.

The proof by now is very standard—we introduce a weighted version of Donaldson’s functional
[
Don85
]
, which is the “log-norm functional” in this context, and show that it is convex along geodesic rays in the space of metrics. Since critical points of this functional are precisely weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics, uniqueness is immediate.

As an application of our techniques, we adapt certain results of Tian
[
Tia92
]
in the setting of Kähler–Einstein metrics to the setting of Kähler–Ricci solitons. In particular, given a Fano manifold
(X,\omega)
with
\mathbb{T}
-action and
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
, we define a certain invariant
\beta_{\xi}(X)\leq 1
in terms of extensions of
TX^{1,0}
, and prove:

Theorem 1.4
.

There is no
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Kähler metric in
c_{1}(X)
satisfying
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)-\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\mu^{\xi}\geq t\omega
and
t>\beta_{\xi}(X)
.

In particular, if
\beta_{\xi}(X)<1
, a Kähler–Ricci soliton with vector field
\xi
does not exist. The proof, similarly to Tian’s considers extensions of the holomorphic tangent bundle of
X
. While we cannot prove the existence of an exponentially weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on such extensions, the existence of a weaker kind of canonical metric is sufficient for our needs.

In the course of the paper, we introduce two tangentially related notions: weighted vortices, and weighted Gieseker stability. The
v
-solitons of Han–Li
[
HL23
]
are shown to induce weighted vortices, and weighted Gieseker stability is related to weighted slope stability. It is clear from the evidence presented here, along with the already existing weighted cscK theory, that these notions form part of an entire “weighted ecosystem” of canonical metrics and stability conditions. We claim that the framework developed here, in particular the extension of Inoue’s intersection theory to arbitrary weight functions, provides the correct tool for further analysis in this field.

2.
The weighted Hermite–Einstein equation

In this section, we define the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation, after first giving the necessary background on vector bundles, connections, and vector bundle valued moment maps.

2.1.
Background

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
of dimension
n
. We recall that for any choice of Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, there is a canonically induced connection
\nabla^{h}
on
E
, called the
Chern connection
—it is the unique Hermitian connection whose
(0,1)
-part is the del-bar operator of
E
.

Suppose we choose a local holomorphic frame for
E
, and identify
h
with a smoothly varying Hermitian matrix. Then the curvature of the Chern connection in this frame is computed as

F_{h}=\bar{\partial}(h^{-1}\partial h),

where the
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
operators are those on
X
.

The metric
h
is called a
Hermite–Einstein metric
if

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}=\lambda\,\mathrm{Id}_{E},

where
\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}:=\frac{nF_{h}\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}}
is the contraction of
F_{h}
with
\omega
, and
\lambda:=\frac{n\deg(E,\omega)}{\mathrm{rk}(E)\mathrm{Vol}(X,\omega)}
is the Einstein constant.

Suppose now that a compact torus
\mathbb{T}
acts on
(X,\omega)
by Hamiltonian isometries. In particular, the action is holomorphic and there exists a moment map
\mu:X\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
for the action. Suppose we are given a linear lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
E
by biholomorphisms, and that
h
is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric on
E
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
moment map
for the curvature
F_{h}
of a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric
h
, is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant section
\Phi_{h}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))
such that

F_{h}(-,\xi)={\nabla^{h}}\langle\Phi_{h},\xi\rangle

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
. Here on the left-hand side we identify
\xi
with the vector field it generates on
X
. On the right-hand side,
{\nabla^{h}}
denotes the canonical extension of the Chern connection to
\mathrm{End}(E)
, and
\langle-,-\rangle
denotes the dual pairing
\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbb{R}
.

When
E=L
is an ample line bundle with Hermitian metric
h
whose curvature is a Kähler form
\omega
, a familiar fact is that a choice of lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
L
canonically induces a moment map for
\omega
. In the vector bundle setting we have a similar fact, which requires the notion of the infinitesimal action of
\mathbb{T}
on sections of
E
.

First, note that a lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
E
induces an action on sections of
E
by

(t\cdot s)|_{x}:=t(s|_{t^{-1}x}),

where
t\in\mathbb{T}
,
s\in\Gamma(E)
and
x\in X
. This action then induces an infinitesimal action on sections, defined by

(2)

\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}(s):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}(\exp(-t\xi)\cdot s)

for all
s\in\Gamma(E)
. It is straightforward to see that

(3)

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}(fs)=\xi(f)s+f\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}(s)

for all
f\in C^{\infty}(X)
and
s\in\Gamma(E)
. Given this definition, we have:

Proposition 2.2

(
[
BGV04
, Definition 7.5]
)
.

Given a lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
(E,h)
, there exists a canonically defined moment map
\Phi_{h}
for the curvature
F_{h}
of the Chern connection, defined by

\langle\Phi_{h},\xi\rangle:=\nabla^{h}_{\xi}-\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}

for each
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
.

The fact that
\Phi_{h}
is a well-defined endomorphism can be observed from (
3
), and that
\Phi_{h}
satisfies the moment map property is a consequence of the equivariant Bianchi identity
[
BGV04
, Proposition 7.4]
. We can also easily see that the endomorphism
\Phi_{h}
is skew-adjoint: for
s,s^{\prime}\in\Gamma(E)
we have

(4)

\begin{split}h(\nabla^{h}_{\xi}s,s^{\prime})+h(s,\nabla^{h}_{\xi}s^{\prime})=&\mathcal{L}_{\xi}(h(s,s^{\prime}))\\
=&(\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}h)(s,s^{\prime})+h(\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}s,s^{\prime})+h(s,\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}s^{\prime})\\
=&h(\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}s,s^{\prime})+h(s,\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}s^{\prime}),\end{split}

where in the final line we use that
h
is
\mathbb{T}
-invariant, so
\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}h=0
. Rearranging the above and using the definition of
\Phi_{h}
yields

h(\Phi_{h}^{\xi}(s),s^{\prime})=-h(s,\Phi_{h}^{\xi}(s^{\prime})).

Thus, given a lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
(E,h)
, we will always take
\Phi_{h}
to be the canonically induced moment map for
F_{h}
from Proposition
2.2
. Note that if we change Hermitian metrics from
h
to
h^{\prime}
, this formula tells us how the canonically induced moment maps change. Indeed, let
\theta:=\nabla^{h^{\prime}}-\nabla^{h}
be the
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued
(1,0)
-form on
X
describing the change of connections. Then

(5)

\langle\Phi_{h^{\prime}},\xi\rangle-\langle\Phi_{h},\xi\rangle=\theta(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
; we will sometimes abuse notation by writing this relations as

\Phi_{h^{\prime}}-\Phi_{h}=\theta.

It will be convenient to introduce the equivariant
\bar{\partial}
-operator
\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}
of
\mathrm{End}(E)
, which takes
\chi\in\Omega^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(E))^{\mathbb{T}}
and maps it to the equivariant
\mathrm{End}(E)
-valued
(1,1)
-form defined by

\langle\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\chi,\xi\rangle:=\bar{\partial}\chi+\chi(\xi),

where
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
and on the right-hand side we conflate
\xi
with the vector field it generates on
X
. If
\theta:=\nabla^{h^{\prime}}-\nabla^{h}
, the relationship between equivariant curvatures may be then expressed succinctly as

(6)

F_{h^{\prime}}+\Phi_{h^{\prime}}=F_{h}+\Phi_{h}+\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\theta.

Remark 2.3
.

In the case where
E=L
is a line bundle,
\frac{i}{2\pi}F_{h}=\omega
is a Kähler metric, and
h^{\prime}=e^{-\varphi}h
for some
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Kähler potential
\varphi
, equation (
5
) reduces to the familiar formula for the change in moment maps

\mu_{\varphi}-\mu=d^{c}\varphi;

see
[
Lah19
, Lemma 1]
.

2.2.
Definition of the equation

As in the previous section, let
(E,h)
be a holomorphic Hermitian vector bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
, with curvature
F_{h}
. We assume that a compact torus
\mathbb{T}
acts on
(X,\omega)
by Hamiltonian isometries, and fix a moment map
\mu:X\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
for the action. We further assume a fixed linear lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to
E
such that
\mathbb{T}
acts on
E
by biholomorphisms preserving
h
. We write
\Phi_{h}\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{t}^{*}\otimes\mathrm{End}(E))
for the canonically induced moment map for
F_{h}
.

Denote by
P:=\mu(X)
the moment polytope of
\mu
, and let
v,w:P\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
be positive smooth functions.

Definition 2.4
.

The
v
-weighted contraction
of
F_{h}+\Phi_{h}
is defined by

\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h}):=v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}+\langle\Phi_{h},dv(\mu)\rangle,

where
dv:T^{*}P\to\mathbb{R}
is considered as a map
P\to\mathfrak{t}\cong\mathfrak{t}^{**}
.

Definition 2.5
.

A
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
h
on
E
is called a
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric
if it satisfies the equation

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})=c_{v,w}w(\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{E},

for a real constant
c_{v,w}
.

When the weight functions are understood or irrelevant, we shall refer to such a metric as a
weighted Hermite–Einstein metric
. The constant
c_{v,w}
will be shown in Lemma
4.4
to depend only on the equivariant cohomology of the manifold and the vector bundle.

This definition is modelled on the weighted cscK metrics of
[
Lah19
]
. However, we will now show that for the purposes of solving the equation, the second weight function
w
is superfluous.

Lemma 2.6
.

A
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric exists on
E
if and only if a
(v,1)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric exists.

A
(v,1)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein satisfies the equation

(7)

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E},

and we shall refer to such a metric simply as a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric
.

Proof.

Let
h
be a Hermitian metric on
E
. Given a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant smooth function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
, let
h^{\prime}:=e^{f}h
. The Chern connections of these metrics satisfy

\nabla^{h^{\prime}}-\nabla^{h}=\partial f\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{e}.

Hence by (
6
), the equivariant curvatures change according to

F_{h^{\prime}}+\Phi_{h^{\prime}}=F_{h}+\Phi_{h}+\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial f\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E},

using that
\bar{\partial}(\mathrm{Id}_{E})=0
; here
\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}
is the equivariant del-bar operator on
\Omega^{1,0}(X)^{\mathbb{T}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h^{\prime}}+\Phi_{h^{\prime}}):=

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})+i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial f)\mathrm{Id}_{E}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})+\Delta_{v}f\cdot\mathrm{Id}_{E},

where

\Delta_{v}f:=i\Lambda_{\omega,v}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial f=i\Lambda_{\omega}\bar{\partial}(v(\mu)\partial f)=\partial^{*}(v(\mu)\partial f)=v(\mu)\Delta(v)+i\langle\partial f,dv(\mu)\rangle

is the weighted Laplacian; in the final equality
\Delta f:=\partial^{*}\partial f
. Thus, if we can solve

(8)

\Delta_{v}f=2\pi c_{v,w}w(\mu)-2\pi c_{v},

then
h^{\prime}
is a
(v,1)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric if and only if
h
is a
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric. The operator
\Delta_{v}
is self-adjoint, elliptic, and has kernel the constant functions, so we can solve the equation
\Delta_{v}f=g
for
f
if and only if the integral of
g
vanishes. The right-hand side of (
8
) has vanishing integral by the definitions of
c_{v,w}
and
c_{v}
, so we are done.
∎

3.
Examples

In this section we give several examples of weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics. We also show in Corollary
3.1
that line bundles admit weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics for arbitrary weight functions, and in Proposition
3.6
that the weight function of a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric may be deformed to produce new weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics.

3.1.
The Hermite–Einstein equation

Choosing
v=w=1
, we recover the usual Hermite–Einstein equation. Note that by Donaldson’s uniqueness of Hermite–Einstein metrics
[
Don85
]
, given a holomorphic lift of the
\mathbb{T}
-action to a simple bundle
E
, any Hermite–Einstein metric must be
\mathbb{T}
-invariant, otherwise we could generate new examples by pulling back the metric.
1
1

1

Uniqueness is only up to constant factors, but one may easily check using compactness of
\mathbb{T}
that pulling back by
\mathbb{T}
does not rescale the metric.
Thus, we do not lose anything by restricting to
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metrics.

3.2.
Weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics on line bundles

The following is a simple corollary of the proof of Lemma
2.6
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
L
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic line bundle. Then for any choice of weight function
v
,
L
admits a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric.

Proof.

For any
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric
h
on
L
,

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})=g\,\mathrm{Id}_{L}

for some
\mathbb{T}
-invariant smooth function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
. Let
f
solve the equation
\Delta_{v}f=-g+c_{v}
, where
c_{v}
is the appropriate constant. Then
h^{\prime}:=e^{f}h
satisfies

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h^{\prime}}+\Phi_{h^{\prime}})=g\mathrm{Id}_{L}-g\mathrm{Id}_{L}+c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{L}=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{L}.\qed

3.3.
The exponentially weighted Hermite–Einstein equation and Kähler–Ricci solitons

Let
(X,\omega,\xi)
be a compact Kähler–Ricci soliton:

(9)

{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\langle\mu,\xi\rangle,

where
\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\in c_{1}(X)
, and let
D
be the corresponding Chern connection on the holomorphic tangent bundle
TX^{1,0}
. Using the Hermitian metric
g
, we may transpose the
(0,1)
-form part of the equation (
9
) to a
(1,0)
-vector field to get

\frac{-1}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}R_{\omega}-i\,\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\Phi_{\omega},\xi\rangle,

where
R_{\omega}
is the full curvature tensor of
(X,\omega)
and
\Phi_{\omega}:=g^{-1}i\partial\bar{\partial}\mu
is a moment map for
R_{\omega}
; see
[
DH23
, Proposition 4.1]
.
2
2

2

In
[
DH23
]
the moment map is written as
g^{-1}i\bar{\partial}\partial\mu
, where
\bar{\partial}
is the del-bar operator of the holomorphic vector bundle
T^{*}X^{1,0}
. Here, we instead work with the del and del-bar operators on forms, and
define

g^{-1}(dz^{j}\wedge d\bar{z}^{k}):=g^{\bar{k}\ell}dz^{j}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}
. With this convention, the endomorphism
g^{-1}i\partial\bar{\partial}\mu
used here agrees with that of
[
DH23
]
; in local coordinates they are both written
ig^{\bar{k}\ell}\partial_{j}\partial_{\bar{k}}\mu\,dz^{j}\otimes\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\ell}}
.
Multiplying by
ie^{\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}
, we see

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,e^{\langle-,\xi\rangle}}(R_{\omega}+\Phi_{\omega})=e^{\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}},

hence a Kähler–Ricci soliton is an exponentially weighted Hermite–Einstein metric with weight functions
v(\mu)=w(\mu)=e^{\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}
. Notice that
\Phi_{\omega}
is
canonically normalized
in the sense of
[
BGV04
]
:

\langle\Phi_{\omega},\xi\rangle=g^{-1}i\partial\bar{\partial}\langle\mu,\xi\rangle=D\xi=-\mathcal{L}^{TX^{1,0}}_{\xi}+D_{\xi},

where
D
is the Chern connection of
g
and
\mathcal{L}^{TX^{1,0}}_{\xi}(V):=[\xi,V]=D_{\xi}V-D_{V}\xi
is the naturally induced infinitesimal action. The second equality follows from the straightforward identity

D\left(i(\bar{\partial}-\partial)\langle\mu,\xi\rangle\right)=i\partial\bar{\partial}\langle\mu,\xi\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}g=i\partial\bar{\partial}\langle\mu,\xi\rangle,

together with
g^{-1}\left(i(\bar{\partial}-\partial)\langle\mu,\xi\rangle\right)=\xi
and the fact that
g^{-1}
commutes with
D
.

3.4.
v
-solitons and weighted vortices

Recall the
v
-soliton equation of Han–Li
[
HL23
]
defined by

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\log v(\mu).

A natural question is whether a
v
-soliton on
TX^{1,0}
induces a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on
TX^{1,0}
. While we cannot ascertain the existence of a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on
X
in this more general setting, we instead show that the existence of a
v
-soliton implies a suitably related “weighted vortex” exists on
TX^{1,0}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a
v
-soliton
{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\omega=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\log v(\mu)
with weight function
v\in C^{\infty}(P,\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. Then the induced Hermitian metric
h_{\omega}:=\frac{1}{2}(g-\sqrt{-1}\omega)
on
TX^{1,0}
solves a
v
-weighted vortex equation

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}^{TX^{1,0}}\varphi=0\\
i(\Lambda_{\omega}R_{\omega}+\langle\Phi_{\omega},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle)-\left\langle\varphi^{\star}\otimes\varphi,\mathrm{Hess}(\log v)(\mu)\right\rangle=2\pi\,\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}.\end{cases}

where
\varphi\in\Gamma(TX^{1,0})\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
is given by
\varphi(\xi):=\xi^{(1,0)}
, and
\varphi^{\star}\in\Gamma(T^{*}X^{1,0})\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
is defined by
\varphi^{\star}(\xi):=h_{\omega}(-,\xi^{1,0})
.

Proof.

Let
(\xi_{a})_{a=1,\cdots,\ell}
be a basis of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
generators of
{\mathfrak{t}}
. Expanding the
v
-soliton equation gives

\begin{split}{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\omega=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\frac{v_{,a}(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\partial\bar{\partial}\mu^{\xi_{a}}+\sum_{a,b=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{v_{,b}(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\right)_{,a}\partial\mu^{\xi_{a}}\wedge\bar{\partial}\mu^{\xi_{b}}\right)\\
=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\langle\partial\bar{\partial}\mu,d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle+\left\langle\partial\mu\wedge\bar{\partial}\mu,\mathrm{Hess}(\log v)(\mu)\right\rangle\right)\end{split}

Hence the Hermitian metric on
TX^{1,0}
induced by the
v
-soliton
(g,\omega)
satisfies the equation

(10)

-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega}R_{\omega}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle\Phi_{\omega},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle-i\,\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\left\langle\Upsilon,\mathrm{Hess}(\log v)(\mu)\right\rangle

where
\Upsilon_{ab}
is the endomorphism of
TX^{1,0}
given by,

\Upsilon_{ab}=g^{-1}(\partial\mu^{\xi_{a}}\wedge\bar{\partial}\mu^{\xi_{b}}).

With the conventions of Section
3.3
, one readily computes

\Upsilon_{ab}=h_{\omega}(-,\xi_{a}^{1,0})\otimes\xi_{b}^{1,0}=\varphi^{\star}(\xi_{a})\otimes\varphi(\xi_{b}).

Hence, the Hermitian metric
h_{\omega}
corresponding to the weighted soliton
(g,\omega)
satisfies the following equation

i(\Lambda_{\omega}R_{\omega}+\langle\Phi_{\omega},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle)-\left\langle\varphi^{\star}\otimes\varphi,\mathrm{Hess}(\log v)(\mu)\right\rangle=2\pi\,\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}.

Lastly, we have
\bar{\partial}^{TX^{1,0}}\varphi=0
as
\mathbb{T}
acts holomorphically on
X
.
∎

More generally, for a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle
E
on a Kähler
\mathbb{T}
-manifold
(X,\omega)
with weight functions
v,w\in C^{\infty}(P,\mathbb{R}_{>0})
, we say that a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
h
on
E
together with a section
\varphi\in\Gamma(E)^{\mathbb{T}}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
form a
(v,w)
-weighted vortex if the pair
(h,\varphi)
solves the equation

(11)

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}^{E}\varphi=0,\\
i\frac{\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})}{v(\mu)}-\left\langle\varphi^{\star}\otimes\varphi,\mathrm{Hess}(\log w)(\mu)\right\rangle=\tau\mathrm{Id}_{E}\end{cases}

for some
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\varphi^{\star}\otimes\varphi
is the
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}\otimes{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
-valued section of
\mathrm{End}(E)
given by

(12)

(\varphi^{\star}\otimes\varphi)(s):=h(s,\varphi)\varphi,

for all
s\in E
. The equation (
11
) is a weighted version of the vortex equation of Bradlow
[
Bra91
]
. A Kobayashi–Hitchin–Bradlow type correspondence for the weighted vortex equation with applications to the weighted Ricci solitons will appear in a subsequent paper
[
HL
]
.

3.5.
Transverse Hermite–Einstein metrics on Sasaki manifolds

In this section, we show that for a certain choice of weight functions, one recovers a special case of the transverse Hermite–Einstein metrics studied by Biswas–Schumacher in the Sasaki setting
[
BS10
]
, and Baraglia–Hekmati on more general foliated manifolds
[
BH22
]
. The result is analogous to the correspondence between weighted cscK metrics with weight functions
v(\mu)=(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle+a)^{-n-1}
,
w(\mu)=(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle+a)^{-n-3}
and constant scalar curvature Sasaki metrics explored in
[
AC21
,
ACL21
,
AJL23
,
Lah19
]
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold whose metric
\omega
is
i
times the curvature of a Hermitian metric on an ample line bundle
L\to X
. Denote by
S
the unit circle bundle of
L^{*}
, which is a principal
S^{1}
-bundle on
X
. The Chern connection on
L
induces a principal bundle connection
A^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(S,i\mathbb{R})
on
S
, whose curvature is
i\omega
; we define
A:=\frac{1}{i}A^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}(S,\mathbb{R})
. Denote by
u
the fundamental vector field on
S
generated by the
S^{1}
-action. Then
A(u)=1
, and
u
is invariant under the
S^{1}
-action. We write
D:=\ker A
for the horizontal subspace of
TS
.

Let
E\to X
be a holomorphic vector bundle with Hermitian metric
h
. We write
P\to X
for the associated principal
U(r)
-frame bundle. The Chern connection on
E
induces a connection
\theta
on
P
, which is a
\mathfrak{u}(r)
-valued 1-form on
P
satisfying:

(1)

g^{*}\theta=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})\theta
for all
g\in U(r)
;

(2)

\theta(a)=a
for all
a\in\mathfrak{u}(r)
, where on the left-hand side
a
is identified with the fundamental vector field it generates on
P
.

Now let
\xi
be a holomorphic Killing field on
M
admitting a Hamiltonian function
f
, which we take to be strictly positive. Following
[
AC21
]
, we twist the contact structure on
S
as follows. On
S
, consider the contact form
A_{\xi}:=\frac{1}{f}A
, and vector field

u_{\xi}=fu+\xi_{D},

where
\xi_{D}
denotes the horizontal lift of
\xi
to
S
. Since
dA_{\xi}=-\frac{1}{f^{2}}df\wedge A+\frac{1}{f}dA
, these satisfy the following properties

(1)

A_{\xi}(u_{\xi})=1
;

(2)

dA_{\xi}(u_{\xi},-)=0
;

(3)

the restriction of
dA_{\xi}
to
D\subset TS
coincides with
\frac{1}{f}\pi^{*}\omega|_{D}
.

We assume that
\xi
has an
F_{h}
-Hamiltonian, i.e. a section
\Phi_{h}^{\xi}
of
\mathrm{End}(E)
satisfying

\iota_{\xi}F_{h}=-\nabla^{h}\Phi_{h}^{\xi},

where
\nabla^{h}
is the Chern connection of
(E,h)
extended to
\mathrm{End}(E)
and
F_{h}
is the curvature of
\nabla^{h}
. In terms of the principal bundle
P
, the section
\Phi_{h}^{\xi}
may be considered as a smooth map
\Phi:P\to\mathfrak{u}(r)
satisfying

g^{*}\Phi=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})\Phi

for all
g\in U(r)
. The moment map condition may then be written on the principal bundle as

\iota_{\bar{\xi}}(d\theta)=-d_{\mathcal{H}}\Phi,

where
d_{\mathcal{H}}\Phi
denotes the restriction of
d\Phi
to the horizontal subspace
\mathcal{H}:=\ker\theta
, and
\bar{\xi}\in\mathcal{H}
denotes the horizontal lift of
\xi
from
X
to
P
.

Now we further pull back the bundles
E
and
P
to
S
; we shall denote these pullbacks via the symbols
\tilde{E}
and
\tilde{P}
respectively. Similarly, the pullback of the connection
\theta
is denoted by
\tilde{\theta}
, etc. Our aim is to modify
\tilde{\theta}
to
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
, so that
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is basic with respect to the horizontal lift of
u_{\xi}
to
\tilde{P}
with respect to
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
. To modify the connection form, we may add to it an arbitrary Ad-invariant horizontal 1-form on
\tilde{P}
with values in
\mathfrak{u}(r)
. We thus define

\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}:=\tilde{\theta}-A_{\xi}\tilde{\Phi},

where
A_{\xi}
is pulled back from
S
. This is a connection on
\tilde{P}
; denote by
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
its horizontal subspace, and let
\bar{u}_{\xi}\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
) be the horizontal lift of
u_{\xi}
to
\tilde{P}
with respect to
\tilde{\theta}
(resp.
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
).

Lemma 3.3
.

The form
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is basic with respect to
\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let us compute the horizontal subspace
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
determined by
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
. The tangent space to
S
decomposes as
TS=D\oplus(\mathbb{R}\cdot u_{\xi})
, where
D=\ker A=\ker A_{\xi}
. Denote by
\bar{D}\subset T\tilde{P}
the horizontal lift of
D
to
\tilde{P}
with respect to the connection
\tilde{\theta}
. Note this is still horizontal for the connection
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
, since
A_{\xi}
vanishes on
D
. Hence the horizontal subspaces
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
differ only in the direction of
u_{\xi}
. Since
\bar{u}_{\xi}
and
\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}
are both horizontal lifts of
u_{\xi}
,

\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}=\bar{u}_{\xi}+v,

for some vertical vector field
v
on
\tilde{P}
. Computing,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}(\bar{u}_{\xi})+\tilde{\theta}(v)-A_{\xi}(\bar{u}_{\xi})\tilde{\Phi}-A_{\xi}(v)\tilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\theta}(v)-\tilde{\Phi}.

Now,
\Phi:P\to\mathfrak{u}(r)
generates a tautological vertical vector field
v_{\Phi}
on
P
defined by letting
v_{\Phi}(p)
be the vertical tangent vector at
p
generated by the Lie algebra element
\Phi(p)
. It is thus clear that the relation
\tilde{\theta}(v)-\tilde{\Phi}=0
implies
v=\tilde{v}_{\Phi}
, so we have computed
\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}=\bar{u}_{\xi}+\tilde{v}_{\Phi}
.

By definition of the horizontal lift,
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}(\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime})=0
. To show
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is basic with respect to
\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}
, it therefore remains to show that
\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}}d\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}=0
. We compute

\displaystyle\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}}d\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}}(d\tilde{\theta}-dA_{\xi}\tilde{\Phi}+A_{\xi}\wedge d\tilde{\Phi})

\displaystyle=\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}}d\tilde{\theta}+d\tilde{\theta}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi},-)-dA_{\xi}(\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime},-)\tilde{\Phi}+A_{\xi}(\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime})d\tilde{\Phi}-A_{\xi}d\tilde{\Phi}(\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}}d\tilde{\theta}+d\tilde{\theta}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi},-)+d\tilde{\Phi}-A_{\xi}d\tilde{\Phi}(\bar{u}_{\xi})-A_{\xi}d\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi}).

First note
d\tilde{\Phi}(\bar{u}_{\xi})=-d\tilde{\theta}(\bar{u}_{\xi},\bar{u}_{\xi})=0
by the moment map condition, since
\bar{u}_{\xi}
projects to
\bar{\xi}
under the map
\tilde{P}\to P
. Next, we claim that
d\tilde{\Phi}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi})
vanishes. To see this, note we may restrict the computation to a fibre of
\tilde{P}
, in particular dropping the tilde notation. By choosing an arbitrary point of the fibre, we may identify the fibre (non-canonically) with
U(r)
. The restricted moment map can be considered as
\Phi:U(r)\to\mathfrak{u}(r)
, satisfying
R_{g}^{*}\Phi=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})\Phi
for all
g\in U(r)
, which implies

\Phi(g)=\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})\Phi(e)=g^{-1}xg

for all
g\in U(r)
, where we define
x:=\Phi(e)
, and
e
is the identity of
U(r)
. To compute
v_{\Phi}
, note that if we have an arbitrary element
y\in\mathfrak{u}(r)
, the vector field at
g
generated by
y
via the right action is computed as

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}g\exp(ty)=gy.

Thus, the vector field generated by
\Phi(g)
at
g
is given by

v_{\Phi}(g)=g\Phi(g)=xg.

We now differentiate
\Phi
in the direction of
v_{\Phi}
, to get

v_{\Phi}(\Phi)(g)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi(\exp(tx)g)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}g^{-1}\exp(-tx)x\exp(tx)g=0.

Returning to
\tilde{P}
, we have now shown that

\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}}d\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}=\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}}d\tilde{\theta}+d\tilde{\theta}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi},-)+d\tilde{\Phi}.

Note that
\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}}d\tilde{\theta}+d_{\mathcal{H}}\tilde{\Phi}=0
by the moment map condition, so we have

\iota_{\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime}}d\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}=d\tilde{\theta}(\tilde{v}_{\Phi},-)+d_{\mathcal{V}}\tilde{\Phi},

and our aim is to show the right-hand side vanishes. The right-hand side is pulled back from
P
, so we may compute there. Note that
d\theta+\frac{1}{2}[\theta,\theta]=F
, where
F
is the curvature of the principal bundle
P
. Isolating the vertical components of this equation, we have
d\theta(v_{\Phi},-)=-[\theta(v_{\Phi}),\theta]=-[\Phi,\theta]
. This is purely vertical, so we may again reduce to computing on a fibre, which we identify non-canonically with
U(r)
. We now wish to show that

d\Phi=[\Phi,\theta],

where
\theta
is now the Maurer–Cartan form (for the right action) of
U(r)
. Let
a\in\mathfrak{u}(r)
be an arbitrary element and denote by
\bar{a}
the right-invariant vector field on
U(r)
generated by
a
, then we have

\displaystyle d\Phi(\bar{a})(g)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\Phi(g\exp(ta))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-ta)g^{-1}xg\exp(ta)

\displaystyle=-ag^{-1}xg+g^{-1}xga

\displaystyle=[g^{-1}xg,a]

\displaystyle=[\Phi,\theta(\bar{a})].\qed

Now, let us compute the curvature of
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
and take its horizontal trace, i.e. its trace over the distribution
D\subset TS
. On
\tilde{P}
, the curvature of
\tilde{\theta}^{\prime}
is the restriction to
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}=\bar{D}\oplus(\mathbb{R}\cdot\bar{u}_{\xi}^{\prime})
of

d\tilde{\theta}-dA_{\xi}\tilde{\Phi}+A_{\xi}\wedge d\tilde{\Phi}.

Restricting this to
\bar{D}
yields
d\tilde{\theta}|_{\bar{D}}-\frac{1}{f}\omega\tilde{\Phi}
. The first term is the curvature of
(\tilde{P},\tilde{\theta})
in the
D
-directions. Computing the trace of this with respect to
dA_{\xi}|_{D}=\frac{1}{f}\omega
and translating back to vector bundles, we get

f\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}-n\Phi_{h}.

Suppose this is a constant multiple of the identity, so that by Lemma
3.3
we have a transverse Hermite–Einstein metric in the sense of
[
BS10
,
BH22
]
. Then

\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(f\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}-n\Phi_{h}^{\xi}\right)=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{E},

so multiplying by
f^{-n-1}
,

\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(f^{-n}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h}-nf^{-n-1}\Phi_{h}^{\xi}\right)=\lambda f^{-n-1}\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

Defining
v(\mu)=f^{-n}
and
w(\mu)=f^{-n-1}
, this is the
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein equation.

Proposition 3.4
.

The metric
h
on
E\to X
is
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein with weights
v(\mu)=(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle+a)^{-n}
and
w(\mu)=(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle+a)^{-n-1}
if and only if the induced connection
\tilde{\theta}
on
\tilde{E}\to S
is transversally Hermite–Einstein with respect to the vector field
u_{\xi}
.

Suppose that
\tilde{E}\to S
admits a transversally Hermite–Einstein connection with respect to the vector field
u_{\xi}
. This connection may not be induced by a connection on
E
in the above manner, so we cannot immediately conclude that
E
admits a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric. We will show in Proposition
5.8
that it is indeed the case that the existence of a transversally Hermite–Einstein connection on
\tilde{E}\to S
implies the existence of a
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on
E\to X
.

3.6.
Polynomially weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics

In this section, after reviewing the semi-simple fibration construction for a Kähler manifold
(X,\omega,\mathbb{T})
, we use the associated bundle construction to construct from a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
, a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle
E_{Q}
over the total space
X_{Q}
of the semi-simple fibration. We show that under the associated connection construction, the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation on
E\to X
corresponds to the Hermite–Einstein equation on
E_{Q}\to X_{Q}
.

Let
(X,\omega)
be a compact Kähler manifold of dimension
n
, endowed with a Hamiltonian isometric action of an
\ell
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}
and
\mu:X\to P\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
a fixed moment map. We let
\pi_{B}:Q\to B
be a principal
\mathbb{T}
-bundle over an
m
-dimensional manifold
B=B_{1}\times\cdots\times B_{N}
, where for each
\alpha=1,\ldots,N
,
(B_{\alpha},\omega_{B_{\alpha}})
is a compact projective manifold of dimension
n_{\alpha}
. We further assume that
Q
is the reduction to
\mathbb{T}
of a holomorphic principal bundle with structure group
\mathbb{T}^{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
\eta\in\Omega^{1}(Q,\mathfrak{t})
be a principal bundle connection on
Q
with curvature

(13)

d\eta=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\pi_{B}^{*}\omega_{B_{\alpha}}\otimes p_{\alpha},\qquad p_{\alpha}\in\Lambda\subset{\mathfrak{t}},

where
\Lambda\subset\mathfrak{t}
denotes the integer lattice in
\mathfrak{t}
; see
[
AJL23
, Remark 5.1]
for a construction of such a principal bundle.
Following
[
AJL23
, Section 5]
, we consider the associated fibration

X_{Q}:=Q\times_{\mathbb{T}}X\overset{\pi}{\to}B,

where
Q\times_{\mathbb{T}}X:=(Q\times X)/\mathbb{T}
with
\mathbb{T}
-action on the product given by
t\cdot(q,x)=(q\cdot t^{-1},t\cdot x)
for
x\in X
,
q\in Q
, and
t\in\mathbb{T}
. As per
[
AJL23
]
, this fibration is endowed with a natural integrable complex structure making
X_{Q}\to B
a holomorphic submersion.

Fixing constants
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that for each
\alpha=1,\cdots,N
, the affine linear function
\langle\mu,p_{\alpha}\rangle+c_{\alpha}>0
is strictly positive on the momentum image
P
of
X
, we define the
2
-form
\omega_{Q}
on
Q\times X
by

(14)

\begin{split}\omega_{Q}&:=\omega+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}(\langle\mu,p_{\alpha}\rangle+c_{\alpha})\pi_{B}^{*}\omega_{B_{\alpha}}+\langle d\mu\wedge\eta\rangle\\
&=\omega+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}c_{\alpha}(\pi_{B}^{*}\omega_{B_{\alpha}})+d\left(\langle\mu,\eta\rangle\right).\end{split}

The form
\omega_{Q}
is
\mathbb{T}
-basic, so is the pull back of a Kähler form, still denoted
\omega_{Q}
, on the quotient
X_{Q}
. The Kähler manifold
(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
is called a
semi-simple
(X,\mathbb{T})
-fibration
associated to the Kähler manifold
(X,\mathbb{T})
and the torus bundle
Q\to B
satisfying (
13
). Any Kähler metric
\omega_{Q}
on
Y
constructed in this way from a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Kähler metric
\omega
on
X
and fixed Kähler metrics
\omega_{\alpha}
on
B_{\alpha}
is called
bundle compatible
.

Now, let
E\to X
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle. We consider the vector bundle

E_{Q}:=Q\times_{\mathbb{T}}E\to X_{Q}

where
Q\times_{\mathbb{T}}E:=(Q\times E)/\mathbb{T}
with
\mathbb{T}
-action on the product given by
t\cdot(q,e)=(q\cdot t^{-1},t\cdot e)
for
e\in E
,
q\in Q
,
t\in\mathbb{T}
. Using the
\mathbb{T}
-bundle structure of
Q\times E\to E_{Q}
, the total space
E_{Q}
inherits a complex structure
J_{E_{Q}}:=J_{E}\oplus\pi^{*}J_{B}
.

For any
\mathbb{T}
-invariant connection
\nabla
on
E
such that
\nabla^{0,1}
induces the holomorphic structure
J_{E}
of
E
, we write
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}\subset TE
the corresponding horizontal distribution inducing the splitting
TE=\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}\oplus VE
. By the Koszul–Malgrange theorem,
J_{E}
is given by
\pi^{*}J_{X}\oplus J_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}
where
\pi^{*}J_{X}
is the complex structure on
\mathcal{H}^{\nabla}
induced from the base and

is the standard complex structure on the fibers of
E\to X
. The relation
J_{E_{Q}}=J_{X_{Q}}\oplus J_{\mathbb{C}^{r}}
, shows that
J_{E_{Q}}
is compatible with the vector bundle structure
E_{Q}\to X_{Q}
.

Let
\Phi_{\nabla}
be
\mathbb{T}
-momentum map for the curvature
F_{\nabla}
of
\nabla
. We define the
\mathbb{T}
-invariant connection on the vector bundle
\pi_{E}\times\mathrm{Id}_{Q}:Q\times E\to Q\times X
by

(15)

\nabla^{Q}:=\nabla+\langle\Phi_{\nabla}\otimes\eta\rangle.

In a basis of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-generators
(\xi_{a})
of
{\mathfrak{t}}

\begin{split}F_{\nabla^{Q}}=&F_{\nabla}+\nabla^{\mathrm{End}(E)}(\langle\Phi_{\nabla}\otimes\eta\rangle)+\langle\Phi_{\nabla}\otimes\eta\rangle\wedge\langle\Phi_{\nabla}\otimes\eta\rangle\\

Proof.

3.7.
Deformations

Following

Suppose that a

3

3

The bundle

4.
Equivariant intersections

Before developing more advanced theory of the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation, we first review the equivariant calculus introduced by Inoue
[
Ino20
]
, and extend the theory from weight functions of the form
\tilde{v}(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle)
for some fixed
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
and real analytic
\tilde{v}
, to arbitrary smooth weight functions
v(\mu)
on the moment polytope. There are two contexts in which we will use the theory:

(1)

Given a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant manifold
X
, we may produce
equivariant intersection numbers
from equivariant data on
X
;

(2)

Let
K
be an auxiliary compact Lie group. Given a
(\mathbb{T}\times K)
-equivariant holomorphic submersion
X\to B
, where
\mathbb{T}
acts trivially on
B
, we may produce closed
K
-equivariant differential forms on
B
of degree 2 from equivariant data on
X
, in a way that is compatible with the projection of such forms to equivariant cohomology. We will call such forms
equivariant intersection 2-forms
.

The equivariant intersection numbers will be used to show that quantities such as
\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{n}
and
\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h}))\omega^{n}
are independent of the choices of equivariant representative
\omega+\mu
for
[\omega+\mu]
and
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric
h
. In particular, we can use the theory to define the
weighted slope
of a sheaf, which will in turn be used to define weighted slope stability of vector bundles. We will further use such equivariant intersection numbers to phrase the correct analogue of the Kobayashi–Lübke inequality in the weighted setting. The equivariant intersection 2-forms will be used to demonstrate that the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation arises naturally as a moment map.

4.1.
Equivariant intersection numbers

4.1.1.
Weight functions of one variable

We first recall the theory of Inoue which produces equivariant intersection numbers. Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real analytic function; denote by
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}x^{k}
its convergent power series centred at the origin of
\mathbb{R}
. Let
X
be a compact complex manifold of dimension
n
, equipped with a
\mathbb{T}
-action. Given an equivariant cohomology class
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}
on
X
, along with an auxiliary class
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\in H^{2k}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mathbb{R})
, we produce a convergent power series

(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))\in\mathbb{R}\llbracket\mathfrak{t}\rrbracket

as follows. The map
\pi:X\to\mathrm{pt}
induces a map
\pi_{*}:H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mathbb{R})\to H^{*-2n}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt})
; on the level of Cartan representatives, this is simply the integration map. There is an isomorphism

\hat{H}^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}\llbracket\mathfrak{t}\rrbracket,

where
\hat{H}^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})
denotes the completion of the even-degree equivariant cohomology ring of a point, and a degree
2j
class in this ring is mapped to a homogeneous polynomial of degree
j
on
\mathfrak{t}
under the isomorphism.

Given this information, we define
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
by first taking the class

\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}^{k}\in\hat{H}^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mathbb{R})

and then applying the pushforward map
\pi_{*}:H^{*}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mathbb{R})\to H^{*-2n}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt})
term-by-term in the series to get

(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})):=\pi_{*}(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))\in\hat{H}^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}\llbracket\mathfrak{t}\rrbracket.

Now, by
[
Ino20
, Theorem 1.1]
, if
f
has a convergent power series on all of
\mathbb{R}
, then the formal power series
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
is in fact convergent on
\mathfrak{t}
. In particular, given an element
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
, we may evaluate the power series at
\xi
to produce an equivariant intersection number

(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))(\xi)\in\mathbb{R}.

The connection to the weighted theory is as follows. Suppose that the weight function
v:P\to\mathbb{R}
is of the form
v(p)=\tilde{v}(\langle p,\xi\rangle)
for some fixed element
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}
and a fixed real analytic function
\tilde{v}
on
\mathbb{R}
. Let
f
be an order
n
antiderivative for
\tilde{v}
, so that
f^{(n)}=\tilde{v}
. Let
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}:=[\omega+\mu]
. Then taking
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=1
,

(f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))(\xi)=\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}.

Here we use the notation
\omega^{[j]}:=\frac{1}{j!}\omega^{j}
. In particular, since the left-hand side is independent of the choice of equivariant representative for
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}
, this implies that the right-hand side
\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{n}
depends only on the equivariant cohomology class
[\omega+\mu]
, and not the choice of equivariant representative. Taking
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}
gives

(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}f^{\prime}(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))(\xi)=\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})\right)\omega^{[n]},

again showing that the right-hand side depends only on the equivariant cohomology classes
c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}
and
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}
; note here we take a derivative of the function
f
, to account for the class
c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}
having degree 2.

Remark 4.1
.

Since the power series
(f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
converges on all of
{\mathfrak{t}}
, we may extend it to an entire function on the complex vector space
\mathfrak{t}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. In particular
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot f(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))(i\xi)
for
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
is well-defined. In the special case
f=\exp
, the function
\xi\mapsto(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)
satisfies

(18)

|(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{k}

for some fixed
C>0
, where
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}
has degree
2k
as an equivariant form. This is easily seen from the fact that
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}
is polynomial in
\xi
of degree
k
, and that
e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}
has modulus 1.

4.1.2.
Arbitrary weight functions

We now show how the above theory may be extended to arbitrary weight functions

on the moment polytope, that are not necessarily of the form

First, given an arbitrary function
u:P\to\mathbb{R}
, we may extend
u
to a compactly supported smooth function on all of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, with support containing
P
. Recall, that the Fourier transform of

is given by

\hat{u}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}}u(\mu)e^{-i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\mu},

where
\underline{d\mu}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, for a fixed choice of metric on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
that will not matter. The Fourier transform defines a bijective map
\mathcal{F}:S({\mathfrak{t}}^{*})\to S({\mathfrak{t}})
on the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing functions, and the Fourier inversion formula yields

u(\mu)=\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\hat{u}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi},

where
\underline{d\xi}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathfrak{t}}
(dual to
\underline{d\mu}
) divided by
(2\pi)^{\dim\mathbb{T}}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}\in H^{2}_{\mathbb{T}}(X;\mathbb{R})

to all of
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}

(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot u(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})):=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi},

where
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)
was defined in Remark
4.1
.

The quantity
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot u(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
manifestly depends only on the equivariant cohomology classes involved, and not on any choice of equivariant representative.

Lemma 4.3

where

Proof.

That the integral converges can be seen easily from (

To see that the quantity does not depend on the extension of

from

to
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}

\beta_{\mathbb{T}}
and compute

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\int_{X}\langle\sigma,i\xi\rangle\wedge e^{\langle\omega+\mu,i\xi\rangle}\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

(19)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\langle\sigma,i\xi\rangle e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\wedge e^{\omega}.

Next, note that
\sigma:\mathfrak{t}\to\Omega^{*}(X)
is an equivariant differential form of degree
2k
, so
\langle\sigma,\xi\rangle
is a sum of terms of the form
\eta\cdot\xi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}
where
\eta\in\Omega^{2j}(X)
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{\ell}=k-j
, for some
0\leq j\leq k
. Replacing
\sigma
in (
19
) with such a term yields

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\eta\cdot i^{k-j}\xi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\wedge e^{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\mathcal{F}(\partial_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}u)(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\right)\eta\wedge e^{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}(\partial_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}u)(\mu)\eta\wedge e^{\omega}

and this final line is clearly independent of the choice of extension of
u
from
P
to
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
.

Finally, we show that the two definitions agree. We may assume
\zeta\neq 0
, as the case
\zeta=0
is easily observed. With this assumption, we may choose a basis
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell}
for
\mathfrak{t}
so that
\zeta=\xi_{1}
, and take the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{t}
determined by this basis. Once again choosing an equivariant representative
\sigma
for
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}
and isolating a particular term
\eta\cdot\xi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}
, note we have

\langle\eta\cdot\xi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\xi_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}},\zeta\rangle=\begin{cases}\eta&\text{if }a_{l}=0\text{ for all }l>1\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Considering only the case
\eta\cdot\xi_{1}^{k-j}
,

\displaystyle\int_{X}\langle\eta\cdot\xi_{1}^{k-j},\zeta\rangle f^{(k)}(\omega+\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\eta f^{(k)}(\omega+\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}f^{(n+k-j)}(\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle)\eta\wedge\omega^{[n-j]}.

Note that similarly to the above calculation of
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot u(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
, in the case
u(\mu)=\tilde{u}(\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle)
we have
\partial_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\ell}^{a_{\ell}}u=0
unless
a_{l}=0
for all
l>0
, and
\partial_{1}^{k-j}u(\mu)=f^{(n+k-j)}(\langle\mu,\zeta\rangle)
. It follows that the two terms agree, and we are done.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

The following formulae hold:

(1)

\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}=(v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
;

(2)

\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h}))\omega^{[n]}=(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
.

In particular, the constant
c_{v}
of equation (
7
) is an equivariant topological constant.

Proof.

In the case
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=1

\displaystyle(v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}})(i\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left(\int_{X}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\omega^{[n]}\right)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\omega^{[n]}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}.

To compute the case
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}
, and later the case
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=c_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}
, we will need the following straightforward formulae:

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\partial_{k}v)(\mu)=i\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)\xi_{k}e^{i\langle\xi,\mu\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\implies dv(\mu)=i\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)\xi e^{i\langle\xi,\mu\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\\
&(\partial_{jk}^{2}v)(\mu)=i^{2}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)\xi_{j}\xi_{k}e^{i\langle\xi,\mu\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\implies{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)=i^{2}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)(\xi\otimes\xi)e^{i\langle\xi,\mu\rangle}\underline{d\xi}.\end{split}

In the case
\beta_{\mathbb{T}}=c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}

\displaystyle\big(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\big(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}}\big)(i\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}(F_{h}+\langle\Phi_{h},i\xi\rangle)\wedge e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

where we used (

4.2.
Equivariant intersection 2-forms

4.2.1.
Weight functions of one variable

Once again, we begin by recalling the theory established by Inoue in
[
Ino20
]
, letting
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be our real analytic function with power series expansion
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{k!}x^{k}
about the origin. In this case, we assume we have a proper holomorphic submersion
\pi:X\to B
. We further assume there exists a compact group
K
so that
\mathbb{T}\times K
acts on both
X
and
B
making the submersion
\pi
equivariant, and so that the
\mathbb{T}
-action on
B
is trivial.

Let
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}\times K}=[\omega+\mu]
be a
\mathbb{T}\times K
-equivariant
2
-form on
X
, and let
\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}=[\sigma]
be an auxiliary
\mathbb{T}\times K
-equivariant
2k
-form on
X
. We will define

(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot f(\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}))_{B}(\xi),

which will be a degree 2
K
-equivariant cohomology class on
B
. Recall we have a pushforward map

\pi_{*}:H^{2d}_{\mathbb{T}\times K}(X;\mathbb{R})\to H^{2d-2n}_{\mathbb{T}\times K}(B;\mathbb{R}),

where
n:=\dim X-\dim B
. Since the torus action on
B
is trivial, the equivariant cohomology of
B
splits as

H^{2l}_{\mathbb{T}\times K}(B;\mathbb{R})=\bigoplus_{j+k=l}H^{2j}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})\otimes H^{2k}_{K}(B;\mathbb{R}).

Since
H^{2j}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})=S^{j}(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
, i.e. the degree
j
homogeneous polynomials on
{\mathfrak{t}}
, evaluating at
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
the component in
H^{2j}_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathrm{pt};\mathbb{R})
we get a map

\mathrm{el}_{\xi}:H^{2l}_{\mathbb{T}\times K}(X;\mathbb{R})\to\bigoplus_{k=0}^{l}H^{2k}_{K}(B;\mathbb{R}).

Denote by
p_{2}:H^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{K}(B;\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}_{K}(B;\mathbb{R})
the projection to the degree 2 component of equivariant cohomology. Combining all of this information, we get a map

p_{2}\circ\mathrm{el}_{\xi}\circ\pi_{*}:\hat{H}^{*,\mathrm{even}}_{\mathbb{T}\times K}(X;\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}_{K}(B;\mathbb{R}),

and we denote the image of
(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot f(\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}))
under this map by

(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot f(\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}))_{B}(\xi).

In terms of the equivariant representatives
\omega+\mu
and
\sigma
for
\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}
and
\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}
respectively, an equivariant representative for
(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot f(\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}))_{B}(\xi)
may be computed naturally as

\left[\int_{X/B}\langle\sigma,\xi\rangle f(\omega+\langle\mu,\xi\rangle)\right]_{\deg=2}.

Here, note that for example
\mu=\mu_{\mathfrak{t}}+\mu_{\mathfrak{k}}
and
\langle\mu,\xi\rangle:=\langle\mu_{{\mathfrak{t}}},\xi\rangle+\mu_{\mathfrak{k}}
, so the result of the fibre integral is a
K
-equivariant differential form on
B
, of which we take the degree 2 component. The result is the sum of a
K
-invariant differential 2-form on
B
and a
K
-equivariant “moment map”
\nu:B\to\mathfrak{k}^{*}
for that form, although the 2-form may not be non-degenerate in general.

4.2.2.
Arbitrary weight functions

Similarly as before, we use Fourier transforms to extend the above to general weight functions. In particular, let
u
be an arbitrary weight function on the moment polytope. Then we define

(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot u(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}\times K}))_{B}:=\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\beta_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}\cdot e^{\alpha_{{\mathbb{T}\times K}}})_{B}(i\xi)\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi},

which is a degree 2
K
-equivariant cohomology class on
B
.

A natural equivariant representative for this class is computed as

\left[\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left(\int_{X/B}\langle\sigma,i\xi\rangle e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\right)\hat{u}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\right]_{\deg=2}.

For the purposes of this paper, we will only need to know that the above expression is an equivariantly closed
K
-equivariant 2-form, and from this we will deduce that the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation occurs as a moment map. One may similarly prove that the above formulae are well-defined and independent of the choice of extension of
u
to
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, and agree with the original definition in the case
u(\mu)=\tilde{u}(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle)
; will shall omit the proofs as they are essentially unchanged from the case of equivariant intersection numbers.

5.
Moment map property and stability

In this section, we use the equivariant intersection theory to develop two fundamental aspects related to the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation: the moment map property, and the notion of weighted slope stability of a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant sheaf. We then prove one direction of the weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, namely that existence of a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric implies
v
-weighted slope polystability. Assuming the full weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence (to be proved in Section
9
), we revisit the examples in Sections
3.5
and
3.6
. As a further application, we introduce a suitable notion of weighted Gieseker stability for vector bundles, and relate it to weighted slope stability.

5.1.
Moment map property

The moment map derivation of the Hermite–Einstein equation was originally given by Atiyah–Bott
[
AB83
]
. Here in the weighted setting we will give a proof along the lines of
[
DH23
, Section 6]
, by integrating equivariant forms over the fibres of a universal family. Let
(E,h)
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant Hermitian vector bundle on
(X,\omega)
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
the space of
\mathbb{T}
-invariant unitary connections on
E
, which is an affine space modelled on
\Omega^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}_{SH}E)^{\mathbb{T}}
, the space of
\mathbb{T}
-invariant 1-forms with values in the skew-Hermitian endomorphisms of
E
. The gauge group
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
of
\mathbb{T}
-commuting unitary endomorphisms of
E
acts on
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
.

We consider
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
as a family over
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
. Denote by
\mathcal{E}
the pullback of
E
from
X
to
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
. We define a universal connection
D_{\mathcal{E}}
on
\mathcal{E}
as follows. For
(u,v)\in T_{(x,\nabla)}(X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}})
and
s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{E})
, define

(D_{\mathcal{E}}s)(u,v):=(\nabla s)(u)+(d_{\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}}s)(v).

That is, in the vertical direction along a fibre
X\times\{\nabla\}
, the connection is simply the basepoint
\nabla\in\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
, and in the horizontal direction,
\mathcal{E}
is trivial so we may extend the usual exterior derivative on
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
to
\mathcal{E}
. This connection has curvature
F_{\mathcal{E}}
, whose pure vertical component over the fibre
X\times\{\nabla\}
coincides with the curvature of
\nabla
, and whose mixed horizontal-vertical component maps a horizontal tangent vector to the associated vertical 1-form with values in
\mathrm{End}_{SH}(\mathcal{E})
under the identification
T_{\nabla}\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}\cong\Omega^{1}(X,\mathrm{End}_{SH}E)^{\mathbb{T}}
. The purely horizontal component of the curvature vanishes. Identifying the Lie algebra of
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
with the
\mathbb{T}
-commuting skew-adjoint endomorphisms of
E
, a moment map
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}
for the curvature
F_{\mathcal{E}}
is given by

\langle\Psi_{\mathcal{E}},e\rangle=-e.

In particular
F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}
is a
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})
-valued form on
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
.

To produce a moment map on
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
, we use the methods of Section
4.2
to generate an equivariant intersection 2-form, beginning with choices of
\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant 2-forms on the total space
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
. A representative for the class
\alpha_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}}
will be the pullback of the equivariant form
\omega+\mu
from
X
to
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
; note that the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
X
is trivial, so this is an equivariant
\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-form on
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
. As an auxiliary equivariant form, note the curvature
F_{\mathcal{E}}
has two moment maps: the map
\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}
for the action of
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
described above, and on each fibre
X\times\{\nabla\}
a moment map
\Phi_{\nabla}
for the
\mathbb{T}
-action. Combining these two moment maps produces the
\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-equivariant
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})
-valued form
F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Phi_{\nabla}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}
; note Proposition
2.2
implies
F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Phi_{\nabla}
is indeed
\mathbb{T}
-equivariantly closed. To represent the auxiliary class
\beta_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}}=\mathrm{ch}_{2}(\mathcal{E})_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}}
, we take

\frac{1}{2!}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}(F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Phi_{\nabla}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})\right)^{2}\right).

It now remains to compute the equivariant representative of the (formal) degree 2 equivariant class
(\beta_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}}))_{\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}}
on
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\left[\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left(\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}+\langle\Phi_{\nabla},i\xi\rangle)^{2})e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\right)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\right]_{\deg=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})\langle\Phi_{\nabla},i\xi\rangle)\wedge\omega^{[n]}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})\int_{\mathfrak{t}}e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\langle\Phi_{\nabla},i\xi\rangle\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\right)\wedge\omega^{[n]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mathcal{E}}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mathcal{E}}\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left((F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})\langle\Phi_{\nabla},dv(\mu)\rangle\right)\wedge\omega^{[n]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mathcal{E}}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left((v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathcal{E}}+\langle\Phi_{\nabla},dv(\mu)\rangle)\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}\right)\omega^{[n]}.

We also normalise by subtracting the appropriate multiple
c_{v,w}
of the corresponding representative for
(c_{1}(\mathcal{E})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot w(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))_{\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}}
, whose 2-form part vanishes,

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}+\langle\Phi_{\nabla},i\xi\rangle)\wedge e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{w}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\right]_{\deg=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}(\Psi_{\mathcal{E}})w(\mu)\omega^{[n]},

producing

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{\mathcal{E}}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}

\displaystyle+\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\mathcal{E}}+\Phi_{\nabla})-c_{v,w}w(\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{E}}\right)\frac{i}{2\pi}\Psi_{\mathcal{E}}\right)\omega^{[n]}.

Note that in the 2-form component of this equivariant form, the only part of
F_{\mathcal{E}}
that contributes to the integral is the mixed component, which takes a tangent vector to
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
and converts it to the corresponding vertical
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{E})
-valued 1-form on
X\times\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
, under the isomorphism
T_{\nabla}\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}\cong\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{End}_{SH}(E))^{\mathbb{T}}
. In particular, for tangent vectors
a,b\in T\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
, this 2-form may be written

\Omega_{v}(a,b):=\frac{-1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(a\wedge b)\wedge\omega^{[n-1]},

which is a weighted modification of the usual Atiyah–Bott symplectic form. We thus conclude:

Proposition 5.1
.

The map
\sigma_{v,w}:\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}\to\Gamma(\mathrm{End}_{SH}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}})^{*}
,

\langle\sigma_{v,w}(\nabla),e\rangle:=-\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\left(\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{E}+\Phi_{\nabla})-c_{v,w}w(\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\frac{i}{2\pi}e\right)\omega^{[n]}

is a moment map for the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbb{T}}
-action on
\mathcal{A}(E,h)^{\mathbb{T}}
with respect to the symplectic form

\Omega_{v}(a,b):=\frac{-1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(a\wedge b)\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}.

Remark 5.2
.

The proof above extends to the finite-dimensional case, where one has a
\mathbb{T}\times K
-equivariant vector bundle
E\to X
along with a
\mathbb{T}\times K
-equivariant proper holomorphic submersion
X\to B
, where the
\mathbb{T}
-action is trivial on
B
. In particular, this allows not only for the complex structure of the vector bundle to vary over the fibres of
X\to B
, but the complex structures of the fibres
X_{b}
themselves may also vary with
b\in B
. The caveat is that the differential 2-form on
B
is no longer guaranteed to be non-degenerate in general.

5.2.
Weighted slope stability

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a subsheaf, associating to each open subset
U\subset X
an
\mathcal{O}(U)
-submodule
\mathcal{O}(U,\mathcal{F})\subset\mathcal{O}(U,E)
. Note that for each
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
s\in\mathcal{O}(U,E)
, the
\mathbb{T}
-action on
E
induces a section
t\cdot s\in\mathcal{O}(t\cdot U,E)
. The subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
is called
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant
if for each
t\in\mathbb{T}
and
s\in\mathcal{O}(U,\mathcal{F})
,

t\cdot s\in\mathcal{O}(t\cdot U,\mathcal{F}).

Recall that for a torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, the
determinant line bundle
of
\mathcal{F}
may be defined as

\det(\mathcal{F}):=(\Lambda^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\mathcal{F})^{**}.

Canonical operations on sheaves such as tensor powers, exterior powers and duals all preserve the property of being
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant, so that if
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant torsion-free coherent sheaf, the line bundle
\det(\mathcal{F})
is naturally
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant. Given a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant torsion-free coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
, we therefore define

c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}:=c_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}))_{\mathbb{T}}.

Note that any
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
is automatically torsion-free, and so
c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}
is defined. We lastly recall that a coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
is called
saturated
if the quotient
E/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free. In particular, a saturated sheaf is reflexive
[
Kob14
, Proposition 5.5.22]
, and has singular locus having complex codimension at least 2 in
X

[
Kob14
, Corollary 5.5.20]
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant torsion-free coherent sheaf on
(X,\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})
. The
v
-weighted slope
of
\mathcal{F}
is

\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}.

A
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle
E
on
(X,\alpha_{T})
is:

(1)

v
-weighted slope semistable
if for all proper non-zero
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaves
\mathcal{F}\subset E
,

\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu_{v}(E);

(2)

v
-weighted slope stable
if for all proper non-zero
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaves
\mathcal{F}\subset E
,

\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})<\mu_{v}(E);

(3)

v
-weighted slope polystable
if
E
is a direct sum of
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant stable vector bundles of the same weighted slope;

(4)

v
-weighted slope unstable
if
E
is not
v
-weighted slope semistable.

Remark 5.4
.

We could similarly define the
(v,w)
-weighted slope
of a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant sheaf
\mathcal{F}
as

\mu_{v,w}(\mathcal{F}):=\frac{(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})(w(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))},

along with various analogues of
(v,w)
-weighted slope stability, etc. Of course, the definitions of
(v,w)
-weighted slope stability and
v
-weighted slope stability coincide, which is the algebro-geometric analogue of Lemma
2.6
.

The following lemma will be needed in the proof of the weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent subsheaf whose singular locus
V
has codimension at least 2 in
X
. Let
h
be a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric on
E
, and denote by
F_{\mathcal{F}}+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}
the curvature and moment map associated to the restricted metric
h|_{\mathcal{F}}
over
X\backslash V
. Then

(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))=\int_{X\backslash V}\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\mathcal{F}}+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})\omega^{[n]}.

In other words, the equivariant intersection number
(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
can be computed by differential geometric means on the smooth locus of the subsheaf.

Proof.

We follow the approach of Kobayashi
[
Kob14
, pp. 181-182]
. First, consider the map

j:\det(\mathcal{F}):=(\Lambda^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\mathcal{F})^{**}\to\Lambda^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}E.

Away from
V
this map is injective on the fibres. Let
\tau
be a local holomorphic non-vanishing section of
\det(\mathcal{F})
, and let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}
be a local holomorphic frame for
E
over the same open subset. Then

j(\tau)=\sum_{I}\tau^{I}s_{I},

where
I:=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})})
for
i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}
,
s_{I}:=e_{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}}
, and the
\tau^{I}
are local holomorphic functions. Denote by
u:=j^{*}\Lambda^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}h
the (degenerate along
V
) metric induced by
h
. Fix an arbitrary smooth
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
\tilde{u}
on
\det(\mathcal{F})
, and define

f:=u(\tau,\tau)/\tilde{u}(\tau,\tau).

In particular,
f
is a smooth non-negative
\mathbb{T}
-invariant function that is independent of the choice of local section
\tau
, so is globally defined on
X
. Furthermore,
f
vanishes if and only if each of the
\tau^{I}
vanish, since the metric on
\Lambda^{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}E
is non-degenerate. Denote by
W\subset V
this vanishing locus, which is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant subvariety of
X
of codimension at least 2.

Next, choose a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant holomorphic map
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
so that
D:=\pi^{-1}(W)
has the following properties:
\mathrm{codim}D=1
,
\pi:\tilde{X}\backslash D\to X\backslash W
is a biholomorphism, and
\pi^{*}\mathcal{I}_{W}=\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-mD)
for some
m>0
, where
\mathcal{I}_{W}
denotes the ideal sheaf of
W
. Let
\zeta
be a local holomorphic function defining
D
, so that for each
I
,
\pi^{*}\tau^{I}/\zeta^{m}
is holomorphic and at least one of these functions is non-vanishing. Then we may further write

\pi^{*}f=a|\zeta|^{2m}

for some local positive smooth function
a
.

We are now in a position to compute
(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))
. Denote by
\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{u})_{\mathbb{T}}
(resp.
\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}}
) the equivariant curvature of the metric
\tilde{u}
on
\det(\mathcal{F})
(resp.
u
on
\det(\mathcal{F})|_{X\backslash W}
). For any
k\geq n-1
, we begin with

\displaystyle\int_{X}\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{u})_{\mathbb{T}}\wedge(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash D}\pi^{*}\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{u})_{\mathbb{T}}\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash D}\pi^{*}\left(\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}}-\frac{i}{2\pi}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log f\right)\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]},

where we used (
6
) in the final line. Note the integral of
\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log\pi^{*}f\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}
over
\tilde{X}\backslash D
converges absolutely, since near
D
we have

\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log\pi^{*}f=\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log a+m\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta},

where the second term is interpreted as a map
\tilde{X}\backslash D\to{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
in the usual way. Moreover, we see that for
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
,

(21)

\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash D}|\langle\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\pi^{*}\log f,\xi\rangle\wedge\pi^{*}(e^{\omega})|\leq C_{1}|\xi|+C_{2},

for certain constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
; here the absolute value in the integral is interpreted as taking the degree
2n
component (as a differential form), writing it as a multiple
g\omega^{[n]}
for some function
g
, and replacing with
|g|\omega^{[n]}
. For all
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, let
N_{\epsilon}
denote a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant tubular neighbourhood of
D
of radius
\epsilon
for a choice of smooth
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric on
\tilde{X}
. Then

\displaystyle-\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash D}\frac{i}{2\pi}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\pi^{*}\log f\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash N_{\epsilon}}\frac{i}{2\pi}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\pi^{*}\log f\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\tilde{X}\backslash N_{\epsilon}}\frac{i}{2\pi}d^{\mathrm{eq}}\left(\partial\pi^{*}\log f\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{\partial N_{\epsilon}}\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\pi^{*}\log f\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]},

where in the final line we used equivariant Stokes theorem (see
[
Ino21
, Lemma 3.14]
, for example), and the minus sign is lost due to the orientation of
\partial N_{\epsilon}
. Now, on a small open set where
\pi^{*}f=a|\zeta|^{2m}
, the contributions from

\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\pi^{*}\log a\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

to the integral become increasingly small as
\epsilon\to 0
, since this form is bounded and
\partial N_{\epsilon}
has volume approaching 0. Thus only the local term

\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\pi^{*}\log|\zeta|^{2m}\wedge\pi^{*}(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

may contribute in the limit. By the usual (i.e. non-equivariant) Poincaré–Lelong formula, the corresponding contributions tend towards

m\int_{D}\mu^{[k-n-1]}\omega^{[n-1]}

as
\epsilon\to 0
. Since
\omega|_{D}
is pulled back from
W
which has codimension at least 2, this integral vanishes, and we have proven

\displaystyle\int_{X}\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{u})_{\mathbb{T}}\wedge(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}=

\displaystyle\int_{X\backslash W}\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}}\wedge(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X\backslash V}\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}}\wedge(\omega+\mu)^{[k]}.

Summing from
k=n-1
to
\infty
, we see that for
\xi\in\mathfrak{t}\oplus i\mathfrak{t}
, the equivariant intersection number
(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{\langle\alpha_{\mathbb{T}},\xi\rangle})
can be computed as

\int_{X\backslash V}\langle\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}},\xi\rangle\wedge e^{\omega+\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}.

Applying the inverse Fourier transform, we have

(c_{1}(\mathcal{F})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))=\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X\backslash V}\langle\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}},i\xi\rangle\wedge e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}.

It remains to observe we may exchange the order of integration. To see this, recall that

\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),u)_{\mathbb{T}}=\tilde{c}_{1}(\det(\mathcal{F}),\tilde{u})_{\mathbb{T}}+\frac{i}{2\pi}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log f;

clearly the corresponding integral with
\tilde{u}
in place of
u
converges absolutely, since
\tilde{u}
extends smoothly over
\tilde{X}
. Finally the inequality (
21
) shows that the integral

\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X\backslash V}\langle i\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial\log\pi^{*}f,i\xi\rangle\wedge e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}

also converges absolutely, since
\hat{v}
is Schwartz class. Applying Fubini’s theorem, we may exchange the order of integration, and we are done.
∎

5.3.
Existence of a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric implies weighted slope polystability

Let
E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler
\mathbb{T}
-manifold
(X,\omega)
, and let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant holomorphic subbundle of
E
. A
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
h_{E}
on
E
induces a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
h_{\mathcal{F}}
on
\mathcal{F}
by restriction. The orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{F}
in
(E,h_{E})
defines a smooth complex subbundle
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
of
E
that is
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant. As a smooth complex vector bundle,
\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
is isomorphic to the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant quotient bundle
Q:=E/\mathcal{F}
. We denote by
h_{Q}
the induced Hermitian metric on
Q
.

Let
\nabla^{E}
,
\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}
, and
\nabla^{Q}
be the Chern connections of
(E,h_{E})
,
(\mathcal{F},h_{\mathcal{F}})
, and
(Q,h_{Q})
respectively. For all
s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})\subset\Gamma(E)
, we have the decomposition

\nabla^{E}s=\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}s+A(s),

relative to
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
where
A\in\Omega^{1,0}({\rm Hom}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\perp}))
is the second fundamental form of
\mathcal{F}
in
(E,h_{E})
. It follows that the bundle moment map
\Phi_{E}
for the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
E
decomposes as follows

\langle\Phi_{E}(s),\xi\rangle=\langle\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}(s),\xi\rangle+A(s)(\xi)

for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
and
s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F})
. Using the smooth isomorphism
Q\to\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, for all
s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F}^{\perp})
we have the decomposition

\nabla^{E}s=\nabla^{Q}s-A^{\dagger}(s)

where
A^{\dagger}
is the adjoint of
A
relative to
h_{E}
. It follows that the bundle moment map
\Phi_{E}
for the action of
\mathbb{T}
on
E
also decomposes as

\langle\Phi_{E}(s),\xi\rangle=\langle\Phi_{Q}(s),\xi\rangle-A^{\dagger}(s)(\xi)

for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
and
s\in\Gamma(\mathcal{F}^{\perp})
.

In summary, relative to the decomposition
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus\mathcal{F}^{\perp}
, the curvature
F_{E}
of
\nabla^{E}
and the bundle moment map admit the following decompositions

(22)

\begin{split}F_{\nabla^{E}}=\begin{pmatrix}F_{\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}}-A^{\dagger}\wedge A&\nabla^{1,0}A^{\dagger}\\
-\bar{\partial}A&F_{\nabla^{Q}}-A\wedge A^{\dagger}\end{pmatrix},\quad\Phi_{E}=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{\mathcal{F}}&-A^{\dagger}\\
A&\Phi_{Q}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

where we use the isomorphism
Q\cong E/\mathcal{F}
to identify
A
with an element of
\Omega^{1,0}(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{F},Q))
and
A^{\dagger}
with an element of
\Omega^{0,1}(\mathrm{Hom}(Q,\mathcal{F}))
.

Of course, in the case where
\mathcal{F}\subset E
is a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf, the above discussion goes through in an identical manner over the open locus where
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(E,h_{E})
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein bundle on
(X,\omega)
and
\mathcal{F}
a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf of
E
with singular locus
V
. Then over
X\backslash V
, the inequality

\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}}+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\leq\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{E}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{E}}+\Phi_{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}

holds pointwise. Moreover, if equality holds on all of
X\backslash V
, then
E

splits as
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus E/\mathcal{F}
over
X\backslash V
, where each component is
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein with the same
v
-weighted slope.

Proof.

By the weighted Hermite–Einstein condition, on
X\backslash V
we have that

\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{E}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{E}}+\Phi_{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}=2\pi c_{v}.

On the other hand, using (
22
) we get

\begin{split}\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}}+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}=&\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{E}}+\Phi_{E})|_{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}+\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\big(iv(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}(A^{\dagger}\wedge A)\big)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\\
=&2\pi c_{v}+\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\big(iv(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}(A^{\dagger}\wedge A)\big)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}.\end{split}

We infer that

\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}}+\Phi_{\mathcal{F}})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}-\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{E}\,i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{\nabla^{E}}+\Phi_{E})}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}=\frac{\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\big(iv(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}(A^{\dagger}\wedge A)\big)}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}\leq 0

with equality if and only if
A=0
. In the case
A=0
on
X\backslash V
, by
[
Sek21
, Proposition 8.7]
we have
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus E/\mathcal{F}
over
X\backslash V
, and using (
22
), each component is
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein. By Lemma
5.5
, we can compute the weighted slopes over the smooth locus, and both
\mathcal{F}
and
E/\mathcal{F}
have the same
v
-weighted slope equal to
\mu_{v}(E)
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

If
E
admits a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric, then
E
is
v
-weighted slope polystable.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf. By integrating the inequality of Lemma
5.6
over the smooth locus of
\mathcal{F}
and applying Lemma
5.5
, we see that

\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})\leq\mu_{v}(E),

and so
E
is
v
-weighted slope semistable.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant proper non-zero coherent saturated subsheaf such that
\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{v}(E)
. Our aim is to show that
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle, and
E
splits as the direct sum
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus E/\mathcal{F}
. The equality
\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{v}(E)
implies that
A=0
on the smooth locus of
\mathcal{F}
, so that the restriction of
E
to
X\backslash V
splits as
E|_{X\backslash V}=\mathcal{F}|_{X\backslash V}\oplus(E/\mathcal{F})|_{X\backslash V}
by Lemma
5.6
. The exact argument in the proof of
[
Sek21
, Theorem 8.1]
gives that the splitting extends to
E=\mathcal{F}\oplus E/\mathcal{F}
over all of
X
. Since
E
is a vector bundle, this implies
E
and
E/\mathcal{F}
are also vector bundles, and they have the same slope by Lemma
5.6
. Both
\mathcal{F}
and
E/\mathcal{F}
admit
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics, and applying the argument recursively on
\mathcal{F}
and
E/\mathcal{F}
, we get that
E
is a direct sum of
v
-slope stable vector bundles of the same slope.
∎

5.4.
Application to transversally Hermite–Einstein metrics and polynomially weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics

Although we have not yet proven the full weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence (see Section
9
), we take the opportunity here to apply the full correspondence to transversally Hermite–Einstein metrics and polynomially weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics.

Using the notation and conventions of Section
3.5
, we prove the following:

Proposition 5.8
.

The bundle
\tilde{E}\to S
admits a transversally Hermite–Einstein connection if and only if
E\to X
admits a
(v,w)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume that
E
is simple. Choose an arbitrary
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric
h
on
E
and denote by
\tilde{\theta}
the induced connection on
\tilde{E}\to S
as per Section
3.5
. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a proper non-zero
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf of
E
, inducing a proper non-zero transverse coherent saturated subsheaf
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}\subset\tilde{E}
, in the sense of
[
BH22
, Section 3.3]
. As per
[
BH22
, Definition 3.19, Proposition 3.21]
, the transverse slope of
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
is

\displaystyle\mu(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{S\backslash\pi^{-1}(V)}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(F_{\tilde{\theta}|_{\mathcal{F}}})\wedge(dA_{\xi})^{[n-1]}\wedge A_{\xi}}{\mathrm{rk}(\tilde{\mathcal{F}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{S\backslash\pi^{-1}(V)}\pi^{*}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(f\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h|_{\mathcal{F}}}-n\Phi_{h|_{\mathcal{F}}})\wedge f^{-n}\pi^{*}\omega^{[n]}\wedge f^{-1}A}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{X\backslash V}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathcal{F}}(f^{-n}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h|_{\mathcal{F}}}-nf^{-n-1}\Phi_{h|_{\mathcal{F}}})\,\omega^{[n]}}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F}),

where
V
denotes the singular locus of
\mathcal{F}
. Hence
E
is
v
-slope stable if and only if
\tilde{E}
is transversally slope stable. The weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence, the foliated Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence of
[
BH22
]
, and Lemma
2.6
immediately imply the result.
∎

Next, following the set-up of Section
3.6
, we prove:

Proposition 5.9
.

The bundle
E_{Q}\to(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
admits a Hermite–Einstein structure if and only if
E\to(X,\omega)
admits a
(p,p)
-weighted Hermite–Einstein structure.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can suppose that
E\to(X,\omega)
is simple. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset E
be a proper non-zero
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf of
E
. Then,
\mathcal{F}_{Q}:=Q\times_{\mathbb{T}}\mathcal{F}
is a coherent saturated subsheaf of
E_{Q}
. Since
E_{Q}\to(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
admits a Hermite–Einstein structure, then by the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence,
E_{Q}\to(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
is slope stable,

\mu(\mathcal{F}_{Q})<\mu(E_{Q}).

Let
\nabla
be an arbitrary
\mathbb{T}
-invariant connection on
E\to(X,\omega)
and
\nabla^{Q}
,
(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}})^{Q}
the induced bundle compatible connections on
E_{Q}\to(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
and
\mathcal{F}_{Q}\to(X_{Q},\omega_{Q})
respectively. By (
17
), we have

\begin{split}\mu(E_{Q})=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{X_{Q}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{Q}}F_{\nabla^{Q}}\right)\omega_{Q}^{[n+m]}}{\mathrm{rk}(E_{Q})([\omega_{Q}])^{[n+m]}}\\
=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{X_{Q}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{Q}}F_{\nabla^{Q}}\right)\omega_{Q}^{[n+m]}}{\mathrm{rk}(E)\int_{X_{Q}}\omega_{Q}^{[n+m]}}\\
=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{Q\times X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{\Lambda_{\omega,p}(F_{\nabla}+\Phi_{\nabla})}{p(\mu)}\right)p(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\wedge\bigwedge_{\alpha=1}^{N}\pi^{*}_{B}\omega_{\alpha}^{[n_{\alpha}]}\wedge\eta^{\ell}}{\mathrm{rk}(E)\int_{Q\times X}p(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\wedge\bigwedge_{\alpha=1}^{N}\pi^{*}_{B}\omega_{\alpha}^{[n_{\alpha}]}\wedge\eta^{\ell}}\\
=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\frac{\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(\Lambda_{\omega,p}(F_{\nabla}+\Phi_{\nabla})\right)\omega^{[n]}}{\mathrm{rk}(E)\int_{X}p(\mu)\omega^{[n]}}\\
=&\mu_{p,p}(E).\end{split}

A similar computation using the induced connection
(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}})^{Q}
on
\mathcal{F}\to(X,\omega)
, gives

\mu(\mathcal{F}_{Q})=\mu_{p,p}(\mathcal{F}).

Hence,
\mu_{p,p}(\mathcal{F})<\mu_{p,p}(E)
showing that
E\to(X,\omega)
is
(p,p)
-weighted slope stable. By Theorem
1.1
,
E\to(X,\omega)
admits a
p
-weighted Hermite–Einstein structure.
∎

Note that by uniqueness of transverse Hermite–Einstein metrics (resp. Hermite–Einstein metrics) the metric on
\tilde{E}\to S
(resp. the metric on
E_{Q}\to X_{Q}
) must be induced from a
(v,w)
-weighted (resp.
(p,p)
-weighted) Hermite–Einstein metric on
(X,\omega)
.

5.5.
The weighted Euler characteristic and weighted Gieseker stability

Let
(X,L)
be a projective Kähler manifold polarized by an ample holomorphic line bundle
L
and let
\mathbb{T}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X,L)
be a real torus acting on the total space of
L
and covering a torus action on
X
. Let
E
be a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle over
X
with
\mathcal{L}^{E}
the induced infinitesimal action on the space of sections. If
s\in H^{0}(E)
is a holomorphic section, so too is
\exp(-t\xi)s
for any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence

0=\frac{d}{dt}(\bar{\partial}\exp(-t\xi)s)=\bar{\partial}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\exp(-t\xi)s\right).

Evaluating at
t=0
we see
\bar{\partial}\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}s=0
, so
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}
acts on
H^{0}(E)
.
For a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric
h
on
E
we let
\langle-,-\rangle_{h}
be the
L^{2}
-inner product on the space of sections:

\langle s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{h}:=\int_{X}h(s,s^{\prime})\omega^{[n]}.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}h=0
, we deduce
\langle\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}s,s^{\prime}\rangle_{h}=-\langle s,\mathcal{L}_{\xi}^{E}s^{\prime}\rangle_{h}
.
It follows that for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
the operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{E}_{\xi}:=-i\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi}:\Omega^{0}(E)\to\Omega^{0}(E)
is Hermitian. Furthermore by the general property of Lie derivatives
[\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi},\mathcal{L}^{E}_{\xi^{\prime}}]=\mathcal{L}_{[\xi,\xi^{\prime}]}^{E}
it follows that
\left[\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{E}_{\xi},\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{E}_{\xi^{\prime}}\right]=0
for all
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. In summary,
\xi\mapsto\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}^{E}
defines a Lie algebra representation of
{\mathfrak{t}}
on
\mathrm{Herm}(H^{0}(E),\langle-,-\rangle_{h})
.

We consider the
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant vector bundle
E_{k}:=E\otimes L^{k}
over
X
with the induced infinitesimal action
\mathcal{L}^{(k)}:=\mathcal{L}^{E}+\mathrm{Id}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{L}^{L^{k}}
on
\Omega^{0}(E_{k})
and we denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}:=-\frac{i}{k}\mathcal{L}^{(k)}
the corresponding Hermitian operators. For
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, the spectrum (counted with multiplicity) of the Hermitian operator
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}:H^{0}(E_{k})\to H^{0}(E_{k})
is given by
\{\langle\lambda^{(k)}_{j},\xi\rangle\mid\lambda_{j}^{(k)}\in W_{k}\}
where
W_{k}=\{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}\mid j=1,\cdots,h^{0}(E_{k})\}\subset\Lambda^{*}
is the finite set of weights of the complexified action of
\mathbb{T}
on
H^{0}(E_{k})
and
\Lambda^{*}
is the dual of the lattice
\Lambda
of circle subgroups of
\mathbb{T}
.

Let
P_{L}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the momentum polytope associated to
(X,L,\mathbb{T})
. We set
h^{k}:=h\otimes(h_{L})^{\otimes k}
where
h
is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric on
E
and
h_{L}
is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric on
L
whose curvature 2-form (times
i/2\pi
) is
\omega
, let
\lambda_{j}^{(k)}\in W_{k}
,
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
, and take
s_{j}\in H^{0}(E_{k})
an eigensection of
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}
associated to the eigenvalue
\langle\lambda_{j}^{(k)},\xi\rangle
. Noting that the moment map for
F_{h^{k}}=F_{h}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{L^{k}}+\mathrm{Id}_{E}\otimes F_{L^{k}}
is
\Phi_{h^{k}}=\Phi_{h}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{L^{k}}+\mathrm{Id}_{E}\otimes k\Phi_{h_{L}}
, we compute

\begin{split}\langle\lambda_{j}^{(k)},\xi\rangle|s_{j}|^{2}_{h^{k}}=&h^{k}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}s_{j},s_{j}\right)\\
=&-h^{k}\left(\frac{i}{k}(\nabla^{h^{k}}_{\xi}-\Phi^{\xi}_{h})(s_{j})-\mu^{\xi}s_{j},s_{j}\right)\\
=&-\frac{i}{k}(\partial|s_{j}|_{h^{k}}^{2})(\xi)+\mu^{\xi}|s_{j}|^{2}_{h^{k}}+\frac{i}{k}h^{k}(\Phi^{\xi}_{h}(s_{j}),s_{j})\end{split}

where we use
s_{j}\in H^{0}(E_{k})
in the last equality to conclude
h^{k}(\nabla^{h^{k}}s_{j},s_{j})=\partial|s_{j}|_{h^{k}}^{2}
. At a global maximum
x_{j,k}\in X
of the smooth function
x\mapsto|s_{j}|^{2}_{h^{k}}(x)
, we get

\langle\lambda_{j}^{(k)},\xi\rangle=\mu^{\xi}(x_{j,k})+\frac{1}{k}\frac{h^{k}_{x_{j,k}}\big([i\Phi^{\xi}_{h}(s_{j})](x_{j,k}),s_{j}(x_{j,k})\big)}{|s_{j}|^{2}_{h^{k}}(x_{j,k})}.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we obtain

|\langle\lambda_{j}^{(k)}-\mu(x_{j,k}),\xi\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{k}\frac{|i\Phi^{\xi}_{h}(s_{j})|_{h_{k}}(x_{j,k})}{|s_{j}|_{h^{k}}(x_{j,k})}\leq\frac{1}{k}|\Phi^{\xi}_{h}|_{h}(x_{j,k})

where
|\Phi^{\xi}_{h}|_{h}
is the pointwise endomorphism norm. Given a basis
(\xi_{a})_{a=1,\cdots,\ell}
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-generators of
{\mathfrak{t}}
, for all
k\geq 1
we have

\begin{split}||\lambda_{j}^{(k)}-\mu(x_{j,k})||_{{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}}&=\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}|\langle\lambda_{j}^{(k)}-\mu(x_{j,k}),\xi_{a}\rangle|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}|\Phi^{\xi_{a}}_{h}|_{h}(x_{j,k})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{k}\sum_{a=1}^{\ell}\max_{X}|\Phi^{\xi_{a}}_{h}|_{h}\\
&\to 0\end{split}

as
k\to\infty
.
Thus, for any compact set
Q\subset{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
whose interior contains
P_{L}
, there exists an integer
K>0
such that for all
k\geq K
we have

W_{k}\subset Q.

In particular, for
k\gg 0
, the sets
W_{k}
are almost contained in
P_{L}
. In fact, one can show that the convex hulls of the
W_{k}
approach
P_{L}
in a suitable sense, by considering for each vertex
\lambda\in P_{L}
a corresponding weight
s\in H^{0}(L)
, and tensoring an arbitrary eigensection of
E_{\ell}
for some
\ell
with
s^{\otimes k}
to get an eigensection of
E_{\ell+k}
, then sending
k\to\infty
. The corresponding weights of the eigensections of
E_{\ell+k}
tend towards
\lambda
in the limit.

Let
v\in C^{\infty}(P_{L})
a weight function. Given an extension of
v
to a compactly supported smooth function on
{\mathfrak{t}}^{*}
, also denoted by
v
, one can use the weight decomposition of
H^{0}(E_{k})
as
a direct sum of common eigenspaces for the commuting operators
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}
,
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
,

H^{0}(E_{k})=\bigoplus_{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}\in W_{k}}H^{0}(E_{k})_{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}},\quad H^{0}(E_{k})_{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}}:=\{s\in H^{0}(E_{k})|\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}s=\lambda_{j}^{(k)}\otimes s\},

to define an operator
v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}):H^{0}(E_{k})\to H^{0}(E_{k})
by

v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)})_{\mid H^{0}(E_{k})_{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}}}=v(\lambda_{j}^{(k)})\mathrm{Id}_{H^{0}(E_{k})_{\lambda_{j}^{(k)}}}.

Let
\hat{v}
denote the Fourier transform of
v
. Using the Fourier inversion formula, we get an alternative formulation of the above operator
v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}):H^{0}(E_{k})\to H^{0}(E_{k})
,

v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)})=\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}}\underline{d\xi}.

By,
[
BGV04
, Theorem 8.2]
(see also
[
Gau10
, Remark 8.9.1]
for the case
E=\mathcal{O}
) the equivariant Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula reads

(23)

\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^{j}\mathrm{tr}_{\mid H^{j}(E_{k})}\left(e^{k\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}^{(k)}}\right)=\int_{X}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathbb{T}}(E_{k})\mathrm{Todd}^{\mathbb{T}}(X).

By the Kodaira–Nakano–Akizuki vanishing theorem (see e.g.
[
Kob14
]
) there is an integer
k_{0}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
we have

H^{j}(E_{k})=\{0\},\quad\forall j\geq 1.

Thus, for
k\geq k_{0}
,

(24)

\mathrm{tr}_{\mid H^{0}(E_{k})}\left(e^{k\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}^{(k)}}\right)=\int_{X}\mathrm{ch}^{\mathbb{T}}(E_{k})\mathrm{Todd}^{\mathbb{T}}(X).

Definition 5.10
.

The
v
-weighted Euler characteristic of
E_{k}
is defined by

\chi_{v}(E_{k}):=\mathrm{tr}_{\mid H^{0}(E_{k})}\left(v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}^{(k)})\right),\quad\forall k\geq k_{0}.

Unfortunately, this definition depends on the choice of extension of
v
to all of
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. However, we will now show that there is an asymptotic expansion of this invariant as
k\to\infty
, which does not depend on the choice of extension—the intuition for this comes from the discussion above, where we note the convex hull of
W_{k}
converges to
P_{L}
as
k\to\infty
. To compute the expansion, we use the equivariant Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem. By (
24
), for all
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
we have

\mathrm{tr}_{\mid H^{0}(E_{k})}\left(e^{i\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}^{(k)}}\right)=\int_{X}(\mathrm{ch}^{\mathbb{T}}(E_{k})\mathrm{Todd}^{\mathbb{T}}(X))\left(\frac{i}{k}\xi\right)

Integrating both sides against the measure
\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}
on
{\mathfrak{t}}
, we obtain (using
F_{E}+\Phi_{E}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})
for notational simplicity),

\begin{split}\chi_{v}(E_{k})=&\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}(\mathrm{ch}^{\mathbb{T}}(E_{k})\mathrm{Todd}^{\mathbb{T}}(X))\left(\frac{i}{k}\xi\right)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(e^{F_{E}+\frac{i}{k}\Phi^{\xi}_{E}+(k\omega+i\mu^{\xi})\mathrm{Id}_{E}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\frac{i}{k}\Delta(\mu^{\xi}))+\cdots\right)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(e^{F_{E}+\frac{i}{k}\Phi^{\xi}_{E}}\right)e^{k\omega+i\mu^{\xi}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\frac{i}{k}\Delta(\mu^{\xi}))+\cdots\right)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(e^{F_{E}+\frac{i}{k}\Phi^{\xi}_{E}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)+\frac{i}{k}\Delta(\mu^{\xi}))+\cdots\right)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n}k^{j}\omega^{[j]}\right)e^{i\mu^{\xi}}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&k^{n}\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_{E})\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\mu^{\xi}}\omega^{[n]}\underline{d\xi}\\
&+k^{n-1}\int_{\mathfrak{t}}\int_{X}\left[\vphantom{\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_{E})}{2}}\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}+i\Phi^{\xi}_{E}\omega^{[n]})\right.\\
&\left.\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_{E})}{2}(\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}+i\Delta_{\omega}(\mu^{\xi})\omega^{[n]})\right]\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\mu^{\xi}}\underline{d\xi}\\
&+k^{n-2}(\cdots)\\
=&k^{n}\mathrm{rk}(E)\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\\
&+k^{n-1}\int_{X}\left[\mathrm{tr}(v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E}+\langle\Phi_{E},dv(\mu)\rangle)+\frac{\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2}(v(\mu)\mathrm{Scal}_{\omega}+\langle\Delta_{\omega}(\mu),dv(\mu)\rangle)\right]\omega^{[n]}\\
&+k^{n-2}(\cdots)\\
=&k^{n}\mathrm{rk}(E)\int_{X}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}+k^{n-1}\int_{X}\left(\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{E}+\Phi_{E}))+\frac{\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2}\mathrm{Scal}_{v}(\omega)\right)\omega^{[n]}+\cdots\\
=&\mathrm{rk}(E)\left(v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)k^{n}+\left(\left(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)+\frac{\mathrm{rk}(E)}{2}\left(c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)\right)k^{n-1}+\cdots\end{split}

where
\mathrm{Scal}_{v}(\omega)
is the weighted scalar curvature of
\omega
(see
[
Lah19
, Definition 1]
) and we use that

\int_{X}\mathrm{Scal}_{v}(\omega)\omega^{[n]}=\int_{X}\left[v(\mu)\mathrm{Scal}_{\omega}+\langle\Delta_{\omega}(\mu),dv(\mu)\rangle\right]\omega^{[n]}=\left(c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right).

To recapitulate,

Proposition 5.11
.

The
v
-weighted Euler characteristic of

admits an asymptotic expansion for

\frac{\chi_{v}(E_{k})}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}=\left(v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)k^{n}+\left(\mu_{v}(E)+\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)\right)k^{n-1}+\cdots

where
\mu_{v}(E)
is the
v
-weighted slope of
E
.

For any
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant coherent saturated subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
, the
v
-weighted Euler characteristic of
\mathcal{F}
is similarly defined by

\chi_{v}(\mathcal{F}_{k}):=\mathrm{tr}_{|H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k})}\left(v(\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\bullet})\right),\quad\forall k\gg 0

where
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{(k)}_{\xi}:H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{F}_{k})
is the induced infinitesimal action of elements

. As in Proposition

-equivariant Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch formula for

gives the asymptotic expansion,

\frac{\chi_{v}(\mathcal{F}_{k})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathcal{F})}=\left(v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)k^{n}+\left(\mu_{v}(\mathcal{F})+\frac{1}{2}\left(c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(c_{1}(L)_{\mathbb{T}})\right)\right)k^{n-1}+\cdots

Definition 5.12
.

Let

\mathbb{T}

The following is a straightforward consequence of the polynomial expansions of the weighted Euler characteristic.

If

Proof.

Indeed, for any proper non-zero

). We leave the differential geometric counterpart of weighted Gieseker stability for future work.

6.
Weighted Kobayashi–Lübke inequality

In this section we prove a weighted analogue of the Kobayashi–Lübke inequality
[
Bog78
,
Gie79
,
Lüb82
]
for vector bundles admitting weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics. We recall the usual Kobayashi–Lübke inequality is stated as follows: if
E
admits a Hermite–Einstein metric, then

(r-1)(c_{1}(E)^{2}\alpha^{n-2})\leq 2r(c_{2}(E)\alpha^{n-2}),

where
r
is the rank of
E
,
\alpha=[\omega]
denotes the Kähler class, and the expressions
(c_{1}(E)^{2}\alpha^{n-2})
and
(c_{2}(E)\alpha^{n-2})
denote ordinary (i.e. non-equivariant) intersection numbers.

Given the equivariant intersection theory, it is fairly straightforward to guess the correct equivariant analogue of this inequality—indeed it is simply

(r-1)(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))\leq 2r(c_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})).

However, we will also require the weight function
v
to satisfy a certain Hessian inequality.

Theorem 6.1
.

Suppose that
E
admits a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric, and that
v
satisfies the inequality

(25)

\mathrm{Hess}(v)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv\otimes dv}{v}\leq 0.

Then

(r-1)(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}))\leq 2r(c_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})).

Furthermore, equality holds if and only if
E
is projectively flat.

Before launching into the proof, we shall verify that the inequality is satisfied by all of the important examples of weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics. Many of these examples stipulate that
v
is of the form
v(\mu)=\tilde{v}(\langle\mu,\xi\rangle)
for some
\xi\in{\mathfrak{t}}
. One observes that the inequality is then equivalent to

\tilde{v}^{\prime\prime}(x)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{\tilde{v}^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{\tilde{v}(x)}\leq 0.

(1)

When
\tilde{v}=1
(Hermite–Einstein case), the inequality reduces to
0\leq 0
;

(2)

When
\tilde{v}(x)=e^{x}
(KR-soliton case), the inequality reduces to
1\leq\frac{n+1}{n}
;

(3)

When
\tilde{v}(x)=x^{-n}
(Sasaki case), the inequality reduces to
0\leq 0
.

Lastly, we consider the polynomial weight

p(\mu):=\prod_{\alpha=1}^{N}(c_{\alpha}+\langle\mu,p_{\alpha}\rangle)^{n_{\alpha}}

from Section
3.6
. A straightforward computation gives

{\rm Hess}(\log p)(\mu)=-\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}\left(\frac{n_{\alpha}}{(\langle\mu,p_{\alpha}\rangle+c_{\alpha})^{2}}\right)p_{\alpha}\otimes p_{\alpha}\leq 0.

Since

\mathrm{Hess}(p)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dp\otimes dp}{p}\leq\mathrm{Hess}(p)-\frac{dp\otimes dp}{p}=\mathrm{Hess}(\log p)\leq 0,

we see that
p
also satisfies the desired inequality.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Using the identity
{\rm ch}_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}=\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}(E)^{2}_{\mathbb{T}}-2c_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}})
, the weighted Kobayashi–Lübke inequality is equivalent to

(26)

2r\left({\rm ch}_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\right)\leq\left(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\right).

We first compute these equivariant intersection numbers using equivariant representatives. Letting
h
be the weighted Hermite–Einstein metric, we define
F_{E}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}F_{h}
and
\Phi_{E}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}\Phi_{h}
, and compute

\begin{split}&\left(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\right)\\
=&\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot e^{\alpha_{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(i\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{X}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left(\left(\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}+\Phi_{E})\right)^{2}\cdot e^{\omega+\mu}\right)(i\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{X}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\left((\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}))^{2}+(\mathrm{tr}(i\langle\Phi_{E},\xi\rangle))^{2}+2\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})\mathrm{tr}(i\langle\Phi_{E},\xi\rangle)\right)e^{\omega+i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\hat{v}(\xi)\underline{d\xi}\\
=&\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right)(\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}))^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}\\
&+2\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\mathrm{tr}(i\langle\Phi_{E},\xi\rangle)\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
&+\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\mathrm{tr}(i\langle\Phi_{E},\xi\rangle))^{2}\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}+2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
&+\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),\mathrm{Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

In the last equality we use (
20
), which in particular gives

\begin{split}&\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\mathrm{tr}(i\langle\Phi_{E},\xi\rangle))^{2}\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\left(\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}\langle\mathrm{tr}(i\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(i\Phi_{E}),\xi\otimes\xi\rangle\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\left\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),i^{2}\int_{{\mathfrak{t}}}(\xi\otimes\xi)\hat{v}(\xi)e^{i\langle\mu,\xi\rangle}\underline{d\xi}\right\rangle\omega^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),\mathrm{Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

Thus we obtain

(27)

\begin{split}\left(c_{1}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}^{2}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\right)=&\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}+2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
&+\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),\mathrm{Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

A similar calculation gives

(28)

\begin{split}2\left({\rm ch}_{2}(E)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot v(\alpha_{\mathbb{T}})\right)=&\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}+2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
&+\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E}),\mathrm{Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}.\\
\end{split}

We reformulate the following term from (
27
):

\begin{split}&2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
=&2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})\omega^{[n]}\\
=&\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}\left(c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E}-v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E}\right)\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{c_{v}}{v(\mu)}\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&-\int_{X}(\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E}))^{2}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}+rc_{v}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})\omega^{[n]}\\
&+rc_{v}\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}-\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

The analogous term from (
28
) is given by

\begin{split}&2\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}\Phi_{E}),dv(\mu)\rangle\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}\\
=&2\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle)v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&-\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2})v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}+c_{v}\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})\omega^{[n]}\\
&+c_{v}\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}-\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2})v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

The terms involving
c_{v}
cancel out in (
26
). Hence the inequality (
26
) is equivalent to

(29)

\begin{split}&r\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-r\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2})v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\\
&+r\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E}),{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}-r\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}(\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2})v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\\
\leq&\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}(F_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-\int_{X}\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E}))^{2}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}-\int_{X}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2}v(\mu)\omega^{[n]}\end{split}

Using the following identities

(30)

\begin{split}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2}=&\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\\
\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle^{2}=&\langle\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle,\end{split}

the inequality (
29
) is equivalent to

(31)

\begin{split}0\leq&\int_{X}\left\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})-r\,\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E}),\left[{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)-\frac{dv(\mu)\otimes dv(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\right]\right\rangle\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\int_{X}v(\mu)\left([(\mathrm{tr}\,F_{E})^{2}-r\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}^{2})]\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}+[r\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2})-(\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2}]\omega^{[n]}\right),\end{split}

At this point we introduce the endomorphism valued 2-form
\tilde{F}_{E}
given by

(32)

\tilde{F}_{E}:=F_{E}+\frac{1}{n}\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\otimes\omega

and notice that the
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein equation is the same as
v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E}=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E}
. We write each term in the integral from the second line of (
31
) in terms of
\tilde{F}_{E}
:

\begin{split}(\mathrm{tr}\,F_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=&(\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde{F}_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-\frac{2r(n-1)c_{v}}{nv(\mu)}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\frac{n-1}{n}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\omega^{[n]},\\
\mathrm{tr}(F_{E}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=&\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-\frac{2(n-1)c_{v}}{nv(\mu)}\mathrm{tr}\left(\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\frac{n-1}{n}\mathrm{tr}(\langle\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle)\omega^{[n]},\\
(\mathrm{tr}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2}=&(\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E}))^{2}-\frac{2rc_{v}}{v(\mu)}\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\\
&+\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}),d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle,\\
\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}F_{E})^{2})=&\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2})-\frac{2c_{v}}{v(\mu)}\mathrm{tr}\left(\langle\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\right)\\
&+\mathrm{tr}\left(\langle\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E},d(\log v)(\mu)\otimes d(\log v)(\mu)\rangle\right),\end{split}

where we use the
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein condition
v(\mu)\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E}=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E}
and (
30
) to simplify the quadratic terms involving
\Phi_{E}
. Substituting back in (
31
) we get the equivalent inequality

(33)

\begin{split}0\leq&\int_{X}\left\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})-r\,\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E}),\left[{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv(\mu)\otimes dv(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\right]\right\rangle\omega^{[n]}\\
&+\int_{X}v(\mu)\left([(\mathrm{tr}\,\tilde{F}_{E})^{2}-r\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E}^{2})]\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}+[r\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2})-(\mathrm{tr}\,\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2}]\omega^{[n]}\right).\end{split}

We consider the integral from the second line of (
33
). Using the following formula

(34)

\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=(\Lambda_{\omega}\alpha\Lambda_{\omega}\beta-\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{\omega})\omega^{[n]}

which holds for any real
(1,1)
-forms
\alpha,\beta
, we get

\tilde{F}_{E}^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=\left((\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})\circ(\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})-|\tilde{F}_{E}|_{\omega}^{2}\right)\otimes\omega^{[n]}

where
|\tilde{F}_{E}|^{2}_{\omega}
is the endomorphism given by the product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\omega}
on the differential form component of
\tilde{F}_{E}
and composition on the endomorphism component of
\tilde{F}_{E}
. Taking the trace of the endomorphism component of both sides gives

(35)

\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E}^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=\left(\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2})-\mathrm{tr}(|\tilde{F}_{E}|_{\omega}^{2})\right)\omega^{[n]}.

Applying (
34
) for
\alpha=\beta=\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})
gives

(36)

(\mathrm{tr}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}=\left((\Lambda_{\omega}\mathrm{tr}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2}-|\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})|_{\omega}^{2}\right)\omega^{[n]}.

combining (
35
) and (
36
) we obtain

\begin{split}&r\left(\mathrm{tr}((\tilde{F}_{E})^{2})\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-\mathrm{tr}((\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E})^{2})\omega^{[n]}\right)-\left((\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E}))^{2}\wedge\omega^{[n-2]}-(\mathrm{tr}(\Lambda_{\omega}\tilde{F}_{E}))^{2}\omega^{[n]}\right)\\
=&-r\left(\mathrm{tr}(|\tilde{F}_{E}|_{\omega}^{2})-\frac{1}{r}|\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})|_{\omega}^{2}\right)\omega^{[n]}\\
=&-r\mathrm{tr}\left(|\tilde{F}_{E}|_{\omega}^{2}-\frac{2}{r}\langle\tilde{F}_{E},\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})\rangle+\frac{1}{r^{2}}|\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})|_{\omega}^{2}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\\
=&-r\mathrm{tr}\left|\tilde{F}_{E}-\frac{1}{r}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right|_{\omega}^{2}.\end{split}

So the inequality (
33
) has become

\begin{split}0\leq&\int_{X}\left\langle\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})\otimes\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E})-r\,\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}\circ\Phi_{E}),\left[{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv(\mu)\otimes dv(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\right]\right\rangle\omega^{[n]}\\
&+r\int_{X}v(\mu)\mathrm{tr}\left|\tilde{F}_{E}-\frac{1}{r}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right|_{\omega}^{2}\omega^{[n]}.\end{split}

We have assumed that

(37)

{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv(\mu)\otimes dv(\mu)}{v(\mu)}\leq 0.

Choosing (pointwise on
X
) a basis
(\xi_{a})_{1\leq a\leq\ell}
such that
{\rm Hess}(v)(\mu)-\frac{n+1}{n}\frac{dv(\mu)\otimes dv(\mu)}{v(\mu)}
is diagonal, it is enough to show that

\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}^{\xi_{a}})^{2}-r\mathrm{tr}(\Phi_{E}^{\xi_{a}}\circ\Phi_{E}^{\xi_{a}})\leq 0,

Since
\mathrm{tr}(H)^{2}\leq r\mathrm{tr}(H^{2})
holds for arbitrary Hermitian matrices, noting that
\Phi_{E}^{\xi_{a}}
is Hermitian gives the desired inequality. Hence we have proven the inequality (
26
).

Note that the weighted Kobayashi–Lübke inequality is an equality if and only if
\tilde{F}_{E}=\frac{1}{r}\mathrm{tr}(\tilde{F}_{E})\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{E}
and
\Phi_{E}=\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{E}
, in which case
E
is projectively flat.
∎

) is clearly satisfied by the weight

Remark 6.3
.

Let

be a weight function and
\Delta(v)
the intersection number

Then, from the calculations in the proof of Theorem

where
K_{v}(h):=\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{h}+\Phi_{h})

(E,h)
and

\int_{X}|K_{v}(h)^{\circ}|^{2}\frac{\omega^{[n]}}{v(\mu)}+\frac{\Delta(v)}{r}\geq\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}{\rm YM}_{v}(h).

) becomes an equality

7.
Extensions of tangent bundles of Kähler–Ricci solitons and bounds on weighted Ricci curvature

In
[
Tia92
]
, Tian showed that
the stability of certain natural extensions of the holomorphic tangent bundle can be used to bound the maximal possible positive lower bound of the Ricci curvature of Kähler metrics in the anticanonical polarization. In this section, we first show in Theorem
7.1
that certain equivariant extensions of the tangent bundle of a Kähler–Ricci soliton admit natural metrics. These metrics, despite not being weighted Hermite–Einstein, are then used to generalise Tian’s observation to lower bounds of the “weighted” Ricci curvature
{\rm Ric}(\omega)-\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\mu^{\xi}
, in Theorem
7.2
.

7.1.
Equivariant extensions of equivariant vector bundles

We start by recalling some natural constructions on extensions of equivariant vector bundles. Let
E_{1}

\mathbb{T}

\mathbb{T}
-manifold

\bar{\partial}_{\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2})}

(0,1)

E_{2}

\mathbb{T}

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(X,\xi)
be a Kähler–Ricci soliton with canonically normalized momentum polytope

\gamma\in\mathbb{R}

where

are weight functions to be specified. By (

(43)

\begin{cases}\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\nabla^{f},\nabla^{u})\Psi^{\omega}\right)+\langle\Psi^{\omega},\xi\rangle=0\\
\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}(F_{e^{f}h_{\omega}}-\gamma^{2}(\Psi^{\omega})^{\dagger}\wedge\Psi^{\omega})+\Phi_{e^{f}h_{\omega}}^{\xi}\right)=w_{1}(\mu)\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}\\
\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}(F_{h_{\mathcal{O}}}-\gamma^{2}\Psi^{\omega}\wedge(\Psi^{\omega})^{\dagger})+\Phi_{h_{\mathcal{O}}}^{\xi}\right)=w_{2}(\mu)\end{cases}

where
\nabla^{f}

(TX^{1,0},e^{f}h_{\omega})

For
\Psi\in\Omega^{0,1}(\mathrm{Hom}(E_{1},E_{2}))=\Gamma(\Lambda^{0,1}\otimes E_{1}^{*}\otimes E_{2})

\Psi

\Psi_{W}(e_{1})\in E_{2},

gives

\Lambda_{\omega}(\Psi^{\dagger}\wedge\Psi_{\omega})=-ie^{u-f}\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}},\quad\Lambda_{\omega}(\Psi\wedge\Psi^{\dagger})=ine^{u-f}.

Plugging (

) in (

\begin{cases}\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\nabla^{f},\nabla^{u})\Psi\right)+\langle\Psi,\xi\rangle=0\\

\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h_{\mathcal{O}}}+\Phi_{h_{\mathcal{O}}}^{\xi}\right)=w_{2}(\mu)-\frac{n\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{u-f}.\end{cases}

We consider the first equation in (

): for all
V\in TX^{1,0}
we have

\begin{split}(\mathrm{Hom}(\nabla^{f},\nabla^{u})\Psi)V=&\Psi(\nabla^{f}V)+\nabla^{u}(\Psi(V))\\
=&\Psi\left(\nabla^{\omega}V+\partial f\otimes V\right)+d(\omega(V,\cdot))+\partial u\wedge(\omega(V,\cdot))\\
=&\Psi\left(\nabla^{\omega}V\right)-\partial f\wedge\Psi(V)+d(\omega(V,\cdot))+\partial u\wedge(\omega(V,\cdot))\\
=&-\omega(\nabla^{\omega}V,\cdot)+(\nabla^{\omega}\omega)(V,\cdot)+\omega(\nabla^{\omega}V,\cdot)+\partial(u-f)\wedge\omega(V,\cdot)\\
=&\partial(u-f)\wedge\omega(V,\cdot)\end{split}

where
\nabla^{u}=d+\partial u
and
\nabla^{f}=\nabla^{\omega}+\partial f
are the Chern connections of
h_{\mathcal{O}}=e^{u}
of
e^{f}h_{\omega}
respectively. It follows that

\displaystyle(\Lambda_{\omega}\left(\mathrm{Hom}(\nabla^{f},\nabla^{u})\Psi\right)+\langle\Psi,\xi\rangle)V

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\omega}(\partial(u-f)\wedge\omega(V,\cdot))+\omega(V,\xi^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=\partial(u-f)(V)+\partial\mu^{\xi}(V).

Hence, the first equation in (
45
) is equivalent to

(46)

u-f=-\mu_{\xi},

where without loss of generality we choose the normalising constant
0
.
For the second equation in (
45
), since
\omega
is a Kähler–Ricci soliton then we have

\begin{split}\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{e^{f}h_{\omega}}+\Phi_{e^{f}h_{\omega}}^{\xi}\right)=&\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h_{\omega}}+\Phi_{h_{\omega}}^{\xi}\right)+\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(f)\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}\\
=&\left(1+\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(f)\right)\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}},\end{split}

where
\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(f):=\partial^{*}(e^{\mu_{\xi}}\partial f)
(see the proof of Lemma
2.6
).
Thus the second equation in (
45
) is equivalent to

1+\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(f)=w_{1}(\mu)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{u-f}.

For the last equation in (
45
), we have
F_{h_{\mathcal{O}}}=\frac{i}{2}dd^{c}u
with a bundle momentum map
\Phi^{\mathcal{O}}
given by

\begin{split}\Phi_{\mathcal{O}}^{\xi}(s)=&\nabla^{\mathcal{O}}_{\xi}s-\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{O}}_{\xi}s\\
=&(ds)(\xi)+\frac{1}{2}(du(\xi)+i(d^{c}u)(\xi))s-(ds)(\xi)\\
=&\frac{i}{2}(d^{c}u)(\xi)s=\frac{i}{2}\langle d\mu_{\xi},du\rangle s.\end{split}

where
du(\xi)=0

\mathbb{T}
-invariance of
u
and
\mathcal{L}^{\mathcal{O}}_{\xi}s=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&0\\
0&\partial_{\theta}\end{pmatrix}s=(ds)(\xi)
since a section

is constant along the fibers. Thus, the last equation of (
45
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(u)=w_{2}(\mu)-\frac{n\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{u-f}.

Thus, the system (
45
) is equivalent to

(47)

\begin{cases}u-f=-\mu_{\xi}\\
1+\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(f)=w_{1}(\mu)+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{u-f}\\
\frac{1}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(u)=w_{2}(\mu)-\frac{n\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{u-f}.\end{cases}

We suppose that the momentum polytope is canonically normalized so that for all
\zeta\in{\mathfrak{t}}
,

e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\Delta_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(\mu_{\zeta})=\mu_{\zeta}.

For the third equation, taking

gives

Substituting in the second equation we get

To recapitulate, for all

the Hermitian metric

on the extension

solves the equation

\frac{i}{2\pi}\left(\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h_{E}}+\Phi_{h_{E}}^{\xi}\right)=\begin{pmatrix}\left(1+\frac{1}{\pi}\mu_{\xi}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\right)\mathrm{Id}_{TX^{1,0}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{2\pi}\mu_{\xi}+\frac{n\gamma^{2}}{2\pi}e^{-\mu_{\xi}}\end{pmatrix}.

Since

splits smoothly as a direct sum

of complex vector bundles,

\begin{split}\mu_{e^{\mu_{\xi}}}(E_{[\gamma\Psi^{\omega}]})=&\frac{\left(c_{1}(E_{[\gamma\Psi^{\omega}]})_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(\xi)}{n+1}\\
=&\frac{\left(c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}\cdot e^{c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(\xi)}{n+1}\\
=&\frac{n}{n+1}\left(e^{c_{1}(X)_{\mathbb{T}}}\right)(\xi),\end{split}

as may be verified directly from the definitions of equivariant intersection numbers.
∎

4
4

4

.

Let

-invariant geodesic path

\partial_{t}\Phi_{h_{t}}=\partial(h_{t}^{-1}\dot{h}_{t}),\quad\quad\partial_{t}\Lambda_{\omega}F_{h_{t}}=\bar{\partial}\partial(h_{t}^{-1}\dot{h}_{t}),

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\mathcal{M}_{v}(h_{t},h^{\prime})

where

denotes the weighted del-Laplacian on endomorphisms of

As a consequence of the above Lemma, we deduce the uniqueness modulo scaling of weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics.

Theorem 8.3
.

A weighted Hermite–Einstein metric on a
\mathbb{T}
-equivariant simple vector bundle is unique up to scaling.

Proof.

Let
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
be two weighted Hermite–Einstein metrics, and let

for

be the unique geodesic joining them. Since

, it follows that

is Hermitian, it further follows that

9.
The weighted Kobayashi–Hitchin correspondence

In this section we prove the following.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
E
be a simple vector bundle. If
E
is
v
-weighted slope stable, then
E
admits a
v
-weighted Hermite–Einstein metric.

The proof will follow closely the method of Uhlenbeck–Yau
[
UY86
]
, namely using a continuity path. In particular, we follow the exposition in the book by Lübke–Teleman
[
LT95
]
. The weighted setting introduces few new challenges; we shall touch on the points that require explanation, but otherwise state results without proof where the adaptation is straightforward, referring to
[
LT95
]
for more details.

9.1.
Set-up of the problem

We are searching for a weighted Hermite–Einstein metric in the space of
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metrics on the vector bundle
E
. Given a reference metric
h_{0}
, any other
\mathbb{T}
-invariant metric
h
may then be identified with a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant positive self-adjoint section
f\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})^{\mathbb{T}}
as follows. The endomorphism
f
is the unique endomorphism satisfying

h(e_{1},e_{2})=h_{0}(fe_{1},e_{2})

for all
e_{1},e_{2}\in\Gamma(E)
. With respect to a local holomorphic frame for
E
, the above equation may be written

h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=f_{\alpha}^{\gamma}h_{0,\gamma\bar{\beta}},

or equivalently

f_{\alpha}^{\beta}=h_{0}^{\beta\bar{\gamma}}h_{\bar{\gamma}\alpha},

which we sometimes abbreviate as
f=h_{0}^{-1}h
. Conversely, given any
f\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E,h_{0})^{\mathbb{T}}
, the sesquilinear form
h(e_{1},e_{2}):=h_{0}(fe_{1},e_{2})
is a
\mathbb{T}
-invariant Hermitian metric on
E
.

Fixing a local holomorphic frame for
E
, and writing
A_{0}
and
A
for the connection matrices of
h_{0}
and
h
respectively, one computes

A=A_{0}+f^{-1}\partial_{0}f,

where
\partial_{0}f:=(\nabla^{h_{0}}f)^{1,0}
, with
\nabla^{h_{0}}
denoting the canonical extension of the Chern connection of
h_{0}
to
\mathrm{End}E
. As per (
6
), the difference between both the curvature and the moment map can be expressed succinctly as

(63)

F+\Phi=F_{0}+\Phi_{0}+\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f).

We also have the useful formula

\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\theta)\omega^{[n]}=\bar{\partial}(v(\mu)\theta)\wedge\omega^{[n-1]}

for
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(X)^{\mathbb{T}}
or
\theta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{End}(E))^{\mathbb{T}}
, so that

\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\theta)=\Lambda_{\omega}(\bar{\partial}(v(\mu)\theta)).

In particular, the weighted
\partial_{0}
-Laplacian of
E
(or
\mathrm{End}E
) is expressed as

\Delta_{v}(f):=\partial_{0}^{*}(v(\mu)\partial_{0}f)=i\Lambda_{\omega,v}\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\partial_{0}f=i\Lambda_{\omega,v}\bar{\partial}(v(\mu)\partial_{0}f).

Similar formulae hold for the weighted
\partial
-Laplacian on functions, which we also denote
\Delta_{v}
.

Using this information, we can see that finding
h
which solves the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation is equivalent to finding
f
which satisfies

\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{0}+\Phi_{0})+\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f))=c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

To solve the weighted Hermite–Einstein equation we set up a continuity method following Uhlenbeck–Yau
[
UY86
]
, perturbing the equation to a family of equations depending on a parameter
\epsilon\geq 0
:

L_{\epsilon}(f):=\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{0}+\Phi_{0})-c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f))+\epsilon\log f=0.

For convenience we introduce the notations

K=K_{h_{0}}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(F_{0}+\Phi_{0}),

and

K^{0}=K_{h_{0}}^{0}=K-c_{v}\mathrm{Id}_{E},

so that

L_{\epsilon}(f)=K^{0}+\frac{i}{2\pi}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f))+\epsilon\log f.

We lastly note one assumption that can be made. First, we may normalise our reference metric
h_{0}
so that
\mathrm{tr}(K^{0})=0
, which follows from the linearisation of the trace of the weighted Hermite–Einstein operator giving the weighted Laplacian; see the proof of Lemma
2.6
. We then have:

Lemma 9.2
.

If
\mathrm{tr}(K^{0})=0
, then a solution
f
to
L_{\epsilon}(f)=0
satisfies
\det f=1
.

Proof.

Taking the trace of the equation
L_{\epsilon}(f)=0
gives

\frac{i}{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f))+\epsilon\mathrm{tr}\log f=0.

Now,

\displaystyle i\mathrm{tr}\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f))=

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(\mathrm{tr}(f^{-1}\partial_{0}f)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega,v}(\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}(\partial(\mathrm{tr}\log f)))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{v}(\mathrm{tr}\log f).

Here between the first and second lines,
\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}
changes from the equivariant del-bar operator on
\Omega^{1,0}(\mathrm{End}(E))
to the equivariant del-bar operator on
\Omega^{1,0}(X)
, defined by
\langle\bar{\partial}^{\mathrm{eq}}\theta,\xi\rangle=\bar{\partial}\theta+\iota_{\xi}\theta
for
\theta\in\Omega^{1,0}(X)
. It follows that
\Delta_{v}(\mathrm{tr}\log f)+2\pi\epsilon\mathrm{tr}\log f=0
. Since the eigenvalues of the weighted
\partial
-Laplacian

are non-negative, it follows that
\mathrm{tr}\log f=0
, so
\det f=e^{\mathrm{tr}\log f}=1
.
∎

\Delta_{v}(|\eta|^{2})+2\epsilon|\eta|^{2}\leq 0.

We now note that since

satisfies the maximum principle. Indeed, suppose

, implying

\varphi=f^{-1/2}\eta f^{-1/2}=0
. Since

is an elliptic operator with index

and trivial kernel, it is an isomorphism.
∎

It immediately follows from the inverse function theorem that the set of

for which

admits a smooth solution, is open.

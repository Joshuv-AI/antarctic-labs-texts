2D Toda τ \tau Functions, Weighted Hurwitz Numbers and the Cayley Graph: Determinant Representation and Recursion Formula
Xiang-Mao DING, Xiang Li

Abstract
We generalize the determinant representation of the KP τ \tau functions to the case of the 2D Toda τ \tau functions. The generating functions for the weighted Hurwitz numbers are a parametric family of 2D Toda τ \tau functions; for which we give a determinant representation of weighted Hurwitz numbers. Then we can get a finite-dimensional equation system for the weighted Hurwitz numbers H G d ​ ( σ , ω ) H^{d}_{G}(\sigma,\omega) with the same dimension | σ | = | ω | = n |\sigma|=|\omega|=n . Using this equation system, we calculated the value of the weighted Hurwitz numbers with dimension 0 , 1 , 2 0,\,1,\,2 and give a recursion formula to calculating the higher dimensional weighted Hurwitz numbers. For any given weighted generating function G ⁡ ( z ) G(z) , the weighted Hurwitz number degenerates into the Hurwitz numbers when d = 0 d=0 . We get a matrix representation for the Hurwitz numbers. The generating functions of weighted paths in the Cayley graph of the symmetric group are a parametric family of 2D Toda τ \tau functions; for which we obtain a determinant representation of weighted paths in the Cayley graph.

[Q,P]=1,\,[\varphi_{k},\varphi_{l}]=k\delta_{k+l,0},\,[\varphi_{k},P]=0,\,[\varphi_{k},Q]=0
to exchange each pair of these operators. Neither the charge operator nor the shift operator acting on
\left<0\right|
or
\left|0\right>
are zero, so it is not necessary to change the positions of them according to the defining principle of the normal order. As Q and P are not interchangeable, without loss of generality, it is an artificial requirement that
\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\scriptstyle{(\displaystyle{\cdots})}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}
makes P on the left of Q. If we require
\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\scriptstyle{(\displaystyle{\cdots})}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}
to make P on the right of Q, all the conclusions of this article will not be changed.

Now we can introduce the chiral bosonic field
\phi(z)
:

\phi(z)=\sum_{k\neq 0}(\frac{\varphi_{-k}}{k}z^{k})+Qln(z)+P,

(2.18)

with

\begin{split}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(z)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}&=\psi(z)=\sum_{i}\psi_{i}z^{i},\\
\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{-\phi(z)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}&=\psi^{*}(z)=\sum_{j}\psi_{j}^{*}z^{-j-1}.\end{split}

(2.19)

A solution satisfying the equations (
2.16
),(
2.17
),(
2.19
) exists. We will find one of them below:

\begin{split}&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\exp^{\alpha\phi(z)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}~\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\exp^{\beta\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\\
=&e^{\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{-k}}{k}z^{k}}e^{\alpha P}z^{\alpha Q}e^{-\alpha\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}}{k}z^{-k}}e^{\beta\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{-k}}{k}w^{k}}e^{\beta P}w^{\beta Q}e^{-\beta\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{k}}{k}w^{-k}}\\
=&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\exp^{\alpha\phi(z)+\beta\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}e^{-\alpha\beta\sum\limits_{k,l=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{kl}[\varphi_{k},\varphi_{l}]\frac{w^{l}}{z^{k}}}e^{\alpha\beta[Qlnz,P]}\\
=&(z-w)^{\alpha\beta}~\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\exp^{\alpha\phi(z)+\beta\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}.\end{split}

(2.20)

Next, let
w
approximate
z
, and always ensure that the norm of
w
is smaller than
z
’s during the approximation

\begin{split}&\psi(z)\psi^{*}(z)\\
=&\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\psi(z)\psi^{*}(w)\\
=&\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{(\exp^{\phi(z)-\phi(w)})}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}}{z-w}\\
=&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\lim_{w\rightarrow z}(\frac{1}{z-w}+\frac{\phi(z)-\phi(w)}{z-w}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\phi(z)-\phi(w))^{2}}{z-w}+\cdots)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\\
=&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{1}{z-w}+\partial_{z}\phi(z)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\\
=&\lim_{w\rightarrow z}\frac{1}{z-w}+\frac{1}{z}\sum\limits_{k\neq 0}(\varphi_{-k}z^{k})+\frac{Q}{z}.\end{split}

(2.21)

Notice that
\psi(z)\psi^{*}(w)-\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi(z)\psi^{*}(w)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}=\frac{1}{z-w}
, substituting this into (
2.21
) yields

\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi(z)\psi^{*}(z)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{=\frac{1}{z}\sum_{k\neq 0}(\varphi_{-k}z^{k})+\frac{Q}{z}}

and

\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi(z)\psi^{*}(z)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{=\sum\limits_{i,j\in\mathbb{Z}}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi_{i}\psi_{i+j}^{*}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{z^{-j-1}}=\displaystyle{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{J_{i}}{z^{j+1}}}.

(2.22)

Noting that
J_{0}=Q
, we find a set of solutions for the system of equations (
2.16
),(
2.17
),(
2.19
) as
\psi_{k}=J_{k}
. Using this set of special solutions, the
loop equation
in the form of operators can be directly deduced.

\begin{split}&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi(z)\psi^{*}(w)}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\\
&=\psi(z)\psi^{*}(w)-\frac{w}{z-w}\\
&=(\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(z)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{-\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{w})-\frac{w}{z-w}\\
&=\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(z)-\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\frac{w}{z-w}-\frac{w}{z-w}}\\
&=\frac{w}{z-w}(\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(z)-\phi(w)}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{-1}).\end{split}

(2.23)

The loop equation has been proven in another way in
[
2
]
. In
[
1
]
, the loop equation can be used to derive the
W_{1+\infty}
algebra.

2.3
2D Toda
\tau

The notation and proofs of the details in this section can be found in
[

For any group-like elements
G=\exp\Bigl(\sum_{i,k\in{\mathbb{z}}}b_{ik}\psi^{*}_{i}\psi_{k}\Bigr)
, it is well known that the KP
\tau
functions is

\tau(\mathbf{t})=\left<0\right|e^{J_{+}({\mathbf{t}})}G\left|0\right>,

(2.24)

where
\mathbf{t}=\{t_{1},t_{2},\cdots\}
.

Using the generalized Wick theorem and the boson-fermion correspondence, S. Kharchev
[
22
]
found the determinant representation of the KP
\tau
functions:

\begin{split}\tau(\mathbf{t})=\frac{\left<0\right|G\left|0\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<0\right|\psi_{-i}\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\left|0\right>}{\left<0\right|G\left|0\right>}\Big),\end{split}

(2.25)

where
\Delta(\nu)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})
is the Vandermonde determinant and
t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i}^{-k}
is the Miwa parameters
[
25
]
.

The 2D Toda
\tau
functions
[
33
,
32
]
are

\tau^{2D}(n,{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\left<n\right|e^{J_{+}({\mathbf{t}})}Ge^{J_{-}({\mathbf{s}})}\left|n\right>,

(2.26)

where
\mathbf{t}=\{t_{1},t_{2},\cdots\}
and
\mathbf{s}=\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots\}
. In the
\mathbf{s}=0
case, the 2D Toda
\tau
functions degenerate to the KP
\tau
functions. Note that in
[
3
]
, the 2D Toda
\tau
functions are defined as
\left<n\right|e^{J_{+}({\mathbf{t}})}Ge^{-J_{-}({\mathbf{s}})}\left|n\right>
, so the same formulas in this article differ a little from those in
[
3
]
. However, the present formulas compared with those of
[
2
,
5
,
15
,
14
,
28
,
29
]
are consistent.

For any partition
\lambda
and integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle{e^{J_{-}({\mathbf{t}})}\left|n\right>=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{b(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}({\mathbf{t}})\left|\lambda,n\right>},

(2.27)

\displaystyle{\left<n\right|e^{J_{+}({\mathbf{t}})}=\sum_{\lambda}(-1)^{b(\lambda)}s_{\lambda}({\mathbf{t}})\left<\lambda,n\right|},

(2.28)

where
s_{\lambda}({\mathbf{t}})
is the Schur function
[
24
]
and
b(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{d(\lambda)}(\beta_{i}+1)
.

For partitions
\{\mu^{(i)}\}_{i=1,\dots,k}
with the same weight, the Hurwitz numbers
H(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\cdots,\mu^{(k)})
are defined as the number of distinct factorizations of the identity element
I_{N}\in\mathbf{S}_{N}
in the symmetric group on
N
elements divided by the normalization factor
N!
.
I_{N}=h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{k},\,cyc(h_{i})=\mu^{(i)}
where the conjugate class
cyc(\mu^{(i)})\in\mathbf{S}_{N}
whose cycle lengths are equal to the partitions of
\mu^{(i)}
[
9
,
10
,
31
]
. Usually, we use the Frobenius-Schur formula:

H(\mu^{(1)},\dots,\mu^{(k)})=\sum_{|\lambda|=N}h(\lambda)^{k-2}\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\frac{\chi_{\lambda}(\mu^{(i)})}{Z_{\mu^{(i)}}}}.

(2.29)

The calculation of the Hurwitz numbers gives an explicit expression of the Hurwitz numbers with hook lengths
h(\lambda)
,
Z_{\mu}=\prod_{i}m_{i}!i^{m_{i}}
and the
\mathbf{S}_{N}
characters
\chi_{\lambda}(\mu^{(i)})
corresponding to the irreducible representations with symmetry class
\lambda
.

The weighted Hurwitz numbers
H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)

[
28
,
14
]
with the weight generating function
G(z)
are

\displaystyle G(z):=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(1+c_{k}z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}G_{k}z^{k}

(2.30)

and
d\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
; two partitions
\mu,\nu
with the same weight are

\begin{split}H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu):=&\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu^{(1)}\cdots\mu^{(k)},\\
|\mu^{(1)}|=\cdots=|\mu^{(k)}|=|\mu|\\
\sum_{i=1}^{k}l^{*}(\mu^{(i)})=d\end{subarray}}W_{G}(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\cdots,\mu^{(k)})H(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\cdots,\mu^{(k)},\mu,\nu),\end{split}

(2.31)

where
\sum^{\prime}
refers to the sum for all
\mu^{(1)}\cdots\mu^{(k)},\,|\mu^{(1)}|=\cdots=|\mu^{(k)}|=|\mu|,\,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}l^{*}(\mu^{(i)})=d
except
\mu^{(i)}=(1^{N})
, and the
W_{G}(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\cdots,\mu^{(k)})
are the weights of
H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)
related to the weight generating function
G(z)
:

\begin{split}W_{G}(\mu^{(1)},\mu^{(2)},\cdots,\mu^{(k)})=&\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}c_{i_{\sigma(1)}}^{l^{*}(\mu^{(1)})}c_{i_{\sigma(2)}}^{l^{*}(\mu^{(2)})}\cdots c_{i_{\sigma(k)}}^{l^{*}(\mu^{(k)})},\end{split}

(2.32)

where
l^{*}(\mu):=|\mu|-l(\mu)
is the co-length of the partition
\mu
, and the
c_{k}
are the parameters in the weight generating function
G(z)
.
H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)
vanishes, if
\mu,\,\nu
have different weights. The weighted Hurwitz numbers also have an equivalent combinatorial definition
[
13
]
. As for
d=0
, the weighted Hurwitz numbers
H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)
degenerate to the Hurwitz numbers
H(\mu,\nu)
, no mater what the weight generating function is.

A parametric family
\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(n,\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})

[
29
,
28
,
14
]
associated to the group-like elements
\hat{C}_{\rho}=e^{\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}T_{j}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\psi_{j}\psi_{j}^{*}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}}
with
\rho=\{\rho_{i}\}_{i=-\infty}^{\infty}
,
\rho_{i}=e^{T_{i}}
,
\rho_{0}=1
,
r_{i}=e^{T_{i}-T_{i-1}}
,
r_{k}=G(\beta k)
,

\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(n,{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})=\left<n\right|e^{J_{+}({\mathbf{t}})}\hat{C}_{\rho}e^{J_{-}({\mathbf{s}})}\left|n\right>,

(2.33)

are the generating functions of the weighted Hurwitz numbers and weighted paths in the Cayley graph of the symmetric group
\mathbf{S}_{n}
. We abbreviate
\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(0,{\mathbf{t}},{\mathbf{s}})
as
\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})
.

\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\beta^{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mu,\nu\\
|\mu|=|\nu|\end{subarray}}H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)p_{\mu}(\mathbf{t})p_{\nu}(\mathbf{s}),

(2.34)

in which
\mathbf{t}
,
\mathbf{s}
are the Miwa parameters
[
25
]
, and
\{\mu\}
,
\{\nu\}
are called the Miwa variables

t_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{i}^{-k},\qquad\qquad s_{l}=\frac{1}{l}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mu_{j}^{-l}.

(2.35)

Simultaneously,

H^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)=F^{d}_{G}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{|\nu|!}\sum\limits_{\lambda,|\lambda|=d}G_{\lambda}m^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu},

(2.36)

where
m^{\lambda}_{\mu\nu}
is the number of monotonic
\lambda
signature paths in the Cayley graph of
\mathbf{S}_{n},\,|\mu|=|\nu|=n
from
cyc(\mu)
to
cyc(\nu)
.

To save space we will not list the specific proof here, but an intermediate step in the proof process will useful later:

\begin{split}\hat{C}_{\rho}\left|\lambda,N\right>=r_{\lambda}(N)\left|\lambda,N\right>,\end{split}

(2.37)

where
r_{\lambda}(N)=r_{0}(N)\prod\limits_{(i,j)}r_{N+j-i}
and
r_{i}=e^{T_{i}-T_{i-1}}
with

r_{0}(N)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{r}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{N-1}e^{T_{j}}\qquad N>0,\\
1\qquad\qquad N=0,\\
\prod\limits_{j=N}^{-1}e^{-T_{j}}\qquad N<0.\end{array}\right.

(2.38)

Furthermore, the 2D Toda
\tau
functions could be written as double Schur function expansions
[
14
]
:

\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(n,\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=\sum_{\lambda}r_{\lambda}(n)s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t})s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{s}).

(2.39)

There is another parametric family
\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(\gamma,n,\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=\sum\limits_{N=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{N}\sum\limits_{\lambda,|\lambda|=N}r_{\lambda}(n)s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{t})s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{s})
. When
\gamma=1
,
\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(\gamma,n,\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=\tau^{2D}_{\hat{C}_{\rho}}(n,\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})
.

3
Determinant representation of 2D Toda
\tau
functions

In the 1990s, S. Kharchev
[
22
]
used the generalized Wick theorem and the boson-fermion correspondence
[
22
,
8
]
to get the determinant of the KP(Kadomtsev-Petviashvili)
\tau
function. We generalize that result to the case of the 2D Toda
\tau
functions in this section.

By the correspondence between the Bosonic field and the Fermion field, we have

\begin{split}&\psi(\mu_{1}^{-1})\cdots\psi(\mu_{M}^{-1})\left|-M\right>\\
=&\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(\mu_{1}^{-1})}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\cdots\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{\phi(\mu_{M}^{-1})}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{\left|-M\right>}\\
=&\prod\limits_{i<j}(\mu_{i}^{-1}-\mu_{j}^{-1})e^{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{-k}}{k}(\mu_{1}^{-k}+\cdots+\mu_{M}^{-k})}e^{MP}...\mu_{1}^{-Q}\cdots\mu_{M}^{-Q}e^{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}-\frac{J_{k}}{k}(\mu_{1}^{k}+\cdots+\mu_{M}^{k})}\displaystyle{\left|-M\right>}\\
=&\Delta(\mu)\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{M}\hat{\gamma}_{-}(\mathbf{s})\left|0\right>,\end{split}

(3.1)

where
\Delta(\mu)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq M}(\mu_{j}-\mu_{i})
is the Vandermonde determinant and
s_{l}=\frac{1}{l}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{M}\mu_{j}^{-l}
. Similarly, we have

\begin{split}&\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})=\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}(\mathbf{t}).\end{split}

(3.2)

Observing that in (
2.35
), if we add
\nu_{N+1}=\infty
after the sequence of Miwa variables
\left\{\mathbf{\nu}\right\}
, the new Miwa variables
\left\{\mathbf{\nu}^{\prime}\right\}=\left\{\mathbf{\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N},\nu_{N+1}}\right\}
also correspond to
\mathbf{t}
, but it changes the form of (
3.2
).

\begin{split}\left<-N-1\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N+1})\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})=\Delta(\nu)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\nu_{N+1}-\nu_{j})\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\nu_{N+1}\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}(\mathbf{t}),\end{split}

(3.3)

where
\Delta(\nu)
is the Vandermonde determinant of
\left\{\nu\right\}
,
\Delta(\nu)=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(\nu_{j}-\nu_{i})
.
Even though the form of (
3.3
) corresponding to
\left\{\mathbf{\nu}^{\prime}\right\}
and (
3.2
) corresponding to
\left\{\mathbf{\nu}\right\}
are different, both of them are equivalent.
For
\nu_{N+1}=\infty
, the left side of (
3.3
) is

\begin{split}&\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\left<-N-1\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N+1})\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})\\
&~=\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\left<-N-1\right|\nu_{N+1}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\displaystyle{e^{-\phi(\nu_{N+1})}}\scriptstyle{\bullet\atop\bullet}\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})\\
&~=\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{N+1}\left<-N\right|\nu_{N+1}^{-Q}e^{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{J_{k}}{k}\nu_{N+1}^{-k}}\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})\\
&~=\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{N+1}^{N+1}\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1}),\\
\end{split}

(3.4)

while the right side of (
3.3
) is

\begin{split}&\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\Delta(\nu)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{N}(\nu_{N+1}-\nu_{j})\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\nu_{N+1}\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}(\mathbf{t})\\
&~=\mathop{lim}_{\nu_{N+1}\rightarrow\infty}\nu_{N+1}^{N+1}\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}(\mathbf{t}).\end{split}

(3.5)

Dividing both sides of (
3.3
) by
\nu_{N+1}^{N+1}
, we get (
3.2
). When the two Miwa variables
\{\mu\}
,
\{\nu\}
have different dimensions, which is likely to happen as
\{\mu\}
and
\{\nu\}
are independent, we could add some
\infty
term after the Miwa variables with the smaller dimension to make
\{\mu\}
,
\{\nu\}
have the same dimensions. We only talk about the situation where
\{\mu\}
,
\{\nu\}
have the same dimension N below.
Considering (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we have

\begin{split}\tau^{2D}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=&\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}({\mathbf{t}})G\hat{\gamma}_{-}({\mathbf{s}})\left|0\right>\\
=&\frac{1}{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})\\
&G\psi(\mu_{1}^{-1})\cdots\psi(\mu_{N}^{-1})\left|-N\right>.\end{split}

(3.6)

In the
\mathbf{s}=0
case, which means
\nu_{i}\rightarrow\infty,\,\forall i
, the right side of (
3.6
) degenerates into
\frac{1}{\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}}

\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})G\left|0\right>
corresponding to the KP
\tau

\tau(\mathbf{t})
in (

\tau

\begin{split}\tau^{2D}(\mathbf{t},\mathbf{s})=&\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}({\mathbf{t}})G\hat{\gamma}_{-}({\mathbf{s}})\left|0\right>\\
=&\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\psi(\mu_{i}^{-1})\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big).\end{split}

(3.7)

Such a determinant representation of the 2D Toda
\tau

\tau
functions, while in the
\mathbf{s}=0

\tau
functions degenerate KP
\tau
functions, we have to prove that the determinant representation of the 2D Toda

\tau

We get (

) and (
2.25

2.25

\begin{split}\tau(\mathbf{t})=&\left<0\right|\hat{\gamma}_{+}({\mathbf{t}})G\left|0\right>\\
=&\frac{1}{\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}}\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{N})\cdots\psi^{*}(\nu_{1})G\psi_{-1}\cdots\psi_{-N}\left|-N\right>\\
=&\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\psi_{-i}\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big).\end{split}

(3.8)

Now, we only need to prove that (

3.8
) when

\mathbf{s}=0
means
\mu_{i}\rightarrow\infty,\quad\forall i

\displaystyle\mathop{lim}_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\psi(\mu_{i}^{-1})\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{lim}_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\psi_{-i}\mu_{k}^{i}\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathop{lim}_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{k,i=1\cdots N}(\mu_{k}^{i})\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\psi_{-i}\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}{\Delta(\nu)\nu_{1}\cdots\nu_{N}}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots N}\Big(\frac{\left<-N\right|\psi^{*}(\nu_{j})G\psi_{-i}\left|-N\right>}{\left<-N\right|G\left|-N\right>}\Big).

The second equal in (

\mu_{i}^{n}=0
,
\forall n<0
for

=&\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots n}\Big(\frac{\nu_{j}^{n}\mu_{i}^{n}}{\nu_{j}\mu_{i}-1}\Big)\\
=&\nu_{1}^{n}\cdots\nu_{n}^{n}\mu_{1}^{n}\cdots\mu_{n}^{n}\mathop{det}\limits_{i,j=1\cdots n}\Big(\frac{1}{\nu_{j}\mu_{i}-1}\Big).\\
\end{split}

(6.5)

Recalling the Cauchy determinant

]
for the sequence ,

\{w_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
,

Substitute

into

and

into

, we then have

det\Big(\frac{1}{\mu_{i}\nu_{j}-1}\Big)=\frac{\Delta(\nu)\Delta(\mu)}{\prod\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(\nu_{j}\mu_{i}-1)}

(6.7)

and

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega,\sigma\\
|\omega|=|\sigma|\end{subarray}}H^{0}_{G}(\omega,\sigma)p_{\omega}(\mathbf{t})p_{\sigma}(\mathbf{s})=&\frac{\nu_{1}^{n}\cdots\nu_{n}^{n}\mu_{1}^{n}\cdots\mu_{n}^{n}}{\prod\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(\nu_{j}\mu_{i}-1)}.\\
\end{split}

(6.8)

Notice that
|\nu_{j}\mu_{i}|>1
, we have
\frac{1}{\nu_{j}\mu_{i}-1}=\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{-1}\nu_{j}^{k}\mu_{i}^{k}
and

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega,\sigma\\
|\omega|=|\sigma|\end{subarray}}H^{0}_{G}(\omega,\sigma)p_{\omega}(\mathbf{t})p_{\sigma}(\mathbf{s})=&~\nu_{1}^{n}\cdots\nu_{n}^{n}\mu_{1}^{n}\cdots\mu_{n}^{n}\prod\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\sum\limits_{k_{ij}=-\infty}^{-1}\nu_{j}^{k}\mu_{i}^{k}\\

(6.9)

\prod\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\nu_{j}^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{ij}}\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\mu_{i}^{\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{ij}}
directly corresponds to an
n\times n
-dimensional square matrix,

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}k_{11}&k_{12}&\cdots&k_{1n}\\
k_{21}&k_{22}&\cdots&k_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
k_{n1}&k_{n2}&\cdots&k_{nn}\end{array}\right),

(6.10)

the added value of
\{k_{ij}\}_{i=1}^{n}
in each column is the power of
\nu_{j}
(the added value of
\{k_{ij}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in each row is the power of
\mu_{i}
.)
\nu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\nu_{n}^{m_{n}}\mu_{1}^{M_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n}^{M_{n}}
may correspond to more than one matrix, like (
6.10
). In this way, it establishes a semigroup homomorphism from an

-dimensional non-positive integer coefficient matrix semigroup with no inverse element to monomial semigroups of

.

\begin{split}\{Mn(n,\mathbb{Z}_{-}),\,+\}&\longrightarrow\{\{\nu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\nu_{n}^{m_{n}}\mu_{1}^{M_{n}}\mu_{n}^{M_{n}}|m_{1},\cdots,m_{n},M_{1}\cdots,M_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{-}\},\,\times\},\\
\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}k_{11}&k_{12}&\cdots&k_{1n}\\
k_{21}&k_{22}&\cdots&k_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
k_{n1}&k_{n2}&\cdots&k_{nn}\end{array}\right)&\longrightarrow\nu_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\nu_{n}^{m_{n}}\mu_{1}^{M_{1}}\cdots\mu_{n}^{M_{n}},\end{split}

in which
\mathbb{Z}_{-}=\{z\in\mathbb{Z}|\,z\leq 0\}
is a non-positive integer. The group addition of the

dimensional non-positive integer coefficient matrix semigroup is matrix addition, and the group multiplication of the monomial semigroups of
\nu_{1},\,\cdots,\,\nu_{n},\,\mu_{1},\,\cdots,\,\mu_{n}
is multiplication.

Similarly,

could be decomposed into the sum of
n\times n
-dimensional non-positive integer coefficient matrix groups,

\bordermatrix{&m_{1}&m_{2}&\cdots&m_{n}\cr M_{1}&k_{11}&k_{12}&\cdots&k_{1n}\cr M_{2}&k_{21}&k_{22}&\cdots&k_{2n}\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\cr M_{n}&k_{n1}&k_{n2}&\cdots&k_{nn}},

(6.11)

with

and
\{M_{1},M_{2},\cdots,M_{n}\}=\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{n}\}
. In this way, we obtain the matrix representation of the Hurwitz numbers. We could calculate the value of the Hurwitz numbers by matrix operations.

When

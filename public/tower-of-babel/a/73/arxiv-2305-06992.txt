Updated analysis of near-threshold heavy quarkonium production for probe of proton’s gluonic gravitational form factors
Yuxun Guo, Xiangdong Ji, Yizhuang Liu, Jinghong Yang

Abstract
There has been growing interest in the near-threshold production of heavy quarkonium which can access the gluonic structure in the nucleon. Previously Guo et al. 2021 we studied this process with quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and showed that it can be factorized with the gluon generalized parton distributions (GPDs) in the heavy quark limit. We further argued that the hadronic matrix element is dominated by its leading moments corresponding to the gluonic gravitational form factors (GFFs) in this limit. Since then, there have been many new developments on this subject. More experimental measurements have been made and published, and the lattice simulation of gluonic GFFs has been improved as well. In this work, we make an important revision to a previous result and perform an updated analysis with the new inputs. We also study the importance of the large momentum transfer to extract these gluonic structures reliably in this framework.



I
Introduction

The gluonic structures have been an important topic in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and hadronic physics. On the one hand, gluons that mediate the strong interaction play a prominent role at the non-perturbative scale in the hadron. On the other hand, they are free of electroweak interaction and much harder to probe than quarks. Consequently, there has been rising interest in the exclusive electro/photo-productions of heavy quarkonium. Assuming suppressed contributions from the intrinsic heavy quarks, these processes are dominated by the exchange of gluons and thus can be used to access the gluonic structures. Experiments with such purposes are planned in the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC)
Accardi et al. 2016
, whereas at Jefferson Laboratory (JLab) the near-threshold production of
J/\psi
that requires less energy has been proposed and measured which revealed exciting results
Joosten and Meziani 2018
;
Ali et al. 2019
;
Duran et al. 2023
;
Adhikari et al. 2023
.

There have been many theoretical developments in the literature to analyze this process
Voloshin 1979
;
Gottfried 1978
;
Appelquist and Fischler 1978
;
Bhanot and Peskin 1979
;
Kharzeev 1996
;
Kharzeev et al. 1999
;
Kharzeev 2021
;
Gryniuk and Vanderhaeghen 2016
;
Gryniuk et al. 2020
;
Hatta and Yang 2018
;
Hatta et al. 2019
;
Mamo and Zahed 2020
;
Mamo and Zahed 2021
;
Mamo and Zahed 2022
;
Sun et al. 2022
;
Sun et al. 2021
. In the previous work of three of the authors
Guo et al. 2021
, we showed that the near-threshold photoproduction of heavy quarkonium can be factorized with gluon generalized parton distributions (GPDs), extending the factorization proved for the diffractive production in the collinear limit
Collins et al. 1997
;
Ivanov et al. 2004
. Utilizing the heavy quark limit, we also argued that the hadronic matrix element will be dominated by the leading moments that correspond to the gluonic gravitational form factors (GFFs) near the threshold. These GFFs carry important information about the nucleon such as their mass, angular momentum and mechanical properties
Ji 1995
;
Ji 1997a
;
Polyakov 2003
;
Polyakov and Schweitzer 2018
;
Ji 2021
;

;

In this work, we perform an updated analysis of the near-threshold
J/\psi
production following the previous work for two main reasons. First, we note that there was a missing factor of 2 in the hadronic matrix element

in ref.
Guo et al. 2021

J/\psi

Duran et al. 2023
and the GlueX collaboration at Hall D
Adhikari et al. 2023
. Moreover, the lattice QCD simulation of gluonic GFFs has been improved
Pefkou et al. 2022
. The large
\xi
expansion essential for relating this process to the gluonic GFFs requires careful treatments. Consequently, we will present more detailed analyses of these new results in this work.

II
Revised formula

We start by noting that the definition of gluon GPDs in eq. (16) of the previous work
Guo et al. 2021
includes an extra factor of
\frac{1}{2}
from the trace operator
Tr
, which should be removed. Correspondingly, the extra factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in eq. (24) in ref.
Guo et al. 2021
shall be removed as well. The revision will leave the main cross-section formula unchanged, i.e., eqs. (13) and (17) in ref.
Guo et al. 2021
. However, one needs to substitute the gluon GPDs with the corrected definition that reads,

\displaystyle F_{g}(x,\xi,t)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\bar{P}^{+})^{2}}\int\frac{\text{d}\lambda}{2\pi}e^{i\lambda x}\left<P^{\prime}\left|F^{a+i}_{\;\;\;\;\;}\left(-\frac{\lambda n}{2}\right)F^{a+}_{\;\;i}\left(\frac{\lambda n}{2}\right)\right|P\right>\ ,

(1)

where the index
a
sums over all colors. The
F_{g}(x,\xi,t)
can be parameterized as
Ji 1997a
;
Diehl 2003

\displaystyle F_{g}(x,\xi,t)=\frac{1}{2\bar{P}^{+}}\bar{u}(P^{\prime})\left[H_{g}\gamma^{+}+E_{g}\frac{i\sigma^{+\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha}}{2M_{N}}\right]u(P)\ ,

(2)

where
H_{g}
and
E_{g}
are the well-known
H_{g}(x,\xi,t)
and
E_{g}(x,\xi,t)
GPDs.
The hadronic matrix element
G(\xi,t)
remains to be
Guo et al. 2021

\displaystyle G(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{2\xi}\int_{-1}^{1}\text{d}x{\cal A}(x,\xi)F_{g}(x,\xi,t)\ ,

(3)

where the Wilson coefficient
{\cal A}(x,\xi)
reads

{\cal A}(x,\xi)\equiv\frac{1}{x+\xi-i0}-\frac{1}{x-\xi+i0}\ .

(4)

On the other hand, the extra factor of
\frac{1}{2}
does affect the relations between the gluon GPDs and GFFs. Removing the extra factor of
\frac{1}{2}
in the gluon GPDs causes an extra factor of 2 to be multiplied to each gluonic GFF when expanding the GPDs in terms of their moments. Consequently, in the unpolarized case we have

\displaystyle|G(t,\xi)|^{2}=\frac{4}{\xi^{4}}\Bigg\{\left(1-\frac{t}{4M_{N}^{2}}\right)E_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle-2E_{2}(H_{2}+E_{2})+\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)(H_{2}+E_{2})^{2}\Bigg\}\ ,

(5)

where an extra factor of
4
appears accordingly, compared to the eq. (26) in ref.
Guo et al. 2021
. The same factor of
4
should be multiplied to the polarized
|G(t,\xi)|^{2}
in eqs. (58) and (59) in ref.
Guo et al. 2021
as well. Recall that the GFFs
H_{2}\equiv H_{2}(t,\xi)
and
E_{2}\equiv E_{2}(t,\xi)
follow the same definition as ref.
Guo et al. 2021
that reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dxH_{g}(x,\xi,t)=A_{2,0}^{g}(t)+(2\xi)^{2}C^{g}_{2}\equiv H_{2}(t,\xi)\ ,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dxE_{g}(x,\xi,t)=B^{g}_{2,0}(t)-(2\xi)^{2}C^{g}_{2}\equiv E_{2}(t,\xi)\ .

(6)

IV
Extracted mass and scalar radii

To make the comparison more intuitive, in this section we also consider the extracted mass and scalar radii of the proton. They
can be defined in terms of the GFFs as
Ji 2021
,

\displaystyle\left\langle r^{2}_{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[6\left.\frac{dA\left(t\right)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}-6\penalty\ \frac{C(0)}{M_{N}^{2}}\right]\ ,

(10)

\displaystyle\left\langle r^{2}_{s}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[6\left.\frac{dA\left(t\right)}{dt}\right|_{t=0}-18\penalty\ \frac{C(0)}{M_{N}^{2}}\right]\ ,

where the
A(t)
and
C(t)
form factors are the sums of the quark and gluon GFFs, respectively. We note that one complexity arises from the
\bar{C}(t)
form factor when individual contributions from the quark and gluon are considered
Ji 2021
. The
\bar{C}(t)
terms exist in the matrix elements of the energy momentum tensor (EMT) of quark and gluon separately, while they cancel each other when summing over the quark and gluon:
\bar{C}_{q}(t)+\bar{C}_{g}(t)=0
due to current conservation
Ji 1997a
. Furthermore, the separation of the quark and gluon GFFs depends on the renormalization scheme and scale. V
Summary and outlook

To summarize, in this work we revise the previous cross-section formula
Guo et al. 2021
with the factor of 2 mismatch, and perform an updated analysis with the latest lattice simulation of gluonic GFFs
Pefkou et al. 2022
and the recently published data from
J/\psi
007 experiment
Duran et al. 2023
and GlueX collaboration
Adhikari et al. 2023
. We show that with the revised formula the agreement between the gluonic GFFs from lattice simulation and extraction with GPD factorization gets improved. On the other hand, we also argue that this framework requires large momentum transfer squared
|t|
and skewness
\xi
, and thus we perform a series of analyses to properly address their effects. We show that the gluonic GFFs can be roughly constrained with a cut of
\xi>0.4
. However, it is crucial to have more high-quality data at large
|t|
to improve the extraction.

The critical future developments include studying the higher order corrections such as the next-to-leading order effects in the strong coupling
\alpha_{S}
as well as the finite quarkonium mass correction in
M_{p}/M_{V}
. In addition, the systematical uncertainties from the large
\xi
expansion also require proper treatment.

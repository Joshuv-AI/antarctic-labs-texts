Abstract




Abstract
In this paper, the authors obtain
the continuity of a class of linear operators
on variable anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz spaces.
In addition, the authors also obtain that the dual space of variable anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz spaces is the anisotropic BMO-type spaces with variable exponents.
This result is still new even when the exponent function
p(\cdot)
is
p
.

1
Introduction

As is known to all, Hardy space on the Euclidean space
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a good substitutes of Lebesgue space
L^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
when
p\in(0,1]
,
and plays an important role in haronmic analysis and
\mathbf{PDE}
s; see, for examples,
[
6
,
12
,
15
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
. Moreover, when studying the boundedness of some
operators in the critical case, the weak Hardy space
w\mathcal{H}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
naturally appears and it is a good substitute of
\mathcal{H}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.
w\mathcal{H}^{p}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
with
p\in(0,\,1)
was first introduced by Fefferman and Soria
[
11
]
to find out the biggest space from which the Riesz transform is bounded to the weak Lebesgue space
wL^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
. In 2007, Abu-Shammala and Torchinsky
[
2
]
introduced the Hardy-Lorentz spaces
\mathcal{H}^{p,\,r}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
for the full range
p\in(0,\,1]
and
r\in(0,\,\infty]
, and
obtained some real-variable characterizations of this space.
In 2016, Liu et al.
[
17
]

introduced the anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz space
\mathcal{H}^{p,\,r}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
associated with a general expansive dilation
A
, including the classical isotropic Hardy-Lorentz space of Abu-Shammala and Torchinsky.

As a generalization, variable exponent function spaces have their applications in fluid dynamics
[
3
]
, image processing
[
5
]
,
\mathbf{PDE}
s and variational calculus
[
10
,
26
]
.
Let
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
be a variable exponent function. Recently, Liu et al.
[
18
]

introduced the variable anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, via the radial grand maximal function, and then established its some real-variable characterizations, respectively, in terms of atom, the radial and the non-tangential maximal functions. For more information about variable function spaces, see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
28
]
.

To complete the theory of the variable anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, in this article, we obtain
the boundedness of a class of Calderón-Zygmund operators
from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to variable Lorentz space
L^{p(\cdot),\,r}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to itself. In addition, we also obtain the dual space of
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the anisotropic
BMO-type space with variable exponents.

Precisely, this article is organized as follows.

In Section
2
, we recall some notations and definitions
concerning expansive dilations, the variable Lorentz space
L^{p(\cdot),\,r}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and the variable anisotropic Hardy-Lorentz space
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, via the radial grand maximal function.

Section
3
is devoted to establishing
the boundedness of anisotropic convolutional
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operators
from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to
L^{p(\cdot),\,r}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
and from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
to itself.

In Section
4
,
we prove that the dual space of
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the anisotropic
BMO-type space with variable exponents (see Theorem
4.6
).
For this purpose, we first introduce a new kind of anisotropic BMO-type spaces with variable exponents
\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in Definition
4.1
, which includes the space
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of John and Nirenberg
[
13
]
. It is worth pointing
out that this result is also new, when
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
is reduced to

\mathcal{H}^{p,\,r}_{A}({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}})
.

2
Preliminaries

Firstly,
we recall the definitions of anisotropic dilations
on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
; see
[
4
, p. 5]
. A real
n\times n
matrix
A
is called an
anisotropic dilation
, shortly a
dilation
, if

\min_{\lambda\in\sigma(A)}|\lambda|>1
, where
\sigma(A)
denotes the set of
all
eigenvalues
of
A
. Let
\lambda_{-}
and
\lambda_{+}
be two
positive numbers
such that

1<\lambda_{-}<\min\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}\leq\max\{|\lambda|:\ \lambda\in\sigma(A)\}<\lambda_{+}.

By
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
, we know that, for a given dilation
A
,
there exist a number
r\in(1,\,\infty)
and a set
\Delta:=\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,|Px|<1\}
, where
P
is some non-degenerate
n\times n
matrix, such that

\Delta\subset r\Delta\subset A\Delta,

and we can
assume that
|\Delta|=1
, where
|\Delta|
denotes the

n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of the set
\Delta
. Let

B_{k}:=A^{k}\Delta
for
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.
Then
B_{k}
is open,

B_{k}\subset rB_{k}\subset B_{k+1}\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ |B_{k}|=b^{k},

here and hereafter,
b:=|\det A|
.
An ellipsoid
x+B_{k}
for some
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
dilated ball
.
Denote

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{B}:=\{x+B_{k}:\ x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}.

Throughout the whole paper, let
\sigma
be the
smallest integer
such that
2B_{0}\subset A^{\sigma}B_{0}

and, for any subset
E
of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
E^{\complement}:={{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus E
. Then,
for all
k,\,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
k\leq j
, it holds true that

(2.2)

\displaystyle B_{k}+B_{j}\subset B_{j+\sigma},

(2.3)

\displaystyle B_{k}+(B_{k+\sigma})^{\complement}\subset(B_{k})^{\complement},

where
E+F
denotes the
algebraic sum

\{x+y:\ x\in E,\,y\in F\}

of sets
E,\,F\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
.

Recall a
quasi-norm
, associated with
dilation
A
, is a Borel measurable mapping

\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, satisfying

(i)

\rho(x)>0
for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\setminus\{\vec{0}_{n}\}
,
here and hereafter,
\vec{0}_{n}
denotes the origin of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
;

(ii)

\rho(Ax)=b\rho(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where, as above,
b:=|\det A|
;

(iii)

\rho(x+y)\leq H\left[\rho(x)+\rho(y)\right]
for
all
x,\,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, where
H\in[1,\,\infty)
is a constant independent of
x
and
y
.

By
[
4
, Lemma 2.4]
,
we know that all homogeneous quasi-norms associated with a given dilation

A
are equivalent. Therefore, for a
fixed dilation
A
, in what follows, for convenience, we
always use the
step homogeneous quasi-norm

\rho
defined by setting, for all
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho(x):=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b^{k}\chi_{B_{k+1}\setminus B_{k}}(x)\ {\rm if}\ x\neq\vec{0}_{n},\hskip 8.5359pt{\mathrm{or\ else}\hskip 8.5359pt}\rho(\vec{0}_{n}):=0.

By (
2.2
), we know that, for all
x,\,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\rho(x+y)\leq b^{\sigma}[\rho(x)+\rho(y)];

Moreover,
({{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}},\,\rho,\,dx)
is a space of
homogeneous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
6
]
,
where
dx
denotes the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
.

A measurable function
p(\cdot):{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}\rightarrow(0,\,\infty)
is called a
variable exponent
. For any variable exponent
p(\cdot)
, let

(2.4)

\displaystyle p_{-}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,inf\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x)\quad\mathrm{and}\ \ p_{+}:=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,ess\,sup\,}_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}p(x).

Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}
the set of all variable exponents
p(\cdot)
satisfying
0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty
.

Let
f
be a measurable function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
and
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
. Define

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in(0,\,\infty):\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

where

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,dx.

Moreover, the
variable Lebesgue space

L^{p(\cdot)}
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f

satisfying that
\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)<\infty
, equipped with the quasi-norm
\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

Remark 2.1
.

[
18
]

Let
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
.

(i)

For any

r\in(0,\,\infty)
and
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|{|f|^{r}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\left\|f\right\|^{r}_{L^{rp(\cdot)}}.

Moreover, for any
\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
f,g\in L^{p(\cdot)}
,

\left\|\mu f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot),}}=|\mu|\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
and

\left\|f+g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq\left\|f\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}+\left\|g\right\|^{\underline{p}}_{L^{p(\cdot)}},

where

(2.5)

\displaystyle\underline{p}:=\min\{p_{-},\,1\}

with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
).

(ii)

For any function
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}

with
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}>0
,

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f/{\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}})=1

and, for
\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq 1
, then

\varrho_{p(\cdot)}(f)\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
. The variable Lorentz space
L^{p(\cdot),\,r}
is defined to be the set of all measurable functions
f
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot),\,r}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left[\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{r}\left\|\chi_{\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\right]^{1/r},&\ r\in(0,\,\infty),\\
\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda\in(0,\,\infty)}\left[\lambda\left\|\chi_{\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:|f(x)|>\lambda\}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\right],&\ r=\infty\end{array}\right.

is finite.

We say that
p(\cdot)\in{\mathcal{P}}
satisfy the
globally log-Hölder continuous condition
, denoted by

p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
, if there exist two positive constants
C_{\log}(p)
and
C_{\infty}
, and
p_{\infty}\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that, for any
x,y\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{\log}(p)}{\log(e+1/\rho(x-y))}

and

\displaystyle|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+\rho(x))}.

A
C^{\infty}
function
\varphi
is said to belong to the Schwartz class
{\mathcal{S}}
if,
for every integer
\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and multi-index
\alpha
,

\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}:=\displaystyle\sup_{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}[\rho(x)]^{\ell}|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|<\infty
.
The dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}
, namely, the space of all tempered distributions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
equipped with the weak-
\ast

topology, is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. For any
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{N}:=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}:\ \|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\ell}\leq 1,\ |\alpha|\leq N,\ \ \ell\leq N\right\}.

In what follows, for
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, let
\varphi_{k}(x):=b^{-k}\varphi\left(A^{-k}x\right)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
. For any given
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
radial grand maximal function

M_{N}(f)
of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
is defined by setting,
for any
x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,

\displaystyle M_{N}(f)(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}}\sup_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f*\varphi_{k}(x)|.

Definition 2.4
.

[
18
]

Let

,

,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,r}:=\left\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}:M_{N}(f)\in L^{p(\cdot),\,r}\right\}

.

[

3
The continuity of Calderón-Zygmund operators

In this section, we get
the continuity of anisotropic convolutional
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operators
from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}
to
L^{p(\cdot),\,r}
or from
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}
to itself.

Let
\delta\in(0,\,\frac{\ln\lambda_{+}}{\ln b})
. We call a linear operator
T
is an anisotropic convolutional
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operator, if
T
is bounded on
L^{2}
with kernel
\mathcal{K}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
coinciding with a locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{\vec{0}_{n}\}
, and satisfying that there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
x,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\rho(x)>b^{2\sigma}\rho(y)
,

|\mathcal{K}(x-y)-\mathcal{K}(x)|\leq C\frac{[\rho(y)]^{\delta}}{[\rho(x)]^{1+\delta}}.

For any
f\in L^{2}
, define
T(f)(x):=\mathrm{p.v.}\,\mathcal{K}\ast f(x)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
r\in(0,\,\infty)
and
\delta\in(0,\,\frac{\ln\lambda_{+}}{\ln b})
. Assume that
T
is an anisotropic convolutional
\delta
-type Calderón-Zygmund operator. If
p_{-}\in(\frac{1}{1+\delta},\,1)
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
), then there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for any
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}
,

(i)

\|T(f)\|_{L^{p(\cdot),\,r}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}};

(ii)

\|T(f)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}}.

Remark 3.2
.

When
p(\cdot):=p
, Theorem
3.1
coincides with
[
17
, Theorem 6.16]
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we need some technical lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
18
, Theorem 4.8]

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
r\in(0,\,\infty)
,
q\in(\max\{p_{+},\,1\},\,\infty]

with
p_{+}
as in (
2.4
) and

s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
).
Then

\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}=\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

By the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 4.8]
, we obtain the following conclusion, which plays an important role in this section.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in C^{\log}
,
r\in(0,\,\infty)
,
q\in(1,\,\infty)
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (

). Then, for any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}\cap L^{q}
, there exist
\{\lambda_{i}^{k}\}_{{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
, dilated balls
\{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
and

(p(\cdot),\,\infty,\,s)
-atoms
\{a_{i}^{k}\}_{{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}}
such that

f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda^{k}_{i}a^{k}_{i}\ {\rm in}\ L^{q}\ {\rm and}\ \mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A},

where the series also converges almost everywhere.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}\cap L^{q}
. For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, by the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 4.8]
, we know that there exist

\left\{x^{k}_{i}\right\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\Omega_{k}:=\left\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\ M_{N}f(x)>2^{k}\right\},\ \ \left\{\ell^{k}_{i}\right\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{Z}},

a sequence of
(p(\cdot),\,\infty,\,s)
-atoms,

Now, we define two new variable anisotropic BMO-type space as follows. In this article, for any

For any

\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}
is defined to be the set of all
f\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
such that

\|f\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}}:=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\frac{|B|^{1-1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\left[\int_{B}|f(x)-P_{B}^{s}(f)(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}<\infty

where
\mathfrak{B}
is as in (
2.1
).

Lemma 4.3
.

[
4
, (8.9)]

Let
q\in[1,\,\infty]
,
A
be a given dilation,
f\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
and
s
be a nonnegative integer and
B\in\mathfrak{B}
. Then
there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
f
and
B
, such that

\sup_{x\in B}\left|P_{B}^{s}(f)(x)\right|\leq C\frac{\int_{B}|f(x)|\,dx}{|B|}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
A
be a given dilation,
p(\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}
,
s
be a nonnegative integer and
q\in[1,\,\infty)
. Then

\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}=\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

Proof.

By the above definition, it is easy to see that

\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}\supseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}.

Conversely, from Lemma
4.3
and the Hölder inequality, we conclude that, for any

B\in\mathfrak{B}
,
Q\in P_{s}
,

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|P_{B}^{s}(Q-f)(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Q(x)-f(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Q(x)-f(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}.

Therefore, by the Minkowski inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|B|}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|P_{B}^{s}(f)(x)-f(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|B|}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|P_{B}^{s}(Q-f)(x)+f(x)-Q(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{|B|}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\left[\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}|Q(x)-f(x)|^{q}\,dx\right]^{1/q},

which implies that

\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.4
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
A
be a given dilation,
p(\cdot)\in C^{\mathrm{log}}
,
r\in(0,\,1]
,
s
be a nonnegative integer and
q\in[1,\,\infty)
. Then, for any
continuous linear functional
{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}=\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{L}}\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}}:=

\displaystyle\sup\left\{|{\mathcal{L}}(f)|:\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup\{|{\mathcal{L}}(a)|:a\ \mathrm{is}\ (p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)\mathrm{-atom}\},

where
(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}
denotes the dual space of
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}
.

Proof.

Let
a
be a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom. Then we have that
\|a\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}}\leq 1
. Therefore,

\sup\{|{\mathcal{L}}(a)|:a\ \mathrm{is}\ (p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)\mathrm{-atom}\}\leq\sup\left\{|{\mathcal{L}}(f)|:\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A}}\leq 1\right\}.

Moreover, let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}
and
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}}\leq 1
. Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we know that there exist
\{\lambda_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence of

(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atoms,
{\{a_{i}^{k}\}}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, supported, respectively,
on
{\{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}^{k}a_{i}^{k}\ \ \mathrm{in\ }\ {\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}\,\,\mathrm{and}\,\,\mathrm{a.}\,\,\mathrm{e.}

and

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kr}\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\chi_{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\right]^{1/r}\leq 1+\varepsilon.

Therefore, from the boundedness of
{\mathcal{L}}
,
\lambda^{k}_{i}\sim 2^{k}\|\chi_{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
and
r\in(0,\,1]
, we further conclude that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}(g)|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\lambda_{i}^{k}\right||{\mathcal{L}}(a_{i}^{k})|\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\lambda_{i}^{k}\right|\sup\{|{\mathcal{L}}(a)|:a\ \mathrm{is}\ (p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)\mathrm{-atom}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kr}\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\chi_{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\right]^{1/r}\sup\{|{\mathcal{L}}(a)|:a\ \mathrm{is}\ (p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)\mathrm{-atom}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\sup\{|{\mathcal{L}}(a)|:a\ \mathrm{is}\ (p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)\mathrm{-atom}\}.

Combined with the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
and hence finishes the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

For any
q\in[1,\,\infty]
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Denote by
L_{\mathrm{comp}}^{q}
the set of all functions
f\in L^{q}
with
compact support and

L_{\mathrm{comp}}^{q,\,s}:=\left\{f\in L_{\mathrm{comp}}^{q}:\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}f(x)x^{\alpha}\,dx=0,\,|\alpha|\leq s\right\}.

The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
A
be a given dilation,
r\in(0,\,1]
,
p(\cdot)\in C^{\mathrm{log}}
,
p_{+}\in(0,\,1]
,
q\in(\max\{p_{+},\,1\},\,\infty)
and
s\in[\lfloor(1/{p_{-}}-1){\ln b/\ln\lambda_{-}}\rfloor,\,\infty)\cap{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
with
p_{-}
as in (
2.4
). Then

(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A})^{*}=(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}=\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}=\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}

in the following sense: for any

\psi\in\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
or
\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
, the linear functional

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(g):=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\psi(x)g(x)\,dx,

initial defined for all
g\in L^{q,\,s}_{\mathrm{comp}}
, has a bounded extension to
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}=\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}
.

Conversely, if
{\mathcal{L}}
is a bounded linear functional on
\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}=\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,r}_{A}
, then
{\mathcal{L}}
has the form as in (
4.1
) with a unique
\psi\in\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
or
\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
.
Moreover,

\|\psi\|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{BMO}}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}\thicksim\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}\thicksim\|{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}},

where the implicit positive constants are independent of
\psi
.

Remark 4.7
.

We should point that,
when

p(\cdot):=p\in(0,\,1]
, this result is also new.

Proof of Theorem
4.6
.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
4.4
, we only need to show

\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}=(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}.

Firstly, we prove that

\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}\subset(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
and
a
be a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom with
{\rm{\,supp\,}}a\subset B\in\mathfrak{B}
. Then, by the vanishing moment condition of
a
, Hölder’s inequality and the size condition of
a
, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\psi(x)a(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle=\inf_{P\in P_{s}}\left|\int_{B}(\psi(x)-P(x))a(x)\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{L^{q}}\inf_{P\in P_{s}}\left[\int_{B}|\psi(x)-P(x)|^{q^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|B|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\inf_{P\in P_{s}}\left[\int_{B}|\psi(x)-P(x)|^{q^{\prime}}\,dx\right]^{1/q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}.

Therefore, for
\{\lambda_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathbb{C}
and a sequence
\{a_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
of
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atoms supported, respectively, on

\{x_{i}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}\}_{i\in{\mathbb{N}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset\mathfrak{B}
and

g=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{i}^{k}a_{i}^{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}},

from (
4.2
), we deduce that

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{\psi}(g)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}}\psi(x)g(x)\,dx\right|\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\lambda_{i}^{k}\right|\left|\int_{B}\left|\psi(x)-P(x)\right|\left|a_{i}^{k}(x)\right|\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\left|\lambda_{i}^{k}\right|\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}2^{kr}\left\|\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}\chi_{x_{i}^{k}+B_{\ell_{i}^{k}}}\right\|^{r}_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\right]^{1/r}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}}}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}.

This implies that
\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}\subset(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}
.

Next we show that
(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}\subset\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
. For any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, let

S_{B}:L^{1}(B)\rightarrow P_{s}

be the natural projection satisfying, for any
g\in L^{1}
and
Q\in P_{s}
,

\int_{B}S_{B}(g)(x)Q(x)\,dx=\int_{B}g(x)Q(x)\,dx.

By a similar proof of
[
4
, (8.9)]
, we obtain that, for any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
and
g\in L^{1}(B)
,

\sup_{x\in B}\left|{S}_{B}(g)(x)\right|\lesssim\frac{\int_{B}|g(z)|\,dz}{|B|}.

Define

L^{q}_{0}(B):=\left\{g\in L^{q}(B):{S}_{B}(g)(x)=0\ \mathrm{and}\ g\ \mathrm{is}\ \mathrm{not}\ \mathrm{zero}\ \mathrm{almost}\ \mathrm{everywhere}\right\},

where

L^{q}(B):=\{f\in L^{q}:\mathrm{supp}f\subset B\}

with
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and
B\in\mathfrak{B}
,

For any
g\in L^{q}_{0}(B)
, set

\displaystyle a(x):=\frac{|B|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(B)}^{-1}g(x)\chi_{B}(x)

Then
a
is a
(p(\cdot),\,q,\,s)
-atom. By this and Lemma
4.5
, we obtain, for any
{\mathcal{L}}\in(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}

and
g\in L^{q}_{0}(B)
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}(g)|\leq\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}{|B|^{1/q}}\|g\|_{L^{q}(B)}\|{\mathcal{L}}\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}}.

Thus, by the Hahn-Banach theorem, it can be extended to a bounded linear functional on
L^{q}(B)
with the same norm.

If
q\in(1,\,\infty]
, by the duality of

L^{q}(B)
is
L^{q^{\prime}}(B)
, we see that there exists a
\Phi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(B)
such that, for any
f\in L^{q}_{0}(B)
,

{\mathcal{L}}(f)=\int_{B}f(x)\Phi(x)\,dx.
In what follows, for any
B\in\mathfrak{B}
, let
P_{s}(B)
denote all the
P_{s}
elements vanishing outside
B
.
Now we prove that, if there exists another function
\Phi^{\prime}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(B)
such that, for any

f\in L^{q}_{0}(B)
and

{\mathcal{L}}(f)=\int_{B}f(x)\Phi^{\prime}(x)\,dx,

then
\Phi^{\prime}-\Phi\in P_{s}(B)
. For this, we only need to show that, if
\Phi,\,\Phi^{\prime}\in L^{1}(B)
such that, for any

f\in L^{\infty}_{0}(B)
,
\int_{B}f(x)\Phi^{\prime}(x)\,dx=\int_{B}f(x)\Phi(x)\,dx,
then
\Phi-\Phi^{\prime}\in P_{s}(B)
. In fact, for any

f\in L^{\infty}_{0}(B)
, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{B}[f(x)-{S}_{B}(f)(x)][\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi(x)]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B}f(x)[\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi(x)]\,dx-\int_{B}f(x){S}_{B}(\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi(x))\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B}f(x)[\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi(x)-{S}_{B}(\Phi^{\prime}-\Phi)(x)]\,dx.

Therefore, for a.e.
x\in B\in\mathfrak{B}
, we have

\Phi^{\prime}(x)-\Phi(x)={S}_{B}(\Phi^{\prime}-\Phi)(x).

Hence
\Phi^{\prime}-\Phi\in P_{s}(B)
. From this, we see that, for any
q\in(1,\,\infty]
and
f\in L^{q}_{0}(B)
,
there exists a unique
\Phi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(B)/P_{s}(B)
such that

{\mathcal{L}}(f)=\int_{B}f(x)\Phi(x)\,dx.

For any
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
g\in L_{0}^{q}(B_{j})
with
q\in(1,\,\infty)
, let

f_{j}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(B_{j})/P_{s}(B_{j})
be a unique function such that

{\mathcal{L}}(g)=\int_{B_{j}}f_{j}(x)g(x)\,dx.

Then, for any
i,\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
i<j
,
f_{j}|_{B_{i}}=f_{i}
. From this and the fact that, for any

g\in(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}
, there exists a number
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
g\in L_{0}^{q}(B_{j_{0}})
, we conclude that, for any

g\in(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}(g)=\int_{B}\psi(x)g(x)\,dx,

where
\psi(x):=f_{j}(x)
with
x\in B_{j}
.

Next we show that
\psi\in\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
. By
[
4
, (8.12)]
, (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we have that, for any
q\in(1,\,\infty)
,

B\in\mathfrak{B}
,

\displaystyle\inf_{P\in P_{s}}\|\psi-P\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(B)}=\|\psi\|_{(L_{0}^{q}(B))^{*}}\leq\frac{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}{|B|^{1/q}}\|{\mathcal{L}}\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}}.

Therefore, we have that, for any
q\in(1,\,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\frac{|B|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\inf_{P\in P_{s}}\|\psi-P\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(B)}=\sup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}}\frac{|B|^{1/q}}{\|\chi_{B}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}}\|\psi\|_{(L_{0}^{q}(B))^{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathcal{L}}\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{p(\cdot),\,q,\,s,\,r}_{A,\,\rm{atom}})^{*}},

which implies
\psi\in\mathcal{BMO}_{A}^{p(\cdot),\,q^{\prime},\,s}
.
This finishes the proof of Theorem
4.6
.
∎

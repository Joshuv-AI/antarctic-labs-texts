1.
Introduction

Some unsolved problems in
H^{\infty}
control theory require a deep understanding
of “inner mappings” from
\mathbb{D}
to certain domains in
\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
d>1
. For example, a special case of the problem of robust stabilization under structured uncertainty, or the
\mu
-synthesis problem
[
13
,
14
,
3
,
5
]
, leads naturally to a class of
\mu
-synthesis domains.
A typical member of this class of domains is

{\mathbb{E}}=\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:\quad 1-x_{1}z-x_{2}w+x_{3}zw\neq 0\mbox{ whenever }|z|\leq 1,|w|\leq 1\}

the
open tetrablock,
as was observed by Abouhajar, White and Young in
[
1
]
. The complex geometry of
\mathbb{E}
was further developed in
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
20
]
and associated operator theory in
[
9
,
8
]
. The solvability of the
\mu
-synthesis problem connected to
\mathbb{E}
can be expressed in terms of the existence of rational inner functions from the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
to the closure of
\mathbb{E}
subject to interpolation conditions
[
10
]
.

Recall that a classical
rational inner function
is a rational map
f
from the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
to its closure
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
with the property that
f
maps the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
into itself. See
[
12
]
for a survey of results, linking inner functions and operator theory.
We denote the closure of
\mathbb{E}
by
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
and we define a
rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner or
(
tetra-inner
)
function
to be a rational analytic map
x:\mathbb{D}\to\overline{\mathbb{E}}
such that
x
maps
\mathbb{T}
into the distinguished boundary
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
of
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
. Here,
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
is the smallest closed subset of
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
on which every continuous function on
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
that is analytic in
\mathbb{E}
attains its maximum modulus. Of course, rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner functions have many similarities with rational inner functions. On the other hand, the complex geometry of
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
is richer than that of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, and so
rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner functions have some striking differences from rational inner functions.

Here are some points of difference between well-studied domains in
\mathbb{C}^{3}
,
such as the tridisc
\mathbb{D}^{3}
and the Euclidean ball
\mathbb{B}_{3}

on the one hand and
\mathbb{E}
on the other.
Firstly, whereas
\mathbb{D}^{3}
and
\mathbb{B}_{3}
are homogeneous (so that the holomorphic automorphisms of these domains act transitively),

\mathbb{E}
is inhomogeneous
[
20
]
.

Secondly, the distinguished boundary of
\mathbb{E}
differs markedly in its topological properties from those of
\mathbb{D}^{3}
and
\mathbb{B}_{3}
.
The distinguished boundaries of
\mathbb{D}^{3}
and
\mathbb{B}_{3}
are the
3
-dimensional torus and the
5
-sphere respectively. They are smooth manifolds without boundary. The distinguished boundary
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
of
\mathbb{E}

is homeomorphic to the solid torus
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\times\mathbb{T}
, which
has a boundary.
For a rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner function
x
the curve
x(\mathrm{e}^{it}),0\leq t<2\pi
, lies in

b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
and may or may not touch the boundary of
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
, and so the algebraic and geometric properties of
x
are dependent on that.

We call the set

(1.1)

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:x_{1}x_{2}=x_{3}\big\}

the
royal variety
of the tetrablock. The complex geodesic

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}\cap\mathbb{E}
is invariant under the group of biholomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{E}

[
20
]
. This paper shows that
the variety
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}
plays a central role in the function theory of
\mathbb{E}
. The intersection

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}\cap b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
is exactly the boundary of
b\overline{\mathbb{E}}
, that is,
\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}x_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|=1\}

[
1
, Theorem 7.1]
, which is homeomorphic to the
2
-torus
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}
.

These geometric properties of
\mathbb{E}
lead to very interesting facts in the theory of rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner functions that do not have analogues in the theory of classical inner functions.

One of the main theorems of this paper is the following.

Let

5.12
.

Another main result is the construction of rational

-inner functions of prescribed degree from the zeros of

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. Suppose
that
\alpha_{1}^{1},...,\alpha_{k_{1}}^{1}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
and
\alpha_{1}^{2},...,\alpha_{k_{2}}^{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}

\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
are distinct from the points of the set
\{\alpha_{j}^{i},j=1,...,k_{i},i=1,2\}\cap\mathbb{T}
. Then there exists a rational
\;\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner function
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}):\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{E}}
such that

(1)

the zeros of
x_{1}
in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, repeated according to multiplicity, are
\alpha_{1}^{1},...,\alpha_{k_{1}}^{1}
;

(2)

the zeros of
x_{2}
in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, repeated according to multiplicity, are
\alpha_{1}^{2},...,\alpha_{k_{2}}^{2}
;

(3)

the royal nodes of
x
are
\sigma_{1},...,\sigma_{n}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, with repetition according to the multiplicity of the nodes.

This function

can be constructed as follows. Let

and let

. Let
R
be defined by

R(\lambda)=t_{+}\prod_{j=1}^{n}(\lambda-\sigma_{j})(1-\overline{\sigma_{j}}\lambda).

Let

be defined by

E_{1}(\lambda)=t\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{2}\lambda).

Then the following statements hold:

(i)

There exists an outer polynomial

of degree at most
n
such that

\lambda^{-n}R(\lambda)+|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}=|D(\lambda)|^{2}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.

(ii)

The function
x
defined by

x=\bigg(\frac{E_{1}}{D},\frac{E_{1}^{\sim n}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)\;\text{where, for any polynomial $D$,}\;D^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{D\big(\frac{1}{\bar{\lambda}}\big)},

is a rational
\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner function such that the degree of

is equal to
n
and conditions (1), (2) and (3) hold. The royal polynomial

of

is equal to

.

Here the royal polynomial of
x
is defined as
R_{x}=\big[D^{\sim n}D-E_{1}E_{1}^{\sim n}\big]
.
The proof of this theorem is constructive: it gives an algorithm for the construction of a
3
-parameter family of such functions

.

we show that there exist useful relations between
\Gamma
-inner functions and
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions. Recall the definition of the symmetrised bidisc
\Gamma
.

Definition 3.1
.

The symmetrised bidisc is the set

\mathbb{G}\ {\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}}\ \big\{(z+w,zw):|z|<1,|w|<1\big\},

and its closure is

\Gamma\ {\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}}\ \big\{(z+w,zw):|z|\leq 1,|w|\leq 1\big\}.

In 1995 Jim Agler and Nicholas Young started the study of the symmetrised bidisc with the aim of solving a robust control problem in
H^{\infty}
control theory. Although, the aim has not yet been achieved, it turned out that the symmetrised bidisc has a rich structure and it has attracted
the attention of specialists in
the several complex variables and in operator theory.

We will use the co-ordinates
(s,p)
for points in the symmetrized bidisc
\mathbb{G}
, chosen to suggest ‘sum’ and ‘product’.
The following result
[
4
, Proposition 3.2]
provides practical criteria for membership of
\mathbb{G}
, of the distinguished boundary
b\Gamma
of
\Gamma
and of the topological boundary
\partial\Gamma
of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
4
, Proposition 3.2]

Let
(s,p)
belong to
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then

(1)

(s,p)
belongs to
\mathbb{G}
if and only if

|s-\overline{s}p|<1-|p|^{2};

(2)

(s,p)
belongs to
\Gamma
if and only if

|s|\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad|s-\overline{s}p|\leq 1-|p|^{2};

(3)

(s,p)
lies in
b\Gamma
if and only if

|p|=1,\quad|s|\leq 2\quad\text{and }\quad s-\overline{s}p=0;

(4)

(s,p)\in\partial\Gamma
if and only if

{\@index{\@indexphrase{$b\Gamma$}}}|s|\leq 2\quad\text{and}\quad|s-\overline{s}p|=1-|p|^{2}.

\Gamma
-inner functions were defined and studied in
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

A
\Gamma
-inner function is an analytic function
h:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\Gamma
such that the radial limit

(3.1)

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}h(r\lambda)

exists and belongs to
b\Gamma
for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with respect to Lebesgue measure.

By Fatou’s Theorem, the limit (
3.1
) exists for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let f be a polynomial of degree less than or equal to
n
, where
n\geq 0
. Then we define the polynomial
f^{\sim n}
by

f^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{f(1/\overline{\lambda})}.

The polynomial
f^{\vee}
is defined by

f^{\vee}(\lambda)=\overline{f\big(\overline{\lambda}\big)}.

Remark 3.5
.

One can see that

(1)

f^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{f^{(}1/\overline{\lambda})}=\lambda^{n}f^{\vee}(1/\lambda).

(2) If
f
is a polynomial of degree
k
, then, for
n\geq k
,
\big(f^{\sim n}\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)=f(\lambda)
.

Algebraic and geometric aspects of rational
\Gamma
-inner functions were studied in
[
6
]
. We are going to use some results from the paper.

Proposition 3.6
.

[
6
, Proposition 2.2]

Let
h=(s,p)
be a rational
\Gamma
-inner function of degree
n
. Then there exist polynomials
E
and
D
such that

(1)

\deg(E),\deg(D)\leq n
,

(2)

E^{\sim n}=E
,

(3)

D(\lambda)\neq 0
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,

(4)

\left|E(\lambda)\right|\leq 2\left|D(\lambda)\right|
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,

(5)

s=\dfrac{E}{D}
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
,

(6)

p=\dfrac{D^{\sim n}}{D}
on
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
.

Furthermore,
E_{1}
and
D_{1}
is a second pair of polynomials satisfying conditions (1)–(6) if and only if there exists a nonzero
t\in\mathbb{R}
such that

E_{1}=tE\qquad\text{and}\qquad D_{1}=tD.

Conversely, if
E
and
D
are polynomials which satisfy conditions (1), (2), (4),
D(\lambda)\neq 0
on
\mathbb{D}
, and
s
and
p
are defined by equations (5) and (6), then
h=(s,p)
is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function of degree less than or equal to
n
.

The royal variety
\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}
of the symmetrised bidisc is

\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}=\{(s,p)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:s^{2}=4p\}.

Definition 3.7
.

[
6
, Page 7]

Let
h=(s,p)
be a
\Gamma
-inner function of degree
n
. Let
E
and
D
be as in Proposition
3.6
. The royal polynomial
R_{h}
of
h
is defined by

R_{h}(\lambda)=4D(\lambda)D^{\sim n}(\lambda)-E(\lambda)^{2}.

Definition 3.8
.

[
6
, Definition 3.6]

Let
h
be a rational
\Gamma
-inner function such that

h(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\nsubseteq\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}\cap\Gamma
. Let
R_{h}
be the royal polynomial of
h
, and let
\sigma
be a zero of
R_{h}
of order
\ell
. We define the multiplicity
\#\sigma
of
\sigma
(as a royal node of

) by

\#\sigma=\begin{cases}\ell\quad\quad\quad\ \ \ \text{if}\ \sigma\in\mathbb{D},\\
\frac{1}{2}\ell\quad\quad\quad\ \text{if}\ \sigma\in\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

We define the type of

to be the ordered pair
(n,k)
, where

, and

Theorem 3.9
.

[

Let

\deg(h)=n
.

4.
Rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions

In this section we give a definition of the degree of a rational tetra-inner function
x
by means of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}
. Recall that the rational inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
of degree
n
are exactly the finite Blaschke products of degree
n
. Similar to this description of rational inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
we give an algorithm for the construction of
all rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
in Theorem
4.15
. In
[
6
]
, the authors describe all rational
\Gamma
-inner functions (see Proposition
3.6
). We use this description and the connection between
\Gamma
-inner functions and
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions to describe all rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions on
\mathbb{D}
.

4.1.
Relations between
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions and
\Gamma
-inner functions

Definition 4.1
.

An
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner or tetra-inner function is a map
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{E}}
that is analytic and is such that the radial limit

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}f(r\lambda)

exists and belongs to b
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
with respect to Lebesgue measure.

Remark 4.2
.

Let
x:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{\overline{E}}
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. Since
x
is rational and bounded on
\mathbb{D}
it has no poles in
\mathbb{\overline{D}}
and hence
x
is continuous on
\mathbb{\overline{D}}
.
Thus one can consider the continuous function

\tilde{x}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow b\mathbb{\overline{E}},\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\tilde{x}(\lambda)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}x(r\lambda)\quad\mbox{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

In future we will use the same notation
x
for both continuous functions
x
and
\tilde{x}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be an
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. Then

(1)

x_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda)
,
|x_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 1
and
|x_{3}(\lambda)|=1
for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
;

(2)

x_{3}
is an inner function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

(1) By the definition of
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function

x(\lambda)=(x_{1}(\lambda),x_{2}(\lambda),x_{3}(\lambda))\in b\mathbb{\overline{E}},\quad\text{for almost every}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}

and, by Theorem
2.10
,

x_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{2}}(\lambda)x_{3}(\lambda),\quad|x_{3}(\lambda)|=1\quad\text{and}\quad|x_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 1\quad\text{for almost all }\lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

(2) Since

x_{3}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{\overline{D}}\quad\text{and},\quad\text{for almost all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T},\ |x_{3}(\lambda)|=1,

x_{3}
is an inner function.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. By Lemma
4.3
,
x_{3}
is an inner function on
\mathbb{D}
, and so
x_{3}
is a finite Blaschke product.

In
[
9
]
the author shows that there is a relation between points in the symmetrised bidisc and the tetrablock as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

[
9
, Lemma 3.2]

A point
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
belongs to the tetrablock if and only if the pair
(x_{1}+zx_{2},zx_{3})
is in the symmetrised bidisc
\mathbb{G}
for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
(1),
(s,p)\in\mathbb{G}
if and only if

(4.1)

|s-\overline{s}p|<1-|p|^{2}.

Suppose that
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{E}
,
s_{z}=x_{1}+zx_{2}
and
p_{z}=zx_{3}
.

\displaystyle|s_{z}-\overline{s_{z}}p_{z}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x_{1}+zx_{2}-\overline{(x_{1}+zx_{2})}zx_{3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}+z(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|,\qquad\text{since}\ |z|=1,

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 1-|x_{3}|^{2}=1-|p_{z}|^{2},\quad\text{by Theorem \ref{tetbnd}}.

Hence
(s_{z},p_{z})\in\mathbb{G}
.

Conversely, let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
and, for
z\in\mathbb{T}
, let

(4.2)

s_{z}=x_{1}+zx_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad p_{z}=zx_{3}.

Suppose for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
, we have
(s_{z},p_{z})\in\mathbb{G}
.
We want to show that
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{E}
. Let us prove that

\displaystyle|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|<1-|x_{3}|^{2}.

By assumption for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
,
|s_{z}-\overline{s_{z}}p_{z}|<1-|x_{3}|^{2}
. By equations (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}+z\big(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}\big)|<1-|x_{3}|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\ z\in\mathbb{T}.

Let

\begin{cases}z=e^{i\theta}\hskip 113.81102pt\ \ \theta\in(0,2\pi];\\
w_{1}=x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}=|w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}\qquad\theta_{1}\in(0,2\pi];\\
w_{2}=x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}=|w_{2}|e^{i\theta_{2}}\qquad\theta_{2}\in(0,2\pi].\end{cases}

Now substitute
z
,
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in inequality (
4.3
)

\big||w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}+e^{i\theta}(|w_{2}|e^{i\theta_{2}})\big|<1-|x_{3}|^{2}.

This implies that

\big||w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}+|w_{2}|e^{i(\theta+\theta_{2})}\big|<1-|x_{3}|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\ e^{i\theta}.

We can choose
\theta
such that
\theta+\theta_{2}=\theta_{1}
, that is,
\theta=\theta_{1}-\theta_{2}
. Hence

\big||w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}+|w_{2}|e^{i\theta_{1}}\big|=|e^{i\theta_{1}}|(|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|)=|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|=|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|<1-|x_{3}|^{2}.

By Theorem
2.4
,
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{E}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

A point
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
belongs to the closed tetrablock if and only if for every
a\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
,
(ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2},x_{3})\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Suppose
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}
. Consider
(s_{a},p_{a})=(ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2},x_{3})
.
By Proposition
3.2
(2),
(s,p)\in\Gamma
if and only if

(4.4)

\displaystyle|s-\overline{s}p|\leq 1-|p|^{2}\;\text{and}\;|s|\leq 2.

(4.5)

\displaystyle|s_{a}-\overline{s_{a}}p_{a}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2}-\overline{(ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2})}x_{3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|a(x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3})+\overline{a}(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|a(x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3})|+|\overline{a}(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3})|,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|,\qquad\text{since}\ |a|\leq 1,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-|x_{3}|^{2},\qquad\text{by Theorem \ref{tetbnd2}}.

Thus,

|s_{a}-\overline{s_{a}}p_{a}|\leq 1-|x_{3}|^{2}=1-|p_{a}|^{2}
and

|s_{a}|=|ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2}|\leq 2.

Hence
(s_{a},p_{a})\in\Gamma
.

Conversely, let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
Suppose for every
a\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
, we have
(s_{a},p_{a})\in\Gamma
where

(4.6)

s_{a}=ax_{1}+\overline{a}x_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad p_{a}=x_{3}.

By equations (
4.5
) and (
4.6
), we have

(4.7)

|s_{a}-\overline{s_{a}}p_{a}|=|a(x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3})+\overline{a}\big(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}\big)|\leq 1-|p_{a}|^{2},\quad\text{for all}\ a\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}.

Take
a\in\mathbb{T}
, then

\begin{cases}a=e^{i\theta}\hskip 113.81102pt\ \ \theta\in(0,2\pi];\\
w_{1}=x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}=|w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}\qquad\theta_{1}\in(0,2\pi];\\
w_{2}=x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}=|w_{2}|e^{i\theta_{2}}\qquad\theta_{2}\in(0,2\pi].\end{cases}

Substitute
a
,
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
into inequality (
4.7
), to get

\displaystyle|a(x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3})+\overline{a}\big(x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}\big)|=\big|e^{i\theta}|w_{1}|e^{i\theta_{1}}+e^{-i\theta}|w_{2}|e^{i\theta_{2}}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big||w_{1}|e^{i(\theta+\theta_{1})}+|w_{2}|e^{i(\theta_{2}-\theta)}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 1-|x_{3}|^{2},

for every
\theta\in(0,2\pi]
. Now choose
\theta=\dfrac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}{2}
to get

\displaystyle|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|e^{i(\frac{\theta_{2}+\theta_{1}}{2})}|\big(|w_{1}|+|w_{2}|\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big||w_{1}|e^{i(\frac{\theta_{2}+\theta_{1}}{2})}+|w_{2}|e^{i(\frac{\theta_{2}+\theta_{1}}{2})}\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big||w_{1}|e^{i(\frac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}{2}+\theta_{1})}+|w_{2}|e^{i(\theta_{2}-\frac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}{2})}\big|\leq 1-|x_{3}|^{2}.

Therefore
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}
.

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
s,p\in\mathbb{C}
be such that
|s|\leq 2
and
|p|\leq 1
. The pair
(s,p)
belongs to
\Gamma
if and only if
\left(\tfrac{1}{2}s,\tfrac{1}{2}s,p\right)\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
,

\left(\tfrac{1}{2}s,\tfrac{1}{2}s,p\right)\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad|\tfrac{1}{2}s-\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{s}p|+|\tfrac{1}{2}s-\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{s}p|\leq 1-|p|^{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle(\tfrac{1}{2}s,\tfrac{1}{2}s,p)\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle 2|\tfrac{1}{2}s-\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{s}p|\leq 1-|p|^{2}\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle|s-\overline{s}p|\leq 1-|p|^{2}.

By assumption
|s|\leq 2
, hence by Proposition
3.2
(2),

(\tfrac{1}{2}s,\tfrac{1}{2}s,p)\in\mathbb{\overline{E}}\Leftrightarrow(s,p)\in\Gamma.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. Then

(1)

h_{1}(\lambda)=\big(x_{1}(\lambda)+x_{2}(\lambda),x_{3}(\lambda)\big)
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function;

(2)

h_{2}(\lambda)=(ix_{1}(\lambda)-ix_{2}(\lambda),x_{3}(\lambda))
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
, is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function.

Proof.

(1)

By Lemma
4.5
, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
x(\lambda)\in\mathbb{E}
implies that

\big(x_{1}(\lambda)+x_{2}(\lambda),x_{3}(\lambda)\big)\in\mathbb{G}.

Consider
h_{1}=(s_{1},p_{1})
where

s_{1}(\lambda)=x_{1}(\lambda)+x_{2}(\lambda)\quad\text{and}\quad p_{1}(\lambda)=x_{3}(\lambda),\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

It is obvious that
h_{1}
is a rational function from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{G}
. By assumption,
x
is an
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. Thus
x(\lambda)\in b\mathbb{\overline{E}}
for almost every
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. By Theorem
2.10
and Lemma
2.11
, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(4.8)

x_{2}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda),\quad x_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda),\quad|x_{3}(\lambda)|=1\quad\text{and}\,\,|x_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 1.

It is clear that

|p_{1}(\lambda)|=|x_{3}(\lambda)|=1\qquad\text{for}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T},

and, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle|s_{1}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|x_{1}(\lambda)+x_{2}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|x_{1}(\lambda)|+|x_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 2.

Since, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,
x_{2}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda)
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{s_{1}(\lambda)}p_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}+\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}]x_{3}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda)+\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda),\quad\text{by equations \eqref{dist-bound},}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}(\lambda)+x_{2}(\lambda)=s_{1}(\lambda).

Hence
s_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{s_{1}(\lambda)}p_{1}(\lambda)
for almost every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{T}}.

Therefore, by Proposition
3.2
(3),
h_{1}
is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function.

(2)
Following the same steps as (1), let
h_{2}(\lambda)=(s_{2}(\lambda),p_{2}(\lambda)),
where

s_{2}(\lambda)=ix_{1}(\lambda)-ix_{2}(\lambda)\qquad\text{and}\qquad p_{2}(\lambda)=x_{3}(\lambda),\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

By Lemma
4.6
,
h_{2}
is rational function from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{G}
. Since
x
is an
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function,
x(\lambda)\in b\mathbb{\overline{E}}
for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.
By Proposition
3.2
, to prove that
h_{2}
is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function we need to show that

|p_{2}(\lambda)|=1,\,\,|s_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 2\,\,\text{and}\,s_{2}(\lambda)=\overline{s_{2}(\lambda)}p_{2}(\lambda)\quad\text{for almost every }\lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

By Theorem
2.10
, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,
|p(\lambda)|=|x_{3}(\lambda)|=1
and

\displaystyle|s_{2}(\lambda)|\leq|ix_{1}(\lambda)|+|ix_{2}(\lambda)|\leq 2.

By Lemma
2.11
,
x_{2}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda)
for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.
Hence, for almost all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle\overline{s_{2}(\lambda)}p_{2}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\overline{ix_{1}(\lambda)}-\overline{ix_{2}(\lambda)}]x_{3}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\big(\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}\big)x_{3}(\lambda)-i\big(\overline{x_{1}(\lambda)}\big)x_{3}(\lambda),\quad\text{ by equations \eqref{dist-bound}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ix_{1}(\lambda)-ix_{2}(\lambda)=s_{2}(\lambda).

Hence
s_{2}(\lambda)=\overline{s_{2}(\lambda)}p_{2}(\lambda)
for almost every
\lambda\in{\mathbb{T}}.

Therefore, by Proposition
3.2
,
h_{2}
is a rational
\Gamma
-inner function.
∎

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function. Then

x_{1}(\lambda)=x_{2}^{\vee}(1/\lambda)x_{3}(\lambda)\quad\text{for all}\ \ \lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.10
, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

x_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{x_{2}(\lambda)}x_{3}(\lambda).

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, we have
|\lambda|=1
, that is,

4.2.

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function

Let us define the notion of the degree

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function

Definition 4.11
.

The degree

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function

x_{*}(1)
, where

Lemma 4.12
.

b\mathbb{\overline{E}}
is homotopic to
\mathbb{T}
and
\pi_{1}(b\mathbb{\overline{E}})=\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

The maps

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle:b\mathbb{\overline{E}}\rightarrow\mathbb{T},\ \text{defined by}\ f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=x_{3},

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow b\mathbb{\overline{E}},\ \text{defined by}\ g(z)=(0,0,z),

satisfy

(g\circ f)(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=g\big(f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\big)=g(x_{3})=(0,0,x_{3})

and

(f\circ g)(z)=f(0,0,z)=z,

that is,
f\circ g=\rm id_{\mathbb{T}}
. If
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in b\mathbb{\overline{E}}
and
0\leq t\leq 1
, then
(tx_{1},tx_{2},x_{3})\in b\mathbb{\overline{E}}
. Let

. Consider the map

which is defined by

One can see that

h(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},0)=(0x_{1},0x_{2},x_{3})=(0,0,x_{3})=(g\circ f)(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\ \text{and}

h(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},1)=(1x_{1},1x_{2},x_{3})=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})={\rm id_{b\mathbb{\overline{E}}}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Therefore

and
\rm id_{b\mathbb{\overline{E}}}

. Hence

and

.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Let

be a finite Blaschke product. Then the degree of

is equal to
B_{*}(1)
.

Proof.

Since

One can consider the map,

, and

B_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T})=\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{T})=\mathbb{Z}.

Now

\rm id_{\mathbb{T}}

, when

is regarded as a continuous map from
\mathbb{T}
to
\mathbb{T}
. Therefore

about

\displaystyle n(\gamma,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}\frac{dz}{z-a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{B^{\prime}(z)dz}{B(z)-a}.

By the Argument Principle,
[
7
, Theorem 18]
, the integral

\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{B^{\prime}(z)}{B(z)-a}dz,

is equal to the number of zeros of

in
\mathbb{D}
.
It is clear that
B
has
N
zeros, counting multiplicities, and has degree

.
Therefore the number of zeros of

is equal to the winding number of
\gamma
about

, and it is equal to

.

∎

By equality (

), for

5.

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions

The formula for a Blaschke product is an explicit representation of a rational inner function in terms of its zeros and one other parameter (a unimodular complex number). In this chapter we aim to find a comparable representation for rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions. The first question is: what is the tetrablock analogue of the zeros of an inner function? We shall show that one satisfactory choice consists of the royal nodes of an
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function
x
together with the zeros of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
.
We construct a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function
x
from its royal nodes and the zeros of
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
. We show that there exists a

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions with prescribed zero sets of

,
x_{2}
and prescribed royal nodes. We also prove that a nonconstant rational

-inner function

of degree

\mathbb{\overline{D}}
to the royal variety of
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
or

n
times.

5.1.

\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function

We define the

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}=\big\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:x_{1}x_{2}=x_{3}\big\}.

It was shown in
[

]

.
By Theorem
4.15
, for a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}(\lambda)\bigg[\dfrac{D^{\sim n}}{D}-\dfrac{E_{1}E_{2}}{D^{2}}\bigg](\lambda)

\displaystyle\big[D^{\sim n}D-E_{1}E_{2}\big](\lambda).

\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)=|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}
.

Proof.

(1) For
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}[D^{\sim n}D-E_{1}E_{2}](\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}[\lambda^{n}\overline{D(1/\overline{\lambda})}D(\lambda)-E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)E_{2}(\lambda)],\quad\text{since}\ \ E_{1}(\lambda)=E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}[\lambda^{n}\overline{D(\lambda)}D(\lambda)-\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}E_{2}(\lambda)],\quad\text{since}\ \ E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})=E_{2}(\lambda)\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}[\lambda^{n}\overline{D(\lambda)}D(\lambda)-\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(\lambda)}E_{2}(\lambda)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{2}(\lambda)|^{2}.

(2) Since
x
is rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function, by Lemma
4.17
,

(5.2)

|E_{1}(\lambda)|=|E_{2}(\lambda)|\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

By equations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
),

\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)=|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}\quad\text{for}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be two polynomials such that
\deg E_{1},\deg E_{2}\leq n
. Then

E_{1}(\lambda)=E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}\quad\text{if and only if}\quad E_{2}(\lambda)=E_{1}^{\sim n}(\lambda)\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

Proof.

Suppose that
E_{1}(\lambda)=E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
. Then by definition,

E_{1}(\lambda)=E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

Therefore, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle E_{1}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(1/\lambda^{n})E_{1}(\lambda)=\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle(1/\overline{\lambda})^{n}\overline{E_{1}(\lambda)}=E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{1}(1/\overline{\lambda})}=E_{2}(\lambda)\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle E_{1}^{\sim n}(\lambda)=E_{2}(\lambda)\ \text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

The converse is obvious.
∎

Definition 5.5
.

A nonzero polynomial
R
is
n
-balanced if

(1)

\deg(R)\leq 2n
,

(2)

R
is
2n
-symmetric, and

(3)

\lambda^{-n}R(\lambda)\geq 0
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.

For completeness, we shall say that zeros of the zero polynomial have infinite order.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
x
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function of degree
n
and let
R_{x}
be the royal polynomial of
x
. Then
R_{x}
is
2n
-symmetric,
\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)\geq 0
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, and the zeros of
R_{x}
on
\mathbb{T}
have even order or infinite order.

Proof.

To show that
R_{x}
is
2n
-symmetric we have to prove that
R_{x}^{\sim{2n}}(\lambda)=R_{x}(\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
.
Recall that

R_{x}(\lambda)=D^{\sim n}(\lambda)D(\lambda)-E_{1}(\lambda)E_{2}(\lambda),\quad\text{where}\ x=\bigg(\frac{E_{1}}{D},\frac{E_{2}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg),

By Theorem
4.15
(7) and Lemma
5.4
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

E_{1}(\lambda)=E_{2}^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})},\qquad E_{2}(\lambda)=E_{1}^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{1}(1/\overline{\lambda})}.

Now

\displaystyle R_{x}^{\sim{2n}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2n}\overline{R_{x}(1/\overline{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2n}\overline{\big[D^{\sim n}(1/\overline{\lambda})D(1/\overline{\lambda})-E_{1}(1/\overline{\lambda})E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})\big]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\overline{D^{\sim n}(1/\overline{\lambda})}\ \lambda^{n}\overline{D(1/\overline{\lambda})}-\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{1}(1/\overline{\lambda})}\ \lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(\lambda)D^{\sim n}(\lambda)-E_{2}(\lambda)E_{1}(\lambda)=R_{x}(\lambda).

Hence
R_{x}
is
2n
-symmetric.

Clearly, if
x(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{\mathbb{\overline{E}}}
, the royal polynomial
R_{x}
is identically zero. Thus the zeros of
R_{x}
on
\mathbb{T}
have infinite order.

On the other hand, if

, by Proposition
5.3
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(5.3)

\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)=|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{2}(\lambda)|^{2}.

By Theorem
4.15
(6),

(5.4)

|D|^{2}-|E_{2}|^{2}\geq 0\ \ \text{on}\ \mathbb{T}.

By equations (
5.3
) and (
5.4
),
\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)\geq 0
on
\mathbb{T}
. By the Fejér-Riesz theorem
[
18
, Section 53]
, there exists an analytic polynomial

of degree
n
such that

is outer and

\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)=|P(\lambda)|^{2}\qquad\text{for all}\ \lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

Hence if
\sigma\in\mathbb{T}
is a zero of
R_{x}
, then
\sigma
is a zero of even order. Therefore in the case
x(\overline{\mathbb{D}})\nsubseteq\mathcal{R}_{\overline{\mathbb{E}}}
, the zeros of
R_{x}
that lie in

∎

Definition 5.9

denote the collection of rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions of type

.

Remark 5.10
.

[
6
, Equations (3.2) and (3.3)]

For any
m
-symmetric polynomial

, the following two relations hold

(1)

\deg(f)=m-\mathrm{ord}_{0}(f).

(2)

Since

is
m
-symmetric, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
is a zero of

is also a zero of

Theorem 5.11
.

Let

be nonconstant. Then the degree of

is equal to
n
.

Proof.

Let
R_{x}
be the royal polynomial of
x
. By assumption

and is nonconstant. Hence

and
x(\mathbb{\overline{\mathbb{D}}})\nsubseteq\mathcal{R}_{\overline{\mathbb{E}}}
. Thus

is not identically zero. By Proposition
5.6
,
R_{x}
is
2\deg(x)
-symmetric. By Remark
5.10

(1)
and (2), it follows that

\deg(R_{x})=2\deg(x)-\mathrm{ord}_{0}(R_{x})

and

\mathrm{ord}_{0}(R_{x})+2\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}}(R_{x})+\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{x})=\deg(R_{x}).

Substitute the first equation in the second equation,

\mathrm{ord}_{0}(R_{x})+2\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}}(R_{x})+\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{x})=2\deg(x)-\mathrm{ord}_{0}(R_{x}),

which implies that

2\mathrm{ord}_{0}(R_{x})+2\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}}(R_{x})+\mathrm{ord}_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{x})=2\deg(x).

Therefore, by Definition
5.9
,

∎

Lemma 5.15
.

Let

and

in

in
\mathbb{\overline{D}}

E_{1}(\lambda)=t\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{2}\lambda),

Since

\alpha_{1}^{2},...,\alpha_{k_{2}}^{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}

and

respectively, we have

E_{1}(\lambda)=(\lambda-\alpha_{1}^{1})...(\lambda-\alpha_{k_{1}}^{1})p_{1}(\lambda)

where the polynomials
p_{1}(\lambda)
and

do not vanish in
\mathbb{\overline{D}}
.

Since
E_{1}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda)}}
on
\mathbb{\overline{D}}
, we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle E_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\overline{(1/\overline{\lambda}-\alpha_{1}^{2})}...\overline{(1/\overline{\lambda}-\alpha_{k_{2}}^{2})}.\overline{p_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n-k_{2}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\lambda)...(1-\overline{\alpha}_{k_{2}}^{2}\lambda).\overline{p_{2}(1/\overline{\lambda})}.

Since
\deg E_{1}\leq n
and

, equations (
5.6
) and (

) implies that

can be written in the form

\displaystyle E_{1}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}(\lambda-\alpha_{1}^{1})...(\lambda-\alpha_{k_{1}}^{1})\ \ (1-\overline{\alpha}_{1}^{2}\lambda)...(1-\overline{\alpha}_{k_{2}}^{2}\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t_{1}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{2}\lambda),\quad\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}},

for some

, and

E_{2}(\lambda)=t_{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{1}\lambda)\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}},

for some

.
Since

,

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\overline{E_{1}(1/\overline{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\bigg(\overline{t_{1}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(1/\overline{\lambda}-\alpha_{j}^{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{2}1/\overline{\lambda})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\overline{t_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(1/\lambda-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(1-\alpha_{j}^{2}1/\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{t_{1}}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{1}\lambda)\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle E_{2}(\lambda)=t_{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k_{2}}(\lambda-\alpha_{j}^{2})\prod_{j=1}^{k_{1}}(1-\overline{\alpha}_{j}^{1}\lambda),\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}},

.

\alpha_{1}^{2},...,\alpha_{k_{2}}^{2}\in\overline{\mathbb{D}}

\overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner function

(2)

the zeros of

. Let

Then the following statements hold.

(ii)

\ \overline{\mathbb{E}}
-inner function such that the degree of

(i) By Lemma

,

is

. Therefore

By the Fejér-Riesz theorem, there exists an outer polynomial

.
By Proposition

, for all

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}R_{x}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}.

Therefore, by equation (

.

6.
Convex subsets of
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
and extremality

In this section we study convex subsets of
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
. We show that, for a fixed
x_{3}\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
, the subset
\mathbb{\overline{E}}\cap\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\{x_{3}\}\big)
is convex. Recall that the distinguished boundaries of the tridisc
\mathbb{D}^{3}
and the ball
\mathbb{B}_{3}
contain no line segments. Thus every inner function in the set of analytic functions
{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D}^{3})
from
\mathbb{D}
to

is an extreme point of
{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{D}^{3})
and every inner function in the set of analytic functions

from
\mathbb{D}
to

is an extreme point of
{\rm Hol}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B}_{3})
. However, this property contrasts sharply with the situation in the tetrablock. Despite the fact that the set
\mathcal{J}
of rational tetra-inner functions is not convex, the conventional notion of extreme point of
\mathcal{J}
is well defined and fruitful. In Theorem
6.19
, we prove that for
x\in\mathcal{R}^{n,k}
with

,

is not an extreme point. A class of extreme points of the set
\mathcal{J}
is given in Proposition
6.21
.

6.1.
Convex subsets in the tetrablock

Definition 6.1
.

A set
\Omega
in a vector space is convex if for all
z,w\in\Omega
and all
t
such that
0\leq t\leq 1
, the point
\;tz+(1-t)w\;

.

Proposition 6.2
.

[
1

\mathbb{\overline{E}}

Proof.

Take

∎

Let us show that the set
\mathbb{\overline{E}}

(x_{1},x_{2})
for a fixed

\mathbb{\overline{E}}\cap\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\times\{x_{3}\}\big)=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:|x_{1}-\overline{x_{2}}x_{3}|+|x_{2}-\overline{x_{1}}x_{3}|\leq 1-|x_{3}|^{2}\}

The following sets are convex:

R=r\prod_{j=1}^{k}Q_{\tau_{j}}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}Q_{\alpha_{j}},

for some
r>0
. Thus for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(6.23)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}R(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\lambda^{-n}\Bigg\{\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})(1-\overline{\tau_{j}}\lambda)\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}(\lambda-\alpha_{j})(1-\overline{\alpha_{j}}\lambda)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}.

By Proposition
5.3
and equation (
6.23
), for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(6.24)

|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}=\lambda^{-n}R(\lambda)=r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}.

Assume first that
n
is even and write
n=2m
. This implies that
k\leq m
. Define a polynomial
g
by

g(\lambda)=\overline{\tau}_{1}\dots\overline{\tau}_{k}\lambda^{m-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}.

Clearly, the polynomial
g
has degree
m+k\leq n
. Moreover,
g
is
n
-symmetric since

\displaystyle g^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{g\big(\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\Bigg\{\overline{\overline{\tau}_{1}\dots\overline{\tau}_{k}\frac{1}{(\overline{\lambda})^{m-k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}-\tau_{j})^{2}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{2m}\Bigg\{\tau_{1}\dots\tau_{k}\frac{1}{\lambda^{m-k}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\overline{\tau_{j}})^{2}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\tau}_{1}\dots\overline{\tau}_{k}\lambda^{m-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}=g(\lambda).

Let

E_{1}^{t}=E_{1}+tg\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{2}^{t}=E_{1}^{\sim n}+tg\qquad\text{for}\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

The polynomial
E_{1}^{t}
has degree at most
n
. We also have, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
,

(6.25)

\displaystyle\big(E_{2}^{t}\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(E_{1}^{\sim n}+tg\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(E_{1}^{\sim n}\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)+\big(tg\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\big(E_{1}^{t}+tg\big)(\lambda)=E_{1}^{t}(\lambda).

Note that, on
\mathbb{T}
,

(6.26)

\displaystyle|D|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D|^{2}-|E_{1}+tg|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D|^{2}-(E_{1}+tg)\overline{(E_{1}+tg)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D|^{2}-|E_{1}|^{2}-t^{2}|g|^{2}-2\text{Re}(tg\overline{E}_{1}).

Let
\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}
denote the supremum of
|E_{1}|
on
\mathbb{T}
. Then, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(6.27)

\displaystyle\text{Re}(tg(\lambda)\overline{E_{1}}(\lambda)\leq|tg(\lambda)E_{1}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|tE_{1}(\lambda)|\ \bigg|\overline{\tau}_{1}\dots\overline{\tau}_{j}\lambda^{m-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|tE_{1}(\lambda)|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}.

Note that, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

|g(\lambda)|^{2}=\big|\overline{\tau}_{1}\dots\overline{\tau}_{k}\lambda^{m-k}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}\big|^{2}=\big|\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}\big|^{2}.

Combine equations (
6.24
) and (
6.26
) and inequality (
6.27
), for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
, to get

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}(\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2\text{Re}\big(tg(\lambda)\overline{E}_{1}(\lambda)\big),\ \text{(by equation}\ \eqref{eqn66})\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2\text{Re}\big(tg(\lambda)\overline{E}_{1}(\lambda)\big),\ \text{(by equation}\ \eqref{eq65})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2},\ \text{(by inequality}\ \eqref{eq67})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}r\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|Q_{\alpha_{j}}(\lambda)|-|t|^{2}\big|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\big|^{2}-2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\Bigg\{rM-\bigg(|t|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}+2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\bigg)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\ \Bigg\{rM-|t|\big(|t|\ \|g\|_{\infty}+2\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\big)\Bigg\},

where
M=\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|Q_{\alpha_{j}}(\lambda)|>0
.

Let us show that, for
|t|
sufficiently small,
|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}(\lambda)|^{2}\geq 0
on
\mathbb{T}
. It suffices to find
|t|
such that

rM-|t|\big(|t|\ \|g\|_{\infty}+2\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\big)>0,

or equivalently,

|t|\bigg(|t|+2\frac{\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}}\bigg)-\frac{rM}{\|g\|_{\infty}}<0.

If we take
|t|\leq\min\bigg\{\dfrac{2\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}},\dfrac{rM}{8\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}\bigg\}
, then

(6.28)

\displaystyle|t|\bigg(|t|+2\frac{\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}}\bigg)-\frac{rM}{\|g\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t|\bigg(2\frac{\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}}+2\frac{\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}}\bigg)-\frac{rM}{\|g\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{rM}{8\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}\bigg(4\frac{\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}}{\|g\|_{\infty}}\bigg)-\frac{rM}{\|g\|_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{rM}{2\|g\|_{\infty}}-\frac{rM}{\|g\|_{\infty}}=-\frac{rM}{2\|g\|_{\infty}}<0.

Therefore

|D|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}|^{2}\geq 0\qquad\ \text{on}\ \mathbb{T},

and, by Theorem
5.17
, the functions

x_{+t}=\bigg(\frac{E_{1}^{+t}}{D},\frac{E_{2}^{+t}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)\qquad\text{and}\qquad x_{-t}=\bigg(\frac{E_{1}^{-t}}{D},\frac{E_{2}^{-t}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)

are rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions. Obviously,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}x_{+t}+\frac{1}{2}x_{-t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{E_{1}^{+t}+E_{1}^{-t}}{2D},\frac{{E_{2}^{+t}+E_{2}^{-t}}}{2D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{E_{1}+tg+E_{1}-tg}{2D},\frac{E_{1}^{\sim n}+tg+E_{1}^{\sim n}-tg}{2D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{E_{1}}{D},\frac{E_{1}^{\sim n}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)=x.

Hence
x
is not an extreme point of
\mathcal{J}
.

If
n
is odd, assume
n=2m+1
. This case requires a slight modification. By assumption,
2k\leq n
thus
2k\leq 2m+1
. This implies that
k\leq m
. Choose
\omega\in\mathbb{T}
such that

\omega^{2}=-\overline{\tau}_{1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\overline{\tau}_{j}^{2}.

Let

g(\lambda)=\omega\lambda^{m-k}(\lambda-\tau_{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

Clearly, the polynomial
g
has degree
m+k+1\leq n
. Let us check that the
g
is
n
-symmetric

\displaystyle g^{\sim n}(\lambda)=\lambda^{n}\overline{g\big(1/\overline{\lambda}\big)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\bigg(\overline{\omega\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}^{m-k}}\big(\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}-\tau_{1}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(\frac{1}{\overline{\lambda}}-\tau_{j}\big)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{n}\bigg(\overline{\omega}\frac{1}{\lambda^{m-k}}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\overline{\tau}_{1}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\overline{\tau}_{j}\big)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}\lambda^{m-k}\overline{\tau}_{1}\big(\tau_{1}-\lambda\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\overline{\tau}_{j}^{2}\big(\tau_{j}-\lambda\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}\ \bigg(-\overline{\tau}_{1}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\overline{\tau}_{j}^{2}\bigg)\ \lambda^{m-k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{1}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{j}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}\ \omega^{2}\ \lambda^{m-k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{1}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{j}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega\ \lambda^{m-k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{1}\big)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(\lambda-\tau_{j}\big)^{2}=g(\lambda).

As in the even case, define the polynomials on
\mathbb{\overline{D}}

E_{1}^{t}=E_{1}+tg\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{2}^{t}=E_{1}^{\sim n}+tg\qquad\text{for}\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

As in equation (
6.25
), for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{\overline{D}}
,
E_{1}^{t}(\lambda)=\big(E_{2}^{t}\big)^{\sim n}(\lambda)
and as in equation (
6.26
), for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

(6.29)

|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}(\lambda)|^{2}=|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}-t^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2\text{Re}\big(tg(\lambda)\overline{E_{1}(\lambda)}\big).

For all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle\text{Re}(tg\overline{E_{1}(\lambda)}\leq|tgE_{1}(\lambda)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|tE_{1}(\lambda)|\bigg|\omega\lambda^{m-k}(\lambda-\tau_{1})\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\lambda-\tau_{j})^{2}\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|t|\ \|E_{1}\|_{\infty}|\lambda-\tau_{1}|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}.

Combine equations (
6.24
), (
6.29
) and inequality (
6.2
) to obtain, for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
,

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}(\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|D(\lambda)|^{2}-|E_{1}(\lambda)|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2\text{Re}\big(tg(\lambda)\overline{E}_{1}(\lambda)\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2\text{Re}\big(tg(\lambda)\overline{E}_{1}(\lambda)\big),\ \text{by equation}\ \eqref{eq65}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|\lambda-\alpha_{j}|^{2}-|t|^{2}|g(\lambda)|^{2}-2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}|\lambda-\tau_{1}|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2},\ \text{by inequality}\eqref{in10}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}r\prod_{j=k+1}^{n}|Q_{\alpha_{j}}(\lambda)|-|t|^{2}|\lambda-\tau_{1}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{4}-2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}|\lambda-\tau_{1}|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\Bigg\{rM-\underbrace{|\lambda-\tau_{1}|}_{\leq 2}\bigg(\underbrace{|t|^{2}|\lambda-\tau_{1}|\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}}_{\leq|t|^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}}+2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\bigg)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}|\lambda-\tau_{j}|^{2}\Bigg\{rM-2\big(|t|^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}+2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\big)\Bigg\},

where
M=\inf_{\mathbb{T}}\prod|Q_{\alpha_{j}}|>0
. By similar arguments to those in equations (

), one can find
|t|
such that

rM-2\big(|t|^{2}\|g\|_{\infty}+2|t|\|E_{1}\|_{\infty}\big)>0.

Therefore,

|D|^{2}-|E_{1}^{t}|^{2}\geq 0,\qquad\text{on}\ \mathbb{T}.

Hence, by Theorem
5.17
, the functions

x_{\pm t}=\bigg(\frac{E_{1}^{\pm t}}{D},\frac{\big(E_{1}^{\sim n}\big)^{\pm t}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\bigg)

are rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions. One can check that
x=\frac{1}{2}x_{+t}+\frac{1}{2}x_{-t}
and therefore
x
is not an extreme point of
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Theorem 6.20
.

[
6
, Theorem 5.13]

A rational
\Gamma
-inner function
h\in\mathcal{R}_{\Gamma}^{n,k}
is extreme in the set of rational
\Gamma
-inner functions if and only if
2k>n
.

Proposition 6.21
.

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{R}^{n,k}
be a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function such that
x_{1}=x_{2}
and
2k>n
. Then
x
is an extreme point of the set
\mathcal{J}
of rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.8
(1), the function
h=(s,p)=(2x_{1},x_{3})
is
\Gamma
-inner. By Theorem
4.15
, there are polynomials
E_{1},E_{2},D
such that
x=\big(\frac{E_{1}}{D},\frac{E_{2}}{D},\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}\big)
. Here, since
x_{1}=x_{2}
, necessarily
E_{1}=E_{2}
.
By Definition
3.7
, the royal polynomial
R_{h}
of
h
is

\displaystyle R_{h}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}(\lambda)\bigg(4x_{3}-4x_{1}^{2}\bigg)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}(\lambda)\bigg(4\frac{D^{\sim n}}{D}-4\frac{E_{1}^{2}}{D^{2}}\bigg)(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\big(DD^{\sim n}-E_{1}^{2}\big)(\lambda)=4R_{x}(\lambda).

It is clear that if
x\in\mathcal{R}^{n,k}
, then
h
has degree
n
and
k
royal nodes on
\mathbb{T}
, counted with multiplicities, such that
2k>n
.
Thus, by Theorem
6.20
,
h
is an extreme point of the set of rational
\Gamma
-inner functions. That is, if
h^{1}=(s^{1},p^{1})
and
h^{2}=(s^{2},p^{2})
are
\Gamma
-inner functions such that

h=th^{1}+(1-t)h^{2}\qquad\mbox{for some}\ t\in(0,1),

then
h=h^{1}=h^{2}
. Note that, in this case, we have

(6.30)

\begin{cases}s=ts^{1}+(1-t)s^{2}\qquad\Rightarrow s=s^{1}=s^{2}\\
p=tp^{1}+(1-t)p^{2}\qquad\Rightarrow p=p^{1}=p^{2}.\end{cases}

Suppose

x=tx^{1}+(1-t)x^{2},\quad\text{for some}\ t\in(0,1),

and for rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions
x^{1}=(x_{1}^{1},x_{2}^{1},x_{3}^{1})
and
x^{2}=(x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2})
.
This implies that

\begin{cases}x_{1}=tx_{1}^{1}+(1-t)x_{1}^{2}\\
x_{1}=tx_{2}^{1}+(1-t)x_{2}^{2}\\
x_{3}=p=tx_{3}^{1}+(1-t)x_{3}^{2}.\end{cases}

Recall that
(s,p)=(2x_{1},x_{3})
, hence

(6.31)

\begin{cases}s=2tx_{1}^{1}+2(1-t)x_{1}^{2}\\
s=2tx_{2}^{1}+2(1-t)x_{2}^{2}\\
p=tx_{3}^{1}+(1-t)x_{3}^{2}.\end{cases}

Therefore

(s,p)=t\big(2x_{1}^{1},x_{3}^{1}\big)+(1-t)\big(2x_{1}^{2},x_{3}^{2}\big)

and

(s,p)=t\big(2x_{2}^{1},x_{3}^{1}\big)+(1-t)\big(2x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2}\big).

Since
h
is an extreme rational
\Gamma
-inner function, we have

\begin{cases}2x_{1}^{1}=2x_{1}^{2}=s\\
2x_{2}^{1}=2x_{2}^{2}=s\\
x_{3}^{1}=x_{3}^{2}=p.\end{cases}

Therefore
x=x^{1}=x^{2}
. Hence
x
is extreme in the set
\mathcal{J}
.
∎

Remark 6.22
.

It is not clear that a rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner function
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathcal{R}^{n,k}
such that
2k>n
and
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
, is an extreme point of the set
\mathcal{J}
of rational
\mathbb{\overline{E}}
-inner functions.
Could we claim that in Lemma
6.16
, if
\tau_{i}\in\mathbb{T}
is a royal node of
x
of multiplicity
\nu
, then
\tau_{i}
is a royal node of
x^{1}
and
x^{2}
of the same multiplicity
\nu
? Here
x=tx^{1}+(1-t)x^{2}
for some
t
such that
0<t<1
. If so then
x
is an extreme point of
\mathcal{J}
if and only if
2k>n
.

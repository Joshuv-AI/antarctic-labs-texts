1.
Introduction

A celebrated result of Rossi
[
25
]
gives a bound for the reduction number of an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
of dimension two. Rossi’s bound is linear in terms of the multiplicity and the first Hilbert coefficient of
I
. It is an open problem to establish the same bound in higher dimensions. This paper is inspired by our interests in extending Rossi’s proof in dimension three. We have two important observations. Firstly, higher Hilbert coefficients of the ideal may appear in the bound, see Theorem
5.6
. Next, it is desirable that the Ratliff-Rush filtration of the ideal behaves well in some sense, see Remark
3.10
.

Throughout the paper, let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
with infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal.
An
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}=\{I_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence of ideals such that
(i)

I_{n+1}\subseteq I_{n}
,

(ii)

I_{m}I_{n}\subseteq I_{m+n}
and
(iii)~I^{n}\subseteq I_{n}\subseteq I^{n-k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A
reduction
of
\mathcal{I}
is an ideal
J\subseteq I_{1}
such that
JI_{n}=I_{n+1}
for
n\gg 0
and it is called a
minimal reduction
if it is minimal with respect to containment among all reductions. If the residue field
R/\mathfrak{m}
is infinite, then a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
exists and is generated by
d
elements. Minimal reductions are important in the study of Hilbert functions and blow-up algebras. For a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
, we define

r_{J}(\mathcal{I})=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mid I_{n}\not=JI_{n-1}\}\mbox{ and }r(\mathcal{I})=\min\{r_{J}(\mathcal{I})~|~J\mbox{ is a minimal reduction of }\mathcal{I}\}.

The above numbers are known as
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
with respect to
J
and
reduction number of
\mathcal{I}
respectively. When
\mathcal{I}
is the
I
-adic filtration
\{I^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we write
r_{J}(I)
and
r(I)
in place of
r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
and
r(\mathcal{I})
respectively.

One of the main difficulties in extending Rossi’s bound in higher dimensions is
that
r_{J}(I)
does not behave well with respect to superficial elements. This fact is closely related to the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
. We recall the definition more generally for an
I
-admissible filtration
\mathcal{I}
. The Ratliff-Rush filtration of
\mathcal{I}
is the filtration
\{\widetilde{I_{n}}=\mathop{\bigcup}\limits_{t\geq 0}(I_{n+t}:I^{t})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Let
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
and
R^{\prime}=R/(x)
. We say that the
Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo
x
if
\widetilde{I^{n}}R^{\prime}=\widetilde{I^{n}R^{\prime}}

for all
n\geq 1.

In Remark
3.10
, we prove that in a three dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring, if the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial element
x\in I
, then Rossi’s bound holds for
r_{J}(I)
. In view of this, it is natural to look for conditions, preferably computable ones, when the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well.
Hilbert coefficients provide some control over this property as we will see in this paper. In dimension three, a sufficient condition, due to Puthenpurakal
[
24
]
, is the vanishing of the Hilbert coefficients
e_{2}(I)
and
e_{3}(I)
(defined below). However, if
I
is integrally closed, then
e_{2}(I)=0
implies that
G(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay which is a stronger condition.

The Hilbert coefficients of
\mathcal{I}
are the unique integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
,
0\leq i\leq d,
such that the function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n):=\ell(R/I_{n})
coincides with the following polynomial for
n\gg 0:

P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)=e_{0}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-1\choose d}-e_{1}(\mathcal{I}){x+d-2\choose d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(\mathcal{I}).

Here
\ell(*)
denotes the length function. The function
H_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
and the polynomial
P_{\mathcal{I}}(x)
are known as the Hilbert-Samuel function and the Hilbert-Samuel polynomial of
\mathcal{I}
respectively. We refer to
[
27
]
for details. For
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
, we write
e_{i}(I)
instead of
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
.
The Hilbert coefficients contain deep information about the ideal
I
, the ring
R
and the blow-up algebras associated to
I
.

There has been a lot of research focused at the first three coefficients, i.e.,
e_{i}(I)
for
0\leq i\leq 2.

The behaviour of these coefficients change as soon as we encounter
e_{3}(I).

For instance, Northcott
[
21
]
proved that
e_{1}(I)\geq 0
and Narita
[
20
]
showed that
e_{2}(I)\geq 0
. In the same paper, Narita showed that
e_{3}(I)
could be negative. However,
e_{3}(I)\geq 0
if

0ptG(I)\geq d-1
due to Marley
[
16
]
or if
I
is a normal ideal due to Itoh
[
13
]
and Huckaba-Huneke
[
11
]
.
Later, Corso-Polini-Rossi
[
4
]
showed that
e_{3}(I)\geq 0
if
I^{q}
is integrally closed for a sufficiently large
q
. When
I
is integrally closed, Itoh
[
12
]
proved that
e_{3}(I)=0
if
e_{2}(I)\leq 2.

In
[
24
]
, Puthenpurakal proved that, for an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
,
e_{3}(I)\leq 0
if
e_{2}(I)=0
or if
r_{J}(I)\leq 2
.
Further, Mafi-Naderi
[
15
]
, proved that
e_{3}(I)\leq 0
, if
e_{2}(I)\leq 1
. When
I
is integrally closed, we recover some of these results as easy consequences of our main theorems. We obtain that
e_{3}(I)\leq 0
if
e_{2}(I)\leq 2
and
e_{3}(I)\leq 2
if
e_{2}(I)=3
. Discussions on the signature and vanishing of Hilbert coefficients can be found in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
28
]
etc.

Suppose
0ptG(I)\geq d-1
. Then Marley
[
16
]
proved that
e_{3}(I)\geq e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)
. The following theorem of this paper is a generalization of Marley’s result. Marley’s hypothesis is stronger as evident by Example
3.4
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
. Then
e_{3}(I)\geq e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I).

The above theorem provides a necessary condition for the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
to behave well modulo a superficial element. Suppose
I
is an integrally closed ideal. In Corollary
4.8
, we show that

e_{3}(I)\leq e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)

if one of the following conditions holds: (i)
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
; (ii)
e_{2}(I)\leq 3
; (iii)
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)\leq 2
or (iv)
e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)\leq 1.

In fact when
d=3
, we prove that in all these cases, the equality
e_{3}(I)=e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)
holds if and only if the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial element. These are consequences of the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
,
I
an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J
a minimal reduction of
I
. Then

(1)

e_{3}(I)\leq\frac{(r_{J}(I)-1)}{2}\big(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell({R}/{I})\big);

(2)

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)\leq\frac{\big(e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)\big)}{2}\big(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell({R}/{I})\big)\text{ and }

(3)

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)\leq\frac{\big(e_{2}(I)-1\big)}{2}\big(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell({R}/{I})\big).

Further, suppose
d=3
and equality holds in any one of (
1
), (
2
) or (
3
). Then the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial element.
Conversely, suppose the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}\in I
. Then (i) equality holds in (
1
) provided
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
; (ii) equality holds in (
2
) provided
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)\leq 2
and (iii) equality holds in (
3
) provided
e_{2}(I)\leq 3
.

The above inequalities may not hold if
I
is not integrally closed as shown by Example
4.7
.
As a corollary, we obtain that

e_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)^{2}/2

for an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
. Theorem
1.2
, provides sufficient and necessary conditions for the Ratliff-Rush filtration behaving well modulo superficial element in dimension three as demonstrated by Examples
5.1
and
5.2
. In addition, it has some interesting applications. In an attempt to extend Rossi’s result in dimension three, we prove the following
result for integrally closed ideals which gives an improvement of the
earlier known bounds.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
,
I
an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal and
J\subseteq I
a minimal reduction of
I
. Suppose
0ptG(I)\geq d-3
. Then

\displaystyle r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1+e_{2}(I)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I))-e_{3}(I).

For a minimal reduction
J
of
\mathcal{I}
, we set

\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I}):=\sup\{n\in\mathbb{Z}|\widetilde{I_{n}}\not=J\widetilde{I_{n-1}}\}.

Suppose
d=2
. Then
it is known that

\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq r_{J}(\mathcal{I})
, see
[
18
]
and
[
26
]
.
Assume that
I_{1}
is integrally closed. In Lemma
3.8
, we show that

(4)

\widetilde{r}_{J}(\mathcal{I})\leq e_{2}(\mathcal{I})-e_{1}(\mathcal{I})+e_{0}(\mathcal{I})-\ell(R/I_{1})+2.

In
[
5
]
, Elias proved that
0ptG(I^{n})
is constant for
n\gg 0
. Also,
0ptG(I^{n})\geq 1
for
n\gg 0

if and only if

I
has a regular element. In
[
23
]
, Puthenpurakal gave a necessary and sufficient condition for
0ptG(I^{n})\geq 2.
Suppose
d\geq 4
. In Theorem
5.3
, we prove that (i) if
e_{4}(I)<0
, then
0ptG(I^{n})\leq 2
for
n\gg 0
and (ii) if
I^{q}
is integrally closed for a sufficiently large

and
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
, then
e_{4}(I)\leq 0.
The later was earlier proved for an asymptotically normal ideal
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
. In Proposition
5.5
, we partially recover
[
4
, Corollary 4.5]
on the vanishing of
e_{3}(I)
and further prove the bound

(5)

e_{3}(I)\leq\ell(I^{3}/JI^{2})+\ell((J\cap I^{3})/JI^{2})

for an integrally closed ideal
I
with
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
in Theorem
5.9
.

This paper is organised in five sections beginning with a brief mention of the notations in Section

2.
Notation

I.
It is well known that the Hilbert series of
\mathcal{I}
, defined as
H(\mathcal{I},t)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{n\geq 0}\ell(I_{n}/I_{n+1})t^{n}
, is a rational function, i.e., there exists a unique rational polynomial
h_{\mathcal{I}}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
with
h_{\mathcal{I}}(1)\neq 0
such that

H(\mathcal{I},t)=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}(t)}{(1-t)^{d}}.

For every
i\geq 0
, we define
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})=\frac{h_{\mathcal{I}}^{(i)}(1)}{i!}
, where
h_{\mathcal{I}}^{(i)}(1)
denotes the
i
-th formal derivative of the polynomial
h_{\mathcal{I}}(t)
at
t=1
. The integers
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
are called the Hilbert coefficients of
\mathcal{I}
and for
0\leq i\leq d
, these are same as defined earlier in the Introduction. We refer to
[
8
]
for details.
We write
\widetilde{e_{i}}(I)
for the coefficients
e_{i}(\mathcal{I})
when
\mathcal{I}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}.

II.

The postulation number
\eta(I)
of
I
is defined to be
\eta(I):=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}~|~P_{I}(m)=H_{I}(m)
for
m\geq n\}
.

III.
For a graded module
M=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}M_{n}
,
M_{n}
denote its
n
-th graded piece and
M(-j)
denote the graded module
M
shifted to the left by degree
j.
Similarly for a complex
C_{.}
, we write
C_{.}(-j)
for the complex
C_{.}
shifted to the left by degree
j.

IV.
The Rees ring of
\mathcal{I}
is
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{I})=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}I_{n}t^{n}\subseteq R[t]
where
t
is an indeterminate and the associated graded ring of
\mathcal{I}
is
G(\mathcal{I})=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}I_{n}/I_{n+1}
. We write
R(I)
and
G(I)
for the Rees ring and the associated graded ring respectively when
\mathcal{I}=\{I^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. Set
\mathcal{R}_{+}=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 1}I_{n}t^{n}.

V.

Suppose
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
is a sequence in
R
.
We write

\underline{x}
and
(\underline{x})
for the sequence

x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
and the ideal
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{s})\subseteq R

respectively. We use the notation
I^{\prime}
and
R^{\prime}
for going modulo superficial element(s).

VI.
For a numerical function
f:\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, we put
\triangle f(n)=f(n+1)-f(n)
and recursively we can define
\triangle^{i}f(n):=\triangle(\triangle^{i-1}f(n))
for all
i\geq 1
.

VII.
We write
\mathcal{F}
for the filtration
\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}_{n\geq 0}
and for an
I

\mathcal{I}

with a minimal reduction
J
,
v_{n}(\mathcal{I})=\ell(I_{n+1}/JI_{n}).

For future reference, we recall the following inequality from the proof of
[

:

(6)

r_{J}(I)\leq\mathop{\sum}\limits_{n\geq 0}v_{n}(\mathcal{F})-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1.

3.
Ratliff-Rush filtration and
e_{3}(I)

Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
. Marley
[
16
, Corollary 2.9]
, proved that if
0ptG(I)\geq d-1,
then
e_{3}(I)\geq e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)
and Rossi
[
25
]
proved that if
0ptG(I)\geq d-2,
then
r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1
. As evident by the next example, both the above bounds hold even if
0ptG(I)=0
. In this section, we discuss
that for both the results, the depth conditions on
G(I)
can be replaced by a weaker condition on the behavior of the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
.

Example 3.1
.

[
15
, Example 3.7]

Let
R=k[x,y,z]_{(x,y,z)}
and
I=(x^{4},y^{4},z^{4},x^{3}y,xy^{3},y^{3}z,yz^{3}).
Then
0ptG(I)=0
, the Hilbert series of
I
is

H(I,t)=\frac{30+12t+22t^{2}+8t^{3}-2t^{4}-12t^{5}+6t^{6}}{(1-t)^{3}}

and the Hilbert polynomial is

P_{I}(n)=64{{n+2}\choose 3}-48{{n+1}\choose 2}+4{n\choose 1}.

Therefore,
e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)=-10
and
e_{3}(I)=0.
We also note that
J=(x^{4},y^{4},z^{4})
is a minimal reduction of
I
and
r_{J}(I)=4\leq 14=e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)
.

Recall that the Ratliff-Rush closure
\widetilde{I}
of an ideal
I
is the stable value of the following chain of ideals:

I\subseteq(I^{2}:I)\subseteq(I^{3}:I^{2})\subseteq\ldots\subseteq(I^{n+1}:I^{n})\subseteq\ldots

It is of interest to understand the interaction between the Ratliff-Rush closure of powers of
I
and a superficial element
x_{1}\in I
.
In general,
\widetilde{I^{n}}R^{\prime}\subseteq\widetilde{I^{n}R^{\prime}}

for all
n\geq 0
and equality holds for
n\gg 0
where
R^{\prime}=R/(x_{1}).

Definition 3.2
.

Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\in I
be a superficial sequence. Then, we say that

(1)

the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo
x_{1}
if
\widetilde{I^{n}}R^{\prime}=\widetilde{I^{n}R^{\prime}}

for all
n\geq 0
and

(2)

the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}
if
I/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
behaves well modulo the image of
x_{i}
in
R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
for all
1\leq i\leq s
, where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})=(0)
for
i=1
.

It is easy to see that the above definition is equivalent to the one given in
[
24
, Definition 4.4]
. If the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo one superficial sequence, then it does so for any superficial sequence. It follows from
[
24
, Corollary 4.7]
.
At this point, we recall some relevant discussions from literature, mainly
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
. As in
[
23
]
, we define
L^{I}(R)=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}R/I^{n+1}.
The exact sequence

0\to R(I)\to R[t]\to L^{I}(R)(-1)\to 0

defines an
R(I)
-module structure on
L^{I}(R).
Note that
L^{I}(R)
is not a finitely generated
R(I)
-module. However, the local cohomology modules
H_{\mathcal{M}}^{i}(L^{I}(R))
, with support in
\mathcal{M}=m\bigoplus R(I)_{+}
,
are
*
-Artinian for
0\leq i\leq 2
, see
[
23
, section 4]
. For a superficial element
x\in I
, we have the following exact sequence of
R
-modules for each
n\geq 1
:

0\longrightarrow\frac{I^{n+1}:x}{I^{n}}\longrightarrow\frac{R}{I^{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi_{n}^{x}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\frac{R}{I^{n+1}}\longrightarrow\frac{{R}^{\prime}}{I^{n+1}{{R}^{\prime}}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
R^{\prime}=R/(x)
and
\psi_{n}^{x}(r+I^{n})=xr+I^{n+1}
for all
r\in R
. We write
I^{\prime}=IR^{\prime}
. This gives an exact sequence of
R(I)
-modules:

(7)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow B^{I}(x,R)\longrightarrow L^{I}(R)(-1){\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi^{x}}}{{\longrightarrow}}}L^{I}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}{L^{I}}^{\prime}({R}^{\prime})\longrightarrow 0

where
\psi^{x}
is multiplication by
x
and

B^{I}(x,R)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\frac{I^{n+1}:x}{I^{n}}.

Note that
I^{n+1}:x=I^{n}
for all
n\gg 0
. So,
H_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}(B^{I}(x,R))=B^{I}(x,R)
. The sequence in (
7
) induces a long exact sequence of local cohomology modules, see
[
23
, Section 6]
:

(8)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow B^{I}(x,R)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}(L^{I}(R))(-1){\longrightarrow}H_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}(L^{I}(R)){\longrightarrow}H_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}({L^{I}}^{\prime}({R}^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(L^{I}(R))(-1){\longrightarrow}H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(L^{I}(R)){\longrightarrow}H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}({L^{I}}^{\prime}({R}^{\prime}))\longrightarrow\ldots.

By
[
23
, Proposition 4.7]
,
H_{\mathcal{M}}^{0}(L^{I}(R))=\mathop{\bigoplus}\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{\widetilde{I^{n+1}}}{I^{n+1}}.
For convenience, we write
b_{I}:=\ell(B^{I}(x,R))
and
s_{I}:=\ell(H^{0}({L^{I}}^{\prime}({R}^{\prime})).
Note that
b_{I}-s_{I}\leq 0.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)

By
[
24
, Theorem 2.3]
, Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo

H_{\mathcal{M}}^{1}(L^{I}(R))=0.
Equivalently,

To see this, consider the length of modules in (

(2)

3.3

(18)

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)=\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)+b_{I}-s_{I}\leq\widetilde{e}_{3}(I)\leq\frac{(e_{2}(I)-1)}{2}(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)).

Now, suppose
e_{3}(I)=\frac{(e_{2}(I)-1)}{2}(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell({R}/{I}))
. Then by (
18
),
\widetilde{e}_{3}(I^{\prime})+(b_{I}-s_{I})=\widetilde{e}_{3}(I^{\prime})
which implies
b_{I}-s_{I}=0
. Therefore, by part (
1
) of Remark
3.3
, the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo superficial element. For the converse, suppose
e_{2}(I)\leq 3
and the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo superficial sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2}
. Then using Theorem
3.5
and (
17
), we get the result.
∎

In
[
24
, Proposition 6.4 ]
and
[

, Proposition 2.6]
, authors proved that for an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal
I
,

implies
e_{3}(I)\leq 0.
For an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal

, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal. Then
e_{3}(I)\leq(e_{2}(I)-1)^{2}/2.

Proof.

Suppose
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)=0
. Then
G(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
e_{3}(I)=0\leq{(e_{2}(I)-1)}^{2}/2.
Now let
e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)\geq 1
. Then by Corollary
4.3
,

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)\leq\frac{(e_{2}(I)-1)(e_{2}(I)-1)}{2}.\qed

Remark 4.5
.

Suppose
d=3
and equality holds in (
14
), then
equality holds in (
11
). Converse is not true as evident by Example
4.6
.

Proof.

Suppose equality holds in (
14
), then the Ratliff-Rush filtration of

behaves well modulo superficial element. Hence by Remark
3.10
and Theorem
4.1
, we get

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(r_{J}(I)-1)}{2}\big(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)}{2}\big(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)\big)

\displaystyle=e_{3}(I).\qed

The bound in Theorem
4.1
is sharp as evident by the following example.

Example 4.6
.

[

, Example 3.2]

Let

, where

Example 4.7

We have

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension

and

\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal. Then

(1)

r_{J}(I)\leq 3

(2)

e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)\leq 1.

(3)

e_{2}(I)\leq 3.

Furthermore, if

e_{3}(I)=e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell({R}/{I}).

Proof.

(1)

It follows from Theorem

.

(2)

Suppose

. Let

(3)

It follows from the bound in Theorem

.

(4)

It follows from the bound in Corollary

.

For the last part, suppose

,

.

.
∎

5.
Applications

In this section, we write some interesting consequences of the theorems of last sections.

I
. In
[
24
, Theorem 6.2]
, Puthenpurakal proved that
e_{2}(I)=e_{3}(I)=\cdots=e_{d}(I)=0
implies that the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo a superficial sequence. However, when
I
is integrally closed, we know that
e_{2}(I)=0
implies that
G(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay by results of Itoh
[
12
, Corollary 13]
. In this context, we discuss two examples below. The first one demonstrate the case when
e_{2}(I)\neq 0
,
0ptG(I)=0
but the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
I
behaves well. The second example is an application of Theorem
3.5
as well as Corollary
4.3
.

Example 5.1
.

[
22
, Theorem 5.2]

Let
m\geq 0
,
d\geq 2
and
k
be an infinite field. Consider the power series ring
D=k[[\{X_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m},Y,\{V_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq d},\{Z_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq d}]]
with
m+2d+1
indeterminates and the ideal
\mathfrak{a}=[(X_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq m)+(Y)].[(X_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq m)+(Y)+(V_{i}~|~1\leq i\leq d)]+(V_{i}V_{j}~|~1\leq i,j\leq d,i\neq j)+(V_{i}^{3}-Z_{i}Y~|~1\leq i\leq d).
Define
R=D/\mathfrak{a}
and
x_{i},y,v_{i},z_{i}
denote the images of
X_{i},Y,V_{i},Z_{i}
in
R
respectively. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq m)+(y)+(v_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq d)+(z_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq d)
be the maximal ideal in
R
and
Q=(z_{j}~|~1\leq j\leq d).
Then

(1)

R
is Cohen-Macaulay local ring with
\dim R=d,

(2)

Q
is a minimal reduction of
\mathfrak{m}
with
r_{Q}(\mathfrak{m})=3,

(3)

0ptG(\mathfrak{m})=0,

(4)

e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})=m+2d+2;

e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})=m+3d+2
;
e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})=d+1
and
e_{i}(\mathfrak{m})=0
for
3\leq i\leq d.

Thus
e_{3}(\mathfrak{m})=0=\frac{\big(r_{Q}(\mathfrak{m})-1\big)}{2}\big(e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})+e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{m})\big)
. When
d=3
, the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
\mathfrak{m}
behaves well modulo superficial element by Theorem
4.1
.

Example 5.2
.

[
4
, Example 3.5]

Let
R=k[[X,Y,Z,U,V,W]]/(Z^{2},ZU,ZV,UV,YZ-U^{3},XZ-V^{3})
be a three dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and
X,Y,Z,U,V,W
indeterminates. Let
x,y,z,u,v,w
denote the corresponding images of
X,Y,Z,U,V,W
in
R
and
m=(x,y,z,u,v,w)
. Then
G(\mathfrak{m})
has depth
1
and

H(\mathfrak{m},t)=\frac{1+3t+3t^{3}-t^{4}}{{(1-t)}^{3}}

which gives
e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})=3,e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})=8,e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})=6,\ell(R/\mathfrak{m})=1,e_{3}(\mathfrak{m})=-1
. Thus
e_{2}(\mathfrak{m})-e_{1}(\mathfrak{m})+e_{0}(\mathfrak{m})-\ell(R/\mathfrak{m})=0
and
e_{3}(\mathfrak{m})=-1.

Therefore the Ratliff-Rush filtration of
\mathfrak{m}
does not behave well modulo superficial element by Theorem
3.5
or by Corollary
4.3
.

In
[
24
, Theorem 3.5]
, it was proved that if
d=2
,
I
is integrally closed and
e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I)=0
, then Ratliff-rush filtration of
I
behaves well modulo superficial element. Example
5.2
shows that the above result can not be extended in dimension three.

II.
We now prove a result on the signature of
e_{4}(I)
. In
[
5
]
, Elias proved that
0ptG(I^{n})
is constant for
n\gg 0
.
In the first part of Theorem
5.3
, we give a necessary condition for
0ptG(I^{n})\geq 3
for
n\gg 0.
The second part is a generalization of
[
15
, Theorem 2.5]
which was the case where
I
is an asymptotically normal ideal.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d=4
. Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R
and
J
a minimal reduction of
I
.

(1)

Suppose
0ptG(I^{q})\geq 3
for some
q\geq\eta(I)
. Then

e_{4}(I)\geq 0.

(2)

Suppose
I^{q}
is integrally closed for some
q\geq\eta(I)
and
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
. Then
e_{4}(I)\leq 0
.

Proof.

Let
q\geq\eta(I)
. We have

(19)

\ell(R/I^{n})=e_{0}(I){n+3\choose 4}-e_{1}(I){n+2\choose 3}+e_{2}(I){n+1\choose 2}-e_{3}(I){n\choose 1}+e_{4}(I)

for
n\geq\eta(I)
and

(20)

\ell(R/(I^{q})^{n})=\epsilon_{0}{n+3\choose 4}-\epsilon_{1}{n+2\choose 3}+\epsilon_{2}{n+1\choose 2}-\epsilon_{3}{n\choose 1}+\epsilon_{4}

for
n\gg 0
where
\epsilon_{i}=e_{i}(I^{q})
for
0\leq i\leq 4
. Since
\ell(R/(I^{q})^{n})=\ell(R/I^{qn})
, using (
19
) and (
20
), we get

\displaystyle\ell(R/I^{qn})

\displaystyle=e_{0}(I){nq+3\choose 4}-e_{1}(I){nq+2\choose 3}+e_{2}(I){nq+1\choose 2}-e_{3}(I){nq\choose 1}+e_{4}(I)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{0}{n+3\choose 4}-\epsilon_{1}{n+2\choose 3}+\epsilon_{2}{n+1\choose 2}-\epsilon_{3}{n\choose 1}+\epsilon_{4}

for
n\gg 0
. On comparing the coefficients, we get

\displaystyle\epsilon_{0}=e_{0}(I)q^{4},~\epsilon_{1}=\frac{3}{2}e_{0}(I)(q^{4}-q^{3})+e_{1}(I)q^{3},~\epsilon_{2}=\frac{e_{0}(I)}{12}(11q^{2}+7q^{4}-18q^{3})+e_{1}(I)(q^{3}-q^{2})+e_{2}(I)q^{2},

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}=e_{0}(I){q\choose 4}+e_{1}(I){q\choose 3}+e_{2}(I){q\choose 2}+e_{3}(I){q\choose 1}\mbox{ and }\epsilon_{4}=e_{4}(I).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}-\epsilon_{2}+\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{0}+\ell(R/I^{q})=

\displaystyle-e_{0}(I){q+3\choose 4}+e_{1}(I){q+2\choose 3}-e_{2}(I){q+1\choose 2}+e_{3}(I){q\choose 1}+\ell(R/I^{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{4}(I).

since
q\geq\eta(I)
. Now suppose
0ptG(I^{q})\geq 3.
Then
e_{4}(I)=\epsilon_{3}-(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{0}-\ell(R/I^{q}))\geq 0
by Corollary
3.7
.

For the second part, suppose
I^{q}
is integrally closed.
Using Theorem
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle e_{4}(I)=

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}-(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{0}-\ell(R/I^{q}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r(I^{q})-3)}{2}\Big(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{0}-\ell(R/I^{q})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\lceil(r(I)-3)/q\rceil\Big)(\epsilon_{2}-\epsilon_{1}+\epsilon_{0}-\ell(R/I^{q}))

where the last inequality follows from
[
9
, Lemma 2.7]
. Here,
\lceil x\rceil:=\min\{m\in\mathbb{Z}~|~m\geq x\}.
Now if
r_{J}(I)\leq 3
, then
\lceil{r(I)-3}/q\rceil\leq 0
and the result follows.
∎

Example 5.4
.

[
15
, Example 3.1]

Let
R=k[x,y,z,u]_{(x,y,z,u)}
and
I=(x^{3},y^{3},z^{3},u^{3},xy^{2},yz^{2},zu^{2},xyz,xyu).
Then
J=(x^{3},y^{3},z^{3},u^{3})
is a minimal reduction of
I
with
r_{J}(I)=4
. Moreover,
0ptG(I)=2,
the Hilbert series is

H(I,t)=\frac{33+19t+21t^{2}+7t^{3}+5t^{4}-3t^{5}-t^{6}}{(1-t)^{4}}

and the Hilbert polynomial is

P_{I}(n)=81{n+3\choose 4}-81{n+2\choose 3}+27{n+1\choose 2}+23{n\choose 1}-50.

Therefore,
e_{0}(I)=81,~e_{1}(I)=81,~e_{2}(I)=27,~e_{3}(I)=-23,~e_{4}(I)=-50.
Hence, using Theorem
5.3
, we have
2=0ptG(I)\leq 0ptG(I^{n})\leq 2
for
n\gg 0
.

A similar calculation in dimension three, as done in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, gives the equality

(21)

e_{3}(I)=e_{2}(I^{q})-e_{1}(I^{q})+e_{0}(I^{q})-\ell(R/I^{q})

for
q\geq\eta(I)
. We refer to the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
4
]
for detailed calculation. We partially recover
[
4
, Corollary 4.5]
below.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d=3
and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose
I^{q}
is integrally closed for some
q\geq\eta(I)
and
r(I^{q})\leq 2
. Then
e_{3}(I)=0
.

Proof.

By (
21
) and Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle e_{3}(I)=e_{3}(I^{q})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(r(I^{q})-1)}{2}\Big(e_{2}(I^{q})-e_{1}(I^{q})+e_{0}(I^{q})-\ell(R/I^{q})\Big)=\frac{(r(I^{q})-1)}{2}e_{3}(I).

Suppose
e_{3}(I)\neq 0
. Then we get
r(I^{q})\geq 3
which is a contradiction.
∎

III.
Next, we prove an upper bound for the reduction number
r_{J}(I)
of an integrally closed ideal
I
. If
0ptG(I)\geq d-2,
then by
[
25
, Corollary 1.5]
, we have

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1.

We prove the following result.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension
d\geq 3
and
I
an integrally closed
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Suppose
0ptG(I)\geq d-3
. Then

\displaystyle r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1+e_{2}(I)(e_{2}(I)-e_{1}(I)+e_{0}(I)-\ell(R/I))-e_{3}(I).

Our proof is an improvement of the methods discussed in
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
.
We briefly recall some ideas from
[
18
]
. As in
[
8
]
, the second Hilbert function of
\mathcal{I}
is defined as
H^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)=\mathop{\sum}\limits_{i=0}^{n}H_{\mathcal{I}}(i)
and the second Hilbert polynomial, denoted by
P^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
is the polynomial which coincides with
H^{2}_{\mathcal{I}}(n)
for large values of
n
. Let
\underline{x}
denote a sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
in
R
and
n
be an integer. We define modified Koszul complex
C_{.}(\underline{x},\mathcal{I},n)
as in
[
10
]
by induction on
m
: For
m=1,

C_{.}(x_{1},\mathcal{I},n)
is the complex

0\longrightarrow R/I_{n-1}\overset{x_{1}}{\longrightarrow}R/I_{n}\longrightarrow 0

and for
m>1
, consider the chain map
f:C_{.}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},\mathcal{I},n-1)\longrightarrow C_{.}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},\mathcal{I},n)
defined as multiplication by
x_{m}
. Define

C_{.}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},\mathcal{I},n)
to be the mapping cylinder of
f
. Then
C_{.}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},\mathcal{I},n)

has the following form

0\longrightarrow R/{I_{n-m}}\longrightarrow(R/{I_{n-m+1}})^{m}\longrightarrow(R/{I_{n-m+2}})^{{m\choose 2}}\longrightarrow\quad...\quad\longrightarrow R/{I_{n}}\longrightarrow 0.

For convenience of notation, we write
{C_{.}}(\mathcal{I},n)

={C_{.}}({x_{1}},{x_{2}},...,{x_{m}},\mathcal{I},n)
and
{C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n)={C_{.}}({x_{1}},{x_{2}},...,{x_{m-1}},\mathcal{I},n)
when the value of
m
is clear. For any integer
n
, there is an
exact sequence of complexes

0\longrightarrow{C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n)\longrightarrow{C_{.}}(\mathcal{I},n)\longrightarrow{C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n-1)[-1]\longrightarrow 0

which induces a long exact sequence of homology modules

\cdots\longrightarrow{H_{i}}({C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n))\longrightarrow{H_{i}}({C_{.}}(\mathcal{I},n))\longrightarrow{H_{i-1}}({C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n-1)){\kern 1.0pt}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\underline{+}x_{m}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{H_{i-1}}({C_{.}}^{\prime}(\mathcal{I},n))\longrightarrow\cdots.

We recall the following formula from
[
10
, §4.]
. For each
n
and
1\leq i\leq d
,

(22)

{e_{i}}(\mathcal{I})=\sum\limits_{n=i}^{\infty}{\left({{}_{i-1}^{n-1}}\right)}\;{\Delta^{d}}[{P_{\mathcal{I}}}(n-d)-{H_{\mathcal{I}}}(n-d)]\;\mbox{ and }

(23)

{\Delta^{d}}[{P_{\mathcal{I}}}(n-d)-{H_{\mathcal{I}}}(n-d)]=\ell({{I_{n}}}/{J{I_{n-1}}})-\sum\limits_{i=2}^{d}{{{(-1)}^{i}}\ell({H_{i}}({C_{.}}(\mathcal{I},n)))}

where
J=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
is a minimal reduction of
\mathcal{I}
and
{H_{i}}({C_{.}}(\mathcal{I},n))={H_{i}}({C_{.}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},\mathcal{I},n)).

Proof of Theorem
5.6
.

Suppose
d=3
. Let
\mathcal{F}=\{\widetilde{I^{n}}\}
and
x\in I
be a superficial element for
I
such that
I^{\prime}
is integrally closed. Then
x
is also superficial for the filtration
\mathcal{F}
. Suppose
J=(x,y,z)
is a minimal reduction of
I
. Let
R^{\prime}=R/(x)
and
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}=\{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n}=\frac{\widetilde{I^{n}}+(x)}{(x)}\}
. Since
0ptG(\mathcal{F})\geq 1
, we have
v_{n}(\mathcal{F})=v_{n}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
.
By using (
22
) and (
23
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle e_{1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}v_{n}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})-\sum_{n\geq 1}\ell(H_{2}(C_{.}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},n))).

Since
x
is a superficial element for
\mathcal{F}
,
e_{1}(I)=e_{1}(\mathcal{F})=e_{1}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
.
Hence, by using (
6
) and (
24
), we get

(25)

r_{J}(I)\leq e_{1}(I)+\sum_{n\geq 1}\ell(H_{2}(C_{.}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},n)))-e_{0}(I)+\ell(R/I)+1.

By
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
, we have
H_{2}(C.({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},n))=\frac{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-1}:(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})}{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-1}:(y^{\prime},z^{\prime})\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}
,

\ell(H_{2}(C.({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},n)))\leq\ell\left(\frac{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}}{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}\right).

Therefore, for
m\gg 0
, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell(H_{2}(C.({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},n)))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell\left(\frac{\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}}{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell(R^{\prime}/{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2})-\sum_{n=0}^{m}\ell(R^{\prime}/\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{n-2}})

\displaystyle=

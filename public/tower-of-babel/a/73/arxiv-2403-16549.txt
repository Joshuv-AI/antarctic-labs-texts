A new invariant for a cycle of an interval map
Sourav Bhattacharya

Abstract
. We propose a new invariant for a cycle of an interval map f : [ 0 , 1 ] → [ 0 , 1 ] f:[0,1]\to[0,1] , called its unfolding number .

Introduction

In his 1964 legendary paper
[
19
]
(see
[
20
]
for English Translation), O.M. Sharkovsky unveiled an elegant
rule
governing the
co-existence
of
periods
of
periodic orbits
(also known as
cycles
) of a
continuous interval map
. He introduced the following
order
among all
natural numbers
:

3\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;5\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;7\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;\dots\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2\cdot 3\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2\cdot 5\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2\cdot 7\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;\dots

\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;\dots 2^{2}\cdot 3\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2^{2}\cdot 5\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2^{2}\cdot 7\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;\dots\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;8\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;4\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;2\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;1

and proved the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
19
,
20
]
)
.

If
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m\succ\mkern-14.0mu_{s}\;n
, then if a continuous mapping
f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
has a cycle of period
m
, then
f
must also have a cycle of period
n
.

Theorem
1.1
reveals an intricate and
concealed combinatorial
framework that
governs
the
disposition
of
cycles
of a
continuous interval
map and led to the inception of a new direction of research in the theory of
dynamical systems
called
combinatorial dynamics
.

Future research stemming from Theorem
1.1
could branch out in numerous directions. For instance, one might
replace
the
interval
with a more
general space
or
replace

periodic orbits
with more
general orbits
(e.g.,
homoclinic trajectories
). Additionally, one could strive to discover a more “
refined
”
rule
for the
coexistence
of
periodic orbits
in a given
continuous interval map
than that provided by Theorem
1.1
. For an extensive list of references and a comprehensive survey of the
evolution
of Theorem
1.1
, its historical context, subsequent advancements, and implications in the
theory of dynamical systems
, one could refer to the books
[
1
]
and
[
11
]
and the paper
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we pursue the latter mentioned approach for
refining
Theorem
1.1
. Let us discuss in details. Observe that Theorem
1.1
elucidates how certain “
type
” of
dynamical behaviour
(here
period
) once exhibited by a map,
forces
some other “
type
” (
period
) and thus, the question of “
co-existence
” among “
types
” becomes a question of “
forcing
” among “
types
”. However, “
periods
” offer only a coarse depiction of a
cycle
, as numerous
cycles
share the same
period
. A more nuanced approach to characterizing
cycles
, involves examining their
cyclic permutations
:
cyclic
permutation
we get when we look at how the map acts on the points
of the
cycle
, ordered from the
left to the right

identify

cycles
having the

and call the corresponding

:

As it turns out (see
[
3
]
), the

for

using this “
type
” is rather too

and doesn’t allow for a transparent description. This
motivates one to look for a, middle-of-the-road way of describing

rotation theory
, a way not as
crude
as
periods
but not as fine as
permutations
,
which would still allow for a
transparent description
.

The concept of
rotation numbers
was historically first introduced by Poincaré

(See
[
18
]
). It was extended to

of

by Newhouse, Palis and Takens (see
[

[

14
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
2
]

can be defined as follows: Let
X

with a Borel
\sigma

\phi:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a

(often called an

map. Then for any

, of all

of the sequence

\left\{{\frac{1}{n}}\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\phi(f^{i}(x))\right\}
is called the
\phi
-rotation set
of
x
. If
I_{f,\phi}(x)=\{\rho_{\phi}(x)\}
is a singleton, then the
number
\rho_{\phi}(x)
is called the
\phi
-rotation number
of

x
. It is easy to see that the
\phi
-
rotation set
,
I_{f,\phi}(x)
is a closed
interval for all
x\in X
. The
union
of all
\phi
-
rotation sets
of all points of

is called
the
\phi
-rotation set
of the map
f
and is denoted by

I_{f}(\phi)
. If
x
is an
f
-
periodic point
of period
n
, then its
\phi
-rotation number

\rho_{\phi}(x)
is well-defined, and a related concept of the

\phi
-rotation pair
of
x
can be introduced: the pair

({\frac{1}{n}}\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\phi(f^{i}(x)),n)
is the

\phi
-rotation pair
of
x
.

The use of
rotation theory
in the study of
dynamical systems
is useful when our

is
complete
. This means that:

(1)

rotation set
is
convex
;

(2)

rotation number
of a

of
period

is of the form
\frac{m}{n}
where
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(3)

if
\frac{m}{n}
belongs to the
interior
of the
rotation set
, then there exists a
cycle
of
period

kn
and
rotation pair

(km,kn)
for every

.

Theorem 1.2

If the

Unfolding numbers: Definition and Consistency

We begin by defining

in details. Consider a
continuous map

f:[0,1]\to[0,1]
with a
unique point
of
absolute

maximum

M_{f}
and a
unique point
of
absolute minimum

with

and

of

whose

f

cycles

. So,

is a

. Thus, the case

even though our construction stated below works in the case

Consider the

g_{f}:[0,\frac{1}{2}]\to[0,\frac{1}{2}]

to the map

via the
linear contraction

\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,\frac{1}{2}]

\lambda(x)=\frac{x}{2}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}]

, the

corresponding to the map

. Let

,

. Then, the map
g_{f}
also has a

of

and a

of
absolute minimum

with

and

; also

(See Figure

).

Define

by
p_{f}(x)=1-g_{f}(x)
. Clearly,
p_{f}(0)=1-c_{g}
,
p_{f}(M_{g})=\frac{1}{2}
,
p_{f}(m_{g})=1
,
p_{f}(\frac{1}{2})=1-d_{g}

graph
of
p_{f}(x)
is obtained by
reflecting vertically
, the

of
g_{f}(x)
with the respect with the line

.

Define

q_{f}(1-m_{g})=1

q_{f}(1-M_{g})=\frac{1}{2}

q_{f}(1)=1-c_{g}

of

of

.

Define

r_{f}(\frac{1}{2})=1+d_{g}

,

.

of

of

p_{f}(x)&\text{if $M_{g}\leqslant x\leqslant\frac{1}{2}$.}\\
q_{f}(x)&\text{if $\frac{1}{2}\leqslant x\leqslant 1-m_{g}$.}\\
r_{f}(x)&\text{if $1-m_{g}\leqslant x\leqslant 1$.}\\
\end{cases}

Observe that

.
Extend

for all

. We shall call the map
F_{f}
, the
heaved map
corresponding to the map

. By construction

is a

of the real line (See Figure

Definition 3.1

corresponding
to

eliminating need to construct

We commence by introducing a few notions which would be used throughout this section: Let

of

be a

-

and

of

are

and

and

be points of

and

of

such that

and

. Likewise,
m_{g}
and

and

of the

, corresponding to

, such that

and

.

Definition 5.1
.

By

of

, we mean the

(

) set
\mathcal{N}(P)=\{n_{1},

n_{2},\dots n_{k}\}

(2)

Theorem 5.2
.

If
P
has
p
unfolding points, then, the unfolding number of

is
\frac{p}{q}
.

Proof.

Definition
of
\mathcal{N}(P)
coupled with the
construction
of the
maps

g_{f}
and
F_{f}
reveals that the
trajectory
of
0
under
g_{f}
and
trajectory
of
0
under
F_{f}
are the
same
till the first
n_{1}-2

iterates
, that is,
g_{f}^{i}(0)=F_{f}^{i}(0)
, for

. Furthermore, since
f^{n_{1}-2}(0)>M_{f}
, we have:
g_{f}^{n_{1}-2}(0)=F_{f}^{n_{1}-2}(0)>M_{g}
. Consequently,
F_{f}^{n_{1}-1}(0)>\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, since,
f^{n_{1}-1}(0)<m_{f}
, we must have:
g_{f}^{n_{1}-1}(0)<m_{g}
. So,
1-m_{g}<F_{f}^{n_{1}-1}(0)<1
and hence,
F_{f}^{n_{1}}(0)\geqslant 1
.

The above mentioned scheme persists with
F_{f}^{i}(0)\in\left(1,2\right)
, for
n_{1}<i\leqslant n_{2}-1
and
F_{f}^{n_{2}}(0)\geqslant 2
; then,

of

of

\alpha
. Clearly,
le(Q)\in J
. Construction of the
loop

\alpha
reveals that, during our
motion
along the
loop
:
\alpha
, the only time we can possibly encounter an
unfolding point
is when we
pass
through the
block

L\to J\to K_{1}
in
\alpha
; also, each time we pass through the
block

L\to J\to K_{1}
, we confront
one
“new”
unfolding point
. It follows, that the number of
unfolding points
of
Q
is
p
. Thus, from Theorem
5.2
, the
unfolding number
of
Q
is
\frac{p}{q}
and the
unfolding pair
of
Q
is
(p,q)
. Since,
p
and
q
are arbitrary,
f
has
cycles
of all possible
unfolding pairs
and hence the result follows.
∎

7.
Comparison with over-rotation numbers

From Theorem
6.3
, it is evident that, for the purpose of studying
co-existence
of
cycles
with different
unfolding pairs
, it is sufficient to study
convergent patterns
, and hence only consider maps
f\in\mathcal{U}
. Further as discussed in the beginning of Section
3
, it is sufficient to consider the case
m_{\pi}>M_{\pi}
, where
m_{\pi}
and
M_{\pi}
are the
points
of
absolute minimum
and
absolute maximum
of
\pi
. We begin this section by providing an example to illustrate that, in general,
over-rotation numbers
and
unfolding numbers
are different entities (See Figure
3
).

Example 7.1
.

Consider the
trimodal pattern
characterised by the
permutation

\Pi=(1,2,5,7,10,3,6,8,9,4,11)
. It has
over-rotation number

\frac{3}{11}
, but
unfolding number

\frac{5}{11}
(See Figure
3
).

In the end, we now portray the existence of a peculiar
patterns
for which
over-rotation number
equals
unfolding number
!

Definition 7.2
.

A
convergent pattern

\pi
is called
sheer
, if it is
convergent
and is
decreasing
in
[M_{\pi},m_{\pi}]
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\pi
be a convergent pattern with over-rotation number
orn(\pi)
and unfolding number
un(\pi)
. Then,
orn(\pi)\leqslant un(\pi)
. Further, if
\pi
is sheer,
orn(\pi)=un(\pi)
.

Proof.

Let
P
be any
cycle
which
exhibits

\pi
and let
f\in\mathcal{U}
be the
P
-
linear
map with
unique fixed point

a_{f}
. Let
m_{f}
and
M_{f}
be points of
absolute minimum
and
absolute maximum
of
f
. We assume
M_{f}<a_{f}<m_{f}
and thus
points
of
P
to the
left
of
a_{f}
map to the
right
of
itself
while
points
of
P
to the
right
of
a_{f}
map to the
left
of
itself
. Let the
over-rotation number
of
P
be
\frac{p}{q}
, indicating that there are
exactly

p
points to the
right of the fixed point

a_{f}
which are mapped to the
left
of
a_{f}
. Let these
p
points be denoted as:
x_{1}>x_{2}>x_{3}>\dots>x_{p}
. It’s easy to see that for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots p\}
, precisely one of
f^{2}(x_{i})
and
f(x_{i})
is an
unfolding point
: let us denote this
unfolding point
by
\xi_{i}
,
\xi_{i}\in\{f^{2}(x_{i}),f(x_{i})\}
for
i\in\{1,2,\dots p\}
. This indicates that the number of
unfolding points
of
P
is at-least
p
. Theorem
5.2
, now yields that
unfolding number
of
P
is at-least
\frac{p}{q}
, establishing the first result.

Let us now assume that
\pi
is
sheer
. With this assumption, let us exhibit that number of
unfolding points
of
P
is precisely
p
. By way of contradiction, let’s entertain the possibility of an
unfolding point

\eta
of
P
such that:
\eta\notin\{\xi_{i}:i=1,2,\dots p\}
. We can then encounter
four
potential
cases
:

(1)

f(m_{f})\leqslant M_{f}\leqslant f^{-2}(\eta)\leqslant a_{f}\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)\leqslant m_{f}\leqslant f(M_{f})
.

(2)

f(m_{f})\leqslant M_{f}\leqslant f^{-2}(\eta)\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)\leqslant a_{f}\leqslant m_{f}\leqslant f(M_{f})
.

(3)

f(m_{f})\leqslant M_{f}\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)\leqslant a_{f}\leqslant f^{-2}(\eta)\leqslant f(M_{f})
.

(4)

f(m_{f})\leqslant M_{f}\leqslant a_{f}\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)\leqslant f^{-2}(\eta)\leqslant f(M_{f})
and
f^{-1}(\eta)\leqslant m_{f}

We illustrate, that all these
four cases
leads us to a contradiction. Let’s first examine the
first

case
. We claim
a_{f}\leqslant\eta\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)
. Indeed,
\eta\leqslant a_{f}
means that
f^{-1}(\eta)\in\{x_{i}:i=1,2,\dots p\}
, and hence
\eta\in\{f(x_{i}):i=1,2,\dots p\}
contradicting the fact that
\eta\notin\{\xi_{i}:i=1,2,\dots p\}
. Consequently,
a_{f}\leqslant\eta\leqslant f^{-1}(\eta)
. But, this in turn means,
f
first
decreases
in the interval
[M_{f},a_{f}]
, then
increases
in the interval
[a_{f},f^{-1}(\eta)]
and then
decreases
in
[f^{-1}(\eta),m_{f}]
which contradicts the fact that
\pi
is a
sheer pattern
.

In the
second case
, since,
\eta\geqslant f^{-1}(\eta)
,
f
first
decreases
in the
interval

[M_{f},f^{-2}(\eta)]
, then
increases
in the interval
[f^{-2}(\eta),f^{-1}(\eta)]
, and finally
decreases
in
[f^{-1}(\eta),m_{f}]
, again contradicting the assumption that
\pi
is a
sheer pattern
.

In the
third case
,
f^{-2}(\eta)\in\{x_{i}:i=1,2,\dots p\}
and hence,
\eta\in\{f^{2}(x_{i}):i=1,2,\dots p\}
again, contradicting the fact that
\eta\notin\{\xi_{i}:i=1,2,\dots p\}
. Lastly, the arguments for the
fourth case
exactly mirror that of the
first
. Thus,
number
of
unfolding points
of
P
is precisely
p
and hence from Theorem
5.2
,
unfolding number
of
P
is
\frac{p}{q}
.
∎

Notice, that every
unimodal
or
bimodal pattern

\pi
inherently qualifies as a
sheer pattern
, leading us to the subsequent corollary:

Corollary 7.4
.

If
\pi
has modality less than or equal to
2
, then over-rotation number of
\pi
= unfolding number of
\pi
.

Abstract
. We study variational problems for second order supremal functionals F ∞ ( u ) = ∥ F ( ⋅ , u , D u , A : D 2 u ) ∥ L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)=\|F(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u,\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)} , where F F satisfies certain natural assumptions, A \mathrm{A} is a positive matrix, and Ω ⋐ ℝ n \Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n} . Higher order problems are very novel in the Calculus of Variations in L ∞ \mathrm{L}^{\infty} , and exhibit a strikingly different behaviour compared to first order problems, for which there exists an established theory, pioneered by Aronsson in 1960s. The aim of this paper is to develop a complete theory for F ∞ \mathrm{F}_{\infty} . We prove that, under appropriate conditions, “localised” minimisers can be characterised as solutions to a nonlinear system of PDEs, which is different from the corresponding Aronsson equation for F ∞ \mathrm{F}_{\infty} ; the latter is only a necessary, but not a sufficient condition for minimality. We also establish the existence and uniqueness of localised minimisers subject to Dirichlet conditions on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega , and also their partial regularity outside a singular set of codimension one, which may be non-empty even if n = 1 n=1 .

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose also that
F:\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a given Carathéodory function, whose arguments will be denoted by
(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)
. In this paper we are interested in studying
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
variational problems for supremal functionals involving higher order derivatives of admissible functions. Specifically, we will consider the second order functional

(1.1)

\ \ \ \ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u):=\big\|F(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u,\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)},\ \ \ \ u\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega),

where the class
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
of weakly twice differentiable functions is defined as

(1.2)

\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,:=\,\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\Big\{u\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)\ \big|\ \mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\Big\}.

In (
1.1
)-(
1.2
),
\mathrm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
denotes a positive symmetric matrix, and “
:
” denotes the Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
(for details on all the symbolisations we employ in this paper, we refer to Subsection
2.1
). Variational problems for second (or higher) order supremal functionals are very novel in the Calculus of Variations in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
, and no general theory is yet available.
The purpose of the present paper is to develop a complete theory for the general second order functional (
1.1
)
.

Since
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
is not Gateaux differentiable, general critical points cannot be defined in the classical sense. The lack of smoothness is a standard intricacy of all supremal functionals, and persists even if e.g.
F
is
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
and we consider only the restriction
\smash{\mathrm{F}_{\infty}|_{\mathrm{C}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})}}
: by Danskin’s theorem (see
[
20
]
), the Gateaux derivative exists only when the argmax set is a singleton. We therefore turn our attention to the
existence, uniqueness and regularity of appropriately defined minimisers, as well in their variational characterisation through PDEs.

Before introducing our assumptions and results, and for the sake of comparison, let us discuss briefly the more classical case of first order
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
functionals, which presents some striking differences. In this case, at least when restricted to scalar-valued admissible functions, there exists a rather complete theory. Let
\mathrm{H}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
be given, and consider

(1.3)

\ \ \ \mathrm{E}_{\infty}(u,\mathcal{O}):=\big\|\mathrm{H}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)},\ \ \ \ u\in\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega),\ \mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega.

Variational problems for (
1.3
) were pioneered by Aronsson in the 1960s, who is considered the founder of the theory (see e.g.
[
3
]
-
[
5
]
). The trademark feature of (
1.3
) is that minimisers of
\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(\cdot,\Omega)
are in general highly non-unique and may not satisfy any PDE, in the sense of some analogue of the Euler-Lagrange equations of integral functionals. If however one builds locality in the minimality notion, namely require
by definition
that
\mathrm{E}_{\infty}(\cdot,\mathcal{O})
is minimised for all
\mathcal{O}\Subset\Omega
with respect to the function’s own boundary values on
\partial\mathcal{O}
, then any such function
u
, satisfies the
Aronsson equation
in the viscosity sense and is called an
absolute minimiser
of (
1.3
) (for a pedagogical introduction, we refer e.g. to
[
28
]
). The Aronsson equation arising from (
1.3
) is the following quasilinear second order degenerate elliptic PDE

(1.4)

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{p}}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\mathrm{D}\big(\mathrm{H}(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\big)=0,\ \ \text{ in }\Omega.

It turns out that the opposite is true as well, and the PDE (
1.4
) is sufficient for absolute minimality, whilst the corresponding Dirichlet problem has a unique solution. All these deep results require appropriate assumptions to be imposed, typically involving convexity of
\mathrm{H}
in
\mathrm{P}
, and no explicit dependence in
u
. Without any attempt to be exhaustive, for some important contributions in the area, we refer e.g. to
[
2
,
8
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
27
,
38
,
40
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
.

The phenomena arising in the study of higher order supremal functionals are radically different from those governing the first order case of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). Firstly, at least for the case of (
1.1
) where the dependence on second derivatives is through the elliptic operator
u\mapsto\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u
, absolute minimisers are not a fundamental notion, as
usual (global) minimisers of (
1.1
) satisfy astounding uniqueness properties
, even with
u
-dependence being allowed and under very weak convexity requirements. In fact, one can take this one step further, and show that even
localised minimisers
, namely those which minimise only in an appropriate neighbourhood of
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, are also locally unique. On the other hand, the corresponding higher order Aronsson equation for (
1.1
), although it can be easily derived, it plays only an ancillary role in this setting, as it
is a necessary but not a sufficient condition for minimality
, even if we restrict our attention to classical solutions. Additionally, it is fully nonlinear and of third order, failing to be elliptic in any reasonable sense. Furthermore, the very definition of generalised solutions for the Aronsson equation is a non-trivial matter, as neither weak nor viscosity solutions apply.

In this paper, following an entirely different approach,
we identify a different set of equations which completely characterises minimisers of (
1.1
), even localised ones, and it allows to ascertain their uniqueness for given prescribed Dirichlet boundary conditions
, under natural assumptions. We may also include explicit
(u,\mathrm{D}u)
-dependence, a fact which is interesting in itself, as
u
-dependence typically precludes uniqueness assertions in the first order case (see e.g.
[
52
]
). Our investigations are in part inspired by curvature problems in Riemannian geometry, specifically the Yamabe and Nirenberg problems, see
[
42
,
49
]
and subsequently. However, our results herein have a different focus and as they stand they do not directly apply to differential geometry.

We now present the main contributions of this work, beginning with the minima- lity notions. To state them concisely, let us define the following subspace of (
1.2

(1.5)

\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\,:=\,\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{W}^{2,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Then,

becomes a dual Banach space with separable predual when endowed with the norm (we postpone a thorough discussion until Subsection

\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\,\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Definition 1

(Global, narrow, local minimisers)
.

Consider a function
u\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
.

(i) We say that

is a
(global) minimiser
of (
1.1
) when

\ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u+\psi),\ \ \ \ \text{for all }\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

(ii) We say that
u
is a
narrow minimiser
of (
1.1
) when there exists
\rho>0
such that

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u+\psi),\ \ \ \ \text{for all }\psi\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}_{\rho}\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

(iii) We say that
u
is a
local minimiser
of (
1.1
) when there exists
\rho>0
such that

\ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u+\psi),\ \ \ \ \text{for all }\psi\in\mathbb{B}^{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}_{\rho}.

(iv) If additionally the inequalities in either of (i)-(ii)-(iii) are strict when
\psi\neq 0
, then

will be called a
strict global/narrow/local minimiser
respectively.

One can define global/narrow/local minimisers of integral functionals in an analogous fashion. Evidently, every global minimiser is narrow, and every narrow minimiser is local, but in general neither of the converse implications are true. The significance of the concept of narrow minimisers is justified by the fact that they satisfy stronger existence, uniqueness and stability properties than local minimisers. Narrow minimisers relate to the concept of strong local minimisers (see e.g.
[
39
]
), but they are different. The space
\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
appearing in their definition is not essential, and can be replaced by any Banach space
(\mathfrak{X},|\hskip-1.0pt\|\cdot\|\hskip-1.0pt|)
into which
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
has a compact embedding (e.g.
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega),\mathrm{C}^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}),\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}),\mathrm{W}^{2-\varepsilon,p}(\Omega)
, etc.), and analogous results are obtainable in this case too. Let us now state our assumptions:

(1.6)

\left\{\ \begin{split}&\text{$F:\Omega\times(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}$ is a Carath\'{e}odory function, satisfying }\\
&\text{that $F(x,\cdot)\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\mathbb{R})$, a.e.\,\,$x\in\Omega$, and $F(\cdot,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)$}\\
&\text{uniformly when $(\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\in\mathcal{M}$, for any bounded $\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\times\mathbb{R}$.}\end{split}\right.

(1.7)

\left\{\ \begin{split}\text{There exist $C\in\mathrm{C}^{\nnearrow}[0,\infty)$ and $c>0$}&\text{ such that}\\
\partial_{\xi}F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\geq\ &c,\phantom{{}^{\big|}}\\

In (

(a)
Let
(u_{\infty},f_{\infty})

\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\times(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\})
satisfy the equations (

\ \ \ u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{C}^{k+2,\gamma}\big(\Omega\setminus\{f_{\infty}=0\}\big),\ \ \ \ \text{for all }\gamma\in(0,1),

and the set

(b)
Under the additional assumptions of Theorem

.

We now connect the system of equations (

)-(
1.9
) to the Aronsson equation which is associated with (

). By a standard formal computational argument based on
\mathrm{L}^{p}
approximations and an appropriate rescaling of the Euler-Lagrange equations (as e.g. in

), which is a fully nonlinear third order PDE:

(1.18)

\partial_{\xi}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})\hskip 1.0pt\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}\big(|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})|\big)\otimes\mathrm{D}\big(|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})|\big)=0,\ \ \text{ in }\Omega.

Since by assumption (
1.7
) we have
\partial_{\xi}F>0
, and also
\mathrm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is a (strictly) positive symmetric matrix, (
1.18
) is (at least formally) equivalent to

\mathrm{D}\big(|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})|\big)=0,\ \ \text{ in }\Omega.

Therefore,
the Aronsson equation (
1.18
) corresponding to (
1.1
) is equivalent to the statement that
|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})|
is constant on connected components of
\Omega
. This is a genuinely weaker statement that the statement encoded in the system of equations (
1.8
)-(
1.9
)
. Indeed, on the one hand, (
1.8
) guarantees that

is not only constant throughout
\Omega
, but in fact it is everywhere equal to the exact value
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})
(even if
\Omega
has more than one connected components). On the other hand, (

), provides sharper information about the sign of

, which is determined by the nodal set of the weak solution
f_{\infty}
to this divergence PDE. By the results in
[
26

, the nodal set is actually a lower dimensional set and in particular has zero Lebesgue measure.
Therefore, the Aronsson equation is only a necessary but not a sufficient condition for minimality.
More precisely, directly from Theorem

, we have that any narrow minimiser of (
1.1
) is a strong solution to (

) in

is in fact a (generalised)

-solution to the

counterpart of the third order fully nonlinear Aronsson equation (

Now we supplement Theorem
4
with certain approximability properties, originating from the method of construction through penalised
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-approximating functionals. Our method is based on a novel adaptation of the trademark method of
\mathrm{L}^{p}
approximations, which consists of the introduction of an extra penalisation term. To the best of our knowledge, this adaptation first appeared in the literature in
[

.
The advantage of this modification is that it forces the approximating
\mathrm{L}^{p}
minimisers to converge to a pre-selected

minimiser as

. For any
u_{0}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
,
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
,
p>n
, and
\epsilon>0
, we set

(1.19)

\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(u)\,:=\,\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\right)^{\!\!{1}/{p}}\,+\,\frac{\epsilon}{2}\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},

for all
u\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)\ (=\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega))
.

Theorem 7

(Necessity of the equations and
\mathrm{L}^{p}
approximation with penalisation)
.

In the setting of Theorem
4
and under the same hypotheses, we have that the pair
(u_{\infty},f_{\infty})
satisfies the following approximation properties:

(i)
There exists a family of functions

, where for each

is a narrow minimiser of the penalised

(given by (

, such that,

(1.20)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\mathrm{F}_{p,1,u_{\infty}}(u_{p})\longrightarrow\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}),\\
&u_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_{\infty},\ \ \text{ in }\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega),\ \forall\ q\in(1,\infty),\\
&u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty},\ \ \,\text{ in }\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}),\ \forall\ \gamma\in(0,1),\end{split}\right.

along a sequence of indices

satisfying

as

.

(ii)
For any

(1.21)

e_{p}^{1-p}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\big(|F|^{p-2}F\partial_{*}F\big)(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\cdot\mathrm{J}^{2}_{*}\phi\Big]\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}+\int_{\Omega}(u_{p}-u_{\infty})\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}=0,

for all

e_{p}\,:=\left(\,-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\!\right)^{\!\!\frac{1}{p}}.

Further, by setting

(1.23)

f_{p}\,:=\,e_{p}^{1-p}\big(|F|^{p-2}F\partial_{\xi}F\big)(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p}),

we have

(1.24)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&f_{p}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}f_{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega},\ \text{ in }\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega}),\\
&f_{p}\longrightarrow f_{\infty},\hskip 53.0pt\text{ in }\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\ \forall\ q\in(1,n^{\prime}),\end{split}\right.

and also

(1.25)

\ \ \ \mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{p}\longrightarrow\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\infty}\ \ \text{ in }\mathrm{L}^{q}\big(\Omega\setminus\{f_{\infty}=0\}\big),\ \forall\ q\in(1,\infty),

along a sequence of indices

Let us now discuss the state of the art for higher order (scalar-valued)
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
variational problems. The relevant higher order Aronsson equation was first derived formally by Aronsson himself and Barron in
[
6
]
, but noting its peculiarities, it was not explored further. In the paper
[
34
]
by the first author and Pryer, higher order
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
problems were considered systematically for the first time, and several initial results where established for the functional
u\mapsto\|H(\mathrm{D}^{2}u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, including existence of generalised solutions to the Dirichlet problem for the corresponding fully nonlinear Aronsson equation. For this task, the general framework of
\mathcal{D}
-solutions was utilised, a systematic approach for fully nonlinear PDE systems, introduced by the first author in
[
29
]
. In
[
44
]
, Papamikos and Pryer studied special solutions to the same higher order Aronsson equation, classifying them by using Lie symmetries. In
[
33
]
, the first author with Parini studied a higher order
\infty
-eigenvalue problem, generalised subsequently to the fully nonlinear setting by the first author with Clark in
[
17
]
. In
[
16
]
, Clark and Muha, jointly with the first author, inspired by questions arising in data assimilation, studied a second order PDE constrained
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
problem involving the Navier-Stokes equations. Higher order
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
variational problems arising in Riemannian geometry were previously studied by the second author with Schwetlick and also by Sakellaris in
[
42
,
49
]
, and more recently again by the second author in
[
41
]
. In
[
31
]
, the authors considered the special case of (
1.1
) given by
u\mapsto\|\mathcal{F}(\cdot,\Delta u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
which, unsurprisingly, is immensely simpler than the case of
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
which involves explicit
(u,\mathrm{D}u)
-dependence.

This paper is organised as follows. This rather long introduction is followed by Section
2
, in which we discuss in some detail the preliminaries, our functional space setup, our assumptions, and also provide explicit classes of integrands (Subsection
2.2
). Therein we also establish some auxiliary results of independent interest. In particular, we identify
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
as a dual Banach space with separable predual, and we also prove a general lemma regarding the existence of predual spaces for weakly* closed subspaces in dual Banach spaces (Subsection
2.3
). We also establish certain auxiliary results of independent interest, including characterisations and examples of compact sets in
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
(Subsection
2.4
), approximability of constrained minimisers in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
via
\mathrm{L}^{p}
constrained minimisers as
p\to\infty
(Subsection
2.5
), and a lemma about strong compactness in
\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
of solutions to divergence PDE with
\mathrm{L}^{1}
bounded coefficients (Subsection
2.6
), and some results regarding the size of nodal sets and other properties of solutions to equations of the form (
1.9
) (Subsection
2.7
). Finally, in Section
3
we establish all our main results, whose statements are given earlier in this introduction.

We close this introduction by noting that, in our recent paper
[
32
]
, we consider the case of the functional
u\mapsto\|\mathrm{A}(\cdot)\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u+b(\cdot,u,\mathrm{D}u)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, which involves a semilinear differential operator. Even though there is some overlap of
[
32
]
with the results obtained herein, the phenomena are significantly different. In particular, in
[
32
]
the natural minimality notion involves
almost minimisers
with a first order error term, and not global/narrow/local minimisers as herein. Additionally, unlike the present work, uniqueness in
[
32
]
is an open question for almost minimisers. Therefore, for endogenous mathematical reasons, as well as for practicality purposes of space and size, we treat these related problems separately, as an attempt for a unified approach would considerably complicate and impair the exposition, rather than enhance it.

2.
Preliminaries and auxiliary results

We begin by discussing in some detail our notation and our functional space setup, and also establish some auxiliary results of independent interest.

2.1.
Preliminaries and notational conventions

Let “
:
” denotes the Euclidean (Frobenius) inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, namely
A\!:\!B=\mathrm{tr}(A^{\top}B)
. For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\equiv\mathbb{R}^{n\times 1}
, we symbolise
a\otimes b:=ab^{\top}
. Further, for any open
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for a twice (weakly) differentiable function
u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the notation
\mathrm{D}u=(\mathrm{D}_{1}u,...,\mathrm{D}_{n}u):\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
will symbolise its gradient, and
\mathrm{D}^{2}u=(\mathrm{D}^{2}_{ij}u)_{i,j=1}^{n}:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
will denote its Hessian, where obviously
\mathrm{D}_{i}\equiv\partial/\partial x_{i}
. For any function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define the translation
\tau_{z}u
by setting
\tau_{z}u(x):=u(x+z)
. The closed ball, open ball and sphere of radius
r
centred at a point
x\in\mathfrak{X}
of a normed space
(\mathfrak{X},\|\cdot\|)
will be denoted by
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{\mathfrak{X}}_{\rho}(x),\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}}_{\rho}(x),\mathbb{S}^{\mathfrak{X}}_{\rho}(x)
respectively. If
\rho=1
and/or
x=0
, we will omit explicitly displaying them, and if
X
is clear from the context, it may be omitted as well. The functional space notation we employ is either self-explanatory, or otherwise a convex combination of standard symbolisations (as e.g. in
[
1
,
19
,
24
,
35
,
48
,
50
]
). For example,
[\,\cdot\,]_{\mathrm{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}
symbolises the Hölder seminorm over
\Omega
,
\smash{\mathrm{C}^{\nnearrow}[0,\infty)}
symbolises the set of continuous increasing functions on
[0,\infty)
, and
\smash{\mathrm{C}_{0^{+}}^{\nnearrow}[0,\infty)}
symbolises the subset of those that vanish at the origin (i.e. moduli of continuity). The
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure will be denoted by
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, and the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure by
\mathcal{H}^{k}
. Further, all
\mathrm{L}^{p}
norms appearing in this paper with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
over
\Omega
will always be the normalised ones, namely with the average over
\Omega
instead of the integral, as e.g. in (
1.19
). Let now
(\mathfrak{X},\|\cdot\|)
be a normed space, with dual Banach space
(\mathfrak{X}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
. Given a subset
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
and a subset
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{*}
, the annihilator
\mathcal{A}^{\bot}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{*}
and the pre-annihilator
{}^{\bot}\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
are defined as

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}^{\bot}&:=\big\{f\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}:f(x)=0,\ \forall\,x\in\mathcal{A}\big\}=\big\{f\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}:f^{-1}(\{0\})\supseteq\mathcal{A}\big\},\\
{}^{\bot}\mathcal{B}&:=\big\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:f(x)=0,\ \forall\,f\in\mathcal{B}\big\}=\bigcap\big\{f^{-1}(\{0\}):{f\in\mathcal{B}}\big\}.\end{split}

(Details can be found e.g. in
[
48
, Ch. 1 and Ch. 4]
.) Then,
\mathcal{A}^{\bot}
is a weakly*-closed subspace in
\mathfrak{X}^{*}
, and
{}^{\bot}\mathcal{B}
is a norm-closed subspace in
\mathfrak{X}
. If
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}
is any norm-closed subspace, the equivalence relation
x^{\prime}\sim x^{\prime\prime}\Leftrightarrow x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}
partitions
\mathfrak{X}
to cosets, the collection of which comprises the quotient vector space
\mathfrak{X}/_{\mathcal{N}}:=\{x+{\mathcal{N}}:x\in\mathfrak{X}\}
. Let
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}/_{\mathcal{N}}
denote the quotient map, given by
\pi(x):=x+{\mathcal{N}}
. Then,
\mathfrak{X}/_{\mathcal{N}}
becomes a normed space, when endowed with the quotient norm

\ \ \ \|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{\mathcal{N}}}:=\inf\big\{\|x-n\|\,:\,n\in{\mathcal{N}}\big\}=\mathrm{dist}(x,{\mathcal{N}}),\ \ \ x\in\mathfrak{X}.

2.2.
Discussion of assumptions and examples

We now focus on assumptions (
1.11
)-(
1.13
), which involve convexity of
|F(x,\cdot)|^{\bar{p}}
on convex subsets of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
for large
\bar{p}\geq 2
, and also on assumption (
1.17
), which requires additional regularity of the restriction of the boundary condition
u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
to
\partial\Omega
.

2.2.1.
The restricted convexity assumptions (
1.11
)-(
1.13
)

A necessary and sufficient condition for the convexity of
|F(x,\cdot)|^{\bar{p}}
on an open convex subset
L\times M\times R\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
, is the following (semi-convexity type) matrix inequality

(2.1)

F\partial^{2}_{**}F+(\bar{p}-1)\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F\geq 0,

which must hold a.e. on
\Omega\times L\times M\times R
. The equivalence follows from the identity

(2.2)

\partial^{2}_{**}\big(|F|^{p}\big)=\,p|F|^{p-2}\Big(F\partial^{2}_{**}F+(p-1)\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F\Big).

As a consequence, if
|F(x,\cdot)|^{p}
is convex on
L\times M\times R
, so is
|F(x,\cdot)|^{q}
for any
q\geq p
. In the case that there is no explicit gradient dependence (and hence no
\mathrm{p}
-variable), a sufficient condition for (
2.1
) is the following: let
\star\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
be the Hodge matrix, satisfying
\star^{2}=-\mathrm{I}
(rotation by
\pi/2
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
). Then, (
2.1
) follows from:

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{split}&\text{$(\partial_{*}F,\star\,\partial_{*}F)$ is an orthogonal eigenframe of $\partial^{2}_{**}(|F|^{2})$ with eigenvalues}\\
&\text{$(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})$, satisfying $\sigma_{2}\geq 0$, $\sigma_{1}\geq-m$ for $m\in\mathbb{N}$, a.e.\ on $\Omega\times L\times R$.}\end{split}\right.

Condition (
2.3
) is a stronger requirement than (
2.1
), but might be easier to verify. The implication “(
2.3
)
\Rightarrow
(
2.1
)” follows by the spectral theorem applied to
\partial^{2}_{**}(|F|^{2})
:

\begin{split}\partial^{2}_{**}(|F|^{2})&=\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|\partial_{*}F|^{2}}\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F\,+\,\frac{\sigma_{2}}{|\partial_{*}F|^{2}}(\star\,\partial_{*}F)\otimes(\star\,\partial_{*}F)\\
&\geq\frac{\sigma_{1}}{|\partial_{*}F|^{2}}\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F\\
&\geq-\frac{m}{c}\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F,\end{split}

which holds a.e. on
\Omega\times L\times R
. This implies (
2.1
) when
\bar{p}\geq(m/c)+1
. In the above inequalities we have used that
|\partial_{*}F|\geq c>0
, which follows from (
1.7
).

2.2.2.
The Whitney differentiability assumption (
1.17
)

By the (Lipschitz) regularity of
\partial\Omega
, we have the Sobolev imbedding
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
, for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
in the usual sense, namely the precise representative from each a.e.-equivalence class has an extension to a function in
\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
is at the outset
\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}
on
\partial\Omega
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. The assumption that
u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
is
\mathrm{C}^{2}
on the compact set
\partial\Omega
is understood in the standard sense of Whitney
[
51
, Sec. 3, p. 64]
, meaning that it possesses (uniform) second order Taylor expansions. More precisely, we assume that there exists a symmetric matrix-valued map
X\in\mathrm{C}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
and a modulus of continuity
\omega\in\mathrm{C}^{\nnearrow}_{0}[0,\infty)
, such that

\left\{\ \ \ \begin{split}\left|u_{0}(x+z)-u_{0}(x)-\mathrm{D}u_{0}(x)z-\frac{1}{2}X(x):z\otimes z\right|&\leq\omega(|z|)|z|^{2},\ \ \ \ \\
\Big|\mathrm{D}u_{0}(x+z)-\mathrm{D}u_{0}(x)-X(x)z\Big|&\leq\omega(|z|)|z|,\end{split}\right.

for all
x,z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
x,x+z\in\partial\Omega
. It can be easily seen that, due to the smoothness of
\partial\Omega
,
\mathrm{C}^{1}
differentiability for a scalar-valued function in the above sense is equivalent to tangential differentiability on
\partial\Omega
(regarded as a
\mathrm{C}^{2}
compact manifold with the induced Riemannian metric). However, requiring a function to be
\mathrm{C}^{2}
on
\partial\Omega
, or requiring its Euclidean gradient vector field be tangentially
\mathrm{C}^{1}
on
\partial\Omega
, are in general slightly stronger requirements that being tangentially
\mathrm{C}^{2}
on
\partial\Omega
.

2.2.3.
Explicit classes of supremands

We demonstrate some classes of functions
F
which satisfy our assumptions, noting that the results proved herein apply to supremands of the form
\mathcal{F}=\Phi\circ F
, for
any
increasing odd function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
(Corollary

). For the sake of clarity, we assume no explicit gradient dependence in these examples (and hence no
\mathrm{p}
-variable). A similar (but more complicated) analysis can be performed by assuming explicit dependence on the gradient as well.

Additive ansatz.
Let
A:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
and

be given. We set

If we have

with
A_{\xi}\geq c>0
, and for all
x\in\Omega
, we have
a(x,\cdot)\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
a,a_{\eta},a_{\eta\eta}\in C(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R})
, then (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) are readily satisfied. In particular, since
A
is strictly increasing,
F(x,\eta,\xi)=0
iff
\xi=A^{-1}(-a(x,\eta))
, and therefore

F(x,\eta,\cdot)^{-1}(\{0\})\subseteq\Big[A^{-1}\Big(-\max_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}a(x,\eta)\Big),A^{-1}\Big(-\min_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}a(x,\eta)\Big)\Big].

Regarding the satisfaction of (
1.11
)-(
1.12
)-(
1.13
), a direct computation gives that, if we set
\mathfrak{F}:=F\partial_{**}^{2}F+M\partial_{*}F\otimes\partial_{*}F
where
M\geq 1
, then we have
\mathfrak{F}\geq 0
if and only if (suppressing the arguments
(x,\eta,\xi)
for brevity)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(\mathfrak{F})=(a+A)(A_{\xi\xi}+a_{\eta\eta})+M\big[(A_{\xi})^{2}+(a_{\eta})^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\det(\mathfrak{F})=(a+A)^{2}A_{\xi\xi}a_{\eta\eta}+M(a+A)\big[a_{\eta\eta}(A_{\xi})^{2}+A_{\xi\xi}(a_{\eta})^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Let us also consider the
hypergraph
of the function
A^{-1}\circ(-a)
, namely

\mathcal{H}:=\Big\{(x,\eta,\xi)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}:\ \xi\geq A^{-1}\big(\!-a(x,\eta)\big)\Big\},

which has the property that
F(x,\eta,\xi)\geq 0
iff
(x,\eta,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}
. Then:

(a) Let
L,R\subseteq\mathbb{R}

2.3.
The functional space setup and duality

Now we expound on the functional spaces we utilise herein, and study some of their properties. We set

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,:=\,\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,p}(\Omega),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,:=\,\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega),

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,:=\,\bigcap_{1<p<\infty}\mathrm{W}_{0}^{2,p}(\Omega).

Then, we have
\mathbb{W}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\supset\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\supset\mathbb{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
, and all three spaces are Fréchet spaces, topologised by the family of seminorms
\big\{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega^{\prime})}:\Omega^{\prime}\!\Subset\Omega,\,p\in(1,\infty)\big\}
. If now
\mathbb{A}:\mathrm{W}^{2,1}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is the (strong) linear differential operator defined by
\mathbb{A}u:=\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u
, the spaces defined in (
1.2
) and (
1.5
) can be written as

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{A}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\big),

(2.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\,

\displaystyle=\,\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\cap\mathbb{A}^{-1}\big(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\big).

As we will show in Subsections
2.3.1
-
2.3.2
,
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
becomes a dual Banach space with (separable) predual
\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))
and convenient weak* density and compactness properties, when endowed with either of the equivalent norms

(2.13)

\left\{\ \ \ \begin{split}\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}&:=\,\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)},\\
\|u\|^{\prime}_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}&:=\,\|\mathrm{J}_{*}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{split}\right.

2.3.1.
Completeness and a priori estimates

We now study some important properties of the space
\mathcal{W}_{0}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
which have independent interest, but are also utilised in the proofs of our main results in Section
3
.

Lemma 9
.

The functional space (
2.12
) is a Banach space when endowed with either of the Lipschitz-equivalent norms defined in (
2.13
). Moreover, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
K=K(p)>0
(depending also on
\Omega,\mathrm{A}
), such that

\|u\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq K\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq K\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)},

for all
u\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof of Lemma

9
. We begin by noting that the Lipschitz equivalence between the two norms in (
2.13
) is an immediate consequence of Morrey’s imbedding theorem, since
\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq K\|u\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,n}(\Omega)}
for some
K=K(\Omega)>0
. Next, by the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
estimates for the Laplacian on a bounded open set
U\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see e.g.
[
25
, Cor. 9.10]
), for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a
K=K(p,U)>0
such that

\|v\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(U)}\,\leq\,K\|\Delta v\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(U)},

for all
v\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{0}(U)
. Let
\mathrm{A}=O^{\top}\!\Lambda^{2}O
be the spectral representation of
\mathrm{A}
, namely
O\in\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{R})
and
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma(\sqrt{\mathrm{A}}))
is a diagonal matrix with entries the eigenvalues of
\sqrt{\mathrm{A}}
. Then, by setting for brevity
\Gamma:=\Lambda O
and selecting
v(x):=u(\Gamma^{\top}x)
and also
U:=\Gamma^{-\!\top}\Omega
, we have
\mathrm{A}=\Gamma^{\top}\Gamma
and the identities
\Delta v(x)=\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u(\Gamma^{\top}x)
and
\mathrm{D}v(x)=\Gamma\hskip 1.0pt\mathrm{D}u(\Gamma^{\top}x)
, for all
x\in\Omega
. By the change of variables’ formula and Hölder’s inequality, there exists a new constant
K=K(p,\Omega,\mathrm{A})>0
, such that

\|u\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,K\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,K\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)},

for all
u\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. If
(u_{m})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is a Cauchy sequence, the above implies

\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq\,K\big\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{m}-\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{n}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow 0,

as
m,n\to\infty
. By a diagonal argument and by the completeness of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and of
\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exist functions
u\in\mathbb{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and
f\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
u_{m}\longrightarrow u
in
\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)
for any fixed
p\in(1,\infty)
, and also
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{m}\longrightarrow f
in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, both as
m\to\infty
. Further, for any fixed
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{m})\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}&=\int_{\Omega}u_{m}(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\!\!\!\longrightarrow\int_{\Omega}u(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\phi(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},\end{split}

as
m\to\infty
, which establishes that
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u=f\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This in turn yields that
u\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, as claimed. ∎

Remark 12
.

Using similar techniques to those used to establish Proposition
11
, one can also show that

\big(\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\big)^{*}\cong\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{ba}(\Omega)),

where
\mathrm{ba}(\Omega)\cong(\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega))^{*}\cong(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))^{**}
is the space of real-valued bounded finitely additive measures, which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on

[

). However, the identification of the dual space of

is not required for the main results, therefore we refrain from this task.

The proof of Proposition
11
is based on the following general result of independent interest about subspaces of dual Banach spaces (Lemma
13
). This is perhaps well-known to the experts of duality theory, but since we could not locate a specific reference of this fact, we do provide a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 13
.

Let
(\mathfrak{X},\|\cdot\|)
be a Banach space, and let
(\mathfrak{X}^{*},\|\cdot\|_{*})
be its dual Banach space. If
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{*}
is any weakly*-closed subspace, then
\mathfrak{M}
is a dual Banach space itself, and in particular

(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}\cong\,\mathfrak{M}.

The isometric isomorphism above is given by
\phi\mapsto\phi\circ\pi
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{X}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}
is the quotient map, and
{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}
denotes the pre-annihilator of
\mathfrak{M}
in
\mathfrak{X}
(Subsection
2.1
).

Proof of Lemma

13
. We endow
\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}
with the quotient norm, and the spaces
\mathfrak{X}^{*}
and
(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}
with their natural dual norms, and define the mapping

\ \ \ \pi^{*}\ :\ \ (\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{*},\ \ \ \pi^{*}(\phi):=\phi\circ\pi.

Then,
\pi^{*}
is linear and well-defined, as for any
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, we have

\big|\big(\pi^{*}(\phi)\big)(x)\big|=|\phi(\pi(x))|\leq\|\phi\|_{(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}}\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}\leq\|\phi\|_{(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}}\|x\|.

Hence,
\pi^{*}(\phi)\in\mathfrak{X}^{*}
. We will now show that

(2.16)

\pi\big(\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}}\big)=\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}.

(This fact is claimed without proof in
[
48
, p. 97]
.) On the one hand, the inclusion
\pi(\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}})\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}
is an immediate consequence of the inequality
\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}\leq\|x\|
, which is valid for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Conversely, if we have any
\pi(x)\in\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}
which satisfies
\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}<1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
small enough there exists
{m_{\varepsilon}\in}\,^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}
such that
\|x-m_{\varepsilon}\|<\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}+\varepsilon<1
. Since
\pi(x-m_{\varepsilon})=\pi(x)
, it follows that there exists a representative
x^{\prime}:=x-m_{\varepsilon}
such that
\pi(x)=\pi(x^{\prime})\in\pi\big(\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}}\big)
and
\|x^{\prime}\|<1
. This proves the reverse inclusion
\smash{\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}\subseteq\pi(\mathbb{B}^{\mathfrak{X}})}
, therefore establishing (
2.16
). We will now show that
\pi^{*}
is an isometry. In view of (
2.16
), we have

\begin{split}\|\pi^{*}(\phi)\|_{*}&=\sup_{x\in\mathfrak{X},\|x\|<1}|\phi(\pi(x))|=\sup_{\pi(x)\in\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\!M},\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}<1}|\phi(\pi(x))|=\|\phi\|_{(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}}.\end{split}

We will now show that the image of
\pi^{*}
lies in
\mathfrak{M}
, namely

\pi^{*}\big(\big(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}\big)^{*}\big)\subseteq\mathfrak{M}.

For any fixed
\phi\in(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}
, we have that
(\pi^{*}(\phi))(x)=\phi(\pi(x))=0
, for all
{x\in}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}
, because
\pi(x)=0
when
{x\in}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}
. This implies that
\pi^{*}(\phi)\in(^{\bot}\mathfrak{M})^{\bot}
. Since
\mathfrak{M}
is by assumption a weakly* closed subspace of
\mathfrak{X}^{*}
, by
[
48
, Th. 4.7, p. 96]
, we have that
(^{\bot}\mathfrak{M})^{\bot}=\mathfrak{M}
. This implies the claimed inclusion. We finally show that
\pi^{*}
is onto
\mathfrak{M}
. To this aim, note that any functional
f\in\mathfrak{M}
induces a functional
\bar{f}\in(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}
in a natural way, by setting
\bar{f}(\pi(x)):=f(x)
,
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. This is well-defined regardless of the choice of representative, because if we have
\pi(x^{\prime\prime})=\pi(x^{\prime})
, then
x^{\prime\prime}-x^{\prime}\in{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}
and hence
f(x^{\prime\prime})=f(x^{\prime})
, because
f|_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}\equiv 0
when
f\in\mathfrak{M}
. Further, by the identity

|\bar{f}(\pi(x))|=|f(x)|=|f(x-z)|,\ \ \ \forall\ {z\in}{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}},

for any
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, we estimate

|\bar{f}(\pi(x))|\leq\|f\|_{*}\inf_{{z\in}{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}\|x-z\|=\|f\|_{*}\|\pi(x)\|_{\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}}}.

This shows that
\bar{f}\in(\mathfrak{X}/_{{}^{\bot}\mathfrak{M}})^{*}
and
\pi^{*}(\bar{f})=f
, completing the proof.
∎

Remark 14
.

The assumption that the subspace
\mathfrak{M}\subseteq\mathfrak{X}^{*}
be weakly*-closed is essential, and in general it cannot be replaced by norm-closedness. For example, the space of sequences

vanishing at infinity is norm-closed (and weakly*-dense) in
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})
, but as it is well known, it possesses no predual space.

Having procured the necessary tools, we main now establish Proposition
11
.

Proof of Proposition

11
. (i) We begin by noting that
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is a separable Banach space and
\mathbb{A}
is a bounded linear operator, because for any

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{A}u\|=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega),\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}u(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\leq\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Hence, the image

is a separable subset of

. Thus,
\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))
becomes a separable normed space when endowed with the dual norm induced by
(\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega))^{*}
. Now we show that it is complete, and hence a Banach space, by showing that it is isometrically isomorphic to a quotient of
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
by a closed subspace. We define

\mathcal{W}:=\Big\{f\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)\ \Big|\ \exists\ \phi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\,:\,f=\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi\text{ a.e.\ on }\Omega\Big\}=\mathbb{A}\big(\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

Then, by arguing as in the proof of Lemma
9
, we have that
\mathcal{W}
is sequentially weakly* closed in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and therefore it is also weakly* closed, because its predual
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is separable and complete, as a consequence of the Krein-Smulian theorem (we refer e.g.
[
18
, Sec. 12, p. 159]
or
[
43
]
). By applying Lemma
13
, it follows that
\pi^{*}:\mathcal{W}\longrightarrow\left(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\!{}^{\bot}{\mathcal{W}}}\right)^{*}
is an isometric isomorphism, and we also have that

\!{}^{\bot}{\mathcal{W}}=\bigg\{u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}u(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}=0,\ \forall\ \phi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\bigg\}=\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A}).

Hence,
\mathcal{W}\cong(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})})^{*}
. Next, note that the induced map on the quotient space

\mathbb{A}\ :\ \ \mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)),\ \ \ \ \mathbb{A}(u+\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})):=\mathbb{A}u,

is a well-defined linear bijection, independent of the representative used in the coset. Further, for any fixed
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
, by standard duality results (see e.g.
[
35
, Corollary 11.46]
) and by the expression of the quotient norm on
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{A}u\|&=\sup_{\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega),\,\|\mathrm{A}:\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}u(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=\sup_{f\in\mathcal{W},\,\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}uf\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.\end{split}

Hence,

\begin{split}\|\mathbb{A}u\|&=\sup_{f\in(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})})^{*},\,\|f\|_{(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})})^{*}}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}uf\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&=\big\|u+\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})}}.\end{split}

Thus, the mapping
\mathbb{A}:\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))
is an isometric isomorphism, and therefore
\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))
is a Banach space. It follows that the induced dual map

\mathbb{A}^{*}:\ \ \big(\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))\big)^{*}\longrightarrow\big(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)/_{\mathrm{N}(\mathbb{A})}\big)^{*},

given by

\ \ \ \ \langle\mathbb{A}^{*}(\psi),u\rangle:=\langle\psi,\mathbb{A}u\rangle,\ \ \ \ u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega),

is also an isometric isomorphism between the corresponding dual spaces. Further, the linear map

\mathbb{A}\ :\ \ \mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathcal{W},\ \ \ \phi\mapsto\mathbb{A}\phi,

is an isometric isomorphism between Banach spaces, since
\|\mathbb{A}\phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}=\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}
. In conclusion, we have the following isometric isomorphism

(\mathbb{A}^{*})^{-1}\circ\pi^{*}\circ\mathbb{A}\ :\ \ \mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\big(\mathbb{A}(\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega))\big)^{*},

which, in view of the expression that the induced duality pairing takes, establishes the desired conclusion.

(ii) We will use the regularisation scheme introduced and studied in
[
30
, Sec. 5]
, whose definition we recall for the convenience of the reader. Since
\partial\Omega
is assumed to be Lipschitz, there exists a smooth vector field
\xi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is transversal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, namely
\xi\cdot\mathrm{n}\geq\delta_{0}
which holds
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\partial\Omega
, for some
\delta_{0}>0
, where
\mathrm{n}:\partial\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{S}
is the unit outer normal vector field, and additionally we have
|\xi|\equiv 1
in an open collar around the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Further, there exist
\ell,\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, we have
\mathrm{dist}(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x),\partial\Omega)\geq 2\varepsilon
, for
x\in\partial\Omega
. Then, for any
\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
, extended by zero on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, we set

\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B})
is a fixed mollifying kernel which satisfies
\eta\geq 0
and
\|\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=1
. This is a variant of the standard mollifier, the difference being that it “compresses”
\psi
to a compactly supported function in
\Omega
before mollifying. This method gives rise to compactly supported smooth approximations, which converge globally on
\Omega
. In
[
30
, Prop. 12]
it is shown that
\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}v\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
and that
\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi\longrightarrow\psi
in
\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for any
q\in(1,\infty)
. By iterating this result, we also have that
\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi\longrightarrow\psi
in
\mathrm{W}^{2,q}_{0}(\Omega)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, for any
q\in(1,\infty)
. Hence, it remains to show that
{\{\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})}}
is bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. To this aim, we compute

\begin{split}\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)(x)=&\ \big(\mathrm{I}+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi(x)\big)^{\!\!\top}\!\mathrm{A}\big(\mathrm{I}+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi(x)\big)\\
&\ :\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\\
&\ +\varepsilon\ell\big(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\xi(x)\big)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. Hence,

\begin{split}\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)(x)=\,&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\,\varepsilon\ell\Big(\mathrm{D}\xi(x)^{\top}\!\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\xi(x)+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi(x)^{\top}\!\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\xi\Big)\\
&\ :\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\varepsilon\ell\big(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\xi(x)\big)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

for any fixed
x\in\Omega
. Since
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B})
, an integration by parts yields

\begin{split}\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)(x)=&\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\,\ell\Big(\mathrm{D}\xi(x)^{\top}\!\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\xi(x)+\varepsilon\ell\mathrm{D}\xi(x)^{\top}\!A\mathrm{D}\xi\Big)\\
&\ :\int_{\mathbb{B}}\mathrm{D}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\cdot\mathrm{D}\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\varepsilon\ell\big(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\xi(x)\big)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{D}\psi\big(x+\varepsilon\ell\xi(x)-\varepsilon y\big)\eta(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. Since
\eta,|\mathrm{D}\eta|
are in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{B})
, by Young’s inequality, we get the estimate

\begin{split}\big|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)(x)\big|\leq&\ \|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,+\,\varepsilon_{0}\ell|\mathrm{A}|\|\mathrm{D}^{2}\xi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathrm{D}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&+\,\ell|\mathrm{A}|\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big(2+\varepsilon_{0}\ell\|\mathrm{D}\xi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)\|\mathrm{D}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\mathrm{D}\eta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\end{split}

for any
x\in\Omega
. This implies that
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(\mathrm{K}_{\varepsilon}\psi)
is indeed bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
. The conclusion ensues.
∎

2.4.
Compact sets in
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
and examples

By using the analogue of the Ascoli-Arzela theorem in
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
(see
[
15
]
), and the results established in Subsection
2.3
, we can explicitly characterise compact sets
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
(arising in Theorem
2
). To state this concisely, we need some additional notation. For any
f\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the
essential oscillation
of
f
over a Lebesgue measurable set
E\subseteq\Omega
is defined as

\underset{E}{\mathrm{ess}\,\mathrm{osc}}\,[f]\,:=\,\underset{x,y\in E}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\big|f(x)-f(y)\big|.

Then, a closed subset
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is (strongly) compact if and only if

(2.17)

\inf_{\big\{\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}\text{ finite Borel partition of }\Omega\big\}}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{K}}\max_{i\in I}\underset{\Omega_{i}}{\mathrm{ess}\,\mathrm{osc}}\,[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}f]=0.\ \ \ \ \

The seemingly complicated condition (
2.17
) is equivalent to the following statement: for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite partition of
\Omega
to Borel sets
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, such that

\big|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}f(x)-\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}f(y)\big|\,\leq\,\varepsilon,\ \ \ \text{a.e.\ }x,y\in\Omega_{i},

for all functions
f\in\mathcal{K}
and all indices
i\in I
. By utilising the above, we have the following immediate consequence of Theorem
2
regarding interesting compact sets of variations.

Corollary 15

(Examples of compact sets of directions of test functions in
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
)
.

In the setting of Theorem
2
and under the same assumptions, the following are true:

(i)
For any
\theta>0
and
p>n
, there exists
\rho>0
such that

\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})<\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}+\psi),

among all
\psi\in\big(\mathrm{W}^{3,p}\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}\big)(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
satisfying
\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}<\rho
,
\|\mathrm{D}^{3}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}<\theta\rho
.

(ii)
Let
\{\Omega_{i}:i\in I\}
be a finite family of disjoint open subsets of
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundaries
\partial\Omega_{i}
, whose closures cover
\Omega
. Then, for any
\theta>0
, there exists
\rho>0
such that

\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})<\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}+\psi),

among all
\psi\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
having piecewise Hölder continuous Hessians (namely
\psi|_{\Omega_{i}}\in\mathrm{C}^{2,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{i})
for all
i\in I
), satisfying
\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}<\rho
,
\underset{i\in I}{\max}\,[\mathrm{D}^{2}\psi]_{\mathrm{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{i})}<\theta\rho
.

Proof of Corollary

15
. (i) Given
\theta>0
and
p>n
, we define

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{\prime}&\,:=\,\Big\{\psi\in\mathbb{S}^{\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}\cap\mathrm{W}^{3,p}(\Omega)\,:\ \|\mathrm{D}^{3}\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta\Big\}.\end{split}

In view of Theorem
2
(that will be established in Section
3
), it suffices to show that
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let

\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

and
\|\mathrm{D}^{3}\psi_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\theta
, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By the compactness of the imbedding
\mathrm{W}^{3,p}(\Omega)\Subset\mathrm{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)

(the proof follows similar lines to those of part (i), and therefore the details are omitted). ∎

2.5.
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-limits of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
constrained minimisers

The proofs of Theorems
4
and
7
are based on the following standalone result of independent interest, which establishes the existence and
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-approximation of narrow (and global) minimisers of (
1.1
) through penalised functionals, treated in a unified fashion as constrained minimisers.

Theorem 16

(Existence and
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-approximation of
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
constrained minimisers)
.

Let
F
satisfy (
1.6
)-(
1.7
) and (
1.15
), and let
\bar{u}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
be fixed. Suppose that
\{\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\}_{n\leq p\leq\infty}
are classes of functions, which satisfy:

(2.18)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega),\text{ when }\,n\leq p<\infty,\\
&\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\text{ is weakly closed, when }\,p<\infty,\\
&\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{E}^{q}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega),\text{ for }n\leq q\leq p\leq\infty,\\
&\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega),\text{ when }\,p=\infty,\\
&\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)\,=\bigcap_{n<p<\infty}\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega),\\
&\bar{u}\in\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega).\end{split}\right.

Then, the following are true:

(i)
For any
\epsilon\geq 0
and
p\geq n+1/c^{3}
, the functional
\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}
(defined in (
1.19
)) has a minimiser
u_{p}
over the admissible class
\mathcal{E}^{p}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)
.

(ii)
For any
\epsilon\geq 0
, the functional
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\cdot-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
has a minimiser
u_{\infty}
over the admissible class
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)
, where
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
is as in (
1.1
). Additionally, there exists a sequence of indices
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
satisfying
p_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, along which we have

(2.19)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}&\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(u_{p})\longrightarrow\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|u_{\infty}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\\
&u_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_{\infty},\hskip 20.0pt\text{ in }W^{2,q}(\Omega),\ \forall\ q\in(1,\infty),\\
&u_{p}\longrightarrow u_{\infty},\hskip 21.0pt\text{ in }\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega}),\ \forall\ \gamma\in(0,1).\end{split}\right.

(iii)
If finally
\bar{u}
minimises
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
over the admissible class
\mathcal{E}^{\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)
and also
\epsilon>0
, then we have that
\bar{u}=u_{\infty}
. In this case, we also have that the modes of convergence in (
2.19
) are in fact full as
p\to\infty
.

The main technical step for the proof of Theorem
16
is the following coercivity estimate for (
1.19
).

Lemma 17

(Coercivity)
.

Let
\delta>0
and let
w\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
be a
\delta
-approximate minimiser of
\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
, namely

\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,\leq\,\delta+\inf_{\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}.

Then, for any
q\in[n,p]
, we have the estimates

(2.20)

\left\{\ \ \begin{split}\|w\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}\,&\leq\,\frac{K(\Omega,q)}{c(1-\alpha)}\Big(M+\delta+\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(\bar{u})\Big),\ \ \ &\mathrm{(i)}\\
\ \ \ \|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\,&\leq\,\frac{1}{c}\Big(1+\delta+\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(\bar{u})+\|w\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}^{\alpha}\Big),\ \ \ &\mathrm{(ii)}\end{split}\right.

where
K(\Omega,q)>0
is the constant appearing in Lemma
9
, and
M>0
is a constant depending only on
\bar{u},\alpha,c,K(q,\Omega),\mathrm{A}
.

Proof of Lemma

17
. Note first that by (
1.1
), (
1.19
), minimality and Young’s inequality, we have

\inf_{\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}\leq\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(\bar{u})\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(\bar{u}).

Further, by (
1.7
) we have that
F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\cdot)^{-1}(\{0\})
is a singleton set. Hence, if we have that if we symbolise

(2.21)

F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\cdot)^{-1}(\{0\})\,=\,\big\{\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big\},

again by (
1.7
), we can easily deduce the inequality

(2.22)

|F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)|\geq c\big|\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big|.

By Lemma
9
and (
1.15
), since
w-\bar{u}\in\mathrm{W}^{2,q}_{0}(\Omega)
, we can estimate

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,&\geq\,\|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}w)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,c\big\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,w,\mathrm{D}w)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,c\big\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}(w-\bar{u})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}-c\big\|\bar{\xi}(\cdot,w,\mathrm{D}w)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,c\frac{\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}}{K(q,\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\big\|\big(|w|+|\mathrm{D}w|\big)^{\alpha}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}-1\\
&\geq\,c\frac{\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}}{K(q,\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\|w\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\alpha q}(\Omega)}-1\\
&\geq\,c\frac{\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}}{K(q,\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\|w\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}-1.\end{split}

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,&\geq\,c\frac{\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}}{K(q,\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\|w-\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \ \ \ -\|\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}-1\\
&\geq\,c\frac{\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}}{K(q,\Omega)}-c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}-\|w-\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \ \ \ -\|\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}-1\\
&\geq\,\frac{c}{K(q,\Omega)}\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}-\big(\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}\big)^{\alpha}\\
&\ \ \ \ -\Big(c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+1\Big).\end{split}

By using the elementary inequality

Nt-t^{\alpha}\geq N(1-\alpha)\big[t-N^{1/(\alpha-1)}\big],\ \ \ t\geq 0,\ N>0,

the previous estimate yields

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,&\geq\,\frac{c(1-\alpha)}{K(q,\Omega)}\bigg[\|w-\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}-\Big(\frac{c}{K(q,\Omega)}\Big)^{1/(\alpha-1)}\bigg]\\
&\ \ \ \ -\Big(c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+1\Big).\end{split}

This implies that

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,&\geq\,\frac{c(1-\alpha)}{K(q,\Omega)}\|w\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \ \ \ -\bigg\{\frac{c(1-\alpha)}{K(q,\Omega)}\bigg[\|\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}+\Big(\frac{c}{K(q,\Omega)}\Big)^{1/(\alpha-1)}\bigg]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\bar{u}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\bar{u}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}+1\bigg\}\\
&=:\,\frac{c(1-\alpha)}{K(q,\Omega)}\|w\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)}-M,\end{split}

from which (
2.20
)(i) follows. Similarly, to obtain (
2.20
)(ii), we have

\begin{split}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(w)\,&\geq\,\|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}w)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,c\big\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,w,\mathrm{D}w)\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\\
&\geq\,c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}-\big\|\big(|w|+|\mathrm{D}w|\big)^{\alpha}\big\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}-1\\
&\geq\,c\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}-\|w\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,q}(\Omega)}-1.\end{split}

The lemma has been established.
∎

Now we may continue with the proof the theorem.

Proof of Theorem

16
. (i) This is a simple application of the direct method. Fix an exponent

and let
(w_{j})_{1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)\subseteq W_{\bar{u}}^{2,p}(\Omega)
be a minimising sequence of
\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}
in
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
17
,
(w_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\mathrm{W}_{\bar{u}}^{2,p}(\Omega)
, hence by the sequential weak compactness of bounded sets in
\mathrm{W}^{2,p}(\Omega)
, there exists
u_{p}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
and a subsequence of indices
({j_{k}})_{1}^{\infty}
such that
w_{j_{k}}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_{p}
in
\mathrm{W}_{\bar{u}}^{2,p}(\Omega)
, as
k\to\infty
. By assumption (
2.18
),
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
is weakly closed, therefore
u_{p}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
. Since we have assumed
p>n+1/c^{3}
, by (
1.15
) and (
2.2
) tested against rank-one matrices of the form
(0,0,0,\xi)\otimes(0,0,0,\xi)
, it follows that
\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\big(|F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\cdot)|^{p}\big)\geq 0
, which implies that
|F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\cdot)|^{p}
is convex. By (
1.7
) and (
1.15
), we have the lower bound

F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\geq c\big(\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)\geq\,c\hskip 1.0pt\xi-\big(|\eta|+|\mathrm{p}|\big)^{\alpha}-1,

and hence the functional (
1.19
) is sequentially weakly lower semi-continuous (see e.g.
[
19
, Th. 3.23, p. 96]
) on the weakly closed set
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
. Hence,
u_{p}
minimises the functional
\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}
over
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{p}(\Omega)
.

(ii) Let
\{u_{p}\}_{n<p<\infty}\subseteq\mathrm{W}^{2,n}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)
be the family of minimisers from part (i). By applying Lemma
17
, for each fixed
q\in(1,\infty)
,
\{u_{p}\}_{n<p<\infty}
is bounded in
\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)
, as a result of (
2.20
)(i). By sequential weak compactness and a standard diagonal argument, there exists a sequence of indices
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
and
u_{\infty}\in\mathbb{\mathrm{}}W^{2,\infty}(\Omega)
(recall (
2.9
)) such that
u_{p_{j}}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
in
\mathrm{W}^{2,q}(\Omega)
, as
j\to\infty
. By Morrey’s theorem and the Lipschitz regularity of
\partial\Omega
, we also have that
u_{p_{j}}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}
in
\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
, as
j\to\infty
. Again by Lemma
17
, for any
q\in(1,\infty)
fixed and
p\geq q
, by (
2.20
)(ii) we have that

\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\frac{1+\delta+\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(\bar{u})+\|u_{p}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}}{c}.

By the sequential weak lower semi-continuity of the
\mathrm{L}^{q}
norm, and the strong convergence of
u_{p_{j}}\longrightarrow u_{\infty}
in
\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
as
j\to\infty
, we have

\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{p_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\,\leq\,\frac{1+\delta+\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(\bar{u})+\|u_{\infty}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}}{c}.

Then, by letting
q\to\infty
we infer that
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, therefore
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{\bar{u}}(\Omega)
. Additionally, by (
2.18
), for any fixed
q\in(1,\infty)
, the family
(u_{p})_{p\geq q}
is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{q}(\Omega)
. Since
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{q}(\Omega)
is weakly closed, by letting
p_{j}\to\infty
we infer that
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{q}(\Omega)
for all
q\in(1,\infty)
. By invoking (
2.18
) once again, we deduce that
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Now we show that
u_{\infty}
minimises
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|\cdot-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}
, and also show the convergence of the energies. For any
v\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
fixed and
p\geq q
, we have

\begin{split}\big\|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|u_{p}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq\big\|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|u_{p}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\\
&=\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(u_{p})\\
&\leq\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(v)\\
&\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(v)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|v-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

By the sequential weak lower semi-continuity of the functional, we deduce

(2.23)

\begin{split}\big\|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|u_{\infty}-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}&\leq\liminf_{p_{j}\to\infty}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(u_{p})\\
&\leq\limsup_{p_{j}\to\infty}\mathrm{F}_{p,\epsilon,\bar{u}}(u_{p})\\
&\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(v)+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\|v-\bar{u}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)},\end{split}

for any
v\in\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By letting
q\to\infty
in inequality (
2.23
), the choice
v:=u_{\infty}
completes the proof of (
2.19
).

(iii) If we have
\epsilon>0
and
\bar{u}
minimises
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
over
\mathcal{E}_{\bar{u}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, choosing
v:=\bar{u}
in inequality (
2.23
) and letting
q\to\infty
yields
\bar{u}=u_{\infty}
.
∎

2.6.
A lemma of strong compactness in
\mathrm{L}^{1}

The following simple result, which is an extension of a lemma proved in the appendix of
[
33
]
, will be used in an essential fashion in Section
3
. (Let us recall here our notation
\tau_{z}
for the translation operator.)

Lemma 18
.

Let
\mathrm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a (strictly) positive matrix,
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\sigma\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
and
\Sigma\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
solves the PDE

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}f-\Sigma\big)+\sigma=0\ \ \text{ in }\big(\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\big)^{\!*},

namely in the sense of distributions. Then, for any
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega\in\mathrm{C}_{0^{+}}^{\nnearrow}[0,\infty)
(depending only on
n,\Omega^{\prime},\Omega,\mathrm{A}
), such that,

\|\tau_{z}f-f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,\leq\,\omega(|z|)\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),

for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|z|<\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
. Further, for any
q\in(1,n^{\prime})
, where
n^{\prime}=\frac{n}{n-1}
, there exists a constant
K=K(q,n,\Omega^{\prime},\Omega,A)>0
such that

\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq K\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).

As a consequence, any set of distributional solutions which is bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
, is also strongly precompact in
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, for all
q\in(1,n^{\prime})
.

Proof of Lemma

18
. Without loss of generality, by a change of variables argument as in Lemma
9
, we may assume that
\mathrm{A}
is the identity matrix, and therefore we have the Poisson equation
\Delta f=\mathrm{div}(\Sigma)-\sigma
in
\Omega
. Fix
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, and let fix also
\Omega^{\prime\prime},\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}\Subset\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}\Subset\Omega
. By the interior nature of the desired estimate and the linearity of the PDE, without loss of generality, a mollification argument allows us to assume that
u,\sigma\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}})
,
\Sigma\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}};\mathbb{R}^{n})

\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}
is piecewise
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}
and the PDE holds classically in
\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}
. Let
\zeta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a cut off function which satisfies that
\chi_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\leq\zeta\leq\chi_{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}}
. By setting
\bar{\sigma}:=\zeta\sigma
and
\bar{\Sigma}:=\zeta\Sigma
the PDE implies that
\Delta f=\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma})-\bar{\sigma}
in
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
, and we have that
\bar{\sigma}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,
\bar{\Sigma}\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and also
\bar{\sigma}|_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\equiv\sigma
and
\bar{\Sigma}|_{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}\equiv\Sigma
. Let now
R=R_{\Omega}>0
be the radius of the smallest open ball such that
\Omega-\Omega+\mathbb{B}_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{R}
. This means that
x-y+z\in\mathbb{B}_{R}
, for all
x,y\in\Omega
and all
z\in\mathbb{B}_{1}
. Let
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a cut off function such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
\mathbb{B}_{R}
. By Green’s formula (see e.g.
[
25
, Ch. 2]
), we can represent the solution
f
on
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
as

f=h-\Phi*\bar{\sigma}+\Phi*\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma}),

for some harmonic function
h
on
\Omega^{\prime\prime}
, where
\Phi
is the fundamental solution of the Laplace equation. Then, for any fixed
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|z|<\mathrm{dist}(\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega^{\prime\prime})
, we have the estimate

\begin{split}\big\|\tau_{z}f-f\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}&\leq\big\|\tau_{z}(\Phi*\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma}))-\Phi*\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma})\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\\
&\ \ \ \,+\big\|\tau_{z}(\Phi*\bar{\sigma})-\Phi*\bar{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}+\|\tau_{z}h-h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}.\end{split}

We will obtain the desired estimate by estimating each of the three terms in the above inequality separately. By the identities

\tau_{z}(\Phi*(\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma}))=\tau_{z}(\mathrm{D}\Phi*\bar{\Sigma})=(\tau_{z}\mathrm{D}\Phi)*\bar{\Sigma},

(where we interpret the convoluted inner product of vector functions in the above identities as
\mathrm{D}\Phi*\bar{\Sigma}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{D}_{i}\Phi*\bar{\Sigma}_{i}
), we estimate by using Young’s inequality

\begin{split}\Big\|\tau_{z}(\Phi*(\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma}))-\Phi*(\mathrm{div}(\bar{\Sigma}))\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,&=\,\Big\|\big(\tau_{z}(\mathrm{D}\Phi)-\mathrm{D}\Phi\big)*\bar{\Sigma}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\\
&=\,\Big\|\big[\big(\tau_{z}(\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi)-\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi\big)\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{R}}\big]*\bar{\Sigma}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq\,\big\|\tau_{z}(\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi)-\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}\|\bar{\Sigma}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\\
&\leq\,\big\|\tau_{z}(\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi)-\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\end{split}

Similarly, we have the estimate

\begin{split}\big\|\tau_{z}(\Phi*\bar{\sigma})-\Phi*\bar{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\,\leq\,\big\|\tau_{z}(\eta\Phi)-\eta\Phi\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Further, by the mean value theorem for harmonic functions and interior derivative estimates, we have

\|\tau_{z}h-h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq|z|\|\mathrm{D}h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})}\leq|z|\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.

Further, by using that
h=f+\Phi*\bar{\sigma}-\mathrm{D}\Phi*\bar{\Sigma}
, Young’s inequality implies

\begin{split}\|h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\,&\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|\Phi*\bar{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|\mathrm{D}\Phi*\bar{\Sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
&=\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|(\Phi\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{R}})*\bar{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|(\mathrm{D}\Phi\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{R}})*\bar{\Sigma}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\\
&\leq\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{D}\Phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.\end{split}

Therefore, we have

\|\tau_{z}h-h\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq|z|\big(1+\|\Phi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{B}_{R})}\big)\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big).

Finally, we conclude by putting the pieces together. Since
\eta\Phi\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and also
\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
, by the continuity of the translation operation, there exists
\omega\in C^{\nnearrow}_{0}[0,\infty)
such that

\big\|\tau_{z}(\eta\Phi)-\eta\Phi\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\big\|\tau_{z}(\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi)-\eta\mathrm{D}\Phi\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\omega(|z|).

By the above estimates, the desired
\mathrm{L}^{1}
equi-continuity estimate ensues. Now we establish the
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
bound. Fix
q\in(1,n^{\prime})
, and fixed also
\zeta\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with
\chi_{\Omega^{\prime}}\leq\zeta\leq\chi_{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}}
. Let
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)
be arbitrary, and test the PDE against
\zeta\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
, to obtain

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\zeta f(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}&=-\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}}f\mathrm{A}\!:\!\Big(\mathrm{D}\zeta\otimes\mathrm{D}\phi+\mathrm{D}\phi\otimes\mathrm{D}\zeta+\phi\mathrm{D}^{2}\zeta\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\ \ \ -\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}}\Sigma\cdot\Big(\phi\mathrm{D}\zeta+\zeta\mathrm{D}\phi\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}-\int_{\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}}\sigma\zeta\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.\end{split}

This identity implies the estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}\zeta f(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right|&\leq C(n)\big(|\mathrm{A}|+1\big)\|\zeta\|_{\mathrm{W}^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\\
&\ \ \ \cdot\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}({\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}})},\end{split}

for some universal
C=C(n)>0
. Fix now any
\psi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. By standard
\mathrm{L}^{p}
theory for elliptic equations and bootstrap regularity (see e.g.
[
25
, Ch. 6 & 9]
, there exists a unique
\phi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{C}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi=\psi
on
\Omega
, and also

\|\phi\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}({\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}})}\leq C\|\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}\phi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}({\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}})},

for some
C=C(q,n,\Omega)>0
, which is possible by Morrey’s embedding theorem because
q^{\prime}>n
(since by assumption
q\in(1,n^{\prime})
), and also
\Omega^{\prime\prime\prime}\Subset\Omega
has piecewise smooth boundary. Therefore, there exists a
K=K(q,n,\Omega^{\prime},\Omega,\mathrm{A})>0
such that

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\Omega}(\zeta f)\psi\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\right|&\leq K\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)},\end{split}

for any
\psi\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. By standard
\mathrm{L}^{p}
theory (see e.g.
[
35
, Ch. 14]
), this implies

\begin{split}\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega^{\prime})}\leq\|\zeta f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\leq K\Big(\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big),\end{split}

as claimed. The last claim of the statement regarding strong compactness follows by the Féchet-Kolmogorov and the Vitali convergence theorems. ∎

2.7.
Nodal sets and non-trivial solutions

The following result lists some properties of solutions to equations of the form (
1.9
), but with general
L^{\infty}
coefficients. It holds true much more generally, but we specialise the statement only to the case of interest in this paper.

Proposition 19
.

Let
\mathrm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a (strictly) positive matrix,
\Omega\Subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathrm{K}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{L}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Suppose that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
solves the PDE

(2.24)

\ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{L}f\big)+\mathrm{K}f=0\ \,\text{ in }\Omega,

in the sense of distributions, namely in
\big(\mathrm{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)\big)^{\!*}
. Then:

(i)
Any solution
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
to (
2.24
) satisfies
f\in{\mathrm{W}}^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, and thus (the precise representative of)
f
is in
\mathrm{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

(ii)
There exist a non-trivial solution
f^{*}\not\equiv 0
to (
2.24
).

(iii)
For any non-constant solution
f
to (
2.24
), the nodal set
\{f=0\}
is closed and countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable. Thus, the
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-Hausdorff measure of
\{f=0\}
is locally finite, and in particular it is an
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-nullset in
\Omega
.

Proof of Proposition

19
. (i) Since the coefficients
\mathrm{K},\mathrm{L}
of (
2.24
) are essentially bounded and
\mathrm{A}
is a constant positive matrix, by the result
[
11
, Corollary 1.2.8, p. 10]
of Bogachev-Krylov-Röckner, it follows that
f\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. Therefore, upon identifying
f
with its precise representative, we have
f\in\mathrm{C}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega)
for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, as a consequence of Morrey’s imbedding theorem. In particular,
\{f=0\}
is a relatively closed set in
\Omega
.

(ii) For any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, we define

\mathbb{B}_{\mu}[f,g]:=\int_{\Omega}\Big(\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{L}f\big)\cdot\mathrm{D}g+(\mu-\mathrm{K})fg\Big)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.

Then,
\mathbb{B}_{\mu}:\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous bilinear form, which is also coercive for all
\mu\geq\mu^{*}
, where the value
\mu^{*}>0
depends only on the coefficients. By the Lax-Milgram theorem, the operator
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}:\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
given by

\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}f:=\mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{L}f\big)+\mathrm{K}f-\mu f,

is a linear isomorphism. Therefore, the inverse
(\mathfrak{L}_{\mu})^{-1}:\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
is a compact operator, as a consequence of the compactness of the canonical inclusions
\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)\Subset\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
. By the Fredholm alternative, the equation

(2.25)

f+(\mu(\mathfrak{L}_{\mu})^{-1})f=(\mathfrak{L}_{\mu})^{-1}\phi

either it has a solution
f\not\equiv 0
when
\phi\equiv 0
, or else it is solvable for all
\phi\in\mathrm{W}^{-1,2}(\Omega)
. By noting that (
2.25
) can be rewritten as
\mathfrak{L}_{\mu}f=\phi-\mu f
, which is equivalent to
\mathfrak{L}_{0}f=\phi
, it follows that either
\mathfrak{L}_{0}f=0
has a solution
f^{*}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, or else the problem
\mathfrak{L}_{0}h=\mathrm{K}-\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{L}
has a solution
h\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. The latter is equivalent to the solvability of the boundary value problem
\mathfrak{L}_{0}f=0
in
\Omega
with
f=1
on
\partial\Omega
by some
f^{*}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
. In either case, it follows that (
2.24
) has a non-trivial solution
f^{*}\not\equiv 0
.

(iii) We begin by considering the auxiliary boundary value problem

\mathrm{div}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi)=\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{L}\,\text{ on }\Omega,\ \ \ \psi=0\,\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

By the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique solution
\psi\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, which by a result of Di Fazio
[
21
, Th. 3.1]
, it satisfies
\psi\in\mathrm{W}^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
. By Morrey’s theorem, (the precise representative of)
\psi
is in
\mathrm{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. We now set
\phi:=e^{-\psi}f
, where
f\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
is any given non-constant solution to (
2.24
). By part (i), all three function
\phi,\psi,f
lie in
\mathrm{W}^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, whilst
\mathrm{L}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Therefore, by using that
\mathrm{div}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L})=0
in
\Omega
, we may rigorously compute

\begin{split}0&=\mathrm{div}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}f-\mathrm{L}f\big)+\mathrm{K}f\\
&=\mathrm{div}\Big(e^{\psi}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\phi+e^{\psi}\phi\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big)\Big)+\mathrm{K}e^{\psi}\phi\\
&=\mathrm{div}\big((e^{\psi}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{D}\phi\big)+\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big)\cdot[e^{\psi}\mathrm{D}\phi+e^{\psi}\phi\mathrm{D}\psi]+(e^{\psi}\mathrm{K})\phi,\end{split}

in
\Omega
. Hence, the function
\phi
is a weak solution to the divergence PDE

\mathrm{div}\big((e^{\psi}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{D}\phi\big)+\big[e^{\psi}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big)\big]\cdot\mathrm{D}\phi+\big[e^{\psi}\big(\!\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big)\cdot\mathrm{D}\psi+\mathrm{K}\big)\big]\phi=0\ \text{ in }\Omega.

By the previous, the coefficients satisfy

e^{\psi}\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big),\ e^{\psi}\big(\!\big(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{D}\psi-\mathrm{L}\big)\cdot\mathrm{D}\psi+\mathrm{K}\big)\,\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\ \ e^{\psi}\mathrm{A}\in\mathrm{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}),

for all
p\in(1,\infty)
, and all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. By the result
[
26
, Th. 1.7 & Rem. 1.8(3)]
of Hardt and Simon, it follows that for any point
\bar{x}\in\Omega
at which
\phi
has finite order of vanishing, the set
\{\phi=0\}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\rho}(\bar{x})
is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable, for all sufficiently small
\rho>0
. Since

, and since

is countably

-rectifiable, and in particular it is a Lebesgue nullset. The proposition ensues. ∎

\mathrm{K}_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\mathrm{K}_{\infty}
in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)
and also
\mathrm{L}_{p}\,-\!\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
in
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as
p_{j}\to\infty
, for all
q\in(1,\infty)
, the Vitali convergence theorem yields that the modes of convergence in (
3.31
) are valid in
\mathrm{L}^{q}\big(\Omega\setminus\{f_{\infty}=0\}\big)
, for all
q\in(1,\infty)
. Further, by the uniqueness of limits, we infer that
\mathrm{K}_{\infty},\mathrm{L}_{\infty}
are characterised as

(3.32)

\left.\begin{split}\mathrm{K}_{\infty}&=\frac{\partial_{\eta}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})}{\partial_{\xi}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})}\ \\
\mathrm{L}_{\infty}&=\frac{\partial_{\mathrm{p}}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})}{\partial_{\xi}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{\infty})}\end{split}\right\}\ \text{ a.e.\ on }\Omega\setminus\{f_{\infty}=0\}.

In view (
3.32
) and (
3.24
), it follows that the PDE (
1.9
) is indeed satisfied by the pair
(u_{\infty},f_{\infty})
. By Proposition (
19
) applied to (
1.9
), we have that
f_{\infty}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
for all
q\in(1,\infty)
, and
\{f_{\infty}=0\}
is a countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable subset of
\Omega
. In particular,
\{f_{\infty}=0\}
is a Lebesgue nullset. We may further assume that
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})>0
. If instead
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})=0
, then (
1.8
) is trivially satisfied a.e. on
\Omega
for any
f_{\infty}
, whilst (
1.9
) is also satisfied by choosing any non-trivial solution
f_{\infty}\neq 0
, guaranteed to exist by Proposition (
19
). The proofs of Theorems
4
and
7
will therefore be completed once we prove that
f_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
. The remainder of the proof is therefore devoted to showing
f_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
, by using assumption (
1.17
). (This is the only point where the
\mathrm{C}^{2}
regularity assumption for
u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
is utilised.) As a first step, we establish the estimate

(3.33)

F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\partial_{\xi}F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\Big(\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{p}-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big)\geq\Big(\frac{c}{C_{\infty}}\Big)^{\!2}\big(F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\big)^{2},

a.e. on
\Omega
, for all sufficiently large
p\geq p_{0}
, where

C_{\infty}:=C\big(1+\|u_{\infty}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\big).

To establish (
3.33
), we use (
1.7
) and (
1.16
) to estimate as follows:

\begin{split}F(x,\eta,\xi,\mathrm{p})\big(\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)&=\Big[F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)-F\big(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)\Big]\big(\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)\\
&=\bigg(\int_{\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})}^{\xi}\partial_{\xi}F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\cdot)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{1}\!\bigg)\big(\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)\\
&\geq c\big|\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big|^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{c}{C(|\eta|+|\mathrm{p}|)^{2}}\big(F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\big)^{2},\end{split}

which, again by (
1.7
), yields

(3.34)

F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\partial_{\xi}F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\big(\xi-\bar{\xi}(x,\eta,\mathrm{p})\big)\geq\frac{c^{2}\big(F(x,\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\big)^{2}}{C(|\eta|+|\mathrm{p}|)^{2}},

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and all
(\eta,\mathrm{p},\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Inequality (
3.34
) readily implies (
3.33
). Next, we establish the following inequality

(3.35)

\left\{\ \ \ \begin{split}e_{p}\Big(\frac{c}{C_{\infty}}\Big)^{\!2}&\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{p}\Big[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\ \ \ +\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{p}\Big[\mathrm{K}_{p}(w-u_{p})-\mathrm{L}_{p}\cdot\mathrm{D}(w-u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\ \ \ +\|w-u_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\|u_{\infty}-u_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)},\phantom{\Big[}\end{split}\right.

for any fixed
w\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
, where
e_{p}
is given by (
1.22
), and satisfies (
3.21
) as
j\to\infty
along a subsequence
(p_{j})_{1}^{\infty}
. To this aim, let
w\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
be fixed. Since
w-u_{p}\in\mathrm{W}^{2,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by testing this in the weak formulation of (
3.19
) and employing (
3.33
) and (
1.23
), we have

\begin{split}\ \ \ &{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,\Big[\Big(\mathrm{K}_{p}f_{p}+(u_{p}-u_{\infty})\Big)(w-u_{p})-(\mathrm{L}_{p}f_{p})\cdot\mathrm{D}(w-u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\ +{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{p}\Big[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}w-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}=\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{p}\Big[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{p}-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\\
&\hskip 165.0pt\geq\,e_{p}^{1-p}\Big(\frac{c}{C_{\infty}}\Big)^{\!2}{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,\big(F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u_{p})\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}.\end{split}

The above inequality readily implies (
3.35
).

By noting that assumption (
1.17
) requires that
u_{0}|_{\partial\Omega}
is
\mathrm{C}^{2}
on the closed set
\partial\Omega
, Whitney’s extension theorem
[
51
, Th. I, p. 65]
, yields the existence of some
u_{*}\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
u_{0}=u_{*}
on
\partial\Omega
, and also
\mathrm{D}u_{0}=\mathrm{D}u_{*}
on
\partial\Omega
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, we set
\Omega^{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\cap\{\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)<\varepsilon\}
. Since
\|u_{*}-u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{C}^{1}(\Omega^{\varepsilon})}\longrightarrow 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, by using a standard regularisation argument involving partitions of unity, we can construct a function
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying that
u_{\varepsilon}\equiv u_{*}
in a inner neighbourhood of the boundary, and
u_{\varepsilon}
equals the standard mollification of
u_{\infty}
(for small enough regularisation parameter), on the complement of such an inner neighbourhood. Then, by construction
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
u_{\infty}=u_{\varepsilon}
on
\partial\Omega
and also
\mathrm{D}u_{\infty}=\mathrm{D}u_{\varepsilon}
on
\partial\Omega
, with the additional property
\|u_{\infty}-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\longrightarrow 0
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
. By recalling that
\{\mathrm{K}_{p}f_{p}\}_{p>p_{0}}
and
\{|\mathrm{L}_{p}f_{p}|\}_{p>p_{0}}
are bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
, the choice
w:=u_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.35
) yields

\begin{split}e_{p}\Big(\frac{c}{C_{\infty}}\Big)^{\!2}&\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{p}\Big[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \\
&\ \ \ +\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{p}\|_{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\Big[\|u_{\infty}-u_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{K}_{p}f_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\mathrm{L}_{p}f_{p}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\Big].\end{split}

By letting
p\to\infty
along a subsequence in the above inequality, we may use that
f_{p}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega}\overset{\,*_{\phantom{|}}}{{\smash{\,-\!\!\!\!-\!\!\!\!\rightharpoonup}}\,}f_{\infty}\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{\LARGE$\llcorner$}_{\Omega}
in
\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega})\cong(\mathrm{C}(\overline{\Omega}))^{*}
(recall Claim
20
), and the fact that
\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{C}(\overline{\Omega})
and that
\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{p},\mathrm{D}u_{p})\longrightarrow\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{\infty},\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
as
p\to\infty
along a subsequence, to deduce the inequality

\begin{split}\ \ \ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})\Big(\frac{c}{C_{\infty}}\Big)^{\!2}-o(1)\leq\,{-\hskip-10.5pt\int_{\Omega}}\,f_{\infty}\Big[\mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}-\bar{\xi}(\cdot,u_{\infty},\mathrm{D}u_{\infty})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{n},\ \ \text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.\end{split}

Since by assumption
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})>0
, if
\varepsilon>0
is chosen small enough, the left hand side in the above inequality becomes positive, and this implies
f_{\infty}\not\equiv 0
. In conclusion, the proof of Theorems
4
and
7
is complete.
∎

We now conclude by establishing Theorem
5
.

Proof of Theorem

5
. (I) Let
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
be a narrow minimiser of the functional
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
. By Definition
1
, there exists
\rho>0
, such that

\ \ \ \ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})\leq\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}+\psi),\ \ \ \ \psi\in\smash{\mathbb{B}_{\rho}^{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)}.

By Theorem
4
, it follows that there exists
f_{\infty}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, such that the pair
(u_{\infty},f_{\infty})
satisfies the system of equations (
1.8
)-(
1.9
). By Theorem
3
, it follows that
u_{\infty}
is actually a
strict
narrow minimiser, therefore there exists
\rho^{\prime}>0
such that

\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})<\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty}+\psi),\ \ \ \ \psi\in\smash{\big(\mathbb{B}_{\rho^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{0}(\Omega)\big)}\setminus\{0\}.

Hence,
u_{\infty}
is the unique narrow minimiser of the functional
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
(at the same energy level) within in the admissible class

\mathbb{B}_{\min\{\rho,\rho^{\prime}\}}^{\mathrm{W}^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}(u_{\infty})\cap\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega).

(II) Let
u_{\infty}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
be a global minimiser of the functional
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
(Definition
1
). By Theorem
4
, it follows that there exists
f_{\infty}\in\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
, such that the pair
(u_{\infty},f_{\infty})
satisfies the system of equations (
1.8
)-(
1.9
). By Theorem
3
, it follows that
u_{\infty}
is a strict global minimiser (Definition
1
). In conclusion,
u_{\infty}
is the unique global minimiser of the functional
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
in the space
\mathcal{W}^{2,\infty}_{u_{0}}(\Omega)
. ∎

Proof of Corollary

6
. Let
\mathrm{J}^{1}u(x):=\big(x,u(x),\mathrm{D}u(x)\big)
denote the first order jet operator. Let us also set
\Omega^{*}:=\Omega\setminus\{f_{\infty}=0\}
. Since
\{f_{\infty}=0\}
is nowhere dense,
\Omega^{*}
is an open dense set in
\Omega
. By equation (
1.8
) and assumption (
1.7
), we have

\ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{A}\!:\!\mathrm{D}^{2}u_{\infty}=F(\mathrm{J}^{1}u_{\infty},\cdot)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u_{\infty})\sgn(f_{\infty})\big),\ \ \text{a.e.\ on }\Omega^{*}.

Since
u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\Omega)
for all
\gamma\in(0,1)
, the right hand side is in
\mathrm{C}^{0,\gamma}(\Omega^{*})
. By the Schauder interior estimates (see e.g.
[
25
, Ch. 6]
), we have that
u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{C}^{2,\gamma}(\Omega^{*})
. Hence, since
F
is by assumption
\mathrm{C}^{1}
, the right hand side is in
\mathrm{C}^{1,\gamma}(\Omega^{*})
, which implies
u_{\infty}\in\mathrm{C}^{3,\gamma}(\Omega^{*})
. The conclusion for general
k\geq 2
is a consequence of a standard bootstrap argument and finite induction. Finally, by Proposition
19
, the nodal set
\{f_{\infty}=0\}
is countably
(n-1)
-rectifiable. ∎

Proof of Corollary

8
. Since any increasing
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
which satisfies
\Phi(-\,\cdot)=-\Phi
commutes with the absolute value and the (essential) supremum, we have

\bar{\mathrm{F}}_{\infty}(u)=\underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\hskip 1.0pt\big|\Phi\big(F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u)\big)\big|=\Phi\Big(\underset{\Omega}{\mathrm{ess}\sup}\hskip 1.0pt|F(\mathrm{J}^{2}u)|\Big)=\Phi(\mathrm{F}_{\infty}(u)),

yielding that
\mathrm{F}_{\infty}
and
\bar{\mathrm{F}}_{\infty}
have the same sets of global/narrow/local minimisers.
∎

1.
Introduction

This paper studies the interaction of two fundamental objects (or classes of objects) in time-frequency analysis: the metaplectic group on the one hand, and the scale of modulation spaces
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
on the other. The metaplectic group can be understood as the fundamental symmetry group in time-frequency analysis; see
[
12
,
17
]
for more details concerning the metaplectic group and its role in time-frequency analysis and the representation theory of the Heisenberg group. The group is often employed to reduce the study of general cases to more specific, concrete settings; recent applications of this principle can be found in
[
15
,
16
,
20
]
as recent illustrations of this principle.
Modulation spaces, on the other hand, are spaces consisting of signals (distributions) whose windowed Fourier transform exhibits a certain decay, as quantified by a weighted mixed
L^{p}
-norm. We refer to
[
6
,
17
]
for more background on modulation spaces.

We are interested in
invariance properties
, asking under which conditions an operator
\widehat{S}
belonging to the metaplectic group, initially defined as a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, extends to a bounded operator
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The question was first raised in
[
4
]
, where the invariance of
\mathrm{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under arbitrary metaplectic operators was instrumental in establishing generalized Strichartz estimates. For a subclass of metaplectic operators which are also Fourier integral operators, sufficient conditions and a counterexample hinting at necessary conditions were already established in
[
5
, Sec. 7]
. Given the relevance both of metaplectic operators and modulation spaces to the field of time-frequency analysis, understanding their interplay can be regarded as a fundamental question of independent interest. As the subsequent results and proofs show, the question turns out to be rather more nuanced than what the already understood special case
p=q
and
m\equiv 1
, considered in
[
4
]
, suggests.

1.1.
Contributions

In this subsection, we provide a brief overview of the main results of the article. For definitions of the various objects in the following theorems, we refer to the subsequent sections and the background material contained in the textbooks
[
6
,
17
]
.

Recall that the metaplectic group is the double cover of the symplectic group, i.e., there exists a surjective Lie group homomorphism

\pi^{\mathrm{Mp}}:\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
.
Our main new result, restated below as ?THM?
, is a characterisation of triples
(p,q,\widehat{S})
of exponents
p,\,q\in[1,\infty]
and metaplectic operators
\widehat{S}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
such that

(1.1)

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is well-defined.

Theorem
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\widehat{S}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
be given. The following statements are equivalent:

(1)

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well-defined.

(2)

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is well-defined and bounded.

(3)

One of the following conditions holds:

(i)

p=q
, or

(ii)

p\neq q
and the projection
\pi^{\mathrm{Mp}}(\widehat{S})=S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
is an upper block triangular matrix.

This can be lifted to weighted modulation spaces if the metaplectic operator is compatible with the weight function (see ?THM?
).

Theorem
.

Let
m
be a moderate polynomially bounded weight,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. If
\widehat{S}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
with projection
\pi^{\tiny Mp}(\widehat{S})=S
satisfies
m\asymp m\circ S^{-1}
, then
\widehat{S}
is a bounded operator from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if it is a bounded operator from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Outline

The paper is structured as follows. The
second section
covers all necessary notions related to modulation spaces and metaplectic operators. The
third section
contains the proof of the classification for unweighted modulation spaces. The
last section
is dedicated to lifting the result from the unweighted to the weighted setting. Two appendices contain somewhat lengthy computations of certain ambiguity functions and their dilates.

2.
Basic definitions and notation

We begin with a short introduction to time-frequency analysis. The
translation
and the
modulation operator
(
time shift and frequency shift
) are denoted with
T_{x}f(t)=f(t-x)
and
M_{\omega}f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot t}f(t)
, respectively. We will make use of the unitary Fourier transform

(2.1)

\mathcal{F}f(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot x}dx,\qquad\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

pointwise defined on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and continuously extended by duality on
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Both types of shifts are unitary representations of the abelian group
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so they commute within their class of shifts. However, this does not transfer to their combinations, as we obtain a non-trivial phase factor

(2.2)

M_{\omega}T_{x}=e^{2\pi i\omega\cdot x}T_{x}M_{\omega},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

A recurring theme in this paper are intertwining properties. The Fourier transform can be seen as the elementary example with
\mathcal{F}T_{x}=M_{-x}\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}M_{\omega}=T_{\omega}\mathcal{F}
, which justifies the name
frequency shift
for the modulations.

The cross-ambiguity function associated with
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the map

(2.3)

\begin{split}\mathrm{A}(f,g)(x,\omega)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(t+\frac{x}{2}\right)\overline{g\left(t-\frac{x}{2}\right)}e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,dt=\left\langle f,T_{x/2}M_{\omega}T_{x/2}g\right\rangle.\end{split}

The latter equality allows us to extend the definition of the cross-ambiguity function for any
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It corresponds to the symmetric time-frequency shift

(2.4)

\rho(\lambda)=T_{x/2}M_{\omega}T_{x/2}=M_{\omega/2}T_{x}M_{\omega/2},\qquad\lambda=\left(x\,,\,\omega\right)^{t},

but each parameterization with
\tau\in[0,1]

(2.5)

\rho_{\tau}(x,\omega)\coloneqq T_{\tau x}M_{\omega}T_{(1-\tau)x}=e^{2\pi i(1-2\tau)\omega\cdot x}M_{\tau\omega}T_{x}M_{(1-\tau)\omega}

induces a sesqui-linear time-frequency representation

(2.6)

\left\langle f,\rho_{\tau}(x,\omega)g\right\rangle=e^{2\pi i\tau\omega\cdot x-\pi i\omega\cdot x}\left\langle f,\rho_{1/2}(x,\omega)g\right\rangle=e^{2\pi i\tau\omega\cdot x-\pi i\omega\cdot x}\mathrm{A}(f,g)(x,\omega).

Taking
\tau=1/2
results in the symmetric time-frequency shift. As another prominent choice, the completely polarized version
\tau=0
reproduces the
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT)

(2.7)

V_{g}f(x,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(t\right)\overline{g\left(t-x\right)}e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,dt=\left\langle f,M_{\omega}T_{x}g\right\rangle.

A further closely related transform is the
cross-Wigner distribution

\mathrm{W}(f,g)(x,\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\left(x+\tfrac{t}{2}\right)\overline{g\left(x-\tfrac{t}{2}\right)}e^{-2\pi i\omega\cdot t}\,dt=2^{d}\mathrm{A}(f,g(-\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}}))(2x,2\omega),

which is often studied in the context of quantum mechanics
[
7
]
.

The relation (
2.2
) does not allow for
\rho_{\tau}(\lambda+\nu)=\rho_{\tau}(\lambda)\rho_{\tau}(\nu)
to hold for arbitrary
\lambda,\,\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, but the appearing phase factor induces a corresponding Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}
. Topologically, the Heisenberg group can be identified with
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
. However, this does not hold on the group level, as the multiplication is non-commuting, chosen in such a way that
(\lambda,t)\mapsto e^{2\pi it}\rho_{\tau}(\lambda)
is a unitary representation on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This allows for methods from representation theory and gives rise to a non-trivial family of special intertwining operators, the metaplectic operators, which will later be addressed in depth. For further exploration of these parameterized representations, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
21
]
.

Throughout this paper,
\left\lVert\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}}\right\rVert_{p}\coloneqq\left\lVert\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, denotes the
p
-norm on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\in[1,\infty]
. We consider modulation spaces associated to mixed-norm weighted
L^{p}
-spaces, more formally defined as the spaces
L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
with the norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{p,q,m}\coloneqq\left\lVert\omega\mapsto\left\lVert f(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\omega)\,m(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\omega)\right\rVert_{p}\right\rVert_{q}.

Here
m:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to(0,\infty)
is a weight that is
v
-moderate
, i.e., there is a
C>0
with

m(x+y)\leq C\,v(x)\cdot m(y),\qquad x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}

for some weight
v
. Throughout this paper we reserve the term
moderate weight
to even weights whose controlling weight can be chosen as
v(x)=(1+\|x\|)^{N}
, for a suitable exponent
N>0
. Note that this implies
m(x)\leq C^{\prime}(1+\|x\|)^{N}
, i.e., a moderate weight
m
is itself polynomially bounded. The control weight
v
is
submultiplicative
, which amounts to the estimate

(2.8)

v(x+y)\leq C\,v(x)\cdot v(y),\qquad x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}.

As a consequence of submultiplicativity,
L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
turns out to be invariant under translations
[
17
, Proposition 11.1.2.]
, which is fundamental for the well-definedness of the associated modulation spaces.

Mixed-norm Lebesgue spaces were systematically explored in
[
1
]
. Most properties, such as completeness,
duality and interpolation relations hold in an analogous fashion to the versions of the standard Lebesgue spaces.
The modulation spaces associated to
L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
are then given by

\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mid\mathrm{A}(f,g)\in L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\right\},

where
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\{0\}
is fixed. It is well-known that
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is independent of
g
, and that the associated norm

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneqq\left\lVert\mathrm{A}(f,g)\right\rVert_{p,q,m}

is independent up to equivalence. Traditionally, the modulation spaces are defined using the short-time Fourier transform, but the phase factor in (
2.6
) has no impact on the definition or the induced norms. Due to the algebraic relation to the ambiguity function, the cross-Wigner distribution can be used as well. Using the ambiguity function instead of the windowed Fourier transform will make the interaction with the metaplectic action somewhat easier, specifically in the connection with twisted convolution in the proof of ?THM?
. In the spirit of the STFT, we will refer to
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
the window function
of
\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},g)
.

Modulation spaces are Banach spaces, with natural, continuous embeddings as closed subspaces of
L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. The reconstruction formula is given by

(2.9)

\left\langle g,\gamma\right\rangle f=\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},g)^{*}\,\mathrm{A}(f,\gamma),

where the adjoint acts (in the weak sense) as

(2.10)

\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},g)^{*}F=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(x,\omega)\,T_{x/2}M_{\omega}T_{x/2}g\ d(x,\omega).

The following result was first noted in
[
11
]
. See also
[
6
, Section 2.3]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p,q,r,s\in[1,\infty]
. The following relations hold:

(a)

\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathrm{M}^{r,s}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
p\leq r
and
q\leq s
.

(b)

(\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\prime}=\mathrm{M}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{1/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, if
1\leq p,q<\infty
, with

(2.11)

\left\lvert\left\langle f,g\right\rangle\right\rvert\lesssim\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{1/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
are the Hölder conjugates of
p
and
q
, respectively.

(c)

If the weight function is polynomially bounded,

(2.12)

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathrm{M}^{1}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathrm{M}^{\infty}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For a more detailed overview of modulation spaces, we refer to
[
6
,
8
,
13
,
17
]
.

2.1.
The symplectic group and the metaplectic group

Both time and frequency shifts are representations of the abelian group
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, so they commute within their class, but symmetric time-frequency shifts do not satisfy
\rho(\lambda)\rho(\nu)=\rho(\lambda+\nu)
for arbitrary
\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
.

In order to describe the symplectic group, we first introduce the
standard symplectic form
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
, which is the bilinear map
[\cdot,\cdot]:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R}^{2d}
given via

[(x,\omega),(x^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})]=x^{\prime}\cdot\omega-x\cdot\omega^{\prime}~,

where
x,x^{\prime},\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The standard symplectic matrix

\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
-I_{d}&0\end{pmatrix},

with
I_{d}
denoting the
d
-dimensional identity matrix,
allows to express this bilinear form as
[\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}]=\lambda\cdot\mathcal{J}\lambda^{\prime}
. Now the symplectic group
\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})\leq\mathrm{SL}(2d,\mathbb{R})
consists of the matrices
S\in\mathrm{GL}(2d,\mathbb{R})
preserving the commutator relation, i.e., they satisfy

(2.13)

[\rho(S\lambda),\rho(S\nu)]_{-}=[\rho(\lambda),\rho(\nu)]_{-},\qquad\lambda,\nu\in\mathbb{R}^{2d},

where
[\cdot,\cdot]_{-}
denotes the commutator of linear operators.
One easily verifies that this condition is equivalent to the equation
S^{T}\mathcal{J}S=\mathcal{J}
. These characterizations readily imply that the symplectic group is indeed a closed subgroup of the full matrix group
\mathrm{GL}(2d,\mathbb{R})
.

Due to the block structure of
\mathcal{J}
, symplectic matrices are often represented in block form as well. The following examples of symplectic matrices will turn out to be important building blocks of the symplectic group:
let
P,\,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be symmetric and
L\in\mathrm{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
be invertible. The following matrices are in
\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
:

(2.14)

U_{P}=\begin{pmatrix}I&P\\
0&I\end{pmatrix},\quad V_{Q}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
Q&I\end{pmatrix},\quad D_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}L&0\\
0&L^{-T}\end{pmatrix}.

The first
d
elementary symplectic quasi-permutation matrices
\Pi_{i}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
,
\ 1\leq i\leq d
, are given by

(2.15)

\Pi_{i}\ e_{j}=\begin{cases}e_{j}\ ,&\;1\leq j\leq 2d,j\neq i,j\neq i+d,\\
-e_{i+d}\ ,&\;j=i,\\
e_{i}\ ,&\;j=i+d.\end{cases}

We observe that each
\Pi_{i}
acts via a combination of a permutation (in fact, a transposition) and a sign change.
Notice that we can represent the standard symplectic matrix as

(2.16)

\mathcal{J}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}\Pi_{i}.

For all
1\leq i\leq d
holds

(2.17)

\Pi_{i+d}\coloneqq\Pi_{i}^{T}=\Pi_{i}^{-1}\;,\quad\Pi_{i+d}\ e_{j}=\begin{cases}e_{j}\ ,&\;1\leq j\leq 2d,j\neq i,j\neq i+d,\\
e_{i+d}\ ,&\;j=i,\\
-e_{i}\ ,&\;j=i+d.\end{cases}

Moreover, all
\Pi_{i}
commute, i.e.
\Pi_{i}\Pi_{j}=\Pi_{j}\Pi_{i}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq 2d
.
These symplectic matrices are significant not only due to the clean definition but also for their generating properties.

Proposition 2.2

(Factorization in
\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
,
[
9
]
)
.

For all
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
there is an index set
J\subseteq\{1,...,2d\}
, symmetric matrices
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and an invertible matrix
L\in\mathrm{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
with

(2.18)

S=\prod\limits_{i\in J}\Pi_{i}\;V_{Q}D_{L}U_{P}.

The symplectic group can also be understood in terms of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
. More precisely, it can be equivalently defined as the group of linear automorphisms
\mathcal{A}
on
\left(\mathbb{R}^{2d},+\right)
which extend to an automorphism
(\lambda,t)\mapsto(\mathcal{A}t,t)
of the Heisenberg group
[
12
]
.
The property (
2.13
) and methods from representation theory, in particular Stone-von Neumann’s theorem, imply the existence of unitary operators
\widehat{S}

with

(2.19)

\rho(S\lambda)=\widehat{S}\rho(\lambda)\widehat{S}^{-1},

that is,
\widehat{S}
arises as an intertwining operator. They generate a group
G
with the composition as the group multiplication. However, any multiple
\tau\widehat{S}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{T}
, is an intertwining operator, implying a high redundancy of the group action of
G
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Nevertheless, there exists a subgroup of
G
such that for all
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
the said subgroup contains exactly two corresponding operators
\widehat{S}
.
The group is called the
metaplectic group
and is denoted by
\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
. It is an explicit realization of the double cover of
\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
and the projection

\pi^{\mathrm{Mp}}:\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})

is a group homomorphism, with kernel
\mathrm{ker}(\pi^{\mathrm{Mp}})=\left\{\mathcal{\mathrm{id},-\mathrm{id}}\right\}
. The metaplectic operators are automorphisms on the Schwartz space, and can therefore be extended by duality to
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

[
12
, Proposition 4.27]
.
Standard examples of metaplectic operators are the unitary dilations
\mathcal{D}_{L}f(t)=\left\lvert\det{L}\right\rvert^{-1}f(L^{-1}t)
, the linear chirps
\mathcal{V}_{Q}f(t)=e^{\pi it\cdot Qt}f(t)
and the Fourier transform. Indeed,

(2.20)

\begin{split}\rho(\mathcal{J}\lambda)&=\mathcal{F}\,\rho(\lambda)\,\mathcal{F}^{-1},\\
\rho(V_{Q}\lambda)&=\mathcal{V}_{Q}\,\rho(\lambda)\,\mathcal{V}_{Q}^{-1},\\
\rho(D_{L}\lambda)&=\mathcal{D}_{L}\,\rho(\lambda)\,\mathcal{D}_{L}^{-1},\\
\rho(U_{P}\lambda)&=\left(\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{V}_{-P}\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\right)\rho(\lambda)\left(\mathcal{F}\,\mathcal{V}_{-P}\,\mathcal{F}^{-1}\right)^{-1}.\end{split}

A slightly more explicit description is based on quadratic Fourier transforms. Detailed constructions can be found in
[
17
, Chapter 9]
,
[
12
, Chapter 4]
and
[
8
, Chapter 7]
.
The connection between the ambiguity function and the metaplectic operators is best summarized by the symplectic covariance of the ambiguity function:

(2.21)

\mathrm{A}(f,g)\circ S^{-1}\ (\lambda)=\mathrm{A}\left(\widehat{S}f,\widehat{S}g\right)(\lambda).

Rewriting this equation slightly as

A(\widehat{S}f,g)(\lambda)=A(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}(g))\circ S^{-1}(\lambda)

shows that the effect of
\widehat{S}
on the ambiguity function amounts to exchanging the window (which induces a well-understood equivalence of modulation space norms, since the window class is invariant under the metaplectic action), and the composition operator obtained by letting
S^{-1}
act on the input variables of the ambiguity function. Hence the focus of the following section will be on the latter.

3.
The metaplectic action on
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
and on
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

In this section we fully characterize the metaplectic operators leaving invariant the unweighted modulation spaces
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This problem turns out to be rather closely related to a purely measure-theoretic question, namely invariance of the associated mixed unweighted spaces
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
under symplectic changes of variable. Before we explain this aspect of our strategy in more detail, we introduce some notation concerning dilation operators and their domains and make some fundamental observations concerning the boundedness of such operators.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}
be an invertible matrix and
V\subseteq L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
a closed subspace. Assume

(3.1)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:V\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}),\quad f\mapsto f\circ S^{-1}

is well-defined. Then
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is bounded.

Proof.

\mathcal{D}_{S}
is obviously a linear operator. We will show that it is also closed. To that end, let
(f_{n},\mathcal{D}_{S}f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq V\times L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be an arbitrary Cauchy sequence. Due to the completeness of
V\times L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, the sequence has a limit
(f,g)
. The convergence in the product space means

(3.2)

\left\lVert f_{n}-f\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}}\to 0\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\left\lVert\mathcal{D}_{S}f_{n}-g\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}}\to 0.

By the theorem of Riesz-Fischer, there is a subsequence
(f_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
f_{n_{k}}(x)\to f(x)
almost everywhere. Hence we get
\mathcal{D}_{S}f_{n_{k}}(x)\to f\circ S^{-1}(x)
almost everywhere. On the other side,

(3.3)

\left\lVert\mathcal{D}_{S}f_{n_{k}}-g\right\rVert_{{p,q}}\to 0

and we can choose a subsequence
(f_{n_{k_{m}}})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\mathcal{D}_{S}f_{n_{k_{m}}}\to g\
almost everywhere. This implies
g=f\circ S^{-1}
almost everywhere, which proves the claim. Since both
V
and
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
are Banach spaces, the closed graph theorem implies that
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is bounded.
∎

In the subsequent applications of this result, the closed subspace

L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\widehat{S}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})

(a)

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(b)

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(c)

One of the following conditions holds:

(i)

, or

(ii)

p\neq q
and the projection

This is a direct consequence of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
and
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
be given. The following statements are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is everywhere defined.

(b)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(c)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\widehat{S}^{-1}g)(\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is everywhere defined.

(d)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\widehat{S}^{-1}g)(\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(e)

One of the following conditions holds:

(i)

p=q
, or

(ii)

p\neq q
and
S
is an upper block triangular matrix.

Let us first comment on the implications that are obvious by now: The equivalences (a)
\Leftrightarrow
(b) and (c)
\Leftrightarrow
(d) are provided by Proposition
. The implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(d) follows from the commutative diagram. Note that the converse (d)
\Rightarrow
(b) appears somewhat unexpected at first. Given a densely defined closed operator

on a general Banach space, and a closed subspace

, there is no reason to expect that boundedness of the restriction

implies boundedness of

itself, and counterexamples to this expectation are easily constructed. Nonetheless, in the setting studied here, the implication holds.

Of the remaining implications required to complete the proof of the theorem, (e)
\Rightarrow
(a) will turn out to be a fairly standard application of the linear change of variable formula; see the proof of ?THM?
below. Hence the main challenge in the following will be the proof of the implication (d)
\Rightarrow
(e). Here we will rely on the factorization of arbitrary symplectic matrices provided by ?THM?
.

But first, we deal with the case
p=q
. Since

, this case is immediate by the standard change of variables formula for integrals. The only fact about symplectic matrices used here is the fact that their determinant equals

.

Theorem 3.4
.

The mapping
\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is everywhere defined and norm-preserving for all
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
and all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

As a consequence of this observation and the already established implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(c), we get the boundedness of
\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for any symplectic matrix
S
. This was noted in
[
4
]
but was probably known prior to that.

From now on, let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
be distinct. In this case, condition (e) of ?THM?
contains restrictions on the matrix
S
, which arise from the fact that the definition of the mixed
L^{p,q}
-norm treats the integration variables
x
and
\omega
in an asymmetric manner. Hence any change of variables that interacts with this asymmetry (”mixes” the
x
- and
\omega
-variables) can be expected to be problematic, i.e., potentially lead to unbounded operators. The challenge in the proof of the remaining direction lies in making this intuition precise.

The following, somewhat extreme example, based on interchanging the variables
x,\omega
, gives a hint why the intuition is correct. Pick radially symmetric functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
g\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The tensor product
(x,\omega)\mapsto f\otimes g(x,\omega)=f(x)g(\omega)
is in
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, but
g\otimes f=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{J}}f\otimes g\notin L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, so intuitively, mixing of the variables should be held under control as much as possible. The example shows that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{J}}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is not well defined.

The following result establishes the implication (e)
\Rightarrow
(a) of ?THM?
for the case
p\not=q
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A,D\in\mathrm{GL}(d,\mathbb{R})
and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be given. Denote
S\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
0&D\end{pmatrix}
, then

\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})

is, up to a constant
C_{S}
, a norm-preserving isomorphism with a bounded inverse
\mathcal{D}_{S}^{-1}=\mathcal{D}_{S^{-1}}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be given.

The inverse of
S
is

S^{-1}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}A^{-1}&-A^{-1}BD^{-1}\\
0&D^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Due to the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure, it holds

(3.5)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\mathcal{D}_{S}f\right\rVert_{{p,q}}{=}&\ \left\lVert\omega\mapsto\left\lVert f(A^{-1}\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}}-A^{-1}BD^{-1}\omega,D^{-1}\omega)\right\rVert_{p}\right\rVert_{q}\\
=&\ \left\lVert\omega\mapsto\left\lvert\det\ A\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert f(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},D^{-1}\omega)\right\rVert_{p}\right\rVert_{q}\\
=&\ \left\lvert\det\ A\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}}\ \left\lvert\det\ D\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}},\end{split}

where
a^{\pm\frac{1}{\infty}}\coloneqq 1
.

The inverse of
S
is also an upper block triangular matrix with
C_{S^{-1}}=C_{S}^{-1}
. It obviously holds
\mathcal{D}_{S}\mathcal{D}_{S^{-1}}f=\mathcal{D}_{S^{-1}}\mathcal{D}_{S}f=f
. Hence
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Before we finish the proof of our main theorem, we prove a lemma that reduces the case of general symplectic matrices
S
to matrices having a specific structure.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
be arbitrary, and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. Then there exists symplectic matrices
S^{\prime},\,S^{\prime\prime}
with the following properties:

(a)

There exist
0\leq k\leq d
and matrices
Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that

S^{\prime}=\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q^{\prime}},\qquad S^{\prime\prime}=V_{Q^{\prime\prime}}\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}~.

(b)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\widehat{S}^{-1}g)(\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is everywhere defined iff
\mathcal{D}_{S^{\prime}}
or
\mathcal{D}_{S^{\prime\prime}}
has the same property.

(c)

S
is upper block triangular iff
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are.

Proof.

Recall the factorization

S=\prod\limits_{i\in K}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q}D_{L}U_{P}~

from ?THM?
, and note that both
\mathcal{D}_{D_{L}}
and
D_{U_{P}}
are bounded operators with bounded inverses on
L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
, by ?THM?
. Hence
\mathcal{D}_{S}:\mathrm{A}(\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}},\widehat{S}^{-1}g)(\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is everywhere defined iff
\mathcal{D}_{M}
has the same property, where

M=\prod\limits_{i\in K}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q}.

Furthermore, since the set of upper block triangular matrices is a subgroup containing
D_{L}
and
U_{P}
,
M
is upper block triangular iff
S
is.

It remains to reduce the case of general index sets
K
to
K=\{k+1,\ldots,d\}
. To begin with,
\Pi_{i}\Pi_{i+d}=I
, so we can assume that for no
i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
both
i
and
i+d
are in
I
.
To separate the indices, we define

(3.6)

I_{<}=I\cap\{1,...,d\}\quad\text{and}\quad I_{>}=\{i\in\{1,\cdots,d\}\mid i+d\in I\}.

By assumption,
I_{<}
and
I_{>}
are disjoint. We define the diagonal matrix
K_{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, which serves to correct the sign:

(3.7)

\left(K_{I}\right)_{j,j}=\begin{cases}-1&,\text{ if }\;j\in I_{>},\\
1&,\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

This results in

(3.8)

\prod\limits_{i\in I}\Pi_{i}=\prod\limits_{i\in I_{>}}\Pi_{i}^{T}\prod\limits_{i\in I_{<}}\Pi_{i}=\diag(K_{I},K_{I})\ \prod\limits_{i\in I_{>}\bigcup I_{<}}\Pi_{i}.

Finally, we observe

(3.9)

\prod\limits_{i\in I}\Pi_{i}\ e_{j}\neq e_{j}\qquad\text{ if and only if}\qquad\prod\limits_{i\in I}\Pi_{i}\ e_{j+d}\neq e_{j+d}.

So as not to have to deal with the order of the indices, we write

(3.10)

\prod\limits_{i\in I_{>}\bigcup I_{<}}\Pi_{i}=\diag(R_{I},R_{I})\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i},

where
k=d-\left\lvert I\right\rvert
and
R_{I}
is the permutation matrix which represents the permutation of the indices in
I
with
\{k+1,\cdots,d\}
.
Altogether, we get the slightly more complicated decomposition

(3.11)

\begin{split}M&=\diag(K_{I},K_{I})\ \diag(R_{I},R_{I})\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q}\\
&=\diag(K_{I}R_{I},K_{I}R_{I})\ \underbrace{\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q}}_{\eqqcolon S^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Hence, one more appeal to ?THM?
provides that
\mathcal{D}_{S^{\prime}}
is everywhere defined iff the same holds for
\mathcal{D}_{S}
.
In addition,
S^{\prime}
is upper block triangular iff
S
has the same property. Hence (a) and (b) are established for the factorization

S^{\prime}=\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}\Pi_{i}\ V_{Q}~.

For the verification of (c), observe that the equation

(3.12)

\widetilde{S}=\diag(K_{I}R_{I},K_{I}R_{I})S^{\prime}

implies that
S^{\prime}
is upper block triangular iff
\widetilde{S}
is, and we already established that this holds iff
S
is upper block triangular.

We have thus shown the desired statements for the first factorization in (a). The second factorization, with the associated properties, is obtained by applying the first factorization to the inverse matrix
S^{-1}
, and taking inverses. Denoting by
I_{l}
the indentity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, we can express the inverse of
S^{\prime}
as

(3.13)

(S^{\prime})^{-1}=V_{-Q}\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}^{T}=\underbrace{V_{-Q}\prod\limits_{i=k+1}^{d}\Pi_{i}}_{\eqqcolon S^{\prime\prime}}\ \diag(I_{k},-I_{d-k},I_{k},-I_{d-k}).

The matrix
S^{\prime\prime}
satisfies the claims by analogous arguments.
∎

The term

(3.17)

The technical calculation is provided in Appendix

It is known that

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
m
be a moderate weight function. Let
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
be given. The following statements hold:

(a)

If
\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is bounded, then
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}
is bounded if and only if

(4.4)

R_{m}\coloneqq\esssup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\ {\frac{m(z)}{m(S^{-1}z)}}<\infty.

(b)

If
\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
is unbounded and

(4.5)

T_{m}\coloneqq\essinf\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\ \frac{m(z)}{m(S^{-1}z)}>0,

then
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}
is unbounded.

Proof.

(a)

If
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is bounded, it is in fact an automorphism, by Theorem
. Then Equation (
4.3
) implies that
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}
is bounded if and only if the multiplication operator

(4.6)

M_{m}:L^{p,q}\to L^{p,q},\qquad f\mapsto\frac{m}{m\circ S^{-1}}\mathop{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Huge$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\Large$\cdot$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\small$\cdot$}}}}f

is bounded. A straightforward, somewhat tedious computation shows that
M_{m}
is bounded if and only if
R_{m}
is finite.

(b)

According to ?THM?
, there is a
f\in L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
with
\mathcal{D}_{S}f\notin L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. It holds

(4.7)

\left\lVert M_{m}\ \mathcal{D}_{S}f\right\rVert_{p,q}\geq T_{m}\left\lVert\mathcal{D}_{S}f\right\rVert_{p,q}=\infty.

∎

Remark
.

If
\mathcal{D}_{S}
is unbounded, but
T_{m}=0
, then it is in general difficult to make a reasonable claim. One can find bounds
\eta_{r}>0
, such that

\frac{1}{\eta_{r}}<\frac{m}{m\circ S^{-1}}<\eta_{r}

on
[-r,r]^{2n}
(cf.
[
17
, Lemma 11.1.1.]
). The problem lies on the behaviour of
\eta_{r}
as
r
tends to
\infty
. If it is particularly slow, then we could expect
M_{m}\,\mathcal{D}_{S}
to be unbounded. It can, though, also happen that
\frac{m}{m\circ S}
decays so fast, that it compensates for the growth of
\mathcal{D}_{S}f
and

(4.8)

M_{m}\,\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}\to L^{p,q}

is well-defined. According to ?THM?
, this would suffice for the boundedness of
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}
.

Observe that the case
m\asymp m\circ S^{-1}
is equivalent to
0<T_{m}\leq R_{m}<\infty
. In this case, the positive results from ?THM?
yield the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
m
be a moderate weight and
S\in\mathrm{Sp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
a symplectic matrix. Assuming
m\asymp m\circ S^{-1}
, the operator

(4.9)

\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}\to L^{p,q}

is bounded if and only if the operator

(4.10)

(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}:L^{p,q}_{m}\to L^{p,q}_{m}

is bounded.

In light of this, we prove an analogous version for weighted modulation spaces.

4.2.
The Lifting on
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

To establish the connection between the metaplectic action on unweighted and on weighted modulation spaces, we make use of
Toeplitz operators
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
a\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
be a symbol and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
a fixed window. Then the Toeplitz operator
\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)
is defined by the formula

(4.11)

\left\langle\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)f_{1},f_{2}\right\rangle_{\tiny L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left\langle a\mathcal{V}_{g}f_{1},\mathcal{V}_{g}f_{2}\right\rangle_{\tiny L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}=\left\langle a\,\mathrm{A}(f_{1},g),\mathrm{A}(f_{2},g)\right\rangle_{\tiny L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}

for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is a well-defined operator which extends uniquely to a continuous operator from
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The class of admissible symbols can be significantly extended (cf.
[
18
, Proposition 1.5.]
). In particular, we can choose the symbol to be our weight function.

Theorem 4.4

(Gröchenig, Toft
[
18
]
)
.

Assume that
m
is an even,
v
-moderate weight function, for the weight
v(x)=(1+\|x\|)^{N}
, and
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then the Toeplitz operator
\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(m)

is an isomorphism from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
onto
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m_{0}/m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
v
-moderate even weight
m_{0}
and every
p,q\in[1,\infty]
.

We will apply this theorem with
m_{0}=m
. Before that, we rewrite the defining property of
\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)f
in terms of its ambiguity function. We recall that the standard symplectic form
[\gamma,\lambda]=\gamma\cdot\mathcal{J}\lambda
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d}
and the symmetric time-frequency shifts satisfy

(4.12)

\rho(\lambda)\rho(\gamma)=\rho(\lambda+\gamma)e^{\pi i[\gamma,\lambda]}.

From the definition of the ambiguity function, one easily sees that
\overline{\mathrm{A}(g,g)(\lambda)}=\mathrm{A}(g,g)(-\lambda)
holds for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
. Let now
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,g\neq 0
be an arbitrary window and
a:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{C}
a symbol. Then it holds

(4.13)

\begin{split}A(\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)f,g)(\lambda)&=\left\langle\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)f,\rho(\lambda)g\right\rangle\\
&=\left\langle a\mathrm{A}(f,g),\mathrm{A}(\rho(\lambda)g,g)\right\rangle\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(\gamma)\mathrm{A}(f,g)(\gamma)\overline{\left\langle\rho(\lambda)g,\rho(\gamma)g\right\rangle}\,d\gamma\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(\gamma)\mathrm{A}(f,g)(\gamma)\overline{\left\langle g,\rho(-\lambda)\rho(\gamma)g\right\rangle}\,d\gamma\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(\gamma)\mathrm{A}(f,g)(\gamma)\overline{\left\langle g,\rho(-\lambda+\gamma)g\right\rangle e^{-\pi i[\gamma,\lambda]}}\,d\gamma\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}a(\gamma)\mathrm{A}(f,g)(\gamma)e^{\pi i[\gamma,\lambda]}\mathrm{A}(g,g)(\lambda-\gamma)e^{\pi i[\gamma,\lambda]}\,d\gamma.\end{split}

This shows that the Toeplitz operator can be understood through the
twisted convolution
. The twisted convolution
F\natural G
of two measurable functions
F,\,G:\mathbb{R}^{2d}\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(4.14)

F\natural G(\lambda)\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(\gamma)G(\lambda-\gamma)e^{\pi i[\gamma,\lambda]}\ d\gamma,

assuming the integral is well-defined. In terms of the Toeplitz operator
\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)
, we can now express its action as

(4.15)

\mathrm{A}(\mathrm{Tp}_{g}(a)f,g)=\left(a\,\mathrm{A}(f,g)\right)\natural\mathrm{A}(g,g).

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
m
be a moderate weight,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. If
\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded and

C_{m}\coloneqq\esssup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}}{\frac{m(z)}{m(S^{-1}z)}}<\infty,

then

\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

is well-defined and bounded.

Proof.

Theorems
and
imply that
(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}:L^{p,q}_{m}\to L^{p,q}_{m}
is bounded. The commutative diagram completes the proof.
∎

We now focus on the opposite implication.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
m
be a moderate weight,
p,q\in[1,\infty]
. If
\widehat{S}\in\mathrm{Mp}(2d,\mathbb{R})
with projection
\pi^{\tiny Mp}(\widehat{S})=S
satisfies
m\asymp m\circ S^{-1}
, then
\widehat{S}
is a bounded operator from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if it is a bounded operator from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The first implication follows from the last corollary. Let now
\widehat{S}
be a bounded operator from
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We fix as a window the normalized Gaussian
g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and recall the symplectic covariance of the ambiguity function (
2.21
).
For all
f\in\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
holds

(4.16)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\widehat{S}f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}&=\left\lVert\mathrm{A}(\widehat{S}f,g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}}\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\tiny\text{Lift}}{\underset{\tiny\eqref{eq:TpTwisted}}{\asymp}}\left\lVert\left(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathrm{A}(\widehat{S}f,g)\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(g,g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}\\[4.30554pt]
\overset{\eqref{eq:symplectic_covariance}}{{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}}&=\left\lVert\left(\tfrac{1}{m}\mathcal{D}_{S}\mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(g,g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}\\[4.30554pt]
&=\left\lVert\left(\tfrac{m\circ S^{-1}}{m\circ S^{-1}}\tfrac{1}{\left(m\circ S\circ\right)S^{-1}}\mathcal{D}_{S}\ \mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(g,g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}\\[4.30554pt]
&=\left\lVert\left((\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m\widetilde{m}^{-1}}\ (\widetilde{m}m^{-1}\mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g))\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(g,g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}},\\
&=\left\lVert(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m\widetilde{m}^{-1}}(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m\widetilde{m}^{-1}}^{-1}\;\left(\left((\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m\widetilde{m}^{-1}}\ (\widetilde{m}m^{-1}\mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g))\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(g,g)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}},\end{split}

where
\widetilde{m}=\tfrac{m}{\mathcal{D}_{S}^{-1}m}
.
Recall that the symplectic matrices are exactly those which preserve the standard symplectic form. Therefore, the twisted convolution of two functions satisfies

(4.17)

\begin{split}\left(F\natural G\right)(S^{-1}\lambda)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(\gamma)G(S^{-1}\lambda-\gamma)e^{\pi i\sigma(\gamma,S^{-1}\lambda)}\ d\gamma\\[4.30554pt]
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(S^{-1}S\gamma)G(S^{-1}(\lambda-S\gamma))e^{\pi i\sigma(S\gamma,SS^{-1}\lambda)}\ d\gamma\\[4.30554pt]
=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}F(S^{-1}\gamma)G(S^{-1}(\lambda-\gamma))e^{\pi i\sigma(\gamma,\lambda)}\ d\gamma=(F\circ S^{-1})\,\natural\,(G\circ S^{-1})(\lambda).\end{split}

This, along with the symplectic covariance of the ambiguity function (
2.21
), leads to

(4.18)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\widehat{S}f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp&\left\lVert(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m\widetilde{m}^{-1}}\;\left(\left(\widetilde{m}m^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(\widehat{S}^{-1}g,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}.\end{split}

We recall that
\widehat{S}^{-1}g
is a Schwartz function. By the lifting Theorem
4.4
and Equation (
4.15
), there exists a tempered distribution
\widetilde{f}\in\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{\widetilde{m}^{-1}m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

(4.19)

\left(\widetilde{m}m^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{A}(f,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right)\ \natural\ \mathrm{A}(\widehat{S}^{-1}g,\widehat{S}^{-1}g)=\mathrm{A}(\widetilde{f},\widehat{S}^{-1}g).

Recall that Theorem
4.4
also provides the norm equivalence
\|\tilde{f}\|_{M^{p,q}_{\widetilde{m}^{-1}m}}\asymp\|f\|_{M^{p,q}}
, where the implied constant is independent of
f\in\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By assumption,
\widetilde{m}\asymp 1
, which implies
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{\widetilde{m}^{-1}m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

(4.20)

\left\lVert\widetilde{f}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp\left\lVert\widetilde{f}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{\widetilde{m}^{-1}m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Putting it all together, we obtain

(4.21)

\begin{split}\left\lVert\widehat{S}f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp&\left\lVert(\mathcal{D}_{S})_{m}\mathrm{A}(\widetilde{f},\widehat{S}^{-1}g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}=\left\lVert\mathrm{A}(\widehat{S}\widetilde{f},g)\right\rVert_{L^{p,q}_{m}}\\[4.30554pt]
\asymp&\left\lVert\widehat{S}\widetilde{f}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\left\lVert\widetilde{f}\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\asymp\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

This proves the claim.
∎

4.3.
Examples

We consider two of the standard weights which are frequently used in time-frequency analysis and determine the cases where our criteria are applicable. We refer to
[
14
]
for a general overview of typical weights.

Example A
.

We begin with

(4.22)

m(z)=m_{s,t}(z)=(1+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)^{s}(\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert))^{t}.

This class contains in particular the radial polynomial weights.
Here it is well-known that
\|z\|\asymp\|Sz\|
holds for
any
invertible matrix
S\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}
. This immediately shows the equivalence
m_{s,0}\asymp m_{s,0}\circ S
.

For the logarithmic part, we spell out the norm equivalence as

A\|z\|\leq\|Sz\|\leq B\|z\|~,

with
0<A\leq B
, and obtain

(4.23)

\begin{split}\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+B\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}\leq\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+\left\lVert Sz\right\rVert)}\leq\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+A\left\lVert z\right\rVert)},\end{split}

We next establish bounds for the ends of this chain of inequalities as
\|z\|\to 0
and
\|z\|\to\infty
. Using l’Hospital’s rule allows computing the limits

(4.24)

\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+A\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}=\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+B\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}=1~,

as well as

(4.25)

\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+A\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}=\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+B\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}=1~.

As a consequence there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}<\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+\left\lVert Sz\right\rVert)}<2

holds for all
z\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}
satisfying either
\left\lVert z\right\rVert<\epsilon
or
\left\lVert z\right\rVert>\epsilon^{-1}
. However, the remaining
z
constitute a compact set, and
z\mapsto\frac{\ln(e+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)}{\ln(e+\left\lVert Sz\right\rVert)}
is a nonvanishing continuous function, hence there exist nontrivial lower and upper bounds valid on this set. This shows
m_{0,t}\asymp m_{0,t}\circ S
, for all
t
.

Combining our results so far, we obtain for fixed
s,t
that

(4.26)

\frac{m_{s,t}}{m_{s,t}\circ S}\asymp 1~.

Hence ?THM?
together with ?THM?
provides the conclusion that the operator
\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m_{s,t}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m_{s,t}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded iff
p=q
, or
p\not=q
and
S
is upper block triangular. Since the latter criterion applies to
S
iff it applies to its inverse, we further obtain that boundedness of
\widehat{S}
on
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m_{s,t}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
already implies that it is an automorphism.

We now turn to the second type of polynomial weight functions, where the condition
m\asymp m\circ S
is actually restrictive.

Example B
.

According to
[
17
, Ex. 11.1.1.]
, it holds

(4.27)

(1+\left\lVert z\right\rVert)^{s}\asymp(1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert+\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert)^{s},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,z=(x,\omega).

This weight is symmetric in
(x,\omega)
. However, sometimes, we are only interested in the growth in one direction. This motivates a different class of weight functions, given by

(4.28)

\begin{split}p_{s}(x,\omega)&=p_{s}(x)=(1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert)^{s},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
q_{t}(x,\omega)&=q_{t}(\omega)=(1+\left\lVert\omega\right\rVert)^{t},\qquad x,\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{split}

with
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Since we are only interested in nontrivial weights, we assume
s\not=0\not=t
throughout.
We use the block-matrix notation

(4.29)

S=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{and}\qquad z=\begin{pmatrix}x\\
\omega\end{pmatrix},

and get

(4.30)

\frac{(p_{s}\circ S)(z)}{p_{s}(z)}=\left(\frac{1+\left\lVert Ax+B\omega\right\rVert}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\right)^{s}

We first show that
p_{s}\circ S\asymp p_{s}
holds iff
S
is lower block triangular, i.e., iff
B=0
.

To see the ”only-if” part,
assume
B\neq 0
and
s>0
. There is
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\left\lVert B\omega\right\rVert>\left\lVert Ae_{1}\right\rVert
. Therefore,

(4.31)

\left(\frac{1+\left\lVert Ae_{1}+B\,n\omega\right\rVert}{1+\left\lVert e_{1}\right\rVert}\right)^{s}\geq\left(\frac{1+n\left\lVert B\omega\right\rVert-\left\lVert Ae_{1}\right\rVert}{2}\right)^{s}\to\infty\quad(n\to\infty).

In the case
s<0
, we observe that if
S
is not a lower block triangular matrix, the same applies to
S^{-1}
. Using
z^{\prime}=Sz
, we can use the previous calculation to establish the unboundedness of
\frac{p_{s}}{p_{s}\circ S}
. Hence we have
p_{s}\circ S\not\asymp p_{s}
for al
s\not=0
.

Conversely, if
B=0
,
A
is invertible, and we have the well-known fact that

(4.32)

\frac{p_{s}\circ S}{p_{s}}(x,\omega)=\left(\frac{1+\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert}{1+\left\lVert x\right\rVert}\right)^{s}\asymp 1~.

This implies that in the case where
S
is not lower block triangular, neither ?THM?
nor ?THM?
is applicable, leaving the question of boundedness of
\widehat{S}
on
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{p_{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
wide open.

In the case where
S
is lower block triangular, the combination of ?THM?
and ?THM?
yields that
\widehat{S}
is bounded iff
S
is upper block triangular, or equivalently, iff
S
is block-diagonal.

For the weight
q_{t}
with
t\not=0
, the analogous reasoning as for
p_{s}
yields that
q_{t}\circ S\asymp q_{t}
holds iff
S
is
upper
block triangular. Again, this leaves the question of boundedness of
\widehat{S}
open for matrices that are not upper block triangular. However, for matrices
S
that are upper block triangular, ?THM?
together with ?THM?
already implies the boundedness of
\widehat{S}
on
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{q_{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, without further restrictions on
S
.

Concluding remarks

Our results concerning the mapping behavior of metaplectic operators
\widehat{S}
on unweighted modulation spaces give a complete picture. To our knowledge, even the mapping properties of the associated dilation operators
\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
were not fully clarified prior to our paper, and the parts of Theorem
dealing with this problem seem to be new.

The weighted case poses several novel challenges. The first one concerns the choice of weights: Our paper concentrates on even polynomial weights, in order to apply results from
[
18
]
, and we are not aware of extensions of these results to other weight classes. Note that the arguments of Gröchenig and Toft rely on some fairly subtle properties of algebras of symbol classes, and to us, it seems open whether generalizations of the desired type are available.

The second unresolved question concerns the mapping properties of metaplectic operators
\widehat{S}:\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathrm{M}^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the case where
\widehat{S}
is unbounded on the unweighted modulation space
\mathrm{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and the weights
m
and
m\circ S^{-1}
are not equivalent. We presented examples that this case can occur for fairly natural choices of weights.
Understanding the associated measure-theoretic question, concerning the boundedness properties of the dilation operator
\mathcal{D}_{S}:L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\to L^{p,q}_{m}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
clearly constitutes an important step towards answering the question in full.

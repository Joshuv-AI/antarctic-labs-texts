On the strong Feller property of the heat equation on quantum graphs with Kirchhoff noise
Mohamed Fkirine, Mihály Kovács, Eszter Sikolya

Abstract
. We consider a so-called quantum graph with standard continuity and Kirchhoff vertex conditions where the Kirchhoff vertex condition is perturbed by Gaussian noise. We show that the quantum graph setting is very different from the classical one dimensional boundary noise setting, where the transition semigroup is known to be strong Feller, by giving examples and counterexamples to the strong Feller property. In particular, when the graph is a tree, and there is noise present in all but one of the boundary vertices, then the transition semigroup associated with the problem is strong Feller at any time T > 0 T>0 . This turns out to be also a necessary condition for equilateral star graphs. We also comment on the existence and uniqueness of the invariant measure and the regularity of the solution.

Introduction

We consider a so-called quantum graph; that is, is a metric graph
\mathsf{G}
, equipped with a diffusion operator on each edge and certain vertex conditions. Our terminology follows
[
9
, Chap. 1]
(see also
[
8
]
and
[
24
]
), we list here only the most important concepts. The graph
\mathsf{G}
consists of a finite set of vertices
\mathsf{V}=\{\mathsf{v}\}
and a finite set
\mathsf{E}=\{\mathsf{e}\}
of edges connecting the vertices. We denote by
n=|\mathsf{V}|
the number of vertices. In general, a metric graph is assumed to have directed edges; that is edges having an origin and a boundary vertex. In our case, dealing with self-adjoint operators, we can just consider undirected edges. Each edge is assigned a positive length
\ell_{\mathsf{e}}\in(0,+\infty)
, and we denote by
x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}]
a coordinate of
\mathsf{G}
.

The metric graph structure enables one to speak about functions
z
on
\mathsf{G}
, defined along the edges such that for any coordinate
x
, the function takes its value
z(x)
. If we emphasize that
x
is taken from the edge
\mathsf{e}
, we write
z_{\mathsf{e}}(x)
. Thus, a function
z
on
\mathsf{G}
can be regarded as a vector of functions that are defined on the edges, therefore we will also write

z=\left(z_{\mathsf{e}}\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}},

and consider it as an element of a product function space.

To write down the vertex conditions in the form of equations, for a given function
z
on
\mathsf{G}
and for each
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
, we introduce the following notation. For any
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
, we denote by
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}
the set of edges incident to the vertex
\mathsf{v}
, and by
d_{\mathsf{v}}=|\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}|
the degree of
\mathsf{v}
. Let
z_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})
denote the value of
z
in
\mathsf{v}
along the edge
\mathsf{e}
in the case
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}
. Let
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}=\{\mathsf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}\}
, and define

(1.1)

Z(\mathsf{v})=\left(z_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}}=\begin{pmatrix}z_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}(\mathsf{v})\\
\vdots\\
z_{\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}}(\mathsf{v})\end{pmatrix}\in\real^{d_{\mathsf{v}}},

the vector of the function values in the vertex
\mathsf{v}
.

For vertices with
d_{\mathsf{v}}>1
, let
I_{\mathsf{v}}
be the bi-diagonal matrix

(1.2)

I_{\mathsf{v}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&-1\end{pmatrix}\in\real^{(d_{\mathsf{v}}-1)\times d_{\mathsf{v}}}.

It is easy to see that if we set

(1.3)

I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(\mathsf{v})=0_{\real{}^{d_{\mathsf{v}}-1}},

this means that all the function values coincide in
\mathsf{v}
. If it is satisfied for each vertex
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
with
d_{\mathsf{v}}>1
for a function
z
on
\mathsf{G}
, which is continuous on each edge, including the one-sided continuity at the endpoints, then we call
z

continuous on

\mathsf{G}
.

Similarly, for a function
z
on
\mathsf{G}
which is differentiable on each edge; that is,
z^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}
exists for each
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}
including the one-sided derivatives at the endpoints, we set

(1.4)

Z^{\prime}(\mathsf{v})=\left(z^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}}=\begin{pmatrix}z^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}(\mathsf{v})\\
\vdots\\
z^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}}(\mathsf{v})\end{pmatrix}\in\real^{d_{\mathsf{v}}},

the vector of the function derivatives in the vertex
\mathsf{v}
. We will assume throughout the paper that derivatives are taken in the directions away from the vertex
\mathsf{v}
(i.e. into the edge), see
[
9
, Sec. 1.4.]
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
is a complete probability space endowed with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}=(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
.
Let the process

(\beta(t))_{t\in[0,T]}=\left(\left(\beta_{\mathsf{v}}(t)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\right)_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}},

be an
n
-valued Brownian motion (Wiener process) with covariance matrix

Q\in\real^{n\times n}

with respect to the filtration
\mathbb{F}
; that is,

(\beta(t))_{t\in[0,T]}
is
(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted and for all
t>s
,
\beta(t)-\beta(s)
is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{s}
.

We consider the problem written formally as

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)&=(c_{\mathsf{e}}z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})^{\prime}(t,x)-p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x),&x\in(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\;t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E},\;\;&(a)\\
0&=I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(t,\mathsf{v}),\;&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(b)\\
\dot{\beta}_{\mathsf{v}}(t)&=C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}Z^{\prime}(t,\mathsf{v}),&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(c)\\
z_{\mathsf{e}}(0,x)&=z_{0,\mathsf{e}}(x),&x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}].\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.\;\;&(d)\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}
and
z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime}
denote the time and space derivative, respectively, of
z_{\mathsf{e}}
. For each
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
,
Z(t,\mathsf{v})
and
Z^{\prime}(t,\mathsf{v})
denote the vector of the function values in
\mathsf{v}
introduced in (
1.1
) and (
1.4
), respectively, for the function on
\mathsf{G}
defined as

z(t,\cdot)=\left(z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,\cdot)\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}.

Analogously to (
1.1
), for each
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
and
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}=\{\mathsf{e}_{1},\dots,\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}\}
the 1-row matrix in
(\ref{eq:stochnet}c)
is defined by

C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}=\left(c_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}(\mathsf{v}),\dots,c_{\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}}(\mathsf{v})\right)\in\real^{1\times d_{\mathsf{v}}}.

Equations
(\ref{eq:stochnet}b)
assume continuity of the function
z(t,\cdot)
on the metric graph
\mathsf{G}
, cf. (
1.3
). If we set
0\in\real^{d_{\mathsf{v}}}
on the left-hand-side of equations
(\ref{eq:stochnet}c)
, we obtain the usual
Kirchhoff vertex conditions
for the system which is the generalization of the Neumann boundary condition on an interval, cf. (
4.3
c). Therefore, we refer to the noise in equation
(\ref{eq:stochnet}c)
as
Kirchhoff noise
.

It is clear by definition, that
(\ref{eq:stochnet}b)
consists of

(1.6)

\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}(d_{\mathsf{v}}-1)=2|\mathsf{E}|-|\mathsf{V}|=2|\mathsf{E}|-n

equations. At the same time,
(\ref{eq:stochnet}c)
consists of
n
equations. Hence, we have altogether
2|\mathsf{E}|
(boundary or vertex) conditions in the vertices.

The functions
c_{\mathsf{e}}
are (variable) diffusion coefficients or conductances, and we assume that

c_{\mathsf{e}}\in C[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}],\quad 0<c_{0}\leq c_{\mathsf{e}}(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}]\text{ and }\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.

The functions
p_{\mathsf{e}}
are non-negative, bounded functions, hence

(1.7)

0\leq p_{\mathsf{e}}\in L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\quad\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.

In equation
(\ref{eq:stochnet}d)
we pose the initial conditions on the edges.

In the recent paper
[
21
]
the authors investigated (a nonlinear version of) (
1.5
) and proved, among other things, that the transition semigroup associated with the (
1.5
) is Feller. However, the strong Feller property of the solution has not been established there and this property is in the focus of the present paper. The model (
1.5
) can be viewed as the quantum graph analogue of the model considered by Da Prato and Zabczyk in
[
14
]
in case of a single interval and classical Neumann boundary noise. In the latter paper the authors show that the transition semigroup associated with the problem is always strong Feller. It turns out that in our setting this is not true in general. We show that in certain situations the transition semigroup is strong Feller, however, we also show that there are ample situations where the strong Feller property fails to hold even for simple configurations such as a equilateral star graph if the system is not noisy enough.

One general strategy for investigating the strong Feller property, see for example,
[
15
, Chapter 9.4]
is to consider an associated null controllability problem. While there exist several results for control problems on quantum graphs, these mainly deal with the wave equation, see for example,
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
7
,
23
,
16
]
for an admittedly incomplete list. The Dirichlet boundary vertex null controllability of the heat equation for metric trees is mainly approached via an exact controllability problem for the wave equation
[
1
,
3
]
with the exception of the recent paper
[
6
]
, where the the Hilbert Uniqueness Method is used, in
particular, the authors study the observability of the adjoint system with a Carleman inequality. We are not aware of results concerning the Neumann vertex control of the heat equation on metric graphs. There are several possible approaches to the problem. One could study the adjoint problem as in
[
6
]
and use the Hilbert Uniqeness Method with a Carleman inequality, consider the exact controllability of the associated wave equation or try to use the method of moments directly. This latter approach would be rather problematic to use due to the lack of sufficient information on the separation of consecutive eigenvalues of the system operator, in general. We will hence consider the exact Neumann-controllability of the associated wave equation which was investigated in
[
5
]
through a method originally introduced in
[
4
]
for Dirichlet boundary control on metric trees. In
[
5
]
, unlike in
[
23
, Chapter 5]
, it is not assumed that there is a boundary vertex with zero Dirichlet boundary condition. This latter is essential in
[
23
, Chapter 5]
as it ensures that the energy in fact is a norm on the state space. We therefore rely on the theory of so-called ST-active sets from
[
5
]
, which is essential in dealing with control problems for the wave equation for general metric graphs, to show that the wave equation is exactly controllable on metric trees if one applies Neumann controls at all but one of the boundary vertices, where Neumann zero boundary condition is assumed. One can then apply the theory of vector-exponentials in a similar fashion as in
[
1
,
3
]
to obtain the null-controllability of the heat equation on metric trees if one applies Neumann controls at all but one of the boundary vertices, where Neumann zero boundary condition is assumed.

Having this null-controllability result at hand, Theorem
4.6
shows that if the covariance matrix
Q=\mathrm{diag}(q_{\mathsf{v}})_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}
in (
4.2
) is diagonal, and
q_{\mathsf{v}}\neq 0
for all boundary vertices except for, possibly, one of them, then the transition semigroup associated with the problem is strong Feller at any time
T>0
. It turns out that for the Laplace operator on the equilateral star-graph the conditions of Theorem
4.6
is also necessary. Finally, we mention here that for the wave equation it is known that for
general metric trees (at least for Dirichlet control) it is also necessary to apply controls at all but one of the boundary vertices. One, unfortunately, cannot use this result to conclude the same general result for the null controllability of the heat equation as one only has the exact controllability of the wave equation yields the null controllability of the heat equation on appropriate state spaces and not vice versa.

In Section

3.1
) and identify the associated abstract null-control problem to consider in order to prove the strong Feller property of the transition semigroup.

In Section

we first show that the problem (
4.1
) can be treated using the setting of the Section

4.6
we show that if
c_{e}=1
and the covariance matrix

in (

) is diagonal, and

.
Then, in Example

which shows that the existence of a loop in the graph always destroys the strong Feller property. We would like to highlight that these examples show that quantum graph setting is very different from the classical one dimensional boundary noise setting, where the transition semigroup is known to be strong Feller
[

]
.

In Section

, in Theorem

In Appendix

2.
A short introduction to control theory

Throughout this section, we assume that
H
,
U
are Hilbert spaces and
T>0
is a real number. First, we introduce some classical background on semigroup theory. Let
A
be the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
, with growth bound
\omega_{0}(\mathbb{T})
. We denote by
H_{-1}
the completion of
H
with respect to the norm
\|x\|_{-1}=\|R(\lambda,A)x\|
, where
R(\lambda,A):=(\lambda-A)^{-1}
, and
\lambda
is an arbitrary (but fixed) element in the resolvent set
\rho(A)
. It is easy to verify that
H_{-1}
is independent of the choice of
\lambda
, since different values of
\lambda
lead to equivalent norms on
H_{-1}
. The space
H_{-1}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{D}(A^{*})^{*}
and we have
\mathcal{D}(A)\subset H\subset H_{-1}
densely and with continuous embedding. The semigroup
\mathbb{T}
extends to a strongly continuous semigroup on
H_{-1}
, whose generator is an extension of
A
, with domain
H
. We denote the extensions of
\mathbb{T}
and
A
by
\mathbb{T}_{-1}:=(\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
A_{-1}
, respectively.

Next, consider the control system

(2.1)

\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=A_{-1}z(t)+Bu(t),&t\in(0,T],\\
z(0)=z_{0}\in H,&\end{cases}

where
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,H_{-1})
and
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
is a control function. The mild solution of the equation (
2.1
) is given by

(2.2)

z(t)=\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}+\Phi_{t}u,\qquad u\in L^{2}(0,T;U),\;\;z_{0}\in H,

where
\Phi_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(0,T;U),H_{-1})
is defined by

(2.3)

\Phi_{t}u:=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t-s)Bu(s)ds.

Notice that in the above formula,
\mathbb{T}_{-1}
acts on
H_{-1}
and the integration is carried out in
H_{-1}
. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

The operator
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,H_{-1})
is called an
admissible control operator

for
A
if
Ran
(\Phi_{\tau})\subset H
for some
\tau>0
(hence, for all
\tau>0
).

It is worth noting that if
B
is an admissible control operator for
A
, then the closed graph theorem guarantees that
\Phi_{t}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(0,T;U),H)
for all
t\geq 0
. As a result, for any
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
and
z_{0}\in H
, the solutions
z(\cdot)
of the initial value problem (
2.1
) stay in
H
and form a continuous
H
-valued function of
t
. The operators
\Phi_{t}
are commonly referred to as
input maps
associated with the pair
(A,B)
, see
[
30
, Def. 2.1]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,H_{-1})
be an admissible control operator for
A
. Given
T>0
, the system (
2.1
) is said to be

\bullet

null-controllable in time
T
, if for any
z_{0}\in H
there exists a control
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
such that the solution of (
2.1
) satisfies
z(T)=0
.

\bullet

exactly controllable in time
T
, if for any
z_{0},\,z_{1}\in H
there exists a control
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
such that the solution of (
2.1
) satisfies
z(T)=z_{1}
.

It is easy to see that null controllability in time
T
is equivalent to the following property:

(2.4)

\mathrm{Ran}\,\mathbb{T}(T)\subset\mathrm{Ran}\,\Phi_{T}.

In the following we will deal with an abstract boundary control problem of the form

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=A_{\max}z(t),&t\in(0,T],\\
Gz(t)=u(t),&t\in(0,T],\\
z(0)=z_{0}\in H,\end{cases}

where
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
is a control function.

Assumptions 2.3
.

assume that
A_{\max}:\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})\subset H\to H
is a closed linear operator, where the embedding
\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})\to H
is a continuous linear map with dense range,
and
G:\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})\to U
is a linear operator such that the following assumptions are satisfied.

(A1)

The operator
G:\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})\to U
is surjective.

(A2)

The operator

(2.6)

\begin{split}A&:=(A_{\max})|_{\mathcal{D}(A)},\\
\mathcal{D}(A)&:=\left\{z\in\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})\colon Gz=0_{H}\right\}=\ker G\end{split}

generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\mathbb{T}:=(\mathbb{T}(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
H
.

It is shown by Greiner
[
19
, Lemmas 1.2, 1.3]
that under the above assumptions, the domain
\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})
can be viewed as the direct sum of
\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})
for any
\lambda\in\rho(A)
. Moreover, the operator
G|_{\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})}
is invertible and the inverse

(2.7)

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}:=\left(G|_{\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})}\right)^{-1}:U\to\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})\subset H,\qquad\lambda\in\rho(A),

is bounded. The operator
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
is called the
Dirichlet operator
associated with
A_{\max}
and
G
.

For a fixed
\lambda\in\rho(A)
we consider the following
control operator

(2.8)

B:=(\lambda-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}(U,H_{-1}).

Using the resolvent identity, it is straightforward that the definition of
B
is independent of the choice of
\lambda
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The boundary control system (
2.5
) can be reformulated as a control problem of the form (
2.1
) with
A
defined in (
2.6
) and
B
given by (
2.8
).

Proof.

As for any
u\in U
and
\lambda\in\rho(A)
we have
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}u\in\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})
so
\lambda\mathbb{D_{\lambda}}u=A_{\max}\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}u
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left(A_{\max}-A_{-1}\right)\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}u=(\lambda-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}u=Bu.

Since
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}
is the inverse of
G|_{\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})}
and
\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})=\mathcal{D}(A)\oplus\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})
, it follows that

A_{\max}=(A_{-1}+BG)|_{\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})}.

Thus, by
Gz(t)=u(t)
, system (
2.5
) is equivalent to the problem

(2.9)

\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=A_{-1}z(t)+Bu(t),&t\in(0,T],\\
z(0)=z_{0}\in H,&\end{cases}

which gives the result.
∎

3.
Strong Feller property of the transition semigroup

We first aim to investigate the strong Feller property of the mild solution of the following formal general boundary noise problem on the separable Hilbert space
H
with boundary space
U
:

(3.1)

\begin{cases}\dot{w}(t)=A_{\max}w(t),&t\in(0,T]\\
Gw(t)=\dot{\beta}(t),&t\in(0,T],\\
w(0)=z_{0},\end{cases}

where the process

(\beta(t))_{t\in[0,T]},

is an
U
-valued Brownian motion with covariance operator
Q
.

Assumptions 3.1
.

(1)

For the operators
A_{\max}
and
G
and for the spaces
H
and
U
Assumptions
2.3
are satisfied, and
\dim U=n<\infty
, hence
Q
is an
n\times n
matrix.

(2)

The generator
(A,\mathcal{D}(A))
in (
2.6
) is self-adjoint, dissipative, has compact resolvent, and
1\in\rho(A)
holds. In particular,
A
generates a contractive analytic semigroup
\left(\mathbb{T}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
on
H
.

(3)

Analogously to (
2.8
), define

(3.2)

B\coloneqq(1-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}\in\mathcal{L}\left(U,H_{-1}\right).

Using the analiticity of the semigroup, we obtain that the operator
\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)B
maps
U
into
H
. Assume that

(3.3)

\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{HS}(U,H)}dt<+\infty,

where
\mathrm{HS}
denotes the space of Hilbert–Schmidt operators between the appropriate spaces.

Assumptions
3.1
imply that
A
has only point spectrum with eigenvalues
(-\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that form a sequence of nonpositive real numbers satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k+1}\leq\cdots

and

(3.4)

\lambda_{k}\to+\infty,\quad k\to\infty.

We may then choose a complete orthonormal set
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
of eigenfunctions such that

(3.5)

Af_{k}=-\lambda_{k}f_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Within this context, the scale of Hilbert spaces

\displaystyle D(A)\subset H\subset H_{-1},

introduced in Section
2
, can be completed to a scale
(H_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}}
as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

For every
\alpha\geq 0
and for any
\lambda>0
we set

H_{\alpha}:=\mathcal{D}((\lambda-A)^{\alpha}),

equipped with its natural norm
\|\cdot\|_{\alpha}
; that is, for
u\in H
we have
u\in H_{\alpha}
if and only if

(3.6)

\|u\|^{2}_{\alpha}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{2\alpha}|\langle u,f_{k}\rangle|^{2}<\infty.

For
\alpha>0
, the space
H_{-\alpha}
is defined as the dual of

with respect to the pivot space
H
. Equivalently,

is the completion of

for the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{-\alpha}^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{-2\alpha}|\langle u,f_{k}\rangle|^{2}.

It is well-known that the above norms are equivalent for all
\lambda>0
.

Remark 3.3
.

If Assumptions
3.1
are satisfied, it follows from
[
14
, Thm 2.2]
that the input map

, defined in (

), is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator from

into

, see also

.

By Assumptions
3.1
, using the terminology of
[

]
, system (

\begin{cases}dw(t)=Aw(t)dt+Bd\beta(t),&t\in(0,T],\\

and the weak solution
w(t)=w(t,z_{0})\in H
,
t\in[0,T]
of (

We mention that, for all

,

is a Gaussian random variable with mean vector
\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}
and covariance operator

given by

\displaystyle Q_{t}x=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{-1}(s)BQB^{*}\mathbb{T}^{*}(s)xds,\qquad\forall x\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})=\mathcal{D}(A).

Let
\mathcal{B}_{b}({H})
(resp.

{H}
to

. We shall denote by

,

, the

corresponding to problem (

):

(3.9)

\mathcal{P}_{t}\phi(z_{0}):=\mathbb{E}\left(\phi(w(t,z_{0}))\right),\qquad t\in[0,T],\;\;\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}({H}).

Definition 3.4
.

The transition semigroup
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is said to be a

semigroup at time

if

(3.10)

\text{ for any }\phi\in\mathcal{B}_{b}({H}),\quad\mathcal{P}_{T}\phi\in C_{b}({H})\text{ holds}.

It is well-known that

is strong Feller at time

if and only if

\displaystyle\text{Ran}\;\mathbb{T}(T)\subset\text{Ran}\;Q_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}},

see for example
[
13
, Thm. 7.2.1]
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
,
t\in[0,T]
, be the input maps associated with
(A,B)
, see (
2.3
). It follows from
[
15
, Cor. B3]
that

\displaystyle\text{Ran}\;\Phi_{T}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}=\text{Ran}\;Q_{T}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Remark 3.5
.

Taking in consideration (
2.4
), we deduce that
\mathcal{P}_{t}
is strong Feller at time

if and only if the problem

(3.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{z}(t)&=Az(t)+BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t),\;t\in(0,T],\\
z(0)&=z_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

is null controllable in time

(see Definition
2.2
).

In the following, we will investigate the null controllability of the above problem to obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for the strong Feller property of the transition semigroup.

By Assumptions
3.1
, for any
z_{0}\in H
and any
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
the problem (
3.11
) has a unique mild solution
z\in C([0,T],H)
given by

(3.12)

z(t)=\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t-s)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)ds=\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}+\Phi_{t}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}u.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let Assumptions
3.1
be satisfied. Let
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
be a set of linearly independent eigenfunctions of
A
corresponding to
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
forming a total subset in
H
. Then the problem (
3.11
) is null controllable in time

if and only if for any
z_{0}\in H
there is a control
u\in L^{2}\left(0,T;U\right)
satisfying

(3.13)

\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{k}\right)_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}(T-s)}u_{j}(s)\,ds\text{ for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

By (
2.2
) we know that for any
z_{0}\in H
and
u\in L^{2}(0,T;U)
, the mild solution
z(t)
defined in (
3.12
) exists and is unique. We take any eigenfunction
f_{k}
from the total subset satisfying (
3.5
). Then

(3.14)

\displaystyle\langle z(T),f_{k}\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{T}(T)z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\langle\Phi_{T}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}u,f_{k}\rangle_{H}

(3.15)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\langle Q^{\frac{1}{2}}u,\Phi_{T}^{*}f_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}\left(0,T,U\right)}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}(T-s)}\langle B^{*}f_{k},Q^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s)\rangle_{U}\,ds

(3.17)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}(T-s)}\langle Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{k},u(s)\rangle_{U}\,ds

(3.18)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}(T-s)}\left(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{k}\right)_{j}u_{j}(s)ds

(3.19)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}T}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{H}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{k}\right)_{j}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{k}(T-s)}u_{j}(s)ds,

where we have used that for every
T>0
the adjoint
\Phi_{T}^{*}
of the operator
\Phi_{T}
is in
\mathcal{L}\left(H,L^{2}(0,T;U)\right)
and is given by

(3.20)

\displaystyle\left(\Phi_{T}^{*}x\right)(t)=B^{*}\mathbb{T}^{*}(T-t)x,\qquad t\in[0,T],

for any

, see
[
26
, Prop. 4.4.1]
.
Since the closure of the linear hull of the functions
(f_{k})
is
H
,

is equivalent to the fact that
\langle z(T),f_{k}\rangle_{H}=0
for each
k
. This implies that the problem is null controllable if and only if for any
z_{0}\in H
there is a control
u\in L^{2}\left(0,T;U\right)
such that (
3.13
) holds.
∎

4.
Strong Feller property of the solution of the network problem with Kirchhoff noise

In this section we aim to investigate the strong Feller property of the transition semigroup of the network problem on the metric graph
\mathsf{G}
with Kirchhoff noise in the vertices, see (
1.5
),

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)&=(c_{\mathsf{e}}z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})^{\prime}(t,x)-p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x),&x\in(0,1),\;t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E},\;\;&(a)\\
0&=I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(t,\mathsf{v}),\;&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(b)\\
\dot{\beta}_{\mathsf{v}}(t)&=C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}Z^{\prime}(t,\mathsf{v}),&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(c)\\
z_{\mathsf{e}}(0,x)&=z_{0,\mathsf{e}}(x),&x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E},\;\;&(d)\end{aligned}\right.

where the process

(\beta(t))_{t\in[0,T]}=\left(\left(\beta_{\mathsf{v}}(t)\right)_{t\in[0,T]}\right)_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}},

is an
n
-valued Brownian motion (Wiener process) with
|\mathsf{V}|=n
and covariance matrix

(4.2)

Q\in\real^{n\times n},

and

\begin{split}0&<c_{0}\leq c_{\mathsf{e}}\in C[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}],\quad\text{for some constant }c_{0},\\
0&\leq p_{\mathsf{e}}\in L^{\infty}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\quad\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.\end{split}

We start with the deterministic problem on the network:

(4.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)&=(c_{\mathsf{e}}z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime})^{\prime}(t,x)-p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x),&x\in(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}),\;t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E},\;\;&(a)\\
0&=I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(t,\mathsf{v}),\;&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(b)\\
0&=C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}Z^{\prime}(t,\mathsf{v}),&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;&(c)\\
z_{\mathsf{e}}(0,x)&=z_{0,\mathsf{e}}(x),&x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}],\;\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.\;\;&(d)\end{aligned}\right.

where
0
denotes the constant
0

d_{\mathsf{v}}-1
on the left-hand-side of
(\ref{eq:netcp}b)
. Recall that equations
(\ref{eq:netcp}b)
assume continuity of the function

, cf. (
1.3

(\ref{eq:netcp}c)
are called

.

We show that this system can be written in the form of (
2.5
) with

. First we consider the Hilbert space

(4.4)

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}L^{2}\left(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}\right)

as the
state space
of the edges, endowed with the natural inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}f_{\mathsf{e}}(x)g_{\mathsf{e}}(x)dx,\qquad f=\left(f_{\mathsf{e}}\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}},\;g=\left(g_{\mathsf{e}}\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\in\mathcal{H}.

On
\mathcal{H}
we define the operator

(4.5)

A_{\max}\coloneqq\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\left(c_{\mathsf{e}}\frac{d}{dx}\right)-p_{\mathsf{e}}\right)_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}

with maximal domain

(4.6)

\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})=\left\{z\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\colon I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(\mathsf{v})=0,\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}\right\},

where

\mathcal{H}^{2}\coloneqq\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}H^{2}(0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}})

with

.
We also introduce the

(4.7)

\mathcal{Y}\coloneqq\ell^{2}(\real^{n})\cong\real^{n}.

Notice that for fixed
z\in\mathcal{D}(A_{\max})
, the boundary (or vertex) conditions can be written as

(4.8)

0_{\real{}^{d_{\mathsf{v}}-1}}=I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(\mathsf{v}),\;0=C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}Z^{\prime}(\mathsf{v}),\quad\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},

cf.
(\ref{eq:netcp}b)
and
(\ref{eq:netcp}c)
.

Remark 4.1
.

Define
A_{\mathsf{v}}
as the square matrix that arises from

by inserting an additional row containing only

A_{\mathsf{v}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-1&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\

Furthermore, let

be the square matrix defined by

(4.10)

B_{\mathsf{v}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\ldots&0\\
c_{\mathsf{e}_{1}}(\mathsf{v})&\ldots&c_{\mathsf{e}_{d_{\mathsf{v}}}}(\mathsf{v}),\end{pmatrix}\in\real^{d_{\mathsf{v}}\times d_{\mathsf{v}}}.

It is straightforward that for a fixed
\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}
, equations (
4.8

(4.11)

A_{\mathsf{v}}Z(\mathsf{v})+B_{\mathsf{v}}Z^{\prime}(\mathsf{v})=0_{\real{}^{d_{\mathsf{v}}}},

where the

matrix
\left(A_{\mathsf{v}},B_{\mathsf{v}}\right)
has maximal rank. Thus, our vertex conditions have the form as in
[
9

We now define the boundary operator

by

(4.12)

\begin{split}Gz\coloneqq\left(C(\mathsf{v})^{\top}Z^{\prime}(\mathsf{v})\right)_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}.\end{split}

With these notations, we can finally rewrite (
4.3
) in form of

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}(t)=A_{\max}z(t),&t\in(0,T]\\
Gz(t)=0,&t\in(0,T],\\
z(0)=z_{0},\end{cases}

cf. (
2.5
). Define

(4.14)

A\coloneqq A_{\max}|_{\mathcal{D}(A)},\;\mathcal{D}(A)=\ker G

see Assumptions
2.3
. We claim now that we are in the situation of Section
2
and
3

Proposition 4.2
.

For the spaces
H=\mathcal{H}
and

Proof.

The surjectivity of

[
21
, Prop. 2.3]

[

, Prop. 3.6]

[
21

.
∎

The above proposition implies that the bounded operators

(4.15)

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda}:=\left(G|_{\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})}\right)^{-1}:\mathcal{Y}\to\ker(\lambda-A_{\max})\subset\mathcal{H},\;\lambda\in(0,+\infty)

and

(4.16)

can be defined, and by Remark

It is shown in

(4.18)

Here

We now introduce the boundary operator

(4.19)

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(L)&=\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{2}},\\

Proposition 4.3
.

For the adjoint of the operator

(4.20)

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{Y}

Using the fact that

is self-adjoint and
[
21
, Prop. 2.3]

\displaystyle\langle B\alpha,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{-1},\mathcal{D}(A)}

\displaystyle=\langle(1-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{-1},\mathcal{D}(A)}=\langle\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha,(1-A^{*})h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha,(1-A)h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha,(-A)h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,dx+\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle+\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,dx.

We now integrate by parts in the second term of (

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\left[c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\right]_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\int_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}\left(c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\,dx.

\displaystyle\langle B\alpha,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\left[c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\right]_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}.

Since

\mathsf{G}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}}\left[c_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(x)\right]_{0}^{\ell_{\mathsf{e}}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}}c_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\cdot h_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}h(\mathsf{v})\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}}c_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})\cdot(\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha)^{\prime}_{\mathsf{e}}(\mathsf{v})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}h(\mathsf{v})\cdot\left(G\mathbb{D}_{1}\alpha\right)_{\mathsf{v}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}h(\mathsf{v})\cdot\left(\alpha\right)_{\mathsf{v}}=\langle\alpha,-Lh\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}},

where we have used that, by definition,

\langle B\alpha,h\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{-1},\mathcal{D}(A)}=\langle\alpha,-Lh\rangle_{\mathcal{Y}}.

B^{*}=-L\text{ on }\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{2}}.

∎

From now on we assume that the diffusion coefficients in (

(4.29)

0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k+1}\leq\cdots,\lambda_{k}\to+\infty,

and a set
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
of eigenfunctions such that

(4.30)

Af_{k}=-\lambda_{k}f_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where the functions
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
form a complete orthornomal system in
\mathcal{H}
. By
[
12
, (2.2) and Thm. 4.3]
we have that there exist constants
l_{1},\,l_{2}>0
such that

(4.31)

l_{1}\cdot k^{2}\leq-\lambda_{k}\leq l_{2}\cdot k^{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

If the (algebraic) multiplicity of an eigenvalue is

, then for some
k
,
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k+1}=\cdots=\lambda_{k+d-1}
holds (where

can happen). Therefore, we also consider the strictly increasing subsequence of
(\lambda_{k})
consisting of distinct eigenvalues of
A
, denoted by
(\mu_{k})
; that is, the sequence

(4.32)

0\leq\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<\cdots<\mu_{k}<\cdots,\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
-\mu_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
are the distinct eigenvalues of
A
. Since the multiplicity of eigenvalues are uniformly bounded (see, for example,
[
12
]
) it follows from (
4.31
) that for some constants
\tilde{l}_{1},\,\tilde{l}_{2}>0
,

(4.33)

\tilde{l}_{1}\cdot k^{2}\leq-\mu_{k}\leq\tilde{l}_{2}\cdot{k}^{2},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

It is well-known that

and the corresponding eigenspace is

.

We are going to prove the strong Feller property of the transition semigroup
\mathcal{P}_{t}
defined in (

), that is, the null-controllability of problem (

) in the case
\mathsf{G}
is a tree and the control acts only in all or all but one boundary vertices of degree

.

Definition 4.4
.

In a graph

we call a vertex of degree

, and denote the set of boundary vertices by

A

in a graph is an alternating sequence of vertices and edges

and

For the proof we will need the following notions which we cite from

Definition 4.5

A system

A system

It is straightforward that by Proposition

, system (

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a tree,
c_{\mathsf{e}}=1
. If the covariance matrix
Q=\mathrm{diag}(q_{\mathsf{v}})_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}

for all boundary vertices except for, possibly, one of them, where it is zero, then the transition semigroup

Proof.

We will deduce the result from the exact controllability of the a boundary control problem for a wave equation on a network:

(4.35)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \ddot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)&=z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)-(p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)+1)\cdot z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x),&x\in(0,\ell_{e}),\;t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}\;\;\\
0&=I_{\mathsf{v}}Z(t,\mathsf{v}),\;&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;\\
Q^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\mathsf{v}}(t)&=\sum_{e\in E_{\mathsf{v}}}z^{\prime}_{e}(t,\mathsf{v}),&t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V},\;\;\\
z_{\mathsf{e}}(0,x)&=\dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(0,x)=0&x\in[0,\ell_{\mathsf{e}}],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}.\;\;\end{aligned}\right.

Defining

\Gamma_{1}:=\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma_{1}\}

for
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma
arbitrary and
J^{*}=\emptyset
, we obtain by Proposition
B.5
that

is an ST active set of
\mathsf{G}
in the sense of Definition

, that is of
[

, Def. 3.2]
. Hence, we can apply
[
5
, Thm. 3.14]
to obtain that the system (
4.35
) is exactly controllable on
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Furthermore, for the corresponding operator

there is a strictly positive sequence

with

\tilde{\lambda}_{k}=\lambda_{k}+1,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

such that

(A-Id)f_{k}=-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}f_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
(-\lambda_{k})
are the eigenvalues of
A
, and the eigenfunctions
f_{k},

k\in\mathbb{N}
form a complete orthonormal system in
\mathcal{H}
, see (
4.29
) and (
4.30
).

We will use the method introduced in
[
3
]
to show that (
4.28
), that is (
4.34
) with first equations

(4.36)

\dot{z}_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x)=z_{\mathsf{e}}^{\prime\prime}(t,x)-(p_{\mathsf{e}}(x)+1)\cdot z_{\mathsf{e}}(t,x),\;x\in(0,\ell_{e}),\;t\in(0,T],\;\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}

is null-controllable at any time
\tau>0
, for any

. For this purpose we introduce the spectral data

\alpha_{k}\coloneqq\left(\frac{f_{k}(\mathsf{v})}{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}}\right)_{\mathsf{v}\in\Gamma_{1}}\in\real^{m},\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

where

\Gamma_{1}

By
[

) at time

is equivalent to the fact that

forms an
\mathcal{L}
-basis in

. From
[
2
, Thm. II.5.20]
we obtain that the system

is minimal in

for any
\tau>0
. Furthermore, for the corresponding bi-orthogonal family

(4.40)

Q_{k}^{\prime}\in L^{2}([0,\tau],\real^{m}),\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

with

(4.41)

\langle Q_{\ell},Q^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,\tau];\real^{m})}\coloneqq\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\Gamma_{1}}\int_{0}^{\tau}(Q_{k}(s))_{\mathsf{v}}\cdot Q^{\prime}_{k}(s)_{\mathsf{v}}\,ds=\delta_{k,\ell},\quad k,\ell\in\mathbb{N},

there exist positive constants

and

\left\|Q^{\prime}_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,\tau];\real^{m})}\leq C(\tau)\cdot\left\|E^{\prime}_{k}\right\|_{L^{2}([0,\tau];\mathbb{C}^{m})}\mathrm{e}^{\beta\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

for any

. Since

-basis in

[
2

\frac{\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{k}T}=\langle Q_{k},\tilde{u}^{T}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,T];\real^{m})},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

By Propositions

Define

for

Turning back to the original problem, we have to show the null-controllability of problem (

where

is the biorthogonal family introduced above, and let

(4.48)

y_{\mathsf{v}}(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{q_{\mathsf{v}}}}u_{\mathsf{v}}(s),&\mathsf{v}\in\Gamma_{1},\\
0,&\mathsf{v}\notin\Gamma_{1}\end{cases}

for

.
Let

arbitrary. Then we have

(4.49)

\begin{split}&-\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\sqrt{q_{\mathsf{v}}}\cdot f_{\ell}(\mathsf{v})\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{\ell}(T-s)}y_{\mathsf{v}}(s)\,ds=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\sqrt{q_{\mathsf{v}}}\cdot f_{\ell}(\mathsf{v})\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{\ell}s}y_{\mathsf{v}}(T-s)\,ds\\
&=\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\Gamma_{1}}f_{\ell}(\mathsf{v})\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{\ell}s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{{-\lambda}_{k}T-s}(Q_{k}^{\prime}(s))_{\mathsf{v}}\,ds\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{{-\lambda}_{k}T}\sum_{\mathsf{v}\in\Gamma_{1}}f_{\ell}(\mathsf{v})\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{e}^{-\tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}s}(Q_{k}^{\prime}(s))_{\mathsf{v}}\,ds\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{k}}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{{-\lambda}_{k}T}\sqrt{\tilde{\lambda}_{\ell}}\cdot\langle Q_{\ell},Q^{\prime}_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,T];\real^{m})}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\langle z_{0},f_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{{-\lambda}_{k}T}\cdot\delta_{k,\ell}=\langle z_{0},f_{\ell}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda_{\ell}T}.\end{split}

Thus, we obtained that for any

and

and equivalently, the transition semigroup

.
∎

Example 4.8

(Equilateral Neumann star graph)

Example 4.9

(General Neumann star graph)
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a star graph having
N
edges, with central vertex
\mathsf{v}_{c}
and boundary vertices
\mathsf{v}_{1},\mathsf{v}_{2},\dots,\mathsf{v}_{N}
. Assume that
p_{\mathsf{e}}=0
,
c_{e}=1
, the edge lengths are
\ell_{i}>0
,
i=1,2,\dots,N
,
Q=\mathrm{diag}(q_{\mathsf{v}})_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}
is diagonal and
q_{\mathsf{v}}=0
holds for at least two boundary vertices
\mathsf{v}_{1}
and
\mathsf{v}_{i}
such that

(4.52)

\frac{\ell_{1}}{\ell_{i}}=\frac{2n_{1}+1}{2n_{i}+1}\text{ for some }n_{1},n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}.

Then the transition semigroup
\mathcal{P}_{t}
defined in (
4.27
) is not strong Feller at any time
T>0
.

Proof.

We parametrize the edges on
\left[0,\ell_{i}\right]
,
i=1,2,\dots,N
, so that the
0
’s correspond to the boundary vertices. We are going to define an eigenfunction supported on the edges
1
and
i
with

(4.53)

f_{k,i}(x)=\left(\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}x),0,0,\dots,s_{k,i}\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}x)),0,\dots,0\right).

Since noise is not present in
\mathsf{v}_{1}
and
\mathsf{v}_{i}
, for
z_{0}=f_{k,i}
(
3.13
) can not be satisfied. Thus, by Proposition
3.6
and the equivalence of the null controllability to the strong Feller property we obtain the statement.

By assumption (
4.52
) there are integers
n_{1}
and
n_{i}
such that

(4.54)

\mu_{k}=\frac{(n_{i}+\frac{1}{2})^{2}\pi^{2}}{\ell_{i}^{2}}=\frac{(n_{1}+\frac{1}{2})^{2}\pi^{2}}{\ell_{1}^{2}}.

Let
f_{k,i}
be the normalized eigenfunction as in (
4.53
) with this
\mu_{k}
and with appropriate coefficients
r_{k,i}
,
s_{k,i}
which we specify below. From (
4.54
) follows that

(4.55)

\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\ell_{1})=\cos(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\ell_{i})=0

thus the continuity condition in
\mathsf{v}_{c}
is satisfied for

Let
s_{k,i}=-1
if the parity of
n_{1}
and
n_{i}
is the same, and let
s_{k,i}=1
otherwise. Then

(4.56)

\sin((n_{1}+\frac{1}{2})\pi)+s_{k,i}\sin((n_{i}+\frac{1}{2})\pi)=0

(4.57)

\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\ell_{1})+\sqrt{\mu_{k}}s_{k,i}\sin(\sqrt{\mu_{k}}\ell_{i})=0

is satisfied. The Neumann-condition in the boundary vertices holds by the definition (
4.53
).
Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

are satisfied, moreover,
B\in\mathcal{L}(U,H_{-\frac{1}{2}})
holds. Then the system (
3.1
), that is, (
3.7
) has an invariant measure if and only if
\ker A
is empty or there exists an orthonormal basis
\Lambda:=(f_{n})_{n\in\Gamma}
of
\ker A
such that

(5.1)

\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{n}\|=0,\qquad\forall f_{n}\in\Lambda.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Thm. 11.17.(iii)]
, the existence of the invariant measure is equivalent to the fact that

(5.2)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}dt<+\infty,

where
\HS
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm between the appropriate spaces.

Let
T>0
and let
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
formed by the eigenfunctions of
A
with corresponding eigenvalues
-\lambda_{k}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}\mathbb{T}(t)\right\|^{2}_{\HS(H,U)}dt

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{k}t}dt\cdot\left\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{k}\right\|^{2}

Now, assume that for all orthonormal basis
\Lambda
of
\ker A
there exists
f_{0}\in\Lambda
such that
\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{0}\|>0
. It follows from (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}dt

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{0}t}dt\cdot\left\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{0}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq T\left\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{0}\right\|^{2}.

Hence

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}dt=+\infty.

Consequently, the system (
3.1
) does not have an invariant measure.

For the converse, we assume that there exists an orthonormal basis
\Lambda:=(f_{n})_{n\in\Gamma}
of
\ker A
such that (
5.1
) holds. By completing
\Lambda
to an orthonormal basis
(f_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H
it follows from (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-2\lambda_{j}t}dt\cdot\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda_{j}T}}{2\lambda_{j}}\cdot\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}B^{*}f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2\lambda_{j}T}}{2\lambda_{j}}\cdot\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{*}(1-A)f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\frac{1-e^{-2\lambda_{j}T}}{2\lambda_{j}}\cdot\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{*}(1-\lambda_{j})f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\frac{|1-\lambda_{j}|}{2\lambda_{j}}\cdot\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\lambda_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{*}f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\lambda_{j}>0}\frac{|1-\lambda_{j}|}{2\lambda_{j}}\sum_{\lambda_{j}>0}\|Q^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\lambda_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{D}_{1}^{*}f_{j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}+1\right)\|(1-A)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{D}_{1}Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{\mu_{1}}+1\right)\|(1-A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}_{\HS(U,H)},

where
\mu_{1}
is defined in (
4.32
) and we have used
B\coloneqq(1-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}
. Moreover, it follows from the assumptions that

\|(1-A)^{-\frac{1}{2}}BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{\HS(U,H)}<\infty.

Hence, by taking
T\to+\infty
, we obtain
that (
5.2
) holds, thus the invariant measure exists.
∎

The result
[
13
, Thm. 9.1.1.(iii)]
states that if the transition semigroup is strong Feller, then the invariant measure exists if and only if the semigroup
\mathbb{T}
is exponentially stable. In the following we show that for the quantum graph problem (
4.1
) the assumption being strong Feller can be omitted.

To obtain the result, we observe that by Proposition
4.3
,
B^{*}
can be extended as a bounded operator
B^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{2}},\mathcal{Y})
. Thus, we conclude that

B\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H}_{-\frac{1}{2}}).

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume that
c_{\mathsf{e}}=1
,
\mathsf{e}\in\mathsf{E}
. Then,
for the network boundary noise problem (
4.1
) the invariant measure exists if and only if the semigroup
\mathbb{T}
generated by
A
in (
4.14
) is exponentially stable. In particular, if an invariant measure for (
4.1
) exists, then it is unique.

Proof.

Propositions
4.2
and
4.3
imply that for the problem (
4.1
) the assumptions of the above theorem are satisfied. By
[
22
, Thm. 1]
, if
\ker A
is not empty, it is a 1 dimensional subspace and the corresponding eigenvector can be chosen to be strictly positive. Hence, by Theorem
5.1
, the invariant measure exists if and only if
\ker A=\emptyset
, thus the claim follows.
∎

Appendix A
Regularity of the solution

We again consider system (
4.1
) having mild solution

w(t)=\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t-s)Bd\beta(s):=\mathbb{T}(t)z_{0}+K(t),

with
B\coloneqq(1-A_{-1})\mathbb{D}_{1}
, see (
4.26
). In this section we show that the uniform boundedness assumption on the vertex values of eigenfunctions of Theorem 3.8 in
[
21
]
is unnecessary to obtain the desired regularity of
K(t)
, that is, of
w(t)
.

Proposition A.1
.

For
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, the stochastic convolution process
K(\cdot)
defined in (
4.26
) has a continuous version in
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
. In particular,
w(\cdot)
has an
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
continuous version.

Proof.

Similarily to the argument in the proof of
[
21
, Thm. 3.8]
, by a straightforward modification of
[
14
, Thm. 2.3]
to include the covariance matrix
Q
, we have to show that if
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
, then there exists
\gamma>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\gamma}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})}\,dt<+\infty.

By
[
11
, Thm. 4.1]
,
for
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough,
(1-A)^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+\varepsilon}\mathbb{D}_{1}
is a bounded operator from
\mathcal{Y}
to
\mathcal{H}
. Hence,
B\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H}_{\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}\right)
holds for any
\alpha<\frac{1}{4}
and
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Thus,

(A.1)

\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\gamma}\left\|\mathbb{T}_{-1}(t)BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H}_{\alpha})}\,dt\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\gamma}\left\|(1-A)^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\mathbb{T}(t)(1-A)^{\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}BQ^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|^{2}_{\HS(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H})}\,dt\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\gamma}\left\|(1-A)^{\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}\mathbb{T}(t)\right\|^{2}\,dt\cdot\left\|(1-A)^{\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}B\right\|^{2}_{\HS(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{H})}\cdot\Tr(Q)\\
&\leq c_{T}\cdot\int_{0}^{T}t^{-\gamma}\cdot\frac{1}{t^{1-2\varepsilon}}\,dt,\end{split}

where, in the last inequality we used the analiticity of the semigroup
\mathbb{T}
and the fact that the Hilbert-Schmidt norm of
(1-A)^{\alpha+\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}B
is finite since
\mathcal{Y}
is finite dimensional. The last integral is finite if
\gamma<2\varepsilon
, which implies the statement.
∎

Appendix B
ST active set of a tree

In this section we show how
[
5
, Thm. 3.14]
can be used in the proof of Theorem
4.6
. To keep the paper self-contained, we cite all necessary definitions and results from
[
4
,
5
]
and prove in Proposition
B.5
how their apply for the situation in Theorem
4.6
.

We denote by
\vec{\mathsf{G}}=(\mathsf{V},\vec{\mathsf{E}})
a directed graph obtained by orienting the edges of
\mathsf{G}=(\mathsf{V},\mathsf{E})
. We call a directed graph
acyclic
, if there is no directed circle (directed path having the same vertex as starting and finishing point, see Definition
4.4
) in it. By
[
5
, Lem. 2.2]
, there is always a directed acyclic graph – abbreviated as DAG –,
\vec{\mathsf{G}}=(\mathsf{V},\vec{\mathsf{E}})
based on
\mathsf{G}
.

Definition B.1
.

[
5
, Def. 3.1]

Let
\vec{\mathsf{G}}=(\mathsf{V},\vec{\mathsf{E}})
be a DAG (directed acyclic graph) based on
\mathsf{G}
. Let
U
be a union of directed paths. We say
U
is a
tangle-free (TF) path union
of
\mathsf{G}
if
U
satisfies the conditions:

(1)

The direction of all edges are the same as the direction of all paths they are on.

(2)

All paths
P\in U
are disjoint except for the starting and finishing vertices.

(3)

If a finishing vertex
\mathsf{v}
of a path is the starting vertex of another path, there must be an incoming edge of
\mathsf{v}
that is not a finishing edge, and an outgoing edge of
\mathsf{v}
that is not a starting edge.

(4)

\vec{\mathsf{G}}=\bigcup_{P\in U}P
.

As before,
\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}
denotes the set of all edges which are incident to
\mathsf{v}
. In
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
, we denote by
\mathsf{E}^{+}_{\mathsf{v}}
and
\mathsf{E}^{-}_{\mathsf{v}}
the set of edges which are outgoing and incoming edges, respectively, for
\mathsf{v}
. A
source
is a vertex without incoming edges; a
sink
is a vertex without outgoing edges. We denote the sets of sources and sinks by
\vec{\mathsf{G}}^{+}
and
\vec{\mathsf{G}}^{-}
, respectively.

The following concept is crucial in the proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Definition B.2
.

[
5
, Def. 3.2]

Let
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
be a DAG based on
\mathsf{G}
.
We say that
I^{*}
is a
single-track (ST) active set of vertices
of
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
if

I^{*}=\{\mathsf{v}:\mathsf{v}\in\vec{\mathsf{G}}^{+}\},

J^{*}
is a
single-track (ST) active set of edges
of
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
if

(B.1)

J^{*}=\bigcup_{\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}}\mathsf{E}^{*}_{\mathsf{v}},

whith

\mathsf{E}^{*}_{\mathsf{v}}=\text{all but one elements of }\mathsf{E}^{+}_{\mathsf{v}},\;\mathsf{v}\in\mathsf{V}.

The set
\{I^{*},J^{*}\}
is then called a
ST active set
.

Observe that
I^{*}
is uniquely determined by
\vec{\mathsf{G}}^{+}
. However, ST active set of edges can be defined in several ways. By the following result, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the ST active set of edges and the TF path unions of
\mathsf{G}
.

Lemma B.3
.

[
5
, Lem. 3.3]

Let
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
be a DAG and
I^{*}
its ST active set of vertices. Then each TF path union
U
corresponds to a ST active set of edges
J^{*}
such that for every path in
U
, the index of its starting edge is in

\overline{J}^{*}=\left(\bigcup_{\mathsf{v}\in I^{*}}\mathsf{E}_{\mathsf{v}}\right)\cup J^{*}.

Conversely, from every ST active set of edge indices
J^{*}
one can construct a TF path union
U
, such that for every
\mathsf{e}\in\overline{J}^{*}
,
\mathsf{e}
is the starting edge of a path in
U
.

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a tree. Our upcoming result is based on the following lemma. We recall that we denote the set of boundary vertices – that is, vertices of degree
1
– by
\Gamma
, and
P(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{v}^{\prime})
denotes the uniquely determined path in
\mathsf{G}
with starting point
\mathsf{v}
and finishing point
\mathsf{v}^{\prime}
, see Definition
4.4
.

Lemma B.4
.

[
4
, Lem. 1]

Let
\mathsf{G}
be a tree graph and
I\subset\Gamma
be a subset of its boundary vertices. Then
\mathsf{G}
has a union representation

(B.2)

\mathsf{G}=\bigcup_{\mathsf{v}\in I}P(\mathsf{v},\mathsf{v}^{\prime}),

of disjoint (except the endpoints) paths if and only if

(B.3)

I=\Gamma\text{ or }I=\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma_{1}\}

for an arbitrary
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma
.

Notice that a path union representation (
B.2
) in the tree graph
\mathsf{G}
uniquely determines an orientation of the edges that is a directed graph,
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
, such that each edge is oriented according to the orinetation of the (unique) path it is contained in.

Proposition B.5
.

For a tree graph
\mathsf{G}
and the vertex set
I=\Gamma
or
I=\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma_{1}\}
(with
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma
arbitrary), consider a path union representation (
B.2
) from Lemma
B.4
and the corresponding directed graph
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
. Then (
B.2
) is a TF path union of
\mathsf{G}
and the corresponding ST active set given by Lemma
B.3
is

I^{*}=I,\quad J^{*}=\emptyset.

Furthermore,
I^{*}\subset\Gamma
,
J^{*}=\emptyset
is an ST active set if and only if
I^{*}=\Gamma
or
I^{*}:=\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma_{1}\}
for some
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

Since
\mathsf{G}
is a tree,
\vec{\mathsf{G}}
is a DAG based on
\mathsf{G}
. It is straightforward that (1), (2) and (4) of Definition
B.1
are satisfied for the path union (
B.2
). Since all paths have starting points in boundary vertices, no interior vertex – that is, a vertex of degree at least
2
– is a starting point of a path, hence (3) of Definition
B.1
is also satisfied. This fact also implies, that each interior vertex has (at least one) incoming edge, and in the case
I=\Gamma\setminus\{\gamma_{1}\}
,
\gamma_{1}
is a sink. Thus, the sources of
\vec{G}
are exactly the elements of
I
, that is,
I^{*}=I
.

Otherwise, since the paths are disjoint, and no interior vertex is a starting point for a path, all interior vertices as well as the elements of
I
have exactly one outgoing edge. Thus, by (
B.1
),
J^{*}=\emptyset
.

For the last statement observe that by Lemma
B.3
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the ST active sets and the TF path unions of
\mathsf{G}
such that the starting deges of the paths are exactly the elements of
\overline{J}^{*}
. Since
I^{*}\subset\Gamma
,
J^{*}=\emptyset
, we have

\overline{J}^{*}=\bigcup_{\mathsf{v}\in I^{*}}\{\mathsf{e}_{\mathsf{v}}\},

where
\mathsf{e}_{\mathsf{v}}
is the unique edge incident to
\mathsf{v}\in I^{*}
. Hence, the paths in the TF path union corresponding to
\{I^{*},J^{*}\}
have starting points only in
I^{*}
. By Lemma
B.4
, this can happen if and only
I^{*}
has the given form.
∎

Transitive ( q − 1 ) (q-1) -fold packings of PG n ⁡ ( q ) \pg_{n}(q)
Daniel R. Hawtin

Abstract
A t t -fold packing of a projective space PG n ⁡ ( q ) \pg_{n}(q) is a collection 𝒫 \mathcal{P} of line-spreads such that each line of PG n ⁡ ( q ) \pg_{n}(q) occurs in precisely t t spreads in 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . A t t -fold packing 𝒫 \mathcal{P} is transitive if a subgroup of P ​ Γ ​ L n + 1 ⁡ ( q ) \PGaL_{n+1}(q) preserves and acts transitively on 𝒫 \mathcal{P} . We give a construction for a transitive ( q − 1 ) (q-1) -fold packing of PG n ⁡ ( q ) \pg_{n}(q) , where q = 2 k q=2^{k} , for any odd positive integers n n and k k , such that n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 . This generalises a construction of Baker from 1976 for the case q = 2 q=2 .

Introduction

In 1976, Baker
[
1
]
constructed a partition of the set of lines of the projective geometry
\pg_{n}(2)
into spreads. (Note that in this paper by a
spread
we mean a set of lines of a projective space inducing a partition of the point-set of the geometry.) Such a construction is often refered to as a
parallelism
or
packing
. Some applications of Baker’s construction include: a description of the Preparata codes
[
2
]
, the construction of a family of antipodal distance-regular graphs
[
5
]
, and the determination of the chromatic number of the Grassmann graph
J_{q}(n+1,2)
(see
[
4
, Section 3.5.1]
).

As well as Baker’s construction, there are several other results results concerning infinite families of packings in projective spaces. In particular, Denniston
[
6
]
proved that packings exist in
\pg_{3}(q)
for all prime powers
q
. Moreover, Penttila and Williams
[
9
]
gave a construction for two inequivalent regular packings of
\pg_{3}(q)
for each
q\equiv{2\pmod{3}}
, where a packing is regular if each of its constituent spreads are regular (Desarguesian). In higher dimensions, Beutelspacher
[
3
]
showed that packings exist in
\pg_{n}(q)
if
n=2^{i+1}-1
and
i
is any positive integer. See
[
7
]
for a fairly comprehensive survey of packings in projective spaces.

As a generalisation of a packing, we are interested here in the concept of a
t
-fold packing
, defined to be a collection
\mathcal{P}
of spreads of
\pg_{n}(q)
such that every line is contained in precisely
t
elements of
\mathcal{P}
. Further, we say that a
t
-fold packing
\mathcal{P}
is
transitive
if there exists a subgroup of
\PGaL_{n}(q)
leaving
\mathcal{P}
invariant and acting transitively on the spreads of
\mathcal{P}
. The main result of this paper, stated below, is a generalisation of
[
1
]
, giving Baker’s construction when
q=2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n
and
k
be odd positive integers, with
n\geqslant 3
, let
q=2^{k}
, and let
\mathcal{P}
be as in (
2.2
). Then
\mathcal{P}
is a transitive
(q-1)
-fold packing of
\pg_{n}(q)
.

The only currently known
t
-fold packing of
\pg_{n}(q)
that the author is aware of is a
5
-fold packing of
\pg_{3}(2)
, a description of which can be found in a comment of John Bamberg in a SymOmega blog post

(in fact, the current author has taken the terminology “

\alt_{7}\leqslant\PGL_{4}(2)

2
The construction

Let
k
and
n
be odd positive integers, with
n\geqslant 3
, and let
q=2^{k}
. Let
U=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, viewed as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of rank
n
, let
W=\langle w\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with
w\notin U
, and let
V=U\oplus W\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n+1}
. We take
V
to be the underlying vector space of the projective geometry
\pg_{n}(q)
. The notation
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
refers to both the set of non-zero elements of the field and the multiplicative group of the field.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
. Then we define
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
to be the set of all those lines
\ell
of
\pg_{n}(q)
such that there exists a basis
x+x_{0}w,y+y_{0}w
for
\ell
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, for which

\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}=\alpha.

(2.1)

Finally, we define

\mathcal{P}=\{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}\}.

(2.2)

Note that the value of left hand side of (
2.1
) depends on the particular choice of basis for
\ell
. However, the next result shows that (
2.1
) is invariant under a determinant
1
change of basis of
\ell
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
A\in\SL_{2}(q)
, the equation (
2.1
) is invariant under the map

\begin{bmatrix}x+x_{0}w\\
y+y_{0}w\end{bmatrix}\mapsto A\begin{bmatrix}x+x_{0}w\\
y+y_{0}w\end{bmatrix},

on the basis for
\ell
.

Proof.

Since
\det A=1
, we have that

\det\left(A\begin{bmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{bmatrix}\right)+\det\left(A\begin{bmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{bmatrix}\right)^{q+1}=\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1},

and the result holds.
∎

The next three lemmas show, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
, that the set
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
is in fact a spread, the first being required to prove Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\alpha,u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
, there exists precisely one
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
ux^{q}+u^{q}x+\lambda u^{q+1}+\alpha
has roots for
x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

Proof.

Replacing
x
by
ux
and dividing by
u^{q+1}
we obtain the polynomial

x^{q}+x+\lambda+\alpha u^{-(q+1)}.

The polynomial
f(x)=x^{q}+x
is a linearised polynomial with set of roots precisely
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus the image
Y
of
f
is a codimension
1
subspace of
U
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Since
n
and
k
are both odd, we have that
\tr(1)=1
, where
\tr
is the absolute trace function
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Applying
[
8
, Corollary 3.79 and Theorem 3.80]
, it follows that
x^{q}+x+1
factors into
q/2
irreducible polynomials, each of degree
2
, and hence
1\notin Y
. Thus the set
\{Y+\lambda\mid\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
of cosets of
Y
forms a partition of
U
and
\alpha u^{-(q+1)}
lies in precisely one such coset. Thus the result holds.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
u,\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
then
u
is contained in a unique element of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Setting
x=u
and
x_{0}=0
in Equation
2.1
, and noting that
y_{0}^{q}=y_{0}
, we consider the following:

uy^{q}+u^{q}y+u^{q+1}y_{0}^{2}=\alpha.

By Lemma
2.2
, there is precisely one value of
y_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that this equation has solutions for
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. If
(y,y_{0})=(v,v_{0})
gives one solution, then
(y,y_{0})=(\lambda u+v,v_{0})
, for
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, give the remaining
q-1
. It follows that
\ell=\langle u,v+v_{0}w\rangle
is the unique element of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
containing
u
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
and let
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Then
u+w
is contained in precisely one element of
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

Setting
x=u,x_{0}=1
and
y_{0}=0
in Equation (
2.1
) gives

uy^{q}+u^{q}y+y^{q+1}=\alpha.

Since
(u+y)^{q+1}=u^{q+1}+uy^{q}+u^{q}y+y^{q+1}
, it follows that
(u+y)^{q+1}=u^{q+1}+\alpha
. The fact that
n
is odd implies that
q+1
is coprime to
q^{n}-1
. It follows that
(q+1)
-st roots are unique in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and we have that there is a unique
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
satisfying the above equation, given by

y=\left(u^{q+1}+\alpha\right)^{1/(q+1)}+u.

This completes the proof.
∎

The following three lemmas demonstrate that
\mathcal{P}
is a transitive
(q-1)
-fold packing.

Lemma 2.5
.

If
x+x_{0}w,y+y_{0}w
is a basis for a line
\ell
of
\pg_{n}(q)
then

\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}\neq 0.

Proof.

If By Lemma
2.1
, we can assume that
x_{0}=0
and that
y_{0}=0
or
1
. If
y_{0}=0
then, since
xy^{q}+x^{q}y=0
if and only if
x
and
y
are linearly dependent, the result holds in this case. Suppose
y_{0}=1
and that the above inequality is instead an equality. Then we have

xy^{q}+x^{q}y+x^{q+1}=0.

Since
(x+y)^{q+1}=y^{q+1}+xy^{q}+x^{q}y+x^{q+1}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the above becomes
(x+y)^{q+1}=y^{q+1}
. However, since
n
is odd, implying that
q+1
is coprime to
q^{n}-1
and
(q+1)
-st roots are unique in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, this implies that
x=0
, which contradicts the assumptions on the basis for
\ell
. Thus the result holds.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Every line of
\pg_{n}(q)
is contained in
q-1
elements of
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
\ell
be a line of
\pg_{n}(q)
and
x+x_{0}w,y+y_{0}w
be a basis for
\ell
, where
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. By Lemma
2.5
, the left hand side of (
2.1
) is never
0
. By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices for us to show that

\begin{vmatrix}\lambda x&\lambda x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}\lambda x&\lambda x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}\neq\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\setminus\{0,1\}
. Suppose, to the contrary, that equality holds in the previous equation. Then, since
\lambda^{2}+1=(\lambda+1)^{2}
, we can rearrange the above to give

(\lambda+1)\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+(\lambda+1)^{2}\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}=\begin{vmatrix}(\lambda+1)x&(\lambda+1)x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}(\lambda+1)x&(\lambda+1)x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}=0.

Since
(\lambda+1)(x+x_{0}w),y+y_{0}w
is also a basis for
\ell
, this contradicts Lemma
2.5
, completing the proof.
∎

In the next lemma we consider the action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
on
V
given by
(x+x_{0}w)^{\beta}=\beta x+x_{0}w
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
,
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
on
U
induces a transitive action on
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
, let
\ell\in\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
and let
x+x_{0}w,y+y_{0}w
be a basis for
\ell
such that (
2.1
) holds, where
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
and
x_{0},y_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Under the map
(x,x_{0},y,y_{0})\mapsto(\beta x,x_{0},\beta y,y_{0})
the left hand side of (
2.1
) becomes

\begin{vmatrix}\beta x&(\beta x)^{q}\\
\beta y&(\beta y)^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\begin{vmatrix}\beta x&x_{0}\\
\beta y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}=\beta^{q+1}\begin{vmatrix}x&x^{q}\\
y&y^{q}\end{vmatrix}+\beta^{q+1}\begin{vmatrix}x&x_{0}\\
y&y_{0}\end{vmatrix}^{q+1}=\alpha\beta^{q+1}.

Hence
\ell^{\beta}
is in
\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}
, where
\gamma=\alpha\beta^{q+1}
. Since
n
and
k
are odd, it follows that
q+1
and
q^{n}-1
are coprime and
\gamma
ranges over all values of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
as
\beta
does. Thus the result holds.
∎

We now prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
show that
\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}
is a spread for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{\times}
. By Lemma
2.6
, we have that
\mathcal{P}
is a
(q-1)
-fold packing. Finally, Lemma
2.7
shows that
\mathcal{P}
is transitive.
∎

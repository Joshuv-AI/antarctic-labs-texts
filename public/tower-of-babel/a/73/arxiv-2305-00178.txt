Algebraic valuation ring extensions as limits of complete intersection algebras
Dorin Popescu

Abstract
. We show that an algebraic immediate valuation ring extension of characteristic p > 0 p>0 is a filtered union of complete intersection algebras of finite type. Key words : immediate extensions, pseudo convergent sequences, pseudo limits, smooth morphisms, complete intersection algebras. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13F30, Secondary 13A18,13F20,13B40.



Introduction

B. Antieau and R. Datta have recently proven a positive characteristic analogue
[
1
, Theorem 4.1.1]
of Zariski’s theorem
[
17
]
. It says that every perfect valuation ring of characteristic
p>0
is a filtered union of its smooth
{\bf F}_{p}
-subalgebras. This result is an application of
[
14
, Theorem 1.2.5]
which relies on some results from
[
2
]
. Also E. Elmanto and M. Hoyois proved that an absolute integrally closed valuation ring of residue field of characteristic
p>0
is a filtered union of its regular finitely generated
{\bf Z}
-subalgebras (see
[
1
, Corollary 4.2.4]
). We remind that a filtered direct limit (in other words a filtered colimit) is a limit indexed by a small category that is filtered (see
[
13
, 002V]
or
[
13
, 04AX]
). A filtered union is a filtered direct limit in which all objects are subobjects of the final colimit, so that in particular all the transition arrows are monomorphisms.

It is well known that if the fraction field extension of an immediate extension
V\subset V^{\prime}
is finite and
p>0
then
V^{\prime}
may fail to be a filtered direct limit of smooth
V
-algebras as shows
[
9
, Example 3.13]
inspired from
[
6
]
(see also
[
9
, Remark 6.10]
). An inclusion
V\subset V^{\prime}
of valuation rings is an
immediate extension
if it is local as a map of local rings and induces isomorphisms between the value groups and the residue fields of
V
and
V^{\prime}
.
After seeing
[
4
, Theorem 6.2]
(see also
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
) we understood that in general we should expect that
V^{\prime}
is a filtered union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras. In the Noetherian case a morphism of rings is a filtered direct limit of smooth algebras iff it is a regular morphism (see
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
).

A
complete intersection

V
-algebra essentially of finite type is a local
V
-algebra of type
C/(P)
, where
C
is a localization of a polynomial
V
-algebra of finite type and
P
is a regular sequence of elements of
C
. Theorem
1
stated below says that
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its
V
-subalgebras of type
C/(P)
. Since
V^{\prime}
is local it is enough to say that
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its
V
-subalgebras of type
T_{h}/(P)
,
T
being a polynomial
V
-algebra of finite type,
0\not=h\in T
and
P
is a regular sequence of elements of
T
. Clearly,
T_{h}
is a smooth
V
-algebra and in fact it is enough to say that
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its
V
-subalgebras of type
G/(P)
, where
G
is a smooth
V
-algebra of finite type and
P
is a regular sequence of elements of
G
. Conversely, a
V
-algebra of such type
G/(P)
has the form
T_{h}/(P)
for some
T,h,P
using
[
12
, Theorem 2.5]
. By abuse we understand by a
complete intersection

V
-algebra of finite type a
V
-algebra of such type
G/(P)
, or
T_{h}/(P)
which are not assumed to be flat over
V
.

The goal
of this paper is to establish the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of a valuation ring
V
and
K\subset K^{\prime}
the fraction field extension. If
K^{\prime}/K
is algebraic then
V^{\prime}
is a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras of finite type.

1.
Algebraic immediate extensions of valuation rings

An inclusion
V\subset V^{\prime}
of valuation rings is an
immediate extension
if it is local as a map of local rings and induces isomorphisms between the value groups and the residue fields of
V
and
V^{\prime}
.

Let
\lambda
be a fixed limit ordinal and
v=\{v_{i}\}_{i<\lambda}
a sequence of elements in
V
indexed by the ordinals
i
less than
\lambda
. Then
v
is
pseudo convergent
if

\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})<\mathrm{val}(v_{i^{\prime}}-v_{i^{\prime\prime}})\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i<i^{\prime}<i^{\prime\prime}<\lambda

(see
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
).
A
pseudo limit
of
v
is an element
w\in V
with

\mathrm{val}(w-v_{i})<\mathrm{val}(w-v_{i^{\prime}})\ \ \mbox{(that is,}\ \ \mathrm{val}(w-v_{i})=\mathrm{val}(v_{i}-v_{i^{\prime}}))\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
. We say that
v
is

(1)

algebraic
if some
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
;

(2)

transcendental
if each
f\in V[T]
satisfies
\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))=\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i^{\prime}}))
for large enough
i<i^{\prime}<\lambda
.

The following lemma is a variant of Ostrowski (
[
6
, page 371, IV and III]
, see also
[
11
, (II,4), Lemma 8]
).

Lemma 2
.

(Ostrowski) Let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
be any elements of an ordered abelian group
G
,
\lambda
a limit ordinal and
let
\{\gamma_{s}\}_{s<\lambda}
be a well-ordered, monotone increasing set of elements of G, without
a last element. Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}
, be distinct integers. Then there exists
an ordinal
\nu<\lambda
such that
\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s}
are different for all
s>\nu
. In particular,
there exists an integer
1\leq r\leq m
such that

\beta_{i}+t_{i}\gamma_{s}>\beta_{r}+t_{r}\gamma_{s}

for all
i\not=r
and
s>\nu
.

Proof.

The known proof from the quoted papers gives the second statement even when the integers
t_{i}
are not necessarily positive. Then apply this statement iteratively to get the first statement.

Lemma 3
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of valuation rings,
K\subset K^{\prime}
its fraction field extension and
(v_{i})_{i<\lambda}
an algebraic pseudo convergent sequence in
V
, which has a pseudo limit
x
in
V^{\prime}
, but no pseudo limit in
K
. Set

x_{i}=(x-v_{i})/(v_{i+1}-v_{i})
. Let
s\in{\bf N}
be the
minimal degree of the polynomials
f\in V[Y]
such that

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
and
g\in V[Y]
a polynomial with
\operatorname{deg}g<s
. Then there exist
d\in V\setminus\{0\}
and
u\in V[x_{i}]
for some
i<\lambda
with
g(x)=du
and
\mathrm{val}(u)=0
.

Proof.

In the Taylor expansion
1
1

1

The polynomials
D^{(n)}f\in R[Y]
for
f\in R[Y]
, the so called Hasse-Schmidt derivatives, make sense for any ring
R
: indeed, one constructs the Taylor expansion in the universal case
R={\bf Z}[a_{0},\dots,a_{\operatorname{deg}f}]
by using the equality
n!\cdot(D^{(n)}f)=f^{(n)}
and verifying over
\Frac(R)
.

\textstyle g(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\operatorname{deg}g}(D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})\cdot(x-v_{i})^{n}\quad\text{with}\quad D^{(n)}g\in V[Y]

the values
\mathrm{val}((D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})\cdot(x-v_{i})^{n})
are pairwise distinct for all
n\leq\operatorname{deg}g
and every large enough
i
because
\mathrm{val}(x-v_{i})=\mathrm{val}(v_{i+1}-v_{i})
increases,
\operatorname{deg}(D^{(n)}g)<s
and so
\mathrm{val}((D^{(n)}g)(v_{i}))
is constant for all
n\leq\operatorname{deg}g
and
i
large (we could also apply Lemma
2
).
We cannot have

\mathrm{val}((D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})\cdot(x-v_{i})^{n})<\mathrm{val}(g(v_{i}))

for some
n>0
and
i
large because otherwise we get from above that

\mathrm{val}(g(x))=\mathrm{val}((D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})\cdot(x-v_{i})^{n})

increases, which is false.
It follows that

\mathrm{val}((D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})\cdot(x-v_{i})^{n})>\mathrm{val}(g(v_{i}))

for all
n>0
and
i
large and so

g(x)=g(v_{i})+\sum_{n=1}^{\operatorname{deg}g}(D^{(n)}g)(v_{i})(v_{i+1}-v_{i})^{n}x_{i}^{n}\in g(v_{i})(1+{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}\cap V[x_{i}]),

Lemma 4
.

Let
V\subset V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of valuation rings,
K\subset K^{\prime}
its fraction field extension and
(v_{i})_{i<\lambda}
an algebraic pseudo convergent sequence in
K
, which has a pseudo limit
x
in
V^{\prime}
, algebraic over
V
, but no pseudo limit in
K
. Assume that
h=
Irr
(x,K)
is from
V[X]
. Then

(1)

\mathrm{val}(h(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(h(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
,

(2)

if
h
has minimal degree among the polynomials
f\in V[X]
such that

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
, then
V^{\prime\prime}=V^{\prime}\cap K(x)
is a filtered union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras.

Proof.

The first part follows from
[
5
, Corollary 5.5]
.
Now, assume that
\operatorname{deg}h
is minimal among
f
having the property from (2). As in Lemma
3
we consider
(x_{i})_{i<\lambda}
and note that for a polynomial
g\in V[X]
of degree
<\operatorname{deg}h
we have
g(x)=du
for some
d\in V\subset\{0\}
and
u\in V[x_{i}]
for some
i<\lambda
with
\mathrm{val}(u)=0
. An element of
V^{\prime\prime}
has the form

g(x)/t
for some
g\in V[X]
with
\operatorname{deg}g<\operatorname{deg}h
and
t\in V^{\setminus}\{0\}
such that
\mathrm{val}(g(x))\geq\mathrm{val}(t)
.
It follows that

g(x)/t=(d/t)u\in V[x_{i}]_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}\cap V[x_{i}]}
, where
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
is the maximal ideal of
V^{\prime}
.

Thus
V^{\prime\prime}
is the union of
(V[x_{i}]_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}\cap V[x_{i}]})_{i<\lambda}
, which is
filtered increasing as in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
, or
[
10
, Lemma 15]
.
We have
V[x_{i}]\cong V[X_{i}]/(h_{i})
, where
h_{i}
is defined below.
Set
g_{i}=h(v_{i}+(v_{i+1}-v_{i})X_{i})\in V[X_{i}]
. By construction
x_{i}=(x-v_{i})/(v_{i+1}-v_{i})
and so
g_{i}(x_{i})=h(x)=0
. As
h
is irreducible in
K[X]
we get
g_{i}
irreducible in
K[X_{i}]
too because it is obtained from
h
by a linear transformation. So
K[x_{i}]\cong K[X_{i}]/(g_{i})
. We have
g_{i}=uh_{i}
for some primitive polynomial
h_{i}
of
V[X_{i}]
and a nonzero constant
u
of
K
.
Then
V^{\prime\prime}
is a filtered union of its complete intersections
V
-subalgebras.

Lemma 6
.

Let
B
be a complete intersection algebra over a ring
A
and
C
a complete intersection algebra over
B
. Then
C
is a complete intersection algebra over
A
.

Proof.

Suppose that
B\cong A[X]/(f)
, where
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
and
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
is a regular sequence of elements in
A[X]
. Similarly,
C\cong B[Y]/(g)
, where
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
and
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{s})
is a regular sequence of elements in
B[Y]
. Since
A[X,Y]
is flat over
A[X]
we see that
f
is a regular sequence of elements in
A[X,Y]
. Note that
g_{i}
is a residue class modulo
fA[X,Y]
of a polynomial
G_{i}\in A[X,Y]
,
1\leq i\leq s
.

We claim that
f,G
form a regular sequence of elements of
A[X,Y]
. It is enough to see that
G
is a regular sequence of elements of
A[X,Y]/(f)\cong B[Y]
, which is true by assumption.
So,
C\cong A[X,Y]/(f,G)
is a complete intersection
A
-algebra.

Lemma 7
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of a valuation ring
V
,
K\subset K^{\prime}
their fraction field extension and
K^{\prime}=K(x)
for some
x\in V^{\prime}
,
x\not\in K
algebraic over
K
. Then there exists
K_{1}\subset K^{\prime}
,
K_{1}\not=K^{\prime}
containing
K
such that
x
is a pseudo limit of an algebraic pseudo-convergent sequence
v=(v_{j})_{j<\lambda}
in
V_{1}=V^{\prime}\cap K_{1}
having
x
as a pseudo limit and for a polynomial
h\in V_{1}[Y]
with minimal degree among the polynomials
f\in V_{1}[Y]
with

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
there exists no
pseudo limit of
v
in
K_{1}(h(x))
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 1]
there exists an algebraic pseudo-convergent sequence
v=(v_{j})_{j<\lambda}
in
V
having
x
as a pseudo limit but no pseudo limits in
K
. Let
h\in V[Y]
be a polynomial with minimal degree among the polynomials
f\in V[Y]
such that

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
. Note that
\operatorname{deg}h>1
because
(v_{j})_{j<\lambda}
has no pseudo limits in
K
(see the proof of

[
3
, Theorem 3]
) and so
[K^{\prime}:K(h(x))]=\operatorname{deg}h>1
.

If
v
has no pseudo limits in
K(h(x))
then we may take
K_{1}=K
. If
v
has a pseudo limit in
K(h(x))
then we may complete it to a pseudo convergent sequence
{\tilde{v}}={\tilde{v}}_{j<{\tilde{\lambda}}}
over
{\tilde{V}}=V^{\prime}\cap K(h(x))
with
x
as a pseudo limit, which has no pseudo limit in
K(h(x))
using again

[
3
, Theorem 1]
. As above there exists a polynomial
{\tilde{h}}\in{\tilde{V}}[Y]
which has minimal degree among all polynomials
f\in{\tilde{V}}[Y]
such that
\mathrm{val}(f({\tilde{v}}_{j}))<\mathrm{val}(f({\tilde{v}}_{j+1}))
for large
j<{\tilde{\lambda}}
.

For our proof it is enough to change
K
by
K(h(x))
,
h
by
\tilde{h}
and
v
by
\tilde{v}
. If the new
v
has a pseudo limit in the new
K(h(x))
then we proceed as above finding a new
\tilde{v}
and a new
\tilde{h}
and again we may change
K
by
K({\tilde{h}}(x))
and
v,h
with
{\tilde{v}},{\tilde{h}}
. Proceeding in this way we arrive in some steps (
K^{\prime}/K
is finite) in the case when
v
has no pseudo limit in
K(h(x))
. It is enough to take
K_{1}
to be this new
K
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
V^{\prime}
be an immediate extension of a valuation ring
V
,
K\subset K^{\prime}
their fraction field extension and
K^{\prime}=K(x)
for some
x\in V^{\prime}
algebraic over
K
. Then
V^{\prime}
is
a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras.

Proof.

We may assume
K\not=K^{\prime}
. By Lemma
7
there exists
K_{1}\subset K^{\prime}
,
K_{1}\not=K^{\prime}
containing
K
such that
x
is a pseudo limit of an algebraic pseudo-convergent sequence
v=(v_{j})_{j<\lambda}
in
V_{1}=V^{\prime}\cap K_{1}
and for a polynomial
h\in V_{1}[Y]
with minimal degree among the polynomials
f\in V_{1}[Y]
with

\mathrm{val}(f(v_{i}))<\mathrm{val}(f(v_{j}))
for large
i<j<\lambda
there exists no
pseudo limit of
v
in
K_{1}(h(x))
.
Set
g=
Irr
(x,K_{1}(h(x)))
. By Lemma
4
(1) we have
\operatorname{deg}h\leq\operatorname{deg}g
. But
x
is a root of
g^{\prime}=h(Y)-h(x)\in K_{1}(h(x))[Y]
and so
g
divides
g^{\prime}
. Thus
g
is
g^{\prime}
multiplied with a unit of
V^{\prime\prime}=V^{\prime}\cap K_{1}(h(x))
because
\operatorname{deg}g^{\prime}=\operatorname{deg}h\leq\operatorname{deg}g\leq\operatorname{deg}g^{\prime}
.

Applying Lemma
4
(2), we see that
V^{\prime}
is
a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V^{\prime\prime}
-subalgebras.
Use induction on
[K^{\prime}:K]
, case
K=K^{\prime}
being trivial. By induction hypothesis
V^{\prime\prime}
is a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras.
Therefore,
V^{\prime}
is also a filtered
union of its complete intersection
V
-subalgebras using Lemma
6
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Firstly assume
K^{\prime}/K
is finite, let us say
K^{\prime}=K(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. Set
V_{i}=V^{\prime}\cap K(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
,
1\leq i<n
and
V_{0}=V
,
V_{n}=V^{\prime}
.
Then
V_{i+1}
is a filtered union of its complete intersection
V_{i}
-algebras for all

8

V^{\prime}

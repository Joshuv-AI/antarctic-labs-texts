1.
Introduction

The main goal of the paper is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{k+3+\alpha}
boundary, where
k
is a non-negative integer and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Let
B(z,w)
be the Bergman kernel for
D
. Then for every
w_{0}\in D
,
B(\cdot,w_{0})\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.

Earlier in her paper
[
Lig84
]
, E. Ligocka showed that if
\Omega
has
C^{k+4}
boundary for non-negative integers
k
, then
B(\cdot,w_{0})\in C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
. Hence Theorem
1.1
is an improvement and generalization of Ligocka’s result to Hölder spaces.

The study of boundary regularity properties of the Bergman projection and Bergman kernel is of fundamental importance in several complex variables, and the subject has found major applications in the theory of biholomorphic mappings and complex geometry, among many other fields. We mention here some brief history for the results on strictly pseudoconvex domains. When the boundary is
C^{\infty}
, Kerzman
[
Ker72
]
used the theory of
\overline{\partial}
-Neumann problem to show that the Bergman kernel function
B(z,w)
is
C^{\infty}\times C^{\infty}(\overline{D}\times\overline{D}\setminus\Delta_{bD})
, where
\Delta_{bD}:=\{(z,w)\in bD\times bD,z=w\}
.
Soon after, C. Fefferman in his seminal paper
[
Fef74
]
gave a description of the behavior of the Bergman kernel
(z,w)\in bD\times bD
near its singular set
\Delta_{bD}
, and as an application he proved the now classical Fefferman’s mapping theorem, which states that a biholomorphic mapping
F:D_{1}\to D_{2}
between two bounded
C^{\infty}
strictly pseudoconvex domains
D_{1},D_{2}
extends to a
C^{\infty}
diffeomorphism
\widetilde{F}:\overline{D_{1}}\to\overline{D_{2}}
. Fefferman’s proof was based on the deep properties of the Bergman kernel and Bergman metric on strictly pseudoconvex domains. The analysis however was very difficult and nearly impossible to generalize to other cases. Later on Webster
[
Web79
]
and Ligocka-Bell
[
BL80
]
independently found conditions on the boundary behavior of the Bergman kernel that can imply the
C^{\infty}
extension of biholomorphic mappings, and consequently they were able to significantly simplify Fefferman’s proof.

Phong-Stein
[
PS77
]
and Ahern-Schneider
[
AS79
]
independently proved the Hölder estimates for the Bergman projection. In both work the boundary is assumed to be
C^{\infty}
and the proof is based on the work of C.Fefferman
[
Fef74
]
and L. Boutet de Monvel and J. Sjöstrand
[
BdMS76
]
.
Later on, Ligocka
[
Lig84
]
constructed a non-orthogonal projection operator with explicit kernels that “approximates” the Bergman projection operator, and she used it to prove the Hölder estimates assuming boundary is
C^{k+4}
. Ligocka based off her construction on a similar work done by Kerzman-Stein
[
KS78
]
for the Szegö projection on
C^{\infty}
strictly pseudoconvex domains.
The idea is to use the symmetry of the Levi polynomial for the defining function to get a third order cancellation, which then allows one to estimate the singular integrals (see Proposition
3.1
). It is also worthwhile to mention that the method of Kerzman-Stein-Ligocka has been used in a number of subsequent works, for example in
[
LS12
]
and
[
LS13
]
.
For a detailed exposition of the work by Ligocka-Bell and Kerzman-Stein-Ligocka, we refer the reader to the book by M.Range
[
Ran86
, Chapter VII]
.

In this paper we shall give a variant of Ligocka’s method which allows us to prove the estimates in Hölder spaces. Our method also has the advantage that the term on the right-hand side of our integral equation behaves much nicer than the one used by Ligocka, which we now explain.
Denote the Bergman projection on
D
by
\mathcal{P}
. It is a standard fact that for
w_{0}\in D
, one can write
B(\cdot,w_{0})=\mathcal{P}\varphi
, where
\varphi=\varphi_{w_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
(see Lemma
2.4
.)
Ligocka showed that
\mathcal{P}\varphi
satisfies an integral equation of the form

(1.1)

(I+\mathcal{K})\mathcal{P}\varphi=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\varphi.

Here
\mathcal{L}
is a non-orthogonal projection operator mapping
L^{2}(\Omega)
into
H^{2}(\Omega)
, the
L^{2}
Bergman space,
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
is the adjoint operator of
\mathcal{L}
, and
\mathcal{K}:=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}-\mathcal{L}
. It was proved in
[
Lig84
]
that if the boundary is
C^{k+4}
, then
\mathcal{K}
is a compact operator mapping
C^{k}(\overline{D})
into
C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
, and
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
map
C^{k+1}(\overline{D})
(in fact only need derivatives of order
k
being Lipschitz continuous) into
C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
. Hence in particular
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\varphi\in C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
. Applying Fredholm theory to the integral equation (
1.1
) then shows that
\mathcal{P}\varphi\in C^{k+\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
.

For our proof we shall use the same operators
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\ast},\mathcal{K}
, but instead of considering the integral equation of
\mathcal{P}\varphi
, we show that the following integral equation holds for the function
\mathcal{P}\varphi-\varphi

(1.2)

(I+\mathcal{K})(\mathcal{P}\varphi-\varphi)=R(\mathcal{P}\varphi-\varphi),

where
R
is some operator that maps
P\varphi-\varphi
to a
C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
function, assuming boundary is only
C^{3}
.
This is in contrast to the right-hand side of (
1.1
), where the regularity of
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}\varphi
depends on the regularity of the boundary and the estimate is much more complicated.

Using (
1.2
), Theorem
1.1
is then an easy consequence of the following compactness result and Fredholm theory.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{k+3+\alpha}
boundary, where
k
is a non-negative integer and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Then
\mathcal{K}
is a bounded operator from
C^{k}(\overline{D})
to
C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.

We remark that Proposition
1.2
is the main estimate of the paper and takes up the majority of the proof.

Using Proposition
1.2
we can also prove the following theorem for the Bergman projection. Similar
result has been obtained by Ligocka under the assumption that the boundary is
C^{k+4}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{k+3+\alpha}
boundary, where
k
is a non-negative integer and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. For
0<\beta\leq 1
, the Bergman projection
\mathcal{P}
for the domain
D
defines a bounded operator from
C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
to
C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{\beta}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.

In the special case
\alpha=1
, we recover Ligocka’s result.
Note that Theorem
1.1
can also be obtained as a consequence of Theorem
1.3
, by the fact that
B=P\varphi
and setting
\beta=1
in Theorem
1.3
. However we shall give independent proofs of the two theorems based on Proposition
1.2
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we prove a simple estimate for Hörmander’s
\overline{\partial}
solution operator on pseudoconvex domains. We also prove a refined version of the regularized defining function introduced in
[
Gon19
]
, which plays an important role in the proof of Proposition
1.2
. In Section 3 we follow Ligocka’s idea to construct the operators
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\ast},\mathcal{K}
, using the regularized defining function from Section 2. We then prove various estimates for the kernels of
\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L}^{\ast},\mathcal{K}
. We note that in our proof (Proposition
3.5
and the remark after) that
\mathcal{L}
defines a bounded projection operator from
L^{2}(D)
to
H^{2}(D)
, only
C^{3}
boundary regularity is needed.

In Section 4 we prove Proposition
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
. The proof of Proposition
1.2
is splitted into two parts. In the first part, we prove the case for
k=0
, i.e. assuming
bD\in C^{3+\alpha}
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we show that
\mathcal{K}
maps
L^{\infty}(D)
boundedly into
C^{\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
. In the second part, we apply the integration by parts techniques from
[
AS79
]
to prove the case for
k\geq 1
. We next turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First we construct the integral equation (
1.2
) using Koppleman’s homotopy formula and show that the right-hand side defines a
C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
function. Theorem
1.1
then follows easily from Proposition
1.2
and standard Fredholm theory.
In Section 5 we prove Theorem
1.3
. To this end we show that
\mathcal{L}
is a bounded operator from
C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
to
C^{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
,
0<\beta\leq 1
, assuming boundary is
C^{3}
.

We now fix some notations used in the paper. The
L^{2}
Bergman space on a domain
D
is denoted by
H^{2}(D)
. The Bergman projection and Bergman kernel is denoted by
\mathcal{P}
and
B
, respectively.
We denote by
C^{r}(\overline{D})
the Hölder space of exponent
r
on
D
, and
C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
the space of
C^{\infty}
functions with compact support in
D
. For simplicity we write
|f|_{r}:=\|f\|_{C^{r}(\overline{D})}
when the domain
D
is clear from context.
We write
x\lesssim y
to mean that
x\leq Cy
for some constant
C
independent of
x
and
y
. By
D^{l}
we mean a differential operator of order
l
:
D^{l}_{z}g(z)=\partial{}_{z_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}\partial{}_{\overline{z}_{j}}^{\beta_{j}}g(z)
,
\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}+\sum_{j}\beta_{j}=l
.

2.
Preliminaries

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be bounded pseudoconvex domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
D^{\prime}\subset\subset D
, and let
l\geq 0
. Suppose
\varphi
is a
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
form in
D
, with coefficients in
W^{l}(D)
. Let
u=S\varphi
, where
S
is Hörmander’s
L^{2}
solution operator which solves
\overline{\partial}
on
D
. Then
u\in W^{l+1}(D^{\prime})
, and

\|u\|_{W^{l+1}(D^{\prime})}\leq C(\delta/2)^{-l-1}\|\varphi\|_{W^{l}(D)},\quad\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(D^{\prime},\partial{}D),

where
C
is an absolute constant depending only on the domain
D
,

Proof.

By Hörmander’s
L^{2}
estimate
[
H6̈5
]
, we have
\overline{\partial}u=\varphi
and

(2.1)

\|u\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{0}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(D)},

where
C_{0}
is a constant which depends only on the the diameter of
D
.
Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
be such that
\chi\equiv 1
on
D^{\prime}
. Further,
\chi
satisfies the estimate
|D^{\gamma}\chi|\leq\delta^{-|\gamma|}
, where
\delta:=\operatorname{dist}(D^{\prime},D)
.
We use the following fact: If
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
has compact support and
\overline{\partial}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, then

(2.2)

\|\partial{}_{z_{i}}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}=\|\partial{}_{\overline{z}_{i}}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}.

This can be proved through a simple integration by parts and approximation argument. (see
[
H9̈0
, Lemma 4.2.4]
). In what follows we let
D^{l}
to denote a differential operator of the form
\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{z_{i}}^{\alpha_{i}}\partial{}_{\overline{z}_{j}}^{\beta_{j}}
, where
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\alpha_{i}|+|\beta_{j}|=l
, and we use
\overline{\partial}^{l}
to denote
\prod_{j=1}^{n}\partial{}_{\overline{z}_{j}}^{\beta_{j}}
, where
\sum\beta_{j}=l
. Applying (
2.2
) repeatedly then gives

(2.3)

\|D^{l+1}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}=|\overline{\partial}^{l+1}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}

for any
v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
with compact support and such that
\overline{\partial}^{l+1}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Applying (
2.3
) with
v=\chi u
, we get

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|D^{l+1}(\chi u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle=\|\overline{\partial}^{l+1}(\chi u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\overline{\partial}^{l+1}\chi)u\|_{L^{2}(D)}+\sum_{1\leq s\leq l+1}\|(\overline{\partial}^{l+1-s}\chi)(\overline{\partial}^{s}u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

By (
2.1
) and estimates for the derivatives of
\chi
, the first integral is bounded by
C_{0}\delta^{-(l+1)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(D)}
. For each integral in the sum, we have for
1\leq s\leq l+1

\displaystyle\|(\overline{\partial}^{l+1-s}\chi)(\overline{\partial}^{s}u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle=\|(\overline{\partial}^{l+1-s}\chi)(\overline{\partial}^{s-1}\overline{\partial}u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{-(l+1-s)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{s-1}(D)}\leq\delta^{-l}\|\varphi\|_{W^{l}(D)}.

Now, there are in total
\sum_{k=0}^{l+1}\binom{l+1}{k}=2^{l+1}
terms on the right-hand side of (
2.4
). Thus by combining the estimates we obtain

\|D^{l+1}(\chi u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{0}2^{l+1}\delta^{-(l+1)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{l}(D)}=C_{0}(\delta/2)^{-(l+1)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{l}(D)}.

Since
\chi\equiv 1
on
D^{\prime}
, we have

\|D^{l+1}u\|_{L^{2}(D^{\prime})}\leq\|D^{l+1}(\chi u)\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq C_{0}(\delta/2)^{-(l+1)}\|\varphi\|_{W^{l}(D)}.\qed

We now show the existence of a defining function that is smooth off the boundary and whose derivatives blow up in a controlled way.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C^{r}
boundary,
r\geq 3
, and let
\rho
be a defining function of
D
of the class
C^{r}
, i.e. there exists a

\mathcal{U}
such that
D\subset\subset\mathcal{U}
,
\nabla\rho\neq 0
on
bD
and
D=\{x\in\mathcal{U}:\rho(D)<0\}
. We denote
|\rho|_{r}:=|\rho|_{C^{r}(\mathcal{U})}
, where
|\cdot|_{C^{r}(\mathcal{U})}
denotes the Hölder-
r
norm on
\mathcal{U}
. Then there exists a defining function
\widetilde{\rho}
of
D
such that

(1)

\widetilde{\rho}\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus bD)
.

(2)

There exists some
\delta_{0}>0
such that for any
x\notin bD
and
0<\delta(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD)<\delta_{0}
,

|D^{j}\widetilde{\rho}(x)|\lesssim C_{j}|\rho|_{r}(1+\delta(x)^{r-j}),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots,\quad\delta(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD).

(3)

There exists a constant
C
depending only on the domain
D
and
|\rho|_{3}
, and a
\delta_{1}>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\delta(x)<\delta_{1}
the following estimate hold

|\widehat{D}^{2}\widetilde{\rho}(x)-\widehat{D}^{2}\rho(x_{\ast})|\lesssim C|x-x_{\ast}|,\quad|x_{\ast}-x|:=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD).

Here we use
\widehat{D}^{2}\rho
to denote derivatives of
\rho
of order
2
and less.

We call
\widetilde{\rho}
a
regularized defining function
of the domain
D
.

Proof.

We will use the argument from
[
Gon19
]
. Let
E_{r}
be the Whitney extension operator for the domain
D
. By
[
Gon19
, Lemma 3.7]
,
E_{r}\rho
is a defining function of
D
(so that
-E_{r}\rho
is a defining function of the domain
(\overline{D})^{c}
,
E_{r}\rho\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}((\overline{D})^{c})
and

|D^{j}E_{r}\rho(x)|\lesssim C_{j}|\rho|_{r}(1+\delta(x)^{r-j}),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots.\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{D}.

Furthermore, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{D}
with
0<\delta(x)<1
, there exists some constant
C
depending only on
D
and
|\rho|_{3}
such that
|\widehat{D}^{2}(E\rho)(x)-\widehat{D}^{2}\rho(x_{\ast})|\leq C|x-x_{\ast}|
, where
x_{\ast}:=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD)
.
Let
E_{r}^{\prime}
be the Whitney extension operator for the domain
(\overline{D})^{c}
. Then by the same reasoning
\widetilde{\rho}:=E_{r}^{\prime}E_{r}\rho
is a defining function of
D
satisfying
\widetilde{\rho}\in C^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus bD)
, and for all
x\in D
with
0<\delta(x)<\delta_{1}
, the following hold

\displaystyle|D^{j}\widetilde{\rho}(x)|\lesssim C_{j}^{\prime}|E_{r}\rho|_{r}(1+\delta(x)^{r-j})\lesssim C_{j}^{\prime\prime}|\rho|_{r}(1+\delta(x)^{r-j}),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots,\quad x\in D;

\displaystyle|\widehat{D}^{2}\widetilde{\rho}(x)-\widehat{D}^{2}\rho(x_{\ast})|=|\widehat{D}^{2}\widetilde{\rho}(x)-\widehat{D}^{2}(E_{r}\rho)(x_{\ast})|\leq C^{\prime}|x-x_{\ast}|,\quad|x_{\ast}-x|:=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD).\qed

We now state a very useful result to prove Hölder estimates, popularly known as the Hardy-Littlewood lemma. For a proof the reader may refer to
[
CS01
, p. 345]
.

Lemma 2.3

(Hardy-Littlewood lemma)
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
C^{1}
boundary. Suppose
g\in C^{k}(\overline{D})
and that for some
0<\beta<1
there is a constant
C
such that

|D^{k+1}g(x)|\leq C\delta(x)^{-1+\beta},\quad x\in D,

where
\delta(x)=\operatorname{dist}(x,bD)
.
Then
g\in C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
.

The following lemma can be found in
[
Bel93
]
. We provide the proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
D
be a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
and let
B(z,w)
and
\mathcal{P}
denote the Bergman kernel and the Bergman projection for
D
, respectively. Given
w_{0}\in D
, there exists a function
\phi_{w_{0}}
in
C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
such that

(2.5)

\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial\overline{w}^{\beta}}B(z,w_{0})=\mathcal{P}\phi^{\beta}_{w_{0}}(z),\quad\phi_{w_{0}}^{\beta}(z):=(-1)^{|\beta|}\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial\overline{z}^{\beta}}\phi_{w_{0}}(z),

where
\beta
is a multi-index.

Proof.

Let
\delta_{0}
denote the distance from
w_{0}
to
bD
and let
B_{1}(\mathbf{0})
the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Set

\phi_{w_{0}}(z)=\delta_{0}^{-2n}\phi\left(\frac{z-w_{0}}{\delta_{0}}\right),\quad z\in D,

where
\phi
is a real-valued function in
C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{1}(\mathbf{0}))
that is radially symmetric about the origin and
\int\phi\,dV=1
. Clearly,
\phi_{w_{0}}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
. By the property of the Bergman projection and the Bergman kernel, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\phi_{w_{0}}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}B(z,\zeta)\phi_{w_{0}}(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}B(z,\zeta)\delta_{0}^{-2n}\phi\left(\frac{\zeta-w_{0}}{\delta_{0}}\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(\mathbf{0})}B(z,\delta_{0}\zeta+w_{0})\phi(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{B_{1}(\mathbf{0})}B(\delta_{0}\zeta+w_{0},z)\phi(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)}

\displaystyle=\overline{B(w_{0},z)}=B(z,w_{0}),

where we used the fact that
B
is holomorphic in the first argument and thus both its real and imaginary parts are harmonic functions which satisfy the mean value property. This proves (
2.5
) for
\beta=0
. The general case follows similarly by repeating the above calculation and integration by parts. We leave the details to the reader.

3.
Estimates of the kernel

In this section we follow Ligocka’s idea to construct the kernel of the projection operator
\mathcal{L}
for a strictly pseudoconvex domain. For now we assume the defining function
\rho
is in the class
C^{3}
.

Suppose a bounded domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is given by
D=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\rho(z)<0\}
. We write

D_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\rho(z)<\delta\},\quad\delta>0.

We shall sometimes write
D_{\delta}(z)
(or
D_{\delta}(\zeta)
) to indicate that the domain is for the
z
(or
\zeta
) variable.
We now construct the kernel to be used in the integral formula. By setting
\rho^{\prime}=e^{A\rho}-1
, for some large
A
, we see that
\rho^{\prime}
is strictly plurisubharmonic in a neighborhood of
\overline{D}
, and from now on we simply assume
\rho
satisfies this property.
Define

(3.1)

F(z,\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\zeta_{i}\partial{}\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(z_{i}-\zeta_{i})(z_{j}-\zeta_{j}).

By Taylor’s formula we have

(3.2)

\rho(z)=\rho(\zeta)-2\operatorname{Re}F(z,\zeta)+\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\zeta;z-\zeta)+o(|z-\zeta|^{2}),

where
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\zeta;t)
is the Levi form of
\rho
at
\zeta
, i.e.
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\zeta;t):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\zeta_{i}\partial{}\overline{\zeta}_{j}}t_{i}\overline{t_{j}}
.
Fix some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
small such that for all
z,\zeta\in D_{\delta}
, we have
\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(\zeta;z-\zeta)\geq c|z-\zeta|^{2}
. It follows from (
3.2
) that

(3.3)

\operatorname{Re}F(z,\zeta)\geq\frac{\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)}{2}+\frac{c}{2}|z-\zeta|^{2},\quad(z,\zeta)\in D_{\delta}\times D_{\delta},\quad|z-\zeta|<\varepsilon_{0};

(3.4)

\operatorname{Re}F(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)\geq-\frac{\rho(\zeta)+\rho(z)}{2}+\frac{c}{2}|z-\zeta|^{2},\quad(z,\zeta)\in D_{\delta}\times D_{\delta},\quad|z-\zeta|<\varepsilon_{0}.

Let
\chi(t)
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\chi(t)\equiv 1
if
t<\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{4}
and
\chi(t)\equiv 0
if
t>\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}
. We define
the following global support function:

(3.5)

G(z,\zeta)=\chi(t)F(z,\zeta)+(1-\chi(t))|z-\zeta|^{2},\quad t=|z-\zeta|.

We also define the vector-valued functions
g_{0}=(g_{0}^{1},\dots,g_{0}^{n})
and
g_{1}=(g_{1}^{1},\dots,g_{1}^{n})
, where

g_{0}^{i}(z,\zeta)=\overline{\zeta_{i}-z_{i}},\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

and

(3.6)

g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)=\chi\left(t\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\zeta_{i}\partial{}\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(z_{j}-\zeta_{j})\right)+\left(1-\chi\left(t\right)\right)(\overline{\zeta_{i}-z_{i}}),\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

where
t=|z-\zeta|
. It follows that
\left<g_{0},\zeta-z\right>=|\zeta-z|^{2}
and
\left<g_{1},\zeta-z\right>=G(z,\zeta)
. In view of (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), there exists some
c>0
such that

(3.7)

\operatorname{Re}G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)\geq c(-\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+|z-\zeta|^{2}),\quad z,\zeta\in\overline{D},

and

(3.8)

\operatorname{Re}G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)\geq\frac{1}{4}[-\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)]+c|z-\zeta|^{2},\quad z,\zeta\in D_{\delta}\times D_{\delta}.

In particular, (
3.7
) implies

(3.9)

|G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)|\gtrsim|\zeta-z|^{2},\quad z,\zeta\in D.

We note that if the boundary is
C^{k+3+\alpha}
, then
g_{1},G\in C^{\infty}\times C^{k+1+\alpha}(D_{\delta}(z)\times D_{\delta}(\zeta))
and holomorphic in
z
whenever
|z-\zeta|<\varepsilon_{0}/4
.
Let

\omega_{\lambda}(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{\left<g_{\lambda}(z,\zeta),d\zeta\right>}{\left<g_{\lambda},\zeta-z\right>},\quad\lambda=0,1.

The associated Cauchy-Fantappie forms are given by

\Omega^{\lambda}=\omega_{\lambda}\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{z,\zeta}\omega_{\lambda})^{n-1},\quad\lambda=0,1.

\Omega^{01}=\omega_{0}\wedge\omega_{1}\wedge\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=n-2}(\overline{\partial}_{z,\zeta}\omega_{0})^{k_{1}}\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{z,\zeta}\omega_{1})^{k_{2}}.

We decompose
\Omega^{\lambda}=\sum_{0\leq q\leq n}\Omega^{\lambda}_{0,q}
and
\Omega^{01}=\sum_{0\leq q\leq n}\Omega^{01}_{0,q}
, where
\Omega^{\lambda}_{0,q}
(resp.
\Omega^{01}_{0,q}
) has type
(0,q)
in
z
and type
(n,n-1-q)
type in
\zeta
. The following Koppleman’s formula holds:

(3.10)

\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\Omega^{01}_{0,q}+\overline{\partial}_{z}\Omega^{01}_{0,q-1}=\Omega^{0}_{0,q}-\Omega^{1}_{0,q},

where we take
\Omega^{01}_{0,-1}\equiv 0
. Write

(3.11)

\Omega^{\lambda}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\frac{1}{\left<g_{\lambda},\zeta-z\right>^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{\lambda}^{i}d\zeta_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{\lambda}^{i}\wedge d\zeta_{i}\right)^{n-1},\quad\lambda=0,1;

(3.12)

\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\frac{\left<\overline{\zeta-z},d\zeta\right>}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\wedge\frac{\left<g_{1},d\zeta\right>}{\left<g_{1},\zeta-z\right>}\wedge\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=n-2}\left(\frac{\left<d\overline{\zeta},d\zeta\right>}{|\zeta-z|^{2}}\right)^{k_{1}}\wedge\left(\frac{\left<\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1},d\zeta\right>}{\left<g_{1},\zeta-z\right>}\right)^{k_{2}}.

Define

(3.13)

\displaystyle N(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\frac{1}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)d\zeta_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)\wedge d\zeta_{i}\right)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=C_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}\frac{g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n}}d\zeta\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\left(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{i}\right)}\wedge\cdots\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{n}),

where
\widehat{\eta}
means
\eta
is being excluded.
Note that for
\zeta\in bD
, we have
N(z,\zeta)=\Omega^{1}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)
. Therefore by (
3.10
) with
q=0
,

(3.14)

\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)=\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)+N(z,\zeta),\quad z\in D_{\varepsilon_{0}},\quad\zeta\in bD.

Let

(3.15)

L(z,\zeta)dV(\zeta):=\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\zeta)-S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\zeta),

where
S_{z}
is Hörmander’s operator that solves
\overline{\partial}
on
D_{\delta}
. In what follows we write

L=L_{0}+L_{1}
, where

L_{0}\,dV(\zeta)=-S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\zeta),\quad L_{1}dV(\zeta)=\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\zeta).

For each
\zeta\in\overline{D}
,
L(\cdot,\zeta)
is holomorphic on
D
. We also note that if
bD\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, then
\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N\in C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(D(z)\times D(\zeta))
. In view of (
3.8
), (
3.13
) and the fact that
\overline{\partial}_{z}G(z,\zeta),\overline{\partial}_{z}g(z,\zeta)\equiv 0
for
|z-\zeta|<\varepsilon_{0}/4
, we see that
\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\zeta)
is a well-defined
\overline{\partial}
-closed
(0,1)
form with coefficients in
C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(D_{\delta}(z)\times D_{\delta}(\zeta))
, if
\delta>0
is sufficiently small.
Write

(3.16)

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\zeta)=L_{1}(z,\zeta)dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=C_{n}\frac{\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n+1}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)d\zeta\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge\widehat{\left(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{i}\right)}\wedge\cdots\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{n})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n}}d\zeta\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{n}).

In the proof we shall use the following convenient expression from
[
Lig84
]
:

(3.17)

L_{1}(z,\zeta)=\frac{\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n+1}},\quad\eta(\zeta):=c_{n}\det\begin{vmatrix}\rho(\zeta)&\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}(\zeta)\\
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}(\zeta)&\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\zeta_{i}\partial{}\overline{\zeta}_{j}}(\zeta)\end{vmatrix}.

Here we note that
\overline{\eta(\zeta)}=\eta(\zeta)
, and
O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)
is some linear combination of products of
[\widehat{D^{3}}\rho(\zeta)](\zeta_{i}-z_{i})
, where
[\widehat{D^{3}}\rho(\zeta)]
denotes products of
\rho(\zeta)
and
D^{k}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)
,
k\leq 3
. In particular,
O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)
satisfies the estimates

(3.18)

\begin{gathered}|O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|,\quad|D_{z}^{l}O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3},\\
|D_{\zeta}^{l}O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{l+2}+|\rho|_{l+3}|\zeta-z|,\quad l\geq 1.\end{gathered}

We now define the integral operator:

(3.19)

\mathcal{L}f(z):=\int_{D}L(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}\left[L_{0}(z,\zeta)+L_{1}(z,\zeta)\right]f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

and the associated adjoint operator

\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f(z):=\int_{D}\overline{L(\zeta,z)}f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}\left[\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}+\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}\right]f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta).

In the same way as (
3.17
), we can also write

(3.20)

\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}=\frac{\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{[\overline{G(\zeta,z)}-\rho(z)]^{n+1}},\quad\eta(z):=c_{n}\det\begin{vmatrix}\rho(z)&\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{i}}(z)\\
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}(z)&\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}\overline{z}_{j}}(z)\end{vmatrix}.

Here
O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)
is some linear combination of products of
[\widehat{D^{3}}\rho(z)](\zeta_{i}-z_{i})
, where
[\widehat{D^{3}}\rho(z)]
denotes products of
\rho(z)
and
D^{k}_{z}\rho(z)
,
k\leq 3
.
O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)
satisfies the estimate

(3.21)

\begin{gathered}|O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|,\quad|D_{\zeta}^{l}O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3},\\
|D^{l}_{z}O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|\lesssim|\rho|_{l+2}+|\rho|_{l+3}|\zeta-z|,\;l\geq 1.\end{gathered}

Hence if
bD\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, then
\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}
is
C^{k+\alpha}\times C^{\infty}(D(z)\times D(\zeta))
.

Let

(3.22)

\displaystyle K(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle:=\overline{L(\zeta,z)}-L(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\left[\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}-L_{0}(z,\zeta)\right]+\left[\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}-L_{1}(z,\zeta)\right],

and

(3.23)

\mathcal{K}f(z):=\int_{D}K(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\int\left[\overline{L(\zeta,z)}-L(z,\zeta)\right]f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f(z)-\mathcal{L}f(z).

For later purpose we note that
\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{K}
is a self-adjoint operator.

The following cancellation estimate is due to
[
KS78
]
. We include a proof here for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain with a
C^{3}
defining function
\rho
, with
0<\alpha<1
. Let
F(z,\zeta)
be the function defined by formula (
3.1
). Then

(3.24)

\left[F(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)\right]-[\overline{F(\zeta,z)}-\rho(z)]=O(|\zeta-z|^{3}),

where
|O(|\zeta-z|^{3})|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|^{3}
.

Proof.

By (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle F(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\zeta_{i}\partial{}\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(z_{i}-\zeta_{i})(z_{j}-\zeta_{j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{j}}(z)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{j}\partial{}z_{k}}(z)(\zeta_{k}-z_{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{j}\partial{}\overline{z}_{k}}(z)(\overline{\zeta_{k}-z_{k}})\right](\zeta_{j}-z_{j})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}z_{j}}(z)(z_{i}-\zeta_{i})(z_{j}-\zeta_{j})+R(z,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}z_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{i}-z_{i})(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}\overline{z}_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{i}-z_{i})(\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}})+R_{0}(z,\zeta),

where we did Taylor expansion for the function
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}
at
z
. Since
\rho\in C^{3}
, the remainder term
R_{0}
satisfies
|R_{0}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|^{3}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\overline{F(\zeta,z)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}(z)(\overline{z_{j}-\zeta_{j}})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}\overline{z}_{i}\partial{}\overline{z}_{j}}(z)\overline{(\zeta_{i}-z_{i})}\overline{(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})}.

Hence

\displaystyle F(z,\zeta)-\overline{F(\zeta,z)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}(z)(\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}})

\displaystyle\quad+\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}z_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{i}-z_{i})(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})\right)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial{}^{2}\rho}{\partial{}z_{i}\partial{}\overline{z}_{j}}(z)(\zeta_{i}-z_{i})(\overline{\zeta_{j}-z_{j}})+R_{0}(z,\zeta),

where
|R_{0}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|^{3}
.
The first four terms on the right-hand side are exactly the first and second order terms in the Taylor polynomial of
\rho
at
z
, which is equal to
\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+R_{1}(z,\zeta)
, where
|R_{1}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|^{3}
. Hence

F(z,\zeta)-\overline{F(\zeta,z)}=\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+R(z,\zeta),

where
|R(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|^{3}
.
∎

In what follows we shall denote

\Phi(z,\zeta)=G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta),\quad\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)=\overline{G(\zeta,z)}-\rho(z).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{3}
boundary in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, Let
\rho
be the defining function of
D
. Let
0<\beta\leq 1
. Let
\Theta(z,\zeta)
denote either
\Phi(z,\zeta)
or
\Phi(\zeta,z)
.

(1)

Let
0<\beta\leq 1
. Then

(3.25)

\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z|^{2-\beta}|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim 1+\delta(z)^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1},

where the constant depends only on
D
.

(2)

Let
\beta>0
. Then

(3.26)

\int_{B(z,\tau)}\frac{|z-\zeta|^{\beta}}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}dV(\zeta)\lesssim\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}},

where the constant depends only on
D
.

Proof.

First, we show that for each fixed
z\in D
, there exists a small neighborhood
U_{z}
and a coordinate chart
\phi_{z}:\mathcal{U}_{z}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
\phi_{z}(\zeta)=((s_{1},s_{2}),t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
and

(3.27)

|\Phi(z,\zeta)|,|\Phi(\zeta,z)|\gtrsim\delta(z)+|s_{1}|+|s_{2}|+|t|^{2},\quad|\zeta-z|\gtrsim|(s_{2},t)|.

Here
\delta(z):=\operatorname{dist}(z,bD)
and in the following computation we shall just write
\delta
.
We define
s_{1}(\zeta)=\rho(\zeta)
and
s_{2}(\zeta)=\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)
. Recall that
\Phi(z,\zeta)=F(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)
when
z,\zeta
are close and

F(z,\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{j}}(\zeta)(\zeta_{j}-z_{j})+O(|\zeta-z|^{2}).

Hence at
\zeta=z
, we have

\displaystyle d_{\zeta}\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)\wedge d_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)

\displaystyle=d_{\zeta}\operatorname{Im}F(z,\zeta)\wedge d_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-1}}(\partial{}_{\zeta}\rho-\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\rho)\wedge(\partial{}_{\zeta}\rho+\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\rho)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}}\partial{}\rho\wedge\overline{\partial}\rho\neq 0.

We can then find smooth real-valued functions
t_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq 2n-2
, with
t_{j}(\zeta)=0
at
\zeta=z
and

d_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)\wedge d_{\zeta}\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)\wedge dt_{1}(\zeta)\wedge\cdots\wedge dt_{2n-2}(\zeta)\neq 0\quad\text{at $\zeta=z$. }

By the inverse function theorem,
\phi_{z}=(s_{1},s_{2},t)
defines a
C^{1}
coordinate map in small neighborhood of
z
.

To prove the first statement in (
3.27
), we use estimate (
3.7
) which says
\operatorname{Re}\Phi(z,\zeta)\gtrsim-\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+|\zeta-z|^{2}
, for all
z,\zeta\in D
. It follows that

\displaystyle|\Phi(z,\zeta)|\gtrsim|\operatorname{Re}\Phi(z,\zeta)|+|\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)|\gtrsim\delta(z)+|s_{1}(\zeta)|+|s_{2}(\zeta)|+|t(\zeta)|^{2}.

For
\Phi(\zeta,z)
the argument goes the same: We note that
\Phi(\zeta,z)=F(\zeta,z)-\rho(z)
when
z,\zeta
are close, and

F(\zeta,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial z_{j}}(z)(z_{j}-\zeta_{j})+O(|\zeta-z|^{2}).

Thus at
\zeta=z
,

\displaystyle\left.d_{\zeta}\operatorname{Im}\Phi(\zeta,z)\wedge d_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)\right|_{\zeta=z}

\displaystyle=d_{\zeta}\operatorname{Im}F(\zeta,z)\wedge d_{z}\rho(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-1}}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\rho(z)-\partial{}_{z}\rho(z))\wedge(\partial{}_{z}\rho(z)+\overline{\partial}_{z}\rho(z))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}}\overline{\partial}\rho\wedge\partial{}\rho\neq 0.

The second statement in (
3.27
) follows from the fact that
s_{2}(z)=t(z)=0
. Now, both
\Phi(z,\zeta)
and
|\zeta-z|
are bounded below by some positive constant for
\zeta\notin\mathcal{U}_{z}
. Hence using partition of unity in
\zeta
space, we can bound the integral on the left-hand-side of (
3.25
) by a constant times

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(s_{2}+t)^{2-\beta}(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{2})^{n+1}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{rt^{2n-5+\beta}\,dr\,dt}{(\delta+r+t^{2})^{n+1}}:=I,

where we used the polar coordinates for
(s_{1},s_{2})
with
r=|s|
. We can estimate the integral
I
by separating into different cases.

Case 1:
\delta>r,t^{2}
.

I\leq\delta^{-(n+1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\delta}r\,dr\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\delta}}t^{2n-5+\beta}\,dt\right)\lesssim 1+\delta^{-n-1+2+\frac{2n-4+\beta}{2}}=1+\delta^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Case 2:
r>\delta,t^{2}
.

I\leq\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{-n}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{r}}t^{2n-5+\beta}\,dt\right)\,dr\lesssim\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{-n+\frac{2n-4+\beta}{2}}\,dr\lesssim 1+\delta^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Case 3:
t^{2}>\delta,r
.

I\leq\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}r\,dr\right)t^{2n-5+\beta-2n-2}\,dt\lesssim\int_{\sqrt{\delta}}^{1}t^{\beta-3}\,dt\lesssim 1+\delta^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Combining the estimates we obtain (
3.25
).

(ii) Since
|\Theta(z,\zeta)|\gtrsim|z-\zeta|^{2}
, the integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\tau}(z)}\frac{|z-\zeta|^{\beta}}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{\tau}(z)}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|z-\zeta|^{2-\beta}|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}\,ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(s_{2}+t)^{2-\beta}(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{2})^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s_{1}=0}^{1}\int_{s_{2}=0}^{\tau}\int_{t=0}^{\tau}\frac{t^{2n-5+\beta}\,ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{2})^{n}}:=I.

Here we used the fact that
|\zeta-z|\gtrsim(s_{2},t)
and thus
\zeta\in B_{\tau}(z)
implies
|s_{2}|,|t|<\tau
.

We consider several cases.

Case 1:
s_{1}>\tau
, the integral is bounded by

\displaystyle I\leq\int_{\tau}^{1}\frac{ds_{1}}{s_{1}^{n}}\int_{0}^{\tau}\,ds_{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{2n-5+\beta}\,dt\lesssim\tau^{-n+1+1+2n-4+\beta}=\tau^{n-2+\beta}\lesssim\tau^{\beta}.

Case 2:
s_{1}<\tau
, then we have
|s|<r
, for
s=(s_{1},s_{2})
. Divide further into subcases. If
t^{2}>s
, then

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\frac{st^{2n-5+\beta}\,ds\,dt}{(s+t^{2})^{n}}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}s\,ds\right)t^{2n-5+\beta-2n}\,dt\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{\beta-1}\,dt\lesssim\tau^{\beta}.

On the other hand, if
t^{2}<s
, then

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{s}}t^{2n-3-2+\beta}\,dt\right)\frac{s}{s^{n}}\,ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}s^{\frac{2n-4+\beta}{2}-n+1}\,ds\lesssim\int_{0}^{\tau}s^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1}\,ds\lesssim\tau^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.\qed

From the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we see that for fixed
\zeta
, we can find a neighborhood
U_{\zeta}
of
\zeta
and a coordinate chart
\phi_{\zeta}:U_{\zeta}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
with
\phi_{\zeta}(z)=(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}
. Indeed, we can set
s_{1}^{\prime}(z)=\rho(z)
and
s_{2}^{\prime}(z)=\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)
. At

,

\displaystyle d_{z}\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)\wedge d_{z}\rho(z)

\displaystyle=d_{z}\operatorname{Im}F(z,\zeta)\wedge d_{z}\rho(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{-1}}(\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)-\partial{}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta))\wedge(\partial{}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta)+\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-1}}\overline{\partial}\rho(\zeta)\wedge\partial{}\rho(\zeta)\neq 0.

Hence there exists smooth real-valued functions

,

d_{z}\rho(z)\wedge d_{z}\operatorname{Im}\Phi(z,\zeta)\wedge dt_{1}^{\prime}(\zeta)\wedge\cdots\wedge dt^{\prime}_{2n-2}(\zeta)\neq 0\quad\text{at $z=\zeta$. }

Consequently
(s_{1}^{\prime},s_{2}^{\prime},t^{\prime})
is the desired coordinate chart in the
z
variable. Now by the same estimate as in the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we can prove the following:

Lemma 3.3
.

Keeping the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
.

(1)

Let
0<\beta\leq 1

(3.28)

\int_{D}\frac{dV(z)}{|\zeta-z|^{2-\beta}|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim 1+\delta(\zeta)^{\frac{\beta}{2}-1},\quad\delta(\zeta):=\operatorname{dist}(\zeta,bD),

where the constant depends only on

(2)

Let

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{3}
boundary in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and let
\rho
be its defining function. Let
\Theta(z,\zeta)
denote either
\Phi(z,\zeta)
or
\Phi(\zeta,z)
. Denote
\delta(z):=\operatorname{dist}(z,bD)
.

(1)

(3.30)

\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim 1+\log\delta(z),\quad z\in D,

where the constant depends only on
D
.

(2)

(3.31)

\int_{bD}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n}}\lesssim 1+\log\delta(z),\quad z\in D,

where the constant depends only on
D
.

Proof.

(i) In the proof we shall write
\delta(z)
simply as
\delta
.
For fixed
z\in D
, let
\zeta\mapsto(s_{1},s_{2},t)
be the coordinate chart in a neighborhood
U_{z}
of
z
as constructed in the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
Let
\chi_{0}
be a smooth cut-off function such that
\operatorname{supp}\chi_{0}\subset E_{0}(z):=\{\zeta\in D:-\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+|z-\zeta|\leq\sigma\}
and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
on the set
E_{1}(z):=\{\zeta\in D:-\rho(\zeta)-\rho(z)+|z-\zeta|\leq\frac{\sigma}{2}\}
. We choose
\sigma
sufficiently small such that
E_{0}(z)\subset U_{z}
.
Then

\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}=\int_{D\cap E_{0}}\frac{\chi_{0}(\zeta)dV(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}+\int_{D\setminus E_{1}}\frac{(1-\chi_{0}(\zeta))dV(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}.

In view of (
3.7
), the second integral is bounded by a constant independent of
z\in D
.

The first integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap E_{0}(z)}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}\,ds_{1}\,ds_{2}\,dt}{(\delta+s_{1}+s_{2}+t^{2})^{n+1}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}r\,dr\,dt}{(\delta+r+t^{2})^{n+1}}:=I,

where we used the polar coordinates for
r=(s_{1},s_{2})
.
We split the integral into the following cases:

Case 1:
\delta+r\geq t^{2}
.

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r}{(\delta+r)^{n+1}}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\delta+r}}t^{2n-3}\,dt\right)\,dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(\delta+r)^{1-n-1+\frac{2n-2}{2}}\,dr=\int_{0}^{1}(\delta+r)^{-1}\,dr\lesssim 1+\log\delta.

Case 2:
\delta+r\leq t^{2}
.

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}r\left(\int_{\sqrt{\delta+r}}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}\,dt}{t^{2n+2}}\right)\,dr\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(\delta+r)^{-1}\,dr\lesssim 1+\log\delta.

(ii)
Since
s_{1}(\zeta)=\rho(\zeta)\equiv 0
for
\zeta\in bD
, for fixed
z
, there exists some neighborhood
U_{z}
of
z
such that
\zeta\mapsto(s_{2},t)
is a coordinate chart for
\zeta\in bD\cap U_{z}
. Let
\chi_{0},E_{0}
be the same as in the proof of (i). We only have to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{bD\cap E_{0}}\frac{\chi_{0}\,d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\Theta(z,\zeta)|^{n}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}ds_{2}\,dt}{(\delta+s_{2}+t^{2})^{n}}:=I.

Split the integral into two cases.

Case 1:
\delta+s_{2}\geq t^{2}
.

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(\delta+s_{2})^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\delta+s_{2}}}t^{2n-3}\,dt\right)\,ds_{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(\delta+s_{2})^{-1}\,ds_{2}\lesssim 1+\log\delta.

Case 2:
\delta+s_{2}\leq t^{2}
.

\displaystyle I\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\sqrt{\delta+s_{2}}}^{1}\frac{t^{2n-3}\,dt}{t^{2n}}\right)\,ds_{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}(\delta+s_{2})^{-1}\,ds_{2}\lesssim 1+\log\delta.

∎

We now prove the
L^{2}
boundedness of the operator
\mathcal{K}
, assuming boundary is only
C^{3}
. This result is stated in
[
Lig84
]
assuming the boundary is
C^{4}
, and the proof over there uses a much more general estimate from
[

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{3}
boundary, and let
\mathcal{K}
be the operator given by formula (
3.23
). Then
\mathcal{K}
defines a bounded operator from
L^{2}(D)
to
L^{2}(D)
.

Proof.

We shall apply Schur’s test (see for example
[
Wol03
]
), which in our case can be formulated as follows. If

(3.32)

\int_{D}|K(z,\zeta)|\,dV(\zeta)\leq A,\quad\text{for each $z$},

and

(3.33)

\int_{D}|K(z,\zeta)|\,dV(z)\leq B,\quad\text{for each $\zeta$},

then for
f\in L^{2}(D)
,
\mathcal{K}_{f}
defined by the integral
\int_{D}K(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,dV(z)
converges a.e. and there is an estimate

\|\mathcal{K}f\|_{L^{2}(D)}\leq\sqrt{AB}\|f\|_{L^{2}(D)}.

Hence it suffices to prove (
3.32
) and (
3.33
).
We can write

\displaystyle\int_{D}|K(z,\zeta)|\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\left|\frac{\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)}-\frac{\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\right|\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\leq J_{1}+J_{2}+J_{3}+J_{4},

where we denote

\displaystyle J_{1}=\int_{D}\frac{|\eta(z)-\eta(\zeta)|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta),\quad J_{2}=\int_{D}\eta(\zeta)\left|\frac{1}{\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)}-\frac{1}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\right|\,dV(\zeta),

\displaystyle J_{3}=\int_{D}\frac{|O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta),\quad J_{4}=\int_{D}\frac{|O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)|}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta).

By the expression for
\eta
(
3.17
), we have
|\eta(z)-\eta(\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|\zeta-z|
. We have

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)\lesssim|\rho|_{3},

where we applied estimate (
3.26
) in the last inequality.
By estimates (
3.9
), (
3.24
) and (
3.26
), we have

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{2}\int_{D}\frac{|\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)-\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}|\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)|}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{2}\int_{D}|\Phi(z,\zeta)-\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}|\left(\frac{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n}+|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n}}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}|\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)|}\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}\left(\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|^{3}}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}|\Phi(z,\zeta)|}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|^{3}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}|\Phi(\zeta,z)|}\,dV(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}\left(\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)\right)\lesssim|\rho|_{3}.

For
J_{3}
, we use estimates (
3.18
), (
3.21
) and (
3.26
):

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)\lesssim|\rho|_{3},

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)\lesssim|\rho|_{3}.

Here we note that all the bounds are uniform in
z\in D
. Hence we have proved (
3.33
).
In a similar way by using estimate (
3.29
), we can prove (
3.32
). The proof is now complete.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{4}
boundary. Suppose
u\in C^{1}(\overline{D})
and the support of
u
is contained in some small neighborhood of
z
. Then the following integration by parts formulae hold

(1)

(3.43)

\int_{D}\frac{u(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m+1}(z,\zeta)}\,=c_{1}^{\prime}\int_{bD}\frac{P^{\prime}(u)(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m-1}(z,\zeta)}+c_{2}^{\prime}\int_{D}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime}(z,\zeta)}\right)\frac{dV(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}.

Here
P^{\prime}
is a first order differential operator in
\zeta
variable (see (
3.51
)).

(2)

(3.44)

\int_{D}\frac{u(\zeta)dV(\zeta)}{\overline{\Phi}^{m+1}(\zeta,z)}=c_{1}^{\prime\prime}\int_{bD}\frac{P^{\prime\prime}(u)(\zeta)\,d\sigma(\zeta)}{\overline{\Phi}^{m-1}(\zeta,z)}+c_{2}^{\prime\prime}\int_{D}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime\prime}(\zeta,z)}\right)\frac{dV(\zeta)}{\overline{\Phi}^{m}(\zeta,z)}.

Here
P^{\prime\prime}
is a first order differential operator in
\zeta
variable (see (
3.55
)).

(3)

(3.45)

\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}=\int_{bD}\frac{P^{\prime}(u)(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m-1}(z,\zeta)},\quad\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m}(\zeta,z)}=\int_{bD}\frac{P^{\prime\prime}(u)(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m-1}(\zeta,z)}.

Here
P^{\prime},P^{\prime\prime}
are first order differential operators in
\zeta
. The coefficients of
P^{\prime}
(resp.
P^{\prime\prime}
) involve derivatives of
\rho
up to order
3
(resp. order
2
).

Proof.

In view of (
3.5
) and (
3.8
), for each fixed
z\in D
we have
\Phi(z,\cdot)\in C^{1}(\overline{D})
. By (
3.42
), and the assumption that
\rho\in C^{3}
, we see that
Q^{\prime},Q^{\prime\prime}\in C^{1}(\overline{D})
. Hence by Stokes’ theorem,

(3.46)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{u(\zeta)}{\Phi^{m+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{m}\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime}(z,\zeta)\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}[d\overline{\zeta}]_{k}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m}\int_{D}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{k}}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime}(z,\zeta)}\right)\frac{1}{\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta).

To finish the proof we need to apply Stokes’ theorem again to the boundary integral. We have on
bD
,

(3.47)

d\rho(\zeta)=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{l}}d\zeta_{l}+\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{l}}d\overline{\zeta}_{l}\right)\equiv 0.

Let
\{\chi_{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{M}
be a partition of unity of
bD
subordinate to the cover
\{U_{\nu}\}_{\nu=1}^{M}
. We can assume that on
U_{\nu}
, there exists an index
i=i(\nu)
such that
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i(\nu)}}(\zeta)\neq 0
.
By (
3.47
), we have for
\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD
:

(3.48)

\displaystyle d_{\zeta}\left(\Phi^{-(m-1)}[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=-(m-1)\Phi^{-m}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\zeta_{l}}d\zeta_{l}+\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{l}}d\overline{\zeta}_{l}\right)\wedge[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}

\displaystyle=-(m-1)\Phi^{-m}\left[\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\zeta_{i}}-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right]d\zeta_{i}\wedge[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}

\displaystyle=-(m-1)\Phi^{-m}(-1)^{i-1}a_{i}(z,\zeta)d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i},\quad i=i(\nu),

where we set

(3.49)

a_{i}(z,\zeta):=\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\zeta_{i}}-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}},\quad\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD.

By assumption,
u
is supported in a small neighorhood of
z
. Hence if for some
\nu
,
\operatorname{supp}u\cap U_{\nu}
is non-empty, then
z
must be sufficiently close to
U_{\nu}
. Hence in view of estimate (
3.35
) and by shrinking
U_{\nu}
if necessary, we can assume that
a_{i}(z,\zeta)\geq c>0
for
\zeta\in\operatorname{supp}u\cap U_{\nu}
.
Accordingly,

\Phi^{-m}d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}=c_{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{a_{i}(z,\zeta)}d_{\zeta}\left(\Phi^{-(m-1)}[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}\right),\quad i=i(\nu),\quad\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD.

Now by (
3.47
) we can write

(3.50)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{k}=\varphi_{\nu}(\zeta)d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)},\quad\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD,

where
\varphi_{\nu}
is a linear combination of products of
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{s}}
and
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{t}}
.
Hence for the boundary integral in (
3.46
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime}(z,\zeta)\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{k}=\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)\varphi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime}(z,\zeta)\Phi^{m}(z,\zeta)}\,d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)\varphi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime}a_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}d_{\zeta}(\Phi^{-(m-1)}[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}),

where the constant is absorbed into
\varphi_{\nu}
.
By Stokes’ theorem, the integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{bD}d_{\zeta}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)(\chi_{\nu}\varphi_{\nu})(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime}a_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}\right)\Phi^{-(m-1)}[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}.

Let
\psi_{\nu}
be the function such that
[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}=\psi_{\nu}(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)
. Summing the above expression over
\nu
, the boundary integral in (
3.46
) can be written as

(3.51)

\int_{bD}P^{\prime}(u)(\zeta)\Phi^{-(m-1)}\,d\sigma(\zeta),\quad P^{\prime}(u)(\zeta):=\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}d_{\zeta}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)(\chi_{\nu}\varphi_{\nu})(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime}a_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}\right)\psi_{\nu}(\zeta).

Hence we obtain formula (
3.43
). This completes the proof of (i).

The proof of (ii) goes similar. By Stokes’ theorem we have

(3.52)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{u(\zeta)}{\overline{\Phi}^{m+1}(\zeta,z)}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{m}\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime\prime}(\zeta,z)\overline{\Phi}^{m}(\zeta,z)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}\left[d\overline{\zeta}\right]_{k}\wedge d\zeta

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m}\int_{D}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{k}}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime\prime}(\zeta,z)}\right)\frac{1}{\overline{\Phi}^{m}(\zeta,z)}\,dV(\zeta).

Let
\chi_{\nu}
,
U_{\nu}
and
i(\nu)
be the same as in the proof of (i). By (
3.47
), we have for
\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD
:

(3.53)

\displaystyle d_{\zeta}\overline{\Phi}^{-(m-1)}(\zeta,z)[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}

\displaystyle=-m\overline{\Phi}^{-m}(\zeta,z)\left(\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\zeta_{i}}d\zeta_{i}+\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}d\overline{\zeta}_{i}\right)\wedge[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}

\displaystyle=-m\overline{\Phi}^{-m}(\zeta,z)\left[\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\zeta_{i}}-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right]d\zeta_{i}\wedge[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}

\displaystyle=-m\overline{\Phi}^{-m}(\zeta,z)(-1)^{i-1}b_{i}(\zeta,z)d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i},\quad i=i(\nu),

where we set

(3.54)

b_{i}(\zeta,z):=\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\zeta_{i}}-\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}}\right)^{-1}\frac{\partial\overline{\Phi}(\zeta,z)}{\partial\overline{\zeta}_{i}},\quad\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD.

Using estimate (
3.37
), we may assume that
b_{i}\geq c>0
for
\zeta\in\operatorname{supp}u\cap U_{\nu}
.
It follows that

\overline{\Phi}^{-m}(\zeta,z)d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}=c_{m}\frac{(-1)^{i-1}}{b_{i}(\zeta,z)}d_{\zeta}\left(\overline{\Phi}^{-(m-1)}(\zeta,z)[d\zeta]_{i}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i}\right),\quad i=i(\nu),\quad\zeta\in U_{\nu}\cap bD.

By (
3.50
), the boundary integral in (
3.52
) can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime\prime}(z,\zeta)\overline{\Phi}^{m}(\zeta,z)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial\zeta_{k}}\left[d\overline{\zeta}\right]_{k}\wedge d\zeta=\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)\varphi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{Q^{\prime\prime}(z,\zeta)\overline{\Phi}^{m}(\zeta,z)}\,d\zeta\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{bD}\frac{u(\zeta)\chi_{\nu}(\zeta)\varphi_{\nu}(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime\prime}b_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}d_{\zeta}(\overline{\Phi}^{-(m-1)}(\zeta,z)[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}),

where the constant is absorbed into
\varphi_{\nu}
.
By Stokes’ theorem, the integral is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{bD}d_{\zeta}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)(\chi_{\nu}\varphi_{\nu})(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime\prime}b_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}\right)\overline{\Phi}^{-(m-1)}(\zeta,z)[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}.

Let
\psi_{\nu}
be the function such that
[d\zeta]_{i(\nu)}\wedge[d\overline{\zeta}]_{i(\nu)}=\psi_{\nu}(\zeta)d\sigma(\zeta)
. Summing the above expression over
\nu
, the boundary integral in (
3.52
) can be written as

(3.55)

\int_{bD}P^{\prime\prime}(u)(\zeta)\overline{\Phi}^{-(m-1)}(\zeta,z)\,d\sigma(\zeta),\quad P^{\prime\prime}(u)(\zeta):=\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}d_{\zeta}\left(\frac{u(\zeta)(\chi_{\nu}\varphi_{\nu})(\zeta)}{(Q^{\prime\prime}b_{i(\nu)})(z,\zeta)}\right)\psi_{\nu}(\zeta).

Hence we obtain formula (
3.44
).

Finally, the proof of (iii) is clear from the proofs of (i) and (ii).

. First we fix some notations.

, where

In this section we prove Theorem

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

We shall assume that
\rho
is a regularized defining function satisfying the properties in Proposition
2.2
. In particular, we have
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\cap C^{k+3+\alpha}(\overline{D})
and

(4.1)

|D^{j}\rho(z)|\lesssim C_{j}|\rho|_{k+2+\alpha}(1+\delta(z)^{k+3+\alpha-j}),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots.

We recall the notation:

\Phi(z,\zeta):=G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta),\quad\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}:=\overline{G(\zeta,z)}-\rho(z).

In view of (
3.22
), we can write

\mathcal{K}f(z)=\mathcal{K}_{0}f(z)+\mathcal{K}_{1}f(z):=\int_{D}K_{0}(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}K_{1}(z,\zeta)f(\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

where
K_{0}(z,\zeta):=\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}-L_{0}(z,\zeta)
and
K_{1}(z,\zeta)=\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}-L_{1}(z,\zeta)
. We first estimate
\mathcal{K}_{0}f
. In view of (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{0}f(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}-L_{0}(z,\zeta)\right)\,dV(\zeta).

where
L_{0}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\zeta)
.
As observed earlier, since
bD\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, the coefficients of
\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\zeta)
belong to the class
C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(D_{\delta}(z)\times D_{\delta}(\zeta))
. By Proposition
2.1
and the fact that
S_{z}
is a linear operator, we see that
L_{0}(z,\zeta)\in C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(D_{\delta}(z)\times D_{\delta}(\zeta))
, which also implies
L_{0}(\zeta,z)\in C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(D_{\delta}(\zeta)\times D_{\delta}(z))
.

Accordingly, we have

(4.2)

\int_{D}f(\zeta)L_{0}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D}),\quad\int_{D}f(\zeta)\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}\,dV(\zeta)\in C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D}).

Here the first statement is clear. We now prove the second statement. By estimate (
4.1
) and the expression for
L_{0}(\zeta,z)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}f(\zeta)D^{k+1}_{z}\overline{L_{0}(\zeta,z)}\,dV(\zeta)\right|\lesssim|f|_{0}(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\alpha}).

Hence by Lemma
2.3
, the second integral in (
4.2
) belongs to
C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D})
. Thus we have shown
\mathcal{K}_{0}f\in C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D})
.

Next we estimate
\mathcal{K}_{1}f
. First we prove for the case
k=0
, i.e.
\rho\in C^{3+\alpha}
.
In view of (
3.17
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}f(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}-L_{1}(z,\zeta)\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left[\frac{\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)}-\frac{\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\right]\,dV(\zeta).

Let
\chi_{0}
be a
C^{\infty}
cut-off function supported in the set
E_{0}:=\{(z,\zeta)\in D\times D:|z-\zeta|<\delta_{0}\}
, and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
in
\{(z,\zeta)\in D\times D:|z-\zeta|<\frac{\delta_{0}}{2}\}
, for some
\delta_{0}>0
. From the definition of
G(z,\zeta)
(see (
3.5
)), we can choose
\delta_{0}
to be sufficiently small such that on the set
E_{0}
, we have
\Phi(z,\zeta)=F(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)
.
Write

\mathcal{K}_{1}f(z)=\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f(z)+\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime\prime}f(z),

where

\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f(z):=\int_{D}f(\zeta)(\chi_{0}K_{1})(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),\quad\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime\prime}f(z)=\int_{D}f(\zeta)[(1-\chi_{0})K_{1}](z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

with
K_{1}(z,\zeta)=\overline{L_{1}(\zeta,z)}-L_{1}(z,\zeta)
.
The function
(1-\chi_{0})K_{1}
is supported in
E_{1}:=\{(z,\zeta)\in D\times D:|z-\zeta|\geq\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{2}\}
. By estimate (
3.7
) and the assumption
\rho\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, we see that
(1-\chi_{0})L_{1}(z,\zeta)\in C^{\infty}\times C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D}(z)\times\overline{D}(\zeta))
and
(1-\chi_{0})L_{1}(\zeta,z)\in C^{k+\alpha}\times C^{\infty}(\overline{D}(z)\times\overline{D}(\zeta))
. By the same argument used to prove (
4.2
), we can show that
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime\prime}f\in C^{k+\alpha}(\overline{D})
.

It remains to estimate
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f
. We will divide the proof into two steps. In the first part, we show that if
bD\in C^{3+\alpha}
, then
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f\in C^{\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}
. In the second part, we use integration by parts to show that if
bD\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, for
k\geq 1
, then
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}
.

4.1.
Case 1:
bD\in C^{3+\alpha}
.

Assume now that
bD\in C^{3+\alpha}
. In what follows we will write
D_{0}=D_{0}(z)=\{\zeta\in D:|\zeta-z|\leq\delta_{0}\}
, and without loss of generality we can assume

is supported in
D_{0}
.
Taking
z_{j}
derivative we get

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f}{\partial z_{j}}(z)=\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}[\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]}{\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)}-\frac{\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}[\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\quad-(n+1)\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left(\frac{\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}\right](\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|))}{\overline{\Phi}^{n+2}(\zeta,z)}-\frac{\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Phi(z,\zeta)\right](\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|))}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle:=I_{1}(z)+I_{2}(z),

where we denote the first and second integral by
I_{1}
and
I_{2}

We first estimate

. By (
3.18

\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}[\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]\right|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}.

Hence by (
3.30

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{\left|\partial{}_{z_{j}}[\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]\right|}{|\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)|}|f(\zeta)|\,dV(\zeta)\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}(1+\log\delta(z)).

On the other hand, using estimate (

) we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}[\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|D^{3}_{z}\rho(z)|+|D^{4}_{z}\rho(z)||\zeta-z|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3+\alpha}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\alpha}|\zeta-z|\right),

where in the last inequality we applied (
4.1
) with
k=0
and
j=4
.

Thus applying (
3.26

3.30

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{\left|\partial{}_{z_{j}}[\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]\right|}{|\overline{\Phi}^{n+1}(\zeta,z)|}|f(\zeta)|\,dV(\zeta)

|I_{1}(z)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3+\alpha}|f|_{0}\delta(z)^{-1+\alpha},\quad 0<\alpha<1.

For the integral

, where

, for any

where in the last inequality we applied estimate (
3.25
) with
\alpha=1
.
For

, we note that

.
By Lemma

(i), we have

(4.7)

\left|\frac{\partial\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\partial z_{j}}\right|\lesssim|\rho|_{1}+|\rho|_{2}|\zeta-z|\lesssim|\rho|_{2}.

Applying estimates (
3.9
), (
3.18
), (
3.21

(4.8)

\displaystyle|J_{2}(z)|\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{\left|\Phi(\zeta,z)\right|^{n+2}}\,dV(\zeta)\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z||\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\delta(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For
J_{3}
we use estimate (
3.24
),

(4.9)

\displaystyle|J_{3}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\int_{D}|\zeta-z|^{3}\left(\frac{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}{|\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}|^{n+2}|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+2}}+\frac{|\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}|^{n+1}}{|\overline{\Phi(\zeta,z)}|^{n+2}|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+2}}\right)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\left(\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z||\Phi(\zeta,z)|^{n+1}}+\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z||\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\right)\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\delta(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

where in the last inequality we applied Lemma
3.2
with
\beta=1
.
Hence we have shown that

(4.10)

|I_{2}(z)|\lesssim|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3}\delta(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining (

) and (

\left|\frac{\partial\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f}{\partial z_{j}}\right|\lesssim\begin{cases}|f|_{0}|\rho|_{3+\alpha}\delta(z)^{-1+\alpha},&\text{if $0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}$};\\

In a similar way we can show that

satisifes the same estimate.
It follows by Lemma

that

\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f\in\begin{cases}C^{\alpha}(\overline{D}),&\text{if $0<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{2}$};\\
C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D}),&\text{if $\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1$}.\end{cases}

This completes the proof for the

case.

4.2.
Case 2:

,

We now assume that
\rho\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
, for

. Taking

derivatives we get

(4.11)

\displaystyle D^{k+1}_{z}\mathcal{K}_{1}^{\prime}f(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\leq k+1}\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left[D^{\gamma_{1}}_{z}\{\eta(z)+O^{\prime\prime}(|z-\zeta|)\}D^{\gamma_{2}}_{z}(\overline{\Phi}^{-(n+1)}(\zeta,z))\right]\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle-\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\leq k+1}\int_{D}f(\zeta)\left[D^{\gamma_{1}}_{z}\{\eta(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)\}D^{\gamma_{2}}_{z}(\Phi^{-(n+1)}(z,\zeta))\right]\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle:=F_{1}+F_{2},

where we denote the first and second sum in (
4.11
) by
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{3}
boundary. Let
f
be a function in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
\overline{\partial}f\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Then the following formula holds

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}f(z)+\int_{D}S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\cdot)\wedge f-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{bD}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f,\quad z\in D.

Here
N
and
\mathcal{L}
are given by formulae (
3.13
) (
3.19
).

Proof.

Starting with the Bochner-Martinelli formula (see for example
[
CS01
, Theorem 2.2.1]
):

f(z)=\int_{bD}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)\wedge f(\zeta)+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)\wedge\overline{\partial}f,\quad z\in D.

By (
3.14
), (
3.15
) and Stokes’ theorem, we have

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{bD}N(z,\cdot)\wedge f+\int_{bD}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N(z,\cdot)\wedge f-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{bD}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}f(z)+\int_{D}S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\cdot)\wedge f-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{bD}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\zeta)\wedge\overline{\partial}f.\qed

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
D
be a bounded strictly pseudoconvex domain with
C^{k+3+\alpha}
boundary, with
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Suppose
f
is orthogonal to the Bergman space
H^{2}(D)
, is
C^{\infty}
in
D
and is holomorphic in
D\setminus D_{-\delta}
, for some
\delta>0
. Then
f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.

Here we recall the notation.

D_{-\delta}:=\{z\in D:\rho(z)<-\delta\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the Bergman projection for
D
. By assumption
\mathcal{P}f\equiv 0
.
By Proposition
3.6
,
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f=(I+\mathcal{K})\mathcal{P}f\equiv 0
, which implies that
\mathcal{K}f=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f-\mathcal{L}f=-\mathcal{L}f
. Consequently by Proposition
4.1
and the assumption that
\overline{\partial}f\equiv 0
on
bD
,

(4.31)

\displaystyle f(z)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{K}f(z)+\int_{D}S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\cdot)\wedge f-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle+\int_{bD}\Omega^{01}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{K}f(z)+\int_{D}S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\cdot)\wedge f-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f+\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f,\quad z\in D.

Here the kernels
\Omega^{0}_{0,0}
and
N
are given by formulae (
3.11
) and (
3.13
) on
D
:

(4.32)

N(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\frac{1}{[G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)]^{n}}\left(\sum g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)d\zeta_{i}\right)\wedge\left(\sum_{i}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}g_{1}^{i}(z,\zeta)\wedge d\zeta_{i}\right)^{n-1};

\Omega_{0,0}^{0}(z,\zeta)=\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{n}}\frac{1}{|\zeta-z|^{2n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\overline{\zeta_{i}-z_{i}})d\zeta_{i}\wedge\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(d\overline{\zeta}_{j}-d\overline{z}_{j})\wedge d\zeta_{j}\right)^{n-1},

where
G
and
g_{1}
are given by expressions (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).
We can rewrite (
4.31
) as

(4.33)

f+\mathcal{K}f=h:=h_{1}+h_{2}+h_{3},

where we denote

h_{1}(z):=\int_{D}S_{z}(\overline{\partial}_{z}\overline{\partial}_{\zeta}N)(z,\cdot)\wedge f,\quad h_{2}(z):=-\int_{D}N(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f,\quad h_{3}(z):=\int_{D}\Omega^{0}_{0,0}(z,\cdot)\wedge\overline{\partial}f.

We show that each
h_{i}
defines a function in
C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
. By the first statement in (
4.2
), we have
h_{1}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
.
For
h_{2}
, note that the functions
G(z,\zeta),g_{1}(z,\zeta)
are
C^{\infty}
in
z
, and the following estimate (see (
3.8
)) holds

G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)\geq c(-\rho(z)-\rho(\zeta)+|z-\zeta|^{2}),\quad z,\zeta\in\overline{D}.

In particular, for
\zeta\in\operatorname{supp}(\overline{\partial}f)
, i.e.
\zeta\in D_{-\delta}
, the function
G(z,\zeta)-\rho(\zeta)
is bounded below by some positive constant for all
z\in\overline{D}
. Hence in view of (
4.32
),
h_{2}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
. To see that
h_{3}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{D})
, we note that by assumption
\overline{\partial}f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
, and the argument is done using integration by parts.

Now, by Proposition
1.2
,
\mathcal{K}
is a compact operator on the Banach space
C^{k}(\overline{D})
. Thus by the Fredholm alternative, either
I+\mathcal{K}
is invertible or
\ker(I+\mathcal{K})
is non-empty. Suppose
f\in\ker(I+\mathcal{K})
; then
f=-\mathcal{K}f
and
\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{K}f=-\sqrt{-1}f
. If
f\neq 0
, this would imply that
-\sqrt{-1}
is an eigenvalue of the operator
\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{K}
, which is impossible since
\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{K}
is self-adjoint and have only real eigenvalues. Therefore we conclude that
f\equiv 0
, and
\ker(I+\mathcal{K})=\emptyset
. This implies
I+\mathcal{K}
is an invertible operator on the space
C^{k}(\overline{D})
.

Applying this to equation (
4.33
) and
h\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, we obtain
f\in C^{k}(\overline{D})
. By Proposition
1.2
, we have
\mathcal{K}f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
. Hence
f=-\mathcal{K}f+h\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.
∎

We can now finally prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Fix
w_{0}\in D
, we can write the
B(\cdot,w_{0})=\mathcal{P}\varphi
, where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(D)
. Applying Proposition
4.2
to
f=\mathcal{P}\varphi-\varphi
we get
\mathcal{P}\varphi-\varphi\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
. Hence
\mathcal{P}\varphi\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
. ∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
, which will also follow from Proposition
1.2
.
First we need an approximation lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
D
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Suppose
f\in C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
, where
k
is a non-negative integer and
0<\beta<1
. Then there exists a family
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}\in C^{\infty}(D)\cap C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
such that
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
uniformly as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Furthermore,
|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}
is uniformly bounded by
|f|_{k+\beta}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Let
f_{\varepsilon}
be constructed as above. It follows from
[
Shi23
, Prop 2.3]
that
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
in
|\cdot|_{\tau}
, for any
0\leq\tau<k+\beta
.

Proof.

It suffices to take
D
as a special Lipschitz domain of the form
\omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x_{N}>\psi(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1}),|\psi|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\}
, as the general case follows by standard partition of unity argument. There exists some cone
K
such that for any
x\in\omega
,
x+K\subseteq\omega
.
Let
\phi
be a
C^{\infty}
with compact supported in
-K
and such that
\phi\geq 0
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\phi=1
. Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\phi(\frac{x}{\varepsilon})
. Then we can define for
x\in\omega
the function

f_{\varepsilon}(x)=f\ast\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{-K}f(x-\varepsilon y)\phi(y)\,dV(y).

It is clear that
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(D)
and

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-K}[f(x-\varepsilon y)-f(x)]\phi(y)\,dV(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-K}\left|f(x-\varepsilon y)-f(x)\right|\phi(y)\,dV(y)

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\beta}\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{-K}y^{\alpha}\phi(y)\,dV(y)\lesssim|f|_{\beta}\varepsilon^{\beta}.

Hence
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
uniformly in
\omega
. Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\omega
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\left|f_{\varepsilon}(x_{1})-f_{\varepsilon}(x_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{-K}[f(x_{1}-\varepsilon y)-f(x_{2}-\varepsilon y)]\phi(y)\,dV(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq|f|_{\beta}|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\beta}.

Accordingly
|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}
is uniformly bounded by
|f|_{\beta}
. This proves the case
k=0
. For
k\geq 1
the proof is similar and we leave the details to the reader.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
C^{k+3}
boundary, where
k
is a non-negative integer. Then for any
\beta>0
,

|\mathcal{L}f|_{k}\lesssim|f|_{k+\beta}.

Proof.

Write
D^{k}_{z}\mathcal{L}f(z)
as a linear combination of

(5.1)

\int_{D}f(\zeta)\frac{W(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1+\mu}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\mu\leq k,

where
W(z,\zeta)
is some linear combination of products of

D^{\tau_{1}}_{z}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)],\quad D_{z}^{\tau_{2}}\Phi(z,\zeta),\quad\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\leq k.

Applying integration by parts formulae (
3.43
) and (
3.45
) iteratively to the integral (
5.1
) until it can be written as a linear combination of

(5.2)

\int_{bD}D^{\eta_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{W_{0}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,d\sigma(\zeta),\quad\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{W_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\eta_{0},\mu_{0}\leq k.

Here
W_{0}
and
W_{1}
are some linear combination of

(5.3)

D_{\zeta}^{\mu_{1}}D^{\tau_{1}}_{z}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)],\;D^{\mu_{2}}_{\zeta}[(Q^{\prime})^{-1}],\;D^{\mu_{3}+1}_{\zeta}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D^{\mu_{4}}_{\zeta}D_{z}^{\tau_{2}}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D^{\mu_{5}+1}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta),

with
\mu_{i}\leq k
,
0\leq i\leq 5
and
\sum_{i=0}^{5}\mu_{i}\leq k
.
We shall only estimate the domain integral in (
5.2
), as the proof of the boundary integral is similar.
In view of (
5.3
), we can write
W_{1}(z,\zeta)=Y_{1}(z,\zeta)+Y_{2}(z,\zeta)
, where
|Y_{1}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+2}
, and
|Y_{2}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|\zeta-z|
. Write

\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{W_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{Y_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{Y_{2}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta).

The
Y_{2}
integral is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{Y_{2}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)\right|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f|_{k}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f|_{k},

where we used Lemma
3.26
. For the
Y_{1}
integral we use the assumption that
f\in C^{k+\beta}
,
\beta>0
.

\displaystyle\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{Y_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}[D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)-D^{\mu_{0}}f(z)]\frac{Y_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)+D^{\mu_{0}}f(z)\int_{D}\frac{Y_{1}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}.

The first integral on the right-hand side is bounded by

|\rho|_{k+2}|f|_{k+\beta}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|^{\beta}\,dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)|}\lesssim|\rho|_{k+2}|f|_{k+\beta}.

For the other integral, since
Y_{1}
involves derivatives of
\rho
up to order
k+2
, we can apply integration by parts and take one more derivative of
\rho
against
\zeta
. The resulting integrals are bounded by
|\rho|_{k+3}
up to a constant. Summing up the estimates we have

\left|\int_{D}D^{\mu_{0}}f(\zeta)\frac{Y_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)\right|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f|_{k+\beta}.

Consequently this shows that
|D^{k}_{z}\mathcal{L}f(z)|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f|_{k+\beta}
, finishing the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
D
be a strictly pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
k
be a non-negative integer, and
0<\alpha,\beta\leq 1
.

(1)

Suppose
bD\in C^{k+3}
. Then
\mathcal{L}
defines a bounded operator from
C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
to
C^{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
.

(2)

Suppose
bD\in C^{k+3+\alpha}
. Then
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{L}^{\ast}
define bounded operators from
C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
to
C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{\beta}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.

Proof.

(i) We first prove the statement for
\mathcal{L}
and we begin by considering the case
k=0
. Assume first that
0<\beta<1
. Let
f\in C^{\beta}(\overline{D})
and
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be the functions constructed in Lemma
5.1
. In particular, we have

(1)

f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(D)\cap C^{\beta}(\overline{D})
;

(2)

|f_{\varepsilon}-f|_{\eta}\to 0
, for any
0\leq\eta<\beta
(Remark
5.2
).

We claim that for each
\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
with
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{\frac{\beta}{2}}
uniformly bounded by some constant
C_{0}
. Assuming the claim holds, then for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in D
, we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}f(z_{1})-\mathcal{L}f(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{L}f(z_{1})-\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})|+|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})-\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{2})|+|\mathcal{L}f(z_{2})-\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{0}+|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{\frac{\beta}{2}}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{0}+C_{0}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Now, given a function
g\in C^{\eta}(\overline{D})
with
\eta>0
, using the reproducing property of
\mathcal{L}
we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{L}g(z)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{D}[g(\zeta)-g(z)]L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)+g(z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{2}|g|_{\eta}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|^{\eta}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)+|g|_{0}\lesssim(|\rho|_{2}+1)|g|_{\eta},

where in the last inequality we applied Lemma
3.2
.

Applying (
5.5
) with
g=f-f_{\varepsilon}
and using property
(
2
) from above, we get
|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{0}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. It follows from
(
5.4
) that
|\mathcal{L}f(z_{1})-\mathcal{L}f(z_{2})|\leq C_{0}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}
. This shows that
\mathcal{L}f\in C^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
.

It remains to prove the claim, namely,
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{\frac{\beta}{2}}
is bounded by some constant
C_{0}
independent of
\varepsilon
. To this end, we will show that
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}
, where
C_{0}^{\prime}
depends only on
|\rho|_{3}
. Since
|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}\leq|f|_{\beta}
, this proves the claim.

For
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(D)\cap C^{\beta}(\overline{D})
, we have

f_{\varepsilon}(z)-\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z)=\int_{D}\left[f_{\varepsilon}(z)-f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)\right]L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta),

where we used the reproducing property of kernel
L
:
\int_{D}L(z,\zeta)dV(\zeta)\equiv 1
.
Then

\frac{\partial f_{\varepsilon}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)=\int_{D}\frac{\partial f_{\varepsilon}}{\partial z_{i}}(z)L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\left[f_{\varepsilon}(z)-f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)\right]\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_{i}}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta).

The first term on each side cancels out, which leaves us with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial z_{i}}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}[f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)-f_{\varepsilon}(z)]\frac{\partial L}{\partial z_{i}}(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\left[f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)-f_{\varepsilon}(z)\right]\left[\frac{\partial{}_{z_{i}}\left[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)\right]}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}-(n+1)\frac{[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]\partial{}_{z_{i}}\Phi(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\right]\,dV(\zeta).

For
\overline{z}_{i}
derivatives we have a similar expression. By estimate (
3.18
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}\left(\int_{D}\frac{|z-\zeta|^{\beta}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\frac{|z-\zeta|^{\beta}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+2}}\,dV(\zeta)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}\left(\int_{D}\frac{|z-\zeta|^{\beta}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z|^{2-\beta}\Phi(z,\zeta)^{n+1}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}\right),

where in the last step we applied Lemma
3.2
. It follows by Hardy-Littlewood lemma that
\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
and
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{\frac{\beta}{2}}
is bounded by
C_{0}^{\prime}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{\beta}
, where
C_{0}^{\prime}
depends only on
|\rho|_{3}
. Combined with the earlier argument, this proves (i) for
k=0
and
0<\beta<1
. If
k=0
and

\beta=1
, we can repeat the above proof without doing the approximation, obtaining in the end

|\nabla\mathcal{L}f(z)|\lesssim|\rho|_{3}|f|_{1}\left((1+\delta(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad z\in D.

Hence by Hardy-Littlewood lemma,
\mathcal{L}f\in C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{D})
.

Next we consider the case
k\geq 1
. Suppose
f\in C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
, for
0<\beta<1
. As before we first construct
\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
such that

(1)

f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(D)\cap C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
;

(2)

|f_{\varepsilon}-f|_{\eta}\to 0
, for any
0\leq\eta<k+\beta
.

We claim that
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}
is bounded uniformly by some constant
C_{0}
. Assuming the validity of the claim, for
z_{1},z_{2}\in D
and
\ell\leq k
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle|D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f(z_{1})-D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq|D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f(z_{1})-D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})|+|D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{1})-D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{2})|

\displaystyle+|D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f(z_{2})-D^{\ell}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{\ell}+|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{k}+C_{0}|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{\frac{\beta}{2}}.

As before we want to show that
|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{k}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Here the estimate is more subtle since
D\mathcal{L}g=\mathcal{L}Dg
does not hold and thus one cannot estimate as easily as in (
5.5
). Instead we apply Lemma
5.3
to get

(5.7)

|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{k}\lesssim|f-f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\tau},\quad\text{ for any $\tau>0$}.

By property (
2
) above, we have
|f-f_{\varepsilon}|_{\eta}\to 0
for any
\eta<k+\beta
. Hence (
5.7
) implies
|\mathcal{L}(f-f_{\varepsilon})|_{k}\to 0
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
5.6
), we get
\mathcal{L}f\in C^{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
, which proves the reduction.

To finish the proof it remains to show that there exists a constant
C_{0}^{\prime}>0
(which we will show depends only on
|\rho|_{k+\beta}
) such that
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}
. Then by Lemma
5.1
we get
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}|f|_{k+\beta}
. We have

\displaystyle D^{k+1}_{z}\left[f_{\varepsilon}(z)-\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z)\right]

\displaystyle=D^{k+1}_{z}\int_{D}[f_{\varepsilon}(z)-f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)]L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}D^{k+1}_{z}f_{\varepsilon}(z)L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=k+1\\
1\leq\gamma_{2}\leq k\end{subarray}}D^{\gamma_{1}}_{z}f_{\varepsilon}(z)D^{\gamma_{2}}_{z}L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{D}(f_{\varepsilon}(z)-f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta))D^{k+1}_{z}L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta).

The first integral is equal to
D^{k+1}_{z}f(z)
. Hence

\displaystyle D^{k+1}_{z}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=-\int_{D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=k+1\\
1\leq\gamma_{2}\leq k\end{subarray}}D^{\gamma_{1}}_{z}f_{\varepsilon}(z)D^{\gamma_{2}}_{z}L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}[f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)-f_{\varepsilon}(z)]D^{k+1}_{z}L(z,\zeta)\,dV(\zeta):

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2},

where we denote the first and second integral by
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
, respectively.
For
I_{1}
, we can write it as a linear combination of integrals of the form

(5.8)

\displaystyle D_{z}^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(z)\int_{D}\frac{W(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1+\mu_{1}}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\leq k,

where
W
is some linear combination of
D^{\mu_{2}}_{z}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)]
and
D_{z}^{\mu_{3}}\Phi(z,\zeta)
with
\mu_{2},\mu_{3}\leq k
. We apply integration by parts formulae (
3.43
) and (
3.44
) iteratively to the integral in (
5.8
) until it can be written as a linear combination of

(5.9)

\int_{bD}\frac{W_{0}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n}(z,\zeta)}\,d\sigma(\zeta),\quad\int_{D}\frac{W_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta).

Here
W_{0},W_{1}
are linear combinations of products of

D_{\zeta}^{\tau_{1}}D_{z_{i}}^{\mu_{2}}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)],\;D_{\zeta}^{\tau_{2}}[(Q^{\prime})^{-1}],\;D^{\tau_{3}+1}_{\zeta}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D_{\zeta}^{\tau_{4}}D_{z}^{\mu_{3}}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D_{\zeta}^{\tau_{5}+1}\rho(\zeta),

with
\tau_{i}\leq k
,
1\leq i\leq 5
.
Note that all these quantities are bounded by some constant multiple of
|\rho|_{k+3}
.
It follows that the integrals in (
5.9
) and hence
I_{1}
is bounded by

(5.10)

|I_{1}|\lesssim|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}|\rho|_{k+3}\left(\int_{bD}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n}}+\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\right)\lesssim|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}|\rho|_{k+3}(1+\log\delta(z)),

where we applied Lemma
3.4
.
The integral
I_{2}
can be written as a linear combination of integrals of the form:

(5.11)

\int_{D}\frac{[f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)-f_{\varepsilon}(z)]W(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2+\mu}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\mu\leq k.

Here
W(z,\zeta)
is some linear combination of

D^{\tau_{0}}_{z}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)],\quad D^{\tau_{1}}_{z}\Phi(z,\zeta),\quad\tau_{0},\tau_{1}\leq k+1.

If
\mu\leq k-1
we can integrate by parts and estimate just like
I_{1}
to show that
|I_{2}|\lesssim|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}|\rho|_{k+3}(1+\log|\delta(z)|)
. If
\mu=k
, we apply integration by parts formulae (
3.43
) and(
3.45
) until the integral (
5.11
) can be expressed as a linear combination of integrals of the form

(5.12)

\int_{bD}\frac{D^{\eta_{0}}_{\zeta}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)A_{0}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+1}(z,\zeta)}\,d\sigma(\zeta),\quad\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)A_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta),\quad\eta_{0},\mu_{0}\leq k.

Here
A_{0},A_{1}
are linear combination of products of

(5.13)

D^{\mu_{1}}_{\zeta}[l(\zeta)+O^{\prime}(|z-\zeta|)],\;D^{\mu_{2}}_{\zeta}[(Q^{\prime})^{-1}],\;D^{\mu_{3}+1}_{\zeta}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D^{\mu_{4}}_{\zeta}D_{z}\Phi(z,\zeta),\;D^{\mu_{5}+1}_{\zeta}\rho(\zeta),

where
\mu_{i}\leq k
, and
\sum_{i=0}^{5}\mu_{i}=k
. We now use the fact that
\rho\in C^{\infty}(D)\cap C^{k+3}(\overline{D})
satisfies the estimate

|D^{j}_{z}\rho(z)|\lesssim C_{j}|\rho|_{k+3}\left(1+\delta(z)^{k+3-j}\right),\quad j=0,1,2,\dots.

We shall only estimate the domain integral in (
5.12
), as the estimate for the boundary integral is similar. In view of (
5.13
) we can write
A_{1}(z,\zeta)=X_{1}(z,\zeta)+X_{2}(z,\zeta)
, where
|X_{1}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+2}
and
|X_{2}(z,\zeta)|\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|\zeta-z|
. Write

\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)A_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)=\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)X_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)+\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)X_{2}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta).

By estimates (
3.9
) and (
3.25
), we see that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)X_{2}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+2}}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z||\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad\mu_{0}\leq k.

On the other hand, we can write

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{D}\frac{D^{\mu_{0}}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)X_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}\frac{[D^{\mu_{0}}_{\zeta}f_{\varepsilon}(\zeta)-D^{\mu_{0}}_{z}f_{\varepsilon}(z)]X_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta)+D^{\mu_{0}}_{z}f_{\varepsilon}(z)\int_{D}\frac{X_{1}(z,\zeta)}{\Phi^{n+2}(z,\zeta)}\,dV(\zeta).

Since
f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
, the first integral on the right-hand side above is bounded up to a constant by

\displaystyle|\rho|_{k+2}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}\int_{D}\frac{|\zeta-z|^{\beta}}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+2}}\,dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{k+2}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\zeta-z|^{2-\beta}|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\rho|_{k+2}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}\right).

For the second integral on the right-hand side of (
5.14
), we can integrate by parts and bound the resulting expression by

|\rho|_{k+3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}\left(\int_{bD}\frac{d\sigma(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n}}+\int_{D}\frac{dV(\zeta)}{|\Phi(z,\zeta)|^{n+1}}\right)\lesssim|\rho|_{k+3}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k}(1+\log\delta(z)).

Hence we have shown that

|I_{2}|\lesssim|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}|\rho|_{k+3}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}\right).

Combined with the estimate (
5.10
) for
I_{1}
, this shows that
|D_{z}^{k+1}\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}(z)|\lesssim|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}|\rho|_{k+3}\left(1+\delta(z)^{-1+\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}\in C^{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
and
|\mathcal{L}f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}|f_{\varepsilon}|_{k+\beta}
where
C_{0}^{\prime}
depends only on
|\rho|_{k+3}
. This proves the claim and hence the case when
0<\beta<1
.
Finally if
\beta=1
, the same proof works without the use of the approximation.

(ii)
From Proposition
1.2
we know that
\mathcal{K}f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
if
f\in C^{k}(\overline{D})
(and in particular if
f\in C^{k+\beta}(\overline{D})
for
0<\beta\leq 1
). By (i),
\mathcal{L}f\in C^{k+\frac{\beta}{2}}(\overline{D})
.
Since
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f=\mathcal{K}f+\mathcal{L}f
, and
\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2},\frac{\beta}{2}\}=\min\{\alpha,\frac{\beta}{2}\}
, we have

\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{\beta}{2}\}}(\overline{D}).

Finally by the integral equation
(I+\mathcal{K})Pf=\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f
, and the fact that
I+\mathcal{K}
is invertible in the space
C^{k}(\overline{D})
, we get
Pf\in C^{k}(\overline{D})
and thus
\mathcal{K}Pf\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
by Proposition
1.2
. Therefore
Pf=-\mathcal{K}Pf+\mathcal{L}^{\ast}f\in C^{k+\min\{\alpha,\frac{\beta}{2}\}}(\overline{D})
.
∎

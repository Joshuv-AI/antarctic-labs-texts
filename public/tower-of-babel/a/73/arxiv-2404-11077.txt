Sylow theorems for supergroups
Vera Serganova, Alexander Sherman, Dmitry Vaintrob

Abstract
. We introduce Sylow subgroups and 0 0 -groups to the theory of complex algebraic supergroups, which mimic Sylow subgroups and p p -groups in the theory of finite groups. We prove that Sylow subgroups are always 0 0 -groups, and show that they are unique up to conjugacy. Further, we give an explicit classification of 0 0 -groups which will be very useful for future applications. Finally, we prove an analogue of Sylow’s third theorem on the number of Sylow subgroups of a supergroup.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we introduce
0-groups
and
Sylow subgroups
of supergroups, which are super-analogues of
p
-groups and Sylow subgroups of finite groups. We classify
0
-groups, and establish analogues of several basic statements concerning Sylow subgroups in the supergroup setting. These include the uniqueness of Sylow subgroups up to conjugacy.

1.1.
Finite groups

Sylow subgroups, and more generally
p
-local subgroups, play a central, if mysterious, role in the representation theory of finite groups. As a simple example, consider the McKay conjecture (see
[
14
]
): the number of simple representations of a finite group
G
whose dimension is coprime to
p
should be the same as that of the normalizer of a
p
-Sylow subgroup.

Sylow subgroups, and more generally
p
-local subgroups, are particularly important when studying modular (i.e. characteristic
p
) representations of a finite group
G
. Any block of representations determines a defect subgroup
H
(which is a
p
-group), and restriction to the the defect is faithful on derived categories. For instance, a
p
-Sylow subgroup is a defect subgroup of the principal block, and thus detects all the homological complexity of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\Bbbk}G
(
\Bbbk
an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p
).

Such guiding principles in modular representation theory often go under the heading of local representation theory. Famous results and conjectures arising from these ideas include Green’s correspondence, the Broué abelian defect conjecture, and the Alperin weight conjecture. In addition, the study of
p
-local subgroups plays a key role in understanding the Balmer spectrum of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\Bbbk}G
.

1.2.
Supergroups

One may initially look suspiciously upon attempted analogies between finite groups and supergroups. Supergroups are geometric in nature, usually involving high-dimensional group manifolds, and are not determined by their closed points. Nevertheless, as we seek to demonstrate here and in future work, there are clear (if not always perfect) analogies, and much can be understood by naively following them.

Perhaps the first indications of an analogy appear in the works of Boe, Kujawa and Nakano on cohomological support varieties (
[
3
]
). For a simple Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
, they define a detecting subalgebra
\mathfrak{f}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, which conjecturally controls the cohomological support variety of a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, similarly to how a Sylow subgroup would. Their ideas led them more recently to a description of the Balmer spectrum for
\operatorname{Rep}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(m|n)
, see
[
4
]
. In a future work, we will use our ideas to answer many questions raised in
[
3
]
about cohomological support varieties

1.3.
Sylow subgroups

We take a broader approach to these questions. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a complex algebraic supergroup such that
\mathcal{G}_{0}
, the even underlying algebraic group of
\mathcal{G}
, has reductive identity component. Such supergroups are called quasireductive. Then
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
, the category of all
\mathcal{G}
-modules (not necessarily finite-dimensional) is a Frobenius category, just as
\operatorname{Rep}_{\Bbbk}G
is. In fact, every finitely generated Frobenius symmetric tensor category of moderate growth over the complex numbers is equivalent to the category of finite-dimensional representations of some quasireductive supergroup. This follows from Deligne’s theorem
[
6
]
and Prop. 2.3.1 of
[
8
]
.

In
[
17
]
, the first two named authors introduced the notion of a
splitting subgroup

\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, which analogizes the condition of containing a Sylow subgroup. Namely, we require that
\mathcal{K}
is quasireductive and that the restriction functor gives a faithful embedding of
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G})
inside
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{K})
.
From this one may define a
Sylow subgroup
of
\mathcal{G}
to be a minimal splitting subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
. With this definition, the existence of Sylow subgroups is a trivial matter.

In
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
, nontrivial splitting subgroups were constructed for simple supergroups by computing volumes of certain homogeneous superspaces. Already these results led to strong projectivity criterion for simple supergroups, as explained in
[
19
]
. However, the questions of whether these subgroups are Sylow, i.e. minimal, and further if they are unique up to conjugacy, were not addressed.

In this paper we deal with these questions for arbitrary quasireductive supergroups. Namely, we prove the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive. Then all Sylow subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
are unique up to conjugacy.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we introduce analogs of
p
-groups to the super setting, which we call
0-groups
. Just as in the case of finite groups, one can view
0
-groups as an orthogonal notion to that of a reductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
, i.e. one for which
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is semisimple.

We use 0 in our notation on the one hand because we are working in characteristic
0
. Another reason is as follows: one may define a
p
-group
P
to be one for which
\mbox{vol}(P/H)=|P/H|
is divisible by
p
for every nontrivial subgroup
H\subseteq P
. Equivalently,
\mbox{vol}(P/H)
is zero in
\Bbbk
. For
0
-groups, we have the following equivalent characterizations. (We refer to Section
7.3
for definitions.)

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem/Definition)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be quasireductive. Then
\mathcal{O}
is a
0
-group if one of any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

\mathcal{O}
is its own Sylow subgroup.

(2)

\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{K})=0
for any proper, quasireductive subgroup
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
with
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{k})
a trivial
\mathcal{K}
-module (a necessary condition in order for volume to be defined).

(3)

\mathcal{O}
is oddly generated and
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}^{neat}=\{0\}
.

(4)

\mathcal{O}
is oddly generated and the nil-cone is contained in the cone of self-commuting odd elements.

In Theorem
1.2
, we write
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{K})
for the volume of an associated compact CS manifold, see Section 3. In the super setting, volume forms are generalized to Berezin forms, hence the importance of the Berezin module
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{k})
in defining volumes.
Understanding the relationship between volumes and splitting subgroups was the key ingredient that allowed us to make progress on these problems. Namely, we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups. Then
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})
is a trivial
\mathcal{K}
-module, and
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K})\neq 0
.

Using Theorem
1.3
, we obtain the following characterization of Sylow subgroups.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups. Then
\mathcal{O}
is a Sylow subgroup if and only if it is a splitting
0
-subgroup.

While Theorem
1.2
is a very neat characterization of
0
-groups, it is useful in practice to have a more explicit description of what a
0
-group looks like. In the setting of finite groups, one could never hope to classify all
p
-groups; however they are all nilpotent, and this controls aspects of their representation theory.

While the problem of classifying
0
-groups is also wild, we have the following:

Theorem 1.5
.

Every
0
-group
\mathcal{O}
is isomorphic to a central extension of
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a Takiff 0-supergroup, and
\mathcal{V}
is an odd abelian supergroup.

For the definition of a Takiff 0-supergroup, we refer to Section
7.1
. Theorem
1.5
allows us to understand something about the representation theory of
0
-groups, which is both very useful in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, and will be critical in future works.

1.4.
Maximal 0-subgroups

Our analogy with finite groups breaks down in the following way: for a finite group
G
, every maximal
p
-subgroup is Sylow. However it is not true that every maximal
0
-subgroup is Sylow, i.e. it need not be splitting. A simple counterexample is provided by the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}(n|n)
corresponding to the following subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(n|n)
:

\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
\lambda I_{n}&A\end{bmatrix},

where
A,B
are arbitrary
n\times n
matrices, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

We expect that there are only finitely many maximal
0
-subgroups up to conjugacy. An important question is whether they have a representation-theoretic meaning, and at this point we have no indications as such. The study of maximal
0
-subgroups will be taken up in future work.

1.5.
The third Sylow theorem

The third Sylow theorem may be stated as follows: if
G
is a finite group and
P
is a Sylow subgroup of
G
, then
|G/N_{G}(P)|
is congruent to
1
(mod
p
), i.e.
\mbox{vol}(G/N_{G}(P))=1
in the base field.

Now let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive with Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}
. Our definition of volume is only well-defined up to non-zero scaling, so we cannot hope to assign meaning to
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O}))
. However we have another approach to understand the ‘size’ of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
. Let
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
, and let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
such that
[x,x]
is semisimple in
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
; we call such an
x
homological. Then we obtain the Duflo-Serganova functor
DS_{x}:\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}\to s\operatorname{Vec}
, a symmetric monoidal functor (see Section
9.2
). For a finite-dimensional module
V\in\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
, we have
\operatorname{sdim}(DS_{x}V)=\operatorname{sdim}V
, so in this sense
DS_{x}
preserves size.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{0}
be a maximal torus of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, and define

W_{\mathcal{G}}:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})/\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T}),\ \ \ W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{T})/\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{T}).

Then
W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}\subseteq W_{\mathcal{G}}
are finite groups, and for a generic (see Section
9.2
) homological
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
, we have a natural isomorphism of algebras:

DS_{x}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})]\cong\mathbb{C}[W_{\mathcal{G}}/W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}].

In this way, we may view
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
as having ‘size’
|W_{\mathcal{G}}/W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}|
, which we note is a finite number, and is often equal to one (see Section
9.3
).

1.6.
Supergroups vs. superalgebras

So far we have only discussed supergroups, but in fact our above discussion works just as well for quasireductive Lie superalgebras (see Definition
2.2
). In fact,
0
-superalgebras and Sylow subalgebra are often easier to discuss and work with than
0
-groups and Sylow subgroups.

In Section 2 we will explain the passages between supergroups and superalgebras, and throughout the paper we will work with one or the other, as is convenient. All structure theorems stated about supergroups, for example, will have an analogue for superalgebras, and vice-versa.

1.7.
Future work and open problems

In an upcoming preprint, the first two named authors and J. Pevtsova will continue the work of extending the analogy between finite groups and supergroups. Namely, we will define analogs of elementary abelian subgroups in the super setting, and prove a strong projectivity criteria for supergroups which analogizes Chouinard’s theorem. Using this, we will give an explicit description of the cohomological support variety.

Further questions of future interest are to develop a theory of defect subgroups for blocks, and understanding what Green’s correspondence looks like for supergroups. We expect that a meaningful, general definition of atypicality and defect should arise from our machinery.

1.8.
Outline of article

In Section 2 we recall the definitions of quasireductive supergroups/Lie superalgebras, and explain how to pass between them via the notion of global forms. The notion of an oddly generated supergroup/superalgebra will be of particular importance later on. In Section 3 we define algebraic integrals on homogeneous superspaces and explain the consequences of admitting an algebraic integral. Section 4 recalls necessary language and facts from the theory of Berezin integration on CS manifolds. In Section 5 we apply Berezin integration to CS manifolds arising from complex homogeneous superspaces, and use this to classify when a homogeneous superspace admits an algebraic integral. Section 6 then defines Sylow subgroups and 0-groups, and relates their definitions to the work on volumes in Section 5. Section 7 characterizes and classifies
0
-groups, and deduces facts about their representation theory. In Section 8 we prove that all Sylow subgroups are unique up to conjugacy. Finally, in Section 9 we prove the third Sylow theorem as stated in the introduction.

2.
Quasireductive supergroups and quasireductive Lie superalgebras

Throughout, unless stated otherwise, we work over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. For a super vector space
V
, we write
V=V_{\overline{0}}\oplus V_{\overline{1}}
for its parity decomposition.

2.1.
Supergroups and superalgebras

By a supergroup
\mathcal{G}
we mean an algebraic supergroup, that is the spectrum of a supercommutative Hopf superalgebra, which we write as
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]
. Given a supergroup
\mathcal{G}
, we write
\mathcal{G}_{0}
for its even part, given by the spectrum of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]/(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]_{\overline{1}})
, which will be an algebraic group.
We use the symbols
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{O}\dots
for supergroups, and the symbols
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{o}\dots
for Lie superalgebras. If not otherwise stated,
\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{o},\dots
will be the Lie superalgebra of
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K},\mathcal{O},\dots
. However in some cases our notation will be set up so that
\mathfrak{g}
and
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
are distinct. However, usually
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
. We hope the relationship between
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathfrak{g}
is always clear from context.

Whenever we discuss subsupergroups of a supergroup
\mathcal{G}
we will simply refer to them as subgroups, and same for Lie subalgebras, etc.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a supergroup
\mathcal{G}
is quasireductive if the connected component of the identity of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is reductive. In particular, we do not assume that
\mathcal{G}
is connected. We write
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
for the abelian, symmetric monoidal category of rational
\mathcal{G}
-modules; that is, the category of comodules over
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]
. We emphasize that infinite-dimensional modules are included.

In much of the rest of the text we use many known facts about quasireductive supergroups. We refer to
[
16
]
for the foundations of their structure and representation theory.

2.2.
Global forms of quasireductive Lie superalgebras

Definition 2.2
.

We say that a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is quasireductive if
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is reductive and acts
\operatorname{ad}
-semisimply on
\mathfrak{g}
. We write
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
for the category of locally finite
\mathfrak{g}
-modules which are semisimple over
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. We say a module is locally finite if it is a union of its finite-dimensional submodules.

Note that there may not exist a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
for which
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are quasireductive superalgebras if
\mathfrak{g}
is quasireductive and
\mathfrak{k}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}}
acts
ad
-semisimply on
\mathfrak{g}
. In particular,
\mathfrak{k}
will also be quasireductive under this definition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a quasireductive Lie superalgebra (see Definition
2.2
). Then we call a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
;

(2)

if
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is any subgroup with
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{K}
, then
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{G}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the quasireductive Lie superalgebra with even basis
x
and odd basis
H
, and such that
[H,H]=x
. Then for any positive integer
n
there exits a global form
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension
(n|1)
. In fact for a fixed
n>1
, there are uncountably many such global forms up to isomorphism.

Lemma 2.6
.

(1)

Every quasireductive Lie superalgebra admits a global form, and it is always connected.

(2)

If
\mathcal{G}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G})_{\overline{1}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a global form of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
.

(3)

If
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are quasireductive Lie superalgebras and
\mathcal{G}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
, then there exists a unique subgroup
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{K}
, and
\mathcal{K}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{k}
.

Proof.

We first prove (3). Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
corresponding to the super Harish-Chandra pair (see
[
5
]
)
(\mathcal{K}_{0},\mathfrak{k})
, where
\mathcal{K}_{0}
is the minimal algebraic subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
whose Lie superalgebra contains
\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}}
. Then it is clear that
\mathcal{K}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{k}
and is connected.

To prove (1), apply Ado’s theorem for Lie superalgebras to obtain a faithful representation
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
which is semisimple over
\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}}
. Now we are reduced to (3) where
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}(V)
.

Finally, to prove (2), if
\mathcal{G}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
, then use (3) to obtain
\mathcal{K}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}
which is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
is
\mathcal{K}_{0}
-stable with respect to the adjoint action and
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\subset\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{K}_{0}
. Hence we obtain a super Harish-Chandra pair
(\mathcal{K}_{0},\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{K}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}})
, which will give a quasireductive subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
whose Lie superalgebra contains
\mathfrak{g}
. Thus
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{G}
by the definition of a global form.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is a full monoidal, topologizing, Serre subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
, and in particular contains the principal block of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

We have a natural faithful, exact tensor functor
F:\operatorname{Rep}^{fd}\mathcal{G}\to\operatorname{Rep}^{fd}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
, where
(-)^{fd}
denotes the full subcategories of finite-dimensional modules. Note that these are super-Tannakian categories. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the finitely generated super-Tannakian subcategory of
\operatorname{Rep}^{fd}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
generated by
F(V)
, where
V
is a finite-dimensional
\mathcal{G}
-module. By super-Tannakian reconstruction,
\mathcal{C}=\operatorname{Rep}^{fd}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
for some supergroup
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, and we have a natural inclusion of supergroups
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. However, we have
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\subseteq\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
, this implies
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}=\mathcal{G}
, meaning that
F
is also full.
∎

2.3.
Oddly generated supergroups and superalgebras

Definition 2.8
.

(1)

We say that a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
is oddly generated if
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a global form of
[(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G})_{\overline{1}},(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G})_{\overline{1}}]
.

(2)

We say that a quasireductive Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is oddly generated if
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}=[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]
.

Remark 2.9
.

(i)

An oddly generated supergroup is necessarily connected.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{g}
is an oddly generated superalgebra, then any global form of it is also oddly generated. However, if
\mathcal{G}
is oddly generated then
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
need not be oddly generated.

To help put the following lemma in context, we remark that given a global form
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and a subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, there may or may not exist a subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
for which
\mathcal{H}
is a global form of
\mathfrak{h}
. Further, when such a subalgebra exists it need not be unique.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{g}
is quasireductive, and let
\mathcal{G}
be a global form of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have a
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant bijection of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-sets

\mathfrak{G}:\text{sAlg}_{ogen}\to\text{sGrp}_{ogen}.

Here
\text{sAlg}_{ogen}
is the set of oddly generated subalgebras
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, and
\text{sGrp}_{ogen}
is the set of oddly generated subgroups
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

The map
\mathfrak{G}
takes an oddly generated subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
to a global form
\mathfrak{G}(\mathfrak{h})\subseteq\mathcal{G}
as in (3) of Lemma
2.6
. The inverse map
\mathfrak{G}^{-1}
takes an oddly generated subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
with the given properties to the Lie superalgebra

\mathfrak{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{H})=[(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H})_{\overline{1}},(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H})_{\overline{1}}]+(\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{H})_{\overline{1}}.

This correspondence is both clearly bijective and intertwines the adjoint action of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
on
\text{sAlg}_{ogen}
with the conjugation action on
\text{sGrp}_{ogen}
.
∎

3.
Algebraic integrals on homogeneous affine supervarieties

3.1.
Homogeneous spaces

Given a subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we may construct the smooth homogeneous supervariety
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
, see
[
13
]
. Note that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
admits an action of
\mathcal{G}
on the left by translation. If both
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
are quasireductive, then
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
will be an affine supervariety.

3.2.
Geometric induction

Given a a subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, we have a functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}:\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{H}\to\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
given by

\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}V=(V\otimes\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}])^{\mathcal{H}},

where we take the
\mathcal{H}
action on
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]
by right translation.
In the case that both
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
are quasireductive,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}(-)
is exact.

Observe that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]^{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
. Further,
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}V
always has the structure of a
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
-module which is compatible with the
\mathcal{G}
-action.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups, and let
V
be a simple
\mathcal{H}
-module.
Then
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}V
is simple as a
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]-\mathcal{G}
module. In particular,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
is a simple
\mathcal{G}
-algebra.

Proof.

This follows immediately from the fact that
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}
is an exact functor and Barr-Beck monadicity.

∎

3.3.
Algebraic integrals

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups. An algebraic integral on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant map
\iota:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\to\mathbb{C}
, which may be either even or odd.

Given an algebraic integral
\iota
on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
, we obtain a symmetric bilinear form
(-,-)_{\iota}
on
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
given by
(f,g)_{\iota}=\iota(fg)
, and satisfying
(fg,h)_{\iota}=(f,gh)_{\iota}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\iota\neq 0
then
(-,-)_{\iota}
is nondegenerate, i.e. its kernel is trivial as a bilinear form.

Proof.

Suppose that
(-,-)_{\iota}
has a nontrivial kernel, which we call
K
. Clearly
K
is
\mathcal{G}
-stable, and if
g\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
,
f\in K
, then
(fg,h)_{\iota}=(f,gh)_{\iota}=0
. Thus
fg\in K
, meaning
K
is a
\mathcal{G}
-stable ideal. This contradicts Corollary
3.1
, so we are done.
∎

Notice that the form
(-,-)_{\iota}
induces a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant map
\Phi_{\iota}:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\to\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\iota\neq 0
then the induced map
\Phi_{\iota}:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\to\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*}
is an isomorphism onto the
\mathcal{G}
-finite vectors
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*})
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
,
\Phi_{\iota}
is injective. For surjectivity, first observe that for any simple
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-module
L
, we have

[\operatorname{Res}_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}^{\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]:L]<\infty.

Indeed, we have:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}(L,\operatorname{Res}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}])

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}L,\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{H}}(\operatorname{Res}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}\otimes_{\mathcal{U}\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}L),\mathbb{C}),

and the latter is clearly finite. Thus we may write

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\cong\bigoplus\limits_{L}L^{\oplus n_{L}},

where the isomorphism is of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-modules, and
L
runs over all simple
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-modules up to parity. It follows that

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*}\cong\prod\limits_{L}(L^{*})^{\oplus n_{L}},

It is straightforward to see that

\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}(\prod\limits_{L}(L^{*})^{\oplus n_{L}})=\bigoplus\limits_{L}(L^{*})^{\oplus n_{L}}.

It follows that
\Phi_{\iota}
defines an injective map

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\cong\bigoplus\limits_{L}L^{\oplus n_{L}}\hookrightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{L}(L^{*})^{\oplus n_{L}}.

Thus we obtain
n_{L^{*}}\leq n_{L}
for all
L
, implying that
n_{L}=n_{L^{*}}
for all
L
, meaning that
\Phi_{\iota}
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
admits a nonzero algebraic integral
\iota
. Then any other algebraic integral on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
is a scalar multiple of
\iota
.

Proof.

Indeed, we see that
\Phi_{\iota}(1)=\iota
, thus the image of
1
determines the integral. However the existence of
\iota\neq 0
implies that
\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
by Corollary
3.4
. Thus

(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*})^{\mathcal{G}}=\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*})^{\mathcal{G}}\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{C}\langle 1\rangle.

∎

4.
CS manifolds, Berezin forms, and Berezin integration

For further details on CS manifolds, Berezins, and Berezin integration, we refer to
[
12
]
,
[
15
]
, and
[
22
]
.

4.1.
Integration on CS manifolds

Recall that a CS supermanifold is a locally ringed space that is locally isomorphic to

\mathbb{R}_{CS}^{m|n}:=(\mathbb{R}^{m},C(\mathbb{R}^{m})\otimes{\bigwedge}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{C}^{n})),

for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Here
C(\mathbb{R}^{m})
denotes the algebra of smooth, complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be either a CS supermanifold or a smooth complex algebraic supervariety. Then we have the usual notion of the cotangent bundle
\Omega_{\mathcal{X}}
on
\mathcal{X}
. Thus we may consider
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}:=\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathcal{X}})
, the sheaf of Berezin forms on
\mathcal{X}
. To be precise, for a vector bundle
\mathcal{V}
of dimension
(r|s)
,
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathcal{V})
is defined to be the line bundle with local generator given by a local, ordered, homogeneous basis
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r},b_{1},\dots,b_{s})
of
\mathcal{V}
, and such that
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r},b_{1},\dots,b_{s})=\operatorname{Ber}(A)(c_{1},\dots,c_{r},d_{1},\dots,d_{s})
if the second basis is transformed into the first basis via
A
, a local automorphism of
\mathcal{V}
.

We now restrict to the CS manifold setting to define Berezin integration. Given homogeneous coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}
, we write
\frac{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}}{d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n}}
for the generator of
\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}})
given by
(dx_{1},\dots,dx_{m},d\xi_{1},\dots,d\xi_{n})
.

Given a compactly supported Berezin form
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}
with coordinates
x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
, we may present it as

\omega=\sum\limits_{I}f_{I}\xi_{I}\frac{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}}{d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n}},

where the
f_{I}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{m})
are compactly supported, and
I
runs over all subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. Then we define

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}}\omega=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}f_{\{1,\dots,n\}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}.

That this expression is independent of coordinate changes is proven in Theorem 11.3.2 of
[
15
]
.

Now let
\omega\in\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
be an arbitrary compactly supported section, and let
(\mathcal{U}_{i},\varphi_{i})
be a partition of unity of
\mathcal{X}
. We define

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\omega=\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\varphi_{i}\omega.

By the independence of the choice of coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}
in the local setting, we find this is again well-defined.

4.2.
Integration along odd fibers

We define an integration along odd fibres morphism, which we emphasize works in both the algebraic and CS settings. At the end we show they are compatible in the setting of interest to us.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth supervariety
or
a CS manifold, and suppose that we have a splitting
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Define the integration along odd fibres map

\pi_{*}:\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{top}

to be the following map of sheaves: first suppose that
\mathcal{X}=\text{Spec}A
is affine if
\mathcal{X}
is a supervariety, and if
\mathcal{X}
is a CS manifold then suppose that
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{m|n}_{CS}
, and set
A=\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
. In both cases write
\overline{A}=A/(A_{\overline{1}})
.

Our splitting
\pi
corresponds to a splitting
\pi^{*}:\overline{A}\to A
of the projection
A\to\overline{A}
. Let us further assume that
A
has global coordinates by shrinking down our affine neighborhood further. If
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
are global coordinates for
\overline{A}\subseteq A
, and
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
are global odd coordinates for
A
, then
x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
will be global coordinates for
A
, and we have an algebra isomorphism

A\cong\overline{A}[\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}].

Since
\frac{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}}{d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n}}
trivializes
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
, we have an isomorphism of
\overline{A}
-modules:

\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}\cong\bigoplus\limits_{I}\overline{A}\xi_{I}\frac{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}}{d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n}},

where
I
runs over all subsets of
\{1,\dots,n\}
. It therefore makes sense to define

\pi_{*}\left(\sum\limits_{I}f_{I}\xi_{I}\frac{dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}}{d\xi_{1}\cdots d\xi_{n}}\right)=f_{\{1,\dots,n\}}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}.

That this is independent of coordinates is given for instance in Section 6 of Chapter 4 of
[
12
]
. To extend to all of
\mathcal{X}
, we patch together the local constructions. The following lemmas are now clear.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be either a smooth algebraic supervariety or a CS manifold. If we have a splitting
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
, then
\pi_{*}:\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{top}
is a surjective map of sheaves. In particular, if
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is an affine variety or a manifold, then it defines a surjective map on global sections.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a CS manifold with a splitting
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
, then for a compactly supported
\omega\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}})
we have

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\omega=\int_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}\pi_{*}(\omega).

Proof.

This follows directly from the definition of Berezin integration and
\pi_{*}
.
∎

4.3.
Real forms

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a smooth algebraic supervariety such that
\mathcal{X}_{0}
has a real form
(\mathcal{X}_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Then by
[
21
]
,
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}:=((\mathcal{X}_{0})_{\mathbb{R}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}^{an}|_{(\mathcal{X}_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}})
has the structure of a CS manifold. Write
i:\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{X}
for the natural map of locally ringed spaces, and let
i_{0}:(\mathcal{X}_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
be the embedding of underlying spaces.

Then we have a natural map
i^{*}:\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}}
, where to emphasize,
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
denotes the Berezin sheaf of the complex algebraic cotangent sheaf
\Omega_{\mathcal{X}}
, and
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}}
is the Berezin sheaf of the CS cotangent sheaf
\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

A splitting of algebraic supervarieties
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
determines a unique, compatible splitting
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}:\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}\to(\mathcal{X}_{0})_{\mathbb{R}}
, and we have for
\omega\in\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
:

i_{0}^{*}(\pi_{*}(\omega))=(\pi_{\mathbb{R}})_{*}(i^{*}(\omega)).

Proof.

The fact that
\pi
determines a unique splitting
\pi_{\mathbb{R}}
is immediate. To obtain the equality, we note that if
x_{1},\dots,x_{m},\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}
are local coordinates on
\mathcal{X}
, then they restrict to local coordinates on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathbb{R}}
. The rest follows from the formula for integration along odd fibers.
∎

5.
Volumes of homogeneous superspaces

5.1.
Existence of compact forms

In the following, we recall that maximal compact subgroups of a complex algebraic group whose identity component is reductive are unique up to conjugacy, see
[
2
]
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quasireductive supergroup with quasireductive subgroup
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{0,c}
denote a maximal compact subgroup of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
, and let
\mathcal{G}_{0,c}
be a maximal compact subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{0}
containing
\mathcal{H}_{0,c}
. These give compact real forms of
\mathcal{G}_{0},\mathcal{H}_{0}
respectively, and so we obtain compact real CS forms
\mathcal{H}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{c}
of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
, respectively.

Further,
\mathcal{G}_{0,c}/\mathcal{H}_{0,c}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}/\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a real form, and so we may consider the compact CS manifold given by

\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{H}_{c}:=(\mathcal{G}_{0,c}/\mathcal{H}_{0,c},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}^{an}|_{\mathcal{G}_{0,c}/\mathcal{H}_{0,c}}).

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups with Lie superalgebras
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have a natural isomorphism of super vector spaces

\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})^{\mathcal{H}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}})^{\mathcal{G}}.

In particular,
\operatorname{dim}\Gamma(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}})^{\mathcal{G}}\leq 1
, and further if
\omega\in\Gamma(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}})^{\mathcal{G}}
is non-zero, then it trivializes
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

Indeed,

\Gamma(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}})^{\mathcal{G}}={{}^{\mathcal{G}}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}]\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}))^{\mathcal{H}}=\mathbb{C}\langle 1\rangle\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})^{\mathcal{H}}.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups, set
\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
, and suppose that
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}})
is non-zero and
\mathcal{G}
-invariant. Let
\mathcal{X}_{c}=\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{H}_{c}
with inclusion
i:\mathcal{X}_{c}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}
.
Then the morphism
\iota_{\omega_{\mathcal{X}}}:\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\iota_{\omega_{\mathcal{X}}}:f\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{X}_{c}}i^{*}(f\omega_{\mathcal{X}}),

is non-zero and
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant. In particular,
\iota_{\omega_{\mathcal{X}}}
defines an algebraic integral on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

We first note that by Lemma
5.1
,
\omega_{\mathcal{X}}
trivializes
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
. Further, since
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is the quotient of a reductive group by a reductive subgroup,
\Omega^{top}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
admits a
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-invariant trivializing form.

Since
\mathcal{X}
is smooth and affine, it is split; thus choose a splitting
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and write
\pi_{c}:\mathcal{X}_{c}\to(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}
for the induced splitting of
\mathcal{X}_{c}
(see Lemma
4.3
). By Lemma
4.1
,
\pi_{*}:\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}\to\Omega^{top}_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
is surjective, so there exists
f\in\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
such that
\pi_{*}(f\omega_{\mathcal{X}})
is
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-invariant and trivializes
\Omega_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}^{top}
. Thus if
i_{0}:(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{0}
is the natural inclusion, we have that
i_{0}^{*}(\pi_{*}(f\omega_{\mathcal{X}}))
is a trivializing top differential form on
(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}
, implying its integral over
(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}
is nonzero.
Thus by Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
,

\displaystyle\iota_{\omega_{\mathcal{X}}}(f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathcal{X}_{c}}i^{*}(f\omega_{\mathcal{X}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}}(\pi_{c})_{*}(i^{*}(f\omega_{\mathcal{X}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}}i_{0}^{*}(\pi_{*}(f\omega_{X}))\neq 0.

We refer to Thm. 16 of
[
19
]
for the proof that
\iota_{\omega_{\mathcal{X}}}
is
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant.
∎

5.2.
Existence of integrals

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive. Then there exists a nontrivial algebraic integral
\iota:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\to\mathbb{C}
if and only if
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is trivial as an
\mathcal{H}
-module (i.e. is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{C}
or
\Pi\mathbb{C}
.)

Note that Theorem
5.3
was proven in the analytic setting in
[
1
]
.

Proof.

Observe that
\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
. We always have a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant natural pairing

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\otimes\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{C}

given by

f\otimes\omega\mapsto\int\limits_{\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{H}_{c}}i^{*}(f\omega)

as in Proposition
5.2
. We claim this defines a perfect pairing. To see this, notice that if it is not zero it is automatically perfect because otherwise a kernel would define nontrivial
\mathcal{G}
-stable ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
or a
\mathcal{G}
-stable submodule of
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
, contradicting Lemma
3.1
. On the other hand it is not zero by Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, using the existence of a nonvanishing top form on
\mathcal{G}_{0,c}/\mathcal{H}_{0,c}
whose integral is 0.

Thus we obtain in every case an isomorphism
\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})\cong\Gamma_{\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]^{*}
as in Corollary
3.4
. Therefore, if
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
has an algebraic integral, we obtain via this isomorphism a
\mathcal{G}
-invariant Berezin form, implying that
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is trivial.
Conversely, if
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is a trivial
\mathcal{H}
-module, then a global
\mathcal{G}
-invariant Berezin form exists, call it
\omega
. Then via Proposition
5.2
we obtain a non-zero algebraic integral
\iota_{\omega}
as desired.
∎

5.3.
Volumes of homogeneous superspaces

Definition 5.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a compact cs manifold and let
\omega\in\operatorname{Ber}_{\mathcal{X}}
be a trivialization. Then we define the volume of
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\omega
to be:

\mbox{vol}(\omega)=\mbox{vol}_{\omega}(\mathcal{X}):=\int\limits_{\mathcal{X}}\omega.

If
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
are quasireductive supergroups, with
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
a trivial
\mathcal{H}
-module, we define

\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}):=\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}|_{\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{H}_{c}}),

where
\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}}
is a non-zero
\mathcal{G}
-invariant Berezin form on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
, and we have chose compact forms
\mathcal{H}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{c}
. Observe that
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
is only well-defined up to non-zero scalar; as a result it is only well-defined to state that
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
is either zero or non-zero.

We remark that for
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})
to be well-defined in Definition
5.4
, we need it to be independent of the choices
\mathcal{H}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{c}
. For this we use Cor. 1.3 of Chpt. 7 in
[
2
]
.

For the following proposition, let
\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{B}
be a locally trivial fibration of compact CS manifolds with fibre
\mathcal{F}
. Then we have a short exact sequence of vector bundles on
\mathcal{E}
:

0\to\pi^{*}\Omega_{\mathcal{B}}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{E}}\to\Omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}\to 0.

This implies that
\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}})\cong\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}})\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(\pi^{*}\Omega_{\mathcal{B}})
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}
,
\omega_{\mathcal{B}}
, and
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
are global Berezin forms on
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{B},
and
\mathcal{F}
respectively. Suppose further that
\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}})
is trivialized by a section
\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}
satisfying that
\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}|_{\mathcal{F}}=\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
along each fibre
\mathcal{F}
, and that
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}=\pi^{*}\omega_{\mathcal{B}}\cdot\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}
. Then,

\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{E}})=\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{B}})\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}}).

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{U}_{i},\varphi)
be a partition of unity of
\mathcal{B}
on which our fibre bundle is trivial, i.e.
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})\cong\mathcal{U}_{i}\times\mathcal{F}
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{E}}\omega_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{U}_{i})}\pi^{*}(\varphi_{i})\pi^{*}(\omega_{\mathcal{B}})\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i}\times\mathcal{F}}\varphi_{i}\omega_{\mathcal{B}}\omega_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\mathcal{F}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\varphi_{i}\omega_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\omega_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}})\sum\limits_{i}\int_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\varphi_{i}\omega_{\mathcal{B}}=\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{F}})\mbox{vol}(\omega_{\mathcal{B}}).

∎

6.
Sylow subgroups and
0
-groups

6.1.
Splitting subgroups

We recall the following definition from
[
17
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive supergroups. Then we say that
\mathcal{H}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

(1)

\mathbb{C}\langle 1\rangle
splits off
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]
as a
\mathcal{G}
-module.

(2)

For every
\mathcal{G}
-module
M
, the natural map of
\mathcal{G}
-modules
M\to\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathcal{H}}^{\mathcal{G}}\operatorname{Res}^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}}(M)
splits.

(3)

The natural map
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{G}}^{i}(M,N)\to\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{H}}^{i}(M,N)
is injective for all
i
and for all
\mathcal{G}
-modules
M,N
.

(4)

The natural map
\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{G}}^{1}(M,N)\to\text{Ext}_{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(M,N)
is injective for all
\mathcal{G}
-modules
M,N
.

(5)

Every
\mathcal{G}
-module
M
is relatively projective over
\mathcal{H}
.

Remark 6.2
.

Applying our definition of splitting to a finite group
G
over a field of characteristic
p
, one sees that a subgroup
K
of
G
is splitting if and only if it contains a Sylow
p
-subgroup, or equivalently
\mbox{vol}(G/K)=|G/K|\neq 0
(mod
p
).

In particular, a Sylow
p
-subgroup is exactly a minimal splitting subgroup in characteristic
p
.

The next lemma and the corollary following it are the main payoffs of our work on Berezin integration on CS manifolds.

Lemma 6.3
.

If
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is quasireductive, then
\mathcal{H}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is trivial and
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})\neq 0
.

See Definition
5.4
for the meaning of the volume of
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
being zero or not.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.3
, if
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is trivial then we obtain a non-zero algebraic integral
\iota:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}]\to\mathbb{C}
. By definition,
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})=\iota(1)
, so it is clear that if
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})\neq 0
we have that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is splitting.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is splitting we obtain by (1) of Definition
6.1
a non-zero algebraic integral
\iota
on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H}
. Once again by Theorem
5.3
, this implies that
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
is trivial, thus giving rise to a non-zero algebraic integral from Berezin integration. However by Lemma
3.5
, all algebraic integrals are scalar multiples of
\iota
, meaning that
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{H})=\iota(1)\neq 0
.
∎

Corollary 6.4
.

If
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
are quasireductive supergroups, then
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

Using Cor. 2.7 of
[
17
]
, we may assume that
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})
,
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})
, and
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k})
are all trivial.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{c}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{c}
be compact CS forms of
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
.
We set
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{K}_{c}
,
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{G}_{c}/\mathcal{H}_{c}
, and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{H}_{c}/\mathcal{K}_{c}
, so that we have a fibration
\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{B}
with fibre
\mathcal{F}
. Choose nonzero elements

b_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}}\in\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}),\ \ \ b_{\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}}\in\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{h}/k),\ \ \ b_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}}:=b_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}}\cdot b_{\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}}\in\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}).

Here we have used that
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})\cong\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h})\otimes\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k})
.

From each
b_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}}
,
b_{\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}}
, and
b_{\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}}
we obtain global Berezin forms
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}
,
\omega_{\mathcal{B}}
, and
\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
which trivialize their respective Berezin sheaves.

Now
\operatorname{Ber}(\Omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}})
will be a
\mathcal{G}
-equivariant bundle on
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}
with fibre naturally isomorphic to
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k})
, so we may trivialize it using
b_{\mathfrak{h}/\mathfrak{k}}
to give a global trivialization
\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}
. By equivariance, it is clear that
\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}|_{\mathcal{F}}=\omega_{\mathcal{F}}
for any fibre, and thus we obtain
\omega_{\mathcal{E}}=\pi^{*}(\omega_{\mathcal{B}})\omega_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{B}}
as required to apply Proposition
5.5
. From this the corollary is clear.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}\cong\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\mathcal{G}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is splitting, where all supergroups are quasireductive. Then
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{G}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{i}
is splitting.

Proof.

We show it for
i=1
. First observe that
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{G}_{1}
is indeed quasireductive being the kernel of
\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

Using Corollary
6.4
,
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\pi_{2}(\mathcal{K})
.
We use that the quotient of a quasireductive group is quasireductive.
On the other hand, we have an isomorphism of quasireductive supergroups

\mathcal{G}_{1}\times\pi_{2}(\mathcal{K})/\mathcal{K}\cong\mathcal{G}_{1}/\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{G}_{1},

and since this isomorphism is
\mathcal{G}_{1}
-equivariant, we learn that
\mathcal{K}\cap\mathcal{G}_{1}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}_{1}
.
∎

6.2.
Sylow subgroups and
0
-groups

In analogy with finite groups, we make the following definition.

Definition 6.6
.

A Sylow subgroup of a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
is a minimal splitting subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
.

Note that the existence of Sylow subgroups follows from the Zariski topology being Noetherian.

Remark 6.7
.

For a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
,
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is semisimple if and only if the trivial subgroup is a Sylow subgroup.

Recall that in the theory of finite-groups, a
p
-group
P
is one for which
\mbox{vol}(P/H)=|P/H|=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for all nontrivial subgroups
H
in
P
. Equivalently,
P
contains no nontrivial splitting subgroups over characteristic
p
.

Definition 6.8
.

We say that a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{O}
is a 0-group if it satisfies any of the following equivalent conditions:

(1)

\mathcal{O}
is its own Sylow subgroup.

(2)

\mathcal{O}
contains no nontrivial splitting subgroups.

(3)

For every quasireductive subgroup
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
such that
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{k})
is a trivial
\mathcal{K}
-module, we have
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{K})=0
.

Remark 6.9
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a
0
-group, then by Lem. 2.11 of
[
17
]
,
\mathcal{O}
must be oddly generated (see Definition
2.8
).

Corollary 6.10
.

Given quasireductive subgroups
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
,
\mathcal{O}
is a Sylow subgroup if and only if it is a splitting
0
-group.

Remark 6.11
.

One point of divergence from the theory of finite groups is that it is not true that every
0
-subgroup of a quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{G}
will lie in a Sylow subgroup. Indeed, if
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}\mathcal{L}(m|n)
, then the lie superalgebra has a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, and if
mn>1
then
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
will not Lie in any Sylow subgroup.

The question of classifying all maximal 0-subgroups will be taken up in future work.

6.3.
Splitting subalgebras

In the following, we write
\text{Ext}^{i}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(-,-)
for the
Ext
groups in the category
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
(see Definition
2.2
).

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive and
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
a quasireductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists global forms
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
of
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
such that
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
;

(2)

if
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
are any global forms of
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
.

(3)

\text{Ext}^{i}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)\to\text{Ext}^{i}_{(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)
is injective for all
i
and for all
M,N
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
;

(4)

\text{Ext}^{1}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)\to\text{Ext}^{1}_{(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)
is injective for all
M,N
in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
and
(4)\Rightarrow(3)
are clear, as is
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
by Lemma
2.7
.
For
(1)\Rightarrow(4)
, it suffices to show that
\text{Ext}^{\bullet}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)\to\text{Ext}^{\bullet}_{(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{0}})}(M,N)
is injective when
N
is a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module, and thus we may assume that
N=\mathbb{C}
by tensoring
M
by
N^{*}
.

We may write
M=M^{\prime}\oplus M^{\prime\prime}
, where
M^{\prime}
lies in the principal block of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})
, and
M^{\prime\prime}
has no simple constituents lying in the principal block. Thus
\text{Ext}^{i}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(M,\mathbb{C})=\text{Ext}^{i}_{(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}})}(M^{\prime},\mathbb{C})
, and so we may assume
M
lies in the principal block. But the principal block of
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
lies in
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
by Lemma
2.7
, so we are done.
∎

Definition 6.13
.

If
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are quasireductive, then we say that
\mathfrak{k}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}

if any of the equivalent conditions of Lemma
6.12
holds.

The following is a consequence of Corollary
6.4
and Corollary
6.5
.

Corollary 6.14
.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are quasireductive, then
\mathfrak{k}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}
if and only if
\mathfrak{k}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}
.

(2)

If
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is splitting, then
\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

6.4.
Sylow subalgebras and 0-superalgebras

Definition 6.15
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive. We say that a quasireductive subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is a Sylow subalgebra if
\mathfrak{o}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}
, and is minimal with this property.

Definition 6.16
.

We say that a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
is a
0
-superalgebra if it is a Sylow subalgebra of itself.

Remark 6.17
.

If
\mathfrak{o}
is a
0
-superalgebra, then it must be oddly generated (see Definition
2.8
).

Lemma 6.18
.

If
\mathfrak{o}
is a 0-superalgebra and
\mathcal{O}
is a connected global form of
\mathfrak{o}
, then
\mathcal{O}
is a 0-group.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
be a Sylow subgroup. By Lemma
6.12
, this implies that
\mathfrak{k}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{K}
is splitting in
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{O}
. On the other hand, since
\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}}+[\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}}]
is splitting in
\mathfrak{k}
and is contained in
\mathfrak{o}
, it must also be splitting inside
\mathfrak{o}
. Since
\mathfrak{o}
is a
0
-superalgebra, we obtain that

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{o}
, which in turn implies
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{O}
.
∎

The following Lemma follows immediately from
6.12
and
6.18
.

Lemma 6.19
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{o}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
are quasireductive Lie superalgebras such that
\mathfrak{o}
is Sylow in
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
are global forms of
\mathfrak{o}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathcal{O}
is Sylow in
\mathcal{G}
.

Corollary 6.20
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is quasireductive, then a quasireductive subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is Sylow if and only if it is a splitting
0
-subalgebra.

6.5.
Bijective correspondence of 0-subalgebras and 0-subgroups

Lemma 6.21
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie superalgebra with global form
\mathcal{G}
.

(1)

The bijection
\mathfrak{G}
of Lemma
2.10
defines a
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant bijection between the 0-subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and the 0-subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
.

(2)

The bijection
\mathfrak{G}
restricts to a
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant bijection between the Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and the Sylow subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
.

(3)

Further,
\mathfrak{G}
restricts to
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-equivariant bijection between the maximal
0
-subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
and the maximal
0
-subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of Lemmas
6.18
,
6.19
and

.
∎

6.6.
Neat and homological elements

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive. We recall from
[
8
]
the following definition.

Definition 6.22
.

An element
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
is called neat if either
x=0
or if there exists a subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2)\cong\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
with
x\in\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}}
.

Note that the definition of neat elements in
[
8
]
is different than the one given here, although is shown to be equivalent in
loc. cit.

Definition 6.23
.

The homological elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
are defined as

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}:[x,x]\text{ is semisimple in }\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\}.

By Thm. 2.15 of
[
17
]
, if
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is splitting then we must have

(6.1)

\mathcal{G}_{0}\cdot\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom},

where
\cdot
stands for the adjoint action.
In particular, if
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is semisimple, then necessarily
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}=\{0\}
. We now show the converse, which can be viewed as a version of Cauchy’s lemma for supergroups.

Proposition 6.24
.

The following conditions on a quasireductive Lie superalgebra

\mathfrak{g}
are equivalent:

(1)

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}=\{0\}
;

(2)

Every odd element of
\mathfrak{g}
is neat;

(3)

\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple.

Proof.

Let us show first that (1) implies (2). Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
,
x\neq 0
and
[x,x]=y_{s}+y_{n}

be the Jordan decomposition of
y=[x,x]
. By our assumption
y_{n}\neq 0
. Therefore by the Jacobson-Morozov theorem
there exists an
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2)
-triple
\{e,h,f\}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{y_{s}}
such that
e=y_{n}
. Since
[e,x]=0
we can write

x=\sum_{k=0}^{l}x_{k}
such that
[h,x_{k}]=kx_{k}
. We first note that
[x_{0},x_{0}]=y_{s}
and hence by (1)

x_{0}=y_{s}=0
. The relations
[h,e]=2e
and
[x,x]=e
imply
[x_{k},x_{k}]=0
for
k>1
and hence by (1) we get

x=x_{1}
. Now one can easily see that
\{e,h,f,x,[f,x]\}
form a basis of the
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2)
subalgebra in
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let us now show that (2) implies (3). Since
\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2)
is simple we have that if (2) holds for
\mathfrak{g}

and
\mathfrak{r}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is an ideal, then (2) holds for
\mathfrak{r}
and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{r}
. On the other hand, if (3) holds for

\mathfrak{r}
and
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{r}
then
\mathfrak{r}
is splitting for
\mathfrak{g}
and therefore (3) holds for
\mathfrak{g}
. Now we can proceed by induction on dimension of
\mathfrak{g}
and we have to check the statement only for simple Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Now the statement follows from the classification of simple Lie superalgebras (
[
11
]
), which shows that only
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(1|2n)
has that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{neat}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
.

Finally, (3) implies (1) by the argument before the proposition.
∎

Corollary 6.25
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is a quasireductive supergroup such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}=\{0\}
, then
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{G}
is semisimple.

6.7.
A priori results on Sylow subgroups and 0-groups

Lemma 6.26
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
0
-group with Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
.

(1)

Any quotient of
\mathcal{O}
is again a
0
-group.

(2)

\mathcal{O}
does not admit a simple quotient.

(3)

\mathcal{O}
does not admit a nontrivial, purely even quotient.

(4)

\mathfrak{o}
does not have simple ideal.

(5)

The product of two
0
-groups is again a
0
-group.

Proof.

For (1), if
\mathcal{O}
is a
0
-group and
\pi:\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{O}
with nontrivial subgroup
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, then

\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K})\cong\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{O}/\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{K}))=0,

which implies that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a
0
-group.

For (2), we use (1) and the results of
[
17
]
and
[
19
]
, whose main theorems show that any simple quasireductive supergroup admits a nontrivial splitting subgroup.

Once again (3) follows from (1) because a nontrivial reductive group is never a
0
-group.

For (4) assume
\mathfrak{s}
is a simple ideal in
\mathfrak{o}
. Let
\mathfrak{i}
be a maximal ideal in
\mathfrak{o}
which intersects

\mathfrak{s}
by zero and set
\bar{\mathfrak{o}}:=\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{i}
. Note that
\bar{\mathfrak{o}}
is the Lie superalgebra of a 0-group. On the other hand,

\mathfrak{o}
is a subalgebra of
\operatorname{der}\mathfrak{s}
. By
[
16
]
(see also Lemma
8.4
,
\operatorname{der}\mathfrak{s}/\mathfrak{s}
is a purely even algebra
unless
\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{q}(n)
for
n\geq 3
and
\operatorname{der}\mathfrak{s}=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}(n)
. Therefore
\bar{\mathfrak{o}}=\mathfrak{s}
unless

\bar{\mathfrak{o}}=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}(n)
.
The former case is impossible by (2) and the latter case is also impossible, see
[
17
]
.

Finally, (5) follows from Corollary
6.5
.
∎

7.
Classification and structure of
0
-groups

In this section, we classify
0
-groups and give several equivalent characterizations.

7.1.
Takiff superalgebras and supergroups

Definition 7.1
.

We call a Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{s}
a Takiff superalgebra, if there exist simple (even) Lie algebras
\mathfrak{s}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{s}_{k}
such that

\mathfrak{s}\cong\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{i}],

where each
\mathbb{C}[\xi_{i}]
is a supercommutative algebra with one odd generator
\xi_{i}
.

Observe that a Takiff superalgebra is never a 0-superalgebra, as it is not oddly generated.

Definition 7.2
.

A Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called a Takiff 0-superalgebra if
\mathfrak{g}
is semisimple
(has trivial radical),
\mathfrak{g}
is oddly generated (i.e.
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
),
and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is a Takiff superalgebra. It is equivalent to the condition that

\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{i}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{i}]\rtimes\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi_{i}}\rangle,

and that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Clearly, we may write
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus\limits_{i}\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{i}]\rtimes\mathfrak{d}
, where
\mathfrak{d}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}},\dots,\partial_{\xi_{k}}\rangle
. Then the condition
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
is equivalent to asking that the natural projections
\mathfrak{d}\to\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi_{i}}\rangle
are surjective for all
i
.

Definition 7.3
.

A supergroup
\mathcal{S}
is called a Takiff supergroup if
\mathfrak{s}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{S}
is a Takiff superalgebra.
Similarly, a supergroup
\mathcal{G}
is called a Takiff 0-supergroup if
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
is a Takiff 0-superalgebra.

Example 7.4
.

Examples of Takiff 0-superalgebras include:

(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1}]\oplus\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{3}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{2}])\rtimes\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}}-\partial_{\xi_{2}}\rangle.

Another example is

\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}(2)\cong\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{pe}(2)\cong(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(2)\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi])\rtimes\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi}\rangle,

where
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{pe}(2)
is the commutator subalgebra of the periplectic Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{pe}(2)
, and
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}(2)
is the quotient of the queer Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{q}(2)
by its centre.

A non-example of a Takiff 0-superalgebra is

(\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{2}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1}]\oplus\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}_{3}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi_{2}])\rtimes\mathbb{C}\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}}\rangle.

Remark 7.5
.

For a Takiff superalgebra
\mathfrak{s}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{s}_{i}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi]
, we have:

\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{s})/\mathfrak{s}=\langle\partial_{\xi_{1}},\dots,\partial_{\xi_{k}},\xi_{1}\partial_{\xi_{1}},\dots,\xi_{k}\partial_{\xi_{k}}\rangle.

See, for instance, Lem. 6.6 of
[
16
]
.

7.2.
Classification theorem

In the following, a central extension may be of arbitrary size.

Theorem 7.6
.

A quasireductive supergroup
\mathcal{O}
is a 0-group if and only if
\mathcal{O}
is oddly generated and isomorphic to a central extension of
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{V}
is an odd abelian supergroup and
\mathcal{T}
is a Takiff 0-supergroup.

We note that by Lemma
6.21
, Theorem
7.6
is equivalent to the statement that a quasireductive Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
is a
0
-superalgebra if and only if it is oddly generated and isomorphic to a central extension of
\mathfrak{t}\times\mathfrak{v}
, where
\mathfrak{t}
is a Takiff 0-superalgebra, and
\mathfrak{v}
is an odd Lie abelian superalgebra.

For the backwards direction of Theorem
7.6
, we will need the following lemma:

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
where
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
are quasireductive, and
\mathcal{D}
is an odd abelian supergroup that commutes with
\mathcal{G}_{0}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
is contained in every splitting subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
. Further,
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}
if and only if
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
is splitting in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{D}
consists of homological,
\mathcal{G}_{0}
-fixed vectors, the first claim follows from
6.1
.

For the second statement, observe that the natural map
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}
is an isomorphism of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
-varieties. We clealy have a natural isomorphism
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})\cong\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})
, and since
\mathcal{K}_{0}^{\prime}=\mathcal{K}_{0}
,
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k})
is trivial as a
\mathcal{K}
-module if and only
\operatorname{Ber}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{k}^{\prime})
is trivial as a
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
-module.

Thus let us assume these Berezin modules are trivial, meaning both
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
admit global, invariant, Berezin forms
\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}}
and
\omega_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}
. Since
\omega_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}}
pulls back to
\omega_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime}}
under the isomorphism
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\cong\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K}
, it is clear that
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{K})\neq 0
if and only if
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{K}^{\prime})\neq 0
, so we may conclude by Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.6
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be oddly generated and a central extension of
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a Takiff 0-supergroup and
\mathcal{V}
is odd abelian. To show that
\mathcal{O}
is a 0-group, we may quotient by the centre, and thus assume that
\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
. However this implies
\mathcal{O}\cong\mathcal{S}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{S}
is a Takiff supergroup and
\mathcal{D}
is an odd abelian subgroup that commutes with
\mathcal{O}_{0}
. Thus we may apply Lemma
7.7
, which implies that any splitting subgroup
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{O}
must be of the form
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\ltimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is splitting. However,
\mathfrak{s}_{\overline{1}}
is an odd abelian ideal of
\mathfrak{s}
, implying that
\mathfrak{s}_{\overline{1}}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}^{\prime}
. Thus
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}=\mathfrak{k}_{\overline{1}}
, implying
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{O}
.

For the converse direction, we apply Lemma
6.26
and the following Lemma
7.8
.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be an oddly generated quasireductive supergroup with Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
such that
\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{o})_{\overline{0}}
contains no simple ideal. Then
\mathcal{O}
is isomorphic to a central extension of
\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a Takiff 0-supergroup and
\mathcal{V}
is odd abelian.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a such a supergroup, and assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{O}
has trivial centre. Thus we want to show that
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{T}\times\mathcal{V}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is a Takiff 0-supergroup and
\mathcal{V}
is odd abelian.

By Lem. 5.6 of
[
16
]
, the minimal ideals of
\mathfrak{o}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{O}
are either simple, odd abelian, or a Takiff superalgebra. Let
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{o})
be the product of minimal ideals of
\mathfrak{o}
. Since by assumption
\mathfrak{o}
does not have simple ideals, by Thm. 6.9 of
[
16
]
,
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{o})
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{v}
for a Takiff superalgebra
\mathfrak{s}
and an odd abelian ideal
\mathfrak{v}
. Further,
\mathfrak{o}/\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{o})=\mathfrak{r}\ltimes\mathfrak{l}
for a reductive Lie algebra
\mathfrak{r}
and an odd abelian ideal
\mathfrak{l}
. However
[\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}]\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{o})
, so we must have
\mathfrak{r}=0
.

Thus we have
\mathfrak{o}=(\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{v})\rtimes\mathfrak{l}
. Hence:

\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\operatorname{Der}(\mathfrak{s}\times\mathfrak{v})_{\overline{1}}.

Clearly
[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{v}]=0
. By Remark
7.5
, we are done.
∎

Let

(1)

8.
Uniqueness of Sylow subgroups up to conjugacy

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quasireductive supergroup. Then any two Sylow subgroups
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}
are conjugate under
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

The proof of Theorem
8.1
will be given in steps, and will occupy the rest of the section. Write
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
.

Using Lemma
6.21
, we easily obtain that Theorem
8.1
holds if and only all Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are conjugate. Thus we sometimes work with
\mathfrak{g}
and sometime with
\mathcal{G}
, as is convenient.

We start with a useful lemma.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a quasireductive supergroup, and suppose that
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{G}_{c}\rtimes\mathcal{D}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{c}
is an even central extension of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{0}
is even reductive. Then the Sylow subgroups of
\mathcal{G}
are in natural bijection with the Sylow subgroups of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, and this bijection respects conjugation by
\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

Proof.

Write
\pi:\mathcal{G}_{c}\to\mathcal{G}
for the natural quotient. Using Lem. 2.9 of
[
17
]
, it is easy to check that a subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{c}
is Sylow if and only if
\pi(\mathcal{O})
is Sylow in
\mathcal{G}
, and this correspondence clearly respects conjugation. Therefore we may assume
\mathcal{G}_{c}=\mathcal{G}
, so that
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}=\mathcal{G}\rtimes\mathcal{D}
.

Because
\mathcal{D}
is reductive, it is clear that every Sylow subgroup of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is contained, and thus also a Sylow subgroup, of
\mathcal{G}
. Conversely, since
\mathcal{G}
is splitting in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, a Sylow subgroup in
\mathcal{G}
is also a Sylow subgroup in
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
, giving our conjugation-preserving bijection.
∎

x\in\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L)
be a generic homological element
such that the semisimple element
h=[x,x]
is diagonal with eigenvalues

a_{1},\dots a_{n}
linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. By
6.1
, we may assume without loss of generality that
x\in\mathfrak{o}
. There exist a basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
of

L_{\bar{0}}
and a basis
\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
of

L_{\bar{1}}
such that
hv_{i}=a_{i}v_{i}
,
hw_{i}=a_{i}w_{i}
.
By Lemma
7.15
(1) we obtain that
L_{\bar{0}}
and
L_{\bar{1}}

are isomorphic irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{0}}
with dimension equal
the rank of
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{0}}
. The latter is only possible if

\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{0}}\simeq\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(n)
is embedded diagonally into
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L)_{\bar{0}}
.
On the other hand,
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}
must contain self-commuting elements
of rank
(0|1)
and
(1|0)
. This forces
\mathfrak{o}\simeq\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(n|n)
and the latter is a zero-superalgebra only for
n=1
.
∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(m|n)
or
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(m|2n)
.
Let
d
be the defect of
\mathfrak{g}
and let
\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\}
be a set of mutually orthogonal linearly independent isotropic roots. Let
\mathfrak{o}
be the Sylow subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by the roots spaces

\mathfrak{g}_{\pm\alpha_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,d
. Then every Sylow subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is conjugate to
\mathfrak{o}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
be a Sylow subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
V
be the standard representation of
\mathfrak{g}

Let us choose a generic homological
x\in\mathfrak{k}_{\bar{1}}
which also a generic homological element in
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
, then
h
has
purely even or purely odd kernel and eigenvalues

a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
linearly
independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)
, the kernel of
h

is purely even, purely odd, or has dimension
(1|2k)
and eigenvalues are

\pm a_{1},\dots,\pm a_{n}
with
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
linearly independent over

\mathbb{Q}
.

Let
L_{1},\dots,L_{k}
denote the simple non-trivial
\mathfrak{k}
-constituents in the restriction of
V
to
\mathfrak{k}
. Note that each
L_{i}
satisfies condition (5) of
Lemma
7.15
. In particular every
L_{i}
is not self dual.
Next we notice that
0\notin\mathcal{P}(L_{i})
. Indeed, the zero weight space of

L_{i}
must have superdimension zero. If
\operatorname{ker}h
is purely odd or purely even,
then clearly
0\notin\mathcal{P}(L_{i})
. If
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{ker}h=(1|2k)
and
0\in\mathcal{P}(L_{i})
, then using the self-duality of
V
, there exists
j\neq i
with
L_{i}^{*}\simeq L_{j}
, and so
0\in\mathcal{P}(L_{j})
. This again is impossible by dimension constraints.
Finally, from weight multiplicities we obtain that
\mathcal{P}(L_{i})\cap\mathcal{P}(L_{j})=\emptyset
if
i\neq j
. Using Lemma
7.15
(4)
we obtain that
V
is semisimple as a
\mathfrak{k}
-module when
\operatorname{ker}h
is purely even or purely odd. If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)
with
\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{ker}h=(1|2k)
, any nontrivial extension between
\mathbb{C}
and
\Pi\mathbb{C}
will violate self-duality of
V
, so once again we find that
V
is semisimple.

Set

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})\subset\mathfrak{g}

for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(V)
.
If
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{o}\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(V)
then we have
k=2l
with
L_{i}\simeq L_{l+i}^{*}
, and we set

\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})\subset\mathfrak{g}.

Since
\mathfrak{k}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{k}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
. That means

\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})
by Corollary
6.5
. Now Lemma
8.6
implies
\operatorname{dim}L_{i}=(1|1)
.
Thus,
\mathfrak{k}
is isomorphic the product of
SL(1|1)^{d}
. After conjugation, we may assume

x\in\mathfrak{o}
, and that forces
\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{k}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 8.8
.

Let
L
be a faithful irreducible representation of
a 0-superalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
such that
L\simeq\Pi L^{*}
and
\mathfrak{o}
is a splitting subalgebra in
\mathfrak{pe}(L)
. Then

\operatorname{dim}L=(2|2)
and
\mathfrak{o}\cong\mathfrak{spe}(2)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.15
, part (2), we know that

L
has zero superdimension. Suppose
\operatorname{dim}L=(n|n)
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{pe}(L)
be a generic homological element.
By
6.1
, we may assume without loss of generality that
x\in\mathfrak{o}
.

If
n=2k
then the eigenvalues of the semisimple element
h=[x,x]
are

\pm a_{1},\dots,\pm a_{k}
such that
a_{1},\dots,a_{k}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
denote the global form of
\mathbb{C}\langle h\rangle
and
\mathfrak{t}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{T}
.
If
n=2k+1
, zero is also an eigenvalue. The dimension of every eigenspace of

equals
(1|1)

and

is a generic semisimple element in
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(n)

L\cong\Pi L^{*}
, the center of

must act trivially, so Lemma

, part (1), implies

and
\Pi L_{\bar{1}}
are isomorphic, simple

-modules. Furthermore, all

have multiplicity

and all non-zero weights are highest

is minuscule for

and quasi-minuscule for

\operatorname{dim}L_{\overline{0}}\leq 2\operatorname{rk}\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}+1

By comparing with the list of quasi-minuscule representations we see only the following possibilities

\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{0}}

\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{l}(L_{\bar{0}})
,
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(L_{\bar{0}})
and
\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(L_{\bar{0}})
(for even

Now
\mathfrak{pe}(L)_{\bar{1}}=S^{2}L_{\bar{0}}\oplus\Lambda^{2}{L^{*}_{\bar{0}}}
decomposes into the sum of
at most three irreducible components
with respect to the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{0}}
.

If
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}=s\mathfrak{l}(L_{\bar{0}})
then

and

\mathfrak{pe}(L)_{\bar{1}}=\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}

If
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{o}(L_{\bar{0}})

is irreducible, and
\Lambda^{2}L^{*}_{\bar{0}}
is irreducible. By
6.1

\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}
must contain elements from both nontrivial components.
It is easy to see that

and
\Lambda^{2}L^{*}_{\bar{0}}
generate
\mathfrak{spe}(L)
and hence
\mathfrak{spe}(L)\subset\mathfrak{o}
.

For
\mathfrak{o}_{\bar{1}}=\mathfrak{s}\mathfrak{p}(L_{\bar{0}})

\mathfrak{o}
is a

is a

-superalgebra. This implies that

or

. But the case

must be excluded as
\mathfrak{spe}(1)
has no faithful irreducible representation. ∎

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{pe}(n)
. Then every Sylow subalgebra is conjugate to

\mathfrak{spe}(2)^{k}
if
n=2k
and
\mathfrak{spe}(2)^{k}\times\mathfrak{spe}(1)
if
n=2k+1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{o}
be a Sylow subalgebra. We choose a generic homological
x
as in the proof of Lemma
8.8
, and assume without loss of generality that
x\in\mathfrak{o}
.

First, we consider the case when
n
is even. As in the proof of Lemma
8.7

we can conclude that the restriction of the standard
\mathfrak{g}
-module
V
to
\mathfrak{o}
is semisimple.
Write
V
as a direct sum of simple
\mathfrak{o}
-modules

V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}(L_{i}\oplus\Pi L_{i}^{*})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}S_{j},

where
S_{j}\simeq\Pi S_{j}^{*}
. Now
\mathfrak{o}
must be splitting in

\prod\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})\times\prod\mathfrak{pe}(S_{j})
. This implies
\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})
is splitting in
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})
,
hence
\operatorname{dim}L_{i}=(1|1)
, and
\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{pe}(S_{j})
is splitting in
\mathfrak{pe}(S_{j})
hence

\operatorname{dim}S_{j}=(2|2)
. However,
\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{l}(L_{i})
is not a splitting subgroup in

. Now by Lemma
8.9
we obtain
\mathfrak{o}=\prod\mathfrak{spe}(S_{j})

which implies the statement for even

.

The case of odd

is more subtle because of the zero weight space of dimension
(1|1)
.
In this case we have
V=V^{\prime}\oplus W
where
W
is a
(1|1)
-dimensional
\mathfrak{o}
-module and

is a semisimple
\mathfrak{o}
-module. The condition

implies
\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{pe}(W)=\mathfrak{spe}(W)
and the proof can be finished as in the first case.
∎

This now completes the proof of Proposition
8.5
.

8.2.
Step 2: the case when
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})_{\overline{0}}=0

Let us now prove Theorem
8.1
in the case when
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})_{\overline{0}}=0
. In this case, by Thm. 6.9 of
[
16
]
, we have that
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})\rtimes(\mathfrak{r}\ltimes\mathfrak{l})
, where
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
is the product of minimal ideals of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{r}
is a reductive Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{l}
is an odd abelian subalgebra with trivial action of
[\mathfrak{r}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{r}_{\overline{0}}]
. Further,
\mathfrak{r}\times\mathfrak{l}
acts faithfully by outer derivations on
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
, i.e. we have a natural embedding
\mathfrak{r}\times\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\operatorname{Out}(\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g}))
.

Lemma 8.10
.

We have a bijection between Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
and Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
given by

\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})\supseteq\mathfrak{o}\mapsto(\mathfrak{o}+[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}])\rtimes\mathfrak{l}.

This bijection preserves conjugacy classes, and so all Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are conjugate if and only if the same is true for
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Proof.

Write
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathfrak{k}_{1}\times\cdots\mathfrak{k}_{l}
as a product of minimal ideals. By Corollary
6.14
, the Sylow subalgebras of
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
are of the form
\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{o}_{1}\times\cdots\mathfrak{o}_{l}
, where
\mathfrak{o}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is a Sylow subalgebra.

By Lem. 5.6 of
[
16
]
,
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is either simple, odd abelian, or isomorphic to
\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi]
for a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
. If
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is simple, then by Proposition
8.5
,
\mathfrak{o}_{i}
is stable under
\mathfrak{r}\ltimes\mathfrak{l}
. If
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is odd abelian, then
\mathfrak{k}_{i}=\mathfrak{o}_{i}
by Lem. 2.12 of
[
17
]
, and again
\mathfrak{o}_{i}
is stable under
\mathfrak{r}\ltimes\mathfrak{l}
.

Finally, if
\mathfrak{k}_{i}\cong\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi]
for a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
, then necessarily
\mathfrak{o}_{i}=\xi\mathfrak{s}
. However, because
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is a minimal ideal,
\mathfrak{o}_{i}+[\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{o}_{i}]=\mathfrak{k}_{i}
. Therefore we obtain that

(\mathfrak{o}+[\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{l}])\rtimes\mathfrak{l}

is a quasireductive subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. We claim that it is a Sylow subalgebra. Indeed, it is a
0
-superalgebra by direct inspection, using Theorem
7.6
. On the other hand it is splitting by Lemma
7.7
.

Conversely, given a Sylow subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
, it must contain
\mathfrak{l}
by
6.1
, since
\mathfrak{l}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
and
\mathcal{G}_{0}
acts on
\mathfrak{l}
by characters. Since
[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]\subseteq\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
, we may write
\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{o}\rtimes\mathfrak{l}
. By Lemma
7.7
,
\mathfrak{o}
will be a splitting subalgebra of

\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})\cong\mathfrak{k}_{1}\times\cdots\mathfrak{k}_{l}.

By Corollary
6.14
,
\mathfrak{o}_{i}:=\mathfrak{o}\cap\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is splitting in
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
, and is clearly
\mathfrak{l}
-stable. From this we see that
\mathfrak{o}_{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is Sylow whenever
\mathfrak{k}_{i}
is simple or odd abelian, while
\mathfrak{o}_{i}=\mathfrak{k}_{i}
when
\mathfrak{k}_{i}\cong\mathfrak{s}\otimes\mathbb{C}[\xi]
. Therefore our correspondence is indeed a bijection of Sylow subalgebras.

That our bijection preserves conjugacy classes is straightforward, completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now using step 1, it is straightforward to see that Sylow subalgebras are unique up to conjugacy in
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})
. Therefore by Lemma
8.10
, Sylows are unique up to conjugacy in
\mathfrak{g}
, completing step 2.

8.3.
Final (third) step:
\mathfrak{g}
arbitrary

For this step we simply apply Lemma
8.2
to reduce to step 2. This completes the proof of Theorem
8.1
.

The proof of Theorem

Corollary 9.4
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is quasireductive with Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
is once again quasireductive, and
\operatorname{Rep}\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})/\mathcal{O}
is semisimple.

The following result is extremely useful. Ideally, one could find a simple proof which doesn’t rely on the explicit description of Sylow subalgebras.

Corollary 9.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive with Sylow subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Let
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}
be a maximal torus. Then
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t})\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{o})
.

Proof.

This follows from our explicit description of
\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{o})
given in Proposition
9.2
.
∎

Corollary 9.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive with Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
. Write
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{0}
for a maximal torus of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
. Then there exists
x\in\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{2})=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T}).
In particular
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{2})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
.

Definition 9.7
.

We call an element
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
generic if for some Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
,
x\in\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{O}
and satisfies the conditions of Corollary
9.6
.

Proof of Corollary
9.6
.

The second statement follows from the connectedness of centralizers of tori and Corollary
9.5
.

For the first statement, write
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{G}
. Clearly we may assume that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})_{\overline{0}}=0
, and so we do assume this. As usual, write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{g})\rtimes(\mathfrak{l}\rtimes\mathfrak{r})
, so that
\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}\rtimes\mathfrak{l}
, where
\mathfrak{o}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{O}
. Writing
\mathfrak{t}=\operatorname{Lie}\mathcal{T}
, it is clear that
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
, so we only need to find such an element
x
when
\mathfrak{g}
is either simple or a Takiff 0-superalgebra.
These cases are easily checked, case by case.
∎

Lemma 9.8
.

The set of generic elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
form a
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
-stable subset of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
.

Proof.

The property of being generic is clearly preserved under any automorphism, so the result is clear.
∎

Lemma 9.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive with Sylow subalgebra
\mathfrak{o}
. Then the set of generic elements in
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}
is open and dense in
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}
.

Proof.

Let
V\subseteq\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}
be the open subset of regular semisimple elements
t
for which
\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(t)=\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{o}}(t))
. Then the set of generic elements is exactly the preimage of
V
under the squaring map
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}\to\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}
. By Corollary
9.6
this preimage is nonempty, and so we are done.
∎

Corollary 9.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be quasireductive with Sylow subalgebras
\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
such that
\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}=\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{t}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}
be a maximal torus, and let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the centralizer of
\mathfrak{t}
in
\mathfrak{o}
. Then by Corollary
9.5
,

\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{c}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t})\subseteq\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}).

Since
\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{1}}=\mathfrak{h}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
, by Corollary
9.3
we must have
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}
. Now we apply Lemma
7.13
to obtain:

\mathfrak{o}=\mathfrak{h}+[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{0}}]=\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}.

∎

.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive with Sylow subgroups

9.2.
The third Sylow Theorem

Let
G
be a finite group, and write
\text{Syl}_{p}(G)
for the set of Sylow
p
-subgroups of
G
. The third Sylow theorem states that
|\text{Syl}_{p}(G)|
is congruent to 1
modulo
p
. Since it is clear that
|\text{Syl}_{p}(G)|=|G/N_{G}(P)|
for any Sylow subgroup
P
, we may view this as saying that
\mbox{vol}(G/N_{G}(P))=1
in the base field.

We would like to give an analogous result in the super setting. One statement is that
\mbox{vol}(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O}))\neq 0
for any Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
; however this is obvious by
6.3
because
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
contains
\mathcal{O}
, and thus is splitting.

We formulate a slightly different claim which is quite useful. For this, recall that if
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
, then we have the Duflo-Serganova functor
DS_{x}:\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}\to s\operatorname{Vec}
given by

DS_{x}M=\frac{\operatorname{Ker}(x:M\to M)}{\operatorname{Im}(x:M\to M)\cap\operatorname{Ker}(x:M\to M)}.

We refer to
[
10
]
for more details about this functor.

If
A
is a supercommutative algebra in
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}\mathfrak{g}
, then a straightforward check shows that
DS_{x}A
will once again be a supercommutative algebra.

Definition 9.12
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive with Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
, and let
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{0}
be a maximal torus in
\mathcal{O}_{0}
. Define
W_{\mathcal{G}}:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})/\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
, and
W_{\mathcal{O}}:=\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{T})/\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{T})
.

Lemma 9.13
.

Both
W_{\mathcal{G}}
and
W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}
are finite groups, and
W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}
is naturally a subgroup of
W_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

That these groups are finite is a consequence of the following statement from the theory of reductive groups: the normalizer modulo the centralizer of any torus is always a finite group. It is clear that
W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}
is a subgroup of
W_{\mathcal{G}}
.
∎

Theorem 9.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be quasireductive with Sylow subgroup
\mathcal{O}
, and let
x\in\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
be generic. Then we have an isomorphism of algebras

DS_{x}\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})]=\mathbb{C}[W_{\mathcal{G}}/W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}],

induced by the inclusion of spaces
W_{\mathcal{G}}/W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}\subseteq\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
.

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(x^{2})\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{0}
, which is a maximal torus of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
by definition of generic element.

Suppose that
x
vanishes at some closed point
g\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
for
g\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{C})
. Then
\operatorname{Ad}(g)(x)\in\mathfrak{n}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{o})^{hom}_{\overline{1}}=\mathfrak{o}_{\overline{1}}^{hom}
, where the last equality is by Corollary
9.3
. If we write
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(\operatorname{Ad}(g)(x^{2}))
, then
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
will also be a maximal torus of
\mathcal{O}_{0}
, so after multiplying by some element of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C})
we may assume that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\mathcal{T}
, and thus
g\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})(\mathbb{C})
. If
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{T})
, then by Corollary
9.5
we have
g\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})(\mathbb{C})
.

Thus we have shown that the zeroes of
x
lie in the image of the natural map
W_{\mathcal{G}}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
, and from this it is clear that the zeroes are exactly given by
W_{\mathcal{G}}/W_{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})}
.

Finally, we observe that for every zero
z
of
x
, the endomorphism
[x,-]
of
T_{z}^{*}\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{O})
is an isomorphism, once again by Corollary
9.5
and the definition of generic elements. Thus we may conclude by Thm. 4.1 of
[
18
]
.
∎

Some computations on instanton knot homology
Zhenkun Li, Yi Liang

Abstract
. In a recent paper, the first author and his collaborator developed a method to compute an upper bound of the dimension of instanton Floer homology via Heegaard diagrams of 3 3 -manifolds. In this paper, for a knot inside S 3 S^{3} , we further introduce an algorithm that computes an upper bound of the dimension of instanton knot homology from knot diagrams. We test the algorithm with all knots up to 7 7 crossings as well as a more complicated knot 10 153 10_{153} . In the second half of the paper, we show that if the instanton knot Floer homology of a knot has a specified form, then the knot must be an instanton L-space knot.



1.
Introduction

Knot theory is a central topic in low dimensional topology. In 1990, Floer introduced a knot invariant called the instanton knot homology in
[
5
]
. It has become a powerful tool in the study of knot theory. For example, the instanton knot homology detects the genus and fibredness of a knot, recovers the Alexander polynomial, and plays an important role in the establishment of the milestone result that Khovanov homology detects the unknot. See Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
11
,
10
,
13
,
12
]
.

The instanton knot homology of a knot
K\subset S^{3}
is a finite-dimensional complex vector space associated to
K
. It is constructed by studying the solutions of sets of partial differential equations over a closed oriented
3
-manifold
Y
and the infinite cylinder
\mathbb{R}\times Y
, where
Y
is obtained from the knot complement by attaching some standard piece of
3
-manifold that also has a toroidal boundary. This nature of instanton knot homology makes it very difficult to compute. The breakthrough in computation was made by Kronheimer and Mrowka. Combining their work that the Euler characteristic of instanton knot homology recovers the Alexander polynomial of the knot, and that there exists a spectral sequence whose
E_{2}
page is the Khovanov homology and whose
E_{\infty}
page is the instanton knot homology. Thus one obtains a lower bound of the dimension of the instanton knot homology via the Alexander polynomial and an upper bound via the Khovanov homology. When these two bounds coincide, for example, for all alternating knots, the computation is done.

Later, several groups of people studied the representation varieties of some special families of knots to write down an explicit set of generators of the chain complex of the instanton knot homology and thus obtained some better upper bounds than the one coming from Khovanov homology. See
[
7
,
4
,
18
]
.

Heegaard diagram is an effective combinatorial way to describe
3
-manifolds and knots. In fact, any knot can be described and is determined by its Heegaard diagrams. Since instanton knot homology serves as a knot invariant, it is a priori determined by the Heegaard diagram of the knot. However, its construction through partial differential equations makes its relation to Heegaard diagrams very implicit. To study this relation, recently, the first author and his collaborator established the following result in
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot, and
(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta)
is a Heegaard diagram of
K
. Let
H
be a handle body with
\partial H=\Sigma
and
\gamma\subset H
is a (disconnected) oriented simple closed curve on
H
so that the following is true.

(1)

We have that
\gamma
has
(g(\Sigma)+1)
components.

(2)

We have that
\Sigma\backslash\gamma
consists of two components of equal Euler characteristics.

(3)

We have that all
\beta
-curves are components of
\gamma
.

Then we have the following inequality.

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}KHI(K)\leq\dim_{\mathbb{C}}SHI(H,\gamma).

Here,
KHI
is the instanton knot homology of
K
, and
SHI
is the sutured instanton Floer homology of the balanced sutured manifold
(H,\gamma)
.

Sutured instanton Floer homology associates a finite-dimensional complex vector space to every balanced sutured manifold. It was introduced by Kronheimer and Mrowka in
[
11
]
. The computation of
SHI(M,\gamma)
for a general balanced sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
is also difficult, due to the same reason as
KHI
. However, in the special case when
M=H
is a handle body, the first author and his collaborator developed an algorithm to compute an upper bound of
SHI(H,\gamma)
in
[
6
]
. So, equipped with Theorem
1.1
, one can obtain an upper bound on the dimension of the instanton knot homology of a knot from any Heegaard diagram of that knot. Following this idea, Li and Ye were able to compute a conjecturally sharp upper bound for all
(1,1)
-knots in
[
17
]
.

In this paper, we utilize Theorem
1.1
further and obtain the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot. Let
D
be any knot diagram of
K
. Then there is an algorithm to compute an upper bound of the dimension of
KHI(S^{3},K)
out of
D
.

Remark 1.3
.

The project in the current paper was launched right after the completion of
[
17
]
, where the first author and his collaborator first introduced Theorem
1.1
. Later, Theorem
1.1
was further used to prove the main result of
[
1
]
. By
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
, one knows that the dimension of
KHI(S^{3},K)
is bounded by the number of generators of
\widehat{CFK}(S^{3},K)
, where
\widehat{CFK}
is the chain complex of the hat version of knot Floer homology introduced by Ozsváth and Szabó
[
19
]
. Since the number of generators of
\widehat{CFK}(S^{3},K)
can also be computed directly from any given diagram of the knot
K
,
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
gives rise to an algorithm that is different from the one in Theorem
1.2
. Though we haven’t compared the effectiveness between these two algorithms.

We further testify the algorithm in Theroem
1.2
with knots of crossing numbers at most
7
. We found that all the bounds from the algorithm are sharp. We also test a more complicated knot
10_{153}
. See Table
1
for more details.

It is worth mentioning that all knots of crossing number at most
7
are alternating. So the dimensions of
KHI
for them have been known due to the work of Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
10
,
12
]
as discussed above. However, the upper bounds coming from Khovanov homology depends on the establishment of a spectral sequence that relates instanton knot homology with Khovanonv homology. So Theorem
1.2
provides an alternative proof that is independent of Kronheimer and Mrowka’s spectral sequence.

Instanton L-space knots are those knots inside
S^{3}
that admits a Dehn surgery whose instanton Floer homology has minimal dimension. As mentioned above, the computation of instanton Floer homology is a big open problem, so in general, it is hard to identify instanton L-space knots. In this paper, we give a sufficient condition in terms of the instanton knot homology.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot of genus
g
. Suppose further that

(1.1)

KHI(K,i)\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{C},&\mid i\mid=g,g-1,0\\
0,&Otherwise\\
\end{array}\right.

Then
K
admits an instanton L-space surgery.

Combined with the results from
[
16
,
3
]
, we conclude the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot of genus
g
whose instanton knot homology is described as in (
1.1
), then one and exactly one of the following two statements is true.

(1).
S_{2g-1}(K)
is an instanton L-space, and for any rational number
r=\frac{p}{q}
with
q\geq 1
, we have

(1.2)

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I^{\sharp}(S^{3}_{r}(K))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}p&{\rm if~}r\geq 2g-1\\
(4g-2)\cdot q-p&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.

(2).
S_{1-2g}(K)
is an instanton L-space, and for any rational number
r=\frac{p}{q}
with
q\geq 1
, we have

(1.3)

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I^{\sharp}(S^{3}_{r}(K))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-p&{\rm if~}r\leq 1-2g\\
(4g-2)\cdot q+p&{\rm otherwise}\end{array}\right.

2.
Preliminaries

Definition 2.1

(
[
9
,
11
]
)
.

A
balanced sutured manifold

(M,\gamma)
consists of a compact oriented 3-manifold
M
with non-empty boundary together with a closed 1-submanifold
\gamma
on
\partial{M}
. Let
A(\gamma)=[-1,1]\times\gamma
be an annular neighborhood of
\gamma\subset\partial{M}
and let
R(\gamma)=\partial{M}\backslash{\rm int}(A(\gamma))
. They satisfy the following properties.

(1)

Neither
M
nor
R(\gamma)
has a closed component.

(2)

If
\partial{A(\gamma)}=-\partial{R(\gamma)}
is oriented in the same way as
\gamma
, then we require this orientation of
\partial{R(\gamma)}
induces one on
R(\gamma)
. The induced orientation on
{R(\gamma)}
is called the
canonical orientation
.

(3)

Let
R_{+}(\gamma)
be the part of
R(\gamma)
so that the canonical orientation coincides with the induced orientation on
\partial{M}
, and let
R_{-}(\gamma)=R(\gamma)\backslash R_{+}(\gamma)
. We require that
\chi(R_{+}(\gamma))=\chi(R_{-}(\gamma))
. If
\gamma
is clear in the contents, we simply write
R_{\pm}=R_{\pm}(\gamma)
, respectively.

Theorem 2.2

(Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
11
]
)
.

For any balanced sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
, we can associate a finite-dimensional complex vector space, which we denote by
SHI(M,\gamma)
, to
(M,\gamma)
. It serves as a topological invariant of the pair
(M,\gamma)
.

Definition 2.3

(Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
11
]
)
.

For knot
K\subset S^{3}
, define its
instanton knot homology
, which we denote by
KHI(K)
, to be

KHI(K)=SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{\mu}),

where
\gamma_{\mu}
consists of two meridians of
K
.

For a connected closed oriented
3
-manifold
Y
, define its
framed instanton Floer homology
, which is denoted by
I^{\sharp}(Y)
, to be

I^{\sharp}(Y)=SHI(Y(1),\delta),

where
Y(1)=Y\backslash D^{3}
is obtained from
Y
by removing a
3
-ball and
\delta\subset\partial Y(1)
is a connected simple closed curve.

Definition 2.4
.

A knot
K\subset S^{3}
is called an
instanton L-space knot
if there exists a non-zero integer
n
so that

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}I^{\sharp}(S^{3}_{n}(K))=|n|.

Here
S^{3}_{n}(K)
is the three manifold obtained from
S^{3}
by an
n
surgery along the knot
K
.

In
[
11
,
10
]
, Kronheimer and Mrowka studied many basic properties of
KHI
, which we summarize as in the following two theorems.

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot of genus
g
, then the following is true.

(1)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}
-grading on
KHI(K)
, which is called the
Alexander grading
:

KHI(K)=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}KHI(K,i).

(2)

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\mid i\mid>g
, we have
KHI(K,i)=0
.

(3)

We have
KHI(K,g)\neq 0
.

(4)

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
KHI(K,i)\cong KHI(k,-i)
.

(5)

The knot
K
is fibred if and only if
KHI(K,g)\cong\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 2.6

(Kronheimer and Mrowka
[
10
]
)
.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot. Let

\Delta_{K}(t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}t^{i}

be its Alexander polynomial. Then we know that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}KHI(K)\geq\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{i}|.

Here
|\cdot|
means the abstract value.

(1)

For any

(2)

We have

(3)

For any

(4)

Proof of Theorem

.

Suppose
K\subset S^{3}
is a knot of genus
g
and its instanton knot homology satisfies the assumption in the hypothesis of the theorem. Note from the assumption that
KHI(K,g)\cong\mathbb{C}
and Theorem
2.5
, we know that
K
is fibred and
g\geq 2
. Then either
K
or the mirror of
K
is not right veering. By passing to its mirror if necessary, we can assume that
K
itself is not right veering. We begin with a few lemmas.

Lemma 4.1
.

We have

(4.1)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g)\cong\mathbb{C}

and

(4.2)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},2g-\frac{1}{2})\cong\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Applying term 2 of Theorem
2.5
, Formula (
2.3
) from Theorem
2.8
, and the assumption that
KHI(K,g)\cong\mathbb{C}
in the hypothesis, we conclude (
4.1
). Similarly, (
4.2
) follows from term 2 of Theorem
2.5
, Formula (
4.1
), and Formula (
2.3
) from Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g-1)=0
.

Proof.

Note we have argued that
K
is fibred and also assumed that it is not right veering. Hence Theorem
2.9
applies. Then the lemma follows from Theorem
2.9
, Formula (
4.2
), the assumption that
KHI(K,g-1)\cong\mathbb{C}
, and Theorem
2.8
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},i)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},i-\frac{1}{2})

for
i>1
.

Proof.

Since
-i-g<-g
, we have
KHI(K,-i-g)=0
by term 2 of Theorem
2.5
.
Thus, by Theorem
2.8
, we have more isomorphisms

(4.3)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},-i)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},-i+\frac{1}{2}).

By term 3 of Theorem
2.7
, we have an isomorphism

(4.4)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},-i)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},i)

and

(4.5)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},-i+\frac{1}{2})\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},i-\frac{1}{2})

The lemma then follows after substituting in Equation (
4.3
).

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&g+1\leq i\leq 2g-1\\
\mathbb{C}&2\leq i\leq g\\
\end{array}\right.

Proof.

To start, in Lemma
4.2
, we have proved that

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g-1)\cong 0.

By Lemma
4.3
, we know that

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},2g-1-\frac{1}{2})\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g-1)=0.

Since
KHI(K,g-2)=0
by the hypothesis of the theorem, taking
i=2g-2
in (
2.3
), we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g-2)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{1,-2g},2g-1-\frac{1}{2})=0.

Repeating the above argument once more for
i=2g-3
, we conclude that

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},2g-3)\cong 0.

We can keep running this argument until we finish the case
i=g+1
, where we have

(4.6)

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(0,-2g-1)},g+1)=0.

By far we have proved the vanishing part of the lemma. For the other half of the lemma, we use a similar argument. Note we have

KHI(K,0)\cong\mathbb{C}

by the hypothesis of the theorem, and

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},g+\frac{1}{2})\cong 0

by Lemma
4.3
and equation (
4.6
). Taking
i=g
in Formula (
2.3
), we conclude
SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g)\cong\mathbb{C}
. Then using a similar repetitive argument as above, we conclude

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},i)\cong\mathbb{C}

for
2\leq i\leq g
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
i\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
-g\leq i\leq g
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},i)\cong\mathbb{C}

Proof.

First, taking
i=g
, by term 1 of Theorem
2.7
, we know that

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},g+i+\frac{1}{2})=SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},2g+\frac{1}{2})=0.

Also, by term 4 of Theorem
2.7
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},-g+i)=SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},0)\cong\mathbb{C}.

Then by Formula (
2.5
) in Theorem
2.8
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},g)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},-g)\cong\mathbb{C}.

Next, we skip the case
i=g-1
and consider
i
so that
g-2\geq i\geq-g+1.
The case
i=g-1
will be dealt with later.

By Lemma
4.4
, when
i\in[0,g-2]
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g+i+1)=0.

Also, by Lemma
4.3
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},g+i+\frac{1}{2})\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g+i+1).

Thus, we derive

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},g+i+\frac{1}{2})=0

for all
i\in[0,g-2]
.

On the other hand, by term 3 of Theorem
2.7
, we have an isomorphism

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},i-g)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g-i),

and by Lemma
4.4
, for any
i\in[0,g-2]
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g-i)\cong\mathbb{C}.

Thus, by Formula (
2.5
), we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},i)\cong\mathbb{C}.

Similarly, for
-g+1\leq i\leq-1
, by Lemma
4.4
, we have

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g-i)\cong 0,

and

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g)},g+i+\frac{1}{2})\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(1,-2g-1)},g+i+1)\cong\mathbb{C}.

Then, by Formula (
2.5
), we conclude

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},i)\cong\mathbb{C}

for
g-2\geq i\geq-g+1
.

Notice by far we have covered all
-g\leq i\leq g
except
i=g-1
, which could be reached by term 3 in Theorem
2.7
and the conclusion above since

SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},g-1)\cong SHI(S^{3}(K),\gamma_{(2,-4g-1)},1-g)\cong\mathbb{C}

The lemma then follows.
∎

Theorem
1.4
then follows directly from Lemma
4.5
and term 5 of Theorem
2.7
.
∎

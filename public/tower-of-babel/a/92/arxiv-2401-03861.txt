Pure Nash Equilibria in Weighted Congestion Games with Complementarities and Beyond Thanks: An extended abstract of this paper [ 25 ] appears in the Proceedings of the 23rd International Conference on Autonomous Agents and Multiagent Systems (AAMAS 2024).
Kenjiro Takazawa

Abstract
Congestion games offer a primary model in the study of pure Nash equilibria in non-cooperative games, and a number of generalized models have been proposed in the literature. One line of generalization includes weighted congestion games, in which the cost of a resource is a function of the total weight of the players choosing that resource. Another line includes congestion games with mixed costs, in which the cost imposed on a player is a convex combination of the total cost and the maximum cost of the resources in her strategy. This model is further generalized to that of congestion games with complementarities. For the above models, the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium is proved under some assumptions, including that the strategy space of each player is the base family of a matroid and that the cost functions have a certain kind of monotonicity. In this paper, we deal with common generalizations of these two lines, namely weighted matroid congestion games with complementarities, and its further generalization. Our main technical contribution is a proof of the existence of pure Nash equilibria in these generalized models under a simplified assumption on the monotonicity, which provide a common extension of the previous results. We also present some extensions on the existence of pure Nash equilibria in player-specific and weighted matroid congestion games with mixed costs. Keywords Non-Cooperative Game Theory; Pure Nash Equilibrium; Congestion Game; Matroid



1
Introduction

The analysis of the existence of pure Nash equilibria in non-cooperative games is a central topic in algorithmic game theory
[
19
,
22
]
,
and the model of
congestion games

[
4
]
play an important role in this topic.
A congestion game models the human behavior of selecting the best strategy
in a situation where congestion results in a larger cost.
In a congestion game,
each strategy is a set of
resources
.
The cost of a resource is represented by a function of the number of the players using it.
The entire cost imposed on a player is the sum of the costs of the resources in her strategy.
For a formal description of congestion games,
see Section
2.2
.

The most important feature of congestion games is that
every congestion game is a
potential game
,
which implies that every congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium
[
21
]
.
Further,
the converse is also true:
every potential game is represented by a congestion game
[
17
]
.
Since those primary results,
a number of generalized models of congestion games have been proposed.
As is mentioned below,
however,
a pure Nash equilibrium may not exist in those generalized models,
and its existence is proved under certain assumptions.
The purpose of this paper is to provide a model
commonly generalizing previous models,
while preserving the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium.

1.1
Previous Work

In this section,
we describe two major directions of
generalizations of congestion games.

1.1.1
Weighted and Player-Specific Congestion Games

In a main direction of the study of congestion games, the cost functions of the resources are generalized.
One model in this direction is a
weighted congestion game
:
each player has a nonnegative weight;
and
the cost of a resource
is a function of the total weight of the players using it.
Another model is a
player-specific congestion game
,
in which
the resources have a cost function specific to each player.

Both of the weighted and player-specific congestion games
do not have
a pure Nash equilibrium in general,
but
are guaranteed to possess one under certain assumptions.
A congestion game is referred to as a
singleton game

if every strategy of every player consists of a single resource,
and as a
matroid game

if the strategy space of each player is a base family of a matroid;
see Section
2.1
for the definition of matroids.
Milchtaich
[
16
]
proved that every singleton player-specific congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium.
Ackermann, Röglin, and Vöcking
[
1
]
extended this result to
matroid congestion games,
i.e., every player-specific matroid congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium.
A further generalized model on polymatroids is studied by Harks, Klimm, and Peis
[
12
]
.
Ackermann, Röglin, and Vöcking
[
1
]
also proved that
every weighted matroid congestion game have a pure Nash equilibrium,
and this result is extended to a further generalized model of a
matroid congestion game with set-functional costs
by Takazawa
[
24
]
.
Some of these results are explained in detail in Section
2.3
.

1.1.2
Congestion Games with Bottleneck Costs, Mixed Costs, and Complementarities

In another major direction of generalization,
the cost imposed on a player is not simply defined as the sum of the costs of the resources in the strategy.
A typical model is a
bottleneck congestion game
,
in which
the cost on a player is defined as the maximum cost of the resources in her strategy.
Banner and Orda
[
3
]
proved the existence of pure Nash equilibria
and analyzed the price of anarchy
in
certain kinds of network bottleneck congestion games,
in which a strategy of a player is a flow in a given network.

Harks, Klimm, and Möhring
[
11
]
introduced a generalized class of
congestion games with lexicographical improvement property
.
They proved
that every bottleneck congestion game belongs to this class,
and that
every congestion game in this class possesses a
strong equilibrium

[
2
]
,
a stronger concept of a pure Nash equilibrium.
Harks, Hoefer, Klimm, and Skopalik
[
10
]
analyzed the complexity of finding a strong equilibrium
in bottleneck congestion games.
In particular,
they presented polynomial-time algorithms for
several classes of bottleneck congestion games,
including
matroid bottleneck congestion games.

Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
9
]

proposed a common generalization of classical congestion games
and bottleneck congestion games,
called
congestion games with mixed costs
.
In this model,
the cost imposed on a player is a convex combination of the
total cost and the maximum cost of the resources in her strategy;
see Section
2.4
for detailed description.
For this model,
a matroid game may fail to possess a pure Nash equilibrium.
Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
9
]

proved the existence of
pure Nash equilibria in certain classes of this model,
including
singleton games,
and matroid games with some more property
(Theorem
2.7
below).

Feldotto, Leder, Skopalik
[
8
]
introduced a further generalized model,

congestion games with complementarities
.
In this model,
the cost imposed on a player
is determined by an
aggregation function
,
which a general function of the costs of the resources in the strategy.
A typical example of an aggregation function is
the
L^{p}
norm of the costs of the resources.
A detailed description of this model appears in Section
2.4
.
For this model,
Feldotto, Leder, Skopalik
[
8
]

proved the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium
in matroid games under an assumption on the monotonicity of the aggregation function,
which is defined in a sophisticated manner (Theorem
2.8
below).
Related work dealing with aggregation cost functions also appears in
[
14
,
15
]
.

1.2
Our Contribution

The aim of this paper is to offer a model of congestion games which commonly generalizes the previous models in the two directions mentioned in Section
1.1
.
In particular,
we provide a generalized model preserving the existence of pure Nash equilibria in matroid games.

Our main contribution is a common extension of the existence of pure Nash equilibria in
weighted congestion games and congestion games with complementarities.
Namely,
our model is

weighted congestion games with complementarities
,
and
its further generalization of

congestion games with set-functional costs and complementarities
.
On the basis of
the previous results
[
1
,
8
,
24
]
,
we prove that every game in this model has a pure Nash equilibrium
if it is a matroid game and the aggregation function has a certain monotonicity (see Theorem
3.3
below).
This class includes
weighted matroid bottleneck congestion games
and
weighted congestion games with
L^{p}
-aggregation functions.
Thus,
our result implies the guaranteed existence of pure Nash equilibria in these models (Corollaries
3.4
and
3.5
below).
We remark here that
the definition of
the monotonicity of the aggregation functions in our model is
simpler than that in the previous work
[
8
]
,
which provides a simpler proof for the existence of pure Nash equilibria and
a clearer comprehension of the model.

We also generalize the model of congestion games with mixed costs
to those with player-specific cost functions
and
set-functional cost functions.
For these models,
we extend the result of Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
9
]

to prove the existence of pure Nash equilibria under the same assumptions.

1.3
Organization of the Paper

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
,
we give a detailed description of the previous models.
In Section
3
,
we present our main contribution:
the generalized model of congestion games with set-functional costs and complementarities;
a simplified definition of the monotonicity of the aggregation functions; and
a proof of the existence of pure Nash equilibria in matroid games with a monotone aggregation function.
Finally,
in Section
4
,
we deal with generalizations of congestion games with mixed costs.

2
Preliminaries

In this section,
we present some basic properties of matroids,
followed by descriptions of classical and recent generalized models of
congestion games.

2.1
Matroids

Let
E
be a finite set and
\mathcal{S}\subseteq 2^{E}
its subset family.
The pair
(E,\mathcal{S})
is a
matroid

if
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
and
it satisfies the following exchange axiom:

(
EX
)

for
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}

and for
e\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
,
there exists
f\in S^{\prime}\setminus S

such that

(S\setminus\{e\})\cup\{f\}\in\mathcal{S}
.

The subset family
\mathcal{S}
is called the
base family
,
and a set
S\subseteq E
belonging to
\mathcal{S}
is called a
base

of the matroid
(E,\mathcal{S})
.
It is noteworthy that all bases have the same cardinality,
i.e.,
|S|=|S^{\prime}|
for each
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
for every matroid
(E,\mathcal{S})
.

Optimization over matroids
has formed an extensive research field in combinatorial optimization,
due to its nice structure
and wide range of application
(see, e.g.,
[
18
,
20
,
23
]
).
Below we show some properties of matroids,
which will be used in subsequent sections.

First, the one-sided exchange axiom (
EX
) is equivalent to
the following stronger
two-sided exchange property.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
5
]
)
.

Every matroid
(E,\mathcal{S})
has the following simultaneous exchange property:

(
\mbox{EX}\pm
)

for
S,S^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}

and for
e\in S\setminus S^{\prime}
,
there exists
f\in S^{\prime}\setminus S

such that

(S\setminus\{e\})\cup\{f\}\in\mathcal{S}

and

(S^{\prime}\setminus\{f\})\cup\{e\}\in\mathcal{S}
.

The next lemma is derived from the exchange axiom (EX) and will be used in the analysis of congestion games with mixed costs.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(E,\mathcal{S})
be a matroid
and let
c_{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}
denote the cost of an element
e\in E
.
Then,
a base
S\in\mathcal{S}
minimizing
the total cost

\sum_{e\in S}c_{e}

among all bases in
\mathcal{S}

also minimizes
the maximum cost

\max_{e\in S}\{c_{e}\}

among all bases in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Let a base
S_{1}\in\mathcal{S}
minimize
\sum_{e\in S}c_{e}
among all bases.
Suppose to the contrary that
S_{1}
does not minimize
\max_{e\in S}\{c_{e}\}
,
i.e., there exists another base
S_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
such that
an element
e^{*}\in S_{1}\setminus S_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle c_{e^{*}}>c_{f}\quad\mbox{for each element $f\in S_{2}$}.

Then,
it follows from the exchange axiom (
EX
) that
there exists an element
f^{*}\in S_{2}\setminus S_{1}

such that

S_{1}^{\prime}=(S_{1}\setminus\{e^{*}\})\cup\{f\}\in\mathcal{S}
.
We now obtain

\sum_{e\in S_{1}^{\prime}}c_{e}=\sum_{e\in S_{1}}c_{e}-c_{e^{*}}+c_{f^{*}}<\sum_{e\in S_{1}}c_{e},

contradicting that
S_{1}
is a base minimizing
\sum_{e\in S}c_{e}
.
∎

2.2
Classical Model of Congestion Games

Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
denote the sets of the nonnegative integers and the nonnegative real numbers, respectively.
The classical model of congestion games is represented as

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E}).

Here,

N=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
E
denote the sets of the players and the resources,
respectively.
For each player
i\in N
,
the resource subset family
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subseteq 2^{E}
denotes the strategy space of
i
.
For each resource
e\in E
,
a cost function
c_{e}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}

is associated.

In a congestion game

(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E})
,
the cost imposed on a player is determined in the following way.
A
strategy profile

\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is a collection of
the strategies of all players,
i.e.,
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
for each
i\in N
.
For a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
and
a resource
e\in E
,
let

N_{e}(\mathbf{S})

denote
the set of the players choosing
e
in her strategy,
and

n_{e}(\mathbf{S})
its cardinality.
Namely,

N_{e}(\mathbf{S})=\{i\in N\mid e\in S_{i}\},\quad n_{e}(\mathbf{S})=\left|\{i\in N\mid e\in S_{i}\}\right|.

Now
c_{e}(n_{e}(\mathbf{S}))\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
represents
the cost of using a resource
e
in a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}
.
The cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
imposed on a player
i\in N

in a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}

is defined as
the total cost of the resources in her strategy
S_{i}
,
that is,

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=\sum_{e\in S_{i}}c_{e}(n_{e}(\mathbf{S})).

For
a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})

and
a player
i\in N
,
let
\mathbf{S}_{-i}
denote
the collection of the strategies in
\mathbf{S}
other than
S_{i}
,
i.e.,

\mathbf{S}_{-i}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{i-1},S_{i+1},\ldots,S_{n})
.
A
pure Nash equilibrium
is a strategy profile in which
no player has an incentive to change her strategy.
Namely,
a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is a pure Nash equilibrium
if

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})\leq\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S}_{-i},S_{i}^{\prime})

holds for each player
i\in N
and
each strategy
S_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
.
As mentioned in Section
1
,
every congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium
[
21
]
.

2.3
Weighted and Player-Specific Congestion Games

The model of
weighted congestion games
is described as

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E},(w_{i})_{i\in N}).

The difference from the classical model is the presence of the nonnegative weight

w_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
of each player
i\in N
.
For each resource
e\in E
,
the argument of the cost function
c_{e}
is
not the cardinality of the players using
e
,
but the sum of the weights of those players.
For a subset
N^{\prime}\subseteq N
of players,
let
w(N^{\prime})
denote the total weight of the players in
N^{\prime}
,
i.e.,
w(N^{\prime})=\sum_{i\in N^{\prime}}w_{i}
.
In a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
,
the cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
imposed on a player
i\in N
is described as

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=\sum_{e\in S_{i}}c_{e}(w(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}))).

Hereafter,
we assume that
c_{e}
is monotonically nondecreasing
for each resource
e\in E
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\mbox{$x<y$ implies $c_{e}(x)\leq c_{e}(y)$}\quad(x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}).

In contrast to the classical model,
weighted congestion games do not necessarily have pure Nash equilibria.
Ackermann, Röglin, and Vöcking
[
1
]
proved that
a weighted congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium
if it is a
matroid game
.
Recall that a congestion game is a matroid game if
the strategy space of each player is a base family of a matroid.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Every weighted matroid congestion game possesses a pure Nash equilibrium.

Ackermann, Röglin, and Vöcking
[
1
]
also proved that
the matroid property is
the maximal assumption
in a certain sense
ensuring the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium.

A further generalized model of weighted congestion games is proposed by Takazawa
[
24
]
.
A distinctive feature of this model is that
the cost function
c_{e}
of each resource
e\in E
is a set function
defined on the power set
2^{N}
of the players:

c_{e}\colon 2^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
Similarly to the cost functions in weighted congestion games,
this cost function

c_{e}

is assumed to be monotonically nondecreasing,
that is,

\mbox{$X\subseteq Y$ implies $c_{e}(X)\leq c_{e}(Y)$ \quad($X,Y\subseteq N$)}.

In a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
,
the cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
imposed on a player
i\in N
is described as

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=\sum_{e\in S_{i}}c_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S})).

We refer to this model as a
congestion game with set-functional costs
.

It is straightforward to see that this model generalizes weighted congestion games.
To see this fact,
for an instance of a weighted congestion game
(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E},(w_{i})_{i\in N})
,
it suffices to define a set function
c^{\prime}_{e}\colon 2^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
by

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{e}(N^{\prime})=c_{e}(w(N^{\prime}))\quad(N^{\prime}\subseteq N)

(1)

for each resource
e\in E
.
Takazawa
[
24
]
extended Theorem
2.3

as follows.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
24
]
)
.

Every matroid congestion game with set-functional costs has a pure Nash equilibrium.

Remark 2.5
.

The same kind of generalization with set-functional costs is proposed by Kiyosue and Takazawa
[
13
]
for
budget games

[
6
,
7
]
.
A budget game is a variant of congestion game in which the players share the budgets, or utilities, of the resources.
In the original model
[
6
,
7
]
,
the utility of a resource is simply divided by the number of the players using that resource and allocated to those players.
In the generalized model
[
13
]
,
the allocated utility is represented by a monotonically nondecreasing set function.
Similarly to the situation of congestion games described in Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
,
the fact that the strategy space of each player is a base family of a matroid
plays an essential role in guaranteeing the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium
in both of the original and generalized models of
budget games.

In a
player-specific congestion game
,
a cost function is defined for each pair of a player and a resource.
Namely,
a player-specific congestion game represented by

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{i,e})_{i\in N,e\in E}),

where

c_{i,e}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a monotonically nondecreasing function for each
i\in N
and
e\in E
.
The cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
imposed on a player
i\in N

in a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
is defined as

\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=\sum_{e\in S_{i}}c_{i,e}(n_{e}(\mathbf{S})).

As mentioned in Section
1
,
every player-specific singleton congestion game has a pure Nash equilibrium
[
16
]
.
Ackermann, Röglin, and Vöcking
[
1
]
extended the existence of pure Nash equilibria in player-specific matroid congestion games.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
1
]
)
.

Every player-specific matroid congestion game possesses a pure Nash equilibrium.

2.4
Congestion Games with Mixed Costs and with Complementarities

Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
9
]

proposed a common generalization of classical congestion games
and bottleneck congestion games,
called
congestion games with mixed costs
.
A congestion game with mixed costs is represented by a tuple

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(\ell_{e})_{e\in E},(b_{e})_{e\in E},(\alpha_{i})_{i\in N}).

For each resource
e\in E
,
let
\ell_{e}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
and
b_{e}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
denote
its
latency cost function
and
bottleneck cost function
,
respectively.
We assume that both

\ell_{e}
and
b_{e}
are monotonically nondecreasing.
For each player
i\in N
,
a real number
\alpha_{i}\in[0,1]
denotes her
preference value
.
In a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
,
the cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
imposed on a player
i\in N
is defined as

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=\alpha_{i}\cdot\sum_{e\in S_{i}}\ell_{e}(n_{e}(\mathbf{S}))+(1-\alpha_{i})\cdot\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{b_{e}(n_{e}(\mathbf{S}))\}.

It is straightforward to see that
a congestion game with mixed costs
is exactly a classical congestion game
if
\alpha_{i}=1
for each player
i\in N
,
and
a bottleneck congestion game
if
\alpha_{i}=0
for each player
i\in N
.

On the existence of pure Nash equilibria in congestion games with mixed costs,
Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
9
]
proved the following theorem.
For a congestion game with mixed costs,
the cost functions

\ell_{e}
and
b_{e}

(e\in E)

have
monotone dependence
if
there exists a monotonically nondecreasing function
d\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfying that

\displaystyle b_{e}(x)=d(\ell_{e}(x))\qquad(x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+})

for each resource
e\in E
.

Theorem 2.7

(
[
9
]
)
.

A congestion game
with mixed costs
has a pure Nash equilibrium
in the following three cases.

(i)

It is a singleton game.

(ii)

It is a matroid game and
\alpha_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for each player
i\in N
.

(iii)

It is a matroid game and the cost functions have monotone dependence.

Feldotto, Leder, and Skopalik
[
8
]
proposed a further generalized model of
a
congestion game with complementarities
,
which is represented by a tuple

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E},g).

In this model,

g\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the aggregation function,
which is defined on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}
for a positive integer
r
,
and
we assume that the sizes of the strategies of all players are equal to
r
,
i.e.,
|S_{i}|=r
for each player
i\in N
and each strategy
S_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
.
For a player
i\in N
and a strategy profile
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
,
where
S_{i}=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
,
the cost
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})
on
i
in
\mathbf{S}
is described as

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=g(c_{e_{1}}(n_{e_{1}}(\mathbf{S})),\ldots,c_{e_{r}}(n_{e_{r}}(\mathbf{S}))).

It is straightforward to see that congestion games with complementarities
generalize classical congestion games and bottleneck congestion games.
An important special case analyzed in detail in
[
8
]
is

congestion games with
L^{p}
-aggregation functions
,
i.e.,

\displaystyle g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}{x_{j}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(2)

for some value
p\geq 1
.
It is also noteworthy that,
if the aggregation function is player-specific,
then this model includes
congestion games with mixed costs.

Feldotto, Leder, Skopalik
[
8
]
proved the existence of a pure Nash equilibrium
under the assumption that it is a matroid game
and
the aggregation function

g
is
weakly monotone
.
Weak monotone functions
generalize the monotonically nondecreasing functions,
and
are defined in a sophisticated manner.
The readers are referred to their original paper
[
8
]

for the definition of weakly monotone functions.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
8
]
)
.

Every matroid congestion game with complementarities
has a pure Nash equilibrium
if
the aggregation function is
weakly monotone.

3
Weighted Congestion Games with Complementarities and Beyond

The central subject of this paper is
described in this section.
We deal with
a common generalization of weighted congestion games and congestion games with complementarities,
referred to as
weighted congestion games with complementarities
,
and a further generalized model of
congestion games with set-functional costs and complementarities
.
Below we investigate

3.1
Our Model

A
congestion game with set-functional costs and complementarities

\displaystyle(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E},g).

Again we assume that

The difference from congestion games with complementarities described in Section

is a set function for each resource

Consequently,
for a player

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S})=g(c_{e_{1}}(N_{e_{1}}(\mathbf{S})),\ldots,c_{e_{r}}(N_{e_{r}}(\mathbf{S}))).

The monotonicity of the cost functions

(
e\in E
) and the aggregation function
g

in the previous models is represented in the following manner.
First,
in the aggregation of the costs of the resources in a strategy of a player,
we take no account of the order of the resources.
Formally,
we assume that

g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}})=g(\sigma({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}))

for each cost vector
{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}

each permutation

of a set of

Then,
we adopt the following definition of weakly monotone functions,
which is simpler than that in
[

]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let

be a positive integer.
A function

is
weakly monotone
if,
for each
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
it holds that

g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},x)\leq g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},y)\quad\mbox{for each ${\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r-1}_{+}$}

or

g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},x)\geq g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},y)\quad\mbox{for each ${\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r-1}_{+}$}.

It is straightforward to see that
a function
g\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{r}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\leq{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}
implies
g({\mbox{\boldmath$u$}})\leq g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}})

{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}},{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}
)

is weakly monotone,
where
{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}\leq{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}
means

for each

.
For instance,

Associated with
Definition

of the weak monotonicity of the aggregation function

,
the monotonicity of the cost function

(
e\in E
)
is adjusted in the following way.

Definition 3.2
.

For a weakly monotone aggregation function

,
a cost function

is
monotonically nondecreasing

with respect to
g
if,
for each
X,Y\subseteq N
with
X\subseteq Y
,
it holds that

g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},c(X))\leq g({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime},c(Y))\quad\mbox{for each ${\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r-1}_{+}$}.

Definition

means that
more players on one resource result in larger cost,
even in the weak monotonicity of the aggregation function.

3.2

Our main technical contribution is the following theorem,

Theorem 3.3

A matroid congestion game

(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E},g)

Before a proof,

Corollary 3.4

Every weighted matroid bottleneck congestion game

Corollary 3.5

Every weighted matroid congestion game with

-aggregation functions

On the basis of Lemma

by showing that

For this purpose,

Let

\mbox{$\Phi(\mathbf{S})=(\phi_{e_{1}}(\mathbf{S}),\ldots,\phi_{e_{m}}(\mathbf{S}))$,

For two strategy profiles

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S}){}

\displaystyle{}=\alpha_{i}\cdot\sum_{e\in S_{i}}\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}))+(1-\alpha_{i})\cdot\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{b_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}))\}

\displaystyle{}=\alpha_{i}\cdot\sum_{e\in S_{i}}\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}))+(1-\alpha_{i})\cdot\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{d(\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S})))\}

for a monotonically nondecreasing function
d\colon{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.

Now construct a congestion game

G^{\prime}=(N,E,(\mathcal{S}_{i})_{i\in N},(c_{e})_{e\in E})
with set-functional costs

c_{e}\colon 2^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
(
e\in E
)
defined by

\displaystyle c_{e}(N^{\prime})=\ell_{e}(N^{\prime})\quad(N^{\prime}\subseteq N).

It follows from Theorem
2.4
that

G^{\prime}
has
a pure Nash equilibrium
\mathbf{S}=(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
.

We have that
S_{i}
minimizes
\sum_{e\in S_{i}}\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}_{-i},S_{i}))

among all the strategies in
\mathcal{S}_{i}
.
Since
G
is a matroid game,
it follows from Lemma
2.2
that

S_{i}
also minimizes
\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}_{-i},S_{i}))\}
.
From the monotonicity of
d
,
it follows that

\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{d(\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S})))\}=d\left(\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}))\}\right),

and
hence
S_{i}
further minimizes
\max_{e\in S_{i}}\{d(\ell_{e}(N_{e}(\mathbf{S}_{-i},S_{i})))\}
.
Consequently,

S_{i}
minimizes
\gamma_{i}(\mathbf{S}_{-i},S_{i})
,
and
therefore
we conclude that
\mathbf{S}
is also a pure Nash equilibrium in
G
.
∎

5
Conclusion

We have presented a model of congestion games
with set-functional costs and complementarities,
which commonly generalizes previous models
such as
weighted congestion games,
congestion games with set-functional costs,
congestion games with bottleneck costs,
and
congestion games with complementarities.
Main technical contributions of this paper are
a simplification of the monotonicity of the aggregation functions
and
a proof of the existence of pure Nash equilibria
for matroid games under the simplified definition of the monotone aggregation functions.
We have also extended the existence of pure Nash equilibria in
congestion games with mixed costs
to
player-specific and weighted congestion games.

A possible direction of future research is to analyze the algorithmic aspects and the efficiency of the pure Nash equilibria,
such as
the convergence time,
the price of anarchy,
and the price of stability.
It is also of interest to investigate
a relation between our model and
congestion games with lexicographical improvement property
[
11
]
.

Abstract
. Given two groups A A and B B , the Kaluzhnin–Krasner universal embedding theorem states that the wreath product A ≀ B A\wr B acts as a universal receptacle for extensions from A A to B B . For a split extension, this embedding is compatible with the canonical splitting of the wreath product, which is further universal in a precise sense. This result was recently extended to Lie algebras and to cocommutative Hopf algebras. The aim of the present article is to explore the feasibility of adapting the theorem to other types of algebraic structures. By explaining the underlying unity of the three known cases, our analysis gives necessary and sufficient conditions for this to happen. From those we may for instance conclude that a version for crossed modules can indeed be attained, while the theorem cannot be adapted to, say, associative algebras, Jordan algebras or Leibniz algebras, when working over an infinite field: we prove that then, amongst non-associative algebras, only Lie algebras admit a universal Kaluzhnin–Krasner embedding theorem.

\langle f,1_{B}\rangle\bigl(\varphi(g,b)\bigr)=\langle f,1_{B}\rangle\bigl(bg(bf(g))^{-1}\bigr)=bf(g)(bf(g))^{-1}=1

so

corestricts to the kernel of
\langle f,1_{B}\rangle
to give us a morphism

.

Conversely, any

can be uniquely written in reduced form
h=b_{1}g_{1}\cdots b_{n}g_{n}
, i.e. with
b_{i}\in B\setminus\{1\}
for
i\in\{2,\dots,n\}
,
g_{i}\in G\setminus\{1\}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
b_{1}\in B
and
g_{n}\in G
. Then we define

by setting

\psi(h)\coloneq(g_{1},b_{1})\bigl(g_{2},b_{1}f(g_{1})b_{2}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(g_{n},b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1})b_{n}\bigr)

for
h=b_{1}g_{1}\cdots b_{n}g_{n}\in\Ker(\langle f,1_{B}\rangle)
in reduced form. This function is well defined since the reduced form is unique. At this point it is not so clear whether or not it is a group homomorphism, but this is not needed for our purposes. It will actually follow once we prove that
\psi
is the inverse function of the group homomorphism
\phi
.

So, let us check that
\phi
and
\psi
are each other’s inverse. For an element
h=b_{1}g_{1}\cdots b_{n}g_{n}
of
\Ker(\langle f,1_{B}\rangle)
written in reduced form, we compute

\displaystyle\phi\bigl(\psi(h)\bigr)=

\displaystyle\;\phi\bigl((g_{1},b_{1})\bigl(g_{2},b_{1}f(g_{1})b_{2}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(g_{n},b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1})b_{n}\bigr)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\bigl(b_{1}g_{1}(b_{1}f(g_{1}))^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(b_{1}f(g_{1})b_{2}g_{2}(b_{1}f(g_{1})b_{2}f(g_{2}))^{-1}\bigr)\cdots

\displaystyle\cdot\bigl(b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1})b_{n}g_{n}(b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1})b_{n}f(g_{n}))^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;b_{1}g_{1}\cdots b_{n}g_{n}=h

as wanted, the last step using that
b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1})b_{n}f(g_{n})=1
since our element
h=b_{1}g_{1}\cdots b_{n}g_{n}
is in the kernel of
\langle f,1_{B}\rangle
. For the other direction, take
p=(g_{1},b_{1})\cdots(g_{n},b_{n})\in P
written in a minimal way (which is possible since the relations of

reduce the length of the words). We compute

\displaystyle\phi(p)

\displaystyle=\bigl(b_{1}g_{1}(b_{1}f(g_{1}))^{-1}\bigr)\cdots\bigl(b_{n}g_{n}(b_{n}f(g_{n}))^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=b_{1}g_{1}\bigl((b_{1}f(g_{1}))^{-1}b_{2}\bigr)g_{2}\cdots g_{n-1}\bigl((b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1}))^{-1}b_{n}\bigr)g_{n}(b_{n}f(g_{n}))^{-1}1\text{.}

Then, since we chose a minimal way of writing

\displaystyle\psi\bigl(\phi(p)\bigr)

\displaystyle=(g_{1},b_{1})\bigl(g_{2},b_{1}f(g_{1})((b_{1}f(g_{1}))^{-1}b_{2})\bigr)\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\bigl(g_{n},b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots((b_{n-2}f(g_{n-2}))^{-1}b_{n-1})f(g_{n-1})((b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1}))^{-1}b_{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\cdot\bigl(1,b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots((b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1}))^{-1}b_{n})f(g_{n})(b_{n}f(g_{n}))^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(g_{1},b_{1})(g_{2},b_{2})\cdots(g_{n},b_{n})(1,1)=p\text{.}

In the other case, the reduced form of

\displaystyle=(g_{1},b_{1})\bigl(g_{2},b_{1}f(g_{1})((b_{1}f(g_{1}))^{-1}b_{2})\bigr)\cdots

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\bigl(g_{n},b_{1}f(g_{1})\cdots((b_{n-2}f(g_{n-2}))^{-1}b_{n-1})f(g_{n-1})((b_{n-1}f(g_{n-1}))^{-1}b_{n})\bigr)

\displaystyle=(g_{1},b_{1})(g_{2},b_{2})\cdots(g_{n},b_{n})=p\text{,}

finishing the proof.
∎

If now
s\colon B\to G
is a set-theoretical splitting of

\chi\colon KL(E)\to U(E)\colon(g,b)\mapsto s(b)\cdot g\cdot s(b\cdot f(g))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\bigl(s(b)\cdot g\cdot s(b\cdot f(g))^{-1}\bigr)\cdot\bigl(s(bf(g))\cdot g^{\prime}\cdot s(bf(g)\cdot f(g^{\prime}))^{-1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=s(b)\cdot gg^{\prime}\cdot s(b\cdot f(gg^{\prime}))^{-1}

The corresponding morphism

4.5.
Further examples

From the above it is clear that, even though a universal Kaluzhnin–Krasner embedding in its standard form does not follow right away from Theorem
3.5
, there is very little hope of establishing such an embedding in contexts where that theorem is not also valid. As a consequence, the category of crossed modules, as well as the examples worked out in
[
13
]
, being (LACC) semi-abelian categories, all satisfy Theorem
3.5
, hence are good candidates for a “classical” Kaluzhnin–Krasner embedding. We will, however, end the article with a slightly different result: a universal embedding theorem for
abelian
split extensions which holds in
any
semi-abelian variety of algebras.

5.
The case of abelian actions

We pick a semi-abelian variety of algebras
\mathbb{X}
. Note that these are exactly the protomodular varieties, which were characterized in
[
5
,
9
]
.
We are now going to explain that the condition (LACC) is not necessary in such a category, if we want to embed just the
abelian
actions, which correspond to split extensions equipped with a Beck module structure
[
4
]
.

Given an object
B
, recall that a
Beck module over
B
is an extension (
¶
‣
3
) that carries an internal abelian group structure in
\mathsf{Ext}(B)
—determined, in particular, by a unit
s\colon 1_{B}\to f
and a multiplication
m\colon f\times_{B}f\to f
in the category
\mathbb{X}/_{B}
of objects over
B
—which automatically makes this extension (
¶
‣
3
) a split extension. Let us write
\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{Ext}(B))
for the category of (split) extensions over
B
, equipped with an abelian group structure; its morphisms are morphisms of split extensions. This category is abelian, and since
\mathbb{X}
is a variety of algebras,
[
20
, Theorem 2.9]
implies that the lifting
\underline{K}\colon{\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{Ext}(B))\to\mathsf{Ab}(\mathbb{X})}
of the functor
K\colon{\mathsf{ExtS}(B)\to\mathbb{X}}
to abelian group objects has a right adjoint
\underline{R}
. From this we deduce:

Theorem 5.1
.

In a semi-abelian variety
\mathbb{X}
, for any object
B
and any abelian group object
A
there exists a universal Beck module

\underline{R}(A)=\bigl({0\to\underline{K}\underline{R}(A)\to A\mathbin{\underline{\wr}}B\leftrightarrows B\to 0}\bigr)

over
B
into which each abelian split extension of the form

S=\bigl({0\to A\to G\leftrightarrows B\to 0}\bigr)

embeds. This embedding is given by the
S

\underline{\eta}_{S}\colon S\to\underline{R}\underline{K}(S)
of the unit of the adjunction
\underline{K}\dashv\underline{R}
. ∎

Note that we underlined the wreath product symbol to distinguish
A\mathbin{\underline{\wr}}B
from the ordinary wreath product
A\wr B
.

Remark 5.2
.

To show that the unit
\underline{\eta}
of the adjunction
\underline{K}\dashv\underline{R}

2.5

Remark 5.3
.

It is well known that the category
\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{Ext}(B))
is again a variety of algebras, and since it is an abelian category as well, it is a category of modules over a ring
[
12

\mathsf{Mod}_{\Pi}\simeq\mathsf{Ab}(\mathsf{Ext}(0))

By the analysis made in
[
10

to

\mathbb{K}
-algebras satisfies this condition. Further examples of such categories are given in

26

\mathbb{K}
-vector space equipped with the trivial multiplication, so that a Beck module over an algebra

S
above where the result of multiplying two elements of

For instance, in the case of

-Lie algebras, we have

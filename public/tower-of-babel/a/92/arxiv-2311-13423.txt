1.
Introduction

1.1.

Take a complex-analytic germ
(X,o)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{N},o).

The first step in its visualization is Milnor’s Conic structure theorem:

The germ
(X,o)
is homeomorphic to the (standard) cone over the link,

Cone[Link[X]]\subset(\mathbb{C}^{N},o).

In “most cases” this homeomorphism cannot be chosen differentiable in any sense.

In some cases this (non-embedded) topological equivalence can be strengthened to bi-Lipschitz equivalence, as follows.
The (standard) metric on
(\mathbb{C}^{N},o)
induces the inner metrics on
(X,o)
and
Cone[Link[X]],
by the length of the shortest path between two points.
The germ
(X,o)
is called inner-metrically-conical (IMC) if the homeomorphism
(X,o)\xrightarrow[\,\smash{\raisebox{3.46594pt}{$\scriptstyle\sim$}}\,]{}{}Cone[Link[X]]
can be chosen
bi-Lipschitz with respect to these inner metrics.
(All the germs, morphisms, and constructions in our paper are subanalytic.)

Any complex analytic curve germ is IMC. In higher dimensions the situation is more complicated.
The first
weighted-homogeneous non-IMC surface germs were found in
[
Birbrair-Fernandes.08
]
.
If
(X,o)
is a weighted-homogeneous IMC-germ of quotient type,
{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\mathbb{C}^{2}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\mu$}\right.},
then its two lowest weights are necessarily equal,
[
B.F.N.08
]
.
The converse statement was verified in
[
B.F.N.09
]
for Brieskorn-Pham singularities in
(\mathbb{C}^{3},o)
.
Finally,
[
B.N.P.14
]
established the thick-thin decomposition for normal surface germs.
In particular, this gave an algorithm to verify the (non)IMC-property via the resolution graph of the singularity.
In higher dimensions, among the
A_{k}
-types (with equation
\sum^{n}_{i=1}x^{2}_{i}+x^{k+1}_{n+1}=0
), the only IMC-case is
A_{1},

[
B.F.N.G.O’S.09
]
.

In all these cases fast loops (and choking horns) were the only obstruction to the IMC property. To detect/forbid these fast loops is in general a complicated task.
The only exception are the normal surface singularities,
[
B.N.P.14
]
, with criteria via the resolution graph.
Fast loops (and fast cycles in higher dimensions) are important far beyond the IMC-context. They carry essential information on the local geometry and topology of
(X,o).

In
[
Kerner-Mendes.23
]
we detect fast loops via coverings and their discriminants.

1.2.

In this paper we show how to detect fast cycles on perturbations of weighted-homogeneous germs.

Let
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}.
Take a weighted-homogeneous complete intersection germ
X_{o}:=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)

of weights
\omega_{1}\leq\omega_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\omega_{N},
and of dimension
n.
It can have arbitrary singularity, can be non-reduced.
Perturb it by higher-order terms,
X:=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+\epsilon\cdot{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o),
here
p_{i}=ord_{\omega}f_{p_{i}}<ord_{\omega}f_{>p_{i}}.

We can alow also
ord_{\omega}f_{p_{i}}\leq ord_{\omega}f_{>p_{i}},
assuming
|\epsilon|\ll 1.

1.2.1.
Deforming the weighted-homogeneous foliation

Take the weighted homogeneous arcs,
\gamma_{\underline{s}}(t)=(s_{1}t^{\omega_{1}},\dots,s_{N}t^{\omega_{N}}).
They foliate the space
\mathbbm{k}^{N},
and this foliation is compatible with the germ
X_{o}\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o).

Given the family
X_{\epsilon}:=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+\epsilon\cdot{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}})

one would like to deform the foliation
\{\gamma_{\underline{s}}\}_{\underline{s}}
to a foliation
\{\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}\}_{\underline{s}},
compatibly with
X_{\epsilon}.

Such a deformation is in general obstructed by
Sing(X_{o})
and by the singularities of the intersection of
X_{o}
with the flag of coordinate planes,
\mathbbm{k}^{N}\supset V(x_{1})\supset\cdots\supset V(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).
Accordingly one defines the “obstruction locus”

(1)

\Sigma=Sing[X_{o}]\cup Sing[X_{o}\cap V(x_{1})]\cup\cdots\cup Sing[X_{o}\cap V(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})]\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o).

Lemma
3.3
tells (roughly): outside of a hornic neighborhood
\mathcal{U}(\Sigma)\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)

the foliation
\{\gamma_{\underline{s}}\}_{\underline{s}}
deforms to a foliation
\{\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}\}_{\underline{s}},
compatibly with
X_{\epsilon},
preserving the (non)tangency of the arcs, and with various other controlled properties. If
\Sigma=\{o\}
then
\{\gamma_{\underline{s}}\}_{\underline{s}}
deforms to a foliation
\{\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}\}_{\underline{s}}
globally on
(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o).

This construction is purely algebraic, no vector field integration is involved. It is based on Tougeron’s implicit function theorem and the study of hornic behaviours and its
arc.foliations (see [Kerner-Mendes.0]).

The deformed foliation
\{\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}\}_{\underline{s}}
is as useful for
X_{\epsilon},
as the weighted homogeneous
\{\gamma_{\underline{s}}\}_{\underline{s}}
is for
X_{o}.

An application is a result on the ambient Lipschitz equivalence of zero-sets.

Corollary
3.7
.

If
\Sigma=\{o\}
then the family
X_{\epsilon}=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+\epsilon\cdot{\underline{f}}_{\geq{\underline{p}}})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)
is ambient-trivializable by a homeomorphism that is Nash off the origin and Lipschitz-semi-algebraic at the origin.

For example, any higher order deformation of the germ
V(\sum x^{p_{i}}_{i})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)
is trivializable in this way.
We get: the
\mu
-constant stratum of
V(\sum x^{p_{i}}_{i})
coincides with the Lipschitz-semi-algebraic stratum.

Recall that the ambient-
C^{0}
-triviality of a family of
\mathbb{C}
-analytic hypersurface germs with isolated singularities implies the right-
C^{0}
-triviality,
[
King.80
]
. The ambient-Lipschitz-triviality coincides with the contact-Lipschitz-triviality for complex plane curve germs, i.e. functions
(\mathbb{C}^{2},o)\to(\mathbb{C}^{1},o),

[
B.F.G.15
]
. But it is much weaker for functions
(\mathbb{R}^{2},o)\to(\mathbb{R}^{1},o),

[
B.F.G.G.17
]
. Hence corollary
3.7
cannot be deduced from the known bounds on the Lipschitz-contact equivalence,
[
Costa-Saia-Soares.12
]
, or Lipschitz-right equivalence,
[
Fernandes-Ruas.04
]
.
In dimension
n\geq 2
this corollary seems to be the first result on ambient-Lipschitz triviality.

1.2.2.
The fast cycles/non-IMC criteria for perturbations of weighted-homogeneous germs

Theorem
4.2
.

Take
X:=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{N},o)
as before, reduced, of
dim(X)=n.

​
1.​

(The surface case,
n=2
.) Suppose for each
0<t_{o}\ll 1
the section
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
contains a smooth irreducible non-contractible component. If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2},
then
X
has a fast loop. (And hence is non-IMC.)

​
2.​

(Arbitrary dimension,
n\geq 2
.)
Suppose
X\cap V(x_{1})
is a reduced complete intersection of dimension
(n-1).

Suppose for all
0<t_{o}\ll 1
the section
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is the (smooth) Milnor fibre of
X\cap V(x_{1}).

Suppose one of the following:

•

either
X=X_{o}
(i.e. the perturbation is trivial);

•

or

dim[X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})]<\frac{n-1}{2}
for
0<t_{o}\ll 1.

Denote
l:=n-dimSing[X\cap V(x_{1})].

If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{l},
then
X
has a fast cycle of dimension
\geq l-1,
whose homotopy type is that of the Milnor fibre of
X\cap V(x_{1}).

This applies easily to (semi)weighted-homogeneous ICIS and to Newton-non-degenerate germs.

Corollary
4.4
.

Suppose the germs
X_{o},X,X\cap V(x_{1})
are ICIS, take the Milnor number
\mu:=\mu(X\cap V(x_{1})).

If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{r}
(for some
r\leq n
), then
X
has a fast cycle of homotopy type

\vee^{\mu}S^{n-1},
whose tangent cone is of dimension
\leq r-1.

In particular, if
X
is IMC then
\omega_{1}=\cdots=\omega_{n}.

This gives a simple way to cook-up exotic Lipschitz structures, see example
4.6
.

Corollary
4.7
.
Let the surface germ
X=V(f)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},o)
be Newton-non-degenerate and IMC. Then for each face of the Newton diagram of
f
the two lowest weights coincide.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations and conventions

Let
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}.
Take the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}:=\{x|\ \|x\|=1\}\subset\mathbbm{k}^{N}.
It is
\mathbb{S}^{N-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
\mathbb{S}^{2N-1}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}.

i.

We use multi-indices,
{\underline{x}}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\mathbbm{k}^{N},

{\underline{s}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})\subset\mathbb{S},

{\underline{\omega}}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{N}),

{\underline{p}}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{r}),

{\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}=(f_{p_{1}},\dots,f_{p_{r}}).

Accordingly, in §
3
, we abbreviate:

(2)

t^{{\underline{\omega}}}\cdot{\underline{s}}:=(t^{\omega_{1}}s_{1},\dots,t^{\omega_{N}}s_{N}),\quad t^{{\underline{p}}}\cdot{\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}:=(t^{p_{1}}f_{p_{1}},\dots,t^{p_{r}}f_{p_{r}}),t^{-{\underline{p}}}\cdot{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}:=(t^{-p_{1}}f_{>p_{1}},\dots,t^{-p_{r}}f_{>p_{r}}).

ii.

Through the paper by germs we mean their small representatives.

All our germs are subanalytic and closed. (And their links as well.)

iii.

All our arcs are (real) subanalytic. Usually we take their length-parametrization,
\gamma(t)=(s_{1}\cdot t+o(t),\dots,s_{N}\cdot t+o(t)),
with
(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})\neq(0,\dots,0).

By a foliation we always mean a singular foliation of a germ
X

by real arcs, all passing through the origin.

iv.

The (inner) tangency order of two arcs
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\subset X
is
tord_{X}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):=ord_{t}d_{X}(\gamma_{1}(t),\gamma_{2}(t)).

Here
d_{X}:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a subanalytic distance equivalent to the distance given by the infimum of lengths of paths connecting points of
X

[
Kurdyka-Orro
]
. We denote this number by
tord_{X}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
.

If
X=\mathbb{R}^{N}
then
d_{X}=\|\dots\|
(the usual Euclidean distance) and we write just
tord(,).

The tangency order of an arc and a subgerm,
\gamma,Y\!\subset\!X,
is
tord_{X}(\gamma,Y\!)\!:=\!\sup\limits_{\!\gamma_{Y}\subset Y}tord_{X}(\gamma,\gamma_{Y})\!\leq\!\infty.
The “order of difference” of
two (subanalytic) subgerms,
Y_{1},Y_{2}\!\subset\!X,
is

diff.ord_{X}(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=inf\{tord_{X}(\gamma_{1},Y_{2});\gamma_{1}\subset Y_{1}\}=inf\{tord_{X}(\gamma_{2},Y_{1});\gamma_{2}\subset Y_{2}\}\leq\infty.

Thus
diff.ord_{X}(Y_{1},Y_{2})=\infty
iff
Y_{1}=Y_{2}.

v.

Let
(X,o)
be a
\mathbbm{k}
-analytic germ. The tangent cone
T_{(X,o)}
can be defined set-theoretically, e.g. as the Whitney cone
C_{3}
,

[
Whitney
, Chapter 7]
,
[
Chirka
, Chapter 2]
. The definition of
T_{(X,o)}
is the same for subanalytic germs.
The tangent cone is subanalytic as well, with
dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{(X,o)}\leq dim_{\mathbb{R}}{(X,o)}.
In particular, if
dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{(X,o)}=1,
then
T_{(X,o)}
is a finite union of lines and half-lines.

vi.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a sub-analytic set.
Every homology class in
H_{*}(X,\mathbb{Z})
admits a subanalytic representative. Indeed, the singular homology is isomorphic to the simplicial homology.
And sub-analytic manifolds admit subanalytic triangulations,
[
Coste
, §4.3]
.

vii.

The singular locus of a (real or complex) complete intersection
X=V(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)
is defined (inside
X
) by the determinantal ideal
I_{r}[f^{\prime}_{1},\dots,f^{\prime}_{r}].
Here
\{f^{\prime}_{j}\}
are the column gradients.

We take
dim_{\mathbbm{k}}Sing(X)
set-theoretically. For
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
this dimension can be computed also via the height of the defining ideal.

2.2.
Fast cycles

A fast cycle is (roughly speaking) a thin germ that does not admit a hornic deformation-retraction onto a non-thin germ.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix a (subanalytic) subgerm
Y\subset X.

​
1.​

Y
is called thin if
dim_{\mathbb{R}}Y>dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{(Y,o)}.

​
2.​

A subanalytic neighborhood
Y\!\subseteq\!\mathcal{U}(Y)\!\subseteq\!X
is called
hornic
if
tord_{\mathcal{U}(Y)}(\gamma,Y)\!\!>\!\!1

for any arc
\gamma\!\subset\!\mathcal{U}(Y).

​
3.​

Y
is called
linkwise retractible
to a subgerm
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
if there exists a deformation-retraction
Y\rightsquigarrow Y^{\prime}
that retracts the links, i.e.
Link_{t}[Y]\rightsquigarrow Link_{t}[Y^{\prime}]
for all
0<t\ll 1.

​
4.​

A thin subgerm
Y\subset X
is called a
fast cycle
if
Y
does not admit
a hornic neighborhood,
Y\subseteq\mathcal{U}(Y)\subset X,
that linkwise retracts to a subgerm
Y^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{U}(Y)
with
dim_{\mathbb{R}}(Y^{\prime})=dim_{\mathbb{R}}(T_{Y^{\prime}})\leq dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{Y}.

​
5.​

Let
Y\subset X
be a fast cycle. Suppose
Y
admits a hornic linkwise-retraction to
Y^{\prime}\subset X,
with
dim_{\mathbb{R}}Y^{\prime}=1+l,
and this
l
is the minimal possible.
Then
Y
is called
a fast cycle of dimension
l
.

A fast cycle whose link is
S^{1}
is called
a fast loop
.

​
6.​

The exponent of a fast cycle is
diff.ord_{X}(Y,T_{Y}),
see §
2.1
.

A remark: these properties depend on the (subanalytic) inner-Lipschitz type of
X
only, not on a particular embedding
X\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{R}^{N},o).

Example 2.2
.

i.

If a (subanalytic) neighborhood,
Y\subset\mathcal{U}(Y),
is hornic, then
T_{(Y,o)}=T_{(\mathcal{U}(Y),o)}.
In particular, if
dim_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{U}(Y)>dim_{\mathbb{R}}Y,
then
\mathcal{U}(Y)
is a thin set.

ii.

A simple fast loop is the horn
Y=\{x^{2}+y^{2}=z^{2\beta},\ z\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3},
with
\beta>1,
considered inside
X=Ball_{1}(o)\setminus\{(0,0,t)|\ t\geq 0\}.
Note that
Link[Y]
is contractible inside
Link[X],
but not hornically.

A simple fast cycle with
dim_{\mathbb{R}}T_{(Y,o)}>1
is the cylinder over the horn,
Y=\{x^{2}_{1}+x^{2}_{2}=x^{2\beta}_{3},\ x_{3}\geq 0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

considered inside the cylinder
X=\mathbb{R}^{n-3}\times[Ball_{1}(o)\setminus\{x_{1}=0=x_{2},\ x_{3}\geq 0\}].

iii.

Suppose
(X,o)
is a hornic neighborhood of its arc
\gamma,
and moreover,
(X,o)
linkwise retracts to
\gamma.
Then
X
has no fast cycles.

For non-hornic neighborhoods
(X,o)
can linkwise retract to
\gamma
and yet have fast cycles. See example ii.

iv.

If
\pi_{1}(Link[X])\neq\{1\}
then one can expect fast loops on
X.
However, fast loops/cycles exist also for exotic spheres,
Link[X]\cong S^{2n-1},
see Example
4.6
.

Lemma 2.3
.

An IMC-germ (subanalytic, with closed link) has no fast cycles.

Proof.
Embed this germ as a cone,
X=Cone[Link[X]]\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}.
Take the radial flow,
\Phi^{X}_{\tau}:X\to X,
with
\tau\in(0,\infty).
Here
\Phi^{X}_{\tau}
preserves the rays through the origin, and sends links to links,
\Phi^{X}_{\tau}Link_{t}[X]=Link_{\tau\cdot t}[X].

Suppose
Y\subset X
is a fast cycle. Take a flow contracting
Y
to the origin and compatible with link-structure. Namely: a family of continuous maps
\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}:Y\to Y,

\tau\in[0,1],
satisfying:

(3)

\Phi^{Y}_{1}=Id_{Y},\quad\Phi^{Y}_{0}(Y)=o,\quad\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}[Link_{t}Y]=Link_{\tau\cdot t}[Y]\text{ and }\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}\text{ is a homeomorphism for }\tau>0.

Finally, define the homotopy of
Y
inside
X
by
\Phi^{X}_{\frac{1}{\tau}}\circ\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}Y,

\tau\in(0,1].

This is a family of continuous maps. It acts linkwise,
Link_{t}[Y]
is sent into
Link_{t}[X].
This homotopy preserves
T_{Y},
i.e.

T_{\Phi^{X}_{\frac{1}{\tau}}\circ\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}Y}=T_{Y}.

And the limit is a cone,
\lim_{\tau\to 0}(\Phi^{X}_{\frac{1}{\tau}}\circ\Phi^{Y}_{\tau}Y)=T_{Y}.

We have constructed the linkwise retraction
Y\rightsquigarrow T_{Y}.
We claim: this retraction occurs inside a hornic neighborhood of
Y.

It is enough to show: the retraction occurs inside a hornic neighborhood of
T_{Y}.

We pass to the polar coordinates and take the strict transforms.
The retraction
Y\rightsquigarrow T_{Y}
lifts to
{\tilde{Y}}\rightsquigarrow\tilde{T}_{Y}
and its goes inside a neighborhood
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}({\tilde{Y}})\subset S^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
that satisfies
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}({\tilde{Y}})\cap E={\tilde{Y}}\cap E.
Its projection,
\mathcal{U}(Y)\subset(\mathbb{R}^{N},o),
is a hornic neighborhood.

\begin{matrix}S^{N-1}\times\{o\}=:E&\subset&S^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}&\to&\mathbb{R}^{N}\\
\hskip 65.44142pt\cup&&\cup&&\cup\\
\hskip 54.06006pt{\tilde{X}}\cap E&\subset&{\tilde{X}}&\to&X\\
\hskip 56.9055pt\cup&&\cup&&\cup\\
{\tilde{Y}}\cap E=\tilde{T}_{Y}\cap E&\subset&{\tilde{Y}},\tilde{T}_{Y}&\to&Y,T_{Y}\end{matrix}

\blacksquare

3.
Deformations of weighted-homogeneous complete intersections and their foliations

3.1.

Let
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}.

Take a weighted-homogeneous complete intersection
X_{o}:=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o),
where
{\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}=(f_{p_{1}},\dots,f_{p_{r}})

and
f_{p_{i}}(t^{\omega_{1}}x_{1},\dots,t^{\omega_{N}}x_{N})=t^{p_{i}}\cdot f_{p_{i}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N}).
W.l.o.g. we assume
({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subseteq({\underline{x}})^{2}\subset\mathbbm{k}[{\underline{x}}].

Here
X_{o}
can have non-isolated singularity and can be non-reduced.

Take the arcs

\gamma_{\underline{s}}(t)=t^{\underline{\omega}}\cdot{\underline{s}}:=(t^{\omega_{1}}s_{1},\dots,t^{\omega_{N}}s_{N})
with
{\underline{s}}\in\mathbb{S},
see §
2.1
.
They form the weighted-homogeneous foliation of
\mathbbm{k}^{N}.
It is compatible with
X_{o}.

Take a higher order deformation
X_{\epsilon}:=V({\underline{f}}_{{\underline{p}}}+\epsilon\cdot{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}})\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o),
for
\epsilon\in Disc,
i.e.
|\epsilon|\leq 1.

Here
ord_{\omega}(f_{p_{i}})<ord_{\omega}(f_{>p_{i}})
and this deformation is flat.
Note, this family is not equisingular in whichever sense, as we did not assume that
X_{o}
has an isolated singularity.

One would like to deform the foliation
\{\gamma_{\underline{s}}\}
to a foliation
\{\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}\}
of
(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o)
(by terms of higher weights),
compatibly with
X_{\epsilon}.
The minimal condition to impose is:

(4)

tord_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(\gamma_{{\underline{s}}},\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}})>1\text{ for each }{\underline{s}}\in Link[X_{o}]=\mathbb{S}\cap X_{o}\text{ and }\epsilon\in Disc.

This holds, e.g. if
X_{o}
is an isolated singularity and the family
\{X_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
is equi-resolvable.
It is not enough to impose just the
\mu=const
condition on family
\{X_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon},
due to examples of Briançon-Speder type.

Example 3.1
.

Let
f_{\epsilon}(x,y,z)=x^{p_{x}}+z^{p_{z}}+xy^{p_{y}}+\epsilon\cdot y^{p_{y}+1},
with the weights
(\frac{1}{p_{x}},\frac{1-\frac{1}{p_{x}}}{p_{y}},\frac{1}{p_{z}}).

Thus
V(f_{o})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},o)
has a weighted-homogeneous isolated singularity.
The perturbation
\epsilon\cdot y^{p_{y}+1}
is of weight
>1
iff
\frac{p_{y}+1}{p_{y}}(1-\frac{1}{p_{x}})>1,
i.e.
p_{x}>p_{y}+1.

In this case
f_{\epsilon}
is a
\mu=const
family. Take the arc
\gamma_{o}(t)=(0,t,0)\subset V(f_{o}).

Its deformation by higher order terms (to ensure
tord(\gamma_{o},\gamma_{\epsilon})>1
) can be presented as
\gamma_{\epsilon}(t)=(t^{d_{x}}(\dots),t,t^{d_{z}}(\dots)),
for some
d_{x},d_{z}>1.
The condition
\gamma_{\epsilon}\subset V(f_{\epsilon})
is then:

t^{p_{x}d_{x}}(\dots)+t^{p_{z}d_{z}}(\dots)+t^{d_{x}+p_{y}}(\dots)+\epsilon t^{p_{y}+1}.

Assuming
p_{z}>p_{y}+1
we have:
p_{x}d_{x},p_{z}d_{z},d_{x}+p_{y}>p_{y}+1.
And this contradicts the condition
f_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{\epsilon}(t))=0.

In this example “the problematic arc” lies inside the section
X_{o}\cap V(x,z).
In fact, the intersection
X_{o}\cap V(x,z)
is not of expected dimension.
We show that such intersections of unexpected dimension or with unexpected singularities are the only obstruction to the deformation of the foliation
\gamma_{\underline{s}}
to
\gamma_{\epsilon,{\underline{s}}}.

3.2.

4.
Detecting fast cycles for germs with deformed weighted-homogeneous foliation

Let
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}.
Take a (reduced) subanalytic set with the group action
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\circlearrowright X\subseteq\mathbbm{k}^{N},

defined by

(20)

t\circ(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=(t^{\omega_{1}}x_{1}+t^{\omega_{1}}g_{1}({\underline{x}},t),\dots,t^{\omega_{N}}x_{N}+t^{\omega_{N}}g_{N}({\underline{x}},t)),\hskip 28.45274pt0<\omega_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\omega_{N}.

Here the higher-order-terms
\{g_{i}({\underline{x}},t)\}
are continuous in
{\underline{x}},t
and vanish at
t=0
and at
{\underline{x}}=o
.
(Thus
\lim_{t\to 0}{\underline{g}}({\underline{x}},t)=o=\lim_{{\underline{x}}\to o}{\underline{g}}({\underline{x}},t)
.) Take the
t
-order,
ord_{t}g_{i}({\underline{x}},t)=sup\{c|\ g_{i}({\underline{x}},t)=O(t^{c})\}.

Many singular germs, or at least their (open dense) subsets, admit such an
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action.
Equivalently, they have foliations by “deformed weighted-homogeneous” arcs. See §
3
.

For some germs the weights are non-unique, and one can choose e.g.
\omega_{1}=\omega_{2}
or
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}.

Thus we make some choice of the weights. See also remark
4.3
.

Split the weights
\omega_{\bullet}
as in (
5
):

\omega_{1}=\cdots=\omega_{r_{1}}<\omega_{r_{1}+1}=\cdots=\omega_{r_{2}}<\cdots<\omega_{r_{k}+1}=\cdots=\omega_{N}.

4.1.
The general criterion for fast cycles

Take a subanalytic germ
(X,o)\subset(\mathbbm{k}^{N},o).

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose its subanalytic subgerm
Y\subseteq X
(not necessarily open or dense) admits the
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action,
(
20
).
Suppose (for some
r_{1}<l
) the image of the homology
H_{l}(Y\cap V(x_{1}-t),\mathbb{Z})
in
H_{l}(X\cap V(x_{1}-t),\mathbb{Z})
is non-zero for all
0<t\ll 1.
Then:

​
1.​

X
has a fast cycle of dimension
l,
whose tangent space lies in the plane
Span_{\mathbbm{k}}(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{r_{1}}).

​
2.​

In particular,
X
cannot be IMC.

​
3.​

The exponent of this fast cycle is at least
min\{1+\frac{ord_{t}g_{1}}{\omega_{1}},\dots,1+\frac{ord_{t}g_{r_{1}}}{\omega_{1}},\frac{\omega_{r_{1}+1}}{\omega_{1}}\}.

Proof.
Suppose
\omega_{1}<\omega_{1+l}.

Rescale the coordinates and
t
to assume: the image of
H_{l}(Y\cap V(x_{1}-t),\mathbb{Z})
in
H_{l}(X\cap V(x_{1}-t),\mathbb{Z})
is non-zero for each
0<t\leq 1.

Take the subset
Z\subset Y\cap V(x_{1}-1)
such that
0\neq[Z]\in H_{l}(X\cap V(x_{1}-1),\mathbb{Z}).
We can assume:
Z
is compact, connected, subanalytic (§
2.1
), of dimension
l.
Moreover,
Z
does not admit any deformation retraction (inside
X\cap V(x_{1}-1)
) to a cycle of smaller dimension.

The
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
-action then gives the sub-analytic germ
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}\subset Y\subset X,
of (real) dimension
l+1.

It is foliated by the arcs
\gamma_{z}
of (
20
).

Take the cylinder
Box:=\{|x_{1}|\leq 1\}\times\{\|x_{2},\dots,x_{N}\|\leq 1\}.

Realize
Link[X]
as the boundary of this cylinder,

(21)

Link[X]\cong\partial Box=[X\cap V(|x_{1}|-1)]\cup[X\cap V(\|x_{2},\dots,x_{N}\|-1)].

Thus
Link[\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z]=\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z\cap V(x_{1}-1.)
(Both for
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R}
and for
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
.)

We claim:
\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z
does not admit any hornic link-wise retraction (inside
X
) to a germ of smaller dimension.
Indeed,
Z
has no retraction inside
X\cap V(x_{1}-1).
It could be retractable inside
X\cap V(|x_{1}|-1)
(for
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
), but the corresponding retraction of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z
will not be hornic.

We claim:
T_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}}\subseteq Span(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{r_{1}}).

Namely: any arc on
\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}
is tangent to the plane
Span(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{r_{1}}).

•

This is obvious for the arcs of the foliation. Indeed, the points of
Z
have the coordinates
z=(1,z_{2},\dots,z_{N}),
thus the parametrization of the arc is

\gamma_{z}(t)=(t,t^{{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\omega_{2}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\omega_{1}$}\right.}}\cdot z_{2}(1+o(t)),\dots,t^{{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\omega_{N}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\omega_{1}$}\right.}}\cdot z_{N}(1+o(t))).

Here
\{z_{i}\}
are bounded. Therefore
\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{\gamma_{z}(t)}{t}=(1,*\dots,*,0,\dots,0)\in Span(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{r_{1}}).

•

It remains to prove: any other (subanalytic) arc
{\tilde{\gamma}}\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}
is tangent to an arc of the foliation.

For
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{R}
we pass to the “weighted-homogeneous polar coordinates”,

(22)

\mathbb{R}^{1}_{t\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{z_{2}\dots z_{N}}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\sigma}}$}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},\hskip 28.45274pt(t,z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\sigma}}$}\ (t,t^{\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}}z_{2},\dots,t^{\frac{\omega_{N}}{\omega_{1}}}z_{N}).

The map
\sigma
is Lipschitz, and its restriction to
\{t>0\}
is a homeomorphism onto its image.

Take the “strict transform”,
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}:=\overline{\sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z)}.

Its “exceptional locus” is
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}_{z_{2}\dots z_{N}}).
It consists of the base points of the lifted arcs.
It is bounded, because
Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
is bounded. Therefore it is compact.

Lift the subanalytic arc
\gamma\subset(\mathbb{R}^{N},o)
to
{\tilde{\gamma}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}.
The arc
{\tilde{\gamma}}
is still subanalytic, in particular

\lim_{t\to 0}{\tilde{\gamma}}(t)={\tilde{\gamma}}(o)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}\cap(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{N-1}).
Thus
{\tilde{\gamma}}(0)={\tilde{\gamma}}_{z}(0)
for some
z\in Z.

And therefore the arcs
\gamma,\gamma_{z}
are tangent.

For
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
the weighted-homogeneous polar coordinates are defined by:

(23)

\mathbb{R}^{1}_{t\geq 0}\times S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{N-1}_{z_{2}\dots z_{N}}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\sigma}}$}\ \mathbb{C}^{N},\hskip 28.45274pt(t,\phi,z_{2},\dots,z_{N})\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{\sigma}}$}\ (t\cdot e^{i\phi},t^{\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}}z_{2},\dots,t^{\frac{\omega_{N}}{\omega_{1}}}z_{N}).

Then one applies the same arguments.

Altogether,
T_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}}\subseteq Span(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{r_{1}}).
Thus

dimT_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}}\leq r_{1}\leq l<dim\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}.

Thus

\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}\subset X
is a fast cycle of
dim=l,
see §
2.2
.

Finally we bound the exponent of the fast cycle. Take an arc of the foliation,

(24)

\gamma_{z}(t)=\Big(t,t\cdot z_{2}(1+g_{2}(t,z)),\dots,t\cdot z_{r_{1}}(1+g_{r_{1}}(t,z)),\hskip 113.81102pt

\hskip 170.71652ptt^{{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\omega_{r_{1}+1}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\omega_{1}$}\right.}}\cdot z_{r_{1}+1}(1+g_{r_{1}+1}(t,z)),\dots,t^{{\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$\omega_{N}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$\omega_{1}$}\right.}}\cdot z_{N}(1+g_{N}(t,z))\Big).

Then
T_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{>0}Z}}
contains the arc
{\tilde{\gamma}}_{z}=(t,t\cdot z_{2},\dots,t\cdot z_{r_{1}},0,\dots,0).
And

(25)

tord_{\mathbbm{k}^{N}}(\gamma_{z},{\tilde{\gamma}}_{z})\geq min\{1+\frac{ord_{t}g_{1}}{\omega_{1}},\dots,1+\frac{ord_{t}g_{r_{1}}}{\omega_{1}},\frac{\omega_{r_{1}+1}}{\omega_{1}}\}.\hskip 113.81102pt{\hfill\blacksquare}\\

4.2.
Fast cycles for perturbations of complex weighted-homogeneous complete intersections

Let
(X_{o},o):=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c},o)
be a (complex-analytic) weighted-homogeneous complete intersection of dimension
n\geq 2
, with
arbitrary singularity, possibly non-reduced.
We assume:
\omega_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\omega_{n+c}
and
({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subseteq({\underline{x}})^{2}.

Take a perturbation by terms of (strictly) higher orders,
(X,o):=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c},o).
We assume:
(X,o)
is reduced.
Take small representatives
X_{o},X.

Theorem 4.2
.

​
1.​

(The surface case,
n=2
.) Suppose for each
0<t_{o}\ll 1
the section
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
contains a smooth irreducible non-contractible component. If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}
then
X
has a fast loop.

​
2.​

(Arbitrary dimension,
n\geq 2
.)
Suppose
X\cap V(x_{1})
is a reduced complete intersection of dimension
(n-1).

Suppose for
0<t_{o}\ll 1
the section
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is a (smooth) Milnor fibre of
X\cap V(x_{1}).
Suppose one of the following:

•

either
X=X_{o},
i.e. the perturbation is trivial;

•

or
dim_{\mathbb{C}}[X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})]<\frac{n-1}{2}
for
0<t_{o}\ll 1.

Denote
l:=n-dim_{\mathbb{C}}Sing[X\cap V(x_{1})].
(Thus
2\leq l\leq n
.)
If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{l}
then
X
has a fast cycle of (real) dimension
\geq l-1,
whose homotopy type is that of the Milnor fibre of
X\cap V(x_{1}).

Therefore: if
X
is IMC then
\omega_{1}=\cdots=\omega_{l}
.

Proof.

1.

The assumption implies a loop
Z,
non-contractible inside an irreducible component of
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o}).
By a small deformation of
Z
(inside a smooth
\mathbb{C}
-analytic curve) we can assume:
Z
does not intersect the subset
X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o}).
(Indeed, as
n=2
and
X
is reduced, this is a finite set of points.) Note that all the other components of
\Sigma
do not intersect
V(x_{1}-t_{o}).

Therefore, pushing
Z
faraway from
X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o}),
we can assume:
Z
lies inside the part of
X
that is foliated in lemma
3.3
.

The statement follows now by lemma
4.1
.

2.

We prove:
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is connected and contains a non-contractible cycle in the foliated part.

i.

By our assumption
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is the (smooth) Milnor fibre for the germ
(\mathbb{C}^{n+c},o)\supset X\cap V(x_{1}).

Therefore
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is
(l-2)
-connected. (See
[
Kato-Matsumoto.75
]
for hypersurface germs and
[
Greuel.77
]
for complete intersections.) In particular,
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is connected.

By the theorem of Andreoti-Frankel the set
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is homotopically equivalent to a set of real dimension
\leq(n-1).

Therefore the only non-vanishing (reduced) homologies of
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
can be in the (real) dimension-range
[l-1,n-1].

ii.

The Milnor fibre
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
of the (reduced, non-smooth, complete intersection)
X\cap V(x_{1})
is non-contractible. This is immediate if
X\cap V(x_{1})
is an ICIS.

In the general case one uses the standard monodromy argument.
Observe the tautology:

(26)

(\mathbb{C}^{n+c},o)\supset X\cap V(x_{1})=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}},x_{1})=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}|_{x_{1}=0})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c-1}_{x_{2}\dots x_{n+c}},o),

Thus
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is diffeomorphic to the Milnor fibre of the germ
V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}|_{x_{1}=0})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c-1}_{x_{2}\dots x_{n+c}},o).

Here
V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}|_{x_{1}=0})
is a (reduced) complete intersection of dimension
n-1.
We realize its Milnor fibre in yet another way.

Take the germ
X^{\prime}=V(f_{p_{2}}+f_{>p_{2}}|_{x_{1}=0},\dots,f_{p_{c}}+f_{>p_{c}}|_{x_{1}=0})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c-1},o).

We can assume:
X^{\prime}
is reduced. (If needed, take generic linear combinations of
{\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}
.)
Consider the map

\phi:X^{\prime}\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\to}\limits^{f_{p_{1}}+f_{>p_{1}}}}$}\ (\mathbb{C}^{1},o).
Then
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is diffeomorphic to the Milnor fibre of the hypersurface singularity

V(f_{p_{1}}+f_{>p_{1}})\subset X^{\prime}
inside an analytic space. For such a hypersurface singularity the total trace of monodromy (on all the vanishing homologies of the Milnor fibre) vanishes, by
[
A’Campo.73
]
.
As the Milnor fibre is connected,
H_{0}(\dots)=\mathbb{C}.
And thus the monodromy on
H_{0}
is identity. Therefore, vanishing of the
total trace implies: at least one other homology is non-zero.

iii.

Altogether, we have a (compact, connected) cycle
Z\subset X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
of dimension
l-1\leq dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z\leq n-1,
that represents a non-zero class in
H_{dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z}(X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o}),\mathbb{Z}).

We can take
Z
of the smallest possible dimension, and assume it is subanalytic, §
2.1
.
Finally, we are in one of the assumed cases.

•

If
X
is weighted-homogeneous, then
Z
lies inside the foliated part of
X.

•

Otherwise use lemma
3.3
to deform the weighted-homogeneous foliation of
X_{o}
to a foliation of
(X,o)\setminus\mathcal{U}[Sing(X_{o})\cup V(x_{1})].
(Here
\mathcal{U}[Sing(X_{o})\cup V(x_{1})]
is a hornic neighborhood.)
As
dim_{\mathbb{R}}Z\leq n-1
and
dim_{\mathbb{C}}[X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})]<\frac{n-1}{2}
, a small deformation (inside the manifold
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
) will move
Z
off the locus

X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o}).
And then we can push
Z
away from this locus to assume:
Z
lies inside the foliated part of
X,

for the foliation of lemma
3.3
.

And now invoke Lemma
4.1
for the cycle
Z
.

\blacksquare

Remark 4.3
.

The assumption “
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is the Milnor fibre” is important.Take the cone
X=V(x_{1}x_{4}-x_{2}x_{3})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{4},o).
It has an isolated singularity and no fast cycles. We can choose weights satisfying
\omega_{1}<\omega_{2}<\omega_{3}<\omega_{4}.
Here
X\cap V(x_{1})=V(x_{1},x_{2}\cdot x_{3})
is reduced, and
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is smooth. But
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is not the Milnor fibre of the germ
X\cap V(x_{1}).
And the conclusion of Theorem
4.2
does not hold.

4.2.1.
An example: semi-weighted-homogeneous ICIS

Let
X_{o}=V({\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+c},o)
be a weighted-homogeneous ICIS of

Take its perturbation by higher order terms,

is of dimension
(n-1),

\mu:=\mu(X\cap V(x_{1})).

Corollary 4.4
.

​
1.​

If
\omega_{1}<\omega_{r}
(for some
r\leq n
), then
X
has a fast cycle of homotopy type

\vee^{\mu}S^{n-1},
whose tangent cone is of dimension
\leq r-1.

​
2.​

If
X
is IMC then
\omega_{1}=\cdots=\omega_{n}.

This follows straight from theorem
4.2
. The sections

are smooth (by Sard’s theorem) and therefore are Milnor fibres of the ICIS

Example 4.5
.

​
i.​

]

​
ii.​

Recall the Briançon-Speder example, of Remark

. In that case
V(f_{\epsilon})\cap V(x)=V(z^{5}+\epsilon\cdot zy^{6},x)

V(f_{\epsilon})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{3},o)
has a fast loop for

​
iii.​

(Semi-Brieskorn-Pham singularities, the hypersurface case,
c=1
,
n\geq 2.
) Take

where
p_{1}\geq\cdots\geq p_{n+1}.

The hypersurface
V(f_{p})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},o)
has an isolated singularity.
Let
f_{>p}(x)
be of order
\geq 1
with respect to the weights
\frac{1}{p_{1}},\dots,\frac{1}{p_{n+1}}.

If

​
iv.​

(Semi-Brieskorn-Pham singularities, the complete intersection case,

.) Take

Here
\frac{r_{j}}{\omega_{i}}\in\mathbb{N}
for all

{\underline{f}}_{\underline{p}}+{\underline{f}}_{>{\underline{p}}}.
The assumptions of the corollary are implied by the genericity of

Example 4.6
.

Take an isolated hypersurface singularity
V(f)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},o),
for

-odd. In some cases one gets exotic spheres,

And then

is (non-embedded) homeomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{n},o).
However, for
\omega_{1}<\omega_{n}
one gets a fast cycle of
dim_{\mathbb{R}}=n-1.
(Note that
H_{\bullet}(S^{2n-1},\mathbb{Z})=0
for
\bullet\neq 0,2n-1
.) Hence, in these cases
V(f)
is not inner-Lipschitz homeomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{n},o).

Similarly, in many cases
Link[V(f)]\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\approx}\limits^{C^{0}}}$}\ {\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$S^{2n-1}$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$G$}\right.},
for a finite group
G.
Thus
V(f)\ \lower 0.86108pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\approx}\limits^{C^{0}}}$}\ {\footnotesize\left.\raisebox{1.37775pt}{$(\mathbb{C}^{n},o)$}\!/\!\raisebox{-1.37775pt}{$G$}\right.},
but this homeomorphism cannot be chosen biLipschitz.

4.2.2.
An example: Newton-non-degenerate hypersurface germs

Take a Newton-non-degenerate germ
X=V(f)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n+1},o),
assume
f
is convenient. Below we consider only top-dimensional faces of the Newton diagram,
\sigma\subset\Gamma_{f}.

We assume:
\Gamma_{f}
has at least two faces.
For each
\sigma\subset\Gamma_{f}
one gets the weights
\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n+1}.
These are the coordinates of the normal to
\sigma.

Restrict the function to the face,
f_{\sigma},
one gets the hypersurface
V(f_{\sigma})\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.
Usually it has non-isolated singularities, often it is non-reduced.

Corollary 4.7
.

Suppose
X
is IMC and
dim_{\mathbb{C}}SingV(f_{\sigma})<\frac{n+3}{2}.
Then the lower
n
weights of the face
\sigma
coincide.

Proof.
Permuting the variables we can assume
\omega_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\omega_{n+1}.

Consider the germ
X=V(f_{\sigma}+f_{>\sigma})
as a perturbation of
V(f_{\sigma}).

We want to apply theorem
4.2
.

•

As
f
is Newton-non-degenerate (and
\sigma
has more than one face), the germ
X\cap V(x_{1})
is of dimension (
n-1
), with an isolated singularity. Thus
s=n-1.

•

The section
X\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})
is smooth (by Sard’s theorem). Therefore it is the Milnor fibre of the hypersurface germ
V(f|_{x_{1}=0})\subset(\mathbb{C}^{n},o).

•

Finally,
dim_{\mathbb{C}}[X\cap Sing(X_{o})\cap V(x_{1}-t_{o})]\leq dim_{\mathbb{C}}Sing(X_{o})-2<\frac{n-1}{2}.

Therefore
\omega_{1}=\cdots=\omega_{n}.

\blacksquare

Example 4.8
.

i.

(Newton-non-degenerate surface germs,
n=2
) The condition
dim\ SingV(f_{\sigma})<\frac{n+3}{2}
is satisfied for each face.
Thus the IMC condition implies: for any face
\sigma\subset\Gamma
the two lowest weights coincide.

A remark: this still allows lots of possible Newton diagrams.

ii.

(Newton-non-degenerate three-fold germs,
n=3
) The condition
dim\ SingV(f_{\sigma})<\frac{n+3}{2}
then means:
V(f_{\sigma})
is reduced.
Equivalently: the distance of
\sigma
from each coordinate plane is at most one.
If this is satisfied for
\sigma,
and
V(f)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{4},o)
is IMC, then the three lowest weights of
\sigma
must coincide.

Observation of a new excited beauty strange baryon decaying to \PGXmb ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm \PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm


Abstract
The \PGXmb ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm \PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm invariant mass spectrum is investigated with an event sample of proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV , collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC in 2016–2018 and corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 \fbinv . The ground state \PGXmb is reconstructed via its decays to \PJGy ​ \PGXm \PJGy\PGXm and \PJGy ​ \PGL ​ \PKm \PJGy\PGL\PKm . A narrow resonance, labeled \PGXb ​ ( 6100 ) − \PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-} , is observed at a \PGXmb ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm \PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm invariant mass of 6100.3 ± 0.2 ​ \stat ± 0.1 ​ \syst ± 0.6 ​ ( \PGXmb ) ​ \MeV 6100.3\pm 0.2\stat\pm 0.1\syst\pm 0.6\,(\PGXmb)\MeV , where the last uncertainty reflects the precision of the \PGXmb baryon mass. The upper limit on the \PGXb ​ ( 6100 ) − \PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-} natural width is determined to be 1.9 \MeV at 95% confidence level. The low \PGXb ​ ( 6100 ) − \PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-} signal yield observed in data does not allow a measurement of the quantum numbers of the new state. However, following analogies with the established excited \PGXc baryon states, the new \PGXb ​ ( 6100 ) − \PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-} resonance and its decay sequence are consistent with the orbitally excited \PGXmb baryon, with spin and parity quantum numbers J P = 3 / 2 − J^{P}=3/2^{-} .

The

J^{P}=1/2^{+}
(the
\PGXprb

and one with
j_{\PQq\PQs}=1
and
J^{P}=3/2^{+}
(the
\PGXstb

of excited
\PGXb
baryons

[

The fourth state,
\PGXprzb

\PGXmb\PGpp

\PGXmb

This Letter presents a search for
\PGXmb
excited states in the
\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
invariant mass spectrum,
performed using proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp

\PGXmb

\PJGy\PGXm

\PJGy\PGL\PKm

\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm

\PGXm\to\PGL\PGpm

\PGSz\to\PGL\PGg

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m
internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T. Within the solenoid
volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic
calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter, each composed of
a barrel and two endcap sections.
Muons are detected in gas-ionization chambers embedded
in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
A more detailed description
of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and
the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Ref.
[
25
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system.
The first level (L1), composed of custom hardware processors,
uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors
[
26
]
.
The second level, known as the high-level trigger (HLT), consists of a farm of processors
running a version of the full event reconstruction software
optimized for fast processing
[
27
]
.
The events used in the analysis were selected at L1 by requiring
the presence of at least two muons, and at HLT by requiring
that the two muons have opposite sign (OS),
with various thresholds on the pseudorapidity
\eta
and momentum transverse to the beam axis
\pt
,
compatible with being produced in the dimuon decay of
\PJGy
mesons.

Several simulated event samples are used in the analysis.
The
\PYTHIA
8.230 package
[
28
]
is used to simulate the production of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
state,
where the
\PGSmb
baryon, with a modified mass value, is used as a proxy for an excited
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
state.
The
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}\to\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
(including both resonant
\PGXstzb\PGpp\to\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
and non-resonant
\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
modes),

\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
,
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
(including
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGSz\PKm
,
\PGSz\to\PGL\PGg
), and
\PJGy\to\PGmp\PGmm
decays
are modeled with
\EVTGEN
1.6.0
[
29
]
, where final-state photon
radiation is included using
\PHOTOS
3.61
[
30
,
31
]
.
The generated events are then passed to a detailed
\GEANTfour
-based
simulation
[
32
]
of the CMS detector, including the
same trigger and reconstruction algorithms as used for the collision data.
The simulation includes effects from multiple
\Pp\Pp
interactions in the same
or nearby bunch crossings (pileup) with a multiplicity distribution
matching the measured one.

The selection criteria are optimized using the Punzi figure of merit
[
33
]
,
which does not rely on the signal normalization.
The expected background is estimated from data using the same-sign (SS) control region
described below, while the signal efficiency is obtained from the simulated
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}\to\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
events.
The
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
and
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
requirements are optimized separately.

Events are required to have two OS muons passing the CMS soft-muon selection criteria
[
34
]

and satisfying
\pt(\PGmpm)>3\GeV
and
\abs{\eta(\PGmpm)}<2.4
.
The muons must form a common vertex with
\chi^{2}
probability
P_{\text{vtx}}
above 1%.
The dimuon invariant mass must be
within 100
\MeV
of
m_{\PJGy}^{\mathrm{PDG}}

(hereafter,
m_{X}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
denotes the world-average mass of hadron
X

[
20
]
),
corresponding to about three times the mass resolution.
The
\PGL
candidates are formed from displaced two-prong vertices, assuming the decay
\PGL\to\Pp\PGpm
,
as described in Ref.
[
35
]
.
The
\Pp\PGpm
reconstructed mass is required to be
within 10
\MeV
of
m_{\PGL}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
,
corresponding to about three times the mass resolution.
The two tracks are then refitted with
their invariant mass constrained to
m_{\PGL}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
.
The obtained
\PGL
candidates are required to have
\pt>1\GeV
and
P_{\text{vtx}}>1\%
.

For the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
channel, the
\PGXm\to\PGL\PGpm
candidates are obtained by combining charged particles
of
\pt>0.25\GeV
with the selected
\PGL
candidates.
The reconstructed
\PGXm
must have
P_{\text{vtx}}>1\%
,
\pt>3\GeV
,
and invariant mass within 9.5
\MeV
of
m_{\PGXm}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
,
corresponding to about three times the mass resolution.
The
\PGXmb
candidates are obtained by performing a
\PGmp\PGmm\PGXm
kinematic vertex fit,
constraining the dimuon invariant mass
to
m_{\PJGy}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
.

For the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
decay channel, the
\PGL
candidates must pass stricter requirements:

\pt>2\GeV
and
\abs{M(\Pp\PGpm)-m^{\mathrm{PDG}}_{\PGL}}<9\MeV
.
The charged kaon candidates are particle tracks with kaon mass assignment
satisfying high purity tracking requirements
[
36
]
and
\pt>1.2\GeV
.
The
\PGXmb
candidates are reconstructed by fitting the
\PGmp\PGmm\PGL\PKm

vertex with the
\PJGy
mass constraint.
Since the photon from the
\PGSz\to\PGL\PGg
decay is not detected,
both
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
and
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGSz\PKm

to the
\PGmp\PGmm\PGL\PKm
reconstructed combination.

The
\PGXmb
candidates are required to have
P_{\text{vtx}}>1\%

and
\pt>10\,(15)\GeV
for the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
(
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
) channel.
From all reconstructed
\Pp\Pp
collision vertices, the primary vertex (PV)
is chosen as the one with the smallest pointing angle, as done in
Refs.
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
.
The pointing angle is the three-dimensional angle between the
\PGXmb
candidate momentum and
the vector joining the PV with the reconstructed
\PGXmb
candidate decay vertex.
The decay length
L_{xy}
of the
\PGXmb
candidate in the transverse plane,
computed as the two-dimensional distance between the PV and the
\PGXmb
decay vertex,
is required to be at least three times larger than its uncertainty
\sigma_{L_{xy}}
.
The
\vec{\pt}(\PGXmb)
is required to be aligned with the
transverse displacement vector:

\cos(\alpha(\PGXmb,\text{PV}))>0.99\,(0.993)
for the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
(
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
) channel,
where
\alpha(\PGXmb,\text{PV})
is the pointing angle in the plane transverse to the beams.
Two additional topological requirements are applied:
the cosine of the pointing angle,
\cos(\alpha(\PGXm,\,\PGXmb))
,
must be larger than 0.999 for the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGXm
channel;
and
L_{xy}/\sigma_{L_{xy}}(\PGL,\PGXmb)>20
for the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
channel.
Besides, the pion emitted in the
\PGXm\to\PGL\PGpm
decay and the kaon emitted in the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGL\PKm
decay
must have
d_{xy}/\sigma_{d_{xy}}>0.9
and 0.6,
respectively, where
d_{xy}
is the impact parameter in the transverse plane with respect to the PV,
and
\sigma_{d_{xy}}
is its uncertainty.

Since the lifetime of the excited
\PGXb
states is expected to be negligible,
the
\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm

\PGXmb

Combinations of a

The analysis is performed using the

\Delta M=M(\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm)-M(\PGXmb)-2\,m^{\mathrm{PDG}}_{\PGppm}

M(\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm)

\PGXmb

According to the simulation studies, this variable also has the advantage of
being insensitive to a potential mass shift caused by the fact that the photon emitted in the

\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGSz\PKm
,
\PGSz\to\PGL\PGg

[

\PGXmb
candidate and all tracks forming the PV are refit to a common vertex,
further improving the
\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm

1.39\pm 0.11
to
0.94\pm 0.06\MeV
(statistical uncertainties only),

,

\PGXb^{\!\!\!\ast{}\ast{}-}\to\PGXstzb\PGpm

\PGXstzb\to\PGXmb\PGpp

As the
\PGXstzb

\MeV

\MeV

\PGXstzb

A simultaneous unbinned extended maximum-likelihood fit is performed on the
two data samples shown in Fig.
3

The signal component is described with a relativistic
Breit–Wigner (RBW) function
[
42
,
43
]
for the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}\to\PGXstzb\PGpm
decay,
convolved with a double-Gaussian resolution function.
The mass and natural width of the signal function are the two parameters of interest in the fit.
The normalization and background parameters are different
for the fully and partially reconstructed channels, as are the resolution parameters,
which are fixed from the simulation studies.
The background component is modeled with the threshold function

(\Delta M)^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha
is a free parameter.

The fitted mass difference of the new
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
state is
\Delta M_{\PGXb\hskip-0.73747pt(6100)^{-}}=24.14\pm 0.22\MeV
,
where the uncertainty is statistical only.
The fitted signal yields are
26\pm 7
and
34\pm 9

for the fully reconstructed and the
\PGXmb\to\PJGy\PGSz\PKm
channels, respectively.
The relative yield of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
, w.r.t. the
\PGXmb
yield, is found to be noticeably larger
in the partially reconstructed
\PGXmb
channel, compared to the fully reconstructed channels.
Given the large uncertainties in the observed small
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
signal yields,
this discrepancy is consistent with being a statistical fluctuation.
The order-of-magnitude larger signals of the
\PGXstzb\to\PGXmb\PGpp
decays,
with respect to the
\PGXmb
ground state signals,
are found to be consistent between all three
\PGXmb
reconstruction channels.

The natural width of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
is too small to be measured with
the present data sample and experimental resolution.
An upper limit on
\Gamma(\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-})
has been obtained through a scan of the
profiled likelihood, assuming an asymptotic distribution.
The measured upper limit, at 95% confidence level,
is
\Gamma(\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-})<1.9\MeV
, where the systematic uncertainties,
discussed below, are taken into account.

The local statistical significance of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
signal is
evaluated with the likelihood ratio technique, comparing the background-only
and signal-plus-background hypotheses (with four additional free parameters),
using asymptotic formulas
[
44
,
45
]
.
The resulting significance of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
signal
varies between 6.2 and 6.7 standard deviations, depending on the
fit model variations used to evaluate the systematic uncertainties.

Several sources of systematic uncertainties in the measured
mass difference
\Delta M_{\PGXb\hskip-0.73747pt(6100)^{-}}
are considered.
To evaluate the systematic uncertainties related to the choice of the fit model,
several alternative functions are tested.
Uncertainties related to the choice of the signal model are estimated
by changing the resolution function from a double-Gaussian function to a single Gaussian function
or a sum of three Gaussian functions.
Two alternative background models are considered:
the threshold function multiplied by an exponential
and the threshold function multiplied by a first-order polynomial.
The largest deviations in the measured mass are 0.01 and 0.04
\MeV
,
respectively, for the variations of the signal and background models;
these values are taken as the two corresponding systematic uncertainties.

The RBW function used in the signal modeling includes Blatt–Weisskopf
barrier factors
[
43
]
, which depend on the radial
parameter
r
and on the angular momentum
l
(spin).
In the baseline fit,
r=3.5\GeV^{-1}
and

l=1
. The corresponding systematic uncertainties are obtained by varying

r
between 1 and
5\GeV^{-1}
or by assigning
l=0
.
The
r
variations have a negligible effect on the results,
while fixing
l=0
changes the signal shape and induces a
mass difference variation of 0.01
\MeV
, taken as the corresponding systematic uncertainty.

To account for a possible difference between the measured and simulated mass resolutions,
the fits are repeated with resolutions scaled up or down by 1.074, a factor
determined from the comparison of the
\PGXmb
resolutions in data and simulation.
The resulting systematic uncertainty of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
mass difference is 0.02
\MeV
.

Simulation studies show a shift of 0.07
\MeV
between the generated and reconstructed mass differences;
this shift is treated as an additional systematic uncertainty in the
\Delta M_{\PGXb\hskip-0.73747pt(6100)^{-}}
measurement.

The systematic uncertainty reflecting the
\Delta M
fit range
is evaluated by changing the upper end of the
\Delta M
fit range from its default 100
\MeV
to 80, 120, and 150
\MeV
.
The largest mass difference change of 0.02
\MeV
is taken as the corresponding systematic uncertainty.

A potential bias due to a possible misalignment of the
tracker detectors is evaluated by comparing the results obtained with the data collected in
2016, 2017, and 2018. This is a reasonable
evaluation, given that the inner part of the CMS tracker was replaced
between the 2016 and 2017 data-taking periods.
The measured mass is found to be insensitive to alignment uncertainties.

The total systematic uncertainty in the measured mass difference
\Delta M_{\PGXb\hskip-0.73747pt(6100)^{-}}
,
calculated as the sum in quadrature of the partial terms, is 0.09
\MeV
.

In summary, we report the observation of a new excited beauty strange baryon,
decaying to
\PGXmb\PGpp\PGpm
. The analysis uses proton-proton collision data collected
by the CMS experiment at
\sqrt{s}=13

\TeV
, corresponding
to an integrated luminosity of 140
\fbinv
.
The measured mass difference of this state is

M(\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-})-M(\PGXmb)-2\,m^{\mathrm{PDG}}_{\PGppm}=24.14\pm 0.22\stat\pm 0.09\syst\MeV
.
The known
\PGXmb
mass of
5797.0\pm 0.6\MeV

[
20
]
is used to obtain

M(\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-})=6100.3\pm 0.2\stat\pm 0.1\syst\pm 0.6\,(\PGXmb)\MeV
.
It is particularly remarkable that if the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
baryon were only 13
\MeV
heavier,
it would be above the
\PGLzb\PKm
mass threshold and could decay to this final state.
The natural width of this resonance is compatible with zero and
a 95% confidence level upper limit of 1.9
\MeV
has been determined.

Following analogies with the established excited
\PGXc
baryon states
[
20
]
, and considering
several theoretical predictions
[
12
,
13
,
21
]
,
the new
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
resonance and its decay sequence are consistent with
the lightest orbitally excited
\PGXmb
baryon,
with the light diquark angular momentum
j_{\PQd\PQs}=1
and
J^{P}=3/2^{-}

(excitation with orbital momentum
L=1
between the
\PQb
quark and the
\PQd\PQs
diquark).
This suggests that it is the beauty analogue of the
\PGXc(2815)
baryon
[
41
]
.
Measuring a natural width of the
\PGXb\hskip-0.89996pt(6100)^{-}
smaller than 1.9
\MeV
comes as a surprise,
given the larger values predicted by the theory calculations
[
12
,
13
,
21
]
,
based on the assumption that the
\PGXb^{\!\!\!\ast{}\ast{}-}\to\PGXstzb\PGpm
decay
proceeds predominantly via
S
-wave (
3/2^{-}\to 3/2^{+}0^{-}
).
The observation of this baryon and the measurement of its properties provide
information that should help to distinguish between different theoretical models
used to calculate the properties of the excited
\PGXb
states.

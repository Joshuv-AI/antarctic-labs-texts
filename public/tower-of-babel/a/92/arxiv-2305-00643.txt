1.
Introduction

Let
N
and
p
be prime numbers
\geq 5
such that
p\mid N-1
. In this situation, Mazur proved in his seminal work
[
16
]
that there is a cuspidal eigenform
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
which is Eisenstein modulo
p
. More precisely, there is a prime above
\mathfrak{p}
in the ring of integer
\mathcal{O}_{f}
of the Hecke field of

(1)

f\equiv E_{2}\text{ (modulo }\mathfrak{p}\text{),}

where

2

\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Actually, there may be several such cuspidal eigenforms

the (finite and non-empty) set of cuspidal eigenforms in

In order to study in more details (

. Let

be the cuspidal Hecke algebra over

(

[
16

\mathfrak{P}
be the maximal ideal of

generated by

and

\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{0}
the

-adic completion of

For simplicity, in all this paper we will assume that

[

Mazur defined in

Note that

Assume

We would like to have a statement more closely related to the BSD conjecture for

The right hand side being an important invariant, we follow Mazur let

The inequality

(i)

Assume Hypothesis

. Assume

,

The assumption that

splits in

is equivalent to the fact that the sign of

is

. As above, this result is predicted by the BSD conjecture, and may be considered as a modulo

version of the BSD conjecture for
\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}
. Our strategy is similar to Mazur’s, which we now recall. For simplicity, let us assume

•

Step 1: congruence formula for the
L
-value. Mazur gives a congruence formula modulo
I
(the Eisenstein ideal) for
\Lambda(U_{\mathfrak{P}},\chi_{D})
. He uses modular symbols and a multiplicity one result to obtain such a congruence. Let us explain intuitively why one can expect such a congruence formula. Let
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
such that
f\equiv E_{2}\text{ (modulo }p\text{)}
as above. We can expect a congruence between the “algebraic parts” of
L(f,\chi_{D},1)
and
L(E_{2},\chi_{D},1)
(such congruences have been established in some cases
eg.
by
[
26
]
). We have

L(E_{2},\chi_{D},1)=L(\chi_{D},1)\cdot L(\chi_{D},0)\text{ ,}

which is proportional to
L(\chi_{D},0)^{2}=B_{1,\chi_{D}}^{2}
by the functional equation. By the class number formula,
B_{1,\chi_{D}}
is essentially the class number
h(K)
of
K
(since
K
is imaginary in Mazur’s result). Thus, one would expect that the algebraic part of
L(f,\chi_{D},1)
is proportional to
h(K)^{2}
modulo
p
. This is what Mazur actually shows using modular symbols in
[
17
, II §7 Proposition]
.

•

Step 2: Modulo
p
Selmer group computation. Mazur performs an Eisenstein descent using flat cohomology. He is able to show in
[
17
, §1 Step 2]
that
p
does not divide
h(K)
if and only if both the rank of the Mordell–Weil group of
\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}
and the
p
-part of its Tate–Shafarevich group are zero. Let us again give a heuristic explanation to this result. Since
\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}(\Q)
does not have any
p
-torsion, this triviality of the rank and the
p
-part of
\Sh
is equivalent to
\Sel_{p}(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}/\Q)=0
, where
\Sel_{p}
is the

-Selmer group, a subgroup of the Galois cohomology group
H^{1}(\Gal(\overline{\Q}/\Q),\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}[p])
(and by restriction a subgroup of
H^{1}(\Gal(\overline{\Q}/K),\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}[p])
). We then have an isomorphism of
\Gal(\overline{\Q}/K)
-modules

\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}[p]\simeq\tilde{J}^{(p)}[p]\simeq\mu_{p}\oplus\Z/p\Z\text{.}

Thus,
\Sel_{p}(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}/\Q)
is a subgroup of

H^{1}(\Gal(\overline{\Q}/K),\mu_{p})\oplus H^{1}(\Gal(\overline{\Q}/K),\Z/p\Z)\text{ .}

The Selmer conditions actually makes

a subgroup of

\Hom(\Pic(A),\Z/p\Z)\oplus H^{1}(\Spec(A),\mu_{p})

where
A=\mathcal{O}_{K}[1/N]
. The group

is an extension of
\Pic(A)[p]
by
A^{\times}\otimes\Z/p\Z
. Since
A^{\times}\otimes\Z/p\Z
is generated by
N
which fixed by

, one may expect that

is non-zero if and only if
p
does not divide
h(K)
.

Let us explain the difficulties which arise when trying to follow Mazur’s approach.

•

Step 1 (the congruence formula for the
L
-value). Since
\chi_{D}
is even in our case, we have
L(\chi_{D},0)=0
. Thus, the naïve congruence argument above yields that
p
always divides the algebraic part of
L(f,\chi_{D},1)
. This can easily be proved rigorously using a congruence for even modular symbols due to Mazur (
cf.
[
17
, p. 211]
). The fact that
p
always divides
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
if
D>0
is actually predicted by the BSD conjecture: the
N
th Tamagawa number of
\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}
is the same as the
N
th Tamagawa number of
\tilde{J}^{(p)}
since
N
splits in
K
, and the latter is divisible by
p
by
[
16
, Theorem A.1]
. In order to understand
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
modulo
p^{2}
, one way would be to have a congruence formula for even modular symbols modulo
p^{2}
. Such a congruence has been given in
[
15
, Theorem 1.12]
using Sharifi’s conjecture at level
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. However, this formula is given in terms of Manin symbols and is not enough for our purposes. One needs to use Sharifi’s conjecture at level
\Gamma_{1}(ND)
. We build on our recent work
[
14
]
to get an unconditional congruence formula.

•

Step 2 (the computation of the
p
-Selmer group). A similar argument as in the case where
K
is imaginary applies: one relates
\Sel_{p}(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}/\Q)
to
H^{1}(\Spec(A),\mu_{p})\oplus\Hom(\Pic(A),\Z/p\Z)
where

. If
K
is real, then

has dimension
3
(one dimension coming from the fundamental unit, and the two others from the prime dividing

in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
), so the computation of
\Sel_{p}(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}/\Q)
is more complicated. A careful analysis of the Selmer conditions is necessary (we rely on the

-adic uniformization of

and results of
[

]
for the local condition at

).

Let us conclude this introduction by a few comments.

The general philosophy behind our result is a relation between the analytic or algebraic aspects of
\GL_{2}
and the ones of
\GL_{1}
. That is, we relate a degree
2

L
-function (the
L
-function of a cuspidal modular form) to a degree
1

L
-function (the
L
-function of a Dirichlet character). Similarly, we relate a Selmer group for an abelian variety to a Selmer group for a character (a class group). The reason behind such a relation is an Eisenstein congruence, between a cusp form and an Eisenstein series (as explained briefly in Step 1 of Mazur’s result). This philosophy is of course not new, and dates back at least to the work of Ribet on the converse of Herbrand’s theorem
[
20
]
. For a recent example, see
[
22
]
. One general phenomenon is that “half” of the
L
-values of the Eisenstein series are zero (here meaning that for
D>0
we have
L(E_{2},\chi_{D},1)=0
), and so direct Eisenstein congruences do not give much information. To go beyond, one needs a “higher” theory of Eisenstein congruences,
eg.
as developed in
[
12
]
.

We expect that for a positive proportion and
<100\%
of the
D
’s as in Theorem
1.7
, we have
\Sel_{p}(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D}/\Q)\neq 0
. By Theorem
1.7
, this is equivalent to saying that
u(K)^{h(K)}
is a
p
th power modulo (a prime above)
N
for a positive proportion and
<100\%
of the real quadratic fields
K
in which
N
splits (here,
N
and
p
are fixed). We do not know if classical techniques from number theory can yield results on this conjecture.

Let us notice that the congruence formula for the
L
-value modulo
p^{2}
we obtain here can be proved using a different approach. Namely, one can use a formula of Popa (based on a general formula of Waldspurger) to express
L(f/K,1)
, where
K
is quadratic real and
f
is a cuspidal eigenform, in terms of closed Heegner geodesics. We refer to
[
11
]
for the details. We like our present approach because it does not rely on the difficult result of Waldspurger and Popa.

Finally, let us comment on the recent work of Castella–Grossi–Skinner
[
3
]
. They prove the
p
-part of the BSD formula for certain elliptic curves
E
over
\Q
of (algebraic) rank
\leq 1
and with a degree
p
-isogeny over
\Q
(
cf.
[
3
, Theorem D]
). Their method is to prove and rely on the Iwasawa main conjecture in this Eisenstein case
[
3
, Theorem A]
. To do so, they actually prove Perrin–Riou’s anti-cyclotomic Iwasawa main conjecture under some asumptions for an auxiliary imaginary quadratic field (
cf.
[
3
, Conjecture B and Theorem C]
).

The results of
[
3
]
and ours, which are independent and use totally different methods, intersect in the following single example: when
E=X_{0}(11)
and
p=5
. They prove the
p
-part of the BSD formula for quadratic twists
E_{D}
of
E
by
K=\Q(\sqrt{D})
where
D>0
is such that
p
is
inert
in
K
(and
N=11
splits in
K
). As mentioned, they also need to assume that the rank of
E_{D}
is zero. We do not have such a restriction on the rank (which could be
\geq 2
for us), and we also handle the case when
p
splits in
K
. On the one hand, their result in this example and under their assumptions is more precise since they prove the exact
p
-part of BSD while we only prove BSD “modulo
p
”. On the other hand, our main result Theorem
1.7
is more explicit since we give a criterion in terms of the fundamental unit and the class number. We also handle higher dimensional abelian varieties, which is not the case in
[
3
]
(we do not know whether they may extend their methods to modular eigenforms with non-rational Hecke fields).

The structure of our paper is as follows. In section

.

4.
L
-values and modular symbols

In this section, we reformulate the problem of computing the
p
-adic valuation of
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
in terms of modular symbols.

Let us first introduce some basic notation regarding modular symbols. If
\Gamma
is a finite index subgroup of
\SL_{2}(\Z)
, we denote by
X(\Gamma)
the compact modular curve of level
\Gamma
and by
C(\Gamma)
the subset of cusps of
X(\Gamma)
. One can consider the relative singular homology group
H_{1}(X(\Gamma),C(\Gamma),\Z)
. If
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbf{P}^{1}(\Q)
, we denote by
\{\alpha,\beta\}
the class in
H_{1}(X(\Gamma),C(\Gamma),\Z)
of the hyperbolic path in the extended upper-half plane from
\alpha
to
\beta
. The element
\{\alpha,\beta\}
is called a
modular symbol
.

Recall that in section
3
we have defined a period
\Omega_{f}^{+}
attached to a normalized eigenform
f\in S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the maximal ideal of residual characteristic
p
corresponding to
f
. If
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
(the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)^{+}
) is free of rank one over
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}
, then one may choose a generator
c
of
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}
and define
\Omega_{f}^{+}=\int_{c}2i\pi f(z)dz
. In this situation, the period map
\varphi_{f}:H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)^{+}\rightarrow\overline{\Q}_{p}
given by
\gamma\mapsto\frac{\int_{\gamma}2i\pi f(z)dz}{\Omega_{f}^{+}}
takes values in
\overline{\Z}_{p}
. Furthermore, if
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\Q}_{p}/\Q_{p})
then we may normalize the periods so that
\varphi_{f^{\sigma}}=\sigma\circ\varphi_{f}
, where
f^{\sigma}
is the conjugate of
f
by
\sigma
.

This discussion applies when
f\in S_{2}^{\Eis}(N,p)
. We fix such a
f
in this section. The assumption that
p\mid\mid N-1
implies that all the other such eigenforms are of the form
f^{\sigma}
for some
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\Q}/\Q)
. Indeed, in this case the Eisenstein ideal
I
is maximal
[
16
, Proposition II.9.7]
, and since it is principal
[
16
, Proposition II.16.6]
then we conclude that
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{0}
is a discrete valuation ring, and in particular has no zero-divisors.

Let us now give a reformulation of Hypothesis
H
. We have just noticed that
\#S_{2}^{\Eis}(N,p)
is the number of conjugates of
f
by automorphisms in
\Gal(\overline{\Q}/\Q)
. On the other hand,
g_{p}
is the number of conjugates of
f
by automorphisms in
\Gal(\overline{\Q}_{p}/\Q_{p})
(recall that we have fixed an embedding
\overline{\Q}\hookrightarrow\overline{\Q}_{p}
). Therefore, Hypothesis
H
holds if and only if all the conjugates of
f
(
i.e.
eigenforms in
S_{2}^{\Eis}(N,p)
) are actually conjugates of
f
by some
\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\Q}_{p}/\Q_{p})
.

As is well-known (
cf.
[
17
, §6 Proposition]
), if
\chi:(\Z/m\Z)^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}^{\times}
is a primitive Dirichlet character of conductor
m>1
coprime to
N
then we have

(3)

\tau(\chi^{-1})\cdot L(f,\chi,1)=\sum_{a\in(\Z/m\Z)^{\times}}\chi(a)^{-1}\cdot\int_{\frac{a}{m}}^{0}2i\pi f(z)dz

where
\tau(\chi^{-1})=\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}\chi(a)^{-1}\cdot e^{\frac{2i\pi a}{m}}
is the Gauss sum attached to
\chi^{-1}
. Since
\gcd(m,N)=1
, the cusps
0
and
\frac{a}{m}
of
X_{0}(N)
are equivalent, so the modular symbol
\{0,\frac{a}{m}\}
is in
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)
. Therefore, if
\chi
is even (
i.e.
\chi(-1)=1
), the formula (
3
) can be rewritten as

(4)

\tau(\chi^{-1})\cdot\frac{L(f,\chi,1)}{\Omega_{f}^{+}}=-\sum_{a\in(\Z/m\Z)^{\times}}\chi(a)^{-1}\cdot\varphi_{f}(\{0,\frac{a}{m}\})\text{ .}

We apply this formula for
\chi=\chi_{D}
. Note that
\tau(\chi)^{2}=D
, so
\tau(\chi)
is coprime to
p
. Following Mazur
[
17
, II §6 and III §2]
, we let

(5)

\Lambda(U_{\mathfrak{P}},\chi_{D})=\sum_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}\chi_{D}(a)\cdot\{0,\frac{a}{D}\}\in H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)_{\mathfrak{P}}^{+}\text{ .}

In view of (
4
), it is natural to call
\Lambda(U_{\mathfrak{P}},\chi_{D})
the
\mathfrak{P}
-part of the universal special value twisted by
\chi_{D}
. We let

\Theta_{D}=\sum_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}\chi_{D}(a)\cdot\{0,\frac{a}{D}\}\in H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)^{+}\text{ .}

Thus,
\Lambda(U_{\mathfrak{P}},\chi_{D})
is just the image of
\Theta_{D}
into the completion at
\mathfrak{P}
of
H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z)^{\pm}
.

We have the following classical
L
-function decomposition

(6)

L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},s)=L(\tilde{J}^{(p)},\chi_{D},s)=\prod_{\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\Q}/\Q)}L(f^{\sigma},\chi_{D},s)\text{ .}

Combining Proposition
3.1
, Proposition
3.2
, (
4
) and (
6
), we get that if Hypothesis
H
is satisfied then

\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}\sim\prod_{\sigma\in\Gal(\overline{\Q}_{p}/\Q_{p})}\sigma\left(\sum_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}\chi_{D}(a)\cdot\varphi_{f}(\{0,\frac{a}{D}\})\right)\text{ .}

Note that
\varphi_{f}(\{0,\frac{a}{D}\})
takes values in a totally ramified finite extension of
\Z_{p}
(indeed,
\mathbb{T}_{\mathfrak{P}}^{0}
is a totally ramified DVR,
cf.
[
16
, Proposition II.19.1]
).
Thus, the
\mathfrak{P}
-adic valuation of
\sum_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}\chi_{D}(a)\cdot\varphi_{f}(\{0,\frac{a}{D}\})
is the same as the
p
-adic valuation of
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
. This proves the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume Hypothesis
H
. Then the
p
-adic valuation of the rational number
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
is the largest integer
n\geq 0
such that

\Theta_{D}\in I^{n}\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}\text{ .}

Proposition
4.1
shows that Corollaries
1.4
and
1.8
follows from Theorems
1.3
and
1.7
respectively.

We have the following “trivial divisibility” due to Mazur when
D>0
.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have
\Theta_{D}\in I\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}
. Therefore, assuming Hypothesis
H
,
p
divides the numerator of
\frac{L(\tilde{J}^{(p)}_{D},1)}{\Omega_{D}}
.

Proof.

Mazur proved in
[
16
, Proposition II.18.8]
that we have an isomorphism

\alpha:H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}/I\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}\xrightarrow{\sim}\Z/p\Z

sending a modular symbol
\{0,\frac{b}{d}\}
(with
\gcd(b,d)=\gcd(d,N)=1
) to
\log(d)
(
cf.
Section
2
for the definition of
\log
). We have

\alpha(\Theta_{D})=\sum_{a\in(\Z/m\Z)^{\times}}\chi_{D}(a)\cdot\log(D)=0

since

is non-trivial. This proves that

. The assertion about

follows from Proposition

.
∎

We have thus proved the first assertion of Theorem

and Corollary

. In order to go further and study the divisibility of

5.
Level compatibility in Sharifi’s conjecture

Let us recall some background on modular symbols and Sharifi’s conjecture. For
M\in\mathbf{N}
, let
\Gamma_{1}(M)=\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{2}(\Z)\text{ such that }a-1\equiv c\equiv 0\text{ (modulo }M\text{)}\}
and let

be the compact modular curve of level
\Gamma_{1}(M)
.

Let

be the set of cusps of

, and
C_{M}^{0}
be those cusps in
C_{M}
of the form
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cdot\frac{a}{b}
with
\gcd(a,b)=1
and

(in the case
b=0
we have the cusp
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cdot\infty

Let
H_{1}(X_{1}(M),C_{M},\Z)
be the singular homology of

relative to

\alpha
and

are in

, let

be the class in

of the hyperbolic geodesic from

to

in

.

Let

be the (modified) Manin map: it sends a coset
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
to

(it is the usual Manin map composed with the Atkin–Lehner involution

is surjective.

Let

be the subset consisting of
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with
M\nmid c
and
M\nmid d
. Note that
S_{M}^{0}
can be identified with the set of pairs
[c,d]
where

modulo the identification
[c,d]=[-c,-d]
.
The restriction

\xi_{M}^{0}:\Z[S_{M}^{0}]\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1}(M),C_{M},\Z)

is surjective (
cf.
[
8
, §2.1.3]
).

If
A
is a commutative ring, let
K_{2}(A)
be the second K-group of
A
, as defined by Quillen. Let
\zeta_{M}\in\overline{\Q}
be a primitive

(and in particular of the complex conjugation) on

. We denote by
\mathcal{K}_{M}
the largest quotient of
K_{2}(\Z[\zeta_{M},\frac{1}{M}])\otimes\Z[\frac{1}{2}]
on which the complex conjugation acts trivially. The map
\Z[S_{M}^{0}]\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{M}
sending
\Gamma_{1}(M)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
to the Steinberg symbol
\langle 1-\zeta_{M}^{c},1-\zeta_{M}^{d}\rangle
factors through
\xi_{M}^{0}
(
cf.
[
8
, §2.1.4]
), and thus induces a map

\varpi_{M}:H_{1}(X_{1}(M),C_{M}^{0},\Z)\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{M}\text{ .}

Sharifi conjectured that
\varpi_{M}
is annihilated by the Hecke operators
T_{\ell}-\ell\langle\ell\rangle-1
for primes
\ell
not dividing
M
(
cf.
the remark after Theorem 4.3.6 in
[
23
]
). This conjecture has a history of partial results:
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
and most recently
[
23
]
and
[
14
]
. In particular, the restriction of
\varpi_{M}
to
H_{1}(X_{1}(M),\Z)
is known to be annihilated by

for primes
\ell
not dividing
M
(
cf.
[

, where we warn the reader that they use

is prime, then a mild improvement on the techniques of Sharifi and Venkatesh shows that

(not restricted) is annihilated by the Hecke operators

for primes

[

We shall make use of the following result of

]
.

We have a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(X_{1}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}}

Let us explain how this result follows from
[

. By
[

, the map

\pi:H_{1}(X_{1}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}

has kernel

, where we identify

(which acts by diamond operators on

By

. Finally, by

Thus, we have a commutative diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{1}(X_{1}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})/J^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varpi_{N}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

where the vertical arrows are surjective.

The map

is annihilated by the Hecke operators

for

.

Thus, the map

factors through a surjective map

By
[
15
, Remark 1.3]
, we have a (canonical) isomorphism

On the other hand,
I\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}/I^{2}\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}
is also (canonically) isomorphic to

J\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})/J^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})\simeq((\Z/N\Z)^{\times})^{\otimes 2}\otimes\Z_{p}

(this is due to Mazur, see
[
15
, p. 2]
). Therefore, the surjective map

I\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}/I^{2}\cdot H_{1}(X_{0}(N),\Z_{p})^{+}\rightarrow J\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})/J^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})

has to be an isomorphism. This concludes the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

We have

\Norm\left(\langle\prod_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}(1-\zeta_{D}^{a})^{\chi_{D}(a)},1-\zeta_{ND}\rangle\right)\in J^{2}\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{N}\otimes\Z_{p})

is a
p

Proof.

Let

be the functorial map coming from the inclusion

. If

Write
\zeta_{ND}=\zeta_{N}^{u}\cdot\zeta_{D}^{v}
for some

We thus get:

\displaystyle\iota\circ

\displaystyle\Norm\left(\langle\prod_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}(1-\zeta_{D}^{a})^{\chi_{D}(a)},1-\zeta_{ND}\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}\langle\prod_{a\in(\Z/D\Z)^{\times}}(1-\zeta_{D}^{ab})^{\chi_{D}(a)},1-\zeta_{N}^{u}\zeta_{D}^{vb}\rangle

.

Let us prove the second part of the lemma.
By the work of Mazur
[

16

•

One easily checks that completing at

and taking Galois cohomology, we get an exact sequence

Lemma 8.6

1.
Introduction

A Lidstone series provides a generalization of Taylor series that approximates a given function in a neighborhood of two points instead of one
[
11
]
. Recently, Ismail and Mansour
[
8
]
introduced a
q
-analog of the Lidstone expansion theorem. They proved that, under certain conditions, an entire function
f(z)
can be expanded in the form

(1.1)

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big[A_{n}(z)D_{q^{-1}}^{2n}\,f(1)-B_{n}(z)D_{q^{-1}}^{2n}\,f(0)\Big],

where
A_{n}(z)
and
B_{n}(z)
are the
q
-Lidstone polynomials defined by

A_{n}(z)=\large{\eta}^{1}_{q^{-1}}B_{n}(z)\mbox{ and }B_{n}(z)=\frac{2^{2n+1}}{[2n+1]_{q}!}B_{2n+1}(z/2;q).

Here
\large{\eta}^{y}_{q^{-1}}
denotes the
q
-translation operator defined by

{\large{\eta}_{q^{-1}}^{y}}z^{n}=q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}z^{n}(-y/z;q^{-1})_{n}=y^{n}(-z/y;q)_{n},

and
B_{n}(z;q)
is the
q
-analog of the Bernoulli polynomials which defined by the
generating function

(1.2)

\dfrac{t\,E_{q}(zt)}{E_{q}(t/2)e_{q}(t/2)-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}(z;q)\frac{t^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},

where
E_{q}(z)
and
e_{q}(z)
are the
q
-exponential functions defined by

E_{q}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}q^{j(j-1)/2}\,\frac{z^{j}}{[j]_{q}!};\,z\in\mathbb{C}\,\mbox{ and }\,e_{q}(z):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{[j]_{q}!};\,|z|<1.

This paper aims to construct the
q
-Lidstone polynomials which are
q
-Bernoulli and
q
-Euler polynomials generated by the third Jackson
q
-Bessel function, and then to derive two formula of
q
-Lidstone expansion theorem. More precisely,
we will prove that the entire function may be expanded in terms of
q
-Lidstone polynomials in two different forms. In the first form, the
q
-Lidstone polynomials are
q
-Bernoulli polynomials and the coefficients are the even powers of the
q
-derivative
\frac{\delta_{q}f(z)}{\delta_{q}z}
at
0
and
1
. The other form expand the function in
q
-Lidstone polynomials based on
q
-Euler polynomials and the coefficients contain the even and odd powers of the
q
-derivative
\frac{\delta_{q}f(z)}{\delta_{q}z}
.
The publications
[
12
,
13
]
are the most affiliated with this work.

This article is organized as follows: in Section 2, we state some definitions and present some background on
q
-analysis which we need in our investigations. In Section 3 and Section 4, we introduce
q
-Bernoulli and
q
-Euler polynomials generated by the third Jackson
q
-Bessel function. Section 5 contains a
q
-Lidstone expansion theorem involving
q
-Bernoulli polynomials while Section 6 contains a
q
-Lidstone series
involving
q
-Euler polynomials.

2.
Definitions and Preliminary results

Throughout this paper, unless otherwise is stated,
q
is a positive number less than one and we follow the notations and terminology in
[
1
,
6
]
.

The symmetric
q
-difference operator
\delta_{q}
is defined by

\delta_{q}f(z)=f(q^{\frac{1}{2}}z)-f(q^{\frac{-1}{2}}z),

(see
[
4
,
6
]
) and then

(2.1)

\frac{\delta_{q}f(z)}{\delta_{q}z}:=\dfrac{f(q^{\frac{1}{2}}z)-f(q^{\frac{-1}{2}}z)}{z(q^{\frac{1}{2}}-q^{\frac{-1}{2}})}\quad z\neq 0.

We use a third
q
-exponential function
exp_{q}(z)
which has the following series representation

(2.2)

exp_{q}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{4}}}{[n]_{q}!}z^{n};\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

This function has the property
\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}exp_{q}(z)=e^{z}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and it is an entire function of
z
of order zero (see
[
6
]
).

Remark 2.1
.

From the identity
[n]_{1/q}!=q^{\frac{n(1-n)}{2}}[n]_{q}!
, one can verify that

(2.3)

exp_{q}(z)=exp_{q^{-1}}(z);\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

We consider the domain
\displaystyle\Omega:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|1-exp_{q}(z)|<1\}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
z\in\Omega
. Then

(2.4)

\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}:=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\,z^{n}\,,

where

c_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}\,\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\ldots+s_{k}=n\atop s_{i}>0\,(i=1,\ldots,k)}\dfrac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(s_{i}-1)/4}}{[s_{1}]_{q}![s_{2}]_{q}!\ldots[s_{k}]_{q}!}\,.

Proof.

Observe that, for
z\in\Omega
the function
\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}
can be represented as

\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}:=\Big[\frac{1}{1+(exp_{q}(z)-1)}\Big]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\Big[exp_{q}(z)-1\Big]^{k}.

Using the series expansion (
2.2
) of
exp_{q}(z)
, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n(n-1)/4}\,\frac{z^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{n(n-1)/4}\,\frac{z^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}z^{n}\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\ldots+s_{k}=n\atop s_{i}>0\,(i=1,\ldots,k)}\dfrac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(s_{i}-1)/4}}{[s_{1}]_{q}![s_{2}]_{q}!\ldots[s_{k}]_{q}!}\,.

Put
\displaystyle a_{n}(k)=\sum_{s_{1}+s_{2}+\ldots+s_{k}=n\atop s_{i}>0\,(i=1,\ldots,k)}\dfrac{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{i}(s_{i}-1)/4}}{[s_{1}]_{q}![s_{2}]_{q}!\ldots[s_{k}]_{q}!}
. Then, the power series of
\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}
takes the form

\dfrac{1}{exp_{q}(z)}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k}a_{n}(k),

and then we obtain the desired result.
∎

The
q
-sine and
q
-cosine,
S_{q}(z)
and
C_{q}(z)
, are defined by

exp_{q}(iz):=C_{q}(z)+iS_{q}(z),

where

(2.5)

\begin{split}C_{q}(z)&:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,\frac{q^{n(n-\frac{1}{2})}}{[2n]_{q}!}z^{2n},\\
S_{q}(z)&:=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,\frac{q^{n(n+\frac{1}{2})}}{[2n+1]_{q}!}z^{2n+1}.\end{split}

These functions can be written in terms of the third Jackson
q
-Bessel function or (Hahn-Exton
q
-Bessel
function
[
10
]
) as

\begin{split}C_{q}(z)&:=q^{-\frac{3}{8}}\,\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}((1-q)z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,J^{(3)}_{-\frac{1}{2}}(q^{\frac{-3}{4}}(1-q)z;q^{2}),\\
S_{q}(z)&:=q^{\frac{1}{8}}\,\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}((1-q)z)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,J^{(3)}_{\frac{1}{2}}(q^{\frac{-1}{4}}(1-q)z;q^{2}),\end{split}

and satisfy

(2.6)

\frac{\delta_{q}C_{q}(wz)}{\delta_{q}z}=-w\,S_{q}(wz),\quad\frac{\delta_{q}S_{q}(wz)}{\delta_{q}z}=w\,C_{q}(wz).

(see
[
4
,
6
]
). Therefore,

(2.7)

\frac{\delta_{q}\,exp_{q}(wz)}{\delta_{q}z}=w\,exp_{q}(wz).

\begin{gathered}Sinh_{q}(z):=-iS_{q}(iz)=\dfrac{exp_{q}(z)-exp_{q}(-z)}{2}\\

3.

q
-Bernoulli polynomials generated by the third Jackson

In this section, we use the third
q
-exponential function
exp_{q}(x)
to define a
q
-analog of the Bernoulli polynomials which are suitable for our approach.

Definition 3.1
.

A
q
-Bernoulli polynomials
\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)
are defined by the generating function

(3.1)

\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(zw)\,exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!},

and
\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q):=\widetilde{B}_{n}(0;q)
are the
q
-Bernoulli numbers. Therefore,

(3.2)

\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(-w/2)}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)}{[n]_{q}!}w^{n}.

Remark 3.2
.

\widetilde{B}_{2n+1}(\frac{1}{2};q)=0
. Indeed, for
z=\frac{1}{2}
, the left hand side of Equation (
3.1
) is an even function. Therefore, the odd powers of
w
on the left hand side vanish. Also, note that

\widetilde{B}_{0}(z;q)=\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(zw)\,exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}\Big|_{w=0}=1.

Proposition 3.3
.

The
q
-Bernoulli polynomials
\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)
are given recursively by
\widetilde{B}_{0}(z;q)=1
, and for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{4}}\,\widetilde{\beta}_{n-k}(q)z^{k}.

Proof.

By substituting (
3.2
) into (
3.1
) and using the series representation of
exp_{q}(wz)
we obtain

\begin{split}\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(zw)\,exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{4}}}{[n]_{q}!}(wz)^{n}\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{4}}\,\widetilde{\beta}_{n-k}(q)z^{k}.\end{split}

This implies

(3.3)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}\,\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}_{q}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{4}}\,\widetilde{\beta}_{n-k}(q)z^{k}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}.

Comparing the coefficient of
\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}
, we obtain the required result.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;1/q),

(3.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(1/q).

Proof.

By replacing
q
by
1/q
on the generating function in (
3.1
), and then using Equation (
2.3
) we obtain

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;1/q)\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}.

Equating the coefficients of
w^{n}
yields (
3.4
) and substituting with
z=0
in (
3.4
) yields directly (
3.5
).
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The
q
-Bernoulli polynomials satisfy the
q
-difference equation

(3.6)

\frac{\delta_{q}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)}{\delta_{q}z}=[n]_{q}\,\widetilde{B}_{n-1}(z;q)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Proof.

Calculating the
q
-derivative
\delta_{q}
of the two sides of (
3.1
) with respect to the variable
z
and using Equation (
2.7
), we obtain

\dfrac{w^{2}exp_{q}(zw)exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{q}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)}{\delta_{q}z}\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}.

This implies

(3.7)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{q}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z;q)}{\delta_{q}z}\,\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{n-1}(z;q)\,\frac{w^{n}}{[n-1]_{q}!}.

Equating the corresponding
n
th power of
w
in the two series of (
3.7
), we obtain
the required result.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

The
q

(3.8)

\widetilde{\beta}_{1}(q)=-\frac{1}{2},\quad\widetilde{\beta}_{2n+1}(q)=0;\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Observe that,

\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}=-w+\dfrac{w\,exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}{exp_{q}(\frac{w}{2})-exp_{q}(\frac{-w}{2})}.

So, we can write Equation (

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)}{[n]_{q}!}w^{n}=-w+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)}{[n]_{q}!}(-w)^{n}.

This implies

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-(-1)^{n})\,\widetilde{\beta}_{n}(q)\,\frac{w^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}=-w.

Therefore,
\widetilde{\beta}_{1}(q)=-\frac{1}{2}
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{2n+1}(q)=0
for every

∎

Proposition 3.10

Theorem 3.12
.

\Big(\Psi_{n+1}/\Psi_{n}\Big)\downarrow 0
(see
[
2
,
14
]
). We denote by

\mathcal{R}_{\Psi}
the class of all entire functions
f
such that, for some numbers
\tau
,

(5.6)

|f(re^{i\theta})|\leq M\Psi(\tau\,r),

as
r\rightarrow\infty
. Here, the complex variable
z
was written as
z=re^{i\theta}
to emphasize that the limit must hold in all directions
\theta
. The infimum of numbers
\tau
for which (
5.6
) holds is the
\Psi
-type of the function
f
. This type can be computed by applying Nachbin’s theorem
[
14
]
which states that a function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{n}z^{n}
is of
\Psi
-type
\tau
if and only if

\tau=\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big|\frac{f_{n}}{\Psi_{n}}\Big|^{\frac{1}{n}}.

In
[
2
]
, the authors applied Nachbin’s theorem for the generalized Borel transform

F(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{f_{n}}{\Psi_{n}w^{n+1}},

and they proved the following result.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
f(z)
belong to the class

\mathcal{R}_{\Psi}
, and let
D(f)
be the closed set consists of the
union of the set of all singular points of
F
and the set of all points
exterior to the domain of
F
. Then

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\Psi(zw)F(w)\,dw

where
\Gamma
encloses
D(f)
.

According to the above arguments and results we will prove the main theorem.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
S_{1}
be the smallest positive zero of
S_{q}(z)
. Assume that one of the following conditions hold:

(i)

The function
f(z)
is an entire function of

q^{-1}
-exponential growth of order
2
and a finite type
\alpha
, where

(5.7)

\alpha<2\,\Big(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\log S_{1}}{\log q}\Big).

(ii)

The function
f(z)
is an entire function of
q^{-1}
-
exponential growth of order less than
2
.

Then
f(z)
has a convergent
q
-Lidstone representation

f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\widetilde{A}_{n}(z)\,\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(1)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}-\widetilde{B}_{n}(z)\,\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(0)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}\right],

where
\widetilde{A}_{n}(z)
is the polynomial of degree
2n+1
defined in
(
5.2
) and

\widetilde{B}_{n}(z):=\frac{2^{2n+1}}{[2n+1]_{q}!}\widetilde{B}_{2n+1}(z/2;q).

Proof.

We apply Theorem
5.9
when
\Psi(z)
chosen as
exp_{q}(z)
and

\Psi_{n}=\frac{q^{\frac{n(n-1)}{4}}}{[n]_{q}!}.

Notice, the sequence

\dfrac{\Psi_{n+1}}{\Psi_{n}}=\frac{q^{n/2}(1-q)}{1-q^{n+1}}=\frac{q^{n/2}}{[n+1]_{q}}

is decreasing and vanishes at
\infty
. By using Proposition
5.3
, we have for any entire function
f(z)=\sum_{=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
of
q^{-1}
- exponential growth of order
k
and a finite type
\alpha
, there exists a real number
K>0
such that

|a_{n}|\leq Kq^{\frac{(n-\alpha)^{2}}{2k}}.

According to the assumption, we have two cases:

Case 1. If
k=2
, then
|a_{n}|\leq Kq^{\frac{(n-\alpha)^{2}}{4}}
. This implies (
5.6
) holds and
f\in\mathcal{R}_{\Psi}
. Here, the
\Psi
-type of the function
f
given by

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big|\frac{a_{n}}{\Psi_{n}}\Big|^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{q^{\frac{1}{4}-\alpha/2}}{(1-q)}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big(K\,(q;q)_{n}q^{\alpha^{2}/4}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q^{\frac{1}{4}-\alpha/2}<S_{1}.

Case 2. If
k<2
, then
\tau=0
.

So, we can take
D(f)
lies in the closed disk
|w|\leq\tau\leq q^{\frac{1}{4}-\alpha/2}<S_{1}
and take the curve
\Gamma
as the circle
|w|=\tau+\epsilon<S_{1}
,
\epsilon>0
which encloses
D(f)
. Note that the inequality
q^{\frac{1}{4}-\alpha/2}<S_{1}
satisfies the condition (
5.7
) on the type of
f(z)
. We obtain

f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}exp_{q}(zw)F(w)\,dw.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(0)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}\,exp_{q}(zw)|_{z=0}\,F(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}w^{2n}\,F(w)\,dw,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(1)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma}w^{2n}\,exp_{q}(w)\,F(w)\,dw

Now, by using Proposition
5.8
we have

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi\,i}\int_{\Gamma}exp_{q}(zw)\,F(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\,i}\int_{\Gamma}\left\{exp_{q}(w)\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{A}_{n}(z)w^{2n}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widetilde{B}_{n}(z)w^{2n}\right\}F(w)\,dw

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[\widetilde{A}_{n}(z)\,\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(1)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}-\widetilde{B}_{n}(z)\,\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(0)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}\right].

∎

Remark 5.11
.

In Theorem
5.10
, it is obvious if

\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(0)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}=\frac{\delta^{2n}_{q}\,f(1)}{\delta_{q}z^{2n}}=0,\,\,(n\in\mathbb{N})

then
f(z)
is identically zero.

The following example shows that the sign of equality can not be admitted in (
5.7
).

Example 5.12
.

Consider
f(z)=S_{q}(S_{1}z)
. Then
f
is an entire function of

-exponential growth of order
2
and a finite type

This implies the
q
-Lidstone expansion of

vanishes identically but the function does not.

Example 5.13
.

7.

The

]
, the authors introduced these polynomials which were

In this paper, we presented

-Lidstone expansion theorem
[
8
]

This work provides the basis for several applications that we can search in the future. Firstly, we are interested in studying the generalization of

-Lidstone’s series. The analogous problem for the classical case was studied in

by Whittaker. Secondly, we are interested in constructing the

-Fourier series for the

-Lidstone polynomials

and

12
]

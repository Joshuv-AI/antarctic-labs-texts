Self-consistent homogenization approach for polycrystals within second gradient elasticity
Y.O. Solyaev

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a generalized variant of Kröner’s self-consistent scheme for evaluation of the effective standard and gradient elastic moduli of polycrystalline materials within Mindlin-Toupin second-gradient elasticity theory. Assuming random orientation of crystallites (grains) we use an extended Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion method and mapping conditions between the prescribed linear distribution of macro-strain and corresponding micro-scale field variables averaged over the volume and all possible orientations of single grain. It is found that developed self-consistent scheme predicts the absence of strong gradient effects at the macro-scale level for the model of spherical grains. However, for the more general shape of the grains, considered approach allows to obtain a set of non-linear relations for determination of all effective standard and gradient elastic moduli of polycrystals.

In order to found the relations between the effective standard and gradient elastic moduli of isotropic polycrystal and the elastic properties of its anisotropic grains we consider the self-consistent scheme, which implies that each grain is regarded as an inclusion embedded in the homogeneous effective medium represented by the surrounding grains.
We propose to consider the linear distribution of macroscopic strain field (instead of classical homogeneous strain field). Introducing the generalized mapping conditions between the averaged strain and second gradient of displacement inside the single grain and corresponding macroscopic quantities we derive the system of seven non-linear equations for determination of the effective material constants of SGET. Averaged field variables inside the single grain are determined based on the extended Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion method that was proposed within SGET for the case of linear distribution of eigenstrain in Refs.
[

,
28
,
21

Based on the obtained results it is shown, that the non-spherical shape of randomly oriented grains can be the source of strong strain gradient effects at the macro-scale level of polycrystals, while for the spherical Cauchy-type grains the method reduces to the classical self-consistent homogenization scheme and trivial solution for the effective gradient moduli.

Second-gradient elasticity theory

Consider the formulation of the second-gradient elasticity theory for the macroscopically isotropic linear elastic material occupying a region
V
. The strain energy density within Mindlin Form I is given by
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle w(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon},\boldsymbol{\kappa})=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}:\textbf{C}:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}\,\vdots\,\textbf{G}\,\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}

(1)

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\textbf{u}+(\nabla\textbf{u})^{T})
is an infinitesimal strain tensor,
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\nabla\nabla\textbf{u}
is the second gradient of displacement;
\textbf{u}(\textbf{x})
is the displacements vector at a point
x
;
\nabla
is nabla operator;
:
and
\vdots
define double and triple contractions between tensors, respectively;
C
and
G
are the fourth-order and sixth-order isotropic tensors, which components are given by
[
10
,
28
]
:

\displaystyle C_{ijkl}=\lambda\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+2\mu I_{ijkl}

(2)

\displaystyle G_{ijklmn}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{a_{1}}{2}(I_{lmin}\delta_{jk}+I_{lmjn}\delta_{ik})+\tfrac{a_{2}}{2}(I_{ijkn}\delta_{lm}+I_{lmkn}\delta_{ij})

(3)

\displaystyle+2a_{3}\delta_{ij}\delta_{lm}\delta_{kn}+a_{4}I_{ijlm}\delta_{kn}+\tfrac{a_{5}}{2}(I_{ijln}\delta_{km}+I_{ijmn}\delta_{kl}))

where
\lambda
and
\mu
are the standard Lame constants;
a_{i}
(
i=1...5
) are the additional material constants of gradient theory that can be denoted as Mindlin second-gradient elastic constants or gradient moduli;
\delta_{ik}
is Kronecker delta, i.e. the second-order identity tensor (which will be denoted as
\textbf{I}^{(2)}
);
I_{ijkl}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})
is the fourth-order isotropic identity tensor (which will be denoted as
\textbf{I}^{(4)}
) and in the following we will also use the six-order isotropic identity tensor
\textbf{I}^{(6)}
, which components are given by
(\textbf{I}^{(6)})_{ijklmn}=I_{ijlm}\delta_{kn}
.

By using standard variational procedure, the following statement of the boundary value problem for the bodies with smooth surface
S
without edges can be obtained within SGET
[
10
]
:

\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\tau}-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu})+\bar{\boldsymbol{b}}=0,\qquad&\textbf{x}\in V\\
\textbf{n}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\tau}-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu})-\nabla_{S}\cdot(\textbf{n}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mu})-H\,\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}=\bar{\boldsymbol{t}},\,\,\,or\,\,\,\textbf{u}=\bar{\textbf{u}}_{s},&\textbf{x}\in S\\
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}=\bar{\boldsymbol{m}}\,\,\,or\,\,\,\partial_{n}\textbf{u}=\bar{\boldsymbol{g}},\quad&\textbf{x}\in S\end{cases}

(4)

where the bar symbol denotes the corresponding quantities prescribed in the body volume and on its surface;

\boldsymbol{\tau}=\partial w/\partial\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}=\textbf{C}:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
is standard stress tensor;
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\partial w/\partial\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\textbf{G}\,\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}
is the third-order double stress tensor;
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{n}=(\textbf{n}\otimes\textbf{n}):\boldsymbol{\mu}
is the surface double-traction;

\nabla_{S}=\nabla-\textbf{n}\partial_{n}
is the surface gradient operator;
\partial_{n}
is the normal gradient;

H=-\nabla_{S}\cdot\textbf{n}
is twice the mean curvature of
S
;

n
is the unit outward normal vector on the boundary
S
.

Discussion on the physical meaning of non-classical constants and stresses as well as on the formulation of SGET can be found elsewhere
[
12
,
29
]
.

3
Self-consistent second-gradient homogenization for polycrystals

Let us consider a polycrystalline material that is an assembly of homogeneous grains with the same anisotropic centrosymmetric elastic properties, defined by constitutive tensors
\textbf{C}^{gr}
and
\textbf{G}^{gr}
within SGET (the structure of constitutive relations for anisotropic materials within SGET can be found in Refs.
[
30
,
31
,
32
]
). We assume that grains have random crystallographic orientations, i.e. that polycrystal has no texture and it is macroscopically isotropic
[
2
,
33
]
. The shapes of grains in a simplest model is approximated by a sphere with mean radius
R
. In more general case, grains shape can be approximated by spheroid or ellipsoid
[
33
]
. The overall elastic behaviour of polycrystall is also described within SGET and its constitutive tensors
C
and
G
are defined by (
2
), (
3
). The goal of homogenization is to derive the relations between the effective constants in tensors
C
,
G
and given properties of grains (
\textbf{C}^{gr}
,
\textbf{G}^{gr}
, grains size and shape).

Following self-consistent approach, we consider single anisotropic grain embedded in the infinite effective isotropic medium with yet unknown properties
C
,
G
. The grain has ellipsoidal shape and the origin of coordinate system is placed at its center. Linear distribution of a strain field is imposed at the macro-scale level so that the displacements, the strain and the second gradient of displacement are given by:

\displaystyle\textbf{u}(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0}\cdot\textbf{x}+\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}:(\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{x}),

(5)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0}+\hat{\boldsymbol{\kappa}}^{0}\cdot\textbf{x},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\textbf{x})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0}
is constant symmetric second-order tensor (
\varepsilon^{0}_{ij}=\varepsilon^{0}_{ji}
), which defines the mean strain field at the macro-scale level; and
\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}
is constant third-order tensor (
\kappa^{0}_{ijk}=\kappa^{0}_{jik}
), which defines the second gradient of displacement at the macro-scale level; the hat symbol defines symmetrization so that
\hat{\kappa}^{0}_{ijk}=\tfrac{1}{2}({\kappa}^{0}_{ijk}+{\kappa}^{0}_{ikj})
.

Adopted linear variation of strain field (
5
) can be realized in the Minlind-type equivalent homogeneous material by definition of the so-called quadratic boundary conditions and distributed volume forces (see
[
34
,
35
]
). According to Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion method generalized for SGET
[
27
,
28
,
21
]
, we can state that under prescribed remote conditions (
5
) the averaged strain field and averaged second gradient of displacement inside the grain can be defined as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\rangle_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v}:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0},

(6)

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{gr}\rangle_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v}\,\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

where
v
denotes the domain occupied by the grain, which volume is
|v|
;
\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v}
is the fourth-order strain concentration (localization) tensor averaged over the grain volume;
\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v}
is the averaged six-order concentration tensor for the second gradient of displacement averaged over the grain volume; and we will use the notation with angle brackets for the averaged quantities:

\langle f\rangle_{v}=\tfrac{1}{|v|}\int_{v}f(\textbf{x})\text{d}v

Averaged concentration tensors can be represented based on the Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion method as follows
[
27
,
28
,
21
]
:

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v}=\left(\textbf{I}^{(4)}+\langle\textbf{S}^{(4)}\rangle_{v}:(\textbf{C})^{-1}:(\textbf{C}^{gr}-\textbf{C})\right)^{-1}

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v}=\left(\textbf{I}^{(6)}+\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}\,\vdots\,(\textbf{G})^{-1}\,\vdots\,(\textbf{G}^{gr}-\textbf{G})\right)^{-1}

where
\textbf{S}^{(4)}
,
\textbf{S}^{(6)}
are the position-dependent Eshelby-like tensors that can be derived based on the solutions of inclusion problems within SGET
[
27
,
28
]
.

Note, that in classical Kröner’s homogenization method the single relation for the local strain (stress) field inside the grain is involved under prescribed remote homogeneous boundary conditions for the overall strain (stress) state
[
2
,
1
,
33
]
. In the present case we propose an extended approach with additional condition for the averaged second gradient of displacement (
6
)
2
under prescribed linear distribution of strain. One can also use the similar additional condition for the gradient of strain within Mindlin Form II, while in the present case we use Form I due to known explicit representation of the six-order tensor
\textbf{S}^{(6)}
in this theory
[
28
]
.

Let us now average Eq. (
6
) with respect to all possible crystallographic orientations of the grain axes to obtain:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v,\Omega}:\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0},\qquad\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{gr}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v,\Omega}\,\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

(8)

where
\langle...\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\langle\langle...\rangle_{v}\rangle_{\Omega}
denotes the averaging over the volume and orientations of axes of grain, that is:

\langle f\rangle_{\Omega}=\tfrac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{\Omega}f(\textbf{x})\text{d}\Omega

where
d\Omega=\sin\theta\,\text{d}\phi\,\text{d}\theta\,\text{d}\psi
, and
\phi
,
\theta
and
\psi
are the Euler angles.

The main idea of the method is the observation that the strain field and the second gradient of displacement averaged over the volume and orientations of grain should be equal to the corresponding prescribed macro-scale fields:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0},\qquad\langle\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{gr}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

(9)

Combining (
8
) and (
9
) we obtain the basic equations of the proposed second-gradient self-consistent method:

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\textbf{I}^{(4)},\qquad\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\textbf{I}^{(6)}

(10)

or by using relations for the concentration tensors (
7
), we have:

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\textbf{I}^{(4)}+\langle\textbf{S}^{(4)}\rangle_{v}:\textbf{C}^{-1}:(\textbf{C}^{gr}-\textbf{C})\right)^{-1}\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\textbf{I}^{(4)}

(11)

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\textbf{I}^{(6)}+\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}\,\vdots\,\textbf{G}^{-1}\,\vdots\,(\textbf{G}^{gr}-\textbf{G})\right)^{-1}\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\textbf{I}^{(6)}

System (
11
) could be reduced to seven coupled non-linear equations for estimation of seven unknown moduli of effective medium
\lambda
,
\mu
(
2
) and

(
i=1...5
) (
3
) in the following way. Averaging over all directions on the left hand side in relations (
10
) and (
11
) implies the equality of the linear invariants of the concentration tensors to the corresponding isotropic identity tensors

2
,
8
]
. Therefore, condition for strain concentration (
10
)
1
reduces to two equations:

(\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v})_{iijj}=I_{iijj}=3,\qquad(\langle\textbf{A}\rangle_{v})_{ijij}=I_{ijij}=6

(\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v})_{ijjikk}=I_{ijjikk}=6,\quad(\langle\textbf{B}\rangle_{v})_{iikkjj}=I_{iikkjj}=3,

Note, that relations (

4
Solution for the Cauchy-type spherical grains

Let us assume that the gradient moduli of crystallites are very small (
\textbf{G}^{gr}\approx 0
) and has spherical shape. The first assumption is realistic and it bases on the known results of atomistic calculations for the gradient moduli of monocrystals, for which it was found that the the length scale parameters of SGET has sub-nanometer order
[
38
,
39
]
. The assumption about spherical shape of grains is just an approximation of the model that is widely used within classical micromechanics
[
2
,
33
]
. For the considered case of Cauchy-type grains, the relation for the averaged second gradient of displacement (
11
)
2
is reduced to:

\left\langle\left(\textbf{I}^{(6)}-\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}\right)^{-1}\right\rangle_{\Omega}=\textbf{I}^{(6)}

(12)

In the left hand side of this relation we have isotropic identity tensor and Eshelby-like tensor that is also isotropic in the case of spherical inclusions
[
28
]
. Therefore, the procedure of averaging over orientations can be omitted and this relation can be immediately reduced to the following condition:

\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}=0

(13)

From the known representation of tensor
\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}
for spherical inclusions within SGET (see
[
28
]
) it follows that all effective gradient moduli should be zero
a_{i}=0
to provide the fulfilment of obtained condition (
13
). Moreover, the fourth-order Eshelby tensor becomes classical in this case (
\langle\textbf{S}^{(4)}\rangle_{v}=\textbf{S}^{(4)}
, see
[
28
,
27
]
) and the first homogenization condition (
11
)
1
reduces to those one of classical Kröner’s method for the effective Lame constants
\lambda
and
\mu
. Thus, for the case of low gradient moduli and spherical shape of grains the presented second-gradient self-consistent method provides classical solution for the effective polycrystalline media with the absence of strain gradient effects.

5
Possible improvements of self-consistent estimates within SGET

The main next step that should be performed within the considered approach is the derivation of the solution for the grains of a spheroidal shape. In this case the averaged six-order Eshelby tensor in condition (
11
)
2
will be non-isotropic and we can obtain the non-trivial system of seven coupled equations for determination of the effective gradient moduli and Lame constants of polycrystals. This system can be additionally simplified without much loss of accuracy assuming infinitely small values of individual gradient moduli of grains and using (
12
) instead of (
11
)
2
. However, for the best of author’s knowledge, up to date the closed form expressions for tensor
\langle\textbf{S}^{(6)}\rangle_{v}
have been established only for the spherical and circular inclusions within SGET
[
28
]
. Thus, we want to stress out the need of such studies for the more general shape of inclusions in the future work.

Also it is notable that Kröner’s self-consistent method can be extended not only for the case of linear distribution of macro-scale strain field (
5
) but for the more general external conditions. Consideration of the
N
-th order polynomial distribution of the strain field together with additional mapping conditions for the averaged gradients of displacement up to (
N
+1)-th order (or gradients of strain field up to
N
-th order) will allow to derive the relations for the effective material constants of polycristals within high-grade elasticity theories in a similar manner that it is done within Mindlin Form I in the present contribution.

There may also arise a question about possibility of introduction of additional mapping conditions between the micro- and macro- strain fields. Without dwelling on the energy-based conditions that were discussed, e.g. in Refs.
[
40
,
34
]
, let us consider the possible additional kinematical measure evaluated as averaged strain field multiplied with position vector:
\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}
. Such quantity arises in the second-gradient self-consistent homogenization of composites
[
21
]
and its static counterpart is an averaged first static moment of stress
\langle\boldsymbol{\tau}^{gr}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}

[
27
,
40
]
. Based on the extended Eshelby’s equivalent inclusion method it was shown that under prescribed quadratic boundary conditions (
5
) the following relation is valid within SGET
[
21
]
:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}

\displaystyle=\langle\tilde{\textbf{B}}\rangle_{v}\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

(14)

where
\langle\tilde{\textbf{B}}\rangle_{v}
is the six-order concentration tensor of special kind, which explicit representation within SGET was given in Ref.
[
21
]
; for the case of Cauchy-type inclusion it was shown that this tensor is simply defined as
\langle\tilde{\textbf{B}}\rangle_{v}=\textbf{I}^{(6)}\cdot\langle\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}
. Therefore, from (
14
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}

\displaystyle=(\textbf{I}^{(6)}\cdot\langle\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v})\vdots\,\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}=\tfrac{1}{5}R^{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

(15)

where we assume that the grain is spherical so that
\langle\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v}=\tfrac{1}{5}R^{2}\textbf{I}^{(2)}
.

Thus, the averaging procedure and mapping to the corresponding macro-scale field become trivial for this measure:

\displaystyle\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{gr}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v,\Omega}\equiv\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\textbf{x})\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\langle\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{0}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v,\Omega}+\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}\langle\textbf{x}\otimes\textbf{x}\rangle_{v,\Omega}=\tfrac{1}{5}R^{2}\boldsymbol{\kappa}^{0}

(16)

where we take into account that the origin of coordinates is placed at the center of spherical grain. Similar trivial result can be also obtained for more complex shape of randomly oriented grains so that we can state that within the proposed second-gradient homogenization approach it is enough to use only two mapping conditions (
9
).

6
Conclusions

In this note we suggest a framework for determination of the effective second-gradient Mindlin elastic properties of polycrystalline materials. Kröner’s self-consistent scheme is extended assuming that the equivalent effective medium can be described by Mindlin-Toupin SGET. Additional micro-macro scale mapping condition for the second gradient of displacement is introduced.
Considered approach predicts absence of gradient effects for the case of randomly oriented spherical grains. However, more general shape of grains (e.g. spheroidal) could be the source of non-local gradient effects in the material microstructure. A set of non-linear relations given by Eqs. (
11
) can be used for determination of the effective standard and gradient moduli of polycrystalline materials. Corresponding explicit representation for the six-order Eshelby-like tensors should derived within SGET and used in such calculations.

Importance and practical application of the obtained results can be related to the description of size-effects in fracture of quasi-brittle polycristaline materials by using estimated values of gradient moduli
[
14
,
15
]
and also for the micromechanical validation of the known phenomenological simplified constitutive models of SGET
[
41
,
32
]
.

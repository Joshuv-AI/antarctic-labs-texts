High Probability Latency Quickest Change Detection over a Finite Horizon
Yu-Han Huang 1 and Venugopal V. Veeravalli 5

Abstract
THIS PAPER IS ELIGIBLE FOR THE STUDENT PAPER AWARD. A finite horizon variant of the quickest change detection problem is studied, in which the goal is to minimize a delay threshold (latency), under constraints on the probability of false alarm and the probability that the latency is exceeded. In addition, the horizon is not known to the change detector. A variant of the cumulative sum (CuSum) test with a threshold that increasing logarithmically with time is proposed as a candidate solution to the problem. An information-theoretic lower bound on the minimum value of the latency under the constraints is then developed. This lower bound is used to establish certain asymptotic optimality properties of the proposed test in terms of the horizon and the false alarm probability. Some experimental results are given to illustrate the performance of the test.



I
Introduction

The problem of detecting changes or anomalies in stochastic systems and time series, often referred to as the quickest change detection (QCD) problem, arises in various engineering and scientific settings. The observations of the system are assumed to undergo a change in distribution at the change-point, and the goal is to detect this change as soon as possible, subject to false alarm constraints. See
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
for books and survey articles on the topic.

The QCD problem is formulated mathematically as a constrained optimization problem to minimize a measure of detection
delay, subject to a constraint on an appropriate false alarm metric. The most commonly used false alarm metric is the
mean time to false alarm
, or its reciprocal, the
false alarm rate
. Detection delay is generally measured by considering the
expected detection delay
, conditioned on the change-point and possibly the history of observations before the change-point, and taking the supremum over all possible change-points over an infinite horizon. However, these metrics for false alarm and detection delay might not be appropriate in many applications.
Our work is inspired by the regret analysis for piecewise stationary bandits in
[
5
]
, in which
the metrics used for detecting changes in the bandit environment are the
probability of false alarm
and the
probability of detection delay exceeding a threshold
. Furthermore, the false alarm and delay events are only relevant over a
finite horizon
, and the horizon is not known to the change detector.

We therefore pose a variant of the QCD optimization problem in which the goal is to minimize the delay threshold (
latency
), under constraints on the probability of false alarm and the probability that the latency is exceeded over a finite horizon, and under the assumption that the horizon is not known to the detector.
We develop a variant of the cumulative sum (CuSum) test with a time-varying threshold (TVT) as a candidate solution to the optimization problem. We further develop a lower bound on the minimum value of the latency under the constraints. We use the lower bound to establish certain asymptotic optimality properties of the TVT-CuSum test in terms of the horizon and the false alarm probability. We believe that a theoretical study of this variant of the QCD problem will be of relevance to the performance analysis of algorithms for piecewise stationary bandits and reinforcement learning that employ QCD tests to detect changes in the environment (see, e.g.,
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
).

To the best of our knowledge, there is no prior work on the variant of the QCD problem described in the previous paragraph. A related variant is investigated in
[
13
]
, where it is assumed that the observations are discrete (we do not require this assumption). In
[
13
]
, the desired latency is fixed and assumed to be known to the detector, and the goal is to maximize the horizon.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: The formulation of our QCD problem is given in Section
II
. Section
III
is devoted to the performance analysis of the TVT-CuSum test. In Section
IV
, we provide a lower bound, which is useful in establishing some asymptotic optimality properties of the TVT-CuSum test.
Numerical results that validate the analysis are given in Section
V
, and some concluding remarks are give in Section
VI
.

II
High Probability Low Latency Quickest Change Detection

Let
\{X_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of independent random vectors whose values are observed sequentially. For the change-point
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle X_{n}\sim\begin{cases}f_{0},\;n<\nu\\
f_{1},\;n\geq\nu\end{cases}.

(1)

In other words, before the change-point
\nu
, the observations follow the pre-change distribution with density
f_{0}
with respect to some dominating measure
\lambda
. The remaining observations follow the post-change distribution with density
f_{1}
with respect to the same dominating measure
\lambda
. We use
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
to denote the probability measure when the change-point occurs at
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
to denote the probability measure when there is no change-point (i.e.,
\nu=\infty
).

For horizon
T\in\mathbb{N}
, we observe the random vectors
X_{1},\dots,X_{T}
sequentially. Let
\tau
be the
stopping time
of a (causal) change detector. Our goal is to minimize the latency
d
, for which
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau\geq\nu+d\right)
is small for all
\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d\right\}
, while the probability of false alarm over the horizon
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau\leq T\right)
is small as well. Therefore, the optimization problem of interest has the following form:

\begin{split}&\underset{\tau}{\textrm{minimize}}\quad d\\
&\;\textrm{s.t.}\kern 5.0pt\quad\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau\leq T\right)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}\\
&\quad\quad\quad\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau\geq\nu+d\right)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{D}},\;\forall\,\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d\right\}\end{split}

(2)

where
\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\in\left(0,1\right)
are some (small) numbers. In addition, we require change detector (equivalently
\tau
) to be oblivious to the knowledge of the horizon
T
.

III
Performance of CUSUM Detector

For the standard Lorden formulation of the QCD problem
[
14
]
, the CuSum test is known to be optimal
[
15
]
. The CuSum test statistic is given by:

W_{n}=\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{f_{1}(X_{i})}{f_{0}(X_{i})}

(3)

which satisfies the recursion

W_{n}=\max\{W_{n-1},0\}+\log\frac{f_{1}(X_{n})}{f_{0}(X_{n})}

(4)

with
W_{0}=0
. The CuSum stopping time is given by:

\tilde{\tau}_{b}\coloneqq\inf\{n\in\mathbb{N}:W_{n}\geq b\}.

(5)

It is therefore natural to consider the CuSum test of (
5
) as a possible candidate for solving the problem of interest given in (
2
). However, for the CuSum test with a constant threshold
b
, the false alarm probability
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tilde{\tau}_{b}<T\right)
goes to
1
as the horizon
T
goes to infinity. This is because
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\tilde{\tau}_{b}\right]<\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
14
]
). Therefore, the false alarm probability cannot be controlled to a given level
\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
without the knowledge of
T
.

Taking a cue from the analysis in
[
16
]
, we will show that if we let the threshold
b
in (
5
) increase logarithmically with
n
, the false alarm probability remains upper bounded by a constant for all
T\in\mathbb{N}
. This leads to the following modified version of the CuSum test
\tau_{r}
, which we refer to as the time-varying threshold CuSum (TVT-CuSum) test:

\displaystyle\tau_{r}\coloneqq\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\;W_{n}\geq\beta\left(n,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},r\right)\right\},\;r>1

(6)

where

\beta\left(n,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},r\right)\coloneqq\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{n^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

(7)

with
\zeta\left(r\right)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{i^{r}}
. Then, the following theorem shows that the TVT-CuSum test satisfies the false alarm probability constraint. Note that
\beta
is not a function of the horizon
T
.

Theorem 1

(TVT-CuSum Test: False Alarm Probability)
.

For any horizon
T\in\mathbb{N}
and
r>1
,

\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau_{r}\leq T\right)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}.

(8)

Proof.

First, we can upper bound the probability of false alarm as follows: For all
T\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau_{r}\leq T\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau_{r}<\infty\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\exists\,n\in\mathbb{N}:\;W_{n}\geq\beta\left(n,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},r\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\exists\,j\leq n:\;\sum_{i=j}^{n}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)\geq\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{n^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\exists\,j\leq n:\;\prod_{i=j}^{n}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\geq\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{n^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\Bigg(\exists\,j\in\mathbb{N},\;\exists\,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}:

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\prod_{i=j}^{j+k}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\geq\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(j+k\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\Bigg(\exists\,j\in\mathbb{N},\;\exists\,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}:

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;\frac{1}{\left(j+k\right)^{r}}\prod_{i=j}^{j+k}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\geq\frac{\zeta\left(r\right)}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\Bigg).

(9)

Next, from (
9
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau_{r}\leq T\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\exists\,k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}:\;\frac{1}{\left(j+k\right)^{r}}\prod_{i=j}^{j+k}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\geq\frac{\zeta\left(r\right)}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{\zeta\left(r\right)}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{j^{r}}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{j}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{j}\right)}\right]=\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{\zeta\left(r\right)}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j^{r}}=\delta_{\mathrm{F}}

where step
(a)
results from union bound. Step
(b)
stems from Doob’s submartingale inequality
[
17
]
since
\left(\frac{1}{\left(j+k\right)^{r}}\prod_{i=j}^{j+k}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a supermartingale; this is shown in Lemma
1
, which, along with its proof, is given in Appendix
A
.
∎

Next, we analyze the high probability latency for the TVT-CuSum test, which is defined as follows:

\displaystyle d_{r}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf\left\{d\in\mathbb{N}:\;\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau_{r}\geq\nu+d\right)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\;\forall\,\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d\right\}\right\}.

(10)

For the purpose of our analysis, we define
\Lambda
to be the cumulant generating function of
\log\left(\frac{f_{0}\left(X\right)}{f_{1}\left(X\right)}\right)
with
X\sim f_{1}
, i.e.,

\Lambda\left(\theta\right)=\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{f_{1}}\left[\exp\left(\theta\log\left(\frac{f_{0}\left(X\right)}{f_{1}\left(X\right)}\right)\right)\right]\right).

(11)

The following theorem gives an upper bound on
d_{r}
.

Theorem 2

(High Probability Latency for TVT-CuSum Test)
.

For all
T\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\in\left(0,1\right)
,
r>1
,

\displaystyle d_{r}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\theta\in\left(0,1\right)}\bigg\{\frac{1}{\lvert\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\rvert}\bigg[\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)+\theta\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;+r\theta\log\left(T\right)+\theta\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\bigg]\bigg\}.

(12)

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
T\in\mathbb{N}
and arbitrary
\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\in\left(0,1\right)
.
First, it is easy to show that
\Lambda\left(0\right)=\Lambda\left(1\right)=0
.
Since
f_{1}\neq f_{0}
,
\Lambda
is strictly convex, and therefore,
\Lambda\left(\theta\right)<0

\forall\,\theta\in\left(0,1\right)
. Next, by the definition of
\tau_{r}
,
\forall\,d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\forall\,\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d\right\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau_{r}\geq\nu+d\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\inf\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\;W_{n}\geq\beta\left(n,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},r\right)\right\}\geq\nu+d\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\Bigg(\forall\,n\in\left\{1,\dots,\nu+d-1\right\}:

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\;\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\sum_{i=j}^{n}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)<\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{n^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\bigg).

(13)

Then, we have
\forall\,\theta\in\left(0,1\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau_{r}\geq\nu+d\right)

(14)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\Bigg(\max_{1\leq j\leq\nu+d-1}\sum_{i=j}^{\nu+d-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad<\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(\nu+d\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)<\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(\nu+d\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(-\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)>-\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(\nu+d\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\exp\left(-d\left(-\frac{1}{d}\theta\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(\nu+d\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)-\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{\left(\nu+d\right)^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)^{\theta}\exp\left(d\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\right)

(15)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{T^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)^{\theta}\exp\left(d\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\right)

(16)

where step
(a)
is due to the fact that
\left\{\nu+d\right\}\subseteq\left\{1,\dots,\nu+d\right\}
, while step
(b)
is owing to the fact that
\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\nu+d-1}\sum_{i=j}^{\nu+d-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)
. Step
(c)
stems from the Chernoff bound
[
18
]
, and step
(d)
is due to the fact that
\nu+d\leq T
.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{d}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\lvert\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\rvert}\bigg[

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{D}}}\right)+\theta\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle+r\theta\log\left(T\right)+\theta\log\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\right)\bigg].

(17)

Then, according to (
16
), we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau_{r}>\nu+\tilde{d}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\zeta\left(r\right)\frac{T^{r}}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)^{\theta}\exp\left(\tilde{d}\Lambda\left(\theta\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mathrm{D}}.

(18)

By the definition of
d_{r}
,
d_{r}\leq\tilde{d}
, and thus (
12
) holds.
∎

Theorem
2
shows that
d_{r}=\mathcal{O}\left(\log T\right)
. In the next section, we demonstrate that this growth of
d_{r}
with
T
is order optimal when
T
is large and
\delta_{\mathrm{F}}+\delta_{\mathrm{D}}<1
.

IV
Asymptotic Information-Theoretic Lower Bound on the High Probability Detection Delay

Let
\tau^{*}
be the solution to (
2
) and
d^{*}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)
be the corresponding minimum value. In this section, we present a lower bound for
d^{*}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)
. For the purpose of our analysis, we define the following constant

\displaystyle C\coloneqq\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{f_{1}}\left[\frac{f_{1}\left(X\right)}{f_{0}\left(X\right)}\right]\right).

(19)

It is easy to see that
C>0
using Jensen’s inequality. Furthermore, we assume that
C<\infty
.

Theorem 3

(Lower Bound for High Probability Latency)
.

For all
\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\in\left(0,1\right)
such that
\delta_{\mathrm{F}}+\delta_{\mathrm{D}}<1

\displaystyle d^{*}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)\geq\left(\frac{1}{C}+o\left(1\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left[\log\left(T\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)+\log\left(1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)+o\left(1\right)\right]

as
T\to\infty
.

Proof.

For
c>C
, define the events

\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\left\{\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*},\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\!\!\!\!\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)\geq d^{*}c\right\}

(20)

and

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\left\{\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*},\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\!\!\!\!\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)<d^{*}c\right\}.

(21)

We note that
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}=\left\{\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*}\right\}
, which we will use later in the proof.

From the problem formulation (
2
) we have that

\forall\,\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d^{*}\right\}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau^{*}\geq\nu+d^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\tau^{*}<\nu\right)-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau^{*}<\nu\right)-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau^{*}\leq T\right)-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)-\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\mathcal{A}\right).

(22)

Next, since
\tau^{*}
satisfies the false alarm probability constraint
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tau^{*}\leq T\right)\leq\delta_{\mathrm{F}}
, by Lemma
2
in Appendix
B
, there exists a change-point
\tilde{\nu}\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d^{*}\right\}
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tilde{\nu}\leq\tau^{*}<\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}\right)\leq\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{\left\lfloor T/d^{*}\right\rfloor}.

(23)

For this choice of
\tilde{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\nu}}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\prod_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}-1}f_{0}\left(x_{i}\right)\prod_{i=\tilde{\nu}}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}f_{1}\left(x_{i}\right)\otimes_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}d\lambda\left(x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{B}}\prod_{i=\tilde{\nu}}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}\frac{f_{1}\left(x_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(x_{i}\right)}\prod_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}f_{0}\left(x_{i}\right)\otimes_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}d\lambda\left(x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}\int_{\mathcal{B}}\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\prod_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}f_{0}\left(x_{i}\right)\otimes_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}d\lambda\left(x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\int_{\mathcal{B}}\prod_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}f_{0}\left(x_{i}\right)\otimes_{i=1}^{\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}-1}d\lambda\left(x_{i}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\mathcal{B}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\leq}\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left(\tilde{\nu}\leq\tau^{*}<\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(d)}{\leq}\frac{\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{\left\lfloor T/d^{*}\right\rfloor}

(24)

where step
(a)
results from the definition of
\mathcal{B}
, and step
(b)
stems from the fact that under
\mathbb{P}_{\infty}
, every
X_{i}
follows the density
f_{0}
. Step
(c)
is owing to the fact that
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\left\{\tilde{\nu}\leq\tau^{*}<\tilde{\nu}+d^{*}\right\}
, and step
(d)
is due to (
23
).

Then, for any
\nu\in\left\{1,\dots,T-d^{*}\right\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\nu\leq\tau^{*}<\nu+d^{*},\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)\geq d^{*}c\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\log\left(\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right)\geq d^{*}c\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left(\prod_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\geq e^{d^{*}c}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\leq}e^{-d^{*}c}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\prod_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-d^{*}c}\prod_{i=\nu}^{\nu+d^{*}-1}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}e^{-d^{*}\left(c-C\right)}

(25)

where step
(a)
results from the Markov inequality.
Step
(b)
is due to the definition of
C
, which is in the theorem statement.

Next, by plugging (
24
) and (
25
) into (
22
) with
\nu=\tilde{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{D}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{\left\lfloor T/d^{*}\right\rfloor}-e^{-d^{*}\left(c-C\right)}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{d^{*}\exp\left(d^{*}c\right)\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{T-d^{*}}-e^{-d^{*}\left(c-C\right)}.

(26)

By Theorem
1
and
2
,
d^{*}\leq d_{r}
for any
r>1
. Since
d_{r}
grows logarithmically with
T
,
d^{*}=\mathcal{O}\left(\log\left(T\right)\right)
; therefore, we can observe that for any
b\in\left(0,1\right)
,
d^{*}\leq bT
for
T
large enough. In addition, this
b
approaches
0
as
T
goes to infinity. Thus, for any
s\in\left(0,1\right)
,
T-d^{*}\geq sT
for
T
large enough, and
s
approaches
1
as
T
goes to infinity.

Furthermore,
\delta_{\mathrm{F}}+\delta_{\mathrm{D}}<1
,
d^{*}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)\rightarrow\infty

. Now, we choose

.

Again, since

as

, for any

goes to

goes to infinity. Hence, by (

), for
T
large enough, we have:

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{sT}\exp\left(\tilde{c}d^{*}\right)-\epsilon

(27)

where
\epsilon\in\left(0,1-\delta_{\textrm{F}}-\delta_{\textrm{D}}\right)
,
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
as
T\rightarrow\infty
. Rearranging the terms in (

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}{sT}\exp\left(\tilde{c}d^{*}\right)\geq 1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\delta_{\mathrm{D}}-\epsilon.

(28)

By taking

on both sides, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{c}d^{*}\geq

\displaystyle\log\left(T\right)

\displaystyle+\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)+\log\left(s\right)+\log\left(1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\delta_{\mathrm{D}}-\epsilon\right).

(29)

This implies that

\displaystyle d^{*}\left(T,\delta_{\mathrm{F}},\delta_{\mathrm{D}}\right)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}\log\left(T\right)+\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}\log\left(\frac{1}{\delta_{\mathrm{F}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}\log\left(s\right)+\frac{1}{\tilde{c}}\log\left(1-\delta_{\mathrm{F}}-\delta_{\mathrm{D}}-\epsilon\right).

(30)

Since
\tilde{c}\rightarrow c
,
c\rightarrow C
,

, and
\epsilon\rightarrow 0

∎

VI
Conclusion

We posed a new variant of the QCD problem with a finite horizon, in which the goal is to minimize the latency, under constraints on the probability of false alarm and the probability of the latency being exceeded. We proposed the TVT-CuSum test with a threshold that increases logarithmically with time as a candidate solution. We show that the growth of the high probability latency
d_{r}
of the TVT-CuSum test with
T
is order optimal, when
T
is large and
\delta_{\mathrm{F}}+\delta_{\mathrm{D}}<1
. As indicated in Section
V
, there might still be room for improvement for the lower bound given in Theorem
3
. We leave this refinement of the lower bound for future work. It is also of interest to generalize the analysis in this paper to the more practical scenario in which there is uncertainty in the pre- and post- change distributions.

Lemma 1
.

\forall\,r>1
, under the probability measure

, i.e.,

\displaystyle\quad\kern 5.0pt\cdot\prod_{i=n}^{n+m-1}\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{i}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{i}\right)}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\frac{f_{1}\left(X_{n+m}\right)}{f_{0}\left(X_{n+m}\right)}\bigg|X_{n},\dots,X_{n+m-1}\right]

Cosmological Constant from Equivalent Transformation in Quantum Cosmology
Andronikos Paliathanasis

Abstract
We explore the introduction of the cosmological constant via equivalent transformations in cosmology. We consider the Wheeler-DeWitt equation for the CDM universe and we construct the Hamilton-Jacobi action for the Λ \Lambda CDM model. We discuss how this approach allows us to relate different physical systems, providing insights into the role of the cosmological constant in cosmology.

The cosmological constant is associated with a constant dark energy density

In this work we deal with the Wheeler-DeWitt (WDW) formulation of quantum

We demonstrate the existence of a map, specifically a point transformation,
that links the WDW equations for the CDM and

illustrate this, we employ an Eisenhart lift and express the field equations

minisuperspace. By applying different quantum operators to solve the WDW

representing the CDM and
\Lambda

cosmological constant to emerge from quantum cosmology through the application
of a quantum operator. Consequently, we can construct new cosmological models
with varying values for the cosmological constant, starting from the
\Lambda
CDM model. The structure of the paper is outlined as follows.

II
\Lambda
CDM Cosmology

We consider a homogeneous and isotropic universe described by the spatially
flat FLRW line element

ds^{2}=-N^{2}\left(t\right)dt^{2}+a^{2}\left(t\right)\left(dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}\right).

(1)

Scalars
N\left(t\right)
,
a\left(t\right)
represent the lapse
function and the scale factor, respectively. The volume of the
three-dimensional space is defined as
V\left(t\right)=a\left(t\right)^{3}
, while the Hubble function reads
H\left(t\right)=\frac{1}{N}\frac{\dot{a}}{a}
.

We consider the gravitational action to be that of General Relativity we a
cosmological constant term

S_{GR}=\int dx^{4}\sqrt{-g}\left(R-2\Lambda\right)+\int dx^{4}\sqrt{-g}L_{m}

(2)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar for the background geometry,
\Lambda
is the
cosmological constant and
L_{m}
is the Lagrangian for the cold dark matter.

For the FLRW spacetime (
1
) the gravitational field equations are

3H^{2}-2\Lambda=\rho_{m},

(3)

\frac{2}{N}\dot{H}+3H^{2}-\Lambda=0,

(4)

while the conservation of energy for the cold dark matter reads

\dot{\rho}_{m}+3\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)\rho_{m}=0.

(5)

The latter equation gives
\rho_{m}=\rho_{m0}a^{-3}
.

Equations (
3
) and (
4
) form a Lagrangian system with
point-like Lagrangian function

L\left(N,a,\dot{a}\right)=\frac{3}{N}a\dot{a}^{2}+N\left(2\Lambda a^{3}+\rho_{m0}\right).

(6)

Friedmann’s equation (
3
) is the Hamiltonian function. We define the
momentum
p_{a}=\frac{6}{N}a\dot{a}
, then it reads

\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}\equiv N\left(\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{12a}-\left(2\Lambda a^{3}+\rho_{m0}\right)\right)=0.

(7)

The Hamiltonian function is of the form
\mathcal{H}=N\left(\gamma^{AB}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)p_{A}p_{B}-V\left(\mathbf{q}\right)\right)
, where
V\left(\mathbf{q}\right)
is the potential function,
and
\gamma^{AB}\left(\mathbf{q}\right)
is known as the minisuperspace.

II.1
Eisenhart-lift

The Eisenhart-lift describes a systematic method for the geometrization of
Hamiltonian dynamical systems
en1
. In Eisenhart’s geometric approach,
new additional degrees of freedom are introduced, which increase the dimension
of the space where the motion occurs, along with the simultaneous introduction
of conservation laws related to the isometries of the new extended geometry.
For some previous studies of the Eisenhart-lift in cosmology, see, for
instance
en2
;
en3
;
en4
.

We introduce the Hamiltonian function

\mathcal{H}_{lift}^{\Lambda}\equiv N\left(\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{12a}+p_{u}p_{v}-\left(\omega a^{3}+\mu\right)p_{v}^{2}\right)=0.

(8)

The equations of motion are

\frac{1}{N}\dot{a}=\frac{p_{a}}{6a}~,~\frac{1}{N}\dot{u}=p_{v}-2p_{u}~,~\frac{1}{N}\dot{v}=p_{u},

(9)

\frac{1}{N}\dot{p}_{a}=3\omega a^{2}p_{u}~,~\frac{1}{N}\dot{p}_{u}=0~,~\frac{1}{N}\dot{p}_{v}=0,

in which
p_{u}=p_{u}^{0}
and
p_{v}=p_{v}^{0}
are conservation laws.

The latter dynamical system describes the (null-) geodesic equations for the
three-dimensional minisuperspace with line element

ds_{lift}^{2}=6ada^{2}+2dudv+2\left(\omega a^{3}+\mu\right)du^{2}.

(10)

We remark that null-geodesics are invariant under conformal transformations,
thus the lapse function
N
play no role on the dynamics.

Moreover, application of the two conservation laws
p_{u}=p_{u}^{0}
and

p_{v}=p_{v}^{0}
in
\mathcal{H}_{lift}^{\Lambda}
leads to the the
Hamiltonian function
\mathcal{H}^{\Lambda}~
for the
\Lambda
CDM with
constraint equations

\rho_{m0}=\mu p_{v}^{0}-p_{u}^{0}p_{v}^{0}\text{ and }\Lambda=\frac{\omega}{2}\left(p_{u}^{0}\right)^{2}.

(11)

II.2
Wheeler-De Witt equation

When a minisuperspace description exist, the WDW equations follows from the
canonical quantization
p=-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
of the
Hamiltonian function. Because the field equations are invariant under
conformal transformations meaning the definition of
N
plays no role in the
dynamics and the conformal Laplace operator is considered instead of the
Laplace operator. Thus, the WDW equation belongs to the family of Yamabe equations.

Consider the Hamiltonian

\mathcal{H}\equiv N\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{AB}\left(q\right)p_{A}p_{B}+V\left(q\right)\right)=0

(12)

where
\gamma^{AB}\left(q\right)
is the minisuperspace of dimension

n=\dim\gamma^{AB}
and Ricci scalar
R_{\gamma}
the WDW equation reads

\Delta\Psi+\left(\frac{n-2}{4\left(n-1\right)}R_{\gamma}\left(q\right)-2V\left(q\right)\right)\Psi=0.

(13)

Operator
\Delta
is the Laplace operator defined as
\Delta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\left|\gamma\right|}}\frac{\partial}{\partial q^{A}}\left(\sqrt{\left|\gamma\right|}\gamma^{AB}\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{B}}\right)
. For
n=2
, equation (
13
) reduces to the usual
Klein-Gordon equation.

Let
\Psi\left(q\right)=A\left(q\right)e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}S\left(q\right)}
be a solution of equation (
13
), then in the WKB
approximation we end

\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{AB}\frac{\partial S}{\partial q^{A}}\frac{\partial S}{\partial q^{B}}+V\left(q\right)-\frac{n-2}{8\left(n-1\right)}R_{\gamma}\left(q\right)=0,

(14)

which is the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the Hamiltonian function
(
12
) with the quantum correction which follows from the
minisuperspace scalar. In cosmological studies, the minisuperspace solves the
Yamabe problem, specifically the minisuperspace is conformally flat

ccd1
; which means that the quantum corrections related to
R_{\gamma}

is eliminated.

III
Introducing
\Lambda
from quantum cosmology

Consider now in the Eisenhart-lift the Hamiltonian for the CDM model, that
is,

\mathcal{H}_{lift}^{CDM}\equiv N\left(\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{12a}+p_{u}p_{v}-\mu p_{v}^{2}\right)=0.

(15)

The corresponding WDW equation is

\frac{1}{6a}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial a^{2}}-\frac{1}{2a}\frac{\partial}{\partial a}\right)\Psi+\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial v}\right)\Psi=0.

(16)

There is an direct relation of the conservation laws for the classical system
and the symmetries of the Yamabe equation
as21
. Specifically, from the
two conservation laws
p_{u}=p_{u}^{0}
and
p_{v}=p_{v}^{0}
we can construct
the quantum operators

\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+p_{u}^{0}\right)\Psi=0~,~\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial v}+p_{v}^{0}\right)\Psi=0

(17)

which gives

\Psi=\bar{\Psi}\left(a\right)e^{-i\left(p_{u}^{0}u+p_{v}^{0}v\right)},

(18)

where now

\bar{\Psi}\left(a\right)=\bar{\Psi}_{1}\exp\left(4\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{m0}}{3}}a^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)+\bar{\Psi}_{2}\exp\left(-4\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{m0}}{3}}a^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)~,~\rho_{m0}=\mu\left(p_{v}^{0}\right)^{2}-p_{u}^{0}p_{v}^{0}.

(19)

The wavefunction is written in the form
~\Psi\simeq e^{-iS_{CDM}\left(a,u,v\right)}
, where function
S_{CDM}\left(a,u,v\right)

S_{CDM}\left(a,u,v\right)=\pm 4\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{m0}}{3}}a^{\frac{3}{2}}+\left(p_{u}^{0}u+p_{v}^{0}v\right),

(20)

is nothing else than the action for the CDM model. Function
S_{CDM}\left(a,u,v\right)~
solves the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for the classical system
(
15
).

The WDW equation (
16
) admits additional symmetries, which can be used
to construct quantum operators different from that of (
17
). There are
ten independent nontrivial linear operators which can constructed by the
symmetries of equation (
16
). These linear operators are related to the
conformal symmetries of the three-dimensional flat minisuperspace.

Consider the conformal symmetry of the minisuperspace

X=2ua\frac{\partial}{\partial a}+3\left(1+u^{2}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial u}+\left(2\frac{\mu}{\beta}+3+6\mu-\left(3\mu u^{2}+2a^{3}\right)\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial v}

(21)

where
\beta
is a constant.

We determine the canonical variables

a=\frac{\alpha}{\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\beta}U\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}}~,~u=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}\tan\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\beta}U\right)~,~v=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{3\beta}}\left(\mu U+V\right)-\frac{\sqrt{\mu}}{3}\left(3+4\alpha^{3}\right)\tan\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\beta}U\right).

(22)

The WdW equation (
16
) reads

\frac{1}{6\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\alpha^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}\right)\Psi+\frac{\partial}{\partial V}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial U}+\left(\mu-2\beta\alpha^{3}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial V}\right)\Psi=0,

(23)

and the conformal symmetry becomes
X=\partial_{V}
. Thus, from the symmetry

\partial_{U}
we can define the quantum operators

\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial U}+p_{U}^{0}\right)\Psi=0~,~\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial V}+p_{V}^{0}\right)\Psi=0,

(24)

that is,
\Psi\left(\alpha,U,V\right)=\hat{\Psi}\left(\alpha\right)e^{-i\left(p_{U}^{0}U+p_{V}^{0}V\right)}
.

In the WKB approximation,
\hat{\Psi}\left(\alpha\right)\simeq e^{iS_{\Lambda}\left(\alpha\right)}
, from (
23
) we find the
Hamilton-Jacobi equation

-\frac{1}{12\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial S_{\Lambda}}{\partial\alpha}\right)^{2}+p_{U}^{0}p_{V}^{0}-\left(\mu-\beta\alpha^{3}\right)p_{V}^{2}=0,

(25)

equivalently,

\frac{1}{12\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial S_{\Lambda}}{\partial\alpha}\right)^{2}-\left(2\Lambda\alpha^{3}+\rho_{m0}\right)=0,

(26)

with constraints

~2\Lambda=-\beta p_{V}^{0}~\text{and~}\rho_{m0}=\mu\left(p_{v}^{0}\right)^{2}-p_{u}^{0}p_{v}^{0}~.

(27)

Equation (
26
) is nothing else than the Hamilton-Jacobi equation for
the
\Lambda
CDM model (
7
) with scale factor
\alpha\left(t\right)
.

In a similar way we can apply similar transformations and construct new
solutions for the WDW equations which describe universes with different value
of the cosmological constant. This is an important observation because it can
relate the solution space for universes with different values of the
cosmological constant.

We have not mentioned that when
\mu\left(p_{v}^{0}\right)^{2}-p_{u}^{0}p_{v}^{0}~=0
, that is,
\rho_{m0}=0
, then we can say that we can
introduce the cosmological constant from the vacuum, and to derive the de
Sitter solution from the vacuum space.

IV
Conclusions

In Newtonian physics, small oscillations are described by a linear
second-order differential equation with solutions that are periodic functions.
According to Sophus Lie theory, there exists a point transformation where the
small oscillations are described by the free particle equation
line
.
Indeed, with the use of nonlinear methods, small oscillations can be depicted
by a linear line in a time-position diagram. Although this is a mathematical
construct, it establishes a relation between the shared conservation laws of
the two classical systems.

This exact equivalence between the two different physical systems was applied
in this study to introduce the cosmological constant. We considered the CDM
universe, and we found that from the WDW equation, we can construct the
Hamilton-Jacobi action for the
\Lambda
CDM model. The assumption that we have
done is the field equations to be described by an extended minisuperspace as
given by the Eisenhart-lift and to apply extend the application of quantum
operators of the WDW equation. The relaxation of the first-class constraints
of quantum cosmology discussed recently in

.

bjar

are related through point transformations.

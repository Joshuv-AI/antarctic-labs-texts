Global regular solutions for 1-D degenerate compressible Navier-Stokes equations with large data and far field vacuum
Yue Cao, Hao Li, Shengguo Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, the Cauchy problem for the one-dimensional (1-D) isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations ( CNS ) is considered. When the viscosity μ ⁡ ( ρ ) \mu(\rho) depends on the density ρ \rho in a sublinear power law ( ρ δ \rho^{\delta} with 0 < δ ≤ 1 0<\delta\leq 1 ), based on an elaborate analysis of the intrinsic singular structure of this degenerate system, we prove the global-in-time well-posedness of regular solutions with conserved total mass, momentum, and finite total energy in some inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces. Moreover, the solutions we obtained satisfy that ρ \rho keeps positive for all point x ∈ ℝ x\in\mathbb{R} but decays to zero in the far field, which is consistent with the facts that the total mass of the whole space is conserved, and CNS is a model of non-dilute fluids where ρ \rho is bounded below away from zero. The key to the proof is the introduction of a well-designed reformulated structure by introducing some new variables and initial compatibility conditions, which, actually, can transfer the degeneracies of the time evolution and the viscosity to the possible singularity of some special source terms. Then, combined with the BD entropy estimates and transport properties of the so-called effective velocity v = u + φ ​ ( ρ ) x v=u+\varphi(\rho)_{x} ( u u is the velocity of the fluid, and φ ⁡ ( ρ ) \varphi(\rho) is a function of ρ \rho defined by φ ′ ​ ( ρ ) = μ ⁡ ( ρ ) / ρ 2 \varphi^{\prime}(\rho)=\mu(\rho)/\rho^{2} ), one can obtain the required uniform a priori estimates of corresponding solutions. Moreover, in contrast to the classical theory in the case of the constant viscosity, one can show that the L ∞ L^{\infty} norm of u u of the global regular solution we obtained does not decay to zero as time t t goes to infinity. It is worth pointing out that the well-posedness theory established here can be applied to the viscous Saint-Venant system for the motion of shallow water.

1.
introduction

The time evolution of the mass density
\rho\geq 0
and the velocity
u=\left(u^{(1)},u^{(2)},\cdots,u^{(d)}\right)^{\top}

\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of a general viscous isentropic compressible fluid occupying a spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is governed by the following isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
ICNS
):

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+\text{div}(\rho u)=0,\\[4.0pt]
(\rho u)_{t}+\text{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\text{div}\mathbb{T}.\end{cases}

Here,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{d})^{\top}\in\Omega
,
t\geq 0
are the space and time variables, respectively.
For the polytropic gases, the constitutive relation is given by

(1.2)

P=A\rho^{\gamma},\quad A>0,\quad\gamma>1,

where
A
is an entropy constant and
\gamma
is the adiabatic exponent.
\mathbb{T}
denotes the viscous stress tensor with the form

(1.3)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{T}=2\mu(\rho)D(u)+\lambda(\rho)\text{div}u\,\mathbb{I}_{d},\end{split}

where
D(u)=\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top}\big)
is the deformation tensor,
\mathbb{I}_{d}
is the
d\times d
identity matrix,

(1.4)

\mu(\rho)=\alpha\rho^{\delta},\quad\lambda(\rho)=\beta\rho^{\delta},

for some constant
\delta\geq 0
,

\mu(\rho)
is the shear viscosity coefficient,
\lambda(\rho)+\frac{2}{d}\mu(\rho)
is the bulk viscosity coefficient,
\alpha
and
\beta
are both constants satisfying

(1.5)

\alpha>0\quad\text{and}\quad 2\alpha+d\beta\geq 0.

In the theory of gas dynamics, the
CNS
can be derived from the Boltzmann equations through the Chapman-Enskog expansion, cf. Chapman-Cowling
[
9
]
and Li-Qin
[
30
]
. Under some proper physical assumptions, one can find that the viscosity coefficients and heat conductivity coefficient
\kappa
are not constants but functions of the absolute temperature
\theta
such as:

(1.6)

\begin{split}\mu(\theta)=&a_{1}\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}F(\theta),\quad\lambda(\theta)=a_{2}\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}F(\theta),\quad\kappa(\theta)=a_{3}\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}F(\theta),\end{split}

for some constants
a_{i}

(i=1,2,3)
(see
[
9
]
). Actually for the cut-off inverse power force models, if the intermolecular potential varies as
r^{-\nu}
,
where
r
is intermolecular distance, then in (
1.6
):

F(\theta)=\theta^{b}\quad\text{with}\quad b=\frac{2}{\nu}\in[0,\infty).

In particular (see §10 of
[
9
]
), for ionized gas,

\nu=1
and
b=2
;
for Maxwellian molecules,

\nu=4
and
b=\frac{1}{2}
;
while for rigid elastic spherical molecules,

\nu=\infty
and
b=0
.

According to Liu-Xin-Yang
[
34
]
, for isentropic and polytropic fluids, such a dependence is inherited through the laws of Boyle and Gay-Lussac:

P=R\rho\theta=A\rho^{\gamma}\quad\text{for \ constant}\quad R>0,

i.e.,
\theta=AR^{-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}
, and one can see that the viscosity coefficients are functions of
\rho
of the form
(\ref{fandan})
. Actually, there do exist some physical models that satisfy the density-dependent viscosities assumption (
1.4
), such as Korteweg system, Shallow water equations, lake equations and quantum Navier-Stokes system (see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
36
,
46
]
).

In the current paper, the following 1-D degenerate
ICNS
is considered:

(1.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\\[4.0pt]
&(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2})_{x}+P(\rho)_{x}=(\mu(\rho)u_{x})_{x},\end{aligned}\right.

where the pressure
P
and the viscosity coefficient
\mu(\rho)
are given by (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), respectively.
We are concerned with the global well-posedness of regular solutions with large data and far field vacuum to the Cauchy problem of (
1.7
) with the following initial data and far field behavior:

(1.8)

\begin{split}\displaystyle(\rho(0,x),u(0,x))=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x))\quad&\text{for}\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\\[4.0pt]
\displaystyle\left(\rho(t,x),u(t,x)\right)\to\left(0,0\right)\quad\text{as}\quad\left|x\right|\to\infty\quad&\text{for}\quad t\geq 0.\end{split}

Throughout this paper, we adopt the following simplified notations, most of them are for the standard homogeneous and inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces:

\begin{split}\displaystyle&\|f\|_{s}=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad|f|_{p}=\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\|f\|_{m,p}=\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle&D^{k,r}=\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):|f|_{D^{k,r}}=|\nabla^{k}f|_{r}<\infty\},\quad D^{k}=D^{k,2},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle&D^{1}_{*}=\{f\in L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):|f|_{D^{1}_{*}}=|\nabla f|_{2}<\infty\},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle&|f|_{D^{1}_{*}}=\|f\|_{D^{1}_{*}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\quad\|f\|_{L^{p}([0,T];L^{q})}=\|f\|_{L^{p}([0,T];L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle&\|f\|_{X_{1}\cap X_{2}}=\|f\|_{X_{1}}+\|f\|_{X_{2}},\quad\int f\text{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\text{d}x,\\[6.0pt]
&X([0,T];Y)=X([0,T];Y(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\quad\|(f,g)\|_{X}=\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}.\end{split}

We will clearly indicate the value of
d
where the above notations are used.
A detailed study of homogeneous Sobolev spaces can be found in
[
15
]
.

For the flows of constant viscosities (
\delta=0
in (
1.4
)), there is a lot of literature on the well-posedness of strong/classical solutions to the system (
1.1
). When
\inf_{x}{\rho_{0}(x)}>0
, the local well-posedness of three-dimensonal (3-D) classical solutions to the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) follows from the standard symmetric hyperbolic-parabolic structure satisfying the well-known Kawashima’s condition, cf.
[
25
,
40
,
42
]
, which has been extended to be a global one by Matsumura-Nishida
[
37
]
for initial data close to a non-vacuum equilibrium in some Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(
s>\frac{5}{2}
). In
\mathbb{R}
, the global well-posedness of strong solutions with arbitrarily large data in some bounded domains has been proven by Kazhikhov-Shelukhin
[
27
]
, and later, Kawashima-Nishida
[
26
]
extended this theory to the unbounded domains. However, these approaches do not work when
\inf_{x}{\rho_{0}(x)}=0
, which occurs when some physical requirements are imposed, such as finite total initial mass and energy in the whole space. One of the main issues in the presence of vacuum is the degeneracy of the time evolution operator, which makes it hard to understand the behavior of the velocity field near the vacuum.
A remedy was suggested by Cho-Choe-Kim
[
11
]
, where they imposed initially a
compatibility condition
:

-\text{div}\mathbb{T}_{0}+\nabla P(\rho_{0})=\sqrt{\rho_{0}}g\quad\text{for\ \ some}\ \ g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),

which leads to
\sqrt{\rho}u_{t}\in L^{\infty}([0,T_{*}];L^{2})
for a short time
T_{*}>0
. Then they established successfully the local well-posedness of smooth solutions with vacuum in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see also Cho-Kim
[
12
]
), which, recently, has been shown to be a global one with small energy by Huang-Li-Xin
[
21
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Later, Jiu-Li-Ye
[
22
]
proved that the 1-D Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) admits a unique global classical solution with arbitrarily large data and vacuum.

For density-dependent viscosities (
\delta>0
in (
1.4
)) which degenerate at the vacuum, system (
1.1
) has received extensive attentions in recent years. When
\inf_{x}{\rho_{0}(x)}>0
, some important progresses on the global well-posedness of classical solutions to the Cauchy problem of (
1.1
) have been obtained, which include
Burtea-Haspot
[
8
]
, Constantin-Drivas-Nguyen-Pasqualotto
[
13
]
, Haspot
[
20
]
, Kang-Vasseur
[
24
]
,
Mellet-Vasseur
[
39
]
for 1-D flow with arbitrarily large data, and Sundbye
[
44
]
and Wang-Xu
[
47
]
for two-dimensional (2-D) flow with initial data close to a non-vacuum equilibrium in some Sobolev spaces. When vacuum appears, instead of the uniform elliptic
structure, the viscosity degenerates when density vanishes, which raises the difficulty of the
problem to another level. Actually, in
[
11
,
21
,
22
]
, the uniform ellipticity of the Lamé operator
L
defined by

Lu=-\alpha\triangle u-(\alpha+\beta)\nabla\mathtt{div}u

(
Lu=-\alpha u_{xx}
in
\mathbb{R}
) plays an essential role in the high order regularity estimates on
u
. One can use the standard elliptic theory to estimate
|u|_{D^{k+2}}
by the
D^{k}
-norm of all other terms in momentum equations. However, for
\delta>0
, viscosity coefficients vanish as the density function connects to vacuum continuously. This degeneracy makes it difficult to adapt the approach of the constant viscosity case in
[
11
,
21
,
22
]
to the current case.
A remarkable discovery of a new mathematical entropy
function was made by Bresch-Desjardins
[
5
]
for

\lambda(\rho)
and
\mu(\rho)
satisfying the relation

(1.9)

\lambda(\rho)=2(\mu^{\prime}(\rho)\rho-\mu(\rho)),

which offers an estimate

\mu^{\prime}(\rho)\nabla\sqrt{\rho}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

provided that

\mu^{\prime}(\rho_{0})\nabla\sqrt{\rho_{0}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
d\geq 1
.
This observation plays an important role in the development of the global existence of weak solutions with vacuum for system (
1.1
) and some related models, see Bresch-Desjardins
[
2
]
, Bresch-Vasseur-Yu
[

]
, Jiu-Xin
[

]
, Li-Xin
[

]
, Mellet-Vasseur
[

]
, Vasseur-Yu
[

]
and so on. However, the regularities and uniquness of such weak solutions with vacuum remain open.

Notice that, for the cases
\delta\in(0,\infty)
, if
\rho>0
,
\eqref{eq:1.1}_{2}
can be formally rewritten as

(1.10)

\begin{split}u_{t}+u\cdot\nabla u+\frac{A\gamma}{\gamma-1}\nabla\rho^{\gamma-1}+\rho^{\delta-1}Lu=\psi\cdot Q(u),\end{split}

where the quantities
\psi
and
Q(u)
are given by

(1.11)

\begin{split}\psi\triangleq&\nabla\ln\rho\quad\text{when}\quad\delta=1;\\
\psi\triangleq&\frac{\delta}{\delta-1}\nabla\rho^{\delta-1}\quad\text{when}\quad\delta\in(0,1)\cup(1,\infty);\\
Q(u)\triangleq&\alpha(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\top})+\beta\mathtt{div}u\mathbb{I}_{d}.\end{split}

When
\delta=1

When

Based on this observation, by using some hyperbolic approach which bridges the parabolic system (

However, due to the complicated mathematical structure of the degenerate
ICNS
and lack of smooth effect on solutions when vacuum appears, even many important progresses have been obtained, a lot of fundamental questions still remain open, such as which kind of initial data can cause the finite time blow-up of smooth solutions, or provide the global existence of smooth solutions with conserved total mass, momentum, and finite total energy. In the current paper, we identify a class of 1-D initial data, which admits a global classical solution with far field vacuum to the Cauchy problem for the cases
0<\delta\leq 1
in some inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces.

1.1.
Global-in-time well-posedness of regular solutions

In order to present our results clearly, we first introduce the following two definitions for the case
0<\delta<1
and the case
\delta=1

of regular solutions with far field vacuum to the Cauchy problem (
1.7
)-(
1.8
), respectively.

Definition 1.1
.

Assume
0<\delta<1
and
T>0
. The pair
(\rho,u)
is called a regular solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, if
(\rho,u)
satisfies this problem in the sense of distributions and:

\displaystyle(1)\ \inf_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\rho(t,x)=0,\quad 0<\rho^{\gamma-1}\in C([0,T];H^{2}),

\displaystyle(\rho^{\delta-1})_{x}\in C([0,T];H^{1});

\displaystyle(2)\ u\in C([0,T];H^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{3}),\quad\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}\in C([0,T];L^{2}),

\displaystyle u_{t}\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}),\quad\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{tx}\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}),

\displaystyle\rho^{\delta-1}u_{xx}\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}).

Definition 1.2
.

Assume
\delta=1
and
T>0
. The pair
(\rho,u)
is called a regular solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
, if
(\rho,u)
satisfies this problem in the sense of distributions and:

\displaystyle(1)\ \inf_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\rho(t,x)=0,\quad 0<\rho^{\gamma-1}\in C([0,T];H^{2}),

\displaystyle(\ln\rho)_{x}\in C([0,T];H^{1});

\displaystyle(2)\ u\in C([0,T];H^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{3}),\quad u_{t}\in C([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];H^{1}).

Remark 1.1
.

First, it follows from the Definitions
1.1
-
1.2
that
(\rho^{\delta-1})_{x}\in L^{\infty}
for the case
0<\delta<1
, and

(\ln\rho)_{x}\in L^{\infty}
for the case
\delta=1
,
which, along with
\rho^{\gamma-1}\in C([0,T];H^{2})
, means that the vacuum occurs if and only if in the far field.

Second, we introduce some physical quantities that will be used in this paper:

\begin{split}m(t)=&\int\rho(t,x){\rm{d}}x\quad\textrm{(total mass)},\\
\mathbb{P}(t)=&\int\rho(t,x)u(t,x){\rm{d}}x\quad\textrm{(momentum)},\\
E_{k}(t)=&\frac{1}{2}\int\rho(t,x)u^{2}(t,x){\rm{d}}x\quad\textrm{ (total kinetic energy)},\\
E(t)=&E_{k}(t)+\int\frac{P(t,x)}{\gamma-1}{\rm{d}}x\quad\textrm{ (total energy)}.\end{split}

Actually, it follows from the definitions that the regular solution satisfies the conservation of total mass (see Lemma
3.3
) and momentum (see Lemma
4.1
). Furthermore, it satisfies the energy equality (see Lemma
3.1
and its proof in Appendix B). Note that the conservation of momentum is not clear for the strong solution with vacuum to the flows of constant viscosities Jiu-Li-Ye
[
22
]
(see an example on non-conservation of momentum in
[
49
]
). In this sense, the definitions of regular solutions here are consistent with the physical background of
CNS
.

The regular solutions select velocity in a physically reasonable way when the density approaches the vacuum at far fields. Under the help of this notion of solutions, the momentum equations can be reformulated into a special quasi-linear parabolic system with some possible singular source terms near the vacuum, and the coefficients in front of
u_{xx}
will tend to
\infty
as
\rho\rightarrow 0
in the far field for
0<\delta<1
. However, the problems become trackable through some elaborate arguments on the energy estimates.

For the case
0<\delta<1
, the global-in-time well-posedness result reads as:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\delta
and
\gamma
satisfy

(1.12)

0<\delta<1,\quad\gamma>1,\quad\gamma\geq\delta+\frac{1}{2}.

If the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

(1.13)

0<\rho_{0}\in L^{1},\quad(\rho_{0}^{\gamma-1},u_{0})\in H^{2},\quad(\rho_{0}^{\delta-1})_{x}\in H^{1},

and the initial compatibility conditions

(1.14)

\partial_{x}u_{0}=\rho_{0}^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}g_{1},\quad\alpha\partial_{x}^{2}u_{0}=\rho_{0}^{1-\delta}g_{2},

for some
g_{i}\in L^{2}(i=1,2)
,
then the Cauchy problem (
1.7
)-(
1.8
) admits a unique global classical solution

(\rho,u)
in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfying that, for any
0<T<\infty
,
(\rho,u)
is a regular one in
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
as defined in Definition
1.1
, and

(1.15)

\begin{split}&\rho\in C([0,T];L^{1}),\quad(\rho^{\gamma-1})_{t}\in C([0,T];H^{1}),\quad(\rho^{\delta-1})_{tx}\in C([0,T];L^{2}),\\
&t^{\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{tx}\in L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{2})\cap L^{2}([0,T];D^{1}),\quad t^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{tt}\in L^{2}([0,T];L^{2}).\end{split}

Remark 1.2
.

First, observe that there are no smallness conditions imposed on
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
.

Second, one can find the following class of initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying the conditions (
1.13
)-(
1.14
):

\rho_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2\sigma}},\quad u_{0}(x)\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where
\max\big\{\frac{3}{4},\frac{1}{4(\gamma-1)}\big\}<\sigma<\frac{1}{4(1-\delta)}.

Particularly, when
\partial_{x}u_{0}
is compactly supported,
the compatibility conditions (
1.14
) are satisfied automatically.

Moreover, the compatibility conditions (
1.14
) are also necessary for the existence of the unique regular solution
(\rho,u)
obtained in Theorem
1.1
.
In particular, the one shown in
\eqref{e1.8}_{2}
plays a key role in the derivation of

, which will be used in the uniform estimates for

.

Remark 1.3
.

First, observe that there are no smallness conditions imposed on
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
.

Second, one can find the following class of initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfying (

):

\rho_{0}(x)=\frac{1}{1+x^{2\sigma}},\quad u_{0}(x)\in C_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

Moreover, it should be pointed out that the theory established in Theorem

can be applied to the viscous

,

, see Gerbeau-Perthame

Remark 1.4
.

It is worth pointing out that in the domain
\Omega=[0,1]
, Li-Li-Xin
[
28
]
proved that the entropy weak solutions for general large initial data satisfying finite entropy to the corresponding initial-boundary value problem of the system (

\delta>\frac{1}{2}
exist globally in time. Moreover, the authors showed that for more regular initial data, there
is a global entropy weak solution which is unique and regular with well-defined velocity
field for short time, and the interface of initial vacuum propagates along the particle path
during this time period. Furthermore, the dynamics of weak solutions and vacuum states are investigated rigorously. It is shown that for any global entropy weak solution, any
(possibly existing) vacuum state must vanish within finite time, which means that there exist some time
T_{0}>0
(depending on initial data) and a constant
\rho_{-}
such that

(1.18)

The velocity (even if
regular enough and well-defined) blows up in finite time as the vacuum states vanish, and after the vanishing of vacuum states, the global entropy weak solution
becomes a strong solution and tends to the non-vacuum equilibrium state exponentially

We consider the upper bound of the mass density

The proof of Lemma

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
T>0
, it holds that

|\rho(t,\cdot)|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

First, integrating the equation
\eqref{e1.1}_{2}
over
(-\infty,x]
with respect to
x
, one has

(3.1)

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\rho u(t,z){\rm{d}}z+\rho u^{2}+A\rho^{\gamma}=\alpha\rho^{\delta}u_{x},

where one has used the far field behavior
\eqref{e1.3}_{2}
.

Denote
\displaystyle\xi(t,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\rho u(t,z){\rm{d}}z
. Then it follows from (
3.1
) that

(3.2)

\xi_{t}+u\xi_{x}+\alpha\rho^{\delta-1}(\rho_{t}+u\rho_{x})+A\rho^{\gamma}=0.

Second, let
y(t;x)
be the solution of the following problem

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\text{d}y(t;x)}{\text{d}t}=u(t,y(t;x)),\\
&y(0;x)=x.\end{aligned}\right.

It follows from (
3.2
) that

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\left(\xi+\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\rho^{\delta}\right)(t,y(t;x))\leq 0,

which, along with the fact

(3.4)

\xi(t,y(t;x))\leq|\sqrt{\rho}u(t,\cdot)|_{2}|\rho(t,\cdot)|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C_{0}\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T,

implies that

(3.5)

\left(\xi+\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\rho^{\delta}\right)(t,y(t;x))\leq\xi(0,y(0;x))+\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\rho_{0}^{\delta}(x)\leq C_{0}.

Finally, according to (
3.4
)-(
3.5
), one can obtain that

|\rho^{\delta}(t,\cdot)|_{\infty}\leq C_{0}\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

The proof of Lemma
3.4
is complete.

∎

3.2.
The
L^{2}
estimate of
u

We consider the
L^{2}
estimate of
u
. For this purpose, one first needs to show the following several auxiliary lemmas. The first one is on the
L^{q}
estimate of
\rho^{\frac{1}{q}}u
for any
2\leq q<\infty
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
2\leq q<\infty
. Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

|\rho^{\frac{1}{q}}u(t,\cdot)|_{q}\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T,

where the constant
C
depends on
q
.

Proof.

First, multiplying
\eqref{e1.1}_{2}
by
|u|^{p}u

(p\geq 2)
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}+\alpha(p+1)\big|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{x}\big|_{2}^{2}\\
=&A(p+1)\int\rho^{\gamma}|u|^{p}u_{x}{\rm{d}}x\\
\leq&\frac{\alpha(p+1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta}|u|^{p}u_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\frac{A^{2}(p+1)}{2\alpha}\int\rho^{2\gamma-\delta}|u|^{p}{\rm{d}}x.\end{split}

When
p=2
, it follows from (
3.6
), Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
-
3.4
that

(3.7)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{4}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}u\big|_{4}^{4}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\big|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}uu_{x}\big|_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&C|\rho|_{\infty}^{2\gamma-\delta-1}|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}u|_{4}|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u|_{2}|\rho|_{1}^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq C(1+|\rho^{\frac{1}{4}}u|_{4}^{4}).\end{split}

When
p>2
, it follows from (
3.6
), Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
-
3.4
that

(3.8)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}+\frac{\alpha(p+1)}{2}\big|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{x}\big|_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&C|\rho|_{\infty}^{2\gamma-\delta-1}|\rho^{\frac{p-2}{p}}u^{\frac{(p-2)(p+2)}{p}}|_{\frac{p}{p-2}}|\rho^{\frac{2}{p}}u^{\frac{4}{p}}|_{\frac{p}{2}}\\
\leq&C|\rho|_{\infty}^{2\gamma-\delta-1}|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u|_{p+2}^{\frac{(p-2)(p+2)}{p}}|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u|_{2}^{\frac{4}{p}}\\
\leq&C|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u|_{p+2}^{\frac{(p-2)(p+2)}{p}}\leq C\big(1+|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u|_{p+2}^{p+2}\big).\end{split}

Second, integrating (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) over
[0,t]
, one can obtain that for any
p\geq 2
,

\begin{split}&\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{x}\big|_{2}^{2}\text{d}s\\
\leq&\big|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u_{0}\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}+C\Big(t+\int_{0}^{t}\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}\text{d}s\Big),\end{split}

which, along with the Gronwall inequality, yields that

(3.9)

\begin{split}&\big|\rho^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u(t,\cdot)\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}u_{x}(s,\cdot)\big|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}s}\\
\leq&C\Big(1+\big|\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{p+2}}u_{0}\big|_{p+2}^{p+2}\Big)\leq C\Big(1+|\rho_{0}|_{1}|u_{0}|_{\infty}^{p+2}\Big)\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.\end{split}

Finally, the desired conclusion can be achieved from (
3.9
) and Lemma
3.1
.

The proof of Lemma
3.5
is complete.
∎

The following lemma gives the estimate of
\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\iota}u(s,\cdot)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s

(\frac{1}{2}\leq\iota<1)
, which plays a crucial role in obtaining the
L^{\infty}
estimate of the so-called effective velocity
v
(see (
3.13
)).

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume

(3.10)

\iota=\frac{1}{2}\quad\rm{for}\quad 0<\delta<1;\quad\frac{1}{2}<\iota<1\quad\rm{for}\quad\delta=1.

Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\iota}u(s,\cdot)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

Case 1:

0<\delta<1
. First, it follows direct calculations that

\partial_{x}(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u)=\frac{1}{2}\rho^{\delta-\frac{3}{2}}\rho_{x}\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}u+\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u_{x}.

According to Lemmas
3.1
-
3.3
and
3.5
, one has

|\rho^{\delta-\frac{3}{2}}\rho_{x}|_{2}+|\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}u|_{\frac{2}{1-\delta}}+|\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}|_{\frac{2}{1-\delta}}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\frac{\delta}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}}s\leq C,

which, along with Lemma
3.4
, yields that

(3.11)

\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u)(s,\cdot)|_{\frac{2}{2-\delta}}^{2}\text{d}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Second, notice that
\frac{2}{2-\delta}\in(1,2)
, then by the Sobolev embedding theorem (or see Lemma
A.1
), one has

|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u|_{\infty}\leq C|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u|_{2}^{1-\aleph}|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u)|^{\aleph}_{\frac{2}{2-\delta}}\quad\text{with}\quad\aleph=\frac{1}{1+\delta}\in(0,1),

which, along with Lemma
3.1
and (
3.11
), yields that

\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u(s,\cdot)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u)|^{2}_{\frac{2}{2-\delta}}\Big)(s,\cdot)\text{d}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Case 2:

\delta=1
. First, it follows direct calculations that

\partial_{x}(\rho^{\iota}u)=\iota\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{x}\rho^{\iota-\frac{1}{2}}u+\rho^{\iota-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{x}.

According to Lemmas
3.1
-
3.3
,
3.5
and
\frac{1}{2}<\iota<1
, one has

|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}\rho_{x}|_{2}+|\rho^{\iota-\frac{1}{2}}u|_{\frac{2}{2\iota-1}}+|\rho^{\iota-\frac{1}{2}}|_{\frac{2}{2\iota-1}}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}}s\leq C,

which yields that

(3.12)

\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\iota}u)(s,\cdot)|_{\frac{1}{\iota}}^{2}\text{d}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Second, notice that
\frac{1}{\iota}\in(1,2)
, then by Sobolev embedding theorem (or see Lemma
A.1
), one has

|\rho^{\iota}u|_{\infty}\leq C|\rho^{\iota}u|_{2}^{1-\Xi}|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\iota}u)|^{\Xi}_{\frac{1}{\iota}}\quad\text{with}\quad\Xi=\frac{1}{3-2\iota}\in(0,1),

which, along with Lemmas
3.1
,
3.4
and (
3.12
), yields that

\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\iota}u(s,\cdot)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(1+|\partial_{x}(\rho^{\iota}u)|^{2}_{\frac{1}{\iota}}\Big)(s,\cdot)\text{d}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

The proof of Lemma
3.6
is complete.
∎

Next we show the
L^{\infty}
estimate of the effective velocity:

(3.13)

v=u+\varphi(\rho)_{x}=u+\alpha\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume additionally

(3.14)

\gamma\geq\delta+\frac{1}{2}\quad\rm{for}\quad 0<\delta<1;\quad\gamma>\frac{3}{2}\quad\rm{for}\quad\delta=1.

Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

|v(t,\cdot)|_{\infty}\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

First, it follows from the system
(
1.7
)
and the definition of
v
that

(3.15)

v_{t}+uv_{x}+\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\rho^{\gamma-\delta}v-\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\rho^{\gamma-\delta}u=0.

Here, the detail derivation of the above equation for
v
can be found in Appendix B.

Via the standard characteristic method, one can obtain

v=\left(v_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\rho^{\gamma-\delta}u\ \exp\Big(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\rho^{\gamma-\delta}{\rm{d}}\tau\Big){\rm{d}}s\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\rho^{\gamma-\delta}{\rm{d}}s\right).

Then according to Lemmas
3.4
and
3.6
, one has

(3.16)

\begin{split}|v|_{\infty}\leq&C\Big(|v_{0}|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\gamma-\delta}u|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big)\\
\leq&C\Big(1+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho|^{\gamma-\delta-\frac{1}{2}}_{\infty}|\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}u|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big)\\
\leq&C\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\delta<1,\quad\gamma\geq\delta+\frac{1}{2},\\
|v|_{\infty}\leq&C\Big(|v_{0}|_{\infty}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\gamma-1}u|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big)\\
\leq&C\Big(1+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho|^{\gamma-1-\iota}_{\infty}|\rho^{\iota}u|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\Big)\\
\leq&C\quad\text{for}\quad\delta=1,\quad\gamma\geq 1+\iota>\frac{3}{2}.\end{split}

The proof of Lemma
3.7
is complete.
∎

Now we show the
L^{2}
estimate of velocity
u
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume (
3.14
) additionally. Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

|u(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

Case 1:

0<\delta<1
.
From
\eqref{e2.2}_{2}
, one has

(3.17)

u_{t}+uu_{x}+A\gamma\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}=\alpha(\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x})_{x}+\alpha\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}.

Multiplying (
3.17
) by
u
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.18)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}|u|_{2}^{2}+\alpha|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}\\
=&\int\big(-uu_{x}-A\gamma\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}+\alpha\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}\big)u{\rm{d}}x\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\text{J}_{i}.\end{split}

According to Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality and Lemmas
3.1
-
3.7
, one can obtain

(3.19)

\displaystyle\text{J}_{1}=

\displaystyle-\int u^{2}u_{x}{\rm{d}}x=0,

\displaystyle\text{J}_{2}=

\displaystyle-A\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}u{\rm{d}}x=-A\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-\delta}\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\int\rho^{\gamma-\delta}(v-u)u{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|\sqrt{\rho}|_{2}|u|_{2}|v|_{\infty}|\rho|_{\infty}^{\gamma-\delta-\frac{1}{2}}+|\rho|_{\infty}^{\gamma-\delta}|u|_{2}^{2}\Big)\leq C|u|_{2}^{2}+C,

\displaystyle\text{J}_{3}=

\displaystyle\alpha\int\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}u{\rm{d}}x=\int\left(v-u\right)u_{x}u{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|v|_{\infty}|\rho|_{\infty}^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}|u|_{2}\leq C|u|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}.

It follows from (
3.18
)-(
3.19
) and the Gronwall inequality that

|u(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Case 2:

\delta=1
.
Multiplying
\eqref{e2.1}_{2}
by
u
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}|u|_{2}^{2}+\alpha|u_{x}|_{2}^{2}=\int\big(-uu_{x}-A\gamma\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}+\alpha\psi u_{x}\big)u{\rm{d}}x.

Via the similar argument for estimating
\text{J}_{1}
-
\text{J}_{3}
, one can obtain

|u(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}|u_{x}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

The proof of Lemma
3.8
is complete.
∎

3.3.
The first order estimates of
u

Now we are ready to consider the first order estimates of
u
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume (
3.14
) additionally. Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}+|\rho^{\delta-1}u_{xx}|_{2}^{2}+|u_{x}|_{\infty}\big)(s,\cdot){\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

Case 1:

0<\delta<1
.
Multiplying (
3.17
) by
u_{t}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

(3.20)

\begin{split}&\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}+|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}\\
=&\int\Big(\frac{\alpha}{2}(\rho^{\delta-1})_{t}u_{x}^{2}-\big(uu_{x}+A\gamma\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}-\alpha\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}\big)u_{t}\Big){\rm{d}}x\triangleq\sum_{i=4}^{7}\text{J}_{i}.\end{split}

According to the equation
\eqref{e1.1}_{1}
, Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
A.1
and Lemmas
3.1
-
3.8
, one can obtain that

(3.21)

\begin{split}\text{J}_{4}=&\frac{\alpha}{2}\int(\rho^{\delta-1})_{t}u_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x=-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int\big((\rho^{\delta-1})_{x}u+(\delta-1)\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}\big)u_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x\\
=&-\frac{\alpha(\delta-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}uu_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x-\frac{\alpha(\delta-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}^{3}{\rm{d}}x\\
=&-\frac{\delta-1}{2}\int(v-u)uu_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x-\frac{\alpha(\delta-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}^{3}{\rm{d}}x\\
=&-\frac{\delta-1}{2}\int vuu_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x+\frac{\delta-1}{2}\int u^{2}u_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x-\frac{\alpha(\delta-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}^{3}{\rm{d}}x\\
\leq&-\frac{\delta-1}{2}\int vuu_{x}^{2}{\rm{d}}x-\frac{\alpha(\delta-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}^{3}{\rm{d}}x\\
\leq&C\big(|v|_{\infty}|u|_{\infty}|\rho|_{\infty}^{1-\delta}+|u_{x}|_{\infty}\big)|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}\\
\leq&C\big(1+|u_{xx}|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Then we need to consider
|u_{xx}|_{2}
. According to (
3.17
), one has

\displaystyle|u_{xx}|_{2}\leq

\displaystyle C|\rho|_{\infty}^{1-\delta}|\rho^{\delta-1}u_{xx}|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(|u_{t}|_{2}+|uu_{x}|_{2}+|\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}|_{2}+|\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(|u_{t}|_{2}+|u|_{2}|u_{x}|_{\infty}+|(v-u)u_{x}|_{2}+|\rho^{\gamma-\delta}(v-u)|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(|u_{t}|_{2}+|u|_{2}|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{xx}|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}+(|v|_{\infty}+|u|_{\infty})|u_{x}|_{2}

\displaystyle+|\rho^{\gamma-\delta}|_{2}|v|_{\infty}+|\rho|_{\infty}^{\gamma-\delta}|u|_{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(1+|u_{t}|_{2}+|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\big)+\frac{1}{2}|u_{xx}|_{2},

which implies that

(3.22)

|u_{xx}|_{2}\leq C\Big(1+|u_{t}|_{2}+|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big).

Consequently, according to (
3.21
)-(
3.22
), Lemma
3.4
and Young’s inequality, one can obtain

(3.23)

\text{J}_{4}\leq C\Big(1+|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{10}{3}}\Big)+\frac{1}{8}|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}.

For the terms
\text{J}_{i}\ (i=5,6,7)
, one can similarly obtain

(3.24)

\begin{split}\text{J}_{5}=&-\int uu_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x\leq C|u|_{\infty}|u_{x}|_{2}|u_{t}|_{2}\\
\leq&C|u|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\rho|_{\infty}^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}|u_{t}|_{2}\\
\leq&C|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{3}+\frac{1}{8}|u_{t}|_{2}^{2},\\
\text{J}_{6}=&-A\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x=-\frac{A\gamma}{\alpha}\int\rho^{\gamma-\delta}(v-u)u_{t}{\rm{d}}x\\
\leq&C\Big(|\rho^{\gamma-\delta}|_{2}|v|_{\infty}+|\rho|_{\infty}^{\gamma-\delta}|u|_{2}\Big)|u_{t}|_{2}\leq C+\frac{1}{8}|u_{t}|^{2}_{2},\\
\text{J}_{7}=&\alpha\int\rho^{\delta-2}\rho_{x}u_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x=\int(v-u)u_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x\\
\leq&C\left(|v|_{\infty}+|u|_{\infty}\right)|u_{x}|_{2}|u_{t}|_{2}\\
\leq&C(1+|u_{x}|_{2})|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{8}|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Substituting (
3.21
)-(
3.24
) into (
3.20
), one gets

(3.25)

\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{2}+|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}\leq C\Big(1+|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{10}{3}}\Big),

which, along with the Gronwall inequality and (
3.22
), yields that

(3.26)

|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{x}(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}+|\rho^{\delta-1}u_{xx}|_{2}^{2}\big)(s,\cdot){\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Case 2:

\delta=1
. First, similarly to the derivation of (
3.22
), one still has

(3.27)

|u_{xx}|_{2}\leq C\Big(1+|u_{t}|_{2}+|u_{x}|_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\Big).

Second, multiplying
\eqref{e2.1}_{2}
by
u_{t}
and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{2}\frac{\text{d}}{\text{d}t}|u_{x}|_{2}^{2}+|u_{t}|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int uu_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x-A\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x+\alpha\int\rho^{-1}\rho_{x}u_{x}u_{t}{\rm{d}}x.

Via the similar arguments for estimating
\text{J}_{5}
-
\text{J}_{7}
, one can get

(3.28)

|u_{x}(t,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}(|u_{t}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}+|u_{xx}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{2}){\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Finally, it follows from (
3.26
), (
3.28
) and Sobolev embedding theorem that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}|u_{x}(s,\cdot)|_{\infty}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\int_{0}^{t}|u_{x}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u_{xx}(s,\cdot)|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}s\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}&\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.\end{split}

The proof of Lemma
3.9
is complete.
∎

3.4.

We consider the second order estimates of

. For this purpose, we first give the

and
L^{4}
estimates for
\psi
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Assume (
3.14
) additionally. Then for any
T>0
, it holds that

|\psi(t,\cdot)|_{2}+|\psi(t,\cdot)|_{4}\leq C\quad{\rm{for}}\ \ 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

Multiplying (
3.15
) by
v
and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{R}

\begin{split}\text{J}_{16}=&\alpha\int(\rho^{\delta-1})_{t}u_{xx}u_{tt}\text{d}x\\
=&-\alpha\int\big((\rho^{\delta-1})_{x}u+(\delta-1)\rho^{\delta-1}u_{x}\big)u_{xx}u_{tt}\text{d}x\\
\leq&C\big(|\psi|_{\infty}|u|_{\infty}|u_{xx}|_{2}+|\rho^{\delta-1}u_{xx}|_{2}|u_{x}|_{\infty}\big)|u_{tt}|_{2}\leq C+\frac{1}{8}|u_{tt}|_{2}^{2},\\

\leq&C(1+|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{tx}|_{2}^{2})+\frac{1}{8}|u_{tt}|_{2}^{2},\\
\text{J}_{21}=&\frac{\alpha}{2}\int(\rho^{\delta-1})_{t}u_{tx}^{2}\text{d}x\\
\leq&C\big(|\psi|_{\infty}|u|_{\infty}|u_{tx}|_{2}^{2}+|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{tx}|_{2}^{2}|u_{x}|_{\infty}\big)\leq C|\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}u_{tx}|_{2}^{2},\end{split}

which, along with (

Case 2:

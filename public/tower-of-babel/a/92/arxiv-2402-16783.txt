Conditional optimal sets and the quantization coefficients for some uniform distributions
1 Evans Nyanney, 2 Megha Pandey, 3 Mrinal Kanti Roychowdhury

Abstract
. Bucklew and Wise (1982) showed that the quantization dimension of an absolutely continuous probability measure on a given Euclidean space is constant and equals the Euclidean dimension of the space, and the quantization coefficient exists as a finite positive number. By giving different examples, in this paper, we have shown that the quantization coefficients for absolutely continuous probability measures defined on the same Euclidean space can be different. We have taken uniform distribution as a prototype of an absolutely continuous probability measure. In addition, we have also calculated the conditional optimal sets of n n -points and the n n th conditional quantization errors for the uniform distributions in constrained and unconstrained scenarios.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
equipped with a metric
d
induced by a norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
Let
S
be a nonempty closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Let
\beta\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}
be given with
\text{card}(\beta)=\ell
for some
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq\ell
, the
n
th conditional constrained quantization
error for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
and the conditional set
\beta
, is defined by

(1)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf_{\alpha}\Big\{\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha\cup\beta}d(x,a)^{2}dP(x):\alpha\subseteq S,~0\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n-\ell\Big\},

where
\text{card}(A)
represents the cardinality of the set
A
.

Definition 1.2
.

A set
\alpha\cup\beta
, where
\alpha\subseteq S
and
P(M(b|\alpha\cup\beta))>0
for
b\in\beta
, for which the infimum in (
1
) exists and contains no less than
\ell
elements, and no more than
n
elements is called a conditional constrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with respect to the constraint
S
and the conditional set
\beta
.

We assume that
\int d(x,0)^{2}dP(x)<\infty
to make sure that the infimum in (
1
) exists (see
[
PR1
]
). For a finite set
\gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
a\in\gamma
, by
M(a|\gamma)
we denote the set of all elements in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
which are nearest to
a
among all the elements in
\gamma
, i.e.,

M(a|\gamma)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:d(x,a)=\mathop{\min}\limits_{b\in\gamma}d(x,b)\}.

M(a|\gamma)
is called the
Voronoi region
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
generated by
a\in\gamma
.

Write
V_{\infty}(P):=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}V_{n}(P)
. The numbers

(2)

\underline{D}(P):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))}\text{ and }\overline{D}(P):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))},

are called the
conditional lower
and the
conditional upper constrained quantization dimensions
of the probability measure
P
, respectively. If
\underline{D}(P)=\overline{D}(P)
, the common value is called the
conditional constrained quantization dimension
of
P
and is denoted by
D(P)
. For any
\kappa>0
, the two numbers
\liminf_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
and
\limsup_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
are, respectively, called the
\kappa
-dimensional conditional lower
and
conditional upper constrained quantization coefficients
for
P
. If both of them are equal, then it is called the
\kappa
-dimensional conditional constrained quantization coefficient
for
P
, and is denoted by
\lim_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
.

If there is no conditional set, then by the
n
th constrained quantization error
for
P
with respect to the constraint
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
, it is meant that

(3)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf\Big\{\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha}d(x,a)^{2}dP(x):\alpha\subseteq S\text{ and }1\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\},

and then the numbers
D(P)
and
\lim_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
, if they exist, are called the
constrained quantization dimension
and the
\kappa
-dimensional constrained quantization coefficient
for
P
, respectively. A set
\alpha\subseteq S
for which the infimum in (
3
) exists is called a
constrained optimal set of
n
-points
for
P
.

If there is no constraint, i.e., if
S=\mathbb{R}^{k}
, then by the
n
th conditional unconstrained quantization error
with respect to the conditional set
\beta
, it is meant that

(4)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf_{\alpha}\Big\{\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha\cup\beta}d(x,a)^{2}dP(x):\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k},~0\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n-\ell\Big\},

and then the numbers
D(P)
and
\lim_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
, if they exist, are called the
conditional unconstrained quantization dimension
and the
\kappa
-dimensional conditional unconstrained quantization coefficient
for
P
, respectively. A set
\alpha\subseteq S
for which the infimum in (
3
) exists is called a
conditional unconstrained optimal set of
n
-points
for
P
.

If there is no constraint and no conditional set, then by the
n
th unconditional quantization error
it is meant that

(5)

V_{n}:=V_{n}(P)=\inf\Big\{\int\mathop{\min}\limits_{a\in\alpha}d(x,a)^{2}dP(x):\alpha\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k},~1\leq\text{card}(\alpha)\leq n\Big\},

and then the numbers
D(P)
and
\lim_{n}n^{\frac{2}{\kappa}}(V_{n}(P)-V_{\infty}(P))
, if they exist, are called the
unconstrained quantization dimension
and the
\kappa
-dimensional unconstrained quantization coefficient
for
P
, respectively. A set
\alpha\subseteq S

optimal set of

-means

Bucklew and Wise (1982) showed that the quantization dimension of an absolutely continuous probability measure on a given Euclidean space is constant and equals the Euclidean dimension of the space where the support of the probability measure is defined, and the quantization coefficient exists as a finite positive number. By giving different examples, in this paper, we have shown that the quantization coefficients for absolutely continuous probability measures defined in the same Euclidean space can be different. We have taken uniform distribution as a prototype of an absolutely continuous probability measure. To determine the quantization coefficient for a Borel probability measure

2.
Preliminaries

For any two elements
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we write

\rho((a,b),(c,d)):=(a-c)^{2}+(b-d)^{2},

which gives the squared Euclidean distance between the two elements
(a,b)
and
(c,d)
. Let
p
and
q
be two elements that belong to an optimal set of
n
-points for some positive integer
n
, and let
e
be an element on the boundary of the Voronoi regions of the elements
p
and
q
. Since the boundary of the Voronoi regions of any two elements is the perpendicular bisector of the line segment joining the two elements, we have

\rho(p,e)-\rho(q,e)=0.

We call such an equation a
canonical equation
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
which is uniform on its support the closed interval
[a,b]
. Then, the probability density function
f
for
P
is given by

\displaystyle f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{b-a}&\text{ if }a\leq x\leq b,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Hence, we have
dP(x)=P(dx)=f(x)dx
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let us now state the following proposition. For the details of the proof see
[
BCDR
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

(see
[
BCDR
]
)
Let
P
be a uniform distribution on the closed interval
[a,b]
and
c,d\in[a,b]
be such that
a<c<d<b
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq 2
, let
\alpha_{n}
be a conditional unconstrained optimal set of
n
-points for
P
with respect to the conditional set
\beta=\{c,d\}
such that
\alpha_{n}
contains
k
elements from the closed interval
[a,c]
,
\ell
elements from the closed interval
[c,d],
and
m
elements from the closed interval
[d,b]
for some
k,\ell,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k,m\geq 1
and
\ell\geq 2
. Then,
k+\ell+m=n+2
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\cap[a,c]

\displaystyle=\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(c-a)}{2k-1}:1\leq j\leq k\Big\},

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\cap[c,d]

\displaystyle=\Big\{c+\frac{j-1}{\ell-1}(d-c):1\leq j\leq\ell\Big\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\cap[d,b]

\displaystyle=\Big\{d+\frac{2(j-1)(b-d)}{2m-1}:1\leq j\leq m\Big\}

with the conditional unconstrained quantization error

V_{n}:=V_{k,\ell,m}(P)=\frac{1}{3(b-a)}\Big(\frac{(c-a)^{3}}{(2k-1)^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{(d-c)^{3}}{(\ell-1)^{2}}+\frac{(b-d)^{3}}{(2m-1)^{2}}\Big).

Remark 2.2
.

If nothing is specified, by conditional optimal sets of
n
-points and the conditional quantization errors, it is meant the conditional unconstrained optimal sets of
n
-points and the conditional unconstrained quantization errors, respectively.

The following theorem is known.

Theorem 2.3
.

(see
[
PR4
]
)
In both constrained and unconstrained quantization, the lower and upper quantization dimensions and the lower and upper quantization coefficients for a Borel probability measure do not depend on the conditional set.

Remark 2.4
.

Given that the underlying spaces for all considered probability measures
P
in this work are one dimensional, their quantization dimensions are given by
D(P)=1
(see
[
BW
]
).
Hence, in the sequel we are mostly interested to calculate the quantization coefficients for different uniform distributions, though in some cases we have also calculated the conditional optimal sets of
n
-points and the
n
th conditional quantization errors in constrained and unconstrained scenarios.

3.
Conditional optimal sets of
n
-points and the quantization coefficients for uniform distributions on line segments

Without any loss of generality we can assume the line segment as a closed interval
[a,b]
, where
0<a<b<+\infty
. Let
P
be the uniform distribution defined on the closed interval
[a,b]
. Then, the probability density function
f
for
P
is given by (
2
). Let us now give the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
P
be the uniform distribution on the line segment joining
a
and
b
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
. Then we have:

(i)
the conditional optimal set of
n
-points with respect to the conditional set
\beta:=\{a,b\}
is

\Big\{a+\frac{j-1}{\ell-1}(b-a):1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\text{ with conditional quantization error }V_{n}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{12(n-1)^{2}};

(ii)
the conditional optimal set of
n
-points with respect to the conditional set
\beta:=\{a\}
is

\Big\{a+\frac{2(j-1)(b-a)}{2n-1}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\text{ with conditional quantization error }V_{n}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{3(2n-1)^{2}};

(iii)
the conditional optimal set of
n
-points with respect to the conditional set
\beta:=\{b\}
is

\Big\{a+\frac{(2j-1)(b-a)}{2n-1}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\text{ with conditional quantization error }V_{n}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{3(2n-1)^{2}}.

Proof.

The proof of the theorem follows by Proposition
2.1
upon substitutions
c=a
and
d=b
,
c=d=a
, and
c=d=b
, respectively.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
P
be the uniform distribution on the line segment joining
a
and
b
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
. Then, the conditional quantization coefficients for
P
with respect to the conditional sets
\{a,b\}
,
\{a\}
, and
\{b\}
exist as finite positive numbers and each equals
\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{12}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.1
(i)
, we obtain the
n
th conditional quantization error for the uniform distribution
P
with respect to the conditional set
\beta:=\{a,b\}
as

V_{n}=\frac{(b-a)^{2}}{12(n-1)^{2}}.

Then,

V_{\infty}=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}V_{n}=0\text{ yielding }\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})=\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{12}.

Similarly, if the conditional set is
\beta:=\{a\}
or
\beta:=\{b\}
, by
(ii)
and
(iii
) in Theorem
3.1
, we obtain
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})=\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{12}
.
Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

By Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
3.2
, we see that the quantization coefficient for the uniform distribution
P
on a line segment depends on the length of the line segment, and does not depend on the conditional sets.

4.
Optimal sets of
n
-points and the quantization coefficients for the uniform distributions on the circles

In this section, we have two subsections. In the first subsection, we calculate the conditional constrained optimal sets of
n
-points and the
n
th conditional constrained quantization errors, the conditional constrained quantization dimension, and the conditional constrained quantization coefficient for a uniform distribution
P
defined on a circle of radius
r
with respect to a given conditional set and a constraint. In the second subsection, for the same probability distribution, we investigate the optimal sets of
n
-means and the
n
th quantization errors, and the quantization coefficient in unconstrained scenario.

Let
L
be the circle of radius
r
. Without any loss of generality, we can take the equation of the circle as
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=r^{2}
, i.e., the parametric equations of the circle is given by

L:=\{(x_{1},x_{2}):x_{1}=r\cos\theta,\,x_{2}=r\sin\theta\text{ for }0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi\}.
Notice that any point on the circle can be given by
(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)
, which will be identified as
\theta
, where
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
.
Let the positive direction of the
x_{1}
-axis cut the circle at the point
A
, i.e.,
A
is represented by the parametric value
\theta=0
. Let
s
be the distance of a point on
L
along the arc starting from the point
A
in the counterclockwise direction. Then,

ds=\sqrt{\Big(\frac{dx_{1}}{d\theta}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{dx_{2}}{d\theta}\Big)^{2}}\,d\theta=rd\theta.

Then, the probability density function (pdf)
f(x_{1},x_{2})
for
P
is given by

f(x_{1},x_{2})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{2\pi r}&\text{ if }(x_{1},x_{2})\in L,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Thus, we have
dP(s)=P(ds)=f(x_{1},x_{2})ds=\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta
.
Moreover, we know that if
\hat{\theta}
radians is the
central angle subtended by an arc of length
S
of the circle, then
S=r\hat{\theta}
, and

P(S)=\int_{S}dP(s)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S}d\theta=\frac{\hat{\theta}}{2\pi}.

4.1.
Conditional quantization in constrained scenario

In this subsection to investigate the conditional quantization in constrained scenario for the uniform distribution
P
on the circle
L
, we take the circle
L
as the constraint and the set
\{(r,0)\}
as the conditional set.
Let us define a function

T:L\to[0,2\pi r]\text{ such that }T(\theta):=T((r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta))=r\theta,

where
0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi
. Then, notice that
T:L\setminus\{(r,0)\}\to(0,2\pi r)
is a bijective function. Let
Q
be the image measure of
P
under the function
T
, i.e.,
Q=TP
such that for any Borel subset
A\subseteq[0,2\pi r]
, we have

Q(A)=TP(A)=P(T^{-1}(A)).

Lemma 4.1.1
.

The image measure
Q
is a uniform distribution on
[0,2\pi r]
.

Proof.

Since
P
is a uniform distribution on
L
, we can assume that
P
is also a uniform distribution on
L\setminus\{(r,0)\}
, as the deletion, or addition, of a finite number of points from, or with, the support of a continuous probability measure does not change the distribution.
Take any
[c,d]\subseteq(0,2\pi r)
, where
0<c<d<2\pi r
. Since
T
is a bijection, there exist
\theta_{1}
,
\theta_{2}
, where
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<2\pi
, such that
T(\theta_{1})=r\theta_{1}=c
and
T(\theta_{2})=r\theta_{2}=d
. Then,

Q([c,d])=P(T^{-1}([c,d]))=P(\{(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta):\theta_{1}\leq\theta\leq\theta_{2}\})=\frac{\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}}{2\pi}=\frac{r\theta_{2}-r\theta_{1}}{2\pi r}=\frac{d-c}{2\pi r}.

Notice
Q([c,d])=\lambda([c,d])
, where
\lambda
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
(0,2\pi r)
. Hence, we can conclude that
Q
is a uniform distribution on
(0,2\pi r
), i.e.,
Q
is a uniform distribution on
[0,2\pi r]
.
Thus, the lemma is obtained.
∎

Notation 4.1.2
.

For any two elements
c,d\in[0,2\pi r]
, by
\ell(\{c,d\})
it is meant the distance between the two elements
c,d
, i.e.,
\ell(\{c,d\})=d-c
. Similarly, for any two elements
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in L
with
\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}
, by
\ell(\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\})
it is meant the arc distance between the two elements
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
, i.e., the length of the arc on
L
subtended by the angle
\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}
, i.e.,
\ell(\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\})=r(\theta_{2}-\theta_{2}).

Lemma 4.1.3
.

The function
T:L\setminus\{(r,0)\}\to(0,2\pi r)
preserves the distance.

Proof.

Take any
c,d\in(0,2\pi r)
such that
0<c<d<2\pi r
. The lemma will be proved if we can prove that
\ell(\{c,d\})=\ell(T^{-1}(\{c,d\}))
. Since
T:L\setminus\{(r,0)\}\to(0,2\pi r)
is a bijection, there exist
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in L\setminus\{(r,0)\}
such that
T(\theta_{1})=r\theta_{1}=c
and
T(\theta_{2})=r\theta_{2}=d
. Then,

\ell(T^{-1}(\{c,d\}))=\ell(\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\})=r(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})=d-c=\ell(\{c,d\}).

Thus, the lemma is yielded.
∎

Lemma 4.1.4
.

The conditional unconstrained optimal set for the uniform distribution
Q
with respect to the conditional set
\{0,2\pi r\}
is given by

\Big\{\frac{(j-1)2\pi r}{n-1}:1\leq j\leq n\Big\}\text{ with conditional unconstrained quantization error }V_{n}(Q)=\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3(n-1)^{2}}.

Proof.

The lemma is followed by Proposition
2.1
upon substitution
a=c=0
and
b=d=2\pi r
.
∎

Remark 4.1.5
.

Let
\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\}
be a conditional unconstrained optimal set of
n
-points for
Q
with respect to the conditional set
\{0,2\pi r\}
, where
a_{1}=0
and
a_{n}=2\pi r
,
Since both
P
and
Q
are uniform distributions, and
Q
is the image measure of
P
under the function
T
, and
T:L\setminus\{(r,0)\}\to(0,2\pi r)
preserves the distance, we can say that the set
T^{-1}(\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\})
, i.e., the set
\{T^{-1}(a_{j}):1\leq j\leq n-1\}
forms a conditional constrained optimal set of
(n-1)
-points for
P
with respect to the conditional set
\{(r,0)\}
and the constraint
L
, as
T^{-1}(0)=T^{-1}(2\pi r)=(r,0)
.

Let us now prove the following theorems, which give the main results in this subsection.

Theorem 4.1.6
.

Let
P
be the uniform distribution on the circle of radius
r
with center
(0,0)
. Then, the set
\{(r\cos\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n},r\sin\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n}):1\leq j\leq n\}
forms a conditional constrained optimal set of
n
-points with respect to the conditional set
\{(r,0)\}
and the constraint
L
with conditional constrianed quantization error

V_{n}=2r^{2}(1-\frac{n}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi}{n}).

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1.4
, we know that the set
\{\frac{(j-1)2\pi r}{n-1}:1\leq j\leq n\}
forms a conditional unconstraied optimal set of
n
-points for
Q
with respect to the conditional set
\{0,2\pi r\}
, where
n\geq 2
. Hence, by Remark
5.3
, the set

\{T^{-1}(\frac{(j-1)2\pi r}{n-1}):1\leq j\leq n-1\}
forms a conditional constrained optimal set of
(n-1)
-points for
P
with respect to the conditional set
\{(0,\pi)\}
and the constraint
L
, where
(n-1)\geq 1
.
Now, notice that

\displaystyle\{T^{-1}(\frac{(j-1)2\pi r}{n-1}):1\leq j\leq n-1\}=\{\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n-1}:1\leq j\leq n-1\}

\displaystyle=\{(\cos\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n-1},\sin\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n-1}):1\leq j\leq n-1\}.

Hence, replacing
n
by
n+1
, we deduce that the set
\{(r\cos\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n},r\sin\frac{(j-1)2\pi}{n}):1\leq j\leq n\}
forms a conditional constrained optimal set of
n
-points with respect to the conditional set
\{(0,\pi)\}
and the constraint
L
. Due to rotational symmetry, we obtain the conditional constrained quantization error as

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=n(\text{distortion error contributed by the element }(r,0))

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{n}}^{\frac{\pi}{n}}\rho((r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta),(r,0))\,d\theta

\displaystyle=2r^{2}(1-\frac{n}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi}{n}).

Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.1.7
.

Let
P
be the uniform distribution on the circle of radius
r
with center
(0,0)
. Then, with respect to the conditional set
\{(r,0)\}
and the constraint
L
, the conditional constrained quantization dimension

exists and equals one, and the conditional constrained quantization coefficient for
P
exists as a finite positive number and equals
\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3}
, i.e.,

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})=\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3}.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1.6
, we obtain the
n
th conditional constrained quantization error for the uniform distribution
P
with respect to the conditional set
\{(r,0)\}
and the constraint

as

V_{n}=2r^{2}(1-\frac{n}{\pi}\sin\frac{\pi}{n}).

Then,
V_{\infty}=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}V_{n}=0
.
Hence,

D(P)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{2\log n}{-\log(V_{n}-V_{\infty})}=1\text{ and }\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}(V_{n}-V_{\infty})=\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3}.

Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Remark 4.1.8

By Theorem

, we know that quantization dimension and the quantization coefficient in constrained and unconstrained cases do not depend on the conditional set. Thus,
by Theorem

with respect to the constraint

. However, we see that the constrained quantization coefficient for the uniform distribution

4.2.
Quantization in unconstrained scenario

In this subsection, we investigate the optimal sets of
n
-means,
n
th quantization errors, and the quantization coefficient for the uniform distribution
P
when there is no constraint and no conditional set. The following lemma is a generalized version of a similar theorem that appears in
[
RR
]
.

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let
\alpha_{n}
be an optimal set of
n
-means for the uniform distribution
P
on the circle
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}=r^{2}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\alpha_{n}:=\Big\{\frac{nr}{\pi}\Big(\sin(\frac{\pi}{n})\cdot\cos((2j-1){\frac{\pi}{n}}),\ \sin(\frac{\pi}{n})\cdot\sin((2j-1){\frac{\pi}{n}})\Big):j=1,2,\cdots,n\Big\}

forms an optimal set of
n
-means, and the corresponding quantization error is given by
r^{2}(1-\frac{n^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{n}).

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}:=\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{n}\}
be an optimal set of
n
-means for
P
. Let the boundaries of the Voronoi regions of
a_{k}
intersect
L
at the points given by the parameters
\theta_{k-1}
and
\theta_{k}
such that
\theta_{k-1}<\theta_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
. Without any loss of generality, we can assume that
\theta_{0}=0
and
\theta_{n}=2\pi
. Since,
P
is a uniform distribution and the circle
L
is rotationally symmetric, without going into the much details of calculations, we see that

\theta_{1}-\theta_{0}=\theta_{2}-\theta_{1}=\theta_{3}-\theta_{2}=\cdots=\theta_{n}-\theta_{n-1}=\frac{\theta_{n}-\theta_{0}}{n}=\frac{2\pi}{n}.

implying
\theta_{k}=\frac{2\pi k}{n}
for
0\leq k\leq n
.
It is well-known that in unconstrained quantization, the elements in an optimal set are the conditional expectations in their own Voronoi regions. Hence, for
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\theta_{k}-\theta_{k-1}}\int_{\theta_{k-1}}^{\theta_{k}}\frac{1}{2\pi}(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)d\theta=\frac{r}{\theta_{k}-\theta_{k-1}}\left(\sin\theta_{k}-\sin\theta_{k-1},\,\cos\theta_{k-1}-\cos\theta_{k}\right),

yielding

a_{k}=\frac{nr}{\pi}\Big(\sin(\frac{\pi}{n})\cdot\cos((2k-1){\frac{\pi}{n}}),\ \sin(\frac{\pi}{n})\cdot\sin((2k-1){\frac{\pi}{n}})\Big).

Let
V_{n}
be the
n
th quantization error. Due to symmetry, the distortion errors contributed by
a_{k}
in their own Voronoi regions are equal for all
1\leq k\leq n
. Again, notice that
\theta_{0}=0
,
\theta_{1}=\frac{2\pi}{n}-\theta_{0}=\frac{2\pi}{n}
, and
a_{1}=\frac{r}{\theta_{1}}(\sin\theta_{1},1-\cos\theta_{1})
. Hence,

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{\theta_{0}}^{\theta_{1}}\rho((r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta),a_{1})\,d\theta=r^{2}(1-\frac{n^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{n}).

Thus, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.2.2
.

Let
P
be the uniform distribution on the circle of radius
r
with center
(0,0)
. Then, the quantization coefficient for
P
exists as a finite positive number and equals
\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.2.1
, we have
V_{n}=r^{2}(1-\frac{n^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{n})
, and hence
\mathop{\lim}\limits_{n\to\infty}n^{2}V_{n}=\frac{\pi^{2}r^{2}}{3},

which is the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.2.3
.

Theorem
4.2.2
implies that the quantization coefficient for a uniform distribution on a circle of radius
r
, though exists as a finite positive number, depends on the radius
r
of the circle.

\ell=\text{length of }A_{j}A_{j+1}=\sqrt{\rho((r\cos\theta_{j},r\sin\theta_{j}),(r\cos\theta_{j+1},r\sin\theta_{j+1}))}=2r\sin\frac{\pi}{m}.

Notice that

A_{j}A_{j+1}=\{(1-t)(r\cos\theta_{j},r\sin\theta_{j})+t(r\cos\theta_{j+1},r\sin\theta_{j+1}):0\leq t\leq 1\}.

for

(8)

\displaystyle a_{j}:

(10)

Remark 5.8
.

By Theorem

and Theorem

, we can say that the quantization coefficient for the uniform distribution

Uniqueness of global weak solutions to the frame hydrodynamics for biaxial nematic phases in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Sirui Li, Chenchen Wang, Jie Xu

Abstract
We consider the hydrodynamics for biaxial nematic phases described by a field of orthonormal frame, which can be derived from a molecular-theory-based tensor model. We prove the uniqueness of global weak solutions to the Cauchy problem of the frame hydrodynamics in dimensional two. The proof is mainly based on the suitable weaker energy estimates within the Littlewood–Paley analysis. We take full advantage of the estimates of nonlinear terms with rotational derivatives on S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) , together with cancellation relations and dissipative structures of the biaxial frame system. Keywords. Liquid crystals, biaxial nematic phase, frame hydrodynamics, uniqueness of weak solutions, Littlewood–Paley theory Mathematics Subject Classification (2020). 35A02, 76A15, 35Q35

1.1
Preliminaries

We denote by
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
the orthonormal frame, which is constituted by three mutually perpendicular unit vectors. The symbol
\otimes
stands for the tensor product. For any two tensors
U
and
V
with the same order, the dot product
U\cdot V
is defined by summing up the product of the corresponding coordinates, i.e.,

\displaystyle U\cdot V=U_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n}}V_{i_{1}\cdots i_{n}},\quad|U|^{2}=U\cdot U.

Hereafter, the Einstein summation convention on repeated indices is assumed.

To express symmetric tensors conveniently, the monomial notation will be adopted as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}^{k_{1}}_{1}\mathbf{n}^{k_{2}}_{2}\mathbf{n}^{k_{3}}_{3}=\Big(\underbrace{\mathbf{n}_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{n}_{1}}_{k_{1}}\otimes\underbrace{\mathbf{n}_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{n}_{2}}_{k_{2}}\otimes\underbrace{\mathbf{n}_{3}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{n}_{3}}_{k_{3}}\Big)_{\rm sym}.

In other words, when the symbol
\otimes
is omitted in a product, it implies that the resulting tensor has been symmetrized. For example, for the frame
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})
and
\alpha,\beta=1,2,3,
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}^{2}=\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{\alpha},\quad\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}\mathbf{n}_{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{\beta}+\mathbf{n}_{\beta}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}),\quad\alpha\neq\beta.

In this way, the
3\times 3
identity tensor
\mathfrak{i}
can be expressed as a polynomial, i.e.,

\mathfrak{i}=\mathbf{n}_{1}^{2}+\mathbf{n}_{2}^{2}+\mathbf{n}_{3}^{3}
.

The differential operators on
SO(3)
will be involved when describing the frame hydrodynamics. For any frame
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
, we denote by
T_{\mathfrak{p}}SO(3)
the tangential space of
SO(3)
at a point
\mathfrak{p}
, which can be spanned by the orthogonal basis:

\displaystyle V_{1}=(0,\mathbf{n}_{3},-\mathbf{n}_{2}),\quad V_{2}=(-\mathbf{n}_{3},0,\mathbf{n}_{1}),\quad V_{3}=(\mathbf{n}_{2},-\mathbf{n}_{1},0).

Then, its orthogonal complement space
(T_{\mathfrak{p}}SO(3))^{\perp}
can be spanned by

\displaystyle W_{1}=

\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{n}_{3},\mathbf{n}_{2}),\quad W_{2}=(\mathbf{n}_{3},0,\mathbf{n}_{1}),\quad W_{3}=(\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{1},0),

\displaystyle W_{4}=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{n}_{1},0,0),\quad W_{5}=(0,\mathbf{n}_{2},0),\quad W_{6}=(0,0,\mathbf{n}_{3}).

Consequently, we define the differential operators
\mathscr{L}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
on
T_{\mathfrak{p}}SO(3)
by taking the inner products of the orthogonal basis
\{V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\}
and

\partial/\partial\mathfrak{p}=(\partial/\partial\mathbf{n}_{1},\partial/\partial\mathbf{n}_{2},\partial/\partial\mathbf{n}_{3})
, that is,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathscr{L}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}V_{1}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}}-\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{3}},\\
&\mathscr{L}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}V_{2}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{3}}-\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}},\\
&\mathscr{L}_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}V_{3}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}}-\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mathscr{L}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
are actually the derivatives along the infinitesimal rotation about
\mathbf{n}_{k}
. Acting the operators
\mathscr{L}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
on
\mathbf{n}_{l}
, it follows that
\mathscr{L}_{k}\mathbf{n}_{l}=\epsilon^{klp}\mathbf{n}_{p}
with
\epsilon^{klp}
being the Levi-Civita symbol.
The operators
\mathscr{L}_{k}
are also suitable for a functional if we replace
\partial/\partial\mathfrak{p}
by the variational derivative
\delta/\delta\mathfrak{p}
.

In order to deal with the estimate of the higher-order derivative terms with the differential operators
\mathscr{L}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
, we will resort to the orthogonal decomposition with respect to the tangential space
T_{\mathfrak{p}}SO(3)
. More specifically, for any two matrices
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
, the inner product
A\cdot B
can be expressed as

\displaystyle A\cdot B=\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{1}{|V_{k}|^{2}}(A\cdot V_{k})(B\cdot V_{k})+\sum_{k=1}^{6}\frac{1}{|W_{k}|^{2}}(A\cdot W_{k})(B\cdot W_{k}).

(1.2)

On the other hand, for any frame
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
, any first-order differential operator
\mathcal{D}
, and
\alpha,\beta=1,2,3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{1}=(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathbf{n}_{2}+(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3})\mathbf{n}_{3}\\
&\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{2}=(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1})\mathbf{n}_{1}+(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3})\mathbf{n}_{3}\\
&\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{3}=(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathbf{n}_{2}+(\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1})\mathbf{n}_{1}\\
&\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\beta}+\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}_{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}=\mathcal{D}(\mathbf{n}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{\beta})=0,\\
&W_{\alpha}\cdot\mathcal{D}\mathfrak{p}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.3)

where we reiterate that
\{W_{\alpha}\}^{6}_{\alpha=1}
is the orthogonal basis of
(T_{\mathfrak{p}}SO(3))^{\perp}
.

1.2
Frame hydrodynamics

The local orientation of biaxial nematic phases is described by an orthonormal frame
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
.
The corresponding orientational elasticity, can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{Bi}[\mathfrak{p}]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{Bi}(\mathfrak{p},\nabla\mathfrak{p})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

(1.4)

where the deformation free energy density
f_{Bi}
has the following form
[
28
,
8
,
33
]
:

\displaystyle f_{Bi}(\mathfrak{p},\nabla\mathfrak{p})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\Big(K_{1}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1})^{2}+K_{2}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}+K_{3}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle\,+K_{4}(\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{1})^{2}+K_{5}(\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}+K_{6}(\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle\,+K_{7}(\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{1})^{2}+K_{8}(\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}+K_{9}(\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle\,+K_{10}(\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{1})^{2}+K_{11}(\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{2})^{2}+K_{12}(\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{n}_{3})^{2}

\displaystyle\,+\gamma_{1}\nabla\cdot[(\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{n}_{1}-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1})\mathbf{n}_{1}]+\gamma_{2}\nabla\cdot[(\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{n}_{2}-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathbf{n}_{2}]

\displaystyle\,+\gamma_{3}\nabla\cdot[(\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{n}_{3}-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3})\mathbf{n}_{3}]\Big).

The elastic energy density
f_{Bi}
is composed of twelve bulk terms and three surface terms.
The coefficients
K_{i}(i=1,\cdots,12)
of bulk terms are all positive.
Each surface term is a null Lagrangian, so that the coefficients
\gamma_{i}>0(i=1,2,3)
will be determined later as needed.
We remark that the above form should be the most convenient one for our analysis later, although other equivalent forms are available.

In order to formulate the frame hydrodynamics, we introduce a set of local basis formed by nine second-order tensors, that is, the identity tensor
\mathfrak{i}
, five symmetric traceless tensors,

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}_{1}=\mathbf{n}^{2}_{1}-\frac{1}{3}\mathfrak{i},\quad\mathbf{s}_{2}=\mathbf{n}^{2}_{2}-\mathbf{n}^{2}_{3},\quad\mathbf{s}_{3}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\mathbf{n}_{2},\quad\mathbf{s}_{4}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\mathbf{n}_{3},\quad\mathbf{s}_{5}=\mathbf{n}_{2}\mathbf{n}_{3},

and three asymmetric traceless tensors,

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{1}=\mathbf{n}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{2}-\mathbf{n}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{1},\quad\mathbf{a}_{2}=\mathbf{n}_{3}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{1}-\mathbf{n}_{1}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{3},\quad\mathbf{a}_{3}=\mathbf{n}_{2}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{3}-\mathbf{n}_{3}\otimes\mathbf{n}_{2}.

The frame hydrodynamics for biaxial nematic phases consists of evolution equations for the orthonormal frame field
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
, coupled with the Navier–Stokes equations for the fluid velocity field
\mathbf{v}
. These equations are given by (see
[
19
]
for details):

\displaystyle\,\chi_{1}\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{3}-\eta_{1}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5}+\mathscr{L}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}=0,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\,\chi_{2}\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}-\eta_{2}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4}+\mathscr{L}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}=0,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\,\chi_{3}\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{3}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}-\eta_{3}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3}+\mathscr{L}_{3}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}=0,

(1.7)

\displaystyle\,\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3),

(1.8)

\displaystyle\,\dot{\mathbf{v}}=-\nabla p+\eta\Delta\mathbf{v}+\nabla\cdot\sigma+\mathfrak{F},

(1.9)

\displaystyle\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0,

(1.10)

where we use the dot derivative to denote the material derivative
\partial_{t}+\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla
.
The constraint (
1.8
) is necessary in this formulation since other equations does not guarantee it.
In the equation of
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})^{T}
, the pressure
p
ensures the incompressibility (
1.10
), and
\eta
is the viscous coefficient.
The divergence of the stress
\sigma
should be comprehended as
(\nabla\cdot\sigma)_{i}=\partial_{j}\sigma_{ij}
.
Now let us specify
\sigma
, for which we introduce
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{\Omega}
to represent the symmetric and skew-symmetric components of the velocity gradient
\kappa_{ij}=\partial_{j}v_{i}
, respectively, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa+\kappa^{T}),\quad\mathbf{\Omega}=\frac{1}{2}(\kappa-\kappa^{T}).

Then, the stress
\sigma=\sigma(\mathfrak{p},\mathbf{v})
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathfrak{p},\mathbf{v})=

\displaystyle\,\beta_{1}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{1})\mathbf{s}_{1}+\beta_{0}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2})\mathbf{s}_{1}+\beta_{0}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{1})\mathbf{s}_{2}+\beta_{2}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2})\mathbf{s}_{2}

\displaystyle\,+\beta_{3}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3})\mathbf{s}_{3}-\eta_{3}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}\Big)\mathbf{s}_{3}

\displaystyle\,+\beta_{4}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4})\mathbf{s}_{4}-\eta_{2}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}\Big)\mathbf{s}_{4}

\displaystyle\,+\beta_{5}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5})\mathbf{s}_{5}-\eta_{1}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{3}\Big)\mathbf{s}_{5}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{3}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3})\mathbf{a}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{3}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}\Big)\mathbf{a}_{1}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{2}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4})\mathbf{a}_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{2}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}\Big)\mathbf{a}_{2}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{1}{2}\eta_{1}(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5})\mathbf{a}_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{1}\Big(\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{3}\Big)\mathbf{a}_{3},

(1.11)

where the coefficients in (
1.2
) are derived from molecular parameters and satisfy the following conditions (see
[
19
]
for details):

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\beta_{i}\geq 0,~i=1,\cdots,5,\quad\chi_{j}>0,~j=1,2,3,\quad\eta>0,\\
\beta^{2}_{0}\leq\beta_{1}\beta_{2},~~\eta^{2}_{1}\leq\beta_{5}\chi_{1},~~\eta^{2}_{2}\leq\beta_{4}\chi_{2},~~\eta^{2}_{3}\leq\beta_{3}\chi_{3}.\end{array}\right.

These conditions are crucial for the energy dissipation law to hold.
The body force
\mathfrak{F}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{i}=\partial_{i}\mathbf{n}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{2}\mathscr{L}_{3}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}+\partial_{i}\mathbf{n}_{3}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{1}\mathscr{L}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}+\partial_{i}\mathbf{n}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{3}\mathscr{L}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}.

(1.14)

It is preferred that the orthonormal constraint (
1.8
) is maintained automatically.
For this purpose, using (
1.3
), we reformulate the equations of
\mathfrak{p}
by all its coordinates:

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{1}=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}+\frac{\eta_{3}}{\chi_{3}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3}-\frac{1}{\chi_{3}}\mathscr{L}_{3}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{2}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}+\frac{\eta_{2}}{\chi_{2}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4}-\frac{1}{\chi_{2}}\mathscr{L}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{3},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{2}=

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{1}+\frac{\eta_{3}}{\chi_{3}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3}-\frac{1}{\chi_{3}}\mathscr{L}_{3}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{1}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{3}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{\chi_{1}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5}-\frac{1}{\chi_{1}}\mathscr{L}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{3},

(1.16)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{n}}_{3}=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{2}+\frac{\eta_{2}}{\chi_{2}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4}-\frac{1}{\chi_{2}}\mathscr{L}_{2}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{1}

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{\Omega}\cdot\mathbf{a}_{3}+\frac{\eta_{1}}{\chi_{1}}\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5}-\frac{1}{\chi_{1}}\mathscr{L}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\Big)\mathbf{n}_{2},

(1.17)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathbf{v}}=

\displaystyle-\nabla p+\eta\Delta\mathbf{v}+\nabla\cdot(\sigma+\sigma^{d}),

(1.18)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=

\displaystyle 0,

(1.19)

where the condition that
\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3})\in SO(3)
is implied by the equations themselves.
We have also rewrite the body force:

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{F})_{i}=\partial_{j}\sigma^{d}_{ij}+\partial_{i}\widetilde{p},\quad\sigma^{d}_{ij}=-\frac{\partial f_{Bi}}{\partial(\partial_{j}\mathfrak{p})}\cdot\partial_{i}\mathfrak{p},

with
\widetilde{p}
absorbed into the pressure term
p
(see Lemma
2.7
for details).

The relations between coefficients (
1.2
) will guarantee that the biaxial hydrodynamics (
1.15
)–(
1.19
) has the following basic energy dissipation law
[
18
]
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\int|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\mathcal{F}_{Bi}[\mathfrak{p}]\Big)=-\eta\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathscr{L}_{k}\mathcal{F}_{Bi}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\bigg(\beta_{1}\|\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+2\beta_{0}\int(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{1})(\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\beta_{2}\|\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad-\Big(\beta_{3}-\frac{\eta^{2}_{3}}{\chi_{3}}\Big)\|\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{3}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\Big(\beta_{4}-\frac{\eta^{2}_{2}}{\chi_{2}}\Big)\|\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{4}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\Big(\beta_{5}-\frac{\eta^{2}_{1}}{\chi_{1}}\Big)\|\mathbf{A}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{5}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(1.20)

To simplify the presentation, compared with the original model derived by
[

, we have assumed that the concentration of rigid molecules, together with the product of the Boltzmann constant and the absolute temperature, are all equal to one.

1.3
The main result

For any given constant orthonormal frame
\mathfrak{p}^{*}=(\mathbf{n}^{*}_{1},\mathbf{n}^{*}_{2},\mathbf{n}^{*}_{3})\in SO(3)
, we denote

\displaystyle H^{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{*}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2},SO(3)\big)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\big\{\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3}):\mathfrak{p}-\mathfrak{p}^{*}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{3}),~|\mathbf{n}_{i}|=1~{\rm a.e.~in}~\mathbb{R}^{2},i=1,2,3\big\}.

Given two constants
\tau
and
T
with
0\leq\tau<T
, two spaces
V(\tau,T)
and
H(\tau,T)
are defined by

\displaystyle V(\tau,T)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathfrak{p}=(\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2},\mathbf{n}_{3}):\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[\tau,T]\rightarrow SO(3)\Big|\mathfrak{p}(t)\in H^{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{*}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2},SO(3)\big)~\text{for a.e.}~t\in[\tau,T]

\displaystyle\quad\text{and satisfies}~\mathop{\mathrm{ess\sup}}_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\mathfrak{p}(\cdot,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int^{T}_{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(|\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{p}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\mathfrak{p}|^{2})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\mathrm{d}t<\infty,

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}~|\nabla\mathfrak{p}(\cdot,t)|^{2}=\sum^{3}_{i=1}|\nabla\mathbf{n}_{i}(\cdot,t)|^{2},~|\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{p}|^{2}=\sum^{3}_{i=1}|\nabla^{2}\mathbf{n}_{i}|^{2},~|\partial_{t}\mathfrak{p}|^{2}=\sum^{3}_{i=1}|\partial_{t}\mathbf{n}_{i}|^{2}\bigg\},

\displaystyle H(\tau,T)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}

\displaystyle\bigg\{\mathbf{v}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[\tau,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}\Big|\mathbf{v}~\text{is measurable and satisfies}

\displaystyle\quad\mathop{\mathrm{ess\sup}}_{\tau\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\mathbf{v}(\cdot,t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\int^{T}_{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla\mathbf{v}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\mathrm{d}t<\infty\bigg\}.

Let us introduce the global existence result of weak solutions of the frame hydrodynamics for biaxial nematics in dimensional two, which is established in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1.1

(see
[
18
]
)
.

Let
(\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})\in H^{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{*}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2},SO(3)\big)\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2})
be given initial data with
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}_{0}=0
and
\mathfrak{p}_{0}=\big(\mathbf{n}_{1}(\mathbf{x},0),\mathbf{n}_{2}(\mathbf{x},0),\mathbf{n}_{3}(\mathbf{x},0)\big)\in SO(3)
. Then there exists a global weak solution

of the biaxial frame system
(
1.15
)–(
1.19
)
such that the solution
(\mathfrak{p},\mathbf{v})
is smooth in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times((0,+\infty)\setminus\{T_{l}\}^{L}_{l=1})
for a finite number of times
\{T_{l}\}^{L}_{l=1}
. Furthermore, there exists two constants
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and

such that each singular point
(x^{l}_{i},T_{l})
is characterized by the condition

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\nearrow T_{l}}\int_{B_{R}(x^{l}_{i})}\big(|\nabla\mathfrak{p}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}|^{2}\big)(\cdot,t)\mathrm{d}t>\varepsilon_{0},\quad|\nabla\mathfrak{p}|^{2}=\sum^{3}_{i=1}|\nabla\mathbf{n}_{i}|^{2},

for any

.

The solution constructed in Theorem
1.1
is also called the Struwe-type weak solution.
In the following, we will show the uniqueness of the weak solution in the class
V(0,T)\times H(0,T)
for arbitrary

.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the assumptions in Theorem
1.1
are satisfied. Let
(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(1)})
and
(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)},\mathbf{v}^{(2)})
be two weak solutions of the frame hydrodynamic system (
1.15
)–(
1.19
)
determined by Theorem
1.1
,
subject to the same initial data
(\mathfrak{p}_{0},\mathbf{v}_{0})
. Then we have
(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(1)})=(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}(t),\mathbf{v}^{(2)}(t))
for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

To show the above theorem, we introduce a suitable weaker metrics to arrive at a closed energy estimate.
The main obstacle is to control the higher-order derivative terms involving
\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
(see (
3.11
) for the definitions).
We overcome it by utilizing the orthogonal decomposition (
1.2
) to obtain the following dissipative estimate (see Lemma
3.1
):

\displaystyle\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\text{lower~order~terms},

where
\gamma=\min\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\}
,
\chi=\max\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3}\}
,
\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}-\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}
, and
\Delta_{j}
is the Littlewood–Paley operator defined in Subsection
2.1
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we briefly introduce the Littlewood–Paley theory and some useful lemmas to be utilized subsequently.
Section 3 is devoted to the proof of the uniqueness of weak solution to the frame hydrodynamics. The dissipative estimate of higher-order derivative terms, which ensures the closure of energy estimates, will be discussed.

2.1
Littlewood-Paley theory

We denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Schwartz space. Let
\mathcal{C}
be the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
and
\mathcal{B}
the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
. There exist two nonnegative radial functions
\chi,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
supported in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,~~\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2\Rightarrow\text{Supp}~\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)\cap\text{Supp}~\varphi(2^{-j^{\prime}}\xi)=\emptyset.

Denoting by
\mathscr{F}
and
\mathscr{F}^{-1}
the Fourier transform and its inverse, respectively, the frequency localization operators
\Delta_{j}
and
S_{j}
can be defined by

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)\mathscr{F}f)=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(2^{j}\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},~~\text{for}~j\geq 0,

\displaystyle S_{j}f\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\chi(2^{-j}\xi)\mathscr{F}f)=\sum_{-1\leq k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}f=2^{jd}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{h}(2^{j}\mathbf{y})f(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{y},

\displaystyle\Delta_{-1}f=

\displaystyle S_{0}f,\qquad\Delta_{j}f=0,~~\text{for}~j\leq-2,

where
h=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\varphi
and
\tilde{h}=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\chi
. By the choice of
\varphi
, it can be proved that

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\Delta_{k}f=0,~~\text{if}~|j-k|\geq 2,

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(S_{k-1}f\Delta_{k}f)=0,~~\text{if}~|j-k|\geq 5.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
. By the operator
\Delta_{j}
, we can define the norm of an element
f
in the nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,q}
as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\big\|\{2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\}_{j\geq-1}\big\|_{\ell^{q}},\quad\|f\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\sup_{j\geq-1}\{2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{p}}\}.

In particular, a function
f\in H^{s}
is characterized as follows:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}}\sim\big\|\{2^{js}\|\Delta_{j}f\|_{L^{2}}\}_{j\geq-1}\big\|_{\ell^{2}}.

For two smooth functions
u
and
v
, the Bony’s paraproduct decomposition in nonhomogeneous case is defined by

\displaystyle uv=T_{u}v+T_{v}u+R(u,v),

where

\displaystyle T_{u}v=\sum_{j}S_{j-1}u\Delta_{j}v,\quad R(u,v)=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 1}\Delta_{j}u\Delta_{j}^{\prime}v.

Lemma 2.1

(

Before presenting the proof the uniqueness, we need to discuss the suitable choice of the energy metric for the resulting system of the difference.
A usual idea to prove the uniqueness is to derive the energy estimates for the system (
3.1
)–(
3.5
) based on the following natural energy metric:

\displaystyle\|\delta_{\mathbf{v}}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\sum^{3}_{k=1}\|\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

However, in the sense of weak solutions, the above approach will no longer be available, since we would encounter nonlinear terms like
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\delta_{\mathbf{F}_{k}}\cdot\Delta\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}(k=1,2,3)
, which cannot result in a closed energy estimate by using the embedding inequalities. A similar problem has been discussed in
[
35
,
17
,
30
]
for the Ericksen–Lesile model.

As a consequence, a key ingredient to control the difference
(\delta_{\mathfrak{p}},\delta_{\mathbf{v}})
in the class
V(0,T)\times H(0,T)
is to introduce a suitable weaker metric:

\displaystyle\Phi(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\mathcal{V}(t)+\mathcal{U}(t),

(3.6)

where for some
s\in(0,1/2)
,
\mathcal{V}(t)
and
\mathcal{U}(t)
are given by, respectively,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(t)=\sup\limits_{j\geq-1}2^{-2sj}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\quad\mathcal{U}(t)=\sup\limits_{j\geq-1}2^{2(1-s)j}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum^{3}_{k=1}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},~\text{for $j\geq-1$},\quad\|\Delta_{-1}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum\limits^{3}_{k=1}\|\Delta_{-1}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Here
\Delta_{j}
is the Littlewood–Paley operator defined in Subsection
2.1
.
Again, we introduce the following functional
\mathcal{W}(t)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t)=\sup\limits_{j\geq-1}2^{-2sj}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\|\Delta_{-1}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
W^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)
is defined as

\displaystyle W^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{3}\gamma_{i}\|\nabla\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}k_{i}\|\nabla\cdot\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}k_{ij}\|\mathbf{n}_{i}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla\times\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{j}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big),

(3.7)

where the coefficients

are expressed by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

Moreover, to close the energy estimate for the difference
(\delta_{\mathfrak{p}},\delta_{\mathbf{v}})
, we also need the following simple relation:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\|\Delta_{-1}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\geq c2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

(3.8)

which implies that
\mathcal{W}(t)\geq c~\mathcal{U}(t)
for some constant

.

In order to present the proof of Theorem
1.2
conveniently, we introduce a locally integrable function on
[0,T]
as follows:

\displaystyle F(t)\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\hbox{\footnotesize def}}}

\displaystyle 1+\|(\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(1)},\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(\mathbf{v}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(2)})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{t}\mathfrak{p}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|(\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

where

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{3}\Big(\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{k}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{P}\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(2)}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}})\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\|\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{P}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{(2)}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{l}})\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\|[\Delta_{j},\mathcal{P}]\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|[\Delta_{j},\mathcal{P}]\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)\|\mathcal{T}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\sum_{i=1}^{3}\bigg(\|\Delta_{j}\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}+2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}+2^{js}\big(\mathcal{U}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)+\mathcal{V}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\big)F^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)

\displaystyle+2^{\frac{js}{2}}F^{\frac{1}{4}}(t)\mathcal{U}^{\frac{1}{4}}(t)\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 9}2^{\frac{j^{\prime}}{2}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 9}2^{4j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{2j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C2^{2js}\big(\mathcal{U}(t)+\mathcal{V}(t)\big)F(t),

where we have used the expression
V^{(1)}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
that are given by

\displaystyle V^{(1)}_{1}=(0,\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{3},-\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{2}),\quad V^{(1)}_{2}=(-\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{3},0,\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{1}),\quad V^{(1)}_{3}=(\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{2},-\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{1},0).

Similarly, the terms
\mathcal{A}_{12}
and
\mathcal{A}_{13}
can be handled as, respectively,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{12}|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{F}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg(\|\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{P}\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(2)}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{k}})\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{l=1}^{3}\|\Delta_{j}(\mathcal{P}\mathbf{h}_{k}^{(2)}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{l}})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\Delta_{j}(\mathbf{v}^{(1)}\cdot\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Delta_{j}(\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\cdot\mathbf{n}_{i}^{(2)})\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 9}2^{4j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{2j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C2^{2js}\big(\mathcal{U}(t)+\mathcal{V}(t)\big)F(t),

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{13}|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\big(\|[\Delta_{j},\mathcal{P}]\nabla\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|[\Delta_{j},\mathcal{P}]\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}\big)\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 9}2^{4j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{2j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C2^{2js}\big(\mathcal{U}(t)+\mathcal{V}(t)\big)F(t).

Combining the above estimates of
\mathcal{A}_{1k}(k=1,2,3)
with (
3.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-\Big\langle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\cdot\mathbf{a}^{(1)}_{1},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{3}\Big\rangle-\Big\langle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\cdot\mathbf{a}^{(1)}_{2},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{2}\Big\rangle-\Big\langle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{\Omega}}\cdot\mathbf{a}^{(1)}_{3},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{1}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\langle\frac{\eta_{3}}{\chi_{3}}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\mathbf{s}^{(1)}_{3},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{3}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\frac{\eta_{2}}{\chi_{2}}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\mathbf{s}^{(1)}_{4},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{2}\Big\rangle+\Big\langle\frac{\eta_{1}}{\chi_{1}}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{A}}\cdot\mathbf{s}^{(1)}_{5},\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{1}\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C2^{2js}\big(\mathcal{U}(t)+\mathcal{V}(t)\big)F(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 9}2^{4j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{2j^{\prime}}\|\Delta_{j^{\prime}}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, plugging the estimates of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
into (

) yields (

). This completes the proof of Claim 2.

In the end, to control the higher order derivative term
\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}
that enable us to close the energy estimate, we need a higher order dissipated estimate for
\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. To be specific, we have the following key lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, it follows that

\displaystyle-\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CF(t)\mathcal{U}(t)+\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where
\chi=\max\{\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\chi_{3}\}
and
\gamma=\min\{\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}\}
, and
\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
are defined by (
3.11
).

Proof.

First of all, we recall the decomposition (
1.2
) formed by the tangential spaces
T_{\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}}SO(3)(\alpha=1,2)
and its orthogonal complements.
In (
1.2
), taking
B=\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle A\cdot\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{3}\frac{1}{|V^{(\alpha)}_{k}|^{2}}(A\cdot V^{(\alpha)}_{k})(\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot V^{(\alpha)}_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{6}\frac{1}{|W^{(\alpha)}_{k}|^{2}}(A\cdot W^{(\alpha)}_{k})(\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot W^{(\alpha)}_{k}),\quad\alpha=1,2,

(3.25)

where
V^{(\alpha)}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
and
W^{(\alpha)}_{k}(k=1,\cdots,6)
are the orthogonal basis of the tangential space
T_{\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}}SO(3)
and its associated orthogonal complement space, respectively, that is,

\displaystyle V^{(\alpha)}_{1}=

\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{3},-\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{2}),\quad V^{(\alpha)}_{2}=(-\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{3},0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{1}),\quad V^{(\alpha)}_{3}=(\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{2},-\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{1},0),

\displaystyle W^{(\alpha)}_{1}=

\displaystyle(0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{3},\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{2}),\quad W^{(\alpha)}_{2}=(\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{3},0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{1}),\quad W^{(\alpha)}_{3}=(\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{2},\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{1},0),

\displaystyle W^{(\alpha)}_{4}=

\displaystyle(\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{1},0,0),\quad W^{(\alpha)}_{5}=(0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{2},0),\quad W^{(\alpha)}_{6}=(0,0,\mathbf{n}^{(\alpha)}_{3}).

Armed with the definitions of
W^{(\alpha)}_{k}(k=1,\cdots,6)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot W^{(\alpha)}_{k}=

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}(\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot W^{(\alpha)}_{k})-[\Delta_{j},W^{(\alpha)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{j}\big(\nabla\cdot(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot W^{(\alpha)}_{k})-\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot\nabla W^{(\alpha)}_{k}\big)-[\Delta_{j},W^{(\alpha)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta_{j}(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}\cdot\nabla W^{(\alpha)}_{k})-[\Delta_{j},W^{(\alpha)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(\alpha)}.

Noticing
|V^{(1)}_{k}|=|V^{(2)}_{k}|
and
|W^{(1)}_{k}|=|W^{(2)}_{k}|
, and using (
3.2
) we derive

\displaystyle A\cdot\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}=

\displaystyle\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{|V^{(1)}_{k}|^{2}}(A\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})(\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})

\displaystyle+\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{|V^{(1)}_{k}|^{2}}\Big((A\cdot\delta_{V_{k}})(\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})+(A\cdot V^{(2)}_{k})(\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot\delta_{V_{k}})\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum^{6}_{k=1}\frac{1}{|W^{(1)}_{k}|^{2}}\bigg\{(A\cdot W^{(1)}_{k})\Big(\Delta_{j}(\nabla\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\nabla W^{(1)}_{k})+[\Delta_{j},W^{(1)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle+(A\cdot W^{(1)}_{k})\Big(\Delta_{j}(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla\delta_{W_{k}})+[\Delta_{j},\delta_{W_{k}}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\Big)

\displaystyle+(A\cdot\delta_{W_{k}})\Big(\Delta_{j}(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla W^{(2)}_{k})+[\Delta_{j},W^{(2)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\Big)\bigg\},

(3.26)

where
\delta_{V_{k}}=V^{(1)}_{k}-V^{(2)}_{k}
and
\delta_{W_{k}}=W^{(1)}_{k}-W^{(2)}_{k}
.

Taking
A=\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}
in (
3.2
) and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and using Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
, we can derive

\displaystyle\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\sum^{3}_{k=1}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\delta_{V_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\sum^{6}_{k=1}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\Delta_{j}(\nabla\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\nabla W^{(1)}_{k})\|_{L^{2}}+\|[\Delta_{j},W^{(1)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+C\sum^{6}_{k=1}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\Delta_{j}(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla\delta_{W_{k}})\|_{L^{2}}+\|[\Delta_{j},\delta_{W_{k}}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+C\sum^{6}_{k=1}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\delta_{W_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big(\|\Delta_{j}(\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\cdot\nabla W^{(2)}_{k})\|_{L^{2}}+\|[\Delta_{j},W^{(2)}_{k}\cdot]\Delta\mathfrak{p}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C2^{2js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)+\varepsilon\sum^{j+9}_{l=j-9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Similarly, by taking
A=\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}
in (
3.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}\big[(\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\big](\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle+C2^{2js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)+\varepsilon\sum^{j+9}_{l=j-9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Furthermore, applying integration by parts, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(k_{i}\|\nabla\rm{div}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}k_{\alpha i}\|\nabla(\nabla\times\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-C2^{2js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)-\varepsilon\sum^{j+9}_{l=j-9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, by the definitions of
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{T}
, and using (
3.20
) and (
3.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}\sum_{k=1}^{3}(\mathcal{G}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})(\mathcal{T}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle C\sum^{3}_{i=1}\|\mathcal{G}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathcal{T}_{i}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C2^{js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)+\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Then, collecting the above estimates, and recalling the definitions of
\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}(k=1,2,3)
in (
3.11
), i.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}=\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}
, together with
\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}}=\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{T}
, we can derive that

\displaystyle\sum^{3}_{k=1}\frac{1}{\chi_{k}}\|\mathcal{H}^{\Delta_{j}}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{\chi}\sum^{3}_{k=1}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{h}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{1}{\chi}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\|\mathcal{G}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{2}{\chi}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}\big(\mathcal{G}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})(\mathcal{T}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{2\gamma}{\chi}\sum_{k=1}^{3}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}\big[(\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\big](\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k})\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\cdot V^{(1)}_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-C2^{js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)-\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{4\gamma}{\chi}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}}}(\mathcal{G}-\gamma\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}})\cdot\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-C2^{js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)-\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geq\frac{4\gamma}{\chi}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\Big(k_{i}\|\nabla\rm{div}\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}k_{\alpha i}\|\nabla\times(\nabla\times\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\cdot\mathbf{n}^{(1)}_{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{2\gamma^{2}}{\chi}\|\Delta\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-C2^{js}F(t)\mathcal{U}(t)-\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we complete the proof the key lemma.

3.3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We are now in a position to complete the proof Theorem
1.2
. First of all, we know from Theorem
1.1
in subsection 1.3 and Proposition 4.2 in
[
18
]
that, for any
\theta,M>0
, the pair
(\mathfrak{p},\mathbf{v})
satisfies

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{p},\mathbf{v})\in

\displaystyle V(0,T_{1}-\theta)\times H(0,T_{1}-\theta)\cup\cdots\cup V(T_{L-1},T_{L}-\theta)\times H(T_{L-1},T_{L}-\theta)

\displaystyle\cup V(T_{L},M)\times H(T_{L},M),

where
\{T_{l}\}^{L}_{l=1}
are the finite number of singular times.
Assume that
T^{(i)}_{1}
is the first blow-up time of
(\mathfrak{p}^{(i)},\mathbf{v}^{(i)})(i=1,2)
. By means of Proposition 4.2 in
[
18
]
, we may find that the following regularity for
(\mathfrak{p}^{i},\mathbf{v}^{(i)})
holds,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T_{1}-\theta]}|\nabla^{2}\mathfrak{p}^{(i)}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathfrak{p}^{(i)}|^{4}+|\nabla\mathbf{v}^{(i)}|^{2}+|\mathbf{v}^{(i)}|^{4}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}\mathrm{d}t<+\infty,

(3.27)

where
\theta>0
and
T_{1}=\min\{T^{(1)}_{1},T^{(2)}_{1}\}
. Then, taking advantage of the equations (
1.15
)–(
1.17
), we can infer that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathfrak{p}^{(i)}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,T_{1}-\theta]).

(3.28)

Further, (
3.27
) and (
3.28
) imply that
F(t)
is a locally integrable function, i.e.,
F(t)\in L^{1}(0,T_{1}-\theta)
.

On the other hand, summing up (
3.1
), (
3.15
) and (
3.2
), and together with Lemma
3.1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}W^{j}(\mathbf{x},t)\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta_{-1}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}\Big)

(3.29)

\displaystyle\qquad+ca_{j}\big(2^{2j}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2^{4j}\|\Delta_{j}\delta_{\mathbf{n}_{i}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

(3.30)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C2^{2js}F(t)\Phi(t)+\varepsilon\sum^{j+4}_{l=j-4}2^{2l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathbf{v}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\sum_{l=j-9}^{j+9}2^{4l}\|\Delta_{l}\delta_{\mathfrak{p}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.31)

Recalling the relation (
3.8
) and taking the supreme in
j
, and choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we can derive from (
3.29
) that

\displaystyle\Phi(t)\leq C\int_{0}^{t}F(\tau)\Phi(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau.

(3.32)

Then, applying Lemma
2.5
, that is the Osgood lemma, to (
3.32
) and using the given same initial data, we find that
\Phi(t)=0
for any
\theta>0
and
t\in[0,T_{1}-\theta]
, where
T_{1}
is the first singular time. Consequently, we have
(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(1)})(t)=(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)},\mathbf{v}^{(2)})(t)
for any
t\in[0,T_{1})
. Indeed, we can further get
(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(1)})(T_{1})=(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)},\mathbf{v}^{(2)})(T_{1})
, since
(\mathfrak{p}^{(i)},\mathbf{v}^{(i)})(t)
is weakly continuous for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
, i.e.,
(\mathfrak{p}^{(i)},\mathbf{v}^{(i)})\in C_{w}([0,+\infty);H^{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{*}}\times L^{2})
.
The similar argument implies that there exists the second singular time
T_{2}>0(T_{2}>T_{1})
and such that
(\mathbf{v}^{(1)},\mathfrak{p}^{(1)})(t)=(\mathbf{v}^{(2)},\mathfrak{p}^{(2)})(t)
with
t\in[T_{1},T_{2})
. We can thus obtain
(\mathfrak{p}^{(1)},\mathbf{v}^{(1)})(t)=(\mathfrak{p}^{(2)},\mathbf{v}^{(2)})(t)
for any
t\in[0,+\infty)
, since the number of singular time for weak solution is finite. Hence, we finish the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

New Estimates of Rychkov’s Universal Extension Operator for Lipschitz Domains and Some Applications
Ziming Shi, Liding Yao

Abstract
. Given a bounded Lipschitz domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , Rychkov showed that there is a linear extension operator ℰ \mathcal{E} for Ω \Omega which is bounded in Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. In this paper we introduce some new estimates for the extension operator ℰ \mathcal{E} and give some applications. We prove the equivalent norms ‖ f ‖ 𝒜 p ​ q s ​ ( Ω ) ≈ ∑ | α | ≤ m ‖ ∂ α f ‖ 𝒜 p ​ q s − m ​ ( Ω ) \|f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\Omega)} for general Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. We also derive some quantitative smoothing estimates of the extended function and all its derivatives on Ω ¯ c \overline{\Omega}^{c} up to the boundary.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we prove the following result on the equivalence of norms.

Theorem 1.1

(Equivalent norms in Lipschitz domains)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
(
p<\infty
for the
\mathscr{F}
-cases) and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For any positive integer
m
, there is a
C=C_{\Omega,p,q,s,m}>0
such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s-m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)},

(1.2)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s-m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}.

At first look, this appears to be a well-known result, but surprisingly
no complete and correct proof has appeared in the literature to the best of the authors’ knowledge. One can indeed find the proof in the case of
C^{\infty}
domains (
[
Tri10
, Theorem 3.3.5]
) and in the case of Sobolev and Hölder spaces (
[
Ste70
, Chapter V]
). See also the discussions in Section
2.2
.

In fact, Theorem
1.1
was stated by Triebel in
[
Tri08
, Proposition 4.21]
. However, there is a gap in his proof, in which the following statement is claimed:

(1.3)

\|f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}\approx\|\mathcal{E}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\mathcal{E}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\Omega)}.

Here
\mathcal{E}
is an extension operator (see (
1.6
) below) which is bounded on
\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<p,q\leq\infty
, where
p<\infty
if
\mathscr{A}=\mathscr{F}
. The problem with this argument lies in the fact that the commutativity used in the equality
\partial^{\alpha}\circ\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}\circ\partial^{\alpha}
in (
1.3
) (see
[
Tri08
, (4.70)]
) cannot be achieved, see Remark
1.8
.

In order to fix the proof, our idea is to modify Triebel’s argument by introducing certain operators
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha,\beta}
, which have “similar structure” and boundedness property as
\mathcal{E}
(see Proposition
6.4
and Remark
6.8
) such that

(1.4)

\partial^{\alpha}\circ\mathcal{E}=\sum_{\beta}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha,\beta}\circ\partial^{\beta}.

For
\mathcal{E}
, we use Rychkov’s extension operator
[
Ryc99b
]
: for a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
there exists an operator
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
that is bounded on Besov spaces
\mathcal{E}:\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and on Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
\mathcal{E}:\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is one single operator which works for all such spaces, we call it an

on
\Omega
.

By carefully studying Rychkov’s construction, we also derive the following quantitative smoothing estimate.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then Rychkov’s extension operator
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
(see (
6.1
)) satisfies the following estimate on the complement domain

:

(1.5)

[f\mapsto(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f)|_{\overline{\Omega}^{c}}]:\mathscr{F}_{p\infty}^{s}(\Omega)\to W^{m,p}(\overline{\Omega}^{c},\operatorname{dist}_{\Omega}^{m-s}),\quad\text{ for }1\leq p\leq\infty,\quad m=0,1,2,\dots,\quad\text{ and }s<m.

In particular,
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega}^{c})
for every
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Omega)
.

As a special case to Theorem

, for
f
in the Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\Omega)(\subsetneq\mathscr{F}_{p\infty}^{k}(\Omega)
) where
1<p\leq\infty
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f\in W^{m,p}(\overline{\Omega}^{c},\operatorname{dist}_{\Omega}^{m-k})
for all

. This estimate is known if
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
is the Stein extension operator
[
Ste70
]
for
k\geq 0
, see
[
Bur98
, Theorem 6.3, Page 285]
. In our case we allow

as well (while still requiring

). The estimate is optimal in the sense that the derivatives of higher orders of
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f
on
\overline{\Omega}^{c}
have the minimal possible growth when approaching

[
Bur98

1.1.
The general results for Rychkov-type operators

We now explain Rychkov’s construction of the universal extension operator on a bounded Lipschitz domain. By partition of unity it suffices to construct the operator on special Lipschitz domains. By a special Lipschitz domain we mean an open set
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the form
\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):x_{n}>\rho(x^{\prime})\}
where
\rho
is a Lipschitz function such that
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
. (See Definition
1.11
and Remark
1.12
.)

On a special Lipschitz domain
\omega
, Rychkov uses a double-convolution construction to define a universal extension operator
E_{\omega}
on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
, the space of restrictions of tempered distributions to
\omega
, as

(1.6)

E_{\omega}f:=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{j}\ast f)),\qquad f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega).

Here
(\psi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
and
(\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
are carefully chosen families of Schwartz functions that depend on
\phi_{0},\psi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{1}
and satisfy the following properties:

•

Scaling condition
:
\phi_{j}(x)=2^{(j-1)n}\phi_{1}(2^{j-1}x)
and
\psi_{j}(x)=2^{(j-1)n}\psi_{1}(2^{j-1}x)
for
j\geq 2
.

•

Moment condition
:
\int\phi_{0}=\int\psi_{0}=1
,
\int x^{\alpha}\phi_{0}(x)dx=\int x^{\alpha}\psi_{0}(x)dx=0
for all multi-indices
|\alpha|>0
, and
\int x^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(x)dx=\int x^{\alpha}\psi_{1}(x)dx=0
for all
|\alpha|\geq 0
.

•

Approximate identity
:
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}=\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta measure, which is the identity element for the convolution.

•

Support condition
:
\phi_{0},\psi_{0},\phi_{1},\psi_{1}
are all supported in the negative cone
-\mathbb{K}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):x_{n}<-|x^{\prime}|\}
.

The first three conditions show that
(\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
induces a Littlewood-Paley decomposition on
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
The support condition guarantees
E_{\omega}
is well-defined for functions defined only on
\omega
, since we have
\omega+\mathbb{K}\subseteq\omega
. That
E_{\omega}
is an extension operator follows from the approximate identity and support condition:
E_{\omega}f|_{\omega}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}\ast f=f
, whenever the summation converges in the sense of distributions.

The smoothing effect of
E_{\omega}
can be seen as follows: for any
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\omega)
,
E_{\omega}f
is a smooth function on
\overline{\omega}^{c}
. This is because when
x_{0}\in\overline{\omega}^{c}
, the summand
\psi_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{j}\ast f))(x)
and all its derivatives decay rapidly in a neighborhood
x_{0}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note that there is no extension operator defined on
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\omega)
, since a distribution
f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\omega)
admits an extension to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
only when it has finite order (as a distribution) near
\partial\omega
. Cf.
[
Ryc99b
, Page 4, (3)]
.

To prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Definition 1.4
.

A
generalized Littlewood-Paley family

\eta=(\eta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is a collection of Schwartz functions depending only on

, such that

•

\eta_{1}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and all its moments vanish. That is,
\int x^{\alpha}\eta_{1}(x)dx=0
for all multi-indices
\alpha
.

•

\eta_{j}(x)=2^{(j-1)n}\eta_{1}(2^{j-1}x)
for
j\geq 2
,

.

We denote by
\mathfrak{G}=\mathfrak{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of all such families

For an open set
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
\mathfrak{G}(V)
as the set of all
(\eta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\eta_{j}\subset V

Clearly, the map

is a bijection between

1.2.
An application to several complex variables

Applying Theorem
1.5

2
in a different way, we have the following formula analogous to (
1.11
):

(1.12)

\tilde{f}=\psi_{0}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{0}\ast\tilde{f}))+\sum_{|\beta|=m}\partial^{\beta}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\eta}^{\beta}_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{j}\ast\tilde{f}))\Big),\quad\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\quad m=1,2,3,\dots.

If
\tilde{f}|_{\omega}\equiv 0
then
\psi_{0}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{0}\ast\tilde{f}))|_{\omega}=\tilde{\eta}^{\beta}_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\phi_{j}\ast\tilde{f}))|_{\omega}\equiv 0
as well. This leads to the following:

Proposition 1.7

(Anti-derivatives with support constraint)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Then for any positive integer
m
, there exist linear operators
\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}:\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
|\alpha|\leq m
, such that

(1)

\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}:\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
0<p,q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}:\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s+m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

(2)

g=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\partial^{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}g)
for all
g\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(3)

If
g\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
g|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
, then
(\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}g)|_{\Omega}\equiv 0
for all
|\alpha|\leq m
.

In a recent paper
[
SY24
]
, the authors applied Proposition
1.7
to several complex variables and obtained a new Sobolev estimate for the
\overline{\partial}
equation on strongly pseudoconvex domains. For the equation
\overline{\partial}u=\varphi
, our solution operator contains an integral of the form

(1.13)

\int_{\mathcal{U}\backslash\overline{\Omega}}K(z,\cdot)\wedge[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi,\quad z\in\Omega,

where
\mathcal{U}
is some small neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
.

To estimate the integral, the differential form
\varphi
cannot be too low in regularity, in particular we need
\varphi
to be in
H^{s,p}
, where
s
cannot be negative. In order to estimate the solution when
\varphi\in H^{s,p}
, for
s<0
, we apply Proposition
1.7
to the commutator so that the integral becomes

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\mathcal{U}\backslash\overline{\Omega}}K(z,\cdot)\wedge\partial^{\alpha}\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}([\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi),\quad z\in\Omega.

Using “integration by parts”, we can move the derivatives to the kernel:

\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}(-1)^{|\alpha|}\int_{\mathcal{U}\backslash\overline{\Omega}}\partial^{\alpha}(K(z,\cdot))\wedge\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}([\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi),\quad z\in\Omega.

Now, the commutator
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi=\overline{\partial}(\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\varphi)-\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}(\overline{\partial}\varphi)
has an important property that it vanishes identically inside
\Omega
. By Proposition
1.7
with
m
chosen to be sufficiently large, we can make
\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}([\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi)\in H^{s,p}(\Omega)
for
s>0
, while
\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}([\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi)
still vanishes inside
\Omega
. As a result,
\mathcal{S}^{m,\alpha}_{\Omega}([\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]\varphi)(\zeta)
will contribute some vanishing order as
\zeta\in\overline{\Omega}^{c}
approaches the boundary, which cancels with the singularity of the kernel
\partial^{\alpha}_{\zeta}K(z,\zeta)
and enables us derive the sharp estimate.

Remark 1.8
.

On any bounded strongly pseudoconvex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, for
1\leq q\leq n-1
one can construct a
\overline{\partial}
-closed continuous
(0,q)
-form
\varphi
such that any solution

to the equation
\overline{\partial}u=\varphi
is not in

\mathscr{C}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for all
\varepsilon>0
(see e.g.
[

). This is one way to see that
[\overline{\partial},\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}]=\overline{\partial}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}-\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}\circ\overline{\partial}
in (

) cannot be the zero operator, since otherwise the solution operator can gain one derivative (see e.g.
[
Gon19
, Section 2]
).

1.3.
Terminology and organization

We denote by
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
the set of non-negative integers, and
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{1,2,3,\dots\}

Let

be an arbitrary set; we use
\mathbf{1}_{E}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\{0,1\}

.

We will use the notation

to mean that
x\leq Cy
where
C
is a constant independent of
x,y

We write
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)

\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\Omega
. There is also an intrinsic definition for
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\Omega)
, see
[
Ryc99b
, (3.1) and Proposition 3.1]
.

.

Definition 1.11
.

A

\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):x_{n}>\rho(x^{\prime})\}

Remark 1.12
.

In many literature, for example
[
Tri06
, Section 1.11.4 (1.322) p. 63]
, the definition for a special Lipschitz domain only requires
\rho
to be a Lipschitz function. In other words,
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}
is finite but can be arbitrary large.
By taking invertible linear transformations we can make
\nabla\rho

The assumption
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}<1
is necessary in order to guarantee the property
\omega+\mathbb{K}\subseteq\omega
, which is convenient in constructing the extension operator.

We define a
bounded Lipschitz domain
to be an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the boundary
\partial\Omega
is locally a Lipschitz graph. See also
[
Tri06
, Definition 1.103(ii)]
.

, such that

•

\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j},\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}\subset-\mathbb{K}\cap\{x_{n}<-2^{-j}\}
for all
j\geq 0
;

•

\phi=(\phi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\psi=(\psi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}(-\mathbb{K})
;

•

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi_{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}\ast\phi_{j}=\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta measure at
0\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Such
(\phi_{j},\psi_{j})_{j}
exists due to
[
Ryc99b
, Theorem 4.1(a)]
. Here the condition
\operatorname{supp}\phi_{j},\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}\subset\{x_{n}<-2^{-j}\}
can be obtained by scaling (see Lemma
5.2
).

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open set. Let
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a positive continuous function, we define

(1.14)

\begin{gathered}W^{m,p}(\Omega,\varphi):=\{f\in W^{m,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega):\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega,\varphi)}<\infty\},\\
\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega,\varphi)}:=\bigg(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{\Omega}|\varphi\partial^{\alpha}f|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\quad 1\leq p<\infty;\quad\|f\|_{W^{m,\infty}(\Omega,\varphi)}:=\sup\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\varphi\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.\end{gathered}

Let
0<p,q<\infty
. For a vector-valued function
g=(g_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}:\Omega\to\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z})
, we adopt the following notations for (quasi-)norms
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{q}(L^{p})}=\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z};L^{p}(\Omega))}
and
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{q})}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\ell^{q}(\mathbb{Z}))}
as

\|g\|_{\ell^{q}(L^{p})}:=\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|g_{j}(x)|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\|g\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{q})}:=\bigg(\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|g_{j}(x)|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p}{q}}dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For
p=\infty
or
q=\infty
we use the usual modification.

When we are dealing with the boundedness of Besov spaces and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, we use the following often-used notion of admissible indices:

Convention 1.14
.

Let
U,V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. We say an operator
T
is bounded linear
T:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(U)\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{t}(V)
for all
admissible indices

(p,q)
, if
T:\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(U)\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{t}(V)
is bounded linear for all
0<p,q\leq\infty
, and
T:\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(U)\to\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{t}(V)
is bounded linear for all
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
, and
p=q=\infty
. We shall always use
\mathscr{A}
as either the Besov class
\mathscr{B}
or the Triebel-Lizorkin class
\mathscr{F}
.

.
In Section

In Section

to provide the reader with the main ideas of the proof, and then we fill in all the details in Section

.
In Section

and Proposition

2.
Historical Remarks

2.1.
Some previous extension operators

When
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{\infty}
-domain, for any
\varepsilon>0
one can find an extension operator
S=S^{\varepsilon}
that is bounded linear
S:\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
S:\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
-\varepsilon^{-1}<s<\varepsilon^{-1}
,
\varepsilon<p\leq\infty
and
0<q\leq\infty
(
p<\infty
for the
\mathscr{F}
-cases). See
[
Tri10
, Section 2.9]
and
[
Tri06
, Section 1.11.5]
. The construction is based on the half-plane reflections: for
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define

(2.1)

Sf(x^{\prime},x_{n}):=\begin{cases}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})&x_{n}>0,\\
\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{j}f(x^{\prime},-b_{j}x_{n})&x_{n}<0.\end{cases}

Here
M
is some large integer (depending on
\varepsilon
),
a_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
b_{j}>0
are numbers satisfying
\sum_{j=1}^{M}a_{j}(-b_{j})^{k}=1
for all integers
k
with
-\frac{M}{2}<k<\frac{M}{2}
.

The half-plane construction can be traced back to Lichtenstein
[
Lic29
]
. By modifying (
2.1
), Seeley
[
See64
]
constructed an extension operator that is bounded
C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The boundedness of
S
on Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces are mostly done by Triebel (see
[
Tri92
, Section 4.5.1]
). See also
[
YSY10
, Section 6.4]
for some boundedness results of
S
on hybrid spaces.

Though
S=S^{\varepsilon}
is defined on Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces with index
s\in(-\varepsilon^{-1},0]
, in general
Sf|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}}
is not more regular than
f
as it reflects all the singularities of
f
. In particular, unlike Rychkov’s extension operator, we cannot expect
Sf|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{-}}
to be a smooth function if
f
is not smooth.

When
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define Sobolev space
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
in the classical way, which is (
1.14
) with
\varphi=1
.

Calderón
[
Cal61
]
showed that for each
k
there is a bounded linear extension operator
\mathcal{E}^{k}:W^{k,p}(\Omega)\to W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1<p<\infty
. Later Stein
[
Ste70
]
used an alternative approach to construct an extension operator
\mathcal{E}^{S}
which is independent of
k
, such that
\mathcal{E}^{S}:W^{k,p}(\Omega)\to W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Both results show that
W^{k,p}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
. Indeed
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1<p<\infty
(see
[
Tri10
, Section 2.5.6]
), so by definition (see Definition
3.3
) we get

(2.2)

\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}=W^{k,p}(\Omega).

The reader can also refer to
[
Kal85
]
for some discussion of Stein’s extension operator on Triebel-Lizorkin spaces.

For some domains whose boundaries are not Lipschitz, it is still possible to define useful extension operators. Jones
[
Jon81
]
introduced
locally uniform domains
, for which Lipschitz domains are a special case. For certain locally uniform domains
\Omega
(in particular all Lipschitz domains) and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, Jones constructed an extension operator that maps
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
to
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Later, the construction was refined by Rogers
[
Rog06
]
where the extension operator was chosen to be independent of
k
.

We also refer the reader to
[
Chr84
]
,
[
See89
, Theorem 2]
and
[
BD23
]
for more discussions on Jones’ extension.
It would be interesting to see whether the results of our paper can be extended to certain locally uniformly domains.

There are also a few studies on the case where the domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an
arbitrary subset
with no assumption on the boundary, starting from
[
Fef07
]
. For an arbitrary one-dimensional subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{1}
, in
[
Isr13
, Proposition 3.5]
there is a Besov extension
E=E_{\Omega}:\mathscr{B}_{pp}^{2-1/p}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{B}_{pp}^{2-1/p}(\mathbb{R})
for
2<p<\infty
. For higher dimension, in
[
Isr13
,
FIL14
]
, it was shown that for any
n<p<\infty
and integer
k\geq 1
, there exists a Sobolev extension
E=E_{\Omega,k,p}:\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It is an open problem whether we can make the extension operators independent of
k
.

2.2.
Two approaches to equivalent norms on domains

As mentioned earlier, Theorem
1.1
was known for bounded
C^{\infty}
domains with all
\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}
,
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}
, and for bounded Lipschitz domains with
\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}
,
\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}
for
s>m
and integer
k\geq m
. The proofs for these two special cases have different approaches. We sketch them below.

For the case where
\Omega
is bounded
C^{\infty}
domain, by partition of unity and composing with diffeomorphisms, we only need to prove (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) for the half plane
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. On half plane the idea is the following: for
\alpha\neq 0
there is an operator
S_{\alpha}
(which turns out to be an extension operator as well) such that

(S.a)

\partial^{\alpha}\circ S=S_{\alpha}\circ\partial^{\alpha}
.

(S.b)

S_{\alpha}
has the similar expression to (
2.1
): we can find some numbers
(a^{\alpha}_{j},b^{\alpha}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}
such that

(2.3)

\textstyle S_{\alpha}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})=f(x^{\prime},x_{n}),\quad x_{n}>0;\qquad S_{\alpha}f(x^{\prime},x_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{M}a^{\alpha}_{j}f(x^{\prime},-b^{\alpha}_{j}x_{n}),\quad x_{n}<0.

Similar to
S
, one can prove the boundedness for
S_{\alpha}
in Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces.
Therefore for every
m\geq 1
and for
\mathscr{A}\in\{\mathscr{B},\mathscr{F}\}
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|Sf\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}Sf\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|S_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})}.

This proves the essential part of (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
. See
[
Tri10
, Theorem 3.3.5]
for details.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we use a similar argument along with the decomposition (
1.11
). See Proposition
6.4
and Remark
6.5
.

In the case of Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(\Omega)
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Omega
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, Theorem
1.1
can be proved easily by using the extension operator and the intrinsic characterization of the spaces: for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and integer
k\geq m
,

(2.4)

\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\sum_{|\beta|\leq k-m}\|\partial^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{W^{k-m,p}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, we have
W^{k,p}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
(see
[
Tri06
, Theorem 1.122 (ii)]
). Since
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
Tri10
, Section 2.5.6]
) we have
W^{k,p}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}=\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}(\Omega)
. So (
1.2
) is done for the
\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}
-spaces for
1<p<\infty
with integer
k\geq m
.

Note that for the
\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{k}
-spaces, (
1.2
) is also true when
\Omega
is a locally uniform domain (which may not be Lipschitz) that satisfies the hypothesis used by
[
Rog06
]
.
For general Triebel-Lizorkin class
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}
, however, we do not have a good intrinsic characterization like (
1.2
), so this approach does not work.

Similarly for the Hölder-Zygmund spaces
\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)=\{f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}):\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)}<\infty\}
we can use the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\textstyle:=\sup|f|+\sup_{x,y\in\Omega;x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}},

\displaystyle\text{provided }0<s<1,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\textstyle:=\sup|f|+\sup_{x,y\in\Omega;x\neq y,\frac{x+y}{2}\in\Omega}\frac{|f(x)+f(y)-2f(\frac{x+y}{2})|}{|x-y|},

\displaystyle\text{provided }s=1,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\textstyle:=\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s-1}(\Omega)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\partial_{x_{j}}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s-1}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\text{provided }s>1.

By (
2.5
), we see that
\|f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)}\approx\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{\mathscr{C}^{s-m}(\Omega)}
if
s>m
.
Again by
\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
(see
[
Tri06
, Theorem 1.122 (ii)]
), since
\mathscr{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
Tri10
, Section 2.5.7]
) we have
\mathscr{C}^{s}(\Omega)=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}=\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\Omega)
, and (
1.1
) is done for the
\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}
-spaces when
s>m
.

3.
Boundedness On Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin Spaces

3.1.
Preliminary and preparations

We recall the characterizations of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces via the Littlewood-Paley decomposition.

Definition 3.1
.

A
classical dyadic resolution
is a sequence
\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, denoted by
\lambda\in\mathfrak{C}
, such that the Fourier transforms
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lambda_{j}(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}dx
satisfies

•

\widehat{\lambda}_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}\{|\xi|<2\}
,
\widehat{\lambda}_{0}|_{\{|\xi|<1\}}\equiv 1
.

•

\widehat{\lambda}_{j}(\xi)=\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)-\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(2^{-(j-1)}\xi)
for
j\geq 1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that for each
j\geq 1
,
\widehat{\lambda}_{j}
is supported on the annulus

\{2^{j-1}<|\xi|<2^{j+1}\}
. Thus for any
j\geq 0
we have
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\lambda}_{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}}=\sum_{k=j-1}^{j+1}\widehat{\lambda}_{k}|_{\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\lambda}_{j}}\equiv 1
, where we set
\lambda_{-1}=0
.
If we denote
\mu_{j}:=\sum_{k=j-1}^{j+1}\lambda_{j}
for
j\geq 0
, then

(1)

\lambda_{j}=\mu_{j}\ast\lambda_{j}
for all
j\geq 0
.

(2)

\mu_{j}(x)=2^{(j-2)n}\mu_{2}(2^{j-2}x)
for
j\geq 2
.

For any
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have the decomposition
f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\widehat{\lambda}_{j}\widehat{f})^{\vee}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\ast f
and both sums converge as tempered distribution. We can define the Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces using such
\lambda
.

Recall from Convention
1.14
that the pair
(p,q)
is said to be
admissible
, if we are discussing the function class
\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}
for
0<p,q\leq\infty
, and the function class
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}
for
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
or for
p=q=\infty
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and let
(p,q)
be admissible. We denote

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}:=\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\|_{\ell^{q}(L^{p})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\big(2^{js}\|\lambda_{j}\ast f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)_{j=0}^{\infty}\right\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}:=\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\|_{L^{p}(\ell^{q})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\big\|(2^{js}\lambda_{j}\ast f)_{j=0}^{\infty}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We define the
Besov space
\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the set of all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}<\infty
; and the
Triebel-Lizorkin space
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be the set of all
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}<\infty
.

We use the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}
for an (implicitly chosen)
\lambda\in\mathfrak{C}
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open subset. We define
\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega):=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
and
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega):=\{\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}:\tilde{f}\in\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
as the subspace of distributions in
\Omega
, with norms

\|f\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\inf\{\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f\},\quad\|f\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\inf\{\|\tilde{f}\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:\tilde{f}|_{\Omega}=f\}.

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

Different choices of
(\lambda_{j})_{j}
produce equivalent Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin norms, see
[
Tri10
, Proposition 2.3.2]
. So we can use
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
by dropping
\lambda
in the notations.
In application we fix one such choice of
(\lambda_{j})\in\mathfrak{C}
.

(2)

When
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}}
are norms. When
p<1
or
q<1
they are only quasi-norms.

(3)

For
1<p<\infty
,
\mathscr{F}_{p2}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the fractional Sobolev space (also known as the Bessel potential space). For
s>0
,
\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathscr{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the Hölder-Zygmund space.

(4)

It is clear from definition that if
(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
then
(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}
(Definition
1.4
). However, by a more sophisticated version of the uncertainty principle (for example, the Nazarov’s uncertainty principle
[
Jam07
]
), if a function has compact support on the Fourier side, then it cannot vanish outside any open subset on the physical side. In particular, for a cone
K\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is no
(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{C}
such that
(\lambda_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}(K)
.

(5)

We do not investigate
\mathscr{F}_{\infty q}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
or
\mathscr{F}_{\infty q}^{s}(\Omega)
for
0<q<\infty
in this paper, since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{\infty q}^{s}(\lambda)}
are not equivalent norms for different
\lambda\in\mathfrak{C}
. Using a correctly modified version of the norm (see
[
Tri20
,
YSY10
]
for examples), the analogs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.5

1
are still true. See
[
Yao23
]
.

(6)

In literatures like
[
Tri10
]
, the results for Triebel-Lizorkin space
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}
are usually only given for
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
, and the case
p=q=\infty
is omitted.
Note that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\lambda)}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{\infty\infty}^{s}(\lambda)}
for every
\lambda=(\lambda_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}
, so if we have a result for Besov space
\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}
, then the same holds for
\mathscr{F}_{\infty\infty}^{s}
since
\mathscr{F}_{\infty\infty}^{s}=\mathscr{B}_{\infty\infty}^{s}
is identically the same space (also see
[
Tri10
, Remark 2.3.4/3]
). This is the reason we include
(p,q)=(\infty,\infty)
as admissible indices (in
Convention
1.14
) in the discussion of Triebel-Lizorkin spaces.

In fact the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}
(
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\lambda)}
) are still equivalent if we merely assume
\lambda_{0},\lambda^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that satisfy
\widehat{\lambda}_{0}(\xi)=1+O(|\xi|^{\infty})
and
\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{0}(\xi)=1+O(|\xi|^{\infty})
as
\xi\to 0
; see
[
Ryc99b
, Proposition 1.2]
. The proof relies on Heideman type estimate
[
Hei74
]
, a more general version of which is the following.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\eta=(\eta_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}
. Then for any
M\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
there is a
C=C_{\eta,M}>0
such that

(3.1)

\int_{|x|>2^{-k}}|\eta_{j}\ast g(x)|(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}dx\leq C\|g\|_{2M+n}\cdot 2^{-M\max(j,j-k)},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},k\in\mathbb{Z},\quad g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Here for
l\in\mathbb{N}
, we use

(3.2)

\|g\|_{l}:=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|x|)^{l}|\partial^{\alpha}g(x)|.

In particular, by letting
k\to+\infty
in (
3.1
), we get

(3.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\eta_{j}\ast g(x)|(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}dx\leq C\|g\|_{2M+n}\cdot 2^{-Mj},\quad\forall j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\quad g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with the same constant
C=C_{\eta,M}>0
.

Proof.

For simplicity we denote
\eta_{0}(x):=2^{-n}\eta_{1}(2^{-1}x)
. So
\eta_{j}(x)=2^{nj}\eta_{0}(2^{j}x)
for all
j\geq 1
.

By assumption
\eta_{0}\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see Definitions
1.4
and
1.9
), we have
\int x^{\alpha}\eta_{0}(x)dx=0
for all
\alpha
. Therefore

\displaystyle\eta_{j}\ast g(x)

\displaystyle=\int\eta_{0}(y)g(x-2^{-j}y)dy=\int\eta_{0}(y)\Big(g(x-2^{-j}y)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}(-2^{-j}y)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}g(x)}{\alpha!}\Big)dy.

Here
N
is some large number to be chosen. By Taylor’s theorem,

\Big|g(x-2^{-j}y)-\sum_{|\alpha|<N}(-2^{-j}y)^{\alpha}\frac{\partial^{\alpha}g(x)}{\alpha!}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{N!}|2^{-j}y|^{N}\sup_{B(x,2^{-j}|y|)}|\nabla^{N}g|,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Using the notion
\|g\|_{N}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\sup_{x}(1+|x|)^{N}|\partial^{\alpha}g(x)|
, we have

(3.4)

\sup\limits_{B(x,2^{-j}|y|)}|\nabla^{N}g|\leq\|g\|_{N}\cdot\begin{cases}2^{N}(1+|x|)^{-N},&|x|\geq 2^{1-j}|y|,\\
1,&|x|<2^{1-j}|y|,\end{cases}\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>2^{-k}}(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}|\eta_{j}\ast g(x)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq 2^{-k}}(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}dx\bigg(\int_{|y|<2^{j-1}|x|}+\int_{|y|\geq 2^{j-1}|x|}\bigg)\frac{|2^{-j}y|^{N}}{N!}\Big(\sup\limits_{B(x,2^{-j}|y|)}|\nabla^{N}g|\Big)|\eta_{0}(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|g\|_{N}\int_{|x|\geq 2^{-k}}(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}dx\bigg(\int_{|y|<2^{j-1}|x|}|2^{-j}y|^{N}(1+|x|)^{-{N}}|\eta_{0}(y)|dy+\int_{|y|\geq 2^{j-1}|x|}|2^{-j}y|^{N}|\eta_{0}(y)|dy\bigg).

Using spherical coordinates
\rho=|x|
,
r=|y|
, and the fact that
\eta_{0}
is rapidly decay,

\displaystyle\int_{|x|\geq 2^{-k}}(1+2^{j}|x|)^{M}dx\bigg(\int_{|y|<2^{j-1}|x|}|2^{-j}y|^{N}(1+|x|)^{-{N}}|\eta_{0}(y)|dy+\int_{|y|\geq 2^{j-1}|x|}|2^{-j}y|^{N}|\eta_{0}(y)|dy\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{\eta_{0}}2^{-j{N}}\int_{2^{-k}}^{\infty}(1+2^{j}\rho)^{M}\rho^{n-1}d\rho\bigg((1+\rho)^{-{N}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{N}(1+r)^{-2{N}-n}r^{n-1}dr+\int_{2^{j-1}\rho}^{\infty}r^{N}(1+r)^{-2{N}-n}r^{n-1}dr\bigg)

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{N}2^{-j{N}}\int_{2^{-k}}^{\infty}\rho^{n-1}(1+2^{j}\rho)^{M}\left((1+\rho)^{-N}+\int_{2^{j-1}\rho}^{\infty}(1+r)^{-{N}-1}dr\right)d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{N}2^{-j{N}}\int_{2^{-k}}^{\infty}\rho^{n-1}(1+2^{j}\rho)^{M}\left(2^{jM}(1+2^{j}\rho)^{-M}(1+\rho)^{M-N}+(1+2^{j-1}\rho)^{-{N}}\right)d\rho

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{N}2^{-j(N-M)}\int_{2^{-k}}^{\infty}\rho^{n-1}(1+\rho)^{M-N}d\rho,\hskip 205.96994pt(\text{assuming }N>M)

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{M,N}2^{-j(N-M)}\min(1,2^{-(M+n-N)k}),\hskip 198.7425pt(\text{assuming }N>M+n).

Taking
{N}=2M+n
, the above is bounded by
2^{-Mj}\min(1,2^{Mk})\leq 2^{-M\max(j,j-k)}
, which concludes the proof.
∎

We will use the homogeneous tempered distributions.

Notation 4.1
.

We denote by
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the dual space of
\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For

we use the
Riesz potential

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}:\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
given by

(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f=(|2\pi\xi|^{s}\widehat{f})^{\vee}.

By
[

we have
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\{\text{polynomials}\}

[

\widehat{f}
makes sense as a (tempered) distribution on

For a tempered distribution

and

We first prove a lemma for homogeneous Littlewood-Paley families.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Denote

\eta_{j}(x):=2^{jn}\eta_{0}(2^{j}x),\quad j\in\mathbb{Z}.

Then

(1)

The sum
\sigma:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}\eta_{j}
converges in
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies
|\xi|^{-r}\widehat{\sigma}(\xi)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
.

(2)

If
f\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
\sigma\ast f=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}(\eta_{j}\ast f)
converges in
\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

In fact, one can show that
f\mapsto\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast f
is a continuous map from
\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to itself.

Note that by definition
(\eta_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\mathfrak{G}
. It is easy to see that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast f
converges in
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

It is not hard to see that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}\eta_{j}
converges in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
r>-n
. However, in general we do not know whether it converges in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
r\leq-n
, as its Fourier transform blows up in the order of
|\xi|^{r}
when
\xi\to 0
. For our application it is enough to show the convergence in
\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case
r=0
. Suppose this case is done, let
\theta_{0}:=(-\Delta)^{-\frac{r}{2}}\eta_{0}\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\theta_{j}(x)=2^{jn}\theta_{0}(2^{j}x)
for
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
2^{jr}\eta_{j}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}\theta_{j}
. Thus
(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}\eta_{j})^{\wedge}=|2\pi\xi|^{r}(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{j})^{\wedge}
and
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast f=(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\theta_{j})\ast(-\Delta)^{\frac{r}{2}}f
. The results
1
and
2
for
\sum_{j}2^{jr}\eta_{j}
then follow.

Now for the case
r=0
, the sum
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi)
converges to an element in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
since

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi)|=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{\eta}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)|\lesssim_{\eta_{0}}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\min(2^{-j}|\xi|,2^{j}|\xi|^{-1})\leq 2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|k|}<\infty,\quad\text{uniformly for }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We see that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{j}
defines an element that belongs to
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose Fourier transform is
L^{\infty}
. In particular
\sigma\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is well-defined with
\widehat{\sigma}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\})
. This gives
1
.

To prove
2
, we need to show that for any
f\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
M\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, the following series converges uniformly for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

(4.1)

|\xi|^{M}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi))||\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|.

By taking

we see that
\sum_{j}\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{j}\cdot\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}
has rapid decay when
|\xi|\to+\infty
. By taking
M\to-\infty
, we see that
\sum_{j}\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{j}\cdot\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}
has infinite order of vanishing at
\xi=0
.

By
\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi)=\widehat{\eta}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)
, we have
\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi)=2^{-j|\alpha|}(\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0})(2^{-j}\xi)
for each

.
Since both
\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}
and
\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0}
have rapid decay as
\xi\to\infty
and is flat at
\xi=0
, we have
\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{\tilde{M}}|\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|+\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{\tilde{M}}|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi)|<\infty
for all
\tilde{M}\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, by taking
\tilde{M}=|\alpha|-r-1
and
\tilde{M}=|\alpha|-r+1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}^{\infty}|\xi|^{M}|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{j}(\xi)||\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j}|2^{-j}\xi|^{|\alpha|-1}|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)||\xi|^{M-|\alpha|+1}|\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|+\sum_{j\leq 0}2^{j}|2^{-j}\xi|^{|\alpha|+1}|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0}(2^{-j}\xi)||\xi|^{M-|\alpha|-1}|\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\||\zeta|^{|\alpha|-1}\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0}(\zeta)\|_{C^{0}_{\zeta}}\||\zeta|^{M-|\alpha|+1}\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\zeta)\|_{C^{0}_{\zeta}}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-j}+\||\zeta|^{|\alpha|+1}\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\eta}_{0}(\zeta)\|_{C^{0}_{\zeta}}\||\zeta|^{M-|\alpha|-1}\partial^{\beta}\widehat{f}(\zeta)\|_{C^{0}_{\zeta}}\sum_{j\leq 0}2^{j}<\infty.

The convergence is uniform in
\xi
, thus we get

and complete the proof.
∎

To construct the anti-derivatives, we need a lemma on the support condition.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose

is supported in

\dot{\mathscr{S}}

\operatorname{supp}v\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}

Proof.

By assumption
\widehat{u}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{u}(0)=0
for all

, so

. Note that

still has rapid decay, therefore
\big((2\pi i\tau)^{-1}\widehat{u}\big)^{\vee}\in\dot{\mathscr{S}}(\mathbb{R})

Since
\big(\operatorname{p.v.}(2\pi i\tau)^{-1}\big)^{\vee}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(t)

\displaystyle\big((2\pi i\tau)^{-1}\widehat{u}(\tau)\big)^{\vee}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}\ast u(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}u(t-s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t-s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds=v(t).

Here we use the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(t-s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u=0
for all

. Therefore
\widehat{v}(\tau)=(2\pi i\tau)^{-1}\widehat{u}(\tau)

Since

\operatorname{supp}u\subseteq[\varepsilon_{0},+\infty)

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}u(t-s)ds=0

\big\|\sup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{js}|\phi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}=\big\|\big(\|\sup\limits_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{sq}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\|_{L^{p}(P_{l})}\big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}.

By (

(hence for almost every

) that

\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-\frac{1}{2}-k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}(x)\leq\operatorname{dist}(x,\omega)\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}(x)=\sqrt{2}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}(x).

Therefore by enlarging the constant we can replace
\operatorname{dist}_{\omega}
with
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-k}\mathbf{1}_{S_{k}}
.

Our goal is to show that for such
\eta,\theta,\phi
, and for

we have

\big\|\big(2^{k(s-r)}\|T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}\big)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\lesssim_{R_{0},\eta,\theta,\phi,r,s}\big\|\big(\|\sup\limits_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{sq}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\|_{L^{p}(P_{l})}\big)_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}.

Assuming for now that (
5.36
) holds, by (
5.33
), (
5.34
) and (
5.35
) we get the boundedness
[f\mapsto(T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f)|_{\overline{\omega}^{c}}]:\mathscr{F}_{p\infty}^{s}(\omega)\to L^{p}(\overline{\omega}^{c},\operatorname{dist}_{\omega}^{r-s})
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
s<r
. This completes the proof.

We now prove (
5.36
).
Using (
5.2
), for each
j\geq 1
we have decomposition
\mathbf{1}_{\omega}=\mathbf{1}_{P_{>j}}+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}\mathbf{1}_{P_{l}}
almost everywhere, so

T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{\omega}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big(2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{>j}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}2^{jr}\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{l}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))\Big).

It follows that for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

2^{k(s-r)}\|T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{(k-j)(s-r)}2^{js}\Big(\|\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{>j}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}\\
+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}\|\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{l}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}\Big).

Applying Corollary
5.9
to
2^{js}\|\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{>j}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}
(via (
5.28
)) and
2^{js}\|\eta_{j}\ast(\mathbf{1}_{P_{l}}\cdot(\theta_{j}\ast f))\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}
(via (
5.29
)), we get, for every

\displaystyle 2^{k(s-r)}\|T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

{}_{M}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{(k-j)(s-r)}\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-M(\max(0,j-k)+|j-l^{\prime}|}\big\|\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{qs}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(P_{l^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{(k-j)(s-r)}\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-M(\max(0,j-k)+|j-l|+|j-l^{\prime}|}\big\|\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{qs}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(P_{l^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{qs}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(P_{l^{\prime}})}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}2^{(k-j)(s-r)-M(\max(0,j-k)+|j-l^{\prime}|)}\Big(1+\sum_{l=-\infty}^{j}2^{-M|j-l|}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{qs}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(P_{l^{\prime}})}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}2^{(j-k)(r-s)-M(\max(0,j-k)+|j-l^{\prime}|)}.

Now by assumption

, thus

and

. Therefore

2^{k(s-r)}\|T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}f\|_{L^{p}(S_{k})}\lesssim\sum_{l^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sup_{q\in\mathbb{N}}2^{qs}|\phi_{q}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}(P_{l^{\prime}})}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-|j-k|(r-s)-|j-l^{\prime}|}.

Set

for

6.
The Results on Bounded Lipschitz Domains

We now apply our results for special Lipschitz domains to bounded Lipschitz domains.
The following convention will be used frequently in this section.

Notation 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let
\mathcal{U}
be an open set containing

.

We use the following objects, which can all be obtained by standard partition of unity argument:

•

(U_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
are finitely many bounded open sets in

.

•

(\Phi_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n})_{\nu=1}^{M}
as invertible affine linear transformations.

•

(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu=0}^{M}
are

-functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that take value in
[0,1]
.

•

(\omega_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
are special type domains on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. That is
\omega_{\nu}=\{x_{n}>\rho_{\nu}(x^{\prime})\}
for some

They have the following properties:

(P.a)

\partial\Omega\subset\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu}\subset\subset\mathcal{U}
.

(P.b)

\chi_{0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\chi_{\nu}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U_{\nu})
for

\chi_{0}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2}\equiv 1
in a neighborhood of
\overline{\Omega}
.

(P.c)

For each
1\leq\nu\leq M
,
U_{\nu}=\Phi_{\nu}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
is the image of the unit ball under
\Phi_{\nu}
, and
U_{\nu}\cap\Omega=U_{\nu}\cap\Phi_{\nu}(\omega_{\nu})
.

.

The condition
(P.b)
and
(P.c)
ensures that for
\nu=1,\dots,M
,
[f\mapsto(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]
defines bounded linear maps
\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\omega_{\nu})
for all

,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{F}_{pq}^{s}(\omega_{\nu})
for all

,
0<q\leq\infty
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
. This can be done by passing to the extension
\tilde{f}
of

(which is defined in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
) and then using
[
Tri10
, Theorems 2.8.2]
with
[
Tri92
, Theorem 4.3.2]
. Cf.
[
Tri10
, Proposition 3.2.3]
.

Similarly
[g\mapsto\chi_{\nu}(g\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})]:\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to\mathscr{B}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

The following construction of extension operator for bounded domains is standard, and we provide the details here for readers’ convenience.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain and let
\mathcal{U}\supset\supset\Omega
be an open subset. Let
(\chi_{\nu})_{\nu=0}^{M}
,
(\omega_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
and
(\Phi_{\nu})_{\nu=1}^{M}
be as in Notation
6.1
.

Suppose
E_{\nu}
is an extension operator for functions defined on
\omega_{\nu}
,
\nu=1,\dots,M
. Define
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
by

(6.1)

\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f:=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}(E_{\nu}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1}),

where

is a function on
\Omega
. Then
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
is an extension operator such that
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f
is supported in
\mathcal{U}
.

Moreover if for every
\nu=1,\dots,M
,
E_{\nu}:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\omega_{\nu})\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is bounded for some
\mathscr{A}\in\{\mathscr{B},\mathscr{F}\}
,

and admissible
(p,q)
(recall it from Convention
1.14
), then
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also a bounded linear map.

Proof.

In view of properties
(P.b)
and
(P.c)
, we have for a function
f
defined on
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f|_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}(E_{\nu}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})|_{U_{\nu}\cap\Omega}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}(E_{\nu}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]|_{\omega_{\nu}}\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})

\displaystyle\textstyle=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}((\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}\circ\Phi_{\nu}^{-1})=\chi_{0}f+\sum_{\nu=1}^{M}\chi_{\nu}^{2}f=f.

Clearly,
\operatorname{supp}\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}f\subseteq\bigcup_{\nu=0}^{M}\operatorname{supp}\chi_{\nu}\subset\Omega\cup\bigcup_{\nu=1}^{M}U_{\nu}\subset\subset\mathcal{U}
. We see that
\mathcal{E}_{\Omega}
is an extension operator supported in
\mathcal{U}
.

Assume that for some
s
and some admissible
(p,q)
, we have
E_{\nu}:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\omega_{\nu})\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for all
1\leq\nu\leq M
.

By the assumption and the fact that
[f\mapsto(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\omega_{\nu})
is a bounded linear operator (see Remark
6.2
), we have boundedness for the map
\big[f\mapsto E_{\nu}[(\chi_{\nu}f)\circ\Phi_{\nu}]\big]:\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{A}_{pq}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Since

is also bounded linear, taking composition we get the boundedness of

. Summing over

and using the boundedness of

Further Remarks and Open Questions

Given an arbitrary extension operator
\mathcal{E}
on a domain
\Omega
not necessarily bounded Lipschitz, apart from considering the boundedness on certain function spaces, we can ask the following questions:

•

For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, can we find some operator
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha,\beta}
(
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
) that has similar properties as
\mathcal{E}
, such that
\partial^{\alpha}\circ\mathcal{E}=\sum_{\beta}\mathcal{E}^{\alpha,\beta}\circ\partial^{\beta}
?

•

For a nonsmooth function
f
on the domain, does
\mathcal{E}f
define a smooth function outside the domain? If yes, can we obtain some kinds of quantitative characterization of the smoothing effect?

Affirmative answers to these questions will allow us to prove the analogs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
for the domain
\Omega
.
In particular, it would be interesting to consider these questions on locally uniform domains.

Some boundedness of
\tilde{T}^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\Omega}

In this paper we do not mention the
\mathscr{F}_{\infty q}^{s}
-spaces for
0<q<\infty
. This is due to the fact that these spaces have rather complicated characterizations, as we cannot use
\|(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}2^{jsq}|\lambda_{j}\ast f|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}\|_{L^{\infty}}
to define the
\mathscr{F}_{\infty q}^{s}
-norm. See
[
Tri20
, Remark 2.84]
for some discussion. Nevertheless the analogous results are done in
[
Yao23
]
.

More generally, we can ask whether we have the boundedness of
\tilde{T}^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\Omega}
and
T^{\eta,\theta,r}_{\omega}
on other types of function spaces. For example, we can consider the anisotropic spaces (see
[
JMHS13
]
), or the variable-exponent spaces (see
[
Kem17
]
).

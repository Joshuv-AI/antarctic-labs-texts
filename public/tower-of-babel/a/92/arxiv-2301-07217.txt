Integral Operator Frames on Hilbert C*-modules
Hatim Labrigui 1 , Mohamed Rossafi ∗2 , Abdeslam Touri 1 and Nadia Assila 1

Abstract
. Introduced by Duffin and Schaefer as a part of their work on nonhamonic fourrier series in 1952, the theory of frames has undergone a very interesting evolution in recent decades following the multiplicity of work carried out in this field. In this work, we introduce a new concept that of integral operator frame for the set of all adjointable operators on a Hilbert C*-modules H and we give some new propertis relating for some construction of integral operator frame, also we establish some new results. Some illustrative examples are provided to advocate the usability of our results.

\mathcal{H}

For a separable Hilbert space

For any

is defined by,

\langle x,y\rangle=\int_{\Omega}\langle x_{\omega},y_{\omega}\rangle d\mu(\omega).

The norme is defined by

Definition 1.1
.

[
12
]
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra and
\mathcal{H}
be a left
\mathcal{A}
-module, such that the linear structures of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{H}
are compatible.
\mathcal{H}
is a pre-Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module if
\mathcal{H}
is equipped with an
\mathcal{A}
-valued inner product
\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

(i)

\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\geq 0
for all

and
\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=0
if and only if
x=0
.

(ii)

\langle ax+y,z\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=a\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}+\langle y,z\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
x,y,z\in\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle y,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
.

For
x\in\mathcal{H},
we define
||x||=||\langle x,x\rangle||^{\frac{1}{2}}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is complete with
||.||
, it is called a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module or a Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-module over
\mathcal{A}
. For every
a
in
C^{\ast}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, we have
|a|=(a^{\ast}a)^{\frac{1}{2}}
and the
\mathcal{A}
-valued norm on
\mathcal{H}
is defined by
|x|=\langle x,x\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
x\in\mathcal{H}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
be two Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-modules, A map
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
is said to be adjointable if there exists a map

such that
\langle Tx,y\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle x,T^{\ast}y\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}

and

.

We also reserve the notation

for the set of all adjointable operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
and

is abbreviated to

.

Definition 1.2
.

[
13
]

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module over a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra. A family
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of elements of
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
, if there
exist two positive constants
A,B
such that,

(1.1)

A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\sum_{i\in I}\langle x,x_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\langle x_{i},x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}},\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

The numbers
A
and
B
are called lower and upper bounds of the frame, respectively. If
A=B=\lambda
, the frame is called
\lambda
-tight. If
A=B=1
, it is called a normalized tight frame or a Parseval frame. If only upper inequality of (
1.1
) hold, then
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is called a Bessel sequence for
\mathcal{H}
.

In
[
9
]
, L. Gavruta introduced

-frames to study atomic systems for operators in Hilbert spaces.

The numbers

and

2.
Integral Operator Frames for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})

Definition 2.1
.

A family of adjointable operators
\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\Omega}\subset End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
on a Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-module
\mathcal{H}
over a unital
C^{\ast}
-algebra is said to be an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
, if there exist two positive constants
A,B>0
such that,

(2.1)

A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\leq B\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}.

If the sum in the middle of (
2.1
) is convergent in norm, the integral operator frame is called standard.

The numbers
A
and
B
are called respectively lower and upper bound of the integral operator frame.

An integral operator frame
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\subset End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
A
-tight if there exists a constant
0<A
such that,

A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega),\quad x\in\mathcal{H}

If
A=1
, it is called a normalized tight integral operator frames or a Parseval integral operator frame.

If only the right-hand inequality of (
2.1
) is satisfied, we call
T:=\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\Omega}
the integral operator Bessel family for
\mathcal{H}
with Bessel bound
B
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space defined by:

\mathcal{H}=\left\{A=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&0\\
0&b\end{array}\right)\text{ / }a,b\in\mathbb{C}\right\}
,

It’s know that
\mathcal{H}
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and it’s a Hilbert
C^{\ast}
-module over it’s self.

We define the inner product :

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}&\rightarrow&\mathcal{H}\\
(A,B)&\mapsto&\langle A,B\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a\bar{a_{1}}&0\\
0&b\bar{b_{1}}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

Now, we consider a measure space
(\Omega=\left[0,1\right],d\lambda)
whose
d\lambda
is a Lebesgue measure restraint on the interval
\left[0,1\right]
.

For all
w\in\left[0,1\right]
, we define :

\displaystyle T_{\omega}:\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a&0\\
0&b\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}wa&0\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{3}wb}{2}\end{array}\right)

It is clear that the family
\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\left[0,1\right]}
is a continuous operator on
\mathcal{H}
.

Moreover, we have :

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\langle T_{w}A,T_{w}A\rangle=w^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}|a|^{2}&0\\
0&\frac{3|b|^{2}}{4}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

Hence,

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{w}A,T_{w}A\rangle d\lambda(w)=\int_{[0,1]}w^{2}d\lambda\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}|a|^{2}&0\\
0&\frac{3|b|^{2}}{4}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{|a|^{2}}{3}&0\\
0&\frac{|b|^{2}}{4}\end{array}\right)\end{array}

So,

\frac{1}{4}\langle A,A\rangle\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{w}A,T_{w}A\rangle d\lambda(w)\leq\frac{1}{3}\langle A,A\rangle.

Which shows that
\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\left[0,1\right]}
is an integral operator frame for
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.

we define the operator
R_{T}
by,

\displaystyle R_{T}:\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longrightarrow R_{T}x=\{T_{\omega}x\}_{\omega\in\Omega},

The operator
R_{T}
is called the analysis operator of the integral operator frame
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
.

The adjoint of the analysis operator
R_{T}
is defined by,

\displaystyle R_{T}^{\ast}:l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\{x_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow R_{T}^{\ast}(\{x_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega})=\int_{\Omega}T_{\omega}^{\ast}x_{\omega}d\mu(\omega).

The operator
R_{T}^{\ast}
is called the synthesis operator of
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
.

By composing
R_{T}
and
R_{T}^{\ast}
, the frame operator
S_{T}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
for the operator frame
T
is given by

S_{T}(x)=R_{T}^{\ast}R_{T}x=\int_{\Omega}T_{\omega}^{\ast}T_{\omega}xd\mu(\omega)\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Assume that
\{T_{\omega}\}_{w\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
and
R_{T}
,
R^{\ast}_{T}
are the analysis integral operator frame and synthesis operator frame of
T
respectively.
We say that
S_{T}
is the frame operator associeted to the integral operator frame.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
S_{T}
is the integral frame operator of an integral operator frame
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
with bounds
A,B
. Then
S_{T}
is selfadjoint, positive and invertible operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, we have a reconstruction formula :

(2.2)

x=\int_{\Omega}S^{-1}_{T}T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}xd\mu(\omega)\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}

Proof.

Let
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator frame with bounds
A,B
. Then for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have:

\langle Ax,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\\
\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{w}x,T_{w}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)=\langle S_{T}x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq B\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle Bx,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}.

This shows that :
A.I\leq S_{T}\leq B.I
. Then

0\leq I-B^{-1}S_{T}\leq\frac{B-A}{B}.I,

and consequently:

\|I-B^{-1}S_{T}\|=\underset{x\in\mathcal{H},\|x\|=1}{\sup}\|\langle(I-B^{-1}S_{T})x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\|\leq\frac{B-A}{B}<1.

By Theorem
1.9
, we shows that
S_{T}
is invertible.

It’s clear that
S_{T}
is a positive operator.

Further, for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have :

(2.3)

x=S^{-1}_{T}S_{T}x=S^{-1}_{T}\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}xd\mu(\omega)=\int_{\Omega}S^{-1}_{T}T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}xd\mu(\omega)

∎

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T=\{T_{w}\}_{w\in\Omega}\subset End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
be an integral operator Bessel family. Then
T
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if there exist a positive constants
A
and
B
such that :

(2.4)

A\|x\|^{2}\leq\|\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\|\leq B\|x\|^{2},\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

Suppose that (
2.4
) holds, we have,

(2.5)

\langle S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}x,S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\langle S_{T}x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(w).

Using (
2.4
), for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
we have,

\sqrt{A}\|x\|\leq\|S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}x\|\leq\sqrt{B}\|x\|,

by Lemma
1.5
, there exist
m,M>0
such that :

m\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\langle S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}x,S^{\frac{1}{2}}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq M\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}.

Therefore
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.

Conversely, let
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
with bounds
A
and
B
.

Then,

(2.6)

A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{w}x,T_{w}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(w)\leq B\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}},\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Since
0\leq\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}
, for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, then we can take the norme in the left, middle and right termes of the above inequality (
2.6
).

Thus,

\|A\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\|\leq\|\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(w)\|\leq\|B\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\|,\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

finally we get,

A\|x\|^{2}\leq\|\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(w)\|\leq B\|x\|^{2},\qquad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Which ends the proof.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Every integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
has a dual integral operator frame.

Proof.

Let
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
with bounds
A
and
B
.

For all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have,

(2.8)

x=S_{T}S^{-1}_{T}x=\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}xd\mu(\omega)

it remains to show that
\Lambda=\{T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
. Indeed,
from (
2.1
), we have,

A\langle S^{-1}_{T}x,S^{-1}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}x,T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\leq B\langle S^{-1}_{T}x,S^{-1}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}.

Since
S^{-1}_{T}
is a surjectif and sef-adjoint operator, then there exists (from Lemma
1.5
)

, such that,

Am\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}x,T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\leq B\|S^{-1}_{T}\|^{-2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}.

which shows that
\Lambda=\{T_{\omega}S^{-1}_{T}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a dual integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})

∎

Definition 2.8
.

A family of operatos
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
in
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be independent if the following condition is satisfied :

\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}x_{\omega}d\mu(\omega)=0,\quad\{x_{w}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\in l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})\Longrightarrow x_{\omega}=0\quad\mu pp.\quad for\;all\quad\omega\in\Omega.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator Bessel family in
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
. Then the following statements are true :

1

-
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
R_{T}
is below bounded.

2

-
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an independent integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
R_{T}
is invertible.

Proof.

(1): obvious.

For (2), let
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an independent integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.

For all
\{x_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\in l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
, we have :

R^{\ast}_{T}(\{x_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega})=\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}x_{\omega}d\mu(\omega)=0\Longrightarrow\{x_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}=0\qquad for{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.}all\quad\omega\in\Omega.

This prove that
R^{\ast}_{T}
is injective.

So,

(\mathcal{R}(R_{T}))^{\bot}=Ker(R^{\ast}_{T})=\{0\}.

This shows that
\mathcal{R}(R_{T})
is dense in
H
.

By (1), we know that
R_{T}
is below bounded and
\mathcal{R}(R_{T})
is closed, hence
R_{T}
is invertible.

For the converse, if
R_{T}
is invertible, then
R_{T}
is below bounded.

Thus
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame.

Now, suppose that
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\subset End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
is not independent, then there exists
0\neq\{\eta_{w}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\in l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
such that,

(2.9)

\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}\eta_{\omega}d\mu(\omega)=0

Since
R_{T}
is surjective then there exists
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that,

R_{T}x=\{T_{\omega}x\}_{\omega\in\Omega}=\{\eta_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}

So

\eta_{\omega}=T_{\omega}x\qquad\forall\omega\in\Omega

On one hand we have,

\displaystyle\langle R_{T}x,\{\eta_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\rangle_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle\{T_{\omega}x\}_{\omega\in\Omega},\{\eta_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\rangle_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,\eta_{\omega}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle x,T^{\ast}_{\omega}\eta_{\omega}\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle x,\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}\eta_{\omega}d\mu(\omega)\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}=0

One other hand, we have

\displaystyle\langle R_{T}x,\{\eta_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\rangle_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle=\langle x,R^{\ast}_{T}(\{\eta_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega})\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle x,\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}\eta_{\omega}d\mu(\omega)\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle x,\int_{\Omega}T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}xd\mu(\omega)\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle x,T^{\ast}_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\|\eta_{\omega}\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}\neq 0

It is a contradiction.

This show that
T=\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is independent integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

3.
Perturbation of Integral Operator Frames

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
with frames bounds
A
and
B
. Let
K\in End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H}),(K\neq 0)
, and
\{c_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
any family of scalars. Then the perturbed family of operator
\{T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frames for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
if
\int_{\Omega}|c_{\omega}|^{2}d\mu(\omega)<\frac{A}{\|K\|}
.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma_{\omega}=T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K
, for all
\omega\in\Omega
. Then for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x-\Gamma_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x-\Gamma_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\langle c_{\omega}Kx,c_{\omega}Kx\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|c_{\omega}|^{2}\|K\|^{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq R\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}.\quad where\quad R=\int_{\Omega}|c_{\omega}|^{2}\|K\|^{2}d\mu(\omega).

On one hand, for all
x\in\mathcal{H}
, we have,

\displaystyle(\int_{\Omega}\langle(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x,(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,H)}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{\omega}x\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,H)}+\|c_{\omega}Kx\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,H)}

\displaystyle=(\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+(\int_{\Omega}\langle(c_{\omega}K)x,(c_{\omega}K)x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{B}(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}+\sqrt{R}(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{R})(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then

(3.1)

\int_{\Omega}\langle(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x,(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\leq(\sqrt{B}+\sqrt{R})^{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}.

On the other hand, for all
x\in H
, we have,

\displaystyle(\int_{\Omega}\langle(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x,(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\|(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\geq\|T_{\omega}x\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}-\|c_{\omega}Kx\|_{l^{2}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})}

\displaystyle\geq(\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.....}-(\int_{\Omega}\langle c_{\omega}Kx,c_{\omega}Kx\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega))^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{A}(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}-\sqrt{R}(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{R})(\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

So,

(3.2)

\int_{\Omega}\langle(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x,(T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K)x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}d\mu(\omega)\geq(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{R})^{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{A}}

From (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) we conclude that
\{T_{\omega}+c_{\omega}K\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
if
R<A
, that is , if :

\int_{\Omega}|c_{\omega}|^{2}d\mu(\omega)<\frac{A}{\|K\|}.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{T_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\subset End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
be an integral operator frame. Let
\{\Lambda_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
be any family of operators on
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\{a_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega},\,\{b_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}
be two positively confined sequences. If there exist a constants
\alpha,\beta
with
0\leq\alpha,\beta<\frac{1}{2}
such that,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x-b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x-b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

(3.4)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.....}+\beta\int_{\Omega}\langle b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega).

Then
\{\Lambda_{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is an integral operator frame for
End_{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

Suppose (
3.3
) holds for some conditions of theorem.

Then for all
x\in H
we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.....}+\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x-b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x-b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)+\alpha\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}.....}+\beta\int_{\Omega}\langle b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)).

So,

(1-2\beta)\int_{\Omega}\langle b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x,b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega))\leq 2(1+\alpha)\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega).

This give,

(1-2\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(b_{\omega})]^{2}\int_{\Omega}\langle\Lambda_{\omega}x,\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)\leq 2(1+\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(a_{\omega})]^{2}\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega).

Thus,

(3.5)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\Lambda_{\omega}x,\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)\leq\frac{2(1+\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(a_{\omega})]^{2}}{(1-2\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(b_{\omega})]^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\langle T_{\omega}x,T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega).

Also, we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x,a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\rangle d\mu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\int_{\Omega}\|a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x-b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)+\int_{\Omega}\|b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\alpha\int_{\Omega}\|a_{\omega}T_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)+\beta\int_{\Omega}\|b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)+\int_{\Omega}\|b_{\omega}\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)).

Therefore,

(1-2\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(a_{\omega})]^{2}\int_{\Omega}\|T_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)\leq 2(1+\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(b_{\omega})]^{2}\int_{\Omega}\|\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega).

This give:

(3.6)

\frac{(1-2\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(a_{\omega})]^{2}}{2(1+\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(b_{\omega})]^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\|T_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}\|\Lambda_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)

From (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we conclude,

\frac{(1-2\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(a_{\omega})]^{2}}{2(1+\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(b_{\omega})]^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\|T_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega)\leq\int_{\Omega}\langle\Lambda_{\omega}x,\Lambda_{\omega}x\rangle\mu(\omega)\leq\frac{2(1+\alpha)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\sup}(a_{\omega})]^{2}}{(1-2\beta)[\underset{\omega\in\Omega}{\inf}(b_{\omega})]^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\|T_{\omega}x\|^{2}d\mu(\omega).

which ends the proof.
∎

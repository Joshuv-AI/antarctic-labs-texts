Improvement of non-integrated defect relation for meromorphic maps from Kähler manifolds
Tran Duc Ngoc, Si Duc Quang

Abstract
. The purpose of this paper is to establish a non-integrated defect relation for meromorphic mappings from a complete Kähler manifold into a projective variety intersecting an arbitrary family of hypersurfaces with explicit truncation level. In our result, both the total defect and the truncation level are estimated independently of the number of involving hypersurfaces. Our result generalizes and improves the previous results in this topic.

Introduction and Main result

Let
V
be a
k
-dimension subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}\}
be a family of hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. We say that the family
\mathcal{D}
is in

V

V\cap\bigcap_{j=0}^{N}D_{i_{j}}\ \forall 1\leq i_{0}<\cdots<i_{N}\leq q.

If
\mathcal{D}
is in
k
-subgeneral position with respect to
V
then we say that it is in general position with respect to
V
.

Let
M
be a complete Kähler manifold of dimension
m

f:M\longrightarrow V
be a meromorphic mapping. We denote by and
\Omega_{f}
the pull-back of the Fubini-Study form
\Omega
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
by
f
. For a positive integer
\mu_{0}
and a hypersurface
D
of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with
f(M)\not\subset D
. Denote by
\nu_{f}(D)(p)
the intersection multiplicity of the image of
f
and

at

for each point

In 1985, H. Fujimoto
[
5

defined the notion of non-integrated defect of
f
with respect to
D
truncated to level
\mu_{0}
by

\delta_{f}^{[\mu_{0}]}(D):=1-\inf\{\eta\geq 0:\eta\text{ satisfies condition }(*)\}.

Here, the condition (*) means that there exists a bounded non-negative continuous function
h
on
M
whose order of each zero is not less than
\min\{\nu_{f}(D),\mu_{0}\}
such that

d\eta\Omega_{f}+\dfrac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\log h^{2}\geq[\min\{\nu_{f}(D),\mu_{0}\}].

With this definition, Fujimoto gave a result analogous to the defect relation in Nevanlinna theory as follows.

Theorem A
(see
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
).
Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold and
\omega
be a Kähler form of
M.
Assume that the universal covering of
M
is biholomorphic to a ball in
\mathbb{C}^{m}.
Let
f:M\to{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be a linearly nondegenerate meromorphic map. Let
H_{1},\ldots,H_{q}
be hyperplanes of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
in general position. Assume that there eixst
\rho\geq 0
and a bounded continuous function
h\geq 0
on
M
such that
\rho\Omega_{f}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\log h^{2}\geq\mathrm{Ric}\ \omega.
Then we have

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\delta_{f}^{[n]}(H_{i})\leq n+1+\rho n(n+1).

The inequality in Theorem A is called the non-integrated defect relation with truncated multiplicity (to level

on Kähler manifold

. The quantity in the right hand side of the inequality is called the total defect. The above two results can be applied to the case of degenerate meromorphic mappings and also the quantity

\rho

2.1

Our aim in this paper is to improve the last above mentioned result (which is the most general case). We will reduce the above bound of the total defect (i.e, the quantity in the right hand side) so that it do not depend on
q
. In order to do so, we will apply the new estimate of Chow weights in
[
12
]
(see Lemma
2.4

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an
m
-dimensional complete Kähler manifold and
\omega
be a Kähler form of
M.
Assume that the universal covering of
M
is biholomorphic to a ball in
\mathbb{C}^{m}.
Let
f
be an algebraically nondegenerate meromorphic map of
M
into a subvariety
V
of dimension
k
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Let
\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}\}
be a family of
q
hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with the distributive constant
\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}
with respect to
V
. Let
d=lcm\{\deg D_{1},\ldots,\deg D_{q}\}
. Assume that for some
\rho\geq 0,
there exists a bounded continuous function
h\geq 0
on
M
such that

\rho\Omega_{f}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\log h^{2}\geq\mathrm{Ric}\ \omega.

Then, for each
\epsilon>0,
we have

\sum_{j=1}^{q}{\delta}^{[L-1]}_{f}(D_{j})\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon+\dfrac{\rho(L-1)(k+1)}{ud},

where
u=\lceil\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(2k+1)(k+1)d^{k}\deg(V)(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)\rceil
and
L=d^{k}\deg(V)^{k}\binom{k+u}{k}
, which satisfies

L\leq d^{k^{2}+k}\deg(V)^{k+1}e^{k}\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}^{k}(2k+5)^{k}(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}
.

Here,
\lceil x\rceil
denotes the smallest integer which is not less than the real number
x
.

Remark:
a) We see that the total defect obtained from Theorem
1.1
is bounded above by the quatity:

\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon+\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}^{k-1}\frac{\rho d^{k^{2}-1}\deg(V)^{k}e^{k}(2k+5)^{k}(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k-1}}{2k+1}.

b) If
V={\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
and
D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}
are in general position then
\deg(V)=1,k=n,\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}=1
and the total defect obtained from Theorem
1.1
is bounded above by

n+1+\epsilon+\frac{\rho d^{n^{2}-1}e^{n}(2n+5)^{n}((n+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{n-1}}{2n+1}\cong n+1+\epsilon+O(2^{n}e^{n}d^{n^{2}}(n\epsilon^{-1})^{n-1})\rho.

Note that, the bound above of the total defect obtained by M. Ru and S. Sogome
[
13
]
in this case is:
n+1+\epsilon+O(2^{2n^{2}+4n}e^{2n}d^{4n-1}(n\epsilon^{-1})^{2n})\rho.

c) The map
f
is said to be ramified over a hypersurface
D_{i}
with multiplicity at least
m_{i}
if either
f(M)\subset D_{i}
or
\nu_{f}(D_{i})(z)\geq m_{i}
for all
z\in\mathrm{Supp}\,\nu_{f}(D_{i})
. By choosing
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}
, from Theorem
1.1
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M,\omega,V,\mathcal{D}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}\}
and
d
be as in Theorem
1.1
. Let
f
be a meromorphic mapping from
M
into
V
. Assume that for some
\rho\geq 0,
there exists a bounded continuous function
h\geq 0
on
M
such that
\rho\Omega_{f}+\mathrm{dd^{c}}\log h^{2}\geq\mathrm{Ric}\ \omega.
If
f
is ramified over each
D_{i}
with multilicity at least
m_{i}
such that

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\left(1-\frac{L-1}{m_{i}}\right)>\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\frac{1}{2}+\dfrac{\rho(L-1)(k+1)}{ud},

where
u=\lceil\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(2k+1)(k+1)d^{k}\deg(V)(2\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+1)\rceil
and
L=d^{k}\deg(V)^{k}\binom{k+u}{k}
, then

is algebraically degenerate.

2.
Basic notions and auxiliary results from Nevanlinna theory

A. Notation.
We set
\|z\|=\big(|z_{1}|^{2}+\dots+|z_{m}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
for

z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
and define

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{m}(r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\|z\|<r\},\ S(r):=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\|z\|=r\}\ (0<r<\infty).

Define

v_{m-1}(z):=\big(\mathrm{dd^{c}}\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1}\quad\quad\text{and}

\sigma_{m}(z):={\rm d^{c}}\text{log}\|z\|^{2}\land\big(\mathrm{dd^{c}}\text{log}\|z\|^{2}\big)^{m-1}\text{on}\quad\mathbb{C}^{m}\setminus\{0\}.

For a divisor
\nu
on a ball
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R)
in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, with
0<r_{0}<r<R
, define

N(r,r_{0},\nu)=\int\limits_{r_{0}}^{r}\dfrac{n(t)}{t^{2m-1}}dt\ \text{ where }\ n(t)=\begin{cases}\int\limits_{|\nu|\,\cap\mathbb{B}^{m}(t)}\nu(z)v_{m-1}&\text{ if }m\geq 2,\\
\sum\limits_{|z|\leq t}\nu(z)&\text{ if }m=1.\end{cases}

For a positive integer
M
or
M=\infty
, we define
\nu^{[M]}(z)=\min\ \{M,\nu(z)\}
. Similarly, we define
n^{[M]}(t)
and
N(r,r_{0},\nu^{[M]})
(denote it by
N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu)
).

For a meromorphic function
\varphi
on
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R)
, denote by
\nu_{\varphi}
its divisor of zeros. Define

N_{\varphi}(r,r_{0})=N(r,r_{0},\nu_{\varphi}),\ N_{\varphi}^{[M]}(r,r_{0})=N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu_{\varphi}).

For brevity, we will omit the character
[M]
if
M=\infty
.

Let
f:\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{m}(R)\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a meromorphic mapping with a reduced representation
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
. Set
\|\tilde{f}\|=\big(|f_{0}|^{2}+\dots+|f_{n}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}
.
The characteristic function of
f
is defined by

T_{f}(r,r_{0})=\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\dfrac{dt}{t^{2m-1}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{B}^{m}(t)}\Omega_{f}\wedge v^{m-1},\ (0<r_{0}<r<R).

By Jensen’s formula, we will have

\displaystyle T_{f}(r,r_{0})=\int\limits_{S(r)}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}-\int\limits_{S(r_{0})}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}+O(1),\text{ (as $r\rightarrow R$)}.

Let
D
be a hypersurface of degree
d
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. The hypersurface
D
has a defining homogeneous polynomial
Q
of degree
d
and of the following form

Q(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{T}_{d}}a_{I}x^{I},\ a_{I}\in{\mathbb{C}},

where
\mathcal{T}_{d}=\{(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}_{\geq 0};i_{0}+\cdots+i_{n}=d\}
,
x^{I}=x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{n}^{i_{n}}
for each
I=(i_{0},\ldots,i_{n})\in\mathcal{T}_{d}
. This means that

D=\{(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}});Q(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=0\}.

The proximity function of
f
with respect to the hypersurface
D
, denoted by
m_{f}(r,r_{0},D)
, is defined by

m_{f}(r,r_{0},D)=\int_{S(r)}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}\|^{d}}{|Q(\tilde{f})|}\sigma_{m}-\int_{S(r_{0})}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}\|^{d}}{|Q(\tilde{f})|}\sigma_{m},

where
Q(\tilde{f})=Q(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
. This definition is independent of the choice of the reduced representation
\tilde{f}
of
f
. Note that, in this case
\nu_{f}(D)=\nu_{Q(\tilde{f})}
, and by Jensen’s formula, we have

N(r,r_{0},\nu_{f}(D))=\int_{S(r)}\log|Q(\tilde{f})|\sigma_{m}-\int_{S(r_{0})}\log|Q(\tilde{f})|\sigma_{m}.

Then, the first main theorem in Nevanlinna theory for is stated as follows

dT_{f}(r,r_{0})=m_{f}(r,r_{0},D)+N(r,r_{0},\nu_{f}(D))+O(1),\ (r_{0}<r<R),

where
O(1)
is independent of
f
. For simplicity we will write
N^{[M]}_{D(f)}(r)
and
N^{[M]}_{Q(f)}(r)
for
N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu_{f}(D))
and
N^{[M]}(r,r_{0},\nu_{Q(\tilde{f})}
respectively.

If
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow 1}\sup\dfrac{T(r,r_{0})}{\log 1/(1-r)}=\infty,
then the Nevanlinna’s defect of
f
with respect to the hypersurface
D
truncated to level
\ell
is defined by

\delta^{[\ell]}_{f,*}(D)=1-\lim\mathrm{sup}\dfrac{N^{[\ell]}_{D(f)}(r)}{\deg D\cdot T_{f}(r,r_{0})}.

There is a fact that

0\leq\delta^{[\ell]}_{f}(D)\leq\delta^{[\ell]}_{f,*}(D)\leq 1.

(See Proposition 2.1 in
[
13
]
)

\displaystyle H_{X}(u):=\dim({\mathbb{C}}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]_{u}/I_{u}).

By the usual theory of Hilbert polynomials, we have

The following theorem is due to J. Evertse and R. Ferretti

Theorem 2.3

(Theorem 4.1
[
3
]
)
.

Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be an algebraic variety of dimension
k
and degree
\delta
. Let
u>\delta
be an integer and let
{\bf c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\geqslant 0}
.
Then

\dfrac{1}{uH_{X}(u)}S_{X}(u,{\bf c})\geq\dfrac{1}{(k+1)\delta}e_{X}({\bf c})-\dfrac{(2k+1)\delta}{u}\cdot\left(\max_{i=0,\ldots,n}c_{i}\right).

The following lemma is due to the second author in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 2.4

(see
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
Y
be a projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{R}({\mathbb{C}})
of dimension
k\geq 1
and degree
\delta_{Y}
. Let
\ell\ (\ell\geq k+1)
be an integer and let
{\bf c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{R})
be a tuple of non-negative reals. Let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{0},\ldots,H_{R}\}
be a set of hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{P}}^{R}({\mathbb{C}})
defined by
H_{i}=\{y_{i}=0\}\ (0\leq i\leq R)
. Let
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{\ell}\}
be a subset of
\{0,\ldots,R\}
such that:

(1)

c_{i_{\ell}}=\min\{c_{i_{1}},\ldots,c_{i_{\ell}}\}
,

(2)

Y\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{\ell-1}H_{i_{j}}\neq\varnothing
,

(3)

and
Y\not\subset H_{i_{j}}
for all
j=1,\ldots,\ell
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathcal{H},Y}
be the distributive constant of the family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{i_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\ell}
with respect to
Y
. Then

e_{Y}({\bf c})\geq\frac{\delta_{Y}}{\Delta_{\mathcal{H},Y}}(c_{i_{1}}+\cdots+c_{i_{\ell}}).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

By using the universal covering if necessary, we may assume that
M=\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
. Let
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{q}
be defining homogeneous polynomials of
D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}
respectively, with
\deg Q_{i}=\deg D_{i}=d_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. Replacing
Q_{i}
by
Q_{i}^{d/d_{j}}\ (j=1,\ldots,q)
if necessary, we may assume that all hypersurfaces
Q_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
are of the same degree
d
.
Consider the mapping
\Phi
from
V
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{q-1}({\mathbb{C}})
, which maps a point
{\bf x}=(x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n})\in V
into the point
\Phi({\bf x})\in{\mathbb{P}}^{q-1}({\mathbb{C}})
given by

\Phi({\bf x})=(Q_{1}(x):\cdots:Q_{q}(x)),

where
x=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
. We set
\tilde{\Phi}(x)=(Q_{1}(x),\ldots,Q_{q}(x))
and fix a reduced representation
\tilde{f}=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{n})
of
f
.

Let
Y=\Phi(V)
. Since
V\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{q}Q_{j}=\varnothing
,
\Phi
is a finite morphism on
V
and
Y
is a complex projective subvariety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{q-1}({\mathbb{C}})
with
\dim Y=k
and of degree
\delta:=\deg(Y)\leq d^{k}.\deg(V).

For every
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}_{\geq 0}
and
{\bf y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{q})
we denote
{\bf y}^{\bf a}=y_{1}^{a_{1}}\ldots y_{q}^{a_{q}}
. Let
u
be a positive integer. We set
\xi_{u}:=\binom{q+u}{u}
and define

Y_{u}:={\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]_{u}/(I_{Y})_{u},

which is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space of dimension
H_{Y}(u)
. Put
n_{u}=H_{Y}(u)-1
and let
v_{0},\ldots,v_{n_{u}}
be homogeneous polynomials in
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]_{u}
, whose equivalent classes form a basis of
Y_{u}
.
Now, consider the meromorphic mapping
F
from
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
into
{\mathbb{P}}^{n_{u}}({\mathbb{C}})
with the reduced representation given by

\tilde{F}=(v_{0}(\tilde{\Phi}\circ\tilde{f}),\ldots,v_{n_{u}}(\tilde{\Phi}\circ\tilde{f})).

Since
f
is algebraically nondegenerate,
F
is linearly nondegenerate.

Then there exists an admissible set
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{u}})\in(\mathbb{Z}^{m}_{+})^{n_{u}+1}
such that

W^{\alpha}(F_{0},\ldots,F_{n_{u}})=\det(D^{\alpha_{i}}(v_{s}(\Phi\circ\tilde{f})))_{0\leq i,s\leq n_{u}}\neq 0.

Fix a point
z\in\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
such that
Q_{i}(\tilde{f}(z))\neq 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,q
. We define

{\bf c}_{z}=(c_{1,z},\ldots,c_{q,z})\in\mathbb{Z}^{q},

where

c_{i,z}:=\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}\|Q_{i}\|}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}\geq 0\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,q.

By the definition of the Hilbert weight, there are
{\bf a}_{0,z},\ldots,{\bf a}_{n_{u},z}\in\mathbb{N}^{q}
with

{\bf a}_{j,z}=(a_{j,1,z},\ldots,a_{j,q,z}),

where
a_{j,i,z}\in\{1,\ldots,u\},
such that the residue classes modulo
(I_{Y})_{u}
of
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{0,z}},\ldots,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{n_{u},z}}
form a basis of
{\mathbb{C}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}]_{u}/(I_{Y})_{u}
and

\displaystyle S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{n_{u}}{\bf a}_{j,z}\cdot{\bf c}_{z}.

We see that
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{j,z}}\in Y_{u}
(modulo
(I_{Y})_{u}
). Then we may write

{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{j,z}}=L_{j,z}(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n_{u}}),

where
L_{j,z}\ (0\leq j\leq n_{u})
are independent linear forms.
We have

\displaystyle\log\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}|L_{j,z}(\tilde{F}(z))|

\displaystyle=\log\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}\prod_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}(\tilde{f}(z))|^{a_{j,i,z}}

\displaystyle=-S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})+du(n_{u}+1)\log\|\tilde{f}(z)\|+O(u(n_{u}+1)).

Therefore

(3.1)

\displaystyle S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})=\log\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}\dfrac{1}{|L_{j,z}(\tilde{F}(z))|}+du(n_{u}+1)\log\|\tilde{f}(z)\|+O(u(n_{u}+1)).

From Theorem
2.3
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{u(n_{u}+1)}S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}_{z})\geq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{(k+1)\delta}e_{Y}({\bf c}_{z})-\dfrac{(2k+1)\delta}{u}\max_{1\leq i\leq q}c_{i,z}

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.2
), we get

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{(k+1)\delta}e_{Y}({\bf c}_{z})\leq&\dfrac{1}{u(n_{u}+1)}\log\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}\dfrac{1}{|L_{j,z}(\tilde{F}(z))|}+d\log\|\tilde{f}(z)\|\\
&+\dfrac{(2k+1)\delta}{u}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}\|Q_{i}\|}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}+O(1/u).\end{split}

Suppose that
c_{1,z}\geq c_{2,z}\geq\cdots\geq c_{q,z}
and denote by
\ell
the smallest index such that
V\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{\ell}D_{j}=\emptyset
. By Lemma
2.2
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e_{Y}({\bf c}_{z})&\geq\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}(c_{1,z}+\cdots+c_{\ell,z})=\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}\|Q_{i}\|}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}\right)\\
&=\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}\|Q_{i}\|}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}\right)+O(1).\end{split}

Then, from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\log\prod_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}\leq&\dfrac{k+1}{u(n_{u}+1)}\log\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}\dfrac{1}{|L_{j,z}(\tilde{F}(z))|}+d(k+1)\log\|\tilde{f}(z)\|\\
&+\dfrac{(2k+1)(k+1)\delta}{u}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{d}\|Q_{i}\|}{|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|}+O(1),\end{split}

where the term
O(1)
does not depend on
z
.

Set
m_{0}=(2k+1)(k+1)\delta
and
b=\dfrac{k+1}{u(n_{u}+1)}
. From (
3.5
), we get

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\log\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}dq-d(k+1)-\frac{dm_{0}q}{u}}|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))|^{b}}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|^{\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}}}\leq b\log\dfrac{|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))|}{\prod_{j=0}^{n_{u}}|L_{j,z}(\tilde{F}(z))|}+O(1).\end{split}

Here we note that
L_{j,z}
depends on
i
and
z
, but the number of these linear forms is finite (at most
\xi_{u}
). We denote by
\mathcal{L}
the set of all
L_{j,z}
occuring in the above inequalities.

Then, from (
3.6
) there exists a positive constant
K_{0}
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}dq-d(k+1)-\frac{dm_{0}q}{u}}.|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))|^{b}}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|^{\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}}}\leq K_{0}^{b}.S_{\mathcal{J}}^{b},

where
S_{\mathcal{J}}=\dfrac{|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))|}{\prod_{L\in\mathcal{J}}|L(\tilde{F}(z))|}
for some
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}
with
\#\mathcal{J}=n_{u}+1
so that
\{L\in\mathcal{J}\}
is linearly independent.

We now estimate the divisor
\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}
. Fix a point
z\in\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
outside the indeterminacy locus of
f
. Suppose that

\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{1}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}\geq\cdots\geq\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{q}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}

and let
\ell
be as above. We set
c_{i}=\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}
and

{\bf c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{q}_{\geq 0}.

It is clear that
c_{i}=0
for all
i\geq\ell
. Then there are

{\bf a}_{j}=(a_{j,1},\ldots,a_{j,p}),a_{j,s}\in\{1,\ldots,u\},j=0,\ldots,n_{u}

such that
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{0}},\ldots,{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{n_{u}}}
is a basis of
Y_{u}
and

S_{Y}(m,{\bf c})=\sum_{j=0}^{n_{u}}{\bf a}_{j}\cdot{\bf c}.

Similarly as above, we write
{\bf y}^{{\bf a}_{j}}=L_{j}(v_{0},\ldots,v_{n_{u}})
, where
L_{0},\ldots,L_{n_{u}}
are independent linear forms in variables
y_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq q)
. By the property of the general Wronskian, we have

W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})=cW^{\alpha}(L_{0}(\tilde{F}),\ldots,L_{n_{u}}(\tilde{F})),

where
c
is a nonzero constant. This yields that

\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(z)=\nu_{W^{\alpha}(L_{1}(\tilde{F}),\ldots,L_{H_{Y}(u)}(\tilde{F}))}(z)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{n_{u}}\max\{0,\nu_{L_{j}(\tilde{F})}(z)-n_{u}\}

We also easily see that
\nu_{L_{j}(\tilde{F})}(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{q}a_{j,i}\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z),
and hence

\max\{0,\nu_{L_{j}(\tilde{F})}(z)-n_{u}\}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{q}a_{j,i}c_{i}={{\bf a}_{j}}\cdot{\bf c}.

Thus, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(z)\geq\sum_{j=0}^{n_{u}}{{\bf a}_{j}}\cdot{\bf c}=S_{Y}(u,{\bf c}).

By Lemma
2.4
we have

e_{Y}({\bf c})\geq\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\sum_{i=1}^{l}c_{i}=\frac{\delta}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}.

On the other hand, by Theorem
2.1
we have

\displaystyle S_{Y}(u,{\bf c})

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{u(n_{u}+1)}{(k+1)\delta}e_{Y}({\bf c})-(2k+1)\delta(n_{u}+1)\max_{1\leq i\leq q}c_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{u(n_{u}+1)}{(k+1)\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}

\displaystyle-(2k+1)\delta(n_{u}+1)\sum_{i=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}.

Combining this inequality and (
3.8
), we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle b\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(z)\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\dfrac{m_{0}}{u}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{q}\max\{0,\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-n_{u}\}.

Therefore,

\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{q}\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-b\nu_{W(\tilde{F})}(z)\leq\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{q}\min\{\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z),n_{u}\}.

Seting
x=\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}
, from the above inequality we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}\right)b\nu_{W(\tilde{F})}(z)-x\left(\sum_{i=1}^{q}\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z)-\min\{\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(z),n_{u}\}\right)\geq 0.

Assume that

\rho\Omega_{f}+\dfrac{\sqrt{-1}}{2\pi}\partial\bar{\partial}\log h^{2}\geq\mathrm{Ric}\omega.

We now suppose that

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{[n_{u}]}_{f}(D_{j})>\frac{k+1}{x}+\dfrac{\rho n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{d}.

Then, for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,q\},
there exist constants
\eta_{j}>0
and continuous plurisubharmonic function
\tilde{u}_{j}
such that

e^{\tilde{u}_{j}}|\varphi_{j}|\leq\|\tilde{f}\|^{d\eta_{j}},
where
\varphi_{j}
is a holomorphic function with
\nu_{\varphi_{j}}=\min\{\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})},n_{u}\}
and

q-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\eta_{j}>\frac{k+1}{x}+\dfrac{\rho n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{dx}.

Put
u_{j}=\tilde{u}_{j}+\log|\varphi_{j}|
, then
u_{j}
is a plurisubharmonic and

e^{u_{j}}\leq\|\tilde{f}\|^{d\eta_{j}},\ j=1,\ldots,q.

Let

v(z)=\log\left|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})^{b}\dfrac{\left|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))\right|^{b}}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}\left|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)\right|^{x}}\right|+x\sum_{j=1}^{q}u_{j}(z).

Therefore, we have the following current inequality

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{dd^{c}}[v]

\displaystyle\geq b[\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}]-x\sum_{j=1}^{q}[\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}]+x\sum_{j=1}^{q}2\mathrm{dd^{c}}[u_{j}]

\displaystyle\geq b[\nu_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}]-x\left(\sum_{j=1}^{q}[\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}]-\sum_{j=1}^{q}[\min\{n_{u},\nu_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}\}]\right)\geq 0.

This implies that
v
is a plurisubharmonic function on
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
.

On the other hand, by the growth condition of
f
, there exists a continuous plurisubharmonic function
\omega\not\equiv\infty
on
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})
such that

\displaystyle e^{\omega}{\rm d}V\leq\|\tilde{f}\|^{2\rho}v_{m}

Set

t=\dfrac{2\rho}{dx\left(q-\frac{k+1}{x}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}\eta_{j}\right)}>0

and

\lambda(z)=\left|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})^{b}\dfrac{\left|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F}(z))\right|^{b}}{\left|Q_{1}(\tilde{f})(z)\cdots Q_{q}(\tilde{f})(z)\right|^{x}}\right|.

It is clear that
\dfrac{n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{2}t<1,
and the function
\zeta=\omega+tv
is plurisubharmonic on the Kähler manifold
M
. Choose a position number
\gamma
such that
0<\dfrac{n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{2}t<\gamma<1.

Then, we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}e^{\zeta}dV&=e^{\omega+tv}dV\leq e^{tv}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2\rho}v_{m}=|\lambda|^{t}(\prod_{j=1}^{q}e^{txu_{j}})\|\tilde{f}\|^{2\rho}v_{m}\\
&\leq|\lambda|^{t}\|\tilde{f}\|^{2\rho+tx\sum_{j=1}^{q}d\eta_{j}}v_{m}=|\lambda|^{t}\|\tilde{f}\|^{dtx(q-\frac{k+1}{x})}v_{m}.\end{split}

(a) We first consider the case where
R_{0}<\infty
and
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow R_{0}}\sup\dfrac{T_{f}(r,r_{0})}{\log 1/(R_{0}-r)}<\infty.

It suffices for us to proof the Theorem in the case where
\mathbb{B}^{m}(R_{0})=\mathbb{B}^{m}(1).

Integrating both sides of (
3.11
) over
\mathbb{B}^{m}(1)
and using (
3.7
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{m}(1)}e^{\zeta}dV&\leq\int_{\mathbb{B}^{m}(1)}|\lambda|^{t}\|\tilde{f}\|^{dtx(q-\frac{k+1}{x})}v_{m}.\\
&=2m\int_{0}^{1}r^{2m-1}\left(\int_{S(r)}\bigl(|\lambda|\|\tilde{f}\|^{dxq-d(k+1)}\bigl)^{t}\sigma_{m}\right)dr\\
&\leq 2m\int_{0}^{1}r^{2m-1}\left(\int_{S(r)}\sum_{\mathcal{J}}\bigl|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})K_{0}S_{\mathcal{J}}\bigl|^{bt}\sigma_{m}\right)dr,\end{split}

where the summation is taken over all
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathcal{L}
with
\sharp J=n_{u}+1
and
\{L\in\mathcal{J}\}
is linearly independent.

We note that
(\sum_{i=0}^{n_{u}}|\alpha_{i}|)bt\leq\dfrac{n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{2}t<\gamma<1
. Then by Proposition
2.2
there exists a positive constant
K_{1}
such that, for every
0<r_{0}<r<r^{\prime}<1,
we have

\displaystyle\int_{S(r)}\left|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})K_{0}S_{\mathcal{J}}(z)\right|^{bt}\sigma_{m}\leq K_{1}\left(\dfrac{r^{\prime 2m-1}}{r^{\prime}-r}dT_{f}(r^{\prime},r_{0})\right)^{\gamma}.

Choosing
r^{\prime}=r+\dfrac{1-r}{eT_{f}(r,r_{0})}
, we get
T_{f}(r^{\prime},r_{0})\leq 2T_{f}(r,r_{0})
outside a subset
E\subset[0,1]
with
\int_{E}\dfrac{dr}{1-r}<+\infty
. Hence, the above inequality implies that

\displaystyle\int_{S(r)}\left|(z^{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})K_{0}S_{J}(z)\right|^{bt}\sigma_{m}\leq\dfrac{K}{(1-r)^{\gamma}}\left(\log\dfrac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\gamma}

for all
r
outside
E
, where
K
is a positive constant. By choosing
K
large enough, we may assume that the above inequality holds for all
r\in(0;1)
.
Then, the inequality (
3.12
) yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}^{m}(1)}e^{\zeta}dV

\displaystyle\leq 2m\int_{0}^{1}r^{2m-1}\dfrac{K}{(1-r)^{\gamma}}\left(\log\dfrac{1}{1-r}\right)^{\gamma}dr<+\infty

This contradicts the results of S. T. Yau and L. Karp (see
[
6
,
17
]
).

Hence, we must have

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{[n_{u}]}_{f}(D_{j})\leq\frac{k+1}{x}+\dfrac{\rho n_{u}(n_{u}+1)b}{d}=\frac{k+1}{x}+\dfrac{\rho n_{u}(k+1)}{ud}.

On the other hand, we have
u\geq\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}m_{0}(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon)\epsilon^{-1}
. This implies that
\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}m_{0}}{u}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon}=1-\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon}
. Therefore

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{k+1}{x}=\frac{k+1}{\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}}-\frac{m_{0}}{u}}=\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)}{1-\frac{\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}m_{0}}{u}}\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon.

We have
n_{u}=H_{Y}(u)-1\leq\delta\binom{k+u}{k}-1\leq d^{k}\deg(V)\binom{k+u}{k}-1=L-1
. Then, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{[L-1]}_{f}(D_{j})\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon+\dfrac{\rho(k+1)(L-1)}{ud}.

We estimate
L
as follows. If
k=1
then

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=d\deg(V)(1+u)

\displaystyle<d\deg(V)\left(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}6d\deg(V)(2\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}\epsilon^{-1}+1)+2\right)

\displaystyle<d^{2}\deg(V)^{2}e\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}7(2\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}\epsilon^{-1})+1).

Otherwise, if
k\geq 2
then

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle\leq d^{k}\deg(V)e^{k}\left(1+\dfrac{u}{k}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle<d^{k}\deg(V)e^{k}\left(1+\dfrac{d^{k}\deg(V)\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(2k+1)(k+1)(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)+1}{k}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle<d^{k^{2}+k}\deg(V)^{k+1}e^{k}\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}^{k}(2k+5)^{k}(\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)\epsilon^{-1}+1)^{k}.

(b) We now consider the remaining case where
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow R_{0}}\sup\dfrac{T(r,r_{0})}{\log 1/(R_{0}-r)}=\infty.

It is clear that we only need to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3.14
.

With the assumption of Theorem 1.1. Then, we have

(q-\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r,r_{0})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d}N^{[L-1]}_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r)+S(r),

where
S(r)
is evaluated as follows:

(i) In the case
R_{0}<\infty,

S(r)\leq K(\log^{+}\dfrac{1}{R_{0}-r}+\log^{+}T_{f}(r,r_{0}))

for all
0<r_{0}<r<R_{0}
outside a set
E\subset[0,R_{0}]
with
\int_{E}\dfrac{dt}{R_{0}-t}<\infty
and
K
is a positive constant.

(ii) In the case
R_{0}=\infty,

S(r)\leq K(\log r+\log^{+}T_{f}(r,r_{0}))

for all
0<r_{0}<r<\infty
outside a set
E^{\prime}\subset[0,\infty]
with
\int_{E^{\prime}}dt<\infty
and
K
is a positive constant.

Proof.
Repeating the above argument, we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{S(r)}

\displaystyle\left|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})^{b}\dfrac{\|\tilde{f}(z)\|^{xdq-d(k+1)}|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})(z)|^{b}}{\prod_{i=1}^{q}|Q_{i}(\tilde{f})(z)|^{x}}\right|^{t}\sigma_{m}\leq K_{1}\left(\dfrac{R^{2m-1}}{R-r}dT_{f}(R,r_{0})\right)^{\delta}.

for every
0<r_{0}<r<R<R_{0}
. Using the concativity of the logarithmic function, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}b&\int_{S(r)}\log|(z^{\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n_{u}}})|\sigma_{m}+(xdq-d(k+1))\int_{S(r)}\log\|\tilde{f}\|\sigma_{m}\\
&+b\int_{S(r)}\log|W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})|\sigma_{m}-x\sum_{j=1}^{q}\int_{S(r)}\log|Q_{j}(\tilde{f})|\sigma_{m}\leq K\left(\log^{+}\dfrac{R}{R-r}+\log^{+}T_{f}(R,r_{0})\right)\end{split}

for some positive constant
K
. By Jensen’s formula, this inequality implies that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bigl(xdq&-d(k+1)\bigl)T_{f}(r,r_{0})+bN_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(r)-x\sum_{i=1}^{q}N_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r)\\
&\leq K\bigl(\log^{+}\dfrac{R}{R-r}+\log^{+}T_{f}(R,r_{0})\bigl)+O(1).\end{split}

From (
3.9
), we have

x\sum_{i=1}^{q}N_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r)-bN_{W^{\alpha}(\tilde{F})}(r)\leq x\sum_{i=1}^{q}N^{[L-1]}_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r).

Combining this estimate and (
3.15
), we get

(3.16)

\displaystyle\bigl(q-\frac{k+1}{x}\bigl)T_{f}(r,r_{0})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d}N^{[L-1]}_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r)+\frac{K}{x}\bigl(\log^{+}\dfrac{R}{R-r}+\log^{+}T_{f}(R,r_{0})\bigl)+O(1).

Choosing
R=r+\dfrac{R_{0}-r}{eT_{f}(r,r_{0})}
if
R_{0}<\infty
and
R=r+\dfrac{1}{T_{f}(r,r_{0})}
if
R_{0}=\infty
, we see that

T_{f}\bigl(r+\dfrac{R_{0}-r}{eT_{f}(r,r_{0})},r_{0}\bigl)\leq 2T_{f}(r,r_{0})

outside a subset
E\subset[0,R_{0})
with
\int_{E}\dfrac{dr}{R_{0}-r}<+\infty
in the case
R_{0}<\infty
and

T_{f}\bigl(r+\dfrac{1}{T_{f}(r,r_{0})},r_{0}\bigl)\leq 2T_{f}(r,r_{0})

outside a subset
E^{\prime}\subset[0,\infty)
with
\int_{E^{\prime}}dr<\infty
in the case
R_{0}=\infty
.
Thus, from (
3.13
) and (
3.16
) we get the desired inequality of Theorem
3.14
.

(q-\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)-\epsilon)T_{f}(r,r_{0})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{q}\dfrac{1}{d}N^{[L-1]}_{Q_{i}(\tilde{f})}(r)+S(r).

Return to the case (b). From Theorem
3.14
, we have

\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{[L-1]}_{f}(D_{j})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{q}\delta^{[L-1]}_{f,*}(D_{j})\leq\Delta_{\mathcal{D},V}(k+1)+\epsilon.

The theorem is proved in this case.

\square

An optimal Liouville theorem for the porous medium equation
Damião J. Araújo, Rafayel Teymurazyan

Abstract
. Under a sharp asymptotic growth condition at infinity, we prove a Liouville type theorem for the inhomogeneous porous medium equation, provided it stays universally close to the heat equation. Additionally, for the homogeneous equation, we show that for the conclusion to hold, it is enough to assume the sharp asymptotic growth at infinity only in the space variable. The results are optimal, meaning that the growth condition at infinity cannot be weakened. MSC 2020: 35B53, 35K55, 35K65, 76S05. Keywords: Porous medium equation, Liouville theorem, intrinsic scaling, degenerate parabolic equations.



1.
Introduction

As it is well known, bounded harmonic function in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
must be a constant (Liouville theorem). This is true for parabolic equations as well, namely, in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{-}
bounded solutions of the heat equation are constant,
[
14
]
. However, there is a key difference between elliptic and parabolic equations. For instance, entire harmonic functions, which are bounded only from below (or above), are constant,
[
7
]
, but one sided bound is not enough to make the same conclusion for entire caloric functions, as shows the example of the function
u(x,t)=e^{x+t}
, which solves the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
, is bounded from below, but obviously is not a constant. Nevertheless, the absence of the two sided bound can be compensated by a growth condition, as established by Bernstein. He argued that in the plane entire solutions of uniformly elliptic equations, growing sublinearly at infinity, must be constant,
[
7
]
. A result of similar spirit was established for entire solutions of the heat equation by Hirschman in
[
8
]
. Observe that for the inhomogeneous equation, even in the uniformly elliptic case, one sided bound is not enough to guarantee a Liouville type result, as shows the example of the non-constant function
u(x)=|x|^{2}\geq 0
, which satisfies
\Delta u=2n
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Recent advances in geometric analysis,
[
5
,
6
,
11
]
, allowed to obtain Liouville type results for degenerate elliptic and parabolic equations. These results are intrinsically related to the regularity estimates (see, for example,
[
9
,
12
]
). In particular, using sharp Hölder regularity of solutions, in
[
12
]
the authors show that weak solutions of

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=0,\,\,\,p>2

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
are constant, provided their growth at infinity is controlled in an intrinsic manner. Local
C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}
estimates for weak solutions of the inhomogeneous porous medium type equation

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=f,

(1.1)

where
\gamma\geq 0
and
f\in L^{\infty}
,
[
5
,
6
]
, suggest similar result also for entire solutions of (
1.1
), particularly, for those behaving like
o(|x|^{\alpha}+|t|^{\frac{\alpha}{{}^{2}}})
, as
|x|+|t|\to\infty
. Note that (
1.1
) admits solutions with faster growth rate, such as, for
\gamma>0
,

u(x,t)=\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}|x|^{\frac{2}{\gamma}}(-t)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\quad\mbox{ in }\quad\;\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{-},

indicating that there is an upper bound for
\alpha
under which Liouville type results hold.

In this note, using a higher (optimal) regularity estimate obtained recently in
[
1
]
, we prove an optimal Liouville theorem for solutions of (
1.1
) in a strip, provided the diffusion parameter
\gamma\geq 0
is small. More precisely, we show that if a solution of (
1.1
) vanishes at a point and

u(x,t)=o\left(|x|+|t|^{\frac{1}{{}^{\sigma}}}\right)^{\sigma},\quad\mbox{as}\quad|x|+|t|\to\infty,

(1.2)

where
\sigma=2(\gamma+1)^{-1}
, then it must vanish at all “ancient” times (Theorem
3.1
). Moreover, our result is optimal, meaning that the growth condition at infinity cannot be weakened, as shows the example of the solution
u(x,t)=|x|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}
, which vanishes at
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
, satisfies (
1.2
) for
\sigma=2(\gamma+1)^{-1}+\delta
,
\delta>0
, and obviously is not identically zero. Furthermore, for the homogeneous equation, we show that for the conclusion to hold, it is enough to assume the sharp asymptotic growth at infinity only in the space variable (Theorem
3.2
). Our result extends similar results for the heat equation,
[
8
,
14
]
, and adjusts the result from
[
6
, Proposition 16.2]
. It also covers equations with obstacle type non-homogeneity (see
[
3
,
4
]
), such as

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=h\chi_{\{u>0\}},

where
h\in L^{\infty}
and
\chi_{\{u>0\}}
is the characteristic function of the set
\{u>0\}
. Unlike
[
2
,
10
]
, the degree of homogeneity in (
1.1
) does not depend on the right hand side.

2.
Preliminaries

One may notice that the classical porous medium equation has the coefficient
(\gamma+1)
in the divergence term of (
1.1
), however, it does not affect the proofs of the arguments, therefore, to keep things simple, we consider the equation (
1.1
).

Next, we introduce notations and recall a regularity result for future reference. We start by defining the intrinsic cylinder by

G_{r}(x,t):=B_{r}(x)\times\left(-r^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}+t,\,t\right],\,\,\,r>0

and the intrinsic norm by

\|(x,t)\|:=|x|+|t|^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2}}.

In what follows, we will use
G_{r}
for
G_{r}(0,0)
. We also define the
T
-level strip by

S_{T}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,T),\,\,\,T\in\mathbb{R}.

Solutions of (
1.1
) are understood in the weak sense (for the precise definition see, for example,
[
6
,
13
]
). Our results makes use of a higher (optimal) regularity result obtained for solutions of the porous medium equation near the heat equation. More precisely, we use the following regularity result from
[
1
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
u\geq 0
is a weak solution of

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=f\,\,\,\textrm{ in }\,\,\,G_{1},

(2.1)

with
f\in L^{\infty}(G_{1})
, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
C>0
, depending only on
\|f\|_{\infty}
and
n
, such that for
\gamma\in(0,\varepsilon)
and
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\partial\{u>0\}\cap G_{1/2}
, in
G_{1/5}(x_{0},t_{0})
, one has

u(x,t)\leq C\|u\|_{\infty}\|(x-x_{0},t-t_{0})\|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}.

We finish this section with the following result from
[
6
, Proposition 16.2]
, which will later be used to prove that for the homogeneous equation, for the Liouville type result to hold, it is enough to assume the sharp asymptotic growth at infinity only in the space variable.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
u\geq 0
is a weak solution of

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=0

in a strip
S_{T}
,
T\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\displaystyle\inf_{S_{T}}u=0
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}u(x,t)=0,\,\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and the limit is uniform in
x
ranging over a compact set
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
Q\times\{\tau\}
is included in a
(y,s)
-paraboloid

P(y,s):=\left\{(x,t)\in S_{T};\,\,t-s\leq-\left(\frac{c}{u(y,s)}\right)^{\gamma}|x-y|^{2}\right\}

for
(y,s)\in S_{T}
such that
u(y,s)>0
for some
\tau<s
.

3.
Liouville type theorems

In this section we prove that if a non-negative weak solution of the inhomogeneous porous medium type equation in a strip vanishes at a point and has a certain intrinsic growth at infinity, then it must vanish at all “ancient” times, provided the equation is universally close to the heat equation. Our result is optimal, i.e., the growth condition at infinity cannot be weakened. Furthermore, for the homogeneous equation, we show that for the conclusion to hold, it is enough to assume the asymptotic growth at infinity only in the space variable. Thus, we assume that
\gamma\geq 0
is small enough, so we are in the regularity regime of Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\geq 0
be as in Theorem
2.1
,
T\in\mathbb{R}
, and
u\geq 0
be a weak solution of

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=f

in
S_{T}
, where
f\in L^{\infty}(S_{T})
. If
u(x_{0},t_{0})=0
for some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in S_{T}
and

u(x,t)=o\left(\|(x,t)\|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\right),\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,\|(x,t)\|\rightarrow\infty,

(3.1)

then
u\equiv 0
in
\overline{S}_{t_{0}}
.

Proof.

Set

\sigma:={\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}

and for
k\in\mathbb{N}
define

u_{k}(x,t):=k^{-\sigma}u(kx+x_{0},k^{\sigma}t+t_{0})

Direct computation shows

(u_{k})_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u_{k}^{\gamma}\nabla u_{k})=u_{t}-\gamma k^{2(1-\sigma)-\sigma(\gamma-1)}u^{\gamma-1}|\nabla u|^{2}-k^{2-\sigma-\gamma\sigma}u^{\gamma}\Delta u,

also

2(1-\sigma)-\sigma(\gamma-1)=0\,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\,2-\sigma-\gamma\sigma=0,

therefore,

\begin{split}(u_{k})_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u_{k}^{\gamma}\nabla u_{k})&=u_{t}-\gamma u^{\gamma-1}|\nabla u|^{2}-u^{\gamma}\Delta u\\
&=u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)\\
&=f(kx+x_{0},k^{\sigma}t+t_{0}),\end{split}

where the right hand side is bounded independent of
k
. Observe also that
u_{k}(0,0)=0
. We divide the rest of the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
We claim that in
G_{1/5}
there holds

\|u_{k}\|_{\infty}\rightarrow 0,\,\,\,\textrm{ as }\,\,\,k\rightarrow\infty.

(3.2)

Indeed, if that is not the case, then there is a constant
c>0
, a sequence
k_{i}\rightarrow\infty
and points
(x_{i},t_{i})\in G_{1/5}
, such that

u_{k_{i}}(x_{i},t_{i})\geq c>0,\,\,\,\textrm{ as }\,\,\,k_{i}\to\infty.

Hence, from the definition of
u_{k}
it follows that the sequence
(k_{i}x_{i},k_{i}^{\sigma}t_{i})
is unbounded. Therefore, recalling the definition of the intrinsic norm, up to a subsequence, we must have

\|(k_{i}x_{i},k_{i}^{\sigma}t_{i})\|=|k_{i}x_{i}|+|k_{i}^{\sigma}t_{i}|^{\frac{1}{\sigma}}=k_{i}\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|\to\infty.

The latter, combined with (
3.1
), yields

\begin{split}u_{k_{i}}(x_{i},t_{i})&=k_{i}^{-\sigma}u(k_{i}x_{i}+x_{0},k_{i}^{\sigma}t_{i}+t_{0})\\
&=k_{i}^{-\sigma}o\left(\|(k_{i}x_{i},k_{i}^{\sigma}t_{i})\|^{\sigma}\right)\\
&=o\left(\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|^{\sigma}\right)\\
&=o(1),\end{split}

a contradiction.

Step 2.
For any given
(y,\tau)\in\overline{S}_{t_{0}}
, one can choose
k\in\mathbb{N}
big enough to guarantee

\left(k^{-1}(y-x_{0}),k^{-\sigma}(\tau-t_{0})\right)\in G_{1/5}.

Using Theorem (
2.1
), we then estimate

\begin{split}u(y,\tau)&=k^{\sigma}u_{k}\left(k^{-1}(y-x_{0}),k^{-\sigma}(\tau-t_{0})\right)\\
&\leq C\|u_{k}\|_{\infty}\|\left(y-x_{0},\tau-t_{0}\right)\|^{\sigma}.\end{split}

The latter with (
3.2
) implies
u(y,\tau)=0
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the next result, which reveals that for the conclusion to hold for the homogeneous equation, it is enough to assume the sharp asymptotic growth only in the space variable.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\geq 0
be as in Theorem
2.1
, and
u\geq 0
be a solution of

u_{t}-\mathrm{div}(u^{\gamma}\nabla u)=0

in a strip
S_{T}
,
T\in\mathbb{R}
. If
u(x_{0},t_{0})=0
for some
(x_{0},t_{0})\in S_{T}
, and

u(x,t)=o\left(|x|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}\right),\,\,\,\text{ as }\,\,\,|x|\rightarrow\infty,

(3.3)

uniformly in
t\in(-\infty,T)
, then
u\equiv 0
in
\overline{S}_{t_{0}}
.

Proof.

Observe that (
3.1
) holds. Indeed, if that is not the case, then for a sequence
(x_{i},t_{i})
and a constant
c_{*}>0
, one has

\frac{u(x_{i},t_{i})}{\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}}\geq c_{*}>0,\,\,\,\textrm{ as }\,\,\,\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|\to\infty.

(3.4)

As
\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|\to\infty
, then either
|x_{i}|\to\infty
or
|x_{i}|<r
for some
0<r<\infty
, and
t_{i}\to-\infty
. In the first case, combining (
3.3
) with (
3.4
), we get a contradiction, since

\frac{u(x_{i},t_{i})}{|x_{i}|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}}\geq\frac{u(x_{i},t_{i})}{\|(x_{i},t_{i})\|^{\frac{2}{\gamma+1}}}.

In the second case, that is, when
|x_{i}|<r
and
t_{i}\to-\infty
, we choose
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\tau\leq t_{1}-\left(\frac{c}{u(x_{1},t_{1})}\right)^{\gamma}(r+|x_{1}|)^{2},

where
c>0
is the universal constant from Proposition
2.1
. Since

\tau-t_{1}\leq-\left(\frac{c}{u(x_{1},t_{1})}\right)^{\gamma}|x-x_{1}|^{2},\,\,\,\forall x\in\overline{B}_{r},

the set

\overline{B}_{r}\times\{\tau\}
is contained in the paraboloid

P(x_{1},t_{1}):=\left\{(x,t)\in S_{T};\,\,t-t_{1}\leq-\left(\frac{c}{u(x_{1},t_{1})}\right)^{\gamma}|x-x_{1}|^{2}\right\}.

By Proposition
2.1
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}u(x,t)=0,\,\,\,\textrm{for all}\,\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and the convergence is uniform in
x\in\overline{B}_{r}
. In particular,

u(x_{i},t_{i})<1,

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
large enough, which contradicts to (
3.4
). Hence, (
3.1
) is true, and Theorem
3.1
implies
u\equiv 0
in
\overline{S}_{t_{0}}
.
∎

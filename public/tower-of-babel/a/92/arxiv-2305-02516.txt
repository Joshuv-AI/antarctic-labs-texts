Quantum Wigner molecules in moiré materials
Constantine Yannouleas, Uzi Landman

Abstract
The few-body problem (with N ≤ 6 N\leq 6 fermionic charge carriers) in isolated moiré quantum dots (MQDs) in transition metal dichalcogenide (TMD) bilayer materials with integer fillings, ν ≥ 2 \nu\geq 2 , is investigated by employing large-scale full configuration interaction (FCI, also termed exact-diagonalization) computations, and by performing a comparative analysis of the ensuing first-order (charge densities, CDs) and second-order (conditional probability distributions, CPDs) correlation functions. With parameters representative of bilayer experimental TMD setups, our investigations reveal the determining role of the strong inter-particle Coulombic repulsion in bringing about Wigner molecularization, which is associated with many-body physics beyond both that described by the aufbau principle of natural atoms, as well as by the widely used Hubbard model for strongly-interacting condensed-matter systems. In particular, for weak and moderate trilobal crystal-field deformations of the MQDs, the imperative employment of the CPDs brings to light the geometrical polygonal-ring configurations underlying the Wigner molecules (WMs) that remain hidden at the level of a charge-density analysis, apart from the case of N = 3 N=3 when a pinned WM emerges in the charge density due to the coincidence of the C 3 C_{3} symmetries associated with both the intrinsic geometry of the N = 3 N=3 WM and the TMD trilobal crystal-field of the confining pocket potential. The FCI numerically exact-diagonalization results provide critical benchmarks for assessing and guiding the development of future computational methodologies of interacting strongly-correlated fermions in isolated MQDs and their superlattices in TMD materials.

Appendix A
THE CONFIGURATION INTERACTION METHOD

The full configuration interaction methodology has a long history, starting in quantum chemistry; see
Refs.
Shavitt 1998
;
Szabo and Ostlund 1989
. The method was adapted to two dimensional problems and found extensive
applications in the fields of semiconductor quantum dots

Yannouleas and Landman 2003
;
Tavernier et al. 2003
;
Rontani et al. 2006
;
Li et al. 2007
;
Yannouleas and Landman 2007
;
Yannouleas and Landman 2022b
;
Yannouleas and Landman 2022c
and of the fractional quantum Hall effect

Yannouleas and Landman 2004
;
Jain 2007
.

Our 2D FCI is described in our earlier publications. The reader will find a comprehensive exposition in
Appendix B of Ref.
Yannouleas and Landman 2022b
, where the method was applied to GaAs double-quantum-dot quantum
computer qubits. The
only difference with the present application to moiré QDs, due to the different external confinement,
concerns the space orbitals,
\varphi_{j}(x,y)
,
j=1,2,\ldots,K
, that are employed in the building of the
spin-dependent, single-particle basis used to construct the Slater determinants
\Psi^{N}_{I}
, which span
the many-body Hilbert space [see Eq. (B4) in Ref.
Yannouleas and Landman 2022b
; the index
I
counts the Slater
determinants]. Indeed, for a moié QD, the
orbitals
\varphi_{j}(x,y)
are determined as solutions (in Cartesian coordinates) of the auxiliary
Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{\rm aux}=\frac{{\bf p}^{2}}{2m^{*}}+\frac{1}{2}m^{*}\omega_{0}^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2}),

(4)

where
m^{*}\omega_{0}^{2}=16\pi^{2}v_{0}\cos(\phi)/a_{M}^{2}
, i.e., only the isotropic parabolic (harmonic)
contribution in
V_{\rm MQD}
is included.

Following Ref.
Yannouleas and Landman 2022b
, we use a sparse-matrix eigensolver based on Implicitly Restarted
Arnoldi methods (see ARPACK
Lehoucq et al. 1998
) to
diagonalize the many-body Hamiltonian in Eq. (3) of the main text. When a finite magnetic field

B
value is used, we replace
{\bf p}
by
{\bf p}-(e/c){\bf A}({\bf r})
, where the vector potential

{\bf A}({\bf r})=0.5B(-y,x)
is taken according to the symmetric gauge.

The required one-body and two-body matrix elements for the ARPACK diagonalization are calculated as
described in Ref.
Yannouleas and Landman 2022b
. In addition, the matrix elements

\langle\varphi_{i}(x,y)|\sin(3\theta)r^{3}|\varphi_{j}(x,y)\rangle

of the anisotropic term in the moiré confinement are calculated analytically using the algebraic
language MATHEMATICA
Wolfram Research, Inc.
and the Hermite-to-Laguerre (Cartesian-to-polar) transformations listed
in Ref.
Kimel and Elias 1993
.

In all calculations, we used at a minimum values of
K=45
and/or
K=55
. For
N=2
and
N=3
, additional
calculations were carried out employing an extended single-particle basis with
K=78
. The maximum
dimension of the many-body Hilbert space (the number of Slater determinants
\Psi^{N}_{I}
in the FCI
expansion) reached a value of
I_{\rm max}\approx 5,000,000
in some cases.

Appendix B
CHARGE DENSITIES AND CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY DISTRIBUTIONS FROM FCI WAVE FUNCTIONS

The single-particle density (charge density) is the expectation value of a one-body operator

\rho({\bf r})=\langle\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}|\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta({\bf r}-{\bf r}_{i})|\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}\rangle,

(5)

where
\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}
denotes the many-body (multi-determinantal) FCI wave function, namely,

\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}({\bf r}_{1},\ldots,{\bf r}_{N})=\sum_{I}C_{I}\Psi^{N}_{I}({\bf r}_{1},\ldots,{\bf r}_{N}).

(6)

Naturally several distinct spin structures can correspond to the same charge density. The spin
structure associated with a specific FCI wave function can be determined with the help of the
many-body spin-resolved CPDs
Yannouleas and Landman 2000a
;
Yannouleas and Landman 2004
;
Li et al. 2006
;
Yannouleas and Landman 2007
;
Li et al. 2009
;
Brandt et al. 2015
.

The spin-resolved CPDs
yield the conditional probability distribution of finding another fermion with up (or down) spin

\sigma
at a position
{\bf r}
, assuming that a given fermion with up (or down) spin
\sigma_{0}

is fixed at
{\bf r_{0}}
. Specifically, the spin-resolved two-point anisotropic correlation function
is defined as the the expectation value of a two-body operator

P_{\sigma\sigma_{0}}({\bf r},{\bf r}_{0})=\langle\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}|\sum_{i\neq j}\delta({\bf r}-{\bf r}_{i})\delta({\bf r}_{0}-{\bf r}_{j})\delta_{\sigma\sigma_{i}}\delta_{\sigma_{0}\sigma_{j}}|\Phi^{\rm FCI}_{N}\rangle.

(7)

Using a normalization constant

{\cal N}(\sigma,\sigma_{0},{\bf r}_{0})=\int P_{\sigma\sigma_{0}}({\bf r},{\bf r}_{0})d{\bf r},

(8)

we define a related conditional probability distribution (CPD) as

{\cal P}_{\sigma\sigma_{0}}({\bf r},{\bf r}_{0})=P_{\sigma\sigma_{0}}({\bf r},{\bf r}_{0})/{\cal N}(\sigma,\sigma_{0},{\bf r}_{0}),

(9)

having the property

\int{\cal P}_{\sigma\sigma_{0}}({\bf r},{\bf r}_{0})d{\bf r}=1
.

Zero Surface Tension Limit of the Free-Boundary Problem in Incompressible Magnetohydrodynamics
Xumin Gu, Chenyun Luo, Junyan Zhang

Abstract
We show that the solution of the free-boundary incompressible ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations with surface tension converges to that of the free-boundary incompressible ideal MHD equations without surface tension given the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition holds initially. This result is a continuation of the authors’ previous works [ 17 , 32 , 16 ] . Our proof is based on the combination of the techniques developed in our previous works [ 17 , 32 , 16 ] , Alinhac good unknowns, and a crucial anti-symmetric structure on the boundary.

Introduction

We consider the following 3D incompressible ideal MHD system which describes the motion of a conducting fluid with free surface boundary in an electro-magnetic field under the influence of surface tension

\begin{cases}D_{t}u-B\cdot\nabla B+\nabla P=0,~~P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D};\\
D_{t}B-B\cdot\nabla u=0,~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D};\\
\text{div }u=0,~~\text{div }B=0,~~~&\text{in}~\mathcal{D},\end{cases}

(1.1)

with boundary conditions

\begin{cases}D_{t}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}\in\mathcal{T}(\partial\mathcal{D}),\\
P=\sigma\mathcal{H}~~~&\text{on}~\partial\mathcal{D},\\
B\cdot\hat{n}=0~~~&\text{on}~\partial\mathcal{D}.\end{cases}

(1.2)

Here,
D_{t}:=\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
denotes the material derivative, and
\mathcal{D}:=\cup_{0\leq t\leq T}\{t\}\times\mathcal{D}_{t}
, where
\mathcal{D}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded domain occupied by the conducting fluid (plasma) whose boundary
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
moves with the velocity of the fluid. Also, we denote by
v=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
the fluid’s velocity,
B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})
the magnetic field,
p
the fluid’s pressure, and
P:=p+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
the total pressure. The quantity
\mathcal{H}
is the mean curvature of the free surface
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
and
\sigma\geq 0
is a given constant, called surface tension coefficient.
Finally, we use
\hat{n}
to denote the exterior unit normal to
\partial\mathcal{D}_{t}
. The equations
\text{div }B=0
and
B\cdot\hat{n}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}}=0
are only the constraints for initial data that propagate to any
t>0
if initially hold (cf.
[
19
]
).

When
\sigma>0
(i.e., the MHD equations are under the influence of the surface tension), we proved in
[
16
]
that given a simply connected domain
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
and initial data
u_{0}
and
B_{0}
satisfying
\text{div }u_{0}=0
and
\text{div }B_{0}=0,B_{0}\cdot\hat{n}|_{\partial\mathcal{D}_{0}}=0
, there exist a set
\mathcal{D}
and vector fields
u
and
B
that solve (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with initial data

\mathcal{D}_{0}=\{x:(0,x)\in\mathcal{D}\},\quad(u,B)=(u_{0},B_{0}),\quad\text{in}\,\,\{t=0\}\times\Omega_{0}.

(1.3)

In addition, when
\sigma=0
(i.e., the surface tension is neglected) the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
) is known to be ill-posed
[
20
,
11
]
unless the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition is imposed

\displaystyle-\nabla_{n}P\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma,

(1.4)

for some

.
In fact,
we only need to impose (
1.4
) on the initial data and then show that it propagates within the interval of existence.
The local well-posedness (LWP) of the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
) was obtained by the first author and Wang in
[
17
]
.

The following terminologies will be used throughout the rest of this manuscript for the sake of clarity: We use “
\sigma>0
problem” to denote the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
), (
1.3
) with a positive surface tension coefficient

)-(

\sigma>0

\sigma=0

1.1
Reformulation in Lagrangian coordinates

We reformulate the MHD equations in the Lagrangian coordinates and thus the free-surface domain becomes fixed. Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded domain. Denoting coordinates on
\Omega
by
y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
, we define
\eta:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathcal{D}
to be the flow map of the velocity
v
, i.e.,

\partial_{t}\eta(t,y)=u(t,\eta(t,y)),\quad\eta(0,y)=\eta_{0}(y),

(1.5)

where
\eta_{0}:\Omega\to\mathcal{D}_{0}
is a diffeomorphism. We introduce the Lagrangian velocity, magnetic field, and pressure respectively by

v(t,y)=u(t,\eta(t,y)),\quad b(t,y)=B(t,\eta(t,y)),\quad q(t,y)=P(t,\eta(t,y)).

(1.6)

Let
\partial
be the spatial derivative concerning the
y
variable. We define the cofactor matrix
A=[\partial\eta]^{-1}
and the Jacobian
J:=\det[\partial\eta]
. Then direct calculation gives the explicit form of
A

\displaystyle A={J^{-1}}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}\eta\times\partial_{3}\eta\\
\partial_{3}\eta\times\partial_{1}\eta\\
\partial_{1}\eta\times\partial_{2}\eta\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon^{\alpha\lambda\tau}\partial_{2}\eta_{\lambda}\partial_{3}\eta_{\tau}\\
\epsilon^{\alpha\lambda\tau}\partial_{3}\eta_{\lambda}\partial_{1}\eta_{\tau}\\
\epsilon^{\alpha\lambda\tau}\partial_{1}\eta_{\lambda}\partial_{2}\eta_{\tau}\end{pmatrix},

(1.7)

where
\epsilon^{\alpha\lambda\tau}
is the fully antisymmetric symbol with
\epsilon^{123}=1
.
In light of (
1.7
), it is easy to see that
A
verifies the Piola’s identity

\partial_{\mu}({J}A^{\mu\alpha})=0,

(1.8)

where the summation convention is used for repeated upper and lower indices. Above and throughout, all Greek indices range over
1,2,3
, and the Latin indices range over
1,2
.
For the sake of simplicity and clean notation, we consider the model case when

\displaystyle\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times(0,1),

(1.9)

where
\partial\Omega=\Gamma_{0}\cup\Gamma
and
\Gamma=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{1\}
is the top (moving) boundary,
\Gamma_{0}=\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{0\}
is the fixed bottom.
This is known to be the reference domain.
Using a partition of unity, e.g.,
[
7
,
10
,
31
]
, a general domain can also be treated with the same tools we shall present. However, choosing
\Omega
as above allows us to focus on the real issues of the problem without being distracted by the cumbersomeness of the partition of unity.

Remark
.

The use of Lagrangian coordinates loosens the restriction on the geometry of the free surface in comparison to treating the free interface as a graph. Specifically, with a large initial velocity, the free surface may fail to form a graph within a very short time interval. Nonetheless, using Lagrangian coordinates opens the possibility to go beyond that time.

The system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be reformulated as:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v_{\alpha}-b_{\beta}A^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}+A^{\mu}_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}q=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}-b_{\beta}A^{\mu\beta}\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha}=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
{\text{div }_{A}v:=}A^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha}=0,~~{\text{div }_{A}b:=}A^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
v\cdot N=b\cdot N=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma_{0};\\
A^{\mu\alpha}N_{\mu}q+\sigma(\sqrt{g}\Delta_{g}\eta^{\alpha})=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma;\\
A^{\mu\nu}b_{\nu}N_{\mu}=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma,\\
(\eta,v,b)=({\eta_{0}},v_{0},b_{0})~~~&\text{on}~\{t=0\}{\times}\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(1.10)

where
A^{\mu}_{\alpha}:=\delta_{\alpha\beta}A^{\mu\beta}
,
N
is the unit outer normal vector to
\partial\Omega
, particularly
N=(0,0,-1)
on
\Gamma_{0}
and
N=(0,0,1)
on
\Gamma
, and
\Delta_{g}
is the Laplacian of the metric
g_{ij}
induced on
\partial\Omega(t)
by the embedding
\eta
. Specifically, we have:

g_{ij}=\overline{\partial}{}_{i}\eta^{\mu}\overline{\partial}{}_{j}\eta_{\mu},~\Delta_{g}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\overline{\partial}{}_{i}(\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\overline{\partial}{}_{j}(\cdot)),\text{ where }g:=\det(g_{ij}),

(1.11)

where
\overline{\partial}{}=(\overline{\partial}{}_{1},\overline{\partial}{}_{2})
denotes the tangential derivative (with respect to
\Gamma
). The symmetric matrix
\{g_{ij}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is indeed a metric on the free surface, as one can easily show that all of its leading principal minors are positive to prove the positive-definiteness.

One can show that
\partial_{t}J=J\text{tr}(A\partial_{t}[\partial\eta])=J\text{div }_{A}v=0
and thus the Jacobian
J=J_{0}=\det[\partial\eta_{0}]
is independent of time. This property corresponds to incompressibility. To get rid of unnecessary terms involving
J_{0}
, we may assume
J_{0}=1
for cleanness in the calculation. Under the Lagrangian formulation, we can express the magnetic field
b
in terms of its initial data and the flow map. Specifically, by the second equation of (
1.10
) and the divergence-free condition on
b
, we get
\partial_{t}(A^{\mu\nu}b_{\nu})=0
which implies
A^{\mu\nu}b_{\nu}=A_{0}^{\mu\nu}b_{0\nu}
and thus
b_{\alpha}=A_{0}^{\mu\nu}b_{0\nu}\partial_{\mu}\eta_{\alpha}:=\left((Ab_{0})\cdot\partial\right)\eta^{\alpha}
. We refer to Gu-Wang
[
17
, (1.13)-(1.15)]
for the proof. Therefore, we denote
\mathbf{b}_{0}=(Ab)_{0}
and thus
b=(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
.

We formulate the free-boundary MHD equations with and without surface tension. For each
\sigma>0
, let
(\eta^{\sigma},v^{\sigma},q^{\sigma})
verifies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{\sigma}=v^{\sigma}~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\partial_{t}v^{\sigma}-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}\eta^{\sigma}+\nabla_{A(\eta^{\sigma})}q^{\sigma}=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\text{div}\,_{A(\eta^{\sigma})}v^{\sigma}:=A(\eta^{\sigma})^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}v_{\nu}^{\sigma}=0,&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\text{div}\,\mathbf{b}_{0}:=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}(\mathbf{b}_{0})^{\nu}=0~~~&\text{in}~\{0\}\times\Omega;\\
(v^{\sigma})^{3}=\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma_{0};\\
A(\eta^{\sigma})^{3\alpha}q^{\sigma}+\sigma(\sqrt{g^{\sigma}}\Delta_{g^{\sigma}}(\eta^{\sigma})^{\alpha})=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma;\\
\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma,\\
(\eta^{\sigma},v^{\sigma})=({\eta_{0}},v_{0})~~~&\text{on}~\{t=0\}{\times}\overline{\Omega},\end{cases}

(1.12)

where
\nabla_{A(\eta^{\sigma})}^{\alpha}:=A(\eta^{\sigma})^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative, and the induced metric
g_{ij}=g_{ij}(\eta^{\sigma})
is given in (
1.11
). Let
\nabla_{A_{0}}:=\nabla_{A(\eta_{0})}
, and
\Delta_{A_{0}}:=\nabla_{A_{0}}\cdot\nabla_{A_{0}}
. The initial pressure
q_{0}
is determined by
\eta_{0}
,
v_{0}
and
\mathbf{b}_{0}
through the elliptic equation

{-\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{0}=(\nabla_{A_{0}}v_{0}):(\nabla_{A_{0}}v_{0})-(\nabla_{A_{0}}\mathbf{b}_{0}):(\nabla_{A_{0}}\mathbf{b}_{0})},\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,

(1.13)

with boundary conditions

\displaystyle q_{0}={\sigma\mathcal{H}_{0}},\quad\text{on}\,\,\,\Gamma,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q_{0}}{\partial N}=0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\,\Gamma_{0}.

Note that the Neumann boundary condition follows from restricting the normal component of the momentum equation to
\Gamma_{0}
. In
[
32
]
, the second and third authors proved the local a priori energy estimate of (
1.12
) for each fixed
\sigma>0
. The local well-posedness of this problem is established by us very recently in
[
16
]
.

On the other hand, when
\sigma=0
, let
(\zeta,w,r)
verifies

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\zeta=w~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\partial_{t}w-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}\zeta+\nabla_{A(\zeta)}r=0~~~&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\text{div}\,_{A(\zeta)}w=0,&\text{in}~[0,T]\times\Omega;\\
\text{div}\,\mathbf{b}_{0}=0~~~&\text{in}~\{0\}\times\Omega;\\
w^{3}=\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma_{0};\\
r=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma;\\
\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0~~~&\text{on}~\Gamma,\\
(\zeta,w)=({\eta_{0}},v_{0})~~~&\text{on}~\{t=0\}{\times}\overline{\Omega}.\end{cases}

(1.15)

The initial pressure
r_{0}
is determined by the elliptic equation

{-\Delta_{A_{0}}r_{0}=(\nabla_{A_{0}}v_{0}):(\nabla_{A_{0}}v_{0})-(\nabla_{A_{0}}\mathbf{b}_{0}):(\nabla_{A_{0}}\mathbf{b}_{0})},\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega,

(1.16)

with the boundary condition
r_{0}=0
on
\Gamma
and
\partial r_{0}/\partial N=0
on
\Gamma_{0}
.
Moreover, for (
1.15
) to be well-posed, we assume the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition

-\partial_{3}r_{0}\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma

(1.17)

holds for some constant
c_{0}>0
.

Remark
.

In fact, the original Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition for (
1.15
) is
-\hat{n}_{0}\cdot\nabla_{A_{0}(\zeta_{0})}r_{0}\geq c_{0}>0
. But since

on
\Gamma
implies
\overline{\partial}{}_{1}r=\overline{\partial}{}_{2}r=0
on
\Gamma
, then the sign condition becomes
-\sqrt{g_{0}(\zeta_{0})}\hat{n}_{0\alpha}\hat{n}_{0}^{\alpha}\partial_{3}r_{0}\geq c_{0}>0
. So it is reasonable to directly assume
-\partial_{3}r_{0}\geq c_{0}>0.

1.2
The main result

This paper aims to show that if
q_{0}
verifies the Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition, i.e.,

-\partial_{3}q_{0}\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma,

(1.18)

then the solution of the “
\sigma>0
problem” (

) converges to the solution of the “
\sigma=0

) as

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose the initial data
(v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0},q_{0})
verifies:

1.

are divergence-free vector fields with
v_{0}^{3}=0
on
\Gamma_{0}
and
\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0

\Gamma\cup\Gamma_{0}
.

2.

\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\in H^{5.5}(\Omega)
;
\eta_{0},v_{0}\in H^{7}(\Gamma)
; the initial mean curvature
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}\in H^{6}(\Gamma)
.

3.

The Rayleigh-Taylor sign condition

-\partial_{3}q_{0}\geq c_{0}>0,\quad\text{on}\,\,\Gamma.

(1.19)

4.

The compatibility conditions up to the
4
-th order, where the

-th order (

) condition reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{j}q(0)=\sigma\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{H}(0),

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma,

(1.20)

\displaystyle\partial_{3}\partial_{t}^{j}q(0)=0,

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{0}.

Then there exists some

independent of
\sigma
, such that the solutions to (
1.12
) and (
1.15
) exists, and as

, we have

\displaystyle(v^{\sigma},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta^{\sigma},q^{\sigma})\xrightarrow{{C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega)}}(w,(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\zeta,r).

(1.21)

We will drop the superscript
\sigma
on
(\eta^{\sigma},v^{\sigma},q^{\sigma})
in the rest of this paper and simply denote them by
(\eta,v,q)
when no confusion can be raised.

Notation 1.2
.

(The Sobolev norms) Let
f=f(t,y)
be a smooth function on
[0,T]\times\Omega
and

be a smooth function on
[0,T]\times\Gamma

Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence of the following theorem where we establish an energy estimate of the

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0},q_{0})
be the initial data given in Theorem

. Let

E(t)=E_{1}(t)+\sigma E_{2}(t),

with

(1.23)

and

E_{2}(t):=\left\|\eta(t)\right\|_{5.5}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{4}\left(\left\|\partial_{t}^{k}v(t)\right\|_{5.5-k}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta(t)\right\|_{5.5-k}^{2}\right)+\sum_{k=0}^{5}\left|\overline{\partial}{}^{6-k}\partial_{t}^{k}\eta(t)\cdot\hat{n}(t)\right|_{0}^{2}+\left|\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta(t)\cdot\hat{n}(t)\right|_{0}^{2},

(1.24)

where

is the Eulerian unit normal vector.
Then

{E(t)\leq P(E(0)),}

(1.25)

where

is a generic polynomial in its arguments. Also,
there exists a constant

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}\left(c_{0},\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},\,\sqrt{\sigma}\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5.5},\,\sum_{k=0}^{3}\sigma^{1+\frac{k}{2}}\left|\eta_{0},v_{0}\right|_{5.5+\frac{k}{2}},\,\sum_{k=0}^{3}\sigma^{1+\frac{k}{2}}|\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5+\frac{k}{2}}\right)>0

\displaystyle{E(t)\leq\mathcal{C},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].}

(1.26)

Theorem
1.3
states that given the initial data for the
\sigma=0

\sigma>0

E(T)\leq\mathcal{P}_{0}+\epsilon E(T)+P(E(T))\int_{0}^{T}P(E(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

Remark
.

The reason for us to require
\eta_{0}
and

to have higher boundary regularity is that we need to express

\partial_{t}^{k}v(0),\quad\partial_{t}^{k}b(0),\quad 1\leq k\leq 5,\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,H^{5-k},

\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{k}v(0),\quad\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{k}b(0),\quad 1\leq k\leq 4,\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,H^{5.5-k},

and

\partial_{t}^{\ell}q(0),\quad 0\leq\ell\leq 4,\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,H^{5-\ell},

\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{\ell}q(0),\quad 0\leq\ell\leq 3,\quad\text{in}\,\,\,\,H^{5.5-\ell},

in terms of
\eta_{0}
,
v_{0}
and
\mathbf{b}_{0}

1.3.1
Difference between Euler and MHD equations

The zero surface tension limit for the free-boundary (compressible) Euler equations is studied in

, but the method developed there does not apply to the free-boundary MHD equations. Their treatment depends on the assumption that

1.3.2
Treating the higher-order interior and boundary terms

The aforementioned regularity issue prevents us from commuting
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
with the equation

\partial_{t}v-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}\eta+\nabla_{A}q=0,

because we are unable to control the term generated when all derivatives land on

v
and
q
(i.e., (
2.1

Nevertheless, the use of good unknowns alone cannot fully resolve the problem, as the surface tension introduces higher-order boundary terms in the energy estimate (i.e., the integrals on the RHS of (
2.2

for the detailed explanations.

1.3.3
Application to other free-boundary fluid models

The method developed in this manuscript is applicable to study the zero surface limit problem for Euler equations without the extra regularity assumption on
\eta

Also, it is possible to adapt our method to study the zero surface tension limit in other complex fluid models, where no extra regularity assumption can be made on the flow map

2.1.1
Necessity of time derivatives

The energy (

2.1.2
Interior tangential estimates: Alinhac good unknowns and cancellation structures

The non-weighted full Sobolev norms are analyzed via div-curl analysis. The curl part can be controlled via the evolution equation of the Eulerian vorticity. As for tangential estimates, if the tangential derivatives contain at least one time derivatives, one may follow the ideas in our previous work
[
32
,
16
]
to close the energy estimates and we refer to Section
6
for the proof.

However, when the tangential derivatives are purely spatial, we have to introduce the Alinhac good unknowns to proceed with the energy estimates because we cannot directly commute
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
with the covariant derivative
\nabla_{A}
falling on
q
or
v
. Instead, we rewrite the term
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\nabla_{A}q)
in terms of the covariant derivative of the Alinhac good unknowns plus a controllable “error” term. See Section
5
for the proof. This remarkable observation was first due to Alinhac
[
1
]
. In the study of free-surface fluid, it is first (implicitly) applied to the
Q
-tensor energy introduced by Christodoulou-Lindblad
[
6
]
. Here, following
[
17
, Section 4]
, the Alinhac good unknowns of

and

with respect to

are

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}v-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}v,~~\mathbf{Q}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}q-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}q.

(2.1)

Under this setting, it suffices to analyze the evolution equation of the good unknowns to derive the
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
-estimates.

Compared with the “
\sigma=0

, becomes very difficult because the top order derivative of the pressure

no longer vanishes due to the presence of surface tension. It contributes to

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma}A^{3\alpha}\overline{\partial}{}^{5}q\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}dS=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma}\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(A^{3\alpha}q)\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Gamma}q(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}A^{3\alpha})\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}S+\int_{\Gamma}A^{3\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}q)\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}S.+\text{lower order terms}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle:\text{ ST}+J_{1}+\text{ RT}+\text{lower order terms},

Due to the presence of

\hat{n}=\overline{\partial}{}\eta\times\overline{\partial}{}\eta
.

Finally, the following
H^{1}
-elliptic estimates will be applied to control
\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}q\|_{1}
and
\|\sqrt{\sigma}\overline{\partial}{}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathfrak{D}^{4}q\|_{1}
for
\mathfrak{D}=\partial_{t}
or
\overline{\partial}{}
.

Lemma 2.8

(
Low regularity elliptic estimates
)
.

Assume
\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}
satisfies
\|\mathfrak{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K
and the ellipticity
\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}(x)\xi_{\mu}\xi_{\nu}\geq\frac{1}{K}|\xi|^{2}
for all
x\in\Omega
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Assume
W
to be an
H^{1}
solution to

\begin{cases}-\partial_{\nu}(\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W)=\text{div }\pi&\text{ in }\Omega\\
\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}WN_{\nu}=h&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(2.19)

where
\pi,\text{div }\pi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
h\in H^{-0.5}(\partial\Omega)
with the compatibility condition

\int_{\partial\Omega}(\pi\cdot N+h)dS=0.

Then we have:

\|W\|_{1}\lesssim_{\text{vol}\,(\Omega)}\|\pi\|_{0}+|h+\pi\cdot N|_{-0.5}.

(2.20)

Proof.

Testing the first equation of (
2.19
) with
\psi\in H^{1}
and then integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}W\partial_{\nu}\psi\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\Omega}\pi\cdot\nabla\psi\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\partial\Omega}(\pi\cdot N+h)\psi\,\mathrm{d}S\leq\|\pi\|_{0}\|\psi\|_{1}+|\pi\cdot N+h|_{-0.5}|\psi|_{0.5}.

(2.21)

Because
\mathfrak{B}
is positive-definite,
\|\mathfrak{B}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K
and
\psi
is arbitrary, this yields

\displaystyle\|\partial W\|_{0}\lesssim\|\pi\|_{0}\|\psi\|_{1}+|\pi\cdot N+h|_{-0.5}|\psi|_{0.5}.

(2.22)

Next, we need to control the
L^{2}
-norm of
W
. Invoking Poincaré’s inequality
\|W-\overline{W}\|_{0}\lesssim\|\partial W\|_{0}
with
\overline{W}:=\frac{1}{\text{vol}(\Omega)}\int_{\Omega}W\,\mathrm{d}y
, we have

\|W\|_{0}\lesssim_{\text{vol}\,(\Omega)}\|\partial W\|_{0}+\int_{\Omega}W\,\mathrm{d}y.

(2.23)

Let
Y=(y^{1},0,0)
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{\mu}Y^{\mu}W\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\Omega}y^{1}\partial_{1}W\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim_{\text{vol}\,(\Omega)}\|\overline{\partial}{}W\|_{0}.

(2.24)

This concludes the proof.
∎

3
Elliptic estimates for the pressure and its time derivatives

In this section, we control the pressure
q
and its time derivatives. The estimates presented in this section are essentially the same as the ones in

. Our conclusion is

Proposition 3.1
.

The total pressure
q
satisfies

\sum_{k=0}^{4}\|\partial_{t}^{k}q\|_{5-k}^{2}+\sum_{k=0}^{3}\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{k}q\|_{5.5-k}^{2}\leq P(E(t)).

(3.1)

Due to the presence of the surface tension, we need to consider the elliptic equation verified by
q
equipped with the Neumann boundary condition. We henceforth let
\Delta_{A}q:=\text{div}\,_{A}(\nabla_{A}q)=A^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}(A^{\nu}_{\alpha}\partial_{\nu}q)
. Taking
\text{div}\,_{A}
in the second equation of (
1.12
) and invoking the identity
\text{div}\,_{A}v=\text{div}\,_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta=0

\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu\alpha}=0

-\Delta_{A}q=-\partial_{\nu}(A^{\nu\alpha}{A^{\mu}}_{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}q)=\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}v_{\beta}\nabla_{A}^{\beta}v_{\alpha}-\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\beta}\nabla_{A}^{\beta}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}.

(3.2)

We define
\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}=A^{\nu\alpha}{A^{\mu}}_{\alpha}

2.8
. In particular, we have
\mathfrak{B}^{33}\geq 1/K
for some

in terms of
\leq 1

plus the terms containing

and

{\partial_{3}^{2}q=\dfrac{1}{\mathfrak{B}^{33}}\left((\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}v_{\beta})(\nabla_{A}^{\beta}v_{\alpha})-(\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}b_{\beta})(\nabla_{A}^{\beta}b_{\alpha})-\sum_{\mu+\nu\neq 6}\partial_{\mu}(\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}q)-\partial_{3}\mathfrak{B}^{3\nu}\partial_{\nu}q\right).}

Repeatedly, one can reduce up to 5 normal derivatives of

\leq 4

and
(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
, and
\leq 1

normal
derivatives of

\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\partial}{}
or
\partial_{t}

To control these tangential derivatives of

Now if we set

(3.6)

Next we let
\mathfrak{D}=\overline{\partial}{}
or
\partial_{t}
be a tangential derivative and take
\mathfrak{D}^{4}

3.4
)-(
3.5

on

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{D}^{4}q\|_{1}\lesssim\|\pi\|_{0}\lesssim P(E_{1}(t))+P(N_{0})(\|\mathfrak{D}^{3}\partial q\|_{0}+\|\mathfrak{D}^{2}\partial q\|_{0}+\|\mathfrak{D}\partial q\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial q\|_{L^{\infty}}).

4
Estimates for the non-weighted full Sobolev norms

We study the estimates for
v
,
(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
, and their time derivatives in full Sobolev spaces. More precisely, we need to estimate

\|\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{5-k}^{2},\quad\|\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{5-k}^{2},\quad\text{for}\,\,k=0,1,2,3,4.

We will adopt the Hodge-type elliptic div-curl estimate (
2.17
) to the quantities above. Specifically, we will replace
X
by
\partial_{t}^{k}v
and
\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
, as well as their
\sigma
-weighted versions. For
0\leq k\leq 4
, using (
2.17
) in Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{5-k}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C\left(\|\sqrt{\sigma}v\|_{0}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\sigma}\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2}+\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}{}^{5-k}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{0}^{2}\right),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{5.5-k}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim C\left(\|(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{0}^{2}+\|\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4.5-k}^{2}+\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4.5-k}^{2}+\|\overline{\partial}{}^{5-k}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{0}^{2}\right)

(4.2)

where the constant
C=C(|\overline{\partial}{}\eta|_{W^{1,\infty}},\|\eta\|_{5-k}^{2})>0
depends linearly on
\|\eta\|_{5-k}^{2}
.

4.1
Divergence estimates

First, we treat

\|\text{div}\,\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2},\quad\|\text{div}\,\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4-k}^{2},\,\,k=0,\cdots,4.

We recall that
\text{div}\,_{A}X:=A^{\mu\alpha}\partial_{\mu}X_{\alpha}
. When
k=0
, since
\text{div}\,_{A}v=0
and
\text{div}\,_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta=0
. When
k\geq 1
, we differentiate the div-free conditions in time and get

\displaystyle\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{k}(\text{div }_{A}v)-[\partial_{t}^{k},A^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha}=-[\partial_{t}^{k},A^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}v_{\alpha},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta=

\displaystyle-[\partial_{t}^{k},A^{\mu\alpha}]\partial_{\mu}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}.

We notice that the right side of (
4.3
) only contains
\leq k-1
time derivatives and the highest order terms contains
4
derivatives. Thus, in light of the Sobolev product estimate (
2.16
), we have

\|\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2}+\|\text{div }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4-k}^{2}\lesssim P\left(\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\|\eta\|_{4},\|\partial_{t}^{i}v\|_{4-i},\|\partial_{t}^{i}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4-i}\right)\lesssim P(E_{1}(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{1}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t.

(4.4)

4.2
Curl estimates

In this part we aim to control
\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}
and
\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4-k}
for
0\leq k\leq 4
, where we recall
(\text{curl }_{A}X)_{\lambda}:=\epsilon_{\lambda\tau\alpha}A^{\mu\tau}\partial_{\mu}X^{\alpha}
.

Applying
\text{curl }_{A}
to the second equation of (
1.12
), we get

\partial_{t}(\text{curl }_{A}v)-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\left(\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right)=\text{curl }_{\partial_{t}A}v+[\text{curl }_{A},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)](\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta:=\mathcal{F},

(4.5)

where
(\text{curl }_{\partial_{t}A}v)_{\lambda}:=\epsilon_{\lambda\tau\alpha}\partial_{t}A^{\mu\tau}\partial_{\mu}X^{\alpha}
. Now, when
k=0
, by taking
\partial^{4}
to (
4.5
), testing it with
\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}(\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v)\left((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\partial^{4}\left(\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left([\partial^{4},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta+\partial^{4}\mathcal{F}\right)(\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v).

(4.6)

Since
\mathbf{b}_{0}^{3}=0
, we integrate
(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)
by parts in the second term on the LHS:

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v)\left((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\partial^{4}\left(\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\right)\right)=\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v)\partial^{4}(\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)v)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)-\int_{\Omega}([\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]v)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta).

(4.7)

Here,
\int_{\Omega}\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)v)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)
contributes to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)|^{2}-\int_{\Omega}\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{\partial_{t}A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta).

(4.8)

Therefore, (
4.2
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{1}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)|^{2}\right)

(4.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left([\partial^{4},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta+\partial^{4}\mathcal{F}\right)(\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}v)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}([\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]v)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)+\int_{\Omega}\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{\partial_{t}A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\partial^{4}\text{curl }_{A}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta).

Now, because

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}\|_{4}^{2}\leq\|\text{curl }_{\partial_{t}A}v\|_{4}^{2}+\|[\text{curl }_{A},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)](\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4}^{2}\leq P(\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},\|\eta\|_{5},\|v\|_{5},\|(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{5}),

it is not hard to see that the terms on the RHS of (
4.9
) can be controlled by
P(E(t))
. Therefore,

\|\text{curl }_{A}v\|_{4}^{2}+\|\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{4}^{2}\bigg|_{0}^{T}\lesssim\int_{0}^{T}P(E(t)).

(4.10)

The estimates for the cases when
k=1,2,3,4
follow from a parallel argument: By taking
\partial^{4-k}\partial_{t}^{k}
(or
\partial^{4.5-k}\partial_{t}^{k}
) to (
4.5
), testing it with
\partial^{4-k}\partial_{t}^{k}\text{curl }_{A}v
, the energy estimate then yields

\sum_{k=1}^{4}\Big(\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2}+\|\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\partial_{t}^{k}\eta\|_{4-k}^{2}\Big)\lesssim P(E_{1}(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E(t)).

(4.11)

Finally, given (
4.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{4}\Big(\|\text{curl }_{A}\partial_{t}^{k}v\|_{4-k}^{2}+\|\text{curl }_{A}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\partial_{t}^{k}\eta\|_{4-k}^{2}\Big)\lesssim P(E_{1}(0))+\int_{0}^{T}P(E_{1}(t)).}

(4.12)

5
Tangential estimate of spatial derivatives

By (
4.1
)-(
4.2
), we still need to do the interior tangential estimate. In this section, we would like to do the
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
-estimates for
v
and

, i.e., the control of tangential spatial derivatives.

5.1
Interior estimates: Alinhac good unknown

However, we cannot directly commute
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
with the covariant derivative
\nabla_{A}
because the commutator contains
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}A=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\partial\eta\times\partial\eta
whose
L^{2}
norm cannot be controlled. The reason is that the essential highest order term in
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\nabla_{A}f)
, i.e., the standard derivatives of a covariant derivative, is the covariant derivative of Alinhac good unknown
\mathbf{f}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}f-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}f
instead of the term produced by simply commuting
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
with
\nabla_{A}
. Specifically,

\displaystyle\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}f)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}f)+(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}A^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}{}^{5},A^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}f)-\overline{\partial}{}^{4}(A^{\mu\gamma}\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\eta_{\gamma}A^{\beta\alpha})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}{}^{5},A^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}f)-A^{\beta\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta_{\gamma}A^{\mu\gamma}\partial_{\mu}f-([\overline{\partial}{}^{4},A^{\mu\gamma}A^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\eta_{\gamma})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}{}^{5},A^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}f-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta_{\gamma}A^{\mu\gamma}\partial_{\mu}f)}_{=\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}\mathbf{f}}+\underbrace{\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta_{\gamma}\nabla_{A}^{\alpha}(\nabla_{A}^{\gamma}g)-([\overline{\partial}{}^{4},A^{\mu\gamma}A^{\beta\alpha}]\overline{\partial}{}\partial_{\beta}\eta_{\gamma})\partial_{\mu}f+[\overline{\partial}{}^{5},A^{\mu\alpha},\partial_{\mu}f]}_{=:C^{\alpha}(f)},

We introduce the Alinhac good unknowns of
v
and
q
with respect to
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
by

\mathbf{V}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}v-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}v,~~\mathbf{Q}:=\overline{\partial}{}^{5}q-\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}q.

(5.1)

Then direct computation (e.g., see
[
17
, Section 4.2.4]
) shows that the good unknowns enjoy the following properties:

\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\underbrace{(\nabla_{A}\cdot v)}_{=0}=\nabla_{A}\cdot\mathbf{V}+C(v),~~\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\nabla_{A}q)=\nabla_{A}\mathbf{Q}+C(q)

(5.2)

and

\|C(f)\|_{0}\lesssim P(\|\eta\|_{5})\|f\|_{5}.

(5.3)

Under this setting, we take
\overline{\partial}{}^{5}
in the second equation of (
1.12
) and invoke (
5.1
) to get the evolution equation of the Alinhac good unknowns

\partial_{t}\mathbf{V}=-\nabla_{A}\mathbf{Q}+(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)+\underbrace{\partial_{t}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}v)-C(q)+[\overline{\partial}{}^{5},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)}_{=:\mathfrak{f}_{0}}.

(5.4)

Taking
L^{2}(\Omega)
inner product with
\mathbf{V}
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\mathbf{V}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{A}\mathbf{Q}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right)\cdot\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y,

(5.5)

where the last term can be directly controlled:

\int_{\Omega}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y\lesssim P(\|\eta\|_{5},\|v\|_{5},\|\partial_{t}v\|_{4},\|q\|_{5},\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},\|(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta\|_{5})\lesssim P(E_{1}(t)).

(5.6)

Then we integrate
(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)
by parts in the second integral of (
5.5
) to produce the tangential energy of the magnetic field
(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta
. Note that since
\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot N=0
on
\partial{\Omega}
and
\text{div }\mathbf{b}_{0}=0
, no boundary term appears in this step.

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right)\cdot\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\Omega}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\mathbf{V}\,\mathrm{d}y

(5.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\partial_{t}\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Omega}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\cdot(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}v)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left|\overline{\partial}{}^{5}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta^{\alpha})([\overline{\partial}{}^{5},(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)]v_{\alpha}+(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\eta\cdot\nabla_{A}v_{\alpha}))\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\left|\overline{\partial}{}^{5}((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta)\right|^{2}+P(E_{1}(t)).

Next, we analyze the first integral of (
5.5
). Integrate by parts, using Piola’s identity
\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu\alpha}=0

\partial_{t}b(0)=\partial_{t}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta(0)=(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)v_{0}

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}v(0)\|_{4}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4}\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{5}\|q_{0}\|_{5},\quad\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}v(0)\|_{4.5}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4.5}\|\sqrt{\sigma}\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5.5}+\|\eta_{0}\|_{5}\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{0}\|_{5.5}+\|\sqrt{\sigma}\eta_{0}\|_{5.5}\|q_{0}\|_{5},

(A.2)

and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}b(0)\|_{4}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4}\|v_{0}\|_{5},\quad\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}b(0)\|_{4.5}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4.5}\|\sqrt{\sigma}v_{0}\|_{5.5}.

(A.3)

These imply that we need to bound
\|q_{0}\|_{5}
and
\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{0}\|_{5.5}
. Invoking the elliptic equation verified by
q_{0}
, i.e.,

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{0}=-(\partial_{t}A(0)^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(v_{0})_{\alpha}-((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)A_{0}^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(\mathbf{b}_{0})_{\alpha}+A_{0}^{\mu\alpha}(\partial_{\mu}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\mathbf{b}_{0})_{\alpha},~~~&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
q_{0}=\sigma\mathcal{H}_{0}=-\sigma\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0},~~~&\text{ on }\Gamma(0),\\
\frac{\partial q_{0}}{\partial N}=0,~~~&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{fix}},\\
\end{cases}

(A.4)

the elliptic estimate then yields

\displaystyle\|q_{0}\|_{5}\leq

\displaystyle~P(\|\eta_{0}\|_{4},\|v_{0}\|_{4},\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4},\sigma|\mathcal{H}_{0}|_{4.5}),

(A.5)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{0}\|_{5.5}\leq

\displaystyle~P(\|\eta_{0}\|_{4.5},\|v_{0}\|_{4.5},\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4.5},\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|\mathcal{H}_{0}|_{5}),

(A.6)

where

\sigma|\mathcal{H}_{0}|_{4.5}=\sigma|\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5},\quad\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|\mathcal{H}_{0}|_{5}=\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{5}.

(A.7)

A.2
Two and more time derivatives

To control

\|\partial_{t}^{k}v(0),\partial_{t}^{k}b(0)\|_{5-k},\quad 2\leq k\leq 5

and

\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{\ell}v(0),\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{\ell}b(0)\|_{5.5-\ell},\quad 2\leq\ell\leq 4

we have to study the time-differentiated elliptic equation restricted on
\{t=0\}
to control
\|\partial_{t}^{k-1}q(0)\|_{5-k}
,
\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{\ell-1}q(0)\|_{5.5-\ell}
. In light of the first remark after Theorem
1.3
, we require the extra boundary regularity of
\eta_{0},v_{0}
when controlling these quantities. This seems to be necessary as if we alternatively consider using the Neumann boundary conditions, we would have to control the term with one more time derivative on the velocity and thus we could never close the loop.

A.2.1
Two time derivatives

When
k=2
, the control of
\partial_{t}^{2}v(0)
requires the bounds for
\|q_{t}(0)\|_{4}
and
\|\sigma q_{t}(0)\|_{4.5}
. The Dirichlet boundary condition then contributes to
|\sigma v_{0}|_{5.5}
and
|\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}v_{0}|_{6}
. Specifically, we study the equations for
q_{1}:=\partial_{t}q(0)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{1}

\displaystyle=-(\partial_{t}^{2}A(0)^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(v_{0})_{\alpha}-(\partial_{t}A(0)^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{t}v(0))_{\alpha}-((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\partial_{t}A(0)^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(\mathbf{b}_{0})_{\alpha}-((\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)A_{0}^{\mu\alpha})\partial_{\mu}(\partial_{t}b(0))_{\alpha}

(A.8)

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}A(0)^{\mu\alpha}(\partial_{\mu}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\mathbf{b}_{0})_{\alpha}+A_{0}^{\mu\alpha}(\partial_{\mu}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)(\partial_{t}b(0))_{\alpha},

with the boundary conditions

\displaystyle q_{1}=\sigma\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(0),~~~

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma(0),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q_{1}}{\partial N}=-(\partial_{t}A^{3\alpha})q_{0},~~~

\displaystyle\text{ on }\Gamma_{\text{fix}}.

We have

\displaystyle\|q_{1}\|_{4}\leq P(\|\eta_{0}\|_{4},\|v_{0}\|_{4},\|\partial_{t}v(0)\|_{3},\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4},\|\partial_{t}b(0)\|_{3},\|q_{0}\|_{4},\sigma|\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(0)|_{3.5}),

(A.9)

and

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{1}\|_{4.5}\leq P(\|\eta_{0}\|_{4.5},\|v_{0}\|_{4.5},\|\partial_{t}v(0)\|_{3.5},\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{4.5},\|\partial_{t}b(0)\|_{3.5},\|q_{0}\|_{4.5},\sigma^{3/2}|\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(0)|_{4}).

(A.10)

Since
\mathcal{H}=-\sigma\sqrt{g}\Delta_{g}\eta\cdot\hat{n}=-\sigma g^{ij}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}_{ij}\eta\cdot(\overline{\partial}{}_{1}\eta\times\overline{\partial}{}_{2}\eta)
, we have
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}=\sigma\left(g^{ij}\overline{\partial}{}^{2}_{ij}v\cdot(\overline{\partial}{}_{1}\eta\times\overline{\partial}{}_{2}\eta)+\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\eta\overline{\partial}{}vP(\overline{\partial}{}\eta)\right).
So, the control of
\sigma|\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(0)|_{3.5}
and
\sigma^{3/2}|\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}(0)|_{4}
respectively reduce to

\displaystyle{\sigma\left|\partial_{t}(\Delta_{g}\eta\cdot\hat{n})(0)\right|_{3.5}\leq P(\|v_{0}\|_{5},\|\eta_{0}\|_{5},\sigma|v_{0}|_{5.5},\sigma|\eta_{0}|_{5.5})},

(A.11)

\displaystyle{\sigma^{3/2}\left|\partial_{t}(\Delta_{g}\eta\cdot\hat{n})(0)\right|_{4}\leq P(\|v_{0}\|_{5},\|\eta_{0}\|_{5},\|\sqrt{\sigma}\eta_{0}\|_{5},\|\sqrt{\sigma}\eta_{0}\|_{5.5},\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|v_{0}|_{6},\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|\eta_{0}|_{6})}.

(A.12)

A.2.2
Three and four time derivatives

We consider the
\partial_{t}^{k-1}
-differentiated elliptic equation for
q
at
t=0

-\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{k-1}=-\partial_{\nu}\left([\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{t}^{k-1}]\partial_{\mu}q\right)-\partial_{t}^{k-1}\left(\partial_{t}A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}v_{\alpha}-A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}\right)\big|_{t=0}=:f_{k-1}.

(A.13)

When
k=3
, the control of
\|\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\|_{2}
requires the bounds for
\|\partial_{t}^{2}q(0)\|_{3}
, whose Dirichlet boundary condition reads

q_{2}=-\sigma\partial_{t}^{2}\mathcal{H}(0)=-\sigma\sqrt{g}\Delta_{g_{0}}v_{t}(0)\cdot\hat{n}_{0}+\cdots.

Here and throughout,
\cdots
denotes all boundary terms without the top order time derivative on the velocity, which do not contribute to the highest order boundary terms of
\eta_{0}
and
v_{0}
.
Using
v_{t}(0)=(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial})\eta_{0}-\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}
on
\Gamma
and
\Delta_{g_{0}}f=P(\overline{\partial}{}\eta)\overline{\partial}{}^{2}f
, we need to control
|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}
and
|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial})^{2}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}
. The latter is controlled by

|\sigma(\overline{\partial}{}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial})^{2}\eta_{0})\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}\lesssim\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5}^{2}|\sigma\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5}.

To control the term involving
q_{0}
, we need the following normal trace lemma.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume the vector field
X
satisfies
X\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\nabla_{A_{0}}\cdot X=0
, then

|X\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|X\|_{0}+\|\nabla_{A_{0}}\cdot X\|_{0}.

(A.14)

Similarly, one has for
s\geq 1

|X\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{s-\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim C(|\overline{\partial}{}\eta_{0}|_{W^{1,\infty}},\|\eta_{0}\|_{s+\frac{1}{2}})\left(\|\overline{\partial}{}^{s}X\|_{0}+\|\nabla_{A_{0}}\cdot X\|_{s-1}\right).

(A.15)

Proof.

We only prove the case
s=0
as one can later replace
X
by
\overline{\partial}{}^{s}X
. The second inequality relies on
\|\eta_{0}\|_{s+\frac{1}{2}}
because
\overline{\partial}{}^{s}
may fall on
\hat{n}_{0}
. For
s=0
, we pick an arbitrary test function
\varphi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
with its harmonic extension
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. Then by the divergence theorem

\int_{\Gamma}X\cdot\hat{n}_{0}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}S=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{A_{0}}\cdot X\Phi\,\mathrm{d}y+\int_{\Omega}X\cdot\nabla_{A_{0}}\Phi\,\mathrm{d}y

and taking supremum over all
\varphi
finished the proof.
∎

Applying Lemma
A.1
to
q_{0}
, we get
|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}\lesssim\sigma\|\overline{\partial}{}^{5}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}\|_{0}+\|\sigma\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{0}\|_{4},
which shows that we need the bounds for
\|\sigma\nabla q_{0}\|_{5}
. This can be controlled by invoking the standard elliptic estimate (parallel to
[
17
, Prop. 5.3]
):

\|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}\|_{5}\lesssim C(|\sigma\eta_{0}|_{5.5})(\|\sigma\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{0}\|_{4}+\sigma^{2}|\mathcal{H}_{0}|_{5.5})\lesssim P(|\sigma\eta_{0}|_{5.5},\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},\sigma^{2}|\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{5.5}).

(A.16)

Summarizing the bounds above, we get

\|q_{2}\|_{3}\leq~P(\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},|\sigma\eta_{0}|_{5.5},|\sigma\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5},|\sigma^{2}\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{5.5})

(A.17)

Similarly, for
\sqrt{\sigma}
-weighted norms, we have
\|\sqrt{\sigma}\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\|_{2.5}\leq\|\sqrt{\sigma}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}v_{t}(0)\|_{2.5}+\|\sqrt{\sigma}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}(0)\|_{2.5}
, and then

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}v_{t}(0)\|_{2.5}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\sqrt{\sigma}\|v_{t}(0)\|_{4.5}+\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{2}(0)\|_{3.5},

(A.18)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{2}\|_{3.5}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(\|\sqrt{\sigma}\eta_{0},\sqrt{\sigma}v_{0},\sqrt{\sigma}\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5.5},|\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}\eta_{0}|_{6},|\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{5},\sigma^{\frac{5}{2}}|\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{6}).

(A.19)

For
k=4
we follow the same strategy to get

\|q_{3}\|_{2}\leq\|f_{3}\|_{0}+|\sigma\sqrt{g}\Delta_{g_{0}}\partial_{t}^{2}v(0)\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{1.5}+\cdots\lesssim\|f_{3}\|_{0}+|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{1}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{1.5}+|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\overline{\partial})^{2}v_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{1.5}+\cdots

where the term
f_{3}
can be controlled by the aforementioned quantities. The third term is controlled by
|\sigma v_{0}|_{5.5}
. Then the second term is controlled by using a parallel argument as in Subsection
A.2.1
,

\displaystyle\|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{1}\|_{4}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|f_{1}\|_{3}+P(\sigma\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0},q_{0}\|_{5},\sigma|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{5.5},\sigma^{2}|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{6.5}),

(A.20)

\displaystyle\|q_{3}\|_{2}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0},q_{0}\|_{5},\sigma|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{5.5},\sigma^{2}|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{6.5}),

(A.21)

\displaystyle\|\sqrt{\sigma}q_{3}\|_{2.5}\lesssim

\displaystyle~P(\sqrt{\sigma}\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0},q_{0}\|_{5.5},\sigma^{\frac{3}{2}}|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{6},\sigma^{\frac{5}{2}}|v_{0},\eta_{0}|_{7}).

(A.22)

A.2.3
Top-order time derivatives

Finally, we control
\|\partial_{t}^{4}q(0)\|_{1}
. Consider the
\partial_{t}^{4}
-differentiated elliptic equations restricted on
\{t=0\}
. Below
q_{4}
stands for
\partial_{t}^{4}q(0)

-\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{4}=-\partial_{\nu}\left([\mathfrak{B}^{\mu\nu},\partial_{t}^{4}]\partial_{\mu}q\right)-\partial_{t}^{4}\big(\partial_{t}A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}v_{\alpha}-A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}-(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)A^{\nu\alpha}\partial_{\nu}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta_{\alpha}\big)\big|_{t=0}=:f_{4}.

(A.23)

We alternatively consider
\|\langle\overline{\partial}{}\rangle^{-1}\partial_{t}^{4}q(0)\|_{2}\gtrsim\|\partial_{t}^{4}q(0)\|_{1}
. The Dirichlet boundary condition reads

q_{4}=-\sigma P(\overline{\partial}{}\eta)\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\cdot\hat{n}_{0}+\cdots

Applying
\overline{\partial}{}^{-1}
to (
A.23
) and using standard elliptic estimates, we get

\|\langle\overline{\partial}{}\rangle^{-1}q_{4}\|_{2}\lesssim\|\langle\overline{\partial}{}\rangle^{-1}f_{4}\|_{0}+|\sigma P(\overline{\partial}{}\eta)\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{0.5},

(A.24)

where the first term is directly bounded

\|\langle\overline{\partial}{}\rangle^{-1}f_{4}\|_{0}\leq\|f_{4}\|_{0}\leq P\left(\sum_{j=0}^{2}\|\partial_{t}^{j}v(0)\|_{3},\|\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\|_{2},\|\partial_{t}^{4}v(0)\|_{1},\|\mathbf{b}_{0},\eta_{0},q_{0},q_{1},q_{2}\|_{3},\|q_{3}\|_{2}\right).

Invoking
\partial_{t}v=(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)^{2}\eta_{0}-\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}
twice, we have

|\sigma P(\overline{\partial}{}\eta)\overline{\partial}{}^{2}\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{0.5}\lesssim P(|\overline{\partial}{}\eta|_{W^{1,\infty}})\left(|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}+\|\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5}^{4}|\sigma\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5}+|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{4.5}\right),

(A.25)

where the second term and the third term also appear in Subsection
A.2.2
. Applying Lemma
A.1
to the first boundary term and invoking (
A.13
) for
k=3
, we get
|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}|_{2.5}\lesssim\|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\|_{3}+\|\sigma\Delta_{A_{0}}q_{2}\|_{2}=\|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\|_{3}+\|f_{2}\|_{2},
, where the top order terms in
f_{2}
is
\partial\partial_{t}^{2}v(0)
,
\partial\partial_{t}^{2}(\mathbf{b}_{0}\cdot\partial)\eta(0)
and
\partial^{2}\partial_{t}q(0)
which have been controlled before.

For the term
\|\sigma\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\|_{3}
, we do not use the Dirichlet boundary condition to control as in Subsection
A.2.2
because this will further increase the regularity of the mean curvature. Instead, we use the Neumann boundary condition as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
. This still makes sense, as we have already solved
q_{2}
and
\partial_{t}^{3}v(0)
at this point. Following (
3.3
), it suffices to control
\|\sigma\overline{\partial}{}^{3}\nabla_{A_{0}}q_{2}\|_{0}
. Then we take
\overline{\partial}{}^{3}
in (
A.13
) for
k=3
and convert the right-hand side to the divergence form. Since the source term contains up to three time derivatives that have been controlled before, we can use the same method as in Proposition
3.1
to finish the control of the initial data.

A.3
The compatibility conditions

The above analysis yields that the initial data of the
\sigma>0
problem has to satisfy the compatibility conditions up to the fourth order, where the
j
-th order (
0\leq j\leq 4
) compatibility conditions read

\displaystyle q_{j}=\sigma\partial_{t}^{j}\mathcal{H}(0)

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\Gamma(0),

(A.26)

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{3}q_{j}=0

\displaystyle\text{on}\,\,\Gamma_{\text{fix}}.

We conclude that the initial value of the energy functional
E(0):=E_{1}(0)+\sigma E_{2}(0)
depends on the following bounds

\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5},~~\sqrt{\sigma}\|\eta_{0},v_{0},\mathbf{b}_{0}\|_{5.5},~~\sum_{k=0}^{3}\sigma^{1+\frac{k}{2}}\left|\eta_{0},v_{0}\right|_{5.5+\frac{k}{2}},~~\sum_{k=0}^{3}\sigma^{1+\frac{k}{2}}\bigg|\underbrace{\Delta_{g_{0}}\eta_{0}\cdot\hat{n}_{0}}_{=\mathcal{H}_{0}}\bigg|_{4.5+\frac{k}{2}}.

Furthermore, this can be carried over to the
\sigma=0
problem, namely, the first line is replaced by
q_{j}=0
on
\Gamma(0)
. And all
\sigma
-weighted norms vanish when there is no surface tension, and that initial data is exactly for the
\sigma=0
case as in
[
17
]
.

On a tracial version of Haemers bound
Li Gao, Sander Gribling, Yinan Li

Abstract
We extend upper bounds on the quantum independence number and the quantum Shannon capacity of graphs to their counterparts in the commuting operator model. We introduce a von Neumann algebraic generalization of the fractional Haemers bound (over ℂ \mathbb{C} ) and prove that the generalization upper bounds the commuting quantum independence number. We call our bound the tracial Haemers bound, and we prove that it is multiplicative with respect to the strong product. In particular, this makes it an upper bound on the Shannon capacity. The tracial Haemers bound is incomparable with the Lovász theta function, another well-known upper bound on the Shannon capacity. We show that separating the tracial and fractional Haemers bounds would refute Connes’ embedding conjecture. Along the way, we prove that the tracial rank and tracial Haemers bound are elements of the (commuting quantum) asymptotic spectrum of graphs (Zuiddam, Combinatorica, 2019). We also show that the inertia bound (an upper bound on the quantum independence number) upper bounds the commuting quantum independence number.

Independence number from nonlocal games

Two players, Alice and Bob, attempt to convince a referee that a given graph

One may also consider quantum strategies, where Alice and Bob are allowed to share quantum entanglement and perform measurements on their own systems. A perfect quantum strategy enables Alice and Bob to win the independent set game for a graph

Note that different choices of the underlying model of quantum mechanics result in different sets of quantum strategies. In the tensor-product model, the shared entangled state is modeled by a bipartite state in a tensor product Hilbert space

1.3
Commuting quantum independence number: known and our results

The central problem in the theory of nonlocal games is to understand the differences between the two different models of quantum mechanics, the (finite-dimensional) tensor-product model and the commuting-operator model. A recent breakthrough (preprint) of Ji, Natarajan, Vidick, Wright and Yuen shows that these two models are not equivalent, which refutes the celebrated Connes’ embedding conjecture
[
JNV+20
]

Among graph-theoretic nonlocal games, the commuting quantum chromatic number and commuting quantum homomorphism have been extensively studied in the past decade. These games are defined in terms of the existence of perfect commuting quantum strategies for the graph coloring and homomorphism games, respectively
[

,
PSS+16

OP16
,
DP16
]

[

[

]
for lower bounding the quantum chromatic number.

Tracial noncommutative polynomial optimization can be used to unify existing bounds and obtain SDP hierarchies of bounds on the commuting quantum independence number

]

In this paper, we further extend recent results on quantum independence numbers to commuting quantum independence numbers. In particular, we prove that several known bounds on the quantum independence number and quantum Shannon capacity are also upper bounds on the commuting quantum independence number and commuting quantum Shannon capacity.

We first establish a characterization of the commuting quantum Shannon capacity, defined as

Our main contribution is a tracial version of the fractional Haemers bound (over
\mathbb{C}
) that upper bounds the commuting quantum independence number of graphs. We call this new bound the tracial Haemers bound. The bound is motivated by the study of the Shannon capacity (resp. quantum Shannon capacity) of graphs, where the fractional Haemers bound over any field (resp. over

\mathbb{C}

We then prove that the tracial Haemers bound satisfies many properties of the fractional Haemers bound: it is additive, multiplicative, normalized and monotonic with respect to the commuting quantum homomorphism. This shows that the tracial Haemers bound is another element in the commuting quantum asymptotic spectrum of graphs. Thus, we provide a (potentially) better upper bound on the Shannon capacity and its quantum variants. We show the tracial Haemers bound of odd cycles equals their fractional clique cover number. This implies that the tracial Haemers bound and the Lovász theta function are incomparable. To compare the tracial Haemers bound with fractional Haemers bound, we show that the existence of a graph

2.2
A dual characterization for the (quantum) Shannon capacity of graphs

The main motivation to study the (quantum) asymptotic spectrum of graphs comes from its relevance to the study of (quantum versions of) independence numbers and the Shannon capacity of graphs.

\alpha(G)\leq\phi(G)\leq\overline{\chi}(G)\quad\text{ for any graph }G.

In addition, one can lift the sandwich theorem to asymptotic quantities. Let

\Theta(G):=\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes k})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\alpha(G^{\boxtimes k})}

be the
Shannon capacity
of
G

[
Sha56
]
. It is proved by Lovász
[
Lov75
]
(see also
[
Sch03
, Theorem 67.17]
) that

\overline{\chi}_{f}(G)=\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}(G^{\boxtimes k})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}(G^{\boxtimes k})}.

As an application of the definitions, for any
\phi\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{loc})
, we have

\Theta(G)\leq\phi(G)\leq\overline{\chi}_{f}(G).

Surprisingly, Zuiddam
[
Zui19
]
proved a stronger result, using Strassen’s theory of asymptotic spectra (cf.
[
Str88
,
Zui18
]
):

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Zui19
]
)
.

For any graph
G
we have

\Theta(G)=\min\left\{\phi(G):~\phi\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{loc})\right\},~\overline{\chi}_{f}(G)=\max\left\{\phi(G):~\phi\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{loc})\right\}.

A follow-up work
[
LZ21
]
extends the above theorem to the quantum setting. The quantum independence number
\alpha_{q}(G)

[
MR16
]
and the quantum clique cover number
\overline{\chi}_{q}(G)

[
CMN+07
]
are quantum analogues of
\alpha(G)
and
\overline{\chi}(G)
, defined as

\alpha_{q}(G)=\max\{d:~\overline{K}_{d}\leq_{q}G\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\chi}_{q}(G)=\min\{d:~G\leq_{q}\overline{K}_{d}\}.

Then the
quantum Shannon capacity
is defined as

\Theta_{q}(G):=\sup_{k}\sqrt[k]{\alpha_{q}(G^{\boxtimes k})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\alpha_{q}(G^{\boxtimes k})}

and the asymptotic
quantum clique cover number
is defined as

\overline{\chi}_{q,\infty}(G)=\inf_{k}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}_{q}(G^{\boxtimes k})}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}_{q}(G^{\boxtimes k})}.

The following quantum analogue of
Theorem

2.5
holds.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
LZ21
]
)
.

For any graph
G
we have

\Theta_{q}(G)=\min\left\{\phi(G):~\phi\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{q})\right\},~\overline{\chi}_{q,\infty}(G)=\max\left\{\phi(G):~\phi\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{q})\right\}.

We point out that
Theorems

2.5
and
2.6
follow from an abstract theorem for preordered semirings (cf.
[
Zui18
, Theorem 2.13, Corollary 2.14-2.15]
). For our purpose, it is sufficient to collect that
\leq_{loc}
and
\leq_{q}
satisfy the following properties:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
t\in\{loc,q\}
. The preorder
\leq_{t}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

d\leq d^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{N}

\Longleftrightarrow

\overline{K}_{d}\leq_{t}\overline{K}_{d^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{G}
.

(ii)

If
G\leq_{t}H
and
K\leq_{t}L
, then
G\sqcup K\leq_{t}H\sqcup L
and
G\boxtimes K\leq_{t}H\boxtimes L
.

(iii)

For every
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
with
H\neq K_{0}
, there exists a finite
d\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
G\leq_{t}\overline{K_{d}}\boxtimes H
.

Remark 2.8
.

These quantum analogues of
\alpha(G)
and
\overline{\chi}(G)
are defined by allowing (finite-dimensional) quantum strategies in corresponding nonlocal games. Another possible quantization is to consider entanglement-assisted zero-error information transmission, which is also studied in
[
LZ21
]
. A major open problem in zero-error quantum information theory is whether these two quantum notions are identical. In this paper we only focus on the nonlocal game setting.

2.3
Notation and preliminaries about operator algebras

We present necessary preliminary knowledge about operator algebra. See
[
Bla06
,
KR83
]
for a more detailed introduction. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
. We denote the norm on
\mathcal{H}
as
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}}
or simply
\left\|\cdot\right\|
if there is no ambiguity. Let
B(\mathcal{H})
be the space of bounded operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{H}
, equipped with the operator norm
\left\|X\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H})}=\sup\{\left\|X\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}:~\eta\in\mathcal{H},~\left\|\eta\right\|\leq 1\}
. We denote the identity operator in
B(\mathcal{H})
by
I_{\mathcal{H}}
. We omit the subscript if it is clear in the context.
For
X\in B(\mathcal{H})
, we write
\mathrm{ran}(X)=\{X\eta:~\eta\in\mathcal{H}\}
(resp.
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(X))
) denote the range of
X
(resp. the closure of
\mathrm{ran}(X)
) and
\ker(X)=\{\eta\in\mathcal{H}:~X\eta=0\}
denotes the kernel of
X
.
The right support projection
P_{X}
of
X
is the projection onto
\ker(X)^{\perp}
and the left support projection
Q_{X}
of
X
is the projection onto
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(X))
. The right and left support projections satisfy the following relations:

\begin{split}XP_{X}=X~\text{and}~P_{X}Y=0~\text{whenever}~XY=0,\\
Q_{X}X=X~\text{and}~YQ_{X}=0~\text{whenever}~YX=0.\end{split}

(1)

A sequence of operators
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
T
in the operator norm topology if
\left\|T_{n}-T\right\|_{B(\mathcal{H})}\to 0
. We denote this using
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}=T
. A sequence of operators
\{T_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
T
in strong operator topology (in short, SOT) if for any
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, we have
\left\|T_{n}\eta-T\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
. We write
\displaystyle\displaystyle\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}=T
. In general, the strong operator topology is weaker than the operator norm topology in
B(\mathcal{H})
, but they are the same if
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional.

A
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is a norm-closed
*
-subalgebra (closed under adjoint) of
B(\mathcal{H})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. We denote the identity element of
\mathcal{A}
by
I_{\mathcal{A}}
(or
I
if there is no confusion). A
state

\tau
on
\mathcal{A}
is a linear functional
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
that is
positive
, i.e.,
\phi(X^{*}X)\geq 0
for any
X\in\mathcal{A}
, and
unital
, i.e.,
\phi(I_{\mathcal{A}})=1
.
For example, a unit vector
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
with
\left\|\eta\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}=1
gives a state
\tau_{\eta}(X)=\langle\eta,X\eta\rangle
. We call such states vector states. We say a state
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
is tracial if
\tau(XY)=\tau(YX)
for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}
. The normalized trace
\tr:M(d,\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}
is a tracial state on the matrix algebra
M(d,\mathbb{C})
. We specify
\Tr(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}X_{i,i}
as the (unnormalized) trace of matrix
X
.

A von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
is a SOT-closed
*
-subalgebra of
B(\mathcal{H})
for some
\mathcal{H}
.
Since the strong operator topology is weaker than the norm topology in
B(\mathcal{H})
, every von Neumann algebra is a (unital)
C^{*}
-algebra. All
C^{*}
-algebras mentioned in this paper are unital (i.e., contain an identity element) and all mentioned von Neumann algebras are finite (i.e., admit a trace).

In contrast to
C^{*}
-algebras, a von Neumann algebra contains “enough” projections. That is, if
X\in\mathcal{M}
, then both its left support projection
Q_{X}
and right support projection
P_{X}
are also elements of
\mathcal{M}
. Moreover, a self-adjoint element
T=T^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
admits a spectral decomposition in
\mathcal{M}
:

T=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\lambda dE_{\lambda}

where
E_{\lambda}
is the spectral projection of
T
onto the interval
(-\infty,\lambda]
. For a bounded Borel measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, the operator
f(T)
by spectrum theorem is given by

f(T)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(\lambda)dE_{\lambda}\ .

If
T\in B(\mathcal{H})
is normal (i.e.,
TT^{*}=T^{*}T
), its left support projection
Q_{X}
and right support projection
P_{X}
coincide. If furthermore
T\geq 0
, then
T(T+\varepsilon I)^{-1}\to P_{T}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
in the strong operator topology.

Let
P,Q\in B(\mathcal{H})
be two projections. We denote
P\vee Q
as the (union) projection onto the closure of
\mathrm{ran}(P)+\mathrm{ran}(Q)
and
P\wedge Q
as the intersection projection onto
\mathrm{ran}(P)\cap\mathrm{ran}(Q)
. We have by functional calculus that (cf.
[
Bla06
, I.5.2.1]
)

\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}(P+Q)^{\frac{1}{n}}=P\vee Q\ ,\ \text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}(PQP)^{n}=P\wedge Q\ .

If
\mathrm{ran}(P)\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(Q)
, then
PQ=QP=P
and
P\leq Q
in the sense that
\langle\eta,P\eta\rangle\leq\langle\eta,Q\eta\rangle
for every
\eta\in\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, by SOT-closedness, if
P
and
Q
belong to some von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, then
P\wedge Q
and
P\vee Q
also belong to
\mathcal{M}
.

A state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is normal if it is weak
∗
-continuous, equivalently,
for any monotone increasing net of positive operators
\{X_{\alpha}\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
X_{\alpha}\to X
, we have
\tau(X_{\alpha})\to\tau(X)
. Vector states are always normal and normal states preserves the SOT-limit. As an example, for two projections
P,Q\in\mathcal{M}
and a normal state
\displaystyle\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
we have
\tau(P\wedge Q)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau((PQP)^{n})
.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{2})
be two
C^{*}
-algebras. Their direct sum
\mathcal{A}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{A}_{2}
is again a
C^{*}
-algebra and similarly for two von Neumann algebras
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{2})
.
A
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
is a linear map
\pi:\mathcal{A}_{1}\to\mathcal{A}_{2}
which preserves multiplication and the
*
-operation. We say
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are
*
-isomorphic if there is a one-to-one and onto
*
-homomorphism from
\mathcal{A}_{1}
to
\mathcal{A}_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}\odot\mathcal{A}_{2}
denote the the algebraic tensor product of
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
.
The minimal
C^{*}
-algebraic tensor product
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes_{\min}\mathcal{A}_{2}
is the norm closure of
\mathcal{A}_{1}\odot\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
. It does not depend on the choice of embeddings
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Let
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
be states on
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
, respectively. The tensor product state
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}:\mathcal{A}_{1}\odot\mathcal{A}_{2}\to\mathbb{C}
is defined by
(\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2})(X_{1}\otimes X_{2})=\phi_{1}(X_{1})\phi_{2}(X_{2})
for
X_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
X_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{2}
. It extends to a state on
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes_{\min}\mathcal{A}_{2}
(and also to the maximal tensor product
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes_{\max}\mathcal{A}_{2}
, see
[
Bla06
, II.9.3.5]
). This state extension is the only property needed of the
C^{*}
-algebraic tensor product that we use in this paper and we therefore simply write
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{2}
for
\mathcal{A}_{1}\otimes_{\min}\mathcal{A}_{2}
.

For two von Neumann algebras
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{2})
, the von Neumann algebra tensor product is
\mathcal{M}_{1}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{2}
is the closure of
\mathcal{M}_{1}\odot\mathcal{M}_{2}
in the strong operator topology, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{1}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{2}=\overline{(\mathcal{M}_{1}\odot\mathcal{M}_{2})}^{\mathrm{sot}}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})
.
\mathcal{M}_{1}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{2}
is also independent of the choice of embeddings
\mathcal{M}_{1}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{2})
. Given two normal states
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
on
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
respectively, the tensor product state
\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}
is also a normal state on
\mathcal{M}_{1}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{2}
. If there is no risk of confusion, we write
\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}
for
\mathcal{M}_{1}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

2.4
An infinite-dimensional rank-nullity theorem

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
S,T\subseteq\mathcal{H}
be two subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. When
\mathcal{H}
is finite dimensional, we have the following well-known identity

\displaystyle\dim(S)+\dim(T)=\dim(S+T)+\dim(S\cap T).

(2)

To see the above as a statement about ranks of operators, let
P
be the orthogonal projector on
S
, and
Q
the orthogonal projector on
T
. Then the above identity becomes

\mathrm{rank}(P)+\mathrm{rank}(Q)=\mathrm{rank}(P\vee Q)+\mathrm{rank}(P\wedge Q).

Moreover, if
S
and
T
are the row spaces of
A
and
B
in
B(\mathcal{H})
, respectively, we can compute the rank of
AB^{T}
by

\mathrm{rank}(AB^{T})=\dim(S)-\dim(S\cap T^{\perp})=\mathrm{rank}(P)-\mathrm{rank}(P\wedge(I-Q)).

In the infinite-dimensional setting, the dimension of closed subspaces can be interpreted as the trace of their corresponding projections, a similar identity can be obtained as a consequence from Kaplansky’s formula of projections (c.f.
[
KR86
, Theorem 6.1.7]
). This identity (and related results) will be a key tool to our proofs.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
be a von Neumann algebra and let
P,Q\in\mathcal{M}
be two projections.
Suppose
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
is a normal tracial state. Then

(i)

\tau(P)+\tau(Q)=\tau(P\vee Q)+\tau(P\wedge Q)
.

(ii)

Let
Y\in\mathcal{M}
be the projection onto
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(QP))
. Then we have

\tau(Q)\geq\tau(Y)=\tau(P)-\tau(P\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)).

(iii)

If additionally
P\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)=0
, then
\tau(Y)=\tau(P)
. Moreover, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists projection
P_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
P_{\varepsilon}\leq P
,
\tau(P-P_{\varepsilon})\leq\varepsilon
, and
\left\|P_{\varepsilon}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)\right\|<1
.

Proof.

Recall the two projections
P,Q\in\mathcal{M}
are Murray-von Neumann equivalent, denoted by
P\sim Q
, if there exists a partial isometry
V\in\mathcal{M}
such that
V^{*}V=P
and
VV^{*}=Q
. It follows from the property of tracial states that
\tau(P)=\tau(Q)
if
P\sim Q
. Then (i) follows from Kaplansky’s formula of projections which states that for any two projections
P,Q\in\mathcal{M}

P\vee Q-P\sim Q-P\wedge Q\ ,

where the “
-
” operation is interpreted in the projection lattice of equivalence classes under
\sim
(see
[
KR83
, Chapter 2]
).
For (ii), it is clear that
\mathrm{ran}(Y)\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(Q)
, hence
Y\leq Q
and
\tau(Y)\leq\tau(Q)
. For the equality, let
X
denote the projector onto
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(PQ))
. It follows from
[
KR86
, Proposition 6.1.6]
that
X\sim Y
. Moreover,
\mathrm{ran}(X)^{\perp}=\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(PQ))^{\perp}=\ker(QP)=\ker{P}\oplus(\mathrm{ran}(P)\cap\ker(Q))
. Hence

1-X\sim(I_{\mathcal{M}}-P)+P\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)\ ,\quad Y\sim X\sim P-P\wedge(I-Q)\ ,

which proves (ii) by taking trace.

We give a detailed proof for (iii). Note that if
P\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)=0
, then
\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}(P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P)^{n}\to 0
(monotone decreasingly) and
\tau(Y)=\tau(P)-\tau(P\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q))=\tau(P)
.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\tau((P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P)^{n_{0}})\leq\varepsilon/2
. Choose
\delta>0
such that
(1-\delta)^{n_{0}}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Let
P_{\delta}
be the spectral projection of
P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P
corresponding to the interval
(1-\delta,1]
. Thus
P_{\delta}\leq P
and
(1-\delta)P_{\delta}\leq P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P
. This implies

\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\geq\tau((P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P)^{n_{0}})\geq\tau((1-\delta)^{n_{0}}P_{\delta})=(1-\delta)^{n_{0}}\tau(P_{\delta}).

Because
(1-\delta)^{n_{0}}\geq 1/2
, we have

\tau(P_{\delta})\leq\frac{\tau((P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P)^{n_{0}})}{(1-\delta)^{n_{0}}}\leq\varepsilon\ .

Note that
\mathrm{ran}(P_{\delta})\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P)\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(P)
. Then
P_{\delta}\leq P
and we define the projection
P_{\varepsilon}=P-P_{\delta}
. We have
\tau(P-P_{\varepsilon})=\tau(P_{\delta})\leq\varepsilon
and by the definition of
P_{\delta}
,

\left\|P_{\varepsilon}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)\right\|^{2}=\left\|P_{\varepsilon}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P_{\varepsilon}\right\|=\left\|(I_{\mathcal{M}}-P_{\delta})P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-P_{\delta})\right\|\leq 1-\delta,

where the equality uses the fact that
P_{\delta}P=P_{\delta}
and the inequality uses the fact that
I_{\mathcal{M}}-P_{\delta}
is the spectral projection of
P(I_{\mathcal{M}}-Q)P
corresponding to the interval
[0,1-\delta]
.
∎

We remark that the above lemma can be alternatively proved via the representation theorem
[
RS89
, Theorem 1.3]
of the universal
C^{*}
-algebra generated by two projections.
This representation theorem is also used in the study of the tracial rank
[
DP16
]
.

3
C^{*}
-algebraic and commuting quantum Shannon capacity and their asymptotic spectral characterizations

3.1
C^{*}
-algebraic and commuting quantum homomorphisms

We first recall the definitions of graph homomorphism via
C^{*}
-algebras that were considered in
[
OP16
]
. They provide infinite-dimensional analogues of the previously mentioned notions of (quantum) graph homomorphism.

Definition 3.1
.

For graphs
G
and
H
, we say
G\overset{\smash{C^{*}}}{\to}H
if there exists a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
containing projections
E_{g,h}\in\mathcal{A}(G,H)
for every
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
, satisfying the conditions:

(i)

\sum_{h\in V(H)}E_{g,h}=I
for any
g\in V(G)
,

(ii)

E_{g,h}E_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}=0
if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
and (
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{H})
or
h=h^{\prime}\in V(H)
).

Definition 3.2
.

For graphs
G
and
H
, we say
G\overset{\smash{qc}}{\to}H
if there exists a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
equipped with a tracial state
\tau
and containing projections
E_{g,h}\in\mathcal{A}(G,H)
for all
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
, satisfying the conditions:

(i)

\sum_{h\in V(H)}E_{g,h}=I
for any
g\in V(G)
,

(ii)

E_{g,h}E_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}=0
if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
and (
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{H})
or
h=h^{\prime}\in V(H)
).

Note that
Definition

3.1
reduces to
Definition

3.2
if
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
admits a tracial state.
Definitions

3.1
and
3.2
reduce to the usual notion of graph homomorphism if
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
has a
1
-dimensional representation, and they reduce to the quantum homomorphism if
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
has a finite-dimensional representation.
Hence we have the following chain of implications:

G\to H~\Longrightarrow~G\overset{\smash{q}}{\to}H~\Longrightarrow~G\overset{\smash{qc}}{\to}H~\Longrightarrow~G\overset{\smash{C^{*}}}{\to}H~\Longrightarrow~G\overset{\smash{B}}{\to}H\Longleftrightarrow\vartheta(G)\leq\vartheta(H),

(3)

where the right most implication is shown in
[
OP16
]
and the last equivalence follows from
Proposition

2.3
.

These graph homomorphisms permit to define the corresponding
C^{*}
-algebraic and commuting quantum analogues of the independence number, clique cover number and asymptotic spectrum.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
t\in\{qc,C^{*}\}
and
G
and
H
be graphs. Define

•

G\leq_{t}H
if
\overline{G}\overset{\smash{t}}{\to}\overline{H}
;

•

\alpha_{t}(G):=\max\{d:~K_{d}\overset{\smash{t}}{\to}\overline{G}\}=\max\{d:~\overline{K_{d}}\leq_{t}G\}
, and
\displaystyle\Theta_{t}(G):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\alpha_{t}(G^{\boxtimes k})}
;

•

\overline{\chi}_{t}(G):=\min\{d:~\overline{G}\overset{\smash{t}}{\to}K_{d}\}=\min\{d:~G\leq_{t}\overline{K_{d}}\}
, and
\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{t,\infty}(G):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}_{t}(G^{\boxtimes k})}
;

•

\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{t}):=\{\phi\in\Hom(\mathcal{G},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}):~\forall\ G,H\in\mathcal{G},~G\leq_{t}H\Rightarrow\phi(G)\leq\phi(H)\}
.

By Fekete’s lemma
[
Fek23
]
, both
\Theta_{t}(G)
and
\overline{\chi}_{t,\infty}(G)
are well-defined and equal
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt[k]{\alpha_{t}(G^{\boxtimes k})}
and
\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt[k]{\overline{\chi}_{t}(G^{\boxtimes k})}
, respectively.
By
Eq.

It is proved that the fractional Haemers bound and the projective rank are elements of the asymptotic spectrum
\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{q})

[

We define the tracial Haemers bound of

4.1
Definition and examples

We start with recalling a coordinate-free definition of the fractional Haemers bound due to Lex Schrijver
[
BC19
, Remark after Prop.
7
]
. The key part of this definition is the notion of a subspace representation of a graph.

Definition 4.1

(Subspace representation)
.

We say a graph
G
has a
(d,r)
-subspace representation (over
\mathbb{C}
) if there exist subspaces
S_{g}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{d}
for all
g\in V(G)
satisfying

(i)

S_{g}\cap(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})=\{0\}
, where the summation is over all
g^{\prime}
satisfying
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
.

(ii)

\dim(S_{g})=r
for all
g\in V(G)
;

The fractional Haemers bound (over
\mathbb{C}
) is then defined as

\mathcal{H}_{f}(G;\mathbb{C})=\inf\{\frac{d}{r}:~G~\text{has a}~(d,r)\text{-subspace representation over }\mathbb{C}\}.

(7)

In the infinite-dimensional setting, the trace of a projection is an analogue of the dimension of a subspace. This suggests to define the von Neumann-algebraic generalization of a subspace representation using projections. In order to do so, recall that the intersection of two subspaces and (the closure of) the sum of two subspaces corresponds to the wedge and the union of the projections, which can be expressed as strong-operator limits. Indeed, for two projections
E,F
in a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
, the projection onto
\mathrm{ran}(E)\cap\mathrm{ran}(F)
is
E\wedge F=\displaystyle\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}(EFE)^{n}
and the projection onto
\text{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(E)\cup\mathrm{ran}(F))
is
E\vee F=\displaystyle\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}(E+F)^{\frac{1}{n}}
.
In light of
Definition

4.1
(i), it will be convenient to use the shorthand notation

E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}:=\bigvee_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}E_{g^{\prime}}=\displaystyle\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}E_{g^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/n}\text{ for }g\in V(G).

(8)

We then have the following von Neumann-algebraic generalization of a subspace representation.

Definition 4.2

(Tracial subspace representation)
.

We say a graph
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation
if there exist a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
containing projections
E_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
, and a normal tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
,
such that

(i)

E_{g}\wedge E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}=0
.

(ii)

\tau(E_{g})=\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
.

We define the tracial Haemers bound of
G
as the infimum over all
\lambda
for which there exists a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
.

Definition 4.3

(Tracial Haemers bound)
.

The tracial Haemers bound of a graph
G
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G):=

\displaystyle\inf\{\lambda\colon G\text{ has a }\lambda\text{-tracial subspace representation}\}.

It is often convenient to work with a concrete representation
\mathcal{M}\subset B(\mathcal{H})
, and we will also refer to a tuple
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
as a (concrete) tracial subspace representation of
G
. Note that for such a concrete von Neumann algebra, item (i) in
Definition

4.2
can be equivalently formulated as

(i)

S_{g}\cap\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})=\{0\}
, where
S_{g}=\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
for all
g\in V(G)
,
4
4

4

The difference with item (i) of

4.1
is the

of
(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})

which gives exactly correspondence to fractional Haemers bound. The next proposition shows a concrete tracial subspace representation is always achievable by GNS representation.

Proposition 4.4
.

Every
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation
(\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)

yields a
\lambda

Note that the GNS representation

\pi(E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})=\bigvee_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\pi(E_{g^{\prime}})

\tau(P)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau(P_{k})
whenever
P=\displaystyle\text{sot-}\lim_{k\to\infty}P_{k}
. Note that the definition of the tracial rank uses only multiplication of operators and this is well-defined within in
C^{*}
-algebra (and therefore does not require normal states).

b) In the definition of a tracial subspace representation we may allow the equality in item (ii) to be an inequality:
\tau(E_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
. The proof is standard, we have included it in
Appendix

A
for completeness.

We first show that the tracial Haemers bound is a proper infinite dimensional generalization of the fractional Haemers bound. It is clear that if a graph admits a
(d,r)
-subspace representation, then there is also a
d/r
-tracial subspace representation. The next proposition shows the converse also holds.

Proposition 4.6
.

If a graph
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation and the corresponding von Neumann algebra is finite-dimensional, then
G
has a
(d,r)
-subspace representation with
d/r\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
be a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
, where
\mathcal{M}
is finite-dimensional. Thus
\mathcal{M}
is (
*
-isomorphic to) a direct-sum of finite-dimensional matrix algebras:
\mathcal{A}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}M(d_{i},\mathbb{C})
. For each projection
E_{g}\in\mathcal{A}
, it can be uniquely represented as
E_{g}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}E_{g}^{i}
, where
E_{g}^{i}\in M(d_{i},\mathbb{C})
is a projection for each
i\in[\ell]
. For the tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
, there exists a probability distribution
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell})\in[0,1]^{\ell}
such that
\tau(\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}X_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{p_{i}}{d_{i}}\Tr(X_{i})
.
Note that since
\tau(E_{g})=1/\lambda
for every
g\in V(G)
, we have that

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{p_{i}}{d_{i}}\Tr(E_{g}^{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}\,\mathrm{rank}(E_{g}^{i})\text{ for all }g\in V(G),

where we set
q_{i}=p_{i}/d_{i}
for each
i\in[\ell]
.
Let

\begin{split}t^{*}=\max\ t~\text{ s.t. }~&t\in[0,1],\ q\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}_{\geq 0},\ \sum_{i=1}^{\ell}d_{i}q_{i}=1,\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}q_{i}\,\mathrm{rank}(E_{g}^{i})=t\quad\forall\ g\in V(G).\end{split}

(9)

It is clear that
t^{*}\geq 1/\lambda
. Since the coefficients of the constraints in the linear program described in
Eq.

9
are all integers, the optimal value
t^{*}
is attained by a vector
(t^{*},q_{1},\dots,q_{\ell})
of rational numbers. Write
q_{i}=m_{i}/d
for some integers
m_{i}
(
i\in[\ell]
) and
d
. Let
E_{g}^{\prime}=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}E_{g}^{i}\otimes I_{m_{i}}
for each
g\in V(G)
and observe that
E_{g}^{\prime}\in M_{d}
since
\sum_{i}d_{i}m_{i}=\sum_{i}d_{i}q_{i}d=d
. Note that

\mathrm{rank}(E_{g}^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}m_{i}\mathrm{rank}(E_{g}^{i})=dt^{*}.

Thus if we can show
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g}^{\prime})\cap(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g^{\prime}}))=\{0\}
for every
g\in V(G)
, then we have obtained a
(d,dt^{*})
-subspace representation of
G
, where
d/(dt^{*})=1/t^{*}\leq\lambda
.

We are only left to prove that
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g}^{\prime})\cap(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g^{\prime}}))=\{0\}
(the closure can be omitted since these projections are finite dimensional). Note that for each
g\in V(G)
,

\mathrm{ran}(E_{g}^{\prime})=\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathrm{ran}(E_{g}^{i})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m_{i}}\subseteq(\oplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathrm{ran}(E_{g}^{i}))\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m}=\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m},

where
m\geq m_{i}
for all
i\in[\ell]

Since
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\mathrm{ran}(E_{g^{\prime}}))=\{0\}
, we have

\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m}\cap(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g^{\prime}})\otimes\mathbb{C}^{m})=\{0\}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, we show that if we further restrict the von Neumann algebra in a tracial subspace representation to be commutative, we can get a fractional clique cover (or equivalently, a fractional coloring of the complement).

Corollary 4.7
.

If a graph
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation and the corresponding von Neumann algebra is commutative, then
\overline{G}
has a
d:r
-coloring with
d/r\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

Assume that
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
is a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation and
\mathcal{M}
is commutative. Note that the
*
-algebra
\mathcal{M}_{0}
generated by the finite family of mutually commuting projection
\{E_{g}\}
is always finite dimensional, since it is spanned by the monomials of the form
\prod_{g\in V(G)}E_{g}^{\varepsilon_{g}}
, where
\varepsilon_{g}\in\{0,1\}
for each
g\in V(G)
. Thus we may assume
\mathcal{M}
is finite-dimensional otherwise replace
\mathcal{M}
by the subalgebra
\mathcal{M}_{0}
.

By the proof of
Proposition

4.6
, we can assume that
\{\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\}
forms a
d/r\leq\lambda
subspace representation. For commuting operators
E,F\in\mathcal{M}
we have
E\wedge F=EF
. Hence

E_{g}E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}=E_{g}\wedge E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}=0.

Using that
E_{g^{\prime}}\leq E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}
for every
g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)
, this implies
E_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
. This gives a commutative feasible solution of the projective rank, which can be transformed into a
d:r
-coloring of
\overline{G}
using the proof of
[
PSS+16
, Theorem 6.8]
.
∎

We show that the tracial Haemers bound is upper bounded by the tracial rank:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)\leq\overline{\xi}_{{tr}}(G)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
be a feasible solution of
\overline{\xi}_{{tr}}(G)
with value
\lambda
, as given in
Definition

3.6
, where
\tau
is a faithful tracial vector state. Taking the GNS construction
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
of
\tau
,
\tau
induces a tracial vector state
\phi_{\tau}
on the
C^{*}
-algebra
\pi(\mathcal{A})
, which natural extends to a normal tracial state on the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}=\overline{\pi(\mathcal{A})^{\text{sot}}}
. Moreover, since
E_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,
we have
\pi(E_{g})\pi(E_{g^{\prime}})=0,
which implies
\pi(E_{g})\pi(E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})=0
(and thus in particular
\pi(E_{g})\wedge\pi(E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})=0
). This shows that
(\mathcal{H}_{\pi},\mathcal{M},\pi(E_{g}),\phi_{\tau})
is a tracial subspace representation of
G
with value
\lambda
.
∎

As a first example, we compute the tracial Haemers bound of odd cycles. To do so, we follow the same strategy that has been used to show the analogous result for the fractional Haemers bound in
[
BC19
]
.

Example 4.9
.

For all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(C_{2d+1})=d+\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(C_{2d+1})\leq\mathcal{H}_{f}(C_{2d+1})=d+\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, we only need to show the lower bound.
Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2d+1}},\tau)
be a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
C_{2d+1}
. First observe that we have
(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})\vee(E_{0}\wedge E_{2d})\leq E_{0}
and using the graph structure of
C_{2d+1}
we have
E_{1}\wedge E_{2d}=0
since
\{1,2d\}\in E(\overline{C_{2d+1}})
.
Lemma

2.9
(i) then implies that

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\tau(E_{0})\geq\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})+\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{2d}).

(10)

We proceed to lower bound
\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})
and
\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{2d})
. Note that both are of the form
\tau(E_{i}\wedge E_{i+1\bmod 2d+1})
. Using the symmetry of the odd cycle, it thus suffices to lower bound
\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
for which
2\leq k\leq d-1
, let
I_{k}
be the independent set
I_{k}=\{3,5,7,\dots,2k-1\}
. Let
E_{\{0,1\}}=E_{0}\vee E_{1}
be the projection onto
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{ran}(E_{0})+\mathrm{ran}(E_{1}))
and
E_{I_{k}}=\bigvee_{i\in I_{k}}E_{i}
be the projection onto
\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\mathrm{ran}(E_{i}))
. Since the edge
\{0,1\}
is not adjacent to any vertex in
I_{d-1}
, we have
\tau(E_{\{0,1\}}\vee E_{I_{d-1}})\leq 1
. We can expand the left hand side using
Lemma

2.9
(i) (iteratively) to show that

1\geq\tau(E_{\{0,1\}}\vee E_{I_{d-1}})=\tau(E_{\{0,1\}})+\sum_{i\in I_{d-1}}\tau(E_{i})=\tau(E_{0})+\tau(E_{1})-\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})+\sum_{i\in I_{d-1}}\tau(E_{i}).

(11)

Here the first equality uses the fact that for every
2\leq k\leq d-1
we have
\tau(E_{\{0,1\}}\vee E_{I_{k}})=\tau(E_{\{0,1\}}\vee E_{I_{k-1}})+\tau(E_{2k-1})
, since vertex
2k-1
is not adjacent with any vertices in
I_{k-1}\cup\{0,1\}
. Combining
Eq.

11
with the identity
\tau(E_{i})=1/\lambda
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{2d+1}
, shows

\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})\geq\frac{d+1}{\lambda}-1.

We can use this lower bound in
Eq.

10
to obtain

\frac{1}{\lambda}=\tau(E_{0})\geq\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1}+E_{0}\wedge E_{2d})=\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{1})+\tau(E_{0}\wedge E_{2d})\geq\frac{2d+2}{\lambda}-2.

Multiplying both sides by
\lambda
and rearranging gives
\lambda\geq d+\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 4.10
.

The tracial Haemers bound and the Lovász theta function are incomparable.

Proof.

The previous example shows that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(C_{5})=2.5>\sqrt{5}=\vartheta(C_{5})
. On the other hand, let
G
be the complement of the Schläfli graph,
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)\leq\mathcal{H}_{f}(G;\mathbb{C})\leq\mathcal{H}(G;\mathbb{C})\leq 7<9=\vartheta(G)
, where the second to last inequality was proved in
[
Hae79
]
.
∎

In the following subsections, we proceed to establish the monotonicity, additivity, normalization and multiplicativity of the tracial Haemers bound, along with some equivalent definitions. These enable us to conclude that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}\in\mathbf{X}(\mathcal{G},\leq_{qc})
and for any graph
G
,

\alpha_{qc}(G)\leq\Theta_{qc}(G)\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G).

4.2
Monotonicity of the tracial Haemers bound

We first introduce an equivalent formulation of a tracial subspace representation of a graph using two subspaces per vertex of
G
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
with a normal tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, containing projections
E_{g}
and
F_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
, such that

(i)

\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
for any
g\in V(G)
,

(ii)

F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,

(iii)

\tau(F_{g})\geq\tau(E_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
be given as in
Proposition

4.11
. Let
S_{g}=\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
and
T_{g}=\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})
for any
g\in V(G)
. We prove that
S_{g}\cap\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})=\{0\}
, which implies that
(\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathcal{M}},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
is a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation, where
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the set of projections
\{E_{g}\}
. Assume
x\in S_{g}\cap\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})
, we show
x=0
. To see this, note that
E_{g^{\prime}}F_{g}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
. Thus
T_{g}\perp S_{g^{\prime}}
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
and therefore
T_{g}\perp\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})
. This shows that
\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}S_{g^{\prime}})\subseteq T_{g}^{\perp}
and hence
x\in T_{g}^{\perp}
. Also, by assumption
x\in S_{g}
. However
S_{g}\cap T_{g}^{\perp}=\{0\}
, so
x=0
.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
be a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation as given in
Definition

4.2
. For each
g\in V(G)
, let
F_{g}=I_{\mathcal{M}}-(\bigvee_{g\in V(G)}E_{g})
. Then
F_{g}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}
and we have
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
by construction. Moreover we have
F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
since the range of
E_{g^{\prime}}
is orthogonal to the range of
F_{g}
. It remains to show that
\tau(F_{g})\geq\tau(E_{g})
for every
g\in V(G)
.
Since
E_{g}\wedge(I-F_{g})=0
,
Lemma

2.9
(ii) then shows that
\tau(F_{g})\geq\tau(E_{g})
for any
g\in V(G)
.
∎

We are ready to prove that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}
is
\leq_{qc}
-monotone.

Proposition 4.12
.

For graphs
G,H\in\mathcal{G}
, we have that
G\leq_{qc}H
implies
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H}_{H},\mathcal{M}_{H},\{E_{h}\},\{F_{h}\},\tau_{H})
be a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
H
as given in
Proposition

4.11
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{G,H}
be a Hilbert space,
and let
\mathcal{A}(G,H)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{G,H})
be a
C^{*}
-algebra containing projections
\{P_{g,h}:~g\in V(G),h\in V(H)\}
, with tracial state
\tau_{G,H}:\mathcal{A}(G,H)\to\mathbb{C}
, which is a feasible solution of
G\leq_{qc}H
. We first show that we can assume that
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
is also a von Neumann algebra. Indeed, we can take the GNS representation
\pi
of
\mathcal{A}(G,H)
with respect to
\tau_{G,H}
, and the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}(G,H)
generated by
\pi(\mathcal{A}(G,H))
, the image of the GNS construction. Then the tracial state
\tau^{\prime}
naturally extends to
\mathcal{M}(G,H)
. For ease of notation, we also use
\tau_{G,H}
to denote the tracial state on
\mathcal{M}(G,H)
and
\{P_{g,h}:~g\in V(G),h\in V(H)\}
to denote their images in
\mathcal{M}(G,H)
.

We construct a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
. Set
A_{g}=\sum_{h\in V(H)}P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}
and
B_{g}=\sum_{h\in V(H)}P_{g,h}\otimes F_{h}
for all
g\in V(G)
and let
\mathcal{M}_{G}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{A_{g}\}
and
\{B_{g}\}
, which is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}(G,H)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{H}
. Let
\tau_{G}=\tau_{G,H}\otimes\tau_{H}
be the product tracial state on
\mathcal{M}(G,H)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}_{H}
, thus also a tracial state on
\mathcal{M}_{G}
.
We first verify that
A_{g}
and
B_{g}
are projections. Indeed,

A_{g}^{2}=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in V(H)}P_{g,h}P_{g,h^{\prime}}\otimes E_{h}E_{h^{\prime}}=\sum_{h\in V(H)}P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}=A_{g},

where the second equality uses the fact that
P_{g,h}P_{g,h^{\prime}}=0
for all
h\neq h^{\prime}
. The same argument works for
B_{g}
as well. We then lower bound the trace of
A_{g}
and
B_{g}
. Note that

\tau_{G}(A_{g})=\sum_{h\in V(H)}\tau_{G,H}(P_{g,h})\otimes\tau_{H}(E_{h})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{h\in V(H)}\tau_{G,H}(P_{g,h})=\frac{1}{\lambda},

where the last equality holds since
\sum_{h\in V(H)}P_{g,h}=I_{\mathcal{M}(G,H)}
. On the other hand,

\tau_{G}(B_{g})=\sum_{h\in V(H)}\tau_{G,H}(P_{g,h})\otimes\tau_{H}(F_{h})\geq\sum_{h\in V(H)}\tau_{G,H}(P_{g,h})\otimes\tau_{H}(E_{h})=\tau_{G}(A_{g}).

We now verify that
B_{g}A_{g^{\prime}}=0
if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
. Note that

B_{g}A_{g^{\prime}}=\sum_{h,h^{\prime}\in V(H)}P_{g,h}P_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}\otimes F_{h}E_{h^{\prime}}=\sum_{\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{H})}P_{g,h}P_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}\otimes F_{h}E_{h^{\prime}}=0,

where the second equality holds since
P_{g,h}P_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}=0
if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
and
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(H)
or
h=h^{\prime}
and the third equality holds since
F_{h}E_{h^{\prime}}=0
if
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{H})
.

We are left to show that
\mathrm{ran}(A_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(B_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
for every
g\in V(G)
. We first observe that

\mathrm{ran}(A_{g})=\oplus_{h\in V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h})~\text{and}~\mathrm{ran}(B_{g})=\oplus_{h\in V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h}\otimes F_{h}),

since
(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h})(P_{g,h^{\prime}}\otimes E_{h^{\prime}})=P_{g,h}P_{g,h^{\prime}}\otimes E_{h}E_{h^{\prime}}=0
and
(P_{g,h}\otimes F_{h})(P_{g,h^{\prime}}\otimes F_{h^{\prime}})=P_{g,h}P_{g,h^{\prime}}\otimes F_{h}F_{h^{\prime}}=0
. Assume
x\in\mathrm{ran}(A_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(B_{g})^{\perp}
, that is, assume
A_{g}x=x
and
B_{g}x=0
. Note that

A_{g}B_{g}A_{g}=\sum_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\in V(H)}P_{g,h_{1}}P_{g,h_{2}}P_{g,h_{3}}\otimes E_{h_{1}}F_{h_{2}}E_{h_{3}}=\sum_{h\in V(H)}P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}F_{h}E_{h},

where the second equality use the fact that
P_{g,h}
is a projection and
P_{g,h}P_{g,h^{\prime}}=0
for any
h\neq h^{\prime}
.
Thus,

0=\langle x,B_{g}x\rangle=\langle x,A_{g}B_{g}A_{g}x\rangle=\sum_{h\in V(H)}\langle x,(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}F_{h}E_{h})x\rangle.

This implies that
\langle x,(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}F_{h}E_{h})x\rangle=0
for any
h\in V(H)
as
P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}F_{h}E_{h}
is a positive operator. Let
x_{h}=(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h})x\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h})\otimes\mathrm{ran}(E_{h})
. We shall prove that
x_{h}=0
for all
h\in V(H)
, then
x=0
and we are done. For every fixed
h
, write the Schmidt decomposition of
x_{h}
as
\sum_{i}\mu_{i}y_{i}\otimes z_{i}
, where
\mu_{i}>0
,
y_{i}\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h})
and
z_{i}\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{h})
for every
i
. Then the identity
\langle x,(P_{g,h}\otimes E_{h}F_{h}E_{h})x\rangle=0
further implies

0=\langle x_{h},(P_{g,h}\otimes F_{h})x_{h}\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\mu_{i}\langle y_{i},P_{g,h}y_{j}\rangle\langle z_{i},F_{h}z_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i}\mu_{i}\langle z_{i},F_{h}z_{i}\rangle,

where the last equality holds since
P_{g,h}y_{i}=y_{i}
for every
i
and
\langle y_{i},y_{j}\rangle=0
for every
i\neq j
. Thus,
F_{h}z_{i}=0
for every
i
. Note that
\mathrm{ran}(E_{h})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{h})^{\perp}=\{0\}
, which implies
z_{i}=0
for all
i
. This concludes our proof.
∎

4.3
Additivity of the tracial Haemers bound

In this subsection, we show additivity of
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
. The subadditivity follows naturally from the original definition of tracial Haemers bound.

Proposition 4.13
.

For graphs
G
and
H
we have
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G\sqcup H)\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)+\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H}_{G},\mathcal{M}_{G},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau_{G})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{H},\mathcal{M}_{H},\{\tilde{E}_{h}\},\{\tilde{F}_{h}\},\tau_{H})
be
\lambda_{G}
-tracial subspace representation of
G
and
\lambda_{H}
-tracial subspace representation of
H
, respectively, given in terms of
Proposition

4.11
. We construct a
(\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H})
-tracial subspace representation of
G\sqcup H
. For
g\in V(G)
define
A_{g}=(E_{g},0)
and
B_{g}=(F_{g},0)
and for
h\in V(H)
let
A_{h}=(0,\tilde{E}_{h})
and
B_{h}=(0,\tilde{F}_{h})
. Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{G}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{H}
and
\mathcal{M}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{A_{v}:v\in V(G)\cup V(H)\}
and
\{B_{v}:v\in V(G)\cup V(H)\}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{G}\oplus\mathcal{M}_{H}
. Finally, for an element
(X,Y)\in\mathcal{M}
, define
\tau((X,Y))=\frac{\lambda_{G}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{G}(X)+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{H}(Y)
. We verify that
\tau
is a tracial state on
\mathcal{M}
. Note that
\tau((X^{*}X,Y^{*}Y)=\frac{\lambda_{G}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{G}(X^{*}X)+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{H}(Y^{*}Y)\geq 0
,
\tau((I_{\mathcal{M}_{G}},I_{\mathcal{M}_{H}}))=1
and

\begin{split}\tau(X_{1}X_{2},Y_{1}Y_{2})&=\frac{\lambda_{G}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{G}(X_{1}X_{2})+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{H}(Y_{1}Y_{2})\\
&=\frac{\lambda_{G}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{G}(X_{2}X_{1})+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{H}(Y_{2}Y_{1})=\tau(X_{2}X_{1},Y_{2}Y_{1}).\end{split}

We show that
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{A_{v}\}_{v\in V(G)\cup V(H)},\{B_{u}\}_{u\in V(G)\cup V(H)},\tau)
is a
(\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H})
-tracial subspace representation of
G\sqcup H
. First note that for
u\in V(G)
we have
\tau(A_{u})=\frac{\lambda_{G}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{G}(E_{u})+0\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}
and similarly for
v\in V(H)
, we have
\tau(A_{v})\geq 0+\frac{\lambda_{H}}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}\tau_{H}(\tilde{E}_{v})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda_{G}+\lambda_{H}}
. It is also straightforward that
\tau(B_{v})\geq\tau(A_{v})
for every
v\in V(G)\cup V(H)
. We then verify that
B_{v}A_{u}=0
for
\{v,u\}\in E(\overline{G\sqcup H})
. If
v\in V(G)
and
u\in V(H)
,
B_{v}A_{u}=(F_{v},0)(0,\tilde{E}_{u})=0
. A similar argument holds for
v\in V(H)
and
u\in V(G)
. If
v,u\in V(G)
and
\{v,u\}\in E(\overline{G})
,
B_{v}A_{u}=(F_{v},0)(E_{u},0)=(F_{v}E_{u},0)=0
. A similar argument holds for
v,u\in V(H)
and
\{v,u\}\in E(\overline{H})
. Finally we verify that
\mathrm{ran}(A_{u})\cap\mathrm{ran}(B_{u})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. We do so for
u\in V(G)
and the argument for
u\in V(H)
is similar. Note that
\mathrm{ran}(A_{u})=(\mathrm{ran}(E_{u}),0)
and
\mathrm{ran}(B_{u})=(\mathrm{ran}(F_{u}),0)
. Then
\mathrm{ran}(B_{u})^{\perp}=(\mathrm{ran}(F_{u})^{\perp},B(\mathcal{H}_{H}))
. Then
\mathrm{ran}(A_{u})\cap\mathrm{ran}(B_{u})^{\perp}=\{0\}
and
\mathrm{ran}(E_{u})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{u})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

To show superadditivity, we first strengthen the notion of a subspace representation to that of a projection representation by replacing the subspace intersection condition by a norm-inequality condition.

Definition 4.14

(Tracial projection representation)
.

We say a graph
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial projection representation
if a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
containing projections
E_{g}
and
F_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
, with a normal tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
, such that

(i)

\left\|E_{g}(I_{\mathcal{A}}-F_{g})\right\|<1
for any
g\in V(G)
,

(ii)

F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,

(iii)

\tau(F_{g})\geq\tau(E_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
.

As for
Definition

4.2
, the Hilbert
\mathcal{H}
in the above definition is unnecessary but we impose it for the convenience of discussion.
Note that condition (i) above is a strengthening of the projection-intersection condition. Indeed, for two projections
P,Q\in B(\mathcal{H})
, if
\left\|PQ\right\|<1
, then
P\wedge Q=0
. If the underlying Hilbert space is finite dimensional, then the reverse direction also holds. In the infinite-dimensional setting, the reverse direction does not necessarily hold. Nevertheless, we shall prove that, for any graph
G
, the infimum over tracial subspace representations and the infimum over tracial projection representation are the same.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then

\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)=\inf\{\lambda:~G~\text{has a}~\lambda\text{-tracial projection representation}\}.

Proof.

We show equality between
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
and the parameter defined in this proposition. Note that the condition
\|E_{g}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-F_{g})\|<1
implies
E_{g}\wedge(I_{\mathcal{M}}-F_{g})=0
. This shows that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
is at most the parameter defined in this proposition. To prove the converse inequality, consider a tuple
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
of
G
satisfying
Proposition

4.11
. Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
.
Lemma

2.9
(iii) implies that for every
g\in V(G)
, there is a projection
E_{g}^{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{M}
such that
E_{g}^{\varepsilon}\leq E_{g}
,
\left\|E_{g}^{\varepsilon}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-F_{g})\right\|<1
and
\tau(E_{g}^{\varepsilon})\geq\tau(E_{g})-\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}-\varepsilon
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{E_{g}^{\varepsilon}\}
and
\{F_{g}\}
. It follows that
(\mathcal{H},\hat{\mathcal{M}},\{E^{\varepsilon}_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
is a
\frac{\lambda}{1-\varepsilon\lambda}
-tracial projection representation of
G
. Letting
\varepsilon
tend to
0
and taking the infimum over
\lambda
concludes the proof.
∎

With
Proposition

4.15
, we can further restrict the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
in
Proposition

4.11
, which will play a crucial rule in the proof of superadditivity (and the proof of supermultiplicativity).

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
G
be a graph. If there exists a
\lambda
-tracial projection representation of
G
, then there exists a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
in the sense of
Proposition

4.11
, where

\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{ran}(\bigvee_{g\in V(G)}F_{g}).

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
be a
\lambda
-tracial projection representation of
G
and
let
F
be the projection onto
\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g\in V(G)}\mathrm{ran}(F_{g}))
. Let
E^{\prime}_{g}
be the left support projection of
FE_{g}
(i.e. the projection onto the closure of
\mathrm{ran}(FE_{g})
) and set
F^{\prime}_{g}=F_{g}
.
Using the inclusions
\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g}),\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{g})\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(F)
, we observe that
F
commutes with both
F_{g}^{\prime}
and
E_{g}^{\prime}
and that it acts as the identity on the algebra generated by the
E_{g}^{\prime}
’s and
F_{g}^{\prime}
’s.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\mathrm{ran}(F)
and let
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by
\{E^{\prime}_{g}\}
and
\{F^{\prime}_{g}\}
, where
F
is the identity element. Define
\tau^{\prime}:\hat{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathbb{C}
as
\tau^{\prime}(X)=\tau(X)/\tau(F)
for
X\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
. This is a tracial state on
\hat{\mathcal{M}}
. We show that
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\hat{\mathcal{M}},\{E^{\prime}_{g}\},\{F^{\prime}_{g}\},\tau^{\prime})
satisfies
Proposition

4.11
with the same value.

We first verify that
F^{\prime}_{g}E^{\prime}_{g^{\prime}}=0
for every
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
. We have

F_{g}FE_{g^{\prime}}=F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0\quad\text{for any $\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})$}.

Using that
E_{g}^{\prime}
is the left support projection on
FE_{g}
(in particular the property in
Eq.

1
), this indeed implies that
F_{g}^{\prime}E_{g^{\prime}}^{\prime}=F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}^{\prime}=0
.
Next we show
\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Let
v\in\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
, we show
v=0
. By the definition of
E^{\prime}_{g}
, there exists a sequence
\{u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
FE_{g}u_{i}\to v
as
i\to\infty
. On the other hand,
v\in\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
implies that
F_{g}v=0
. Thus,

0=F_{g}v=F_{g}\lim_{i\to\infty}FE_{g}u_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}F_{g}FE_{g}u_{i}=\lim_{i\to\infty}F_{g}E_{g}u_{i}.

In other words,
\|F_{g}E_{g}u_{i}\|\to 0
as
i\to\infty
. We show this implies
\|E_{g}u_{i}\|\to 0
.
Let
\left\|E_{g}(I_{\mathcal{A}}-F_{g})\right\|=\delta_{g}<1
for any
g\in V(G)
. Then by the triangle inequality, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|F_{g}E_{g}u_{i}\right\|\geq\left\|E_{g}u_{i}\right\|-\left\|(I_{\mathcal{A}}-F_{g})E_{g}u_{i}\right\|\geq\left\|E_{g}u_{i}\right\|-\left\|E_{g}(I_{\mathcal{A}}-F_{g})\right\|\left\|E_{g}u_{i}\right\|=(1-\delta_{g})\left\|E_{g}u_{i}\right\|.

Thus,

\displaystyle\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\|E_{g}u_{i}\right\|\leq(1-\delta_{g})^{-1}\lim_{i\to\infty}\left\|F_{g}E_{g}u_{i}\right\|=0,

which implies
v=\lim_{i\to\infty}FE_{g}u_{i}=0
. (The second inequality uses the submultiplicativity of the operator norm.)

Finally we show
\tau^{\prime}(E^{\prime}_{g})=\tau(E^{\prime}_{g})/\tau(F)\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
. Since
\tau(F)\leq 1
, it suffices to show
\tau(E^{\prime}_{g})=\tau(E_{g})
.
Since
\mathrm{ran}(F)^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
, we have
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F)^{\perp}\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. Applying
Lemma

2.9
(ii) with
Q=F
and
P=E_{g}
therefore shows that for any tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
we have
\tau(E_{g}^{\prime})=\tau(E_{g})
.
∎

We are now ready to prove superadditivity of tracial Haemers bound.

Proposition 4.17
.

For graphs
G
and
H
we have
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G\sqcup H)\geq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)+\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
(\hat{\mathcal{H}},\hat{\mathcal{M}},\{\hat{E}_{v}\},\{\hat{F}_{v}\},\hat{\tau})
be a
\lambda
-tracial projection representation of
G\sqcup H
as formulated in
Definition

4.14
.
Proposition

4.16
shows that we can obtain a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{v}\},\{F_{v}\},\tau)
with
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{ran}(F)
, where
F
is the projection onto the closure of
\sum_{v\in V(G)\cup V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(F_{v})
. Using the graph structure of
G\sqcup H
we see that the projections
\{E_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
and
\{F_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
satisfy

(1)

\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
for any
g\in V(G)
,

(2)

F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
.

Similar conditions hold for
\{E_{h}\}_{h\in V(H)}
and
\{F_{h}\}_{h\in V(H)}
. Let
E_{G}
be the projection onto the closure of
\sum_{g\in V(G)}\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
and similarly let
E_{H}
be the projection onto the closure of
\sum_{h\in V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(E_{h})
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{G}=\mathrm{ran}(E_{G})
and
\mathcal{H}_{H}=\mathrm{ran}(E_{H})
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{G}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{H}
) be the von Neumann algebra generated by the projections
\{E_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
with identity element
E_{G}
(resp.
\{E_{h}\}_{h\in V(G)}
with identity
E_{H}
). Thus
\mathcal{M}_{G}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{G})
and
\mathcal{M}_{H}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}_{H})
. Let
\tau_{G}:\mathcal{M}_{G}\to\mathbb{C}
be defined as
\tau_{G}(X)=\tau(X)/\tau(E_{G})
for
X\in\mathcal{M}_{G}
and
\tau_{H}:\mathcal{M}_{H}\to\mathbb{C}
be defined as
\tau_{H}(Y)=\tau(Y)/\tau(E_{H})
for
Y\in\mathcal{M}_{H}
. Since
\mathcal{M}_{G}
and
\mathcal{M}_{H}
are subsets of
\mathcal{M}
,
\tau_{G}
and
\tau_{H}
are tracial states on
\mathcal{M}_{G}
and
\mathcal{M}_{H}
respectively. Analogously to the first part of the proof of
Proposition

4.11
one can show that
(\mathcal{H}_{G},\mathcal{M}_{G},\{E_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)},\tau_{G})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{H},\mathcal{M}_{H},\{E_{h}\}_{h\in V(H)},\tau_{H})
are
(\tau(E_{G})\lambda)
- and
(\tau(E_{H})\lambda)
-tracial subspace representations of
G
and
H
respectively. It follows that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)+\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)\leq\lambda(\tau(E_{G})+\tau(E_{H}))
. If we can show that
\tau(E_{G})+\tau(E_{H})\leq 1
, then we indeed obtain
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G\sqcup H)\geq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)+\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)
.

To show that
\tau(E_{G})+\tau(E_{H})\leq 1
we will apply
Lemma

2.9
to
E_{G}
and
E_{H}
. To do so we are left to prove that
\mathrm{ran}(E_{G})\cap\mathrm{ran}(E_{H})=\{0\}
. Assume there is a nonzero vector
0\neq u\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{G})\cap\mathrm{ran}(E_{H})
. Note that for any
g\in V(G)
,
h\in V(H)
, we have
F_{g}E_{h}=0
and
F_{h}E_{g}=0
, since there are no edges between vertices in
G
and vertices in
H
. This shows that
F_{h}E_{G}=0
for any
h\in V(H)
and
F_{g}E_{H}=0
for any
g\in V(G)
. Since
E_{G}u=u
and
E_{H}u=u
, we have
F_{v}u=F_{v}E_{H}u=0
for every
v\in V(G)
and
F_{v}u=F_{v}E_{G}u=0
for every
v\in V(H)
. Thus,

u\in\bigcap_{g\in V(G)}\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}\cap\bigcap_{h\in V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(F_{h})^{\perp}=\bigcap_{v\in V(G)\cup V(H)}\mathrm{ran}(F_{v})^{\perp}=\mathrm{ran}(F)^{\perp}=\{0\},

where the last equality holds due to the assumption that
\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{ran}(F)
.
∎

By the additivity, we can easily show that the tracial Haemers bound is normalized:

Example 4.18
.

Let
\overline{K_{d}}
be the empty graph with
d
vertices. Then
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\overline{K}_{d})=d
.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\overline{K}_{1})=1
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\overline{K}_{d})=\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\sqcup_{i=1}^{d}\overline{K}_{1})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\overline{K}_{1})=d
.
∎

The normalization of the tracial Haemers bound, together with the monotonicity with respect to the commuting quantum homomorphism, implies that the tracial Haemers bound is an upper bound on the commuting quantum independence number.

Corollary 4.19
.

For every graph
G
, we have
\alpha_{qc}(G)\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
.

Proof.

By definition, if
\alpha_{qc}(G)=d
, then
\overline{K_{d}}\leq_{qc}G
. The monotonicity and normalization of
\mathcal{H}_{tr}
together show that
d=\mathcal{H}_{tr}(\overline{K_{d}})\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
.
∎

4.4
Multiplicativity of the tracial Haemers bound

We now discuss the multiplicativity of
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
. We first use
Proposition

4.11
to prove that the tracial Haemers bound is submultiplicative.

Proposition 4.20
.

For any graphs
G
and
H
,
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G\boxtimes H)\leq\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)\mathcal{H}_{tr}(H)
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{H}_{G},\mathcal{M}_{G},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau_{G})
and
(\mathcal{H}_{H},\mathcal{M}_{H},\{E^{\prime}_{h}\},\{F^{\prime}_{h}\},\tau_{H})
be tracial subspace representations of
G
and
H
, respectively, in the formulation of
Proposition

4.11
. Set
P_{g,h}=E_{g}\otimes E^{\prime}_{h}
and
Q_{g,h}=F_{g}\otimes F^{\prime}_{h}
for any
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
. It is clear that
P_{g,h}
and
Q_{g,h}
are projections. Let
\mathcal{M}_{G,H}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by the projections
\{P_{g,h},Q_{g,h}:~g\in V(G),~h\in V(H)\}
. It follows that
\mathcal{M}_{G,H}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}_{G}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{H}
, the latter of which is equipped with the product tracial state
\tau_{G}\otimes\tau_{H}
. Thus
\tau=\tau_{G}\otimes\tau_{H}
is a tracial state on
\mathcal{M}_{G,H}
, and it is easy to see that
\tau(Q_{g,h})\geq\tau(P_{g,h})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda\mu}
for any
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
.

For
\{(g,h),(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}\in E(\overline{G\boxtimes H})
,
P_{g,h}Q_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}=E_{g}F_{g^{\prime}}\otimes E^{\prime}_{h}F^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}}=0
because
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
or
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{H})
. This verifies the orthogonality conditions between
P_{g,h}
and
Q_{g^{\prime},h^{\prime}}
.

We now show that
\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h})\cap\mathrm{ran}(Q_{g,h})^{\perp}=\{0\}
for every
g\in V(G)
and
h\in V(H)
. Note that
\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h})=\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\otimes\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{h})
and

\mathrm{ran}(Q_{g,h})^{\perp}=(\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})\otimes\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h}))^{\perp}=\left(\mathcal{H}_{G}\otimes\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h})^{\perp}\right)\oplus\left(\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}\otimes\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h})\right).

Thus for
x\in\mathrm{ran}(P_{g,h})\cap\mathrm{ran}(Q_{g,h})^{\perp}
, we have
x=y+z
, where
x\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\otimes\mathrm{ran}(E^{\prime}_{h})
,
y\in\mathcal{H}_{G}\otimes\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h})^{\perp}
and
z\in\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}\otimes\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h})
. For any linear functional
\mathcal{L}:\mathrm{ran}(F^{\prime}_{h})\to\mathbb{C}
, we have
(I_{G}\otimes\mathcal{L})(x)\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
,
(I_{G}\otimes\mathcal{L})(y)=0
and
(I_{G}\otimes\mathcal{L})(z)\in\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
. Thus we have
(I_{G}\otimes\mathcal{L})(x)=(I_{G}\otimes\mathcal{L})(z)\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. This implies
z=0
because
\mathcal{L}
is arbitrary. Similarly, we have
y=0
and hence
x=0
.
∎

To prove the supermultiplicativity of
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)
, we need to relax the conditions in the
\lambda
-tracial subspace (resp. projection) representation by modifying (i) and (iii) in
Proposition

4.11
(resp.
Proposition

4.15
):

Proposition 4.21
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
containing projections
E_{g}
and
F_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
, with a normal tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}
, such that

(i)

F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,

(ii)

\tau(E_{g})-\tau(X_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
, where
X_{g}
is the projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
.

Proof.

We first prove that a tuple
(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\{F_{g}\},\tau)
which satisfies
Proposition

4.21
can be used to construct a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
in terms of
Proposition

4.11
. Let
\tilde{E}_{g}=E_{g}-X_{g}
, for every
g\in V(G)
, where
X_{g}
is the projection onto the subspace
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
, and let
\tilde{F}_{g}=F_{g}
. Note that
\tilde{E}_{g}
is a projection since
\mathrm{ran}(X_{g})\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
be the von Neumann algebra generated by the projections
\tilde{E}_{g}
and
\tilde{F}_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
. Then
\tilde{\tau}=\tau
is a tracial state on
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
and we observe that
\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{E}_{g})=\tau(E_{g})-\tau(X_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
.

It remains to show that (i)
\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{F}_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
and (ii)
\tilde{F}_{g}\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}}=0
if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
. For (i), since
\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g})
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathrm{ran}(X_{g})=\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})^{\perp}
in
\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
, it is straightforward to see that
\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{F}_{g})^{\perp}=\{0\}
. For (ii), since
\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}})\subseteq\mathrm{ran}(E_{g^{\prime}})\perp\mathrm{ran}(F_{g})
for every
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
, we have
\tilde{F}_{g}\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}}=0
as well.
Thus,
(\mathcal{H},\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\{\tilde{E}_{g}\},\{\tilde{F}_{g}\},\tilde{\tau})
is a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
in terms of
Proposition

4.11

4.5
Comparing the fractional and tracial Haemers bounds

A natural question to ask is whether the fractional and tracial Haemers bounds can be strictly separated. Here we show a connection between this question and Connes’ embedding conjecture. Connes’ embedding conjecture (CEC), first proposed by Connes in his famous work
[
Con76
]
, is a fundamental problem in the field of operator algebras (we give a precise formulation below). Over decades CEC has been shown to be equivalent to many important conjectures in various branches of operator algebras, mathematics and computer science. A disproof of CEC is announced in the preprint
[
JNV+20
]
.
In
[
JNV+20
]
the authors take a computational complexity approach based on an equivalent formulation of CEC in terms of non-local games. Given the importance of CEC, finding an alternative proof of its failure is of great interest. We shall show that finding a graph

would provide such an alternative proof.
This is similar to
[

which showed the analogous statement for a separation between projective rank and tracial rank.

\tau(F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}})<\delta\qquad\text{ for all }\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G),

then there exist projections
\{\tilde{E}_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
and
\{\tilde{F}_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
in
\mathcal{M}
satisfying

\tilde{F}_{g}\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}}=0\quad\text{for all }\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G),\quad\text{and }\ \|E_{g}-\tilde{E}_{g}\|_{\tau,2},\,\|F_{g}-\tilde{F}_{g}\|_{\tau,2}\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for every }g\in V(G).

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph and let
H
be the bipartite graph constructed from
G
whose left and right vertex sets are two copies of the vertices of
G
. There is an edge between a vertex
g
in the left copy and a vertex
g^{\prime}
in the right copy if and only if
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
. For a vertex
g\in V(G)
we assign
E_{g}
to the left copy of
g
and
F_{g}
to the right copy of
g
. The corollary then follows from applying
Lemma

4.27
to
H
.
∎

We then show the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.29
.

If
\mathcal{H}_{tr}(G)<\mathcal{H}_{f}(G)
for a graph
G
, then the von Neumann algebra
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
in any
\lambda
-tracial projection representation of
G
for
\lambda<\mathcal{H}_{f}(G)
does not satisfy Connes’ embedding conjecture. In particular, the projections
\{E_{g},F_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
in the
\lambda
-tracial projection representation (as given in
Definition

4.14
) do not admit a matricial microstate.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with a normal tracial state
\tau
. Assume that
\{e_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
and
\{f_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
are projections satisfying the conditions in
Definition

4.14
, i.e.

(i)

\left\|e_{g}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-f_{g})\right\|{}<1
for any
g\in V(G)
.

(ii)

f_{g}e_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,

(iii)

\tau(e_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)
.

We show by contradiction that
\{e_{g}\}\cup\{f_{g},I_{\mathcal{M}}-f_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
cannot have a matricial microstate. By condition (i), we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|(e_{g}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-f_{g})e_{g})^{n}\right\|{}=0
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a finite integer
\ell
such that for any
g\in V(G)
and
n\geq\ell
,

\tau((e_{g}(I_{\mathcal{M}}-f_{g})e_{g})^{n})\leq\varepsilon.

Take the
\delta
corresponding to
\varepsilon/(3\ell)
in
Corollary

4.28
. Suppose

\{e_{g}\}\cup\{f_{g},I_{\mathcal{M}}-f_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}
admits a
(3\ell,\varepsilon_{2})
-matrical microstate for
\varepsilon_{2}:=\min\{\delta,\varepsilon\}
. Then there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and projections
E_{g},F_{g}\subseteq M_{k}
for
g\in V(G)
satisfying

(i)

\tr((E_{g}(I_{k}-F_{g})E_{g})^{\ell})\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon_{2}
for each
g\in V(G)
,

(ii)

\tr(F_{g}E_{g^{\prime}})\leq\varepsilon_{2}
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
,

(iii)

\tr(E_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}-\varepsilon_{2}
for all
g\in V(G)
.

We now use
Corollary

4.28
(on the complement graph
\overline{G}
) to convert (ii) to an orthogonality condition. There exist projections (again of size
k\times k
)
\tilde{E}_{g},\tilde{F}_{g}
for
g\in V(G)
such that
\tilde{F}_{g}\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
and for all
g\in V(G)
we have

\left\|E_{g}-\tilde{E}_{g}\right\|_{\tr,2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3\ell},~\left\|F_{g}-\tilde{F}_{g}\right\|_{\tr,2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3\ell}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\tr((\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{\ell})

\displaystyle\leq\tr((E_{g}(I_{k}-F_{g})E_{g})^{\ell})+\tr(|(E_{g}(I_{k}-F_{g})E_{g})^{\ell}-(\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{\ell}|)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon_{2}+\left\|(E_{g}(I_{k}-F_{g})E_{g})^{\ell}-(\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{\ell}\right\|_{\tr,2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\varepsilon+3\ell\cdot\frac{\varepsilon}{3\ell}=3\varepsilon,

where the second inequality uses
\tr(|A|)\leq\left\|A\right\|_{\tr,2}
for the normalized trace “
\tr
” and the third inequality uses the triangle inequality

3\ell
times and the fact that the norm of products of projections is at most
1
.

This shows that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\times k
projections
\tilde{E}_{g}
,
\tilde{F}_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
satisfying:

•

\tr((\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{\ell})\leq 3\varepsilon
for any
g\in V(G)
,

•

\tilde{F}_{g}\tilde{E}_{g^{\prime}}=0
for any
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(\overline{G})
.

Let
X_{g}=\tilde{E}_{g}\wedge(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})
be the projection onto
\text{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g})\cap\text{ran}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})
. By monotonicity of the sequence

(\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{n}\to X_{g}
, we have

\tr(X_{g})\leq\tr((\tilde{E}_{g}(I_{k}-\tilde{F}_{g})\tilde{E}_{g})^{\ell})\leq 3\varepsilon
. We show that this gives a feasible solution of
\mathcal{H}_{f}(G)
in terms of
Proposition

4.26
. Let
S_{g}=\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{E}_{g})
and
T_{g}=\mathrm{ran}(\tilde{F}_{g})
.
Note that

\dim(S_{g})-\dim(S_{g}\cap T_{g}^{\perp})=k(\tr(\tilde{E}_{g}-X_{g}))\geq k(\tr(E_{g})-\tr(|E_{g}-\tilde{E_{g}}|)-3\varepsilon)\geq k(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\varepsilon_{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{3\ell}-3\varepsilon).

Thus

\mathcal{H}_{f}(G)\leq\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\lambda}-5\varepsilon}=\frac{\lambda}{1-5\lambda\varepsilon}.

Taking
\varepsilon
such that
\frac{\lambda}{1-5\lambda\varepsilon}<\mathcal{H}_{f}(G)
, we reach a contradiction.
∎

5
Inertia upper bound on the commuting quantum independence number

We recall the infinite-dimensional version of the projective packing number.

Definition 5.1

(Tracial packing number)
.

Let
G
be a graph. A
\lambda
-tracial packing of
G
is a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
containing projections
\{E_{g}\}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and equipped with a tracial state
\tau
, such that
E_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
and
\lambda=\sum_{g\in V(G)}\tau(E_{g})
. The tracial packing number of
G
, which we denote by
\alpha_{tr}(G)
, is defined as the supremum of
\lambda
over all possible
\lambda
-tracial packing of
G
.

Note that the value
\alpha_{tr}(G)
in the above definition remains the same if we replace
C^{*}
-algebras by von Neumann algebras and replace tracial states by normal tracial states.
When restricting to finite-dimensional
C^{*}
-algebras
\mathcal{A}
, the above definition gives the projective packing number
\alpha_{p}(G)
, which is an upper bound on
\alpha_{q}(G)

[
Rob13
]
. Similarly, the tracial packing number
\alpha_{tr}(G)
upper bounds
\alpha_{qc}(G)
(cf.
[
GdLL18
, Proposition 9]
).

Definition 5.2

(Inertia bound)
.

Let
A\in M_{s}
be a Hermitian matrix. A subspace
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{s}
is
totally isotropic with respect to
A
if for any vector
v\in S
,
\langle v,Av\rangle=0
.
The inertia bound of
A
, denoted as
n(A)
, is the maximum dimension of totally isotropic subspaces of
A
.

It was proved in
[
WE18
,
WEA19
]
that for any weighted adjacency matrix
A
of
G
, we have
\alpha_{p}(G)\leq n(W)
. We show this is also true for the tracial packing number. We first prove the following characterization of the inertia bound. Note that the original definition can be viewed as taking
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A\in M_{s}
be a Hermitian matrix and let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal tracial state
\tau
. Then

n(A)=\sup_{E}\{\Tr\otimes\tau(E)\ |\ E\in M_{s}\otimes\mathcal{M}\text{ projection s.t.~for any $0\leq P\leq E$, }\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})P)=0\}.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{s}
be a totally isotropic subspace of
A
and let
E_{S}
be the projection onto
S
. Then the direction “
\leq
” follows from the fact that
E_{S}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
is feasible for the right hand side supremum.
For the other direction, recall from
[
WE18
, Lemma 1]
that

n(A)=\Tr(E_{0})+\min\{\Tr(E_{+}),\Tr(E_{-})\}.

where
E_{0}
(resp.
E_{+}
and
E_{-}
) is the projection onto the eigenspace of
A
of zero spectrum (resp. positive spectrum and negative spectrum). Without loss of generality, we assume
\mathrm{rank}(E_{+})=\Tr(E_{+})\geq\Tr(E_{-})=\mathrm{rank}(E_{-})
and the smallest positive eigenvalue of
A
is
\lambda>0
. Then
E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
is the spectral projection of
A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
on
[\lambda,\infty)
.
For any projection
E\in M_{s}\otimes\mathcal{M}
such that

\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})P)=0,\forall\ 0\leq P\leq E,

we have
(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})\wedge E=0
. Indeed, for any nonzero projection
P\leq E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}
and
P\leq E
,

\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})P)\geq\lambda\Tr\otimes\tau(P)>0=\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})P)\ .

which is a contradiction.
Thus by
Lemma

2.9
(i),

\displaystyle\Tr\otimes\tau(E)=

\displaystyle\Tr\otimes\tau(E)+\Tr\otimes\tau(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})-\Tr\otimes\tau(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Tr\otimes\tau(E\vee(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}))+\Tr\otimes\tau(E\wedge(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}}))-\Tr\otimes\tau(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Tr\otimes\tau(I_{s}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})-\Tr\otimes\tau(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})=s-\Tr(E_{+})=\Tr(E_{0})+\Tr(E_{-})=n(A),

where the inequality uses the fact that
E\wedge(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})=0
and
E\vee(E_{+}\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})
is a projection on
M_{s}\otimes\mathcal{M}
.
Taking the supremum over all such
E
completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
A
be a weighted adjacency matrix of
G
. We have
\alpha_{qc}(G)\leq\alpha_{tr}(G)\leq n(A)
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the last inequality. Let
(\mathcal{M},\{E_{g}\},\tau)
be a tracial packing of
G
. Define the projection
E=\sum_{g\in V(G)}e_{g}e_{g}^{*}\otimes E_{g}\in M_{n}\otimes\mathcal{M}
, where
n=|V(G)|
and
\{e_{g}\}
is a set of standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Note that

E(A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})E=\sum_{g,g^{\prime}\in V(G)}\langle e_{g},Ae_{g^{\prime}}\rangle e_{g}e_{g^{\prime}}^{*}\otimes E_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0,

since
\langle e_{g},Ae_{g^{\prime}}\rangle=0
for all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\notin E(G)
and
E_{g}E_{g^{\prime}}=0
for all
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in E(G)
. Then for any
0\leq P\leq E
,

\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})P)=\Tr\otimes\tau((A\otimes I_{\mathcal{M}})EPE)=\Tr\otimes\tau(E(A\otimes I_{\mathcal{\mathcal{M}}})EP)=0.

It follows from
Proposition

5.3
that

\Tr\otimes\tau(E)=\sum_{g\in V(G)}\tau(E_{g})\leq n(A),

which completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Relaxing the equality in
Definition

4.2

We first show a similar result as
Definition

3.6
: in the definition of a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation we may relax the equality in (ii) to an inequality
\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
. The proof strategy is similar to the one for tracial rank in
[
PSS+16
, Theorem 6.11]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. If
\mathcal{M}\subseteq B(\mathcal{H})
is a von Neumann algebra containing projections
E_{g}
for all
g\in V(G)
, with a normal tracial state
\tau:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{C}

E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}=\bigvee_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}E_{g^{\prime}}=\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}E_{g^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/n}\text{ for }g\in V(G),

we have

(i)

\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(E_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})=\{0\}
.

(ii)

\tau(E_{g})\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}
for all
g\in V(G)

then
G
has a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation.

Proof.

Define the scalars
c_{g}=\tau(E_{g})
for
g\in V(G)
. By (ii) we have
c_{g}\geq 1/\lambda
for all
g\in V(G)
. Consider the von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(0,1)
represented on the Hilbert space
L_{2}(0,1)
with tracial state
\tau_{2}(f)=\int_{(0,1)}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
,
where
\mathrm{d}x
is the Lebesgue measure. For a scalar
r\in(0,1)
, let
P_{r}=I_{(0,r)}
be the characteristic function on the open interval
(0,1)
. Since
c_{g}\geq 1/\lambda
for all
g\in V(G)
, we have
(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}\leq 1
. For
g\in V(G)
, define
\widehat{E}_{g}=E_{g}\otimes P_{(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}}
, which is a projection in
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,1)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}\otimes L_{2}(0,1))
. Similarly, let
\widehat{E}_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}
be the projection

\widehat{E}_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)}:=\text{sot-}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\widehat{E}_{g^{\prime}}\Big)^{1/n}\text{ for }g\in V(G).

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(0,1)\subseteq B(\mathcal{H}\otimes L^{2}(0,1))
be the von Neumann algebra generated by the set of projections
\{\widehat{E}_{g}:~g\in V(G)\}
, equipped with the tracial state
\widehat{\tau}=\tau\otimes\tau_{2}
.
Then, for
g\in V(G)
, we have

\widehat{\tau}(\widehat{E}_{g})=\tau(E_{g})\tau_{2}(P_{(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}})=c_{g}\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda c_{g}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}.

We are only left to prove
\mathrm{ran}(\widehat{E}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(\widehat{E}_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})=\{0\}

x\in\mathrm{ran}(\widehat{E}_{g})\cap\mathrm{ran}(\widehat{E}_{N_{\overline{G}}(g)})

for all
g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

x=\widehat{E}_{g}y=(E_{g}\otimes P_{(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}})y

and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\widehat{E}_{g^{\prime}}y_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)})-x\|=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}(E_{g^{\prime}}\otimes P_{(\lambda c_{g^{\prime}})^{-1}})y_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)})-x\|=0.

Note that for any linear functional (dual vector)
\mathcal{L}\in(L^{2}(0,1))^{*}
, we have
(I_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L})(x)\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})
. Moreover, let
z_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)}=(I_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L})((E_{g^{\prime}}\otimes P_{(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}})y_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)})\in\mathcal{H}
for every
g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}z_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)})-(I_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L})(x)\|\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\cdot\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}(E_{g^{\prime}}\otimes P_{(\lambda c_{g})^{-1}})y_{g^{\prime}}^{(N)})-x\|=0

Thus

(I_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\mathcal{L})(x)\in\mathrm{ran}(E_{g})\cap\mathrm{cl}(\sum_{g^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{G}}(g)}\mathrm{ran}(E_{g^{\prime}}))=\{0\},

for any
\mathcal{L}\in L^{2}(0,1)^{*}
,
which implies that
x=0
. Thus we obtain a
\lambda
-tracial subspace representation of
G
.
∎

Equivalent formulation of the subspace representation of a graph

We prove the following equivalent formulation of the subspace representation of a graph (cf.

4.1
).
See
4.26

Proof.

\{S_{g}\}_{g\in V(G)}

(d,r)

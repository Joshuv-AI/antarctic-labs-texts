1.
Introduction

A group
G
is said to be an F-group if for every
x,y\in G\setminus Z(G)
,
C(x)\leq C(y)
implies that
C(x)=C(y)
, where
C(x)
and
C(y)
are centralizers of
x
and
y
respectively.
In 1953, Ito
[
17
]
introduced the notion of the class of finite F-groups and since then the influence of the element centralizers on the structure of groups has been studied extensively. An interesting subclass of finite F-groups is the class of I-groups, consisting of groups in which all centralizers of non-central elements are of same order. Ito in
[
17
]
proved that I-groups are nilpotent and direct product of an abelian group and a group of prime power order. Later on in 2002, Ishikawa
[
15
]
proved that I-groups are of class at most
3
. In 1971, Rebmann
[
22
]
investigated and classified F-groups.

In 1970, Schmidt
[
23
]
introduced the notion of CA-groups (another important subclass of F-groups) consisting of groups in which all centralizers of non-central elements are abelian. He classified the finite CA-groups, whose structure is very much similar to that of finite F-groups. In 1973, the author
[
19
]
studied finite groups in which the centralizer of every non-central element is a maximal subgroup. In 2009, the authors
[
10
]
introduced and characterised CH-groups, consisting of finite groups
G
in which for every
x,y\in G\setminus Z(G)
,
xy=yx
implies that
\mid C(x)\mid=\mid C(y)\mid
. Recently, in 2017 the author
[
9
]
studied finite groups in terms of central intersections of element centralizers. More recently, the present author in
[
6
]
used the influence of centralizers to characterise capable groups of order
p^{2}q
,
p\neq q
are primes (recall that a group is said to be capable if it is the central factor of some group).

Starting with Belcastro and Sherman
[
8
]
in 1994, the characterization of groups in terms of the number of element centralizers (denoted by
\mid\Cent(G)\mid
) have been considered by many researchers (see for example
[
28
,
3
,
6
,
7
,
18
,
30
,
31
]
for finite groups and
[
32
,
29
]
for infinite groups). In this paper, for any
n
-centralizer non-abelian group
G
, we prove that if
n>12
, then
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq 2(n-4)^{{log}_{2}^{(n-4)}}
and if
n\leq 12
, then
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq(n-2)^{2}
. This improves
[
30
, Theorem B]
. Among other results, we prove that if
G
is an
n
-centralizer non-abelian F-group, then gcd
(n-2,\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid)\neq 1
. For a finite group
G
with trivial center and largest prime divisor
q
, we prove that
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq q+2
, with equality iff
G=C_{q}\rtimes C_{n}
is a Frobenius group. For a finite F-group
G
, we prove that
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
iff
G\cong A_{4}
, an extraspecial
2
-group or a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
. It is also proved that if
G
is a finite group with non-trivial center, then
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
, with equality iff
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group. Finally, we conclude the paper with a family of F-groups which are not CA-groups and extend an earlier result.

Throughout this paper,
G
is a group with center
Z(G)
, commutator subgroup
G^{\prime}
and the set of element centralizers
\Cent(G)
. We write
Z(x)
to denote the center of the centralizer
C(x)
and
C_{n}
to denote the cyclic group of order
n
.

2.
The main results

We begin with the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
x,y\in G
. Then
C(x)\subseteq C(y)
iff
Z(y)\subseteq Z(x)
.

Proof.

Suppose
C(x)\subseteq C(y)
. If
a\in Z(y)
, then
a\in C(x)
and consequently,
a\in Z(x)
. Conversely, if
Z(y)\subseteq Z(x)
, then
y\in Z(x)
and so
C(x)\subseteq C(y)
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
x,y\in G
. Then
y\in Z(x)
iff
Z(y)\subseteq Z(x)
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian group such that
\mid C(x)\mid=p
(
p
a prime) for some
x\in G
. Then
C(x)\subsetneq C(y)
if and only if
y=1
.

Proof.

If
C(x)\subsetneq C(y)
, then by Lemma
2.1
,
Z(y)\subsetneq Z(x)
, forcing
y=1
. Converse is trivial.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
x\in G
. Then
\mid\frac{C(x)}{Z(G)}\mid\leq\mid C(xZ(G))\mid\leq\mid C(x)\mid
, where
C(xZ(G))
is the centralizer of
xZ(G)
in
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
12
, Lemma 1]
, noting that
\frac{C(x)}{Z(G)}\leq C(xZ(G))
.
∎

For a group
G
and any
g,x\in G
, it is well known that
g^{-1}C(x)g=C(g^{-1}xg)
. One can easily verify the following analogues result.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
G
be a group and
x\in G
. Then
g^{-1}Z(x)g=Z(g^{-1}xg)
for any
g\in G
.

The following key result gives a characterization of an
n
-centralizer F-group.
Recall that a group
G
is said to be
n
-centralizer if its number of element centralizers
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=n
. A cover for a group
G
is a collection of non-trivial subgroups whose union is
G
itself. A collection
\Pi
of non-trivial subgroups of a group
G
is called a partition if every non-trivial element of
G
belongs to a unique subgroup in
\Pi
. A partition
\Pi
of a group
G
is said to be non-trivial if
\mid\Pi\mid\neq 1
and normal if
g^{-1}Xg\in\Pi
for every
X\in\Pi
and
g\in G
.

Proposition 2.6
.

A non-abelian group
G
is an
n
-centralizer F-group if and only if
\Pi=\{\frac{Z(x_{i})}{Z(G)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
is a non-trivial normal partition of
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
, where
\{Z(x_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
is the set of all centers of the proper centralizers of
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a non-abelian
n
-centralizer F-group. Suppose
x,y\in G\setminus Z(G)
such that
C(x)\neq C(y)
. Let
z\in(Z(x)\cap Z(y))\setminus Z(G)
. Then
C(x),C(y)\subseteq C(z)
and consequently,
C(x)=C(z)=C(y)
, which is a contradiction. Hence
\Pi
is a non-trivial partition of
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
. In the present scenario, in view of Lemma
2.5
,
we have
g^{-1}Z(G)\frac{Z(x_{i})}{Z(G)}gZ(G)=\frac{g^{-1}Z(x_{i})g}{Z(G)}=\frac{Z(g^{-1}x_{i}g)}{Z(G)}
for every
\frac{Z(x_{i})}{Z(G)}\in\Pi
and any
gZ(G)\in\frac{G}{Z(G)}
. Hence
\Pi
is a non-trivial normal partition.

Conversely, suppose
\Pi
is a partition of
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
. Then
Z(x_{i})\nsubseteq Z(x_{j})
for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
i\neq j
. Therefore using Lemma
2.1
,
G
is an F-group.
∎

As an immediate application of the above result, we also have the following characterization of an arbitrary F-group.

Proposition 2.7
.

If
G
is a non-abelian
n
-centralizer F-group, then gcd
(n-2,\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid)\neq 1
.

Proof.

Let
C(x_{1}),C(x_{2}),\dots,C(x_{n-1})
be the proper centralizers of
G
. In view of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2 (Baer)]
,
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is finite by noting that
G=Z(x_{1})\cup\dots\cup Z(x_{n-1})
. Moreover, since
G
is an F-group, using Proposition
2.6
,
\{\frac{Z(x_{i})}{Z(G)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
is a non-trivial normal partition of
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
. Therefore by
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have the result.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian
n
-centralizer nilpotent F-group. Then
p\mid(n-2)
, where
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid=p^{k}
,

a prime.

Proof.

In view of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2 (Baer)]
,
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is finite by noting that

is a

.

Let

be a finite

-centralizer group of conjugate type

. Then

, where

,

In view of Ito

\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is a

The author in

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
G
be a non-abelian
n
-centralizer group.

(a)

If

is an F-group, then
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq(n-2)^{2}

for all

\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid<(n-2)^{2}

(b)

If
G

\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq max\{(n-3)^{2},2(n-4)^{{log}_{2}^{(n-4)}}\}

Proof.

Let
C(x_{1}),C(x_{2}),\dots,C(x_{n-1})
be the proper centralizers of

[
24
, Theorem 1.2 (Baer)]
,
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is finite by noting that

a) Now, if

\{\frac{Z(x_{i})}{Z(G)}\mid 1\leq i\leq n-1\}
is a partition of

[

\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq(n-2)^{2}

[

, Corollary 1]

Every arbitrary

a prime. Moreover, he mentioned that he did not have examples for other values of
n
. It may be mentioned here that any group
G
with
\frac{G}{Z(G)}\cong C_{p}\times C_{p}
can be covered by
p+1
abelian subgroups. Moreover, in view of Proposition
2.16
, we can see that if
G
is a finite
(n+1)
-centralizer group of conjugate type
(p^{2},1)
,
p
a prime and
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid=p^{4}
, then
G
is covered by
n
abelian subgroups (note that in this case
G
is a CA-group) and
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid=(n-1)^{2}
. For example, if
G
is any ultraspecial group of order
p^{6}
,
p
a prime, then
G
is of conjugate type
(p^{2},1)
and
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid=p^{4}
. Therefore from the proof of Proposition
2.16
,
G
is covered by
n(=p^{2}+1)
abelian subgroups (centralizers) and
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid=(n-1)^{2}
.

Recall that a group
G
is semi-simple if
G
has no non-trivial normal abelian subgroup. The author in
[
31
, Proposition 2.5]
proved that if
G
is a semi-simple
n
-centralizer group, then
\mid G\mid\leq(n-1)!
. Using Corollary
2.11
, we can improve this result as follows: [note that max
\{(n-2)^{2},2(n-4)^{{log}_{2}^{(n-4)}}\}<(n-1)!

[
30
]
)].

Corollary 2.19
.

If
G
is an
n
-centralizer non-abelian group, then
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid<(n-1)!
.

We now give the following result concerning bounds of
\mid\Cent(G)\mid
for a finite centerless group. For the bounds of
\mid\Cent(G)\mid
of an arbitrary finite group, see
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
. In the following result
\alpha(I(G))
denotes the number of centralizers of
G
produced by the elements of
I(G)
, where
I(G)=\{x\in G\mid x=x^{-1}\}
.

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
G
be a finite centerless group and
q
be the largest prime divisor of its order. Then

(a)

\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq q+2
, with equality iff
G=C_{q}\rtimes C_{n}
is a Frobenius group.

(b)

\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\mid G\mid-1
, with equality iff
G\cong S_{3}
.

Proof.

a) Suppose
x\in G
is an element of order
q
and let
a\in G\setminus C(x)
. Clearly,
ax^{i}\in G\setminus C(x)
for any
i
. Consider the set
A=\{x,a,ax,ax^{2},\dots,ax^{q-1}\}
. Observe that if
ax^{i}ax^{j}=ax^{j}ax^{i}
for some
0\leq i<j\leq q-1
, then
a\in C(x^{j-i})=C(x)
, a contradiction (noting that
gcd((j-i),o(x))=1
). Therefore
A
is a set of pairwise non-commuting elements of
G
and
\mid A\mid=q+1
. Hence
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq q+2
.

Now, suppose
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=q+2
. Then the proper centralizers of
G
are precisely the centralizers given by the elements of
A
. Let
C(u)
be non-abelian for some
u\in A
. Let
v\in C(u)\setminus Z(u)
. Then
C(v)=C(w)
for some
w\in A
and consequently,
u\in C(v)=C(w)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
G
is a CA-group. Now, if
G
is non-solvable, then by
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
,
G\cong PSL(2,2^{m}),2^{m}>3
. But then using
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\neq q+2
. Therefore
G
is solvable and consequently, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.1.1, Proposition 1.2.4]
,
G
is a Frobenius group with cyclic complement. In the present scenario, the number of Frobenius complements is
q
and consequently, by
[
25
]
, the Frobenius kernel is
C_{q}
. Hence the result follows. Conversely, if
G=C_{q}\rtimes C_{n}
is a Frobenius group, then
G
has trivial center. Moreover, by the property of Frobenius groups,
q
is the largest prime divisor of
\mid G\mid
and
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=q+2
.

b) Clearly,
G
must have an element
x
such that
x\neq x^{-1}
. Therefore
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\mid G\mid-1
by noting that
C(x)=C(x^{-1})
.

Now, if
\mid G\mid
is odd, then
o(y)=p>3
for some
y\in G\setminus Z(G)
and some prime
p
. But then

\mid\Cent(G)\mid<\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+1<\mid G\mid-1
, a contradiction. Hence
\mid G\mid
is even. In the present scenario, it is easy to see that
\mid G\setminus\I(G)\mid=2k
for some
k
and consequently, we have
\mid\I(G)\mid=\mid G\mid-2k
. Since
C(a)=C(a^{-1})
for any
a\in G
, therefore we have

\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\setminus\I(G)\mid}{2}+\mid\I(G)\mid=\mid G\mid-k\leq\mid G\mid-1.

It now follows that
k=1
and hence
\mid\I(G)\mid=\mid G\mid-2=\alpha(\I(G))
. In the present scenario, it is easy to verify that
C(b)=\langle b\rangle
for any
b\in G\setminus Z(G)
. Hence
G\cong S_{3}
. Conversely, we have
\mid\Cent(S_{3})\mid=\mid S_{3}\mid-1
.
∎

Let
q
be the largest prime divisor of the order of a group
G
. The authors in
[
8
, Theorem 6]
proved that
\frac{\mid\Cent(G)\mid}{\mid G\mid}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, if
q=2
and
\frac{\mid\Cent(G)\mid}{\mid G\mid}\leq\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4q}
otherwise. Accordingly, they raised the question whether or not there exists a finite group other than
Q_{8}
and
D_{2p}
(
p
a prime) such that
\frac{\mid\Cent(G)\mid}{\mid G\mid}\geq\frac{1}{2}
. In
[
3
]
, the author answered negatively that question by showing that
\mid\Cent(D_{2n})\mid=n+2
, if
n
is odd (
D_{2n}
denotes the dihedral group of order
2n
). In this connection, we prove the following result which gives a new property of extraspecial
2
-groups. This also gives yet another two families of counterexamples to the above question.

Recall that a finite group
G
is extraspecial if
G
is a special
p
-group and
\mid G^{\prime}\mid=\mid Z(G)\mid=p
. It is well known that every extraspecial
p
-group has order
p^{2a+1}
for some positive integer
a
. Moreover, for each prime
p
and for every positive integer
a
, there exists, upto isomorphism, exactly two extraspecial groups of order
p^{2a+1}
.

Proposition 2.21
.

If
G
is a finite group with non-trivial center, then
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
, with equality iff
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group.

Proof.

Since
C(x)=C(xz)
for any
x\in G
and any
z\in Z(G)
, therefore
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
.

Now, suppose
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
. Then we have
\mid Z(G)\mid=2
by noting that
C(x)=C(xz)
for any
x\in G
and any
z\in Z(G)
. In the present scenario, using
[
7
, Proposition 3.1]
, we have
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is an elementary abelian
2
-group. Therefore
G^{\prime}=Z(G)
and consequently,
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group. Conversely, if
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group, then by
[
7
, Proposition 3.13]
,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
.
∎

It may be mentioned here that the alternating group
A_{4}
of degree
4
has trivial center and
\mid\Cent(A_{4})\mid=\frac{\mid A_{4}\mid}{2}
. In the present scenario, the following problem is worth mentioning:

Problem 2.1
.

Classify the finite groups
G
with
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
.

We now classify finite F-groups with
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
.

Theorem 2.22
.

G
is a finite F-group with
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
iff
G\cong A_{4}
, an extraspecial
2
-group or a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a finite F-group. Suppose
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
. Now, if
\mid Z(G)\mid>1
, then by Proposition
2.21
,
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group. On the other hand if
\mid Z(G)\mid=1
, then using classification of groups of rank 1 (see
[
27
, p.578]
),
G
is a CA-group. Now, if
G
is non-solvable, then by
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
,
G\cong PSL(2,2^{m}),2^{m}>3
and using
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid<\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
G
is solvable and consequently, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.1.1, Proposition 1.2.4]
,
G\cong K\rtimes H
is a Frobenius group with abelian kernel
K
and cyclic complement
H
. Now, using properties of Frobenius groups we have
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\mid K\mid+2=\frac{\mid K\mid\mid H\mid}{2}
. It now follows that
\mid H\mid=3
and
\mid K\mid=4
. Therefore
\mid G\mid=12
and hence
G\cong A_{4}
.

Next, suppose
\mid\Cent(G)\mid>\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
. We have
C(x)=C(xz)
for any
x\in G
and any
z\in Z(G)
and consequently,
\mid Z(G)\mid=1
. Now, using classification of groups of rank 1 (see
[
27
, p.578]
),
G
is a CA-group. Suppose
G
is non-solvable. Then by
[
1
, Lemma 3.9]
,
G\cong PSL(2,2^{m}),2^{m}>3
and using
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid<\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
G
is solvable and consequently, by
[
26
, Proposition 3.1.1, Proposition 1.2.4]
,
G\cong K\rtimes H
is a Frobenius group with abelian kernel
K
and cyclic complement
H
. In the present scenario, using the properties of Frobenius groups, we have
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\mid K\mid+2
. Now, suppose
\mid H\mid\geq 3
.
Then
\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}=\frac{\mid K\mid\mid H\mid}{2}\geq\mid K\mid+\frac{\mid K\mid}{2}>\mid K\mid+2
, which is a contradiction by noting that we must have
\mid K\mid>4
in this situation. Therefore we have
H\cong C_{2}
.

Conversely, if
G\cong A_{4}
, an extraspecial
2
-group or a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
, then
G
is an F-group (see classification of groups of rank 1
[
27
, p.578]
. Clearly,
\mid\Cent(A_{4})\mid=6
. Moreover, if
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group, then by Proposition
2.21
,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
. On the other hand, if
G
is a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
, then from the properties of Frobenius groups, we have
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+2
.
∎

Corollary 2.23
.

G
is a finite CA-group with
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\geq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}
iff
G\cong A_{4},Q_{8},D_{8}
or a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
.

Proof.

The result follows from Theorem
2.22
by noting that if
G
is an extraspecial
2
-group of order
>8
, then
G
is not a CA-group (see classification of groups of rank 1
[
27
, p.578]
).
∎

In view of Theorem
2.10
and Theorem
2.22
, we now have the following result:

Proposition 2.24
.

If
G
is a finite
n
-centralizer non-abelian F-group, then
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid\leq(n-2)^{2}\leq\frac{{\mid G\mid}^{2}}{4}
.

There exists finite groups, for example, say
G=S_{3}\times S_{3}
such that
\mid\Cent(G)\mid>\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+2
. However, it follows from Theorem
2.22
that for any finite F-group
G
, we have
\mid\Cent(G)\mid\leq\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+2
, with equality iff
G
is a Frobenius group with abelian kernel and complement of order
2
. It may be mentioned here that
\mid\Cent(S_{4})\mid=\frac{\mid S_{4}\mid}{2}+2
but
S_{4}
is not an F-group. In this connection it is natural to study the following problem:

Problem 2.2
.

Classify the finite groups
G
with
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\frac{\mid G\mid}{2}+2
.

Let
H
be any subgroup of a group
G
. It is easy to see that
\mid\Cent(H)\mid\leq\mid\Cent(G)\mid
. The authors in
[
18
]
studied some conditions for the equality and among other results they showed that (
[
18
, Proposition 3.6]
) if
G
is a finite group such that
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is isomorphic to a simple group, then
G
and
G^{\prime}
are isoclinic groups and consequently,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\mid\Cent(G^{\prime})\mid
(for basic notions of isoclinism see
[
20
,
32
]
). Here we give a generalization of this result (a group
G
is called perfect if
G=G^{\prime}
):

Proposition 2.25
.

Let
G
be an
n
-centralizer non-abelian group. Then
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is perfect if and only if
G
is isoclinic with
G^{\prime}
. In particular,
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\mid\Cent(G^{\prime})\mid
.

Proof.

In view of
[
24
, Theorem 1.2 (Baer)]
,
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is finite by noting that
G=Z(x_{1})\cup\dots\cup Z(x_{n-1})
. Suppose
\frac{G}{Z(G)}
is perfect. Then
\frac{G}{Z(G)}=(\frac{G}{Z(G)})^{\prime}=\frac{G^{\prime}Z(G)}{Z(G)}
and consequently,
G^{\prime}Z(G)=G
. Therefore in view of
[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
,
G
is isoclinic to
G^{\prime}
. Conversely, if if
G
is isoclinic with
G^{\prime}
, then using
[
20
, Lemma 2.7]
we have
G^{\prime}Z(G)=G
, which implies
\frac{G}{Z(G)}=(\frac{G}{Z(G)})^{\prime}
. Moreover, by
[
32
, Lemma 3.2]
we have
\mid\Cent(G)\mid=\mid\Cent(G^{\prime})\mid
.
∎

Finally, we conclude the paper with a family of groups which are F-groups but not CA-groups. In this connection, we want to point out that the author in
[
9
, Proposition 1.3]
proved that an extraspecial
p
-group (
p
a prime) of order
>p^{3}
is an F-group which is not a CA-group. However, this is already a known result which was initially obtained by Zappa (see
[
27
, pp. 578]
). The following proposition extends this result and gives a large family of F-groups which are not CA-groups. Note that every extraspecial
p
-group is of conjugate type
(p,1)
.

Proposition 2.26
.

Let
G
be a finite group of conjugate type
(p^{k},1)
for some prime
p
. If
\mid\frac{G}{Z(G)}\mid>p^{2k}
, then
C(x)
is non-abelian for any
x\in G\setminus Z(G)
.

Proof.

Following Ito
[
17
]
, without any loss we may assume that
G
is a
p
group. Moreover, in view of
[
21
, Proposition 1]
, we have
\mid\frac{Z(x)}{Z(G)}\mid\leq p^{k}
for any

x\in G\setminus Z(G)
. Hence the result follows.

∎

Some Fixed-Circle Results in C ∗ − C^{\ast}- Algebra Valued Metric Spaces
Nilay DEĞİRMEN

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider fixed-circle problem in C ∗ − C^{\ast}- algebra valued metric spaces and prove some fixed-circle theorems for self-mappings by defining the notion of fixed-circle on such spaces with geometric interpretation. Furthermore, we give some illustrative examples to substantiate the importance of our newly obtained results.

In the most existing literature, it has been observed that fixed circles
have not been defined in

algebra valued metric spaces. This
motivates us to analyze solutions of the fixed-circle problem in such spaces
with geometric interpretation. So, our purpose in this study is to define

and obtain some fixed-circle theorems for self-mappings on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces. Also, we construct some nontrivial
illustrative examples of mappings which have or not fixed circles. Since the

having huge applications to theoretical physics and noncommutative geometry,

We start with the definition of a
C^{\ast}-
algebra and its some
properties used in this research.

Definition 1
.

[
17
]
A mapping
x\rightarrow x^{\ast}
of a complex algebra
\mathbb{A}
into
\mathbb{A}
is called an involution on
\mathbb{A}
if the following
properties hold for all
x,y\in\mathbb{A}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:

(i)
\left(x^{\ast}\right)^{\ast}=x,

(ii)
\left(xy\right)^{\ast}=y^{\ast}x^{\ast},

(iii)
\left(\lambda x+y\right)^{\ast}=\overline{\lambda}x^{\ast}+y^{\ast}.

A complex Banach algebra
A
with an involution such that
for every
x
in
A

\left\|x^{\ast}x\right\|=\left\|x\right\|^{2}

is called a
C^{\ast}-
algebra.

In the rest of paper,
\mathbb{A}
will denote a unital
C^{\ast}-
algebra
with a unit
I.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{h}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{A}:x=x^{\ast}\right\}.
An
element
x\in\mathbb{A}
is called positive if
x\in\mathbb{A}_{h}
and
\sigma\left(x\right)\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
where
\sigma\left(x\right)=\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:x-\lambda I\text{ is non-invertible}\right\},
the spectrum of
x
in
\mathbb{A}
. If
x\in\mathbb{A}
is positive, we write it as
\theta\preceq x,
where
\theta
is the zero element in
\mathbb{A}.
We denote
the set of all positive elements of
\mathbb{A}
by
\mathbb{A}_{+}.
Also,
\mathbb{A}_{h}
becomes a partially ordered set by defining
x\preceq y
to
mean
y-x\in\mathbb{A}_{+}

[
17
]
.

The following statements about involution on
\mathbb{A}
, positive elements
of
\mathbb{A}
and partial order
\preceq
on
\mathbb{A}_{h}
are true:

(i)
\left\|x^{\ast}\right\|=\left\|x\right\|
for all
x\in\mathbb{A}
.

(ii) If
x\in\mathbb{A}
is invertible, then
x^{\ast}
is invertible and
\left(x^{\ast}\right)^{-1}=\left(x^{-1}\right)^{\ast}.

(iii) If
x,y,z\in\mathbb{A}_{h}
, then
x\preceq y
implies
x+z\preceq y+z.

(iv) If
x,y\in\mathbb{A}_{+}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\left\{0\right\},
then
\alpha x+\beta y\in

\mathbb{A}_{+}.

(v)
\mathbb{A}_{+}=\left\{x^{\ast}x:x\in\mathbb{A}\right\}
.

(vi) If
x,y\in\mathbb{A}_{h}
and
z\in\mathbb{A}
, then
x\preceq y

implies
z^{\ast}xz\preceq z^{\ast}yz.

(vii) If
\theta\preceq x\preceq y,
then
\left\|x\right\|\leq\left\|y\right\|

[
17
]
.

Using the concept of a positive element in a
C^{\ast}-
algebra, in 2014,
Ma and Jiang
[
2
]
introduced the notion of a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued
metric space in the following way:

Definition 2
.

[
2
]
Let
X
be a nonempty set. Suppose the mapping
d:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
satisfies:

(i)
\theta\preceq d\left(x,y\right)
for all
x,y\in X
and
d\left(x,y\right)=\theta\Leftrightarrow x=y;

(ii)
d\left(x,y\right)=d\left(y,x\right)
for all
x,y\in X;

(iii)
d\left(x,y\right)\preceq d\left(x,z\right)+d\left(z,y\right)
for all
x,y,z\in X.

Then,
d
is called
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued
metric on
X
and
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
is
called a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space.

It is clear that such spaces generalize the concept of metric spaces. The
main idea consists in using the set of all positive elements of a unital
C^{\ast}-
algebra instead of the set of real numbers.

Example 1
.

[
2
]
Let
E
be a Lebesgue measurable set and
L\left(L^{2}\left(E\right)\right)
denote the set of bounded linear operators on Hilbert
space
L^{2}\left(E\right).
Define
d:L^{\infty}\left(E\right)\times L^{\infty}\left(E\right)\rightarrow L\left(L^{2}\left(E\right)\right)

by

d\left(f,g\right)=\pi_{\left|f-g\right|}

for all
f,g\in L^{\infty}\left(E\right)
, where
\pi_{h}:L^{2}\left(E\right)\rightarrow L^{2}\left(E\right)
is the multiplication operator
defined by

\pi_{h}\left(\varphi\right)=h\cdot\varphi

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}\left(E\right).
Then,
d
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric and
\left(L^{\infty}\left(E\right),L\left(L^{2}\left(E\right)\right),d\right)
is a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra
valued metric space.

Example 2
.

[
2
]
Define
d:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
by

d\left(x,y\right)=diag\left(\left|x-y\right|,\alpha\left|x-y\right|\right)

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\alpha\geq 0
is a constant, the norm on
M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is defined by

\left\|\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right]\right\|=\max\left\{\left|a\right|,\left|b\right|,\left|c\right|,\left|d\right|\right\},

and partial ordering on
M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
is given by

\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{1}&b_{1}\\
c_{1}&d_{1}\end{array}\right]\preceq\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{2}&b_{2}\\
c_{2}&d_{2}\end{array}\right]\Longleftrightarrow a_{1}\leq a_{2},\text{ }b_{1}\leq b_{2},\text{ }c_{1}\leq c_{2}\text{ and }d_{1}\leq d_{2}.

Then,
d
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric and
\left(\mathbb{R},M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right),d\right)
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space.

Example 3
.

[
3
]
Let
X=\left[-1,1\right]\times\left[-1,1\right].
Define
d:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
by

d\left(x,y\right)=\left(\left|x_{1}-y_{1}\right|,\left|x_{2}-y_{2}\right|\right)

for all
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right),y=\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\in X,

where the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined by

\left\|\left(a,b\right)\right\|=\max\left\{\left|a\right|,\left|b\right|\right\},

and partial ordering on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is given by

\left(a,b\right)\preceq\left(c,d\right)\Longleftrightarrow a\leq c\text{
and }b\leq d.

Then,
d
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric and
\left(X,\mathbb{R}^{2},d\right)
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space.

Example 4
.

[
6
]
Define
d:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
by

d\left(x,y\right)=\left\{\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right],\text{ \ }x\neq y\\
0,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }x=y\end{array}\right]\right.

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
d
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric and
\left(\mathbb{R},M_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}\right),d\right)
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space.

Based on the idea of the Banach contraction principle
[
1
]
in classical
metric spaces, Ma and Jiang
[
2
]
established the following main theorems
which implies the existence and uniqueness of fixed point on complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces.

Theorem 1
.

[
2
]
If
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
is a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
is a
C^{\ast}-
algebra
valued contractive mapping on
X
, that is, there exists an
A\in\mathbb{A}

with
\left\|A\right\|<1
such that

(1.1)

d\left(Tx,Ty\right)\preceq A^{\ast}d\left(x,y\right)A

for all
x,y\in X,
then, there exists a unique fixed point in
X.

In the following theorem and Theorem 12, we denote the set
\left\{a\in\mathbb{A}:ab=ba\text{ for all }b\in\mathbb{A}\right\}
by
\mathbb{A}^{\prime}.

Theorem 2
.

[
2
]
Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space. Suppose the mapping
T:X\rightarrow X

satisfies for all
x,y\in X
such that

(1.2)

d\left(Tx,Ty\right)\preceq A\left(d\left(Tx,y\right)+d\left(Ty,x\right)\right)

where
A\in\mathbb{A}_{+}^{\prime}
and
\left\|A\right\|<\frac{1}{2}.
Then, there exists a unique fixed point in
X.

Inspired by preceding observation, many authors stated new types of
contractive mappings and studied fixed point theorems. Shehwar et al.
[
4
]
gave the extension of Caristi’s fixed point theorem
[
18
,
19
]
for
self-mappings defined on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces, which
guarantees the existence of fixed point as follows:

Theorem 3
.

[
4
]
Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a complete
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space,
\phi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{+}
be a lower
semi continuous map and
T:X\rightarrow X
be such that

(1.3)

d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\phi\left(x\right)-\phi\left(Tx\right)

for all
x\in X.
Then,
T
has at least one fixed point in
X.

Kadelburg and Radenovic
[
5
]
introduced the extension of Ćirić’s
fixed point theorem
[
20
]
for self-mappings defined on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces, which guarantees the existence and uniqueness
of fixed point as follows:

Theorem 4
.

[
5
]
Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued
metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
be a mapping on
X
. Suppose that there
exists an
A\in\mathbb{A}
with
\left\|A\right\|<1
such that for
all
x,y\in X
there exists
u\left(x,y\right)\in\left\{d\left(x,y\right),d\left(x,Tx\right),d\left(y,Ty\right),d\left(x,Ty\right),d\left(y,Tx\right)\right\}
such that

(1.4)

d\left(Tx,Ty\right)\preceq A^{\ast}u\left(x,y\right)A.

Then,
T
has a unique fixed point in
X.

2.
Main Results

We begin this section by introducing the concept of a circle on a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space.

Definition 3
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
x_{0}\in X
and
r\in\mathbb{A}_{+}.
Then, the circle with the
centered
x_{0}
and the radius
r
is defined by

C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}=\left\{x\in X:d\left(x,x_{0}\right)=r\right\}.

Example 5
.

Consider the
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space
\left(L^{\infty}\left(E\right),L\left(L^{2}\left(E\right)\right),d\right)
given in
Example 1 for
E=\left[0,1\right]
. Choose the center
x_{0}
as the
function
f
defined by

f:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},~~f\left(x\right)=\chi_{\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]}\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,\text{ \ }x\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]\\
0,\text{ \ }x\notin\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]\end{array}\right.

and the radius
r
as the multiplication operator
\pi_{h}
defined by

\pi_{h}:L^{2}\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow L^{2}\left[0,1\right],\text{ \ }\pi_{h}\left(\varphi\right)=h\cdot\varphi

for the function
h:\left[0,1\right]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},

h\left(x\right)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}1,\text{ \ \ }x\in\left(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}\right)\cap\left[0,1\right]\\
\infty,\text{ \ \ }x\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\left[0,1\right]\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\end{array}\right.
. Then, it can be easily seen that
f,h\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]
and
\pi_{h}\in L\left(L^{2}\left[0,1\right]\right).
Thus,
we get

\displaystyle C_{f,\pi_{h}}^{C^{\ast}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:d\left(g,f\right)=\pi_{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\pi_{\left|g-f\right|}=\pi_{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\left|g-f\right|=h\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\left|g\left(x\right)-f\left(x\right)\right|=h\left(x\right)\text{ for each }x\in\left[0,1\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\left|g\left(x\right)-0\right|=1\text{ for each }x\in\left(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}\right)\cap\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\left|g\left(x\right)-0\right|=\infty\text{ for each }x\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:\left|g\left(x\right)-1\right|=\infty\text{ for each }x\in\mathbb{Q}\cap\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{g\in L^{\infty}\left[0,1\right]:g\left(x\right)=-1\text{ or }g\left(x\right)=1\text{ for each }x\in\left(\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}\right)\cap\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right)\right\}

g

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\left|e^{x}-e\right|&0\\
0&2\left|e^{x}-e\right|\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{e}{2}&0\\
0&e\end{array}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}:\left|e^{x}-e\right|=\frac{e}{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{1+\ln 3-\ln 2,1-\ln 2\right\}.

2.1.
The existence of fixed circles

In this part, we introduce the notion of a fixed circle on a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space. Then, we prove some fixed-circle theorems that
guarantee the existence of fixed-circles for a self-mapping satisfying some
conditions on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces.

Now, we define the new concept as follows:

Definition 4
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be a circle on
X
and
T:X\rightarrow X

be a self-mapping. If
Tx=x
for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}},
then
the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is called as the fixed circle of
T.

The first solution of fixed-circle problem in
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued
metric spaces is given using the inequality (1.3) in Theorem 3 which is an
extension of Caristi’s fixed point theorem for mappings defined on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces as follows:

Theorem 5
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be any circle on
X.
Define the mapping
\varphi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{+}
as

(2.10)

\varphi\left(x\right)=d\left(x,x_{0}\right)

for all
x\in X.
If
T
is a self-mapping defined on
X
satisfying the
conditions

(2.11)

d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

and

(2.12)

r\preceq d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}},
then the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a fixed circle of
T.

Proof.

Let
x
be any point in the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
Then, using
the (2.2), (2.1), (2.3) and the definition of the relation
\preceq,
we
get

\displaystyle d\left(x,Tx\right)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(x,x_{0}\right)-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle r-r=0

and so
d\left(x,Tx\right)=0
which means that
Tx=x.
Then, we obtain
that
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a fixed circle of
T.

Remark 1
.

Theorem 5 guarantees the existence of at least one fixed circle of a
self-mapping on a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space, while Theorem 3
guarantees the existence of at least one fixed point of a self-mapping on a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space. We note that if the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
has only one element, Theorem 5 is a special case
of Theorem 3.

Remark 2
.

The inequality (2.2) says that
Tx
is not in the exterior of the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
for each
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
In the
same way, the inequality (2.3) says that
Tx
is not in the interior of the
circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
for each
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
It
follows that
T\left(C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}\right)\subset C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
under the conditions (2.2) and (2.3).

C^{\ast}-

Theorem 6
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be any circle on
X
and the mapping
\varphi
be as in (2.1). If
T
is a self-mapping defined on
X
satisfying
the conditions

(2.13)

d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\varphi\left(x\right)+\varphi\left(Tx\right)-2r

and

(2.14)

d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)\preceq r

for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}},
then the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a fixed circle of
T.

Proof.

Let
x
be any point in the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
Then, using
the (2.4), (2.1), (2.5) and the definition of the relation
\preceq,
we
get

\displaystyle d\left(x,Tx\right)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x\right)+\varphi\left(Tx\right)-2r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(x,x_{0}\right)+d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)-2r

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)-r

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle r-r=0

and so
d\left(x,Tx\right)=0
which implies that
Tx=x.
Then, we deduce
that
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a fixed circle of
T.

Remark 3
.

The inequality (2.4) means that
Tx
is not in the interior of the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
for each
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
In the
same fashion, the inequality (2.5) means that
Tx
is not in the exterior of
the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
for each
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.

These two results show that
T\left(C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}\right)\subset C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
under the conditions (2.4) and (2.5).

The following theorem presents a new solution for fixed-circle problem
obtained by the help of the inequality (1.3) in Theorem 3.

Theorem 7
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be any circle on
X
and the mapping
\varphi
be as in (2.1). If
T
is a self-mapping defined on
X
satisfying
the conditions

(2.15)

d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

and

(2.16)

r\preceq A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A+d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and some
A\in\mathbb{A}
with
\left\|A\right\|<1,
then the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a
fixed circle of
T.

Proof.

Let
x
be any point in the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.
Let
x\neq Tx.

Then, using the (2.6), (2.1) and (2.7) we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(x,x_{0}\right)-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A+d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A

and so

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|\leq\left\|A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A^{\ast}\right\|\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|\left\|A\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|^{2}\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|.

This yields a contradiction with our assumption. Therefore, we conclude that

x=Tx
for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and hence
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is a fixed circle of
T.

We finish this section by give our last existence theorem for fixed circles
of a self-mapping on a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric space as follows:

Theorem 8
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be any circle on
X
and the mapping
\varphi
be as in (2.1).
T
is a self-mapping defined on
X
satisfying
the condition

(2.17)

A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A\preceq\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

for all
x\in X
where
A\in\mathbb{A}
is an invertible element and
\left\|A^{-1}\right\|<1
if and only if
T
fixes the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and
T=I_{X}.

Proof.

Suppose that
T
be a self-mapping defined on
X
satisfying the condition
(2.8). Let
x
be any point in
X.
Let
x\neq Tx.
Then, using the (2.8),
(2.1) and (iii) given in Definition 2 we get

\displaystyle A^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\varphi\left(x\right)-\varphi\left(Tx\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(x,x_{0}\right)-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle d\left(x,Tx\right)+d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)-d\left(Tx,x_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d\left(x,Tx\right)

and so

d\left(x,Tx\right)\preceq\left(A^{\ast}\right)^{-1}d\left(x,Tx\right)A^{-1}=\left(A^{-1}\right)^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A^{-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(A^{-1}\right)^{\ast}d\left(x,Tx\right)A^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(A^{-1}\right)^{\ast}\right\|\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|\left\|A^{-1}\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|A^{-1}\right\|^{2}\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(x,Tx\right)\right\|.

But it is impossible. Hence, we can write
x=Tx
for all
x\in X
and
T=I_{X}.

Conversely, suppose that
T
fixes the circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and
T=I_{X}.
Then, since
Tx=x
for all
x\in X,
the condition (2.8) holds for
any invertible element
A\in\mathbb{A}
with
\left\|A^{-1}\right\|<1.
This completes the proof.

Remark 4
.

Theorem 8 says that if a self-mapping fixes a circle by satisfying the
conditions (2.2) and (2.3) (or the conditions (2.4) and (2.5)), but does not
satisfy the condition (2.8), then the self-mapping is different from the
identity map.

2.2.
The uniqueness of fixed circles

In this part, we discuss the uniqueness of fixed circles in the existence
theorems proved in subsection 2.1.

Before giving our uniqueness theorems, we emphasize that the fixed circles
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
in Theorem 5, Theorem 6 and Theorem 7 is not unique
with the following result.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space and
C_{x_{1},r_{1}}^{C^{\ast}},C_{x_{2},r_{2}}^{C^{\ast}},...,C_{x_{n},r_{n}}^{C^{\ast}}
be any given circles. Then, there exists
at least one self-mapping
T
of
X
such that
T
such that
T
fixes all
the circles
C_{x_{1},r_{1}}^{C^{\ast}},

C_{x_{2},r_{2}}^{C^{\ast}},

...,C_{x_{n},r_{n}}^{C^{\ast}}.

Proof.

Let the self-mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
be defined as

Tx=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}x,x\in\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\cup}}C_{x_{i},r_{i}}^{C^{\ast}}\\
\alpha,x\notin\overset{n}{\underset{i=1}{\cup}}C_{x_{i},r_{i}}^{C^{\ast}}\end{array}\right.

where
\alpha\in X
is a constant satisfying
d\left(\alpha,x_{i}\right)\neq r_{i}
and the mapping
\varphi_{i}:X\rightarrow\left[0,\infty\right)
be defined as

\varphi_{i}\left(x\right)=d\left(x,x_{i}\right)

for
i=1,2,...n.
Then it is not hard to verify that the conditions (2.2)
and (2.3) in Theorem 5 are satisfied for the circles
C_{x_{1},r_{1}}^{C^{\ast}},C_{x_{2},r_{2}}^{C^{\ast}},...,C_{x_{n},r_{n}}^{C^{\ast}}.

Therefore, all the circles
C_{x_{1},r_{1}}^{C^{\ast}},C_{x_{2},r_{2}}^{C^{\ast}},...,C_{x_{n},r_{n}}^{C^{\ast}}
are fixed
circles of the self-mapping
T
by Theorem 5.

inequality (1.1) in Theorem 1 in the following theorem.

.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)

and the

be a

C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and
C_{x_{1},\delta}^{C^{\ast}},
respectively. Then, we get by (2.10)

d\left(a,b\right)=d\left(Ta,Tb\right)\preceq A\left(d\left(Ta,b\right)+d\left(Tb,a\right)\right),

so that

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A\left(d\left(Ta,b\right)+d\left(Tb,a\right)\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|\left\|d\left(a,b\right)+d\left(b,a\right)\right\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left\|A\right\|\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|

which is a contradiction which means that
a=b.
This shows that the
self-mapping
T
fixes only circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.

Finally, we state our last uniqueness theorem for the fixed circles in
Theorem 7 using the condition (1.4) in Theorem 4.

Theorem 11
.

Let
\left(X,\mathbb{A},d\right)
be a
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric
space,
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
be any circle on
X
and the
T
be a
self-mapping satisfying the conditions (2.6) and (2.7) given in Theorem 7.
If
T
satisfies the contraction condition that there exists
u\left(x,y\right)\in\left\{d\left(x,y\right),d\left(x,Tx\right),d\left(y,Ty\right),d\left(x,Ty\right),d\left(y,Tx\right)\right\}
such that

(2.20)

d\left(Tx,Ty\right)\preceq A^{\ast}u\left(x,y\right)A

for all
x\in C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
,
y\in X-C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}

and some
A\in\mathbb{A}
with
\left\|A\right\|<1,
then the
circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
is unique fixed circle of
T.

Proof.

Assume that
C_{x_{1},\delta}^{C^{\ast}}
is another fixed circle of
T.

Let
a
and
b
be any points in
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}
and
C_{x_{1},\delta}^{C^{\ast}},
respectively. Then, we get by (2.11)

d\left(a,b\right)=d\left(Ta,Tb\right)\preceq A^{\ast}u\left(a,b\right)A,

so that

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A^{\ast}u\left(a,b\right)A\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|^{2}\left\|u\left(a,b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\left\|u\left(a,b\right)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|,\left\|d\left(a,Tb\right)\right\|,\left\|d\left(b,Tb\right)\right\|,\left\|d\left(a,Tb\right)\right\|,\left\|d\left(b,Ta\right)\right\|\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|^{2}\max\left\{\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|,0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|d\left(a,b\right)\right\|

which is a contradiction. Hence
a=b.
This implies that the self-mapping
T

fixes only circle
C_{x_{0},r}^{C^{\ast}}.

3.
Conclusion and Future Works

In this article, we discuss the existence and uniqueness of the fixed-circle
for self-mappings satisfying some special conditions on
C^{\ast}-
algebra
valued metric spaces. We also furnish some examples to show effectiveness of
our theoretical results. Using similar approaches and new contractive
conditions, it can be studied new fixed-circle results on
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces. Since with this article we develop a new and
different perspective instead of the classical fixed point theory in
C^{\ast}-
algebra valued metric spaces, we hope that our results will
support researchers for future works and applications to other related areas.

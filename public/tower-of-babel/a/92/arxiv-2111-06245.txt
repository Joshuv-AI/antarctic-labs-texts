Abstract
. We investigate the behaviour of orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series at their harmonic points by using theta lifts. In the case of singular weight, we show that the orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series that can be written as a theta lift have a simple pole at s = 1 s=1 whose residues yield holomorphic orthogonal modular forms that are Eisenstein series on the boundary and we give a sufficient condition on the surjectivity of this construction.



1.
Introduction

Let
L
be an even lattice in a rational quadratic space
V
of signature
(2,l)
. There is an index
2
subgroup of the corresponding orthogonal group
O^{+}(2,l)\subseteq O(2,l)
acting on the orthogonal upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}_{l}
. Similar to the case of elliptic modular forms, it is possible to define orthogonal modular forms, which can be seen to be global sections of a hermitian line bundle. For
l=1
we obtain the classical case of elliptic modular forms and for
l=2
we obtain Hilbert modular forms for real quadratic number fields.

An important problem is the construction of such modular forms, in particular for low weight. It turns out that there is a minimal weight for non-zero holomorphic modular forms, which is given by
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
for
l>2
, see
[
Bun01
]
. The weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
is called the singular weight, and modular forms of singular weight have many vanishing Fourier coefficients. In particular, there are no cusp forms of singular weight (for an analogous theory in the Siegel case see
[
Fre83
]
, where it is shown that every holomorphic modular form of singular weight is a linear combination of theta functions). In contrast to the symplectic case, there are no holomorphic theta series for the orthogonal group (except for some low dimensional examples where we have an exceptional isomorphism from the orthogonal group to the symplectic group). Moreover, the usual constructions of holomorphic modular forms do not work in low weight. For example Eisenstein series of low weight do not converge. On the other hand, using the celebrated multiplicative Borcherds lift of
[
Bor98
, Theorem 13.3]
, examples of holomorphic modular forms of singular weight can be constructed. For results in this direction see
[
DHS15
]
,
[
OS19
]
,
[
Sch17
]
. Another method is to use the additive Borcherds lift of
[
Bor98
, Theorem 14.3]
using holomorphic modular forms of weight
0
, i.e. invariant vectors, as input functions.

Our aim is to investigate Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,\lambda}(Z)
of low weight, in particular of singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
by considering the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
\mathcal{E}_{\kappa,\lambda}(Z,s)
, get a meromorphic continuation to all
s\in\mathbb{C}
and hope that they yield holomorphic modular forms for special values of
s
. This will be done using the results of
[
Kie21
]
.

We will give more details now. As above, let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,l)
and
L^{\prime}
the corresponding dual lattice. Throughout we will assume that
L
has Witt rank
2
and that
l>2
. For a primitive isotropic vector
z\in L
and
z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
consider
K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
. Let
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
, where
C
is a fixed connected component of

\{Y\in K\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid q(Y)>0\}.

Then
\mathbb{H}_{l}
is a hermitian symmetric domain and there is an index
2
subgroup
O^{+}(L\otimes\mathbb{R})\subseteq O(L\otimes\mathbb{R})
acting on
\mathbb{H}_{l}
. Let
\Gamma(L)
be the kernel of the natural map
O^{+}(L)\to O(L^{\prime}/L)
. By the theory of Baily-Borel (see
[
BB66
]
,
[
BJ06
]
) we can add
0
-dimensional boundary components (points) and
1
-dimensional boundary components (isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}
) to obtain
\mathbb{H}_{l}^{*}
, such that the quotient
\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}^{*}
is a compact complex analytic space, which can be shown to be projective and hence, by the Chow’s theorem, algebraic. For an holomorphic orthogonal modular form
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
of weight
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}
with respect to
\Gamma(L)
one can now define its restriction
F|_{I}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
to a
1
-dimensional boundary component
I
. One easily sees that
F|_{I}
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
\kappa
for an appropriate arithmetic subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, it makes sense to talk about the space
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
of holomorphic orthogonal modular forms that are linear combinations Eisenstein series on the boundary. If
F|_{I}
vanishes for every boundary component, we say that
F
is a cusp form and we write
\operatorname{S}_{\kappa}(\Gamma(L))
for the space of cusp forms.

For an easier exposition, we assume throughout the introduction that
L
splits two hyperbolic planes over
\mathbb{Z}
. Then the
0
-dimensional cusps of
\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}
are in bijective correspondence to the isotropic elements in
L^{\prime}/L
(which we denote by
\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
) up to
\pm 1
. For an appropriate automorphic form
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
of weight
k
consider the additive Borcherds lift defined by
[
Bor98
]

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi(Z,f):=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}}^{\text{reg}}\langle f(\tau),\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)\rangle v^{k}\frac{\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v}{v^{2}},

where
\Theta_{L}
is a certain Siegel theta function of weight
k
in
\tau
(see Section
6
). In
[
Kie21
]
we have seen that the additive Borcherds lift
\Phi_{k,\beta}(Z,s)
of a vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s),\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
of weight
k
for the Weil representation
\rho_{L}
defined by
[
BK03
]
(in
[
BK03
]
the dual Weil representation
\rho_{L}^{*}
is considered) is a linear combination of orthogonal non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}(Z,s)
of weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1+k
with respect to
\Gamma(L)
corresponding to a
0
-dimensional cusp
\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
. To obtain holomorphic Eisenstein series one would try to evaluate this at the harmonic point
s=0
. By calculating the Fourier expansion, one can split up
\Phi_{k,\beta}(Z):=\Phi_{k,\beta}(Z,0)
into a holomorphic part
\Phi_{k,\beta}^{+}(Z)
and a non-holomorphic part
\Phi_{k,\beta}^{-}(Z)
. We will show

Proposition 1.1

(see Proposition
8.6
)
.

The holomorphic part
\Phi_{k,\beta}^{+}(Z)
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{k,\beta}^{+}(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\kappa)N_{z}^{\kappa}}{(-2\pi i)^{\kappa}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\delta_{\beta,\frac{bz}{N_{z}}}\zeta^{b}(\kappa)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in K\\
q(\lambda)=0\\
(\lambda,Y)>0\\
\lambda\text{ primitive}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}\\
c\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{\lambda}\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\delta_{\beta,\frac{c\lambda}{N_{\lambda}}-\frac{c(\lambda,\zeta)}{N_{\lambda}N_{z}}z+\frac{bz}{N}}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa-1}^{c,b}(m)e\left(\frac{m(\lambda,Z-\frac{\zeta_{K}}{N_{z}})}{N_{\lambda}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in K^{\prime}\\
q(\lambda)>0\\
(\lambda,Y)>0\end{subarray}}b(\lambda)e\left(\frac{(\lambda,Z-\frac{\zeta_{K}}{N_{z}})}{N_{\lambda}}\right)

where
N_{z}
and
N_{\lambda}
are the levels of
z
and
\lambda
,

\tilde{\sigma}_{\kappa-1}^{c,b}(m)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\mid m\\
\frac{m}{n}\equiv c\bmod{N_{\lambda}}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{sgn}(n)n^{\kappa-1}e\left(\frac{nb}{N_{z}}\right)

is a divisor sum over positive and negative divisors and
b(\lambda)
are certain Fourier coefficients.

Since only the terms with
q(\lambda)=0
in the Fourier expansion contribute to the restrictions to boundary components, one easily sees that the holomorphic part restricted to a boundary component is given by an Eisenstein series, see
[
DS05
, Theorem 4.2.3]
for their Fourier expansions. By inspecting the non-holomorphic part and using that vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series converge at
s=0
for
k>2
we obtain

Theorem 1.2

(see Theorem
8.9
)
.

The non-holomorphic part
\Phi_{k,\beta}^{-}(Z)
vanishes for
k>2
and hence
\Phi_{k,\beta}(Z)=\Phi_{k,\beta}^{+}(Z)
is a holomorphic orthogonal modular form that is an Eisenstein series on the boundary. Moreover, every holomorphic orthogonal modular form that is a linear combination of Eisenstein series on the boundary is obtained as a theta lift (up to cusp forms).

For
k=0
and hence singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
this does not work immediately, since the non-holomorphic part does not vanish in general. Using the functional equation relating the values at
s
and
1-s
we will instead inspect the behaviour at
s=1
. We obtain

Theorem 1.3

(see Theorem
8.10
)
.

The additive Borcherds lift
\Phi_{0,\beta}(Z,s)
has a simple pole at
s=1
with residue given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\kappa)N_{z}^{\kappa}}{(-2\pi i)^{\kappa}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{res}_{s=1}c_{0,\beta}\left(\frac{bz}{N_{z}},0,s\right)\zeta^{b}(\kappa)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in K^{\prime}\\
q(\lambda)=0\\
(\lambda,Y)>0\end{subarray}}e\left(-\frac{(\lambda,\zeta)}{N_{z}}\right)\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\mid\lambda}n^{\kappa-1}e\left(\frac{nb}{N_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\operatorname{res}_{s=1}c_{0,\beta}\left(\frac{\lambda}{n}-\frac{(\lambda,\zeta)}{nN_{z}}z+\frac{bz}{N_{z}},0,s\right)e(\lambda,Z).

where
c_{0,\beta}(0,0,s)
is a Fourier coefficients of the vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
E_{0,\beta}(\tau,s)
. In particular, it is a holomorphic orthogonal modular form of singular weight that is an Eisenstein series on the boundary.

In fact this is just the Borcherds lift of the invariant vector
\operatorname{res}_{s=1}E_{0,\beta}(\tau,s)
so that the theorem reads
\operatorname{res}_{s=1}\Phi_{0,\beta}(Z,s)=\Phi(Z,\operatorname{res}_{s=1}E_{0,\beta}(\cdot,s))
.

As in the higher weight case, it is natural to ask whether all holomorphic orthogonal modular forms of singular weight that are linear combinations of Eisenstein series on the boundary can be obtained in this way. Therefore we consider the adjoint theta lift
\Phi^{*}
mapping holomorphic orthogonal modular forms of singular weight to modular forms of weight
0
. For an orthogonal modular form
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
it is given by

(2)

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(\tau,F):=\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}.

We will show that if
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
is holomorphic of singular weight, then
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
is a harmonic function of weight
0
(see Lemma
9.2
). In Section
9
we prove the following main theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(see Theorem
9.3
)
.

For an orthogonal modular form of singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1>0
the theta lift
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
is an invariant vector for the Weil representation given by

\frac{\Gamma(l/2)}{2(2\pi)^{l/2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)\\
\gamma=k_{\delta}\delta\end{subarray}}\zeta_{+}^{k_{\delta}}(l-\kappa)a_{F,\delta}(0)C(\delta)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma},

where
a_{F,\delta}(0)
is the value of
F
in the
0
-dimensional cusp corresponding to
\delta
and
C(\delta)
is a non-zero constant.

As a corollary one obtains

Corollary 1.5

(see Corollary
9.6
)
.

Assume that
L
splits two hyperbolic planes. The theta lift
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
vanishes if and only if
F
vanishes in every
0
-dimensional cusp.

Since the theta lifts (
1
) and (
2
) are adjoint to each other we obtain

Corollary 1.6

(see Corollary
9.8
)
.

Assume that
L
splits two hyperbolic planes. Then every holomorphic orthogonal modular form of singular weight for
\Gamma(L)
that is an Eisenstein series on the boundary is the residue at
s=1
of some non-holomorphic Eisenstein series and the additive Borcherds lift is an isomorphism

\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])\to\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L)).

If
L
is a maximal lattice, then
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
is either
1
-dimensional (if
L
is unimodular) or
0
-dimensional (if
L
is not unimodular). Since maximal lattices of Witt rank
2
always split two hyperbolic planes over
\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain for singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
.

Corollary 1.7

(see Corollary
9.9
)
.

If
L
is a maximal lattice of Witt rank
2
, then the space
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
is either
1
-dimensional (if
L
is unimodular) or
0
-dimensional (if
L
is not unimodular). Moreover, if
\kappa=2,4,6,8,10,14
, i.e.
l=6,10,14,18,22,30
, then we have
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}(\Gamma(L))=\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
and the space of holomorphic modular forms of singular weight is either
0
or
1
-dimensional depending on
L
being unimodular or not.

If
L
does not split two hyperbolic planes over
\mathbb{Z}
, then we can still fully determine the image of the theta lift (see Corollary
9.7
).

2.
Vector-Valued Non-Holomorphic Eisenstein Series

We will now introduce the Weil representation and vector-valued modular forms. Therefore, let
\mathbb{H}:=\{\tau=u+iv\in\mathbb{C}\mid v>0\}
be the usual upper half-plane. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
we write
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
and we denote by
\sqrt{z}=z^{\frac{1}{2}}
the principal branch of the square-root, i.e.
\arg(\sqrt{z})\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
the
metaplectic cover
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It is realized as pairs
(M,\phi)
, where
M\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
\phi:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic square root of
\tau\mapsto c\tau+d
. The product for
(M_{1},\phi_{1}),(M_{2},\phi_{2})\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
is given by

(M_{1},\phi_{1}(\tau))(M_{2},\phi_{2}(\tau)):=(M_{1}M_{2},\phi_{1}(M_{2}\tau)\phi_{2}(\tau)),

where
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\tau=\frac{a\tau+d}{c\tau+d}
is the usual action of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

By
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
we denote the inverse image of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
under the covering map. It is generated by

T=\left(\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},1\right)\quad\text{and}\quad S=\left(\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\sqrt{\tau}\right).

We have the relation
S^{2}=(ST)^{3}=Z
, where
Z=\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right),i\right)
is the standard generator of the center of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Furthermore we will write
\Gamma_{\infty}:=\{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
and
\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}:=\{\left(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&n\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right),1\right)\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For an even non-degenerate lattice
L
of signature
(b^{+},b^{-})
consider the group ring
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
with standard basis
(\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}
. For
\mathfrak{v}=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathfrak{v}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
we write
\mathfrak{v}^{*}:=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathfrak{v}_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{-\gamma}
. Moreover we write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard inner product on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
which is anti-linear in the second variable. Write
\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)\subseteq L^{\prime}/L
for the set of isotropic elements and denote the subspace of vectors that are supported on isotropic elements by
\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
. Moreover we introduce the notation
\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(\tau):=e(\tau)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}=e^{2\pi i\tau}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
Weil representation
is the unitary representation
\rho_{L}
of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
defined by

\rho_{L}(T)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}:=\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(q(\gamma))\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{L}(S)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}:=\frac{\sqrt{i}^{b^{-}-b^{+}}}{\sqrt{L^{\prime}/L}}\sum_{\delta\in L^{\prime}/L}\mathfrak{e}_{\delta}(-(\gamma,\delta)).

The Weil representation factors through a finite quotient of
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. The space of invariant vectors under the Weil representation is denoted by
\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
.

A short calculation using orthogonality of characters shows for example
\rho_{L}(Z)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}=i^{b^{-}-b^{+}}\mathfrak{e}_{-\gamma}
. For
\beta,\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L
we define the coefficients

\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(M,\phi):=\langle\rho_{L}(M,\phi)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma},\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\beta,\gamma}^{-1}(M,\phi):=\langle\rho_{L}^{-1}(M,\phi)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma},\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\rangle.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
Shi75
, Proposition 1.6]
)
.

For
M\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
the coefficient
\rho_{\beta,\gamma}(\tilde{M})
is given by

\sqrt{i}^{(b^{-}-b^{+})(1-\operatorname{sgn}(d))}\delta_{\beta,a\gamma}e(abq(\beta))

if
c=0
and by

\frac{\sqrt{i}^{(b^{-}-b^{+})\operatorname{sgn}(c)}}{\lvert c\rvert^{(b^{-}+b^{+})/2}\sqrt{\lvert L^{\prime}/L\rvert}}\sum_{r\in L/cL}e\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,\beta+r)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

if
c\neq 0
.

For a vector-valued function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
we write
f_{\gamma}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
for its components, i.e.
f=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}f_{\gamma}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
. For
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
we define the
Petersson slash operator

f\mapsto f|_{k,L}(M,\phi)
by

(f|_{k,L}(M,\phi))(\tau)=\phi(\tau)^{-2k}\rho_{L}^{-1}(M,\phi)f(M\tau).

If
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
is smooth and invariant under the action of
T
, i.e.
f|_{k,L}T=f
, then we have a Fourier expansion

f(\tau)=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)}c(\gamma,n,v)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu).

Definition 2.4
.

A function
f:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
is said to be a
modular form
of weight
k
with respect to the Weil representation if
f|_{k,L}(M,\phi)=f
for all
(M,\phi)\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We call
f
a
holomorphic modular form
if
f
is holomorphic with Fourier expansion

f(\tau)=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n\geq 0\end{subarray}}c(\gamma,n)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(n\tau).

Obviously, non-trivial modular forms only exist for weights with
2k+b^{-}-b^{+}=0\bmod 2
.

Assume now that
b^{+}-b^{-}
is even and let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover set
\kappa=\frac{b^{-}-b^{+}}{2}+k
. Let
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
and similar to
[
BK03
]
define the
vector-valued non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
of weight
k
by

E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{s}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}|_{k,L}M.

Observe that
[
BK03
]
consider the dual Weil representation
\rho_{L}
. For
\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)
of order
N_{\beta}
and a character
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\beta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
we define

E_{k,\beta,\chi}(\tau,s):=\sum_{n\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\beta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(n)E_{k,n\beta}(\tau,s).

More generally, for
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
we define

\displaystyle E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{M\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{\infty}\backslash\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})}v^{s}\mathfrak{v}|_{k,L}M

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}\mathfrak{v}_{\beta}E_{k,\beta}(\tau,s).

We have
E_{k,\mathfrak{v}^{*}}=(-1)^{\kappa}E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}
and a Fourier expansion of the form

\displaystyle E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{v}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}^{*})v^{s}+\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,0,s)v^{1-s-k}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n\neq 0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n,s)\mathcal{W}_{s}(4\pi ny)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(nu),

where
\mathcal{W}_{s}
is a special Whittaker function. For the precise coefficients see
[
BK03
]
,
[
Wil19
]
(or
[
BK01
]
,
[
Sch06
]
,
[
Sch18
]
for the holomorphic case), we will not need them here. It can be easily seen that the vector-valued Eisenstein series are
\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
-translates of usual scalar-valued Eisenstein series. They are normalized such that their meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
are holomorphic in
s=0
and have a simple pole for
k=0
at
s=1
whose residue is an invariant vector. For an invariant vector
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
we have

E_{k}(\tau,s)\mathfrak{v}=E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s),

where
E_{k}(\tau,s)
is the suitably normalized Eisenstein series for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. For
k>2
the series converges at
s=0
and defines a holomorphic Eisenstein series
E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau)=E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,0)
with Fourier expansion

E_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau)=\mathfrak{v}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}^{*}+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n>0\end{subarray}}c_{k,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(n\tau).

For
k=2
they have a Fourier expansion of the form
[
Miy06
]
,
[
DS05
]

E_{2,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau)=\mathfrak{w}v^{-1}+\mathfrak{v}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}^{*}+\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}+q(\gamma)\\
n>0\end{subarray}}c_{2,\mathfrak{v}}(\gamma,n)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}(n\tau).

Applying the lowering operator shows that
\mathfrak{w}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
is an invariant vector. For
k=0
their residue at
s=1
yields an invariant vector and if
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
we obtain

\operatorname{res}_{s=1}E_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(\tau,s)=\operatorname{res}_{s=1}E_{0}(\tau,s)\mathfrak{v},

in particular these residues span the space of invariants.

3.
Orthogonal Modular Forms

From now on let
L

Definition 3.1
.

A function

(ii)

\sigma\in\Gamma
.

For a modular form

of weight

with respect to

Then

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a modular form

Remark 3.3
.

For

. Moreover, if

Definition 3.4
.

For

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\lvert q(X_{D})\rvert

\displaystyle 1/t

\displaystyle<y_{1},

\displaystyle y_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle<t^{2}q(Y),

\displaystyle<t^{2}y_{1}^{2}.

We have the following

(i)

4.
Siegel Operator

For an isotropic line
I\subseteq V(\mathbb{R})
generated by some isotropic vector
\lambda\in I
the corresponding point
[\lambda]
is in the closure of
\mathcal{K}^{+}
in
\mathcal{N}
. To see this, take two sequences
x_{n},y_{n}\in V(\mathbb{R})
with positive norm which are orthogonal and converge to
\lambda
. Then
[x_{n}+iy_{n}]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
with limit
[(1+i)\lambda]=[\lambda]\in\mathcal{N}
for
n\to\infty
.

Definition 4.1
.

A boundary point of
\mathcal{K}^{+}
of the form
[\lambda]\in\mathcal{N}
which is represented by a real isotropic line is called
special boundary point
. A set consisting of one special boundary points is called
0
-dimensional boundary component
. A non-special boundary point is called
generic boundary point
.

Let now
I\subseteq V(\mathbb{R})
be an isotropic plane and consider the set of all boundary points which can be represented by elements of
I\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 4.2
.

For an totally isotropic plane
I\subseteq V(\mathbb{R})
the set of all generic boundary points which can be represented by an element of
I\otimes\mathbb{C}
is called
1
-dimensional boundary component
attached to
I
. By a
boundary component
we mean a
0
-dimensional or
1
-dimensional boundary component.

Lemma 4.3
.

The
1
-dimensional boundary components are isomorphic to usual upper half-planes. Moreover, there is a bijective correspondence between boundary components and non-zero isotropic subspaces of
V(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
I\subseteq V(\mathbb{R})
be an isotropic plane. Take a basis
z,d
of
I
and consider
\tilde{z},\tilde{d}
isotropic such that
(z,\tilde{z})=(d,\tilde{d})=1
and all other products vanish. We will use the shorthand notation
(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})
for
z_{1}\tilde{z}+z_{2}z+z_{3}\tilde{d}+z_{4}d
. Then the elements of
I
have the form
(0,z_{2},0,z_{4})
. Assume that this is not a multiple of a real point, i.e. it is not a special boundary points. Then
z_{2}\neq 0\neq z_{4}
and we can normalize it such that
z_{4}=1
, i.e. we have a point of the form
(0,\tau,0,1)
for some
\tau\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. making suitable choices of the basis and
\mathcal{K}^{+}
we can assume that
[1,1,i,i]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
. Then we have an embedding

\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{H}\to\mathcal{K}^{+},\quad(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\mapsto[1,-\tau_{1}\tau_{2},\tau_{1},\tau_{2}].

In the projective space we have

\lim_{t\to\infty}[1,-\tau it,\tau,it]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\left[\frac{1}{it},-\tau,\frac{\tau}{it},1\right]=[0,-\tau,0,1].

Thus the point
[0,-\tau,0,1]
is in the boundary of
\mathcal{K}^{+}
if and only if
v=\operatorname{Im}(\tau)>0
. The set of all boundary points represented by elements in
I\otimes\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
\mathbb{H}\cup\mathbb{R}\cup\infty
. In particular, if we let
\tau=iv
with
v\to\infty
, we obtain the special boundary point
[z]
.
∎

Definition 4.4
.

A boundary component is called
rational boundary component
if the corresponding isotropic subspace is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Write
\mathbb{H}_{l}^{*}
for the union of
\mathbb{H}_{l}\simeq\mathcal{K}^{+}
with all rational boundary components. Then the rational orthogonal group
O^{+}(V):=O^{+}(V(\mathbb{R}))\cap O(V)
acts on

By the theory of Baily-Borel (see
[
BB66
]
,
[
BJ06
]
), there is a topology on
\mathbb{H}_{l}^{*}
such that for congruence subgroups
\Gamma\subseteq O^{+}(V)
the quotient
X_{\Gamma}=\mathbb{H}_{l}^{*}/\Gamma
carries the structure of a projective variety, which contains
\mathbb{H}_{l}/\Gamma
as a Zariski open subvariety.

Let now
I\subseteq L\otimes\mathbb{Q}
be an isotropic plane. Let
z\in L\cap I
be primitive and
z^{\prime}\in L
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
. Let
K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
and take
d\in K\cap I
primitive,
d^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
with
(d,d^{\prime})=1
and let
D=K\cap d^{\perp}\cap d^{\prime\perp}
. As before, write
\tilde{z}=z^{\prime}-q(z^{\prime})z,\tilde{d}=d^{\prime}-q(d^{\prime})d
and let
d_{3},\ldots,d_{l}
be a basis of
D
. Then we obviously have
I=\langle z,d\rangle
. Recall the orthogonal upper half plane corresponding to

\mathbb{H}_{l}:=\{Z=X+iY\in K\otimes\mathbb{C}\mid q(Y)>0,(Y,d)>0\}

and that we write
Z=z_{1}\tilde{d}+z_{2}d+Z_{D}
. Let
C\subseteq K\otimes\mathbb{R}
be the positive cone such that

\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC

and write
\overline{C}
for its closure. For
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and

we have
\tau\tilde{d}+itd\in\mathbb{H}_{l}
, which corresponds to
[\tilde{z}-\tau itz+\tau\tilde{d}+itd]\in\mathcal{K}^{+}

we define the Siegel operator corresponding to the boundary component

as (if it exists)

F|_{I}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\tau\mapsto\lim_{t\to\infty}F(\tau\tilde{d}+itd).

For

we have

(\lambda,\tau\tilde{d}+itd)=it\lambda_{1}+\tau\lambda_{2}.

Assume that

has a Fourier expansion of the form

F(Z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in K^{\prime}\\
\lambda\in\overline{C}\end{subarray}}a(\lambda)e(\lambda,Z),

then the Siegel operator exists and we have

\displaystyle F|_{I}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})\in K^{\prime}\\
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})\in\overline{C}\end{subarray}}a(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})e(\lambda_{1}\tilde{d}+\lambda_{2}d+\lambda_{D},\tau d^{\prime}+itd)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})\in K^{\prime}\\
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})\in\overline{C}\end{subarray}}a(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{D})\exp(-2\pi t\lambda_{1})e(\lambda_{2}\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(0,\lambda_{2},0)\in K^{\prime}\\
\lambda_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}a(0,\lambda_{2},0)e(\lambda_{2}\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}a(0,m/N_{d},0)e(m\tau/N_{d}).

The value in the
0
-dimensional cusp

is given by the constant term of the Fourier expansion, i.e.

a(0)=\lim_{t\to\infty}F(tZ)

for arbitrary
Z\in\mathbb{H}_{l}
.

Let now
F:\tilde{\mathcal{K}}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic modular form of weight
\kappa
for some congruence subgroup
\Gamma
. Then its restriction to the boundary component
F_{z}|_{I}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic modular form of weight
\kappa
for some subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. One easily sees that for the constant Fourier coefficient we have
a_{-z}(0)=(-1)^{\kappa}a_{z}(0)
.

Definition 4.5
.

We call a holomorphic modular form
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
of weight
\kappa
for some congruence subgroup
\Gamma
a
cusp form
if
F|_{I}
vanishes identically for all boundary components
I
. We say that
F
is an
Eisenstein series on the boundary
if
F|_{I}
is an Eisenstein series for all boundary components
I
. We write
\operatorname{S}_{\kappa}(\Gamma)
for the space of cusp forms and
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma)
for the space of holomorphic modular forms that are Eisenstein series on the boundary. In particular we have
\operatorname{S}_{\kappa}(\Gamma)\subseteq\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 4.6
.

By the theory of singular weights, there are no cusp forms of singular weight. In particular, a holomorphic modular form of singular weight is completely determined by its values at the boundary and a holomorphic modular form of singular weight that is an Eisenstein series on the boundary is completely determined by its values in the
0
-dimensional cusps.

Remark 4.7
.

If a holomorphic modular form
F
is an Eisenstein series on the boundary, then its restrictions to the boundary are fully determined by the values in the
0
-dimensional cusps, i.e. the constant Fourier coefficients.

Definition 4.8
.

For
\Gamma=\Gamma(L)
recall the map

\pi_{L}:\{\text{$0$-dimensional cusps of }\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}\}\simeq\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L).

We write
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\pi}(\Gamma(L))
for the subspace of

that consists of holomorphic modular forms whose values in the

-dimensional cusps only depend on their image in

. In particular, if

, the value in the

and if

Lemma 4.9

(

If

5.

Let
M
be a hermitian manifold and
E
a holomorphic vector bundle. We write
\mathcal{E}(E)=\mathcal{E}(M,E)
for the space of (global) sections of
E
and, more generally, for
U\subseteq M
open, we write
\mathcal{E}(U,E)
for the space of sections over
U
. Assume now that
E
carries an hermitian metric. Then the hermitian metric on
E
induces a hermitian metric on
E
-valued differential forms
\mathcal{A}^{*}(M,E)
locally given by

(\varphi\otimes\sigma,\varphi^{\prime}\otimes\sigma^{\prime})=(\varphi,\varphi^{\prime})(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})

on decomposable forms, where the first factor is the hermitian metric on differential forms coming from the hermitian metric on
M
and the second factor is the hermitian metric on the vector bundle
E
. Moreover, the hermitian metric on
E
together with the Hodge-
*
-operator
*:\mathcal{A}^{*}(M)\to\mathcal{A}^{*}(M)
induces a Hodge-
*
-operator

\overline{*}_{E}:\mathcal{A}^{*}(M,E)\to\mathcal{A}^{*}(M,E^{*}).

The evaluation map
E\otimes E^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
induces a wedge product
\wedge
which satisfies

\alpha\wedge\overline{*}_{E}\beta=(\alpha,\beta)\operatorname{vol},

where
\operatorname{vol}
is the volume form induced from the hermitian metric on
M
. Moreover we have a hermitian inner product on
E
-valued differential forms given by

(\alpha,\beta)_{2}=\int_{M}\alpha\wedge\overline{*}_{E}\beta=\int_{M}(\alpha,\beta)\operatorname{vol}.

Let
\overline{\partial}^{*}_{E}=\overline{*}_{E^{*}}\overline{\partial}_{E^{*}}\overline{*}_{E}
and
\Delta_{E}=\overline{\partial}^{*}_{E}\overline{\partial}_{E}
be the Laplace operator on sections of
E
. We will need the following

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Che73
, Section 3, Example (B)]
)
.

Let
M
be a complete connected hermitian manifold and let
E
be an hermitian vector bundle. If
u,v
are smooth square integrable sections of
E
such that
\Delta_{E}u,\Delta_{E}v
are also square integrable, then

(\Delta_{E}u,v)=(u,\Delta_{E}v).

L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,l)
. For
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
and
z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1

K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
. Let

q(y_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+y_{l}b_{l})=y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2}-\ldots\ldots y_{l}^{2}.

If
Z=z_{1}b_{1}+z_{2}b_{2}+z_{3}b_{3}+\ldots z_{l}b_{l}\in K\otimes\mathbb{C}
, we write
Z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l})
and similarly
X=(x_{1},\ldots x_{l}),Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{l})
if

with

. Denote by
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
the corresponding tube domain model, where

C=\{Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{l})\in K_{z}\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid y_{1}>0,q(Y)>0\}.

The Kähler form is given by

w=-\frac{i}{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\log(q(Y)))=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{i,j}h_{ij}(Z)\mathrm{d}z_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j},

where
h(Z)=h(Y)=(h_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq l}
is the associated hermitian form given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(Y)^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}^{2}&-y_{1}y_{2}&-y_{1}y_{3}&\ldots&-y_{1}y_{l}\\
-y_{1}y_{2}&y_{2}^{2}&y_{2}y_{3}&\ldots&y_{2}y_{l}\\
-y_{1}y_{3}&y_{2}y_{3}&y_{3}^{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&y_{l-1}y_{l}\\
-y_{1}y_{l}&y_{2}y_{l}&\ldots&y_{l-1}y_{l}&y_{l}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{1}{2q(Y)}\begin{pmatrix}-1&&&\\
&1&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}

and its inverse is

\displaystyle h^{-1}(Y)=4\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}^{2}&y_{1}y_{2}&y_{1}y_{3}&\ldots&y_{1}y_{l}\\
y_{1}y_{2}&y_{2}^{2}&y_{2}y_{3}&\ldots&y_{2}y_{l}\\
y_{1}y_{3}&y_{2}y_{3}&y_{3}^{2}&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&y_{l-1}y_{l}\\
y_{1}y_{l}&y_{2}y_{l}&\ldots&y_{l-1}y_{l}&y_{l}^{2}\end{pmatrix}+2q(Y)\begin{pmatrix}-1&&&\\
&1&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&1\end{pmatrix}.

Its determinant is

and hence the volume form is given by

\displaystyle\omega_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(4iq(Y))^{l}}\mathrm{d}z_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{l}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{l}q(Y)^{l}}\mathrm{d}x_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}x_{l}\wedge\mathrm{d}y_{l}.

We write

\widehat{\mathrm{d}z_{i}}=\mathrm{d}z_{1}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z_{i-1}}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}\wedge\ldots\wedge\mathrm{d}z_{l}\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{l}

and similarly for
\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}}
. Then we have

\mathrm{d}z_{i}\wedge\widehat{\mathrm{d}z_{i}}=(4iq(Y))^{l}\omega_{g},\quad\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}\wedge\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}}=-(4iq(Y))^{l}\omega_{g}.

Now the Hodge-
\overline{*}
-operator is defined by the equality

\alpha\wedge\overline{*}\beta=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\omega_{g},

and thus we have

\overline{*}\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{h^{ji}(Y)}{(4iq(Y))^{l}}\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j}}.

Modular forms of weight
\kappa
form a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
which carries a hermitian metric given by the Petersson metric, i.e.
F(Z)\overline{G(Z)}q(Y)^{\kappa}
on the fiber. The dual bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}^{*}
can be identified using the hermitian metric with the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{-\kappa}
of modular forms of weight
-\kappa
and the mapping
F(Z)\mapsto q(Y)^{\kappa}\overline{F(Z)}
defines an anti-linear bundle isomorphism. This gives us the Hodge-
\overline{*}
-operator

\overline{*}_{\kappa}:\mathcal{A}^{p,q}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l},\mathcal{L}_{\kappa})\to\mathcal{A}^{n-p,n-q}(\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l},\mathcal{L}_{-\kappa}),\quad\overline{*}_{\kappa}(\phi\otimes F):=(\overline{*}\phi)\otimes(q(Y)^{\kappa}\overline{F}).

The weight
\kappa
Laplace operator is then given by

\Omega_{\kappa}=\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\overline{\partial}\overline{*}_{\kappa}\overline{\partial}.

Theorem 5.2
.

For a modular form
F
of weight
\kappa
the weight
\kappa
Laplace operator is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa}F(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{q(Y)^{l-\kappa}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\left(h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}y_{i}y_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}-q(Y)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{1}\partial\overline{z}_{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{l}\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\right)-i\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{l}y_{i}\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\overline{*}_{\kappa}\overline{\partial}F(Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}q(Y)^{k}\frac{\partial\overline{F(Z)}}{\partial z_{i}}\overline{*}\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(4i)^{l}}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial\overline{F(Z)}}{\partial z_{i}}\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j}}.

Applying
\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\overline{\partial}
yields

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa}F(Z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(4i)^{l}}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\overline{\partial}\left(h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial\overline{F(Z)}}{\partial z_{i}}\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(4i)^{l}}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\left(h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial\overline{F(Z)}}{\partial z_{i}}\right)\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j}\wedge\widehat{\mathrm{d}\overline{z}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(Y)^{l}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\overline{*}_{-\kappa}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}\left(h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial\overline{F(Z)}}{\partial z_{i}}\right)\omega_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{q(Y)^{l-\kappa}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\left(h^{ji}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\right)\omega_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}h^{ij}(Y)\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}+\frac{q(Y)^{l-\kappa}}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}h^{ij}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\right)\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}.

A short calculation yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q(Y)}{\partial y_{1}}=2y_{1},\quad\frac{\partial q(Y)}{\partial y_{j}}=-2y_{j},j>1,\quad\frac{\partial h^{ij}(Y)}{\partial y_{1}}=4y_{i}.

Thus, for
i=1
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}h^{ij}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left(q(Y)^{\kappa-l}\frac{\partial h^{ij}(Y)}{\partial y_{j}}+(\kappa-l)h^{ij}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l-1}\frac{\partial q(Y)}{\partial y_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\left(4q(Y)^{\kappa-l}ly_{1}+2(\kappa-l)q(Y)^{\kappa-l-1}\left((4y_{1}^{2}-2q(Y))y_{1}-4y_{1}\sum_{j=2}^{l}y_{j}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\left(4q(Y)^{\kappa-l}ly_{1}+2y_{1}(\kappa-l)q(Y)^{\kappa-l-1}\left(4y_{1}^{2}-2q(Y)+4\sum_{j=2}^{l}y_{j}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-2i\kappa q(Y)^{\kappa-l}y_{1}

and similarly for
i>1

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}h^{ij}(Y)q(Y)^{\kappa-l}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\left(4q(Y)^{\kappa-l}ly_{i}+2(\kappa-l)q(Y)^{\kappa-l-1}\left(4y_{i}y_{1}^{2}-4y_{i}\sum_{j=2}^{l}y_{j}^{2}-2q(Y)y_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=-2i\kappa q(Y)^{\kappa-l}y_{i}

and hence

\displaystyle\Omega_{\kappa}F(Z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}h^{ij}(Y)\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}-i\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{l}y_{i}\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{j=1}^{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}y_{i}y_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}-q(Y)\left(\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{1}\partial\overline{z}_{1}}-\sum_{i=2}^{l}\frac{\partial^{2}F(Z)}{\partial z_{i}\partial\overline{z}_{i}}\right)-i\kappa\sum_{i=1}^{l}y_{i}\frac{\partial F(Z)}{\partial\overline{z}_{i}}.

∎

6.
Siegel Theta Function

Let
p
be a polynomial on
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
which is homogeneous of degree
\kappa
in the positive definite variables and independent negative definite variables. For an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
we write
\nu^{+}
and
\nu^{-}
for the inverse image of
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},0)}
and
\mathbb{R}^{(0,b^{-})}
. For an element
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{\nu^{\pm}}
for the projection of
\lambda
onto
\nu^{\pm}
. The positive definite
majorant
associated to
\nu
is then given by
q_{\nu}(\lambda)=q(\lambda_{\nu^{+}})-q(\lambda_{\nu^{-}})
. For
\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L,\tau\in\mathbb{H}
and an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
we define the
Siegel theta function

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\lambda\in\gamma+L}\exp\left(\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(p)(\nu(\lambda+\beta))

\displaystyle\times e(\tau q((\lambda+\beta)_{\nu^{+}})+\overline{\tau}q((\lambda+\beta)_{\nu^{-}}-(\lambda+\beta/2,\alpha))),

where
\Delta
is the usual
Laplace operator
on
\mathbb{R}^{b^{+}+b^{-}}
and
\alpha,\beta\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Moreover we define

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

For
\alpha=\beta=0
we write

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\nu,p):=\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,0,0,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\in\gamma+L}\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta}{8\pi v}\right)(p)(\nu(\lambda))e(\tau q(\lambda_{\nu^{+}})+\overline{\tau}q(\lambda_{\nu^{-}}))

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p):=\Theta_{L}(\tau,0,0,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L}\theta_{\gamma}(\tau,\nu,p)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

Using Poisson summation one obtains

Theorem 6.1

(
[
Bor98
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

For
(M,\phi)\in\operatorname{Mp}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}),M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
we have

\Theta_{L}(M\tau,a\alpha+b\beta,c\alpha+d\beta,\nu,p)=\phi(\tau)^{b^{+}+2\kappa^{+}}\overline{\phi(\tau)}^{b^{-}}\rho_{L}(M,\phi)\Theta_{L}(\tau,\alpha,\beta,\nu,p).

In particular, for
\alpha=\beta=0
, the theta function
\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)
has weight
(\frac{b^{+}}{2}+\kappa^{+},\frac{b^{-}}{2})
.

Borcherds shows in
[
Bor98
]
that the theta function can be written as a Poincaré series. We will indicate the construction. Write
\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
for the primitive isotropic elements of
L
and let
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L),z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
. Let
N_{z}
be the level of
z
and define the lattice

K=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}.

Then
K
has signature
(b^{+}-1,b^{-}-1)
. For a vector
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{K}
for its orthogonal projection to
K\otimes\mathbb{R}
, which is given by

\lambda_{K}=\lambda-(\lambda,z)z^{\prime}+(\lambda,z)(z^{\prime},z^{\prime})z-(\lambda,z^{\prime})z.

Let
\zeta\in L
such that
(z,\zeta)=N_{z}
and write

\zeta=\zeta_{K}+N_{z}z^{\prime}+Bz

for some
B\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then we have

L=K\oplus\mathbb{Z}\zeta+\mathbb{Z}z.

Consider the sublattice

L_{0}^{\prime}=\{\lambda\in L^{\prime}|(\lambda,z)\equiv 0\bmod{N_{z}}\}\subseteq L^{\prime}

and the projection

\pi:L_{0}^{\prime}\to K^{\prime},\lambda\mapsto\pi(\lambda)=\lambda_{K}+\frac{(\lambda,z)}{N_{z}}\zeta_{K}.

This projection induces a surjective map
L_{0}^{\prime}/L\to K^{\prime}/K
which we also denote by
\pi
and we have

L_{0}^{\prime}/L=\{\lambda\in L^{\prime}/L|(\lambda,z)\equiv 0\bmod{N_{z}}\}.

For an isometry
\nu:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
we write
\omega^{\pm}
for the orthogonal complement of
z_{\nu^{\pm}}
in
\nu^{\pm}
. This yields a decomposition

L\otimes\mathbb{R}=\omega^{+}\oplus\mathbb{R}z_{\nu^{+}}\oplus\omega^{-}\oplus\mathbb{R}z_{\nu^{-}}

and for
\lambda\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
we write
\lambda_{\omega^{\pm}}
for the corresponding projections of
\lambda
onto
\omega^{\pm}
. Additionally the map

\omega:L\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})},\lambda\mapsto\nu(\lambda_{\omega^{+}}+\lambda_{\omega^{-}})

can be seen to be an isometry
K\otimes\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+}-1,b^{-}-1)}
by restriction. For a polynomial
p
on
\mathbb{R}^{(b^{+},b^{-})}
as above we now define the homogeneous polynomials
p_{\omega,h}
of degree
\kappa-h
in the positive definite variables by

p(\nu(\lambda))=\sum_{h}(\lambda,z_{\nu^{+}})^{h}p_{\omega,h}(\omega(\lambda)).

We have the following

Theorem 6.2

(
[
Bor98
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
\mu=-z^{\prime}+\frac{z_{\nu^{+}}}{2z_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}+\frac{z_{\nu^{-}}}{2z_{\nu^{-}}^{2}}\in L\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\theta_{\gamma+L}(\tau,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c,d\in\mathbb{Z}\\
c\equiv(\gamma,z)\bmod N_{z}\end{subarray}}\sum_{h}(-2iv)^{-h}

\displaystyle\times(c\overline{\tau}+d)^{h}e\left(-\frac{\lvert c\tau+d\rvert^{2}}{4ivz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}-(\gamma,z^{\prime})d+q(z^{\prime})cd\right)

\displaystyle\times\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma-cz^{\prime})}(\tau,d\mu_{K},-c\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h}).

We will need the following

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\gamma\in K^{\prime}/K
. Then

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}}\left(-\frac{mn}{N_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle=(\rho_{K}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma})\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{mz}{N_{z}}-ncz^{\prime}}\left(-\frac{amn}{N_{z}}+q(z^{\prime})acn^{2}\right).

Proof.

Write
\lambda=\gamma+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}
We first consider the case
M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)
with
c=0
. Then by Shintani’s formula
2.3

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i}^{(b^{-}-b^{+})(1-\operatorname{sgn}(d))}\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\delta_{\beta,a\lambda}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}(abq(\beta))

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i}^{(b^{-}-b^{+})(1-\operatorname{sgn}(d))}\mathfrak{e}_{a\lambda}(abq(a\lambda))

\displaystyle=\sqrt{i}^{((b^{-}-1)-(b^{+}-1))(1-\operatorname{sgn}(d))}\mathfrak{e}_{a\gamma}(abq(a\gamma))\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{amz}{N_{z}}}=(\rho_{K}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma})\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{amz}{N_{z}}}.

Multiplying by

and summing over

c\neq 0

\rho_{L}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}=C_{L}(c)\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{r\in L/cL}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\lambda,\beta+r)+d(\lambda,\lambda)}{2c}\right),

Since
L=K\oplus\mathbb{Z}\zeta\oplus\mathbb{Z}z
we can instead sum over
r+k\zeta+k^{\prime}z,r\in K/cK,k,k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}
to obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=C_{L}(c)\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in K/cK\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

\displaystyle\times e\left(\frac{2a(\beta+r,k\zeta)+a(k\zeta,k^{\prime}\zeta)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)-2(\frac{mz}{N_{z}},\beta+k\zeta)}{2c}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}}e\left(\frac{2a(\beta,k^{\prime}z)+2a(k\zeta,k^{\prime}\zeta)}{2c}\right)\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}

\displaystyle=C_{L}(c)\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in K/cK\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

\displaystyle\times e\left(\frac{2a(\beta+r,k\zeta)+a(k\zeta,k^{\prime}\zeta)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)-2(\frac{mz}{N_{z}},\beta+k\zeta)}{2c}\right)

Now the last sum vanishes by orthogonality of characters unless

in which case it sums to
c

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=C_{L}(c)c\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in K/cK\\

c\mid kN_{z}+(\beta,z)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

We now multiply this by
e\left(-\frac{mn}{N_{z}}\right)
and sum over
m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}
to obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}}\left(-\frac{mn}{N_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{L}(c)c\sum_{\beta\in L^{\prime}/L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in K/cK\\
k\in\mathbb{Z}/c\mathbb{Z}\\
c\mid kN_{z}+(\beta,z)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,k\zeta)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

The latter sum again vanishes unless

in which case it is equal to
N_{z}
. In particular we have
(z,\beta+ncz^{\prime})\equiv 0\bmod{N_{z}}
and thus
\beta+ncz^{\prime}\in L_{0}^{\prime}/L
. But every element in

\alpha+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}

\displaystyle\rho_{L}(M)\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}}\left(-\frac{mn}{N_{z}}\right)

\displaystyle=C_{K}(c)\sum_{\beta\in K^{\prime}/K}\sum_{r\in K/cK}\mathfrak{e}_{\beta}\left(\frac{a(\beta+r,\beta+r)-2(\gamma,\beta+r)+d(\gamma,\gamma)}{2c}\right)

which shows the claim.

Theorem 6.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\Theta_{K}(\tau,\omega,p_{\omega,0})\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{mz}{N_{z}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2z_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{M\in\Gamma_{\infty}\backslash\Gamma}\sum_{h}(-2i)^{-h}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{h}

\displaystyle\times\frac{(c\overline{\tau}+d)^{-\frac{b^{-}}{2}-\kappa^{-}}(c\tau+d)^{-\frac{b^{+}}{2}-\kappa^{+}}}{\operatorname{Im}(M\tau)^{h+\frac{1}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi n^{2}}{2\operatorname{Im}(M\tau)z_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\rho_{L}(M)^{-1}\left(\Theta_{K}(\tau,n\mu_{K},0,\omega,p_{\omega,h})\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{mz}{N}}\left(-\frac{mn}{N}\right)\right).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\Theta_{L}(\tau,\nu,p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{c,d\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L\\

\displaystyle\times(c\overline{\tau}+d)^{h}e\left(-\frac{\lvert c\tau+d\rvert^{2}}{4ivz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}-(\gamma,z^{\prime})d+q(z^{\prime})cd\right)

\displaystyle\times\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma-cz^{\prime})}(\tau,d\mu_{K},-c\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h})\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

We make the change
\gamma\mapsto\gamma+cz^{\prime}
and sum over coprime

to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{\gamma\in L_{0}^{\prime}/L}\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma)}(\tau,\omega,p_{\omega,0})\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2vz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}}\sum_{(c,d)=1}\sum_{h}(-2iv)^{-h}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{h}(c\overline{\tau}+d)^{h}e\left(-\frac{n^{2}\lvert c\tau+d\rvert^{2}}{4ivz_{\nu^{+}}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\sum_{\gamma\in L_{0}^{\prime}/L}\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma)}(\tau,nd\mu_{K},-nc\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h})\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+ncz^{\prime}}\left(-(\gamma,z^{\prime})nd-q(z^{\prime})n^{2}cd\right).

The elements
\gamma\in L_{0}^{\prime}/L
are represented by
\gamma+\frac{mz}{N_{z}}
for
\gamma\in K^{\prime}/K,m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}
. Hence we obtain using the transformation formula for the theta function

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in L_{0}^{\prime}/L}\theta_{K+\pi(\gamma)}(\tau,nd\mu_{K},-nc\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h})\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma+ncz^{\prime}}\left(-(\gamma,z^{\prime})nd-q(z^{\prime})n^{2}cd\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in K^{\prime}/K}\theta_{K+\gamma}(\tau,nd\mu_{K},-nc\mu_{K},\omega,p_{\omega,h})\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{e}_{\frac{mz}{N_{z}}+ncz^{\prime}}\left(-\frac{mnd}{N_{z}}-q(z^{\prime})n^{2}cd\right)

Theorem 7.1

(

\delta
. Choose such
\delta
with minimal order. Then by assumption the value in the
0
-dimensional cusp
z
is

\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}\zeta^{b}(\kappa)=0.

But of course this is also true for the
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to
c\delta
for
c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, i.e. we have

\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathfrak{v}_{bc\delta}\zeta^{b}(\kappa)=\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}\zeta^{bc^{*}}(\kappa)=0.

Rewrite this using
\zeta^{bc^{*}}(\kappa)=\zeta_{+}^{bc^{*}}(\kappa)+(-1)^{\kappa}\zeta_{+}^{-bc^{*}}(\kappa)
to obtain

\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}(\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta})\zeta_{+}^{bc^{*}}(\kappa)=0.

For a character
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
consider now

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(c)\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}(\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta})\zeta_{+}^{bc^{*}}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(b)(\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta})\sum_{c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(c)\zeta_{+}^{c^{*}}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=L(\overline{\chi},\kappa)\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(b)(\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta}).

Since
L(\overline{\chi},\kappa)\neq 0
we have

\sum_{b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(b)(\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta})=0

for all Dirichlet characters
\chi
. But that means
\mathfrak{v}_{b\delta}+(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-b\delta}=0
for all
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{z}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
contradicting the assumption
\mathfrak{v}_{\delta}\neq-(-1)^{\kappa}\mathfrak{v}_{-\delta}
.
∎

Of course, if
\pi
is not surjective, then the theta lift is not injective, i.e. the converse of the previous theorem is also true.

Proposition 8.8
.

For
\kappa
even and every
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L
isotropic there is a theta lift
\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}
for some
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
such that the holomorphic part vanishes in all
0
-dimensional cusps except for the
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to
\pm\delta
. For
\kappa
odd this is true if
\delta\neq-\delta
. Moreover, in this case we have
\Phi_{k,\mathfrak{v}}=\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}
.

Proof.

If
\delta
generates a maximal cyclic isotropic subgroup, then the holomorphic part of
\Phi_{c\delta},c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
vanishes in every
0
-dimensional cusp except for the
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to the generators of
\langle\delta\rangle
. In the
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to
b\delta
for
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
the value is given by

\frac{\Gamma(\kappa)N_{\delta}^{\kappa}}{(-2\pi i)^{\kappa}}\zeta^{cb^{*}}(\kappa).

Now consider the linear combination

\frac{(-2\pi i)^{\kappa}}{2\varphi(N_{\beta})\Gamma(\kappa)N_{\delta}^{\kappa}}\sum_{\chi}\frac{1}{L(\chi,\kappa)}\sum_{c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(c)\Phi_{c\delta},

whose value in the
0
-dimensional cusp
b\delta
for
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\varphi(N_{\beta})}\sum_{\chi}\frac{1}{L(\chi,\kappa)}\sum_{c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(c)\zeta^{cb^{*}}(\kappa)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\varphi(N_{\beta})}\sum_{\chi}\frac{\chi(b)}{L(\chi,\kappa)}\sum_{c\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\delta}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(c)\zeta^{c}(\kappa)=\frac{1}{2\varphi(N_{\beta})}\sum_{\chi}\left(\chi(b)+(-1)^{\kappa}\chi(-b)\right),

i.e. the value in the
0
-dimensional cusps vanishes except for the
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to
\delta
, where the value is
1
. Moreover, using Theorem
7.1
shows that this linear combination is in fact given by
\mathcal{G}_{\kappa,\delta}
. Now do induction over the maximal length of chains of cyclic isotropic subgroups containing
\delta
.
∎

This means in particular that for
F\in\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\pi}(\Gamma(L))
there is a theta lift
\Phi_{\mathfrak{v}}
for some
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Iso}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
whose holomorphic boundary part is given by the boundary part of
F
(observe that for
\kappa
odd the values in
0
-dimensional cusps corresponding to

with

must be zero).

Theorem 8.9

\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\pi}(\Gamma(L))=\operatorname{S}_{\kappa}(\Gamma(L))+\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\Phi}(\Gamma(L))

We obtain the Fourier expansion of the Eisenstein series

The question if this yields all holomorphic modular forms of singular weight which are Eisenstein series on the boundary will be answered in the next section. We want to mention that we can also construct these in a different way. For an invariant vector

9.
Lifting Holomorphic Orthogonal Modular Forms

Let
L
be an even lattice of signature
(2,l),l\geq 3
. For
z\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L)
and
z^{\prime}\in L^{\prime}
with
(z,z^{\prime})=1
write
K=K_{z}=L\cap z^{\perp}\cap z^{\prime\perp}
. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{l}
be a basis of
K\otimes\mathbb{R}
with
b_{1}\perp\langle b_{2},\ldots,b_{l}\rangle
and
q(b_{1})>0
. If
Z=z_{1}b_{1}+z_{2}b_{2}+z_{3}b_{3}+\ldots+z_{l}b_{l}\in K\otimes\mathbb{C}
, we write
Z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{l})
and similarly
X=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}),Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{l})
if
Z=X+iY
with
X,Y\in K\otimes\mathbb{R}
. Denote by
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
the corresponding tube domain model, where

C=\{Y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{l})\in K\otimes\mathbb{R}\mid y_{1}>0,q(Y)>0\}.

For
\lambda\in\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})
let
\sigma_{\lambda}\in O^{+}(V)
with
\sigma_{\lambda}\lambda=z
and set
\lambda^{\prime}=\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}z^{\prime}
. Define

K_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\perp}\cap\lambda^{\prime\perp}\cap L=L\cap\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}(K\otimes\mathbb{R}).

Then

\sigma_{\lambda}\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}=\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}L)_{z}\supseteq(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\rtimes\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}),

where
\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}
acts via translation and
\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})
via multiplication on
\mathbb{H}_{l}=K_{z}\otimes\mathbb{R}+iC
. A fundamental domain is given by
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{1}+i\mathcal{F}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a fundamental domain of the action
\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}
on
K_{z}\otimes\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a fundamental domain of the action
\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})
on
C
. By abuse of notation we will write
\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}\backslash K_{z}\otimes\mathbb{R}+i\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\backslash C
. Moreover, recall the map

\pi_{L}:\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L).

Let
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
be a modular form of weight
\kappa
. We define its theta lift to be (if it exists)

\Phi^{*}(\tau,F):=\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}.

Proposition
6.8
and Lemma
3.7
show that the theta lift exists for holomorphic modular forms of singular weight (in fact the lift exists for weight
\kappa<l-1
and for arbitrary weight if
F
is a cusp form). A straight forward calculation yields

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic modular form of singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
and
\mathfrak{v}\in\operatorname{Inv}(\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L])
an invariant vector. Then

\displaystyle\langle\Phi^{*}(\tau,F),\mathfrak{v}\rangle=\langle F,\Phi(Z,\mathfrak{v})\rangle,

where the left hand side denotes the Petersson inner product on invariant vectors and the right hand side denotes the Petersson inner product on holomorphic modular forms of singular weight. In particular, the theta lifts are adjoint to eachother.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
l\geq 3,\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1
. If
F
and
\Omega_{\kappa}F
are square-integrable, then the theta lift exists and we have
\Phi^{*}(\tau,\Omega_{\kappa}F)=\Delta_{0}\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
. In particular, if
F
is holomorphic, then
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
exists and is harmonic.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(L)
be a finite index subgroup which acts freely. Then
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}
is a complete connected hermitian manifold. By Corollary
6.9
,
\Theta_{L}
and
\Omega_{\kappa}\Theta_{L}
are square-integrable. Using the assumption on
F
and
\Omega_{\kappa}F
, we can apply Theorem
5.1
to square-integrable sections of the hermitian line bundle of modular forms of weight
\kappa
. This yields, using
\overline{\Omega_{\kappa}\overline{\Theta_{L}}}=\Delta_{0}\Theta_{L}
and Theorem
5.1
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{0}\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L):\Gamma]\Delta_{0}\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L):\Gamma]\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Delta_{0}\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L):\Gamma]\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\overline{\Omega_{\kappa}\overline{\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)}}q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L):\Gamma]\int_{\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}\Omega_{\kappa}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}\Omega_{\kappa}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}=\Phi^{*}(\tau,\Omega_{\kappa}F).

The other assertions follow immediately.
∎

Theorem 9.3
.

Let
F
be a holomorphic modular form of singular weight
\kappa=\frac{l}{2}-1>0
. Then its theta lift

\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)=\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

is an invariant vector given by

\frac{\Gamma(l/2)}{2(2\pi)^{l/2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)\\
\gamma=k_{\delta}\delta\end{subarray}}\zeta_{+}^{k_{\delta}}(l-\kappa)\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}a_{F,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma},

where

\displaystyle C(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})[\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}L)_{z}:\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})]C(\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{vol}(K_{\lambda})[\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}:K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(K_{\lambda})]C(\Gamma(K_{\lambda}))

for
\sigma_{\lambda}\in O^{+}(V)
with
\sigma_{\lambda}\lambda=z
and
C(\Gamma(K_{\lambda}))
is a positive constant.

Proof.

First observe that the integral converges since holomorphic modular forms of singular weight are square integrable. We have

\displaystyle\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)=\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\Theta_{L}(\tau,Z)q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\frac{l}{2}}}{2}\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}\frac{(\lambda,Z_{L})^{\kappa}}{q(Y)^{\kappa}}\exp(-2\pi vq_{Z_{L}}(\lambda))\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}(uq(\lambda))q(Y)^{\kappa}\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\frac{l}{2}}}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}\int_{\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)(\lambda,Z_{L})^{\kappa}\exp(-2\pi vq_{Z_{L}}(\lambda))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}(uq(\lambda))

and remark that the integral is bounded for
v\to\infty
and hence the lift grows polynomially. Moreover, by Lemma
9.2
it is harmonic of weight
0
and thus its growth comes from the constant Fourier coefficient. The constant Fourier coefficients are then given by (the
\lambda=0
term vanishes since
\kappa>0
)

\displaystyle\frac{v^{\frac{l}{2}}}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Gamma(L)\backslash L^{\prime}\\
q(\lambda)=0\end{subarray}}\int_{\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)(\lambda,Z_{L})^{\kappa}\exp(-2\pi vq_{Z_{L}}(\lambda))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}\mathfrak{e}_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{v^{\frac{l}{2}}}{2}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\kappa}\int_{\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}F(Z)(\lambda,Z_{L})^{\kappa}\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}q_{Z_{L}}(\lambda))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}\mathfrak{e}_{m\lambda}.

As above let
\sigma_{\lambda}\in O^{+}(V)
such that
\sigma_{\lambda}\lambda=z
and write
\lambda^{\prime}=\sigma_{\lambda}^{-1}(z^{\prime})
. Then we can rewrite the integral to (observe that
(z,Z_{L})=1
and
q_{Z_{L}}(z)=1/q(Y)
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}L)_{z}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}(F\mid_{\kappa}\sigma_{\lambda})(Z)\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/q(Y))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}.

and hence, using the Fourier expansion of
F\mid_{k}\sigma_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\sum_{\delta\in\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}^{\prime}}a_{F,\lambda}(\delta)\int_{\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}L)_{z}\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}e(\delta,Z)\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/q(Y))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}:K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(K_{\lambda})]\sum_{\delta\in\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}^{\prime}}a_{F,\lambda}(\delta)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}}e(\delta,Z)\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/q(Y))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=[\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}:K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(K_{\lambda})]\sum_{\delta\in\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}^{\prime}}a_{F,\lambda}(\delta)

\displaystyle\times\int_{\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda}\backslash K_{z}\otimes\mathbb{R}}e(\delta,X)\mathrm{d}X\int_{\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\backslash C}e(\delta,iY)\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/q(Y))\frac{\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}

\displaystyle=a_{F,\lambda}(0)\operatorname{vol}(K_{\lambda})[\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}:K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(K_{\lambda})]\int_{\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\backslash C}\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/q(Y))\frac{\mathrm{d}X\mathrm{d}Y}{q(Y)^{l}}.

Consider the diffeomorphism

\displaystyle\varphi:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{l-1}\to C,\quad(r,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l})\mapsto\sqrt{r}(\sqrt{1-q(0,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l})},y_{2},\ldots,y_{l})

with inverse

\displaystyle\varphi^{-1}:C\to[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{l-1},\quad Y\mapsto(q(Y),y_{2}/\sqrt{q(Y)},\ldots,y_{l}/\sqrt{q(Y)}).

Then we have for integrable
f:C\to\mathbb{C}

\displaystyle\int_{C}f(Y)\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{l-1}}f(\varphi(r,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l}))\lvert\det(\varphi^{\prime}(r,y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}))\rvert\mathrm{d}y_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{l}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{l-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\frac{l}{2}}f(\varphi(r,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l}))\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\lvert\det(\varphi^{\prime}(1,y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}))\rvert\mathrm{d}y_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}y_{l}.

Here
f(Y)=g(q(Y))
for
g(r)=\exp(2\pi vn^{2}/r)r^{-l}
and thus

f(\varphi(r,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l}))=g(r)=\exp(2\pi vn^{2}/r)r^{-l}.

Hence we obtain

\displaystyle a_{F,\lambda}(0)\operatorname{vol}(K_{\lambda})[\Gamma(L)_{\lambda}:K_{\lambda}\rtimes\Gamma(K_{\lambda})]

\displaystyle\times\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-2\pi vm^{2}/r)r^{-\frac{l}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{d}r}{r}\int_{\Gamma(\sigma_{\lambda}K_{\lambda})\backslash\mathbb{R}^{l-1}}\lvert\det(\varphi^{\prime}(1,y_{2},\ldots,y_{l}))\rvert\mathrm{d}y_{2},\ldots,y_{l}

\displaystyle=a_{F,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)(2\pi v)^{-\frac{l}{2}}m^{-l}\Gamma(l/2).

Hence the constant Fourier coefficient is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(l/2)}{2(2\pi)^{l/2}}\sum_{\lambda\in\Gamma(L)\backslash\operatorname{Iso}_{0}(L^{\prime})}a_{F,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}m^{\kappa-l}\mathfrak{e}_{m\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(l/2)}{2(2\pi)^{l/2}}\sum_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in\operatorname{Iso}(L^{\prime}/L)\\
\gamma=k_{\delta}\delta\end{subarray}}\zeta_{+}^{k_{\delta}}(l-\kappa)\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}a_{F,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}.

In particular, this is independent of
v
. Hence
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
is bounded and since it is harmonic of weight
0
it is an invariant vector.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

Observe that
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
only depends on the values in the
0
-dimensional cusps. In particular it vanishes on functions that are zero in every
0
-dimensional cusp.

Remark 9.5
.

Let
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
be a cusp form of arbitrary weight. Then
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
exists and one can show as above that it is harmonic. Moreover, the same calculation as in the previous proof shows that the constant Fourier coefficient vanishes. Hence,
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
decays exponentially and hence is square-integrable and harmonic of weight
k
. Thus,
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
is a holomorphic cusp form. This reproduces the result of
[
Oda78
]
.

Corollary 9.6
.

For
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L
isotropic let
a_{F,\delta}(0):=\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}a_{F,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)
. Then
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
vanishes if and only if
a_{F,\delta}(0)
vanishes for all isotropic
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L
. In particular, if
\pi_{L}
is injective, then the theta lift
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
vanishes if and only if
F
vanishes in every
0
-dimensional cusp.

Proof.

If
\Phi^{*}(\tau,F)
vanishes, then the coefficient of
\mathfrak{e}_{\gamma}
, given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\in L^{\prime}/L\\
q(\delta)=0\\
\gamma=k_{\delta}\delta\end{subarray}}\zeta_{+}^{k_{\delta}}(l-\kappa)a_{F,\delta}(0),

vanishes for all isotropic
\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L
. In particular, if
\gamma\in L^{\prime}/L
is isotropic with maximal order such that
a_{F,\gamma}(0)\neq 0
, then for
k^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
the coefficient of
\mathfrak{e}_{k^{\prime}\gamma}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\zeta_{+}^{k^{*}}(l-\kappa)a_{F,kk^{\prime}\gamma}(0)=\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\zeta_{+}^{k^{\prime}k^{*}}(l-\kappa)a_{F,k\gamma}(0)

vanishes. Thus, for all Dirichlet characters
\chi:(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}
, the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{k^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k^{\prime})\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\zeta_{+}^{k^{\prime}k^{*}}(l-\kappa)a_{F,k\gamma}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k^{\prime})\zeta_{+}^{k^{\prime}}(l-\kappa)\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k)a_{F,k\gamma}(0)

\displaystyle=L(l-\kappa,\chi)\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k)a_{F,k\gamma}(0)

vanishes. Now
L(l-\kappa,\chi)\neq 0
and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\chi(k)a_{F,k\gamma}(0)=0

for all Dirichlet characters
\chi
. But then we must have
a_{F,k\gamma}(0)=0
for all
k
, since Dirichlet characters form an orthogonal basis of
(\mathbb{Z}/N_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
∎

Corollary 9.7
.

The theta lift
\Phi
surjects onto the space of holomorphic modular forms of singular weight which are Eisenstein series on the boundary and whose value in a
0
-dimensional cusp only depends on its image in
L^{\prime}/L
, i.e. we have
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\Phi}(\Gamma(L))=\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\pi}(\Gamma(L))
.

Proof.

Let
F:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
be a holomorphic modular form which is an Eisenstein series on the boundary whose value in a
0
-dimensional cusp only depends on its image in
L^{\prime}/L
. Since
\Phi
and
\Phi^{*}
are adjoint to each other by Lemma
9.1
, we can write
F=\Phi_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(Z)+G
for an invariant vector
\mathfrak{v}
and a holomorphic modular form
G:\mathbb{H}_{l}\to\mathbb{C}
of singular weight with
\Phi^{*}(\tau,G)=0
. By Corollary
9.6
we have
a_{G,\delta}(0)=0
for all isotropic
\delta\in L^{\prime}/L
. Moreover, the value of
G=F-\Phi_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(Z)
in a
0
-dimensional cusp only depends on its image in
L^{\prime}/L
. Hence we have

0=a_{G,\delta}(0)=\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}a_{G,\lambda}(0)C(\lambda)=a_{G,\tilde{\lambda}}(0)\sum_{\lambda\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)}C(\lambda)

for some
\tilde{\lambda}\in\pi_{L}^{-1}(\delta)
. But then
a_{G,\tilde{\lambda}}(0)=0
and hence
G
vanishes in every
0
-dimensional cusp and thus is a cusp form on the boundary. But since
F
and
\Phi_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(Z)
are Eisenstein series on the boundary,
G=F-\Phi_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(Z)
must vanish on the boundary and hence
G
is a cusp form. Since we are in singular weight,
G
must vanish and thus
F=\Phi_{0,\mathfrak{v}}(Z)
.
∎

Corollary 9.8
.

If
\pi_{L}
is injective, then the theta lift
\Phi
surjects onto the space of holomorphic modular forms of singular weight which are Eisenstein series on the boundary, i.e. we have
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\Phi}(\Gamma(L))=M_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{L}
is injective we have
M_{\kappa}^{\pi}(\Gamma(L))=M_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
.
∎

Corollary 9.9
.

If
L
is a maximal lattice of Witt rank
2
, then the space
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
is either
1
-dimensional (if
L
is unimodular) or
0
-dimensional (if
L
is not unimodular). Moreover, if
\kappa=2,4,6,8,10,14
, i.e.
l=6,10,14,18,22,30
, then we have
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}(\Gamma(L))=\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
. In the other cases there could be additional holomorphic modular forms that are cusp forms on the boundary.

Proof.

Let
L
be maximal of signature
(2,l),l\geq 6
. Then
L
splits two hyperbolic planes over
\mathbb{Z}
and hence the map
\pi_{L}
is bijective. Thus the space of holomorphic modular forms of singular weight which are linear combinations of Eisenstein series on the boundary has the same dimension as the space of invariant vectors in
\mathbb{C}[L^{\prime}/L]
. The latter is either
0
-dimensional (if
L
is not unimodular) or
1
-dimensional (if
L
is unimodular). Since we are in singular weight, there are no cusp forms. Since the
1
-dimensional boundary components of
\Gamma(L)\backslash\mathbb{H}_{l}
are given by
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathbb{H}
and since there are no cusp forms of weight
2,4,6,8,10,14
for
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we obtain
\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}(\Gamma(L))=\operatorname{M}_{\kappa}^{\partial\operatorname{Eis}}(\Gamma(L))
if
l=6,10,14,18,22,30
.
∎

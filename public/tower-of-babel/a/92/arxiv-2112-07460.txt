Abstract
. We consider infinite programming problems with constraint sets defined by systems of infinite number of inequalities and equations given by continuously differentiable functions defined on Banach spaces. In the approach proposed here we represent these systems with the help of coefficients in a given Schauder basis. We prove the Abadie condition under the new infinite-dimensional Relaxed Constant Rank Constraint Qualification Plus and we discuss the existence of Lagrange multipliers. The main tools are: Rank Theorem and Ljusternik Theorem.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

(b_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}

.

If

is finite-dimensional, the notion of Schauder basis coincides with the linear basis (see
[

).

We say that sequence

is a

is Schauder basis of
\overline{\operatorname{span}}(b_{i},i\in\mathbb{N})
, where
\overline{\operatorname{span}}
denotes the closure of the span and the closure is taken in the strong topology of the space.

Definition 2.2
.

For a given, possibly nonconvex, set

and

\mathcal{T}_{Q}(x):=\{d\in E\ |\ \exists\ \{x_{k}\}\subset Q\,\ \{t_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}\,\ x_{k}\rightarrow x,\,t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ (x_{k}-x)/t_{k}\rightarrow d\}.

The cone

(2.2)

\mathcal{F}
at

.

Fact 2.1

\displaystyle 0=

Hence

Df(x_{0})(e)=\sum_{j\in J_{1}}Df_{j}(x_{0})(e)b_{j}=\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\beta_{j}(e)b_{j}.

Proposition 4.7

Let

be a Banach space,

,

open, be a

Assume that CRC+ holds for
(f_{i})_{i\in J_{1}}

at
x_{0}
with a neighbourhood

with index set

.

Then

.

Proof.

By
1.
of CRC+, for any

for any

,

can be expressed by

Since

\displaystyle Df_{i}(x)(e_{1})=\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\beta_{j}(e_{1})Df_{i}(x)Df_{j}(x_{0})^{*},\quad i\in J_{2}.

Since

is a
C^{1}
mapping,

is a continuous (linear) mapping. Now we show that

,

, is a bijection.

1. Step: injectivity.
Let

.
Let us take

.

2. Step: surjectivity. Let

of CRC+,

\displaystyle Df_{l}(x)(e)=\sum_{i\in J_{2}}\alpha_{i}^{l}(x)Df_{i}(x)(e),\quad l\in J_{1}\setminus J_{2}.

For

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Df_{i}(x)(e)&=\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\beta_{j}(e)(Df_{i}(x)Df_{j}(x_{0})^{*})\\
&=Df_{i}(x)(\sum_{j\in J_{2}}\beta_{j}(e)Df_{j}(x_{0})^{*})\end{aligned}

and for

(4.13)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} Df_{l}(x)(e)&=\sum_{i\in J_{2}}\alpha_{i}^{l}(x)Df_{i}(x)(e)\\

The following condition will be used in our main theorem.

(H1)

For all

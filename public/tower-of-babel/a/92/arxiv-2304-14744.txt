Jean-Baptiste Casteras, Ilkka Holopainen, Léonard Monsaingeon

Abstract
. We construct a blowing-up solution for the energy critical focusing biharmonic nonlinear Schrödinger equation in infinite time in dimension N ≥ 13 N\geq 13 . Our solution is radially symmetric and converges asymptotically to the sum of two bubbles. The scale of one of the bubble is of order 1 1 whereas the other one is of order | t | − 2 N − 12 |t|^{-\frac{2}{N-12}} . Moreover, the phase between the two bubbles form a right angle.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the following fourth order Schrödinger equation

(1.1)

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta^{2}u+\alpha\Delta+|u|^{p}u=0,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
N>4
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<p\leq 2^{\ast}-2=\frac{4}{N-4}
.
The fourth order term in equation (
1.1
) has been introduced by Karpman and
Shagalov
[
26
]
. It allows to regularize and stabilize solutions to the classical
Schrödinger equation as observed through numerical simulations by Fibich, Ilan,
and Papanicolaou
[
17
]
. We mention briefly a few selected results for (
1.2
).
Well-posedness has been established by Pausader
[
33
,
32
]
(using the dispersive estimates of

[
1
]
) as well as some scattering results (we also refer to
[
18
]
and to
[
8
]
for related results on Cartan-Hadamard manifolds). Recently, Boulenger and Lenzmann obtained (in)finite time blowing-up results in
[
6
]
. Standing wave,
u(t,x)=e^{i\beta t}v(x)
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and traveling wave,
u(t,x)=e^{i\beta t}v(x+tV)
with
V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, solutions to (
1.1
) have been studied in
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
7
]
. Since we will be interested in blowing-up solutions let us only mention that under suitable assumptions radially symmetric ground state solutions
U
were proven to be unstable by blow-up in the mass critical or super-critical regime
p\geq 8/N
in
[
4
]
. Namely, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\tilde{U}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\|\tilde{U}-U\|_{H^{2}}<\varepsilon
and the solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
u(0)=\tilde{U}
blows-up in the
H^{2}
-norm. This result is based on the local virial inequality proved by Boulenger and Lenzmann which will be stated below.

In the following, we will focus on the pure biharmonic Schrödinger equation in the energy critical regime

(1.2)

i\partial_{t}u-\Delta^{2}u+|u|^{2^{\ast}-2}u=0,\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We will also only work with radially symmetric functions. Before proceeding let us recall that the energy

E(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\dfrac{1}{2^{\ast}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2^{\ast}}dx,

is a conserved quantity by equation (
1.2
). We define

v_{\lambda}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}}v\big(\frac{x}{\lambda}\big).

Notice that, for any
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{2}
, we have

E((u_{0})_{\lambda})=E(u_{0}).

Equation (
1.2
) is invariant under the same scaling in the sense that if
u(t)
is a solution to (
1.2
) then
U(t)=u(t_{0}+\frac{t}{\lambda^{2}})_{\lambda}
is also a solution to (
1.2
) such that
U(0)=(u_{0})_{\lambda}
. Observe that
E
is defined on
\dot{H}^{2}
functions thanks to the Sobolev inequality, for some constant
C_{d}
, there holds

\|u\|_{L^{2^{\ast}}}\leq C_{d}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{2}}.

The extremizer
W
of this inequality will play a fundamental role in the following. It is known that it is unique (up to scaling and translation) in
\dot{H}^{2}
and solves the equation

\Delta^{2}W=|W|^{2^{\ast}-2}W\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

In fact, it is explicitely given by

W(x)=\dfrac{C_{N}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}}},

with
C_{N}=(N(N-4)(N^{2}-4))^{\frac{N-4}{8}}
. It is also known (see
[
30
]
) that it is non-degenerate in the sense that the kernel of the operator

\Delta^{2}\cdot-\dfrac{N+4}{N-4}W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\cdot

restricted to radial functions belonging to
\dot{H}^{2}
is given by
\big((N-4)/2+x\cdot\nabla\big)W
. In this setting, the result of Boulenger and Lenzmann
[
6
]
reads as follows: suppose that either
E[u_{0}]<0
or
E[u_{0}]\geq 0
,
E[u_{0}]<E[W]
, and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}>\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
, then the solution
u\in C^{0}([0,T);\dot{H}^{2})
blows-up in finite time in the sense
T<\infty
and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(t,x)|^{\frac{2(d+4)}{d-4}}dxdt=\infty.

Let us also recall that global well-posedness was established by Pausader
[
33
]
for initial data
u_{0}
satisfying
E[u_{0}]<E[W]
and
\|\Delta u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\|\Delta W\|_{L^{2}}
.

Our goal in this paper is to construct a global radial solutions which converges in the energy space to a sum of two bubbles
W_{\lambda_{i}}
. We will see that one of the bubbles blows-up in the sense that
\lambda_{1}(t)\rightarrow 0
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, whereas the second one will not, i.e.
\lambda_{2}(t)\rightarrow c
for some constant
c>0
. More precisely, our result reads as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 13
. There exists a radially symmetric solution
u:(-\infty,T_{0}]\rightarrow\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{C})
of (
1.2
) such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\|u(t)-(-iW+W_{\tilde{C}|t|^{-\frac{2}{N-12}}})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}=0,

where
\tilde{C}
is an explicit constant; see (
3.1
).

Such a result was first obtained for the classical energy critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation by Jendrej
[
19
]
. Our paper owes a lot to his article, in fact, the core method of our proof is the same as his.
We also refer to
[
22
]
where they constructed the same kind of solutions for the energy critical equivariant wave maps equation and the critical wave equation. We emphasize that Theorem
1.1
is the first constructive blow-up result for 4NLS. We conjecture that there is a certain rigidity in our construction: if we assume that
u:(-\infty,T_{0}]\rightarrow\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{C})
is a radially symmetric solution of (
1.2
) such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\|u(t)-(e^{i\theta_{1}(t)}W_{\lambda_{1}(t)}+e^{i\theta_{2}(t)}W_{\lambda_{2}(t)})\|_{\dot{H}^{2}}=0,

then
\theta_{1}(t)\rightarrow-i
,
\theta_{2}(t)\rightarrow 0
,
\lambda_{1}(t)\rightarrow 1
, and
\lambda_{2}(t)|t|^{\frac{2}{N-12}}\rightarrow c
for some constant
c
. A result in this direction has been obtained in
[
23
]
for the wave maps equation. A related (very partial) result for the critical wave equation has been obtained by Jendrej
[
21
]
. He showed that there is no radial solution decomposing asymptotically as a sum of two bubbles with opposite signs.

Our result is also related to the so-called soliton resolution conjecture. Roughly speaking, a solution bounded in an appropriate sense should decomposes asymptotically into a sum of bubbles at different scales and a solution to the linear equation called a radiation term. This conjecture is completely open for the
Schrödinger equation but it was proved to hold for the radial critical wave equation very recently; see
[
24
]
(which generalizes previous works of Duyckaerts, Kenig and Merle
[
15
,
13
,
14
,
11
]
in odd dimension as well as
[
10
]
resp.
[
9
]
of the same authors along with Martel resp. Collot for the dimension 4 resp. 6). In relation to this conjecture, let us point out that a solution decomposing asymptotically in finite time as a sum of a radiation plus a bubble has been constructed in
[
20
]
for the critical wave equation and in
[
25
]
for the critical wave maps equation. Very interestingly, in this case, the scale of the bubble depends on the behavior of the radiation at
0
. So it is possible by playing with the radiation to obtain a continuum of blowing-up speeds. We refer to
[
29
,
28
]
and
[
31
]
for related results using a different method based on distorted Fourier transforms.

As already said the proof of Theorem
1.1
follows closely
[
19
]
. The solution
u
will be obtained by taking a weak limit, as
T_{n}\to-\infty
, of a sequence
u_{n}:[T_{n},T_{0}]\rightarrow\dot{H}^{2}
of solutions to (
1.2
) close to a two-bubble solution. So our main goal is to obtain uniform energy estimate on the sequence
u_{n}
.

We are looking for solution to (
1.2
) of the form

u(t)=e^{i\zeta(t)}W_{\mu(t)}+e^{i\theta(t)}W_{\lambda(t)}+g(t),

for some parameters
\zeta,\mu,\theta,\lambda
and a function
g
assumed to be small in
\dot{H}^{2}
norm and satisfy suitable orthogonality conditions. More precisely, we assume that
g
is orthogonal to the kernel of

e^{i\zeta(t)}W_{\mu(t)}+e^{i\theta(t)}W_{\lambda(t)},

that is, to
e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},\ e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},\ i\varepsilon^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}
, and
ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}
, where

\Lambda v:=-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=1}(v_{\lambda}).

To determine the value of the different parameters, we perform a modulation analysis, namely we differenciate with respect to time the orthogonality relations satisfied by
g
. This way, we obtain a system of
4
equations involving the four parameters we want to determine. Intuitively, we first determine
\lambda(t)
(we assume that
\lambda(t)\ll\mu(t)
) such that
\lambda^{\prime}(t)\approx\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}
, i.e.
\lambda(t)\approx|t|^{-\frac{2}{N-12}}
. More precisely, we find that
\lambda(t)
has to satisfy (
3.10
). We point out that this relation involves the parameters of the two bubbles as well as the fact that their phases form a ’right angle’ i.e. differ by a factor
i
. We already see at this point that the condition
N\geq 13
appears. We should also be able to deal with the case
N=12
but in this case,
\lambda(t)
should decay exponentially. In view of the estimate of the energy (
4.1
), we choose
|\zeta+\frac{\pi}{2}|\approx|\mu-1|\approx|\theta|^{3}
. From a technical point of view, we establish bounds on the parameters under some bootstrap assumptions. In order to close the estimates, we need to improve these bounds. The main difficulty is to improve the estimate of
g
. To do so, we use the method introduced by Jendrej which consists in expanding the energy of
E(u)
and using the coercivity of the energy near a bubble. The main step to implement this strategy is to estimate
\theta(t)
. Thanks to the modulation analysis, we see that
\theta
satisfies

\theta^{\prime}(t)+\frac{C_{2}}{2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\theta(t)\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-12}{2}}-\dfrac{K(t)}{2\lambda(t)^{4}\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\approx 0

for some explicit
K
satisfying
|K|\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}
(see (
3.11
)). To close the estimate on
\theta
, we need to get rid of the term

\dfrac{K(t)}{2\lambda(t)^{4}\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

This is done by introducing a localized virial correction to
\theta(t)
. Such virial was already used by Boulenger and Lenzmann to prove their blow-up results. Finally, we use the Brouwer fixed point theorem to deal with the linear instabilities of the flow i.e. the terms
a_{i}^{\pm}
; see Lemma
3.1
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce notation and recall some well-known facts. We begin by recalling the definition of the energy functional

E(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u)dx,

where we set
F(z)=\dfrac{N-4}{2N}|z|^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}
. We also let
f(z)=|z|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}z
. A direct computation gives that

f^{\prime}(z)z_{1}=|z|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\big(z_{1}+\frac{8}{N-4}z\mathcal{R}(\frac{z_{1}}{z})\big).

We denote by
W
the unique (up to scaling)
\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
solution to
\Delta^{2}u=f(u)
. It is given explicitely by

W=C_{N}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}},

where
C_{N}=\big(N(N-4)(N^{2}-4)\big)^{\frac{N-4}{8}}
. We denote by
u_{\lambda}
the scaling leaving invariant this equation namely, for any function
u\in\dot{H}^{2}
,

u_{\lambda}(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{(N-4)/2}}u(\frac{x}{\lambda}).

We set

\Lambda v=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=1}v_{\lambda}=\left(\frac{N-4}{2}+x\cdot\nabla\right)v.

More generally, we define

\Lambda_{s}:=\frac{N}{2}-s+x\cdot\nabla

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence
\Lambda=\Lambda_{2}
above.
Using the result of
[
30
]
(
W
is non-degenerate), we deduce that

\langle g,L^{-}g\rangle\geq 0,\ \ker L^{-}=\Span(W),

and

\ker L^{+}=\Span(\Lambda W),

where

L^{+}=\Delta^{2}-\frac{N+4}{N-4}W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\text{ and }L^{-}=\Delta^{2}-W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}.

We denote the standard complex
L^{2}
scalar product by

\langle v,w\rangle=\mathcal{R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\overline{v(x)}w(x)dx

for any
v,w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{C})
.
We will also denote by
\dot{H}^{2}_{rad}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\mathcal{E}
the space of radial function belong to
\dot{H}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We equipped it with the usual norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Delta u|^{2}dx.

Let
Z_{\theta,\lambda}
be the linearisation of our equation at the function
e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}
multiplied by
-i
, namely

Z_{\theta,\lambda}=-i\Delta^{2}+if^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}).

Denoting
g_{1}=\mathcal{R}g
and
g_{2}=\mathcal{I}g
, we see that

Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda})=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(L^{-}g_{2}-iL^{+}g_{1})_{\lambda}.

Using this last formula and recalling that
W
(resp.
\Lambda W
) is in the kernel of
L^{-}
(resp.
L^{+}
), we get that

Z_{\theta,\lambda}(ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(L^{-}W)_{\lambda}=0

and

Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda})=\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(-iL^{+}\Lambda W)_{\lambda}=0.

Integrating by parts, we also have that

\displaystyle\langle e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda})\rangle=\langle e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(L^{-}g_{2}-iL^{+}g_{1})_{\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle W,L^{-}g_{2}\rangle=\langle L^{-}W,g_{2}\rangle=0

and

\displaystyle\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},\frac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(L^{-}g_{2}-iL^{+}g_{1})_{\lambda}\rangle

(2.1)

\displaystyle=-\langle\Lambda W,L^{+}g_{1}\rangle=-\langle L^{+}\Lambda W,g_{1}\rangle=0.

Adapting the proof of
[
16
]
(see section
7
), we can prove that there exist
Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)}\in S
(the space of Schwartz functions) and
\nu>0
such that

(2.2)

L^{+}Y^{(1)}=-\nu Y^{(2)},\ L^{-}Y^{(2)}=\nu Y^{(1)}.

We can assume that
\|Y^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}}=\|Y^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Next, we define

\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+}=\dfrac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(Y_{\lambda}^{(2)}+iY_{\lambda}^{(1)})\text{ and }\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-}=\dfrac{e^{i\theta}}{\lambda^{4}}(Y_{\lambda}^{(2)}-iY_{\lambda}^{(1)}).

For later purpose, notice that
\|\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{\pm}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}}\lesssim 1
.
As previously, decomposing
g
as
g=g_{1}+ig_{2}
, we have

(2.3)

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+},e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle Y^{(2)},g_{1}\rangle+\langle Y^{(1)},g_{2}\rangle

and

(2.4)

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-},e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle Y^{(2)},g_{1}\rangle-\langle Y^{(1)},g_{2}\rangle.

Recalling once more that
W\in\ker L^{-}
and
\Lambda W\in\ker L^{+}
and integrating by parts, we get

\langle W,Y^{(1)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\nu}\langle W,L^{-}Y^{(2)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\nu}\langle L^{-}W,Y^{(2)}\rangle=0

and

\langle\Lambda W,Y^{(2)}\rangle=-\frac{1}{\nu}\langle\Lambda W,L^{+}Y^{(1)}\rangle=-\frac{1}{\nu}\langle L^{+}(\Lambda W),Y^{(1)}\rangle=0.

Plugging these expressions into (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we obtain

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+},ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-},ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle=0,

and

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle=0.

Since
Y^{(2)}\neq W
and
\langle g,L^{-}g\rangle>0
if
g\notin\Span(W)
, we deduce that

\langle Y^{(1)},Y^{(2)}\rangle=\frac{1}{\nu}\langle Y^{(2)},L^{-}Y^{(2)}\rangle>0.

Finally, one can check that
\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{\pm}
are eigenfunctions of
Z^{\ast}_{\theta,\lambda}
(the adjoint operator of
Z_{\theta,\lambda}
with respect to our scalar product) with eigenvalues
\pm\frac{\nu}{\lambda^{4}}
, namely

(2.5)

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+},Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda})=\frac{\nu}{\lambda^{4}}\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{+},e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda}\rangle

and

(2.6)

\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-},Z_{\theta,\lambda}(e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda})=-\frac{\nu}{\lambda^{4}}\langle\alpha_{\theta,\lambda}^{-},e^{i\theta}g_{\lambda}\rangle.

We finish this section with a simple lemma based on Taylor’s expansions.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 13
. For any
z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,2,3
, we have

(2.7)

|f^{\prime}(z_{1}+z_{2})-f^{\prime}(z_{1})|\lesssim|f^{\prime}(z_{2})|,\ |f^{\prime}(z_{1}+z_{2})-f^{\prime}(z_{1})|\lesssim|z_{1}|^{-\frac{N-12}{N-4}}|z_{2}|,\ if\ z_{1}\neq 0,

(2.8)

|f(z_{1}+z_{2})-f(z_{1})|\lesssim|f^{\prime}(z_{1})||z_{2}|+|f(z_{2})|,

(2.9)

|f(z_{1}+z_{2})-f(z_{1})-f^{\prime}(z_{1})z_{2}|\lesssim f(|z_{2}|),

(2.10)

|f(z_{1}+z_{2})-f(z_{1})-f^{\prime}(z_{1})z_{2}|\lesssim|z_{1}|^{-\frac{N-12}{N-4}}|z_{2}|^{2},

(2.11)

|F(z_{1}+z_{2})-F(z_{1})-\mathcal{R}(\overline{f(z_{1})}z_{2})|\lesssim|f^{\prime}(z_{1})||z_{2}|^{2}+|F(z_{2})|,

(2.12)

|F(z_{1}+z_{2})-F(z_{1})-\mathcal{R}(\overline{f(z_{1})}z_{2})-\mathcal{R}(\overline{f^{\prime}(z_{1})z_{2}}z_{2})|\lesssim|F(z_{2})|.

3.
Modulation analysis

In this section, we are going to perfom a modulation analysis. This will give us a precise idea of what should be requiered on our parameters. Let us recall that we are looking for a solution
u
be of the form

u(t)=e^{i\zeta(t)}W_{\mu(t)}+e^{i\theta(t)}W_{\lambda(t)}+g(t)

with
|\zeta+\pi/2|+|\mu-1|+|\theta|+\lambda+\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\ll 1
.
Differentiating this expression with respect to
t
, we get

\partial_{t}u=\zeta^{\prime}ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}+\theta^{\prime}ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}+\partial_{t}g.

On the other hand, using that
-\Delta^{2}W_{\mu}+f(W_{\mu})=-\Delta^{2}W_{\lambda}+f(W_{\lambda})=0
, we have

-i\Delta^{2}u+if(u)=-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big).

We deduce from the two previous lines that
g
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g

\displaystyle=-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)

\displaystyle-\zeta^{\prime}ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}-\theta^{\prime}ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}.

We set

C_{1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}dx,

C_{2}=\dfrac{N+4}{N-4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda Wdx,

and

(3.1)

\tilde{C}=\left(\frac{4\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{(N-12)C_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{N-12}}.

We also impose that
g
satisfies the following orthogonality conditions

(3.2)

\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle=\langle-e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle=\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle=\langle-e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},g\rangle=0.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
c>0
be an arbitrarily small constant. Let
T_{0}=T_{0}(c)<0
with
|T_{0}|
large enough and
T<T_{1}\leq T_{0}
. Suppose that for
T\leq t\leq T_{1}
, we have

(3.3)

|\zeta(t)+\frac{\pi}{2}|\leq|t|^{-\frac{3}{N-12}},

(3.4)

|\mu(t)-1|\leq|t|^{-\frac{3}{N-12}},

(3.5)

|\theta(t)|\leq|t|^{-\frac{1}{N-12}},

(3.6)

|\lambda(t)-\tilde{C}|t|^{-\frac{2}{N-12}}|\leq|t|^{-\frac{5}{2(N-12)}},

(3.7)

\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}.

Then, for
T\leq t\leq T_{1}
, there hold

(3.8)

|\zeta^{\prime}(t)|\leq c|t|^{-\frac{N-5}{N-12}},

(3.9)

|\mu^{\prime}(t)|\leq c|t|^{-\frac{N-5}{N-12}},

(3.10)

\left|\lambda^{\prime}(t)-\frac{C_{1}}{2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-10}{2}}\right|\leq c|t|^{-\frac{2N-19}{2(N-12)}},

and

(3.11)

\left|\theta^{\prime}(t)+\frac{C_{2}}{2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\theta(t)\lambda(t)^{\frac{N-12}{2}}-\frac{K(t)}{2\lambda(t)^{4}\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right|\leq c|t|^{-\frac{N-11}{N-12}},

where

K=-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})g\rangle.

Let us notice for later purpose that, since
|K|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}
, a simple consequence of (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) is that

(3.12)

|\theta^{\prime}(t)|+\frac{|\lambda^{\prime}(t)|}{\lambda(t)}\lesssim|t|^{-1}.

Proof.

Differentiating in time the orthogonality conditions (
3.2
), we will get a linear system of the following form:

\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}&M_{12}&M_{13}&M_{14}\\
M_{21}&M_{22}&M_{23}&M_{24}\\
M_{31}&M_{32}&M_{33}&M_{34}\\
M_{41}&M_{42}&M_{43}&M_{44}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mu^{4}\zeta^{\prime}\\
\mu^{3}\mu^{\prime}\\
\lambda^{4}\theta^{\prime}\\
\lambda^{3}\lambda^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}B_{1}\\
B_{2}\\
B_{3}\\
B_{4}\end{pmatrix}.

In a first time, we will focus on the first row of this system.

First row.

Differentiating
\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle=0
with respect to time, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle=-\zeta^{\prime}\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle+\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},\partial_{t}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}(-\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle)+\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}(\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle)

\displaystyle+\theta^{\prime}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},-ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},-i\Delta^{2}g+i(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}))\rangle.

Noticing that
\langle-\Lambda W_{\mu},W_{\mu}\rangle=2\|W_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=2\mu^{4}\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we infer that

\displaystyle M_{11}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\big(-\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{12}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\big(\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)=O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})=O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{13}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},-ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle=O(1),

and

\displaystyle M_{14}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle=O(1).

Consider next

B_{1}=-\big\langle ie^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)\big\rangle.

Using (
2
), this can be rewritten as

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})g\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})g\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},(f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}))g\rangle

\displaystyle=B_{11}+B_{12}+B_{13}.

We will show that

\displaystyle|B_{11}|

\displaystyle=|\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2},

\displaystyle|B_{12}|

\displaystyle=|\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\rangle|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}},

and

\displaystyle|B_{13}|

\displaystyle=|\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},(f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}))g\rangle|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

Therefore, we will get the following estimate for
B_{1}

|B_{1}|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-4}{N-12}}.

Let us start by the estimation of
|B_{11}|
. Since
\zeta\approx-\pi/2
and
\theta\approx 0
, there holds
|e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}|\gtrsim W_{\mu}
. So, Taylor’s expansion (
2.10
) gives

|f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})g|\lesssim W_{\mu}^{-\frac{N-12}{N-4}}|g|^{2}.

Since
|\Lambda W|\lesssim W
, using Hölder’s and Sobolev’s inequalities, we get

\displaystyle|B_{11}|=

\displaystyle|\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})g)\rangle|

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|W_{\mu}|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}|g|^{2}dx\leq\|W_{\mu}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}}^{2}\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}.

Next let us estimate
|B_{12}|
. Using a Taylor’s expansion see (
2.8
), we find

|f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})|\lesssim W_{\mu}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}W_{\lambda}+|f(W_{\lambda})|.

Recalling that
W(x)=c_{N}(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{4-N}{2}}
and
f(u)=|u|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}u
, we observe that
f(W)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Moreover, using that
u_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{-\frac{N-4}{2}}u(x/\lambda)
, one can check that

\|f(W_{\lambda})\|_{L^{1}}=\dfrac{1}{\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W(x/\lambda)^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}dx\approx\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

Since
|\Lambda W_{\mu}|\lesssim 1
, this takes care of the term involving
|f(W_{\lambda})|
. Concerning the one involving
W_{\mu}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}W_{\lambda}
, we will split it into two parts. First, when
|x|\leq 1
, we have

\|W_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}(|x|\leq 1)}=\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}\|W\|_{L^{1}(|x|\leq\lambda^{-1})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda^{-1}}r^{4-N}r^{N-1}dr\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

Using once more that
|\Lambda W_{\mu}|\lesssim 1
, we get the desired estimate. Finally, for
|x|\geq 1
, we have
\|W_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(|x|\geq 1)}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}
and
|\Lambda W_{\mu}||W_{\mu}|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}
is bounded in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Combining the previous estimates, we have shown that

|B_{12}|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

Finally, let us turn to the estimate of
|B_{13}|
. We will also split the integral into two parts. When
|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}
, observe that

|f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})|\lesssim W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}.

So, in this region, we have

\displaystyle|\langle 1_{\{|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\}}e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},(f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}))g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim|\int_{\{|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\}}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}|g|dx|\lesssim\|W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda)}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\|W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(|x|\leq 1/\sqrt{\lambda})}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

On the other hand in the region
|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda}
, using Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle|\langle 1_{\{|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda}\}}e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},(f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}))g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda W_{\mu}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{4}}(|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda})}\|W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda})}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\int_{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}r^{-2N}r^{N-1}dr)^{\frac{N-4}{2N}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

This proves that

|B_{13}|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

Next, let us deal with the second row.

Second row.

This time, we differentiate
\langle-e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle=0
with respect to time. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle-e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle=-\zeta^{\prime}\langle ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle+\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle-\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},\partial_{t}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}\big(\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)+\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}\big(-\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle+\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)

\displaystyle+\theta^{\prime}\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\langle-e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle

\displaystyle-\big\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)\big\rangle.

We deduce from this expression that

\displaystyle M_{21}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\big(\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)=O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})=O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{22}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\big(-\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle+\langle e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu},g\rangle\big)=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{23}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle=O(1),

and

\displaystyle M_{24}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\langle-e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle=O(1).

We also find that

B_{2}=\big\langle e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu},-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)\big\rangle.

Proceeding as previously, we get that

|B_{2}|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-4}{N-12}}.

Third row.

Differentiating
\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle=0
with respect to time, we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle=-\theta^{\prime}\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle+\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},\partial_{t}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\mu},-ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle+\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle+\theta^{\prime}\big(\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},-ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle\big)+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\big(\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle\big)

\displaystyle+\big\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},-i\Delta^{2}g+i\big(f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)\big\rangle.

Therefore, we find

\displaystyle M_{31}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},-ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle=O\big(\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}\big)=O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N+4}{N-12}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{32}

\displaystyle=\mu^{-4}\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle=O\big(\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}\big)=O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N+4}{N-12}}\big),

\displaystyle M_{33}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\big(\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},-ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}\rangle-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle\big)=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})

\displaystyle=2\|W\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big),

and

\displaystyle M_{34}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-4}\big(\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\rangle-\langle ie^{i\theta}\Lambda\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle\big)=O(\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}})=O\big(|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{2(N-12)}}\big).

As before,
B_{3}
can be rewritten as

B_{3}=-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})g\rangle.

We set

K=-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})g\rangle.

First we will estimate
B_{3}-K
by writing

\displaystyle B_{3}-K

\displaystyle=-\big\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},\big(f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)g\big\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle+C_{2}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}-C_{2}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}

\displaystyle=B_{31}+B_{32}+B_{33}-C_{2}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}},

where
C_{2}=-\frac{N+4}{N-4}\lambda^{-\frac{N-4}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx=\frac{N+4}{N-4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda Wdx
. We will show that

|B_{31}|=\big|\big\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},\big(f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)g\big\rangle\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}},

|B_{32}|=\big|\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle\big|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}},

and

\displaystyle|B_{33}|

\displaystyle=|\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})\rangle+C_{2}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\theta|^{3}+|\zeta+\pi/2|)\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}+|1-\mu|\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

So, by above estimates and noticing that
|K|\lesssim\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}
(cf. (
3
)), we find

\displaystyle|B_{3}|

\displaystyle\lesssim|B_{3}-K|+|K|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}+\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}+(|\theta|^{3}+|\zeta+\pi/2|)\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}+|1-\mu|\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+|\theta|\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}+|\theta|\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+\|g\|^{2}_{\mathcal{E}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{N-12}}.

So we are left with estimating the
B_{3i}
,
i=1,2,3
. We begin with
B_{31}
. We will split the integral into two parts depending whether

|x|\leq\lambda^{\gamma}
, with
\gamma=\frac{N-8}{2(N-4)}
, or not. First, notice that

\big|e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda}\big|\big|\big(f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)g\big|\lesssim W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8-N}{N-4}}W_{\mu}|g|\lesssim W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}|g|.

So, using Hölder’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle 1_{\{|x|\leq\lambda^{\gamma}\}}e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},\big(f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})\big)g\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(|x|\leq\lambda^{\gamma})}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda^{\gamma-1}}r^{-\frac{16N}{N+4}}r^{N-1}dr\right)^{\frac{N+4}{2N}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{(\gamma-1)\frac{N-12}{2}+\frac{N-4}{2}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

On the other hand, when
|x|\geq\lambda^{\gamma}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda W_{\lambda}\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(|x|\geq\lambda^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle=\|W\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(|x|\geq\lambda^{\gamma-1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\int_{\lambda^{\gamma-1}}^{\infty}r^{-2N}r^{N-1}dr\Big)^{\frac{N-4}{2N}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{(1-\gamma)\frac{N-4}{2}}=\lambda^{\frac{N}{4}}.

Using Hölder’s inequality as well as the fact that
\|\Lambda W_{\mu}\|_{L^{\frac{N}{4}}(|x|\geq\lambda^{\gamma})}\lesssim 1
, we get that

|B_{31}|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}.

Next, let us estimate
B_{32}
. Notice that, if

|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda}
, we have

|f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})|\lesssim W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}W_{\mu}.

Since
|\Lambda W|\lesssim W
, we have

\|W_{\lambda}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\|_{L^{1}(|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\int_{\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{\infty}r^{-(N+4)}r^{N-1}dr\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}.

So the estimate follows from the fact that
W_{\mu}\lesssim 1
. Now, if
|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}
, notice that
W_{\mu}\lesssim W_{\lambda}
, so

|f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})-f(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu})|\leq W_{\mu}^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}}\lesssim 1.

Then the estimate of
B_{32}
follows from

\|W_{\lambda}\|_{L^{1}(|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda})}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N+4}{2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}}r^{4-N}r^{N-1}dr\leq\lambda^{\frac{N}{2}}.

Finally, we estimate
B_{33}
.
Recall that, by definition,

f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}=W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\left(e^{i\zeta}+\tfrac{8}{N-4}e^{i\theta}\mathcal{R}(e^{i(\zeta-\theta)})\right).

This implies that

\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},f^{\prime}(e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle=\frac{N+4}{N-4}\mathcal{R}\left(e^{i(\zeta-\theta)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx.

Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}
and
|\mathcal{R}(e^{i(\zeta-\theta)})+\theta|\lesssim|\theta|^{3}+|\zeta+\pi/2|
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\frac{N+4}{N-4}\mathcal{R}\left(e^{i(\zeta-\theta)}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx+\frac{N+4}{N-4}\theta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\theta|^{3}+|\zeta+\pi/2|)\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

At this point, the estimate of
B_{33}
follows from the estimate

(3.14)

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}W_{\mu}W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}\Lambda W_{\lambda}dx\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{N-2}{2}}+|1-\mu|\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}.

One can verify the estimate above by rescaling if
|x|\geq\sqrt{\lambda}
. When
|x|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}
, we have
|W_{\mu}-\mu^{-(N-4)/2}|\lesssim|x|^{2}\lesssim\lambda
and
|\mu^{-(N-4)/2}-1|\leq|1-\mu|
and (
3.14
) follows.

Fourth row.

Finally we deal with the fourth row. In this case, we differentiate
\langle-e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},g\rangle=0
with respect to time to find

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\langle-e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},g\rangle=-\theta^{\prime}\langle ie^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},g\rangle+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda}\langle e^{i\theta}\Lambda W_{\lambda},g\rangle-\langle e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},\partial_{t}g\rangle

\displaystyle=\zeta^{\prime}\langle e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},ie^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}\rangle-\frac{\mu^{\prime}}{\mu}\langle e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda},e^{i\zeta}\Lambda W_{\mu}\rangle

W_{\lambda}\big|f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda}+g)-f(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})-f^{\prime}(e^{i\zeta}W_{\mu}+e^{i\theta}W_{\lambda})g\big|\lesssim W_{\lambda}^{\frac{8}{N-4}}|g|^{2}.

Integrating the last term over the region

, we find overall that

\frac{1}{2}\dfrac{d}{dt}\langle g,iA_{0}(\lambda)g\rangle\leq\frac{c_{1}}{2\lambda^{4}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+\dfrac{K}{\lambda^{4}}.

So we proved that

\psi^{\prime}(t)\geq-c_{1}|t|^{-\frac{N-11}{N-12}}.

Since
\theta(T)=0
, we have
|\psi(T)|\lesssim\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\ll|t|^{-\frac{1}{N-12}}
. Integration of (
5.19
) over
[T,t]
yields
\psi(t)\gtrsim-c_{1}|t|^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}
. But, on the other hand,

|\langle g(t),A_{0}(\lambda)g(t)\rangle|\lesssim\|g(t)\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{N-12}}\ll|t|^{-\frac{1}{N-12}}.

Hence
\theta(t)\gtrsim-c_{1}|t|^{-\frac{1}{N-12}}
that implies (
5.18
) provided
c_{1}>0
is chosen small enough. We have proved (
5.13
).

Finally, from (
4.1
) we get

\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}+C_{0}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\leq C|t|^{-\frac{N-2}{N-12}}
, and therefore

\|g\|_{\mathcal{E}}^{2}\leq C_{0}\theta\lambda^{\frac{N-4}{2}}+C|t|^{-\frac{N-2}{N-12}}\leq\frac{1}{8}|t|^{-\frac{N-3}{N-12}}+C|t|^{-\frac{N-2}{N-12}}

if the constant
c_{0}
in (
5.16
) is small enough.
This proves (
5.14
).
∎

The Impact of LoRA on the Emergence of Clusters in Transformers
Hugo Koubbi, Matthieu Boussard, Louis Hernandez

Abstract
In this paper, we employ the mathematical framework on Transformers developed by Sander et al. 2022 ; Geshkovski et al. 2024 ; Geshkovski et al. 2023 to explore how variations in attention parameters and initial token values impact the structural dynamics of token clusters. Our analysis demonstrates that while the clusters within a modified attention matrix dynamics can exhibit significant divergence from the original over extended periods, they maintain close similarities over shorter intervals, depending on the parameter differences. This work contributes to the fine-tuning field through practical applications to the LoRA algorithm ( Hu et al. 2022 ; Mangrulkar et al. 2022 ) , enhancing our understanding of the behavior of LoRA-enhanced Transformer models.

Introduction and Main Results

Transformers

(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
represented a significant breakthrough in the fields of natural language processing and computer vision, due to their ability to handle sequential data with unprecedented efficiency. The subsequent availability of open-source pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs)
(
Touvron et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2023
)
has enabled LLMs to be widely used. For more task-specific models, fine-tuning is needed. However, since those models are characterized by a vast number of parameters, it still represents a computational challenge.

To mitigate this issue, Parameter-Efficient Fine-tuning
(
Mangrulkar et al. 2022
)
methods like LoRA
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
have been introduced. LoRA reduces parameter count by employing low-rank matrix factorization on attention mechanisms. This approach preserves the functionality of the pre-trained models while improving efficiency in parameter usage and reducing computational demands. In this work, we want to improve comprehension of representations learned by LoRA.

Our setting is based on the concepts of Neural ODE. This mathematical framework
(
Haber & Ruthotto 2017
;
Weinan 2017
;
Chen et al. 2018
)

proposes to see layers as time and to take the continuum limit of residual networks.
This framework allows us to study ResNet
(
He et al. 2016
)
which can be seen as the discretized version of the ODE:

\dot{x}(t)=f_{\theta}(x(t)),

(1)

where
\theta
is a learned parameter of the model, and
f_{\theta}
is a map of the inputs.
Building upon this concept,
Sander et al. 2022
has extended this analogy to Transformers, and proposes a novel framework that interprets the self-attention dynamics of Transformers as an interacting particle system of the tokens given by

\dot{x}_{i}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{e^{\langle Qx_{i}(t),Kx_{j}(t)\rangle}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}e^{\langle Qx_{i}(t),Kx_{k}(t)\rangle}}Vx_{j}(t),

(2)

where the tokens are initialized with
(x_{1}(0),\dots,x_{n}(0))\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}
,
(K,Q,V)\in M_{d}(\mathbb{R})^{3}
is a triple of trained attention matrix,
V
is called the Value matrix,
Q
the Query matrix and
K
the Key matrix. In the following, we will refer to the resulting tokens from the dynamics (
2
) at time
T
i.e.
(x_{1}(T),\dots,x_{n}(T))
as the
representation of the Transformers
at layer
T
.
A recent line of work,
(
Geshkovski et al. 2024
;
Geshkovski et al. 2023
)
has focused on describing the asymptotic geometry of the Transformers representation and exhibits clustering phenomena in various settings.

In this study, we examine a Transformers model architecture characterized by the use of an
learned identical, repeated
attention head across each layer. For this analysis, it is presumed that the model has already undergone the training process. The primary interest of this study lies in the properties of token clusters after the fine-tuning process with LoRA.

Contributions

This paper aims to build upon the clustering results of
Geshkovski et al. 2024
to make them amenable to practical design. We intend to deepen the understanding of how low-rank perturbations in parameters influence the representations of tokens within Transformers. To the best of our knowledge, this work represents one of the first theoretical analyses providing such insights on LoRA algorithm.
The contributions of this paper are:

•

In Section (

•

In Section (

•

In Section (

inspired by both theoretical considerations and empirical evidence, we demonstrate that low-rank attention matrices (i.e.

•

In Section (

In Section (

Related work

LoRA fine-tuning

The increase in the number of LLM parameters makes fine-tuning increasingly costly. One way of reducing the cost is to reduce the number of parameters to be trained. The algorithm LoRA
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
developed for NLP applications proposes to use a pre-trained foundation model such as Llama
(
Touvron et al. 2023
)
, Mistral
(
Jiang et al. 2023
)
, to finetune it by freezing all its weights and to train attention matrices
\tilde{Q},\tilde{K}
and
\tilde{V}
of low rank (see (
3
)).
This approach allows a more affordable pipeline to train a LLM, because low-rank matrices have a very small number of parameters compared to the number of parameters of the original model. For example, the initial training cost of a model like Llama 2 is prohibitive (see Section 2.2.1 Table 2
(
Touvron et al. 2023
)
), but its fine-tuning can be done thanks to LoRA algorithms at reasonable costs.
Since the introduction of LoRA fine-tuning
(
Hu et al. 2022
)
, many variants have emerged. These include QLoRA
(
Dettmers et al. 2023
)
, which quantizes the model weights, avoiding GPU memory problems and allowing individuals to support the model on their own hardware. Another variant of interest is
Wang et al. 2023
, which proposes to use as prior LoRA updates matrices as orthogonal to pre-trained weights.

Residual neural networks (ResNets)

ResNets
(
He et al. 2016
)
can be seen as a discretization of the following ODE parameterized by
\theta\in\mathbb{R}

\dot{x}(t)=f_{\theta}(x(t))\qquad t\in[0,T],

(6)

where
f_{\theta}
a map and the time
t
represents the number of layers such as neural ordinary differential equations (ODEs)
(
Weinan 2017
;
Chen et al. 2018
)
.

Neural networks on measures

The self-attention
mechanism (
2
) acts on sets
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
where the ordering of the elements does not matter. An equivalent way to model such invariant architectures is to
consider them as acting on probability measures or
point clouds
(
De Bie et al. 2019
;
Vuckovic et al. 2020
;
Zweig & Bruna 2021
;
Sander et al. 2022
)
.
Specifically, a collection of points
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
, where
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, can also be seen as a discrete measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mu:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}}
. A map
T_{\mu}
then
acts on
\mu
through
F(\mu):=(T_{\mu})_{\sharp}\mu.

One interest of such a point of view is to consider
the evolution of non-ordered sets of points.

Analysis of attention-based models

Since the pioneering article
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
, the self-attention mechanism has drawn attention from theorists.
Sander et al. 2022
introduces the formalism of seeing Attention as a map from measures space to measure space, and where tokens follow the dynamics (
2
). Besides,
Sander et al. 2022
proves that the dynamics on the space of measures is not associated with a Wasserstein flow.

Recent works
(
Vuckovic et al. 2020
;
Vuckovic et al. 2021
;
Kim et al. 2021
)
have bounded from below Lipschitz constant of the self-attention (as a function of measures),
Castin et al. 2023
improves the lower bound, and show numerically that the proposed bound seems sharp.
Geshkovski et al. 2024
gives an upper bound on the Lipschitz constant of self-attention.
Castin et al. 2023
proposes a way to implement a residual masked self-attention to probability measures by introducing a new independent variable for the mask.

The first study of the emergence of clustering structures is presented in
Geshkovski et al. 2024
, which focuses on the dynamics given by (
2
), and proves asymptotic clustering phenomena of rescaled tokens i.e. tokens given by (
8
).
More recently,
Geshkovski et al. 2023
proposes a way to add a layer normalization in the Transformer dynamics, resulting in a dynamics supported on the unit sphere. This new framework presents some enhanced aspects due to the boundness of the trajectories. They prove that a (modified) dynamics can be seen as a (reversed)
W_{2}
-gradient flow for an energy functional.

Additionally, observations by
Dong et al. 2021
and
Noci et al. 2022
have shown that clustering phenomena occur in Transformers without skip connections and in those with Post-LayerNorm and without normalization, which
Dong et al. 2021
refers to as
rank collapse
.

Clustering in particle systems

The interacting particle system, a simplified version of which was
first written down in
Lu et al. 2019
;
Sander et al. 2022
importantly contains the true
novelty that Transformers carry concerning other models: the self-attention
mechanism. The equation satisfied by the Transformers dynamics generalizes models of interacting particle systems
(
Kuramoto 1975
;
Kurause 2000
)
which exhibit clustering phenomena, see
(
Jabin & Motsch 2014
;
Motsch & Tadmor 2014
;
Tadmor 2023
)
. The model has deep links with pre-existing models of interacting particle models, we refer the reader to Section 2.2 of
Geshkovski et al. 2023
for a discussion of this topic.

Phase transition in the emergence of clustering dynamics

This section investigates how low-norm and low-rank perturbations of the values matrix
V
lead to the emergence of distinct clustering on different time scales. The main result of this section states that, under the hypotheses detailed below, the perturbed dynamics of the tokens follow two phases. The first phase is very close to the initial dynamics and tends towards clusters identical to those of the initial dynamics. The second phase is when the tokens bifurcate from this state and form new clusters.
This situation is relevant regarding the spectrum of some values attention matrices in ALBERT (see Figure
1
). The results of this section need that self-attention dynamics converge towards a clustering structure which is believed true in a very broad range of settings.

Clustering due to attention part

In this section, we have focused on low-rank attention matrices, more precisely in settings where the attention matrix
A
is a low-rank matrix. This hypothesis has been explored in several theoretical works
(
Merrill et al. 2023
;
Tarzanagh et al. 2023
;
Boix-Adsera et al. 2024
)
. We engaged in empirical studies examining the attention matrices of ALBERT and Llama2 models (see Figure

Driven by these observations both in theory and in our own experiments, we take a closer look at how clusters form when
A
is low-rank, aiming to understand the geometry and structure of these clusters in more detail.

6.1
Low rank attention matrices
Q
and
K

Suppose

, the tokens can be decomposed as
z_{i}(t)=z_{i}(t)^{\perp}+z_{i}^{A}(t)

If the tokens are initialized in the orthogonal of the image of

Representations Learned by LoRA

In this subsection, we investigate how LoRA fine-tuning enables the emergence of new representations. In particular, when tokens are arranged according to a certain geometry derived from low-dimensional features space.
Motivated by
Wang et al. 2023
, we study the case where the LoRA matrices have images that are orthogonal to the pre-trained weights parameters. In our case, we will focus on attention matrices which are low-rank, and also with LoRA matrices which satisfy

\mathrm{Im}(K_{A})\subset\mathrm{Im}(K)^{\perp},\mathrm{Im}(Q_{A})\subset\mathrm{Im}(Q)^{\perp}.

(36)

Assumption 7.1
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

C

separated

\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\},|\langle z_{i},v\rangle|\geq C

Affirmatively, this can be achieved, and the formal proof is articulated in Section

.

.

Let

\forall(z_{1}(0),\dots,z_{n}(0))\in{\mathrm{Im}(A)^{\perp}}^{n}

Optimal Transport Toolbox

Optimal Transport is a theory that endows probability measures, and general measures with natural and meaningful distances called Wasserstein distances. We
refer to the books
(
Villani 2008
;
Santambrogio 2015
)
for a general view of Optimal Transport from a theoretical point of view and to
(

Definition A.1
.

Coupling
Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
a Polish metric space. Let
\mu,\nu
two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
we say that
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X})
is a coupling between
\mu
and
\nu
if and only if the marginals of
\pi
are
\mu
and
\nu.
We denote
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
the set of couplings between
\mu
and
\nu
.

Definition A.2
.

(Wasserstein distances)
Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
a Polish metric space, and let
p\in[1,\infty(
. For any two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathcal{X}
, the Wasserstein distance of order
p
between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined by

W_{p}(\mu,\nu)=\left(\underset{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}{\inf}\int_{\mathcal{X}}d(x,y)^{p}d\pi(x,y)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

(40)

where the set
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the set of couplings between
\mu,\nu
.

We often call

the Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance. This distance has nice properties and satisfies a duality property.

Proposition A.3
.

(Kantorovich-Rubinstein duality)
Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
a Polish metric space, and let
p\in[1,\infty[
. For any two probability measures
\mu,\nu
on
\mathcal{X}
,

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\underset{\|\phi\|_{\text{Lip}\leq 1}}{\sup}\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi d\mu-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi d\nu\right\}.

(41)

Lemma A.4
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Consider a measurable function
f:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, and two probability measures
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
. Then, it holds

W_{p}\left(f_{\sharp}\alpha,f_{\sharp}\beta\right)\leq\text{Lip}(f)W_{p}\left(\alpha,\beta\right).

(42)

Proof.

We remark that a coupling
\gamma\in\Pi(\alpha,\beta)
induces a coupling
\gamma^{\prime}\in\Pi(f_{\sharp}\alpha,f_{\sharp}\beta)
defined as

\gamma^{\prime}(A\times B)=\gamma\left(f^{-1}(A)\times f^{-1}(B)\right).

(43)

We deduce the following inequalities

\displaystyle W_{p}(f_{\sharp}\alpha,f_{\sharp}\beta)^{p}

\displaystyle=\underset{\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma\left(f_{\sharp}\alpha,f_{\sharp}\beta\right)}{\inf}\int\|x-y\|^{p}d\gamma^{\prime}(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{\inf}\int\|f(x)-f(y)\|^{p}d\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\text{Lip}(f)^{p}\underset{\gamma\in\Gamma\left(\alpha,\beta\right)}{\inf}\int\|x-y\|^{p}d\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle\leq\text{Lip}(f)^{p}W_{p}(\alpha,\beta)^{p}.

It finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.5
.

Let
p\geq 1
.
Consider two measurable functions
\varphi,\psi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
, and a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
such that
\varphi_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
and
\psi_{\sharp}\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathcal{X})
.
Then, it holds

W_{p}(\varphi_{\sharp}\mu,\psi_{\sharp}\mu)\leq{\|\varphi-\psi\|}_{L^{p}(\mu)}.

Proof.

Recall that

W_{p}(\varphi_{\sharp}\mu,\psi_{\sharp}\mu)^{p}=\inf_{\pi^{\prime}\in\Pi(\varphi_{\sharp}\mu,\psi_{\sharp}\mu)}\int\|x-y\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}(x,y).

Now consider the following coupling between
\varphi_{\sharp}\mu
and
\psi_{\sharp}\mu
, defined by the relation

\pi^{\prime}(B\times C)\coloneqq\mu(\varphi^{-1}(B)\cap\psi^{-1}(C))

for every Borel sets
B,C\subset\mathcal{X}
.
In other words, we set
\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}(y,z)\coloneqq\int_{\varphi^{-1}(y)\cap\psi^{-1}(z)}\,\mathrm{d}\mu
, and
\,\mathrm{d}\pi^{\prime}(y,z)=0
if

With this definition of

\displaystyle W_{p}(\varphi_{\sharp}\mu,\psi_{\sharp}\mu)^{p}

\displaystyle=\int\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

∎

General results

We denote by

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of probability measures
\mu

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu(x)<+\infty
.
Let

denote the Banach space of continuous curves

B.1
General definition

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex open set. Let
T\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

be a bounded vector field. We say that

is Lipschitz continuous in space (uniformly) if it exists

|v(t,x)-v(t,y)|\leq L|x-y|\quad\forall t\leq T,(x,y)\in\Omega.

(45)

We remind the reader of the Cauchy-Lipchitz theorem

Theorem B.1
.

Given
T>0
, let
v:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded vector field
which is Lipschitz in space. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

t\mapsto X_{t}(x)
solving the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}X_{t}(x)&=v(X_{t}(x),t)\\
X_{0}(x)&=x.\end{cases}

(46)

in the integral sense. The map
X:[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

is the flow of
v
starting at time

We can apply this theorem to the flow associated to the velocity field
x\mapsto\mathcal{X}[\mu](x)
, which gives the flow associated to the

velocity-field, resulting that the empirical measure
\mu_{t}
of the tokens is the pushforward of the flow of the initial empirical measure

\mu_{t}={X_{t}}_{\sharp}(\mu_{0}).

(47)

Besides, this measure
\mu_{t}
is a solution to the continuity equation

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x)d{X_{t}}_{\sharp}(\mu_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(X_{t}(x))d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}g(X_{t}(x))d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla g(X_{t}(x)),\dot{X}_{t}(x)\rangle d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla g(X_{t}(x)),\mathcal{X}[\mu_{t}](X_{t}(x))\rangle d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\nabla g(x),\mathcal{X}[\mu_{t}](x)\rangle d\mu_{t}(x).

As seen below, for compactness purposes regarding solutions to the continuity equation, we consider an additional property on the support of such curves, summarized by the following definition.

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(C_{1}(R_{s})+C_{2}(R_{s})W_{2}(\mu_{s},\nu_{s})\right)e^{K_{t}(t-s)}ds\right)^{2}d\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\leq t\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{t}\left(C_{1}(R_{s})^{2}+C_{2}(R_{s})W_{2}(\mu_{s},\nu_{s})\right)^{2}e^{2K_{t}(t-s)}dsd\mu_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t}C_{1}(R_{s})^{2}e^{3K_{t}t}+\left(C_{2}(R_{s})W_{2}(\mu_{s},\nu_{s})\right)^{2}e^{3K_{t}t}ds.

We have used Cauchy-Schwarz inequality at the second line, at the third line we used the inequality

for

, and at the last line, we have made use of

. Now, if we consider the function

, we have used the fact that

. It yields the desired inequality.
∎

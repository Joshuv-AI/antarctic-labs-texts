Monk rules for type G ​ L n GL_{n} Macdonald polynomials
Tom Halverson, email: halverson@macalester.edu, email: aram@unimelb.edu.au

Abstract
In this paper we give Monk rules for Macdonald polynomials which are analogous to the Monk rules for Schubert polynomials. These formulas are similar to the formulas given by Baratta [ Ba08 ] , but our method of derivation is to use Cherednik’s interwiners. Deriving Monk rules by this technique addresses the relationship between the work of Baratta and the product formulas of Yip [ Yi10 ] . Specializations of the Monk formula’s at q = 0 q=0 and/or t = 0 t=0 provide Monk rules for Iwahori-spherical polynomials and for finite and affine key polynomials.

Introduction

In this paper, we use the term
electronic Macdonald polynomials
for what are commonly called
‘nonsymmetric’ Macdonald polynomials in the literature (see
[
CR22
]
for motivation for this terminology).
The (type
GL_{n}
) electronic Macdonald polynomials
\{E_{\mu}\ |\ \mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}

form a
\mathbb{C}
-basis for the ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.
The goal of this paper is to give Monk type rules for the products

x_{j}E_{\mu}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{j})E_{\mu}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad E_{\varepsilon_{j}}E_{\mu},

x^{-1}_{j}E_{\mu}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad(x^{-1}_{j}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n})E_{\mu}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad E_{-\varepsilon_{j}}E_{\mu},

expanded in terms of electronic Macdonald polynomials
(here
\varepsilon_{j}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
is the
n
-tuple with
1
in the
j
th
entry and all other entries 0).
We derive our formulas by viewing multiplication by

x_{j}
, multiplication by
(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{j})
, multiplication by
E_{\varepsilon_{j}}
etc.
as operators on the ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
.
Expanding a product like
x_{j}E_{\mu}
in terms of electronic Macdonald polynomials is equivalent to writing
the operator of multiplication by
x_{j}
in terms of intertwiners and Cherednik-Dunkl operators.
The expression of the operator
x_{j}
in terms of intertwiners and Cherednik-Dunkl operators
can be viewed as a
universal formula
for multiplication by
x_{j}
in the basis of
electronic Macdonald polynomials. These universal formulas are given in Theorem
2.1
.

To obtain the explicit expansions of the products above it is then necessary to (carefully)
“evaluate” the universal formula at
\mu
. These explicit expansions of the products are given in
Theorem
3.1
.

Letting
e_{1}=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}
and
e_{n-1}=x_{1}\cdots x_{n}(x^{-1}_{1}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n})
be the
first and
(n-1)
st elementary symmetric functions,
Baratta
[
Ba08
, Prop. 7, Prop. 8]
gives formulas for

\displaystyle x_{j}E_{\mu},\qquad e_{1}E_{\mu}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad e_{n-1}E_{\mu}

expanded in terms of electronic Macdonald polynomials.
Baratta indicates that the formula for
x_{j}E_{\mu}
also appears in Lascoux
[
La08
]
.
The formulas of Baratta must be the same as ours, although unwinding
and comparing the notations is not immediate (at least for us).
In
[
Ba10
]
Baratta computes the products

e_{r}E_{\mu}
where
e_{r}
denotes the
r
th elementary symmetric function.
It might be possible to give alternate derivations
of the products
e_{r}E_{\mu}
for general
r
using the methods of this paper.

Baratta’s approach is similar to that of Lascoux, using the interpolation Macdonald polynomials
and results of Knop and Sahi
[
Kn96
]
and
[
Sa96
]
.
Our approach uses the intertwiners and their relation with the Cherednik-Dunkl operators.
Computing these formulas via intertwiners addresses the relationship between the formulas
of Baratta and the methods of Yip
[
Yi10
]
,
who gives some related expansions, but in an alcove walk form.

The motivation for the term “Monk rules” comes from Schubert calculus.
In Schubert calculus, Monk’s rules for the Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{w}
are

x_{j}\mathfrak{S}_{w}=\Big(\!\!\!\!\sum_{1\leq i<j\atop\ell(ws_{ij})=\ell(w)+1}\mathfrak{S}_{ws_{ij}}\Big)-\Big(\!\!\!\!\sum_{j<i\leq n\atop\ell(ws_{ji})=\ell(w)+1}\mathfrak{S}_{ws_{ji}}\Big)\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{s_{r,r+1}}\mathfrak{S}_{w}=\sum_{i\leq r<j\atop\ell(ws_{ij})=\ell(w)+1}\mathfrak{S}_{ws_{ij}}.

(here
w
is a permutation in the
symmetric group
S_{n}
and
s_{ij}
denotes the transposition which switches
i
and
j
).
These rules are proved in
[
Mac91
, (4.15),(
4.15^{\prime}
),(
4.15^{\prime\prime}
)]
.
A compendium of similar formulas for type
GL_{n}
Grothendieck polynomials is given in

[
LS04
, §1,2, §1.3]
. Though the analogies are tantalizing, we have not, in any generality,
made a concrete connection between our ‘Monk formulas’ for Macdonald poylnomials
and the formulas which appear in Schubert calculus. Part (c) of Corollary
4.4

provides a different formulation and proof of
[
AQ19
, Theorem 3.3.6]
.
Other formulas related to Corollary
4.4
appear in Assaf
[
As21
]

and Gibson
[
Gib19
]

who, respectively, use the combinatorics of Kohnert diagrams and monomial crystals.

1
Macdonald polynomials

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.
The (Laurent) polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
has basis

\{x^{\mu}\ |\ \mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\},\qquad\hbox{where}\quad x^{\mu}=x_{1}^{\mu_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\mu_{n}}\quad\hbox{for $\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}$.}

The symmetric group

S_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]

by permuting the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
.
The symmetric group
S_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by permuting the positions of the entries. The two actions
are related by
wx^{\mu}=x^{w\mu}
for
w\in S_{n}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Let
q,t^{\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. For
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
let
X_{j}
be the operator
on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
given by multiplication by
x_{j}
.
For
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
let

s_{i}\in S_{n}
be the transposition which switches
i
and
i+1
. For
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

let
y_{j}
be the operator on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
which replaces each
occurrence of
x_{j}
with
q^{-1}x_{j}
. In formulas, if
f\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
then

\displaystyle(X_{j}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=x_{j}\cdot f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

\displaystyle(s_{i}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}),

(1.1)

\displaystyle(y_{j}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

\displaystyle=f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-1},q^{-1}x_{j},x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{n}).

Define operators
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n-1}
,
T_{\pi}
and
T^{\vee}_{\pi}
on

\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
by

T_{i}=-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}+t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+s_{i})\frac{1-tx_{i}^{-1}x_{i+1}}{1-x^{-1}_{i}x_{i+1}},\qquad T_{\pi}=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n-1}y_{n},\qquad T^{\vee}_{\pi}=X_{1}T_{1}\cdots T_{n-1}.

(1.2)

The
Cherednik-Dunkl operators
are

Y_{1}=T_{\pi}T_{n-1}\cdots T_{1},\quad Y_{2}=T_{1}^{-1}Y_{1}T_{1}^{-1},\quad Y_{3}=T_{2}^{-1}Y_{2}T_{2}^{-1},\quad\ldots,\quad Y_{n}=T_{n-1}^{-1}Y_{n-1}T_{n-1}^{-1}.

(1.3)

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
let
v_{\mu}\in S_{n}
be the minimal length permutation which rearranges

\mu
into weakly increasing order. Explicitly, the permutation
v_{\mu}
is given by

v_{\mu}(r)=1+\#\{r^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}\ |\ \mu_{r^{\prime}}\leq\mu_{r}\}+\#\{r^{\prime}\in\{r+1,\ldots,n\}\ |\ \mu_{r^{\prime}}<\mu_{r}\}.

(1.4)

By definition, the electronic Macdonald polynomials
E_{\mu}
are the simultaneous
eigenvectors for the action of the Cherednik-Dunkl operators,

Y_{i}E_{\mu}=q^{-\mu_{i}}t^{-v_{\mu}(i)}t^{\frac{1}{2}(n+1)}E_{\mu}.

(1.5)

The “evaluate at
\mu
” homomorphism
\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}\colon\mathbb{C}[Y^{\pm 1}_{1},\ldots,Y^{\pm 1}_{n}]\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}(Y_{i})=q^{-\mu_{i}}t^{-(v_{\mu}(i)-1)+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)}

(1.6)

(so that
\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}
specializes
Y_{i}
to the value

q^{-\mu_{i}}t^{-(v_{\mu}(i)-1)+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)}
). Extend
\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}
to those elements of the
field
\mathbb{C}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
for which the specialized denominator does not vanish.
By (
1.5
), if
f(Y)\in\mathbb{C}(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n})
and
\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}(f)
is defined then

f(Y)E_{\mu}=\mathrm{ev}^{t}_{\mu}(f(Y))E_{\mu}.

(1.7)

The
interwiners
are

\tau_{\pi}^{\vee}=T^{\vee}_{\pi},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\tau_{i}^{\vee}=T_{i}+\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)}{1-Y^{-1}_{i}Y_{i+1}}\quad\hbox{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}$.}

(1.8)

Using the definition of
\tau^{\vee}_{i}
and the relation
T_{i}-T^{-1}_{i}=t^{\frac{1}{2}}-t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,

\tau^{\vee}_{i}=T_{i}+f_{i+1,i}^{+}=T^{-1}_{i}+f_{i+1,i}^{-},

(1.9)

where

\qquad\hbox{where}\quad f_{ij}^{+}=\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)}{1-Y_{i}Y^{-1}_{j}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad f_{ij}^{-}=\frac{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)Y_{i}Y^{-1}_{j}}{1-Y_{i}Y^{-1}_{j}},

(1.10)

for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
i\neq j
.
The following key relations are proved (for example) in
[
GR21
, Prop. 5.5]
,

Y_{1}\tau^{\vee}_{\pi}=q^{-1}\tau^{\vee}_{\pi}Y_{n}\quad\hbox{and}\quad Y_{i}\tau^{\vee}_{\pi}=\tau^{\vee}_{\pi}Y_{i-1}\quad\hbox{for $i\in\{2,\ldots,n\}$, and}

(1.11)

Y_{i}\tau^{\vee}_{i}=\tau^{\vee}_{i}Y_{i+1},\qquad Y_{i+1}\tau^{\vee}_{i}=\tau^{\vee}_{i}Y_{i},\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad Y_{k}\tau^{\vee}_{i}=\tau^{\vee}_{i}Y_{k},

(1.12)

for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
with
k\not\in\{i,i+1\}
.

The following Proposition gives an explicit expression for
E_{\mu}

as a sequence of intertwiners acting on the polynomial 1.

Proposition 1.1
.

[
GR21
, Proposition 5.7 and Proposition 2.2(a)]

Let
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{\geq 0}
and write
(r,c)\in\mu
if

r\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
c\in\{1,\ldots,\mu_{r}\}
. For
(r,c)\in\mu
define

u_{\mu}(r,c)=\#\{r^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}\ |\ \mu_{r^{\prime}}<c\leq\mu_{r}\}+\#\{r^{\prime}\in\{r+1,\ldots,n\}\ |\ \mu_{r^{\prime}}<c-1<\mu_{r}\}.

Then

E_{\mu}=t^{-\frac{1}{2}\ell(v^{-1}_{\mu})}\Big(\prod_{r=1}^{n}\prod_{c=1}^{\mu_{r}}(\tau^{\vee}_{u_{\mu}(r,c)}\cdots\tau^{\vee}_{2}\tau^{\vee}_{1}\tau^{\vee}_{\pi})\Big)\cdot 1.

where the product is taken in order defined by

(r_{1},c_{1})<(r_{2},c_{2})
if
c_{1}<c_{2}
, and
(r_{1},c)<(r_{2},c)
if
r_{1}<r_{2}
.

If
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has a negative entry then

E_{\mu}=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{-m}E_{(\mu_{1}+m,\ldots,\mu_{n}+m)},\qquad\hbox{where $-m$ is the most negative entry of $\mu$.}

Proposition 1.2
.

For
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
let
\varepsilon_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with

1
in the
i
th entry and all other entries 0.
Then

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon_{i}}

\displaystyle=x_{i}+\frac{(1-t)}{(1-qt^{n-i+1})}(x_{i-1}+\cdots+x_{1})\quad\hbox{and}

\displaystyle E_{-\varepsilon_{i}}

\displaystyle=x^{-1}_{i}+\Big(\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)(x^{-1}_{i+1}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n}).

Proof.

Since
v_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=s_{1}\cdots s_{i}
then

Y^{-1}_{i}Y_{i+1}E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=q^{0}t^{v_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}(i)}q^{1}t^{-v_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}(i+1)}E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=qt^{i+1}t^{-1}E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=qt^{i}E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}

The base case is
E_{-\varepsilon_{n}}=x_{n}^{-1}
and the induction step is

\displaystyle E_{-\varepsilon_{i}}

\displaystyle=t^{\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{\vee}_{i}E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=\Big(t^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{i}+\frac{(1-t)}{1-Y^{-1}_{i}Y_{i+1}}\Big)E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}=\Big(t^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{i}+\frac{(1-t)}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)E_{-\varepsilon_{i+1}}

\displaystyle=\Big(t^{\frac{1}{2}}T_{i}+\frac{(1-t)}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)(x^{-1}_{i+1}+\Big(\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i+1}}\Big)(x^{-1}_{i+2}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n})

\displaystyle=x^{-1}_{i}+\Big(\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)x^{-1}_{i+1}+\Big(\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i+1}}\Big)\Big(t+\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)(x^{-1}_{i+2}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n})

\displaystyle=x^{-1}_{i}+\Big(\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{i}}\Big)(x^{-1}_{i+1}+\cdots+x^{-1}_{n}).

The proof of the first statement is similar (see
[
GR21
, Prop. 3.5]
for details).

Theorem 2.1
.

(Monk rules: operator form)

Let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. As in (
1.1
), let
X_{j}
denote the operator on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]

given by multiplication by
x_{i}
and let
E_{\varepsilon_{j}}
and
E_{-\varepsilon_{j}}
be the Macdonald polynomials
of Proposition
1.2
, identified with the operators on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]

given by multiplication by
E_{\varepsilon_{j}}
and
E_{-\varepsilon_{j}}
, respectively.
Use the notations of (
2.1
)-(
2.7
).
Then, as operators on
\mathbb{C}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
,

(a)

\displaystyle{X_{j}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop C\cap\{j\}\neq\emptyset}\tau_{C,j}F_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y),}

(b)

\displaystyle{X_{1}+\cdots+X_{j}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop C\cap\{1,\ldots,j\}\neq\emptyset}\tau_{C,j}A_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y),}

(c)

\displaystyle{E_{\varepsilon_{j}}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop C\cap\{1,\ldots,j\}\neq\emptyset}\tau_{C,j}B_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y).}

(d)

\displaystyle{X^{-1}_{j}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop D\cap\{j\}\neq\emptyset}\rho_{C,j}\Phi_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y),}

(e)

\displaystyle{X^{-1}_{j}+\cdots+X^{-1}_{n}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop C\cap\{j,\ldots,n\}\neq\emptyset}\rho_{C,j}\Psi_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y)}

(f)

\displaystyle{E_{-\varepsilon_{j}}=\sum_{C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}\atop C\cap\{j,\ldots,n\}\neq\emptyset}\rho_{C,j}\Omega_{C,j}(Y)f_{C}(Y).}

Proof.

Since the proof of (a) is longer, let us first make remarks about the proofs of (b)-(f).

(b) This follows from (a) and the observation that
A_{C,j}=F_{C,1}+\cdots+F_{C,j}
.

(c) By the first identity in Proposition
1.2
,

E_{\varepsilon_{j}}=x_{j}+\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{n-j+1}}(x_{j-1}+\cdots+x_{1}),\qquad\hbox{so that}\quad B_{C,j}=F_{C,j}+\frac{1-t}{1-qt^{n-j+1}}A_{C,{j-1}}.

(d) The proof is analogous to the proof of (a) by expanding

\displaystyle X^{-1}_{j}

\displaystyle=T_{j}\cdots T_{n-1}(\tau^{\vee}_{\pi})^{-1}T^{-1}_{1}\cdots T^{-1}_{j-1}

\displaystyle=(\tau^{\vee}_{j}-f^{+}_{j+1,j})\cdots(\tau^{\vee}_{n-1}-f^{+}_{n,n-1})(\tau^{\vee}_{\pi})^{-1}(\tau^{\vee}_{1}-f^{-}_{2,1})\cdots(\tau^{\vee}_{j-1}-f^{-}_{j,j-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{L\subseteq\{j,\ldots,n-1\}\atop R\subseteq\{1,\ldots,j-1\}}(-1)^{|L|+|R|}\varpi_{L}\tau^{\vee}_{\pi}\varpi_{R},

\varpi_{L}=\varpi_{j}\cdots\varpi_{n-1}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\varpi_{i}=\begin{cases}\tau^{\vee}_{i},&\hbox{if $i\in\{j,\ldots,n-1\}$ and $i\not\in L$,}\\

Specializations of the electronic Macdonald polynomials at
q=0
,
t=0
,
q=\infty
and
t=\infty

are of interest. For example,

(a)

are the Iwahori-spherical functions of
[
Ion04
]
, also called

t
-deformations of Demazure characters and Demazure atoms in
[
Al16
]
;

(b)

are (level 1 or level 0) affine Demazure characters,
or (affine) key polynomials, or non-symmetric
q
-Whittaker polynomials
(see
[
Ion01
]
,
[
MRY19
]
and
[
AG20
]
).

(c)

E_{\mu}(0,0)
are Demazure characters or (finite) key polynomials.
The finite key polynomials are special cases of the affine key polynomials.

By appropriately packaging the weights in the Monk formulas in Theorem
3.1
it is
easy to specialize these formulas and obtain formulas at
t=0
and
q=0
.
Proposition
4.2

does this repackaging for the product
x_{j}E_{\mu}
and the resulting formulas at

q=0
and
t=0
are given in Corollary
4.4
.
Similar formulas could be given for the other products in Theorem
3.1

and also for specializations at
t=\infty
and
q=\infty
(by packaging the coefficients
in terms of
q^{-1}
and
t^{-1}
and then setting
q^{-1}=0
and/or
t^{-1}=0
).

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let

C=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}\qquad\hbox{with}\quad 1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{m}\leq n.

For parsing the following definitions it is useful to note that

\displaystyle\mu_{a_{i-1}}>\mu_{a_{i}}

if and only if

\displaystyle v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})>v_{\mu}(a_{i}),

and

\displaystyle\mu_{a_{1}}<\mu_{a_{m}}+1

if and only if

\displaystyle v_{\mu}(a_{1})<v_{\mu}(a_{m}).

Assume
j\in C
and let
p\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be given by
j=a_{p}
. Let

\displaystyle S^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\#\{i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}\ |\ \mu_{a_{i-1}}>\mu_{a_{i}}\}+\begin{cases}1,&\hbox{if $v_{\mu}(a_{1})>v_{\mu}(a_{m})$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $v_{\mu}(a_{1})<v_{\mu}(a_{m})$,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle A^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}\atop\mu_{a_{i-1}}>\mu_{a_{i}}}(\mu_{a_{i-1}}-\mu_{a_{i}})+\begin{cases}\mu_{a_{1}}-(\mu_{a_{m}}+1),&\hbox{ if $\mu_{a_{1}}\geq\mu_{a_{m}}+1$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $\mu_{a_{1}}<\mu_{a_{m}}+1$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle B^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\{i\ |\mu_{i}>\mu_{a_{m}}\}+\{k\not\in C\ |\ b(k)<\mu_{k}\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}\atop v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})>v_{\mu}(a_{i})}(v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})-v_{\mu}(a_{i}))+\begin{cases}v_{\mu}(a_{1})-v_{\mu}(a_{m}),&\hbox{if $v_{\mu}(a_{1})>v_{\mu}(a_{m})$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $v_{\mu}(a_{1})<v_{\mu}(a_{m})$.}\end{cases}

Then define

\displaystyle S_{j,\mu}(C)

\displaystyle=S^{\prime}+\begin{cases}1,&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $\mu_{a_{p}}\geq\mu_{a_{p-1}}$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $\mu_{a_{p}}<\mu_{a_{p-1}}$,}\\
1,&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $\mu_{a_{m}}+1\leq\mu_{a_{1}}$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $\mu_{a_{m}}+1>\mu_{a_{1}}$,}\end{cases}

(4.1)

\displaystyle A_{j,\mu}(C)

\displaystyle=A^{\prime}+\begin{cases}\mu_{a_{p-1}}-\mu_{a_{1}},&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $\mu_{a_{p}}\geq\mu_{a_{p-1}}$,}\\
\mu_{a_{p}}-\mu_{a_{1}},&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $\mu_{a_{p}}<\mu_{a_{p-1}}$,}\\
-(\mu_{a_{1}}-(\mu_{a_{m}}+1)),&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $\mu_{a_{m}}+1\leq\mu_{a_{1}}$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $\mu_{a_{m}}+1>\mu_{a_{1}}$,}\end{cases}

(4.2)

\displaystyle B_{j,\mu}(C)

\displaystyle=B^{\prime}+\begin{cases}v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})-v_{\mu}(a_{1}),&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})<v_{\mu}(a_{p})$,}\\
v_{\mu}(a_{p})-v_{\mu}(a_{1}),&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})>v_{\mu}(a_{p})$,}\\
-(v_{\mu}(a_{1})-v_{\mu}(a_{m})),&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $v_{\mu}(a_{1})>v_{\mu}(a_{p})$,}\\
0,&\hbox{if $p=1$ and $v_{\mu}(a_{1})<v_{\mu}(a_{p})$.}\end{cases}

(4.3)

Remark 4.1
.

The statistics
S_{j,\mu}(C)
,

A_{j,\mu}(C)
and
B_{j,\mu}(C)
are
interesting statistics on

\mu
and on the permutation

What properties do these statistics have? How do they change when parts of

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and let
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
C\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let

C=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}\qquad\hbox{with}\quad 1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{m}\leq n.

Assume

and let

a_{0}=a_{m},\qquad\gamma_{0}=\mu_{a_{m}}+1,\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{i}=\mu_{a_{i}},\quad\hbox{for $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$,}

W_{\mu,k\in C}=\left(\prod_{i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\atop i\neq p}\frac{1-t}{1-q^{|\gamma_{i}-\gamma_{i-1}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{i})-v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})|}}\right)

and

S_{j,\mu}(C)
,

and

), (

By (

),

\prod_{k\not\in C}\mathrm{wt}_{\mu}(C,k)=t^{\#\{k\not\in C\ |\ b(k)<\mu_{k}\}}W_{\mu,k\not\in C}.

and let

If

\frac{1-t}{1-q^{\mu_{a_{i}}-\mu_{a_{i-1}}}t^{v_{\mu}(a_{i})-v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})}}=\frac{-q^{\mu_{a_{i-1}}-\mu_{a_{i}}}t^{v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})-v_{\mu}(a_{i})}(1-t)}{1-q^{|\mu_{a_{i-1}}-\mu_{a_{i}}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{i-1})-v_{\mu}(a_{i})|}}

\prod_{k\in C}\mathrm{wt}_{\mu}(C,k)=(-1)^{S^{\prime}+\#\{i\ |\ \mu_{i}>\mu_{a_{m}}\}}q^{A^{\prime}}t^{B^{\prime\prime}}W_{\mu,k\in C}\cdot\frac{1}{1-q^{|\gamma_{p}-\gamma_{p-1}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{p})-v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})|}}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{wt}_{\mu}(C)

\displaystyle=t^{-\#\{i\ |\ \mu_{i}>\mu_{a_{m}}\}}\Big(\prod_{k\in C}\mathrm{wt}_{\mu}(C,k)\Big)\Big(\prod_{k\not\in C}\mathrm{wt}_{\mu}(C,k)\Big)

then

\displaystyle F_{\mu}(C,j)=\begin{cases}-q^{\mu_{a_{p-1}}-\mu_{a_{1}}}t^{v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})-v_{\mu}(a_{1})}(1-q^{|\mu_{a_{p}}-\mu_{a_{p-1}}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{p})-v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})|}),&\hbox{if $\mu_{a_{p}}>\mu_{a_{p-1}}$,}\\
q^{\mu_{a_{p}}-\mu_{a_{1}}}t^{v_{\mu}(a_{p})-v_{\mu}(a_{1})}(1-q^{|\mu_{a_{p}}-\mu_{a_{p-1}}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{p})-v_{\mu}(a_{p-1})|}),&\hbox{if $\mu_{a_{p}}<\mu_{a_{p-1}}$.}\end{cases}

-q^{-(\mu_{a_{1}}-(\mu_{a_{m}}+1)}t^{-(v_{\mu}(a_{1})-v_{\mu}(a_{m})}(1-q^{|\mu_{a_{m}}+1-\mu_{a_{1}}|}t^{|v_{\mu}(a_{m})-v_{\mu}(a_{1})|}),&\hbox{if $\mu_{a_{m}}+1<\mu_{a_{1}}$.}\end{cases}

Thus

(a).

Proposition 4.3

To keep track of signs, write

\displaystyle A_{j,\mu}(C)

\displaystyle=A^{\prime}+\begin{cases}(\mu_{a_{p-1}}-\mu_{a_{1}}),&\hbox{if $p\neq 1$ and $\mu_{a_{1}}\leq\mu_{a_{p-1}}\leq\mu_{a_{p}}$,}\\

Note that
A^{\prime}\geq 0
since it is a sum of positive integers.
Let us consider the cases when the term added to

Case

Since the total of the descents of the sequence

A critical analysis of ‘Relative facts do not exist. Relational quantum mechanics is incompatible with quantum mechanics’ by Jay Lawrence, Marcin Markiewicz and Marek Źukowski. The present arXiv version corrects some typos included in the final version published in Foundations of Physics [Found. Phys. 54 :5 (2024)].
Aurélien Drezet

Abstract
We discuss a recent work by J. Lawrence et al.[arxiv.org/abs/2208.11793] criticizing relational quantum mechanics (RQM) and based on a famous nonlocality theorem Going back to Greenberger Horne and Zeilinger (GHZ). Here, we show that the claims presented in this recent work are unjustified and we debunk the analysis. Key words: Relational quantum mechanics, Greenberger Horne Zeilinger nonlocality, Wigner friends paradox



I
Introduction

The Relational Quantum Mechanics (RQM) is an alternative interpretation of quantum mechanics that was proposed originally by C. Rovelli
Rovelli1996
;
RovelliBook2021
;
Laudisa
;
RovelliFP
. RQM can be seen as a logical completion and generalization of the Copenhagen (orthodox) interpretation but where the arbitrariness of Heisenberg’s quantum ‘shifty-split’ or ‘cut’, which is separating observed and observing subsystems, is taken more seriously. Unlike, the Copenhagen interpretation the cut is not confined to the macroscopic domain and the roles of observed and observing systems are relative and can be inverted. RQM is therefore a more symmetric and general approach.

Moreover, recently RQM has been criticized and assessed by various authors. The aim of the present comment (see also the analysis by Cavalcanti et al.
RovelliReply
) is to give a short reply to the recent J. Lawrence et al. article
Zukowski
that concerns RQM and the role of quantum contextuality (for previous claims see
Brukner
;
Pienaar
and see the replies by Di Biagio and Rovelli
Rovellian
and Drezet
Drezet
;
Drezetb
). Lawrence et al. have recently replied to the present analysis in
Zukowskireply2
, and to the independent analysis
RovelliReply
in
Zukowskireply1
.

II
The starting point

Following the recent analysis
Drezet
;
Drezetb
, I remind that in RQM the main issue concerns the interpretation of the full wavefunction
|\Psi_{SO}\rangle
involving observer (O) and observed system (S). In RQM the fundamental object
relatively to (O)
is not
|\Psi_{SO}\rangle
but the reduced density matrix

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|O}=\textrm{Tr}_{O}[\hat{\rho}_{SO}]=\textrm{Tr}_{O}[|\Psi_{SO}\rangle\langle\Psi_{SO}|].

(1)

As it is well known
\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|O}
is independent of the basis chosen to represent the degrees of freedom for (O). In
Drezet
I showed that it solves the dilemma discussed in
Brukner
;
Pienaar
concerning the ‘preferred basis problem’. Here I show that the same features debunk the claims of
Zukowski
concerning non-contextuality.

Zukowski
starts with a GHZ state
GHZ
for a system S of 3 spins
m=1,2,3
:

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(1)},+1^{(1)},+1^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(1)},-1^{(1)},-1^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}]

(2)

where
|p^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{m}}\equiv|\textrm{sign}(p)z\rangle_{S_{m}}
(with
p=\pm 1
) are spin eigenstates along the
z
direction.
We also have in different spin bases:

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{2}[|+1^{(2)},+1^{(3)},-1^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|+1^{(2)},-1^{(3)},+1^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(2)},+1^{(3)},+1^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(2)},-1^{(3)},-1^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}]

(3)

where we used
|p^{(2)}\rangle_{S_{m}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{m}}+p^{(2)}|-1^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{m}}]\equiv|\textrm{sign}(p^{(2)})x\rangle_{S_{m}}
, and
|p^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{m}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(1)}\rangle_{S_{m}}+p^{(3)}i|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{m}}]\equiv|\textrm{sign}(p^{(3)})y\rangle_{S_{m}}
(with
p^{(2)},p^{(3)}=\pm 1
). This implies

\displaystyle{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{3}}|GHZ\rangle_{S}=-|GHZ\rangle_{S}

(4)

and

\displaystyle p^{(2)}_{S_{1}}q^{(3)}_{S_{2}}r^{(3)}_{S_{3}}=-1.

(5)

Similar expressions are obtained by circular permutations:

\displaystyle p^{(3)}_{S_{1}}q^{(2)}_{S_{2}}r^{(3)}_{S_{3}}=-1,

(6)

and

\displaystyle p^{(3)}_{S_{1}}q^{(3)}_{S_{2}}r^{(2)}_{S_{3}}=-1.

(7)

We can also write:

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{S}=\frac{1}{2}[|+1^{(2)},+1^{(2)},+1^{(2)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|+1^{(2)},-1^{(2)},-1^{(2)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(2)},+1^{(2)},-1^{(2)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(2)},-1^{(2)},+1^{(2)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}]

(8)

implying

\displaystyle{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{3}}|GHZ\rangle_{S}=+|GHZ\rangle_{S}

(9)

and thus

\displaystyle p^{(2)}_{S_{1}}q^{(2)}_{S_{2}}r^{(2)}_{S_{3}}=+1.

(10)

It is well known that quantum mechanics is highly contextual. If the results of spin measurements were non-contextual Eqs. 5,6,7 and 10 could be true together and this, clearly, is not possible: multiplying Eqs. 5,6,7 contradicts Eq. 10. This incompatibility also leads to a well known proof of quantum nonlocality without inequality
GHZ
.

III
Debunking a paradox

In
Zukowski
the authors use the previous results. First, they consider a single observer Alice (A) (composed of 3 qubits
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}
) who measures the spins of the GHZ system S in the y bases. The GHZ states of Eq. 2 reads in the y bases:

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{S}=\sum_{p,q,r}C^{333}_{pqr}|p^{(3)},q^{(3)},r^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1},S_{2},S_{3}}

(11)

where the amplitudes
C^{333}_{pqr}
are non vanishing for each of the 8 combinations
p,q,r
and where
|C^{333}_{pqr}|^{2}=\frac{1}{8}
. After entanglement with Alice’s qubits we have

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{SA}=\sum_{p,q,r}C^{333}_{pqr}|p^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{1}}|q^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|r^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}

(12)

with
|k^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{m}}:=|k^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{m}}|k^{(3)}\rangle_{A_{m}}
and
|k^{(3)}\rangle_{A_{m}}
is the state of the qubit
A_{m}
,
m=1,2,3
(for
k=\pm 1
).

Importantly, for Alice this state shows no correlation. This is clear in RQM where the reduced density matrix reads
\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}=\textrm{Tr}_{A}[\hat{\rho}_{SA}]
:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}=\frac{1}{8}\sum_{p,q,r}|p^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{1}}{}_{S_{1}}\langle p^{(3)}|\otimes|q^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{2}}{}_{S_{2}}\langle q^{(3)}|

\displaystyle\otimes|r^{(3)}\rangle_{S_{3}}{}_{S_{3}}\langle r^{(3)}|

(13)

Due to decoherence i.e., entanglement with the environement (Alice) we have lost coherence and correlations between spins. In particular we have

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S|A}]=0,

which contrast with

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S}]=+1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S}]=-1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S}]=-1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{S}[{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{S_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{S_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{S}]=-1,

In RQM Eq. 15 actually describes the correlations available to A before the interaction occurred, i.e., when the full state of SA is still factorized. It represents a catalog of knowledge or potentiality in the sense of Heisenberg. The actualization of measurements in RQM is a debatable issue and we will not consider this problem here (see e.g.
Drezet
).

In the next step the authors of
Zukowski
consider a second observer Bob (B) (also composed of 3 qubits
B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}
) who measures the spins of the entangled GHZ system SA in the x bases of the joint system. In analogy with Eq. 8 we write after entanglement with Bob qubits:

\displaystyle|GHZ\rangle_{SAB}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p,q,r}|p^{(2)}\rangle_{SAB_{1}}|q^{(2)}\rangle_{SAB_{2}}|r^{(2)}\rangle_{SAB_{3}}

(16)

with
|k^{(2)}\rangle_{SAB_{m}}:=|k^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{m}}|k^{(2)}\rangle_{B_{m}}
and
|k^{(2)}\rangle_{B_{m}}
is the state of the qubit
B_{m}
,
m=1,2,3
(for
k=\pm 1
) and where we introduced the entangled ‘x’ states for the SA system:
|k^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{m}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{m}}+ik^{(2)}|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{m}}]\equiv|\textrm{sign}(k^{(2)})x\rangle_{SA_{m}}
. Crucially, the numbers
p,q,r=\pm 1
in Eq. 16 must obey the GHZ-constraint:

\displaystyle p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}q^{(2)}_{SAB_{2}}r^{(2)}_{SAB_{3}}=+1.

(17)

Once more, in RQM we need to consider the reduced density matrix
\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}=\textrm{Tr}_{B}[\hat{\rho}_{SAB}]
:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{p,q,r}|p^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{1}}{}_{SA_{1}}\langle p^{(2)}|\otimes|q^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}{}_{SA_{2}}\langle q^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\otimes|r^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}{}_{SA_{3}}\langle r^{(2)}|

with again
pqr=+1
. This defines the information available to B in RQM and this density matrix shows partial coherence since we have

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=+1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=0.

The first line of Eq. 19 is of course reminiscent of Eq. 17 and shows that there is a preferred pointer basis defined by the specific measurement protocol. This is associated with a specific interaction Hamiltonian
H_{SA,B}
leading to the state given by Eq. 16.

However, the authors of
Zukowski
didn’t correctly analyze the structure of RQM and the meaning of relative facts. They claim that we can find relations between facts or information available to Bob and facts or information available to Alice. This we show below is actually a misunderstanding. More precisely, they consider that Bob only measures one of the 3 qubits belonging to SA. In the following we consider the particular case
m=1
and therefore only the system
SA_{1}
will interact with
B_{1}
. This requires to let the two other qubits of Bob
B_{2}
and
B_{3}
in their respective ground states. As the authors show we get the new state

\displaystyle|GHZ^{\prime}\rangle_{SAB}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p,q,r}|p^{(2)}\rangle_{SAB_{1}}|q^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|in\rangle_{B_{2}}|r^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}|in\rangle_{B_{3}}

(20)

with now the constraint:

\displaystyle p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}}=-1.

(21)

Of course, we could develop two similar procedures acting only on the qubit
B_{2}
or alternatively the qubit
B_{3}
. We will obtain two different states
|GHZ^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{SAB}
and
|GHZ^{\prime\prime\prime}\rangle_{SAB}
leading to the relations

\displaystyle p^{(3)}_{SA_{1}}q^{(2)}_{SAB_{2}}r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}}=-1,

(22)

and

\displaystyle p^{(3)}_{SA_{1}}q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}r^{(2)}_{SAB_{3}}=-1.

(23)

With these mathematical properties the deduction of
Zukowski
goes as follows:

i) It is visible from Eq. 21 (and similarly for Eqs. 22,23 by cyclic permutation) that the number
p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}
characterizes the entangled system
SAB_{1}
whereas
q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}
and
r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}}
characterize
SA_{2}
and
SA_{3}
. Therefore it is tempting to call
p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}
a relative fact for Bob and
q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}
and
r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}}
relative facts for Alice. This idea, which is central for their paper, is clearly summarized by the analysis surrounding their equation 17 in
Zukowski
. They call
\mathcal{B}_{m}
the number
k^{(2)}_{SAB_{m}}
defined in our Eqs. 17, 21-23 and similarly they call
\mathcal{A}_{m}
the number
k^{(3)}_{SA_{m}}
. Assuming this we go to the next step of their ‘no-go’ deduction.

ii) The 4 relations Eq. 17, 21-23. are clearly incompatible. If we multiply Eq. 21 by Eqs. 22 and 23 we obtain

\displaystyle p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}q^{(2)}_{SAB_{2}}r^{(2)}_{SAB_{3}}(p^{(3)}_{SA_{1}}q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}})^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}q^{(2)}_{SAB_{2}}r^{(2)}_{SAB_{3}}=-1,

(24)

which clearly contradicts Eq. 17. Now as they emphasize it clearly: ‘
Note that, most importantly for the sequel, the
three [unitary] transformations [acting on
m=1,2,3
] mutually commute, and thus their order of application is immaterial.
’. In other words: Since the 3 operations leading to
|GHZ^{\prime}\rangle_{SAB}
,
|GHZ^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{SAB}
,
|GHZ^{\prime\prime}\rangle_{SAB}
and thus Eqs. 21-23 are acting ‘locally’ only on one of the sub systems
SA_{m}
their meaning should be non contextual and absolute. This following
Zukowski
justifies why we should apriori compare these states with
|GHZ\rangle_{SAB}
and Eq. 17. This noncontextual reading is what they believe is contained in RQM.

If we accept this reasoning then RQM contradicts quantum mechanics. Relative facts for Alice and Bob are contradictory.

However, this deduction is false and we now debunk the contradiction. First, consider point i): It is clear that
p^{(2)}_{SAB_{1}}
must be a relative fact for Bob. However, there is no reason to consider that
q^{(3)}_{SA_{2}}
and
r^{(3)}_{SA_{3}}
should be relative facts for Alice. Actually, the quantum state Eq. 20 contains quantum numbers
p,q,r
but in RQM the fundamental description for Bob is the reduced density matrix
\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}=\textrm{Tr}_{B}[\hat{\rho^{\prime}}_{SAB}]
obtained by using
\hat{\rho^{\prime}}_{SAB}=|GHZ^{\prime}\rangle_{SAB}{}_{SAB}\langle|GHZ^{\prime}|
. We have:

\displaystyle\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}=\frac{1}{2}|+1^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{1}}{}_{SA_{1}}\langle+1^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\otimes|\Phi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}{}_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}\langle\Phi|

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}|-1^{(2)}\rangle_{SA_{1}}{}_{SA_{1}}\langle-1^{(2)}|

\displaystyle\otimes|\Psi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}{}_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}\langle\Psi|

(25)

where

\displaystyle|\Phi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|+1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}],

\displaystyle|\Psi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[|+1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|+1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}

\displaystyle+|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{2}}|-1^{(3)}\rangle_{SA_{3}}]

(26)

are two Bell states. The fact that EPR-like Bell states are present show that there is a coherence preserved in the description of the system SA by Bob. In particular we deduce

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=+1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=-1,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=0,

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}_{SA}[{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{1}}{\sigma_{y}}_{SA_{2}}{\sigma_{x}}_{SA_{3}}\hat{\rho^{\prime}}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}]=0.

The second line is of course reminiscent of Eq. 21 but the meaning is here different from what is claimed in
Zukowski
. Indeed, this a relation for Bob measurements not for Alice. The idea to have a mixture of information for Bob and Alice in the same Eq. 21 is thus unjustified and is not a part of RQM but instead of the reading of RQM made by the authors of
Zukowski
. The first line is also very interesting: It shows coherence associated with the
|\Phi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}
and
|\Psi\rangle_{SA_{2},SA_{3}}
Bell states. Moreover this result can be compared with Eq. 19 for
\hat{\rho}^{(red.)}_{SA|B}
. The difference clearly stresses that the measurement procedures are more invasive that claimed in
Zukowski
. In RQM like in the Copenhagen interpretation the experimental context and the choice of the interaction Hamiltonian is key. This allows us us to answer to point ii) concerning non-contextuality. Indeed, the non contextuality supposed by the authors of
Zukowski
is not a part of the RQM. For RQM (like in the orthodox interpretation) the different contexts are not compatible and we have no right to compare Eqs. 17 and Eqs. 21-23 as claimed. This would otherwise contradict the central axioms of RQM and therefore the reasoning discussed in
Zukowski
is unjustified.

We conclude this analysis by adding that RQM is a self-consistent interpretation of quantum mechanics extending the old Copenhagen interpretation. The formalism is perfectly agreeing with standard quantum mechanics and recovers the orthodox interpretation at the limit were observers are essentially macroscopic but without the problems concerning the definition of agents in Qbism. Several issues concerning interactions can still be debated but we should not focus on wrong problems. Often objections are done without carefully considering the philosophy of RQM but by adding some prejudices or preconceptions concerning the interpretation (i.e.,
Zukowski
;
Brukner
;
Pienaar
). I hope this note will contribute to clarify a bit this issue.

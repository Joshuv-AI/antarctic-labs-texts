The Dark Universe after Reheating in String Inflation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Michele Cicoli, Kuver Sinha and Robert Wiley Deal

Abstract
We study the production of dark matter and dark radiation after reheating in string inflation models where the Calabi-Yau has a fibred structure and the visible sector lives on D3 branes. We show how the interplay between different physical constraints from inflation, reheating, supersymmetry breaking and dark radiation, leads to distinct predictions for the nature of dark matter.

Cicoli et al. 2017b
;

(

) Proceeding along statistical lines, by drawing statistical conclusions about the distribution of important phenomenological quantities like the scale of supersymmetry breaking and the axion decay constant, from the ensemble of type IIB flux vacua
Douglas 2012
;
Denef and Douglas 2007
;
Denef and Douglas 2004
;
Broeckel et al. 2020
;
Broeckel et al. 2021a
;
Broeckel et al. 2021b
;

Demirtas et al. 2020
.

Amongst various classes of models in the first direction, type IIB Large Volume flux compactifications are particularly well-developed. Two main inflationary scenarios emerge in this context: Kähler moduli inflation (KMI)
Conlon and Quevedo 2006
;
Bond et al. 2007
and Fibre Inflation (FI)
Cicoli et al. 2009
;
Broy et al. 2016
;
Cicoli et al. 2016b
. KMI is a small-field model where inflation is driven by a blow-up mode with a non-perturbative scalar potential. The Hubble scale during inflation is relatively low,
H_{I}\sim 5\times 10^{8}
GeV, and the tensor-to-scalar ratio is unobservable
r\simeq 10^{-10}
. On the other hand, FI is a large field model characterized by
H_{I}\simeq 5\times 10^{13}
GeV and
r\simeq 0.007

Cicoli and Di Valentino 2020
, where the inflaton is a fibration bulk modulus with a perturbative scalar potential.

In these constructions the visible sector can live on either D7-branes wrapping 4-cycles in the geometric regime, or D3-branes at singularities. In the first case, the soft terms are around the gravitino mass,
M_{1/2}\sim m_{0}\sim m_{3/2}
, while in the second case the visible sector can be sequestered from the sources of supersymmetry breaking in the bulk, resulting in soft terms which can be hierarchically smaller than the gravitino mass
Blumenhagen et al. 2009
;
Aparicio et al. 2014
. Two limits can arise: a so-called
local limit
with a split-SUSY spectrum featuring
M_{1/2}\ll m_{0}\ll m_{3/2}
, and an
ultralocal limit
with a more standard MSSM-like spectrum with
M_{1/2}\sim m_{0}\ll m_{3/2}
.

Reheating via the decay of the modulus with the smallest decay width has already been studied in both KMI and FI for several D-brane configurations which can realize an MSSM-like sector together with additional hidden sectors.
In particular, reheating for KMI in the simplest Swiss-cheese LVS models has been studied in
Cicoli and Mazumdar 2011
;
Cicoli et al. 2013b
;
Higaki and Takahashi 2012
for D3-branes in the ultralocal limit, in
Cicoli and Muia 2015a
for D3-branes in the local limit, and in
Cicoli and Mazumdar 2010
;
Hebecker et al. 2014
for D7-branes. On the other hand, reheating for FI with the MSSM on D3-branes has been studied in
Angus 2014
, and in
Cicoli and Piovano 2019a
for the D7-brane case. Each of these references analyzed in detail the constraints arising from the requirement to avoid an excessive production of ultra-light bulk axions that behave as dark radiation. Moreover
Allahverdi et al. 2013
;
Allahverdi et al. 2014
;
Aparicio et al. 2015
;
Aparicio et al. 2016
studied the implications for non-thermal neutralino dark matter for KMI with the MSSM on D3-branes in the ultralocal limit. The constraints on the nature of dark matter for KMI with the visible sector on D7-branes has instead been analyzed in
Allahverdi et al. 2021
for superheavy WIMPs and in
Hebecker et al. 2022
for the QCD axion realized as a closed string mode.

The largest production of dark radiation from the decay of the lightest modulus has been found in
Angus 2014
for FI with the MSSM on D3-branes. This result relies on a particular expression of the moduli-dependence of the Giudice-Masiero contribution to the Kähler potential which determines an effective decoupling of the lightest modulus from the Higgs degrees of freedom. In this paper we will revisit this result by considering a more general moduli-dependence of the Giudice-Masiero term that allows to considerably reduce the production of dark radiation. In doing so, we shall follow the results of
Aparicio et al. 2008
which constrained the form of the Kähler potential by analogy with explicit toroidal computations.

We will then study the associated production of dark matter after reheating in FI. We will find that WIMPs are always overproduced, requiring a mechanism of R-parity breaking to make them unstable. In this case, a very promising dark matter candidate is instead the QCD axion realized as the phase of a charged open string field. The axion decay constant is around
f_{\rm QCD}\simeq 5\times 10^{10}
GeV, which can avoid isocurvature bounds and lead to dark matter in a rather natural way. We will extend our analysis also to fibred Calabi-Yau compactifications with KMI and the MSSM on D3-branes. The best case scenario to avoid dark radiation overproduction with the minimal tuning of the coefficient of the Giudice-Masiero interaction among the moduli and the Higges, is the ultralocal limit. In this case, we will find that dark matter can be TeV-scale non-thermal neutralinos which are produced from the inflaton decay and then undergo annihilation.

Our analysis shows how UV correlations among different observables in string compactifications, like inflation, supersymmetry breaking and reheating, is very powerful to obtain clear predictions for the nature of dark matter. Moreover, we will see that avoiding dark radiation overproduction severely constrains the moduli-dependence of the Kähler metric for matter fields.

We will also point out that, contrary to what was claimed in previous studies
Cicoli et al. 2013b
, the moduli decay to open string axions can compete with the decay to bulk axions, and so lead to an additional source of dark radiation.

V.1
and
V.2

VI
.

II
Non-thermal dark matter

Before focusing on string models, we first briefly review non-thermal dark matter (DM) produced by heavy scalar decay
Kane et al. 2015
;
Allahverdi et al. 2020
. The lightest modulus,
\phi
, dominates the energy density of the universe from the onset of its oscillations until its decay. The decay then produces a significant amount of entropy, diluting any previously existing DM particles to a negligible abundance. Depending on the reheat temperature of
\phi
,
T_{\text{rh}}
, the DM particles produced by
\phi
decay may fall into a few different scenarios depending on the DM freeze-out temperature
T_{\rm f}\simeq m_{\rm DM}/20

Allahverdi et al. 2013
.

T_{\text{rh}}\gtrsim T_{\text{f}}

In this case, the DM may equilibrate. Due to the DM-DM annihilations, the DM abundance is simply given by that of the thermal scenario.

T_{\text{rh}}\lesssim T_{\text{f}}

Here, we have two sub-cases depending on the efficiency of the DM annihilations. More precisely, defining the critical abundance of the DM,
Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}
, as the attractor of the relevant Boltzmann equation, we have

\displaystyle Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}\simeq\frac{H}{\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle s}\Big|_{T_{\text{rh}}}.

(1)

If the produced DM abundance is larger than the critical abundance, i.e.
Y_{\text{DM}}>Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}
, some annihilations still occur until the abundance reaches
Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}
, at which point the DM becomes too dilute to annihilate efficiently. We refer to this case as the
annihilation scenario.
In the annihilation scenario, the final DM abundance can be estimated by

\displaystyle Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}\simeq\left(\frac{n_{\text{DM}}}{s}\right)_{\text{obs}}\frac{\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle_{\text{f}}^{\text{th}}}{\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle_{\text{f}}}\left(\frac{T_{\text{f}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)

(2)

where
\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle_{\text{f}}^{\text{th}}\simeq 3\times 10^{-26}\text{ cm}^{3}s^{-1}
is the value needed in the thermal case
Albert et al. 2017
to match the observed DM abundance

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n_{\text{DM}}}{s}\right)_{\text{obs}}\simeq 5\times 10^{-10}\left(\frac{1\text{ GeV}}{m_{\text{DM}}}\right).

(3)

The DM abundance in the annihilation scenario is thus enhanced by
T_{\text{f}}/T_{\text{rh}}
in comparison to the thermal case, and can accommodate thermally underproduced DM candidates, such as wino-like and higgsino-like DM. Since
T_{\text{rh}}\lesssim T_{f}\sim m_{\text{DM}}/20
, we must have
\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle_{f}^{\text{th}}<\langle\sigma_{\text{ann}}v\rangle_{f}
to reproduce the observed DM abundance.

If the produced DM abundance is lower than the critical abundance, i.e.
Y_{\text{DM}}<Y_{\text{DM}}^{c}
, then annihilations cannot occur significantly. We refer to this case as the
branching scenario.
The late-time DM abundance is given simply by the fraction of the light modulus abundance,
Y_{\phi}
, that decays to DM

\displaystyle Y_{\text{DM}}=Y_{\phi}\,\text{Br}_{\text{DM}}.

(4)

If the branching ratio to DM is sufficiently low, the branching scenario can accommodate thermally overproduced DM, such as bino-like DM.

III
Fibred Calabi-Yau Models

Fibred Calabi-Yau manifolds have been shown to be very promising for cosmological and phenomenological applications. The simplest setup involves three Kähler moduli. To settle notation for the rest of the paper, we briefly review this class of compactifications.

The first Kähler modulus we consider is
T_{1}=\tau_{1}+{\rm i}c_{1}
. The scalar
\tau_{1}
parametrizes the dimensionless volume in string units of a K3 or
T^{4}
fibre over a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
base. The axionic component
c_{1}
arises from the reduction of the 10D RR form
C_{4}
on the K3 or
T^{4}
divisor. The scalar component of the second modulus
T_{2}=\tau_{2}+{\rm i}c_{2}
controls the volume of the base of the fibration, while
c_{2}
is the associated axion. The scalar component of the third modulus
T_{3}=\tau_{3}+{\rm i}c_{3}
parametrizes the size of a blow-up mode required to stabilize the volume at leading order, and
c_{3}
is an additional closed string axion. The volume takes the form (for explicit Calabi-Yau models with this volume form see
Cicoli et al. 2012c
;
Cicoli et al. 2016a
;
Cicoli et al. 2017b
)

\mathcal{V}=\sqrt{\tau_{1}}\tau_{2}-\tau^{3/2}_{3}\,.

(5)

The Kähler potential and the superpotential read

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{\rm tree}+K_{\alpha^{\prime}}+K_{g_{s}}

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{0}+A_{1}e^{-\mathfrak{a}_{1}T_{1}}+A_{2}e^{-\mathfrak{a}_{2}T_{2}}+A_{3}e^{-\mathfrak{a}_{3}T_{3}}.

(6)

The tree-level Kähler potential is
K_{\rm tree}=-2\ln{\mathcal{V}}
(setting
m_{P}=1
) and it enjoys a well-known no-scale property which makes all Kähler moduli flat at semiclassical level. At this order of approximation, background 3-form fluxes freeze the complex structure moduli and the dilaton whose VEV sets the string coupling
g_{s}
which we assume to be in the weak coupling regime, i.e.
g_{s}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(0.1)
, to trust perturbation theory. The leading order
\alpha^{\prime 3}
correction is given by
K_{\alpha^{\prime}}=-\frac{\xi}{g^{3/2}_{s}\mathcal{V}}
, where
\xi
is the Calabi-Yau Euler number,
\xi\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, while for string loop corrections to
K
, given by
K_{g_{s}}
, we refer to
Cicoli and Piovano 2019a
. The superpotential contains instead the tree-level flux-generated contribution
W_{0}
(which is a tunable constant after complex structure and dilaton stabilization), and non-perturbative corrections for each of the three Kähler moduli, where the
A_{i}
’s are expected to be
\mathcal{O}(1)
constants while
\mathfrak{a}_{i}=2\pi/N_{i}
with
N_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.

In the large volume limit where the effective field theory is under control,
\tau_{2}\gtrsim\tau_{1}\gg\tau_{3}>1
, the leading order contribution to the potential for the Kähler moduli arises from
K_{\alpha^{\prime}}
and the
T_{3}
-dependent non-perturbative correction to
W
. This potential fixes
\tau_{3}
,
c_{3}
and the overall volume
\mathcal{V}
at

\langle\tau_{3}\rangle\simeq g_{s}^{-1}\qquad\langle\mathcal{V}\rangle\simeq W_{0}\,e^{\mathfrak{a}_{3}/g_{s}}\qquad\langle c_{3}\rangle=\pi/\mathfrak{a}_{3}.

(7)

The minimum is AdS and breaks supersymmetry spontaneously. There are several known mechanism to uplift it to Minkowski, among which anti-D3 branes
Kachru et al. 2003
, T-branes
Cicoli et al. 2016c
, or non-zero F-terms of the complex structure moduli
Gallego et al. 2017
. At subleading order,
K_{g_{s}}
(or higher order
\alpha^{\prime}
corrections
Ciupke et al. 2015
;
Cicoli et al. 2016b
), fix the direction orthogonal to the volume mode
u\equiv\tau_{1}/\tau_{2}
at
Cicoli and Piovano 2019a

\langle u\rangle=\lambda\,g_{s}^{2}\,,

(8)

where
\lambda
depends on the tunable coefficients of string loop corrections to
K
. Finally, tiny
T_{1}
- and
T_{2}
-dependent non-perturbative effects fix the two ultra-light axions
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
at
\langle c_{i}\rangle=\pi/\mathfrak{a}_{i}

\forall i=1,2
.

The gravitino mass takes the form (restoring appropriate powers of
m_{P}
)

m_{3/2}^{2}\simeq\kappa\epsilon^{2}m_{P}^{2}

(9)

where
Burgess et al. 2010

\epsilon\equiv W_{0}/\mathcal{V}\ll 1\quad\text{and}\quad\kappa\equiv g_{s}/(8\pi)\ll 1

(10)

and the moduli mass spectrum is given by
Cicoli and Mazumdar 2010
;
Cicoli and Mazumdar 2011
;
Cicoli et al. 2017c

\displaystyle m_{\tau_{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq m_{c_{3}}^{2}\simeq\left(\ln\epsilon\right)^{2}m_{3/2}^{2}>m_{3/2}^{2}

\displaystyle m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{\epsilon}{g_{s}^{3/2}W_{0}|\left(\ln\epsilon\right)^{3}|}\right)m_{\tau_{3}}^{2}\ll m_{\tau_{3}}^{2}

\displaystyle m_{u}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{\epsilon^{1/3}g_{s}^{5/6}|\ln\epsilon|}{W_{0}^{1/3}\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}<m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}

\displaystyle m_{c_{i}}^{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\tau_{i}^{3}}{W_{0}}\,e^{-\mathfrak{a}_{i}\tau_{i}}m_{3/2}^{2}\ll m_{u}^{2}\quad\forall i=1,2

(11)

The visible sector lives on D3-branes at singularities which can give rise to a scenario of sequestered SUSY breaking
Blumenhagen et al. 2009
. The common gaugino masses,
M_{1/2}
, can then be estimated using the results of
Aparicio et al. 2014

M_{1/2}\simeq\frac{3\omega}{2}\frac{m_{3/2}}{\mathcal{V}}\,\tau_{3}^{3/2}\sim\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{m_{3/2}}{\mathcal{V}}\left(\ln\mathcal{V}\right)^{3/2}\right)

(12)

where
\omega
is a tunable flux-dependent parameter. The
\mu
-term, if generated by a Giudice-Masiero contribution to the Kähler potential, is expected to be approximately of order
M_{1/2}
since it turns out to be
\mu\simeq\tilde{\omega}\,M_{1/2}
where
\tilde{\omega}
is another tunable flux-dependent parameter. On the other hand, the value of the common slepton and squark mass, denoted as
m_{0}
, depends on the exact moduli-dependence of the Kähler metric for matter fields. The so-called “local limit” is defined as the case where the physical Yukawas are independent of the volume
only to leading order
in
\mathcal{V}^{-1}
, while the “ultralocal limit” is defined to be the case where the physical Yukawas are independent of the volume
to any order
in
\mathcal{V}^{-1}

Aparicio et al. 2014
. Consequently, the local limit leads to a split SUSY scenario, whereas the ultralocal case can give a standard MSSM-like spectrum

\displaystyle\text{local limit}:\qquad\quad m_{0}\simeq M_{1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}\gg M_{1/2}

\displaystyle\text{ultralocal limit}:\quad m_{0}\simeq M_{1/2}.

(13)

IV
Moduli decays and dark radiation

As argued above, reheating is given by the decay of the lightest modulus, which in the case of fibred Calabi-Yaus is
u
. This Kähler modulus can decay to visible sector fields on D3-branes at singularities, but also to the two ultra-light closed string axions
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
which behave as dark radiation.

The decay to SUSY scalars is kinematically forbidden in the local limit, whereas it is mass-suppressed in the ultralocal limit. Similarly, the decay to SM fermions, Higgsinos and gauginos is chirality suppressed. Moreover, the decay to SM gauge bosons is loop suppressed since the gauge kinetic function for D3-branes is controlled by the dilaton. The only relevant decay rates are therefore to Higgses via a Giudice-Masiero interaction term in
K
, and to the closed string axions
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
. We will also argue that, if the QCD axion is realized as the phase of a charged open string field, the lightest modulus decay into this mode should also be taken into account since it is not mass-suppressed.

Assembling all the results, we then provide constraints on the parameter space coming from observed upper bounds on the effective number of neutrino species
N_{\rm eff}
.

IV.1
Canonical normalization

We begin by generalizing the volume form (
5
) to

\mathcal{V}=\tau_{1}^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}}\tau_{2}^{\frac{n_{2}}{2}}

(14)

with the implicit constraint that
n_{1}+n_{2}=3
. Notice that we ignored the blow-up mode
\tau_{3}
given that it does not play any relevant role in reheating. This volume corresponds to a Kähler potential of the form

\displaystyle\frac{K}{m_{P}^{2}}

\displaystyle=-n_{1}\ln(T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1})-n_{2}\ln(T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2}).

(15)

The Kähler metric,
K_{i\overline{\jmath}}\equiv\partial_{i}\partial_{\overline{\jmath}}K
, is then given by

\displaystyle K_{i\overline{\jmath}}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{P}^{2}}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{n_{1}}{\tau_{1}^{2}}&&0\\
0&&\frac{n_{2}}{\tau_{2}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

(16)

As expected, since the Kähler potential is separable, we have already a diagonal Kähler metric.

The kinetic term in the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=K_{i\overline{\jmath}}\partial_{\mu}T^{i}\partial^{\mu}\overline{T}^{\overline{\jmath}}\supset\frac{m_{P}^{2}}{4}\frac{n_{i}}{\tau_{i}^{2}}\partial_{\mu}\tau_{i}\partial^{\mu}\tau_{i}

(17)

can then be put into canonical form with the field redefinitions

\displaystyle\tau_{i}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{i}}}\frac{\phi_{i}}{m_{P}}\right)

(18)

where
\phi_{i}
are the new fields with canonical kinetic terms.

There is, however, still a degeneracy - the moduli
\phi_{i}
are, in general, not mass eigenstates. As we have seen in Section
III
, the Calabi-Yau volume is fixed by the leading order dynamics, and so the mass eigenstates are the volume mode and its transverse direction. The volume mode is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}

\displaystyle\propto\ln\mathcal{V}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\tau_{1}^{n_{1}}\tau_{2}^{n_{2}}\right).

(19)

Plugging in our field redefinitions, Eq. (
18
), into this expression and multiplying by an overall normalization constant, we have the volume mode in terms of
\phi_{i}
:

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{2}.

(20)

The transverse mode,
\phi_{u}
, can then be constructed simply by orthogonality:

\displaystyle\phi_{u}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{2}.

(21)

We will also utilize the inverse of these transformations:

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{\mathcal{V}}-\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{u}

\displaystyle\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{\mathcal{V}}+\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{1}+n_{2}}}\,\phi_{u}.

(22)

IV.2
Decays to closed string axions

We now discuss the moduli decays into closed string axions
c_{i}
. We start by returning to the kinetic term (
17
), which also contains the terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=K_{i\overline{\jmath}}\partial_{\mu}T^{i}\partial^{\mu}\overline{T}^{\overline{\jmath}}\supset\frac{m_{P}^{2}}{4}\frac{n_{i}}{\tau_{i}^{2}}\partial_{\mu}c_{i}\partial^{\mu}c_{i}.

(23)

Applying the field redefinition (
18
), expanding the exponential, and rescaling the axion fields by

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{i}}}\,\frac{a_{i}}{m_{P}}

(24)

we obtain canonical kinetic terms for the axion fields
a_{i}
in addition to the interaction terms

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset-\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{i}}}\,\frac{\phi_{i}}{m_{P}}\,\partial_{\mu}a_{i}\partial^{\mu}a_{i}.

(25)

Applying now the transformations into the moduli mass eigenbasis, Eq. (
IV.1
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}\left(\partial_{\mu}a_{1}\partial^{\mu}a_{1}+\partial_{\mu}a_{2}\partial^{\mu}a_{2}\right)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\phi_{u}}{m_{P}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}\,\partial_{\mu}a_{2}\partial^{\mu}a_{2}-\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\,\partial_{\mu}a_{1}\partial^{\mu}a_{1}\right)

(26)

where we have explicitly used the constraint
n_{1}+n_{2}=3
.

Focusing on the transverse mode,
\phi_{u}
, we can now write down the total decay width to closed string axions (considering both axions to be massless)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi_{u}\rightarrow aa)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48\pi}\left(\frac{n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\right)\frac{m_{u}^{3}}{m_{P}^{2}}\equiv c_{\rm hid}\,\Gamma_{0}

(27)

where we have defined

\displaystyle c_{\rm hid}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\frac{n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}\right)

(28)

to be the coefficient of hidden sector decays.
We have also made the definition

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{48\pi}\frac{m_{u}^{3}}{m_{P}^{2}}

(29)

for future convenience.

IV.3
Decays to open string axions

Open string axions, which arise as the phase of charged matter fields acquiring a non-zero VEV, are more model-dependent than the closed string bulk axions we considered above. In
Cicoli et al. 2013b
, the modulus decay to open string axions was considered and claimed to be negligible. Here, we demonstrate that this is not necessarily the case - both within our fibred LVS scenario and the minimal LVS scenario considered in
Cicoli et al. 2013b
. We begin by considering open string axions within the context of minimal LVS with only one bulk Kähler modulus corresponding to the volume mode. For matter fields, collectively denoted as
C
, the relevant term in the Kähler potential is

\displaystyle\frac{K}{m_{P}^{2}}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{C\overline{C}}{T_{b}+\overline{T}_{b}}

(30)

where
T_{b}
is the bulk modulus, corresponding to the choice
n_{1}=3
,
n_{2}=0
in Eq. (
15
).
We can then write down the Lagrangian for the canonical moduli after using Eq. (
18
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{m_{P}}{2\sqrt{6}}\,\phi_{\mathcal{V}}\left(C\Box\overline{C}+\overline{C}\Box C\right).

(31)

The matter field can then be parameterized by
C=\rho\,\exp({\rm i}\,\theta),
where
\rho
is the radial component which acquires a VEV
\langle\rho\rangle\neq 0
via D-term stabilization, while
\theta
is the phase field taking the role of the axion. Once
\rho
takes on its VEV, and after going to canonically normalized fields
\tilde{\rho}
and
\tilde{\theta}
, defined as
\rho=\sqrt{\langle\tau_{b}\rangle}\tilde{\rho}/m_{P}
and
\tilde{\theta}=\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle\theta
, Eq. (
31
) gives

\mathcal{L}\supset-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}\,\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\theta}\,\partial^{\mu}\tilde{\theta}.

(32)

Notice that the axion decay constant
f_{\theta}
is set by
\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle
, i.e.
f_{\theta}=\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle
. To put Eq. (
32
) in a more illuminating form, we can integrate by parts which gives us

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}\,m_{P}}\left[2\phi_{\mathcal{V}}\,\tilde{\theta}\Box\tilde{\theta}-\tilde{\theta}^{2}\Box\phi_{\mathcal{V}}\right].

(33)

The first term leads to a decay width which is mass suppressed, that was considered in
Cicoli et al. 2013b
. However, the second term leads to a decay width proportional to
\Gamma_{0}
. Thus, the decay width into open string axions in minimal LVS is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\phi_{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\tilde{\theta}\tilde{\theta}\right)=\frac{1}{16}\,\Gamma_{0}\,.

(34)

While this is not as significant as moduli decays to closed string axions for the production of dark radiation, it slightly increases the tension for minimal LVS models which consider open string axions as the QCD axion. Notice moreover that the modulus decay width into the radial part is instead mass suppressed since it arises from an interaction term of the form

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}\,\tilde{\rho}\Box\tilde{\rho}.

(35)

The decay to a bulk axion and an open string axion is also possible. This interaction comes also from expanding Eq. (
30
), leading to a Lagrangian of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{{\rm i}}{3}\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}^{2}}\left(C\,\partial_{\mu}\overline{C}\,\partial^{\mu}a_{b}-\overline{C}\,\partial_{\mu}C\,\partial^{\mu}a_{b}\right).

(36)

When
\tilde{\rho}
takes on its VEV, this becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle}{m_{P}}\right)\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}\,\partial_{\mu}\tilde{\theta}\,\partial^{\mu}a_{b}

(37)

and although this term leads to a decay width proportional to
\Gamma_{0}
, it is also suppressed by
\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle/m_{P}\ll 1
. Thus, we find the contributions from the
\phi_{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow\tilde{\theta}a_{b}
decay to be negligible.

We now consider open string axions within fibred LVS. In this scenario, we consider a general Kähler potential for matter fields of the form

\displaystyle\frac{K}{m_{P}^{2}}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{C\overline{C}}{\left(T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1}\right)^{x_{1}}\left(T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2}\right)^{x_{2}}}

(38)

where
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are constants which fix the moduli dependence of the matter Kähler metric. These constants are not entirely arbitrary, but rather primarily fixed by the brane configuration.
Based on results from toroidal orientifolds - which have a similar volume scaling (
\mathcal{V}=\sqrt{\tau_{1}\tau_{2}\tau_{3}}
) to the fibred Calabi-Yau case (
\mathcal{V}=\sqrt{\tau_{1}}\tau_{2}
) through the limit
\tau_{3}\rightarrow\tau_{2}
- in the case at hand where the matter fields live on D3-branes,
Aparicio et al. 2008
suggests two cases

•

x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=0

•

x_{1}=0
and
x_{2}=1
.

Notice that both of them reproduce the scaling of Eq. (
30
).

Keeping for now the general form, we can utilize the field redefinitions from Eq. (
18
) and have the following Kähler potential

\displaystyle\frac{K}{m_{P}^{2}}

\displaystyle\supset\frac{1}{2}C\overline{C}\left(1-x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}-x_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

(39)

after expanding the exponentials. The relevant interaction terms in the Lagrangian in terms of the moduli mass eigenbasis turn out to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\frac{m_{P}}{2\sqrt{6}}\left(C\Box\overline{C}+\overline{C}\Box C\right)\phi_{\mathcal{V}}

(40)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{m_{P}}{2\sqrt{6}}\left(x_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}-x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\right)\left(C\Box\overline{C}+\overline{C}\Box C\right)\phi_{u}

where we have explicitly used the constraints
n_{1}+n_{2}=3
and
x_{1}+x_{2}=1
. Focusing on the transverse mode,
\phi_{u}
, we can write down the interaction with the open string axion once the radial component acquires a VEV

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2\sqrt{6}\,m_{P}}\left(x_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}-x_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\right)\left[2\phi_{u}\,\tilde{\theta}\Box\tilde{\theta}-\tilde{\theta}^{2}\Box\phi_{u}\right].

Once again, the normalization of the kinetic terms gives
\langle\tilde{\rho}\rangle=f_{\theta}
. Specializing to
n_{1}=1
and
n_{2}=2
, which reproduces the volume form (
5
), the decay to open string axions is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(\phi_{u}\rightarrow\tilde{\theta}\tilde{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\Gamma_{0}/8&\text{for }\,x_{1}=1\text{ and }x_{2}=0\\
\Gamma_{0}/32&\text{for }x_{1}=0\text{ and }x_{2}=1\,.\end{cases}

(41)

Thus, we see an enhancement compared to the minimal LVS case if the Kähler metric depends on
\tau_{1}
, and a reduction if the Kähler metric depends on
\tau_{2}
. In this work, we focus on the case where
x_{1}=0
and
x_{2}=1
, so that additional contributions to dark radiation are minimized.

IV.4
Decays to Higgses

We analyze the Giudice-Masiero terms for separable moduli Kähler potentials by starting with the form

\displaystyle\frac{K}{m_{P}^{2}}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\frac{H_{u}\overline{H}_{u}}{(T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1})^{y_{1}}(T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2})^{y_{2}}}+\frac{H_{d}\overline{H}_{d}}{(T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1})^{w_{1}}(T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2})^{w_{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{ZH_{u}H_{d}+\text{h.c.}}{(T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1})^{k_{1}}(T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2})^{k_{2}}}

(42)

where we assume
Z
is constant with respect to the
T_{i}
. Moreover, making again an analogy with the toroidal case and following
Aparicio et al. 2008
, we set
y_{1}+y_{2}=w_{1}+w_{2}=1
. We also focus on cases where the Giudice-Masiero term has a Kähler metric of product form (i.e. assume that
K_{H_{u}H_{d}}=\sqrt{K_{H_{u}}K_{H_{d}}}
), so that
k_{i}=(y_{i}+w_{i})/2

\forall i=1,2
. This leads to three possibilities

•

k_{1}=1
and
k_{2}=0

•

k_{1}=0
and
k_{2}=1

•

k_{1}=k_{2}=1/2
.

We may now utilize the field redefinitions from Eq. (
18
). After expanding the exponentials and going to canonically normalized Higgs fields defined by

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H_{u}}{\sqrt{\langle T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1}\rangle^{y_{1}}\langle T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2}\rangle^{y_{2}}}}

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{H_{d}}{\sqrt{\langle T_{1}+\overline{T}_{1}\rangle^{w_{1}}\langle T_{2}+\overline{T}_{2}\rangle^{w_{2}}}}\,,

(43)

we obtain the following interaction terms

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle-\tilde{H}_{u}\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}\left(y_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}+y_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

\displaystyle-\tilde{H}_{d}\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}\left(w_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}+w_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle-(Z\tilde{H}_{u}\tilde{H}_{d}+\text{h.c.})\left(k_{1}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}+k_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

which generate the following contributions to the interaction Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{H}_{u}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}\Box\tilde{H}_{u}\right)\left(\frac{y_{1}}{\sqrt{2n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}+\frac{y_{2}}{\sqrt{2n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\tilde{H}_{d}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}\Box\tilde{H}_{d}\right)\left(\frac{w_{1}}{\sqrt{2n_{1}}}\frac{\phi_{1}}{m_{P}}+\frac{w_{2}}{\sqrt{2n_{2}}}\frac{\phi_{2}}{m_{P}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{m_{P}}\left(Z\tilde{H}_{u}\tilde{H}_{d}+\text{h.c.}\right)\left(\frac{k_{1}}{\sqrt{2n_{1}}}\Box\phi_{1}+\frac{k_{2}}{\sqrt{2n_{2}}}\Box\phi_{2}\right).

(45)

We now move to the moduli mass eigenbasis using Eq. (
IV.1
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\tilde{H}_{u}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}\Box\tilde{H}_{u}\right)\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\tilde{H}_{d}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}\Box\tilde{H}_{d}\right)\frac{\phi_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{P}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}\,m_{P}}(Z\tilde{H}_{u}\tilde{H}_{d}+\text{h.c.})\,\Box\phi_{\mathcal{V}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\tilde{H}_{u}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{u}\Box\tilde{H}_{u}\right)\frac{\phi_{u}}{m_{P}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{6}}\left(\tilde{H}_{d}\Box\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}+\overline{\tilde{H}}_{d}\Box\tilde{H}_{d}\right)\frac{\phi_{u}}{m_{P}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{6}\,m_{P}}(Z\tilde{H}_{u}\tilde{H}_{d}+\text{h.c.})\,\Box\phi_{u}

(46)

where we have explicitly restored the constraints
y_{1}+y_{2}=w_{1}+w_{2}=k_{1}+k_{2}=1
and
n_{1}+n_{2}=3
and we made the definitions

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\equiv\left(y_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}-y_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle\equiv\left(w_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}-w_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\right)

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\alpha+\beta)=\left(k_{2}\sqrt{\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}}}-k_{1}\sqrt{\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}}}\right).

(47)

Focusing on the transverse mode,
\phi_{u}
, we note that the final term will be dominant - the others will be suppressed by a factor of
m_{H}^{2}/m_{u}^{2}
. The dominant term is therefore proportional to the coefficient
\gamma
which for
n_{1}=1
and
n_{2}=2
, reproducing Eq. (
5
), reduces to

\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(k_{2}-2k_{1}\right).

(48)

It is worth stressing that this coefficients vanishes only for
k_{2}=2k_{1}
which is however never the case for our options since it would be inconsistent with the results of
Aparicio et al. 2008
. The fact that
\gamma\neq 0
is crucial to avoid dark radiation overproduction, contrary to the results of
Angus 2014
which considered
k_{1}=1/3
and
k_{2}=2/3
. The intuitive reason why
k_{2}=2k_{1}
would imply no interaction between
\phi_{u}
and the Higgses (at least at leading order) is that
\phi_{u}
is orthogonal to the volume mode and in this case the Kähler moduli dependence of the Giudice-Masiero term in Eq. (
IV.4
) would scale exactly as an inverse power of the overall volume since
\tau_{1}^{-k_{1}}\tau_{2}^{-k_{2}}=\mathcal{V}^{-2k_{1}}
for
k_{2}=2k_{1}
.

Let us close this section by commenting briefly on a recent result. In
Hebecker et al. 2022
, it was argued that there should be an additional coupling between the Higgs sector and the moduli. This coupling is induced through loop corrections to the Higgs mass term due to the volume dependence of the running from some high scale (e.g. the Kaluza-Klein scale) down to the SUSY breaking scale, and is of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle\supset c_{\rm loop}\left(\frac{m_{u}}{m_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{2}\,\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{m_{P}}\,\phi_{u}h^{2}

(49)

where
h
is the SM Higgs field,
c_{\rm loop}\sim 1/(16\pi^{2})
is a typical loop coefficient and the
(m_{u}/m_{\mathcal{V}})^{2}\sim\mathcal{V}^{-1/3}
factor is due to the mixing between
\phi_{\mathcal{V}}
and
\phi_{u}
induced by string loop corrections to
K

Cicoli et al. 2012d
. Thus, we would expect the decay width to be proportional to

\Gamma\sim c_{\rm loop}^{2}\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{4}\frac{m_{u}^{3}}{m_{P}^{2}}.

(50)

In the case of D3-branes at singularities, we have seen that scalar masses scale as
m_{0}\sim M_{1/2}\sim m_{\mathcal{V}}/\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}
in the ultralocal limit, and so we can safely neglect this induced coupling. On the other hand, in the local limit
m_{0}\simeq M_{1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}\simeq m_{\mathcal{V}}
, and so a more careful study is needed. The exact ratio between SUSY scalar masses and the mass of the volume mode has been derived in
Cicoli and Muia 2015a
and takes the form

\left(\frac{m_{0}}{m_{\mathcal{V}}}\right)^{4}\simeq\left(\ln\mathcal{V}\right)^{2}.

(51)

At first sight this factor might look large but it has to be multiplied by
c_{\rm loop}^{2}\sim 1/(16\pi^{2})^{2}
in the prefactor in Eq. (
50
). For the values of the volume which we will consider in this paper,
\mathcal{V}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(10^{7})
(that also keep soft terms above the TeV-scale), one has
c_{\rm loop}^{2}\left(m_{0}/m_{\mathcal{V}}\right)^{4}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(0.01)
, showing that this decay channel can be neglected also in the local limit.

IV.5
Dark radiation predictions

Let us now derive the prediction for the production of axionic dark radiation from the decay of the lightest modulus
\phi_{u}
. The produced dark radiation can be parameterized by the effective number of neutrinos,
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
,
given by
Cicoli et al. 2013b

\displaystyle\Delta N_{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=3\frac{\rho_{\text{hid}}}{\rho_{\text{neutrinos}}}=\frac{43}{7}\frac{\rho_{\text{hid}}}{\rho_{\text{SM}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{43}{7}\frac{f_{\text{hid}}}{1-f_{\text{hid}}}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\text{dec}})}{g_{*}(T_{\rm rh})}\right)^{1/3},

(52)

where
g_{*}(T)
is the number of relativistic degrees of freedom at a given temperature
T
, while
f_{\rm hid}
is the branching fraction into hidden sector particles (bulk axions) defined as

f_{\text{hid}}\equiv\frac{\Gamma(\phi_{u}\to aa)}{\Gamma(\phi_{u}\to aa)+\Gamma(\phi_{u}\to H_{u}H_{d})}\,.

(53)

The decay width into Higgses however depends on the SUSY breaking scale, and so we study the ultralocal and local limits separately.

Ultralocal limit:

In the ultralocal limit, as can be seen from Eq. (
13
), the soft scalar masses scale as
m_{0}\simeq M_{1/2}\sim m_{P}/\mathcal{V}^{2}
. This mass scale is lower than the mass of
\phi_{u}
since
m_{0}/m_{u}\simeq\mathcal{V}^{-1/3}\ll 1
, implying that all MSSM Higgs degrees of freedom are accessible in the decay of
\phi_{u}
. Thus, taking the dominant decay term of Eq. (
IV.4
), we can approximate the decay width into the Higgs sector as

\displaystyle\Gamma(\phi_{u}\rightarrow H_{u}H_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\gamma^{2}Z^{2}}{48\pi}\frac{m_{u}^{3}}{m_{P}^{2}}\equiv c_{\text{vis}}\,\Gamma_{0}

(54)

where we defined

\displaystyle c_{\text{vis}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2Z^{2}\gamma^{2}

(55)

as the approximate coefficient of visible sector decays. Thus the branching fraction into hidden sector degrees of freedom takes the form

f_{\text{hid}}\simeq\left[1+2Z^{2}\,\frac{\left(k_{2}n_{1}-k_{1}n_{2}\right)^{2}}{n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}}\right]^{-1}.

(56)

Local limit:

In the the local limit, as can be seen from Eq. (

m_{0}\simeq M_{1/2}\sqrt{\mathcal{V}}\sim m_{P}/\mathcal{V}^{3/2}

\phi_{u}

VI
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the production of dark matter and dark radiation after reheating in string inflation models with a fibred Calabi-Yau structure and the visible sector on D3-branes.

We have imposed several physical constraints, related to the need to match observed quantities, like the amplitude of the density perturbations generated during inflation and the dark matter density, or coming from the requirement to avoid the overproduction of dark radiation and isocurvature modes. The interplay between these different physical quantities is determined by the consistency of the underlying UV model which fixes also the scale of supersymmetry breaking, that determines the WIMP mass scale, and the decay constant of both closed and open string axions. We have seen that this UV correlation is very powerful in constraining the nature of dark matter.

In fact, choosing benchmark points preferred by cosmological data for each of our string inflation scenarios, we have found distinct predictions for the nature of dark matter which are compatible with current experimental bounds. In the case of Kähler moduli inflation, we have found TeV-scale WIMPs which can easily reproduce the observed DM density in both the local and ultralocal limits. The case of Fibre inflation instead predicts open string axions as the DM candidate within the ultralocal limit, where isocurvature constraints exclude the local limit.

While we found that an excess of dark radiation is generic in fibred LVS models, we have shown that this can be compatible with the latest Planck measurements without any significant tuning. Let us also point out that Planck bounds on
N_{\rm eff}
can be relaxed if local measurements of
H_{0}
are assumed as a prior, and a small but non-zero amount of dark radiation could also be an intriguing effect to weaken the present
H_{0}
tension
Brinckmann et al. 2021
. Let us finally stress that our analysis involved KMI and FI for fibred Calabi-Yaus with the MSSM on D3-branes. We will instead leave the study of the case where the MSSM is on D7-branes for future work
Cicoli et al.
.

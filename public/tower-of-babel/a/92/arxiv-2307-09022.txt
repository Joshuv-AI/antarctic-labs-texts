Harnessing the mathematics of matrix decomposition to solve planted and maximum clique problem Journal: Noname
Salma Omer, M Montaz Ali

Abstract
We consider the problem of identifying a maximum clique in a given graph. We have proposed a mathematical model for this problem. The model resembles the matrix decomposition of the adjacency matrix of a given graph. The objective function of the mathematical model includes a weighted ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm of the sparse matrix of the decomposition, which has an advantage over the known ℓ 1 − \ell_{1}- norm in reducing the error. The use of dynamically changing the weights for the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -norm has been motivated. We have used proximal operators within the iterates of the ADMM (alternating direction method of multipliers) algorithm to solve the optimization problem. Convergence of the proposed ADMM algorithm has been provided. The theoretical guarantee of the maximum clique in the form of the low-rank matrix has also been established using the golfing scheme to construct approximate dual certificates. We have constructed conditions that guarantee the recovery and uniqueness of the solution, as well as a tight bound on the dual matrix that validates optimality conditions. Numerical results for planted cliques are presented showing clear advantages of our model when compared with two recent mathematical models. Results are also presented for randomly generated graphs with minimal errors. These errors are found using a formula we have proposed based on the size of the clique. Moreover, we have applied our algorithm to real-world graphs for which cliques have been recovered successfully. The validity of these clique sizes comes from the decomposition of input graph into a rank-one matrix (corresponds to the clique) and a sparse matrix.

Introduction

Consider an undirected graph
G(V,E)
, where
V
is the set of vertices and
E
is the set of edges,
|V|=N
. A clique is a subset of vertices of
G
, such that every two distinct vertices are adjacent (a complete subgraph). The maximum clique problem (MCP) is the problem of finding the clique with maximum cardinality from an undirected input graph.
The clique number,
\omega(G)
, on a graph
G
is the number of vertices in a maximum clique. The MCP belongs to the class of NP-hard problems
karp1972reducibility
. Application areas for the MCP include data mining
eblen2012maximum
, community detection
pattabiraman2015fast
;
arias2014community
, and bioinformatics
strickland2005optimal
;
malod2010maximum
.

There are several different formulations of the MCP found in the literature such as the continuous quadratic formulation
hungerford2019general
, integer programming formulation
bomze1999maximum
, and quadratic zero-one formulation
pardalos1992branch
. The indefinite quadratic models have also been suggested
pardalos1990global
;
pardalos1987constrained
;
al1990constrained
.

Tomita and Kameda
tomita2007efficient
have presented a branch and bound algorithm for the MCP based on approximate colouring and proper sorting of the vertices.
There are algorithms suggested for the continuous formulation
belachew2017solving
. Algorithms have also been suggested basing on the mathematical model for the maximum independent set problem
howbert2007maximum
.

In this paper, we consider the planted clique problem, where a single
n
-node clique is planted first and the remaining non-clique edges are inserted independently with a probability. This problem has been studied by a number of authors
alon1998finding
;
ames2011convex
;
ames2011nuclear
;
feige2000finding
.
A polynomial-time algorithm that finds, almost surely, the unique planted clique of size
n\geq a\sqrt{N}
, for sufficiently large constant
a
, in the random graph
G(N,\frac{1}{2})
has been presented in
alon1998finding
.
Feige and Krauthgamer
feige2000finding
have proposed an algorithm based on the Lovasz theta function for finding the planted clique of size
n\geq\Omega(\sqrt{N})
in
G(N,\frac{1}{2})
. Their algorithm also works in the semi-random hidden clique model, in which an adversary can remove edges from the random portion of the graph.

Ames
ames2011convex
, and Ames and Vavasis
ames2011nuclear
have taken a rank minimization approach in modelling the planted clique problem. In addition, Ames
ames2015guaranteed
has established the guaranteed recovery for planted cliques and dense subgraph when the size of the planted clique (the cardinality of the dense subgraph) is known a priori.

More recently, Bombina and Ames
bombina2020convex
have studied the problem of identifying the densest subgraph and densest submatrix problems which can be thought of as a generalization of the MCP.

Our contributions in this paper include a mathematical model of the planted and the maximum clique problem and some theoretical results. Our model follows the core concept of the matrix decomposition problem
candes2011robust
;
chandrasekaran2011rank
but uses different technique approaches. Our model is such that the integer entries of the solution matrix occur as a natural process of optimization. This was shown by implementing the ADMM algorithm for both the planted and ‘unplanted’ maximum clique problems. Theoretical results established include convergence of the ADMM algorithm, and guaranteed recovery of the low-rank and sparse matrices. We have established conditions that guarantee the recovery and the uniqueness of the solution. Moreover, we have derived a tight bound of the dual matrix that certifies the optimality conditions of our proposed model. Our sufficient conditions are closely related to those given in the references
ames2015guaranteed
;
ames2011nuclear
;
bombina2020convex
as they also consider the low rank matrix and its dual. However, our sufficient conditions are stronger in that we do not require a number of additional assumptions such as the number of vertices adjacent to clique vertices (Theorems 2.1 & 2.2
ames2015guaranteed
, also assumptions 1, 2, and 3 in
ames2011nuclear
).Our theoretical and computational results are based on the planted clique problem. However, our algorithm equally works for the general and real-world MCP for which we have provided the numerical evidence.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the proposed mathematical model. Section
3
presents a number of proximal operators which are used in the iterates of the ADMM algorithm. Then we present the convergence of the proposed algorithm in Section
4
. Section
5
presents the theoretical recovery and the uniqueness of the solution. In Section
6
, we evaluate the performance of the proposed algorithm for recovering the planted clique, and also recovering the maximum clique from randomly generated and real-world graphs. Finally, we make concluding remarks in Section
7
.

2
The mathematical model

We have taken the matrix decomposition approach for the planted clique problem. The matrix decomposition problem separates a given matrix
M
into its low-rank and sparse component by solving the problem

\displaystyle\min_{L,S\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}}~\rank(L)+\lambda\|S\|_{0}

\displaystyle\qquad\textit{s.t.}~~L+S=M,

where
\|S\|_{0}=\card(S)
is the number of non-zero entries in
S
. Both the rank function and
\ell_{0}
-norm minimization are non-convex. The nuclear norm
\|L\|_{*}
is the sum of singular values
\sigma_{i}(L)
; it is used as the convex relaxation of rank function, and the
\ell_{1}
-norm,
\|S\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|S_{ij}|
, is used as the convex relaxation of
\|S\|_{0}
.

In the context of the planted clique problem of size
n
, if we include self-loops and assign 1 to the diagonal elements of the adjacency matrix
M
, then
M
can be split into a rank-one matrix
L
(corresponding to the maximum clique) and a sparse matrix
S
.
Thus, the formulation for MCP is

\displaystyle\min_{L,S\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}}~\|L\|_{*}+\lambda\|S\|_{1}

(1)

\displaystyle\qquad\textit{s.t.}~~M-L-S=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\qquad S_{ij}\in[0,1],

(3)

where
M\in\mathbb{R}^{{N\times N}}
is the adjacency matrix of graph
G
;
L
and
S
are the optimization variables.
\lambda>0
is the regularization parameter. We refer to problem (
1
)-(
3
) as the ‘regular’ matrix decomposition formulation for the planted clique problem.

The main difficulty of the above model is that the entries of the optimal
L
and
S
may not be integers. Indeed, this was observed for the nuclear norm minimization model by Ames
ames2011convex
who rounded each entry of the optimal
L
to the nearest integers. The rounding of entries causes noisy recovery as indicated by a number of figures presented in
ames2011convex
. Below, we propose our mathematical model which can overcome the above difficulty. The main concept of our approach is to use the weighted
\|S\|_{1}
norm in the objective function (
1
). We have demonstrated a posteriori that the weighted
\|S\|_{1}
norm is central to achieving integer value of the entries of
L
and
S
. We have taken a systematic approach to generate the weights.
We approximate each term of
\|S\|_{0}
,

\|S\|_{0}=\#\begin{cases}1&\textit{if}~S_{ij}\neq 0,\\
0&~\textit{otherwise},\end{cases}

with the function

\displaystyle\psi(S_{ij})=\frac{S_{ij}}{S_{ij}+\epsilon},S_{ij}\neq-\epsilon,~\epsilon>0.

(4)

The function
\psi
is concave in
\left[0,\infty\right)
with
\psi^{\prime}(S_{ij})>0
for all
S_{ij}\geq 0
,
\psi^{\prime}(0)<\infty
.

Before presenting the mathematical model we make a graphical comparison of
\psi(x)
in (
4
) with other, approximations for
\|x\|_{0}
in the case of single variable. The comparison in Figure
1
shows that

,
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, gives a better approximation of

than

\log(|x|+\epsilon)

We write our relaxed objective function as

\displaystyle\Phi(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi(S_{ij}).

(5)

We now construct a convex surrogate of
\Phi(S)
at a known feasible

(J-1)
-th iteration of an algorithm. It follows from the concavity of

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi(S_{ij})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\psi((S_{J-1})_{ij})+\psi^{\prime}((S_{J-1})_{ij})(S_{ij}-(S_{J-1})_{ij})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\widetilde{\Phi}(S),

where
\psi^{\prime}((S_{J-1})_{ij})=\frac{\epsilon}{((S_{J-1})_{ij}+\epsilon)^{2}}
.

\displaystyle\Phi(S)\leq\widetilde{\Phi}(S)~~\textit{and}~~\Phi(S_{J-1})=\widetilde{\Phi}(S_{J-1}).

(6)

We now ignore the constant terms in

and treat the remaining expression as the surrogate for

. The concept of the surrogate function has been reported in

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi^{\prime}((S_{J-1})_{ij})S_{ij}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}|C_{ij}S_{ij}|=\|C\circ S\|_{1},

where

is a constant matrix with entries
\frac{\epsilon}{((S_{J-1})_{ij}+\epsilon)^{2}}
. It is clear that the entries of

are strictly positive.
The symbol

is known as Hadamard product. Function

3
The alternating direction method of multipliers

In this section, we present the ADMM algorithm which we have used to solve (
8
)-(
9
). We begin with the singular value thresholding and proximal operator which have been used in the iterates of ADMM.

Let
X
be a matrix of size
N_{1}\times N_{2}
and of rank
r
. Assume that the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
X
is defined by
X=U\Sigma V^{T}
, where
U\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}\times r}
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}\times r}
and
\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
.

For
\tau>0
, we define the SVT (singular value thresholding) operator
cai2010singular
as

\displaystyle SVT_{\tau}(X)=U{\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(\Sigma)}V^{T},

(10)

\mathcal{D}_{\tau}(\Sigma)=\Diag(\max\{(\sigma_{i}-\tau),0\})
, taking note of the fact that
\sigma_{i}=|\lambda_{i}|
(eigenvalues) for real symmetric
X
.

Let
ST_{\tau}
be the soft thresholding or shrinkage operator with parameter
\tau
. Then for
\tau>0
,
ST_{\tau}
can be defined on each element of
X
by:

\displaystyle ST_{\tau}(X)=\sgn(X)\circ\max(|X|-\tau E,0),

(11)

where
\sgn
is the sign function;
E
is a matrix of all ones.

The unified shrinkage operator
parikh2014proximal
can be defined by

\displaystyle\Prox_{\tau f}(X)=\textit{$\argmin$}_{Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}\times N_{2}}}~\tau f(Y)+\frac{1}{2}\|Y-X\|_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}SVT_{\tau}(X)&if~f(Y)=\|Y\|_{*},\\
ST_{\tau}(X)&if~f(Y)=\|Y\|_{1},\end{cases}

(12)

where
\|.\|_{F}
denotes the Frobenius norm.

It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\argmin_{Y\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}\times N_{2}}}

\displaystyle~~\tau\|C\circ Y\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|Y-X\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=\sgn(X)\circ\max(|X|-\tau C,0)=STT_{\tau}(C,X),

(13)

STT_{\tau}(C,X)=ST_{\tau}(X)
when
C=E
.

We now summarize the iterates of the ADMM algorithm for the problem (

)-(

The augmented Lagrangian of problem (
8
)-(
9

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(L,S,\mu)=\|L\|_{*}+\lambda\|C\circ S\|_{1}+\langle\mu,M-L-S\rangle+\frac{\rho}{2}\|M-L-S\|^{2}_{F}.

(14)

The scaled form of the augmented Lagrangian is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(L,S,\mu)=\|L\|_{*}+\lambda\|C\circ S\|_{1}+\frac{\rho}{2}\|M-L-S+\frac{1}{\rho}\mu\|^{2}_{F}-\frac{1}{2\rho}\|\mu\|_{F}^{2},

(15)

where

is a constant matrix;

is the Lagrange multiplier. The implementation of constants (

The ADMM iterates are as follows:

\displaystyle L_{J}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{L}~\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(L,S_{J-1},\mu_{J-1})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{L}~~\rho\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\|L\|_{*}+\frac{1}{2}\|M-L-S_{J-1}+\frac{1}{\rho}\mu_{J-1}\|^{2}_{F}\right).

\displaystyle=\argmin_{S}~\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(L_{J},S,\mu_{J-1})

\displaystyle=\argmin_{S}~~\rho\left(\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\|C\circ S\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|M-L_{J}-S-\frac{1}{\rho}\mu_{J-1}\|^{2}_{F}\right).

The Lagrangian multipliers

\displaystyle\mu_{J}=\mu_{J-1}+\rho(M-L_{J}-S_{J}).

Since

\displaystyle L_{J}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{L}~~\frac{1}{\rho}\|L\|_{*}+\frac{1}{2}\|M-L-S_{J-1}+\frac{1}{\rho}\mu_{J-1}\|^{2}_{F}

Similarly, the proximal operator (

\displaystyle S_{J}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{S}~~\frac{\lambda}{\rho}\|C\circ S\|_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\|M-L_{J}-S-\frac{1}{\rho}\mu_{J-1}\|^{2}_{F}

\displaystyle=STT_{\frac{\lambda}{\rho}}(C,M-L_{J}+\frac{1}{\rho}\mu_{{J-1}})

4
Convergence analysis

We now present the convergence of the proposed algorithm to the optimal solution. For
Algorithm

1

Theorem 4.1

Any accumulation point
(L_{*},S_{*})
of the sequence
\left\{(L_{J},S_{J})\right\}

Algorithm

1
is an optimal solution of (
8
)-(
9

Proof

Algorithm

1

(L_{J},S_{J})
by alternate minimization with respect to one variable while keeping the other one fixed.
The problem being convex, for sufficiently large

\mu_{J-1}
close to
\mu_{*}

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}(\sgn(C\circ S_{*}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{H}(\sgn(C\circ S_{*}))\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{S}^{N-1}}\langle\mathcal{H}(yx^{T}),\sgn(C\circ S_{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\leq(1-2\epsilon)^{-1}\sup_{x,y\in N}\langle\mathcal{H}(yx^{T}),\sgn(C\circ S_{*})\rangle

\displaystyle=4\sup_{x,y\in N}\langle\mathcal{H}(yx^{T}),\sgn(C\circ S_{*})\rangle,\textit{ using }\epsilon=\frac{3}{8}.

\displaystyle\leq 2N\exp\left(\frac{-a^{2}/2}{\frac{1}{N^{2}}\|Z(x,y)\|_{F}^{2}+a/3}\right),

\displaystyle=2N\exp\left(\frac{-(a/\lambda)^{2}/32}{\frac{1}{N^{2}}\|\mathcal{H}\|^{2}+a/12\lambda}\right).

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{H}\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}\right)^{k}\big\|

7
Conclusions

We have suggested a mathematical model for the clique problem that differs from the known matrix decomposition model in that it produces naturally integer solution required. This has been possible due to the dynamic nature of the weighted
\ell_{1}
-norm. We have established conditions that guarantee the recovery and the uniqueness of the solution, and we have derived a tight bound of the dual matrix that certifies the optimality conditions of our proposed model. Our approach produces much superior solution quality when compared to other known approaches. This has been possible due to the dynamic nature of our mathematical model. Our algorithm requires no input from the user other than the adjacency matrix of the input graph. In addition, the algorithm can be implemented at easy without needing any external solvers. Although the algorithm has been proposed for the planted clique problem, it has been tested on the maximum clique problem using random graphs with almost error-free results. We have also suggested a new expression for error calculations. Moreover, we have applied our algorithm to some real-world graphs and DIMACS data sets, and cliques are recovered successfully using our matrix decomposition model.

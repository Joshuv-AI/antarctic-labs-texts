Well-Posedness of Quasilinear Parabolic Equations in Time-Weighted Spaces
Bogdan–Vasile Matioc, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. Well-posedness in time-weighted spaces of certain quasilinear (and semilinear) parabolic evolution equations u ′ = A ⁡ ( u ) ​ u + f ⁡ ( u ) u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u) is established. The focus lies on the case of strict inclusions dom ⁡ ( f ) ⊊ dom ⁡ ( A ) \mathrm{dom}(f)\subsetneq\mathrm{dom}(A) of the domains of the nonlinearities u ↦ f ⁡ ( u ) u\mapsto f(u) and u ↦ A ⁡ ( u ) u\mapsto A(u) . Based on regularizing effects of parabolic equations it is shown that a semiflow is generated in intermediate spaces. In applications this allows one to derive global existence from weaker a priori estimates. The result is illustrated by examples of chemotaxis systems.

1.
Introduction

Let
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
be two Banach spaces over
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}\}
with continuous and dense embedding

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\hookrightarrow}}E_{0}\,.

For each
\theta\in(0,1)
, let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
be an arbitrary admissible interpolation functor of
exponent
\theta
and denote by
{E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}}
the corresponding Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
. Then

E_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\hookrightarrow}}E_{\theta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\hookrightarrow}}E_{\vartheta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\hookrightarrow}}E_{0}\,,\qquad 0\leq\vartheta\leq\theta\leq 1\,.

In this paper we shall focus our attention on quasilinear parabolic problems

(1.1)

u^{\prime}=A(u)u+f(u)\,,\quad t>0\,,\qquad u(0)=u^{0}\,.

Here, we assume for the quasilinear part

(1.2a)

A\in C^{1-}\big(O_{\beta},\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\,,

where

(1.2b)

\beta\in[0,1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\emptyset\not=O_{\beta}\,\text{ is an open subset of }E_{\beta}\,,

and where
\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
is the open subset of the bounded linear operators
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})

consisting of generators of strongly continuous analytic semigroups on
E_{0}
.
Moreover, for the semilinear part we assume that there are numbers

(1.2c)

0<\gamma<1\,,\qquad\beta\leq\xi<1\,,\qquad q\geq 1\,,

such that
f:O_{\xi}\to E_{\gamma}
is locally Lipschitz continuous on the open subset
O_{\xi}:=O_{\beta}\cap E_{\xi}
of
E_{\xi}
in the sense that for each
R>0
there is
c(R)>0
such that,
for all
w,\,v\in O_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(0,R)
,

(1.2d)

\|f(w)-f(v)\|_{\gamma}\leq c(R)\big[1+\|w\|_{\xi}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q-1}\big]\big[\big(1+\|w\|_{\xi}+\|v\|_{\xi}\big)\|w-v\|_{\beta}+\|w-v\|_{\xi}\big]\,.\\

As for the initial value we fix

(1.2e)

\alpha\in(\beta,1)\quad\text{ with }\quad(\xi-\alpha)q<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\alpha\}\,.

Under assumptions (
1.2
) we shall prove that problem (
1.1
) is well-posed for any
u^{0}\in O_{\alpha}:=O_{\beta}\cap E_{\alpha}

and in fact generates a semiflow on
O_{\alpha}
. Note that (
1.2d
) includes in particular the case when

(1.3)

\|f(w)-f(v)\|_{\gamma}\leq c(R)\big(1+\|w\|_{\xi}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q-1}\big)\|w-v\|_{\xi}\,,\qquad w,v\in O_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(0,R)\,,

and that (
1.2e
) is satisfied if
\alpha\in(\xi,1)
.
The local Lipshitz continuity property (
1.2d
) and its stronger version (
1.3
) appear quite naturally in applications,
see Lemma
4.1
and the examples in Section
5
and Section
6
.

(and established in

]
, see also
[

) for the case that the nonlinearities

for

is a semiflow on

, and hence

In fact, as pointed out above, the interesting and new case occurs when
{\beta<\alpha<\xi}

Note that then

and hence, the semilinear part

, being defined on

Our first main result is Theorem

and establishes the well-posedness of the quasilinear evolution problem (
1.1
) restricted to the assumptions (

).

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose (
1.2
).

(i)

(Existence)
Given any
u^{0}\in O_{\alpha}
, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) possesses a maximal strong solution

\displaystyle u(\cdot;u^{0})

\displaystyle\in C^{1}\big((0,t^{+}(u^{0})),E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,t^{+}(u^{0})),E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,t^{+}(u^{0})),O_{\alpha}\big)

with
t^{+}(u^{0})\in(0,\infty]
. Moreover,

u(\cdot;u^{0})\in C^{\min\{\alpha-\theta,\,(1-\mu q)_{+}\}}\big([0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\cap C_{\mu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

for all
T<t^{+}(u^{0})
, where
\theta\in[0,\alpha]
and
\mu>(\xi-\alpha)_{+}
.

(ii)

(Uniqueness) If

\tilde{u}\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C^{\vartheta}\big([0,T],O_{\beta}\big)\cap C_{\nu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

is a solution to (
1.1
) for some
T>0
,
\vartheta\in(0,1)
, and
\nu\geq 0
with
q\nu<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\alpha\}
, then
T<t^{+}(u^{0})
and
\tilde{u}=u(\cdot;u^{0})
on
[0,T].

(iii)

(Continuous dependence) The map
(t,u^{0})\mapsto u(t;u^{0})
is a semiflow on
O_{\alpha}
.

(iv)

(Global existence) If the orbit
u([0,t^{+}(u^{0}));u^{0})
is relatively compact in
O_{\alpha}
, then
{t^{+}(u^{0})=\infty}
.

(v)

(Blow-up criterion) Let
u^{0}\in O_{\alpha}
be such that
t^{+}(u^{0})<\infty
.

(
a
)

If

u(\cdot;u^{0}):[0,t^{+}(u^{0}))\to E_{\alpha}
is uniformly continuous, then

(1.4)

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{+}(u^{0})}\mathrm{dist}_{E_{\alpha}}\big(u(t;u^{0}),\partial O_{\alpha}\big)=0\,.

(
b
)

If
E_{1}
is compactly embedded in
E_{0}
, then

(1.5)

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{+}(u^{0})}\|u(t;u^{0})\|_{\theta}=\infty\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\lim_{t\nearrow t^{+}(u^{0})}\mathrm{dist}_{E_{\beta}}\big(u([0,t];u^{0}),\partial O_{\beta}\big)=0

for each
\theta\in(\beta,1)
with
(\xi-\theta)q<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\theta\}
.

Criterion (iv) yields global existence when the orbit is relatively compact in
E_{\alpha}
.
In particular, if
E_{1}
embeds compactly in
E_{0}
,
then a priori estimates on the solution in
E_{\alpha}
are sufficient for global existence as noted in condition (
1.5
) (in contrast e.g. to
[
7
]

where estimates in
E_{\xi}
would be needed for the same conclusion).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a classical fixed point argument.
However, the technical details do not seem to be completely straightforward due to the singularity of
t\mapsto f(u(t))
at
t=0
which has to be monitored carefully.

Semilinear Parabolic Problems

Of course, the result for the quasilinear case remains true for semilinear parabolic equations

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u)\,,\quad t>0\,,\qquad u(0)=u^{0}\,,

or, more generally, for parabolic evolution equations

(1.6)

u^{\prime}=A(t)u+f(u)\,,\quad t>0\,,\qquad u(0)=u^{0}\,,

with time-dependent operators
A=A(t)
.
In this setting Theorem
1.1
can be sharpened though.
We present with Theorem
1.2
below a result for the particular case that
f
is defined on the whole interpolation space
E_{\xi}
. More precisely, let

(1.7a)

A\in C^{\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0}))

for some
\rho>0
and let

(1.7b)

0\leq\alpha\leq\xi\leq 1\,,\quad 0\leq\gamma<1\,,\quad(\gamma,\xi)\not=(0,1)\,,\quad q\geq 1\,,\quad(\xi-\alpha)q<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\alpha\}\,.

Assume that
f:E_{\xi}\to E_{\gamma}
is locally Lipschitz continuous in the sense that for each
R>0
there is a constant
{c(R)>0}
such that

(1.7c)

\|f(w)-f(v)\|_{\gamma}\leq c(R)\big[1+\|w\|_{\xi}^{q-1}+\|v\|_{\xi}^{q-1}\big]\big[\big(1+\|w\|_{\xi}+\|v\|_{\xi}\big)\|w-v\|_{{\alpha}}+\|w-v\|_{\xi}\big]

for all
w,\,v\in E_{\xi}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\alpha}}(0,R)
.

It is worth pointing out that
we may choose the phase space of the evolution as well as the target space of the semilinearity
f
as
E_{0}
(that is, we may set
\alpha=\gamma=0
) and that the nonlinearity
f(u)

need not be defined on the phase space
E_{\alpha}
; see also Remark
2.2
below for more details.
The well-posedness result regarding the semilinear problem (
1.6
) under assumption (
1.7
) reads as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose (
1.7
).

(i)

(Existence) Given any
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
, the Cauchy problem (
1.6
) possesses a maximal strong solution

\displaystyle u(\cdot;u^{0})

\displaystyle\in C^{1}\big((0,t^{+}(u^{0})),E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,t^{+}(u^{0})),E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,t^{+}(u^{0})),E_{\alpha}\big)

with
t^{+}(u^{0})\in(0,\infty]
. Moreover,

u(\cdot;u^{0})\in C^{\min\{\alpha-\theta,\,(1-\mu q)_{+}\}}\big([0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\cap C_{\mu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

for all
\theta\in[0,\alpha]
,
\mu>\xi-\alpha
, and
T<t^{+}(u^{0})
.

(ii)

(Blow-up criterion) If
u^{0}\in E_{\alpha}
is such that
t^{+}(u^{0})<\infty
, then

(1.8)

\underset{t\nearrow t^{+}(u^{0})}{\limsup}\|u(t;u^{0})\|_{\alpha}=\infty\,.

(iii)

(Uniqueness) If

\tilde{u}\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{\alpha}\big)\cap C_{\nu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

is a solution to (
1.6
) for some
T>0
and
\nu\geq 0
with
q\nu<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\alpha\}
, then
T<t^{+}(u^{0})
and
\tilde{u}=u(\cdot;u^{0})
on
[0,T].

Moreover, if
A(t)=A\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
for all
t\geq 0
, then:

(iv)

(Continuous dependence) The map
(t,u^{0})\mapsto u(t;u^{0})
is a semiflow on
E_{\alpha}
.

(v)

(Global existence) If the orbit
u([0,t^{+}(u^{0}));u^{0})
is relatively compact in
E_{\alpha}
, then
{t^{+}(u^{0})=\infty}
.

Note that an priori bound in
E_{\alpha}
already ensures that the solution is globally defined even
in the case of a non-compact embedding
{E_{1}\hookrightarrow E_{0}}
.

is presented in Section

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on Proposition
2.1
below. Before we address the latter result, let us first recall some basic facts used in the proofs.

Preliminaries

Let
T>0
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, and consider a Banach space
E
. We denote by
C_{\mu}((0,T],E)
the Banach space of all functions

u\in C((0,T],E)
such that

t^{\mu}u(t)\rightarrow 0
in
E
as
t\rightarrow 0
, equipped with the norm

u\mapsto\|u\|_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E)}:=\sup\left\{t^{\mu}\,\|u(t)\|_{E}\,:\,t\in(0,T]\right\}\,.

Note that

(2.1)

C_{\mu}((0,T],E)\hookrightarrow C_{\nu}((0,T],E)\,,\quad\mu\leq\nu\,.

Given
\omega>0
and
\kappa\geq 1
, we denote by
{\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0};\kappa,\omega)}
the class of all operators
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})
such
that
\omega-\mathcal{A}
is an
isomorphism from
E_{1}
onto
E_{0}
and

\frac{1}{\kappa}\,\leq\,\frac{\|(\mu-\mathcal{A})z\|_{0}}{|\mu|\,\|z\|_{0}+\|z\|_{1}}\,\leq\,\kappa\ ,\qquad{\rm Re\,}\mu\geq\omega\ ,\quad z\in E_{1}\setminus\{0\}\,.

Then

\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})=\bigcup_{\omega>0\,,\,\kappa\geq 1}\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0};\kappa,\omega)\,.

For time-dependent operators
\mathcal{A}\in C^{\rho}(I,\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0}))
with
\rho\in(0,1)

there exists a unique parabolic evolution operator
U_{\mathcal{A}}(t,s)
,
0\leq s\leq t<\sup I
, in the sense of
[
8
, II. Section 2]
.

Based on this, we may reformulate the quasilinear Cauchy problem (
1.1
) as a fixed point equation of the form

(2.2)

u(t)=U_{A(u)}(t,0)u^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}U_{A(u)}(t,\tau)f(u(\tau))\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,,\quad t>0\,,

see the proof below of Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose (
1.2
).
Let
S_{\alpha}\subset O_{\alpha}
be a compact subset of
E_{\alpha}
.
Then, there exist a neighborhood
Q_{\alpha}
of
S_{\alpha}
in
O_{\alpha}
and
{T:=T(S_{\alpha})>0}
such that, for each
u^{0}\in Q_{\alpha}
,
the problem (
1.1
) has a strong solution

(2.3)

\displaystyle u=u(\cdot;u^{0})\in

\displaystyle C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],O_{\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\cap C^{\min\{\alpha-\theta,\,(1-\mu q)_{+}\}}\big([0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\cap C_{\mu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

for any
\theta\in[0,\alpha]
and
\mu>(\xi-\alpha)_{+}
.
Moreover, there is a constant
c_{0}:=c_{0}(S_{\alpha})>0
such that

\|u(t;u^{0})-u(t;u^{1})\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{0}\|u^{0}-u^{1}\|_{\alpha}\,,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T\,,\quad u^{0},u^{1}\in Q_{\alpha}\,.

Finally, if

(2.4)

\tilde{u}\in C^{1}((0,T],E_{0})\cap C((0,T],E_{1})\cap C^{\vartheta}([0,T],O_{\beta})\cap C_{\nu}\big((0,T],E_{\xi}\big)

with
\vartheta\in(0,1)

and
0\leq q\nu<\min\{1,1+\gamma-\alpha\}

is a solution to (
1.1
) satisfying
\tilde{u}(0)=u^{0}\in Q_{\alpha}
, then
\tilde{u}=u(\cdot;u^{0})
.

Proof.

We devise the proof into several steps.

The fixed point formulation.

Since
S_{\alpha}\subset O_{\alpha}
is compact in
E_{\alpha}
and since
E_{\alpha}
embeds continuously into
E_{\beta}
, we find a constant
\delta>0

such that
\mathrm{dist}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},\partial O_{\beta})>2\delta>0
.
Moreover, due to (
1.2a
) and
[
2
, II. Proposition 6.4]
,
A
is uniformly Lipschitz continuous on some neighborhood of
S_{\alpha}
, hence there are
\varepsilon>0
and
L>0
such that

\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},2\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{B}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},\delta)\subset O_{\beta}

and

(2.5)

\|A(x)-A(y)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})}\leq L\|x-y\|_{\beta}\ ,\quad x,y\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},2\varepsilon)\ .

The compactness of
A(S_{\alpha})
in
\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})
implies according to

[
8
, I. Corollary 1.3.2]
that there are
{\kappa\geq 1}
and
\omega>0
such that (making
\varepsilon>0
smaller, if necessary)

(2.6)

A(x)\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0};\kappa,\omega)\,,\quad x\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},2\varepsilon)\,.

Recalling (
1.2e
) and (
2.1
), we may choose
\alpha_{0}\in(\beta,\alpha)
if
\alpha<\xi
respectively put
\alpha_{0}:=\xi
if
\alpha\geq\xi
, choose
\gamma_{0}\in(0,\gamma)
, and assume that

\mu
satisfies

(2.7)

\xi-\alpha_{0}<\mu<\min\left\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1+\gamma_{0}-\alpha}{q}\right\}\,.

Fix
\rho\in(0,\min\{\alpha-\beta,1-\mu q\})
.
Given
T\in(0,1)
(chosen small enough as specified later on), define a closed subset of
C([0,T],E_{\beta})
by

\mathcal{V}_{T}:=\left\{v\in C([0,T],E_{\beta})\,:\,v(t)\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}_{E_{\beta}}(S_{\alpha},2\varepsilon)\,,\,\|v(t)-v(s)\|_{\beta}\leq|t-s|^{\rho}\ ,~0\leq s,t\leq T\right\}\,.

Hence, if
v\in\mathcal{V}_{T}
, then (
2.6
) ensures

(2.8a)

A(v(t))\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0};\kappa,\omega)\,,\quad t\in[0,T]\,,

while (
2.5
) implies

(2.8b)

A(v)\in C^{\rho}\big([0,T],\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})\big)\quad\text{with}\quad\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|A(v(t))-A(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{1},E_{0})}}{(t-s)^{\rho}}\leq L\,.

For each
v\in\mathcal{V}_{T}
, the evolution operator

U_{A(v)}(t,s)\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,,

is thus well-defined and (
2.8
) guarantees that we are
in a position to use the results of
[
8
, II.Section 5]
.
In particular, due to
[
8
, II.Lemma 5.1.3]
there exists
c=c(S_{\alpha})>0
such that

(2.9)

\|U_{A(v)}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}+(t-s)^{\theta-\vartheta_{0}}\,\|U_{A(v)}(t,s)\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta},E_{\theta})}\leq c\,,\quad 0\leq s\leq t\leq T\,,

for
0\leq\vartheta_{0}\leq\vartheta\leq\theta\leq 1
with
\vartheta_{0}<\vartheta
if
0<\vartheta<\theta<1
.
In the following, we write
{c=c(S_{\alpha})}
for positive constants depending only on
S_{\alpha}
and
{\alpha,\,\beta,\,\gamma,\,\xi,\,\mu,\alpha_{0},\,\gamma_{0},\,\varepsilon,\,\delta}
,
but neither on
v\in\mathcal{V}_{T}
nor on
{T\in(0,1)}
.

We introduce the complete metric space

\mathcal{W}_{T}:=\mathcal{V}_{T}\cap\bar{\mathbb{B}}_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\xi})}(0,1)

equipped with the metric

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

and let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Consider

with

(4.1)

|\nabla g(r)-\nabla g(s)|\leq c\big(1+|r|^{q-1}+|s|^{q-1}\big)|r-s|\,,\quad r,s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

for some constants
q\geq 1
and

Let

and
\mu\in(0,1)
. Then

for every
{w\in W_{p}^{\mu}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}

Moreover,
there is
K>0
with

\begin{split}\|g(w_{1})-g(w_{2})\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}&\leq K\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q}\big)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}\\
&\quad+K\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\big)\big(\|w_{1}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}+\|w_{2}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}\big)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\infty}\end{split}

for all
w_{1},\,w_{2}\in W_{p}^{\mu}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

Note that (
4.1
) implies

(4.2)

|\nabla g(r)|\leq c_{2}\big(1+|r|^{q}\big)\,,\quad r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Let
w_{1},\,w_{2}\in W_{p}^{\mu}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It then follows from the fundamental theorem of calculus, (

), and (

\begin{split}&\big|\big(g(w_{1}(x))-g(w_{2}(x))\big)-\big(g(w_{1}(y))-g(w_{2}(y))\big)\big|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{1}\nabla g\big(w_{1}(x)+\tau[w_{1}(x)-w_{2}(x)]\big)\,\mathrm{d}\tau\,\cdot\big(w_{1}(x)-w_{2}(x)-w_{1}(y)+w_{2}(y)\big)\right|\\
&\ +\left|\int_{0}^{1}\left\{\nabla g\big(w_{1}(x)+\tau[w_{1}(x)-w_{2}(x)]\big)-\nabla g\big(w_{1}(y)+\tau[w_{1}(y)-w_{2}(y)]\big)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}\tau\cdot\left(w_{1}(y)-w_{2}(y)\right)\right|\\
&\leq c_{3}\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q}\big)\,\big|w_{1}(x)-w_{2}(x)-w_{1}(y)+w_{2}(y)\big|\\
&\quad+c_{3}\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\big)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\infty}\,\big(|w_{1}(x)-w_{1}(y)|+\big|w_{2}(x)-w_{2}(y)\big|\big)\,.\end{split}

The latter estimate together with (
4.2

\begin{split}\|g(&w_{1})-g(w_{2})\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}^{p}\\
&\leq\,\|g(w_{1})-g(w_{2})\|_{L_{p}}^{p}+\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\dfrac{\big|\big(g(w_{1}(x))-g(w_{2}(x))\big)-\big(g(w_{1}(y))-g(w_{2}(y))\big)\big|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+\mu p}}\,\mathrm{d}(x,y)\\
&\leq c_{4}\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q}\big)^{p}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}^{p}\\
&\quad+c_{4}\big(1+\|w_{1}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}+\|w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{q-1}\big)^{p}\,\big(\|w_{1}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}+\|w_{2}\|_{W_{p}^{\mu}}\big)^{p}\,\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\infty}^{p}\,,\end{split}

as claimed.

Introduction and main results

2.1.
Graded Lie groups

A graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
is a connected and simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is
graded
, that is,
\mathfrak{g}
admits a vector space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i},
for which all but finitely many
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
’s are
\{0\}
and satisfy the inclusions
[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}.
Such a decomposition of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
gradation
of
\mathfrak{g}.
A graded Lie algebra is
stratifiable
if there exists a gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{i}]=\mathfrak{g}_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
A Lie group
\mathbb{G}
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is stratifiable is called a stratified Lie group. This immediately shows that every stratified Lie group is graded. The Heisenberg group, more generally,
H
-type groups, Engel groups and Cartan groups are examples of stratified Lie groups.

We define a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the vector space automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
of the form
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}:=\exp(\ln(r)A)
for some diagonalisable matrix
A\sim\text{diag}[\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n}]
with positive eigenvalues
0<\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\nu_{n}
on
\mathfrak{g}
such that

D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}[X,Y]=[D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}X,D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}Y],

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
and
r>0.
The positive eigenvalues
0<\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\nu_{n}
of
A
are called the dilations’ weights of
\mathfrak{g}.
A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
homogeneous
if there exists a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on
\mathfrak{g}.
It is well known that the existence of a family of dilations on
\mathfrak{g}
implies that
\mathfrak{g}
is a nilpotent Lie algebra. The Lie group
\mathbb{G}:=\exp{\mathfrak{g}},
which is connected and simply connected, is called
homogeneous
if
\mathfrak{g}
is a homogeneous Lie algebra. The family of dilations
\{D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}:r>0\}
on Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
induces a family of dilations
\{D_{r}:r>0\}
on the group
\mathbb{G}
by
D_{r}:=\exp\circ D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\exp^{-1},\,\,r>0.

It is easy to note that for a given graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, the sequence of subspaces
\mathfrak{I}_{k}:=\bigoplus_{i=k}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
forms a finite nested sequence of ideals in
\mathfrak{g}.
Thus, using these ideals
\mathfrak{I}_{k},
any basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
given as the union of the bases
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n_{i}}\}
is necessarily a strong Malcev basis of
\mathfrak{g}.
Such a basis of a graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to a family of dilations
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}},\,r>0,
on
\mathfrak{g}
using the matrix given by
AX_{j}=iX_{j}
for every
X_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}_{i},
that is,
D_{r}^{\mathfrak{g}}X_{j}=r^{i}X_{j}.

We may identify
\mathbb{G}
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=\text{dim}~\mathfrak{g}
via the exponential map
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow G
given by
x=\exp(x_{1}X_{1}+x_{2}X_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}X_{n})\in\mathbb{G}
having a basis of
\mathfrak{g}.
Using this identification, we can naturally identify a function on
\mathbb{G}
as a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
These exponential coordinates allow us to represent the action of
D_{r}
on
\mathbb{G}
explicitly:

D_{r}(x)=rx:=\exp(r^{\nu_{1}}X_{1}+r^{\nu_{2}}X_{2}+\cdots+r^{\nu_{n}}X_{n})=(r^{\nu_{1}}x_{1},r^{\nu_{2}}x_{2},\ldots,r^{\nu_{n}}x_{n}),

for
x:=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{G},\,\,r>0.
This notion of dilation on
\mathbb{G}
is crucial to define the notion of homogeneity for functions, measures, and operators. For examples, the bi-invariant Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{G},
which is just a Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n},
is
Q
-homogeneous in the sense that

d(D_{r}(x))=r^{Q}dx,

where
Q:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}i\dim\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\ldots+\nu_{n}
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}.
It is customary to jointly rescale weights so that
\nu_{1}=1.
This also shows that
Q\geq n.

Identifying the elements of
\mathfrak{g}
with the left-invariant vector fields, each

is a homogeneous differential operator of degree
\nu_{j}.
For every multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
, we set
X^{\alpha}=X_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}X_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\ldots X_{r}^{\alpha_{n}}
in the universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}.
Then
X^{\alpha}
is of homogeneous degree
[\alpha]:=\alpha_{1}\nu_{1}+\alpha_{2}\nu_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}\nu_{n}.

2.2.
Homogeneous quasi-norms on homogeneous groups and polar decomposition

A
homogeneous quasi-norm
on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is a continuous function
|\cdot|:\mathbb{G}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that it satisfies the following properties:

•

|x|=0
if and only if
x=e_{\mathbb{G}}.

•

|x^{-1}|=|x|

•

|D_{r}x|=r|x|
for
r>0.

There always exists a homogeneous quasi-norm in any homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
(
[
9
]
). One can show the existence of a homogeneous quasi-norm on
\mathbb{G}
, which is
C^{\infty}
-smooth on
\mathbb{G}\backslash\{e_{\mathbb{G}}\}.
Every homogeneous quasi-norm satisfies the following triangle inequality with the constant
C\geq 1:

|xy|\leq C(|x|+|y|)\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{G}.

In fact, it is always possible to choose a homogeneous quasi-norm on any homogeneous group that satisfies the triangle inequality with constant
C=1.
Any two homogeneous quasi-norms on
\mathbb{G}
are equivalent.

Similar to the Euclidean space, there is a notion of polar decomposition on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
for the homogenous quasi-norm
|\cdot|.
Let

\mathfrak{S}:=\{x\in G:\,\,\,|x|=1\}

be the unite sphere with respect to the homogenous quasi-norm
|\cdot|.
Then there exists a unique Radon measure
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{S}
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{G}),
we have

\int_{G}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{S}}f(ry)r^{Q-1}d\sigma(y)\,dr.

2.3.
Positive Rockland operators on graded Lie groups

Now it is time to introduce the main object of the discussion, namely, Rockland operators. To define them, we first need to fix some notation for the continuous unitary representations of the group. Let
(\pi,\mathcal{H}_{\pi})
be a continuous unitary representation of a graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}.
Denote the set of equivalence classes of all strongly continuous unitary representations of
\mathbb{G}
by
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}.
Here, the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
denotes the representation space of
\pi.
We also denote the space of all smooth vectors of
\pi
by
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty},
which is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}.

The infinitesimal representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and its extension to the universal enveloping Lie algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
will also be denoted by
\pi.
We note here that the space of left-invariant vector fields and the algebra of left-invariant differential operators on
\mathbb{G}
can be identified with
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g}),
respectively. For a left-invariant differential operator
T
, let us denote by
\pi(T),
the infinitesimal representation
d\pi(T)
associated with
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}.

A left-invariant differential operator
\mathcal{R}
on a homogeneous group
\mathbb{G}
is called a
Rockland operator
if it is homogeneous of positive degree
\nu,
that is,

\mathcal{R}(f\circ D_{r})=r^{\nu}(\mathcal{R}f)\circ D_{r},\quad r>0,\,\,f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}),

and
the operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
is injective on
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\pi}
for every nontrivial representation
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},
that is,

\forall v\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\pi}\quad\pi(\mathcal{R})v=0\implies v=0.

(2.2)

The condition (
2.2
) is known as the Rockland condition. The Rockland condition for
\mathcal{R}
is equivalent to the
hypoellipticity
of
\mathcal{R},
that is locally,
\mathcal{R}f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G})\implies f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{G}).
This equivalence is commonly known as the
Rockland conjecture
(see
[
37
]
) and was resolved in
[
16
]
(see also
[
29
]
).
A Rockland operator is
positive
when

\int_{\mathbb{G}}\mathcal{R}f(x)\overline{f(x)}dx\geq 0,\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G}).

It is a celebrated result of Miller
[
29
]
(see also
[
42
]
and
[
7
, Proposition 4.1.3]
) which says that if there is a Rockland operator on a homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}
, then the group
\mathbb{G}
must be graded.
On the other hand, an infinite family of positive Rockland operators can be created for any graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}.
Indeed, the operators given by

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}a_{j}X_{j}^{2\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}},\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}>0

(2.3)

for any strong Malcev basis
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\}
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and any common multiple
\nu_{0}
of
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n},
are positive Rockland operators of homogeneous degree
\nu=2\nu_{0}.
It is easy to see that if
\mathcal{R}
is a positive Rockland operator, then its powers
\mathcal{R}^{k},\,k\in\mathbb{N},
and complex conjugate
\overline{\mathcal{R}}
are also Rockland operators.

Throughout this paper, we will always assume that a Rockland operator is always positive and essentially self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{G}).

In the stratified case, assume that
\{X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}\}
is a basis of the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
of the stratified Lie algebra. Then any left-invariant
sub-Laplacian
(with geometers sign convention) on
\mathbb{G}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{G}}:=-(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}+\cdots+X_{n_{1}}^{2})

(2.4)

is a positive Rockland operator of the homogeneous degree
\nu=2.

On
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{n},
with the trivial stratification and canonical family of dilation
D_{r}(x)=rx,\,\,r>0,
on the group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
, the Laplace operator
-\Delta_{x}:=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{x_{i}}^{2}
is a particular case of a positive sub-Laplacian. By equipping the group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
with another isotropic or anisotropic family of dilations with the dilations’ weights
\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n})\neq(1,1,\ldots,1)
determined by the canonical basis of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{n},
the operator

\mathcal{R}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}}a_{j}\partial_{j}^{2\frac{\nu_{0}}{\nu_{j}}},\quad\text{with}\,\,a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}>0

(2.5)

is a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu:=2\nu_{0}
on
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
provided
\nu_{0}
is any common multiple of
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{n}.

2.4.
Fourier transform on graded Lie groups

One of the important tools to deal with PDEs on graded Lie groups is the operator-valued group Fourier transform on
\mathbb{G}
. The group Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\pi):\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
of
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
at
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},
is a linear mapping that can be represented by an infinite matrix once we choose a basis for
the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi},
and defined by

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(f)(\pi)=\widehat{f}(\pi):=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)\pi(x)^{*}dx=\int_{\mathbb{G}}f(x)\pi(x^{-1})\,dx.

(2.6)

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G}),
the operator
\widehat{f}(\pi)
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
for each
\pi\in\widehat{G}.
Moreover, there exists a measure
\mu
on
\widehat{G}
such that the following inversion formula

f(x)=\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{G}}}\text{Tr}(\pi(x)\widehat{f}(\pi))d\mu(\pi)

holds for every
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G})
and
x\in\mathbb{G}.

Additionally, the following
Plancherel identity
is also true for
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{G}):

\int_{\mathbb{G}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\widehat{G}}\|\widehat{f}(\pi)\|_{\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\,d\mu(\pi).

(2.7)

Furthermore, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}
extends uniquely to a unitary isomorphism from
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
onto the space
L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}),
where the space
L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})
is defined as the direct integral of Hilbert spaces of measurable fields of operators

L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}}):=\int_{\widehat{G}}^{\oplus}\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})d\mu(x)

with the norm

\|\tau\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{\mathbb{G}})}=\left(\int_{\widehat{\mathbb{G}}}\|\tau_{\pi}\|_{\text{HS}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\,d\mu(\pi)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The measure
\mu
is called the Plancherel measure on
\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
.

Moreover, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathcal{R}f)(\pi)=\pi(\mathcal{R})\widehat{f}(\pi).

The authors in
[
17
]
proved that the spectrum of the operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
with
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}\backslash\{1\}
, is discrete and lies in
(0,\infty)
. Thus we can choose an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that the infinite matrix associated
to the self-adjoint operator
\pi(\mathcal{R})
has the following representation

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{R})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\pi_{1}^{2}&0&\cdots&\cdots\\
0&\pi_{2}^{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\ddots\end{array}\right)

where
\pi_{i},\,i=1,2,\ldots,
are strictly positive real numbers and
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}\backslash\{1\}
.

2.5.
Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups and interpolation inequalities

The Sobolev spaces on graded Lie groups were systematically studied by Fischer and the third author in
[
8
,
7
]
.

The inhomogeneous Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{G}):=H^{s}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{G}),s\in\mathbb{R}
, associated to positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
of homogeneous degree
\nu
, is defined as

H^{s}\left(\mathbb{G}\right):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\left(\mathbb{G}\right):(I+\mathcal{R})^{s/\nu}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)\right\},

with the norm

\|f\|_{H^{s}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}:=\left\|(I+\mathcal{R})^{s/\nu}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}.

Similarly, we define the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{p,s}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{G}):=\dot{H}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})
on
\mathbb{G}
as the space of all
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{G})
such that

\mathcal{R}^{{s}/{\nu}}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{G})
with the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})}:=\left\|\mathcal{R}^{s/\nu}f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{G}\right)}.

The reason for omitting the subscript
\mathcal{R}
is that these Sobolev spaces are independent of the choice of a Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a graded Lie group with homogeneous dimension
Q
. Then we use the following inequalities developed in
[
39
,
7
,
8
]
throughout this work.

•

Sobolev inequality

[
7
,
8
]
: Let
s>0
and
1<p<q<\infty
be such that

\frac{s}{Q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}.

Then

\displaystyle\|f\|_{{L}^{q}(\mathbb{G})}\lesssim\|f\|_{{\dot{H}}^{p,s}(\mathbb{G})}\simeq\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{G})}.

(2.12)

•

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

[
39
]
:
Let

s\in(0,1],1<r<\frac{Q}{s},\text{ and }~2\leq q\leq\frac{rQ}{Q-sr}.
Then

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{G})}\lesssim\|u\|_{{\dot{H}}^{r,s}(\mathbb{G})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}^{1-\theta},

(2.13)

for
\theta=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)/{\left(\frac{s}{Q}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\in[0,1]
, provided that
\frac{s}{Q}+\frac{1}{2}\neq\frac{1}{r}
.

3.
Philosophy of the approach

In this section, we will describe the approach to prove the main results of this paper. First, we provide the notion of mild solutions to (
1.1
) in our framework. Consider the inhomogeneous system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+\mathcal{R}u+u_{t}=F(t,x),&x\in\mathbb{G},~t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\\
u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

By applying Duhamel’s principle, the solution to the above system can be written as

u(t,x)=u_{0}*E_{0}(t,x)+u_{1}*E_{1}(t,x)+\int_{0}^{t}F(s,x)*E_{1}(t-s,x)\;ds,

where
*
denotes the group convolution product on
\mathbb{G}
with respect to the
x
variable, and
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
represent the propagators to (
3.1
) in the homogeneous case
F=0
with initial data
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)=\left(\delta_{0},0\right)
and
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)=

\left(0,\delta_{0}\right)
, respectively. Our main interest lies in considering power-type nonlinearities, that is,
F(t,x)=|u(t,x)|^{p}
, and this will always be the case throughout the paper.

A function
u
is said to be a
mild solution
to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]
if
u
is a fixed point for the integral operator
N:u\in X_{s}(T)\mapsto Nu(t,x),
given by

\displaystyle Nu(t,x):=u^{\text{lin}}(t,x)+u^{\text{non}}(t,x),

(3.2)

in the energy evolution space
X_{s}(T)\doteq\mathcal{C}\left([0,T],H^{1}(\mathbb{G})\right),

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle:=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left((1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+(1+t)^{\frac{1+\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\right)

(3.3)

with
\gamma>0,

where

u^{\text{lin}}(t,x)=u_{0}*E_{0}(t,x)+u_{1}*E_{1}(t,x)

is the solution to the corresponding linear Cauchy problem (
1.1
), and
u^{\text{non}}
is an integral operator with the following representation

u^{\text{non}}(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}F(s,x)*E_{1}(t-s,x)\;ds.

According to the Duhamel’s principle, we will prove the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of small data Sobolev solutions of low regularity to the semilinear damped wave equation (
1.1
) with
the help of the Banach’s fixed point theorem argument. We find a unique fixed point (say)
u^{*}
of the operator
N
, which means
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all positive
T
. More precisely, to find such a unique fixed point, we will establish two crucial inequalities of the form

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(3.4)

and

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left[\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right],

(3.5)

for any
u,v\in X_{s}(T)
with initial data space
\mathcal{A}^{s}:=\ (H^{1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )\times\ (L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )
and the positive constant constant
C
is independent of
T
.
We will consider sufficiently small
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
so that combining (
3.4
) with (
3.5
) we can apply Banach’s fixed point theorem to ensure that there exists a global-in-time small data unique Sobolev solution
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all
T>0
, which also gives the solution to (
1.1
). Here we want to note that the treatment of the power-type nonlinear term is based on the applications of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality on the graded Lie group
\mathbb{G}
.

In the proof of the global existence result for
p=p_{\text{crit}}
in the next section, the following integral inequality will be useful:
Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\alpha}(1+\kappa)^{-\beta}\;d\kappa\lesssim\begin{cases}(1+t)^{-\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}&\text{ if }\max\{\alpha,\beta\}>1;\\
{}\\
(1+t)^{-\min\{\alpha,\beta\}}\log(2+t)&\text{ if }\max\{\alpha,\beta\}=1;\\
{}\\
(1+t)^{1-\alpha-\beta}&\text{ if }\max\{\alpha,\beta\}<1.\end{cases}

4.
Global existence for critical higher order hypoelliptic damped wave equation

This section is devoted to proving the global-in-time existence of small data Sobolev solutions to (
1.1
) of lower regularity in the critical case. Before going to investigate the global-in-time existence result in the critical case, we first present the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu\geq 2.

Let
s\in(0,1]
and
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\left(H^{s}\cap L^{m}\right)\times\left(L^{2}\cap L^{m}\right)
for some
m\in(1,2]
. Then the solution
u\in\mathcal{C}^{1}\left([0,\infty),H^{s}\right)
of (
1.6
) satisfies the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{m}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}\cap L^{m}}\right),

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{s}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap L^{m}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap L^{m}}\right).

(4.2)

Proof.

The case
m=2
directly follows from
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
by taking
\gamma=0.

For
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
and for
1<m<2
with

\frac{\gamma}{Q}=\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2},

from Sobolev inequality (
2.12
), we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}\simeq\|\mathcal{R}^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}u(t,\cdot)\|_{{L}^{2}}\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{m}},

and hence

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{s-1}\cap L^{m}}.

(4.3)

Now using (
4.3
), from
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
with
s\in(0,1]
, we get

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{s}{\nu}}u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=C\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{s}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap L^{m}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap L^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{m}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}\cap L^{m}}\right)

for all
t>0.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that, the estimate (

) can also be written as (using Sobolev inequality)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{m}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{-1}\cap L^{m}}\right)

(4.4)

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu\geq 2.
Let
s\in(0,1].
Assume that

\begin{cases}\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{Q}{2}\right)\quad\hskip 34.14322pt\text{if}\quad Q=1,2;\\

\gamma\in\left(\frac{Q\nu}{2s}-\frac{Q}{2}-\nu,\min\{\frac{Q}{2},\tilde{\gamma}\}\right)\quad\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if}\quad Q=5,6,\end{cases}

where
\tilde{\gamma}
denotes the positive root of the quadratic equation
2\tilde{\gamma}^{2}+Q\tilde{\gamma}-\nu Q=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{-Q+\sqrt{Q^{2}+8\nu Q}}{4}
for
Q\geq 3
. Also, let the exponent
p
satisfy

\displaystyle p=p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu):=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}.

(4.5)

Then, there exists a small positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}
such that for any
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\mathcal{A}^{s}:=\ (H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )\times\ (L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\ )

1.1
) has a uniquely determined Sobolev solution

u\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,\infty),H^{s}\right).

Proof.

Our main aim is to prove the following two crucial inequalities

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right),

where the energy evolution space
X_{s}(T)\doteq\mathcal{C}\left([0,T],H^{s}(\mathbb{G})\right)
is
equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle:=\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\left((1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}+(1+t)^{\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right).

(4.6)

Now from the estimate
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, for
s\geq 0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s+\gamma\geq 0
, we recall that

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{{H}}^{s}(\mathbb{G})}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{{H}}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap\dot{{H}}^{-\gamma}}\right).

(4.7)

Now depending on the value of
\gamma
, from (
4.7
), using the Sobolev embedding
L^{2}\subset H^{s-1}
for
s\leq 1
and
H^{s}\subset L^{2}
for
s\geq 0
, we have the following crucial estimates for Sobolev solutions to the linear Cauchy problem.
For
\gamma>0
and
s\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{{H}}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}\right).

(4.8)

Thus

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}\right)

(4.9)

and for
s\in(0,1]

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{{H}}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{{H}}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap\dot{{H}}^{-\gamma}}\right).

(4.10)

Now from (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we can write

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{{\dot{H}}^{s}}+(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap{\dot{H}}^{-\gamma}}\right)=\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}.

Thus from above, the solution
u^{\text{lin}}

to the linear problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|u^{\text{lin}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq C_{1}\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}

(4.11)

where
C_{1}>0
is independent of
T
.

Now we will evaluate
\|u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}
and try to prove that

\|u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq C_{2}\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right),

for some
C_{2}>0
independent of
T
. Notice that

u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}(t,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{t}E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)*\left(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right){d}\kappa.

In order to estimate
\|u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}
, we first evaluate
\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}(u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}})\|_{L^{2}}
for
j=0,1
with the help of Proposition
4.1
.

Now applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.13
), for any
u\in X_{s}(T)
, we get

\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q}}\lesssim\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\theta}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{(1-\theta)}

for
t\in[0,T]
with
\theta=\frac{Q}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})\in[0,1]
. The above inequality also can be written as

\displaystyle\left\|u(t,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left[s\theta+\gamma\right]}\left\{(1+t)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right\}^{\theta}\left\{(1+t)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\}^{(1-\theta)}

\displaystyle\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left[s\theta+\gamma\right]}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}

\displaystyle=(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left[{Q}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})+\gamma\right]}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)},

(4.12)

for
2\leq q\leq\frac{2Q}{Q-2s}.
Now for
j=0,1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}(u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)*\left(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right){d}\kappa\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}E_{1}(t-\kappa,\cdot)*\left(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}}{d}\kappa.

Next, note that

\tilde{u}(t,x):=(E_{1}(t,\cdot)*|u|^{p})(x)

is also a solution to the linear system (
1.6
) with initial data
u_{0}=0
,
u_{1}=|u|^{p}
. To estimate Duhamel’s term on the interval
[0,t]
, from Proposition
4.1
(also see (
4.4
) of Remark
4.2
) with
u_{0}=0
,
u_{1}=|u|^{p}-|v|^{p}
and by the Sobolev embedding
L^{2}\subset H^{s-1}
for
s<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}(u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap L^{m}}{d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{m}}{d}\kappa

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t/2}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{1}{m}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\|(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p})(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{2}{p}}}\;d\kappa

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\|(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p})(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\;d\kappa,

(4.14)

where we consider
m=\frac{2}{p}
for
p\in(1,2)
and
m=1
for
p\geq 2
, however, we use
m=2
for the interval
[\frac{t}{2},t]
.
Now using the fact that

||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}|\leq p|u-v|(|u|^{p-1}+|v|^{p-1})

along with Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}\left(\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}+\|v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}\right).

(4.15)

Now we have to estimate three terms on the right-hand side of the previous inequality (
4.15
). Applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.13
) for each term that appeared on the right-hand side of (
4.15
), we get

\displaystyle\left\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\theta_{1}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+s{\theta_{1}}\right)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\right\}^{\theta_{1}}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\}^{(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{1}{\nu}\left({\gamma}+s{\theta_{1}}\right)}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}

(4.16)

and

\displaystyle\left\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{2p}}^{p-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{G})}^{(p-1)\theta_{1}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}^{(p-1)(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle=(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{(p-1)}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{(s+\gamma)}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{G})}\right\}^{(p-1)\theta_{1}}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}\right\}^{(p-1)(1-\theta_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{(p-1)}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1},

(4.17)

for
\kappa\in[0,T]
with
\theta_{1}=\frac{Q}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2p})\in[0,1]
.
Using the above estimates, (
4.15
) reduces to

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{p}{\nu}(s\theta_{1}+\gamma)}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=C(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{Q(p-1)}{2\nu}-\frac{p\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=C(1+\kappa)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

(4.18)

where in the last step we used that
p=p_{\text{Crit }}(Q,\gamma,\nu):=1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}.
Then the second integral in (
4.13
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{js}{\nu}}\|(|u|^{p}-|v|^{p})(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\;d\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{js}{\nu}}(1+\kappa)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\;d\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)\int_{t/2}^{t}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{js}{\nu}}\;d\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{\gamma+js}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

(4.19)

For the first integral of (
4.13
), we first consider
p\geq 2
so that
m=1
. Then

\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}\left(\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}+\|v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{p}}^{p-1}\right)

and proceeding similarly as in (
4.16
), (
4.17
), and (
4.18
) with
\theta_{1}=\frac{Q}{s}(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})
, we get

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+\kappa)^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

(4.20)

Then using the fact that
\gamma\in(0,\frac{Q}{2})
, i.e.,
\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}-1>-1
along with the
L^{2}
and
L^{1}
norm of
|u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}
from (
4.18
) and (
4.20
), the first integral of (
4.13
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{1}}{d}\kappa

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left(\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{1}}+\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right){d}\kappa

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right){d}\kappa

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right){d}\kappa

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right){d}\kappa

\displaystyle=C(1+t)^{-\frac{Q}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{\frac{Q}{2\nu}-1-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}{d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\frac{sj+\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

(4.21)

Now considering the case
p\leq 2
so that
m=\frac{2}{p}
, we get

\displaystyle\left\||u(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}-|v(\kappa,\cdot)|^{p}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\left(\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}+\|v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle=C(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}-\frac{\gamma}{\nu}(p-1)}\left\{(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)-v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left((1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}(p-1)}\|u(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}+(1+\kappa)^{\frac{\gamma}{\nu}(p-1)}\|v(\kappa,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma p}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

Then using the fact that
\frac{\gamma p}{\nu}=\frac{\gamma}{\nu}\left(1+\frac{2\nu}{Q+2\gamma}\right)<1
for
\gamma<\min\{\tilde{\gamma},\frac{Q}{2}\}
with
Q\geq 3,
we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-\kappa)^{-\frac{Q}{\nu}\left(\frac{p}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\||u|^{p}-|v|^{p}\right\|_{L^{2}\cap L^{\frac{2}{p}}}{d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{Q(p-1)}{2\nu}-\frac{sj}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+\kappa)^{-\frac{\gamma p}{\nu}}{d}\kappa

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}(1+t)^{-\frac{sj+\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right).

(4.22)

Thus combining all the cases (
4.19
), (
4.21
), and (
4.22
), the inequality (
4.13
) becomes

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}^{\frac{sj}{\nu}}(u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\leq A(1+t)^{-\frac{sj+\gamma}{\nu}}\left\|u-v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right),

for some constant independent of
T.
This gives us

\displaystyle\|u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq A\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left(\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right)

(4.23)

and from the definition of
N
in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}=\|u^{\text{non}}-v^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq A\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)}\left[\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}+\|v\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p-1}\right],

(4.24)

where the positive constant
A
is independent of
T
.

In particular, from (
4.23
), we also have

\displaystyle\|u^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq C\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p}

(4.25)

and cobning it with (
4.11
), we can write

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}=\|u^{\text{lin}}+u^{\text{non}}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq B\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}+B\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}^{p},

(4.26)

for some positive constant
B
(independent of
T
) with initial data space
\mathcal{A}^{s}:=(H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma})\times(L^{2}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma})
.
Consequently, for some
r>1
, we choose
R_{0}=rB\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}
with sufficiently small
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
so that

BR_{0}^{p}<\frac{R_{0}}{r}\quad\text{and}\quad 2AR_{0}^{p-1}<\frac{1}{r}.

Then (
4.24
) and (
4.26
) reduce to

\displaystyle\|Nu\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq\frac{2R_{0}}{r}

(4.27)

and

\displaystyle\|Nu-Nv\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq\frac{1}{r}\|u-v\|_{X_{s}(T)},

(4.28)

respectively for all
u,v\in\mathcal{B}(R_{0}):=\{u\in X_{s}(T):\|u\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq R_{0}\}
.

Since
\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}<\varepsilon
is sufficiently small, (
4.27
) implies that
Nu\in X_{s}(T)
, that is,
N
maps
X_{s}(T)
into itself.
On the other hand, (
4.28
) implies that the map
N
is a contraction mapping on the ball
\mathcal{B}(R_{0})
around the origin in the Banach space
X_{s}(T)
. Using Banach’s fixed point theorem, we can say that there exists a uniquely determined fixed point
u^{*}
of the operator
N
, which means
u^{*}=Nu^{*}\in X_{s}(T)
for all positive
T
. This implies that there exists a global-in-time small data Sobolev solution
u^{*}
of the equation
u^{*}=Nu^{*}
in
X_{s}(T)
, which also gives the solution to the semilinear damped wave equation (
1.1
). Moreover, the fixed point satisfies the estimate
\|u^{*}\|_{X_{s}(T)}\leq R_{0}=rB\left\|\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathcal{A}^{s}}
.
Due to the fact that no constant depends on time
T
, this argument gives us the existence
of a unique global-in-time solution in
u\in\mathcal{C}\left([0,\infty),H^{s}\right)
and this completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Diffusion phenomenon of damped wave equations on
\mathbb{G}

In this section, we establish the diffusion phenomenon with initial data belonging additionally to Sobolev spaces of negative order. Particularly, we show how the diffusion phenomenon bridges decay properties of solutions to the linear Cauchy problem
problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}+\mathcal{R}u+u_{t}=0,&x\in\mathbb{G},~t>0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\\
u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),&x\in\mathbb{G},\end{cases}

(5.1)

and solutions to the Cauchy problem for the linear heat equation (
1.7
).

Now invoking the group Fourier transform with respect to
x
on (
1.7
), for all
\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}}
, we get a Cauchy problem related to a parameter-dependent functional differential equation for
\widehat{u}(t,\pi)
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widehat{w}(t,\pi)+\pi(\mathcal{R})\widehat{u}(t,\pi)=0,&\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},~t>0,\\
\widehat{w}(0,\pi)=\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)+\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi),&\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},\end{cases}

where
\pi(\mathcal{R})
is the symbol of the Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
on
{\mathbb{G}}
. For
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the notation

\displaystyle\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}\doteq\left(\widehat{w}(t,\pi)e_{k},e_{m}\right)_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}},

(5.2)

where
\{e_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is the same orthonormal basis in the representation space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
that gives us (
2.4
). Then
\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}
solves the following infinite system of ordinary differential equation with respect to
t
variable

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}+\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}=0,&\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},~t>0,\\
\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}=\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}&\pi\in\widehat{\mathbb{G}},\end{cases}

(5.3)

where
\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}=\pi_{m}^{2}.
The solution to (
1.7
) in the Fourier space can be written by

\displaystyle\widehat{w}(t,\pi)_{m,k}={e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\left(\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}\right).

(5.4)

For
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\left({H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)\times\left({H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)
with
s\geq 0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s+\gamma\geq 0
, proceeding similarly as in the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have the following

\dot{H}^{s}
-decay estimate for the solution to the Cauchy problem (
1.7
) as

\displaystyle\|w(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\right)

(5.5)

for any
t\geq 0
.

Before going to discuss how the diffusion phenomenon bridges decay properties of solutions to the linear damped wave equation (
5.1
) and solutions to the linear heat equation (
1.7
), we recall, from
[
5
]
, that the solution to the linear problem (
5.1
) can be written in the Fourier space as

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(t,\pi)_{m,k}

\displaystyle=K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k},

(5.6)

where

\displaystyle K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})\right){e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}-\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right){e}^{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}&\text{ for }|\beta_{m,\pi}|<\delta,\\
\\
\frac{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right){e}^{\left(-i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}&\\
\\
\qquad-\frac{\left(-i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right){e}^{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}&\text{ for }|\beta_{m,\pi}|>N.\end{cases}

(5.7)

and

\displaystyle K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{{e}^{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)\right)t}-{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4}\right)\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}&\text{ for }|\beta_{m,\pi}|<\delta,\\
\\
\frac{{e}^{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}-{e}^{\left(-i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}&\text{ for }|\beta_{m,\pi}|>N.\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, from
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have the following
\dot{H}^{s}
-decay estimate for the solution to (
5.1
)

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{H^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\right),

(5.11)

for any
t\geq 0
.

From (
5.5
) and (
5.11
), we see that the solutions to the linear damped wave equation (
5.1
) and for the heat equation (
1.7
) satisfy the same decay estimates with slightly different regularity of initial data. This observation allows us to investigate decay properties for the difference of the solutions for the wave equation (
5.1
) and for the heat equation (
1.7
) in
\dot{H}_{{\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}}}^{s}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a graded Lie group of homogeneous dimension
Q
and let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree
\nu\geq 2.

Let
\left(u_{0},u_{1}\right)\in\left({H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)\times\left({H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}\right)
with
s\geq 0
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
such that
s+\gamma+\nu\geqslant 0
. Let
u
and
w
be the solutions to the linear Cauchy problems (
5.1
) and (
1.7
), respectively. Then,
u-w
satisfies

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)-w(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}-1}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\right).

(5.12)

Proof.

First we notice that for
|\beta_{m,\pi}|<\varepsilon\ll 1
, we see that

\displaystyle\left|K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})\right){e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}-\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right){e}^{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})\right){e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|+e^{-ct}

\displaystyle=\left|{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|+e^{-ct}

and

\displaystyle\left|K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{{e}^{\left(-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)\right)t}-{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4}\right)\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{-{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|+e^{-ct}

for some positive constant
c
. Similarly, for
|\beta_{m,\pi}|\gg 1
, we see that

\displaystyle\left|K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right){e}^{\left(-i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\frac{\left(-i|\beta_{k\lambda}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right){e}^{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq{e}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}+{e}^{-t}+{e}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}+{e}^{-t}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-ct},

and

\displaystyle\left|K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{{e}^{\left(i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}-{e}^{\left(-i|\beta_{m,\pi}|-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{2i|\beta_{m,\pi}|+\mathcal{O}(1)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{e}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}+{e}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\mathcal{O}\left(|\beta_{m,\pi}|^{-1}\right)\right)t}}{\sqrt{4\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1)^{2}}}+{e}^{-t}

\displaystyle\lesssim(|\beta_{m,\pi}|+1)^{-1}{e}^{-ct}

for some positive constant
c
.

Now define the cut-off function
\chi_{\text{int }}
with its support in
\{\beta_{m,\pi}\in\mathbb{R}^{*}:|\beta_{m,\pi}|<\varepsilon\ll 1\}
. Then using the above estimates, directly we have

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{u}(t,\lambda)_{kl}-\widehat{w}(t,\lambda)_{kl}\right|

\displaystyle=|\left(K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+\left(K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}|

\displaystyle=\chi_{\text{int }}|\left(K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+\left(K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}|

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\chi_{\text{int }})|\left(K_{0}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}+\left(K_{1}(t,\pi)_{m,k}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right)\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\chi_{\text{int }}\left(\left|{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|+e^{-ct}\right)\left|\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\chi_{\text{int }}\left(\left|\frac{-{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}}{-1+\mathcal{O}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}\right)}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\right|+e^{-ct}\right)\left|\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\chi_{\text{int}}\right){e}^{-ct}\left|\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\chi_{\text{int }}(\xi)\right)(|\beta_{m,\pi}|+1)^{-1}{e}^{-ct}\left|\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|.

(5.13)

For
|\beta_{m,\pi}|<\varepsilon\ll 1
, using the Newton-Leibniz integral formula, we have

\displaystyle{e}^{\left(-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\right)t}-{e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}

\displaystyle={e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})s}~ds

\displaystyle={e}^{-\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{4})\int_{0}^{t}e^{\mathcal{O}(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2})s}~ds

\displaystyle\leq C\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}{e}^{-c\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t},

where
C
is a positive constant. Thus from (
5.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\widehat{u}(t,\lambda)_{kl}-\widehat{w}(t,\lambda)_{kl}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\chi_{\text{int }}\left(\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}{e}^{-c\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}+e^{-ct}\right)\left(\left|\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|+\left|\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(1-\chi_{\text{int }}\right)\left(\left|\widehat{u}_{0}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|+\left|\widehat{u}_{1}(\pi)_{m,k}\right|\right).

(5.14)

Now following same procedure as in the proof of in
[
5
, Theorem 1.1]
, we can conclude that

\|u(t,\cdot)-w(t,\cdot)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-\frac{s+\gamma}{\nu}-1}\left(\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{{H}^{s}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}+\left\|u_{1}\right\|_{{H}^{s-1}\cap\dot{H}^{-\gamma}}\right),

completing the proof. ∎

We may immediately make the following observations based on the preceding finding.

Remark 5.2
.

Based on the results obtained from (
5.12
), it is evident that the decay rate is significantly higher compared to (
5.11
) and (
5.5
). This is due to the additional coefficient
\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}
with the factor
{e}^{-c\beta_{m,\pi}^{2}t}
in (
5.14
).

Remark 5.3
.

Concerning the decay rate in (
5.12
), we see that the decay is enhanced by a factor of
(1+t)^{-1}
when we subtract the solution to wave equation (
5.1
) by the solution to heat equation (
1.7
). This concludes that the diffusion phenomenon is also valid in the framework of the negative order Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-\gamma}
.

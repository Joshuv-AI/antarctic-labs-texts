Introduction.

A very particular case of the classical Nevanlinna-Pick problem states that for every point
\zeta_{0}
of the open unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
(
|\zeta_{0}|<1
) and for every
complex number
w_{0}
,
|w_{0}|\leq 1
there exists function
w(\zeta)
analytic on
{\mathbb{D}}
,
|w(\zeta)|\leq 1
such that
w(\zeta_{0})=w_{0}
.

A character automorphic analogue of this theorem was proved in
[
6
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Conformal maps of the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
onto itself
are of the form

\zeta\to\frac{a\zeta+b}{\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a}},\quad|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=1.

They form a group under composition.
Discrete subgroups of this group are called
Fuchsian groups
. By
\Gamma^{*}
we will
denote the group of unitary characters of
\Gamma
.

Automorphic analogue of the Nevanlinna-Pick problem reads as follows: given Fuchsian
group
\Gamma
and a unitary character
\beta
of this group. Given point
\zeta_{0}\in{\mathbb{D}}
and number
w_{0}
.
Find
\beta

automorphic
analytic on
{\mathbb{D}}
function
w(\zeta)
, that is,

w(\gamma(\zeta))=\beta(\gamma)w(\zeta),\quad\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad|\zeta|<1,

such that
|w(\zeta)|\leq 1
and
w(\zeta_{0})=w_{0}
.

It was shown in
[
6
]
that in this case the constraint on
w_{0}

is more restrictive than just
|w_{0}|\leq 1
. However, even before looking for this constraint one needs to know that
\beta
-automorphic
bounded analytic functions exist at all.

Widom gave a remarkable characterization of groups
\Gamma
such that
for every unitary character
\alpha
of
\Gamma
there exists a non-zero

\alpha
-automorphic bounded analytic function. We present here one of the equivalent forms
of this theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Widom-Pommerenke
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
)
.

Assume that group
\Gamma
is of convergent type, that is the Blaschke product over the orbit of
\Gamma
converges

g_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta)=\prod\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{\zeta-\gamma(\zeta_{0})}{1-\zeta\overline{\gamma(\zeta_{0})}}C_{\gamma}=\prod\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{\gamma(\zeta)-\zeta_{0}}{1-\gamma(\zeta)\overline{\zeta}_{0}}\widetilde{C}_{\gamma}.

(This function is called the Green function of group
\Gamma
). Also assume that
\Gamma
does not contain elliptic elements.
Then for every unitary character
\alpha
of
\Gamma
there exists a non-zero

\alpha
-automorphic bounded analytic function
if and only if

g^{\prime}_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta)
is a function of bounded characteristic.

Fuchsian group
\Gamma
is said to be of
Widom type
if
g^{\prime}_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta)
is of bounded characteristic.
If
g^{\prime}_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta)
is of bounded characteristic, then, by straightforward use of
Poincaré theta series associated with
g^{\prime}_{\zeta_{0}}
(see, e.g.
[
7
]
), one can show that
for every character
\alpha
there exist nonconstant
\alpha
automorphic bounded analytic functions.
The converse is a long and subtle story (
[
9
]
).

Let
H^{2}(\alpha)
be the set of all
\alpha
automorphic functions in the Hardy space
H^{2}
.
For groups of Widom type it is a nonzero closed subspace.
We denote by
k^{\alpha}_{\zeta_{0}}
the orthogonal projection of
\dfrac{1}{1-t\overline{\zeta}_{0}}
on
H^{2}(\alpha)
.
The following theorem is a special case of what was proved in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 1.3

(Kupin-Yuditskii)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Fuchsian group of Widom type. Let
\beta
be a unitary character of
\Gamma
. Let

.
There exists a
\beta

analytic

if and only if

(1.1)

|w_{0}|^{2}\leq\underset{\alpha\in\Gamma^{*}}{\inf}\dfrac{k^{\beta\alpha}_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta_{0})}{k^{\alpha}_{\zeta_{0}}(\zeta_{0})}.

Boundary analogue of the Nevanlinna - Pick theorem is the following classical

Definition 1.5

Let
\Gamma

\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|<\infty.

Then

(1.7)

m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(\zeta)=i\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{\gamma(t_{0})|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{(\gamma(t_{0})-\zeta)^{2}}=it_{0}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})}{(\gamma(t_{0})-\zeta)^{2}}.

The later equality holds since

|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|=t_{0}\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})}{\gamma(t_{0})}.

The derivative of the Martin function also equals

(1.8)

(see Lemma

The goal of this section is to prove Theorems

We will need two lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
|p|\geq 1
. Then

|p-1|\leq 2|p-r|,\quad 0\leq r\leq 1.

Proof.

We write
p
as

p=A(x+iy),\quad A\geq 1,\quad x^{2}+y^{2}=1.

Then

\dfrac{|p-1|^{2}}{|p-r|^{2}}=\dfrac{(Ax-1)^{2}+(Ay)^{2}}{(Ax-r)^{2}+(Ay)^{2}}=\dfrac{A^{2}-2Ax+1}{A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2}},\quad-1\leq x\leq 1.

The derivative with respect to
x
is

\dfrac{-2A}{A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2}}-\dfrac{A^{2}-2Ax+1}{(A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2})^{2}}(-2Ar)

=\dfrac{-2A}{(A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2})^{2}}\left[(A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2})-(A^{2}-2Ax+1)r\right]

=\dfrac{-2A}{(A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2})^{2}}\left[A^{2}+r^{2}-A^{2}r-r\right]

=\dfrac{-2A}{(A^{2}-2Arx+r^{2})^{2}}(A^{2}-r)(1-r)\leq 0,

since
A\geq 1
and
r\leq 1
. Therefore, the maximum is assumed at
x=-1
. Respectively,
y=0
and
p=-A
. Then

\dfrac{|p-1|}{|p-r|}=\dfrac{|-A-1|}{|-A-r|}=\dfrac{A+1}{A+r}\leq\dfrac{A+1}{A}=1+\dfrac{1}{A}\leq 2,

since
r\geq 0
and
A\geq 1
.
∎

In this case

and

(2.3)

itm^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(t)=\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}.

Proof.

This is Frostman Theorem

Theorem 2.3
.

m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
is of bounded characteristic if and only if

(2.4)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left(\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\right)\mu(dt)<\infty,

where
\mu(dt)
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

If
m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
is of bounded characteristic, then inequality (
2.4
) follows from formula (
2.3
).
Conversely, for
0<r<1

|m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(rt)|\leq\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-rt|^{2}}\leq 4\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}.

The first inequality is immediate from formula (
1.7
) for
m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
,
the second inequality is due to Lemma
2.1
applied to
p=\gamma(t_{0})\overline{t}
.
Therefore,

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log^{+}|m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(rt)|\mu(dt)\leq\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log^{+}\left(4\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\right)\mu(dt)

=\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left(4\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\right)\mu(dt).

The later equality holds since

4\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\geq\dfrac{4}{|t_{0}-t|^{2}}\geq 1.

Theorem follows.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In what follows we assume that

Since also measure corresponding to

is pure point, the boundary values

Theorem 2.5
.

If
m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
is of bounded characteristic, then

(2.5)

m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}=\dfrac{\Delta_{t_{0}}}{\varphi},

where
\Delta_{t_{0}}
is an inner function and
\varphi
is an outer function,
|\varphi|\leq 4
.

Proof.

We enumerate elements of the group
\Gamma
,
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{k}\}
, and consider functions (for
0<r<1
)

u_{n,r}(\zeta)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-r\zeta|^{2}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}\phi_{k}(\zeta),

where

\phi_{k}(\zeta)=\dfrac{\sqrt{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}}{\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-r\zeta}.

\log u_{n,r}
is a subharmonic function on
{\mathbb{D}}
. Indeed,

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\zeta\partial\overline{\zeta}}\log u_{n,r}(\zeta)=-\frac{1}{u_{n,r}^{2}(\zeta)}\frac{\partial u_{n,r}}{\partial\zeta}\frac{\partial u_{n,r}}{\partial\overline{\zeta}}+\frac{1}{u_{n,r}}\frac{\partial^{2}u_{n,r}}{\partial\zeta\partial\overline{\zeta}}

=\frac{1}{u_{n,r}^{2}(\zeta)}\left\{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}\phi_{k}(\zeta)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\overline{\phi^{\prime}_{k}(\zeta)}\phi^{\prime}_{k}(\zeta)-\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\overline{\phi_{k}(\zeta)}\phi^{\prime}_{k}(\zeta)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\overline{\phi^{\prime}_{k}(\zeta)}\phi_{k}(\zeta)\right\},

which is nonnegative by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. Since
\log u_{n,r}
is also continuous on
\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}
, we have

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-rt|^{2}}\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\mu(dt)\geq\log\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-r\zeta|^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.1
,

\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-rt|^{2}}\leq 4\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-t|^{2}}.

Therefore, Dominated Convergence Theorem can be used to pass to the limit when
r\nearrow 1

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\mu(dt)\geq\log\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-\zeta|^{2}}.

Since

\log\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}_{k}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma_{k}(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\geq\log\dfrac{1}{4},

we can apply Monotone Convergence Theorem to send
n
to
\infty

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-t|^{2}}\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\mu(dt)\geq\log\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-\zeta|^{2}}.

The latter inequality yields, in view of Lemma
2.2
,

(2.6)

\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\log|m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(t)|\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|t-\zeta|^{2}}\mu(dt)\geq\log\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{|\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})|}{|\gamma(t_{0})-\zeta|^{2}}\geq\log|m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(\zeta)|.

Since
m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}
is of bounded characteristic, it can be written as

m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}=\dfrac{\Delta_{1}O_{1}}{\Delta_{2}O_{2}},

where
\Delta_{1},\Delta_{2}
are inner functions,
O_{1},O_{2}
are bounded outer functions. Then (
2.6
) reads as

\log\left|\dfrac{O_{1}(\zeta)}{O_{2}(\zeta)}\right|\geq\log\left|\dfrac{\Delta_{1}(\zeta)O_{1}(\zeta)}{\Delta_{2}(\zeta)O_{2}(\zeta)}\right|,

that is,

\left|\dfrac{\Delta_{1}(\zeta)}{\Delta_{2}(\zeta)}\right|\leq 1.

This means that

\Delta_{t_{0}}=\dfrac{\Delta_{1}}{\Delta_{2}}

is an inner function. Since
|m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(t)|\geq 1/4
,

\dfrac{O_{1}}{O_{2}}=\dfrac{1}{\varphi},

\varphi
is outer,
|\varphi|\leq 4
.
∎

∎

Lemma 3.2

\frac{1-|\gamma(\zeta)|^{2}}{|t-\gamma(\zeta)|^{2}}=\frac{1-|\zeta|^{2}}{|\gamma^{-1}(t)-\zeta|^{2}}\left|(\gamma^{-1})^{\prime}(t)\right|.

m^{\prime}_{t_{0}}(\zeta)=it_{0}\sum\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)}{(\gamma(\zeta)-t_{0})^{2}}.

Proof.

Let,

\gamma(\zeta)=\dfrac{a\zeta+b}{\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a}},

where
|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}=1
. Then

(8.1)

\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)=\dfrac{1}{(\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a})^{2}}

and

\dfrac{\gamma^{\prime}(\zeta)}{(\gamma(\zeta)-t_{0})^{2}}

=\dfrac{1}{(\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a})^{2}\left[\dfrac{a\zeta+b}{\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a}}-t_{0}\right]^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{[(a\zeta+b)-t_{0}(\overline{b}\zeta+\overline{a})]^{2}}

=\dfrac{1}{[(-\overline{b}t_{0}+a)\zeta-(\overline{a}t_{0}-b)]^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{(-\overline{b}t_{0}+a)^{2}\left[\zeta-\dfrac{\ \overline{a}t_{0}-b}{-\overline{b}t_{0}+a}\right]^{2}}

=\dfrac{(\gamma^{-1})^{\prime}(t_{0})}{(\zeta-\gamma^{-1}(t_{0}))^{2}},

since

\gamma^{-1}(\zeta)=\dfrac{\ \overline{a}\zeta-b}{-\overline{b}\zeta+a}

(the inverse matrix).
∎

Theorem 8.2

(Carathéodory–Julia,
[
2
]
)
.

Let function
w
be analytic in the unit disk and bounded in modulus by
1
.
Let
t
be a point on the unit circle. The following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(1)\quad d_{1}:={\displaystyle\liminf_{\zeta\to t}\frac{1-|w(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}}<\infty\quad(|\zeta|<1,\zeta{\text{\ }approaches\ }t{\text{\ }in\ an\ arbitrary\ way});

\displaystyle(2)\quad d_{2}:={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}\frac{1-|w(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}}<\infty\quad(\zeta{\text{\ }approaches\ }t{\text{\ }nontangentially});

\displaystyle(3)\quad\mbox{Finite nontangential limits}\;\;

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad w(t):={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}w(\zeta)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;d_{3}:={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}\frac{1-w(\zeta)\overline{w(t)}}{1-\zeta\bar{t}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad w(t):={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}w(\zeta)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;w^{\prime}(t)={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}\frac{w(\zeta)-w(t)}{\zeta-t}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad w(t):={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}w(\zeta)}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;w^{\prime}_{0}:={\displaystyle\lim_{\zeta\to t}w^{\prime}(\zeta)}

\displaystyle\qquad\ \mbox{exist},\ |w(t)|=1.

\displaystyle(6)\quad\mbox{There exist a constant $w_{0}$, $|w_{0}|=1$ and a constant $d\geq 0$

Theorem 8.3

(Frostman,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
w
,
w_{n}
be analytic functions bounded in modulus by
1
on the unit disk.
Assume that
|w_{n+1}(\zeta)|\leq|w_{n}(\zeta)|
for every
|\zeta|<1
and every
n
.
Assume that
w_{n}(\zeta)
converges to
w(\zeta)
for every
|\zeta|<1
.
Let
|t|=1
. Assume that nontangential boundary values
w_{n}(t)
,

w^{\prime}_{n}(t)
exist and that
|w_{n}(t)|=1
,
w^{\prime}_{n}(t)

are finite. Then nontangential boundary values
w(t)
,

w^{\prime}(t)
exist and
|w(t)|=1
,
w^{\prime}(t)
is finite if and only if

|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|
are bounded above. In this case

w(t)=\lim w_{n}(t),\quad w^{\prime}(t)=\lim w_{n}^{\prime}(t).

Proof.

Observe first that the sequence
|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|
is increasing.
Indeed, by assumption,

\frac{1-|w_{n}(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}\leq\frac{1-|w_{n+1}(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}.

Therefore,

\lim_{\zeta\to t}\frac{1-|w_{n}(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}\leq\lim_{\zeta\to t}\frac{1-|w(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}}.

That is, in view of Theorem
8.2
,

|w^{\prime}_{n}(t)|\leq|w^{\prime}(t)|.

If
|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|
are bounded above, then a finite limit exists

d=\lim|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|.

We have boundary Schwarz - Pick inequalities (
8.2
)

\left|\frac{w_{n}(\zeta)-w_{n}(t)}{\zeta-t}\right|^{2}\leq|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|\cdot\frac{1-|w_{n}(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},\quad|\zeta|<1.

Let
w_{0}
be any subsequential limit of
w_{n}(t)
(
|w_{0}|=1
), then

(8.5)

\left|\frac{w(\zeta)-w_{0}}{\zeta-t}\right|^{2}\leq d\cdot\frac{1-|w(\zeta)|^{2}}{1-|\zeta|^{2}},\quad|\zeta|<1.

From here we conclude that nontangential limit
w(t)
of
w
exists and equals
w_{0}
.
On the other hand this means that
w_{n}(t)
has only one subsequential limit
w(t)
, therefore,

\lim w_{n}(t)=w(t).

We also conclude from (
8.5
) that a
finite nontangential limit
w^{\prime}(t)
exists and

|w^{\prime}(t)|\leq d.

Conversely, if nontangential limits
w(t)
and
w^{\prime}(t)
exist,
|w(t)|=1
,
w^{\prime}(t)
finite, then since

|w_{n}(\zeta)|\geq|w(\zeta)|
, we get that
|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|\leq|w^{\prime}(t)|
. Therefore,
|w_{n}^{\prime}(t)|
bounded above and

d\leq|w^{\prime}(t)|.

Thus,

|w^{\prime}(t)|=\lim|w^{\prime}_{n}(t)|.

Since

|w^{\prime}(t)|=t\frac{w^{\prime}(t)}{w(t)},

we also get

w^{\prime}(t)=\lim w^{\prime}_{n}(t).

∎

(Frostman,

]
)
.

Theorem 8.7
.

Let

u(\zeta)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{t_{k}+\zeta}{t_{k}-\zeta}c_{k},\quad|\zeta|<1,

where
|t_{k}|=1
,
c_{k}>0
. Let
|t|=1
,
t\neq t_{k}
.
Nontangential boundary values
u(t)
,

u^{\prime}(t)
exist and
u(t)\in\mathbb{R}
,
u^{\prime}(t)
is finite

if and only if

(8.7)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c_{k}}{|t_{k}-t|^{2}}<\infty.

In this case

(8.8)

u(t)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{t_{k}+t}{t_{k}-t}c_{k}

and

(8.9)

u^{\prime}(t)=2i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{t_{k}}{(t_{k}-t)^{2}}c_{k}=-2i\overline{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c_{k}}{|t_{k}-t|^{2}}.

Proof.

Consider

u_{n}(\zeta)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{t_{k}+\zeta}{t_{k}-\zeta}c_{k}.

Then

u^{\prime}_{n}(\zeta)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{2t_{k}}{(t_{k}-\zeta)^{2}}c_{k}.

Therefore,

u_{n}(t)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{t_{k}+t}{t_{k}-t}c_{k}

and

u^{\prime}_{n}(t)=i\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{2t_{k}}{(t_{k}-t)^{2}}c_{k}=-2i\overline{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{(t_{k}-t)(\overline{t_{k}}-\overline{t})}c_{k}

=-2i\overline{t}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{c_{k}}{|t_{k}-t|^{2}}.

Hence
u^{\prime}_{n}(t)
are bounded if and only if

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{c_{k}}{|t_{k}-t|^{2}}<\infty.

Then we apply Theorem
8.6
.

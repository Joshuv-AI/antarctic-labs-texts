Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative Noetherian ring and 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} be an ideal of R R . Suppose M M is a finitely generated R R -module and N N is an Artinian R R -module. We define the concept of filter coregular sequence to determine the infimum of integers i i such that the generalized local homology H i 𝔞 ​ ( M , N ) \textrm{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N) is not finitely generated as an R ^ 𝔞 \widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}} -module, where R ^ 𝔞 \widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}} denotes the 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} -adic completion of R R . In particular, if R R is a complete semi-local ring, then H i 𝔞 ​ ( M , N ) \textrm{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N) is a finitely generated R ^ 𝔞 \widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}} -module for all non-negative integers i i if and only if ( 0 : N 𝔞 + Ann ( M ) ) (0:_{N}\mathfrak{a}+\textrm{Ann}(M)) has finite length.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider a commutative Noetherian ring
R
with non-zero identity, and an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq R
, as well as two
R
-modules
M
and
N
. We denote the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion of
N
by
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
, and note that the
\mathfrak{a}
-adic completion functor
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
is an additive covariant functor on the category of
R
-modules. We use
L_{i}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
to denote the
i
-th left derived functor of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
. However, since the tensor functor is not left exact and the inverse limit is not right exact on the category of
R
-modules, computing the left-derived functors of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
is generally difficult. Moreover, it is important to note that
L_{0}^{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)\ncong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
.

Matlis studied
L^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(\cdot)
in the case where
\mathfrak{a}
is generated by a regular sequence and
R
is a local ring in
[
9
,
10
]
, and proved some duality between this functor and the local cohomology functor. Recently, Divaani-Aazar et al. in
[
4
]
studied the containment of
L^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(\cdot)
in a Serre class of
R
-modules up to a given upper bound
s\geq 0
.

Cuong and Nam in
[
2
]
defined the
i
-th
local homology

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)
of
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
as follows:

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right):=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\varprojlim}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a}^{n},N\right).

They also showed that
L^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)
when
N
is Artinian. Similarly, the
i
-th
generalized local homology

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
of
M
and
N
with respect to
\mathfrak{a}
is defined by

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N):=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\varprojlim}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M/\mathfrak{a}^{n}M,N\right);

see
[
13
]
for basic properties and more details.

Matlis in
[
8
]
introduced the concept of cosequence (or coregular sequence) as a dual of the concept of regular sequence (see
[
14
]
and
[
16
]
for more details and basic properties). If
N
is Artinian and
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})\neq 0
, then all maximal coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
have the same length, denoted by
0pt{\mathfrak{a}}N
, where
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
denotes the set of all elements
x\in N
such that
rx=0
for all
r\in\mathfrak{a}
. Moreover,
0pt{\mathfrak{a}}N=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)\neq 0\}
(see
[
3
, Theorem 4.11]
).

The filter regular sequences can be used to study the Artinianess of local cohomology modules of finitely generated
R
-modules (see
[
5
, Sec. 3]
).
In this paper as a dual of the concept of filter regular sequence, we introduce the concept of filter coregular sequence to study the finiteness of local homology modules of Artinian
R
-modules.

Let
\operatorname{Cosupp}(N)
denote the set of all prime ideals of
R
containing
\operatorname{Ann}(N)
.
A sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of elements of
\mathfrak{a}
is called a filter coregular
N
-sequence (of length
n
) in
\mathfrak{a}
if

\operatorname{Cosupp}\big((0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)/x_{i}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)\big)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\operatorname{Max}(R)
denotes the set of all maximal ideals of
R
.

Assuming that
M
is finitely generated and
N
is Artinian, we prove that if there exists a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of infinite length, then every filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
can be extended to a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of infinite length, and in this case we set
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
. Now suppose that all filter coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
have finite length. Then all maximal filter coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
are of the same length, denoted by
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)
. We prove (see Theorem
2.8
and Remark
2.9
) that:

\displaystyle\operatorname{f-width}(\operatorname{Ann}(M),N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)\textrm{ has infinite length as an }R\textrm{-module}\}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(M),N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)\textrm{ is not a finitely generated }\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}\textrm{-module}\}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)\textrm{ is not a finitely generated }\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}\textrm{-module}\}.

We also show in Corollary
2.11
that if
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(M))
has finite length. The converse statement is true when
R
is a semi-local ring that is complete with respect to its Jacobson radical.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N
be an

-module. We say a prime ideal

of

For an
R

(we refer the reader to
[

When

[

Definition 2.2
.

Let
N
be an
R
-module. A sequence
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of elements of
R
is called a filter coregular
N
-sequence (of length

) whenever

\operatorname{Cosupp}\big((0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)/x_{i}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)\big)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all

, where
\operatorname{Max}(R)
denotes the set of all maximal ideals of

. If, in addition,
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N
be an Artinian
R
-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

N
has finite length;

(ii)

\operatorname{Cosupp}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
; and

(iii)

\operatorname{Att}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
.

Proof.

Assume that
N
has finite length. Since
N
is finitely generated, we have
\operatorname{Cosupp}(N)=\operatorname{Supp}(N)
. Also, the Artinianness of
N
implies that
\operatorname{Supp}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
, and so
\operatorname{Cosupp}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
. This proves the implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii). The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is clear.
Finally, to prove the implication (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i), suppose that
\operatorname{Att}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
. Then, by
[
1
, Proposition 7.2.11]
, we have

\sqrt{\operatorname{Ann}(N)}=\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Att}(N)}\mathfrak{q}
,
and so
\big(\bigcap_{\mathfrak{q}\in\operatorname{Att}(N)}\mathfrak{q}\big)^{n}N=0
for some positive integer
n
.
It follows that
N
has finite length because
\operatorname{Att}(N)
consists of finitely many maximal ideals.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
be elements of
R
, and let
N
be an Artinian
R
-module. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is a filter coregular
N
-sequence;

(ii)

(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)/x_{i}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)
has finite length for all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(iii)

\operatorname{Att}\left((0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)/x_{i}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
for all
1\leq i\leq n
; and

(iv)

x_{i}\notin\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}\left(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R\right)\setminus\operatorname{Max}(R)}\mathfrak{p}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

The statements (i)–(iii) are equivalent by Lemma
2.3
. For each
1\leq i\leq n
, we set
N_{i-1}:=(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{i-1})R)

∎

\operatorname{Supp}(L)=\operatorname{Supp}(M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
for all
i<n
and we prove by induction on
n
that for every finitely generated
R
-module
L
with
\operatorname{Supp}(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(M)
,
\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L,N\right)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
for all
i<n
. Since
\operatorname{Supp}(L)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(M)
, by Gruson’s theorem
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
there exists a chain

0=L_{0}\subseteq L_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq L_{m}=L

of submodules of
L
such that, for each
1\leq j\leq m
,
L_{j}/L_{j-1}
is a homomorphic image of a direct sum of finitely many copies of
M
.
For each
1\leq j\leq m
, the exact sequence

0\rightarrow L_{j-1}\rightarrow L_{j}\rightarrow L_{j}/L_{j-1}\rightarrow 0

induces the following exact sequence

\displaystyle\dots\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j-1},N\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j},N\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j}/L_{j-1},N\right)\rightarrow\cdots.

Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j},N\right))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j-1},N\right))\cup\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j}/L_{j-1},N\right))

for all
1\leq j\leq m
and all
i
. It follows that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L,N\right))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{m},N\right))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{m-1},N\right))\cup\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{m}/L_{m-1},N\right))

\displaystyle\ \vdots

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{0},N\right))\cup\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j}/L_{j-1},N\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j}/L_{j-1},N\right)

for all
i<n
. Thus to prove the assertion it is sufficient for us to prove that
\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L_{j}/L_{j-1},N\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all
1\leq j\leq m
and all
i<n
. Hence we can assume that
m=1
and there exists an exact sequence

0\rightarrow K\rightarrow M^{t}\rightarrow L\rightarrow 0

for some
t\in\mathbb{N}
and some finitely generated
R
-module
K
. This exact sequence induces the following long exact sequence

(2.19)

\displaystyle\dots\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)^{t}\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L,N\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i-1}\left(K,N\right)\rightarrow\cdots.

For
n=1
, it follows from the exact sequence

(M\otimes_{R}N)^{t}\rightarrow L\otimes_{R}N\rightarrow 0
that

\operatorname{Cosupp}(L\otimes_{R}N)\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(M\otimes_{R}N)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R).

Therefore the result holds for
n=1
. Now assume, inductively, that
n>1
and the result has been proved for smaller values of
n
. It follows from the exact sequence (
2.19
) that

(2.20)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L,N\right))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right))\cup\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i-1}\left(K,N\right))

for all
i
. Since
\operatorname{Supp}(K)\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(M)
, the induction hypothesis implies that

\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(K,N\right))\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all
i<n-1
.
Thus, by the hypothesis and the inclusion (
2.20
), we have

\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(L,N\right))\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all
i<n
. This completes the inductive step.
∎

Now, we are ready to state and prove the main result of this paper. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
and let
N
be an Artinian
R
-module. Among the other things, the following theorem shows that the infimum of integers
i
with the property that the local homology module
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)
is not finitely generated as an
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module and the common length of all maximal filter coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
are same.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
, and let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules such that
M
is finitely generated and
N
is Artinian. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

there is a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of length
n
;

(ii)

any filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of length less than
n
can be extended to a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of length
n
;

(iii)

\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
(or equivalently
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right)
has finite length) for all
i<n
;

(iv)

if
\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)
, then
\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
(or equivalently
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)
has finite length) for all
i<n
; and

(v)

if
\operatorname{Ann}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
, then
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i<n
.

Proof.

The statements (iii) and (iv) are equivalent by Lemma
2.7
. The implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) is clear. Also, (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is an immediate consequence of the inclusion (
2.14
) in Corollary
2.6
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii). Assume that
\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right)\right)\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)
for all
i<n
, and suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
is a maximal filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of length
0\leq m<n
. The maximality of
x_{1},\dots,x_{m}
yields

\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}\left(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R\right)\setminus\operatorname{Max}(R)}\mathfrak{p}.

Since
\operatorname{Att}\left(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R\right)
is a finite set, it follows from the Prime Avoidance Theorem that
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}\left(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R\right)\setminus\operatorname{Max}(R)
. Hence, by the equation (
2.15
) in Corollary
2.6
and the hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\in

\displaystyle\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)\cap\operatorname{Att}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Att}(R/\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{R}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(R/\mathfrak{a}\otimes_{R}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})R))

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{m}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right))\cup\operatorname{Max}(R)

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R),

which is a contradiction. Hence the statements (i)–(iv) are equivalent.

(i)
\Leftrightarrow
(v). We prove, by induction on
n
, that (i) and (v) are equivalent. Assume that
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{Ann}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
.
We first assume that
n=1
. Since
M\otimes_{R}N
is Artinian, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}(M,N)\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong(M\otimes_{R}N)/\mathfrak{a}^{s}(M\otimes_{R}N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}\left(M/\mathfrak{a}^{s}M,N\right)

for all sufficiently large integers
s
. Also, since
\operatorname{Supp}(M/\mathfrak{a}^{s}M)=\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)
, the equivalence of (i) and (iv)
implies that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
R
-module or equivalently it is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module if and only if
\mathfrak{a}
contains a filter coregular element on
N
(note that since
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}(M,N)
is
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion, its submodules as an
R
-module and as an
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module are same; see
[
6
, Lemma 1.3]
). Thus the result holds in the case
n=1
.

Now assume, inductively, that
n>1
and the result has been proved for smaller values of
n
. Since
N
is Artinian, there exists
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{s}N=\mathfrak{a}^{t}N
for all
s\geq t
and so
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong N/\mathfrak{a}^{t}N
. Assume that either (i) or (v) holds. Since
n>1
and
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{0}(M,N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}\left(M/\mathfrak{a}^{s}M,N\right)
for sufficiently large integers
s
, if (v) holds, then
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}\left(M/\mathfrak{a}^{s}M,N\right)
has finite length by the hypothesis of (v). Since
\operatorname{Supp}(R/\mathfrak{a}^{t})=\operatorname{Supp}(M/\mathfrak{a}^{s}M)
, by Lemma
2.7
,
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}\left(R/\mathfrak{a}^{t},N\right)
has finite length in this case. Also, if (i) holds, then, by the equivalence of (i) and (iv),
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{0}\left(R/\mathfrak{a}^{t},N\right)
has finite length. Therefore in the either cases
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
has finite length.
Now, the exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathfrak{a}^{t}N\rightarrow N\rightarrow\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\rightarrow 0

of Artinian
R
-modules induces the following long exact sequences

(2.21)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i+1}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\mathfrak{a}^{t}N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\rightarrow\cdots

(see
[
13
, Proposition 2.4]
), and

(2.22)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i+1}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}^{t}N\right)\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)\rightarrow\cdots.

Since
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is Artinian, by
[
12
, Theorems 2.3(i) and 3.2]
, we have

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\cong H_{i}\left(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M\otimes_{R}F_{\bullet})\right),

where
F_{\bullet}
is a free resolution of
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
. Now
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
is finitely generated and so we can assume that every component of
F_{\bullet}
is finitely generated. On the other hand,
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(\cdot)
is an additive exact functor on the category of finitely generated
R
-modules, and hence it commutes with the homological functor in this category. Therefore

H_{i}\left(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(M\otimes_{R}F_{\bullet})\right)\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}\left(H_{i}\left(M\otimes_{R}F_{\bullet}\right)\right)\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)\right).

Since
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)
is Artinian, we obtain

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)\right)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)/\mathfrak{a}^{r}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)

for all sufficiently large integers
r
.
Since
\mathfrak{a}^{r}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)=0
for
r\geq t
, the above isomorphisms yield

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N/\mathfrak{a}^{t}N\right).

Hence
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))

is a finitely generated
R
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Also, the above isomorphism shows that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
is
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion, and so

\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
by
[
6
, Lemma 1.3]
. Now, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it follows from the long exact sequence (
2.21
) that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module if and only if
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,\mathfrak{a}^{t}N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module. Also, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
it follows from the long exact sequence (
2.22
) that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right)
has finite length if and only if
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{a}^{t}N\right)
has finite length because
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)\right)
has finite length for all
i
. Thus to prove the equivalence of (i) and (v), in view of the equivalence of (i) and (iii), we can replace
N
by

\mathfrak{a}^{t}N
and assume, in addition, that
\mathfrak{a}N=N
. Therefore
\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)\cap\operatorname{Att}(N)=\emptyset
, and so

\mathfrak{a}\nsubseteq\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}(N)}\mathfrak{p}
. Let
x_{1}\in\mathfrak{a}\setminus\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Att}(N)}\mathfrak{p}
.
Then
\operatorname{V}\left(x_{1}R\right)\cap\operatorname{Att}(N)=\emptyset
, and so
N=x_{1}N
.
The exact sequence

0\rightarrow(0:_{N}x_{1}R)\rightarrow N\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}N\rightarrow 0

induces the long exact sequence

(2.23)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i+1}(M,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i+1}(M,N)\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,(0:_{N}x_{1}R))

\displaystyle\rightarrow\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots.

We first assume that (i) holds. By the equivalence of (i) and (ii), we can extend
x_{1}
to a filter coregular
N
-sequence of length
n
, say
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
. Hence
x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
is a filter coregular
(0:_{N}x_{1}R)
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
, and so, by the inductive hypothesis,
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,(0:_{N}x_{1}R))
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i<n-1
. It follows from the long exact sequence (
2.23
) that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)/x_{1}\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
and consequently its homomorphic image
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)/(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}})\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
are finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-modules for all
i<n
. Also, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.3(i)]
, we have

\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{N}}(\mathfrak{a}\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}})^{t}\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)=\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{N}}({\mathfrak{a}}^{t}\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}})\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)=\bigcap_{t\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathfrak{a}}^{t}\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)=0.

Hence, by
[
11
, Theorem 8.4]
,
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i<n
. Conversely, assume that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i<n
. It follows from the long exact sequence (
2.23
) that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,(0:_{N}x_{1}R))
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i<n-1
, and so, by the inductive hypothesis, there is a filter coregular
(0:_{N}x_{1}R)
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of length
n-1
, say
x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
. Therefore
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
is a filter coregular
N
-sequence. This completes the inductive step.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
, and let
N
be an Artinian
R
-module. When there exists a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of infinite length, then, by the equivalence of (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.8
, any filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
can be extended to a filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
of arbitrary length, and in this case we set
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
. Now assume that all filter coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
have finite length. Again, by the equivalence of (i) and (ii) in Theorem
2.8
,
we can extend any filter coregular
N
-sequence in
\mathfrak{a}
to a maximal one, and all maximal filter coregular
N
-sequences in
\mathfrak{a}
are of the same length which we denote this common length by
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)
.
Moreover, if
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module such that
\operatorname{Supp}(M)=\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)
, then, by Theorem
2.8
, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{Cosupp}\left(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)\right)\nsubseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)\textrm{ has infinite length as an }R\textrm{-module}\}

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)\textrm{ is not a finitely generated }\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}\textrm{-module}\}

(we note that
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(R,N)=\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)
).
Also, for an arbitrary finitely generated
R
-module
L
, since
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(L)}_{i}(L,N)\cong\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(L,N)
, if we replace
\mathfrak{a}
by
\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(L)
in Theorem
2.8
, then the equivalence of (ii) and (v) in Theorem
2.8
yields

(2.25)

\displaystyle\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(L),N)

\displaystyle=\inf\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(L,N)\textrm{ is not a finitely generated }\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}\textrm{-module}\}.

Finally, since
\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)=\operatorname{V}\left(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}\right)
, it follows from the first equality in the equation (
2.24
) that
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\operatorname{f-width}(\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}},N).

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
, and let
N
be an Artinian
R
-module. If
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
, then
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
has finite length. The converse statement holds whenever
R
is a semi-local ring which is complete with respect to its Jacobson radical.

Proof.

Assume that
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
, and
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots
is a filter coregular
N
-sequence of infinite length in
\mathfrak{a}
. There is the following descending chain of submodules of
N

(0:_{N}x_{1}R)\supseteq(0:_{N}(x_{1},x_{2})R)\supseteq(0:_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})R)\supseteq\cdots.

Hence
(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)=(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})R)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and so
x_{n}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)=0
. Thus

(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)
has finite length because
(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)/x_{n}(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)
has finite length by definition. Hence
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})\subseteq(0:_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})R)
has finite length. To prove the converse statement,
assume that
R
is a complete semi-local ring and that
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
has finite length. Hence

\operatorname{Cosupp}(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})=\operatorname{Supp}(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R).

On the other hand, for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(N)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right))
. Therefore, in view of
[
14
, Proposition 2.12]
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Cosupp}(\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(R/\mathfrak{a},N\right))\subseteq\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(N)\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{V}\left(\mathfrak{a}\right)\cap\operatorname{Cosupp}(N)=\operatorname{Cosupp}(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\operatorname{Max}(R)

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence Theorem
2.8
implies that
\operatorname{f-width}(\mathfrak{a},N)=\infty
.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
R
, and let
M
and
N
be
R
-modules such that
M
is finitely generated and
N
is Artinian.

(i)

If
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}\left(M,N\right)
has finite length for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
(0:_{N}\operatorname{Ann}(M))
has finite length.

(ii)

If
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}(M,N)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a}+\operatorname{Ann}(M))
has finite length. In particular,
(0:_{N}\mathfrak{a})
has finite length whenever
\operatorname{H}^{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}\left(N\right)
is a finitely generated
\widehat{R}^{\mathfrak{a}}
-module for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Moreover, the converse statements hold when
R
is a complete semi-local ring.

Proof.

It follows by the equations (
2.24
), (
2.25
) and Proposition
2.10
.
∎

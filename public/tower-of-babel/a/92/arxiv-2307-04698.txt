1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of work of the author together with Bergelson and Best
[
ABB21
]
and Bergelson and Shalom
[
ABS22
]
investigating the phenomenon of multiple recurrence with large intersections for actions of countable abelian groups (see also
[
Sha22
]
).

1.1.
Background and motivation

The impetus for studying large intersections for multiple recurrence comes from the following two classical results:

Theorem 1.1

(Khintchine’s recurrence theorem
[
Kh35
]
)
.

For any invertible measure-preserving system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T\right)
, any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\right)>\mu(A)^{2}-\varepsilon\right\}

has bounded gaps.

Theorem 1.2

(Furstenberg’s multiple recurrence theorem
[
Fur77
]
)
.

For any invertible measure-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, any
A\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
, and any positive integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\liminf_{N-M\to\infty}{\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}{\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-kn}A\right)}}>0.

In particular, there exists
c>0
such that the set

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap\cdots\cap T^{-kn}A\right)>c\right\}

has bounded gaps.

A subset of
\mathbb{Z}
with bounded gaps is called
syndetic
.
More generally, in a countable discrete abelian group
(\Gamma,+)
, a subset
S\subseteq\Gamma
is
syndetic
if finitely many translates of
S
cover
\Gamma
.

With the aim of finding a common refinement of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
,
Bergelson, Host, and Kra
[
BHK05
]
asked whether, for a measure-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, a set
A\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
, and
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap\dots\cap T^{-kn}A\right)>\mu(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.
They found that the answer depends on the length
(k+1)
of the arithmetic progression:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
BHK05
]
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3)
.

(1)

For any ergodic measure-preserving system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T\right)
,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the sets

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap T^{-2n}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

and

\left\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap T^{-2n}A\cap T^{-3n}A\right)>\mu(A)^{4}-\varepsilon\right\}

are syndetic.

(2)

There exists an ergodic measure-preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
with the following property.
For any
l\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
A=A(l)\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(A)>0
such that

\mu\left(A\cap T^{-n}A\cap T^{-2n}A\cap T^{-3n}A\cap T^{-4n}A\right)\leq\mu(A)^{l}

for every
n\neq 0
.

Remark 1.4
.

The ergodicity assumption in item (1) cannot be dropped.
An adaptation of Behrend’s construction of sets avoiding 3-term arithmetic progressions
[
Beh46
]

can be used to produce a counterexample for the non-ergodic transformation
T(x,y)=(x,y+x)
on the 2-torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
;
see
[
BHK05
, Theorem 2.1]
.

The combinatorial content of Theorem
1.3
(1) is expressed by the following closely related result:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
Gre05
]
, Theorem 1.10;
[
GT10
]
, Theorem 1.12)
.

Let
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
.

(1)

There exists
N_{3}=N_{3}(\alpha,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
if
N\geq N_{3}
and
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
has cardinality
|A|\geq\alpha N
,
then there exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|\left\{a\in\mathbb{N}:\{a,a+d,a+2d\}\subseteq A\right\}\right|>(\alpha^{3}-\varepsilon)N.

(2)

There exists
N_{4}=N_{4}(\alpha,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that
if
N\geq N_{4}
and
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}
has cardinality
|A|\geq\alpha N
,
then there exists
d\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left|\left\{a\in\mathbb{N}:\{a,a+d,a+2d,a+3d\}\subseteq A\right\}\right|>(\alpha^{4}-\varepsilon)N.

Remark 1.6
.

(1) The positive integers
d\in\mathbb{N}
appearing in Theorem
1.5
are sometimes referred to as

popular differences
, since they are common differences for many arithmetic progressions contained in
A
.

(2) Theorem
1.3
can be converted directly into a combinatorial statement involving sets of positive upper Banach density by a version of the Furstenberg correspondence principle that produces ergodic measure-preserving systems; see
[
BHK05
, Section 1.2]
.
However, no simple argument is known to deduce Theorem
1.5
from Theorem
1.3
or
vice versa
.

In other contexts in which a multiple recurrence result is known,
one may again ask whether it is possible to find a corresponding Khintchine-type enhancement.
Pursuing this line of inquiry, Bergelson, Tao, and Ziegler
[
BTZ15
]
established a Khintchine-type recurrence result
for actions of the group
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
:

Theorem 1.7

(
[
BTZ15
]
, Theorems 1.12 and 1.13)
.

Fix a prime
p
and
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
For any ergodic measure-preserving
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}}\right)
,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the sets

\left\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}:\mu\left(A\cap T_{ag}^{-1}A\cap T_{bg}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

and

\left\{g\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\infty}:\mu\left(A\cap T_{ag}^{-1}A\cap T_{bg}^{-1}A\cap T_{(a+b)g}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{4}-\varepsilon\right\}

are syndetic.

A finitary combinatorial analogue of Theorem
1.7
along the lines of Theorem
1.5

can be deduced using the methods established in
[
Gre05
]
and
[
GT10
]
, which in fact apply to general finite abelian groups.
See also
[
Gre07
, Lecture 4]
.

The most general multiple recurrence result with which we will concern ourselves is the following,
which can be seen as a consequence of
[
FK85
]
or
[
Aus16
, Theorem B]
:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
.
For any measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

and any set
A\in\mathcal{X}
, the set

\left\{g\in\Gamma:\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi_{1}(g)}^{-1}A\cap\dots\cap T_{\varphi_{k}(g)}^{-1}A\right)>0\right\}

is syndetic.

Remark 1.9
.

When dealing with topological groups, one may wish to impose various continuity assumptions (for instance, on the endomorphisms
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}
or on the action of
\Gamma
on
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
).
Moreover, notions of largeness for subsets of
\Gamma
such as syndeticity and upper Banach density (discussed below in Section
1.3
) depend on the topology on
\Gamma
.
We assume that
\Gamma
is discrete in order to avoid such topological issues.

The foregoing discussion motivates the following general definition:

Definition 1.10
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
A family of endomorphisms
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
has the
large intersections property

if the following holds:
for any ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{g\in\Gamma:\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi_{1}(g)}^{-1}A\cap\dots\cap T_{\varphi_{k}(g)}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{k+1}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

We now give a brief summary of the previously known results about the large intersections property
in general countable discrete abelian groups.

In
[
ABB21
]
, a far-reaching generalization of Theorems
1.3
and
1.7

for configurations of length 3 and 4 was obtained (in a slight abuse of notation,
we abbreviate a family of endomorphisms of the form
\{g\mapsto a_{1}g,\dots,g\mapsto a_{k}g\}
by
\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
):

Theorem 1.11

(
[
ABB21
]
, Theorems 1.10 and 1.11)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.

(1)

If
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
are such that all three subgroups

\varphi(\Gamma)
,
\psi(\Gamma)
, and
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
have finite index
in
\Gamma
, then
\{\varphi,\psi\}
has the large intersections property.

(2)

If
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
and all four subgroups
a\Gamma
,
b\Gamma
,
(a+b)\Gamma
, and
(b-a)\Gamma

have finite index in
\Gamma
, then
\{a,b,a+b\}
has the large intersections property.

Remark 1.12
.

Endomorphisms of groups with finite index conditions of the kind appearing in item (1) of Theorem
1.11

have led to fruitful developments in a number of areas of ergodic theory and combinatorics;
see, e.g.,
[
Gri09
,
Pre15
,
LL21
,
GLL23
,
Pil22
]
.

Item (2) in Theorem
1.11
was also obtained independently by Shalom; see
[
Sha22
, Theorem 1.3]
.
In joint work with Bergelson and Shalom, item (1) of Theorem
1.11
was strengthened as follows:

Theorem 1.13

(
[
ABS22
]
, Theorems 1.11 and 1.13)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.

(1)

Suppose
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
and two of the three subgroups
\varphi(\Gamma)
,
\psi(\Gamma)
,
and
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
have finite index in
\Gamma
.
Then
\{\varphi,\psi\}
has the large intersections property.

(2)

Suppose
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
are distinct, nonzero integers such that
(b-a)\Gamma
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
Then
\{a,b\}
has the large intersections property.

This result leaves the following as a natural open question:

Question 1.14

(
[
ABS22
]
, Question 1.12)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Suppose
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
Does
\{\varphi,\psi\}
have the large intersections property?

Remark 1.15
.

There are a variety of examples of pairs
\{\varphi,\psi\}
without the large intersections property
(see
[
ABB21
, Example 10.2]
and
[
ABS22
, Theorem 1.14]
),
so it is necessary to impose some condition on
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)

(such as the finite index assumption in Question
1.14
)
in order to hope for the large intersections property.

The goal of this paper is to extend the techniques in
[
ABS22
]
to answer Question
1.14
affirmatively
under a mild additional technical assumption.
As we will see, this condition is always satisfied when the endomorphisms are obtained as multiplication by integers
or when the group
\Gamma
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
Hence, we are able to reproduce Theorem
1.13
(2)
and fully resolve Question
1.14
for
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

1.2.
Main results

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
.
Suppose there exist endomorphisms
\eta,\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime},\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that:

(i)

\eta(\Gamma)
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
;

(ii)

\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}\circ\eta
and
\psi=\psi^{\prime}\circ\eta
;

(iii)

\theta_{1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}+\theta_{2}\circ\psi^{\prime}
is injective; and

(iv)

(\psi^{\prime}-\varphi^{\prime})(\Gamma)
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
.

Then for any ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{g\in\Gamma:\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi(g)}^{-1}A\cap T_{\psi(g)}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

Remark 1.17
.

The conditions (i)–(iv) in Theorem
1.16
may be forbidding at first glance.
We give a brief explanation here and also refer the reader to the special cases outlined below
for developing stronger intuition about each condition.

Conditions (i) and (ii) can be interpreted as follows.
Define a new
\Gamma
-action on
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
by
S_{g}=T_{\eta(g)}
.
Then

\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi(g)}^{-1}A\cap T_{\psi(g)}^{-1}A\right)=\mu\left(A\cap S_{\varphi^{\prime}(g)}^{-1}A\cap S_{\psi^{\prime}(g)}^{-1}A\right),

so it suffices to prove a statement about the system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(S_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

and the endomorphisms
\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
.
The key consequence of (i) is that the new system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(S_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

has only finitely many ergodic components, and our dynamical tools are flexible enough to handle this situation.

Condition (iii) imposes a level of nontriviality to the pair
\{\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\}

by ensuring that the map
g\mapsto(\varphi^{\prime}(g),\psi^{\prime}(g))
is injective.
This condition also turns out to be crucial to describing the limiting behavior of double ergodic averages
associated with
\{\varphi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\}
.

Finally, condition (iv) is the key assumption in order to get started with analyzing the relevant double ergodic average
by invoking
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.10]
, which is proved using the van der Corput differencing trick
(see Lemma
2.1
below).
It is absolutely essential to the method used in this paper,
though it is less clear whether condition (iv) is needed to obtain the desired conclusion.
A concrete example where we do not know whether or not the large intersections property holds
(and for which condition (iv) does not hold) is the following.
Let
\Gamma=(\mathbb{Q}_{>0},\cdot)
be the group of positive rational numbers under multiplication (considered as a discrete group).
Let
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
be coprime.
It was asked in
[
ABS22
, Question 1.18]
whether the pair
\{q\mapsto q^{a},q\mapsto q^{b}\}

has the large intersections property, and we are presently unable to make any substantial progress on this question.
For this example, although condition (iv) does not hold, it is nevertheless the case that
the group generated by
\{q^{a}:q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\}
and
\{q^{b}:q\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}\}
has finite index in
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}

(in fact, it is equal to
\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
), which eliminates many of the possible approaches to producing a counterexample.
Additional discussion of the difficulties involved in this problem can be found in
[
ABS22
, Section 2.7]
.

We now turn to several consequences of Theorem
1.16
.

Theorem
1.16
includes Theorem
1.13
(2) as a special case.
Given
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
(b-a)\Gamma
has finite index in
\Gamma
,
let
d=\gcd(a,b)
,
a^{\prime}=\frac{a}{d}
, and
b^{\prime}=\frac{b}{d}
.
Since
d\mid b-a
, we have
d\Gamma\supseteq(b-a)\Gamma
, so
d\Gamma
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
The integers
a^{\prime}
and
b^{\prime}
are coprime, so there exist
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
c_{1}a^{\prime}+c_{2}b^{\prime}=1
.
Finally
(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime})\mid(b-a)
, so
(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime})\Gamma\supseteq(b-a)\Gamma
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
Taking
\varphi(g)=ag
,
\psi(g)=bg
,
\eta(g)=dg
,
\varphi^{\prime}(g)=a^{\prime}g
,
\psi^{\prime}(g)=b^{\prime}g
,

\theta_{1}(g)=c_{1}g
, and
\theta_{2}(g)=c_{2}g
and applying Theorem
1.16

reproduces the conclusion of Theorem
1.13
(2).

Another illustrative special case of Theorem
1.16
is the following:

Corollary 1.18
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
\psi-\varphi
is injective with finite index image.
Then for any ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{g\in\Gamma:\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi(g)}^{-1}A\cap T_{\psi(g)}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

For the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, Corollary
1.18
takes the following shape:

Corollary 1.19
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{\vec{n}})_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\right)
be an ergodic
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-system.
Then for any integer matrices
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
M_{2}-M_{1}
is nonsingular,
any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\mu\left(A\cap T_{M_{1}\vec{n}}^{-1}A\cap T_{M_{2}\vec{n}}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

In the case
d=2
, Corollary
1.19
was established in
[
ABS22
]
using a combination of different methods.
If
M_{1}
is also nonsingular, then the conclusion follows from
[
ABS22
, Theorem 1.11]
,
which is proved using methods similar to the current paper involving characteristic factors for multiple ergodic averages.
In the case that both
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are rank one matrices, basic linear algebra combined with the Fubini property
for uniform Cesàro limits (see
[
BL15
, Lemma 1.1]
) allows one to prove a stronger result
that implies, in particular that the set

\left\{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\mu\left(A\cap T_{M_{1}\vec{n}}^{-1}A\cap T_{M_{2}\vec{n}}^{-1}A\right)>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic for any (not necessarily ergodic) measure-preserving system

\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{\vec{n}})_{\vec{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\right)
, any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
; see
[
ABS22
, Theorem 7.1]
.

The method for handling the case where both
M_{1}
nor
M_{2}
are singular matrices
does not easily generalize for
d\geq 3
.
Instead, we produce a new proof avoiding any use of matrix manipulations
that unifies the two different cases in order to apply to general
d\in\mathbb{N}

and in fact to general countable discrete abelian groups.

1.3.
Combinatorial consequences and questions

Recurrence results in ergodic theory translate into combinatorial statements about sets of positive density.
Let us first make precise what we mean by the density of a subset of an abelian group.
A
Følner sequence
in a countable discrete abelian group
\Gamma
is a sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}

of finite subsets of
\Gamma
such that for any
x\in\Gamma
,

\frac{\left|(\Phi_{N}+x)\triangle\Phi_{N}\right|}{|\Phi_{N}|}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

The
upper density of a set
E\subseteq\Gamma
along a Følner sequence
\Phi=(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is the quantity

\overline{d}_{\Phi}(E)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\left|E\cap\Phi_{N}\right|}{|\Phi_{N}|}.

The
upper Banach density
of
E\subseteq\Gamma
is
d^{*}(E)=\sup_{\Phi}\overline{d}_{\Phi}(E)
, where the supremum is over all Følner sequences in
\Gamma
.
An immediate consequence of Theorem
1.16
together with a version of the Furstenberg correspondence principle for ergodic systems (see
[
BF21
, Theorem 2.8]
) is the following:

Theorem 1.20
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
be as in Theorem
1.16
.
Then for any
E\subseteq\Gamma
and any
\varepsilon>0
, the set

\left\{g\in\Gamma:d^{*}\left(E\cap(E-\varphi(g))\cap(E-\psi(g))\right)>d^{*}(E)^{3}-\varepsilon\right\}

is syndetic.

This strongly suggests that various finitary combinatorial results hold.
Namely, we conjecture that the following finitary version of Corollary
1.18
is true:

Conjecture 1.21
.

For
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\alpha,\varepsilon)
with the property:
for any finite abelian group
G
of order
N\geq N_{0}
,
any
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(G)
such that
\psi-\varphi
is an automorphism,
and any set
A\subseteq G
with
|A|\geq\alpha N
,
there exists
y\in G\setminus\{0\}
such that

\left|\left\{x\in G:\{x,x+\varphi(y),x+\psi(y)\}\subseteq A\right\}\right|>(\alpha^{3}-\varepsilon)N.

A natural conjecture in the setting of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, building on Corollary
1.19
, is:

Conjecture 1.22
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{d\times d}(\mathbb{Z})
such that
M_{2}-M_{1}
is nonsingular.
For any
\alpha,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
N_{0}=N_{0}(\alpha,\varepsilon,M_{1},M_{2})\in\mathbb{N}
with the property:
for any
N\geq N_{0}
and any set
A\subseteq\{1,\dots,N\}^{d}
with
|A|\geq\alpha N^{d}
,
there exists
\vec{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
such that

\left|\left\{\vec{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\left\{\vec{x},\vec{x}+M_{1}\vec{y},\vec{x}+M_{2}\vec{y}\right\}\subseteq A\right\}\right|>(\alpha^{3}-\varepsilon)N^{d}.

If one imposes the additional condition in Conjecture
1.21
that
\varphi
and
\psi
are automorphisms,
then the conjecture is known to be true by
[
BSST22
, Theorem 7.3]
.
Similarly, if the matrices
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
, and
M_{2}-M_{1}
in Conjecture
1.22
are all nonsingular, then
the conclusion holds by
[
BSST22
, Theorem 1.1]
.
(For the particular matrices

M_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),

this was also shown by
[
Kov21
, Theorem 1]
.)

The ergodic theoretic methods used in this paper are not immediately applicable in the finitary setting.
In order to resolve Conjectures
1.21
and
1.22
,
one should replace the dynamical tools with suitable analogues from higher order Fourier analysis.

1.4.
Outline of the paper

The structure of the paper is as follows.
Section

is preparatory, collecting the relevant background material
that will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.16
.
The main technical results appear in Sections
3
and

,
where we prove the existence of extensions in which the Kronecker and quasi-affine factors

We then prove a formula for the limit of double ergodic averages associated with

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Uniform Cesàro limits and the van der Corput differencing lemma

Just as Furstenberg’s multiple recurrence theorem (Theorem
1.2
) establishes a recurrence result
by working with a multiple ergodic average,
we will prove Theorem
1.16
by studying an associated double ergodic average.
A sequence
(v_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
in a (real or complex) topological vector space
V
has
uniform Cesàro limit
equal to
v\in V
, denoted
\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{v_{g}}=v
, if for any Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
, one has

\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{g\in\Phi_{N}}{v_{g}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}v.

In the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
, the uniform Cesàro limit corresponds to the limit of averages appearing in Theorem
1.2
, i.e.

\textup{UC-}\lim_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{v_{n}}=\lim_{N-M\to\infty}{\frac{1}{N-M}\sum_{n=M}^{N-1}{v_{n}}}

One of the main tools for handling uniform Cesàro limits is the following version of the van der Corput differencing lemma:

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
ABB21
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group,
and let
(u_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
be a bounded sequence in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
If

\xi_{h}:=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\left\langle u_{g+h},u_{g}\right\rangle}

exists for every
h\in\Gamma
and
\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\xi_{h}}=0
,
then
\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{u_{g}}=0
.

2.2.
Host–Kra factors

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group,
and let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system.
A
factor
of
\mathbf{X}
is a
(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
-invariant sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
We may also refer to the system
\mathbf{Y}=\left(X,\mathcal{Y},\mu|_{\mathcal{Y}},(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
,
or any system isomorphic to
\mathbf{Y}
, as a factor of
\mathbf{X}
.

The most important family of factors for our consideration is the family of
Host–Kra factors
.
These factors are defined in terms of a family of seminorms, known as the
Host–Kra seminorms

[
HK05
]
,
which are an ergodic-theoretic counterpart to the Gowers uniformity norms
[
Gow01
]
in additive combinatorics.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system.
For
g\in\Gamma
and
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, define
\Delta_{g}f:=\overline{f}\cdot T_{g}f
.
Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in\Gamma
, we define
\Delta_{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}}
inductively by

\Delta_{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}}f:=\Delta_{g_{k}}\left(\Delta_{g_{1},\dots,g_{k-1}}f\right)
.
For
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
Host–Kra seminorm of order
k
by

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}^{2^{k}}=\textup{UC-}\lim_{(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})\in\Gamma^{k}}{\int_{X}{\Delta_{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}}f~d\mu}}.

It is shown that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k}}
is indeed a seminorm for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
in
[
BTZ10
, Appendix A]
.
The corresponding Host–Kra factors are guaranteed by the following proposition:

Proposition 2.2

(
[
BTZ10
]
, Proposition 1.10)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group, let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

be a measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system, and let
k\geq 0
.
There exists a factor
\mathcal{Z}^{k}
with the property that for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, one has

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{U^{k+1}}=0\iff\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{Z}^{k}\right]=0.

If
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is an ergodic system,
then the first several Host–Kra factors are as follows:

•

\mathcal{Z}^{0}
is the trivial factor consisting of null and co-null subsets of
X
.
(If
\mathbf{X}
is not ergodic, then
\mathcal{Z}^{0}=\mathcal{I}
, the
\sigma
-algebra of all
(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
-invariant sets.)

•

\mathcal{Z}^{1}
is the
Kronecker factor
.
This is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra with respect to which all eigenfunctions are measurable.
As a measure-preserving system,
\mathbf{Z}^{1}
is isomorphic to a rotational system.
That is, there exists a compact abelian group
Z
and a homomorphism
\alpha:\Gamma\to Z
with dense image
such that
\mathbf{Z}^{1}
is isomorphic to the system
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
, where
Z
is equipped with the Haar measure
and
g\in\Gamma
acts by the rotation
z\mapsto z+\alpha_{g}
.
Because of its relationship to the Kronecker factor, we refer to any ergodic rotational system
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)

as an
ergodic Kronecker system
.
Such systems are uniquely determined (up to isomorphism) by their
discrete spectrum

(i.e., the group of eigenvalues),
which is given by

\Lambda=\left\{\lambda\circ\alpha:\lambda\in\widehat{Z}\right\}.

Moreover, the topological system underlying any ergodic Kronecker system is uniquely ergodic.
See
[
ABB21
, Section 2.4]
for a more in-depth discussion of Kronecker systems
in the context of actions of countable discrete abelian groups.

•

\mathcal{Z}^{2}
is the
quasi-affine
(or
Conze–Lesigne
) factor.
As a measure-preserving system,
\mathbf{Z}^{2}
is isomorphic to a group extension of the Kronecker factor,

\mathbf{Z}^{2}\cong\mathbf{Z}^{1}\times_{\sigma}H
, where the cocycle
\sigma
satisfies a certain functional equation
known as the
Conze–Lesigne equation
; see Definition
2.11
below.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group, and let
k\geq 0
.
An ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

is a
system of order
k
if
\mathcal{Z}^{k}=\mathcal{X}
.

Systems of order
k
have the following properties:

Proposition 2.4

(cf.
[
HK05
]
, Section 4.6)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group,
k\geq 0
,
and
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
an ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system.

(1)

The Host–Kra factor
\mathbf{Z}^{k}
is an order
k
system.

(2)

If
\mathbf{X}
is an order
k
system and
\mathbf{Y}
is a factor of
\mathbf{X}
, then
\mathbf{Y}
is again a system of order
k
.

(3)

If
\mathbf{Y}
is a system of order
k
and a factor of
\mathbf{X}
, then
\mathbf{Y}
is a factor of the Host–Kra factor
\mathbf{Z}^{k}
.

2.3.
Relatively independent joinings

The proof of Theorem
1.16
requires that the Host–Kra factors have certain convenient properties
that they may not have in general systems.
In order to produce these desirable properties, we will work with an extension of the original system.
The key construction to that end is the relatively independent joining of systems with respect to a common factor.
Let
\mathbf{X}_{1}=\left(X_{1},\mathcal{X}_{1},\mu_{1},\left(T_{1,g}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

and
\mathbf{X}_{2}=\left(X_{2},\mathcal{X}_{2},\mu_{2},\left(T_{2,g}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

be measure-preserving
\Gamma
-systems.
Suppose
\mathbf{Y}=\left(Y,\mathcal{Y},\nu,(S_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is another measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
that arises as a factor of both of the systems
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
,
say with factor maps
\pi_{1}:X_{1}\to Y
and
\pi_{2}:X_{2}\to Y
.
The
relatively indpendent joining
(or
fiber product
) of
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
with respect to
\mathbf{Y}
is the system

\mathbf{X}_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}\mathbf{X}_{2}=\left(X_{1}\times X_{2},\mathcal{X}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{X}_{2},\mu_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}\mu_{2},\left(T_{1,g}\times T_{2,g}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right),

where the measure
\mu_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}\mu_{2}
is defined by the equation

\int_{X_{1}\times X_{2}}{(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})~d(\mu_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}\mu_{2})}=\int_{Y}{\mathbb{E}\left[f_{1}\mid\mathcal{Y}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f_{2}\mid\mathcal{Y}\right]~d\nu}.

Note that the measure
\mu_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}\mu_{2}
is supported on the set

X_{1}\times_{\mathbf{Y}}X_{2}=\left\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in X_{1}\times X_{2}:\pi_{1}(x_{1})=\pi_{2}(x_{2})\right\}.

The relatively independent joining construction allows us to take an extension of a Host–Kra factor of a given system
and turn it into an extension of the full system:

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system.
Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Suppose
\mathbf{Z}^{k}
is the Host–Kra factor of
\mathbf{X}
of order
k
.
Given any ergodic order
k
extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}
of
\mathbf{Z}^{k}
,
there exists an ergodic extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of
\mathbf{X}
such that

\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}
is the Host–Kra factor of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of order
k
.

Proof.

Define
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
as the relatively independent joining
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}=\mathbf{X}\times_{\mathbf{Z}^{k}}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}
.
First,
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}
is an order
k
factor of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
, so it is a factor of the Host–Kra factor of order
k

by Proposition
2.4
(3).

Conversely, we want to show that the Host–Kra factor of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of order
k
is a factor of
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}^{k}
.
Let
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\widetilde{Z}^{k})
.
Suppose
f\in L^{2}(\widetilde{X})
is measurable with respect to the Host–Kra factor of order
k
.
Expand
f
in the basis
(u_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
:

f(x,y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{a_{i}(x)u_{i}(y)}.

Fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Since
\mathbbm{1}_{X}\otimes u_{i}
is
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}
-measurable and hence measurable
with respect to the Host–Kra factor of order
k
, the product
(\mathbbm{1}_{X}\otimes\overline{u_{i}})f

remains measurable with respect to the Host–Kra factor of order
k
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{E}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{X}\otimes\overline{u_{i}})f\mid\mathcal{X}\right]
is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{Z}^{k}

by items (2) and (3) in Proposition
2.4
.
By direct computation, since
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\widetilde{Z}^{k})
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[(\mathbbm{1}_{X}\otimes\overline{u_{i}})f\mid\mathcal{X}\right]=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}{\mathbb{E}\left[a_{j}\otimes\overline{u_{i}}u_{j}\mid\mathcal{X}\right]}=a_{i}.

Thus,
a_{i}
is
\mathcal{Z}^{k}
-measurable for each
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
That is,
a_{i}(x)=b_{i}(\pi_{1}(x))
for some function
b_{i}:Z^{k}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\pi_{1}:X\to Z^{k}
is the factor map.
But, letting
\pi_{2}:\widetilde{Z}^{k}\to Z^{k}
be the other factor map, one has
\pi_{1}(x)=\pi_{2}(y)
for a.e.
(x,y)\in\widetilde{X}
.
Hence,

f(x,y)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{a_{i}(x)u_{i}(y)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{b_{i}(\pi_{1}(x))u_{i}(y)}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}{b_{i}(\pi_{2}(y))u_{i}(y)}

is
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}^{k}
-measurable.
∎

2.4.
Hilbert space-valued functions and unique ergodicity

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Given a compact metric space
X
, a probability measure
\mu
on
X
, and a continuous function
F:X\to\mathcal{H}
,
one can define the integral
\int_{X}{F~d\mu}
to be the element of
\mathcal{H}
satisfying

\left\langle\int_{X}{F~d\mu},v\right\rangle=\int_{X}{\left\langle F(x),v\right\rangle~d\mu(x)}

for every
v\in\mathcal{H}
.

A characterizing property of uniquely ergodic systems is the following:
a topological system
\left(X,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is uniquely ergodic (with unique invariant measure
\mu
)
if and only if for any continuous function
F:X\to\mathbb{C}
and any
x_{0}\in X
,

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(T_{g}x_{0})}=\int_{X}{F~d\mu}.

The following lemma shows that the same result holds for Hilbert space-valued functions:

Lemma 2.6

(cf.
[
HK01
]
, Lemma 4.3)
.

Let
\left(X,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be a topological
\Gamma
-system.
Let
\mathcal{H}
be a nontrivial Hilbert space.
Then
\left(X,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is uniquely ergodic (with unique invariant measure
\mu
) if and only if for any continuous function
F:X\to\mathcal{H}
and any
x_{0}\in X
,

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(T_{g}x_{0})}=\int_{X}{F~d\mu}

in
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Suppose that for any continuous function
F:X\to\mathcal{H}
and any
x_{0}\in X
,

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(T_{g}x_{0})}=\int_{X}{F~d\mu}

in
\mathcal{H}
.
Since
\mathcal{H}
is nontrivial, it contains a copy of
\mathbb{C}
, so this implies if
F:X\to\mathbb{C}
is continuous and
x_{0}\in X
, then

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(T_{g}x_{0})}=\int_{X}{F~d\mu}

in
\mathbb{C}
.
Therefore,
(X,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma})
is uniquely ergodic.

Now suppose
\left(X,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is uniquely ergodic with unique invariant measure
\mu
.
Replacing
F
by
F-\int_{X}{F~d\mu}
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\int_{X}{F~d\mu}=0
in
\mathcal{H}
.
Put
u_{g}=F(T_{g}x_{0})\in\mathcal{H}
.
For any
h\in\Gamma
, the function
\varphi_{h}:X\to\mathbb{C}

defined by
\varphi_{h}(x)=\left\langle F(T_{h}x),F(x)\right\rangle
is continuous.
Hence, by unique ergodicity of
(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
,

\xi_{h}:=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\left\langle u_{g+h},u_{g}\right\rangle}=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\varphi_{h}(T_{g}x_{0})}=\int_{X}{\varphi_{h}~d\mu}.

For
x\in X
, consider the function
\psi_{x}(y)=\left\langle F(y),F(x)\right\rangle
.
This is a continuous function from
X
to
\mathbb{C}
, so by unique ergodicity of
(T_{h})_{h\in\Gamma}
, we have

\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\psi_{x}(T_{h}y)}=\int_{X}{\psi_{x}~d\mu}=\left\langle\int_{X}{F~d\mu},F(x)\right\rangle=0

for every
y\in X
.
In particular, we may take
y=x
, in which case

\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\varphi_{h}(x)}=\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\left\langle F(T_{h}x),F(x)\right\rangle}=\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\psi_{x}(T_{h}x)}=0.

Integrating over
x\in X
and applying the dominated convergence theorem, it follows that

\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\xi_{h}}=0.

Thus, by Lemma
2.1
,
\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(T_{g}x_{0})}=0
in
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

2.5.
Cocycles and coboundaries

An important construction in ergodic theory is that of group extensions.
For our purposes, we will need only to consider extensions by abelian groups, which creates various simplifications.
Suppose
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is an ergodic
\Gamma
-system.
Given a compact abelian group
(H,+)
and a measurable function
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to H
,
we can define the
group extension of
\mathbf{X}
by
H
over
\sigma
as the system

\mathbf{X}\times_{\sigma}H:=\left(X\times H,\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{B}_{H},\mu\times m_{H},\left(T^{\sigma}_{g}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right),

where
T^{\sigma}_{g}(x,y):=(T_{g}x,y+\sigma_{g}(x))
.
In order to obtain a
\Gamma
-action in this manner, the function
\sigma
must satisfy the
cocycle
equation

\sigma_{g+h}(x)=\sigma_{g}(T_{h}x)+\sigma_{h}(x)

for every
g,h\in\Gamma
and
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.

Given any measurable function
F:X\to H
, one may construct a cocycle

\Delta_{g}F(x):=F(T_{g}x)-F(x).

Such a cocycle is called a
coboundary
.

Two cocycles
\sigma
and
\tau
are
cohomologous
, denoted
\sigma\sim\tau
,
if their difference
\tau-\sigma
is a coboundary.
The following result is an easy exercise:

Proposition 2.7

(cf.
[
Gla03
]
, Lemma 3.20)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be a
\Gamma
-system.
Suppose
H
is a compact abelian group and
\sigma,\tau:\Gamma\times X\to H

are cocycles such that
\sigma\sim\tau
.
Then
\mathbf{X}\times_{\sigma}H\cong\mathbf{X}\times_{\tau}H
.

When analyzing a cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to H
taking values in a compact abelian group
H
,
it is often useful to consider the family of cocycles
\chi\circ\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to S^{1}

given by composition with characters
\chi\in\widehat{H}
.

The following lemma gives a criterion for checking that a cocycle taking values in
S^{1}
is a coboundary,
when the base system is an action by rotations on a compact abelian group.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
ABB21
]
, Proposition 7.12)
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be a Kronecker system and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
a cocycle.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

\sigma
is a coboundary;

(ii)

for any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
such that
\alpha_{g_{n}}\to 0
in
Z
,
one has
\sigma_{g_{n}}\to 1
in
L^{2}(Z)
.

Remark 2.9
.

We do not assume that the Kronecker system
(Z,\alpha)
appearing in Lemma
2.8
is ergodic.
This will be important for some later applications, e.g., Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be a Kronecker system, and suppose
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
is a coboundary.
Then there is a function
\omega:Z\times Z\to S^{1}
such that

t\mapsto\omega(t,\cdot)
is a continuous map from
Z
to
L^{2}(Z)
and

\omega(\alpha_{g},z)=\sigma_{g}(z).

If
\mathbf{Z}
is ergodic, then
\omega
is defined uniquely almost everywhere.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of
[
ABB21
, Proposition 7.12]
.
We give a different proof here.
Since
\sigma
is a coboundary, we may write
\sigma=\Delta F
for some
F:Z\to S^{1}
.
That is,

\sigma_{g}(z)=\frac{F(z+\alpha_{g})}{F(z)}.

We may then take
\omega(t,z)=\frac{F(z+t)}{F(z)}
.
∎

Two additional families of cocycles will play an important role in this paper.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group, and let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be an ergodic Kronecker system.
A cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
is

(1)

cohomologous to a character
if there exists
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}

such that
\sigma_{g}(z)\sim\gamma(g)
.
That is, there exists a measurable function
F:Z\to S^{1}
such that

\sigma_{g}(z)=\gamma(g)\frac{F(z+\alpha_{g})}{F(z)}

for every
g\in\Gamma
and a.e.
z\in Z
.

(2)

quasi-affine
(or a
Conze–Lesigne
cocycle) if for every
t\in Z
, the cocycle

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}

is cohomologous to a character.

Remark 2.12
.

According to the definition above, a cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
is quasi-affine if and only if for each
t\in Z
, there exists a measurable function
F_{t}:Z\to S^{1}
and a character
\gamma_{t}\in\widehat{\Gamma}
such that

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}=\gamma_{t}(g)\frac{F_{t}(z+\alpha_{g})}{F_{t}(z)}

for every
g\in\Gamma
and a.e.
z\in Z
.
One may additionally ensure that the maps
(t,z)\mapsto F_{t}(z)
from
Z\times Z
to
S^{1}
and
t\mapsto\gamma_{t}
from
Z
to
\widehat{\Gamma}
are Borel measurable by
[
Les93
, Proposition 2]
and
[
FW96
, Proposition 10.5]
.

Suppose
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
is an ergodic Kronecker system,
H
is a compact abelian group,
and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to H
is a cocycle.
If
\chi\circ\sigma
is quasi-affine for every
\chi\in\widehat{H}
, then the group extension

\mathbf{X}\times_{\sigma}H
is called a
quasi-affine
or
Conze–Lesigne
system.

We now give characterizations of cocycles that are cohomologous to a character or quasi-affine
in the same vein as Lemma
2.8
:

Lemma 2.13

(
[
ABB21
]
, Proposition 7.13)
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be an ergodic Kronecker system and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
a cocycle.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

\sigma
is cohomologous to a character;

(ii)

for every
t\in Z
,

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}

is a coboundary;

(iii)

there is a Borel set
A\subseteq Z
with
m_{Z}(A)>0
such that

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}

is a coboundary for every
t\in A
;

(iv)

for any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
with
\alpha_{g_{n}}\to 0
in
Z
,
there is a sequence
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S^{1}
such that
c_{n}\sigma_{g_{n}}(z)\to 1
in
L^{2}(Z)
.

Lemma 2.14

(
[
ABB21
]
, Proposition 7.15)
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be an ergodic Kronecker system and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to S^{1}
a cocycle.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

\sigma
is quasi-affine;

(ii)

there is a Borel set
A\subseteq Z
with
m_{Z}(A)>0
such that

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}

is cohomologous to a character for every
t\in A
;

(iii)

for any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
with
\alpha_{g_{n}}\to 0
in
Z
,
there are sequences
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S^{1}
and
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\widehat{Z}
such that

c_{n}\lambda_{n}(z)\sigma_{g_{n}}(z)\to 1
in
L^{2}(Z)
.

2.6.
Mackey groups

Mackey groups play an essential role in this paper.
We review the definition and the basic properties of the Mackey group in this section.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system,

H
a compact abelian group, and
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to H
a cocycle.
The
range
of
\sigma
is the closed subgroup
G_{\sigma}
generated by
\{\sigma_{g}(x):g\in\Gamma,x\in X\}
.
The cocycle
\sigma
is
minimal
if there is no cohomologous cocycle
\tau
with
G_{\tau}\subsetneq G_{\sigma}
.

Proposition 2.15

(cf.
[
Gla03
]
, Theorem 3.25)
.

Any cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to H
is cohomologous to a minimal cocycle.
Moreover, if two minimal cocycles are cohomologous, then they have the same range.

The
Mackey group associated to
\sigma
is defined to be the range of a minimal cocycle cohomologous to
\sigma
.
Several important properties of Mackey groups are collected in the following proposition:

Proposition 2.16
.

(1)

If
M
is the Mackey group associated to
\sigma
, then the annihilator of
M
is

M^{\perp}=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\chi\circ\sigma~\text{is a coboundary}\right\}.

(2)

If
\sigma
and
\tau
are cohomologous, then their Mackey groups are equal.

(3)

The system
\mathbf{X}\times_{\sigma}H
is ergodic if and only if
\sigma
is minimal with range
G_{\sigma}=H
.

(4)

If
f:X\times H\to\mathbb{C}
is
(T^{\sigma}_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
-invariant,
then
f(x,y+m)=f(x,y)
for every
m\in M
and
(\mu\times m_{H})
-a.e.
(x,y)\in X\times H
.

Proof.

For property (1), see
[
HK01
, Proposition 2.5]
.
Properties (2) and (3) are proved in
[
Gla03
, Theorem 3.25]
.

In the case
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
, property (4) appears in
[
HK01
, Proposition 2.4]
(see also
[
FW96
, Proposition 7.1]
).
We give a quick proof for general
\Gamma
.
Suppose
f:X\times H\to\mathbb{C}
is
\left(T^{\sigma}_{g}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}
-invariant.
We may expand
f
as a Fourier series:

f(x,y)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{c_{\chi}(x)\chi(y)}.

Then

\left(T^{\sigma}_{g}f\right)(x,y)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{c_{\chi}(T_{g}x)\chi(y+\sigma_{g}(x))}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{H}}{c_{\chi}(T_{g}x)\chi(\sigma_{g}(x))\chi(y)}.

The assumption that
T^{\sigma}_{g}f=f
then implies

(2.1)

c_{\chi}(x)=c_{\chi}(T_{g}x)\chi(\sigma_{g}(x))

for every
\chi\in\widehat{H}
.
Hence, if
c_{\chi}\neq 0
, then (
2.1
) expresses
\chi\circ\sigma
as a coboundary.
Thus,
c_{\chi}=0
for
\chi\notin M^{\perp}
.
For any
m\in M
, we therefore have

f(x,y+m)=\sum_{\chi\in M^{\perp}}{c_{\chi}(x)\chi(y+m)}=\sum_{\chi\in M^{\perp}}{c_{\chi}(x)\chi(y)}=f(x,y)

as claimed.
∎

For a compact abelian group
K
, let
\mathcal{M}(Z,K)
be the space of measurable functions
Z\to K

with the topology of convergence in measure.
One can show that a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}(Z,K)
converges
if and only if
\left(\chi\circ f_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
L^{2}(Z)
for every
\chi\in\widehat{K}
;
see, e.g.,
[
ABB21
, Lemma 7.28]
.
The following is an easy consequence of Corollary
2.10

combined with the description of the Mackey group in item (1) of Proposition
2.16
:

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be an ergodic Kronecker system,
H
a compact abelian group,
and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to H
a cocycle with Mackey group
M\subseteq H
.
Then there is a function
\omega:Z\times Z\to H/M
such that
t\mapsto\omega(t,\cdot)
is a continuous
map from
Z
to
\mathcal{M}(Z,H/M)
and

\omega(\alpha_{g},z)\equiv\sigma_{g}(z)\pmod{M}.

Remark 2.18
.

If the cocycle
\sigma
is minimal, then it takes values in the Mackey group
M
, in which case
\omega=0
.

The Mackey group plays an important role in the analysis of ergodic averages, as demonstrated by the following result:

Proposition 2.19

(cf.
[
ABB21
]
, Proposition 7.7)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system,

H
a compact abelian group, and
\sigma:\Gamma\times X\to H
a cocycle.
Let
M
be the Mackey group associated to
\sigma
.
Let
f\in L^{2}(\mu\times m_{H})
.
If for every
\chi\in M^{\perp}
and
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
, one has

\int_{H}{f(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}=0,

then

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{T^{\sigma}_{g}f}=0

in
L^{2}(\mu\times m_{H})
.

Proof.

By the mean ergodic theorem, let
\widetilde{f}=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{T^{\sigma}_{g}f}
.
We want to show
\widetilde{f}=0
.
Since
L^{2}(H,m_{H})
is spanned by characters, it suffices to show

\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}=0

for every
\chi\in\widehat{H}
and
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.
We will prove this identity in cases, depending on whether or not
\chi
annihilates the Mackey group
M
.

Case 1
:
\chi\notin M^{\perp}
.

By item (4) of Proposition
2.16
,
for any
m\in M
and
(\mu\times m_{H})
-a.e.
(x,y)\in X\times H
, we have
\widetilde{f}(x,y+m)=\widetilde{f}(x,y)
.
Therefore, for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}=\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y+m)\chi(y+m)~dy}=\chi(m)\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}.

Taking
m\in M
such that
\chi(m)\neq 1
, this implies
\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}=0
as claimed.

Case 2
:
\chi\in M^{\perp}
.

Note that

\int_{H}{\widetilde{f}(x,y)\chi(y)~dy}=\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{f}\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\mid\mathcal{X}\right](x).

By item (1) of Proposition
2.16
, there is a measurable function
F:X\to S^{1}
such that

\chi(\sigma_{g}(x))=F(T_{g}x)\overline{F}(x)

for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.
We then compute directly:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\widetilde{f}\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\mid\mathcal{X}\right]

\displaystyle=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(T^{\sigma}_{g}f\right)\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\mid\mathcal{X}\right]}

\displaystyle=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\left(\overline{\chi}\circ\sigma_{g}\right)\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[T^{\sigma}_{g}\left(f\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\right)\mid\mathcal{X}\right]}

\displaystyle=F\cdot\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{T_{g}\left(\overline{F}\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\mid\mathcal{X}\right]\right)}

\displaystyle=0.

In the last step, we have used the hypothesis
\mathbb{E}\left[f\cdot(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\chi)\mid\mathcal{X}\right]=0
.
∎

Corollary 2.20
.

Let
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
be an ergodic Kronecker system,

H
a compact abelian group, and
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to H
a cocycle.
Let
M
be the Mackey group associated to
\sigma
, and let
\omega:Z\times Z\to H

be a measurable map such that
t\mapsto\omega(t,\cdot)+M
is a continuous map
Z\to\mathcal{M}(Z,H/M)

and
\omega(\alpha_{g},z)\equiv\sigma_{g}(z)\pmod{M}
.
Then for any
f\in L^{2}(Z\times H)
,

(2.2)

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{f(z+\alpha_{g},x+\sigma_{g}(z))}=\int_{Z\times M}{f(z+t,x+m+\omega(t,z))~dt~dm}

in
L^{2}(Z\times H)
.

Remark 2.21
.

Proposition
2.17
provides a function
\widetilde{\omega}:Z\times Z\to H/M

corresponding to the cocycle
\sigma:\Gamma\times Z\to H
.
This can be lifted to a measurable function
\omega:Z\times Z\to H

satisfying the conditions in the statement of Corollary
2.20

by the Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardewski measurable selection theorem (see
[
Sri98
, Section 5.2]
).

Proof of Corollary
2.20
.

By linearity, we may assume
f
is of the form
f=h\otimes\chi
with
\chi\in\widehat{H}
.
Then the right hand side of (
2.2
) is equal to

(2.3)

\chi(x)\left(\int_{Z}{h(z+t)\chi(\omega(t,z))~dt}\right)\left(\int_{M}{\chi(m)~dm}\right).

If
\chi\notin M^{\perp}
, then
\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{T^{\sigma}_{g}f}=0
by Proposition
2.19
.
Moreover, (
2.3
) is clearly equal to zero.

Suppose
\chi\in M^{\perp}
.
Then (
2.3
) reduces to

\chi(x)\left(\int_{Z}{h(z+t)\chi(\omega(t,z))~dt}\right).

The left hand side of (
2.2
) is equal to

\chi(x)\cdot\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{h(z+\alpha_{g})\chi(\sigma_{g}(z))}.

Define
F:Z\times Z\to S^{1}
by
F(t,z):=h(z+t)\chi(\omega(t,z))
.
Then by Proposition
2.17
,

t\mapsto F(t,\cdot)
is a continuous map from
Z
to
L^{2}(Z)

and
F(\alpha_{g},z)=h(z+\alpha_{g})\chi(\sigma_{g}(z))
.
Since
(Z,\alpha)
is uniquely ergodic, it follows by Lemma
2.6
that

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(\alpha_{g},z)}=\int_{Z}{F(t,z)~dt}

in
L^{2}(Z)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Extensions

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Phi
be a family of endomorphisms of
\Gamma
.
A group of characters
\Lambda\subseteq\widehat{\Gamma}
is

•

\Phi
-complete
if
\lambda\circ\varphi\in\Lambda

for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and every
\varphi\in\Phi
.

•

\Phi
-divisible
if for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and every
\varphi\in\Phi
,
there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda^{\prime}\circ\varphi=\lambda
.

The main result of this section is the following extension theorem:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Phi
be any countable family of endomorphisms of
\Gamma
,
and let
\Psi
be a countable family of injective endomorphisms of
\Gamma
.
Then for any ergodic
\Gamma
-system
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
,
there exists an ergodic extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of
\mathbf{X}
such that the discrete spectrum of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}

is
\Phi
-complete and
\Psi
-divisible.

We will derive Theorem
3.2
as a consequence of the following general result:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system,
and let
\Lambda
be the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{X}
.
For any countable set
C\subseteq\widehat{\Gamma}
, there is an ergodic extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of
\mathbf{X}

such that the discrete spectrum
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
is equal to the group generated by
\Lambda
and
C
.

Proof.

This is a special case
(k=1)
of Theorem
2.5
.
Indeed, if
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
is an ergodic Kronecker system with discrete spectrum

\widetilde{\Lambda}=\left\langle\Lambda,C\right\rangle
, then
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
is an order 1 extension of
\mathbf{Z}
,
and so there exists by Theorem
2.5
an ergodic extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of
\mathbf{X}

whose Kronecker factor is isomorphic to
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

(1) A similar statement to Theorem
3.3
is proved in
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.3]
.
However, in
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.3]
, only one inclusion is established
(namely, that
\widetilde{\Lambda}
contains
\Lambda
and
C
).
It is nevertheless true that the construction in the proof of
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.3]

produces a system with the correct discrete spectrum, as can be seen with some additional work.
(In fact, though differently-phrased, the construction is equivalent to taking the relatively independent joining
of
\mathbf{X}
with the appropriate ergodic Kronecker system
\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
).

(2) The extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
produced via Theorem
3.3
is not unique.
For instance, any weakly mixing extension of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
will again satisfy the conclusion of the theorem.
The minimal such extension (i.e., the one appearing as a factor of any such extension)
is precisely the relatively independent joining
\mathbf{X}\times_{\mathbf{Z}}\widetilde{\mathbf{Z}}
.

Now we can prove Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{X}
.
We extend
\Lambda
in stages, alternating between
\Phi
-completeness and
\Psi
-divisibility.

First, we make a couple of convenient reductions to make the notation less cumbersome.
Replacing
\Phi
by
\Phi\cup\Psi
, we may assume that
\Psi\subseteq\Phi
.
Let
\widetilde{\Phi}
be the semigroup

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}

\displaystyle=\left\{\varphi_{1}\circ\dots\circ\varphi_{k}:k\geq 0,\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\in\Phi\right\},

and let
\widetilde{\Psi}
be the semigroup

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Psi}

\displaystyle=\left\{\psi_{1}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k}:k\geq 0,\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{k}\in\Psi\right\}.

Since
\Phi
and
\Psi
are countable,
\widetilde{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
are also countable.
Moreover, for
\widetilde{\psi}=\psi_{1}\circ\dots\circ\psi_{k}\in\widetilde{\Psi}
, we have that
\widetilde{\psi}
is injective,
since it is a composition of injective maps.
Thus, replacing
\Phi
and
\Psi
with
\widetilde{\Phi}
and
\widetilde{\Psi}
,
we may assume without loss of generality that
\Phi
and
\Psi
contain the identity map
and are closed under composition.

Now we set up the induction process.
Let
\Lambda_{0}:=\Lambda
.
Suppose we have defined
\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\Lambda_{2j}
for some
j\geq 0
.
Let

\Lambda_{2j+1}:=\left\langle\lambda\circ\varphi:\lambda\in\Lambda_{2j},\varphi\in\Phi\right\rangle.

By the induction hypothesis,
\Lambda_{2j}
is a countable group, so
\Lambda_{2j+1}
is also a countable group.
Moreover, since
\Phi
is a semigroup,
\Lambda_{2j+1}
is
\Phi
-complete.

We now perform subinduction to define
\Lambda_{2j+2}
.
Put
S_{0}:=\Lambda_{2j+1}
.
Suppose we have defined
S_{k}
for some
k\geq 0
.
For
\lambda\in S_{k}
and
\psi\in\Psi
, there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}

such that
\lambda^{\prime}\circ\psi=\lambda
.
To see this, first define
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}:\psi(\Gamma)\to S^{1}
by
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}(\psi(g))=\lambda(g)
.
This is well-defined since
\psi
is injective.
Then by
[
Rud90
, Theorem 2.1.4]
,
\lambda^{\prime}_{0}
extends to a character
\lambda^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}
,
and we have
\lambda^{\prime}\circ\psi=\lambda
.
Define a choice function
\gamma_{k}:S_{k}\times\Psi\to\widehat{\Gamma}

so that
\gamma_{k}(\lambda,\psi)\circ\psi=\lambda
for every
\lambda\in S_{k}
and
\psi\in\Psi
.
Then let

S_{k+1}:=\left\{\gamma_{k}(\lambda,\psi):\lambda\in S_{k},\psi\in\Psi\right\}.

By induction, the set
S_{k+1}
is countable.
Therefore,
S=\bigcup_{k\geq 0}{S_{k}}
is countable and hence generates a countable group

\Lambda_{2j+2}:=\left\langle S\right\rangle
.

We claim that
\Lambda_{2j+2}
is
\Psi
-divisible.
Let
\lambda\in\Lambda_{2j+2}
, and let
\psi\in\Psi
.
We may write
\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\gamma_{k_{i}}^{\varepsilon_{i}}(\lambda_{i},\psi_{i})}

with
\lambda_{i}\in S_{k_{i}}
,
\psi_{i}\in\Psi
, and
\varepsilon_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
.
Let
\lambda^{\prime}_{i}=\gamma_{k_{i}+1}\left(\gamma_{k_{i}}(\lambda_{i},\psi_{i}),\psi\right)\in S
,
and let
\lambda^{\prime}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{(\lambda^{\prime}_{i})^{\varepsilon_{i}}}\in\Lambda_{2j+2}
.
Then

\lambda^{\prime}\circ\psi=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{(\lambda^{\prime}_{i}\circ\psi)^{\varepsilon_{i}}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}{\left(\gamma_{k_{i}}(\lambda_{i},\psi_{i})\right)^{\varepsilon_{i}}}=\lambda.

Thus,
\Lambda_{2j+2}
is
\Psi
-divisible as claimed.

By induction, we have constructed an infinite sequence of countable groups

\Lambda_{0}\subseteq\Lambda_{1}\subseteq\Lambda_{2}\subseteq\dots
such that

\Lambda_{2j+1}
is
\Phi
-complete and
\Lambda_{2j+2}
is
\Psi
-divisible for
j\geq 0
.
Let
\Lambda_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{\infty}{\Lambda_{j}}
.
Then
\Lambda_{\infty}
is a countable group that is
\Phi
-complete and
\Psi
-divisible.

Finally, we apply Theorem
3.3
to obtain an ergodic extension
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
of
\mathbf{X}

such that the discrete spectrum
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of
\widetilde{\mathbf{X}}
is equal to
\Lambda_{\infty}
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The subinduction and use of the choice functions
\gamma_{k}
in the construction of the group
\Lambda_{2j+2}

is solely used to ensure that
\Lambda_{2j+2}
is countable.
A
\Psi
-divisible group can be defined more directly, namely

D_{2j+2}:=\left\langle\lambda\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\lambda\circ\psi\in\Lambda_{2j+1}~\text{for some}~\psi\in\Psi\right\rangle.

In general,
D_{2j+2}
is uncountable.
However, if
\psi(\Gamma)
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
for each
\psi\in\Psi
,
then the set
\{\lambda\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\lambda\circ\psi=\lambda_{0}\}

has cardinality
[\Gamma:\psi(\Gamma)]<\infty
for each
\lambda_{0}\in\Lambda_{2j+1}
and
\psi\in\Psi
.
Thus, in this case,
D_{2j+2}
is countable, so one may take
\Lambda_{2j+2}=D_{2j+2}

rather than using the more complicated construction
appearing in the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
For the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, an endomorphism is
injective if and only if it has finite index image if and only if the corresponding matrix is nonsingular.
Therefore, the simpler construction
\Lambda_{2j+2}=D_{2j+2}

can always be used when dealing with the group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

4.
Mackey group associated with
\{\varphi,\psi\}

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
has finite index in
\Gamma
,
and suppose
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
are such that
\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi
is injective.

Let
\mathbf{X}
be an ergodic quasi-affine
\Gamma
-system, and write
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
.
Assume that the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{X}
is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete
and
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible.
In this section, we consider a variant of the notion of Mackey groups as discussed in Section
2.6
that is tailored to analyzing ergodic averages of the form

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}T_{\varphi(g)}f_{1}\cdot T_{\psi(g)}f_{2}

for
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Z\times H)
.

Since the discrete spectrum
\Lambda
of
\mathbf{X}
is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete,
we have induced continuous endomorphisms of
Z=\widehat{\Lambda}
,
which we denote by
\widehat{\varphi}
,
\widehat{\psi}
,
\widehat{\theta}_{1}
, and
\widehat{\theta}_{2}
.
To see this, view
Z
as the dual group, expressed additively as the group of homomorphisms
z:\Lambda\to\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.
For
f\in\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
, the map
\widehat{f}:Z\to Z
is then given by

\widehat{f}(z):\lambda\mapsto z\left(\lambda\circ f\right).

Since
\Lambda
is
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible,
we claim that
\widehat{\theta}_{1}\circ\widehat{\varphi}+\widehat{\theta}_{2}\circ\widehat{\psi}
is injective.
Indeed, suppose
(\widehat{\theta}_{1}\circ\widehat{\varphi}+\widehat{\theta}_{2}\circ\widehat{\psi})(z)=0
.
Then for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

z\left(\lambda\circ(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)\right)=0.

But for any
\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists
\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda

with
\lambda^{\prime}\circ(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)=\lambda
, so

z(\lambda)=z\left(\lambda^{\prime}\circ(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)\right)=0.

That is,
z=0
.

Let

W=\left\{(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),z+\widehat{\psi}(t)):z,t\in Z\right\},

and let
\widetilde{\alpha}:\Gamma\to W
be the homomorphism

\widetilde{\alpha}_{g}=\left(\alpha_{\varphi(g)},\alpha_{\psi(g)}\right).

Let
\widetilde{\sigma}:\Gamma\times W\to H^{2}
be the cocycle

\widetilde{\sigma}_{g}(w)=\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}),\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right).

Now we define the Mackey group to be the closed subgroup
M\leq H^{2}
with annihilator

M^{\perp}=\left\{\widetilde{\chi}\in\widehat{H^{2}}:\widetilde{\chi}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}~\text{is a coboundary over}~(W,\widetilde{\alpha})\right\}.

The Kronecker system
\mathbf{W}=(W,\widetilde{\alpha})
is not necessarily ergodic.
However, its ergodic decomposition is easy to describe and interacts well with the Mackey group
M
.
Namely, we may express
W
as the union of the subsets

W_{z}=\left\{(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),z+\widehat{\psi}(t)):t\in Z\right\}=(z,z)+W_{0},

each supporting a Haar measure
m_{z}
.
The system
(W_{0},\widetilde{\alpha})
is uniquely ergodic, and each of the systems
(W_{z},\widetilde{\alpha})
for
z\in Z

is an isomorphic copy.
It is easily verified that the Haar measure on
W
decomposes as
m_{W}=\int_{Z}{m_{z}~dz}
.
An important property of this ergodic decomposition is that,
letting
M_{z}
denote the Mackey group corresponding to the ergodic component
(W_{z},\widetilde{\alpha})
,
one has
M_{z}=M
for a.e.
z\in Z
(see
[
ABB21
, Proposition 7.9]
).

We now seek to describe the structure of the Mackey group
M
.
A classical fact in ergodic theory is that, given two measure-preserving systems
\mathbf{X}_{1}
and
\mathbf{X}_{2}
,
invariant functions for the product system
\mathbf{X}_{1}\times\mathbf{X}_{2}
are formed from functions of the form
f_{1}\otimes f_{2}
,
where
f_{1}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathbf{X}_{1}
,
f_{2}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathbf{X}_{2}
, and the corresponding eigenvalues
are conjugates of one another.
The following result describes the Mackey group
M
in an analogous manner:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
M
be the Mackey group as defined above.
Then

M^{\perp}=\left\{\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in\widehat{H^{2}}:\exists\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma},\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\sim\gamma(g)~\text{and}~\chi_{2}(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2}))\sim\overline{\gamma}(g)~\text{over}~(W,\widetilde{\alpha})\right\}.

Remark 4.2
.

Throughout this section, we treat
g\in\Gamma
and
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in W
as variables and write expressions of the form
\rho(g,w_{1},w_{2})\sim\tau(g,w_{1},w_{2})
as shorthand for the statement that the cocycles
(g,(w_{1},w_{2}))\mapsto\rho(g,w_{1},w_{2})
and
(g,(w_{1},w_{2}))\mapsto\tau(g,w_{1},w_{2})
are cohomologous.
For example, the notation
\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\sim\gamma(g)
means that there is a measurable function
F:W\to S^{1}
such that

\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))=\gamma(g)\frac{F\left(w_{1}+\alpha_{\varphi(g)},w_{2}+\alpha_{\psi(g)}\right)}{F(w_{1},w_{2})}

for every
g\in\Gamma
and almost every
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in W
.

Proof.

Suppose
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\in\widehat{H}
and
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
such that

\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\sim\gamma(g)
and
\chi_{2}(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2}))\sim\overline{\gamma}(g)
.
Then

\left(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\right)(\widetilde{\sigma}_{g}(w))=\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\chi_{2}(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2}))\sim\gamma(g)\overline{\gamma}(g)=1

so
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in M^{\perp}
.

Conversely, suppose
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in M^{\perp}
.
Let
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
\Gamma
such that
\widetilde{\alpha}_{g_{n}}\to 0
in
W
.
By Lemma
2.8
,

(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2})\circ\widetilde{\sigma}_{g_{n}}\to 1

in
L^{2}(W)
.
That is,

(4.1)

\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t)\right)\right)\chi_{2}\left(\sigma_{\psi(g_{n})}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t)\right)\right)\to 1

in
L^{2}(Z\times Z)
.

The cocycle
\sigma
is quasi-affine, so by Lemma
2.14
,
there are sequences
(c_{i,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
S^{1}
and
(\lambda_{i,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\widehat{Z}
for
i=1,2
such that

(4.2)

c_{1,n}\lambda_{1,n}(z)\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(z)\right)\to 1\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{2,n}\lambda_{2,n}(z)\chi_{2}\left(\sigma_{\psi(g_{n})}(z)\right)\to 1

in
L^{2}(Z)
.
We will combine (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) to show that

\chi_{1}(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(z))\sim\gamma(g)
and
\chi_{2}(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(z))\sim\overline{\gamma}(g)

for some
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
.

For convenience, let
\mu_{n}=\chi_{1}\circ\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}
and
\nu_{n}=\chi_{2}\circ\sigma_{\psi(g_{n})}
.
Now we perform a change of coordinates.
Define
\eta:Z^{2}\to Z^{2}
by

\eta(z,t)=\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),z+\widehat{\psi}(t)\right)

and, for
u\in Z
, let
\zeta_{u}:Z^{2}\to Z^{2}
be the map

\zeta_{u}(z,t)=\left(u+\widehat{\psi}(z)-\widehat{\varphi}(t),t-z\right).

Note that (
4.1
) is equivalent to

(4.3)

(\mu_{n}\otimes\nu_{n})\circ\eta\to 1

in
L^{2}\left(Z^{2}\right)
.

We claim

(4.4)

(\mu_{n}\otimes\nu_{n})\circ\eta\circ\zeta_{u}\to 1

in
L^{2}\left(Z^{2}\right)
.
Let
\zeta:=\zeta_{0}
.
Fix
u\in Z
, and let
f_{n}(z,t)=\left(\left(\mu_{n}\otimes\nu_{n}\right)\circ\eta\right)(z+u,t)
.
Then
\left(\mu_{n}\otimes\nu_{n}\right)\circ\eta\circ\zeta_{u}=f_{n}\circ\zeta
.
We then want to show
f_{n}\circ\zeta\to 1
in
L^{2}\left(Z^{2}\right)
.
Since the Haar measure on
Z^{2}
is invariant under shifting by
(u,0)
, we have
f_{n}\to 1
in
L^{2}\left(Z^{2}\right)

by (
4.3
).
It therefore suffices to show that
\zeta\left(Z^{2}\right)
has positive measure (equivalently, finite index) in
Z^{2}
.
By assumption,
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
has finite index in
\Gamma
.
Hence,
\left[Z:\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)\right]\leq\left[\Gamma:(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)\right]<\infty
.
Let
F\subseteq Z
be a finite set such that
\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)+F=Z
.
Let
(z,t)\in Z^{2}
be given.
Choose
x\in Z
and
s\in F
such that
\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(x)+s=z+\widehat{\varphi}(t)
.
Put
y=x+t
.
Then

\zeta(x,y)+(s,0)=\left(\widehat{\psi}(x)-\widehat{\varphi}(x)-\widehat{\varphi}(t)+s,t\right)=(z,t).

This shows that
\zeta\left(Z^{2}\right)+\left(F\times\{0\}\right)=Z^{2}
, so

m_{Z^{2}}\left(\zeta\left(Z^{2}\right)\right)\geq\frac{1}{|F|}>0.

Thus, (
4.4
) holds.

But

\left(\eta\circ\zeta_{u}\right)(z,t)=\eta\left(u+\widehat{\psi}(z)-\widehat{\varphi}(t),t-z\right)=\left(u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(z),u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(t)\right).

We therefore deduce from (
4.4
) that

(4.5)

\mu_{n}\otimes\nu_{n}\to 1

in
L^{2}\left(\left(u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)\right)^{2}\right)
.
Taking a conjugate and multiplying by (
4.2
), we deduce

c_{1,n}c_{2,n}(\lambda_{1,n}\otimes\lambda_{2,n})\to 1

in
L^{2}\left(\left(u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)\right)^{2}\right)
.
That is,

\int_{Z^{2}}{\left|c_{1,n}c_{2,n}\lambda_{1,n}\left(u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(z)\right)\lambda_{2,n}\left(u+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(t)\right)-1\right|^{2}~dz~dt}\to 0.

Multiplying by
\overline{\lambda}_{2,n}
in the integrand and using the fact that each
\lambda_{i,n}
is a homomorphism,

\int_{Z^{2}}{\left|c_{1,n}c_{2,n}\lambda_{1,n}(u)\lambda_{1,n}\left(\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(z)\right)-\overline{\lambda}_{2,n}(u)\overline{\lambda}_{2,n}\left(\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(t)\right)\right|^{2}~dz~dt}\to 0.

It follows that for all sufficiently large
n
, we have

\lambda_{1,n},\lambda_{2,n}\in\left(\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)\right)^{\perp}
.

Let
A=\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(Z)
, and put
A_{1}=\left\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in W:w_{1}\in A\right\}
.
Note that
A_{1}
is a Borel set, and
m_{W}(A_{1})=m_{Z}(A)>0
.
Fix
t=(t_{1},t_{2})\in A_{1}
and let

\rho_{g}(w)=\frac{\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}+t_{1})\right)}{\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)}.

By Lemma
2.13
, our goal is to show that
\rho
is a coboundary.
Since
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an arbitrary sequence in
\Gamma
with
\widetilde{\alpha}_{g_{n}}\to 0
,
it suffices by Lemma
2.8
to show
\rho_{g_{n}}\to 1
in
L^{2}(W)
.
We have already seen that

c_{1,n}\lambda_{1,n}(z)\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(z)\right)\to 1

in
L^{2}(Z)
and
\lambda_{1,n}\in A^{\perp}
.
In particular,
\lambda_{1,n}(z+t_{1})=\lambda_{1,n}(z)
, since
t_{1}\in A
.
Therefore,

\rho_{g_{n}}(w)=\frac{\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(w_{1}+t_{1})\right)}{\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(w_{1})\right)}=\frac{c_{1,n}\lambda_{1,n}(w_{1}+t_{1})\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(w_{1}+t_{1})\right)}{c_{1,n}\lambda_{1,n}(w_{1})\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g_{n})}(w_{1})\right)}\to 1

in
L^{2}(W)
.
This proves that
\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)
is cohomologous to a character.
The same argument applies to
\chi_{2}\left(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)
,
taking the set
A_{2}=\left\{(w_{1},w_{2})\in W:w_{2}\in A\right\}
.

Therefore, we may assume in the above that
\lambda_{i,n}=1
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
i=1,2
.
We may also assume
c_{i,n}=\gamma_{i}(g_{n})
, where
\chi_{1}\left(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)\sim\gamma_{1}(g)

and
\chi_{2}\left(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)\sim\gamma_{2}(g)
.
We have thus shown that for any sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
\widetilde{\alpha}_{g_{n}}\to 0
, we have

\left(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\right)(g_{n})\to 1.

Hence, by Lemma
2.8
,
\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\sim 1
.
By tweaking
\gamma_{2}
up to cohomology, we may assume
\gamma_{2}=\overline{\gamma}_{1}
, completing the proof.
∎

By passing to an extension of the original system, we will show that the Mackey group
M
associated with
\{\varphi,\psi\}

decomposes into the Cartesian product of Mackey groups associated with
\varphi
and
\psi
respectively:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
has finite index in
\Gamma
,
and suppose
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)

such that
\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi
is injective.
Let
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
be an ergodic quasi-affine
\Gamma
system
such that the discrete spectrum
\widehat{Z}
is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete
and
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible.
There is an ergodic quasi-affine extension
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
of
\mathbf{X}

such that the Mackey group
M^{\prime}
decomposes as
M^{\prime}=M^{\prime}_{\varphi}\times M^{\prime}_{\psi}
, where

\displaystyle\left(M^{\prime}_{\varphi}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)~\text{is a coboundary over}~\left(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}\right)\right\}

and

\displaystyle\left(M^{\prime}_{\psi}\right)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\left\{\chi\in\widehat{H}:\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)~\text{is a coboundary over}~\left(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}\right)\right\}.

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle C_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\left\{\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\exists\chi\in\widehat{H},\chi(\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\sim\gamma(g)~\text{over}~(W,\widetilde{\alpha})\right\}

and

\displaystyle C_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\left\{\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}:\exists\chi\in\widehat{H},\chi(\sigma_{\psi(g)}(w_{2}))\sim\gamma(g)~\text{over}~(W,\widetilde{\alpha})\right\}.

Then let
C=C_{\varphi}\cap C_{\psi}
.
Note that a character
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
is cohomologous to another character
\gamma^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}

if and only if
\overline{\gamma}\gamma^{\prime}
is an eigenvalue for the system
(W,\widetilde{\alpha})
.
That is,

\overline{\gamma}\gamma^{\prime}\in\left\{(\lambda_{1}\circ\varphi)(\lambda_{2}\circ\psi):\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\widehat{Z}\right\}\subseteq\widehat{Z}.

Moreover, the groups
\widehat{Z}
and
\widehat{H}
are countable, so
C
is a countable set of characters.

By Theorem
3.2
, let
\Lambda^{\prime}
be a countable subgroup of
\widehat{\Gamma}

that is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete,
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible,
and contains the group generated by
\Lambda=\widehat{Z}
and
C
, and let
Z^{\prime}=\widehat{\Lambda}^{\prime}
.
For
g\in\Gamma
, let
\alpha^{\prime}_{g}\in Z^{\prime}
be the element such that
\alpha^{\prime}_{g}(\lambda)=\lambda(g)

for every
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Since
\Lambda\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
,
there is a surjective homomorphism
\pi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
such that
\pi(\alpha^{\prime}_{g})=\alpha_{g}
.
Define
\sigma^{\prime}:\Gamma\times Z^{\prime}\to H
by
\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z)=\sigma_{g}(\pi(z))
.

Claim 1
:
\sigma^{\prime}
is a cocycle.

Indeed, for any
g,h\in\Gamma
and
z\in Z^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}_{g+h}(z)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{g+h}(\pi(z))

\displaystyle(\text{definition of}~\sigma^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{g}(\pi(z)+\alpha_{h})+\sigma_{h}(\pi(z))

\displaystyle(\sigma~\text{is a cocycle})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{g}(\pi(z+\alpha^{\prime}_{h}))+\sigma_{h}(\pi(z))

\displaystyle(\pi~\text{is a factor map})

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z+\alpha^{\prime}_{h})+\sigma^{\prime}_{h}(z).

This proves the claim.

Claim 2
:
\sigma^{\prime}
is quasi-affine.

The cocycle
\sigma
is quasi-affine, so there exist measurable functions
F:Z\times Z\to S^{1}

and
\gamma:Z\to\widehat{\Gamma}
such that

\frac{\sigma_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma_{g}(z)}=\gamma(t,g)\frac{F(t,z+\alpha_{g})}{F(t,z)}.

Define
F^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\times Z^{\prime}\to S^{1}
by
F^{\prime}(t,z)=F(\pi(t),\pi(z))
and
\gamma^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\to\widehat{\Gamma}
by
\gamma^{\prime}(t,\cdot)=\gamma(\pi(t),\cdot)
.
Then

\frac{\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z+t)}{\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z)}=\frac{\sigma_{g}(\pi(z)+\pi(t))}{\sigma_{g}(\pi(z))}=\gamma(\pi(t),g)\frac{F(\pi(t),\pi(z)+\alpha_{g})}{F(\pi(t),\pi(z))}=\gamma^{\prime}(t,g)\frac{F^{\prime}(t,z+\alpha^{\prime}_{g})}{F^{\prime}(z)},

so
\sigma^{\prime}
is quasi-affine as claimed.

Claim 3
: The quasi-affine system
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
is an ergodic system
with Kronecker factor
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}=(Z^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
.

We need to check that
\sigma^{\prime}
is a weakly mixing cocycle.
That is, for any
\chi\in\widehat{H}
, if
\chi\circ\sigma^{\prime}
is cohomologous to a character, then
\chi=1

(see
[
ABB21
, Proposition 7.5]
).
Suppose
\chi\in\widehat{H}
and
\chi\circ\sigma^{\prime}\sim\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
over
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
.
Let
F:Z^{\prime}\to S^{1}
such that

\chi(\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z))=\gamma(g)\frac{F(z+\alpha^{\prime}_{g})}{F(z)}

for every
g\in\Gamma
and almost every
z\in Z^{\prime}
.
Define
G:Z\times H\to S^{1}
by
G(z,x)=\overline{F}(z)\chi(x)
.
Then

G\left(z+\alpha^{\prime}_{g},x+\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z)\right)=\overline{F}\left(z+\alpha^{\prime}_{g}\right)\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{g}(z)\right)\chi(x)=\gamma(g)\overline{F}(z)\chi(x)=\gamma(g)G(z,x).

Hence,
G
is an eigenfunction for the system
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
(with eigenvalue
\gamma
).
The function
F\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{H}
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor of
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
,
since
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}
is a Kronecker system and therefore contained in the Kronecker factor.
Therefore,
\mathbbm{1}_{Z^{\prime}}\otimes\chi=(F\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{H})G
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor.

Now, the projection of
\mathbbm{1}_{Z^{\prime}}\otimes\chi
under the factor map
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H\to\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H

is the function
\mathbbm{1}_{Z}\otimes\chi
.
Thus,
\mathbbm{1}_{Z}\otimes\chi
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor of
\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
,
but by assumption, the Kronecker factor of
\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
is
\mathbf{Z}
.
It follows that the function
(z,x)\mapsto\chi(x)=(\mathbbm{1}_{Z}\otimes\chi)(z,x)
does not depend on
x
.
That is,
\chi=1
.

As a brief aside, it is worth remarking that the system
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
is (isomorphic to)
the relatively independent joining of
\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
and
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}

with respect to the common factor
\mathbf{Z}
.
We therefore could have shown the previous three claims by establishing this isomorphism
and referring to Theorem
2.5
.
However, in order to prove that
\mathbf{X}^{\prime}=\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
is the desired extension of
\mathbf{X}
,
it is more convenient to work with the system written explicitly as a group extension over its Kronecker factor
rather than appealing to general abstract statements about Host–Kra factors.

It remains to show that the ergodic quasi-affine system
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
is the desired extension.
Let us introduce some notation.
We define a system
(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})
by

W^{\prime}=\left\{\left(z+\varphi(t),z+\psi(t)\right):z,t\in Z^{\prime}\right\}

and

\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{g}=\left(\alpha^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)},\alpha^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}\right).

We then define the cocycle
\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}:\Gamma\times W^{\prime}\to H^{2}
by

\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}_{g}(w)=\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}),\sigma^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)

and associate a Mackey group
M^{\prime}
with annihilator

(M^{\prime})^{\perp}=\left\{\widetilde{\chi}\in\widehat{H^{2}}:\widetilde{\chi}\circ\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}~\text{is a coboundary over}~(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})\right\}.

We want to show
M^{\prime}=M^{\prime}_{\varphi}\times M^{\prime}_{\psi}
.
To this end, we prove one more claim:

Claim 4
: Let
\chi\in\widehat{H}
and
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
.

(a)

If
\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)\sim\gamma(g)
over
(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})
,
then there exists
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{\varphi}
such that
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
.

(b)

If
\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)\sim\gamma(g)
over
(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})
,
then there exists
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{\psi}
such that
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
.

The proofs of items (a) and (b) are the same, so we prove only (a).
Suppose
\chi\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)\sim\gamma(g)
over
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}=(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})
.
Arguing as in the proof of Claim 3 above, the function
\mathbbm{1}_{W^{\prime}}\otimes\chi\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{H}:W^{\prime}\times H^{2}\to S^{1}

is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor of
\mathbf{W}^{\prime}\times_{\widetilde{\sigma}^{\prime}}H^{2}
.
Projecting onto the factor
\mathbf{W}\times_{\widetilde{\sigma}}H^{2}
, it follows that
\mathbbm{1}_{W}\otimes\chi\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{H}

is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor of
\mathbf{W}\times_{\widetilde{\sigma}}H^{2}
.
We then project again to the factor
(Z,\alpha\circ\varphi)\times_{\sigma_{\varphi}}H

(with the action of
\Gamma
given by
g\cdot(z,x)=\left(z+\alpha_{\varphi(g)},x+\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(z)\right)
)
to conclude that
\mathbbm{1}_{Z}\otimes\chi
is measurable with respect to the Kronecker factor
\mathbf{Z}_{\varphi}

of
(Z,\alpha\circ\varphi)\times_{\sigma_{\varphi}}H
.
Noting that
L^{2}(\mathcal{Z}_{\varphi})
is spanned by functions of the form
F(z)\zeta(x)

with
\zeta\in\widehat{H}
such that
\zeta\circ\sigma_{\varphi}
is cohomologous to a character
(see the proof of
[
ABB21
, Proposition 7.5(2)]
), it follows that
\chi\circ\sigma_{\varphi}
is cohomologous
to a character
\gamma^{\prime}\in\widehat{\Gamma}
over
(Z,\alpha\circ\varphi)
.
Therefore,
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{\varphi}
and
\gamma\sim\gamma^{\prime}
.

Let
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in(M^{\prime})^{\perp}
.
By Theorem
4.1
, there exists
\gamma\in\widehat{\Gamma}
such that

\chi_{1}(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1}))\sim\gamma(g)
and

\chi_{2}(\sigma^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}(w_{2}))\sim\overline{\gamma}(g)
over
(W^{\prime},\widetilde{\alpha}^{\prime})
.
By Claim 4, we may assume
\gamma\in C_{\varphi}\cap C_{\psi}=C
.
We constructed the extension
\mathbf{Z}^{\prime}\times_{\sigma^{\prime}}H
so that
\Lambda^{\prime}\supseteq C
.
Therefore,
\gamma\in\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Also by construction,
\Lambda^{\prime}
is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete
and (
\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible.
Using the divisibility condition, let
\lambda\in\Lambda^{\prime}

such that
\lambda\circ(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)=\gamma
.
Let
\lambda_{1}=\lambda\circ\theta_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}=\lambda\circ\theta_{2}
.
By the completeness condition,
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Moreover,
(\lambda_{1}\circ\varphi)(\lambda_{2}\circ\psi)=\gamma
.
So, taking
F=\left.\left(\lambda_{1}\otimes\lambda_{2}\right)\right|_{W^{\prime}}:W^{\prime}\to S^{1}
,
we have
\gamma(g)=\Delta_{g}F
, so
\gamma\sim 1
.
Thus,

\chi_{1}\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\varphi(g)}(w_{1})\right)\sim\chi_{2}\left(\sigma^{\prime}_{\psi(g)}(w_{2})\right)\sim 1.

That is,
\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in\left(M^{\prime}_{\varphi}\right)^{\perp}\times\left(M^{\prime}_{\psi}\right)^{\perp}
as desired.
∎

5.
Limit formula

In this section, we use the Mackey group
M
defined in the previous section in order to derive a limit formula
for double ergodic averages over quasi-affine systems:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
\psi-\varphi
has finite index image in
\Gamma

and
\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi
is injective.
Let
\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
be an ergodic quasi-affine
\Gamma
-system
whose discrete spectrum is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete
and
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible.
Let
M\leq H^{2}
be the Mackey group associated with
\{\varphi,\psi\}
.
Then there is a measurable function
\omega:Z\times Z\to H^{2}
such that

(1)

\omega(0,z)\in M
for all
z\in Z

and
t\mapsto\omega(t,\cdot)+M
is a continuous function from
Z
to
\mathcal{M}(Z,H^{2}/M)
; and

(2)

for any
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Z\times H)
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}

\displaystyle~{f_{1}(T_{\varphi(g)}(z,x))f_{2}(T_{\psi(g)}(z,x))}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{Z\times M}{f_{1}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),x+u+\omega_{1}(t,z)\right)f_{2}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t),x+v+\omega_{2}(t,z)\right)~dm_{Z}(t)~dm_{M}(u,v)}

in
L^{2}(Z\times H)
, where
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
.

Before proving Theorem
5.1
, we note an immediate corollary:

Corollary 5.2
.

In the setup of Theorem
5.1
, for any
f_{0},f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Z\times H)

and any continuous function
\kappa:Z\to\mathbb{C}
, one has

(5.2)

\begin{split}\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}&~{\kappa(\alpha_{g})\int_{Z\times H}{f_{0}\cdot T_{\varphi(g)}f_{1}\cdot T_{\psi(g)}f_{2}~d(m_{Z}\times m_{H})}}\\
=&~\int_{Z^{2}\times H\times M}{\kappa(t)~f_{0}(z,x)~f_{1}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),x+u+\omega_{1}(t,z)\right)}\\
&\qquad\qquad{~f_{2}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t),x+v+\omega_{2}(t,z)\right)~dm_{Z}(z)~dm_{Z}(t)~dm_{H}(x)~dm_{M}(u,v)}.\end{split}

Proof.

By the Stone–Weierstrass theorem, we may assume
\kappa
is a character on
Z
.
Since the discrete spectrum
\Lambda\cong\widehat{Z}
is
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible,
there exists
\kappa^{\prime}\in\widehat{Z}
such that

\kappa^{\prime}\circ\left(\widehat{\theta}_{1}\circ\widehat{\varphi}+\widehat{\theta}_{2}\circ\widehat{\psi}\right)=\kappa
.
Define functions
\widetilde{f}_{i}\in L^{\infty}(Z\times H)
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{0}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\overline{\kappa^{\prime}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{1}(z)+\widehat{\theta}_{2}(z)\right)}f_{0}(z,x),

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{1}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{\prime}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{1}(z)\right)f_{1}(z,x),

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{2}(z,x)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{\prime}\left(\widehat{\theta}_{2}(z)\right)f_{2}(z,x).

The left hand side of (
5.2
) is equal to

(5.3)

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\int_{Z\times H}{\widetilde{f}_{0}\cdot T_{\varphi(g)}\widetilde{f}_{1}\cdot T_{\psi(g)}\widetilde{f_{2}}}},

while the right hand side of (
5.2
) is equal to

(5.4)

\begin{split}\int_{Z^{2}\times H\times M}&{\widetilde{f}_{0}(z,x)~\widetilde{f}_{1}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t),x+u+\omega_{1}(t,z)\right)}\\
&~{\widetilde{f}_{2}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t),x+v+\omega_{2}(t,z)\right)~dm_{Z}(z)~dm_{Z}(t)~dm_{H}(x)~dm_{M}(u,v)}.\end{split}

The quantities in (
5.3
) and (
5.4
) are equal by Theorem
5.1
.
∎

The first step in the proof of Theorem
5.1
is the following enhancement of Proposition
2.19
:

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2}\in L^{\infty}(Z\times H)
.
Suppose that for every
\widetilde{\chi}\in M^{\perp}
, one has

(5.5)

\int_{H^{2}}{f_{1}(w_{1},x_{1})f_{2}(w_{2},x_{2})\widetilde{\chi}(x)~dx}=0

for a.e.
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in W
.
Then

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{f_{1}(z+\alpha_{\varphi(g)},x+\sigma_{\varphi(g)}(z))f_{2}(z+\alpha_{\psi(g)},x+\sigma_{\psi(g)}(z))}=0

in
L^{2}(Z\times H)
.

Proof.

The proof is very much in the spirit of
[
ABB21
, Proposition 7.10]
.
Define
\widetilde{T}_{g}:W\times H^{2}\to W\times H^{2}
by

\widetilde{T}_{g}(w,x)=\left(w+\widetilde{\alpha}_{g},x+\widetilde{\sigma}_{g}(w)\right).

Set
F(w,x):=f_{1}(w_{1},x_{1})f_{2}(w_{2},x_{2})
for
w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in W
and
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in H^{2}
.
We claim
F
is orthogonal to the space of
(\widetilde{T}_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}
-invariant functions in
L^{2}(W\times H^{2})
.

Let
E_{1}:=\{z\in Z:M_{z}=M\}
.
As discussed in Section
4
,
E_{1}
has full measure.
Let
E_{2}:=\{z\in Z:\eqref{eq: orthogonal to chi}~\text{holds for a.e.}~w=(w_{1},w_{2})\in W_{z}\}
,
and let
E_{3}:=\{z\in Z:F\in L^{\infty}\left(W_{z}\times H^{2}\right)\}
.
By Fubini’s theorem, both of the sets
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
have full measure in
Z
.
Put
E:=E_{1}\cap E_{2}\cap E_{3}
.

Suppose
z\in E
, and let
\widetilde{\chi}\in M_{z}^{\perp}=M^{\perp}
.
Then

\int_{H^{2}}{F(w,x)\widetilde{\chi}(x)~dx}=0

for a.e.
w\in W_{z}
.
Therefore,

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\widetilde{T}_{g}F}=0

in
L^{2}\left(W_{z}\times H^{2}\right)
by Proposition
2.19
.

Fix a Følner sequence
(\Phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\Gamma
, and define the average

A_{N}:=\frac{1}{|\Phi_{N}|}\sum_{g\in\Phi_{N}}{\widetilde{T}_{g}F}\in L^{2}\left(W\times H^{2}\right).

We want to show
A_{N}\to 0
in
L^{2}\left(W\times H^{2}\right)
.
Decompose the Haar measure on
W
as
m_{W}=\int_{Z}{m_{z}~dz}
.
Then

\displaystyle\left\|A_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(W\times H^{2}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{W}{\int_{H^{2}}{|A_{N}(w,x)|^{2}~dx}~dw}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}{\left(\int_{W_{z}}{\int_{H^{2}}{|A_{N}(w,x)|^{2}~dx}~dm_{z}}\right)~dz}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z}{\left\|A_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(W_{z}\times H^{2}\right)}^{2}~dz}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

To complete the proof, we apply the van der Corput trick.
Let
u_{g}:=T_{\varphi(g)}f_{1}\cdot T_{\psi(g)}f_{2}\in L^{2}(Z\times H)
.
Then

\left\langle u_{g+h},u_{g}\right\rangle=\int_{Z\times H}{(\overline{f}_{1}\cdot T_{\varphi(h)}f_{1})\cdot T_{(\psi-\varphi)(g)}(\overline{f}_{2}\cdot T_{\psi(h)}f_{2})~d\mu}.

Since
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
has finite index in
\Gamma
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{\psi-\varphi}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}
,
so by the mean ergodic theorem,

\displaystyle\xi_{h}

\displaystyle:=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\left\langle u_{g+h},u_{g}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int_{Z\times H}{(\overline{f}_{1}\cdot T_{\varphi(h)}f_{1})(z,x)\cdot\left(\int_{Z}{\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{f}_{2}\cdot T_{\psi(h)}f_{2}\mid\mathcal{Z}\right]\left(z+\left(\widehat{\psi}-\widehat{\varphi}\right)(t)\right)}~dt\right)~dz~dx}

\displaystyle=\int_{W}{\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{f}_{1}\cdot T_{\varphi(h)}f_{1}\mid\mathcal{Z}\right](w_{1})\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[\overline{f}_{2}\cdot T_{\psi(h)}f_{2}\mid\mathcal{Z}\right](w_{2})~dw}

\displaystyle=\int_{W\times H^{2}}{\left(\overline{f}_{1}\cdot T_{\varphi(h)}f_{1}\right)(w_{1},x_{1})\left(\overline{f}_{2}\cdot T_{\psi(h)}f_{2}\right)(w_{2},x_{2})~dw~dx}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\widetilde{T}_{h}F,F\right\rangle_{L^{2}\left(W\times H^{2}\right)}.

Now since
\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\widetilde{T}_{h}F}=0
in
L^{2}(W\times H^{2})
,
we have
\textup{UC-}\lim_{h\in\Gamma}{\xi_{h}}=0
.
By the van der Corput lemma (Lemma
2.1
),
it follows that
\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{u_{g}}=0
in
L^{2}(Z\times H)
as desired.
∎

The next step is to construct the function
\omega
appearing in Theorem
5.1
.
Fix
z\in Z
such that
M_{z}=M
.
Note that our assumptions on the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{X}
ensure that
(W_{z},\widetilde{\alpha})
is isomorphic to
(Z,\alpha)

under the map
(w_{1},w_{2})\mapsto\widehat{\theta}_{1}(w_{1}-z)+\widehat{\theta}_{2}(w_{2}-z)
.
Hence, by Proposition
2.17
,
there is a function
\widetilde{\omega}:Z\times Z\to H^{2}/M

such that
t\mapsto\widetilde{\omega}(t,\cdot)
is a continuous map
Z\to\mathcal{M}(Z,H^{2}/M)
and

\widetilde{\omega}(\alpha_{g},z)\equiv\widetilde{\sigma}_{g}(z)\pmod{M}.

By the Kuratowski and Ryll-Nardewski measurable selection theorem (see
[
Sri98
, Section 5.2]
),
we can lift
\widetilde{\omega}
to a function
\omega:Z\times Z\to H^{2}
such that
\omega+M=\widetilde{\omega}
.

Now we put everything together to prove Theorem
5.1
:

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By linearity, we may assume
f_{i}=h_{i}\otimes\chi_{i}
with
\chi_{i}\in\widehat{H}
for
i=1,2
.
Let
\widetilde{\chi}=\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\in\widehat{H^{2}}
.
Then the right hand side of (
2
) is equal to

(5.6)

\chi_{1}(x)\chi_{2}(x)\left(\int_{Z}{h_{1}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t)\right)h_{2}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t)\right)\widetilde{\chi}\left(\omega(t,z)\right)~dt}\right)\left(\int_{M}{\widetilde{\chi}~dm_{M}}\right).

We break the proof into two cases depending on whether or not
\widetilde{\chi}
belongs to
M^{\perp}
.

Case 1
:
\widetilde{\chi}\notin M^{\perp}
.

In this case, the expression in (
5.6
) is equal to 0.
Proposition
5.3
guarantees that the left hand side of (
2
) is also equal to zero.

Case 2
:
\widetilde{\chi}\in M^{\perp}
.

Let
F:Z\times Z\to\mathbb{C}
be given by

F(t,z)=h_{1}\left(z+\widehat{\varphi}(t)\right)h_{2}\left(z+\widehat{\psi}(t)\right)\widetilde{\chi}\left(\omega(t,z)\right).

Note that
t\mapsto F(t,\cdot)
is a continuous function from
Z
to
L^{2}(Z)
,
and the expression in (
5.6
) simplifies to
\chi_{1}(x)\chi_{2}(x)\int_{Z}{F(t,z)~dt}
.

Moving to the left hand side of (
2
), for
g\in\Gamma
, we have

f_{1}(T_{\varphi(g)}(z,x))f_{2}(T_{\psi(g)}(z,x))=\chi_{1}(x)\chi_{2}(x)F(\alpha_{g},z)

Therefore, since
(Z,\alpha)
is uniquely ergodic, we have

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{F(\alpha_{g},z)}=\int_{Z}{F(t,z)~dt}

in
L^{2}(Z)
by Lemma
2.6
.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.
Khintchine-type recurrence

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.16
.
We will first prove the following statement (corresponding to the case
\eta=\textup{id}_{\Gamma}
),
from which Theorem
1.16
can be quickly deduced:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete abelian group.
Let
\varphi,\psi\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that
(\psi-\varphi)(\Gamma)
is a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma
.
Suppose there exist
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in\textup{End}(\Gamma)
such that

\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi
is injective.
Then for any ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

with Kronecker factor
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
, any
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
,
there is a continuous function
\kappa:Z\to[0,\infty)
with
\int_{Z}{\kappa(z)~dz}=1
such that

\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\kappa(\alpha_{g})~\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi(g)}^{-1}A\cap T_{\psi(g)}^{-1}A\right)}>\mu(A)^{3}-\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic
\Gamma
-system
with Kronecker factor
\mathbf{Z}=(Z,\alpha)
, let
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Put
f=\mathbbm{1}_{A}
.
Let
\kappa:Z\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function (to be specified later) with
\int_{Z}{\kappa(z)~dz}=1
.
By
[
ABS22
, Theorem 4.10]
, we may assume without loss of generality (by passing to an extension if necessary) that

\displaystyle\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}

\displaystyle{~\kappa(\alpha_{g})~\int_{X}{f\cdot T_{\varphi(g)}f\cdot T_{\psi(g)}f~d\mu}}

\displaystyle=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\kappa(\alpha_{g})~\int_{X}{f\cdot T_{\varphi(g)}\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{Z}^{2}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}\right]\cdot T_{\psi(g)}\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{Z}^{2}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\psi}\right]~d\mu}}.

We may further assume that the discrete spectrum of
\mathbf{X}
is
\{\varphi,\psi,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
-complete
and
(\theta_{1}\circ\varphi+\theta_{2}\circ\psi)
-divisible
by passing to another extension if necessary with the help of Theorem
3.2
.

Let
f_{\varphi}=\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{Z}^{2}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}\right]
and
f_{\psi}=\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{Z}^{2}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\psi}\right]
.
We may expand

f_{\varphi}=\sum_{i}{c_{i}h_{i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{\psi}=\sum_{j}{d_{j}k_{j}},

where
c_{i}
is
\varphi(\Gamma)
-invariant,
d_{j}
is
\psi(\Gamma)
-invariant, and
h_{i}
,
k_{j}
are
\mathcal{Z}^{2}
-measurable.
Write
\mathbf{Z}^{2}=\mathbf{Z}\times_{\sigma}H
and apply Corollary
5.2
:

\displaystyle\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}

\displaystyle{~\kappa(\alpha_{g})~\mu\left(A\cap T_{\varphi(g)}^{-1}A\cap T_{\psi(g)}^{-1}A\right)}

\displaystyle=\textup{UC-}\lim_{g\in\Gamma}{\kappa(\alpha_{g})~\sum_{i,j}{\int_{X}{fc_{i}d_{j}\cdot T_{\varphi(g)}h_{i}\cdot T_{\psi(g)}k_{j}~d\mu}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}{\int_{X\times Z\times M}{f(x)c_{i}(x)d_{j}(x)\cdot\kappa(t)\cdot h_{i}\left(\pi_{Z}(x)+\widehat{\varphi}(t),\pi_{H}(x)+u+\omega_{1}\left(t,\pi_{Z}(x)\right)\right)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{{k_{j}\left(\pi_{Z}(x)+\widehat{\psi}(t),\pi_{H}(x)+v+\omega_{2}\left(t,\pi_{Z}(x)\right)\right)~d\mu(x)~dm_{Z}(t)~dm_{M}(u,v)}}.

Taking
\kappa
supported on a sufficiently small neighborhood of
0
in
Z
, it suffices to establish the inequality

(6.1)

\sum_{i,j}{\int_{X\times M}{f(x)c_{i}(x)d_{j}(x)h_{i}\left(\pi_{Z}(x),\pi_{H}(x)+u\right)k_{j}\left(\pi_{Z}(x),\pi_{H}(x)+v\right)~d\mu(x)~dm_{M}(u,v)}}\geq\mu(A)^{3}.

By applying Theorem
4.3
together with Theorem
2.5

and passing to yet another extension,
we may assume the Mackey group
M
decomposes as
M=M_{\varphi}\times M_{\psi}
.
Let
\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by functions
f:Z\times H\to\mathbb{C}
satisfying
f(z,x)=f(z,x+y)

for
y\in M_{\varphi}
, and let
\mathcal{W}_{\psi}
be defined similarly.
Then the left hand side of (
6.1
) is equal to

\int_{X}{f\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{W}_{\varphi}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\varphi}\right]\cdot\mathbb{E}\left[f\mid\mathcal{W}_{\psi}\lor\mathcal{I}_{\psi}\right]~d\mu},

which is bounded below by
\left(\int_{X}{f~d\mu}\right)^{3}=\mu(A)^{3}
by
[
Chu11
, Lemma 1.6]
.
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
1.16
:

Proof of Theorem
1.16
.

Let
\mathbf{X}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,(T_{g})_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
be an ergodic measure-preserving
\Gamma
-system,
let
A\in\mathcal{X}
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.

The
\Gamma
-system
\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,\left(T_{\eta(g)}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right)

has finitely many ergodic components (at most the index of
\eta(\Gamma)
in
\Gamma
).
Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{l}{\mu_{i}}
be the ergodic decomposition.
Then each of the systems
\mathbf{X}_{i}=\left(X,\mathcal{X},\mu_{i},\left(T_{\eta(g)}\right)_{g\in\Gamma}\right)
is ergodic,
and they all have the same Kronecker factor
\mathbf{Z}_{\eta}=(Z_{\eta},\alpha\circ\eta)
,
where
Z_{\eta}=\overline{\left\{\alpha_{\eta(g)}:g\in\Gamma\right\}}
.
By Theorem
6.1
, we may therefore find a continuous function

\kappa:Z_{\eta}\to[0,\infty)
such that
\int_{Z_{\eta}}{\kappa(t)~dt}=1
and

Description of electromagnetic fields in uniformly accelerated frame
Bartłomiej Ba̧k

Abstract
It is shown that Maxwell equations for electromagnetic fields generated by the uniformly accelerated charge could be reduced to the Laplace equation (in Łobaczewski geometry) for a single scalar potential. The full solution of this equation is presented.

2
Laboratory and co-moving frames

The starting point is a four dimensional manifold with a flat Lorentzian metric tensor
g
:

\displaystyle g=-\text{d}\tau^{2}+\left(\text{d}\eta^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\text{d}\eta^{2}\right)^{2}+\text{d}\zeta^{2}\,,

(1)

where
(\eta^{\mu})=(\tau,\eta^{1},\eta^{2},\zeta)
are Cartesian coordinates, what is called as a
laboratory frame
.

The trajectory of the particle with constant acceleration in
\zeta
direction is described by the hyperbola:

\displaystyle\zeta^{2}-\tau^{2}={\rm const}>0\,.

(2)

Of course, this equation allows two arms of the hyperbola, therefore to disambiguate the trajectory, the range of the
\zeta
coordinate has to be restricted to
\zeta>0
. It means, that in this analysis only half of the space is taken into account.

The
co-moving (Fermi) frame
, associated with the accelerated particle, with coordinate system
(x^{\mu})=(t,x^{1},x^{2},\xi)
is defined via the following transformation
[
8
]
,
[
10
]
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\tau\ =&\frac{1+a\xi}{a}\,\sinh\left(at\right)\,,\\
\eta^{1}=&x^{1}\,,\\
\eta^{1}=&x^{2}\,,\\
\zeta=&\frac{1+a\xi}{a}\,\cosh\left(at\right)\,,\par\end{array}\right.

where
a>0
is an acceleration parameter, coordinate
\xi
“numerates” hyperboloids and
t
is a proper time coordinate on each hyperboloid. Due to the fact, that only half of the space is parametrized (
\zeta
has to be positive), it implies that
\xi>-\frac{1}{a}
. Then, the metric tensor (
1
) takes form

\displaystyle g=-N^{2}\,\text{d}t^{2}+\left(\text{d}x^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\text{d}x^{2}\right)^{2}+\text{d}\xi^{2}\,,

(8)

where

\displaystyle N:=1+a\xi

(9)

denotes the

. In the Fermi frame, the particle is located in the origin and its trajectory is given by
\xi(t)=0
. Importantly, when

, and coordinates
(x^{\mu})
stay Cartesian. Indeed, the metric (

1

It will be useful to introduce the following Euclidean metric
g_{F}
:

\displaystyle g_{F}:=g\big|_{t=const}=\left(\text{d}x^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\text{d}x^{2}\right)^{2}+\text{d}\xi^{2}\,,

(10)

which is the induced metric on hyperboloid

, and the associated Riemannian metric
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\gamma:=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\,g_{F}=\frac{1}{(1+a\xi)^{2}}\,\left[\left(\text{d}x^{1}\right)^{2}+\left(\text{d}x^{2}\right)^{2}+\text{d}\xi^{2}\right]\,.

(11)

Obviously, metric
\gamma
is conformally flat (conformally equivalent to

). Following Turakulov
[

, the simplification of Maxwell equations is obtained via passing to the bispherical coordinates

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}x^{1}=&\frac{1}{a}\frac{\sin\theta\cos\phi}{\cosh\mu-\cos\theta}\,,\\
x^{2}=&\frac{1}{a}\frac{\sin\theta\sin\phi}{\cosh\mu-\cos\theta}\,,\\
\xi=&\frac{1}{a}\frac{\sinh\mu}{\cosh\mu-\cos\theta}-\frac{1}{a}\,.\end{array}\right.

As before, the ranges of new coordinates have to be specified. Angle coordinates have standard domains:
\theta\in]0,\pi[
,
\phi\in]0,2\pi[
, whereas radial coordinate
\mu>0
. The above transformation implies the following form of metric

(
11

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a^{2}\sinh^{2}\mu}\,\left(\text{d}\mu^{2}+\text{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\text{d}\phi^{2}\right)\,,

(16)

The Riemannian manifold described by the metric
\gamma
is isomorphic to the one-sheet hyperboloid in Minkowski space, what corresponds to Łobaczewski (hyperbolic) geometry, and will naturally appear in the description of the electromagnetic field.

4
Solution of the Laplace equation

The solution of the Laplace equation (
30
) is made in few steps which, due to the complexity of this problem, are explicitly presented below.

Firstly, the equation (
30
) has to be written in bispherical coordinates
(\mu,\theta,\phi)
, then:

\displaystyle\sinh\mu\,\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}U\right)+\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Delta}}U=0\,,

(31)

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Delta}}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{2}
with coordinates
(\theta,\phi)
. The solution
U
of the above equation is assumed to be a product of an eigenvector
Y(\theta,\phi)
of the operator
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Delta}}
:

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\circ}}{{\Delta}}Y_{l}(\theta,\phi)=-l(l+1)Y_{l}(\theta,\phi)\,,

(32)

which are called as
spherical harmonics
, and the “radial part”
R(\mu)
:

\displaystyle U(\mu,\theta,\phi):=R(\mu)Y(\theta,\phi)\,.

(33)

Hence, the equation (
31
) takes the following form:

\displaystyle\sinh\mu\,\partial_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{\sinh\mu}\,\partial_{\mu}R_{l}(\mu)\right)-l(l+1)R_{l}(\mu)=0\,,

(34)

where
l\geq 0
numerates solutions with respect to the multipole (harmonic) expansion. Finally, taking

\displaystyle R_{l}(\mu)=\frac{1}{\sinh^{l}\mu}\,G_{l}(\coth\mu)

(35)

and defining a new variable

\displaystyle z:=\coth\mu\,,

(36)

where
z>1
(because
\mu>0
), this equation corresponds to the special case of the Gegenbauer (hypergeometric) equation
[
3
]
:

\displaystyle\left[(1-z^{2})\,\partial_{z}^{2}-2(1+\alpha)z\,\partial_{z}+\lambda^{2}-\left(\alpha+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}\right]G_{\alpha,\lambda}(z)=0\,,

(37)

with parameters
\alpha=l+\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda=1
. Hence:

\displaystyle\left[(1-z^{2})\,\partial_{z}^{2}-(2l+3)z\,\partial_{z}+1-\left(l+1\right)^{2}\right]G_{l}(z)=0\,.

(38)

Surprisingly, solutions
G_{l}(z)
of the equation (
38
) are expressed by elementary functions, although they split into two cases:
l=0
:

\displaystyle G_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}+\beta_{0}\left(1-\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}-1}}\right)\,,

(39)

and
l\geq 1
:

\displaystyle G_{l}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{l}\,Q_{l}(z)+\beta_{l}\,Z_{l}(z)\,,

(40)

where
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\alpha_{l},\beta_{l}
are real constants, whereas
Q_{l}(z),Z_{l}(z)
are functions presented below:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}l&Q_{l}(z)&Z_{l}(z)\\
\hline\cr 1&\frac{z\sqrt{z^{2}-1}-\log\left(z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1}\right)}{\left(z^{2}-1\right)^{3/2}}&\frac{1}{\left(z^{2}-1\right)^{3/2}}\\

3&\frac{-\left(13+2z^{2}\right)z\,\sqrt{z^{2}-1}+3\left(4z^{2}+1\right)\,\log\left(z+\sqrt{z^{2}-1}\right)}{2\left(z^{2}-1\right)^{7/2}}&\frac{3\left(4z^{2}+1\right)}{\left(z^{2}-1\right)^{7/2}}\\

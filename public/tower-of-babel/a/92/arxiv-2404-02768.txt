Abstract
. The hybrid-high order (HHO) scheme has many successful applications including linear elasticity as the first step towards computational solid mechanics. The striking advantage is the simplicity among other higher-order nonconforming schemes and its geometric flexibility as a polytopal method on the expanse of a parameter-free refined stabilization. This paper utilizes just one reconstruction operator for the linear Green strain and therefore does not rely on a split in deviatoric and spherical behaviour as in the classical HHO discretization. The a priori error analysis provides quasi-best approximation with λ \lambda -independent equivalence constants. The reliable and (up to data oscillations) efficient a posteriori error estimates are stabilization-free and λ \lambda -robust. The error analysis is carried out on simplicial meshes to allow conforming piecewise polynomials finite elements in the kernel of the stabilization terms. Numerical benchmarks provide empirical evidence for optimal convergence rates of the a posteriori error estimator in some associated adaptive mesh-refining algorithm also in the incompressible limit, where this paper provides corresponding assertions for the Stokes problem.

1.2.
Mathematical model

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n=2,3
be a bounded polyhedral Lipschitz domain in two or three space dimensions with boundary

that is split into a closed Dirichlet part
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\subset\partial\Omega

Given

and

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{div}\,\sigma=f\text{ in }\Omega,\quad\sigma\coloneqq\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(u),\quad u=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}},\quad\sigma\nu=g\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}.

The linearized Green strain

is the symmetric part of the gradient
\mathrm{D}v
and the isotropic elasticity tensor
\mathbb{C}
acts as
\mathbb{C}\tau\coloneqq 2\mu\tau+\lambda\mathrm{tr}(\tau)\mathrm{I}_{n\times n}
for any
\tau\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{n\times n}

with the Lamé parameters
\lambda\geq 0
and
\mu>0
.
We assume that, for
|\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}|>0
, the parameters
\lambda
,
\mu
are piecewise constant such that
\mu
is bounded away from zero

by positive numbers
\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}
and, for
|\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}|=0
,
\lambda
and
\mu
are positive constants.
The weak formulation seeks the solution
u\in V\coloneqq\{v\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{n}:v=0\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\}
to

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(u):\varepsilon(v)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}g\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}s\quad\text{for all }v\in V.

Main results

The HHO methodology

[

The a posteriori error analysis combines the techniques from the residual and equilibrium error estimation of

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{rel}}^{-1}(\mu_{0}/\mu_{1})\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|

\displaystyle\leq\eta

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu_{1}/\mu_{0})(\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+\mathrm{osc}(g,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}})).

with the error estimator

\displaystyle\eta^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\|h_{\mathcal{T}}(f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\sigma_{h})\|^{2}+\min_{v\in V}\|\mu(\varepsilon(v)-\varepsilon_{h}u_{h})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq h_{T}^{-2}(\delta_{T}^{k}u_{h},\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})_{L^{2}(T)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}h_{F}^{-1}(\delta_{TF}^{k}u_{h},\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h})_{L^{2}(F)}

and

.

Theorem 2.1

(equivalence of stabilizations)
.

Any
v_{h}=(v_{\mathcal{T}},v_{\mathcal{F}})\in V_{h}
and

satisfy

.

Proof.

The assertion
\lesssim
follows immediately from the triangle and the discrete trace inequality. The remaining parts of this proof are therefore devoted to the reverse direction

.
A triangle and a discrete trace inequality imply

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\|\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h}-\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}+h_{T}^{-1/2}\|\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}

for any
F\in\mathcal{F}(T)
. Therefore, it remains to prove
h_{T}^{-1}\|\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\mathrm{s}_{T}(u_{h},v_{h})^{1/2}
.
Let
x_{T}
denote the midpoint of
T
.
Define the rigid-body motion
\varphi_{\mathrm{RM}}\coloneqq\Pi_{T}^{0}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}+(\Pi_{T}^{0}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{ss}}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})(x-x_{T})
as in
[
23
]
.
Since
\Pi_{T}^{0}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}=0
from (
2.4
), we infer

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|\varphi_{\mathrm{RM}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\approx\|\Pi_{T}^{0}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{ss}}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}=|T|^{-1/2}\Big|\int_{T}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{ss}}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big|.

The outer normal vector
\nu_{T}
is constant along any
F\in\mathcal{F}(T)
and hence,
the convention (
2.4
) in the definition of
\mathcal{R}
and an integration by parts show

\displaystyle\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\int_{F}\mathrm{asym}(\nu_{T}\otimes\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h})\,\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\int_{F}\mathrm{asym}(\nu_{T}\otimes(v_{F}-\mathcal{R}v_{h}|_{T}))\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

In combination with another integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{T}\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{ss}}\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\int_{F}\mathrm{asym}(\nu_{T}\otimes\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}\int_{F}\mathrm{asym}(\nu_{T}\otimes(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}-\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h}))\,\mathrm{d}s.

This, (
2.10
), and a Cauchy inequality reveal

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|\varphi_{\mathrm{RM}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h}-\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\mathrm{s}_{T}(v_{h},v_{h})^{1/2}.

Since
h_{T}^{-1}\|\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}-\varphi_{\mathrm{RM}}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\|\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}
from a Poincaré and the (second) Korn inequality (

),

(2.11)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{-1}\|\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim\mathrm{s}_{T}(v_{h},v_{h})^{1/2}+\|\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}.

The proof of

below generalizes that of
[
4
, Theorem 4]
.
An integration by parts provides

(2.12)

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle=-(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h},\mathrm{div}\,\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}))_{L^{2}(T)}+(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h},\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\nu_{T})_{L^{2}(\partial T)}.

Since

with the convention

) of

result in

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-(\Pi_{T}^{k}\mathcal{R}v_{h},\mathrm{div}\,\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}))_{L^{2}(T)}=-(\mathcal{R}v_{h},\mathrm{div}\,\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}))_{L^{2}(T)}\\
&\qquad=-(v_{T},\mathrm{div}\,\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}))_{L^{2}(T)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(v_{F}-\mathcal{R}v_{h}|_{T},\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\nu_{T})_{L^{2}(F)}.\end{split}

Recall

\displaystyle(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h},\mathrm{div}\,\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h}))_{L^{2}(T)}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(T)}(\delta_{TF}^{k}v_{h},\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\nu_{T})_{L^{2}(F)}

The combination of (

Hence,
\|\varepsilon(\delta_{T}^{k}v_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}\lesssim s_{T}(v_{h},v_{h})
, (
2.9
), and (

) conclude the proof.
∎

3.4

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{J}u_{h}-\mathcal{R}u_{h})\|\lesssim\|\mathrm{I}u-u_{h}\|_{h}+\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\mathcal{R}\mathrm{I}u)\|+|\mathrm{I}u|_{\widehat{\mathrm{s}}}.

This, (
4.7
),
\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\mathcal{R}\mathrm{I}u)\|\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\mathcal{R}\mathrm{I}u)\|
from (
2.16
),
\|\mathrm{I}u-u_{h}\|_{h}\lesssim\mu_{0}^{-1/2}\|\mathrm{I}u-u_{h}\|_{a_{h}}
from
Theorem

2.7
, and
\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|
imply

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\mathcal{R}\mathrm{I}u)\|+\mu_{0}^{-1/2}\|\mathrm{I}u-u_{h}\|_{a_{h}}+|u_{h}|_{\widehat{\mathrm{s}}}+|\mathrm{I}u|_{\widehat{\mathrm{s}}}.

From
Lemma

2.6
and a triangle inequality, we deduce that
\|\varepsilon_{h}u_{h}-\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|\lesssim\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(\mathcal{R}u_{h}-\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|+|u_{h}|_{\widehat{\mathrm{s}}}
.
Hence, the a priori result

\mu_{0}^{1/2}|u_{h}|_{\mathrm{s}}\leq\mu_{0}^{1/2}\|\mathrm{I}u-u_{h}\|_{a_{h}}\lesssim\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+\mathrm{osc}(g,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}})
from (
1.3
), the quasi-optimality
|\mathrm{I}u|_{\widehat{\mathrm{s}}}+\|\varepsilon_{\mathrm{pw}}(u-\mathcal{R}\mathrm{I}u)\|\lesssim\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{k})\varepsilon(u)\|
from (
3.7
), and (
4.8
) show

(4.9)

\displaystyle(\mu_{0}/\mu_{1})\|\mu(\varepsilon_{h}u_{h}-\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h}))\|\lesssim\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+\mathrm{osc}(g,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}).

4.3.2.
Efficiency of Neumann data approximation

Given
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}
, let
T\in\mathcal{T}
be the unique simplex with
F\in\mathcal{F}(T)
. Abbreviate
e_{k}\coloneqq\Pi_{F}^{k}g-\sigma_{h}\nu_{F}\in P_{k}(F)^{n}
. There exists
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{n}
with
\varphi=0
in
\Omega\setminus T
,
\Pi_{F}^{k}\varphi=h_{F}e_{k}
, and

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{F}^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(T)}\lesssim h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{F}^{1/2}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(F)}

as in
[
29
, Subsection 4.3]
.
An integration by parts and a Cauchy inequality show

\displaystyle((\sigma-\sigma_{h})\nu_{F},\varphi)_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle=(f+\mathrm{div}\,\sigma_{h},\varphi)_{L^{2}(T)}+(\sigma-\sigma_{h},\mathrm{D}\varphi)_{L^{2}(T)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f+\mathrm{div}\,\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}\|\mathrm{D}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(T)}.

Since
h_{F}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}=((\sigma-\sigma_{h})\nu_{F},\varphi)_{L^{2}(F)}+((1-\Pi_{F}^{k})g,\varphi)_{L^{2}(F)}
from
\sigma\nu_{F}=g
on
F
, this, a Cauchy inequality, and (
4.10
) reveal

\displaystyle h_{F}^{1/2}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim h_{T}\|f+\mathrm{div}\,\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{F}^{1/2}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{k})g\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

Therefore, the Pythagoras theorem provides

\displaystyle h_{F}\|g

\displaystyle-\sigma_{h}\nu_{F}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(F)}=h_{F}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{k})g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(F)}+h_{F}\|e_{k}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{k})g\|^{2}_{L^{2}(F)}+\|h_{\mathcal{T}}(f+\mathrm{div}\sigma_{h})\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}+\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}.

4.3.3.
Finish of the proof

The efficiency
h_{T}\|f+\mathrm{div}\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\lesssim\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|+h_{T}\|(1-\Pi_{T}^{k})f\|_{L^{2}(T)}
for any
T\in\mathcal{T}
has been established in
[
4
, Proof of Theorem 2]
for the Poisson equation. An extension to the case at hand is straightforward. The efficiency
h_{F}^{1/2}\|[\sigma_{h}]\nu_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\omega(F))}+\|h_{\mathcal{T}}(f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\sigma_{h})\|_{L^{2}(\omega(F))}
can be established with the arguments from
[
44
, Section 1.4.5]
.
Further details on these two terms are therefore omitted.
In combination with (
4.9
) and (
4.11
), we conclude
(\mu_{0}/\mu_{1})\eta\lesssim\|(1-\Pi_{\mathcal{T}}^{k})\sigma\|+\mathrm{osc}(f,\mathcal{T})+\mathrm{osc}(g,\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}})
. ∎

4.4.
Extension to inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary data in 2D

In the following, we derive reliable error estimates for inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary data in 2D. We assume that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
lies on one connectivity component of
\partial\Omega
.
Given
u_{\mathrm{D}}\in C(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}\cap H^{1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}
,
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
, and
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})^{2}
, the continuous (resp. discrete) problem seeks the exact (resp. discrete) solution
u\in u_{\mathrm{D}}+V
(resp.
u_{h}\in\mathrm{I}u_{\mathrm{D}}+V_{h}
) to (
1.2
) (resp. (
2.17
)).
(Here,
u_{\mathrm{D}}
is understood as an
H^{1}
extension in the domain
\Omega
.)
The proof of reliability in
Section

4.2
and the observation
\mathbb{C}^{-1}\tau\perp\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(u_{\mathrm{D}}+V)
(instead of
\mathbb{C}^{-1}\tau\perp\mathbb{C}\varepsilon(V)
in (
4.6
)) lead to

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|\sigma-\sigma_{h}\|\lesssim(\mu_{1}/\mu_{0})\eta

with
\eta
from (
1.5
), where the minimum in (
1.5
) is taken over the set
u_{\mathrm{D}}+V
(instead of
V
). Let
\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}}
denote the nodal interpolation of
u_{\mathrm{D}}
in
P_{k+1}(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}\cap C(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}
.
We choose the nodal average
v\coloneqq\mathcal{A}u_{h}
of
\mathcal{R}u_{h}
in (
4.12
); the degrees of freedom of
\mathcal{A}u_{h}
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
are fixed by the point evaluation of
u_{\mathrm{D}}
so that
\mathcal{A}u_{h}|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}=\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}}
.
Since
\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}}\neq u_{\mathrm{D}}
in general, additional error quantities occur.
A triangle inequality shows

(4.13)

\displaystyle\min_{w\in u_{\mathrm{D}}+V}\|\varepsilon(w)-\varepsilon_{h}u_{h}\|\leq\min_{w\in u_{\mathrm{D}}+V}\|\varepsilon(w)-\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|+\|\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})-\varepsilon_{h}u_{h}\|.

The minimizer
z
of
\|\varepsilon(w)-\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})\|
among
w\in u_{\mathrm{D}}+V
defines
\varrho\coloneqq\varepsilon(z)-\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
with the boundary condition
(z-\mathcal{A}u_{h})|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}=u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}}
and
L^{2}
orthogonality
\varrho\perp\mathrm{D}\,V
from the Euler-Lagrange equations. Therefore,
\mathrm{div}\,\varrho=0
in
\Omega
and
\varrho\nu=0
along
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
.
The Helmholtz decomposition
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
shows the existence of
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{2}
(even as a
\mathrm{Curl}
of an
H^{2}
function) with

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{2}\beta_{1}&-\partial_{1}\beta_{1}\\
\partial_{2}\beta_{2}&-\partial_{1}\beta_{2}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\varrho,\qquad\|\beta\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|\varrho\|,\qquad\text{and}

\beta
is constant on each connectivity component of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
, i.e.,
\partial\beta/\partial s=0
on
\mathrm{relint}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}})
.
(The assumptions in
[
17
]
that (a)
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
is connected and (b)
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}
have positive distance, can be relaxed to the present one with the arguments from here and
[
17
]
.)
The a.e. pointwise symmetry of
\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta
and an integration by parts reveal

\displaystyle\|\varrho\|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\varrho,\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(\mathrm{D}z-\mathrm{D}\mathcal{A}u_{h},\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial\Omega}(z-\mathcal{A}u_{h})\cdot\partial\beta/\partial s\,\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})\cdot\partial\beta/\partial s\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{F}(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})\cdot\partial\beta/\partial s\,\mathrm{d}s,

because
(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})|_{F}\in H^{1}_{0}(F)^{2}
on
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}
and
\partial\beta/\partial s\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
vanishes on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
(written
\partial\beta/\partial s\in\widetilde{H}^{-1/2}(\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}
as its zero extension belongs to
H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega)
). This holds at least for smooth
\beta
, where an edge-wise integration by parts shows

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\varrho\|^{2}=(\mathrm{D}(z-\mathcal{A}u_{h}),\mathrm{Curl}\,\beta)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\int_{F}\beta\cdot\partial(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})/\partial s\,\mathrm{d}s.

The second equation (4.15.b) in (
4.15
) holds for all smooth functions
\beta
with
\partial\beta/\partial s=0
on
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
. Since those functions can approximate
\beta\in H^{1}(\Omega)^{2}
from (
4.14
) (by density in
H^{1}(\Omega)^{2}
even with the side restriction that
\beta
is constant on each connectivity component of
\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
), (4.15.b) holds for
\beta
from (
4.14
) as well.
Nodal interpolation leads to
\int_{F}\partial(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})/\partial s\,\mathrm{d}s=0

for any
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}
with the fundamental theorem of calculus along the 1D edge
F
.
This and the Cauchy inequality in (
4.15
) provide

\displaystyle\|\varrho\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{0})\beta\|_{L^{2}(F)}\|\partial(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})/\partial\tau\|_{L^{2}(F)}

(4.16)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}h_{F}^{-1}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{0})\beta\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\big)^{1/2}\mathrm{osc}(u_{\mathrm{D}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})

with
\mathrm{osc}(u_{\mathrm{D}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}h_{F}\|\partial(u_{\mathrm{D}}-\mathrm{I}_{\mathrm{D}}u_{\mathrm{D}})/\partial s\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}
.
Given
F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}
, let
T_{F}\in\mathcal{T}
be the unique triangle with
F\in\mathcal{F}(T_{F})
. A trace and Poincaré inequality imply

\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}h_{F}^{-1}\|(1-\Pi_{F}^{0})\beta\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\leq\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}}h_{F}^{-1}\|(1-\Pi_{T_{F}}^{0})\beta\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\|\mathrm{D}\beta\|^{2}\lesssim\|\varrho\|^{2}

with (
4.14
) in the last step.
From this and (
4.16
), we infer

\displaystyle\|\varrho\|\lesssim\mathrm{osc}(u_{\mathrm{D}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}}).

This and (
4.12
)–(
4.13
) reveal the reliability of the error estimator
\widetilde{\eta}
,

\displaystyle(\mu_{0}/\mu_{1})^{2}\|\sigma

\displaystyle-\sigma_{h}\|^{2}\lesssim\widetilde{\eta}^{2}\coloneqq\|h_{\mathcal{T}}(f+\mathrm{div}_{\mathrm{pw}}\sigma_{h})\|^{2}+\|\mu(\varepsilon(\mathcal{A}u_{h})-\varepsilon_{h}u_{h})\|^{2}

(4.17)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle h_{F}\|[\sigma_{h}]_{F}\nu_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{N}}}h_{F}\|g-\sigma_{h}\nu_{F}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+\mu_{1}^{2}\mathrm{osc}(u_{\mathrm{D}},\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{D}})^{2}.

5.4.
Conclusions

In all three numerical examples, the a posteriori error estimator
\eta
is reliable, efficient, and
\lambda
-robust. This allows for the approximation of the Stokes equations in the incompressible limit as
\lambda\to\infty
.
The adaptive algorithm driven by the local contributions of
\eta
recovers the optimal convergence rates for the approximation of singular
solutions and enables higher order convergence rates in typical computational benchmarks.
A comparison with the results for
\nu=0.3
displayed in
Figure

2
.b and
Figure

5
.b verifies empirically the
\lambda
-robustness of the method.

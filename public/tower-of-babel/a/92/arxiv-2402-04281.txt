Proximal-point-like algorithms for abstract convex minimisation problems
R. Díaz Millán, J. Ugon

Abstract
In this paper we introduce two conceptual algorithms for minimising abstract convex functions. Both algorithms rely on solving a proximal-type subproblem with an abstract Bregman distance based proximal term. We prove their convergence when the set of abstract linear functions forms a linear space. This latter assumption can be relaxed to only require the set of abstract linear functions to be closed under the sum, which is a classical assumption in abstract convexity. We provide numerical examples on the minimisation of nonconvex functions with the presented algorithms.

Introduction

Abstract convexity was first proposed by
[
13
,
14
]
to generalise the notion of (Minkowski) duality to nonconvex functions, and was extensively developed in the subsequence decades
[
16
,
17
,
19
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
]
just to name a few. A function is abstract convex with respect to a set of functions
L
if it is the supremum of functions from
L
.

Many theoretical results from convex analysis have been generalised to the framework of abstract convexity, including duality
[
13
]
, conjugacy
[
17
]
, the subdifferential
[
17
]
and normal cones
[
10
]
. Building on these results, in
[
8
]
we developed a calculus for the subdifferential operator. However, few algorithms have been proposed to build on that theory to minimise abstract convex functions. A notable exception is the cutting angle method
[
1
,
2
]
. Some conceptual algorithms are also described in
[
17
, chapter 9]
.

Creating algorithms is a robust approach to tackling optimisation challenges. Within the literature, there is a plethora of algorithms and their variations specifically tailored for convex functions. However, designing algorithms for non-convex optimisation problems has been limited due to computational costs and the challenge of proving convergence. Nonetheless, by selecting an appropriate set of
abstract linear functions
, it is feasible to transform any function into an abstract convex one. Our focus is on developing algorithms capable of addressing optimisation problems across a broader spectrum of functions. Specifically, we aim to create two distinct proximal-point-like algorithms to solve the targeted optimisation problem, with the condition that the set of abstract linear functions forms a linear vector space.

In both algorithms, we introduce an abstract Bregman distance. The first algorithm is a Bregman proximal-point method, and we provide proof of convergence for the function values of the iterates. Additionally, we demonstrate that all cluster points in the sequence generated by the algorithm belong to the solution set of the problem.

The second algorithm, referred to as abstract mirror descent, exhibits the convergence of the limitinf of the sequence of function values.

In both cases, we present numerical implementations involving non-convex functions.

The paper is structured into four sections. Initially, in Section
2
, we revisit crucial results and definitions pertaining to abstract convexity, elucidating the notations employed across the entirety of the paper. In the Section
3
, we introduce the notion of abstract Bregman divergences, distances and projection. Then, Section
4
and Section
5
are dedicated to presenting the algorithm
Bregman Proximal-point algorithm for abstract convex functions
and
Bregman Mirror Descent algorithm for abstract convex functions
with they convergence and numerical examples, respectively.

3.1
Strict Abstract Convexity

Strict convexity is a robust property exhibited by certain functions. In this context, we introduce the notion of Strict Abstract Convexity.

Definition 4

(Strict Abstract Convexity)
.

A function
f
is said to be
strictly
L
-convex
at a point
x
if it is
L
-convex, and for any
u\in\partial_{L}f(x)

y\in X

f(y)>f(x)+u(y)-u(x).

The function

nearly strictly

-convex
at a point
x
if it is
L
-convex and there exists
u\in\partial_{L}f(x)
, such that for any
y\in X

f(y)>f(x)+u(y)-u(x).

If the function
f
is (nearly) strictly
L
-convex at every point
x
of its domain, we say that
f
is (nearly) strictly
L
-convex.

If

3.2
Abstract Bregman Divergences

Bregman divergence, or Bregman distance, is a robust tool for developing algorithms in convex analysis. With the idea of simplifying the projection step in many algorithms, the notion of Bregman divergence plays a crucial role. In this section, we introduce the concept of Abstract Bregman divergence, which generalizes the concept in the classic convex case.

Definition 6

(Abstract Bregman Divergence
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a
(L,X)
-function and
y\in X
, and let
\lambda\in\partial\phi(y)
. We the
L
-Bregman divergence from
y
with respect to
\lambda
as

D_{\phi}^{\lambda}(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)-(\lambda(x)-\lambda(y))

For
x,y\in X
we also define the following divergences (see
[
11
,
3
]
)

\displaystyle D^{\flat}_{\phi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\phi(y)-\phi(x)-\sup_{\lambda\in\partial_{L}\phi(x)}(\lambda(y)-\lambda(x))

\displaystyle D^{\sharp}_{\phi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\phi(y)-\phi(x)-\inf_{\lambda\in\partial_{L}\phi(x)}(\lambda(y)-\lambda(x))

It is clear that for any abstract Bregman divergence, we have
0\leq D_{\phi}^{\flat}(x,y)\leq D_{\phi}^{\lambda}(x,y)\leq D_{\phi}^{\sharp}(x,y)
for any
x,y\in X
, and that
D_{\phi}^{\flat}(y,y)=D_{\phi}^{\lambda}(y,y)=D_{\phi}^{\sharp}(y,y)=0
. Furthermore, when the function
\phi
is strictly
L
-convex, we have that
D_{\phi}^{\flat}(x,y)=0\implies x=y
, and if
\phi
is nearly strictly
L
-convex, then there exists a subgradient
\lambda\in\partial_{L}\phi(y)
for which
D_{\phi}^{\lambda}(x,y)=0\implies x=y
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
a,b,c
be points in
X
,
\alpha\in\partial_{L}\phi(a)
and
\beta\in\partial_{L}\phi(b)
. Then we have the following equality:

D^{\alpha}_{\phi}(c,a)+D^{\beta}_{\phi}(a,b)-D^{\beta}_{\phi}(c,b)=\beta(c)-\beta(a)-(\alpha(c)-\alpha(a))

(1)

Proof.

Using the definition of the abstract Bregman divergence, we have:

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{\phi}(c,a)+D^{\beta}_{\phi}(a,b)-D^{\beta}_{\phi}(c,b)=

\displaystyle\phi(c)-\phi(a)+\alpha(a)-\alpha(c)

\displaystyle+\phi(a)-\phi(b)+\beta(b)-\beta(a)

\displaystyle-\phi(c)+\phi(b)+\beta(c)-\beta(b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(a)-\alpha(c)-\beta(a)+\beta(c)

which proves the result.
∎

3.3
Abstract Bregman Projection

Let
\phi
a
(L,X)
-convex function, and consider
D^{\lambda}_{\phi}(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)+\lambda(y)-\lambda(x)
an abstract Bregman divergence of
x
from
y
induced by
\phi
between the points
x
and
y
in
X
, where
\lambda\in\partial_{L}\phi(y)
.

Definition 7
.

Let
C\subset X
and
y\in X
. The point
x\in C
is a Bregman projection of the point
y
on the set
C
with respect to
D_{\phi}^{\lambda}
, if
D^{\lambda}_{\phi}(x,y)=\min\{D_{\phi}(z,y):z\in C\}
. We denote the set of Bregman projections by
P_{C}^{\phi,\lambda}(y)
.

Note that if
y\in C
then
y\in P_{C}^{\phi,\lambda}(y)
for any
\lambda\in\partial_{L}\phi(y)
.

4
Proximal point method

Next we discuss a generalisation of the proximal point method to the abstract convex case, with Bregman distance. The (convex) Bregman proximal minimisation algorithms was orginally proposed by
[
6
,
12
]
.

In the sequel, we assume that
L
satisfies the following conditions:

Assumption 1
.

We assume that the set
L
is a linear vector space.

The first assumption is usual.
[
3
]
include it as part of their definition of
abstract linear spaces
. The second assumption is less common. It makes it possible to have decreasing step sizes.

Let
\phi
be a
(L,X)
-convex function.

Algorithm 1

Bregman proximal point algorithm for abstract convex functions

Step 1:

Select
x_{0}\in X
,
\lambda_{0}\in\partial_{L}\phi(x_{0})
and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\subset\mathds{R}^{+}
. Set
k=0
.

Step 2:

repeat

Step 3:

Pick
x_{k+1}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmin}}_{x\in X}\{f(x)+\frac{1}{c_{k}}D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}(x,x_{k})\}

Step 4:

Set
\lambda_{k+1}=\lambda_{k}-c_{k}g_{k+1}
, where
g_{k+1}\in\partial_{L}f(x_{k+1})
.

Step 5:

Set
k=k+1
.

Step 6:

until

0\in\partial_{L}f(x_{k})

Note that Step
4
is well defined when the sum rule for the subgradient applies to
f(\cdot)+c_{k}D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}(\cdot,x_{k})
and
\lambda_{k+1}\in L
by Assumption
1
. We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for a discussion of the sum rule for the abstract convex subdifferential.

Assumption 2
.

(A1)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}=\infty
.

(A2)

The sublevel sets of the function
D^{\lambda}_{\phi}(\cdot,y)
are bounded, for any
\lambda>0
and
y\in X
.

(A3)

D^{\lambda_{0}}_{\phi}(x^{*},x_{0})<+\infty
for some
x^{*}\in X^{*}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
the sequence of iterates produced by Algorithm
1
, then

\lim_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x).

Proof.

Applying Lemma
1
, we find that at every iteration
k
and for any
x\in X
,

D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k+1}}(x,x_{k+1})+D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k+1},x_{k})-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x,x_{k})\\
=\lambda_{k}(x)-\lambda_{k}(x_{k+1})-(\lambda_{k+1}(x)-\lambda_{k+1}(x_{k+1}))\\
=c_{k}(g_{k+1}(x)-g_{k+1}(x_{k+1}))\leq c_{k}(f(x)-f(x_{k+1})).

Summing this inequality over
0\leq k\leq l
and defining
s_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}
, gives

D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{l+1}}(x,x_{l+1})-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})+\sum_{k=0}^{l}D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\leq s_{l}f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{l}c_{k}f(x_{k+1}),

which implies that

D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{l+1}}(x,x_{l+1})-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})\leq s_{l}f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{l}c_{k}f(x_{k+1}),

Since the sequence
(f(x_{k}))_{k\in\mathcal{N}}
is decreasing, because of the definition of the
x_{k+1}
, and since
D^{\lambda}_{\phi}(y,z)\geq 0
for any
y,z\in X
, we can simplify the inequality above to:

-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})\leq s_{l}(f(x)-f(x_{l+1}))

That is, for any
x\in X
,

f(x_{l})\leq f(x)+\frac{D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})}{s_{l-1}}.

Since
\lim_{l\to\infty}s_{l}=+\infty
by Assumption
2

(A1)
, and since Assumption
2

(A2)
ensures that the function
D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(\cdot,x_{0})
has a bounded sublevel set
S_{\alpha}=\{x:D^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})\leq\alpha<+\infty\}
(by Assumption
2

(A3)
), then we obtain that for any
x\in S_{\alpha}
,

\lim_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})\leq f(x)

From this we conclude that

\lim_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)\qed

Remark 1
.

Note that Assumption
1
is not strictly necessary in what follows. The algorithms will converge, for example, when
L+L=L
if we choose
c_{k}=1
for all
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 2
.

If the set
X^{*}
is not empty then every cluster point of the sequence
x_{k}
belongs to
X^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}
be a cluster point of the sequence
x_{k}
. By lower semi-continuity of the function
f
, this implies that
f(\bar{x})\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}f(x_{k})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)
. and therefore
f(\bar{x})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)
.
∎

Remark 2
.

By definition of
x_{k+1}
, we have:

f(x_{k+1})+c_{k}D_{\phi}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\leq f(x)+c_{k}D_{\phi}(x,x_{k}).

(2)

Therefore, if
f(x)\leq f(x_{k+1})
, then
D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\leq D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}(x,x_{k})
, and so the point
x_{k+1}
is a
D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}
-projection of
x_{k}
onto the
L
-convex set
\Omega=\{x\in X:f(x)\leq f(x_{k+1})\}
.

[

.

(L,X)
-convex function
\phi
.

Select
x_{0}\in X
,
\lambda_{0}\in\partial\phi(x_{0})
, and
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}\in(0,1)
. Set
k=0
.

repeat

Take
u_{k}\in\partial_{L}f(x_{k})

Pick
x_{k+1}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Argmin}}_{x\in X}\{u_{k}(x)+\frac{1}{c_{k}}D^{\lambda_{k}}_{\phi}(x_{k},x)\}

Define
\lambda_{k+1}=\lambda_{k}-c_{k}u_{k}

.

until

5.1
Convergence

We will prove, under the Assumptions
2
, that this algorithm converges to a solution to our problem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence generated by the Algorithm
2
, then

\liminf_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)

Proof.

Applying the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
1
, we find that

D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{l+1}}(x,x_{l+1})-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})+\sum_{k=0}^{l}D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k+1},x_{k})\leq s_{l}f(x)-\sum_{k=0}^{l}c_{k}f(x_{k+1})

Since
D^{\lambda}_{\phi}(y,z)\geq 0
for any
y,z
, we can simplify the inequality above to:

-D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})\leq s_{l}(f(x)-\min_{1\leq k\leq l+1}f(x_{k}))

That is, for any
x\in X
,

\min_{1\leq k\leq l+1}f(x_{k})\leq f(x)+\frac{D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(x,x_{0})}{s_{l}}

Since
\lim_{l\to\infty}s_{l}=+\infty
by Assumption
2

(A1)
, and since Assumption
2

(A2)
ensures that the function
D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{0}}(\cdot,x_{0})
has a bounded sublevel set
S_{\alpha}=\{x:D^{\lambda}(x,x_{0})\leq\alpha<+\infty\}
(by Assumption
2

(A3)
), then we obtain that for any
x\in S_{\alpha}
,

\liminf_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})\leq f(x)

From this we conclude that

\liminf_{l\to\infty}f(x_{l})=\inf_{x\in X}f(x)\qed

Remark 3
.

Note that by construction,
x_{k+1}
is chosen to be the projection of
x_{k}
onto an abstract hyperplane. Indeed, for any
x\in X
we have that
c_{k}u_{k}(x)+D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x)\geq c_{k}u_{k}(x_{k+1})+D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k+1})
.

In particular, when
u_{k}(x)\leq u_{k}(x_{k+1})
, this implies that
D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x)\geq D_{\phi}^{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k+1})
.

6
Conclusion

The main purpose of this paper is to propose conceptual algorithms based on the framework of abstract convexity.

We developed two such algorithms, one a proximal point algorithm and the other one a mirror descent algorithm, both using a Bregman-like proximal term. We showed their convergence, under classical assumptions, and demonstrated how they can be applied to minimise abstract convex functions.

One restriction of any proximal based algorithm (including the convex programming ones) is that its convergence depends on the sum rule for subdifferentiability. Previous work, by the authors of this paper and by other authors, provide conditions for this property to be satisfied. In future research we will investigate familie of abstract linear functions satisfying these properties and adapt our algorithms. The other restriction of proximal based algorithms is the need to solve a subproblem, and our future research will focus on solving these subproblems for specific classes of functions.

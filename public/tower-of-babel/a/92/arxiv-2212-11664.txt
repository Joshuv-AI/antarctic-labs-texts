Spectral analysis of a family of nonsymmetric fractional elliptic operators Journal: Journal for review
Quanling Deng, Yulong Li

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the spectral problem { 𝒟 a + α ​ 𝒟 b − β ​ u = λ ​ u , x ∈ ( a , b ) u ( a ) = u ( b ) = 0 , 1 < α + β < 2 , \begin{cases}{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\alpha}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}u=\lambda u,\quad x\in(a,b)\\ u(a)=u(b)=0,1<\alpha+\beta<2,\end{cases} where the operators 𝒟 a + α {\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\alpha}} and 𝒟 b − β {\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}} are left- and right-sided Riemann-Liouville derivatives. These operators are nonlocal and nonsymmetric, however, share certain classic elliptic properties. The eigenvalues correspond to the roots of a class of certain special functions. Compared with classic Sturm-Liouville problems, the most challenging part is to set up the framework for analyzing these nonlocal operators, which requires developing new tools. We prove the existence of the real eigenvalues, find the range for all possible complex eigenvalues, explore the graphs of eigenfunctions, and show numerical findings on the distribution of eigenvalues on the complex plane.

3
Preliminary on fractional Sobolev space

The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,2}(\mathbb{R})=\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 0
is a useful Hilbert space, which can be defined in different ways with equal or equivalent (semi)norms. For elliptic problems, it provides a natural functional space where weak solutions are sought. Furthermore, the true solutions would be recovered by raising the regularity therein. It turns out that the classical definitions of
\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
are not quite fit for the analysis of many types of fractional-order differential equations and therefore need to be suitably tailored to fit the analysis. We shall adopt the following characterization via weak fractional derivatives for our purpose.

Definition 1
(Weak fractional derivative)

(
[
13
]
, Section 3)
Assume
s\geq 0
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
u\in\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if there is a unique
\psi_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{s}}\psi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{1}\cdot\psi\,dx,\quad\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}).

(4)

Similarly,

u\in\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
if and only if there is a unique
\psi_{2}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{s}}\psi\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{2}\cdot\psi\,dx\quad\forall\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}).

(5)

Herein,
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are called the weak Riemann-Liouville derivatives of
u
, denoted by
{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{s}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{s}}u
, respecitively.
They are understood in the weak sense as in (
4
) and (
5
).

It is well-known that
\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
can be defined via Fourier transform, i.e.,

\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+|2\pi\xi|^{2s})|\widehat{w}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi<\infty\right\},

(6)

where
\widehat{w}
is Fourier transform and

\|w\|_{\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}:=\left(\|w\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+|w|^{2}_{\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{1/2},~~\text{with}~~|w|_{\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|(2\pi\xi)^{s}\widehat{w}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

The following property implies that, with the weak R-L derivatives, one can define the norm and seminorm that are equivalent to the ones defined via the Fourier transform.

Property 1

(
[
13
]
, Section 3)
Assume
u\in\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s\geq 0
, then

|u|_{\widehat{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R})}=\|{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(7)

where
{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{s}}u
and
{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{s}}u

We use in the analysis below these equivalent seminorms and norms to analyze equations involving Riemann-Liouville derivative operators.

Since

Definition 2

Property 3

For the case

\begin{split}\left|B\left[u,v\right]\right|\leq\begin{cases}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\|{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha.\end{cases}\end{split}

Since
0<|\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}|<1/2
, we apply
Lemma

3
to both cases of
\beta>\alpha
and
\beta<\alpha
above. We obtain

\begin{split}\left|B\left[u,v\right]\right|&\leq\begin{cases}\cos^{-1}(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos^{-1}(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}\\
&\leq\begin{cases}\cos^{-1}(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos^{-1}(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\|v\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}\\
&\leq c_{1}||u||_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}||v||_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},\end{split}

where
c_{1}=\max\{\cos^{-1}(\frac{|\beta-\alpha|}{2}),1\}
. We have proved (
22
).

2. To show (
23
), pick any
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. Let us consider
\tilde{u}
, the trivial zero extension of
u
from
\Omega
to
\mathbb{R}
, and write it into
\tilde{u}(x)=f_{1}(x)+if_{2}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}
are real-valued functions (Note since
\tilde{u}
has compact support in
\Omega
, so are
f_{1},f_{2}
). Then

B\left[u,u\right]=B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]+B\left[f_{2},f_{2}\right]+i(B\left[f_{2},f_{1}\right]-B\left[f_{1},f_{2}\right]).

(24)

In the following, we first calculate
B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]
. Due to
f_{1},f_{2}\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
, we directly obtain

\begin{split}B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]&=\begin{cases}({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1})_{\Omega},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}\\
&=\begin{cases}({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1})_{\Omega},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha.\end{cases}\end{split}

Note
0<\frac{|\alpha-\beta|}{2}<1/2
, which allows us to invoke
Lemma

2
to obtain that
{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}(\Omega)
when
\beta>\alpha
, and that
{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\beta}}f_{1}\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}(\Omega)
when
\alpha>\beta
. Applying identity (
15
) for these two different cases, we arrive at

\begin{split}&=\begin{cases}\cos(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)({\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}},{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}})_{\mathbb{R}},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)({\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1}},{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1}})_{\mathbb{R}},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha.\end{cases}\\
&\geq\begin{cases}\cos(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1})_{\Omega},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1},{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{s}}f_{1})_{\Omega},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha.\end{cases}\end{split}

By applying
Lemma

1
, this further becomes

\begin{split}&\geq\begin{cases}\cos(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)|\cos(\frac{\beta+\alpha}{2}\pi)|\cdot|f_{1}|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad&\text{if}\quad\beta>\alpha,\\
|\cos(\frac{\beta+\alpha}{2}\pi)|\cdot|f_{1}|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2},&\text{if}\quad\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)|\cos(\frac{\beta+\alpha}{2}\pi)|\cdot|f_{1}|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2},&\text{if}\quad\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}\\
&\geq|\cos(s\pi)|\cdot|f_{1}|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}.\end{split}

(25)

On the other hand, by the Fractional Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality (
4
in
A
)

\|g\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{(b-a)^{t}}{t\Gamma(t)}|g|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{t}(\Omega)},\forall g\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{t}(\Omega),t>0,

we know that

|f_{1}|^{2}_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\geq\left(1+\frac{(b-a)^{2s}}{s^{2}\Gamma^{2}(s)}\right)^{-1}\|f_{1}\|^{2}_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}.

Applying back substitution into the last line of (
25
), we finally have

B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]\geq|\cos(s\pi)|\left(1+\frac{(b-a)^{2s}}{s^{2}\Gamma^{2}(s)}\right)^{-1}\|f_{1}\|^{2}_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}.

3. Similarly, we can verify that

B\left[f_{2},f_{2}\right]\geq|\cos(s\pi)|\left(1+\frac{(b-a)^{2s}}{s^{2}\Gamma^{2}(s)}\right)^{-1}\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}.

Hence, recalling (
24
), we obtain

\big|B\left[u,u\right]\big|\geq B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]+B\left[f_{2},f_{2}\right]\geq|\cos(s\pi)|\left(1+\frac{(b-a)^{2s}}{s^{2}\Gamma^{2}(s)}\right)^{-1}\|u\|^{2}_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}.

This gives (
23
) by setting
c_{2}=|\cos(s\pi)|\left(1+\frac{(b-a)^{2s}}{s^{2}\Gamma^{2}(s)}\right)^{-1}
, which completes the proof.

Once we have the boundedness and coercivity, by using the complex variants of the Lax-Milgram Theorem and the regularity of weak solution, we have the following.

Lemma 5

Given any
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, there is a unique weak solution
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
to the boundary-value problem

\begin{cases}Au=f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u(a)=u(b)=0.\end{cases}

(26)

And
u
is also the true solution (namely,
Au=f
a.e. with
u(a)=u(b)=0
) that can be represented as

u(x)={\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{\beta}}\,({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\alpha}}f+c\,{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}\,1),x\in\Omega

(27)

where

c=-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)(\alpha+\beta-1)}{(b-a)^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\cdot{\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{\beta}}{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\alpha}}f\big|_{x=a}.

(28)

Furthermore, we have the norm estimate

\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

(29)

with a certain positive constant
C_{\alpha,\beta}
independent of
f
.

Proof 6

1. Fix a function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and define
\left<f,g\right>:=\int_{\Omega}f\overline{g}\,dx
for any
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. This is a bounded linear functional on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, and thus on
\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. From
Lemma

4
,
B\left[\cdot,\cdot\right]
is bounded and coercive on
\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. We apply the Lax-Milgram Theorem to prove the existence and uniqueness of a function
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
satisfying

B\left[u,v\right]=\left<f,v\right>

(30)

for all
v\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
;
u
is consequently the unique weak solution of (
26
) by definition.

2. Now, we need to show
Au=f
a.e. from
B\left[u,v\right]=\left<f,v\right>
,
\forall v\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
.

Since
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
,
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{t}(\Omega)
for any
0\leq t\leq s
and the R-L derivatives
{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{t}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{t}}u
exist and belong to
L^{2}(\Omega)
. For any fixed
t
, by
Definition

2
, there exists a corresponding sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}u_{n}\longrightarrow u,\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{t}}u_{n}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{t}}u,\,\text{and}\,\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{t}}u_{n}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{t}}u\,\,\text{in}\,\,L^{2}(\Omega).

(31)

The fact (
31
) allows (
30
) to be written into the limit form

B\left[u,v\right]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B\left[u_{n},v\right]=(f,v)_{\Omega},\quad\forall v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

(32)

by observing that

\begin{split}B\left[u,v\right]=\begin{cases}({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta=\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}=\begin{cases}({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta>\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta=\alpha,\\
({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}u,{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega},&\beta<\alpha.\end{cases}\end{split}

(33)

3. In the following, we only discuss one of the cases,
\beta>\alpha
, and other cases can be justified similarly. Now suppose
\beta>\alpha
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}B\left[u_{n},v\right]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}}u_{n},{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}v)_{\Omega}.

Since
u_{n},v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we use the fractional integration by parts and differentiation by parts, respectively, to reduce it to

-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}u_{n},Dv)_{\Omega}.

We add a suitable constant
c_{n}={\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u_{n}\big|_{x=a}
such that we can exchange the differentiation and integration operators

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}D({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u_{n}-c_{n}),Dv)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(D{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u_{n}-c_{n}),Dv)_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u_{n}-c_{n}),v^{\prime\prime})_{\Omega}.

Now, it is easy to verify that (
31
) guarantees the convergence of the first component, that is

-({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u-\tilde{c}),v^{\prime\prime})_{\Omega},

(34)

with
\tilde{c}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}c_{n}
.
On the other hand, notice

(f,v)_{\Omega}=({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{2}}f,v^{\prime\prime})_{\Omega}.

(35)

Taking into account (
34
) and (
35
), (
32
) now produces

({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u-\tilde{c})+{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{2}}f,v^{\prime\prime})_{\Omega}=0,\quad\forall v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(36)

Hence, (
36
) yields

{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{1-\beta}}u-\tilde{c})+{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{2}}f=\tilde{c}_{1}x+\tilde{c}_{2},\quad\text{for certain constants}\quad\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2}\in\mathbb{C}.

(37)

4. Utilizing the boundary condition
u(a)=u(b)=0
to simplify and solve (
37
) for
u
with a bit effort, we have

u(x)={\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{\beta}}\,({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{\alpha}}f+c\,{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{1-\alpha}}\,1),x\in\Omega

with
c
satisfying (
28
). Therefore, we have obtained the desired results
(
27
) and (
28
). It is a true solution, i.e.,
Au=f
a.e.

5. Lastly, for the estimate (
29
), observe from the coercivity (
23
) that

c_{2}\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq|B\left[u,u\right]|=|\left(f,u\right)_{\Omega}|\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\|u\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)},

then (
29
) follows immediately. The proof is complete.

5
The distribution of eigenvalues

According to
Lemma

5
,
u
is a weak solution if and only if it is a true solution, hence, there is no need to distinguish the weak and true solution in the following. We specify the definition of eigenfunctions as follows.

Definition 4

We say the complex-valued function
u
is an eigenfunction of (
1
) if
u
is a nonzero weak solution of (
1
), and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is called the eigenvalue associated with
u
.

The following theorem characterizes the distribution of all possible eigenvalues.

Theorem 1

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is an eigenvalue of (
1
), then
|\Arg\lambda|\leq\frac{|\beta-\alpha|\pi}{2}
.

Proof 7

1. Suppose
u
is an eigenfunction corresponding to eigenvalue
\lambda
, then

B\left[u,u\right]=\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}.

Since
u
is nonzero, we observe

\Arg\lambda=\Arg\frac{B\left[u,u\right]}{\|u\|^{2}_{\Omega}}=\Arg B\left[u,u\right].

(38)

2. We rewrite
u=f_{1}+if_{2}
with
f_{1},f_{2}
being real-valued functions. Then

B\left[u,u\right]=B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]+B\left[f_{2},f_{2}\right]+i(B\left[f_{2},f_{1}\right]-B\left[f_{1},f_{2}\right]).

By repeating a similar calculation as in the proof of
Lemma

4
for each of
B\left[f_{1},f_{1}\right]
,
B\left[f_{2},f_{2}\right]
,
B\left[f_{2},f_{1}\right]
and
B\left[f_{1},f_{2}\right]
and then adding, we obtain

\begin{split}&B\left[u,u\right]=\\
&\begin{cases}\cos(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\pi)\left(\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}+\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)+\\
i\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx\right),&\beta>\alpha,\\
\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}\|^{2}_{\Omega}+\|{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}\|^{2}_{\Omega},&\beta=\alpha,\\
\cos(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\pi)\left(\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}+\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\right)+\\
i\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\beta}}f_{2}}\,dx\right),&\beta<\alpha,\end{cases}\end{split}

(39)

where the notation
\widetilde{\cdot}
represents the zero extension as before.

3. Recall (
38
), it is clear from the above expresses in (
39
) that, when
\beta=\alpha
,
\Arg\lambda=0
. In the next step, we only discuss
\Arg\lambda
for the case
\beta>\alpha
and the same conclusion can be obtained for the other case of
\beta<\alpha
.

4. Let us estimate the imaginary part of
B\left[u,u\right]
,
\Im(B\left[u,u\right])
, for the case
\beta>\alpha
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx.

(40)

Note with the aid of
Lemma

2
that
\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}},\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}(\Omega)
, hence, we apply the Plancherel’s formula to (
40
) to arrive at

\displaystyle\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\cdot{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\,dx\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(2\pi i\xi)^{\beta-\alpha}\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}\,d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}}(-2\pi i\xi)^{\beta-\alpha}\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}\,d\xi\big|

where
g_{1}(x):=\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}
,
g_{2}(x):=\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}},x\in\mathbb{R}
, the Fourier transform is defined as
\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}f(x)\,dx
and
(\mp i\xi)^{\sigma}
is understood as the corresponding values of the main branch of the analytic function
z^{\sigma}
in the complex plane with the cut along the positive half-axis, i.e.
(\mp i\xi)^{\sigma}=|\xi|^{\sigma}e^{\mp\sigma\pi i\cdot\text{sign}(\xi)/2}
.
Simplifying the terms leads to

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}((2\pi i\xi)^{\beta-\alpha}-(-2\pi i\xi)^{\beta-\alpha})\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}\,d\xi\big|

(41)

\displaystyle=2\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|2\pi\xi|^{\beta-\alpha}\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}\,d\xi-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}|2\pi\xi|^{\beta-\alpha}\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}\,d\xi\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}||2\pi\xi|^{\beta-\alpha}\hat{g_{2}}\overline{\hat{g_{1}}}|\,d\xi

\displaystyle=2\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{2}}\cdot\overline{\widehat{{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{1}}}\,d\xi

by the second application of Plancherel’s formula and Hölder inequality, this is

\displaystyle\leq 2\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\cdot\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}

(42)

\displaystyle\leq\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\left(\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}g_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\right).

By back substitution for
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, we see

=\sin\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}\left(\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}+\|{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{\alpha}}f_{2}}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}}\right).

Therefore,

\big|\Arg\lambda\big|=\big|\arctan B\left[u,u\right]\big|=\arctan\frac{\big|\Im(B\left[u,u\right])\big|}{\Re(B\left[u,u\right])}\leq\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\pi}{2}.

5. Similarly, it can be checked for the case
\beta<\alpha
that

\big|\Arg\lambda\big|\leq\frac{(\alpha-\beta)\pi}{2}.

Combining all these three different cases, we thus have

\big|\Arg\lambda\big|\leq\frac{|\alpha-\beta|\pi}{2},

which closes the proof.

6
Principal eigenvalue

In this section, we prove that there exists at least one real eigenvalue and the corresponding eigenfunction is strictly positive in
\Omega
. This is an analogy of the classic result of principal eigenvalue for nonsymmetric elliptic operators. The proof is constructive, following closely the original proof in Theorem 3, section 6.5.2,
[
18
]
, which is based on the application of Schaefer’s Fixed Point Theorem for convex sets, Hopf’s Lemma and Maximum principle of fractional versions:

Lemma 6
(Schaefer’s Fixed Point Theorem)

Let
T
be a continuous and compact mapping of a nonempty convex subset
K
of a Banach space
X
into itself, such that the set

\{x\in K:x=\lambda Tx\mbox{ for some }0\leq\lambda\leq 1\}

is bounded. Then
\displaystyle T
has a fixed point in
K
.

Lemma 7
(Maximum principle)

Suppose
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}}(\Omega)
is a true solution of

\begin{cases}Au=f\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\quad f\in L^{2}(\Omega),\\
u(a)=u(b)=0.\end{cases}

(43)

If
f\geq 0
a.e., then
u(x)\geq 0
in
\Omega
; furthermore, if
f
is a nonzero function, then the strict inequality holds, i.e.,
u(x)>0
in
\Omega
.

Lemma 8

Proof 8

1. We consider real-valued functions throughout the proof. Let us define the linear operator
A^{-1}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
,
s=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
as

A^{-1}:f\rightarrow u,\quad f\in L^{2}(\Omega),

(45)

where

is the corresponding weak solution (therefore, true solution) of (

A^{-1}
is bounded from

to
\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
by (

\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded into
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have that
A^{-1}
:

L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is a compact operator. On the other hand, if we focus on the cone

C:=\{u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)|u\geq 0,x\in\Omega\},

we further have that
A^{-1}
maps
C
into
C
, according to maximum principle in
Lemma

7
.

2. We claim that

\text{there exist $\aleph>0$ and a function $g\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)$ such that $A^{-1}g\geq\frac{1}{\aleph}g,x\in\Omega$}.

(46)

In particular, we choose a function
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

g\in C,g\not\equiv 0.

Then by applying the maximum principle and Hopf’s lemma, we obtain

\tilde{g}:=A^{-1}g>0,x\in\Omega,\lim_{x\rightarrow a^{+}}D\tilde{g}=+\infty,\lim_{x\rightarrow b^{-}}D\tilde{g}=-\infty.

This implies that there is a certain real constant
\aleph>0
so that

\tilde{g}\geq\frac{1}{\aleph}g,x\in\Omega,

(47)

hence the claim holds.

3. Hereafter, we pick and fix an arbitrary pair
(\aleph,g)
satisfying the claim (
46
). We next assert that the set

G_{\epsilon}:=\{u\in C|\text{there exists $0\leq t\leq\aleph+\epsilon$ such that $u=tA^{-1}[u+\epsilon g]$}\}

is unbounded in
\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
for any fixed
\epsilon>0
. For otherwise we could apply Schaefer’s Fixed Point Theorem
6
to deduce that for a certain
\epsilon>0
the equation

u=(\aleph+\epsilon)A^{-1}[u+\epsilon g]

(48)

has a solution
u\in C
. However, this is impossible. To verify this, suppose (
48
) holds, using (
47

u=(\aleph+\epsilon)A^{-1}[u]+(\aleph+\epsilon)A^{-1}[\epsilon g]\geq(\aleph+\epsilon)A^{-1}[\epsilon g]\geq\frac{\aleph+\epsilon}{\aleph}\epsilon g.

(49)

Hence, (
49

u\geq(\aleph+\epsilon)A^{-1}[u]\geq\frac{(\aleph+\epsilon)^{2}}{\aleph}A^{-1}[\epsilon g]\geq\left(\frac{\aleph+\epsilon}{\aleph}\right)^{2}\epsilon g.

Repeating this process, we inductively obtain

u\geq\left(\frac{\aleph+\epsilon}{\aleph}\right)^{n}\epsilon g

for any integer
n

\left(\frac{\aleph+\epsilon}{\aleph}\right)^{n}\rightarrow+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, it contradicts the boundedness of

in
\Omega

4. Owing to the unboundedness of

and the arbitrariness of
\epsilon>0

\epsilon\rightarrow 0

0\leq t_{\epsilon}\leq\aleph+\epsilon,v_{\epsilon}\in C\quad\text{and}\quad\|v_{\epsilon}\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}\rightarrow+\infty,

v_{\epsilon}=t_{\epsilon}A^{-1}[v_{\epsilon}+\epsilon g].

Therefore, we construct a bounded sequence
\{u_{\epsilon}\}
by renormalizing

u_{\epsilon}:=\frac{v_{\epsilon}}{\|v_{\epsilon}\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}}.

Using the compactness of intervals
[0,\aleph+\epsilon]
and the compactness of

t_{\epsilon_{n}}\rightarrow t,\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\epsilon_{n}}\rightarrow u\quad\text{in}\quad\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega),

(51)

satisfying

u_{\epsilon_{n}}=t_{\epsilon_{n}}A^{-1}[u_{\epsilon_{n}}+\epsilon_{n}\frac{g}{\|v_{\epsilon}\|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}}].

We deduce in limit that
u=tA^{-1}u

\begin{cases}Au=tu\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\\
u(a)=u(b)=0.\end{cases}

(52)

Clearly,
0<t\leq\aleph

\lambda=t

\Omega

\lambda

This section is devoted to the study of numerical spectral approximation of the nonsymmetric elliptic operator defined in (
1

In particular, we adopt the widely-used finite element method (FEM) to solve the problem (
1
).
The method reaches a mature stage and we refer to, for example,
[
19
,
20

21

In this section, we first present the linear FEM for problem (
1
) to reach the matrix eigenvalue problem.
We then show various numerical examples to demonstrate the spectral properties of the fractional diffusion operator.

7.1
The linear finite element method

The variational formulation of (
1
) at the continuous level is to find the eigenpair
(\lambda\in\mathbb{C},u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega))
with
\|u\|_{\Omega}=1
such that

B[u,w]=\lambda(u,w)_{\Omega},\quad\forall\ w\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega),

(53)

where the sesquilinear form is defined in (
21
).
The eigenvalue problem (
53
) is equivalent to the original problem (
1
).
They have a countable set of eigenvalues and an associated set of eigenfunctions
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.
When
\alpha=\beta
, the bilinear form is symmetric and both matrices
K
and
M
are symmetric.
In this case, the eigenfunctions can be orthnormalized,
meaning
(u_{j},u_{k})_{\Omega}=\delta_{jk},
where
\delta_{jk}=1
is the Kronecker delta.
In our numerical study, we always sort the eigenvalues accordingly to their magnitudes in ascending order.
Each eigenvalue is paired with the corresponding eigenfunctions.

At the discrete level, we first partition the domain
\Omega=[0,1]
into a grid with nodes
\{0=x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{N}=1\}
.
Let
\tau_{j}=[x_{j-1},x_{j}]
and
\mathcal{T}_{h}
denote a general element and its collection, respectively, such that
\overline{\Omega}=\cup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\tau
.
Also, let
h_{\tau}=\text{diameter}(\tau)
and
h=\max_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{\tau}
.
For simplicity, we use a mesh with
N
uniform elements.
Thus,
h=1/N
and
x_{j}=jh,j=0,1,\cdots,N
.
With this in mind, the linear FEM space is

V^{h}=\{w\in C^{0}(\Omega):w|_{\tau}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\tau),w(0)=w(1)=0,\forall\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

(54)

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}
is a space of linear polynomials.
The linear FEM of (
1
) or equivalently (
53
) seeks an eigenvalue
\lambda^{h}
and its associated eigenfunction
u^{h}\in V^{h}
with
\|u^{h}\|_{\Omega}=1
such that

B[u^{h},w^{h}]=\lambda^{h}(u^{h},w^{h})_{\Omega},\quad\forall\ w^{h}\in V^{h}.

(55)

The linear FEM space is characterized by a set of basis functions which are the so-called “hat" functions
\phi_{j},j=1,2,\cdots,N-1
(the first one when
j=0
and the last one when
j=N
are removed to satisfy the homogeneous boundary condition). Thus,
V^{h}=\{\phi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N-1}
.
With this characterization, an eigenfunction can be written as a linear combination of the basis functions, i.e.,

u^{h}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}U_{j}\phi_{j}.

(56)

At the algebraic level, substituting all the basis functions for
w^{h}
in (
55
) leads to the generalized matrix eigenvalue problem (GMEVP)

KU=\lambda^{h}MU,

(57)

where
K_{kl}=B[\phi_{l},\phi_{k}],M_{kl}=(\phi_{l},\phi_{k})_{\Omega},
and
U
is the eigenvector representing the coefficients of the basis functions.
In general, the matrix
K
is dense while the mass matrix
M
is sparse.
Once the matrix eigenvalue problem (
57
) is solved, we sort the eigenpairs in ascending order.

8
Conclusion

In this work, we study various spectral properties of problem (
1
) involving both left- and right-sided Riemann-Liouville derivatives. This fractional diffusion operator is nonlocal and non-symmetric, which brings a challenge for doing analysis and requires developing new tools and framework. We find that all the possible eigenvalues are located at the core on the complex plane given by
|\Arg\lambda|\leq\frac{|\beta-\alpha|\pi}{2}
, and we also prove that there exists at least one real eigenvalue. All these results on the distribution of eigenvalues are confirmed in our numerical experiments. However, many other important questions remain open such as the simplicity of eigenvalues, the existence of complex eigenvalues with non-zero imaginary parts (numerically observed, but theoretical justification is missing), the finitude of real eigenfunctions, etc.

Combing our current results and other elliptic results established in previous work
[
11
]
, we see that the classic elliptic theory has its counterparts for problem (
1
), however, exhibiting new interesting features and differences in the distribution of eigenvalues and the graphs of eigenfunctions. By constructing the suitable sesquilinear form, we can conduct numerical experiments with the finite element method, visualize the distribution of eigenvalues, and plot eigenfunctions from different angles. The numerical analysis supports our analytical results and, more importantly, suggests that there are only finitely many real eigenvalues except the symmetric case
\alpha=\beta
and the number of real eigenpairs increase as either
\alpha+\beta
increases or
\alpha
and
\beta
are getting closer (or both). These instructive numerical observations provide insights into the asymptotic behavior of the spectral problem (
1
) and deserve further theoretical exploration in the future.

Appendix A
Definitions of fractional Riemann-Liouville integrals and derivatives and Poincaré-Friedrichs inequality

We follow
[
16
]
and list the definition of the fractional Riemann-Liouville integrals and derivatives as below.

Definition 5

Let
t\geq 0
and
n=\lceil t\rceil
be the smallest integer such that
n>t
. The left-sided Riemann-Liouville integral and derivative are formally defined as

({\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{t}}u)(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t)}\int_{a}^{x}\frac{u(t)dt}{(x-t)^{1-t}},\,\,\,({\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{t}}u)(x):=\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{n-t}}u,\quad x>a.

Similarly, the right-sided Riemann-Liouville integral and derivative are defined as

({\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{t}}u)(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(t)}\int_{x}^{b}\frac{u(t)dt}{(t-x)^{1-t}},\quad({\mathcal{D}_{b-}^{t}}u)(x):=\left(-\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}{\mathcal{I}_{b-}^{n-t}}u,\quad x<b.

For the sake of notational convenience, if
a=-\infty
, we write

{\mathcal{I}_{+}^{t}}:={\mathcal{I}^{t}_{-\infty+}},\quad{\mathcal{D}_{+}^{t}}:={\mathcal{D}^{t}_{-\infty+}},

respectively; and if
b=+\infty
, write

{\mathcal{I}_{-}^{t}}:={\mathcal{I}^{t}_{+\infty-}},\quad{\mathcal{D}_{-}^{t}}:={\mathcal{D}^{t}_{+\infty-}},

respectively.

Property 4

(Fractional Poincaré-Friedrichs)
Given
s>0
and bounded interval
\Omega=(a,b)
, there holds

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{(b-a)^{s}}{s\Gamma(s)}|u|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}

for any
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
.

Proof 9

Given
u\in\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
, by
3
,
u
can be represented as

{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{s}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad u(x)={\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{s}}{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{s}}u.

By the boundedness of
{\mathcal{I}_{a+}^{s}}
(eq. (2.72), p. 48,
[
16
]
), we have

\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{(b-a)^{s}}{s\Gamma(s)}\|{\mathcal{D}_{a+}^{s}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{(b-a)^{s}}{s\Gamma(s)}|u|_{\widehat{H}_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}.

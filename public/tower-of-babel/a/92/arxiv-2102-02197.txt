Contributions to Z ​ Z ​ V ∗ ZZV^{\ast} ( V = γ , Z , Z ′ V=\gamma,Z,Z^{\prime} ) couplings from C ​ P CP violating flavor changing couplings
A. I. Hernández-Juárez e1,addr1, A. Moyotl e2,addr2, G. Tavares-Velasco e3,addr1

Abstract
The one-loop contributions to the trilinear neutral gauge boson couplings Z ​ Z ​ V ∗ ZZV^{\ast} ( V = γ , Z , Z ′ V=\gamma,Z,Z^{\prime} ), parametrized in terms of one C ​ P CP -conserving f 5 V f_{5}^{V} and one C ​ P CP -violating f 4 V f_{4}^{V} form factors, are calculated in models with C ​ P CP -violating flavor changing neutral current couplings mediated by the Z Z gauge boson and an extra neutral gauge boson Z ′ Z^{\prime} . Analytical results are presented in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions. Constraints on the vector and axial couplings of the Z Z gauge boson | g V ​ Z t ​ u | < 0.0096 \left|g_{{VZ}}^{tu}\right|<0.0096 and | g V ​ Z t ​ c | < 0.011 \left|g_{{VZ}}^{tc}\right|<0.011 are obtained from the current experimental data on the t → Z ​ q t\rightarrow Zq decays. It is found that in the case of the Z ​ Z ​ γ ∗ ZZ\gamma^{\ast} vertex the only non-vanishing form factor is f 5 γ f_{5}^{\gamma} , which can be of the order of 10 − 3 10^{-3} , whereas for the Z ​ Z ​ Z ∗ ZZZ^{\ast} vertex both form factors f 5 Z f_{5}^{Z} and f 4 Z f_{4}^{Z} are non-vanishing and can be of the order of 10 − 6 10^{-6} and 10 − 5 10^{-5} , respectively. Our estimates for f 5 γ f_{5}^{\gamma} and f 5 Z f_{5}^{Z} are smaller than those predicted by the standard model, where f 4 Z f_{4}^{Z} is absent up to the one loop level. We also estimate the Z ​ Z ​ Z ′ ∗ ZZ{Z^{\prime}}^{*} form factors arising from both diagonal and non-diagonal Z ′ Z^{\prime} couplings within a few extension models. It is found that in the diagonal case f 5 Z ′ f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}} is the only non-vanishing form factor and its real and imaginary parts can be of the order of 10 − 1 − 10 − 2 10^{-1}-10^{-2} and 10 − 2 − 10 − 3 10^{-2}-10^{-3} , respectively, with the dominant contributions arising from the light quarks and leptons. In the non-diagonal case f 5 Z ′ f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}} can be of the order of 10 − 4 10^{-4} , whereas f 4 Z ′ f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}} can reach values as large as 10 − 7 − 10 − 8 10^{-7}-10^{-8} , with the largest contributions arising from the Z ′ ​ t ​ q Z^{\prime}tq couplings.

;

;

;
Sirunyan:2020pub

ZZV^{\ast}
and
Z\gamma V^{\ast}
(
V=Z
,

Gounaris:2000tb
;
Choudhury:2000bw

;

;

, the

, models with axial and vector fermion couplings

, models with extended scalar sectors

Larios:2000ni

;

;

. Even more, study of the potential effects of TNGBCs at future colliders has been the source of renewed interest very recently

;
Rahaman:2016pqj
;
Behera:2018ryv
;
Rahaman:2018ujg
;
Yilmaz:2021qnv

;
Larios:2000ni
;
Gounaris:2000dn

CP

CP

CP
violation

;
Gounaris:2000dn
. In the SM,
CP
violation is generated via the Cabbibo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (CKM) mixing matrix, though the respective amount is not enough to explain the asymmetry between matter and anti-matter in the universe,
i.e.
the so-called baryogenesis problem. Therefore, new sources of
CP

Sakharov:1967dj
. In this work we are interested in the study of possible
CP
-violating effects in the TNGBCs via tree-level flavor changing neutral currents (FCNCs) mediated by the
Z

;
Mohanta:2005gm

Z
-mediated FCNC couplings on
CP
-conserving TNGBCs have already been studied
Gounaris:2000tb
, nevertheless, possible contributions to the
CP
-violating ones have not been reported yet to our knowledge. These new contributions are worth studying as they could shed some light in the path to a more comprehensive SM extension.

Possible evidences of new heavy gauge boson have been searched for at the LHC by the CMS collaboration
Sirunyan:2019jbg
, which has been useful to set bounds on the masses of new neutral and charged heavy vector bosons. Such particles are predicted by a plethora of SM extensions with extended gauge sector, for instance, little Higgs models
Perelstein:2005ka
;
delAguila:2011wk
, 331 models
Dong:2014wsa
, left-right symmetric models
Mohapatra:1974gc

Z^{\prime}

CP
-violating contributions to
VZZ^{\prime\ast}
couplings (
V=\gamma
,
Z
) are possible. To our knowledge TNGBCs with new neutral bosons have not received much attention in the literature up to now, though decays of the kind

(

,

)

and
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow\gamma A_{H}

CortesMaldonado:2011dv

,

Theoretical framework

2.1
Trilinear neutral gauge boson couplings

We now turn to discuss the Lorentz structure of TNGBCs, which are induced by dimension-six and dimension-eight operators. In this work we only focus on the contribution of dimension-six operators as it is expected to be the dominant one. In particular, the TNGBC
ZZV^{*}
(
V=\gamma
,
Z
) coupling can be parametrized by two form factors:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ZZV^{\ast}}^{\alpha\beta\mu}\left(p_{1},p_{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i(q^{2}-m_{V}^{2})}{m_{Z}^{2}}\Big[f_{4}^{V}\left(q^{\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}+q^{\beta}g^{\mu\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle-f_{5}^{V}\epsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\rho}\left(p_{1}-p_{2}\right)_{\rho}\Big],

(1)

where we have followed Ref.
Gounaris:2000tb
, with the notation for the gauge boson four-momenta being depicted in Fig.
1
. From Eq. (
2.1
) it is evident that when the
V^{*}
gauge boson becomes on-shell (
q^{2}=m_{V}^{2}
),
\Gamma_{ZZV^{\ast}}^{\alpha\beta\mu}\left(p_{1},p_{2},q\right)
vanishes, which is due to Bose statistics and angular momentum conservation. The general form of this vertex for three off-shell gauge bosons can be found in
Gounaris:2000dn
;
Larios:2000ni
. The form factor
f_{5}^{V}
is
CP
-conserving, whereas
f_{4}^{V}
is
CP
-violating. The former is the only one induced at the one-loop level in the SM via a fermion loop since
W^{\pm}
boson loops give vanishing contributions
Gounaris:2000tb
. It was found that
f_{5}^{V}
decreases quickly as
q^{2}
becomes large
Gounaris:2000tb
. The current bounds on the form factors
f_{4}^{V}
and
f_{5}^{V}
(
V=Z
,
\gamma

\sqrt{s}=13

\displaystyle-0.00066<

\displaystyle f_{4}^{Z}<0.0006,

(2)

\displaystyle-0.00055<

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z}<0.00075,

\displaystyle-0.00078<

\displaystyle f_{4}^{\gamma}<0.00071,

\displaystyle-0.00068<

\displaystyle f_{5}^{\gamma}<0.00075.

(5)

These constrains are of the order of the SM prediction for the

f_{4}^{V}
(
V=\gamma
,

As far as TNGBCs with new gauge bosons are concerned, they remain almost unexplored. For our purpose, following Eq. (
2.1

\displaystyle\Gamma_{ZZ{Z^{\prime}}^{\ast}}^{\alpha\beta\mu}\left(p_{1},p_{2},q\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{iq^{2}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\Big[f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}\left(q^{\alpha}g^{\mu\beta}+q^{\beta}g^{\mu\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle-f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}\epsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\rho}\left(p_{1}-p_{2}\right)_{\rho}\Big],

(6)

where we only consider the contributions of the dimension-six operators given in Ref.

. Of course, the form factor

(

Beyond the SM, there are some extension theories that allow FCNC couplings mediated by the
Z
gauge boson
Buchalla:2000sk
;
Mohanta:2005gm
. Such an interaction can be expressed by the following Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2s_{W}c_{W}}Z^{\mu}\overline{F}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}\left({g^{i}_{VZ}}-\gamma^{5}g^{i}_{AZ}\right)F_{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{e}{2s_{W}c_{W}}Z^{\mu}\overline{F}_{i}\gamma_{\mu}\left({g^{ij}_{VZ}}-\gamma^{5}{g^{ij}_{AZ}}\right)F_{j}{,}

(7)

where
F_{i,j}
are SM fermions in the mass eigenbasis. Here
g^{i}_{VZ,AZ}
are the diagonal SM couplings, whereas the non-diagonal couplings
g^{ij}_{VZ,AZ}

(i\neq j)
will be taken as complex since we are interested in the
CP
-violating contribution. The latter must fulfil
g^{ij\ast}_{VZ,AZ}=g^{ji}_{VZ,AZ}
because of their hermiticity. It is also customary to express the Lagrangian of Eq. (
2.2

P_{L}
and
P_{R}
, with the chiral couplings denoted by
\epsilon^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}
, which are given in terms of the vector and vector-axial couplings
g^{ij}_{VZ,AZ}
as follows

g^{ij}_{VZ,AZ}=\frac{\epsilon^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\pm\epsilon^{Z}_{R_{ij}}}{2}.

(8)

Below we will use both the
g^{ij}_{VZ,AZ}
and
\epsilon^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}
parametrizations. The former is useful for the purpose of comparison with previous works, whereas the latter is best suited for our numerical analysis.

In this case there are 4 contributing Feynman diagrams, but due to gauge invariance we only need to calculate the amplitude of diagram
2

{\cal M}^{\alpha\beta\mu}_{2}

f_{i}\leftrightarrow f_{j}

{\cal M}^{\alpha\beta\mu}_{2}

{\cal M}^{\alpha\beta\mu}_{2}(f_{i}\leftrightarrow f_{j})

Z

f_{5}^{\gamma}
, which can be written as

f_{5}^{\gamma}=-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{N_{i}Q_{i}e^{2}m_{Z}^{2}{\rm Re}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)}{8\pi^{2}s_{W}^{2}c_{W}^{2}\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right)^{2}q^{2}}R_{ij},

(16)

where
m_{i}
,
N_{i}
and
Q_{i}
are the mass, color number and electric charge of the fermion
f_{i}
. Note that
Q_{j}=Q_{i}
since we are considering neutral currents. The analytical expression for
R_{ij}
is somewhat cumbersome and is presented in
A
in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions. We have verified that Eq. (
16
) reduces to that reported in Ref.
Gounaris:2000tb
for real FCNC couplings of the
Z
gauge boson.

3.1.1
Asymptotic behavior

It is straightforward to obtain the high-energy limit
q^{2}\gg m_{i}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}

f_{5}^{\gamma}\approx-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{e^{2}Q_{i}N_{i}m_{Z}^{2}{\rm Re}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)}{4\pi^{2}q^{2}c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}},

(17)

which agrees up to terms of the order
q^{-2}
with the one reported in
Gounaris:2000tb
for the
CP
-conserving case (
m_{i}=m_{j}
), though we must consider a factor of
1/2
as we are counting twice the number of Feynman diagrams (our results include the contribution of the diagrams with the exchange
f_{i}\leftrightarrow f_{j}
). It is evident that
f_{5}^{\gamma}\rightarrow 0
in the high-energy limit as required by unitarity.

In the scenario where an ultra heavy fermion runs into the loop
m_{i}^{2}\gg q^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
must be worked out more carefully as the expansion of the two- and three-point scalar Passarino-Veltman functions around small
m_{j}
diverge. This scenario could arise in 331 model
Ferreira:2011hm
or little Higgs models
delAguila:2011wk
, for instance, where new heavy quarks and neutrinos are predicted.

3.2
ZZZ^{\ast}
coupling

The calculation of this coupling is more intricate than the previous one since there are 36 contributing Feynman diagrams, though we
only need to calculate the three generic Feynman diagrams of Fig.
2
, which by Bose symmetry must be complemented with the diagrams obtained by performing six permutations of four-momenta and Lorentz indices as well as the exchange of the fermions running into the loops. In this case there are both
CP
-violating and
CP
-conserving form factors. The former is due to the fact that the virtual boson is assumed to have complex FCNC couplings. As for the
CP
-conserving form factor
f^{Z}_{5}
, it can be written as

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{e^{2}N_{i}m_{Z}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}c_{W}^{3}s_{W}^{3}\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{g^{i}_{{AZ}}\left(\left|g_{{AZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}+\left|g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}\right)\left(R_{1ij}+R_{2ij}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle+2g^{i}_{{VZ}}{\rm Re}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)\left(R_{1ij}-R_{2ij}\right)

\displaystyle+g^{i}_{{AZ}}\left[\left|g_{{AZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}-\left|g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}\right]R_{3ij}+\left(i\leftrightarrow j\right)\Big\},

(19)

where the
R_{kij}
(
k=1,2,3
) functions are presented in
A
in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions.
This results is in agreement with that reported in Ref.
Gounaris:2000tb
for real FCNC couplings of the
Z
gauge boson.

As for the
CP
-violating form factor
f_{4}^{Z}
, it reads

\displaystyle f_{4}^{Z}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{N_{i}e^{2}m_{i}m_{j}m_{Z}^{2}}{24\pi^{2}c_{W}^{3}s_{W}^{3}\left(q^{2}-m_{Z}^{2}\right)\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right)q^{2}}

\displaystyle\times{\rm Im}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)g^{i}_{{AZ}}S_{ij},

(20)

where
S_{ij}
is presented in
A
. It is easy to see that
f_{4}^{Z}
vanishes for real couplings, which is also true if we consider the same fermion running into the loop (
i=j
). Thus, a non-vanishing
f_{4}^{Z}
requires complex FCNC couplings. Furthermore, we can also see that we need different complex phases for
g_{{VZ}}^{ij}
and
g_{{AZ}}^{ij}
to obtain a non-vanishing
CP
-violating form factor. Since
f_{4}^{Z}
is proportional to
m_{i}m_{j}
we expect that the main contribution comes from FCNC couplings associated with the top quark. We would like to stress that the result of Eq. (
3.2
) has never been reported in the literature.

3.2.1
Asymptotic behavior

As we did it with the
ZZ\gamma^{\ast}
vertex, we study the high-energy limit
q^{2}\gg m_{i}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z}

\displaystyle\approx-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{e^{2}N_{i}m_{Z}^{2}}{8\pi^{2}q^{2}c_{W}^{3}s_{W}^{3}}\Big(g_{{AZ}}^{i}\left(\left|g_{{AZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}+\left|g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2g_{{VZ}}^{i}{\rm Re}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)\Big).

(21)

Our result for
f_{5}^{Z}
also reproduces the one reported in Ref.
Gounaris:2000tb
for the
CP
-conserving case (
m_{i}=m_{j}
), though this time we must consider a factor of 1/6 as we are considering twice the three kinds of Feynman diagrams of Fig.
2
by the exchange
m_{i}\leftrightarrow m_{j}
. In ths case we also observe that
f_{5}\rightarrow 0
in the high-energy limit, which is consistent with unitarity. The same is true for the
CP
-violating form factor
f_{4}^{Z}
, which behaves in the high-energy limit as
f_{4}^{Z}\sim 1/q^{4}
, since
S_{ij}\sim q^{2}
in this limit.

In the case
m_{i}^{2}\gg q^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
, both form factors diverge similar as in the case of the
ZZ\gamma^{\ast}
vertex.

3.3
ZZZ^{\prime*}
coupling

For the sake of completeness we now consider a
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson with complex FCNCs couplings and calculate the corresponding contributions to the TNGBC
ZZZ^{\prime*}
. We first present the diagonal case, where there is no flavor violation. Since
m_{i}=m_{j}
, there is only one independent diagram in Fig.
2
and we only need to add one extra diagram obtained after the exchange
p_{1\mu}\leftrightarrow p_{2\nu}
. After some algebra, the
CP
-conserving form factor
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
reads

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\frac{eN_{i}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}q^{2}\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right){}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{g_{{AZ^{\prime}}}^{i}\Big[(g_{{VZ}}^{i})^{2}\ L_{1i}+(g_{{AZ}}^{i})^{2}\ L_{2i}\Big]

\displaystyle+g_{{VZ^{\prime}}}^{i}\ g_{{VZ}}^{i}\ g_{{AZ}}^{i}\ L_{3i}\Big\},

(22)

where the
L_{ji}
(
j=1
, 2, 3) functions are presented in
A
. The
CP
-violating form factor
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
is not induced at the one-loop level in this scenario.

As far as the non-diagonal case with complex FCNCs couplings, it requires more effort. Apart from the three generic Feynman diagram of Fig.
2
, we must add those diagrams obtained after the exchanges
p_{1\mu}\leftrightarrow p_{2\nu}
and
f_{1}\leftrightarrow f_{2}
, so there are 12 contributing Feynman diagrams in total. However, we only need to calculate the amplitudes of the three generic diagrams. In this scenario both
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
and
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
form factors are non-vanishing. The
CP
-conserving form factor can be written as

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{eN_{i}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}q^{2}\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{2g_{{AZ}}^{i}\Big[{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij\ast}\right)U_{1ij}

\displaystyle+{\rm Re}\left(g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)U_{2ij}\Big]+2g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{i}{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)U_{3ij}

\displaystyle+2g_{{VZ}}^{i}\Big[{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij\ast}\right)U_{4ij}+{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)U_{5ij}\Big]

\displaystyle+g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{i}\Big[\left|g_{{AZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}U_{6ij}+\left|g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}U_{7ij}\Big]\Big\},

(23)

whereas the
CP
-violating one reads

\displaystyle f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{eN_{i}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{12\pi^{2}c_{W}^{2}s_{W}^{2}q^{6}\left(q^{2}-4m_{Z}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{g_{VZ}^{i}\Big[{\rm Im}\left(g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{VZ}^{ij\ast}\right)T_{1ij}+{\rm Im}\left(g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)T_{2ij}\Big]

\displaystyle+g_{{AZ}^{\prime}}^{i}{\rm Im}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)T_{3ij}

\displaystyle+g_{AZ}^{i}\Big[{\rm Im}\left(g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{VZ}^{ij\ast}\right)T_{4ij}+{\rm Im}\left(g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)T_{5ij}\Big]\Big\},

(24)

where the
U_{kij}
(
k=1\ldots 7
) and
T_{kij}
(
k=1\ldots 5
) functions are presented in
A
. We note that

f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
(
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
) depends only on the real (imaginary) part of the combinations of products of the vector and axial couplings. We also note that it is not necessary that both
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
gauge bosons have simultaneously complex FCNC couplings to induce the
CP
-violating form factor.

3.3.1
Asymptotic behavior

In the diagonal case the form factor
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
can be written in the high-energy limit
q^{2}\gg m_{i}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}
as

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\sum_{i}\frac{e^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}N_{i}}{32\pi^{2}c_{W}^{3}s_{W}^{3}q^{2}}\Big\{g_{{AZ^{\prime}}}^{i}\left((g_{{AZ}}^{i})^{2}+(g_{{VZ}}^{i})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2g_{{AZ}}^{i}\ g_{{VZ}}^{i}\ g_{{VZ^{\prime}}}^{i}\Big\},

(25)

whereas in the non-diagonal case we obtain

\displaystyle f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\simeq-\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{e^{2}m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}N_{i}}{16\pi^{2}c_{W}^{3}s_{W}^{3}q^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big\{g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{i}\left(\left|g_{{AZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}+\left|g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2\Big[g_{AZ}^{i}\Big({\rm Re}\left(g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)+{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij\ast}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle+g_{VZ}^{i}\Big({\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)+{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{AZ^{\prime}}^{ij\ast}\right)\Big)

\displaystyle+g_{VZ^{\prime}}^{i}{\rm Re}\left(g_{VZ}^{ij}g_{AZ}^{ij\ast}\right)\Big]\Big\}.

(26)

We note that in both scenarios
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}\sim m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}/q^{2}
, thereby decreasing quickly when
q^{2}\geqslant m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
. However this effect is attenuated for
q^{2}\lesssim m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}
due to the mass of the heavy
Z^{\prime}
boson. We also note that Eq. (
3.3.1
) reduces to Eq. (
3.3.1
) except by a factor of 6, which is due to the fact that the
CP
-violating form factor receives the contribution of twelve Feynman diagrams in the diagonal scenario instead of two as in the diagonal case.

On the other hand, the
CP
-violating form factor
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
is of the order of
m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}/q^{4}
in the high energy limit and decreases quickly as
q^{2}
increases. The functions
T_{kij}
behave in this limit as
T_{kij}\sim q^{4}
, therefore the form factor
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
as functions of
q^{2}
has the form
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}\sim 1/q^{4}
, which is similar to the vertex
ZZZ^{\ast}
in the high energy limit.

Furthermore, when
m_{i}^{2}\gg q^{2}
,
m_{Z}^{2}
,
m_{j}^{2}
both form factors also show the same behavior observed in the case of the vertices
ZZ\gamma^{\ast}
and
ZZZ^{\ast}
.

5
Numerical Analysis

We now turn to present the numerical evaluation of the TNGBCs. For the numerical analysis we evaluate the Passarino-Veltman scalar functions via the LoopTools
Hahn:1998yk
package and independently by the Collier
Denner:2016kdg
package, which give a good agreement. We first analyze the case of the
ZZV^{*}
(
V=\gamma,Z
) couplings. As a matter of convenience, we write the complex chiral FCNC
Z
couplings as

\epsilon^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}=\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}+i\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}},

(36)

where the bar (tilde) denotes the real (imaginary) part of each coupling. The
CP
-violating phase can then be written as

\arctan\left(\phi_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}\right)=\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}}{\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}}.

(37)

We thus can write the real and imaginary terms that enter into the
f_{4}^{V}
and
f_{5}^{V}
(
V=\gamma,Z,Z^{\prime}
) form factors [Eqs. (
16
)-(
3.2
)] as follows

\left|g_{{VZ},AZ}^{ij}\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\left(\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\pm\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\pm\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}\right)^{2}\right),

(38)

\displaystyle 2{\rm Re}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\left(\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\right)^{2}-\left(\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}\right)^{2}+\left(\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\left(\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}\right)^{2}\Big),

(39)

2{\rm Im}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{ij\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{ij}\right)=\left(\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}-\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}\right).

(40)

Below we will analyze the behavior of the
f_{4,5}^{V}
form factors as functions of the
\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}
and
\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}
parameters as well as the transfer momentum
q^{2}
of the
V
gauge boson.

5.1
ZZ\gamma^{\ast}
coupling

It is convenient to assume small phases of the FCNC
Z
couplings, namely, we consider that the imaginary parts of the left-handed couplings are smaller than ten percent of their real parts:

\phi_{L_{ij}}\simeq\frac{\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}}{\overline{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij}}}\leq O\left(10^{-1}\right),

(41)

whereas for the right-handed couplings we assume by simplicity that
\phi_{R_{ij}}=0
(
\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{R_{ij}}=0
).
As far as the size of the chiral couplings
\tilde{\epsilon}^{Z}_{L_{ij},R_{ij}}
we consider the bounds shown in Table
3
to obtain an estimate of
f_{5}^{\gamma}
, which is the only non-vanishing
ZZ\gamma^{*}
form factor.

We show the behavior of the FCNC contributions to
f_{5}^{\gamma}
as a function of the photon transfer momentum
q^{2}
in Fig.

6
Conclusions and outlook

We have presented a calculation of the TNGBCs
ZZV^{\ast}
(
V=\gamma
,
Z
,
Z^{\prime}
) in models where FCNCs couplings mediated by the
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
gauge bosons are allowed. These TNGBCs are given in terms of one
CP
-conserving form factor
f_{5}^{V}
and another
CP
-violating one
f_{4}^{V}
, for which we present analytical results in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions. Such results reduce to the contributions with diagonal
Z
couplings already studied in the literature. To asses the behavior of
f_{4}^{V}
and
f_{5}^{V}
, for the numerical analysis we obtain constraints on the FCNCs couplings of the
Z
gauge boson to up quarks, which are the less constrained by experimental data: it is found that the current constraints on the
t\rightarrow qZ
branching ratios obtained at the LHC translate into the following constraints on the vector and axial
Z
couplings
\left|g_{{VZ}}^{tu}\right|<0.0096
and
\left|g_{{VZ}}^{tc}\right|<0.011
. As far as the
ZZ\gamma^{\ast}
coupling is concerned, it is found that the only non-vanishing form factor is the
CP
-conserving one
f_{5}^{\gamma}
, whose real and imaginary parts are of the order of
10^{-3}
, with the dominant contributions arising from the heavier up and down quarks. On the other hand, as for the
ZZZ^{\ast}
coupling, both the
CP
-conserving and the
CP
violating form factors are non-vanishing. We consider two scenarios for the FCNC
Z
couplings (scenario I and scenario II) and find that the magnitude of the real and imaginary parts of these form factors are of the order of
|f_{5}^{Z}|\sim 10^{-6}
and
|f_{4}^{Z}|\sim|{\rm Im}\left(g_{{AZ}}^{tc\ast}g_{{VZ}}^{tc}\right)|\times 10^{-5}
, with the dominant contributions arising from the non-diagonal top quark couplings. Our estimates for the FCNC contributions to the
CP
-conserving
f_{5}^{\gamma}
and
f_{5}^{Z}
form factors are smaller than the prediction of the SM, whereas the
f_{4}^{Z}
form factor is not induced in the SM up to the one loop level.

We also consider the case of a new heavy neutral
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson with FCNCs and obtain the TNGBC
ZZ{Z^{\prime}}^{*}
, for which we present analytical results in the case of both diagonal and non-diagonal
Z^{\prime}
couplings in terms of Passarino-Veltman scalar functions. In the diagonal case we find the following numerical estimate for the
CP
-conserving
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
form factor, which is the only non-vanishing,
|{\rm Re}f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}|\sim 10^{-1}-10^{-2}
and
|{\rm Im}f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}|\sim 10^{-2}-10^{-3}
, with the dominant contributions arising from the light quarks and leptons. In the non-diagonal case we also consider two scenarios for the FCNC couplings of the
Z
gauge boson (scenario I and scenario II). It is found that both the real and imaginary parts of
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
are of the order of
10^{-4}
in scenario I, whereas in scenario II
|{\rm Re}f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}|\sim 10^{-6}
and
|{\rm Im}f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}|\sim 10^{-5}
. In general, in the non-diagonal case the magnitude of both real and imaginary parts of the
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
form factor are one order of magnitude larger for moderate energies and an ultra heavy
Z^{\prime}
gauge boson than for high energies, with the dominant contributions arising from the
Z^{\prime}tu
and
Z^{\prime}tc
couplings. The real (imaginary) part of the non-diagonal contributions to
f_{5}^{Z^{\prime}}
are at least two (one) orders of magnitude smaller than the real (imaginary) parts of the diagonal contributions.

As far as the
CP
-violating form factor
f_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}
is concerned, we obtain similar estimates for its real and imaginary parts, of the order of
10^{-7}-10^{-8}
in both scenarios of the FCNC couplings of the
Z
gauge boson. In closing we would like to stress that FCNC couplings can also yield
CP
violation in the TNGBCs of a new neutral gauge boson, which may be on interest.

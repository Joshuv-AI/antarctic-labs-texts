Holevo Cramér-Rao bound: How close can we get without entangling measurements?
Aritra Das, Lorcán O. Conlon, Jun Suzuki, Simon K. Yung, Ping K. Lam, Syed M. Assad

Abstract
In multi-parameter quantum metrology, the resource of entanglement can lead to an increase in efficiency of the estimation process. Entanglement can be used in the state preparation stage, or the measurement stage, or both, to harness this advantage—here we focus on the role of entangling measurements. Specifically, entangling or collective measurements over multiple identical copies of a probe state are known to be superior to measuring each probe individually, but the extent of this improvement is an open problem. It is also known that such entangling measurements, though resource-intensive, are required to attain the ultimate limits in multi-parameter quantum metrology and quantum information processing tasks. In this work we investigate the maximum precision improvement that collective quantum measurements can offer over individual measurements, calling this the ‘collective quantum enhancement’. We show that, whereas the maximum enhancement can, in principle, be a factor of n n for estimating n n parameters, this bound is not tight for large n n . Instead, our results prove an enhancement linear in dimension of the qudit probe is possible using collective measurements and lead us to conjecture that this is the maximum collective quantum enhancement in any local estimation scenario.

Background: Precision Bounds for Parameter Estimation

In this section, we present a brief recap on
quantum parameter estimation
and introduce
precision bounds
for the collective and the individual
measurement scenarios.
The general recipe
to estimate
n
parameters
\theta\coloneqq\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

(where we define
[n]\coloneqq\{1,\dots,n\}

and denote by
\Theta
the set of all possible parameter values)
of a quantum state
\rho_{\theta}

belonging to a
d
-dimensional Hilbert
space
\mathcal{H}_{d}

involves two steps.
First, one performs quantum measurements,
generally positive operator-valued measures (POVMs)
\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l\in[m]}

with
m
outcomes, on
\rho_{\theta}
.
Second, a classical estimator operator
\hat{\theta}_{jl}

is constructed that assigns an estimated value
to
\theta_{j}
for each measurement outcome
l\in[m]
,
which occurs with probability
p_{l}\coloneqq\Tr(\rho_\theta\Pi_l)
.
Here
\Tr
(in serif font) denotes
tracing over the quantum system.

The performance of the estimator
is quantified via its
mean squared error (MSE) matrix

(V_{\theta})_{jk}\coloneqq\sum_{l\in[m]}(\hat{\theta}_{jl}-\theta_{j})(\hat{\theta}_{kl}-\theta_{k})p_{l}\,,

(1)

the trace of which gives the
total average squared deviation
\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})=\sum_{l,j}(\hat{\theta}_{jl}-\theta_{j})^{2}p_{l}
.
Here
\mathsf{Tr}
(in sans serif font)
denotes tracing over the classical
or parameter indices.
In this work, we focus on
the local setting,
where the parameters of interest
are close to
their true values,
\theta^{*}\coloneqq\{\theta_{j}^{*}\}_{j\in[n]}
,
i.e.,
\theta\approx\theta^{*}
.
For locally-unbiased (LUB) estimators,
which have zero bias at the true parameter
values,
V_{\theta}

is equivalent to the covariance matrix
of parameter estimates
and
\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})
is simply the
sum of variances of each parameter.

Precision bounds lower-bound
the uncertainties
in estimating multiple (possibly) incompatible parameters.
In this work, we focus on precision bounds on
\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})
;
the classical CRB yields a lower bound to this via

V_{\theta}\succcurlyeq J^{-1}\implies\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})\geq\mathsf{Tr}(J^{-1})\,,

(2)

where
A\succcurlyeq B
denotes positive semi-definiteness
of
A-B
, and
J\equiv J(\rho_{\theta},\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l\in[m]})
is the classical Fisher information (CFI) matrix.
The CFI
(defined later in Eq. (
40
))
is best understood as a measure
on the parameter space
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

of the local sensitivity of measurements
\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l\in[m]}

towards each
\theta_{j}
when measuring state
\rho_{\theta}
.
Minimising
\mathsf{Tr}(J^{-1})

in Eq. (
2
)
over all possible measurements
\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l\in[m]}

yields the tight
MICRB
[
36
,
38
,
39
]
,

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}\coloneqq\min_{\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l\in[m]}}\mathsf{Tr}(J^{-1})\,,

(3)

stipulating the ultimate precision attainable
via individual measurements.
A recent reformulation of the MICRB
[
39
]

recasts Eq. (
3
) into a conic program
(see Eq. (
147
) for definition)
and presents SDP lower bounds to it.
However, neither closed-form expressions
nor efficient numerical techniques
are known for evaluating the conic program
(the SDP approximation’s complexity
scales as
d^{12}
for qudit tomography)
and analytic solutions are known
only for qubit models,
single-parameter problems,
and Gaussian models
[
39
]
.

A different precision bound on
\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})

for the separable-measurement case,
developed by Nagaoka
[
37
]

and Hayashi
[
16
]
,
is the NHCRB,

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbb{L},\mathbb{X}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\;|\;\mathbb{L}\succcurlyeq\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb\displaystyle L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}\;\mathrm{Hermitian}\,\Big\}-\mathsf{Tr}\left(\theta\theta^{\top}\right).

Here
\mathbb{X}\coloneqq\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}^{\top}

are the Hermitian LUB operators
that satisfy (abbreviating
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta_{j}}

\partial_{j}
)

\Tr(\rho_\theta X_j)=\theta_{j}\quad\&\quad\Tr(\partial_j\rho_\theta X_k)=\delta_{jk}\,,

(5)

\mathbb{S}_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\rho_{\theta}

\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}

classical and quantum
subsystems,
(\,\cdot\,)^{\top}
denotes transpose with respect to
the classical (parameter) index,
\mathds{1}_{n}

denotes the
n
-dimensional identity matrix,
and blackboard fonts represent classical-quantum matrices.
The minimisation in Eq. (
4
) is
a conic optimisation over the extended Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}:=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}
,
combining parameter space
\mathbb{C}^{n}

and Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{d}
of the qudit.
The other optimisation variable
\mathbb{L}

is a positive operator on
\mathbb{H}

that is also symmetric under
a partial transpose with respect to the first subspace
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
so as to reduce to valid covariance matrices on
\mathbb{C}^{n}

under
\Tr[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]

[

,
39
]
.

Although
the NHCRB is not always tight (
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\leq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}
),
it is an analytically-tractable

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}

that is often provably tight (

)

,

]
,
and always efficiently-computable

for qudit tomography)
[

]
.
Further, Ref.
[

]

reformulated the tight bound and demonstrated
the NHCRB to be a good approximation to

The GMCRB, another

is defined as
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}\coloneqq\left(\mathsf{Tr}[J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1/2}]\right)^{2}/(d-1)

]
,
where

is the

The GMCRB
is generally not tight for

is known to be weaker than the SLD CRB
[

Nonetheless, the GMCRB is analytically-tractable
and, for the problem of

to be at most a factor of 2 away from

[

.

The HCRB is a collective-measurement

\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta})
,
defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\min{X}\left\{\mathsf{Tr}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\right)+\left\|\imaginary\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\right\|_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad-\mathsf{Tr}\left(\theta\theta^{\top}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]_{jk}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\Tr( \rho_\theta X_j X_k )\,,

(6)

where
\|X\|_{1}\coloneqq\Tr(\sqrt{X^\dagger X})

denotes the trace norm.
An equivalent expression for
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
written in a similar form as Eq. (

),
is

\begin{gathered}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}}{\coloneqq}\min_{\mathbb{L},\mathbb{X}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\,|\,{\Tr}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\;{\mathrm{{real},{symmetric,}}}\,\\
\Tr[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\succcurlyeq\Tr[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}]\Big\}-\mathsf{Tr}\left(\theta\theta^{\top}\right)\,.\end{gathered}

(7)

Note that the minimisations
in Eqs. (

)
and (

)
have no explicit closed-form solution

]

Besides
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}\leq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
,

{\max}\left({\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}},{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RLD}}}\right)\leq{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}}\leq{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}}\leq{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}}\,.

Here

and

and the right-logarithmic derivative (RLD) CRB

By definition,
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}

is the tightest precision bound for separable measurements,
and hence is greater than or equal to

).

[

]
.

.

]
.

in Eq. (

.
Surprisingly, a direct ratio relationship
between
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}
and
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
or even between
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
and
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}

is not known
[
59
]
,
leading to a gap in our knowledge
of the potential quantum advantage
offered by collective measurements.

3
Results

3.1
Collective Quantum Enhancement

We first define a measure of collective
enhancement for
estimation tasks.
Whereas the truly attainable
ratio of collective and separable
variances is given by
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
the analytic intractability of
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}

renders this quantity beyond the reach
of currently available tools.
Instead, we consider the ratio
between the NHCRB and the HCRB,
which provides a lower bound to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
.
Specifically, in this work,
we shall analyse
the collective quantum enhancement

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\left[\{\rho_{\theta}|\,\theta\in\Theta\}\right]\coloneqq\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\;\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}\,,

(9)

for a given quantum statistical
model
\{\rho_{\theta}|\,\theta\in\Theta\}

with
n
parameters for
d
-dimensional qudit states,
where the maximum is over
all allowed parameter values for this model.
This quantity can be interpreted as
a measure of the maximum
quantum enhancement obtainable from using collective measurements
over separable measurements for this particular model.

A further maximisation over all
quantum models with the
same number of parameters,
n
,
and for the same probe dimension,
d
,
is possible,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\coloneqq\max_{n\text{-parameter models}}\;\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\left[\{\rho_{\theta}|\,\theta\in\Theta\}\right]\,,

(10)

where we have suppressed the dependence
on
d
for conciseness.
A final maximisation over
all possible
n
for a given system dimension
d

(
1\leq n\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}
) then leads to

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq n\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}}\;\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\,,

(11)

corresponding to
the ultimate collective quantum enhancement
in precision
for this system
dimension
[
3
,
60
]
.
Here
n_{\mathrm{max}}
represents the maximum possible number
of independent parameters
and equals
2(d-1)
for pure qudits
and
d^{2}-1
for mixed qudits.
In this work we consider general mixed states
and, thus, define
n_{\mathrm{max}}\coloneqq d^{2}-1
.

Physically,
Eqs. (
10
)
& (
11
)
define model-free quantities that capture
the maximum potential advantage
of collective measurements across various quantum models,
comparing their general utility
beyond particular estimation scenarios.
Practically, these two quantities,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}
, offer insights into
the behaviour of
the maximum utility
of collective measurements
from two opposing extremes
of the number of parameters.
As depicted in Fig.
1
,
analysis using
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

reveals a maximum utility linear in
n

for small
n
whereas
analysis using
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

reveals a plateauing utility,
fixed by
d
instead of
n
,
for large
n
.
In the following we
abbreviate the model
representation
\{\rho_{\theta}|\,{\theta\in\Theta}\}

to
\{\rho_{\theta}\}

while specifying the model explicitly.

The NHCRB is generally close
to the MICRB
[
39
]
,
but whether
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}
(or
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}
) corresponds to
the true maximum collective enhancement
depends on the NHCRB’s attainability
for the model maximising
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}

in the setting of Eq. (
10
)
(Eq. (
11
)).
The non-attainability
of the NHCRB in specific
cases
[
39
]

means that the quantities
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

could be smaller than
the corresponding true
maximum collective enhancements,
denoted
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}

and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}
. These are defined
similarly to Eqs. (
9
)—(
11
)
through
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\coloneqq\max_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}[\rho_{\theta}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]
,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}\coloneqq\max_{n\text{-param. models}}\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

and

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}\coloneqq\max_{1\leq n\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}\,.

(12)

The same construction of maximum ratio quantities
applies to other choices of separable
measurement bounds,
leading to
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}

for the GMCRB, studied later
and depicted in Fig.
2
.

The quantities
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}
can only increase with
d
.
This is because of two reasons:
(i) higher dimensions allow more incompatible parameters
to estimate, and
(ii) a lower-dimensional model
can always be embedded in a higher dimension
keeping the ratio invariant.
Note that we only consider full-rank models
in defining Eqs. (
9
), (
10
),
& (
11
),
i.e., we assume
\rho_{\theta}
to be
non-singular or non-degenerate
for all
\theta\in\Theta
.
1
1

1

Singular or degenerate
states can be approximated
arbitrarily well by non-degenerate
ones via regularisation
[
50
]
.

Our definitions imply
that for any
n
-parameter model
over
d
-dimensional states,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}
.

Below, in Sec.
3.2
,
we present our analytical results.
First, in Sec.
3.2.1
,
we present our results on
the ratio quantities
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

and on
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
for the linear GMM model.
The GMM model results apply
to tomography in arbitrary ONB, and
we conjecture that
this model at
\theta=0

maximises Eq. (
11
),
thereby attaining
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}
;
we prove the attainability
of the NHCRB at this point.
Then, in Sec.
3.2.2
,
we present our analytical result
using the MICRB to upper-bound
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

for the problem of ONB tomography
and compare this to
an upper bound derived from
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}
.
Finally,
in Sec.
3.3
,
we present
our numerical results including
the extension of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
to tomography in non-orthogonal bases
and to tomography under fixed probe purity,
as well as random sampling experiments
addressing
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

for generic qudit estimation models.

3.2
Analytical Results

3.2.1
Results for NHCRB ratio

In Sec.
5.1
,
we prove
a model-independent and attainable upper bound
on the collective precision enhancement.
Specifically,
by using an upper bound to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}

[
46
,
39
]

and using
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}
to lower-bound
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
we prove that for estimating
n
independent parameters
of a quantum state, the collective enhancement
is at most a factor of
n
,
i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq n
.
We state this as
Theorem
1

(see Sec.
5.1
for proof):

Theorem 1
.

For estimating
n
parameters of any
qudit state
\rho_{\theta}
,
the collective enhancement
{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}/{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}\,{\leq}\,{n}
,
i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq n
.

The factor-of-
n
smaller
attainable precision
intuitively aligns with
the approach of dividing the
multi-parameter problem into
n

single-parameter problems,
where the SLD-optimal separable measurement for each
parameter
\theta_{j}
individually
is performed on a fraction
\nicefrac{{1}}{{n}}

of the number of available state copies.
2
2

2

However,
this does not constitute a locally-unbiased multi-parameter
estimation strategy, which must assign locally-unbiased estimates
to all the parameters in each trial.
When performing the SLD-optimal measurement for
\theta_{j}
,
the procedure for assigning locally-unbiased estimates
for
\theta_{k}
(
k\neq j
) is discussed in Ref.
[
61
]
.

Due to technical difficulties
in guaranteeing the local-unbiasedness
of parameter estimates within this approach
[
61
]
,
we instead use the analytic upper bound
to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
developed in Ref.
[
46
]

(summarised in Appendix
G
)
to prove Theorem
1
.

The upper bound in Theorem
1

can be a tight relation for
models with small
n
.
For example, a ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n=2}=2
is attained
for
d=3
by the model of estimating
the coefficients of
\lambda_{1}
&
\lambda_{2}

(see Appendix
A
for definitions)
in the maximally-mixed qutrit state,
and a ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n=3}=3
is attained
for
d=2
by the model of
estimating the three Pauli
coefficients of the maximally-mixed
qubit state
[
48
]
.
We also expect the ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

to increase with
n
, because
having more parameters to estimate
can lead to higher incompatibility—this
implies
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}
.
On the other hand,
at the
{n=n_{\mathrm{max}}=d^{2}-1}
limit,
the upper bound from
Theorem
1

would imply
an enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\leq d^{2}-1
—quadratic
in
d
—is attainable;
this is not the case,
as we subsequently establish.

In Sec.
5.2
,
we introduce the ‘linear GMM model’:
an
n_{\mathrm{max}}
-parameter family of

d
-dimensional qudit states
given by

\rho_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{d}/d+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}

[
50
]
.
Here, the
n_{\mathrm{max}}
parameters
of interest,
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}
,
are the coefficients
of the GMMs
\Lambda_{d}\coloneqq\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}
.
The GMMs
\Lambda_{d}
are traceless,
Hermitian generalisations
of the qubit Pauli operators
(see Appendix
A
)
and the parameterisation
is valid for any qudit state.
Estimating
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}

thus corresponds to tomography
in the GMM basis,
which is an ONB
given that
\Tr(\lambda_j \lambda_k)=\delta_{jk}
.
In fact,
the unweighted GMM model at any
\rho_{\theta}

is equivalent to tomography of
\rho_{\theta}

in any other ONB,
as we show using Lemma
1
in
Sec.
5.2
.
This means that the QCRBs
as well as ratios between them are invariant
to the particular choice of ONB
and the following results derived for
the GMM basis hold for any ONB.

For the GMM model,
we prove that the maximum collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

is a factor linear in dimension
d
,
as opposed to quadratic.
Specifically,
we show that the collective enhancement
is exactly
d+1
for estimating
from the maximally-mixed state,

\rho_{\mathrm{m}}=\mathds{1}_{d}/d
,
in Theorem
2

(see Sec.
5.3
for proof):

Theorem 2
.

For ONB tomography of the
maximally-mixed qudit state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
, the collective enhancement
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=d+1
.

The factor of
d+1
here previously
appeared in the relation
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}=(d+1)J

satisfied by Fisher SIC measurements for
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}

[
56
]
.
This is because for the particular
model considered in Theorem
2
,
the NHCRB is tight (see below)
and the SLD CRB equals the HCRB.
Therefore, the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}

reduces to the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}
, which equals
the proportionality constant between the
QFI
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}
and the CFI
J
.
However, the true merit of Theorem
2

lies in our novel method for its proof,
which directly extends
to upper-bound the collective enhancement
in several settings beyond Theorem
2
.
Specifically, this lets us
prove that the maximum enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

is less than
d+2
for estimating
from arbitrary states in
Theorem
3

(see Sec.
5.4
for proof):

Theorem 3
.

For ONB tomography of arbitrary
d
-dimensional qudit state
\rho_{\theta}
, the maximum collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq d+2
.

Although we prove an upper bound
of
d+2
,
based on numerical evidence
shown in Fig.
3
,
we expect the attainable bound to be
d+1

and expect
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
to be maximised
at
\theta=0
over
\Theta
.
Thus for the linear GMM model,
we propose
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq d+1
.
Further, for the maximally-mixed case,
we prove the SIC POVM
to be an optimal individual measurement.
This proves the NHCRB to be
attainable or tight in this case,
implying
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}=d+1
as well
at this maximum point.

We then treat two extensions
of the linear GMM model.
The first is the weighted version,
where an arbitrary, full-rank,
parameter-independent weight
matrix
W
is included
in the cost function (Appendix

).
Importantly,
weighted models are
equivalent to reparameterisations

i.e., estimating parameters

13

.
Our results in Appendix

prove that for the maximally-mixed state,
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
,
and for estimating any

independent parameters,

.
We also numerically show (Fig.

the collective enhancement for
estimating from
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}

under the same weight
W
.
This suggests the maximum collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}

for any full-parameter problem
to be at most
d+1
,
i.e.,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{\mathrm{max}}\text{-parameter}\\
\mathrm{models}\end{subarray}}\;\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\left[\{\rho_{\theta}\}\right]\leq d+1\,,

(13)

and this upper bound is attained by the model
studied in Sec.
5.3
.

In Sec.
5.5
,
we treat the extension of the linear
GMM model to the
{n<n_{\mathrm{max}}}
case,
assuming the remaining
{n_{\mathrm{max}}-n}

GMM coefficients to be zero.
In this case, we prove that for
estimating from
the maximally-mixed state,
the maximum collective enhancement
is
d+1
in
Theorem
4

(Sec.
5.5
):

Theorem 4
.

For estimating fewer-than-

coefficients
of GMMs of the maximally-mixed qudit state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
,
the collective enhancement
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq d+1
.

We do not analytically solve this model
for other states, or for states
with the remaining
n_{\mathrm{max}}-n

GMM coefficients non-zero.
However, the
n
bound in
Sec.

and numerical results in

suggest that the maximum enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

is non-decreasing with increasing

.
Having analysed the

that for any
{n}\,{<}\,{n_{\mathrm{max}}}

the same bound
of
{d+1}
should hold,
i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\leq d+1
.

In summary,
we find that there

Taking the example of qutrits (

3.2.2
Results for MICRB & GMCRB ratios

The results on
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

in Sec.

lower-bound the corresponding quantities
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}

and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
.
In particular,
for the linear GMM model
that addresses ONB tomography,
we have

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\geq d+1
.
Based on analytical and numerical results,
we conjecture that
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\leq d+1
;
accordingly, Theorem
2

implies
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}\geq d+1
.
In Sec.
5.6
,
we use the NHCRB solution
behind

2

to solve the MICRB for the linear
GMM model at
\theta=0
,
obtaining a ratio of
d+1
.
We then extend this solution
to upper-bound the true ratio
for arbitrary
\theta

in Theorem
5

(see Appendix

for proof):

Theorem 5
.

For ONB tomography of arbitrary
d
-dimensional qudit state
\rho_{\theta}
, the maximum true collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq d+2
.

Theorem
5
therefore
constrains the true maximum enhancement
for ONB tomography as

d+1\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq d+2\,.

(14)

A different way to quantify
collective precision enhancement
is to consider the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}
.
As noted previously, the SLD CRB
can capture collective performance
up to a factor, and the GMCRB,
though not tight for qudit problems with small
n
,
can be directly computed given the SLD QFI
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}
.
This significantly simplifies
the evaluation of the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}
,
which then upper-bounds
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
compared to the ratios considered in Sec.

.

Let us denote the maximum GMCRB-to-HCRB ratio
for a fixed model as
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
,
for any
n
-parameter
d
-dimensional model as
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}
,
and for any
d
-dimensional model as
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}

(similar to Eqs. (
9
), (
10
)
and (
11
) for the NHCRB).
A straightforward application
of the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality
to the eigenvalues
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}
of
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1/2}

(the positive square-root of the inverse of the SLDQFI matrix)
leads to

\frac{\left(\mathsf{Tr}[J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1/2}]\right)^{2}}{\mathsf{Tr}[J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1}]}=\frac{(\sum_{j}\nu_{j})^{2}}{\sum_{j}\nu_{j}^{2}}\leq n\,,

(15)

with equality attained if
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}
is a scalar matrix.
The ratio in Eq. (
15
)
proves the upper bound

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}&\coloneqq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta\\
n\text{-parameter}\\
\text{models}\,\{\rho_{\theta}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}\\
&\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in\Theta\\
n\text{-parameter}\\
\text{models}\,\{\rho_{\theta}\}\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}[\rho_{\theta}]}\leq\frac{n}{d-1}\end{split}

for any
n
-parameter
d

reducing to
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}/(d-1)=d+1

at the maximum number of parameters.
Moreover, Eq. (
16
)
is a tight bound:
a ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}=n/(d-1)
is attained
by the model of estimating any
n

GMM coefficients of the maximally-mixed state.
This is because
J_{\mathrm{SLD}}=d\,\mathds{1}_{n}

here, so that
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}=n^{2}/(d(d-1))
, dividing which
by
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}=n/d

thereby proving

In Fig.

The comparison with the NHCRB,

reveals

upper-bounding

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}

itself generally underestimates collective enhancement.

can be far from the attainable

For ONB tomography, which

\displaystyle d+1\leq

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]=2(d+1)\,.

4
Discussion

In this work,
we explored the
ratio between separable-
and collective-measurement precision bounds
in order to identify
the maximum collective quantum enhancement
in a range of settings.
Considering the ratio between
the NHCRB and the HCRB,
we established that for estimating any
n

independent parameters,
the maximum precision-enhancement
from collective measurements
can, in principle, be
n
.
However, at the maximum value of
n
,
we proved this maximum enhancement
to be only
\mathrm{O}(d)
or
\mathrm{O}(\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{max}}})
.
Specifically, for the model of qudit tomography in
the Gell-Mann basis,
we proved the maximum
collective quantum enhancement to be
d+2
,
revealing the SIC POVM to be
an individual-optimal measurement
for the maximally-mixed case.
Based on the specific examples
provided and our numerical results,
we expect the attainable maximum enhancement
to be
d+1
instead.
We also established a maximum enhancement
of
d+1

for tomography in any other basis,
i.e., for estimating any other
n_{\mathrm{max}}

parameters,
as well as for estimating fewer than
n_{\mathrm{max}}

parameters
of the maximally-mixed state.
Finally, we numerically demonstrated
a maximum enhancement in
\mathrm{O}(d)

for states of a fixed known purity.
Our work thus suggests supplementing the known ratio
result
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}\leq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}\leq 2\,\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}

with
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}\leq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\leq(d+1)\,\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
.
Throughout,
we have demonstrated our analytical findings
via numerics and figures for specific
d
.

It is, however, important to note
that, though our methodology leveraging
the NHCRB as a separable-measurement precision bound
leads to several analytical results,
the NHCRB is a substitute here
for the most informative bound
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}
.
The NHCRB can overestimate the
best attainable separable precision
whereas
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}
is tight by definition,
so the attainable maximum advantage
from collective measurements could be larger than
d+1
,
as in Eq. (
12
).
However, numerical evidence for large qudit dimensions
[
39
]

suggests the gap
(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}-\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}})/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
to be small,
so that the NHCRB-maximum ratio should be close
to the MICRB-maximum ratio
[
47
]
.
Indeed, for qudit tomography in orthonormal bases,
we prove the attainable maximum collective enhancement
to be bounded between
d+1
and
d+2
—the
same bound that applies for the NHCRB-maximum ratio.
We then compare this result to
a bound based on the GMCRB,
demonstrating that our approach
imposes a stronger constraint
on attainable enhancement.
Notably, both approaches predict
a maximum enhancement
linear in dimension, and not quadratic,
at the maximum number of parameters.
However,
beyond full-parameter models,
the NHCRB reflects
more accurately
a non-linear dependence of maximum collective enhancement
on number of parameters
n
,
whereas the GMCRB predicts a linear relationship
(Fig.
2
).
These factors justify our choice of
the NHCRB as the individual measurement precision bound.

Our choice of the Gell-Mann basis
for tomography
was motivated by its symmetry and simplicity,
apart from being a generalisation of Pauli matrices.
The orthonormality of the basis
leads to (local) parameter orthogonality of the basis coefficients
for estimating from the maximally-mixed state
[
62
]
,
making the
classical and quantum (SLD)
Fisher information matrices
diagonal.
As further captured by the theory of
Fisher SIC measurements
[
56
]
,
the two Fisher information matrices are proportional.
In general, any full-parameter model is D-invariant
[
35
,
13
,
63
]
,
leading to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RLD}}
.
Additionally, for GMM tomography,
the SLD and RLD CRBs equal
the HCRB, which is a signature of
a locally-classical model
[
13
,
63
]
.
In fact, the theory of quantum local
asymptotic normality
[
42
,
44
,
64
,
65
]

predicts that
in the asymptotic limit of number of copies,
this model becomes a completely classical Gaussian model
[
51
]
.
This theory therefore offers a physical
explanation for why collective enhancement
might be maximised in the orthonormal tomography setting:
the single-copy measurements
are subject to maximal parameter incompatibility
whereas all the parameters can be estimated
simultaneously in the asymptotic limit.

However, the significance of considering
the Gell-Mann basis cannot be overstated.
Generally, any parameter estimation problem
may be linearised about the true parameter values
as
\rho_{\theta}\approx\rho_{\theta^{*}}+\sum(\theta_{j}-\theta_{j}^{*})\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta}^{*}
,
with the partial derivatives
\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta}

necessarily traceless and Hermitian,
meaning they are combinations of GMMs
(Sec. VC in
[
23
]
).
This estimation problem can then
be linearly transformed
to the equivalent problem of
estimating some number of GMM coefficients
[
13
]
,
precisely the model solved in Sec.
5.5
.
For example, the simple result of
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=n/d

from Sec.
5.3

can be directly linearly transformed to
obtain a closed-form expression for
the HCRB for estimating any
d^{2}-1
independent parameters
from any full-rank qudit state.
Lastly,
although our main analysis
was specific to GMM tomography,
we proved
that the bounds
and their ratios
are invariant for tomography in any other orthonormal
basis for the same space.
These results lay a foundation for the future exploration
of the enhancement potential of entangling measurements
in multi-parameter quantum estimation.

Our approach in this work
was to study quantities
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}

to assess collective quantum enhancement.
The collective- and separable-optimal
precisions are identical for single-parameter problems,
but can grow increasingly farther apart
with increasing number of
parameters, disagreeing the most at the
maximum number of incompatible parameters.
Therefore, the maximum ratio of precisions
effectively
compares how parameter incompatibility
affects separable versus collective
measurement strategies.
Our results motivate defining
a finite incompatibility measure
through the gap
\delta=(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}-\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}})/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
,
which complements
the normalised gap
\Delta=(\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}-\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}})/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}

that
was connected to the asymptotic incompatibility
between parameters in Refs.
[
53
,
57
]
.
In contrast to Ref.
[
57
]
, our results
show that even if the asymptotic incompatibility
vanishes
[
53
]
,
the individual and collective attainable precisions
can still disagree maximally
and the finite incompatibility
\delta

can be as large as the dimension
d
,
a significant difference from the asymptotic case.

The results in Figs.
1
,
2

and
4

indicate that the maximum collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

increases with number of parameters,
and hence is largest for state tomography.
Moreover, for tomography, the maximum
enhancement
decreases with purity (Fig.
3
)
and is maximised by minimum-purity states.
Based on this,
we conjecture that maximum enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}

is attained for orthonormal tomography of the maximally-mixed state.
This case was studied analytically
to find a ratio of
d+1
.
Hence, we conjecture that
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\leq(d+1)\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}

for all smooth full-rank models
in the local estimation setting, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}=d+1
.
Notably, both the
n
bound (Sec.
5.1
)
and the
d+1
bound
(Secs.
5.3

&
5.4
)
can be tight,
and for qubit tomography
(Eq. (
24
)),
they are tight and identical.
Resultantly, if our conjecture holds true,
we would also expect
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\min(n,d+1)

(red line in Figs.
1
&
2
)
to hold for qudits.
For qutrits, Fig.
1

shows this conjectured
upper bound to be attainable for all
n

except
n=4
. The value of
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{4}

for qutrits
remains an open problem
(we report a ratio of 3.25
in Fig.
4
),
motivating further inquiry
into the utility of collective measurements
in multi-parameter quantum estimation.

In conclusion,
we find that for
local estimation problems
involving
n
parameters,
the optimal collective measurements
are at most
n
times more precise
than the optimal individual measurements.
Although this suggests
that a collective quantum enhancement of
n

is possible, and that
the utility of collective measurements
scales with the number of estimated parameters,
our further analysis indicates otherwise.
By taking the probe dimension
d

into account, we upper-bound
the collective enhancement
by
d+1
,
which is a tighter bound for large
n
(
n>d
).
Our investigation into the utility
of collective measurements
thus reveals a diminishing payoff
in the asymptotic limit.
Whereas collective measurements
on two copies, three copies, and so on,
are practically feasible
and outperform
the optimal individual measurements,
the optimal collective measurements
that saturate the HCRB require
entangling measurements on
asymptotically-large number of copies
but only enhance precision by a
factor at most linear in dimension,
underscoring their non-utility.

Our results apply to
multi-parameter quantum metrology
and quantum sensing, where
a judicious choice
between
measurement strategies
would be resource-wise beneficial.
Conversely, our work questions
the choice of the HCRB
when benchmarking the performance
of real-world quantum measurements,
and suggests the (finite-copy) NHCRB
as a more suitable alternative.
Investigating the advantage offered
by finite-copy collective measurements,
or extending to Bayesian settings
could offer valuable insights
into the potential of entangling measurements.

5.1
Ratio of
n

We now establish a model-agnostic
(or problem-independent)
upper bound of
n
on
the collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

Theorem 1
.

For estimating
n
parameters of any
qudit state
\rho_{\theta}
,
the collective enhancement
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq n
,

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq n\,.

(20)

Proof.

Using Ref.
[
46
]

(based on Ref.
[
39
]
)
to the NHCRB
(see summary in Appendix

),

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq\min{X}\Bigg\{&\mathsf{Tr}(\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}])\\
&+\sum_{j,k\in[n]}\left\|\rho_{\theta}[X_{j},X_{k}]\right\|_{1}\Bigg\}\,,\end{split}

(21)

and
\left\|[\rho_{\theta}[X_{j},X_{k}]\right\|_{1}\leq 1/2\Tr[\rho_{\theta}(X_{j}^{2}+X_{k}^{2})]
,
we get

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq n\min{X}\mathsf{Tr}\left(\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\right)=n\,\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}[\rho_{\theta}]\,.

(22)

On the other hand,
from Eq. (
6
),
we have
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\geq\min{X}\mathsf{Tr}(\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}])
, so that

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq n

for all smooth models
and, thus,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq n
.
∎

Note that under the assumption
of independent parameters
\theta
,
we have
n\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}

[
23
]
,
so that Eq. (
20
) implies
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}\leq n_{\mathrm{max}}=d^{2}-1
.

5.2
Model: Estimating GMMs from Qudits

We now introduce our quantum statistical model,
which is an
n_{\mathrm{max}}
-parameter family of

d
-dimensional qudit states. This model,
which we call the ‘linear GMM model’,
involves estimating the
n_{\mathrm{max}}

coefficients
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}

of the GMMs
\Lambda_{d}\coloneqq\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}

from the Bloch representation of a qudit state
[
50
]
,

\rho_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{d}/d+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}\,.

(23)

The GMMs
\Lambda_{d}
are traceless,
Hermitian generalisations
of the qubit Pauli operators
(see Appendix
A
),
and the decomposition in Eq. (
23
)
is a one-to-one map between the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{d}
of
\rho_{\theta}

and the parameter space
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}
.
Estimating
\theta
is thus equivalent to
qudit state tomography.
Note that we adopt the convention of normalising the GMMs
such that
\Tr(\lambda_j \lambda_k)=\delta_{jk}
.
5
5

5

Some authors
[
66
]

instead normalise as
\Tr(\lambda_j \lambda_k)=2\delta_{jk}

to be consistent with the
d=2
case for Pauli matrices.
Our convention rescales
the parameter values and bounds,
but leaves their ratios invariant.

It is useful to summarise the
d=2
case results here
[
48
]
;
the HCRB and NHCRB are

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{RLD}}=&=3-r^{2}+2r\,,\\
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}&=5-r^{2}+4\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\,\end{split}

(24)

with
r^{2}=\sum_{j}\theta_{j}^{2}=\Tr(\rho^2)-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}

the squared length of the Bloch vector.
In this case,
the NHCRB is attained by measuring
informationally-complete (IC) POVMs,
simplifying to
symmetric informationally-complete (SIC)
POVMs (see Eq. (
39
) for definition) at
r=0

[
48
]
.
It is straightforward
6
6

6

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
increases with
r
whereas
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
decreases.

to see from Eq. (
24
) that
the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}
is maximised at
r=0
,
corresponding to estimating parameters of
the maximally-mixed state.
Thus, for the qubit tomography model,
the maximum enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

is three,
and this ratio is attained when
estimating the three Pauli matrix coefficients
of the maximally-mixed qubit state
[
48
]
.

An important simplifying feature of
the linear GMM model
is that the LUB operators

\mathbb{X}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}^{\top}

are uniquely fixed
to be the GMMs themselves, i.e.,

X_{j}=\lambda_{j}\,.

(25)

That there is exactly one feasible
solution for the LUB operators
significantly simplifies the evaluation
of the bounds.
7
7

7

This is not generally true;
in most cases there are multiple
feasible LUB operators
\mathbb{X}
, which need
to be optimised over to calculate
the bounds,
see for example Refs.
[
21
,
26
]
and Appendix
F
.

To see this unique feature of our model,
consider that the true (unknown) state is

\rho_{\theta}^{*}=\mathds{1}_{d}/d+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\theta_{j}^{*}\lambda_{j}\,.

The LUB constraints (Eq. (
5
))
at
\theta^{*}
are then

\begin{gathered}\Tr(\rho_\theta X_k){\big|}_{\theta=\theta^{*}}=\theta^{*}_{k}\,,\\
\Tr(\partial_j \rho_\theta X_k){\big|}_{\theta=\theta^{*}}=\delta_{jk}\,.\end{gathered}

(26)

Writing
X_{j}=\sum_{k}c_{jk}\lambda_{k}
,
where
c_{jk}
are unknown real numbers
(to preserve Hermicity of
X_{j}
),
reduces Eq. (
26
)
to

\sum_{j}c_{kj}\theta_{j}^{*}=\theta_{k}^{*}\quad\&\quad c_{kj}=\delta_{jk},

which immediately implies
X_{j}=\lambda_{j}
,
as claimed.

The simplification
from having a unique solution
for feasible LUB operators,
\mathbb{X}
,
extends more generally to tomography
in any ONB (
n=n_{\mathrm{max}}
). The model here,

\rho_{\theta^{\prime}}=\mathds{1}_{d}+\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\theta_{j}^{\prime}\,\mathcal{B}_{j}\,,

(27)

is called the ONB model, and corresponds to
tomography in the basis
\{\mathcal{B}_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}
,
which is orthonormal,
\Tr(\mathcal{B}_j \mathcal{B}_k)=\delta_{jk}
.
Further,
\mathcal{B}_{j}
are traceless
and Hermitian and,
therefore,
can be written as
a real linear combinations of the GMMs
\lambda_{k}

through
\mathcal{B}_{j}=\sum_{k}\eta_{jk}\lambda_{k}
,
so that

\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{B}_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\mathcal{B}_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\eta_{11}&\dots&\eta_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{n1}&\dots&\eta_{nn}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}\\
\vdots\\
\lambda_{n}\end{bmatrix}\,,

(28)

or in short,
with vectorised notation
\mathcal{B}_{d}

for the left hand side,

\mathcal{B}_{d}=(\eta\otimes\mathds{1}_{d})\Lambda_{d}\,.

(29)

Now imposing the condition
that
\mathcal{B}_{d}

is orthonormal, i.e.,

\Tr(\mathcal{B}_j \mathcal{B}_k)=\delta_{jk}\,,

(30)

implies that the transformation
matrix
\eta
is orthogonal,
so that the inner-products between
its different rows (or different columns) is zero.
This is stated and proved as
Lemma
1
below.

Lemma 1
.

For transforming from the GMM basis
to any other ONB for
the space of

d\times d
traceless Hermitian matrices
over reals,
the transformation matrix
\eta
is real
and orthogonal, meaning

\eta\eta^{\top}=\eta^{\top}\eta=\mathds{1}_{n}\,.

(31)

Proof.

The orthonormality condition
from Eq. (
30
)
in the GMM basis simplifies to

\displaystyle\Tr(\B_j \B_k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\eta_{ja}\eta_{kb}\Tr(\lambda_a \lambda_b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\eta_{ja}\eta_{ka}=(\eta\eta^{\top})_{jk}=\delta_{jk}\,,

so that
\eta\eta^{\top}=\mathds{1}_{n}
.
As both
\mathcal{B}_{d}
and
\Lambda_{d}
are bases for the space,
the matrix
\eta
is full-rank and therefore invertible.
This makes
\eta^{\top}
the inverse of
\eta
, so that

\eta\eta^{\top}=\eta^{\top}\eta=\mathds{1}_{n}
,
as claimed.
∎

Clearly, the GMM model is an ONB model,
and Lemma
1

says any two ONB models are related
by a real unitary (orthogonal) transformation
of the parameter derivatives.
Conversely, this connects the ONB parameters
\theta^{\prime}

(in Eq. (
27
))
to the GMM parameters
\theta

(in Eq. (
23
))
through

\theta_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\eta_{jk}\,\theta_{j}^{\prime}\implies\theta=\eta^{\top}\theta^{\prime}\implies\theta^{\prime}=\eta\,\theta\,.

This relation enables a much stronger connection
between different ONB models.
In particular, any ONB model
can now be considered
as a reparameterised GMM model
with
\theta^{\prime}=\eta\,\theta

[
61
,
13
,
21
]
.
From Sec. V of Ref.
[
61
]
,
we know that such a reparameterisation of any model
is equivalent to the weighted version of the original model
with weight matrix
W=\eta^{\top}\eta
.
For the reparameterised GMM model,
the orthogonality of
\eta
(Lemma
1
)
makes this weight
W=\eta^{\top}\eta=\mathds{1}_{n}
,
thereby proving that the GMM model
and the ONB model are completely equivalent.
This means that both individual
(NHCRB, MICRB, GMCRB)
and collective
(SLD CRB, HCRB) precision bounds
are invariant to the particular choice of ONB
for tomography.
Therefore, the ratios
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

obtained below for the GMM model
also hold for the model of tomography in any other ONB.

(35)

Lemma 2
.

The HCRB for estimating

.

The NHCRB for estimating

Our first main result

Theorem 2
.

For ONB tomography of the
maximally-mixed qudit state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
, the collective enhancement
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=d+1

The HCRB is already known to be asymptotically attainable,

Specifically, we show that the NHCRB in Lemma

can be attained by measuring any rank-one

dimensions
(assuming one exists).
The SIC POVM is a set of

(39)

,
establishing

The CFI matrix

simplifies to (see Lemma

(41)

in this case, so that Eq. (
2

(42)

From Eq. (
3

Moreover, for this choice of
\mathbb{X}
,

\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}

satisfies all the HCRB constraints
(Eq. (
7
))
and yields

\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]=\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}]=\frac{n}{d}\,,

so that
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=n/d
, as claimed.

For the NHCRB, the
X_{j}

are linear combinations of the
d^{2}-1
GMMs,
and can be written as

\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\Lambda_{d}\,,

(52)

where
\mathbb{C}^{(2)}
is a real matrix.
Then,
\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}=\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}
.
We similarly modify
\mathbb{L}^{*}

from Eq. (
37
)
to define
\mathbb{L}^{**}\coloneqq\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\mathbb{L}^{*}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}
,
which ensures
\mathbb{L}^{**}\succcurlyeq\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}

because of
\mathbb{L}^{*}\succcurlyeq\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}

from Lemma
4

in Appendix
E
.
The NHCRB in Eq. (
4
)
then becomes a minimisation
over
\mathbb{L}
and
\mathbb{C}^{(2)}
.
However, if we choose our ansatz
\mathbb{L}^{**}

for
\mathbb{L}

and minimise only over
\mathbb{C}^{(2)}
,
we should get a larger value,
i.e.,

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=\min_{\mathbb{L},\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\,|\,\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}\,,\\
\quad\quad\mathbb{L}_{jk}\,\mathrm{Hermitian}\,,\\
\qquad\quad\quad\;\;\mathbb{L}\succcurlyeq\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\;\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}\Big\}\,\\
\leq\min_{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}^{**}]\;|\;\mathbb{L}^{**}=\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\;\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}\Big\}\\
=\frac{(d+1)n}{d}\,.\end{gathered}

(53)

The inequality in Eq. (
53
) holds
because the second minimisation
is performed over a subset of the set
over which the first minimisation is performed
and the last equality follows after some algebra
(see Appendix
F
).
Combining this upper bound on the NHCRB
with
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}=n/d
,
we get the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

For estimating fewer-than-
n_{\mathrm{max}}
coefficients
of GMMs of the maximally-mixed qudit state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
,
the collective enhancement
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq d+1
.

Numerically,
we see this ratio actually depends on
n
:
as
n
increases from 2 to
d^{2}-1
,
the ratio increases from 2 to
d+1
.
Table
1
in Appendix
F

depicts this increase,
though not monotonic,
by listing the two bounds and their ratios for
d=3
.
As proven here, the HCRB only depends on
n
and
d
.
Interestingly,
when the true values of the parameters
not being estimated are non-zero,
the maximally-mixed state is no longer
the ratio-maximising state.
However, the
n
bound in
Sec.
5.1

and numerical results in
Sec.
3.3

suggest that the maximum enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

cannot decrease with increasing
n
at fixed
d
,
meaning
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n+1}
.
And we have analysed the
n=n_{\mathrm{max}}

case in depth, so we expect
that for any
n<n_{\mathrm{max}}
model,
the same bound
of
d+1
should hold,
i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}\leq d+1
.

5.6
True Ratio for GMM Model using MICRB

To establish an upper bound to
the true collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]
,
we use the MICRB,
which quantifies the optimal precision
attainable with separable measurements
[
36
,
38
,
39
]
.
The MICRB is formulated in Ref.
[
39
]

as a conic optimisation over an
(n+1)d\times(n+1)d
-sized
operator
X
that lies
in a separable cone,
\mathcal{S}_{\text{SEP}}
,
within the space of positive operators of this size
(see definition in Eq. (
147
)
in Appendix
I
).
Additionally, the operator
X
satisfies linear trace constraints
arising from the POVM condition
and from the local unbiasedness condition
(Eqs. (
148
), (
149
)
in Appendix
I
).
Subject to these constraints,
the MICRB minimises
the objective
\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[(W\otimes\rho_{\theta})X]
,
where the typically
n\times n
weight matrix
W

is modified to be
(n+1)\times(n+1)
-sized
by padding its first row and column with zeros
(
W_{1j}=W_{j1}=0
for
j\in[n+1]
).

It is important to note that
one of the main contributions
of Ref.
[
39
]

was showing that various
precision bounds, including the NHCRB
and the HCRB, can all be recast as
the minimisation of this same objective
but subject to different inclusion constraints
on operator
X
.
In particular, the NHCRB was shown to
require the positive semi-definiteness of
X
,
whereas the MICRB was shown to require
X

to also be separable,
over an
(n+1)
-dimensional real space
and a
d
-dimensional complex space.
Our key observation is to
find that for the linear GMM model,
at the maximally-mixed state,
a solution to the MICRB is given by

X_{\mathrm{sol}}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{d}&\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{2}&\dots&\lambda_{n}\\
\lambda_{1}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{11}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{12}&\dots&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{1n}\\
\lambda_{2}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{21}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{22}&\dots&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\lambda_{n}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{n1}&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{n2}&\dots&\mathbb{L}^{*}_{nn}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(54)

where
\mathbb{L}^{*}_{jk}

is the NHCRB-optimal argument
presented in Eq. (
37
) of
Sec.
5.3
.
This connection between the NHCRB solution
and the MICRB solution is not surprising,
given that two bounds
minimise the same objective subject to different constraints,
and are equal for the linear GMM model
at the maximally-mixed state.

In Appendix
I
,
we rigorously prove that
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

satisfies all the MICRB constraints,
including the POVM constraint,
the LUB constraints
and the separability constraint
(Lemma
10
).
The MICRB objective value
for
X_{\mathrm{sol}}
equals
the NHCRB,
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=n(d+1)/d
,
as expected.
Further, we show that
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

is a feasible candidate
(but not necessarily optimal)
for the MICRB for arbitrary qudit states,
thereby leading to the upper-bound,

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq n\left(\frac{d+1}{d}\right)-\sum_{j}\theta_{j}^{2}\,,

(55)

which was derived for
the NHCRB in Eq. (
48
)
of Sec.
5.4
.
Combining this upper bound
for the MICRB
with the lower bound
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\geq n/d-\sum_{j}\theta_{j}^{2}
,
we prove in Theorem
5

of Appendix
I

that the true maximum ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

in the linear GMM model
is upper-bounded by
d+2
.

Theorem 5
.

For ONB tomography of arbitrary
d
-dimensional qudit state
\rho_{\theta}
, the maximum true collective enhancement
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]\leq d+2
.

This theorem
reproduces the upper bound
derived on the maximum ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}[\{\rho_{\theta}\}]

in Theorem
3

and similarly extends to tomography in any ONB.
However,
as with Theorem
3
,
we do not expect the upper-bound of
d+2

to be attainable.

Appendix A
Gell-Mann Matrices and Tomography via Parameter Estimation

The main advantage of the Bloch representation
for qubits,

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathds{1}_{2}+\sum_{j\in\{x,y,z\}}\theta_{j}\sigma_{j}\right)\,,

(56)

where
\mathcal{P}\coloneqq\{\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z}\}

is the Pauli basis,
is the convenience of working with
the real-valued Bloch vector
\theta\coloneqq\{\theta_{x},\theta_{y},\theta_{z}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

instead of the equivalent
complex operator
\rho\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
.
The same convenience is availed in
three dimensions by replacing
\mathcal{P}

with the GMMs,

\Lambda_{3}\coloneqq\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{8}
.
These constitute an orthonormal basis
over the reals
for the space of
3\times 3

traceless Hermitian matrices
and generalise the Pauli matrices to three dimensions.
So for a qutrit state
\rho
, we can write

\rho=\mathds{1}_{3}/3+\sum_{j=1}^{8}\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}

(57)

with

\begin{gathered}\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&-i&0\\
i&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\;\\
\lambda_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&-1&0\\
0&0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\lambda_{4}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
1&0&0\end{bmatrix},\;\\
\lambda_{5}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&-i\\
0&0&0\\
i&0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad\lambda_{6}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix},\\
\lambda_{7}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-i\\
0&i&0\end{bmatrix},\;\lambda_{8}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
0&0&-2\end{bmatrix}.\end{gathered}

(58)

Note that
we choose a different convention in Eq. (
57
)
from that in
Eq. (
56
),
and we set
\Tr(\lambda_j \lambda_k)=\delta_{jk}

instead of the standard
2\delta_{jk}
in Eq. (
58
) for convenience.

The eight GMMs in Eq. (
58
)
for
d=3

can be extended to
d>3

leading to the generalised GMMs
\Lambda_{d}

(that we shall also refer to as GMMs).
In fact,
\Lambda_{d}
consists of
\binom{d}{2}
real, symmetric matrices that generalise
\sigma_{x}
,

\binom{d}{2}
imaginary, skew-symmetric matrices that generalise
\sigma_{y}
,
and
d-1
real, diagonal matrices that generalise
\sigma_{z}
.
In total, we have

d^{2}-1
matrices,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d^{2}-1}
,
in
\Lambda_{d}
, and,
for arbitrary qudit density matrix
\rho
in
d
dimensions,
we can write

\rho=\mathds{1}_{d}/d+\sum_{j=1}^{d^{2}-1}\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}

(59)

to get a one-to-one map between
\rho\leftrightarrow\theta
.
Resultantly, a qudit state estimation or tomography problem
can be treated as a parameter estimation problem
with
\theta
as the unknown parameter.
Note that our convention
in Eq. (
59
) is different from that used in some existing literature
[
66
]

but
is equivalent up to a re-scaling of the parameters,
which leaves the ratio unchanged.

{\mathbb{G}^{(1)}_{21}}&{0}&{\dots}&{\mathbb{G}^{(1)}_{2n}}&{\vdots}\\
{\vdots}&{\vdots}&{\ddots}&{\mathbb{G}^{(1)}_{(n-1)n}}&\;\\
{\mathbb{G}^{(1)}_{n1}}&{\dots}&{\mathbb{G}^{(1)}_{n(n-1)}}&{0}&{\mathbb{G}_{n}^{(2)}}\\
{\mathbb{G}_{1}^{(2)}}&{\dots}&\;&{\mathbb{G}_{n}^{(2)}}&{\mathbb{G}^{(3)}}\end{bmatrix}}

(100)

with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{G}_{jk}^{(1)}&=-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}\;\left[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\right]\\
\mathbb{G}_{j}^{(2)}&=\nicefrac{{d+1}}{{d}}\;\;\lambda_{j}\\
\mathbb{G}^{(3)}&=-\nicefrac{{(d^{2}-1)(d+1)}}{{d^{2}}}\;\;\mathds{1}_{d},\end{aligned}

(101)

where
j,k\in[n]
and
\left[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\right]
is the commutator.

The proof of Lemmas
4

and
5

is broken up into the following three subsections.
In Subsec.
E.2
,
we prove the feasibility of
\mathbb{L}^{*}

from Lemma
4
.
In Subsec.
E.3

we prove the feasibility of
y^{*}

from Lemma
5
.
Finally in Subsec.
E.4

we prove that the primal-objective value
from
\mathbb{L}^{*}
equals the
dual objective value from
y^{*}
,
thus establishing their optimality
and proving Lemma
3

from the main text.

E.2
Feasibility of
\mathbb{L}^{*}
for the Primal Problem

The
\mathbb{L}^{*}_{jk}
from Lemma
4
is easily seen to be symmetric in
j
and
k
, meaning
\mathbb{L}^{*}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}^{*}_{kj}
.

\mathbb{L}^{*}_{jk}
is also seen to be Hermitian.
To show
\mathbb{L}^{*}
is feasible,
it only remains to show
\mathbb{L}^{*}-\mathbb{X}{\mathbb{X}}^{\top}\succcurlyeq 0
.
Writing
\mathbb{L}^{*}-\mathbb{X}{\mathbb{X}}^{\top}

as a block matrix,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathbb{L}^{*}-\mathbb{X}{\mathbb{X}}^{\top})_{jk}&=\frac{d+1}{d+2}\left(\{\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\}+\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}\right)-\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\\
&=\frac{d+1}{d+2}\left(\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}+{\mathbb{N}_{1}}_{jk}-{\mathbb{N}_{2}}_{jk}\right)\end{aligned}

(102)

we see that we need to prove
\mathbb{N}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{nd}+\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2}\succcurlyeq 0
,
where we have defined block matrices

{\left(\mathbb{N}_{1}\right)}_{jk}\coloneqq\lambda_{k}\lambda_{j}\quad\&\quad{\left(\mathbb{N}_{2}\right)}_{jk}\coloneqq\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}}{d+1}.

We first evaluate
(\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2})^{2}=\mathbb{N}_{1}^{2}+\mathbb{N}_{2}^{2}-\mathbb{N}_{1}\mathbb{N}_{2}-\mathbb{N}_{2}\mathbb{N}_{1}
to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (\mathbb{N}_{1}^{2})_{jk}&=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{d}}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\\
(\mathbb{N}_{2}^{2})_{jk}&=\frac{d-1}{d(d+1)}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\\
(\mathbb{N}_{1}\mathbb{N}_{2})_{jk}&=-\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\\
(\mathbb{N}_{2}\mathbb{N}_{1})_{jk}&=-\frac{1}{d(d+1)}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k},\end{aligned}

(103)

where we have used the identities proven in Appendix
B
.
Combining these results we arrive at
\big((\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2})^{2}\big)_{jk}=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}
which means
(\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2})^{2}=\mathds{1}_{nd}
. From this, and using that
\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2}
is Hermitian,
we can conclude that the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2}
are
\pm 1
.
Hence the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{N}=\mathds{1}_{nd}+\mathbb{N}_{1}-\mathbb{N}_{2}
are 0 and 2.
This proves that
\mathbb{N}
is a positive semi-definite operator,
and that
\mathbb{L}^{*}-\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\geq 0
.
∎

E.3
Feasibility of
y^{*}
for the Dual Problem

We need to show that the matrix
\sum_{k}y^{*}_{k}\mathbb{F}_{k}
,
which explicitly is

\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac{1}{d}[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}]&\dots&\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{1}]}&\frac{d+1}{d}\lambda_{1}\\
\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]}&0&\dots&\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{2}]}&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n-1}]}&\;\\
\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{n}]}&\dots&\frac{1}{d}{[\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}]}&0&\frac{d+1}{d}\lambda_{n}\\
\frac{d+1}{d}\lambda_{1}&\dots&\;&\frac{d+1}{d}\lambda_{n}&-\frac{n(d+1)}{d^{2}}\mathds{1}_{d}\end{bmatrix},

(104)

satisfies
\mathbb{F}_{0}-\sum_{k}y^{*}_{k}\mathbb{F}_{k}\succcurlyeq 0
.
Note that this
\sum_{k}y^{*}_{k}\mathbb{F}_{k}

corresponds to
y^{*}_{j}
values

\displaystyle y^{(1)}_{j}=0,\quad y^{(2)}_{jk}=\frac{d+1}{d}\delta_{jk},\quad y^{(3)}_{jk}=0

(105)

\displaystyle y^{(4)}_{jkl}=-f_{jkl}/d,\quad y^{(5)}_{j}=-\frac{n(d+1)}{d\sqrt{d}}\delta_{j1}

(106)

whereas the corresponding
c_{j}
values are

\displaystyle c^{(1)}_{j}=0,\quad c^{(2)}_{jk}=2\delta_{jk},\quad c^{(3)}_{jk}=0

(107)

\displaystyle c^{(4)}_{jkl}=0,\quad c^{(5)}_{j}=\sqrt{d}\delta_{j1}

(108)

so that the dual objective value is

\begin{split}\sum_{j}2y^{(2)}_{jj}+\sqrt{d}y^{(5)}_{j}&=\frac{2n(d+1)}{d}-\frac{n(d+1)}{d}\\
&=\frac{n(d+1)}{d}.\end{split}

(109)

To show this
y^{*}
is feasible,
note that
proving
\mathbb{F}_{0}-\sum_{k}y^{*}_{k}\mathbb{F}_{k}\succcurlyeq 0

is equivalent to showing

\begin{bmatrix}\mathds{1}_{d}&[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]&\dots&{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{n}]}&-(d+1)\lambda_{1}\\
{[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}]}&\mathds{1}_{d}&\dots&{[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}]}&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&{[\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}]}&\;\\
{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{1}]}&\dots&{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n-1}]}&\mathds{1}_{d}&-(d+1)\lambda_{n}\\
-(d+1)\lambda_{1}&\dots&\;&-(d+1)\lambda_{n}&\frac{n(d+1)}{d}\mathds{1}_{d}\end{bmatrix}

is positive semi-definite.
Using Schur’s complement lemma,
this can be simplified to showing

\begin{bmatrix}\mathds{1}_{d}&[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]&\dots&{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{n}]}\\
{[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}]}&\mathds{1}_{d}&\dots&{[\lambda_{2},\lambda_{n}]}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&{[\lambda_{n-1},\lambda_{n}]}\\
{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{1}]}&\dots&{[\lambda_{n},\lambda_{n-1}]}&\mathds{1}_{d}\end{bmatrix}-\frac{d}{d-1}\begin{bmatrix}\lambda_{1}^{2}&\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}&\dots&\lambda_{1}\lambda_{n}\\
\lambda_{2}\lambda_{1}&\ddots&\dots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\;&\vdots\\
\lambda_{n}\lambda_{1}&\lambda_{n}\lambda_{2}&\dots&\lambda_{n}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\succcurlyeq 0

(110)

We rewrite the left hand side
of Eq. (
110
)
in the block-matrix representation as

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}+[\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}]-\frac{d}{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}-\frac{1}{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k}\lambda_{j}

\displaystyle=(\mathds{1}_{nd})_{jk}-\left(\left(\mathbb{M}_{1}\right)_{jk}+\left(\mathbb{M}_{2}\right)_{jk}\right)\,,

where we have defined

(\mathbb{M}_{1})_{jk}\coloneqq\frac{1}{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\quad\&\quad(\mathbb{M}_{2})_{jk}\coloneqq\lambda_{k}\lambda_{j}.

(111)

Thus,
we finally need to prove the following theorem to establish the feasibility of
y^{*}
.

Lemma 6
.

The operator
\mathbb{M}\coloneqq\mathds{1}_{nd}-(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})
is positive semi-definite.

Before we can prove Lemma
6
,
we first need to prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 7
.

\mathbb{M}_{1}
and
\mathbb{M}_{2}
commute, i.e.,

\mathbb{M}_{1}\mathbb{M}_{2}=\mathbb{M}_{2}\mathbb{M}_{1}
.

Proof.

Using Identity 2 from Appendix
B
, we have

\begin{split}(\mathbb{M}_{1}\mathbb{M}_{2})_{jk}&=\sum_{l}(\mathbb{M}_{1})_{jl}(\mathbb{M}_{2})_{lk}\\
&=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{l}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}\\
&=\frac{1}{d-1}\lambda_{j}\left(\frac{-1}{d}\lambda_{k}\right)\\
&=-\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\,,\end{split}

(112)

whereas

\begin{split}(\mathbb{M}_{2}\mathbb{M}_{1})_{jk}&=\sum_{l}(\mathbb{M}_{2})_{jl}(\mathbb{M}_{1})_{lk}\\
&=\frac{1}{d-1}\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{k}\\
&=\frac{1}{d-1}\left(\frac{-1}{d}\lambda_{j}\right)\lambda_{k}\\
&=-\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}.\end{split}

(113)

Hence
\mathbb{M}_{1}\mathbb{M}_{2}=\mathbb{M}_{2}\mathbb{M}_{1}
,
which also implies that
\mathbb{M}_{1}
and
\mathbb{M}_{2}
share some eigenvectors.
∎

Lemma 8
.

\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2}
satisfies

(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})^{2}=\mathds{1}_{nd}

or, equivalently,

\left[(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})^{2}\right]_{jk}=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}

where
j,k\in[n]
.

Proof.

\begin{split}&\left[(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})^{2}\right]_{jk}\\
&=\sum_{l}\left(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2}\right)_{jl}\left(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2}\right)_{lk}\\
&=\sum_{l}\left(\frac{1}{d-1}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}+\lambda_{l}\lambda_{j}\right)\left(\frac{1}{d-1}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{(d-1)^{2}}\lambda_{j}\left(\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}^{2}\right)\lambda_{k}+\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}\\
&+\frac{1}{d-1}\left[\lambda_{j}\left(\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}\right)+\left(\sum_{l}\lambda_{l}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{l}\right)\lambda_{k}\right]\\
\intertext{so using Corollary~\ref{cor:corollary3} and Identities~1 and~2,}&=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}\\
&\;+{\left({\frac{d^{2}-1}{d(d-1)^{2}}}-{\frac{1}{d}}-{\frac{1}{d(d-1)}}-{\frac{1}{d(d-1)}}\right)}{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{k}}\\
&=\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}.\end{split}

∎

Now we can prove Lemma
6
as follows.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

From Lemma
8
,
the eigenvalues of
(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})^{2}
must all be
1
. As
(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})
is Hermitian, its eigenvalues must be
\pm 1
.
It follows that the eigenvalues of
\mathbb{M}=\mathds{1}_{nd}-(\mathbb{M}_{1}+\mathbb{M}_{2})
must be either
2
or
0
.
Hence
\mathbb{M}
, being a Hermitian matrix with non-negative eigenvalues, must be positive semi-definite.
∎

E.4
Optimality of Solutions &
Proof of Lemma
3

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Note that, by direct calculation,

\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}^{*})=\sum_{k}y^{*}_{k}c_{k}=\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d+1)}{d}\;.

(114)

In other words,
\mathbb{L}^{*}
is primal-feasible
and
y^{*}
is dual-feasible
and the primal value equals the dual value.
This lets us conclude that
\nicefrac{{(d^{2}-1)(d+1)}}{{d}}

is the true optimal value of the primal and dual problems,
and that
\mathbb{L}^{*}
and
y^{*}
are optimal solutions
to the primal and dual problems, respectively.
As a result, we have

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}=\frac{(d^{2}-1)(d+1)}{d}.

(115)

∎

E.5
Attainability of NHCRB via SIC POVMs

Lemma 9
.

The CFI matrix
for estimating all GMMs from the maximally-mixed state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}

by measuring the SIC POVM in
d
dimensions is

J=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{d}{d+1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\frac{d}{d+1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&\frac{d}{d+1}\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n}.

(116)

Proof of Lemma
9
.

In the multi-parameter case,
the CFI matrix
J_{jk}
(
j,k\in[n]
)
is given by

J_{jk}\left[\{\Pi_{l}\}\right]=\sum_{l=1}^{d^{2}}\frac{\Tr\left[\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta}\Pi_{l}\right]\Tr\left[\partial_{k}\rho_{\theta}\Pi_{l}\right]}{\Tr\left[\rho_{\theta}\Pi_{l}\right]}\,,

(117)

From
[
71
]
we have that for any (rank-one)
SIC POVM
\{\Pi_{l}\}_{l=1}^{d^{2}}
,

\sum_{l=1}^{d^{2}}\Tr[\rho\Pi_{l}]^{2}=\frac{\Tr[\rho^{2}]+1}{d(d+1)}

(118)

for arbitrary density matrix
\rho
.
For the diagonal elements in Eq. (
117
),
substituting
\rho=\mathds{1}/d+\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}
into Eq. (
118
)
and using
\Tr(\rho^2)=1/d+\theta_{j}^{2}
gives

J_{jj}=d^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d^{2}}\Tr\left[\lambda_{j}\Pi_{l}\right]^{2}=\frac{d}{d+1},

(119)

whereas for the off-diagonal elements,
substituting
\rho=\mathds{1}/d+\theta_{j}\lambda_{j}+\theta_{k}\lambda_{k}
into Eq. (
118
) and using
\Tr(\rho^2)=1/d+\theta_{j}^{2}+\theta_{k}^{2}
gives

J_{jk}=d^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d^{2}}\Tr[\lambda_{j}\Pi_{l}]\Tr[\lambda_{k}\Pi_{l}]=0\quad(j\neq k),

(120)

thus proving Eq. (
116
).
∎

Appendix F
Estimating a Subset of GMMs

Consider estimating a subset
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j\in K}

of GMMs from the maximally-mixed state
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Here
K
denotes a subset of
n
indices from
1
to
n_{\mathrm{max}}

(
K\subseteq[n_{\mathrm{max}}],|K|=n
).
We denote the maximum NHCRB-to-HCRB
ratio here as
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}[\{\rho_{\mathrm{m}}\}]
,
referring to the model
\{\rho_{\mathrm{m}}\}
of
estimating
n
GMM coefficients from
\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
.
Now, the corresponding unbiased operators
can be written as

X_{j}=\lambda_{j}+\sum_{m\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]\setminus K}c_{jm}\lambda_{m},\quad j\in K,\,c_{jm}\in\mathbb{R},

(121)

which follows from
the unbiasedness conditions in Eq. (
26
).
Specifically,

\Tr(\partial_j \rho_\theta X_k)=\Tr(\lambda_j X_k)=\delta_{jk}

forces each
X_{j}
to contain a unit
contribution from
\lambda_{j}

due to the orthonormality of the GMMs
and
\Tr(\rho_\theta X_j)=\theta_{j}

implies the only other GMMs contributing
to
X_{j}
must be the ones not being estimated.

Notice that at the block-matrix level,
this can be rewritten as

\mathbb{X}=\bigg[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{n\times(n_{\mathrm{max}}-n)}\end{array}\bigg]_{n\times n_{\mathrm{max}}}\Lambda_{d}\,,

(122)

where
\mathbb{X}\coloneqq[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]^{\top},\Lambda_{d}\coloneqq[\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n_{\mathrm{max}}}]^{\top}

and
\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{ab}=c_{ab}
.
For convenience, we also define

\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\coloneqq\bigg[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{n\times(n_{\mathrm{max}}-n)}\end{array}\bigg]_{n\times n_{\mathrm{max}}}

(123)

so that
\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}=\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\Lambda_{d}\Lambda_{d}^{\top}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}
.

We can now use
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\geq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=\min_{\mathbb{X}}\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\right]

to get

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]&\geq\frac{1}{d}\min_{\mathbb{C}^{(1)}}\left(n+\sum_{a,b}(\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{ab})^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{n}{d}=\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{SLD}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\,.\end{split}

(124)

Moreover,
as in Appendix
C
,

\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}

is a valid choice leading to

\left(\Tr[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\right)_{j,k}=\frac{1}{d}\left(\delta_{jk}+\sum_{l\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]\setminus K}\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{jl}\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{kl}\right)\,,

(125)

which is real, symmetric and gives
\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]=\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}]
. This proves

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]=\frac{n}{d}

(126)

following the same arguments as in Appendix
C
.
For estimating all
n_{\mathrm{max}}
parameters
this reduces to Lemma
2
.
Numerical checks also verify this result,
as shown, e.g., in Table
1
.

Notice that
\Lambda_{d}\Lambda_{d}^{\top}
is the same as
\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}

from Lemma
4
,
so that,
using the fact that
{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\succcurlyeq 0
,
we can modify
\mathbb{L}^{*}
from Lemma
4

as

\mathbb{L}^{**}(\mathbb{C}^{(1)})\coloneqq\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\mathbb{L}^{*}{\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}\,.

(127)

It then follows from Lemma
4

that

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{L}^{*}-\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\succcurlyeq 0\\
\implies\mathbb{C}^{(2)}\left(\mathbb{L}^{*}-\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\right){\mathbb{C}^{(2)}}^{\top}\succcurlyeq 0\\
\implies\mathbb{L}^{**}-\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\succcurlyeq 0\,.\end{gathered}

(128)

That this
\mathbb{L}^{**}
satisfies
the other NHCRB constraints
(
\mathbb{L}^{**}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}^{**}_{kj}
Hermitian
from Eq. (
4

for all
\mathbb{C}^{(1)}

is also easy to check.

Note that
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]
is now defined by the following minimisation:

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\coloneqq\min_{\mathbb{L},\mathbb{C}^{(1)}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}\;|\;\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}\;\mathrm{Hermitian}\,,\\

(129)

whereas if we restrict the minimisation over
\mathbb{L}

to a minimisation over our ansatz
\mathbb{L}^{**}(\mathbb{C}^{(1)})
,
we should get a larger value than
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}
,
i.e.,

\begin{gathered}\min_{\mathbb{L},\mathbb{C}^{(1)}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}\;|\;\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}\;\mathrm{Hermitian}\,,\\
\mathbb{L}\succcurlyeq[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}\end{array}]\;\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\;{[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}\end{array}]}^{\top}\Big\}\,\\
\leq\min_{\mathbb{C}^{(1)}}\Big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}^{**}\;|\;\mathbb{L}^{**}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}^{**}_{kj}\;\mathrm{Hermitian}\,,\\
\mathbb{L}^{**}\succcurlyeq[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}\end{array}]\;\Lambda\Lambda^{\top}\;{[\begin{array}[]{c|c}\mathds{1}_{n}&\mathbb{C}^{(1)}\end{array}]}^{\top}\Big\}\,.\end{gathered}

(130)

This is because the minimisation on the RHS of Eq. (
130
) is over a subset of the set over which the minimisation on the LHS is performed.
The quantity on the RHS of Eq. (

)
can then be simplified to

\min_{\mathbb{C}^{(1)}}\left\{\frac{d+1}{d}\left(n+\sum_{a,b}\left(\mathbb{C}^{(1)}_{ab}\right)^{2}\right)\right\}=\frac{(d+1)n}{d}\,.

This lets us upper-bound

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq\frac{(d+1)n}{d}\,,

(131)

which for estimating all
n_{\mathrm{max}}
parameters reduces to
Eq. (
48
) from Subsec.
5.4

Combining with

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\leq\min{X}\bigg\{&\mathsf{Tr}(\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}])\\
&+\sum_{j,k\in[n]}\left\|\rho_{\theta}[X_{j},X_{k}]\right\|_{1}\bigg\}\,.\end{split}

(133)

Ref.
[
46
]
analyses the concept of gap persistence
between the NHCRB
and the HCRB with increasing
number of copies in multi-parameter quantum estimation.
In doing so, the authors
upper-bound and lower-bound the NHCRB
(Secs. 3.2 & 3.3, pg. 58 in
[
46
]
).

Say the
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
of the qudit,
\mathcal{H}_{d}

is combined with the
n
-dimensional
(complex) parameter space
to define an extended
Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{d}
.
Ref.
[
46
]
then
defines the NHCRB as

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\coloneqq\min_{\mathbb{X}}F_{\mathrm{NH}}(\mathbb{X})

(134)

subject to
\mathbb{X}
being LUB operators,
and with the Nagaoka-Hayashi (NH)
function
F_{\mathrm{NH}}(\mathbb{X})
given by

\begin{gathered}F_{\mathrm{NH}}(\mathbb{X})\coloneqq\min_{\mathbb{L}}\big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{S}\mathbb{L}]\,|\,\mathbb{L}\in\mathcal{L}_{+,\operatorname{sym}}(\mathbb{H}),\\
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\mathbb{L}\geq\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\big\}\,,\end{gathered}

(135)

where
\mathcal{L}_{+,\operatorname{sym}}(\mathbb{H})

denotes the set of all positive semidefinite
operators
\mathbb{L}\succcurlyeq 0
on
\mathbb{H}

that are also symmetric under
the partial transpose with respect to the
first Hilbert space, i.e.,
\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}
for all
j,k\in[n]
.

Ref.
[
46
]
then proves that
the NH function can be rewritten as

\begin{split}F_{\mathrm{NH}}(\mathbb{X})&=\mathsf{Tr}\{\operatorname{Re}\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\}+F_{\mathrm{NH},2}(\mathbb{X})\,,\\
F_{\mathrm{NH},2}(\mathbb{X})&\coloneqq\min_{\mathbb{V}}\big\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{V}]\,|\,\mathbb{V}\in\mathcal{L}_{+,\operatorname{sym}}(\mathbb{H}),\\
&\qquad\quad\mathbb{V}\geq\operatorname{sym}_{-}(\sqrt{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}}\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\sqrt{\mathbb{S}_{\theta}})\big\}\,,\end{split}

(136)

where
\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]_{jk}=\Tr( \rho_\theta X_j X_k )
as in Eq. (
6
),

\mathbb{S}_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\rho_{\theta}
,
and
\mathrm{sym}_{-}(\mathbb{A})=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{A}-\mathbb{A}^{\top})
, with
⊤
denoting
partial transpose with respect to parameter space.
This rearrangement makes

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}=\min_{\mathbb{X}}\left\{\mathsf{Tr}\{\operatorname{Re}\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\}+F_{\mathrm{NH},2}(\mathbb{X})\right\}\,,

(137)

in which the first term
is equal to the Holevo
objective function in Eq. (
6
).

Finally, the authors prove
as one of their results
(Theorem 1, Sec. 3.2 in Ref.
[
46
]
)
that
the second term of the
NH function is bounded from above as

F_{\mathrm{NH},2}(\mathbb{X})\leq\sum_{j,k}\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}}[X_{j},X_{k}]\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}}\right\|_{1}\,,

(138)

so that the NHCRB can be upper-bounded
as

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}\leq\min_{\mathbb{X}}\bigg\{&\mathsf{Tr}\{\real\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\}\\
&\quad\,+\sum_{j,k}\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}}[X_{j},X_{k}]\sqrt{\rho_{\theta}}\right\|_{1}\bigg\}\,,\end{split}

(139)

from which
main-text Eq. (
21
)
follows.
Physically, this argument reveals
the difference between the HCRB and the NHCRB
to originate from the second term,
F_{\mathrm{NH},2}(\mathbb{X})
;
this term captures the non-commutativity inherent in quantum measurements for multi-parameter estimation.
This result from Ref.
[
46
]
forms
a basis of our proof of Theorem
1

showing that the ratio
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]
is upper-bounded by
n
.
For further details, readers are encouraged to consult Ref.
[
46
]
, which
presents an in-depth exploration
of the relationship between these two bounds.

Appendix H
Extension to Arbitrary Weight Matrices

In this section, we extend the
ratio bound of
d+1
for
the linear GMM model
to arbitrary, parameter-independent, positive weight matrices
W
.
For fair comparison with the
unweighted case,
corresponding to
W=\mathds{1}_{n}
,
we trace-normalise
\mathsf{Tr}(W)=n
.
Additionally,

W
must be real, symmetric
and positive
(W\succ 0)
.
Below, we shall refer to
estimating from
\rho_{\theta}

under weight matrix
W

as estimating from
(\rho_{\theta},W)

and denote the corresponding
precision bounds by
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]

and
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]
.

This weighted model corresponds
to reparameterisations
of the linear GMM model
[
21
,
61
,
13
]
,
i.e., estimating any
n_{\mathrm{max}}
parameters
that are not necessarily coefficients of the GMMs.
Similar to the other cases
where all
n_{\mathrm{max}}

parameters are estimated,
the unbiased operators
are uniquely fixed to be
X_{j}=\lambda_{j}
.
We first bound the weighted
HCRB and the weighted NHCRB
to prove that the collective enhancement
is at most
d+1
for
estimating from
(\rho_{\mathrm{m}},W)

for any
W
.
Then, to extend to arbitrary states
\rho_{\theta}\neq\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
,
we numerically
demonstrate that
the collective enhancement for estimating from
(\rho_{\theta},W)

is always smaller
than the collective enhancement
for estimating from
(\rho_{\mathrm{m}},W)
.
However, we do not prove this.

The weighted HCRB is defined via
[
45
]

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]\coloneqq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}V\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},\\
V=V^{\top}\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathsf{Tr}[WV]\;|\;V\succcurlyeq\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]\right\}\,,

(140)

where, by explicit computation
for the maximally-mixed case,
\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]_{jk}=\Tr[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}X_{j}X_{k}]=\delta_{jk}/d

\mathbb{Z}_{\theta}[\mathbb{X}]=1/d\,\mathds{1}_{n}

Then, it follows
from the positivity of
W

that
V\succcurlyeq 1/d\;\mathds{1}_{n}
implies

WV\succcurlyeq\frac{1}{d}W\implies\mathsf{Tr}[WV]\geq\mathsf{Tr}[W]/d=\frac{n}{d}\,.

(141)

This proves
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\geq n/d
.

The weighted NHCRB is defined via
[
21
]

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]\coloneqq\min_{\mathbb{L}}\Bigg\{\mathsf{Tr}[WV]\;|\;V=\Tr[\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}]\,\\
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\rho_{\theta},\,\mathbb{L}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}_{kj}\mathrm{Hermitian}\,,\\
\mathbb{L}\succcurlyeq\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\Bigg\}\,.\end{split}

(142)

Notably, the feasibility constraints on
\mathbb{L}

are unchanged from the unweighted case,
i.e.,
the optimal
\mathbb{L}^{*}
from
Lemma
4

still satisfies
\mathbb{L}^{*}_{jk}=\mathbb{L}^{*}_{kj}

Hermitian
and
\mathbb{L}^{*}\succcurlyeq\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}
,
despite not being optimal for
the minimisation in Eq. (
142
).
This sub-optimal
\mathbb{L}^{*}

thus yields an upper bound to the minimum
in Eq. (
142
),

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq\mathsf{Tr}\left[W\Tr\left[\frac{1}{d}\mathds{1}_{nd}\mathbb{L}^{*}\right]\right]\\
=\frac{d+1}{d}\mathsf{Tr}[W]=\frac{n(d+1)}{d}\,,\end{split}

(143)

which proves
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq n(d+1)/d
.
Combining with
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\geq n/d

then proves the claim,

\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]}\leq d+1\,.

(144)

We do not prove the bound

By generating random

such that
\mathsf{Tr}(W)=n
,

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]/\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}^{W}[\rho_{\theta}]

Appendix I
True Maximum Collective Enhancement in GMM Model

The tight bound for individual precision
called the MICRB
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}

was reformulated in Ref.
[
39
]

using the following conic
optimisation problem (Eq. (39) in Ref.
[
39
]
),

S(P_{1})\coloneqq\min_{X\in\mathcal{S}_{SEP}}\left\{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[\left(W\otimes\rho\right)X\right]{\big|}\mathsf{C}_{1},\mathsf{C}_{2}\mathrm{hold}.\right\},

(147)

where
W
is the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
weight matrix
defined with
W_{11}=W_{1j}=W_{j1}=0

for all
j\in\{1,\dots,n+1\}
,
and
\mathsf{C}_{1},\mathsf{C}_{2}

refer to two equality constraints
on the
(n+1)d\times(n+1)d
-sized
operator
X
,

\mathsf{C}_{1}:\,\,\,\Tr_{\mathcal{X}}\left[(\outerproduct{0}{0}\otimes\mathds{1}_{d})X\right]=\mathds{1}_{d}\,,

(148)

and

\begin{split}\mathsf{C}_{2}:\,\,\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[\left(\frac{\ket{0}\bra{j}+\ket{j}\bra{0}}{2}\otimes\partial_{k}\rho\right)X\right]&=\delta_{jk}\\
(\text{for all}\;1<j,k\leq n&+1)\,,\end{split}

(149)

which ensure the POVM constraint
and the local unbiasedness constraint, respectively.
The constraint
\mathsf{C}_{1}

involves partial tracing
over space
\mathcal{X}
, which
denotes an
(n+1)
-dimensional
real vector space spanned by
\{\ket{0},\dots,\ket{n}\}
.
Lastly, the
optimisation domain in Eq. (
147
)
is the separable cone
\mathcal{S}_{SEP}
,
denoting
the convex hull of
(n+1)d
-dimensional operators
that are tensor products of
real, symmetric, positive-semidefinite operators on the space
\mathcal{X}

(denoted
\mathcal{M}_{rs,+}(\mathcal{X})
in Ref.
[
39
]
),
and complex, Hermitian, positive-semi-definite operators
on the
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{d}

(denoted
\mathcal{B}_{sa,+}(\mathcal{H}_{d})
in Ref.
[
39
]
).
In the notation of Ref.
[
39
]
,
this cone is denoted
\mathcal{S}_{SEP}\coloneqq\mathrm{conv}(\mathcal{M}_{rs,+}(\mathcal{X})\otimes\mathcal{B}_{sa,+}(\mathcal{H}_{d}))
.

For the problem of GMM tomography
of arbitrary qudit states,
the derivatives appearing in constraint
\mathsf{C}_{2}
,

\partial_{k}\rho=\lambda_{k}
,
are parameter independent.
Thus, feasibility for the MICRB minimisation
(comprising inclusion in the separable cone
X\in\mathcal{S}_{SEP}
,
and constraints
\mathsf{C}_{1}
&
\mathsf{C}_{2}
)
does not depend on the state
\rho
being estimated,
i.e., the true values of the parameters
\theta
.
Let us define a candidate solution
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

to the MICRB in a block-wise manner,

(X_{\mathrm{sol}})_{jk}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\mathds{1}_{d}&j=k=1\\
\lambda_{j}&j>k=1\\
\lambda_{k}&k>j=1\\
\frac{d+1}{d+2}\left(\{\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\}+\delta_{jk}\mathds{1}_{d}\right)&j,k>1\end{cases}\,,

(150)

where block indices
j,k
run from 1
to
n+1
.
In fact, this candidate solution
can be rewritten as

X_{\mathrm{sol}}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathds{1}_{d}&\mathbb{X}^{\top}\\
\mathbb{X}&\mathbb{L}^{*}\end{bmatrix}

(151)

where
\mathbb{X}=\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}\}^{\top}

(from Eq. (
25
)) and
\mathbb{L}^{*}

(from Eq. (
37
))
are the NHCRB-optimal solutions
(see Lemma
4
).
The NHCRB optimisation constraint
was the positivity of
X_{\mathrm{sol}}
above
[
39
]
,
which was proven
through
\mathbb{L}^{*}-\mathbb{X}\mathbb{X}^{\top}\succcurlyeq 0

in Lemma
4
(Appendix
E
).
Thus, Lemma
4
solved
the NHCRB for the maximally-mixed state.

It can be easily checked
that
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

satisfies both constraints
\mathsf{C}_{1}
,
through the
j=k=1
term,
and
\mathsf{C}_{2}
,
through the
j>k=1
and the
k>j=1
terms.
The proof that
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

belongs to the separable cone
\mathcal{S}_{SEP}

(deferred to Lemma
10
below)
follows by expressing
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

as the sum over
d^{2}
operators,
\sum_{l\in[d^{2}]}\Xi_{l}\otimes\Pi_{l}
,
where each
\Xi_{l}
is an estimator
matrix in
\mathcal{M}_{rs,+}(\mathcal{X})

and
\Pi_{l}
is the
l^{\text{th}}
element
of a SIC POVM, therefore belonging to
\mathcal{B}_{sa,+}(\mathcal{H}_{d})
.
Thus,
X_{\mathrm{sol}}
is feasible for the MICRB
minimisation for GMM tomography of arbitrary qudits.

In fact, while
X_{\mathrm{sol}}

is feasible (not necessarily optimal) for all true states,

Tomography in the GMM basis corresponds to
the identity-weighted full GMM model,
so the appropriate matrix
W
is

W_{\mathrm{id}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&\dots 0\\
0&1&\dots 0\\
0&0&\dots 0\\
0&0&\dots 1\end{bmatrix}_{(n+1)\times(n+1)}\,.

(152)

In this case,
the objective in Eq. (
147
)
equals
\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[(\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\rho)X_{22}\right]

denotes the block of

starting from row and column indices
d+1

up to indices
(n+1)d
.
For the candidate solution,
X_{22}
is
\mathbb{L}^{*}
,
so the objective equals
\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}(\mathbb{S}_{\theta}\mathbb{L}^{*})

where
\mathbb{S}_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{n}\otimes\rho

the NHCRB objective (see Eq. (
4
))
that was computed in Lemma

(and proved in Appendix

For the maximally-mixed state

therefore equals

\begin{split}\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[(W_{\mathrm{id}}\otimes\rho_{\mathrm{m}})X_{\mathrm{sol}}\right]=\frac{\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[\mathbb{L}^{*}]}{d}\\

(153)

As
X_{\mathrm{sol}}
is feasible,
this upper-bounds the minimum in Eq. (

)

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]\leq\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{NHCRB}}[\rho_{\mathrm{m}}]

whereas, by definition,

X_{\mathrm{sol}}
.
Further, since

is feasible for any

\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}[(W_{\mathrm{id}}\otimes\rho)X_{\mathrm{sol}}]

\mathbb{T}\mathrm{r}\left[(W_{\mathrm{id}}\otimes\rho)X_{\mathrm{sol}}\right]=\frac{n(d+1)}{d}\,,

\frac{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}[\rho_{\theta}]}{\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{HCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]}\leq\frac{d^{2}+d-1-\mathrm{P}(\rho_{\theta})}{d-\mathrm{P}(\rho_{\theta})}.

Then, using

)
to be

is pure.

In fact,

as our main tool

In short, the NHCRB is
generally tighter
than the GMCRB across a range
of qudit estimation models
(e.g., models comprising a few parameters,
n<n_{\mathrm{max}}
,
see Figs.
2
,

)
and thus is better-suited
for the exploration of model-free quantities
like
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}_{n}
and
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{MI}}
.
In Fig.
2
,
we compare the GMCRB-to-HCRB ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}

(dark gray bars, blue line)
and the NHCRB-to-HCRB ratio
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}

(light gray bars, red line)
by combining ratios from known analytic models
and from 1300
randomly-sampled numerical models
for each
n
from 1 to
n_{\mathrm{max}}=8
and
d=3
.
The NHCRB ratio satisfies
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{NH}}_{n}\leq n
,
whereas the GMCRB ratio satisfies
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{GM}}_{n}\leq n/(d-1)
,
proved in Sec.
3.2.2
.

Similarly, the NHCRB
shows a sub-additive scaling with number of copies
similar to
\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{MI}}
, whereas
the standard GMCRB is additive with number of copies.
This means that NHCRB ratios
can be directly used to compare multi-copy collective precision
to separably-attainable precision,
whereas the GMCRB requires a modification
for the two-copy setting
[
56
]
.
The multi-copy GMCRB
[
23
]
is
defined via

\begin{split}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}^{\otimes k}]&\coloneqq\min_{V_{\theta}^{(k)}\succcurlyeq 0}\Big\{\mathsf{Tr}(V_{\theta}^{(k)}){\Big|}\\
&\mathsf{Tr}(J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1}(kV^{(k)}_{\theta})^{-1})\leq d-1\Big\}\,,\end{split}

(159)

where
(k)
represents
k
-copy quantities.
Rephrasing the minimisation
in Eq. (
159
)
in terms of
kV_{\theta}^{(k)}

directly leads to

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}^{\otimes k}]=\frac{1}{k}\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}]\,,

(160)

meaning the GMCRB is additive
for measuring
k
copies of
\rho_{\theta}

simultaneously.
This complements
the well-known additivity of the SLD QFI
(Eqs. (72) & (73) in
[
72
]
),
on which the GMCRB is based.
We note that
the minimisation
in Eq. (
159
)
has the closed-form solution:

\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{GMCRB}}[\rho_{\theta}^{\otimes k}]=\frac{\left(\mathsf{Tr}[J_{\mathrm{SLD}}^{-1/2}]\right)^{2}}{k(d-1)}\,.

(161)

For
k=2
, Eq. (
160
)
implies that the ratio of the two-copy bound
to the one-copy bound is exactly half
for the GMCRB,
as can be seen in Fig.
8
.
In Fig.
8
,
we compare the ratio of two-copy to one-copy
bounds for the NHCRB and the GMCRB
over 5000 randomly generated qutrit states.
It is clear that the NHCRB
is not additive with respect to number of copies;
instead, the two-copy NHCRB is always
smaller than the two-copy GMCRB,
except for pure states where the two bounds agree.
This subadditivity of the NHCRB
and additivity of the GMCRB
can be attributed to the fact
that
the
k
-copy GMCRB considers
individually measuring each
of the
k
copies,
whereas the
k
-copy NHCRB
considers measuring the
k
-copies
simultaneously or collectively.
As a result, the gap between
two-copy NHCRB and GMCRB represents
the increase in precision from
two-copy measurements compared to
one-copy measurements.
Notably the optimal Fisher information
is also not additive under tensoring.

Moreover, for estimating fewer than
n_{\mathrm{max}}
parameters,
the NHCRB is strictly higher than the GMCRB
even in the one-copy case,
i.e., the former is a tighter bound.
Figure
9
depicts this
by considering the estimation of 2 to 8

qutrit states
(following the same methodology
as used in Fig.
4

to generate the states and parameters).
The GMCRB and NHCRB are computed for this model
and are both normalised by the HCRB.
It is clear that all the plotted points
lie above the
y=x
line,
numerically demonstrating that the NHCRB
is tighter than the GMCRB in this case.
Nonetheless, Fig.
9
also
reveals an increasing trend of the
ratio between the individual-optimal
and collective-optimal precisions
with number of parameters,
irrespective of the particular choice
of the individual-precision bound.

Appendix L
Random-Sampling of States and Parameters

For the random-sampling experiments
in Fig.
3
,
we generate random mixed qudit states
by first generating
an entry-wise random
d\times d
complex matrix
S
,
and then assigning
\rho_{\theta}=SS^{\dagger}/\Tr(S S^\dagger)
.
This procedure ensures
\rho_{\theta}=\rho_{\theta}^{\dagger}
,

\rho_{\theta}\succcurlyeq 0

and
\Tr(\rho_\theta)=1
.
The true GMM coefficients
(
\theta^{*}
for the GMM model)
can be found via
\Tr(\rho_\theta\lambda_j)
.
Unfortunately, this procedure
generates low-purity states
with a much higher probability
than high-purity states, which becomes
a problem for
d=3
and
4
.
We circumvent this issue
by generating additional samples
of the form
(1-p)\rho_{\theta}+p\,\mathds{1}_{d}/d

and
(1-p)\rho_{\theta}+p\outerproduct{+}{+}_{d}
,
where
p\in[0,1]
.
This sampling method is
non-uniform but our aim here is
not to sample uniformly
according to some measure,
but rather to find models
with extremal properties.
We compute
the ratio for the full-parameter
linear GMM model for all these states,
the random samples and their convex combinations,
to produce the yellow points in Fig.
3
.
The ratio-maximising (red) and ratio-minimising (blue) states
at fixed purity are found by numerically
maximising and minimising the ratio
over the state space.

For the random-sampling experiments
in Figs.
4

and
11
,
we generate random mixed qudit states
by the following technique.
For each
d
and
n
,
we uniformly-randomly choose
n_{\mathrm{max}}

coefficients
\{\phi_{j}\}_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}

from the interval
\left[-\sqrt{(d-1)/d},\sqrt{(d-1)/d}\right]
.
These define a
random state
\rho_{\theta}=\mathds{1}_{d}+\sum_{j\in[n_{\mathrm{max}}]}\phi_{j}\lambda_{j}

which is guaranteed to be trace-one and Hermitian,
but not positive.
We ensure the positive semi-definiteness of
\rho_{\theta}

by rejection sampling (discarding if it is not positive).
This process generates a valid random qudit state.
Next we generate the
n
arbitrary
parameters
\{\theta_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}

by generating at random the parameter derivatives

\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta}
,
which must be Hermitian and traceless.
We do this by writing each
\partial_{j}\rho_{\theta}

in the GMM basis and randomly generating
the coefficients in this basis.
Then we rejection-sample to ensure
the
n
parameter derivatives are linearly-independent,
and lead to a valid model.

Figure
11
indicates that
the
10^{4}
number of samples is relatively small
for higher
d
and
n
—the minimum ratio observed,
which should be close to one, is much larger
for large
d
and
n
.
This is because our sampling method
generates states with low purity with higher probability
and states with high purity with lower probability.
As a result, the increasing or decreasing trends
of the maximum observed ratio
with
n
or
d
are not perfect
for large
d
and
n
in Fig.
4
.

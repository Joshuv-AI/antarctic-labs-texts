Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the existence of free involutions on some Wall manifolds and we compute the mod 2 cohomology algebra of the correspondent orbit space.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 57S17; Secondary 55R20, 57S25.

1.
introduction

Let
X
be a topological space and let
G
be a topological group. A relevant question associated to a pair
(X,G)
is to determine when there is a continuous free action of
G
on
X,
as the classical example proposed by Hopf in
[
8
]
of classify all manifolds with universal cover homeomorphic to the
n-
dimensional sphere
S^{n},
which is equivalent to determine all finite groups that can act freely on
S^{n}.
In
[
13
]
J. Milnor provided an answer in this direction, showing that the symmetric group
\mathbb{S}_{3}
cannot act freely on
S^{n}.
A possible generalization can be made considering
X
as a product of spheres, as proposed by Dotzel et al. in
[
7
]
(for free actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
on
S^{m}\times S^{n}
) and by Jahren and Kwasik in
[
9
]
(for free involutions on
S^{1}\times S^{n}
).

When
G
acts freely on
X
a question that arises is about the nature of the corresponding orbit space, as in the standard examples: the real, complex and quaternionic projective spaces, which are orbit spaces of standard actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{2},

S^{1}
and
S^{3}
on spheres, respectively. The general examples that may appear suggest that calculating the cohomology of these orbit spaces can be complicated, particularly for compact manifolds other than spheres, as done, for example, in the work of Pergher et al.
[
19
]
about involutions on spaces of cohomology type
(a,b)
and Singh
[
20
]
for involutions on lens spaces. This paper concerns this question for
X
a Wall manifold of the type
Q(m,n)
and
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

The Wall manifolds were introduced by C. T. C. Wall in
[
21
]
, where it was shown that these manifolds give generators in odd dimensions for the unoriented bordism ring, as occur with the Dold manifolds and the Milnor manifolds, as shown by Dold in
[
6
]
and Milnor in
[
14
]
, respectively. Therefore, it is a relevant question to determine algebraic invariants of these manifolds, as done for example by Mukerjee in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
.

For free actions of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(or equivalently free involutions) on Dold manifolds, the problem was answered in 2018 by Morita et al. in
[
15
]
for the cases
P(1,\text{odd}),
and for all cases in 2019 by Dey in
[
3
]
using slight different techniques. About the Milnor manifolds, the problem was solved in 2019 by Dey and Singh in
[
4
]
. The main result that we present in this paper is the complete description of the cohomology of the orbit space of free involution on a Wall manifold of the type
Q(1,\text{odd}),
according to Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let us consider
G=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acting freely on a compact Hausdorff space
X
with mod 2 cohomology of a Wall manifold
Q(1,n),
with
n\geq 3
odd and such that the induced action on the mod 2 cohomology is trivial. Then

H^{\ast}(X/G;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta]}{\langle\alpha^{3},\beta^{3},\gamma^{2},\beta^{2}+\beta\gamma,\delta^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\rangle},

where
\deg\alpha=\deg\beta=\deg\gamma=1
and
\deg\delta=4.

The choice of the case
Q(1,n),
as an initial stage, is motivated by the work of Khare in
[
10
]
, where he shows that
Q(m,n)
bounds, if and only if,
n
is odd or
n=0
and
m
is odd. Therefore, these spaces can admit a free involution and particularly the choice
m=1
is convenient, because of the relationships established between the generators of the cohomology ring, while for arbitrary values of
m
may be very difficult to find obstructions for the large numbers of possible cohomology ring structures.

For this purpose this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we established the results on spectral sequences and the Borel fibration which are the main tools that we use. In Section
3
, we define the Wall manifolds, as a mapping torus of a Dold manifold, and do some hypotheses about the dimension to ensure that these manifolds admit a free involution and that the system of local coefficients induced by Borel fibration is simple. The Section
4
is dedicated to the proof that there is only one possible homotopy class of the orbit space of free involutions on some Wall manifolds and in Section
5
, we proved the main Theorem
1.1
.

3.
Wall manifolds

Let
m
and
n
be non negative integers. In 1956 A. Dold
[
6
]
defined the differentiable manifolds
P(m,n),
as the orbit space of the free involution

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{rccc}T:&S^{m}\times\mathbb{C}P^{n}&\to&S^{m}\times\mathbb{C}P^{n}\\
&(x,[z])&\mapsto&(-x,[\overline{z}])\\
\end{array}.

Dold further showed that the mod 2 cohomology ring of
P(m,n)
is a graduated polynomial ring in the variables
c\in H^{1}(P(m,n))
and
d\in H^{2}(P(m,n)),
truncated by the relations
c^{m+1}=0
and
d^{n+1}=0.

In 1960, Wall defined in
[
21
]
a new set of generators to
\mathfrak{R}_{\ast},
using the following general construction: Let
M
be a closed differentiable manifold of dimension
n
and let
f:M\to M
be a diffeomorphism. On the cartesian product
M\times[0,1]
we define the equivalence relation
(x,s)\sim_{f}(y,t)\Leftrightarrow y=f(x),
for
s=0
and
t=1,
or
(x,s)=(y,t)
otherwise, which identifies only the boundary points of
M\times[0,1].
The quotient space of
M\times[0,1]
by relation
\sim_{f}
is a closed differentiable manifold of dimension
n+1,
which we will denote by
\mathcal{W}(M,f).

The free involution
R\times 1:S^{m}\times\mathbb{C}P^{n}\to S^{m}\times\mathbb{C}P^{n},
where
R:S^{m}\to S^{m}
is the reflection of the last coordinate given by
R(x_{0},\cdots,x_{m})=(x_{0},\cdots,x_{m-1},-x_{m})
and
1:\mathbb{C}P^{n}\to\mathbb{C}P^{n}
is the identity, commutes with the involution
T
given in (
3.1
), so it induces a free involution
S:P(m,n)\to P(m,n).

Definition 3.1
.

For each pair of non negative integers
m,n,
the space
\mathcal{W}\left(P(m,n),S\right)
is a closed differentiable manifold of dimension
m+2n+1,
called Wall manifold and denoted by
Q(m,n).
Alternatively,
Q(m,n)
is defined as the mapping torus of the homeomorphism
S:P(m,n)\to P(m,n),
that is,

(3.2)

Q(m,n)=\dfrac{P(m,n)\times[0,1]}{([x,z],0)\sim(S[x,z],1)}.

Proposition 3.2

(
[
21
]
, Lemma 4)
.

The mod 2 cohomology ring of
Q(m,n)
is polynomially generated by elements
x,c\in H^{1}(Q(m,n))
and
d\in H^{2}(Q(m,n)),
truncated by the relations
x^{2}=0,

d^{n+1}=0
and
c^{m+1}=c^{m}\cdot x,
that is,

(3.3)

H^{\ast}(Q(m,n))=\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x,c,d]/\langle x^{2},c^{m+1}+c^{m}x,d^{n+1}\rangle.

Definition 3.3
.

A Wall structure on a manifold
M
is a homotopy class of maps
\beta:M\to S^{1}
such that
\beta^{\ast}(\iota)=w_{1}(M)
the first Stiefel-Whitney class, where
\iota\in H^{1}(S^{1})
is the generator.

The projection
P(m,n)\times[0,1]\to[0,1]
induces a fibration
\beta:Q(m,n)\to S^{1}
such that
\beta^{\ast}(\iota)=x=w_{1}(Q(m,n))
and this provides a Wall structure on the manifolds
Q(m,n)
as in the Definition
3.3
. In
[
21
]
, Wall showed through these constructions that

(3.4)

\{[\mathbb{R}P^{2i}],[Q(2^{r}-2,s2^{r})]\text{ };\text{ }i,r,s\geq 1\}

is a generator set of
\mathfrak{R}_{\ast}.
Before that, in 1956, Dold in
[
6
]
has shown that

(3.5)

\{[\mathbb{R}P^{2i}],[P(2^{r}-1,s2^{r})]\text{ };\text{ }i,r,s\geq 1\}

is a generator set of the bordism ring, and in 1965 Milnor showed in
[
14
]
that

(3.6)

\{[\mathbb{R}P^{2i}],[H(2^{k},2t2^{k})]\text{ };\text{ }i,k,t\geq 1\}

is also a generator set of
\mathfrak{R}_{\ast},
where
H(m,n)
is a (m+n-1)-dimensional Milnor manifold. Therefore it is a relevant question to get invariants of these manifolds.

Remark 3.4
.

If a topological space
X
admits a free action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle g\rangle
then this induces a free involution
T:X\to X
defined by
T(x)=g\ast x.
On other hand, the involution
T
induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}-
action on the cohomology ring of
X,
given by
T^{\ast}:H^{\ast}(X)\to H^{\ast}(X).

From the bordism theory (
[
2
]
, Theorem 24.2) we know that if a closed differentiable manifold
X
does not bounds, so it cannot admits a free involution. However, when
X
bounds there may or may not exists such action. In
[
10
]
, S. S. Khare shows that
Q(m,n)
bounds, if and only if,
n
is odd or
n=0
and
m
odd. Therefore, for the particular cases where
n\geq 1
is an odd integer such manifolds may or may not admits a free
\mathbb{Z}_{2}-
action.

Proposition 3.5
.

For all odd integer
n\geq 1
, the Wall manifold
Q(m,n)
admits a free involution.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:P(m,n)\times[0,1]\to P(m,n)\times[0,1]
the free involution defined by

\varphi\left([x,[z_{0}:\cdots:z_{n}]],s\right)=\left([-x,[-\overline{z}_{1}:\overline{z}_{0}:\cdots:-\overline{z}_{n}:\overline{z}_{n-1}]],s\right).

If
a=\left([x,[z]],s\right)\sim_{S}\left([y,[w]],t\right)=b,
that is,
s=0,

t=1
and

\left[y,[w]\right]=S\left[x,[z]\right]=\left[(x_{0},\cdots,x_{m-1},-x_{m}),[\overline{z}]\right],

then
\varphi(a)\sim_{S}\varphi(b).
Therefore,
\varphi
induces a free involution
F
on
Q(m,n).

∎

For
X=Q(1,n),
we have the relations
d^{n+1}=0,

c^{2}=c\cdot x
and
x^{2}=0,
which provides the following description of cohomology the groups:

\begin{array}[]{rcclcl}H^{2k+1}(X)&\cong&\langle c\cdot d^{k}\rangle\oplus\langle x\cdot d^{k}\rangle&\cong&\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2};&0\leq k\leq n;\\
H^{2k}(X)&\cong&\langle d^{k}\rangle\oplus\langle d^{k-1}\cdot c^{2}\rangle&\cong&\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2};&1\leq k\leq n,\end{array}

and
H^{2n+2}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\langle d^{n}\cdot c^{2}\rangle,
where
2n+2
is the dimension of
X.
Therefore, even when
m=1,
it is not immediate that an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on
H^{\ast}(Q(m,n))
has to be necessarily trivial.

By Proposition
3.5
, we can ask about the nature of the orbit space of some free
\mathbb{Z}_{2}-
action in a Wall manifold of the type
Q(m,n),
for
n
an odd natural. We will show, in Section
4
, that there is an unique mod 2 cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(Q(m,n)/\mathbb{Z}_{2}),
when
m=1
, whose structure is given in Theorem
1.1
. For the others values of
m,
we have at least an estimate for the number of possible cohomology ring structures.

To obtain this results, we will use the spectral sequence associated to the Borel fibration (
4.1
) and the equality (
2.5
). For that we need to impose some conditions to ensure that the action of
\pi_{1}(B_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}})=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}P^{\infty})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
in the cohomology of the fiber
Q(m,n)
is trivial, what we do in the next results.

Remark 3.6
.

If there is an action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle g\rangle
on
H^{\ast}(Q(m,n)),
as the cohomology ring is generated by the elements
x,c
and
d,
this action is entirely determined when we know the operations
g\ast x,

g\ast c
and
g\ast d,
that is, we just have to analyze the actions

(3.7)

g^{\ast}:H^{j}(Q(1,n))\to H^{j}(Q(1,n)),\,\,\alpha\mapsto g\ast\alpha,\text{ for }j=1,2,

and to analyze it we note that by the naturality of cup product,

(3.8)

g\ast(\alpha^{r}\cdot\beta^{s})=(g\ast\alpha)^{r}\cdot(g\ast\beta)^{s},\,\,\,\forall\alpha,\beta\in H^{\ast}(Q(m,n)),

for all
r,s\geq 0,
which will be useful in the next calculations.

Clearly, any action of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle g\rangle
on
H^{\ast}(Q(m,n))
must be trivial on the generator
x
, otherwise if
g\ast x=c
or
g\ast x=c+x,
then
0=g\ast x^{2}=c^{2},
which is a contradiction for all
m\geq 1.
So, we only need to analyze
g\ast c
and
g\ast d,
for which there are only the possibilities
g\ast c\in\{c,c+x\}
and
g\ast d\in\{d,cx,c^{2},cx+d,c^{2}+d,cx+c^{2},cx+c^{2}+d\}.

For
n>1,
if
g\ast d=cx,
then
g\ast d^{2}=(cx)^{2}=0,
which implies that
d^{2}=0,
but it is a contradiction. When
m=1,
then
c^{2}=cx,
and there is only the possibility
g\ast d\in\{d,cx+d\}.
In this case, we will see that the possibility
g\ast d=cx+d
also cannot to be occurs under some dimensional condition and for that we will use the following general theorem about involutions.

Theorem 3.7

(
[
1
]
, p. 407)
.

Let
T
be an involution on a finitistic space
X.
Suppose that
H^{i}(X)=0,
for
i>2l
and let
T^{\ast}
be the identity on
H^{2l}(X).
If there is an element
a\in H^{l}(X)
such that
a\cdot T^{\ast}(a)\neq 0
then the fixed point set of
T
is nonempty.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
T
be a free involution in
Q(1,n)
and let
T^{\ast}
be the induced homomorphism on the cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(Q(1,n)).
If
n\equiv 1(\text{mod }4),
then
T^{\ast}(d)=d.

Proof.

Let us consider
\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\langle g\rangle
and let us suppose that
T^{\ast}(d)=g\ast d=cx+d.
For
s=(n+1)/2,
let us take
d^{s}\in H^{n+1}(Q(1,n)).
As
n\equiv 1(\text{mod }4),
then
s=\binom{s}{s-1}
is odd, therefore

d^{s}T^{\ast}(d^{s})=d^{s}(cx+d)^{s}=d^{s}(cxd^{s-1}+d^{s})=d^{n}cx\neq 0,

since
\langle d^{n}cx\rangle=H^{2n+2}(Q(1,n)).
Taking
a=d^{s}
and
l=n+1,
it follows from Theorem
3.7
that the fixed point set of
T
is nonempty, what is not true.
∎

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
T
be a free involution on
Q(1,n)
and let
T^{\ast}
be the induced
\mathbb{Z}_{2}-
action on
H^{\ast}(Q(1,n)).
If
T^{\ast}(c)=c
and
n\equiv 1(\text{mod }4),
then
T^{\ast}
is trivial.

We observe that even if
c\neq T^{\ast}(c)=c+x,
we have
T^{\ast}(\alpha)=\alpha,
for all
\alpha\in H^{i}(Q(1,n)),

i>1,
that is, the action is trivial, except for the elements
c
and
c+x
in the cohomology ring.

When
m
is even, then the induced action has to be trivial on the generators
x
and
c
of
H^{\ast}(Q(m,n))
, otherwise if
g\ast c=c+x,
then

(3.9)

g\ast c^{m+1}=(c+x)^{m+1}=c^{m+1}+c^{m}x=0,

but we know that
c^{m+1}=c^{m}x\neq 0.
It still implies that
g\ast d\neq c^{2}
and
g\ast d\neq c^{2}+cx.
In fact, as the action induces an automorphism
\varphi
on the cohomology groups, if
g\ast d=c^{2},
then
d+c^{2}\in\ker\varphi=\{0\}
and if
g\ast d=c^{2}+cx,
then
d+c^{2}+cx\in\ker\varphi=\{0\}
. So, in this case there is only the possibility

(3.10)

T^{\ast}(d)=g\ast d\in\{d,c^{2}+d,cx+d,c^{2}+cx+d\}.

4.
Uniqueness of cohomology ring

The main purpose of this section is to show that there is only one possible homotopy class of the orbit space of free involutions on
Q(1,n),
under some hypotheses on
n.
For this, let us consider the
G-
space
Q(1,n),
where
G
is the group
\mathbb{Z}_{2},
and

(4.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q(1,n)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q(1,n)_{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B_{G}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

is the associated Borel fibration.

Under the hypothesis of Corollary
3.9
, the group
\pi_{1}(B_{G})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts trivially on the cohomology of the fiber
Q(1,n),
so there is a first quadrant cohomology spectral sequence
\{E_{r}^{\ast,\ast},d_{r}\},
where

(4.2)

E_{2}^{p,q}\cong H^{p}(B_{G})\otimes_{G}H^{q}(Q(1,n)),

which converges as an algebra to
H^{\ast}(Q(1,n)_{G})\cong H^{\ast}\left(Q(1,n)/G\right).

We will use the spectral sequence whose
E_{2}-
term is given in (
4.2
) to get possibles structures of the cohomology ring
H^{\ast}(X/G),
through the isomorphism (
2.3
), where
X=Q(1,n),
for an appropriate
n.

As the action is free it follows from Theorem
2.2
that this sequence does not collapse in the
E_{2}-
term, otherwise there would be infinite nonzero elements in the orbit space cohomology. So, there are
r_{i}\geq 2
, for which some differentials
d_{r_{i}}^{p,q}:E_{r_{i}}^{p,q}\to E_{r_{i}}^{p+r_{i},q+1-r_{i}}
are non-trivial. If
r\geq 2
is the smaller integer among the
r_{i}^{\prime}
s, then
E_{2}=\cdots=E_{r},
because
d_{s}=0,
for any
2\leq s\leq r-1.
Therefore,

(4.3)

E_{s+1}^{p,q}=\ker(d_{s}^{p,q})/\text{im}(d_{s}^{p-s,q+s-1})=E_{s}^{p,q}/\{0\}=E_{s}^{p,q},

for all
p,q\geq 0.

In order to analyze the possible cases when
d_{r}
is nontrivial, as
x,c
and
d
are the generators of
H^{\ast}(X),
it is enough to look at the actions under such elements, that is, we will look at the possible values to
d_{r}^{0,1}(1\otimes x),

d_{r}^{0,1}(1\otimes c)
and
d_{r}^{0,2}(1\otimes d).

Knowing that the sequence is a first quadrant spectral sequence, moreover
\text{im}(d_{r}^{0,1})\subseteq E_{r}^{r,2-r}
and
\text{im}(d_{r}^{0,2})\subseteq E_{r}^{r,3-r},
then these differentials can be non trivial only when
r=2
or
r=3.
However, at
E_{3}-
page it is immediate that
d_{3}^{0,1}(1\otimes x)=d_{3}^{0,1}(1\otimes c)=0,

which implies that only
d_{3}^{0,2}(1\otimes d)

Lemma 4.1

If

d_{i}^{l,k}(t^{l}\otimes c^{k})=0,

d_{i}^{l,2k}(t^{l}\otimes d^{k})=0

The other cases are similar.

Remark 4.3

For

Dielectric response of electrons with strong local correlations and long-ranged Coulomb interactions
B Sriram Shastry, Michael Arciniaga

Abstract
Motivated by recent experiments, we append long ranged Coulomb interactions to dominant strong local correlations and study the resulting t t - J J - V C V_{C} model for the 2-dimensional cuprate materials. This model includes the effect of short ranged Hubbard-Gutzwiller-Kanamori type correlations and long ranged Coulomb interactions on tight binding electrons. We calculate the { q → , ω } \{{\vec{q}},\omega\} dependent charge density fluctuations in this model using the extremely correlated Fermi liquid theory characterized by quasiparticles with very small weight Z Z . We develop a novel set of formulae to represent the dynamical charge susceptibility and the dielectric function, using a version of the charge-current continuity equation for a band system, valid for arbitrary q → {\vec{q}} . Combining these ingredients, we present results for the irreducible dynamical charge susceptibility χ ~ ρ ​ ρ ​ ( q → , ω ) \widetilde{\chi}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega) , (longitudinal) dielectric function ε ⁡ ( q → , ω ) \varepsilon({\vec{q}},\omega) , current susceptibility χ ~ J ​ J ​ ( q → , ω ) \widetilde{\chi}_{JJ}({\vec{q}},\omega) , conductivity σ ⁡ ( q → , ω ) \sigma({\vec{q}},\omega) , and the plasma frequency for any q → {\vec{q}} . We also present calculations for the first moment of the structure function and discuss a characteristic energy scale Ω p ​ ( q → ) \Omega_{p}({\vec{q}}) which locates a peak in Im ​ χ ~ ρ ​ ρ ​ ( q → , ω ) \mathrm{Im}\,\widetilde{\chi}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega) .

Introduction

The role of strong local correlations and their interplay with long ranged Coulomb interactions, is an important problem in condensed matter physics. In the context of the metal insulator (Mott-Hubbard) transition of a Hubbard-Gutzwiller-Kanamori type model of strong correlations with added long ranged Coulomb interactions, early work

4

We turn to the irreducible susceptibilities

We denote the pair of subscripts
\{\rho,W\}
by a symbol
\mu

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\mu\nu}(q)=\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\mu\nu}(q)-\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}}V({\vec{q}})\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\mu\rho}(q)\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\nu}(q).

(31)

This can be solved for all the components and displays the screened nature of the resulting susceptibilities. The density-density response
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}
is simplest since all terms on the right have the same subscripts. Gathering terms
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\mu\nu}(q)
on the left, we find

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}(q)=\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(q)}{1+\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}}V({\vec{q}})\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(\vec{q},\omega)}.

Using Eq. (
30

\displaystyle\varepsilon(q)\equiv\varepsilon(\vec{q},\omega)=1+\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}}V({\vec{q}})\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(\vec{q},\omega),

(33)

with the Coulomb potential given by Eq. (
6
,
7

the relationships between the four susceptibilities are given by

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}(q)=\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(q)}{\varepsilon({q})}

(34)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho W}(q)=\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho W}(q)}{\varepsilon(q)},

(35)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{W\rho}(q)=\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{W\rho}(q)}{\varepsilon(q)},\;\;\;

(36)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{WW}(q)=\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}(q)-\frac{V({\vec{q}})}{q_{e}^{2}\varepsilon(q)}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{W\rho}(q)\;\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho W}(q).

It is worth noting the connection between these results and the equations presented by Nozières
[

for the homogeneous electron gas — denoted by a prefix “N”. The vertex

(see Eq. (

in Ref.
[

\frac{i}{2\pi\Omega}\times S_{\alpha,\beta}

The main problem of interest in this work is the

\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(\vec{q},\omega)
and
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}(\vec{q},\omega)
at all
\{{\vec{q}},\omega\}
, using only the above
{\cal G}
within a bubble scheme
{\cal G}{\cal G}
as described below in Eq. (

5.1
Formula for irreducible susceptibility in terms of a self-energy
\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)

We begin with a novel representation for the susceptibility using the freedom to define suitable generalized self-energies of Green’s functions, as discussed in
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
. We start from the high frequency moment expansion Eq. (
F.8
), in inverse powers of
\omega^{2}
as discussed in Appendix-(
F.2
). This series can be formally rewritten in a continued fraction representation following Mori
[
36
,
37
]
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{1}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{2}-\alpha_{1}({\vec{q}})-\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)}

(47)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{2}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{2}-\alpha_{2}({\vec{q}})-\Sigma^{(1)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)}

(48)

where
\beta_{1}=-\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}_{{\vec{q}}}
is the negative of the first moment of frequency Eq. (
F.10
,
F.14
), and
\Sigma^{(m)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)
with
m=0,1,\ldots
represent the successive “self-energies”. They are characterized by the property that for
\omega\gg t
they behave as
\Sigma^{(m)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)\sim\frac{\beta_{m+2}}{\omega^{2}}
, and thus vanish. The coefficients
\alpha_{m},\beta_{m}
are functions of
{\vec{q}}
and can be found in principle, in terms of the frequency moments. It is more convenient for our purpose to rewrite Eq. (

\alpha_{1}
in favor of the static limit of
\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}
, and using
\beta_{1}=-\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}_{{\vec{q}}}
. This leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\left(\frac{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}+\frac{1}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}\left(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)-\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},0)\right)\right)^{-1}.

(49)

We can simplify the notation by defining a new self-energy type function

\displaystyle\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)=\frac{1}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}\left(\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},0)-\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right),

(50)

with
\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})
detailed in Eq. (
F.10
,
F.12
). The irreducible susceptibility is now given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\left(\frac{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}-\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)\right)^{-1}.

(51)

This self-energy
\Psi
can be found from
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)
, if the latter is known, by inversion of Eq. (
51
), and can be expressed formally in terms of the higher moments
\widetilde{\omega}^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})
using Eq. (
F.8
)
[
35
,
36
,
37
]
. The self energy vanishes in the static limit by construction

\displaystyle\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)|_{\omega\to 0}=0,

(52)

and has a finite high frequency limit (from the first term in Eq. (
50
)).

We note that from the Lehmann representation of
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)
that the
\Sigma^{(m)}
in complex
\omega
are analytic everywhere except the real axis. This implies that all singularities are located on the real axis, and hence these can be further represented in the form

\displaystyle\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\omega-\nu+i\eta}

(53)

where
\eta=0^{+}

\Sigma^{(0)}_{\chi}({\vec{q}},0)
and
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},0)
are real, it follows from Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)=-q_{e}^{2}N_{s}\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{-1}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega).

(54)

Using the analyticity of
\Psi
in the upper half complex
\omega
plane, together with Eq. (
52

\Psi
in terms of the imaginary part of the inverse susceptibility

\displaystyle\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)=(q_{e}^{2}N_{s})\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{-1}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\omega-\nu+i\eta}+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{-1}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\nu}\right).

(55)

Here the second term is expected to be finite due to the odd-ness in frequency of
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{-1}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)

\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)

\mathrm{Re}\,\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)

\omega

In summary the susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)

\Psi({\vec{q}},\omega)
satisfying Eq. (

{\vec{q}}
only:
(a) the static susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},0)

{\vec{q}}

F.12

Separating

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\left(\frac{1}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}\{\Omega^{2}({\vec{q}},\omega)-\omega^{2}\}-i\Psi^{\prime\prime}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right)^{-1},

(56)

and hence

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\frac{[\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})]^{2}\Psi^{\prime\prime}({\vec{q}},\omega)}{[\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})\Psi^{\prime\prime}({\vec{q}},\omega)]^{2}+\{\Omega^{2}({\vec{q}},\omega)-\omega^{2}\}^{2}}.

(57)

In these expressions the characteristic energy scale
\Omega

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})\left(\frac{d\mu}{dn}\gamma({\vec{q}})-\Psi^{\prime}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right)

(58)

\displaystyle\gamma({\vec{q}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}(0,0)}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},0)},\;\;\gamma(0)=1,

(59)

and we made use of the exact result Eq. (
E.1
) to express the static limit of the susceptibility in terms of the thermodynamic variable
\frac{d\mu}{dn}
. Recall that the compressibility
\chi_{comp}=\frac{1}{2n(0)}\frac{dn}{d\mu}\,\chi^{(non)}_{comp}
, where
n(0)
is the density of states per site per spin, and hence this representation also satisfies the compressibility sum-rule Eq. (
E.1
).

From Eq. (
57
) we see that
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)

is expected to have peaks. The peak frequency is termed as
\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
, and identified with
\omega_{0}
, the positive root of

\displaystyle\omega_{0}^{2}=\Omega^{2}({\vec{q}},\omega_{0}),\;\;\mbox{ i.e. }\;\;\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})=\omega_{0}.

(60)

The root is approximately located at the energy scale
\Omega({\vec{q}},0)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})\sim\Omega({\vec{q}},0)=\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})\,\frac{d\mu}{dn}\gamma({\vec{q}})}.

(61)

We display alternate versions of this expression in Eq. (
84
,
87
).
The width of the peak is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}({\vec{q}})=\sqrt{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})\Psi^{\prime\prime}({\vec{q}},\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}}))}.

(62)

As we explicitly see later, the approximation Eq. (
61
) for
\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
at low
{\vec{q}}
, is larger than the exact peak frequency
\omega_{0}
in Eq. (
60
) by a factor of
\sim
2, the discrepancy arising from the substantial breadth of the peak,
\Gamma_{p}\ {\raise-2.15277pt\hbox{$\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\sim}\limits^{>}}$}}\ \Omega_{p}
.
In terms of these variable we can approximately write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)\sim\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})\Gamma^{2}_{p}({\vec{q}})}{\Gamma^{4}_{p}({\vec{q}})+\{\Omega_{p}^{2}({\vec{q}})-\omega^{2}\}^{2}}.

(63)

The representation Eq. (
51
) also exactly satisfies the known high
\omega
behavior Eq. (
F.8
), and therefore reproduces the correct plasma frequency Eq. (
E.7
). It should also be clear that with obvious changes to the variables, the above formulas Eq. (
51
,
56
) can be useful for other physical situations such as the homogeneous electron gas etc.

5.2
Approximate formulas for the irreducible susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)

It is very convenient to calculate the susceptibility starting from formulas Eq. (
51
). The input variables in Eq. (
51
,
56
) are found from the ECFL theory, using suitable approximations described next. We make extensive use of the
bubble approximation
, where in taking the derivative with respect to the external potential in Eq. (
D.7
), the
{\cal G}
is assumed to depend on this potential
only
through the explicit terms as in Eq. (
D.5
), and the implicit dependence via the other factors are thrown out. For
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}
we find an approximate expression from this bubble approximation

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}(\vec{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q_{e}^{2}\sum_{k\sigma}{\cal G}(k){\cal G}(k+q),

(64)

and evaluating
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}
within the bubble approximation

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}(\vec{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-q_{e}^{2}\sum_{k\sigma}{\cal G}(k){\cal G}(k+q)(\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{k+q})^{2}.

(65)

Using the spectral representation Eq. (
A.13
) for
{\cal G}
the latter reduces to

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}(\vec{q},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2q_{e}^{2}\sum_{k}(\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{k+q})^{2}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}}\frac{f(\nu_{1})-f(\nu_{2})}{\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}-\omega-i0^{+}}A(k,\nu_{1})A(k+q,\nu_{2}),\;\;

where
\int_{\nu}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu
. The density response
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}(\vec{q},\omega)
is found by dropping the factor
(\varepsilon_{k}-\varepsilon_{k+q})^{2}
in this formula. The spectral functions in our model (see Fig. (
1
)) consist of a quasiparticle part with a much reduced weight
Z\ll 1
, and an extended background part. The indicated integrations can be performed numerically.

Our two starting points are susceptibilities found from these bubble estimates and Eq. (
43
)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)

(67)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{s}\kappa({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{2}}\left(\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}_{WW}({\vec{q}},\omega)/\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}_{WW}({\vec{q}},0)-1\right).

(68)

The estimate
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}
provides a reasonable estimate in the static limit for the susceptibility. The magnitude of the compressibility, found by taking the
{\vec{q}}\to 0
limit, is much smaller than the band value, as seen in Fig. (
3
). It is comparable for most densities to that found from thermodynamical evaluation of
\frac{dn}{d\mu}
(see Fig. (
3
)). At finite
{\vec{q}}
its shape is compared to that of the band susceptibility apart from some interchanges of magnitudes between different directions (see Fig. (
4
)). The imaginary part of
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}
shows a quasiparticle contribution of the type
\chi^{\prime\prime}\propto\frac{\pi\omega}{|{\vec{q}}|v_{F}}
for very small
\omega<|{\vec{q}}|v_{F}Z
. For larger
\omega
, it has a broad contribution from the background spectral functions, but does not give the first moment of frequency, and is therefore not satisfactory.

For
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}
we verify that
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{WW}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}(\vec{q},0)
agrees closely with
N_{s}\kappa({\vec{q}})
, calculated independently using a single Green’s function
{\cal G}
from Eq. (
20
), at all
{\vec{q}}
(see Fig. (
5
)). The estimate
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}
is expected to be satisfactory at finite (high) frequencies since it is constructed to satisfy the first moment of frequency in the high
\omega
limit. However at low
\omega
it is does not capture the quasiparticle contribution discussed above. Further the static limit — found from the
{\cal O}(\omega^{2})
limiting behavior of
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{\mbox{\tiny Bub}}_{WW}({\vec{q}},\omega)
— does not display the behavior expected for an incompressible system discussed above. Thus the two estimates are successful in almost non-overlapping regimes of frequency.

Before proceeding we note that the two expressions Eq. (
67
,
68
) lead to two different self energies

\displaystyle\Psi_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}_{A}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}=\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)}

(69)

\displaystyle\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}_{B}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}=\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)}.

(70)

The first frequency moment
\widetilde{\omega}_{B}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})
in the second equation Eq. (
70
) is in fact exact, i.e.
\widetilde{\omega}_{B}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})=\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})
, as explained above. The corresponding frequency
\widetilde{\omega}_{A}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})
is not correct, and we show that it is possible to avoid using it altogether.

We next construct two approximations to the irreducible susceptibility

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}^{(I)}({\vec{q}},\omega)\;\mbox{ and }\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}^{(II)}({\vec{q}},\omega).

(71)

When the context is clear we drop the subscript and use the simplified notation

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}^{(I,II)}({\vec{q}},\omega)\leftrightarrow\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I,II)}({\vec{q}},\omega).

(72)

Consider the approximate susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}
combining the two susceptibilities
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A},\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}
in the form

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{1}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}({\vec{q}},0)}+\frac{1}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)}\right\}^{-1}.

(73)

We can rewrite this using Eq. (
70
) in the form

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{s}q_{e}^{2}\left\{\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}-\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right\}^{-1}.

(75)

Since
\Psi_{B}(\vec{q},0)=0
, we see that
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}({\vec{q}},\omega)
has the correct static limit, and since
\Psi_{B}(\vec{q},\omega)
vanishes at high
\omega
, the approximate
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}({\vec{q}},\omega)
also has the correct plasma frequency, while respecting the strong local correlations. It therefore serves as a reasonable first approximation over the entire frequency domain.

A feature that is missing from
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(I)}
in Eq. (
75
), is the quasi-particle contribution. This was present in Eq. (
67
), but was left out in Eq. (
75
) since we threw out all the frequency dependence of
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}
. We can incorporate this contribution, again approximately, by making a correction to
\Psi_{B}
taken from
\Psi_{A}
. Inspection shows that for small
{\vec{q}},\omega
the quasiparticle feature in
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}
arises from a contribution
\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{A}\propto\frac{\omega}{|{\vec{q}}|v_{f}}
. It is analogous to the familiar correction that arises in the Lindhard function from quasiparticles
[
5
,
38
,
39
]
. This quasiparticle contribution leads to
|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)|>|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)|
for small enough
\omega
at a fixed
{\vec{q}}
, while for larger
|\omega|
we
find

|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)|\gg|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)|
. To further refine the approximation, we keep this observation in mind and add the incremental
\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)
containing the quasiparticle damping to
\Psi_{B}
,

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{(II)}({\vec{q}},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{s}q_{e}^{2}\left\{\frac{N_{s}q_{e}^{2}}{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{A}({\vec{q}},0)}-\frac{\omega^{2}}{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}-\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)-\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right\}^{-1}.

In order to determine the appropriate correction term
\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)
in the above expression, we argue as follows.

Since
\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}
should add the damping due to quasiparticles, with
\omega>0
we choose

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)+\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\mbox{Max}\{\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega),\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)\}.

(77)

This construct isolates the excess damping present in
\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)
over and above that in
\Psi_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)
, due to quasiparticles at low
\omega
. In slightly more technical terms

\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)
vanishes outside the region
|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{A}({\vec{q}},\omega)|>|\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi_{B}({\vec{q}},\omega)|
. For
\omega<0
a similar argument can be used keeping in mind the odd-ness of
\mathrm{Im}\,\Psi^{\prime}s
in
\omega
, we use
Min
instead of
Max
in Eq. (
77
). The real part of
\delta\Psi_{QP}
can be calculated using the Kramers-Kronig relation, i.e. by taking the real part in Eq. (
55
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-{\cal P}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\omega-\nu}-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\,\frac{\mathrm{Im}\,\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\nu},

(78)

whereby we guarantee that
\delta\Psi_{QP}({\vec{q}},0)=0
.

On further separating the complex self-energies, these two approximate susceptibilities Eq. (
75
,
) lead to expressions analogous to Eq. (
56

59

\Omega
in Eq. (
58

With the 2-d lattice constant

6.1
Basic results from ECFL on spectral function, momentum distribution and compressibility

We begin by illustrating the basic results of the ECFL theory for
A({\vec{k}},\omega)
the single electron spectral function, and
m_{k}
the momentum distribution function, which display the strong redistribution of spectral weight from a Fermi gas due to correlations. This is followed by showing the compressibility within ECFL. The compressibility is reduced considerably from the Fermi gas due to Gutzwiller type correlations, as argued originally in
[
1

3
]

Conclusions and Discussion

We have presented results from our calculation of the dynamics of electron fluctuations in the
t
-
J
-
V_{C}
model of Eq. (
2
). We see that the small quasiparticle weight in the normal state gives rise to a broad background in the electron spectral weight Fig. (
1
). This in turn leads to a smearing of sharp features in the dynamical correlations, as we see in Fig. (
9
). The small
Z_{K_{F}}
also reflects in the flattening of the momentum distribution, as seen in Fig. (
2
).

The plasmon energy
\omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
can be extracted in several distinct ways. We have discussed two methods already, from the peaks in
-\mathrm{Im}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon({\vec{q}},\omega)})
, or from the zeroes of
\mathrm{Re}\,\varepsilon({\vec{q}},\omega)
as seen in Fig. (
11
,
12
). There is yet another possibility, namely from a measurement of the first frequency moment of the structure function
S({\vec{q}},\omega)
as in Eq. (
F.10
,
F.12
). Here the frequency integration must be large enough to contain all the weight from the primary band containing the Fermi level, but small enough to exclude interband effects. This balance is familiar from studies of optical conductivity in cuprates
[
42
]
, where satisfying the various versions of the
f
-sumrule involves parallel issues.

The results for
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)
presented in Fig. (
10
) display a slow fall off for
\omega>\Omega_{p}
over a substantial range. This behaviour is similar to the fall off seen experimentally
[
11
,
10
]
. From Eq. (
83
) this implies that the current susceptibility
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{JJ}({\vec{q}},\omega)
should flatten out in the same
\omega
range. This is indeed seen in Fig. (
15
) in panels (a,c). We should note that in the panels (b,d) of this figure, the conductivity shows a related sluggish fall off with
\omega
, consistent with Eq. (
83
).

In the region
|\omega|\leq\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
, our calculations show that the quasiparticle contribution to
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)
leads to a linear in
\omega
behavior, as seen in the contrast between the two plots in Fig. (
9
), and in all the low
{\vec{q}}
plots of in Fig. (
10
). A low magnitude of
Z_{K_{F}}
, as in the ECFL theory makes the linear regime small, but remain non-zero, and hence worth looking for in data.

Finally we believe that extracting systematically the energy scale
\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
for a range of small
{\vec{q}}
values is an important task for future experimental studies. In addition to tracking the peaks of the imaginary parts of the various susceptibilities noted in Eq. (
83
), as well as Fig. (
15
) and related figures, approximately evaluating the formula for the first frequency moment Eq. (
87
,
F.20
) using data could provide a useful alternative. It is possibly a difficult task if the
\Omega_{p}({\vec{q}})
is not sufficiently larger than the experimental resolution, and if other sources such as phonons contribute strongly to the scattering intensity. Such a study would provide insight into the nature of the metallic state in the cuprates.

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{AB}(\tau)=\langle T_{\tau}A(\tau)B(0)\rangle,\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{AB}(i\Omega_{\nu})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\beta}^{\beta}d\tau e^{i\Omega_{\nu}\tau}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{AB}(\tau),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma({\vec{q}},\omega)=\frac{1}{\omega N_{s}}\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{JJ}({\vec{q}},\omega),

(E.16)

thus relating the dissipative part of conductivity with
\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{JJ}({\vec{q}},\omega)/\omega
. In Eq. (
E.16
) we have suppressed an implicit prefactor
\frac{1}{a_{0}}
, which needs modification for quasi 2-dimensional system such as the cuprate materials analyzed in
[
22
,
20
,
21
]
. Here the theory proceeds by assuming that the unit cell is body centered tetragonal instead of cubic. Here
a_{0}
is replaced by
c_{0}
, the separation between two copper oxide layers in the simple case of single layer cuprates, so that
c_{0}\gg a_{0}
. The different layers are assumed to be decoupled as far as electron hopping is concerned, while their polarizations add up. We then obtain an appropriate generalization of Eq. (
E.16
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma({\vec{q}},\omega)=\frac{q_{e}^{2}}{c_{0}h}\left(\frac{h}{q_{e}^{2}\omega N_{s}}\mathrm{Im}\,\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{JJ}({\vec{q}},\omega)\right),

(E.17)

where the object in parentheses is
{\cal O}(1)
and dimensionless. We note that Eq. (
E.15
) is almost identical to the standard formula for the optical conductivity
\sigma(\omega)
obtained from the Kubo formula for Hubbard model or
t
-
J
model type systems without the long ranged Coulomb interaction, e.g. see Eq. (A1-A5) in
[
45
]
. The only change is that the screened current susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{JJ}
replaces the unscreened
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{JJ}
. This object can be obtained from Eq. (
37

{\vec{q}}
. Physically the tilde means that the calculation of the current-current correlators must discard direct contributions from the Coulomb potential. The f-sumrule for the conductivity
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\;\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(\omega)={\cal T}
given in Eq. (
23

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\,\sigma(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\nu\frac{\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma(\nu)}{\omega-\nu},

(E.18)

taking the limit
\omega\gg 0
, and finally comparing the expression with the coefficient of
1/\omega
in Eq. (
E.15

Appendix F
Structure Function Frequency Moments

The recent momentum dependent electron energy loss experiments (M-EELS)
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
probe charge response inferred from the inelastic momentum resolved scattering of electrons from the surface of the high
T_{c}
superconductor Bi2212
Bi_{2}Sr_{2}CaCu_{2}O_{8+x}

\displaystyle S_{\rho\rho}(\vec{q},\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{2\pi}e^{i\omega t}\langle\rho_{\vec{q}}(t)\rho_{-\vec{q}}(0)\rangle=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)}{1-e^{-\beta\omega}},

over a substantial portion of the
\vec{q},\omega
region with remarkably high precision. The energy resolution
\Delta\omega\sim 2
meV. Here
\vec{q}

\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}
, in fact using the odd-ness of
\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}
we can extract this object by combining energy loss and energy gain data:

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\pi\left(S_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)-S_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},-\omega)\right)

(F.2)

The work of
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
presents data for the
\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}(\omega)
as well as the inferred screened susceptibility
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}
.

F.1
High frequency moments: reducible susceptibility

Using the familiar analyticity of
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)
in the upper half of the complex
\omega
plane, we can write a spectral representation

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\,\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\omega-\nu+i0^{+}}.

(F.3)

We note that
\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)
is odd in
\omega
and hence as
\omega\gg 0
we get a moment expansion with even terms
[
46
]

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\gg 0}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-q_{e}^{2}N_{s}\left(\frac{\omega^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{(3)}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{4}}+\ldots\right),

(F.4)

where the frequency moments
\omega^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})
are given by

\displaystyle\omega^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})=\frac{1}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\pi}\omega^{2j+1}\mbox{\large$\chi$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega),

(F.5)

or upon using Eq. (
F.2
)

\displaystyle\omega^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})=\frac{2}{q_{e}^{2}N_{s}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{d\omega}\omega^{2j+1}S({\vec{q}},\omega).

(F.6)

F.2
High frequency moments: irreducible susceptibility

In the presence of long-ranged Coulomb interactions it is necessary
[
5
]
to distinguish between reducible susceptibility (or polarization)
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}
and the irreducible susceptibility (or polarization)
\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}

\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}
can be shown to satisfy a spectral representation

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\nu\,\frac{\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}^{\prime\prime}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\nu)}{\omega-\nu+i0^{+}}.

(F.7)

This is completely analogous to Eq. (
F.3
), and using a moment expansion analogous to Eq. (
F.4
) we get

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\gg 0}\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=-q_{e}^{2}N_{s}\left(\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{2}}+\frac{\widetilde{\omega}^{(3)}({\vec{q}})}{\omega^{4}}+\ldots\right),

(F.8)

In order to determine the moments
\widetilde{\omega}^{(2l+1)}({\vec{q}})

32

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\widetilde{\chi}$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)=\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)}{1-\frac{V({\vec{q}})}{N_{s}}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\rho\rho}({\vec{q}},\omega)}.

(F.9)

We next plug into this expression the high frequency expansion Eq. (
F.4
) giving an infinite series in
\frac{1}{\omega^{2}}

F.8

\widetilde{\omega}^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})

{\omega}^{(2j+1)}({\vec{q}})

\displaystyle\widetilde{\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}})={\omega}^{(1)}({\vec{q}}).

We make extensive use of the first moment

\kappa({\vec{q}})

Comparing the leading term with the expression in Eq. (
E.6

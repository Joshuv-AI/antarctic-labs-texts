Viennot Shadows and Graded Module Structure in Colored Permutation Groups
Jasper M. Liu

Abstract
. Let 𝐱 n × n \mathbf{x}_{n\times n} be a matrix of n × n n\times n variables, and let ℂ ⁡ [ 𝐱 n × n ] \mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}] be the polynomial ring on these variables. Let 𝔖 n , r \mathfrak{S}_{n,r} be the group of colored permutations, consisting of n × n n\times n complex matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column, where each nonzero entry is an r r -th root of unity. We associate an ideal I 𝔖 n , r ⊆ ℂ ⁡ [ 𝐱 n × n ] I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}] with the group 𝔖 n , r \mathfrak{S}_{n,r} , and use orbit harmonics to give an ideal-theoretic extension of the Viennot shadow line construction to 𝔖 n , r \mathfrak{S}_{n,r} . This extension gives a standard monomial basis of ℂ ⁡ [ 𝐱 n × n ] / I 𝔖 n , r \mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}} , and introduces an analogous definition of “longest increasing subsequence” to the group 𝔖 n , r \mathfrak{S}_{n,r} . 1.
Introduction

Longest increasing subsequences in the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
have been a well-studied object in combinatorics. In this paper, we apply orbit harmonics and ideal-theoretic extensions of Viennot’s shadow line construction to introduce an analogous definition of longest increasing subsequence for the colored permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the symmetric group on
[n]=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
. Given

, we write
w
in its one line notation
w=[w(1),w(2),\dots,w(n)]
. An

l

is a sequence of integers
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{l}\leq n
such that
w(i_{1})<w(i_{2})<\dots<w(i_{l})
. We write

(1.1)

\mathrm{lis}(w)=\max\{k:w\text{ has an increasing subsequence of length }k\}

for the length of the longest increasing subsequence of
w
. Let
a_{n,k}
denote the number of permutations
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\mathrm{lis}(w)=k
. For any positive integer
n
, Chen
[
2
]
conjectured that the sequence
(a_{n,1},a_{n,2},\dots,a_{n,n})
is log-concave, that is,

(1.2)

a_{n,i}\cdot a_{n,i+2}\leq a_{n,i+1}^{2}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq n-2.

Let
\mathbf{x}
be a finite set of variables, and let
\mathbb{C}
be the field of complex numbers. Let
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
be the polynomial ring with variable set
\mathbf{x}
. If

is a graded ideal in

of

(1.3)

where

is the degree

.

In
[

]
, Rhoades studied the following ideal:

.

and showed that (Theorem

Focusing on the case of

2.
Background

2.1.
Gröbner Theory

Let
\mathbf{x}_{N}=(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})
be a finite list of variables, and let
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
be the polynomial ring over these variables. A total order
<
on the set of monomials in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
is called a
monomial order
if

•

1\leq m
for any monomial
m
,

•

for any monomials
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
, we have that
m_{1}<m_{2}
implies
m_{1}m_{3}<m_{2}m_{3}
.

Given any polynomial
f
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
, the
initial monomial

\textrm{in}_{<}f
of
f
is defined to be the largest monomial term in
f
with respect to
<
. The
initial ideal
of an ideal
I
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
is the ideal generated by the initial monomials of all polynomials in
I
, i.e.

(2.1)

\textrm{in}_{<}I=\langle\textrm{in}_{<}f:f\in I\rangle

A monomial in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]
which is not an element of
\textrm{in}_{<}I
is called a
standard monomial
. It is known that

(2.2)

\{m+I:m\text{ is a standard monomial}\}

forms a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/I
, and this is called the
standard monomial basis
.
More detailed descriptions of Gröbner theory can be found in
[
3
]
.

2.2.
Orbit Harmonics

Given
Z
a finite locus of points in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, the
vanishing ideal

\mathbf{I}(Z)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
is the collection of all polynomials
f
satisfying
f(x)=0
for all
x
in
Z
. It is known that we have the isomorphism of vector spaces

(2.3)

\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathbf{I}(Z)\cong\mathbb{C}[Z],

where we see
\mathbb{C}[Z]
as the vector space of functions from
Z
to
\mathbb{C}
. Note that the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathbf{I}(Z)
is usually ungraded.

Given a polynomial
f
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]
, we can write
f=f_{0}+f_{1}+\dots+f_{d}
where
f_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
i
and
f_{d}
is nonzero. Denote
\tau(f)
as the highest degree homogeneous part of

, i.e.
\tau(f)=f_{d}
. Given an ideal
I
, the
associated graded ideal

\mathrm{gr}I
is defined to be

(2.4)

\mathrm{gr}I=\langle\tau(f):f\in I\rangle.

The isomorphism (
2.3
) of ungraded vector spaces above can be further extended to

(2.5)

\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathbf{I}(Z)\cong\mathbb{C}[Z]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(Z),

and since
\tau(f)
is homogeneous for all
f
,
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathrm{gr}I
has an additional structure as a graded vector space.

Orbit harmonics has seen applications to presenting cohomology rings
[
4
]
, Macdonald theory
[
5
]
, cyclic sieving
[
7
]
, Donaldson-Thomas theory
[
8
]
, and Ehrhart theory
[
9
]
. One expects algebraic properties of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{N}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(Z)
to be governed by combinatorial properties of
Z
.

2.4.
Signed and Colored Permutation Groups

Recall that the group
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
consists of all
n\times n
complex matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column, and each nonzero entry is an
r
-th root of unity. We introduce a more combinatorial interpretation of the group in this section:

The
r
-colored permutation group

\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
is defined to be the wreath product

. In particular, when
r=2
, the group is called the

, and we denote the group as
B_{n}
. An element
w
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
can be viewed as a pair
(\sigma,\kappa)
, where
\sigma
is a permutation in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and
\kappa:[n]\rightarrow\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}
is a coloring of elements in
[n]
. Writing

\{i^{j}:i\in[n],j\in\{0,1,\dots,r-1\}\}
, we can see

as a function
[n]^{r}\rightarrow[n]^{r}
via the rule

(2.7)

(w)(i^{j})=\sigma(i)^{\kappa(\sigma(i))+j},

where addition is modulo
r
. An example is when
n=5,r=3
, and
w=(\sigma,\kappa)
where
\sigma=[4,2,5,3,1]=(1435)(2),\kappa=(2,1,2,2,0)
, then we have
w(1^{0})=4^{2},w(2^{0})=2^{1},w(3^{0})=5^{0},w(4^{0})=3^{2},w(5^{0})=1^{2}
. In one-line notation, we denote
w=[4^{2},2^{1},5^{0},3^{2},1^{2}]
, and we write the cycle notation of
w
as
(1^{2},4^{2},3^{2},5^{0})(2^{1})
. For a cycle in the cycle decomposition of
w
, we refer to the
color
of the cycle to be the sum of colors of elements in it, reduced modulo
r
. For example, the color of
(1^{2},4^{2},3^{2},5^{0})
is

, and the color of
(2^{1})
is

.

Definition 2.1
.

Given integers

r\geq 1
, an

-partition of
n
is a sequence of
r

-partition of

Definition 2.2

The cycle type of an element

is the
r
-partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r-1})

Two elements in

are conjugates if and only if they have the same cycle type. Thus, the conjugacy classes of

are labelled by

-partitions of

.

\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
is
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}|=\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}|\lambda^{i}|-|\mu^{i}|
.

Given an
r
-skew partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
such that
|\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}|=n
, we also have a character
\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}
of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
associated to it. It is defined to be

(2.10)

\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\nu}\vdash_{r}n}c^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}\chi^{\boldsymbol{\nu}}

where the sum is over all
r
-partitions
\boldsymbol{\nu}=(\nu^{1},\nu^{2},\dots,\nu^{r})\vdash_{r}n
, and
c^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}
is the
generalized Littlewood-Richardson coefficient
, defined as

(2.11)

c^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu},\boldsymbol{\nu}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}c^{\lambda^{i}}_{\mu^{i},\nu^{i}},

which is the product of Littlewood-Richardson coefficients.

2.6.1.
Branching Rule

The characters labelled by
r
-skew partitions play an important role in the branching rule of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
representations. Let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
be an
r
-partition of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
. For
k<n
,
\mathfrak{S}_{k,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k,r}
is a natural subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
, and we have the branching rule

(2.12)

\mathrm{Res}^{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}_{\mathfrak{S}_{k,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n-k,r}}\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\chi^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\times\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}

where the sum is over all
r
-partitions
\boldsymbol{\mu}\vdash_{r}k
such that
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
is well defined. The proof of the branching rule can be found in
[
15
]
.

2.6.2.
Generalized Murnaghan-Nakayama Rule

We then recall how to evaluate
\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}
on
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
. A
ribbon
is a skew partition with a rookwise connected diagram that does not contain a
2\times 2
square as a sub-diagram, and the
height
of a ribbon is defined as the number of rows in it minus
1
. The diagram at the beginning of the section is an example of a ribbon of height
2
. We say that an
r
-skew partition
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
is a
r
-ribbon
if only one of the skew partitions
\lambda^{i}/\mu^{i}
is non-empty and the non-empty skew partition is a ribbon. If
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
is an
r
-ribbon with
\lambda^{j}/\mu^{j}
non-empty, we say
\theta
is the
character of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
, where
\theta
is the irreducible character of
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}

(2.13)

\theta:\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{C})\quad\quad\theta(z)=e^{2j\pi iz/r}.

A decomposition of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
into
r
-ribbons is a nested sequence of
r
-partitions

(2.14)

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{0}\subseteq\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{l}=\boldsymbol{\lambda}

where
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}/\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i-1}
is an
r
-ribbon for
1\leq i\leq l
. Let
\beta_{i}=|\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i}/\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{i-1}|
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{l})
, we call the decomposition in (
2.14
) a
\beta
-decomposition
. With this definition, we introduce the generalized Murnaghan-Nakayama rule.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w
be an element of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
. Suppose the cycles of
x
have lengths
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\dots,\beta_{l}
, and the cycles have colors
c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{l}
. Then, we have the evaluation of
\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}
on
w
:

(2.15)

\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}(w)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}\prod_{i=1}^{l}(-1)^{h_{i}}\theta_{i}(c_{i})

where the sum is over
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}
of
\beta
-decompositions of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}
,
h_{i}
is the height of the
r
-ribbon in the
i
-th step of the
\beta
-decomposition, and
\theta_{i}
is the character of the
r
-ribbon in the
i
-th step as defined above.

The proof of Theorem
2.3
can also be found in
[
15
]
.

3.
Standard Monomial Basis

3.1.
Viennot Shadow

Given a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, we associate to it a diagram on a
n\times n
grid, labelling all points
(i,w(i))
on the grid. Below is the diagram of
w=[5,1,3,6,7,2,4]\in\mathfrak{S}_{7}

Viennot proved

that the diagram can be used to construct the pair of standard Young tableau in the Schensted correspondence. Imagine a light source at the bottom left corner which shines northeast. Each point
(i,w(i))
in the diagram blocks light to its northeast. Consider the boundary of the shadow region, and call it the

above is depicted in the leftmost diagram below. Then, remove all points on the first shadow line and iterate the process, we get the
second shadow line

the third shadow line
, and so on. The diagram below in the middle depicts all shadow lines of permutation
w=[5,1,3,6,7,2,4]
.

Suppose the shadow lines of
w
are
L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{k}
, and we have
w\rightarrow(P(w),Q(w))
under the Schensted correspondence, Viennot proved
[
16
]
the
y
coordinates of the infinite horizontal rays of
L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{k}
form the first row of
P(w)
, and the
x
coordinates of the infinite vertical rays of
L_{1},L_{2},\dots,L_{k}
for the first row of
Q(w)
. In the example above, we have the first row of
P(w)
is
1,2,4,7
; and the first row of
Q(w)
is
1,3,4,5
.

Definition 3.1
.

Given the permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the shadow set
\mathcal{S}(w)
of
w
is the collection of points that lie on the northeast corners of the shadow lines.

In the example above, we have that for
w=[5,1,3,6,7,2,4]\in\mathfrak{S}_{7}
,
\mathcal{S}(w)=\{(2,5),(6,3),(7,6)\}
.

Remark 3.2
.

From the construction of shadow lines, we can see that the shadow set of
w
will contain no two points in the same row or column, which will induce the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

A subset
\mathcal{R}
of points in the
n\times n
diagram is a (non-attacking) rook placement if it contains at most one point in each row or column.

As mentioned in the remark, the shadow set of any permutation is a rook placement. However, not every rook placement is the shadow set of a permutation. An algorithm for identifying whether a rook placement is the shadow set of a permutation is specified in
[
10
]
.

After obtaining the shadow set of a permutation, we can iterate the shadow line construction on
\mathcal{S}(w)
, as depicted in the diagram below on the left. Viennot proved
[
16
]
that the
y
coordinates of the infinite horizontal rays of the new shadow lines correspond to the second row of
P(w)
, and the
x
coordinates of the infinite vertical rays correspond to the second row of
Q(w)
. Keep iterating this process on the new shadow set, and we will get the third row of the pair of tableau. The process ends when the new shadow set is empty, and we would have the complete pair of tableaux
(P(w),Q(w))
.

Given the connections between the shadow sets and the Schensted correspondence, the following lemma is immediate:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. The size of the shadow set is
|\mathcal{S}(w)|=n-\mathrm{lis}(w)
, where
\mathrm{lis}(w)
is the length of the longest increasing subsequence of
w
, as defined in the introduction.

3.2.
Shadow monomials

Given a collection of points
S\in[n]\times[n]
, we denote
m(S)
to be the square-free monomial
\prod_{(i,j)\in S}x_{i,j}
. Let
I_{n}
be defined as in the introduction. It is straightforward that

\{m(\mathcal{R}):\mathcal{R}\text{ is a rook placement}\}

is a spanning set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
, since the product of any two variables in the same row or column is in
I_{n}
.

Recall that given a set of variables
y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{N}
, the
lexicographical order
is defined such that
y_{1}>y_{2}>\dots>y_{N}
and
y_{1}^{a_{1}}y_{2}^{a_{2}}\dots y_{N}^{a_{n}}<y_{1}^{b_{1}}y_{2}^{b_{2}}\dots y_{N}^{b_{N}}
if and only if there exists
j\leq n
such that
a_{i}=b_{i}
for all
i<j
, and
a_{j}<b_{j}
. The standard monomial basis of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
was computed in
[
10
]
with respect to the monomial order defined as follows.

Definition 3.5
.

The Toeplitz order, denoted by
<_{\mathrm{Top}}
, is the lexicographical order with respect to the following ordering on the variables:

(3.1)

x_{1,1}>x_{2,1}>x_{1,2}>x_{3,1}>x_{2,2}>x_{1,3}>\dots>x_{n,n-1}>x_{n-1,n}>x_{n,n}.

Rhoades defined the shadow monomials as follows:

Definition 3.6
.

Given a permutation
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the shadow monomial of
w
, is the square-free monomial

(3.2)

\mathfrak{s}(w)=m(\mathcal{S}(w)).

Rhoades then proved
[
10
, Lemma 3.11]

Theorem 3.7
.

The monomials
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
span the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
.

Then, identifying
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
as the group of
n\times n
permutation matrices (i.e. matrices such that all entries are
0
and
1
, with exactly one
1
in each row and column), we have that the vanishing ideal of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is generated by

•

x_{i,j}^{2}-x_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i,j^{\prime}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
,
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,1}+x_{i,2}+\dots+x_{i,n}-1
for
1\leq i\leq n
;

•

x_{1,j}+x_{2,j}+\dots+x_{n,j}-1
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

A quick observation is that
I_{n}\subseteq\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
, since
I_{n}
is generated by the highest degree homogeneous parts of the generators of
\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
. The orbit harmonics (
2.5
) gives

(3.3)

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n}).

Using an argument on the dimensions of the vector spaces, Rhoades showed
[
10
]
that the ideals
I_{n}
and
\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n})
are actually the same, and showed that

\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}

is the standard monomial basis of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
with respect to the Toeplitz order. Thus, let
a_{n,k}
be the number of permutations
w
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\mathrm{lis}(w)=k
, the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
is

(3.4)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n};q)=a_{n,n}+a_{n,n-1}\cdot q+a_{n,n-2}\cdot q^{2}+\dots+a_{n,1}\cdot q^{n-1}.

3.3.
Generalization to Signed Permutations

3.3.1.
Spanning Set

Given a signed permutation
w=(\sigma,\kappa)
, we define its diagram on the
n\times n
grid. A point
(i,j)
on the grid is colored green if
\sigma(i)=j
and
\kappa(j)=1
. It is colored blue if
\sigma(i)=j
and
\kappa(j)=0
. For example, when
n=6
and the one-line notation of
w
is
[2^{1},5^{0},3^{0},1^{0},6^{0},4^{1}]
, the diagram is below on the left:

We define the
negative set
of
w
as

(3.5)

\mathrm{neg}(w)=\{(i,j):\sigma(i)=j\text{ and }\kappa(j)=1\ \}.

or equivalently, it’s the collection of points colored red in the diagram. Similarly, we define the
positive set
of
w
to be

(3.6)

\mathrm{pos}(w)=\{(i,j):\sigma(i)=j\text{ and }\kappa(j)=0\}.

Remark 3.8
.

It is an immediate result that
\mathrm{neg}(w)
is a rook placement, and for any rook placement
\mathcal{R}
, there is
w\in B_{n}
such that
\mathrm{neg}(w)=\mathcal{R}
.

Then, we do the shadow line construction on the positive set of
w
, and obtain the shadow set
\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{pos}(w))
, as depicted above on the right. The shadow lines are drawn in brown, and the points in the shadow set are labelled green. With this construction, we associate to the signed permutation
w
a monomial

(3.7)

\mathfrak{s}(w)=m(\mathrm{neg}(w))\cdot m(\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{pos}(w)))^{2}.

In the example above, the monomial is
x_{1,2}x_{6,4}x_{3,5}^{2}x_{4,3}^{2}
, and we can introduce one of the main results:

Theorem 3.9
.

The monomials
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in B_{n}\}
form a spanning set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
.

Before presenting the proof of Theorem
3.9
, we first introduce the following lemma:

Lemma 3.10
.

The following set descends to a spanning set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
:

(3.8)

\{m(\mathcal{R}_{1})m(\mathcal{R}_{2})^{2}:\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}\in[n]\times[n],\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{1}\cup\mathcal{R}_{2}\text{ is a rook placement}\}.

Proof.

Since the ideal
I_{B_{n}}
contains the cubes of all variables, as well as the products of any two variables in the same row or column, the result is immediate.
∎

With the lemma, we present the proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.9
) According to Lemma
3.10
, we can fix a rook-placement
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. By Remark
3.8
, the set of
w\in B_{n}
with
\mathrm{neg}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{1}
is nonempty, so it suffices to show

(3.9)

\{m(\mathcal{R}_{1})m(\mathcal{R}_{2})^{2}:\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}_{1}\cup\mathcal{R}_{2}\text{ is a rook placement}\}

lies, modulo the ideal
I_{B_{n}}
, in the linear span of

(3.10)

\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in B_{n},\mathrm{neg}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{1}\}.

Let

(3.11)

I=\{i:\text{there exists }j\text{ such that }(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}\},

and

(3.12)

J=\{j:\text{there exists }i\text{ such that }(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}\}.

Fixing
i\in[n]\setminus I
, since the sum of squares of all variables in the same row is in
I_{B_{n}}
, we have

(3.13)

\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus J}x_{i,j}^{2}\equiv-\sum_{j\in J}x_{i,j}^{2}\mod I_{B_{n}}.

Similarly, fixing
j\in[n]\setminus J
, we also have the equation

(3.14)

\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus I}x_{i,j}^{2}\equiv-\sum_{i\in I}x_{i,j}^{2}\mod I_{B_{n}}.

Thus, since
I_{B_{n}}
contains the products of any two variables in the same row or column, we obtain the following relations:

•

m(\mathcal{R}_{1})\cdot\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus J}x_{i,j}^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{B_{n}}

•

m(\mathcal{R}_{1})\cdot\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus I}x_{i,j}^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{B_{n}}

We also have the following relations directly from the definition of
I_{B_{n}}
:

•

(x_{i,j}^{2})^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{B_{n}}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I
,
j\in[n]\setminus J
;

•

x_{i,j}^{2}\cdot x_{i,j^{\prime}}^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{B_{n}}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I
,
j,j^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus J
;

•

x_{i,j}^{2}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j}^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{B_{n}}
for
i,i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus I
,
j\in[n]\setminus J
.

Then, if we look at the
(n-|\mathcal{R}_{1}|)\times(n-|\mathcal{R}_{1}|)
matrix of variables, where each variable is
x_{i,j}^{2}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I,j\in[n]\setminus J
, the collection of relations are exactly the ones we used to define
I_{n}
in Definition
1.1
. For a signed permutation
w
with
\mathrm{neg}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{1}
, if we delete the rows and columns that
\mathcal{R}_{1}
appears in,
\mathrm{pos}(w)
will form a permutation in the remaining
(n-|I|)\times(n-|J|)
grid. And since

(3.15)

\{\mathrm{pos}(w):w\in B_{n},\mathrm{neg}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{1}\}

ranges over all possible permutations in the grid labelled by
([n]\setminus I)\times([n]\setminus J)
, according to Theorem
3.7
, the proof is complete.
∎

3.3.2.
Standard Monomial Basis

Viewing
B_{n}
as
n\times n
matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row or column, where the nonzero entries can be
\pm 1
, we have that the following polynomials all vanish on
B_{n}
:

•

x_{i,j}^{3}-x_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i,j^{\prime}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
,
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,1}^{2}+x_{i,2}^{2}+\dots+x_{i,n}^{2}-1
for
1\leq i\leq n
;

•

x_{1,j}^{2}+x_{2,j}^{2}+\dots+x_{n,j}^{2}-1
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

Let
J
be the ideal generated by these polynomials, it follows immediately that
J\subseteq\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
. Comparing these polynomials with the generators of
I_{B_{n}}
, it follows that
I_{B_{n}}\subseteq\mathrm{gr}J\subseteq\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
. We further improve this containment to an equality.

Theorem 3.11
.

We have the equalities of ideals
I_{B_{n}}=\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
and
J=\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
. Moreover, the collection of monomials
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in B_{n}\}
descends to a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
. This is the standard monomial basis with respect to the Toeplitz order.

Proof.

Applying orbit harmonics, we have the following sequence of isomorphisms of (ungraded) vectors spaces

(3.16)

\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(B_{n})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n}).

With
I_{B_{n}}\subseteq\mathrm{gr}J\subseteq\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
, we have

(3.17)

2^{n}n!=\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n}))\leq\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}J)\leq\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}).

Since there is a unique pair
(\mathrm{neg}(w),\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{pos}(w))
for each signed permutation
w
, the spanning set
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in B_{n}\}
in Theorem
3.9
has size
2^{n}n!
. This forces all inequalities in (
3.17
) to be equalities. This proves the equalities of ideals, and hence proves that
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in B_{n}\}
descends to a basis of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
.

To prove that this is actually the standard monomial basis with respect to the Toeplitz order, let
\mathcal{R}_{1}
be any rook placement, and let
f
be a monomial with square-free part
m(\mathcal{R}_{1})
. We claim that if
f
is in the standard monomial basis then
f
is of the form
\mathfrak{s}(w)
for some
w\in B_{n}
. To see this, recall that
f
being in the standard monomial basis means that
f
is not the initial monomial of any polynomial in
I_{B_{n}}
. Then, using the notation in the end of the proof of Theorem
3.9
, the perfect square part
f/m(\mathcal{R}_{1})
of
f
must not be the initial monomial of any polynomial in
I_{n-|\mathcal{R}_{1}|}
, where the variables are
x_{i,j}^{2}
with
i\in[n]\setminus I
and
j\in[n]\setminus J
. Then, according to the result on the standard monomial basis of
I_{n}
by Rhoades
[
10
, Theorem 3.12]
, the claim is proved; and since the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
has dimension
2^{n}n!
, all monomials of the form
\mathfrak{s}(w)
for
w\in B_{n}
must be in the standard monomial basis.
∎

With the following two definitions, we will be able to find the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
.

Definition 3.12
.

Given
w\in B_{n}
, we define
\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w))
to be the size of the largest subset
L
of
\mathrm{pos}(w)
such that given
(i,j)
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
in
L
,
i<i^{\prime}
implies
j<j^{\prime}
.

Example 3.13
.

Let
w\in B_{7}
with one-line notation
w=[3^{0},1^{1},6^{0},4^{0},7^{0},2^{1},5^{1}]
, we have
\mathrm{pos}(w)=\{(1,3),(3,6),(4,4),(5,7)\}
, and
\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w))=3
.

Definition 3.14
.

For any pair of integers
n,k
, we define

(3.18)

b_{n,k}=|\{w\in B_{n}:2\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w))+|\mathrm{neg}(w)|=k\}|

From the definition, it is immediate that
b_{n,1}=0
for
n\geq 2
, as
|\mathrm{neg}(w)|\geq 2
or
\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w))\geq 1
. Then, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 3.15
.

We have the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}

(3.19)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}};q)=b_{n,2n}+b_{n,2n-1}\cdot q+\cdots+b_{n,1}\cdot q^{2n-1}.

Proof.

For
w\in B_{n}
,

\displaystyle\deg\mathfrak{s}(w)

\displaystyle=2|\mathcal{S}(\mathrm{pos}(w))|+|\mathrm{neg}(w)|

\displaystyle=2(|\mathrm{pos}(w)|-\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w)))+|\mathrm{neg}(w)|

\displaystyle=2n-|\mathrm{neg}(w)|-2\mathrm{lis}(\mathrm{pos}(w)).

Thus,
|\{w\in B_{n}:\deg\mathfrak{s}(w)=k\}|=b_{n,2n-k}
and Theorem
3.11
completes the proof.
∎

Example 3.16
.

When
n=3
, the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{3}}
is

(3.20)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{3}};q)=1+9q+22q^{2}+9q^{3}+q^{4}.

3.4.
Generalization to
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}

Given an
r
-colored permutation
w=(\sigma,\kappa)
, we also draw it on the
n\times n
grid. A point
(i,j)
is labelled in color
c_{l}
if
\sigma(i)=j
and
\kappa(j)=l
, where
l
ranges over
0,1,\dots,r-1
. We define the sets
C_{l}(w)
to be the collection of points colored in
c_{l}
. Then, we do the shadow set construction on the set
C_{0}(w)
and obtain the shadow set
\mathcal{S}(C_{0}(w))
, and we associate a monomial
\mathfrak{s}(w)
to
w
, defined as

(3.21)

\mathfrak{s}(w)=m(C_{1}(w))\cdot m(C_{2}(w))^{2}\cdot\dots\cdot m(C_{r-1}(w))^{r-1}\cdot m(\mathcal{S}(C_{0}(w)))^{r}.

With this construction, we give the following theorem:

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
be defined as in Definition
1.2
. The monomials
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\}
descends to a spanning set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
.

The proof of Theorem
3.17
is similar to Theorem
3.9
. We need the following lemma, which is similar to Lemma
3.10
.

Lemma 3.18
.

The following set of monomials descends to a spanning set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
:

(3.22)

~\left\{\prod_{l=1}^{r}m(\mathcal{R}_{l})^{l}:\mathcal{R}_{l}\subset[n]\times[n],\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{l=1}^{r}\mathcal{R}_{l}\text{ is a rook placement}\right\}

Proof.

Since the ideal
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
contains the
r+1
-th power of all variables, as well as the products of any two variables in the same row or column, the result is immediate.
∎

With Lemma
3.18
, we give the proof of Theorem
3.17
.

Proof.

(of Theorem
3.17
) According to Lemma
3.18
, we can fix a collection of rook placements
\mathcal{R}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{R}_{r-1}
such that

(3.23)

\bigcup_{l=1}^{r-1}\mathcal{R}_{l}

is also a rook placement. Let
f
be the monomial

(3.24)

f=\prod_{l=1}^{r-1}m(\mathcal{R}_{l})^{l},

it suffices to show

(3.25)

\{f\cdot m(\mathcal{R}_{r})^{r}:\bigcup_{l=1}^{r}\mathcal{R}_{l}\text{ is a rook placement}\}

lies, modulo
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
, in the linear span of

(3.26)

\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r},C_{l}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{l}\text{ for }1\leq l\leq r-1\}.

Let

(3.27)

I=\{i:\text{there exists }j\in[n],1\leq l\leq r-1\text{ such that }(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_{l}\},

and

(3.28)

J=\{j:\text{there exists }i\in[n],1\leq l\leq r-1\text{ such that }(i,j)\in\mathcal{R}_{l}\}.

Note that we have

(3.29)

|I|=|J|=|\bigcup_{l=1}^{r-1}\mathcal{R}_{l}|.

Fixing
i\in[n]\setminus I
, since the sum of
r
-th powers of all variables in the same row is in
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
, we have

(3.30)

\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus J}x_{i,j}^{r}\equiv-\sum_{j\in J}x_{i,j}^{r}\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}.

Similarly, fixing
j\in[n]\setminus J
, we also have the equation

(3.31)

\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus I}x_{i,j}^{r}\equiv-\sum_{i\in I}x_{i,j}^{r}\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}.

Thus, since
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
contains the products of any two variables in the same row or column, we obtain the following relations:

•

f\cdot\sum_{j\in[n]\setminus J}x_{i,j}^{r}\equiv 0\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
;

•

f\cdot\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus I}x_{i,j}^{r}\equiv 0\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
.

We also have the following relations directly from the definition of
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
:

•

(x_{i,j}^{r})^{2}\equiv 0\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I
,
j\in[n]\setminus J
;

•

x_{i,j}^{r}\cdot x_{i,j^{\prime}}^{r}\equiv 0\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I
,
j,j^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus J
;

•

x_{i,j}^{r}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j}^{r}\equiv 0\mod I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
for
i,i^{\prime}\in[n]\setminus I
,
j\in[n]\setminus J
.

If we look at the
(n-|I|)\times(n-|J|)
matrix of variables, where each variable is
(x_{i,j})^{r}
for
i\in[n]\setminus I,j\in[n]\setminus J
, the collection of relations are exactly the ones we used to define
I_{n}
in Definition
1.1
. For a colored permutation
w
with
C_{l}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{l}
for
1\leq l\leq r-1
, if we delete the rows and columns with labels
I
and
J
respectively,
C_{0}(w)
will form a permutation in the remaining
(n-|I|)\times(n-|J|)
grid. Since

(3.32)

\{C_{0}(w):w\in B_{n};C_{l}(w)=\mathcal{R}_{l}\}

ranges over all possible permutations in the grid labelled by
([n]\setminus I)\times([n]\setminus J)
, the proof is complete by Theorem
3.7
.
∎

Viewing
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
as the collection of
n\times n
matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column, and every nonzero entry being an
r
-th root of unity, we have that the following polynomials all vanish on
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
:

•

x_{i,j}^{r+1}-x_{i,j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i,j^{\prime}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
,
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,j}\cdot x_{i^{\prime},j}
for
1\leq j\leq n
,
1\leq i<i^{\prime}\leq n
;

•

x_{i,1}^{r}+x_{i,2}^{r}+\dots+x_{i,n}^{r}-1
for
1\leq i\leq n
;

•

x_{1,j}^{r}+x_{2,j}^{r}+\dots+x_{n,j}^{r}-1
for
1\leq j\leq n
.

Let
J
be the ideal generated by these polynomials, we have
J\subseteq\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
and
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}\subseteq\mathrm{gr}J\subseteq\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
. Thus, we have a theorem that is an analogy to Theorem
3.11
.

Theorem 3.19
.

We have the equalities of ideals
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}=\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
and
J=\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
. Moreover, the collection of monomials
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\}
descends to a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
. This is the standard monomial basis with respect to the Toeplitz order.

Proof.

Applying orbit harmonics, we have the following sequence of isomorphisms of (ungraded) vector spaces

(3.33)

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}).

With
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}\subseteq\mathrm{gr}J\subseteq\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
, we have

(3.34)

r^{n}n!=\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}))\leq\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/grJ)\leq\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}).

Since there is a unique tuple
(C_{1}(w),\dots C_{r-1}(w),\mathcal{S}(C_{r}(w))
for each colored permutation
w
, the spanning set
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\}
in Theorem
3.17
has size
r^{n}n!
. This forces all inequalities in (
3.34
) to be equalities. This proves the equalities of ideals, and hence proves that
\{\mathfrak{s}(w):w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\}
descends to a basis of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
.
The proof that this is actually the standard monomial basis with respect to the Toeplitz order is very similar to the proof of Theorem
3.11
.
∎

We also generalize the notion of longest increasing subsequence to the colored permutation group. We first give the following two definitions:

Definition 3.20
.

Given
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
, we define
\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w))
to be the size of the largest subset
L
of
C_{0}(w)
such that given
(i,j)
and
(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
in
L
,
i<i^{\prime}
implies
j<j^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.21
.

For integers
n,r,k
, we define

(3.35)

c_{n,r,k}=\left|\left\{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}:r\cdot\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w))+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(r-i)\cdot|C_{i}(w)|=k\right\}\right|

Example 3.22
.

Let
n=6
,
r=4
, and
w=[4^{2},1^{0},5^{2},6^{3},2^{1},3^{0}]
. We have
C_{0}(w)=\{(2,1),(6,3)\}
, so
\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w))=2
, and we have

(3.36)

4\cdot\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w))+3|C_{1}(w)|+2|C_{2}(w)|+|C_{3}(w)|=4\times 2+3\times 1+2\times 2+1\times 1=16,

so
w
would contribute 16 to
c_{6,4,16}
.

With these two definitions, we give the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
.

Theorem 3.23
.

The Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
is

(3.37)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}};q)=\sum_{k=1}^{rn}c_{n,r,k}q^{rn-k}

Proof.

For
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
,

\displaystyle\deg\mathfrak{s}(w)

\displaystyle=r\cdot|\mathcal{S}(C_{0}(w))|+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i\cdot|C_{i}(w)|

\displaystyle=r\cdot(C_{0}(w)-\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w)))+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i\cdot|C_{i}(w)|

\displaystyle=rn-r\cdot\mathrm{lis}(C_{0}(w))-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}(r-i)\cdot|C_{i}(w)|

Thus,
|\{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}:\deg\mathfrak{s}(w)=k\}|=c_{n,r,rn-k}
and Theorem
3.19
completes the proof.
∎

4.
Module Structure

As mentioned in the Introduction, treating
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
as the group of
n\times n
matrices with exactly one nonzero entry in each row and column, and every nonzero entry being an
r
-th root of unity, we can let
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
act on
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]
, induced by its action on the matrix of variables

(4.1)

(A,A^{\prime})\cdot\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}=A\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}A^{\prime-1}.

Since the collection of generators of
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
is stable under this action, we have that
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
module. By Theorem
3.19
, we have an isomorphism and an equality of ungraded vector spaces

(4.2)

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}.

Since
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]
is semi-simple, (
4.2
) upgrades to an isomorphism and an equality of ungraded
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
modules.

To investigate the graded structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
, we first introduce a lemma, which is a crucial part in orbit harmonics. Let
\mathbf{x}
be a collection of variables and
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
be the polynomial ring. Given a graded
\mathbb{C}
-algebra
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
, let
A_{\leq d}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}
be the direct sum of its homogeneous components with degree less than or equal to
d
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I
be an ideal in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]
and
\mathrm{gr}I
be the associated graded ideal of it. Let
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]_{\leq d}
be a collection of homogeneous polynomials with degree at most
d
. If
\mathcal{B}
descends to a basis of the vector space
(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]/grI)_{\leq d}
, then
\mathcal{B}
descends to a basis of the vector space
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]_{\leq d}/(I\cap\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}]_{\leq d})
.

Proof.

The proof can be found in the proof of a known result
[
10
, Lemma 3.15]
.
∎

4.1.
Graded Module Structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}

We first work on the case when
r=2
. In this case, the irreducible
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules are of form
V^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes V^{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}
, where
(\lambda,\mu)
and
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})
are bipartitions of
n
.
Equation (
4.2
) now reads:

(4.3)

\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n})=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}.

We give the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any
k\geq 0
, the degree
k
piece
(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}})_{k}
has graded
B_{n}\times B_{n}
module structure

(4.4)

(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}})_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n\\
2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-k\end{subarray}}V^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes V^{(\lambda,\mu)}.

Proof.

For
W
any
B_{n}\times B_{n}
module,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W)
is also a
B_{n}\times B_{n}
module with the action

(4.5)

((u,v)\cdot\varphi)(w)=u\cdot\varphi(v^{-1}\cdot w)\text{ for all }u,v\in B_{n}\text{, }\varphi\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W),w\in W.

We also have that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W)
is isomorphic to
W\otimes W^{*}
as
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules, where
W^{*}
is the dual module of
W
, via the isomorphism

(4.6)

W\otimes W^{*}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W),\quad a\otimes x\mapsto\varphi,\text{ with }\varphi(w)=x(w)\cdot a,

where
a,w\in W
and
x\in W^{*}
. Then, since all matrix representations of
B_{n}
can be realized over the real numbers, we have that
W
is isomorphic to
W^{*}
. So we have an isomorphism of
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules

(4.7)

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W)\cong W\otimes W.

Since the algebra
\mathbb{C}[B_{N}]
is semi-simple, the
Artin-Wedderburn Theorem
gives the isomorphism of
\mathbb{C}
-algebras

(4.8)

\Psi:\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]\xrightarrow{\,\,\sim\,\,}\bigoplus_{(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(\lambda,\mu)}),

where the
(\lambda,\mu)
-piece of
\Psi(a)
maps
v
to
a\cdot v
for
a\in\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]
,
v\in V^{(\lambda,\mu)}
. Since
\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]
is a
B_{n}\times B_{n}
module via left and right action, (
4.7
) and (
4.8
) imply that we have the isomorphism of ungraded
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules

(4.9)

\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]\cong\bigoplus_{(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(\lambda,\mu)})\cong\bigoplus_{(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n}V^{(\lambda,\mu)}\otimes V^{(\lambda,\mu)}.

Returning to the statement of the theorem, by an inductive argument, it suffices to prove that for any
k
, we have the isomorphism of ungraded
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules:

(4.10)

(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}})_{\leq k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n\\
2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(\lambda,\mu)}).

With Theorem
3.11
, we have a chain of isomorphisms and an equality of ungraded
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules:

(4.11)

\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(B_{n})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(B_{n})=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}.

Then, consider the image of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]_{\leq k}
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
, denoted by
L_{k}
, i.e.

(4.12)

L_{k}=\mathrm{Image}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]_{\leq k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(B_{n})).

By Lemma
3.10
and Lemma
4.1
, we have that

(4.13)

L_{k}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{m(\mathcal{R}_{1})m(\mathcal{R}_{2})^{2}+\mathbf{I}(B_{n}):\mathcal{R}_{1}\cup\mathcal{R}_{2}\text{ is a rook placement and }|\mathcal{R}_{1}|+2|\mathcal{R}_{2}|\leq k\}.

Then, Lemma
4.1
implies that we have the isomorphism of
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules

(4.14)

L_{k}\cong(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}})_{\leq k}.

So we are reduced to showing that

(4.15)

L_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n\\
2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(\lambda,\mu)}).

For any
j\leq k
, consider the collection of monomials

(4.16)

M^{j}=\left\{m_{i}^{j}=x_{1,1}^{2}x_{2,2}^{2}\cdots x_{i,i}^{2}x_{i+1,i+1}x_{i+2,i+2}\cdots x_{j-i,j-i}:0\leq i\leq\lfloor j/2\rfloor\right\}.

For example, when
j=4
, the collection of monomials is

(4.17)

M^{j}=\left\{x_{1,1}^{2}x_{2,2}^{2},x_{1,1}^{2}x_{2,2}x_{3,3},x_{1,1}x_{2,2}x_{3,3}x_{4,4}\right\}.

Since nonzero monomials in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
are cube-free, the degree
j
monomials in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
are generated by the action of
B_{n}\times B_{n}
on
M^{j}
. Thus,
L_{k}
is generated by the action of
B_{n}\times B_{n}
on
\bigcup_{j\leq k}M^{j}
.

Fix
0\leq j\leq k
. For all
i
satisfying
0\leq i\leq\lfloor j/2\rfloor
, embed
B_{n-j+i}\subseteq B_{n}
by letting it act on the last
n-j+i
letters. Let
\epsilon_{i}^{j}
be the group algebra element

(4.18)

\epsilon_{i}^{j}=(e+t_{1,-1})(e+t_{2,-2})\cdots(e+t_{i,-i})(e-t_{i+1,-i-1})\cdots(e-t_{j-i,-j+i})\left(\sum_{w\in B_{n-j+i}}w\right),

where
e
denotes the identity element and
t_{l,-l}
denotes the transposition in
B_{n}
that interchanges
l
and
-l
, and fixing all other elements. Identify
\mathbb{C}[B_{n}]
with the collection of functions from
B_{n}
to
\mathbb{C}
, via the rule

(4.19)

\sum_{w\in B_{n}}a_{w}w\mapsto f:f(w)=a_{w}.

Since
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(B_{n})
is naturally identified with functions from
B_{n}
to
\mathbb{C}
, we can identify
\epsilon_{i}^{j}
with
m_{i}^{j}
. Let
\Sigma^{j}=\{\epsilon_{i}^{j}:0\leq i\leq\lfloor j/2\rfloor\}
, and let
J_{k}
be the two-sided ideal generated by
\bigcup_{0\leq j\leq k}\Sigma^{j}
. We have the identification

(4.20)

J_{k}=L_{k}.

So we are now reduced to find the image of
J_{k}
under the Artin-Wedderburn map in (
4.8
). Note that the image of
J_{k}
would be
direct sum of endomorphisms of irreducible modules such that not all elements of
J_{k}
act as the
0
operator. Fixing
j\leq k
, we need to find the irreducible modules such that not all elements of
\Sigma_{j}
act as
0
.

Note that
\epsilon_{i}^{j}
is naturally an element of the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[B_{j-i}\times B_{n-j+i}]
. Given
V^{(\lambda,\mu)}
an irreducible module of
B_{n}
labelled by bipartition
(\lambda,\mu)
, it suffices to analyze the character of

(4.21)

\mathrm{Res}^{B_{n}}_{B_{j-i}\times B_{n-j+i}}V^{(\lambda,\mu)},

evaluated on
\epsilon_{i}^{j}
. By the Branching Rule (
2.12
),

(4.22)

\mathrm{Res}^{B_{n}}_{B_{j-i}\times B_{n-j+i}}\chi^{(\lambda,\mu)}=\sum_{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}\chi^{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}\times\chi^{(\lambda/\tilde{\lambda},\mu/\tilde{\mu})},

where the sum is over all
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})
that are bipartitions of
j-i
and that
(\lambda/\tilde{\lambda},\mu/\tilde{\lambda})
is well-defined. Denote

(4.23)

\eta_{i}^{j}=(e+t_{1,-1})(e+t_{2,-2})\dots(e+t_{i,-i})(e-t_{i+1,-i-1})\dots(e-t_{j-i,-j+i}),

that is,
\epsilon_{i}^{j}=\eta_{i}^{j}\sum_{w\in B_{n-k+i}}w
, we have

(4.24)

\chi^{(\lambda,\mu)}(\epsilon^{j}_{i})=\sum_{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}\chi^{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}(\eta_{i}^{j})\chi^{(\lambda/\tilde{\lambda},\mu/\tilde{\mu})}\left(\sum_{w\in B_{n-j+i}}w\right).

Let
\theta
be an irreducible character of
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\cong B_{1}
, we have that
\theta(e+t_{l,-l})\neq 0
if and only if
\theta
is the trivial character, and
\theta(e-t_{l,-l})\neq 0
if and only if
\theta
is the signed character, i.e.
\theta(t_{l,-l})=-1
. By the Generalized Murnaghan-Nakayama Rule (
2.15
), we have that
\chi^{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})}(\eta_{i}^{j})
is nonzero if and only if
|\tilde{\lambda}|=i
and
|\tilde{\mu}|=j-2i
. On the other hand,

(4.25)

\chi^{(\lambda/\tilde{\lambda},\mu/\tilde{\mu})}\left(\sum_{w\in B_{n-j+i}}w\right)

is nonzero if and only if the trivial representation
\chi^{((n-j+i),\emptyset)}
has non-zero multiplicity in
\chi^{(\lambda/\tilde{\lambda},\mu/\tilde{\mu})}
. This forces
\mu/\tilde{\mu}=\emptyset
, and the diagram of
\lambda/\tilde{\lambda}
has no two boxes in the same row. Combining these conditions, we have that
\epsilon_{i}^{j}
acts as a nonzero operator on
V^{(\lambda,\mu)}
if and only if the following holds:

•

|\mu|=j-2i
, thus
|\lambda|=n-j+2i
;

•

there exists
\tilde{\lambda}\vdash i
such that the diagram of
\lambda/\tilde{\lambda}
has no two boxes in the same column.

Note that the second condition is equivalent to

•

the diagram of
\lambda
has less than
i
boxes beyond the first row, i.e.
\lambda_{1}\geq|\lambda|-i=n-j+i
.

Since the first condition is an equality, the two conditions can be combined to

(4.26)

2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-j.

Since
j\leq k
is arbitrary, we can conclude that elements of
J_{k}
act as nonzero operators on all
V^{(\lambda,\mu)}
satisfying

(4.27)

2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-k.

This gives the isomorphism of
B_{n}\times B_{n}
modules

(4.28)

J_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\lambda,\mu)\vdash_{2}n\\
2\lambda_{1}+|\mu|\geq 2n-k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{(\lambda,\mu)}),

which completes the proof.
∎

4.2.
Graded Module Structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}

We now investigate the graded module structure of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
for general
r
. Given
\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\dots,\lambda^{r-1})
an
r
-partition of
n
, let
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{r-1},\lambda^{r-2},\dots,\lambda^{1})
be the
dual
r
-partition of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
, obtained by fixing
\lambda^{0}
and interchanging
\lambda^{i}
with
\lambda^{r-i}
. As the dual module of a unitary matrix representation is its conjugate, by the generalized Murnaghan-Nakayama rule (
2.15
), the dual module of
V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
is
V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}
, and we have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

For any
k\geq 0
, the degree
k
piece
(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{k}
has graded
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
structure

(4.29)

(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n\\
r\lambda^{0}_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i|\lambda^{i}|=rn-k\end{subarray}}V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\otimes V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we have that for any
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
module
W
,

(4.30)

\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(W)\cong W\otimes W^{*}.

Then, similar to (
4.9
), we have the isomorphisms of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
modules

(4.31)

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]\cong\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})\cong\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n}V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\otimes(V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}})^{*}\cong\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n}V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\otimes V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\prime}}.

By Theorem
3.19
, we have a chain of isomorphisms and an equality of ungraded
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
modules:

(4.32)

\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})=\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}.

Again, let
L_{k}
be the image of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]_{\leq k}
to
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
, that is,

(4.33)

L_{k}=\mathrm{Image}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]_{\leq k}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})).

By Lemma
4.1
, we have that

(4.34)

~L_{k}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\left\{\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}m(\mathcal{R}_{i})^{i}\right)+\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}):\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{R}_{i}\text{ is a rook placement and }\sum_{i=1}^{r}i\cdot|\mathcal{R}_{i}|\leq k\right\}.

Applying Lemma
4.1
again, we have the isomorphism of ungraded
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
modules

(4.35)

L_{k}\cong(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{\leq k},

qnd we are reduced to showing that

(4.36)

L_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n\\
r\lambda^{0}_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i|\lambda^{i}|\geq rn-k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}).

Let
M_{k}
be the subset of the spanning set in (
4.34
), containing all monomials

(4.37)

\prod_{i=1}^{r}m(\mathcal{R}_{i})^{i}

where the rook placements
\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
satisfy the criteria in (
4.34
), and for all
j\leq r
,

(4.38)

~\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}\mathcal{R}_{i}=\{(a,a):1\leq a\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j}|\mathcal{R}_{i}|\}.

In other words, in the
n\times n
grid, the rook placements
\{\mathcal{R}_{i}\}
are arranged on the diagonals, from bottom left to top right. For example, when
k=5
and
r=3
, we have

(4.39)

M_{k}=\{x_{1,1}x_{2,2}x_{3,3}x_{4,4}x_{5,5},x_{1,1}x_{2,2}x_{3,3}x_{4,4}^{2},x_{1,1}x_{2,2}x_{3,3}^{3},x_{1,1}x_{2,2}^{2}x_{3,3}^{2},x_{1,1}^{2}x_{2,2}^{3}\}.

Monomials in
L_{k}
are generated by monomials in
M_{k}
by two sided action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
.

Again, identifying
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}]
and
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
with functions from
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
to
\mathbb{C}
, we can identify
\mathbf{x}_{i,i}^{j}
in
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
with the group algebra element

(4.40)

(e+\omega^{j}(i^{0},i^{1})+\omega^{2j}(i^{0},i^{2})+\cdots+\omega^{(r-1)j}(i^{0},i^{r-1}))\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-1,r}}w,

where
\mathfrak{S}_{n-1,r}
is embedded in
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
by acting on the last
n-1
letters,
e
denotes the identity in
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
,
\omega
is the primitive
r
-th root of unity in
\mathbb{C}
, and
(i^{0},i^{l})
denotes the element in
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
that maps
i^{0}
to
i^{l}
while fixing all other elements. Let
\Sigma_{k}
be the collection of group algebra elements identified with monomials in
M_{k}
, and let
J_{k}
be the two-sided ideal generated by
J_{k}
. We have identification

(4.41)

L_{k}=J_{k}.

So we are reduced to finding
r
-partitions
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
such that not all elements of
J_{k}
acts as zero operator. Denote

(4.42)

\epsilon_{i}^{j}=e+\omega^{j}(i^{0},i^{1})+\omega^{2j}(i^{0},i^{2})+\cdots+\omega^{(r-1)j}(i^{0},i^{r-1}).

For rook placements
\{\mathcal{R}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
satisfying the criteria in (
4.34
) and (
4.38
), let
\mathcal{R}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\mathcal{R}_{j}
, we have that the group algebra element that is identified with
\prod_{j=1}^{r}m(\mathcal{R}_{j})^{i}
is

(4.43)

\epsilon=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{(i,i)\in\mathcal{R}_{j}}\epsilon_{i}^{j}\right)\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-|\mathcal{R}|,r}}w.

Let
\eta=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{(i,i)\in\mathcal{R}_{j}}\epsilon_{i}^{j}
, we can embed
\mathfrak{S}_{n-|\mathcal{R}|,r}
in
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
by letting it act on the last
n-|\mathcal{R}|
letters. Then, by the Branching Rule (
2.12
), we have

(4.44)

\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\epsilon)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\mu}\vdash_{r}|\mathcal{R}|}\chi^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\eta)\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-|\mathcal{R}|,r}}w\right).

Let
\theta
be any irreducible
\mathbb{C}
-character of
\mathfrak{S}_{1,r}\cong\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
. Note that we have
\theta(\epsilon^{j}_{i})\neq 0
if and only if
\theta((i^{0},i^{1}))=\omega^{-j}
. Thus, by the generalized Murnaghan-Nakayama rule (
2.15
), we have that for
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu^{0},\mu^{1},\dots\mu^{r-1})\vdash_{r}|\mathcal{R}|
,
\chi^{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\eta)
is nonzero if and only if
|\mu^{j}|=|\mathcal{R}_{r-j}|
. On the other hand,

(4.45)

\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n-|\mathcal{R}|,r}}w\right)

is nonzero if and only if the trivial character has nonzero multiplicity in
\chi^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\boldsymbol{\mu}}
, which indicates that
|\mu^{j}|=|\lambda^{j}|
for
1\leq j\leq r-1
, and there are no two boxes in the same column in the diagram of
\lambda^{0}/\mu^{0}
. That is, there are less than
|\mu^{0}|=|\mathcal{R}_{r}|
boxes beyond the first row in the diagram of
\lambda^{0}
.

Let
d=\sum_{j=1}^{r}j\cdot|\mathcal{R}_{j}|
be the degree of the monomial
\prod_{j=1}^{r}m(\mathcal{R}_{j})^{i}
. Combining these conditions, we have that
\epsilon
acts as a nonzero operator on
V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
if there exists
\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\mu^{0},\mu^{1},\dots,\mu^{r-1})
such that the following holds:

(1)

r|\mu^{0}|+(r-1)|\mu^{1}|+\dots+|\mu^{r-1}|=d
;

(2)

\lambda^{j}=\mu^{j}
for
j\neq 0
;

(3)

diagram of
\lambda^{0}/\mu^{0}
has no two boxes in the same column.

Note that the third condition is equivalent to

(4.46)

\lambda^{0}_{1}\geq|\lambda^{0}|-|\mu^{0}|=n-|\lambda^{1}|-|\lambda^{2}|-\dots-|\lambda^{r-1}|-|\mu^{0}|.

Multiplying both sides by
r
, we get

(4.47)

r\lambda^{0}_{1}\geq rn-r|\lambda^{1}|-r|\lambda^{2}|-\dots-r|\lambda^{r-1}|-r|\mu^{0}|.

Then, the first and second condition combined gives that

(4.48)

r|\mu^{0}|=d-(r-1)|\lambda^{1}|-(r-2)|\lambda^{2}|-\dots-|\lambda^{r-1}|.

Substituting this into the inequality (
4.47
), we have

(4.49)

r\lambda^{0}_{1}+|\lambda^{1}|+2|\lambda^{2}|+\dots+(r-1)|\lambda^{r-1}|\geq rn-d.

This shows the isomorphism of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
modules

(4.50)

J_{k}\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\lambda}\vdash_{r}n\\
r\lambda^{0}_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}i|\lambda^{i}|\geq rn-k\end{subarray}}\mathrm{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(V^{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}),

which completes the proof.

∎

5.
Conclusions and Conjectures

Given a (possibly infinite) sequence
{a_{i}}
of positive real numbers, we say that the sequence is
log-concave
if for all positive integer
i
, we have

(5.1)

a_{i}\cdot a_{i+2}\leq a_{i+1}^{2};

and we say the sequence is
uni-modal
if there exists a positive integer
k
such that

(5.2)

a_{i}\leq a_{i+1}\text{ for }i<k;\quad a_{i}\geq a_{i+1}\text{ for }i\geq k.

In the case of Hilbert series, we say a Hilbert series is log-concave or uni-modal if its sequence of coefficients is log-concave or uni-modal. Recall that the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n}
is

(5.3)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n};q)=a_{n,n}+a_{n,n-1}\cdot q+a_{n,n-2}\cdot q^{2}+\dots+a_{n,1}\cdot q^{n-1},

where
a_{n,k}
counts the number of permutations
w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
\mathrm{lis}(w)=k
. Chen conjectured
[
2
]
that the sequence
\{a_{n,k}\}
is log-concave, that is, fixing an integer
n
, for all
k
such that
2\leq k\leq n-1
,

(5.4)

a_{n,k}^{2}\geq a_{n,k-1}\cdot a_{n,k+1}.

We found some interesting patterns when we tried to generalize this to the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
. Recall that we showed in Corollary
3.15
that the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
is

(5.5)

{\mathrm{Hilb}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}};q)=b_{n,2n}+b_{n,2n-1}\cdot q+\cdots+b_{n,1}\cdot q^{2n-1},

where
\{b_{n,k}\}_{k=1}^{2n-2}
is defined in Definition
3.14
. By testing

n
up to
40
, we found that

•

for
n\leq 8
, the sequence
\{b_{n,k}\}
is log-concave;

•

for
9\leq n\leq 17
, we have
b_{n,k}^{2}<b_{n,k-1}\cdot b_{n+1}
when
k=3
, and log-concavity holds for the remaining of the sequence;

•

for
18\leq n\leq 27
, we have
b_{n,k}^{2}<b_{n,k-1}\cdot b_{n+1}
when
k=3\text{ and }5
, and log-concavity holds for the remaining of the sequence;

•

for
27\leq n\leq 40
, we have
b_{n,k}^{2}<b_{n,k-1}\cdot b_{n+1}
when
k=3,5,\text{ and }7
, and log-concavity holds for the remaining of the sequence.

From this observation, we conjecture that the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{B_{n}}
is “almost log-concave”: log-concavity breaks for some small
k
’s, but holds for the majority of the sequence.

In
[
1
]
, Baik, Deift, and Johansson proved that as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the distribution function for
\mathrm{lis}
on
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, with proper rescaling and recentering, converges to the Tracy-Widom distribution of the largest eigenvalue of a random GUE matrix. That is, as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the histogram of
\{a_{n,k}\}
for a fixed
n
will converge in distribution (with rescaling and recentering) to the Tracy-Widom distribution. In Figure
1
, we give the histogram of
\{a_{n,k}\}
when
n=65
. The horizontal axis corresponds to values of
k
, and the height of each bar represents the corresponding
a_{n,k}
.

Figure 1.
a_{n,k}
when
n=65

One possible direction for future studies is to study the limit of the probability distributions corresponding to
\{b_{n,k}\}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. In analogy with the case of
\{a_{n,k}\}
, one could hope that we have convergence to a natural probability distribution. In Figure
2
, we give the histogram of
\{b_{n,k}\}
when
n=40
. The horizontal axis corresponds to values of
k
, and the height of each bar represents the corresponding
b_{n,k}
.

b_{n,k}
when
n=40

Our testing on uni-modality also yields a very nice pattern, and we give the following conjecture:

Conjecture 5.1
.

For any integers
n,r
, the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
is uni-modal, that is, there exists
k
such that

(5.6)

c_{n,r,d}\leq c_{n,r,d+1}\text{ for }d<k\text{, and }c_{n,r,d}\geq c_{n,r,d+1}\text{ for }d\geq k.

Conjecture
5.1
is tested to be true for
r\leq 4
and
n\leq 20
.

As mentioned in
[
10
]
, orbit harmonics played a key tool in our analysis. We treated the colored permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
as a collection of
n\times n
matrices, and proved that
I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}=\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(\mathfrak{S}_{n,r})
. We provide one possible direction for future studies below.

A
complex reflection
on a finite-dimensional vector space
V
is an element of finite order in
\mathrm{GL}(V)
which fixes a hyperplane pointwise. A
complex reflection group

W
is a finite group generated by complex reflections. Shepherd and Todd showed in
[
13
]
that any complex reflection group is either one of the
34
exceptional cases, or in the form
G(r,p,n)
, where
r,p,n
are integers with
p
divides r, and the group
G(r,p,n)
consists of
n\times n
matrices with

•

exactly one nonzero entry in each row or column;

•

each nonzero entry is an
r
-th root of unity;

•

the product of all the nonzero-entries is an
r/p
-th root of unity.

When
p=1
,
G(r,1,n)
is the colored permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
, and we calculated
\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(G(r,1,n))=I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
in this paper. It may be interesting to study
\mathrm{gr}\mathbf{I}(G(r,p,n))
for different
p
’s that divides
r
. For example,
G(2,2,n)
coincides with the Coxeter group of type
D
, and it would be a good object for future study.

Another possible direction is on the equivariant log-concavity of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
. In
[
10
]
, Rhoades conjectured that with
\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}
acting on
(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}])_{d}
by independent row and column operation, there exists an injection

(5.7)

(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d+1}\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d-1}\xhookrightarrow{\phi}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d}\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d}

such that given
(w,v)\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n}
,
f\in(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d+1}
,
g\in(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{n})_{d-1}
,

(5.8)

\phi\left((w,v)\cdot(f\otimes g)\right)=(w,v)\cdot\left(\phi(f\otimes g)\right).

This conjecture would imply both Chen’s Conjecture
[
2
]
and the Novak-Rhoades Conjecture
[
6
]
. As the Hilbert series of
\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}}
is not always log-concave, it would be interesting to study given
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
, for which
d
’s we can have an injection

(5.9)

(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{d+1}\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{d-1}\xhookrightarrow{}(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{d}\otimes(\mathbb{C}[\mathbf{x}_{n\times n}]/I_{\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}})_{d}

that commutes with the diagonal action of
\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}\times\mathfrak{S}_{n,r}
. Our calculation shows that when
n=1
such an injection exists for any
r,d
.

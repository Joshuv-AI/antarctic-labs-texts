Extremal Chemical Graphs for the Arithmetic-Geometric Index
Alain Hertz, Sébastien Bonte, Gauvain Devillez, Valentin Dusollier, Hadrien Mélot, David Schindl, [2ex] Department of Mathematics, Industrial Engineering[-3pt] Polytechnique Montréal - Gerad, Montréal, Canada

Abstract
The arithmetic-geometric index is a newly proposed degree-based graph invariant in mathematical chemistry. We give a sharp upper bound on the value of this invariant for connected chemical graphs of given order and size and characterize the connected chemical graphs that reach the bound. We also prove that the removal of the constraint that extremal chemical graphs must be connected does not allow to increase the upper bound.



1
Introduction

In mathematical chemistry, and more specifically in chemical graph theory, topological indices are values that characterize a graph representing a molecule. This value is correlated with some properties of said molecule. One largely studied class of such indices is composed of degree-based invariants. A well known example of these is the Randić index, proposed by Randić
[
14
]
in 1975. Following the interest in this index, many others were later introduced. Of interest here is the
arithmetic-geometric index
proposed in 2015 by Shegehalli and Kanabur
[
17
]
. Let
G
be a graph with edge set
E
and let
d_{u}
and
d_{v}
be the degrees of the endpoints of an edge
uv\in E
. The arithmetic-geometric index
\texttt{AG}(G)
of
G
, is defined as

\texttt{AG}(G)=\sum_{uv\in E}\frac{d_{u}+d_{v}}{2\sqrt{d_{u}d_{v}}}.

The summand in the above formula is the ratio between the arithmetic and geometric means of
d_{u}
and
d_{v}
. If we replace each summand by its inverse, we obtain another graph invariant known as the
geometric-arithmetic index introduced in 2009 by Vukičević and Furtula
[
20
]
and studied for example in
[
15
,
2
,
1
,
5
]
.
Several papers have focused on properties of the arithmetic-geometric index. For example, Shegehalli and Kanabur
[
17
,
18
]
give the value of this index for some families of graphs. Lower bounds for graphs of fixed size (i.e., number of edges) are provided in
[
16
,
12
,
19
]
, while an upper bound for graphs with fixed size and order (i.e., number of vertices) is established in
[
19
]
. Upper bounds for graphs of fixed size and fixed minimum and maximum degrees are given in
[
16
,
11
,
12
]
.
The maximum value of the arithmetic-geometric index of graphs of fixed order is known for
unicyclic graphs
[
21
]
,
bicyclic graphs
[
13
]
, bipartite graphs and trees
[
19
]
. In addition, Vujošević et al.
[
19
]
have characterized the chemical trees with maximum arithmetic-geometric index value. The relationship between the arithmetic-geometric index and other topological indices is studied, for example in
[
4
,
16
,
11
,
12
,
8
,
3
,
9
]
.

In this paper, we prove the following upper bound on the value of the arithmetic index of a connected chemical graphs
G
of order
n
and size
m
:

\texttt{AG}(G)\leq\frac{2n+5m}{6}+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{\emph{if} }2m-n\equiv 0\bmod{3},\\
\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}-\frac{13}{6}&\mbox{\emph{if} }2m-n\equiv 1\bmod{3},\\
\frac{21}{4\sqrt{3}}-\frac{37}{12}&\mbox{\emph{if} }2m-n\equiv 2\bmod{3}.\end{array}\right.

We show that with the exception of 22
(n,m)
pairs, the bound is sharp, and we characterize the connected chemical graphs of order
n
and size
m\geq n-1
that reach the bound. We also prove that no better value can be obtained by removing the constraint that the graph must be connected. Note that for
m=n-1
, this gives a characterization of extremal chemical trees of fixed order
n
. While such a characterization is given in
[
19
]
, we show that their result is not valid for 7 values of
n
.

In the next section we fix some notations, while Section
3
is devoted to observations that will motivate our characterization of connected chemical graphs with maximum arithmetic-geometric index value. Lemmas are proved in Section
4
and then used in Section
5
to prove the main theorem.

2
Notations

For basic notions of graph theory that are not defined here, we refer to Diestel
[
7
]
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple undirected graph. The
order

n=|V|
of
G
is its number of vertices and the
size

m=|E|
of
G
is its number of edges. We write
G\simeq H
if
G
and
H
are isomorphic. The
degree
of
v
, denoted
d_{v}
is the number of edges incident to
v
, and we say that
v
is
isolated
if
d_{v}=0
.

A
chemical graph
is a graph whose vertices have degree at most 4. The arithmetic-geometric index
\texttt{AG}(G)
of a graph
G
can be seen as a sum of costs on the edges of
G
. In particular, if we deal with chemical graphs, there is a limited number of possible values for the costs since they are computed from the degrees of the endpoints of the edges. Let
c_{i,j}=\frac{i+j}{2\sqrt{ij}}
be the cost of an edge with endpoints of degree
i
and
j
. The

4\times 4
cost matrix
C_{\texttt{AG}}
associated with the arithmetic-geometric index of chemical graphs is

C_{\texttt{AG}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}&\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}&\frac{5}{4}\\[5.16663pt]
\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}&1&\frac{5}{2\sqrt{6}}&\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}\\[5.16663pt]
\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}&\frac{5}{2\sqrt{6}}&1&\frac{7}{4\sqrt{3}}\\[5.16663pt]
\frac{5}{4}&\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}&\frac{7}{4\sqrt{3}}&1\\[5.16663pt]
\end{pmatrix}\approx\begin{pmatrix}1.0000&1.0607&1.1547&1.2500\\
1.0607&1.0000&1.0206&1.0607\\
1.1547&1.0206&1.0000&1.0104\\
1.2500&1.0607&1.0104&1.0000\\
\end{pmatrix}.

For a chemical graph
G
, let
n_{i}(G)
(
i=0,\ldots,4
) be the number of vertices of degree
i
and let
x_{i,j}(G)
(
1\leq i\leq j\leq 4
) be the number of edges with extremities of degrees
i
and
j
in
G
. Then, since
C_{\texttt{AG}}
is symmetric, we have

\texttt{AG}(G)=\sum_{1\leq i\leq j\leq 4}c_{i,j}\,x_{i,j}(G).

In what follows, we say that a chemical graph
G
is
extremal
if
\texttt{AG}(G)\geq\texttt{AG}(G^{\prime})
for all chemical graphs
G^{\prime}
with the same order and the same size as
G
.

.

.

If

Let

Lemma 1
.

Let
G
be a connected extremal chemical graph. Assume that
G
has a vertex

of degree 2 where

and
w

(a)

If
v
and

(b)

If
v
and
w

and
d_{w}{\leq}3
, then no vertex nonadjacent to
w

Proof.

(a)

Assume that
v
and
w
are nonadjacent, and that one of them, say
v
, has degree 3. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by replacing
uw
with
vw
. Then, with
i=d_{u}
and
j=d_{x}
, where
x
is any neighbor of

other than

, we have

\displaystyle\texttt{AG}(G^{\prime}){-}\texttt{AG}(G)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1,4}-c_{2,3}+\min_{i=1,\ldots 4}(c_{4,i}-c_{2,i})+2\min_{j=1,\ldots 4}(c_{4,j}{-}c_{3,j})

\displaystyle\approx 0.1479>0.

(b)

Assume that
v
and
w
are adjacent with
d_{v}\geq 3
and
d_{w}\leq 3
, and let
x
be a vertex nonadjacent to
w
such that
d_{x}=2
or 3. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by replacing
uw
with
xw
. Then, with
i=d_{v}
,
j=d_{w}
,
k=d_{x}
and
\ell=d_{y}
, where
y
is any neighbor of

, we have

\displaystyle\texttt{AG}(G^{\prime}){-}\texttt{AG}(G)\;{\geq}

\displaystyle\;\min_{i=3,4}(c_{1,i}-c_{2,i})

\displaystyle\;{+}\min_{k=2,3}{\bigg(}\min_{j=2,3}(c_{j,k{+}1}{-}c_{j,2}){+}k\min_{\ell=1,\ldots,4}(c_{\ell,k{+}1}{-}c_{\ell,k}){\bigg)}

\displaystyle\;{\approx}

\displaystyle\;0.0128>0.

In both cases,

•

Theorem 2
.

Let
G
be a non-connected chemical graph of order

and size

is extremal, then

belongs to
\mathcal{G}_{n,m}

Hence, since

contains only one vertex

be the graph obtained from

by replacing the edge

by the edge

y
belongs to a cycle, at least one of its neighbors

and

other than

and
w

\displaystyle\texttt{AG}(G^{\prime}){-}\texttt{AG}(G)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{1,4}+\min_{i=3,4}\bigl(\min_{j=2,3,4}(c_{i-1,j}{-}c_{i,j})-c_{4,i}\bigr)

Conclusion

We have determined a sharp upper bound on the value of the arithmetic-geometric index of chemical graphs of order
n
and size
m\geq n-1
, and we have characterized the chemical graphs that reach the bound.
This allows, for example, to characterize extremal chemical trees as well as extremal unicyclic or bicyclic chemical graphs. For
m\leq n-2
, we have shown that there is an extremal chemical graph or order
n
and size
m
which is a disjoint union of extremal chemical trees.

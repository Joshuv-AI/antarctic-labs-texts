Abstract
We show that the affine closure T ∗ ​ ( SL n / U ) ¯ \overline{T^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{n}/U)} has symplectic singularities, in the sense of Beauville. In the special case n = 3 n=3 , we show that the affine closure T ∗ ​ ( SL 3 / U ) ¯ \overline{T^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}/U)} is isomorphic to the closure 𝒪 ¯ min \overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}} of the minimal nilpotent orbit 𝒪 min \mathcal{O}_{\textrm{min}} in 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 8 \mathfrak{so}_{8} . Moreover, the quasi-classical Gelfand-Graev action of the Weyl group W W on T ∗ ​ ( SL 3 / U ) ¯ \overline{T^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}/U)} can be identified with the restriction to 𝒪 ¯ min \overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}} of E. Cartan’s triality action on 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 8 \mathfrak{so}_{8} .

Introduction

Let
G
be a complex connected semisimple algebraic group,
B
a Borel subgroup of
G
, and
U
the unipotent radical of
B
. For example, in the case
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
, we can take
U
to be the collection of all upper triangular matrices with all diagonal entries equal to
1
.
The homogeneous space
G/U
is called the “basic affine space”. While
G/B
is projective, the basic affine space
G/U
is a quasi-affine variety. It turns out that many interesting problems in representation theory are related to the basic affine space. In particular, the algebra
\mathcal{D}(G/U)
of algebraic differential operators on
G/U
is well-studied, for example in
[
BGG75
]
[
GK22
]
.
In this paper, we study the total space of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}(G/U)
, of which the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}(G/U)]
is the quasi-classical counterpart of
\mathcal{D}(G/U)
. From a result by Ginzburg and Riche
[
GR15
]
, the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[T^{*}(G/U)]
is finitely generated, and the affine closure of the basic affine space is defined as

\overline{T^{*}(G/U)}\coloneqq\Spec\mathbb{C}[T^{*}(G/U)].

Symplectic singularities, a notion of which was first introduced by Beauville in
[
Bea00
]
, play an important role in representation theory. For instance, conic symplectic singularities (affine symplectic singularities with a good
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action) admit universal flat Poisson deformations
and filtered quantizations.
There are many examples of symplectic singularities, for example, finite quotient singularities
[
Bea00
]
, normalization of the closure of nilpotent coadjoint orbits
[
Pan91
]
, and Nakajima quiver varieties
[
BS21
]
.
In
[
GK22
]
, Ginzburg and Kazhdan conjectured that

Conjecture 1.1
.

The affine closure
\overline{T^{*}(G/U)}
has symplectic singularities.

And we prove the conjecture in section
2
for the special case
G=\mathrm{SL}_{n}
.

In the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
, there is a unique nilpotent adjoint orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{min}}\subset\mathfrak{so}_{8}
of minimal (positive) dimension
10
. The closure of the minimal orbit is
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}=\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{min}}\cup\{0\}.

In section
3
, we show that there is an isomorphism of affine varieties

\overline{T^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}/U)}\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}.

(1)

In
[
GK22
]
, Ginzburg and Kazhdan constructed an action on
\overline{T^{*}(G/U)}
by the Weyl group
W
of
G
, called the “Gelfand–Graev action”. So in the case
G=\mathrm{SL}_{3}
we have an
S_{3}
-action on
\overline{T^{*}(\mathrm{SL}_{3}/U)}
. On the other hand, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}_{8}
has an
S_{3}
-symmetry called the triality action
[
Car25
]
[
MW15
]
, and the restriction of the triality action gives an
S_{3}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{\textrm{min}}
. In section
4
, we give a new interpretation of this triality action. In section
5
, we show that the isomorphism (
1
) is
S_{3}
-equivariant.
In section
6
, we show the map (

Lemma 2.4
.

Then we have an exact sequence

where

is a complex torus of rank

By Theorem 6.13 in

Symplectic form on the smooth locus

Let us recall the following known results from
[
CG97
]
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}
be a nilpotent adjoint orbit in
\mathfrak{g}
.
Since
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}
is a nilpotent orbit, there is a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on it such that
\omega^{\textrm{KKS}}=\mathcal{L}_{\textsl{Eu}}\omega^{\textrm{KKS}},
where
Eu
is the Euler vector field on
\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}
. Define

\lambda^{\textrm{KKS}}\coloneqq\iota_{\textsl{Eu}}\omega^{\textrm{KKS}}.

So we have

d\lambda^{\textrm{KKS}}=d\iota_{\textsl{Eu}}\omega^{\textrm{KKS}}=\mathcal{L}_{\textsl{Eu}}\omega^{\textrm{KKS}}=\omega^{\textrm{KKS}}.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then

\lambda^{\textrm{KKS}}_{X}(\xi_{Y})=\kappa(X,Y).

Proof.

Since
X
is nilpotent, we have
Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{X}
if and only if
\kappa(X,Y)=0
. So the RHS of the formula only depends on
\xi_{Y}
(i.e. independent of the choice of
Y
). Let
E\in\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}
. Fix some
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triple
(E,H,F)
in
\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have

(\xi_{H})_{E}=2\,\textsl{Eu}_{E}\in T_{E}\mathcal{O}_{\textrm{nilp}}.

Since
\lambda^{\textrm{KKS}}
is invariant under the
G
-action, it suffices to prove the statement for
X=E
. Let
Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. We compute

(\iota_{\textsl{Eu}}\omega_{E}^{\textrm{KKS}})(\xi_{Y})=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{E}^{\textrm{KKS}}(\xi_{Y},\xi_{H})=\frac{1}{2}\kappa(E,[Y,H])=\frac{1}{2}\kappa([H,E],Y)=\kappa(E,Y).\qed

\lambda^{\prime}
is the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
-invariant one form on
\Hom(\mathbb{C}^{2},\mathbb{C}^{8})=\mathbb{C}^{8}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{8}
defined by

\lambda^{\prime}_{v_{1}\oplus v_{2}}(x_{1}\oplus x_{2})=(v_{1},x_{2})-(v_{2},x_{1}).

Lift the tangent vector
\xi_{w_{1}\wedge w_{2}}
to
(x_{1}\oplus x_{2})\in T_{v_{1}\oplus v_{2}}(\mathbb{C}^{8}\oplus\mathbb{C}^{8})
, where

\displaystyle x_{1}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{w_{1}\wedge w_{2}}(v_{1})=(w_{1},v_{1})w_{2}-(w_{2},v_{1})w_{1},

\displaystyle x_{2}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{w_{1}\wedge w_{2}}(v_{2})=(w_{1},v_{2})w_{2}-(w_{2},v_{2})w_{1}.

So we have

\lambda^{\prime}_{v_{1}\oplus v_{2}}(x_{1}\oplus x_{2})=2((w_{1},v_{2})(v_{1},w_{2})-(w_{2},v_{2})(v_{1},w_{1}))=\lambda^{\textrm{KKS}}_{v_{1}\wedge v_{2}}(\xi_{w_{1}\wedge w_{2}}).\qed

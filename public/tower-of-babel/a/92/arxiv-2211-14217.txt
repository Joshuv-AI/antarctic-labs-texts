Graded Lie algebras, Fourier transform and primitive pairs
Tamanna Chatterjee

Abstract
. In this paper we study the Fourier transform on graded Lie algebras. Let G G be a complex, connected, reductive, algebraic group, and χ : ℂ × → G \chi:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G be a fixed cocharacter that defines a grading on 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , the Lie algebra of G G . Let G 0 G_{0} be the centralizer of χ ⁡ ( ℂ × ) \chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times}) . Here under some assumptions on the field 𝕜 \Bbbk and also assuming two conjectures for the group G G , we prove that the Fourier transform sends parity complexes to parity complexes. Primitive pairs have played an important role in Lusztig’s paper [ Lu ] to prove a block decomposition in the graded setting. A long term goal of this project is to prove a similar block decomposition in positive characteristic. In this paper we have tried to understand the primitive pair and its relation with the Fourier transform.

2.
Background

In this section we go over some definitions, notations and some already known results.
Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic
l\geq 0
. We consider sheaves with coefficients in
\Bbbk
. The varieties we work on will be over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
H
be a linear algebraic group and
X
be a
H
-variety. We denote by
D^{b}_{H}(X,\Bbbk)
or
D^{b}_{H}(X)
, the
H
-equivariant derived category of constructible sheaves, which is defined in
[
BL
]
. Let
\p_{H}(X,\Bbbk)
be the full subcategory of
H
-equivariant perverse
\Bbbk
-sheaves. The constant sheaf on
X
is denoted by
{\underline{V}}_{X}
or more simply by

\underline{V}
, where
V
is a
\Bbbk
-vector space.

Let
G
be a connected, reductive, algebraic group over

\mathbb{C}
and
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
.
We fix a cocharacter map,
\chi:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to G
and define,

G_{0}=\{g\in G|g\chi(t)=\chi(t)g,\forall{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, define,

\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}|\mathrm{Ad}(\chi(t))x=t^{n}x,\forall{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}.

This defines a grading on
\mathfrak{g}
,

\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\lie(G_{0})
and

G_{0}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. For
n\neq 0
,
G_{0}
acts on
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
with only finitely many orbits. In
[
Ch
]
we studied the
G_{0}
-equivariant bounded derived category of sheaves,
D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},\Bbbk)
with some restriction on the field characteristic of
\Bbbk
, which will be discussed later in this section.

Here we will review some notations that already have been introduced in
[
Ch
]
.

Recall that
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
is the Lie algebra of
SL_{2}
generated by,

e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\\
\end{pmatrix},h=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

\end{pmatrix},f=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Let
J_{n}=\{\phi:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{g}|\hskip 2.84526pt\phi(e)\in\mathfrak{g}_{n},\phi(f)\in\mathfrak{g}_{-n},\phi(h)\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\}
. We have an action of
G_{0}
on

by

The map from the set of

-orbits on

2.2.
Induction and restriction

Induction and restriction are two important functors both on nilpotent cone and graded Lie algebras which allow us to go from Levi to the actual group or from the group to a Levi subgroup. Let
P
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
and
L
be a Levi factor in
P
with
U_{P}
, the unipotent radical. For parabolic induction and restriction on nilpotent cone we use the following diagram,

\mathcal{N}_{L}\xleftarrow{\pi_{P}}\mathcal{N}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}\xrightarrow{e_{P}}G\times^{P}(\mathcal{N}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{P})\xrightarrow{\mu_{P}}\mathcal{N}_{G}.

Here
\mathfrak{u}_{P}=\lie(U_{P})
,
\pi_{P},e_{P}
are the obvious maps and
\mu_{P}(g,x)=\mathrm{Ad}(g)x
. Let

i_{P}=\mu_{P}\circ e_{P}:\mathcal{N}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}\to\mathcal{N}_{G}.

The parabolic restriction functor,

\rnn^{G}_{P}:D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{L}(\mathcal{N}_{L},\Bbbk)

is defined by
\rnn^{G}_{P}(\mathcal{F})={\pi_{P}}_{!}e_{P}^{*}\mu_{P}^{*}\f^{G}_{L}(\mathcal{F})={\pi_{P}}_{!}i_{P}^{*}\f^{G}_{L}(\mathcal{F})
. Here

\f^{G}_{L}:D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{L}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk)

is the forgetful functor.
The parabolic induction comes from the same diagram above,

\inn^{G}_{P}:D^{b}_{L}(\mathcal{N}_{L},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk),

and is defined by

\inn^{G}_{P}(\mathcal{F}):={\mu_{P}}_{!}(e_{P}^{*}\f^{G}_{P})^{-1}\pi_{P}^{*}(\mathcal{F})
.
Here again
\f^{G}_{P}
denotes the forgetful functor and,
e_{P}^{*}\f^{G}_{P}:D^{b}_{G}(G\times^{P}(\mathcal{N}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{P}))\to D^{b}_{P}(\mathcal{N}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{P})
is the induction equivalence map.

Here we make the assumption that
L
defined above contains
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.
Let
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{n}
be the Lie algebras of
P,L,U_{P}
respectively. Then
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{n}
inherit grading from
\mathfrak{g}
.

For induction and restriction on graded Lie algebras we use the diagram below,

\mathfrak{l}_{n}\xleftarrow{\pi}\mathfrak{p}_{n}\xrightarrow{e}G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}\mathfrak{p}_{n}\xrightarrow{\mu}\mathfrak{g}_{n}

All the maps here have the same meaning as in the diagram above.
The restriction,

\rnn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}:D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{L_{0}}(\mathfrak{l}_{n},\Bbbk)

is defined by
\rnn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathcal{F})={\pi}_{!}e^{*}\mu^{*}\f^{G_{0}}_{L_{0}}(\mathcal{F})={\pi}_{!}i^{*}\f^{G_{0}}_{L_{0}}(\mathcal{F})
with
i:\mathfrak{p}_{n}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is the inclusion map. Here
the parabolic induction comes from the same diagram above,

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}:D^{b}_{L_{0}}(\mathfrak{l}_{n},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n},\Bbbk)

and is defined by,

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathcal{F}):={\mu}_{!}(e^{*}\f^{G_{0}}_{P_{0}})^{-1}\pi^{*}(\mathcal{F})
.

In this paper we will use a different notation of induction and restriction when we deal with the Fourier transform. The notations will be
\ind_{n}
for induction , and
\res_{n}
for the restriction
on the
n
-th graded setting. This notation will be used in Section
4
and later on. The reason behind this is that Fourier transform sends sheaf complexes on
n
-th graded pieces to sheaf complexes on
(-n)
-th graded pieces.

2.3.
Parity sheaves

Parity sheaves were first introduced by Juteau, Mautner and Williamson
[
JMW
]
. They are constructible complexes on some stratified space, where the stratas satisfy some cohomology vanishing properties. Once these conditions are there then for any stratified space
X
with strata
X_{\lambda}
and local system
\mathcal{L}
on
X_{\lambda}
, there exists at-most one parity sheaf, we denote this by
\mathcal{E}(X_{\lambda},\mathcal{L})
. A detailed discussion is in
[
JMW
]
and a summarized version can be found in
[
Ch
, Subsection 2.5]
. The cohomology vanishing property for our case is the following theorem that has been proved in
[
Ch
, Th. 6.4]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

With the assumptions on the field charateristic, for any pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
we have,

\h^{*}_{G_{0}}(\mathcal{L})=0\text{, for $*$ odd.}

The next theorem provides the cohomology vanishing condition for the nilpotent cone.

Theorem 2.4
.

With the assumptions on the field charateristic, for any pair
(C,\mathcal{F})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
we have,

\h^{*}_{G}(\mathcal{F})=0\text{, for $*$ odd.}

This theorem has been proved in
[
JMW
]
.
Once we have these theorems we can talk about parity sheaves on both nilpotent cone and graded pieces.
But unlike
\mathcal{IC}
’s, the existence of parity is not automatic. The existence for nilpotent cone has been discussed in
[
JMW
, 4.3]
. The existence for graded Lie algebras has been proved in
[
Ch
, Th. 6.11]
.
The assumptions that were needed in
[
Ch
]
were the followings,

Assumption 2.5
.

(1)

The characteristic
l
of
\Bbbk
is a pretty good prime for
G

[
Ch
, 2.6]
.

(2)

The field
\Bbbk
is big enough for
G
;
i.e, for every Levi subgroup
L
of
G
and pair
(C_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(L)
, the irreducible
L
-equivariant
\Bbbk
-local system
\mathcal{E}_{L}
is absolutely irreducible.

(3)

We assume the Mautner’s cleanness conjecture is true in our setting, that is every
0
-cuspidal in
\mathscr{I}(G)
is
l
-clean
[
Ch
, Conjecture 2.10]
.

(4)

For any parabolic
P
and Levi subgroup
L\subset P
with
(C,\mathcal{F})\in\mathscr{I}(G)^{\cu}
,
\inn^{G}_{P}\mathcal{E}(C,\mathcal{F})
is parity
[
Ch
, Conjecture 2.28]
.

We make these same assumptions in this paper. The cause is that the results of this paper are dependent on the the results in
[
Ch
]
.

4.
Fourier Sato transform

Fourier transform was studied by Hotta-Kashiwara
[
HK
]
for
\mathcal{D}
-modules and later by Brylinski
[
Bry
]
in characteristic
0
. Study of Fourier transform in the context of Springer sheaf in positive characteristic has been initiated by Juteau
[
Ju
]
. As
\mathcal{N}
is not a vector space, Fourier transform in the usual sense does not make sense. In
[
AM
]
a new functor
\mathcal{R}
from
D_{G}^{b}(\mathcal{N},\kp)
to
D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N},\kp)
has been introduced, which is given by the Fourier transform on
\mathfrak{g}
followed by the restriction on the nilpotent cone. This is an auto-equivalence
[
AM
]
. Later in
[
AHJR
]
it has been proved that the Fourier transform of the Springer sheaf is associated to the sign change representation of the Weyl group.
In this section we will recall the Fourier-Sato transform
[
AHJR
]
and prove that it sends cuspidal pairs to cuspidal pairs and parity complexes to parity complexes.

4.1.
Fourier-Sato transform on vector spaces

Let
V
be a complex vector space and
H
be an algebraic group acting linearly on
V
. An object
\mathcal{F}
in the
H
-equivariant bounded derived category of
V
,
D^{b}_{H}(V,\Bbbk)
will be called conic if for any
v\in V
and
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, the sheaf
\h^{i}(\mathcal{F})|_{\mathbb{C}.v-0}
is locally constant. Or we can say a sheaf complex is conic if it is contructible with respect to the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
orbits in
V
.
D^{b}_{H,con}(V,\Bbbk)
be the full-subcategory of conic objects in
D^{b}_{H}(V,\Bbbk)
.
We will denote the dual of
V
by
V^{*}
.

The Fourier-Sato transform is a functor,

\Phi_{V}:D^{b}_{H,con}(V,\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{H,con}(V^{*},\Bbbk).

This functor was initially introduced in
[
KS
, 3.7]
and modified in
[
AM
]
with a shift of
[\dim V]
so that the functor is
t
-exact for the perverse
t
-structure
[
KS
, Prop. 10.3.8]
. This functor is an equivalence of categories with the following properties,

(1)

\Phi_{V}(\delta_{V})=\Bbbk_{V^{*}}[\dim V^{*}]
, where
\delta_{V}
is the skyscraper sheaf on
V
.

(2)

It commutes with the external tensor product, that is for
V=V_{1}\times V_{2}
,

\Phi_{V}(M_{1}\boxtimes M_{2})\cong\Phi_{V_{1}}(M_{1})\boxtimes\Phi_{V_{2}}(M_{2}),

with
M_{1}\in D^{b}_{H,con}(V_{1})
and
M_{2}\in D^{b}_{H,con}(V_{2})
.

4.2.
Fourier transform on Nilpotent cone

Fourier transform on nilpotent cone has been studied by Achar and Mautner
[
AM
]
. As the nilpotent cone is not a vector space the Fourier-Sato transform does not make sense there. So they define the functor in the following way. We fix a
G
-equivariant isomorphism,
\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, which allows us to consider the Fourier-Sato tranformation
\phig
as a functor,

D^{b}_{G,con}(\mathfrak{g},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{G,con}(\mathfrak{g},\Bbbk).

Let
i:\mathcal{N}_{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the inclusion map. The Fourier transform on
\mathcal{N}_{G}
is defined by,

\mathscr{R}:D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk)\to D^{b}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{G},\Bbbk),

with
\mathscr{R}:=i^{*}\phig i_{*}[\dim\mathcal{N}_{G}-\dim\mathfrak{g}]
.

i_{*}
takes values in
D^{b}_{G,con}(\mathfrak{g},\Bbbk)
, therefore
\mathscr{R}
makes sense.
The functor
\mathscr{R}
is an auto-equivalence with the inverse
\mathscr{R}^{\prime}
, which is defined as,

\mathscr{R}^{\prime}:=i^{!}\phig^{\prime}i_{*}[\dim\mathfrak{g}-\mathcal{N}_{G}].

Here
\phig^{\prime}
is the inverse of
\phig
. One important property of the functor
\mathcal{R}
is that for
GL_{n}
it is a geometric version of Ringel duality
[
AM
]
.

To deal with this grading change, in this section particularly we will use the notations,

Corollary 4.2
.

For
P\subset G
with
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\subset P
,

\phign\ind_{n}\cong\ind_{-n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that the restriction is left adjoint to the induction.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

The Fourier Sato transform sends cuspidal pairs to cuspidal pairs.

Proof.

\phign:D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})\to D^{b}_{G_{0}}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
sends simple perverse sheaves to simple perverse sheaves. Now our claim is that it sends non-cuspidal pairs to non-cuspidal pairs, which will be enough to prove that
\phign
sends cuspidal pairs to cuspidal pairs as it is an equivalence of categories.
We will prove this by induction on the support. For the base case,

\phign(\underline{\kp}_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{-n}])=\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{0},\underline{\kp}_{pt}).

We know
\underline{\kp}_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}
is non-clean, hence non-cuspidal. Therefore the base case is proved. Now we want to show that for the non-cuspidal pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
,
\phign(\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L}))
is non-cuspidal. By
[
Ch
, Theorem 6.11]
,
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
appears as direct summand of
\ind_{n}\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
, where
P
is a parabolic containing a Levi
L
with
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\subset L
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
. By corollary
4.2
,
\phign
commutes with induction, so
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is a direct summand of
\ind_{n}\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. By induction,
\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
is cuspidal. This implies
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is non-cuspidal. The triangulated category generated by
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
and all the
\mathcal{IC}
’s coming from smaller orbits is same as the triangulated category generated by
\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
and every
\mathcal{IC}
’s coming from smaller orbits. But from the induction and the above argument the former triangulated category is subset of the Fourier-Sato transform of non-cuspidal pairs. Therefore same is true for the later triangulated category. Hence
\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is non-cuspidal.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

\phign
sends parity complexes to parity complexes.

Proof.

Let
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
and
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
be the associated parity sheaf. By
[
Ch
, Theorem 6.11]
, there exists parabolic subgroup
P
with a Levi
L
containing
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
such that, so
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is direct summand of
\ind_{n}\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. By Corollary
4.2
,
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is direct summand of
\ind_{n}\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. By Theorem
4.3
,
\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
is cuspidal. By
[
Ch
, Theorem 6.12]
,
\ind_{n}\phign\mathcal{IC}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
is parity. Therefore
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is parity.
∎

5.
Primitive pair

Primitive pairs have been introduced by Lusztig
[
Lu
]
both on the nilpotent cone and the graded Lie algebra. One of the main goal of that paper was to define a block decomposition of
\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
using the primitive pairs on the nilpotent cone. In this section we will try to prove some of the results of
[
Lu
]
in positive characteristic. A long term goal will be to study the block decomposition of
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
in positive characteristic.

First we will introduce the primitive pair on the nilpotent cone.

Definition 5.1
.

A pair
(C,\mathcal{E})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
is said to be primitive if there exists a parabolic subgroup
P
with Levi
L
and a cuspidal pair
(C_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\ii
such that the following holds,

(1)

There exists a
P
-orbit
C^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{N}_{P}
, so that for all
x\in C^{\prime}
,
\pi(x)\in C_{L}
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{l}
.

(2)

For any
x\in C^{\prime}
, we have,

L^{\pi(x)}/(L^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong P^{x}/(P^{x})^{\circ}\cong G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.

(3)

\mathcal{E}|_{C^{\prime}}\cong(\pi|_{C^{\prime}})^{*}\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

The definition of the primitive pair on
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is similar to the above definition on the nilpotent cone.

Definition 5.2
.

A pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is said to be primitive if there exists a parabolic subgroup
P
containing the image of
\chi
with Levi
L
and a cuspidal pair
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
such that the following holds,

(1)

There exists a
P_{0}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, so that for all
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
,
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
, where
\pi:\mathfrak{p}_{n}\to\mathfrak{l}_{n}
.

(2)

For any
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, we have,

L_{0}^{\pi(x)}/(L_{0}^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong P_{0}^{x}/(P_{0}^{x})^{\circ}\cong G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}.

(3)

\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\cong(\pi|_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}})^{*}\mathcal{E}_{L}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Assume
(C,\mathcal{E})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
be a primitive pair with
C\cap\mathfrak{g}_{n}\neq\emptyset
associated to the parabolic subgroup
P
containing a Levi
L
and
(C_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(L)^{0-\cu}
with
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\subset L
and
C_{L}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{n}\neq\emptyset
. Then
(C,\mathcal{E})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
gives rise to a primitive pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
.

Proof.

Let
C^{\prime}
be the
P
-orbit in
\mathcal{N}_{P}
which satisfies the conditions in Definition
5.1
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{L}=C_{L}\cap\mathfrak{l}_{n}
and
\mathcal{F}_{L}=\mathcal{E}_{L}|_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}
. Then by the definition of cuspidal pair in the graded setting,
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{F}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})^{\cu}
. We define,

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=\{x\in\mathfrak{p}_{n}|\text{ there exists a Levi }M^{\prime}\subset P,\text{ with }\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\subset M^{\prime},x\in\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}_{n},\text{ and }\pi(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{L}\}

As all the Levi inside
P
are
P_{0}
-conjugate to each other, therefore
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is a single
P_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the unique
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
containing
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{E}|_{\mathcal{O}}
. Our claim is that
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is primitive. By the construction of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, it satisfies the first condition in Definition
5.2
. As
(C,\mathcal{E})\in\mathscr{I}(G)
is primitive, for
x\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, we have,

L^{\pi(x)}/(L^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong P^{x}/(P^{x})^{\circ}\cong G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.

First we want to show
\mathcal{L}
is irreducible. For that it is enough to show that
G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}\to G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}
is surjective.

We have the following commutative diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{0}^{\pi(x)}/(L_{0}^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P_{0}^{x}/(P_{0}^{x})^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L^{\pi(x)}/(L^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{x}/(P^{x})^{\circ}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.}

The lower horizontal maps are all isomorphisms by the definition of the primitive pair on the nilpotent cone. For the map
P_{0}^{x}\to L_{0}^{\pi(x)}
, we have connected kernel, hence induces isomorphism on the component groups.
By
[
Lu
, Prop. 4.3]
,
(L,\chi)
is
n
-rigid and therefore by
[
Lu
, Prop. 4.2(c)]
,

(5.2)

L^{\pi(x)}/(L^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}\cong L_{0}^{\pi(x)}/(L_{0}^{\pi(x)})^{\circ}.

Hence the middle vertical arrow is an isomorphism. Which tells us that in the right square the composition of top horizontal with the right vertical map is an isomorphism. This implies the map
G^{x}_{0}/(G^{x}_{0})^{\circ}\to G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}
is at least surjective. So we are done with the claim that
\mathcal{L}
is irreducible.

But to show that
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is primitive, we need isomorphisms for the upper horizontal maps.

By the definition of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, there exists
M^{\prime}
, a Levi subgroup of
P

\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})\subset M^{\prime}
,
x\in\mathfrak{m}_{n}^{\prime}
and
\pi(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{L}

\phi:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
with
\phi(e)=x
,
\phi(f)\in\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
and
\phi(h)\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, where
\{e,h,f\}
is the standard triple in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Let
\tilde{\phi}:SL_{2}\to M^{\prime}
be such that
d\tilde{\phi}=\phi
and we define
\chi^{\prime}:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to M^{\prime}
by,

\chi^{\prime}(a)=\tilde{\phi}\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\phi
satisfies the condition in
[
Ch
, Definition 6.1]
with respect to
\chi:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\to M^{\prime}
. Considering
\chi
as a map to
G
, we use the construction
[
Ch
, 6.2]

\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{m}
. It is not hard to see that
x
is in
\mathfrak{m}
. By
[
Ch
, Th. 6.3]
,
(M,\chi)
is
n
-rigid and hence,

(5.3)

M^{x}/(M^{x})^{\circ}\cong M_{0}^{x}/(M_{0}^{x})^{\circ},\text{ by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Lu}{}{}, Prop. ~4.2(c)]}}.

Also by
[
Lu
, Prop. 5.8]
,

(5.4)

M_{0}^{x}/(M_{0}^{x})^{\circ}\cong G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}.

As
M^{\prime}
is a Levi subgroup of
P
conjugate to
L
, we have from Definition
5.1
,

(5.5)

M^{\prime x}/(M^{\prime x})^{\circ}\cong G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.

Now as
\phi:\mathfrak{sl}_{2}\to\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
is
n
-adapted to
\chi
,
we have,

{}_{m}\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}_{\frac{mn}{2}}\text{ if $mn/2\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

By the construction
[
Ch
, 6.2]
,
\mathfrak{m}=\bigoplus_{m}(_{m}\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{\frac{mn}{2}})
, when
mn/2\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{m}
. Hence the triple
(M,M^{\prime},x)
plays the same role as
(G,M^{\prime},x)
. Hence we have,

(5.6)

M^{\prime x}/(M^{\prime x})^{\circ}\cong M^{x}/(M^{x})^{\circ}.

Combining (
5.3
), (
5.4
), (
5.5

5.6

G_{0}^{x}/(G_{0}^{x})^{\circ}\cong G^{x}/(G^{x})^{\circ}.

we already know the top left horizontal map is isomorphism. We want to show the right top horizontal map is isomorphism too. As we have already seen the left and right vertical and the lower horizontal maps are isomorphisms in the right square, hence the top horizontal map is isomorphism too.

Now we have from the following commutative diagram,

Our next claim is that,

G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})\cong G_{0}\times^{G_{0}^{y}}(\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})).

We define a map,

G_{0}\times(\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}))\to G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}),

by

(g,(hP_{0},y))\to(ghP_{0},\mathrm{Ad}(g)y).

Recall
\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
is a
P_{0}
-orbit. Hence
G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
is isomorphic to a
G_{0}
-orbit. Therefore any element in
G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
is of the form
(gP_{0},y)
, whose inverse image under the above map is
(g,(P_{0},y))
. Hence the above map is surjective. If
(ghP_{0},\mathrm{Ad}(g)y)=(P_{0},y)
, then
\mathrm{Ad}(g)y=y
. This implies
g\in G_{0}^{y}
. So we get a bijection between
G_{0}\times^{G_{0}^{y}}(\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}))
and
G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
. Now
G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
is smooth as
\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
is a
P_{0}
-orbit. Also by Lemma
5.7
, we have
\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})\cong(G_{0}^{y})^{\circ}/(P_{0}^{y})^{\circ}
. Recall
(G_{0}^{y})^{\circ}/(P_{0}^{y})^{\circ}
is a flag variety, therefore smooth. Bijection between two smooth varieties induces isomorphism of varieties. Hence we are done with our claim.
This means the map
\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})\xhookrightarrow{}G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
induces induction equivalence.

The projection,

G_{0}\times^{G_{0}^{y}}(\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}))\to G_{0}/G_{0}^{y}\cong\mathcal{O}

is a fiber bundle with the fiber
\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
. But being a flag variety,
\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})
is simply connected. Hence we get the following isomorphism of the fundamental groups,

\pi_{1}(\mathcal{O})\cong\pi_{1}(G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})).

The
G_{0}
-equivariant local systems on
\mathcal{O}

\mathrm{Loc}_{G_{0}}(\mathcal{O})

\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{O}))

\displaystyle\mathrm{Loc}_{G_{0}}(\mathcal{O})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Rep}(\pi_{1}(G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n})))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Loc}_{G_{0}^{y}}(\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}))\text{, by induction equivalence.}

Hence pullback along the map,

\mu^{-1}(y)\cap G_{0}\times^{P_{0}}(\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}))\to\{y\}

induces an equivalence of categories.

\mathcal{O}
, so we are done.

(3)

By
[
Ch
, Th. 5.3]
,
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{O}_{L}})
is parity. Therefore by (1) we can conclude (3).

(4)

It follows from (1), (2) and (3).

∎

5.3.
Primitive pair and Fourier transform

The goal of this section is to study the Fourier transform of the primitive pairs.
We make the assumption that
(G,\chi)
is
n
-rigid.

Proposition 5.10
.

Assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
be a primitive pair associated to the parabolic
P
containing a Levi
L
with
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
and
\pi:\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{l}
be the projection map. Also assume
\mathcal{O}_{n},\mathcal{O}_{-n}
be the unique open
G_{0}
orbits in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
respectively and,
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}
be the unique open
L_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{l}_{-n}
. If,

\mathcal{O}_{n}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\neq\emptyset\text{ and }\mathcal{O}_{-n}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L})\neq\emptyset,

then
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

The above proposition has been proved in
[
Lu
, Prop. 12.2]
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
with
\mathcal{O}
being the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. Also assume
\phi_{\mathfrak{g}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{n}]=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})[\dim\mathfrak{g}_{-n}]
with
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
being the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
. Then
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is a primitive pair.

Proof.

By
[
Ch
, Th. 6.11]
,
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is normal. Therefore there exists a parabolic
P
with Levi
L
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
such that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
appears as a direct summand of
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{O}},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
be the associated primitive pair to
P,L
and
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})^{\cu}
. By the Corollary
4.2
, induction commutes with Fourier transform. Hence
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
appears as direct summand of
\ind_{-n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. We know from the definition of induction for cuspidal pair
[
Ch
, Lemma 5.2]
and our assumption that there exists
x\in\mathcal{O}
and
g\in G_{0}
such that,
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})x\in\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
. Also the assumption that
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
implies
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
appears as a direct summand of
\ind_{-n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
. But Fourier transform sends cuspidal pairs to cuspidal pairs by Theorem
4.3
. Hence there exists
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{L})\in\i^{\prime}(\mathfrak{l}_{-n})^{\cu}
, such that
\Phi_{\mathfrak{l}_{n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{L})
. Which implies
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}
is the unique open
L_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{l}_{-n}
. Hence there exists
y\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
g^{\prime}\in G_{0}
such that
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{\prime-1})y\in\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{-n}
. If we replace
x
by
\mathrm{Ad}(g^{-1})x
and
g
by
1
, then we see the new
x
belongs to both
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
. Therefore,

\mathcal{O}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{L})\neq\emptyset.

Similarly we can define a new
y
which belongs to both
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L}+\mathfrak{u}_{n}
and hence,

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\cap\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L})\neq\emptyset.

Therefore by Proposition
5.10
,
\mathcal{O}=\tilde{\mathcal{O}}
.
Now we can apply Theorem
5.9
(a), which says that
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
is direct sum of copies of
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
upto shift. Combining our assumption we get,
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\tilde{\mathcal{L}})
, which implies
\mathcal{L}=\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
.
∎

6.
Quasi-monomial

In this section we introduce good pairs, which can be thought as the more generalization of the normal complex, that has been introduced in
[
Lu
]
and
[
Ch
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

A pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is called
quasi monomial
if there exists a parabolic subgroup
P
with Levi
L
and the following condition holds,

(1)

(L,\chi)
is
n
-rigid.

(2)

There exists a primitive pair
(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is isomorphic to
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L})
upto shift. We will call
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
to be a quasi monomial object.

Remark 6.2
.

A quasi-monimial object will be called
proper quasi monomial
if
P
is a proper parabolic subgroup of
G
.

Definition 6.3
.

A pair
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
is called
good pair
if
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is direct sum of quasi monomial objects. It will be called
proper
if all the quasi monomial objects appearing in the direct sum are proper. We will call
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
to be a good object.

Theorem 6.4
.

Assume
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
. Then there exists good pairs
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
such that,
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\cong\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime})
.

Proof.

In this proof we will use induction on the reductive quotient of proper parabolic subgroups containing
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
. We will prove the above theorem in several steps.

(1)

First we want to prove that if
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
with either
(G,\chi)
is not
n
-rigid or
\mathcal{O}
is not the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
, then the statement of the theorem is true. We will proceed with induction on the dimension of
\mathcal{O}
. Let
x\in\mathcal{O}
. We can construct
P,L,U,\mathfrak{p,n,l}
as in
[
Ch
, 6.2]
. Then
P\neq G
. By
[
Ch
, 6.3]
,
x
is contained in the open
L_{0}
-orbit
\mathcal{O}_{L}\subset\mathcal{O}
in
\mathfrak{l}_{n}
and
\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}
is a irreducible local system, we called it
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
. Now by
[
Ch
, Th 6.5]

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
has support
\bar{\mathcal{O}}
with,

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})|_{\mathcal{O}}=\mathcal{L}[\dim\mathcal{O}_{L}].

Therefore combining the fact that
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
is a parity complex
[
Ch
, Th 6.12]
, we get,

(6.8)

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime},

where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a parity complex supported on
\bar{\mathcal{O}}-\mathcal{O}
. Now by the induction, there exists good pairs
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{L})
and
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{L})
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{l}_{n})
such that,

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\oplus\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{L}).

Hence we get,

(6.9)

\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{L},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime})=\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{L}).

Combining (
6.8
) and (
6.9
) we get,

(6.10)

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime})=\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{L}).

Now the support of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
has dimension less that the dimension of
\mathcal{O}
. So by induction of the statement of (1), we know that there exists good objects
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}
such that,

(6.11)

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}=\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}.

combining (
6.10
) and (
6.11
) we get,

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime\prime}\oplus\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime})=\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{L})\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}.

As induction is transitive
[
Ch
, Th 4.2]
, so
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime})
and
\inn^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime}_{L},\mathcal{E}_{L}^{\prime\prime})
are again good objects. Hence (1) is true.

(2)

Now we want to prove that if
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
with either
(G,\chi)
is not
(-n)
-rigid or
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is not the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit in
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
then the statement of the theorem is true. The proof will be similar to (1).

(3)

Now our claim is that if
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{-n})
is a proper pair then there exists good pairs
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
such that,

\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}).

As
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is proper, so we can assume
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{-n}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M})
where
Q
is a proper parabolic subgroup of
G
containing
\chi(\mathbb{C}^{\times})
and
(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{m}_{-n})
is a primitive pair with
M
being the Levi inside
Q
. Now as induction commutes with the Fourier transform we have,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})=^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\Phi_{\mathfrak{m}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M}).

Now by induction there exists good objects
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M}
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{m}_{n})
such that,

\Phi_{\mathfrak{m}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}_{M},\mathcal{E}_{M})\oplus\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M}.

Therefore

\Phi_{\mathfrak{g}_{-n}}\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{M}=^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{q}}\mathcal{I}_{n}\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{M}

Now the above statement follows by the transitivity of induction.

(4)

Now we claim if
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\in\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
with
\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and the support of
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is distinct from
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
, then the statement of the theorem is true. By (2), there exist proper pairs
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime})
in
\mathscr{I}(\mathfrak{g}_{n})
such that,

\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}).

Therefore we have,

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\oplus\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})=\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}).

We can apply (3) to the proper pairs
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})
and
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime})
which proves our claim.

(5)

Now we are ready to prove the theorem. By (1) if
(G,\chi)
is not
n
-rigid or
\mathcal{O}
is not the unique open
G_{0}
-orbit then the statement of the theorem is true. Hence we assume
(G,\chi)
is
n
-rigid. If the condition of (4) holds then also the statement is true. Therefore we assume
\bar{\mathcal{O}}=\mathfrak{g}_{n}
and the support of
\phign\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
. Then by Proposition
5.11
,
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
is primitive, in particular good.

∎

Remark 6.5
.

We can prove the same statement as in Theorem
6.4
for
\mathfrak{g}_{-n}
and proof will follow the same steps.

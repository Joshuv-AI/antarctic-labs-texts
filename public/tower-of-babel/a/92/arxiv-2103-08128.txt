Tangent-Chebyshev rational maps and Rédei functions Thanks: The second author thanks the National Science Foundation for support under grant DMS-1601844.
Zhiguo Ding, Michael E. Zieve

Abstract
. Recently Lima and Campello de Souza introduced a new class of rational functions over odd-order finite fields, and explained their potential usefulness in cryptography. We show that these new functions are conjugate to the classical family of Rédei rational functions, so that the properties of the new functions follow from properties of Rédei functions. We also prove new properties of these functions, and introduce analogous functions in characteristic 2 2 , while also introducing a new version of trigonometry over finite fields of even order, which is of independent interest.

Introduction

Recently Lima and Campello de Souza

They showed that these functions have properties suitable for use in cryptography. In this note we prove a relationship between these tangent-Chebyshev maps and a classical family of rational functions introduced by Rédei

2.
Rédei functions

We begin with a quick development of the theory of Rédei functions. Let
q
be a prime power, and let
h(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be an irreducible polynomial of the form

\begin{array}[]{c@{\mathrel{}{}}l@{}}x^{2}-\alpha&\qquad\text{ if $q$ is odd}\\[3.0pt]
x^{2}+x+\alpha&\qquad\text{ if $q$ is even}.\end{array}

It is known that for each
q
there are exactly
\lfloor q/2\rfloor
such
irreducible polynomials
h(x)
.
Let
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be a root of
h(x)
, so that
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}:=\beta^{q}
is the other root of
h(x)
, namely

\begin{array}[]{c@{\mathrel{}{}}l@{}}\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}=-\beta&\qquad\text{ if $q$ is odd}\\[3.0pt]
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}=\beta+1&\qquad\text{ if $q$ is even}.\end{array}

Definition 2.1
.

For any positive integer
n
, the degree-
n

Rédei function
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameter
\alpha
is

R_{n}(x,\alpha):=\rho^{-1}\circ x^{n}\circ\rho

where
\rho(x):=(x-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})/(x-\beta)
and
\rho^{-1}(x):=(\beta x-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})/(x-1)
are degree-one rational functions in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}(x)
such that
\rho^{-1}\circ\rho=x
.

Explicitly,

R_{n}(x,\alpha)=\frac{\beta(x-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})^{n}-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}(x-\beta)^{n}}{(x-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})^{n}-(x-\beta)^{n}}.

Thus
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
is unchanged if we interchange
\beta
and
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}
, so that
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
does not depend on the choice of
\beta
. Moreover, since the
q
-th power map interchanges
\beta
and
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}
, it fixes each coefficient of
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
, so that
R_{n}(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
.

We compute the coefficients of
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
via the binomial theorem. For odd
q
this yields

R_{n}(x,\alpha)=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2i}\alpha^{i}x^{n-2i}}{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2i+1}\alpha^{i}x^{n-2i-1}},

while for even
q
the function
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
equals

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Bigl((\beta+1)\beta^{i}+\beta(\beta+1)^{i}\Bigr)\binom{n}{i}x^{n-i}}{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Bigl(\beta^{i}+(\beta+1)^{i}\Bigr)\binom{n}{i}x^{n-i}}.

The function
\rho(x)
induces a bijection from
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\mathbb{F}_{q}\cup\{\infty\}
to the set
\mu_{q+1}
of
(q+1)
-th roots of unity in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

[
19
]
. Thus all properties of the function induced by
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
correspond to properties of the function induced by
X^{n}
on
\mu_{q+1}
. This may be formally expressed in terms of functional graphs:

Definition 2.2
.

For any set
S
and any function
f\colon S\to S
, the
functional graph
of
f
is the directed graph with vertices labelled by
S
, and with an edge from
s
to
t
if and only if
f(s)=t
.

Proposition 2.3
.

The functional graph of
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is isomorphic to the functional graph of
x^{n}
on
\mu_{q+1}
.

Corollary 2.4
.

The function
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
.

Proof.

We see that
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
x^{n}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
, or equivalently
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The functions
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
and
R_{m}(x,\alpha)
commute under composition.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
x^{n}
and
x^{m}
commute under composition.
∎

We now prove the following identity which seems to be new:

Proposition 2.6
.

For any
m,n>0
, the function
R_{m+n}(x,\alpha)
equals

\frac{R_{m}(x,\alpha)R_{n}(x,\alpha)+\alpha}{R_{m}(x,\alpha)+R_{n}(x,\alpha)-(\beta+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})}.

Remark
.

In the above expression, we note that
\beta+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}
is
0
for odd
q
and
1
for even
q
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\rho\,\circ\,

\displaystyle R_{m+n}(x,\alpha)=\rho(x)^{m+n}

\displaystyle=\rho(x)^{m}\cdot\rho(x)^{n}

\displaystyle=\rho(R_{m}(x,\alpha))\cdot\rho(R_{n}(x,\alpha))

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{m}(x,\alpha)-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}}{R_{m}(x,\alpha)-\beta}\cdot\frac{R_{n}(x,\alpha)-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}}{R_{n}(x,\alpha)-\beta},

and the result follows by composing on the left with
\rho^{-1}(x)
.
∎

Remark
.

For odd
q
, the case
m=n
of Proposition
2.6
is essentially the case
y=x
of the identity of bivariate rational functions in
[
3
, §4]
, after one observes that
R_{2n}(x,\alpha)=R_{n}(x,\alpha)\circ R_{2}(x,\alpha)
. It would be interesting to explore bivariate versions of other special cases of Proposition
2.6
.

Remark
.

One can show that if
n
is not a power of the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
then distinct values
\alpha
yield distinct rational functions
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
. Thus, for any such
q
and
n
, the number of degree-
n
Rédei functions over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the number of possibilities for
\alpha
, namely
\lfloor q/2\rfloor
.

The arguments in this section immediately extend to the case that
h(x)
is an arbitrary irreducible degree-
2
polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. We required
h(x)
to be
x^{2}-\alpha
or
x^{2}+x+\alpha
since these are the polynomials occurring in the original papers on the topic
[
17
,
18
]
, and also these yield simpler expressions for the coefficients of
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
.

3.
Tangent-Chebyshev functions

The tangent-Chebyshev rational functions
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
were defined in
[
9
]
for any odd prime power
q
via a somewhat involved procedure. This led to the explicit expression
[
9
, (9)]
, which we use as our definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, let
q
be an odd prime power, and let
\alpha
be a nonsquare in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the degree-
n
tangent-Chebyshev rational function over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with parameter
\alpha
is

C_{n}(x,\alpha):=\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2i+1}\alpha^{i}x^{2i+1}}{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2i}\alpha^{i}x^{2i}}.

Comparing this definition to the explicit expression for
R_{n}(x,\alpha)
yields the following new and fundamental equality:

(3.2)

C_{n}(x,\alpha)=\frac{1}{x}\circ R_{n}(x,\alpha)\circ\frac{1}{x}.

Conversely, it is natural to take (
3.2
) to be the definition of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
in case
q
is an even prime power and
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
equals
\beta^{2}+\beta
with
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
By the results of the previous section, in this case
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
equals

\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Bigl(\beta^{i}+(\beta+1)^{i}\Bigr)\binom{n}{i}x^{i}}{\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Bigl((\beta+1)\beta^{i}+\beta(\beta+1)^{i}\Bigr)\binom{n}{i}x^{i}}.

In the rest of this section,
q
is any prime power (odd or even), and
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}
are as at the start of the previous section.
We now present consequences of (
3.2
).

Corollary 3.3
.

We have

C_{n}(x,\alpha)=\eta^{-1}\circ x^{n}\circ\eta

where
\eta(x):=\rho(1/x)=(\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}x-1)/(\beta x-1)
and
\eta^{-1}(x)=1/\rho^{-1}(x)=(x-1)/(\beta x-\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})
. Here
\eta^{-1}\circ\eta=x
, and
\eta
maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
bijectively onto
\mu_{q+1}
.

Corollary 3.4
.

The rational functions
C_{m}(x,\alpha)
and
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
commute under composition.

Proof.

This follows from the previous corollary since
x^{m}
and
x^{n}
commute.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

The function
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
.

Proof.

Since
\eta
maps
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
bijectively onto
\mu_{q+1}
, we see that
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
x^{n}
permutes
\mu_{q+1}
, or equivalently
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For odd
q
, the function
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
\gcd(n,q+1)=1
.

Proof.

If
n
is even then
\gcd(n,q+1)
is even and

C_{n}(x,\alpha)
does not permute
\mathbb{F}_{q}
since

C_{n}(0,\alpha)=\infty
.
Now suppose
n
is odd. Then
C_{n}(x,\alpha)

fixes
\infty
, and hence permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if it permutes
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, which says
\gcd(n,q+1)
by the previous result.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

The functional graph of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is isomorphic to the functional graph of
x^{n}
on
\mu_{q+1}
.

Corollary 3.8
.

The function
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
induces an involution on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
n^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{q+1}
.

Proof.

The function
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
induces an involution on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if
x^{n}
induces an involution on
\mu_{q+1}
, or equivalently
x^{n}\circ x^{n}=x^{n^{2}}
induces the identity map on
\mu_{q+1}
. This says
x^{n^{2}-1}
maps each element of
\mu_{q+1}
to
1
, which says
(q+1)\mid(n^{2}-1)
since
\mu_{q+1}
is a cyclic group of order
q+1
.
∎

The above corollaries include generalizations of the main results from
[
9
]
. Specifically,
[
9
, Prop. 7]
is the odd
q
case of Corollary
3.4
;
[
9
, Prop. 11]
is the odd
q
case of Corollary
3.8
;
[
9
, Prop. 10]
is Corollary
3.6
, which follows from the odd
q
case of Corollary
3.5
;
and
[
9
, Prop. 5 and 6]
describe the zeroes and poles of
C_{n}(x)
for odd
q
, which is immediate for all
q
from Corollary
3.3
.
Corollaries
3.3
and
3.7
are new, and explain much about the functions
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
.

Next, Proposition
2.6
yields the following new identity, which seems useful for calculating values of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
and for analyzing cryptosystems built from those values:

Proposition 3.9
.

For any
m,n>0
, we have

C_{m+n}(x,\alpha)=H(C_{m}(x,\alpha),\,C_{n}(x,\alpha))

where

H(x,y):=\frac{x+y-(\beta+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$})xy}{1+\alpha xy}.

Remark
.

If
q
is odd then
H(x,y)=(x+y)/(1+\alpha xy)
, which intriguingly coincides with Einstein’s velocity-addition formula in special relativity when
\alpha=-1/c^{2}
with
c
being the speed of light in a vacuum
[
4
, §5]
.

4.
Trigonometry in characteristic
2

Analogues of
\cos x
and
\sin x
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
were introduced for odd
q
in
[
5
]
, where they were used to define a Hartley number-theoretic transform. Analogues of the tangent and inverse tangent function for odd
q
were introduced in
[
9
]
and used in the definition of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
for odd
q
.
An analogue of
\cos x
was introduced in
[
8
]
for even
q
.
It appears that the literature does not contain an analogue of
\sin x
or
\tan x
over finite fields of even order. We introduce such an analogue now, by combining our definition of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
for even
q
from the previous section with the analogy with classical trigonometric functions that motivated the definition of
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
in
[
9
]
.

For the rest of this section,
q
is an even prime power, and we fix some
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and some
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\alpha:=\beta^{2}+\beta
is in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Write
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}:=\beta^{q}
, so that as noted previously we have
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}=\beta+1
. For any integer
k
we define

\displaystyle\sin_{\zeta}(k)

\displaystyle:=\zeta^{k}+\zeta^{-k}

\displaystyle\cos_{\zeta,\beta}(k)

\displaystyle:=\beta\zeta^{k}+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}\zeta^{-k}

\displaystyle\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(k)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\sin_{\zeta}(k)}{\cos_{\zeta,\beta}(k)}.

We note that what we call
\sin_{\zeta}(k)
is called
\cos_{\zeta}(k)
in
[
8
]
; but we will show that the properties of this function resemble those of
\sin k
rather than
\cos k
.

The crucial property of
\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(x)
is as follows:

Proposition 4.1
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

C_{n}(\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(k),\alpha)=\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(nk).

This result is the analogue for even
q
of the equation used in
[
9
]
for odd
q
to define
C_{n}(x,\alpha)
.

Proof.

Writing
x:=\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(k)
and
u:=\zeta^{k}
, we have

x=\frac{u+u^{-1}}{\beta u+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}u^{-1}}=\eta^{-1}(u^{2})

and likewise

\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(nk)=\eta^{-1}(u^{2n}).

Thus

\displaystyle\eta(C_{n}(x,\alpha))

\displaystyle=\eta(x)^{n}

\displaystyle=u^{2n}

\displaystyle=\eta(\tan_{\zeta,\beta}(nk)),

as desired.
∎

Our characteristic
2
trigonometric functions satisfy the following easily verified properties for any integers
k,\ell
; here we suppress the subscripts for ease of typography:

\displaystyle\zeta^{k}

\displaystyle=\cos(k)+\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}\sin(k)

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\cos(k)^{2}+\sin(k)\cos(k)+\alpha\sin(k)^{2}

\displaystyle\sin(k+\ell)

\displaystyle=\sin(k)\cos(\ell)+\sin(\ell)\cos(k)+\sin(k)\sin(\ell)

\displaystyle\cos(k+\ell)

\displaystyle=\cos(k)\cos(\ell)+\alpha\sin(k)\sin(\ell)

\displaystyle\tan(k+\ell)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tan(k)+\tan(\ell)+\tan(k)\tan(\ell)}{1+\alpha\tan(k)\tan(\ell)}.

The analogy with trigonometric functions on
\mathbb{R}
can be seen by viewing
\zeta
as analogous to
e^{i}
and
\beta\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\overline{\phantom{\rm\beta}}$}
as analogous to
i
, with
\alpha
analogous to
-1
; then the formulas for
\zeta^{k}
and
\cos(k+\ell)
become precise analogues of the familiar formulas.

Remark
.

To complete the development of trigonometry over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we mention a new identity for odd
q
, using the notation from
[
9
]
:

\tan_{\zeta}(x+y)=\frac{\tan_{\zeta}(x)+\tan_{\zeta}(y)}{1+i^{2}\tan_{\zeta}(x)\tan_{\zeta}(y)}.

5.
Concluding Remarks

In this note we showed that the tangent-Chebyshev rational functions introduced in
[
9
]
are conjugate to Rédei functions, which explains the properties of these functions discovered in
[
9
]
, while also providing new properties.
Moreover, this enables one to deduce cryptographic information about tangent-Chebyshev maps from the known cryptographic results about Rédei functions.
We also introduced a characteristic
2
analogue of both tangent-Chebyshev rational functions and finite field trigonometry.
It seems plausible that one could similarly define tangent-Chebyshev rational maps on
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
by using the analogues of Rédei functions on
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
introduced in
[
15
,
17
]
, but we have not explored that possibility.

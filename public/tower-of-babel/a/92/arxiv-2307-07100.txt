Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behavior of the N N -dimensional colored Jones polynomial of the figure-eight knot evaluated at exp ⁡ ( ( κ + 2 ​ p ​ π ​ − 1 / N ) CLOSE \exp\bigl((\kappa+2p\pi\sqrt{-1}/N\bigr) , where κ := arccosh ⁡ ( 3 / 2 ) \kappa:=\operatorname{arccosh}(3/2) and p p is a positive integer. 1.
Introduction

For a knot
K
in the three-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
and a positive integer
N
, let
J_{N}(K;q)
be the
N
-dimensional colored Jones polynomial associated with the
N
-dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2;\mathbb{C})
, where we normalize it as
J_{N}(U;q)=1
for the unknot
U
, and when
N=2
, it satisfies the following skein relation:

qJ_{2}\left(\raisebox{0.5pt}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{positive.eps}}};q\right)-q^{-1}J_{2}\left(\raisebox{0.5pt}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{negative.eps}}};q\right)=(q^{1/2}-q^{-1/2})J_{2}\left(\raisebox{0.5pt}{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\includegraphics[scale]{null.eps}}};q\right).

If we replace
q
with
e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}
, we obtain a complex number
J_{N}(K;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})
, which is known as the Kashaev invariant
[
15
,
27
]
.
The volume conjecture
[
16
,
27
]
states that the series
\{J_{N}(K;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})\}_{N=1,2,3,\dots}
would grow exponentially with growth rate proportional to the simplicial volume
\operatorname{Vol}(K)
of
S^{3}\setminus{K}
.
Here the simplicial volume is also known as the Gromov norm
[
8
]
.
It coincides with the hyperbolic volume divided by the volume
v_{3}
of the ideal regular hyperbolic tetrahedron if the knot is hyperbolic, that is, its complement
S^{3}\setminus{K}
processes a complete hyperbolic structure with finite volume.
If the knot is not hyperbolic, then the simplicial volume times
v_{3}
is the sum of the hyperbolic volumes of the hyperbolic pieces of
S^{3}\setminus{K}
after the Jaco–Shalen–Johannson decomposition
[
13
,
14
]
.

Conjecture 1.1

(Volume conjecture)
.

For any knot
K
in
S^{3}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|J_{N}(K;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})\right|}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}(K)}{2\pi}.

The volume conjecture has been generalized in various ways.
It can be complexified as follows
[
28
]
.
Let
\mathscr{H}\subset S^{3}
be a hyperbolic knot, and
\operatorname{cv}(\mathscr{H}):=\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Vol}(\mathscr{H})-2\pi^{2}\operatorname{CS}^{\rm{SO(3)}}(\mathscr{H})
be the complex volume of
S^{3}\setminus\mathscr{H}
, where
\operatorname{CS}^{\rm{SO(3)}}(\mathscr{H})\pmod{\pi^{2}}
is the Chern–Simons invariant of the Levi–Civita connection of
S^{3}\setminus\mathscr{H}
associated with the complete hyperbolic structure.

Conjecture 1.2

(Complexification of the volume conjecture)
.

For any hyperbolic knot
\mathscr{H}
in
S^{3}
, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}(\mathscr{H};e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{cv}(\mathscr{H})}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}.

The volume conjecture and its complexification can be refined as follows
[
9
]
(see also
[
10
,
6
,
32
]
):

Conjecture 1.3

(Refined volume conjecture)
.

Let
\mathscr{H}\subset S^{3}
be a hyperbolic knot.
Then the following asymptotic equivalence holds.

J_{N}(\mathscr{H};e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N})\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\left(\frac{T(\mathscr{H})}{2\sqrt{-1}}\right)^{1/2}N^{3/2}\exp\left(\frac{\operatorname{cv}(\mathscr{H})}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}N\right),

where
F(N)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}G(N)
means
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{F(N)}{G(N)}=1
, and
T(\mathscr{H})
is the adjoint
(
cohomological
)
Reidemeister torsion twisted by the holonomy representation
\rho_{0}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus{\mathscr{H}})\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
.

The refined volume conjecture has been proved for the figure-eight knot
[
1
]
, and hyperbolic knots with as most seven crossings
[
32
,
34
,
33
]
.

We can also generalize the refined volume conjecture by replacing
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
in
e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}
with a complex number.

Let
\rho_{u}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\mathscr{H})\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
be an irreducible representation, which is a small deformation of the holonomy representation
\rho_{0}
.
Then it defines an incomplete hyperbolic structure of
S^{3}\setminus\mathscr{H}
.
Up to conjugation, we can assume that
\rho_{u}
sends the meridian (preferred longitude, respectively) of
\mathscr{H}
to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{u/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-u/2}\end{pmatrix}
(
\begin{pmatrix}e^{v(u)/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-v(u)/2}\end{pmatrix}
, respectively).
Then we can define the cohomological adjoint Reidemeister torsion
T_{u}(\mathscr{H})

[
35
]
, and the Chern–Simons invariant
\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u})

[
18
]
.

The following conjecture was proposed in
[
25
]
(see also
[
10
,
5
]
).

Conjecture 1.4

(Generalized volume conjecture)
.

For a hyperbolic knot
\mathscr{H}
, there exists a neighbourhood
U\in\mathbb{C}
of
0
such that if
u\in U\setminus\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathbb{Q}
, then the following asymptotic equivalence holds.

J_{N}(\mathscr{H};e^{(u+2\pi\sqrt{-1})/N})\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{-\pi}}{2\sinh(u/2)}T_{u}(\mathscr{H})^{1/2}\left(\frac{N}{u+2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{S_{u}(\mathscr{H})}{u+2\pi\sqrt{-1}}N\right),

where
S_{u}(\mathscr{H}):=\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u})+u\pi\sqrt{-1}+uv(u)/4
.

The generalized volume has been proved just for the figure-eight knot
[
30
]
.
The asymptotic equivalence in Conjecture
1.4
was also proved in the case where
0<u<\kappa:=\operatorname{arccosh}(3/2)
in
[
25
]
.

In the previous paper
[
26
]
, the author proved the following theorem generalizing the result in
[
25
]
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be the figure-eight knot.
For a real number
u
with
0<u<\kappa
and a positive integer
p
, we have

\begin{split}&J_{N}(\mathscr{E};e^{(u+2p\pi\sqrt{-1})/N})\\
\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}&J_{p}(\mathscr{E};e^{4N\pi^{2}/(u+2p\pi\sqrt{-1})})\\
&\times\frac{\sqrt{-\pi}}{2\sinh(u/2)}T_{u}(\mathscr{E})^{1/2}\left(\frac{N}{u+2p\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{S_{u}(\mathscr{E})}{u+2p\pi\sqrt{-1}}N\right).\end{split}

Note that in the case of the figure-eight knot, we have

\displaystyle T_{u}(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{(2\cosh{u}+1)(2\cosh{u}-3)}},

\displaystyle S_{u}(\mathscr{E})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u-\varphi(u)}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-u+\varphi(u)}\right)+u\bigl(\varphi(u)+2\pi\sqrt{-1}\bigr),

where we put

\varphi(u):=\log\left(\cosh{u}-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(2\cosh{u}+1)(2\cosh{u}-3)}\right)

and
\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z):=-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-x)}{x}\,dx
is the dilogarithm function.

So it is impossible to extend Theorem
1.5
to the case where
u=\kappa
because
T_{u}(\mathscr{E})
is not defined.
Topologically/geometrically speaking, the corresponding hyperbolic structure of the figure-eight knot complement collapses at
u=\kappa
.

On the other hand, for the figure-eight knot, we have the following theorems.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
23
]
)
.

If
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies the inequality
|\cosh\zeta-1|<1/2
and
|\operatorname{Im}\zeta|<\pi/3
, then we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}J_{N}(\mathscr{E};e^{\zeta/N})=\frac{1}{\Delta(\mathscr{E};e^{\zeta})},

where
\Delta(K;t)
is the Alexander polynomial of a knot
K
.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
12
]
)
.

If
\zeta=\kappa
, then the colored Jones polynomial
J_{N}(\mathscr{E};e^{\kappa/N})
grows polynomially.
More precisely, we have

J_{N}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{\kappa/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\Gamma(1/3)}{3^{2/3}}\left(\frac{N}{\kappa}\right)^{2/3},

where
\Gamma(x)
is the gamma function.

In this paper, we will extend Theorem
1.5
to the case
u=\kappa
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\mathscr{E}
be the figure-eight knot, and
\xi:=\kappa+2p\pi\sqrt{-1}
with
\kappa:=\operatorname{arccosh}(3/2)
and
p
a positive integer.
Then we have the following asymptotic equivalence.

J_{N}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{\xi/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}J_{p}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{4\pi^{2}N/\xi}\right)\frac{\Gamma(1/3)e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/6}}{3^{1/6}}\left(\frac{N}{\xi}\right)^{2/3}\exp\left(\frac{S_{\kappa}(\mathscr{E})}{\xi}N\right),

where
S_{\kappa}(\mathscr{E}):=2\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}
, and we put
\xi^{1/3}:=|\xi|^{1/3}e^{\arctan(2p\pi/\kappa)\sqrt{-1}/3}
.

As a corollary, we obtain a similar result for
J_{N}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{\xi^{\prime}/N}\right)
with
\xi^{\prime}:=-\kappa+2p\pi\sqrt{-1}
.

Corollary 1.9
.

We have

J_{N}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{\xi^{\prime}/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}J_{p}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{4\pi^{2}N/\xi^{\prime}}\right)\frac{\Gamma(1/3)e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/6}}{3^{1/6}}\left(\frac{N}{\xi^{\prime}}\right)^{2/3}\exp\left(\frac{S_{-\kappa}(\mathscr{E})}{\xi^{\prime}}N\right),

where we put
S_{-\kappa}(\mathscr{E}):=-2\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}
.

See §
6
for a topological interpretation of
S_{u}(\mathscr{E})
for
|u|\leq\kappa
.
It is defined to be
\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u})+u\pi\sqrt{-1}+uv(u)/4
, where
\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u})
is the Chern–Simons invariant of a non-Abelian representation
\rho_{u}\colon\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus\mathscr{E})\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 1.10
.

Since the highest degree term of the Laurent polynomial
J_{p}(\mathscr{E};q)
is
q^{p(p-1)}
, we have
J_{p}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{4\pi^{2}N/\xi}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}e^{4p(p-1)\pi^{2}N/\xi}
.
So we also have

J_{N}\left(\mathscr{E};e^{\xi/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\Gamma(1/3)e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/6}}{3^{1/6}}\left(\frac{N}{\xi}\right)^{2/3}\exp\left(\frac{4p^{2}\pi^{2}}{\xi}N\right)

because
2\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}/\xi+4p(p-1)\pi^{2}/\xi=4p^{2}\pi^{2}/\xi+2\pi\sqrt{-1}
.
A similar result holds for
\xi^{\prime}
.

There are two difficulties to prove Theorem
1.8
.

The first one is that when we apply the saddle point method to the integral that approximates
J_{N}(\mathscr{E};e^{\xi/N})
, the saddle point is of order two, that is, it looks like the saddle point of
\operatorname{Re}{z^{3}}
.
See Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
Contour plots of the functions
\operatorname{Re}{z^{3}}
(left) and
\operatorname{Re}{z^{2}}
(right) around their saddle points.
The saddle point
O
of
\operatorname{Re}{z^{3}}
is of order two, and that of
\operatorname{Re}{z^{2}}
is of order one.

To approximate the colored Jones polynomial by an integral as above, we use a quantum dilogarithm function, which converges to a function described by the dilogarithm function.
However, the second difficulty is that our saddle point is on the boundary of the region of convergence.
So we need to extend the domain of definition of the quantum dilogarithm slightly by using a functional identity.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we define the quantum dilogarithm and extend it as we require.
In Section
3
, we express the colored Jones polynomial as a sum of the terms described by the quantum dilogarithm.
To approximate the sum by an integral, we use the Poisson summation formula in Section
4
.
Then, in Section
5
we use the saddle point method to obtain the asymptotic formula, proving Theorem
1.8
.
Appendices
A
and
B
are devoted to proofs of the Poisson summation formula and the saddle point method, respectively.
In Appendix
C
, we give some computer calculations about the asymptotic behavior of
J_{N}(\mathscr{S};e^{(\pm\tilde{\kappa}+2\pi\sqrt{-1})/N})
for the stevedore knot
\mathscr{S}
, where
\tilde{\kappa}:=\log{2}
.
Since we know that
e^{\pm\kappa}
(
e^{\pm\tilde{\kappa}}
, respectively) are zeros of the Alexander polynomial of the figure-eight knot (the stevedore knot, respectively), we try to generalize Theorem
1.8
to another knot in vain.

2.
Quantum dilogarithm

In this section, we fix a complex number
\gamma
with
\operatorname{Re}{\gamma}>0
and
\operatorname{Im}{\gamma}<0
.
We will introduce a quantum dilogarithm
[
7
]
.
See also
[
16
,
1
,
32
]
.

We put

(2.1)

T_{N}(z):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x/N)}\,dx

for an integer
N>|\gamma|/\pi
, where
\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}:=(-\infty,-1]\cup\{w\in\mathbb{C}\mid|w|=1,\operatorname{Im}{w}\geq 0\}\cup[1,\infty)
with orientation from
-\infty
to
\infty
.
Note that
\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}
avoids the poles of the integrand.
We can prove that the integral above converges if
-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1+\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The integral in the right-hand side of (
2.1
) converges if
-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1+\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}
.

Proof.

First note that

\sinh(as)\underset{s\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{e^{as}}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\sinh(as)\underset{s\to-\infty}{\sim}\frac{-e^{-as}}{2},

for a complex number
a
with
\operatorname{Re}{a}>0
.
So we have

\displaystyle\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x/N)}

\displaystyle\underset{x\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{4}{x}\exp\bigl((2z-2-\gamma/N)x\bigr),

\displaystyle\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x/N)}

\displaystyle\underset{x\to-\infty}{\sim}-\frac{4}{x}\exp\bigl((2z+\gamma/N)x\bigr)

since
\operatorname{Re}{\gamma}>0
.

Therefore if
-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1+\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}
the integral converges.
∎

Thus
T_{N}(z)
is a holomorphic function in the region
\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1+\frac{\operatorname{Re}\gamma}{2N}\right\}
.

We will study properties of
T_{N}(z)
.

First of all we introduce three related functions:

Definition 2.2
.

For a complex number
z
with
0<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1
, we put

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{\mathrel{\mathop{\mathbb{R}}\limits^{\vbox to0.0pt{\kern-1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\frown$}\vss}}}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\,dx.

In a similar way to the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we can prove that the three integral above converge if
0<\operatorname{Re}{z}<1
.
The functions above can be expressed in terms of well-known functions.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
29
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

We have the following formulas.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$},\\
\pi\sqrt{-1}(2z-1)+\log\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$},\end{cases}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$},\\
\pi^{2}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$}.\end{cases}

Here the branch cuts of
\log
and
\operatorname{Li}_{2}
are
(-\infty,0]
and
[1,\infty)
, respectively.

The proof is similar to that of
[
29
, Lemma 2.5]
, and so we omit it.

The function
\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)
can be extended to the whole complex plane
\mathbb{C}
except for integers.
The functions
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
can be extended to holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigl((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)\bigr)
as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

For a complex number
z
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\bigl((-\infty,0]\cup[1,\infty)\bigr)
, we put

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$},\\
\pi\sqrt{-1}(2z-1)+\log\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$},\end{cases}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$},\\
\pi^{2}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\quad\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.5
.

When
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0
, the functions
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
can also be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)=

\displaystyle\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)+2\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor\pi\sqrt{-1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)=

\displaystyle\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)-2\pi^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor\left(\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor-2z+1\right),

where
\lfloor x\rfloor
is the greatest integer that does not exceed
x
.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
, we have

\displaystyle\log\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left[\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z+\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z+\pi\sqrt{-1}+2\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor\pi\sqrt{-1}.

The last equality follows because

•

if
0<\operatorname{Re}{z}-\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor<1/2
, then
-\pi<\arg\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)<0
,

•

if
1/2\leq\operatorname{Re}{z}-\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor<1
, then
0\leq\arg\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)<\pi
,

and so the imaginary part of the rightmost side is between
-\pi
and
\pi
.
Thus we obtain
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)=\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)+2\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor\pi\sqrt{-1}
from (
2.4
).

For
L_{2}(z)
, from the well known formula

(2.7)

\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w^{-1})=-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w)-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\log(-w)\bigr)^{2},

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z})-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(2\pi\sqrt{-1}z-(2\pi\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor+\pi)\sqrt{-1})\bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z})+\pi^{2}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)+2\pi^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor^{2}-4\pi^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor z+2\pi^{2}\lfloor\operatorname{Re}{z}\rfloor

and the result follows.
∎

As a corollary, we have

Corollary 2.6
.

If
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0

\mathcal{L}_{1}(z+1)-\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)=2\pi\sqrt{-1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z+1)-\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)=4\pi^{2}z
.

Lemma 2.7
.

The derivatives of

(

(2.8)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\,z}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(z),

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\,z}\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)=\frac{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}}.

Proof.

The first equality follows from the well-known equality
\frac{d}{d\,w}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w)=-\frac{\log(1-w)}{w}
.
The second one also follows easily.
∎

The first one is as follows:

Lemma 2.8
.

If

(2.10)

T_{N}(z)-T_{N}(z+1)=\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}z+\frac{1}{2}\right).

Remark 2.9
.

Note that since

Therefore from (

\begin{split}&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)\\
=&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}+1\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
=&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}+1\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}z\right)\\
=&\mathcal{L}_{1}(z+1)-\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}z+1\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}z\right),\end{split}

where the last equality follows from Lemma
2.11
since
\operatorname{Re}{z}<0
.
If
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
, then
\operatorname{Im}(z/\gamma)>0
, and so (

) turns out to be
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
.
If
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0

We consider the case where

.
Note that
-1<\operatorname{Re}\bigl(z\pm\frac{\gamma}{2N}\bigr)<0

Therefore from (

\begin{split}&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)\\

&+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\

where we use (
2.11
) because

\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

), we have

\displaystyle T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

where we use (
2.12
) for

at the second identity, noting that
1\not\in\Omega

\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0

If

and

from Corollary
2.6
, which implies (
2.11
).

Lastly, we consider the case where

.
Since
1\leq\operatorname{Re}\bigl(z\pm\frac{\gamma}{2N}\bigr)<2

(2.20)

\begin{split}&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}\right)\\
=&T_{N}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)-T_{N}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)\\
&-\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z-\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}\left(z+\frac{\gamma}{2N}-1\right)+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
=&\mathcal{L}_{1}(z-1)-\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}(z-1)\right)+\mathcal{L}_{1}\left(\frac{N}{\gamma}(z-1)+1\right),\end{split}

where we use (
2.11
) at the last equality.
If
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
, then we have
\operatorname{Im}\bigl((z-1)/\gamma\bigr)=\frac{1}{|\gamma|^{2}}\bigl(\operatorname{Im}\gamma(1-\operatorname{Re}{z})+\operatorname{Re}\gamma\operatorname{Im}{z}\bigr)>0
since
\operatorname{Re}{z}>1

So (

\log\big(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\bigr)=\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

If
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0

\operatorname{Im}\bigl((z-1)/\gamma\bigr)<0
.
Therefore (
2.20
) becomes

\log\bigl(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\bigr)+2\pi\sqrt{-1}=\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

from Lemma
2.5
.

The proof is complete.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
m
be a positive integer.
The
m
-th derivative of
\csc^{2}(\pi z)
can be expressed as

\frac{d^{m}\,\csc^{2}(\pi z)}{d\,z^{m}}=2(-\pi)^{m}\csc^{m+2}(\pi z)P_{m}(z),

where
P_{m}(z)
is of the form

P_{m}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}a_{m,j}\cos(j\pi z)

with

(i)

a_{m,j}>0
for
0\leq j\leq m
and
j\equiv m\pmod{2}
,

(ii)

\sum_{0\leq j\leq m,j\equiv m\pmod{2}}a_{m,j}=\frac{(m+1)!}{2}
, and

(iii)

a_{m,m}=1
.

Proof.

First of all, recall that
\csc^{\prime}(x)=-\cos(x)\csc^{2}(x)
.

We proceed by induction on
m
.

For
m=1
, since
\frac{d}{d\,z}\csc^{2}(\pi z)=2\csc(\pi z)\times\bigl(-\pi\cos(\pi z)\csc^{2}(\pi z)\bigr)=-2\pi\csc^{3}(\pi z)\cos(\pi z)
, we have
P_{1}(z)=\cos(\pi z)
, which agrees with (i), (ii), and (iii).

Suppose that the lemma is true for
m
.
We calculate the
(m+1)
-st derivative by using the inductive hypothesis for
P_{m}(z)
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{m+1}\,\csc^{2}(\pi z)}{d\,z^{m+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-\pi)^{m}\ \frac{d}{d\,z}\left(\csc^{m+2}(\pi z)P_{m}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-\pi)^{m}\left((m+2)\csc^{m+1}(\pi z)\bigl(-\pi\cos(\pi z)\csc^{2}(\pi z)\bigr)P_{m}(z)+\csc^{m+2}(\pi z)P^{\prime}_{m}(z)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-\pi)^{m}\csc^{m+3}(\pi z)\bigl[-(m+2)\pi\cos(\pi z)P_{m}(z)+\sin(\pi z)P^{\prime}_{m}(z)\bigr]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-\pi)^{m}\csc^{m+3}(\pi z)\left[-(m+2)\pi\cos(\pi z)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}a_{m,j}\cos(j\pi z)\right.

\displaystyle-\left.\sin(\pi z)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}j\pi a_{m,j}\sin(j\pi z)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-\pi)^{m+1}\csc^{m+3}(\pi z)\left[(m+2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}a_{m,j}\cos(\pi z)\cos(j\pi z)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}ja_{m,j}\sin(\pi z)\sin(j\pi z)\right].

Now, we will calculate the terms inside the square brackets.
We write
x:=\pi z
.
From the product-sum identities, we have

\displaystyle\sin(x)\sin(jx)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cos\bigl((j-1)x\bigr)-\frac{1}{2}\cos\bigl((j+1)x\bigr),

\displaystyle\cos(x)\cos(jx)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cos\bigl((j-1)x\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\cos\bigl((j+1)x\bigr).

So we have

(2.24)

\begin{split}&(m+2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}a_{m,j}\cos(x)\cos(jx)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}ja_{m,j}\sin(x)\sin(jx)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}(m+2)a_{m,j}\left(\cos\bigl((j-1)x\bigr)+\cos\bigl((j+1)x\bigr)\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}ja_{m,j}\left(\cos\bigl((j-1)x\bigr)-\cos\bigl((j+1)x\bigr)\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\bigl((m+j+2)a_{m,j}\cos\bigl((j-1)x\bigr)+(m-j+2)a_{m,j}\cos\bigl((j+1)x\bigr)\bigr)\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-1\leq k\leq m-1\\
k\equiv m+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}(m+k+3)a_{m,k+1}\cos(kx)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq m+1\\
k\equiv m+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}(m-k+3)a_{m,k-1}\cos(kx)\\
=&\begin{cases}&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq m-1\\
k\equiv m+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\left((m+k+3)a_{m,k+1}+(m-k+3)a_{m,k-1}\right)\cos(kx)\\
&\quad+a_{m,m}\cos\bigl((m+1)x\bigr)\hfill\text{(if $m$ is odd)},\\[14.22636pt]
&\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq k\leq m-1\\
k\equiv m+1\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\left((m+k+3)a_{m,k+1}+(m-k+3)a_{m,k-1}\right)\cos(kx)\\
&\quad+a_{m,m}\cos\bigl((m+1)x\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}(m+2)a_{m,0}\cos(x)\hfill\text{(if $m$ is even)}.\end{cases}\end{split}

Therefore we obtain the following recursive formula for
a_{m,k}
:

2a_{m+1,k}=\begin{cases}(m+k+3)a_{m,k+1}+(m-k+3)a_{m,k-1}&\text{if $k\neq 1$},\\
(m+k+3)a_{m,2}+2(m-k+3)a_{m,0}&\text{if $k=1$}.\end{cases}

Note that this also holds for
k=0
and
k=m+1
by putting
a_{m,-1}=a_{m,m+2}=0
.
Then, (i) follows since
m-k+3\geq 3
, (iii) follows since
a_{m+1,m+1}=1
, and (ii) follows since the sum of the coefficients in the third expression of (

) equals

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\left((m+j+2)a_{m,j}+(m-j+2)a_{m,j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m+2)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j\leq m\\
j\equiv m\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}a_{m,j}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We would like to show that the sum

\sum_{mN/p<k\leq(m+1)N/p}\exp\left(N\varphi_{m,N}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)\right)

is approximated by the integral

N\int_{m/p}^{(m+1)/p}e^{N\varphi_{m,N}(z)}\,dz.

To do that, we use the following proposition, known as the Poisson summation formula.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a
and
b
be real numbers with
a<b
, and
\{\psi_{N}(z)\}_{N=1,2,3,\dots}
be a series of holomorphic functions in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
containing the closed interval
[a,b]
.
We assume that
\psi_{N}(z)
uniformly converges to a holomorphic function
\psi(z)
in
D
.
We also assume that
\operatorname{Re}{\psi(a)}<0
and
\operatorname{Re}{\psi(b)}<0
.

Putting
R_{+}:=\{z\in D\mid\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0,\operatorname{Re}{\psi(z)}<2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}\}
and
R_{-}:=\{z\in D\mid\operatorname{Im}{z}\leq 0,\operatorname{Re}{\psi(z)}<-2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}\}
, we also assume that there are paths
C_{\pm}
connecting
a
and
b
such that
C_{\pm}\subset R_{\pm}
and that
C_{\pm}
is homotopic to
[a,b]
in
D
with
a
and
b
fixed.

Then we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a\leq k/N\leq b}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}=\int_{a}^{b}e^{N\psi_{N}(z)}\,dz+O(e^{-\varepsilon N})

for some
\varepsilon>0
independent of
N
.

A proof, which is essentially the same as that of
[
32
, Proposition 4.2]
, is given in Appendix
A
.

We would like to know the asymptotic behavior of the integrals appearing in the right hand sides of (

To describe it, let us consider a holomorphic function
\eta(z)

\eta(0)=\eta^{\prime}(0)=\eta^{\prime\prime}(0)=0

\eta^{(3)}(0)\neq 0

\eta(z)=re^{\theta\sqrt{-1}}z^{3}\times g(z)

is holomorphic with

\operatorname{Re}{\eta(z)}

We put

\hat{D}\subset D

\hat{D}

gives a bijection from
\hat{D}
to

from the inverse function theorem because

\rho_{u}\colon G_{\mathscr{E}}\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
sending
x
to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{u/2}&1\\
0&e^{-u/2}\end{pmatrix}
and
y
to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{u/2}&0\\
d&e^{-u/2}\end{pmatrix}
, where
d
is given as

d:=\frac{3}{2}-\cosh{u}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(2\cosh(u)+1)(2\cosh(u)-3)}.

The preferred longitude is sent to
\begin{pmatrix}e^{v(u)/2}&\ast\\
0&e^{-v(u)/2}\end{pmatrix}
,
where

\begin{split}v(u):=&2\log\left(\cosh(2u)-\cosh(u)-1-\sinh(u)\sqrt{(2\cosh(u)+1)(2\cosh(u)-3)}\right)\\
&+2\pi\sqrt{-1}.\end{split}

Here we add
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
so that
v(0)=0
.

It is well known
[
40
]
that when
u=0
, the irreducible representation
\rho_{0}
induces a complete hyperbolic structure in
S^{3}\setminus{\mathscr{E}}
, and when
0<u<\kappa
,
\rho_{u}
is irreducible and induces an incomplete hyperbolic structure.
When
u=\kappa
, the representation
\rho_{\kappa}
becomes reducible (and non-Abelian), and the hyperbolic structure collapses.
In fact, in this case, both
x
and
y
are sent to upper triangular matrices, and so every element of
G_{\mathscr{E}})
is sent to an upper triangular matrix, which means that
\rho_{\kappa}
is reducible.
This kind of reducible and non-Abelian representation is called affine, and corresponds to the zeroes of the Alexander polynomial.
See
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
17
, Exercise 11.2]
,
[
19
, 2.4.3. Corollary]
.

Now, we calculate the
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
Chern–Simons invariant
\operatorname{CS}_{\kappa,v(\kappa)}(\rho_{\kappa})
associated with
(\kappa,v(\kappa))=(\kappa,2\pi\sqrt{-1})
.
See
[
18
]
for details.

Since the Chern–Simons invariant of a representation is determined by its character, and
\rho_{\kappa}
shares the same character (trace) with the Abelian representation
\rho^{\rm{Abel}}_{\kappa}
sending
\mu:=x
to the diagonal matrix
\begin{pmatrix}e^{\kappa/2}&0\\
0&e^{-\kappa/2}\end{pmatrix}
and
\lambda:=l
to the identity matrix, it can be easily seen that
\mathbf{cs}_{W}(\rho^{\rm{Abel}}_{\kappa})=\left[\frac{\kappa}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},0;1\right]
, where we put
W:=S^{3}\setminus{N(\mathscr{E})}
with
N(\mathscr{E})
is the open tubular neighborhood of
\mathscr{E}
in
S^{3}
.
Since we have

\left[\frac{\kappa}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},0;1\right]=\left[\frac{\kappa}{4\pi\sqrt{-1}},\frac{1}{2};e^{\kappa}\right]

from (
6.1
), we conclude that
\operatorname{CS}_{\kappa,2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(\rho_{\kappa})=\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}/2
.
Note that here we change the pair
(\kappa,0)
to
(\kappa,2\pi\sqrt{-1})
.

As in
[
25
]
, if we define

(6.2)

S_{u}(\mathscr{E}):=\operatorname{CS}_{u,v(u)}(\rho_{u})+\pi\sqrt{-1}u+\frac{1}{4}uv(u)

for
0\leq u\leq\kappa
, then we see that
S_{\kappa}(\mathscr{E})=2\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}
when
\bigl(u,v(u)\bigr)=(\kappa,2\pi\sqrt{-1})
.

Similarly, we see that
\operatorname{CS}_{-\kappa,2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(\rho_{-\kappa})=-\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}/2
, and that
S_{-u}(\mathscr{E})=-2\kappa\pi\sqrt{-1}
.

Appendix A
Proof of the Poisson summation formula

In this appendix, we give a proof of the Poisson summation formula following

.

Proof of Proposition

Let
\varepsilon>0

\operatorname{Re}{\psi(a)}<-\varepsilon

\operatorname{Re}{\psi(b)}<-\varepsilon

\operatorname{Re}{\psi(z)}-2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}<-\varepsilon

\operatorname{Re}{\psi(z)}+2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}<-\varepsilon
if
z\in C_{-}
.
Then for sufficiently large
N
, the following also hold:

(i)

\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(a)}<-\varepsilon
,

(ii)

\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(b)}<-\varepsilon
,

(iii)

\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(z)}-2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}<-\varepsilon
if
z\in C_{+}
,

(iv)

\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(z)}+2\pi\operatorname{Im}{z}<-\varepsilon
if
z\in C_{-}
.

Moreover, there exists
\delta>0
such that
\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(t)}<-\varepsilon
if
t\in[a,a+\delta]
or
t\in[b-\delta,b]
from (
i
) and (
ii
) for such
N
.

Let
\beta\colon\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be a
C^{\infty}
-function such that

\beta(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $t\in[a+\delta,b-\delta]$},\\
0&\text{if $t<a$ or $t>b$}.\end{cases}

We also assume that
\beta(t)
is in the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, that is,
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|x^{m}f^{(n)}(x)\right|<\infty
for any non-negative integers
m
and
n
.
Put
\Psi_{N}(x):=\beta(x/N)e^{N\psi_{N}(x/N)}
.

We have

(A.1)

\left|\sum_{a\leq k/N<a+\delta}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}\right|\leq\sum_{a\leq k/N<a+\delta}e^{N\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(k/N)}}<\delta Ne^{-\varepsilon N},

where the second inequality follows since
\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(k/N)}<-\varepsilon
when
a\leq k/N\leq a+\delta
.
Similarly we have

(A.2)

\left|\sum_{b-\delta\leq k/N<b}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}\right|<\delta Ne^{-\varepsilon N}.

We also have

(A.3)

\left|\sum_{k/N<a+\delta}\Psi_{N}(k)\right|\leq\sum_{a\leq k/N<a+\delta}\beta(k/N)e^{N\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(k/N)}}<\delta Ne^{-\varepsilon N}

and

(A.4)

\left|\sum_{k/N>b-\delta}\Psi_{N}(k)\right|<\delta Ne^{-\varepsilon N}.

Since
\Psi_{N}(k)=e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}
if
a+\delta\leq k/N\leq b-\delta
, we have

(A.5)

\begin{split}&\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi_{N}(k)-\sum_{a\leq k/N\leq b}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}\right|\\
\leq&\left|\sum_{k/N<a+\delta}\Psi_{N}(k)\right|+\left|\sum_{a\leq k/N<a+\delta}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)})\right|\\
&+\left|\sum_{b-\delta<k/N\leq b}\Psi_{N}(k)\right|+\left|\sum_{k/N>b-\delta}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)})\right|\\

from (

Since

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, we can apply the Poisson summation formula (see for example

(A.6)

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi_{N}(k)=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l),

where
\hat{\Psi}_{N}
is the Fourier transform of
\Psi_{N}
, that is,
\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l):=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Psi_{N}(t)e^{-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}t}\,dt
.

Putting
s:=t/N
, we have

(A.7)

\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)=N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\beta(s)e^{N\bigl(\psi_{N}(s)-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}s\bigr)}\,ds.

From the properties of
\beta(s)
, we have

(A.8)

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{N}\hat{\Psi}_{N}(0)-\int_{a}^{b}e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\,ds\right|\\
\leq&\left|\int_{a}^{a+\delta}\bigl(\beta(s)-1\bigr)e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\,ds\right|+\left|\int_{b-\delta}^{b}\bigl(\beta(s)-1\bigr)e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\,ds\right|\\
\leq&\int_{a}^{a+\delta}\bigl(1-\beta(s)\bigr)e^{N\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(s)}}\,ds+\int_{b-\delta}^{b}\bigl(1-\beta(s)\bigr)e^{N\operatorname{Re}{\psi_{N}(s)}}\,ds\\
<&2\delta e^{-\varepsilon N}.\end{split}

Therefore we have

(A.9)

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a\leq k/N\leq b}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}-\int_{a}^{b}e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\,ds\right|\\
\leq&\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{a\leq k/N\leq b}e^{N\psi_{N}(k/N)}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)\right|+\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)-\int_{a}^{b}e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\,ds\right|\\
&\text{(from \eqref{eq:F_Fhat})}\\

&+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z},l\neq 0}|\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)|\\
&\text{(from \eqref{eq:F_f} and \eqref{eq:Fhat_0})}\\
<&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z},l\neq 0}|\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)|+6\delta e^{-\varepsilon N}.\end{split}

Next we calculate

for

.
Integrating the right hand side of (

) by parts twice, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\Psi}_{N}(l)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4l^{2}\pi^{2}N}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d^{2}}{d\,s^{2}}\left(\beta(s)e^{N\psi_{N}(s)}\right)e^{-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}Ns}\,ds.

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}B_{N}(s)e^{N\bigl(\psi_{N}(s)-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}s\bigr)}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{a}^{a+\delta}B_{N}(s)e^{N\bigl(\psi_{N}(s)-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}s\bigr)}\,ds+\int_{b-\delta}^{b}B_{N}(s)e^{N\bigl(\psi_{N}(s)-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}s\bigr)}\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}\tilde{B}_{N}(s)e^{N\bigl(\psi_{N}(s)-2l\pi\sqrt{-1}s\bigr)}\,ds

\displaystyle I_{2}

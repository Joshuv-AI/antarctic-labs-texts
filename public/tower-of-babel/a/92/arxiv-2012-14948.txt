The homotopy type of the contactomorphism groups of tight contact 3 3 -manifolds, part I
Eduardo Fernández, Javier Martínez-Aguinaga, Francisco Presas

Abstract
. We compute the homotopy type of the space of embeddings of convex disks with Legendrian boundary into a tight contact 3 3 -manifold, whenever the sum of the absolute value of the rotation number of the boundary with the Thurston-Bennequin invariant is − 1 -1 , proving that it is homotopy equivalent to the space of smooth embeddings. Using the same ideas it is also determined the homotopy type of the space of embeddings of convex spheres into a tight 3 3 -fold in terms of the space of smooth spheres. As a consequence we determine the homotopy type of the space of long Legendrian unknots, satisfying the previous condition, into a tight 3 3 -fold and also of the space of long transverse unknots with self-linking number − 1 -1 , proving that these spaces are homotopy equivalent to the space of smooth long unknots. We also determine the homotopy type of the contactomorphism group of every universally tight handlebody, the standard 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 2 {\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2} and every Legendrian fibration over a compact orientable surface with non-empty boundary, partially solving a conjecture due to E. Giroux. Finally, we show that the space of embeddings of Legendrian ( n , n ) (n,n) -torus links with maximal Thurston-Bennequin invariant is homotopy equivalent to U ⁡ ( 2 ) × K ⁡ ( ℳ n , 1 ) \operatorname{U}(2)\times K(\mathcal{M}_{n},1) , where ℳ n \mathcal{M}_{n} is the mapping class group of the 2 2 -sphere with n n -holes.

1.2.
Legendrian embeddings.

We would like to discuss where we got the motivation to write this article. The study of the homotopy type of the space of Legendrian embeddings has become a central problem in Contact Topology. The most studied case was the classification of the connected components, the Legendrian knots. The tactic was to study the inclusion map rwith the connected components of the space of formal Legendrian knots. For instance, formal Legendrian knots are classified in
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
by three invariants: the smooth knot type of the embedding and two integer valued invariants: the rotation invariant
\operatorname{rot}
and the Thurston-Bennequin invariant
\operatorname{tb}

]

To show whether the map was injective was next in line. The first examples of non-injectivity were for Legendrian knots in

Let
(M,\xi)
be a contact

that is transverse to

contactomorphic to
(S\times{\mathbb{R}},\ker(fdt+\beta))
where

{\mathbb{R}}
–coordinate,

is the pullback, via the inclusion, of some contact form
\alpha
of

to

defines an embedded
1

\Gamma\subseteq S
that is called the dividing set. This construction depends on the choice of the contact vector field
X

is contractible the isotopy class of

is well–defined. We will write

\Sigma\setminus\Gamma_{X}=\Sigma_{+}\sqcup\Sigma_{-}

is the subsurface of

in which the orientation of the line field generated by

, determined by the orientation of

and the orientation of
M
; and the coorientation of
\xi
coincide; and
\Sigma_{-}
in which they are opposite.

(Giroux Approximation Theorem
[
Gir01b
,
Hon00a
]
)
.

Let
(M,\xi)
be any contact
3
–manifold. Let

•

If
\partial S=\emptyset

perturbation of

that makes it convex.

•

If
\partial S\neq\emptyset
there exists a pertubation, fixed at the boundary, which is
C^{0}
near the boundary and
C^{\infty}
in the interior, of
S
that makes it convex.

We will apply the previous Theorem to families of disks which share a small neighborhood of the boundary, so we will be assuming that they will be convex near the boundary. This allows us to assume that the perturbation is

\xi^{z,t}

z\in K

{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(n)

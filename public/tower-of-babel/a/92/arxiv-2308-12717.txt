Erfonium: A Hooke Atom with Soft Interaction Potential
Jacek Karwowski, Andreas Savin

Abstract
Properties of erfonium , a Hooke atom with the Coulomb interaction potential 1 / r 1/\/r replaced by a non-singular erf ⁡ ( μ ​ r ) / r \mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)/\/r potential are investigated. The structure of the Hooke atom potential and properties of its energy spectrum, relative to the ones of the spherical harmonic oscillator and of harmonium, are analyzed. It is shown, that at a certain value of μ \mu the system changes its behavior from a harmonium-like regime to a harmonic-oscillator-like regime.



I
Motivation

The interaction between electrons can be split to two parts:

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{r}+\frac{\mathrm{erfc}(\mu\,r)}{r}.

(1)

The first, long-range, term is smooth at
r\,\sim\,0
and behaves as the
Coulomb potential at
r\rightarrow\infty
. The second, short-range one,
correctly represents the Coulomb potential at small
r
and decays
exponentially as
r\,\rightarrow\,\infty
. From this observation stems
the concept of range separation
Gori-Giorgi and Savin 2006
;
Savin 2011
;
Savin 2020
,
where the long-range section and the short-range section are treated in
different ways.

In theories of many-electron systems, the replacement of the singular
Coulomb potential by a smooth long-range potential, signifficantly
reduces the computational effort. On the other hand, the short-range
behavior of the wave function is mostly defined by the universal
properties of the Coulomb singularity. Therefore the idea of improving
models which utilize the long-range interactions only, by some
universal corrections describing the short-range properties is very
tempting. Indeed, correcting solutions of the Schrödinger equation
with the long-range part of the interaction potential, by using the
generalized cusp conditions to represent the wave functions in the
small
r
area, was recently shown not only feasible, but also very
accurate
Savin and Karwowski 2023
.

The long-range Coulomb interaction potentials,

\mathsf{w}(r;\mu)=\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{r},

(2)

have been introduced in several areas of chemistry and physics. For example,
a model with the Coulomb interaction replaced by
\mathsf{w}(r;\mu)
correctly
reproduces some selected properties of the hydrogen atom, harmonium, and
electron gas
González-Espinoza et al. 2016
. In quantum chemistry, the replacement of
the singular Coulomb potential by a smooth one represented by the error
function is particularly convenient with the Gaussian basis sets: the
integrals with this potential are easy to calculate.
Therefore, the applications of the range separation concept, though most
common in the density functional theory
Savin 2011
;
Pernal and Hapka 2022
, extend
beyond this field
Savin 2020
.

Also in theory of complex systems, as crystals, liquids, or plasmas, using

\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)/r
potential is motivated by its mathematical properties.
Probably for the first time it was used by Ewald in 1921, well before the
formulation of quantum mechanics, to replace the Coulomb interaction in order
to secure the convergence of some series describing infinite systems of
particles
Ewald 1921
. A reader interested in these areas of applications
is referred to a recent paper
Demyanov and Levashov 2022
, where also references to the
earlier works are supplied. Due to its convenient mathematical form and
simple relation to the Coulomb potential,
\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)/r
potential is also used
in many other sections of physics, just to mention so distant fields as
interactions between quarks
Lucha et al. 1992
, or general theory of
relativity
Plamondon 2018
.

Two electrons with interaction represented by
\mathsf{v}(r)
, in an
external potential
\mathsf{W}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1})+\mathsf{W}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2})
, are described by the
Hamiltonian

\mathsf{H}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1},\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2})=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigtriangleup_{\,\mbox{\boldsymbol{$\textstyle{r}$}}_{1}}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\bigtriangleup_{\,\mbox{\boldsymbol{$\textstyle{r}$}}_{2}}+\mathsf{W}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1})+\mathsf{W}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2})+\mathsf{v}(r),

(3)

where
r=|\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}|\equiv\left|\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1}-\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2}\right|
. Introducing

\mbox{\boldsymbol{$R$}}=\left(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1}+\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2}\right)/2
we get

\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{1}=\mbox{\boldsymbol{$R$}}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}},\;\;\;\ \mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{2}=\mbox{\boldsymbol{$R$}}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\,\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}.

(4)

If
\mathsf{W}(\mbox{\boldsymbol{$r$}}_{i})\,\propto\,r_{i}^{2}
,
i=1,2
, then the Hamiltonian is
separable and the two-electron eigenvalue equation splits to two
spherically-symmetric equations
Kestner and Sinanoglu 1962
. The first one, in

R
, is independent of the interaction potential, and describes the
motion of the center of mass of two electrons in the parabolic confining potential.
The second equation, in
r
, describes the relative motion of two
interacting electrons in the confining potential
(\omega\,r/2)^{2}
,
where parameter
\omega
defines the strength of the confinement.
We assume that the interaction potential is equal to
\mathsf{w}(r;\mu)
,
defined in (
2
). The resulting model potential reads

\mathsf{V}(r)\,\equiv\,\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)\,=\,\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{r}+\left(\frac{\omega\,r}{2}\right)^{2}.

(5)

The elimination of the angular dependence
from the second equation gives an infinite set of radial equations labelled
by the angular momentum quantum number
\ell

\mathsf{H}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)\,\phi_{n,\ell}(r)=E_{n,\ell}(\omega,\mu)\,\phi_{n,\ell}(r),\;\;\;n,\ell=0,1,2,\ldots,

(6)

where
\phi_{n,\ell}(r)=r\,\psi_{n,\ell}(r)
is the reduced radial function,

\psi_{n,\ell}(r)
is the radial part of the one-particle wave function, and

\mathsf{H}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)=\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}+\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)\right)

(7)

is the radial Hamiltonian.

If the interaction potential
\mathsf{v}(r)
is repulsive then its energy
spectrum is continuous. The introduction of a parabolic confinement leads
to the discretization of the spectrum. Two confined electrons form a bound
system which resembles a two-electron atom. It is referred to as a Hooke atom
(due to the confining Hooke force). If the two particles interact by the Coulomb
potential, the system is called
harmonium
. We propose here the name

erfonium
for a Hooke atom with the Coulomb interatcion
between electrons replaced by
\mathsf{w}(r;\mu)
. Erfonium is a generalization and
unification of two well-studied systems: the spherical harmonic oscillator and
harmonium. Its radial Hamiltonian (
7
) transforms to the Hamiltonian of
the spherical harmonic oscillator if
\mu=0
, and to the Hamiltonian of harmonium,
if
\mu\rightarrow\infty
(
10
). Accordingly, at these two limits the
eigenvalues
E_{n,\ell}(\omega,\mu)
, and other quantities characterizing the
system, approach the corresponding quantities of the harmonic oscillator and
of harmonium. They are marked hereafter by superscripts
o
and
h
,
respectively.

The close relation between the Coulomb and
\mathsf{w}(r;\mu)
potentials can
be seen by comparig their integral representations. We have

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(x\,r)^{2}}\,dx\;\;\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\;\;\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{r}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\mu}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(x\,r)^{2}}\,dx.

(8)

Then,

\frac{1}{r}\,=\,\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\,\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{r}

(9)

and at the limit of large
\mu
the model potential (
5
) transforms to the
potential of harmonium:

\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)\,\mathrel{\raisebox{-6.0pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\displaystyle{\rightarrow}}}{{\scriptstyle{\mu\to\infty}}}$}}\,\mathsf{V}^{\mathrm{h}}(r;\omega)=\frac{1}{r}+\left(\frac{\omega\,r}{2}\right)^{2}.

(10)

The
\ell
-dependent term in equation (
7
) describes the
centrifugal force, and together with the model potential is called the
effective radial potential:

\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)\,=\,\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}+\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu).

(11)

Notice, that the parameter
\mu
in

\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)
describes the adiabatic connection
between spherical harmonic oscillator

\left[\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,0)\right]
, and harmonium

\left[\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\infty)\right]
.

For
\ell=0
,
\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)
is equal to the model
potential (
5
):

\mathcal{V}(r;0,\omega,\mu)\,=\,\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu).

(12)

But, for
l>0
these two potentials are notably different. The most
significant difference is in the area of small
r
. At
r=0
,

\lim_{r\rightarrow\,0}\mathcal{V}(r;0,\omega,\mu)\,=\,\lim_{r\rightarrow\,0}\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)=\frac{2\,\mu}{\sqrt{\pi}},

(13)

while

\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)\mathrel{\raisebox{-6.0pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\displaystyle{\sim}}}{{\scriptstyle{r\to 0}}}$}}\frac{\ell\,(\ell+1)}{r^{2}}

(14)

i.e. it is singular if
\ell\,\neq\,0
.

This paper is aimed at the exploration of some general properties of
erfonium. General characteristics of the model potential (
5
) are presented
in the next section. The effective radial potential (
11
) is discussed
in section 3. The energy spectrum of erfomium is analyzed in section 4.
Final remarks complete the work. All equations and numerical values are here
expressed in the Hartree atomic units.

II
The model potential

The radial potential of erfonium (
5
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu\,\left[\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{(\mu\,r)}+\frac{2\,q}{3\,\sqrt{\pi}}(\mu\,r)^{2}\right]

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\mu}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left[1-\frac{(1-q)}{3\cdot 1!}(\mu\,r)^{2}+\frac{(\mu\,r)^{4}}{5\cdot 2!}-\frac{(\mu\,r)^{6}}{7\cdot 3!}+\cdots\right]

(16)

where

q=\frac{3\omega^{2}\sqrt{\pi}}{8\,\mu^{3}}.

(17)

Potential
\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)
has interesting scaling properties.
According to equation (
15
)

\mathsf{V}(r/\mu;\omega,\mu)=\mu\,\left(\frac{\mathrm{erf}(r)}{r}+\frac{2\,q}{3\,\sqrt{\pi}}\,r^{2}\right).

(18)

As we can see, all potentials with the same value of
q
have
exactly the same shape - they only differ by units in which
\mathsf{V}
and
r

are expressed. Therefore, for an arbitrary pair
\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\right)

\frac{\mathsf{V}(r/\mu_{1};\omega_{\,1},\mu_{1})}{\mathsf{V}(r/\mu_{2};\omega_{\,2},\mu_{2})}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}

(19)

if

\omega_{\,2}=\omega_{\,1}\,\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}}\right)^{3/2},

(20)

i.e. if the cofinement parameters
\omega_{\,1}
and
\omega_{\,2}

are related to
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
as

\left(\frac{\omega_{\,1}}{\omega_{\,2}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\right)^{3}.

(21)

For example, the potential energy curve corresponding to (
\mu=1
,

\omega=1
) is, except for scaling, the same as the curve for
(
\mu=10^{-2/3}\,\approx\,0.2154
,
\omega\,=\,0.1
) or for
(
\mu=100^{-2/3}\,\approx\,0.0464
,
\omega\,=\,0.01
).
From equation (
18
) we can get similar scaling relations for the
derivatives of
\mathsf{V}
with respect to
r
:

\frac{\mathsf{V}^{(n)}(r/\mu_{1};\omega_{\,1},\mu_{1})}{\mathsf{V}^{(n)}(r/\mu_{2};\omega_{\,2},\mu_{2})}=\left(\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\right)^{n+1},

(22)

where
\mathsf{V}^{(n)}
is the
n
-th derivative of
\mathsf{V}
with respect to

r
.

The first and the second derivatives of
\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)
are expressed
as

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}^{\prime}(r;\omega,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\,\left[-\frac{\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)}{(\mu\,r)^{2}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu\,r)^{2}}}{(\mu\,r)}+\frac{4\,q}{3\,\sqrt{\pi}}(\mu\,r)\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\mu^{2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left[-\frac{2\,(1-q)}{3\cdot 1!}(\mu\,r)+\frac{4}{5\cdot 2!}(\mu\,r)^{3}-\frac{6}{7\cdot 3!}(\mu\,r)^{5}+\cdots\right];

(24)

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}^{\prime\prime}(r;\omega,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{r}\,\frac{d\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)}{dr}-\mu^{3}\,\frac{4}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu\,r)^{2}}-q\right)

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\,\mu^{3}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\left[-\frac{2\,(1-q)}{3\cdot 1!}+\frac{3\cdot 4}{5\cdot 2!}(\mu\,r)^{2}-\frac{5\cdot 6}{7\cdot 3!}(\mu\,r)^{4}+\cdots\right].

(26)

For finite
\mu
,
\mathsf{V}^{\prime}(0;\omega,\mu)=0
, and the potential has an
extremum at
r=0
– a minimum if
q>1
and a maximum if
q<1
[cf.
equations (
24
) and (
26
)]. If
q\neq{1}
then for small
r
,
the potential is a quadratic function of
r
. If
q=1
then, at

r\,\sim\,0
, the dependence on
r
is quartic. The threshold value of

\mu
corresponding to
q=1
is equal to

\mu_{0}(\omega)=\frac{1}{2}\left(3\sqrt{\pi}\omega^{2}\right)^{1/3}.

(27)

Then,
\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)
at
r=0
has a minimum if
\mu<\mu_{0}(\omega)

and a maximum if
\mu>\mu_{0}(\omega)
. If
\mu\rightarrow\infty
then, for

r\,\rightarrow\,0
,
\mathsf{V}\,\sim\,1/r
.
Potential
\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)
grows up to infinity with increasing

r
. Therefore, a maximum at
r=0
implies the existence of a minimum at

r=r_{\mathrm{e}}(\omega,\mu)\equiv r_{\mathrm{e}}>0
. In the case of harmonium,

\displaystyle r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}\,\equiv\,r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}r_{\mathrm{e}}(\omega,\mu)=\left(\frac{2}{\omega^{2}}\right)^{1/3},

(28)

\displaystyle\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}\,\equiv\,\mathsf{V}_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{V}(r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}};\omega,\mu)\,=\,\frac{3}{4}\,\left[\omega\,r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)\right]^{2}\,=\,\frac{3}{4}\,\left(2\,\omega\right)^{2/3},

(29)

Taut 1993
;
Mandal et al. 2003
. Notice that

\mu_{0}(\omega)\,r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)=\left(\frac{3\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\right)^{1/3}\equiv\alpha

(30)

is independent of
\omega
.

For a finite
\mu
, the requirement that
\mathsf{V}^{\prime}(r_{\mathrm{e}};\omega,\mu)=0

yields:

{\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}\,}^{3}=\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r_{\mathrm{e}})-\frac{2\,\mu\,r_{\mathrm{e}}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu\,r_{\mathrm{e}})^{2}},

(31)

where

\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}\,\equiv\,\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}(\omega,\mu)=\frac{r_{\mathrm{e}}(\omega,\mu)}{r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)}=r_{\mathrm{e}}(\omega,\mu)\,\left(\frac{\omega^{2}}{2}\right)^{1/3}

is the normalized coordinate of the minimum.
Notice, that rhs of equation (
31
) is equal to
0
if
\mu=0
and
approaches
1
if
\mu\rightarrow\infty
. Its derivative with respect
to
x=\mu\,r_{\mathrm{e}}
is
(4\,x^{2}/\sqrt{\pi})\,\mathrm{e}^{-x^{2}}\,>\,0
. Therefore,
with increasing
\mu
,
r_{\mathrm{e}}
monotonically increases from
0
to
r_{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathrm{h}}
.
The dependence of
\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}
on
\mu
, obtained by numerical solving
equation (
31
), is presented, for several values of omega, in
the upper left panel of Fig. 1.

Using equation (
30
) we can rewrite equation (
31
) as

{\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}\,}^{3}\,=\,\mathrm{erf}(\widetilde{\mu}\,\widetilde{r_{\mathrm{e}}}\,\alpha)-\frac{2\,\widetilde{\mu}\,\widetilde{r_{\mathrm{e}}}\,\alpha}{\sqrt{\pi}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(\widetilde{\mu}\,\widetilde{r_{\mathrm{e}}}\,\alpha)^{2}},

(32)

where

\widetilde{\mu}\equiv\widetilde{\mu}(\omega)=\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}(\omega)}.

Equation (
32
) shows, that the relation between
\widetilde{r}_{\mathrm{e}}
and

\widetilde{\mu}
is independent of
\omega
. It is plotted in the upper right

III
The effective potential

The shape of
\mathcal{V}(r;\ell,\omega,\mu)
, similarly as the shape of

\mathsf{V}(r;\omega,\mu)
, is conserved under some specific transformations.
Equation (
11
) can be rewritten as

\mathcal{V}(r/\mu;\ell,\omega,\mu)=\mu\left[\frac{\Lambda}{r^{2}}+\frac{\mathrm{erf}(r)}{r}+\frac{\omega^{2}\,r^{2}}{4\,\mu^{3}}\right],

(37)

where
\Lambda=\ell\,(\ell+1)\,\mu
. Then,

\frac{\mathcal{V}(r/\mu_{1};\ell_{1},\omega_{\,1},\mu_{1})}{\mathcal{V}(r/\mu_{2};\ell_{2},\omega_{\,2},\mu_{2})}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}

(38)

if

\left(\frac{\omega_{\,1}}{\omega_{\,2}}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\right)^{3},\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda_{2},

(39)

[compare eq. (
21
)]. This means, that two effective potentials have the
same shape if,

\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}\,\frac{\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}+1)}{\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}+1)},\;\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\;\omega_{2}=\omega_{1}\,\left[\frac{\ell_{1}(\ell_{1}+1)}{\ell_{2}(\ell_{2}+1)}\right]^{3/2}

(40)

\frac{\partial\,E_{n,\ell}(\omega,\mu)}{\partial\,\mu}\,=\,\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\langle\phi_{n,\ell}\left|\mathrm{e}^{-(\mu\,r)^{2}}\right|\phi_{n,\ell}\rangle\,\longrightarrow\,\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}2/\sqrt{\pi},\;\;\mbox{if}\;\;\mu=0,\\

As it was already mentioned, using the smooth long-range interaction
potential instead of the singular Coulomb one, reduces the computational
effort in the approximate solving the Schrödinger equation for
many-electron systems
Gori-Giorgi and Savin 2006
;
Savin 2011
;
Savin 2020
. Therefore, one of the
objectives of studying properties of erfonium, is an exploration
of the possibility of an extrapolation of the energy derived from a model
with a finite
\mu
to the limit of electrons interacting by the Coulomb
force, i.e. the transition from the spectrum of a finite-
\mu
erfonium to
the spectrum of harmonium. The first step in such a procedure is the
approximation of the energies of harmonium by the expectation values of
the harmonium Hamiltonian calculated using the wave functions of erfonium

Savin and Karwowski 2023
. The resulting correction to the energy, equal to

\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{h}}\,\equiv\,\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{h}}(n,\ell;\omega,\mu)=\langle\,\phi_{n,\ell}(\mu)|\mathsf{H}^{\mathrm{h}}|\phi_{n,\ell}(\mu)\,\rangle-E_{n,\ell}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega),

(56)

where

\langle\,\phi_{n,\ell}(\mu)|\mathsf{H}^{\mathrm{h}}|\phi_{n,\ell}(\mu)\,\rangle\,\equiv\,\langle\,\phi_{n,\ell}(r;\omega,\mu)|\mathsf{H}(r;\ell,\omega,\infty)|\phi_{n,\ell}(r;\omega,\mu)\,\rangle,

is plotted in Fig. 8. As one can see by comparing with Fig. 7, the
difference between
\left|\Delta_{0}^{\mathrm{h}}\right|
and

\left|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{h}}\right|
depends on the value of
\omega
.
If
\omega
is large (left panels), then

\left|\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{h}}\right|\ll\left|\Delta_{0}^{\mathrm{h}}\right|
(for

\omega=0.5
by factor
\approx\,10
). But if
\omega
is small (right
panels), then both differences are of the same order of magnitude.

The difference between
E_{n,\ell}(\omega,\mu)
and

E_{n,\ell}^{\mathrm{h}}(\omega)
can be dramatically reduced if the generalized
cusp conditions are used to represent the wave function at small
r
.
But, if
\mu
is smaller than a certain threshold, a further reduction of
the difference using this method proved to be impossible
Savin and Karwowski 2023
.
From the perspective of the present analysis it is understandable that
the threshold value is close to
\mu_{0}(\omega)
, where the character of the
potential (and of the spectrum) changes from a harmonium-like to an
oscillator-like.

V
Final remarks

Erfonium, a Hooke atom with a soft interaction potential
represented by
\mathrm{erf}(\mu\,r)/r
, makes a bridge between harmonium
(
\mu\,\rightarrow\,\infty
) and the spherical harmonic oscillator (
\mu=0
).
The parameter
\mu
establishes an adiabatic connection between these
two limits. An interplay between the effects specific for harmonium and
for the harmonic oscillator creates a rich and interesting pattern which
can be observed in the structure of the potential and of the spectrum.
The parameter
\mu_{0}(\omega)
determined by the strength of the confinement
(
27
), marks a fuzzy boundary between these two ”spheres of influence”.
If
\mu>\mu_{0}(\omega)
then the regime of harmonium dominates, if

\mu<\mu_{0}(\omega)
, then the features of the harmonic oscillator prevail.

A vast field for further studies remains open. Particularly interesting (and
difficult) are issues related to the behavior of erfonium at the limit
of
\omega\,\rightarrow\,0
. Notice that at this limit the spectrum abruptly
changes from entirely discrete, to entirely continuous. If simultaneously

\mu\,\rightarrow\,0
, then at the limit we get two unconfined and
non-interacting ’electrons’.

Last, but not least, a broad class of semi-analytic (cf.

Taut 1993
;
Mandal et al. 2003
) and variational approaches may result in precise
analytic approximations of the wave functions and of the energies of erfonium.

Quantum Speedups for Approximating the John Ellipsoid
Xiaoyu Li, Zhao Song, Junwei Yu

Abstract
In 1948, Fritz John proposed a theorem stating that every convex body has a unique maximal volume inscribed ellipsoid, known as the John ellipsoid. The John ellipsoid has become fundamental in mathematics, with extensive applications in high-dimensional sampling, linear programming, and machine learning. Designing faster algorithms to compute the John ellipsoid is therefore an important and emerging problem. In [Cohen, Cousins, Lee, Yang COLT 2019], they established an algorithm for approximating the John ellipsoid for a symmetric convex polytope defined by a matrix A ∈ ℝ n × d A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d} with a time complexity of O ⁡ ( n ​ d 2 ) O(nd^{2}) . This was later improved to O ​ ( nnz ​ ( A ) + d ω ) O(\text{nnz}(A)+d^{\omega}) by [Song, Yang, Yang, Zhou 2022], where nnz ⁡ ( A ) \nnz(A) is the number of nonzero entries of A A and ω \omega is the matrix multiplication exponent. Currently ω ≈ 2.371 \omega\approx 2.371 [Alman, Duan, Williams, Xu, Xu, Zhou 2024]. In this work, we present the first quantum algorithm that computes the John ellipsoid utilizing recent advances in quantum algorithms for spectral approximation and leverage score approximation, running in O ⁡ ( n ​ d 1.5 + d ω ) O(\sqrt{n}d^{1.5}+d^{\omega}) time. In the tall matrix regime, our algorithm achieves quadratic speedup, resulting in a sublinear running time and significantly outperforming the current best classical algorithms.

Problem Formulation

In this section, we formally formulate the problem of the John Ellipsoid computation as a convex optimization problem. We first define several main concepts.

4.1
Background on Polytope and Ellipsoid

Definition 4.1

(Symmetric convex polytope)
.

We define the symmetric convex polytope as a convex set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
that takes the form of

\displaystyle P:=

\displaystyle~\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\langle a_{i},x\rangle|\leq 1,\forall i\in[n]\}.

where
a_{i}^{\top}
is the
i
-th row of a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
.

We assume that
A
is full rank. The symmetry of
P
implies that any maximum volume ellipsoid inside
P
must be centered at origin.

Definition 4.2

(Origin-centered ellipsoid)
.

An origin-centered ellipsoid is a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
takes the form of

\displaystyle E:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:x^{\top}G^{-2}x\leq 1\}

where
G\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is a positive semi-definite matrix.

4.2
Primal and Dual Formulation

By simple linear algebra, we know that
\vol(E)
is proportional to
\det G
. In
[
18
]
, it is shown that an ellipsoid
E
is a subset of a polytope
P
if and only if for every
i\in[n]
,
\|Ga_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
. Therefore, we formulate the John ellipsoid computation problem as the following optimization problem.

Definition 4.3

\displaystyle~d

The optimality condition of program (

\displaystyle a_{i}^{\top}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}a_{j}a_{j}^{\top})^{-1}a_{i}=1,\mathrm{if}~w_{i}\neq 0,

Instead of finding an exact solution, we are interested in approximate solutions to program (

Next, the following lemma states that an approximate solution is a good approximation of the exact John Ellipsoid. Recall that the exact John Ellipsoid

Lemma 4.7

(Approximate John Ellipsoid, variant of Lemma 2.3 in
[

]
)
.

Let

be a symmetric convex polytope. Let

be a
(1+\epsilon)
-approximate solution to program (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}\cdot E\subseteq P\subseteq\sqrt{(1+\epsilon)d}\cdot E.

\displaystyle\vol(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}E)\geq e^{-\epsilon d}\cdot\vol(E^{*}).

Proof.

First, we show that
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}\cdot E\subseteq P
. Let

. Then

. For every

\displaystyle(a_{i}^{\top}x)^{2}=

\displaystyle~((Ga_{i})^{\top}G^{-1}x)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~\|Ga_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\|G^{-1}x\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~(a_{i}^{T}G^{2}a_{i})(x^{\top}G^{-2}x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~(1+\epsilon)\cdot\frac{1}{1+\epsilon}=1

4.6

Next, we show that

.

\displaystyle x^{\top}(A^{\top}\diag(w)A)x=

\displaystyle~x^{\top}(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}^{\top}a_{i})x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}(a_{i}^{\top}x)^{2}

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}

where the first step uses Fact

\displaystyle~(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}-\log\det(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})-d)-\log\det((1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})^{-1}

(6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~((1+\epsilon)d-\log\det(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})-d)-\log\det((1+\epsilon)\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})^{-1}

\displaystyle~((1+\epsilon)d-\log\det(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})-d)-\log((1+\epsilon)^{-d}\det(\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}a_{i}a_{i}^{\top})^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2\epsilon d

where the first step uses Definition

for all

. Let

\displaystyle\log\det(G^{\prime})^{2}\geq

\displaystyle~\log\det(G^{*})^{2}-2\epsilon d

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\log(\det(G^{*})^{2}e^{-2\epsilon d})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\log(e^{-\epsilon d}\det(G^{*}))^{2}

where the first step follows from Eq. (

5.1

Given a matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, let

be a function defined as

\displaystyle f_{i}(w):=a_{i}^{\top}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}a_{j}a_{j}^{\top})^{-1}a_{i}=a_{i}^{\top}(A^{\top}\diag(w)A)^{-1}a_{i}.

Lemma 5.1

(Fixed point optimality of program (
2
), implicitly in Section 2 of
[

.

A vector

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=d,

\displaystyle w_{i}=w_{i}f_{i}(w),\forall i\in[n].

Suppose that

is an optimal solution to program (
2

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=d,

\displaystyle f_{i}(w)=1,\text{if }w_{i}\neq 0,

For any
i\in[n]

i\in[n]

w_{i}=w_{i}f_{i}(w)

Conversely, suppose that the following holds:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=d,

\displaystyle w_{i}=w_{i}f_{i}(w),\forall i\in[n].

For any
i\in[n]

follows from Lemma

Next, we have the following lemma which states that there is a quantum algorithm which can efficiently approximate the fixed point iteration, which is the key ingredient of our algorithm for computing the John ellipsoid.

Lemma 5.2

(Quantum fixed point iteration)
.

•

Since we are given query access to

6.1
Kronecker Product

We state the definition of Kronecker product and some basic properties of it.

Definition 6.1
.

For two matrices
A\in R^{n_{1}\times d_{1}},B\in R^{n_{2}\times d_{2}}
, we use
A\otimes B
to denote the Kronecker product of
A
and
B
where

\displaystyle(A\otimes B)_{i_{1}+(i_{2}-1)n_{1},j_{1}+(j_{2}-1)d_{1}}=A_{i_{1},j_{1}}B_{i_{2},j_{2}}.

Fact 6.2

(Properties of Kronecker product)
.

Let
A,B,C,D\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we have the following properties

•

A\otimes(B+C)=A\otimes B+A\otimes C

•

(B+C)\otimes A=B\otimes A+C\otimes A

•

(A\otimes B)\otimes C=A\otimes(B\otimes C)

•

(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=(AC)\otimes(BD)

•

(A\otimes B)^{\top}=A^{\top}\otimes B^{\top}

•

(A\otimes B)^{-1}=A^{-1}\otimes B^{-1}

•

\diag(w_{1}\otimes w_{2})=\diag(w_{1})\otimes\diag(w_{2})

6.2
Problem Formulation

Definition 6.3

(Symmetric convex polytope defined by Kronecker product)
.

We define the symmetric convex polytope as a convex set
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
that takes the form of

\displaystyle P:=

\displaystyle~\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}:|\langle a_{1,i_{1}}\otimes a_{2,i_{2}},x\rangle|\leq 1,\forall i,j\in[n]\}.

where
a_{1,i_{1}}^{\top}
and
a_{2,i_{2}}^{\top}
are
i_{1}
-th row of
A_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
i_{2}
-th row of
A_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
, respectively.

We assume
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are full rank.
We also define
\mathsf{A}:=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
. Then the
i_{1}+n(i_{2}-1)
-th row in
\mathsf{A}
, denoted by
\mathsf{a}_{i_{1}+(i_{2}-1)n}^{\top}
, is equal to
a_{1,i_{1}}^{\top}\otimes a_{2,i_{2}}^{\top}
. Note that
\rank(\mathsf{A})=\rank(A_{1})\cdot\rank(A_{2})
, so
\mathsf{A}
is also full rank. Similarly as before, we define the origin-centered ellipsoid as follows.

Definition 6.4

(Origin-centered ellipsoid)
.

An origin-centered ellipsoid is a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
takes the form of

\displaystyle E:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}:x^{\top}G^{-2}x\leq 1\}

where
G\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}
is a positive semidefinite matrix.

Definition 6.5

(Primal program, tensor version of Definition
4.3
)
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}
be defined in Definition
6.3
and let
\mathsf{A}=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
. Let
G
be defined in Definition
6.4
. The optimization problem of John Ellipsoid computation could be formulated as

\displaystyle\max_{G}

\displaystyle~\log\det G^{2},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle~\|G(a_{1,i_{1}}\otimes a_{2,i_{2}})\|_{2}\leq 1,\forall i_{1},i_{2}\in[n],

\displaystyle~G\succeq 0,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\max_{G}

\displaystyle~\log\det G^{2},

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle~\|G\mathsf{a}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1,\forall i\in[n^{2}],

\displaystyle~G\succeq 0.

Lemma 6.6

(Dual program, tensor version of Lemma
4.4
)
.

The dual program of program (
15
) is

\displaystyle\min_{\mathsf{w}}

\displaystyle~\sum_{i=1}^{n^{2}}\mathsf{w}_{i}-\log\det(\sum_{i=1}^{n^{2}}\mathsf{w}_{i}\mathsf{a}_{i}\mathsf{a}_{i}^{\top})-d^{2},

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle~\mathsf{w}_{i}\geq 0,\forall i\in[n^{2}].

We remark that our results can be generalized to
\mathsf{A}
is defined by the Kronecker product of
m
matrices, i.e.,
\mathsf{A}=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes A_{m}
. As long as
m\leq O(1)
, our algorithm will eventually achieve the same running time. For simplicity, we only present the case of
\mathsf{A}=A_{1}\otimes A_{2}
.

6.3
Optimality Conditions and Approximate Solution

Lemma 6.7

(Optimality condition of program (
16
), tensor version of Lemma
4.5
)
.

A vector

is an optimal solution to program (
16

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n^{2}}\mathsf{w}_{i}=d^{2},

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{i}^{\top}(\sum_{j=1}^{n^{2}}\mathsf{w}_{j}\mathsf{a}_{j}\mathsf{a}_{j}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{a}_{i}=1,\mathrm{if}~\mathsf{w}_{i}\neq 0,

\displaystyle\mathsf{a}_{i}^{\top}(\sum_{j=1}^{n^{2}}\mathsf{w}_{j}\mathsf{a}_{j}\mathsf{a}_{j}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{a}_{i}<1,\mathrm{if}~\mathsf{w}_{i}=0.

(1+\epsilon)
-approximate solution, tensor version of Definition
4.6
)

For any
\epsilon>0

(1+\epsilon)

Lemma 6.9

(Approximate John Ellipsoid, tensor version of Lemma

)
.

Let

\displaystyle E:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}}:x^{\top}(\mathsf{A}^{\top}\diag(\mathsf{w})\mathsf{A})x\leq 1\}.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}\cdot E\subseteq P\subseteq\sqrt{(1+\epsilon)}\cdot d\cdot E.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\vol(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon}}E)\geq e^{-\epsilon d^{2}}\cdot\vol(E^{*}).

7
Conclusion

In conclusion, we have developed the first quantum algorithm for approximating the John Ellipsoid of a symmetric convex polytope that achieves quadratic speedup and leads to a sublinear running time. We use quantum sketching techniques to speedup the classical state-of-the-art
O(\nnz(A)+d^{\omega})
time
[
83
]
and achieve
O(\sqrt{n}d^{1.5}+d^{\omega})
time. Quantum computing has emerged as a powerful tool for designing exceptionally fast optimization algorithms in high-dimensional settings. Future work could explore the extension of these techniques to other geometric optimization problems and further enhancing the efficiency and practicality of quantum algorithms.

Abstract
. Deformed preprojective algebras are generalizations of the usual preprojective algebras introduced by Crawley-Boevey and Holland, which have applications to Kleinian singularities, the Deligne-Simpson problem, integrable systems and noncommutative geometry. In this paper we offer three contributions to the study of such algebras: (1) the 2-Calabi-Yau property; (2) the unification of the reflection functors of Crawley-Boevey and Holland with reflection functors for the usual preprojective algebras; and (3) the classification of tilting ideals in 2-Calabi-Yau algebras, and especially in deformed preprojective algebras for extended Dynkin quivers.

Introduction

Deformed preprojective algebras were introduced in
[
12
]
in order to study noncommutative deformations of Kleinian singularities; they have been studied further in
[
10
]
, and used in
[
11
]
to solve an additive analogue of the Deligne-Simpson problem. They also have a role in integrable systems, see e.g.
[
4
]
, and noncommutative geometry, see e.g.
[
1
]
.
In this paper we further develop the theory of these algebras.

The definition is as follows. Let
K
be a field and let
Q
be a quiver with vertex set
Q_{0}
and arrow set
Q_{1}
. We assume always that
Q
is finite, and write
h(a)
and
t(a)
for the head and tail vertices of an arrow
a
.
The
double

\overline{Q}
of
Q
is obtained by adjoining a reverse arrow
a^{*}:j\to i
for each arrow
a:i\to j
in
Q
.
We use the notation
(a^{\ast})^{\ast}=a
, and for an arrow
a\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, we set
\varepsilon(a)=1
if
a\in Q_{1}
and
\varepsilon(a)=-1
otherwise.
Let
I=Q_{0}
and fix a
weight

\lambda\in K^{I}
. The corresponding
deformed preprojective algebra
is

\Pi^{\lambda}(Q):=K\overline{Q}/(\rho_{\lambda})\quad\text{where}\quad\rho=\sum_{a\in\overline{Q}_{1}}\varepsilon(a)aa^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{\lambda}=\rho-\sum_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}e_{i}.

Here
K\overline{Q}
denotes the path algebra of
\overline{Q}
. We use the convention that the path
ba
exists if
h(a)=t(b)
, and the trivial path at vertex
i
is denoted
e_{i}
. The usual (undeformed) preprojective algebra is
\Pi^{0}(Q)
.

Given a ring
R
, we usually consider left
R
-modules, and write
\Mod R
for the corresponding category.
Given an algebra
A
, we consider
A\otimes_{K}A
as an
A
-bimodule using the outer
A
-actions, so
b(a\otimes a^{\prime})c=ba\otimes a^{\prime}c
. If
M
is an
A
-bimodule, the space
\Hom_{AA}(M,A\otimes_{K}A)
of
A
-bimodule homomorphisms becomes an
A
-bimodule using the inner
A
-actions, that is, for
f\in\Hom(M,A\otimes_{K}A)
we have
(bfc)(m)=\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}c\otimes ba^{\prime}_{\lambda}
where
f(m)=\sum_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}\otimes a^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.
The algebra
A
is said to be
homologically smooth
if
A
has a finite projective resolution by finitely generated
A
-bimodules, so
A
is isomorphic to a perfect complex in the derived category of
A
-bimodules, and it is
said to be
d
-Calabi-Yau, for a natural number
d
, if it is homologically smooth and
\RHom_{AA}(A,A\otimes_{K}A)\cong A[-d]
in that same derived category
[
17
, Definition 3.2.3]
,
[
28
, Definition 7.2]
. Our first result may already be known to specialists, but we didn’t find a reference.
After releasing our work as a preprint, Travis Schedler explained to us a different proof, based on the methods of
[
20
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
Q
is a connected non-Dynkin quiver, then
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
is 2-Calabi-Yau.

This is proved in section
2.2
. If
Q
is a Dynkin quiver, then
\Pi^{0}(Q)
is a finite-dimensional self-injective algebra, so not in general 2-Calabi-Yau. If
Q
is connected and non-Dynkin, it is known that
\Pi^{0}(Q)
is 2-Calabi-Yau, see
[
6
,
9
,
7
,
16
]
.

The
dimension vector
of a finite-dimensional
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
-module
M
is
\Dim M\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
. We write
\varepsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
for the coordinate vector of a vertex
i
. The
Ringel form
of
Q
is the bilinear form

\left\langle-,-\right\rangle:\mathbb{Z}^{I}\times\mathbb{Z}^{I}\to\mathbb{Z}

given by

(1.1)

\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right\rangle:=\sum_{i\in I}\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}-\sum_{a\in Q_{1}}\alpha_{t(a)}\beta_{h(a)}.

We call
q(\alpha):=\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle
the
quadratic form of
Q
,
and have a symmetric bilinear form
(\alpha,\beta):=\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right\rangle+\left\langle\beta,\alpha\right\rangle
.

We say that a vertex
i
in
Q
is
loop-free
if there are no arrows with both head and tail at
i
, or equivalently
q(\varepsilon_{i})=1
. For such a vertex, the corresponding reflections
s_{i}\in\Aut(\mathbb{Z}^{I})
and
r_{i}\in\Aut(K^{I})
are defined by

s_{i}\alpha:=\alpha-(\alpha,\varepsilon_{i})\varepsilon_{i},\qquad(r_{i}\lambda)_{j}=\lambda_{j}-(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j})\lambda_{i}.

For
\lambda\in K^{I}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
, let
\lambda\cdot\alpha=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}
.
Then it is easy to see that
r_{i}(\lambda)\cdot\alpha=\lambda\cdot s_{i}(\alpha)
for
\lambda,\alpha\in K^{I}
.
The
Weyl group

W
is the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
generated by the simple reflections for loop-free vertices.
Then
W
acts on
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
and
K^{I}
with
(w\lambda)\cdot\alpha=\lambda\cdot(w^{-1}\alpha)
for
w\in W
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
and
\lambda\in K^{I}
.
According to
[
12
]
, deformed preprojective algebras come equipped with
reflection functors

E_{i}:\Mod\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)\to\Mod\Pi^{r_{i}(\lambda)}(Q)

which exist when
i
is a loop-free vertex and
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
. Moreover, if
M
is finite-dimensional, then

\Dim E_{i}(M)=s_{i}(\Dim M).

The condition
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
means that these reflection functors do not exist for
\Pi^{0}(Q)
. Instead there is a theory of tilting modules and functors, see
[
19
,
8
,
2
,
3
]
. Our next results unify these two situations. Using cokernels and kernels, for any loop-free vertex
i
we construct functors

C_{i}^{\lambda},K_{i}^{\lambda}:\Mod\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)\to\Mod\Pi^{r_{i}\lambda}(Q)

in section
3.1
. Since
r_{i}(r_{i}\lambda)=\lambda
, it follows that
C_{i}^{r_{i}\lambda}
and
K_{i}^{r_{i}\lambda}
are functors in the reverse direction. Note that if
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
, then also
(r_{i}\lambda)_{i}\neq 0
. If
\lambda_{i}=0
, then
r_{i}\lambda=\lambda
, so
C_{i}^{\lambda}
and
K_{i}^{\lambda}
are functors from
\Mod\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
to itself.

If
i
is a loop-free vertex and
\lambda_{i}=0
, there is a unique trivial simple module
S_{i}
for
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
with dimension vector the coordinate vector for
i
. We denote its annihilator by
I_{i}
. This is a 2-sided ideal in
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
with
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)/I_{i}\cong K
. Also
I_{i}=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)(1-e_{i})\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
, from which it follows that
I_{i}I_{i}=I_{i}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
i
is a loop-free vertex, then

(1)

C_{i}^{\lambda}
is left adjoint to
K_{i}^{r_{i}\lambda}
.

(2)

If
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
, then
C_{i}^{\lambda}\cong K_{i}^{\lambda}\cong E_{i}
, and
C_{i}^{\lambda}
is an equivalence of categories which satisfies
C_{i}^{r_{i}\lambda}C_{i}^{\lambda}\cong\id_{\Mod\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)}
.

(3)

If
\lambda_{i}=0
, then
C_{i}^{\lambda}\cong I_{i}\otimes_{\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)}(-)
and
K_{i}^{\lambda}\cong\Hom_{\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)}(I_{i},-)
.

This is proved in section
3.1
. In future we drop the superscript
\lambda
and just write
C_{i}
and
K_{i}
, leaving it to the reader to interpret appropriately. In section
3.2
we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
i
and
j
are loop-free vertices, then

(1)

C_{i}C_{j}\cong C_{j}C_{i}
and
K_{i}K_{j}\cong K_{j}K_{i}
if there is no arrow between
i
and
j
in
Q

(2)

C_{i}C_{j}C_{i}\cong C_{j}C_{i}C_{j}
and
K_{i}K_{j}K_{i}\cong K_{j}K_{i}K_{j}
if there is exactly one arrow between
i
and
j
in
Q
.

We remark that part (ii) of the theorem is known in the case where
\lambda=0
by
[
2
]
. (It can also can be shown by using relations on tilting ideals as in
[
8
]
.) On the other hand, our statement (ii) does not assume that
\lambda=0
.

Let
A
be a
K
-algebra. Given a left
A
-module
M
, one writes
\add M
for the full subcategory of
\Mod A
consisting of the modules isomorphic to a direct summand of a finite direct sum of copies of
M
, so
\add A
is the category of finitely generated projective left
A
-modules.
Let
n\geq 1
. Recall that an
A
-module
T
is an
n
-tilting
A
-module
if it satisfies the following three conditions.

(1)

There is an exact sequence
0\to P_{n}\to\dots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to T\to 0
with
P_{0},P_{1},\dots,P_{n}\in\add A
.

(2)

\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,T)=0
for all

.

(3)

There is an exact sequence
0\to A\to T_{0}\to T_{1}\to\dots\to T_{n}\to 0
with
T_{0},T_{1},\dots,T_{n}\in\add T
.

In this paper, by a
tilting module
we always mean a 1-tilting module.
Note that by (1), any tilting module is finitely generated. One says that
T
is

if
T
satisfies (1) (with

) and (2). One says that an ideal
I
of

is a

if

is a tilting module as both left and right

-modules.

Tilting ideals for

If

Suppose that

2.
The PBW and 2-Calabi-Yau properties

2.1.
Filtrations and the PBW property

Let
A
be a
K
-algebra.
A
filtration
of
A
is a family
\mathcal{F}=\{A_{\leq i}\}_{i\geq 0}
(
i\in\mathbb{Z}
) of
K
-subspaces
A_{\leq i}
of
A
satisfying
A=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}A_{\leq i}
,
A_{\leq i}\subseteq A_{\leq i+1}
and
A_{\leq i}A_{\leq j}\subseteq A_{\leq i+j}
for any
i,j\geq 0
.
If
A
admits a filtration, then we say that
A=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}A_{\leq i}
is a
filtered
K
-algebra
.
In this case, the associated graded
K
-algebra
\mathsf{gr}A
is defined as follows:

\gr_{\mathcal{F}}A=\gr A:=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{\leq i}/A_{\leq i-1},

where
A_{\leq-1}=0
.
The algebra
\gr A
is a (
\mathbb{N}
-) graded
K
-algebra such that the
i
-th component is
A_{\leq i}/A_{\leq i-1}
.

If
A=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}A_{i}
is a graded
K
-algebra then
A_{\leq i}=\bigoplus_{j\leq i}A_{j}
defines a filtration of
A
, and the associated graded algebra is isomorphic to
A
as graded
K
-algebras.
More precisely, if
x=\sum_{i=0}^{l}x_{i}\in A
with
x_{i}\in A_{i}
, then the isomorphism is described as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle A\to\gr A,\qquad x\mapsto(x_{i}+A_{\leq i-1})_{i}.

Let
A
be a filtered
K
-algebra and let
M
be an
A
-module.
A filtration of
M
is a family
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{\leq i}\}_{i\geq 0}
of
K
-subspaces
M_{\leq i}
of
M
satisfying
M=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}M_{\leq i}
,
M_{\leq i}\subset M_{\leq i+1}
and
A_{\leq i}M_{\leq j}\subset M_{\leq i+j}
.
For filtered
A
-modules
M=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}M_{\leq i}
and
N=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}N_{\leq i}
, a morphism of filtered modules of degree
j\in\mathbb{Z}
is a morphism of
A
-modules
f:M\to N
such that
f(M_{\leq i})\subset N_{\leq i+j}
holds for any
i\geq 0
.
In this case, the morphism
f
induces a morphism

\gr f:\gr M\to\gr N

of graded
(\gr A)
-modules of degree
j
.
We have two well-known and straightforward lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M,N
be filtered
A
-modules and
f:M\to N
be a morphism of filtered modules of degree
j\geq 0
. If
\gr f
is injective, then so is
f
.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
such that
f(x)=0
.
There exists
i\geq 0
such that
x\in M_{\leq i}
.
Because
f(x)=0
, we have
(\gr f)(\overline{x})=\overline{f(x)}=0
in
N_{\leq i+j}/N_{\leq i+j-1}
, where
\overline{x}\in M_{\leq i}/M_{\leq i-1}
.
Since
\gr f
is injective, we have
\overline{x}=0
, that is,
x\in M_{\leq i-1}
.
By using this argument inductively, we have
x\in M_{\leq-1}=0
.
∎

Let
A
and
B
be two filtered
K
-algebras.
For a filtered left
A
-module
M
and a filtered right
B
-module

, we have the following filtration of

:

(2.2)

\displaystyle(M\otimes_{K}N)_{\leq i}:=\sum_{i=j+k}M_{\leq j}\otimes_{K}N_{\leq k}.

In particular this induces a filtration on the

-algebra

, and then

is a filtered

-

-bimodule, by which we mean that it is a filtered

-module.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be two filtered
K
-algebras.
Let
M
be a filtered left
A
-module and
N
be a filtered right
B
-module.
Then
\gr(M\otimes_{K}N)
is isomorphic to
\gr M\otimes_{K}\gr N
as a
\gr A
-
\gr B
-bimodule.
If

and

, then this is an isomorphism of graded algebras.

Proof.

A morphism
\phi
from
\gr(M\otimes_{K}N)
to
\gr M\otimes_{K}\gr N
is given as follows: for
z=x\otimes y+(M\otimes_{K}N)_{\leq i-1}\in\gr(M\otimes_{K}N)_{i}
with
x\in M_{\leq j},y\in N_{\leq k}
for
j+k=i
, let
\phi(z)=(x+M_{\leq j-1})\otimes(y+N_{\leq k-1})\in(\gr M\otimes_{K}\gr N)_{i}
.
Then this assignment induces a morphism of

-

-bimodules.
By comparing

-bases of

,

and their images under

∎

The deformed preprojective algebra

.

If

2.2.
Projective resolution and Calabi-Yau property

We begin with results about a partial projective resolution of
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
. The results are already known for the undeformed preprojective algebra
[
9
, Lemma 1]
, and although not written anywhere, it has long been known to the first author that they generalize to
\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
, since they were further adapted to multiplicative preprojective algebras in
[
13
, section 3]
.

Let
\Pi=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
.
Let
P_{0}
and
P_{1}
be the following projective
\Pi
-bimodules,

P_{0}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\Pi e_{i}\otimes_{K}e_{i}\Pi\quad\text{and}\quad P_{1}=\bigoplus_{a\in\overline{Q}_{1}}\Pi e_{h(a)}\otimes_{K}e_{t(a)}\Pi.

For any
i\in I
, we write
\eta_{i}
for the element
e_{i}\otimes e_{i}
in the
i
th summand of
P_{0}
, and for any arrow
a\in\overline{Q}_{1}
, we write
\eta_{a}
for the element
e_{h(a)}\otimes e_{t(a)}
in the
a
th summand of
P_{1}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is an exact sequence

P_{0}\xrightarrow{f}P_{1}\xrightarrow{g}P_{0}\xrightarrow{h}\Pi\to 0

of
\Pi
-bimodules, where the maps are given by

f(\eta_{i})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\overline{Q}_{1}\\
h(a)=i\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(a)(\eta_{a}a^{*}+a\eta_{a^{*}}),\quad g(\eta_{a})=a\eta_{t(a)}-\eta_{h(a)}a,\quad h(\eta_{i})=e_{i}.

Proof.

We have
\Pi=K\overline{Q}/I
, where
I
is the ideal in
K\overline{Q}
generated by the elements
\rho_{i}-\lambda_{i}e_{i}
. Let
S=K\overline{Q}_{0}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}Ke_{i}
. Combining the exact sequence
[
26
, Theorem 10.1]
for
\Omega_{S}(\Pi)
with
[
26
, Theorem 10.3]
, gives an exact sequence

I/I^{2}\xrightarrow{\alpha}\Pi\otimes_{K\overline{Q}}\Omega_{S}(K\overline{Q})\otimes_{K\overline{Q}}\Pi\xrightarrow{\beta}\Pi\otimes_{S}\Pi\xrightarrow{\gamma}\Pi\to 0.

Here
\alpha(I^{2}+x)=1\otimes(x\otimes 1-1\otimes x)\otimes 1
for
x\in I
,
\beta(1\otimes\omega\otimes 1)
is the image of
\omega\in\Omega_{S}(K\overline{Q})\subseteq K\overline{Q}\otimes_{S}K\overline{Q}
in
\Pi\otimes\Pi
, and
\gamma
is the multiplication map.

We identify
\Pi\otimes_{S}\Pi
with
P_{0}
, and then
\gamma
is identified with the map
h
.
Let
V=K\overline{Q}_{1}
, the vector space spanned by the arrows in
\overline{Q}
, which is naturally an
S
-bimodule. By
[
26
, Theorem 10.5]
, there is an isomorphism

K\overline{Q}\otimes_{S}V\otimes_{S}K\overline{Q}\to\Omega_{S}(K\overline{Q})

sending
1\otimes a\otimes 1
to
a\otimes 1-1\otimes a
. Thus we can identify

P_{1}\cong\Pi\otimes_{S}V\otimes_{S}\Pi\cong\Pi\otimes_{K\overline{Q}}\Omega_{S}(K\overline{Q})\otimes_{K\overline{Q}}\Pi

with
\eta_{a}
corresponding to
1\otimes(a\otimes 1-1\otimes a)\otimes 1
. Now under the identification of
\Pi\otimes_{S}\Pi
with
P_{0}
, the element
a\otimes 1-1\otimes a
corresponds to

a\otimes e_{t(a)}-e_{h(a)}\otimes a=a\eta_{t(a)}-\eta_{h(a)}a.

It follows that
\beta
corresponds to the map
g
. Finally
I
is generated as an ideal by the elements
\rho_{i}-\lambda_{i}e_{i}
, so there is a surjective map
\phi:P_{0}\to I/I^{2}
sending
\eta_{i}
to
I^{2}+\rho_{i}-\lambda_{i}e_{i}
. Its composition with
\alpha

sends
\eta_{i}
to
1\otimes(\rho_{i}\otimes 1-1\otimes\rho_{i})\otimes 1
since
\lambda_{i}e_{i}\otimes 1=\lambda_{i}e_{i}\otimes e_{i}=1\otimes\lambda_{i}e_{i}
.
Now

\displaystyle\rho_{i}\otimes 1-1\otimes\rho_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\overline{Q}_{1}\\
h(a)=i\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(a)(aa^{*}\otimes 1-1\otimes aa^{*})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\overline{Q}_{1}\\
h(a)=i\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(a)\left(a(a^{*}\otimes 1-1\otimes a^{*})+(a\otimes 1-1\otimes a)a^{*}\right)

which corresponds in
P_{1}
to the same element as
f(\eta_{i})
. Thus
\alpha\phi
corresponds to
f
, giving the result.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

The complexes
P_{0}\xrightarrow{f}P_{1}\xrightarrow{g}P_{0}
and

\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{0},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\xrightarrow{-g^{*}}\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{1},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{0},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)

are isomorphic as complexes of
\Pi
-bimodules.

Note that the change of sign for
g^{*}
is irrelevant for the truth of the proposition; it is introduced because it arises in the definition of

\RHom_{\Pi}(\Pi,\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\cong\Hom^{\bullet}_{\Pi\Pi}(P^{\bullet},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)
.

Proof.

Clearly we have
\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(\Pi e_{i}\otimes_{K}e_{j}\Pi,\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\cong e_{i}\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi e_{j}
. Thus there are isomorphisms

\alpha:\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{0},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\to P_{0},\quad\alpha(\phi)=\sum_{i,\lambda}p^{\prime}_{i\lambda}\eta_{i}p_{i\lambda},

where
\phi(\eta_{i})=\sum_{\lambda}p_{i\lambda}\otimes p^{\prime}_{i\lambda}\in e_{i}\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi e_{i}
, and

\beta:\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{1},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\to P_{1},\quad\beta(\psi)=\sum_{a\in\overline{Q}_{1}}\varepsilon(a)p^{\prime}_{a\lambda}\eta_{a^{*}}p_{a\lambda},

where
\psi(\eta_{a})=\sum_{\lambda}p_{a\lambda}\otimes p^{\prime}_{a\lambda}\in e_{h(a)}\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi e_{t(a)}
.
Now we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{0},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)@>{-g^{*}}>{}>\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{1},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)@>{f^{*}}>{}>\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P_{0},\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\\
@V{\alpha}V{}V@V{\beta}V{}V@V{\alpha}V{}V\\
P_{0}@>{f}>{}>P_{1}@>{g}>{}>P_{0}.\end{CD}

∎

Let
\kD
be the duality
\Hom_{K}(-,K)
.

Proposition 2.6
.

For finite dimensional
\Pi
-modules
M
,
N
, we have
\Ext^{1}_{\Pi}(N,M)\cong\kD\Ext^{1}_{\Pi}(M,N)
and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\dim_{K}\Ext_{\Pi}^{1}(M,N)=\dim_{K}\Hom_{\Pi}(M,N)+\dim_{K}\Hom_{\Pi}(N,M)-(\Dim M,\Dim N).

Proof.

Since the sequence in Proposition
2.4
is split as a sequence of right
\Pi
-modules, it induces an exact sequence

P_{0}\otimes_{\Pi}M\to P_{1}\otimes_{\Pi}M\to P_{0}\otimes_{\Pi}M\to M\to 0

which is the start of a projective resolution of
M
. Applying
\Hom_{\Pi}(-,N)
gives a complex

(2.4)

0\to\Hom_{\Pi}(P_{0}\otimes_{\Pi}M,N)\to\Hom_{\Pi}(P_{1}\otimes_{\Pi}M,N)\to\Hom_{\Pi}(P_{0}\otimes_{\Pi}M,N)\to 0.

Since
\Hom_{\Pi}(\Pi e_{i}\otimes_{K}e_{j}\Pi\otimes_{\Pi}M,N)\cong\Hom_{K}(e_{j}M,e_{i}N)
, we see that the spaces in the complex are finite dimensional, and the alternating sum of their dimensions is
(\Dim M,\Dim N)
. Now the cohomology in the first two places is
\Hom_{\Pi}(M,N)
and
\Ext^{1}_{\Pi}(M,N)
. For
P
a f.g. projective
\Pi
-bimodule, we have natural isomorphisms

\kD\Hom_{\Pi}(P\otimes_{\Pi}M,N)\cong\kD\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P,\Hom_{K}(M,N))\cong\kD\left(\kD M\otimes_{\Pi}\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P,\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\otimes_{\Pi}N)\right)

\cong\Hom_{\Pi}(\Hom_{\Pi\Pi}(P,\Pi\otimes_{K}\Pi)\otimes_{\Pi}N,M).

Using Proposition
2.5
, we see that the dual of the complex (
2.4
) is isomorphic to the same complex, but with
M
and
N
exchanged. Thus the cohomology in the second two places of the complex (
2.4
) is isomorphic to
\kD\Ext^{1}_{\Pi}(N,M)
and
\kD\Hom_{\Pi}(N,M)
. The result follows.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

If

is a connected non-Dynkin quiver, then the map

in Proposition

is injective, so the exact sequence given there is a projective resolution of

as a

-bimodule, and the global dimension of

Proof.

For

this follows from the fact, mentioned in the proof of Lemma

, that

is Koszul with known Hilbert series. In general, to show the dependence on

, we write the map

in Proposition

as

. Now the filtration on

induces filtrations on the bimodules

and

, and there are natural maps

, which are isomorphisms thanks to Lemmas
2.2
and

. We have a commutative square

\begin{CD}P^{0}_{0}@>{f^{0}}>{}>P_{1}^{0}\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\gr P^{\lambda}_{0}@>{\gr f^{\lambda}}>{}>\gr P^{\lambda}_{1}\end{CD}

and since

is injective, Lemma
2.1
implies that

is injective. Tensoring the bimodule projective resolution of

with a

-module

, the resulting sequence remains exact (since the projective resolution of

is split as a sequence of one-sided

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

By Theorem

-bimodules,

3.
Reflection functors

In this section, we define reflection functors on the module categories of deformed preprojective algebras and observe relations on these functors. Since these functors changes weights, contrary to section
2
, we denote by
\Pi^{\lambda}=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
the deformed preprojective algebra of a quiver
Q
with a weight
\lambda
.

3.1.
Reflection functors via cokernels and kernels

In this subsection, we induce and study reflection functors via cokernels and kernels on modules over deformed preprojective algebras.

A representation

of
\Pi^{\lambda}=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
is a set

with head at

for any

of categories given by

and

.

and

-modules by this equivalence.

Fix a loop-free vertex

and let

and

and

Let

\displaystyle V_{i}\xrightarrow{\mu^{V}}V_{\oplus}\xrightarrow{\pi^{V}}V_{i}.

holds.
If
V
is clear from the context, then we write

,

, etc.
For

(3.2)

\pi_{c}\mu\pi\mu_{b}=\varepsilon(b)V_{c^{\ast}}V_{b}.

We next show that this equation is compatible with an action of
\Pi^{\lambda^{\prime}}
.
Let

.
We show that
C_{iji}(V)_{b}=C_{jij}(V)_{b}
holds.
If

C_{iji}(V)_{b}=V_{b}=C_{jij}(V)_{b}
holds.

If

and

, recall that

.
Thus we have
C_{iji}(V)_{b}=\varepsilon(b)e_{2}\mu_{b}=\varepsilon(b)d_{1}\mu_{b}=C_{ij}(V)_{b}=C_{jij}(V)_{b}
.
In a similar way, the equation holds if

and
t(b)\neq i
.

Assume that
t(b)=i
and
h(b)\neq j
.
Then
C_{iji}(V)_{b}=\pi_{b^{\ast}}\varepsilon_{2}
and
C_{jij}(V)_{b}=C_{ij}(V)_{b}=\pi_{b^{\ast}}\delta_{1}
hold.
We have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}e_{2}=\gamma_{2}c_{2}-(r_{j}r_{i}\lambda)_{i}\id=X^{i}Y^{i}-\lambda_{i}\id-(r_{j}r_{i}\lambda)_{i}\id=X^{i}Y^{i}-(r_{j}\lambda)_{i}\id=\delta_{1}d_{1},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}e_{2}^{\prime}d_{2}=\gamma_{2}\delta_{2}^{\prime}d_{2}=\gamma_{2}c_{2}^{\prime}Y^{j}=X^{i}V_{\alpha}Y^{j}=X^{i}\gamma_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}=\delta_{1}d_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}.

Therefore
\varepsilon_{2}(e_{2}\,\,e_{2}^{\prime}d_{2})=\delta_{1}(d_{1}\,\,d_{1}^{\prime}c_{1})
.
Since
(e_{2}\,\,e_{2}^{\prime}d_{2})=(d_{1}\,\,d_{1}^{\prime}c_{1})
is surjective, we have
\varepsilon_{2}=\delta_{1}
.
Thus
C_{iji}(V)_{b}=\pi_{b^{\ast}}\varepsilon_{2}=\pi_{b^{\ast}}\delta_{1}=C_{jij}(V)_{b}
.
In a similar way, the equation holds if
t(b)=j
and
h(b)\neq i
.

Assume that
b=\alpha^{\ast}
.
Then
C_{iji}(V)_{b}=\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}
and
C_{jij}(V)_{b}=-e_{1}^{\prime}
.
Since
(e_{1}\,e_{1}^{\prime}d_{1})=(d_{2}\,d_{2}^{\prime}c_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}e_{2}

\displaystyle=-d_{2}^{\prime}c_{2}=-e_{1}^{\prime}d_{1}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}e_{2}^{\prime}d_{2}

\displaystyle=(-d_{2}^{\prime}\delta_{2}^{\prime}-(r_{j}r_{i}\lambda)_{i}\id)d_{2}=-d_{2}^{\prime}c_{2}^{\prime}Y^{j}-(r_{j}r_{i}\lambda)_{i}d_{2}

\displaystyle=d_{2}(X^{j}Y^{j}-(r_{j}r_{i}\lambda)_{i}\id)=e_{1}(X^{j}Y^{j}-\lambda_{j}\id)

\displaystyle=e_{1}\gamma_{1}c_{1}=-e_{1}^{\prime}d_{1}^{\prime}c_{1}.

Namely,
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}(e_{1}\,e_{1}^{\prime}d_{1})=-e_{1}^{\prime}(d_{2}\,d_{2}^{\prime}c_{2})
.
Therefore
\varepsilon_{2}^{\prime}=-e_{1}^{\prime}
.
In a similar way, the equation holds if
b=\alpha
.
This completes the proof that
C_{iji}(V)=C_{jij}(V)
as
\Pi^{\lambda^{\prime}}
-modules.

Finally we show that this identification is a morphism of functors, that is, if
f:U\to V
is a morphism of
\Pi^{\lambda}
-modules, then

.
Let

be a vertex of
Q
.
If
k\neq i,j
, then
C_{iji}(f)_{k}=f_{k}=C_{jij}(f)_{k}
.
If
k=i

C_{jij}(f)_{i}=C_{ij}(f)_{i}
.
Since
C_{ij}(f)_{i}
is induced from (
3.11
) and

is induced from (

C_{ji}(f)_{i}=C_{jij}(f)_{i}
.
In a similar way, we have
C_{iji}(f)_{j}=C_{jij}(f)_{j}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The relations on the

4.1.

We denote by

Proposition 4.1
.

If

-Calabi-Yau algebra, then

in

In particular

.

Proof.

For part (a) use

Proposition
1.4
is a special case of the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A
be a
d
-Calabi-Yau algebra with
d\geq 2
, let
S
be a finite-dimensional simple left
A
-module and let
I=\Ann_{A}(S)
.

(1)

As a left
A
-module,
A/I
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of
S
, and as a right
A
-module it is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of
\kD(S)
.

(2)

If
\Ext_{A}^{1}(S,S)=0
, then
I
is a faithfully balanced
A
-bimodule, that is, the natural maps

A\to\End(I_{A})
and
A^{op}\to\End({}_{A}I)
given by the homotheties of left and right multiplication, are isomorphisms.

(3)

If
\Ext_{A}^{i}(S,S)=0
for
i=1,2,\dots,d-1
, then as a left
A
-module or as a right
A
-module,
I
is a
(d-1)
-tilting module.

Proof.

(i) Use that
A/I
embeds in
\End_{K}(S)\cong S\otimes_{K}\kD(S)
.

(ii) By the
d
-Calabi-Yau property, we have
\kD\Ext_{A}^{i}(A/I,A)\cong\Ext_{A}^{d-i}(A,A/I)=0
for
i<d
.
It follows that the restriction map
\Hom_{A}(A,A)\to\Hom_{A}(I,A)
is an isomorphism.
Since
\Ext^{1}(S,S)=0
, the restriction map
\Hom_{A}(A,A/I)\to\Hom_{A}(I,A/I)
is surjective,
but since the natural map
\Hom_{A}(A/I,A/I)\to\Hom_{A}(A,A/I)
is onto, we have
\Hom_{A}(I,A/I)=0
.
This implies that the natural map
\Hom_{A}(I,I)\to\Hom_{A}(I,A)
is an isomorphism.
This gives an isomorphism
A^{op}\to\End({}_{A}I)
. The other isomorphism follows by symmetry.

(iii) Since
A
is
d
-Calabi-Yau, the global dimension of
A
is
d
.
Therefore
I
has a projective dimension at most
d-1
on both sides.
It is equal to
d-1
since

\Ext^{d-1}_{A}(I,S)\cong\Ext^{d}_{A}(A/I,S)\cong\kD\Hom_{A}(S,A/I)\neq 0.

We show that
{}_{A}I
admits a projective resolution with finitely generated projective
A
-modules. Since
A
is homologically smooth, it has a finite projective resolution by finitely generated
A
-bimodules. By applying
(-)\otimes_{A}A/I
to a bimodule resolution of
A
,
A/I
has a finite projective resolution by finitely generated projective
A
-modules. This implies that
{}_{A}I
has a projective resolution with finitely generated projective
A
-modules by Schanuel’s lemma, using, for example
[
22
, Lemma 2.5]
. By symmetry also
I_{A}
has a projective resolution with finitely generated projective right
A
-modules.

Next we show that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(I,I)=0
for any
i>0
.
For
i=1,\dots,d-1
, we have

\kD\Ext_{A}^{i}(I,I)\cong\kD\Ext_{A}^{i+1}(A/I,I)\cong\Ext_{A}^{d-i-1}(I,A/I)\cong\Ext_{A}^{d-i}(A/I,A/I)=0

where the second isomorphism comes from the
d
-Calabi-Yau property and the last equality comes from the condition that
\Ext_{A}^{i}(S,S)=0
for
i=1,2,\dots,d-1
.
Since the projective dimension of
I
is at most
d-1
,
\Ext_{A}^{i}(I,I)=0
for any
i>0
.
By symmetry also
\Ext^{i}(I_{A},I_{A})=0
for
i>0
.

Finally, we show that
A
admits a finite coresolution by modules in
\add({}_{A}I)
.
Let
0\to P_{d-1}\to\dots\to P_{0}\to I\to 0
be resolution of
I
by finitely generated projective right
A
-modules. Applying the functor
\Hom(-,I_{A})
to this resolution, using that
\End(I_{A})\cong A
, that
\Ext^{i}(I_{A},I_{A})=0
for

, and
\Hom(P_{i},I_{A})\in\add({}_{A}I)
, we obtain the desired exact sequence.
By symmetry also

admits a finite coresolution by modules in
\add(I_{A})
.
∎

The following lemma is standard. See for example
[

, Lemma III.1.1]
in the Krull-Schmidt case and
[

in the tilting module case.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
T
is a partial tilting
A
-module and

is a finite-dimensional simple right

-module, then at least one of

\Tor_{1}^{A}(S,T)=0

Since

4.2.
Tilting ideals

For an
A
-module
S
, let
I_{S}=\Ann_{A}(S)
.
In the proof of the following proposition, we refer the proof of
[
8
, Proposition III.1.5]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
A
be 2-Calabi-Yau, let
T
be a tilting module for
A
and let
S
be a finite-dimensional rigid simple
A
-module.

(1)

We have
I_{S}\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}T\cong I_{S}\otimes_{A}T
in the derived category of
A
-modules.

(2)

If
\Tor_{1}^{A}(\kD(S),T)=0
, then the natural map
I_{S}\otimes_{A}T\to I_{S}T
is an isomorphism.

(3)

I_{S}T
is a tilting module for
A
and
\End_{A}(I_{S}T)\cong\End_{A}(T)
.

Proof.

(a) We have a short exact sequence

(4.1)

\displaystyle 0\to I_{S}\to A\to A/I_{S}\to 0.

Applying
-\otimes_{A}T
to this short exact sequence, we have
\Tor_{1}^{A}(I_{S},T)\cong\Tor_{2}^{A}(A/I_{S},T)=0
, giving the result.

(b) We have an exact sequence

\Tor_{1}^{A}(A/I_{S},T)\to I_{S}\otimes_{A}T\to T\to(A/I_{S})\otimes_{A}T\to 0,

and
A/I_{S}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of
\kD(S)
as a right
A
-module, so if
\Tor_{1}^{A}(\kD(S),T)=0
, then the map
I_{S}\otimes_{A}T\to I_{S}T
is an isomorphism.

(c) By Lemma
4.3
we have
\kD(S)\otimes_{A}T=0
or
\Tor_{1}^{A}(\kD(S),T)=0
. In the first case,
I_{S}T=T
and the assertion is trivial. Thus we may assume that
\Tor_{1}^{A}(\kD(S),T)=0
. Now the projective dimension of
T
is at most one and the projective dimension of
T/I_{S}T
is at most two, since the global dimension of
A
is two. Thus the projective dimension of
I_{S}T
is at most one. By (a) and (b) we have
I_{S}T\cong I_{S}\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}T
. Since
I_{S}
is a tilting ideal and
T
is a tilting module, this is tilting complex; see
[
8
, Lemma III.1.2(a)]
. Now the condition on projective dimension implies that
I_{S}T
is a tilting module.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

We show by induction on

that
I_{S_{1}S_{2}\dots S_{r}}=I_{S_{1}}I_{S_{2}}\cdots I_{S_{r}}
is a tilting ideal of finite codimension in

.
This is clear for

. If we know it for
r-1
, then

I_{S_{2}\dots S_{r}}
is a tilting ideal of finite codimension.
Now

I_{S_{2}\dots S_{r}}/I_{S_{1}S_{2}\dots S_{r}}\cong(A/I_{S_{1}})\otimes_{A}I_{S_{2}\dots S_{r}}

which is finite dimensional since
A/I_{S_{1}}
is finite dimensional and
I_{S_{2}\dots S_{r}}
is a finitely generated left
A
-module. Also
I_{S_{1}\dots S_{r}}=I_{S_{1}}I_{S_{2}\dots S_{r}}
is a tilting module by Proposition
4.4
. Similarly,
I_{S_{1}\dots S_{r}}=I_{S_{1}\dots S_{r-1}}I_{S_{r}}
is tilting as a right
A
-module.

Now let
I
be a partial tilting left ideal in
A
with
A/I\in\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})
. We show by induction on
\dim_{K}(A/I)
that

. If
I\neq A
, choose a simple submodule
S
of
A/I
.

We know
\kD\Hom_{A}(S,A)\cong\Ext^{2}_{A}(A,S)=0
, so
\Hom_{A}(S,A)=0
.
It follows that
\Ext^{1}(S,I)\neq 0
. Thus
\Tor_{1}^{A}(\kD(S),I)\neq 0
.
Thus by Lemma

,
\kD(S)\otimes_{A}I=0
.
Thus
(A/I_{S})\otimes_{A}I=0
, so

.

Let

. It is a left ideal with

. Since

it follows that
I_{S}U=I
.

Since the natural map

.
Since

Since there is a surjection from

By induction,

.

Finally, if

by the argument of

.
Namely, by the 2-Calabi-Yau property

, so an isomorphism

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be finite-dimensional rigid simple

\mathsf{D}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\Mod A)
to itself, and
F(I_{S}\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}X)=\sigma_{S}(F(X))
for
X
in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\Mod A)
.

Proof.

By assumption
\End_{A}(S)=K
, so by the Jacobson Density Theorem,
A/I_{S}\cong\End_{K}(S)\cong S\otimes_{K}\kD(S)
as
A
-bimodules. Thus also
\kD(A/I_{S})\cong S\otimes_{K}\kD(S)
. For
i\geq 0
, we have

\Tor_{i}^{A}(A/I_{S},T)\cong\kD\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,\kD(A/I_{S}))\cong\kD\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,S\otimes_{K}\kD(S))

\cong\kD\left(\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,S)\otimes_{K}\kD(S)\right)\cong S\otimes_{K}\kD\Ext^{i}_{A}(T,S)

as left
A
-modules. Thus, tensoring the exact sequence (
4.1
) with
T
, gives an exact sequence

(4.2)

\displaystyle 0\to S\otimes_{K}\kD\Ext^{1}_{A}(T,S)\to I_{S}\otimes_{A}T\to T\to S\otimes_{K}\kD\Hom_{A}(T,S)\to 0,

as well as
\Tor_{1}^{A}(I_{S},T)\cong\Tor_{2}^{A}(A/I_{S},T)\cong S\otimes_{K}\kD\Ext^{2}_{A}(T,S)
.

(a) Taking
S=T
, by the
2
-Calabi-Yau property,
\Ext_{A}^{2}(S,S)
is one dimensional over
K
, so
\Tor_{1}^{A}(I_{S},S)
is isomorphic to
S
.

(b) Follows from the exact sequence (
4.2
).

(c) Follows from (a), (b) and Lemma
4.3
.

(d) Follows from (c).
∎

The following proposition is the

.
We omit the proof since it is essentially the same as the tensor version.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
S,T\in\mathcal{S}
and let
m=\dim_{K}\Ext^{1}_{A}(S,T)
.

(1)

\Ext_{A}^{1}(I_{S},S)\cong S
as left
A
-modules.

(2)

If
S\not\cong T
, then the
A
-module
\Hom_{A}(I_{S},T)
has exactly two composition factors
S
and

, with multiplicities
m
and
1
, respectively.

(3)

We have

\RHom_{A}(I_{S},T)\cong\begin{cases}\Hom_{A}(I_{S},T)&(S\not\cong T),\\
\Ext^{1}_{A}(I_{S},T)[1]&(S\cong T).\end{cases}

(4)

The functor
\RHom_{A}(I_{S},-)
sends
\mathsf{D}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\Mod A)
to itself, and
F(\RHom_{A}(I_{S},X))=\sigma_{S}(F(X))
for
X
in
\mathsf{D}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\Mod A)
.

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We adapt the argument of
[
8
, Theorem III.1.9]
. By
[
5
, Theorem 3.3.1(ii)]
one can pass between any two reduced expressions for
w
by the operations of (i) replacing
\sigma_{S}\sigma_{T}
with
\sigma_{T}\sigma_{T}
, if there is no arrow in
Q(\mathcal{S})
from
S
to
T
, and (ii) replacing
\sigma_{S}\sigma_{T}\sigma_{S}
with
\sigma_{T}\sigma_{S}\sigma_{T}
if there is a unique arrow in
Q(\mathcal{S})
from
S
to
T
. Thus by Proposition
1.6
the mapping is well-defined.

To show the mapping is surjective, let
I\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{S})
, take an expression
I=I_{S_{1}}I_{S_{2}}\dots I_{S_{k}}
with
k
minimal and let
w=\sigma_{S_{1}}\dots\sigma_{S_{k}}
. By
[
5
, Theorem 3.3.1(i)]
, one can pass to a reduced expression for
w
by the operations (i) and (ii), as above, and (iii) remove
\sigma_{S}\sigma_{S}
. By Proposition
1.6
(i) and the minimality of
k
, operation (iii) doesn’t occur, so the expression
w=\sigma_{S_{1}}\dots\sigma_{S_{k}}
is reduced.

To prove that the mapping is injective, we may assume that
\mathcal{S}
contains only finitely many isomorphism classes of rigid simple modules, for if two Coxeter group elements
w,w^{\prime}
with reduced expressions involving the generators
\sigma_{S_{1}},\dots,\sigma_{S_{\ell}}
are sent to the same ideal, then they are equal in
W(\{S_{1},\dots,S_{\ell}\})
, so also equal in
W(\mathcal{S})
. Now the action of
W(\mathcal{S})
on
\mathcal{Z}
is the ‘geometric representation’ considered in
[
5
, section 4.2]
.

We claim that if
w=\sigma_{S_{1}}\dots\sigma_{S_{k}}
is a reduced expression, then
I=I_{S_{1}}\dots I_{S_{k}}\cong I_{S_{1}}\otimes_{\Lambda}^{\mathbb{L}}\dots\otimes_{\Lambda}^{\mathbb{L}}I_{S_{k}}
. Then
F(I\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}X)=w(F(X))
for
X\in\mathsf{D}_{\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{S})}(\Mod A)
by Proposition
4.7
(d), and since the geometric representation is faithful by
[
5
, Theorem 4.2.7]
, it follows that
I
determines
w
.

We prove the claim by induction on
k
, so let
w^{\prime}=\sigma_{S_{1}}\dots\sigma_{S_{k-1}}
and
I^{\prime}=I_{S_{1}}\dots I_{S_{k-1}}
. By Proposition
4.4
, for the opposite algebra, and with
S=D(S_{k})
, it suffices to show that
\Tor_{1}^{A}(I^{\prime},S_{k})=0
. By Lemma
4.3
, for the opposite algebra, if this fails, then
I^{\prime}\otimes_{A}S_{k}=0
. Thus it suffices to show that
F(I^{\prime}\otimes_{A}^{\mathbb{L}}S_{k})
is positive, but by the induction this is
w^{\prime}(F(S_{k}))
, and this is positive by
[
5
, Proposition 4.2.5(i)]
.
∎

4.4.
Extended Dynkin quivers

In this subsection
Q
is an extended Dynkin quiver and
\delta
is the minimal positive imaginary root.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Let
X
be the set of roots for
Q
together with zero. If
\alpha\in X
then the orbit
\{\alpha+n\delta\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
under addition of
\delta
is a subset of
X
, and since any orbit contains a root for the Dynkin quiver, the set of orbits is finite. But each orbit can contain at most one element of
\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
, for if
\alpha
and
\beta=\alpha+m\delta
belong to
\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
, with
m>0
, then the decomposition
\beta=\alpha+\gamma
with
\gamma=m\delta
contradicts that
\beta\in\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
.

Suppose
I
is a tilting ideal in
A=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
with
A/I
finite dimensional. If
S
is a finite-dimensional rigid simple module for
A=\Pi^{\lambda}(Q)
, and
T
is a finite-dimensional simple module which is not rigid, then
\Dim T
is an imaginary root, so a multiple of
\delta
, so
(\Dim S,\Dim T)=0
. Thus
\Ext^{1}_{A}(S,T)=\Ext^{1}_{A}(T,S)=0
by Proposition
2.6
. It follows that
A/I
decomposes as a direct sum
Y\oplus Z
where
\Dim Y
is a multiple of
\delta
and
Z
has composition factors in
\mathcal{R}
. Now if
Y\neq 0
, then
\End_{A}(Y)\neq 0
, so
\Ext^{1}_{A}(Y,Y)\neq 0
by Proposition
2.6
. Thus
\Ext^{1}_{A}(A/I,A/I)\neq 0
. On the other hand, since
\Ext^{1}_{A}(I,I)=0
we have a commutative diagram with exact rows

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(A,I)@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(A,A)@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(A,A/I)@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{}V@V{f}V{}V@V{g}V{}V\\
0@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(I,I)@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(I,A)@>{}>{}>\Hom_{A}(I,A/I)@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Thus there is an induced surjection from
\Coker f
to
\Coker g
. Now we have
\kD\Coker f\cong\kD\Ext^{1}_{A}(A/I,A)\cong\Ext^{1}_{A}(A,A/I)=0
by the 2-Calabi-Yau property, and
\Coker g\cong\Ext^{1}_{A}(A/I,A/I)
, so this vanishes, a contradiction. Thus
Y=0
, so
A/I
has composition factors in
\mathcal{R}
.
∎

Now suppose that
\lambda\cdot\delta=0
, let
\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}=\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\}
and let
S_{\alpha_{i}}
be the rigid simple module of dimension vector
\alpha_{i}
. We have
\alpha_{i}<\delta
, for
\alpha_{i}-\delta
is a real root with
\lambda\cdot(\alpha_{i}-\delta)=0
, and it can’t be positive, since that would contradict that
\alpha_{i}\in\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
.

Let
Q(\mathcal{R})
be the Ext-quiver of
\mathcal{R}
, so with vertex set
\{1,\dots,k\}
and the number of arrows from
i
to
j
is zero if
i=j
and is
-(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})_{Q}
if
i\neq j
. Here
(-,-)_{Q}
is the symmetric bilinear form for
Q
, and we write
q_{Q}
for the corresponding quadratic form. Now
Q(\mathcal{R})
is the double of some quiver
\Gamma
, that is,
\overline{\Gamma}=Q(\mathcal{R})
. We denote by
q_{\Gamma}
the quadratic form for
\Gamma
.

Proposition 4.9
.

The following statements hold.

(1)

We have
q_{\Gamma}(d)=q_{Q}(\Sigma_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\alpha_{i})
for
d=(d_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
.

(2)

\Gamma
is a disjoint union of extended Dynkin quivers.

Proof.

(a)
For
d=(d_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, we have

2q_{Q}(\Sigma_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\alpha_{i})=\Sigma_{i,j}(d_{i}\alpha_{i},d_{j}\alpha_{j})_{Q}=\Sigma_{i,j}d_{i}d_{j}(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})_{Q}=2q_{\Gamma}(d),

where
(-,-)_{Q}
is the symmetric bilinear form of
Q
.
Thus we have the assertion.

(b) By (a),
q_{\Gamma}
is positive semi-definite, since so is
q_{Q}
. Therefore
\Gamma
is a disjoint union of Dynkin quivers and extended Dynkin quivers. We show that the connected components of
\Gamma
are not Dynkin. For
\alpha_{j}\in\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
, the element
\beta=\delta-\alpha_{j}
is a positive real root and
\lambda\cdot\beta=0
. Consider a decomposition
\beta=\gamma_{1}+\dots+\gamma_{r}
with the
\gamma_{i}
positive (and necessarily real) roots with
\lambda\cdot\gamma_{i}=0
, and with
r
as large as possible. The maximality implies that
\gamma_{i}\in\Sigma_{\lambda}^{\rm re}
, so each
\gamma_{i}
is an
\alpha_{\ell}
for some
\ell
. Collecting terms, this implies that for any vertex
j
of
\Gamma
, there is a dimension vector
d=(d_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
such that
d_{j}>0
and
\delta=\Sigma_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\alpha_{i}
. By (a), we have
q_{\Gamma}(d)=q_{Q}(\delta)=0
. Thus connected component of
\Gamma
containing vertex
j
cannot be Dynkin.
∎

Lemma 4.10
.

For

is a multiple of

Proof.

This assertion directly follows from Proposition

Let

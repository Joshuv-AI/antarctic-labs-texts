Thin-shell gravastar in a noncommutative BTZ geometry
A. T. N. Silva, M. A. Anacleto, L. Casarini

Abstract
In this paper, we build a thin-shell gravastar model within a noncommutative BTZ geometry. For this, we consider a noncommutative BTZ metric in the inner region and a geometry associated with a BTZ solution in the outer region, joined by the generalized join technique. After investigating the inner spacetime, surface and outer spacetime, we observe that there is a surface energy density and surface pressure, such as to make gravastar stable. This effect persists even when the cosmological constant is zero. Besides, we found a bound for the noncommutativity parameter. In addition, we examine the thermodynamics of the noncommutative BTZ black hole in Schwarzschild-type form in three-dimensional spacetime. We also check the stability condition by calculating the specific heat capacity, as well as for the formation of black hole remnants.

II
BTZ metric on a noncommutative geometry

In this section, we construct the BTZ metric in the noncommutative background, and
then, we incorporate noncommutativity through a Lorentzian mass density of a spherical region of radius
r
, given by
Liang:2012vx
;
Anacleto:2020efy

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}=\frac{M_{0}\sqrt{\theta}}{2\pi(r^{2}+\theta)^{3/2}},

(1)

here
\theta
is the noncommutative parameter with dimension of length
2

and
M_{0}
is the total mass spread over the entire linear sized region
\sqrt{\theta}
. In this case, the “stained” mass is distributed as follows
Anacleto:2020efy
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}=\int_{0}^{r}\rho(r)2\pi rdr=M_{0}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\theta}}\right).

(2)

Note that when
r\rightarrow\infty
, the noncommutative parameter disappears, thus becoming a point mass with value
M_{0}
,
losing its noncommutative characteristic.
Now, using the
\mathcal{M}
mass distribution, we have the metric of the noncommutative, non-rotating BTZ black hole which is given by
Anacleto:2020efy
:

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-f(r)dt^{2}+f(r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\phi^{2},

(3)

where the metric function reads

\displaystyle f(r)=-\mathcal{M}+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}=-M_{0}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}.

(4)

Here
l
is the radius of curvature and that provides the length scale needed to have an event horizon radius.

For
\theta\ll 1
, we can write the metric function as follows
Anacleto:2020efy
:

\displaystyle f(r)=-M_{0}+\frac{M_{0}\sqrt{\theta}}{r}+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(\theta^{3/2}).

(5)

In this approximation, a term,
{M_{0}\sqrt{\theta}}/{r}
, of the Schwarzschild type is generated as an effect of noncommutativity.
The impact of this term on the thermodynamics of the BTZ black hole was investigated in
Anacleto:2020efy
, showing that a logarithmic correction term for entropy is obtained.
Also, by calculating the specific heat capacity its stability analysis was verified.
As a result, it becomes a remnant of a black hole with a minimum mass dependent on the parameter
\theta
.

By setting
f(r)=0
, the event horizon is given by
Anacleto:2020efy

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{h}\approx r_{h}-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2},\quad\mbox{or}\quad\tilde{M}\approx\frac{r^{2}_{h}}{l^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{r_{h}}\right)=M_{0}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{l^{2}M_{0}}}\right),

(6)

where
r_{h}=\sqrt{l^{2}M_{0}}
is the event horizon of the usual BTZ black hole.

For the Hawking temperature, we have
Anacleto:2020efy

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=\frac{\tilde{r}_{h}}{2\pi l^{2}}-\frac{M_{0}\sqrt{\theta}}{4\pi\tilde{r}^{2}_{h}}.

(7)

In terms of
r_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{H}=\frac{{r}_{h}}{2\pi l^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{4\pi l^{2}}-\frac{M_{0}\sqrt{\theta}}{4\pi\left({r}_{h}-\sqrt{\theta}/2\right)^{2}}.

(8)

The result can be expressed in Schwarzschild-type form as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{H}=\frac{\tilde{T}_{H}}{M_{0}}=\frac{1}{2\pi\left[r_{h}+\sqrt{\theta}+\dfrac{\theta}{2r_{h}}\right]}.

(9)

In Fig.
1
, we have Hawking temperature as a function of the horizon radius,
r_{h}
.
As shown in the graph, we obtain the Hawking temperature for
\theta=0
and
\theta=0.03
.

Note that the Hawking temperature reaches a maximum point before going to zero when the horizon radius,

Therefore, noncommutativity has the effect of removing the Hawking temperature singularity.
Furthermore, from the condition

Next, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{max}=\frac{\mathcal{T}_{H}}{2+\sqrt{2}}=\frac{1}{2\pi(2+\sqrt{2})r_{min}}=\frac{1}{2\pi(1+\sqrt{2})\sqrt{\theta}}.

(10)

In particular, for
\theta=0.03

r_{min}=0.122474
and
\mathcal{T}_{max}=0.380613
.
Now, we will determine the entropy by applying the following equation

\displaystyle S=\int\frac{1}{\mathcal{T}_{H}}\frac{\partial\tilde{M}}{\partial{r}_{h}}d{r}_{h},

(11)

where, from Eq.
6
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{M}}{\partial{r}_{h}}\approx\frac{2{r}_{h}}{l^{2}}-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{l^{2}}=\frac{2M_{0}}{r_{h}}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2r_{h}}\right).

(12)

So, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{S}{M_{0}}=\int\frac{2}{r_{h}\mathcal{T}_{H}}\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2r_{h}}\right)d{r}_{h}=4\pi\int\left[1+\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2r_{h}}\right]dr_{h},

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi r_{h}+2\pi\sqrt{\theta}\ln r_{h}.

(14)

Therefore, we find a logarithmic correction term for the entropy of the noncommutative BTZ black hole.
Moreover, for
\theta=0

Now, we can determine the specific heat capacity through the relationship

\displaystyle C=\frac{\partial\tilde{M}}{\partial r_{h}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{T}_{H}}{\partial r_{h}}\right)^{-1}.

(15)

Hence, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\frac{C}{M_{0}}=-4\pi r_{h}\left(1+\frac{3}{2}\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{r_{h}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{r_{h}}\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{r_{h}}\sqrt{\frac{\theta}{2}}\right).

(16)

By setting
r_{h}=\sqrt{\theta/2}
, the specific heat capacity cancels out and the BTZ black hole in Schwarzschild-type form stops evaporating completely.
Thus, becoming a black hole remnant.
Moreover, since
r_{min}=\sqrt{l^{2}M_{min}}
, we have the following minimum mass

\displaystyle M_{min}=\frac{r^{2}_{min}}{l^{2}}=\frac{\theta}{2l^{2}}=-\frac{\Lambda\theta}{2}.

(18)

Thus, for a positive minimum mass (
M_{min}>0
) the cosmological constant must be negative (
\Lambda<0
).
In addition, for
\theta=0
, we have
\mathcal{C}=-4\pi r_{h}
which is the specific heat capacity of the Schwarzschild black hole projected in 3 dimensions.

III
STRUCTURE EQUATIONS OF NONCOMMUTATIVE BTZ GRAVASTARS

To build the BTZ gravastars, we first consider two manifolds of spacetime, inspired by noncommutative geometry. The outer is defined by
M_{+}
, and the inner is
M_{-}

Ovgun:2017jzt
. Then we join them together using the cut and paste method in a surface layer, which in this work will be called
\Sigma

Lobo:2015lbc
. The metric of the exterior is the non-singular
anti-de Sitter spacetime in (2 + 1)-dimensions
Banados:1992wn
:

ds^{2}=-f(r)_{+}dt_{+}^{2}+f(r)_{+}^{-1}dr_{+}^{2}+r_{+}^{2}d\phi_{+}^{2},

(19)

where
t
is the physical time in the outer region, and

\displaystyle f(r)_{+}=-M_{0}+\frac{r^{2}}{l^{2}}=M-\Lambda r^{2}.

(20)

Here
M=-M_{0}
and
\Lambda=-1/l^{2}
is the cosmological constant.

We can also write the metric function
f(r)_{+}
in Schwarzschild-type form as follows:

\displaystyle f(r)_{+}=1-\frac{b_{+}}{r},

(21)

being

\displaystyle b_{+}=-r\left(M-\Lambda r^{2}\right)+r.

(22)

And the metric inside is given by the noncommutative BTZ geometry. So, we have

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-g(r)_{-}dt_{-}^{2}+f(r)_{-}^{-1}dr_{-}^{2}+r_{-}^{2}d\phi_{-}^{2},

(23)

where

\displaystyle g(r)_{-}=M\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\theta}}\right)-\Lambda r^{2},

(24)

and

\displaystyle f(r)_{-}=1-\frac{b_{-}}{r},

(25)

where

\displaystyle b_{-}=-r\,M\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\theta}}\right)+r.

(26)

Here
\pm
represents the outer and inner geometry, respectively.

III.1
Transition layer

The distributions, both inside and outside, are bounded by isometric hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{+}
and
\Sigma_{-}
. Our goal is to join
M_{+}
and
M_{-}
in their limits to obtain a single variety
M
such that
M=M_{+}\cup M_{-}
so that, in these limits,
\Sigma=\Sigma_{+}=\Sigma_{-}
. So, to calculate the components of the energy-momentum tensor, we will use the intrinsic metric as follows
Lobo:2015lbc
:

ds^{2}_{\Sigma}=-d\tau^{2}+a(\tau)^{2}(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\,\ d\phi^{2}),

(27)

where
\tau
is the physical time in the junction surface. As we are working in (2+1)-dimensions, we assume,
d\phi^{2}=0
.

The junction surface is given by
x^{\nu}(\tau,\theta,\phi)=(t(\tau),a(\tau),\theta)
, where the unit normal vectors with respect to this surface are the following
MartinMoruno:2011rm
:

n^{\mu}_{+}=\left(\frac{1}{M-\Lambda a^{2}}\dot{a},\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}},0\right),

(28)

and

n^{\mu}_{-}=\left(\dfrac{1}{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)}\dot{a},\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}},0\right),

(29)

where the dot over
a
represents a derivative with respect to
\tau
.

The extrinsic curvatures are calculated by the following equation
Visser:1995cc
:

K^{\psi\pm}_{\,\ \psi}=\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{1-\frac{b_{\pm}(a)}{a}+\dot{a}^{2}},

(30)

and

K^{\tau\pm}_{\,\ \tau}=\left\{\dfrac{\ddot{a}+\dfrac{b_{\pm}(a)-b^{{}^{\prime}}_{\pm}(a)a}{2a^{2}}}{\sqrt{1-\dfrac{b_{\pm}(a)}{a}+\dot{a}^{2}}}\right\}.

(31)

Thus, the extrinsic curvatures in the outer region are given by

K^{\psi+}_{\,\ \psi}=\frac{1}{a}\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}},

(32)

K^{\tau+}_{\,\ \tau}=\left\{\frac{\ddot{a}-\Lambda{a}^{2}}{\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}}}\right\},

(33)

and in the interior region, we have

K^{\psi-}_{\,\ \psi}=\dfrac{1}{a}\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}},

(34)

K^{\tau-}_{\,\ \tau}=\left\{\dfrac{\ddot{a}-\dfrac{a^{2}M\sqrt{\theta}}{2(a^{2}+\theta)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}}}\right\}.

(35)

In the following, we will apply these equations to analyze the energy conditions for the stability of gravastar in the thin shell using the Lanczos equations.

III.2
Lanczos equations: Surface tension

Now, to determine the stability in the thin shell, we will use the Lanczos equations, which derive from Einstein’s equations applied to the hypersurface that joins the mass space-times, and are given by
Lobo:2015lbc

S^{i}_{\,j}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(k^{i}_{\,j}-\delta^{i}_{\,j}\,\ k^{k}_{\,\,k}\right),

(36)

where
S^{i}_{\,j}
is the surface energy-momentum tensor in
\Sigma
. Now, from the Lanczos equation in (2+1)-dimensional spacetime, the surface tension energy-momentum tensor
S^{i}_{\,j}=diag(-\sigma,\mathscr{P})
where
\sigma
is the surface density and
\mathscr{P}
is the surface pressure
Perry:1991qq
. These are given as follows:

\sigma=-\frac{K^{\psi}_{\,\ \psi}}{4\pi}=-\dfrac{1}{4\pi a}\left[\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}}-\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}}\,\right],

(37)

\mathscr{P}=\frac{K^{\tau}_{\,\ \tau}+K^{\psi}_{\,\ \psi}}{8\pi}=\frac{1}{8\pi a}\left[\frac{M+\dot{a}^{2}+\ddot{a}-2\Lambda a^{2}}{\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}}}-\dfrac{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}+\ddot{a}-\dfrac{a^{2}M\sqrt{\theta}}{2(a^{2}+\theta)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}}}\right].

(38)

Using the equations (
37
) and (
38
), we have:

\sigma+2\mathscr{P}=\frac{K^{\tau}_{\,\ \tau}}{4\pi}=\dfrac{1}{4\pi a}\left[\dfrac{\ddot{a}-\Lambda{a^{2}}}{\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}+\dot{a}^{2}}}-\dfrac{\ddot{a}-\dfrac{a^{2}M\sqrt{\theta}}{2(a^{2}+\theta)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}+\theta}}\right)+\dot{a}^{2}}}\right].

(39)

For the sake of discussion, let us consider a static solution where

a_{0}\in(r_{-},r_{+})
. So, we have:

{\sigma}(a_{0})=-\frac{1}{4\pi a_{0}}\left[\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}_{0}}-\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}_{0}+\theta}}\right)}\,\right],

(40)

{\mathscr{P}}(a_{0})=\frac{1}{8\pi a_{0}}\left[\frac{M-2\Lambda a^{2}_{0}}{\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}_{0}}}-\dfrac{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}_{0}+\theta}}\right)-\dfrac{a_{0}^{2}M\sqrt{\theta}}{2(a^{2}_{0}+\theta)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}_{0}+\theta}}\right)}}\right],

(41)

\sigma(a_{0})+2{\mathscr{P}}(a_{0})=\dfrac{1}{4\pi a_{0}}\left[\dfrac{-\Lambda a^{2}_{0}}{\sqrt{M-\Lambda a^{2}_{0}}}+\dfrac{\dfrac{a_{0}^{2}M\sqrt{\theta}}{2(a^{2}_{0}+\theta)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\sqrt{\theta}}{\sqrt{a^{2}_{0}+\theta}}\right)}}\,\right].

(42)

Now we can write the above equations in terms of the dimensionless parameters
\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda a^{2}_{0}
and

\tilde{\sigma}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\sqrt{M-\tilde{\Lambda}}-\sqrt{M\left(1-\frac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)}\,\right],

(43)

\tilde{\mathscr{P}}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left[\frac{M-2\tilde{\Lambda}}{\sqrt{M-{\tilde{\Lambda}}}}-\dfrac{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)-\dfrac{\Theta M}{2(1+\Theta^{2})^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)}}\right],

\tilde{\sigma}+\tilde{{\mathscr{P}}}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left[\frac{-M}{\sqrt{M-{\tilde{\Lambda}}}}+\dfrac{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)+\dfrac{\Theta M}{2\left(1+\Theta^{2}\right)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)}}\,\right],

(45)

\tilde{\sigma}+2\tilde{{\mathscr{P}}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{-{\tilde{\Lambda}}}{\sqrt{M-{\tilde{\Lambda}}}}+\dfrac{\dfrac{\Theta M}{2\left(1+\Theta^{2}\right)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)}}\,\right],

(49)

\tilde{\sigma}+2\tilde{{\mathscr{P}}}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\frac{\dfrac{\Theta M}{2\left(1+\Theta^{2}\right)^{3/2}}}{\sqrt{M\left(1-\dfrac{\Theta}{\sqrt{1+\Theta^{2}}}\right)}}\,\right].

Now, for

{\sigma}\approx-\frac{\sqrt{M}\Theta}{8\pi a_{0}}=-\frac{\sqrt{M\theta}}{8\pi a^{2}_{0}},

(51)

{\mathscr{P}}\approx\dfrac{\sqrt{M}\Theta}{8\pi a_{0}}=\frac{\sqrt{M\theta}}{8\pi a^{2}_{0}},

(52)

\sigma+2{\mathscr{P}}\approx\frac{\sqrt{M}\Theta}{8\pi a_{0}}=\frac{\sqrt{M\theta}}{8\pi a_{0}^{2}}.

(53)

However, from the above equations, we have the following equation of state

\displaystyle{\sigma}+{\mathscr{P}}=0,\,\quad\quad{\mathscr{P}}=-{\sigma}=\rho.

(54)

Thus, since
\sigma<0
, we have
p=\rho
in the thin shell, an effect due to noncommutativity.
On the other hand, considering
\Lambda=0
, inside the shell, we have the state equation
p=-\rho
,
with
\rho=\sigma\sim\sqrt{\theta}/M_{0}r^{3}>0
.
Therefore, the equation of state,
p=-\rho
, represents a repulsive pressure.
In the context of an accelerated Universe, this would be related to dark energy arising due to the effect of noncommutativity
(
\theta
-dark energy
). Here, we show that by setting
\Lambda=0

\theta

Here it is opportune to mention that noncommutativity plays a vital role in black hole physics. Some effects that disappear in the usual case can be observed due to noncommutativity.
For example, in

By considering the noncommutative BTZ black hole, Anacleto and collaborators

Furthermore, in

In addition, the stability condition and remainders for the noncommutative BTZ black hole and the noncommutative Schwarzschild black hole via calculating specific heat were examined in
Anacleto:2020efy
;
Anacleto:2020zfh

IV
Conclusion

In this paper, we have considered the noncommutative BTZ black hole with noncommutativity introduced via the Lorentzian distribution Therefore, we have performed its thermodynamic analysis and investigated a thin-shell gravastar model.
We show that noncommutativity plays the role of regularizing the temperature of the three-dimensional Schwarzschild anti-de Sitter black hole.
In addition, by computing entropy we have found a logarithmic correction term,
2\pi\sqrt{\theta}\ln r_{h}
.
Furthermore, we examine the stability of the black hole by calculating the specific heat capacity.
We have found that for a given minimum radius dependent on the parameter
\theta
the specific heat capacity goes to zero, indicating the formation of a hole remnant as the final stage.
By analyzing the gravastar model of a thin-shell noncommutative BTZ black hole,
we have found that the energy density
\sigma
is negative and the pressure
\mathscr{P}
is positive.
Also, we have verified that the state
\tilde{\sigma}+\tilde{\mathscr{P}}
and
\tilde{\sigma}+2\tilde{\mathscr{P}}
are positive for quite small
\Theta
values.
Moreover, even for
{\Lambda}=0
, the results obtained above are maintained.
Besides, even in the absence of the cosmological constant, we have obtained that the condition
p=\rho
in the thin shell is satisfied with
\rho\approx{\sqrt{M\theta}}/{(8\pi a^{2}_{0})}
(for small
\theta
)
and in the inner region the equation of state
p=-\rho
(
\rho\sim\sqrt{\theta}/M_{0}r^{3}
) is also satisfied showing the effect of noncommutativity for repulsive pressure and dark energy.
Therefore, in our calculations we have shown that the
\theta
parameter plays the role of the cosmological constant for the gravastar energy stability condition.
Hence, we have found a relationship between the noncommutativity parameter and the cosmological constant.
Thus, we have estimated a value of the order of
\theta\sim[10
TeV
]^{-2}
for the noncommutativity parameter.
By comparing with Ref.
Banerjee:2016bry
which deals with the gravastar solution in noncommutative geometry. The authors introduced noncommutativity via Gaussian distribution and verified that
\sigma<0
and
p>0
in the thin shell. In our analysis, we have introduced noncommutativity using a Lorentzian distribution that made it possible to find the conditions on the energy density and pressure in the thin shell in the limit of small
\theta
, as well as for the zero cosmological constant. Furthermore, it was possible to obtain a bound for the noncommutativity parameter.

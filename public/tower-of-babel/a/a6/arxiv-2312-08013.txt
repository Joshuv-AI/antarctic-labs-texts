Abstract
. Motivated by the study of the recurrent orbits in a Morse set of a Morse decomposition, we introduce the concept of Morse predecomposition of an isolated invariant set in the setting of combinatorial and classical dynamical systems. We prove that a Morse predecomposition indexed by a poset is a Morse decomposition and we show how a Morse predecomposition may be condensed back to a Morse decomposition.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
primary 37B30, secondary 37B20,37B35

1.
Introduction

A Morse decomposition of a flow on a compact metric space is a finite collection of disjoint, compact invariant sets, referred to as
Morse sets
, such that all the recurrent dynamics of the flow is contained in the union of the Morse sets and the Morse sets can be indexed by a partially ordered set (poset).
Morse decompositions provide a rigorous, computable approximation to C. Conley’s fundamental result on the decomposition of a compact invariant set into its chain recurrent part and gradient-like part
[
7
,
8
]
.
Identification of nontriviality of Morse sets can be done via the Conley index
[
7
]
, but more detailed understanding of the structure of the dynamics within a Morse sets requires additional information.
With this in mind, in this paper we seek to extend the concept of a Morse decomposition to a Morse predecomposition that provides a more refined representation of the internal structure of Morse sets.
In essence, we give up the partial order condition and we weaken the concept of a connection between two isolated invariant sets.
We present the definition both in the combinatorial setting of multivector fields
[
24
,
20
]
(see Definition
4.3
) and in the classical setting of flows (see Definition
5.2
).
We prove that a Morse predecomposition with a partial order as the preorder is always a Morse decomposition (see Corollary
4.15
and Theorem
5.13
) and show how, in the combinatorial case, one can reconstruct a Morse decomposition from a given Morse predecomposition (see Theorem
4.13
).

Our presentation is motivated by the fact that algorithms for constructing Morse decompositions are based on a combinatorial representation of dynamics in the form of a multivalued map acting on a collection of cells decomposing the phase space.
The multivalued map sends a cell
Q
into a collection of cells that captures the forward evolution of
Q
under the dynamics.
The action of the multivalued map may be interpreted as a directed graph with the collection of cells as vertices.
The strongly connected components of this graph identify isolating blocks and the associated isolated invariant
sets form the constructed Morse decomposition.

In the context of discrete dynamics, i.e., dynamics generated by a continuous function
f\colon X\to X
, there is extensive work and broadly applicable software for identification of Morse decompositions
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
21
]
and analysis of the structure of dynamics within the Morse sets
[
22
,
32
,
10
,
9
,
29
]
.
Comparable tools are much less developed in the context of flows, e.g., dynamics generated by a differential equation
[
34
]
.
For the purposes of this paper we focus on the theory of combinatorial multivector fields
[
24
,
20
,
26
,
12
]
(an extension of the methods of combinatorial topological dynamics introduced by R. Forman
[
14
,
15
]
) for which the above mentioned graph theoretic algorithms produce Morse decompositions and for which identification of finer structure of dynamics within Morse sets is possible, e.g., the identification of periodic orbits and chaotic dynamics
[
27
]
.
We expect that effective extension of these latter results will require combinatorial decompositions of strongly connected components and a deeper understanding of the dynamic interpretations of these decompositions, which are subjects of this paper.

To understand the idea of a Morse predecomposition on a simple example, consider the flow visualized in Figure

(top)
and the isolated invariant set
S
highlighted in pink.
We recall that a
Morse decomposition
of

is an indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mbox{$\mathbb{P}$}\}
of mutually disjoint isolated invariant sets
(called Morse sets) with a partial order
(\mbox{$\mathbb{P}$},\leq)
such that for every point

we can find

such that
q<p
,
\alpha(x)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(x)\subset M_{q}
.
The finest Morse decomposition of
S

consists of three Morse sets,
M_{b}=\{B\}
,
M_{d}=\{D\}
and set
M_{o}
composed of stationary points
A
,
C
,
E
,
together with the heteroclinic orbits connecting them.
The connections between the Morse sets are presented in Figure
1
(bottom left) in the form of an acyclic directed graph (digraph).
The Morse decomposition cannot capture the stationary points
A
,
C
,
E
and the heteroclinic connections between them.

We define a
Morse predecomposition
of
S
as an indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mbox{$\mathbb{P}$}^{\prime}\}
of mutually disjoint, closed invariant sets
such that

and
\omega(x)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
for some

is an arbitrary finite set.
With such a definition we can split

,
M_{c}
and

corresponding to stationary points

,

, and

as independent elements of
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}
.
Thus, the resulting Morse predecomposition is
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}=\mbox{$\{\,M_{b},M_{d},M_{a},M_{b},M_{c}\,\}$}
.
The corresponding digraph representing the connections is presented in Figure
1
(bottom right).
We call it the

of

(see Section

The directed graph presented in Figure
2
has four strongly connected components marked in light green.
There are also three strongly connected sets which are not strongly connected components.
They are marked in orange.
Every strongly connected set is contained in a strongly connected component.
However, a strongly connected component may have many non-empty, proper, disjoint, strongly connected subsets.
Hence, even on a purely combinatorial level the internal structure of a strongly connected component may vary.

Under weak conditions, every strongly connected component of a digraph obtained from a cellular decomposition of the phase space of a flow identifies an isolating neighborhood and a Morse set inside.
This raises the question whether a strongly connected subset of a strongly connected component also identifies an isolating neighborhood and an isolated invariant set.
This is not true in general, but in the context of the combinatorial multivector field models discussed above we can provide conditions under which the answer is positive.
Thus, we first introduce Morse predecompositions for multivector fields where the concept arises naturally, is relatively simple, and suggests how to set up the analogous definition for flows.

2.
Preliminaries

This section gathers notation, terminology and basic results needed in the sequel.

2.1.
Sets, families and maps

We denote the sets of real numbers, non-negative real numbers, integers, non-negative integers and natural numbers respectively
by
\mathbb{R}
,
\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+}

\mathbb{Z}
,
\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{+}
, and
\mathbb{N}
.

By a
family
we mean a set whose elements are sets.
Given a set
X
, we denote the family of all subsets of
X
by
\text{$\mathcal{P}$}(X)
.

An
indexed family
is, formally speaking, a function assigning to every
\iota\in J
the set
A_{\iota}
(see
[
13
, Section I.1]
).
Informally, we will write an indexed family as

\{A_{\iota}\mid\iota\in J\}
.
Every family
\mathcal{A}
may be consider an indexed family
\{A\mid A\in\text{$\mathcal{A}$}\}
.
The converse is not true. Although an indexed family is often identified with the family of its values, there is a subtle difference. The family of values may be smaller, because
A_{\iota}
and
A_{\kappa}
may be equal as sets, but they are different in the indexed family if
\iota\neq\kappa
.
In the case when the elements of a family are mutually disjoint, the difference between families and indexed families matters only in the case of the empty set, which may appear only once in a family but many times in an indexed family.

A
partition
of a set
X
is a family
\text{$\mathcal{E}$}\subset\text{$\mathcal{P}$}(X)

of mutually disjoint, non-empty subsets of
X
such that
\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{E}$}=X
.
Given two families
\text{$\mathcal{A}$},\text{$\mathcal{B}$}\subset\text{$\mathcal{P}$}(X)
we say that
\mathcal{A}
is
inscribed
in
\mathcal{B}

or
\mathcal{A}
is a refinement of
\mathcal{B}

if for each
A\in\text{$\mathcal{A}$}
there is a
B\in\text{$\mathcal{B}$}
such that
A\subset B
.

We write
f:X\nrightarrow Y
for a
partial map
from
X
to
Y
, that is, a map
defined on a subset
\operatorname{dom}f\subset X
, called the
domain
of
f
,
and such that the set of values of
f
, denoted by

\operatorname{im}f
, is contained in
Y
.

We write
F:X\multimap Y
to denote a
multivalued map
, that is a map
defined on
X
with subsets of
Y
as values.
We identify such an
F
with the relation

\mbox{$\{\,(x,y)\in X\times Y\mid y\in F(x)\,\}$}.

Such an identification lets us define the
composition

G\circ F
of multivalued maps
F:X\multimap Y
and
G:Y\multimap Z

as the composition of relations.
In particular, we are interested in the iterates of a multivalued map
F:X\multimap X

considered as a multivalued dynamical system.

2.2.
Relations

Let
X
be a set and let
R\subset X\times X
be a binary relation in
X
.
Relation
R^{\prime}\subset X\times X
is an
extension
of relation
R
if
R\subset R^{\prime}
.
A set
X
with a reflexive and transitive relation
R
is called a
preordered set
; relation
R
is called a
preorder
.
If
R
is additionally antisymmetric we call it a
partial order
, and
X
a
partially ordered set
(or a poset).
The total relation
X\times X
is clearly a preorder containing every binary relation
R
in
X
.
Hence, the family of preorders extending given
R
is not empty.
The intersection of this family is easily seen to be the smallest preorder in
X
extending
R
.
We call it the
preorder induced by
R
and denote it
\leq_{R}
.
The following proposition is straightforward.

Proposition 2.1
.

If the preorder
\leq_{R}
induced by relation
R\subset X\times X
admits an extension which is a partial order,
then
\leq_{R}
itself is a partial order.
∎

2.3.
Digraphs

Note that the pair
(X,R)
may be considered a
directed graph
(a
digraph
)
with
X
as its set of
vertices
and
R
as its set of
edges
.
Vice versa, given a digraph
(V,E)
, the collection of edges
E
is a relation on the set of vertices
V
.
This lets us freely mix the terminology typically used for relations with the terminology used for digraphs.

Given a digraph
G=(V,E)
, by a
walk in
G
from
v
to
w
of length
k>0

we mean a sequence
v=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots v_{k}=w
of vertices in
V
such that
(v_{i-1},v_{i})\in E
for
i=1,2,\ldots k
.
Such a walk is a
cycle
if
v_{0}=v_{k}
. A cycle of length one is a
loop
.
Digraph is
acyclic
if it has no cycles other then loops.
A subset
A\subset V
of vertices is
strongly connected
if for any
v,w\in A
there is a walk

v=v_{0},v_{1},\ldots v_{k}=w
such that
v_{i}\in A
for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
.
A vertex
v\in V
is strongly connected if
\{v\}
is strongly connected which happens if and only if there is a loop at
v
.
A
strongly connected component
of
G
is a strongly connected subset of vertices which is maximal
with respect to inclusion. Note that two strongly connected components of
G
are either disjoint or equal
but, in general, the family of all strongly connected components need not be a partition of
V
.

By mixing the terminology of relations and digraphs,
we say that
G=(V,E)
is
reflexive
if
E
considered as a relation in
V
is reflexive
or, equivalently, if there is a loop at every vertex
v
. Note that the family of strongly connected components
of a reflexive digraph
G=(V,E)
is a partition of the vertex set
V
.
Given a reflexive digraph
G=(V,E)
, we write
\leq_{G}
for the
preorder in
V
induced by
E
.
We note that the preorder
\leq_{G}
of an acyclic digraph
G
is a always a partial order.

2.4.
Topological spaces

For the basic terminology concerning topological
spaces we refer the reader to
[
13
,
28
]
.
In terms of notation, given a topological space
X
, we denote by
\operatorname{int}A
the
interior
of
A\subset X

and by
\operatorname{cl}A
the closure of
A
.
A subset
A\subset X
is
locally closed
if
\operatorname{mo}A:=\operatorname{cl}A\setminus A
is closed
(see
[
13
, Problem 2.7.1]
for several equivalent conditions for local closedness).

A topological space
X
is a
finite topological space
if the underlying set
X
is finite.
In this paper we consider finite topological spaces satisfying
T_{0}
separation axiom.
By Alexandrov Theorem
[
2
]
every finite,
T_{0}
topological space
(X,\text{$\mathcal{T}$})
can be uniquely identified with a finite poset
(X,\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{T}$}})
defined by

(1)

x\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{T}$}}y\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x\in\operatorname{cl}y.

In particular, a subset
A
of a finite topological space is locally closed if and only if
it is
convex
with respect to the poset, that is if
x\leq y\leq z
and
x,z\in A
imply
y\in A
.

3.
Dynamical systems

In this section we gather basic concepts and results in dynamics needed in the paper.
We first recall classical definitions and statements for flows and then we introduce
the concept of a combinatorial multivector field and the associated combinatorial dynamical system.
Note that our notation and terminology for combinatorial systems shadows that for flows.
We do so to emphasize the similarities.

3.1.
Flows

Assume that
(X,d)
is a locally compact metric space and
\varphi:X\times\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\rightarrow X
is a flow on
X
.

We recall that a partial map
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\nrightarrow X
is a
solution
of
\varphi
if
\gamma(s+t)=\varphi(\gamma(s),t)

for all
s,s+t\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
.
A
solution through
x\in X
is a solution such that
x\in\operatorname{im}\gamma
.
A
solution in
S\subset X
is a solution such that
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset S
.
A solution
\gamma
is
full
if
\operatorname{dom}\gamma=\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
; otherwise it is
partial
.

We recall that the
maximal invariant set
of
N\subset X
is given by

\operatorname{Inv}(N)=\operatorname{Inv}(N,\varphi):=\mbox{$\{\,x\in N\mid\varphi(\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$},x)\subset N\,\}$}.

We say that a set
S\subset X
is
invariant
if
\operatorname{Inv}S=S
.
A compact set
N\subset X
is an
isolating neighborhood
if
\operatorname{Inv}N\subset\operatorname{int}N
.
An invariant set
S\subset X
is
an isolated invariant set
if
there exists an isolating neighborhood
N
such that
\operatorname{Inv}N=S
.

A fundamental feature of isolating neighborhoods is that they persist under a small perturbation.
More precisely, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

[
23
, Proposition 1.1]

Given a continuously parametrized family of dynamical systems

\varphi_{\lambda}:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\times X\rightarrow X,\quad\lambda\in[-1,1]

and an isolating neighborhood for
\varphi_{0}
, there is an
\varepsilon>0
such that

N
is also an isolating neighborhood for
\varphi_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
.
∎

The
omega limit set of a point
x\in X
, denoted by
\omega(x)
, is the maximal invariant set contained in the closure of the forward solution passing through
x
:

\displaystyle\omega(x)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{t>0}\operatorname{cl}\varphi(x,[t,+\infty)).

Symmetrically, we have the
alpha limit set of
x
, denoted by
\alpha(x)
defined as

\displaystyle\alpha(x)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{t<0}\operatorname{cl}\,\varphi(x,(-\infty,t]).

As point
x
uniquely determines the corresponding solution
\gamma(t):=\varphi(x,t)
we use also notation
\alpha(\gamma)
and
\omega(\gamma)
.
This will allow us to be more consistent with the notation used for multivalued combinatorial dynamical systems.

Let
S\subset X
be a compact invariant set.
An invariant set
A\subset S
is an
attractor in
S
if there exists a neighborhood
U
of
A
such that
\omega(U\cap S)=A
. A compact invariant set
R\subset S
is a
repeller in
S
if there exists a neighborhood
V
of
R
such that
\alpha(V\cap S)=R
. If
S=X
we just say that
A
is an attractor (repeller).

3.2.
Chain recurrence

Let
S
be a compact metric space.
Fix an open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
S
and a
T>0
.
A
(\text{$\mathcal{U}$},T)
-
chain from
x
to
y
of length
n
is a sequence
x=x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{n}=y

such that there exist sequences
t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}
of reals numbers and
U_{1},U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\in\text{$\mathcal{U}$}

satisfying
t_{i}\geq T
and
x_{i},\varphi(x_{i-1},t_{i})\in U_{i}

for each
i=1,2\ldots n
.
Let us denote by
R(S)
the set of points
x\in S
such that for every open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
S
, and every
T>0
there exists a non-constant
(\text{$\mathcal{U}$},T)
-chain in
S
from
x
to itself.
A set
A\subset S
is
chain recurrent
if
R(A)=A
.

As the consequence of
[
7
, 4.1.D]
and
[
7
, 6.3.C]
and dual results for
\alpha
limit sets we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

For every point
x\in S
limit sets
\alpha(x)
and
\omega(x)
are non-empty, compact, invariant, connected and chain recurrent.

By
[
1
, Theorem 4.12(5)]
we immediately get the following property.

Proposition 3.3
.

If
C\subset S
is a connected, invariant, chain recurrent set and there exists
a non-empty isolated invariant set
A
such that
A\varsubsetneq C
, then
A
is neither an attractor nor a repeller in
C
.

The following lemma may be proved analogously to
[
6
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
S
is connected and
A\varsubsetneq S
is a non-empty, isolated invariant set.
If
A
is not an attractor (respectively not a repeller),
then there exists an
x\in S\setminus A
such that
\alpha(x)\subset A
(respectively
\omega(x)\subset A
).

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
C\subset S
is a connected, invariant, chain recurrent set and
A
is a non-empty isolated invariant set in
C
.
If
A\varsubsetneq C
, then there exist points
x,y\in C\setminus A
such that
\alpha(x)\subset A
and
\omega(y)\subset A
.

Proof:

It follows from Proposition
3.3

that
A
is neither an attractor nor a repeller in
C
.
Hence, the conclusion follows immediately from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

3.3.
Combinatorial dynamical systems

Let
X
be a finite,
T_{0}
topological space.
A
combinatorial dynamical system
on
X
induced by a multivalued map
\Pi:X\multimap X
is a multivalued map

\Pi:X\times\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{+}\multimap X
defined recursively for
x\in X
and
n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}^{+}
by
\Pi(x,0):=\{x\}
and
\Pi(x,n+1):=\Pi(\Pi(x,n))
.
Formally, the map
\Pi:X\multimap X
is the
generator
of the combinatorial dynamical system but, to simplify the terminology,
we often refer to
\Pi:X\multimap X
as the combinatorial dynamical system.

A
solution
of
\Pi
is a partial map
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\nrightarrow X
such that
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
is an interval in
\mathbb{Z}

and
\gamma(t+1)\in\Pi\left(\gamma(t)\right)
for
t,t+1\in\operatorname{dom}\gamma
.
We recall that
\Pi
may be considered as a binary relation in
X
, hence,
also as a directed graph
G_{\Pi}
.
In terms of the digraph
G_{\Pi}
, a solution may be identified with a walk in
G_{\Pi}
.
If
\operatorname{dom}\gamma
is finite, we call
\gamma
a
path
.
If
\operatorname{dom}\gamma=\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
, we call
\gamma
a
full solution
.
We denote the restriction of a full solution
\gamma
to the intervals
[p,q]
,
[0,\infty)
, and
(-\infty,0]

respectively by
\gamma_{[p,q]}
,
\gamma^{+}
, and
\gamma^{-}
.

A
shift
is a map
\tau_{s}:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\ni t\mapsto t+s
.
Let
\gamma
and
\psi
be solutions such that
\min\operatorname{dom}\gamma=p
and
\max\operatorname{dom}\psi=q
with
p,q\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
.
Moreover, assume that
\gamma(p)\in\Pi(\psi(q))
.
Then, we define the
composition
of
\gamma
and
\psi
by
\gamma\cdot\psi:=\gamma\cup(\psi\circ\tau_{q-p})
.
It is straightforward to observe that the composition of solutions is also a solution.

3.4.
Combinatorial dynamics induced by multivector fields

In this subsection we briefly recall basic concepts of combinatorial dynamics generated by multivector fields.
For a comprehensive introduction into multivector fields see
[
20
]
.

A
multivector field
on
X
is a partition
\mathcal{V}
of
X
into locally closed subsets of
X
(see Sec.
2.4
for the definition of local closedness).
We refer to the elements of the partition as
multivectors
.
Note that
\operatorname{mo}V=\operatorname{cl}V\setminus V
is always closed for a multivector
V
, because a multivector is locally closed.
We say that multivector
V
is
critical
if the relative singular homology
H(\operatorname{cl}V,\operatorname{mo}V)
is non-trivial.
Otherwise, we say that
V
is
regular
.

For
A\subset X
we set

\text{$\mathcal{V}$}_{A}:=\{V\in\text{$\mathcal{V}$}\mid V\cap A\neq\varnothing\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left[A\right]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}:=\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{V}$}_{A}.

We say that
A
is
\mathcal{V}
-compatible
if
A=\left[A\right]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
.
In particular, for every
x\in X
set
\left[x\right]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}:=\left[\{x\}\right]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
is the unique multivector
V\in\text{$\mathcal{V}$}
containing
x
.

A multivector field
\mathcal{V}
induces a combinatorial dynamical system generated by
\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}:X\multimap X

given for an
x\in X
by

(2)

\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}(x):=\operatorname{cl}x\cup[x]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}.

As mentioned
\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
induces a directed graph
G_{\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}}
.
We will denote it for short as
G_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
.

By a solution of
\mathcal{V}
we mean a solution of the combinatorial dynamical system
\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
.
For a full solution
\gamma
we define combinatorial
limit sets
as

(3)

\displaystyle\alpha(\gamma):=\big\langle\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}^{-}}}\gamma((-\infty,t])\big\rangle_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega(\gamma):=\big\langle\bigcap_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}}\gamma([t,\infty))\big\rangle_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}},

where
\big\langle A\big\rangle_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
denotes the
\mathcal{V}
-hull of
A
, that is the smallest locally closed and
\mathcal{V}
-compatible set containing
A
.

We say that a full solution
\gamma
is
non-essential
if either
\alpha{(\gamma)}
or
\omega{(\gamma)}
is contained in a single regular multivector;
otherwise we say that
\gamma
is
essential
. These definitions of non-essential and essential solutions are easily seen to be equivalent to the
definitions given in
[
20
]
.
We denote by
\operatorname{eSol}(A)
the set of all essential solutions in
A
.
Additionally we set

\displaystyle\operatorname{eSol}(x,A):=\{\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(A)\mid\gamma(0)=x\}.

and we define the
maximal invariant subset of

as

\operatorname{Inv}S:=\{x\in S\mid\operatorname{eSol}(x,S)\neq\varnothing\}.

We say that set
S
is
invariant
if
\operatorname{Inv}S=S
.
A closed set
N
is an
isolating set
for an invariant set
S

if
\Pi_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}(S)\subset N
and every path in
N
with endpoints in
S
is contained in
S
.
If an invariant set admits an isolating set we call it an
isolated invariant set
.
Note that the combinatorial notion of isolation is not an exact counterpart of the classical one.
In particular, the same set can be an isolating set for two different isolated invariant sets.
The need for a modification of isolation in finite topological spaces
comes from the tightness of such spaces caused by the lack of Hausdorff axiom.

Theorem 3.6
.

[
20
, Propositions 4.10 and 4.13,Theorem 4.13]

Assume
S\subset X
is invariant. Then

is an isolated invariant set if and only if

is locally closed and
\mathcal{V}
-compatible.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem

we get the following corollary.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
S
is an isolated invariant set of
\mathcal{V}
. Then
\text{$\mathcal{V}$}_{S}:=\mbox{$\{\,V\in\text{$\mathcal{V}$}\mid V\subset S\,\}$}

is a multivector field on

. We call it the

multivector field.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

[
20
, Theorem 6.15]

Let
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(X)
.
Then both
\alpha(\gamma)
and
\omega(\gamma)
are non-empty, connected, isolated invariant sets.
Moreover, sets
\alpha(\gamma)
and
\omega(\gamma)
are strongly connected with respect to

.

The following proposition follows immediately from the definition of a strongly connected set.

Proposition 3.9
.

Assume

is strongly connected with respect to

.
Then, there exists a path
\rho:[0,n]_{\mathbb{Z}}\to C
such that
\operatorname{im}\rho=C
and
\rho(0)=\rho(n)
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(X)
.
Then there exist
\rho,\rho^{\prime}\in\operatorname{eSol}(\alpha(\gamma))
such that
\operatorname{im}\rho=\alpha(\gamma)
and
\operatorname{im}\rho^{\prime}=\omega(\gamma)
.

Proof:

By Theorem
3.8
set
\alpha(\gamma)
is strongly connected with respect to

.
Hence, by Proposition

we can construct a path

such that
\operatorname{im}\theta=\alpha(\gamma)
and

.
Define

∎

3.5.
Morse decompositions in classical and combinatorial dynamics

We now recall the classical concept of Morse decomposition for flows and its counterpart for multivector fields.
Later in the paper, after introducing Morse predecompositions, we will give different but equivalent
definitions of Morse decomposition, formulated in terms of Morse predecompositions.

Consider an isolated invariant set
S
of a flow
\varphi
on a locally compact metric space
X
.
The following definition of Morse decomposition, in the formulation by J. Reineck
[
30
, Definition 1.3]
,
goes back to R. Franzosa
[
17
]
.

Definition 3.11
.

An indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
of mutually disjoint isolated invariant subsets of
S

indexed by a poset
\mathbb{P}
is a
Morse decomposition
of
S
if for every
x\in S

either
x\in M_{p}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
or there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(x)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(x)\subset M_{q}
.

Consider now an isolated invariant set
S
of a combinatorial multivector field
\mathcal{V}
on a finite topological space
X
.
The following definition of Morse decomposition for multivector fields was introduced in
[
20
, Definition 7.1]

Definition 3.12
.

An indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
of mutually disjoint isolated invariant subsets of
S

indexed by a poset
\mathbb{P}
is a
Morse decomposition
of
S

if for every essential solution
\gamma
in
X

either
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{p}
for some
p\in\mathbb{P}
or
there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.

As pointed out in
[
19
]
, Morse decomposition is an extremely general tool, but this generality has led to subtle variances
in its definition. Some approaches exclude the empty set as a Morse set, some allow for many empty Morse sets.
The latter approach requires a properly set up definition but has many benefits,
in particular in the context of the stability of various invariants associated with Morse decomposition.
In
[
19
]
, the issue is resolved by differentiating Morse representation from Morse decomposition.
This paper is based on the original definition by Franzosa
[
16
]
in which Morse decomposition is considered as a properly understood
indexed family. An indexed family also allows for many empty Morse sets, as pointed out in Section
2.1
.

4.
Morse predecomposition in combinatorial dynamics

In this section we introduce Morse predecompositions in the setting of combinatorial multivector fields.
We assume in this section that
\mathcal{V}
is a multivector field on a finite topological space
X
and
X
is invariant
with respect to
\mathcal{V}
. Such an assumption is not restrictive, because if
X
is not invariant, we can replace
X

by its invariant part and
\mathcal{V}
by its restriction to the maximal invariant subset.

4.1.
Combinatorial Morse predecomposition

In order to generalize the concept of Morse decomposition we first introduce the following definition classifying full solutions
which keep visiting isolated invariant set
S_{1}
backward in time and isolated invariant set
S_{2}
forward in time.

Definition 4.1
.

Consider invariant sets
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
of a multivector field
\mathcal{V}
in
X
.
A full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}\rightarrow X
is a
link from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
if
\alpha(\gamma)\cap S_{1}\neq\varnothing\neq\omega(\gamma)\cap S_{2}
.
Link
\gamma
is
homoclinic
if
S_{1}=S_{2}
. Otherwise it is
heteroclinic
.
A homoclinic link
\gamma
is
trivial
if
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset S_{1}=S_{2}
.
Otherwise it is
non-trivial
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
S_{1},S_{2}\subset X
be isolated invariant sets.
There exists a link
\gamma
from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}

if and only if
there exists a path
\rho:[a,b]\rightarrow X
such that
\rho(a)\in S_{1}
and
\rho(b)\in S_{2}
.

Proof:

Let
\gamma
be a link from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
.
Let
a,b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
such that
\gamma(a)\in\alpha(\gamma)
and
\gamma(b)\in\omega(\gamma)
.
By Theorem
3.8
for any
x_{1}\in S_{1}\cap\alpha(\gamma)
there exist a path
\rho_{1}
from
x_{1}
to
\gamma(a)
.
Similarly we can find
x_{2}\in S_{2}\cap\omega(\gamma)
a path
\rho_{2}
from
\gamma(b)
to
x_{2}
.
Hence,
\rho:=\rho_{1}\cdot\gamma_{[a,b]}\cdot\rho_{2}
is a path from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
.

To prove the opposite implication consider a path
\rho:[a,b]\rightarrow X

such that
\rho(a)\in S_{1}
and
\rho(b)\in S_{2}
. Take
\varphi_{1}\in\operatorname{eSol}(\rho(a),S_{1})
and
\varphi_{2}\in\operatorname{eSol}(\rho(b),S_{2})

and define essential solution
\gamma:=\varphi_{1}^{-}\cdot\rho\cdot\varphi_{2}^{+}
.
We get
\alpha(\gamma)=\alpha(\varphi_{1})\subset S_{1}
and
\omega(\gamma)=\omega(\varphi_{2})\subset S_{2}
.
Hence,
\gamma
is a link from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

An indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
of mutually disjoint isolated invariant subsets in
X

is a
Morse predecomposition
of
X
if every essential solution
\gamma
in
X
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}

for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
.
We refer to the sets in
\mathcal{M}
as
pre-Morse sets
.
We also define a Morse predecomposition of an isolated invariant set
S\subset X
as a Morse predecomposition
with respect to the induced multivector field
\text{$\mathcal{V}$}_{S}
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Morse predecomposition of
X
.
Then, for every non-empty isolated invariant set
S
we have
S\cap\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}\neq\varnothing
.
In particular, if
V\in\text{$\mathcal{V}$}
is a critical multivector then
V\subset\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
.

Proof:

Assume to the contrary that
S
is a non-empty isolated invariant set such that
S\cap\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\varnothing
.
Since
S
is non-empty, we can find a
\varphi\in\operatorname{eSol}(S)
.
Clearly,
\alpha(\varphi)\subset S
and
\omega(\varphi)\subset S
because of Theorem
3.6
and (
3
).
Thus,
\varphi
is an essential solution while not being a link which contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition.
∎

Links of a system forms a relation
L
on an indexing set
\mathbb{P}
of a Morse predecomposition
\mathcal{M}
defined as

L:=\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{P}\times\mathbb{P}\mid\text{there exists a non-trivial link from $M_{p}$ to $M_{q}$}\}.

The digraph

We say that a preorder

is

4.2.

In this section we present a definition of Morse decomposition formulated as a special case of Morse predecomposition.
We later prove (see Theorem

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
be an indexed family of mutually disjoint subsets of

.
Then, the following conditions are mutually equivalent.

(i)

The family
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse decomposition of
\mathcal{S}
.

(ii)

Each

is a saturated, isolated invariant subset of
X
and

\mathbb{P}
admits a partial order
\leq
such that for every essential solution
\gamma
in

there exist

satisfying

,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.

(iii)

Each
M_{p}
is an isolated invariant subset of
X
and

\mathbb{P}
admits a partial order
\leq
such that for every essential solution
\gamma
in
X

either

for some

satisfying

,

and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}

In particular, every Morse set in a Morse decomposition is saturated and isolated.

Proof:
Assume (i). Then

is a saturated Morse predecomposition and the flow induced preorder

is a partial order.
In particular, each

is a saturated, isolated invariant subset of

We claim that the partial order

To see this consider an essential solution
\gamma
in

.
By the definition of Morse predecomposition there exist

such that

and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
.
By the definition of flow induced preorder we get
p\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}q
and by Theorem
3.8

and Lemma
4.10
we conclude that
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
Hence, (ii) follows from (i).

Assume in turn (ii). Then, each
M_{p}
is an isolated invariant subset of
X
.
We claim that the partial order
\leq
given in (ii) fulfills the requirements of condition (iii).
To see this, consider an essential solution
\gamma
in
X
and assume there is no
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{p}
.
By (ii) there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
satisfying
p\geq q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
We claim that
p>q
. To see this assume to the contrary that
p=q
.
We first prove that under this assumption we have
\mathbb{P}_{\operatorname{im}\gamma}\subset\{p\}
.
To prove this consider an
r\in\mathbb{P}_{\operatorname{im}\gamma}
such that
r\neq p
.
Then, by Proposition
4.2
, one can construct a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{r}
and a link from
M_{r}
to
M_{q}
, which gives
q\leq r\leq p=q
, which contradicts with the assumption that
\leq
is a partial order.
It proves that
\mathbb{P}_{\operatorname{im}\gamma}\subset\{p\}
.
However, this means that
\gamma
is a homoclinic connection to
M_{p}
.
Since
M_{p}
is saturated, we get
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{p}
, a contradiction.
Hence,
p>q
which completes the proof of (iii).

Finally, assume (iii) and consider an essential solution
\gamma
in
X
.
In order to prove that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition, we have to show that
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
.
If there is a
p\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{p}
, then also
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
,
which shows that
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{p}
.
Otherwise, we get from (iii) that there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
,
which also proves that
\gamma
is a link. This completes the proof that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition.

Clearly,
\leq
is then an admissible partial order for
\mathcal{M}
. We still need to prove that each
M_{r}\in\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
is saturated.
To see this consider a full solution
\gamma
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cup\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{r}
.
If
\operatorname{im}\gamma\not\subset M_{r}
, then
we get from (iii) the existence of
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
However, Morse sets are mutually disjoint and limit sets are non-empty. Therefore
p=r=q
, a contradiction
proving that
M_{r}
is saturated.
∎

4.3.
Consolidation theorem

Assume that
\mathcal{V}
is a combinatorial multivector field on a finite topological space
X
and
consider a fixed Morse predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
.
Given
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{P}
we denote the family of links in
X
between pre-Morse sets with indexes in
{\mathbb{Q}}
by

\operatorname{eSol}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X):=\{\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(X)\mid\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\cap{{\mathbb{Q}}}\neq\varnothing\neq\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}\cap{\mathbb{Q}}\},

and we define the
connection set
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
by

(5)

M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}:=\bigcup\{\operatorname{im}\gamma\mid\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(X)\}.

Lemma 4.12
.

Assume
\leq
is an admissible preorder with respect to Morse predecomposition
\mathcal{M}
.
Then for every
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{P}
the set
M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is a saturated isolated invariant set.
Moreover, if
{\mathbb{Q}}
is convex with respect to
\leq
then
M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\cap M_{r}=\varnothing
for all
r\in\mathbb{P}\setminus{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

Proof:

First observe that
\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{\mathbb{Q}})
is non-empty for every
x\in M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
directly by definition (
5
).
Thus,
M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is invariant.
We will show that
M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is
\mathcal{V}
-compatible and locally closed.
To prove
\mathcal{V}
-compatibility take an
x\in M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and a
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
.
Consider a
y\in[x]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}
and an essential solution
\gamma^{\prime}:=\gamma^{-}\cdot y\cdot\gamma^{+}
.
Clearly,
\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{eSol}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
It follows that
y\in M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.
Therefore, if
x\in M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
then
\left[x\right]_{\text{$\mathcal{V}$}}\subset M_{\mathbb{Q}}
, which proves the
\mathcal{V}
-compatibility of
M_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
.

To show that
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is locally closed it suffices to prove that
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is convex with respect to the order
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{T}$}}
on
X
given by the topology (see (
1
) in Section
2.4
).
Hence, consider
x,z\in M_{\mathbb{Q}}
and a
y\in X
such that
x\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{T}$}}y\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{T}$}}z
.
Since there exist
\gamma_{x}\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\gamma_{z}\in\operatorname{eSol}(z,M_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we can construct an essential solution
\psi:=\gamma_{z}^{-}\cdot y\cdot\gamma_{x}^{+}
.
We have
\alpha(\psi)\cap M_{p}=\alpha(\gamma_{z})\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing
and
\omega(\psi)\cap M_{q}=\omega(\gamma_{x})\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
for some
p,q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Hence,
\psi\in\operatorname{eSol}_{\mathbb{Q}}
and
y\in\operatorname{im}\psi\subset M_{\mathbb{Q}}
. This proves that
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is convex with respect to
\leq_{\mathbb{Q}}
, that is locally closed.
Hence, we get from Theorem
3.6
that
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is an isolated invariant set. It follows directly from the definition of
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
that
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
is saturated.

To prove the remaining assertion assume that
{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{P}
is convex with respect to the admissible preorder
\leq
and
M_{\mathbb{Q}}\cap M_{r}\neq\varnothing
for some
r\in\mathbb{P}\setminus{\mathbb{Q}}
.
Then we can select an
x\in\operatorname{im}\gamma\cap M_{r}
for some
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{\mathbb{Q}})

and, by Definition (
5
), we can find
p,q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing
and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
.
Since
M_{r}
is invariant there exists a
\psi\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{r})
.
Consider essential solutions
\gamma_{q}:=\psi^{-}\cdot\gamma^{+}
and
\gamma_{p}:=\gamma^{-}\cdot\psi^{+}
.
We have

\displaystyle\alpha(\gamma_{q})\subset M_{r},\ \omega(\gamma_{q})\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing,\ \alpha(\gamma_{p})\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing\text{, and}\ \omega(\gamma_{p})\subset M_{r}.

By the admissibility of
\leq
we get
q\leq r\leq p
. Since
p,q\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and
r\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
this contradicts the assumption that
{\mathbb{Q}}
is convex.
∎

Theorem 4.13
.

(Consolidation theorem)

Let
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
be a Morse predecomposition of
X
and let
\leq
be an admissible preorder.
Assume
\mathcal{Q}
is a partition of
\mathbb{P}
consisting of non-empty,
convex with respect to
\leq
subsets of
\mathbb{P}
such that the induced relation
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}
is a partial order,
where
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}
is defined for
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}
by
Q\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}Q^{\prime}
if and only if there exist a
q\in Q
and a
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}
such that
q\leq q^{\prime}
.
Then,
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}:=\left\{M_{Q}\mid Q\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}\right\}
is a Morse decomposition of
X
.

Proof:

We see from Lemma
4.12
that
M_{Q}
is a saturated isolated invariant set for every
Q\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}
.
Consider
M_{Q},M_{Q^{\prime}}\in\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}
for some
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}
such that
Q\neq Q^{\prime}
.
To see that
M_{Q}
and
M_{Q^{\prime}}
are disjoint, suppose to the contrary that there exists an
x\in M_{Q}\cap M_{Q^{\prime}}
.
Select a
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{Q})
and a
\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{eSol}(x,M_{Q^{\prime}})
.
Then
\gamma_{QQ^{\prime}}:=\gamma^{-}\cdot(\gamma^{\prime})^{+}
is an essential solution satisfying

\alpha(\gamma_{QQ^{\prime}})=\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{Q}
and

\omega(\gamma_{QQ^{\prime}})=\omega(\gamma^{\prime})\subset M_{Q^{\prime}}
.
It follows that
\gamma_{QQ^{\prime}}
is a link from
M_{q}
to
M_{q^{\prime}}
for some
q\in Q
and
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}
.
Since
\leq
is admissible, we get
q^{\prime}\leq q
and in consequence, also
Q^{\prime}\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}Q
.
Similarly, considering
\gamma_{Q^{\prime}Q}:=\gamma^{-}\cdot(\gamma^{\prime})^{+}
we get
Q\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}Q^{\prime}
.
Since
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}
is a partial order, we get
Q=Q^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
Hence,
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}
is a Morse predecomposition.

Consider now a
\gamma\in\operatorname{eSol}(X)
.
By the definition of Morse predecomposition we can find
q,q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}

such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q^{\prime}}\neq\varnothing
.
Since
\leq
is an admissible preorder, we get
q^{\prime}\leq q
.
Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a partition, there are some
Q,Q^{\prime}\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}
such that
q\in Q
and
q^{\prime}\in Q^{\prime}
.
By Proposition
3.10
we can find a
\psi\in\operatorname{eSol}(X)
such that
\alpha(\psi)=\omega(\psi)=\operatorname{im}\psi=\alpha(\gamma)
.
Therefore,
\psi\in\operatorname{eSol}_{Q}
and
\alpha(\gamma)=\operatorname{im}\psi\subset M_{Q}
.
Similarly, we argue that
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{Q^{\prime}}
.
Since
q^{\prime}\leq q
we get
Q^{\prime}\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}}Q
. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}
satisfies condition (ii) of Theorem
4.11

which means that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse decomposition.
∎

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
be a Morse predecomposition of
X
.
If
\mathcal{Q}
is a partition of
\mathbb{P}
into strongly connected components of graph
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}

then

\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}:=\{M_{Q}\mid Q\in\text{$\mathcal{Q}$}\}

is a Morse decomposition of
X
.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Morse predecomposition of
X
.
If there is a partial order among admissible preorders in
\mathbb{P}
then

\text{$\mathcal{M}$}^{\prime}:=\{M_{\{p\}}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}

is a Morse decomposition of
X
.

We note that
M_{\{p\}}
in general may be bigger than
M_{p}
, because it may contain points on solutions homoclinic to
M_{p}
.
However,
M_{\{p\}}=M_{p}
if
M_{p}
is saturated. Therefore, we also have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a saturated Morse predecomposition of
X
.
If there is a partial order among admissible preorders in
\mathbb{P}
then

\mathcal{M}
is a Morse decomposition of
X
.

5.
Predecompositions and decompositions for flows

In this section we introduce the concept of Morse predecomposition for flows.
We show that, as in the combinatorial case, it generalizes Morse decomposition.
Moreover, we introduce the notion of Conley predecomposition which allows us to formulate a stability result.
However, the analysis of Morse predecompositions for flows becomes more complicated.
In particular, it is an open question if a counterpart of the consolidation theorem (Theorem
4.13
) holds for flows.

5.1.
Morse predecompositions

Let
\varphi:X\times\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\to X
be a fixed flow on a locally compact metric space
X

and let
S
be a compact, non-empty invariant set with respect to
\varphi
.
Consider closed invariant sets
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
contained in
S
.

Definition 5.1
.

A full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\rightarrow S

is a
link from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
in
S
if
\alpha(\gamma)\cap S_{1}\neq\varnothing\neq\omega(\gamma)\cap S_{2}
.
Link
\gamma
is
homoclinic
if
S_{1}=S_{2}
. Otherwise it is
heteroclinic
.
Link
\gamma
is a
connection from
S_{1}
to
S_{2}
if
\alpha(\gamma)\subset S_{1}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset S_{2}
.
A homoclinic link
\gamma
is
trivial
if
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset S_{1}=S_{2}
.
Otherwise it is
non-trivial
.
Clearly, a trivial link is a homoclinic connection. However, note that not every homoclinic connection is a trivial link.

Observe that a link need not be a connection. However, since
S
is compact, limit sets of full solutions in
S
are always non-empty
and, in consequence, every connection is a link.

Definition 5.2
.

By a
Morse predecomposition of

S
we mean
an indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
of mutually disjoint closed, invariant subsets of
S

such that every full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\rightarrow S
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
.
We refer to the sets in
\mathcal{M}
as
pre-Morse
sets.

Proposition 5.3
.

An indexed family
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
of mutually disjoint closed, invariant subsets of
S

is a Morse predecomposition of
S
if and only if
we have
T\cap\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}\neq\varnothing
for every non-empty closed invariant set
T\subset S
.

Proof:

Assume
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is a Morse predecomposition of
S
and
T\subset S
is a non-empty invariant set.
Select an
x\in T
. Since
T
is invariant, there is a full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\to T
through
x
.
Let
q\in\mathbb{P}
be such that
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
. Since
\omega(\gamma)\subset\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset\operatorname{cl}T=T
,
we conclude that
\varnothing\neq\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\subset T\cap\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
.

Assume in turn that
T\cap\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}\neq\varnothing
for every non-empty closed invariant set
T\subset S

and consider a full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\to S
.
By Proposition
3.2
sets
\alpha(\gamma)
and
\omega(\gamma)
are closed and invariant.
Hence, we will find some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing
and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
,
which proves that
\gamma
is a link and, therefore,
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition of
S
.
∎

The
digraph induced by Morse predecomposition
\mathcal{M}
has
\mathbb{P}
as its vertex set
and an edge from
p
to
q
if there exists a non-trivial link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
in
S
.
We denote it
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
.

We say that a preorder
\leq
in
\mathbb{P}
is
admissible
if the existence of a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
implies
q\leq p
.
Clearly, the preorder induced by the transpose of
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
is admissible.
We denote it by
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
and we call it the
flow induced preorder
.

A
Conley model of
\mathcal{M}
is a digraph
C=(V,E)
with a map

\nu_{G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}},C}:\text{$\mathcal{M}$}\to V
such that it preserves the graph induced preorders.
Clearly,
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
itself is a Conley model. We call
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
the
flow-defined Conley model
of
\mathcal{M}
.

.

5.2.
Saturated invariant sets and Morse predecompositions.

We say that an invariant subset
T
of an invariant set
S
is
saturated in

if
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset T

for every homoclinic connection
\gamma
from
T
to
T
in
S
.

\mathcal{M}
is

if every
M\in\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
is saturated.

.

Proposition 5.6
.

Given a pre-Morse set
M
in a saturated Morse predecomposition
\mathcal{M}
,
every compact neighborhood
N
of
M
disjoint from all other pre-Morse sets and
such that
M\subset\operatorname{int}N
is an isolating neighborhood isolating
M
.
In particular, every pre-Morse set in a saturated Morse predecomposition is an isolated invariant set.

Proof:

Fix a pre-Morse set
M
in
\mathcal{M}
.
Let
N
be a compact neighborhood of
M
which is disjoint from all other pre-Morse sets in
\mathcal{M}
.
We claim that
\operatorname{Inv}N=M
. Clearly,
M\subset\operatorname{Inv}N
. To see the opposite inclusion assume to the contrary that there exists an
x\in\operatorname{Inv}N\setminus M
.
Then
x\in S\setminus\bigcup\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
and by the definition of Morse predecomposition we can find
M_{p},M_{q}\in\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
such that

\alpha(x)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing
, and
\omega(x)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
.
Since
x\in\operatorname{Inv}N
, we have
\varphi(x,\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$})\subset N
and in consequence,
\alpha(x)\cup\omega(x)\subset N
.
This implies
M_{p}=M=M_{q}
, because
N
is disjoint from pre-Morse sets other than
M
.
If
M
is empty then by Proposition
3.2
we get a contradiction.
Otherwise, since every pre-Morse set in
\mathcal{M}
is saturated, we conclude that
x\in M
.
This is a contradiction proving that
N
is an isolating neighborhood isolating
M
.
∎

Example 5.9
.

Take as the phase space
X
a torus represented as the quotient of the square
[0,1]\times[0,1]
in the complex plane through the relation identifying its left edge with the right edge and its bottom edge with the top edge.
We can identify the equivalence classes with points in
[0,1)\times[0,1)
.
Consider the flow
\varphi
on
X
(see Figure
8
left) induced by the vector field

v:X\ni z\rightarrow k(z)w(z)\in X

where
w(z):=1+\theta i
with
\theta
an irrational number and

k(z):=\min\{|z^{\prime}-z|\mid z\in\{A,B,C,D\}
where

A=\frac{i}{8}
,
B=\frac{3i}{8}
,
C=\frac{5i}{8}
, and
D=\frac{7i}{8}
.
Observe that
v
is a continuous vector field with four stationary points
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
.
To understand the features of full solutions of
\varphi
it is convenient to look into the flow
\bar{\varphi}

on
X
induced by
w
.
This flow may be considered a suspension of an irrational rotation on a circle.
It is well known (for instance see
[
31
, Theorem 3.2.3]
)
that trajectories of an irrational rotation are dense. In consequence, the alpha and omega limit sets
of a full solution of
\bar{\varphi}
coincide with the torus.
Since vector fields
v
and
w
differ only by a scalar factor, a full solution
\gamma
of
\varphi
is a rescaled
full solution of
\bar{\varphi}
. It follows that an alpha or omega limit set of
\gamma
is either the whole torus
X

or one of the four stationary points.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Morse predecomposition of an invariant set
S

and let
C\subset S
be a closed, connected and chain recurrent subset of

.
If
\mathbb{P}_{C}
admits a minimal element
r
with respect to an admissible preorder
\leq
and
M_{r}
is saturated in

,
then
C\subset M_{r}
. In particular,
\mathbb{P}_{C}=\{r\}
. Moreover, the conclusion follows if
\mathcal{M}
allows for an admissible partial order,
because every finite set has a minimal element with respect to a poset.

Proof:

Assume to the contrary that the inclusion
C\subset M_{r}
does not hold.
Then
A:=C\cap M_{r}\varsubsetneq C
.
We claim that
A
is an isolated invariant subset of
C
.
Clearly,
A=C\cap M_{r}
is invariant as an intersection of invariant sets.
To see that

is isolated in

, an isolating neighborhood for
M_{r}
.
We have

\operatorname{Inv}(C\cap N)\subset\operatorname{Inv}C\cap\operatorname{Inv}N=C\cap M_{r}=A

which shows that

is an isolating neighborhood for
A
in
C
.
This proves that
A
is an isolated invariant set in
C
.
By Theorem

we can find an

such that
\alpha(x)\subset A\subset M_{r}
.
We get
\omega(x)\subset C
, because

is invariant and closed.
By the definition of Morse predecomposition there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
, such that
\alpha(x)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing

and
\omega(x)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
.
Since
\alpha(x)\subset M_{r}
and pre-Morse sets are mutually disjoint, we must have

.
We also get

Since

and

.
Hence,

, because

\mathcal{M}
are mutually disjoint, we also get

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Morse predecomposition.

\mathcal{M}
is saturated if and only if for every non-trivial link
\gamma

there are
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
such that
p\neq q
and
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
.

Proof:
Assume
\mathcal{M}
is a saturated Morse predecomposition and
\gamma
is a non-trivial link.
Then, by the definition of Morse predecomposition we know that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\neq\varnothing\neq\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}
.
We claim that there is no
r\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}=\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}=\{r\}
.
Indeed, if such an
r\in\mathbb{P}
exists, then by Lemma
5.10
we get

\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{r}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{r}
.
Since
M_{r}
is saturated we conclude that
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{r}
, which contradicts the non-triviality of
\gamma
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\cup\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}
contains at least two different elements, among which we can find
p
and
q
such that
\gamma
is a link from a
p
to a
q
and
p\neq q
.

To prove the opposite implication suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is not saturated.
It means that we can find
r\in\mathbb{P}
with a full solution
\gamma
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cup\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{r}
and
\operatorname{im}\gamma\not\subset M_{r}
.
It follows that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\cup\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}=\{r\}
.
On the other hand, assumptions implies that
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
for some
p\neq q
in
\mathbb{P}
.
Therefore,
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\cup\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}
contains at least two elements, which gives a contradiction.
∎

5.3.
Morse decompositions

We now present a definition of Morse decomposition for flows formulated in terms of Morse predecomposition.
However, by Theorem
5.13
below, this definition is equivalent to the classical definition (Definition
3.11
).

Definition 5.12
.

A Morse predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is called a
Morse decomposition
if it is saturated
and among admissible preorders there is a partial order.
Then, by Proposition
2.1
, the flow induced preorder is a partial order.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
be an indexed family of mutually disjoint subsets of
S
.
Then, the following conditions are mutually equivalent.

(i)

The family
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse decomposition of
\mathcal{S}
.

(ii)

Each
M_{p}
is a closed, saturated invariant subset of
S
and

\mathbb{P}
admits a partial order
\leq
such that for every full solution
\gamma
in
S
there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

satisfying
p\geq q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.

(iii)

Each
M_{p}
is a closed, isolated invariant subset of
S
and

\mathbb{P}
admits a partial order
\leq
such that for every full solution
\gamma
in
S

through an
x\not\in\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}

there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.

In particular, every Morse set in a Morse decomposition is saturated and isolated.

Proof:
Assume (i). Then
\mathcal{M}
is a saturated Morse predecomposition and the flow induced preorder
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
is a partial order.
In particular, each
M_{p}
is closed, invariant and saturated.
We claim that the partial order
\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
fulfills the requirements in condition (ii).
To see this consider a full solution
\gamma
in
S
.
By the definition of Morse predecomposition there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing

and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
. By the definition of flow induced preorder we get
p\leq_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}q

and from Lemma
5.10
we conclude that
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
Hence, (ii) follows from (i).

Assume in turn (ii). Then, obviously, each
M_{p}
is a closed, invariant subset of
S
.
By Proposition
5.6
each
M_{p}
is also isolated.
We claim that the partial order
\leq
given in (ii) fulfills the requirements in condition (iii).
To see this consider a full solution
\gamma
in
S
through an
x\not\in\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
.
By (ii) there exist
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
satisfying
p\geq q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
We cannot have
p=q
, because then
M_{p}=M_{q}
is not saturated, contradicting (ii). Therefore,
p>q
, proving (iii).

Finally, assume (iii) and consider a full solution
\gamma
in
S
.
In order to prove that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition, we have to show that
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
.
Consider first the case when
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{r}\mid r\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
.
Since
\operatorname{im}\gamma
is connected and sets
M_{r}
are compact and mutually disjoint, we must have
\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{r}
for some
r\in\mathbb{P}
.
Then
\alpha(\gamma)\cup\omega(\gamma)\subset\operatorname{cl}\operatorname{im}\gamma\subset M_{r}
. Therefore,
\gamma
is a link from
M_{r}
to
M_{r}
.
Consider now the case when
\operatorname{im}\gamma\not\subset\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{r}\mid r\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
.
Then there is an
x\in\operatorname{im}\gamma\setminus\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{r}\mid r\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
and we get from (iii)
the existence of
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
In particular,
\gamma
is a link from
M_{p}
to
M_{q}
. This completes the proof that
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse predecomposition.
Clearly,
\leq
is then an admissible partial order for
\mathcal{M}
. We still need to prove that each
M_{r}\in\text{$\mathcal{M}$}
is saturated.
To see this consider a full solution
\gamma
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cup\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{r}
.
If
\operatorname{im}\gamma\not\subset M_{r}
, then also
\operatorname{im}\gamma\not\subset\bigcup\mbox{$\{\,M_{r}\mid r\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
and we get from (iii)
the existence of
p,q\in\mathbb{P}
satisfying
p>q
,
\alpha(\gamma)\subset M_{p}
and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
However, Morse sets are mutually disjoint and limit sets are non-empty. Therefore
p=r=q
, a contradiction
proving that
M_{r}
is saturated.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

Assume
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
is a saturated Morse predecomposition of
S
.
If there is a partial order among the admissible preorders, then
\mathcal{M}
is a Morse decomposition.

Proof:

Assume
\leq
is an admissible partial order for
\mathcal{M}
.
Consider a non-trivial link
\gamma
.
By Proposition

p,q\in\mathbb{P}
such that

q<p
and
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing\neq\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}

By Proposition
3.2

\alpha(\gamma)
and
\omega(\gamma)
are invariant, chain recurrent and connected.
It follows that
\mathbb{P}_{\alpha(\gamma)}\neq\varnothing\neq\mathbb{P}_{\omega(\gamma)}

From Lemma

and
\omega(\gamma)\subset M_{q}
.
This proves that

is actually a Morse decomposition.

5.4.
Conley predecompositions

A set
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is
right infinite
if

\sup\mbox{$\{\,b-a\mid a,b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+},\;[a,b]\subset A\,\}$}=\infty.

Similarly,
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is
left infinite
if

\sup\mbox{$\{\,b-a\mid a,b\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{-},\;[a,b]\subset A\,\}$}=\infty.

We say that
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is
bi-infinite
if it is both left and right infinite.

Definition 5.15
.

A finite collection
\text{$\mathcal{N}$}=\{N_{v}\mid v\in\mathbb{V}\}
of mutually disjoint isolating neighborhoods in
X

is called a
Conley predecomposition of

S

if for every
x\in X
,
\{\,t\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\mid\varphi(t,x)\in\bigcup\{\operatorname{int}N\mid N\in\text{$\mathcal{N}$}\}\,\}
is bi-infinite.

The
associated digraph

G_{\text{$\mathcal{N}$}}
has
\mathbb{V}
as its set of vertices and an edge from
v
to
w
if there is a partial solution
\rho:[a,b]\rightarrow S
satisfying
\gamma(a)\in\operatorname{int}N_{v}
and
\gamma(b)\in\operatorname{int}N_{w}
.

A Conley predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{N}$}=\{N_{v}\mid v\in\mathbb{V}\}
is
inscribed
in Conley predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{N}$}^{\prime}=\{N^{\prime}_{w}\mid w\in\mbox{$\mathbb{W}$}\}

if for every
v\in\mathbb{V}
there exists a
\nu(v):=w\in W
such that
N_{v}\subset N^{\prime}_{w}
.
Then, we have a well defined map preserving the digraph induced preorders

\nu:\mathbb{V}\rightarrow\mbox{$\mathbb{W}$}
.

We say that a Conley predecomposition
\mathcal{N}
is an
approximation
of a Morse predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
if for every
p\in\mathbb{P}
there is a
\mu(p):=v\in\mathbb{V}
such that
\operatorname{Inv}N_{v}=M_{p}
.
Clearly, such a
v
is uniquely determined by
p
, because if
M_{p}=\operatorname{Inv}N_{v_{i}}
for
i=1,2
then
\varnothing\neq M_{p}=\operatorname{Inv}N_{v_{i}}\subset\operatorname{int}N_{v_{i}}
, which gives
v_{1}=v_{2}
.
Therefore, we have a well defined map
\mu:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}
.

Proposition 5.16
.

Let
\text{$\mathcal{N}$}=\{N_{v}\mid v\in\mathbb{V}\}
be a Conley predecomposition approximating Morse predecomposition
\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\{M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\}
.
Then
G_{\text{$\mathcal{N}$}}
is a Conley model for
\mathcal{M}
with map
\mu:\mathbb{P}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}
preserving the digraph induced orders given by
\mu(p):=v
, where
\operatorname{Inv}N_{v}=M_{p}
.

Proof:

To see that
\mu
is injective, observe that
\mu(p)=\mu(q)
for some
p,q\in\mathbb{P}

implies
M_{p}=\operatorname{Inv}N_{\mu(p)}=\operatorname{Inv}N_{\mu(q)}=M_{q}
which gives
p=q
.
If
(p,q)
is an edge in
G_{\text{$\mathcal{M}$}}
, then there is a full solution
\gamma:\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}\to S
such that
\alpha(\gamma)\cap M_{p}\neq\varnothing

and
\omega(\gamma)\cap M_{q}\neq\varnothing
. Hence, we can find
t,s\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
,
t<s
such that
\gamma(t)\in\operatorname{int}N_{\mu(p)}
and
\gamma(s)\in\operatorname{int}N_{\mu(q)}
.
This shows that there is also an edge in
G_{\text{$\mathcal{N}$}}
from
\mu(p)
to
\mu(q)
, which proves that
\mu
induces a relation preserving map.
∎

5.5.
Asymptotic characterization of Morse predecompositions

In this section we study Morse predecompositions whose pre-Morse sets are isolated invariant sets.
In such a case we provide theorems which characterize Morse predecomposition in terms of the asymptotic behavior
of trajectories in isolating neighborhoods surrounding the isolated invariant sets.

Proposition 5.20
.

A subset
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is right infinite if and only if

(6)

\forall K>0\;\exists a\geq K\;\;[a,a+K]\subset A.

A subset
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is left infinite if and only if

\forall K>0\;\exists a\leq-K\;\;[a-K,a]\subset A.

Proof:
Obviously condition (
6
) implies that
A
is right infinite.
To see the opposite implication assume
A\subset\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}
is right infinite.
Fix a
K>0
.
We can find
c,d\in\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+}
such that
[c,d]\subset A
and
d-c\geq 2K
.
Let
a:=\max(K,c)
.
Then
K\leq a\leq K+c
and
a+K\leq c+2K\leq d
.
Therefore,
[a,a+K]\subset[c,d]\subset A
, which proves (
6
).
The proof of the remaining assertion is analogous.
∎

Let
N
be an isolating neighborhood and let
\gamma
be a full solution.
We say that
\gamma
that
wades through
N
in plus (minus) infinity
if there exists sequences
t^{-},t^{+}:\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}\to\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+}

such that
t^{-}
,
t^{+}
as well as
t^{+}-t^{-}
tend to plus (minus) infinity and for all
n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}

(7)

\gamma([t^{-}_{n},t^{+}_{n}])\subset N.

If
\gamma
wades through
N
in plus (minus) infinity we write
\gamma\xrightarrow{+}N
(
\gamma\xrightarrow{-}N
).

Proposition 5.21
.

Assume
N
is an isolating neighborhood and
\gamma
is a full solution.
Then the following conditions are mutually equivalent.

(i)

The preimage
\gamma^{-1}(N)
is right (respectively left) infinite.

(ii)

Solution
\gamma
wades through
N
in plus (respectively minus) infinity.

(iii)

The intersection of
\operatorname{Inv}N
and
\omega(\gamma)
(respectively
\alpha(\gamma)
) is non-empty.

Proof:
We give the proofs that (i) is equivalent to (ii) in the case when
\gamma^{-1}(N)
is right infinite.
The proof in the other case is analogous.
Assume (i) and set

t^{+}(0):=t^{-}(0):=\inf\mbox{$\{\,s\geq 0\mid\gamma(s)\in N\,\}$}.

Proceeding recursively, assume
t^{+}_{n-1}
and
t^{-}_{n-1}
are already defined for some
n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}
.
Using Proposition
5.20
for
K:=\max(n,t^{+}_{n-1})

select an
a\geq K
such that
[a,a+K]\subset\gamma^{-1}(N)
and set
t^{-}_{n}:=a
,
t^{+}_{n}:=a+K
.
Then
t^{+}_{n}>t^{-}_{n}\geq n
,
t^{+}_{n}-t^{-}_{n}\geq n
and
[t^{-}_{n},t^{+}_{n}]\subset\gamma^{-1}(N)
.
In particular, sequences
t^{-}
,
t^{+}
,
t^{+}-t^{-}
tend to infinity
Hence,
\gamma
wades through
N
in plus infinity, which proves (ii).
Obviously, (ii) implies (i). Hence the proof of the equivalence of (i) and (ii) is completed.

We now give the proofs that (ii) is equivalent to (iii) in the case when
\gamma
wades through
N
in plus infinity.
The proof in the other case is analogous.
Assume (ii). Then we can choose sequences
t^{-},t^{+}:\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}\to\mbox{$\mathbb{R}$}^{+}

such that
t^{-}
,
t^{+}
as well as
t^{+}-t^{-}
tend to plus infinity and (
7
) is satisfied for all
n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}
.
Set
t_{n}:=\frac{t^{-}_{n}+t^{+}_{n}}{2}

x_{n}:=\gamma(t_{n})
and
\gamma_{n}(t):=\gamma(t_{n}+t)

By taking a subsequence, if necessary, we may assume that

\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}=x_{*}

Since
\gamma_{n}([t^{-}_{n}-t_{n},t^{+}_{n}-t_{n}])=\gamma([t^{-}_{n},t^{+}_{n}])\subset N
, we get

.
Hence,
\operatorname{Inv}N\cap\omega(\gamma)\neq\varnothing
, which proves (iii).

Finally assume (iii).
As a consequence of

6
, Theorem 2.1]
,
for each
n\in\mbox{$\mathbb{N}$}
we can choose
U_{n}
, an open neighborhood of
M
such that

u\in U_{n}\;\Rightarrow\;\varphi(u,[-n,n])\subset N.

Since
\omega(\gamma)\cap M\neq\varnothing
, we can choose a

Set
t^{-}_{n}:=T_{n}-n

t^{+}_{n}>t^{-}_{n}>n

that
t^{+}

\varphi(x,[t^{-}_{n},t^{+}_{n}])=\varphi(\varphi(x,T_{n}),[-n,n])=\varphi(u_{n},[-n,n])\subset N,

which proves (ii).

.

\text{$\mathcal{M}$}=\mbox{$\{\,M_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}
of mutually disjoint isolated invariant sets in
S

and assume (i). Since
\mathcal{M}
is finite and its elements are mutually disjoint,
we can choose a family
\text{$\mathcal{N}$}=\mbox{$\{\,N_{p}\mid p\in\mathbb{P}\,\}$}

is a link from

that

and

6.
Concluding remarks

The proposed concept of Morse predecomposition is a natural generalization of Morse decomposition.
It provides insight into the internal structure of Morse sets.
It is defined in the classical setting of flows as well as in the combinatorial setting
of multivector fields. Therefore, it may be a useful tool in the algorithmic analysis of recurrent dynamics.

Several other questions remain to be answered.
A particularly interesting problem concerns the counterpart of Theorem
4.13
for flows,
that is, whether every Morse predecomposition naturally induces a Morse decomposition via consolidation of cliques in the flow-defined Conley model.
The problem is open.

Another important issue which needs to be addressed is the algorithm for the construction of Morse predecompositions in the combinatorial setting.
Theorem 4.1 in
[
11
]
shows that
the algorithm for minimal Morse decompositions of combinatorial multivector fields reduces to the algorithm for strongly connected components in a directed graph.
In the case of predecomposition one would search for possibly small strongly connected subsets of strongly connected components.
This may be achieved by computing the simple cycles in the graph.
However, unlike the decomposition case where every strongly connected component is automatically an isolated invariant set
(see
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
), in the predecomposition case one has to take the smallest locally closed,

\mathcal{V}
-compatible superset of the strongly connected set to guarantee isolation.
Moreover, contrary to Morse decomposition, there may be many different minimal Morse predecompositions in the combinatorial setting (see Example
4.8
).
Therefore, to fully understand the structure of an isolated invariant set algorithm should be able to retrieve all minimal predecompositions.

Finally, it would be interesting to investigate the relation between Morse predecomposition in the combinatorial case and
Morse predecomposition in the classical case in the spirit of the result for Morse decomposition
[
25
]
.

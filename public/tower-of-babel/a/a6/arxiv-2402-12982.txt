Fractional Boundary Value Problems and Elastic Sticky Brownian Motions, I: The half line
Mirko D’Ovidio

Abstract
We extend the results obtained in [ 14 ] by introducing a new class of boundary value problems involving non-local dynamic boundary conditions. We focus on the problem to find a solution to a local problem on a domain Ω \Omega with non-local dynamic conditions on the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . Due to the pioneering nature of the present research, we propose here the apparently simple case of Ω = ( 0 , ∞ ) \Omega=(0,\infty) with boundary { 0 } \{0\} of zero Lebesgue measure. Our results turn out to be instructive for the general case of boundary with positive (finite) Borel measures. Moreover, in our view, we bring new light to dynamic boundary value problems and the probabilistic description of the associated models.



Keywords and phrases:
Sticky Brownian motions, dynamic boundary conditions, fractional boundary value problems, fractional derivatives, time changes

1.3
Motivations

The present work brings new light to the boundary value problems with dynamic conditions. Although the main interest would be apparently given by motions on bounded domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d>1
our problem could be very attractive for a number of applications. Indeed, the study of non-local dynamic problems on higher dimension introduces motions on the lower dimensional space
\partial\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. This is obviously of interest and reasonably leads to pure jump processes on
\partial\Omega
, the associated trace process. This is motivated by our construction given in terms of
X^{+}
, instantaneous reflections may only give holding times (we do not have boundary motions). On the other hand, the fact that we deal with a zero Lebesgue measure boundary, the point
\{0\}
, it does not seem to be restrictive. Indeed, the independent holding times at
\{0\}
(or a general point
x>0
) for a Brownian motion find interesting applications in different fields of applied sciences. We mention the financial models in which investors update their beliefs too slowly or traffic models in which we construct motions on metric graphs with (independent) delay on a vertex. In general, the sticky point for the process
X
becomes a trap point for the process
\bar{X}
. In our analysis, the boundary point
\{0\}
can be regarded as a trap point for the Brownian motion
X^{+}
on
[0,\infty)
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The Caputo-Dzherbashian fractional derivative

For a function
\varphi(\cdot,x):(0,\infty)\mapsto(0,\infty)
,
\forall\,x\in[0,\infty)
we consider the convolution-type operator defined as

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t,x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial s}(s,x)\,(t-s)^{-\alpha}ds

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

We notice that the Caputo-Dzherbashian derivative is well-defined for functions which are exponentially bounded together with their first derivative. In particular,
D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi
is well defined for
\varphi(\cdot,x)\in W^{1,\infty}(0,\infty)
,
\forall\,x\in[0,\infty)
and this ensures existence of the Laplace transform

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t,x)\,dt=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}(t,x)\,dt\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\big(\lambda\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda,x)-u(0,x)\big),\quad\lambda>0

(22)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}(\lambda,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi(t,x)dt,\quad\lambda>0.

Formula (
22
) can be obtained by observing that
D^{\alpha}_{t}
is a convolution operator. Notice that we do not require
\varphi(\cdot,x)\in L^{1}(0,\infty)
for some
x
.

2.2
The process
X

Our discussion is mainly based on the well-known book
[
4
]
and the pioneering work
[
25
]
. Let us consider the natural filtration

\mathbf{E}[f(X_{s})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\mathbf{E}[f(X_{s})|X_{t}]

is written in terms of the multiplicative functional

where the lifetime

well accords with an elastic kill. There exists an independent exponential random variable (with parameter

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[M_{t}|X_{t}]=e^{-(c/\eta)\gamma_{t}}.

(23)

On the other hand, from the sticky condition,

is a Lebesgue measurable set obtained from the holding times of

on

of (identically distributed) independent exponential random variables (with parameter

) for which
\mathbf{P}_{x}(e_{1}>t,X_{e_{1}}\in dy)=e^{-(\sigma/\eta)t}\mathbf{P}_{x}(X_{e_{1}}\in dy)
for

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}(e_{1}>t|X_{e_{1}})=e^{-(\sigma/\eta)t},\quad x\in\partial\Omega.

(24)

As announced we consider
\overline{\Omega}=[0,\infty)
with the boundary point
\{0\}
. Since

is an elastic sticky Brownian motion we have the representation (

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}[f(X_{t})]=\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[f(X^{+}\circ V^{-1}_{t})\exp\left(-c/\sigma\,\gamma^{+}\circ V^{-1}_{t}\right)\right]

(25)

where

\displaystyle V^{-1}_{t}=\inf\{s\geq 0\,:\,V_{s}>t\}

is the inverse of

\displaystyle V_{t}=t+(\eta/\sigma)\gamma^{+}_{t}.

Notice that
V_{t}
and

are both continuous and strictly increasing. Moreover,

\displaystyle V\circ V^{-1}_{t}=t,\quad t\geq 0.

Under the representation (

can be given as the composition (see

]
, formula 18, Section 10)

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}=(\eta/\sigma)\gamma^{+}\circ V^{-1}_{t},\quad t>0.

(26)

We recall the resolvent

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}f(x)=e^{-x\sqrt{\lambda}}\frac{\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\sqrt{\lambda}}f(y)dy+\eta f(0)}{c+\eta\lambda+\sigma\sqrt{\lambda}}+R^{\dagger}_{\lambda}f(x),\quad x\in[0,\infty),\;\lambda>0

where

. For the explicit calculation we refer to

]
and

]

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}(X_{t}\in dy)=p(t,x,y)dy

Theorem 3.1

Proof.

Let us consider the problem (
1.1
). The problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t,x)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t,x),\quad t>0,\,x>0\\
\\
\displaystyle\eta\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t,0)=\sigma\varphi^{\prime}(t,0)-c\,\varphi(t,0),\quad t>0\\
\\
\displaystyle\varphi(0,x)=f(x),\quad x\geq 0,\quad f\in C_{b}[0,\infty)\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution with probabilistic representation

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)=\mathbf{E}_{x}[f(X\circ L_{t})]=\mathbf{E}_{x}[f(X^{+}\circ V^{-1}\circ L_{t})\,M\circ V^{-1}\circ L_{t}].

To prove our statement we first assume that the representation (
37
) holds true. Then we consider the
\lambda
-potential

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi(t,x)dt=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\mathbf{E}_{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda^{\alpha}t}f(X^{+}\circ V^{-1}_{t})M\circ V^{-1}_{t}\,dt\right]=\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}R_{\lambda^{\alpha}}f(x)

where
R_{\lambda}f
has been introduced in (

). Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi(t,x)dt=\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\frac{\sigma\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}}f(y)dy+\eta f(0)}{c+\eta\lambda^{\alpha}+\sigma\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}}=:\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)

We get

\displaystyle\eta\lambda^{\alpha}\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)-\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\eta f(0)=\sigma\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}}f(y)dy-\sigma\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)-c\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)

where

\displaystyle\eta\lambda^{\alpha}\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)-\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\eta f(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\eta D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi(t,0)dt

and

\displaystyle\sigma\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-y\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}}f(y)dy-\sigma\sqrt{\lambda^{\alpha}}\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)-c\widetilde{\varphi}(\lambda,0)

equals

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\left(\sigma\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x}(t,0)-c\,\varphi(t,0)\right)dt.

We conclude that
\varphi=w
, the solution of (
1.1
).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to 0}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi^{\prime\prime}(t,x)dt=

(38)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\left(\lambda^{\alpha}R_{\lambda^{\alpha}}f(0)-f(0)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\dot{\varphi}(t,0)\,dt.

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}\left(\lambda\frac{\lambda^{\alpha}}{\lambda}R_{\lambda^{\alpha}}f(0)-f(0)\right)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\overline{\Pi}(t)\,dt\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}\varphi^{\prime}(t,0)dt\right)

which is the fractional derivative
D^{\alpha}_{t}\varphi
at

\varphi=v
is the solution of (
1.1
).

In both cases we have a continuous inverse of the Laplace transform, then there exist at most one inverse. The uniqueness of the continuous solutions for the two problems says that
w=v
pointwise on
[0,\infty)\times[0,\infty)

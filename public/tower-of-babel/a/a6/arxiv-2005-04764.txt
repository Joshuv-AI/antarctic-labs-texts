Observation of the \PBzs → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PGf \PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf decay


Abstract
Using a data sample of proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV , corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 140 \fbinv collected by the CMS experiment in 2016–2018, the \PBzs → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PGf \PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf decay is observed. Decays into \PJGy ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm \PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm and \PKp ​ \PKm \PKp\PKm are used to reconstruct, respectively, the \HepParticleResonance X3872 \Xspace and \PGf \PGf . The ratio of the product of branching fractions ℬ ⁡ ( \PBzs → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PGf ) ​ ℬ ​ ( \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace → \PJGy ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm ) \mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)\,\mathcal{B}(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm) to the product ℬ ⁡ ( \PBzs → \Pgy ​ \PGf ) ​ ℬ ​ ( \Pgy → \PJGy ​ \PGpp ​ \PGpm ) \mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)\,\mathcal{B}(\Pgy\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm) is measured to be ( 2.21 ± 0.29 ​ \stat ± 0.17 ​ \syst ) % (2.21\pm 0.29\stat\pm 0.17\syst)\% . The ratio ℬ ⁡ ( \PBzs → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PGf ) / ℬ ⁡ ( \PBz → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PKz ) \mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)/\mathcal{B}(\PBz\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PKz) is found to be consistent with one, while the ratio ℬ ⁡ ( \PBzs → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PGf ) / ℬ ⁡ ( \PBp → \HepParticleResonance ​ X ​ 3872 ​ \Xspace ​ \PKp ) \mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)/\mathcal{B}(\PBp\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PKp) is two times smaller. This suggests a difference in the production dynamics of the \HepParticleResonance X3872 \Xspace in \PBz and \PBzs meson decays compared to \PBp . The reported observation may shed new light on the nature of the \HepParticleResonance X3872 \Xspace particle.

This Letter reports the first observation of the

\begin{split}R\equiv\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)\,\mathcal{B}(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)}{\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)\,\mathcal{B}(\Pgy\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)}=\frac{N(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)}{N(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)}\,\frac{\epsilon_{\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf}}{\epsilon_{\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf}}.\end{split}

(1)

In this expression,
N

of signal events in data, and
\epsilon

The
\PJGy

\PGfP
1020 (referred to as
\PGf

reconstructed in the
\PGmp\PGmm
and
\PKp\PKm

The normalization is done via the
\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf
decay, with a subsequent

\Pgy\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
decay.
The similarity of the decay topology of the signal and
normalization channels results in nearly identical kinematics, leading to the
cancellation of many systematic uncertainties in the ratio.

The central feature of the CMS apparatus
[
15
]

is a superconducting solenoid of
6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
Within the solenoid volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead
tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator
hadron calorimeter. Muons are detected in gas-ionization chambers embedded in
the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
The analysis uses proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collision data recorded by the CMS detector during
the LHC Run 2 in 2016–2018 at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
, corresponding to an
integrated luminosity of
140\fbinv
.
Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system
[
16
]
.
The first level (L1), composed of custom hardware processors, uses information from the
calorimeters and muon detectors to select events at a rate of around 100
\unit
kHz within a time
interval of less than 4
\mus
. The L1 trigger used in the analysis requires at least two muons.
The second level, known as the high-level trigger, consists of a farm of processors running
a version of the full event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing that reduces the
event rate to around 1
\unit
kHz before data storage. The high-level trigger algorithm used in the analysis
requires two opposite-sign (OS) muons compatible with the dimuon decay of a
\PJGy
meson at
a significant distance from the beam axis, as well as an additional track with
transverse momentum
\pt>1.2\GeV
, compatible with being produced in the dimuon vertex.

Simulated event samples for the
\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
and
\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf
decays are generated in the analysis.
The
\PYTHIA
8.230 package
[
17
]
is used to simulate the production of the
\PBzs
mesons,
which are subsequently decayed with
\EVTGEN
1.6.0
[
18
]
, where the final-state photon
radiation is included using
\PHOTOS
3.61
[
19
,
20
]
.
Generated events are then passed to a detailed
\GEANTfour
-based
simulation
[
21
]
of the CMS detector, followed by the
same trigger and reconstruction algorithms as used for the collision data.
The simulation includes effects from multiple
\Pp\Pp
interactions in the same
or nearby bunch crossings (pileup) with the multiplicity distribution tuned to match the data.

The event selection begins by requiring two OS muons with
\pt>4\GeV

passing the soft-muon identification criteria
[
22
]

and matching those that triggered the event readout.
The dimuon mass is required to be compatible with the world-average

\PJGy
mass
[
4
]
,
m^{\text{PDG}}_{\PJGy}
.
The
\PBzs\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PKp\PKm\PGpp\PGpm
candidates are obtained by combining the selected
\PJGy
candidate with four high-purity tracks
[
23
]
with a
total charge of zero that are not matched with the selected muons.
At least one of the four tracks is required to have
\pt>1.2\GeV
and a
transverse impact parameter significance greater than 2 to match the trigger requirement.
A kinematic vertex fit that constrains the dimuon invariant mass to
m^{\text{PDG}}_{\PJGy}

is performed on the two muons and four tracks.
From all reconstructed
\Pp\Pp
collision vertices, the primary vertex
is chosen as the one with the smallest pointing angle, as done in
Refs.
[
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
.
The pointing angle is the angle between the
\PBzs
candidate momentum and
the vector joining the primary vertex and the reconstructed
\PBzs
candidate decay vertex.
Signal events are eventually selected based on the corrected
invariant mass
m(\PBzs)=m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PKp\PKm\PGpp\PGpm)-m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)+m^{\text{PDG}}_{\Pgy/\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace}
,
where
m^{\text{PDG}}_{\Pgy}
and
m^{\text{PDG}}_{\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace}
are the world-average

\Pgy
and
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
masses, respectively.
This approach ensures the independence between the reconstructed
\PBzs
and

\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
masses and improves the
\PBzs
mass resolution.

To select the
\PBzs\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PKp\PKm\PGpp\PGpm
candidates,
one must choose which two OS tracks are from kaons, with the other two tracks then
being associated with pions.
Since the decays of interest have narrow intermediate states
\PGf\to\PKp\PKm
and
either
\Pgy
or
\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
, the following criteria are used to assign the tracks
for the selected
\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PKp\PKm\PGpp\PGpm
candidates:

•

3.60<m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)<3.95\GeV

•

1.00<m(\PKp\PKm)<1.04\GeV

•

5.32<m(\PBzs)<5.42\GeV

•

if more than one of the mass assignments passes the three
selections above, the candidate is discarded.

The selected mass windows are wide enough to allow fits to the mass distributions,
while maintaining a selection efficiency above 99%.

The selection criteria are optimized
using the Punzi figure of merit
[
30
]
,
which does not rely on the signal normalization.
Data sidebands are used to estimate the background, and the
\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
simulated
sample is used to measure the signal efficiency.
The resulting selection criteria are as follows:

\pt(\PBzs)>10\GeV
, vertex
\chi^{2}
fit probability

P_{\text{vtx}}(\PBzs)>7\%
,

\pt(\PGppm)>0.7\GeV
,

\text{min}(\pt(\PKpm))>1.5\GeV
,
\text{max}(\pt(\PKpm))>2.2\GeV
,
and the decay length of the
\PBzs
candidate in the transverse plane

L_{xy}(\PBzs)>15\sigma_{L_{xy}}(\PBzs)
, where
\sigma_{L_{xy}}
is the uncertainty in
L_{xy}
.
Additionally, the cosine of the angle between the transverse momentum of the

\PBzs
candidate and the displacement vector must satisfy
\cos(\vec{\pt},\,\vec{L}_{xy})>0.999
,
and the invariant mass of the two pions is required to be above

0.45\,(0.70)\GeV
in the
\Pgy\,(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace)
channel.

The signal yields of the
\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
and
\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf
decays are extracted using
a two-dimensional (2D) maximum likelihood fit to the
m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)
and
m(\PKp\PKm)
distributions for
\PBzs
candidates in the range
5.32<m(\PBzs)<5.42\GeV
.
The numbers of
\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
and
\Pgy\PGf
signal events from the fit are assumed to
come solely from the corresponding
\PBzs
decays. A systematic uncertainty related
to this assumption is evaluated below.

m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)

•

•

•

Each component is a product of two one-dimensional functions.
For the

\PGf

[

The mass resolution is determined from simulated event samples to be about

The background in the
\PKp\PKm

a first-order polynomial:
(m(\PKp\PKm)-x_{0})^{\alpha}{Pol_{1}(m(\PKp\PKm))}

where
x_{0}

\alpha
is a free parameter.
The
\Pgy\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
signal is described with a double-Gaussian (DG) function with all parameters left free.
The background in the
m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)
distribution is modeled with a modified threshold function:

(m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)-y_{0})^{\beta}{Pol_{1}(m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm))}
, where
y_{0}
is the threshold value equal to

m^{\text{PDG}}_{\PJGy}+0.45\GeV
(corresponding to the requirement
m(\PGpp\PGpm)>0.45\GeV
), and

\beta
is a free parameter.

The following parameters are free in the fit:
numbers of events in the four components,
\PGf
and
\Pgy
meson masses,

\Pgy
resolution parameters,
and background parameters of
m(\PKp\PKm)
and
m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)
.
The fitted yield for the
\Pgy+\PGf
component is
N(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)=15\,359\pm 171

The efficiencies for the signal and normalization channels are calculated
using the simulated event samples. The total efficiency includes
the detector acceptance, trigger, and candidate reconstruction efficiencies.
Only the ratio of the efficiencies for the
\Pgy
and
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
decay modes is needed
to calculate the ratio
R
, which eliminates the systematic uncertainties related to
the track and muon reconstruction.
The obtained efficiency ratio is

\epsilon_{\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf}/\epsilon_{\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf}=1.136\pm 0.026
. It is larger than unity due to
a tighter requirement on the dipion mass
m(\PGpp\PGpm)>0.7\GeV
, applied in the
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
channel.
The reported uncertainty is related to the size of the simulated samples.
The simulated event samples are validated by comparing distributions of variables used in
the candidate selection between the background-subtracted data and simulation.
As no significant deviation is found, no additional systematic
uncertainty in the efficiency ratio is assigned.

Several sources of systematic uncertainty in the measured ratio
R
are considered.
To evaluate the systematic uncertainties related to the choice of the fit model,
several alternative functions are tested.
Uncertainties related to the choice of the signal and background models are
calculated separately.

The systematic uncertainty in the modeling of the
\PGf\to\PKp\PKm
signal is estimated by varying the

\PGf
natural width and the
m(\PKp\PKm)
resolution within their uncertainties.
The corresponding changes in the ratio
R
are negligible.
The systematic uncertainty in the
m(\PKp\PKm)
and
m(\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)
background model is estimated
by testing alternative models. Instead of the baseline model,
either a second-order polynomial or a threshold function multiplied
by this polynomial is used.
The systematic uncertainty in the
\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
signal model is estimated by replacing
the DG function with a Student’s t-distribution
[
35
]
or,
for the
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
channel, by conservatively scaling the resolution obtained
in the
\Pgy
channel by the ratio of the resolutions of the two
channels observed in the simulation.

The systematic uncertainty related to the non-
\PBzs
background
is estimated using the
{}_{s}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{lot}
technique to subtract the contributions from
nonresonant
\PKp\PKm
and
\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm
combinations from the
m(\PBzs)
distribution,
as described above and shown in Fig.
3
.
A systematic uncertainty of 1.2% is assigned, based
on the fit results to the background-subtracted
m(\Pgy\PGf)
and
m(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)

distributions.

The uncertainty related to the simulation sample size is 2.2%,
as evaluated above.
Changes in the detector and trigger conditions in the course of the
2016–2018 data taking are shown to have a negligible effect on the
measured ratio, as the signal and normalization
processes are very similar. The ratio
R
is found to be stable
across different years of data taking,
therefore no related systematic uncertainty is assigned.

\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
and
\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf
decays and the corresponding efficiency ratio, the
product of the branching fractions, with respect to that of the
\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf
decay, is
measured to be

R=(2.21\pm 0.29\stat\pm 0.17\syst)\%.

Multiplying the measured ratio
R
by the known branching fractions

\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)

and

\mathcal{B}(\Pgy\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)

[
4
]
, we obtain

\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)\,\mathcal{B}(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)=(4.14\pm 0.54\stat\pm 0.32\syst\pm 0.46\,(\mathcal{B}))\times 10^{-6},

where the last uncertainty is related to the uncertainties in the
aforementioned world-average branching fractions.

This branching fraction product can be compared to similar
ones in
\PBz
and
\PBp
decays
[
4
]
:

\mathcal{B}(\PBz\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PAKz)\mathcal{B}(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)=(4.3\pm 1.3)\times 10^{-6}
and

\mathcal{B}(\PBp\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PKp)\mathcal{B}(\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\to\PJGy\hskip 0.59998pt\PGpp\PGpm)=(8.6\pm 0.8)\times 10^{-6}
.
The measured value for
\PBzs
is consistent with that for
\PBz
but
about two times smaller than the one for
\PBp
:

\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf)}{\mathcal{B}(\PBp\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PKp)}=0.482\pm 0.063\stat\pm 0.037\syst\pm 0.070\,(\mathcal{B}).

This ratio is significantly lower than the corresponding
one for decays to the charmonium state
\Pgy
of

\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\to\Pgy\PGf)/\mathcal{B}(\PBp\to\Pgy\PKp)=0.87\pm 0.10

[
4
]
.
While this work was in the journal review, an explanation of the observed
difference in the decay branching fractions has been proposed
[
36
]

within the tetraquark model of the
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
state.

In summary, using a data sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of

140\fbinv
of proton-proton collisions collected by the CMS experiment
at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
in 2016–2018, the
\PBzs\to\HepParticleResonance{X}{3872}{}{}\Xspace\PGf
decay is observed for
the first time.
The comparison with similar decays of
\PBz
and
\PBp
mesons indicates that the
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
formation in
\PB
meson decays is different from
\Pgy
formation, suggesting that

\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
is not a pure charmonium state, supporting similar conclusions derived
from other experimental
measurements
[
12
,
13
,
11
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
2
]
.
This observation may shed new light on the nature of the
\HepParticleResonance
X3872
\Xspace
particle.

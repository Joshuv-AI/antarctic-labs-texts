Free multiderivations of connected subgraph arrangements
Paul Mücksch, Gerhard Röhrle, Sven Wiesner

Abstract
. Cuntz and Kühne [ 5 ] introduced the class of connected subgraph arrangements 𝒜 G {\mathscr{A}}_{G} , depending on a graph G G , and classified all graphs G G such that the corresponding arrangement 𝒜 G {\mathscr{A}}_{G} is free. We extend their result to the multiarrangement case and classify all graphs G G for which the corresponding arrangement 𝒜 G {\mathscr{A}}_{G} supports some multiplicity μ \mu such that the multiarrangement ( 𝒜 G , μ ) ({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu) is free.



1.
Introduction

The study of modules of logarithmic vector fields tangent to hyperplane arrangements (in the sequel also referred to as derivation modules)
and in particular the question about their freeness over the coordinate ring of the ambient space
is a classic topic at the crossroads
of discrete geometry and combinatorics, commutative algebra and algebraic geometry initiated in seminal work
by K. Saito
[
12
]
and H. Terao
[
13
]
.
Arrangements with free derivation modules are simply called free arrangements.
Subsequently, G. Ziegler
[
19
]
extended the theory by also taking multiplicities into account.
Such multiarrangements naturally arise from arrangement constructions, i.e. restrictions to hyperplanes, and the freeness of their
mulitderivation modules is closely linked to the freeness of the original simple arrangement due to fundamental work by Ziegler
and M. Yoshinaga
[
17
]
.

To decide whether a given (multi-)arrangement is free is a hard problem in general and at its core lies
Terao’s conjecture, asserting that freeness only depends on the underlying combinatorial structure provided by the intersection lattice. To this day the conjecture is wide open.
Therefore, a natural approach in the investigation of such freeness questions is to restrict the view to special
classes of arrangements with additional structure.
A pivotal result along this line is a theorem of Terao stating that all (complex) reflection arrangements are free
[
14
]
.
Regarding multiarrangements, another milestone is also due to Terao
[
15
]
establishing
the freeness of certain natural multiplicities on Coxeter arrangements.
This was subsequently used by Yoshinaga in
[
17
]

to establish a conjecture of Edelman and Reiner about the freeness of certain deformations of Weyl arrangements.

In the recent work
[
5
]
, M. Cuntz and L. Kühne introduce a new class stemming from simple graphs
which are defined as follows.

Definition 1.1

(
[
5
, Def. 1.1]
)
.

Let
G=(N,E)
be an undirected (simple) graph with a set of vertices
N=\{1,\dots,n\}
and a set of edges
E
.
The
connected subgraph arrangement

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
in
V=\mathbb{Q}^{n}
associated to
G
is defined as

{\mathscr{A}}_{G}:=\{H_{I}\mid\emptyset\neq I\subseteq N\text{ if }G[I]\text{ is connected}\},

where
H_{I}
is the hyperplane

H_{I}=\ker\sum_{i\in I}x_{i}

and
G[I]
is the induced subgraph on the vertices
I\subseteq N
.

Cuntz and Kühne gave a complete characterization of free arrangements within this class (see Theorem
2.23
).
In view of their work and because of the importance of the broader perspective on freeness within the framework of multiarrangements,
the natural question arises if one can describe certain free multiplicities extending their classification.
This is the aim of this paper. Our first main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.2

(Corollary
3.6
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. There exists a multiplicity
\mu
such that the connected subgraph multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is free if and only if
G
is
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
, a path-graph, a cycle-graph, an almost-path-graph, or a path-with-triangle-graph (see Definition
2.18

and Figure
1
).

A natural and important question stemming from the study of free multi-Coxeter arrangements by Terao
[
15
]

is about the existence of constant free multiplicities.
Our next result gives the following answer for connected subgraph arrangements.

Theorem 1.3

(Proposition
4.6
and Corollary
4.9
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with at least three vertices and
\mu\equiv c>1
is
a constant multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
.
Then
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is free if and only if
G
is a path-graph
P_{n}
or
G
is the cycle-graph
C_{3}
and
c=3
.

To classify free multiplicities in general is a notoriously hard problem. Complete answers are known only in very
few cases. E.g. characterizing the free multiplicities even on braid arrangements of rank greater than
3

is an unresolved problem, cf.
[
6
]
.
Nonetheless, in our study it turns out that for a certain subarrangement
{\mathscr{D}}
of
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
which is obtained from
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
by deleting
a single hyperplane, the task is feasible. This constitutes our third theorem (Theorem
5.9
) which was previously obtained by M. DiPasquale and M. Wakefield in
[
7
]
by different methods.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
be the multiarrangement

Q(\mathscr{D},\mu)=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}x_{3}^{c}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{e}(x_{2}+x_{3})^{f}

with
a,b,c,d,e,f\geq 1
. Then

(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free if and only if
\mu=(2k,2k,2k,1,1,1)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
where the order of the hyperplanes is as above.

To conclude, our last chief result describes certain families of free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
.

Theorem 1.5

(Proposition
5.13
)
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
be the multiarrangement

Q({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}x_{3}^{c}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{e}(x_{2}+x_{3})^{f}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{g}

with multiplicity
\mu=(a,b,c,d,e,f,g),(d\geq e\geq f)
. Suppose that either

(i)

\mu=(k,k,k,r,1,1,k)
, where
1\leq k\leq 3
and
r\geq 1
, or

(ii)

\mu=(k,k,k,r,1,1,k)
, where
k>3
and
r\geq 2k-5
.

Then
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is free. Moreover, if
r\geq 2k
, then
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is inductively free with exponents
(r,2k+1,2k+1)
.
For
\mu=(3,3,3,1,1,1,3)
,
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is not inductively free.

For the notion of inductive freeness, see Definition
2.12
.

The paper is organized as follows.
In §
2
we review the required notions from the theory of free multiarrangements
and connected subgraph arrangements.
The next section
presents the arguments leading to a proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In §
4
, we investigate the natural question which connected subgraph arrangements support free constant
multiplicities leading to our second main
Theorem
1.3
.
Along the way, in §
4.1
we present a non-computational proof
of the non-freeness of the connected subgraph arrangements stemming from
G_{1}
up to
G_{8}
from Figure
1
. This was derived in

[
5
]
by means of
elaborate computer calculations.
In our final section
we study free multiplicities on two special arrangements,
culminating in Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
.
In the appendices
we list some tables containing data which we computed and utilised for our
proofs.

2.
Recollections and Preliminaries

2.1.
Hyperplane arrangements, multiarrangements and their freeness

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of characteristic
0
and let

V={\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}

be an
\ell
-dimensional
{\mathbb{K}}
-vector space.
A
hyperplane arrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},V)
, where
{\mathscr{A}}
is a finite collection of hyperplanes in
V
.
Usually, we simply write
{\mathscr{A}}
in place of
({\mathscr{A}},V)
.
We write
|{\mathscr{A}}|
for the number of hyperplanes in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
The empty arrangement in
V
is denoted by
\Phi_{\ell}
.

The
lattice

L({\mathscr{A}})
of
{\mathscr{A}}
is the set of subspaces of
V
of
the form
H_{1}\cap\ldots\cap H_{i}
where
\{H_{1},\ldots,H_{i}\}
is a subset
of
{\mathscr{A}}
.
For
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
, we have two associated arrangements,
firstly

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}:=\{H\in{\mathscr{A}}\mid X\subseteq H\}\subseteq{\mathscr{A}}
,
the
localization of
{\mathscr{A}}
at
X
,
and secondly,
the
restriction of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
X
,
({\mathscr{A}}^{X},X)
, where

{\mathscr{A}}^{X}:=\{X\cap H\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\}
.
Note that
V
belongs to
L({\mathscr{A}})

as the intersection of the empty
collection of hyperplanes and
{\mathscr{A}}^{V}={\mathscr{A}}
.
The lattice
L({\mathscr{A}})
is a partially ordered set by reverse inclusion:

X\leq Y
provided
Y\subseteq X
for
X,Y\in L({\mathscr{A}})
. The rank function on
L({\mathscr{A}})
is given by
\operatorname{rank}(X)=\operatorname{codim}X
for
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
and we define
L({\mathscr{A}})_{k}=\{X\in L({\mathscr{A}})\mid\operatorname{rank}(X)=k\}
.

Let
S=S[V]\cong{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}]
be the coordinate ring of the ambient space with its usual grading by polynomial degree:

S=\oplus_{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}}S_{p}
, where
S_{p}
is the
{\mathbb{K}}
-subspace of polynomials of degree
p
and
S_{p}=0
in case
p<0
.

A
multiarrangement
is a pair

({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
consisting of a hyperplane arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
and a

multiplicity
function

\mu:{\mathscr{A}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
associating
to each hyperplane
H
in
{\mathscr{A}}
a non-negative integer
\mu(H)
.
The
order
of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

is defined by

|\mu|:=\sum_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\mu(H)
.
For a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, the underlying
arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is sometimes called the associated

simple
arrangement, and so
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
itself is
simple if and only if
\mu(H)=1
for each
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. We sometimes denote this simple multiplicity by
\mu=\mathbbm{1}
.
For two multiplicities
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
on an arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
we sometimes write
\mu\geq\mu^{\prime}
if
\mu(H)\geq\mu^{\prime}(H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement in
V
and let

X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
. The

localization of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
at
X
is defined to be
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},\mu_{X})
,
where
\mu_{X}=\mu|_{{\mathscr{A}}_{X}}
.

The
defining polynomial

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

of the multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is given by

Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu):=\prod_{H\in{\mathscr{A}}}\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)},

a polynomial of degree
|\mu|
in
S
.

Let
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
be the
S
-module of algebraic
{\mathbb{K}}
-derivations of
S
.
Using the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on
S
,
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
becomes a graded
S
-module.
For
i=1,\ldots,\ell
,
let
D_{i}:=\partial/\partial x_{i}
be the usual derivation of
S
.
Then
D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell}
is an
S
-basis of
{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
.
We say that
\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
is

homogeneous of polynomial degree p

provided

\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}D_{i}
,
where
f_{i}
is either
0
or homogeneous of degree
p

for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
.
In this case we write
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta=p
.

The
module of
{\mathscr{A}}
-derivations
of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is
defined as

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu):=\{\theta\in{\operatorname{Der}}(S)\mid\theta(\alpha_{H})\in\alpha_{H}^{\mu(H)}S\text{ for each }H\in{\mathscr{A}}\}.

We say that
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is
free
if

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is a free
S
-module – a notion introduced by G. Ziegler

[
19
, Def. 6]
, extending K. Saito’s
[
12
]
and H. Terao’s
[
13
]
freeness for simple arrangements.

The derivation module

D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded
S
-module and
thus, if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free, there is a
homogeneous basis
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{\ell}
of
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
.
The multiset of the unique polynomial degrees
\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{i}

forms the set of
exponents
of the free multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)

and is denoted by
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
. Freeness is a local property for multiarrangements.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
2
, Prop. 1.7]
)
.

For
U\subseteq V
a subspace, the localization
({\mathscr{A}}_{U},\mu_{U})
at
U
is free provided
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free.

If
\mu=\mathbbm{1}
, we also write
D({\mathscr{A}})
for
D({\mathscr{A}},\mathbbm{1})
and say that
{\mathscr{A}}
is free
if
({\mathscr{A}},\mathbbm{1})
is.
In any case the Euler derivation,
\theta_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}x_{i}D_{i}
belongs to
D({\mathscr{A}})
and so if
{\mathscr{A}}
is free,
1
is always an exponent of
{\mathscr{A}}
,
[
11
, Prop. 4.27]
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be an arrangement and
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. Define a multiplicity
\kappa
on
{\mathscr{A}}^{H}
by

\kappa(X)=|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|-1,\quad X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H}

and call the pair
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
the
Ziegler restriction
of
{\mathscr{A}}
to
H
. Ziegler showed the following connection between the freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}

a simple arrangement
and the freeness of any of its Ziegler restrictions.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
19
, Thm. 11]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with exponents
(1,e_{2},\dots,e_{\ell})
. Let
H
be a hyperplane in
{\mathscr{A}}
, then
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
is free with exponents
(e_{2},\dots,e_{\ell})
.

Ziegler also generalizes Saito’s criterion to the multiarrangement case.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
19
, Thm. 8]
, Saito’s criterion)
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
and for each
1\leq i\leq\ell
let

\theta_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}p_{ij}D_{j}\in D({\mathscr{A}},\mu).

Then
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\dots,\theta_{\ell}\}
is a basis for
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
if and only if
\det(p_{ij})=c\cdot Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
for some
c\in\mathbb{K}\setminus\{0\}
.

An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
locally free along

H
for some
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
, if
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
is a free arrangement for all subspaces
X
of
V
with
\{0\}\neq X\subset H
. The following result is due to Yoshinaga.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
17
, Thm. 2.2]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is an arrangement in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}
with
\ell>3
.
Then
{\mathscr{A}}
is free with exponents
(1=e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{\ell})
if and only if it is locally free along
H
for some
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
is free with exponents
(e_{2},\dots,e_{\ell})
.

Theorem
2.4

readily implies the following.

Corollary 2.5
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is an arrangement in
{\mathbb{K}}^{\ell}
with
\ell>3
.
Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be locally free along
H\in{\mathscr{A}}
. If
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
fails to be free for some
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
with
X\not\subset H
, then
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
is not free.

Proof.

Suppose
({\mathscr{A}}^{H},\kappa)
is free. Then so is
{\mathscr{A}}
, by Theorem
2.4
, and so is
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}
for any
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})
,
by Theorem
2.1
(with simple multiplicities), a contradiction. ∎

Recall that an
\ell
-arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
with
\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}})=r
is called
generic
if
|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|=k
for all
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})_{k}
and
1\leq k\leq r-1
. An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is called
totally non-free
if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
fails to be free for any multiplicity
\mu\geq\mathbbm{1}
. The following result states that generic arrangements are totally non-free.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
18
, Prop. 4.1]
)
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}
be a generic arrangement in
V
with
\ell=\dim V\geq 3
. If
|{\mathscr{A}}|>\ell
, then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is totally non-free.

The following construction from
[
4
]
leads to
an extension of Terao’s seminal Addition-Deletion Theorem
[
13
]
for free simple arrangements
to multiarrangements.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\neq\Phi_{\ell}
be a multiarrangement. Fix
H_{0}=\ker(\alpha_{0})
in
{\mathscr{A}}
.
We define the
deletion

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and
restriction

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})

of
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
with respect to
H_{0}
as follows.
If
\mu(H_{0})=1
, then set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}

and define
\mu^{\prime}(H)=\mu(H)
for all
H\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}
.
If
\mu(H_{0})>1
, then set
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}={\mathscr{A}}

and define
\mu^{\prime}(H_{0})=\mu(H_{0})-1
and

\mu^{\prime}(H)=\mu(H)
for all
H\neq H_{0}
.

Let
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}=\{H\cap H_{0}\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}\setminus\{H_{0}\}\ \}
.
The
Euler multiplicity

\mu^{*}
of
{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
is defined as follows.
Let
Y\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since the localization
{\mathscr{A}}_{Y}
is of rank
2
, the
multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}}_{Y},\mu_{Y})
is free,

[
19
, Cor. 7]
.
According to

[
4
, Prop. 2.1]
,
the module of derivations

D({\mathscr{A}}_{Y},\mu_{Y})
admits a particular homogeneous basis

\{\theta_{Y},\psi_{Y},D_{3},\ldots,D_{\ell}\}
,
such that
\theta_{Y}\notin\alpha_{0}{\operatorname{Der}}(S)

and
\psi_{Y}\in\alpha_{0}{\operatorname{Der}}(S)
,
where
H_{0}=\ker\alpha_{0}
.
Then on
Y
the Euler multiplicity
\mu^{*}
is defined
to be
\mu^{*}(Y)=\operatorname{pdeg}\theta_{Y}
.

Often,

({\mathscr{A}},\mu),({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})

is referred to as the
triple
of

({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
with respect to
H_{0}
.

The following observation is immediate from Definition
2.7
.

Remark 2.8
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement and
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
and let
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})
be the Euler multiplicity corresponding to
H_{0}
. For
X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime}
the value
\mu^{*}(X)
is simply the common value of the non-zero exponent of
({\mathscr{A}}_{X},\mu_{X})
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime}_{X},\mu_{X}^{\prime})
.

It is useful to be able to determine the Euler multiplicity explicitly in some
relevant instances. For that purpose,
we recall parts of
[
4
, Prop. 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement. Let
X\in{\mathscr{A}}^{H_{0}}
,
m_{0}=\mu(H_{0})
,
k=|{\mathscr{A}}_{X}|
, and
m_{1}=\max\{\mu(H)\mid H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{X}\backslash\{H_{0}\}\}
.

(i)

If
k=2
, then
\mu^{*}(X)=m_{1}
.

(ii)

If
|\mu_{X}|\leq 2k-1
and
m_{0}>1
, then
\mu^{*}(X)=k-1
.

(iii)

If
\mu_{X}\equiv 2
, then
\mu^{*}(X)=k
.

We can now state the general Addition-Deletion Theorem for free multiarrangements.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
4
, Thm. 0.8]

Addition-Deletion Theorem for Multiarrangements)
.

Suppose that
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)\neq\Phi_{\ell}
.
Fix
H_{0}
in
{\mathscr{A}}
and
let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu),({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})
be the triple with respect to
H_{0}
.
Then any two of the following statements imply the third:

(i)

({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1},b_{\ell})
;

(ii)

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1},b_{\ell}-1)
;

(iii)

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{\ell-1})
.

Remark 2.11
.

If we restrict to simple multiplicities, Theorem
2.10

recovers Terao’s original Addition-Deletion Theorem
from
[
13
]
. As in the simple case if
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
are free, then all three statements of Theorem
2.10
hold (see
[
4
, Thm. 0.4]
).

Analogous to the simple case, Theorem
2.10
motivates
the notion of inductive freeness.

Definition 2.12

(
[
4
, Def. 0.9]
)
.

The class
{\mathcal{IFM}}
of
inductively free
multiarrangements
is the smallest class of multiarrangements subject to

(i)

\Phi_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{IFM}}
for each
\ell\geq 0
;

(ii)

for a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, if there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that both

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and
({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})
belong to
{\mathcal{IFM}}
, and
\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime\prime},\mu^{*})\subseteq\exp({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
,
then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
also belongs to
{\mathcal{IFM}}
.

Similarly, we also have the notion of recursive freeness for multiarrangements.

Definition 2.13

(
[
10
, Def. 2.21]
)
.

The class
{\mathcal{RFM}}
of
recursively free
multiarrangements
is the smallest class of multiarrangements subject to

(i)

\Phi_{\ell}\in{\mathcal{RFM}}
for each
\ell\geq 0
;

(ii)

for a multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
, if there exists a hyperplane
H_{0}\in{\mathscr{A}}
such that both

({\mathscr{A}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
and

4.
Constant multiplicities on connected subgraph arrangements

4.1.
Investigating
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

The proof in
[
5
, §6.2]
of the non-freeness of the connected subgraph arrangements stemming from
G_{1}
up to
G_{8}
depends on tedious computer calculations.
In this section, we provide a non-computational, conceptional proof giving a complete list of free constant multiplicities on the family of graphs from Theorem
2.23
, as well as giving a non-computational proof for the non-freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
in the process. Combined with Proposition
3.1
we obtain a proof for the non-freeness of the simple connected subgraph arrangements stemming from
G_{1}
up to
G_{20}
free of any machine calculations. We use the following method for calculating
\LMP(2)
of the connected subgraph arrangements. Then we utilize Lemma
2.32
.

Remark 4.1
.

Algorithm to calculate
\LMP(2)
for a simple
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
:

(1)

Determine the number of all types (i.e. path-graph, almost-path-graph, etc.) of connected
induced subgraphs
G[S]
of
G=(N,E)
, where
S\subseteq N
and
|S|\geq 2
.

(2)

For every subgraph
G[S]
from step
(1)
we determine the number of triples of the form
(G[S],G[I],G[J])
,
where
G[I],G[J]
are connected induced subgraphs of
G[S]
with
I\,\dot{\cup}\,J=S
. Adding up the number of all such triples for all connected induced subgraphs
G^{\prime}
from (1) provides an invariant
\nu_{1}
. Using Lemma
2.20
we see that for a fixed graph
G^{\prime}
this is just the number of rank
2
localizations with
N(G^{\prime})=A_{i_{3}}
, with the notation as in the lemma.

(3)

Calculate
\nu_{2}:=\binom{|{\mathscr{A}}_{G}|}{2}
. If all rank
2
localizations were to consist of exactly two elements, then
\nu_{2}
would be the number of all rank
2
localizations.

(4)

We have
\LMP(2)=\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}
. To see this, a rank
2
localization with two elements is free with exponents
(1,1)
, while a rank
2
localization with three elements is free with exponents
(1,2)
. So if instead of having three rank
2
localizations with two elements we have one rank
2
localization with three elements, then
\LMP(2)
gets decreased by
1
(since we assume for
\nu_{2}
that all localizations contain exactly two elements). Since
\nu_{1}
is the number of rank
2
localizations with three elements,
\nu_{2}-\nu_{1}
coincides with
\LMP(2)
.

We demonstrate how this algorithm works in the cases where
G\in\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
.

Example 4.2
.

We begin with
G=G_{1}
.

•

5\cdot P_{2},2\cdot P_{3},2\cdot C_{3},1\cdot G_{1}

•

G^{\prime}=P_{2}:1\cdot(P_{2},P_{1},P_{1})

G^{\prime}=P_{3}:2\cdot(P_{3},P_{2},P_{1})

G^{\prime}=C_{3}:3\cdot(C_{3},P_{2},P_{1})

G^{\prime}=G_{1}:2\cdot(G_{1},C_{3},P_{1}),2\cdot(G_{1},P_{3},P_{1}),2\cdot(G_{1},P_{2},P_{2})

\nu_{1}=5\cdot 1+2\cdot 2+2\cdot 3+1\cdot 6=21

•

\nu_{2}=\binom{14}{2}=91

•

\LMP(2)=91-21=70

For
G=G_{2}
we get the following result.

•

6\cdot P_{2},4\cdot C_{3},1\cdot G_{1}

•

G^{\prime}=P_{2}:1\cdot(P_{2},P_{1},P_{1})

G^{\prime}=C_{3}:3\cdot(C_{3},P_{2},P_{1})

G^{\prime}=G_{2}:4\cdot(G_{1},C_{3},P_{1}),3\cdot(G_{1},P_{2},P_{2})

\nu_{1}=6\cdot 1+4\cdot 3+1\cdot 7=25

•

\nu_{2}=\binom{15}{2}=105

•

\LMP(2)=105-25=80

In view of Lemma
2.32
,
we derive the non-freeness of
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
and
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
without any machine
calculations as follows.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
G\in\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
, then
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
is not free.

Proof.

Since
|{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}|=14
, a quadruple of potential exponents for
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
has to be
(1,4,4,5)_{\leq}
, or less balanced. Calculating
\GMP(2)
for this tuple gives
\GMP(2)=69
. Since exponents that are less balanced afford a smaller
\GMP(2)
and since
\LMP(2)=70
,
we have
\LMP(2)\neq\GMP(2)
and so
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
is not free, by Lemma
2.32
.

Since
|{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}|=15
, choose potential balanced exponents
(1,4,5,5)_{\leq}
for
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
to maximize
\GMP(2)
. Using this quadruple, we get
\GMP(2)=79
. Since exponents that are less balanced yield a smaller value for
\GMP(2)
and since
\LMP(2)=80
, we have
\LMP(2)\neq\GMP(2)
and so
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
can’t be free, thanks to Lemma
2.32
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Propositions
3.1
and

4.3
combined give a non-computational, conceptional proof for the non-freeness of the simple arrangements
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{i}}
, for
i\in\{1,2,\dots,20\}
.

Next we modify the algorithm from Remark
4.1
to calculate
\LMP(2)
when
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
gets equipped with a constant multiplicity
\mu=c\mathbbm{1}
for

.

Remark 4.5
.

Algorithm to calculate
\LMP(2)
for a constant multiplicity
\mu\equiv c
:

(1)

Determine the number of all types of connected induced subgraphs of
G
with at least
2
vertices (i.e. path-graph, almost-path-graph, etc.).

(2)

For every subgraph
G^{\prime}
from step (1) we determine the number of triples
(G^{\prime},G[I],G[J])
, where
G[I],G[J]
are connected induced subgraphs of
G^{\prime}
with
N(G[I])\cup N(G[J])=N(G^{\prime})
. Let
\nu_{1}
be the sum of the number of all such triples for all connected induced subgraphs
G^{\prime}
from part (1).

(3)

Calculate
\nu_{2}:=\binom{|{\mathscr{A}}_{G}|}{2}
.

(4)

Since
\nu_{1}
is the number of rank
2
localizations with three elements, the number of rank
2
localizations with two elements is equal to
\nu_{3}:=\nu_{2}-3\nu_{1}
, because if there is one rank
2
localization with three elements, then the hyperplanes of this localization could have potentially formed three rank
2
localizations with two elements.

(5)

Using Theorem
2.16
we derive the exponents of all rank
2
localizations with three elements (which depends on the parity of
c
), while localizations of rank
2
with two elements are free with exponents
(c,c)
. We get

\LMP(2)=\begin{cases}\nu_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{3c}{2}\right)^{2}+\nu_{3}c^{2},&\text{ (if }c\text{ is even),}\\
\nu_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{3c-1}{2}\cdot\frac{3c+1}{2}\right)+\nu_{3}c^{2},&\text{ (if }c\text{ is odd).}\end{cases}

We now use Lemma
2.32
to extend Proposition
4.3
to arbitrary constant multiplicities.

Proposition 4.6
.

For
G\in\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
and
\mu\equiv c\geq 1
a constant multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
,
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is not free.

Proof.

The case
c=1
is Proposition
4.3
. So let
c\geq 2
.
From the calculation in the example we know that:

G_{1}:

\nu_{1}=21,\nu_{2}=91
and

G_{2}:

\nu_{1}=25,\nu_{2}=105
, which gives
\nu_{3}=28
for
G_{1}
and
\nu_{3}=30
for
G_{2}
.
So for
G_{1}
we get

\LMP(2)=\begin{cases}21\cdot\left(\frac{3c}{2}\right)^{2}+28c^{2}=75.25c^{2},&\text{if $c$ is even.}\\
21\cdot\left(\frac{3c-1}{2}\cdot\frac{3c+1}{2}\right)+28c^{2}=75.25c^{2}-5.25,&\text{if $c$ is odd,}\end{cases}

and for
G_{2}
we get

\LMP(2)=\begin{cases}25\cdot\left(\frac{3c}{2}\right)^{2}+30c^{2}=86.25c^{2},&\text{if $c$ is even.}\\
25\cdot\left(\frac{3c-1}{2}\cdot\frac{3c+1}{2}\right)+30c^{2})=86.25c^{2}-6.25,&\text{if $c$ is odd.}\end{cases}

If we assume perfectly balanced exponents for
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{1}}
(assuming
14c\equiv 0\mod 4
) we get the set of exponents
(\frac{14c}{4},\frac{14c}{4},\frac{14c}{4},\frac{14c}{4})_{\leq}
which results in
\GMP(2)=73.5c^{2}
.

If we assume perfectly balanced exponents for
{\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}}
(assuming
15c\equiv 0\mod 4
) we get the set of exponents
(\frac{15c}{4},\frac{15c}{4},\frac{15c}{4},\frac{15c}{4})_{\leq}
which results in
\GMP(2)=84.375c^{2}
.

Since

, in any case, we have
\LMP(2)>\GMP(2)
and thus
({\mathscr{A}}_{G_{2}},\mu)
can’t be free, by Lemma

.
∎

4.2.
Constant multiplicities on a free
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}

In this subsection, we inspect free constant multiplicities on a free
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
. We give a complete list when
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is free for
G\in\{P_{n},A_{n,k},\Delta_{n,k},C_{n}\}
and

\mu\equiv c\geq 1
.

First we consider the case
G=P_{n}
.
Terao showed in
[
15
]
that every constant multiplicity on a Coxeter arrangement is free. Since
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{n}}
is the Coxeter arrangement of type

, we derive the freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}_{P_{n}},\mu)

for
\mu\equiv c\geq 1
.

Now suppose that
G\neq P_{n}
. We start with
G=C_{3}
and follow the idea described in Lemma
2.32
during every proof.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
G=C_{3}
and
\mu\equiv c\geq 1
is a constant multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{G}
. Then
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is free if and only if
c\in\{1,3\}
.

Proof.

For the forward implication, using the algorithm above, we get
\nu_{1}=1\cdot 3+3\cdot 1=6,\nu_{2}=\binom{7}{2}=21,\nu_{3}=21-3\cdot 6=3
. This gives

\LMP(2)=\begin{cases}6\cdot\left(\frac{3c}{2}\right)^{2}+3c^{2}=16.5c^{2}&\text{ (if }c\text{ is even), or }\\
6\cdot\left(\frac{3c-1}{2}\cdot\frac{3c+1}{2}\right)+3c^{2}=16.5c^{2}-1.5&\text{ (if }c\text{ is odd).}\end{cases}

Assuming balanced exponents we get

\GMP(2)=\begin{cases}16\frac{1}{3}c^{2},&\text{if }7c\equiv 0\mod 3\text{ or}\\
16\frac{1}{3}c^{2}-\frac{1}{3},&\text{else.}\end{cases}

For
c\geq 4
we get that
\LMP(2)>\GMP(2)
. For
c=3
we get equality and for
c=2
we get
\LMP(2)=66
and depending on whether the assumed exponents are
(3,5,6)_{\leq},(4,4,6)_{\leq}
, or
(4,5,5)_{\leq}
, a value of
63,64
, or
65
, respectively for
\GMP(2)
.
So if
c\not\in\{1,3\}
we have
\LMP(2)>\GMP(2)
and thanks to Lemma
2.32
, we deduce non-freeness.

Next we address the reverse implication.
The freeness of
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mathbbm{1})
follows from Theorem
2.23
.
In Table
3
,
we present an induction table for
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},3\mathbbm{1})
showing that the latter is actually inductively free.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G\in\{C_{n},\Delta_{n,k},A_{n,k}\}
. Suppose
\operatorname{rank}{\mathscr{A}}_{G}\geq 4
and
\mu\equiv c\geq 1
a constant multiplicity on

. Then

is free if and only if

.

First let

. Since

either has

as an induced subgraph or becomes

after contracting edges, we can use Lemma

and Proposition

to focus on the cases where

. If

we know that
({\mathscr{A}}_{G},\mu)
is free. So let

and

.

Calculating

we get
\nu_{1}=1\cdot(4+2)+4\cdot 2+4\cdot 1=18,\nu_{2}=\binom{13}{2}=78,\nu_{3}=78-3\cdot 18=24

Since

consists of

. So

. Using Theorem

, we see that

fails to be free. For a given

(for

. The result follows.

Let
G=\Delta_{3,1}
and note that every

can get transformed into

by contracting edges. If
\mu\equiv 3
we get
\LMP(2)=489
and assuming exponents

we get

. So only the simple multiplicity can be free.

Let

. Let

, then we get

\GMP(2)=\begin{cases}45.375c^{2},&\text{if }11c\equiv 0\mod 4,\\

45.375c^{2}-\frac{3}{8},&\text{else.}\\

Moreover,

(if

Once more we see that

is free if and only if

5.

Since connected subgraph arrangements are locally

to attack the problem of classifying free multiplicities on connected subgraph arrangements and their subarrangements.

We define a subarrangement of

for which we give a complete list of free multiplicities.
This classification was achieved previously by DiPasquale and Wakefield in
[

,
where this arrangement appeared in a different context, using homological algebra methods.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the
deleted
C_{3}
arrangement

\mathscr{D}
as
\mathscr{D}:={\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}\backslash\{H_{123}\}
. The defining polynomial of
\mathscr{D}
is given by

(5.2)

Q(\mathscr{D})=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}(x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{3})(x_{2}+x_{3}).

To prove the freeness of a certain family of multiplicities on
\mathscr{D}
and to identify some free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
at the end of this section, we require the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),\mu)
be the multiarrangement

Q({\mathscr{A}}(B_{2}),\mu)=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{c}(2x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}

with multiplicity
\mu=(a,b,c,d)
.

(1)

If
\mu=(2k,1,2k,1)
, then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free with exponents
(2k+1,2k+1)
.

(2)

If
\mu=(2k+1,1,2k+1,1)
, then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free with exponents
(2k+1,2k+3)
.

(3)

If
\mu=(k,1,k,2k-4)
or
\mu=(k,2k-4,k,1)
, then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free with exponents
(2k-2,2k-1)
.

(4)

If
\mu=(k+1,1,k+1,2k-4)
or
\mu=(k+1,2k-4,k+1,1)
, then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is free with exponents
(2k-1,2k)
.

Proof.

(1) and (2): We give an explicit basis for the module of derivations
D({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
in both cases. We utilize the linear isomorphism from
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
to itself, defined by
x_{1}\mapsto x_{2}
and
x_{2}\mapsto x_{1}-x_{2}
, sending

\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2},2x_{1}+x_{2}\}
to
\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\}
. Thus it is sufficient to calculate a basis for
D(\mathscr{B},\nu)
, where
Q(\mathscr{B},\nu)=x_{1}^{c}x_{2}^{a}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{b}
.

For (1), we have
Q(\mathscr{B},\nu)=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{1}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{1}
and a basis for
D(\mathscr{B},\nu)
is given by

\theta_{1}=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}dx_{1}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2k}dx_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\theta_{2}=x_{1}^{2k+1}dx_{1}+x_{2}^{2k+1}dx_{2}.

For (2), we have
Q(\mathscr{B},\nu)=x_{1}^{2k+1}x_{2}^{2k+1}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{1}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{1}
and a basis for
D(\mathscr{B},\nu)
is given by

\theta_{1}=x_{1}^{2k+1}dx_{1}+x_{2}^{2k+1}dx_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\theta_{2}=(x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2k+1}-x_{2}^{2k+3})dx_{2}.

Verifying that
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in D(\mathscr{B},\nu)
in both cases and checking the determinant condition in Theorem
2.3
(which gives
\det(p_{ij})=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k+2}-x_{1}^{2k+2}x_{2}^{2k}=(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{2})(-x_{1}^{2k})x_{2}^{2k}
in the first case and
\det(p_{ij})=(x_{1}-x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{2})x_{1}^{2k+1}x_{2}^{2k+1}
in the second case) shows that in both cases
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\}
is a basis for
D(\mathscr{B},\nu)
, which completes the proof.

(3) and (4): Start with
\mu^{\prime}=(k,0,k,2k-4)
resp.
\mu^{\prime}=(k+1,0,k+1,2k-4)
and use Theorem
2.16
to see that
({\mathscr{A}},\mu^{\prime})
is free with exponents
(2k-2,2k-2)
resp.
(2k-1,2k-1)
. So
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
has to be free with exponents
(2k-2,2k-1)
resp.
(2k-1,2k)
.

The argument is the same for the other two multiplicities, but we delete
\ker(2x_{1}+x_{2})
instead.
∎

5.1.
Free multiplicities on
\mathscr{D}
.

We first introduce a special family of multiplicities
\mu
such that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is a free multiarrangement. Then we proceed to show that there are no other free multiplicities on
\mathscr{D}
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\mu=(2k,2k,2k,1,1,1)
(for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
) be a multiplicity on
\mathscr{D}
where the order on
\mathscr{D}
is as in (
5.2
). Then
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free with exponents
(2k+1,2k+1,2k+1)
.

Proof.

The subarrangement
\mathscr{B}_{0}
of
\mathscr{D}
defined by
Q(\mathscr{B}_{0},\mu_{0})=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k}x_{3}^{2k}
is boolean and therefore free with exponents
(2k,2k,2k)
. Let
Q(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mu_{1}):=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k}x_{3}^{2k}(x_{1}+x_{2})
and
H_{1}=\ker(x_{1}+x_{2})
. Then we get
\mathscr{B}_{1}^{H_{1}}=\{\ker(x_{1}),\ker(x_{2})\}
. Using Proposition
2.9
, we get
\mu_{1}^{*}=(2k,2k)
for the corresponding Euler-multiplicity. Therefore,
(\mathscr{B}_{1}^{H_{1}},\mu_{1}^{*})
is free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{B}_{1}^{H_{1}},\mu_{1}^{*})=(2k,2k)
.
Using Theorem
2.10
we deduce that
(\mathscr{B}_{1},\mu_{1})
is free with exponents
(2k,2k,2k+1)
.
Now let
Q(\mathscr{B}_{2},\mu_{2}):=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k}x_{3}^{2k}(x_{1}+x_{2})(x_{1}+x_{3})
and
H_{2}=\ker(x_{1}+x_{3})
, then we get
\mathscr{B}_{2}^{H_{2}}=\{\ker(x_{1}),\ker(x_{2}),\ker(x_{1}-x_{2})\}
and Proposition
2.9
gives
\mu_{2}^{*}=(2k,2k,1)
. Therefore,
(\mathscr{B}_{2}^{H_{2}},\mu_{2}^{*})
is free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{B}_{2}^{H_{2}},\mu_{2}^{*})=(2k,2k+1)
, so
(\mathscr{B}_{2},\mu_{2})
is free with exponents
(2k,2k+1,2k+1)
.
Finally, restricting
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
to
H_{3}=\ker(x_{2}+x_{3})
gives
\mathscr{D}^{H_{3}}=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{1}+x_{2},x_{1}-x_{2}\}
with Euler-multiplicity
\mu^{*}=(2k,2k,1,1)
. In the proof of Lemma
5.3
we show that
(\mathscr{D}^{H_{3}},\mu^{*})
is free with exponents
\exp(\mathscr{D}^{H_{3}},\mu^{*})=(2k+1,2k+1)
. Using Theorem
2.10
once more completes the proof.
∎

The proof above actually shows that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is inductively free
with
\mu
as in the statement of the proposition.
Next, we prove that there are no other free multiplicities on
\mathscr{D}
.

5.2.
Non-Free multiplicities on
\mathscr{D}

The following Lemma shows that the non-trivial multiplicities as in Proposition
5.4
all have to coincide.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
Q(\mathscr{D},\mu)=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}x_{3}^{c}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{e}(x_{2}+x_{3})^{f}
with multiplicity
\mu=(a,b,c,d,e,f),a\geq b\geq c
. If
\mu=(even,even,even,1,1,1)
, then

(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free if and only if
a=b=c
.

Proof.

The reverse implication follows from Proposition
5.4
.

For the forward implication assume that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free.
For some
X\in L({\mathscr{A}})_{2}
and the corresponding rank
2
localization
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}\}
or
{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{H_{1},H_{2}\}
which is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{X},\mu_{X})=(d_{1}^{X},d_{2}^{X})
, we write its summand
d_{1}^{X}d_{2}^{X}
in
\LMP(2)
as
[\mu(H_{1}),\mu(H_{2}),\mu(H_{3})]
or
[\mu(H_{1}),\mu(H_{2})]
respectively. Using this notation,
\LMP(2)
of
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
equals

\LMP(2)=[a,b,d]+[a,c,e]+[b,c,f]+[a,f]+[b,e]+[c,d]+[d,e]+[d,f]+[e,f].

With our assumption
d=e=f=1
, we get

\LMP(2)=[a,b,1]+[a,c,1]+[b,c,1]+a+b+c+3,

which (because of
a\geq b\geq c
and Theorem
2.16
) equals

a(b+1)+a(c+1)+b(c+1)+a+b+c+3=3a+2b+c+ab+ac+bc+3.

Define the multiplicity
\mu_{1}=(a,b,c,0,0,0)
on
\mathscr{D}
, then
(\mathscr{D},\mu_{1})
is free with exponents
(a,b,c)
since it is boolean. Assuming that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free, the arrangement has exponents equal to one of
(a+3,b,c)_{\leq},(a+2,b+1,c)_{\leq},\dots,(a,b,c+3)_{\leq}
, thanks to
[
10
, Rem. 2.8]
. Writing
E:=ab+ac+bc
this gives
\GMP(2)
as follows:

Exponents

(a+3,b,c)_{\leq}

(a,b+3,c)_{\leq}

(a,b,c+3)_{\leq}

(a+2,b+1,c)_{\leq}

(a+2,b,c+1)_{\leq}

\GMP(2)

E+3b+3c

E+3a+3c

E+3a+3b

E+a+2b+3c+2

E+a+3b+2c+2

Exponents

(a+1,b+2,c)_{\leq}

(a,b+2,c+1)_{\leq}

(a+1,b,c+2)_{\leq}

(a,b+1,c+2)_{\leq}

(a+1,b+1,c+1)_{\leq}

\GMP(2)

E+2a+b+3c+2

E+3a+b+2c+2

E+2a+3b+c+2

E+3a+2b+c+2

E+2a+2b+2c+3

Since each of
a,b,c
is even by assumption, we get that in all but the last case (where
\exp(\mathscr{D},\mu)=(a+1,b+1,c+1)_{\leq}
),
\GMP(2)
is even. But since

\LMP(2)=E+3a+2b+c+3

is odd, we see that
\LMP(2)\neq\GMP(2)
in those cases. In particular, the arrangement cannot be free because of Theorem
2.28
. So let us assume
\exp(\mathscr{D},\mu)=(a+1,b+1,c+1)_{\leq}
which gives
\GMP(2)=E+2a+2b+2c+3
.
But if we do not have
a=b=c
, then
a>b
or
b>c
. This implies
a>c
and we have

\GMP(2)=2a+2b+2c+E+3<3a+2b+c+E+3=\LMP(2).

So using Theorem
2.28
again, we see that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
cannot be free and we have
a=b=c
.
∎

Theorem
2.25
allows us to focus on the cases where
\mu
does not satisfy (
2.26
) as follows.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\mu
be a multiplicity on
\mathscr{D}
that satisfies (
2.26
). Then
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is not free. In particular,
\mathscr{D}
is not free.

Proof.

By
[
5
, Prop. 3.1]
,
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
is free with exponents
(1,3,3)
. Let
H=H_{123}
and note that
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{H}
is free with exponents
(1,2)
, since
|{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{H}|=3
. According to Theorem
2.10
,

{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}\backslash\{H\}=\mathscr{D}
is not free.
By Theorem
2.25
,
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is free if and only if
{\mathscr{E}}(\mathscr{D},\mu)
free. However,
{\mathscr{E}}(\mathscr{D},\mu)_{X}=\mathscr{D}
for

. So
{\mathscr{E}}(\mathscr{D},\mu)
cannot be free, by Theorem

.
∎

We use a similar argument when (
2.26
) is not satisfied. For this, we define a free extension for the multiarrangement
x^{a}y^{b}(x+y)^{c}
when
(a,b,c)=(even,even,odd)
. With this tool in hand, we construct an extension of
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
which is locally free along
H_{4}
for an arbitrary multiplicity
\mu
. This enables us to use Corollary

to show that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is not free.

Next we consider a class of extensions of a given multiarrangement
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
which generalizes the one from Definition
2.24
.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
be a multiarrangement in

.
Define the extension
{\mathscr{E}}_{\sigma}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
in
\mathbb{K}^{\ell+1}
depending on another fixed multiplicity

Note that
{\mathscr{E}}_{0}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)={\mathscr{E}}({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
as in Definition
2.24
.

We occasionally denote a multiplicity
\mu
by

, likewise for

.
We use this notation in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
Q({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=x_{1}^{m_{1}}x_{2}^{m_{2}}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{m_{3}}

(i)

If
\mu=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})=(even,even,odd)

(ii)

The idea of the proof is to increase the multiplicity of

by

which results in a multiplicity

such that (
2.26

Now we remove a hyperplane

For (i) let

. The extended arrangement

2.10

{\mathscr{A}}_{2}
. Note that the exponents of

\{\ker(x_{2}),\ker(x_{3}),\ker(x_{1}-x_{4}),\ker(x_{2}+x_{3}),\ker(x_{1}+x_{2}-x_{4}),\ker(x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{4})\},

(which is isomorphic to
\mathscr{D}
and therefore non-free) we can apply Corollary
2.5
to show that
(\mathscr{D},\mu)
is not free.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Deleting a hyperplane from
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
results in either
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{3}}={\mathscr{A}}(A_{3})
or in
\mathscr{D}
. All free multiplicities on the reflection arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}(A_{3})
have been classified, see
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
6
]
. Since all free multiplicities are known on both types of deletions on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
, the next step is to classify all free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
itself.

5.3.
Some free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}

In this final section we present some free multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
that we found by starting with a free multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{P_{3}}
or
\mathscr{D}
and adding a hyperplane via Theorem
2.10
to create a free multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
. It turns out that there is only one free multiplicity on
\mathscr{D}
that extends to a free multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
in such a way.

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
H=H_{123}
and

({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)=x_{1}^{2k}x_{2}^{2k}x_{3}^{2k}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{1}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{1}(x_{2}+x_{3})^{1}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{1}.

Then
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is free if and only if
k=1
.

Proof.

Let
H=H_{123}
. Owing to Theorem
5.9
, we see that
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is free with exponents
(2k+1,2k+1,2k+1)
.
Now restrict
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
to
H
and calculate
\mu^{*}
by using Theorem
2.16
on the following rank
2
flats:

\{H_{123},H_{12},H_{3}\},\{H_{123},H_{2},H_{13}\},\{H_{123},H_{1},H_{23}\}.

This gives
\mu^{*}=(2k,2k,2k)
and therefore (again by Theorem
2.16
)
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{H},\mu^{*})
is free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{H},\mu^{*})=(3k,3k)
. So by Remark
2.11

({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is free if and only if
k=1
.
∎

Definition 5.11

(
[
4
, Def. 5.8]
)
.

An arrangement
{\mathscr{A}}
is supersolvable if there exists a filtration

{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}_{r}\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{r-1}\supset\dots\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{1}

such that:

•

\operatorname{rank}({\mathscr{A}}_{i})=i
for
i=1,\dots,r
;

•

for any
H,H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{i}
, there exists some
H^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{i-1}
such that
H\cap H^{\prime}\subset H^{\prime\prime}
.

Theorem 5.12

(
[
4
, Thm. 5.10]
)
.

Suppose
{\mathscr{A}}
is supersolvable with a filtration
{\mathscr{A}}={\mathscr{A}}_{r}\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{r-1}\supset\dots\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{2}\supset{\mathscr{A}}_{1}
and
r\geq 2

as in Definition
5.11
.
Let
\mu
be a multiplicity on
{\mathscr{A}}
and let
\mu_{i}=\mu|_{{\mathscr{A}}_{i}}
. Let
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{2},\mu_{2})=(e_{1},e_{2},0,\dots,0)
. Suppose that for each
H^{\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{d}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{d-1}
,
H^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathscr{A}}_{d-1}

(d=3,\dots,r)
and
X:=H^{\prime}\cap H^{\prime\prime}
, we either have

{\mathscr{A}}_{X}=\{H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}\}

or

\mu(H^{\prime\prime})\geq\left(\sum_{X\subset H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{d}\backslash{\mathscr{A}}_{d-1}}\mu(H)\right)-1.

Then
({\mathscr{A}},\mu)
is inductively free with
\exp({\mathscr{A}},\mu)=(e_{1},e_{2},|\mu_{3}|-|\mu_{2}|,\dots,|\mu_{r}|-|\mu_{r-1}|,0,\dots,0)
.

We now use Theorem
5.12
to derive some
classes of multiplicities on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
be the multiarrangement

Q({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)=x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}x_{3}^{c}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{d}(x_{1}+x_{3})^{e}(x_{2}+x_{3})^{f}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{g}

with multiplicity
\mu=(a,b,c,d,e,f,g),(d\geq e\geq f)
. Suppose that

(i)

\mu=(k,k,k,r,1,1,k)
, where
1\leq k\leq 3
arbitrary and
r\geq 1
, or

(ii)

\mu=(k,k,k,r,1,1,k)
, where
k>3
arbitrary and
r\geq 2k-5
.

Then
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is free. Moreover, if
r\geq 2k
, then
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is inductively free with exponents
(r,2k+1,2k+1)
.
For
\mu=(3,3,3,1,1,1,3)
,
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is not inductively free.

Proof.

Let
\mu(H_{1})=\mu(H_{2})=\mu(H_{3})=\mu(H_{123})=k
and
\mu(H_{12})=r\geq 2k-1,\mu(H_{13})=\mu(H_{23})=1
and fix
H=H_{23}
, so
\mu^{\prime}=(k,k,k,r,1,0,k)
. We choose the following supersolvable filtration for
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
:

\{H_{1}\}\subset\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{12}\}\subset\{H_{1},H_{2},H_{3},H_{12},H_{13},H_{123}\}.

This filtration satisfies the requirements of Theorem
5.12
, because

\mu^{\prime}(H_{1})\geq\mu^{\prime}(H_{3})+\mu^{\prime}(H_{13})-1\iff k\geq k
and
\mu^{\prime}(H_{2})\geq\mu^{\prime}(H_{13})+\mu^{\prime}(H_{123})-1\iff k\geq k
and

\mu^{\prime}(H_{12})\geq\mu^{\prime}(H_{3})+\mu^{\prime}(H_{123})-1\iff 2k-1\geq 2k-1
. So
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
is inductively free with exponents
(r,2k,2k+1)
. Restricting
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
to
H_{23}
gives hyperplanes in correspondence to the rank
2
flats

\{H,H_{2},H_{3}\},\{H,H_{1},H_{123}\},\{H,H_{12}\},\{H,H_{13}\}

with
\mu^{*}=(k,k,r,1)
.
If
r\geq 2k
, then the restriction is free with exponents
(2k+1,r)
since deleting the hyperplane with multiplicity
1
gives a free arrangement with exponents
(2k,r)
and using Theorem
2.10
shows that
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is inductively free with exponents
(r,2k+1,2k+1)
.
Deleting
H_{12}
with Theorem
2.10
and Lemma
5.3
until
r=2k-5
(and we end up at exponents
(2k-1,2k-1,2k-1)
) shows the other claim. In particular: If
r=3
, then
\mu=(3,3,3,1,1,1,3)
and
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is free with exponents
\exp({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)=(5,5,5)
. Restricting to an arbitrary
H\in{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
results in
|({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu^{*})|=8
. So Remark
2.11
shows that
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})
can never be free and therefore
({\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}},\mu)
is neither inductively nor additively free.
∎

It can be shown that
\mu=(3,3,3,1,1,1,3)
is the minimal multiplicity (with respect to
|\mu|
) on
{\mathscr{A}}_{C_{3}}
that is free but not inductively free.

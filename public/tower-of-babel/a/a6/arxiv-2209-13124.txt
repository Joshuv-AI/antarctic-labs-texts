Morse-Novikov numbers, tunnel numbers, and handle numbers of sutured manifolds
Kenneth L. Baker, Fabiola Manjarrez-Gutiérrez

Abstract
. Developed from geometric arguments for bounding the Morse-Novikov number of a link in terms of its tunnel number, we obtain upper and lower bounds on the handle number of a Heegaard splitting of a sutured manifold ( M , γ ) (M,\gamma) in terms of the handle number of its decompositions along a surface representing a given 2nd homology class. Fixing the sutured structure ( M , γ ) (M,\gamma) , this leads us to develop the handle number function h : H 2 ​ ( M , ∂ M , ℝ ) → ℕ h\colon H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{N} which is bounded, constant on rays from the origin, and locally maximal. Furthermore, for an integral class ξ \xi , h ⁡ ( ξ ) = 0 h(\xi)=0 if and only if the decomposition of ( M , γ ) (M,\gamma) along some surface representing ξ \xi is a product manifold.

1.
Introduction

Let
L
be an oriented link in
S^{3}
with Morse-Novikov number
{\rm{MN}}(L)
and tunnel number
{\rm{TN}}(L)
.
Letting
M=S^{3}\backslash\mathcal{N}(L)
be the link exterior,
2{\rm{TN}}(L)+2
counts the minimum number of critical points of a Morse function on
M
for which
\partial M
is the maximal level while

{\rm{MN}}(L)
counts the minimum number of critical points among regular circle valued Morse functions on
M
dual to the meridional class.
In
[
Paj10
]
A. Pajitnov proved that
{\rm{MN}}(L)\leq 2{\rm{TN}}(L)
using a perturbation argument to construct a circle-valued Morse map from a given real valued Morse function on the link exterior.
Discussions about a constructive geometric argument for this upper bound on
{\rm{MN}}(L)
led to developing a lower bound and further generalizations for sutured manifolds.

1.1.
Bounds between linear and circular Heegaard splittings of sutured manifolds

For our constructive geometric arguments, we shift from Morse functions and circular Morse functions to handle decompositions, Heegaard splittings, and circular Heegaard splittings where we count handles instead of critical points.

Given a connected sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
without toroidal sutures, a Heegaard surface is a connected decomposing surface
S
for which
\partial S
is the union of the cores of the annular sutures
A(\gamma)
so that
(M,\gamma)
decomposed along
S
yields a pair of connected compression bodies for which
S
is the “thick” surface and any annular suture is a vertical boundary component. The surface
S
defines a
linear
Heegaard splitting of
(M,\gamma)
.

More generally, for a “thin” decomposing surface
F
for
(M,\gamma)
that meets every toroidal suture, let the “thick” surface
S
be the union of a Heegaard surface for each component of the decomposition of
(M,\gamma)
by
F
. Then together
F
and
S
define a
circular
Heegaard splitting of
(M,\gamma)
. Note that a linear Heegaard splitting is simply a circular Heegaard splitting with no thin surface,
F=\emptyset
.

Dropping
\gamma
from the notation, the
handle number

h(M,F,S)
of the splitting of
(M,\gamma)
with the thin and thick surfaces
F
and
S
counts the minimum number of handles needed to create the compression bodies in the decomposition of
(M,\gamma)
first along
F
and then along
S
. Then we define the handle number
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
of a class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
, to be the minimum of
h(M,F,S)
among circular Heegaard splittings for which
[F]=\xi
. Of course this is only defined for classes
\xi
represented by decomposing surfaces that meet every toroidal suture, and we say such classes are
regular
. This regularity is a kind of “non-zero linking number” condition.

When
(M,\gamma)
has no toroidal sutures, every class is regular. In particular
\xi=0
is a regular class and the linear handle number,
h(M,\gamma,0)
, is realized by a linear Heegaard splitting with
F=\emptyset
.

Moreover, as we require neither the thin surface
F
nor the result of the decomposition of
(M,\gamma)
along
F
to be connected, our circular Heegaard splittings
(M,F,S)
may actually be what are commonly called generalized Heegaard splittings or generalized circular Heegaard splittings according to whether or not the homology class of
F
is trivial. For simplicity, we will just call them all Heegaard splittings, using the adjectives
linear
,
circular
, and
generalized
for emphasis where useful. Indeed, note that a Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
in which
[F]
is a non-primitive homology class is necessarily a generalized circular Heegaard splitting.

Finally, we also define a sort of Euler characteristic complexity
\chi^{h}(M,\gamma,\xi)=\chi^{h}_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)
that measures the minimum “size” of thin surface needed in a Heegaard splitting that realizes the handle number of the class
\xi
.
This complexity arises naturally when we bound the circular handle number from below by the linear handle number.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)

Then for any

we have

h(M,\gamma,\xi)\leq h(M,\gamma,0)-2d\leq h(M,\gamma,\xi)+2\chi^{h}_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)

where
d=0,1,2
depending on whether neither, just one, or both of
R_{+}(\gamma)
and
R_{-}(\gamma)
are empty.

Proof.

Theorem
5.4
develops the first inequality
while
Theorem
6.5
develops the second.
∎

As an application, let
(M,\gamma_{+})
be the sutured manifold where
M=S^{3}\backslash\mathcal{N}(L)
is the exterior of an oriented link
L
in
S^{3}
with
R_{+}(\gamma_{+})=\partial M
. Let
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
be the class of a Seifert surface for

.
For the lower bound on

in this context, let us define the

g_{{\rm{MN}}}(L)
to be the minimum genus among (possibly disconnected) Seifert surfaces

It turns out that in this situation

For an oriented link

If

Proof.

If

and

1.2.
Handle indices

Theorem
6.6
has a somewhat cleaner statement using
handle indices
rather than
handle numbers
. Instead of adding up the handles of all dimensions, the handle index counts the number of
1
and
2
handles minus the number of
0
and
3
handles. Effectively, the handle index
j(M,F,S)
of a splitting is the handle number
h(M,F,S)
minus twice the number of handlebodies in the decomposed manifold. It turns out that at most one
0
–handle and one
3
–handle is used in splittings realizing
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
, corresponding directly to whether
R_{-}(\gamma)=\emptyset
or
R_{+}(\gamma)=\emptyset
; see Lemma
5.2
. Consequently
j(M,\gamma,\xi)=h(M,\gamma,\xi)
for all
\xi\neq 0
while
j(M,\gamma,0)=h(M,\gamma,0)-2d
for
d
as in Theorem
6.6
. Hence Theorem
6.6
may be rewritten as:

Corollary 1.1
.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
is a sutured manifold without toroidal sutures. Then for any
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
we have

j(M,\gamma,\xi)\leq j(M,\gamma,0)\leq j(M,\gamma,\xi)+2\chi^{h}(M,\gamma,\xi)

Written as such, this prompts one to examine the relationships among
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
over all regular classes
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
.

1.3.
A function on homology

The arguments that prove Theorem
6.6
reveal interesting structure of
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
as a function of the regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. Here we fix the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
and write
h(\xi)
for
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
, viewing it as a function on homology.

Theorem
8.3
.

For a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
,
the handle number function

h\colon H_{2}(M,\partial M)\to\mathbb{N}

has the following properties for any regular classes
\alpha,\beta\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
:

(1)

h(\beta)\leq N
for some constant
N
(depending on
(M,\gamma)
),

(2)

h(k\beta)=h(\beta)
for any integer

k>0
, and

(3)

h(\alpha+m\beta)\leq h(\beta)
for sufficiently large
m
.

As the regular classes form a dense open set of
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
as shown in Lemma
8.2
, these properties enable a natural extension to a function on homology with real coefficients.

Theorem
8.4
.

The handle number extends to a function

h\colon H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{N}

that is

(1)

bounded by constant,

(2)

constant on rays from the origin, and

(3)

locally maximal.

As discussed in Section
9.1
, if
(M,\gamma)
is a sutured manifold with
\partial M
toroidal and no annular sutures, then
h=0
exactly on fibered faces of the Thurston norm. More generally, for an integral class
\xi
,
h(\xi)=0
if and only if the decomposition of
(M,\gamma)
along some surface representing
\xi
is a product manifold.

2.
Terminology

Much of this can be found throughout the literature in various forms, though we take some liberty with resetting terminology so as to not be encumbered with modifiers.
We largely follow and broaden the perspective in
[
Bak21
]
based on work of Goda
[
God06
]
.

2.1.
Notations and conventions

All manifolds are assumed to be compact and oriented. All submanifolds are assumed to be properly embedded. If
Y
is a
n
–manifold and
S\subset Y
is a properly embedded submanifold we denote by
\mathcal{N}(S)
an open regular neighborhood around
S
,
and by
\mathcal{N}(\partial S)
the open regular neighborhood
\mathcal{N}(S)\cap\partial Y
of
\partial S
in
\partial Y
.

If the codimension of
S
in
Y
is one, then
\mathcal{N}(S)
is called a (bi)collar neighborhood of
S
and is equivalent to
S\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
, for
\epsilon>0
. The product
S\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]
, for
\epsilon>0
, is called a closed collar neighborhood of
S
, or simply a collar of
S
.

We use
Y\backslash S
to denote
Y-\mathcal{N}(S)
.

2.2.
Sutured manifolds and compression bodies

Sutured manifolds were introduced by Gabai
[
Gab83
]
and provide a convenient framework for discussing compression bodies and various generalizations of Heegaard splittings, e.g.
[
God06
,
Bak21
]

A
sutured manifold
is a compact oriented
3
–manifold
M
with a disjoint pair of subsurfaces
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
of
\partial M
such that

•

the orientation of
R_{+}
is consistent with the boundary orientation of
\partial M
while the orientation of
R_{-}
is reversed,

•

\partial M\backslash(R_{+}\cup R_{-})
is a collection
\gamma
of annuli
A(\gamma)
and tori
T(\gamma)
, collectively called the
sutures
, and

•

each annular suture joins a component of
\partial R_{+}
to a component of
\partial R_{-}
.

Typically, sutured manifolds are written as
(M,\gamma)
where
\gamma
represents the sutured structure. With this sutured structure understood, we also may use
\gamma
to represent the core curves of
A(\gamma)
, oriented to be isotopic to
\partial R_{+}
.
At times it is convenient to use
\partial_{+}M
and
\partial_{-}M
to refer to the surfaces
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
.
(One may also care to use
\partial_{v}M
, for
vertical
boundary), to refer to the annular sutures
A(\gamma)
.)
We may also use variations of
R_{\pm}(M)
or
R_{\pm}(\gamma)
for these surfaces
R_{-}
and
R_{+}
.

Suppose
(S,\partial S)
and
(T,\partial T)
are two embedded oriented surfaces in general position in
(M,\partial M)
. Define
T\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{-30.0}{$\asymp$}}S
to be the
double curve sum of
T
and
S
, i.e., cut and paste along the curves of intersection to get an embedded oriented surface representing the cycle
T+S
. The surface
T\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{-30.0}{$\asymp$}}S
is an embedded oriented surface coinciding with
T\cup S
outside a regular neighborhood of the intersections
T\cap S
. See
[
Sch89
, Definition 1.1]
.

A
decomposing surface

(S,\partial S)\subset(M,\partial M)
is an oriented properly embedded surface which intersects each toroidal suture in coherently oriented parallel essential circles and each annular suture either in circles parallel to the core of the annulus or in essential arcs (not necessarily oriented coherently). There is a natural sutured manifold structure
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
on
M^{\prime}=M\backslash S
, where:

•

\gamma^{\prime}=(\gamma\cap M^{\prime})\cup\mathcal{N}(S_{+}\cap R_{-})\cup\mathcal{N}(S_{-}\cap R_{+})
,

•

R_{+}^{\prime}=((R_{+}\cap M^{\prime})\cup S_{+})-int(A(\gamma^{\prime}))
, and

•

R_{-}^{\prime}=((R_{-}\cap M^{\prime})\cup S_{-})-int(A(\gamma^{\prime}))
.

Here
\mathcal{N}(S)=S\times(-\epsilon,\epsilon)
is a bicollar neighborhood of
S
where

S=S\times\{0\}
and we set
S_{\pm}=S\times\{\mp\epsilon\}
. With this arrangement, the normal of the oriented surface
S_{+}
points out of
M\backslash S
and of
S_{-}
points into
M^{\prime}=M\backslash S
.
We say
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is obtained from a
sutured manifold decomposition
of
(M,\gamma)
along
S
, and we denote this decomposition as
(M,\gamma)\overset{S}{\leadsto}(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
. See Figure
2
.

A
compression body

C
is a special kind of sutured manifold that may be formed from a collar of
R_{-}
and a collection of
0
–handles by attaching
1
–handles. The collar of
R_{-}
is a product
R_{-}\times[-1,1]
of a non-empty and possibly disconnected compact oriented surface
R
. Then
1
–handles are attached to
R_{-}\times\{1\}
and the boundaries of the
0
–handles.
As a sutured manifold, the surface
\partial_{-}C=R_{-}
is identified with
R_{-}\times\{-1\}
, the annular sutures are
\partial_{v}C=\partial R_{-}\times[-1,1]
, and
\partial_{+}C=R_{+}
is what remains of
\partial C
.

A
meridional disk
of a compression body is a compressing disk for
R_{+}
. In the above formulation of compression bodies,
R_{-}
is necessarily incompressible in the compression body. For such a compression body
C
, we may say that
\partial_{-}C=R_{-}
is the
thin boundary
,
\partial_{+}C=R_{+}
is the
thick boundary
, and
\partial_{v}C=A(\gamma)
is the
vertical boundary
.

A
trivial
compression body is a compression body without any handles; hence it is a product. A
product ball
is a trivial compression body homeomorphic to a
3
–ball when the sutured manifold structure is forgotten; it has the form
D^{2}\times[-1,1]
.

A
handlebody
is a connected compression body in which
R_{-}=\emptyset
. The entire boundary is
R_{+}
, a closed oriented surface. Hence, a handlebody may be viewed as a single
0
–handle with
g

1
–handles attached where
g
is the genus of
R_{+}
. We also say
g
is the genus of the handlebody. More generally one may regard a handlebody as a closed regular neighborhood of a connected finite graph in which the vertices give rise to the collection of
0
–handles and the edges determine the
1
–handles.

We may “dualize” a compression body by swapping
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
.
Then a
dualized compression body
may be regarded as a sutured manifold formed from a collar of
R_{-}
by attaching
2
–handles and filling with
3
–handles a collection of resulting spherical boundary components that are disjoint from the vertical boundary. In passing between a compression body and a dualized compression body, duality exchanges the
1
– and
2
–handles, the
0
– and
3
–handles, and the roles of the surfaces
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
. So, dually,
R_{+}
is the thin boundary,
R_{-}
is the thick boundary, and a handlebody is a connected dualized compression body in which
R_{+}=\emptyset
. See Figure
3
.

.

Forgetting about the gradient flows implied by the indices of handles and the (normal) orientations of

\partial_{-}C

2.3.
Handle numbers, Heegaard splittings

The
handle number

h(C)
of a compression body
C
is the minimum number of
0
– and
1
–handles needed to form
C
. The handle number of a handlebody
H
of genus
g
is
h(H)=g+1
.
In the dualized setting, the handle number is the minimum number of
2
– and
3
–handles needed to form the compression body.

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a sutured manifold without toroidal sutures.
A
linear Heegaard splitting
of
M
, denoted
(M,S)
or
(A,B;S)
, is a decomposition of
M
into a compression body
A
and dual compression body
B
along a surface
S=R_{+}(A)=R_{-}(B)
.

Observe that
S
is then a decomposing surface with
\partial S
contained in the annular sutures of
M
.
Then the sutured manifold decomposition
M^{\prime}=M\backslash S
yields a disjoint union of a
compression body
A^{\prime}
and a
dualized compression body
B^{\prime}

where
S_{+}=R_{+}(A^{\prime})
,
S_{-}=R_{-}(B^{\prime})
, and
M=A^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{N}(S)\cup B^{\prime}
.
In particular,
A=A^{\prime}\cup S\times[-\epsilon,0]
and
B=B^{\prime}\cup\times[0,\epsilon]
so that
A\cong A^{\prime}
and
B\cong B^{\prime}
as sutured manifolds.
Note that
R_{-}(M)=R_{-}(A)
and
R_{+}(M)=R_{+}(B)
.
See Figure
4
.

The surface
S
is called a
Heegaard surface
for
(M,\gamma)
. As
S
is the thick boundary of both
A
and
B
, we also refer to
S
as a
thick
surface.
The
Heegaard genus

g(M,\gamma)
of the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
is the minimum genus of its Heegaard surfaces.

Remark 2.2
.

Traditionally, the term
Heegaard splitting
is used for a decomposition of a manifold
M
along a connected surface
S
into either two handlebodies when
\partial M=\emptyset
or two connected compression bodies without vertical boundary when
\partial M\neq\emptyset
. We include such splittings among
linear Heegaard splittings
. Ultimately, we opt to use the term
Heegaard splitting
to encompass a much larger class of splittings.

Remark 2.3
.

Let us restrict attention here to sutured manifolds
(M,\gamma)
without spherical components of
R(\gamma)
.
In
[
Juh06
]
, Juhász introduces
sutured diagrams
which are Heegaard diagrams for sutured manifolds
(M,\gamma)
. Implicitly, these define Heegaard splittings for such sutured manifolds. Conversely, given a Heegaard splitting
(A,B;S)
of a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
, a minimal choice of handle structure on
A
and
B
induces a sutured diagram on
S
. We leave the details of this to the reader as we are not concerning ourselves with sutured diagrams in this article.

The
(linear) handle number

Observe that this is the minimum number of

In the case of a product sutured manifold

where

While we often consider situations in which
M
is connected, note that these definitions all apply when
M
is a disconnected sutured manifold. Indeed, a Heegaard splitting of
M
restricts to a Heegaard splitting on each connected component and its handle number is the sum of the handle numbers of its connected components.
Furthermore, note that the Heegaard surface restricts to a connected Heegaard surface for each connected component of
M
.

Remark 2.4
.

Goda defines the (linear) handle number of a connected sutured manifold
M
in the situation where the surfaces
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
are non-empty and homeomorphic (and have no sphere components). Then for any Heegaard splitting
(A,B;S)
of
M
,
h(A)
is just a count of
1

h(A)=h(B)
. So Goda defines

to be the minimum of

over all Heegaard splittings
[

]
. Our definition takes

to be twice that.

2.4.
Circular Heegaard splittings

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a sutured manifold, possibly with toroidal sutures. A
circular Heegaard splitting
of
M
, or for simplicity just a
Heegaard splitting
of
M
, is a decomposition of
M
along a pair of disjoint properly embedded oriented surfaces
F
and
S
so that

•

F
is a decomposing surface for
M

•

the sutured manifold decomposition of
M
along
F
yields a sutured manifold
M\backslash F
without toroidal sutures, and

•

S
is a Heegaard surface for
M\backslash F
.

This last condition implies that
\partial S
is contained in the union
\gamma\cup\mathcal{N}(\partial F)
of the sutures
\gamma
and a collar neighborhood of
\partial F
in
\partial M
so that
\partial S
is the union of the cores of the annular sutures of
M\backslash F
.
Furthermore, as
M\backslash F
is potentially disconnected, the surface
S
restricts to a Heegaard surface on each component of
M\backslash F
.
We may denote the Heegaard splitting as the triple
(M,F,S)
or the quadruple
(A,B;F,S)
where
(A,B;S)
is a linear Heegaard splitting of
M\backslash F
.
We say
S
and
F
are the
thick
and
thin
surfaces of the splitting as they are respectively the thick and thin boundaries of both
A
and
B
, aside from
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Our definition of
Heegaard splitting
encompasses linear Heegaard splittings as defined above as well as the splittings often called “generalized (linear) Heegaard splittings” or “generalized circular Heegaard splittings”.
Indeed a linear Heegaard splitting is just a Heegaard splitting with
F=\emptyset
.

Observe that in a Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
, we have
[F,\partial F]=[S,\partial S]\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. Furthermore, for each toroidal suture
T
of
\partial M
, each
[\partial F]
and
[\partial S]
must restrict to the same non-zero class in
H_{1}(T)
.
Let us say a class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is
regular
(with respect to the sutured manifold structure) if any surface
F
representing
\xi
meets each toroidal suture.

Note that if
(M,\gamma)
has no toroidal sutures, then every class is regular. However, if
(M,\gamma)
has toroidal sutures, then a regular class is necessarily non-zero.
For the exterior of a knot in
S^{3}
whose boundary is a toroidal suture, then every non-zero class is regular.
For the exterior of a two-component link in
S^{3}
whose boundary is a pair of toroidal sutures, then every non-zero class is regular unless the linking number is zero; in that case each component bounds a Seifert surface disjoint from the other component.

The
handle number
of a (circular) Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)=(A,B;F,S)
is
h(M,F,S)=h(A)+h(B)
.
For a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
and decomposing surface
F
that meets all toroidal sutures,
the
handle number

h(M,F)
is the minimum handle number
h(M,F,S)
among Heegaard splittings of
(M,\gamma)
with thin level
F
. (In other words,
h(M,F)
is the linear handle number of the sutured manifold
M\backslash F
obtained by decomposing along
F
.)
For a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
and regular class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
,
the
handle number

h(M,\gamma,\xi)
is the minimum handle number
h(M,F,S)
among Heegaard splittings of
(M,\gamma)
(or equivalently, the minimum handle number
h(M,F)
among decomposing surfaces) in which
F
represents
\xi
, i.e. in which
[F,\partial F]=\xi
.
We say a surface
F
representing a regular class
\xi

realizes

h(M,\gamma,\xi)
if there is a surface
S
in
M
such that
h(M,\gamma,\xi)=h(M,F,S)
.

Observe that
0
is a regular class for a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
without toroidal sutures. Hence the handle number for the trivial class is the linear handle number
h(M,\gamma,0)
. This is in accordance with Remark
2.5
and Lemma
3.1
below.

2.5.
Product decompositions and handle numbers

Consider a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
. A
product disk
is a properly embedded disk whose boundary is a union of an arc in
R_{+}(\gamma)
, an arc in
R_{-}(\gamma)
, and two spanning arcs of the annular sutures. A
product annulus
is a properly embedded annulus with one boundary component in
R_{+}(\gamma)
and the other in
R_{-}(\gamma)
. A
product decomposition
is a sutured manifold decomposition where the decomposing surface is a union of product disks and product annuli.

The following lemma is well known in the theory of sutured manifold decompositions. We include it without proof.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose
E
and
F
are transversally intersecting decomposing surfaces for
(M,\gamma)

such that in any component of
A(\gamma)
all arcs of
\partial E
and
\partial F
are coherently oriented.
Let
\delta
be the collection of product disks and product annuli induced from the double curve sum.

Then we have the following diagram of sutured manifold decompositions.

Remark 2.7
.

The condition that all arcs of
\partial E
and
\partial F
are coherently oriented in any component of
A(\gamma)
will be called condition (C1) in Section
3.3
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
(M,\gamma)
has no toroidal sutures, and
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
is obtained from
(M,\gamma)
by a product decomposition. Equivalently, suppose
(M,\gamma)
is obtained from
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
by gluing a pair of spanning rectangles in
A(\gamma^{\prime})
or a pair of components of
A(\gamma^{\prime})
.
Then
h(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},0)\geq h(M,\gamma,0)
.

Proof.

We state the proof for a decomposition along a product disk. The case of a product annulus carries through similarly.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a Heegaard surface for
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
that realizes
h(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},0)
, splitting
(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
into compression bodies
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
.
Let
\delta_{\pm}
be two spanning rectangles in
A(\gamma^{\prime})
so that identifying them produces
(M,\gamma)
. Observe that
S^{\prime}
may be taken to intersect each of
\delta_{\pm}
as the core arc of the annular sutures does. The identification of
\delta_{\pm}
may be done to also identify these arcs so that
S^{\prime}
becomes a surface
S
in the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
. Likewise, the identification of
\delta_{\pm}
restricts to an identification of the rectangles
A^{\prime}\cap\delta_{\pm}
of
A^{\prime}
and also of the rectangles
B^{\prime}\cap\delta_{\pm}
of
B^{\prime}
. Consequently these identifications yield sutured manifolds
A
and
B
with
\partial_{+}A=S=\partial_{-}B
that contain product disks, and the decompositions along these product disks yield the compression bodies
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
. By
[
God92
, Lemma 2.4]
,
A
and
B
are compression bodies with
h(A)=h(A^{\prime})
and
h(B)=h(B^{\prime})
. Hence
S
is a Heegaard surface for
(M,\gamma)
with
h(M,S)=h(A)+h(B)=h(A^{\prime})+h(B^{\prime})=h(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},0)
. Since
h(M,\gamma,0)\leq h(M,S)
, we have
h(M,\gamma,0)\leq h(M^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime},0)
.
∎

2.6.
Handle structures, handle counts, and handle indices

Given a handle structure on a manifold
X
, let
\mathcal{H}^{k}(X)
be the collection of its
k
–handles, and let
h^{k}(X)=|\mathcal{H}^{k}(X)|
be the number of
k
–handles.

The
handle number
of a sutured
3
–manifold
M
with a handle structure is
h(M)=h^{0}(M)+h^{1}(M)+h^{2}(M)+h^{3}(M)
.
The
handle index
of a sutured
3
–manifold
M
with a handle structure is
j(M)=(h^{1}(M)-h^{0}(M))+(h^{2}(M)-h^{3}(M))
.

Observe that for a
minimal
handle structure on a connected compression body
A
and a
minimal
handle structure on a connected dualized compression body
B
we have

h^{0}(A)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\partial_{-}A\neq\emptyset\\
1&\mbox{ if }\partial_{-}A=\emptyset\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad h^{3}(B)=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{ if }\partial_{+}B\neq\emptyset\\
1&\mbox{ if }\partial_{+}B=\emptyset\end{cases}

so that

h(A)=\begin{cases}j(A)&\mbox{ if }A\mbox{ is not a handlebody}\\
j(A)+2&\mbox{ if }A\mbox{ is a handlebody}\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad h(B)=\begin{cases}j(B)&\mbox{ if }B\mbox{ is not a handlebody}\\
j(B)+2&\mbox{ if }B\mbox{ is a handlebody}\end{cases}

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a connected sutured manifold without toroidal sutures.
Define
d=d(M,\gamma)
to be
0
,
1
, or
2
depending on whether neither, just one, or both of
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
are empty.

Then for any linear Heegaard splitting of
(M,\gamma)
realizing
h(M,\gamma,0)
with handle structure that is minimal on each compression body, observe that
d=h^{0}(M)+h^{3}(M)
.

More generally, let
(M,F,S)=(F,S;A,B)
be a circular Heegaard splitting of the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
. Since
h(M,F,S)=h(A)+h(B)=h^{0}(A)+h^{1}(A)+h^{2}(B)+h^{3}(B)
, the number
d(M,F,S)=h^{0}(A)+h^{3}(B)
counts the number of handlebodies in the splitting. Then if
(M,F,S)
is a splitting realizing
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
, we may define
d(M,\gamma,\xi)=d(M,F,S)
. Observe that
d(M,\gamma,0)=d(M,\gamma)
as above while
d(M,\gamma,\xi)=0
if
\xi\neq 0
.

3.
Some operations on Heegaard splittings

3.1.
Amalgamations

Consider a circular Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
of
M
into a compression body
A
and dual compression body
B
. Suppose a collection of components
A_{1}
of
A
and
B_{0}
of
B
meet along a collection of components
F_{1}
of
F
(so that
F_{1}=\partial_{-}A_{1}\cap\partial_{+}B_{0}
). If
\partial_{+}A_{1}\cap\partial_{-}B_{0}=\emptyset
then we can
amalgamate

(M,F,S)
along
F_{1}
to produce a new circular Heegaard splitting
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
with
F^{\prime}=F-F_{1}
.

Since we assume
\partial_{+}A_{1}\cap\partial_{-}B_{0}=\emptyset
, the definition of amalgamation along a surface given in
[
Bak21
, Definition 12]
extends to this case where
A_{1}
and
B_{0}
may be disconnected. (As noted in Remark
2.5
, our definition of
circular Heegaard splitting
encompasses splittings that are often called
generalized Heegaard splittings
or
generalized circular splittings
.) See also
[
Sch93
, §2]
and
[
Lac08
, §3]
for other treatments of the amalgamation operation.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is an amalgamation of
(M,F,S)
, then
h(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})\leq h(M,F,S)
.

Proof.

From the definition of amalgamation (see
[
Bak21
, Definition 12]
), the initial handle structure on
(M,F,S)
extends to a handle structure on
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
with the same number of handles of each dimension. Hence we have
h(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})\leq h(M,F,S)
.
(Note that equality does not necessarily hold since it is possible that induced handle structure on
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
now allows for some cancellations.)
∎

Remark 3.2
.

If
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is an amalgamation of
(M,F,S)
along the surface
F_{1}\subset F
, then observe that
[F^{\prime}]+[F_{1}]=[F]
. Hence
[F^{\prime}]=[F]
only if
[F_{1}]=0
.

3.2.
Realization by homologically essential surfaces

A properly embedded surface
F
in a
3
–manifold
M
is
homologically essential
if no non-empty subcollection of its components is null-homologous in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\xi
is a regular class.
If
\xi\neq 0
and
F
realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
, then there is a homologically essential subsurface
\overline{F}\subset F
that also realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
.

If
\xi=0
, then
h(M,\gamma,0)
is realized by a Heegaard splitting
(M,S)
.

Proof.

Suppose
F
realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
with the Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
.
Further suppose
F
contains a non-empty collection of components that is null-homologous. Then
F
contains a minimal such collection
F_{1}
, for which no proper non-empty subcollection is null-homologous. In particular,
F_{1}
is the boundary of a submanifold of
M
.
Then we have collections of components
A_{1}
of
A
and
B_{0}
of
B
for which
F_{1}=\partial_{-}A_{1}\cap\partial_{+}B_{0}
. They have their corresponding collections of components
B_{1}
of
B
and
A_{0}
of
A
where
\partial_{+}A_{i}=\partial_{-}B_{i}=S_{i}
is a collection of components of
S
for
i=0,1
.
Suppose
S_{0}\cap S_{1}\neq\emptyset
so that
S_{0}
and
S_{1}
share a component
S^{*}
. Then there are components
A^{*}
of
A
and
B^{*}
of
B
so that
S^{*}=\partial_{+}A^{*}=\partial_{-}B^{*}
and
F_{1}
has a component in each
\partial_{-}A^{*}
and
\partial_{+}B^{*}
. Thus there is a path in
A^{*}\cup_{S^{*}}B^{*}
from one side of
F_{1}
to the other side of
F_{1}
. However that is contrary to
F_{1}
bounding a submanifold of
M
.
Thus
S_{0}\cap S_{1}=\emptyset
and we may amalgamate
(M,F,S)
along
F_{1}
to obtain the splitting
(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})
where
F^{\prime}=F-F_{1}
. By Lemma
3.1
,
h(M^{\prime},F^{\prime},S^{\prime})\leq h(M,F,S)
. Since
F_{1}
is null-homologous,
[F^{\prime}]=[F]
as noted in Remark
3.2
. Since
F
realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
, the previous inequality is an equality and
F^{\prime}
realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
too. This may be repeated until we are left with a Heegaard splitting
(M,\overline{F},\overline{S})
that also realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
where
\overline{F}\subset F
is a homologically essential subsurface, possibly the empty set.
∎

3.3.
Conditioned splittings

Given a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
, a
conditioned
surface
F
is a decomposing surface of
(M,\gamma)
such that

(C1)

all arcs of
\partial F
in any component of
A(\gamma)
are coherently oriented, and

(C2)

no non-empty collection of simple closed curves
\partial F\cap R(\gamma)
is trivial in
H_{1}(\partial M,\gamma)
.

We further say
F
is
well-conditioned
if

(C3)

F
is homologically essential.

A Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
of
(M,\gamma)
is
conditioned
if
F
is conditioned, and it is
well-conditioned
if
F
is well-conditioned.

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose
\xi
is a regular class for
(M,\gamma)
. Then there is a surface
F
realizing
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
that satisfies condition (C1).

Proof.

Let
F_{0}
be a decomposing surface for
(M,\gamma)
that represents

.
Suppose two adjacent arcs of

Then let

along

defined by alpha and these two arcs. Then

has a boundary compressing disk

that crosses

F_{1}
back into
F_{0}
.
(Note that

also represents
\xi

along

is a product disk. Furthermore, the decomposition along

results in the sutured manifold

obtained from decomposing
(M,\gamma)
along

. Consequently,

can be regarded as being obtained from

by regluing a product disk corresponding to

.

of

such that

of

Lemma 3.7

4.
Restructuring sutures

To get around the issue of non-zero classes that may fail to be regular for link exteriors where the boundary tori are all toroidal sutures, we may instead choose the boundary tori to all belong to
R_{\pm}
.
This latter sutured manifold structure allows the circular handle number to be defined for all non-zero classes by ensuring they are all regular.
Moreover, for regular classes of the former “all toroidal suture” sutured manifold structure, the resulting circular handle number is the same as with this latter “all
R_{\pm}
” structure. More generally, we have the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider sutured manifolds structures
(M,\gamma)
and
(M,\gamma^{*})
which agree on
\partial M\backslash T(\gamma)
and for which
T(\gamma^{*})=\emptyset
.

If
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a regular class for
(M,\gamma)
,
then either

•

,

•

there is a component of

in which
\partial\xi
and
A(\gamma^{*})
have the same slope, or

•

there is a component of
T(\gamma)
in which some component of
R_{\pm}(\gamma^{*})
is a disk.

In particular, if

then

for every regular class of
(M,\gamma)
.

Proof.

Let us assume the last conclusion of the theorem does not occur; that is, in no component of
T(\gamma)
is some component of
R_{\pm}(\gamma^{*})
a disk.
Then this theorem is primarily a consequence of the following observation.
Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a collection of torus boundary components of a
3
–manifold
M
. Let
F
be a properly embedded, oriented surface in
M
whose boundary meets each component of
{\mathcal{T}}
in a non-empty collection of coherently oriented essential simple closed curves. Then if
(M,\gamma)
and
(M,\gamma^{*})
are two sutured manifold structures on

that differ only on

such that

is a decomposing surface for each, the sutured manifolds that result from their decompositions along

of the same slope as the curves of

.

Now, with the hypotheses of the theorem, suppose

is a regular class for
(M,\gamma)
.
Then for every torus

in
T(\gamma)
, the restriction of

\partial\xi
to

is not
0
.
So assume the following:

(
\dagger
)
The slopes of
\partial\xi
and

are distinct in each component of

.

Hence if

is a decomposing surface for

representing

.

On the other hand, suppose

is a splitting of

realizing

By Theorem

5.2.
An Upper Bound

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a connected sutured manifold without toroidal sutures.
For any non-zero class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
, there is a surface
F
representing
\xi
so that
(M,\gamma)
has a Heegaard splitting
(M,F,S)
such that
h(M,F,S)=h(M,\gamma,0)-2d
.
Consequently,

h(M,\gamma,\xi)\leq h(M,\gamma,0)-2d.

Equivalently,

j(M,\gamma,\xi)\leq j(M,\gamma,0).

Recall that
d=0
,
1
, or
2
depending on whether neither, just one, or both of
R_{+}
and
R_{-}
are empty and that
h(M,\gamma,0)
is the linear handle number of
(M,\gamma)
.

Proof.

Since
(M,\gamma)
has no toroidal sutures, every
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is regular.

Let
(M,\Sigma)=(A,B;\Sigma)
be a Heegaard splitting of the sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
. So the splitting has handle number
h(M,\Sigma)=h(A)+h(B)
.

By Theorem
3.6
, there is a conditioned surface
F_{0}
representing
\xi
that realizes
h(M,\gamma,\xi)
. By a small isotopy, one may assume it is transverse to
\Sigma
.

In particular,
\partial F_{0}
intersects
A(\gamma)
in essential simple closed curves and coherently oriented spanning arcs.
Since
\Sigma
is a Heegaard surface,
\partial\Sigma
is the union of the core curves of
A(\gamma)
. So for any given component of
\partial\Sigma
, its intersections with
\partial F_{0}
are all of the same sign. Hence if a non-empty collection of components of
F_{0}\cap\Sigma
is trivial in
H_{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
, the conditioning of
F_{0}
implies that the collection must be disjoint from the boundary.
Thus we may further assume
F_{0}
has been chosen so that no non-empty submanifold of
F_{0}\cap\Sigma
is trivial in
H_{1}(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
. For example, one may modify an initial conditioned surface by taking its double curve sum with a collection of parallel copies of
\Sigma
.

For each
C=A,B
, the restriction of
F_{0}
to
C
is a conditioned surface
F_{0}\cap C
.
Then Theorem
5.3
implies there is a conditioned surface
F_{C}
in
C
homologous to
F_{0}\cap C
with
\partial(F_{0}\cap C)\cap\Sigma=\partial F_{C}\cap\Sigma
so that the sutured manifold decomposition of
C
along
F_{C}
is a compression body

with
h(C^{\prime})=h(C)
or
h(C^{\prime})=h(C)-2
if

is a handlebody. Recall that a connected compression body is a handlebody exactly when
\partial_{-}(C)=\emptyset
.

So then let
F=F_{A}\cup F_{B}
. Observe that by construction
F
is homologous to
F_{0}
so that it represents
\xi
, and moreover
F\cap\Sigma=F_{0}\cap\Sigma
. Let
S
be the surface
F\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{-30.0}{$\asymp$}}\Sigma
pushed off

. Explicitly, we may take a closed collar neighborhood
N_{F}=F\times[-\epsilon,\epsilon]

of
F
so that it intersects
\Sigma
in a collar of
F\cap\Sigma
. Then
S=F_{A}\times\{-\epsilon\}\cup(\Sigma\backslash N_{F})\cup F_{B}\times\{+\epsilon\}
is a properly embedded oriented surface disjoint from
F
. We then claim that
(M,F,S)
is a circular Heegaard splitting with handle number
h(A^{\prime})+h(B^{\prime})
. Here
A^{\prime}
and

are the compression bodies obtained by decomposing
A
and
B
along
F_{A}
and
F_{B}
.

Since
\partial\Sigma
is the collection of core curves of the annular sutures of

, we may regard the compression bodies
A^{\prime}
and
B^{\prime}
as being the components on either side of
\Sigma
in
(M\backslash\Sigma)\backslash N_{F}
or equivalently in
(M\backslash N_{F})\backslash\Sigma
.
Then as
F
splits

into

and

, we observe that

splits

into the two manifolds

and
\overline{B^{\prime}}=B^{\prime}\cup F\times[-\epsilon,0]

.

Let us examine

along the vertical disks and annuli of its intersection with

, we have
\overline{A^{\prime}}=(A^{\prime}\cup F_{A}\times[0,\epsilon])\cup(F_{B}\times[0,\epsilon])

Thus

6.
A lower bound for sutured manifolds without toroidal sutures

6.1.
Sutured Characteristic

A
sutured surface
is a compact oriented surface
F
with a disjoint pair of submanifolds
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
of
\partial F
such that

•

the orientation of
r_{+}
is consistent with the boundary orientation of
\partial F
while the orientation of
r_{-}
is reversed,

•

\partial F\backslash(r_{+}\cup r_{-})
is a collection of intervals and circles, collectively called the
sutures
, and

•

each interval suture joins a component of
\partial r_{+}
to a component of
\partial r_{-}
.

At times it is convenient to use
\partial_{+}F
and
\partial_{-}F
to refer to the
1
–manifolds
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
,
\partial_{v}F
(for
vertical
boundary) to refer to the interval sutures, and
\partial_{0}F
for the circle sutures.

We now define a modified Euler characteristic for sutured surfaces.
When the sutured surface
F
is connected, we define the
sutured characteristic
of
F
to be

\chi_{sut}(F)=-\chi(F)+d(F)+\frac{1}{2}\#\partial_{v}F+\#\partial_{0}F.

where
d(F)=0
if
\partial_{0}F\neq\emptyset
and otherwise counts how many of
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
are empty. That is, if
\partial_{0}F=\emptyset
then
d(F)=0
,
1
, or
2
depending on whether neither, just one, or both of
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
are empty.
When
F
has multiple components, then its sutured characteristic is the sum of
\chi_{sut}
of its components.

A sutured surface
F
without circle sutures can be viewed as a cobordism from
\partial_{-}F
to
\partial_{+}F
, built from attaching
1
–handles to a collar of
\partial_{-}F
and a collection of
0
–handles followed by attaching
2
–handles as needed. Let
h^{i}(F)
is the minimum number of
2
–dimensional
i
–handles used in such a handle structure.

Lemma 6.1
.

For a sutured surface without circle sutures and an associated minimal handle structure

\chi_{sut}(F)=-\chi(F)+h^{0}(F)+h^{2}(F)+\frac{1}{2}\#\partial_{v}F=h^{1}(F).

Proof.

It suffices to prove this lemma for connected
F
.

Since
\partial_{0}F=\emptyset
by assumption,
d(F)
counts how many of
r_{+}
and
r_{-}
are empty. If
r_{-}=\emptyset
, then there must be
0
–handles used in its handle structure. Since
F
is connected, a minimal handle structure has just one
0
–handle. Similarly if
r_{+}=\emptyset
, then a minimal handle structure has just one
2
–handle. If neither of
r_{+}
or
r_{-}
are empty, then only
1
–handles are needed.
This gives the first equality.

Next, observe that any component of
\partial F
contains an even number of interval sutures. One could glue these in pairs to make an oriented surface
{\widehat{F}}
without sutures. Then we may count that

\chi({\widehat{F}})=\chi(F)-\frac{1}{2}\#\partial_{v}F=h^{0}(F)-h^{1}(F)+h^{2}(F),

which implies the second equality.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
F
be a sutured surface.
Then

\chi_{-}(F)\leq\chi_{sut}(F)
.

Proof.

Recall that (i)
\chi_{-}
of a connected surface is just the maximum of
-\chi
and
0
and (ii)
\chi_{-}
is additive on disjoint unions. Therefore we may restrict attention to the case that
F
is connected. If
\chi(F)\leq 0
then the result is immediate since
d(F)
,
\#\partial_{v}F
, and
\#\partial_{0}F
are all non-negative.
If
\chi(F)>0
so that
\chi_{-}(F)=0
, then
F
is either a sphere
S^{2}
or disk
D^{2}
since
F
is orientable. Since
S^{2}
is closed, it has
d=2
. So
\chi_{sut}(S^{2})=0
. For the disk however,
\chi_{sut}
depends on the suture structure. Let
D^{2}_{n}
denote the disk with
2n
interval sutures, for
n\geq 0
. Then one finds that
\chi_{sut}(D^{2}_{0})=0
while
\chi_{sut}(D^{2}_{n})=n-1
if
n\geq 1
. Lastly, a disk whose boundary is a circular suture also has
\chi_{sut}=0
.
∎

Any decomposing surface

F
in a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
inherits the structure of a sutured surface:
r_{\pm}=\partial F\cap R_{\pm}
while the sutures of
F
are the intersections of
\partial F
with the sutures of
(M,\gamma)
.
Thus we may define

\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(F)=\chi_{sut}(F)

where
F
has the induced sutured structure.
Then, for any class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
, we may define its
sutured characteristic
to be

\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)=\min_{F\in\xi}\{\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(F)\}

where the minimum is taken among decomposing surfaces
F
that represent
\xi
.

Remark 6.3
.

Since
\chi_{sut}
is additive on disjoint unions of surfaces, it follows that for any class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)

\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(n\xi)=|n|\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)\mbox{ for all integers }n>0.

Lemma 6.4
.

For a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
, the Thurston norm of a class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a lower bound for its sutured characteristic. That is,
x(\xi)\leq\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)
.

Proof.

Suppose a properly embedded surface
F
in
(M,\gamma)
represents
\xi
so that
\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(\xi)=\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(F)
. Then by Lemma
6.2
,
\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(F)\geq\chi_{-}(F)
. Since
\chi_{-}(F)\geq x([F])=x(\xi)
, the result follows.
∎

6.2.
A Lower Bound

Recall, we say a surface
F
representing a regular class
\xi

realizes

h(M,\gamma,\xi)
if there is a surface
S
in
M
such that
h(M,\gamma,\xi)=h(M,F,S)
.
Then the

handle
sutured characteristic
of a
regular class
\xi
is

\chi_{(M,\gamma)}^{h}(\xi)=\min_{F\in\xi}\{\chi_{(M,\gamma)}(F)|F\mbox{ realizes }h(M,\gamma,\xi)\}.

By Lemma
3.3
,
h(M,\gamma,0)
is realized by a splitting with
F=\emptyset
. So for the trivial class
\xi=0
, we have
\chi_{(M,\gamma)}^{h}(0)=0
since
\chi_{sut}(\emptyset)=0
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(M,\gamma)
a sutured manifold without toroidal sutures.
Then for any

where

depending on whether neither, just one, or both of
R_{+}(\gamma)
and
R_{-}(\gamma)
are empty.

Proof.

Since
\chi_{(M,\gamma)}^{h}(\xi)\geq 0
for all

,
the conclusion is trivially satisfied if

. So assume

.

Given

F
be a decomposing surface representing

and realizing

with

, so that

Recall, we identify

with

and set

points out of

and of
F_{-}
points into

. Next choose the surface
S
so that
(M,F,S)=(A,B;F,S)
a circular Heegaard splitting with handle number

, where:

•

\partial_{-}A=R^{\prime}_{-}=F_{-}\cup(R_{-}\backslash F)

•

•

For each

.

in

has no

that it meets.

Next, consider the manifold

of

.

7.
Bounds for knots and links

7.1.
Tunnel numbers and Morse Novikov numbers of links

Let
M
be a connected, compact oriented
3
–manifold with non-empty boundary. A
tunnel system
for
M
is a properly embedded collection of arcs
\tau
so that
M-\mathcal{N}(\tau)
is a handlebody. The
tunnel number

{\rm{TN}}(M)
of

is the minimum cardinality of its tunnel systems. Equivalently, if we regard
M
as a connected sutured manifold
(M,\gamma_{+})
for which
\partial M=R_{+}
, then the tunnel number of
M
is the minimum of
h(B)
among Heegaard splittings
(A,B;S)
for
M
.
This gives the equality

{\rm{TN}}(M)+g(M,\gamma_{+})+1=h(M,\gamma_{+},0).

Observation 7.1
.

For manifolds
M
with non-empty toroidal boundary,

h(M,\gamma_{+},0)=2{\rm{TN}}(M)+2
.

Let
L
be a link in a closed
3
–manifold

.
The
exterior
of
L
is the complement

of a regular neighborhood.
Let
(M,\gamma_{+})
be obtained by setting
R_{+}(\gamma_{+})=\partial M
.
The
tunnel number

{\rm{TN}}(L)
of
L
is
{\rm{TN}}(M,\gamma_{+})
.

Further suppose
L
is an oriented, null-homologous link in
Y
.
Let
(M,\gamma_{0})
be obtained by setting
T(\gamma_{0})=\partial M
.
Then its Seifert surfaces define a regular class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
.
The

of

is

{\rm{MN}}(L)=h(M,\gamma_{0},\xi)
. (Note that this definition can be extended to rationally null-homologous links as well.)
By Theorem

. Hence we also have

.

Lemma 7.3

Suppose a surface

The circular handle number function on homology

8.1.
Regular classes

Recall that for a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
, a class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is
regular
if every surface representing
\xi
meets every toroidal suture. A class is
non-regular
if it is not regular. To phrase this more homologically, let
T(\gamma)\subset\partial M
be the toroidal sutures of
(M,\gamma)
. Then a class
\xi
is
non-regular
if
\partial\xi\in H_{1}(\partial M)
is zero on some component of
T(\gamma)
. This second version of the definition extends to other coefficients. In particular, we may now speak of regular classes and non-regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a connected sutured manifold.
Unless
M
is a rational homology sphere, the set of non-regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
is a finite union of subspaces of positive codimension.

Proof.

Since
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})=0
exactly when
M
is a rational homology sphere, let us assume
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})\neq 0
.
If
T(\gamma)=\emptyset
, then every non-zero class is regular and the result holds. So now further assume
T(\gamma)\neq\emptyset
.

Let
T
be a non-empty subset of the components of
T(\gamma)
. Then we claim that the set
V_{T}
of classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
whose boundary vanishes on
T
form a subspace. By definition, the members of
V_{T}
are necessarily non-regular classes.
So now suppose
\sigma
and
\tau
are non-regular classes where
\partial\sigma
and
\partial\tau
are zero on
T
.
Since
\partial(r\sigma+s\tau)=r\partial\sigma+s\partial\tau
for any
r,s\in\mathbb{R}
,
\partial(r\sigma+s\tau)
is zero on
T
. Hence
r\sigma+s\tau\in V_{T}
. Thus
V_{T}
is a subspace.

Consequently, letting
\mathcal{T}
be the collection of all non-empty subsets of components of
T(\gamma)
, the set of non-regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
is
\bigcup_{T\in\mathcal{T}}V_{T}
. Since
T(\gamma)
is finite,
\mathcal{T}
is finite, so this is a finite union.

Finally, we claim that there exists a regular class. By the Half-Lives Half-Dies Lemma (that half of the rank of
H_{1}(\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
dies upon the inclusion of
\partial M
into
M
), for each component of
\partial M
there is a primitive class
\eta\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
for which
\partial\eta
is non-zero on that component. In particular, for each torus
T_{i}
of
T(\gamma)
let
\eta_{i}
be such a primitive class (where
i
runs from
1
to
n=|T(\gamma)|
). Then for each
k
from
1
to
n
we may successively find a non-zero integral coefficient
c_{k}
so that the class
\zeta_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\eta_{i}
has
\partial\zeta_{k}
non-zero on each of
T_{1},\dots,T_{k}
. Thus
\zeta_{n}
will be a regular class.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

For a connected sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
,
the set of regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
is a dense open set.

Proof.

This follows from the non-regular classes being a finite union of subspaces of positive codimension, Lemma

8.2.

Fix a sutured manifold structure
(M,\gamma)
on a

for an integral regular class

examines properties of this function. We extend

to real and non-regular classes in Theorem

Theorem 8.3
.

For a sutured manifold
(M,\gamma)
,
the handle number function

(1)

for some constant

(depending on
(M,\gamma)
),

(2)

h(k\beta)=h(\beta)
for any integer

, and

(3)

h(\alpha+m\beta)\leq h(\beta)
for sufficiently large
m
.

Proof of Theorem
8.3
.

(1) This follows immediately from Theorem
5.4

(and Theorem
4.1
in case

(M,\gamma)
has toroidal sutures).

(2)
Let
k
be a positive integer. If
h(k\beta)=h(\beta)
holds for primitive homology classes, then it holds for non-primitive ones too. So assume
\beta
is a primitive homology class.

Say
F
realizes
h(\beta)
. Then let
F_{k}
be
k
parallel copies of
F
(in a collar neighborhood of
F
disjoint from
S
), and let
S_{k}
be

along with
k-1
copies of
F
interlaced between the previous stack. Then the Heegaard splitting
(M,F_{k},S_{k})
has the same handle number as
(M,F,S)
. Since
[F_{k}]=k[F]=k\beta
,
h(\beta)\geq h(k\beta)
.

Now for the other direction. Among surfaces realizing
h(k\beta)
, say
\tilde{F}
is one with the least number of components. By Theorem
3.6
and its proof, we may assume
\tilde{F}
is well-conditioned. In particular,
[\tilde{F}]=k\beta
, there is a Heegaard splitting
(M,\tilde{F},\tilde{S})
with
h(M,\tilde{F},\tilde{S})=h(M,\tilde{F})=h(k\beta)
,
\tilde{F}
satisfies condition (C1), and no other such surface has fewer components.
For each connected compression body
A_{i}
of the compression body

Theorem 8.4
.

The handle number extends to a function
h\colon H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{N}
that is

(1)

bounded by constant,

(2)

constant on rays from the origin, and

(3)

locally maximal.

Proof.

Let
\beta
be a non-zero regular class.
Since
h(k\beta)=h(\beta)
for any positive integer
k
, we may define
h(r\beta)=h(\beta)
for any positive real number
r
. Hence
h
is constant on rays from the origin in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
that meet integral regular classes.

Let
S
be the quotient space of rays from the origin in
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
. Say a point in
S
is rational if its ray contains an integral class; otherwise say it is irrational. Similarly, say a point in
S
is regular if its ray belongs to the regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
. We just showed that
h
is well-defined on the rational regular points of
S
. Observe that property (3) of Theorem
8.3
implies that
h
is locally maximal on the rational regular points of
S
. That is, for any neighborhood
U
of a given rational regular point
b\in S
that consists of regular points, if
a
is a rational point
U
then
h(a)\leq h(b)
. We may use this property to extend
h
to the irrational regular points of
S
and hence to the rest of the regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
.

Let
z
be an irrational regular point of
S
. Then define
h(z)
so that:
For any neighborhood
U
of
z
consisting of regular points, there exists a neighborhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
z
for which
h(z)
is the maximum of
h
among the rational points in
U^{\prime}
.
Then we can see that
h
is locally maximal on the regular points of
S
.

Since the regular classes of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
are a dense open set by Lemma
8.2
, the regular points of
S
also form a dense open set of
S
. Thus we may extend
h
to irregular points similarly to how we extended
h
to irrational regular points. Let
z
be an irregular point of
S
and define
h(z)
as follows: For any neighborhood
U
of
z
, there is a neighborhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
z
for which
h(z)
is the maximum of
h
among the regular points in
U^{\prime}
. As before, we similarly see that
h
is locally maximal on
S
.

Consequently, with
h
defined on
S
, we may extend
h
to all of
H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})-\{0\}
so that it is constant on rays.
If
0
is a regular class, then
h(M,\gamma,0)
is defined and
h(M,\gamma,\xi)\leq h(M,\gamma,0)-2d
for any regular class
\xi
by Theorem
6.5
. Thus it follows that
h(0)\geq h(\xi)
for any class
\xi\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
since

.
If
0
is not a regular class, then we may just define
h(0)
to be the maximum of
h(\xi)
among the regular classes
\xi
. By Theorem
8.3
(1) and our extension of

, this maximum exists. Hence, in either case,

is bounded by
h(0)
.
∎

Remark 8.5
.

It may happen that
h(M,\gamma,\xi)\neq h(M,\gamma,-\xi)
. Flipping the orientation of a decomposing surface
F
may significantly change the resulting suture structure of the decomposed manifold.

Remark 8.6
.

As shown in the proof of Theorem
8.4
, when
0
is a regular class
h(\xi)
is bounded above by the linear handle number of
(M,\gamma)
. Consequently, the handle number function of a product sutured manifold is the constant function
h=0
.

Further remarks and questions.

9.1.
Regarding handle numbers and faces of the Thurston norm

Remark 9.1
.

For integral classes
\beta\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{Z})
, we have
h(\beta)=0
precisely when there is a surface representing
\beta
that decomposes
(M,\gamma)
into a product sutured manifold. In light of Theorems
8.3
and
8.4
, one may regard any class
\beta\in H_{2}(M,\partial M;\mathbb{R})
with
h(\beta)=0
as a
fibered class
with respect to the sutured manifold structure
(M,\gamma)
.

Indeed, if
\partial M
is a union of tori and
\gamma
has no annular sutures, then for
\beta\neq 0
we have
h(\beta)=0
if and only if
\beta
is a fibered class in the traditional sense (ie.
\beta
lies in the cone over a fibered face of the Thurston norm). When
\gamma
has annular sutures we will have
h(\beta)>0
for such a fibered class if
\partial\beta
has the same slope as an annular suture in some component of
\partial M
.

Question 9.2
.

Let
(M,\gamma_{+})
be a sutured manifold where
\partial M
is a union of tori and
R_{+}(\gamma_{+})=\partial M
.
Is the handle number constant on any face of the Thurston norm ball?

Remark 9.3
.

If
M
has tunnel number
1
, then this indeed is the case since the handle number of any regular class is bounded above by
2
as discussed in Observation
7.1
. Since the handle number is
0
exactly on the fibered faces (cf. Remark

), it must be
2
elsewhere. Hence the handle number is constant on any face.

Exteriors of two-bridge links provide a class of such examples. Following
[
EMnMGRLRV21
, Theorem 28]
, one may determine all the fibered faces of the Thurston norm of a two-bridge link exterior and hence the handle number function. Note that some two-bridge links, such as
[0;4,4,-2]
, have no fibered class, so their handle number functions are the constant function
h=2
.

Remark 9.4
.

Let
(M,\gamma)
be a sutured manifold where
\partial M
is a union of tori and
R_{+}(\gamma)
is a union of essential annuli. In particular each component of
\partial M
has some annular sutures of some slope. Then the handle number function of
(M,\gamma)
need not be constant on faces of the Thurston norm.

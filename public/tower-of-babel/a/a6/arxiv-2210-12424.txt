Noise based on vortex structures in 2D and 3D
Franco Flandoli, Ruojun Huang

Abstract
A new noise, based on vortex structures in 2D (point vortices) and 3D (vortex filaments), is introduced. It is defined as the scaling limit of a jump process which explores vortex structures and it can be defined in any domain, also with boundary. The link with Fractional Gaussian Fields and Kraichnan noise is discussed. The vortex noise is finally shown to be suitable for the investigation of the eddy dissipation produced by small scale turbulence.



1
Introduction

The theory of Stochastic Partial Differential Equations (SPDEs) is nowadays
very well developed, see for instance
[
8
,
21
,
30
,
32
]
, with many contributions on fluid dynamics
models, like
[
3
,
7
,
11
,
12
,
25
,
34
]
. However, with the exception of the
literature making use of Kraichnan noise, which is motivated in Fluid Dynamics
by its invariance and scaling properties, in most cases there is no discussion
about the origin of noise and its form, in connection with the fact that it is
part of a fluid dynamic model. The purpose of this work is to introduce an
example of noise based on vortex structures, both in 2D (point vortices) and
3D (vortex filaments). We discuss its motivations and interest for the
understanding of fluid properties.

Some preliminary forms in 2D have been introduced in
[
14
,
20
]
, but the noise defined here is different and goes much beyond,
in particular because we treat the 3D case on the basis of the theory of
random vortex filaments, see Section
3.2
.

Usually, in general or theoretical works on SPDEs, the noise is either
specified by means of its covariance operator, or by means of a finite or
countable sum of space-functions multiplied by independent Brownian motions.
Here we start from a different viewpoint. Motivated by the emergence of vortex
structures in turbulent fluids, we idealize their production/emergence process
by means of a sequence of vortex impulses, mathematically structured using a
jump process taking values in a set of vortex structures. This is described in
Section
2
. A suitable scaling limit of this jump process gives rise to
a Gaussian noise in a suitable Hilbert space. Different examples of such noise
depend on different choices of the vortex structures and their statistics, at
the level of the jump process. An heuristic picture then emerges, of a process
that fluctuates very rapidly between the elements of a family of vortex
structures. And the realizations of this noise are made of vortex structures
which idealize those observed in turbulent fluids - point vortices in 2D and
vortex filaments in 3D.

This noise is motivated by turbulent fluids. In the Physical literature, the
most common noise related to turbulence are the Fractional Gaussian Field
(FGF) and Kraichnan noise, see for instance
[
1
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
27
]
. In Section
4
we show that, on a torus in two and three dimensions,
the vortex noise covers FGF and Kraichnan noise by a special choice of the
statistical properties of the regularization parameter and the vortex
intensity. The vortex noise is thus a flexible ensemble - it may cover also
multifractal formalisms, see also
[
15
]
- and its realizations are the limit,
as described in Sections
2
-
3
, of localized-in-space vortex structures similar
to those observed in turbulent fluids.

Finally, another main motivation for this investigation has been the recent
results on eddy dissipation, showing that a transport type noise depending in
a suitable way on a scaling parameter, in a transport-diffusion equation, in
the scaling limit gives rise to an additional diffusion operator

[
18
,
14
]
. These results requires that the covariance
function of the noise, computed along the diagonal,
Q\left(x,x\right)
,
is large; but the operator norm of the covariance is small. We check when the
vortex noise satisfies these conditions. Heuristically speaking, they are
satisfied when, in the scaling limit, the vortex structures defining the noise
are more and more concentrated at
small scales
. This confirms the
belief that eddy diffusion is a consequence of turbulence, but only when it is
suitably small scale.

2
Jump noise and its Gaussian limit

2.1
Why jump vortex noise in fluid modeling

When a fluid moves through the small obstacles of a boundary (hills, trees,
houses for the lower surface wind, mountains for the lower atmospheric layer,
coast irregularities for the sea, vegetation for a river) or it moves through
small obstacles in the middle of the domain (like islands in the sea),
vortices are created by these obstacles, sometimes with a regular rhythm (von Kármán vortices) sometimes else more irregularly. In principle, these vortices
are the deterministic consequence of the dynamical interaction between fluid
and structure but in very many applications we never write the details of
those obstacles, when a larger scale investigation is done. Hence it is
reasonable to re-introduce the appearance of these vortices, so important for
turbulence, in the form of an external perturbation of the equations of motion.

Assume that the velocity field at time
t
is
u\left(t,x\right)
. We may
idealize the modification of
u\left(t,x\right)
due to the emergence of a
new vortex near an obstacle as an event occuring in a very short time around
time
t
, so that we have a jump:

u\left(t^{+},x\right)=u\left(t^{-},x\right)+\sigma\left(x\right)

where
\sigma\left(x\right)
is presumably localized in space and
corresponds to a vortex structure. Continuum mechanics does not make jumps; we
idealize a fast change due to an instability as a jump, for a cleaner
mathematical description.

We may develop the previous idea in two directions. The simplest one is
suitable for investigations like the effect of turbulence on passive scalars

[
5
]
, where a simple model of random velocity field is chosen: we consider a
stepwise constant velocity field with jumps like those described above; later
on we shall take a suitable scaling limit and get a Gaussian velocity field,
delta correlated in time, with space correlation of very flexible form. A more
elaborate proposal is to consider the Navier-Stokes equations with an
impulsive force given by a process with jumps:

\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla p=\nu\Delta u+\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{i}\delta\left(t-t_{i}^{k}\right)\sigma_{k}.

Here
K
is an index set and, for each
k\in K
, we denote by
t_{1}^{k}<t_{2}^{k}<...
the sequence of jump times of class
k
and by
\sigma_{k}

the vortex structure (described at the level of velocity field) arisen at time

t_{i}^{k}
. This way the fluid moves according to the free Navier-Stokes
equations between two consecutive jumps times. In the next section we
formalize the noise
\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{i}\delta\left(t-t_{i}^{k}\right)\sigma_{k}
or more precisely, similarly to what it is done for White noise
and Brownian motion, we formalize the time integral of this distributional
process:

W_{t}^{0}\left(x\right)=\sum_{k\in K}\sum_{i}1\left\{t\geq t_{i}^{k}\right\}\sigma_{k}.

(1)

In this first heuristic formulation it is natural to introduce an index set

K
but below we shall avoid this.

2.2
Jump vortex noise

Given an open domain
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2,3
, denote by

C_{\text{c,sol}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
the space of
smooth solenoidal vector fields with compact support in
\mathbb{D}
, and denote by
H

the closure of
C_{\text{c,sol}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
in

L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
. One can prove, under some regularity
of the boundary, that
u\in H
is an
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
-vector field, with distributional divergence equal to zero, tangent to the
boundary
[
33
]
. The norm
\left\|u\right\|_{H}
is given by

\left\|u\right\|_{H}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|u\left(x\right)\right|^{2}dx
.

The following scheme is taken from Métivier
[
29
]
, first three
Chapters. The main tightness and convergence results for martingales, as
described in
[
29
]
, are due to Rebolledo
[
31
]
.

Let
P
be a Borel probability measure on
H
. Assume that

\int_{H}\varphi\left(\left\|h\right\|_{H}\right)P(dh)<\infty

(2)

for some nondecreasing
\varphi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
that
grows faster than quadratic, i.e.

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n^{2}}=\infty.

(3)

Denote by
Q_{P}
the trace class covariance operator defined as

Q_{P}=\int_{H}h\otimes hP\left(dh\right).

Assume
P
has zero average

m_{P}=\int_{H}hP\left(dh\right)=0.

(4)

We may also define, a.s. in
x,y\in\mathbb{D}
, the covariance (matrix-valued)
function

Q_{P}\left(x,y\right)=\int_{H}h\left(x\right)\otimes h\left(y\right)P\left(dh\right).

Indeed,
\int_{H}\left|h\left(x\right)\right|^{2}P\left(dh\right)<\infty
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{D}
, thanks to Fubini-Tonelli theorem, since

\int_{H}\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}\left|h\left(x\right)\right|^{2}dx\right)P\left(dh\right)<\infty
.

Consider the continuous time jump Markov process in
H
with law of jumps

p\left(v,v+A\right)=\frac{1}{\tau}P\left(A\right)

(
v\in H
,
A\in\mathcal{B}\left(H\right)
), namely with infinitesimal
generator

\left(\mathcal{L}F\right)\left(v\right)=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{H}\left(F\left(v+h\right)-F\left(v\right)\right)P\left(dh\right)

for all bounded continuous functions
F:H\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. Here
\tau>0

is the average interarrival between jumps. Denote by
W_{t}^{0}
the
corresponding Markov process with initial condition
W_{0}^{0}=0
. Dynkin
formula

F\left(W_{t}^{0}\right)-F\left(0\right)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\mathcal{L}F\right)\left(W_{s}^{0}\right)ds+M_{t}^{F}

gives us a the decomposition in a finite variation plus a martingale term.
Consider first the case when
F_{1}\left(v\right)=v
(here we do not write
down classical details, namely that the computation should be done for a
continuous bounded cut-off of each component
\left\langle v,e_{i}\right\rangle
, where
\left(e_{i}\right)
is a complete orthonormal
system, see
[
29
, page 14]
). One has

\left(\mathcal{L}F_{1}\right)\left(v\right)=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{H}\left(v+h-v\right)P\left(dh\right)=0

because
m_{P}=0
. Hence
W_{t}^{0}=M_{t}^{F_{1}}
namely the process

W_{t}^{0}
is a martingale. Let us compute its Hilbert-space-valued Meyer
process
\left\langle\left\langle W^{0}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{t}
. We
use the function
F_{2}\left(v\right)=v\otimes v
(again one has to do the
computation first for a cut-off of the functions
\left\langle v,e_{i}\right\rangle\left\langle v,e_{j}\right\rangle
):

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}F_{2}\right)\left(v\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{H}\left(\left(v+h\right)\otimes\left(v+h\right)-v\otimes v\right)P\left(dh\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{H}\left(v\otimes h+h\otimes v+h\otimes h\right)P\left(dh\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau}Q_{P}.

Therefore
W_{t}^{0}\otimes W_{t}^{0}=\frac{t}{\tau}Q_{P}+M_{t}^{F_{2}}
. The
Meyer process
\left\langle\left\langle W^{0}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{t}
is thus (see the definition in
[
29
, pp. 8-12]
)

\left\langle\left\langle W\right\rangle\right\rangle_{t}=\frac{t}{\tau}Q_{P}.

2.3
Convergence of the rescaled process to a Brownian motion

Let us now parametrize and rescale the previous process. We take average
interarrival between jumps given by

\tau_{N}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}

and we reduce by
\frac{1}{N}
the size of jumps by considering a probability
measure
P_{N}
on
H
with zero average
m_{P}=\int_{H}hP_{N}\left(dh\right)=0
and covariance
Q_{P_{N}}
given by

Q_{P_{N}}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}Q_{P}.

Consider the associated process
W_{t}^{N}
, a martingale with Meyer process

\left\langle\left\langle W^{N}\right\rangle\right\rangle_{t}=\frac{t}{\tau_{N}}Q_{P_{N}}=tQ_{P}.

Definition 1

Given
Q_{P}
, denote by
\left(W_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
a Brownian motion
on
H
with incremental covariance
Q_{P}
.

Theorem 2

The process
\left(W_{t}^{N}\right)_{t\geq 0}
converges in law to
\left(W_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, uniformly on every compact set of time, as processes
with values in
H
.

Proof.
Using classical theorem of tightness for martingales (cf.
[
29
]
,
Chapter 2,
[
31
]
), we have that the family of laws of the processes

\left(W^{N}\right)_{N}
is tight in the Skorohod space (because the
family of laws of
\left\langle\left\langle W^{N}\right\rangle\right\rangle
is tight) and every convergent subsequence has limit given by the law of a
martingale
W_{t}
with
W_{0}=0
and Meyer process

\left\langle\left\langle W\right\rangle\right\rangle_{t}=tQ_{P}.

If we establish that
W
has continuous paths, then it is a Brownian motion
with incremental covariance
Q_{P}
. One can prove that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|\Delta_{s}W^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)=0

(5)

where
\left\|\Delta_{s}W^{N}\right\|_{H}
is the size of the jump (if
any) at time
s
(
W^{N}
is càdlàg). Since the set
\left\{\sup_{s\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|\Delta_{s}w^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right\}
is open in the Skorohod topology, from Portmanteau
theorem we get

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|\Delta_{s}W\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)=0

for every
\epsilon>0
, hence
W
is continuous. To show (
5
),
denote by
\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N_{T}}\subset[0,T]
the Poisson
(\tau_{N}^{-1})
arrival times, then we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|\Delta_{s}W^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{\{s_{i}\}\subset[0,T]}\left\{\left\|\Delta_{s_{i}}W^{N}\right\|_{H}\leq\epsilon\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{\{s_{i}\}\subset[0,T]}\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\Delta_{s_{i}}W^{N}\right\|_{H}\leq\epsilon\;\left|\;\{s_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N_{T}}\right.\right)\right]

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{E}\left[\left[1-\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\Delta W^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)\right]^{N_{T}}\right]

where we used that given the Poisson arrival times, the laws of each jump size

\left\|\Delta_{s_{i}}W^{N}\right\|_{H}
is independent of it, and
identically distributed as what we simply denote by
\left\|\Delta W^{N}\right\|_{H}
. By the elementary inequality
(1-y)^{n}\geq 1-ny
for
any
y\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and Markov’s inequality, we have
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in\left[0,T\right]}\left\|\Delta_{s}W^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[N_{T}]\mathbb{P}\left(\left\|\Delta W^{N}\right\|_{H}>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{TN^{2}}{\varphi(N\epsilon)}\mathbb{E}\left[\varphi(\left\|\Delta W\right\|_{H})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{TN^{2}}{\varphi(N\epsilon)}\int_{H}\varphi(\left\|h\right\|_{H})P(dh)

which is finite by (
2
), and converges to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
by (
3
).

2.4
Reformulation as a PPP

This is a side section which however may help the intuition (see also
(
1
)): we reformulate the jump process
W_{t}^{0}
as a
Poisson Point Process (PPP). On a probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
, Let
\mathcal{P}
be a PPP on

[0,\infty)\times H
with intensity measure
\lambda Leb\otimes P
, where

\lambda Leb
is Lebesgue measure scaled by
\lambda>0
, and
P
is the
probability measure introduced in the previous subsections. Heuristically

\mathcal{P}\left(dt,du\right)=\sum_{i}\delta_{\left(t_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)}\left(dt,du\right)

where
\left(t_{i},\sigma_{i}\right)
is an i.i.d. sequence with
t_{i}

“uniformly distributed on
[0,\infty)
”,
\sigma_{i}
distributed according to

P
,
t_{i}
and
\sigma_{i}
independent of each other. Define the vector
valued random field, defined on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
,

W_{t}^{0}\left(x\right)=\sum_{t_{i}\leq t}\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)=\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)1\left\{t_{i}\leq t\right\}.

Compared to (
1
), we may think that
K
in that formula was a
finite set and we have simply reordered the jump times
\left(t_{i}^{k}\right)
in a single sequence
\left(t_{i}\right)
and we have
renamed the jump velocity fields. This definition is slightly heuristic
because it makes use of the representation as infinite sum which is true only
in a suitable limit sense; a rigorous definition of
W\left(t,x\right)
is

W_{t}^{0}\left(x\right)=\int_{[0,\infty)\times H}u\left(x\right)1\left\{t^{\prime}\leq t\right\}\mathcal{P}\left(dt^{\prime},du\right).

However, in the sequel, for the sake of interpretability, we shall always use
the heuristic expressions.

The intuition is that eddies
\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)
are chosen at
random with distribution
P
, with exponential inter-arrival times of rate

\lambda
. Condition (
4
) asks, heuristically
speaking, that both an eddy and its opposite are equally likely to be chosen.

Rescale
W_{t}^{0}\left(x\right)
as

W_{t}^{N}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)1\left\{t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right\}.

Let us compute the expectation and the covariance function of this process.
One has (
\mathbb{E}
denotes the Mathematical expectation on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
)

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{t}^{N}\left(x\right)\right]=0

from the independences and condition (
4
). Moreover,

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{t}^{N}\left(x\right)\otimes W_{t}^{N}\left(y\right)\right]=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[\sigma_{i}\left(x\right)\otimes\sigma_{i}\left(y\right)1\left\{t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right\}\right]

having used the independence when
i\neq j
and property
(
4
) again; hence

=\frac{Q_{P}\left(x,y\right)}{N^{2}}\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}\left(t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right).

Proposition 3

\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}\left(t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right)=N^{2}\lambda t.

Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[W_{t}^{N}\left(x\right)\otimes W_{t}^{N}\left(y\right)\right]=\lambda tQ_{P}\left(x,y\right).

Proof.
We note that

\sum_{i}\mathbb{P}\left(t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right)=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i}1\left\{t_{i}\leq N^{2}t\right\}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{\lambda}(N^{2}t)\right]=N^{2}\lambda t.

where
\eta_{\lambda}(\cdot)
denotes a Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

with intensity
\lambda
.

This is another way of seeing the link between the noise with jumps and the
covariance of the limit Brownian motion.

3
Examples in 2D and 3D

The mathematical object discussed in the previous section, although initially
motivated by vortex structures, were completely general: given any probability measure
P
on
H
with covariance
Q_{P}
, the previous
construction and results apply and defines a Brownian motion
W_{t}
in
H
with covariance operator
Q_{P}
. Notice that
P
is not necessarily Gaussian:
P
and
W_{1}
both have covariance
Q_{P}
, but only
W_{1}
needs to be Gaussian. In a sense,
we “realize” approximately samples of the Brownian motion
W_{t}
by means of
samples of a possibly ”nonlinear” (non Gaussian) process
W_{t}^{N}
.

In this section we give our two main examples of the measure
P
, highly non
Gaussian. It is inspired by vortex structures.

Common to both descriptions are a few objects. First, given
\delta>0
, we
define

\mathbb{D}_{\delta}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{D}:\;\text{dist}(x,\mathbb{D}^{c})>\delta\right\}.

Second, we have a filtered probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P}\right)
and several
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable
r.v.’s: a)
X_{0}
with law
p_{0}\left(dx\right)
supported on

\mathbb{D}_{\delta}
, which will play the role of the center of the vortex in 2D and
the initial position of the vortex filament in 3D; b)
\Gamma
, real
valued, with the physical meaning of circulation, with

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma\right]

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\Gamma\right|^{p}\right]<\infty\text{ for some }p>2

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\right];

c)
L
, positive valued, randomizing the size of the mollification, with the
property

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(L\in\left(0,\delta/2\right)\right)=1;

(6)

d)
U
, positive valued, randomizing the length of the vortex filament.
Moreover, in 3D, we also have: e) a Brownian motion on
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}_{t},\mathbb{P}\right)
with values in

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In the 2D case we just take
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{0}

and do not need the filtration.

For sake of simplicity of exposition, we shall assume that
X_{0},\Gamma,L
and
U
are independent, but most of the results can be extended to more general cases.

The last common element of the theory is a smooth symmetric probability
density
\theta
supported in the ball
B\left(0,1\right)
and its
rescaled mollifiers

\displaystyle\theta_{\ell}\left(x\right)=\ell^{-d}\theta\left(\ell^{-1}x\right),

(7)

with support in
B\left(0,\ell\right)
. In particular,
\theta_{\ell}\geq 0
and
\int\theta_{\ell}(x)dx=1
.

3.1
Point vortices and definition of
P
in the 2D case

In 2D, by a point vortex we mean a vorticity field of delta Dirac type,

\delta_{x_{0}}
; its use in 2D fluid mechanics is manifold, see for instance

[
28
]
. If the vorticity is assumed distributional and
equal to
\delta_{x_{0}}
, with
x_{0}
in the interior of
\mathbb{D}
, then the
so-called stream function
\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}
is given by the solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{x_{0}}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}

\displaystyle=0

and the associated velocity vector field is given by:

u_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)=\nabla^{\perp}\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)

where
\nabla^{\perp}f=\left(\partial_{2}f,-\partial_{1}f\right)
. One has

\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\frac{1}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}+h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)

where
h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}
is a smooth function, solution of the problem

\displaystyle-\Delta_{x}h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log\left|x-x_{0}\right|\text{ for }x\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

In the sequel, as it is customary, we shall denote
u_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)
simply by
K\left(x,x_{0}\right)
. Hence

\displaystyle K\left(x,x_{0}\right)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{\perp}}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}}+\nabla^{\perp}h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right),

(8)

where
x^{\perp}=(x_{2},-x_{1})
.

Recall that
\theta_{\ell}
(
7
), as
\ell\to 0
is an approximation of the Dirac delta function. Expressions of the form
\theta_{\ell}(x-x_{0})
are idealized smoothed point
vortices, at the vorticity level, and the associated velocity field is

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(x,x_{0}):=\int_{D}K\left(x,y\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(y-x_{0}\right)dy.

With
these preliminaries, let us define
P
.

Definition 4

In the 2D case, the probability measure
P
on the space
H

is the law of the
H
-valued r.v.

\displaystyle\Gamma K_{L}(x,X_{0})=\Gamma\int_{\mathbb{D}}K\left(x,y\right)\theta_{L}\left(y-X_{0}\right)dy.

(9)

For future reference, the spatial covariance matrix of the vortex noise in 2D is given by

Proposition 5

The random vector field of Definition

takes values in

Proof.
Fixing any

\displaystyle\int_{H}\|h\|_{H}^{p}P(dh)

because the second expectation is finite and the first one is equal to zero,
by assumption.

3.2

The following construction of a vortex filament in 3D is due to

(which we slightly modify). Let

distributed with a probability

\mathbb{D}_{\delta}

. We call
\mathcal{W}

its law which we assume to be independent of
\nu(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
. Define the first
exit time from
\mathbb{D}_{\delta}
of

by

\displaystyle\tau=\tau^{\mathbb{D}_{\delta}}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\;X_{t}\in\mathbb{D}_{\delta}^{c}\right\}\in[0,\infty).

(11)

We consider random vorticity fields defined as

\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\left(\theta\ast\delta_{X_{t}}\right)\left(x\right)dX_{t}=\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\theta(x-X_{t})dX_{t}.

Let

be the vector potential defined path by path by the

\displaystyle-\Delta A\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\theta(x-X_{t})dX_{t}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle A|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}

\displaystyle=0

and extend

Concerning Biot-Savart kernel, here we have

\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}+h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)

where

is a smooth function, solution of the problem

\displaystyle-\Delta_{x}h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}

\displaystyle=0\text{ in }\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}\text{ for }x\in\partial\mathbb{D}.

As usual, we shall denote
\operatorname{curl}\psi_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)
simply by

, which now is vector valued and its
action on a generic vector

\displaystyle K\left(x,x_{0}\right)\times v:=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\left(x-x_{0}\right)\times v}{\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{3}}+\nabla_{x}h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)\times v.

(12)

Proposition 8

The random vector field of Definition

Proof.
Fix any

\displaystyle\int_{H}\|h\|_{H}^{p}P(dh)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\Gamma u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p/2}\right].

Fixing any realization of
(\Gamma,U,L)

\displaystyle=|\mathbb{D}|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\int_{\mathbb{D}}\mathcal{W}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\int_{\mathbb{D}_{\delta}}K(x,y)\theta_{L}\left(y-X_{t}\right)dy\times dX_{t}\right|^{p}\right)dx

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}\nabla_{x}h_{\mathbb{D},y}(x)\theta_{L}(y-X_{t})dy\right|^{2}dt\right|^{p/2}\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left(\left|\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\left|\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}\theta_{L}(y-X_{t})dy\right|^{2}dt\right|^{p/2}\right)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|\Gamma u(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{p/2}\right]

4
Vortex noises reproduce Fractional Gaussian Fields and Kraichnan noise

In this section, we analyse the covariance operators of our vortex noises constructed above in 2D and 3D, and show that our vortex noises are instances of Fractional Gaussian Fields (
[
27
]
, which is a broad class of Gaussian generalized random fields that includes Gaussian Free Field and Kraichnan noise). We show that, by choosing the statistical parameters of our model suitably, we can reproduce a large class of FGF. It may also reproduce multifractal vector fields, which was the main motivation of study in
[
15
]
.

For simplicity, our fields are defined on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
d=2,3
.

In the scalar case and on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, the
classical
d
-dimensional FGF of index
s\in\mathbb{R}
is the Gaussian field
with covariance
\left(-\Delta\right)^{-s}
, where
\Delta
is the
Laplacian in on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(see
[
27
]
). The case
s=1
is called Gaussian
Free Field (GFF). Similarly let us introduce a Gaussian measure on solenoidal
vector fields. Let
H
be the space of mean zero periodic
L^{2}
solenoidal
vector fields. The Stokes operator is defined as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:D\left(A\right)\subset H\rightarrow H

\displaystyle D\left(A\right)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle Av

\displaystyle=\Delta v

(no projection of
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{T}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
to
H

is needed here, opposite to the case of a bounded domain with Dirichlet
boundary conditions). The Laplacian
\Delta v
is computed componentwise. The
operator
A
is invertible in
H
(see
[
33
]
). With these definitions
at hand, we call Solenoidal Fractional Gaussian Field (SFGF) of index

s\in\mathbb{R}
the Gaussian measure with covariance
\left(-A\right)^{-s}
. The case
s=1
will be called Solenoidal Gaussian Free Field (SGFF).

4.1
Covariance of 2D vortex noise

Let us first consider the 2D case, and recall the definition of the noise
based on point vortices (
9
).

The covariance operator of our noise is given by

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K_{L}\left(x,X_{0}\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)dx\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K_{L}\left(x^{\prime},X_{0}\right)\cdot w\left(x^{\prime}\right)dx^{\prime}\right].

Call
Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)
its covariance function
(matrix-valued), such that

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v\left(x\right)^{T}Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)w\left(x^{\prime}\right)dxdx^{\prime}.

It is clear (and proved below) that it is homogeneous:

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)

for a matrix function
Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)
. In the sequel we
denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{d}
the set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
.

Proposition 9

Assume
\theta
symmetric, and
X_{0}
independent of
\left(\Gamma,L\right)

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{k\in}\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(L\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

Proof.
We may rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K_{L}\left(x,X_{0}\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}K\left(x,y\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)\theta_{L}\left(y-X_{0}\right)dydx

\displaystyle=\left(\theta_{L}\ast K\ast v\right)\left(X_{0}\right)

Therefore

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left(\theta_{L}\ast K\ast v\right)\left(X_{0}\right)\left(\theta_{L}\ast K\ast w\right)\left(X_{0}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(\theta_{L}\ast K\ast v\right)\left(x\right)\left(\theta_{L}\ast K\ast w\right)\left(x\right)dx\right].

By Parseval theorem

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\widehat{\theta_{L}\ast K\ast v}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\overline{\widehat{\theta_{L}\ast K\ast w}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k\in}\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}^{T}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}\left\langle P_{\mathbf{k}}\widehat{v}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),\overline{\widehat{w}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}\right\rangle

recalling that

\widehat{K}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=i\frac{\mathbf{k}^{\perp}}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}

and calling
P_{\mathbf{k}}=I-\frac{\mathbf{k}\otimes\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}
is the projection on the orthogonal to
\mathbf{k}
.
Therefore

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{k\in}\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

Since
\widehat{\theta_{\ell}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\widehat{\theta}\left(\ell\mathbf{k}\right)
, we get the result.

Corollary 10

\theta
is a smooth function with
\widehat{\theta}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\widehat{\theta}\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|\right)

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=D\sum_{\mathbf{k\in}\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{3+\alpha}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

This is the covariance function of a SFGF of index

s=\frac{3+\alpha}{2}.

Proof.
Since
\theta
is smooth,
\widehat{\theta}\left(r\right)
has a fast
decay which makes
\widehat{\theta}\left(r\right)r^{\alpha}
integrable at

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(L\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\gamma^{2}\left(L\right)\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(\left|L\mathbf{k}\right|\right)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\gamma^{2}\left(\ell\right)\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(\ell\left|\mathbf{k}\right|\right)\right|^{2}f_{L}\left(\ell\right)d\ell

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(r\right)\right|^{2}\gamma^{2}\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-1}r\right)f_{L}\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-1}r\right)dr

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-1-\alpha}D.

Notice that
\alpha>-1
corresponds to

s>1

so the SGFF (

Recall that the solenoidal Kraichnan model with scaling parameter
\zeta
is
defined, on the torus

We see thus that the vortex noise, in dimension

The space-scale
\ell

restrict ourselves to small vortices, namely we take

r>k_{0}^{-1}
we get:

Corollary 11

Under the same assumptions of the previous corollary except for

\gamma^{2}\left(r\right)f_{L}\left(r\right)=Cr^{\alpha}1_{\left\{r\leq k_{0}^{-1}\right\}}

for some
C,k_{0}>0
and
\alpha>-1
, we get

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{k_{0}^{3+\alpha}}\sum_{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|>k_{0}}\frac{D\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|/k_{0}\right)}{\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|/k_{0}\right)^{3+\alpha}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}+R_{k_{0}}\left(x\right)

where

\lim_{\kappa\rightarrow\infty}D\left(\kappa\right)=D

\left\|R_{k_{0}}\left(x\right)\right\|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}\frac{\log k_{0}}{k_{0}^{1+\alpha}}

for some constant
C^{\prime}>0
.

Proof.
As above,

\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(L\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]=\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-1-\alpha}D\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|/k_{0}\right).

The first limit property is obvious. Moreover (using also
\left\|\widehat{\theta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1
)

D\left(\kappa\right)\leq C\frac{\kappa^{\alpha+1}}{\alpha+1}

hence

\frac{1}{k_{0}^{3+\alpha}}\sum_{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|\leq k_{0}}\frac{D\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|/k_{0}\right)}{\left(\left|\mathbf{k}\right|/k_{0}\right)^{3+\alpha}}\leq\frac{C}{\alpha+1}\frac{1}{k_{0}^{1+\alpha}}\sum_{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|\leq k_{0}}\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{-2}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}\frac{1}{k_{0}^{1+\alpha}}\log k_{0}.

We thus see that, up to lower order terms, the vortex model with cut-off
corresponds to Kraichnan model with infrared cut-off
k_{0}
(cf.
[
10
, eq. (2.3)]
).

Finally, we remark that the model has the flexibility of multifractality. To
explain it in the simplest possible case, assume

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}\left(r\right)f\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{i}r^{\alpha_{i}}

\displaystyle D_{i}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(r\right)\right|^{2}C_{i}r^{\alpha_{i}}dr.

Then we get

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}D_{i}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{3+\alpha_{i}}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

Clearly one can do the same with a continuously distributed multifractality in
place of the finite sum (we void to introduce additional notations to explain
this point).

Remark 12

An intriguing but extremely difficult question (we thank an anonymous referee
for it) is whether we may infer the value of the scaling exponent
\zeta
of
Kraichnan model, or a multifractal version of it, from the similarity with the
vortex noise. It was the main aim of the outstanding book
[
6
]
, which
- as admitted by the author - remained open at the time of the book and it is
still open now. Two examples of attempts in this direction have been

[
26
]
and
[
15
]
; in the latter work a multifractal formalism based
on vortex filaments was developed. However, it must be stressed that no one of
these works deduced K41 or other scalings from vortex models; they could only
reproduce scalings chosen a priori.

4.2
Covariance of 3D vortex noise

Next, we turn to the 3D case, and recall the definition of the noise based on vortex filaments (
13
). The covariance of the noise is given by

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\right\rangle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}K_{L}\left(x,X_{\cdot}\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)dx\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}K_{L}\left(x^{\prime},X_{\cdot}\right)\cdot w\left(x^{\prime}\right)dx^{\prime}\right],

where

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}K_{L}\left(x,X_{\cdot}\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\cdot K(x,y)\times\int_{0}^{U}\theta_{L}(y-X_{t})\,dX_{t}\,dxdy.

For simplicity we set from now on the time-horizon
U=1
, and assume that the 3D Brownian motion
(X_{t})
starts from uniform distribution on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, hence for any time
t>0
, the distribution of
X_{t}
remains uniform.
(X_{t})
is also independent of
(\Gamma,L)
. Using vector identity we may rewrite

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}K_{L}\left(x,X_{\cdot}\right)\cdot v\left(x\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\theta_{L}(y-X_{t})v(x)\times K(x,y)\cdot dX_{t}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left[\theta_{L}^{T}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\right)\right](X_{t})\,\cdot dX_{t}.

For the 3D kernel
K
(
12
), we still have the property that
K\left(x,a\right)=K\left(x-a\right)=-K\left(a-x\right)
.

Our first result is that in 3D the vortex noise has the same covariance structure as in 2D case.

Proposition 13

Assume
\theta
symmetric, and
(X_{t})
independent of
\left(\Gamma,L\right)
and starts from uniform distribution on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Then

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{k\in}\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{3}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta}\left(L\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot x}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\rangle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\right)\right](X_{t})\,\cdot dX_{t}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\int_{0}^{1}\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}w(x^{\prime})\times K(x^{\prime}-\cdot)dx^{\prime}\right)\right](X_{t})\,\cdot dX_{t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\right)\right](X_{t})\cdot\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}w(x^{\prime})\times K(x^{\prime}-\cdot)dx^{\prime}\right)\right](X_{t})\,dt\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\right)\right](z)\cdot\left[\theta_{L}*\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}w(x^{\prime})\times K(x^{\prime}-\cdot)dx^{\prime}\right)\right](z)\,dz\right],

where we take conditional expectation with respect to
(X_{t})
first, using its time-stationarity and uniform distribution, whereas the randomness of
(\Gamma,L)
remains.

By Parseval theorem and vector identities, we may rewrite

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}}\widehat{\theta_{L}}(\mathbf{k})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\Big)^{\wedge}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\overline{\widehat{\theta_{L}}(\mathbf{k})\Big(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}w(x)\times K(x-\cdot)dx\Big)^{\wedge}(\mathbf{k})}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\times\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\big)\cdot\big(\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\times\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\cdot\Big(\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\times\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\times\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\big)\Big)\right].

By properties of triple cross product, we have that

\displaystyle\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\times\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\times\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\left(\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\right)-\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\left(\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\cdot\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\right),

hence

\displaystyle\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\cdot\Big(\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\times\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\times\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\left|\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\big)-\big(\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\cdot\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\big)\big(\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\cdot\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\big)

\displaystyle=\left|\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\big)-\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}^{T}\big(\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})\otimes\overline{\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})}\big)\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\big(\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})\cdot\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\big)-\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}^{T}\big(\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\otimes\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\big)\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\left\langle\left(I-\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|}\otimes\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|}\right)\widehat{v}(\mathbf{k}),\overline{\widehat{w}(\mathbf{k})}\right\rangle,

recalling that in 3D,

\widehat{K}(\mathbf{k})=i\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}.

Thus, we may conclude that

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}v,w\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}}(\mathbf{k})\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{|\mathbf{k}|^{2}}\left\langle P_{\mathbf{k}}\widehat{v}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),\overline{\widehat{w}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)}\right\rangle,

where
P_{\mathbf{k}}=I-\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|}\otimes\frac{\mathbf{k}}{|\mathbf{k}|}
is the projector on the orthogonal to
\mathbf{k}
. This yields in turn that the covariance matrix of the noise is given by

Q_{\text{vortex}}\left(x,x^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|\widehat{\theta_{L}}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\right|^{2}\right]\frac{1}{\left|\mathbf{k}\right|^{2}}P_{\mathbf{k}}e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\left(x-x^{\prime}\right)}.

This formula agrees with the formula (
15
) obtained for 2D, hence Corollary
10
applies in 3D without change (except for summation over
\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}_{0}
).

Our result in 3D covers Kraichnan noise with parameter

\zeta=\alpha>-1.

We can also restrict the vortices to small scales by introducing a cutoff
k_{0}
, as in Corollary
11
. Here, we need to restrict to
\alpha>0
in its statement, so that the remainder
R_{k_{0}}(x)
is of lower order:

\left\|R_{k_{0}}\left(x\right)\right\|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{\alpha+1}\frac{1}{k_{0}^{\alpha}}.

5
The effect of vortex structure noise on passive scalars

5.1
Introduction

Regarding eddy diffusion enhancement in domains with boundary, we recall the
following theorem proved in
[
14
, Theorems 1.1, 1.3]
. Here, we have a
passive scalar
T
driven by the white-in-time, correlated-in-space noise
\partial_{t}W
produced by our vortex
structures, where
W(t,x)
is the limit Gaussian process obtained via the invariance principle in Theorem
2
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}T+\partial_{t}W\circ\nabla T=\kappa\Delta T,

\circ
denotes Stratonovich integration, and scalar
\kappa>0
. We denote the smallest eigenvalue of the matrix
Q(x,x)
by

\displaystyle q(x,x)

\displaystyle:=\min_{0\neq\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\xi^{T}Q(x,x)\xi}{\xi^{T}\xi},

and the squared operator norm
\|Q^{1/2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{D})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{D})}^{2}
by

\displaystyle\epsilon_{Q}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{0\neq v\in H}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{D}}\int_{\mathbb{D}}v^{T}(x)Q(x,y)v(y)dxdy}{\int_{\mathbb{D}}v(x)^{T}v(x)dx}.

Theorem 14

[
14
, Theorems 1.1, 1.3]

(a).
For any
T_{0}\in H
measurable, and any
t\geq 0
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|T(t,x)|dx\right)^{2}\right]\leq\left(\frac{\epsilon_{Q}}{\kappa}+2|\mathbb{D}|e^{-2t\lambda_{\mathbb{D},\kappa,Q}}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[||T_{0}||^{2}_{L^{2}}\right],

where
\lambda_{\mathbb{D},\kappa,Q}
is the first eigenvalue of the elliptic operator
-A_{Q}
, for

A_{Q}:=\kappa\Delta+\frac{1}{2}\text{div}(Q(x,x)\nabla\cdot).

(b). There exists a constant
C_{\mathbb{D},d}>0
such that

\lambda_{\mathbb{D},\kappa,Q}\geq C_{\mathbb{D},d}\min\left(\sigma^{2},\kappa/\delta\right),

for every
Q
such that

\inf_{x\in\mathbb{D}_{\delta}}q(x,x)\geq\sigma^{2}.

In view of this theorem, our aim is to show that the noises based on vortex structures in 2D and 3D that we constructed in Sections
2
-
3
, for small
L
, enjoy the property that they have small
\epsilon_{Q}
and large
q(x,x)
, simultaneously, once the other parameters of the model are tuned properly. Here, we assume that
\Gamma,U,L,X_{\cdot}
are independent.

For technical reasons, we demonstrate this only for the torus
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
d=2,3
. The same conclusions should hold true for any regular domains
\mathbb{D}
, but the corrector part of the Green function
h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}(x)
is difficult to handle, hence we prefer to state in the simple case of torus. Note in this case we do not have a boundary hence
\mathbb{D}_{\delta}=\mathbb{D}
,
\delta=0
, and we can put the stopping time
\tau=\infty
in the 3D case.

5.2
The 2D case

The following theorem applies to any realization
\ell
of
L
. For fixed
\ell>0
, we shall use (recall (
9
))

\displaystyle Q_{\ell}\left(x,y\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\otimes K_{\ell}(y,X_{0})\right].

Therefore we have, for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

\xi^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)\xi=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\cdot\xi\right|^{2}\right]

while for
v\in H

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v\left(x\right)^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,y\right)v\left(y\right)dxdy=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v\left(x\right)\cdot K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})dx\right)^{2}\right].

In the next statement we set
\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})
.

Theorem 15

i) There exists a finite constant
C
such that for every
v\in H
and
\ell\in(0,1)
,

\frac{\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle}{\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}}\leq C\sigma^{2}.

ii) There exists a constant
c>0
such
that for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\ell\in(0,1)
,

\frac{v^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)v}{\left|v\right|^{2}}\geq c\;\sigma^{2}|\log\ell|.

Remark 16

We can choose
\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})
to be small, then choose
\ell
small enough such that
\sigma^{2}|\log\ell|
is large, to fulfill the conditions in Theorem
14
.

Proof.
Since
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the function
\nabla_{x}^{\perp}h_{\mathbb{D}}\left(x,y\right)
is bounded above uniformly and does not affect the computations on
K\left(x,y\right)
, which essentially can be based only on the term

\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\left(x-y\right)^{\perp}}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}
. Thus, we use
the approximation, for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
, a.s.,

\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|\leq\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|K\left(x,y\right)\right|\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dy\sim\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dy.

Let
C_{K_{\ell}}
be the random variable defined as

C_{K_{\ell}}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|dx

Under our approximation we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dydx+C_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}dx\right)\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dy+C_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dy+C_{1}=C+C_{1},

hence
C_{K_{\ell}}
is finite a.s. and even uniformly bounded above. Then

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|dx\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}C_{K_{\ell}}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|\frac{\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|}{C_{K_{\ell}}}dx\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}C_{K_{\ell}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|}{C_{K_{\ell}}}dx\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}C_{K_{\ell}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|v\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|dx\right]

Let
\widetilde{C}_{K_{\ell}}
be the deterministic constant
defined as

\widetilde{C}_{K_{\ell}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}C_{K_{\ell}}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|\right]<\infty.

We have proved

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle\leq\widetilde{C}_{K_{\ell}}\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}.

Concerning the size of
\widetilde{C}_{K_{\ell}}
, we have, under the assumptions that
p_{0}
has a bounded
density

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{K_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|K_{\ell}(x,X_{0})\right|\right]

\displaystyle\sim\frac{C}{2\pi}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{0})dy\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{2\pi}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\theta_{\ell}(y-x_{0})p_{0}(x_{0})dx_{0}dy\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cp_{max}}{2\pi}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}dy\right]

\displaystyle\leq C(p_{max},\mathbb{T}^{2})\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})

since

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}dy\leq C_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}.

Therefore

\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle\leq C\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}.

This quantity is small if
\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})
is small.

Concerning
v^{T}Q\left(x,x\right)v
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have, using
again the simplified asymptotics,

\displaystyle v^{T}Q\left(x,x\right)v

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\Gamma^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|K_{\ell}(x,x_{0})\cdot v\right|^{2}p_{0}\left(dx_{0}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\frac{\left(x-y\right)^{\perp}\cdot v}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\theta_{\ell}(y-x_{0})dy\right|^{2}p_{0}\left(dx_{0}\right)\right).

Given any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and unit vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
there is a cone
C\left(x,v\right)\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
(a set of the form
x+rw
,

r\in\left[0,r_{0}\right]
,
\left|w\right|=1
,
w\cdot e\geq\alpha
for some
\left|e\right|=1
and
\alpha\in\left(0,1\right)
) such that

\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{\perp}\cdot v\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|x-x_{0}\right|\left|v\right|\text{ for every }x_{0}\in C\left(x,v\right)

and

\left|C\left(x,v\right)\right|\geq\eta>0.

Moreover, assume
p_{0}\left(dx_{0}\right)
is bounded below by
p_{\min}Leb
for some constant
p_{\min}>0
. We then have

v^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)v\geq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}p_{\min}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{C\left(x,v\right)}\left|\int_{B\left(x_{0},\ell\right)}\frac{\left(x-y\right)^{\perp}\cdot v}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\theta_{\ell}(y-x_{0})dy\right|^{2}dx_{0}\right).

Taking
\ell>0
very small, reduce the cone
C\left(x,v\right)
to the
set

C_{\ell}\left(x,v\right)\subset C\left(x,v\right)

of points
x_{0}
such that

\text{dist}\left(x_{0},\partial C\left(x,v\right)\right)\geq 2\ell.

We then have

y\in C\left(x,v\right)\text{ if }y\in B\left(x_{0},\ell\right)\text{
with }x_{0}\in C_{\ell}\left(x,v\right)

and thus

\displaystyle v^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)v

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}p_{\min}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{C_{\ell}\left(x,v\right)}\left|\int_{B\left(x_{0},\ell\right)}\frac{\frac{1}{2}\left|x-y\right|\left|v\right|}{\left|x-y\right|^{2}}\theta_{\ell}(y-x_{0})dy\right|^{2}dx_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}p_{\min}\left|v\right|^{2}}{4\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{C_{\ell}\left(x,v\right)}\left|\int_{B\left(x_{0},\ell\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|}\theta_{\ell}(y-x_{0})dy\right|^{2}dx_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}p_{\min}\left|v\right|^{2}}{4\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{C_{\ell}\left(x,v\right)}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}(x-x_{0})dx_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\frac{\Gamma^{2}p_{\min}\left|v\right|^{2}}{4\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\int_{C_{\ell}\left(0,v\right)}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}(x_{0})dx_{0}\right)

\displaystyle\geq c(p_{\text{min}},\eta)|v|^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\Gamma^{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x_{0}\right)dx_{0}.

The last inequality is because the quantity
x_{0}\mapsto\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x_{0}\right)
is rotationally invariant, hence the integral
\int_{C_{\ell}\left(0,v\right)}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x_{0}\right)dx_{0}
does not depend on
v
. Since
|C(0,v)|\geq\eta
, we have that

\int_{C_{\ell}\left(0,v\right)}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x_{0}\right)dx_{0}\geq c\eta^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x_{0}\right)dx_{0}.

Let us investigate the problem of the scaling
in
\ell
of the quantity
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x\right)dx
. Given the
mollifier
\theta_{\ell}\left(x\right)=\ell^{-2}\theta\left(\ell^{-1}x\right)
, that we assume the best possible one (nonnegative, smooth, symmetric), let us introduce the smooth symmetric pdf, compactly supported in
B(0,2)
,

\theta^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right):=\int\theta\left(z-z^{\prime}\right)\theta\left(z^{\prime}\right)dz^{\prime}.

Then

\displaystyle\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\ell^{-2}\theta^{\left(2\right)}\left(\ell^{-1}x\right)=\int\ell^{-2}\theta\left(\ell^{-1}z-z^{\prime}\right)\theta\left(z^{\prime}\right)dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\overset{z^{\prime}=\ell^{-1}w}{=}\int\ell^{-2}\theta\left(\ell^{-1}\left(z-w\right)\right)\theta\left(\ell^{-1}w\right)\ell^{-2}dw

\displaystyle=\int\theta_{\ell}\left(z-w\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(w\right)dw

\displaystyle=\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\theta_{\ell}\right)\left(z\right).

Below we shall use the formula

\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)=\int\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)dx

true because

\displaystyle\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\theta_{\ell}\left(y-y^{\prime}-w\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(w\right)dw

\displaystyle\overset{w=x-y^{\prime}}{=}\int\theta_{\ell}\left(y-x\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)dx

(recall
\theta
is symmetric).
After these preliminaries, we have

\int\left(\theta_{\ell}\ast\frac{1}{\left|\cdot\right|}\right)^{2}\left(x\right)dx=\int\left(\int\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y\right)\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}dy\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int\int\int\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y\right)\theta_{\ell}\left(x-y^{\prime}\right)\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y^{\prime}\right|}dydy^{\prime}dx

\displaystyle=\int\int\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(y-y^{\prime}\right)\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y^{\prime}\right|}dydy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int\int\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right)\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y-z\right|}dydz

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y-z\right|}dy\right)\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right)dz.

Now we have to understand first the bahavior of

z\mapsto\int\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y-z\right|}dy.

We can prove that, for
\left|z\right|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y-z\right|}dy\geq\left|\log\left|z\right|\right|.

Indeed, since
|y-z|\leq|y|+|z|
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|y|}\frac{1}{|y-z|}dy\geq\int\frac{1}{|y|}\frac{1}{|y|+|z|}dy

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{1}{\rho+|z|}\rho\;d\rho=\log(1+|z|)-\log|z|

\displaystyle\geq-\log|z|=|\log|z||.

Then,
\int\left(\int\frac{1}{\left|y\right|}\frac{1}{\left|y-z\right|}dy\right)\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right)dz
can be bounded below by

\displaystyle\int\theta_{\ell}^{\left(2\right)}\left(z\right)\left|\log\left|z\right|\right|dz

\displaystyle\geq\ell^{-2}\int_{\left|z\right|\leq\ell}\theta^{(2)}(\ell^{-1}z)\left|\log\left|z\right|\right|dz

\displaystyle\geq-c_{\theta}\ell^{-2}\int_{0}^{\ell}r\log rdr

\displaystyle=c_{\theta}\left(-\ell^{-2}\left[\frac{r^{2}}{2}\log r\right]_{r=0}^{r=\ell}+\ell^{-2}\int_{0}^{\ell}\frac{r^{2}}{2}\frac{1}{r}dr\right)

\displaystyle=c_{\theta}\left(-\ell^{-2}\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\log\ell+\ell^{-2}\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{\theta}\left(\left|\log\ell\right|+\frac{1}{4}\right),

where w.l.o.g.

c_{\theta}:=\inf_{z\in B(0,1)}\theta^{(2)}(z)>0.

This yields that

\frac{v^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)v}{|v|^{2}}\geq c\;\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})|\log\ell|

for some
c>0
and any
\ell\in(0,1)
.

5.3
The 3D case

Recall that we take
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}^{3}
, hence the computation below can be based solely on the
\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{\left|x-y\right|^{3}}\times
part of the kernel
K(x,y)
(
12
), with the other part from
\nabla_{x}h_{\mathbb{D},x_{0}}\left(x\right)\times
uniformly bounded. We also set
\tau=\infty
. The following theorem applies to any realization
\ell
of
L
. We shall use the notation
Q_{\ell}(x,y)
and
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
for fixed
\ell
, similarly to what is done in the 2D case, while recalling (
13
).

In the next statement we set
\sigma^{2}=\mathbb{E}(\Gamma^{2})
.

Theorem 17

i) There exists a constant
C<\infty
such that for every
v\in H
and
\ell\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{\left\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\right\rangle}{\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}}\leq C\;\mathbb{E}(U)\sigma^{2}.

ii)
There exists a constant
c>0
such
that for all
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
,
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\ell\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\frac{v^{T}Q_{\ell}\left(x,x\right)v}{\left|v\right|^{2}}\geq c\;\mathbb{E}(U)\sigma^{2}\ell^{-1}.

Remark 18

We can choose the distribution of
(\Gamma,U)
such that
\mathbb{E}(U)\sigma^{2}
is small, then choose
\ell
small enough such that
\mathbb{E}(U)\sigma^{2}\ell^{-1}
is large, to fulfill the conditions in Theorem
14
.

Proof.
Taking any
v\in H
, we consider

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)^{T}Q_{\ell}(x,y)v(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}v(x)\cdot\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}K\left(x,y\right)\times\left(\int_{0}^{U\wedge\tau}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})dX_{t}\right)dydx\right)^{2}\right].

For any fixed realization of
(\Gamma,U)
, we take expectation over

\mathcal{W}
first

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}v,v\rangle

\displaystyle\sim\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{U}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v(x)\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}\times dX_{t}dydx\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{U}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v(x)\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dydx\cdot dX_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v(x)\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dydx\right|^{2}dt\right]

where the last step is due to Itô isometry. We further bound it above by
moving the norm inside the integral

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})|v(x)|\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dydx\right)^{2}dt\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}C_{u}^{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|v(x)|\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dy}{C_{u}}dx\right)^{2}dt\right]

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}C_{u}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|v(x)|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dydxdt\right]

where the random constant
C_{u}

C_{u}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dydx\leq C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}

for some deterministic finite constant
C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}
(integrate first
dx
then

dy
). Set

\displaystyle C_{u}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dydt\right].

Recall that
X_{0}
has density
p_{0}(x)
which is bounded above uniformly by

p_{\text{max}}
. Since the heat semigroup is an
L^{\infty}
-contraction, the
density of
X_{t}
at any later time
t
is bounded above by
p_{\text{max}}
,
thus we have

\displaystyle C^{\prime}_{u}

\displaystyle\leq Up_{\text{max}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-z)\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}dydz\leq UC^{\prime}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}

(integrating first
dz
then
dy
), for some deterministic finite constant

C_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}^{\prime}
. We conclude with

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{Q}v,v\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\Gamma^{2}C^{\prime}_{u}\right)\|v\|_{H}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left(U\right)\left\|v\right\|_{H}^{2}.

Taking now any unit vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we consider for any

x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
, the quantity

\displaystyle v^{T}Q(x,x)v

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|v\cdot u(x)\right|^{2}\right].

We again fix any realization of
(\Gamma,U,\ell)
, and take expectation
over
\mathcal{W}
first

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|v\cdot u(x)\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\int_{0}^{U}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v\cdot\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}\times dX_{t}dy\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\left(\int_{0}^{U}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dy\cdot dX_{t}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{2}\mathcal{W}\left[\int_{0}^{U}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-X_{t})v\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dy\right|^{2}dt\right]

where the last step is due to Itô isometry.

Since
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{T}^{3}
compact, the density of
X_{t}
, denoted
p_{t}(z)
, converges to the uniform distribution, hence it is not hard to see that there exists some
p_{\text{min}}>0
independent of
t
such that

p_{t}(z)\geq p_{\text{min}},\quad z\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\;t\in[0,U].

Then, we can continue to bound below
\mathcal{W}\left[\Gamma^{2}\left|v\cdot u(x)\right|^{2}\right]
by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{2}\int_{0}^{U}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-z)v\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dy\right|^{2}p_{t}(z)dz

\displaystyle\geq\Gamma^{2}p_{\text{min}}U\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-z)v\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dy\right|^{2}dz.

For any
x\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
, there exist a cone
C(x,v)
and a ball

B=B(x^{\ast},\ell/2)\subset C(x,v)
of radius

\ell/2
with center
x^{\ast}
with
|x-x^{\ast}|=2\ell
, such that provided

z\in B
, we have all the
y
that contribute to the above integral be
contained in
B(x^{\ast},3\ell/2)
and
\ell/2\leq|x-y|\leq 7\ell/2
, and on the other hand the orientation of the cone is chosen
such that
v\times(x-y)
are roughly in the same direction for all the
y
.
This implies that for some absolute constant
c>0
and any
z\in B
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-z)v\times\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{x-y}{|x-y|^{3}}dy\right|\geq c|v|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\theta_{\ell}(y-z)\ell^{-2}dy=c\ell^{-2}.

Thus, we have that, upon squaring and using
|B|\asymp\ell^{3}
,

v^{T}Q_{\ell}(x,x)v\geq c\,p_{\text{min}}\mathbb{E}\left(\Gamma^{2}U\int_{B}\ell^{-4}dz\right)=c\,p_{\text{min}}\mathbb{E}\left(\Gamma^{2}U\right)\ell^{-1}.

This completes the proof.

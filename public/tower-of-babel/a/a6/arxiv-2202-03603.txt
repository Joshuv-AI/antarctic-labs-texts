Abstract
The restricted partition function p N ​ ( n ) p_{N}(n) counts the partitions of n n into at most N N parts. In the nineteenth century Sylvester showed that these partitions can be expressed as a sum of k k -periodic quasi-polynomials ( 1 ≤ k ≤ N 1\leq k\leq N ) which he termed as Waves. It is now well-known that one can easily perform a wave decomposition using a special type of partial fraction decomposition (the so-called q q -partial fractions) of the generating function of p N ​ ( n ) p_{N}(n) . In this paper we show that the coefficients of these q q -partial fractions can be expressed as a linear combination of the Ramanujan sums. In particular, we show, for the first time, an appearance of the degenerate Bernoulli numbers, the degenerate Euler numbers and a special generalization of the Ramanujan sums, which we term as a Gaussian-Ramanujan sum, in the formulae for certain waves. These coefficients not only provide a good approximation of p N ​ ( n ) p_{N}(n) but they can also be used for obtaining good bounds. Further, we provide a combinatorial meaning to these sums. Our approach for partial fractions is based on a projection operator on the I I -adic completion of the ring of polynomials, where I I is an ideal generated by the Cyclotomic polynomial. The eval operator

The key to our methodology is the polynomial-valued eval operator. In
[
14
]
we defined the operator and demonstrated its efficacy to perform partial fractions. As the current work also relies heavily on eval, we first briefly summarize its properties and prove some more basic results necessary for this work.

Let
r(x),s(x)
and
p(x)
be in the ring of polynomials
\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that
s(x)
and
p(x)
are relatively prime. By Bézout’s identity there exist two polynomials
\alpha(x),\beta(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that

\alpha(x)s(x)+\beta(x)p(x)=1.

(10)

Definition 3.0.1
.

Given non-constant polynomials
r(x),s(x),p(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and
p(x),s(x)
satisfy (
10
), we define the evaluation of the rational polynomial
\frac{r(x)}{s(x)}
modulo
p(x)
as

\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r(x)}{s(x)};\ p(x)\right)=(\alpha(x)r(x))\;\textnormal{rem}\;p(x),

where
\alpha(x)s(x)=1\textnormal{ mod }p(x)
and rem is the polynomial remainder operator.

So, essentially the eval operator takes in a rational polynomial and gives out a polynomial with degree less than
\textnormal{deg}(p)
. This operator is well behaved under polynomial arithmetic
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.0.1
.

Given
p(x),r_{i}(x),s_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and
\textnormal{gcd}(s_{i}(x),p(x))=1
for
i=0,1
. The following are some properties of
eval
function:

1.

\textnormal{eval}\left(r_{0}(x);\ p(x)\right)=r_{0}(x)\;\textnormal{rem}\;p(x)

2.

\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)}{s_{0}(x)}+\frac{r_{1}(x)}{s_{1}(x)};\ p(x)\right)=\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)}{s_{0}(x)};p(x)\right)+\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{1}(x)}{s_{1}(x)});p(x)\right)

3.

\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)r_{1}(x)}{s_{0}(x)s_{1}(x)};\ p(x)\right)=\left\{\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)}{s_{0}(x)};\ p(x)\right)\textnormal{eval}\left(\\
\frac{r_{1}(x)}{s_{1}(x)};\ p(x)\right)\right\}\;\textnormal{rem}\;p(x).

The practical utility of eval relies on the following two results.

Lemma 3.0.2

(Substitution Rule 1,
[
14
]
)
.

\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)-p_{0}(x)p(x)}{s_{0}(x)-q_{0}(x)p(x)};\ p(x)\right)=\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{r_{0}(x)}{s_{0}(x)};\ p(x)\right),

for some
p_{0}(x),q_{0}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
. Stated in words: one can successively substitute the occurrences of

by

in both numerator and denominator of the rational polynomial given in the first argument without changing the result.

Theorem 3.0.3

(Extended Cover-Up Method,
[
14
]
)
.

Let
p_{1}(x),\cdots,p_{n}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
be pairwise relatively prime polynomials and let
f(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
satisfying
deg(f)<deg(p_{1}\cdots p_{k})
. Then, we have the following identity

f(x)\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{p_{j}(x)}=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\frac{\textnormal{eval}(f(x)/g_{j}(x);p_{j}(x))}{p_{j}(x)},

(11)

where
g_{j}(x)=p_{1}(x)\cdots\widehat{p_{j}(x)}\cdots p_{k}(x)
and
\ \widehat{}
refers to dropping the corresponding factor.

It is easier to visualize the mechanism of extended cover-up method for a smaller case. The partial fractions (
11
) can be viewed as

\frac{f}{p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}}=\frac{\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f}{\hskip 8.19447ptp_{2}p_{3}};p_{1}\right)}{p_{1}}+\frac{\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f}{p_{1}\hskip 8.19447ptp_{3}};p_{2}\right)}{p_{2}}+\frac{\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f}{p_{1}p_{2}\hskip 8.19447pt};p_{3}\right)}{p_{3}}.

From the above expression one can easily notice that in order to obtain the term associated with
p_{i}(x)
one has to drop
p_{i}(x)
factor from the denominator (as if covering it) and evaluate the resulting expression with
p_{i}(x)
.

For our further computations we need to perform eval with respect to powers of irreducible polynomials.

(

Lemma 3.0.5

Let

Proof.

The idea is to apply the extended cover-up Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{a^{(n)}(x)}{p(x)f(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a^{(n)}(x)a(x)\textnormal{ rem }p(x)}{p(x)}+\frac{a^{(n)}(x)a(x)\textnormal{ rem }f(x)}{f(x)}

\displaystyle\frac{a_{n+1}(x)}{p(x)}+\frac{a^{(n+1)}(x)}{f(x)},

Cyclotomic Polynomials and Fourier Analysis

In this section we discuss the fruitful interaction between eval operator and the cyclotomic polynomials. The
n^{th}
cyclotomic polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
denoted
\Phi_{n}(x)
is a monic irreducible polynomial whose roots are the
n^{th}
primitive roots of unity and satisfy

1-x^{m}=\prod_{d|m}\Phi_{d}(x).

(17)

The degree of
\Phi_{n}(x)
is
\phi(n)
the Euler’s totient function. We also denote
\Psi_{m}(x)
as

\Psi_{m}(x)=\frac{1-x^{m}}{1-x}=1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}.

In Section
6
, we observe that the polynomial
\Psi_{m}(x)
has an algebraic connection to the degenerate Bernoulli and degenerate Euler numbers.

Remark 4.0.1
.

Note that many authors consider
\Phi_{1}(x)=x-1
. But, in view of factors in the generating function
F_{N}(x)
we take
\Phi_{1}(x)=1-x
.

Let us denote the primitive
n^{th}
roots of unity by

\Delta_{n}=\{\xi:\Phi_{n}(\xi)=0\}.

The inverse cyclotomic polynomial, denoted
\Theta_{k}(x)
, is defined by Moree
[
7
]
as

1-x^{k}=\Theta_{k}(x)\Phi_{k}(x).

Therefore,
\Theta_{k}(\xi)\neq 0
if
\xi\in\Delta_{k}
. In
[
7
]
, it was shown that the coefficients of
\Theta_{k}(\xi)
also have very good symmetric properties.

Distinct cyclotomic polynomials are pairwise relatively prime, that is

\textnormal{gcd}(\Phi_{m}(x),\Phi_{n}(x))=1,\textnormal{for }m\neq n.

As a consequence we can express the product as

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}(1-x^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(x)^{N}\Phi_{2}(x)^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\cdots\Phi_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}(x)^{2}\Phi_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor+1}(x)\cdots\Phi_{N}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Phi_{k}(x)^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor}.

In view of the above equation, to perform
q
-partial fraction expansion of

with expressions such as (

Lemma 4.0.1

(Dresden
[
2
]
)
.

Let
m<n
positive integers. Then, we have

\Phi_{m}(x)u(x)+\Phi_{n}(x)v(x)=1.

(18)

The polynomials

and

Case

Let

From the above lemma we have

\displaystyle\alpha(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{1}{\Phi_{m}(x)};\Phi_{n}(x)\right)=u(x)\textnormal{ rem }\Phi_{n}(x)

(19)

\displaystyle\beta(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{1}{\Phi_{n}(x)};\Phi_{m}(x)\right)=v(x)\textnormal{ rem }\Phi_{m}(x),

(20)

where

are given in (

). By Lemma

Corollary 4.0.2

For

distinct positive integers and

Lemma 4.0.3
.

Let

be integers. Then, we have

\textnormal{eval}\left(x^{k};\Phi_{m}(x)\right)=x^{k\%m}\textnormal{ rem }\Phi_{m}(x).

\textnormal{eval}\left(x^{k+n};\Phi_{m}(x)\right)=-\textnormal{eval}\left(x^{k};\Phi_{m}(x)\right),

Suppose

Furthermore, it is easy to see that

∎

(

Let

(

For

\frac{1}{(1-x)(1+x^{m})}=\frac{1}{2(1-x)}+\frac{\Psi_{m}(m)}{2(1+x^{m})}.

Proceeding inductively we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.0.6
.

For

the following partial fraction holds:

\frac{1}{(1-x)^{k}(1+x^{m})}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{g_{j}^{(m)}(1)}{2(1-x)^{k-j}}+\frac{g_{k}^{(m)}(x)}{1+x^{m}},

(22)

where
g_{j}^{(m)}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{2}\Psi_{m}(x)\right)^{j}\textnormal{ rem }(1+x^{m})
and
g_{0}^{(m)}(x)=1
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
k

21

. Suppose the result holds for

By performing partial fractions on the second term we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)^{k+1}(1+x^{m})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}g_{j}^{(m)}(1)(1-x)^{j}}{2(1-x)^{k+1}}+\frac{g_{k}^{(m)}(1)}{2(1-x)}+\frac{g_{k+1}^{(m)}(x)}{1+x^{m}}

where we used the fact

4.1
Fourier Series and
\textnormal{eval}(\cdot,\Phi_{m}(x)^{r})

The power of eval with respect to cyclotomic polynomials emerges in the context of finite Fourier series. In this regard, the following substitution plays a vital role.

Lemma 4.1.1

(Substitution Rule 2)
.

Let
m,k
be positive integers and
f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
be two polynomials such that
\textnormal{gcd}(g(x),\Phi_{m}(x))=1.
Then,

\left.\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)};\ \Phi_{m}(x)^{r}\right)\right|_{x=\xi}=\frac{f(\xi)}{g(\xi)}\quad\textnormal{ for }\xi\in\Delta_{m}.

(23)

Proof.

Since
\textnormal{gcd}(g(x),\Phi_{m}(x)^{r})=1
we have

\alpha(x)g(x)+\beta(x)\Phi_{m}(x)^{r}=1.

Setting
x=\xi
in the above equation we get
\alpha(\xi)=1/g(\xi)
. Therefore,

\left.\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)};\ \Phi_{m}(x)^{r}\right)\right|_{x=\xi}=f(x)\alpha(x)\textnormal{ rem }\Phi_{m}(x)^{r}|_{x=\xi}=f(\xi)\alpha(\xi)-q(\xi)\Phi_{m}(\xi)^{r}

for some
q(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
.
∎

For the purpose of determining the coefficients of the
q
-partial fractions we need to determine the Fourier series of

H(x)=\frac{h(x)}{\Phi_{m}(x)^{r}}=\frac{\Theta_{m}(x)^{r}h(x)}{(1-x^{m})^{r}}\quad\textnormal{ for }h(x)=\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)};\Phi_{m}(x)^{r}\right),

f(x),g(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and
g(x)
relatively prime to
\Phi_{m}(x)
.

It is well known that when
r=1
the Fourier series of
H(x)
is a finite Fourier series. So, we first determine the finite Fourier series for the case
r=1
.

H(x)=\frac{\Theta_{m}(x)h(x)}{1-x^{m}},\quad\textnormal{ for }\quad h(x)=\textnormal{eval}\left(\frac{f(x)}{g(x)};\Phi_{m}(x)\right).

Let the sequence
(a(n))
be a periodic function on
\mathbb{Z}
with period
m
and let
H(x)
be a generating function for
(a(n))
. Then, the finite Fourier series expansion is given by

a(n)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\xi^{m}=1}}{{\xi\neq 1}}}\Theta_{m}(\xi)h(\xi)\xi^{-n}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{m}}\Theta_{m}(\xi)h(\xi)\xi^{-n},

as
\Theta_{m}(\xi)=0
if
\Phi_{m}(\xi)\neq 0
the sum in the above equation runs over
\Delta_{m}
. By Substitution Rule 2 (Lemma

) and the finite Fourier series expansion we deduce

\displaystyle H(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta_{m}(x)h(x)}{1-x^{m}}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}a(n)x^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{m}}\Theta_{m}(\xi)h(\xi)\xi^{-n}\right)x^{n}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{m}}\frac{\Theta_{m}(\xi)f(\xi)}{g(\xi)}\xi^{-n}\right)x^{n}.

Using the above reasoning we could prove that the degenerate Euler number (Equation (

6
The Waves
W_{1}(n;N)
and
W_{2}(n;N)

In this section we provide direct and explicit formulas for the second wave. In
[
14
]
the current author has demonstrated that the first wave
W_{1}(n;N)
can be expressed in terms of the so called degenerate Bernoulli numbers. Here we show that the second wave
W_{2}(n;N)
can be expressed in terms of degenerate Euler and degenerate Bernoulli numbers. The crucial point of our derivation is that the first and second cyclotomic polynomials are linear, i.e.,
\Phi_{1}(x)=1-x
and
\Phi_{2}(x)=1+x
.

Degenerate Bernoulli and degenerate Euler numbers have been defined by Carlitz
[
1
]

[

the current author demonstrated that the polynomial

\Psi_{m}(x)=\frac{1-x^{m}}{1-x}=1+x+\cdots+x^{m-1}

\frac{m(1-x)}{1-x^{m}}=\frac{m}{\Psi_{m}(x)}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{k}(m)}{k!}(1-x)^{k},

(32)

where
\tilde{\beta}_{k}(m)=m^{k}\beta_{k}(1/m)
where
\beta_{k}(m)
is the degenerate Bernoulli number. Furthermore, if
m
is odd and replacing
x

-x

\frac{m(1+x)}{1-x^{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\beta}_{k}(m)}{k!}(1+x)^{k}.

(33)

Recasting the result in
[
14
]
we have the result.

Theorem 6.0.1

(
[
14
]
)
.

\Gamma_{01(N-j)}(N)=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{N!}\sum_{j_{2}+\cdots+j_{N}=j}\frac{\tilde{\beta}_{j_{2}}(2)\cdots\tilde{\beta}_{j_{N}}(N)}{j_{2}!\cdots j_{N}!},

for

Using (

\Gamma_{01(N-j)}(N+1)=\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{k=0}^{j+1}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}\tilde{\beta}_{k}(N+1)\Gamma_{01(N+k-j-1)}(N).

Now, we derive a formula for the coefficients associated with the second wave. Both degenerate Bernoulli and the degenerate Euler numbers appear in coefficients associated with the second wave. Degenerate Euler number was defined by Carlitz (see

]
and the references therein) as

\frac{2}{(1+\lambda t)^{1/\lambda}+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}(\lambda)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

We consider
\lambda=1/m
, for some positive integer
m
, and substitute
1+\lambda t=x
in (

\frac{2}{1+x^{m}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(m)}{k!}(1-x)^{k},\textnormal{ where }\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(m)=m^{k}\varepsilon_{k}(m),

For

, we can replace

\frac{2}{1-x^{2n+1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(2n+1)}{k!}(1+x)^{k}.

(36)

Lemma 6.0.2
.

The degenerate Euler number
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(m)

.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.0.6

For

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\xi^{k}=1,\xi\neq 1}g_{k\lfloor N/k\rfloor}^{(N)}(\xi)\xi^{-n}

\displaystyle P_{k}^{(N)}(x)

\displaystyle\prod_{s=0}^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor-1}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(1-x^{sk+j})\prod_{i=1}^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor}\frac{1-x^{ik}}{1-x^{k}}\prod_{j=k\lfloor N/k\rfloor+1}^{N}(1-x^{j})

Theorem 7.0.1
.

For
k
an integer such that

\Gamma_{j,k,\lfloor N/k\rfloor}(N)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{k}}\frac{\xi^{-j}}{P_{k}^{(N)}(\xi)}

(41)

where

is given in (

In order to further simplify (

Definition 7.0.1
.

Given
0\leq s<k
two integers, we define the Gaussian-Ramanujan sum as

\sigma_{k}(t;s)=\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{k}}\frac{\xi^{-t}}{(\xi)_{s}},

where
\Delta_{k}
is the set of all
k^{th}
primitive roots of unity, and
(q)_{n}
is given in the
q

(q)_{n}=(q;q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}),
for
n\geq 1
and
(q)_{0}=1.

It can be easily seen that
\sigma_{k}(t;s)
is periodic in
t
with a periodicity
k
. The values of
\sigma_{k}(t;s)
are of the form
l/k
, where
l
is an integer. We call the sum
\sigma_{k}(t;s)
the Gaussian-Ramanujan sum as it can be expressed as a linear combination of Ramanujan sums with coefficients associated with the Gaussian binomial coefficients evaluated at the primitive roots of unity. In particular, we have
\sigma_{k}(t;0)=c_{k}(-t)=c_{k}(t),
and
\sigma_{k}(t;k-1)=\frac{1}{k}\sigma_{k}(t;0)=\frac{1}{k}c_{k}(t)
, where
c_{k}(t)
is the Ramanujan sum.

Substituting
\xi\in\Delta_{k}
for
x
in
P_{k}^{(N)}(x)
given in (
40

\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(1-\xi^{j})=k\quad\textnormal{ with }\quad\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\xi^{kj}=i

(42)

we have

P_{k}^{(N)}(\xi)=k^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor}\lfloor N/k\rfloor!(1-\xi)\cdots(1-\xi^{N\%k}).

Therefore, by (

\displaystyle\Gamma_{jk\lfloor N/k\rfloor}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{k}}\frac{\xi^{-j}}{P_{k}^{(N)}(\xi)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor+1}\lfloor N/k\rfloor!}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{k}}\frac{\xi^{-j}}{(1-\xi)\cdots(1-\xi^{N\%k})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{\lfloor N/k\rfloor+1}\lfloor N/k\rfloor!}\sigma_{k}(j;N\%k).

Let
R_{k,s}(x)=\sum^{k-1}_{t=0}\sigma_{k}(t;s)x^{t}

Lemma 7.0.3

.

For

\sigma_{k}(t;s)=\sigma_{k}(t;s-1)+\sigma_{k}(t-s;s),

The result is straightforward by
(\xi)_{s}=(\xi)_{s-1}-\xi^{s}(\xi)_{s-1}

∎

By Theorem

From the above recurrence relation we have

R_{k,s-1}(x)=(1-x^{k-s})R_{k,s}(x)\mod 1-x^{k},

Lemma 7.0.4
.

R_{k,s}(x)=\frac{1}{k}(x)_{k-1}(x)_{k-1-s}\mod(1-x^{k}).

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\Delta_{k}

We solve this by taking an inverse discrete Fourier transform to obtain

a_{t}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}R_{k,s}(\xi^{j})\xi^{-jt}.

(43)

R_{k,s}(\xi^{j})=\begin{cases}0&\textnormal{gcd}(j,k)\neq 1\\
(\xi^{j})_{k-1-s}=k/(\xi^{j})_{s}&\textnormal{gcd}(j,k)=1.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have

a_{t}=\frac{1}{k}\mathop{\sum_{0\leq j\leq k}}_{\textnormal{gcd}(j,k)=1}\xi^{-jt}\frac{k}{(\xi^{j})}_{s}=\sum_{\eta\in\Delta_{k}}\eta^{-t}/(\eta)_{s}=\sigma_{k}(t;s).

∎

Lemma 7.0.7

\sigma_{k}(t;s)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{k}}\xi^{t}(\xi)_{k-1-s}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\sum_{d|k}\sigma_{d}(t;s)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\sum_{\xi\in\Delta_{d}}\xi^{t}(\xi^{j})_{k-1-s}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\eta^{jt}(\eta^{j})_{k-1-s},

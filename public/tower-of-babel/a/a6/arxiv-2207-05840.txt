Danila Cherkashin, Alexey Gordeev, Georgii Strukov

Abstract
This paper deals with the minimum number m H ​ ( r ) m_{H}(r) of edges in an H H -free hypergraph with the chromatic number more than r r . We show how bounds on Ramsey and Turán numbers imply bounds on m H ​ ( r ) m_{H}(r) .

Introduction

A
hypergraph

G=(V,E)
consists of a finite set of vertices
V
and a family
E
of subsets of
V
, which are called edges.
A hypergraph
G
is called
n
-graph
if every edge in
G
has size
n
.
A
vertex
r
-coloring
of a hypergraph
G=(V,E)
is a map from
V
to
\{1,\dots,r\}
.
A coloring is
proper
if there are no monochromatic edges, i.e. any edge
g\in E
contains two vertices of different colors.
The
chromatic number
of a hypergraph
G
is the smallest number
\chi(G)
such that there exists a proper
\chi(G)
-coloring of
G
.
A vertex subset is
independent
in
G
if it does not contain an edge of
G
.
The
independence number
of
G
is the maximal size
\alpha(G)
of an independent set.

Although the subjects are defined and studied for
n
-graphs, this paper deals with 3-graphs only.
For convenience, we restrict cited results on 3-graphs despite most of them being more general.

Let us start with best known bounds for 3-graphs without restrictions.
Let
m(r)
be the smallest number of edges in a 3-graph with the chromatic number at least
r+1
. Then

(1+o(1))\frac{4}{e^{2}}r^{3}\leq m(r)\leq\binom{2r+1}{3}=(1+o(1))\frac{4}{3}r^{3},

see
[
2
]
for details. See also the survey on related problems on
n
-graphs
[
10
]
.

A subgraph of a 3-graph
G=(V,E)
is a 3-graph
G^{\prime}=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
, where
V^{\prime}\subset V
,
E^{\prime}\subset E
and
E^{\prime}\subset\binom{V^{\prime}}{3}
.
An
induced subgraph
of a 3-graph
G=(V,E)
is a 3-graph
G[V^{\prime}]
, formed from a vertex set
V^{\prime}\subset V
and
all
of the edges from
G
contained in
V^{\prime}
. An
H
-free 3-graph is a 3-graph that does not contain
H
as a subgraph.

Bohman, Frieze and Mubayi
[
1
]
introduced the quantity
m_{H}(r)
, which is the minimal number of edges in an
H
-free 3-graph with chromatic number more than
r
.
First of all, note that sometimes
m_{H}(r)
is undefined. More precisely, this is the case iff
H
is a
hyperforest
, i.e. every two edges of
H
share at most one vertex and
H
has no cycles.
We discuss it with more details later.

Structure of the paper.

The rest of the introduction is devoted to the known results, our contribution and classical tools.
Subsection
1.1
contains known bounds on
m_{H}(r)
, mostly from paper
[
1
]
.
Our results are listed in Subsection
1.2
, where we show some relations
m_{H}(r)
has with Ramsey and Turán numbers.
The tools which we use in proofs of these relations are recalled in Subsection
1.3
.
The correctness of the definition of

is discussed in Subsection
1.4
.
In Section
2
we give proofs of the results. In Section
3
we list some open questions.

1.1
Known bounds

Definition 1
.

Let
H
be a 3-graph. Define the balance
\rho(H)
of
H
as follows

\rho(H):=\max_{H^{\prime}}\frac{e^{\prime}-1}{v^{\prime}-3},

where
H^{\prime}
is a (nonempty) subgraph of
H
with
v^{\prime}
vertices and
e^{\prime}
edges.
We say that
H
is balanced if this maximum occurs for
H
.

Theorem 1

(
[
1
]
)
.

If
H
is a balanced 3-graph with
\rho(H)>\frac{1}{2}
, then

m_{H}(r)=O\left((r^{2}\log r)^{\frac{3\rho-1}{2\rho-1}}\right).

In the following case an
H
-free 3-graph is also called a simple or linear 3-graph.

Theorem 2

(
[
7
,
1
]
)
.

Let
H
be the 3-graph on 4 vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\}\}
, then

m_{H}(r)=\theta\left(r^{4}\log^{2}r\right).

In the following case an
H
-free 3-graph is also known as a graph with independent neighbourhoods. The upper bound is a corollary of Theorem
1
.

Theorem 3

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
H
be the 3-graph on 5 vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{1,2,5\},\{3,4,5\}\}
, then

cr^{\frac{7}{2}}\leq m_{H}(r)\leq Cr^{\frac{7}{2}}\log^{\frac{7}{4}}r.

Hypergraph
H
in the following theorem is also known as
K^{3}_{4}
; further we denote it by
K_{4}
since we have a deal with 3-graphs only.

Theorem 4

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
H
be the 3-graph on 4 vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,2,4\},\{1,3,4\},\{2,3,4\}\}
, then

m_{H}(r)=O\left(r^{3}\log^{3}r\right).

1.2
Results

1.2.1
Relation with Ramsey numbers

A
Ramsey number

R(H,K_{t})
is the minimal number of vertices in a 3-graph
G
that guarantees existence of a copy of
H
or an independent set on
t
elements in
G
.
We show that bounds on Ramsey numbers imply bounds on
m_{H}(r)
.

Theorem 5
.

Consider a 3-graph
H
. Let
a,b>1
and
A,B>0
be numbers such that for all
t\geq 1

At^{a}<R(H,K_{t})\leq Bt^{b}.

(1)

Then for all
r\geq 1

B^{\prime}r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}\leq m_{H}(r)\leq A^{\prime}r^{\frac{3a}{a-1}},

(2)

where

A^{\prime}=\frac{(1+o(1))^{3a}}{6A^{\frac{3}{a-1}}},\qquad B^{\prime}=\left(\frac{(1-2^{1-b})}{2^{\frac{b}{b-1}+b+3}(12e)^{b-1}B\bigl(\frac{b}{b-1}+2\bigr)^{b}}\right)^{1/(b-1)}.

(3)

In
[
8
]
it was shown that for all
t\geq 1

\frac{at^{3/2}}{(\log t)^{3/4}}\leq R(C_{3},K_{t})\leq bt^{3/2},

(4)

where
C_{3}
is a
loose cycle
with 3 edges, i.e. the 3-graph on 6 vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{3,4,5\},\{5,6,1\}\}
, and
a,b>0
are some constants.
A straightforward application of Theorem
5
gives

Corollary 1
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
r\geq 1

Cr^{5}\leq m_{C_{3}}(r).

(5)

However, the upper bound can be made more precise than the one implied by Theorem
5
.

Proposition 1
.

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
r\geq 1

m_{C_{3}}(r)\leq Cr^{5}(\ln r)^{5/2}.

(6)

Theorem 6
.

Suppose that for a 3-graph
H
and some constants
A,B>0
and
b>1
we have

t^{At}<R(H,K_{t})\leq t^{Bt^{b}}.

(7)

Then

r^{3}\ln^{1/b-o(1)}r\leq m_{H}(r)\leq(1+o(1))\frac{8}{A^{3}}r^{3}\left(\frac{\ln r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}.

(8)

Let
K_{4}^{-}
be the 3-graph with four vertices and three edges.
In
[
5
]
it was stated that

R(K_{4}^{-},K_{t})=t^{\Theta(t)}.

(9)

Then Theorem
6
immediately yields

Corollary 2
.

For some constant
C>0
,

r^{3}\ln^{1-o(1)}r\leq m_{K_{4}^{-}}(r)\leq Cr^{3}\left(\frac{\ln r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}.

(10)

The following bounds are from
[
4
]
:

2^{c_{1}n\log n}\leq R(K_{4},K_{n})\leq 2^{c_{2}n^{2}\log n}

(11)

for some constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and any sufficiently large
n
.
A direct application of Theorem
6
gives

Corollary 3
.

For some constant
C>0
,

r^{3}\ln^{1/2-o(1)}r\leq m_{K_{4}}(r)\leq Cr^{3}\left(\frac{\ln r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}.

(12)

Moreover, using the same idea of graph reduction by coloring some big independent set in the same color, as in proof of theorems of this section (but with much less effort), we obtain

Proposition 2
.

Let
H
be the hypergraph on 7 vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{1,4,5\},\{1,6,7\},\{2,4,6\}\}
. Then

m_{H}(r)>Cr^{4},

(13)

where
C=e/288
.

1.2.2
Relation with Turán numbers

A
Turán number

\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
is the maximum number of edges in an
H
-free 3-graph with
n
vertices. As with Ramsey numbers, we can obtain bounds for
m_{H}(r)
from bounds on Turán numbers.

Theorem 7
.

Let
H
be a 3-graph and let
C_{1}>0
be a constant such that for all
n\geq 1

\mathrm{ex}(n,H)\leq C_{1}n^{b}.

(14)

Then for any constant
C_{2}>0
there exists some
R=R(C_{2})
such that for any
r>R

m_{H}(r)>\frac{C_{2}r^{\frac{2b}{b-1}}}{(\ln\ln r)^{1+\frac{2b}{b-1}}}.

(15)

The following proposition is folklore.

Proposition 3
.

Let
H
be a 3-graph, such that
t=|V(H)|=|E(H)|+2
. Suppose that there is an ordering of edges
h_{1},\dots,h_{t-2}
such that
h_{i}=\{a_{i},b_{i},c_{i}\}
for each
i>1
, where
\{a_{i},b_{i}\}\subset h_{j(i)}
for some
{j(i)}<i
and
c_{i}\not\in h_{j}
for
j<i
. Then

ex(n,H)\leqslant(t-3)\binom{n}{2}.

(16)

Note that such a graph
H
is balanced with balance
1
, so Theorems
7
and
1
give

Corollary 4
.

Suppose that 3-graph
H
satisfies the conditions of Proposition
3
. Then for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and for any sufficiently large
r

C_{1}\frac{r^{4}}{(\ln\ln r)^{5}}<m_{H}(r)<C_{2}r^{4}(\ln r)^{2}.

(17)

1.3
Tools

1.3.1
Applications of LLL

We are going to use the following classical technique.

Lemma 1

(Symmetric Lovász local lemma)
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
be events with probabilities

\mathbf{P}(A_{i})\leq p
for
1\leq i\leq n
.
Suppose that each event
A_{i}
is mutually independent of all the other events except at most
d
of them.
If
ep(d+1)<1
, then
\mathbf{P}(\wedge_{i=1}^{n}\overline{A}_{i})>0
.

The following lemma is a folklore corollary of Lemma
1
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a 3-graph. Let
r>0
be some integer. Suppose that for every
v\in V(G)

\deg v\leq\Delta=\frac{r^{2}}{3e}.

(18)

Then
G
can be properly colored with

r

colors.

Proof.

Let us assign to each vertex one of
r
colors at random uniformly and independently.
For each
f\in E
consider an event
A_{f}=\text{``$f\in E$ is monocromatic''}
. The probability of such event is
1/r^{2}=:p
. Clearly each event
A_{f}
is independent of
\{A_{g}\colon f\cap g=\emptyset\}
. There are at most
3(\Delta-1)+1<3\Delta
events (including
A_{f}
itself) that are not in this family, so

ep(d+1)<\frac{3e\Delta}{r^{2}}=1,

(19)

and, by Lemma
1
,
G
has some coloring in
r
colors with no monochromatic edges.
∎

Lemma
2
allows us to care only about vertices with big degree when we are coloring some graph in
r
colors. Let us make this statement more precise.

Definition 2
.

Denote
c=(12e)^{-1}
.
Consider an integer
r
and a 3-graph
G
.
Define

V_{small}=\{v\in V(G)\mid\deg v\leq cr^{2}\},

(20)

V_{big}=V(G)\setminus V_{small}.

(21)

By a direct application of Lemma
2
we obtain

Lemma 3
.

Let
r
be an integer and
G
be a 3-graph.
Then
G[V_{small}]
is
\lceil r/2\rceil
-colorable.

1.3.2
Greedy coloring

The following approach was introduced by Pluhár
[
9
]
. Let us consider an arbitrary linear order
\pi
on the vertex set of a given 3-graph
G
.
Now one can go through vertices in order
\pi
and color each considered vertex into the minimal color which does not create a monochromatic edge at this moment.
By the construction, the result is a proper coloring
f
in some number of colors.

Let an
r
-chain
be a set of edges
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}
such that
|g_{i}\cap g_{j}|=1
if
|i-j|=1
, and 0 otherwise.
We say that an
r
-chain is
ordered in
\pi
if
i<j
implies that every vertex of
g_{i}
is no bigger that every vertex of
g_{j}
with respect to
\pi
.

Pluhár noted that the number of colors in
f
is the maximal size of an ordered chain plus one.
In particular, if
f
uses more than
r
colors, then
G
contains an
r
-chain (in fact, a lot of
r
-chains).
Then Gyárfás and Lehel
[
6
]
extended the latter statement as follows.
If a greedy coloring of a 3-graph
G
uses more than
r
colors, then
G
contains a copy of every hypertree (connected hyperforest) with
r
edges.

1.4
Correctness

We have claimed earlier that the function
m_{H}(r)
is properly defined for all
H
except hyperforests.
Here we provide a brief proof of this statement. Indeed, if
H
is a hyperforest, then, as was discussed in the previous subsection, any
H
-free hypergraph can be properly colored in
|E(H)|
colors using greedy coloring method, so
m_{H}(r)
is not defined for
r>|E(H)|
.

Otherwise,
H
contains either a pair of edges intersecting by two vertices, or a
loose cycle

C_{l}
for some
l\geq 3
, i.e. a 3-graph on
2l
vertices with the set of edges
\{\{1,2,3\},\{3,4,5\},\dots,\{2l-1,2l,1\}\}
.
In the first case any simple 3-graph is
H
-free, so, in view of Theorem
2
,
m_{H}(r)=O(r^{4}\log^{2}r)
.
For a graph with a loose cycle
C_{l}
one may count the balance of the cycle as follows

\rho(C_{l})=\frac{l-1}{2l-3}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4l-6}.

The maximum in the definition of the balance is achieved on the whole cycle, so
C_{l}
is balanced with the balance greater than
\frac{1}{2}
.
Thus one can apply Theorem
1

to obtain the inequality

m_{H}(r)\leq m_{C_{l}}(r)=O\left((r^{2}\log r)^{\frac{3\rho-1}{2\rho-1}}\right)=O(r^{2l}\log^{l}r).

2.1
Proof of Theorem

2.1.1
The upper bound

The lower bound on
R(H,K_{t})
states that for each
t\geq 1
there is an
H
-free 3-graph
G
with
|V(G)|=At^{a}
and the independence number
\alpha(G)<t
. The chromatic number of
G
satisfies

\chi(G)\geq\frac{|V(G)|}{\alpha(G)}\geq At^{a-1}.

(22)

Now for a given
r
we choose integer
t
such that
A(t-1)^{a-1}\leq r<At^{a-1}
. Then

|E(G)|\leq\frac{|V(G)|^{3}}{6}=\frac{A^{3}t^{3a}}{6}=\frac{A^{3-\frac{3a}{a-1}}}{6}(At^{a-1})^{\frac{3a}{a-1}}<\frac{A^{3-\frac{3a}{a-1}}}{6}\biggl(A\Bigl(\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)(t-1)\Bigr)^{a-1}\biggr)^{\frac{3a}{a-1}}\leq\frac{(1+o(1))^{3a}}{6A^{\frac{3}{a-1}}}\cdot r^{\frac{3a}{a-1}}.

(23)

2.1.2
The lower bound

Suppose that
G
is an
H
-free 3-graph with
|E(G)|<B^{\prime}r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}
that is not
r
-colorable. Let such
r
be minimal possible.
From Lemma
3

it follows that
G[V_{big}]
cannot be colored with no more than
r/2
colors.
Then

|E(G[V_{big}])|\geq\frac{B^{\prime}}{2^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}}\cdot r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2},

(24)

Let
V_{big}=V_{0}\cup V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\dots
, where

V_{k}=\{v\in V(G)\mid 2^{k}cr^{2}\leq\deg v<2^{k+1}cr^{2}\},

(25)

and
c=(12e)^{-1}
is from the definition of
V_{big}
.
Suppose there is some
k
for which

\alpha(G[V_{k}])\geq\frac{B^{\prime}}{2^{k-1}cr^{2}}\bigl(r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}-(r-1)^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}\bigr).

(26)

Then we can paint the vertices from a corresponding independent set in one color. Now if we remove colored vertices from
G
, we get a 3-graph with less than

B^{\prime}r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot 2^{k}cr^{2}\cdot\alpha(G[V_{k}])=B^{\prime}(r-1)^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}

(27)

edges that is not
(r-1)
-colorable. That is in contradiction to the minimality of

.
So we can assume that for every

|V_{k}|<B\left(\frac{B^{\prime}}{2^{k-1}cr^{2}}\bigl(r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}-(r-1)^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}\bigr)\right)^{b}.

(28)

Using the fact that

|V_{k}|<B\left(\frac{B^{\prime}}{2^{k-1}cr^{2}}\cdot\biggl(\frac{b}{b-1}+2\biggr)\cdot r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+1}\right)^{b}=C\left(\frac{r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+1}}{2^{k-1}r^{2}}\right)^{b}=\frac{1}{2^{b(k-1)}}\cdot Cr^{\frac{b}{b-1}},

(29)

where

(30)

Now let
g_{k}
be the number of edges that pass through vertices of

|E(G[V_{big}])|<\sum_{k\geq 0}g_{k}<\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{k+1}cr^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{b(k-1)}}\cdot Cr^{\frac{b}{b-1}}=cC2^{b+1}r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}\sum_{k\geq 0}2^{(1-b)k}<\frac{cC2^{b+1}}{1-2^{1-b}}\cdot r^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}.

(31)

Combining this with (

) and substituting

\frac{B^{\prime}}{2^{\frac{b}{b-1}+2}}<\frac{(1/12e)B{B^{\prime}}^{b}\bigl(\frac{b}{b-1}+2\bigr)^{b}2^{b+1}}{(1/12e)^{b}(1-2^{1-b})}

(32)

or

{B^{\prime}}^{b-1}>\frac{(1-2^{1-b})}{2^{\frac{b}{b-1}+b+3}(12e)^{b-1}B\bigl(\frac{b}{b-1}+2\bigr)^{b}},

2.2
Proof of Proposition
1

A direct application of Theorem
5
gives the upper bound
r^{9+o(1)}
.
To obtain a better upper bound, let us count the number of edges in the example constructed in
[
8
]
.
The construction is as follows: let
G_{q}
be a generalized quadrangle, i.e. a
(q+1)
-regular
(q+1)
-uniform simple
C_{3}
-free hypergraph on
q^{3}+q^{2}+q+1
vertices.
It is known that such hypergraph exists whenever
q
is a power of a prime number.
Each edge of
G_{q}
is then replaced by a random 3-graph
F_{q}
which is built as follows: vertices of
F_{q}
are randomly split into a set
V=\{v_{ij}\ |\ 1\leq i,j\leq\tau\}
and
2\tau

of roughly the same size, where
\tau=\lfloor 8\sqrt{\log q}\rfloor
.
Edges of
F_{q}
are all sets of the form
\{v_{ij},a,b\}
, where

,

.

The result of the construction is a hypergraph on

vertices.
With a high probability its independence number does not exceed

\frac{2\tau n}{q}=16q^{2}\sqrt{\log q},

i.e. its chromatic number is at least
cq/\sqrt{\log q}
.

At the same time, this hypergraph has

cq^{3}\tau^{2}\left(\frac{q+1-\tau^{2}}{2\tau}\right)^{2}=cq^{5}

2.3
Proof of Theorem
6

2.3.1
The upper bound

For any
t\geq 1
there is a
H
-free 3-graph
G_{t}
with
|V(G_{t})|=t^{At}
and
\alpha(G_{t})<t
.
Then

\chi(G_{t})\geq\frac{|V(G_{t})|}{\alpha(G_{t})}>\frac{t^{At}}{t}=t^{At-1},

so one may put
r=t^{At-1}
. First we prove the desired bound only for such
r
. Hence

\ln r=(At-1)\ln t,

so

\frac{1}{A}\frac{\ln r}{\ln\ln r}=\frac{1}{A}\frac{(At-1)\ln t}{\ln((At-1)\ln t)}=(1+o(1))t.

Finally,

|E(G_{t})|\leq|V(G_{t})|^{3}=t^{3At}=r^{3}t^{3}=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{r\ln r}{A\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}.

Then, consider any
r
between
(t-1)^{A(t-1)-1}
and
t^{At-1}
. Let
A^{\prime}
be such that
r=t^{A^{\prime}t-1}
.
For a large enough
t
one has

t^{At/2-1}<(t-1)^{A(t-1)-1},

so
A>A^{\prime}>A/2
.
Consider any subgraph
G^{\prime}_{t}
of
G_{t}
on
t^{A^{\prime}t}
vertices.
It is
H
-free and has the independence number smaller than
t
, so the chromatic number of
G^{\prime}_{t}
is at least
r
.
Then one may repeat the previous argument to show

|E(G^{\prime}_{t})|\leq|V(G^{\prime}_{t})|^{3}=t^{3A^{\prime}t}=r^{3}t^{3}=(1+o(1))\left(\frac{r\ln r}{A^{\prime}\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}<(1+o(1))\left(\frac{2r\ln r}{A\ln\ln r}\right)^{3}.

2.3.2
The lower bound

Essentially, this proof follows along the proof of the lower bound in Theorem
5
.
First, we show that for each
\varepsilon\in(0,1/b)
there is some constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

m_{H}(r)\geq C_{\varepsilon}r^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r.

(34)

Suppose that
G
is an
H
-free 3-graph with
|E(G)|<C_{\varepsilon}r^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r
that is not
r
-colorable. Here
C_{\varepsilon}
is some constant which depends only on

and which will be chosen later.
Let such
r
be minimal possible.
By Lemma
3
we have that
G[V_{big}]
should not be
\lfloor r/2\rfloor
-colorable.

Let us look at the value of
\alpha(G[V_{big}])
.
If

\alpha(G[V_{big}])\geq\frac{2C_{\varepsilon}}{cr^{2}}\bigl(r^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r-(r-1)^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}(r-1)\bigr),

(35)

then we can paint vertices of the biggest independent set of
G[V_{big}]
in one color.
By removing colored vertices from
G
we get
H
-free 3-graph that is not
(r-1)
-colorable but has less than

C_{\varepsilon}r^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r-\frac{cr^{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{2C_{\varepsilon}}{cr^{2}}\bigl(r^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r-(r-1)^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}(r-1)\bigr)=C_{\varepsilon}(r-1)^{3}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}(r-1)

(36)

edges, which contradicts the minimality of
r
.

So the contrary to (
35
) must hold.
Since
r^{3}\ln^{a}r-(r-1)^{3}\ln^{a}(r-1)<4r^{2}\ln^{a}r
for all
r\geq 1
and
a\geq 0
, we have

\alpha(G[V_{big}])<\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\cdot\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r.

(37)

Due to the bound (
7
),

\left|V_{big}\right|<\left(\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\cdot\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r\right)^{B((8C_{\varepsilon}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r)/c)^{b}}.

(38)

We take logarithm of the right-hand side and see that when
C_{\varepsilon}\leq c/8

B\left(\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r\right)^{b}\cdot\left(\ln\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}+\ln\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r\right)\leq B\left(\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\right)^{b}\ln^{1-b\varepsilon}r\cdot\ln\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}r<B\left(\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\right)^{b}\ln^{1-b\varepsilon}r\cdot\ln\ln^{1/b}r.

(39)

There exists
x_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the function
\ln^{b\varepsilon}x/\ln\ln^{1/b}x
is positive and strictly increases when
x\geq x_{\varepsilon}
.
Then for any
C_{\varepsilon}
such that

\left(\frac{8C_{\varepsilon}}{c}\cdot\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}x\right)^{B((8C_{\varepsilon}\ln^{1/b-\varepsilon}x)/c)^{b}}<x\text{ for any }x<x_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }C_{\varepsilon}^{b}<\frac{1}{B}\cdot\left(\frac{c}{8}\right)^{b}\frac{\ln^{b\varepsilon}x_{\varepsilon}}{\ln\ln^{1/b}x_{\varepsilon}},

(40)

we either have
|V_{big}|<r
if
r<x_{\varepsilon}
, or we have

\ln|V_{big}|<BC_{\varepsilon}^{b}\left(\frac{8}{c}\right)^{b}\ln^{1-b\varepsilon}r\cdot\ln\ln^{1/b}r<\ln^{1-b\varepsilon}r\cdot\ln^{b\varepsilon}r=\ln r

(41)

if
r\geq x_{\varepsilon}
, which also means that
|V_{big}|<r
. So vertices of
V_{big}
can be colored in
\lfloor r/2\rfloor
colors with no more than two vertices of same color. It means that there is a proper coloring of
G
in
r
colors. Obtained contradiction finishes the proof of (
34
).

It remains to construct some function
g
such that
g(r)\to 0
as
r\to\infty
and

m_{H}(r)\geq r^{3}\ln^{1/b-g(r)}r.

(42)

For each
n
there is some
r_{n}
such that for
r\geq r_{n}

C_{1/n}\ln^{1/n}r>1,

(43)

which means that

m_{H}(r)\geq r^{3}\ln^{1/b-2/n}r.

(44)

Finally, for
r\geq r_{1}
let

g(r)=\frac{2}{\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq r\\
r_{n}\leq r\end{subarray}}n}.

(45)

Clearly,
g(r)
is non-increasing and can be arbitrary small, so
g(r)\xrightarrow[r\to\infty]{}0
.

2.4
Proof of Proposition
2

Suppose the contrary, i. e. there is an
H
-free 3-graph
G
with the chromatic number more than
r
and the number of edges at most
Cr^{4}
. Let such
r
be minimal possible.

Let us try to color
G[V_{big}]
in
r^{\prime}=\lfloor r/2\rfloor
colors greedily with respect to an arbitrary linear order
\pi
on the vertex set.
If it is impossible then there is a vertex
v
such that it is the last
vertex in edges
f_{1},\dots,f_{r^{\prime}}
such that
set
f_{i}\setminus\{v\}
has color
i
. Then an arbitrary choice of one vertex from each set
f_{i}\setminus\{v\}
leads to an independent set
I
of size
r^{\prime}
, since
G
is
H
-free.

By removing
I
from
G
we get some 3-graph that is not
(r-1)
-colorable, so it must have more than
C(r-1)^{4}
edges.
So we removed less than

Cr^{4}-C(r-1)^{4}<C\cdot 4r^{3}=\frac{er^{3}}{72}

(46)

edges. But the sum of degrees of vertices in
I
(in the initial graph
G
) is at least
r^{\prime}\cdot cr^{2}
, so it intersects at least

\frac{cr^{2}r^{\prime}}{2}\geq\frac{cr^{3}}{6}=\frac{er^{3}}{72}

(47)

edges. That is a contradiction.

2.5
Proof of Theorem
7

Let
C_{2}>0
.
Consider a 3-graph
G
with

|E(G)|\leq\frac{C_{2}r^{\frac{2b}{b-1}}}{(\ln\ln r)^{1+\frac{2b}{b-1}}}.

(48)

We will show that it is
r
-colorable if
r
is sufficiently big.
Let
V_{0}=V(G)
and

V_{i}=\left\{v\in V_{i-1}:\deg_{G[V_{i-1}]}v\geq\frac{1}{C_{3}}\left(\frac{r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{2}\right\},\quad i\geq 1,

(49)

where
C_{3}>0
is chosen to satisfy the inequality

\frac{1}{C_{3}}\left(\frac{r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3e}\left[\frac{r}{\log_{b}\ln r}\right]^{2}

(50)

for, let’s say,
r>100
,
so, by Lemma
2
, every 3-graph
G[V_{i}\setminus V_{i+1}]
for
i\geq 0
has a proper coloring in
\bigl[\frac{r}{\log_{b}\ln r}\bigr]
colors. Clearly,
C_{3}
can be chosen independently of
r
.

Now we are going to estimate sizes of
E(G[V_{i}])
and
V_{i+1}
from above for each
i\geq 0
.
As
V_{i+1}\subset V_{i}
, we have

\left|E(G[V_{i}])\right|\geq\frac{1}{3}\sum_{v\in V_{i+1}}\deg_{G[V_{i}]}v\geq\frac{1}{3}\cdot\left|V_{i+1}\right|\cdot\frac{1}{C_{3}}\left(\frac{r}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{2},

(51)

so

\left|V_{i+1}\right|\leq 3C_{3}\left(\frac{\ln\ln r}{r}\right)^{2}\left|E(G[V_{i}])\right|.

(52)

Also, we have bound (
48
) for
\left|E(G[V_{0}])\right|=|E(G)|
, and, by (
14
),

\left|E(G[V_{i}])\right|\leq C_{1}(\left|V_{i}\right|)^{b}\ \text{for $i\geq 1$}.

(53)

Combining (
53
) and (
52
) and using induction on
i
, we obtain for
i\geq 0

\left|E(G[V_{i}])\right|\leq C_{1}^{\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}}C_{2}^{b^{i}}(3C_{3})^{\frac{b^{i+1}-b}{b-1}}\cdot\frac{r^{\frac{2b}{b-1}}}{(\ln\ln r)^{b^{i}+\frac{2b}{b-1}}},

(54)

\left|V_{i+1}\right|\leq C_{1}^{\frac{b^{i}-1}{b-1}}C_{2}^{b^{i}}(3C_{3})^{\frac{b^{i+1}-1}{b-1}}\cdot\frac{r^{\frac{2}{b-1}}}{(\ln\ln r)^{b^{i}+\frac{2}{b-1}}}.

(55)

The last inequality implies that
V_{N}
is empty for
N
and
r
large enough.
Namely, let
C=C_{1}^{\frac{1}{b-1}}C_{2}(3C_{3})^{\frac{b}{b-1}}
. Then for
r>r_{0}(C_{1},C_{3})
from last inequality we get

\left|V_{i+1}\right|\leq\frac{r^{\frac{2}{b-1}}}{C_{1}^{\frac{1}{b-1}}(3C_{3})^{\frac{1}{b-1}}\cdot(\ln\ln r)^{\frac{2b}{b-1}}}\bigg/\left(\frac{\ln\ln r}{C}\right)^{b^{i}}\leq r^{\frac{2}{b-1}}\bigg/\left(\frac{\ln\ln r}{C}\right)^{b^{i}}.

(56)

So for
N>\log_{b}\ln r

|V_{i+1}|\leq r^{\frac{2}{b-1}}\bigg/\left(\frac{\ln\ln r}{C}\right)^{\ln r}=\left(\frac{Ce^{\frac{2}{b-1}}}{\ln\ln r}\right)^{\ln r},

(57)

which is less than 1 when
\ln\ln r>Ce^{2/(b-1)}
, i.e.

r>e^{e^{Ce^{2/(b-1)}}}.

(58)

We have divided
V
into at most
\log_{b}\ln r
sets of form
V_{i}\setminus V_{i+1}
, and each 3-graph
G[V_{i}\setminus V_{i+1}]
has a proper coloring with at most
\frac{r}{\log_{b}\ln r}
colors, so
G
is
r
-colorable. Therefore, we can put
R=\max\left(100,r_{0},e^{e^{Ce^{2/(b-1)}}}\right)
.

2.6
Proof of Proposition
3

Suppose that a 3-graph
G
has more than
(t-3)\binom{n}{2}
edges.
We will show that some subgraph of
G
is isomorphic to
H
.

Let us repeat the following procedure: if there is an edge
g=\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
such that the pair
(v_{1},v_{2})
is contained in at most
t-3
edges (including
g
), remove
g
.
There will be removed at most
(t-3)\binom{n}{2}
edges, and after that each pair of vertices will be joined by either 0 or at least
t-2
edges. Denote resulting 3-graph by
G^{\prime}
.

Let
H_{i}
be a subgraph of
H
induced by edges
h_{1},\dots,h_{i}
.
We have removed at most
(t-3)\binom{n}{2}
edges from
G
, so
\left|E(G^{\prime})\right|\geq 1
. A subgraph of
G^{\prime}
induced by one edge is isomorphic to
H_{1}
. Now we will show that if there is a subgraph
H^{\prime}_{i-1}\subset G^{\prime}
isomorphic to
H_{i-1}
, then there is a subgraph isomorphic to
H_{i}
. All we have to do is find an edge which can be put in correspondence to
h_{i}
.

H_{i-1}
has an edge
h_{j(i)}\supset\{a_{i},b_{i}\}
. Consider
v,u\in V(H^{\prime}_{i-1})
that correspond to
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
. There is an edge in
H^{\prime}_{i-1}
that corresponds to
h_{j(i)}
, so
v
and
u
are joined by at least
t-2
edges in
G^{\prime}
. We have
\left|V(H^{\prime}_{i-1})\right|-2=\left|V(H_{i-1})\right|-2=i-1<t-2
, so there exists
e\in E(G^{\prime})
such that
e=\{v,u,w\}
and
w\notin V(H^{\prime}_{i-1})
.
This edge
e
can be put in correspondence to
h_{i}
.

So there is a subgraph of
G^{\prime}
that is isomorphic to
H_{t-2}=H
and, since
G^{\prime}\subset G
, it is a subgraph of
G
.

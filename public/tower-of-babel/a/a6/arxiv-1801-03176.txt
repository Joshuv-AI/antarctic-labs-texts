The Fourier Restriction and Kakeya Problems over Rings of Integers Modulo N N
James Wright

Abstract
The Fourier restriction phenomenon and the size of Kakeya sets are explored in the setting of the ring of integers modulo N N for general N N and a striking similarity with the corresponding euclidean problems is observed. One should contrast this with known results in the finite field setting.

Naturally, questions arise regarding how well the finite field variant models the euclidean setting for these problems. One obvious difference is that there are few scales to work with in the finite field setting. Tools and considerations arising from restriction theory

\mathbb{T}_{d}:=\Theta_{d}\times\Lambda(N;d)^{n-1}.

The notation is chosen here to mirror that recently used in euclidean restriction theory (see, for example,
[
22
,
21
]
). For
(\theta,\vec{v})\in\mathbb{T}_{d}
the
wave packet

\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
is defined to be the function given by

\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}(\vec{\omega}):=d^{n-1}e^{-2\pi i\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\omega}/N}\chi_{\theta}(\vec{\omega}).

Generalising the Fourier inversion formula for the discrete Fourier transform, any
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued function on
[Z/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n-1}
can be written as a superposition of wave packets in a natural manner.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any divisor
d\mid N
the formula

H(\vec{\omega})=\sum_{(\theta,\vec{v})\in\mathbb{T}_{d}}(\chi_{\theta}H)\,\widecheck{}\,(\vec{v}\,)\cdot\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}(\vec{\omega})

holds for any function
H\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n-1}\to{\mathbb{C}}
.

If
d=1
, then the collection
\Theta_{d}
comprises of a single set
\theta=[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-1}_{*}
and the above identity reduces to

H(\vec{\omega})=\sum_{\vec{v}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-1}}\check{H}(\vec{v}\,)e^{-2\pi i\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\omega}/N},

which is precisely the Fourier inversion formula over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof (of Lemma
3.1
).

The functions
\chi_{\vec{v}+\mathcal{B}_{d}}
for
\vec{v}\in\Lambda(N;d)^{n-1}
form a partition of unity of
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-1}
and thus, by the Fourier inversion formula,

H(\vec{\omega})=\sum_{\vec{v}\in\Lambda(N;d)^{n-1}}\hat{\chi}_{\vec{v}+\mathcal{B}_{d}}\ast H(\vec{\omega}).

A simple computation shows that

\hat{\chi}_{\mathcal{B}_{d}}=d^{n-1}\chi_{\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}}

and therefore

\hat{\chi}_{\vec{v}+\mathcal{B}_{d}}\ast H(\vec{\omega})=d^{n-1}(e^{2\pi i\vec{v}\cdot(\,\cdot\,)/N}\chi_{\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}})\ast H(\vec{\omega})=d^{n-1}(\chi_{\vec{\omega}+\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}}H)\;\widecheck{}\;(\vec{v}\,)\cdot e^{-2\pi i\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\omega}/N}.

If
\vec{\omega}\in\theta
, then
\vec{\omega}+\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}=\theta
, and so the desired identity immediately follows.
∎

The extension operator
\mathcal{E}
has a particularly simple action on wave packets, mapping each
\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}
to a modulated characteristic functions of a ‘tube’. In particular, given a divisor
d\mid N
and
(\theta,\vec{v})\in\mathbb{T}_{d}
, define the
d

to be the set

T_{\theta,\vec{v}}:=\big\{\vec{x}=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:\|x^{\prime}+x_{n}\partial_{\omega}h(\vec{\omega}_{\theta})-\vec{v}\,\|\preceq d\big\},

where
\vec{\omega}_{\theta}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n-1}
denotes the unique coset representative of
\theta
lying in
\Lambda(N;d^{\prime})^{n-1}
. With this definition, one has the following identity.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
N,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
are such that
d\mid N
and
N\mid d^{2}
. Then for all
(\theta,\vec{v})\in\mathbb{T}_{d}
one has

\mathcal{E}\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}(\vec{x}\,)=e^{2\pi i(\phi(\vec{x}\,;\vec{\omega}_{\theta})-\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\omega}_{\theta})/N}\chi_{T_{\theta,\vec{v}}}(\vec{x}\,).

Proof.

It follows from the definitions that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\psi_{\theta,\vec{v}}(\vec{x}\,)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(d^{\prime})^{n-1}}\sum_{\vec{\omega}\in\theta}e^{2\pi i(\phi(\vec{x}\,;\vec{\omega})-\vec{v}\cdot\vec{\omega})/N}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(d^{\prime})^{n-1}}\sum_{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1}=0}^{d^{\prime}-1}e^{2\pi i(\phi(\vec{x}\,;\vec{\omega}_{\theta}+d\vec{\omega})-\vec{v}\cdot(\vec{\omega}_{\theta}+d\vec{\omega}))/N},

where
\vec{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1})
. Since
N\mid d^{2}
, one may verify that

\phi(\vec{x}\,;\vec{\omega}_{\theta}+d\vec{\omega})\equiv\phi(\vec{x}\,;\vec{\omega}_{\theta})+d\big(x^{\prime}+x_{n}\partial_{\omega}h(\vec{\omega}_{\theta})\big)\cdot\vec{\omega}\mod N.

On the other hand, the basic properties of character sums imply the identity

\chi_{T_{\theta,\vec{v}}}(\vec{x}\,)=\frac{1}{(d^{\prime})^{n-1}}\sum_{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n-1}=0}^{d^{\prime}-1}e^{2\pi i(x^{\prime}+x_{n}\partial_{\omega}h(\vec{\omega}_{\theta})-\vec{v})\cdot\vec{\omega}/d^{\prime}}.

Combining these observations, the desired result immediately follows.
∎

4
Kakeya sets in
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}

4.1
Discrete formulations of the Kakeya conjectures

The previous section highlighted a connection between estimates for the parabolic extension operator over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
and a discrete variant of the Kakeya maximal operator. Here the theory of Kakeya sets over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
is explored in a more systematic manner, beginning with a cleaner formulation of the maximal inequality (
13
).

The Kakeya problem over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
concerns configurations of lines in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
that point in ‘different directions’. An elegant way to formulate a notion of direction for lines lying in these modules is to use the ring-theoretic construction of the projective space.
9
9

9

This perspective was recently used in connection with the Kakeya problem by Caruso
[
5
]
.

For future reference it is useful to formulate the definitions at the general level of unital rings. Given a ring
R
with identity define the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(R)
to be the set of all elements of
R^{n}
that have at least one invertible component. In particular, note that
\mathbb{S}^{0}(R)=R^{\times}
is the group of units of
R
, which acts on the set
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(R)
by left multiplication. The
(n-1)
-dimensional projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(R)
is defined to be the set of orbits of this action; that is,

\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(R):=\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(R)/\mathbb{S}^{0}(R).

Finally, given
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(R)
a set
\ell_{\omega}\subseteq R^{n}
is said to be a
line in the direction of

\omega
if there exists some
\vec{v}\in R^{n}
such that

\ell_{\omega}=\big\{t\vec{\omega}+\vec{v}:t\in R\big\}

for some (and therefore any) choice of representative
\vec{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(R)
for
\omega
. In the case
R={\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
it will often be notationally convenient to write
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
and
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
rather than
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Conjecture 4.1

(Kakeya maximal conjecture over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the following holds. If
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\ell_{\omega}
is a choice of line in the direction of
\omega
for each
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
, then

\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{n/(n-1)}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|\big)^{(n-1)/n}.

(14)

If all the lines
\ell_{\omega}
happened to be disjoint, then the above inequality would hold with equality and the constant
C_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}
replaced with 1. Thus, the estimate can be interpreted as stating that collections of direction-separated lines in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
are ‘almost disjoint’.

It is not difficult to adapt the analysis of the previous section to show that (at least for
N
odd) the restriction conjecture for the paraboloid over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
implies the Kakeya maximal conjecture over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. This closely mirrors the euclidean case; as mentioned previously, the relationship between Kakeya and restriction over finite fields is far more tentative
[
44
,
40
]
.

Given a commutative ring with identity
R
, a set
K\subseteq R^{n}
is said to be
Kakeya
if for every
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(R)
there exists a line
\ell_{\omega}
in the direction of
\omega
contained in
K
. The maximal inequality (
14
) implies a lower bound on the cardinality of Kakeya sets in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
. Indeed, suppose
K\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
is Kakeya so that
\bigcup_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\ell_{\omega}\subseteq K
where each
\ell_{\omega}
is a line in the direction of
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
. Observe that (
14
) together with Hölder’s inequality imply that

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|

\displaystyle=\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{1}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}|K|^{1/n}\big(\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|\big)^{(n-1)/n}.

(15)

The sum
\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|
appearing on both sides of this inequality can be explicitly computed. Indeed,

|\ell_{\omega}|=N\quad\textrm{for all $\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)$}

(16)

whilst the cardinality of the projective space is given by

|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)|=N^{n-1}\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p^{-j}\geq N^{n-1},

(17)

where the product is taken over the set of all distinct prime factors of
N
. The latter identity is a direct consequence of the formula

|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|=N\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}(1-1/p);

(18)

the details of the (simple) proofs of the identities (
17
) and (
18
) are provided at the end of the section.

Rearranging (
15
) and applying the identities (
16
) and (
17
), one concludes that the Kakeya maximal conjecture implies the following variant of the Kakeya set conjecture.

Conjecture 4.2

(Kakeya set conjecture over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that the density bound

\frac{|K|}{N^{n}}\geq c_{\varepsilon,n}N^{-\varepsilon}

holds for any Kakeya set
K\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
.

This can be understood as a discrete analogue of the upper-Minkowski dimension conjecture for Kakeya sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. It is remarked that similar discrete variants of the Kakeya conjecture have previously appeared in the literature: see, for instance,
[
5
,
16
,
18
]
.

4.2
Sharpness of the Kakeya conjecture

It is natural to ask whether the
\varepsilon
-loss in
N
is necessary in Conjecture
4.2
: that is, whether there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that
N^{-n}|K|\geq c_{n}
holds for all Kakeya sets
K\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
(independently of
N
). It transpires that such an estimate is false, even if one restricts
N
to vary over powers of a fixed prime.

Proposition 4.3
.

For all primes
p
there exists a strictly increasing integer sequence
(\alpha(s))_{s\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and family of Kakeya sets
K_{s}\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha(s)}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
such that

\lim_{s\to\infty}\frac{|K_{s}|}{p^{\alpha(s)n}}=0.

This observation should be contrasted with Dvir’s theorem in the finite field setting
[
17
]
. The latter states that there exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
such that
q^{-n}|K|\geq c_{n}
holds whenever
K\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is a finite field Kakeya set.
10
10

10

The finite field analogue of the stronger maximal function estimate was established by Ellenberg, Oberlin and Tao in
[
18
]
. It is also useful to contrast the form of the conjectured maximal function estimate (
14
) (and, in particular, the (necessary)
\varepsilon
-loss in
N
in the constant) with the finite field result from
[
18
]
.

Proposition
4.3
follows by adapting a (euclidean-based) construction due to Sawyer
[
47
]
(see also
[
54
,
56
]
and
[
19
]
).

It suffices to consider the case

: the general case then follows by taking the Cartesian product of the set

given by the 2-dimensional example with
[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha(s)}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-2}
for each
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Furthermore, it suffices to construct a sequence of sets containing lines in only those directions which can be represented by an element of the form
(1,\omega)
for some
\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
. Indeed, one may then form a sequence of true Kakeya sets by taking finite unions of rotated copies of these objects.

Fixing
p
and

, let
\alpha:=sp^{s}

\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}

\omega_{j}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}

denote the coefficients in the

\omega
in
\{0,1\dots,p^{\alpha}-1\}
.
11
11

11

That is, the
\omega_{j}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
are uniquely defined by the formula
\displaystyle\omega=\big[\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-1}\omega_{j}p^{j}\big]
.
Using this notation, for each
\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
define a map
\phi_{\omega}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
by

\phi_{\omega}(t):=t\omega+\big[\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-1}\big\lfloor\frac{j}{s}\big\rfloor\cdot\omega_{j}p^{j}\big],

where
\lfloor\,\cdot\,\rfloor\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the floor function. Thus,

\ell_{[(1,\omega)]}:=\big\{(t,\phi_{\omega}(t)):t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}

is a line in the direction of

. Since, by Fubini,

\big|\bigcup_{\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}}\ell_{[(1,\omega)]}\big|=\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}}|\{\phi_{\omega}(t):\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\}|,

(19)

it suffices to show that

(20)

Indeed, once this is established one may define
K_{s}
to be the union of lines appearing on the left-hand side of (
19
), noting that the above inequality implies that
p^{-2\alpha}|K_{s}|\leq p^{-s}
.

Fix
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
and identify this element with a coset representative
t\in\{0,1,\dots,p^{\alpha}-1\}
. Let
t^{\prime}\in\{0,\dots,p^{s}-1\}
be the unique element satisfying
t^{\prime}\equiv-t\bmod p^{s}
and define

t\equiv-\big\lfloor\frac{j}{s}\big\rfloor\mod p^{s}\quad\textrm{for all $k\leq j\leq k+s-1$.}

For any
\omega^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
there exists a unique
\omega\in\Lambda(p^{\alpha},p^{\alpha-k})
such that

. Here
\Lambda(p^{\alpha},p^{\alpha-k})
is the maximal set of
p^{\alpha-k}
-separated points in
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
defined in (
9
). In particular,
\omega_{j}=\omega_{j}^{\prime}
for
0\leq j\leq k-1
and so

|\phi_{\omega}(t)-\phi_{\omega^{\prime}}(t)|=\bigg|\sum_{j=k}^{\alpha-1}\big(t+\big\lfloor\frac{j}{s}\big\rfloor\big)\big(\omega_{j}-\omega_{j}^{\prime}\big)p^{j}\bigg|.

\big|t+\lfloor\tfrac{j}{s}\rfloor\big|\preceq p^{\alpha-s}

from which it follows that
|\phi_{\omega}(t)-\phi_{\omega^{\prime}}(t)|\preceq p^{\alpha-k-s}

\{\phi_{\omega}(t):\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}\}\subseteq\bigcup_{\omega\in\Lambda(p^{\alpha},p^{\alpha-k})}\mathcal{B}_{p^{\alpha-k-s}}(\phi_{\omega}(t)),

which immediately yields (

).

\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
, then

\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{2}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{n/2-1+\varepsilon}\big(\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|\big)^{1/2}.

(21)

At its heart, the proof of Proposition
4.4
(that is to say, Córdoba’s argument) exploits the following simple geometric fact (here and below
\mathbb{F}
denotes a field):

A pair of direction-separated lines in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
can intersect in at most one point.

(22)

The relevance of (
22
) is most clearly understood by considering Proposition
4.4
in the finite field setting, given by restricting
N=p
to vary over primes
p
. Indeed, in this case the argument is particularly elementary: by (
22
) one has

\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(p)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{2}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{2}=\sum_{\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(p)}|\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}|\leq|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(p)|\big(|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(p)|+p-1\big),

and bounding the right-hand side of this inequality using (
17
) yields the desired estimate.

The original euclidean problem, as investigated in
[
10
]
, studies configurations of
\delta
-tubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
rather than lines. In this context one does not work with (
22
)
per se
, but rather a quantitative version of this fact, which states that the measure of the intersection of two tubes is inversely proportional to the angle between their directions. In this respect, the
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
setting behaves much more like euclidean space than a vector space over a finite field. Indeed, owing to the presence of zero divisors, (
22
) can fail dramatically for lines over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
: a pair of direction-separated lines in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
can meet at many points. However, in analogy with tubes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the number of points of intersection is inversely proportional to the angle between the directions.

To make the above discussion precise requires a notion of angle between elements of
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
; such a notion is formulated presently. Let
\vec{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
,
\vec{\omega}^{\prime}=(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\omega_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
be class representatives of
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
, respectively, and define the angle
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
by

\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime}):=\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big|\det\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{i}&\omega_{j}\\
\omega_{i}^{\prime}&\omega_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\big|.

(23)

Here
|\,\cdot\,|
is the size function on
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
introduced at the beginning of
\lx@sectionsign
2
: that is,
|x|:=N/\gcd(x,N)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. Note that the right-hand side of (
23
) does not depend on the choice of representatives
\vec{\omega}
and
\vec{\omega}^{\prime}
and therefore
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
is well-defined. A few further comments regarding the definition of
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
are in order.

i)

The definition (
23
) is motivated by the formula
\sin\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=|\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}|
for the angle between unit vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Note, in particular, that
|\omega\wedge\omega^{\prime}|
can be written in terms of the determinants

\det\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{i}&\omega_{j}\\
\omega_{i}^{\prime}&\omega_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\qquad\textrm{for $1\leq i<j\leq n$}

via the Cauchy–Binet formula.

ii)

By adapting an argument of Caruso
[
5
]
, one may easily show that

\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\min_{(\vec{\omega},\vec{\omega}^{\prime})\in\omega\times\omega^{\prime}}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|\omega_{j}-\omega_{j}^{\prime}|,

where the minimum is over all pairs of class representatives
\vec{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
,
\vec{\omega}^{\prime}=(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\omega_{n}^{\prime})
for
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
, respectively.

In place of the basic geometric fact (
22
) valid over fields, over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
there is the following quantitative statement.

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
, then

|\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}|\leq\frac{N}{\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})}.

Proof.

If
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=1
, then the result trivially holds and so one may assume that
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\succ 1
. Let
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})
,
(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\omega_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
be class representatives for
\omega,\omega^{\prime}
, respectively. It is easy to see that the cardinality of
\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}
is given by the number of solutions
(t,t^{\prime})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{2}
to the system
\begin{pmatrix}t&t^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\cdot\Omega=\vec{v}
for some
\vec{v}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
, where

\Omega:=\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{1}&\dots&\omega_{n}\\
\omega_{1}^{\prime}&\dots&\omega_{n}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

By definition, there exists some
2\times 2
submatrix
A
of
\Omega
such that
|\det A|=\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
. Since
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\succ 1
, Lemma
A.1
of the appendix implies that for any
\vec{b}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{2}
the system

\begin{pmatrix}t&t^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\cdot A=\vec{b}

has at most
N/|\det A|
solutions, and the desired result follows.
∎

Given this inequality, it is a simple exercise to translate Córdoba’s approach
[
10
]
into the current setting (see also
[
5
,
16
]
) and thereby prove Proposition
4.4
.

Proof (of Proposition
4.4
).

Expanding the left-hand
\ell^{2}
-norm, one obtains

\displaystyle\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{2}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\sum_{d\mid N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)\\
\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\end{subarray}}|\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}|.

By Lemma
4.5
and (
16
), it follows that

\big\|\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}\chi_{\ell_{\omega}}\big\|_{\ell^{2}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{2}\leq\sum_{\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)}|\ell_{\omega}|\sum_{d\mid N}\frac{|\{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N):\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\}|}{d}.

Recalling the standard asymptotics for the divisor function (see, for example,
[
25
]
*Chapter XVIII), it suffices to show that for all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|\{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N):\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\}|\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{n-2+\varepsilon}d.

The simple proof of this inequality is postponed until the end of the section.
∎

The slicing argument

Although Còrdoba’s argument is effective for
n=2
, it produces very poor estimates in higher dimensions. Here a
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
-analogue of the elementary
(n+1)/2
-dimensional lower bound for Kakeya sets is established. This gives improved partial results towards Conjecture
4.2
in higher dimensions.

Proposition 4.6
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that the density bound

\frac{|K|}{N^{n}}\geq c_{\varepsilon,n}N^{-(n-1)/2-\varepsilon}

holds for any Kakeya set
K\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
.

In essence, the proof of Proposition
4.6
relies on the following variant of the key geometric fact (
22
) used above:

For
x,y\in\mathbb{F}^{n}
distinct there exists precisely one line in
\mathbb{F}^{n}
passing through both
x
and
y
.

Once again, owing to the presence of zero divisors, this property no longer holds over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. In its place there is the following quantitative version, where the separation between the points is quantified.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\ell_{\omega},\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}
be lines in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
in the directions
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
, respectively. If there exist points
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\ell_{\omega}\cap\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}
such that
\|\vec{x}-\vec{y}\|=N
, then
\ell_{\omega}=\ell_{\omega^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

The simple proof is left to the reader.
∎

Proposition
4.6
is proved by combining Lemma
4.7
with (an adaptation of) a simple and well-known slicing argument from euclidean analysis (see, for instance,
[
35
]
).

Proof.

Suppose
K\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
is a Kakeya set and let

K[t]:=K\cap\{\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:x_{1}=t\}\quad\textrm{for all $t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}$.}

Let
\bar{C}>0
be a uniform constant, to be determined later in the proof, and define

E:=\Big\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:|K[t]|\leq\frac{\bar{C}\log N}{N}|K|\Big\}.

It follows from Chebyshev’s inequality that

|E|\geq(1-1/\bar{C}\log N)N.

(24)

If
\bar{C}
is chosen to be sufficiently large, then, by pigeonholing,
E
will necessarily contain a pair of well-separated points. Indeed, without loss of generality (by translating the Kakeya set) one may suppose that
\vec{0}\in E
and, recalling (
18
), it follows that

|\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:|t|<N\}|=N-|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|=N\cdot\big(1-\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}(1-1/p)\big).

By Mertens’ theorem (see, for instance,
[
25
]
*Chapter XXII), the constant
\bar{C}>0
may be chosen so that

\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}(1-1/p)\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq p\leq N\\
p\,\mathrm{prime}\end{subarray}}(1-1/p)\geq\frac{2}{\bar{C}\log N}.

Combining these observations with (
24
), one concludes that

|\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:|t|<N\}|\leq N(1-2/\bar{C}\log N)<|E|,

and so there must exist an element
t\in E
with
|t|=N
. By applying a group automorphism to the set
K
one may further assume that
t=1
.

Let
\Omega(N)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
denote the set of all
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
for which the first component of some (and therefore every) class representative
\vec{\omega}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
lies in
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
. Thus,
\Omega(N)
is the collection of orbits of the free action of
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
on
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)\times[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-1}
and therefore has cardinality
N^{n-1}
. Furthermore, for any
\omega\in\Omega(N)
the line
\ell_{\omega}
intersects each of the slices
K[0]
and
K[1]
at a unique point, denoted by
\ell_{\omega}[0]
and
\ell_{\omega}[1]
, respectively. On the other hand, Lemma
4.7
implies that for any pair of points
(\vec{x}\,,\vec{y}\,)\in K[0]\times K[1]
there exists at most one line
\ell_{\omega}
such that
\ell_{\omega}[0]=\vec{x}
and
\ell_{\omega}[1]=\vec{y}
. Consequently,

N^{n-1}=|\Omega(N)|\leq|K[0]||K[1]|\leq\frac{\bar{C}^{2}\log^{2}N}{N^{2}}|K|^{2},

which implies the desired inequality.
∎

4.4
Remaining estimates and identities

The proofs of a small number of basic estimates and identities were not presented in the above text; these remaining issues are collected in the following lemma and addressed presently.

Lemma 4.8
.

For all
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
the following statements hold.

i)

The cardinalities of
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
and
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
are given by the formulae

|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|=N\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}(1-1/p)\quad\textrm{and}\quad|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)|=N^{n-1}\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}p^{-j}.

ii)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|\{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N):\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\}|\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{n-2-\varepsilon}d

holds for all
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
and all
d\mid N
.

Proof of Lemma
4.8

i
).

The cardinality of the group of units is well-known, but nevertheless a proof is included in order to express the argument in terms of the
|\,\cdot\,|
,
\preceq
notation introduced in this article. Observe that

\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:|t|\prec N\}=\bigcup_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}\mathcal{B}_{N/p}

and, by the inclusion-exclusion principle,

\displaystyle\big|\bigcup_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}\mathcal{B}_{N/p}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\omega(N)}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\dots<p_{k}\\
p_{j}\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}\end{subarray}}\big|\bigcap_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}_{N/p_{j}}\big|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\omega(N)}(-1)^{k+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}<\dots<p_{k}\\
p_{j}\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}\end{subarray}}\big|\mathcal{B}_{N/p_{1}\dots p_{k}}\big|

\displaystyle=N\cdot\big(1-\prod_{p\mid N\,\mathrm{prime}}(1-1/p)\big),

where
\omega(N)
is the number of distinct prime factors of
N
. The desired identity for
|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|
immediately follows.

Since
|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)|
is a multiplicative function of
N
, it suffices to establish the second formula for
N=p^{\alpha}
a power of a prime
p
; in this case, the desired result was observed by Caruso
[
5
]
. A slightly different argument to that of
[
5
]
is as follows. Since the action of
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
is free, one deduces that

|\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)|=\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)|}{|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|}=\frac{N^{n}-(N-|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|)^{n}}{|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|}

and, after a short computation, the result follows from the above formula for
|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.8

ii
).

The argument here is rather crude and more precise estimates could be obtained (see
[
5
]
). Nevertheless, the resulting bounds suffice for the purposes of this article.

Fix
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N)
and
d\mid N
and define

P^{n-1}(\omega;d):=\{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}(N):\measuredangle(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d\},

so that the desired estimate reads

|P^{n-1}(\omega;d)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{n-2+\varepsilon}d.

The group of units
\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
acts freely on

S^{n-1}(\omega;d):=\Big\{(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\omega_{n}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N):\max_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\big|\det\begin{pmatrix}\omega_{i}&\omega_{j}\\
\omega_{i}^{\prime}&\omega_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\big|=d\Big\},

and it follows that

|P^{n-1}(\omega;d)|=\frac{|S^{n-1}(\omega;d)|}{|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|}.

(25)

If
d=N
, then one may use the trivial estimate

|S^{n-1}(\omega;d)|\leq|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)|\leq nN^{n-1}|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|

which, combined with (
25
), yields the desired bound.

Now suppose
d\mid N
is a proper divisor and fix a representative
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{n})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}(N)
of
\omega
. Without loss of generality, one may assume that
\omega_{1}\in\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
and, by possibly choosing an alternative class representative, moreover, that
\omega_{1}=1
. If
\vec{\omega}^{\prime}=(\omega_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\omega_{n}^{\prime})\in S^{n-1}(\omega;d)
, then it follows that
\omega_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
. Indeed, otherwise
|\omega_{1}^{\prime}|\prec N
and there must exist some
2\leq j\leq n
such that
\omega_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
; in this case

\big|\det\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega_{j}\\
\omega_{1}^{\prime}&\omega_{j}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\big|=N,

contradicting the assumption that
\vec{\omega}^{\prime}\in S^{n-1}(\omega;d)
. Thus, one deduces that

S^{n-1}(\omega;d)\subseteq\{\vec{\omega}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)\times[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n-1}:\big|\det\begin{pmatrix}1&\omega_{2}\\
\omega_{1}^{\prime}&\omega_{2}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\big|\leq d\Big\}.

For any fixed
\omega_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)
and
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

|\big\{\omega_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:|\omega_{2}^{\prime}-\omega_{1}^{\prime}\omega_{2}|\leq d\}|=\big|\bigcup_{d^{\prime}\mid N:d^{\prime}\leq d}\mathcal{B}_{d^{\prime}}\big|\leq\sum_{d^{\prime}\mid N:d^{\prime}\leq d}d^{\prime}\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}d.

Consequently,

|S^{n-1}(\omega;d)|\leq C_{\varepsilon}N^{n-2+\varepsilon}d|\mathbb{S}^{0}(N)|

and combining this inequality with (
25
) concludes the proof.
∎

5
Fourier restriction over
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
and
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}

5.1
Analysis over the
p
-adic field

In this section the key features of the
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
formulation of the restriction problem (that is, Problem
2.2
) are described. In particular, a correspondence principle is demonstrated that allows one to lift the analysis from the finite rings
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
to the field of
p
-adic numbers
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. This correspondence helps to explain many of the apparent similarities between the
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
and euclidean theories, since the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{R}}
are in many respects related. Furthermore, there are a number of euclidean-based techniques which have no obvious counterpart or are considerably more difficult to implement in the discrete setting, but can be easily adapted to work over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
. Thus, lifting the problem to the
p
-adics often significantly simplifies the analysis (a striking example of this occurs when one studies the restriction theory for the moment curve; this is described in detail in
\lx@sectionsign
7
). The
p
-adic field also has a relatively simple algebraic structure since, in particular, there are no zero divisors.

Before proceeding some basic facts regarding analysis over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
are reviewed, and the relevant notational conventions are established. Fixing a prime
p
, recall that the
p
-adic absolute value
|\,\cdot\,|_{p}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}\to\{0,p^{-1},p^{-2},\dots\}
is defined by

|x|_{p}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{-k}&\textrm{if $x\neq 0$ and $p^{k}\,\|x$ for $k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$}\\
0&\textrm{otherwise}\end{array}\right.,

where the notation
p^{k}\,\|\theta
is used to denote that
p^{k}
divides
\theta
(that is,
p^{k}\mid\theta
) and no larger power of
p
divides
\theta
. The function
|\,\cdot\,|_{p}
uniquely extends to a non-archimedean absolute value on the rationals
{\mathbb{Q}}
.
12
12

12

That is,
|\,\cdot\,|_{p}\colon{\mathbb{Q}}\to[0,\infty)
satisfies the following properties:

i)

(Positive definite)
|x|_{p}\geq 0
for all
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and
|x|_{p}=0
if and only if
x=0
;

ii)

(Multiplicative)
|xy|_{p}=|x|_{p}|y|_{p}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}
;

iii)

(Strong triangle inequality)
|x+y|_{p}\leq\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}
for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The field of
p
-adic numbers
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is defined to be the metric completion of
{\mathbb{Q}}
under the metric induced by
|\,\cdot\,|_{p}
. One may verify that
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
indeed has a natural field structure and contains
{\mathbb{Q}}
as a subfield.

Any element
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
admits a unique
p
-adic series expansion

x=\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}x_{j}p^{j}

(26)

where
J\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
x_{j}\in\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
x_{J}\neq 0
(and
x_{j}:=0
for
j<J
). The sum is understood as the limit of a sequence of rationals, where the convergence is with respect to the
p
-adic absolute value. In this case,
|x|_{p}=p^{-J}
. The ring of
p
-adic numbers
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is defined to be the set comprised of 0 together with all the elements
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
for which
J\geq 0
in the expansion (
26
). Thus,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}=\{x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:|x|_{p}\leq 1\}
, and this clearly forms a subring of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
by the multiplicative property of the absolute value.

The field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is a locally compact abelian group under the addition operation and the Haar measure of a Borel subset
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is denoted by
|E|
; this measure is normalised so that
|{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}|=1
. The notation
\mathrm{d}x
is used to indicate that an integral is taken with respect to Haar measure (hence,
|E|=\int_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\chi_{E}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x
for all
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
Borel). For any
r>0
and
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
the ball
B_{r}(x)
is defined by

B_{r}(x):=\{y\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}:|x-y|_{p}\leq r\};

these balls are
not
defined using a strict inequality so that, for instance,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}=B_{1}(0)
. For each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the ball
B_{p^{\alpha}}(0)=p^{-\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is an additive subgroup of
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
(furthermore, if
\alpha\leq 0
, then
B_{p^{\alpha}}(0)
is an ideal of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
), and all other balls of radius
p^{\alpha}
arise as cosets of
B_{p^{\alpha}}(0)
. It immediately follows from the translation invariance property of the Haar measure (together with the choice of normalisation) that
|B_{p^{\alpha}}(x)|=p^{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
.

There is an alternative algebraic description of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
as the inverse limit of the inverse system of groups
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}}
: that is,

{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}=\varprojlim_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}.

The
p
-adic numbers
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
can then be described algebraically as the field of fractions of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. This perspective will not feature heavily here, but it is noted that that the inverse system induces a family of natural projection homomorphisms
\pi_{\alpha}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
. The
\pi_{\alpha}
are given by reduction modulo the ideal
p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and can be expressed in terms of the
p
-adic expansion; in particular,

\pi_{\alpha}(x)=\big[\sum_{j=0}^{\alpha-1}x_{j}p^{j}\big]\qquad\textrm{for $x=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}x_{j}p^{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}$ and $\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}$.}

The vector space
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
is endowed with the norm

|x|_{p}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|x_{j}|_{p}\qquad\textrm{for all $x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}$.}

All the above definitions and conventions then naturally extend to vector spaces
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
.

By the first isomorphism theorem, the
\pi_{\alpha}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}\to[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
induce a natural isomorphism between
[{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}]^{n}
and
[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
. In particular, the sets
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(\vec{x}\,)
for
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
are precisely the cosets of
p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and so

\pi^{-1}\{\vec{x}\}=B_{p^{-\alpha}}(y)\qquad\textrm{for all $\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}$ and $y\in\pi^{-1}\{\vec{x}\}$.}

(27)

This is the key observation which governs the correspondence principle.

5.2
Restriction and Kakeya over the
p
-adics

The
p
-adic field
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is self-dual in the Pontryagin sense. In particular, if one fixes an additive character
e\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbb{T}
such that
e
restricts to the constant function 1 on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and to a non-principal character on
p^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, then for any integrable
f\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
the Fourier transform
\hat{f}
can be defined by

\hat{f}(\xi):=\int_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}}f(x)e(-x\cdot\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\qquad\textrm{for all $\xi\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}$.}

It will be useful to work with an explicit choice of character
e
. Define the fractional part function
\{\,\cdot\,\}_{p}\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\to{\mathbb{Q}}
as follows: given
x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
with
p
-adic expansion
\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}x_{j}p^{j}
, let
\{x\}_{p}:=\sum_{j=J}^{-1}x_{j}p^{j}
. Observe that
\{x\}_{p}=0
if and only if
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. Defining
e\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\to\mathbb{T}
by

e(x):=e^{2\pi i\{x\}_{p}}\quad\textrm{for all $x\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}$,}

it is easy to check that this function has the desired properties.

Fix
1\leq d\leq n-1
and
P_{n-d+1},\dots,P_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{d}]
. Let
\Sigma\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
denote the image of the the mapping

\Gamma\colon\omega\mapsto(\omega,P_{n-d-1}(\omega),\dots,P_{n}(\omega))

(28)

as a function
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{d}\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
and
\mu
the measure on
\Sigma
given by the push-forward of the Haar measure on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{d}
under (
28
). One is interested in studying Fourier restriction estimates of the form

\|\hat{f}|_{\Sigma}\|_{L^{s}(\mu)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{r}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})}.

(29)

The conjectural range of estimates for restriction to, say, a compact piece of the paraboloid over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
is easily seen to imply a
p
-adic version of the Kakeya conjecture. To make this precise, first note that the ring-theoretic definitions of projective space, lines in a given direction and Kakeya sets, as described in
\lx@sectionsign
4
, can all be applied to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, and so it makes sense to discuss Kakeya sets
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
. By essentially a repeat of the discussion from
\lx@sectionsign
3
and
\lx@sectionsign
4
, the study of restriction estimates over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
leads one to consider the following geometric problem.
13
13

13

For brevity, only the
p
-adic Kakeya set conjecture is stated, but it certainly makes sense to also consider the corresponding maximal conjecture.

Conjecture 5.1

(Kakeya set conjecture over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
)
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that for any Kakeya set
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
the bound

|\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(K)|\geq c_{\varepsilon,n}p^{-\varepsilon\alpha}

holds for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Here for any set
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the
p^{-\alpha}
-neighbourhood of
E
is defined to be the set

\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(E):=\bigcup_{y\in E}B_{p^{-\alpha}}(y).

(30)

It transpires that Conjecture
5.1
is equivalent to the weakened version of Conjecture
4.2
where
N
varies only over powers of the fixed prime
p
. This equivalence is discussed below, and provides a simple instance of the correspondence principle.

5.3
Equivalence of the Kakeya problem over
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}

The proof of the equivalence between the
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
formulations of the Kakeya set conjecture relies on two ingredients, described presently.

1. Correspondence for sets

Given a set
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
one may easily deduce from (
27
) and (
30
) that

\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(E)=\bigcup_{\vec{x}\in\pi_{\alpha}(E)}\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\{\vec{x}\},

(31)

where the union is, of course, disjoint. The identity (
27
) also implies that each of the sets
\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\{\vec{x}\}
has Haar measure
p^{-\alpha n}
and, consequently,

|\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(E)|=\sum_{\vec{x}\in\pi_{\alpha}(E)}|\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\{\vec{x}\}|=\frac{|\pi_{\alpha}(E)|}{p^{\alpha n}}.

Note that the expression on the far left-hand side of this chain of equalities involves the Haar measure on
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
, whilst the expression on the right involves normalised counting measure.

2. Correspondence for directions

The projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
is naturally related to the discrete projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})
. Indeed, as discussed in
[
5
]
, the inverse system on the family of groups
({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}})_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}}
naturally induces an inverse system on the family of sets
(\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}))_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
can be realised as the inverse limit

\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})=\varprojlim_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}).

This gives rise to a family of natural projection mappings

\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}).

The
\tilde{\pi}_{\alpha}
may also be defined in terms of the
p
-adic expansion for (class representatives of) the
\omega\in\mathbb{P}^{n-1}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
; the details are left to the reader. In particular, using this observation one may show that
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
is Kakeya if and only if
\pi_{\alpha}(K)\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
is Kakeya for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Combining these correspondences, it is clear that the two formulations of the Kakeya problem are completely equivalent. Indeed, given any Kakeya set
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
, it follows that
K_{\alpha}:=\pi_{\alpha}(K)\subseteq[Z/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
is Kakeya for all
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assuming Conjecture
4.2
for
N=p^{\alpha}
, given
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
c_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that

|\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(K)|=\frac{|K_{\alpha}|}{p^{\alpha n}}\geq c_{\varepsilon,n}p^{-\varepsilon\alpha}\qquad\textrm{for all $\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}$},

as required. Conversely, if
K_{\alpha}\subseteq[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
is Kakeya, then
K:=\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}(K_{\alpha})
is easily seen to be Kakeya with
\mathcal{N}_{p^{\alpha}}(K)=K
. Thus, assuming Conjecture
5.1
, given
\varepsilon>0
there exists some
c_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that

\frac{|K_{\alpha}|}{p^{\alpha n}}=|\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(K)|\geq c_{\varepsilon,n}p^{-\varepsilon\alpha},

as required.

The construction described in Proposition
4.3
can be combined with the above observations to yield the following result.

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists a Kakeya set
K\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
of Haar measure zero.

Proof.

The sets constructed in Proposition
4.3
can be lifted to produce Kakeya sets in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
of arbitrarily small measure. A measure zero set is then obtained by a limiting procedure. The details are omitted; see, for instance,
[
56
]
for a similar argument in the euclidean case.
∎

It is remarked that such sets have been observed to exist in
[
5
,
19
]
(see also
[
16
]
). The construction described here is closely related to that given in
[
19
]
, and stems from euclidean constructions described in
[
47
,
54
]
(see also
[
56
]
).

5.4
A correspondence principle for functions

Developing a correspondence principle for the restriction problem is a little more involved than the Kakeya case. For restriction, one is required to lift
functions
on
[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
, rather than just sets, and the lifting procedure must behave well with respect to taking Fourier transforms. Moreover, one must also work over the entire vector space
{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}
, and not just the compact piece
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
, and this necessitates the use of a 2-parameter family of correspondences (the analysis of the previous subsection used just a 1-parameter family of maps, namely
\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(E)\mapsto\pi_{\alpha}(E)
).

It is first remarked that, for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, the observations of the previous subsections imply a correspondence between functions
f\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that are supported in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}=B_{1}(0)
and are constant on cosets of
p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}=B_{p^{-\alpha}}(0)
and functions
F\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Indeed, given such a function
f
, one may simply define
F_{\alpha}[f]\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

F_{\alpha}[f](\vec{x}):=f(y)\quad\textrm{if $y\in\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\{\vec{x}\,\}$}

for all
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
. This is well-defined by the hypotheses on
f
and
f\mapsto F_{\alpha}[f]
is an isomorphism between the relevant function spaces.

For the purposes of restriction theory, it is useful to consider a 2-parameter family
F_{k.l}
comprised of rescaled versions of the isomorphisms
F_{\alpha}
. For
k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
let
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n};k,l)
denote the vector subspace of
L^{1}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
consisting of all
f\colon{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
that are supported on
B_{p^{l}}(0)
and constant on cosets of
B_{p^{-k}}(0)
. The union of the
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n};k,l)
over all
k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is denoted
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
. Note that
f\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
if and only if it is a finite linear combination of characteristic functions of balls.

Given
f\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n};k,l)
, define
F_{k,l}[f]\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

F_{k,l}[f](\vec{x}):=p^{-kn}f(p^{-l}y)\quad\textrm{for $y\in\pi_{k+l}^{-1}\{\vec{x}\,\}$}

(32)

for all
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
. Once again, this is well-defined and
F_{k,l}[f]\colon\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n};k,l)\to\ell^{1}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})
is an isomorphism.

The space
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
is the
p
-adic analogue of the Schwartz class
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
from Euclidean analysis. It is remarked that both spaces can be viewed as particular instances of a more general construction, namely the
Schwartz–Bruhat class
on an arbitrary LCA group
[
4
,
45
]
(see also
[
51
]
).
14
14

14

Strictly speaking, the Schwartz–Bruhat spaces are, by definition,
topological
vector spaces and therefore their full definition requires a description of their topology. The topology is not discussed here as it plays no rôle in the forthcoming analysis.
It is a simple consequence of the Stone–Weierstrass theorem that
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
is dense in
L^{r}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n})
for
1\leq r<\infty
. Furthermore, for all
k,l\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
the Fourier transform restricts to a bijection from
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p}^{n};k,l)
to
\mathscr{S}(\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}^{n};l,k)
.

It is useful to set up a similar correspondence between functions on the dual groups. In particular, given
g\in\mathscr{S}(\hat{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}^{n};l,k)
define
\widehat{F}_{l,k}[g]\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

\widehat{F}_{l,k}[g](\vec{\xi}\,):=g(p^{-k}\eta\,)\qquad\textrm{for $\eta\in\pi^{-1}_{k+l}\{\vec{\xi}\,\}$ }

(33)

for all
\vec{\xi}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}
.

These definitions extend the correspondence for sets detailed above. Indeed, one may easily verify that (
31
) implies that

\widehat{F}_{\alpha,0}[\chi_{\mathcal{N}_{p^{-\alpha}}(E)}]=\chi_{\pi_{\alpha}(E)}

(34)

for all
E\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{n}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

The normalisation factors
p^{-kn}
and 1 are chosen so that (
32
) preserves the
L^{1}
-norm and (
33
) preserves the
L^{\infty}
-norm; this is natural in view of the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma. More generally, the following norm identities hold.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
1\leq r\leq\infty
and any

and

Since b) is essentially just a renormalised version of a), it suffices only to prove a). Fix

\|F_{k,l}[f]\|_{\ell^{r}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}=p^{-kn}\big(\sum_{\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{k+l}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}}|f(p^{-l}y_{\vec{x}})|^{r}\big)^{1/r}

where each

One may easily observe that

The mappings

Observe that

\gcd(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k},p_{t})=1\end{subarray}}\prod_{l=1}^{n-k}e^{2\pi ix_{l}\xi_{l}/p_{t}^{L_{t}}}

\|S_{nL}\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nL}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{r^{\prime}}
may be bounded below by

\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p^{nm}\,\|x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nL}-1}\>\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n-1}=0\\[5.0pt]
p^{nm}\mid x_{n-1}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nL}-1}\>\cdots\>\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}=0\\[5.0pt]
p^{nm}\mid x_{1}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nL}-1}\Bigl|p^{-nL}\sum_{t=0}^{p^{nL}-1}e^{2\pi i(x_{1}t+\cdots+x_{n}t^{n})/p^{nL}}\Bigr|^{r^{\prime}};

recall, the notation
p^{k}\,\|\theta
is used to denote that
p^{k}
divides
\theta
and no larger power of
p
divides
\theta
. Splitting the above exponential sum by writing
t=y+p^{n(L-m)}z
where
0\leq y\leq p^{n(L-m)}-1
and
0\leq z\leq p^{nm}-1
, it follows that

\|S_{nL}\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nL}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{r^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}A(L-m)

(60)

where

A(M):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\nmid\,x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\sum_{x_{n-1}=0}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\cdots\>\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{p^{nM}-1}\bigl|S_{nM}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\bigr|^{r^{\prime}}.

for
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Claim 7.4
.

The inequality
A(M)\geq p^{n(n+1)/2+1-r^{\prime}}A(M-1)
holds for all
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Once the claim is established it may be applied iteratively to bound each of the summands in (
60
) and thereby deduce that

\|S_{nL}\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nL}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{r^{\prime}}\geq\sum_{m=0}^{L-1}p^{(L-m)(n(n+1)/2+1-r^{\prime})}.

This yields the desired blowup for
r^{\prime}<n(n+1)/2+1
(and for
r^{\prime}=n(n+1)/2+1
, in the context of Corollary
7.3
).

In order to verify the claim, first note that

A(M)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\nmid\,x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n-1}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\mid\,x_{n-1}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\!\cdots\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1}=0\\[5.0pt]
p^{n-1}\,\mid\,x_{1}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\sum_{c=0}^{p-1}\Bigl|p^{-nM}\sum_{t=0}^{p^{nM}-1}e^{2\pi i(x_{1}(t+c)+\cdots+x_{n}(t+c)^{n})/p^{nM}}\Bigr|^{r^{\prime}},

(61)

where the right-hand side of the above display can be expressed as

p\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\nmid\,x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\sum_{x_{n-1}=0}^{p^{nM-1}-1}\!\!\cdots\!\!\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{p^{nM-n+1}-1}\Bigl|p^{-nM}\sum_{t=0}^{p^{nM}-1}e^{2\pi i(x_{1}t/p^{n(M-1)+1}+\cdots+x_{n}t^{n}/p^{nM})}\Bigr|^{r^{\prime}}.

(62)

To see this, consider the map
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\times[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nM}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}\to[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nM}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
given by

\Phi(c;x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):=\begin{pmatrix}\binom{1}{1}&\binom{2}{1}c&\dots&\binom{n}{1}c^{n-1}\\[2.0pt]
0&\binom{2}{2}&\dots&\binom{n}{2}c^{n-2}\\[2.0pt]
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\[2.0pt]
0&0&\dots&\binom{n}{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n}\end{pmatrix},

noting that

x_{1}(t+c)+\cdots+x_{n}(t+c)^{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Phi_{j}(c;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})t^{j}.

To establish the lower bound (
61
) for
A(M)
it suffices to show that
\Phi
restricts to an injection on the set

\Omega:=\big\{(c;x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p{\mathbb{Z}}\times[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{nM}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:p\mid x_{n-1}\textrm{ and }p\nmid x_{n}\big\}.

If
\Phi(c;\vec{a})=\Phi(d;\vec{b})
for
(c;\vec{a}),(d;\vec{b})\in\Omega
, then it immediately follows that
x_{n}=b_{n}
and
nx_{n}c+x_{n-1}=nb_{n}d+b_{n-1}
. Combining these identities, reducing modulo
p
and using the fact that
p\nmid nx_{n}
, one concludes that
c=d
. The injectivity of
\Phi
is now immediate, since the matrix in the definition of
\Phi
has determinant 1.

Now consider the inner exponential sum in (
62
); by decomposing the sum by writing
t=z+p^{nM-1}y
this can be expressed as

\sum_{z=0}^{p^{nM-1}-1}e^{2\pi i(x_{1}z/p^{n(M-1)+1}+\cdots+x_{n}z^{n}/p^{nM})}\sum_{y=0}^{p-1}e^{2\pi inx_{n}z^{n-1}y/p}.

Since
p>n
and
p\nmid x_{n}
, the sum in
y
vanishes unless
p\mid z
and hence the above expression is equal to

p\sum_{w=0}^{p^{nM-2}-1}e^{2\pi i(x_{1}w/p^{n(M-1)}+\cdots+x_{n}w^{n}/p^{n(M-1)})}=p^{n-1}S_{n(M-1)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

Thus, the right-and side of (
62
) is equal to

p^{-r^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\nmid\,x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{nM}-1}\>\sum_{x_{n-1}=0}^{p^{nM-1}-1}\>\cdots\;\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{p^{nM-n+1}-1}|S_{n(M-1)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|^{r^{\prime}};

this can, in turn, be written as

p^{-r^{\prime}+n+(n-1)+\cdots+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{n}=0\\[5.0pt]
p\,\nmid\,x_{n}\end{subarray}}^{p^{n(M-1)}-1}\>\sum_{x_{n-1}=0}^{p^{n(M-1)}-1}\>\cdots\;\sum_{x_{1}=0}^{p^{n(M-1)}-1}|S_{n(M-1)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|^{r^{\prime}},

which establishes the claim.
∎

7.3
Sufficient conditions

Combining the necessary conditions discussed in the previous section, it is natural to conjecture the following.

Conjecture 7.5
.

If
1\leq r,s\leq\infty
satisfy
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+1)/2+1
and
r^{\prime}\geq sn(n+1)/2
, then the discrete restriction estimate (
56
) holds whenever each prime factor
p
of
N
satisfies
p>n
.

In contrast with the
{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
case treated in Theorem
7.1
, there appear to be significant challenges in establishing Conjecture
7.5
. To exemplify this, it is instructive to attempt to follow the classical argument of Prestini
[
46
]
and Christ
[
7
]
in the mod
N
setting, with the aim of establishing restriction estimates for
\Sigma:=\{(t,t^{2},\dots,t^{n}):t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
in the restricted
\ell^{r}
range
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+2)/2
.

Proceeding by duality, one wishes to prove (
57
) holds for exponents
(r,s)
satisfying
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+2)/2
and
r^{\prime}\geq sn(n+1)/2
. The desired estimate can be written succinctly as

\|(H\mathrm{d}\mu)\;\widecheck{}\;\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}\ \leq\ C_{\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|H\|_{\ell^{s^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{avg}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}

(63)

where
\mu
is the measure whose Fourier transform is the exponential sum in (
58
). Letting
H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu
denote the
n
-fold convolution of
H\mathrm{d}\mu
, one observes by the Hausdorff–Young inequality that

\displaystyle\|(H\mathrm{d}\mu)\;\widecheck{}\;\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}^{n}

\displaystyle=\|\big[(H\mathrm{d}\mu)\;\widecheck{}\;\big]^{n}\|_{\ell^{r^{\prime}/n}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\|(H\mathrm{d}\mu)*\cdots*(H\mathrm{d}\mu)\|_{\ell^{\rho}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n})}

(64)

where
n\rho^{\prime}=r^{\prime}
(note that, since
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+2)/2
, the exponent
\rho
satisfies
1\leq\rho\leq 2
).
Now, for any test function
\phi\colon[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
one has

H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu(\phi)\>=\>\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\phi\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma(t_{i})\bigr)\prod_{i=1}^{n}H(t_{i}),

where
\vec{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and
\gamma(s)=(s,s^{2},\ldots,s^{n})
is the map parametrising the curve
\Sigma
. Set
\Phi(\vec{t}\,):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma(t_{i})
so that

H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu(\phi)\>=\>\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{y}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\phi(\vec{y}\,)\sum_{\vec{t}:\Phi(\vec{t}\,)=\vec{y}}\ \ \prod_{i=1}^{n}H(t_{i})\>

and hence

H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu(\vec{y}\,)\ =\ \sum_{\vec{t}:\Phi(\vec{t}\,)=\vec{y}}\ \ \prod_{i=1}^{n}H(t_{i}).

Taking the
\ell^{\rho}
-norm, one therefore deduces that

\|H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu\|_{\ell^{\rho}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n})}^{\rho}\ =\ \frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{y}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\bigl|\sum_{\vec{t}:\Phi(\vec{t}\,)=\vec{y}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}H(t_{i})\ \bigr|^{\rho}.

Let
\mathbf{N}(\vec{s};N)
denote the number of solutions
\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
to the system
\Phi(\vec{X})=\Phi(\vec{s}\,)
; that is,

\mathbf{N}(\vec{s};N):=\{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:\Phi(\vec{t}\,)=\Phi(\vec{s}\,)\}.

With this notation, one may write the above
\ell^{\rho}
-norm as

\|H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu\|_{\ell^{\rho}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n})}^{\rho}\ =\ \frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{s}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{N}(\vec{s};N)}\bigl|\sum_{\vec{t}:\Phi(\vec{t}\,)=\Phi(\vec{s})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}H(t_{i})\ \bigr|^{\rho}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality in the
{\vec{t}}
sum yields

\|H\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*H\mathrm{d}\mu\|_{\ell^{\rho}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n})}^{\rho}\ \leq\ \frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|H(t_{i})|^{r}\,\mathbf{N}(\vec{t};N)^{{\rho}-1}.

(65)

Up to this point the analysis has closely followed the euclidean argument of Prestini
[
46
]
and Christ
[
7
]
. In the euclidean case the change of variables
\vec{y}=\Phi(\vec{t}\,)
(performed twice) introduces a power of the Jacobian factor

J_{\Phi}(\vec{t}\,)\ =\ \prod\limits_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|t_{j}-t_{k}|,

leading to the estimate

\|h\mathrm{d}\mu*\cdots*h\mathrm{d}\mu\|_{L^{\rho}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{\rho}\ \leq C_{n}\int_{\vec{t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|h(t_{i})|^{\rho}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\frac{1}{|t_{j}-t_{k}|^{{\rho}-1}}\,d\vec{t}.

(66)

Comparing (
65
) and (
66
) suggests the following conjecture on the number of solutions
\mathbf{N}(\vec{t};N)
.

Conjecture 7.6
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C_{\varepsilon,n}>0
such that if
\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
, then

\mathbf{N}(\vec{t};N)\leq\min\big\{C_{\varepsilon,n}N^{\varepsilon+n(n-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|t_{j}-t_{k}|^{-1},N^{n}\big\}.

(67)

The right-hand expression in (
67
) is written in terms of the absolute value on
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
which, by definition, satisfies
N|y|^{-1}=\gcd(y,N)
for all
y\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus, to establish Conjecture
7.6
(since the estimate
\mathbf{N}(\vec{t};N)\leq N^{n}
holds trivially) it suffices to show that

\mathbf{N}(\vec{t};N)\leq C_{\varepsilon,n}N^{\varepsilon}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{k},N).

Assuming the conjecture holds, one is in position to appeal to a multilinear fractional integral inequality of Christ
[
7
]
; although presented in the euclidean setting in
[
7
]
, the statement and proof of the multilinear inequality translate directly into the mod
N
setting. The result may be stated in the following way: if
F_{i}
and
G_{j,k}
are functions on
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}
, then the multilinear form

\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}F_{i}(t_{i})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}G_{j,k}(t_{j}-t_{k})

(68)

is dominated by (a constant multiple of)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|F_{i}\|_{\ell^{\alpha}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\|G_{j,k}\|_{\ell^{\beta,\infty}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}

(69)

whenever
\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}(n-1)/2\leq 1
and
1\leq\alpha<n
, where

\|G\|_{\ell^{\beta,\infty}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}\ :=\ \sup_{\lambda>0}\lambda\,\Bigl(N^{-1}\bigl|\{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}:G(t)\geq\lambda\}\bigr|\Bigr)^{1/\beta}

denotes the weak-type Lorentz norm. The first step in Christ’s proof is to observe that a simple interpolation argument bounds (
68
) by an expression given by replacing the weak-type norms
{\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{\beta,\infty}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}}
by the strong-type
\ell^{\beta}
-norms
{\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{\beta}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}}
in (
69
) in the larger
\ell^{\alpha}
-range
1\leq\alpha\leq n
. In fact, here this weakened estimate is all one needs. Indeed, the multilinear inequality will be applied to the functions
G_{j,k}(t):=\gcd(t,N)^{\rho-1}
with
\beta=1/(\rho-1)
; note that

\displaystyle\|\gcd(\cdot,N)\|_{\ell^{1}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}}\gcd(t,N)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{d\mid N}d\sum_{t:\gcd(t,N)=d}1,

where the latter expression is clearly bounded above by the divisor function and therefore grows sub-polynomially in
N
. Since an
\varepsilon
-loss in
N
is permissible for the present purpose of establishing inequalities of the form (
56
), one may work with the strong-type
\ell^{\beta}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})-
norms of the
G_{j,k}
. It is remarked that if
N=p^{M}
where
p
is prime, then the divisors are totally ordered and the weak-type norms of
\gcd(\cdot,p^{M})
are uniformly bounded, whereas the
\ell^{1}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})
norm is equal to
M
. Thus, if one were to restrict
N
to powers of
p
and seek stronger Fourier restriction estimates with bounds which are uniform in the power
M
, then the full strength of Christ’s multilinear inequality would be needed.

Returning to the present situation, one obtains via the first step of Christ’s argument (simple interpolation) the inequality

\frac{1}{N^{n}}\sum_{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*}^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}F(t_{i})\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{k},N)^{\gamma}\ \leq\ C_{s,\gamma,\varepsilon}N^{\varepsilon}\|F\|_{\ell^{\alpha}([{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]_{*})}^{n}

(70)

for
\gamma\leq 2/n
and
\alpha^{-1}+\gamma(n-1)/2\leq 1
.

Remark 7.7
.

As in the euclidean setting, the stated range of exponents for (
70
) is sharp. In fact, the familiar scaling argument given by taking
F:=\chi_{{\mathcal{B}}_{d}}
for
d
a divisor of
N
shows necessarily that
\alpha^{-1}+\gamma(n-1)/2\leq 1
. Furthermore, plugging the function
F:=1
into (
70
) shows that
\gamma\leq 2/n
must hold. Indeed, if
N=p^{M}
where
M>n
and
p
is a prime, then, by restricting the range of summation, the left-hand side of (
70
) is bounded below by

\frac{1}{p^{Mn}}\sum_{0\leq u_{1}<\dots<u_{n-1}\leq M}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{i}=0\\[5.0pt]
p^{u_{i}}\|t_{i}-t_{i-1}\end{subarray}}^{p^{M}-1}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{k},p^{M})^{\gamma}.

Each summand
\prod_{j<k}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{k},p^{M})^{\gamma}
over this restricted range of summation is equal to

\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n-1}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{j+1},p^{M})^{\gamma(n-j)}

and this readily shows that

p^{-Mn}\sum_{\vec{t}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{M}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\gcd(t_{j}-t_{k},p^{M})^{\gamma}\ \geq\ 2^{-(n-1)}p^{(n-1)(\gamma n/2-1)M},

forcing
\gamma\leq 2/n
.

Assuming Conjecture
7.6
one may apply (
70
) to bound the right-hand side of (
65
) with
\gamma=\rho-1
and
\alpha
satisfying
\alpha\rho=r^{\prime}
(the restriction
\gamma\leq 2/n
needed for the application of (
70
) is equivalent to
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+2)/2
and the condition
\alpha^{-1}+\gamma(n-1)/2\leq 1
is equivalent to
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+1)/2s
). Combining this inequality with (
64
), one concludes that, conditionally on Conjecture
7.6
, the desired restriction estimate (
63
) holds with
sn(n+1)/2\leq r^{\prime}
in the range
r^{\prime}\geq n(n+2)/2
.

7.4
Remarks and partial progress towards Conjecture
7.6

In the previous subsection restriction estimates for the moment curve
\{(t,t^{2},\dots,t^{n}):t\in{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}\}
were shown to follow from (the purely number-theoretic) Conjecture
7.6
which concerns the number of mutually incongruent solutions to a simple system of equations. In particular, for each fixed
\vec{y}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
one wishes to determine an upper bound for the number
\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};N)
of solutions in
[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
to the polynomial system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}X_{1}+\dots+X_{n}&\equiv&y_{1}+\dots+y_{n}\\
&\vdots&\\
X_{1}^{n}+\dots+X_{n}^{n}&\equiv&y_{1}^{n}+\dots+y_{n}^{n}\end{array}\mod N.

(71)

This problem is arguably of interest in its own right; for instance, it can be reinterpreted as a natural question regarding factorisations of polynomials over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 7.8
.

Suppose every prime factor
p
of
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
p>n
and that
F\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X]
splits over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
, so that
F(X)\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-y_{j})\bmod N
for some choice of roots
\vec{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
. The number of ways
F
can be factorised as a product of linear factors over
{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}
is
\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};N)
.

Proof.

Under the hypotheses of the lemma, it suffices to show that the set of solutions to (
71
) is precisely

\big\{\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-x_{j})\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-y_{j})\bmod N\big\}.

Since the coefficients of a polynomial are elementary symmetric functions of the roots, it follows that
\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-x_{j})\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{n}(X-y_{j})\bmod N
for some
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
if and only if
e_{k}(\vec{x}\,)\equiv e_{k}(\vec{y}\,)\bmod N
for
1\leq k\leq n
, where
e_{k}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
is the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial. By the classical Newton–Girard formulæ, if
p>n
, then this is equivalent to the condition that
\vec{x}
solves (
71
).
∎

Although Conjecture
7.6
remains open, there has been some partial progress on the problem. First observe that, by the Chinese remainder theorem, the function
\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};N)
is multiplicative in
N
and it therefore suffices to prove that

\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};p^{\alpha})\leq C_{n}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}\gcd(y_{j}-y_{k},p^{\alpha})

uniformly over all primes
p>n
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Indeed, this follows from the asymptotics for the distinct divisor function
\omega(N):=\sum_{p\mid N}1
, as discussed in the previous section.

If the
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}
are sufficiently separated in the
p
-adic sense, then Conjecture
7.6
is a simple consequence of Hensel’s classical lemma.

Lemma 7.9
.

Suppose
p>n
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If
\vec{y}=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
satisfies
\delta<\alpha/2
where
p^{\delta}\|\prod_{j<k}(y_{j}-y_{k})
, then

\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};p^{\alpha})\leq n!p^{\delta}=n!p^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq n}|y_{j}-y_{k}|^{-1}.

This bound quickly leads to a resolution of Conjecture
7.6
in the
n=2
case.

Corollary 7.10
.

Suppose
p
is an odd prime and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For all
\vec{y}=(y_{1},y_{2})\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{2}
one has

\mathbf{N}(\vec{y};p^{\alpha})\leq 2p|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{-1}.

(72)

Proof.

Let
p^{\delta}\|\gcd(y_{1}-y_{2},p^{\alpha})
. Lemma
7.9
implies that (
72
) holds whenever
\delta<\alpha/2
and so one may assume without loss of generality that
\delta\geq\alpha/2
. Thus, in particular,

p^{\lceil\alpha/2\rceil}|(y_{1}-y_{2}).

(73)

Let
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}]^{2}
be a solution to the system

\begin{array}[]{rcl}X_{1}+X_{2}&\equiv&y_{1}+y_{2}\\
X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2}&\equiv&y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}\end{array}\mod p^{\alpha}.

(74)

By the elementary formula
(X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}=2(X_{1}^{2}+X_{2}^{2})-(X_{1}+X_{2})^{2}
one deduces that
(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}\equiv(y_{1}-y_{2})^{2}\bmod p^{\alpha}
and, recalling (
73
), the solution
\vec{x}
satisfies

Appendix A
Counting solutions to linear systems of congruences

The following lemma was used a number of times in the text.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\vec{b}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
and suppose
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
satisfies
\det A\not\equiv 0\bmod N
. The number of solutions
\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}
to the system of linear congruences
A\vec{x}=\vec{b}
is either 0 or
N/|\det A|
.

Proof.

Since the desired estimate is multiplicative, one may assume without loss of generality that
N=p^{\alpha}
is a power of a fixed prime. Furthermore, for any
A\in\mathrm{Mat}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
there exist unimodular matrices
U,V\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
VAU
is diagonal (this a consequence of the existence of the
Smith normal form
of the matrix
A
, which holds for arbitrary (that is, not necessarily square) matrices over any principal ideal domain: see, for instance,
[
37
]
). Since
|\det A|=|\det VAU|
and

|\{\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:A\vec{x}=\vec{b}\}|=|\{\vec{x}\in[{\mathbb{Z}}/N{\mathbb{Z}}]^{n}:VAU\vec{x}=V\vec{b}\}|,

one may further assume that
A
itself is diagonal.

Let
\lambda_{i}
denote the
(i,i)
-entry of
A
and write
p^{\psi_{i}}=\gcd(\lambda_{i},p^{\alpha})
for
i=1,\dots,n
. The number of solutions
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}/p^{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}
to the univariate system

\lambda_{i}x=b_{i}

is equal to 0 if
p^{\psi_{i}}\nmid b_{i}
and is equal to
p^{\psi_{i}}
otherwise. Thus, the total number of solutions to the system is at most
p^{\psi_{1}+\dots+\psi_{n}}
. Finally, by hypothesis
p^{\alpha}\nmid\det A=\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}
and so

p^{\psi_{1}+\dots+\psi_{n}}=\gcd(\det A,p^{\alpha})=\frac{N}{|\det A|},

as required.
∎

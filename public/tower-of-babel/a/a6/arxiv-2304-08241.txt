Decentralized projected Riemannian gradient method for smooth optimization on compact submanifolds
Kangkang Deng, Jiang Hu

Abstract
We consider the problem of decentralized nonconvex optimization over a compact submanifold, where each local agent’s objective function defined by the local dataset is smooth. Leveraging the powerful tool of proximal smoothness, we establish local linear convergence of the projected gradient descent method with a unit step size for solving the consensus problem over the nonconvex compact submanifold. This serves as the basis for designing and analyzing decentralized algorithms on manifolds. Subsequently, we propose two decentralized methods: the decentralized projected Riemannian gradient descent (DPRGD) and the decentralized projected Riemannian gradient tracking (DPRGT). We establish their convergence rates of 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / K ) \mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{K}) and 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 / K ) \mathcal{O}(1/K) , respectively, to reach a stationary point. To the best of our knowledge, DPRGT is the first decentralized algorithm to achieve exact convergence for solving decentralized optimization over a compact submanifold. Beyond the linear convergence results on the consensus, two key tools developed in the proof are the Lipschitz-type inequality of the projection operator and the Riemannian quadratic upper bound for smooth functions on the compact submanifold, which could be of independent interest. Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed methods compared to state-of-the-art ones through numerical experiments on eigenvalue problems and low-rank matrix completion.

Introduction

Decentralized optimization has gained significant attention in recent years due to its potential applications in large-scale distributed systems, such as sensor networks, distributed computing systems, and machine learning. In these systems, data is often distributed across multiple agents or nodes, and a centralized optimization approach may not be feasible due to issues such as privacy concerns and limited computational resources. In this paper, we consider the distributed smooth optimization over a compact submanifold

1.1
Contribution

This paper involves proving the linear convergence of consensus error and, based on this, developing two decentralized gradient-type algorithms to solve (
1.1
). We summarize our contributions as follows:

•

Linear consensus of the projected gradient method.
We present an Euclidean distance-based consensus problem as minimizing a quadratic function over smooth submanifolds (defined in (
3.1
)). To address the inherent nonconvexity from the submanifold constraint, we leverage the powerful tool of proximal smoothness from variational analysis, establishing the linear convergence of the projected gradient descent within a neighborhood with explicit characterization (see Section
3
). Such results are instrumental for analyzing decentralized manifold optimization algorithms. Compared to the results for the Stiefel manifold
[
13
]
, our analysis allows the use of the unit step size and is applicable to a broader range of compact smooth submanifolds. Notably, our approach can be extended to other nonconvex sets or functions, such as strongly prox-regular functions
[
26
]
.

•

Derivation of essential equalities on the compact submanifold.

We derive a Riemannian quadratic upper bound for differentiable functions on compact submanifolds with locally Lipschitz continuous gradients. This serves as a key tool for analyzing the convergence of Riemannian gradient-type algorithms, paralleling its counterpart in the Euclidean space. Using the proximal smoothness, we present a Lipschitz-type inequality for the projection operator on compact manifolds, analogous to the Lipschitz-type inequalities of the retraction operator in
[
9
]
, playing a central role in analyzing projected gradient methods.

•

The first decentralized algorithms for solving (
1.1
) with exact convergence.

Building upon the linear consensus of the projected gradient method, we propose a decentralized projected Riemannian gradient descent method (Algorithm
1
) for solving (
1.1
), where each step combines a projected gradient step for the consensus and a Riemannian gradient step for
f
. We show that the iteration complexity of obtaining an
\epsilon
-stationary point (see Definition
2.2
) is
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
(see Theorem
5.1
). To achieve exact convergence with a constant step size, we propose a decentralized projected Riemannian gradient tracking method (Algorithm
2
) and establish an improved iteration complexity of
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-1})
(see Theorem
5.2
). Numerical results on (generalized) eigenvalue problems and low-rank matrix completion demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed method compared with state-of-the-art ones.

3
Consensus problem over a compact submanifold

To achieve the stationarity given in Definition

We assume that the undirected graph

k\geq 0
and the following linear convergence with rate
\rho_{t}:=2\sigma_{2}^{t}<1
holds,

\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\|\leq\rho_{t}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|.

Proof.

Since
\|\hat{x}_{0}-\bar{x}_{0}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\gamma
, by invoking Lemma
3.2
with
\alpha_{k}=0,u_{i,k}=0,i\in[n]
, it follows that for any
k>0
,
\mathbf{x}_{k}\in\mathcal{N}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}{W_{ij}^{t}}x_{j,k}\in\bar{U}_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma),~i\in[n].

Based on the iterative scheme in (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|=\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{n}}(\mathbf{W}^{t}\mathbf{x}_{k})-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k})\|

(3.7)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathbf{W}^{t}\mathbf{x}_{k}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|=2\|(W^{t}\otimes I_{d})\mathbf{x}_{k}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|

\displaystyle=2\|((W^{t}-J)\otimes I_{d})(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sigma_{2}^{t}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|\leq 2\sigma_{2}^{t}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|,

where the first inequality utilizes (
3.3
). The proof is completed.

The result above is also applicable to the case when
\mathcal{M}
is a convex set. In this scenario, by utilizing the 1-Lipschitz continuity of the projection operator, the linear rate can be improved to
\sigma_{2}^{t}
, which is consistent with the results in
[
39
,
36
]
. It is worth noting that the rate in Theorem
3.3
will asymptotically go to
\sigma_{2}^{t}
as
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathcal{M}}
is approximately
1
-Lipschitz continuously near
\mathcal{M}
(i.e., the second inequality in (
3.7
) can be tighter when
\mathbf{W}^{t}\mathbf{x}_{k}
and
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k}
are closer to
\mathcal{M}^{n}
).

4
Useful inequalities about compact submanifolds

In this section, we will present several useful inequalities related to the compact submanifold, which will serve as key components in the analysis of decentralized algorithms for solving (
1.1
) in the next section. We believe that such inequalities will be useful in tackling manifold optimization problems of a more general nature. Let us start with the following assumption concerning problem (
1.1
).

Assumption 4.1
.

Each objective function
f_{i}
is of gradient Lipschitz continuous with modulus
L_{f}
on the convex hull of
\mathcal{M}
, denoted by
{\rm conv}(\mathcal{M})
, i.e., for any
x,y\in{\rm conv}(\mathcal{M})
, it holds that

\|\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(y)\|\leq L_{f}\|x-y\|,\quad i\in[n].

(4.1)

Moreover, the Euclidean gradient is bounded by
L_{G}
, i.e.,
\max_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|\leq L_{G},\;i\in[n].

The above assumption is standard and commonly used in decentralized optimization
[
40
,
61
,
12
]
. Given the compactness of
\mathcal{M}
, Assumption
4.1
holds for any differentiable function
f
with a locally Lipschitz continuous gradient. Using (
4.1
), we can readily obtain a quadratic upper bound for
f_{i}
as follows:

f_{i}(y)\leq f_{i}(x)+\left<\nabla f_{i}(x),y-x\right>+\frac{L_{f}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\;\;\forall x,y\in{\rm conv}(\mathcal{M}),\;\;i\in[n].

(4.2)

Based on (
4.2
) and the properties of proximally smooth sets, we show Riemannian quadratic upper bound for
f_{i}
in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
(Riemannian quadratic upper bound).

Under Assumption
4.1
, for any
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
, the following inequality holds:

f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(x)\leq\left<\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x),y-x\right>+\frac{L_{g}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},~i\in[n],

(4.3)

where

L_{g}:=L_{f}+\frac{1}{2\gamma}L_{G}.

Moreover, we have

\|\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x)-\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(y)\|\leq(L_{f}+L_{G}L_{\mathcal{P}})\|x-y\|,~i\in[n],

(4.4)

where
L_{\mathcal{P}}
is a positive constant.

Proof.

Under Assumption
4.1
, for any
i\in[n]
, it holds that

\displaystyle f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(x)-\left<\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x),y-x\right>

(4.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{i}(y)-f_{i}(x)-\left<\nabla f_{i}(x),y-x\right>+\left<\mathcal{P}_{N_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(x)),y-x\right>

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{L_{f}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}+\frac{\left\|\mathcal{P}_{N_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(x))\right\|}{4\gamma}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{L_{f}}{2}+\frac{1}{4\gamma}\max_{z\in\mathcal{M}}\|\nabla f_{i}(z)\|\right)\|y-x\|^{2}.

where the first inequality utilizes (
4.2
) and (
3.4
). This implies (
4.3
). It follows from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x)-\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(y)\|=\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(x))-\mathcal{P}_{T_{y}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(y))\|

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(y))\|+\|\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(y))-\mathcal{P}_{T_{y}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla f_{i}(y))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L_{f}\|y-x\|+\|\nabla f_{i}(y)\|L_{\mathcal{P}}(\|y-x\|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(L_{f}+L_{G}L_{\mathcal{P}})\|y-x\|,

where we use the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}
over
x\in\mathcal{M}
(
L_{\mathcal{P}}
is the associate modulus). This gives (
4.4
). The proof is completed.

The above Riemannian quadratic upper bound will serve as a key tool for analyzing the convergence of Riemannian gradient-type algorithms, paralleling its counterpart in the Euclidean space.

Analogous to the role of Lipschitz-type inequalities of the retraction for Riemannian gradient algorithms
[
9
]
, the following Lipschitz-type inequality for the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
is crucial in the analysis of projection-based methods.

Lemma 4.3
(Lipschitz-type inequalities on the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
).

For any
x\in\mathcal{M},u\in\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}:\|u\|\leq\gamma\}
,
there exists a constant
Q
such that

\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)-x-\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(u)\|\leq Q\|u\|^{2}.

(4.7)

Proof.

Let us denote
u_{1}=\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(u)
,
u_{2}=u-u_{1}
. Since the projection operator is a retraction operator
[
2
]
, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1})-x-u_{1}\|\leq M_{1}\|u_{1}\|^{2}\leq M_{1}\|u\|^{2}.

(4.8)

For a
2\gamma
-proximally smooth
\mathcal{M}
, by (
3.4
), it holds
\left\langle u,y-x\right\rangle\leq\frac{\|u\|}{4\gamma}\|y-x\|^{2}
for any
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and
u\in N_{x}\mathcal{M}
. Then

\|y-(x+u)\|^{2}=\|y-x\|^{2}-2\left\langle u,y-x\right\rangle+\|u\|^{2}\geq(1-\frac{\|u\|}{2\gamma})\|y-x\|^{2}+\|u\|^{2}\geq\|u\|^{2}.

Hence, for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
\|u\|\leq 2\gamma
,

x=\arg\min_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\|y-(x+u)\|^{2}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u).

(4.9)

Let
K=\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}:\|\eta\|\leq\gamma\}
be a compact subset of the tangent bundle. For all
u\in K
, by noting the smoothness of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}

[
20
, Lemma]
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1})\|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1}+tu_{2}))\right\|dt

(4.10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\|D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1}+tu_{2})[u_{2}]\right\|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left\|D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1}+tu_{2})[u_{2}]-D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+tu_{2})[u_{2}]\right\|dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\eta\in K}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+\eta)\|_{\rm op}\|u_{1}\|\|u_{2}\|,

where
\|\cdot\|_{\rm op}
represents the operator norm and the first inequality uses
D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+tu_{2})[u_{2}]=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+tu_{2}+\epsilon u_{2})-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+tu_{2}))/\epsilon=0
by (
4.9
). For all
u\notin K
, we have

\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1})\|\leq\zeta\leq\frac{\zeta}{\gamma^{2}}\|u\|^{2},

(4.11)

where
\zeta=\max_{x,y\in\mathcal{M}}\|x-y\|
is the maximal distance between any two points on
\mathcal{M}
.
Defining

Q_{0}=\max\left(\max_{\eta\in K}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+\eta)\|_{\rm op},\frac{\zeta}{\gamma^{2}}\right)

and combining (
4.11
) with (
4.8
), we have the following result:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)-x-u_{1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u)-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1})\|+\|\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x+u_{1})-x-u_{1}\|

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq Q_{0}\|u\|^{2}+M_{1}\|u_{1}\|^{2}\leq(Q_{0}+M_{1})\|u\|^{2},

which gives (
4.7
) with
Q=Q_{0}+M_{1}
.

Another useful inequality that we establish is the control of the distance between the Euclidean mean
\hat{x}
and the manifold mean
\bar{x}
by the square of consensus error.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{M}^{n}
satisfying
\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\leq\gamma
, we have

\|\bar{x}-\hat{x}\|\leq M_{2}\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}}{n},

(4.13)

where
M_{2}=\max_{x\in{\rm conv}(\mathcal{M})}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x)\|_{\rm op}
.

Proof.

Since
x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
, the definitions of
\hat{x}
and
\bar{x}
yield

\displaystyle\hat{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i}),~\bar{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x}).

(4.14)

Since
\bar{x}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\hat{x})
, we get
\hat{x}-\bar{x}\in N_{\bar{x}}\mathcal{M}.
Therefore,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})[x_{i}-\bar{x}]=D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-\bar{x}\right]=D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})[\hat{x}-\bar{x}]=0,

(4.15)

where we use the fact in
[
2
, Lemma 3.1]
that
D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})=\mathcal{P}_{T_{\bar{x}\mathcal{M}}}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\hat{x}-\bar{x}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x_{i})-\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})\right)\right\|=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x}+t(x_{i}-\bar{x}))\right)\right\|dt

(4.16)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x}+t(x_{i}-\bar{x}))[x_{i}-\bar{x}]\right\|dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x}+t(x_{i}-\bar{x}))[x_{i}-\bar{x}]-D\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{x})[x_{i}-\bar{x}]\right)\right\|dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max_{x\in\text{conv}(\mathcal{M})}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x)\|_{\rm op}\left\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in\text{conv}(\mathcal{M})}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(x)\|_{\rm op}\frac{\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}}{n},

where we use the smoothness of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}
over
\bar{U}_{\mathcal{M}}(\gamma)

[
20
, Lemma]
. The proof is completed.

5
Decentralized projected Riemannian gradient-type methods

In this section, we present two decentralized projected Riemannian gradient-type methods, the decentralized projected Riemannian gradient method (DPRGD) and the decentralized projected Riemannian gradient tracking method (DPRGT) for solving problem (
1.1
), and corresponding convergence analysis.

5.1
The Algorithms

In the DPRGD method for solving (
1.1
), each step consists of a projected gradient step for the consensus and a Riemannian gradient step for the local objective function
f_{i}
.
Specifically, given an adjacency matrix
W
of the communication network, in the
k
-th iteration, the DPRGD performs the following update

\displaystyle x_{i,k+1}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j,k}-\alpha_{k}\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x_{i,k})\right),\;\;i\in[n],

(5.1)

where
\alpha_{k}>0
is the step size and
t\geq 1
is an integer. The DPRGD method is presented in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Decentralized Projected Riemannian Gradient Descent (DPRGD) for solving (
1.1
)

0:

Initial point
\mathbf{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{N}
, an integer
t
, set
k=1
.

1:

while
the condition is not met
do

2:

Choose diminishing step size
\alpha_{k}=\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k})
.

3:

Update

x_{i,k+1}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j,k}-\alpha_{k}\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x_{i,k})\right),

for each node
i\in[n]
, in parallel.

4:

Set
k=k+1
.

5:

end

while

As demonstrated in Section
3
, our projected gradient step with a unit step size, i.e.,
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{jk})
, for the consensus problem (
3.1
) achieves locally linear convergence, which enables us to establish the convergence of Algorithm
1
without introducing an additional step size parameter on the consensus.

Algorithm 2

)

Initial point

, an integer

, the step size
\alpha
. Set
k=0
.

on each node

.

do

(5.2)

Next, we investigate a gradient tracking method for solving (

) by leveraging the gradient tracking techniques introduced in

,

,
12
]
to get a better estimate for the full gradient.
In the
k
-th iteration, our DPRGT method performs the following update, for all
i\in[n]
,

\displaystyle x_{i,k+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j,k}-\alpha\mathcal{P}_{T_{x_{i,k}}\mathcal{M}}(s_{i,k})\right),

\displaystyle s_{i,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}s_{j,k}+\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x_{i,k+1})-\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x_{i,k}),

where
\alpha>0
is the step size and the projection of
s_{i,k}
to

is used in

. The detailed description is presented in Algorithm

. A crucial advantage of gradient tracking-type methods lies in the applicability of the use of a constant step size

.

5.2
Convergence analysis

This subsection focuses on the complexity results for DPRGD and DPRGT algorithms. Let us first present the following main theorem on the
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{-2})
iteration complexity of the DPRGD method (i.e., Algorithm
1
) to reach the

-stationary point of (

.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}_{k}

3.1
and
4.1

it follows that:

\frac{1}{n}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{K}-\mathbf{x}_{K}\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{K}),

(5.3)

\min_{k\leq K}\|\mathrm{grad}f(\bar{x}_{k})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{K+1}}).

(5.4)

We would like to highlight that it is possible to develop a stochastic variant of Algorithm
1
by employing a stochastic estimation of the gradient
\mathrm{grad}f_{i}(x_{i,k})

The following theorem shows that by incorporating gradient tracking, Algorithm
2
yields improved complexity in reaching an

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\{\mathbf{x}_{k}\}_{k}

and

(5.5)

and

5.2.1
Two key lemmas

In the following lemma, we show that
\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{grad}\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x})\right\|
is bounded by the square of consensus error.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
L_{2}:=2\max_{x\in{\rm conv}(\mathcal{M})}\|D^{2}\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)\|_{\rm op}
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{M}^{n}
, it holds that

\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{grad}\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x})\|\leq\sqrt{n}L_{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2}.

(5.6)

Proof.

Using the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[x_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j}\right]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{grad}\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x})\right\|=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\mathcal{P}_{T_{x_{i}}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}))-\mathcal{P}_{T_{\bar{x}}\mathcal{M}}(\nabla\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}))\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{2}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\|\nabla\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x})\|\right]\leq\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left(\max_{i\in[n]}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-\sum_{j=1}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{L_{2}}{2}\left(\max_{i\in[n]}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\right)\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|+\|\sum_{j=1}W_{ij}^{t}x_{j}-\bar{x}\|\right]

\displaystyle\leq L_{2}\left(\max_{i\in[n]}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-\bar{x}\|\leq\sqrt{n}L_{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|^{2},

where the first inequality is due to the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathcal{P}_{T_{x}\mathcal{M}}(\cdot)
over
x\in\mathcal{M}
, the third inequality is due to the triangle inequality of
\|\cdot\|
, the fourth inequality uses the convexity of
\|\cdot\|
, and the last inequality comes from
\|a\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{n}\|a\|
and
\|a\|_{\infty}\leq\|a\|
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The next technical result bounds the distance between
\bar{x}_{k+1}
and
\bar{x}_{k}
for a given iterative process.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
x_{i,k+1}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{ij}^{t}x_{i,k}-\alpha_{k}u_{i,k}\right)
, where
u_{i,k}\in T_{x_{i,k}}\mathcal{M}
. Denote
\hat{u}_{k}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i,k}
. Suppose that Assumption
3.1
holds.
It holds that

\displaystyle\|\bar{x}_{k+1}-\bar{x}_{k}\|

(5.7)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{8Q+\sqrt{n}L_{2}+M_{2}}{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2Q\alpha_{k}^{2}}{n}\|\mathbf{u}_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}\|\hat{u}_{k}\|+\frac{M_{2}}{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\|\nabla\phi^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{k})\|=\|(I_{nd}-\mathbf{W}^{t})\mathbf{x}_{k}\|=\|(I_{nd}-\mathbf{W}^{t})(\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k})\|\leq 2\|\mathbf{x}_{k}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{k}\|
, we have

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{k+1}-\hat{x}_{k}\|

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{k+1}-\hat{x}_{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathrm{grad}\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}u_{i,k})\|+\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathrm{grad}\phi_{i}^{t}(\mathbf{x}_{k})+\alpha_{k}u_{i,k})\|

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{projec-second-order1}}{\leq}

6.2
Decentralized generalized eigenvalue problem

Consider the following decentralized generalized eigenvalue problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\top}A_{i}^{\top}A_{i}x_{i},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n},

\displaystyle x_{i}^{\top}Bx_{i}=I_{r},\quad\forall i\in[n],

where
B
is a positive definite matrix. The optimization algorithms for solving the centralized version of (
6.2
) have been well studied in
[
21
]
. Applications with form (
6.2
) appear in the kernel supervised PCA
[
5
]
, Fisher discriminant analysis
[
19
]
, and canonical correlation analysis
[
23
,
11
]
.

In the test, we set
n=8
and use the same way in Section
6.1
to generate
A_{i}
’s. For
B
, we first generate an orthogonal matrix
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and then set
B=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}
with
\Lambda={\rm diag}(1.1,1.1^{0.5},\ldots,1.1^{d/2-0.5})
. We set
d=10
and
r=5
, i.e.,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{10\times 5}
for any
i\in[n]
. For the network topology matrix, we use the ER network with
p=0.6
. For the generalized Stiefel manifold
{\rm St}_{B}(d,r):={x\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}:x^{\top}Bx=I_{r}}
, the projection is the polar decomposition on

defined in
[

]

6.3
Decentralized low-rank matrix completion

The goal of the low-rank matrix completion problem (LRMC) is to recover a low-rank matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times T}
from its partial observations. Let
\Omega
be the set of indices of known entries in
A
, the rank-
r
LRMC problem can be written as

\min_{X\in{\rm Gr}(d,r),V\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times T}}\quad\frac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(XV-A)\|^{2},

(6.3)

where
{\rm Gr}(d,r):=\{{\rm all~}r{\rm~dimensional~subspaces~in~}\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is the Grassmann manifold and the projection operator
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}
is defined in an element-wise manner with
(\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(A))_{ij}=A_{ij}
if
(i,j)\in\Omega
and
0
otherwise.

For the decentralized setting, let us consider the case when the data matrix
\mathcal{P}_{\Omega}(A)
is equally divided into
n
agents by columns, denoted by
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n}
. As the Grassmann manifold
{\rm Gr}(n,d)
is a quotient manifold with its total space being
{\rm St}(n,d)
, we can also regard (
6.3
) as an optimization on the Stiefel manifold, which is similar to the case of the principal component analysis in Section
6.1
. We refer to
[
1
,
24
]
for the equivalence of the optimization algorithms between the quotient manifold and its total space.
Then, the decentralized LRMC problem is

\displaystyle\min

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{i}}(X_{i}V_{i}(X)-A_{i})\|^{2},

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{s.t.}

\displaystyle X_{1}=X_{2}=\cdots=X_{n},

\displaystyle X_{i}\in{\rm St}(d,r),\quad\forall i\in[n],

where
\Omega_{i}
is the corresponding indices set of
\Omega
and
V_{i}(X):={\rm argmin}_{V}\|\mathcal{P}_{\Omega_{i}}(XV-A_{i})\|
.

For numerical tests, we consider random generated
A
. To be specific, we first generate two random matrices
L\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times r}
and
R\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times T}
, where each element obeys the standard Gaussian distribution. For the indices set
\Omega
, we generate a random matrix
B
with each element following from the uniform distribution, then set
\Omega_{ij}=1
if
B_{ij}\leq\nu
and
0
otherwise. The parameter
\nu
is set to
r(m+T-r)/(mT)
. In the implementations, we set
T=1000,m=100,r=5
. The fixed step sizes are chosen for all algorithms. The Ring graph is used. We set
\alpha=n\hat{\beta}
For DPRGT and DRGTA and
\alpha=\frac{\hat{\beta}}{\sqrt{K}}
with
K
is the maximal number of iterations for DPRGD and DRDGD.
\hat{\beta}
is tuned to get the best performance for each algorithm individually.
The results are reported in Figure
6
. We see that DPRGT performs slightly better than DRGTA for both cases. Similarly, DPRGD can achieve lower objective function values compared to DRDGD.

n=16
, right:
n=32
.

7
Conclusion

We propose two distributed algorithms for solving decentralized optimization problems over a compact submanifold. To analyze these algorithms, we study the linear convergence of the projected gradient method with unit step size for solving the consensus problem, utilizing the proximal smoothness of the compact submanifold and analyzing several essential Lipschitz-type inequalities. By putting these together, we present the complexity results of the proposed algorithms, which match the best-known results in the Stiefel manifold case
[
12
]
.
Numerical results demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed algorithms. It is worth mentioning that the linear convergence of the projected gradient method and the Lipschitz-type inequality established here can be applied to analyze more general manifold optimization and decentralized optimization problems, e.g., problem (
1.1
) with weakly convex
f_{i}

[
53
,
41
,
56
]
and decentralized composite optimization with strongly prox-regular regularizer
[
27
]
.

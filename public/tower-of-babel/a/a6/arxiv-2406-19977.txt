Graded differential groups, Cartan-Eilenberg systems and conjectures in Conley index theory
Kelly Spendlove, Robert Vandervorst

Abstract
Cartan-Eilenberg systems play an prominent role in the homological algebra of filtered and graded differential groups and (co)chain complexes in particular. We define the concept of Cartan-Eilenberg systems of abelian groups over a poset. Our main result states that a filtered chain isomorphism between free, 𝖯 {\mathsf{P}} -graded differential groups is equivalent to an isomorphism between associated Cartan-Eilenberg systems. An application of this result to the theory of dynamical systems addresses two open conjectures posed by J. Robbin and D. Salamon regarding uniqueness type questions for connection matrices, as detailed in [ 11 ] and [ 4 ] . The main result of this paper also proves that the connection matrix theories in [ 3 ] , [ 11 ] and [ 5 ] are equivalent in the setting of vector spaces, as well as uniqueness of connection matrices for Morse-Smale gradient systems, cf. [ 10 ] .

Theorem A
(cf. Thm.

)

Two

{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential groups
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphic
if and only if
there respective Cartan-Eilenberg systems are isomorphic.

An important application of Theorem A is to the theory of connection matrices developed by R. Franzosa, who introduces
connection matrices in the algebraic topological theory of Morse representations for dynamical systems, cf.
[
3
]
and Sect.
6

Connection matrices contain information about the connection orbit structure between the Morse sets in a Morse representation and generate the central algebraic structure referred to as a
module braid
in
[
2

3
]
, which serves as an invariant for the Morse representation in question.
In Section
7
we prove that Franzosa’s module braids in the setting of Morse representations are examples of Cartan-Eilenberg systems. The main result in
[
3
]
states that these module braids are always generated, under some mild conditions, by an appropriately constructed free, graded differential group — a
connection matrix
.
Theorem A shows that two connection matrices assigned to a Morse representation are conjugate via filtered chain isomorphisms and thus unique up to filtered conjugacy.
A first partial result in this direction was obtained in
[
4
]
and
[
5
]
. Theorem A settles the two central conjectures formulated in
[
11
]

concerning the similarity of connection matrices, and completes the result in
[
4
]
.
In Section
7
we give an extensive account of these conjectures and their proofs.
Another consequence of Theorem A concerns connection matrix theories in the setting of vector spaces.

Theorem B
(cf. Thm.
7.1
)

Let
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
be an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space.
Then, every strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})

whose Cartan-Eilenberg systems is isomorphic to
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
,
is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent to
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.

As a consequence of Theorem B the connection matrix theories of
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
5

in the setting of finite dimensional differential vector spaces are equivalent. Without the restriction of finite dimensionality the notions in
[
11
]
and
[
3
]
are equivalent.

Another application of Theorem A (Thm.
5.1
) involves the uniqueness of connection matrices for Morse-Smale gradient flows. A
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded chain complex
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector spaces is Morse-Smale graded if there exists an order-preserving map
\mu\colon{\mathsf{P}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

\mu(p)=\mu(q)
,
p\neq q
, then
p~\|~q
, and
G_{p}C_{k}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.
The pair
({\mathsf{P}},\mu)
is called a Morse-Smale grading.
For such chain complexes we have the following uniqueness result.

Theorem C
(cf. Thm.
7.2
)

Let
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
and
(C,{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C})
be algebraic Morse-Smale graded chain complexes with Morse-Smale grading
({\mathsf{P}},\mu)
for both chain complexes.
Then,
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}={\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C}
if and only if
\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\cong\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C})

Theorem C applies to Morse-Smale gradient flows and provides an algebraic proof of a result by J. Reineck, cf.
[

, that connection matrices for such systems are unique, cf. Sect.

Graded and filtered differential groups

An
ordered decomposition
of an abelian group
C

is a finite poset
\mathrm{\Pi}=(\mathrm{\Pi},\leq)
consisting of non-trivial projections
\pi\colon C\to C
such that

(i)

\pi\circ\pi^{\prime}=0
for all distinct pairs
\pi,\pi^{\prime}\in\mathrm{\Pi}
;

(ii)

\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{\Pi}}\pi=\mathrm{id}
.

Let
({\mathsf{P}},\leq)
be a finite poset and let
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
be the bounded, distributive lattice of down-sets in
{\mathsf{P}}
.
A
{\mathsf{P}}
-grading
on
C

is an order-embedding

\mathrm{grd}\colon\mathrm{\Pi}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{P}},

and
the pair
(C,\mathrm{grd})
is called a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group
.
A (finite) filtering on an abelian group
C
is a homomorphism

\mathrm{flt}\colon{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})\to\mathsf{Sub}~C,

where
\mathsf{Sub}~C
is the lattice of subgroups of
C
.
The image
of
\mathrm{flt}
is a sublattice consisting of subgroups
F_{\alpha}C\subseteq C
ordered by inclusion.
Note that
\alpha\mapsto F_{\alpha}C
being a lattice homomorphism implies that
F_{\alpha\cup\beta}C=F_{\alpha}C+F_{\beta}C
and
F_{\alpha\cap\beta}C=F_{\alpha}C\cap F_{\beta}C
.
The pair
(C,\mathrm{flt})
is called an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered group
.
A graded abelian group yields a filtering
\mathrm{flt}\colon{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})\to\mathsf{Sub}~C
of
C

given by
\alpha\mapsto F_{\alpha}C:=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\mathrm{grd}^{-1}(\alpha)}\pi C
.
We use the convention that the empty sum is
0
.
The homomorphism
\mathrm{flt}
extends to a meet-semilattice homomorphism

\beta\smallsetminus\alpha\mapsto G_{\beta\smallsetminus\alpha}C:=\bigoplus_{\pi\in\mathrm{grd}^{-1}(\beta\smallsetminus\alpha)}\pi C
. In particular, this assigns the ‘atoms’
\{p\}\mapsto G_{p}C
and associated decomposition:

G_{\beta\smallsetminus\alpha}C=\bigoplus_{p\in\beta\smallsetminus\alpha}G_{p}C,

with
G_{p}C=\pi C
, if
\mathrm{grd}^{-1}(\{p\})=\{\pi\}
and

G_{p}C=0
if
\mathrm{grd}^{-1}(\{p\})=\varnothing
.
We refer to the subgroups
G_{p}C
as the subgroups of homogeneous elements of degree
p
.
Since
\mathrm{grd}
need not be onto, some of the subgroups
G_{p}C
may be the trivial subgroup
0
for some
p
and therefore the subgroups
G_{p}C
coincide with the components
\img\pi
whenever
G_{p}C
is non-trivial. It is customary to denote the
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group by its decomposition
C=\bigoplus_{p\in{\mathsf{P}}}G_{p}C
.
A
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group
(C,\mathrm{grd})
, with
C=\bigoplus_{p\in{\mathsf{P}}}G_{p}C
,
is
free abelian
if and only if the components
G_{p}C
are free abelian.
Conversely, a filtered abelian group also yields a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group
A=\bigoplus_{p\in{\mathsf{P}}}G_{p}A
, by defining
G_{p}A\cong F_{\beta}C/F_{\alpha}C
,
\beta\smallsetminus\alpha=\{p\}
. In general
A
is not isomorphic to
C
.
If the groups
G_{p}A
are free there exist subspaces
G_{p}C\subseteq C
, with
C_{p}C\cong G_{p}A
, such that
F_{\alpha}C=\bigoplus_{p\in\alpha}G_{p}C
, cf.
[
11
]
.

A homomorphism
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
of
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian groups that satisfies the property
f\bigl(G_{p}C\bigr)\subseteq G_{q}C^{\prime}
is called
homogeneous
, or
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded
. A homomorphism
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
of
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded, or
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered groups that satisfies
f\bigl(F_{\alpha}C\bigr)\subseteq F_{\alpha}C^{\prime}
is called
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered
.
Note that homomorphisms of graded groups may be filtered when the graded groups are regarded as filtered groups,
with
F_{\alpha}C=\bigoplus_{p\in\alpha}G_{p}C
being the filtering induced by the
{\mathsf{P}}
-grading on
C
.
A homomorphism
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
is an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered isomorphism if

f(F_{\alpha}C)=F_{\alpha}C^{\prime},\quad\forall\alpha\in{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}}).

(4)

If the order structure of the grading, or filtering is clear from context we refer to (homogeneous) graded and filtered homomorphisms.

Remark 2

An isomorphism that preserves the filterings
is not necessarily an filtered isomorphism in the sense of Condition
(
4
) as the following example shows. Let
C={\mathbb{Z}}
and
C^{\prime}={\mathbb{Z}}
and
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
is the identity homomorphism.
Suppose
C
is filtered as
0\subseteq 4{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
and
C^{\prime}
is filtered as

0\subseteq 2{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}
. Clearly
f
is an isomorphism
but
f(4{\mathbb{Z}})\subsetneq 2{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If both maps
f
and
f^{-1}
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered, then

F_{\alpha}C=f^{-1}f(F_{\alpha}C)\subseteq f^{-1}(F_{\alpha}C^{\prime})\subseteq F_{\alpha}C
which yields (
4
). Thus filtered isomorphism is an isomorphism for which
f
and
f^{-1}
are filtered.

2.1
Differential groups

A
differential group

(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
consists of an abelian group
C
and endomorphism
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}\colon C\to C
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}^{2}=0
, cf.
(
8
, II.1)
. The elements
c\in C
are referred to as
chains
and the endomorphism
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
is referred to as the
differential
.
We fix the following notation:

Z(C):=\ker{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
are the
cycles
in
C
and
B(C):=\mathrm{im~}{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
are the
boundaries
in
C
. The
homology
of
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is defined as
H(C):=Z(C)/B(C)
.
A homomorphism
f\colon(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\to(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
of differential groups is a homomorphism which
satisfies
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}f=f{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
and is called a

chain homomorphism
, or
chain isomorphism
if
f
is an isomorphism of abelian groups.
Differential groups and chain homomorphisms form the category
\mathbf{Ab}_{\mathrm{d}}
. Homology for a differential group defines a covariant functor
{\mathsf{H}}\colon\mathbf{Ab}_{\mathrm{d}}\to\mathbf{Ab}

given by
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\mapsto H(C)
, called the
homology functor
.
The homology may be regarded as a differential group with
{\mathrm{d}}=0
.
Next we consider grading and filtering in combination with the differental
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
.

Definition 1

A triple
\bigl(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C},\mathrm{grd}\bigr)
is called a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential group
if

(i)

(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is a differential group;

(ii)

(C,\mathrm{grd})
is a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group with grading
\mathrm{grd}\colon\mathrm{\Pi}\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{P}}
;

(iii)

{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered.

A
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential group is
strict
if

(iv)

{\mathrm{d}}_{C}F_{\alpha}C\subseteq F_{\alpha\smallsetminus\{p\}}C
, for all
p
maximal in
\alpha
.

A chain homomorphism, or chain isomorphism between graded or filtered differential groups preserving the filtering is called an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain homomorphism
, or
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphism
.
The condition on
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
to be
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered implies that the restriction

{\mathrm{d}}_{C}\colon G_{q}C\to G_{p}C
is well-defined. The latter is non-trivial only if
p\leq q
. If the differential is strict then the latter inequality is strict, i.e.
p<q
. For any convex set
\beta\smallsetminus\alpha
the pair
(G_{\beta\smallsetminus\alpha}C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is a differential subgroup.
More generally a triple
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C},\mathrm{flt})
is called an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential group
if

(C,\mathrm{flt})
is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered and the differential satisfies
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}F_{\alpha}C\subseteq F_{\alpha}C
for all
\alpha\in{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
.
The
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtering induced by a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded group automatically
satisfies the conditions of a
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential group
via
F_{\alpha}C:=\bigoplus_{p\in\alpha}G_{p}C
.
As before the converse is not true in general.

Example 1

A chain complex
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is an example of a graded differential group, traditionally denoted by
C=\bigoplus_{k}C_{k}
, where the differential
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
is homogeneous of degree
-1
, and thus filtered. This is equivalent to

{\mathrm{d}}_{C}=\bigoplus_{k}{\mathrm{d}}_{k}
, with
{\mathrm{d}}_{k}\colon C_{k}\to C_{k-1}
and

{\mathrm{d}}_{k}{\mathrm{d}}_{k+1}=0
.
The chain maps are taken be to homogeneous, i.e.
f\colon C\to C^{\prime}
,
f=\bigoplus_{k}f_{k}
, with
f_{k}\colon C_{k}\to C^{\prime}_{k}
and
{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{k}f_{k}=f_{k-1}{\mathrm{d}}_{k}
, for all
k
.
The same holds for cochain complexes.

Remark 3

In the notation for graded and filtered differential groups we will omit
\mathrm{grd}
and
\mathrm{flt}
from the notation if there is no ambiguity about the grading or filtering, and simply write
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
.

3
Induced chain homomorphisms

In this section we prove two results, Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
, concerning chain homomorphisms that lift homomorphisms on homology.
We start with a number of preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 1

Let
D
be a free abelian group,
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
a differential group, and let
h\colon D\to A
be a homomorphism such that
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}h=0
and

.
4
4

4

If
D
is regarded as differential group with
{\mathrm{d}}=0
, then the condition

{\mathrm{d}}_{A}h=0

being a chain homomorphism. The induced homology map is denoted by

.

D
is a
boundaryless
differential group.

Then,
h\colon D\to B(A)

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

(6)

commutes, i.e.
h={\mathrm{d}}_{A}l
.

Proof

By the assumption that
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}h=0
we have that
h\colon D\to Z(A)
. Observe the short exact sequence for homology

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H(A)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}

Remark 5

For the action of differentials we omit parentheses and for other homomorphisms we adopt the usual functional notation, i.e.
{\mathrm{d}}c
and
h(c)
. Parentheses are also omitted in compositions of maps.

Remark 6

In the remainder of the paper
C\oplus C^{\prime}
(and
A\oplus A^{\prime}
) denotes the Cartesian product as direct sum with specifically constructed differentials to emphasize that this is not the product of differential groups with the product differential but a product group with a chosen differential.

Remark 7

If
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is a chain complex and
h_{k}\colon D\to A_{k}

h_{k}\colon D\to B_{k}(A)

be an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential group and let
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
be the associated Cartan-Eilenberg system.
We say that the latter is free and finitely generated if the groups
E_{\alpha}^{\beta}
are free and finitely generated for each pair
(\alpha,\beta)
with
\beta\smallsetminus\alpha=\{p\}
for all
p\in{\mathsf{P}}
.
Define a free, finitely generated
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded abelian group
A
with the decomposition

A=\bigoplus_{p\in{\mathsf{P}}}G_{p}A,\quad G_{p}A\cong E_{\alpha}^{\beta},~~~\beta\smallsetminus\alpha=\{p\}.

(53)

An appropriate differential is constructed by the following theorem:

Theorem 6.2
(cf.
[
3
]
, Thm. 4.8)

Let
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
be an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential group with the property that
the associated Cartan-Eilenberg system
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
is free and finitely generated.
Let
A
be defined as in (
53
). Then, there exists an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential

{\mathrm{d}}_{A}\colon A\to A
such that

\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D}).

In particular,
there exists
an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered quasi-isomorphism
13
13

13

A quasi-isomorphism is a chain homomorphism that is an isomorphism on homology.

f\colon(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\to(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
,
such that the isomorphisms
H(f)\colon E_{\alpha}^{\beta}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\to E_{\alpha}^{\beta}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
, for all
\alpha\subseteq\beta
, define an isomorphism of Cartan-Eilenberg systems.

In
[
3
]
the strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential group
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is called a
connection matrix
for
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.
Note that
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}
restricted to the groups
G_{p}A
is the zero map
and therefore
H(G_{p}A)=G_{p}A
.
The differential may be regarded as a strict, upper-triangular matrix on
A
. In dynamical systems theory the non-trivial entries of
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}
contain information about heterclinic orbits between Morse sets.
In
[
3
]
the notion of connection matrix is defined in a slightly more general context. If we translate this to Cartan-Eilenberg systems we obtain:

Definition 4
(
[
3
]
, Defn. 3.6.B)

Let
E
be a Cartan-Eilenberg system over
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
. A strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential group
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
as defined (
53
), such that

\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong\text{\bf E}
is called a connection matrix for
E
.

Remark 21

The above theorem is a translation of the more general result in
(
3
, Thm. 4.8)
to the case of Cartan-Eilenberg systems generated by filtered differential groups. Existence of connection matrices is only established in the setting of
(
3
, Thm. 4.8)
, i.e. for Cartan-Eilenberg systems
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
in which case a connection matrix is quasi-isomorphic to
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.

7
Discussion

The main result of this paper in Theorem
5.1
has various implications for the theory of connection matrices as used in dynamical systems theory. In this section we discuss the applications and resolutions of the conjectures by Robbin and Salamon, as well as the implication for the transition matrices by Franzosa and Mischaikow, cf.
[
4
]
.

7.1
The conjectures by J. Robbin and D. Salamon

In their seminal paper on dynamical systems and connection matrices, cf.
[
11
]
, Robbin and Salamon formulate a number of conjectures concerning non-uniqueness of connection matrices.
Theorem
5.1
is posed as Conjecture 8.5 in
[
11
]

in the setting of flows. Theorem
5.1
extends the original conjecture by replacing field coefficients by ring coefficients (
R
is a principal ideal domain).
Theorem
5.1
resolves non-uniqueness issues in Franzosa’s connection matrix theory (see
(
3
, Section 6.3)
): all connection matrices are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphic, i.e.
all connection matrices according to Definition
4
are conjugated via
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphisms, which
generalizes the main result in
(
4
, Theorem 3.5)
.

In
(
11
, Thm. 8.1)
it is proven
that an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})

is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent to a strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
, such that

\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
.
In this setting our main result solves the following conjecture:

Corollary 1
(cf.
[
11
]
, Conj. 7.4)

Let
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
be
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector spaces over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
. Then,
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
if and only if
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent, i.e.
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\simeq(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
.

Proof

If
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-chain equivalent then the equivalence
\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
is immediate.
For vector spaces
the existence theorems in

[
11
,
5
]
show that there exists strict,

{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector spaces
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
and
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
, and chain equivalences

(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\simeq(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\simeq(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
, which implies that

\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})

and
\text{\bf E}(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})\cong\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
. By assumption,

\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
and thus

\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
. Consequently,
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-chain isomorphic via Theorem
5.1
, and therefore,
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\simeq(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\simeq(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
,
which proves
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\simeq(D^{\prime},{\mathrm{d}}_{D^{\prime}})
, i.e. they are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-chain equivalent.
∎

Remark 22

Conjecture 8.4 in
[
11
]
is false in the setting of connection matrices since in the case of ring coefficients the homologies
E^{\beta}_{\alpha}
,
\beta\smallsetminus\alpha=\{p\}
are not necessarily free, or projective, cf.
(
3
, Exam. 6.3)
. Therefore a connection matrix is not an option.
This problem is a subject of further study.

7.2
Connection matrix theories for vector spaces

In
[
5
]
the theory of connection matrices for field coefficients is treated in the setting of homotopy categories without using homology.
For an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
, or equivalently, a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space consider the decomposition

D=\bigoplus_{p\in{\mathsf{P}}}G_{p}D,\quad\alpha\mapsto F_{\alpha}D=\bigoplus_{p\in\alpha}G_{p}D,\quad\alpha\in{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}}).

In
(
5
, Defn. 4.23)
, a strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is a connection matrix for
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
if
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent to
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.
An algorithm is proven in
[
5
]
to construct a
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
given a
finite dimensional
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.
This procedure is reminiscent of the inductive existence result in
(
11
, Thm. 8.1)
.
In
(
5
, Prop. 4.27)
it is proven that connection matrices defined in the sense of

(
5
, Defn. 4.23)
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphic, which covers a special case of Theorem
5.1
.

In Section
6
we have summarized the connection matrix theory by Franzosa. Consider the
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.
In Franzosa’s theory a strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is a connection matrix for an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
if

\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
, cf. Defn.
4
.
Existence of a connection matrix is given by
(
3
, Thm. 4.8)
.
The latter is not assumed to be a chain equivalence. Note that Franzosa’s notion of connection matrix not even requires a map at the chain level.
The following result concerns all possible connection matrices according to Definition
4
.

Theorem 7.1

Let
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
be an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector space.
Then, every strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
which satisfies

\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\cong\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D}),

is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent to
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
, i.e.
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})\simeq(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
.

Proof

The
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded differential vector space
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is also
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered.
The
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered differential vector spaces
(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
satisfy

\text{\bf E}(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})\cong\text{\bf E}(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
. By Corollary
1
this implies that

(D,{\mathrm{d}}_{D})
and
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain equivalent, which proves the theorem.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
7.1
the connection matrix theories of
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
5
]
in the setting of (finite dimensional) differential vector spaces are equivalent. The advantage of the latter theory is that the existence theory is based on an effective algorithm for producing connection matrices.

7.3
Morse-Smale gradings and unique connection matrices

Suppose
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is a chain complex, in which case the chain homomorphisms are given by
f_{k}\colon C_{k}\to C_{k}
, i.e.
f_{k}(F_{\alpha}C_{k})\subseteq F_{\alpha}C_{k}
, for all
\alpha\in{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
and for all
k
, cf. Ex.
1
and Rmk.
15
.
For simplicity of exposition we consider

{\mathsf{P}}
-graded chain complexes in the category of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-vector spaces, where both gradings satisfy a natural order-preserving property.

Definition 5

A strict,
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded chain complex
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is referred to as an
algebraic Morse-Smale graded chain complex

if there exists a map
\mu\colon{\mathsf{P}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(i)

p<q
implies
\mu(p)<\mu(q)
;

(ii)

G_{p}C_{k}=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}&\text{if }k=\mu(p);\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The pair
({\mathsf{P}},\mu)
is called a
Morse-Smale grading

for
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
.

By definition algebraic Morse-Smale graded chain complexes are finitely generated and
C={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{|{\mathsf{P}}|}
, where
|{\mathsf{P}}|
is the number of elements in
{\mathsf{P}}
. As a consequence of Condition (i) we have that

\mu(p)=\mu(q),~~p\neq q\quad\implies\quad p~\|~q.

(54)

The Morse-Smale grading restricts the differentials on
C
.

Theorem 7.2

Let
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
and
(C,{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C})
be algebraic Morse-Smale graded chain complexes with Morse-Smale grading
({\mathsf{P}},\mu)
for both chain complexes.
Then,
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}={\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C}
if and only if
\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\cong\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C})
.

Proof

By assumption both chain complexes have the same Morse-Smale grading with associated choice of basis.
Theorem
5.1
yields the existence of an
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain automorphism
f\colon C\to C
given by
f_{k}\colon C_{k}\to C_{k}
, which are
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered for all
k
. By the
{\mathsf{P}}
-grading each
f_{k}
may be regarded as matrix with entries

f_{k}^{pq}\colon G_{q}C_{k}\to G_{p}C_{k}
, for all
\mu(p)=\mu(q)=k
.
The filtering condition on
f_{k}
translates to:
f_{k}^{pq}\neq 0
implies
p\leq q
.
Consequently, by (
54
), and thus Definition
5
(i),
f_{k}^{pq}=0
for all

p\neq q
and
f_{k}^{pp}
are automorphisms. By Definition
5
(ii) the factors are given by
G_{p}C_{k}={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
and therefore
f_{k}^{pp}=\mathrm{id}
for all
p
with
\mu(p)=k
since the only group automorphism on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is the identify map. We conclude that
f=\mathrm{id}
and thus
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}={\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C}
.
∎

The term Morse-Smale graded chain complex is motivated by Morse-Smale flows in dynamical systems. In
[
10
]
J. Reineck proves that connection matrices for Morse-Smale flows without periodic orbits are unique. Via Conley index theory such flows yield a Morse-Smale graded chain complex where the groups
G_{p}C_{k}
record the critical points of index
k
, cf.
[
2
]
,
[
12
]
. Theorem
7.2
provides an algebraic proof of Reineck’s result.

Remark 23

The proof of Theorem
7.2
can be repeated for chain complexes of
R
-modules. For example for chain complexes of abelian groups (
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules) the result stays the same except
for the group automorphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}
. The only options are
\pm\mathrm{id}
.
This implies that the entries in
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
and
{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C}
may defer by a
\pm
sign. This is exactly the choice of the orientation of the basis for the non-trivial entries of
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
.
We conclude that the entries of the differentials
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
and
{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C}
are the same up to conjugacy, which is a statement of uniqueness.

Remark 24

Theorem
7.2
can be proved in a more general context when
(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})
is an algebraic Morse-Smale graded chain complex and
(A,{\mathrm{d}}_{A})
is a free, finitely generated, strict
{\mathsf{P}}
-graded chain complex of
R
-modules. Under the condition that
\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}_{C})\cong\text{\bf E}(C,{\mathrm{d}}^{\prime}_{C})
, the modules
G_{p}C_{k}
and
G_{p}A_{k}
are isomorphic and
and the entries in
{\mathrm{d}}_{C}
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{A}
are related by conjugation. This is the general statement that there exists only one strict differential to represent
E
in the Morse-Smale setting.

Remark 25

The condition that the
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered chain isomorphisms are also homogeneous with respect to the chain complex grading is crucial for Theorem
7.2
.
If the latter is not satisfied, i.e.
f
is
{\mathsf{O}}({\mathsf{P}})
-filtered, but necessarily homogeneous,
may yield multiple similar differentials.

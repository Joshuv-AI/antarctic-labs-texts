1.
Introduction

Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal in a polynomial ring
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Brodmann
[
Br
]
proved that the depth function of powers
k\to\depth S/I^{k}
is convergent. Ha, Nguyen, Trung, and Trung
[
HNTT
]
proved that the depth function of powers of monomial ideals could be any non-negative integer-valued convergent function. On the other hand, when restricting to edge ideals of graphs, one expects that the depth function of their powers is non-increasing. This phenomenon has been verified for several classes of graphs (see
[
HH
,
KTY
,
Mo
]
).

Let us now recall the notion of the edge ideals of graphs. Let
G
be a simple graph on
n
vertices. The edge ideal of
G
, denoted
I(G)
is the squarefree monomial ideal generated by
x_{i}x_{j}
where
\{i,j\}
is an edge of
G
. For a homogeneous ideal
I
, we denote
\dstab(I)
the
index of depth stability
of
I
, i.e., the smallest number
k
such that
\depth S/I^{\ell}=\lim_{i\to\infty}\depth S/I^{i}
for all
\ell\geq k
. In a fundamental paper
[
T
]
, the second author found a combinatorial formula for
\dstab(I(G))
for large classes of graphs including unicyclic graphs. In particular, when
G
is a tree,
\dstab(I(G))=n-\epsilon_{0}(G)
where
\epsilon_{0}(G)
is the number of leaves of
G
; when
G
is a cycle of length
n\geq 5
,
\dstab(I(G))=\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil
. Though we know the limit depth and its index of depth stability, intermediate values for depth of powers of edge ideals were unknown even for cycles. The depth of powers of edge ideals of paths was only given recently by Balanescu and Cimpoeas
[
BC1
]
. For general graphs, Seyed Fakhari
[
SF
]
gave a sharp lower bound for the depth of the second power of their edge ideals. In this paper, we compute the depth of powers of edge ideals of cycles.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
I(C_{n})
be the edge ideal of a cycle of length
n\geq 5
. Then

\depth S/I(C_{n})^{t}=\begin{cases}\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil&\text{ if }t=1\\
\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil&\text{ if }2\leq t<\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil\\
1&\text{ if }n\text{ is even and }t\geq n/2+1\\
0&\text{ if }n\text{ is odd and }t\geq(n+1)/2.\end{cases}

In particular, the depth function of powers of cycles makes a big drop just before it stabilizes. The initial value and limiting values were well-known (see
[
Mo
,
BC2
,
T
]
), so our contribution is the computation of the intermediate values
\depth S/I(C_{n})^{t}
for
2\leq t<\lceil(n+1)/2\rceil
. We now outline the ideas to carry out this computation. For simplicity of notation, we set
I=I(C_{n})
and
e_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
,
e_{n}=x_{1}x_{n}
. Denote

\varphi(n,t)=\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil.

(1)

First, we show that
\depth S/I^{t}\leq\depth(S/(I^{t}:e_{2}\cdots e_{t}))\leq\varphi(n,t)
.

(2)

Let
f=x_{1}\cdots x_{2t-2}
. By
[
CHHKTT
, Theorem 4.3]
, we need to show that
\depth(S/(I^{t}:f))\geq\varphi(n,t)
and
\depth(S/(I^{t},f))\geq\varphi(n,t)
. For the first term, we use induction on
t
as
I^{t}:f
is well-understood. For the second term, we note that
(I^{t},f)=(I^{t},x_{1}x_{2})\cap(I^{t},x_{3}\cdots x_{2t-2})
. By repeated use of the depth lemma along a short exact sequence, we reduce to proving that
\depth(S/(I^{t}+I(H)))\geq\varphi(n,t)
for all non-zero subgraph
H
of
C_{n}
. We accomplish that by induction on
t
and downward induction on the size of
H
.

To compute
\depth S/I(G)^{t}
for general graph
G
, by the result of Nguyen and the last author
[
NV2
, Theorem 1.1]
, we may assume that
G
is connected. We also note that the regularity of powers of edge ideals of graphs has been known for many classes of graphs (see
[
MV
]
for a recent survey on the topic). This is partly due to the fact that the regularity of powers of these edge ideals behaves nicely. On the other hand, the depth of powers of edge ideals was much more subtle as the drop at each step could be arbitrary, even for trees.

Example 1.2
.

Let
G
be a caterpillar tree based on a path of length
2
on
3
vertices
1,\ldots,3
. Assume that
d_{i}
is the number of leaves at
i
. By Proposition
4.14
, we have

\depth S/I(G)^{t}=\begin{cases}\min(d_{1}+d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2)&\text{ if }t=1\\
\min(d_{1}+1,d_{2}+2,d_{3}+1)&\text{ if }t=2\\
1&\text{ if }t\geq 3.\end{cases}

Hence, if we choose
d_{2}<d_{1},d_{3}
then
\depth S/I=\depth S/I^{2}=d_{2}+2
but then drop to
1
for the next powers. If
d_{1}+d_{3}<d_{2}
, the drop at the first step could be arbitrary.

We will now describe the formula for depth of powers of edge ideals of starlike trees. We first introduce some notation. Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Let
\alpha_{i}
be the number of
a_{j}
such that
a_{j}=i\pmod{3}
. We define
g:\NN^{k}\to\NN
by

g(\mathbf{a})=\begin{cases}&\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil\text{ if }\alpha_{1}=0\text{ and }\alpha_{2}\neq 0\\
&1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}.

We may further assume that
a_{j}=2\pmod{3}
for
j=1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}
,
a_{j}=0\pmod{3}
for
j=\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{0}
and
a_{j}=1\pmod{3}
for
j=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,k
. Let
\beta_{1}=\min(\alpha_{2},t-1)
,
\beta_{2}=\min(\alpha_{0},\lfloor\frac{\max(0,t-1-\alpha_{2})}{2}\rfloor)
and
\beta_{3}=\lfloor\frac{\max(0,t-1-\beta_{1}-2\beta_{2})}{3}\rfloor
. We define
\mathbf{b}\in\NN^{k}
as follows.

b_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}-1&\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,\beta_{1},\\
a_{i}-2&\text{ for }i=\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}.\end{cases}

With this notation, we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Denote
T_{\mathbf{a}}
the starlike trees obtained by joining paths of length
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
at the root
1
. Then for all
t
such that
1\leq t\leq|\mathbf{a}|-k=s
,

\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}=g(\mathbf{b})-\beta_{3}.

Example 1.4
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(3,3,5)
. Then
\alpha_{0}=2
,
\alpha_{1}=0
and
\alpha_{2}=1
. By Theorem
1.3
, we see that the sequence
\{\depth(S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t})\mid 1\leq t\leq 9\}
is
\{4,4,4,3,3,2,2,2,1\}
.

Thus, in general, one cannot expect a simple formula for the depth of powers of the edge ideal of a tree in terms of its combinatorial invariants. We also note that starlike trees are natural generalizations of paths, as the join of two paths of length
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
is a path of length
a_{1}+a_{2}+1
.

We structure the paper as follows. In Section
2
, we set up the notation and provide some background. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.3
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some definitions and properties concerning the depth of monomial ideals and edge ideals of graphs. The interested readers are referred to
[
BH
,
D
]
for more details.

Throughout the paper, we denote
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathrm{k}
. Let
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be the maximal homogeneous ideal of
S
.

2.1.
Depth

For a finitely generated graded
S
-module
L
, the depth of
L
is defined to be

\depth(L)=\min\{i\mid H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}(L)\neq 0\},

where
H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(L)
denotes the
i
-th local cohomology module of
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
. We have the following estimates on depth along a short exact sequence (see
[
BH
, Proposition 1.2.9]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated graded
S
-modules. Then

(1)

\depth M\geq\min(\depth L,\depth N),

(2)

\depth L\geq\min(\depth M,\depth N+1).

We make repeated use of the following two results in the sequence. The first one is
[
R
, Corollary 1.3]
. The second one is
[
CHHKTT
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
f
a monomial such that
f\notin I
. Then

\depth S/I\leq\depth S/(I:f)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal and
f
a monomial. Then

\depth S/I\in\{\depth(S/I:f),\depth(S/(I,f))\}.

We also use the following result about the depth of powers of sums of ideals
[
NV2
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
I
and
J
be monomial ideals in standard graded polynomial rings
R
and
S
over a field
\mathrm{k}
. Denote
P=I+J
the sum of ideals in
T=R\otimes_{\mathrm{k}}S
. Then
\depth T/P^{s}
equals

\min_{i\in[1,s-1],j\in[1,s]}\{\depth R/I^{s-i}+\depth S/J^{i}+1,\depth R/I^{s-j+1}+\depth S/J^{j}\}.

2.2.
Graphs and their edge ideals

Let
G
denote a finite simple graph over the vertex set
V(G)=[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and the edge set
E(G)
. For a vertex
x\in V(G)
, let the neighbours of
x
be the subset
N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G)\mid\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
, and set
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
.

The edge ideal of
G
is defined to be

I(G)=(x_{i}x_{j}~|~\{i,j\}\in E(G))\subseteq S.

For simplicity, we often write
i\in G
(resp.
ij\in G
) instead of
i\in V(G)
(resp.
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
). By abuse of notation, we also call
x_{i}x_{j}\in I(G)
an edge of
G
.

We now recall several classes of graphs that we study in this work. A tree is a connected acyclic graph. A path
P_{n}
of length
n-1
is the graph on
[n]
whose edges are
\{i,i+1\}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. A starlike tree consists of several paths attached to a root.

A cycle
C_{n}
of length
n\geq 3
is the graph on
[n]
whose edges are
\{i,i+1\}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
\{1,n\}
. A graph
G
is called weakly chordal if
G
and its complement do not contain an induced cycle of length
\geq 5
.

We will use the following result
[
Mo
, Lemma 2.10]
later.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
G
is a graph,
x
is a leaf of
G
and
y
is the unique neighbour of
x
. Then for any
t\geq 2
,

I(G)^{t}:(xy)=I(G)^{t-1}.

Furthermore, the depth of
I(P_{n})
and
I(C_{n})
are well known
[
Mo
,
BC2
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

We have

(1)

\depth S/I(P_{n})=\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil
for
n\geq 2
,

(2)

\depth S/I(C_{n})=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil
for
n\geq 3
.

2.3.
Even-connection and colon of edge ideals

Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of a graph
G
. When
f=e_{1}\cdots e_{t-1}
is a product of edges of
G
, Banerjee
[
B
]
showed that the colon ideal
I^{t}:f
is generated by quadratic monomials
uv
such that
u
and
v
are even-connected via
f
. We now describe this result and an application that will be useful later.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that
u
and
v
are
f
-even connected if there exists vertices
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k}
such that
ux_{1},x_{i}x_{i+1}
and
x_{2k}v
are edges of
G
and
x_{1}x_{2},x_{3}x_{4},\ldots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k}
are among
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{t-1}\}
and
\prod_{j=1}^{2k}x_{j}
divides
f
.

The following is
[
B
, Theorem 6.7]
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
I=I(G)
be the edge ideal of a graph
G
and
f=e_{1}\cdots e_{t-1}
be a product of edges of
G
. Then
I^{t}:f
is generated by quadratic monomials
uv
such that
u
and
v
are
f
-even connected.

Using this, we prove

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f=e_{1}\cdots e_{t}
where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
are distinct edges of
G
. Assume that the induced subgraph of
G
on
N[\supp f]=\cup_{i=1}^{t}N[\supp e_{i}]
does not contain an odd cycle. For each
s
, denote
J_{s}=I^{s}:(e_{1}\cdots e_{s-1})
. Then

J_{t+1}=J_{t}:u\cap J_{t}:v

where
e_{t}=uv
.

Proof.

First, we prove that the left hand side is contained in the right hand side. Assume that
wz\in J_{t+1}
. If
wz\in I
, then clearly
wz\in J_{t}\subseteq J_{t}:u\cap J_{t}:v
. Thus, we may assume that
wz\notin I
. By Theorem
2.8
there exists
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k}
such that
wx_{1},x_{i}x_{i+1},x_{2k}z
are edges of
G
and
x_{2i+1}x_{2i+2}
are among
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
. If
e_{t}
does not appears among the edges
\{x_{1}x_{2},x_{3}x_{4},\ldots,x_{2k-1}x_{2k}\}
then
wz\in J_{t}\subseteq J_{t}:u\cap J_{t}:v
. Thus, we may assume that
x_{2i-1}=u
and
x_{2i}=v
. Hence, by Theorem
2.8
,
wu
and
vz
are in
J_{t}
. Hence
wz\in J_{t}:u\cap J_{t}:v
.

It remains to prove that
g\in J_{t}:u\cap J_{t}:v
then
g\in J_{t+1}
. First, note that
J_{t}:u=J_{t}+(U)
and
J_{t}:v=J_{s}+(V)
where
U
and
V
consisting of variables that are even-connected to
u
and
v
respectively. Hence,
f\in J_{s}+(U)\cap(V)
. First, we prove that
U\cap V=\emptyset
. Indeed, if
w\in U\cap V
, then
w
is even-connected to
u
and
v
. Hence, the walks from
w
to
u
and
w
to
v
form an closed odd walks, a contradiction to the assumption. Now, if
f\in J_{t}
then clearly
f\in J_{t+1}
. Thus, we may assume that
f=wz
with
w\in U
and
z\in V
. If either
wu
or
zv
belongs to
I
, then it is clear that
wz\in J_{s+1}
. Thus, we may assume that there exists even walks
wx_{1}\ldots x_{2k}u
and
vy_{1},\ldots,y_{2l}z
. If
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k}\}\cap\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{2l}\}=\emptyset
then
wz
are
f
-evenly connected by definition. If
x_{2i}=y_{2j+1}
then the walk
x_{2i}\ldots x_{2k}uvy_{1}\ldots y_{2j+1}
is a closed odd walk. Hence, the restriction of
G
on
N[\supp f]
contains an odd cycle, a contradiction. Thus, we must have
x_{2i}=y_{2j}
for some
i
and
j
. Now along the even walk
wx_{1}\ldots x_{2k}uvy_{1}\ldots y_{2l}z
if
x_{2i}=y_{2j}
then we shorten the walk by omitting the middle path from
x_{2i}
to
y_{2j}
and still have an even walk. We can repeat this until there is no further repetition of the vertices on the walk. The conclusion follows.
∎

2.4.
Projective dimension of edge ideals of weakly chordal graphs

We note that the colon ideals of powers of edge ideals of trees by products of edges are edge ideals of weakly chordal graphs. Their projective dimension can be computed via the notion of strongly disjoint families of complete bipartite subgraphs, introduced by Kimura
[
K
]
. For a graph G, we consider all families of (non-induced) subgraphs

B_{1},\ldots,B_{g}
of
G
such that

(1)

each
B_{i}
is a complete bipartite graph for
1\leq i\leq g
,

(2)

the graphs
B_{1},\ldots,B_{g}
have pairwise disjoint vertex sets,

(3)

there exist an induced matching
e_{1},\ldots,e_{g}
of
G
for each
e_{i}\in E(B_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq g
.

Such a family is termed a strongly disjoint family of complete bipartite subgraphs. We define

d(G)=\max(\sum_{1}^{g}|V(B_{i})|-g),

where the maximum is taken over all the strongly disjoint families of complete bipartite subgraphs
B_{1},\ldots,B_{g}
of
G
. We have the following
[
NV1
, Theorem 7.7]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
G
be a weakly chordal graph with at least one edge. Then

\pd(S/I(G))=d(G).

3.
Depth functions of powers of cycles

In this section, we compute the depth of powers of paths and cycles. For a real number
a
, denote
\lceil a\rceil
the least integer at least
a
,
\lfloor a\rfloor
the largest integer at most
a
. First, we have a simple lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
a,b
be integers. Then

\lceil\frac{a}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{b}{3}\rceil\geq\lceil\frac{a+b}{3}\rceil.

Proof.

There exist unique integers
k
,
a_{1}
,
l,b_{1}
such that
a=3k+a_{1}
and
b=3l+b_{1}
with
1\leq a_{1},b_{1}\leq 3
. By definition,
\lceil a/3\rceil=k+1
and
\lceil b/3\rceil=l+1
. Since
a_{1},b_{1}\leq 3
,
a_{1}+b_{1}\leq 6
, hence
2\geq\lceil(a_{1}+b_{1})/3\rceil
. The conclusion follows.
∎

We fix the following notation throughout the rest of the paper. For each
n
,
P_{n}
denotes a path of length
n-1
and
C_{n}
denotes a cycle of length
n
. We denote
e_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
e_{n}=x_{1}x_{n}
. We define
\varphi(n,t)=\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil
.

The following lemma is a crucial step in computing the depth of powers of edge ideals of paths and cycles.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be any subgraph of
P_{n}
. Then for any
t<n

\depth(S/(I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)))\geq\varphi(n,t).

Proof.

We will prove the statement by downward induction on the size of
H
and induction on
t
. If
|H|=n-1
or
t=1
, then
I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)=I(P_{n})
. By Lemma
2.6
, we have
\depth S/I(P_{n})=\lceil n/3\rceil\geq\varphi(n,t)
. Furthermore,
\sqrt{I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)}=I(P_{n})
. In particular,
\mathfrak{m}
is not an associated prime of
I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)
for any
t
and
H
. Hence, if
n\leq 4
and
t\geq 2
then
\depth(S/(I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)))\geq 1\geq\varphi(n,t)
. Thus, we may assume that
n\geq 5
and
t\geq 2
.

For each
j=1,\ldots,n-1
, denote
e_{j}
the edge
x_{j}x_{j+1}
. Now assume that
i
is the smallest index such that
e_{i}\notin H
, i.e.,
e_{1},\ldots,e_{i-1}\in H
. Let
J=I(P_{n})^{t}+I(H)=I(P_{n-i+1})^{t}+I(H)
, where
P_{n-i+1}
is the path consisting of the edges
e_{i},\ldots,e_{n-1}
. By Lemma
2.3
,

\depth(S/J)\in\{\depth(S/J:e_{i}),\depth(S/(J,e_{i})\}

By induction, it suffices to prove that

(3.1)

\depth(S/J:e_{i})\geq\varphi(n,t).

By Lemma
2.5
,

(3.2)

J:e_{i}=I(P_{n-i+1})^{t-1}+(I(H):e_{i}).

There are two cases as follows.

Case 1.

i=1
. There are two subcases:

Case 1.a.

e_{2}\in H
. Equation (
3.2
) becomes

(3.3)

J:e_{1}=(e_{1}+P_{n-3})^{t-1}+(x_{3})+I(H^{\prime}),

where
P_{n-3}
is the path from
x_{4}
to
x_{n}
and
H^{\prime}
is the induced subgraph of
H
on
\supp H\setminus[3]
. For each
\ell\geq 1
, denote
K_{\ell}=(e_{1}+P_{n-3})^{\ell}+(x_{3})+I(H^{\prime})
. In particular,
J:e_{1}=K_{t-1}
. Since
x_{1},x_{2}
does not appears in
P_{n-3}
nor
I(H^{\prime})
. We have,

(3.4)

K_{\ell+1}:e_{1}=K_{\ell}.

Applying Lemma
2.3
with
f=e_{1}
, we have

(3.5)

\depth(S/K_{\ell+1})\in\{\depth(S/K_{\ell}),\depth(S/(K_{\ell+1}+e_{1})).

Hence,

(3.6)

\depth(S/J:e_{1})=\depth(S/K_{t-1})\in\{\depth(S/(K_{\ell}+e_{1}))\mid\ell=1,\ldots,t-1\}.

Since
K_{\ell}+e_{1}=P_{n-3}^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime})+e_{1}+(x_{3})
, to establish (
3.1
) it remains to prove that

\depth S/(P_{n-3}^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime})+e_{1}+(x_{3}))\geq\varphi(n,t),

for all
\ell=1,\ldots,t-1
. Since
\supp P_{n-3}\cap\supp e_{1}=\emptyset
, by Theorem
2.4

\depth S/(P_{n-3}^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime})+e_{1}+(x_{3}))=\depth R/(P_{n-3}^{t-1}+I(H^{\prime}))+1,

where
R=\mathrm{k}[x_{4},\ldots,x_{n}]
. By induction, we deduce that

\depth S/(P_{n-3}^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime})+e_{1}+(x_{3}))\geq\varphi(n-3,\ell)+1=\lceil\frac{n-\ell+1}{3}\rceil\geq\varphi(n,t),

for all
\ell=1,\ldots,t-1
. The conclusion follows.

Case 1.b.

e_{2}\notin H
. Equation (
3.2
) becomes
J:e_{1}=I(P_{n})^{t-1}+I(H)
. The conclusion follows from induction on
t
.

Case 2.

i>1
. By definition
e_{1},\ldots,e_{i-1}\in H
. There are two cases as before:

Case 2.a.

e_{i+1}\in H
. Equation (
3.2
) becomes

(3.7)

J:e_{i}=(e_{i}+P_{n-i-2})^{t-1}+(x_{i-1},x_{i+2})+I(P_{i-2})+I(H^{\prime}),

with
P_{n-i-2}
is the path

,
P_{i-2}
is the path
1,\ldots,i-2
and
H^{\prime}
is the induced subgraph of
H
on
\supp H\setminus\{1,\ldots,i-1,i+2\}.
With an argument similar to Case 1.a, by repeated use of Lemma
2.3
it suffices to prove that

\depth S/(P_{n-i-2}^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime})+e_{i}+I(P_{i-2})+(x_{i-1},x_{i+2}))\geq\varphi(n,t),

for all
\ell=1,\ldots,t-1
. Note that
P_{i-2}
,
e_{i}
, and

have support on a different set of variables. By induction, Lemma
2.6
, and Theorem
2.4
, the left hand side is at least

\varphi(n-i-2,\ell)+1+\lceil\frac{i-2}{3}\rceil=\lceil\frac{n-i-\ell-1}{3}\rceil+1+\lceil\frac{i-2}{3}\rceil\geq\varphi(n,t).

Case 2.b.

e_{i+1}\notin H
. Equation (
3.2
) becomes

(3.8)

J:e_{i}=I(P_{n-i+1})^{t-1}+(x_{i-1})+I(P_{i-2})+I(H^{\prime}),

with
P_{n-i+1}
is the path
i,\ldots,n
,
P_{i-2}
is the path
1,\ldots,i-2
and
H^{\prime}
is the induced subgraph of
H
on
\supp H\setminus\{1,\ldots,i-1\}.
By induction and Theorem
2.4
, we have

\depth(S/J:e_{i})\geq\varphi(n-i+1,t-1)+\lceil\frac{i-2}{3}\rceil=\lceil\frac{n-i-t+3}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{i-2}{3}\rceil\geq\varphi(n,t).

The conclusion follows.
∎

To obtain an upper bound for
\depth S/I(P_{n})^{t}
, we prove

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I=(x_{1}x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}x_{n})
be the edge ideal of a path of length
n-1
on
n
vertices. Denote
e_{i}=x_{i}x_{i+1}
for all
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. Then for all
t\leq n-2
, we have

\depth(S/(I^{t}:(e_{2}\ldots e_{t})))=\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
, we see that
I^{t}:(e_{2}\ldots e_{t})
is the edge ideal of the following graph

G_{n,t}=P_{n}\cup\{x_{i}x_{j}\mid i<j\leq t+2\text{ is of different parity}\}.

We will prove by induction on
n
and
n-t
that

\depth(S/I(G_{n,t}))=\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil.

If
t=n-2
, then
G_{n,t}
is a complete bipartite graph, hence
\depth(S/I(G_{n,t}))=1
. Thus, we may assume that
t\leq n-3
. Hence,

I(G_{n,t})=I(G_{n-1,t})+e_{n-1}
. Furthermore, this decomposition is a Betti splitting by
[
NV1
, Corollary 4.12]
. Since
I(G_{n-1,t})\cap e_{n-1}=e_{n-1}(x_{n-2}+I(G_{n-3,t}))
, by
[
NV1
, Corollary 4.8]
and induction we have

\displaystyle\pd(S/I(G_{n,t}))

\displaystyle=\max(\pd(S/I(G_{n-1,t})),1,\pd(S/I(G_{n-3,t}))+1)

\displaystyle=\max(n-1-\varphi(n-1,t),1,n-\varphi(n-3,t)-1)=n-\varphi(n,t).

The conclusion follows from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I(P_{n})
be the edge ideal of a path of length
n-1
. Then

\depth(S/I^{t})=\max(\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil,1),

for all

.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
and
[
T
]
, we may assume that

. By Lemma
3.2
, take

be the empty graph, we deduce that

. The conclusion then follows from Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Note that for any integer

,
\lceil\frac{n}{3}\rceil=n+1-\lfloor\frac{n+1}{3}\rfloor-\lceil\frac{n+1}{3}\rceil
. In particular, Theorem

is a special case of
[

, Theorem 1]
. We include a simple argument here because Lemma

will be critical to deduce the formula for depth of powers of edge ideals of cycles.

We will now turn to the edge ideals of cycles
C_{n}
. The depth of powers of

in the case

and

are clear. Thus, we may assume that

. By
[
T
]
, we know that
\dstab(I(C_{n}))=\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil.
Thus, we may assume that
2\leq t<\lceil\frac{n+1}{2}\rceil.
First, we note that
f=x_{1}\cdots x_{2t-2}
is a product of distinct variables. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

(3.9)

\depth S/I(C_{n})^{t}\in\{\depth(S/I(C_{n})^{t}:f),\depth(S/(I(C_{n})^{t},f))\}.

Hence, to establish the lower bound for

and

Assume that

and

\depth(S/I(C_{n})^{t}:(x_{1}\cdots x_{2t-2}))\geq\varphi(n,t).

Proof.

For each

, let

2.9
,

(3.10)

J_{t+1}=(J_{t}:x_{2t-1})\cap(J_{t}:x_{2t}).

Note that
\depth(S/J_{1})=\depth S/I(C_{n})=\lceil\frac{n-1}{3}\rceil=\varphi(n,2)
. First, consider the base case

. Note that

, where
P_{n-4}
is the path from
4
to
n-1
. Hence,

(3.11)

\depth(S/(I:x_{1}+I:x_{2}))=\lceil\frac{n-4}{3}\rceil=\varphi(n,2)-1.

By Lemma
2.1
,

(3.12)

\depth(S/J_{2})\geq\min\{\depth S/J_{1},\depth(S/(I:x_{1}+I:x_{2})+1)\}=\varphi(n,2).

Now, consider the induction step. We have

(3.13)

J_{t}:x_{2t-1}+J_{t}:x_{2t}=(x_{n},x_{2},x_{4},\ldots,x_{2t-4},x_{2t-2},x_{2t-1},x_{2t},x_{2t+1})+I(P_{n-2t-2})

to

are variables that do not appear in

. Hence,

(3.14)

\depth(S/(J_{t}:x_{2t-1})+(J_{t}:x_{2t}))=t-1+\lceil\frac{n-2t-2}{3}\rceil\geq\varphi(n,t+1)-1.

Together with equation (
3.10
), Lemma
2.1
, Lemma

, and induction, we have

\displaystyle\depth(S/J_{t+1})

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\varphi(n,t),\depth S/(J_{t}:x_{2t-1}+J_{t}:x_{2t})+1\}

\displaystyle\geq\varphi(n,t+1).

The conclusion follows.
∎

The second inequality is established in the following

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume that
t\geq 2
and
f=x_{1}\cdots x_{2t-2}
. Then
\depth(S/(I^{t},f))\geq\varphi(n,t)
.

Proof.

For each

, denote

. Then

and

. In other words, for any subgraph
H
of

consisting of edges which are subsets of

, we have

(3.15)

I^{t}+I(H)+(f_{j})=(I^{t}+I(H)+(e_{2j-1}))\cap(I^{t}+I(H)+(f_{j+1})).

The conclusion follows from Lemma
2.1
and the following Lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
H
be a non-empty subgraph of
G
. Then for

,

\depth(S/(I(C_{n})^{t}+I(H)))\geq\varphi(n,t).

Proof.

Since

. We now proceed similarly as in Lemma

. We will prove by downward induction on the size of

. If

, then

. Hence

\depth(S/I(C_{n})^{t}+I(H))=\varphi(n,2)\geq\varphi(n,t).

Let

be the smallest index such that

, i.e.,
e_{0}=e_{n},e_{1},\ldots,e_{i-1}\in H
. Let

. By Lemma

, we have

\depth(S/J\in\{\depth(S/J:e_{i},\depth S/(J,e_{i}))\}.

By induction, it suffices to prove that

(3.16)

\depth(S/J:e_{i})\geq\varphi(n,t).

Note that
e_{i-1}\in H
. Hence
x_{i-1}\in I(H):e_{i}
. There are two cases.

Case 1.

. By Lemma
2.5
, we have

(3.17)

.

Case 2.

(3.18)

, it suffices to prove that

(3.20)

. By Theorem

(3.21)

\depth S/(K_{\ell}+e_{i})=1+\depth R/(I(P_{n-4})^{\ell}+I(H^{\prime}))\geq 1+\varphi(n-4,\ell)\geq\varphi(n,t),

We now give an upper bound for the depth of powers of cycles.

Lemma 3.9
.

Assume that
I=I(C_{n})
and

. Then

\depth(S/I^{t}:(e_{2}\cdots e_{t}))\leq\lceil\frac{n-t+1}{3}\rceil.

Proof.

Let
J=I^{t}:(e_{2}\cdots e_{t})
. By Lemma
2.9
, we see that
J
is the edge ideal of the following graph

G_{n,t}=C_{n}\cup\{x_{i}x_{j}\mid i<j\leq t+2\text{ is of different parity}\}.

First, assume that
t=n-2
. If
n
is even, then
G_{n,t}
is a complete bipartite graph, hence
\depth S/J=1
.
If
n
is odd, let
H
be the restriction of

to

. Then

is a complete bipartite graph. Furthermore, we have

. By

, this is a Betti splitting. Furthermore,
x_{1}(x_{2},x_{4}\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n})\cap I(H)=x_{1}I(H)
. Hence, by
[

, Corollary 4.8]
,

\pd(S/J)=\pd(S/x_{1}I(H))+1=n-1.

If

, hence

. Now assume that

. Then

(3.22)

,

2.2
, the conclusion follows.
∎

We are now ready for the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let

be a cycle on

\depth(S/I(C_{n})^{t})=\varphi(n,t).

By Lemma

, Lemma

and Lemma

, we get

. By Lemma

4.
Depth of powers of edge ideals of trees

In this section, we study the depth of powers of edge ideals of trees. First, we have

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
T
be a tree. Then
\depth S/I(T)=q(T)
, where
q(T)
is the minimum size of a maximal independent set of
T
.

Proof.

This follows from work of Kimura
[
K
]
. We give an alternative proof here.

Let
G
be any simple graph. Let
F
be any maximal independent set of
G
. Then
I(G):x_{F}=(x_{j}\mid j\in[n]\setminus F)
. Hence,
\depth S/(I(G):x_{F})=|F|
. By Lemma
2.2
,
\depth S/I(G)\leq q(G)
, where
q(G)
is the minimum size of a maximal independent set of
G
.

It remains to prove that
\depth S/I(T)\geq q(T)
. Let
F
be a maximal independent set of minimum size. We first claim that we can take
F
so that
F
contains a near leaf. Indeed, if all vertices of
F
are leaves, we may replace a leaf with its unique neighbor
w
and remove all leaves in
F\cap N(w)
. Then we have another maximal independent set of size at most
F
. Thus, we may assume that
w
is a near leaf. Write
F=w\cup F_{1}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have
\depth S/I\in\{\depth S/I:w,\depth S/(I,w)\}
. Let
T_{1}
be the tree obtained by removing
w
and all of its neighbors that are leaves. Then
\depth S/(I,w)=|L(w)|+\depth R/I(T_{1})
where
L(w)
is the set of leaves attached to
w
. By induction,
\depth R/I(T_{1})\geq q(T_{1})
. Furthermore,
q(T)\leq q(T_{1})+1
. Hence,
\depth S/(I,w)\geq q(T)
.

Finally,
I:w=(x_{j}\mid j\in N(w))+I(T_{2})
where
T_{2}
is the tree obtained by removing
N[w]
. We have
\depth S/I:w=1+\depth R/I(T_{2})
and
q(T)\leq q(T_{2})+1
. The conclusion follows.
∎

In particular, we have the following interesting property on the depth of trees with respect to inclusion.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
T_{1}
be a subtree of
T_{2}
. Then

\depth R/I(T_{1})\leq\depth S/I(T_{2}),

where
R
and
S
are polynomial rings over the variables corresponding to supports of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
.

We will now compute the depth of powers of edge ideals of starlike trees. First, we introduce some notation and a function. We use bold letters for vectors in
\NN^{k}
. For a vector
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{k}
, its components are
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
and
|\mathbf{a}|=a_{1}+\ldots+a_{k}
. The vectors
\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{k}
are the canonical unit vectors of
\RR^{k}
;
\mathbf{1}
denotes the vector whose all components are
1
. For a tuple
\mathbf{a}\in\NN^{k}
, we let
\alpha_{j}
be the number of
a_{i}
such that
a_{i}=j\pmod{3}
. Let
g:\NN^{k}\to\NN
be defined by

g(\mathbf{a})=\begin{cases}&\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil\text{ if }\alpha_{1}=0\text{ and }\alpha_{2}\neq 0\\
&1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The following properties of
g
follow immediately from the definition.

Lemma 4.3
.

We have

(1)

Assume that
a_{i}\geq 3
then
g(\mathbf{a})=g(\mathbf{a}-3\mathbf{e}_{i})+1
.

(2)

Assume that
a_{i}\geq 2
then
g(\mathbf{a})\leq 1+g(\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{e}_{i}).

First, we compute the depth of a starlike tree.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Denote
T_{\mathbf{a}}
the starlike trees obtained by joining paths of length
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
at the root
1
. Then

\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})=g(\mathbf{a}).

Proof.

We prove by induction on
s=|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}|
. For simplicity of notation, we denote
I=I(T_{\mathbf{a}})
. When
s=0
, then
\depth S/I=1
. Assume that
s>0
and
a_{1}
is largest, so
a_{1}>1
. We may assume that
x
is a leaf and
y
is a near leaf of the first branch. First, consider the case
a_{1}\geq 3
. In this case, by induction and Lemma
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\depth S/(I,y)

\displaystyle=1+g(\mathbf{a}-2\mathbf{e}_{1})\geq g(\mathbf{a})

\displaystyle\depth(S/I:y)

\displaystyle=1+g(\mathbf{a}-3\mathbf{e}_{1})=g(\mathbf{a}).

The conclusion then follows from Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.2
.
Now, assume that
a_{1}=2
. Assume that
a_{i}=2
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
and
a_{j}=1
for
\ell<j\leq k
. Let
y
be the root of
T_{\mathbf{a}}
. We have

\displaystyle\depth S/(I,y)

\displaystyle=k

\displaystyle\depth(S/I:y)

\displaystyle=1+\ell.

If
\ell<k
then by Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.2

\depth S/I=1+\ell=g(\mathbf{a})
. If
\ell=k
, we prove that
\depth S/I=k
. We have
S=\mathrm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{2k+1}]
and
I=x_{1}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{2k})+(x_{2i}x_{2i+1}\mid i=1,\ldots,k)
. Then we have
I:x_{2}=(x_{1},x_{3})+(x_{2j}x_{2j+1}\mid j=2,\ldots,k)
. Hence,
\depth S/(I:x_{2})=k
. The conclusion follows.
∎

Before studying the depth of powers of starlike trees, we give a general procedure for bounding the depth of powers of trees. Assume that the edges of a tree
T
are labeled
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1}
such that
e_{i}
is a leaf of the tree
T_{i}
with edges
e_{i},\ldots,e_{n-1}
. We denote by
H_{i}
the ideal generated by
e_{1},\ldots,e_{i}
. By abuse of notation, we also denote
T_{i}
the ideal generated by
e_{i},\ldots,e_{n-1}
. By repeated use of Lemma
2.3
, we deduce that

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
T
be a tree. Let
t\geq 2
be an integer. Then
\depth S/I^{t}
belongs to the set

\{\depth S/(I(T)^{t}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}\mid\ell=0,\ldots n-2\}.

Note that
I(T)^{t}+H_{\ell}=T_{\ell+1}^{t}+H_{\ell}
and since
e_{\ell+1}
is a leaf edge of
T_{\ell+1}
we deduce that

(4.1)

(I(T)^{t}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}=T_{\ell+1}^{t-1}+(H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}).

From that, we can deduce a lower bound on the depth of powers of trees by induction. Now, let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
be a tuple of positive integers. We assume that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{k}
. We label the edges of the starlike tree
T_{\mathbf{a}}
by going from the outside of each branch to the center from one branch to another. Assume that
e_{\ell+1}=uv
is the
b
th edge of the
i
th branch. Then
H_{\ell}
is the union of a starlike tree on the
i-1
branches of
T
with a path of length
b-1
.
T_{\ell+1}
is the starlike tree
T_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}
with
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=(0,\ldots,0,a_{i}-b+1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k})
. For simplicity of notation, we assume that
u
is farther from the center than
v
. We now give a formula for the colon ideal
H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

(1)

Assume that
u
is the leaf of the
i
th branch and
v
is the center. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
be the unique neighbours of
v
on the first
i-1
branches of
T
. Then
H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}=H_{\ell}+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
.

(2)

Assume that
u
is a leaf of the
i
th branch and
v
is not the center. Then
H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}=H_{\ell}
.

(3)

Assume that
u
is not a leaf and
v
is not the center. Let
w
be the unique neighbor of
u
other than
v
. Then

H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}=H_{\ell}+(w)
.

(4)

Assume that
u
is not a leaf and
v
is the center. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1}
be the unique neighbors of
v
on the first
i-1
branches of
T
and
w
be the unique neighbor of
u
other than
v
. Then
H_{\ell}:e_{\ell+1}=H_{\ell}+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},w)
.

Proof.

These statements follows from the definition of
H_{\ell}
and the fact that for a monomial ideal
I
generated by
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
and a monomial
g
, then
I:g
is generated by
f_{1}/\gcd(f_{1},g),\ldots,f_{t}/\gcd(f_{t},g).

∎

Let
\mathbf{t}\in\NN^{k}
be another tuple of non-negative integers. We write
\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{b}
if
t_{i}\leq b_{i}
for all
i
. Throughout, we assume that
\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}
. We assume that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\ldots\geq a_{k}
. First, we prove a lower bound for the depth of powers of starlike trees.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Assume that
2\leq t<|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}|
, then

\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}\geq\min(g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t})\mid\mathbf{t}\in\NN^{k},\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1},|\mathbf{t}|=t-1).

Proof.

We use the notation from Lemma
4.5
. We will prove by induction on
t
that there exists a
\mathbf{t}_{0}
such that
\mathbf{t}_{0}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}
,
|\mathbf{t}_{0}|=t-1
and
\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}+H_{\ell}\geq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}_{0})
. By induction and Lemma
2.3
, it suffices to prove that for each
\ell
, there exists such a
\mathbf{t}_{0}
and
\depth(S/(I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1})\geq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}_{0})
. For simplicity of notation, we denote
J=(I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}
, where
e_{\ell+1}=uv
. Let
S^{\prime}
be the polynomial on the variables corresponding to vertices of
T_{\ell+1}
. By Lemma
4.6
, there are four cases as follows.

Case 1.

u
is a leaf and
v
is the center. Then
J=I(T_{\ell+1})^{t-1}+K+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
, where
T_{\ell+1}
is the starlike tree of size
1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k}
and
K
is the unions of paths of lengths
a_{j}-2
for
j=1,\ldots,i-1
. With our assumption,
a_{j}=1
for all
j\geq i
. Hence, by Theorem
2.4
and Lemma
2.6
,

(4.2)

\depth S/J=\sum_{1}^{i-1}\lceil\frac{a_{j}-1}{3}\rceil+1=\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})=g(\mathbf{a}).

Case 2.

u
is a leaf and
v
is not the center. Then
J=I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t-1}+H_{\ell}
. Thus, the conclusion follows from induction on
t
as
g(\mathbf{a})\leq g(\mathbf{a}^{\prime})
if
\mathbf{a}\ll\mathbf{a}^{\prime}
.

Case 3.

u
is not a leaf and
v
is not the center. In particular,
b\geq 2
. Then
J=I(T_{\ell+1})^{t-1}+(w)+K
where
T_{\ell+1}
is the starlike tree of size
a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}-b+1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k}
and
K=K_{1}+K_{2}
with
K_{1}
is the edge ideal of the starlike tree which is the union of the first
i-1
branches of
T_{\mathbf{a}}
,
K_{2}
is a path of length
\max(b-3,0)
. By induction, we have

(4.3)

\depth S/J=\lceil\frac{b-2}{3}\rceil+\depth(S^{\prime}/(T_{\ell+1}^{t-1}+K_{1}))\geq\lceil\frac{b-2}{3}\rceil+g(\mathbf{a}-(b-1)\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{t}^{\prime}),

for some
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\ll\mathbf{a}-(b-1)\mathbf{e}_{i}-\mathbf{1}
and
|\mathbf{t}^{\prime}|=t-2
. Let
\mathbf{t}_{0}=\mathbf{t}^{\prime}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\lceil\frac{b-2}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{a_{i}-(b-1)-t_{i}^{\prime}}{3}\rceil\lceil\geq\lceil\frac{a_{i}-t_{i}}{3}\rceil.

Hence,
\depth S/J\geq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}_{0})
.

Case 4.

u
is not a leaf and
v
is the center. Then
J=T_{\ell+1}^{t-1}+K+(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},w)
where
K
is the union of paths of length
a_{j}-2
for
j=1,\ldots,i-1
and a path of length
\max(a_{i}-3,0)
and
T_{\ell+1}
is the starlike tree of size
1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k}
. Hence,

(4.4)

\depth S/J=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\lceil\frac{a_{j}-1}{3}\rceil+\lceil\frac{a_{i}-2}{3}\rceil+\depth(S^{\prime}/T_{\ell+1}^{t-1}).

Let
\mathbf{a}^{\prime}=(1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{k})\in N^{k-i+1}
. By induction, there exists
\mathbf{t}^{\prime}\ll\mathbf{a}^{\prime}-\mathbf{1}
with
|\mathbf{t}^{\prime}|=t-2
such that
\depth(S^{\prime}/T_{\ell+1}^{t-1})\geq g(\mathbf{a}^{\prime}-\mathbf{t}^{\prime})
. Let
\mathbf{t}_{0}=\mathbf{t}^{\prime}+\mathbf{e}_{i}
. Hence,
\depth S/J\geq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}_{0})
. The conclusion follows.
∎

The following lemma is essential for establishing an upper bound for the depth of powers of edge ideals of trees.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
G
be a weakly chordal graph. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}\}
be a strongly disjoint family of complete bipartite graphs of
G
with
e_{1},\ldots,e_{g}
the induced matching of
G
with
e_{i}\in B_{i}
. Let
H
be the restriction of
G
to
[n]\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{s}V(B_{i})
. Assume that
H
is non-empty, has no isolated vertices, and
N(e_{i})\cap V(H)=\emptyset
for all
i=1,\ldots,s
. Then

\depth S/I(G)\leq s+\depth R/I(H),

where
R
is the polynomial ring on the vertices of
H
.

Proof.

Since
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
,
H
is weakly chordal. By Theorem
2.10
, there exists a strongly disjoint family of complete bipartite graphs
B_{s+1},\ldots,B_{g}
of
H
such that

\pd(R/I(H))=\sum_{i=s+1}^{g}|V(B_{i})|-(g-s)
. Now, with the assumption of the lemma, we see that
B_{1},\ldots,B_{g}
form a strongly disjoint family of complete bipartite graphs of
G
. Hence,

\pd(S/I(G))\geq(\sum_{i=1}^{s}|V(B_{i})|)-s+\pd(R/I(H)).

The conclusion follows from the Auslander-Buchsbaum formula.
∎

We are now ready to give an upper bound for the depth of powers of edge ideals of starlike trees. We will use the following notation. Assume that the edges of the
i
th branch of
T_{\mathbf{a}}
are labeled
e_{i,1},\ldots,e_{i,a_{i}}
where
e_{i,1}
is the leaf and
e_{i,a_{i}}
is the one that contains the root.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\mathbf{t}\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple such that
\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}
. Let

f=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\prod_{j=2}^{t_{i}+1}e_{i,j}

be a product of
t_{i}
edges from the
i
th branch. Then

\depth S/(I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}:f)\leq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}),

where
t=|\mathbf{t}|+1
.

Proof.

For this lemma, we label the vertices of the
i
th branch
T_{\mathbf{a}}
by
x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,a_{i}+1}
where
x_{i,1}
is the leaf and
x_{i,a_{i}+1}=1
is the root.

First assume that
t_{i}\leq a_{i}-3
for all
i
. Then
I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}:f=I(G)
where
G
is the union of complete bipartite graphs
B_{i}
on
\{x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,t_{i}+3}\}
if
t_{i}>0
and the starlike tree
T_{\mathbf{a}^{\prime}}
where
a^{\prime}_{i}=a_{i}-t_{i}-2
if
t_{i}>0
. Let
\mathbf{b}
be defined by
b_{i}=a_{i}-t_{i}-3
if
t_{i}>0
. Then
\{B_{i}\mid t_{i}>0\}
and
T_{\mathbf{b}}
is disjoint. For simplicity, we assume that
t_{i}>0
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
and
t_{i}=0
for
i=\ell+1,\ldots,k
. By Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.4
, we have

\depth S/I(G)\leq\ell+g(\mathbf{b})=g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}).

Now assume that there are some
t_{i}
such that
t_{i}=a_{i}-2
and
t_{i}<a_{i}-1
for all
i
. After rearranging
a_{i}
, assume that
t_{i}=a_{i}-2
for
i=1,\ldots,s
and
0<t_{i}\leq a_{i}-3
for
s+1\leq i\leq\ell
. Then
I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}:f=I(G)
where
G
is the union of complete bipartite graphs
B_{i}
on
\{x_{i,1},\ldots,x_{i,a_{i}+1}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
and
B_{j}
on
\{x_{j,1},\ldots,x_{j,t_{j}+3}\}
for
j=s+1,\ldots,\ell
. Let
B_{i}^{\prime}=B_{i}\setminus\{x_{i,a_{i}+1}\}
for
i=1,\ldots,s
. Then
B_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,B_{s}^{\prime},B_{s+1},\ldots,B_{\ell}
and
T_{\mathbf{b}}
are disjoint, where
\mathbf{b}=(0,\ldots,0,a_{s+1}-t_{s+1}-3,\ldots,a_{\ell}-t_{\ell}-3,a_{\ell+1},\ldots,a_{k})
. By Lemma
4.8
and Lemma
4.4
, we have

\depth S/I(G)\leq\ell+g(\mathbf{b})=g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}).

Finally, assume that there are some
t_{i}
such that
t_{i}=a_{i}-1
. Assume that
0<t_{i}=a_{i}-1
for
i=1,\ldots,s
,
0<t_{j}=a_{j}-2
for
j=s+1,\ldots,u
,
0<t_{j}\leq a_{j}-3
for
j=u+1,\ldots,v
and
t_{j}=0
for
j\geq v+1
. By Lemma
2.9
,
I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}:f=I(G)
where
G
is the union of complete bipartite graphs
B_{1},B_{s+1},\ldots,B_{v}
and paths
P_{u+1},\ldots,P_{k}
, where the graphs
B_{i}
and paths
P_{j}
are:

(1)

B_{1}
consists of vertices
\{1,x_{1,1},\ldots,x_{1,a_{1}},x_{2,1},\ldots,x_{2,a_{2}},\ldots,x_{s,1},\ldots,x_{s,a_{s}},x_{j,a_{j}}\mid j=s+1,\ldots,k\}
,

(2)

For
j=s+1,\ldots,u
,
B_{j}
consists of vertices
\{x_{j,1},\ldots,x_{j,a_{j}-1}\}
,

(3)

For
m=u+1,\ldots,v
,
B_{m}
consists of vertices
\{x_{m,1},\ldots,x_{m,b_{m}}\}
, where

.

(4)

For

P_{j}
is a path of length

,

(5)

For

,
P_{m}
is a path of length

,

where by a path of length

we mean it does not exist. By Lemma
4.8
and Lemma

, we deduce that

\depth S/I(G)\leq 1+v-s+\sum_{m=u+1}^{k}\lceil\frac{p_{m}+1}{3}\rceil\leq g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}).

The conclusion follows.
∎

It remains to compute
\min(g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t}\mid\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1},|\mathbf{t}|=t-1))
in terms of
\mathbf{a}
and
t
. To do so, we note the following properties of
g
.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\mathbf{b}=(a_{3},\ldots,a_{k})
. We have

(1)

g(3k+1,3l+3,\mathbf{b})\leq g(3k+2,3l+2,\mathbf{b})
.

(2)

g(3k+1,3l+1,\mathbf{b})\leq g(3k,3l+2,\mathbf{b})
.

(3)

g(3k-2,3l+2,\mathbf{b})\leq g(3k,3l,\mathbf{b})
.

Proof.

These properties follow easily from the definition of
g
. We prove one of them for completeness. For (1), we have

g(3k+1,3l+3,\mathbf{b})=1+k+l+1+\sum_{i=3}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil.

Furthermore,

g(3k+2,3l+2,\mathbf{b})=\epsilon+k+1+l+1+\sum_{i=3}^{k}\lceil\frac{a_{i}-1}{3}\rceil,

where
\epsilon=1
if
a_{j}=1\pmod{3}
for some
j\geq 3
and
0
otherwise. In any cases, we have
g(3k+2,3l+2,\mathbf{b})\geq g(3k+1,3l+3,\mathbf{b})
.
∎

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Let
\alpha_{i}
be the number of
a_{j}
such that
a_{j}=i\pmod{3}
. We may assume that
a_{j}=2\pmod{3}
for
j=1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}
,
a_{j}=0\pmod{3}
for
j=\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{0}
and
a_{j}=1\pmod{3}
for
j=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,k
. Let
\beta_{1}=\min(\alpha_{2},t-1)
,
\beta_{2}=\min(\alpha_{0},\lfloor\frac{\max(0,t-1-\alpha_{2})}{2}\rfloor)
and
\beta_{3}=\lfloor\frac{\max(0,t-1-\beta_{1}-2\beta_{2})}{3}\rfloor
. We define
\mathbf{b}\in\NN^{k}
as follows.

b_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}-1&\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,\beta_{1},\\
a_{i}-2&\text{ for }i=\alpha_{2}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}.\end{cases}

Then

\min(g(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{t})\mid\mathbf{t}\ll\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{1}\text{ and }|\mathbf{t}|=t-1)=g(\mathbf{b})-\beta_{3}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.10
, we can choose an optimal
\mathbf{t}
such that
t_{j}=a_{j}-1\pmod{3}
for all but one value of
j
. By Lemma
4.3
, the optimal
\mathbf{t}
is obtained when we get the maximal number of indices
j
such that
t_{j}=a_{j}-1\pmod{3}
. Let
\gamma_{i}
be the number of
t_{j}
such that
t_{j}=j\pmod{3}
. Then, we have
\gamma_{1}\leq\alpha_{2}
,
\gamma_{2}\leq\alpha_{0}
and
\gamma_{1}+2\gamma_{2}\leq t-1
. The conclusion follows. Thus, the optimal values for
\gamma
s are precisely the
\beta
s.
∎

We are now ready for the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})\in\NN^{k}
be a tuple of positive integers. Denote
T_{\mathbf{a}}
the starlike trees obtained by joining paths of length
a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
at the root
1
. With the notation as in Lemma
4.11
, for all
t
such that
1\leq t\leq|\mathbf{a}|-k=s
,

\depth S/I(T_{\mathbf{a}})^{t}=g(\mathbf{b})-\beta_{3}.

Proof.

Follows from Lemmas
4.5
,
4.7
,
4.9
, and
4.11
.
∎

.

,

and

Finally, we compute the depth of powers of a caterpillar tree to show that depth of powers of edge ideals of trees could drop arbitrarily at a step.

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
G
be a caterpillar tree based on a path of length
2
on
3
vertices
1,\ldots,3
. Assume that
d_{i}
is the number of leaves at
i
. We have

\depth S/I(G)^{t}=\begin{cases}\min(d_{1}+d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2)&\text{ if }t=1\\
\min(d_{1}+1,d_{2}+2,d_{3}+1)&\text{ if }t=2\\
1&\text{ if }t\geq 3.\end{cases}

Proof.

We have
I=x_{1}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d_{1}})+x_{2}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d_{2}})+x_{3}(w_{1},\ldots,w_{d_{3}})+(x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}x_{3})
. Thus,
I:x_{2}=(x_{1},x_{3},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d_{2}})
. Hence,
\depth S/(I:x_{2})=d_{2}+2.
Also,
I:x_{1}x_{3}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d_{1}},w_{1},\ldots,w_{d_{3}},x_{2})
. Hence,
\depth S/(I:x_{1}x_{3})=d_{1}+d_{3}+1
. Hence,
\depth S/I\leq\min(d_{1}+d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2)
.

Furthermore, we have
\depth S/(I,x_{2})=d_{1}+d_{3}+1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.3
we have
\depth S/I=\min(d_{1}+d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2)
.

Now, we prove the formula for
\depth S/I^{2}
. First, we have
\depth S/I^{2}\leq\depth S/I\leq d_{2}+2
. Now, consider
J=I^{2}:x_{1}x_{2}
. Let
B_{1}
be a complete bipartite graph on
\{x_{1},x_{2},u_{1},\ldots,u_{d_{1}},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d_{2}}\}
. Then, we have
\pd S/J\geq d_{1}+d_{2}+2
. Hence,
\depth S/I^{2}\leq\depth S/J\leq d_{3}+1
. Similarly, we have
\depth S/I^{2}\leq\depth S/(I^{2}:x_{2}x_{3})\leq d_{1}+1
.

Now, we prove that
\depth S/I^{2}\geq\min(d_{1}+1,d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2).
We label the edges of
T_{\mathbf{a}}
as follows,
x_{1}u_{1},\ldots,x_{1}u_{d_{1}},x_{3}w_{1},\ldots,x_{3}w_{d_{3}},x_{1}x_{2},x_{2}v_{1},\ldots,x_{2}v_{d_{2}},x_{2}x_{3}
. By Lemma
4.5
, it suffices to prove that
\depth S/(I^{2}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}\geq\min(d_{1}+1,d_{3}+1,d_{2}+2)
. Let
J=(I^{2}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}
. There are several cases as follows.

Case 1.

e_{\ell+1}=x_{1}u_{j}
. Then
J=(I^{2}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{j-1})+I(T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime})
where
T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}
is the caterpillar tree with
d_{1}^{\prime}=d_{1}-j+1
. Thus,
\depth S/J=\min(d_{2}+2,d_{1}-j+1+d_{3})\geq\min(d_{2}+2,d_{3}+1)
.

Case 2.

e_{\ell+1}=x_{3}w_{j}
. Then
J=(I^{2}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{j-1})+I(T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime})
where
T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}
is the caterpillar tree with
d_{3}=d_{3}-j+1
. Thus,
\depth S/J=\min(d_{2}+2,d_{1}+d_{3}-j+1)\geq\min(d_{2}+2,d_{1}+1)
.

Case 3.

e_{\ell+1}=x_{1}x_{2}
. Then
J=(I^{2}+H_{\ell}):e_{\ell+1}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{d_{1}})+I(T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime})
where
T_{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}
is the caterpillar tree on
x_{2}x_{3}
with
d_{2}^{\prime}=d_{2}+1
. Hence,
\depth S/J=\min(d_{2}+2,d_{3}+1)
.

Case 4.

e_{\ell+1}=x_{2}v_{j}
. Then
J=(x_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{j-1})
. Hence,
\depth S/J=d_{1}+\min(d_{2}-j+2,d_{3}+1)>d_{3}+1
.

Case 5.

e_{\ell+1}=x_{2}x_{3}
. Then
J=(x_{1},v_{1},\ldots,v_{d_{2}},w_{1},\ldots,w_{d_{3}})
. Hence,
\depth S/J=d_{1}+1
. The conclusion follows.
∎

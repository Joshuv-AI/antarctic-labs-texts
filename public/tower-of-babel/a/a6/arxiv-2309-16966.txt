The Mahler measure of a family of polynomials with arbitrarily many variables
Siva Sankar Nair

Abstract
. We present an exact formula for the Mahler measure of an infinite family of polynomials with arbitrarily many variables. The formula is obtained by manipulating the integral defining the Mahler measure using certain transformations, followed by an iterative process that reduces this computation to the evaluation of certain polylogarithm functions at sixth roots of unity. This yields values of the Riemann zeta function and the Dirichlet L L -function associated to the character of conductor 3.

Primary 11R06; Secondary 11M06, 11G55

1.
Introduction

For a non-zero rational function in
n
variables,
P\in\mathbb{C}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, the (logarithmic) Mahler measure of
P
is defined to be

\mathrm{m}(P):=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\log|P(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n}}{x_{n}},

where the integration path is taken along the unit
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|x_{1}|=\cdots=|x_{n}|=1\}
with respect to the Haar measure.

While Jensen had already studied this integral for single-variable holomorphic functions in the late
19^{\text{th}}
century, it was in Lehmer’s work
[
15
]
that this quantity first appeared in the context of single-variable polynomial functions. One may note that using Jensen’s formula, the Mahler measure of a univariate polynomial can be expressed in terms of the absolute value of its roots that lie outside the unit circle. The generalization to multivariate polynomials was made by Mahler
[
16
]
when he studied the quantity
\mathrm{M}(P)=\exp(\mathrm{m}(P))
in his work related to polynomial heights. In the 1980s, it was Smyth
[
2
,
21
]
who first observed a relation between Mahler measures and values of certain
L
-functions when he showed the following equalities:

(1)

\mathrm{m}(x+y+1)=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}L(\chi_{-3},2)=L^{\prime}(\chi_{-3},-1),

(2)

\mathrm{m}(x+y+z+1)=\frac{7}{2\pi^{2}}\zeta(3)=-14\cdot\zeta^{\prime}(-2),

where
L(\chi_{-3},s)
is the Dirichlet
L
-function in the character of conductor 3 and
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Interest in multivariate Mahler measures grew following these results, and several relations between Mahler measures and special values of
L
-functions associated to various arithmetic objects were observed. For instance, Deninger
[
5
]
and Boyd
[
3
]
conjectured the following relation involving an elliptic curve, which was later proven by Rogers and Zudilin
[
19
]
:

\mathrm{m}\left(x+\frac{1}{x}+y+\frac{1}{y}+1\right)=\frac{15}{4\pi^{2}}L(E_{15a8},2)=L^{\prime}(E_{15a8},0).

Here
L(E_{15a8},s)
denotes the
L
-function associated to the elliptic curve defined by the Weierstrass equation

E_{15a8}:y^{2}+xy+y=x^{3}+x^{2},

with Cremona label
15a8
. While much remains unknown regarding this mysterious link between Mahler measures and
L
-functions, there has been remarkable work that sheds light on this phenomenon. In the 1990s, Deninger
[
5
]
related Mahler measures to certain regulator values from
K
-theory. In view of Beilinson’s conjectures, these regulators are expected to appear as values of
L
-functions, as well as polylogarithm functions evaluated at algebraic numbers. In turn, certain combinations of these polylogarithms yield values of the Riemann zeta function and various other Dirichlet
L
-functions, explaining their presence in Mahler measure computations. For example, Smyth’s first example (
1
) is the Mahler measure of a two-variable polynomial expressed in terms of a dilogarithm, and his second example (
2
) is that of a three-variable polynomial in terms of a trilogarithm. On a slightly different but related note, one may view this in context of Zagier’s grand conjecture
[
24
]
– the value
\zeta_{K}(n)
of the Dedekind zeta function corresponding to a number field
K
evaluated at an integer
n>1
can be expressed as the determinant of a matrix whose entries are linear combinations of polylogarithms evaluated at certain elements of
K
. Once again, this has connections to the (Borel) regulator, as seen in the proofs for the case
n=3
by Goncharov
[
6
]
and
n=4
by Goncharov and Rudenko
[
9
]
.

A particularly interesting result was given by Lalín
[
11
]
involving rational functions with arbitrarily many variables whose Mahler measure can be computed explicitly. These formulae arise by evaluating polylogarithms at the fourth root of unity which yield values of the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(\chi_{-4},s)
associated to the character of conductor 4. This is one of the few results in the literature where the exact Mahler measure of multivariable polynomials with arbitrarily many variables has been calculated (see
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
for other examples). We explain the result in greater detail. Let

\displaystyle R_{m}(z_{1},\dots,z_{m},y):=y+\left(\frac{1-z_{1}}{1+z_{1}}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{1-z_{m}}{1+z_{m}}\right),

and for
a_{1},\dots a_{m}\in\mathbb{C}
, define the elementary symmetric polynomials

s_{\ell}(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if }\ell=0,\\
\sum_{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\ell}}a_{i_{1}}\cdots a_{i_{\ell}}&\mbox{if }0<\ell\leq m,\\
0&\mbox{if }\ell>m.\end{array}\right.

We have an explicit formula for the Mahler measure of the rational function
R_{m}
:

Theorem
.

(
[
11
,
12
]
) For
n\geq 1
,

\mathrm{m}\left(R_{2n}\right)=\sum_{h=1}^{n}\frac{s_{n-h}(2^{2},4^{2},\dots,(2n-2)^{2})}{(2n-1)!}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2h}\mathcal{A}(h),

where

\mathcal{A}(h):=(2h)!\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}\right)\zeta(2h+1).

For
n\geq 0
,

\mathrm{m}\left(R_{2n+1}\right)=\sum_{h=0}^{n}\frac{s_{n-h}(1^{2},3^{2},\dots,(2n-1)^{2})}{(2n)!}\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{2h+1}\mathcal{B}(h),

where

\mathcal{B}(h):=(2h+1)!L(\chi_{-4},2h+2).

The purpose of this article is to express the Mahler measure of another family of polynomials with arbitrarily many variables in terms of zeta values and certain
L
-values. The method of obtaining these results is similar to that of Lalín
[
11
]
applied to a more complicated family of functions. However, the polynomials we consider have algebraic coefficients rather than integral. The interesting outcome is that in this case, the calculations involve the polylogarithm evaluated at sixth roots of unity and we get values of the
L
-function
L(\chi_{-3},s)
associated to the character of conductor 3. This is the first example of an infinite family of polynomials with arbitrarily many variables yielding Mahler measures that involve special values of the
L
-function
L(\chi_{-3},s)
. In particular, we consider the rational function

Q_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y)=y+\left(\frac{z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)

where

\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}

is a third root of unity. We let

\mathcal{C}(h)=(2h)!\left(1-\frac{1}{3^{2h+1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}\right)\zeta(2h+1),

and

\mathcal{D}(h)=(2h+1)!\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{2h+2}}\right)L(\chi_{-3},2h+2).

Then

Theorem 1
.

For
n\geq 1
,

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2n})=\frac{2}{12^{n}}\Bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{n}a_{n,h-1}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h}\mathcal{C}(h)\;+\;\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}b_{n,h}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h+1}\mathcal{D}(h)\Bigg),

and for
n\geq 0
we have

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2n+1})=\frac{1}{{12}^{n}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{h=1}^{n}c_{n,h-1}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h}\mathcal{C}(h)\;+\;\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{h=0}^{n}d_{n,h}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h+1}\mathcal{D}(h)\Bigg),

where the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s},d_{r,s}
are real numbers given recursively by equations (
91
)-(
96
) starting from the initial value
d_{0,0}=1
.

Using the respective functional equations, we may also express these Mahler measures as
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combinations of derivatives of the Riemann zeta function evaluated at negative even integers and derivatives of the
L
-function
L(\chi_{-3},s)
evaluated at negative odd integers (see equations (
103
) and (
104
) in Section
4.5
). The first few examples of this family are given in Table
1
.

We remark that using an alternate method, we can write the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s},d_{r,s}
in a more explicit form. This method, described in Section
3.6
, uses polynomials that arise as coefficients in certain power series, and whose explicit expressions may be obtained using standard convolution techniques. The first two polynomials in this family have been computed (equations (
49
) and (
50
)).

To evaluate the above Mahler measures, we first use contour integration to derive general formulae for certain integrals of the form

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t(t+a)\log^{k}t}{(t^{3}-a^{3})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t,

for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. This is done in Section
3
. We also present an alternate method to evaluate these integrals, which results in more explicit expressions rather than recursive ones. This is done in Section
3.6
, and summarized in Proposition
2
. The multiple integral appearing in the Mahler measure computation can be evaluated using the formulae from Section
3
one after the other for each variable. To systematically capture the details of this process, we lay out an iterative procedure in Section
4
. This lets us express the Mahler measure as an integral of a univariate function, and these integrals in turn can be related to values of certain polylogarithms at sixth roots of unity via hyperlogarithms, which we introduce in Section
2
. The polylogarithm values can then be expressed in terms of the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions, giving us the desired result.

2.
Hyperlogarithms and multiple polylogarithms

We first give a brief introduction to hyperlogarithms and multiple polylogarithms and relate them via iterated integrals. One may refer to
[
7
, Section 12]
,
[
8
, Section 2]
and
[
11
, Section 3]
for more details. We follow the same notation to denote an iterated integral

\int_{0}^{a_{k+1}}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{k}}:=\underset{P}{\int\cdots\int}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t_{1}}{t_{1}-a_{1}}\cdots\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t_{k}}{t_{k}-a_{k}},

where the
a_{j}
’s are complex numbers and the path of integration
P
is a path
0\to t_{1}\to t_{2}\to\cdots\to t_{k}\to a_{k+1}
joining 0 and
a_{k+1}
. The value of this integral depends on the homotopy class of
P
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{m}\}
. With this notation in mind, the
hyperlogarithm
is defined as

\mathrm{I}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}(a_{1}:a_{2}:\cdots:a_{m+1}):=\\
\int_{0}^{a_{m+1}}\underbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{1}}\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}_{n_{1}\text{ times}}\circ\underbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{2}}\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}_{n_{2}\text{ times}}\circ\cdots\circ\underbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a_{m}}\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}_{n_{m}\text{ times}},

where
n_{1},\dots,n_{m}
are positive integers. We also define the
multiple polylogarithm
of length
m
and height
w=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{m}
as

\mathrm{Li}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}):=\sum_{1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}<\cdots<k_{m}}\frac{x_{1}^{k_{1}}x_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots x_{m}^{k_{m}}}{k_{1}^{n_{1}}k_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots k_{m}^{n_{m}}}.

The sum is absolutely convergent for
|x_{j}|<1
, and also for
|x_{j}|\leq 1
if
n_{m}\geq 2
. One can show (see
[
8
]
) the following identities

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\,\mathrm{I}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\left(\frac{1}{x_{1}\cdots x_{m}}:\frac{1}{x_{2}\cdots x_{m}}:\dots:\frac{1}{x_{m}}:1\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}(a_{1}:\cdots:a_{m+1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\,\mathrm{Li}_{n_{1},\dots,n_{m}}\left(\frac{a_{2}}{a_{1}},\frac{a_{3}}{a_{2}},\dots,\frac{a_{m+1}}{a_{m}}\right).

Note that identity (
3
) enables an analytic continuation of the multiple polylogarithm using the definition of the hyperlogarithm. The value of the hyperlogarithm function depends on the homotopy class of the path joining 0 and
a_{m+1}
in
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}
. This means that these multivalued functions may not be defined uniquely. However, we will always encounter linear combinations of these functions that yield single-valued functions. For example, in Section
4.2
, we will see that the Mahler measure of
Q_{n}
can be expressed as sums of integrals such as

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log^{2h+1}t}{t^{2}+t+1}\;\mathrm{d}t,

which are well-defined. Note that we have

\frac{1}{t^{2}+t+1}=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{1}{t-\omega}-\frac{1}{t-\omega^{2}}\right),

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
is a third root of unity. For
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\,\frac{1}{t-a}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2h}(2h)!\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a}\circ\overbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}^{\text{$2h$ times}}

\displaystyle=(2h)!\cdot\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(a,1)=-(2h)!\,\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(1/a).

Using these, we may write

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log^{2h+1}t}{t^{2}+t+1}\;\mathrm{d}t=(2h)!\left(\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(\omega,1)-\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(\omega^{2},1)\right),

which shows that while the individual hyperlogarithms
\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(\omega,1)
and
\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(\omega^{2},1)
are undetermined, due to the multi-valued nature of the hyperlogarithm, their difference is well-defined.

In Section
4.2
, we will use the hyperlogarithm identities to write the integral defining the Mahler measure in terms of polylogarithms, which would then give us certain
L
-values.

3.
Some general integrals

In this section, we will evaluate certain single-variable integrals involving arbitrary powers of the logarithm. At the end of this section, we will also discuss an alternate method of computing these integrals. In Section
4
we will use these formulae to explicitly compute the Mahler measure of a certain family of polynomials.

3.1.
The integral
f_{1}(k)

First, we wish to evaluate the following integral which we denote by
f_{1}(k)
:

f_{1}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t,

for any integer
k\geq 0
and
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Here, and in the entire article, the symbol
\infty
will always mean
+\infty
. To compute the integral
f_{1}(k)
, we evaluate a contour integral

(5)

\oint_{C}\frac{z\log^{k+1}z}{(z^{2}+az+a^{2})(z^{2}+bz+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}z,

with the keyhole contour
C
as given by Figure
1
, comprising of the paths labelled
C_{\varepsilon},\gamma_{1},C_{R}
and
\gamma_{2}
. Note that this keyhole contour is chosen in order to skip the point
z=0
. We choose the branch cut of the logarithm to be the positive real axis so that the imaginary part of the logarithm function has the range
(0,2\pi)
. The path
C_{\varepsilon}
is a circular path centred at
z=0
with small radius
\varepsilon>0
, drawn such that it doesn’t cross the positive real axis. Similarly,
C_{R}
is another circular path centred at the origin, with large radius
R
, and drawn such that it doesn’t cross the positive real axis. The integrals corresponding to paths
C_{\varepsilon}
and
C_{R}
will vanish as
\varepsilon\to 0
and
R\to\infty
. Indeed, we can parametrize the integration path along
C_{\varepsilon}
by
z=\varepsilon e^{i\theta}
, where
\theta
varies from
2\pi^{-}
to
0^{+}
. Thus, taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, the integral in (
5
) along
C_{\varepsilon}
is given by

(6)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{2\pi^{-}}^{0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}(\log\varepsilon+i\theta)^{k+1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2})(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2})}\cdot i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\;\mathrm{d}\theta.

Let the integrand in (
6
) be given by the function

h(\varepsilon,\theta)=i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}(\log\varepsilon+i\theta)^{k+1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2})(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2})}.

Choose a positive real number
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that
\varepsilon_{0}<\min\{a,b\}
. This means that the roots of the equation
z^{2}+az+a^{2}=0
and
z^{2}+bz+b^{2}=0
lie outside
C_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, and thus, if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, then

|\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2}|\geq|a-\varepsilon_{0}|^{2}>0,

and

|\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2}|\geq|b-\varepsilon_{0}|^{2}>0.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|h(\varepsilon,\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon+2\pi i|}{|\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2}|\cdot|\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\log^{2}\varepsilon+4\pi^{2}}}{(a-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}(b-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}},

for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Using this upper bound in (
6
), we get

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{2\pi^{-}}^{0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}(\log\varepsilon+i\theta)^{k+1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2})(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2})}\cdot i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\;\mathrm{d}\theta\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left|\int_{2\pi^{-}}^{0^{+}}\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}(\log\varepsilon+i\theta)^{k+1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2})(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2})}\cdot i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\;\mathrm{d}\theta\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0^{+}}^{2\pi^{-}}\left|\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\theta}(\log\varepsilon+i\theta)^{k+1}}{(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+a\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+a^{2})(\varepsilon^{2}e^{2i\theta}+b\varepsilon e^{i\theta}+b^{2})}\cdot i\varepsilon e^{i\theta}\right|\;\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{0^{+}}^{2\pi^{-}}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\log^{2}\varepsilon+4\pi^{2}}}{(a-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}(b-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}\theta=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\log^{2}\varepsilon+4\pi^{2}}}{(a-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}(b-\varepsilon_{0})^{2}}\cdot 2\pi=0.

Thus, the contribution to the contour integral (
5
) along
C_{\epsilon}
is zero.

Similarly, the contribution along
C_{R}
as
R\to\infty
is given by the integral

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0^{+}}^{2\pi^{-}}h(R,\theta)\;\mathrm{d}\theta.

For large enough
R
, we have that
|R^{2}e^{2i\theta}+aRe^{i\theta}+a^{2}|>(R-a)^{2}
and
|R^{2}e^{2i\theta}+bRe^{i\theta}+b^{2}|>(R-b)^{2}
, which means that

\displaystyle|h(R,\theta)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}|\log R+2\pi i|}{|R^{2}e^{2i\theta}+aRe^{i\theta}+a^{2}|\cdot|R^{2}e^{2i\theta}+bRe^{i\theta}+b^{2}|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R^{2}\sqrt{\log^{2}R+4\pi^{2}}}{(R-a)^{2}(R-b)^{2}}.

As
R\to\infty
, we have

\frac{R^{2}\sqrt{\log^{2}R+4\pi^{2}}}{(R-a)^{2}(R-b)^{2}}\to 0,

and a similar argument as before tells us that the contribution corresponding to
C_{R}
is also zero.

What remains is the combination of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

\gamma_{1}
is a straight line just above the positive real axis that connects the end of the path

. The argument of the complex logarithm on this path is a small positive angle which goes to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Similarly, the path
\gamma_{2}
is a straight line just below the real axis, joining the end of
C_{R}
and the start of
C_{\varepsilon}
, where the argument of the complex logarithm is taken to be
2\pi
in the limit. Thus, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the respective contributions of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
give the sum

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t\log^{k+1}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\infty}^{0}\frac{t(\log t+2\pi i)^{k+1}}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Usually, we need to specify initial values to completely determine a family of recursive polynomials. However, in our case we do not need to do so since there is also a non-recursive part in the definition of the polynomials which serves to define the initial values (at

g_{1}(k)=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j>1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}{{k+1}\choose j}(3\theta)^{j-1}g_{1}(k+1-j)\\
-\frac{1}{6(k+1)(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}\theta^{j}{{k+1}\choose{j}}(2^{j}+1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-3\log^{k+1-j}b)\right]\\
-\frac{a+b}{2\sqrt{3}(k+1)(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\theta^{j}{{k+1}\choose{j}}(2^{j}-1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-\log^{k+1-j}b)\right]\\
-\frac{1}{3(k+1)(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[\log^{k+1}a+\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}{{k+1}\choose{j}}(2\pi)^{j}\log^{k+1-j}a\right].

Note that this time in (
28
), we have an additional sum along with the usual three parts. To incorporate this extra sum, we need to define another family of polynomials with rational coefficients:

Y_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j>1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}{{k+1}\choose j}3^{j-1}Y_{k+1-j}(x)\\
-\frac{1}{(k+1)}\left[x^{k+1}+\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}{{k+1}\choose{j}}3^{j}x^{k+1-j}\right].

Here are the first few examples of the polynomial
Y_{k}(x)
:

\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr Y_{k}(x)\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Y_{0}(x)=-2x\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Y_{1}(x)=-x^{2}+\frac{9}{2}\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Y_{2}(x)=-\frac{2x^{3}}{3}+3x\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Y_{3}(x)=-\frac{x^{4}}{2}+\frac{9x^{2}}{2}+\frac{81}{4}.\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

This means, using the definitions of the polynomials
R_{k}
in (
13
) and
S_{k}
in (
14
), we can write

(29)

g_{1}(k)=\frac{\theta^{k+1}}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[-R_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)+3R_{k}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)+Y_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)\right]\\
+\frac{\theta^{k+1}(a+b)}{\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[S_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)-S_{k}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)\right].\\

In our calculations in Section
4
,
a
will invariably be equal to 1, so that all the
\log a
terms above vanish.

3.4.
The integral
g_{2}(k)

The final integral we will consider is

g_{2}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t(t-a)\log^{k}t}{(t^{3}+a^{3})(t^{2}-bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t,

again with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and the corresponding contour integral

\oint_{C}\frac{z(z-a)\log^{k+1}z}{(z^{3}+a^{3})(z^{2}-bz+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}z.

In this case the integral along
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
is zero, however there is an additional residue contribution from the pole at
z=-a
. The contour integral
evaluates, as before, to

(30)

-3i\theta(k+1)g_{2}(k)-\sum_{j>1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}{{k+1}\choose j}(3\theta)^{j}\,i^{j}\,g_{2}(k+1-j),

and the residues at
z=-a\omega,-a\omega^{2},-b\omega,-b\omega^{2}
and
-a
(denoted by red dots in Figure
2
) are given by

\frac{2\pi i}{3(a^{3}-b^{3})}\Biggl[-(\log a+5i\delta)^{k+1}(a\omega^{2}-b\omega)-(\log a+i\delta)^{k+1}(a\omega-b\omega^{2})\\
+(\log b+5i\delta)^{k+1}(\omega^{2}-1)(b\omega+a)+(\log b+i\delta)^{k+1}(\omega-1)(b\omega^{2}+a)\Biggr]\\
+\frac{4\pi i(\log a+\pi i)^{k+1}}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}.

Again, we collect the purely imaginary terms in the above expression

(31)

\frac{2\pi i}{6(a^{3}-b^{3})}\Biggl[(a-b)\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}{{k+1}\choose{j}}i^{j}\delta^{j}(5^{j}+1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-3\log^{k+1-j}b)\\
-\sqrt{3}(a+b)\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}{{k+1}\choose{j}}i^{j-1}\delta^{j}(5^{j}-1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-\log^{k+1-j}b)\Biggr]\\
+\frac{4\pi i}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}{{k+1}\choose{j}}i^{j}\pi^{j}\log^{k+1-j}a.

Therefore, by equating (
30
) and (
31
), we get

(32)

g_{2}(k)=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j>1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}{{k+1}\choose j}(3\theta)^{j-1}g_{2}(k+1-j)\\
-\frac{1}{6(k+1)(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}\delta^{j}{{k+1}\choose{j}}(5^{j}+1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-3\log^{k+1-j}b)\right]\\
+\frac{a+b}{2\sqrt{3}(k+1)(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j-1}{2}}\delta^{j}{{k+1}\choose{j}}(5^{j}-1)(\log^{k+1-j}a-\log^{k+1-j}b)\right]\\
-\frac{2}{3(k+1)(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}{{k+1}\choose{j}}\pi^{j}\log^{k+1-j}a\right].

Once again, owing to the presence of an additional sum in (
32
),we define another family of polynomials with rational coefficients, defined by the recursion:

Z_{k}(x)=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{j>1,\text{ odd}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j+1}{2}}{{k+1}\choose j}6^{j-1}Z_{k+1-j}(x)\\
-\frac{2}{(k+1)}\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{k+1}(-1)^{\frac{j}{2}}{{k+1}\choose{j}}3^{j}x^{k+1-j}.

Here are the first few examples of the polynomial
Z_{k}(x)
:

\begin{array}[]{|c|}\hline\cr Z_{k}(x)\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Z_{0}(x)=-2x\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Z_{1}(x)=-x^{2}+9\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Z_{2}(x)=-\frac{2x^{3}}{3}-6x\\
\hline\cr\displaystyle Z_{3}(x)=-\frac{x^{4}}{2}-9x^{2}+\frac{567}{4}.\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Finally, using this and the polynomials
P_{k}
and
Q_{k}
defined in (
19
) and (
20
) respectively, we have

(33)

g_{2}(k)=\frac{\delta^{k+1}}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[-P_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\delta}\right)+3P_{k}\left(\frac{\log b}{\delta}\right)+Z_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\delta}\right)\right]\\
+\frac{\delta^{k+1}(a+b)}{\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log a}{\delta}\right)-Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log b}{\delta}\right)\right].

3.5.
Dependence on the sign of
a
and
b

We end this section with a discussion on the sums of the integrals
f_{1}(k)
and
f_{2}(k)
as well as
g_{1}(k)
and
g_{2}(k)
. We also investigate how these integrals depend on the sign of the parameters
a
and
b
.

First, we note that the results for integrals
f_{1}(k)
and
f_{2}(k)
from equations (
15
) and (
21
) respectively, only work when both the parameters
a
and
b
are positive. Consider the sum
f_{1}(k)+f_{2}(k)
given by

(34)

\displaystyle f_{1}(k)+f_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}+\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}-at+a^{2})(t^{2}-bt+b^{2})}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2t\log^{k}t\cdot(t^{4}+(a^{2}+b^{2}+ab)t^{2}+a^{2}b^{2})}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}-at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})(t^{2}-bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t.

We already know this integral evaluates to the sum of the equations (
15
) and (
21
) (corresponding to
f_{1}(k)
and
f_{2}(k)
respectively). However, we may also use the same procedure of computing a contour integral of the form

\oint_{C}\frac{2z\log^{k+1}z(z^{4}+(a^{2}+b^{2}+ab)z^{2}+a^{2}b^{2})}{(z^{2}+az+a^{2})(z^{2}-az+a^{2})(z^{2}+bz+b^{2})(z^{2}-bz+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}z,

with an appropriate keyhole contour
C
, as done in Sections
3.1
and
3.2
to compute the integrals
f_{1}(k)
and
f_{2}(k)
. In this case, the residues are obtained at the poles of the integrand which will be located at
z=\pm a\omega,\pm a\omega^{2},\pm b\omega,\pm b\omega^{2}
. We remark that the location of these poles does not change on changing the sign of
a
or
b
from positive to negative. Therefore, the evaluation of the residues and the entire contour integration remains the same even if
a
or
b
is negative. This means that the expression for
f_{1}(k)+f_{2}(k)
is the same if
a
or
b
is negative, with the understanding that
\log a
and
\log b
would be replaced by
\log|a|
and
\log|b|
respectively. A similar argument shows that the same is true for the expression for

(35)

\displaystyle g_{1}(k)+g_{2}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t(t+a)\log^{k}t}{(t^{3}-a^{3})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}+\frac{t(t-a)\log^{k}t}{(t^{3}+a^{3})(t^{2}-bt+b^{2})}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t,

obtained by adding equations (
29
) and (
33
) (corresponding to
g_{1}(k)
and
g_{2}(k)
respectively).

Thus, despite the results for
f_{1}(k),f_{2}(k),g_{1}(k)
and
g_{2}(k)
being individually valid only for
a,b>0
, the following expression for the sum
f_{1}(k)+f_{2}(k)
,

\displaystyle f_{1}(k)+f_{2}(k)=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{k+1}(a+b)}{a^{3}-b^{3}}\Bigg[R_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\theta}\right)-R_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\theta}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta^{k+1}}{\sqrt{3}(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\Bigg[S_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\theta}\right)+S_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\theta}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{k+1}(a+b)}{a^{3}-b^{3}}\Bigg[P_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\delta}\right)-P_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\delta}\right)\Bigg]

(36)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{k+1}}{\sqrt{3}(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\Bigg[Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\delta}\right)+Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\delta}\right)\Bigg],

is valid for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}
, and so is the expression for the sum
g_{1}(k)+g_{2}(k)
,

\displaystyle g_{1}(k)+g_{2}(k)=

\displaystyle\frac{\theta^{k+1}}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[-R_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\theta}\right)+3R_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\theta}\right)+Y_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\theta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\theta^{k+1}(a+b)}{\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[S_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\theta}\right)-S_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\theta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{k+1}}{3(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\left[-P_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\delta}\right)+3P_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\delta}\right)+Z_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\delta}\right)\right]

(37)

\displaystyle+\frac{\delta^{k+1}(a+b)}{\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log|a|}{\delta}\right)-Q_{k}\left(\frac{\log|b|}{\delta}\right)\right],

where, as before,
\theta=2\pi/3
and
\delta=\pi/3
.

3.6.
An alternate method

In this section, we provide an alternate method to calculate the integrals
f_{1}(k),f_{2}(k),g_{1}(k)
and
g_{2}(k)
evaluated in the previous sections. The advantage of the following method is that it sheds light on the explicit form of the polynomials
R_{k},S_{k},P_{k},Q_{k},Y_{k}
and
Z_{k}
and we obtain a non-recursive expression. This section serves as an analogue of
[
12
, Lemma 5.2]
to Lalín’s result in
[
11
]
.

3.6.1.
The integral
f_{1}(k)

Consider the integral

I_{1}(\beta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{\beta}}{(x^{2}+ax+a^{2})(x^{2}+bx+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}x,

where
a,b>0
are real numbers, and let
k
be any non-negative integer. The value of the
k^{\text{th}}
-derivative
I_{1}^{(k)}(\beta)
,with respect to
\beta
, gives the value of the integral

f_{1}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t,

under the limit
\beta\to 1
.

Integral
I_{1}(\beta)
can be evaluated using residue theorems (see
[
1
, Chapter 4, Section 5.3.4]
or
[
17
, Remark 2, p.129]
). The residues are calculated at
x=\omega a,\overline{\omega}a,\omega b,\overline{\omega}b
, where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
. For instance, the residue at
x=\omega a
is given by

\frac{(\omega a)^{\beta-1}}{(a-b)(\omega-\overline{\omega})(\omega a-\overline{\omega}b)}.

Then, using residue theorems,
I_{1}(\beta)
is given by the sum of residues multiplied by a factor of
\frac{2\pi i}{1-e^{2\pi i\beta}}
, which gives

(38)

I_{1}(\beta)=\frac{2\pi i}{1-e^{2\pi i\beta}}\cdot\frac{1}{(a-b)(\sqrt{3}i)}\cdot\left[\frac{(a^{\beta}-b^{\beta})\omega^{\beta-2}(1-\omega^{\beta-2})-ab(a^{\beta-2}-b^{\beta-2})\omega^{\beta}(1-\omega^{\beta})}{a^{2}+ab+b^{2}}\right].

Using the factorization of
(1-x^{3})
, we have

1-e^{2\pi i\beta}=(1-\omega^{\beta})(1-\omega^{\beta-2})(1-\omega^{\beta+2}),

and using this to simplify (
38
), we get

I_{1}(\beta)=\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[\frac{(a^{\beta}-b^{\beta})\omega^{\beta-2}}{(1-\omega^{\beta})(1-\omega^{\beta+2})}-\frac{ab(a^{\beta-2}-b^{\beta-2})\omega^{\beta}}{(1-\omega^{\beta-2})(1-\omega^{\beta+2})}\right].

We may rewrite this as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{(a^{\beta}-b^{\beta})\omega^{\beta-2}}{(1-\omega^{\beta})(1-\omega^{\beta+2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{\beta}-b^{\beta}}{(\omega^{-\beta/2}-\omega^{\beta/2})(\omega^{-\beta/2-1}-\omega^{\beta/2+1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{\beta}-b^{\beta}}{-4\sin(\theta{\frac{\beta}{2}})\sin(\theta({\frac{\beta}{2}+1}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{\beta}-a^{\beta}}{4}\csc\left(\theta{\frac{\beta}{2}}\right)\csc\left(\theta\frac{\beta}{2}+\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{a^{\beta}-b^{\beta}}{4}\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)+\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right),

where
\theta=2\pi/3
, and we have used
\csc\left(\theta\frac{\beta}{2}+\theta\right)=-\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{ab(a^{\beta-2}-b^{\beta-2})\omega^{\beta}}{(1-\omega^{\beta-2})(1-\omega^{\beta+2})}

\displaystyle=\frac{ab(a^{\beta-2}-b^{\beta-2})}{(\omega^{-\beta/2+1}-\omega^{\beta/2-1})(\omega^{-\beta/2-1}-\omega^{\beta/2+1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{ab(a^{\beta-2}-b^{\beta-2})}{-4\sin(\theta(\frac{\beta}{2}-1))\sin(\theta(\frac{\beta}{2}+1))}

\displaystyle=\frac{ba^{\beta-1}-ab^{\beta-1}}{-4}\csc\left(\theta\frac{\beta}{2}-\theta\right)\csc\left(\theta\frac{\beta}{2}+\theta\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{ba^{\beta-1}-ab^{\beta-1}}{4}\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right).

Thus,

(39)

I_{1}(\beta)=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\left[(a^{\beta}-b^{\beta})\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)+\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)\right.\\
\left.-(ba^{\beta-1}-ab^{\beta-1})\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)\right].

We wish to write the power series expansion of
I_{1}(\beta)
in a neighbourhood of
\beta=1
. Note that the function
\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)
has a simple pole at
\beta=1
, while all the other functions are holomorphic in a neighbourhood of
\beta=1
. Also observe that if we write the Laurent series expansion around
\beta=1
,

(40)

\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)=\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}v_{j}\theta^{j}(\beta-1)^{j},

with
v_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, then we have

\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)+\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)=\sum_{j=-1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}v_{j}\theta^{j}(\beta-1)^{j}.

Now, the power series expansion of
a^{\beta-1}
is given by

a^{\beta-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{j}a}{j!}(\beta-1)^{j}.

This means

a^{\beta-1}\cdot\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{\theta}{2}(\beta-1)\right)=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\frac{\log^{j}a}{j!}\,v_{k-j}\theta^{k-j}\right)(\beta-1)^{k}.

Using this idea, we may write

I_{1}(\beta)=\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}w_{k}(\beta-1)^{k},

where

w_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}(a\log^{j}a-b\log^{j}b)-b\log^{j}a+a\log^{j}b}{j!}v_{k-j}\theta^{k-j}.

We note that
w_{-1}=0
, which means
I_{1}(\beta)
is a holomorphic function around
\beta=1
. For
k\geq 0
,
w_{k}
represents the
k^{\text{th}}
-derivative of
I_{1}(\beta)
at
\beta=1
, which tells us that

f_{1}(k)=I_{1}^{(k)}(1)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t\log^{k}t}{(t^{2}+at+a^{2})(t^{2}+bt+b^{2})}\;\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\pi(k!)}{2\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}w_{k}.

We can rewrite
w_{k}
as follows:

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\frac{(-1)^{k-j}(a\log^{j}a-b\log^{j}b)-b\log^{j}a+a\log^{j}b}{j!}v_{k-j}\theta^{k-j}

\displaystyle=\theta^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\left[(a-(-1)^{k-j}b)\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)^{j}-(b-(-1)^{k-j}a)\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{v_{k-j}}{j!}.

Suppose
k=2n
is even. Then, splitting the cases where
j
is even or odd, we obtain

w_{2n}=\theta^{2n}(a-b)\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{2n+1}\left[\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)^{j}+\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{v_{2n-j}}{j!}\\
+\theta^{2n}(a+b)\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{2n+1}\left[\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)^{j}-\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{v_{2n-j}}{j!}.

Recall that the coefficients
v_{j}
are obtained via the power series expansion (
40
), and moreover, for any real number
x
, the hyperbolic functions
\sinh(x(\beta-1))
and
\cosh(x(\beta-1))
are given by

\sinh(x(\beta-1))=\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}(\beta-1)^{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\cosh(x(\beta-1))=\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}(\beta-1)^{j},

around
\beta=1
.
This means that the sums

\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{2n+1}v_{2n-j}\cdot\frac{x^{j}}{j!}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{2n+1}v_{2n-j}\cdot\frac{x^{j}}{j!}.

represent the
(2n)^{\text{th}}
-coefficients of

\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\sinh(xT)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\cosh(xT),

respectively. We write

(41)

\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\sinh(xT)=\sum_{m=-1}^{\infty}A_{m}(x)\frac{T^{m}}{m!},

and

(42)

\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\cosh(xT)=\sum_{m=-1}^{\infty}B_{m}(x)\frac{T^{m}}{m!},

where
A_{m}(x)
and
B_{m}(x)
are polynomials in
x
with real coefficients. Then,

\displaystyle f_{1}(2n)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi(2n)!}{2\sqrt{3}(a^{3}-b^{3})}w_{2n}

(43)

\displaystyle=\frac{\theta^{2n+1}(a+b)}{a^{3}-b^{3}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left[-A_{2n}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)+A_{2n}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\theta^{2n+1}}{\sqrt{3}(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\cdot\frac{3}{4}\left[B_{2n}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)+B_{2n}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)\right]

Similarly, if
k=2n+1
is odd, then

w_{2n+1}=\theta^{2n+1}(a+b)\sum_{j=0,\text{ even}}^{2n+2}\left[\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)^{j}-\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{v_{2n+1-j}}{j!}\\
+\theta^{2n+1}(a-b)\sum_{j=1,\text{ odd}}^{2n+2}\left[\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)^{j}+\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)^{j}\right]\frac{v_{2n+1-j}}{j!},

and

(44)

f_{1}(2n+1)=\frac{\theta^{2n+2}(a+b)}{a^{3}-b^{3}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\left[-B_{2n+1}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)+B_{2n+1}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)\right]\\
+\frac{\theta^{2n+2}}{\sqrt{3}(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})}\cdot\frac{3}{4}\left[A_{2n+1}\left(\frac{\log b}{\theta}\right)+A_{2n+1}\left(\frac{\log a}{\theta}\right)\right].

Recall that the polynomials
A_{m}(x)
and
B_{m}(x)
are obtained as coefficients of the functions
\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\sinh(xT)
and
\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}\right)\cosh(xT)
, and can be computed explicitly using standard convolution techniques. We have the following Taylor series in the variable
T
around the point
T=0
:

(45)

\sinh(xT)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2j+1}}{(2j+1)!}\;T^{2j+1},

and

(46)

both of which consist of odd powers of
T
. Here
\mathscr{B}_{j}
denotes the
j^{\text{th}}
-Bernoulli number, defined as coefficients of the Taylor series

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathscr{B}_{j}x^{j}}{j!}.

For
\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)

(47)

\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n}}{n!}\;T^{n},

where
\mu_{n}
is the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative evaluated at zero. We use Faà di Bruno’s formula to calculate the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative. Recall that if
f(x)
and
g(x)
are functions for which all necessary derivatives defined, then the
n^{\text{th}}
derivative of

is given by

\frac{\mathrm{d^{n}}}{\mathrm{dx^{n}}}\;f(g(x))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{partitions}\\
\text{of $n$}\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}f^{(k)}(g(x))\left(\frac{g^{\prime}(x)}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{g^{\prime\prime}(x)}{2!}\right)^{k_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{g^{(n)}(x)}{n!}\right)^{k_{n}},

where the sum runs over the
n
-tuples of non-negative integers
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{n})
satisfying
k_{1}+2k_{2}+3k_{3}+\cdots+nk_{n}=n
with
k:=k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n}
. In other words, the sum is over all partitions of
n
with
k_{j}
denoting the number of blocks of length
j
. Additionally, we define the following sums:

\epsilon:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\text{$j$ odd}\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{j},\quad\text{and}\quad\xi:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\text{$j\equiv 0,-1{\text{\rm\ (mod~$4$)}}$}\end{subarray}}^{n}k_{j}.

Taking

f(x)=x^{-1}\quad\text{ and}\quad g(x)=\sin\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right),

we obtain

f(g(T))=\left[\sin\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]^{-1}=\csc\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{n}}{n!}\;T^{n}.

Using Faà di Bruno’s formula we can write the

\displaystyle\mu_{n}

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\mathrm{d^{n}}}{\mathrm{dT^{n}}}\;\left[\sin\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right]^{-1}\right|_{T=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{partitions}\\
\text{of $n$}\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{\sin^{k+1}\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}\right]\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\cos\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{\frac{-1}{4}\sin\left(-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}{2!}\right)^{k_{2}}\cdots\left(\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d^{n}}}{\mathrm{dT^{n}}}\left.\sin\left(\frac{T}{2}-\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\right|_{T=0}}{n!}\right)^{k_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{partitions}\\
\text{of $n$}\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{k+1}}\right]\,\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2^{2}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{2!}\right)^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{-\frac{1}{2^{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}}{3!}\right)^{k_{3}}\left(\frac{-\frac{1}{2^{4}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{4!}\right)^{k_{4}}\cdots

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{partitions}\\
\text{of $n$}\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k}k!}{\left(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)^{k+1}}\right]\,\frac{(-1)^{k_{3}+k_{4}+k_{7}+k_{8}+\cdots}}{2^{k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+nk_{n}}}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{1!}\right)^{k_{1}}\left(\frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{2!}\right)^{k_{2}}\left(\frac{\frac{1}{2}}{3!}\right)^{k_{3}}\left(\frac{\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}{4!}\right)^{k_{4}}\cdots

\text{of $n$}\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!\cdots k_{n}!}\left[\frac{(-1)2^{k+1}k!}{(\sqrt{3})^{k+1}}\right]\,\frac{(-1)^{\xi}}{2^{n}}\cdot\frac{1}{2^{k}}\cdot\frac{(\sqrt{3})^{k_{2}+k_{4}+k_{6}+\cdots}}{(1!)^{k_{1}}(2!)^{k_{2}}\cdots(n!)^{k_{n}}}

are of the form

Now, consider the rational function

G_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y)=y+\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right),

is the complex conjugate of

. Making the transformation

for each variable

, shows that the Mahler measure of

is exactly the same as that of

, since these transformations do not affect the Mahler measure. Thus, we have

\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})=\mathrm{m}(G_{n}).

We shall evaluate the Mahler measure of

by evaluating the Mahler measure of
G_{n}
.

Note that using Jensen’s formula (see
[

), the Mahler measure of the polynomial

is given by
\log^{+}|\gamma|:=\log\max\{1,|\gamma|\}
. Thus, we may write the integral defining the Mahler measure of

as

\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})=\mathrm{m}(G_{n})=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cdots\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\mathrm{m}\left(P_{\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)}\right)\;\mathrm{d}\theta_{1}\cdots\;\mathrm{d}\theta_{n},

where
z_{j}=e^{i\theta_{j}}.
Let

(60)

\tan\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}=\frac{x_{j}-\cos\frac{2\pi}{m}}{\sin\frac{2\pi}{m}},

so that

x_{j}=\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{j}+\omega}{z_{j}+1}.

Differentiating equation (

\displaystyle\left(1+\tan^{2}\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\right)\frac{\;\mathrm{d}\theta_{j}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\;\mathrm{d}x_{j}}{\sin\frac{2\pi}{m}},

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\;\mathrm{d}{\theta_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sin\frac{2\pi}{m}}{x_{j}^{2}-2\cos\frac{2\pi}{m}x_{j}+1}\;\mathrm{d}x_{j}

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{2\sin\frac{2\pi}{m}}{(x_{j}-\omega)(x_{j}-\overline{\omega})}\;\mathrm{d}x_{j}.

Q_{n}
is given by

\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})=\left(\frac{\sin\frac{2\pi}{m}}{\pi}\right)^{n}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}})\frac{\;\mathrm{d}x_{1}}{(x_{1}-\omega)(x_{1}-\overline{\omega})}\cdots\frac{\;\mathrm{d}x_{n}}{(x_{n}-\omega)(x_{n}-\overline{\omega})}.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{y_{k}+1}{y_{k}^{3}-1}\log^{2h-1}|y_{k}|\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}=\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\,\frac{1+t}{1-t^{3}}\;\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\,\frac{1-t}{1+t^{3}}\;\mathrm{d}t,

and

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{\log^{2h}|y_{k}|}{y_{k}^{2}+y_{k}+1}\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log^{2h+1}t}{t^{2}+t+1}\;\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log^{2h+1}t}{t^{2}-t+1}\;\mathrm{d}t.

Using the following expansions

\displaystyle\frac{1+t}{1-t^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{1-t}+\frac{1}{t-\omega}+\frac{1}{t-\omega^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1-t}{1+t^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\left(\frac{2}{t+1}-\frac{1}{t+\omega}-\frac{1}{t+\omega^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}+t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{1}{t-\omega}-\frac{1}{t-\omega^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t^{2}-t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{3}}\left(\frac{1}{t+\omega^{2}}-\frac{1}{t+\omega}\right),

where
\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}
is a third root of unity, we may write the Mahler measure in terms of hyperlogarithms as follows.
We can write

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{y_{k}+1}{y_{k}^{3}-1}\log^{2h-1}|y_{k}|\;\mathrm{d}y_{k}=\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\left(-\frac{2}{t-1}+\frac{1}{t-\omega}+\frac{1}{t-\omega^{2}}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t\\
+\frac{1}{3}\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\left(\frac{2}{t+1}-\frac{1}{t+\omega}-\frac{1}{t+\omega^{2}}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t.

Using the definition of hyperlogarithms as given in Section
2
and identity (
4
), for
a\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h}t\,\frac{1}{t-a}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2h}(2h)!\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a}\circ\overbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}^{\text{$2h$ times}}

\displaystyle=(2h)!\cdot\mathrm{I}_{2h+1}(a,1)=-(2h)!\,\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(1/a).

This gives, for
h\geq 1
,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{y_{k}+1}{y_{k}^{3}-1}\log^{2h-1}|y_{k}|\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}=-\frac{(2h)!}{3}\Big(-2\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(1)+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega^{2})+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega)\\
+2\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-1)-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-\omega^{2})-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-\omega)\Big).

We have the following identities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(1)

\displaystyle=\zeta(2h+1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-1)

\displaystyle=-\zeta(2h+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{4^{h}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega)+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega^{2})

\displaystyle=-\zeta(2h+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{9^{h}}\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-\omega)+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(-\omega^{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta(2h+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{9^{h}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{4^{h}}\right).

We will show the third identity, since the rest can be proved in a similar manner. Since
2h+1\geq 3
, the sum

\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n^{2h+1}}

converges absolutely for
|z|\leq 1
and we are free to change the order of the terms. We write

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega)+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+1}(\omega^{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega^{j}+\omega^{2j}}{j^{2h+1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\cos(2\pi j/3)}{j^{2h+1}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1^{2h+1}}-\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}+\frac{2}{3^{2h+1}}-\frac{1}{4^{2h+1}}-\cdots

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{1^{2h+1}}+\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}+\frac{1}{3^{2h+1}}+\cdots\right)+3\left(\frac{1}{3^{2h+1}}+\frac{1}{6^{2h+1}}+\frac{1}{9^{2h+1}}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle=-\zeta(2h+1)+\frac{1}{3^{2h}}\cdot\zeta(2h+1)

\displaystyle=-\zeta(2h+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{9^{h}}\right),

as desired.

Using these identities, we obtain

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{y_{k}+1}{y_{k}^{3}-1}\log^{2h-1}|y_{k}|\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}=2(2h)!\zeta(2h+1)\left(1-\frac{1}{3^{2h+1}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}\right).

Similarly, we have for
h\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{\log^{2h}|y_{k}|}{y_{k}^{2}+y_{k}+1}\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{i\sqrt{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h+1}t\left(\frac{1}{t-\omega}-\frac{1}{t-\omega^{2}}+\frac{1}{t+\omega^{2}}-\frac{1}{t+\omega}\right)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2h+1)!}{i\sqrt{3}}\Big(\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(\omega^{2})-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(\omega)+\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(-\omega)-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(-\omega^{2})\Big),

where we use

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\log^{2h+1}t\,\frac{1}{t-a}\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=(-1)^{2h+1}(2h+1)!\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t-a}\circ\overbrace{\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}\circ\cdots\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}t}{t}}^{\text{$2h+1$ times}}

\displaystyle=-(2h+1)!\,\mathrm{I}_{2h+2}(a,1)=(2h+1)!\,\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(1/a).

In this case, we will use the following identities

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(\omega^{2})-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{3}iL(\chi_{-3},2h+2),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(-\omega)-\mathrm{Li}_{2h+2}(-\omega^{2})

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{3}i\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{2h+1}}\right)L(\chi_{-3},2h+2).

This means

(84)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{k}})\frac{\log^{2h}|y_{k}|}{y_{k}^{2}+y_{k}+1}\;\mathrm{d}{y_{k}}=2(2h+1)!L(\chi_{-3},2h+2)\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{2h+2}}\right).

Therefore, plugging the above relations into equations (

+\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}b_{n,h}\;\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)^{2n-2h-1}2(2h+1)!L(\chi_{-3},2h+2)\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{2h+2}}\right),

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=h}^{n}a_{n,l-1}(2^{2l-2h}r_{2l-1,h}+p_{2l-1,h})+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{l=h}^{n-1}b_{n,l}(2^{2l-2h+1}s_{2l,h}+q_{2l,h}).

Finally, to complete the recursive formula, we must calculate initial values. To do this, we evaluate the Mahler measure of the first polynomial in the family as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(Q_{1})=\mathrm{m}\left(y+\left(\frac{\overline{\omega}z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\cdot F(1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{m}(P_{y_{1}})\frac{\;\mathrm{d}{y_{1}}}{y_{1}^{2}+y_{1}+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\cdot 2\cdot L(\chi_{-3},2)\left(1+\frac{1}{2^{2}}\right)=\frac{5\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}\,L(\chi_{-3},2),

using equation (

). This means that we can set
d_{0,0}=1
. From this base value, we can obtain all subsequent values of
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s},d_{r,s}
using equations (

)-(

) above, with appropriate choices of

and

. For example, with

in (

) and
n=0
in (

a_{1,0}=2
and
b_{1,0}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}
respectively, giving us the Mahler measure of
Q_{2}
:

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2})=\frac{91}{18\pi^{2}}\zeta(3)+\frac{5}{4\sqrt{3}\pi}\,L(\chi_{-3},2).

One can also confirm that these values of
a_{1,0}
and
b_{1,0}
agree with the results of Section

The final Mahler measure

Proof of Theorem

.

Observe that from the definition of

given in (

\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})=\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\right)^{n}\cdot F(n).

(97)

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2n})=\frac{2}{12^{n}}\Bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{n}a_{n,h-1}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h}\mathcal{C}(h)\;+\;\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}b_{n,h}\left(\frac{3}{\pi}\right)^{2h+1}\mathcal{D}(h)\Bigg),

4.5.
More on the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}

In this section, we show that the coefficients
a_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
are always rational, whereas the coefficients
b_{r,s}
and
c_{r,s}
are always a rational number multiplied by
\sqrt{3}
. This lets us express the Mahler measure
\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})
as a sum of rational multiples of derivatives of the Riemann zeta function and the
L
-function
L(\chi_{-3},s)
evaluated at certain negative integers.

Proposition 4
.

Let
n\geq 0
be an integer. Then the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
appearing in the recursive expressions (
91
)-(
96
) satisfy

a_{n,j},d_{n,k}\in\mathbb{Q},

and

b_{n,j},c_{n,j}\in\sqrt{3}\cdot\mathbb{Q},

for all integers
0\leq j\leq n-1
and
0\leq k\leq n
.

Remark 5
.

Following the discussions of Section
3.6
and Proposition
2
, we can obtain an alternate expression for the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
using polynomials that can be calculated explicitly (for instance, the polynomials
A_{m}(x)
and
B_{m}(x)
in equations (
49
) and (
50
) respectively). Since these polynomials have coefficients that are rational multiples of integer powers of
\sqrt{3}
, we can immediately deduce that
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
lie in
\mathbb{Q}\cup\sqrt{3}\cdot\mathbb{Q}
. Proposition
4
makes this observation more precise.

Proof of Proposition.

We begin with the numbers
a_{r,s}
and
b_{r,s}
and proceed by induction. The base case is when
a_{1,0}=2
, which is clearly rational, and
b_{1,0}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{3}
, a rational multiple of
\sqrt{3}
, confirming the hypothesis for these cases. Now assume that for all
1\leq n\leq k
and
0\leq j\leq n-1
, we have

a_{n,j}\in\mathbb{Q}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{n,j}\in\sqrt{3}\cdot\mathbb{Q}.

We consider
a_{n+1,j}
and
b_{n+1,j}
for
0\leq j\leq n
. It can be observed that in each of the expressions (
91
)-(
96
), the numbers
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
that appear inside the summations are multiplied by certain expressions involving rational coefficients of the polynomials
R_{k},S_{k},P_{k},Q_{k},Y_{k}
and
Z_{k}
. For example, we have the terms

(2^{2l-2h+1}s_{2l-1,h}+q_{2l-1,h})\quad\text{and}\quad(2^{2l-2h+2}r_{2l,h}+p_{2l,h}),

in the two summations in equation (
91
). These expressions are always rational, and for ease of notation, we will denote them by the symbol
\Delta\in\mathbb{Q}
everywhere. Note that we may replace the expressions for the coefficients
c_{r,s}
(from (
94
)), and for
d_{r,s}
(from (
95
) and (
96
)) back into the expression for
a_{r,s}
in (
91
). This lets us write
a_{n+1,j}
as a linear combination of
a_{n,k}
and
b_{n,k}
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
as follows,

(99)

a_{n+1,j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{i=l}^{n}a_{n,i-1}(\Delta)+\sum_{i=l-1}^{n-1}b_{n,i}(\Delta)\right)\\
+\sum_{l=j}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=l}^{n}a_{n,i-1}(\Delta)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{i=l}^{n-1}b_{n,i}(\Delta)\right),

where, as discussed above, we combine all obviously rational expressions and replace them by the symbol
\Delta
. Distributing the products, this can be rewritten as

(100)

a_{n+1,j}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=l}^{n}a_{n,i-1}(\Delta)\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{l=j+1}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=l-1}^{n-1}b_{n,i}(\Delta)\right)\\
+\sum_{l=j}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=l}^{n}a_{n,i-1}(\Delta)\right)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sum_{l=j}^{n}(\Delta)\cdot\left(\sum_{i=l}^{n-1}b_{n,i}(\Delta)\right).

Note that all sums involving the numbers
a_{n,i-1}
have rational coefficients, whereas all sums involving
b_{n,i}
have rational multiples of
\sqrt{3}
. Using the induction hypothesis, this means that
a_{n+1,j}\in\mathbb{Q}
, confirming the statement for
a_{r,s}
. A similar argument will show that
b_{n+1,j}\in\sqrt{3}\cdot\mathbb{Q}
, confirming the statement for
b_{r,s}
and completing the induction procedure. The same process can be applied to the numbers
c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
to conclude the proof of the proposition.
∎

Following this proposition, we can rewrite the expressions for the Mahler measures in equations (
97
) and (
98
). First, using the functional equation of the Riemann zeta function, we can express its derivative at negative even integers in terms of its value at positive odd integers as follows

(101)

\zeta^{\prime}(-2h)=\frac{(-1)^{h}(2h)!}{2^{2h+1}}\cdot\frac{\zeta(2h+1)}{\pi^{2h}}.

Similarly, we can express derivatives of Dirichlet
L
-functions at negative odd integers in terms of its values at positive even integers. Indeed, the functional equation of the Dirichlet
L
-function
L(\chi,s)
associated to an odd, primitive, non-principal Dirichlet character
\chi
of conductor
q
is given by (see
[
18
, Corollary 10.9]
)

L(\chi,1-s)=2\left(\frac{q}{2\pi}\right)^{s}\,\frac{\tau(\chi)}{iq}\,\Gamma(s)\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)L(\overline{\chi},s),

where
\Gamma(s)
is the Gamma function and
\tau(\chi)
is the Gauss sum of
\chi
. Note that all functions appearing on the right-hand side are holomorphic on
\mathrm{Re}(s)>0
, and the function
\sin\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)
has zeros at even integers. Thus, taking derivatives on both sides of the functional equation, and evaluating at
s=2n
for a positive integer
n
, we obtain

-L^{\prime}(\chi,1-2n)=\pi\left(\frac{q}{2\pi}\right)^{2n}\,\frac{\tau(\chi)}{iq}\,\Gamma(2n)\cos\left(\frac{\pi s}{2}\right)L(\overline{\chi},2n).

Taking
\chi=\chi_{-3}
so that
\overline{\chi}=\chi,\;q=3
and
\tau(\chi)=\sqrt{3}i
, we finally get

(102)

L^{\prime}(\chi_{-3},1-2n)=\frac{(-1)^{n+1}(2n-1)!\,3^{2n}}{2^{2n}\sqrt{3}}\cdot\frac{L(\chi_{-3},2n)}{\pi^{2n-1}}.

Plugging in (
101
) and (
102
) into equations (
97
) and (
98
), we obtain for
n\geq 1
,

(103)

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2n})=\frac{2}{12^{n}}\Bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{h}a_{n,h-1}}{3}\,(3^{2h+1}-1)(2^{2h+1}-1)\zeta^{\prime}(-2h)\\
+\sum_{h=0}^{n-1}\frac{(-1)^{h}b_{n,h}}{\sqrt{3}}\,(2^{2h+2}+1)L^{\prime}(\chi_{-3},-2h-1)\Bigg),

and for
n\geq 0
we have

(104)

\mathrm{m}(Q_{2n+1})=\frac{1}{12^{n}}\Bigg(\sum_{h=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{h}c_{n,h-1}}{3\sqrt{3}}\,(3^{2h+1}-1)(2^{2h+1}-1)\zeta^{\prime}(-2h)\\
+\sum_{h=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{h}d_{n,h}}{3}\,(2^{2h+2}+1)L^{\prime}(\chi_{-3},-2h-1)\Bigg),

where, as a result of Proposition
4
,
a_{r,s},d_{r,s}\in\mathbb{Q}
while
b_{r,s},c_{r,s}\in\sqrt{3}\cdot\mathbb{Q}
. Thus, the Mahler measure
\mathrm{m}(Q_{n})
is always a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of derivatives of the Riemann zeta function evaluated at negative even integers and derivatives of the
L
-function
L(\chi_{-3},s)
evaluated at negative odd integers. The first few examples of the Mahler measure computation have been listed in Table

5.
Conclusion

These results show that the techniques employed in
[
11
]
can be extended to a more general family of polynomials. It is interesting to note that our formulae have a combination of zeta-values and
L
-values corresponding to the Dirichlet character of conductor 3 in both the odd and even cases. The appearance of
L(\chi_{-3},s)
is due to the evaluation of polylogarithms at the third and sixth root of unity, as opposed to the fourth root of unity in
[
11
]
which yields values of
L(\chi_{-4},s)
. This motivates the question of whether these techniques can be further generalized to higher roots of unity to obtain
L
-values corresponding to higher conductors. The integrals involving powers of the logarithm as seen in Section
3
and the limits of the integrals in the iteration step discussed in
4
give rise to some complicated calculations. It would be interesting to see if these calculations can be captured in a more general setting. In Section
3.6
, we saw an alternate method to evaluate integrals
f_{1}(m),f_{2}(m),g_{1}(m)
and
g_{2}(m)
. This method resulted in polynomials that have explicit expressions, as compared to the recursive polynomials that arose from the first method. While the explicit expressions for the first two polynomials was obtained, it would be worthwhile to use the same techniques and obtain such expressions for all the remaining polynomials. Another intriguing question would be to investigate whether these explicit expressions enable us to write the coefficients
a_{r,s}\,,b_{r,s}\,,c_{r,s}
and
d_{r,s}
in an elegant closed formula, like the coefficients that appear in
[
11
]
.

In Section
3
, we used the fact that the polynomial
P_{\gamma}(y)
has Mahler measure
\log^{+}|\gamma|
, and is thus dependent only on the absolute value of
\gamma
. In
[
11
]
, other families of polynomials were also studied – those that stemmed from simpler polynomials (like
P_{\gamma}(y)
) whose Mahler measures only depended on the absolute value of a parameter. For instance, we have the following identities:

\mathrm{m}(1+\gamma x+(1-\gamma)y)=|\mathrm{arg}\gamma|\log|1-\gamma|+|\mathrm{arg}(1-\gamma))|\cdot\log|\gamma|+\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}D(\gamma)&\text{if Im}(\gamma)\geq 0,\\
D(\overline{\gamma})&\text{if Im}(\gamma)<0,\end{array}\right.

which follow from the Cassaigne-Maillot formula (see
[
4
, Proposition 4.3]
) where
D(z)
is the Bloch-Wigner dilogarithm (see
[
25
]
); and

\mathrm{m}(1+x+\gamma(1+y)z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{3}(|\gamma|)&\text{for }|\gamma|\leq 1,\\
\log|\gamma|+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{3}(|\gamma|^{-1})&\text{for }|\gamma|>1,\\
\end{array}\right.

which was proved by Vandervelde in
[
23
]
(see also
[
10
, Theorem 17]
), where

\mathcal{L}_{3}(\gamma)=-2\int_{0}^{\gamma}\frac{\;\mathrm{d}s}{s^{2}-1}\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}s}{s}\circ\frac{\;\mathrm{d}s}{s}.

It would be worth exploring whether similar families can be constructed from such
P_{\gamma}
and their Mahler measures studied in context of our results.

Recently, the method of Lalín was extended to certain families of
n
-variable polynomials with non-linear degree in
[
14
]
, by evaluating the Mahler measure of rational functions
S_{n,r}
of the form

S_{n,r}(x,y,z,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(1+x)z+\left[\left(\frac{1-x_{1}}{1+x_{1}}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{1-x_{n}}{1+x_{n}}\right)\right]^{r}(1+y),

for any integer
r\geq 1
. Interestingly, considering such a family under the construction mentioned in this article leads to trivial evaluations. Indeed, if we let

Q_{n,r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y):=y+\left[\left(\frac{z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)\right]^{r},

then, we trivially have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(Q_{n,r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y))=

\displaystyle\mathrm{m}(Q_{n,r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},-y^{r}))=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\mathrm{m}\left(y-\xi_{r}^{j}\left(\frac{z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r\mathrm{m}(Q_{n,1}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y))=r\mathrm{m}(Q_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},y)),

where
\xi_{r}
is an
r^{\text{th}}
root of unity. Thus, the Mahler measure of this new family is simply
r
times that of the family
Q_{n}
considered in this article. However, it will be interesting to explore the following family of rational functions

T_{n,r}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},x,y):=1+\left[\left(\frac{z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)\right]^{r}x+\left(1-\left[\left(\frac{z_{1}+\omega}{z_{1}+1}\right)\cdots\left(\frac{z_{n}+\omega}{z_{n}+1}\right)\right]^{r}\right)y.

Finally, we note that in
[
13
]
, certain transformations are described which when applied on a polynomial, preserve the Mahler measure. In Section 4 of the same article, these transformations are applied on the polynomials appearing in
[
11
]
to obtain infinitely many new rational functions with the same Mahler measure. We remark that one can apply these transformations to the family of polynomials considered in Theorem
1
as well, and obtain many more polynomials with the same Mahler measure.

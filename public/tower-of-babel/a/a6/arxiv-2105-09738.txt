Superalgebra structure on differential forms of manifold
Kentaro Mikami, Tadayoshi Mizutani

Abstract




1
Introduction

Typical examples of Lie superalgebra are the direct sum of exterior power of
tangent bundle of a differential manifold with the Schouten bracket or the
matrix algebra whose elements are divided into 4 parts, and the artificial
bracket operation. The Schouten bracket in the first example is
useful to describe a 2-vector field
\pi
to be a
Poisson tensor by
[\pi,\pi]=0
.

In this article, we introduce a notion of Lie superalgebra structure on
the direct sum of exterior power of
cotangent bundle of a differential manifold with a natural bracket.
Also we introduce an extension of the Lie superalgebra structure by the
1-vector fields.

1.1
Quick review of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebras

First we recall the definition of Lie superalgebra or pre Lie
superalgebra we call sometimes.

Definition 1

(
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra)
.

Suppose a real vector space

\mathfrak{g}
is graded by
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}
as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{j}

and has a
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear operation
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{g}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}

(1.2)

\displaystyle[X,Y]+(-1)^{xy}[Y,X]=0\quad\text{ where }X\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}\text{ and }Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}

(1.3)

\displaystyle(-1)^{xz}[[X,Y],Z]+(-1)^{yx}[[Y,Z],X]+(-1)^{zy}[[Z,X],Y]=0\;.

Then we call
\mathfrak{g}
a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
or
(pre) Lie superalgebra
.

Remark 1
.

Super Jacobi identity (
1.3
) above is equivalent to the one of
the following.

(1.4)

\displaystyle[X,[Y,Z]]

\displaystyle=[[X,Y],Z]+(-1)^{xy}[Y,[X,Z]]

(1.5)

\displaystyle[[X,Y],Z]

\displaystyle=[X,[Y,Z]]+(-1)^{yz}[[X,Z],Y]

Definition 2

(Sub superalgebra)
.

For a general
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,
if
\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is a subspace of
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
for each
i
,
and satisfy
[\mathfrak{h}_{i},\mathfrak{h}_{j}]\subset\mathfrak{h}_{i+j}
,
then
\displaystyle\sum_{i}\mathfrak{h}_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie
superalgebra, we call a Lie

sub
-superalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 2
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie
superalgebra.
From definitions, we see some obvious properties.

(1)

Assume
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}\neq(0)
. Then

\displaystyle\bar{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ell}=\sum_{i>0}\mathfrak{g}_{i\ell}
or

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geqq 0}\mathfrak{g}_{i\ell}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}+\bar{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ell}

are
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie
sub
-superalgebras of

\mathfrak{g}
. If
\ell
is even, they are Lie algebras.

(2)

Fix
\ell\geqq 0
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geqq\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a

\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie
sub
-superalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(
2^{\prime}
)

Fix
\ell\leqq 0
.

\displaystyle\sum_{i\leqq\ell}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a

\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie
sub
-superalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 5
.

The direct sum

-graded Lie

Proof:

Suppose

and

, where

and

Then
[\![\alpha,\beta]\!]\in\Lambda^{1+a+b}\mathrm{T}^{*}(M)=\mathfrak{g}_{[a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}]}
because
-1-(1+a+b)=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}
.

\displaystyle[\![\beta,\alpha]\!]

\displaystyle=\beta\wedge d\alpha-(-1)^{b^{\prime}a^{\prime}}\alpha\wedge\beta=(-1)^{1+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}(\alpha\wedge d\beta-(-1)^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}\beta\wedge d\alpha)=(-1)^{1+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}[\![\alpha,\beta]\!]

\displaystyle(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}[\![\alpha,[\![\beta,\gamma]\!]]\!]

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(\alpha\wedge d([\![\beta,\gamma]\!])-(-1)^{a^{\prime}(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})}[\![\beta,\gamma]\!]\wedge d\alpha)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(0-(-1)^{a^{\prime}(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})}(\beta\wedge d\gamma-(-1)^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\gamma\wedge d\beta)\wedge d\alpha)

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(-1)^{a^{\prime}(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})}\beta\wedge d\gamma\wedge d\alpha+(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}(-1)^{a^{\prime}(b^{\prime}+c^{\prime})}(-1)^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\gamma\wedge d\beta\wedge d\alpha

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{a^{\prime}b^{\prime}}\beta\wedge d\gamma\wedge d\alpha+(-1)^{b^{\prime}c^{\prime}}\gamma\wedge d\alpha\wedge d\beta

and so

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}

\displaystyle(-1)^{a^{\prime}c^{\prime}}[\![\alpha,[\![\beta,\gamma]\!]]\!]=0\;.

∎

Homology groups of our Lie superalgebra

3.1
Quick review of homology groups of general Lie superalgebra

In a usual Lie algebra homology theory,
m
-th chain space is the
exterior product
\displaystyle\Lambda^{m}\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
and the
boundary operator essentially comes from the operation
\displaystyle X\wedge Y\mapsto[X,Y]
.

Likewise, in the case of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebras,
”exterior algebra” is defined as the quotient of the tensor
algebra
\displaystyle\otimes^{m}\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
by the two-sided
ideal generated by

(3.1)

\displaystyle X\otimes Y+(-1)^{xy}Y\otimes X\quad\text{where }\quad X\in\mathfrak{g}_{x},Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{y}\;,

and we denote the equivalence class of
\displaystyle X\otimes Y

by
\displaystyle X\bigtriangleup Y
.

Since
\displaystyle X_{\text{odd}}\bigtriangleup Y_{\text{odd}}=Y_{\text{odd}}\bigtriangleup X_{\text{odd}}
and

\displaystyle X_{\text{even}}\bigtriangleup Y_{\text{any}}=-Y_{\text{any}}\bigtriangleup X_{\text{even}}
hold,
\bigtriangleup^{m}\mathfrak{g}_{k}
has a
symmetric property for odd
k
and has a skew-symmetric property
for even
k
with respect to
\bigtriangleup
.

Definition 6
.

For a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded Lie superalgebra

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,
m
-th chain space is defined by

\text{C}_{m}=\underbrace{\mathfrak{g}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup\mathfrak{g}}_{m}

and the boundary operator is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial(A_{1}\bigtriangleup A_{2}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{m})=

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i-1+a_{i}\sum_{i<s<j}a_{s}}A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\widehat{A_{i}}\cdots\bigtriangleup[\![A_{i},A_{j}]\!]\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{m}

where
A_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}_{a_{i}}
. We see that
\partial\circ\partial=0

holds, and have the homology groups by

\textrm{H}_{m}(\mathfrak{g})=\ker(\partial:\text{C}_{m}\rightarrow\text{C}_{m-1})/\partial(\text{C}_{m+1})\;.

Definition 7

(Weight)
.

For each non-zero element
u
in
\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{i_{1}}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup\mathfrak{g}_{i_{m}}
, we define
u
has the weight

\displaystyle i_{1}+\dots+i_{m}
.
We
define the subspace of

\text{C}_{m}
by

\displaystyle\text{C}_{m,w}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leq\ldots\leq i_{m}\\
\sum_{s=1}^{m}i_{s}=w\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{g}_{i_{1}}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup\mathfrak{g}_{i_{m}}\;,

which is the space of common weight
w
.

The following is known well.

Proposition 3.1
.

The weight
w
is preserved by
\displaystyle\partial
, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\partial(\text{C}_{m,w})\subset\text{C}_{m-1,w}
. Thus,
for a fixed
w
, we have

w
-weighted homology groups

\displaystyle\textrm{H}_{m,w}(\mathfrak{g})=\ker(\partial:\text{C}_{m,w}\rightarrow\text{C}_{m-1,w})/\partial(\text{C}_{m+1,w})\;.

Since the boundary operator is defined by
(
3.2
), we see

(3.3)

\displaystyle\partial(A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p}\wedge B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q})

\displaystyle=\partial(A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p})\wedge B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p}A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p}\wedge\partial(B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q})

\displaystyle\quad+[A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p},B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}]_{sz}

where

(3.4)

\displaystyle[A_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p},B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=\sum(-1)^{i-1+a_{i}(\sum_{i<s}a_{s}+\sum_{s<j}b_{s})}\cdots\widehat{A_{i}}\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p}\wedge B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots[\![A_{i},B_{j}]\!]\cdots

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum(-1)^{p+j+(\sum_{i<s}a_{s}+\sum_{t<j}b_{t})b_{j}}\cdots[\![A_{i},B_{j}]\!]\cdots\bigtriangleup A_{p}\wedge B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\widehat{B_{j}}\cdots

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\sum(-1)^{i+a_{i}\sum_{s>i}a_{s}+j+b_{j}\sum_{s<j}b_{s}}\cdots\widehat{A_{i}}\cdot\cdot A_{p}\bigtriangleup[\![A_{i},B_{j}]\!]\bigtriangleup B_{1}\cdot\cdot\widehat{B_{j}}\cdots\;.

This new bracket

, which is similar to the Schouten bracket in some
case, satisfies

\displaystyle[A_{1}\cdots A_{p},B_{1}\cdots B_{q}\bigtriangleup C_{1}\cdots C_{r}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=[A_{1}\cdots A_{p},B_{1}\cdots B_{q}]_{sz}\bigtriangleup C_{1}\cdots C_{r}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{1+q+(\sum_{i}a_{i})(\sum_{j}b_{j})}B_{1}\cdots B_{q}\bigtriangleup[A_{1}\cdots A_{p},C_{1}\cdots C_{r}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}A_{1}\cdots A_{p}\bigtriangleup[B_{1}\cdots B_{q},C_{1}\cdots C_{r}]_{sz}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{1+(\sum_{j}b_{j})(\sum_{k}c_{k})}[A_{1}\cdots A_{p},C_{1}\cdots C_{r}]_{sz}\bigtriangleup B_{1}\cdots B_{q}\;.

Assume that
A_{1}=\cdots=A_{p}=A\in\mathfrak{g}_{\text{odd}}
in (
3.4
).
Then we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle[\bigtriangleup^{p}A,B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=p(-1)^{p}\bigtriangleup^{p-1}A\bigtriangleup\sum_{j}(-1)^{j+b_{j}\sum_{s<j}b_{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{q}[\![A,B_{j}]\!]\bigtriangleup B_{1}\cdots

\displaystyle=p\bigtriangleup^{p-1}(-A)\bigtriangleup[A,{B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}}]_{sz}\;.

Furthermore, assume every

has degree

\displaystyle[A,B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup[A,B_{j}]_{sz}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}\;.

Contrary, assume every
C_{j}
has degree

(3.10)

\displaystyle[A,C_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup C_{r}]_{sz}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(-1)^{j+1}C_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup[A,C_{j}]_{sz}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup C_{r}

(3.11)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}[A,C_{j}]_{sz}\bigtriangleup C_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup\widehat{C_{j}}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup C_{r}

(3.12)

\displaystyle[A,B_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup B_{q}\bigtriangleup C_{1}\bigtriangleup\cdots\bigtriangleup C_{r}]_{sz}

In particular,

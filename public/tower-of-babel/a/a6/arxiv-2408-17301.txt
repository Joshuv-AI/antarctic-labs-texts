Integral cohomology of dual boundary complexes is motivic
Tao Su

Abstract
. In this note, we give a motivic characterization of the integral cohomology of dual boundary complexes of smooth quasi-projective complex algebraic varieties. As a corollary, the dual boundary complex of any stably affine space (of positive dimension) is contractible. In a separate paper [ 25 ] , this corollary has been used by the author in his proof of the weak geometric P=W conjecture for very generic G ​ L n ​ ( ℂ ) GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}) -character varieties over any punctured Riemann surfaces.



Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth complex quasi-projective variety.
Take a log compactification
\overline{X}
with simple normal crossing divisor
\partial X=\overline{X}\setminus X
.
The dual boundary complex
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is the dual complex of the irreducible components of
\partial X
, encoding how the irreducible components intersect.
If necessary, we refer to
[
25
]
for a crash review.
Up to homotopy,
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is an invariant of
X
as an algebraic variety
[
4
]
, independent of the choice of the log compactifications.
In general, the homotopy type of any finite simplicial complex can be realized by a dual boundary complex
[
22
, E.g.2.6]
.
On the contrary, a conjecture attributed to M. Kontsevich (and indepdently, J. Kollár and C. Xu) states the following:

Conjecture 0.1

(Kontsevich(-Kollár-Xu))
.

The dual boundary complex of any log
CY
variety is (a finite quotient of) a sphere.

The conjecture is termed as the Algebra-geometric version of the Poincaré conjecture in
[
16
, Conj.1.2]
.
So far, the main general result is due to Kollár-Xu
[
13
]
, which proves the finite quotient version of the conjecture in dimension at most
5
.
In higher dimensions, only partial results are known
[
14
,
3
,
18
,
6
,
16
]
, see
[
16
, §1.6]
for a quick summary.
Among them, we recall that the rational cohomology of the dual boundary complex is well understood due to an intrinsic characterization via the mixed Hodge structure on the bulk variety (see e.g.
[
22
]
):

\widetilde{H}^{i-1}(\mathbb{D}\partial X;\mathbb{Q})\cong\Gr_{0}^{W}H_{c}^{i}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

On the other hand, the torsion of the integral cohomology remains a mystery (see
[
13
, P.529]
,
[
16
, §1.6.(2)]
).
Our main purpose in this note is to enhance the previous characterization to a motivic level so as to encode integral cohomology:

Proposition 0.2
.

For any smooth complex quasi-projective variety
X
, we have

(0.0.1)

H^{i-1}(\mathbb{D}\partial X;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{i,0}(X;\mathbb{Z}),

where
H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a,b}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is the
integral singular weight cohomology with compact support
of
X
(Definition
1.7
).

An important class of examples for Conjecture
0.1
comes from the geometric P=W conjecture
[
11
,
23
]
.
By a folklore conjecture, the smooth character variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}
over any punctured Riemann surfaces is expected to be log Calabi-Yau.
See
[
30
,
5
]
for the proof for
SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-character varieties.
The weak version of the geometric P=W conjecture then becomes a special case of the conjecture above: the dual boundary complex of
\mathcal{M}_{B}
has the homotopy type of a sphere
of one dimension lower.
For previous results for lower genera or lower ranks, see
[
12
,
23
,
26
,
27
,
24
,
21
,
17
,
5
]
.

Our second goal is to pave the way for a separate paper
[
25
]
, in which we prove the weak geometric P=W conjecture for all very generic character varieties.
That is, we prove the following corollary:

Corollary 0.3
.

The dual boundary complex of any stably affine space (of positive dimension) is contractible.

Here, a
stably affine space
is an affine variety
Y
such that
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{\ell}
is an affine space for some
\ell\geq 0
.
Let’s mention that the famous
Zariski cancellation problem
for dimension at least
3
in characteristic zero is still widely open, i.e. if
Y
has complex dimension
d\geq 3
, is it always true that
Y\cong\mathbb{C}^{d}
? On the other hand, however, there are counterexamples for each dimension
\geq 3
in positive characteristics
[
9
,
10
]
.

Definition 1.6

By definition, the weight cohomology with compact support satisfies the following properties:

(1)

H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a}(X,\mathfrak{R})
is contravariant in
X
, and equals zero for
a<0
or
a>\dim X
.

(2)

For

for
a=0
, and vanishes for all
a\neq 0
.

(3)

If
i:Z\hookrightarrow X
is a closed subvariety with open complement
j:U\hookrightarrow X
, then there is a long exact sequence

\cdots\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a}(U,\mathfrak{R})\xrightarrow{j_{*}}H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a}(X,\mathfrak{R})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a}(Z,\mathfrak{R})\rightarrow H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a+1}(U,\mathfrak{R})\rightarrow\cdots.

The main example of interest is the singular cohomology with compact support:
Let

For any

(

denote the singular cohomology with integer coefficients.

Definition 1.7

(Singular weight cohomology with compact support)
.

i)

The
b

of effective pure Chow motives is
the contravariant additive functor into abelian groups:

(1.3.1)

\mathfrak{R}_{B}^{b}:\mathbf{Chow}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbb{K})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}b:\mathfrak{R}_{B}^{b}(X,p):=p^{*}H^{b}(X).

In particular, for any
X\in\mathbf{SmProj}(\mathbb{K})
, we have
\mathfrak{R}_{B}^{b}(X)=\mathfrak{R}_{B}^{b}(X,\mathsf{id}_{X})=H^{b}(X)
.
By definition,
\mathfrak{R}_{B}^{b}(X,p)
is always a finitely generated abelian group.

ii)

For any
X\in\mathbf{Var}(\mathbb{K})
, its
(a,b)
-th singular/Betti weight cohomology with compact support
is:

For any commutative ring

Lemma 1.8

As an application, we obtain:

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X,Y
are stably isomorphic
\mathbb{K}
-varieties:
X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}\cong Y\times\mathbb{A}^{j}
for some

\mathrm{M}^{c}(X)\cong\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y)\in\mathbf{DM}_{\mathrm{Nis}}^{\mathrm{eff},-}(\mathbb{K}).

In particular,
\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{c}}(X)\cong\mathrm{W}_{\mathrm{c}}(Y)\in K^{b}(\mathbf{Chow}_{\mathrm{rat}}^{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbb{K})^{\mathrm{op}})
, and hence
H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a,b}(X;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{a,b}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.1
.(1), the isomorphism
\phi:X\times\mathbb{A}^{j}\cong Y\times\mathbb{A}^{j}
induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{M}^{c}(\phi):\mathrm{M}^{c}(X\times\mathbb{A}^{j})=\mathrm{M}^{c}(X)(j)[2j]\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y\times\mathbb{A}^{j})=\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y)(j)[2j],

hence equivalently,

\mathrm{M}^{c}(\phi):\mathrm{M}^{c}(X)(j)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y)(j)
.
But by the Cancellation theorem
[
29
]
(see Theorem
1.1
.(3)), we have a natural isomorphism

-\otimes\mathbb{Z}(j):\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{DM}_{\mathrm{Nis}}^{\mathrm{eff},-}(\mathbb{K})}(\mathrm{M}^{c}(X),\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbf{DM}_{\mathrm{Nis}}^{\mathrm{eff},-}(\mathbb{K})}(\mathrm{M}^{c}(X)(j),\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y)(j))\ni\mathrm{M}^{c}(\phi).

This shows that
\mathrm{M}^{c}(\phi)
induces an isomorphism
\mathrm{M}^{c}(X)\cong\mathrm{M}^{c}(Y)
, as desired.
∎

3.
Dual boundary complexes of stably affine spaces

For the proof of Corollary
0.3
, we will need some constraints on the fundamental group of a dual boundary complex.
This is pioneered by Kollár-Xu
[
13
, §5]
. In our case, what we need is the following variation:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth connected affine algebraic variety of dimension
d\geq 3
, with any log compactification
(\overline{X},D=\overline{X}-X)
.
Then
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is connected, and we have a natural surjection

\pi_{1}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\overline{X})\simeq\pi_{1}(D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{D}\partial X).

Proof.

This is a consequence of the Lefschetz hyperplane theorem. We give some details for completeness.
Say,
X
is a proper closed subset of
\mathbb{A}^{N}
. Take a generic point
p\in\mathbb{A}^{N}\setminus X
, and define a squared-distance function
L_{p}:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as in
[
15
, p.41]
. Then define
f:\overline{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by:
f(x):=0
if
x\in D
, and
f(x):=\frac{1}{L_{p}(x)}
. Now the same argument as in
[
15
, Thm.7.4]
shows that
\pi_{i}(\overline{X},D)=0
for all
i<d
.
In particular,
\pi_{i}(D)\simeq\pi_{i}(\overline{X}),i=0,1
by the long exact sequence of homotopy groups. By definition,
\pi_{0}(\mathbb{D}\partial X)\simeq\pi_{0}(D)
. So,
\pi_{0}(\mathbb{D}\partial X)\simeq\pi_{0}(\overline{X})=0
. Now, by
[
13
, Lem.26]
, there is a natural surjection
\pi_{1}(D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathbb{D}\partial X)
. Finally, as
D
has complex codimension
1
, hence real codimension
2
in
\overline{X}
, we have a surjection
\pi_{1}(X=\overline{X}-D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(\overline{X})
by a perturbation argument. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we come to our second goal.

Proof of Corollary
0.3
.

Let
Y
be a stably affine space of dimension
d>0
. Say,
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{\ell}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d+\ell}
.
As
Y\times\mathbb{A}^{\ell}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d+\ell}
is smooth, so is
Y
.
Clearly,
Y
is connected and contractible as an analytic topological space.

If
d\leq 2
, then
Y\cong\mathbb{A}^{d}
by
[
1
,
7
,
19
]
, so
\mathbb{D}\partial Y\simeq\mathbb{D}\partial\mathbb{A}^{d}=\mathrm{pt}
, as desired.

If
d\geq 3
, by Lemma
3.1
,
\mathbb{D}\partial Y
is connected, and

\pi_{1}(\mathbb{D}\partial Y)\cong\pi_{1}(Y)=0
.
In addition, by Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
0.2
,

\widetilde{H}^{i}(\mathbb{D}\partial Y,\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{i+1,0}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{\mathrm{W},c}^{i+1,0}(\mathbb{A}^{\ell};\mathbb{Z})=0,

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus,
H_{i}(\mathbb{D}\partial Y,\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{i}(\mathrm{pt},\mathbb{Z})
and
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{D}\partial Y)=0
. Now, by the Hurewicz theorem,
\pi_{i}(\mathbb{D}\partial Y)\simeq 0
for all
i\geq 0
. Then by the Whitehead theorem for CW complexes,
\mathbb{D}\partial Y\sim\mathrm{pt}
.
∎

Explicit height estimates for CM curves of genus 2
Linda Frey, Samuel Le Fourn, Elisa Lorenzo García

Abstract
. In this paper we make explicit the constants of Habegger and Pazuki’s work from 2017 on bounding the discriminant of cyclic Galois CM fields corresponding to genus 2 curves with CM by them and potentially good reduction outside a predefined set of primes. We also simplify some of the arguments.

11G15, 11G30, 14G50, 11L07, 11R29, 11R42, 11Y40.

1.
Introduction

Serre and Tate’s work in 1968
[
ST68
]
demonstrated that an abelian variety with complex multiplication exhibits potentially good reduction everywhere. This principle applies not only to elliptic curves but also to Jacobians of curves of genus
g\geq 2
. However, it is important to note that these curves can still encounter geometrically bad reduction.

In 1983, Faltings provided a groundbreaking result when he proved the Shafarevich Conjecture
[
Fal83
]
. This conjecture implies, when we fix the field of definition for our varieties, the existence of at most finitely many curves of a given genus with potentially good reduction outside a predefined set of primes. This can be viewed as the analog result for curves in a manner similar to the classical result in number fields, which states that there are only a finite number of unramified extensions of bounded degree outside a fixed set of primes. It is important to emphasize that this is while keeping the base field fixed.

Habegger and Pazuki, in their recent work
[
HP17
]
from 2017, provided an elegant finitness result for curves of genus 2 without the need to fix the field of definition. The trade-off is that one must fix the discriminant of the real multiplication field contained in the complex multiplication of the Jacobian of the curve.

More specifically, the main results in the aforementioned source bound the discriminant of such quartic complex multiplication fields in terms of the minimal discriminant

\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x^{\prime})/2
. In particular, our hypothesis implies that
x^{\prime}\neq 0
. If
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})
have the same sign, we can write

|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x^{\prime})|=|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})|+|\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|\geq 2\sqrt{|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})||\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|}\geq 2|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(x^{\prime})|^{1/4}\geq 2|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/4},

which proves the first case.

If
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})
do not have the same sign, independently of the choice of type
\Phi
,
\{\sigma\circ\phi_{1},\sigma\circ\phi_{2}\}
is
\{\phi_{1},\overline{\phi_{2}}\}
or
\{\phi_{2},\overline{\phi_{1}}\}
. As a consequence,

|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x^{\prime}))|=(|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})+\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})||\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})-\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|)^{2}=|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})^{2}|^{2}.

As
x^{\prime}
is totally imaginary and nonzero by hypothesis, we cannot have
\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})=\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})
so

|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x^{\prime})|=\frac{|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x^{\prime}))|^{1/2}}{(|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})|+|\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|)}\geq\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/2}}{(|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})|+|\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|)}.

Now, for
j\in\{1,2\}
we can define the hermitian (positive semi-definite)
form

H_{j}(z,t):=(-i)\phi_{j}(\xi)\overline{z_{j}}t_{j}

such that for all
a,b\in K
:

H_{j}(\Phi(a),\Phi(b))=(-i)\phi_{j}(\xi\overline{a}b).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (still valid for positive hermitian forms), we have

|H_{j}(\Phi(a),\Phi(b))|\leq\sqrt{H_{j}(\Phi(a))H_{j}(\Phi(b))},

which gives

\displaystyle|\phi_{j}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\phi_{j}(x)|\leq 2\sqrt{|\phi_{j}(\xi|a|^{2})\phi_{j}(\xi|b|^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(|\phi_{j}(\xi|a|^{2})|+|\phi_{j}(\xi|b|^{2})|).

So we obtain (as the conjugation does not change the absolute value, and the
\phi_{j}(\xi|a|^{2})
are always totally imaginary with positive imaginary part):

\displaystyle|\phi_{1}(x^{\prime})|+|\phi_{2}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi|a|^{2})|+|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi|b|^{2}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(a),\Phi(a))+H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(b),\Phi(b))).

By combining this with the previous inequality, the lemma is proved.
∎

Let
B=(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})
be a symplectic basis. By Equation (
2.2
),
\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2})
is purely imaginary so we can apply the Lemma to
a=e_{1}
and
b=e_{2}
, assuming this type trace is nonzero. In the opposite sign case, we get by Equation (
3.1
) that

|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2})|\geq\frac{2|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/2}}{(y_{2}+y_{1})/\det Y},

which gives by applying again Equation (
3.1
)

|y_{12}|\geq\frac{2(\det Y)}{(y_{1}+y_{2})|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}.

Recall that we are assuming
Z
to be reduced. Then Lemma
3.1
and the proof of Lemma
3.2
give

\det Y\geq\frac{3}{4}y_{1}\cdot y_{2},\quad y_{2}\geq y_{1}\geq\sqrt{\frac{3}{4}}.

Using these to provide crude bounds, we get

(3.4)

|y_{12}|\geq\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}\geq|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/2}.

In the case of same sign for both values of
\operatorname{Im}\phi_{i}(x)
, the bound predicted by the Lemma is better,
so (
3.4
) always holds.

We consider now the situation in which
y_{12}=0
. In this case, as
\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2})
is purely imaginary with imaginary part
2y_{12}/\det Y
:

–

z_{12}=x_{12}
is real,
Y
is diagonal and
\det Y=y_{1}y_{2}
.

–

\phi_{2}(\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2})=-\phi_{1}(\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2})
(definition of the trace) so
\xi\overline{e_{1}}e_{2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{\Delta_{F}}
) where
F
is the totally real quartic field contained in
K
.

–

\Phi(e_{1})
and
\Phi(e_{2})
are orthogonal for
H_{\Phi,\xi}
by (
2.4
).

Recall that by (
2.6
),
\Phi(e_{3})=z_{1}\Phi(e_{1})+z_{12}\Phi(e_{2}).

Now, consider
x=\xi\overline{e_{2}}e_{3}
. By symplecticity hypothesis,
\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(x)
is again purely imaginary, and can be rewritten by orthogonality as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{2}}e_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{2}),\Phi(e_{3}))=\frac{iz_{12}}{2}H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{2}),\Phi(e_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{iz_{12}y_{1}}{\det Y}=\frac{iz_{12}}{y_{2}}.

In particular, this type trace cannot be 0: as an abelian surface with CM by
K
cyclic has necessarily a primitive CM-type and hence, it is not a product of elliptic curves.

We can therefore apply Lemma
3.5
to state that either

|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{2}}e_{3})|\geq{|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/4}}

or (opposite sign case)

|\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(\xi\overline{e_{2}}e_{3})|\geq\frac{2|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/2}}{H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{2}),\Phi(e_{2}))+H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{3}),\Phi(e_{3}))}

Using the orthogonality of
\Phi(e_{1})
and
\Phi(e_{2})
again, we have

\displaystyle H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{2}),\Phi(e_{2}))+H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{3}),\Phi(e_{3}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{y_{1}+|z_{1}|^{2}y_{2}+z_{12}^{2}y_{1}}{\det Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{y_{2}}+\frac{y_{1}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}}{y_{1}}+\frac{z_{12}^{2}}{y_{2}}.

Assuming now that
Z
is in the fundamental domain,

\sqrt{3}/2\leq y_{1}\leq y_{2}
,
\det Y\geq 1
,
|x_{1}|\leq 1/2
,
|z_{12}|\leq 1/2
. Using these inequalities, we obtain

H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{2}),\Phi(e_{2}))+H_{\Phi,\xi}(\Phi(e_{3}),\Phi(e_{3}))\leq\frac{4}{3}y_{2}+y_{2}+\frac{1}{{3}}y_{2}+\frac{1}{{3}}y_{2}\leq 3y_{2}

hence

|z_{12}|\geq{\frac{2}{3}}|\Delta_{K}|^{-1/2},

which concludes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Remark 3.6
.

The bound of Proposition
3.3
holds for all reduced
Z
corresponding to a CM triple
(I,\xi,\Phi)
, but one might hope for a better (explicit) exponent than
-1/2
on average over the whole Galois orbit of a CM abelian variety associated to the triple by using variants of this method.

3.3.
Bounding the trace of the conjugated period matrices

We recall that
(I,\xi,\Phi)
is a fixed CM triple with
Z
as corresponding associated reduced matrix.
For an ideal class
{\mathcal{C}}\in\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
, we denote by
N({\mathcal{C}})
the minimal norm of the integral ideals in
{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

(
[
HP17
, Lemma 3.6 (i)]
)

Let
Z
be a reduced period matrix. There exists a constant
c>0
which depends only on
g
with the following property:

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(\operatorname{Im}(Z))\leq c\left(\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N([I]^{-1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{g}}.

Our explicit version is

Lemma 3.8
.

With the same notations as in Lemma
3.7
above, for
g=2
one can take the constant
c=2/3
.

Proof.

We closely follow the proof of
[
HP17
, Lem. 3.6(i)]
.
Define
Y:=\operatorname{Im}(Z)=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&y_{12}\\
y_{12}&y_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. By construction of
Z
(relative to a choice of symplectic
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})
of
I
),
Y^{-1}
is the matrix of the hermitian form
H_{\Phi}
in
\Phi(e_{1}),\Phi(e_{2})
, define
y^{\prime}_{1}
and
y^{\prime}_{2}
as its diagonal elements.

For any
x\in I
non zero, we have

\displaystyle H_{\Phi}(\Phi(x),\Phi(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{2}|\phi_{j}(\xi)||\phi_{j}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4\sqrt{|\phi_{1}(\xi)\phi_{2}(\xi)|}|\phi_{1}(x)\phi_{2}(x)|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 4|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\xi)|^{1/4}|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(x)|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{4|N_{K/{\mathbb{Q}}}(x)|^{1/2}}{N(I)^{1/2}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/4}}.

Now, as
x\in I
, we can write
(x)=IJ
for some integral ideal
J\in[I]^{-1}
, and we get by passing to the norm

H_{\Phi}(\Phi(x),\Phi(x))\geq\frac{4N(J)^{1/2}}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/4}}\geq\frac{4N([I]^{-1})^{1/2}}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/4}},

which holds for all nonzero
x\in I
so provides a lower bound for
y^{\prime}_{1}
and
y^{\prime}_{2}
. Now, as
Z
is reduced,
Y
is Minkowski-reduced. Hence,
\det Y\geq\frac{3}{4}y_{1}y_{2}
by Proposition
3.1
. We can thus write

y^{\prime}_{1}\leq\frac{4}{3y_{1}}
and
y^{\prime}_{2}\leq\frac{4}{3y_{2}}
. The lower bounds on
y^{\prime}_{1},y^{\prime}_{2}
obtained above thus transform into an upper bound on
y_{1},y_{2}
and the proposition follows.
∎

4.
Subconvexity bounds and the average of the inverses of the minimal ideals norms

In the previous section we bounded the trace of the imaginary part of a reduced period matrix by
\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}{N([I]^{-1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
in Lemma
3.8
. While we do not have individual control on those terms, we can still bound them on average when we let the ideals
I
vary on a coset in the class group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
. This gives an explicit version of
[
HP17
, Proposition 5.9
(i)
]
, which we state now.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field. There is an absolute constant
c
and a constant
c^{\prime}(F)
such that for every imaginary quadratic extension
K/F
(which is Galois quartic over
{\mathbb{Q}}
) of discriminant
|\Delta_{K}|^{1/4}\geq 98
and every coset
H
of a subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
, assuming GRH for the Hecke L-functions of all characters
\chi_{K}:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, one has

\frac{1}{\#H}\sum_{[I]\in H}\left(\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}{N([I])}\right)^{1/2}\leq c\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{\#H}+c^{\prime}(F).

Furthermore, we can take
c=80
and
c^{\prime}(F)=\frac{5800R_{F}\log|\Delta_{K}|}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/32}}+20R_{F}\leq 1.4\cdot 10^{5}R_{F}
.

Remark 4.2
.

This result (even without stating the explicit constants) is slightly stronger than
[
HP17
, Proposition 5.9
(i)
]
: indeed, our constant
c
is actually absolute and the dependency in
F
in the bound is only in the additive error term
c^{\prime}(F)
. This is most likely due to the assumption of GRH (whose original goal was making the exponent gain explicit), and the authors do not know if one can attain such an explicit result without assuming GRH. Indeed, looking at unconditional subconvexity results in the literature, the existence of an absolute exponent gain on
|\Delta_{K}|
(here
1/32
) is known but not made explicit and the fact that the constant before this power of
|\Delta_{K}|
can be made absolute is not clear to us.

The proof of this result has the same structure as in
[
HP17
]
:

(a)

State a general subconvexity result on the critical line for Hecke L-functions
L(\chi,s)
where
\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is a character, which can then be extended for
\operatorname{Re}(s)\in[1/2,2]
by the Phragmen-Lindelöf principle.

(b)

For any
x\geq 0
large enough, bound

(4.1)

\frac{1}{\#H}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[I]\in H\\
N([I])\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}{N([I])}\right)^{1/2}

thanks to weighted sums of integrals of
L(\chi,s)
along vertical lines of the domain of convergence of the series.

(c)

Use residue theorems to shift these vertical lines near the critical lines, and obtain bounds on (
4.1
).

(d)

Finally, use Minkowski bounds on the smallest integral ideal in a given class to find a proper
x
, and obtain the Proposition.

We will now proceed along those lines (notice at steps
(a)
and
(c)
, the case
\chi=1
has to be treated slightly differently because
\zeta_{K}
has a pole at
s=1
).

For any number field
K
of degree
d
and any character
\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, the associated Hecke
L
-function is defined for
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
by

L(\chi,s):=\sum_{I}\frac{\chi(I)}{N(I)^{s}}

where
I
goes through nonzero integral ideals of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
. This is a Hecke
L
-series associated to a Grossencharakter of modulus 1 (see
[
Neu13
, paragraph VII.8]
for details on what follows).

Our first use of GRH is here. It produces completely explicit (and sufficiently strong) bounds on our
L
-functions on the critical line.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any CM quartic field
K
, any nontrivial
\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, assuming GRH for this
L
-function:

(4.2)

|L(\chi,1/2+it)|\leq 263(1+|t|)^{3/4}|\Delta_{K}|^{3/16}.

For
\chi
trivial, we have for any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, assuming GRH for
\zeta_{K}
:

(4.3)

|\zeta_{K}(1/2+it)|\leq 839(1+|t|)^{3/4}|\Delta_{K}|^{3/16}.

The proof of the previous lemma will use a general subconvexity result of Chandee
[
Cha09
]
on entire
L
-functions assuming GRH, for which we need some reminders.

For any
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the vertically shifted
L
-function

L_{t}(\chi,s):=L(\chi,s+it)=\sum_{I}\frac{\chi(I)N(I)^{-it}}{N(I)^{s}}=L(\chi_{t},s)\textrm{ with }\chi_{t}(I)=\chi(I)N(I)^{-it}

is also a Hecke
L
-series associated to a Grossencharakter of modulus 1. The gamma factor of
L(\chi,\cdot)
is defined by

L_{\infty}(\chi,s):=\pi^{-ds/2}2^{(1-s)r_{2}}\gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^{r_{1}}\gamma(s)^{r_{2}}=\pi^{-ds/2-r_{2}/2}\gamma\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^{r_{1}+r_{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)^{r_{2}}

where
r_{1}
is the number of real embeddings and
r_{2}
the number of pairs of complex embeddings of
K
(the second equality coming from Legendre duplication formula). The completed
L
-series

\Lambda(\chi,s):=|\Delta_{K}|^{s/2}L_{\infty}(\chi,s)L(\chi,s)

has a meromorphic continuation to
{\mathbb{C}}
, satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(\chi,s)=\Lambda(\overline{\chi},1-s)

and it is entire unless
\chi
is trivial.

Following the definitions and notation of
[
Cha09
]
, the L-function
L(\chi,\cdot)
thus has degree
d=[K:{\mathbb{Q}}]
,
k_{j}=0
for
j\in\{1,\cdots,r_{1}+r_{2}\}

and
k_{j}=1
for
j\in\{r_{1}+r_{2}+1,\cdots,d\}
. The arithmetic conductor
q(L(\chi,\cdot))
is
|\Delta_{K}|
, and the
analytic conductor

C(L(\chi,\cdot))
is

C(L(\chi,\cdot))=\frac{q(L)}{\pi^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left|\frac{1}{4}+\frac{k_{j}}{2}\right|=\frac{3^{r_{2}}|\Delta_{K}|}{(4\pi)^{d}}.

For the vertically shifted
L
-function
L(\chi_{t},s)
,
k_{j}=-it
for
j\in\{1,\cdots,r_{1}+r_{2}\}
and
k_{j}=1-it
for
j\in\{r_{1}+r_{2}+1,\cdots,d\}
. The conductor of
L_{t}(\chi,s)
is then

C_{t}:=C(L(\chi_{t},\cdot))=\frac{|\Delta_{K}|}{(4\pi)^{d}}\sqrt{(1+4t^{2})^{r_{1}+r_{2}}(9+4t^{2})^{r_{2}}}.

We now apply these equalities in our case:
K
is CM of degree 4, so
d=4
,
r_{2}=2
and
r_{1}=0
. We obtain that for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\frac{C(L(\chi_{t},\cdot))}{(1+|t|)^{4}|\Delta_{K}|}\in[10^{-4},7\cdot 10^{-4}].

Furthermore, remark that if we define the coefficients
a(n)
by the Dirichlet series

-\frac{L^{\prime}(\chi,s)}{L(\chi,s)}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}},

using the Euler product,
|a(n)|\leq d\Lambda(n)
where
\Lambda
is the von Mangoldt function.

We now recall the main result of
[
Cha09
]
.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
Cha09
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

Assume GRH. If
L(f,s)
is an L-function of analytic conductor
C_{L}
and degree
d
and
a(n)
is the sequence of coefficients of
-L^{\prime}/L
, for any parameters
\lambda\geq 1/2
and

such that

\displaystyle\log|L(f,1/2)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\left(\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{a(n)}{n^{1/2+\lambda/\log x}}\frac{\log(x/n)}{\log x\log n}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\lambda}{2}\right)\frac{\log C_{L}}{\log x}+\frac{(\lambda^{2}+\lambda)d}{(\log x)^{2}}+\frac{de^{-\lambda}}{x^{1/2}\log(x)^{2}}.

Proof.

(of Lemma
4.3
)
Proposition
4.4
applies to all
L(\chi_{t},\cdot)
for
\chi
nontrivial (as these L-functions are admissible under Chandee’s definition), and that paper actually provides an explicit (and sufficient) bound when
\log\log C(L(\chi_{t},\cdot))\geq 10
, but we need a result for all possible analytic conductors. We thus follow the outline
[
Cha09
, Proof of Corollary 1.1]
.

Trying to find acceptable values for the first term and the factor before
\log C_{L}
in the right-hand side (the latter has to be
<1/4
to attain subconvexity), we settle for

and

) after numerical computation.

Regarding

, we notice

is an entire L-function of degree 3 admissible for this method, and we obtain with the same choice of

and

inside Proposition

that for all

:

\left|\frac{\zeta_{K}(1/2+it)}{\zeta(1/2+it)}\right|\leq 181\left|\frac{|\Delta_{K}|(|1+2it||3+2it|^{2}}{(4\pi)^{3}}\right|^{3/16}\leq 60(1+|t|)^{9/16}|\Delta_{K}|^{3/16}.

Next, following
[

]
(and checking for small

:

Combining both bounds, we obtain (

).
∎

We can now explain the link between (
4.1
) and the studied
L
-functions.

Define the function
f(y):=y^{-1/2}e^{-y}
on
(0,+\infty)
so that its Mellin transform is
\Gamma(s-1/2)
.

Definition 4.5
.

For any coset
H=hH_{0}
of a subgroup of
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
, and any
x>0
, we define

S_{H}(x):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\textrm{ ideal}\\
[I]\in H\\
N(I)\leq x\end{subarray}}f\left(\frac{N(I)}{x}\right).

We define similarly for any character
\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{K}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
and any
x>0

S(x,\chi):=\sum_{I\textrm{ ideal}}\chi(I)f\left(\frac{N(I)}{x}\right).

These sums will allow us to compute the average sum (
4.1
), thanks to the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

With the same notation as in Definition
4.5
, we have for every character
\chi
and
x>0
:

S(x,\chi)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\sigma}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)L(s,\chi)x^{s}ds,

and for any
x>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\textrm{ ideal}\\
[I]\in H\\
N(I)\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{x}{N(I)}\right)^{1/2}\leq eS_{H}(x)\leq\frac{e}{[\operatorname{Cl}_{K}:H_{0}]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\\
\chi_{|H_{0}}\equiv 1\end{subarray}}\chi(h)^{-1}S(x,\chi).

Proof.

First, discrete Fourier transform on the abelian group
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
directly gives that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\\
[I]\in H\end{subarray}}f\left(\frac{N(I)}{x}\right)\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\\
[I]\in H\\
N(I)\leq x\end{subarray}}f\left(\frac{N(I)}{x}\right)\geq\frac{1}{e}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\\
[I]\in H\\
N(I)\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{x}{N(I)}\right)^{1/2}.

Next, by absolute convergence of the series defining
S(x,\chi)
(
f
decays rapidly), by
\Gamma
decaying quickly on vertical lines, and by the inverse Mellin formula we can write for
\sigma>1
:

\displaystyle S(x,\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{I}\chi(I)\cdot\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\sigma}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)(N(I)/x)^{-s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\sigma}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{I}\frac{\chi(I)}{N(I)^{s}}x^{s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\sigma}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)L(s,\chi)x^{s}ds.

∎

We can now explain how to bound the sums
S(x,\chi)
.

Proposition 4.7
.

For any
x=\varepsilon|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle|S(x,\chi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 163\varepsilon^{1/2+1/12}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}\quad(\chi\neq 1)

\displaystyle|S(x,1)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 393\varepsilon^{1/2+1/12}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}+\frac{\pi^{5/2}}{2}\varepsilon h_{K}R_{K}.

Proof.

First, we shift the vertical integrals appearing to some
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
(to be fixed later). For
\chi
nontrivial, there is no pole between the two vertical lines and for
\chi
trivial, there is a pole at
s=1
with residue
\Gamma(1/2)\kappa_{K}x
(where
\kappa_{K}
is the residue of
\zeta_{K}
at
s=1
), so we get

S(x,\chi)=\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\operatorname{Re}(s)=\sigma}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)L(s,\chi)x^{s}ds+\mathbb{1}_{\chi\equiv 1}\Gamma(1/2)\kappa_{K}x

Define
\ell
the affine function interpolating 1 at
1/2
and 0 at 2 (i.e.
\ell(\sigma)=-2/3\sigma+4/3
).

Assume first that
\chi
is nontrivial. As
L(\chi,\cdot)
is then holomorphic, by the Phragmen-Lindelöf principle
[
IK04
, Theorem 5.53]
, for every
\sigma\in[1/2,2]
:

|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|\leq 263^{\ell(\sigma)}(1+|t|)^{3\ell(\sigma)/16}\zeta(2)^{4(1-\ell(\sigma))}|\Delta_{K}|^{3\ell(\sigma)/16}.

Define
c_{\sigma}:=263^{\ell(\sigma)}\zeta(2)^{4(1-\ell(\sigma))}
(as
\sigma
will be close to
1/2
, it will be close to but slightly inferior to 263). We use for the
\Gamma
function the Lerch bound
[
God01
, p. 15]

(4.4)

|\Gamma(\sigma+it)|\leq\Gamma(1+\sigma)\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}{\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+t^{2}}}\sqrt{\frac{t\pi}{\sinh(t\pi)}}.

With this and writing
x=\varepsilon|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}
with
\varepsilon\leq 1
, we obtain

|S(x,\chi)|\leq\frac{c_{\sigma}\Gamma(1/2+\sigma)}{2\pi}\varepsilon^{\sigma}|\Delta_{K}|^{\sigma/2+3\ell(\sigma)/16}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(1+|t|)^{3/16}\sqrt{\frac{(1+t^{2})\pi t}{((\sigma-1/2)^{2}+t^{2})\sinh(\pi t)}}dt.

Now, we need the exponent of
|\Delta_{K}|
to be less than
1/2-1/32
. For this, an acceptable value of
\sigma
is

\sigma_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{12},

so
\ell(\sigma_{1})=17/18
and
c_{\sigma_{1}}\simeq 216
. For this value, we then get by numerical computation

|S(x,\chi)|\leq 163\varepsilon^{\sigma_{1}}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}.

Assume now that
\chi
is trivial. We can apply the Phragmen-Lindelöf bound to
\zeta_{K}(s)/\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(s)
with the bounds in the proof of Lemma
4.3
, and separately to
\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}
. For the latter, we make use of Theorem 1 of
[
Yan23
]
(for
k=4
) which implies (after real analysis and completing it for small values of
t
) that for
\sigma^{\prime}=5/7
and all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

|\zeta(\sigma^{\prime}+it)|\leq 6.4(1+|t|)^{3/16\ell(\sigma^{\prime})}.

Using Phragmen-Lindelöf interpolation principle now between
1/2
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, we get the bound

|\zeta(\sigma_{1}+it)|\leq 10(1+|t|)^{3/16\ell(\sigma_{1})}.

Applying now the same method as above (which boilds down to replacing
c_{\sigma_{1}}\simeq 216
by 520 after computations and evaluating the same terms otherwise), we get

|S(x,1)|\leq 393\varepsilon^{\sigma_{1}}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}+\kappa_{K}\Gamma(1/2)x.

As the residue
\kappa_{K}
of
\zeta_{K}
at
s=1
is
h_{K}R_{K}\pi^{2}/2
and
\Gamma(1/2)=\sqrt{\pi}
, we obtain the result.

∎

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

We now give the final steps of the proof. First, for any
x=\varepsilon|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, combining Proposition
4.7
and
4.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[I]\in H\\
N([I])\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{x}{N(I)}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e}{h_{K}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\\
\chi_{|H_{0}}\equiv 1\end{subarray}}|S(x,\chi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e}{h_{K}}163[\operatorname{Cl}_{K}:H_{0}]\varepsilon^{1/2+1/12}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{e}{h_{K}}(230\varepsilon^{1/2+1/12}|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}+\frac{\pi^{5/2}\varepsilon h_{K}R_{K}}{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(H)\varepsilon^{1/2+1/12}+\varepsilon\frac{e\pi^{5/2}R_{K}}{2}.

\displaystyle C(H)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 163e\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{|H|}+230e\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{h_{K}}.

Now, for any
\varepsilon>0
, for each class
[I]
of
H
, either the class has an integral ideal with norm less than
\varepsilon|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}
(hence it is counted in the previous sum), or it does not have it and then
|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}/N([I])\leq\varepsilon^{-1}
.
By an immediate minimisation of the function
C(H)\varepsilon^{1/12}+\varepsilon^{-1/2}
, we obtain after bounding roughly

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{[I]\in H}\left(\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}{N([I])}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 80\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{|H|}+108\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{h_{K}}+10R_{K}.

The constant
c
in Proposition
4.1
will thus be given by 80, and we will now bound the two last terms on the right to compute
c^{\prime}(F)
.

First, considering the residue of
\zeta_{K}
as
s=1
again
[
Lan94
, Thm. XIII.3.2]
, we have (for
K\neq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{5})

\kappa_{K}=\frac{2\pi^{2}h_{K}R_{K}}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}.

That is bounded, according to
[
Lou05

(assuming

) by

\kappa_{K}=\frac{2\pi^{2}h_{K}R_{K}}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}\geq\frac{2}{e\log|\Delta_{K}|}.

This gives

108\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2-1/32}}{h_{K}}\leq 5800\frac{R_{K}\log|\Delta_{K}|}{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/32}}\leq 1.4\cdot 10^{5}R_{F}.

Where the last inequality comes from
[

and that the maximum of the function

is attained at

. This concludes the proof of Proposition

∎

4.1.
From a polynomial term to a logarithmic one

The goal of this section is to explicitly upper bound
\frac{|\Delta_{K}|^{1/2}}{\#H}
in terms of

Lemma 4.10
.

For any positive integer

we denote by

the number of different prime numbers dividing

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of
[

\log(2)\times 1.39\sim 0.96<1
.
∎

Proposition 4.11
.

Assuming GRH, for any CM triple
(\xi,I,\Phi)

As suspected, we find a small number of such curves and they have a very small discriminant.

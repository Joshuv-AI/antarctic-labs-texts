Abstract
This article looks at the eigenvalues of magicsquares generated by the MATLAB’s magic( n n ) function. The magic() function constructs doubly even ( n = 4 ​ k n=4k ) magic squares, singly even ( n = 4 ​ k + 2 n=4k+2 ) magic squares and odd ( n = 2 ​ k + 1 n=2k+1 ) magic squares using different algorithms. The doubly even magic squares are constructed by a criss-cross method that involves reflecting the entries of a simple square about the center. The odd magic squares are constructed using the Siamese method. The singly even magic squares are constructed using a lower-order odd magic square (Strachey method). We obtain approximations of eigenvalues of odd and singly even magic squares with error bounds. Further, the eigenvalues of doubly even magic squares are exactly obtained. The approximation of the spectra involves some interesting connections with the spectrum of g-circulant matrices and the use of Bauer-Fike theorem.

2.3
Spectrum of circulant matrices

In this section we recall some spectral properties of circulant matrices. Let
R
be a general circulant matrix.
The vectors
v_{j}
are the eigenvectors of the circulant matrix
R
. The eigenvalue corresponding to the vector
v_{j}
is given by

\lambda_{j}=\dfrac{v_{j}^{*}Rv_{j}}{v_{j}^{*}v_{j}}=\dfrac{v_{j}^{*}Rv_{j}}{n}.

(5)

If
\mathbf{a}
is the first row of the circulant matrix
R
, we then have

\lambda_{j}=\mathbf{a}v_{j}.

(6)

This can be proved by looking at the
i^{\text{th}}
entry of the vector
Rv_{j}
. We have

(Rv_{j})(i)=\mathbf{a}C^{n-(i-1)}v_{j}=\mathbf{a}\omega^{((i-1)j)}v_{j}=\mathbf{a}v_{j}\omega^{((i-1)j)}=(\mathbf{a}v_{j})v_{j}(i).

If
\Lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is the matrix of eigenvalues of
R
, i.e.,

\Lambda_{n}\left(i,j\right)=\begin{cases}\lambda_{j}&\text{ if }i=j,\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(7)

we then have

\Lambda_{n}=\dfrac{F_{n}^{*}RF_{n}}{n},

(8)

R=\dfrac{F_{n}\Lambda_{n}F_{n}^{*}}{n}.

(9)

2.5
Spectrum of reverse circulant matrix

In this subsection, we obtain the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a reverse circulant matrix.
Let
R
be the circulant matrix with eigenpairs given by
\left(\lambda_{j},v_{j}\right)_{j=0}^{n-1}
, i.e.,

Rv_{j}=\lambda_{j}v_{j}.

(11)

We now find the eigenpairs of the matrix
JR
, where
J
is the flipped identity matrix. Note that
(\lambda_{0},\mathbb{I}_{n\times 1})
is also an eigenpair of the reverse circulant matrix.

We have

JRv_{j}=\lambda_{j}Jv_{j}.

(12)

We also have

\displaystyle Jv_{j}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\omega^{(n-1)j}&\omega^{(n-2)j}&\cdots&\omega^{2j}&\omega^{j}&1\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(13)

\displaystyle=\omega^{-j}\begin{bmatrix}1&\omega^{-j}&\cdots&\omega^{-(n-2)j}&\omega^{-(n-1)j}\end{bmatrix}^{T}

(14)

\displaystyle=\omega^{-j}v_{n-j}.

(15)

Note that
v_{n-j}=\overline{v_{j}}
and
\lambda_{n-j}=\overline{\lambda_{j}}
. Hence, we obtain

JRv_{j}=\lambda_{j}Jv_{j}=\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}v_{n-j}.

(16)

Replacing
j
by
n-j
, we obtain

JRv_{n-j}=\lambda_{n-j}Jv_{n-j}=\omega^{-\left(n-j\right)}\lambda_{n-j}v_{j}=\omega^{j}\lambda_{n-j}v_{j}=\overline{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}v_{j}.

(17)

Hence, we see that

\displaystyle JR\left(v_{j}\pm\dfrac{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}{\left\lvert\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}\right\rvert}v_{n-j}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}v_{n-j}\pm\left\lvert\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}\right\rvert v_{j},

(18)

\displaystyle=\mp\left\lvert\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}\right\rvert\left(v_{j}\pm\dfrac{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}{\left\lvert\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}\right\rvert}v_{n-j}\right).

(19)

We consider
j\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
to get
n-1
the eigenpairs (We are interested with odd
n
in this article).
Hence, we obtain the
n-1
eigenpairs of
JR
to be

\left(\pm\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert,v_{j}\mp\dfrac{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}{\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert}v_{n-j}\right)\text{ for }j\leq\frac{n-1}{2},

where
\left(\lambda_{j},v_{j}\right)
are the eigenpairs of
R
.

Note that if we let
u_{0}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\mathbb{I}
, and
u_{j}=\dfrac{v_{j}\pm\dfrac{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}{\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert}v_{n-j}}{\left\lVert v_{j}\pm\dfrac{\omega^{-j}\lambda_{j}}{\left\lvert\lambda_{j}\right\rvert}v_{n-j}\right\rVert_{2}}
,

then we have

u_{j}^{*}u_{k}=\delta_{j,k}.

(20)

Hence, the eigenvectors of the reverse circulant matrix are orthonormal.

The main theorem proved in this section is below (Theorem
1
).

Non-trivial eigenvalues of odd magic squares

Theorem 1
.

Let
n=2m+1
and let

\Lambda=\left\{\lambda_{j}=\dfrac{n^{2}}{2\sin\left(j\pi/n\right)}:j\in\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,\pm m\}\right\}

Then the set of non-trivial eigenvalues of an odd magic square generated using the
Siamese method
is
\frac{1}{n}
-approximated by
\Lambda
.

A similar result can be found in
[
15
]
. The remainder of this section discusses the proof of the above theorem (Theorem
1
).

Proof.

From Equation (
33
), we have
M_{n}=J_{n}S_{n}+J_{n}T_{n}
.
We now look at
J_{n}T_{n}
as a perturbation to
J_{n}S_{n}
to get
M_{n}
and claim that the eigenvalues of
M_{n}=J_{n}S_{n}+J_{n}T_{n}
are well approximated by the eigenvalues of
J_{n}S_{n}
. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
J_{n}S_{n}
can be easily computed since
S_{n}
is a circulant matrix. We now rely on the Bauer-Fike theorem (Corollary 2.2 of
[
7
]
) to make the above statement precise.

Theorem 2
.

Bauer-Fike theorem
: Let
U_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a diagonalizable matrix. Let
\Lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be a diagonal matrix with eigenvalues of
U_{n}
and
W_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
be the matrix containing the eigenvectors of
U_{n}
such that
U_{n}=W_{n}\Lambda_{n}W_{n}^{-1}
. Let
\kappa_{p}\left(W_{n}\right)=\left\lVert W_{n}\right\rVert_{p}\left\lVert W_{n}^{-1}\right\rVert_{p}
be the condition number of the matrix
W_{n}
in
p
-norm.

If
\mu
is an eigenvalue of
U_{n}+V_{n}
, where
V_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, then there exists
\lambda
, an eigenvalue of
U_{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lvert\mu-\lambda\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\leq\kappa_{p}\left(W_{n}\right)\left\lVert U_{n}^{-1}V_{n}\right\rVert_{p}

(35)

We utilize Theorem
2
, by choosing
U_{n}=J_{n}S_{n}
and
V_{n}=J_{n}T_{n}
. The matrix
J_{n}S_{n}
is a reverse circulant matrix and is diagonalizable. The eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
J_{n}S_{n}
are derived in Section
2.5
. Further, the eigenvectors can be chosen to be orthonormal. Hence, the matrix
W_{n}
in Bauer-Fike theorem (Theorem
2
) is orthogonal matrix. Hence from Theorem
2
, and choosing
p=2
, we have that if
\mu
is an eigenvalue of
M_{n}
, then there exists
\lambda
, an eigenvalue of
J_{n}S_{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lvert\mu-\lambda\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\leq\left\lVert\left(J_{n}S_{n}\right)^{-1}\left(J_{n}T_{n}\right)\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\right\rVert_{2}.

(36)

Lemma 3
.

The eigenvalues of
U_{n}=J_{n}S_{n}
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n\left(n^{2}+1\right)}{2}&j=0\\
\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{2\sin\left(\pi j/n\right)}&j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}\end{cases}

(37)

The eigenvectors of
U_{n}=J_{n}S_{n}
are orthonormal.

Proof.

From Lemma
10
, we get the eigenvalues of
X_{n}
. The eigenvalues of
S_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
X_{n}
, except the first eigenvalue,
\lambda_{0}\left(S_{n}\right)
, which is given by
\lambda_{0}\left(X_{n}\right)+\left(m+1\right)
. Note that the matrix
U_{n}
is a reverse circulant matrix. Hence, from Section
2.5
eigenpairs of
U_{n}
can be obtained in terms of eigenpairs of
S_{n}
.

Lemma 4
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\right\rVert_{2}=\dfrac{1}{n}.

(38)

Proof.

First note that
X_{n}\mathbb{I}=\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}\mathbb{I}
and
Y_{n}\mathbb{I}=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}\mathbb{I}
. We have
S_{n}=nX_{n}+\left(m+1\right)\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}
and
T_{n}=Y_{n}-(m+1)\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}
. By using the Sherman–Morrison formula, we have

S_{n}^{-1}=\frac{1}{n}X_{n}^{-1}-\frac{n+1}{n^{3}(n^{2}+1)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}

(39)

Further,
T_{n}=Y_{n}-\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}=\frac{1}{n}X_{n}^{-1}Y_{n}-\frac{n+1}{n^{2}(n-1)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}.

(40)

Let

S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}=\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\left(nG-\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}\right),

(41)

where
G=X_{n}^{-1}Y_{n}-\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}
.

From Equation (
31
), we have

X_{n}^{-1}Y_{n}=\left(C^{m+1}+\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}\right)P=C^{m+1}P+\dfrac{2}{n\left(n-1\right)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}.

(42)

This gives us

G=X_{n}^{-1}Y_{n}-\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}=C^{m+1}P.

(43)

Hence,
G
is a permutation matrix.

Now we seek bound on
\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\right\rVert_{2}
. We have

\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}=\max_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\dfrac{\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

(44)

We have

S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}x=\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\left(nGx-\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}x\right)=\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\left(nGx-\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)\mathbb{I}\right).

(45)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{n^{4}}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(nx_{j}-\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)\right)^{2}\right),

(46)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\left(nx_{j}\right)^{2}+\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)^{2}-2nx_{j}\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)\right)}{n^{4}},

(47)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2}-n\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)^{2}}{n^{4}},

(48)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{n^{2}}-\dfrac{\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+\cdots+x_{n}\right)^{2}}{n^{3}}.

(49)

Hence, we have

\dfrac{\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}=\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}-\dfrac{\left(\mathbb{I}^{T}x\right)^{2}}{n^{3}\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}.

Hence, we obtain that
\left\lVert S_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\right\rVert_{2}=\dfrac{1}{n}
.

Hence, from Theorem
2
, Lemma
3
and Lemma
4
, we have that if
\mu
is an non-trivial eigenvalue of
M_{n}
, then there exists

\displaystyle\lambda\in\left\{\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{2\sin\left(\pi/n\right)},\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{2\sin\left(2\pi/n\right)},\ldots,\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{2\sin\left(m\pi/n\right)}\right\}

(50)

such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lvert\mu-\lambda\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\leq\dfrac{1}{n}.

(51)

This proves Theorem
1
.

.

We, in fact, numerically observe (see Tables

,

), namely, for each eigenvalue of

\displaystyle\dfrac{\left\lvert\mu-\lambda\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\lambda\right\rvert}\leq\dfrac{1}{n},

M_{n}
.

Non-trivial eigenvalues of singly even magic squares

Theorem 7
.

Let
M_{n}
be a singly even magic square, where
n=2m=4k+2
, generated using the
Strachey method
and let

\Lambda=\left\{\lambda_{j}=\dfrac{n^{2}}{4\sin\left(j\pi/m\right)}:j\in\{\pm 1,\pm 2,\ldots,\pm k\}\right\}

1.

Two of the non-trivial eigenpairs of
M_{n}
are
\left(-3m^{2},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{m}\end{bmatrix}\right)
and
\left(3m^{2},\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m}-3e_{k+1}\\
-\left(\mathbb{I}_{m}-3e_{k+1}\right)\end{bmatrix}\right)
.

2.

2k-1
of the non-trivial eigenvalues are zero.

3.

The set of remaining
2k
eigenvalues are
\frac{2}{n}
-approximated by
\Lambda

Proof.

Consider the orthogonal matrix
Q_{n}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}&-I_{m}\\
I_{m}&I_{m}\end{bmatrix}
. The eigenvalues of
M_{n}
and the similarity transform
Q_{n}M_{n}Q_{n}^{T}
are the same. Let us now evaluate
Q_{n}M_{n}Q_{n}^{T}
. Note that

\displaystyle Q_{n}\begin{bmatrix}I_{m}\\
I_{m}\end{bmatrix}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{O}_{m\times m}\\
2I_{m}\end{bmatrix},

(62)

\displaystyle Q_{n}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\end{bmatrix}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}2\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\\
\mathbb{O}_{m\times 1}\end{bmatrix}.

(63)

\displaystyle Q_{n}u=\sqrt{2}e_{k+1}.

(64)

\displaystyle Q_{n}v=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tilde{v},

(65)

where
\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times 1}
with
\tilde{v}_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j=1,m+1\\
-1&\text{ if }j=k+1,m+k+1\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus

\displaystyle Q_{n}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\\
\mathbb{O}_{m\times 1}\end{bmatrix},

(66)

\displaystyle Q_{n}\begin{bmatrix}3\mathbb{I}_{k\times 1}\\
-3\mathbb{I}_{\left(k+1\right)\times 1}\\
\mathbb{I}_{\left(k+2\right)\times 1}\\
-\mathbb{I}_{\left(k-1\right)\times 1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{k}\\
-2\\
-2\\
-\mathbb{I}_{k-1}\\
2\mathbb{I}_{k}\\
-1\\
-1\\
-2\mathbb{I}_{k-1}\end{bmatrix}.

(67)

Hence,

\displaystyle\boxed{Q_{n}M_{n}Q_{n}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}m^{2}A_{m}&m^{2}B_{m}\\
\mathbb{O}_{m\times m}&2M_{m}+3m^{2}\mathbb{I}_{m\times m}\end{bmatrix}}.

(68)

where

\displaystyle A_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m\times k}&-2\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}&-2\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}&-\mathbb{I}_{m\times\left(k-1\right)}\end{bmatrix}-3e_{k+1}w^{T},

(69)

and

\displaystyle B_{m}=\begin{bmatrix}2\mathbb{I}_{m\times k}&-\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}&-\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}&-2\mathbb{I}_{m\times\left(k-1\right)}\end{bmatrix}-3e_{k+1}w^{T}.

(70)

where
w,e_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times 1}
with

\displaystyle w_{j}=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }j=1\\
-1&\text{ if }j=k+1\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(71)

and
e_{k+1}
is a unit vector whose
\left(k+1\right)^{th}
entry is
1
. Hence, the eigenvalues of
M_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
Q_{n}M_{n}Q_{n}^{T}
, which inturn are the eigenvalues of
m^{2}A_{m}
and
2M_{m}+3m^{2}\mathbb{I}_{m\times m}
.

Note that
A_{m}
is a rank
2
matrix, since

\displaystyle A_{m}=\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{1\times k}&-2&-2&-\mathbb{I}_{1\times\left(k-1\right)}\end{bmatrix}-3e_{k+1}w^{T}.

(72)

Further, note that

\displaystyle A_{m}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{1\times k}&-2&-2&-\mathbb{I}_{1\times\left(k-1\right)}\end{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}-3e_{k+1}w^{T}\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1},

(73)

\displaystyle=-3\mathbb{I}_{m\times 1}.

(74)

since
w^{T}\mathbb{I}=0
. Also, note that the trace of
A_{m}
is zero. Hence, the non-zero eigenvalues of
A_{m}
are
\pm 3
. Hence, two of the non-zero eigenvalues of
M_{n}
are
\pm 3m^{2}
, i.e.,
\pm\dfrac{3n^{2}}{4}
. The remaining non-zero eigenvalues of
M_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
2M_{m}+3m^{2}\mathbb{I}_{m\times m}
. From the previous section, we have that the eigenvalues of
M_{m}
to be its magic sum,
\dfrac{m\left(m^{2}+1\right)}{2}
and the remaining are
\frac{1}{m}
-approximated by

\displaystyle\left\{\pm\dfrac{m^{2}}{\sin\left(j\pi/m\right)}\text{ where }j\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\}\right\}.

(75)

Of course the magic sum of
M_{n}=\dfrac{n\left(n^{2}+1\right)}{2}
is nothing but
2\left(\dfrac{m\left(m^{2}+1\right)}{2}\right)+3m^{3}
. The remaining non-trivial non-zero eigenvalues of
M_{n}
are
\frac{2}{n}
-approximated by

\displaystyle\left\{\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{4\sin\left(\pi/m\right)},\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{4\sin\left(2\pi/m\right)},\ldots,\pm\dfrac{n^{2}}{4\sin\left(k\pi/m\right)}\right\}.

(76)

Remark 8
.

If we could prove the stronger
\frac{1}{n}
-well-approximated remark as in Remark
5
, then Theorem
7
could also be further strengthened appropriately.

Non-trivial eigenvalues of doubly even magic squares

Theorem 9
.

The rank of the doubly even magic square (where
n=4m
) generated by MATLAB’s
criss-cross
method is
3
and the non-zero non-trivial eigenvalues are

\pm\dfrac{n}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{n^{3}-n}{3}}

The corresponding eigenvectors are
\sqrt{n^{2}-1}v\pm\sqrt{3n}u
, where

u=\begin{bmatrix}w\\
-\text{flip}\left(w\right)\end{bmatrix}_{4m\times 1}

and

v^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&-1&1&\cdots&1&-1&-1&1\end{bmatrix}_{1\times 4m}

with
w^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}(n-1)&-(n-3)&-(n-5)&(n-7)&\cdots&(-1)^{m}\end{bmatrix}
.

The proof of the above theorem (Theorem
9
) constitutes this section.

Let
P_{4}=I_{4}+J_{4}
, where
I_{4}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
is the identity matrix and
J_{4}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
is the reverse identity matrix. If
S_{n}
denotes the
n\times n
matrix with numbers arranged row wise from
1
to
n^{2}
, i.e.,
S_{n}\left(i,j\right)=j+(i-1)n
, it is fairly easy to show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{n}\left(i,j\right)&=S_{n}\left(i,j\right)\left(1-P_{4}\left(i\bmod 4,j\bmod 4\right)\right)\\
&+S_{n}\left(n+1-i,n+1-j\right)P_{4}\left(i\bmod 4,j\bmod 4\right).\end{split}

(79)

It is a fairly elementary algebra to show that

M_{n}=\dfrac{n^{2}+1}{2}\mathbb{I}\mathbb{I}^{T}+\dfrac{n}{2}uv^{T}+\dfrac{vu^{T}}{2},

(80)

where

u=\begin{bmatrix}w\\
-\text{flip}\left(w\right)\end{bmatrix}_{4m\times 1},

(81)

where
w^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}(n-1)&-(n-3)&-(n-5)&(n-7)&\cdots&(-1)^{m}\end{bmatrix}
and

v^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1&-1&1&\cdots&1&-1&-1&1\end{bmatrix}_{1\times 4m}.

(82)

This gives us

\displaystyle\begin{split}M_{n}&=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}&u&v\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\dfrac{n^{2}+1}{2}&0&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{n}{2}\\
0&\dfrac{1}{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}^{T}\\
u^{T}\\
v^{T}\end{bmatrix},\\
&=USU^{T}.\end{split}

(83)

Here,
U=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{I}&u&v\end{bmatrix}
and
S=\dfrac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}n^{2}+1&0&0\\
0&0&n\\
0&1&0\end{bmatrix}
.

We have

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=U^{T}U=\dfrac{n}{3}\begin{bmatrix}3&0&0\\
0&n^{2}-1&0\\
0&0&3\end{bmatrix}.

(84)

Since
M_{n}
is rank
3
, we immediately obtain that
n-3
eigenvalues are zeros and the corresponding eigenvectors are the vectors orthogonal to
\mathbb{I}
,
u
and
v
. Now let’s obtain the non-zero eigenvalues of
M_{n}
. Let
\left(\lambda,x\right)
be a non-zero non-trivial eigenpair of
M_{n}
. This implies
M_{n}x=\lambda x
. Since eigenvector is in the range of
M_{n}
, and range of
M_{n}
is same as range of
U
(from Equation (
83
)), we have
x=Uy
, where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 1}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle USU^{T}Uy

\displaystyle=\lambda Uy,

(85)

Multiplying by the left inverse
(U^{T}U)^{-1}U^{T}
on both sides of (
85
), we get

\displaystyle SDy=\lambda y.

(86)

Hence,
\left(\lambda,y\right)
is an eigenpair of
SD\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
. We have

\displaystyle SD

\displaystyle=\dfrac{n}{6}\begin{bmatrix}3\left(n^{2}+1\right)&0&0\\
0&0&3n\\
0&n^{2}-1&0\end{bmatrix}.

(87)

The eigenpairs corresponding to non-zero eigenvalues are

1.

\left(\dfrac{n\left(n^{2}+1\right)}{2},\mathbb{I}_{n}\right)

2.

\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{n^{3}-n}{3}},\sqrt{3n}u+\sqrt{n^{2}-1}v\right)

3.

-\left(\dfrac{n}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{n^{3}-n}{3}},-\sqrt{3n}u+\sqrt{n^{2}-1}v\right)
.

This gives us the non-zero eigenvalues to be
\dfrac{n\left(n^{2}+1\right)}{2},\pm\dfrac{n}{2}\sqrt{\dfrac{n^{3}-n}{3}}
and thereby proving Theorem

.

6
Conclusion

Eigenvalues of the magic squares obtained using the magic() function in MATLAB are investigated. We obtain approximations of eigenvalues of odd and singly even magic squares with error bounds. Further, the eigenvalues of doubly even magic squares are exactly obtained. The approximation of the spectra involves some interesting connections with the spectrum of g-circulant matrices and the use of the Bauer-Fike theorem. We also highlight some observations which we have not been able to prove and hope to address at least some of them in the future.

Let
n
be an odd number of the form
2m+1
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Let
X_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a circulant matrix whose first row is

\bf{a}=\begin{bmatrix}m&m+1&m+2&\cdots&2m&0&1&\cdots&m-1\end{bmatrix}.

(88)

We then have the eigenvalues of
X_{n}
to be given by

\lambda_{j}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}&\text{ if }j=0\\
\dfrac{\left(-1\right)^{j}in}{2}\csc\left(\dfrac{\pi j}{n}\right)&\text{ if }j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}.\end{cases}

(89)

Proof.

The
j^{th}
eigenvalues is given by

\lambda_{j}={\bf{a}}v_{j}.

(90)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{j}&=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\left(\left(m+k\right)\mod n\right)\omega^{kj}=\omega^{-mj}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\left(\left(m+k\right)\mod n\right)\omega^{(m+k)j}\right)\\
&=\omega^{-mj}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2m}k\omega^{kj}\right).\end{split}

(91)

For
j=0
, we have
\lambda_{n}=0+1+\cdots+2m=\dfrac{n\left(n-1\right)}{2}
. Let
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
. We then have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{j}&=\omega^{-mj}\left(\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2m}k\omega^{kj}\right)=\dfrac{n\omega^{-mj}}{\omega^{j}-1}=\dfrac{n\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi imj}{n}\right)}{\exp\left(-\dfrac{2\pi ij}{n}\right)-1}=\dfrac{n\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ij(n-1)}{2n}\right)}{\exp\left(-\dfrac{2\pi ij}{n}\right)-1},\\
&=\dfrac{\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)n\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ij(n-1)}{2n}\right)}{\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)\left(\exp\left(-\dfrac{2\pi ij}{n}\right)-1\right)},\\
&=\dfrac{n\exp\left(\dfrac{2\pi ij(n)}{2n}\right)}{\left(\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)-\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)\right)}=\dfrac{n\exp\left(\pi ij\right)}{-2i\frac{\exp\left(\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)-\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi ij}{n}\right)}{2i}},\\
&=\dfrac{(-1)^{j+1}n}{2i\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi j}{n}\right)}=\left(-1\right)^{j}\dfrac{in}{2\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi j}{n}\right)}=\boxed{\dfrac{(-1)^{j}in}{2}\csc\left(\dfrac{\pi j}{n}\right)}.\end{split}

(92)

Hence,

X_{n}=\dfrac{F_{n}\Lambda_{n}F_{n}^{*}}{n},

(93)

where
\Lambda_{n}(j,j)=\lambda_{j}=\boxed{\dfrac{(-1)^{j}in}{2}\csc\left(\dfrac{\pi j}{n}\right)}
.

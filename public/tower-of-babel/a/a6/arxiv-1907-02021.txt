Holonomy groups of compact flat solvmanifolds
A. Tolcachier

Abstract
. In this article we study the holonomy group of flat solvmanifolds. It is known that the holonomy group of a flat solvmanifold is abelian; we give an elementary proof of this fact and moreover we prove that any finite abelian group is the holonomy group of a flat solvmanifold. Furthermore, we show that the minimal dimension of a flat solvmanifold with holonomy group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} coincides with the minimal dimension of a compact flat manifold with holonomy group ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} . Finally, we give the possible holonomy groups of flat solvmanifolds in dimensions 3, 4, 5 and 6; exhibiting in the latter case a general construction to show examples of non cyclic holonomy groups.

20H15, 22E25, 22E40, 53C29

1.
Introduction

A solvmanifold is defined as a compact homogeneous space
G\backslash\varGamma
of a simply connected solvable Lie group
G
by a discrete subgroup
\varGamma
. Solvmanifolds generalize the well known nilmanifolds which are defined similarly when
G
is nilpotent. Both nilmanifolds and solvmanifolds have provided a large number of examples and counterexamples in differential geometry . For instance, the first example of a symplectic manifold without Kähler structure, the so-called “Kodaira-Thurston manifold”, is a four dimensional nilmanifold
[
29
]
. However, many important global properties of nilmanifolds cannot be generalized to solvmanifolds, and for this reason these manifolds are currently widely studied. One of the most important features of nilmanifolds is a result proved by Nomizu, which says briefly that the cohomology of the nilmanifolds can be computed using invariant differential forms, since it is isomorphic to the cohomology of its Lie algebra
[
24
]
. Unfortunately this is not true in general for solvmanifolds, but only in particular cases. For example, when
G
is completely solvable
[
17
]
or when the “Mostow condition” holds
[
23
]
.

Solvmanifolds have had several applications in complex geometry. For instance let us mention the well known Oeljeklaus-Toma manifolds. In
[
25
]
, for any integers
s>0
and
t>0
, Oeljeklaus and Toma constructed compact complex manifolds by using number theory and they showed that for
t=1
these manifolds admit locally conformally Kähler metrics; they provide a counterexample to a conjecture made by Vaisman. Kasuya proved in
[
19
]
that OT-manifolds can be represented as solvmanifolds and using this description he proved that they do not admit any Vaisman metric.

Solvmanifolds have also had applications in theoretical physics, for instance in
[
14
]
, solutions of the supersymmetric equations of type II A are exhibited on six-dimensional solvmanifolds admitting an invariant symplectic half-flat structure.
In
[
26
]
the authors provide an example of a six-dimensional complex solvmanifold which admits many invariant solutions to the Strominger system with respect to certain family of Hermitian connections in the Green-Schwarz anomaly cancelation condition. Moreover, some of these solutions satisfy in addition the heterotic equations of motion.

We are interested in flat solvmanifolds, i.e, solvmanifolds admitting a flat Riemannian metric induced by a flat left invariant metric on the corresponding Lie group. Milnor
[
21
]
gave a characterization of Lie groups which admit a flat left invariant metric and he showed that they are all solvable of a very restricted form, proving that its Lie algebra decomposes orthogonally as an abelian subalgebra and an abelian ideal, where the action of the subalgebra on the ideal is by skew-adjoint endomorphisms. Moreover, such a Lie group
G
is of type (I), that is the eigenvalues of
\operatorname{ad}_{x}
are pure imaginary for all
x\in\operatorname{Lie}(G)
, and in particular
G
is unimodular. Some simply connected Lie groups of this class admit lattices (i.e. cocompact discrete subgroups), therefore the corresponding solvmanifolds admit a flat Riemannian metric induced by the flat left invariant metric on the Lie group.

Flat solvmanifolds are a special class of compact flat manifolds. A classical theorem asserts that a complete Riemannian manifold is flat if and only if its universal covering is isometric to Euclidean space. In particular, the manifold is isometric to a quotient of the form
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
for some discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and its fundamental group is isomorphic to
\Gamma
. In the compact case, such subgroups are well described by the well known Bieberbach’s theorems (
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
) and consequently they are called “Bieberbach groups”. Particularly, a Bieberbach group
\Gamma
admits a unique maximal normal abelian subgroup
\Lambda
of finite index. Furthermore, the finite group
\Gamma/\Lambda
can be identified with the Riemannian holonomy group of the compact flat manifold. A famous result of Auslander and Kuranishi
[
5
, Theorem 3]
establishes that every finite group is the holonomy group of a compact flat manifold.

The fundamental group of a solvmanifold
\varGamma\backslash G
is isomorphic to the lattice
\varGamma
and when the solvmanifold is flat it is isomorphic to a Bieberbach group. In
[
4
]
, fundamental groups of flat solvmanifolds were characterized.

The purpose of this article is to study the holonomy group of flat solvmanifolds. It was proved in
[
4
]
that these finite holonomy groups are abelian. In §3, by using Milnor’s result mentioned above, we provide an elementary proof of this fact (Theorem
3.2
). The main result in this section is the proof of the converse. In order to do this, we focus on a special class of flat solvmanifolds which are obtained as quotients of almost abelian Lie groups, since for these Lie groups there exists a criterion to determine the existence of lattices (see
[
10
]
). We show that the holonomy group of a flat almost abelian solvmanifold is cyclic (Theorem
3.7
), and furthermore for
n=p^{k}
and
n=2p^{k}
, with
p
a prime and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we give an explicit construction of an almost abelian Lie group and a lattice such that the quotient solvmanifold has holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
(Theorem
3.9
). Combining these results we prove the converse, namely, that every finite abelian group is the holonomy group of a flat solvmanifold (Theorem
3.10
).

Due to the result of Auslander and Kuranishi, it is interesting to know, given a finite group
G
, the minimal dimension of a compact flat manifold with holonomy group
G
. The minimal dimension was determined for several groups, for example for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
p
prime in
[
11
]
, and more generally for cyclic groups in
[
18
]
. Concerning this, we prove in §4 that the minimal dimension of a flat solvmanifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
coincides with the minimal dimension of a compact flat manifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, for
n\geq 3
(Theorem
4.4
).

The last section of the article (§5) is devoted to studying holonomy groups of low-dimensional flat solvmanifolds. By using a refined version of Milnor’s result proved by Barberis, Dotti and Fino in
[
6
]
, we give the possible holonomy groups of flat solvmanifolds in dimensions 3, 4, 5 and 6. Up to dimension 5 all flat solvmanifolds are quotients of almost abelian Lie groups, therefore their holonomy groups are cyclic. On the other hand, in dimension 6 we exhibit examples with non-cyclic abelian holonomy groups as particular cases of a general construction.

The results of this article are from part of my bachelor thesis under the direction of Adrian Andrada. I would like to thank Roberto Miatello and Juan Pablo Rossetti for their useful suggestions and comments.

2.
Preliminaries

In this short section we outline the basic theory of the topics studied in this article, namely compact flat manifolds and solvmanifolds equipped with invariant flat metrics.

2.1.
Bieberbach groups and compact flat manifolds

It is well known that the group of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong{\sf O}(n)\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\varphi(A)(v)=Av
. The pair
(A,v)
is identified with the isometry
f
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
f(x)=Ax+v
. This group is called the
group of rigid transformations
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Equipped with the product topology of
{\sf O}(n)
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a topological group. The
affine group

\mathcal{A}_{n}
is defined by
\mathcal{A}_{n}={\sf GL}(n,\mathbb{R})\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{n}
. An element
(A,v)\in\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called a
pure translation
if
A=\operatorname{Id}
. If
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the subgroup
\Gamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}
of pure translations of
\Gamma
will be usually denoted by
\Lambda
, and it is a torsionfree abelian normal subgroup of
\Gamma
.

Definition 2.1
.

A subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called
co-compact
if
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
is compact. A co-compact and discrete subgroup
\Gamma
is called a
crystallographic
group. If
\Gamma
is also torsionfree then
\Gamma
is called a
Bieberbach
group.

For a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subset\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, an equivalent condition to be torsionfree is that the action of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
be free. A nice class of group actions are the properly discontinuous actions. In general, we say that an action of a subgroup
G
of the group of homeomorphisms of a topological space
X
is
properly discontinuous
if for all
x\in X
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x
such that
gU\cap U=\emptyset
for all
g\neq e
. It is known that if a discrete subgroup
\Gamma
of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
acts freely on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the action is properly discontinuous and
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
admits a structure of a differentiable manifold. As a corollary, we have the next important theorem about Bieberbach groups.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a Bieberbach subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
the canonical projection. Then
\Gamma
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in a properly discontinuous manner, hence
\pi
is a covering map and
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma)\cong\Gamma
. Furthermore
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
admits a structure of a
n
-dimensional differentiable manifold such that
\pi
is a local diffeomorphism.

Bieberbach groups, and more generally crystallographic groups, are well described by three classic theorems known as “Bieberbach’s theorems”. A proof of them can be found in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Bieberbach’s First Theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a crystallographic subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\Lambda
the subgroup of pure translations of
\Gamma
. Then
\Lambda
is a normal free abelian subgroup of rank
n
such that
\Gamma/\Lambda
is a finite group. Furthermore,
\Lambda
is maximal as a normal abelian subgroup, in the sense that it contains every other normal abelian subgroup of
\Gamma
.

The group
\Gamma/\Lambda
is known as the
point group
of
\Gamma
or the
holonomy group
of
\Gamma
, in the case that
\Gamma
is a Bieberbach subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The point group of
\Gamma
can be identified with
r(\Gamma)\subset{\sf O}(n)
, which is a finite group, where
r
is the projection homomorphism given by
r(A,v)=A.
Indeed, the kernel of
r:\Gamma\to{\sf O}(n)
is
\Lambda
and thus
\Gamma/\Lambda\cong r(\Gamma)
.

Theorem 2.4

(Bieberbach’s Second Theorem)
.

Let
\Gamma_{1}
and
\Gamma_{2}
be crystallographic subgroups of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and let
f:\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
a group isomorphism. Then there exists
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
such that
f(\beta)=\alpha\beta\alpha^{-1}
for all
\beta\in\Gamma_{1}
. In other words, every isomorphism of crystallographic groups can be realized by an affine change of coordinates.

Theorem 2.5

(Bieberbach’s Third Theorem)
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of crystallographic subgroups of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Bieberbach’s theorems are fundamental tools to study compact flat manifolds. Indeed, a classical theorem asserts that a Riemannian manifold
M
is flat if and only if
M
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
where
\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that acts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in a properly discontinuous manner, that is to say,
\Gamma
is a Bieberbach subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Furthermore, the holonomy group of the Riemannian manifold
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
can be identified with what is called the holonomy group of the Bieberbach group, that is
\hol(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma)\cong r(\Gamma)
, see for instance
[
11
, Example 3.1]
.

We can reinterpret Bieberbach’s theorems in the context of compact flat manifolds.

Theorem 2.6

(Bieberbach First)
.

Let
M
a compact flat manifold. Then
M
admits a Riemannian covering by a flat torus and the covering is a local isometry. Furthermore, the holonomy group
\hol(M)
is finite.

Theorem 2.7

(Bieberbach Second)
.

Let
M
and
N
be compact flat manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups. Then there exists an affine equivalence
1
1

1

An
affine equivalence
between two Riemannian manifolds
M
and
N
equipped with connections
\nabla^{M}
and
\nabla^{N}
respectively, is a diffeomorphism
F:M\to N
such that
\nabla_{X}^{M}Y=\nabla^{N}_{dFX}(dFY)
for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.
between

and
N
equipped with the corresponding Levi-Civita connections.

Theorem 2.8

(Bieberbach Third)
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there are only finitely many classes of affine equivalence of compact flat manifolds of dimension
n
.

2.2.
Lie groups and flat solvmanifolds

In the context of Lie groups, the most interesting metrics are the left invariant ones, which satisfy that the left translations are isometries. When
G
is an abelian Lie group, it is clear that every left invariant metric is flat. An interesting fact proved by Milnor is that some left invariant metrics may be flat, even though
G
is not abelian. Indeed, he described the structure of a Lie group admitting one of these flat metrics (see
[
21
, Theorem 1.5]
). The precise criterion is stated as follows.

Theorem 2.9
.

A left invariant metric on a Lie group
G
is flat if and only if the associated Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
splits as an orthogonal direct sum
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra,
\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal and the linear transformation
\operatorname{ad}_{b}
is skew-adjoint for every
b\in\mathfrak{b}
.

We will call
(G,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
a flat Lie group if
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is a flat left invariant metric on the Lie group
G
and
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{e})
will be called a flat Lie algebra.

Using Milnor’s characterization of flat Lie algebras, Barberis, Dotti and Fino decompose even more a flat Lie algebra
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{e})
be a flat Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}
splits as an orthogonal direct sum,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{b}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

where
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is abelian and the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{so}[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is injective,
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is even and
\dim\mathfrak{b}\leq\frac{\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}{2}
;

(2)

\nabla_{X}=\operatorname{ad}_{X}
for all
X\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\nabla_{X}=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.

As a consequence, since
\{\operatorname{ad}_{X}\mid X\in\mathfrak{b}\}
is an abelian subalgebra of
\mathfrak{so}[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, it is included in a maximal abelian subalgebra and as all of these are conjugated, there exists an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}
of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that for
X\in\mathfrak{b}
,

[\operatorname{ad}_{X}]_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&&&\\
-a_{1}&0&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&0&a_{m}\\
&&&-a_{m}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\;a_{1},\,\ldots\,,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R},\;m=\frac{\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}{2}.

Note that a flat Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{e})
is
2
-step solvable, since
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is abelian; and unimodular
2
2

2

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
unimodular
if
\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{ad}_{X}=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.
, since
\operatorname{ad}_{X}
is skew-adjoint for all
X\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, we will see that we can obtain a special class of compact flat manifolds as quotients of Lie groups with a flat invariant metric. We recall first a theorem about the differentiable structure of a quotient manifold
G/\varGamma
in the case that
\varGamma
is a closed subgroup of the Lie group
G
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\varGamma
be a closed subgroup of a Lie group
G
. The left coset space
G/\varGamma
is a topological manifold of dimension equal to
\dim G-\dim\varGamma
, and has a unique smooth structure such that the quotient map
\pi:G\to G/\varGamma
is a smooth submersion.

When
\varGamma
is a discrete subgroup of a Lie group
G
, hence closed and
0
-dimensional, the quotient map
\pi:G\to G/\varGamma
is a local diffeomorphism. Let us assume now that
G
carries a left invariant Riemannian metric. Instead of working with the left coset space, we will consider the right coset space
\varGamma\backslash G
(note that the theorem above is also valid for the right coset space), to be able to induce a Riemannian metric on
\varGamma\backslash G
so that
\pi
results an isometry: Given
g\in G
and
u,v\in T_{\pi(g)}(\varGamma\backslash G)
, we define

\langle u,v\rangle_{\pi(g)}=\langle(d\pi)_{g}^{-1}u,(d\pi)_{g}^{-1}v\rangle_{g}.

The metric is well defined since
L_{\gamma}
is an isometry of
G
and
\pi\circ L_{\gamma}=\pi
for all
\gamma\in\varGamma
, where
L_{\gamma}(g)=\gamma g
for all
g\in G
. It is clear that
\pi
is a local isometry. In particular, if the metric on
G
is flat, the induced metric on
\varGamma\backslash G
.

The most interesting case is when
\varGamma
is a co-compact discrete subgroup of
G
, which is called a
lattice
of
G
.

Definition 2.12
.

A
solvmanifold

M
is a compact quotient
\varGamma\backslash G
where
G
is a simply connected solvable Lie group and
\varGamma
is a lattice of
G
. When
G
is nilpotent,
M
is said to be a
nilmanifold
.

Remark 2.13
.

It is worth mentioning that in the literature, different definitions of solvmanifolds are also considered. For example, L. Auslander defines a solvmanifold as a “homogeneous space of connected solvable Lie group”,
[
1
, p. 398]
, see also
[
2
,
3
]
. We will only consider solvmanifolds as in our definition above, and as a consequence they are always compact, orientable and paralellizable.

Every simply connected solvable Lie group is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see for instance
[
30
]
) for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This implies that a solvmanifold is aspherical, that is, the higher homotopy groups vanish; and
\pi_{1}(\varGamma\backslash G)\cong\varGamma
. The fundamental group plays an important role in the study of solvmanifolds. Indeed, Mostow’s Theorem show that the solvmanifolds are classified, up to diffeomorphism, by their fundamental groups. A proof of this can be found in
[
27
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Theorem 2.14

(Mostow)
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be simply connected solvable Lie groups with
\varGamma_{i}
a lattice in
G_{i}
for
i=1,2
. If
\phi:\varGamma_{1}\to\varGamma_{2}
is an isomorphism then there exists a diffeomorphism
\tilde{\phi}:G_{1}\to G_{2}
such that
\tilde{\phi}|_{\varGamma_{1}}=\phi
and
\tilde{\phi}(\gamma g)=\phi(\gamma)\tilde{\phi}(g)
for all
\gamma\in\varGamma_{1},g\in G_{1}
.

Corollary 2.15
.

Two solvmanifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups are diffeomorphic.

Concerning the construction of solvmanifolds and nilmanifolds, Malcev’s theorem
[
20
]
asserts that a simply connected nilpotent Lie group
G
admits a lattice if and only if its Lie algebra has a basis such that the structure constants are rational numbers, so it is not so difficult to construct nilmanifolds. Unfortunately, it is not easy to construct solvmanifolds, since there is not a general method to find lattices in a given solvable Lie group. It is known (
[
21
]
) that a necessary condition for a solvable Lie group to admit lattices is to be unimodular, but in general there are no known sufficient conditions.

A special class of solvmanifolds that we are going to consider later are those obtained as quotients of almost abelian Lie groups, which we will call
almost abelian solvmanifolds
.

Definition 2.16
.

A non-abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
almost abelian
if
\mathfrak{g}
has a codimension 1 abelian ideal, that is
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

It is clear that an almost abelian Lie algebra is solvable and it is nilpotent if and only if
\operatorname{ad}_{x}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is nilpotent. Moreover, if we denote by
\mathfrak{g}_{A}
the almost abelian Lie algebra with
\operatorname{ad}_{x}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=A
, it can be proved that
\mathfrak{g}_{A}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{B}
if and only if there exist
0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
C\in{\sf GL}(n,\mathbb{R})
such that
B=\lambda CAC^{-1}
.

The simply connected Lie group
G
corresponding to an almost abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is called an
almost abelian
Lie group, and can be written as
G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\varphi(t)=\exp(t\operatorname{ad}_{x})
.

A great advantage of almost abelian Lie groups is that there exists a criterion to determine whether they admit lattices or not (see
[
10
]
).

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{m}
be an almost abelian Lie group. Then
G
admits a lattice
\varGamma
if and only if there exists
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
\varphi(t_{0})
is conjugate to an integer matrix invertible in
\mathbb{Z}
. In this case, the lattice
\varGamma
is given by

\varGamma=t_{0}\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\varphi}P\mathbb{Z}^{m},

where
P^{-1}{\varphi(t_{0})}P
is an integer matrix.

3.
Holonomy of flat solvmanifolds

In this section we are going to consider solvmanifolds
\varGamma\backslash G
equipped with a flat metric induced by a flat left invariant metric on
G
, using the characterization given by Milnor mentioned above.

We do not assume that
G
acts by isometries on
\varGamma\backslash G
. In fact, it is well known that if
M
is a flat Riemannian manifold with a transitive group of isometries, then
M\cong T^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
for some
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
(see
[
31
]
).

In general, if a simply connected Lie group
G
admits a flat left invariant metric, then
G
is isometric as a Riemannian manifold to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the usual metric. We can identify
\varGamma
with a subgroup of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

Let
\psi:G\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
an isometry. Define a map
i:G\to\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by
i(g)=\tau_{g}
, where
\tau_{g}=\psi\circ L_{g}\circ\psi^{-1}
. It is clear that
i
is an injective group homomorphism, and therefore we can identify
\varGamma
with its image and consider
\varGamma\subset\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The solvmanifold
\varGamma\backslash G
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}/\varGamma
by considering the map
\phi([g])=[\psi(g)]
. Observe that
\phi
is well defined since

\phi([\gamma g])=[\psi(\gamma g)]=[\tau_{\gamma}(\psi(g))]=[\psi(g)]=\phi([g]).

Furthermore it is injective, since if
[\psi(g)]=[\psi(h)]
then
\psi(g)=\tau_{\gamma}(\psi(h))
, which implies
\psi(g)=\psi(\gamma h)
. Thus,
g=\gamma h
, or equivalently,
[g]=[h]
. The surjectivity is clear, and as
\phi
is a composition of local isometries and bijective,
\phi
results an isometry. Therefore
\varGamma
is a Bieberbach subgroup of
\Iso(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\hol(\mathbb{R}^{n}/\varGamma)\cong r(\varGamma)
. We recall that
r(\varGamma)
is identified with the finite group
\varGamma/\Lambda
where
\Lambda
is the maximal normal abelian subgroup of
\varGamma
. In conclusion,

(1)

\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)\cong\varGamma/\Lambda.

Remark 3.1
.

A compact flat manifold
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\Gamma
is called
primitive
when its first Betti number
\beta_{1}(M)
is zero. It is known that
M
is primitive if and only if the center of
\Gamma
is trivial, see for instance
[
28
]
. Any solvmanifold has
\beta_{1}\geq 1
due to a result of Hattori
[
17
]
. Therefore, any flat solvmanifold
\varGamma\backslash G
is not primitive and
\varGamma
has non trivial center.

Combining Milnor’s result (Theorem
2.9
) with equation (
1
) and the maximality of the normal abelian subgroup
\Lambda
, we give an elementary proof of the result in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The holonomy group of every flat solvmanifold is abelian.

Proof:.

Let
G
be a simply connected Lie group with a flat left invariant metric and let
\varGamma
be a lattice in
G
. If
\mathfrak{g}=\lie(G)
, it follows from Theorem
2.9
that we can write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathfrak{u}
where
\mathfrak{b}
is an abelian subalgebra,
\mathfrak{u}
is an abelian ideal and
\operatorname{ad}_{x}:\mathfrak{u}\to\mathfrak{u}
is skew-adjoint for every
x\in\mathfrak{b}
. We can rewrite
\mathfrak{g}
as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}}\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
where
k=\dim\mathfrak{b}
and
\ell=\dim\mathfrak{u}\geq 2
. Therefore
G
can be written as the semidirect product
G=\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
for some homomorphism
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
.

Since
\varGamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is a Bieberbach group, there exists a maximal normal abelian subgroup
\Lambda
of finite index in
\varGamma
. The subgroup
\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
is a normal abelian subgroup of
\varGamma
. Since
\Lambda
is maximal (Proposition
2.3
) we have that
\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\subset\Lambda
. We show next that
\varGamma/(\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
is abelian. Indeed, if we consider the composition

\varGamma\xhookrightarrow{\iota}\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{\ell})/\mathbb{R}^{\ell},

we have

\varGamma/(\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\cong\im(\pi\circ\iota)<(\mathbb{R}^{k}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{\ell})/\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\cong\mathbb{R}^{k},

then the group
\varGamma/(\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
, being isomorphic to a subgroup of an abelian group, is abelian.

Now, the natural map
\varGamma/(\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\to\varGamma/\Lambda
is an epimorphism and
\varGamma/(\varGamma\cap\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
is abelian, therefore
\varGamma/\Lambda
is abelian. It follows from (
1
) that the holonomy group is abelian.
∎

Our next goal is to prove the converse of Theorem
3.2
, namely, that every finite abelian group can be realized as the holonomy group of a flat solvmanifold. To do this, we focus on studying flat almost abelian Lie groups, in which we can construct lattices using Proposition
2.17
. First, we characterize the almost abelian Lie algebras in terms of the decomposition of a flat Lie algebra as in Theorem
2.10
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a flat Lie algebra and let

(2)

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{b}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]

be its decomposition as an orthogonal direct sum as in Theorem
2.10
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is almost abelian if and only if
\dim\mathfrak{b}=1
.

Proof:.

Let us assume first that
\mathfrak{g}
is almost abelian and let
\mathfrak{u}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an abelian ideal of codimension 1. Let
x\neq 0
such that
x\in\mathfrak{u}^{\perp}
, then
\mathfrak{g}
can be written as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathfrak{u}
, with
\mathbb{R}x\perp\mathfrak{u}
. We will prove that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathbb{R}x
in several steps:

{\rm(i)}
First, we prove that
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\oplus\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{u}
. Indeed,
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{u}
given that
\mathfrak{u}
is an ideal and also we have that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subset\mathfrak{u}
because if
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
decomposes as
z=ax+u
for
a\in\mathbb{R},a\neq 0,
and
u\in\mathfrak{u}
, then

0=[z,x]=[u,x].

Since
\mathfrak{u}
is abelian, it follows that
u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and thus,
x\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
which contradicts the fact that
\mathfrak{g}
is not abelian. Therefore,
a=0
and
z\in\mathfrak{u}
so that

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]\subset\mathfrak{u}.

{\rm(ii)}
We show next that
x\in\mathfrak{b}
. We write
x=z+b+y
according to (
2
) with
z\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}),b\in\mathfrak{b}
and
y\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
. Due to
{\rm(i)}
we get
0=\langle x,z\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle
. Since
\langle x,z\rangle=\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{2}+\langle b,z\rangle+\langle y,z\rangle=\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{2}
and similarly
\langle x,y\rangle=\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}
, it follows that
0=\left\lVert z\right\rVert^{2}=\left\lVert y\right\rVert^{2}
which implies
x\in\mathfrak{b}
.

{\rm(iii)}
Finally, we will prove that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathbb{R}x
. If we consider an element
v\in\mathfrak{b},v\perp x
, then
v\in\mathfrak{b}\cap\mathfrak{u}
. As
x\in\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{u}
is abelian it follows that
v\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and thus
v\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\cap\mathfrak{b}=\{0\}
. This proves that
\mathfrak{b}=\mathbb{R}x
.

Conversely, if
\dim\mathfrak{b}=1
then the ideal
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is abelian and has codimension 1. Therefore,
\mathfrak{g}
is almost abelian.
∎

As a consequence of this theorem we have that if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a flat Lie algebra, then
\operatorname{ad}_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is skew-adjoint and therefore

\mathbb{R}^{m}=\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{ad}_{x})\oplus\im(\operatorname{ad}_{x}),

where
\operatorname{Ker}(\operatorname{ad}_{x})=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
,
\im(\operatorname{ad}_{x})=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is even. Therefore there exist bases
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{1},f_{1}\,\ldots\,,e_{n},f_{n}\}
of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
of
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

[\operatorname{ad}_{x}]=\begin{pmatrix}0_{s\times s}&&&&&\\
&0&a_{1}&&&\\
&-a_{1}&0&&&\\
&&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&0&a_{n}\\
&&&&-a_{n}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
s=\dim\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathbb{R}^{s+2n}
is completely determined by
s,a_{1},\,\ldots\,,a_{n}
. Moreover, by reordering the basis we can assume that
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}>0
.

The simply connected Lie group associated to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\mathbb{R}^{s+2n}
is

G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{s+2n},

where

\varphi(t)=\exp(t\operatorname{ad}_{x})=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{s\times s}&0\\
0&\tilde{\varphi}(t)\end{pmatrix}

with

(3)

\tilde{\varphi}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(a_{1}t)&\sin(a_{1}t)&&&\\
-\sin(a_{1}t)&\cos(a_{1}t)&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\cos(a_{n}t)&\sin(a_{n}t)\\
&&&-\sin(a_{n}t)&\cos(a_{n}t)\end{pmatrix}

and
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}>0
.

Now we want to construct lattices in these almost abelian Lie groups. Concerning Proposition
2.17
, it can be guessed that the values of
t_{0}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
so that
\tilde{\varphi}(t)
is conjugate to an integer matrix are closely related to the multiples of
\pi
. We prove next that this assertion is true, proving that the order of the matrix
\tilde{\varphi}(t)
is finite, using a theorem of Kronecker about roots over
\mathbb{C}
of monic integer polynomials. A proof can be found in
[
15
]
.

Theorem 3.4

(Kronecker)
.

Let
\lambda\neq 0
be a root of a monic polynomial
f(z)
with integer coefficients. If all the roots of
f(z)
are in the unit disc
\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid|z|\leq 1\}
then
\lambda
is a root of unity.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
t_{0}\neq 0
such that the matrix
\varphi(t_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{s\times s}&0\\
0&\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})\end{pmatrix}
as in (
3
) is conjugate to an integer matrix. Then
\ord(\varphi(t_{0}))<\infty
.

Proof:.

It suffices to show that the matrix
\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})
has finite order. We write

\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(a_{1}t_{0})&&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&\theta(a_{n}t_{0})\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{where}\;\theta(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t)&\sin(t)\\
-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\end{pmatrix}.

If there exists
P\in{\sf GL}(s+2n,\mathbb{R})
such that
P^{-1}\varphi(t_{0})P
is an integer matrix, then the coefficients of the characteristic and minimal polynomials of
\varphi(t_{0})
are integers. It is straightforward to prove that the eigenvalues of
\varphi(t_{0})
are

1,\ldots,1,\cos(a_{1}t_{0})\pm i\sin(a_{1}t_{0}),\,\ldots\,,\cos(a_{n}t_{0})\pm i\sin(a_{n}t_{0}),

which all lie in the closed unit disc.

Therefore, we apply Kronecker’s Theorem and we conclude that all the eigenvalues of
\varphi(t_{0})
are roots of unity. In particular, given
j\in\mathbb{N},1\leq j\leq n
, there exists
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{(\cos(a_{j}t_{0})+i\sin(a_{j}t_{0}))}^{k_{j}}=1
, which implies
\theta(a_{j}t_{0})^{k_{j}}=\operatorname{Id}
. Thus,

\ord(\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0}))=\lcm(k_{1},\,\ldots\,,k_{n}).

Hence,
\ord\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})<\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Note that
\ord(\theta(t))<\infty
if and only if
t\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
. Therefore, a necessary condition for
\varphi(t_{0})
to be conjugate to an integer matrix is that
a_{i}t_{0}\in\pi\mathbb{Q}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
. However, we will see later that this is not a sufficient condition. A sufficient condition is that the complex eigenvalues of
\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})
are all different and the coefficients of its characteristic polynomial are integers. In this case, it is well known that
\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})
is conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
to the companion matrix over
\mathbb{C}
of its characteristic polynomial, which is an integer matrix. However, if two real matrices are conjugate over
\mathbb{C}
then they are conjugate over
\mathbb{R}
.

Once we know how to construct lattices in flat almost abelian Lie groups, we need to compute the holonomy group of the corresponding solvmanifolds, what we do in the next result. In particular, we show that the holonomy group of a flat almost abelian solvmanifold is cyclic.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{s+2n}
be a flat almost abelian Lie group and let
t_{0}\neq 0
such that the matrix
\varphi(t_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\operatorname{Id}_{s\times s}&0\\
0&\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})\end{pmatrix}
is conjugate to an integer
matrix through a matrix
P\in{\sf GL}(s+2n,\mathbb{R})
, where

\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(a_{1}t_{0})&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\theta(a_{n}t_{0})\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{with}\;\theta(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t)&\sin(t)\\
-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\end{pmatrix}.

If
\varGamma
is the lattice given by
\varGamma=t_{0}\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\varphi}P\mathbb{Z}^{s+2n}
and

d=\lcm\left(\ord(\theta(a_{1}t_{0})),\,\ldots\,,\ord(\theta(a_{n}t_{0}))\right),

then

\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{d}&\text{if}\;d>1,\\
\{e\}&\text{if}\;d=1.\end{cases}

Proof:.

If
d=1
, then
\varphi(t_{0})=\operatorname{Id}
, hence
G
is abelian and thus the solvmanifold
\varGamma\backslash G
is a torus, which has trivial holonomy group. If
d>1
, we claim that the maximal normal abelian subgroup is
\Sigma=dt_{0}\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\varphi}P\mathbb{Z}^{s+2n}
. Indeed, it is clear that
dt_{0}\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\varphi}P\mathbb{Z}^{s+2n}
is normal in
\varGamma
and abelian. Furthermore, we show next that
\Sigma
is maximal: let
\Lambda
be the maximal normal abelian subgroup of
\varGamma
. According to Proposition
2.3
,
\Sigma\subset\Lambda
, and we will prove that
\Lambda\subset\Sigma
. Let
\gamma_{\lambda}=(t_{0}k,Pm)\in\Lambda
and
\gamma_{\sigma}=(dt_{0}\ell,Pr)\in\Sigma
. It suffices to show that
k=dk_{0}
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Due to the fact that
\gamma_{\lambda}
and
\gamma_{\sigma}
commute, we deduce, looking at the last coordinates, that

Pm+\varphi(t_{0})^{k}Pr=Pr+Pm,

which implies
\varphi(t_{0})^{k}Pr=Pr
. Then
P^{-1}\varphi(t_{0})^{k}P
is a matrix that fixes
r
for all
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{s+2n}
. If we let
r
vary through the vectors of the canonical basis, it follows that
P^{-1}\varphi(t_{0})^{k}P=\operatorname{Id}
and thus
\varphi(t_{0})^{k}=\operatorname{Id}
. Now,
d=\ord(\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0}))
, so
k=dk_{0}
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Therefore
\Lambda\subset\Sigma
and this concludes the proof of the statement.

Finally, let
\phi:\varGamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{d}
be the epimorphism defined by

\phi(t_{0}k,Pm)=[k]_{d}.

It is clear that
(t_{0}k,Pm)\in\operatorname{Ker}(\phi)\iff k=dk_{0}\iff(t_{0}k,Pm)\in\Lambda
. The formula
\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)\cong\varGamma/\Lambda
implies
\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Note that if
\varphi(t_{0})
is conjugate to an integer matrix
E
, then
\det(E)=1
, so
E
is invertible over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Finally, to prove the converse of Theorem
3.2
, we consider the case when
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is a flat Lie algebra with
\dim\mathfrak{g}=2n+1
and
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=2n
for some

. Hence,
\dim\mathfrak{b}=1
so
\mathfrak{g}
is almost abelian and has trivial center, or equivalently,
\operatorname{ad}_{x}:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}^{2n}
is invertible. We can write

[\operatorname{ad}_{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a_{1}&&\\
-a_{1}&0&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&0&a_{n}\\
&&&-a_{n}&0\end{pmatrix},

for some orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
and
a_{1}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}>0
. The corresponding simply connected Lie group is

where

(4)

\varphi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos(a_{1}t)&\sin(a_{1}t)&&&\\

&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\cos(a_{n}t)&\sin(a_{n}t)\\

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and let

. Then there exist flat solvmanifolds of dimension
(p-1)p^{k-1}+1
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2p^{k}}
, respectively. If
p=2
the assertion is valid for
k\geq 2
.

Proof:.

We will start by constructing a flat solvmanifold of dimension
2^{k-1}+1
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{2^{k}}
, for
k\geq 2
. Let
n=2^{k-2}
, we choose
t_{0}=\frac{\pi}{2^{k-1}}
and
a_{\ell}=2\ell-1
for
1\leq\ell\leq n
, so that

a_{1}t_{0}=\frac{\pi}{2^{k-1}},\quad\ldots\quad,\quad a_{n}t_{0}=\frac{(2^{k-1}-1)\pi}{2^{k-1}}.

The eigenvalues of
\varphi(t_{0})
as in (
4
) for this choice of
t_{0}
and
a_{\ell}
are

\exp\left({\pm\frac{j\pi i}{2^{k-1}}}\right),\;\;1\leq j\leq 2^{k-1}-1,\;\;j\,\text{odd}.

Note that

\exp\left(-\frac{j\pi i}{2^{k-1}}\right)=\exp\big((2^{k}-j)\frac{2\pi i}{2^{k}}\big),

and if
1\leq j\leq 2^{k-1}-1
then
2^{k-1}+1\leq 2^{k}-j\leq 2^{k}-1
. In conclusion, the set of eigenvalues of
\varphi(t_{0})
is
\{\exp(\frac{2\pi i}{2^{k}}j)\mid 1\leq j\leq 2^{k},\,\gcd(j,2^{k})=1\}

\varphi(t_{0})
are different,
\varphi(t_{0})
is conjugate to the companion matrix of

which has integer coefficients. Then, by Proposition
2.17
, the associated Lie group

(for this choice of

and

3.7
that the holonomy group of the solvmanifold

is
\mathbb{Z}_{d}

with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
for

. Let

and

. To choose the parameters

, note that there are exactly
n
integers

since the numbers that are not relatively prime to

for

. We choose

are

\exp\left(\pm\frac{2\pi i}{p^{k}}\ell\right),\quad 1\leq\ell\leq\frac{p^{k}-1}{2},\;\gcd(\ell,p^{k})=1.

Again, note that
\exp(-\frac{2\pi i}{p^{k}}\ell)=\exp(\frac{2\pi ip^{k}-2\pi i\ell}{p^{k}})=\exp((p^{k}-\ell)\frac{2\pi i}{p^{k}})

then

. Therefore, the eigenvalues of

-th roots of unity. Thus, the characteristic polynomial of

It follows from Theorem

\mathbb{Z}_{2p^{k}}

. Let

Finally, we prove the converse of Theorem

.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
A
be a finite abelian group. Then there exists a flat solvmanifold with holonomy group
A
.

Proof:.

We may decompose

A\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p_{m}^{k_{m}}},

according to the structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups, where
p_{1},\,\ldots\,,p_{m}
are primes (not necessarily distinct) and
k_{1},\,\ldots\,,k_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
. In Theorem
3.9
we have constructed a solvmanifold
\varGamma_{i}\backslash G_{i}
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{p_{i}^{k_{i}}}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Let
G
be the Lie group
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m}
and
\varGamma
the lattice of
G
given by the product of the lattices
\varGamma_{i}
,
\varGamma=\varGamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\varGamma_{m}.
We consider the solvmanifold

\varGamma\backslash G\cong\varGamma_{1}\backslash G_{1}\times\cdots\times\varGamma_{m}\backslash G_{m}

equipped with the product metric, which is flat. As the holonomy group of a Riemannian product is the product of the holonomy groups, it follows that

\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)=\hol(\varGamma_{1}\backslash G_{1})\times\cdots\times\hol(\varGamma_{m}\backslash G_{m})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p_{1}^{k_{1}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{p_{m}^{k_{m}}}\cong A.

∎

Corollary 3.11
.

Given a finite abelian group
A
there exists a Kähler flat solv manifold with holonomy group
A
.

Proof:.

Let
A
be a finite abelian group. By Theorem
3.10
there exists a flat solvmanifold
\varGamma\backslash G
of dimension
n
with holonomy group
A
. In
[
6
]
, it is proved that every flat solvmanifold of even dimension admits an invariant complex structure compatible with the metric which is Kähler. Therefore, if
n
is even, there is nothing to prove, and if
n
is odd, it suffices to take the solvmanifold obtained as the quotient of the Lie group
\mathbb{R}\times G
by the lattice
\mathbb{Z}\times\varGamma
equipped with the product metric, which is flat. Then

\hol(\mathbb{Z}\times\varGamma\backslash\mathbb{R}\times G)\cong\hol(S^{1})\times\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)\cong\{e\}\times A\cong A.

∎

4.
Minimal dimension

The dimension of the solvmanifold constructed in Theorem
3.10
might not be the minimal one. It is well known that every finite group
G
is the holonomy group of some compact flat manifold
[
5
]
. It is interesting to find the minimal dimension of a compact flat manifold with holonomy group
G
. In
[
11
]
, L. Charlap classified the compact flat manifolds with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, called
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-manifolds, and he proved that the minimal dimension of such a manifold is
p
. More generally, Hiller
[
18
]
proved that the minimal dimension, denoted
d(n)
, of a compact flat manifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is
\Phi(n)+1
, where
\Phi
is an “additive” version of the Euler totient function,
\varphi
.

Definition 4.1
.

The function
\Phi
is defined by the two following rules:

(1)

If
p
is prime,
\Phi(p^{k})=\varphi(p^{k})
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(2)

If
m
and
n
are coprime, then
\Phi(mn)=\Phi(m)+\Phi(n)
, unless
m=2
and
n
is an odd number,
n\geq 3
, in which case
\Phi(2n)=\Phi(n)
.

Note that
\Phi(1)=0
,
\Phi(2)=1
and
\Phi(n)
is even for all
n\geq 3
.

Consider now the problem of finding the minimal dimension of a flat solvmanifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, which we will denote
d_{S}(n)
. At first glance, we know that
d(n)\leq d_{S}(n)
for all
n
. If we look at
n=2
the inequality is strict. Indeed,
d(2)=2
because the Klein Bottle is a compact flat manifold of dimension 2 with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, and
d_{S}(2)=3
given that every flat solvmanifold of dimension 2 is diffeomorphic to a torus, and the “dicosm” (see next section) is a flat solvmanifold of dimension 3 with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Fortunately, this is the only case of the strict inequality, and that is what we are going to prove immediately, namely,

d(n)=d_{S}(n),\;\text{for all}\;n\geq 3.

The main idea to obtain this result is to reduce the dimension of the solvmanifold in Theorem
3.10
.

First we prove an auxiliary result in which we exhibit a way to construct a flat solvmanifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{\lcm(m,n)}
beginning with flat solvmanifolds with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, respectively.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G_{a}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a}}\mathbb{R}^{2d_{1}}
and
G_{b}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{b}}\mathbb{R}^{2d_{2}}
be flat almost abelian Lie groups. If there exist
t_{0},\,t_{1}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
such that
\varphi_{a}(t_{0})
and
\varphi_{b}(t_{1})
are conjugate to integer matrices with
\ord(\varphi_{a}(t_{0}))=m
and
\ord(\varphi_{b}(t_{1}))=n
, then there exists a flat almost abelian solvmanifold of dimension
2d_{1}+2d_{2}+1=\dim G_{a}+\dim G_{b}-1
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{\lcm(m,n)}
.

Proof:.

Let
\varphi_{a}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(a_{1}t)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\theta(a_{d_{1}}t)\end{pmatrix}
,
\varphi_{b}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\theta(b_{1}t)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\theta(b_{d_{2}}t)\end{pmatrix}
and
G_{d}
the Lie group
G_{d}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{d}}\mathbb{R}^{2d_{1}+2d_{2}}
where

\varphi_{d}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{a}(t)&\\
&\varphi_{c}(t)\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\;c_{j}t_{0}=b_{j}t_{1},\,\text{for}\;1\leq j\leq d_{2}.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
invertible matrices such that
P_{1}^{-1}\varphi_{a}(t_{0})P_{1}=E_{1}
and
P_{2}^{-1}\varphi_{b}(t_{1})P_{2}=E_{2}
, with
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
invertible integer matrices. Then
P=\begin{pmatrix}P_{1}&\\
&P_{2}\end{pmatrix}
conjugates the matrix
\varphi_{d}(t_{0})
to the integer matrix
\begin{pmatrix}E_{1}&\\
&E_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. It follows from Proposition
2.17
that the Lie group
G_{d}
admits a lattice
\varGamma_{d}
, and by Theorem
3.7
the corresponding solvmanifold has holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
with

d=\ord(\varphi_{d}(t_{0}))=\lcm(\ord(\varphi_{a}(t_{0})),\ord(\varphi_{c}(t_{1}))=\lcm(m,n).

∎

We can generalize this result inductively and thus we obtain

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
G_{1}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{1}}}\mathbb{R}^{2d_{i}},\ldots,G_{n}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{n}}}\mathbb{R}^{2d_{n}}
be flat almost abelian Lie groups. If there exist
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
such that
\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i})
is conjugate to an invertible integer matrix
E_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq n
and
\ord(\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i}))=m_{i}
then there exists a flat almost abelian solvmanifold of dimension

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\dim G_{i}-1)+1=1+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}

with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
with
d=\lcm(m_{1},\,\ldots\,,m_{n})
.

Next we prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n\geq 3
, then
d(n)=d_{S}(n)
.

Proof:.

First we prove the statement in the particular case when
n
is a power of a prime. If
n=p^{k}
with
p
prime and
k\in\mathbb{N}
(
k\geq 2
if
p=2
), by Theorem
3.9
there exists a flat almost abelian solvmanifold of dimension
(p-1)p^{k-1}+1
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{p^{k}}
, which implies

d_{S}(p^{k})\leq(p-1)p^{k-1}+1=\varphi(p^{k})+1=\Phi(p^{k})+1=d(p^{k}).

Therefore,
d_{S}(p^{k})=d(p^{k})
.

According to the definition of
\Phi
we will have to consider separately the case when
n
is
2q
with
q
odd. We begin by considering the other cases.

First let
n=2^{k_{0}}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}}
with
k_{0}\geq 2
and
p_{i}
different odd primes with
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Theorem
3.9
, we deduce that there exist
t_{0},t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
such that
\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i})
(for some choice of
a_{i}
depending on
t_{i}
) are conjugate to integer matrices
E_{i}
and
\ord(\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i}))=p_{i}^{k_{i}}
for all
0\leq i\leq m
, where
p_{0}=2
. Considering the almost abelian Lie groups

G_{0}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{0}}}\mathbb{R}^{\Phi(2^{k})},G_{1}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{1}}}\mathbb{R}^{\Phi(p_{1}^{k_{1}})},\;\ldots\;,\;G_{m}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{m}}}\mathbb{R}^{\Phi(p_{m}^{k_{m}})},

it follows from Corollary
4.3
that there exists a flat solvmanifold of dimension
\Phi(2^{k_{0}})+\Phi(p_{1}^{k_{1}})+\cdots+\Phi(p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1
with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. In consequence,

\displaystyle d_{S}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(2^{k_{0}})+\Phi(p_{1}^{k_{1}})+\cdots+\Phi(p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=\Phi(2^{k_{0}}p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=d(n).

Therefore
d_{S}(n)=d(n)
. Clearly, if
k_{0}=0
the proof follows analogously.

Finally, if
n=2q
with
q
odd, we can write
n=2p_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}}
with
p_{i}
different odd primes and
k_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. According to Theorem
3.9
, there exist
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\in\mathbb{R}-\{0\}
such that
\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i})
(for some choice of
a_{i}
depending on
t_{i}
) are conjugate to integer matrices
E_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
and, furthermore
\ord(\varphi_{a_{1}}(t_{1}))=2p_{1}^{k_{1}}
and
\ord(\varphi_{a_{i}}(t_{i}))=p_{i}^{k_{i}}
for
2\leq i\leq m
. Thus, if we consider the almost abelian Lie groups
G_{1}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{1}}}\mathbb{R}^{\Phi(2p_{1}^{k_{1}})}
and
G_{i}=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi_{a_{i}}}\mathbb{R}^{\Phi(p_{i}^{k_{i}})}
for
2\leq i\leq m
, it follows from Corollary
4.3
that

\displaystyle d_{S}(n)

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(2p_{1}^{k_{1}})+\Phi(p_{2}^{k_{2}})+\cdots+\Phi(p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=\Phi(p_{1}^{k_{1}})+\Phi(p_{2}^{k_{2}})+\cdots+\Phi(p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=\Phi(p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=\Phi(2p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}})+1

\displaystyle=d(n).

Therefore,
d_{S}(n)=d(n)
.

In conclusion,
d_{S}(n)=d(n)
for all
n\geq 3
.
∎

In other words, this theorem says that the compact flat manifold with holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
of minimal dimension can be chosen as a solvmanifold.

As a consequence of the proof of Theorem
4.4
we obtain the following result, which provides another proof of Theorem
3.10
for cyclic groups.

Corollary 4.5
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N},n\geq 2
, there exists a flat almost abelian solvmanifold which has holonomy group
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.

5.4.
Dimension 6

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{b}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
a non abelian flat Lie algebra of dimension 6. There are two possibilities for
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, namely
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=2
or
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=4
.

If
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=2
, then
\dim\mathfrak{b}=1
(recall that in general
\dim\mathfrak{b}\leq\frac{\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}{2}
) and thus
\dim\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=3
. Therefore the simply connected Lie group corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}=\mathbb{R}x\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{5}
can be written as
G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\tilde{\varphi}}\mathbb{R}^{5}
, with
\tilde{\varphi}(t)=\operatorname{Id}_{3}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}\cos(t)&\sin(t)\\
-\sin(t)&\cos(t)\end{pmatrix}
. It can be easily seen that
\tilde{\varphi}(t_{0})
is conjugate to an integer matrix if and only if

t_{0}\in\{\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi}{2},\frac{2\pi}{3},\frac{4\pi}{3},\frac{\pi}{3},\frac{5\pi}{3},\pi,2\pi\}+2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. In this case, all the possible holonomy groups are
\{e\},\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4},\mathbb{Z}_{6}
.

If
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=4
, then
\dim\mathfrak{b}
can be 1 or 2.

Let
\dim\mathfrak{b}=1
, so that
\dim\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=1
. The corresponding simply connected Lie group is
G=\mathbb{R}\ltimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{R}^{5}
, where
\varphi(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
&\theta(at)&\\
&&\theta(bt)\end{pmatrix}
with
a\geq b>0
. As in the proof of Lemma
5.5
, it can be seen that
\varphi(t_{0})
is conjugate to an integer matrix if and only if
at_{0}
and
bt_{0}
are as in Lemma
5.5
. Therefore, the possible holonomy groups are
\{e\},\mathbb{Z}_{2},\mathbb{Z}_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{4},\mathbb{Z}_{5},\mathbb{Z}_{6},\mathbb{Z}_{8},\mathbb{Z}_{10}
and

\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{b}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
be a flat Lie algebra with
\dim\mathfrak{b}=\frac{\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}{2}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic as a Lie algebra to
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\times\mathfrak{e}(2)^{\dim\mathfrak{b}}
.

Proof:.

Let
\dim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=2n
. It follows from Theorem
2.10
that there exists an orthonormal basis
\mathcal{B}=\{f_{1},\,\ldots\,,f_{2n}\}
of
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathfrak{b}^{*}
such that for all
x\in\mathfrak{b}
,

[\operatorname{ad}_{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{pmatrix}&\alpha_{1}(x)&&&\\
-\alpha_{1}(x)&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&\alpha_{n}(x)\\
&&&-\alpha_{n}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\operatorname{ad}:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{so}[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
is injective, we have
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\ker\alpha_{i}=\{0\}
. It follows that
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{b}^{*}
. Now let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the dual basis of
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}.
Then

[\operatorname{ad}_{e_{i}}]_{\mathcal{B}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&\\
&&0&1&&\\
&&-1&0&&\\
&&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&&0\end{pmatrix},

i.e. the only brackets different from zero are
[e_{i},f_{2i-1}]=-f_{2i}
and
[e_{i},f_{2i}]=f_{2i-1}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{i}=\text{span}\{e_{i},f_{2i-1},f_{2i}\}
, which is an ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{e}(2)
for all
i
. Therefore
\mathfrak{g}
splits as a direct product of Lie algebras,

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{g}_{n}\simeq\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\times\mathfrak{e}(2)^{n}.

∎

Remark 5.8
.

The basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{b}
appearing in the proof is not necessarily orthonormal and therefore the decomposition of Theorem
5.7
is not necessarily orthogonal.

The associated simply connected Lie group is
G=\mathbb{R}^{s}\times E(2)^{n}
, where
s=\dim\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and
n=\dim\mathfrak{b}
. Let
\varGamma_{1},\varGamma_{2},\,\ldots\,,\varGamma_{n}
be lattices in
E(2)
. We construct a lattice in
G
choosing the product of the lattices,
\varGamma=\mathbb{Z}^{s}\times\varGamma_{1}\times\cdots\times\varGamma_{n}
. The corresponding solvmanifold,
\varGamma\backslash G\cong\mathbb{Z}^{s}\backslash\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\varGamma_{1}\backslash E(2)\times\cdots\times\varGamma_{n}\backslash E(2)
is equipped with a flat metric
g_{0}
induced by the flat left invariant metric on
G
. On the other hand, if we consider on
\varGamma\backslash G
the product metric
g_{*}
where each factor
\varGamma_{i}\backslash E(2)
carries a flat metric induced by a flat left invariant metric on
E(2)
, then
(\varGamma\backslash G,g_{0})
and
(\varGamma\backslash G,g_{*})
are two compact flat manifolds with isomorphic fundamental groups. By Theorem
2.7
there exists an affine equivalence between them, which implies
\hol(\varGamma\backslash G,g_{0})\cong\hol(\varGamma\backslash G,g_{*})
, but the last one is isomorphic to
\hol(T^{s})\times\hol(\varGamma_{1}\backslash E(2))\times\cdots\times\hol(\varGamma_{n}\backslash E(2)).

In conclusion

\hol(\varGamma\backslash G)=\mathbb{Z}_{k_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{k_{n}},\quad\text{with}\;k_{j}=|\Lambda_{j}\backslash\varGamma_{j}|,

where
\Lambda_{j}
is the maximal normal abelian subgroup of
\varGamma_{j}
. Moreover, it follows from Corollary
5.2
that
k_{i}\in\{2,3,4,6\}
.

Remark 5.9
.

As mentioned in Corollary

, every flat solvmanifold of even dimension admits a Kähler structure. In

, the direct sums in (

Non-uniform continuity of the Fokas-Olver-Rosenau-Qiao equation in Besov spaces
Xing Wu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we prove that the solution map of Fokas-Olver-Rosenau-Qiao
equation (FORQ) is not uniformly continuous
on the initial data in Besov spaces. Our result extends the previous non-uniform continuity in Sobolev spaces (Nonlinear Anal., 2014)
[
11
]
to Besov spaces and is consistent with the present work (J. Math. Fluid Mech., 2020)
[
17
]
on Novikov equation up to some coefficients when dropping the extra term
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
in FORQ.

Keywords:
Fokas-Olver-Rosenau-Qiao equation, Non-uniform continuous dependence, Besov spaces

MSC (2010):
35B30; 35G25; 35Q53

1
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following Fokas-Olver-Rosenau-Qiao equation (FORQ)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-u_{xxt}+3u^{2}u_{x}-u_{x}^{3}-4uu_{x}u_{xx}+2u_{x}u_{xx}^{2}-u^{2}u_{xxx}+u_{x}^{2}u_{xxx}=0,~~t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},~~x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

Eq. (
1
) written in a slightly different form was first derived
by Fokas
[
3
]
as an integrable generalisation of the modified KdV equation. Soon after, Fuchssteiner
[
5
]

and Olver-Rosenau
[
19
]
independently obtained similar versions of this equation by performing a simple explicit algorithm based on the bi-Hamiltonian representation of the classically integrable system. Several years later, the concise form written above was recovered by Qiao
[
20
]
from the two-dimensional Euler equations by using an approximation procedure.

The entire integrable hierarchy related
to the FORQ equation was proposed by Qiao
[
21
]
. It also has bi-Hamiltonian structure, which was first derived in
[
19
]
and then in
[
20
]
, admits Lax pair
[
20
]
and peakon travelling wave solutions that are orbitally stable
[
6
,
22
,
18
]
. For more discussion about Lax integrability and peakon solutions of FORQ we refer to
[
2
]
, where this equation is also referred as the modified Camassa-Holm equation.

The local well-posedness and
ill-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the FORQ equation (
1
) in Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces were studied in the series of papers
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
4
]
. It was showed by Himonas-Mantzavinos
[
11
]
that the FORQ is well-posed in Sobolev space
H^{s}
with
s>\frac{5}{2}
in the sense of Hadamard. Fu et al.
[
4
]
established the local well-posedness in Besov space
B_{p,r}^{s}
with
s>\max\{2+\frac{1}{p},\frac{5}{2}\}
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
.
After the non-uniform dependence for some dispersive equations was studied by Kenig et al.
[
14
]
, the issue of non-uniform continuity of solutions on initial data has attracted much more attention, such as on classical Camassa-Holm equation
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
16
]
and on famous Novikov equation
[
10
,
17
]
. It was further proved in
[
11
]
that the dependence on initial data is sharp, i.e. the data-to-solution map is continuous but not uniformly continuous.

For studying the non-uniform continuity of the FORQ equation, it is
more convenient to express (
1
) in the following equivalent nonlocal form

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u^{2}\partial_{x}u=\frac{1}{3}(\partial_{x}u)^{3}-\frac{1}{3}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[(\partial_{x}u)^{3}]-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[\frac{2}{3}u^{3}+u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}],\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\;\qquad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.4)

When removing
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
from (
1.4
), (
1.4
) becomes the following Novikov equation up to some coefficients

\begin{cases}u_{t}+u^{2}\partial_{x}u=-\frac{1}{3}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[(\partial_{x}u)^{3}]-\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[\frac{2}{3}u^{3}+u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}],\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\;\qquad t>0,~x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

(1.5)

Recently, Li, Yu and Zhu
[
17
]
have proved that the solution map of Novikov equation is not uniformly continuous dependence on the initial data in the Besov spaces
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
s>\max\{1+\frac{1}{p},\frac{3}{2}\}
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
. It is noticed that well-posedness for Novikov equation holds for
s>\max\{1+\frac{1}{p},\frac{3}{2}\}
while well-posedness for FORQ holds
for
s>\max\{2+\frac{1}{p},\frac{5}{2}\}
. This difference between the well-posedness index of these equations may be explained by the presence of the extra term
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
in FORQ, which is not quasi-linear and is absent from Novikov equation
[
11
]
.

Up to the present, there is no result for the non-uniform continuous dependence of FORQ in Besov space and it seems more difficult due to the presence of the extra term
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
, which elevates two regularities, compared with Novikov equation and the method developed for the Novikov equation in
[
17
]
will make it more complex. In this paper, we will follow a different route to bypass this problem. Firstly, consider a new system satisfied by
(1-\partial_{x})u:\triangleq v
. Secondly, for any bounded set
v_{0}
in working space, the corresponding solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})
can be approximated by a function of first degree of time
t
with convective term and nonlocal term being coefficients. With suitable choice of initial data, the difference between two solutions will produce a term from convective term which will not be small for small time, and thus we obtain the non-uniform continuous dependence of FORQ.
These will be described in more detail later.

Now, we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
s>\max\{2+\frac{1}{p},\frac{5}{2}\}
,
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
. The solution map
u_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
of the initial value problem (
1.4
) is not
uniformly continuous from any bounded subset of
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
. More precisely,
there exist two sequences
u^{1,n}(0,x)
and
u^{2,n}(0,x)
such that

\displaystyle\|u^{1,n}(0,x),u^{2,n}(0,x)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}\lesssim 1,\qquad\qquad\qquad\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u^{1,n}(0,x)-u^{2,n}(0,x)\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}=0,

but

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{1,n}(0,x))-\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{2,n}(0,x))\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}\gtrsim t,\qquad t\in[0,T_{0}],

with small positive time
T_{0}
for
T_{0}\leq T
.

Remark 1.1

Since
B_{2,2}^{s}=H^{s}
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
, our result extends the previous non-uniform continuity in Sobolev spaces
[
11
]
to Besov spaces.

Remark 1.2

When dropping the extra term
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
in FORQ, we can get the same result on Novikov equation up to some coefficients, which is consistent with the present work
[
17
]
on Novikov equation. The method we use in proving Theorem
1.1
is different from
[
17
]
and is more general.

Notations
: Given a Banach space
X
, we denote the norm of a function on
X
by
\|\|_{X}
, and

\displaystyle\|\cdot\|_{L_{T}^{\infty}(X)}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\cdot\|_{X}.

The symbol

A\lesssim B
means that there is a uniform positive constant
C
independent of
A
and
B
such that
A\leq CB
.

2
Littlewood-Paley analysis

In this section, we will review the definition of Littlewood-Paley decomposition and nonhomogeneous Besov space, and then list some useful properties. For more details, the readers can refer to
[
1
]
.

There exists a couple of smooth functions
(\chi,\varphi)
valued in
[0,1]
, such that
\chi
is supported in the ball
\mathcal{B}\triangleq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
,
\varphi
is supported in the ring
\mathcal{C}\triangleq\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
and
\varphi\equiv 1
for
\frac{4}{3}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{2}
. Moreover,

\forall\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\,\chi(\xi)+{\sum\limits_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)}=1,

\forall\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},\,\,{\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)}=1,

|j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2\Rightarrow\textrm{Supp}\,\ \varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\cap\textrm{Supp}\,\,\varphi(2^{-j^{\prime}}\cdot)=\emptyset,

j\geq 1\Rightarrow\textrm{Supp}\,\,\chi(\cdot)\cap\textrm{Supp}\,\,\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)=\emptyset.

Then, we can define the nonhomogeneous dyadic blocks
\Delta_{j}
and nonhomogeneous low frequency cut-off operator
S_{j}
as follows:

\Delta_{j}{u}=0,\,\,if\,\,j\leq-2,\quad\Delta_{-1}{u}=\chi(D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

\Delta_{j}{u}=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u),\,\,if\,\,j\geq 0,

S_{j}{u}={\sum\limits_{j^{\prime}=-\infty}^{j-1}}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}{u}.

Definition 2.1
(
[
1
]
)

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
. The nonhomogeneous Besov space
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
consists of all tempered distribution
u
such that

\displaystyle||u||_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\triangleq\Big|\Big|(2^{js}||\Delta_{j}{u}||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big|\Big|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty.

In the following, we list some basic lemmas and properties about Besov space which will be frequently used in proving our main result.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
1
]
)
(1) Algebraic properties:
\forall s>0,

B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

\cap

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach algebra.
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a Banach algebra
\Leftrightarrow B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\Leftrightarrow s>\frac{d}{p}
or
s=\frac{d}{p},

r=1
.

(2) For any
s>0
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, there exists a positive constant
C=C(d,s,p,r)
such that

\|uv\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Big(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).

(3) Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
f
be an
S^{m}-
multiplier (i.e.,
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is smooth and satisfies that
\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
such that
|\partial^{\alpha}f(\xi)|\leq\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
). Then the operator
f(D)
is continuous from
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
B_{p,r}^{s-m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(4) For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
(1-\partial_{x})^{-1}
is an isomorphic mapping from
B_{p,r}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
1
,
15
]
)
Let
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
and

\sigma>-\min\{\frac{1}{p},1-\frac{1}{p}\}.

There exists a constant
C=C(p,r,\sigma)
such that for any smooth solution to the following linear transport equation:

\quad\partial_{t}f+v\partial_{x}f=g,\quad\;f|_{t=0}=f_{0}.

We have

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|f(s)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}\leq Ce^{CV_{p}(v,t)}\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}+\int^{t}_{0}\|g(\tau)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}\tau\Big),

(2.6)

with

\displaystyle V_{p}(v,t)=\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{B^{\frac{1}{p}}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}s,&\ \ \mathrm{if}\;\sigma<1+\frac{1}{p},\\
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{B^{\sigma}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}s,&\ \ \mathrm{if}\;\sigma=1+\frac{1}{p}\mbox{ and }r>1,\\
\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{B^{\sigma-1}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R})}\mathrm{d}s,&\ \ \mathrm{if}\;\sigma>1+\frac{1}{p}\ \mathrm{or}\ \{\sigma=1+\frac{1}{p}\mbox{ and }r=1\}.\end{cases}

3
Reformulation of the System

Due to the presence of the extra term
(\partial_{x}u)^{3}
in FORQ, it seems difficult to deal with Eq. (
1.4
) directly. Therefore, we shall first differentiate FORQ with respect to
x
and then simplify the resulting expression, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\partial_{x}u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}u)^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u-2u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}-u^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u+[\frac{2}{3}u^{3}+u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}]

(3.1)

\displaystyle-(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\partial_{x}[\frac{1}{3}(\partial_{x}u)^{3}]-(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[\frac{2}{3}u^{3}+u(\partial_{x}u)^{2}].

Let
v=(1-\partial_{x})u
, we have from (
1.4
) and (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}v=(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v-\frac{1}{3}u^{3}-\frac{1}{3}v^{3}-\Phi_{1}(v)-\Phi_{2}(v),\\
u=(1-\partial_{x})^{-1}v,\\
v(0,x)=(1-\partial_{x})u_{0}(x)\triangleq v_{0},\end{array}\right.

where the nonlocal terms
\Phi_{1}(v),\Phi_{2}(v)
are defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(v)=(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}[\frac{8}{3}u^{3}-\frac{1}{3}v^{3}-3u^{2}v],\;\;\;\Phi_{2}(v)=\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{2}[\frac{1}{3}v^{3}-u^{2}v].

Since
(1-\partial_{x})^{-1}
is an isomorphic mapping from
B_{p,r}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})
into
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, the non-uniform continuous dependence of
u
in
B_{p,r}^{s}
then can be transformed into that of
v
in
B_{p,r}^{s-1}.

4
Non-uniform continuous dependence

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. However, as explained above, we will directly consider Eq. (
3
) satisfied by
v
.

Firstly, we establish the estimates of the difference between the solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})
and initial data
v_{0}
in different Besov norms. That is

Proposition 4.1

Assume that
||v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, we have

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})-v_{0}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\lesssim t||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim t\big(||v_{0}||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\big),

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})-v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim t\big(||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\big).

Proof
For simplicity, denote
v(t)=\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})
. Firstly, according to the local well-posedness result
[
4
,
11
]
, there exists a positive time
T=T(||u_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}},s,p,r)
such that the solution
u(t)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B_{p,r}^{s})
. Moreover, by Lemmas
2.1
-
2.2
, for all
t\in[0,T]
and
\gamma\geq s-2
, there holds

\displaystyle||u(t)||_{B^{\gamma}_{p,r}}\leq C||u_{0}||_{B^{\gamma}_{p,r}}\;\;\text{or}\;\;||v(t)||_{B^{\gamma-1}_{p,r}}\leq C||v_{0}||_{B^{\gamma-1}_{p,r}}.

(4.1)

Now we shall estimate the different Besov norms of the term
v(t)-v_{0}
, which can be bounded by
t
multiplying the corresponding Besov norms of initial data
v_{0}
.

It follows by differential mean value theorem and the Minkowski inequality that

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}||\partial_{\tau}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\int^{t}_{0}||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}v^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}u^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{1}(v)||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{2}(v)||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Here, we shall only have to estimate
||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
and
||\frac{1}{3}v^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
, since the other terms can be processed in a similar more relaxed way and have the same bound as
||v^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
.

Using the fact that
B_{p,r}^{s-2}
is an Banach algebra with
s-2>\max\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{2}\}
, together with the product estimates (2) in Lemma
2.1
, one has

\displaystyle||v^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-2uv)||_{L^{\infty}}||\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||(v^{2}-2uv)||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||\partial_{x}v||_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

where we have used the relation
u=(1-\partial_{x})^{-1}v
, and
(1-\partial_{x})^{-1}
is a
S^{-1}-
multiplier, which is continuous from
B_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}-1}(\mathbb{R})
to
B_{p,r}^{s^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
, thus
||u||_{B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r}}\lesssim||v||_{B^{s^{\prime}-1}_{p,r}}
for any
s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Therefore, in view of (
4.1
), for
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t||v||^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{s-2}_{p,r})}||v||_{L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{s}_{p,r})}+||v||^{3}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{s-1}_{p,r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim t(||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}).

Following the same procedure of estimate as above, we have

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||\partial_{\tau}v||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}v^{3}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}u^{3}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{1}(v)||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{2}(v)||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t||v||^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{s-2}_{p,r})}||v||_{L_{t}^{\infty}(B^{s-1}_{p,r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}},

and

\displaystyle||v^{3}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v^{2}||_{L^{\infty}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v^{2}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}||v||_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v||^{2}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-2uv)||_{L^{\infty}}||\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||(v^{2}-2uv)||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}||\partial_{x}v||_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}+||v||^{2}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}},

hence,

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||\partial_{\tau}v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||(v^{2}-2uv)\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}v^{3}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}u^{3}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{1}(v)||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{2}(v)||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t(||v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}(B^{s-2}_{p,r})}||v||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(B^{s+1}_{p,r})}+||v||^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t}(B^{s-1}_{p,r})}||v||_{L^{\infty}_{t}(B^{s}_{p,r})})

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t(||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}),

Thus, we finish the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2

Assume that
||v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1
. Then under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, there holds

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v_{0})-v_{0}-t\mathbf{v}_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim t^{2}\big(||v_{0}||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{4}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\big),

where
\mathbf{v}_{0}=(v_{0}^{2}-2u_{0}v_{0})\partial_{x}v_{0}+\frac{1}{3}v_{0}^{3}+\frac{1}{3}u_{0}^{3}+\Phi_{1}(v_{0})+\Phi_{2}(v_{0}).

Proof
Using differential mean value theorem and the Minkowski inequality, we first arrive at

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}-t\mathbf{v}_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||\partial_{\tau}v-\mathbf{v}_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}||v^{2}\partial_{x}v-v_{0}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||2uv\partial_{x}v-2u_{0}v_{0}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}v^{3}-\frac{1}{3}v_{0}^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\frac{1}{3}u^{3}-\frac{1}{3}u_{0}^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{1}(v)-\Phi_{1}(v_{0})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int^{t}_{0}||\Phi_{2}(v)-\Phi_{2}(v_{0})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using (3) in Lemma
2.1
, it is sufficient to estimate
||v^{2}\partial_{x}v-v_{0}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
,
||2uv\partial_{x}v-2u_{0}v_{0}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
and
||v^{3}-v_{0}^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
, since the other terms can be processed in a similar more relaxed way and have the same bound as
||v^{3}-v_{0}^{3}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}
.

It should be noticed that according to (
4.1
),
||v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim||v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1
, which will be frequently used later.

Due to the fact that
B_{p,r}^{s-2}
is an Banach algebra with
s-2>\max\{\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{2}\}
, combining with the product estimates (2) in Lemma
2.1
, we get

\displaystyle||v^{2}\partial_{x}v-v_{0}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-v_{0}^{2})\partial_{x}v+v_{0}^{2}(\partial_{x}v-\partial_{x}v_{0})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||(v^{2}-v_{0}^{2})\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}^{2}(\partial_{x}v-\partial_{x}v_{0})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v^{2}-v_{0}^{2}||_{L^{\infty}}||\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v^{2}-v_{0}^{2}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||\partial_{x}v||_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+||v_{0}^{2}||_{L^{\infty}}||\partial_{x}v-\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}^{2}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||\partial_{x}v-\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}},

\displaystyle||2uv\partial_{x}v-2u_{0}v_{0}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle||2(u-u_{0})v\partial_{x}v+2u_{0}(v\partial_{x}v-v_{0}\partial_{x}v_{0})||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||u-u_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v\partial_{x}v||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||u_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}||v\partial_{x}v-v_{0}\partial_{x}v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}},

and

\displaystyle\|v^{3}-v_{0}^{3}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}=\|(v-v_{0})(v^{2}+vv_{0}+v_{0}^{2})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}.

Taking the above estimates into (
4.2
), which together with Proposition
4.1
yield

\displaystyle||v(t)-v_{0}-t\mathbf{v}_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}d\tau+||v-v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}})d\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle t^{2}\big(||v_{0}||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v_{0}||^{4}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v_{0}||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\big).

Thus, we complete the proof of Proposition
4.2
.
\Box

Now, we move on the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
Let
\hat{\phi}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
be an even, real-valued and non-negative funtion on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)=

\displaystyle 1,

if
|x|\leq\frac{1}{4}
,

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)=

\displaystyle 0,

if
|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}
.

Define the high frequency function
f_{n}
and the low frequency function
g_{n}
by

f_{n}=2^{-ns}\phi(x)\sin\Big(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x\Big),\qquad g_{n}=2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\phi(x),\quad n\gg 1.

It has been showed in
[
16
]
that
\|f_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{\sigma}}\lesssim 2^{n(\sigma-s)}
.

Let

v^{1,n}(0,x)=(1-\partial_{x})(f_{n}+g_{n}),\;v^{2,n}(0,x)=(1-\partial_{x})f_{n}.

Consider Eq. (
3
) with initial data
v^{1,n}(0,x)
and
v^{2,n}(0,x)
, respectlively. Obviously, we have

\displaystyle||v^{1,n}(0,x)-v^{2,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}=||(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\leq C2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

which means that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}||v^{1,n}(0,x)-v^{2,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}=0.

It is easy to show that

\displaystyle||v^{1,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||f_{n}+g_{n}||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim||f_{n}+g_{n}||_{B^{s-\frac{1}{2}}_{p,r}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle||v^{1,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s+\sigma}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||f_{n}+g_{n}||_{B^{s+\sigma+1}_{p,r}}\lesssim 2^{n(\sigma+1)}\qquad\mathrm{for}\qquad\sigma\geq-\frac{3}{2},

\displaystyle||v^{2,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s+t}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle||f_{n}||_{B^{s+t+1}_{p,r}}\lesssim 2^{n(t+1)}\qquad\qquad\;\;\mathrm{for}\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

which imply

\displaystyle\big(||v^{1,n}(0,x)||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v^{1,n}(0,x)||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v^{1,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v^{1,n}(0,x)||^{4}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v^{1,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\big)\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\big(||v^{2,n}(0,x)||^{3}_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+||v^{2,n}(0,x)||^{2}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v^{2,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s}_{p,r}}+||v^{2,n}(0,x)||^{4}_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}||v^{2,n}(0,x)||_{B^{s+1}_{p,r}}\big)\lesssim 1.

Furthermore, since
v^{1,n}(0,x)
and
v^{2,n}(0,x)
are both bounded in
B_{p,r}^{s-1}
, according to Proposition
4.2
, we deduce that

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{1,n}(0,x))-\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{2,n}(0,x))||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle t\big|\big|([v^{1,n}(0,x)]^{2}-2u^{1,n}(0,x)v^{1,n}(0,x))\partial_{x}v^{1,n}(0,x)

\displaystyle-([v^{2,n}(0,x)]^{2}-2u^{2,n}(0,x)v^{2,n}(0,x))\partial_{x}v^{2,n}(0,x)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{3}([v^{1,n}(0,x)]^{3}-[v^{2,n}(0,x)]^{3})+[\frac{1}{3}[u^{1,n}(0,x)]^{3}+\Phi_{1}(v^{1,n}(0,x))+\Phi_{2}(v^{1,n}(0,x))]

\displaystyle-[\frac{1}{3}[u^{2,n}(0,x)]^{3}+\Phi_{1}(v^{2,n}(0,x))+\Phi_{2}(v^{2,n}(0,x))]\big|\big|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}-\big|\big|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}\big|\big|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}-Ct^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~t\big|\big|([v^{1,n}(0,x)]^{2}-2u^{1,n}(0,x)v^{1,n}(0,x))\partial_{x}v^{1,n}(0,x)

\displaystyle-([v^{2,n}(0,x)]^{2}-2u^{2,n}(0,x)v^{2,n}(0,x))\partial_{x}v^{2,n}(0,x)\big|\big|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}-C2^{-\frac{n}{2}}-Ct^{2}.

For the sake of simplicity and convenience, in the following we denote

v^{i,n}(0,x)\triangleq v_{i},\;\;u^{i,n}(0,x)\triangleq u_{i},\;\;i=1,2.

The coefficient of the first order term of
t
in the last inequality in (

) is simplified as

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{2}-2u_{1}v_{1})\partial_{x}v_{1}-(v_{2}^{2}-2u_{2}v_{2})\partial_{x}v_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{1}-v_{2}^{2}\partial_{x}v_{2})-2(u_{1}v_{1}\partial_{x}v_{1}-v_{2}\partial_{x}v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}-I_{2}.

Bring in the concrete form of
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
where necessary, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}v_{1}+v_{2}^{2}(\partial_{x}v_{1}-\partial_{x}v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x})(f_{n}+g_{n})+v_{2}^{2}(\partial_{x}v_{1}-\partial_{x}v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})-(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}-(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}+v_{2}^{2}\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2}),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}v_{1}+2v_{2}(u_{1}\partial_{x}v_{1}-u_{2}\partial_{x}v_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}(1-\partial_{x})(f_{n}+g_{n})+2v_{2}u_{1}\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})+2v_{2}(u_{1}-u_{2})\partial_{x}v_{2}

\displaystyle 2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})-2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}-2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}

\displaystyle+2v_{2}u_{1}\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})+2v_{2}(u_{1}-u_{2})\partial_{x}v_{2}.

, after simple calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{n}+g_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s}}+\|v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\|(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\|v_{2}^{2}\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v_{2}^{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\|2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}(f_{n}+g_{n})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\|2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|u_{1}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}g_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v_{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|u_{1}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}},

\displaystyle\|2v_{2}(u_{1}-u_{2})\partial_{x}v_{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|v_{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}v_{2}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

While

\displaystyle[2u_{1}(v_{1}-v_{2})-(v_{1}^{2}-v_{2}^{2})]\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}[g_{n}+\partial_{x}(2f_{n}+g_{n})]\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}

\displaystyle(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}+2(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n},

using product law (2) in Lemma
2.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}f_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\|_{B_{p,r}^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot 2^{0}\cdot 2^{n(2-s)}+2^{-\frac{n}{2}}\cdot 2^{n(1-s)}\cdot 2^{n}\lesssim 2^{-n(s-\frac{3}{2})}.

Taking the above estimates into (
4.3
), we find that

\displaystyle||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{1,n}(0,x))-\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{2,n}(0,x))||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\geq t\big|\big|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\big|\big|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}-C2^{-\frac{n}{2}}-Ct^{2}.

(4.4)

For the term
(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}
, it can be verified that
\Delta_{j}\big((1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\big)=0,

and
\Delta_{n}\big((1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\big)=(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}

for
n\geq 5.
Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\big|\big|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\big|\big|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}=2^{n(s-1)}\big|\big|(1-\partial_{x})g_{n}(1+\partial_{x})g_{n}\partial_{x}^{2}f_{n}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\|2^{-2n}(1-\partial_{x})\phi(1+\partial_{x})\phi\partial_{x}^{2}\phi\sin(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x)+\frac{17}{12}2^{-n}(1-\partial_{x})\phi(1+\partial_{x})\phi\phi\cos(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x)

\displaystyle+\frac{17}{12}2^{-n}(1-\partial_{x})\phi(1+\partial_{x})\phi\partial_{x}\phi\cos(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x)+\frac{17}{12}(1-\partial_{x})\phi(1+\partial_{x})\phi\partial_{x}\phi\sin(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x)\big|\big|_{L^{p}}

by the Riemann Theorem, where
(1-\partial_{x})\phi(1+\partial_{x})\phi\partial_{x}\phi\triangleq\psi(x),

which together with (
4.4

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}||\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{1,n}(0,x))-\mathbf{S}_{t}(v^{2,n}(0,x))||_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\gtrsim t\quad\text{for}\ t\ \text{small enough}.

That is to say, the solution map
v_{0}\rightarrow S_{t}(v_{0})
of the initial value problem (
3
) depends not uniformly continuous on initial data in
B_{p,r}^{s-1}
.

Since

into
B_{p,r}^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, hence the non-uniform continuous dependence of
v
in
B_{p,r}^{s-1}
is consistent with that of
u
in
B_{p,r}^{s}.

Thus, this completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

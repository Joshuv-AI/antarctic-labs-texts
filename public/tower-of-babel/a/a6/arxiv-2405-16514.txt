The stable category of monomorphisms between (Gorenstein) projective modules with applications Thanks: The research of the second author was in part supported by a grant from IPM
Abdolnaser Bahlekeh, Fahimeh Sadat Fotouhi, Mohammad Amin Hamlehdari and Shokrollah Salarian

Abstract
. Let ( S , 𝔫 ) (S,{\mathfrak{n}}) be a commutative noetherian local ring and let ω ∈ 𝔫 \omega\in{\mathfrak{n}} be non-zero divisor. This paper is concerned with the two categories of monomorphisms between finitely generated (Gorenstein) projective S S -modules, such that their cokernels are annihilated by ω \omega . It is shown that these categories, which will be denoted by 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ⁡ ( ω , 𝒫 ) {\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P}) and 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ⁡ ( ω , 𝒢 ) {\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G}) , are both Frobenius categories with the same projective objects. It is also proved that the stable category 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ¯ ​ ( ω , 𝒫 ) \underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P}) is triangle equivalent to the category of D-branes of type B, 𝖣𝖡 ⁡ ( ω ) \mathsf{DB}(\omega) , which has been introduced by Kontsevich and studied by Orlov. Moreover, it will be observed that the stable categories 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ¯ ​ ( ω , 𝒫 ) \underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P}) and 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ¯ ​ ( ω , 𝒢 ) \underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G}) are closely related to the singularity category of the factor ring R = S / ( ω ) R=S/({\omega)} . Precisely, there is a fully faithful triangle functor from the stable category 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ¯ ​ ( ω , 𝒢 ) \underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G}) to 𝖣 𝗌𝗀 ⁡ ( R ) \operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R) , which is dense if and only if R R (and so S S ) are Gorenstein rings. Particularly, it is proved that the density of the restriction of this functor to 𝖬𝗈𝗇 ¯ ​ ( ω , 𝒫 ) \underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P}) , guarantees the regularity of the ring S S .

2.
The categories of monomorphisms between (Gorenstein) projective modules

This section is devoted to investigating two kinds of subcategories of the monomorphism category of finitely generated
S
-modules. Precisely, we focus on monomorphisms between (Gorenstein) projective
S
-modules in which their cokernels are annihilated by
\omega
. It will be shown that these categories are Frobenius with the same projective objects. Let us begin this section with the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

By the category
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
, we mean a category that whose objects are those
S
-monomorphisms
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
, where
G\in\mathcal{G}(S),

P\in\mathcal{P}(S)
and
{\rm{Coker}}f
is an
R
-module. Here
\mathcal{G}(S)
(resp.
\mathcal{P}(S)
) is the category of all finitely generated Gorenstein projective (resp. projective)
S
-modules. Moreover, a morphism
\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{0}):(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P)\longrightarrow(G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{\prime}}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{\prime})
between two objects is a pair of
S
-homomorphisms
\psi_{1}:G\rightarrow G^{\prime}
and
\psi_{0}:P\rightarrow P^{\prime}
such that
\psi_{0}f=f^{\prime}\psi_{1}
. It is clear that
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is a full additive subcategory of the monomorphism category of finitely generated
S
-modules. The full subcategory of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
consisting of those objects
(P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Q)
with
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}(S)
, will be denoted by
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
.

Recall that an exact category in the sense of Quillen is an additive category endowed with a class of kernel-cokernel pairs, called conflations, subject to certain axioms, see
[
8
, Definition 2.1]
and also
[
16
, Appendix A]
. Let us give the precise definition.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
8
, Definition 2.1]
) Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category and let
\mathcal{E}
be a class of kernel-cokernel pairs in
\mathcal{C}
. A kernel-cokernel pair
(i,p)
in
\mathcal{E}
, which is also called an admissible pair, is a pair of composable morphisms
X^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\rightarrow}}X^{\prime\prime}
, where
i
is a kernel of
p
and
p
is a cokernel of
i
. In this case,
i
(resp.
p
) is called an admissible monic (resp. admissible epic).

The pair
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{E})
is called an exact category, if the following axioms are satisfied:

(E0)

For each object
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the identity morphism
{\mathsf{id}}_{C}
is an admissible monic.

(E0^{op})

For each object
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the identity morphism
{\mathsf{id}}_{C}
is an admissible epic.

(E1)

The class of admissible moics is closed under composition.

(E1^{\rm op})

The class of admissible epics is closed under composition.

(E2)

The push-out of an admissible monic along an arbitrary morphism exists and yields an admissible monic.

(E2^{op})

The pull-back of an admissible epic along an arbitrary morphism exists and yields an admissible epic.

Admissible pairs, admissible monics, and admissible epics are also called conflations, inflations and deflations, respectively, see
[
16
]
and
[
24
]
.

For example, an abelian category is naturally an exact category whose conflations are the class of all short exact sequences. More generally, an extension-closed subcategory of an abelian category is an exact category in the same manner. This is a basic recognition principle of exact categories, for many examples arise in this way, see
[
8
, Lemma 10.20]
.

It is known that the morphism category of
S
-modules,
\mathsf{Mor}(S),
is an abelian category, and so, it will be an exact category. As
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is a full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mor}(S)
,
we will observe that this exact structure is inherited by
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. Namely, we show that, considering those short exact sequences in
\mathsf{Mor}(S)
with terms in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
as conflations,
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is an exact category. In this direction, a natural way is to show that
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is an extension-closed subcategory of
\mathsf{Mor}(S)
. But, as indicated in Remark
2.3
, this is not the case. So, we need to check all the axioms of exact categories directly. This will be done through a series of results.

Remark 2.3
.

Take a short exact sequence
0\longrightarrow(G^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P^{\prime})\longrightarrow(X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}Y)\longrightarrow(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\rightarrow}}P)\longrightarrow 0
in
\mathsf{Mor}(S)
such that the left and the right terms are in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. So the snake lemma gives the short exact sequence of
S
-modules,
0\rightarrow{\rm{Coker}}f\rightarrow{\rm{Coker}}g\rightarrow{\rm{Coker}}h\rightarrow 0

and
{\rm{Coker}}h
are

-modules, this need not be true for

{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is not an extension-closed subcategory of
\mathsf{Mor}(S)
.

(Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l_{0}}}{{\rightarrow}}G^{\prime}\oplus P)
, where
l_{0}=[h^{\prime}~~-\alpha]^{t}
, we have
l_{0}l=\omega\oplus\omega
, and in particular,

is a monomorphism. Now it is easy to see that

0\longrightarrow(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(Q\oplus P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega\oplus{\mathsf{id}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\oplus P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(G^{\prime}\oplus P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l}}{{\rightarrow}}Q)\longrightarrow 0,

with
\psi_{0}=[{\mathsf{id}}~~-hg_{\operatorname{\mathsf{\Sigma}}}]
and
\psi_{1}=\tiny{\left[\begin{array}[]{ll}h^{\prime}&0\\
-\alpha&{\mathsf{id}}\end{array}\right]}
, is a short exact sequence of monomorphisms. Since
{\rm{Coker}}(\omega\oplus{\mathsf{id}})
is annihilated by
\omega
, applying the snake lemma yields that the same is true for
{\rm{Coker}}l
. Consequently, the latter sequence is a short exact sequence in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
, and in particular,
{\rm{Coker}}\varphi\in{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. Hence the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Each projective and injective object in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is equal to direct summands of finite direct sums of objects of the form
(Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathsf{id}}}}{{\rightarrow}}Q)\oplus(P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
for some projective
S
-modules
P,Q
. In particular, an object is injective if and only if it is projective.

Proof.

Let us deal only with the projective case, because the other one is obtained dually. Take an arbitrary projective object
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}P)\in{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,G)
. According to the proof of Proposition
2.14
, there is an epimorphism
(Q\oplus P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathsf{id}}\oplus\omega}}{{\longrightarrow}}Q\oplus P)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi}}{{\longrightarrow}}(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
, with
Q
projective. Since
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
is projective,
\varphi
is a split epimorphism, and so,
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
will be a direct summand of
(Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathsf{id}}}}{{\rightarrow}}Q)\oplus(P\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\omega}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
, giving the desired result. Moreover, the second assertion follows from the first assertion together with Lemma
2.12
. So the proof is finished.
∎

Theorem 2.16
.

{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is a Frobenius category.

Proof.

According to Proposition
2.8
,
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is an exact category. Moreover, this category has enough projective and injective objects by Proposition
2.14
. Finally, Lemma
2.15
reveals that projective objects are the same as injective objects in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. So the proof is finished.
∎

Corollary 2.17
.

{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
is a Frobenius subcategory of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. In particular, they have the same projective objects.

Proof.

At first, one should note that by Corollary
2.9
,
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
is an extension-closed exact subcategory of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G}).
Take an arbitrary object
(P\rightarrow Q)\in{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
. Since
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
is a Frobenius category, one may get short exact sequences
0\longrightarrow(G\rightarrow P_{1})\longrightarrow(Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{1})\longrightarrow(P\rightarrow Q)\longrightarrow 0
and
0\longrightarrow(P\rightarrow Q)\longrightarrow(Q^{\prime}_{1}\rightarrow Q^{\prime}_{1})\longrightarrow(G^{\prime}\rightarrow P^{\prime}_{1})\longrightarrow 0
in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
, such that the middle terms are projective objects of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. Now since
\mathcal{P}(S)
is closed under kernels of epimorphisms,
G
will be a projective
S
-module. Also, by making use of the fact that any Gorenstein projective
S
-module of finite projective dimension is projective, we infer that
G^{\prime}
is projective. Consequently, these short exact sequences lie in the category
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
, and in particular,
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
is an admissible subcategory of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
, see for example
[
10
, page 46]
. This ensures that
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
is a Frobenius category and its projective objects are the same as
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
. So the proof is finished.
∎

The significance of Frobenius categories lies in their natural connection to triangulated categories. Namely, if
\mathcal{A}
is a Frobenius category, then the stable category
\underline{\mathcal{A}}
is a triangulated category such that its shift functor is given by the inverse of the syzygy functor on
\underline{\mathcal{A}}
, see
[
15
,
16
]
. These types of triangulated categories are referred to as “algebraic” in the context of Keller’s definition
[
17
]
.

Remark 2.18
.

In
[
3
]
, a homotopy category

of
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
has been introduced and studied. The objects of
\mathsf{H}{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
are the same as

and its morphism sets are morphism sets in
{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})
modulo null-homotopic. A morphism

such that

[

. As shown in

Theorem 2.19
.

The inclusion functor

induces a fully faithful triange functor

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

There is a fully faithful triangle functor
T:\underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
sending each object
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Q)
to
{\rm{Coker}}f
, viewed as a stalk complex. Moreover,
T
is dense if and only if
R
(and so,
S
) are Gorenstein rings.

Proof.

It is known that
T_{2}:\underline{\mathcal{G}}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
assigning each object to its stalk complex, is a fully faithful triangle functor, see
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
. This, in conjunction with Proposition
3.4
, yields that
T=T_{2}\circ\underline{T}_{1}:\underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
sending each object
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P)
to
{\rm{Coker}}f
, as a stalk complex, is a fully faithful triangle functor, giving the first assertion. For the second assertion, assume that
R
is Gorenstein. By a fundamental result of Buchweitz and Happel
[
7
]
, the triangle functor
T_{2}
is equivalence. This together with Proposition
3.4
, would imply that
T
is an equivalence functor, as well.
Conversely, assume that the functor
T
is dense. Assume that
k
is the residue field of
S
. Since
T
is dense, there is an object
(G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}P)\in{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{G})
such that
T(f)={\rm{Coker}}f\cong k
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
. Assume that
e:{\rm{Coker}}f\longrightarrow k
is an isomorphism of stalk complexes in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
. So
e
is represented by a diagram

\textstyle{X^{\bullet}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{s_{1}}
\scriptstyle{s_{2}}
\textstyle{{\rm{Coker}}f}
\textstyle{k}

of complexes and maps in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D^{b}}}(R)
, where the mapping cones
\mathsf{cone}(s_{1})
and
\mathsf{cone}(s_{2})
are prefect complexes. Take the exact triangle
X^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm{Coker}}f\longrightarrow\mathsf{cone}(s_{1})\longrightarrow X^{\bullet}[1]
in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D^{b}}}(R)
. Since
\mathsf{cone}(s_{1})
, as a complex of
R
-modules, has finite projective dimension and
{\rm{pd}}_{S}R=1
, its projective dimension over
S
, will be also finite. Combining this with the fact that the Gorenstein projective dimension of the
S
-module
{\rm{Coker}}f
is at most one, ensures that the Gorenstein projective dimension of
X^{\bullet}
over
S
is finite, see
[
27
, Theorem 3.9(1)]
. Now consider the exact triangle
X^{\bullet}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}k\longrightarrow\mathsf{cone}(s_{2})\longrightarrow X^{\bullet}[1]
. Since
X^{\bullet}
and
\mathsf{cone}(s_{2})
, as complexes of
S
-modules, have finite projective dinension, one may deduce that the Gorenstein projective dimension of
k
over
S
is finite. Thus
S
, and so
R
, will be Gorenstien rings, see
[
11
, (1.4.9)]
. The proof then is completed.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

The fully faithful triangle functor
T\circ\underline{i}:\underline{\mathsf{Mon}}(\omega,\mathcal{P})\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathsf{D_{sg}}}(R)
is dense if and only if
S
is a regular ring.

Proof.

The result indeed follows from the proof of Theorem
3.5
, and using the facts that over regular rings, every Gorenstein projective module, is projective, and the finiteness of the projective dimension of the residue field
k
of
S
, yields that
S
is regular. Hence, the proof is finished.
∎

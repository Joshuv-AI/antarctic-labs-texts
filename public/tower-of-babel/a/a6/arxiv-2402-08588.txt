Couplings and Poisson approximation for stabilizing functionals of determinantal point processes
Moritz Otto

Abstract
. We prove a Poisson process approximation result for stabilizing functionals of a determinantal point process. Our results use concrete couplings of determinantal processes with different Palm measures and exploit their association properties. Second, we focus on the Ginibre process and show in the asymptotic scenario of an increasing window size that the process of points with a large nearest neighbor distance converges after a suitable scaling to a Poisson point process. As a corollary, we obtain the scaling of the maximum nearest neighbor distance in the Ginibre process, which turns out to be different from its analogue for independent points.

1.
Introduction

Determinantal point processes (DPPs) were introduced in quantum mechanics to study configurations of fermions
[
21
]
. Due to their repulsive nature, the play a fundamental role in applied sciences, e.g. as a model for base stations in a wireless network
[
22
]
. In mathematics, DPPs arise naturally in different fields, such as eigenvalues of random matrices
[
13
]
and random spanning trees
[
6
]
. DPPs have notable probabilistic properties. Amongst others, the (reduced) Palm process of a DPP is again a determinantal process and important quantities such as the Laplace transform and Janossy densities admit closed-form expressions (see e.g.
[
11
]
). A DPP on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is determined by its correlation kernel
K
, which is a Hermitian function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{C}
. An important DPP is the Ginibre process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with Gaussian kernel given in Section 2.

Let
\xi
be a stationary DPP on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
g
be a measurable function from
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{N}
to
\{0,1\}
, where we write
\mathbf{N}
for the set of
\sigma

-finite point configurations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
\delta_{x}
denote the Dirac measure in
x
. For some measurable
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let

\Xi[\xi]:=\sum_{x\in\xi\cap W}g(x,\xi)\delta_{x}.

Here, the function
g
has the effect of a thinning of
\xi
, in the sense that
\Xi
is the point process of all points
x\in\xi
in the set
W
which satisfy
g(x,\xi)=1
. Random measures of this type are flexible models that appear in the study of random spatial graphs, stochastic topology and geometric extreme value theory.

In this article, we study the distance (in an appropriate distance of point processes) of
\Xi
and a Poisson point process. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first paper which systematically studies Poisson approximation for determinantal processes. This continues the studies for stabilizing functionals of Poisson point processes
[
12
,
5
,
25
]
, Poisson hyperplanes processes
[
24
]
and Gibbs point processes
[
18
]
. However, the tools used in our paper are different then in above mentioned. As main contributions, this article shows:

(i) If the correlation kernel
K
is fast decaying and if the thinning function
g
is stabilizing and satisfies some natural assumptions, the bound of the distance of
\Xi
and a Poisson process is analogous to the bounds obtained for thinned Poisson point processes (see
[
5
]
and
[
24
]
).

(ii) If
\xi
is the Ginibre process and
g(x,\xi)
is indicator which is one if
\xi\setminus\{x\}
does not have points in a ball with a given radius
v
around
x
, we prove in as asymptotic scenario where the size of
W
and
v
tend to infinity, that an appropriate scaling of
\Xi
converges to a Poisson process.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce determinantal point processes state our two main results. In Section
3
, we provide important notions such as Palm theory, negative association and correlation decay. The proof of Theorem
1
is given in Section
4
. In Section
5
we provide the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.
Model and main results

We work on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\geqslant 1
) equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}^{d}
and Euclidean norm
\|\cdot\|
. We denote by
\mathbf{N}
the space of all
\sigma
-finite counting measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and by
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}
the space of all finite counting measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and equip
\mathbf{N}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{N}}
with their corresponding
\sigma
-fields
\mathcal{N}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
, which are induced by the maps
\omega\mapsto\omega(B)
for all
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. A
point process
is a random
element
\xi
of
\mathbf{N}
, defined over some fixed
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.
The
intensity measure
of
\xi
is the
measure
\mathbb{E}[\xi]
defined by
\mathbb{E}[\xi](B):=\mathbb{E}[\xi(B)]
,
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. For
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
r>0
let
B_{r}(z)
be the closed Euclidean ball with radius
r
around
z
. For
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
we write
|B|
for the Lebesgue measure of
B
.

Let
K:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a complex function. We say that
\xi
is a
determinantal point process with correlation kernel
K
, if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and pairwise disjoint
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\xi(A_{1})\cdots\xi(A_{n})]=\int_{A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{n}}\det(K(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}(x_{1},\dots x_{n}),

where
\mathrm{d}\dots
denotes integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(K(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m}
is the
m\times m
-matrix with entry
K(x_{i},x_{j})
at position
(i,j)
, and
\det M
is the determinant of a complex-valued
m\times m
-matrix
M
. This says that
\xi
has correlation functions of all orders and the
m
th order correlation function
\rho^{(m)}
is given by

\displaystyle\rho^{(m)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\det(K(x_{i},x_{j}))_{i,j=1}^{m},\quad x_{1},\dots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

and that it is locally integrable. In this article we assume that
K
satisfies the following assumptions (i)-(iv).

(i)

K
is Hermitian, i.e.
K(x,y)=\overline{K(y,x)}
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(ii)

K
is locally square integrable, i.e. for every compact
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
the integral

\int_{B}\int_{B}|K(x,y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

is finite,

(iii)

K
is locally of trace class, i.e. for every compact
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
the integral
\int_{B}K(x,x)\mathrm{d}x
is finite,

Under the assumptions (i)–(iii), it follows from Mercer’s theorem that for every compact
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the restriction
\xi_{B}
of
\xi
to
B
is a determinantal point process whose kernel
K_{B}
is for almost all
(x,y)\in B\times B
given by

\displaystyle K_{B}(x,y)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{B}\phi_{k}^{B}(x)\overline{\phi_{k}^{B}(y)},

where
\lambda_{k}^{B}\in\mathbb{R}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and the functions
\phi_{k}^{B}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, form on orthonormal base of
L^{2}(B)
. Finally, we assume that

(iv)

0\leqslant\lambda_{k}^{B}\leqslant 1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and all compact
B\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
.

Under the assumptions (i)–(iv) there is a unique (in distribution) determinantal point process with correlation kernel
K
(see
[
29
, Theorem 3]
).

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we call
\xi^{x}
a Palm version of the point process
\xi
at
x
, if for all measurable
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int f(x,\xi)\xi(\mathrm{d}x)\Big]=\int f(x,\xi^{x})\mathbb{E}[\xi](\mathrm{d}x).

Later, we will generalize this definition and define a Palm process with respect to another point process.

Let
\xi
be a stationary determinantal process satisfying (i)–(iv) with intensity
\rho>0
. Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{N}\to\{0,1\}
be a measurable function (called
score function
) and let
W\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
. We define

(2)

\displaystyle\Xi[\omega]:=\sum_{x\in\omega\cap W}g(x,\omega)\delta_{x},

and set
\Xi:=\Xi[\xi]
. Note that by (
1
), the intensity measure
\mathbf{L}
of
\Xi
is given by

\mathbf{L}(A)=\rho\int_{W\cap A}\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})]\,\mathrm{d}x,\quad A\in\mathcal{B}^{d}.

In this article we study the Kantorovich-Rubinstein (KR) distance of
\Xi
and a finite Poisson process. We recall the definition of the KR distance from
[
12
]
. For finite point processes
\zeta
and
\xi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
the KR distance is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{d_{KR}}(\zeta,\xi):=\sup_{h\in\text{Lip}}|\mathbb{E}h(\zeta)-\mathbb{E}h(\xi)|,

where
Lip
is the class of all measurable 1-Lipschitz functions
h:\widehat{\mathbf{N}}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to the total variation between measures
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by

\displaystyle d_{\text{TV}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):=\sup|\omega_{1}(A)-\omega_{2}(A)|,

where the supremum is taken over all
A\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
with
\omega_{1}(A),\omega_{2}(A)<\infty
. Under appropriate conditions on
\xi
and
g
, we prove that
\Xi
can be approximated by a Poisson process.

We suppose that there exists
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
A\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and all
\omega\in\mathbf{N}
,

(3)

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\omega\cap A}g(x,\omega)<\alpha|A|,

and assume that
g
is monotonic in the sense that for all
x\in W
, it holds that

(4)

\displaystyle g(x,\omega_{1})\leqslant g(x,\omega_{2})\quad\text{or}\quad g(x,\omega_{1})\geqslant g(x,\omega_{2}),\qquad\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}.

We further assume that
g
is stabilizing, by which we mean that there es a measurable function
\mathcal{S}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{N}\to\mathcal{F}
such that

g(x,\omega)=g(x,\omega\cap\mathcal{S}(x,\omega))

holds for any
\omega\in\mathbf{N}
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the set of closed subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Further suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a
stopping set
, which says that

\{\omega\in\mathbf{N}:\,\mathcal{S}(x,\omega)\subset S\}=\{\omega\in\mathbf{N}:\,\mathcal{S}(x,\omega\cap S)\subset S\}.

Moreover, we assume that the kernel
K:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
satisfies

(5)

\displaystyle|K(x,y)|\leqslant\phi(\|x-y\|),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some decreasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\lim_{r\to\infty}\phi(r)=0
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\xi
be a stationary determinantal process with kernel
K
satisfying (
5
) and intensity
\rho\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\Xi
be the score sum defined in (
2
) with intensity measure
\mathbf{L}
and suppose that
g
satisfies (
3
) and (
4
) and is stabilizing with respect to the stopping set
\mathcal{S}
. For Borel sets
S,T\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
o\in S\subset T
let
S_{x}:=x+S
and
T_{x}:=x+T,\,x\in W,
and define

\tilde{g}(x,\omega):=g(x,\omega)\mathds{1}\{\mathcal{S}(x,\omega)\subseteq S_{x}\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\omega\in\mathbf{N}.

Let
\zeta
be a finite Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with intensity measure
\mathbf{M}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{d_{KR}}(\Xi,\zeta)\leqslant d_{TV}(\mathbf{L},\mathbf{M})+2(E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3})+F,

where

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle:=\rho\int_{W}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S}(x,\eta^{x})\not\subset S)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle:=\rho^{2}\int_{W}\int_{W\cap T_{x}}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}(x,\xi^{x})]\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}(y,\xi^{y})]\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle E_{3}

\displaystyle:=\rho^{2}\int_{W}\int_{W\cap T_{x}}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}(x,\xi^{x,y})\tilde{g}(y,\xi^{x,y})]\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:=c\|K\|\max(|S|,1)|W\oplus S|^{2}\phi(d(T,S)),

where
\|K\|=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K(x,y)|
,
W\oplus S
is the Minkowski sum of
W
and
S
,
d(T,S)
is the Hausdorff distance of
T
and
S
and the constant
c>0
does not depend on
K
,
g
,
W
,
S
and
T
.

In the second part of this paper, we give an application of Theorem
1
for a concrete choice of
\xi
and of
g
. Let
\xi
be the (infinite) Ginibre process, which is a stationary determinantal point process on
\mathbb{C}
with correlation kernel given by

K(z,w)=\pi^{-1}e^{-(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})/2}e^{z\overline{w}},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{C}.

Hence,
\xi
has intensity
\rho=\pi^{-1}
and it holds that
|K(z,w)|\leqslant\phi(\|z-w\|)
with
\phi(r):=\pi^{-1}\exp(-r^{2}/2)
,
r>0
, for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
.

In Theorem
2
below, we choose
g
depending on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g_{n}
is the indicator function which is one if and only if the process
\xi\setminus\{x\}
is empty in a ball with a certain radius
v_{n}
(chosen such that
v_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
) around
x
. This choice leads to the study of large nearest neighbor balls. It is also an important prototype for more sophisticated models in stochastic geometry and has been studied extensively for different point processes in various spaces (see
[
26
]
).

We consider
\xi
as a random set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
B_{n}:=B_{n}(o)
the closed ball with radius
n>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
centered at the origin
o
. We consider the process

(6)

\Xi_{n}:=\sum_{x\in\xi\cap B_{n}}\mathds{1}\{\xi(B_{v_{n}}(x)\setminus\{x\})=0\}\ \delta_{x}.

as well as the scaled process

(7)

\Psi_{n}:=\sum_{y\in\Xi}\delta_{y/n}=\sum_{x\in\xi\cap B_{n}}\mathds{1}\{\xi(B_{v_{n}}(x)\setminus\{x\})=0\}\ \delta_{x/n}.

In the following theorem we compare
\Psi_{n}
with a Poisson process on the unit ball
B_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\tau>0
and let
\nu
be a stationary Poisson process on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with intensity
\tau>0
. There exists a sequence
(v_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
v_{n}^{4}\sim 4\log n
as
n\to\infty
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbf{d_{KR}}(\Psi_{n},\nu\cap B_{1})\leqslant Cn^{\varepsilon-1/16}.

As an application of the above theorem, we consider largest distances to the nearest neighbor. That is, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we denote by
\mathsf{nn}(x,\xi):=\arg\min_{y\in\xi\setminus\{x\}}|x-y|
the nearest neighbor of
x
in
\xi\setminus\{x\}
.

Corollary 3
.

We have as
n\to\infty
,

\frac{1}{2\pi\sqrt{\log n}}\max_{x\in\xi\cap B_{n}}|B_{\mathsf{nn}(x,\xi)}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}}}{{\longrightarrow}}1.

The proof of Corollary
3
is quite standard (see e.g.
[
25
, Corollary 4.2]
or
[
9
, Corollary 1]
) and therefore omitted.

Remark 4
.

(i) One should compare Theorem
1
with
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
(or the refined version
[
24
, Theorem 7]
) that discusses Poisson process approximation for score sums built on a Poisson process. The terms
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
in Theorem
1
are the analogues to the terms
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
in
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
. Due to the spatial independence property of the Poisson process, there is no analogue of the term

(which reflects the correlation decay of the determinantal process

) in
[
5
]
and
[
24
]
. Note also that for a wide class of determinantal point processes (including the Ginibre process), the
\beta
-mixing coefficient does not decay exponentially fast (see
[
27
, Proposition 4.2]
). Therefore, the
exponential decay dependence (EDD)
property from
[

]
is violated and general results for Poisson approximation of strongly mixing processes do not apply.

(ii) Note that the scaling of the maximum nearest neighbor ball in Corollary

is different from its analogue for independent points, which is proportional to
\log n
as

(see
[
8
]
). It seems interesting to investigate whether Theorem

can be extended to

). However, this extension would require fine probabilistic estimates on empty-space probabilities of the Ginibre process, which are beyond the scope of this article.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Palm calculus and negative association

Following
[
16
, Chapter 6]
we next introduce Palm measures and thereby generalize the definition given in (
1
). Let
\xi,\Xi
be point processes on
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{B}^{d})
and assume that
\Xi
has
\sigma
-finite intensity measure
\mathbf{L}
. Then there are point processes
\xi^{x,\Xi},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that

(8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int f(x,\xi)\,\Xi(\mathrm{d}x)\Big]=\int\mathbb{E}[f(x,\xi^{x,\Xi})]\mathbf{L}(\mathrm{d}x),

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{N}\to[0,\infty)
is assumed to be measurable. The processes
\xi^{x,\Xi},x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
are called
Palm processes
of
\xi
with respect to
\Xi
at
x
. If
\Xi
is simple,
\xi^{x,\Xi}
can be interpreted as the process
\xi
seen from
x
and conditioned on
\Xi
having a point in
x
. From
[
16
, Lemma 6.2(ii)]
it follows that
\delta_{x}\in\xi^{x,\Xi}
a.s. This allows us to define the reduced Palm process
\xi^{x!,\Xi}:=\xi^{x,\Xi}-\delta_{x}
. If
\xi=\Xi
a.s., we write
\xi^{x}
for a Palm process of
\xi
(with respect to itself) at
x
(c.f. (
1
)) and
\xi^{x!}
for a reduced Palm process.

Let
\xi
be a determinantal point process satisfying the conditions (i)–(iv) from Section
2
with correlation kernel
K
. Then the reduced Palm processes
\xi^{x!},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
are determinantal processes with correlation kernel
K^{x},\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
given by

(9)

\displaystyle K^{x}(z,w)=K(z,w)-\frac{K(z,x)K(x,w)}{K(x,x)},\quad z,w\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

whenever
K(x,x)>0
(see
[
28
, Theorem 1.7]
). By
[
14
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
23
]
), the process
\xi^{x!}
is stochastically dominated by
\xi
(denoted by
\xi^{x!}\leqslant\xi
) which means that

(10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x!})]\leqslant\mathbb{E}[F(\xi)]

for each measurable
F:\mathbf{N}\to\mathbb{R}
which is bounded and increasing, by which we mean that
F(\omega_{1})\leqslant F(\omega_{2})
if
\omega_{1}\subset\omega_{2}
.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
\xi^{x}
be a Palm process of
\xi
at
x
and
\xi^{x,\Xi}
a Palm process of
\xi
with respect to
\Xi
at
x
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int f(x,\xi)\,\Xi(\mathrm{d}x)\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int f(x,\xi)g(x,\xi)\xi(\mathrm{d}x)\Big]

\displaystyle=\int\mathbb{E}[f(x,\xi^{x})g(x,\xi^{x})]\rho(x)\lambda(\mathrm{d}x)

(11)

\displaystyle=\int\mathbb{E}[f(x,\xi^{x,\Xi})]\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})]\rho(x)\lambda(\mathrm{d}x).

An important property of determinantal point processes is that they are negatively associated (NA) (see
[
20
, Theorem 3.7]
). We say that a point process
\xi
is NA if for each collection of disjoint sets
B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\in\mathcal{B}^{d}
and each subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
we have that

(12)

\displaystyle\mathsf{Cov}(F(\xi(B_{i}),\,i\in I),G(\xi(B_{i}),\,i\in I^{c}))\leqslant 0,

where
F,G
are real bounded and increasing functions (see
[
7
]
).

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be bounded and increasing and assume that
g
is increasing in the second argument. Then we find for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
from (
11
) and (
12
) (applied to the determinantal point process
\xi^{x!}
) that

(13)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x,\Xi})]\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})]=\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x})g(x,\xi^{x})]\leqslant\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x})]\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})],

implying that
\xi^{x,\Xi}\leqslant\xi^{x}
for
\mathbb{E}[\Xi]
-a.a.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
On the other hand, if
g
is decreasing in the second argument, then we find by taking
-g
in (
12
) that

(14)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x,\Xi})]\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})]=\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x})g(x,\xi^{x})]\geqslant\mathbb{E}[F(\xi^{x})]\mathbb{E}[g(x,\xi^{x})],

implying that
\xi^{x,\Xi}\geqslant\xi^{x}
for
\mathbb{E}[\Xi]
-a.a.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

3.2.
Fast decay of correlation

Let
\xi
be a stationary determinantal process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance kernel
K
that satisfies the conditions (i)–(iv) and
|K(x,y)|\leqslant\phi(\|x-y\|)
for some exponentially decreasing function
\phi
(see (
5
)). Then we have from
[
4
, Lemma 1.3]
that the correlation functions
\rho^{(m)}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, of
\xi
satisfy

(15)

\displaystyle|\rho^{(p+q)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p+q})-\rho^{(p)}(x_{1},\dots,x_{p})\rho^{(q)}(x_{p+1},\dots,x_{p+q})|\leqslant m^{1+\frac{m}{2}}\phi(s)\|K\|^{m-1},

where
m:=p+q
,
s:=d(\{x_{1},\dots,x_{p}\},\{x_{p+1},\dots,x_{p+q}\}):=\inf_{i\in\{1,\dots,p\},j\in\{p+1,\dots,p+q\}}|x_{i}-x_{j}|
, and
\|K\|:=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K(x,y)|
.

3.3.
Poisson process approximation

Let
\mathbf{L}
be the (finite) intensity measure of the point process
\Xi
defined at (
2
). Suppose for
x\in W
that
\Xi^{x}
is a Palm version of
\Xi
at
x
and that
\tilde{\Xi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\Xi
. Then we obtain from
[
5
, Theorem 3.1]
that the Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance of
\Xi
defined at (
2
) and a finite Poisson process
\zeta

j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{P}(\forall k\in\{1,\dots,v_{n}^{2}\}\setminus\{i,j\}:\,X_{k}>v_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{v_{n}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\mathbb{P}(X_{k}^{2}>v_{n}^{2}).

Let
t<1
. The moment generating function
M_{X_{k}^{2}}(t)=\mathbb{E}[e^{tX_{k}^{2}}]=(1-t)^{-k}
of
X_{k}^{2}
exists and we obtain from the Chernoff bound that

\mathbb{P}(X_{k}^{2}>r^{2})\leqslant e^{-tr^{2}}\mathbb{E}[e^{tX_{k}^{2}}]=e^{-tr^{2}}(1-t)^{-k}.

For
k<r^{2}
, this bound is maximized for
t=1-\frac{k}{r^{2}}
, which gives

\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{v_{n}})\leqslant 2)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{v_{n}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}e^{-(1-\frac{k}{v_{n}^{2}})v_{n}^{2}-k\log\big(\frac{k}{v_{n}^{2}}\big)}=\sum_{i=1}^{v_{n}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=1\\
k\neq i,j\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}e^{-v_{n}^{2}+k-k\log\big(\frac{k}{v_{n}^{2}}\big)}.

Using here that
u\mapsto u-u\log(u/r^{2})
is increasing for
u\leqslant r^{2}
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(X_{k}^{2}>r^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{v_{n}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{v_{n}^{2}}\prod_{k=3}^{v_{n}^{2}}e^{-v_{n}^{2}+k-k\log\big(\frac{k}{v_{n}^{2}}\big)}

\displaystyle\leqslant v_{n}^{4}\prod_{k=3}^{v_{n}^{2}}e^{-v_{n}^{2}+k-k\log\big(\frac{k}{v_{n}^{2}}\big)}

\displaystyle=v_{n}^{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}(v_{n}^{2}-3)(v_{n}^{2}-2)-v_{n}^{4}\int_{3/v_{n}^{2}}^{1}x\log(x)\mathrm{d}x+O(v_{n}^{2}\log v_{n})}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{4}v_{n}^{4}(1+o(1))}

as
n\to\infty
, where we have used that
\int_{0}^{1}x\log(x)\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{1}{4}
.

Next we bound the second probability in (
30
). By the same coupling argument as above we find that

\mathbb{P}(\xi^{x!,y!}(B_{v_{n}}(y)\setminus B_{v_{n}}(x))=0)\leqslant\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{v_{n}}(y)\setminus B_{v_{n}}(x))\leqslant 2).

Next we note that
B_{v_{n}/2}\left(y+\frac{v_{n}(y-x)}{2|y-x|}\right)\subset B_{v_{n}}(y)\setminus B_{v_{n}}(x)
. Hence, the last probability is bounded by

\mathbb{P}\Big(\xi\big(B_{v_{n}/2}\big(y+\frac{v_{n}(y-x)}{2|y-x|}\big)\big)\leqslant 2\Big)=\mathbb{P}(\xi(B_{v_{n}/2})\leqslant 2)\leqslant e^{-\frac{1}{4}(v_{n}/2)^{4}(1+o(1))}

by the same estimates as above (with
v_{n}/2
instead of
v_{n}
). Since
\rho_{2}(x,y)\leqslant 1/\pi^{2}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we arrive for all
\varepsilon>0
at the bound

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}\int_{T_{x}}\mathbb{P}(\xi^{x!,y!}(B_{v_{n}}(x)\cup B_{v_{n}}(y))=0)\rho_{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}(x,y)\leqslant\frac{n(\log n)^{2}}{\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{4}v_{n}^{4}}e^{-\frac{1}{64}v_{n}^{4}}\leqslant\frac{(\log n)^{2}}{\pi}n^{\varepsilon-1/16},

where we have used (
27
) and that
\frac{v_{n}^{4}}{\log n}\to 4
as
n\to\infty
. Hence, the assertion follows from (
29
).
∎

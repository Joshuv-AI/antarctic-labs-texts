Random Tensors and their Normal Distributions
Changqing Xu, Kaijie Xu

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to introduce the random tensor with normal distribution, which promotes the matrix normal distribution to a higher order case. Some basic knowledge on tensors are introduced before we focus on the random tensors whose entries follow normal distribution. The random tensor with standard normal distribution(SND) is introduced as an extension of random normal matrices. As a random multi-array deduced from an affine transformation on a SND tensor, the general normal random tensor is initialised in the paper. We then investigate some equivalent definitions of a normal tensor and present the description of the density function, characteristic function, moments, and some other functions related to a random matrix. A general form of an even-order multi-variance tensor is also introduced to tackle a random tensor. Finally some equivalent definitions for the tensor normal distribution are described.

Tensor; mixed effect tensor model; parameter estimation; Normal distribution; Characteristic function.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times n_{1}},Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times n_{2}},Z\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{3}\times n_{3}}

\frac{dZ}{dX}=\frac{dY}{dX}\times\frac{dZ}{dY}

(2.6)

Proof.

We denote
\mathcal{A}=\frac{dY}{dX},\mathcal{B}=\frac{dZ}{dY}
and
\mathcal{C}=\frac{dZ}{dX}
. By definition we have

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times n_{1}\times m_{2}\times n_{2}},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times n_{2}\times m_{3}\times n_{3}},\mathcal{C}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times n_{1}\times m_{3}\times n_{3}}.

Then for any given
(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})\in[m_{1}]\times[n_{1}]\times[m_{3}]\times[n_{3}]

\displaystyle A_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{dZ}{dX})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}=\frac{dz_{i_{3}i_{4}}}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j_{1},j_{2}}\frac{dy_{j_{1}j_{2}}}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}\frac{dz_{i_{3}i_{4}}}{dy_{j_{1}j_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{dY}{dX}\times\frac{dZ}{dY})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X=(X_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
be a matrix whose elements are independent variables. Then

(1)

\frac{dX}{dX}=I_{m}\times_{c}I_{n}
.

(2)

\frac{dX^{\top}}{dX}=I_{m}\times_{(2,3)}I_{n}=\mathcal{K}_{m,n}

(3)

\frac{d(YZ)}{dX}=\frac{dY}{dX}\times_{4}Z+\frac{dZ}{dX}\times_{3}Y^{\top}

(4)

\frac{dX^{2}}{dX}=I_{n}\times_{c}X+X^{\top}\times_{c}I_{n}
when

(5)

\frac{dX^{k}}{dX}=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k-1}\left[(X^{\top})^{p}\times_{c}X^{k-1-p}\right]
where
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\frac{dX}{dX}
. Then we have by definition and the independency of the elements of

A_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}=\frac{dx_{i_{3}i_{4}}}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{3}}\delta_{i_{2}i_{4}}=(I_{m}\times_{c}I_{n})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}

Thus (1) is proved. Similarly we can prove (2) by noticing that

\left[\frac{dX^{\top}}{dX}\right]_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}=\delta_{i_{1}i_{4}}\delta_{i_{2}i_{3}}

which implies

\frac{dX^{\top}}{dX}=I_{m}\times_{(2,3)}I_{n}=\mathcal{K}_{m,n}.

To prove (3), we let
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r},Z\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times q}
. Then
\frac{d(YZ)}{dX}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times p\times q}
whose elements are

\displaystyle(\frac{d(YZ)}{dX})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d[(YZ)_{i_{3}i_{4}}]}{dX_{i_{1}i_{2}}}=\frac{d[\sum\limits_{k}y_{i_{3}k}z_{ki_{4}}]}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}=\sum\limits_{k}\frac{d(y_{i_{3}k}z_{ki_{4}})}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k}\left[\frac{d(y_{i_{3}k})}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}z_{ki_{4}}+y_{i_{3}k}\frac{d(z_{ki_{4}})}{dx_{i_{1}i_{2}}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\frac{dY}{dX}\times_{4}Z)_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}+(\frac{dZ}{dX}\times_{3}Y^{\top})_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}i_{4}}

To prove (4), we let
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and take
Y=Z=X
. By (3) and (1), we have

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{2}}{dX}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{dX}\times_{4}X+X\times_{3}\frac{dX}{dX}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{n}\times_{c}I_{n})\times_{4}X+X\times_{3}(I_{n}\times_{c}I_{n})

\displaystyle I_{n}\times_{c}X+X^{\top}\times_{c}I_{n}

To prove (5), we use the induction method to

The result is also valid for

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{k+1}}{dX}

\displaystyle\frac{dX^{k}}{dX}\times_{4}X+(X^{\top})^{k}\times_{3}\frac{dX}{dX}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k-1}\left[(X^{\top})^{p}\times_{c}X^{k-p}\right]+(I_{n}\times_{c}I_{n})\times_{3}(X^{k})^{\top}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k-1}\left[(X^{\top})^{p}\times_{c}X^{k-p}\right]+(X^{\top})^{k}\times I_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{p=0}^{k}\left[(X^{\top})^{p}\times X^{k-p}\right]

Thus we complete the proof of (5).

In this section, we introduce and study the random matrices with Gaussian distributions and investigate the tensor products of such matrices. We denote
\parallel\!\cdot\!\parallel
for

\phi_{\textbf{X}}(T)=E[exp(\imath\texttt{Tr}(T^{\prime}\textbf{X}))],\quad\forall T\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}

The following lemma concerning a necessary and sufficient condition for a SND random vector will be frequently used in the paper.

Lemma 3.1

Let

The following lemma presents equivalent conditions for a random matrix to be SND:

Lemma 3.2

Let

Now set

Z(:,j,k)\sim\mathcal{N}_{n_{1}}(0,I_{n_{1}})
for all
j,k
. On the other hand, for any
i\in[n_{1}]
, we have

\texttt{vec}(Z(i,:,:))\sim\mathcal{N}_{m_{1}}(0,I_{m_{1}})
by
Z(i,:,:)\sim\mathcal{N}_{n_{2},n_{3}}(0,I_{n_{2}},I_{n_{3}})
and Lemma
3.2
. Thus

Z[1](i,:)=(\texttt{vec}(Z(i,:,:)))^{\top}\sim\mathcal{N}_{m_{1}}(0,I_{m_{1}})
. The implication
(2)\implies(1)
is obvious.

The equivalence
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
is directly from Lemma
3.2
and
(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)
is obvious. We now prove
(3)\implies(5)
. Let

\alpha^{(l)}\in S^{n_{l}-1}
for
l=1,2,3
and let
\beta=\alpha^{(2)}\otimes\alpha^{(3)}
. Then we have
\beta\in S^{m_{1}-1}
. Furthermore, we have

\mathcal{Z}\times_{1}\alpha^{(1)}\times_{2}\alpha^{(2)}\times_{3}\alpha^{(3)}=(\alpha^{(1)})^{\top}Z[1]\beta

which is SND by Lemma
3.2
. Thus we have
\mathcal{Z}\times_{1}\alpha^{(1)}\times_{2}\alpha^{(2)}\times_{3}\alpha^{(3)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
for all

\alpha^{(l)}\in S^{n_{l}-1}
(
l=1,2,3
). Thus (5) holds.

Conversely, we let (5) hold and want to show (2). For any given
\alpha\in S^{n_{1}-1}
, we denote
A(\alpha)=\mathcal{Z}\times_{1}\alpha
. Then

A(\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}\times n_{3}}
. Furthermore, for any
\alpha^{(2)}\in S^{n_{2}-1},\alpha^{(3)}\in S^{n_{3}-1}
, we have

\mathcal{Z}\times_{1}\alpha^{(1)}\times_{2}\alpha^{(2)}\times_{3}\alpha^{(3)}=(\alpha^{(2)})^{\top}A(\alpha)\alpha^{(3)}\in\mathcal{N}(0,1)

It follows by Lemma
3.2
that
A(\alpha)\in\mathcal{N}_{n_{2},n_{3}}(0,I_{n_{2}},I_{n_{3}})
for every
\alpha\in S^{n_{1}-1}
. Specifically, if we take

\alpha=e_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
to be the
i
th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}
, then we have
A(\alpha)=Z(i,:,:)
(
i\in[n_{1}]
). Hence we have

Z(i,:,:)\in\mathcal{N}_{n_{2},n_{3}}(0,I_{n_{2}},I_{n_{3}}),\forall i\in[n_{1}].

This shows that all the slices of
\mathcal{Z}
along the mode-1 is a SND matrix. We can also show that all the slices (along the other two directions) are SND matrices by
employing the same technique. This complete the proof that all the five items are equivalent.
∎

Let
\mathcal{X}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\rm{I}}(0,I_{d_{1}},\ldots,I_{d_{m}})
be a random following a standard normal distribution(SND). The density function of
\mathcal{X}
is defined by

f_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{T})=(2\pi)^{-\frac{1}{2}d}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left<\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}\right>)

where
d=d_{1}d_{2}\ldots d_{m}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{X}\sim\mathcal{N}_{\rm{I}}(0,I_{d_{1}},\ldots,I_{d_{m}})
be a random following a SND. Then the CF of
\mathcal{X}
is

\phi_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{T})=\exp(-\frac{1}{2}\left<\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}\right>)

where
\mathcal{T}=(T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{1}\times d_{2}\times\ldots\times d_{m}}
.

Proof.

By definition of CF, we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathcal{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[\exp(\imath\left<\mathcal{T},\mathcal{X}\right>)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[\exp(\imath\sum\limits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}X_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E[\prod\limits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\exp(\imath(T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}X_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}E[\exp(\imath(T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}X_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp[-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}T_{i_{1}\ldots i_{m}}^{2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp[-\frac{1}{2}\left<\mathcal{T},\mathcal{T}\right>]

∎

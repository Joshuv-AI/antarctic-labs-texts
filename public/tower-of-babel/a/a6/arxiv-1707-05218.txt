Higher Derivatives of Airy Functions and of their Products
Eugeny G. ABRAMOCHKIN and Evgeniya V. RAZUEVA

Abstract
The problem of evaluation of higher derivatives of Airy functions in a closed form is investigated. General expressions for the polynomials which have arisen in explicit formulae for these derivatives are given in terms of particular values of Gegenbauer polynomials. Similar problem for products of Airy functions is solved in terms of terminating hypergeometric series.

For any solution
y(x)
of a homogeneous differential equation of second order

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}+p(x)y^{\prime}+q(x)y=0

one can obtain that

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}=-qy-py^{\prime},

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime\prime}=(pq-q^{\prime})y+\big(p^{2}-p^{\prime}-q\big)y^{\prime},

\displaystyle y^{\rm IV}=\big({-}p^{2}q+pq^{\prime}+2p^{\prime}q+q^{2}-q^{\prime\prime}\big)y+\big({-}p^{3}+3pp^{\prime}+2pq-p^{\prime\prime}-2q^{\prime}\big)y^{\prime},

etc. However, it would be quite difficult to get the explicit formula in general:

\displaystyle y^{(n)}=P_{n}(x;p,q)y+Q_{n}(x;p,q)y^{\prime},

because successful finding of coefficients
P_{n}(x;p,q)
and
Q_{n}(x;p,q)
in a closed form depends on many circumstances.

The main purpose of this paper is to obtain general formulae for
n
-th derivatives of Airy functions,
{\rm Ai}(x)
and
{\rm Bi}(x)
. Both functions satisfy the same equation

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}=xy.

(1.1)

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\rm Ai}^{(n)}(x)=P_{n}(x){\rm Ai}(x)+Q_{n}(x){\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle{\rm Bi}^{(n)}(x)=P_{n}(x){\rm Bi}(x)+Q_{n}(x){\rm Bi}^{\prime}(x),

(1.2)

where
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
are some polynomials and the index
n
corresponds to the derivative order but not the polynomials degree. By differentiating the first equation of (
1.2
),

\displaystyle{\rm Ai}^{(n+1)}(x)=\bigl[P_{n}^{\prime}(x)+xQ_{n}(x)\bigr]{\rm Ai}(x)+\bigl[P_{n}(x)+Q_{n}^{\prime}(x)\bigr]{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\rm Ai}^{(n+1)}(x)}{}=P_{n+1}(x){\rm Ai}(x)+Q_{n+1}(x){\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x),

we have two differential difference relations

\displaystyle P_{n+1}(x)=P_{n}^{\prime}(x)+xQ_{n}(x),\qquad Q_{n+1}(x)=P_{n}(x)+Q_{n}^{\prime}(x)

(1.3)

with initial conditions
P_{0}(x)=1
and

which help to determine
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)

\displaystyle P_{3m+\delta}(x)=\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant\lfloor\frac{m-k_{1}}{2}\rfloor}\frac{3^{m+m_{1}+k}x^{3k+\ell_{1}}}{(3k+\ell_{1})!}\sum_{0\leqslant\ell\leqslant 3k+\ell_{1}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{3k+\ell_{1}}{\ell}\left(\frac{1-\ell}{3}\right)_{m+m_{1}+k},

\displaystyle Q_{3m+\delta}(x)=\sum_{0\leqslant k\leqslant\lfloor\frac{m-k_{2}}{2}\rfloor}\frac{3^{m+m_{2}+k}x^{3k+\ell_{2}}}{(3k+\ell_{2})!}\sum_{0\leqslant\ell\leqslant 3k+\ell_{2}}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{3k+\ell_{2}}{\ell}\left(\frac{2-\ell}{3}\right)_{m+m_{2}+k},

where

is the integral part of

,
\delta=0,1,2
, and

\displaystyle k_{1}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{1}=0,\quad

\displaystyle m_{1}=0,\quad

\displaystyle k_{2}=1,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{2}=1,\quad

\displaystyle m_{2}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{if $\delta=0$},

\displaystyle k_{1}=1,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{1}=2,\quad

\displaystyle m_{1}=1,\quad

\displaystyle k_{2}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{2}=0,\quad

\displaystyle m_{2}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{if $\delta=1$},

\displaystyle k_{1}=0,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{1}=1,\quad

\displaystyle m_{1}=1,\quad

\displaystyle k_{2}=1,\quad

\displaystyle\ell_{2}=2,\quad

\displaystyle m_{2}=1,\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{if $\delta=2$}.

\frac{1}{3}-k,\frac{2}{3}-k\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,1\right)\cdot{}_{2}F_{3}\left(\begin{matrix}1-\frac{k}{2},\frac{1-k}{2}\\
1-k,\frac{4}{3}-k,\frac{2}{3}\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{4x^{3}}{9}\right),

where

2
Higher derivatives of
\boldsymbol{{\rm Ai}(x)}
and
\boldsymbol{{\rm Bi}(x)}

For evaluation of polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
it is convenient to use
Airy atoms

f(x)
and
g(x)
which are connected with Airy functions by the relations
[
18
]

\displaystyle{\rm Ai}(x)=c_{1}f(x)-c_{2}g(x),\qquad\frac{{\rm Bi}(x)}{\sqrt{3}}=c_{1}f(x)+c_{2}g(x),

where
c_{1}=3^{-2/3}/\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)
and
c_{2}=3^{-1/3}/\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)
.

The reasons are the following. First of all,
f(x)
and
g(x)
are solutions of (
1.1
). As result,

\displaystyle f^{(n)}(x)=P_{n}(x)f(x)+Q_{n}(x)f^{\prime}(x),

\displaystyle g^{(n)}(x)=P_{n}(x)g(x)+Q_{n}(x)g^{\prime}(x).

(2.1)

Second, both Airy atoms have a simple hypergeometric representation:

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\frac{3^{k}x^{3k}}{(3k)!}={}_{0}F_{1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{x^{3}}{9}\right),

\displaystyle g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{k}\frac{3^{k}x^{3k+1}}{(3k+1)!}=x\cdot{}_{0}F_{1}\left(\frac{4}{3}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{x^{3}}{9}\right).

Then

\displaystyle f^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{3k\geqslant n}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\frac{3^{k}x^{3k-n}}{(3k-n)!},\qquad g^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{3k+1\geqslant n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{k}\frac{3^{k}x^{3k+1-n}}{(3k+1-n)!}.

And third, the Wronskian of
f(x)
and
g(x)
is equal to 1,

\displaystyle W[f,g]=f(x)g^{\prime}(x)-g(x)f^{\prime}(x)\equiv 1,

that helps to simplify polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
as solutions of the system (
2.1
)

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=g^{\prime}(x)f^{(n)}(x)-f^{\prime}(x)g^{(n)}(x),

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=f(x)g^{(n)}(x)-g(x)f^{(n)}(x).

(2.2)

Substitution of the series expansions of Airy atoms and their derivatives into (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=\sum_{3m\geqslant n}\frac{3^{m}x^{3m-n}}{(3m-n)!}\sum_{k}\left\{\binom{3m-n}{3k-n}-\binom{3m-n}{3k-1}\right\}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{m-k},

(2.3)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{3m\geqslant n-1}\frac{3^{m}x^{3m+1-n}}{(3m+1-n)!}\sum_{k}\left\{\binom{3m+1-n}{3k}-\binom{3m+1-n}{3k-n}\right\}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{m-k},

where both series on
k
are naturally terminating, i.e., the index of summation runs over all values that produce non-zero summands.

Let us define
\gamma_{m}
by the formula

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(m_{0},k_{0})=\sum_{k}\binom{3m-m_{0}}{3k-k_{0}}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{m-k}.

Then (
2.3
) can be rewritten as follows

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=\sum_{3m\geqslant n}\frac{3^{m}x^{3m-n}}{(3m-n)!}\bigl[\gamma_{m}(n,n)-\gamma_{m}(n,1)\bigr],

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{3m\geqslant n-1}\frac{3^{m}x^{3m+1-n}}{(3m+1-n)!}\bigl[\gamma_{m}(n-1,0)-\gamma_{m}(n-1,n)\bigr].

(2.4)

Reducing the product of Pochhammer symbols to Euler’s beta function

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{m-k}=\frac{\Gamma\bigl(k+\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(m-k+\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}{\rm B}\left(k+\frac{1}{3},m-k+\frac{2}{3}\right),

and applying one of the function integral definitions

\displaystyle{\rm B}(x,y)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\tau^{x-1}}{(1+\tau)^{x+y}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,\qquad\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits x>0,\qquad\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits y>0,

we have

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(m_{0},k_{0})=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k}\binom{3m-m_{0}}{3k-k_{0}}\tau^{k}\cdot\frac{\tau^{-2/3}}{(1+\tau)^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\hphantom{\gamma_m(m_0,k_0)}{}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k}\binom{3m-m_{0}}{3k-k_{0}}t^{3k}\cdot\frac{3}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hphantom{\gamma_m(m_0,k_0)}{}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{k}\binom{3m-m_{0}}{k}\bigl[1+\omega^{k+k_{0}}+\bar{\omega}^{k+k_{0}}\bigr]\frac{t^{k+k_{0}}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hphantom{\gamma_m(m_0,k_0)}{}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant 2}\omega^{jk_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k_{0}}(1+\omega^{j}t)^{3m-m_{0}}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.5)

Here,
\omega=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}
and
\bar{\omega}=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}/3}
are the cubic roots of unity, and an overline means complex conjugation.

Changing the variable in the last integral,
t\to 1/t
, we get another expression for
\gamma_{m}
:

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(m_{0},k_{0})=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\sum_{0\leqslant j\leqslant 2}\omega^{j(k_{0}-m_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m_{0}-k_{0}+1}(1+\bar{\omega}^{j}t)^{3m-m_{0}}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

(2.6)

Then the differences in square brackets in (
2.4
) can be written as

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(n,n)\Bigl|_{(\ref{eq:H2})}{}-\gamma_{m}(n,1)\Bigl|_{(\ref{eq:H1})}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\cdot 2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\biggl\{(1-\omega)\int_{0}^{\infty}t\frac{(1+\omega t)^{3m-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr\},

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(n-1,0)\Bigl|_{(\ref{eq:H1})}{}-\gamma_{m}(n-1,n)\Bigl|_{(\ref{eq:H2})}=\frac{m!\sqrt{3}}{2\pi}\cdot 2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\biggl\{(1-\bar{\omega})\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+\omega t)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t\biggr\}.

Expansions (
2.4
) show that exponents of the factor
(1+\omega t)
in both integrals are nonnegative.

The integral for
\gamma_{m}(n-1,0)-\gamma_{m}(n-1,n)
looks a little simpler than for
\gamma_{m}(n,n)-\gamma_{m}(n,1)
, so we begin with it. Integrating the analytic function
(1+z)^{3m+1-n}/(1+z^{3})^{m+1}
over the contour
[0,R]\cup\{R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi},\,\phi\in[0,2\pi/3]\}\cup[R\omega,0]
and taking
R\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+t)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\infty}^{0}\frac{(1+\omega t)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\omega\,\mathrm{d}t=2\pi\mathrm{i}\mathop{\rm res}_{z=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}}\frac{(1+z)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+z^{3})^{m+1}}.

Then the integral of our interest can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+\omega t)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t=\bar{\omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+t)^{3m+1-n}}{(1+t^{3})^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}t-\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}\bar{\omega}}{m!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{2m-n}}{(t-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/3})^{m+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}}.

It is worth noting that the first term on the right side makes a zero contribution to the difference
\gamma_{m}(n-1,0)-\gamma_{m}(n-1,n)
because
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\{(1-\bar{\omega})\bar{\omega}\}=\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits(-\mathrm{i}\sqrt{3})=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\gamma_{m}(n-1,0)-\gamma_{m}(n-1,n)=-6\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{(1+t)^{2m-n}}{(t-\mathrm{e}^{-\pi\mathrm{i}/3})^{m+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/3}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\gamma_m(n-1,0)-\gamma_m(n-1,n)}{}=-\frac{2}{(\sqrt{3})^{n-1}}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{(t+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{2m-n}}{(t+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=0}.

(2.7)

Using the generating function, we prove that the right part of (
2.7
) vanishes for
n=0,1,\ldots,2m
. Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle F(s)=\sum_{n=0}^{2m}\binom{2m}{n}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{(t+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{2m-n}}{(t+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=0}{}\cdot s^{n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{F(s)}{}=\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{(t+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}+s)^{2m}}{(t+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=0}=\frac{m!}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{|z|=\epsilon}\frac{(z+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}+s)^{2m}}{(z+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}z^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}z.

Changing the variable
\zeta=(z+a)/(1+bz)
, where parameters
a
and
b
will be chosen later, one gets

\displaystyle F(s)=\frac{m!}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}(1-ab)\oint_{|\zeta-a|=\epsilon}\frac{(\zeta[1-b\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}-bs]+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}+s-a)^{2m}}{(\zeta[1-\mathrm{i}b]+\mathrm{i}-a)^{m+1}(\zeta-a)^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

With the aim to simplify the integrand, we set
b=-\mathrm{i}
and
a=\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}+s
. Then

\displaystyle 1-b\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6}-bs=1-ab=\mathrm{i}\bar{a},\qquad\mathrm{i}-a=-\bar{a},

and

\displaystyle F(s)=\frac{m!}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\frac{(\mathrm{i}\bar{a})^{2m+1}}{(-\bar{a})^{m+1}}\oint_{|\zeta-a|=\epsilon}\frac{\zeta^{2m}}{(\zeta-a)^{m+1}}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=-\mathrm{i}\bar{a}^{m}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}t^{2m}\biggl|_{t=a}=-\mathrm{i}\frac{(2m)!}{m!}|a|^{2m}.

Therefore,
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits F(s)=0
, and the expansion of
Q_{n}(x)
in (
2.4
) can be simplified by throwing out the terms with
n<2m+1
. As result, the function

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)=\sum_{\frac{n-1}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{x^{3m+1-n}}{(3m+1-n)!}

\displaystyle\hphantom{Q_n(x)=\sum_{\frac{n-1}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}} }{}\times\frac{-2}{(\sqrt{3})^{n-2m-1}}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{1}{(t+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}(t+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{n-2m}}\right)\!\biggl|_{t=0}

(2.8)

is actually a polynomial.

The double inequality
\frac{n-1}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n-1}{2}
is quite restrictive for the summation index. In particular, it leads immediately to zero polynomials when
n
is equal to
0
or
2
.

Now we need to evaluate the derivative in (
2.8
). As before, we use the generating function approach. Let

\displaystyle g_{m,n}=\frac{1}{m!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\left(\frac{1}{(t+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}(t+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{n+1}}\right)\biggl|_{t=0},\qquad m,n\geqslant 0

and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle G(s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}g_{m,n}s^{m}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{|z|=\epsilon}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{s^{m}}{z^{m+1}(z+\mathrm{i})^{m+1}}\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{(z+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{n+1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{G(s)=\sum_{m=0}^\infty g_{m,n}s^m}{}=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\oint_{|z|=\epsilon}\frac{\mathrm{d}z}{(z^{2}+\mathrm{i}z-s)(z+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{n+1}},

assuming that
\epsilon
is a small positive number and
|s|<\min_{|z|=\epsilon}|z(z+\mathrm{i})|=\epsilon(1-\epsilon)
for the geometric series convergence. Roots of the equation
z^{2}+\mathrm{i}z-s=0
are
z_{\pm}=-\mathrm{i}(1\pm\sqrt{1-4s}\,)/2
, and
z_{-}
is the only pole of the integrand within the contour
|z|=\epsilon
. Therefore,

\displaystyle G(s)=\mathop{\rm res}_{z=z_{-}}\frac{1}{(z^{2}+\mathrm{i}z-s)(z+\mathrm{e}^{\pi\mathrm{i}/6})^{n+1}}=\frac{2^{n+1}}{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{1-4s}(\sqrt{3}+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{1-4s})^{n+1}},

\displaystyle g_{m,n}=[\![s^{m}]\!]G(s)=\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n+1}[\![s^{m}]\!]\left\{\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{1-4s}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+k}{n}\left(-\frac{\mathrm{i}\sqrt{1-4s}}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{k}\right\}.

Here, we use the notation proposed in
[
14
]
. Namely, if
A(z)
is any power series
\sum_{k}a_{k}z^{k}
, then
[\![z^{k}]\!]A(z)
denotes the coefficient of
z^{k}
in
A(z)
. In our view, this notation is more convenient to manipulate power series than usual analytic description,
[\![z^{k}]\!]A(z)=A^{(k)}(0)/k!
.

Returning to (
2.8
) and renaming the coefficients for brevity, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{m,n}=\frac{-2}{(\sqrt{3})^{n}}\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits g_{m,n}=\frac{2^{n+2}}{3^{n+1}}\,[\![s^{m}]\!]\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{n+2k+1}{n}\left(-\frac{1-4s}{3}\right)^{k}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{g}_{m,n}}{}=\frac{2^{n+2}}{3^{n+1}}\sum_{k=m}^{\infty}\binom{n+2k+1}{n}\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)^{k}\cdot\binom{k}{m}(-4)^{m}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{g}_{m,n}}{}=\frac{2^{n+2m+2}}{3^{n+m+1}}\binom{n+2m+1}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\frac{n+2m+3}{2},\frac{n+2m+2}{2};\frac{2m+3}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right).

Using Euler’s transformation
{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;\,c\,|\,z)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}(c-a,c-b;\,c\,|\,z)
and one of hypergeometric representations of Gegenbauer’s polynomials
[
21
, equation (7.3.1.202)]
,

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n-1}{2};\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\,\Bigl|\,1-z^{2}\right)=\frac{n!\,z^{n}}{(2\lambda)_{n}}\,C_{n}^{\lambda}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right),

we can simplify the coefficients to

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{m,n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\binom{n+2m+1}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n-1}{2};m+\frac{3}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{g}_{m,n}}{}=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{3})^{n}}C_{n}^{m+1}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right)=[\![t^{n}]\!]\frac{1}{\bigl(1-t+\tfrac{1}{3}t^{2}\bigr)^{m+1}}

(2.11)

and obtain required formulae for both polynomial families, first for
Q_{n}(x)
, then for
P_{n}(x)=Q_{n+1}(x)-Q_{n}^{\prime}(x)
. We write them in the same style, shifting the index in
Q_{n}(x)

\displaystyle Q_{n+1}(x)=\sum_{\frac{n}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m}\frac{m!x^{3m-n}}{(3m-n)!},

(2.12)

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=\sum_{\frac{n}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}\{\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m}-\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m-1}\}\frac{m!x^{3m-n}}{(3m-n)!}.

(2.13)

The last expression in (
2.11
) follows from the familiar generating function for Gegenbauer polynomials
[
18
]

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)t^{n}=\frac{1}{(1-2xt+t^{2})^{\lambda}},\qquad|t|<1,\qquad|x|<1.

Of course, all the coefficients
\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m}
and
\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m}-\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m-1}
are positive but this is easier to deduce from (
1.3
) than from (
2.11
). It is worth noting, that the term
\tilde{g}_{m,n-2m-1}
in (
2.13
) vanishes for the case
n=2m
since (
2.10
) holds.

Left and right inequalities on the summation index in (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) reveal the different structures of analytic expressions of the polynomials for small and large values of
x
. Namely, for
x\sim 0
the expressions depend on
n\,({\rm mod}\,3)
:

\displaystyle P_{3n}(x)=3^{n}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}+3^{n}\left\{(n+1)\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{3}+\cdots,

\displaystyle P_{3n+1}(x)=\frac{3^{n}}{2}\left\{\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{2}+\frac{3^{n}}{40}\left\{(n+8)\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}-(10n+8)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{5}+\cdots,

\displaystyle P_{3n+2}(x)=3^{n}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}x+\frac{3^{n}}{8}\left\{(n+4)\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}-4\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{4}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Q_{3n}(x)=3^{n}\left\{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}-\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x+\frac{3^{n}}{8}\left\{(n+2)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}-(4n+2)\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{4}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Q_{3n+1}(x)=3^{n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}+\frac{3^{n}}{2}\left\{(n+1)\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}-\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{3}+\cdots,

(2.14)

\displaystyle Q_{3n+2}(x)=3^{n}\left\{\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)_{n}\!-\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{2}+\frac{3^{n}}{40}\left\{(2n+10)\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)_{n}\!-(5n+10)\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}x^{5}+\cdots,

while for
x\to\infty
they depend on
n

({\rm mod}~2)
:

\displaystyle P_{2n}(x)=x^{n}+\binom{n}{3}(3n-5)x^{n-3}+10\binom{n}{6}(3n^{2}-15n+10)x^{n-6}+\cdots,

\displaystyle P_{2n+1}(x)=n^{2}x^{n-1}+4\binom{n}{4}(n^{2}-2n-1)x^{n-4}+14\binom{n}{7}(3n^{3}-17n^{2}+8n+8)x^{n-7}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Q_{2n}(x)=2\binom{n}{2}x^{n-2}+20\binom{n}{5}(n-1)x^{n-5}+112\binom{n}{8}(3n-2)(n-3)x^{n-8}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Q_{2n+1}(x)=x^{n}+\binom{n}{3}(3n+1)x^{n-3}+10\binom{n}{6}(3n^{2}-3n-2)x^{n-6}+\cdots.

(2.15)

In addition, it may be worth noting that the methods of finding these expansions are essentially different: (
2.15
) follows directly from (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), while for proving (
2.14
) the easiest way is to apply (
2.3
).

3
Hypergeometric series and difference equations

The initial statement of the problem (
1.2
) and our final expressions for polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
lead to some corollaries whose relation to the Airy functions does not look evident a priori. Below we consider two of them. The first helps to find the Gauss hypergeometric function special values. The second is connected with difference equations of third and fourth orders.

3.1
Some special values of the hypergeometric function of Gauss

Power series expansions of polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
help to find values of the Gauss hypergeometric function in some special cases. The simplest of them is obtained equating the terms
Q_{3n+1}(0)
in (
2.12
) and (
2.14
):

\displaystyle Q_{3n+1}(0)=3^{n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}=n!\tilde{g}_{n,n}=\frac{n!}{2^{n}}\binom{3n+1}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n-1}{2};n+\frac{3}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right).

Then

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n-1}{2};\,n+\frac{3}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=6^{n}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}\cdot\frac{(2n+1)!}{(3n+1)!}=\left(\frac{8}{9}\right)^{n}\cdot\frac{\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}\bigr)_{n}}{\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}\bigr)_{n}}.

This formula is not new (see Exercise 15 in
[
25
, Chapter 14]
) and can be extended to real or complex
a
’s by replacing
n
by
-2a

[
21
, equation (7.3.9.15)]
:

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};\frac{3}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}-2a\bigr)\Gamma(2-4a)}{6^{2a}\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)\Gamma(2-6a)}=\left(\frac{9}{8}\right)^{2a}\cdot\frac{2\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}-2a\bigr)\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}\bigr)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}-2a\bigr)}.

(3.1)

As is typical in the theory of hypergeometric functions, the extension from
n
to
a
is valid due to
Carlson’s theorem

[
23
, Section 5.8.1]
:
If
f(z)
is regular and of
the form
{\cal O}(\mathrm{e}^{c|z|})
, where
c<\pi
, for
\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z\geqslant 0
, and
f(z)=0
for
z=0,1,2,\ldots
, then
f(z)=0
identically.
(See
[
4
,
5
,
8
]
for many examples of application of Carlson’s theorem to
hypergeometric series.)

Next two hypergeometric function special values are related to coefficients of
Q_{3n+3}(x)
and
Q_{3n+2}(x)
:

\displaystyle[\![x]\!]Q_{3n+3}(x)=3^{n+1}\left\{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n+1}-\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n+1}\right\}=(n+1)!\tilde{g}_{n+1,n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\![x]\!]Q_{3n+3}(x)}{}=\frac{(n+1)!}{2^{n}}\binom{3n+3}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n-1}{2};n+\frac{5}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right),

\displaystyle[\![x^{2}]\!]Q_{3n+2}(x)=3^{n}\left\{\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)_{n}-\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}\right\}=\frac{(n+1)!}{2}\tilde{g}_{n+1,n-1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{[\![x^2]\!]Q_{3n+2}(x)}{}=\frac{(n+1)!}{2^{n}}\binom{3n+2}{n-1}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n-1}{2},-\frac{n-2}{2};n+\frac{5}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right).

By replacing
n
by
-2a
in the first relation and by
1-2a
in the second, we get

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};\frac{5}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{6^{-2a}}{1-2a}\left\{2\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{5}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{5}{3}\bigr)}-\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}\bigr)}\right\}\frac{\Gamma(4-4a)}{\Gamma(4-6a)},

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};\frac{7}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{6^{1-2a}}{(1-2a)(2-2a)}\left\{\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{8}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{5}{3}\bigr)}-\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{7}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{4}{3}\bigr)}\right\}\frac{\Gamma(6-4a)}{\Gamma(6-6a)}.

In order to use the coefficients of
P_{n}(x)
, we need to do some preliminary work. Substitution of (
2.9
) in both terms of the difference
\tilde{g}_{m,n}-\tilde{g}_{m,n-1}
and the binomial identity

\displaystyle\binom{N}{n-1}+\binom{N}{n}=\binom{N+1}{n}

lead to the relation

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{m,n}-\tilde{g}_{m,n-1}=\frac{3}{2^{n+1}}\binom{n+2m}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n}{2},-\frac{n+1}{2};m+\frac{1}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\tilde{g}_{m,n}-\tilde{g}_{m,n-1} =}{}-\frac{1}{2^{n+1}}\binom{n+2m+1}{n}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-\frac{n-1}{2},-\frac{n}{2};m+\frac{3}{2}\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right).

Then, by considering the first nonzero coefficients of
P_{3n}(x)
and

P_{3n+2}(x)
,

\displaystyle P_{3n}(0)=3^{n}\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}=n!\{\tilde{g}_{n,n}-\tilde{g}_{n,n-1}\},

\displaystyle[\![x]\!]P_{3n+2}(x)=3^{n}\left(\frac{4}{3}\right)_{n}=(n+1)!\{\tilde{g}_{n+1,n}-\tilde{g}_{n+1,n-1}\},

we get the following special values of the Gauss hypergeometric function:

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};-\frac{1}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{6^{-2a}}{3}\left\{\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)}+\frac{1+3a}{1+6a}\cdot\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)}\right\}\frac{\Gamma(-1-4a)}{\Gamma(-1-6a)},

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};\frac{1}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)=\frac{6^{-2a}}{2}\left\{\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{1}{3}\bigr)}+\frac{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}-2a\bigr)}{\Gamma\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}\bigr)}\right\}\frac{\Gamma(1-4a)}{\Gamma(1-6a)}.

It is quite clear that this approach provides a way for evaluation of

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,a+\frac{1}{2};n+\frac{1}{2}-2a\,\Bigl|\,-\frac{1}{3}\right)

in a closed form for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, while, of course, the expressions will become more and more cumbersome as
|n|
increases.

3.2
Difference equations of third and fourth orders

Relations (
2.2
) help to find generating functions for polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
:

\displaystyle{\bf P}(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=g^{\prime}(x)f(x+t)-f^{\prime}(x)g(x+t),

\displaystyle{\bf Q}(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Q_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=f(x)g(x+t)-g(x)f(x+t).

Since
f(x)
and
g(x)
satisfy the equation (
1.1
), then
{\bf P}(x,t)
and
{\bf Q}(x,t)
being the functions on
t
are solutions of the differential equation

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}-(x+t)\right\}{\bf Y}(x,t)=0.

If a solution of this equation is expanded in a power series

\displaystyle{\bf Y}(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}Y_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

then the series substitution into the equation leads to a recurrence relation for its coefficients, functions
Y_{n}(x)
:

\displaystyle Y_{n+3}(x)=xY_{n+1}(x)+(n+1)Y_{n}(x).

(3.2)

As result, polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
are two of three solutions of (
3.2
), corresponding to different initial conditions:

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)\colon\quad

\displaystyle P_{0}(x)=1,\qquad

\displaystyle P_{1}(x)=0,\qquad

\displaystyle P_{2}(x)=x,

\displaystyle Q_{n}(x)\colon\quad

\displaystyle Q_{0}(x)=0,\qquad

\displaystyle Q_{1}(x)=1,\qquad

\displaystyle Q_{2}(x)=0.

It is not evident how to find the third independent solution of (
3.2
), say

\displaystyle Z_{n}(x)\colon\quad Z_{0}(x)=0,\qquad Z_{1}(x)=0,\qquad Z_{2}(x)=1,

and how this relates with Airy functions’ derivatives.

Investigation of the polynomials
Z_{n}(x)
shows that

\displaystyle Z_{n+2}(x)=\sum_{\frac{n}{3}\leqslant m\leqslant\frac{n}{2}}\lambda_{m,n}\frac{m!x^{3m-n}}{(3m-n)!},

(3.3)

where coefficients
\lambda_{m,n}
satisfy the relation

\displaystyle m\lambda_{m,n}=(3m-n)\lambda_{m-1,n-2}+n\lambda_{m-1,n-3},\qquad m\geqslant 1.

In particular, for large values of
x
we have

\displaystyle Z_{2n+2}(x)=x^{n}+(n-1)(n-2)\frac{3n^{2}+7n+6}{6}x^{n-3}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Z_{2n+3}(x)=n(n+2)x^{n-1}+\binom{n-1}{3}(n+2)(n^{2}+2n+3)x^{n-4}+\cdots,

while for
x\sim 0
the first nonzero coefficients are

\displaystyle[\![x^{2}]\!]Z_{3n}(x)=\frac{3^{n}}{2}\left\{n!-2\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{n}\right\},

\displaystyle[\![x]\!]Z_{3n+1}(x)=3^{n}\left\{n!-\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)_{n}\right\},\qquad Z_{3n+2}(0)=3^{n}n!.

Nevertheless, we could not reveal an analytic source of polynomials
Z_{n}(x)
in order to find their expansions by applying the calculus methods as above.

We mention also two difference equations for the coefficients of polynomials
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
. These equations can be found considering the Laplace transform of the shifted Airy function,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-pt){\rm Ai}(t+x)\,\mathrm{d}t.

We will use a formal power series approach for simplicity, while there is, of course, a more rigorous but tedious way based on an asymptotic behaviour of
{\rm Ai}(t)
for large
t
’s. Assuming that
p\to+\infty
, the asymptotic expansion of
{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)
can be written in two ways. The first follows from (
1.2
)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm Ai}^{(n)}(x)}{n!}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-pt)t^{n}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{P_{n}(x){\rm Ai}(x)+Q_{n}(x){\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)}{p^{n+1}}.

(3.4)

On the other hand, the function
{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)
satisfies a differential equation of first order

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}p}+\big(p^{2}-x\big)\right\}{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)={\rm Ai}(x)p+{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x).

(3.5)

With initial condition

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(0,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}{\rm Ai}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t={\rm Ai}_{1}(x),

where the last notation is due to Aspnes
[
3
]
, the solution is

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)=\exp\left(xp-\frac{p^{3}}{3}\right)\left\{{\rm Ai}_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{p}\bigl({\rm Ai}(x)t+{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)\bigr)\exp\left(-xt+\frac{t^{3}}{3}\right)\mathrm{d}t\right\}.

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)={\rm Ai}(x)\left\{\frac{p}{p^{2}-x}+\frac{1}{(p^{2}-x)^{2}}+\frac{2x}{(p^{2}-x)^{3}}+\frac{4p}{(p^{2}-x)^{4}}+\cdots\right\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)=}{}+{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)\left\{\frac{1}{p^{2}-x}+\frac{2p}{(p^{2}-x)^{3}}+\frac{10}{(p^{2}-x)^{4}}+\ldots\right\}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{1}{p^{\infty}}\right).

Then, in general,
{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)
has the form

\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}(p,x)={\rm Ai}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}p+\nu_{k}}{(p^{2}-x)^{k+1}}+{\rm Ai}^{\prime}(x)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{k}p+\tilde{\nu}_{k}}{(p^{2}-x)^{k+1}}

(3.6)

with coefficients
\mu_{k}
,
\nu_{k}
,
\tilde{\mu}_{k}
,
\tilde{\nu}_{k}
depending on
x
.

Substituting (
3.6
) in (
3.5
), we obtain the same recurrence relations for the pairs
(\mu_{k},\nu_{k})
and
(\tilde{\mu}_{k},\tilde{\nu}_{k})

\displaystyle\mu_{k+2}=(2k+2)\nu_{k},\qquad\nu_{k+2}=(2k+3)\mu_{k+1}+(2k+2)x\mu_{k},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{k+2}=(2k+2)\tilde{\nu}_{k},\qquad\tilde{\nu}_{k+2}=(2k+3)\tilde{\mu}_{k+1}+(2k+2)x\tilde{\mu}_{k},

(3.7)

but different initial conditions

\displaystyle(\mu_{0},\nu_{0})=(1,0),\qquad(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})=(0,1),

\displaystyle(\tilde{\mu}_{0},\tilde{\nu}_{0})=(0,1),\qquad(\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\nu}_{1})=(0,0).

Now we need to reduce (
3.6
) to the form of (
3.4
). Transforming the first series of (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}p+\nu_{k}}{(p^{2}-x)^{k+1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}p+\nu_{k}}{p^{2k+2}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-x/p^{2})^{k+1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\sum_{k=0}^\infty\frac{\mu_{k} p+\nu_{k}}{(p^{2}-x)^{k+1}}}{}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}p+\nu_{k}}{p^{2k+2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\binom{m+k}{k}\frac{x^{m}}{p^{2m}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{p^{2n+2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(\mu_{k}p+\nu_{k})x^{n-k}.

For the second series of (
3.6
), transformation is the same. As result, we obtain expansions of
P_{n}(x)
and
Q_{n}(x)
depending on parity of
n

\displaystyle P_{2n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\mu_{k}(x)x^{n-k},\qquad P_{2n+1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\nu_{k}(x)x^{n-k},

\displaystyle Q_{2n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\tilde{\mu}_{k}(x)x^{n-k},\qquad Q_{2n+1}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\tilde{\nu}_{k}(x)x^{n-k},

where we add an explicit dependence of the coefficients on
x
. All the coefficients satisfy difference equations of fourth order based on (
3.7
):

\displaystyle\mu_{k},\ \tilde{\mu}_{k}\colon\quad

\displaystyle y_{k+4}=(2k+3)(2k+6)y_{k+1}+(2k+2)(2k+6)xy_{k},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\nu_{k},\ \tilde{\nu}_{k}\colon\quad

\displaystyle y_{k+4}=(2k+4)(2k+7)y_{k+1}+(2k+2)(2k+6)xy_{k}.

(3.9)

In particular,

\displaystyle\mu_{k}=\{1,0,0,4,12x,0,280,\ldots\},\qquad

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{k}=\{0,0,2,0,0,80,120x,\ldots\},

\displaystyle\nu_{k}=\{0,1,2x,0,28,140x,120x^{2},\ldots\},\qquad

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}_{k}=\{1,0,0,10,12x,0,880,\ldots\}.

Unfortunately, two other pairs of independent solutions of (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) are yet remain unknown, while a relation of one of the pairs (say,
\tilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\tilde{\mu}$}}_{k}
and
\tilde{\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.85pt]{$\tilde{\nu}$}}_{k}
) to polynomials (
3.3
) seems quite predictable.

4
Higher derivatives of
\boldsymbol{{\rm Ai}^{2}(x)}
,
\boldsymbol{{\rm Ai}(x){\rm Bi}(x)}
and
\boldsymbol{{\rm Bi}^{2}(x)}

In a similar fashion to the evaluation of higher derivatives of Airy functions considered in Section
2
, let us investigate the same problem for the products of these functions. It is quite evident that the problem is reduced to a determination of polynomials
R_{n}(x)
,
S_{n}(x)
and
T_{n}(x)
satisfying the following three equations

\displaystyle\left(\!\!\begin{matrix}[{\rm Ai}^{2}(x)]^{(n)}\\
[{\rm Ai}(x){\rm Bi}(x)]^{(n)}\\

{\rm Bi}^{2}(x)&2{\rm Bi}(x){\rm Bi}^{\prime}(x)&{\rm Bi}^{\prime 2}(x)\end{matrix}\!\!\right)\left(\!\begin{matrix}R_{n}(x)\\
S_{n}(x)\\
T_{n}(x)\end{matrix}\!\right)\!,

(4.1)

where
R_{0}(x)=1
,
S_{0}(x)=0

T_{0}(x)=0

By taking the derivative of any equation above, we get the system of differential difference relations

\displaystyle R_{n+1}(x)=R^{\prime}_{n}(x)+2xS_{n}(x),

\displaystyle S_{n+1}(x)=R_{n}(x)+S^{\prime}_{n}(x)+xT_{n}(x),

\displaystyle T_{n+1}(x)=2S_{n}(x)+T^{\prime}_{n}(x),

which help to determine
R_{n}(x)
,

and
T_{n}(x)
for any

(see the Table

5
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we have found higher derivatives of Airy functions and their products in a closed form. Explicit formulae for the polynomials which are contained in these derivatives help to obtain special values of hypergeometric functions
{}_{2}F_{1}
and
{}_{3}F_{2}
. We did not try to give a complete solution of this problem and considered only a few simple cases. In our view, a construction of the general solution will not require significant efforts. Moreover, combining this approach with the ideas presented in
[
13
]
, one can find more general values of

-function depending on two parameters. For example (cf. with (

) and (

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}b,\frac{1}{2}-3a,\frac{1}{2}+3a\\

\displaystyle{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}b,1-3a,1+3a\\
3b-1,\frac{3}{2}\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{3}{4}\right)=\frac{4\Gamma(1+a-b)\Gamma(3b-1)}{3^{3b}a\Gamma(a+b)\Gamma(b)}\sin\pi a\sin[\pi(b-a)].

Nevertheless, there are some problems which are yet remain open and look quite difficult. For example, how to find the solution

\displaystyle{\bf F}(z)={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}z,3z-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}-3z\\
3z,\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{3}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\bf F}(z)}{}=\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{z-1}(1-t)^{2z-1}\cdot{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}3z-\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}-3z\\
\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,\frac{3t}{4}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Using a closed-form expression for the
{}_{2}F_{1}
-function
[
21
, equation (7.3.1.90)]

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}a,1-a\\
\frac{1}{2}\end{matrix}\,\Bigl|\,z\right)=\frac{\cos([2a-1]\arcsin\sqrt{z})}{\sqrt{1-z}},

one can obtain

\displaystyle|{\bf F}(z)|\leqslant\left|\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\right|\int_{0}^{1}t^{\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z-1}(1-t)^{2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z-1}\cdot\frac{\bigl|\cos\bigl([6z-2]\arcsin\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}\,t\bigr)\bigr)\bigr|}{\sqrt{1-\tfrac{3}{4}t}}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Estimating the gamma function ratio outside the integral and the cosine term in the integrand, one finds

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\sim\frac{\sqrt{z}}{\sqrt{3\pi}}\cdot\frac{3^{3z}}{2^{2z}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\left|\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\right|={\cal O}\left\{\sqrt{|z|}\exp\left(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z\ln\frac{27}{4}\right)\right\},\quad\ln\frac{27}{4}<2,

\displaystyle|\cos(x+\mathrm{i}y)|\leqslant\cosh y\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{|y|}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\bigl|\cos\bigl([6z-2]\arcsin\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{3}t\bigr)\bigr)\bigr|\leqslant\mathrm{e}^{2\pi|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits z|}

for all
t\in[0,1]
. As result,

\displaystyle|{\bf F}(z)|\leqslant 2\mathrm{e}^{2\pi|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits z|}\cdot\left|\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\right|\int_{0}^{1}t^{\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z-1}(1-t)^{2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hphantom{|{\bf F}(z)|}{}=2\mathrm{e}^{2\pi|\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits z|}\cdot\left|\frac{\Gamma(3z)}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(2z)}\right|\cdot\frac{\Gamma(\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z)\Gamma(2\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z)}{\Gamma(3\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z)}

and, therefore,
|{\bf F}(z)|={\cal O}\big(\mathrm{e}^{c|z|}\big)
with
c<3\pi
.

In conclusion, it is interesting to note that for proving identities (
4.15
)–(
4.18
) the numerical part of the method above leads to the same constant,
-2
, as in (
A.1
), while other components of final expressions are rather
different.

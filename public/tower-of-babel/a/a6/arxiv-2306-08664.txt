On uniconnected solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation and Dehornoy’s class
M. CASTELLI, S. RAMIREZ

Abstract
In the first part, we focus on indecomposable involutive solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation whose permutation group forces them to be uniconnected. Indecomposable involutive solutions with a permutation group isomorphic to a dihedral group or a minimal non-cyclic group are studied in detail. In the last part, we study the Dehornoy’s class of involutive solutions (not necessarily indecomposable) and its link with left braces. As an application, we give an upper bound for several families of indecomposable involutive solutions and we compute the precise value in some other cases.

Introduction

The quantum Yang-Baxter equation comes from theoretical physics and it first appeared in a paper by C.N. Yang
[
32
]
. In 1992 Drinfel’d
[
12
]
suggested the study of the set-theoretical version of this equation. Specifically, a pair
(X,r)
is said to be a
set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation
if
X
is a non-empty set, and
r:X\times X\to X\times X
is a map such that the relation

\displaystyle\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)=\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)\left(r\times\id_{X}\right)\left(\id_{X}\times r\right)

is satisfied.
Writing a solution
(X,r)
as
r\left(x,y\right)=\left(\sigma_{x}\left(y\right),\tau_{y}\left(x\right)\right)
, with

\sigma_{x},\tau_{x}
maps from
X
into itself, for every
x\in X
, we say that
(X,r)
is
non-degenerate
if
\sigma_{x},\tau_{x}\in\Sym_{X}
, for every
x\in X
.

After the papers of Gateva-Ivanova and Van Den Bergh
[
15
]
and Etingov, Schedler, and Soloviev
[
13
]
, the attention of several people focused on the
involutive
non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions, where a set-theoretic solution
(X,r)
is said to be involutive if
r^{2}
is the identity map on
X\times X
. From now on, even if not specified, every involutive non-degenerate set-theoretic solution of the Yang-Baxter equation will be simply called
involutive solution
.

Among involutive solutions, in
[
13
, Section 2]
the class of
indecomposable solutions
was introduced. The interest in these solutions is motivated by the fact that they are the basic solutions that allow to construct and classify all the other ones, not necessarily indecomposable, by suitable construction-tools, such as twisted union, dynamical extension and retraction (see
[
3
,
13
,
30
]
for more details). However, the classification-problem of involutive solutions is far from solved even if we restrict to indecomposable ones. In this context, several machineries were developed. In particular, several people considered the structure of left brace on the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
of an involutive solution
(X,r)
. Remarkable results obtained in
[
1
,
28
]
states that every indecomposable involutive solution
(X,r)
having permutation left brace
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
isomorphic to a left brace
B
can be obtained by means of a suitable core-free subgroup
H
of the multiplicative group of
B
and considering the left cosets of
B
by
H
as underlying set. Moreover, by
[
28
, Theorem 1]
, every indecomposable involutive solution
(X,r)
is an epimorphic image of an indecomposable involutive solution
(Y,s)
such that
\mathcal{G}(Y,s)
acts regularly on the set
Y
. Therefore, the classification of indecomposable solutions
(X,r)
such that
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
acts regularly on
X
, which in
[
28
, Definition 1]
are named
uniconnected
, is the first milestone for the classification of all the indecomposable involutive solutions. A natural approach consists in the study of the uniconnected ones for which the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
belong to a specific class. Indecomposable solutions with abelian permutation group were recently studied (of course, these solutions always are uniconnected) and very strong results were obtained. Indeed, in
[
17
]
the ones with multipermutation level
2
were completely classified, while in
[
18
,
29
]
the restriction on the multipermutation level was removed for the ones with cyclic permutation group. Further theoretical results on these solutions are recently showed in
[
5
]
, where the infinite case also is considered. Much less is known about uniconnected solutions with non-abelian permutation group. In
[
2
]
a description of the ones with a Z-group permutation group of odd order were given, giving a complete classification if the underlying set has square-free odd order, while in
[
24
, Section 4]
uniconnected solutions provided by primary cyclic left braces (i.e. left braces having prime-power order and cyclic additive group) were completely classified. Indecomposable involutive solutions not necessarily uniconnected are recently studied in
[
7
,
10
]
, where the ones having square-free order are considered, and in
[
16
]
, where the ones with multipermutation level
2
are characterised.

In this paper, we study indecomposable involutive solutions
(X,r)
for which the structure of the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
force them to be uniconnected. After recovering basic definition and results in the first section, in Section
2
we consider indecomposable solutions provided by cyclic left braces and we show that these solutions and their retractions always are uniconnected. As an application, we show that indecomposable solutions with dihedral permutation group of size
2n
(where
n
is an odd number) and indecomposable solutions with square-free permutation group always are uniconnected. They are completely classified in Section
3
. In Section
4
, we turn our attention to indecomposable solutions for which the permutation group is such that all its non-trivial subgroups are cyclic. Since the ones with cyclic permutation group are completely classified (see
[
18
]
), we focus to the non-cyclic case. These groups are completely classified in
[
21
]
and we will call them
minimal non-cyclic
. Apart two exceptions, we have to consider non-abelian groups having size
q^{n}p
(where
q
and
p
are distinct prime numbers) and with a normal subgroup of size
p
. We show that indecomposable solutions with minimal non-cyclic group are uniconnected and, as a final result of the section, a quite explicit classification is given.

In the last part of the paper, we study the Dehornoy’s class of a (not necessarily indecomposable) solution, an important invariant related to Garside monoids and groups (see
[
8
,
9
]
for more details). Following the approach of
[
20
]
, where the Dehornoy’s class is related to left braces, we show that, given an involutive solution
(X,r)
, its Dehornoy’s class concides with the maximum order of an element in the additive group of the permutation left brace
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
. We use this fact to provide an upper bound of the Dehornoy’s class to several families of involutive solutions, giving an evidence to
[
14
, Conjecture
3.6
]
. Finally, we specialize our results to indecomposable involutive solutions, computing the Dehornoy’s class of some families of indecomposable involutive solutions and providing a positive answer to
[
14
, Conjecture
3.4
]
in several cases.

1
Basic definitions and results

We collect in this section some basic definitions and results on left braces and set-theoretical solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation.

Definition 1.1
.

A
set-theoretical solution to the Yang-Baxter equation
is a pair
(X,r)
, with
X

a set and
r:X\times X\to X\times X
a function that satisfies

(r\times\id)(\id\times r)(r\times\id)=(\id\times r)(r\times\id)(\id\times r).

We will restrict to the class of
non-degenerate
and
involutive
set-theoretical solutions.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
(X,r)
be a set-theoretical solution to the Yang-Baxter equation and write

r(x,y)=(\sigma_{x}(y),\tau_{y}(x))
. We say the solution is
non-degenerate

if all the maps
\sigma_{x},\tau_{y}:X\to X
are bijections. We say the solution
is
involutive
if
r^{2}=\id
.

As for other algebraic objects, we can define the notion of isomorphism. Specifically, two set-theoretical solutions to the Yang-Baxter equation
(X,r)
and
(X^{\prime},r^{\prime})
are
isomorphic
if there exist a bijective map
f
from
X
to
X^{\prime}
(that will be called
isomorphism
) such that
r^{\prime}(f\times f)=(f\times f)r
.

Convention 1
.

From now on, even if not specified, every set-theoretical solution to the Yang Baxter equation, which we will simply call
solution
, will be involutive and non-degenerate.

In
[
13
]
, Etingof, Schedler and Soloviev introduced the
retract relation
for involutive non-degenerate solutions, an equivalence relation which we denote by
\sim_{r}
. If
(X,r)
is an involutive non-degenerate solution, then
x\sim_{r}y
if and only if
\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{y}
, for all
x,y\in X
. In this way, we can define canonically another solution, having the quotient
X/\sim_{r}
as underlying set, which is named
retraction
of
(X,r)
and is indicated by
\Ret(X,r)
.
Of course, one can iterate the retraction-process obtaining the
n
-th retraction, for an arbitrary natural number
n
, which we indicate by
\Ret^{n}(X,r)
.

Definition 1.3
.

An involutive solution
(X,r)
is said to be a
multipermutation solution of level
n
if
n
is the minimal non-negative integer such that
\Ret^{n}(X)
has cardinality one.

By a standard exercise, one can verify that the multipermutation level of an involutive solution is an isomorphisms invariant. An other important invariant, studied in several papers
[
20
]
, is the Dehornoy’s class, that we define below. At first, recall that if
(X,r)
is an involutive solution and
\cdotp
is the binary operation on
X
given by
x\cdotp y:=\sigma_{x}^{-1}(y)
for all
x,y\in X
, the map
\Omega_{n}
from
X^{n}
to
X
(where
X^{n}
is the cartesian product of
X

n
times) is inductively given by

\Omega_{1}(x_{1})=x_{1}\hskip 5.69054ptand\hskip 5.69054pt\Omega_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n}):=\Omega_{n-1}(x_{1},...,x_{n-1})\cdotp\Omega_{n-1}(x_{1},...,x_{n-2},x_{n})

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n>1
and
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in X
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(X,r)
be an involutive solution. Then,
(X,r)
has
Dehornoy’s class
n
if
n
is the minimal non-negative integer such that
\Omega_{n+1}(x,x,...,x,y)=y
for all
x,y\in X
.

Given an involutive solution
(X,r)
, we
define its
permutation group

\mathcal{G}(X,r)
as the group of permutations
on
X
generated by the maps
\sigma_{x}
. Using the permutation group we can define
indecomposable
solutions as follows (our definition is equivalent to the original one given in
[
13
]
).

Definition 1.5
.

We say that
(X,r)
is
indecomposable

if its permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
acts transitively on
X
. If furthermore
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
acts regularly on
X
, then
(X,r)
will be called
uniconnected
.

Besides, the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
of an involutive solution can be
endowed with an additional group structure that always is abelian. This two group operations
make
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
into an algebraic structure called
left brace
.

Definition 1.6

(
[
6
]
, Definition 1)
.

A set
B
endowed of two operations
+
and
\circ
is said to be a
left brace
if
(B,+)
is an abelian group,
(B,\circ)
a group, and the equality
a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c
follows, for all
a,b,c\in B
.

From now on, if
(B,+,\circ)
is a left brace, the group
(B,+)
will be called the
additive group
and the group
(B,\circ)
will be called the
multiplicative group
. The most elementary example of left brace can be constructed taking and abelian group
(B,+)
and setting
a\circ b:=a+b
for all
a,b\in A
. Through the paper, we call these left braces
trivial
.

Example 1
.

Let
p
be a prime number,
n
a natural number and
t\in\{1,...,n\}
. Set
(B,+):=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z},+)
. Then, the operation

a\circ b:=a+b+p^{t}ab
makes
(B,+)
into a left brace that we will denote by
C(p,n,t)
. Moreover, by
[
25
, Proposition

every left brace with cyclic multiplicative group is isomorphic to the direct product of such a left braces.

Convention 2
.

Given a left brace
(B,+,\circ)
and an element

of

, we will indicate by

the order of

in
(B,+)
and by

the order of

in
(B,\circ)
.

As we said before, every involutive solution give rise to a left brace on the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
, where the multiplicative operation coincides with the usual composition. We will refer to
(\mathcal{G}(X,r),+,\circ)
as the

The multiplicative group of a left brace acts by group automorphisms on the additive
group. The action is given by

\displaystyle\lambda_{-}:(B,\circ)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Aut}(B,+)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto(\lambda_{a}:x\mapsto-a+a\circ x)

Similarly to rings, the notion of ideal of a left brace was given in
[

]
and reformulated in

Definition 1.7
.

Let
B
be a left brace. A subset

if it is a subgroup of the multiplicative group and

If

Definition 1.8

Let

A left brace

A subset

2
Uniconnection of some indecomposable involutive solutions

The goal of this section is showing that the the permutation group of some indecomposable solutions force them to be uniconnected. As a main result, we will show that left braces with cyclic additive group only provide uniconnected solutions.

We start by the following easy result. Recall that a group is said to be a
Dedekind group
if all its subgroups are normal.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(X,r)
be an indecomposable solution such that
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
is a Dedekind group. Then,
(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
can not have non-trivial core-free subgroup, hence the thesis follows by
1.11
.
∎

A left brace is said to be
cyclic
if so is its additive group. This class of left braces are considered in several papers (see for example
[
26
,
27
]
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
B
be a finite cyclic left brace and
(X,r)
be an indecomposable solution with
B
as permutation left brace. Then,
(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution.

Proof.

By
1.11
,
(X,r)
can be constructed starting from a transitive cycle base
Y
, an element
a_{1}\in Y
and a core-free subgroup
K
of
B
contained in
St(a_{1})
. Moreover, by
[
2
, Lemma
3.2
]
,
a_{1}
is an additive generator of
B
. Now, by
[
2
, Proposition
3.3
]
we have that
St(a_{1})
coincides with
Soc(B)
, therefore it is a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
and, since
(Soc(B),\circ)=(Soc(B),+)
, we have that
(Soc(B),\circ)
is cyclic. Moreover, being all the subgroups of
(Soc(B),\circ)
characteristic in
Soc(B)
, we obtain that every multiplicative subgroup contained in
Soc(B)
is a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
, hence
K
must be trivial.
∎

In addition, cyclic left braces provide further solutions that are uniconnected by retractions.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
B
be a finite cyclic left brace and
(X,r)
be an indecomposable solution with
B
as permutation left brace. Then,
\Ret^{n}(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Proof.

If
n=0
the thesis follows by
2.2
. Since by
[
2
, Theorem 3.5]
, the permutation left brace of
\Ret(X,r)
is the factor left brace
B/Soc(B)
, that clearly has cyclic additive group, the thesis follows by an easy induction on
n
.
∎

In general, the previous result can not be extended if
B
has not cyclic additive group, as we can see in the following example.

Example 2
.

Let
(X,r)
be the involutive solution given by
X:=\{1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8\}
,
\sigma_{1}=\sigma_{2}:=(1,2)(3,5)(4,7)(6,8)
,
\sigma_{3}=\sigma_{8}:=(1,6,4,3)(2,5,7,8)
,
\sigma_{4}=\sigma_{7}:=(1,3,4,6)(2,8,7,5)
,
\sigma_{5}=\sigma_{6}:=(1,7)(2,4)(3,8)(5,6)
and
\tau_{y}(x):=\sigma^{-1}_{\sigma_{x}(y)}(y)
for all

. Then,
(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution, but
\Ret(X,r)
is not uniconnected.

In this way, indecomposable solutions with a Z-group permutation group are uniconnected, with one exception.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
(X,r)
be an indecomposable solution with a Z-group permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
having no Sylow
2
-subgroups of size
4
. Then,
(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution.

Proof.

By

, the permutation left brace of
(X,r)
is a cyclic left brace. Then, the thesis follows by

.
∎

In
[

the second author conjectured that if
(X,r)
is an indecomposable involutive solutions such that the permutation group

is isomorphic to a dihedral group, then it is uniconnected. As an application of the previous results, we show the conjecture for several values of

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
(X,r)
be an indecomposable solution such that the permutation group
\mathcal{G}(X,r)
is isomorphic to the dihedral group having order

(X,r)
is an uniconnected solution.

Proof.

Since

is a Z-group with no Sylow subgroups having order

, hence the thesis follows by

.
∎

Example 3
.

Let
X:=\{1,2,3,4\}
and

be the solution given by

In this section, we extend the results obtained in

At first, we need a couple of lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

be a cyclic left brace of size

, for some odd prime number

has an automorphism

of order

If

is clearly one of its automorphisms. Conversely, suppose that

\alpha
is an automorphism of order

\alpha(a):=-a

-(a\circ b)=(-a)\circ(-b)

Lemma 3.2

Let

4
Indecomposable solutions with minimal non-cyclic permutation group

In this section, we study indecomposable involutive solutions with minimal non-cyclic group. In particular, we prove that these solutions always are uniconnected and we classify them.

First of all, recall that a finite group

Lemma 4.1

(See

are distinct prime numbers,

and

Convention 3
.

From now on, the groups of the previous lemma will be called of

Let

2.1

At first, we consider indecomposable involutive solutions with permutation group of type a) and b).

Proposition 4.5
.

Let

be distinct prime numbers with

A_{p}\rtimes_{\alpha}A_{q}

\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}

Proof.

At first, recall that by
[

(A_{q},+)

If

Conversely, suppose that

(k_{1}(a_{1}+\alpha_{a_{2}}(b_{1})),k_{2}(a_{2}+b_{2}+q^{t}a_{2}b_{2}))=(k_{1}a_{1}+\alpha^{\prime}_{k_{2}a_{2}}(k_{1}b_{1}),k_{2}a_{2}+k_{2}b_{2}+k_{2}^{2}q^{t}a_{2}b_{2})

In this section, which is independent from the previous ones, we focus on the Dehornoy’s class of involutive solutions (not necessarily indecomposable). In

Conjecture 1

)

Remark 5.1

Using the same terminology of

5.1
Dehornoy’s class and left braces

This section is devoted to explain the precise link between an involutive solution
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
and its permutation left brace
B
. Before giving the first result, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For an involutive solution
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
the equality

\Omega_{n}(x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},...,x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})=(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}\in X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}}

Proof.

We show the thesis by induction on
n
. If
n\in\{1,2\}
, the thesis is clear. Now, suppose the thesis for
n
and we show the thesis for
n+1
. Then

\begin{split}&\Omega_{n+1}(x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},...,x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})=\noindent\\
&=\Omega_{n}(x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},...,x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}})\cdotp\Omega_{n}(x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},...,y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})\\
&=(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}})\cdotp(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})\\
&=(\lambda_{((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-}\circ(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})\\
&=(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))\circ\lambda_{((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ x}(a_{i_{1}})))^{-}\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})\\
&=(((n-1)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))+\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}})))^{-}\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}})\\
&=(n\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}})))^{-}\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}\\
\end{split}

for all
x\circ K_{i_{1},j_{1}},y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}\in X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}}
, where in the first equality we used the inductive hypothesis and in the fifth equality we used the left brace equality
z\circ\lambda_{z^{-}\circ w}(v)=z+\lambda_{w}(v)
.
∎

In
[
20
, Theorem G]
, it was shown that the class of an involutive solution
(X,r)
is the least common multiple of the orders of the permutations
\sigma_{x}
in the additive group of
(\mathcal{G}(X,r),+,\circ)
. Here, we recover this result showing the precise link between the orders of the
\sigma_{x}
and the elements of the permutation left brace.

Proposition 5.3
.

The Dehornoy’s class of
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is equal to the least common multiple of the additive orders of the
\{a_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
(B,+)
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

5.2
, we have that
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is of class
m
, for a natural number
m
, if and only if
(((m)\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}}))^{-})\circ y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}=y\circ K_{i_{2},j_{2}}
for all
x,y\in B
,
i_{1},i_{2}\in I
,
j_{1}\in J_{i_{1}}
and
j_{2}\in J_{i_{2}}
, and this is equivalent to have
m\lambda_{x}(a_{i_{1}})\in\bigcap_{i,j}core(K_{i,j})
. Since
o(a_{i})_{+}=o(\lambda_{x}(a_{i}))_{+}
for all
x\in B
and, by
1.9
, we have
|\bigcap_{i,j}core(K_{i,j})|=1
, it follows that
m
must be equal to the least common multiple of the orders of the
\{a_{i}\}_{a_{i}\in I}
in
(B,+)
.
∎

Given a finite group
(G,\circ)
, recall that the
exponent
of
G
, which we indicate by
exp(G,\circ)
, is the natural number given by the least common multiple of their elements.

Corollary 5.4
.

The Dehornoy’s class
d
of
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is equal to
exp(B,+)
. In particular,
d
divides
|B|
.

Proof.

By
5.3
, we have that
d
divides
exp(B,+)
. Since
\bigcup_{i\in I}B_{i}
additively generates
B
, we have
o(z)_{+}
divides
d
, for every
z\in B
, hence
exp(B,+)
divides
d
and the equality
d=exp(B,+)
follows. The second part follows since exponent divides order in a finite group.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

The Dehornoy’s class
d
of
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is equal to the maximum of the set
\{o(x)_{+}|x\in B\}
.

Proof.

Since in every finite abelian group there exist an element such that its order is equal to the exponent, the statement follows by
5.4
.
∎

The previous results state that if we have suitable informations on the additive group of a left brace
(B,+,\circ)
, then we can estimate the Dehornoy’s class of the involutive solutions provided by
B
.

Definition 5.6
.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we indicate by
g(n)
the maximum order of a permutation in
Sym(n)
.

By a standard calculation, we have that
g(n)\leq a_{n}
for all

. In
[
14
, Proposition 3.11]
the author showed that the bound part of
2
follows if
(B,\circ)
is abelian and
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is square-free. In particular, it was shown that, in this case, the Dehornoy’s class is bounded by
g(n)
. If
(B,\circ)
is cyclic, the square-freeness can be dropped.

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
has cyclic permutation group (in other words,
(B,\circ)
is a cyclic group) and let
n
be such that

. Then the Dehornoy’s class of

(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is bounded by
g(n)
.

Proof.

We have that
(B,\circ)
is generated by a permutation of
Sym(n)
, hence the size of
B
is bounded by
g(n)
. Moreover, by
5.4

d
divides
|B|
, therefore the thesis follows.
∎

The following easy example shows that the bound obtained in the previous proposition is the best possible, if
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
has cyclic permutation group.

Example 4
.

Let
X:=\{1,...,n\}
and
\gamma
a permutation of
Sym(X)
having order
s
. Then, the map
\sigma_{x}
from
X
to itself given by
\sigma_{x}(y):=\gamma(y)
for all

give rise to an involutive solution

having cyclic permutation group. Moreover, since
\Omega_{n}(x_{1},...,x_{n}):=\gamma^{n-1}(x_{n})
for all

, we have that
(X,r)
is of class
s
.

It is well-known that every finite abelian group is isomorphic to a direct product of cyclic groups having prime-power size. In the following result we show that the bound part of
2
follows for a large family of involutive solutions with abelian permutation group (not necessarily square-free).

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose that
(B,\circ)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\mathbb{Z}\times...\times\mathbb{Z}/p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\mathbb{Z}
, where
p_{1},...,p_{r}
are prime number not necessarily distinct. Moreover, suppose that
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
whenever
p_{i}=p_{j}
. Then the class of
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)
is bounded by

.

Proof.

Let
m:=p_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}+...+p_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}
, then we have that

d=exp(B,+)\leq\prod_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\leq a_{m}

where the last inequality follows since by hypothesis
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}
whenever
p_{i}=p_{j}
. By
[
22
, Theorem 4]
, we have that
n=|X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}}|\geq m
and hence
d\leq a_{m}\leq a_{n}
.
∎

By results of
[
14
, Sections 3-4]
, we know that every involutive solution having size at most

has class bounded by

(where
n
is the size of the solution). If

, the unique value for which

is

. By an inspection of involutive solutions having size

, we know that all the ones with abelian permutation group have Dehornoy’s class bounded by

. Thus, it is natural to ask if this is true for every involutive solution with abelian permutation group.

Conjecture 3
.

The Dehornoy’s class of an involutive solution
(X,r)
with abelian permutation group is bounded by

.

Other involutive solutions
(X_{B,I,\mathcal{A},\mathcal{K}},r)

Suppose that

By induction on

Let

We conclude the subsection giving the computation of the Dehornoy’s class for a large class of involutive solutions with dihedral permutation group.

Proposition 5.11

Suppose that

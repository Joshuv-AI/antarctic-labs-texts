Normalized Gompertz wavelets and their application


Abstract
In the present paper, we define the Gompertz wavelets and show their basic properties. In particular, we prove that the admissibility condition holds for them. We also compute the normalizing factors in the space of square intergrable functions L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) and present an explicit formula for them in terms of the Bernoulli numbers. Then, after implementing the second-order Gompertz wavelets into Matlab’s Wavelet Toolbox, we apply them to study the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic in Saudi Arabia.

Introduction

The Gompertz function is described by the following autonomous differential equation of the first order

x^{\prime}(t)=sx\log\frac{x_{max}}{x}\quad\quad x(0)=x_{0}>0,

(1)

with parameters
s-
growth rate and
x_{max}-
saturation level (asymptote),
0<x_{0}<x_{max}
;
\log
is the natural logarithm. After solving (
1
) we can write the Gompertz function in the following convenient form

x(t)=x_{max}e^{-e^{-s(t-t_{0})}},

(2)

where constant
t_{0}
appears in the integration process of (
1
) and is connected with the initial condition
x(0)=x_{0}=x_{max}e^{-e^{st_{0}}}
, thus

t_{0}=\frac{1}{s}\log\log(x_{max}/x_{0})
. It is easy to check that
t_{0}
is also the inflection point of
x(t)
(
2
).

Function (
2
) was first described and applied in actuarial mathematics in 1825 by Mr. Benjamin Gompertz
[
6
]
. Since then, the Gompertz function has found applications in probability theory (Gumbel distribution), biology, medicine, economics, engineering, physics and many other fields. The first hundred years of the use of this function are well described by Winsor
[
19
]
. The interesting story of the next almost one hundred years can be found in the article by Tjørve and Tjørve
[
18
]
.

In recent years, many articles have appeared in which the Gompertz function was used to describe Covid-19 cases (infected people who were tested positive). Ohnishi et al
[
12
]
showed that the first waves of Covid-19 cases in 11 selected countries (Japan, USA, Russia, Brazil, China, Italy, Indonesia, Spain, South Korea, UK, and Sweden) can be modeled using the Gompertz function. They also compared the mechanism of the appearance of the Gompertz function with the mechanism of the time dependence of the number of pions produced in nucleus-nucleus collisions, which is also described by the Gompertz function. Dhahbi et al
[
2
]
used the Gompertz model to describe the first wave of cases in Saudi Arabia. Kundu et al
[
8
]
proposed an
automated COVID-19 detection system based on convolution neural networks using the Gompertz function. Estrada and Bartesaghi
[
5
]
linked the networked SIS model with the Gompertz function.

The structure of the article is as follows. In Sec.
2
we describe the basic properties of the Gompertz function and its derivatives. Sec.
3
is devoted to the Gompertz wavelets based on the second derivative, that we use later. Then, in the same section, we define the Gompertz wavelets for any derivative and prove that the admissibility condition holds for them. In Sec.
4
we present some applications of the Gompertz wavelets, in particular for modeling the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic. The paper is concluded in Sec.
5
. All the data, which we analyze, were taken from the website Our World in Data
[
13
]
.

The Gompertz wavelets

3.1
Wavelets

We briefly outline the basic general properties of wavelets (cf.
[
1
,
10
,
9
]
), which we will need later. A wavelet or mother wavelet (see Daubechies
[
1
]
, p.24 ) is a function
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that the following
admissibility condition holds:

C_{\psi}=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\xi|^{-1}|\widehat{\psi}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty,

(6)

where
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)
is the Fourier transform
F(\psi)
of
\psi
, i.e.,

F(\psi)(\xi)=\widehat{\psi}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)e^{-i\xi x}dx.

Since for
\psi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
\widehat{\psi}(\xi)
is continuous then condition (
6
) is only satisfied if
\widehat{\psi}(0)=0
, which is equivalent to
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)dx=0
. On the other hand, Daubechies
[
1
]
, p.24 points out that condition
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)dx=0
together with a slightly stronger than the integrability condition
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|(1+|x|)^{\alpha}dx<\infty
, for some
\alpha>0
are sufficient for (
6
). Usually, in practice much more is assumed for the function
\psi
hence, from a practical point of view, conditions
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi(x)dx=0
and (
6
) are equivalent. Suppose the function
\psi
is also square-integrable,
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with the norm

||\psi||=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}.

Having a mother wavelet we can generate a doubly-indexed family of wavelets (called children wavelets), by dilating and translating

\psi^{a,b}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|a|}}\psi\Big(\frac{x-b}{a}\Big),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R},\;a\neq 0
. The normalization has been chosen so that
||\psi^{a,b}||=||\psi||
for all
a,b
. It is assumed usually that
||\psi||=1
. The continuous wavelet transform (CWT) of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for this wavelet
family is defined as

(T^{wav}f)(a,b)=\langle f,\psi^{a,b}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\psi^{a,b}(x)dx.

(7)

3.2
Wavelets based on the second derivative of the Gompertz function

Consider second derivative of the Gompertz function (
2
) with parameters
x_{max}=1,s=1,t_{0}=0
,
x(t)=e^{-e^{-t}}
. Since

x^{\prime}(t)=-x\log x=e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}
, then by (
4
) or directly we get

x^{\prime\prime}(t)=x\log x(1+\log x)=e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}(e^{-t}-1).

(8)

Note that
x^{\prime}(t)=e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}
is also the probability density function (pdf) of the Gumbel distribution. We calculate the three, following integrals related to (
8
), each by substituting
x=e^{-e^{-t}},x^{\prime}(t)=-x\log x=e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}
:

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{\prime\prime}(t)dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}(e^{-t}-1)dt=-\int_{0}^{1}(\log x+1)dx=(-x\log x)|_{0}^{1}=0,

(9)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x^{\prime\prime}(t)|dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}|e^{-t}-1|dt=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}(e^{-t}-1)dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}(1-e^{-t})dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1/e}(\log x+1)dx+\int_{1/e}^{1}(\log x+1)dx=\frac{1}{e}+\frac{1}{e}=\frac{2}{e},

(10)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x^{\prime\prime}(t))^{2}dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(e^{-e^{-t}})^{2}(e^{-t})^{2}(e^{-t}-1)^{2}dt=-\int_{0}^{1}x\log x(1+\log x)^{2}dx=\frac{1}{8}.

(11)

Let us define now the Gompertz mother wavelet
\psi_{2}(t)
(Figure
2
) by:

\psi_{2}(t)=2\sqrt{2}x^{\prime\prime}(t)=2\sqrt{2}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}(e^{-t}-1),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(12)

Figure 2
:
Gompertz mother wavelet
\psi_{2}(x)

By definition (
12
) and (
9
-
11
) we have

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\psi_{2}(t)dt=0,\;||\psi_{2}||=1,\;\psi_{2}(t)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Although it is easy to prove that in our case, the sufficient conditions for the admissibility condition (
6
), described in the previous subsection are fulfilled, we will show a direct and interesting calculation to prove (
6
). Let us start with an observation concerning the Euler Gamma function

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}e^{-x}dx,\quad Re(z)>0.

(13)

After substituting in (
13
)
z=1+i\xi
and changing the integration variable
x=\exp(-t)
we obtain

\Gamma(1+i\xi)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{i\xi}e^{-x}dx=\int_{\infty}^{-\infty}e^{-i\xi t}e^{-e^{-t}}(-e^{-t})dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}e^{-i\xi t}dt,

which shows that the Fourier transform of the first derivative of the Gompertz function
x^{\prime}(t)=e^{-e^{-t}}e^{-t}
is as follows

F(x^{\prime})(\xi)=\widehat{x^{\prime}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(1+i\xi).

(14)

Using the formula for the Fourier transform of a derivative, (
14
) and then definition (
12
) we get

F(x^{\prime\prime})(\xi)=\widehat{x^{\prime\prime}}(\xi)=\frac{i\xi}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(1+i\xi),

and

F(\psi_{2})(\xi)=\widehat{\psi_{2}}(\xi)=\frac{2i\xi}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(1+i\xi).

(15)

Now we can show that for the Gompertz mother wavelet
\psi_{2}(t)
the admissibility condition (
6
) is satisfied and even the integral can be expressed in a closed form in terms of the Riemann Zeta function
\zeta(z)
. Namely, using (
15
), the well-known property of the Gamma function

|\Gamma(1+i\xi)|^{2}=\Gamma(1+i\xi)\Gamma(1-i\xi)=\frac{\pi\xi}{\sinh\pi\xi},

(16)

and the following formula from Dwight’s Tables
[
4
]
(item no
860.502
):

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2}}{\sinh ax}dx=\frac{7}{2a^{3}}\zeta(3),

we obtain

\displaystyle C_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\xi|^{-1}|\widehat{\psi_{2}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\xi|}\left|\frac{2i\xi}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(1+i\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\xi|}\frac{4\xi^{2}}{\pi}\frac{\pi\xi}{\sinh\pi\xi}d\xi

(17)

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{4\xi^{2}}{|\sinh\pi\xi|}d\xi=\frac{56\zeta(3)}{\pi^{2}}<\infty.

(18)

We can now generate a doubly-indexed family of the Gompertz wavelets from the mother Gompertz wavelet
\psi_{2}
by dilating and translating

\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\psi_{2}\Big(\frac{t-b}{a}\Big),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R},\;a>0
.

3.3
Wavelets based on higher derivatives of the Gompertz function

Similarly, as in the previous subsection, we consider the
n
th (
n=3,4,...
) derivative
x^{(n)}(t)
of the Gompertz function
x(t)=e^{-e^{-t}}
with parameters
x_{max}=1,s=1,t_{0}=0
. For this particular case formula (
3
) reads

x^{(n)}(t)=(-1)^{n}x(t)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{n\brace k}\log^{k}x(t).

(19)

Since

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|x^{(n)}(t)|dt=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big|(-1)^{n}x(t)\log x(t)\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{n\brace k}\log^{k-1}x(t)\Big|dt=\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{n\brace k}\log^{k-1}x\Big|dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{n\brace k}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\log^{k-1}x\Big|dx=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{n\brace k}(k-1)!<\infty

we see that
x^{(n)}(t)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Rza̧dkowski et al
[
15
]
(Theorem 3.2, p. 377) proved the following formula for derivatives of the Gompertz function
x(t)=e^{-e^{-t}}
:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(x^{(n)}(t))^{2}dt=(-1)^{n}\frac{B_{2n}(1-2^{2n})}{2n}=\frac{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)}{2n},\quad n=1,2,\ldots,

(20)

where
B_{n}
is the
n
th Bernoulli number. The Bernoulli numbers are well described in the book by Duren
[
3
]
. For the convenience of the reader, we sketch here only some of their basic properties.

The Bernoulli numbers
B_{n},n=1,2,\ldots
have the following exponential generating function

B_{0}+B_{1}z+B_{2}\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+\cdots=\frac{z}{e^{z}-1},\qquad|z|<2\pi.

and vanish for all odd
n\geq 3
. The numbers are rational and they appear in relations such that

\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k^{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n+1\choose j}B_{j}m^{n+1-j},\quad m,n\geq 1,

or

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2n}}=(-1)^{n+1}\frac{2^{2n-1}\pi^{2n}}{(2n)!}B_{2n},\quad n=1,2,\ldots

The first few nonzero Bernoulli numbers are as follows

B_{0}=1,\;B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\;B_{2}=\frac{1}{6},\;B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30},\;B_{6}=\frac{1}{42},\;B_{8}=-\frac{1}{30},\;B_{10}=\frac{5}{66}.

Note that in case
n=2
formula (
20
) agrees with calculation (
11
) because

(-1)^{2}\frac{B_{4}(1-2^{4})}{4}=\frac{1}{4}\cdot\Big(-\frac{1}{30}\Big)\cdot(-15)=\frac{1}{8}.

We can define now the Gompertz mother wavelet
\psi_{n}(t)
, related to the
n
th derivative of the Gompertz function (
19
) as

\psi_{n}(t)=\Big(\frac{2n}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)}\Big)^{1/2}x^{(n)}(t).

(21)

Because of (
20
) the
L^{2}
norm
||\psi_{n}||=1
.

We will show now that, for
\psi_{n}
the admissibility condition (
6
) is fulfilled. Applying in (
14
)
(n-1)
-times the formula for the Fourier transform of a derivative we get

F(x^{(n)})(\xi)=\widehat{x^{(n)}}(\xi)=\frac{(i\xi)^{n-1}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(1+i\xi),

which gives by definition (
21
)

F(\psi_{n})(\xi)=\widehat{\psi_{n}}(\xi)=\Big(\frac{n}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)\pi}\Big)^{1/2}(i\xi)^{n-1}\Gamma(1+i\xi).

(22)

Using (
16
) and the following formula from Dwight’s Tables
[
4
]
(item no
860.509
):

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p-1}}{\sinh ax}dx=\frac{2\Gamma(p)}{a^{p}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\Big)\zeta(p),\quad a>0,p>1

we obtain

\displaystyle C_{\psi_{n}}

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\xi|^{-1}|\widehat{\psi_{n}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=2\pi\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|\xi|}\cdot\frac{n}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)\pi}\cdot\xi^{2n-2}\cdot\frac{\pi\xi}{\sinh\pi\xi}d\xi

(23)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi n}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\xi^{2n-2}}{|\sinh\pi\xi|}=\frac{2\pi n}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)}\cdot\frac{4\Gamma(2n-1)}{\pi^{2n-1}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{2^{p}}\Big)\zeta(2n-1)

(24)

\displaystyle=\frac{n(2^{2n-1}-1)\Gamma(2n-1)}{|B_{2n}|(2^{2n}-1)\cdot 2^{2n-4}\cdot\pi^{2n-2}}\cdot\zeta(2n-1)<\infty,

(25)

where
\zeta(z)
is the Riemann Zeta function.

As usually, we generate from the mother wavelet a doubly-indexed family of wavelets from
\psi_{n}
by dilating and translating

\psi_{n}^{a,b}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\psi_{n}\Big(\frac{t-b}{a}\Big),

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R},\;a>0,\;n=2,3,\ldots
.

4
Applications

We will look, in a time series
(y_{n})
, for points corresponding to zeros of the second or the third derivative of the Gompertz function. This is equivalent to detecting points, where the sequence of second differences,

\Delta^{2}y_{n}=y_{n+1}-2y_{n}+y_{n-1},

takes a value close to zero or a maximum respectively.

To calculate the CWT (Continuous Wavelet Transform) coefficients for
(\Delta^{2}y_{n})
, we implement the mother wavelet
\psi_{2}(t)
, (
12
) into Matlab’s wavelet toolbox. Two parameters of the Gompertz wavelet,
b
- shift (translation) and
a
- dilation, can be read from the CWT scalogram by finding a point where the sum (
26
) (denoted on the scalogram by Index) is locally maximal. It remains to determine the third parameter of the wave, i.e., its saturation level
y_{max}
. Assume that time series
(y_{n})
locally follows the Gompertz function
\displaystyle y_{n}\approx y(n)=y_{max}e^{-e^{-\frac{n-b}{a}}}
. By definition (
12
) we have

y^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t).

Lemma
.

The continuous wavelet transform CWT (
7
) of the function
y^{\prime\prime}(t)
, by using Gompertz wavelets
\psi_{2}^{c,d}

(T^{wav}y^{\prime\prime})(c,d)=\langle y^{\prime\prime},\psi_{2}^{c,d}\rangle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{\prime\prime}(t)\psi_{2}^{c,d}(t)dt,

takes the maximum value when
c=a
and
d=b
.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality

|(T^{wav}y^{\prime\prime})(c,d)|=|\langle y^{\prime\prime},\psi_{2}^{c,d}\rangle|\leq||y^{\prime\prime}||\;||\psi_{2}^{c,d}||=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}||\psi_{2}^{a,b}||\;||\psi_{2}^{c,d}||=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}.

However the maximum is reached for
c=a
,
d=b
, because:

(T^{wav}y^{\prime\prime})(a,b)=\langle y^{\prime\prime},\psi_{2}^{a,b}\rangle=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}\langle\psi_{2}^{a,b},\psi_{2}^{a,b}\rangle=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}.

∎

In view of Lemma, for the maximal value of Index we get successively

\displaystyle\textrm{Index}=\sum\limits_{n}

\displaystyle\Delta^{2}y_{n}\psi_{2}^{a,b}(n)\approx\sum\limits_{n}\Delta^{2}y(n)\psi_{2}^{a,b}(n)\approx\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{\prime\prime}(t)\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t)dt=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t)\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t)dt

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\psi_{2}^{a,b}(t))^{2}dt=\frac{y_{max}}{2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}}.

Using (
26
) we can estimate the saturation level
y_{max}
as follows

y_{max}\approx 2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}\sum\limits_{n}\Delta^{2}y_{n}\psi_{2}^{a,b}(n)=2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}\textrm{Index}.

(27)

y_{max}\approx 2\sqrt{2}a^{3/2}\sum\limits_{n}\Delta^{2}y_{n}\psi_{2}^{a,b}(n)=2\sqrt{2}\cdot 20^{3/2}\cdot 789.5=199,729.

(29)

Since the second differences (not the second derivatives) were used for the analysis, then the calculated saturation levels (28) and (29) cannot be expected to be exactly equal to the theoretical ones, i.e., 100,000 and 200,000 respectively.

5
Conclusions and further work

In this paper we defined Gompertz wavelets of any order. We have shown that the admissibility condition holds for them. We also calculated their normalizing factors in the space of square intergrable functions
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and showed that they are expressed by an explicit formula in terms of Bernoulli numbers. Next, we illustrated the utility of second-order Gompertz wavelets in the theoretical situation, where the signal consists of two exact Gompertz functions. Then we used them to study the spread of the Covid-19 pandemic in Saudi Arabia.

In further work, we plan to apply the second- or higher-order Gompertz wavelets to some real-world data from the fields such as economics, finance or biology. One could also deal with some generalizations of the basic S-shaped curves to define the corresponding wavelets for them. For this purpose, formulas from the paper Rza̧dkowski and Urlińska
[
17
]
could be used, allowing for efficient computation of successive derivatives for a large class of S-shaped functions.

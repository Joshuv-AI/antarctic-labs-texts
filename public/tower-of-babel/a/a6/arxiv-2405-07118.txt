Abstract
. We provide in this Letter a two-point generalisation of the Agmon estimate for Schrödinger operators on graphs recently established by S. Steinerberger. It reduces to his estimate when the two points belong to different sets separated by the potential and the energy, i.e., the allowed and forbidden regions.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 31B15. Secondary 35J10, 35R02.

1.
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a (finite) connected graph with vertices
V=\{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}\}
,
and
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E
means there is an edge between
v_{i}
and
v_{j}
(we simply write
v_{i}\sim v_{j}
).
For
v_{i}\in V
, denote by
\text{deg\,}(v_{i})
the number of vertices connected to
v_{i}
by an edge in
E
.

Consider now the following discrete Laplacian
L
on the graph
G
(acting on real valued functions
\phi:V\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
),
which is formally given by

(L\phi)(v)=\text{deg\,}(v)\phi(v)-\sum_{w\sim v}\phi(w),\quad v\in V.

Given potential
W:V\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
and eigenvalue
E\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
we can further consider the following (spectral) Schrödinger equation

L\phi+W\phi=E\phi.

(1.1)

The motivation of this letter is to understand the global behaviour of such eigenfunctions
\phi
.
For this purpose we also have to introduce the notion
allowed region

{\mathcal{A}}_{V}=\{v\in V:W(v)\leq E\},

the
forbidden region

{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
being its complement in
V
.

Given
W
and
E
, we define the Agmon distance
\rho
on
(V\times V)\backslash({\mathcal{A}}_{V}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{V})
by

\rho(u,v)=\min\{\rho_{1}(u,v),\rho_{2}(u,v)\},

where

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right):

\displaystyle u=v_{1}\sim\cdots\sim v_{\ell}\sim v_{\ell+1},\,\frac{(W(v_{\ell+1})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{\ell+1})}\geq\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\bigg\}

and

\displaystyle\rho_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\inf\bigg\{\log\left(1+\frac{(W(u)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(u)}\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right):

\displaystyle v=v_{1}\sim\cdots\sim v_{\ell}\sim v_{\ell+1},\,\frac{(W(v_{\ell+1})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{\ell+1})}\geq\frac{(W(u)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(u)}\bigg\},

and we set

\rho|_{{\mathcal{A}}_{V}\times{\mathcal{A}}_{V}}\equiv 0.

(1.2)

Here as usual,
(a)_{+}=a
if
a\geq 0
and
(a)_{+}=0
if
a<0
.
For other distances on graphs (combinatorial, resistance, intrinsic, transportation …),
see e.g. the monographs by Bakry-Gentil-Ledoux
[
BGL14
]
,
and by Keller-Lenz-Wojciechowski
[
KLW21
]
.

The following result describes the exponential decay of
\phi
in terms of
\rho
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\phi
a solution of (
1.1
), and for any
u,v\in V
, we have

\min\{|\phi(u)|,|\phi(v)|\}\leq\frac{\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}}{e^{\rho(u,v)}}.

(1.3)

Remark 1.2
.

By the maximum principle, we have

\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}=\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathcal{A}}_{V})}.

Thus for
u\in{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
and
v\in{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
, (
1.3
) reduces to Steinerberger’s Agmon estimate
[
Ste23
]
.
In the continuous setting such estimates can be found in Agmon
[
Agm14
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

We think the assumption (
1.2
) is overlooked in
[
Ste23
]
,
since it is not for sure that two points in
{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
have to be connected by a path with all nodes in
{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The theorem is trivial if both
u
and
v
belong to
{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
as
\rho(u,v)=0
.
So without loss of generality we can assume
u
is in
{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
hence
\frac{W(u)-E}{\text{deg\,}(u)}>0
.
The theorem is also trivial if either
\phi(u)
or
\phi(v)
is zero.
So we can assume
\min\{|\phi(u)|,|\phi(v)|\}>0
.

First we write down the Schrödinger equation on
v_{1}=u

\text{deg\,}(v_{1})\phi(v_{1})-\sum_{w\sim v_{1}}\phi(w)=(E-W(v_{1}))\phi(v_{1}),\quad v_{1}\in V.

This can be rewritten as

\phi(v_{1})=\left[1+\frac{W(v_{1})-E}{\text{deg\,}(v_{1})}\right]^{-1}\frac{1}{\text{deg\,}(v_{1})}\sum_{w\sim v_{1}}\phi(w).

(2.1)

Note that connectedness is used here.
Taking absolute value on both sides we get

\displaystyle|\phi(v_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\left[1+\frac{W(v_{1})-E}{\text{deg\,}(v_{1})}\right]^{-1}\frac{1}{\text{deg\,}(v_{1})}\sum_{w\sim v_{1}}|\phi(w)|

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq\left[1+\frac{W(v_{1})-E}{\text{deg\,}(v_{1})}\right]^{-1}\max_{w\sim v_{1}}|\phi(w)|.

Moreover, we deduce from above estimate

\max_{w\sim v_{1}}|\phi(w)|>|\phi(v_{1})|\geq\min\{|\phi(u)|,|\phi(v)|\}>0.

Hence we can now move from
v_{1}
to its neighbour maximising
|\phi(w)|
(such a vertex is recorded as
v_{2}
)
and then apply the very same argument again on the vertex
v_{2}
.
But this argument can be applied iteratively as long as the new vertex still lies in the forbidden region
{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
.
Altogether this results in a path such that

u=v_{1}\sim\cdots\sim v_{m}\sim v_{m+1},\quad\frac{(W(v_{m+1})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{m+1})}\geq\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}.

(2.3)

Note in particular that
v\in{\mathcal{A}}_{V}\Rightarrow v_{m+1}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
and
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{V}\Rightarrow v_{m+1}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
.

Now, collecting the estimates similar to (
2.2
) that are produced along the path
u=v_{1}\sim\cdots\sim v_{m}\sim v_{m+1}
,
and if
v\in{\mathcal{A}}_{V}
, we then deduce

\displaystyle|\phi(v_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{W(v_{i})-E}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)|\phi(v_{m+1})|

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)|\phi(v_{m+1})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)\left[1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right]^{-1}\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}.

If
v\in{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
,
using the equation (
2.1
) for
v_{m+1}
instead of
v_{1}

(note that in this case we have in addition
v_{m+1}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{V}
), and the stopping condition from (
2.3
),
we then deduce

\displaystyle|\phi(v_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{W(v_{i})-E}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)|\phi(v_{m+1})|

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{m+1})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{m+1})}\right]^{-1}\left|\frac{1}{\text{deg\,}(v_{m+1})}\sum_{w\sim v_{m+1}}\phi(w)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)\left[1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right]^{-1}\|\phi\|_{\ell^{\infty}(V)}.

Note that

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right]^{-1}\right)\left[1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right)\right).

By the definition of
\rho(u,v)
, we have

\exp\left(-\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v)-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v)}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\log\left(1+\frac{(W(v_{i})-E)_{+}}{\text{deg\,}(v_{i})}\right)\right)\leq e^{-\rho(u,v)}.

This proves the theorem.

Coordinating Planning and Tracking in Layered Control Policies via Actor-Critic Learning
Fengjun Yang, Nikolai Matni

Abstract
We propose a reinforcement learning (RL)-based algorithm to jointly train (1) a trajectory planner and (2) a tracking controller in a layered control architecture. Our algorithm arises naturally from a rewrite of the underlying optimal control problem that lends itself to an actor-critic learning approach. By explicitly learning a dual network to coordinate the interaction between the planning and tracking layers, we demonstrate the ability to achieve an effective consensus between the two components, leading to an interpretable policy. We theoretically prove that our algorithm converges to the optimal dual network in the Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) setting and empirically validate its applicability to nonlinear systems through simulation experiments on a unicycle model.



1
Introduction

Layered control architectures
(
Matni et al., 2024
;
Chiang et al., 2007
)
are ubiquitous in complex cyber-physical systems, such as power networks, communication networks, and autonomous robots. For example, a typical autonomous robot has an autonomy stack consisting of decision-making, trajectory optimization, and low-level control. However, despite the widespread presence of such layered control architectures, there has been a lack of a principled framework for their design, especially in the data-driven regime.

In this work, we propose an algorithm for jointly learning a trajectory planner and a tracking controller. We start from an optimal control problem and show that a suitable relaxation of the problem naturally decomposes into reference generation and trajectory tracking layers. We then propose an algorithm to train a layered policy parameterized in a way that parallels this decomposition using actor-critic methods. Different from previous methods, we show how a
dual network
can be trained to coordinate the trajectory optimizer and the tracking controller. Our theoretical analysis and numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed algorithm can achieve good performance in various settings while enjoying inherent interpretability and modularity.

1.1
Related Work

1.1.1
Layered control architectures

The idea of layering has been studied extensively in the multi-rate control literature
(
Rosolia et al., 2022
;
Csomay-Shanklin et al., 2022
)
, through the lens of optimization decomposition
(
Chiang et al., 2007
;
Matni and Doyle, 2016
)
, and for specific application domains
(
Samad et al., 2007
;
Samad and Annaswamy, 2017
;
Jiang, 2018
)
. Recently, Matni et al.
(
Matni et al., 2024
)
proposed a quantitative framework for the design and analysis of layered control architectures, which has since been instantiated to various control and robotics applications
(
Srikanthan et al., 2023a
;
Srikanthan et al., 2023b
;
Zhang et al., 2024
)
. Within this framework, our work is most related to
Srikanthan et al. (2023b)
;
Zhang et al. (2024)
, which seek to design trajectory planners based on past data of a tracking controller. However, we consider the case where the low-level tracking controller is not given and has to be learned with the trajectory planner. We also provide a more principled approach to coordinating planning and tracking that leverages a dual network.

1.1.2
Hierarchical reinforcement learning

Recently, reinforcement learning-based methods have demonstrated impressive performance on highly complex dynamical systems
(
Kumar et al., 2021
;
Kaufmann et al., 2023
)
. Within the RL literature, our approach is most closely related to the idea of
goal-conditioned
reinforcement learning
(
Dayan and Hinton, 1992
;
Kulkarni et al., 2016
;
Levy et al., 2017
;
Nachum et al., 2018a
;
Vezhnevets et al., 2017
;
Nachum et al., 2018b
)
. In this framework, an upper-level agent periodically specifies a goal for the lower-level agent to execute. However, the “intrinsic” reward used to train the lower-level agent is usually heuristically chosen. Nachum et al.
(
Nachum et al., 2018b
)
derived a principled objective for the lower-level agent based on a suboptimality bound introduced by the hierarchical structure, but they focus on the case where the goal is specified as a learned low-dimensional representation. We focus on the case where the dynamics are deterministic and derive a simple quadratic objective for the lower-level agent (tracking layer). We also structure our upper-level agent (planning layer) to generate full trajectories instead of single waypoints.

1.1.3
Actor-critic methods

The actor-critic method
(
Silver et al., 2014
;
Lillicrap et al., 2015
;
Fujimoto et al., 2018
)
describes a class of reinforcement learning algorithms that simultaneously learn a policy and its associated value function. These algorithms have achieved great success with continuous control tasks and have found various applications in the controls and robotics community
(
Wang and Fazlyab, 2024
;
Grandesso et al., 2023
)
. In this paper, we use actor-critic methods to learn a tracking controller and its value function, where the latter is used to help the trajectory planner determine how difficult a generated trajectory is for the tracking controller to follow.

1.2
Statement of Contributions

Our contribution is three-fold. First, we propose a novel way of parameterizing layered policies based on a principled derivation. In this parameterization, we introduce a
dual network
to coordinate the trajectory planner and the tracking controller. We show how this dual network can be trained jointly with other components in the layered policy in an RL fashion. Secondly, we show theoretically and empirically that our algorithm for updating the dual network can recover the optimal dual network parameters for unconstrained linear quadratic regulator (LQR) problems. Finally, we evaluate our method empirically on constrained LQR problems and the unicycle environment to demonstrate its potential to be applied to more complex systems.

Problem Formulation

We consider a discrete-time finite-horizon optimal control problem with state
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
and control input
u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
:

\displaystyle\underset{x_{0:T},u_{0:T-1}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi\sim D_{\xi}}\left[\mathcal{C}(x_{0:T})+\mathcal{D}(u_{0:T-1})\right]

(1)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t=0,1,...,T-1,

\displaystyle x_{0:T}\in\mathcal{X},\quad u_{0:T-1}\in\mathcal{U},\quad x_{0}=\xi.

Here,
T\in\mathbb{Z^{+}}
is a fixed time horizon,
x_{0:T}=[x_{0}^{\top},...,x_{T}^{\top}]^{\top}
and
u_{0:T-1}=[u_{0}^{\top},...,u_{T-1}^{\top}]^{\top}
respectively denote the state and control trajectory.
\mathcal{C}(x_{0:T})
and
\mathcal{D}(u_{0:T-1})
are the state and control costs, respectively. We assume that the input cost and the state and input constraints decouple across time, and denote them respectively by
{\mathcal{D}(u_{0:T-1})=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathcal{D}_{t}(u_{t})}
,
\mathcal{X}=\prod_{t=0}^{T}\mathcal{X}_{t}
, and
\mathcal{U}=\prod_{t=0}^{T}\mathcal{U}_{t}
. The initial condition
\xi
is sampled i.i.d. from a possibly unknown distribution
D_{\xi}
.

As per the reinforcement learning convention, we assume that we only have access to the dynamics via a simulator, i.e., that we do not know
f(x_{t},u_{t})
explicitly, but can simulate the dynamics for any
x_{t}
and
u_{t}
. However, we do assume that we have access to the cost functions
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{D}
, as they are usually designed by the users, instead of being an inherent hidden part of the system. We also assume that we know the constraints
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{U}
for the same reason.

Our goal is to learn a layered policy
{\pi=(\pi^{\mathrm{plan}},\pi^{\mathrm{track}})}
that consists of 1) a trajectory planner

\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{Td_{x}}

that takes in an initial condition
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
and outputs a reference trajectory
r_{0:T}\in\mathcal{X}
, and 2) a tracking controller

\quad\pi^{\mathrm{track}}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{Td_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}

that takes in the current state and a reference trajectory to output a control action to best track the given trajectory. We now decompose problem (
1
) such that it may inform a suitable parameterization for the planning and tracking policies,
\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}
and
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}
.

3
Layered Approach to Optimal Control

We first consider a variation of problem (
1
) with a fixed initial condition
\xi
, and rewrite it into a form that has a natural layered control architecture interpretation. For ease of notation, we use unsubscripted letters
x,u,r
to denote the respective trajectories stacked as a column vector

x:=x_{0:T},\quad u:=u_{0:T-1},\quad r:=r_{0:T}.

We begin the rewrite of problem (
1
) by introducing a redundant variable
r=x
to get an equivalent problem

\displaystyle\underset{r,x,u}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{D}(u)

(2)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t=0,1,...,T-1,

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X},\quad u\in\mathcal{U},\quad x_{0}=\xi,\quad r=x,

where we use the fact that
r=x
to move the state cost and constraint from
x
onto
r
. Defining the indicator functions

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{dyn}(x,u)=\begin{cases}0,&\begin{aligned} &x_{0}=\xi,\\
&x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),u\in\mathcal{U},\end{aligned}\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{state}(r)=\begin{cases}0,&r\in\mathcal{X}\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},

we write the partial augmented Lagrangian of problem (
2
) in terms of the (scaled) dual variable
\nu

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rho}(r,x,u,\nu)

\displaystyle=\;\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{D}(u)+\mathbb{I}_{dyn}(x,u)+\mathbb{I}_{s}(r)+\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert r+\nu-x\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert\nu\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(3)

Applying dual ascent to this augmented Lagrangian, we obtain the following method-of-multiplier updates

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \quad(r^{+},x^{+},u^{+})=\underset{x,u,r}{\text{argmin}}\;&\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{D}(u)+\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert r+\nu-x\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}\;&x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t,\\
&r\in\mathcal{X},\;u\in\mathcal{U},\;x_{0}=\xi\end{aligned}

(4)

\displaystyle\quad\nu^{+}=\nu+(r^{+}-x^{+}),

(5)

which will converge to locally optimal primal and dual variables
r^{*},x^{*},u^{*},\nu^{*}
given mild assumptions on the smoothness and convexity of
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
and the constraints in the neighborhood of the optimal point (See
Bertsekas (2014, §2)
).

For a layered interpretation, we note that the primal update (
4
) can be written as a nested optimization problem

\displaystyle r^{+}=\underset{r}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)

(6)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X}

where
p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)
is the locally optimal value of the
(x,u)
-minimization step

\displaystyle p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)=\underset{x,u}{\text{min}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(u)+\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert r+\nu-x\rVert_{2}^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\;u\in\mathcal{U},\;\forall t,

\displaystyle x_{0}=\xi.

We immediately recognize that optimal control problem (
7
) is finding the control action
u
to minimize a quadratic tracking cost for the reference trajectory

\tilde{r}:=r+\nu.

Thus, this nested rewrite can be seen as breaking the primal minimization problem (
4
) into a trajectory optimization problem (
6
) that seeks to find the best reference
r
and a tracking problem (
7
) that seeks to best track the
perturbed
trajectory
\tilde{r}
. A subtlety here is that the planned trajectory,
r
, and the trajectory sent to the tracking controller,
\tilde{r}
, are different. To understand this discrepancy, let us first consider a similar, but perhaps more intuitive, reference optimization problem:

\displaystyle\underset{r}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+p^{*}(r;\xi)

(8)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X}.

This heuristics-based approach, employed in previous works such as
Srikanthan et al. (2023b)
;
Zhang et al. (2024)
, seeks to find a reference that balances minimizing the nominal cost
\mathcal{C}(r)
and not incurring high tracking cost
p^{*}(r;\xi)
.
In these works, the solution
r
is then sent to the tracking controller unperturbed.

A problem with this approach is that unless the tracking controller can execute the given reference perfectly, the executed trajectory
x
will differ from the planned reference
r
. One can mitigate this deviation by multiplying the tracking cost with a large weight, but this can quickly become numerically ill-conditioned, or bias the planned trajectory towards overly conservative and easy-to-track behaviors.

and
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}

7

can be written as a function of the initial condition

Actor-Critic Learning in the Layered Control Architecture

4.1
Parameterization of the Layered Policy

We parameterize our layered policy
\pi=(\pi^{\mathrm{plan}},\pi^{\mathrm{track}})
so that its structure parallels the dual ascent updates (
6
) and (
7
). The tracking controller
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{Td_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}
, specified by learnable parameters
\phi
, seeks to approximate a feedback controller that solves the tracking problem (
7
).

2

2

The finite-horizon nature of (

) calls for a time-varying controller. Thus, the correct

and associated value function

need to be conditioned on the time step

The trajectory generator
\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}_{\theta,\psi}
seeks to approximately solve the planning problem (
6
). It has learnable parameters
\theta
and
\psi
and is defined as the solution to the optimization problem

\displaystyle\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}_{\theta,\psi}(\xi)=\underset{r}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}(r+v_{\theta}(\xi);\xi)

(9)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X}.

Thus
\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}
generates a reference trajectory from initial condition
\xi
by solving problem (
9
). The objective of this optimization problem contains two learned components,
v_{\theta}
and
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}
, specified by parameters
\theta
and
\psi
, respectively. First,
v_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}^{Td_{x}}
is a dual network that seeks to predict the locally optimal dual variable
\nu^{*}
from initial condition
\xi
. Then, the tracking value function
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}:\mathbb{R}^{Td_{x}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}\to\mathbb{R}
takes in an initial state
\xi
and a reference trajectory
r
and learns to predict the quadratic tracking cost (
7
) that the policy
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}
will incur on this reference trajectory. Summarizing, our layered policy consists of three learned components: the dual network
v_{\theta}
, the low-layer tracking policy
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}
, and its associated value function
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}
. In what follows, we explain how we learn the tracking value function
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}
and policy
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}
jointly via the actor-critic method, and how to update the dual network
v_{\theta}
in a way similar to dual ascent.

4.2
Learning the Tracking Controller via Actor-Critic Method

We use the actor-critic method to jointly learn the tracking value function
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}
and policy
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}
. We are learning a deterministic policy and its value function, a setting that has been extensively explored and for which many off-the-shelf algorithms exist
(
Silver et al., 2014
;
Lillicrap et al., 2015
;
Fujimoto et al., 2018
)
. In what follows, we specify the RL problem for learning the tracking controller and treat the actor-critic algorithm as a black-box solver for finding our desired parameters
\phi
and
\psi
.

We define an augmented system with the state
x^{aug}_{t}=(x_{t},\mathbf{r}_{t})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{(H+1)d_{x}}
, which concatenates
x_{t}
with a
H
-step reference trajectory
\mathbf{r}_{t}=(r_{t}^{\top}\;r_{t+1}^{\top}\;\cdots\;r_{t+H-1}^{\top})^{\top}
, where
H\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
specifies the tracking controller’s horizon of look-ahead. The augmented state transitions are then given by

x^{aug}_{t+1}=\begin{bmatrix}f(x_{t},u_{t})\\
\mathcal{Z}\mathbf{r}_{t}\end{bmatrix},t=1,...T,

(10)

where
\mathcal{Z}
is a block-upshift operator that shifts the reference trajectory forward by one timestep. The cost of the augmented system
c^{aug}
is chosen to match the tracking optimization problem (
7
), i.e., we set

c^{aug}(x^{aug}_{t},u_{t})=\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert x_{t+1}-r_{t+1}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}+\mathcal{D}_{t}(u_{t}).

(11)

The initial condition
x^{aug}_{0}
is found by first sampling
\xi\sim D_{\xi}
, and then setting
\mathbf{r}_{0}
to the first
H
steps of the reference generated by
\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}(\xi)
. We then run the actor-critic algorithm on this augmented system to jointly learn
p_{\psi}^{\pi^{\mathrm{track}}}
and
\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}
.

4.3
Learning the Dual Network

We design our dual network update as an iterative procedure that mirrors the dual ascent update step (
5
), which moves the dual variable in the direction of the mismatch between reference
r^{+}
and execution
x^{+}
. At each iteration, we sample a batch of initial conditions
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{B}
, and for each
\xi_{i}
, we solve the planning problem (
9
) with current parameters
\phi
and
\theta
to obtain reference trajectories
r_{i}=\pi^{\mathrm{plan}}_{\theta^{(k)},\psi}(\xi).
We then send the perturbed trajectories
\tilde{r}_{i}=r_{i}+v_{\theta}(\xi_{i})
to the tracking controller to obtain the executed trajectories

x_{i,t+1}=f(x_{i,t},\pi^{\mathrm{track}}_{\phi}(x_{i,t},\tilde{\mathbf{r}}_{i,t})),\quad t=0,...,T.

Similar to the dual ascent step, we then perform a gradient ascent step in
\theta
to move
v_{\theta^{(k)}}(\xi_{i})
in the direction of
r_{i}-x_{i}
:

\displaystyle\theta^{+}

\displaystyle\leftarrow\theta+\eta\left(\nabla_{\theta}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\left(r_{i}-x_{i}\right)^{\top}v_{\theta}(\xi_{i})\right)

(12)

\displaystyle=\theta+\eta\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\left(r_{i}-x_{i}\right)^{\top}J_{v,\theta}(\xi_{i};\theta),

where
J_{v,\theta}
denotes the Jacobian of
v
w.r.t.
\theta
. Note that even though
r_{i}
and
x_{i}
implicitly depend on
\theta

5

if the tracking problem is solved to sufficient accuracy.

Conclusion

We proposed a principled way of parameterizing and learning a layered control policy composed of a trajectory planner and a tracking controller. We derived our parameterization from an optimal control problem and showed that a dual network emerges naturally to coordinate the two components. We showed that our algorithm can learn to predict the optimal dual variable for unconstrained LQR problems and validated this theory via simulation experiments. Further simulation experiments also demonstrated the potential of applying this method to nonlinear control problems. Future work will explore using the dual network to inform constraint tightening and parameterizing the planner (
9
) directly as a neural network to reduce online computation.

The LQR problem considered in Section

16

17

\xi
. We then derive the closed-form solutions to the updates (
16
) and (
17
). Finally, we use a contraction argument to show our desired result in Theorem
1
. In the process, we make clear the conditions on step size
\eta
and batch size
B
to guarantee the contraction.

For easing notation, for the rest of this section, we again define
\tilde{r}:=r+\nu
. We also define the matrices

E:=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&&\ldots&0\\
B&0&&\ldots&0\\
AB&B&&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&&&\vdots\\
A^{T-1}B&A^{T-2}B&&\ldots&B\end{bmatrix},\qquad F:=\begin{bmatrix}I\\

Lemma 2
.

For the problem considered in section
2

\xi
, the optimal dual variable can be expressed as a unique linear map from
\xi
as

\nu^{*}=-\frac{2}{\rho}Q\left(-E(E^{\top}QE+R)^{-1}E^{\top}QF+F\right)\xi.

Proof.

From the KKT condition for the optimization problem (
2
), we have that

\nabla_{r}\left(r^{\top}Qr+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert r+\nu^{*}-x^{*}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\Big|_{r=r^{*}}=0.

Solving for
r^{*}
, we get that

2(Q+\frac{\rho}{2}I)r^{*}=-\rho\nu^{*}+\rho x^{*}.

Also from the KKT condition, we have that
r^{*}=x^{*}
. Subbing this into the above expression and rearranging terms, we get that

\nu^{*}=-\frac{2}{\rho}Qr^{*}=-\frac{2}{\rho}Qx^{*}.

Finally, from the equivalence of the original problem (
1
) and the redundant problem (
2
), we see that
x^{*}
can be expressed in closed form as

x^{*}=\left(-E(E^{\top}QE+R)^{-1}E^{\top}QF+F\right)\xi.

∎

We now derive the closed-form update rules and show the following.

Lemma 3
.

In the LQR setting, we have that the difference between the updates
r^{+}
and
x^{+}
can be written as a linear map of the initial condition
\xi

r^{+}-x^{+}=H\Theta^{(k)}\xi+G\xi,

where

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=-\frac{2}{\rho}P(Q+P)^{-1}P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top},

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:=HF+F.

H
is symmetric negative definite.

Proof.

We start by deriving the closed-form expressions for solving both the updates (
16
) and (
17
). We begin by writing out the update rule more explicitly. First, we note that we can write all

satisfying the dynamics constraint as

x=Eu+F\xi,\quad u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{u}}.

Define

\displaystyle p(\tilde{r},\xi,u)

\displaystyle=u^{\top}Ru+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert\tilde{r}-x\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=u^{\top}Ru+\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert\tilde{r}-(Eu+F\xi))\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

We can solve for the optimal control action in closed form as

\displaystyle u^{*}

\displaystyle=\text{argmin}_{u}p(\tilde{r},\xi,u)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho}{2}\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-\tilde{r}),

where we defined

\bar{R}=R+\frac{\rho}{2}E^{\top}E.

Subbing this back, we get that

\displaystyle p^{*}(\tilde{r},\xi)

\displaystyle=p(\tilde{r},\xi,u^{*})

\displaystyle=(F\xi-\tilde{r})^{\top}(\frac{\rho}{2}I-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top})(F\xi-\tilde{r})

\displaystyle=:(F\xi-\tilde{r})^{\top}P(F\xi-\tilde{r}),

where we defined

P:=\frac{\rho}{2}I-\frac{\rho^{2}}{4}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}.

Note that we arrived at
p^{*}
from a partial minimization on
u
, which preserves the convexity of the problem on
r
. Thus, we have that

P\succ 0.

With the knowledge of
p^{*}
, we can now solve for
r
in closed-form.
Since
Q+P\succ 0
, we have that

\displaystyle r^{+}

\displaystyle=\text{argmin}_{r}r^{\top}Qr+p^{*}(r+\nu,\xi)

\displaystyle=\text{argmin}_{r}r^{\top}Qr+(F\xi-r-\nu)^{\top}P(F\xi-r-\nu)

\displaystyle=(Q+P)^{-1}P(F\xi-\nu).

Thus, overall, we update rules are given as

\displaystyle r^{+}

\displaystyle=(Q+P)^{-1}P(F\xi-\nu),

\displaystyle u^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\rho}{2}\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-r^{(k+1)}-\hat{\nu}^{(k)}),

\displaystyle x^{+}

\displaystyle=Eu^{(k+1)}+F\xi.

From the closed-form update rules specified above, we have that

\displaystyle r^{+}-x^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r^{+}-(E(-\frac{\rho}{2}\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-r^{(k+1)}-\hat{\nu}^{(k)}))+F\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top})r^{+}+\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-\nu)-F\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\frac{2}{\rho}P(Q+P)^{-1}P+\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top})\nu-(HF+F)\xi

Denote

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle:=-\frac{2}{\rho}P(Q+P)^{-1}P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle:=HF+F,

we get the expression that we desire.
From the fact that
P\succ 0
,
Q\succeq 0
, and
\bar{R}\succ 0
, it follows that
H\prec 0
.
∎

Now, recall that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
, the optimal dual map
\Theta^{*}
satisfies that
\nu^{*}=\Theta^{*}\xi
, where
\nu^{*}
is the optimal dual variable for the given
\xi
. From the KKT condition of (
2
), we know that
\nu^{*}
induces a fixed point to the update rules (
16
) and (
17
). Thus,

r^{+}-x^{+}=r^{*}-x^{*}=H\Theta^{*}\xi+G\xi=0.

Since this holds for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
, we have that

H\Theta^{*}+G=0.

Before starting with the main proof, we present the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For a set of i.i.d normal vectors
\xi_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-I\right\rVert_{F}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}.

Proof.

We begin by rewriting the above expression with a change of variables, where we define

X:=\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}.

Since
X
is a sum of outer products of independently distributed normal random vectors,
X
follows the Wishart distribution. Specifically, we have that

X\sim W_{d_{x}}(I,B).

The above expression can then be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-I\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X-BI\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left\lVert X-BI\right\rVert_{F}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\Tr\mathbb{E}(X-BI)(X-BI)^{\top}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\sqrt{\Tr\Var(X)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{B}\sqrt{2d_{x}B}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}},

where we first used Jensen’s inequality and then, in the following equalities, used the properties of Wishart random variables.
∎

We can now start analyzing the progress of the dual update. First, combining Lemma
3
with the dual update rule (
18
), we have that

\displaystyle\Theta^{(k+1)}

\displaystyle=\Theta^{(k)}+\eta\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}(H\Theta^{(k)}\xi_{i}+G\xi_{i})\xi_{i}^{\top}

(21)

\displaystyle=\Theta^{(k)}+\eta(H\Theta^{(k)}+G)\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}.

Thus, for the expected norm of interest, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k+1)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}+\eta(H\Theta^{(k)}+G)\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}+\eta(H\Theta^{(k)}+G)\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-\eta(H\Theta^{*}+G)\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}+\eta H(\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*})\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(I+\eta H)(\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*})+\eta H(\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*})(\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-I)\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-I\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}\right)\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert,

where in step
(a)
we used the above fact that
H\Theta^{*}+G=0
, and in the final step, we used Lemma
4
. We have the desired contraction if

\gamma(\eta,B):=\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}<1.

Note that choosing

\eta^{*}=\frac{2}{\sigma_{\text{max}}(H)+\sigma_{\text{min}}(H)}

minimizes the norm
\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert
. Solving for
B
with this choice of
\eta
, we have that
B
needs to satisfy that

B>\frac{2d_{x}\sigma_{\text{max}}^{2}(H)}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(H)^{2}}.

Following these appropriate choices of
\eta
and
B
, we have

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{k}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(0)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert.

Lemma 5
.

Consider the perturbations in

and
G

H^{\prime}=H+\Delta_{H},\quad G^{\prime}=G+\Delta_{G}.

If the perturbation in

\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert<\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B_{0}}}}\frac{\sigma_{\text{max}}(H)-\sigma_{\text{min}}(H)}{\sigma_{\text{max}}(H)+\sigma_{\text{min}}(H)},

for any

, then we can pick step size
\eta
and batch size
B
such that the update (
21

\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert\leq\gamma^{k}\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(0)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert+\frac{1-\gamma^{k}}{1-\gamma}e(\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert),

where

, and

e(\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert):=(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\left(\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Theta^{*}\right\rVert\right)

(22)

Proof.

We follow similar steps as when we showed the similar result for the unperturbed case.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k+1)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}+\eta((H+\Delta_{H})\Theta^{(k)}+(G+\Delta_{G}))\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\Bigg\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Bigg\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}+\eta(H\Theta^{(k)}+G+(\Delta_{H}\Theta^{(k)}+\Delta_{G}))\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}-\eta(H\Theta^{*}+G)\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\Bigg\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}+\eta H(\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*})\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert+\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\Delta_{H}\Theta^{(k)}+\Delta_{G}))\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}\right)\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert_{F}+\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\Delta_{H}\Theta^{(k)}+\Delta_{G}))\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert,

where the last step followed the same steps in the proof of Theorem
1
. We now proceed to bound the second term.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert(\Delta_{H}\Theta^{(k)}+\Delta_{G})\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}^{\top}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\Delta_{H}(\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*})+(\Delta_{G}+\Delta_{H}\Theta^{*}))\right\rVert\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\sum_{i=0}^{B}\frac{1}{B}\xi_{i}\xi_{i}\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\Big(\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Theta^{(k)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Theta^{*}\right\rVert\Big)

Combining this with the unperturbed result, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\lVert\Theta^{(k+1)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert_{F}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}+(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\right)\left\lVert\Theta^{(0)}-\Theta^{*}\right\rVert+e(\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert),

where

\displaystyle e(\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert,\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert):=(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\left(\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert+\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Theta^{*}\right\rVert\right)

For the iterations to be contractive, we would need that

\left(\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}+(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\right)<1.

Again, note that choosing

\eta^{*}=-\frac{2}{\lambda_{\text{max}}(H)+\lambda_{\text{min}}(H)}

minimizes the norm
\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert
. Thus, for the inequality to hold,
\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert
needs to satisfy

\displaystyle\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B_{0}}}\right)\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert<\left\lVert 1+\eta^{*}H\right\rVert=\frac{\lambda_{\text{max}}(H)-\lambda_{\text{min}}(H)}{\lambda_{\text{max}}(H)+\lambda_{\text{min}}(H)},

for some
B_{0}\geq 1
or equivalently

\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert<\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B_{0}}}}\frac{\lambda_{\text{max}}(H)-\lambda_{\text{min}}(H)}{\lambda_{\text{max}}(H)+\lambda_{\text{min}}(H)}.

We can then pick

B>\text{max}\left(\frac{2d_{x}\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}{(1-\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert)^{2}-(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B_{0}}})\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert},B_{0}\right),

so that

\gamma:=\left\lVert I+\eta H\right\rVert_{2}+\eta\left\lVert H\right\rVert_{2}\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}}+(1+\sqrt{\frac{2d_{x}}{B}})\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert<1.

The result then follows from telescoping the sum.
∎

We now consider the perturbations we described in Section

and bound the terms
\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert
in terms of the perturbations
\epsilon_{P},\epsilon_{u,r},\epsilon_{u,\xi}

Lemma 6
.

Consider the perturbations specified in (
19
) and (
20
). Denote the norms of the perturbations as

\epsilon_{P}=\left\lVert\Delta_{P}\right\rVert,\epsilon_{u,r}=\left\lVert\Delta_{u,r}\right\rVert,\epsilon_{u,\xi}=\left\lVert\Delta_{u,\xi}\right\rVert.

If
\epsilon_{P}<\frac{\lambda_{\text{min}}(Q+P)}{2}
, we have that
\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert
and
\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert
as

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert

\displaystyle<e_{H}(\epsilon_{P},\epsilon_{u,r}),

(23)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\epsilon\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(Q+P)}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(Q+P)}+\frac{2\epsilon(\left\lVert P\right\rVert+\epsilon)^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(Q+P)^{2}},

where
\epsilon=\text{max}(\epsilon_{P},\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert E\right\rVert\epsilon_{u,r})
.

\displaystyle\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert

\displaystyle<e_{G}(\epsilon_{P},\epsilon_{u,r},\epsilon_{u,\xi})

(24)

\displaystyle:=\left\lVert F\right\rVert e_{H}(\epsilon_{P},\epsilon_{u,r})+\epsilon_{u,\xi}.

Proof.

We first note that the perturbed update rule gives the updates

\displaystyle r^{+}=(Q+P+\Delta_{P})^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P})(r+\nu-F\xi),

\displaystyle\hat{u}(\tilde{r},\xi)=u^{*}(\tilde{r},\xi)+\Delta_{u,r}\tilde{r}+\Delta_{u,\xi}\xi.

The difference between
r^{+}
and
x^{+}
can be summarized as

\displaystyle r^{+}-x^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I-\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}-E\Delta_{u,r})r^{+}+\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-\nu)-(F+\Delta_{u,\xi})\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\rho}(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})r^{+}+\frac{\rho}{2}E\bar{R}^{-1}E^{\top}(F\xi-\nu)-(F+\Delta_{u,\xi})\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(H+\Delta_{H})\nu-(G+\Delta_{G})\xi

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{H}

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\rho}(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})(Q+P+\Delta_{P})^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P})-\frac{2}{\rho}P(Q+P)^{-1}P

\displaystyle\Delta_{G}

\displaystyle:=\Delta_{H}F+E\Delta_{u,\xi}.

We now proceed to bound
\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert
. Denote the reduced SVD of
\Delta_{P}
as

\Delta_{P}=UCV^{\top}.

For the sake of simplicity, we denote

. By Woodbury matrix identity, we have that

(A+UCV^{\top})^{-1}\\
=A^{-1}-A^{-1}U(C^{-1}+V^{\top}A^{-1}U)^{-1}V^{\top}A^{-1}.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})(Q+P+\Delta_{P})^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle PD^{-1}P+\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r}D^{-1}P+PD^{-1}\Delta_{P}-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r}D^{-1}\Delta_{P}+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})D^{-1}U(C^{-1}+V^{\top}D^{-1}U)^{-1}V^{\top}D^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P}).

The first term corresponds to the unperturbed

\epsilon=\text{max}(\epsilon_{P},\frac{\rho}{2}\left\lVert E\right\rVert\epsilon_{u,r}),

we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r}D^{-1}P\right\rVert\leq\frac{\rho\left\lVert E\Delta_{u,r}\right\rVert\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{2\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}\leq\frac{\epsilon\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)},

\displaystyle\left\lVert PD^{-1}\Delta_{P}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\left\lVert\Delta_{P}\right\rVert\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}\leq\frac{\epsilon\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)},

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r}D^{-1}\Delta_{P}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\rho\left\lVert E\Delta_{u,r}\right\rVert\left\lVert\Delta_{P}\right\rVert}{2\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}\leq\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}.

To bound the last term, we use the fact that

\displaystyle\Big\lVert(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})D^{-1}U(C^{-1}+V^{\top}D^{-1}U)^{-1}V^{\top}D^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P})\Big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\left\lVert P\right\rVert+\epsilon)^{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}\cdot\frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(C^{-1}+V^{\top}D^{-1}U)}

We invoke the reverse triangle inequality to get that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(C^{-1}+V^{\top}D^{-1}U)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{\text{min}}(C^{-1})-\left\lVert V^{\top}D^{-1}U\right\rVert}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\left\lVert C\right\rVert}-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{P}\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)-\epsilon_{P}}

From the assumption that
\epsilon_{P}<\frac{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}{2}
, we have that

\frac{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)-\epsilon_{P}}\leq 2.

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\Big\lVert(P-\frac{\rho}{2}E\Delta_{u,r})D^{-1}U(C^{-1}+V^{\top}D^{-1}U)^{-1}V^{\top}D^{-1}(P+\Delta_{P})\Big\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\epsilon(\left\lVert P\right\rVert+\epsilon)^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)^{2}}.

Thus, we overall have that

\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\leq\frac{2\epsilon\left\lVert P\right\rVert}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)}+\frac{2\epsilon(\left\lVert P\right\rVert+\epsilon)^{2}}{\lambda_{\text{min}}(D)^{2}}

and that

\left\lVert\Delta_{G}\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert\Delta_{H}\right\rVert\left\lVert F\right\rVert+\epsilon_{u,\xi}.

∎

Appendix D
Planning Only Subset of States

We consider the case where the state cost
\mathcal{C}
and constraints
\mathcal{X}
only require a subset of the states, i.e., if they are defined in terms of
z_{t}=g(x_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d_{z}}
, with
d_{z}<d_{x}
. Specifically, we consider the problem

\displaystyle\underset{x,u,z}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(z)+\mathcal{D}(u)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t=0,1,...,T-1,

\displaystyle z\in\mathcal{X},\quad u\in\mathcal{U},

\displaystyle x_{0}=\xi,

\displaystyle z_{t}=g(x_{t}).

In this case, one can modify the redundant constraint to be
r_{t}=z_{t}
to arrive at the following redundant problem

\displaystyle\underset{r,x,u}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{D}(u)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t=0,1,...,T-1,

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X},\quad u\in\mathcal{U},

\displaystyle x_{0}=\xi,\quad r=g(x),

where we wrote
g(x)
to denote
[g(x_{0}),g(x_{1}),...,g(x_{T})]^{\top}
with a slight abuse of notation. A similar derivation to that in Section
3
then arrives at the following iterative update

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \quad(r^{+},x^{+},u^{+})=\underset{r,x,u}{\text{argmin}}\;&\mathcal{C}(r)+\mathcal{D}(u)+\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert r+\nu-g(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}\\
\text{s.t.}\;&x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\quad\forall t,\\
&r\in\mathcal{X},u\in\mathcal{U},\\
&x_{0}=\xi\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\quad\nu^{+}=\nu+(r^{+}-x^{+})

and the nested optimization

\displaystyle r^{+}=\underset{r}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(r)+p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle r\in\mathcal{X}

where
p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)
is the locally optimal value of the
(x,u)
-minimization step

\displaystyle p^{*}(r+\nu;\xi)=\underset{x,u}{\text{min}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(u)+\frac{\rho}{2}\lVert r+\nu-g(x)\rVert_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle x_{t+1}=f(x_{t},u_{t}),\;u\in\mathcal{U},\;\forall t,

\displaystyle x_{0}=\xi.

Note that the only difference is that the trajectory planner only generates reference trajectories on the states
z
required for the state cost and constraints, and that the tracking cost for the lower-level controller also only concerns tracking those states.

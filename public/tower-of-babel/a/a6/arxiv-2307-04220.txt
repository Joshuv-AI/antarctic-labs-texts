All meromorphic traveling waves of cubic and quintic complex Ginzburg-Landau equations Journal: Physics letters A
Robert Conte, Micheline Musette, Tuen Wai Ng, Chengfa Wu

Abstract
For both cubic and quintic nonlinearities of the one-dimensional complex Ginzburg-Landau evolution equation, we prove by a theorem of Eremenko the finiteness of the number of traveling waves whose squared modulus has only poles in the complex plane, and we provide all their closed form expressions. Among these eleven solutions, five are provided by the method used. This allows us to complete the list of solutions previously obtained by other authors.

Introduction

In 1950 Ginzburg and Landau
[

introduced a description of superconductivity,
in the absence of an external magnetic field,
as a second order phase transition
in which the order parameter is a complex function

(which they denoted

deriving from a free energy quartic in

(“

This assumption naturally led them, in the one-dimensional case,
to an evolution equation invariant under a translation of the phase
\arg{A}
,
now known as the one-dimensional cubic complex Ginzburg-Landau equation CGL3

\displaystyle{\hskip-51.21495pt}\hbox{(CGL3) }iA_{t}+pA_{xx}+q|A|^{2}A-i\gamma A=0,p\gamma\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(q/p)\not=0,

This CGL3 equation later turned out to be a generic equation
arising from the approximation of a slowly varying amplitude,
with applications to quite various physical phenomena, such as
spatio-temporal turbulence,
Bose-Einstein condensation,
and more recently numerous fields of nonlinear optics,
as detailed in several reviews

[

[

[

[

[

[

CGL3 describes for instance the formation of patterns near a Hopf bifurcation,

When the bifurcation is subcritical,
the cubic term is insufficient to describe the system and
one must take account of the next nonlinearity compatible with
the phase invariance,
thus defining the complex quintic equation CGL5,

\displaystyle{\hskip-51.21495pt}\hbox{(CGL5) }iA_{t}+pA_{xx}+q|A|^{2}A+r|A|^{4}A-i\gamma A=0,pr\gamma\mathop{\rm Im}\nolimits(r/p)\not=0,

When they are traveling waves,

6
Conclusion and perspectives

In a previous version of the method
[
14
]
,
for each solution we performed the independent integration of two elliptic equations,
namely the ODE for
M
and the ODE for
(\log a)^{\prime}
,
with the result that the two elliptic functions
\wp
involved
had different invariants
g_{2},g_{3}
linked by a Landen transformation.
The present method avoids this useless complication.

Searching for additional meromorphic traveling waves of CGL
is now proven to be hopeless.

The eleven meromorphic traveling waves of CGL require between one and five constraints
on the parameters of the ODE,
while the local representation of
M(\xi)
by
a Laurent series near one of its movable singularities does not require
any constraint.
In order to fill this gap,
i.e. to build new closed form singlevalued traveling wave solutions,
necessarily nonmeromorphic,
able to remove at least some of these constraints,
the guidelines
indicated by Painlevé in his “Théorème général”

[
45
, pages 381–382]

and in his proof of the theorem of addition of Weierstrass

[
46
, §41 p 51]

would certainly be quite useful.

This could provide an analytic description of the
CGL3 homoclinic traveling hole,
observed in spatiotemporal intermittency
[
23
]

but never found analytically.

At the PDE level, only one closed form solution is known:
the CGL3 collision of two fronts
[
44
]
,

\displaystyle\hbox{(CGL3) }\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{A=A_{0}e^{-i\omega t}\left[\frac{k}{2}\sinh\frac{k}{2}x\right]\left[\cosh\frac{k}{2}x+e^{-(3/2)\gamma t}\right]^{-1+i\alpha},}\\
\displaystyle{c=0,p_{r}=0,k^{2}=-\frac{2\gamma}{p_{i}},\omega=-\frac{3\gamma}{2},}\end{array}\right.

which involves only one double pole of
M
.
It would be worth extending the present method to PDEs
and looking for solutions
M(x,t)
with two poles (CGL3)
or at most four poles (CGL5).

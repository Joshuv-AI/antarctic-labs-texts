Golden-Thompson via pinching inequality
Saket Choudhary saketkc@gmail.com

Abstract
For two hermitian matrices A A and B B , Golden-Thompson inequality [ 1 , 2 ] states that tr ⁡ [ exp ⁡ ( A + B ) ] ≤ tr ⁡ [ exp ⁡ ( A ) ​ exp ⁡ ( B ) ] \mathrm{tr}\left[\operatorname{exp}\left(A+B\right)\right]\leq\mathrm{tr}\left[\operatorname{exp}\left(A\right)\operatorname{exp}\left(B\right)\right] Multiple proofs exist for this inequality [ 3 ] . Though, most of them lack an intuitive argument. Sutter et al . [ 4 ] presented an intuitive proof using spectral pinching. We elaborate on their approach here.



1
Introduction

Given
A,B
hermitian matrices, Golden-Thompson inequality
[
1
,
2
]
states that:

\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(A+B\right)]\leq\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(A\right)\operatorname{exp}\left(B\right)]

It is trivial if
A,B
commute i.e.
AB=BA
. A quick example would be
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
and
B=\mathbb{I}_{2}
, while it does not hold for
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&2\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
B=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\
2&3\\
\end{pmatrix}
as
AB\neq BA
.
There have been multiple proofs
[
3
]
, but they lack an intuitive motivation. Sutter
et al
.
[
4
]
presented a more intuitive proof using spectral pinching. We elaborate on their approach here. There are no contributions in terms of novelty here and is completely expository. We believe it might be helpful for new learners in the field.

2
Spectral Pinching

Consider a square complex matrix
A
partitioned as a
r\times r
block matrix:
A=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&A_{12}&\cdots&A_{1r}\\
A_{21}&A_{22}&\cdots&A_{2r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{r1}&A_{r2}&\cdots&A_{rr}\\
\end{pmatrix}
.
We can decompose this matrix intro two matrices comprising the diagonal and the off-diagonal elements respectively,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=A_{D}+A_{\tilde{D}},

where,

\displaystyle A_{D}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}A_{11}&&&\\
&A_{22}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&A_{rr}\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle A_{\tilde{D}}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&A_{12}&\cdots&A_{1r}\\
A_{21}&0&\cdots&A_{2r}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{r1}&A_{r2}&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

A_{D}
is called a
pinching
of
A
. The simplest case is that of
A_{ij}
being 1 dimensional that we will be using here.

Any positive semi-definite matrix
A
can be written as
A=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}
where
\lambda_{i}
are
n
distinct eigen values of
A
.
P_{\lambda_{i}}
are orthogonal projectors such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{i}}=\mathbb{I}
and hence
P_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}=P_{\lambda_{i}}
. The
spectral pinching map
of
A
is then given by

\mathcal{P}_{A}:X\mapsto\sum_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}XP_{\lambda}.

The pinching map in turn has the following properties:

(i)

\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]A=A\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]
.

(ii)

\mathrm{tr}[\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]A]=\mathrm{tr}[XA]
.

(iii)

\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]\geq\frac{1}{n}X
.

Lemma 1
.

\mathcal{P}_{A}\left[X\right]A=A\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]
.

Proof:

Given that
P_{\lambda_{i}}^{2}=P_{\lambda_{i}}
and
P_{\lambda_{i}}\perp P_{\lambda_{j}}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{A}\left[X\right]A

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}P_{\lambda_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}P_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}P_{\lambda_{j}}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}P_{\lambda_{j}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}

\displaystyle=A\mathcal{P}_{A}[X].

∎

Lemma 2
.

\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]A\right]=\mathrm{tr}\left[XA\right]
.

Proof:

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{A}\left[X\right]A\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}\right].

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}\left[P_{\lambda_{i}}XP_{\lambda_{i}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}(P_{\lambda_{i}}X)_{jj}

\displaystyle\implies\mathrm{tr}\left[\mathcal{P}_{A}\left[X\right]A\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sum_{j}(P_{\lambda_{i}}X)_{jj}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\left[AX\right].

∎

Lemma 3
.

\mathcal{P}_{A}\left[X\right]\geq\frac{1}{n}X
.

Proof:

Proving this part is probably the trickiest among the four lemmata here, but is the entire key behind deducing the final Golden-Thompson inequality. Consider a unitary matrix
U_{y}
defined as
U_{y}=\sum_{u=1}^{n}e^{i2\pi yu/n}P_{\lambda_{u}}
. It is easy to verify that
U_{y}U_{y}^{*}=\mathbb{I}
as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{i}}=\mathbb{I}
. Also
U_{y}\succcurlyeq 0
and
U_{n}=\mathbb{I}

\displaystyle\sum_{y=1}^{n}U_{y}XU_{y}^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{y=1}^{n}\sum_{s=1}^{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}e^{i2\pi ys/n}P_{\lambda_{s}}XP_{\lambda_{t}}e^{-i2\pi yt/n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{s}}XP_{\lambda_{t}}\sum_{y=1}^{n}e^{i2\pi y(s-t)/n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{s}}XP_{\lambda_{t}}(n\mathbf{1}_{\{s=t\}})

\displaystyle=n\sum_{s=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{s}}XP_{\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle\implies\mathcal{P}_{A}[X]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{n}P_{\lambda_{s}}XP_{\lambda_{s}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{y=1}^{n}U_{y}XU_{y}^{*}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}X.

∎

Now, once Lemma 3 is proved, the rest of the steps for proving the Golden-Thompson inequality are straightforward.
For a semi-positive definite
d\times d
matrix
A
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

|\mathrm{spec}(A^{\bigotimes m})|\leq O(\mathrm{poly}(m))

Proof:

The number of eigen values for
A^{\bigotimes m}
is bounded by the number of the number of possible possible combinations of a sequence of
d
symbols (the maximum possible distinct eigen values of
A
) of length
m
which is given by
\binom{m+d-1}{d-1}\leq\frac{(m+d-1)^{d-1}}{(d-1)\,!}=O(\mathrm{poly}(m))
.

3

Proof of Golden-Thompson inequality

Given positive definite matrices
A,B
and using the facts that
\operatorname{exp}\left(\right)
and
\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\right)]
are operator monotone:

\displaystyle\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log A+\log B\right)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log A^{\bigotimes m}+\log B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]|\mathrm{spec}(A^{\bigotimes m})|+\log B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}

Using Lemma 3

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]+\log|\mathrm{spec}(A^{\bigotimes m})|+\log B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]+\log B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}+\frac{1}{m}|\mathrm{spec}(A^{\bigotimes m})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]+\log B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}+\frac{O(\mathrm{poly}(m))}{m}

Using Lemma 4

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]B^{\bigotimes m}\right)]}+\frac{O(\mathrm{poly}(m))}{m}

Using Lemma 1

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[\mathcal{P}_{B^{\bigotimes m}}[A^{\bigotimes m}]B^{\bigotimes m}]}+\frac{O(\mathrm{poly}(m))}{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\log{\mathrm{tr}[A^{\bigotimes m}B^{\bigotimes m}]}+\frac{O(\mathrm{poly}(m))}{m}

Using Lemma 2

\displaystyle=\log{\mathrm{tr}[AB]}+\frac{O(\mathrm{poly}(m))}{m}

\displaystyle\implies\log{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log A+\log B\right)]}

\displaystyle\leq\log{\mathrm{tr}(AB)}

\displaystyle\implies{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(\log A+\log B\right)]}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{tr}(AB)}

\displaystyle\implies{\mathrm{tr}[\operatorname{exp}\left(A+B\right)]}

\displaystyle\leq{\mathrm{tr}(\operatorname{exp}\left(A\right)\operatorname{exp}\left(B\right))}.

∎

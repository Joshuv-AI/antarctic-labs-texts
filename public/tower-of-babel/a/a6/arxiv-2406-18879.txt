A New Diophantine Approximation Inequality on Surfaces and Its Applications
KEPING HUANG, AARON LEVIN, AND ZHENG XIAO

Abstract
We prove a Diophantine approximation inequality for closed subschemes on surfaces which can be viewed as a joint generalization of recent inequalities of Ru-Vojta and Heier-Levin in this context. As applications, we study various Diophantine problems on affine surfaces given as the complement of three numerically parallel ample projective curves: inequalities involving greatest common divisors, degeneracy of integral points, and related Diophantine equations including families of S S -unit equations. We state analogous results in the complex analytic setting, where our main result is an inequality of Second Main Theorem type for surfaces, with applications to the study and value distribution theory of holomorphic curves in surfaces.



1
Introduction

Schmidt’s Subspace Theorem occupies a central place in the theory of Diophantine approximation, and it is known to have deep and far-reaching applications. Stated in the language of heights (and with improvements due to Schlickewei
[
Sch77
]
allowing for arbitrary finite sets of places), the Subspace Theorem may be stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Schmidt Subspace Theorem)
.

Let
S
be a finite set of places of a number field
K
. For each
v\in S
, let
H_{0,v},\ldots,H_{n,v}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be hyperplanes over
K
in general position. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a finite union of hyperplanes
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that for all points
P\in\mathbb{P}^{n}(K)\setminus Z
,

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\lambda_{H_{i,v},v}(P)<(n+1+\varepsilon)h(P).

Here,
\lambda_{H_{i,v},v}
is a local height function (also known as a local Weil function) associated to the hyperplane
H_{i,v}
and place
v
in
S
, and
h
is the standard (logarithmic) height on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

The Subspace Theorem has been generalized to the setting of hypersurfaces in projective space by Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ04a
]
, and more generally, by Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ04a
]
(for complete intersections) and Evertse and Ferretti
[
EF08
]
(for arbitrary projective varieties) to divisors which possess a common linearly equivalent multiple. Building on work of Autissier
[
Aut11
]
, Ru and Vojta
[
RV20
]
proved a general version of the Subspace Theorem in terms of beta constants (see Definition
3.1
). Their inequality was subsequently extended to the context of closed subschemes by Ru and Wang
[
RW22
]
and by Vojta
[
Voj23
]
(under slightly different intersection conditions). We state the following general form of the inequality, due to Vojta
[
Voj23
]
(in fact, Vojta proves a stronger version of Theorem
1.2
with the condition “intersect properly” (Definition
2.10
) replaced by “weakly intersect properly”
[
Voj23
, Definition 4.1(c)]
).

Theorem 1.2

(Ru-Vojta
[
RV20
]
, Ru-Wang
[
RW22
]
, Vojta
[
Voj23
]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
defined over a number field
K
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
. For each
v\in S
, let
Y_{0,v},\ldots,Y_{n,v}
be closed subschemes of
X
, defined over
K
, that intersect properly. Let
A
be a big divisor on
X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all points
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
,

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta(A,Y_{i,v})\lambda_{Y_{i,v},v}(P)<(1+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

Heier and the second author
[
HL21
]
proved an inequality using Seshadri constants (Definition
3.4
) in place of beta constants, and with the proper intersection condition of Theorem
1.2
replaced by a flexible notion of general position for closed subschemes (Definition
2.12
).

Theorem 1.3

(Heier-Levin
[
HL21
]
)
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
defined over a number field
K
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
. For each
v\in S
, let
Y_{0,v},\ldots,Y_{n,v}
be closed subschemes of
X
, defined over
K
, and in general position. Let
A
be an ample Cartier divisor on
X
, and
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all points
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
,

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\epsilon(A,Y_{i,v})\lambda_{Y_{i,v},v}(P)<(n+1+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

In both Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, one may also apply the results with fewer than
n+1
chosen closed subschemes at
v
(e.g., by appropriately arbitrarily choosing the remaining closed subschemes and using positivity of the associated local heights outside a closed subset); we will use this fact without further remark.

Before stating our main result, we give some further remarks comparing Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. For simplicity and to avoid technical issues, we assume now for the discussion that
X
is nonsingular of dimension
n
and we fix an ample divisor
A
on
X
. If the closed subschemes
Y_{i,v}=D_{i,v}
are all ample effective divisors, then by our nonsingularity assumption,
D_{0,v},\ldots,D_{n,v}
intersect properly if and only if they are in general position (Remark
2.13
). Furthermore, we note that if
D_{i,v}\sim d_{i,v}A
, then it follows easily from the asymptotic Riemann-Roch formula and the definitions that

\displaystyle\beta(A,D_{i,v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)d_{i,v}},

\displaystyle\epsilon(A,D_{i,v})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{i,v}}.

In this case, Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
coincide and yield the inequality (outside a Zariski-closed subset)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{D_{i,v},v}(P)}{d_{i,v}}<(n+1+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P),

which is precisely the inequality of the aforementioned theorem of Evertse and Ferretti.

Another interesting example comes from considering a single closed subscheme
Y
of
X
, which we assume additionally to be a local complete intersection (to satisfy the proper intersection hypothesis of Theorem
1.2
). In this case, if
\codim Y=r
, from
[
HL21
]
we have the inequality

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\frac{r}{n+1}\epsilon(A,Y),

and so Theorem
1.2
yields (choosing
Y_{0,v}=Y
for all
v
)

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\epsilon(A,Y)\lambda_{Y,v}(P)<\left(\frac{n+1}{r}+\varepsilon\right)h_{A}(P).

(1.1)

This is a generalization of an inequality of McKinnon and Roth
[
MR15
]
(who proved the case when
Y
is a point). On the other hand, in Theorem
1.3
one may take
Y_{0,v}=\cdots=Y_{r-1,v}=Y
for all
v
(i.e., repeat
Y
as the choice of closed subscheme
r
times), as is allowed by the used notion of general position (Definition
2.12
), and one again obtains the McKinnon-Roth type inequality (
1.1
). In short, there seems to be a tradeoff between the flexible notion of general position in Theorem
1.3
and the “better” beta constant of Theorem
1.2
(as compared to the Seshadri constant).

The goal of our main result is to provide, in the case of surfaces, an inequality which combines the separate advantages of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
; we prove an inequality in terms of beta constants of closed subschemes, under hypotheses which permit the use of nested closed subschemes (as allowed in Theorem
1.3
, but not in Theorem
1.2
).

Theorem 1.4

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a projective surface defined over a number field
K
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
. For each
v\in S
, let
D_{v}\supset Y_{v}
be a regular chain of nonempty closed subschemes of
X
(see Definition
2.14
). Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(A,D_{v})\lambda_{D_{v},v}(P)+\left(\beta(A,Y_{v})-\beta(A,D_{v})\right)\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)\bigg)<(1+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

(1.2)

In Section
4
, we show that our main result implies the Ru-Vojta inequality for surfaces, and Theorem
1.3
for surfaces under regularity assumptions. Thus, we may view Theorem
1.4
as a kind of joint generalization, for surfaces, of the inequalities of Ru-Vojta and of Heier and the second author.

We remark that the regular chain assumption is quite natural from the point of view of classical Diophantine approximation, where one is interested in sums of local heights associated to divisors. Indeed, if
D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}
are effective divisors in general position on a nonsingular projective surface
X
, all defined over a number field
K
,
S
is a set of places of
K
, and we fix a point
P\in X(K)
, then from general position a point can be
v
-adically close to at most two divisors
D_{i}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{D_{i},v}(P)=\sum_{v\in S}\left(\lambda_{D_{i_{v}},v}(P)+\lambda_{D_{j_{v}},v}(P)\right)+O(1),

for some choice of
i_{v},j_{v}\in\{1,\ldots,q\}
depending on
P
and
v
(but with the constant in the
O(1)
independent of
P
). Now assuming
\lambda_{D_{i_{v}},v}(P)\geq\lambda_{D_{j_{v}},v}(P)
(as we may), from a basic property of heights associated to closed subschemes (see Subsection
2.1
) we have (up to
O(1)
)

\displaystyle\lambda_{D_{i_{v}}\cap D_{j_{v}},v}(P)=\min\{\lambda_{D_{i_{v}},v}(P),\lambda_{D_{j_{v}},v}(P)\}=\lambda_{D_{j_{v}},v}(P).

Therefore, we may write

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\lambda_{D_{i},v}(P)=\sum_{v\in S}\left(\lambda_{D_{i_{v}},v}(P)+\lambda_{D_{i_{v}}\cap D_{j_{v}},v}(P)\right)+O(1),

(1.3)

where now
D_{i_{v}}\supset D_{i_{v}}\cap D_{j_{v}}
is a regular chain of closed subschemes of
X
for all
v\in S
. Although the inequality (
1.3
) is essentially elementary (relying at its base on Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz), we will find the underlying argument useful in a number of proofs and applications.

Among the potential applications of our main inequality (
1.2
), in Section
5
we study inequalities related to greatest common divisors, the Diophantine equation
f(a^{m},y)=b^{n}
, integral points on certain affine surfaces, and solutions to families of unit equations. For instance, we prove the following result for integral points on certain complements of three curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
be distinct irreducible projective curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, defined over a number field
K
, of degrees
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3}\neq\emptyset,

and for every point
Q\in(D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3})(\overline{K})
and
1\leq i<j\leq 3
,

\displaystyle(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q}<\frac{4}{9}d_{i}d_{j},

(1.4)

where
(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q}
denotes the local intersection multiplicity of
D_{i}
and
D_{j}
at
Q
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
containing all the archimedean places. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that for any set
R\subset X(K)
of
(D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3},S)
-integral points, the set
R\setminus Z
is finite.

When
d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}>1
and
D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3}
consists of a single point, at which the divisors intersect transversally, the result follows from work of Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ06
]
(see Theorem
5.8
). The factor
\frac{4}{9}
in the intersection condition (
1.4
) of Theorem
1.5
cannot be replaced by anything larger than
\frac{1}{2}
(Example
5.11
), and in fact a more general version of the theorem stated in terms of beta constants (Theorem
5.9
) can be shown to be sharp (Example
5.12
). We note that in general it seems to be a difficult problem to prove the degeneracy of integral points on the complement of three curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. For instance, if
D=D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3}
is a normal crossings divisor of degree
\deg D\geq 4
with
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
geometrically integral curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
(all defined over some number field
K
), then a suitable version of Vojta’s conjecture predicts the same conclusion as in Theorem
1.5
, but this does not appear to be known (for all
K
and
S
) for even a single such divisor
D
.

For a further application, by applying a general form of the above theorem (Theorem
5.9
) to certain surfaces, we study families of unit equations of the form:

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v=f_{3}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*},

where
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
are polynomials in
K[t]
, and
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
is the group of
S
-units of a ring of
S
-integers
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
of a number field
K
. This equation was treated in the case
\deg f_{1}=\deg f_{2}=\deg f_{3}
by Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ06
,
CZ10
]
, and in the case
\deg f_{1}+\deg f_{2}=\deg f_{3}
by the second author
[
Lev06
]
. We prove a result whenever the degrees of the polynomials are roughly within a factor of
2
of each other.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in K[t]
be nonconstant polynomials without a common zero of degrees
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
, respectively, and suppose that

\displaystyle\max_{i}(d_{i}+1)<\frac{9}{5}\min_{i}d_{i}.

Then the set of solutions
(t,u,v)\in\mathbb{A}^{3}(K)
of the equation

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v=f_{3}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*},

is contained in a finite number of rational curves in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
.

Excluding the linear case (proved in
[
CZ06
]
), this recovers Corvaja-Zannier’s result
[
CZ10
]
(i.e., the case
d_{1}=d_{2}=d_{3}
).

Finally, we mention that via Vojta’s dictionary
[
Voj87
, Ch. 3]
between Diophantine approximation and Nevanlinna theory, by substituting Vojta’s version
[
Voj97
]
of Cartan’s Second Main Theorem in place of Schmidt’s Subspace Theorem, one can prove a result analogous to Theorem
1.4
, giving the following inequality in the vein of the Second Main Theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a complex projective surface. Let
q
be a positive integer and let
D_{i}\supset Y_{i}
be a regular chain of nonempty closed subschemes of
X
for
i=1,\ldots,q
. Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset

such that for all holomorphic maps

whose image is not contained in

, the inequality

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\max_{i}\bigg(\beta(A,D_{i})\lambda_{D_{i}}(f(re^{i\theta}))+(\beta(A,Y_{i})-\beta(A,D_{i}))\lambda_{Y_{i}}(f(re^{i\theta}))\bigg)

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}

\displaystyle<(1+\varepsilon)T_{f,A}(r).

holds for all
r\in(0,\infty)
outside of a set of finite Lebesgue measure.

One may similarly apply Theorem
1.7
to obtain results on holomorphic curves. For instance, using Vojta’s dictionary, one may use Theorem
1.7
to prove the following analogue of Theorem
1.5
for holomorphic curves:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
be distinct irreducible complex projective curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, of degrees
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3}\neq\emptyset,

and for every point
Q\in(D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3})(\mathbb{C})
and
1\leq i<j\leq 3
,

\displaystyle(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q}<\frac{4}{9}d_{i}d_{j},

where
(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q}
denotes the local intersection multiplicity of
D_{i}
and
D_{j}
at
Q
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that every nonconstant holomorphic map
f:\mathbb{C}\to X\setminus(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3})
has image contained in

.

As the proofs of these analogous results in the complex setting are similar to the proofs of their arithmetic counterparts (after making the appropriate “translations”), we omit the details.

The organization of this paper is as follows. Section
2
gives relevant background material, including a summary of Silverman’s theory of heights associated to closed subschemes, and some needed results in commutative algebra, algebraic geometry, and Diophantine approximation. In Section
3
we give some basic inequalities involving beta constants, and then in Section
4
we prove our main theorem and show how it implies, for surfaces, versions of the inequality of Ru-Vojta and the inequality of Heier and the second author. Finally, in Section
5
we give some illustrative Diophantine applications of our results.

2
Background

2.1
Heights Associated to Closed Subschemes

In
[
Sil87
]
, Silverman generalized the Weil height machine for Cartier divisors to height functions on projective varieties with respect to closed subschemes.
More precisely, let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
K
, and let
Z(X)
denote the set of closed subschemes of
X
. Let
M_{K}
be the set of places of
K
.
Note that the closed subschemes
Y\in Z(X)
are in one-to-one correspondence with quasi-coherent ideal sheaves

\mathcal{I}_{Y}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and we identify a closed subscheme
Y
with its ideal
sheaf
\mathcal{I}_{Y}
. Generalizing the Weil height machine for Cartier divisors, Silverman assigned to each
Y\in Z(X)
and each place
v\in M_{K}
a local height function
\lambda_{Y,v}
,
and to each
Y\in Z(X)
a global height function
h_{Y}=\sum_{v\in M_{K}}\lambda_{Y,v}
(both uniquely determined up to a bounded function). We now summarize some of the basic properties of height functions associated to closed subschemes.

Theorem 2.1
.

(
[
Sil87
]
)
Let
X
be a projective variety over a number field
K
. Let
Z(X)
be the set of closed subschemes of
X
. There are maps

\displaystyle Z(X)\times M_{K}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\{\text{functions }X(K)\rightarrow[0,+\infty]\},

\displaystyle(Y,v)

\displaystyle\mapsto\lambda_{Y,v},

\displaystyle Z(X)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\{\text{functions }X(K)\rightarrow[0,+\infty]\},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{Y},

satisfying the following properties (we also write
\lambda_{X,Y,v}
and
h_{X,Y}
for clarity in (
6
)):

1.

If
D\in Z(X)
is an effective Cartier divisor, then
\lambda_{D,v}
and
h_{D}
agree with the classical height functions associated to
D
.

2.

If
W,Y\in Z(X)
satisfy
W\subseteq Y
, then
h_{W}\leq h_{Y}+O(1)
and
\lambda_{W,v}\leq\lambda_{Y,v}+O(1)
for all
v\in M_{K}
.

3.

If
W,Y\in Z(X)
satisfy
\mathrm{Supp}(W)\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(Y)
,
then there exists a constant
C

such that
h_{W}\leq C\cdot h_{Y}+O(1)
and
\lambda_{W,v}\leq C\cdot\lambda_{Y,v}+O(1)
for all
v\in M_{K}
.

4.

For all
W,Y\in Z(X)
,

\lambda_{W\cap Y,v}=\min\{\lambda_{W,v},\lambda_{Y,v}\}+O(1).

5.

For all
W,Y\in Z(X)
, we have
h_{W+Y}=h_{W}+h_{Y}+O(1)
and
\lambda_{W+Y,v}=\lambda_{W,v}+\lambda_{Y,v}+O(1)
for all
v\in M_{K}
.

6.

Let
\phi:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
be a morphism of projective varieties over
K
, and let
Y\in Z(X)
. Then

\displaystyle h_{X^{\prime},\phi^{*}Y}

\displaystyle=h_{X,Y}\circ\phi+O(1),

\displaystyle\lambda_{X^{\prime},\phi^{*}Y,v}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{X,Y,v}\circ\phi+O(1),

for all
v\in M_{K}
.

7.

If
D
and
E
are numerically equivalent Cartier divisors on
X
and
A
is an ample divisor on
X
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle|h_{D}(P)-h_{E}(P)|<\varepsilon h_{A}(P)+O(1)

for all
P\in X(K)
.
[
Voj87
, Proposition 1.2.9(d)]
.

Here,
Y\subset Z
,
Y+Z
, and

are all defined in terms of the associated ideal sheaves (see
[
Sil87
]
). For a closed subscheme
Y
and finite set of places of
S
of
K
, we let
m_{Y,S}(P)=\sum_{v\in S}\lambda_{Y,v}(P)
. For Cartier divisors
D
and
E
on a variety

, we will also write
D\geq E
(or

) if

Remark 2.2
.

If
Y
is a zero-dimensional closed subscheme of a projective variety

, then we can write

\displaystyle Y=Y_{Q_{1}}+\cdots+Y_{Q_{r}},

where

Since we also have

, for any point

there exists

(depending on

) such that

\displaystyle\lambda_{Y,v}(P)=\lambda_{Y_{Q_{j}},v}(P)+O(1),

.

If

We also recall the notion of a set of

]

Definition 2.4
.

Let

2.2
Multiplicities, Regular Sequences, and the Filtration Lemma

In this section, we collect together some useful facts involving multiplicities and regular sequences, and state the Filtration Lemma which will play a basic role in many of the proofs.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring and let
\mathfrak{q}
be a parameter ideal for
R
. Let
L_{\mathfrak{q},R}(n)=\length(R/\mathfrak{q}^{n})
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
Eis95
, Section 12.4]
)
.

For
n\gg 0
,
L_{\mathfrak{q},R}(n)
agrees with a polynomial of degree
\dim R
in
n
.

The leading term of any polynomial of degree
d
in
\mathbb{Q}[n]
which takes only integer values for
n\gg 0
is of the form
cn^{d}/d!
for some integer
c
. Then we have the following notions of multiplicity going back to Hilbert and Samuel.

Definition 2.6

(
[
Eis95
]
)
.

We define the
multiplicity
of
\mathfrak{q}
in
R
to be the integer
e=e(\mathfrak{q},R)
such that
|L_{\mathfrak{q},R}(n)-en^{\dim R}/(\dim R)!|=O(n^{\dim R-1}).

Definition 2.7

(
[
Ful89
, Ex. 4.3.4]
)
.

Let
Z
be a zero-dimensional closed subscheme of a projective variety
X
with ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
and let
P\in\Supp Z
. We define the
multiplicity
of
Z
at
P
to be
e(\mathcal{I}_{P},\mathcal{O}_{X,P})
, and denote it by
\mu_{P}(Z)
or simply
\mu_{P}
if the context is clear.

We also need some facts about certain ideal quotients.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
I
and
J
be ideals in
R
. The ideal quotient (or colon ideal)
(I:J)
is defined by

\displaystyle(I:J)=\{r\in R\mid rJ\subset I\}.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
I=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
be an ideal of a ring
R
generated by a regular sequence
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
in
R
. Then for any positive integers
n\geq i>0
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we have

\displaystyle(I^{n}:(f_{j})^{i})=I^{n-i}.

Proof.

Since
f_{j}^{i}\in I^{i}
,
I^{n-i}\subset(I^{n}:(f_{j})^{i})
is clear.

For the other inclusion, suppose that
af_{j}^{i}\in I^{n}
. Consider the associated graded ring
G_{I}(R):=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}I^{r}/I^{r+1}
. By
[
Mat80
, Theorem 27]
,
G_{I}(R)
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
(R/I)[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
, where the indeterminates
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
correspond to the images of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
in the first graded piece of
G_{I}(R)
.

Suppose
a\in I^{\ell}\setminus I^{\ell+1}
, so that
0\neq\bar{a}\in I^{\ell}/I^{\ell+1}
. Since
x_{j}
is not a zero divisor in
(R/I)[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
, we have
0\neq\overline{af_{j}^{i}}\in I^{\ell+i}/I^{\ell+i+1}
. Therefore
af_{j}^{i}\notin I^{\ell+i+1}
and it follows that
\ell+i+1\geq n+1
.
Hence
\ell\geq n-i
.
∎

We now make definitions involving proper intersections, general position, and regular sequences, in the context of closed subschemes.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
be effective Cartier divisors on a projective variety
X
. We say that
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
intersect properly if for every nonempty subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,r\}
and every point
P\in\Supp\cap_{i\in I}D_{i}
, the sequence
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
is a regular sequence in the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,P}
, where
f_{i}
locally defines
D_{i}
at
P
,
i=1,\ldots,r
. More generally, the definition can be extended to closed subschemes
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{r}
of
X
(see
[
RW22
]
or
[
Voj23
]
).

Remark 2.11
.

If
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are Cartier divisors on
X
that intersect properly, then
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{1}-D_{2})=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{1})\cap\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D_{2})
(see
[
Voj23
, §3, §4]
for a vast abstraction of this property).

Definition 2.12
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
. We say that closed subschemes
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{q}
of
X
are in general position if for every subset
I\subset\{1,\ldots,q\}
with
|I|\leq n+1
we have
\emph{codim}\cap_{i\in I}Y_{i}\geq|I|
, where we use the convention that
\dim\emptyset=-1
.

Remark 2.13
.

If the Cartier divisors
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
on
X
intersect properly, then they are in general position. If
X
is Cohen-Macaulay then the converse holds
[
Mat80
, Theorem 17.4]
. In particular, if
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a hypersurface,
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
are effective divisors on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, and
X,D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
are in general position (on
\mathbb{P}^{n}
), then
X,D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
intersect properly, and from the definitions it follows that
D_{1}|_{X},\ldots,D_{r}|_{X}
intersect properly, as divisors on
X
.

We make a related definition for a sequence of nested closed subschemes.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
Y_{1}\supset Y_{2}\supset\cdots\supset Y_{r}
be closed subschemes of a projective variety
X
. We say that
Y_{1}\supset\cdots\supset Y_{r}
is a regular chain of closed subschemes of
X
if for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
and every point
P\in\Supp Y_{i}
, there is a regular sequence
f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}_{X,P}
such that for
1\leq j\leq i
, the ideal sheaf of
Y_{j}
is locally given by the ideal
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{j})
in
\mathcal{O}_{X,P}
.

Remark 2.15
.

If
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
intersect properly on
X
, then
D_{1}\supset D_{1}\cap D_{2}\supset\cdots\supset D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap\cdots\cap D_{r}
is a regular chain of closed subschemes of
X
.

We will use the following consequence of Lemma
2.9
.

Definition 2.16
.

For an effective Cartier divisor
D
and closed subscheme
Y
of
X
, let
n_{Y}(D)
be the largest nonnegative integer such that
n_{Y}(D)Y\subset D
, as closed subschemes.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}
be effective Cartier divisors on a surface
X
and let
Y
be a closed subscheme supported at a point
P
. Suppose that
mD\supset Y
is a regular chain of closed subschemes for some positive integer
m
. Then for any positive integer
i
,

\displaystyle|n_{Y}(iD)+n_{Y}(D^{\prime})-n_{Y}(iD+D^{\prime})|\leq 1,

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle n_{Y}(iD)=\lfloor i/m\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}
be the ideal sheaf of
Y
and let
\mathcal{I}_{P}\subset\mathcal{O}_{P}
be the localization. Let
n=n_{Y}(iD+D^{\prime})
. Suppose that
D
and
D^{\prime}
are represented by
f
and
f^{\prime}
locally at
P
. Then since
nY\subset iD+D^{\prime}
, we have
f^{i}f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{n}
. It follows that
(f^{m})^{\lceil i/m\rceil}f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{n}
and, since
mD\supset Y
is a regular chain of closed subschemes, by Lemma
2.9
,
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{n-\lceil i/m\rceil}
. On the other hand, since
f^{i}=(f^{m})^{\lfloor i/m\rfloor}f^{i-m\lfloor i/m\rfloor}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{\lfloor i/m\rfloor}
, if
f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{n-\lfloor i/m\rfloor+1}
then
f^{i}f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}_{P}^{n+1}
, contradicting the definition of
n
. It follows that

\displaystyle n-\lceil i/m\rceil\leq n_{Y}(D^{\prime})\leq n-\lfloor i/m\rfloor.

The inequality (
2.1
) (and the remainder of the lemma) now follows if we show that
n_{Y}(iD)=\lfloor i/m\rfloor
. If
i
divides
m
and
iD=\frac{i}{m}(mD)
then it follows from Lemma
2.9
that
n_{Y}(iD)=i/m
. Since
\lfloor i/m\rfloor(mD)\subset iD\subset(\lfloor i/m\rfloor+1)(mD)
, we have
\lfloor i/m\rfloor\leq n_{Y}(iD)\leq\lfloor i/m\rfloor+1
. But if
n_{Y}(iD)=\lfloor i/m\rfloor+1
, then

\displaystyle n_{Y}(miD)\geq mn_{Y}(iD)>i,

contradicting
n_{Y}(miD)=i
. Therefore we must have
n_{Y}(iD)=\lfloor i/m\rfloor
as claimed.
∎

Finally, we recall the elementary, but very useful, Filtration Lemma whose utility in Diophantine approximation was recognized and introduced by Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ04b
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 2.18

(Filtration Lemma)
.

Let
V
be a vector space of finite dimension
d
over a field
K
. Let
V=W_{1}\supset W_{2}\supset\cdots\supset W_{h}
and
V=W_{1}^{*}\supset W_{2}^{*}\supset\cdots\supset W_{h^{*}}^{*}
be two filtrations on
V
. There exists a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
of
V
that contains a basis of each
W_{j}
and
W_{j}^{*}
.

In the situation of Lemma
2.18
, we say that the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}
of
V
is adapted to the two filtrations.

3
Beta constant estimates

Throughout this section we work over a field
K
of characteristic
0
. For a line bundle
\mathscr{L}
on a projective variety
X
over
K
, we will write
h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L})
(or simply
h^{0}(\mathscr{L})
) for
\dim_{K}H^{0}(X,\mathscr{L})
, and if
D
is a Cartier divisor on
X
, we write
h^{0}(D)
for
h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(D))
. We first recall the definition of the beta constant.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a field
K
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line bundle on
X
and let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
with associated sheaf of ideals
\mathcal{I}
. Then

\displaystyle\beta(\mathscr{L},Y)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{N}\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})}{Nh^{0}(X,\mathscr{L}^{N})},

where
\mathscr{L}^{N}
denotes the tensor product of
N
copies of
\mathscr{L}
.

By a result of Vojta
[
Voj20
]
(assuming as we do that
K
has characteristic
0
) the
\liminf
in the definition can be replaced by a limit. By abuse of notation, if
D
is a big Cartier divisor on
X
, we also write
\beta(D,Y)
for
\beta(\mathcal{O}(D),Y)
.

It follows easily that for any positive integer
n
,

\displaystyle\beta(nD,Y)

\displaystyle=n\beta(D,Y),

\displaystyle\beta(D,nY)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\beta(D,Y).

In particular, we can use these properties to canonically extend the definition of
\beta(D,E)
to
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
D
and
E
(with
D
big). We also show that
\beta(D,E)
depends only on the numerical equivalence class of
D
and
E
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
D,D^{\prime},E,E^{\prime}
be nonzero effective Cartier divisors on a projective variety
X
over
K
with
D
and
D^{\prime}
big. Suppose that
D\equiv D^{\prime}
and
E\equiv E^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\beta(D,E)=\beta(D^{\prime},E^{\prime}).

Proof.

We prove that
\beta(D,E)=\beta(D,E^{\prime})
(the proof that
\beta(D,E)=\beta(D^{\prime},E)
being similar). Let
n=\dim X
. Since
E\equiv E^{\prime}
and
D
is big, for any positive rational
\varepsilon
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
E-E^{\prime}+\varepsilon D
is big. It follows that for a sufficiently large positive integer
N
,
h^{0}(N(E-E^{\prime}+\varepsilon D))>0
, and so
E+\varepsilon D\sim E^{\prime}+F
for some
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
F
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\beta(D,E^{\prime})\geq\beta(D,E^{\prime}+F)=\beta(D,E+\varepsilon D).

Fix an ample divisor
A
on
X
and let
c>\frac{A^{n-1}.D}{A^{n-1}.E}
be a positive integer. Then
(ND-NmE).A^{n-1}<0
for all
m\geq c
, and so
h^{0}(ND-NmE)=0
for all
m\geq c
. Let
M
be a positive integer such that
M(E+\varepsilon D)
is an integral Cartier divisor and let
N
be a positive integer such that
M|N
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(ND-mM(E+\varepsilon D))

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{cN/M}h^{0}(ND-mM(E+\varepsilon D))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=1}^{cN/M}h^{0}((N-c\varepsilon N)D-mME)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(N(1-c\varepsilon)D-mME),

and so

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(ND-mM(E+\varepsilon D))}{Nh^{0}(ND)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(N(1-c\varepsilon)D-mME)}{Nh^{0}(N(1-c\varepsilon)D)}\frac{h^{0}(N(1-c\varepsilon)D)}{h^{0}(ND)}.

Taking limits (see Definition
3.6
and Remark
3.7
) gives

\displaystyle\beta(D,E+\varepsilon D)=M\beta(D,M(E+\varepsilon D))

\displaystyle\geq M\beta((1-c\varepsilon)D,ME)\frac{\vol((1-c\varepsilon)D)}{\vol(D)}

\displaystyle\geq(1-c\varepsilon)^{n+1}\beta(D,E).

Thus,

\displaystyle\beta(D,E^{\prime})\geq(1-c\varepsilon)^{n+1}\beta(D,E).

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, we find that
\beta(D,E^{\prime})\geq\beta(D,E)
. Interchanging the roles of
E
and
E^{\prime}
yields that
\beta(D,E)=\beta(D,E^{\prime})

∎

We now prove some useful inequalities involving the beta constant.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of a projective variety
X
. Let
D,E
be effective Cartier divisors on
X
intersecting properly and suppose that
mY\subset D
and
nY\subset E
(as closed subschemes). Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
. Then

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq m\beta(A,D)+n\beta(A,E).

In particular,

\displaystyle\beta(A,D\cap E)\geq\beta(A,D)+\beta(A,E).

Proof.

We first note that if
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a regular sequence, then for any positive integers
i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}
,
(x_{1}^{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{n}^{i_{n}})
is again a regular sequence
[
Mat80
, Theorem 26]
. This implies that if
i
and
j
are positive integers, then
iD
and
iE
intersect properly and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-iD)\cap\mathcal{O}_{X}(-jE)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-iD-jE)
.

Let
N>0
. We consider the two filtrations on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
given by order of vanishing along
D
and
E
, respectively:

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-D))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-2D))\supset\cdots

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-E))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-2E))\supset\cdots.

Let
s_{1},\dots,s_{h^{0}(NA)}
be a basis of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
that is adapted to both filtrations. Let
\mathcal{I}
denote the sheaf of ideals associated to
Y
.

For a nonzero section
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
, let
\mu_{D}(s),\mu_{E}(s)
, and
\mu_{Y}(s)
, respectively, be the largest nonnegative integer
\mu
such that
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-\mu D)),s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-\mu E))
, and
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{\mu})
, respectively. Since
s_{1},\dots,s_{h^{0}(NA)}
is adapted to the filtrations above, one easily finds

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA-jD))

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mu_{D}(s_{l}),

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA-jE))

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mu_{E}(s_{l}).

We also have the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{j})\geq\sum_{l=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mu_{Y}(s_{l}).

Since
D
and
E
intersect properly, if
s_{l}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-iD))
and
s_{l}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-jE))
, then
s_{l}\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-iD-jE))\subset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{im+jn})
. Therefore,
\mu_{Y}(s_{l})\geq m\mu_{D}(s_{l})+n\mu_{E}(s_{l})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{j})\geq m\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA-iD))+n\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA-jE)).

Dividing by
Nh^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA))
and taking a limit then gives the result.
∎

Before stating and proving the next inequality, we recall the definition of the Seshadri constant.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
X
be a projective variety over a field
K
. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
and
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blowing-up along
Y
. Let
A
be a nef Cartier divisor on
X
. Define the Seshadri constant
\epsilon(A,Y)
of
Y
with respect to
A
to be the real number

\displaystyle\epsilon(A,Y)=\sup\{\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}\ |\ \pi^{*}A-\gamma E\text{ is }\mathbb{Q}\text{-nef}\},

where
E
is an effective Cartier divisor on
\tilde{X}
whose associated invertible sheaf is the dual of
\pi^{-1}\mathcal{I}_{Y}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety of dimension
r
. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
with
\codim Y\geq 2
, let
D\supset Y
be an effective Cartier divisor, and let
A
be an ample Cartier divisor on
X
. Then

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\beta(A,D)+\frac{1}{r+1}\epsilon(A,Y).

Proof.

Let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blowup of
X
along
Y
, and let
E
be the associated exceptional divisor. Let
\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}\leq\epsilon(A,Y)
be a positive rational number and let
\delta>0
be a rational number. By the same proof as in
[
HL21
]
, for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
(depending on
\delta
) and sufficiently large and divisible
N
, we can find an effective divisor
F
such that

1.

N(1+\delta)A\sim F

2.

\pi^{*}F\geq N(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)E

3.

D
and
F
are in general position.

As
X
is normal, the last condition and Serre’s criterion imply that
D
and
F
intersect properly. For all sufficiently large and divisible
N
,
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}(-N(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)E)=\mathcal{I}_{Y}^{N(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)}

[
HL21
]
and since
X
is normal and
\pi
is birational, we have
\pi_{*}\pi^{*}F=F

[
Voj23
, Lemma 6.2(a)]
. Then
\beta(A,F)=\frac{1}{(r+1)N(1+\delta)}
,
N(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)Y\subset F
(for sufficiently large and divisible
N
),
Y\subset D
, and Lemma
3.3
gives

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\beta(A,D)+N(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{(r+1)N(1+\delta)}=\beta(A,D)+(\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}+\varepsilon)\frac{1}{(r+1)(1+\delta)}.

Since we may choose
\varepsilon,\delta
, and
\epsilon_{Y}^{\prime}-\epsilon(A,Y)
arbitrarily small, we find that

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\beta(A,D)+\frac{1}{r+1}\epsilon(A,Y)

as desired.
∎

When
Y
is a closed subscheme supported at a point
P
, we will relate
\beta(A,Y)
,
\mu_{P}(Y)
, and the volume of
A
. We first recall the definition of the volume of a divisor.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
X
be a projective variety of dimension
n
, and let
D
be a Cartier divisor on
X
. The
volume of
D
is defined to be the non-negative real number

\displaystyle\emph{vol}(D)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(X,mD)}{m^{n}/n!}.

Remark 3.7
.

The
\limsup
in Definition
3.6
can be replaced by a limit
[
Laz04
, Remark 2.2.50]
, and the definition can be extended to
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
[
Laz04
, Remark 2.2.39]
.

Remark 3.8
.

We have
\mathrm{vol}(D)>0
if and only if
D
is big.

Remark 3.9
.

If
D
is nef, then
\emph{vol}(D)=D^{n}
is the top self-intersection of
D
.

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on a projective variety
X
of dimension
r
. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
supported at a point
P
of
X
, let
\mathcal{I}
be the associated ideal sheaf, and let
\mu_{P}
be the multiplicity of
Y
at
P
.
Then

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})\geq h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA))-\mu_{P}\frac{m^{r}}{r!}+O(m^{r-1}).

(3.1)

In particular, let
c\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that

\displaystyle 0<c<\sqrt[r]{\frac{\emph{vol}(A)}{\mu_{P}}}.

Then for
n\gg 0
there exists an effective divisor
F
such that

1.

F\sim nA
,

2.

\lfloor cn\rfloor Y\subset F
(as closed subschemes).

Proof.

From the definition of the multiplicity
\mu_{P}
, we have

\displaystyle\dim\mathcal{O}_{P}/\mathcal{I}_{P}^{m}=\mu_{P}\frac{m^{r}}{r!}+O(m^{r-1}).

(3.2)

By definition of the ideal sheaf, we have an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{I}^{m}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}\to 0,

where
i:mY\to X
is the inclusion map. Tensoring with
\mathcal{O}(nA)
, we have an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m}\to\mathcal{O}(nA)\to\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}=i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}\to 0,

where the equality
\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}=i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}
follows easily from the fact that
\mathcal{O}(nA)
is locally free of rank
1
and
i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY}
is supported at a point. Taking global sections, we find

\displaystyle 0\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA))

\displaystyle\to H^{0}(X,i_{*}\mathcal{O}_{mY})

\displaystyle=H^{0}(mY,\mathcal{O}_{mY})=\mathcal{O}_{P}/\mathcal{I}_{P}^{m}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})\geq\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(nA))-\dim\mathcal{O}_{P}/\mathcal{I}_{P}^{m},

and then (
3.1
) follows from (
3.2
).

From (
3.1
), the definition of the volume, and Remark
3.7
, it follows that if
c<\sqrt[r]{\frac{\vol(A)}{\mu_{P}}}
, then there exists a nonzero global section
s
of
\mathcal{O}(nA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{\lfloor cn\rfloor}
for
n\gg 0
, and taking
F=\dv(s)
gives the desired divisor.
∎

We now prove a basic inequality between
\beta(A,Y)
,
\mu_{P}(Y)
, and
\vol(A)
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on a projective variety
X
of dimension
r
. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
supported at a point
P
of
X
, and let
\mu_{P}
be the multiplicity of
Y
at
P
.
Then

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\frac{r}{r+1}\sqrt[r]{\frac{\vol(A)}{\mu_{P}}}.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that

\displaystyle 0<c<\sqrt[r]{\frac{\vol(A)}{\mu_{P}}}.

Let
N
be a positive integer such that
cN
is an integer. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the sheaf of ideals associated to
Y
. Then we calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{cN}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=1}^{cN}\left(h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA))-\mu_{p}\frac{m^{r}}{r!}+O(N^{r-1})\right)

\displaystyle\geq cNh^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA))-\mu_{P}\frac{(cN)^{r+1}}{(r+1)!}+O(N^{r}).

Dividing by
Nh^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA))
we find that

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{cN}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}^{m})}{Nh^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA))}

\displaystyle\geq c-\mu_{P}\frac{c^{r+1}N^{r}}{(r+1)!h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)}+O\left(\frac{N^{r-1}}{h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)}\right).

Letting
N\to\infty
gives

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\frac{c}{(r+1)\vol(A)}\left((r+1)\vol(A)-\mu_{P}c^{r}\right).

Since this is true for all appropriate values of
c
, letting
c\to\sqrt[r]{\frac{\vol(A)}{\mu_{P}}}
gives the inequality.
∎

Under more hypotheses, we give another estimate involving
\beta(A,Y)
,
\mu_{P}(Y)
, and
\vol(A)
. Towards this end, we first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
A
and
D
be big Cartier divisors on a normal projective surface
X
, with
D
a positive multiple of a prime Cartier divisor. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
supported at a point
P
, and let
\mu_{P}
be the multiplicity of
Y
at
P
. Suppose that
D\supset Y
is a regular chain of closed subschemes of
X
and

\displaystyle\mu_{P}<\vol(D).

Let
c\in\mathbb{Q}
be such that

\displaystyle c<\sqrt{\frac{\vol(A)}{\mu_{P}}}.

Then for
n\gg 0
there exists an effective divisor
F
such that

1.

F\sim nA
,

2.

\lfloor cn\rfloor Y\subset F
(as closed subschemes),

3.

D
and
F
intersect properly.

Proof.

For simplicity and ease of notation, we assume throughout that
cn\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
D=mD_{0}
where
D_{0}
is a prime Cartier divisor on
X
. Since
\mu_{P}<\vol(D)
, applying Lemma
3.10
with
A=D
and an appropriate rational number
c^{\prime}>1
, it follows that for any sufficiently large and divisible integer
n^{\prime}
, there exists an effective divisor
F^{\prime}
such that
F^{\prime}\sim n^{\prime}D
and
n^{\prime}c^{\prime}Y\subset F^{\prime}
. Since
X
is normal, we can write
F^{\prime}=iD_{0}+D^{\prime}
, where
i
is a nonnegative integer,
D^{\prime}
is effective, and
\Supp D_{0}\not\subset\Supp D^{\prime}
. By Lemma
2.17
,

\displaystyle n_{Y}(D^{\prime})\geq n_{Y}(F^{\prime})-n_{Y}(iD_{0})-1\geq n^{\prime}c^{\prime}-i/m-1.

Let
n_{0}=mn^{\prime}-i
, and note also that
D^{\prime}\sim n_{0}D_{0}
. Clearly
i\leq mn^{\prime}
, and in fact
i<mn^{\prime}
for sufficiently large
n^{\prime}
(or equivalently,
n_{0}>0
, as we now assume) since
c^{\prime}>1
and therefore
n_{Y}(D^{\prime})\geq n^{\prime}c^{\prime}-i/m-1\geq n^{\prime}(c^{\prime}-1)-1>0
for large
n^{\prime}
. Choosing
n^{\prime}
sufficiently large, we also have
n_{Y}(D^{\prime})>n^{\prime}-i/m=n_{0}/m
.

By Lemma
3.10
again, there exists an effective divisor
F^{\prime\prime}
such that
F^{\prime\prime}\sim nA
and
cnY\subset F^{\prime\prime}
. Write
F^{\prime\prime}=jD_{0}+D^{\prime\prime}
, where
j
is a nonnegative integer,
D^{\prime\prime}
is effective, and
\Supp D_{0}\not\subset\Supp D^{\prime\prime}
. Replacing
F^{\prime\prime}
,
n
,
j
, and
D^{\prime\prime}
by suitable multiples, we may assume that
n_{0}|j
. Then
F^{\prime\prime}=jD_{0}+D^{\prime\prime}\sim\frac{j}{n_{0}}D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}
. By the same argument as before,

\displaystyle n_{Y}(D^{\prime\prime})\geq n_{Y}(F^{\prime\prime})-n_{Y}(jD_{0})-1\geq cn-j/m-1.

Let
F=\frac{j}{n_{0}}D^{\prime}+D^{\prime\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle n_{Y}(F)\geq\frac{j}{n_{0}}n_{Y}(D^{\prime})+n_{Y}(D^{\prime\prime})\geq\frac{j}{n_{0}}\frac{n_{0}}{m}+cn-j/m-1=cn-1.

Replacing
c
by a slightly large constant and taking
n
sufficiently large then gives

\displaystyle n_{Y}(F)\geq cn.

It follows that
cnY\subset F
, and we end by noting that
F\sim nA
and
\Supp D\not\subset\Supp F=\Supp D^{\prime}\cup\Supp D^{\prime\prime}
, which implies that
D
and
F
are in general position, and hence intersect properly (as a normal surface is Cohen-Macaulay).
∎

We prove the following inequality, which will be used in Corollary
4.4
. For simplicity, we state it under an ampleness assumption.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
A
and
D
be ample Cartier divisors on a normal projective surface
X
, with
D
a positive multiple of a prime Cartier divisor. Let
Y
be a closed subscheme of
X
supported at a point
P
, and let
\mu_{P}
be the multiplicity of
Y
at
P
. Suppose that
D\supset Y
is a regular chain of closed subschemes of
X
and

\displaystyle\mu_{P}<D^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}\right)\beta(A,D)

(3.3)

and

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)-\beta(A,D)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{Q}
with
0<c<\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}
. Then by Lemma
3.12
, for
n
sufficiently large with
nc\in\mathbb{Z}
, we can find an effective divisor
F
such that
F\sim nD
,
ncY\subset F
, and
D
and
F
intersect properly. Note that
\beta(A,F)=\beta(A,nD)=\frac{1}{n}\beta(A,D)
. Then by Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\beta(A,D)+cn\beta(A,F)=\beta(A,D)+c\beta(A,D).

Since this holds for every positive rational
c<\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}
, the inequality (
3.3
) holds. This implies

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)-\beta(A,D)

\displaystyle\geq\beta(A,Y)-\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}\beta(A,Y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}\beta(A,Y)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{\mu_{P}}}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{P}}},

where the last line uses that
\mu_{P}<D^{2}
.
∎

Finally, we prove a useful estimate for certain beta constants on the blowup of a projective variety
X
of dimension
n
at a point (the idea is to gives a slight improvement to the equality
\beta(D,D)=\frac{1}{n+1}
for an ample divisor
D
on
X
).

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
D
be an ample effective Cartier divisor on a projective variety
X
over
K
of dimension
n
. Let
P\in X(K)
, and let
\pi:\tilde{X}\to X
be the blowup at
P
, with associated exceptional divisor
E
. Then for all sufficiently small positive
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
,

\displaystyle\beta(\pi^{*}D-\delta E,\pi^{*}D-E)>\frac{1}{n+1}.

(3.4)

Proof.

First, we recall that for some positive
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\pi^{*}D-\delta E
is ample for all
0<\delta<\delta^{\prime}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}

[
Har77
, II, Prop. 7.10(b)]
. Let
\delta\in\mathbb{Q}
satisfy
0<\delta<\min\{\delta^{\prime},1\}
. Since
\pi^{*}D-\delta E
is ample,

\displaystyle(\pi^{*}D-\delta E)^{n-1}.E=(-\delta)^{n-1}E^{n}>0.

Let

\displaystyle\gamma:=(-1)^{n-1}E^{n}>0.

Then

\displaystyle(a\pi^{*}D-bE)^{n}

\displaystyle=a^{n}(\pi^{*}D)^{n}+(-1)^{n}b^{n}E^{n}

\displaystyle=a^{n}D^{n}-\gamma b^{n}.

Let
N
be such that
N\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
. We need to estimate

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\tilde{X},N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E)-m(\pi^{*}D-E)\right)=h^{0}\left(\tilde{X},(N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E\right)

for
m>0
. We break the estimate into two cases. We first consider
0<m\leq N\delta
. Since

\displaystyle\frac{N\delta-m}{N-m}<\delta<\delta^{\prime},

(N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E
is ample, and it follows from Riemann-Roch estimates (e.g., using that
\pi^{*}D
and
\pi^{*}D-\delta E
are
\mathbb{Q}
-nef; see
[
Aut09
, p. 233, Cas
k\leq n
]
) that

\displaystyle h^{0}\left((N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{((N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E)^{n}}{n!}+O(N^{n-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}-\gamma(N\delta-m)^{n}}{n!}+O(N^{n-1})

for
0<m\leq N\delta
. Next, for
N\delta<m<N
, note that

\displaystyle(N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E\geq(N-m)\pi^{*}D,

and so for
N\delta<m<N
,

\displaystyle h^{0}\left((N-m)\pi^{*}D-(N\delta-m)E\right)\geq h^{0}((N-m)\pi^{*}D)=\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}}{n!}+O(N^{n-1}).

Therefore,

\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}\left(N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E)-m(\pi^{*}D-E)\right)\\
\geq\sum_{m=1}^{N\delta}\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}-\gamma(N\delta-m)^{n}}{n!}+\sum_{m=N\delta+1}^{N}\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}}{n!}+O(N^{n}).

By standard formulas,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N\delta}\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}-\gamma(N\delta-m)^{n}}{n!}=\frac{N^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}(D^{n}-(1-\delta)^{n+1}D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n+1})+O(N^{n})

and

\displaystyle\sum_{m=N\delta+1}^{N}\frac{(N-m)^{n}D^{n}}{n!}=\frac{N^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}(1-\delta)^{n+1}D^{n}+O(N^{n}).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}(N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E)-m(\pi^{*}D-E))\geq\frac{N^{n+1}}{(n+1)!}(D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n+1})+O(N^{n}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle Nh^{0}(N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E))

\displaystyle=N\left(\frac{N^{n}}{n!}(D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n})+O(N^{n-1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{n+1}}{n!}(D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n})+O(N^{n}).

Note also that, in particular,
\gamma\delta^{n}<D^{n}
. Putting things together, we find

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{m\geq 1}h^{0}(N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E)-m(\pi^{*}D-E))}{Nh^{0}(N(\pi^{*}D-\delta E))}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n+1}\frac{D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n+1}}{D^{n}-\gamma\delta^{n}}

\displaystyle>\frac{1}{n+1}

as
\delta<1
.
∎

4
Main Result and Some Consequences

We now prove (and restate) the Main Theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a projective surface defined over a number field
K
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
. For each
v\in S
, let
D_{v}\supset Y_{v}
be a regular chain of nonempty closed subschemes of
X
. Let
A
be a big Cartier divisor on
X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all

P\in X(K)\setminus Z
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(A,D_{v})\lambda_{D_{v},v}(P)+\left(\beta(A,Y_{v})-\beta(A,D_{v})\right)\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)\bigg)<(1+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

Proof.

From the regular chain assumption, for all
v\in S
,
D_{v}
is an effective Cartier divisor and
\dim Y_{v}=0
. We may further reduce to the case that
Y_{v}
is supported at a single point: after replacing
K
by a finite extension
L
and replacing
S
by the set of places of
L
lying above
S
, we may assume that every point in the support of
Y_{v}
is
K
-rational. Then we may write
Y_{v}=Y_{1,v}+\cdots Y_{r,v}
, where
Y_{1,v},\ldots,Y_{r,v}
are closed subschemes supported at distinct
K
-rational points. Now the reduction follows from observing that
\beta(A,Y_{i,v})\geq\beta(A,Y_{v})
for all
i
, and
\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)=\max_{i}\lambda_{Y_{i,v},v}(P)+O(1)
for all
P\in X(K)\setminus Y_{v}
(see Remark
2.2
).

Let
N>0
and let
v\in S
. We consider the two filtrations on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
given by order of vanishing along
D_{v}
and
Y_{v}
, respectively:

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-D_{v}))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA-2D_{v}))\supset\cdots

and

\displaystyle H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v})\supset H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v}^{2})\supset\cdots.

By Lemma
2.18
, there exists a basis
s_{1,v},\ldots,s_{h^{0}(NA),v}
of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
adapted to both filtrations. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mathrm{div}(s_{i,v})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})-h^{0}(NA-(i+1)D_{v})\right)iD_{v}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)D_{v}

and similarly,

\sum_{i=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mathrm{div}(s_{i,v})\supset\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v}^{i})\right)Y_{v}.

as closed subschemes. Then we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mathrm{div}(s_{i,v})=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)D_{v}+F_{v}

for some effective divisor
F_{v}
. By Lemma
2.17
,

\displaystyle n_{Y_{v}}(F_{v})

\displaystyle\geq n_{Y_{v}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{h^{0}(NA)}\mathrm{div}(s_{i,v})\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)-1

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v}^{i})\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)-1.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lambda_{F_{v},v}(P)

\displaystyle\geq n_{Y_{v}}(F_{v})\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)+O(1)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v}^{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})-1\right)\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)+O(1)

for
P\in X(K)\setminus\Supp F_{v}
.

We now apply the Subspace Theorem in the form of
[
RV20
, Theorem 2.10]
, with the union of the bases
s_{1,v},\ldots,s_{h^{0}(NA),v},v\in S,
of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(NA))
constructed above; we obtain that for
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X
such that, up to
O(1)
,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)\lambda_{D_{v},v}(P)+

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(\mathcal{O}(NA)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{v}^{i})-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})-1\right)\lambda_{Y_{v},v}(P)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in S}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}h^{0}(NA-iD_{v})\right)\lambda_{D_{v},v}(P)+\lambda_{F_{v},v}(P)

\displaystyle\leq(h^{0}(NA)+\varepsilon)h_{NA}(P)

\displaystyle\leq(Nh^{0}(NA)+N\varepsilon)h_{A}(P)

for all
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
. Dividing by
Nh^{0}(NA)
and taking
N
sufficiently large gives the desired inequality.
∎

We show that Theorem
1.4
implies the Ru-Vojta inequality
[
RV20
]
for surfaces (a similar, but more complicated argument, could be used to derive Theorem
1.2
in the case of surfaces).

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a projective surface over a number field
K
, and let
D_{1},\ldots,D_{q}
be nonzero effective Cartier divisors intersecting properly on
X
. Let
\mathscr{L}
be a big line bundle on
X
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all

, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{i})m_{D_{i},S}(P)\leq(1+\varepsilon)h_{\mathscr{L}}(P).

Proof.

For any point
P\in X(K)
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{q}\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{i})m_{D_{i},S}(P)\leq\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{P,1,v})\lambda_{D_{P,1,v},v}(P)+\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{P,2,v})\lambda_{D_{P,2,v},v}(P)+O(1)\bigg),

for some divisors
D_{P,1,v}
and
D_{P,2,v}
depending on
P
and
v
(but with the
O(1)
term independent of
P
). Then by considering the finitely many choices for
D_{P,1,v}
and
D_{P,2,v}
, it suffices to study the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v})\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P)+\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{2,v})\lambda_{D_{2,v},v}(P)\bigg),

where for each
v\in S
the divisors
D_{1,v},D_{2,v}
intersect properly. Again, by considering finitely many cases we may assume that
\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P)\geq\lambda_{D_{2,v},v}(P)
for all
v\in S
and thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{D_{2,v},v}(P)=\min\{\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P),\lambda_{D_{2,v},v}(P)\}=\lambda_{D_{1,v}\cap D_{2,v},v}(P)+O(1)

for all
v\in S
. By Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v}\cap D_{2,v})\geq\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v})+\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{2,v}).

Therefore, by Theorem
1.4
, up to

,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v})\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P)+\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{2,v})\lambda_{D_{2,v},v}(P)\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v})\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P)+\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{2,v})\lambda_{D_{1,v}\cap D_{2,v},v}(P)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\bigg(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v})\lambda_{D_{1,v},v}(P)+(\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v}\cap D_{2,v})-\beta(\mathscr{L},D_{1,v}))\lambda_{D_{1,v}\cap D_{2,v},v}(P)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle~(1+\varepsilon)h_{\mathscr{L}}(P)

for all
K
-rational points outside a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z
of
X
. Finally, we may omit any
O(1)
term by enlarging
Z
.
∎

From

]

and in particular, on a surface

\displaystyle\beta(A,D)\geq\frac{1}{3}\epsilon(A,D).

(4.1)

Using inequality (

) and Lemma

, we immediately derive from Theorem

the inequality of Heier and the second author
[

]
for surfaces, under a smoothness and regular chain assumption:

Corollary 4.4

Let
X
be a normal projective surface defined over a number field
K
. Let

be a finite set of places of
K
. For each

, let

such that
D_{v}
is a positive multiple of an ample prime Cartier divisor,
Y_{v}
is supported at a point

, and

\displaystyle\mu_{v}=\mu_{Q_{v}}(Y_{v})<D_{v}^{2}.

Let
A
be an ample divisor on
X
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\left(3\beta(A,D_{v})\lambda_{D_{v}}(P)+2\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{v}}}\frac{\sqrt{\frac{D_{v}^{2}}{\mu_{v}}}}{1+\sqrt{\frac{D_{v}^{2}}{\mu_{v}}}}\lambda_{Y_{v}}(P)\right)<(3+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

In particular, there exists a proper Zariski-closed subset
Z\subset X
such that for all
P\in X(K)\setminus Z
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S}\left(3\beta(A,D_{v})\lambda_{D_{v}}(P)+\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{v}}}\lambda_{Y_{v}}(P)\right)<(3+\varepsilon)h_{A}(P).

Remark 4.5
.

For purposes of comparison, if
X
is a nonsingular projective surface over
K
and
Y
is supported at a point
Q\in X(K)
, then we have the inequality
[
CEL01
, Remark 2.4]

\displaystyle\epsilon(A,Y)\leq\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{Q}(Y)}}.

When
\mu_{Q}(Y)=1
and
A^{2}
is not a perfect square, this inequality has been conjectured to always be strict (more precisely, it has been conjectured that the Seshadri constant at a point is always rational).

In the situation of Remark
4.5
, it follows that we have the inequalities

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{Q}(Y)}}\geq\frac{2}{3}\epsilon(A,Y).

We give a simple example where the inequalities are all strict.

Example 4.6
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, let
A
be a divisor of type
(1,1)
on
X
, and let
Y=Q\in X(K)
be a point (viewed as a closed subscheme with the reduced induced structure). Then elementary computations give

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{Q}(Y)}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2},

\displaystyle\epsilon(A,Y)

\displaystyle=1.

Then we have strict inequalities

\displaystyle\beta(A,Y)>\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{A^{2}}{\mu_{Q}(Y)}}>\frac{2}{3}\epsilon(A,Y).

5
Applications

In the remaining sections, we investigate some Diophantine applications of our main result Theorem

5.1
Greatest Common Divisors on Surfaces

In 2003, Bugeaud, Corvaja, and Zannier
[

]
initiated a new line of results with the following inequality involving greatest common divisors:

Theorem 5.1

(Bugeaud-Corvaja-Zannier
[

]
)

, and therefore
C\cap(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3})=\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
. Since
C\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have
C\setminus(D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3})\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}
, and
C
will contain an infinite set
R
of
(D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3},S)
-integral points (we use
|S|\geq 2
here, so that
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
is infinite). Since the union of such elements
C\in\Lambda
is Zariski dense in
X
, the conclusion of Theorem
5.9
does not hold.

We do not assert that there exists a Zariski dense set of
(D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3},S)
-integral points; in fact, the pencil yields a morphism
X\setminus\{D_{1}\cup D_{2}\cup D_{3}\}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
and Siegel’s theorem shows that there is no Zariski dense set of
(D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3},S)
-integral points in
X(K)
.

We first give an example showing that the factor
\frac{4}{9}
in the intersection condition (
1.4
) (or (
5.10
)) cannot be replaced by anything larger than
\frac{1}{2}
.

Example 5.11
.

Consider the pencil of plane conics
\Lambda=\{C_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in K\}
, where
C_{\lambda}=\{y^{2}-\lambda xz=0\}
. Then any two distinct curves
C_{\lambda},C_{\lambda}^{\prime}\in\Lambda
intersect precisely at the two points
Q_{1}=[0:0:1],Q_{2}=[1:0:0]
, each with multiplicity
2
. By Example
5.10
, if
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}\in\Lambda
are distinct elements, then the conclusion of Theorem
5.9
does not hold (for appropriate
K
and
S
), and we note that

\displaystyle(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q_{1}}=(D_{i}.D_{j})_{Q_{2}}=2=\frac{1}{2}(D_{i}.D_{j}).

Somewhat surprisingly, the next example shows that the beta constant condition in Theorem
5.9
is sharp, in the sense that the condition

\displaystyle\beta(D,(a_{i}D_{i}\cap a_{j}D_{j})_{Q})>1

cannot be replaced by the inequality

\displaystyle\beta(D,(a_{i}D_{i}\cap a_{j}D_{j})_{Q})\geq 1.

Example 5.12
.

Let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
be two distinct irreducible curves of type
(1,1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, defined over a number field
K
, intersecting in two distinct
K
-rational points
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
. Let
\Lambda
be the pencil of curves containing
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, and let
D_{3}\in\Lambda\setminus\{D_{1},D_{2}\}
be another irreducible element of the pencil.

For any point
Q\in(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1})(K)
(viewed as a closed subscheme with the reduced induced structure) an elementary computation gives

\displaystyle\beta(D_{1},Q)=1.

Since
D_{i}\cap D_{j}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}
(as closed subschemes) when
i\neq j
, we see from Example

(with this pencil) that the conclusion of Theorem

does not hold in general when (
5.8
) is replaced by
\beta(D,(a_{i}D_{i}\cap a_{j}D_{j})_{Q})\geq 1

5.4
Families of Unit Equations

The unit equation theorem, proved by Siegel (when
S
is the set of archimedean places) and Mahler, is a fundamental and ubiquitous result in number theory:

Theorem 5.13

(Siegel-Mahler)
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
containing the archimedean places. Let
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
be the ring of
S
-integers of
K
and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
be the group of
S
-units of
K
. Let
a,b,c\in K^{*}
. The
S
-unit equation

\displaystyle au+bv=c,\quad u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*},

has only finitely many solutions.

From another viewpoint, the theorem is equivalent to Siegel’s theorem on integral points on the affine curve
\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
. In this section, we study the one-parameter family of unit equations

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v=f_{3}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*},

where
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
are polynomials over a number field
K
. This equation was treated in the case
\deg f_{1}=\deg f_{2}=\deg f_{3}
by Corvaja and Zannier
[
CZ06
,
CZ10
]
, and in the case
\deg f_{1}+\deg f_{2}=\deg f_{3}
by the second author
[
Lev06
]
. Applying the results of the previous section to certain surfaces, we handle a wide range of new values of the triple
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})=(\deg f_{1},\deg f_{2},\deg f_{3})
.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}\in K[t]
be nonconstant polynomials without a common zero of degrees
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}
, respectively. Let
\{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}\}=\{1,2,3\}
be such that
d_{i_{1}}\geq d_{i_{2}}\geq d_{i_{3}}
and suppose that

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\frac{d_{i_{1}}+1}{d_{i_{1}}+1-d_{i_{2}}}}-1\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{{d_{i_{1}}+1}}{d_{i_{1}}+1-d_{i_{3}}}}-1\right)>\frac{1}{4}.

(5.16)

In particular, (
5.16
) holds when

\displaystyle\max_{i}(d_{i}+1)<\frac{9}{5}\min_{i}d_{i}.

Then the set of solutions
(t,u,v)\in\mathbb{A}^{3}(K)
of the equation

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v=f_{3}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*},

(5.17)

is contained in a finite number of rational curves in
\mathbb{A}^{3}
.

We note that if
d_{i_{1}}>d_{i_{2}}
in Theorem
5.14
, then by
[
Lev06
, Lemma 5]
, one may drop the integrality condition on
t
in (
5.17
) (i.e., consider solutions with
t\in K
).

Proof.

We first remark that the statement of the theorem is independent of the ordering of
f_{1},f_{2},f_{3}
; this follows from noting, for example, that if
(t_{0},u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
is a solution to
f_{1}(t)u+f_{3}(t)v=f_{2}(t)
, then
(t_{0},-u_{0}/v_{0},1/v_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
is a solution to
f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v=f_{3}(t)
, and this relation corresponds to a birational automorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{3}
. Thus, after permuting the
f_{i}
, we may assume that
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq d_{3}>0
,
d_{i_{j}}=d_{j}
,
j=1,2,3
, and

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+1-d_{2}}}-1\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{{d_{1}+1}}{d_{1}+1-d_{3}}}-1\right)>\frac{1}{4}.

In place of (
5.17
), we will actually study the slightly modified equation

\displaystyle f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v^{d_{1}+1-d_{2}}=f_{3}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S},u,v\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}.

(5.18)

Since
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
is finitely generated, we can find a number field
L
and a finite set of places
S^{\prime}
of
L
such that every element of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}
has a
(d_{1}+1-d_{2})
-th root in
\mathcal{O}_{L,S^{\prime}}^{*}
. Then (
5.17
) reduces to studying the equation (
5.18
) (with
(K,S)
replaced by
(L,S^{\prime})
).

We let
F_{i}\in K[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
be the homogeneous polynomial
F_{i}=f_{i}(x_{0}/x_{3})x_{3}^{d_{i}}
,
i=1,2,3
, and let
X
be the hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
defined by the equation

\displaystyle F:=x_{1}F_{1}+x_{2}^{d_{1}+1-d_{2}}F_{2}-x_{3}^{d_{1}+1-d_{3}}F_{3}=0.

Since the polynomials
f_{i}
do not have a common zero, it follows easily that
F
is irreducible in
\overline{K}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]
(and
X
is a projective surface).

Let
H_{i}
be the hyperplane of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
defined by
x_{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
, and let
D_{i}=H_{i}|_{X}
be the divisor on
X
defined by
x_{i}=0
,
i=1,2,3
. Since
X
is a hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
H_{i},H_{j},X
,
i\neq j
, are in general position on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
(as
F_{i}(1,0,0,0)\neq 0
), by Remark
2.13
,
D_{i}
and
D_{j}
intersect properly on
X
if
i\neq j
. Letting
P_{0}=[1:0:0:0]\in X(K)
, we have
D_{1}\cap D_{2}\cap D_{3}=\{P_{0}\}\neq\emptyset
, and by construction
D_{1}\sim D_{2}\sim D_{3}
. We also set
D=D_{1}
.

We note that
P_{0}
is a nonsingular point of
X
, with maximal ideal
(x_{2},x_{3})
in the local ring at
P_{0}
. Then if
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
, from the equation for
F
we find
(x_{i},x_{j})\mathcal{O}_{P_{0}}=(x_{i},x_{j},x_{k}^{d_{1}+1-d_{k}})\mathcal{O}_{P_{0}}
. It follows that

\displaystyle(D_{i}.D_{j})_{P_{0}}=d_{1}+1-d_{k}.

Note also that
D^{2}=\deg X=d_{1}+1
. Then by Lemma
3.11
,

\displaystyle\beta(D,(D_{i}\cap D_{j})_{P_{0}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{D^{2}}{(D_{i}.D_{j})_{P_{0}}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+1-d_{k}}}.

Then it is not hard to show that the condition (
5.8
) of Theorem
5.9
is satisfied if

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+1-d_{3}}}-1\right)+\left(\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+1-d_{2}}}-1\right)\left(2\sqrt{\frac{d_{1}+1}{d_{1}+1-d_{3}}}-2\right)>0,

which is equivalent to (
5.16
). Now we let

\displaystyle R=\{[t:u:v:1]\in X(K)\mid f_{1}(t)u+f_{2}(t)v^{d_{1}+1-d_{2}}=f_{3}(t),(t,u,v)\in\mathcal{O}_{K,S}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}\times\mathcal{O}_{K,S}^{*}\}.

Then
R
is easily seen to be a set of
(D_{1}+D_{2}+D_{3},S)
-integral points on
X
. Now under the assumption (
5.16
), we can apply Theorem
5.9
with the divisors
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
on
X
and conclude that
R
lies in a finite union of curves on
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Finally, we note that since the divisors
D_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
are ample on
X
, Siegel’s theorem on integral points on curves implies that if
C
is a curve in
X
and
C\cap R
is infinite, then
C
is a rational curve, and thus we may take a finite union of rational curves in the conclusion of the theorem.

Dynamics of dilute nuclear matter with light clusters and in-medium effects
Rui Wang ID, Stefano Burrello ID, Maria Colonna ID, Francesco Matera

Abstract
We investigate the dynamics of dilute systems composed of nucleons and light clusters within a linear response approach, taking into account the in-medium Mott effects on cluster appearance, through a density-dependent momentum cut-off. We find that spinodal instabilities and associated growth rates are severely affected by the presence of light clusters and, in particular, by the treatment of in-medium effects, foreshadowing intriguing consequences for fragment formation in heavy-ion collisions and in the broader astrophysical context.

Summary and outlook.

Within a linearized Vlasov approach, we have explored the
occurrence of spinodal instabilities in dilute nuclear matter with light-cluster degrees of freedom. We show that the presence of light clusters and, in particular, in-medium (Mott) effects on their propagation have a crucial impact on the features of the unstable modes responsible for the system disassembly. Whereas, if in-medium effects in the dynamics are neglected, the light clusters move in phase with nucleons, cooperating in the formation of fragments, local in-medium effects induce a sort of distillation mechanism
[
23
,
47
]
, with clusters moving towards the lower density regions, slowing down the instability growth and eventually leading to the dominance of different fragmentation modes. These findings underline the relevance of a proper inclusion of light-cluster degrees of freedom and of in-medium effects in the description of dilute nuclear systems, revealing important consequences for the understanding of heavy-ion collisions and astrophysical phenomena taking place in low-density and moderate-temperature environments
[
51
,
52
]
.

f_{d}\left(\lambda_{d}\right)
denoting the cluster distribution function at equilibrium,
evaluated at
\lambda_{d}=\dfrac{\Lambda_{d}^{2}}{2m_{d}}
.
By taking into account that, by virtue of Eq. (6) in the main text,

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{j}\left(1+\delta_{jd}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dj}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{j}\int_{\Lambda_{j}}\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\delta f_{j}-\delta_{jd}\sum_{q=n,p}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dq}\delta\rho_{q}

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{j}\int_{\Lambda_{j}}\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\delta f_{j}-\delta_{jd}\sum_{q=n,p}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dq}g_{q}\int\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\delta f_{q},

one finally obtains

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{E}\left[\delta f_{n},\delta f_{p},\delta f_{d}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j}g_{j}\int_{\Lambda_{j}}\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\left(\epsilon_{j}+\dfrac{U_{j}-\lambda_{d}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dj}}{1+\delta_{jd}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dj}}\right)\delta f_{j}-\dfrac{U_{d}-\lambda_{d}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dd}}{1+\Phi_{\lambda}^{dd}}\sum_{q=n,p}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dq}g_{q}\int\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\delta f_{q},

Then, the single-particle energy, as defined in Eq. (4) of the main text, can be written as

\varepsilon_{j}=\epsilon_{j}+U_{j}+\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}^{\lambda},

(21)

where, beside the momentum-independent mean-field potential
U_{j}
, it appears the extra term

\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}^{\lambda}=-\dfrac{\lambda_{d}+U_{d}}{1+\Phi_{\lambda}^{dd}}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dj}

(22)

playing the role of an effective potential contribution, which arises from the density dependence of the cluster kinetic energy cut-off
\lambda_{d}
. The variation of the single particle energy
\delta\varepsilon_{j}
in the space of density fluctuations
\delta\rho_{j}
, entering the
linearized Vlasov equations (see Eq. (3) in the main text), is then easily evaluated as:

\displaystyle\delta\varepsilon_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l}\dfrac{\partial U_{j}}{\partial\rho_{l}}\delta\rho_{l}+\sum_{l}\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}^{\lambda}}{\partial\rho_{l}}\delta\rho_{l}

(23)

II
Linearized Vlasov equations and Landau parameters

Let us follow a standard Landau procedure, starting from the system of three coupled equations for the fluctuations of neutron, proton and light cluster distribution functions
\delta f_{j}
, given by Eq. (3) in the main text. In the case of a momentum-independent interaction,
one has

\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}\varepsilon_{j}=\dfrac{\mathbf{p}}{m_{j}}\qquad\nabla_{\mathbf{p}}f_{j}=\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}}\dfrac{\mathbf{p}}{m_{j}},

(24)

and the linearized Vlasov equations reduce to:

\frac{\partial(\delta f_{j})}{\partial t}+\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}(\delta f_{j})\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}-\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}(\delta\varepsilon_{j})=0,

(25)

where
\mathbf{v}_{j}=\dfrac{\mathbf{p}}{m_{j}}
denotes the velocity. Then, for solutions of the type
\delta f_{j}\sim\sum_{\mathbf{k}}\delta f_{j}^{\,\mathbf{k}}\,e^{i(\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{r}-\omega t)}
and exploiting Eq. (
23
), the system becomes

\left(-i\omega+i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}\right)\delta f_{j}-\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}}\mathbf{v}_{j}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf{r}}\sum_{l}\left(\dfrac{\partial U_{j}}{\partial\rho_{l}}+\dfrac{\partial\tilde{\varepsilon}_{j}^{\lambda}}{\partial\rho_{l}}\right)\delta\rho_{l}=0

(26)

and, taking into account the definition of the Landau parameters given by Eq. (8) in the main text, one gets

\delta f_{j}=\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}}\dfrac{i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}}{\left(-i\omega+i\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}-i0^{+}\right)}\dfrac{1}{N_{j}}\sum_{l}\left(F_{0}^{jl}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{jl}\right)\delta\rho_{l},

(27)

where

N_{j}=-g_{j}\int_{\Lambda_{j}}\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi\hbar)^{3}}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}}

(28)

is the thermally averaged level density of the considered species
j
.

Finally, by taking the momentum integrals on both sides and bearing in mind the definition of the Lindhard function:

\chi_{j}\left(\omega,\mathbf{k}\right)=\dfrac{g_{j}}{N_{j}}\int_{\Lambda_{j}}\dfrac{d\mathbf{p}}{\left(2\pi\hbar\right)^{3}}\dfrac{\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}}{\omega-\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{v}_{j}+i0^{+}}\dfrac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial\epsilon_{j}},

(29)

one may express the system in the following compact form

\delta\rho_{j}=-\chi_{j}\sum_{l}\left(F_{0}^{jl}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{jl}\right)\delta\rho_{l}-\delta_{jd}\sum_{l}\Phi_{\lambda}^{dl}\delta\rho_{l},

(30)

or, more expicitly, as:

\displaystyle[left=\empheqlbrace]\left[1+\left(F_{0}^{nn}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{nn}\right)\chi_{n}\right]\delta\rho_{n}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\left(F_{0}^{np}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{np}\right)\chi_{n}\delta\rho_{p}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\left(F_{0}^{nd}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{nd}\right)\chi_{n}\delta\rho_{d}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\left(F_{0}^{pn}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{pn}\right)\chi_{p}\delta\rho_{n}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\left[1+\left(F_{0}^{pp}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{pp}\right)\chi_{p}\right]\delta\rho_{p}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\left(F_{0}^{pd}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{pd}\right)\chi_{p}\delta\rho_{d}

\displaystyle{}={}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\left[\left(F_{0}^{dn}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{dn}\right)\chi_{d}+\Phi_{\lambda}^{dn}\right]\delta\rho_{n}

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\left[\left(F_{0}^{dp}+\tilde{F}_{\lambda}^{dp}\right)\chi_{d}+\Phi_{\lambda}^{dp}\right]\delta\rho_{p}

\displaystyle{}+{}

(31)

In case of SNM with deuterons, by introducing

(32)

where
F_{0}=F_{0}^{qq}+F_{0}^{qq^{\prime}}
,
q,q^{\prime}=n,p
with
q\neq q^{\prime}

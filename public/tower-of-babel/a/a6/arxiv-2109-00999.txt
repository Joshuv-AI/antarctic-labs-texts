On the Bands of the Schrödinger Operator with a Matrix Potential
O.A.Veliev

Abstract
In this article we consider the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator L ⁡ ( Q ) L(Q) with a Hermitian periodic m × m m\times m matrix potential Q Q . We investigate the bands and gaps of the spectrum and prove that most of the positive real axis is overlapped by m m bands. Moreover, we find a condition on the potential Q Q for which the number of gaps in the spectrum of L ⁡ ( Q ) L(Q) is finite. Key Words: Self-adjoint differential operator, Spectral bands, Periodic matrix potential. AMS Mathematics Subject Classification: 34L05, 34L20.

Finally note that a great number of paper is devoted to the scalar case

Main Results

Theorem 2

The length of the spectral gaps
(\alpha,\beta)
lying in

By Corollary 1 we have

(\left(s\pi\right)^{2}+\mu_{1}-\varepsilon(s-1)\leq\alpha<\beta\leq\left(\pi s\right)^{2}+\mu_{p}+\varepsilon(s).

Now suppose on the contrary that the length of the gap
(\alpha,\beta)

2\max\left\{\varepsilon(s-1),\varepsilon(s)\right\}.
Then
it follows from last equalities that

\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\in(\left(s\pi\right)^{2}+\mu_{1},\left(\pi s\right)^{2}+\mu_{p}),\text{ }\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}>\max\left\{\varepsilon(s-1),\varepsilon(s)\right\}

Using (6) one can easily conclude that there exist
t\in(-\pi,\pi]
and

such that the equality
\mu_{s,j}(t)=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}
holds. On the other hands, by Theorem 4
(a)
of
[16] there exists an eigenvalue
\lambda
of

lying in
\max\left\{\varepsilon(s-1),\varepsilon(s)\right\}
neighborhood of
\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}.
Therefore

\lambda\in\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}-\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2},\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)=(\alpha,\beta).

Hence
(\alpha,\beta)
is not a gap in the spectrum of
L(Q).
This
contradiction imply the proof of the theorem.

To investigate the spectrum of

and the exceptional points of the spectrum of

\mu_{k,j}(t)

and
k\in\mathbb{Z}.
Moreover, we investigate the large
exceptional Bloch eigenvalues
\left(2\pi k+t\right)^{2}+\mu_{j}
of

\sigma(L(C)),
because we are going to investigate the spectrum of the

by using the asymptotic formulas for the large eigenvalues. In

is a large number. Then (12) has a solution

only in the cases

In these cases (12) implies that the
large eigenvalue
\left(2\pi k+t\right)^{2}+\mu_{j}
is an exceptional
Bloch eigenvalue of

if either

\mu_{k,j}(t)-\mu_{-k,i}(t)=8\pi kt+\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}=0

(13)

(14)

for some

We denote by

and

t(2k+1,j,i)=\pi+\frac{\mu_{i}-\mu_{j}}{4\pi(2k+1)}

the solutions of equations (13) and (14) lying in

. Thus eliminating
the sets

However,
to investigate the perturbation of these non-exceptional Bloch eigenvalues by
using the asymptotic formulas obtained in [16], we eliminate

-neighborhoods

and

Moreover,

\left[a(k,j,s),b(k,j,s)\right]

[0,\pi]\backslash\left({\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{i=1,2,...,p}}\left(U_{\delta_{k}}(t(2k,j,i))\cup U_{\delta_{k}}(t(2k+1,j,i))\right)\right)=

(15)

{\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{s=1}^{v}}\left[a(k,j,s),b(k,j,s)\right],

Lemma 1

There exists

\mid

4\pi(2\left|k\right|-2)\delta_{k}=2\max\left\{\varepsilon_{k},\varepsilon_{-k},\varepsilon_{-k-1}\right\},

(16)

and the quasimomentum

(a)
The inequality

(b)
The closed interval

\overline{U_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\mu_{k,j}(t))}=\left[\mu_{k,j}(t)-\varepsilon_{k},\mu_{k,j}(t)+\varepsilon_{k}\right]

\overline{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\mu_{n,i}(t))}\
for

\mid

(n,i)\neq\left(k,j\right)

Proof.

(a)
Introduce the notations
f(t)=\mu_{k,j}(t)-\mu_{-k,i}(t)
and

By the definition of

and

and

(see (13) and
(14)). On the other hand, the derivatives of the functions

and

are

8\pi k
and
4\pi(2k+1)
respectively. Therefore if
t
does not belong to
the
\delta_{k}
-neighborhood of
t(2k,j,i)
and
t(2k+1,j,i)
for

that is, if

belong to the intervals
\left[a(k,j,s),b(k,j,s)\right]

(b)
If (16) holds then it follows from (17) that the distance
\left|\mu_{k,j}(t)-\mu_{n,i}(t)\right|
between the centres of the intervals

and

is

\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{n}
of the radii of

\overline{U_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\mu_{k,j}(t))}
has no common points with the intervals
\overline{U_{\varepsilon_{n}}(\mu_{n,i}(t))}\
for

\mid

k\mid>N_{2},

\mid

n\mid\geq N_{2}
and

then
\left|\mu_{k,j}(t)-\mu_{n,i}(t)\right|
is a

If

and

then
\left|\mu_{k,j}(t)-\mu_{n,i}(t)\right|=\left|\mu_{j}-\mu_{i}\right|>\varepsilon_{k}+\varepsilon_{n},

since

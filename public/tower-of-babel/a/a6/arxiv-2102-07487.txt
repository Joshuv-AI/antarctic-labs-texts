A max inequality for spectral invariants of disjointly supported Hamiltonians.
Shira Tanny

Abstract
We study the relation between spectral invariants of disjointly supported Hamiltonians and of their sum. On aspherical manifolds, such a relation was established by Humilière, Le Roux and Seyfaddini. We show that a weaker statement holds in a wider setting, and derive applications to Polterovich’s Poisson bracket invariant and to Entov and Polterovich’s notion of superheavy sets.

1.2

The main application of the max inequality (

, with different upper bounds and constraints. Ishikawa proved that, on negatively monotone manifolds, the spectral invariant of every Hamiltonian supported in a disjoint union of embeddings of strictly convex sets into

As explained in

A closed subset

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{c(kH)}{k}\leq\sup_{X\times S^{1}}H,\quad\forall H\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M\times S^{1}).

(6)

Corollary 1.13
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a monotone symplectic manifold with monotonicity constant
\kappa

be a portable Liouville domain with a dynamically convex incompressible boundary, and assume in addition that either

Let us review the necessary preliminaries and fix some notations. Note that throughout the paper we assume

2.1
Hamiltonian Floer homology.

Given a Hamiltonian
H:M\times S^{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, its symplectic gradient is the vector field defined by the equation
\omega(X_{H},\cdot)=-dH
and the flow
\varphi_{H}^{t}
of this vector field is called the Hamiltonian flow of
H
.
The set of 1-periodic orbits of
\varphi_{H}^{t}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}}(H)
. The Hamiltonian
H
is called
non-degenerate
if the graph of
d\varphi_{H}^{1}
is transversal to the diagonal in
TM\times TM
. Equivalently,
H
is non-degenerate if every
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
is non-degenerate, that is, if 1 is not an eigenvalue of
d\varphi_{H}^{1}(\gamma(0))
for every
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{L}}M
the space of contractible loops in
M
. A capping disk of
\gamma\in{\mathcal{L}}M
is a map
u:D\rightarrow M
from the unit disk to
M
, satisfying
u|_{\partial D}=\gamma
. Two capping disks
u_{1},u_{2}
of
\gamma
are equivalent if
[u_{1}\#(-u_{2})]\in\ker\omega\cap\ker c_{1}
. We denote by
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
the space of equivalence classes of capped loops,
(\gamma,u)
.
The action functional corresponding to
H
is defined on the space
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
by

{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u)=\int_{0}^{1}H(\gamma(t),t)\ dt-\int_{u}\omega.

The critical points of the action functional are (equivalence classes of) capped 1-periodic orbits of
\varphi_{H}^{t}
and the set of their values is denoted by
{\operatorname{Spec}}(H)
. For a non-degenerate Hamiltonian
H
and a generic
\omega
-compatible almost complex structure
J
, the Floer chain complex
CF_{*}(H,J)
is generated by these critical points and its differential is defined by counting certain negative gradient flow lines of
{\mathcal{A}}_{H}
(with respect to a metric induced by
J
on
\widetilde{{\mathcal{L}}M}
).
For more details, see, e.g.,
[
12
,
17
]
.
The chain complex
CF_{*}(H,J)
is graded by the Conley-Zehnder (abbreviated to CZ) index, whose definition is recalled below. More formally,
CF_{k}(H,J)
is generated by (equivalence classes of) capped 1-periodic orbits whose index
6
6

6

We add the subscript
H
to the notation in order to distinguish this index from the contact CZ index, which will be discussed later.
,
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma,u)
, is equal to
-k
. For

, we denote by
{\operatorname{Spec}}_{k}(H)
the set of action values of the generators of

2.1.1
The Conley-Zehnder and Robbin-Salamon indices.

The Conley-Zehnder index is defined for non-degenerate capped 1-periodic orbits, through an index of a path of symplectic matrices which is obtained from the linearized flow after trivializing the tangent bundle. In
[
18
]
, Robbin and Salamon defined a Maslov-type index for possibly degenerate orbits, that coincides with the Conley-Zehnder index on non-degenerate ones. Since we will consider degenerate Hamiltonians as well, we give here the definition of the Robbin-Salamon index, following the exposition in
[
7
]
.

•

Let
\Phi=\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}\subset{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n)
be a path of symplectic matrices. A number
t\in[0,T]
is called a
crossing
if
\det(\Phi(t)-{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}})=0
. The
crossing form

\Gamma_{t}=\Gamma_{t}(\Phi)
is the quadratic form obtained by restricting the symmetric matrix
S(t):=-J_{0}\dot{\Phi}(t)\Phi^{-1}(t)
to
\ker(\Phi(t)-{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}})
. A crossing
t_{0}
is called
regular
if the crossing form
\Gamma_{t_{0}}
is non-degenerate.

•

For a path
\Phi=\{\Phi(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
having only regular crossings, the
Robbin-Salamon index
(abbreviated to RS) is defined to be

{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi):=\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{sign}\,}(\Gamma_{0})+\sum_{0<t<T}{\operatorname{sign}\,}(\Gamma_{t})+\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{sign}\,}(\Gamma_{T}),

(7)

where the sum is taken over all crossings
t\in(0,T)
.
The definition of the RS index for general paths of symplectic matrices is given in
[
7
]
. This index admits several useful properties:

–

(homotopy) The RS index is invariant under homotopies with fixed ends.

–

(products)
{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi_{1}\oplus\Phi_{2})={\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi_{1})+{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi_{2})
.

–

(conjugation)
{\operatorname{RS}}(\Psi\Phi\Psi^{-1})={\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi)
for every path
\Psi
of symplectic matrices.

–

(concatenation)
{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi|_{[t_{1},t_{3}]})={\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]})+{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi|_{[t_{2},t_{3}]})
.

–

(inverse)
{\operatorname{RS}}(\bar{\Phi})={\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi^{T})=-{\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi)
, where
\bar{\Phi}(t):=\Phi(-t)
and
\Phi^{T}
is the path of transposed matrices.

Given a general path we may perturb it with fixed ends to obtain a path with only regular crossings, and use (
7
) to compute the index.
For a path
\Phi
with only regular crossings such that
\Phi(0)={1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}}
, and
t=1
is not a crossing, the RS index coincides with the Conley-Zehnder index of the path.

•

To define the index of a capped periodic orbit
(\gamma,u)
, consider a trivialization of the tangent bundle
TM
along
u
. Then, the differential of the flow
\varphi_{H}^{t}
along the loop
\gamma
is identified with a path of symplectic matrices,
d\varphi_{H}^{t}(\gamma(0))\mapsto\Phi(t)\in{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n)
. The RS index of the capped orbit is given by
{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u):={\operatorname{RS}}(\Phi)
.
Throughout the paper, we use the notation
{\operatorname{CZ}}
for non-degenerate orbits and
{\operatorname{RS}}
for degenerate ones.
A useful property of the CZ index is that the indices with respect to different capping disks differ by twice the first Chern class of the connected sum. More formally, let
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
be a non-degenerate 1-periodic orbit of a Hamiltonian
H
and let
u,v:D\rightarrow M
be two different capping disks for
\gamma
. Then, for
A:=u\#(-v)\in\pi_{2}(M)
,

{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma,u)={\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma,v\#A)={\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma,v)+2c_{1}(A),

(8)

where
c_{1}
denotes the first Chern class of
M
, see, e.g.,
[
12
, Sections 2.6-7]
.

We remark that there are texts choosing an opposite sign for the CZ index, such as
[
20
]
. In our sign convention, the index of a critical point
p
of a
C^{2}
-small Morse function with a constant capping disk,
u_{p}(D)=\{p\}
, is related to the Morse index via
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(p,u_{p})=n-i_{Morse}(p)
.

2.2
Spectral invariants.

The Floer complex admits a natural filtration by the action value. Let
CF_{*}^{a}(H,J)
be the sub-complex generated by (equivalence classes of) capped 1-periodic orbits whose action is bounded by
a
from above. Since the Floer differential is action decreasing, it restricts to the sub-complex
CF_{*}^{a}(H,J)
and the homology
HF_{*}^{a}(H,J)
is well defined.
The spectral invariant with respect to the fundamental class is defined to be the smallest value of
a
for which the fundamental class appears in
HF^{a}_{*}(H,J)
, namely,

c(H):=\inf\{a:[M]\in{\operatorname{Im}\,}(\iota^{a}_{*})\},

(9)

where
\iota^{a}_{*}:HF_{*}^{a}(H,J)\rightarrow HF_{*}(H,J)
is the map induced by the inclusion
\iota^{a}:CF_{*}^{a}(H,J)\hookrightarrow CF_{*}(H,J)
.
We remark that spectral invariants are defined for general quantum homology classes, but we consider only the spectral invariant with respect to the fundamental class.
Spectral invariants have several useful properties, let us state the relevant ones:

•

(stability) For any Hamiltonians
H
and
G
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\min_{x\in M}(H(x,t)-G(x,t))dt\leq c(H)-c(G)\leq\int_{0}^{1}\max_{x\in M}(H(x,t)-G(x,t))dt.

In particular,
c:{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M\times S^{1})\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous functional and is extended by continuity to degenerate Hamiltonians. Moreover, this implies that the spectral invariant is monotone: If
G(x,t)\leq H(x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in M\times S^{1}
, then
c(G)\leq c(H)
.

•

(spectrality)
c(H)\in{\operatorname{Spec}}(H)
. Moreover, if
H
is non-degenerate,
c(H)\in{\operatorname{Spec}}_{n}(H)
.

•

(subadditivity) For every Hamiltonians
H
and
G
, one has
c(H\#G)\leq c(H)+c(G)
, where
H\#G:=H+G\circ(\varphi_{H}^{t})^{-1}
. In particular, if
H
and
G
are disjointly supported then
c(H+G)\leq c(H)+c(G)
.

•

(energy-capacity inequality) If the support of
H
is displaceable, its spectral invariant is bounded by the displacement energy of the support, namely,
c(H)\leq e(supp(H))
.
We remind that a subset
X\subset M
is displaceable if there exists a Hamiltonian
G
such that
\varphi_{G}^{1}(X)\cap X=\emptyset
. In this case, the displacement energy of
X
is given by

e(X):=\inf_{G:\varphi_{G}^{1}(X)\cap X=\emptyset}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\max_{M}G(\cdot,t)-\min_{M}G(\cdot,t)\right)\ dt.

(10)

For a wider exposition see, for example,
[
12
,
17
]
.
A standard method for estimating the spectral invariant of a Hamiltonian
H
is through a
bifurcation diagram
. Given a continuous deformation
\{H_{\tau}\}_{\tau}
of
H
, the corresponding bifurcation diagram is the set
\cup_{\tau}\left(\{\tau\}\times{\operatorname{Spec}}(H_{\tau})\right)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. By the spectrality and stability properties, the spectral invariant of
H_{\tau}
moves continuously in the diagram as
\tau
varies. Therefore, if we construct a deformation such that the value of the spectral invariant is known at a certain point of the deformation, we can study the bifurcation diagram in order to estimate
c(H)
. This approach was used by Polterovich in
[
16
]
, by Seyfaddini in
[
20
]
and by Ishikawa in
[
11
]
, and is frequently used in the present paper as well.

2.3
Contact hyper-surfaces, the Reeb flow and Liouville domains.

As mentioned earlier, a domain
U\subset M
has a
contact type boundary
if there exists a vector field
Y
, called
the Liouville vector field
, that is defined on a neighborhood of the boundary
\partial U
, satisfies
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y}\omega=\omega
, is transverse to the boundary and points outwards. The flow
\psi^{s}
of
Y
defines a radial coordinate, called the
Liouville coordinate
, on a tubular neighborhood of the boundary, by

\partial U\times(1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon)\cong{\mathcal{N}}(\partial U),\ \ (y,s)\mapsto x=\psi^{\log(s)}y.

(11)

Note that the Liouville flow expands the symplectic form, namely
\left(\psi^{\log(s)}\right)^{*}\omega=s\cdot\omega
.
The 1-form
\lambda:=\iota_{Y}\omega
is a primitive of
\omega
and the kernel of its restriction to
T\partial U
, namely
\xi:=\ker\lambda|_{T\partial U}
, is called the
contact distribution
. We denote by
R
the
Reeb vector field
, which is defined on a neighborhood of

\partial U
by

\lambda(R)=1,\ R_{(y,s)}\in\ker d\lambda_{(y,s)}|_{T\psi^{\log(s)}\partial U}.

(12)

We remark that the Liouville vector field is not unique (and hence so are the 1-form
\lambda
and the Reeb vector field). In fact, for every
C^{1}
-small Hamiltonian
H
defined near
\partial U
,
Y^{\prime}:=Y-X_{H}
is also a Liouville vector field.
If the vector filed
Y
extends to
U
(and satisfies
{\mathcal{L}}_{Y}\omega=\omega
there), we say that
U
is a
Liouville domain
.

Denote by
\varphi_{R}^{t}:\partial U\rightarrow\partial U
the flow of the Reeb vector field, and let
{\mathcal{P}}(\partial U)
be the set of all contractible periodic orbits of
\varphi_{R}^{t}
(of any period). The action of such orbits is defined to be the integral of the 1-form
\lambda
along the orbit, and coincides with the period. The set of action values is called
the Reeb spectrum
of
\partial U
and is denoted by
{\operatorname{Spec}}(\partial U)
. We say that the Reeb flow is
non-degenerate
if the graph of the restriction of
d\varphi_{R}^{t}
to the contact distribution
\xi
intersects the diagonal in
\xi\times\xi
transversely. In this case, for each orbit
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(\partial U)
and a capping disk
u\subset\partial U
, one can assign an integer, which we call the contact Conley-Zehnder index and denote
7
7

7

We add the subscript
R
to the notation in order to distinguish the contact CZ index from the Hamiltonian CZ index that appeared earlier.
by
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma,u)
, in the following way. Trivializing the contact distribution
\xi
along the disk
u
, the restriction of the linearized flow
d\varphi_{R}^{t}
to
\xi
along
\gamma
is identified with a path of symplectic matrices,
d\varphi_{R}^{t}|_{\xi}(\gamma(0))\mapsto\Phi(t)\in{\operatorname{Sp}}(2n-2)
, see e.g.,
[
1
]
. The index

is defined to be the RS index of the path
\Phi
, as in (

).

2.3.1
Portable Liouville domains.

As mentioned earlier, a Liouville domain
U
is called portable if its core, which is given by
Q:=\cap_{s\in(0,1]}\psi^{\log(s)}U
, is displaceable in
U
. In this case, the displacement energy of
\psi^{\log(s)}U
is arbitrarily small as
s

[
16

3
Constructing spectral killers.

In
[
20
]
, Seyfaddini presented a construction of certain functions, called
spectral killers
, which can be used to produce upper bounds for the spectral invariant of a sum of disjointly supported Hamiltonians. A spectral killer for a Hamiltonian
H
supported in a domain
U
is a function
K:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
supported in
U
such that
c(H+K)=0
.

Claim 3.1

(Seyfaddini)
.

Let
H_{1},\dots,H_{N}
be Hamiltonians supported in pairwise disjoint domains
U_{1},\dots,U_{N}\subset M
and suppose there exist Hamiltonians
K_{i}
supported in
U_{i}
, such that
c(H_{i}+K_{i})=0
. Then,

c(H_{1}+\cdots+H_{N})\leq\max_{i}\|K_{i}\|_{C^{0}}.

(13)

Proof.

The following argument is taken from
[
20
]
. Using the stability and subadditivity properties of spectral invariants and noticing that
\{H_{i}+K_{i}\}
are all disjointly supported, we have

\displaystyle c(H_{1}+\cdots+H_{N})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\sum_{i}(H_{i}+K_{i})\right)+\Big\|-\sum_{i}K_{i}\Big\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i}c(H_{i}+K_{i})+\Big\|\sum_{i}K_{i}\Big\|_{C^{0}}=\sum_{i}0+\max_{i}\|K_{i}\|_{C^{0}}.

∎

In
[
20
]
, Seyfaddini constructed spectral killers for Hamiltonians supported in displaceable balls having small displacement energies in monotone manifolds. The norms of the spectral killers are bounded by the capacities of the balls.

In order to obtain the max inequality (
1
) we will construct spectral killers whose norms are equal to the spectral invariants of the Hamiltonians
\{H_{i}\}
whenever
c(H_{i})>0
. Note that for Hamiltonians with non-positive spectral invariants the max inequality follows from the subadditivity property of spectral invariants.
In this section we show that under a certain condition on the domain
U
and the spectral invariant
c(H)
of
H
, there exists such a spectral killer
K
. We begin with a simpler construction of what we call a “slow spectral killer”, which is supported on a domain larger than
U
.

3.1
A simpler case: “slow spectral killers”.

In this section we use the notion of
\sigma
-extendable domains from Definition
1.1
. Our goal is to prove the following statement.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a rational symplectic manifold, namely
\omega(\pi_{2}(M))=\kappa\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}
, and let
U\subset M
be a
\sigma
-extendable domain with an incompressible contact type boundary. Then, for every Hamiltonian
H
supported in
U
such that

0\leq c(H)<\min\{\kappa,\sigma\cdot\min{\operatorname{Spec}}(\partial U)\}

(14)

there exists
K:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
supported in
(1+\sigma)U
with
\|K\|_{C^{0}}=c(H)
such that
c(H+K)=0
.

The above proposition guarantees that the max inequality (
1
) holds for Hamiltonians supported in disjoint extendable domains that satisfy (
14
), only if we assume in addition that the supports
(1+\sigma_{i})U_{i}
of the spectral killers are disjoint. In particular, Theorem
1
is an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.2
and Claim
3.1
:

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
\{H_{i}\}
be Hamiltonians supported in domains
\{U_{i}\}
with incompressible contact type boundaries such that
U_{i}
is
\sigma_{i}
-extendable, the sets
\{(1+\sigma_{i})U_{i}\}
are pairwise disjoint, and
c(H_{i})<\min\{\kappa,\sigma_{i}\cdot\min{\operatorname{Spec}}(\partial U_{i})\}
. By Proposition
3.2
there exist
K_{i}:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
supported in
(1+\sigma_{i})U_{i}
such that
\|K_{i}\|_{C^{0}}=c(H_{i})
and
c(H_{i}+K_{i})=0
. Applying Claim
3.1
to
\{H_{i}\}
and
\{K_{i}\}
with the disjoint domains
\{(1+\sigma_{i})U_{i}\}
, we conclude that the max inequality (
1
) holds for
\{H_{i}\}
.
∎

In order to prove Proposition
3.2
we need some preliminary notations and calculations.
We use the notations of Section
2
and, in particular, the Liouville coordinate
s
defined in (
11
), using the Liouville vector field.
The spectral killer
K
will be a function of the Liouville coordinate. This will enable us to relate its 1-periodic orbits to the Reeb orbits on
\partial U
.

Definition 3.3
.

We say that an autonomous Hamiltonian
H
is
radial
if there exists
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
such that
H=\chi(s)
wherever the the Liouville coordinate is defined, and is locally constant elsewhere.

Radial Hamiltonians and their periodic orbits were studied in
[
9
,
11
]
for the case where
U
is a strictly convex domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. The following lemma relates between the 1-periodic orbits of radial Hamiltonians and the Reeb orbits on
\partial U
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
H=\chi(s)
be a radial Hamiltonian, then the Hamiltonian flow of
H
is conjugated to the Reeb flow up to a time reparametrization:

\varphi_{H}^{t}=\psi^{\log s}\circ\varphi_{R}^{\chi^{\prime}(s)\cdot t}\circ\psi^{-\log s}.

(15)

In particular, every non-constant 1-periodic orbit
\gamma
of
H
is contained in a level set of the Liouville coordinate
s=s(\gamma)
and is conjugated to a periodic Reeb orbit,
\hat{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\partial U)
, via
\hat{\gamma}(\chi^{\prime}(s)\cdot t)=\psi^{-\log s}\gamma(t)
. Moreover, the Reeb action of
\hat{\gamma}
is equal to the absolute value of the derivative of
\chi
at
s=s(\gamma)
:

\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda=|\chi^{\prime}(s)|.

(16)

In particular,
|\chi^{\prime}(s)|
belongs to the Reeb spectrum of
\partial U
.

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Let us prove the following relation between the Hamiltonian vector field
X_{H}
of
H
and the Reeb vector field:

d\psi^{-\log s}X_{H}\circ\psi^{\log s}=\chi^{\prime}(s)\cdot R.

(17)

Note that (
15
) will follow from uniqueness of solutions of ODEs. Let us show that the LHS of (
17
) satisfies the equations defining the Reeb vector field with a factor of
\chi^{\prime}(s)
. Given any vector
v\in T_{x}M
for
x\in\partial U
,

\displaystyle\omega_{x}(v,d\psi^{-\log s}X_{H}\circ\psi^{\log s}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\psi^{-\log s}_{x})^{*}\omega_{\psi^{\log s}x}(d\psi^{\log s}v,X_{H})

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\omega_{\psi^{\log s}x}(d\psi^{\log s}v,X_{H})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}dH_{\psi^{\log s}x}(d\psi^{\log s}v).

When
v\in T\partial U
, its image under the linearized Liouville flow,
d\psi^{\log s}v
, is tangent to a level set of the Liouville coordinate
s
and hence to a level set of
H
. In this case,

\omega_{x}(v,d\psi^{-\log s}X_{H}\circ\psi^{\log s}(x))=s^{-1}dH_{\psi^{\log s}x}(d\psi^{\log s}v)=0

On the other hand, taking
v
to be the Liouville vector field, equation (
18

\displaystyle\omega_{x}(Y,d\psi^{-\log s}X_{H}\circ\psi^{\log s}(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}dH_{\psi^{\log s}x}(d\psi^{\log s}Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big|_{\tau=0}H\circ\psi^{\log(s)}\psi^{\tau}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big|_{\tau=0}H\circ\psi^{\log(s\cdot e^{\tau})}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\cdot\frac{d}{d\tau}\Big|_{\tau=0}\chi(s\cdot e^{\tau})=\chi^{\prime}(s).

Having established the conjugation of the Hamiltonian and the Reeb flows (
15
), we turn to prove the relation between the Reeb action and the derivative of
\chi
stated in equation (
16
). For a 1-periodic orbit
\gamma
of
H
lying in level
s
, let
\hat{\gamma}\subset\partial U
be the curve defined by
\hat{\gamma}(\chi^{\prime}(s)\cdot t)=\psi^{-\log s}\gamma(t)
. Then, using (
15

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\gamma}(t)

\frac{1}{2}\dim\ker(d\varphi_{H}^{1}(\gamma(0))-{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}})
. The second assertion of Lemma
3.9
states that the kernel of
d\varphi_{H}^{1}(\gamma(0))-{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}}
is 1-dimensional if
\chi^{\prime\prime}(s)\neq 0
. Therefore,
|{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma^{\prime},u_{0}^{\prime})-{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, where
u_{0}^{\prime}
is a capping disk of
\gamma^{\prime}
that is contained in a small neighborhood of
u_{0}
. We conclude that

-n={\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma^{\prime},u^{\prime})={\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma^{\prime},u_{0}^{\prime})+2c_{1}(A)\leq{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})+\frac{1}{2}+2c_{1}(A),

and, together with (
28
), this yields that

c_{1}(A)\geq\frac{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n-1}{2}.

Having a lower bound for the Chern class of
A
, it remains to use the monotonicity of
M
to bound the action of the index
-n
capped orbit
(\gamma^{\prime},u^{\prime})
. Since the action
(\gamma^{\prime},u^{\prime})
is close to
{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\gamma,u)
, we will estimate the latter. Recalling the formula for the action of orbits of radial Hamiltonians, which was established in Corollary
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\gamma,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{\tau}(s)-s\cdot\chi_{\tau}^{\prime}(s)-\omega(A)\approx 0-\tau(1-\delta)\cdot\chi_{\tau}^{\prime}(s)-\omega(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(1-\delta)\cdot\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-\omega(A)\leq(1-\delta)\cdot\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-\omega(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\cdot\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-\kappa\cdot c_{1}(A)

\displaystyle{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\cdot\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-\kappa\cdot\left(\frac{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n-1}{2}\right).

Our assumption that
\partial U
is dynamically convex implies that
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})\geq n+1
. Since
C(U)
is assumed to be not-greater than
\kappa
, this yields

{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\gamma,u)\leq(1-\delta)\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-C(U)\cdot\left(\frac{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n-1}{2}\right).

The next step is to bound the contact CZ index by the Reeb action divided by
C(U)
. Recall that
C(U)
was defined as the supremum over ratios
{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}/\left({{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n+1}\right)
. Therefore,
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n-1\geq{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}/{C(U)}-2
, and we can bound the action by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\gamma,u)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-C(U)\cdot\left(\frac{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n-1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-C(U)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{C(U)}-2\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\delta)\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda-\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda+C(U)\leq C(U)-\delta\min{\operatorname{Spec}}(\partial U).

This finishes the proof, as the first step guaranteed that the spectral invariant
c(H)
is not-greater than the maximal action of such a capped orbit. In particular, we conclude that
c(H)<C(U)
as required.
∎

5.2
Negatively monotone manifolds.

In
[
16
, Proposition 5.4]
Polterovich proved that on an aspherical manifold
M
, the spectral invariant of every Hamiltonian supported in a disjoint union
U
of portable Liouville domains is bounded by the portability number
p(U)
. Theorem
6
asserts that this holds true for negatively monotone manifolds, if one demands in addition that
\partial U
is dynamically convex. As before, the Liouville coordinate of the disjoint union is given be the Liouville coordinate on each connected component.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

Following
[
16
]
, the idea is to use
symplectic contraction
and follow the spectral invariant in the corresponding bifurcation diagram. The symplectic contraction of a Hamiltonian
H
supported in the Liouville domain
U
is defined to be

H_{\tau}:=\begin{cases}\tau\cdot H\circ\psi^{-\log\tau}&\text{ on }\psi^{\log\tau}U,\\
0&\text{ on }M\setminus\psi^{\log\tau}U\end{cases}

for
\tau\in[0,1]
, see Figure
9
for an illustration.

Figure 9:
Symplectic contraction of a radial non-increasing Hamiltonian
H
.

The flow of the contracted Hamiltonian
H_{\tau}
is the composition of the Liouville flow on flow of
H
, namely,
\varphi_{H_{\tau}}^{t}=\psi^{\log\tau}\varphi_{H}^{t}
.
In the aspherical case there are no intersections in the bifurcation diagram corresponding to this deformation, and the spectral invariant changes linearly,
c(H_{\tau})=\tau\cdot c(H)
. However, on monotone manifolds, there could be a lot of intersections and the change of
c(H_{\tau})
is in general more complicated. To simplify the situation, we consider only non-increasing radial Hamiltonians. The claim for general Hamiltonians will follow from the monotonicity property of spectral invariants. Let
H:=\chi(s)
where
s
is the Liouville coordinate on
U
and
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a non-increasing function that vanishes on
s\geq 1
and is constant near
s=0
. We assume that the second derivative of
\chi
does not vanish on level sets containing 1-periodic orbits, i.e., whenever
|\chi^{\prime}(s)|\in{\operatorname{Spec}}(\partial U)
. Let us compute the change in the action spectrum when symplectically contracting
H
. The non-constant 1-periodic orbits of
H
and
H_{\tau}
are in bijection: for
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
, its image
\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma
under the Liouville flow is a 1-periodic orbit of
H_{\tau}
. Given a capping disk
u_{0}\subset\{s=s(\gamma)\}
of
\gamma
, its image
\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0}
is a capping disk of
\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma
. The action of
(\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma,\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0})
with respect to
H_{\tau}
is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma,\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma)-\int\left(\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0}\right)^{*}\omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\cdot H(\gamma)-\tau\cdot\int u_{0}^{*}\omega=\tau\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u_{0}).

In addition, the RS indices of these capped orbits are equal. Indeed, given a trivialization
T:u_{0}^{*}TM\rightarrow D^{2}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
, we can define a trivialization along
\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0}
by
T\circ d\psi^{-\log\tau}
. Under these trivializations, the differentials of the flows coincide and thus they have the same RS index.
More generally, if
u
is any capping of
\gamma
, we may write
u=u_{0}\#A
for
A\in\pi_{2}(M)
and then
(\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0})\#A
is a capping of
\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma
. In that case,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma,(\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0})\#A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{H_{\tau}}(\psi^{\log\tau}\gamma,\psi^{\log\tau}u_{0})-\omega(A)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\cdot{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u_{0})-\omega(A).

If follows that the corresponding bifurcation diagram consists of lines whose slopes equal to
{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u_{0})
for
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
and
u_{0}\subset\{s=s(\gamma)\}
and whose starting points (that is, the values at
\tau=0
) are
-\omega(A)
. After perturbing
H
into a non-degenerate Hamiltonian, it is possible to choose non-degenerate perturbations of
H_{\tau}
such that 1-periodic orbits will still be in bijection with the same relations for the actions and indices. After this perturbation the spectral invariant will lie in the spectrum of index
-n
capped orbits.
Our next goal is to show that the slopes and the starting points are all non-negative, as illustrated in Figure
10
.

Figure 10:
An illustration of the bifurcation diagram for symplectic contraction of a non-increasing radial Hamiltonian on a dynamically convex domain. When all slopes and starting points are non-negative, the spectral invariant
c(H_{\tau})
can only move along lines of slope not greater than the initial slope.

This will allow us to estimate the path of the spectral invariant in the bifurcation diagram. We start with the slopes, which corresponds to actions of orbits
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(H)
with capping disks
u_{0}
that are contained in the Liouville level set:

•

{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u_{0})\geq 0
:

Since we assumed
\chi
to be non-increasing, the non-negativity of the slopes in the bifurcation diagram follows immediately from the formula for actions of orbits of radial Hamiltonians, that was stated in Corollary
3.8

,
A=0

{\mathcal{A}}_{H}(\gamma,u_{0})=\chi(s)-s\cdot\chi^{\prime}(s)\geq 0.

•

-\omega(A)\geq 0
:
The non-negativity of the stating points requires index computations.
We start by recalling that the index difference between the perturbed and original orbits is bounded by half the dimension of
\ker(d\varphi_{H}^{1}(\gamma(0))-{1\hskip-2.5pt{\rm l}})
, as stated in (
22
).
Due to our choice of

does not vanish whenever

and Lemma

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})-{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma^{\prime},u_{0}^{\prime})|=|{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})-{\operatorname{CZ}}_{H}(\gamma^{\prime},u^{\prime})+2c_{1}(A)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})+n+2c_{1}(A)|,

where

Next, we use Lemma

of slope bigger than

. Thus, when starting on the line

can only move along lines of slope

for all

. To conclude a bound for the spectral invariant of

.

6
Estimating the invariant
C(U)
in special cases.

In this section we provide upper bounds for the invariant
C
from Definition
1.3
on several classes of domains. The domains considered here are all topological balls, which means that their boundaries are aspherical,
\pi_{2}(S^{2n-1})=0
. As a consequence, the contact CZ index is independent of the choice of a capping disk, and we will use the notation
{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma)

Let us start with the simplest example, a generic ellipsoid.

Example 6.1
.

Let
U=E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\pi|z_{j}|^{2}}{a_{j}}\leq 1\}
be an ellipsoid such that

is irrational when
i\neq j
. The periodic Reeb orbits on
\partial E
are
\gamma_{k,\ell}:=\{e^{2\pi it/a_{k}}z_{k}\}_{t\in[0,a_{k}\ell]}
for

and
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{>0}
. The action (or period) of
\gamma_{k,\ell}
is

and its CZ index (with respect to any capping disk

see e.g.
[
8
, p.16]

Wider classes of examples that are considered below are convex and concave toric domains. Since the Reeb flow on toric domains is degenerate (unless the domain is an ellipsoid), we need to extend Definition
1.3
of the invariant

to degenerate domains.

Definition 6.2
.

Let
U\subset M
be a domain with an incompressible contact type boundary.

•

Assume that the Reeb flow on
\partial U
is non-degenerate. For every
T>0
, define

C_{T}(U):=\sup\left\{\frac{2\int_{\gamma}\lambda}{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma,u_{0})-n+1}\ :\ \gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(U),\ \int_{\gamma}\lambda\leq T,\ u_{0}\subset\partial U\right\},

and notice that
C(U)=\sup_{T>0}C_{T}(U)
.

•

When the Reeb flow on
\partial U
is degenerate, we define

\displaystyle C_{T}(U):=\liminf_{U^{\prime}\rightarrow U}C_{T}(U^{\prime}),\quad C(U):=\sup_{T>0}C_{T}(U),

where the limit is over domains

with incompressible contact type boundaries and non-degenerate Reeb flows that

-converge to
U
.

6.1
Convex and concave toric domains.

Denote by
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\geq 0}
the set of points

with non-negative entries,
x_{i}\geq 0
. Consider the moment map

(that is, a connected set that is open in the topology of

•

If

.

•

If

{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma)=1+2=3
.
As a result, we obtain a domain
U
with a contact type boundary and non-degenerate flow, that is contained in
E
and is Hausdorff-close to it, such that

C(U)\geq\frac{2\int_{\gamma}\lambda}{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma)-n+1}\approx\frac{2a_{2}^{\prime}}{3-2+1}=a_{2}^{\prime}\approx a_{2}.

By Example
6.1
,
C(E)=a_{1}
and we conclude that
C
is neither monotone nor Hausdorff-continuous.

We turn to prove Claim
6.3
.

Proof of Claim
6.3
.

By Definition
6.2
, it is enough to prove the upper bound for
C_{T}(U)
, for arbitrary
T>0
.
In
[
8
, p.22-23]
, Gutt and Hutchings show that one can perturb
U
into a domain with a contact type boundary and non-degenerate flow, such that, after the perturbation, every Reeb orbit
\gamma\in{\mathcal{P}}(\partial U)
either has a large contact CZ index (in which case, either the action is larger than
T
, or the ratio in the definition of
C(U)
is small) or corresponds to a vector
v\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}_{>0}
, such that

\int_{\gamma}\lambda\leq[v]_{\Omega}+\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad 1-n+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\leq{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\gamma)\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i},

where
[v]_{\Omega}:=\inf\{\left<v,w\right>:w\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}
and
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrarily small.
As a consequence,

C_{T}(U)\leq\sup\left\{\frac{[v]_{\Omega}+\varepsilon}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}-n+1}\ \Big|\ v\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}_{>0}\right\}.

(33)

Fix
\hat{v}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}_{>0}
such that
C_{T}(U)\leq\frac{[\hat{v}]_{\Omega}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{v}_{i}-n+1}+2\varepsilon
. Consider the ellipsoid defined by

E:=\mu^{-1}\left(\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{\geq 0}:\left<x,\hat{v}\right><[\hat{v}]_{\Omega}\}\right),

then
E\subset U
and
[\hat{v}]_{\mu(E)}=[\hat{v}]_{\Omega}
. Let
0<a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}
such that
E=E(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
, then

\displaystyle C_{T}(U)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{[\hat{v}]_{\Omega}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{v}_{i}-n+1}+2\varepsilon=\frac{[\hat{v}]_{\mu(E)}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{v}_{i}-n+1}+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\min_{i}a_{i}\hat{v}_{i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{v}_{i}-n+1}+2\varepsilon\leq\frac{\min_{i}a_{i}\hat{v}_{i}}{\max_{i}\hat{v}_{i}}+2\varepsilon\leq a_{1}+2\varepsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{G}(E)+2\varepsilon\leq c_{G}(U)+2\varepsilon.

∎

6.2
Strictly convex domains: comparison to Ishikawa’s constant.

In this section, we show that when
U
is strictly convex,
C(U)<\infty
. This follows from computations carried by Ishikawa in
[
11
]
. On strictly convex domains, Ishikawa bounded the ratio between the action and CZ index of 1-periodic orbits of radial Hamiltonians in terms of the sectional curvatures of the boundary.

Definition 6.8

(
[
11
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be a strictly convex open set with a smooth boundary, such that
0\in U
. Denote by
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
the squared semi-norm associated to
U
, namely
f(ty)=t^{2}
for
y\in\partial U
.
Define

\hat{C}_{0}(U):=\inf_{V\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n},a>0}\frac{2\pi}{a}

where the infimum is taken over all one-dimensional complex subspaces
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
and
a>0
such that
D^{2}f(x)>a|_{V\times V}\oplus 0|_{V^{\perp}\times V^{\perp}}
, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
.

Roughly speaking,
\hat{C}_{0}(U)
is
2\pi
over the maximum over all complex planes of the minimal sectional curvature in
\partial U
, restricted to the plane. In particular, it is finite for every strictly convex domain.

Claim 6.9
.

Let
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
be a strictly convex open set with a smooth boundary, such that
0\in U
. Then,
C(U)\leq\hat{C}_{0}(U)
.

Proof.

Let
\hat{\gamma}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\partial U)
be a periodic Reeb orbit and denote its action by
\alpha:=\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda>0
. In what follows we show that the ratio
2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda/\left({\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma})-n+1\right)
in the definition of
C(U)
is bounded by
\hat{C}_{0}(U)
.
The argument goes through radial Hamiltonians in order to use a result from
[
11
]
.
Consider a radial Hamiltonian
H
that is linear with slope
-\alpha
with respect to the Liouville coordinate near the boundary of
U
, i.e.,
H|_{{\mathcal{N}}(\partial U)}=-\alpha\cdot s=-\alpha\cdot f
, where
f
is the squared semi-norm associated to
U
, as in Definition
6.8
. Lemma
3.4
states that
\gamma(t)=\hat{\gamma}(\chi^{\prime}(s)\cdot t)=\hat{\gamma}(-\alpha\cdot t)
is a 1-periodic orbit of the Hamiltonian flow of
H
.
The linearized flow
\Phi(t):=d\varphi_{H}^{t}(\gamma(0))
of
H
along
\gamma
satisfies the equation

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(t)=J_{0}D^{2}H(\gamma(t))\cdot\Phi(t)=-\alpha J_{0}D^{2}f(\gamma(t))\cdot\Phi(t).

Lemma 3.1, item (ii) from
[
11
]
states that, given a complex plane
V\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and a path
\{\Phi(t)\}
of symplectic matrices satisfying
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(t)=J_{0}S(t)\Phi(t)
for
S(t)\leq-c|_{V\times V}\oplus 0|_{V^{\perp}\times V^{\perp}}
, its RS index is bounded by
11
11

11

Lemma 3.1 from
[
11
]
is formulated for the
\max{\operatorname{CZ}}
, which was defined by Ishikawa on p.8. It follows from (
22
) that in our case
\max{\operatorname{CZ}}(\gamma,u_{0})={\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})+1
.

RS(\Phi)\leq-n-2[c/2\pi]^{<}-1,

where
[x]^{<}
stands for the largest integer that is smaller than
x
.
Applying this statement to
S(t)=-\alpha D^{2}f(\gamma(t))
and
c=\alpha\cdot a
, it follows that the RS index of the orbit
\gamma
, with respect a capping disk
u_{0}\subset\{s=s(\gamma)\}
is bounded by

{\operatorname{RS}}(\gamma,u_{0})\leq-n-2\Big[\frac{\alpha\cdot a}{2\pi}\Big]^{<}-1\leq-n-2\Big[\frac{\alpha}{\hat{C}_{0}(U)}\Big]^{<}-1.

(34)

By Lemma
3.9
, the RS index of
(\gamma,u_{0})
is equal to minus the contact CZ index of
\hat{\gamma}
. Therefore, inequality (
34
) yields

{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma})\geq n+2\Big[\frac{\alpha}{\hat{C}_{0}(U)}\Big]^{<}+1=n+2\Big[\frac{\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{\hat{C}_{0}(U)}\Big]^{<}+1.

As the above inequality holds for every Reeb orbit, we can use it to bound the invariant
C(U)
as follows:

\displaystyle C(U)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\hat{\gamma}}\frac{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{{\operatorname{CZ}}_{R}(\hat{\gamma},\hat{u}_{0})-n+1}\leq\sup_{\hat{\gamma}}\frac{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{n+2\Big[\frac{\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{\hat{C}_{0}(U)}\Big]^{<}+1-n+1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\hat{\gamma}}\frac{2\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{2\cdot\frac{\int_{\hat{\gamma}}\lambda}{\hat{C}_{0}(U)}-2+2}=\hat{C}_{0}(U),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
[x]^{<}\geq x-1
.
∎

Constraining λ B s \lambda_{B_{s}} by B s → γ ∗ B_{s}\to\gamma^{*} and B s → ϕ B_{s}\to\phi form factors
Mikhail A. Ivanov a , Dmitri Melikhov a,b,c , and Silvano Simula d

Abstract
We calculate the form factors F V ​ ( q 2 , q ′ 2 ) F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}) and F T ​ V ​ ( q 2 , q ′ 2 ) F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}) describing the B s → γ ∗ B_{s}\to\gamma^{*} transition induced by the vector and tensor weak currents in a broad range of values of q 2 q^{2} and q ′ 2 q^{\prime 2} far below the quark thresholds in both q 2 q^{2} and q ′ 2 q^{\prime 2} channels. These form factors are calculated via the distribution amplitudes of the B s B_{s} -meson. We then interpolate the obtained results by a formula that contains pole at q ′ 2 = M ϕ 2 q^{\prime 2}=M_{\phi}^{2} and extract the residue which gives the B s → ϕ B_{s}\to\phi transition form factors V ⁡ ( q 2 ) V(q^{2}) and T 1 ​ ( q 2 ) T_{1}(q^{2}) . In this way we obtain theoretical predictions for these form factors without invoking quark-hadron duality and QCD sum rules. Furthermore, we calculate the relationship between V ⁡ ( 0 ) V(0) and T 1 ​ ( 0 ) T_{1}(0) and the parameter λ B s ​ ( μ ) \lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu) , the inverse moment of the B s B_{s} -meson distribution amplitude. Using the available predictions for V ⁡ ( 0 ) V(0) and T 1 ​ ( 0 ) T_{1}(0) coming from approaches not referring to the B s B_{s} -meson distribution amplitudes, we obtain the estimate λ B s ​ ( μ ≃ m b ) = ( 0.62 ± 0.10 ) \lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu\simeq m_{b})=(0.62\pm 0.10) GeV.

In this paper, we discuss a new way of constraining
\lambda_{B_{s}}
.

The first step is the calculation of the form factors
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})

14

describing the
B_{s}\to\gamma^{*}
transition (see Fig. 1) in a broad
range of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
far below quark thresholds via
the 2DAs of the
B_{s}
-meson. For the relevant 2DAs we make use of the local duality (LD) model of
[
2
]
.

Then, we interpolate these results by an analytic formula which contains a number of poles
corresponding to vector meson resonances with quantum numbers appropriate to
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}

channels. This formula is based on a dispersion representation for
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in
q^{\prime 2}

with one subtraction
[
16
]
and the spectral density of this representation
is saturated by two resonances - the known lowest resonances
\phi

The numerical parameters in this analytic formula are obtained by fitting the results of our calculations
in a broad range of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
far below the quark thresholds in
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}

The analytic formula contains a pole at
q^{\prime 2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
; the residue of this pole
provides the
B_{s}\to\phi
form factor
V(q^{2})
in the case of
F_{V}
and
T_{1}(q^{2})
in the case of
F_{TV}
.
Noteworthy, our estimate does not employ quark-hadron duality and QCD sum rules and is therefore free
of the intrinsic uncertainties of the method of QCD sum rules. Our approach thus provides an interesting
complementary method to the widely used method of QCD sum rules.

For comparison, we present in Appendix
A
the application of the standard QCD sum rule machinery

[
17
]
in order to calculate
T_{1}(0)
using the
B_{s}
-meson 2DAs
[
18
]
.
We recall that QCD sum rules calculate the residue from the very
same Green function which determines the form factor
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
. However, the QCD sum-rule extraction contains
auxiliary parameters (Borel parameter and effective threshold
s_{\rm eff}
) which are not fixed by any rigorous criteria, leading to
uncertainties that are difficult to control. Still we observe that using some commonly adopted ways of
fixing these auxiliary parameters leads to a good agreement between the estimates for
T_{1}(0)

obtained by a direct calculation of the correlation function and extrapolation to the pole
and by a direct evaluation of the residue by a quark-hadron duality Ansatz.

The approach formulated above is based on using the representations for the form factors

F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
up to leading-order in the strong coupling constant
\alpha_{s}
.
Making use of the known radiative corrections for the form factor
F_{V}(q^{2},0)

[
9
]
,
we give arguments that for the form factors
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
the radiative corrections evaluated at the scale
\mu=m_{b}

are at the level of less than 0.5% in the broad range of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
used in our analysis.
This property means that the leading-order expression for
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
expressed via the
2DAs at the scale
\mu=m_{b}
should give a good estimate for the physical form factors
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}|\mu=m_{b})
.

We then obtain the dependence of the
B_{s}\to\phi
form factors
V(0)
and
T_{1}(0)

on
\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu=m_{b})

V(0)
and
T_{1}(0)
obtained by
approaches not referring to 2DAs of the
B_{s}
-meson, we extract

\displaystyle\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu\simeq m_{b})=(0.62\pm 0.10)\mbox{ GeV}.

2.3
Radiative corrections and constraints on
\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)

It is time to recall that the analysis of the previous Section does not include the radiative corrections,
whereas for the form factors of the tensor current,
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
T_{1}(q^{2})
which depend on the scale
\mu

the radiative corrections are crucial for controlling this scale-dependence. Also for the form factors

F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
V(q^{2})

\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)
.

Two remarks are in order.

\bullet
The radiative corrections are known for the case
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}=0)
as the function of

E_{\gamma}=(M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2})/(2M_{B_{s}})

[
9
]

Thus, for the analysis of the previous Section, the radiative corrections in the form factor

\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu=m_{b})

\bullet

The radiative corrections for the form factor
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
have not yet been calculated for any
values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
. Again, since the behaviours of the form factors and of the radiative correction are determined by the
same denominator appearing in the analytic formulas for
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
, we
conjecture
that
the radiative corrections at
\mu=m_{b}
in the considered range of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
may be neglected too.

Moreover, the form factors of the tensor current depend on the scale,
in particular, one has
F_{TV}(0,0|\mu)

T_{1}(0|\mu)

Our arguments for a negligible size of the radiative corrections at
\mu=m_{b}
imply that the analysis
of the previous Section yields
F_{TV}(0,0|\mu=m_{b})
and
T_{1}(0|\mu=m_{b})
as soon as
the leading-order contributions in
\alpha_{s}

2.4

\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu=m_{b})
.

We would like to emphasize that for our extraction of
\lambda_{B_{S}}(\mu)

\mu=m_{b}

\mu\sim m_{b}

We shall see later (Fig.

\lambda_{B_{S}}(\mu)
in the region
\mu\sim m_{b}

is relatively flat and, therefore, the impact of the precise value of the scale at which the radiative corrections vanish,
has only a moderate impact on, e.g., the extracted value of
\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu=m_{b})
.

3
Discussion and conclusions

A. We have formulated an efficient algorithm of obtaining the form factors
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in a broad range
of momentum transfers including the region of meson resonances where the form factors cannot be calculated
using finite-order QCD diagrams. Our algorithm is based on the following steps:

i. We calculate the form factors
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
at the lowest order in
\alpha_{s}

via the 2DAs of the
B_{s}
meson in a broad range of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
far below quark thresholds where the calculation
based on finite order Feynman diagrams may be trusted. In this way we obtain, in particular,
the dependence of
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
on the parameter
\lambda_{B_{s}}
making use of the lowest order in
\alpha_{s}
.

ii. We interpolate the obtained results by an analytic formula based on dispersion representation
for
F_{V,TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in
q^{\prime 2}
with one subtraction. The considered analytic formula takes into account
the presence of the
\phi
-meson pole in the physical form factor and takes into account contribution of
the excited hadron states via an effective pole whose mass and coupling are fitting parameters.

iii. We obtain predictions for the form factors
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in the physical region
by using the fitting formula in a broad range of momentum transfers including the resonance region.

B. We applied this algorithm and obtained the
B_{s}\to\phi
form factors
V(q^{2})
(
T_{1}(q^{2})
) as a residue of
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})

(
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
) of the pole located at
q^{\prime 2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
without using QCD sum
rules. In this way we avoid the systematic uncertainties related to the method of QCD sum rules
which are not possible to control in a rigorous way

[
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
.
4
4

4

We are not saying that our method avoids systematic uncertainties:
e.g., the residue in the pole has sensitivity to the ranges of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}

where the interpolation is performed.
However, taking into account the precise knowledge of the pole location (i.e. the physical
masses of the lightest vector mesons) reduces this uncertainty considerably.

C. Our analysis is based on the correlation function calculated at leading order in
\alpha_{s}
.
Making use of the fact that the radiative corrections for the form factor
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}=0)

evaluated at the scale
\mu=m_{b}
turn out to be small in a broad range of photon energies
E_{\gamma}
, we have given arguments
that the same property is valid for the form factors
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in a broad range of values of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
far below the quark thresholds,
where our fitting procedure is carried out. In the case of the form factor
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
the radiative corrections are
not known at all, but also in this case we may conjecture that the same property is valid.

We emphasize that it is not necessary that the radiative corrections vanish precisely at the scale
\mu=m_{b}
;
it is sufficient that they vanish at some scale not far from
m_{b}
. Then, the obtained estimate for

\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu=m_{b})
would change only marginally as the dependence of
\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)
in the region
\mu\sim m_{b}

is relatively flat. But, obviously, for putting our analysis on a solid theoretical basis, a direct evaluation of
the radiative corrections for the form factors of the tensor currents would be crucial.

Strictly speaking, the radiative corrections in
F(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
are of course different from those in

F(q^{2},0)
. However, taking into account the arguments given in Section 2.C, we believe that one can draw a reasonable
conclusion about the magnitude of the radiative corrections in
F(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in the interesting range
of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
based on the results for the radiative corrections in
F(q^{2},0)
.
Let us also emphasize that we rather need a qualitative result on the smallness of the radiative corrections
in
F(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
in the interesting range of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
.
Even if the actual size of these corrections in
F(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
is a factor of 2-3 larger than the estimate
obtained on the basis of the known radiative corrections in

F(q^{2},0)
, this will not invalidate our results. To get a better control over
the radiative corrections in
F(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
a calculation of such corrections would be plausible,
which is however a very formidable task.

Given the property that the radiative corrections are small at
\mu=m_{b}
, our leading-order expressions for the form factors
evaluated via the 2DAs at the scale
\mu=m_{b}
, containing
\lambda_{B_{s}}(m_{b})
,
should provide a good approximation for the form factors
F_{V}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and
V(q^{2})
as well as for

F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2}|\mu=m_{b})
and
T_{1}(q^{2}|\mu=m_{b})
.

Then, having at hand the dependence of
T_{1}(0)
and
V(0)
on
\lambda_{B_{s}}(m_{b})
and making use of the theoretical results for

B_{s}\to\phi
form factors
V(0)
and
T_{1}(0|\mu=m_{b})
, obtained by approaches which do not use the 2DAs of
B_{s}
-meson,
we extract the following allowed range s

\displaystyle\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu\simeq m_{b})=0.62\pm 0.10\mbox{ GeV}~,~

(3.13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{B_{s}}(1\mbox{ GeV})=0.49\pm 0.07\mbox{ GeV}.

(3.14)

The latter result agrees nicely with thea recent estimate
\lambda_{B_{s}}(1\mbox{ GeV})=0.48\pm 0.09
GeV
obtained using QCD sum rules in
[
31
]
.

Closing this paper, we believe that the proposed method for extracting the meson-to-meson transition form factor
as a residue of a dominant pole, like
T_{1}(q^{2})
in the form factor
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
, may be promising for calculating
other meson transition form factors and may be used as a complementary method to QCD sum rules.

Appendix A
Form factor
T_{1}(q^{2})
for
B_{s}\to\phi
from QCD sum rule

Here we would like to recall briefly a sum-rule calculation of the form factor
T_{1}(q^{2})
.
The starting point is the same correlation function (
2.2
) and the form factor
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
.
Eq. (
2.4
) based on the calculation of QCD diagrams,
may be identically rewritten in the form of a dispersion representation in
q^{\prime 2}
:

\displaystyle F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})=\int\limits_{0}^{2\omega_{0}}d\xi\frac{\phi_{B_{s}}(\xi)}{1-\xi}\int ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}-q^{\prime 2}}\delta\left(s^{\prime}-\frac{m_{s}^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}\xi}{1-\xi}\right),

(A.15)

where we introduce a short-hand notation

\displaystyle\phi_{B_{s}}(\xi)\equiv-Q_{s}M_{B_{s}}f_{B_{s}}\left[\phi_{+}(\xi)(1-\xi)+\bar{\Phi}(\xi)\right].

(A.16)

We can now make a subtraction in this spectral representation and write:

\displaystyle F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})=F_{TV}(q^{2},0)+q^{\prime 2}\int\limits_{0}^{2\omega_{0}}d\xi\frac{\phi_{B_{s}}(\xi)}{1-\xi}\int ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}-q^{\prime 2})}\delta\left(s^{\prime}-\frac{m_{s}^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}\xi}{1-\xi}\right).

(A.17)

Obviously, the representation (
A.15
) may not be used in the region of
q^{\prime 2}
close to the quark threshold at
4m_{s}^{2}
.
On the other hand, from the general property of the correlation function,
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})

contains a pole at
q^{\prime 2}=M_{\phi}^{2}
.

The dual form factor
T_{1}(0|\tau,\Delta)
, a QCD sum rule estimate for
T_{1}(0)
;
\Delta=0.5
GeV.

We can now equate to each other two different representations for
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
–
the phenomenological side of a QCD sum rule (in the language of hadron states)
and the theoretical side (based on QCD diagrams):

\displaystyle F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R_{\phi}T_{1}(q^{2})\frac{1}{M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}+\ldots

(A.18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{TV}(q^{2},0)+q^{\prime 2}\int\limits_{0}^{1}d\xi\frac{\phi_{B_{s}}(\xi)}{1-\xi}\int ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{s^{\prime}(s^{\prime}-q^{\prime 2})}\delta\left(s^{\prime}-\frac{m_{s}^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}\xi}{1-\xi}\right),

where
\dots
denote the contribution of hadron continuum and the excited resonances.
To isolate the pole at

(i)

Introduce the effective threshold

and apply the duality cut

We shall make use of the standard hypothesis (“Braun prescription”) based on the properties of hadron spectrum:

\displaystyle s_{\rm eff}=(M_{\phi}+\Delta)^{2},\quad\Delta\simeq 500\;{\rm MeV}.

(A.19)

(ii)

Perform Borel transform
q^{\prime 2}\to\tau

(\equiv 1/M_{\rm Borel}^{2})
:

\displaystyle 1/(s^{\prime}-q^{\prime 2})\to\exp(-s^{\prime}\tau),\quad 1/(M_{\phi}^{2}-q^{\prime 2})\to\exp(-M_{\phi}^{2}\tau).

(A.20)

This Borel transform suppresses the contributions of higher-mass resonances on the phenomenological side of the QCD
sum rule for
F_{TV}(q^{2},q^{\prime 2})
and kills all polynomial terms in
q^{\prime 2}
on the OPE side of the QCD sum rule,
including possible subtraction terms.

Using the
\delta
-function to take the

integral, we obtain the dual form factor

from which one should obtain a sum-rule estimate for the physical form factor

\displaystyle T_{1}(q^{2}|\tau,\Delta)=\frac{1}{R_{\phi}}\int\limits_{0}d\xi\frac{\phi_{B_{s}}(\xi)}{1-\xi}\exp\left[\tau\left(M_{\phi}^{2}-\frac{m_{s}^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}\xi}{1-\xi}\right)\right]\theta\left(\frac{m_{s}^{2}+\xi(1-\xi)M_{B_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}\xi}{1-\xi}\leq s_{\rm eff}\right).

(A.21)

Note that the actual upper limit of the
\xi
-integration in Eq. (
A.21
)
is determined by the
\theta
-function (and not by the parameter
2\omega_{0}
expected to be of the order of
\sim 0.5
)
leaving the
\xi
-integration region in the dual form factor closer to zero, i.e.
\xi\leq s_{\rm eff}/M_{B_{s}}^{2}\sim 0.1
.

The dual form factor contains two auxiliary parameters, the effective threshold

\Delta
and the Borel parameter

Appendix B
Radiative corrections to the form factor
F_{V}

The radiative corrections for the tensor current form factors
F_{TV,TA}
are not known.
We therefore refer to the known radiative corrections for the form factors
F_{A,V}

[
9
]
.
The necessary formulas from
[

are reproduced here for the convenience of the reader.

To the leading order in
1/m_{b}
, the form factors
F_{A,V}
have the following behaviour:

\displaystyle F_{A,V}(E_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Q_{u}M_{B_{s}}f_{B_{s}}}{2E_{\gamma}\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)}\,R(E_{\gamma},\mu)+\dots

(B.22)

In Eq. (
B.22
),
f_{B_{s}}
is the physical decay constant of the
B_{s}
-meson in QCD, and since
the l.h.s. does not depend on
\mu

is also
\mu
-independent.

The factor
R(E_{\gamma},\mu)
describing the radiative corrections can be written as a product of several factors,

R(E_{\gamma},\mu)=C(E_{\gamma},\mu_{h1})K^{-1}(\mu_{h2})\times U(E_{\gamma},\mu_{h1},\mu_{h2},\mu)\times J(E_{\gamma},\mu),

(B.23)

which come from the different scales contributing to the process, namely:

\bullet

the factor that emerges due to the replacement of the scale-dependent decay constant in HQET by
the physical decay constant of the
B_{s}
-meson in QCD:

K(\mu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\left(\frac{3}{2}\ln\frac{m_{b}^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-2\right).

(B.24)

\bullet

the factor related to the matching between the weak currents in QCD and SCET

C(E_{\gamma},\mu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\left(-2\ln^{2}\frac{2E_{\gamma}}{\mu}+5\ln\frac{2E_{\gamma}}{\mu}-\frac{3-2x}{1-x}\ln x-2{\rm Li}_{2}(1-x)-6-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right),\quad x=2E_{\gamma}/m_{b}.

(B.25)

\bullet
the hard-collinear radiative correction

\displaystyle J(E_{\gamma},\mu)=1+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{4\pi}\left(\ln^{2}\frac{2E_{\gamma}\mu_{0}}{\mu^{2}}-2\sigma_{1}(\mu,\mu_{0})\ln\frac{2E_{\gamma}\mu_{0}}{\mu^{2}}-1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\sigma_{2}(\mu,\mu_{0})\right)

(B.26)

with the inverse-logarithmic moments defined as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\,\phi_{+}(\omega,\mu),\qquad\sigma_{n}(\mu,\mu_{0})=\lambda_{B_{s}}(\mu)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\omega}{\omega}\,\ln^{n}\frac{\mu_{0}}{\omega}\,\phi_{+}(\omega,\mu)

(B.27)

where
\mu_{0}=1\mbox{ GeV}
is a fixed reference scale which is part of the definition of the inverse-logarithmic
moments adopted in
[
9
]
. Parameter
\omega
in (
B.26
) is related to dimensionless parameter
\xi
in
(
2.6
) as
\omega=M_{B_{s}}\xi
.

In
[
9
]
the authors notice the absence of a common scale

\mu
that avoids parametrically large logarithms of order
\ln m_{b}/\mu_{0}
in (
B.24
), (
B.25
) and (
B.26
)
and introduce the evolution factor

Our observation based on the above formulas and on the explicit form of the 2DAs is however a bit

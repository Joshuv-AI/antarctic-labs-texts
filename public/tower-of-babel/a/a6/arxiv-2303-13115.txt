Blockwise Simple Permutations


Abstract
. A permutation is called block-wise simple if it contains no interval of the form p 1 ⊕ p 2 p_{1}\oplus p_{2} or p 1 ⊖ p 2 p_{1}\ominus p_{2} . We present this new set of permutations and explore some of its combinatorial properties. We present a generating function for this set, as well as a recursive formula for counting block-wise simple permutations. Following Tenner, who founded the notion of interval posets, we characterize and count the interval posets corresponding to block-wise simple permutations. We also present a bijection between these interval posets and certain tiling’s of the n n -gon. Finally, we prove that the bi-variate distribution of the descent and inverse descent numbers are gamma-positive, provided the correctness of our recent conjecture on simple permutations.



Eli Bagno, Estrella Eisenberg, Shulamit Reches and Moriah Sigron

Enumeration of block-wise simple permutations

3.1.
An alternate definition of block-wise simple permutations

Recall Definition
1.1
.
We begin this section with another definition of block-wise simple permutations.

Definition 3.1
.

A permutation
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
is block-wise simple if and only if it has no interval of the form
p_{1}\oplus p_{2}
or
p_{1}\ominus p_{2}

Theorem 3.2
.

The two definitions of block-wise simple permutations are equivalent.

Proof.

If
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
is block-wise simple according to definition
1.1
,
it is clear that
\pi
contains no interval of the form
p_{1}\oplus p_{2}
or
p_{1}\ominus p_{2}
.

On the other hand, let
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n},n>1
, be such that
\pi
has no interval of the form
p_{1}\oplus p_{2}
or
p_{1}\ominus p_{2}
. Based on theorem
2.9
, we can write
\pi=\sigma_{1}[\alpha_{1}^{1},\dots,,\alpha_{k}^{1}]
with
\sigma_{1}\in Simp_{k}
and assume to the contrary that
\pi
is not block-wise simple. Because
\pi
has no intervals of the form
p_{1}\oplus p_{2}
or
p_{1}\ominus p_{2}
, we must have

and there is some

such that
\alpha_{i}^{1}
is not block-wise simple. Assuming without loss of generality that

, we can write
\alpha_{1}^{1}=\sigma_{2}[\alpha_{1}^{2},\dots\alpha_{l}^{2}]
.
As
\pi
has no intervals of the form
p_{1}\oplus p_{2}
or
p_{1}\ominus p_{2}
,
\sigma_{2}
is also simple of order
l\geq 4
. Without loss of generality,
\alpha_{1}^{2}
is not block-wise simple. Proceeding in this manner, we obtain a sequence of non-block-wise simple permutations
(\alpha_{1}^{j})
which are inflation’s of simple permutations, such that the sequence of the orders
(|\alpha_{1}^{j}|)
is strictly decreasing. Hence, some
j
exists, such that
|\alpha_{1}^{j}|=1
whereas
\alpha_{1}^{j}
is not block-wise simple. However, this is a contradiction.
∎

W(x)=x+(S\circ W)(x).

6.
gamma positivity

Eulerian numbers enumerate permutations according to their descent numbers. The descent number of a permutation is defined as follows:

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\pi\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
.

\des(\pi)=|\{i\in[n-1]\mid\pi(i)>\pi(i+1)\}|.

The descent number of the inverse of a permutation
\pi
is defined as

\ides(\pi)=\des(\pi^{-1}).

For example, if
\pi=246135
then the only descent of
\pi
is
3
; thus, we obtain
\des(\pi)=1
, whereas
\ides(\pi)=3
.
Many studies have been made on the distribution of descents on certain subsets of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and on some other permutation groups.

The
two-sided Eulerian numbers
, studied by Carlitz, Roselle, and Scoville
[
12
]
constitute a natural generalization. These numbers count permutations according to their number of descents as well as the number of descents of the inverse permutation.

Gessel conjectured that the corresponding generating function
A_{n}(s,t)=\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}s^{\des(\pi)}t^{ides(\pi)}
has a nice symmetry property, namely, gamma-positivity. His conjecture was proven in
[
6
]
.
To present the idea of gamma-positivity in a formal way, we present some definitions.

A polynomial
f(q)
is
palindromic
if its coefficients are the same when read from left to right and from right to left.
If
f(q)=a_{r}q^{r}+a_{r+1}q^{r+1}+\cdots+a_{s}q^{s}
with
a_{r},a_{s}\neq 0
and
r\leq s
, then
a_{r+i}=a_{s-i}

(\forall i)
; equivalently,

f(q)=q^{r+s}f(1/q)
.

Following Zeilberger
[
8
]
, we define the
darga
of a palindromic polynomial
f(q)
as
r+s
; the zero polynomial is considered palindromic for each non-negative darga.
The set of palindromic polynomials of darga
n-1
is a vector space of dimension
\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor
, with a
gamma basis

\{q^{j}(1+q)^{n-1-2j}\mid 0\leq j\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor\}.

The (one-sided)
Eulerian polynomial

A_{n}(q)=\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}q^{\des(\pi)}

is palindromic of darga
n-1
and thus there are real numbers
\gamma_{n,j}
such that

A_{n}(q)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor}\gamma_{n,j}q^{j}(1+q)^{n-1-2j}.

See
[
7
, pp. 72 - 79]
for details.

For every set
\mathcal{C}
of permutations, we define the
two-sided Eulerian polynomial
of
\mathcal{C}
as

A_{\mathcal{C}}(s,t)=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{C}}s^{\des(\pi)}t^{\ides(\pi)}.

Let
A_{Simp_{n}}(s,t)
and
A_{W_{n}}(s,t)
be the
two-sided Eulerian polynomials for simple permutations and block-wise simple permutations of order
n
, respectively.

For each positive integer
n
, the set
Simp_{n}
of simple permutations of length
n
is invariant under taking inverses and reverses. Consequently, the set
W_{n}
of blockwise simple permutations of length
n
is also invariant under taking inverses and reverses.

Thus, it can be easily proven that the bi-variate polynomial
A_{W_{n}}(s,t)
satisfies

A_{W_{n}}(s,t)=A_{W_{n}}(t,s)

(6)

as well as

A_{W_{n}}(s,t)=(st)^{n-1}A_{W_{n}}(1/s,1/t).

(7)

In fact, (
7
) follows from the bijection from
W_{n}
onto itself, transforming a permutation to its reverse, whereas (
6
) follows from the bijection taking each permutation to its inverse.

A bivariate polynomial satisfying Equations (
6
) and (
7
) is called (bivariate)
palindromic of darga

n-1
.

It can be proven (see
[
7
, pp. 72-79]
) that the set of bivariate palindromic polynomials of darga
n-1
is a vector space of dimension
\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor\cdot\lfloor(n+2)/2\rfloor
with
bivariate gamma basis
:

\{(st)^{i}(s+t)^{j}(1+st)^{n-1-j-2i}\mid i,j\geq 0,\,2i+j\leq n-1\}.

Definition 6.2
.

A bivariate palindromic polynomial is called
gamma-positive
if all coefficients in its expression in terms of the bivariate gamma basis are non-negative integers.

Example 6.3
.

Recall that

(1)

Simp_{4}=\{2413,3142\}
. The two-sided Eulerian polynomial

A_{Simp_{4}}(s,t)=s^{2}t+st^{2}=(st)(s+t)

is gamma-positive.

(2)

The polynomial

A_{Simp_{6}}(s,t)=st(s+t)^{2}(1+st)+5(st)^{2}(1+st)+14(st)^{2}(s+t)

is also gamma-positive.

In our previous paper
[
1
]
we presented the following conjecture and used it for reducing the problem of gamma-positivity of the entire group
\mathcal{S}_{n}
to that of
Simp_{n}
.

Conjecture 6.4
.

For each positive
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the two-sided Eulerian polynomial,

A_{Simp_{n}}(s,t)=\sum_{\sigma\in Simp_{n}}s^{\des(\sigma)}t^{\ides(\sigma)}

is gamma-positive.

Assuming that Conjecture
6.4
holds, we can prove the following theorem:

Theorem 6.5
.

For each
n\geq 1
there exist non-negative integers
\gamma_{n,i,j}

(i,j\geq 0,\,2i+j\leq n-1)
such that

A_{W_{n}}(s,t)=\sum\limits_{i,j}{\gamma_{n,i,j}(st)^{i}(s+t)^{j}(1+st)^{n-1-j-2i}}.

To prove Theorem
6.5
, we require some observations.

Observation 6.6
.

Inflation is additive on both
\des
and
\ides
. Explicitly:

Let
\sigma=\pi[\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{k}]
, then

\des(\sigma)=\des(\pi)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\des(\tau_{i})}

and

\ides(\sigma)=\ides(\pi)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\ides(\tau_{i})}

thus,

s^{\des(\sigma)}t^{\ides(\sigma)}=s^{\des(\pi)}t^{\ides(\pi)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}s^{\des(\tau_{i})}t^{\ides(\tau_{i})}.

Definition 6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subset S_{k}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}
be sets of permutations. Let us define

\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}]=\{\alpha[\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{k}]\mid\alpha\in\mathcal{A},\,\beta_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i}\}.

If
\mathcal{B}_{i}\subset S_{n_{i}}
(
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
), then
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}]\subset S_{n}
where
n=\sum n_{i}
.

Proposition 6.8
.

The two-sided Eulerian polynomial of
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}]
is the product of the two-sided Eulerian polynomials of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}
.

Proof.

From Observation
6.6
, the following can be obtained

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma=\pi[\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{k}]\in\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}]}s^{\des(\sigma)}t^{\ides(\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{A},\tau_{1}\in\mathcal{B}_{1},\dots,\tau_{k}\in\mathcal{B}_{k}}\bigg(s^{\des(\pi)}t^{\ides(\pi)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}s^{\des(\tau_{i})}t^{\ides(\tau_{i})}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{A}}s^{\des(\pi)}t^{\ides(\pi)}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{\tau_{i}\in\mathcal{B}_{i}}s^{\des(\tau_{i})}t^{\ides(\tau_{i})}\bigg)

as required.
∎

Observation 6.9
.

If the two-sided Eulerian polynomials of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}
are gamma positive, then the two-sided Eulerian polynomial of
\mathcal{A}[\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k}]
is also gamma positive.

We proceed to the proof of Theorem
6.5
.

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

Use induction on
n
.
For
n=1
and
n=4
we have
A_{W_{1}}(s,t)=1
and
A_{W_{4}}(s,t)=(st)(s+t)
which are gamma positive.

Let
n>4
and assume that the polynomial
A_{W_{k}}(s,t)
is gamma-positive for every
k<n
. Note that
W_{n}=Simp_{n}\cup U_{n}
where

U_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{4\leq k\leq n-3\atop n_{1}+\cdots n_{k}=n}Simp_{k}[W_{n_{1}}\ldots,W_{n_{k}}]
. Therefore, the two-sided Eulerian polynomial of
W_{n}
is the sum of the two-sided Eulerian polynomial of
Simp_{n}
and the two-sided Eulerian polynomials of
Simp_{k}[W_{n_{1}}\ldots,W_{n_{k}}]
. By Conjecture
6.4
, the induction hypothesis, and Observation
6.9
, these polynomials are gamma-positive and we are done.

∎

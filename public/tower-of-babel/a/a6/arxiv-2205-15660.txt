Comparing orthogonal calculus and calculus with Reality
Niall Taggart

Abstract
. We show that there exists a suitable C 2 C_{2} -fixed points functor from calculus with Reality to the orthogonal calculus of Weiss which recovers orthogonal calculus “up to a shift” in an analogous way with the recovery of real topological K K -theory from Atiyah’s K K -theory with Reality via appropriate C 2 C_{2} -fixed points.



1.
Introduction

Background

Orthogonal calculus
[
Wei95
]
is a homotopy theoretic tool developed to study geometric topology. Weiss’ key observation, stemming from work of Weiss and Williams
[
WW88
]
, is that many objects of interest to geometric topologists may be packaged as functors from the category of Euclidean spaces and linear isometries to the category of (based) spaces. Key examples include the functor which assigns to a Euclidean space
V
the classifying space
\mathsf{BDiff}(V)
of the group of diffeomorphisms of
V
, or the functor which assigns to a Euclidean space
V
the space of (smooth) embeddings
\mathsf{Emb}(M\times V,N\times V)
for
M
and
N
fixed smooth manifolds, possibly with boundary conditions.

To a functor
F
from the category of Euclidean spaces to (based) spaces, the calculus assigns a tower of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{n}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{n-1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{1}F}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{0}F}}

under
F
, which we refer to as
the Weiss tower
. The
n
-th layer of the Weiss tower is the homotopy fibre
D_{n}F
of the map
T_{n}F\to T_{n-1}F
induced by the inclusion
\mathbb{R}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Weiss
[
Wei95
]
classified the homotopy type of the
n
-th layers of this tower in terms of spectra with an action of the
n
-th orthogonal group
O(n)
, and the orthogonal calculus program aims to understand the homotopy type of the input functor
F
in terms of the homotopy type of the spectra classifying the layers. Informally, one may think of this as a calculus in which total Stiefel-Whitney classes appear as first-order approximations and total Pontryagin classes as second-order approximations.

Orthogonal calculus fits into a larger framework of functor calculus, a categorification of Taylor’s theorem from differential topology, pioneered by Goodwillie
[
Goo90
,
Goo92
,
Goo03
]
. The aim of any version of functor calculus is to split a functor into “polynomial” parts, and reconstruct the homotopy type of the functor from these polynomial pieces. Depending on the natural structure of the functor you wish to study, one may want a calculus which captures this structure. For example a version of orthogonal calculus based on complex inner product spaces was of fundamental importance to Arone’s study of Mitchell’s finite spectra with
\mathcal{A}_{k}
-free cohomology
[
Aro98
]
which arise naturally as stabilisations of Weiss’
cross-effects
or
derivatives
, and to Hahn and Yuan’s
[
HY19
]
study of multiplicative structures in the stable splittings of
\Omega\mathsf{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Since the original version of functor calculus there has been a small industry focused on the development of other versions of calculus that capture different structures, and in investigating the relationships between these various versions of functor calculus. Examples include the discrete calculus of Bauer, Johnson and McCarthy
[
BJM15
]
together with its comparison to Goodwillie calculus, and the manifold calculus of Goodwillie and Weiss
[
Wei96
,
Wei99
,
GW99
]
and its relationship with orthogonal calculus as exploited by Arone, Lambrechts and Volić
[
ALV07
]
in their study of the stable rational homotopy type of spaces of embeddings.

Motivated by the analogy between orthogonal calculus and real topological
K
-theory, the author developed a series of other calculi: unitary calculus
[
Tag19
]
, which sits in analogy with complex topological
K
-theory, and calculus with Reality
[
Tag22
]
, which is the calculus version of Atiyah’s
K
-theory with Reality. These calculi are related in precisely the same manner as their analogous
K
-theories. The complexification-realification adjunction on the level of vector spaces induces a relationship between orthogonal and unitary calculus
[
Tag21
]
which is well-behaved if the functors have convergent Weiss towers. This is the calculus version of the existence of maps (of spectra)

\displaystyle r:KU\to KO

\displaystyle c:KO\to KU,

relating real and complex topological
K
-theory. Moreover, we have a calculus version of the statement that the underlying non-equivariant spectrum of Atiyah’s
K
-theory with Reality spectrum
KR
is the complex topological
K
-theory spectrum
KU
; forgetting the “complex conjugation” action on calculus with Reality recovers unitary calculus
[
Tag20
]
.

2.
A cofree model for calculus with Reality

2.1.
Models for equivariant spaces

In the construction of calculus with Reality
[
Tag22
]
, we considered the
underlying
or
coarse
model structure on based
C_{2}
-spaces in which a map
f:X\to Y
is a weak equivalence or fibration if and only if
f
is a weak equivalence or fibration in the Quillen model structure on based spaces. The cofibrant objects are the
free

C_{2}
-spaces, i.e., those spaces which are (up to underlying weak equivalence) of the form
(EC_{2})_{+}\wedge X
for
X
a based space. This model structure is the transfer of the Quillen model structure on based spaces along the adjoint pair

\textstyle{(C_{2})_{+}\wedge(-):\T\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces C_{2}\T:i^{\ast}}

and we denote this model structure by
C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}
. In this section we present an alternative model for this homotopy theory which is presented by
cofree

C_{2}
-spaces, i.e., we give a model structure on
C_{2}\T
in which the weak equivalences are still underlying equivalences but the fibrant objects are
C_{2}
-spaces of the form
\Map_{\ast}((EC_{2})_{+},X)
for
X
a based space. Much of what we say in this section is true for arbitrary groups
G
, but we specialise to
G=C_{2}
as it is the only case of interest for us.

We start by recalling the
fine
(or “genuine”) model structure on
C_{2}
-spaces of which our cofree model structure will be a localization.

Lemma 2.1.1

(
[
MM02
, Theorem III.1.8]
)
.

There is a model structure on the category of
C_{2}
-spaces such that a map
f:X\to Y
is a weak equivalence or fibration if and only if the induced map on
H
-fixed points
f^{H}:X^{H}\to Y^{H}
is a weak equivalence or fibration of based spaces for all subgroups
H
of
C_{2}
. We denote this model structure by
C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{fine}}
. This model structure is cellular, proper and topological.

We now state the
cofree
model structure as a localization of the fine model structure, see also
[
Law20
, Example 7.9]
. In particular homotopy
C_{2}
-fixed points are precisely the right derived functor of
C_{2}
-fixed points in this model structure.

Proposition 2.1.2
.

There is a model structure on the category of
C_{2}
-spaces such that a map
f:X\to Y
is a weak equivalence if it is an underlying weak homotopy equivalence or a cofibration if it is a cofibration in the fine model structure. The fibrant objects are the cofree
C_{2}
-spaces. We denote this model structure by
C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}
. This model structure is cellular, proper and topological.

Proof.

This model structure is the left Bousfield localization of the fine model structure at the map
(EC_{2})_{+}\to\ast
, which exists by
[
Hir03
, Theorem 4.1.1]
. A space
X
is
(EC_{2})_{+}
-local if and only if the induced map

X^{C_{2}}\longrightarrow X^{hC_{2}}

is an underlying weak equivalence, i.e., if and only if
X
is a cofree
C_{2}
-space. In particular, a localization functor is given by

X\longmapsto\Map_{\ast}((EC_{2})_{+},X).

It follows that map
f:X\to Y
is a weak equivalence if and only if

\Map_{\ast}((EC_{2})_{+},X)^{H}\longrightarrow\Map_{\ast}((EC_{2})_{+},Y)^{H}

is a weak equivalence of based spaces for all
H\leq C_{2}
. This last is equivalent to
f:X\to Y
and
f^{hC_{2}}:~X^{hC_{2}}\to Y^{hC_{2}}
being underlying weak equivalences, which since homotopy fixed points preserve underlying weak equivalences, is equivalent to
f:X\to Y
being an underlying weak equivalence.

The general theory of left Bousfield localization implies all of the properties of this model structure except for right properness, which follows since right properness is determined by the weak equivalence class, and the free model structure on
C_{2}
-spaces is right proper with the same weak equivalences as in the cofree model structure, see e.g.,
[
Bal21
, Remark 3.5.6]
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.3
.

The identity functor

\mathds{1}:C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}\longrightarrow C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}

is a left Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

The identity functor

\mathds{1}:C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}\to C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{fine}}

is a left Quillen functor from the free model structure to the fine model structure, and since the cofree model structure is a left Bousfield localization of the fine model structure, we obtain a left Quillen functor

\mathds{1}:C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}\longrightarrow C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}.

The weak equivalences in the free and cofree model structure agree, hence it suffices to show that either of the (co)units is a weak equivalence. This follows readily from the fact that for any
C_{2}
-space
X
, the map

(EC_{2})_{+}\wedge\Map_{\ast}((EC_{2})_{+},X)\longrightarrow X

is an underlying weak equivalence.
∎

2.2.
A cofree model for input functors

Let
\mathds{k}
denote either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and let
\mathcal{U}
denote the infinite-dimensional
\mathds{k}
inner product spaces
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
,
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
or
\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
. The infinite-dimensional inner product space
\mathcal{U}
is the indexing universe for the various versions of orthogonal calculus, e.g.,
\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
indexes orthogonal calculus and
\mathcal{U}=\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
indexes calculus with Reality. Define
\mathcal{J}
to be the category of finite-dimensional inner product subspaces of
\mathcal{U}
with morphism the linear isometries. The category
\mathcal{J}
is topologically enriched as
\mathcal{J}(V,W)
is the Stiefel manifold of
\dim(V)
-frames in
W
. By adding a disjoint basepoint to the morphism space produces a
\T
-enriched category, which we continue to denote by
\mathcal{J}
. In the case of calculus with Reality this category is
C_{2}\T
-enriched with
C_{2}
acting by complex conjugation, see
[
Tag22
, §1.2]
.

The input category for calculus with Reality is the category of
C_{2}
-equivariant continuous functors
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J}^{\mathbf{R}},C_{2}\T)
with
C_{2}
acting by conjugation, while the input category for orthogonal calculus is the category of continuous functors
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J}^{\mathbf{O}},\T)
. When discussing both calculi at once the phrase “continuous functors” should be interpreted in the appropriate sense. For calculus with Reality, the adjunction between free and cofree
C_{2}
-spaces induces an Quillen equivalence on the level of functor categories.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. The adjoint pair

\textstyle{\mathds{1}:\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J}^{\mathbf{R}},C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J}^{\mathbf{R}},C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}):\mathds{1}}

is a Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

Since (acyclic) fibrations are defined levelwise in
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J},C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}})
, and the identity functor

\mathds{1}:C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}\longrightarrow C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}

is right Quillen, it follows that the induced adjunction on projective model structures is a Quillen adjunction.

To show that the adjunction is a Quillen equivalence it suffices to show that the derived (co)units are equivalences. Unravelling the definition of the (co)unit, one observes that the Quillen equivalence between
C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}}
and
C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}
forces the (co)units to be equivalences on the level of functor categories.
∎

2.3.
A cofree model for polynomial functors

For the remainder of this section we let
\mathcal{C}
denote either
\T
or
C_{2}\T
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. A functor
F:\mathcal{J}\to\mathcal{C}
is said to be
polynomial of degree less than or equal
n
or equivalently
n
-polynomial
if for all
V\in\mathcal{J}
, the canonical map

F(V)\longrightarrow\underset{0\neq U\subseteq\mathds{k}^{n+1}}{\holim}~F(V\oplus U)=:\tau_{n}F(V)

is a weak equivalence in
\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 2.3.2
.

The poset of subspaces of
\mathds{k}^{n+1}
is a topological poset in that it has a
space
of object and
space
of morphisms, with continuous source, target, composition and insertion of identity maps. The space of objects is a disjoint union of Grassmannian manifolds, while the space of morphisms is a disjoint union of flag manifolds.

There is a model structure which captures the homotopy theory of
n
-polynomial functors, and hence in which fibrant replacement is
n
-polynomial approximation.

Lemma 2.3.3

(
[
BO13
, Proposition 6.5
\&
Proposition 6.6]
,
[
Tag22
, Proposition 2.15]
)
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. There is a cellular, proper and topological model category structure on the category of continuous functors
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C})
with cofibrations the projective cofibrations and fibrant objects are the levelwise fibrant
n
-polynomial functors. We denote this model structure by
\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq n}(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C})
.

Remark 2.3.4
.

The
n
-polynomial model structure is the left Bousfield localization of the projective model structure at the set

\left\{\ \underset{0\neq U\subset\mathds{k}^{n+1}}{\hocolim}~\mathcal{J}(V\oplus U,-)\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}(V,-)\ \mid\ V\in\mathcal{J}\ \right\}.

By the relevant version of
[
Wei95
, Proposition 4.2]
to the known version of Weiss calculus under consideration, there is an identification

S\gamma_{n+1}(V,W)_{+}\cong\underset{0\neq U\subset\mathds{k}^{n}}{\hocolim}~\mathcal{J}_{0}(V\oplus U,W)

of the sphere bundle of
\gamma_{n+1}(V,W)
with a topological homotopy colimit of Stiefel manifolds, hence the set of maps is equivalently described via the projection maps of the bundle
S\gamma_{n+1}(V,-)
.

Remark 2.3.5
.

The underlying
\infty
-category of the
n
-polynomial model structure is the
\infty
-category of
n
-polynomial functors. This should be equivalently defined as the full
\infty
-subcategory of the
\infty
-category of functors
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J},\mathcal{C})
spanned by the
n
-polynomial functors, if one suitably interprets Definition
2.3.1
in an appropriate
\infty
-categorical sense.

For calculus with Reality, the adjunction between free and cofree
C_{2}
-spaces induces a Quillen equivalence between
n
-polynomial model structures.

Proposition 2.3.6
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. The adjoint pair

\textstyle{\mathds{1}:\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq n}(\mathcal{J},C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{free}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\mathsf{Poly}^{\leq n}(\mathcal{J},C_{2}\T^{\mathsf{cofree}}):\mathds{1}}

is a Quillen equivalence.

Proof.

Apply
[
Hir03
, Theorem 3.3.20]
, noticing that the localizing set of the cofree
n
-polynomial model structure is the derived image of the localizing set of the free

2.4.

2.5.
A cofree model for homogeneous functors

In the known versions of Weiss calculus the layers of the tower, i.e., the homotopy fibres

D_{n}F=\hofibre(T_{n}F\longrightarrow T_{n-1}F)

have two useful properties: firstly they are polynomial of degree less than or equal

-polynomial approximations are trivial.

Definition 2.5.1

n

There is a model structure which captures the homotopy theory of
n
-homogeneous functors. The weak equivalences are the
D_{n}
-equivalences
, i.e., those maps

such that

are levelwise weak equivalences.

Lemma 2.5.2

(
[
BO13
, Proposition 6.9]
,
[
Tag22
, Proposition 3.2]
)
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. There is a model category structure on the category of continuous functors

such that the fibrations are the fibrations of the
n
-polynomial model structure, and the weak equivalences are the

-equivalences. The fibrant objects are the
n
-polynomial functors, and the cofibrant objects are the projectively cofibrant

-reduced functors. We call this the

-homogeneous model structure and denote it by

3.
Polynomial functors

3.1.
Recovering input functors

Using the invariance of Weiss calculus under Quillen equivalence, we shall base our comparison between calculus with Reality and orthogonal calculus on the cofree model for calculus with Reality. To ease exposition we will neglect the superscripts for model categories when it will not cause confusion.

The complexification of real vector spaces induces a functor

extends to an adjunction between spaces and cofree

since cofree

\textstyle{i^{\ast}:\mathsf{Fun}(\mathcal{J}^{\mathbf{R}}_{0},\T)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

-enriched functors, while on the right-hand side we have a category of

-enriched functors. This difference in enrichment does not interfere with the existence of the adjunction. The result is a composite Quillen adjunction

3.2.
Polynomial functors

We begin our comparison with the polynomial functors. Unlike many other comparisons between calculi, see e.g.,
[

we do not need to invoke the classification theorem for homogeneous functors in order to compare polynomial functors. In both versions of Weiss calculus the

-polynomial functors boils down to a (co)finality statement of homotopy (co)limits indexed on such topological categories, see e.g.,

topologically terminal object

3.3.

To give an equivalence on polynomial approximations, we first provide an equivalence on iterations of

Inductively use Lemma

-fixed points commute with sequential homotopy colimits.

4.
The Weiss tower

4.1.
Homogeneous functors

The next step in understanding the relationship between Weiss towers is understanding how the layers of the towers interact. More generally, we obtain the following relationship between homogeneous functors.

Theorem 4.1.1
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer. If
F
is a homogeneous functor of degree
n
in the cofree model for calculus with Reality, then
c^{\ast}F^{C_{2}}
is a homogeneous functor of degree
n
in orthogonal calculus.

Proof.

A homogeneous functor of degree
n
is precisely a polynomial functor of degree less than or equal

, such that the
(n-1)
-polynomial approximation vanishes. In light of Theorem
3.2.1
, it suffices to show that triviality of
(n-1)
-polynomial approximations is preserved, which follows from Theorem

∎

As with the

\infty
-categories of homogeneous functors. In the following proof we make use of the fact that many of the results in unitary calculus hold also in calculus with Reality. The proofs of the results uses in this way can be verbatim translated to calculus with Reality since they only rely on formal calculus arguments and the classification of homogeneous functors.

Proposition 4.1.2

The right adjoint preserves fibrations since the fibrations of the

-polynomial model structure, which are preserved by Proposition

. By (the calculus with Reality version of)

c^{\ast}(D_{n}^{\mathbf{R}}F)^{C_{2}}\longrightarrow D_{n}^{\mathbf{O}}(c^{\ast}F^{C_{2}}).

4.2.

We gather together the results obtained so far to give the relationship between Weiss towers in orthogonal calculus and Weiss towers in calculus with Reality. Since the Weiss tower of a functor

(\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{\mathbb{C}}}\wedge\Theta)\wedge_{U(n)}EU(n)_{+}])^{C_{2}}\simeq\Omega^{\infty}[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{\mathbb{C}}}\wedge\Theta)\wedge_{U(n)}EU(n)_{+}]^{C_{2}}.

Since
C_{2}
acts diagonally on the right-hand side, there is a natural isomorphism

[(S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{\mathbb{C}}}\wedge\Theta)\wedge_{U(n)}EU(n)_{+}]^{C_{2}}\simeq((S^{\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}V_{\mathbb{C}}})^{C_{2}}\wedge\Theta^{C_{2}})\wedge_{U(n)^{C_{2}}}(EU(n)_{+})^{C_{2}},

which by calculation is naturally isomorphic to

(S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V}\wedge\Theta^{C_{2}})\wedge_{O(n)}EO(n)_{+}=(S^{\mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}V}\wedge\Theta^{C_{2}})_{hO(n)},

hence the result follows.
∎

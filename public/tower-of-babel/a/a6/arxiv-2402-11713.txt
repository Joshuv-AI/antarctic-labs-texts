Existence of an optimal shape for the first eigenvalue of polyharmonic operators
Roméo Leylekian

Abstract
We prove the existence of an open set minimizing the first eigenvalue of the Dirichlet polylaplacian of order m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 under volume constraint. Moreover, the corresponding eigenfunction is shown to enjoy C m − 1 , α C^{m-1,\alpha} Hölder regularity. This is performed for dimension 2 ≤ d ≤ 4 ​ m 2\leq d\leq 4m . In particular, our analysis answers the question of the existence of an optimal shape for the clamped plate up to dimension 8 8 .

1
Introduction

The purpose of this document is to prove the existence and the partial regularity of an optimal shape for the first eigenvalue of Dirichlet polylaplacians with volume constraint. More precisely, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
(fixed from now on), we consider the following eigenvalue problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)^{m}u&=&\Gamma u&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{n}^{i}u&=&0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\text{ for all }0\leq i<m,\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\Omega
is an open subset of finite volume of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
is fixed as well),
\partial_{n}=\nabla\cdot\vec{n}
is the outward normal derivative,
\Gamma
is a real number, and
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is nontrivial and belongs to the Sobolev space
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
. It is known that there exists an unbounded sequence of positive such eigenvalues
\Gamma
. Here, we only focus on the lowest one, that we denote
\Gamma(\Omega)
. Then, we are interested in solving

\min\{\Gamma(\Omega):\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ open set, }|\Omega|=c\},

(2)

where
|\cdot|
is the Lebesgue measure, and
c
is a given positive real number. For
m=1
, the Faber-Krahn inequality
[
Fab23
,
Kra25
]
shows that (
2
) admits the ball as a solution. For
m=2
, in dimension
d=2,3
, it has been proved in 1995 in
[
Nad95
]
and
[
AB95
]
that the ball also solves (
2
). Unfortunately, the method employed does not give the result when
d\geq 4
. For
m\geq 3
, it seems that nothing is known about the solution of (
2
) in any dimension (except dimension
1
). In this context, it is a natural question to ask whether there is at least one solution to (
2
). We will give a positive answer to this question for a certain range of dimensions by proving the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
2\leq d\leq 4m
. Problem (
2
) admits a solution
\Omega
. Moreover, any corresponding eigenfunction extended by
0
ouside
\Omega
is
C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0<\alpha<1
.

Remark 2
.

1.

We do not claim anything on the boundedness of an optimal shape.

2.

Even if the eigenfunction is somewhat regular, we do not claim any regularity on the optimal shape more than its openness.

3.

The regularity of the eigenfunction is not sharp in general (see
[
BMPV15
, Theorem 6.1]
).

4.

In the case
m=1
, the restriction
d\leq 4m
can be removed (see also Remark
6
).

5.

The result also holds when
d=1
, for which the problem is trivial. Indeed, any one-dimensional solution for (
2
) must be an interval (otherwise a competitor could be built from one of its connected components), but all the admissible intervals have equal eigenvalue.

The proof of Theorem
1
will be divided into two steps, following the approach of
[
Sto23
]
. First, we prove the existence of an optimal shape in a wider class than the class of open sets. As we shall see, this part of the proof does not require the restriction
2\leq d\leq 4m
. Then, we show that the generalised optimal shape enjoys some regularity properties allowing to solve (
2
). Let us begin with the existence of a generalised optimal shape.

1.1
Existence of an optimal shape

Several breakthroughs have been performed in the direction of exhibiting an optimal shape for (
2
) or relaxed versions. We cite
[
AB03
,
Sto23
]
, in which an analogous problem is tackled for the buckling bilaplace eigenvalue problem, using a concentration-compactness technique.
Another interesting result is
[
Buc05
, Theorem 3.5]
, which establishes the existence of a
quasi-open
optimal shape for a family of spectral optimization problems similar to (
2
). However, the class of admissible shapes is restricted to some design region. Moreover, we emphasize that the definition chosen in
[
Buc05
]
for the Sobolev space
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
does not match the classical one, unless
\Omega
enjoys some regularity (see
[
Buc05
, p. 109]
for details). As a consequence, even if the optimal shape would be open, it would not necessarily solve (
2
).
In the same fashion, a similar result but with a different approach shall be found in
[
Sto21
]
. There, the existence of an
open
optimal shape for problem (
2
) with
m=2
in dimension

is evidenced, but still among sets lying in a design region.

As a first step towards a proof of Theorem
1
, we would like to provide an optimal shape for (
2
) in the wider class of
quasi-open sets
. Even if we need preliminary notions to understand it correctly, let us give a precise statement for our first main result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. The following problem admits a solution.

\displaystyle\min\{\Gamma(\Omega):\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{, }\Omega\text{ 1-quasi-open, }|\Omega|=c\}

(3)

Remark 4
.

1.

Note that the optimal shape need not to be bounded, we only know that it has bounded measure.

2.

At first glance it should be suprising that for
m
arbitrary, the appropriate class of quasi-open sets for getting existence is the class of
1
-quasi-open sets instead of
m
-quasi-open sets. This will become clear as soon as we will understand how the optimal shape is constructed.

The notion of quasi-open sets involved in the statement of Theorem
3
will be investigated in section
2
. Then, the proof of Theorem
3
is achieved in section
3.2
. However, without entering into details, we shall briefly describe the strategy of the proof: We follow
[
AB03
]
, and apply the concentration-compactness principle (see paragraph
3.1
) directly to the first eigenfunctions of a minimizing sequence of open sets. We show that only compactness shall occur, hence we are able to extract a converging subsequence of first eigenfunctions. The optimal shape is identified as the set of points where the limit function or one of its derivatives up to order
m-1
does not vanish. Such a set is only
1
-quasi-open in general, which explains why we needed to widen the class of admissible sets.

1.2
Regularity of the optimal shape

After providing an optimal shape among quasi-open sets, the next step in the proof of Theorem
1
would be to show that this optimal set is actually open, and to give some hint on its regularity. A wide literature have been produced on this subject, especially in the case
m=1
(see for instance
[
BMPV15
,
BBV13
]
and the references given at the end of section
5
). For the case
m=2
, the work
[
Sto21
]
, following
[
Sto22
]
, gives partial answers, but again with the assumption of a prescribed design region. This assumption was dropped successfully only in the case of the buckling bilaplacian
[
Sto23
]
. Therefore, as for the existence, the regularity of an optimal shape seems still open in full generality. Indeed, gaining regularity from scratch is a quite difficult task in general. As a consequence, we will not prove that any optimal shape for (
3
) is a regular open set. Alternatively, in the wake of
[
BMPV15
,
Sto23
]
, we will obtain regularity for the first eigenfunction, and from this fact build an open set competing with the optimal quasi-open set of Theorem

Theorem 5
.

Assume that
2\leq d\leq 4m
and that there exists a measurable set
\Omega
solving

\min\{\Gamma(\omega):\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ measurable, }|\omega|=c\}.

(4)

Then, any first eigenfunction over
\Omega
extended by
0
outside
\Omega
is
C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0<\alpha<1
. As a consequence, there exists an(other) optimal set
\tilde{\Omega}
for (
4
) which is open.

.

1.

The regularity which is obtained for the eigenfunction is not sharp in general (see
[

, Theorem 6.1]
).

2.

The restriction

comes from the fact that Theorem

2
Preliminaries

We recall that if
\Omega
is an open set of bounded measure,
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
is defined as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
in the Sobolev space
H^{m}(\Omega)
, and the eigenvalue
\Gamma(\Omega)
is then characterized variationally by the formula

\Gamma(\Omega)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)\\
v\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{\Omega}|D^{m}v|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega}v^{2}}.

(5)

Here,
D^{m}v
denotes the differential of order
m
of
v
. Its Euclidean norm
|D^{m}v|^{2}=D^{m}v:D^{m}v
is inherited from the Euclidean scalar product denoted

D^{m}v:D^{m}w=\sum_{i\in\{1,...,d\}^{m}}\partial_{i}v\partial_{i}w=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d},\\
|\gamma|=m\end{subarray}}^{d}\frac{m!}{\gamma!}\partial^{\gamma}v\partial^{\gamma}w,

where for
i=(i_{1},...,i_{m})\in\{1,...,d\}^{m}
we write
\partial_{i}v=\partial_{i_{1}}...\partial_{i_{m}}v
, whereas for
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we write
\partial^{\gamma}v=\partial_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}...\partial_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}v
and
\gamma!=\gamma_{1}!...\gamma_{d}!
.
It can be shown, thanks to the compact embedding
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
, that the minimum of (
5
) is achieved for some function
u
. Then,
u
is a first eigenfunction, that is
(u,\Gamma(\Omega))
is a distributional solution to (
1
). This last point is a consequence of the integration by parts formula,

\int_{\Omega}\left((-\Delta)^{m}u\right)v=\int_{\Omega}D^{m}u:D^{m}v,\qquad\forall u,v\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(6)

As explained in the introduction, we will need to define the first eigenvalue on sets which are not necessarily open. To this end, we will use the right-hand side of formula (
5
) as a definition, hence we need to investigate the functional space
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
in the case where
\Omega
is a general set. This involves the notions of capacities of orders
1
up to
m
. For the sake of brevity, we will use the prefix
k
when talking about capacity of order
k
and related notions.

We refer to
[
HP05
, section 3.3]
for the definition and properties of
1
-capacity and
1
-quasi-open sets. We recall that a
1
-quasi-continuous function is a function whose preimage of any open set is a
1
-quasi-open set, and that any function in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has a
1
-quasi-continuous representative uniquely defined
1
-quasi everywhere (
1
-q.e. in short, which means up to sets of null
1
-capacity). In the following, we shall always identify
H^{1}
functions with their
1
-quasi-continuous representative. Then, Theorem 3.3.42 of
[
HP05
]
shows that, when
\Omega
is open,

H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):u=0\text{ 1-q.e. in }\Omega^{c}\}.

(7)

In the same fashion, one may define an
m
-capacity and the corresponding notions of
m
-quasi-open sets and
m
-quasi-continuous functions (see
[
AH96
]
for a very general theory of capacities). Formally, for
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the
m
-capacity of
E
, denoted
cap_{m}(E)
, is given by

cap_{m}(E):=\inf\{\|v\|_{H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}:\text{ $v\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, $v\geq 1$ a.e. on a neighbourhood of $E$}\}.

Actually, this is not the traditional definition, but an equivalent characterization (see
[
HP05
, Proposition 3.3.5]
). For our purpose, it is enough to think of the
m
-capacity as an outer measure over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which is finer than the
(m-1)
-capacity in the sense that
cap_{m}(E)=0\Rightarrow cap_{m-1}(E)=0
for any
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,
m
-quasi-open sets are defined to be sets which are open up to a residue of arbitrary small
m
-capacity. Lastly,
m
-quasi-continuous functions are functions whose preimages of open sets are
m
-quasi-open sets. In other words,

Definition 7
.

A set
E
is
m
-quasi-open if for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
\omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
E\cup\omega
is open and
cap_{m}(\omega)<\epsilon
. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
m
-quasi-continuous if for any open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
f^{-1}(U)
is
m
-quasi-open.

Moreover, any
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has an
m
-quasi-continuous representative defined
m
-q.e. (that is up to sets of null
m
-capacity), and we will always identify
u
with its representative. When
\Omega
is open, the space
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
shall be characterised thanks to the capacities up to order
m
in the following way (see for instance
[
GLM23
, p.2]
):

H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)=\{u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):D^{k}u=0\text{ }(m-k)\text{-q.e. in }\Omega^{c}\text{, for }0\leq k<m\}.

(8)

Let us point out that in general
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
does not coincide with the set of functions in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
vanishing a.e. outside
\Omega
. Indeed, such a property requires some regularity on
\Omega
(see
[
GLM23
]
). Observe that as the right-hand member of (
8
) makes sense regardless of the regularity of
\Omega
, we shall use it to define
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
even when
\Omega
is not open. Moreover, the compact injection

H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)

(9)

holding when
\Omega
was open, remains when
\Omega
is only a measurable set of finite measure, as explained below.

Definition 8
.

Let
\Omega
be a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
is defined by (
8
).

Lemma 9
.

Let
\Omega
be a measurable set of finite measure. Then, injection (
9
) is compact.

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a sequence in
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
. By outer regularity of the Lebesgue measure, there exists an open set
\tilde{\Omega}\supseteq\Omega
of measure
|\Omega|+1<+\infty
. Then,

H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)\subseteq H_{0}^{m}(\tilde{\Omega})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega})

is compact, so we shall extract from
(u_{n})_{n}
a subsequence converging in
L^{2}(\tilde{\Omega})
, hence in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lastly, as a consequence of Lemma
9
, formula (
5
) generalises to any measurable set of finite measure, and the minimum in (
5
) is also achieved in this setting. Before going further, let us mention the following useful observation, which follows immediately from (

).

Lemma 10
.

Let
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then,
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\bigcup_{k=0}^{m-1}\{D^{k}u\neq 0\})
.

Remark 11
.

Although Lemma
10
looks obvious in view of the previous definition of
H_{0}^{m}
, it has to be noted that the set
\omega:=\bigcup_{k=0}^{m-1}\{D^{k}u\neq 0\}
depends upon the choice of a quasi-continuous representative not only for
u
but also for its derivatives. Consequently, one may find a set
\tilde{\omega}
coinciding with
\omega
only
1
-q.e. and such that
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\tilde{\omega})
. This might appear paradoxical at first glance, since the assertion
“
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\tilde{\omega})
for all
\tilde{\omega}=\omega

1
-q.e.”
is false when
m>1
. The nuance is that we only claim the existence of
some

\tilde{\omega}=\omega

1
-q.e. such that
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\tilde{\omega})
. In other words,
\omega
is defined more than just
1
-quasi everywhere.

To conclude this section, let us explain the relevance of our procedure of relaxing the class of admissible domains for problem (
2
). This is justified by the fact that minimizing
\Gamma
among open sets, quasi-open sets, or even measurable sets does not change the value of the infimum, as stated in the next lemma. Its proof relies on the homogeneity of
\Gamma
, which reads, for any set
\Omega
,

\Gamma(\tau\Omega)=\tau^{-2m}\Gamma(\Omega),\qquad\forall\tau>0.

(10)

Lemma 12
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ open}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ k-quasi-open}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ measurable}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega)>0.

Proof.

Clearly,

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ open}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ k-quasi-open}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega)\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ measurable}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega),

since any open set is also quasi-open, and any quasi-open set is measurable. For the converse inequality, observe that if
\Omega
is a measurable set of volume
c
, by outer regularity of the Lebesgue measure there exists for every
\epsilon>0
an open set
\Omega_{\epsilon}
of volume
c+\epsilon
such that
\Omega\subseteq\Omega_{\epsilon}
, and hence
H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)\subseteq H_{0}^{m}(\Omega_{\epsilon})
. Then, by (
10
),

\Gamma(\Omega)\geq\Gamma(\Omega_{\epsilon})\geq\left(\frac{c}{c+\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{2m}{d}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega\text{ open}\\
|\Omega|=c\end{subarray}}\Gamma(\Omega),

and we get the result by taking
\epsilon\to 0
. In order to prove that the infimum does not vanish, one may consider an open set
\Omega
and combine the classical Faber-Krahn inequality with the following one:

\lambda(\Omega)^{m}\leq\Gamma(\Omega),

(11)

where
\lambda(\Omega)
is the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian, which is defined by taking
m=1
in (
5
). To prove (
11
), we let
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
be an eigenfunction (of the polylaplacian of order
m
) normalised in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. After an integration by parts and two Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, we observe that for any
1\leq k<m
,

\left(\int_{\Omega}|D^{k}u|^{2}\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}|D^{k-1}u|^{2}\int_{\Omega}|D^{k+1}u|^{2}.

In other words, denoting
v_{k}=\int_{\Omega}|D^{k}u|^{2}
, we have

v_{k}^{2}\leq v_{k-1}v_{k+1}.

As a consequence, we prove by recursion that
(v_{k})_{k}
obeys the rule

v_{m-k}^{p_{k}}\leq v_{m-k-1}^{q_{k}}v_{m},\qquad\forall 1\leq k<m,

where
(p_{k})_{k}
and
(q_{k})_{k}
are defined by
(p_{1},q_{1})=(2,1)
, and for all
k\geq 1
,

p_{k+1}=2p_{k}-q_{k},\qquad q_{k+1}=p_{k}.

The couple
(p_{k},q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
forms a constant-recursive sequence of order
1
, the solution of which is
(p_{k},q_{k})=(k+1,k)
. Consequently,

v_{1}^{m}\leq v_{0}^{m-1}v_{m},

(12)

which gives the result since
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is normalised in
L^{2}
.
∎

3.1
Concentration-Compactness principle

As mentioned in the introduction, the concentration-compactness principle is the main tool used for proving Theorem
3
. In this paragraph, we adapt to our needs the standard concentration-compactness Lemma of Lions (see Lemma I.1 and III.1 of
[
Lio84
]
). We will use the notation
\tau_{y}u
to designate the translation of a given function
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by a given point
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is the function defined by
\tau_{y}u(x)=u(x-y)
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
(u_{n})_{n}
be a bounded sequence of functions in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\lambda>0
. Then, up to extracting a subsequence, one of the following occurs:

(i)

(compactness) there exists a sequence of points
(y_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\forall\epsilon>0,\exists R>0,\quad\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}\geq\lambda-\epsilon.

(ii)

(vanishing)

\forall R>0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\tau_{y}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}=0.

(iii)

(dichotomy) there exists
\alpha\in(0,\lambda)
and functions
u_{n}^{1}
, and
u_{n}^{2}
in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-(u_{n}^{1}+u_{n}^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\alpha,\qquad\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\lambda-\alpha,

\displaystyle\liminf\left[\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\geq 0.

Moreover, for any
0\leq k<m
, up to some sets of zero
(m-k)
-capacity,

\displaystyle dist\left(\omega_{n,k}^{1},\omega_{n,k}^{2}\right)\to\infty,

\displaystyle\omega_{n,k}^{1},\omega_{n,k}^{2}\subseteq\omega_{n,k},

where
\omega_{n,k}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\{D^{j}u_{n}\neq 0\}
and
\omega_{n,k}^{i}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\{D^{j}u_{n}^{i}\neq 0\}
for
i=1,2
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Lemma III.1 of
[
Lio84
]
and define

Q_{n}(R):=\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|\tau_{y}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}

which induces a sequence of nonnegative nondecreasing uniformly bounded functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Q_{n}(R)\xrightarrow[R\to\infty]{}\lambda_{n}
, with
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda
. Then, by Helly’s selection Theorem, up to extracting a subsequence,
Q_{n}
pointwise converges to some function
Q
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
Q
, like
Q_{n}
, enjoys the properties of being nonnegative and nondecreasing. Furthermore
Q
is bounded by
\lambda
. Now set

\alpha:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\uparrow Q(R)\in[0,\lambda].

If
\alpha=0
, we are in the vanishing case, because for any
R
,
0\leq Q(R)\leq\alpha=0
, hence
Q(R)=0
which is the result. If
\alpha=\lambda
, we are in case of compactness. Indeed, let
\epsilon>0
and choose
r
such that
\lambda-\epsilon<Q(r)
. Because
Q_{n}(r)\to Q(r)
, for all
n
large enough,
\lambda-\epsilon<Q_{n}(r)
. Then, there exists
z_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\lambda-\epsilon<\|\tau_{z_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}^{2}
. Note that the radius
r=r(\epsilon)
and the translation points
z_{n}=z_{n}(\epsilon)
depend on
\epsilon
. It remains to show that, up to increasing
r
, the points
z_{n}
shall be replaced by points
y_{n}
which are independent of
\epsilon
.

But as long as
\epsilon<\lambda/3
, the balls
B(z_{n}(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
and
B(z_{n}(\lambda/3),r(\lambda/3))
intersect each other for all
n
large enough, otherwise we would have, up to a subsequence,

\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\|\tau_{z_{n}(\epsilon)}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r(\epsilon)})}^{2}+\|\tau_{z_{n}(\lambda/3)}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r(\lambda/3)})}^{2}>\lambda-\epsilon+\lambda-\lambda/3>4\lambda/3,

which is impossible since
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\lambda
.
As a consequence, the distance between
z_{n}(\epsilon)
and
z_{n}(\lambda/3)
does not exceed
r(\epsilon)+r(\lambda/3)
. Then, setting
R(\epsilon):=r(\lambda/3)+2r(\epsilon)
, we get that
B(z_{n}(\epsilon),r(\epsilon))
is included in
B(z_{n}(\lambda/3),R(\epsilon))
. Therefore, the sequence
y_{n}=z_{n}(\lambda/3)
satisfies that for any
0<\epsilon<\lambda/3
, there exists
R:=R(\epsilon)
such that for all
n
large enough,

\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}^{2}\geq\|\tau_{z_{n}(\epsilon)}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r(\epsilon)})}^{2}>\lambda-\epsilon.

This concludes the case of compactness. If now
\alpha\in(0,\lambda)
, let
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and
R_{n}\to\infty
such that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\geq Q(R_{n})>\alpha-\epsilon_{n}
. Because
Q_{n}\to Q
once again, we may extract a subsequence
(u_{n})_{n}
and define a sequence
(y_{n})_{n}
such that,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha-\epsilon_{n}<\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}
. In the next lines, we will build
u_{n}^{1}
in a way that
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}^{1}=\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}
over
B_{R_{n}}
and with support in
B_{2R_{n}}
. We will also build
u_{n}^{2}
in a way that
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}^{2}
is supported in the complement of
B_{3R_{n}}
and equals
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}
in the complement of
B_{6R_{n}}
. In this fashion, the supports of
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}^{1}
and
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}^{1}
will be separated by an annulus of width
R_{n}\to\infty
. Furthermore, in order to analyse the convergence of
u_{n}^{1}
and
u_{n}^{2}
, we will need that
\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R_{n}})}^{2}<\alpha+\epsilon_{n}
and also
\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{6R_{n}})}^{2}<\alpha+\epsilon_{n}
, which can be assumed without loss of generality since
Q_{n}\to Q\leq\alpha
.

Now, let us actually construct
u_{n}^{1}
and
u_{n}^{2}
. We use a smooth cut-off function over
B_{1}
and with support in
B_{2}
, say
\chi^{1}
, and set
\chi^{2}:=1-\chi^{1}
. Moreover, we define the
m
-quasi-continuous functions

\displaystyle u_{n}^{1}=u_{n}\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1},\qquad u_{n}^{2}=u_{n}\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{2},

where
\delta_{r}v
is the dilation of a function
v
by
r
, i.e.
\delta_{r}v(x):=v(x/r)
. Note that
\delta_{r}\chi^{1}
and
\delta_{3r}\chi^{2}
, as well as their derivatives, are respectively supported in
B_{2r}
and in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus B_{3r}
. As a consequence, we have

dist\left(\omega_{n,k}^{1},\omega_{n,k}^{2}\right)\geq R_{n}\to\infty.

Moreover, for
i=1,2
, the inclusion
\omega_{n,k}^{i}\subseteq\omega_{n,k}
follows from the definition of
u_{n}^{1}
and
u_{n}^{2}
.
Now let us check that

\displaystyle\left\|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1})D^{m}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\underset{n\to\infty}{=}O(1/R_{n}),

\displaystyle\left\|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\left\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{2})D^{m}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\underset{n\to\infty}{=}O(1/R_{n}).

For that purpose, take
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
a multi-index of length
m
, and recall that

\partial^{\gamma}(fg)=\sum_{\beta\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\beta}\partial^{\beta}f\partial^{\gamma-\beta}g,

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|\partial^{\gamma}u_{n}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\chi_{R_{n}}^{1})\partial^{\gamma}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta\leq\gamma\\
\beta\neq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{\gamma}{\beta}^{2}\frac{1}{R_{n}^{2|\beta|}}\left\|(\partial^{\gamma-\beta}u_{n})(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\partial^{\beta}\chi^{1})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta,\beta^{\prime}\leq\gamma\\
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\binom{\gamma}{\beta}\binom{\gamma}{\beta^{\prime}}\frac{1}{R_{n}^{|\beta|+|\beta^{\prime}|}}\left\langle(\partial^{\gamma-\beta}u_{n})(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\partial^{\beta}\chi^{1})\text{ , }(\partial^{\gamma-\beta^{\prime}}u_{n})(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\partial^{\beta^{\prime}}\chi^{1})\right\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Since
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and since
\chi^{1}\in W^{m,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the two sums above are
O(1/R_{n})
, and we obtain as expected

\left\|\partial^{\gamma}u_{n}^{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\underset{n\to\infty}{=}\left\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\chi_{R_{n}}^{1})\partial^{\gamma}u_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(1/R_{n}).

An analogous discussion leads to an analogous result regarding
\left\|\partial^{\gamma}u_{n}^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
Now, using also the inequality
\left|\delta_{r}\chi^{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\delta_{3r}\chi^{2}\right|^{2}\leq 1
, the previous justifies that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-

\displaystyle\|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1})D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|(\tau_{-y_{n}}\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{2})D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+O(1/R_{n})

\displaystyle\geq\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tau_{-y_{n}}\left(\left|\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1}\right|^{2}+\left|\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{2}\right|^{2}\right)|D^{m}u_{n}|^{2}+O(1/R_{n})

\displaystyle\geq O(1/R_{n}).

This means that
\liminf_{n}\left[\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\geq 0
.
Moreover,

\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1}\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}>\alpha-\epsilon_{n}\to\alpha.

On the other hand, since
\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1}
is supported in
B_{2R_{n}}
,

\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1}\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R_{n}})}^{2}<\alpha+\epsilon_{n}\to\alpha.

Hence
\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\alpha
. In the same spirit, regarding
u_{n}^{2}
,

\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{2}\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{3R_{n}})}^{2}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\alpha+\epsilon_{n}\to\lambda-\alpha,

and

\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{6R_{n}})}^{2}>\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\alpha-\epsilon_{n}\to\lambda-\alpha,

showing that
\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\lambda-\alpha
.
To conclude,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-(u_{n}^{1}+u_{n}^{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\delta_{3R_{n}}\chi^{1}-\delta_{R_{n}}\chi^{1}\right)^{2}|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{6R_{n}})}^{2}-\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{n}})}^{2}\leq\alpha+\epsilon_{n}-\alpha+\epsilon_{n}\to 0.

∎

3.2
Proof of Theorem
3

Equipped with the concentration-compactness principle, we can now turn to the proof of Theorem
3
, which claims the existence of a
1
-quasi-open set minimizing
\Gamma
under volume constraint. Following
[
AB03
]
, the strategy is to apply the concentration-compactness procedure to the eigenfunctions of a minimizing sequence of sets, and to show that only compactness occurs. As a consequence, the eigenfunctions are shown to converge in
L^{2}
towards a limit function. The final step is to identify the optimal set from the limit function, which is performed in the next proposition.

Proposition 14
.

Let
\Omega_{n}
be a sequence of measurable sets of volume
c
, and
u_{n}\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega_{n})
the corresponding eigenfunctions converging to some
u
in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
weakly and in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
strongly. Then there exists a
1
-quasi-open set
\Omega
of volume
|\Omega|\leq c
such that

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\Gamma(\Omega).

To prove Proposition
14
, one builds the limit shape
\Omega
as the set of points where
u
or one of its derivatives up to order
m-1
does not vanish. The conclusion is then straightforward, except the fact that
|\Omega|\leq c
. This actually follows from a pointwise convergence of the functions and from Egorov’s Theorem, as shown in the lemma below.

Lemma 15
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
U_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
form a sequence of measurable functions converging a.e. toward
U:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then
|\{U\neq 0\}|\leq\liminf|\{U_{n}\neq 0\}|
.

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
c:=\liminf|\{U_{n}\neq 0\}|
is finite, and proceed by contradiction, pretending that
|\{U\neq 0\}|>c
. Since

\{U\neq 0\}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{|U|\geq 1/k\},

there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
|\{|U|\geq 1/k\}|>c
. In the same fashion, there exists a large enough ball
B
such that
|\{|U|\geq 1/k\}\cap B|>c
. By Egorov’s Theorem,
B
being of finite volume, there exists
\omega\subseteq B
such that
|\{|U|\geq 1/k\}\cap\omega|>c
and
U_{n}\to U
uniformly over
\omega
. As a result for all large enough
n
,
|U_{n}|>0
over
\{|U|\geq 1/k\}\cap\omega
, which is of volume greater than
c
. In other words,
\liminf|\{U_{n}\neq 0\}|>c
, a contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
14
.

Thanks to weak
H^{m}
and strong
L^{2}
convergence of
(u_{n})_{n}
, we immediately have

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}u|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}}.

That’s why one would like to define the optimal shape as the domain where
u
or one of its derivatives does not vanish. Fortunately, the set
\Omega:=\cup_{k=0}^{m-1}\{D^{k}u\neq 0\}
is
1
-quasi-open, and
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
by Lemma
10
. Therefore,

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\Gamma(\Omega).

It remains only to show that
|\Omega|\leq c
. For that purpose, observe that not only
(u_{n})_{n}
but also
(D^{k}u_{n})_{n}
converges strongly in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0\leq k<m
, by interpolation (recall Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities). Moreover, up to a subsequence, we shall assume that the convergence holds almost everywhere. Applying Lemma
15
to the functions
U_{n}:=(u_{n},\nabla u_{n},\dots,D^{m-1}u_{n})
, we find that
|\Omega|\leq\liminf|\{U_{n}\neq 0\}|
, and thanks to the
1
-q.e. inclusion
\{U_{n}\neq 0\}\subseteq\Omega_{n}
, this shows as desired that
|\Omega|\leq c
.
∎

Let us now apply the concentration-compactness principle and proceed to the proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Consider a minimizing sequence
\Omega_{n}
for problem (
3
). Actually, thanks to Lemma
12
, we can take the same minimizing sequence for problems (
3
) and (
2
), and hence choose
\Omega_{n}
to be open. Moreover, we have
\inf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})=:I>0
. We note
u_{n}\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega_{n})
the eigenfunction on
\Omega_{n}
, and assume without loss of generality that
u_{n}
is normalised in
L^{2}
. Then, by Lemma
13
, we extract a subsequence from
(u_{n})_{n}
such that three situations shall occur: compactness, vanishing, or dichotomy.

If we are in the vanishing situation, for any
0<R<\infty
,

\lim_{n}\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{y}u_{n}|^{2}=0.

In this case, if
(u_{n})_{n}
were bounded in
H^{1}
, then thanks to
[
AB03
, Lemma 3.6]
there would exist a sequence of points
(y_{n})_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}
admit no subsequence weakly converging to
0
in
H^{1}
. Consequently, there would exist
\epsilon>0
and a test function
\varphi
with support in a ball of radius say
R>0
, such that for
n
large enough,

\epsilon\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\varphi\right)^{2}\leq\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{B_{R}}|\tau_{y}u_{n}|^{2}\int_{B_{R}}\varphi^{2}.

This would contradict the fact that
(u_{n})_{n}
vanishes. Therefore, we can extract a subsequence such that
\int_{\Omega_{n}}|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\to\infty
. But thanks to inequality (
12
), we obtain that
\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}|^{2}\to\infty
, showing in turn that
\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\to\infty
. To conclude, the vanishing situation cannot occur.

If the dichotomy occurs, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
and sequences
(u_{n}^{1})_{n}
,
(u_{n}^{2})_{n}
in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\displaystyle\|u_{n}-(u_{n}^{1}+u_{n}^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0,

\displaystyle\|u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\alpha,\qquad\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to\lambda-\alpha,

\displaystyle\liminf\left[\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right]\geq 0,

\displaystyle dist\left(\omega_{n,k}^{1},\omega_{n,k}^{2}\right)\to\infty,

\displaystyle\text{up to sets of null }(m-k)\text{-capacity},

\displaystyle\omega_{n,k}^{1},\omega_{n,k}^{2}\subseteq\omega_{n,k}\subseteq\Omega_{n},

\displaystyle\text{up to sets of null }(m-k)\text{-capacity},

where
\omega_{n,k}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\{D^{j}u_{n}\neq 0\}
and
\omega_{n,k}^{i}:=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\{D^{j}u_{n}^{i}\neq 0\}
for
i=1,2
. Then, thanks to the disjunction of
\{u_{n}^{1}\neq 0\}
and
\{u_{n}^{2}\neq 0\}
, we have that for any
\epsilon>0
, for any
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{2}|^{2}+2\epsilon,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}|^{2}\geq\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}|^{2}-2\epsilon.

Therefore,

\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}u_{n}^{2}}\geq\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}|^{2}-2\epsilon}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{1}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{2}|^{2}+2\epsilon}.

Note that
u_{n}^{1},u_{n}^{2}\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega_{n})
, hence we have,

\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\Omega_{n})}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{i}|^{2}\leq I^{-1}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{i}|^{2},\qquad i=1,2.

As a consequence, both
\liminf_{n}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}|^{2}>0
and
\liminf_{n}\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}|^{2}>0
, so we may choose
\epsilon
small enough for having
\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{i}|^{2}-\epsilon>0
for
i=1,2
and any large
n
. Then, using the algebraic property
\frac{a+b}{c+d}\geq\min\left(\frac{a}{c},\frac{b}{d}\right)
for
a,b,c,d>0
, and passing to the limit
n\to\infty
, we obtain that

\liminf_{n}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}u_{n}^{2}}\geq\min\left(\liminf_{n}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}|^{2}-\epsilon}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{1}|^{2}+\epsilon},\liminf_{n}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}|^{2}-\epsilon}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{2}|^{2}+\epsilon}\right).

Shrinking
\epsilon\to 0
and assuming that the minimum is achieved for the quotient involving
u_{n}^{1}
, we get

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\liminf_{n}\frac{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|D^{m}u_{n}^{1}|^{2}}{\int_{\Omega_{n}}|u_{n}^{1}|^{2}}.

Now, for
i=1,2
, define
\omega_{n}^{i}:=\omega_{n,m-1}^{i}=\bigcup_{0\leq k<m}\{D^{k}u_{n}^{i}\neq 0\}
. Observe that
\omega_{n}^{i}
is
1
-quasi-open, and that, by Lemma
10
,
u_{n}^{i}\in H_{0}^{m}(\omega_{n}^{i})
for
i=1,2
. In particular,

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\omega_{n}^{1}).

Moreover, since
\omega_{n}^{1}
and
\omega_{n}^{2}
are
1
-q.e. disjoint (recall that
dist(\omega_{n}^{1},\omega_{n}^{2})\to\infty
) subsets of
\Omega_{n}
, we have

|\omega_{n}^{1}|\leq|\Omega_{n}|-|\omega_{n}^{2}|.

But
\liminf_{n}|\omega_{n}^{2}|>0
. Indeed, since
u_{n}^{2}
belongs to
H_{0}^{m}(\omega_{n}^{2})
, we have thanks to relation (
10
) and to Lemma
12
,

|\omega_{n}^{2}|^{\frac{2m}{d}}\frac{\int\left|D^{m}u_{n}^{2}\right|^{2}}{\int\left|u_{n}^{2}\right|^{2}}\geq|\omega_{n}^{2}|^{\frac{2m}{d}}\Gamma(\omega_{n}^{2})\geq c^{\frac{2m}{d}}\inf\{\Gamma(\Omega):\Omega\text{ 1-quasi-open, }|\Omega|=c\}=c^{\frac{2m}{d}}I>0.

Now because
u_{n}^{2}
is uniformly bounded in
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and because
\|u_{n}^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\to 1-\alpha\neq 0
, we end up with

\liminf_{n}|\omega_{n}^{2}|>0.

As a result, we have
\limsup_{n}|\omega_{n}^{1}|=:c^{\prime}<c
. Consequently,
\Omega_{n}^{\prime}:=\left(c/|\omega_{n}^{1}|\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}\omega_{n}^{1}
is an admissible sequence made of
1
-quasi-open sets of volume
c
, hence the following contradiction

\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\omega_{n}^{1})\leq\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\leq\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n}^{\prime})=\left(c^{\prime}/c\right)^{\frac{2m}{d}}\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\omega_{n}^{1}).

We have shown that neither vanishing nor dichotomy can occur. The only remaining possibility is compactness. This means that there exists a sequence of points
(y_{n})_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for all
\epsilon>0
, there exists some radius
R>0
such that, for any
n
large enough,

\|\tau_{y_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}\geq 1-\epsilon.

First, for readability, considering
\Omega_{n}+y_{n}
instead of
\Omega_{n}
, it is possible to assume that
y_{n}=0
. Now, note that
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence converges weakly to some
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, up to a subsequence. We will see that the convergence is also
L^{2}
strong. Indeed, let
(R_{k})_{k}
be an increasing sequence of radiuses such that, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, for each large
n\in\mathbb{N},\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{k}})}\geq 1-1/k
.
Note that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
is bounded in
H^{m}(B_{R_{k}})
, hence we may extract diagonaly a subsequence converging strongly in each
L^{2}(B_{R_{k}})
to
u
. As a consequence,
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\geq 1
since for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\geq\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{k}})}=\lim_{n}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R_{k}})}\geq 1-1/k
. On the other hand, by weak convergence
\|u\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
. Therefore, in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the convergence holds simultaneously weakly and in norm, hence strongly.

Weak
H^{m}
and strong
L^{2}
convergence of
(u_{n})_{n}
towards
u
, together with Proposition
14
show that it is possible to build a
1
-quasi-open set
\Omega
, of volume lower or equal than
c
such that
\liminf_{n}\Gamma(\Omega_{n})\geq\Gamma(\Omega)
. Therefore,
\Omega
, or more precisely its dilation fitting the volume constraint, solves (
3

∎

Regularity of an optimal shape

4.1
Quasi-minimizers

In optimization, a common strategy for getting regularity is to resort to the notion of local quasi-minimizers
1
1

1

We mention that as far as we know, there is no direct link between capacity and quasi-minimizers. In particular, the prefix
“quasi”
of quasi-minimizers does not refer to any capacity.
as defined in
[
BMPV15
]
, based on the ideas of

,
AC81
]
. In this paragraph, we recall the notion of local quasi-minimizers of a functional defined on
H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Definition 16
.

Let
J:H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
be a functional and
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We say that

is a local quasi-minimizer for

if there exists
C,\alpha,r_{0}>0
such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<r_{0}

J(u)\leq J(v)+Cr^{d},

(13)

for all
v\in\mathcal{A}_{r,\alpha}:=\{v\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):u-v\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0})),\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v)|^{2}\leq\alpha\}
.

Remark 17
.

The constants
C
,
\alpha
, and

may depend not only on

For the archetypal case of Dirichlet energy functionals, a central property of quasi-minimizers is given in the next lemma.

Lemma 18
.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
J_{f}:H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

J_{f}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}v|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fv.

Let
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a local quasi-minimizer for
J_{f}
. Then, there exists
C,r_{0}>0
(depending upon
d
,
m
,
f
and
u
) such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<r_{0}
,

\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}\leq Cr^{d/2}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}\varphi|^{2}\right)^{1/2},\qquad\forall\varphi\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0})).

(14)

Remark 19
.

In the setting of Lemma
18
, (
14
) is not only implied but is actually equivalent to (
13
) (see Remark 3.3 and Remark 3.4 of
[
BMPV15
]
).

Proof.

Let
\psi\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, such that
\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}\psi|^{2}\leq\alpha
. Because
\psi\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, we may integrate by parts (recall (
6
)) to obtain

\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,\psi\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{m}u:D^{m}\psi-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\psi.

On the other hand,
v:=u-\psi\in\mathcal{A}_{r,\alpha}
, hence from the quasi-minimality of
u
we get

J_{f}(u)\leq J_{f}(v)+Cr^{d}=J_{f}(u)+J_{f}(-\psi)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{m}u:D^{m}\psi+Cr^{d},

from which we deduce

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}D^{m}u:D^{m}\psi-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\psi\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}\psi|^{2}+Cr^{d}.

Now if
\varphi\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
, set
\psi=\alpha^{1/2}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{d/2}\|D^{m}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}^{-1}\varphi
so that
\psi\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
and
\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}\psi|^{2}\leq\alpha
. The previous discussion shows that

\alpha^{1/2}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{d/2}\|D^{m}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}^{-1}\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{r}{r_{0}}\right)^{d}+Cr^{d}.

In other words, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that for all
r<r_{0}
,

\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}\leq C^{\prime}r^{d/2}\left(\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}\varphi|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

which concludes.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
5

To prove Theorem
5
, the first step is to gain regularity on an optimal eigenfunction. For that purpose, the concept of local quasi-minimizer provides an approriate framework. Indeed, the following result gives the regularity of local quasi-minimizers of Dirichlet energy functionals. The regularity obtained is not sharp in general, as one shall see in
[
BMPV15
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Theorem 20
.

Assume
d\geq 2
. Let
f\in L^{2}\cap L_{loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p\geq\frac{d}{m}
and
J_{f}:H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

J_{f}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}v|^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}fv.

(15)

Let
u\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a local quasi-minimizer for
J_{f}
. Then,
u\in C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
0<\alpha<1
.

Proof.

We proceed in a similar way than
[
Sto22
, Theorem 3 to Theorem 4]
. More precisely, our goal is to prove that for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
R_{0},C>0
(depending on
d
,
m
,
p
,
f
,
u
, and
\alpha
) such that, for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<R_{0}
,

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\leq Cr^{d-2+2\alpha}.

(16)

Indeed, due to Morrey’s Theorem (see
[
Gia83
, Chapter III, Theorem 1.1]
), this yields that
D^{m-1}u\in C^{0,\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In order to show (
16
), we let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
R>0
and define
v_{x_{0},R}
such that
u-v_{x_{0},R}\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
and
(-\Delta)^{m}v_{x_{0},R}=f
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Such a function may be constructed by minimizing
J_{f}
over the set
u-H_{0}^{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
. Now we will estimate the two terms in the right-hand side of

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\leq 2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v_{x_{0},R})|^{2}+2\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}v_{x_{0},R}|^{2}.

(17)

We will control the first term in the larger ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
proceeding as in the proof of
[
BMPV15
, inequality (A.8)]
. Indeed, integrating by parts, and then using the definition of
v_{x_{0},R}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v_{x_{0},R})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)^{m}(u-v_{x_{0},R}),(u-v_{x_{0},R})\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}

\displaystyle=\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,(u-v_{x_{0},R})\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}.

(18)

The quasi-minimality of
u
and Lemma
18
show that there exists
C,R_{0}>0
such that, as long as
R<R_{0}
,

\langle(-\Delta)^{m}u-f,(u-v_{x_{0},R})\rangle_{H^{-m},H_{0}^{m}}\leq CR^{d/2}\left(\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v_{x_{0},R})|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(19)

Finally, combining (
4.2
) and (
19
), we obtain (still for all
R<R_{0}
),

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v_{x_{0},R})|^{2}\leq CR^{d}.

(20)

For the second term in (
17
), let us decompose
v_{x_{0},R}
into
y_{x_{0},R}
and
z_{x_{0},R}
, which are defined in the following way:
y_{x_{0},R}
is the function in
H_{0}^{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
such that
(-\Delta)^{m}y_{x_{0},R}=f
; and
z_{x_{0},R}
is the
m
-polyharmonic function such that
v_{x_{0},R}-z_{x_{0},R}\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
. Notice that, using the definition of
y_{x_{0},R}
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and relation (
10
), we find

\begin{split}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}y_{x_{0},R}|^{2}=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}fy_{x_{0},R}&\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\|y_{x_{0},R}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\\
&\leq CR^{m}\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\|D^{m}y_{x_{0},R}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}.\end{split}

In particular, using Hölder’s inequality (with
q=p/2
) and the fact that
f\in L^{p}(B_{R_{0}}(x_{0}))
,

\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}y_{x_{0},R}|^{2}\leq CR^{2m}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}f^{2}\leq CR^{2m+d(1-2/p)}\leq CR^{d},

(21)

the last inequality coming from
m-d/p\geq 0
and
R\leq R_{0}
. Observe that Hölder’s inequality cannot be applied when
p\leq 2
, but in this case
d\leq 2m
, hence (
21
) follows from
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the other hand, using inequality (3.2) of
[
GM79
]
for polyharmonic functions (see also
[
Sto22
, Lemma 2]
), we obtain the existence of
C>0
such that, for all
0<r<R<R_{0}
,

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}z_{x_{0},R}|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}z_{x_{0},R}|^{2}.

Because
z_{x_{0},R}
minimizes
J_{0}
amongst functions
z
such that
v_{x_{0},R}-z\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
, the previous turns into the next

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}z_{x_{0},R}|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}.

(22)

Combining (
21
) and (
22
), we find some
C>0
such that for all
0<r<R<R_{0}
,

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}v_{x_{0},R}|^{2}\leq C\left(R^{d}+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\right).

(23)

Thanks to (
20
) and (

), we can now conclude that for some
C>0
and for all
0<r<R<R_{0}
,

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\leq C\left[R^{d}+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\right].

(24)

In view of
[
Gia83
, Chapter III, Lemma 2.1, p.86]
applied to
\phi(r)=\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}
with
\alpha=d
and
\beta=d-\gamma
for any

, we conclude that, for all
0<r<R<R_{0}
,

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{d-\gamma}\left[\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}+R^{d-\gamma}\right],

where
C
depends as usual on
d
,
m
,
p
,
f
,
u
, but now also on
\gamma
. Fixing for instance

, we end up with

\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}u|^{2}\leq Cr^{d-\gamma},

for all

, which is exactly (

) as long as we set

where

.

Thanks to Theorem
20
, it remains only to show that an optimal eigenfunction is a local quasi-minimizer, which is performed below and allows to conclude the proof of Theorem
5
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

To begin with, we explain the last part of Theorem
5
, which asserts that, as long as an eigenfunction
u
associated with
\Gamma(\Omega)
is
C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we shall construct an open set
\tilde{\Omega}
which is optimal. Indeed, we proceed in a similar fashion to the proof of
[
BMPV15
, Theorem 6.1]
, and define

\tilde{\Omega}:=\bigcup_{0\leq k<m}\{D^{k}u\neq 0\}.

Then,
\tilde{\Omega}
is open and, by Lemma
10
,
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
. In particular,
\Gamma(\tilde{\Omega})\leq\Gamma(\Omega)
. It remains to check that
\tilde{\Omega}
satisfies the volume constraint. But since
u\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)
, we know that, for each
k<m
,
\{D^{k}u\neq 0\}
is included in
\Omega
up to a set of null
(m-k)
-capacity, and hence
\tilde{\Omega}\subseteq\Omega
up to a set of null
1
-capacity. As a consequence,
|\tilde{\Omega}|\leq|\Omega|
, and
\tilde{\Omega}
satisfies the volume constraint up to a dilation, which concludes.

Let us now turn to the proof of the fact that
u
is
C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In view of Theorem
20
, it is enough to show that
u
is a local quasi-minimizer of the functional
J_{f}
defined in (
15
) for some
f\in L^{2}\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
p\geq d/m
. For that purpose, we introduce the concept of quasi-minimizer of a shape functional: we say that
\Omega
is a local quasi-minimizer of the functional
\Gamma
if there exists
C,r_{0}>0
such that, for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
0<r<r_{0}
, and for any measurable set
\Omega^{\prime}
,

\Omega^{\prime}\Delta\Omega\subseteq B_{r}(x_{0})\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\Gamma(\Omega)\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+Cr^{d},

where
\Omega^{\prime}\Delta\Omega
is the symmetric difference between
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
. As shown in
[
BMPV15
, Lemma 4.6]
, the local quasi-minimality of
u
with respect to
J_{f}
shall be deduced from the local quasi-minimality of
\Omega
with respect to
\Gamma
. Indeed, without loss of generality, we assume
u
to be
L^{2}
normalised. Then, as long as
\Omega
is a local quasi-minimizer, for any
v\in H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
v-u\in H_{0}^{m}(B_{r}(x_{0}))
and
r\in(0,r_{0})
, we have that
v\in H_{0}^{m}(\Omega\cup B_{r}(x_{0}))
, hence, using the variational definition of
\Gamma(\Omega\cup B_{r}(x_{0}))
,

\Gamma(\Omega)\leq\Gamma(\Omega\cup B_{r}(x_{0}))+Cr^{d}\leq\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}v|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{2}}+Cr^{d},

(25)

Now let
\alpha>0
and take an arbitrary
v\in\mathcal{A}_{r,\alpha}
(recall Definition
16
). Because
u
is normalised,
r<r_{0}
and
\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|D^{m}(u-v)|^{2}<\alpha
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{2}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u-v)^{2}\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}+\frac{2\alpha}{\Gamma(B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}))}\leq C,

where
C
does not depend on
v
. Hence after multiplying (
25
) by
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{2}
we obtain

\Gamma(\Omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}v|^{2}+Cr^{d}.

Using the inequality
v^{2}\geq 2uv-u^{2}
, we find

-\Gamma(\Omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{m}v|^{2}-2\Gamma(\Omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}uv+Cr^{d}.

Observe that this reads
J_{f}(u)\leq J_{f}(v)+Cr^{d}
for
f=\Gamma(\Omega)u
. As a result,
u
is a local quasi-minimizer of
J_{f}
with
f=\Gamma(\Omega)u
. At this point, we need to investigate the summability of
f
. Remark that thanks to Sobolev injections,
u\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
q=2d/(d-2m)
if
2m<d
, whereas
u\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
r\in[2,\infty)
if
2m\geq d
. The restriction
d\leq 4m
yields
q\geq d/m
. Consequently, in any case
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
p\geq d/m
, and thanks to Theorem
20

u
is then
C^{m-1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, it remains only to show the local quasi-minimality of
\Omega
with respect to
\Gamma
. As mentioned in the second point of Remark 5.2 in
[
BMPV15
]
, this will follow if we show that
\Omega
is a local shape supersolution for
\Gamma+\Lambda|\cdot|
for some
\Lambda>0
, or, in other words, that there is some
r_{0}>0
such that for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all measurable set
\Omega\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega\cup B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
,

\Gamma(\Omega)+\Lambda|\Omega|\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+\Lambda|\Omega^{\prime}|.

Indeed, if
\Omega
is such a shape super-solution, for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, any
0<r<r_{0}
, and any measurable set
\Omega^{\prime}
with
\Omega^{\prime}\Delta\Omega\subseteq B_{r}(x_{0})
, we have that
\Omega\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega\subseteq\Omega\cup B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, hence

\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})\geq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega)\geq\Gamma(\Omega)+\Lambda|\Omega|-\Lambda|\Omega^{\prime}\cup\Omega|\geq\Gamma(\Omega)-\Lambda|\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega|,

which yields as expected

\Gamma(\Omega)\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+\Lambda|B_{r}(x_{0})|.

To conclude, let us show that
\Omega
is a shape super-solution. This is a consequence of the optimality of
\Omega
, as explained in the proof of
[
BMPV15
, Theorem 6.1]
, since for any
\Omega\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega\cup B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
, setting
t=\left(|\Omega^{\prime}|/|\Omega|\right)^{\frac{1}{d}}
and recalling (
10
),

\displaystyle\Gamma(\Omega)\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime}/t)\leq t^{2m}\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+(t^{2m}-1)\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+C_{r_{0}}(t^{d}-1)\Gamma(\Omega)\leq\Gamma(\Omega^{\prime})+\Lambda(|\Omega^{\prime}\setminus\Omega|),

with
\Lambda=C_{r_{0}}\Gamma(\Omega)/|\Omega|
. Note that, besides L’Hôpital’s rule, we used the fact that
t\geq 1
is bounded when
\Omega^{\prime}\subseteq\Omega\cup B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
in order to get the bound
t^{2m}-1\leq C_{r_{0}}(t^{d}-1)
.

.

Let

. Due to Theorem

Theorem
1
is of course an advance in the context of shape optimization. However, as stated in Remark
2
, it has several limits besides the dimensional restriction. Indeed, in optimization, existence results are often used as a preliminary step in the derivation of optimality conditions in view of identifying an optimizer. Nevertheless, in shape optimization, some regularity is frequently needed on the optimal shape to apply the theory of shape derivatives in order to derive optimality conditions. See
[
HP05
, Chapter 5]
for a general theory on shape derivatives. See for instance
[
BL13
,
BL15
,
OZ00
,
Moh75
]
,
[
ABF19
, section 5]
, and
[
Ley23
, section 3]
for results on shape derivatives involving fourth and higher order operators. Finally, see
[
AB03
]
for an example where shape derivatives are performed without regularity assumption on the optimal shape.

Unfortunately, as mentioned in Remark
2
, the optimal shape of Theorem
1
enjoys only little regularity. A first step towards upgrading it would be to prove boundedness. Some results are known in this direction, but only in the case of second order operators, up to our knowledge (see
[
Buc12
,
MP13
]
). After boundedness, the question of finer regularity of the optimal set shall be tackled. See
[
LP17
]
for a review on this subject. The main results known are covered by some works of Lamboley et al.
[
BL09
,
DLPV18
,
LP23
]
. However, as far as we know, those results hold only in special situations (for instance
m=1
with
[
BL09
]
), or when ancillary constraints allow to gain regularity (see
[
DLPV18
,
LP23
]
). To conclude let us mention that
[
Sto21
]
also contains some hints on this topic in the case
m=2
(see for instance
[
Sto21
, Lemma 9]
).

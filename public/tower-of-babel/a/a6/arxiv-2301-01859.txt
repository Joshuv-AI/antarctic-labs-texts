Abstract
Zernike circular polynomials (ZCP) play a significant role in optics engineering. The symbolic expressions for ZCP are valuable for theoretic analysis and engineering designs. However, there are still two problems which remain open: firstly, there is a lack of sufficient mathematical formulas of the ZCP for optics designers; secondly the formulas for inter-conversion of Noll’s single index and Born-Wolf’s double indices of ZCP are neither uniquely determinate nor satisfactory. An automatic method for generating symbolic expressions for ZCP is proposed based on five essential factors: the new theorems for converting the single/double indices of the ZCP, the robust and effective numeric algorithms for computing key parameters of ZCP, the symbolic algorithms for generating mathematical expressions of ZCP, and meta-programming & L a T e X programming for generating the table of ZCP. The theorems, method, algorithms and system architecture proposed are beneficial to both optics design process, optics software, computer-output typesetting in publishing industry as well as STEM education. Keywords : Zernike circular polynomial, Symbolic computation, Mathematical table, Computer-output typesetting, L a T e X programming, STEM education

2.1
Fundamentals of Mathematics

2.1.1
Notations for Integers

For an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}=\left\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\cdots\right\}
, it is even if and only if
2\mid n
, i.e.,
2
divides
n
or equivalently
n\equiv 0\penalty\ (\MOD 2)
; otherwise, it is odd if and only if
2\nmid n
or equivalently
n\equiv 1\penalty\ (\MOD 2)
. The sets of even and odd integers are denoted by

\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{odd}}=\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:2\nmid i\right\}=\left\{\pm 1,\pm 3,\pm 5,\cdots\right\}

and

\mathbb{Z}_{\mathrm{even}}=\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:2\mid i\right\}=\left\{0,\pm 2,\pm 4,\pm 6,\cdots\right\}

respectively. The set of positive integers are
\mathbb{N}=\left\{1,2,3,\cdots\right\}
and the set of non-negative integers is

\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}=\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\}
. Similarly, the set of negative integers is
\mathbb{Z}^{-}=-\mathbb{N}=\left\{-1,-2,-3,\cdots\right\}
. In consequence, the sets of non-negative even integers and non-negative odd integers can be denoted by

{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{even}}^{+}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{+}
respectively.

For any real number
x\in\mathbb{R}
, the unique integer
n=\left\lfloor{x}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n\leq x<n+1
is called the floor of
x
.
Similarly, the integer
n^{\prime}=\left\lceil{x}\right\rceil\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n^{\prime}-1<x\leq n^{\prime}
is called the ceiling of
x
.

2.1.2
Expression of General Polynomials

A polynomial
T_{n}(x)
with argument
x
and degree
n
can be written by

T_{n}(x)=\sum^{n}_{i=0}a_{i}x^{i}=a_{0}+a_{1}x+\cdots+a_{i}x^{i}+\cdots+a_{n}x^{n}

(1)

If
a_{i}=0
for all odd
i
, then
T_{n}(x)
is an even polynomial. Similarly, if
a_{i}=0
for all even
i
, then
T_{n}(x)
is an odd polynomial.
Futhermore, some of the coefficients
a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}
may be missing if they equal zero. In consequence, we can denote a polynomial as

T(x)=\sum^{k}_{s=0}c_{s}x^{p_{s}},\quad p_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

(2)

where
p_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. In other words, a polynomial can be described by the sequence of coefficients
\texttt{c}=\langle c_{0},\cdots,c_{k}\rangle
and the corresponding sequence of power indices
\texttt{p}=\langle p_{0},\cdots,p_{k}\rangle
.

2.1.3
Binomial and Tri-nomial Coefficients

In general, for any real number
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and non-negative
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}=\left\{0,1,2,\cdots\right\}
the generalized binomial expansion can be expressed by

(1+x)^{\alpha}=\sum^{\infty}_{i=0}\binom{\alpha}{i}x^{i},\quad x\in\mathrm{ROC}=(-1,1)

in which ROC is the region of convergence and

\binom{\alpha}{i}=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-i+1)}{i!}

(3)

is the binomial coefficient
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Particularly, if
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is a positive integer, we have

\binom{\alpha}{i}=\frac{\alpha!}{i!(\alpha-i)!}.

(4)

For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
we have

(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})^{r}=\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}=r}\binom{r}{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}x^{i_{1}}_{1}x^{i_{2}}_{2}x^{i_{3}}_{3}

where

\binom{r}{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}=\frac{(i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3})!}{i_{1}!i_{2}!i_{3}!},\quad i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}=r

(5)

is the tri-nomial coefficients, which is symmetric for a permutation of
i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}
and can be expressed in terms of binomial coefficients:

\binom{r}{i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}}=\binom{i_{1}+i_{2}}{i_{1}}\binom{i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}}{i_{1}+i_{2}}.

(6)

Therefore, for
r=n-s,p=3,i_{1}=s,i_{2}=k-s,i_{3}=n-k-s,i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}=n-s
, we can obtain

\binom{n-s}{s,k-s,n-k-s}=\binom{k}{s}\binom{n-s}{k}.

(7)

When computing the value of binomial coefficients with computer programs, we should keep an eye on the overflow problem since the factorial
r!
increases rapidly with the integer
r
. We can avoid such a risk by reformulating the binomial coefficient properly, i.e., for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}=\left\{0\right\}\cup\mathbb{N}
,

\binom{\alpha}{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&i=0;\\
\prod\limits^{i-1}_{t=0}\cfrac{\alpha-t}{i-t},&i\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(8)

The value of

can be computed with

2.2
Zernike Circular Polynomials

2.2.1
Definition of Zernike Circular Polynomials

Generally, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\left|m\right|\leq n
and
2\mid(n-m)
, the ZCP can be denoted by
[
1
,
3
,
4
]

\operatorname{Z}_{n}^{m}(\rho,\theta)=N^{m}_{n}\operatorname{R}_{n}^{m}(\rho)\Theta_{m}(\theta),\quad\rho\in[0,1],\theta\in[0,2\pi]

(12)

where

N^{m}_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{2(n+1)}{1+\delta_{m0}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{2(n+1)},&m\neq 0\\
\sqrt{n+1},&m=0\end{array}\right.

(13)

is the coefficients for normalization,

\Theta_{m}(\theta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&m=0,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{+}\\
\cos(\left|m\right|\theta),&m\neq 0,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{even}}^{+}\\
\sin(\left|m\right|\theta),&m\neq 0,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{+}\end{array}\right.

(14)

is the angular function with equivalent complex form
\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\left|m\right|\theta}
,

\begin{split}\operatorname{R}_{n}^{m}(\rho)&=\frac{1}{(\frac{n-\left|m\right|}{2})!\rho^{\left|m\right|}}\left[\frac{\dif}{\dif(\rho^{2})}\right]^{\frac{n-\left|m\right|}{2}}\left[(\rho^{2})^{\frac{n+\left|m\right|}{2}}(\rho^{2}-1)^{\frac{n-\left|m\right|}{2}}\right]\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{\frac{n-\left|m\right|}{2}}(-1)^{s}\cdot\frac{(n-s)!}{s!\left(\frac{n+\left|m\right|}{2}-s\right)!\left(\frac{n-\left|m\right|}{2}-s\right)!}\cdot\rho^{n-2s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{k}(-1)^{s}\cdot\binom{n-s}{s,k-s,n-k-s}\cdot\rho^{n-2s}\\
&=\sum_{s=0}^{k}c_{s}\rho^{p_{s}}=c_{0}\rho^{n}+c_{1}\rho^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{k}\rho^{n-2k}\end{split}

(15)

is the radial Zernike polynomials in which

k=\frac{1}{2}(n-\left|m\right|)

(16)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} c_{s}&=(-1)^{s}\binom{n-s}{s,k-s,n-k-s},\quad 0\leq s\leq k;\\
p_{s}&=n-2s,\quad 0\leq s\leq k.\end{aligned}\right.

(17)

Substituting (
7
) into (
17
), we obtain the following efficient expression for computing
c_{s}

c_{s}=(-1)^{s}\binom{k}{s}\binom{n-s}{k},\quad 0\leq s\leq k.

(18)

The second scheme is characterized by a single index

[
11
]
, in which

is used to represent the order instead of
n
and

. In the software ZEMAX for optics design, the Noll’s notation

is taken.

•

ZEMAX ”Zernike Fringe Polynomials”

3
Inter-conversion of Single/Double Indices of Zernike Circular Polynomials

The inter-conversion of the double indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
and single index
j
is significant for generating the table of ZCP and looking up the table for the polynomials of interests automatically. In this section, we will establish new formula and theorems to specify the implementation of the inter-conversion for Noll’s single index
j
and Born-Wolf’s double indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
.

3.1
Conversion of Double/Single Indices

The Noll’s index
j
and the Born-Wolf’s indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
can be converted to each other and the conversions can be uniquely determined.

Theorem 1
.

The Noll’s index
j
can be computed with Born-Wolf’s indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
by

j(n,m)=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+\left|m\right|+u(m)

(24)

for
\left|m\right|\leq n
and
2\mid(n-m)
.

Proof
: Let

\displaystyle V_{n}

\displaystyle=\langle v_{1},\cdots,v_{r},\cdots,v_{n+1}\ \rangle

(25)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle 0,-2,2,-4,4,\cdots,-n,n\rangle,&n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{even}}^{+}\\
\langle-1,1,-3,3,\cdots,-n,n\rangle,&n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathrm{odd}}^{+}\end{array}\right.

be the sequence of possible integers
m
such that

m=v_{r}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r-1,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\nmid r\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\mid n;\\
-r,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\mid r\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\mid n;\\
-r,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\nmid r\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\nmid n;\\
r-1,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\mid r\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\nmid n.\end{array}\right.

(26)

In other words, the integer

r=\left|m\right|+u(m)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m+1,&m\geq 0\\
-m,&m<0\end{array}\right.

(27)

is the subscript
r
or the position in the sequence
V_{n}
for
m=v_{r}\in V_{n}
. It is easy to find that the cardinality of
V_{n}
is

\left|V_{n}\right|=n+1,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

(28)

Therefore, for
i\in\left\{0,1,\cdots,n-1\right\}
, the total number of Zernike polynomial
\operatorname{Z}_{i}^{m}(\rho,\theta)
is

\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}\left|V_{i}\right|=\sum^{n-1}_{i=0}(i+1)=1+2+\cdots+n=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}.

Thus for the given
n
and
m
, let
r
be position of
m\in V_{n\MOD 2}
, then the corresponding index
j
for the

\Zern_{j}(\rho,\theta)=\operatorname{Z}_{n}^{m}(\rho,\theta)
must be the sum of base position
n(n+1)/2
and relative position
r
, i.e.,

j=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+r

(29)

This implies that (
24
) holds since we have (
27
). Q.E.D.

Conversion of Single/Double Indices

As mentioned above, there is a lack of simple formulae for converting Noll’s single index
j
to Born-Wolf’s double indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
. The following theorem remedies the defects.

Theorem 2
.

Given the Noll index
j
, the Born-Wolf’s indices
\left\langle{n},{m}\right\rangle
can be computed with the following
expressions:

\displaystyle n(j)

\displaystyle=\left\lceil{\frac{-3+\sqrt{8j+1}}{2}}\right\rceil,

(30)

\displaystyle r(j)

\displaystyle=j-\frac{n(j)[n(j)+1]}{2},

(31)

\displaystyle m(j)

\displaystyle=v_{r(j)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}r(j)-1,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\nmid r(j)\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\mid n(j);\\
-r(j),&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\mid r(j)\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\mid n(j);\\
-r(j),&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\nmid r(j)\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\nmid n(j);\\
r(j)-1,&\mathrm{for}\penalty\ 2\mid r(j)\penalty\ \mathrm{and}\penalty\ 2\nmid n(j).\end{array}\right.

Proof
: By (
24
), we have

\frac{n(n+1)}{2}<j\leq\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+n+1.

(36)

Consequently,

\frac{-3+\sqrt{8j+1}}{2}\leq n<\frac{-1+\sqrt{8j+1}}{2}

(37)

This implies (
30
) by the definition of floor of real number. (
31
) is obvious by (
29
).
With the help of (
26
) we immediately have (
2
). Q.E.D.

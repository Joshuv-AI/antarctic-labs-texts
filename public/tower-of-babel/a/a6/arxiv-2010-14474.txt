Standard monomials of 1 1 -skeleton ideals of multigraphs
Amit Roy

Abstract
. Given a graph G G on the vertex set { 0 , 1 , … , n } \{0,1,\ldots,n\} with the root vertex 0 0 , Postnikov and Shapiro associated a monomial ideal ℳ G \mathcal{M}_{G} in the polynomial ring R = 𝕂 ⁡ [ x 1 , … , x n ] R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}] over a field 𝕂 \mathbb{K} such that dim 𝕂 ( R / ℳ G ) = det L ~ G \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G})=\det\widetilde{L}_{G} , where L ~ G \widetilde{L}_{G} is the truncated Laplacian of G G . Dochtermann introduced the 1 1 -skeleton ideal ℳ G ( 1 ) \mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)} of ℳ G \mathcal{M}_{G} which satisfies the property that dim 𝕂 ( R / ℳ G ( 1 ) ) ≥ det Q ~ G \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})\geq\det\widetilde{Q}_{G} , where Q ~ G \widetilde{Q}_{G} is the truncated signless Laplacian of G G . In this paper we characterize all subgraphs of the multigraph K n + 1 a , 1 K_{n+1}^{a,1} , in particular all simple graphs G G , such that dim 𝕂 ( R / ℳ G ( 1 ) ) = det Q ~ G \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G} . Moreover, we give examples of subgraphs G G of the complete multigraph K n + 1 a , b K_{n+1}^{a,b} , in which the equality dim 𝕂 ( R / ℳ G ( 1 ) ) = det Q ~ G \dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G} holds. We also provide a conjecture on the structure of a general multigraph satisfying the above-mentioned equality.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a multigraph on the vertex set
V=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}=\{0\}\cup[n]
with root
0
and the set of edges
E(i,j)=E(j,i)
between
i,j\in V
. The adjacency matrix of
G
is given by
A(G)=[a_{i,j}]_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
, where
a_{i,j}=a_{j,i}=|E(i,j)|
for
i\neq j
, and
a_{i,i}=0
for all vertex
i,j
in
G
. Therefore,
G
is assumed to be undirected and loopless. For any nonempty subset
S
of
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, let
d_{S}(i)=\sum_{j\notin S}a_{i,j}
for
i\in S
. Thus
d_{S}(i)
is the number of edges incident to the vertex
i
whose other end points are not in
S
. In particular,
d_{i}:=d_{\{i\}}(i)
is the degree of the vertex
i
in
G
. For a graph
G
, the Laplacian
L_{G}
and the signless Laplacian
Q_{G}
are defined as

L_{G}=D(G)-A(G)\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{G}=D(G)+A(G),

where
D(G)=\operatorname{diag}[d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}]
is the diagonal matrix of order
n+1
. Let
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
\mathbb{K}
. Given a multigraph
G
on the vertex set
V
, we consider the monomial ideal

\mathcal{M}_{G}=\left\langle m_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}^{d_{S}(i)}\mid\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[n]\right\rangle

in
R
, called the
G
-parking function ideal or the graphical parking function ideal
[
6
]
. More generally, for a directed graph
G
, Postnikov and Shapiro introduced the Artinian monomial ideal
\mathcal{M}_{G}
and studied their combinatorial and homological properties in
[
13
]
. The standard monomials
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{a_{i}}
of
R/\mathcal{M}_{G}
(i.e.,
\mathbf{x}^{\mathbf{a}}\notin\mathcal{M}_{G}
) correspond to the
G
-parking functions
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, a natural generalization of the classical parking functions introduced by Konheim and Weiss
[
9
]
. Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G})=\det\widetilde{L}_{G}
, where
\widetilde{L}_{G}
is the truncated Laplacian obtained from
L_{G}
by deleting the row and column corresponding to the root vertex. Thus by the Matrix-Tree theorem
[
14
, Theorem 5.6.8]
, the number of
G
-parking functions equals the number of spanning trees of
G
. For directed graphs Chebikin and Pylyavskyy
[
3
]
, for simple graphs Perkinson, Yang and Yu
[
12
]
, and for multigraphs Gaydarov and Hopkins
[
6
]
have provided an explicit bijection between the set of spanning trees of
G
and the set of
G
-parking functions. The ideals
\mathcal{M}_{G}
have connections to ‘chip firing’
[
2
]
and a discrete Riemann-Roch theory for graphs
[
1
]
. For instance, Baker and Norine reinterpreted the standard monomials of
\mathcal{M}_{G}
as ‘
q
-reduced divisors’ in
[
1
]
, where they proved a Riemann-Roch theorem for graphs analogous to the classical statement for Riemann surfaces. Motivated by certain constructions in ‘hereditary chip firing’ models, Dochtermann
[
4
]
introduced the notion of
k
-skeleton ideals
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(k)}
for a simple graph
G
. However, as noted in
[
4
]
the definitions are easily extended for a multigraph.

For
0\leq k\leq n-1
, the ideals

\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(k)}:=\left\langle m_{S}=\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}^{d_{S}(i)}\mid\emptyset\neq S\subseteq[n]\quad\text{and}\quad|S|\leq k+1\right\rangle

are by definition subideals of
\mathcal{M}_{G}
, where
G
is a multigraph on the vertex set
\{0\}\cup[n]
. Note that for
k=n-1
,
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(n-1)}=\mathcal{M}_{G}
. For a complete simple graph
G
and for all positive integer
k
, the Betti numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(k)}
are determined in
[
11
]
. Moreover, the standard monomials of
R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(k)}
for such a
G
are identified with certain kind of vector-parking functions in
[
5
]
.

Analogous to the ideal
\mathcal{M}_{G}
, the number of standard monomials of
R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
has a determinantal interpretation. Let
a,b\geq 1
be integers and
G
be a multigraph with adjacency matrix
A(G)=[a_{i,j}]_{0\leq i,j\leq n}
, where
a_{0,i}=a
and
a_{i,j}=b
for
i,j\in[n]
;
i\neq j
. The graph
G
described above is called a complete multigraph and is denoted by
K_{n+1}^{a,b}
. Dochtermann showed that for the complete simple graph
G=K_{n+1}:=K_{n+1}^{1,1}
, the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
holds, where
\widetilde{Q}_{G}
is the truncated signless Laplacian of
G
obtained from
Q_{G}
by deleting the row and column corresponding to the root
0
. Moreover, based on some initial computations, he asked whether it is true that for any simple graph
G
,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})\geq\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. This question has been answered affirmatively in
[
10
]
. In fact, it is shown that for any multigraph
G
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})\geq\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
; thus providing a lower bound for the number of standard monomials of
R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
. As mentioned earlier, the graph
K_{n+1}
attains this lower bound. Under certain conditions a characterization of the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
for simple graphs
G
is obtained in
[
10
]
.

In this article, we consider the question of classifying all multigraphs
G
which satisfy the property that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. For this, we first define a
d
-fold product

G_{1}*_{d}G_{2}
between two graphs
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, where
d\geq 1
and
|V(G_{1}*G_{2})|=|V(G_{1})|+|V(G_{2})|-1
(see Definition
2.5
). In Section
2
, we prove the following result which gives a new family of multigraph
G
that satisfies the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
.

(i) (Corollary
2.7
)
Let
G_{i}
be a multigraph on the vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n_{i}\}
obtained from a complete multigraph
K_{n_{i}+1}^{a_{i},b_{i}}
by removing some edges through the root
0
, where
1\leq i\leq r
. Suppose
n=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
. Let
G=((\cdots(G_{1}*_{d_{1}}G_{2})*_{d_{2}}\cdots*_{d_{r-2}}G_{r-1})*_{d_{r-1}}G_{r})
be the multigraph on the vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, where
d_{1}\geq d_{2}\geq\dots\geq d_{r-1}
are nonnegative integers,
b_{1}\geq d_{1}
, and
b_{i}\geq d_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq r
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
.

In Section
3
we define the
maximally connected subgraphs
of a graph
G
and prove the following classification result for subgraphs of the multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
.

(ii) (Theorem
3.11
) Let
G
be a subgraph of the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
on the vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
. The graph
G
satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
if and only if each maximally connected subgraph
G_{i}
of
G
with
|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}
, is obtained from a complete multigraph
K_{n_{i}+1}^{a_{i},1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
.

As an immediate corollary we obtain the following classification result for simple graphs.

(iii) (Corrolary
3.12
)
Let
G
be a simple graph on
n+1
vertices
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
holds if and only if each maximally connected subgraph
G_{i}
of
G
with
|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}
, is obtained from a complete simple graph
K_{n_{i}+1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
.

Finally, based on some computations we conjecture in Section
4
that if
G
is a multigraph satisfying the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
, then after a renumbering of the vertices,
G
is the graph described as in Corollary
2.7
(see Conjecture
4.1
).

2.
Standard monomials of a family of multigraphs

In this section we consider a monomial ideal defined by certain symmetric matrices. Using this we provide a new family of multigraphs
G
satisfying the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
M_{n}(\mathbb{N})
be the set of
n\times n
matrices over nonnegative integers
\mathbb{N}
. Let

\mathcal{G}_{n}=\{H=[h_{i,j}]\in M_{n}(\mathbb{N})\mid H^{t}=H\,\text{and}\,h_{ii}\geq\max_{j\neq i}h_{i,j}\,\text{for}\,1\leq i\leq n.\}

For
H=[h_{i,j}]\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
with
\alpha_{i}=h_{i,i}
, we associate a monomial ideal

\mathcal{J}_{H}=\left\langle x_{t}^{\alpha_{t}},x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}-h_{i,j}}x_{j}^{\alpha_{j}-h_{i,j}}\mid 1\leq t\leq n,\,1\leq i<j\leq n\right\rangle

in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Note that if we consider the matrix
H=\widetilde{Q}_{G}
, then
\mathcal{J}_{H}=\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
. The ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}
was introduced in
[
10
]
where the following lower bound for the number of standard monomials was given in terms of the determinant of the matrix
H
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
10
, Theorem 3.3]

Let
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
be positive semidefinite and let
\mathcal{J}_{H}
be the monomial ideal in the polynomial ring
R
associated to
H
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}\right)\geq\det H.

By the above theorem, for a multigraph
G
we have
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})\geq\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
, since
\widetilde{Q}_{G}
is a positive semidefinite matrix
[
10
]
. Theorem
2.1
is one of the key ingredients to obtain our results for the ideals
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
. In fact, we obtain all our results for the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}
, where
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
and then specialize them to the
1
-skeleton ideals
\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
.

Consider the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,b}
and the monomial ideal
\mathcal{M}_{K_{n+1}^{a,b}}^{(1)}
in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Sometimes we write
R=R_{n}
to indicate the number of variables in
R
. Observe that the matrix
\widetilde{Q}_{K_{n+1}^{a,b}}=[h_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, where
h_{i,i}=a+(n-1)b
for
i\in[n]
and
h_{i,j}=b
for
i\neq j
. The matrix
\widetilde{Q}_{K_{n+1}^{a,b}}
is in
\mathcal{G}_{n}
. It has been shown in
[
11
, Remark 2.7]
that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{K_{n+1}^{a,b}}^{(1)}\right)=\det\widetilde{Q}_{K_{n+1}^{a,b}}
. This result has been generalized in the following way.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
10
, Theorem 2.5]

Let
G
be a multigraph on
V=\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
obtained from the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,b}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
. Then

(2.1)

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}}\right)=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}.

We now proceed to give a generalization of Theorem
2.2
in the context of the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}
for
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
. Recall that given a matrix
H\in\mathcal{G}_{n}
we have associated the monomial ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}\subseteq R_{n}
such that
\mathcal{J}_{H}=\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)}
when
H=\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. For the graph
G
in Theorem
2.2
, the truncated signless Laplacian
\widetilde{Q}_{G}=[h_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
, where
b\leq h_{i,i}\leq a+(n-1)b
for
i\in[n]
and
h_{i,j}=b
for
i\neq j
.

In the calculations below the
i^{th}
row and
j^{th}
column of a matrix
H
are denoted by
\mathcal{R}_{i}
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}
, respectively.
The elementary column operation
\mathcal{C}_{j}\pm(\mathcal{C}_{k_{1}}+\dots+\mathcal{C}_{k_{r}})
on
H
means the matrix
H
is transformed to a matrix
H^{\prime}
, where only
j^{th}
column
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\prime}
of
H^{\prime}
differs from the
j^{th}
column
\mathcal{C}_{j}
of
H
and
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}_{j}\pm(\mathcal{C}_{k_{1}}+\dots+\mathcal{C}_{k_{r}})
. The elementary row operations are also defined in a similar way.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
H=[h_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

be a matrix over nonnegative integers such that
h_{i,i}=a_{i}
for
i\in[n]
and
h_{i,j}=b
for
i\neq j
, where
a_{i}\geq b
for each
i\in[n]
. Then

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}\right)=\det H.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
n
. For
n=2
we have that
H=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}&b\\
b&a_{2}\end{bmatrix}_{2\times 2}
and the ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}=\left\langle x_{1}^{a_{1}},x_{2}^{a_{2}},x_{1}^{a_{1}-b}x_{2}^{a_{2}-b}\right\rangle
. Thus
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{2}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=a_{1}a_{2}-b^{2}=\det H
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
and the theorem is true for any
m
with
m<n
. If
b=0
, then
\mathcal{J}_{H}=\left\langle x_{i}^{a_{i}}:1\leq i\leq n\right\rangle
. Thus
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\det H
. If
a_{i}=b
for each
i
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=0=\det H
. Hence, without loss of generality, assume that
a_{1}>b>0
. Let
r=a_{1}-b>0
. The ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}=\left\langle x_{l}^{a_{l}},x_{i}^{a_{i}-b}x_{j}^{a_{j}-b}:1\leq l\leq n,~1\leq i<j\leq n\right\rangle
.

Let
H_{1}=\operatorname{diag}[b,a_{2}-b,a_{3}-b,\ldots,a_{n}-b]
be the
n\times n
diagonal matrix and let
H_{2}
be the matrix obtained from
H
by deleting row
\mathcal{R}_{1}
and column
\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
We see that
\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{1}^{r}\right)=\mathcal{J}_{H_{1}}
and
\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{1}^{r}\right\rangle=\langle\mathcal{J}_{H_{2}},x_{1}^{r}\rangle
. Therefore,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{1}^{r}\right))=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H_{1}})=\det H_{1}
(since
H_{1}
is a diagonal matrix). Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{1}^{r}\right\rangle)=r\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n-1}/\mathcal{J}_{H_{2}})=r\det H_{2}
(by induction hypothesis). Consider the short exact sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces,

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\frac{R_{n}}{\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{1}^{r}\right)}\xrightarrow{\mu_{x_{1}^{r}}}\frac{R_{n}}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}x\xrightarrow{\nu}\frac{R_{n}}{\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{1}^{r}\right\rangle}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mu_{x_{1}^{r}}
is the map induced by multiplication by
x_{1}^{r}
and
\nu
is the natural quotient map. We have,
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{1}^{r}\right))+\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{1}^{r}\right\rangle)=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H_{1}})+r\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n-1}/\mathcal{J}_{H_{2}})
. Hence,

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\det H_{1}+r\det H_{2}.

Writing
a_{1}=b+r
and using the additivity property of the determinant, we see that
\det H=r\det H_{2}+\det A
, where
A
is the matrix obtained from
H
by replacing the element
a_{1}
with
b
. On applying elementary column and row operations,
\mathcal{C}_{2}-\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{2}-\mathcal{R}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{n}-\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{R}_{n}-\mathcal{R}_{1}
on
A
, it reduces to the matrix
H_{1}
.
Thus
\det A=\det H_{1}
and hence
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\det H
. ∎

In the next theorem we further generalize Theorem
2.3
. Consequently, we provide some new families of multigraphs
G
which satisfy
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
H_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{i,1}&b_{i}&\cdots&b_{i}\\
b_{i}&\alpha_{i,2}&\cdots&b_{i}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{i}&b_{i}&\cdots&\alpha_{i,n_{i}}\end{bmatrix}_{n_{i}\times n_{i}}
with
\alpha_{i,j}\geq b_{i}
and let
A_{i}
be the
n_{i}\times n_{i}
matrix with all entries equal to
d_{i}
. Consider the matrix

(2.3)

\displaystyle H=\begin{bmatrix}H_{1}&A_{1}&A_{2}&\cdots&A_{r-1}\\
A_{1}&H_{2}&A_{2}&\cdots&A_{r-1}\\
A_{2}&A_{2}&H_{3}&\cdots&A_{r-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
A_{r-1}&A_{r-1}&A_{r-1}&\cdots&H_{r}\end{bmatrix}_{\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}\times\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}}

with
b_{1}\geq d_{1},\,b_{i}\geq d_{i-1}
for
2\leq i\leq r
, and
d_{i}\geq d_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq r-2
. Assume that
\alpha_{i,j},\,b_{i}
and
d_{i}
are all nonnegative integers. Suppose
n=\sum_{i=1}^{r}n_{i}
. Then
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\det H
.

Proof.

If
n=2
, then we see that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{2}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\det H
. We prove the theorem by induction on the order
n
of
H
. Assume that
n\geq 3
and the theorem holds for every
m\times m
matrix of the above form for
m<n
. The monomial ideal
\mathcal{J}_{H}
is generated by the following monomials

\displaystyle x_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}

\displaystyle:1\leq i\leq r,~1\leq j\leq n_{i}\,,

\displaystyle x_{i,u}^{\alpha_{i,u}-b_{i}}x_{i,v}^{\alpha_{i,v}-b_{i}}

\displaystyle:1\leq i\leq r,~1\leq u<v\leq n_{i}\,,

\displaystyle x_{s,w}^{\alpha_{s,w}-d_{t-1}}x_{t,l}^{\alpha_{t,l}-d_{t-1}}

\displaystyle:1\leq s\leq r-1,~1\leq w\leq n_{s},~s+1\leq t\leq r,~1\leq l\leq n_{t}\,.

We now divide the proof into two cases:

Case I :

n_{r}=1
. Let
B_{1}
be the matrix obtained from
H
by deleting the row and column containing the diagonal element
\alpha_{r1}
and
B_{2}
be an
(n-1)\times(n-1)
matrix whose all entries are
d_{r-1}
. Let
B_{3}=B_{1}-B_{2}
. We see that the ideals
\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right)=\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{B_{3}},x_{r,1}^{d_{r-1}}\right\rangle
and
\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{B_{1}},x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right\rangle
. By the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n-1}}{\mathcal{J}_{B_{3}}}\right)=\det B_{3}\quad\text{and}\quad\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n-1}}{\mathcal{J}_{B_{1}}}\right)=\det B_{1}.

Thus
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(R_{n}/(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}})\right)=d_{r-1}\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n-1}/\mathcal{J}_{B_{3}})=d_{r-1}\det B_{3}
. Moreover, we see that
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(R_{n}/\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right\rangle\right)=(\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1})\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n-1}/\mathcal{J}_{B_{1}})=(\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1})\det B_{1}
.
Using the short exact sequence of
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\frac{R_{n}}{\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right)}\xrightarrow{\mu_{x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}}}\frac{R_{n}}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}\xrightarrow{\nu}\frac{R_{n}}{\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{r,1}^{\alpha_{r,1}-d_{r-1}}\right\rangle}\rightarrow 0,

we get

. Here

is the map induced by multiplication by

and

is the natural quotient map. As the determinant is linear on columns, writing

, we have

, where

is the matrix obtained from

with

on

Case II :

. Let

be the matrix obtained from

. Thus

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{r,n_{r}}^{\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r}}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n_{r}-1}(\alpha_{r,i}-b_{r})\right)\dim_{\mathbb{K}}\left(\frac{R_{n+1-n_{r}}}{\mathcal{J}_{B_{5}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n_{r}-1}(\alpha_{r,i}-b_{r})\right)\det B_{5}

\displaystyle=(\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r})\det B_{6}.

-vector spaces

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\frac{R_{n}}{\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{r,n_{r}}^{\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r}}\right)}\xrightarrow{\mu_{x_{r,n_{r}}^{\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r}}}}\frac{R_{n}}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}\xrightarrow{\nu}\frac{R_{n}}{\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{r,n_{r}}^{\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r}}\right\rangle}\rightarrow 0,

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{J}_{H})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n_{r}-1}(\alpha_{r,i}-b_{r})\right)\det B_{5}+(\alpha_{r,n_{r}}-b_{r})\det B_{6}

3.
Characterizing subgraphs of

In this section we classify all subgraphs
G
of the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
that satisfy
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. In order to prove this we need the following results related to Hermitian matrices and their eigenvalues.

Let
M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a Hermitian matrix. The eigenvalues of
M
are arranged in a non-decreasing order
\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\lambda_{2}(M)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(M)
. The Courant-Weyl inequalities
[
8
, Theorem 4.3.1]
compare eigenvalues of two Hermitian matrices with the eigenvalues of their sum.

Theorem 3.1

(Courant-Weyl)
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
be Hermitian matrices. Then

\lambda_{i}(M_{1}+M_{2})\leq\lambda_{i+j}(M_{1})+\lambda_{n-j}(M_{2})~{\rm for}~j=0,1,\ldots,n-i.

The following application of the Courant-Weyl inequalities will be used in our proofs.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
M=(a_{i,j})_{n\times n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a Hermitian positive semidefinite matrix. Suppose
N
is obtained from
M
by replacing one diagonal element, say
a_{i,i}
, with a real number
b
such that
a_{i,i}\geq b
. If
\det N>0
, then
N
is a positive definite matrix.

Proof.

Let
\epsilon_{i,j}
be the
n\times n
matrix with
1
at the
(i,j)^{th}
place and zero elsewhere. Then
M=N+N^{\prime}
, where
N^{\prime}=(a_{i,i}-b)\epsilon_{i,i}
. Clearly,
\lambda_{1}(N^{\prime})=\cdots=\lambda_{n-1}(N^{\prime})=0
and
\lambda_{n}(N^{\prime})=a_{i,i}-b
. Since
M
is positive semidefinite,
0\leq\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}(M)
. Taking
i=j=1
in the Courant-Weyl inequalities for
M=N+N^{\prime}
, we obtain
\lambda_{1}(M)\leq\lambda_{2}(N)+\lambda_{n-1}(N^{\prime})=\lambda_{2}(N)
. Thus
0\leq\lambda_{2}(N)\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}(N)
. As
\det N=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(N)>0
,
N
must be positive definite. ∎

Given a block Hermitian matrix
M
the Fischer’s inequality provides an upper bound for
\det M
in terms of the determinants of its diagonal blocks.

Theorem 3.3

(Fischer)
.

[
8
, Theorem 7.8.5]

Let
M\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
be a positive semidefinite matrix having block decomposition

M=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}A&B\\
\hline\cr B^{*}&C\end{array}\right)
with square matrices
A
and
C
. Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\det M\leq\det(A)\det(C).

If
M
is positive definite then equality occurs in (
3.1
) if and only if
B=0
.

The following Lemma will show us that in order to check whether a graph

satisfy
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
, we only need to consider those graphs which have exactly two connected components after removing all the rooted-edges. We call these graphs as ‘maximally connected’ graphs (see Definition
3.6
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a multigraph on
\{0,1,\ldots,t\}
and let
\widetilde{G}
be the induced subgraph on the vertex set
[t]
. Suppose the connected components of
\widetilde{G}
are
\widetilde{G}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{G}_{r}
with

, where

for

. Consider the induced subgraphs
G_{i}=G_{V_{i}\cup\{0\}}
of

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{t}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{t_{i}}/\mathcal{M}_{G_{i}}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G_{i}}
for

Proof.

We observe that the

such that

Also, the truncated signless Laplacian

is a block-diagonal matrix with each block corresponds to the truncated signless Laplacian of

. Therefore,
\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\det\widetilde{Q}_{G_{i}}
. By Theorem

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{t}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
if and only if
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{t_{i}}/\mathcal{M}_{G_{i}}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G_{i}}
for

. ∎

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\frac{R_{t}}{\left(\mathcal{J}_{H}:x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}-b}\right)}\xrightarrow{\mu_{x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}-b}}}\frac{R_{t}}{\mathcal{J}_{H}}\xrightarrow{\nu}\frac{R_{t}}{\left\langle\mathcal{J}_{H},x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}-b}\right\rangle}\rightarrow 0,

In view of Theorem
2.2
, if
G
is a simple graph on the vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, obtained from a complete simple graph
K_{n+1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
3.4
, in order to check for a graph
G
when the equality
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
holds we just need to check for its maximally connected subgraphs. Now as a consequence of Theorem
3.10
we can characterize all simple graphs
G
which satisfy the property
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. More generally, we prove the following:

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
G
be a subgraph of the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
on the vertex set
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
. The graph
G
satisfies
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
if and only if each maximally connected subgraph
G_{i}
of
G
with
|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}
, is obtained from a complete multigraph
K_{n_{i}+1}^{a_{i},1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
.

Proof.

First suppose that
G
is a subgraph of the complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
such that
G
is maximally connected and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
. We proceed in the same line as in Discussion
3.8
. Suppose
\widetilde{G}
is the induced subgraph
G_{\{1,\ldots,n\}}
of
G
. We find two vertices in
\widetilde{G}
such that there is an edge between them and rename these two vertices as
1
and
2
. Now find another vertex (if it exists) which has edges connecting both
1
and
2
. Rename the new vertex as
3
and continue this way to find a maximal clique on the vertex set say
\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
for
r\leq n
. The truncated signless Laplacian of
G
will be of the form

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{G}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}&\cdots&1&1&d_{1,1}&d_{1,2}&\cdots&d_{1,m}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\cdots&\alpha_{r-1}&1&d_{r-1,1}&d_{r-1,2}&\cdots&d_{r-1,m}\\
1&\cdots&1&\alpha_{r}&d_{r,1}&d_{r,2}&\cdots&d_{r,m}\\
d_{1,1}&\cdots&d_{r-1,1}&d_{r,1}&\beta_{1}&c_{1,2}&\cdots&c_{1,m}\\
d_{1,2}&\cdots&d_{r-1,2}&d_{r,2}&c_{1,2}&\beta_{2}&\cdots&c_{2,m}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
d_{1,m}&\cdots&d_{r-1,m}&d_{r,m}&c_{1,m}&c_{2,m}&\cdots&\beta_{m}\end{bmatrix}_{n\times n},

where
r+m=n
, and for each
1\leq j\leq m
there exists some
i\in[r]
such that
d_{i,j}=0
. Moreover, the entries of
\widetilde{Q}_{G}
satisfies the equations (
3.3
) to (
3.8
). Since
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
, we must have
d_{i,j}=0
for all
i
and
j
, by Theorem
3.10
. Hence, we have
r=n
since
G
is maximally connected. Thus
G
is obtained from a complete multigraph
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
.

If
G
is a subgraph of
K_{n+1}^{a,1}
and
\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}

and by the above discussion we see that each maximally connected subgraph

is obtained from a complete multigraph
K_{n_{i}+1}^{a_{i},1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0
.

The converse follows from Lemma
3.4
and Theorem

. ∎

Thus for simple graphs we have the following result:

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
G
be a simple graph on

\{0,1,\ldots,n\}

\dim_{\mathbb{K}}(R_{n}/\mathcal{M}_{G}^{(1)})=\det\widetilde{Q}_{G}
holds if and only if each maximally connected subgraph

of
G

|V(G_{i})|=n_{i}
, is obtained from a complete simple graph
K_{n_{i}+1}
by deleting some edges through the root
0

Proof.

Taking

Normalized solutions of L 2 L^{2} -supercritical NLS equations on noncompact metric graphs with localized nonlinearities
Jack Borthwick, Xiaojun Chang, Louis Jeanjean, Nicola Soave

Abstract
In this paper we are concerned with the existence of normalized solutions for nonlinear Schrödinger equations on noncompact metric graphs with localized nonlinearities. In a L 2 L^{2} -supercritical regime, we obtain the existence of solutions for any prescribed mass. This result is obtained through an approach which could prove successful to treat more general equations on noncompact graphs.



Keywords:
Nonlinear Schrödinger equations;
L^{2}
-supercritical;
Noncompact metric graphs; Localized nonlinearities; Variational methods.

Mathematics Subject Classification:
35J60, 47J30

Introduction and main results

This paper is devoted to the existence of bound states of prescribed mass for the
L^{2}
-supercritical
nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) on
\mathcal{G}

-u^{\prime\prime}+\lambda u=\kappa(x)|u|^{p-2}u,

(1.1)

coupled with Kirchhoff conditions at the vertices, see (
1.4
) below. Here
\lambda
appears as a Lagrange multiplier,
p>6
,
\mathcal{G}
is a noncompact connected metric graph, and
\kappa
is the characteristic function of the compact core
\mathcal{K}
of
\mathcal{G}
. We recall, see
[
4
]
, that if
\mathcal{G}
is a metric graph with a finite number of vertices its compact core
\mathcal{K}
is defined as the metric subgraph of
\mathcal{G}
consisting of all the bounded edges of
\mathcal{G}
.

A solution with prescribed mass is often referred to as a
normalized solution
. For Equation (
1.1
) they correspond to
critical points of the NLS energy functional
E(\cdot\,,\mathcal{G}):H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle E(u,\mathcal{G})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}|u|^{p}\,dx

(1.2)

under a mass constraint

\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}\,dx=\mu>0.

(1.3)

The study of NLS on metric graphs has attracted much attention over the last few decades. Among the physical motivations to consider Schrödinger equations on a metric graph, the most prominent one comes from the study of quantum graphs, see for example
[
6
,
11
,
29
,
32
]
and the references therein. In particular, the investigation of NLS with localized nonlinearities appeared in optical fibers and Bose-Einstein condensates in non-regular traps (see
[
24
,
32
]
).

Recently, much effort has been devoted to the existence of normalized solutions of NLS on metric graphs, in the
L^{2}
-subcritical
(i.e.,
p\in(2,6)
) or
L^{2}
-critical regimes
(i.e.,
p=6
). In these two regimes, the energy functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
is bounded from below on the mass constraint and one may develop minimization methods. A relevant notion is indeed the one of ground states, namely of solutions which minimize the energy functional on the constraint. For the existence of ground states solutions, see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
33
,
35
]
for noncompact
\mathcal{G}
, and
[
14
,
16
]
for the compact case; some studies are also conducted on the existence of local minimizers, see
[
36
]
. Regarding problems with a localized nonlinearity as in Equation (
1.1
), existence or non-existence of ground states solutions was discussed in
[
39
,
42
]
for the
L^{2}
-subcritical case, and in
[
18
,
19
]
for the
L^{2}
-critical case. Moreover, in the
L^{2}
-subcritical regime, genus theory was applied in
[
38
]
to obtain the existence of multiple bound states solutions at negative energy levels.

In the
L^{2}
-supercritical regime on general metric graphs, i.e., when
p>6
, the energy functional
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
is always unbounded from below.
Moreover, due to the fact that graphs are not scale invariant, the techniques based on scalings, usually employed in the Euclidean setting and related to the validity of a Pohozaev identity (see
[
26
]
or
[
8
,
25
,
40
,
41
]
), do not work. These two features make the search for normalized solutions in the

L^{2}
-supercritical regime delicate. Recently, in
[
15
]
, this issue was considered on compact metric graphs. The existence of solutions was proved for small values of
\mu>0
, see
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
for a precise statement. In the present paper we pursue this study by allowing the graph to be noncompact. Although in our proofs we make use of the fact that the nonlinearity in Equation (
1.1
) only acts on a compact part of the graph, we believe that our approach could prove fruitful to study more general situations.

Basic notations and main result

Throughout this paper, we consider a connected noncompact metric graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V})
, where
\mathcal{E}
is the set of edges and
\mathcal{V}
is the set of vertices. We assume that
\mathcal{G}
has a finite number of edges and the compact part
\mathcal{K}
is non-empty.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\{{\rm e}_{1},\cdots,{\rm e}_{m_{1}}\}
are the bounded edges,
\{l_{1},\cdots,l_{m_{2}}\}
are the half-lines, and
\{{\rm{v}}_{1},\cdots,{\rm{v}}_{m_{3}}\}
are the finite vertices of
\mathcal{G}
. Here
m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}
are positive integers.
Moreover, any bounded edge
{\rm e}
is identified with a closed bounded interval
I_{\rm e}
, typically
[0,\ell_{\rm e}]
(where
\ell_{\rm e}
is the length of
{\rm e}
), while each unbounded edge is identified with a closed half-line
I_{\rm e}=[0,+\infty)
. For details, one can refer to
[
3
,
4
,
11
]
. A function on metric graph
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
is identified with a vector of functions
\{u_{\mathrm{e}}\}
, where each
u_{\mathrm{e}}
is defined on the corresponding interval
I_{\rm e}
such that
u|_{\mathrm{e}}=u_{\mathrm{e}}
. Endowing each edge with Lebesgue measure, one can define the space
L^{p}(\mathcal{G})
in a natural way, with norm

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathcal{G})}^{p}=\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\|u_{\mathrm{e}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathrm{e})}^{p}.

The Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
consists of the set of continuous functions
u:\mathcal{G}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{\mathrm{e}}\in H^{1}([0,\ell_{\mathrm{e}}])
for every edge
\mathrm{e}
(note that
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
can be
+\infty
); the norm in
H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is defined as

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2}=\sum_{\mathrm{e}}\|u_{\mathrm{e}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{e})}^{2}+\|u_{\mathrm{e}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{e})}^{2}.

We shall study the existence of critical points of the functional
E(u,\mathcal{G})
constrained on the
L^{2}
-sphere

H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G}):=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}):\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u|^{2}\,dx=\mu\right\}.

If
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
is such a critical point, then there exists a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u
satisfies the following problem:

\begin{cases}-u^{\prime\prime}+\lambda u=\kappa(x)|u|^{p-2}u~~&\mbox{on every edge}~{\rm e}~\mbox{of}~\mathcal{G},\\
\sum\limits_{{\rm e}\succ{\rm v}}u_{{\rm e}}^{\prime}({\rm v})=0~~&\mbox{at every vertex}~{\rm v}\in\mathcal{K},\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
{\rm e}\succ{\rm v}
means that the edge
{\rm e}
is incident at
{\rm v}
, and the notation
u^{\prime}_{{\rm e}}({\rm v})
stands for
u^{\prime}_{{\rm e}}(0)
or
-u^{\prime}_{{\rm e}}(\ell_{{\rm e}})
, according to whether the vertex
{\rm v}
is identified with
0
or
\ell_{\mathrm{e}}
(namely, the sum involves the outer derivatives at
{\rm v}
). The second equation is the so-called
Kirchhoff condition
.

Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be any noncompact metric graph having a non-empty compact core
\mathcal{K}
, and let
p>6
. Then, for any
\mu>0
, problem (
1.4
) with the mass constraint (
1.3
) has a positive solution
u
associated to a Lagrange multiplier
\lambda>0
. This solution corresponds to a critical point of
E(\cdot\,,\mathcal{G})
on
H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
at a strictly positive energy level.

Let us present the main ideas to prove Theorem
1.1
.

It is simple to check that the functional
E(\cdot\,,\mathcal{G})
admits a mountain pass geometry in
H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
for any
\mu>0
. However, the existence of a bounded Palais-Smale sequence at the mountain pass level, a key preliminary step to obtain a critical point, is not directly available. To overcome this issue, following the strategy presented in
[
15
]
, we first consider the family of functionals
E_{\rho}(\cdot,\mathcal{G}):H^{1}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(u,\mathcal{G})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx-\frac{\rho}{p}\int_{\mathcal{K}}|u|^{p}dx,\qquad\forall u\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G}),\,\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(1.5)

As in
[
15
]
we shall rely on a parametrized version of a mountain-pass theorem on a constraint established in
[
12
]
. Roughly, this theorem, recalled here as Theorem
2.4
, guarantees, for almost every value of a parameter, the existence of a bounded Palais-Smale sequence with second-order information.
Applying Theorem
2.4
on the family
E_{\rho}(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
we deduce that, for almost every
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, these functionals admit a bounded Palais-Smale sequence
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
of non negative functions with “approximate Morse index” information.

The next step is to show that, for such
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, the sequence
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
converges to some
u_{\rho}
. If this is the case, as a consequence of the Morse type information on
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
one can directly deduce that
u_{\rho}
has a Morse index
m(u_{\rho})
at most 2, see Definition
3.4
.
In
[
15
]
the convergence of
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
was immediate due to the compactness of
\mathcal{G}
. In our setting, despite the fact that the nonlinearity acts only on the compact core, the non-compactness of the graph prevents us from directly concluding that bounded Palais-Smale sequences converge. This kind of difficulty is regularly encountered in the search of normalized solutions in
L^{2}
-supercritical problems when the underlying domain is noncompact, see for example
[
28
,
31
]
. To overcome it one needs to benefit from information on the sign of an associated sequence of
almost
Lagrange multipliers. In our problem we derive this information by using the “approximate Morse index” information carried by the sequence
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
. This argument seems new and we suspect that it could be used in other problems. In order to emphasize its generality we present it, in Section
2
, in a general framework.

Having been able to show, for almost every
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
, the convergence of
\{u_{\rho,n}\}
to some
u_{\rho}
with Morse index at most 2 (see Proposition
3.5
for a precise statement), our proof follows the lines of
[
15
]
. Namely, taking a sequence
\rho_{n}\to 1^{-}
, we adapt the blow-up analysis, developed in
[
15
]
, to show that
\{u_{n}\}:=\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}
is bounded. Then, we use some arguments previously developed in the proof of Proposition
3.5
to deduce that
\{u_{n}\}
converges and to end the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The rest of the paper is mainly devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Before proceeding, we would like to discuss some open problems and possible further directions of research.

Remark 1.2
.

Since they are obtained through a mountain pass procedure on a codimension
1
manifold, it is natural to expect that the solutions we found are orbitally unstable. This is what happens in mass-superlinear problems when the underlying equation is autonomous and set on all the space. See, for example, [39] in that direction. However, here things are more complex since we have little information on the variational characterizations of the solutions that we obtain. In comparison to [39], we do not know if our solutions can be characterized as a
ground state
or as a global minimizer on some additional constraint. More globally, the issue of orbital stability (and the related issue of the monotonicity of the mass with respect to the Lagrange multiplier) appears complex
on a general non-compact graph
. So far, orbital stability/instability of bound states on metric graphs has only been characterized for some specific examples, see
[
2
,
14
,
34
]
and references therein.

Remark 1.3
.

Another challenging issue would consist in characterizing precisely the properties of the solutions in terms of the number and the location of local and global maximum points, at least in the limit of large/small mass parameter. This is also related to multiplicity issues. We refer to
[
7
,
13
]
for some recent results in the subcritical case, however it seems unlikely that they are directly adaptable to the supercritical case (the former exploits a doubly constrained minimization argument; the latter uses some techniques specifically suited to deal with the cubic NLS).

On the other hand, it is not difficult to prove the following basic result, which actually holds for
any
one-signed critical point of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
on
S_{\mu}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

For
p>6
and
\mu>0
, let
u\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
be a critical point of
E(\cdot,\mathcal{G})
defined in (
1.2
) under the mass constraint (
1.3
), and suppose that
u>0
and
\lambda>0
. If
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{G}
is a local maximum point of
u
, then
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{K}
, the compact core. Furthermore, on any half-line
\ell_{i}\simeq[0,+\infty)
one has
u(x)=c_{i}e^{-\sqrt{\lambda}x}
for some
c_{i}>0
.

The proof follows directly by integrating the equation on each half-line, and using the fact that
\lambda>0
.

Remark 1.5
.

Finally, we make a short remark on extending our methods to study the existence of bound states in cases where the nonlinearity acts on the full graph, and not only its compact core. In this direction, our min-max geometric argument still works, but some issues arise in studying the compactness of Palais-Smale sequences. Considering what happens in the subcritical and critical cases (see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
), it is expected that topological and metric properties of the underlying graph play a role.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall in Theorem
2.4
the main result of
[
15
]
and explore some of its consequences. In particular, we show that the second order information on the Palais-Smale sequences can be used to get uniform bounds from below on the associated sequences of almost Lagrange parameter, see Lemma
2.5
. In Section
3
we show that
E_{\rho}(\cdot\,,\mathcal{G})
has a mountain pass critical point for almost every
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
and we prove Proposition
3.5
. Section
4
presents the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2
Some preliminary definitions and results

In this section, we recall
[
12
, Theorem 1]
and explore some of its consequences.

Let
\left(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\right)
and
\left(H,(\cdot,\cdot)\right)
be two
infinite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces and assume that
E\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow E^{\prime}
,
with continuous injections. For simplicity, we assume that the continuous injection
E\hookrightarrow H
has norm at most
1
and identify
E
with its image in
H
.
Set

\begin{cases}\|u\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle,\\
|u|^{2}=(u,u),\end{cases}\quad u\in E,

and we define for
\mu>0
:

S_{\mu}=\{u\in E\big|\,|u|^{2}=\mu\}.

In the context of this paper, we shall have
E=H^{1}(\mathcal{G})
and
H=L^{2}(\mathcal{G})
.
Clearly,
S_{\mu}
is a smooth submanifold of
E
of codimension
1
. Its tangent space at a given point
u\in S_{\mu}
can be considered as the closed codimension
1
subspace of
E
given by:

T_{u}S_{\mu}=\{v\in E\big|\,(u,v)=0\}.

In the following definition, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and
\|\cdot\|_{**}
, respectively, the operator norm of
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R})
and of
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R}))
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We say that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets if for any
R>0
one can find
M=M(R)>0
such that, for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in B(0,R)
:

||\phi^{\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{2})||_{*}\leq M||u_{1}-u_{2}||^{\alpha},\quad||\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{2})||_{**}\leq M||u_{1}-u_{2}||^{\alpha}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in E
, we define the continuous bilinear map:

D^{2}\phi(u)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)\cdot u}{|u|^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot).

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in S_{\mu}
and
\theta>0
, we define
the
approximate Morse index
by

\tilde{m}_{\theta}(u)=\sup\left\{\dim\,L\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\ L\text{ is a subspace of $T_{u}S_{\mu}$ such that: }D^{2}\phi(u)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\theta\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad\forall\varphi\in L\setminus\{0\}\end{array}\right.\right\}.

If
u
is a critical point for the constrained functional
\phi|_{S_{\mu}}
and
\theta=0
, we say that this is the
Morse index of
u
as constrained critical point
.

The following abstract theorem was established in
[
12
]
. Its derivation is based on a combination of ideas from
[
22
,
23
,
27
]
implemented in a convenient geometric setting. Note that related theorems, but dealing with unconstrained functionals, were recently developed in
[
10
,
30
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(Theorem 1 in
[
12
]
)
.

Let
I\subset(0,+\infty)
be an interval and consider a family of
C^{2}
functionals
\Phi_{\rho}:E\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\Phi_{\rho}(u)=A(u)-\rho B(u),\qquad\rho\in I,

where
B(u)\geq 0
for every
u\in E
, and

either
A(u)\to+\infty
or
B(u)\to+\infty
as
u\in E
and
\|u\|\to+\infty
.

(2.2)

Suppose moreover that
\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Finally, suppose that there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in S_{\mu}
(independent of
\rho
) such that, setting

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu})\big|\ \gamma(0)=w_{1},\quad\gamma(1)=w_{2}\right\},

we have

c_{\rho}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ \max_{t\in[0,1]}\Phi_{\rho}(\gamma(t))>\max\{\Phi_{\rho}(w_{1}),\Phi_{\rho}(w_{2})\},\quad\rho\in I.

(2.3)

Then, for almost every
\rho\in I
, there exist sequences
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that, as
n\to+\infty
,

(i)

\Phi_{\rho}(u_{n})\to c_{\rho}
;

(ii)

||\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}|_{S_{\mu}}(u_{n})||_{*}\to 0
;

(iii)

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
;

(iv)

\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1
.

It is easily observed, see
[
12
, Remarks 1.3]
, from Theorem
2.4
(ii)-(iii) that

\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})+\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(u_{n},\cdot)\to 0\,\mbox{ in }E^{\prime}\mbox{ as }n\to+\infty

(2.4)

where we have set

\lambda_{n}:=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}(\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})\cdot u_{n}).

(2.5)

Also, Theorem
2.4
(iv) directly implies that if there exists a subspace
W_{n}\subset T_{u_{n}}S_{\mu}
such that

D^{2}\Phi_{\rho}(u_{n})[w,w]=\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[w,w]+\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(w,w)<-\zeta_{n}||w||^{2},\quad\mbox{for all }w\in W_{n}\setminus\{0\},

(2.6)

then necessarily
\dim W_{n}\leq 1
. We called the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
defined in (
2.5
) the sequence of
almost Lagrange multipliers
.

We shall see in Section
3
that if
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
converges to some
u\in S_{\mu}
then information on the Morse index of
u
as constrained critical point can be obtained. First, let us show how information on the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
can be derived.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
,
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\{\zeta_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(i)

For large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, all subspaces
W_{n}\subset E
with the property

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}<-\zeta_{n}\|\varphi\|^{2},\qquad\mbox{for all }\,\varphi\in W_{n}\backslash\{0\},

(2.7)

satisfy:
\dim W_{n}\leq 2
.

(ii)

There exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a subspace
Y
of
E
with
\dim Y\geq 3
and
a>0
such that, for large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda|\varphi|^{2}\leq-a||\varphi||^{2},\qquad\mbox{for all }\,\varphi\in Y.

(2.8)

Then
\lambda_{n}>\lambda
for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if (
2.8
) holds for any
\lambda<0
, then
\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\geq 0
.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda
along a subsequence. From (
2.8
) we have, for all
\varphi\in Y\backslash\{0\},

\begin{split}\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}&=\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda|\varphi|^{2}+(\lambda_{n}-\lambda)|\varphi|^{2}\\
&\leq-a||\varphi||^{2}+(\lambda_{n}-\lambda)|\varphi|^{2}.\end{split}

Now, since
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that:
\forall n\geq n_{0}
,

\zeta_{n}<a
. Thus, for an arbitrary
n\geq n_{0}
, we obtain

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}\leq-\zeta_{n}\|\varphi\|^{2},\qquad\mbox{for all }\,\varphi\in Y\backslash\{0\},

in contradiction with (
2.7
) since dim
Y>2
.
∎

3
Mountain pass solutions for approximating problems

Theorem
2.4
will be a key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To show that the family
E_{\rho}(\cdot\,\mathcal{G})
enters into the framework of Theorem
2.4
we first need to show that it has a mountain pass geometry on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G})
uniformly with respect to
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For every
\mu>0
, there exist
w_{1},w_{2}\in S_{\mu}
independent of
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
such that

\displaystyle c_{\rho}:=\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\,\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\rho}(\gamma(t),\mathcal{G})>\max\{E_{\rho}(w_{1},\mathcal{G}),E_{\rho}(w_{2},\mathcal{G})\},\qquad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],

where

\Gamma:=\left\{\gamma\in C([0,1],H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathcal{G}))\big|~\gamma~\mbox{is continuous},~\gamma(0)=w_{1},\gamma(1)=w_{2}\right\}.

Proof.

For any
\mu,k>0
, denote

\displaystyle A_{\mu,k}:=\{u\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})\big|\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx<k\},

\displaystyle\partial A_{\mu,k}:=\{u\in H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathcal{G})\big|\int_{\mathcal{G}}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx=k\}.

First note that, since the graph is noncompact, for any

Lemma 3.2

For any
\lambda<0
, there exists a subspace

of

with dim

such that

\int_{\mathcal{G}}|w^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx+\lambda\int_{\mathcal{G}}|w|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\lambda}{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}(\mathcal{G})}^{2},\qquad\forall\,w\in Y.

(3.5)

Proof.

Take
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
with
{\rm{supp}}\,\phi\subset[\frac{3}{4},\frac{5}{4}]
such that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\phi|^{2}dx=1
. Recalling that
l_{1}
is an arbitrary half-line of
\mathcal{G}
, we define the following functions for
\sigma>0
:

\displaystyle\psi_{0,\sigma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{for}~x\in\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}\setminus l_{1}\\
\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\sigma x)&~\mbox{for}~x\in l_{1},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\psi_{1,\sigma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{for}~x\in\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}\setminus l_{1}\\
\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\sigma x-1)&~\mbox{for}~x\in l_{1},\end{array}\right.

and

\displaystyle\psi_{2,\sigma}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{for}~x\in\mathcal{\mathcal{G}}\setminus l_{1}\\
\sigma^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(\sigma x-2)&~\mbox{for}~x\in l_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Clearly, for any
\sigma>0
,
\int_{\mathcal{G}}|\psi_{j,\sigma}|^{2}\,dx=1
for all

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{G}}|w^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx+\lambda\int_{\mathcal{G}}|w|^{2}\,dx&=\sigma^{2}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{2}\theta_{j}^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\phi^{\prime}|^{2}\,dx\Big)+\lambda\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{2}\theta_{j}^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\phi|^{2}\,dx\Big)\\

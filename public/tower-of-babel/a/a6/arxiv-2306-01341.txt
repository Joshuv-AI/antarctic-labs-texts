New bounds for odd colourings of graphs
Tianjiao Dai, Qiancheng Ouyang, François Pirot

Abstract
. Given a graph G G , a vertex-colouring σ \sigma of G G , and a subset X ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq V(G) , a colour x ∈ σ ⁡ ( X ) x\in\sigma(X) is said to be odd for X X in σ \sigma if it has an odd number of occurrences in X X . We say that σ \sigma is an odd colouring of G G if it is proper and every (open) neighbourhood has an odd colour in σ \sigma . The odd chromatic number of a graph G G , denoted by χ o ​ ( G ) \chi_{o}(G) , is the minimum k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} such that an odd colouring σ : V ⁡ ( G ) → [ k ] \sigma\colon V(G)\to[k] exists. 1.
Introduction

All graphs considered here are finite, undirected, and simple. Let
[k]
denote the set of first
k
positive integers. For a graph
G
, let
V(G)
and
E(G)
denote its vertex and edge sets, respectively. A hypergraph
\mathcal{H}=(V(\mathcal{H}),E(\mathcal{H}))
is a generalisation of a graph; its (hyper-)edges are subsets of
V(\mathcal{H})
of arbitrary positive size. A vertex
k
-colouring
of a graph or a hypergraph
G
is an assignment
\sigma\colon V(G)\to[k]
, whose images are referred to as
colours
. Motivated by a frequency assignment problem in cellular networks, Even, Lotker, Ron, and Smorodinsky
[
11
]
introduced the notion of conflict-free colourings of hypergraphs. A colouring
\sigma
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is
conflict-free
if for every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
there exists a colour appearing exactly once in
e
. Pach and Tardos
[
16
]
studied
this notion and proved that every hypergraph with fewer than
\binom{s}{2}
edges (for some integer
s
) has a
conflict-free colouring with fewer than
s
colours. Kostochka, Kumbhat, and Luczak
[
14
]

further studied conflict-free colouring for uniform hypergraphs.

1.1.
A motivation coming from proper conflict-free colourings

A colouring
\sigma
of a graph
G
is
proper
if
\sigma(u)\neq\sigma(v)
for all
uv\in E(G)
. The
chromatic number
of a graph
G
, denoted by
\chi(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
such that there is a
k
-colouring of
G
.
One can observe that a conflict-free colouring of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
is in particular a proper colouring of the graph formed by the hyper-edges of size
2
in
\mathcal{H}
. Given a graph
G
and a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
on the same vertex set
V
, we say that
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a
graph-hypergraph pair
. A
proper conflict-free
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
(
pcf
k
-colouring
for short) is a mapping
\sigma\colon V\to[k]
that is both a proper colouring of
G
and a conflict-free colouring of
\mathcal{H}
. By the previous observation, this can be seen as a conflict-free colouring of the hypergraph
(V,E(\mathcal{H})\cup E(G))
.
There are many classical constraints for a proper colouring of a given graph
G
that are in particular satisfied by a pcf-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
, for some hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
carefully chosen. For instance, if
E(\mathcal{H})
contains a maximum independent set of every even cycle in
G
, then a pcf-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
is in particular an acyclic colouring of
G
.
As another example, if
E(\mathcal{H})
contains all
(\beta+1)
-subsets of every neighbourhood in
G
, then a pcf-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
is in particular a
\beta
-frugal colouring of
G
.

There has been a specific focus on the special case of pcf-colourings
\sigma
of
(G,\mathcal{H})
when
\mathcal{H}
is the neighbourhood-hypergraph of
G
, i.e.
E(\mathcal{H})=\{N(v):v\in V(G),\deg(v)>0\}
.
In that case, we say that
\sigma
is a pcf-colouring of
G
(so when we omit
\mathcal{H}
, it is implicitly the neighbourhood-hypergraph of
G
). In other words, a
pcf-colouring
of
G
is a proper colouring of
G
such that for every non-isolated vertex
v
, there is a colour that appears exactly once among the neighbours of
v
. We let
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)
be the smallest integer
k
such that a pcf
k
-colouring of
G
exists. This notion is the combination of proper colouring and the pointed conflict-free chromatic parameter introduced by Cheilaris
[
4
]
.

The notion of pcf-colourings of graphs was formally introduced by Fabrici, Lužar, Rindošová, and Soták
[
12
]
, where they investigated the pcf-colourings of planar and outerplanar
graphs, among many other related variants of a proper conflict-free colouring. They proved that
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq 8
for all planar graphs and
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq 5
for all outerplanar graphs. Further studies in pcf-colourings of sparse graphs can be found in
[
3
,
6
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
.

Given a (hyper-)graph
\mathcal{H}
,
the
degree
of a vertex
v
, denoted by
\deg_{\mathcal{H}}(v)
, is the number of edges of
\mathcal{H}
containing
v
.
We denote by
\delta(\mathcal{H})
and
\Delta(\mathcal{H})
the minimum and maximum degrees of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively.
We denote
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})
the minimum size of an edge in
\mathcal{H}
.
Given a graph
G
, we let
\delta^{*}(G)
denote the
degeneracy
of
G
, that is
\delta^{*}(G)=\max_{H\subseteq G}\delta(H)
.
Caro, Petruševski, and Škrekovski
[
3
]
proposed the following conjecture about pcf-colourings.

Conjecture 1.1

(Caro, Petruševski, Škrekovski
[
3
, Conjecture 6.4]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
, then
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\Delta+1
.

As a first step toward their conjecture, Caro, Petruševski, and Škrekovski
[
3
]
proved that for such a graph
G
,
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\left\lfloor 2.5\Delta\right\rfloor
. Recently, it has been observed by Cranston and Liu
[
10
]
that
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\Delta(G)+\delta^{*}(G)+1
(they actually more generally proved that there always exists a pcf
(\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\delta^{*}(G)+1)
-colouring of any given pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
).
They further reduced the gap to Conjecture
1.1
by proving that
\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)\leq\left\lceil 1.6550826\Delta+\sqrt{\Delta}\right\rceil
, given that
\Delta
is large enough.

1.2.
Odd colourings

In
[
5
]
, Cheilaris, Keszegh, and Pálvölgyi introduced a weakening of conflict-free colourings. An
odd colouring

\sigma
of a hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the constraint that in every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
there is a colour
x
with an odd number of occurrences in
\sigma
; we say that
x
is an
odd colour
of
e
in
\sigma
. It is straightforward that a conflict-free colouring of
\mathcal{H}
is in particular an odd colouring of
\mathcal{H}
.
Petruševski and Škrekovski
[
18
]
later considered that notion applied to the neighbourhood-hypergraph of a graph
G
. An
odd colouring
of
G
is a proper colouring of
G
with the additional constraint that each non-isolated vertex has a colour appearing an odd number of times in its neighbourhood. The
odd chromatic number
of
G
, denoted by
\chi_{o}(G)
, is the minimum integer
k
such that an odd
k
-colouring of
G
exists. Since odd colourings are a weakening of pcf-colourings, it always holds that
\chi_{o}(G)\leq\chi_{\rm pcf}(G)
. In the last couple of years, there has been some interest in determining the extremal value of
\chi_{o}
in various classes of graphs.

In
[
18
]
, Petruševski and Škrekovski showed that
\chi_{o}(G)\leq 9
for every planar graph
G
with a proof that relies on the discharging method (note that this is weaker than the result about pcf-colourings of planar graphs from
[
6
]
stated earlier, which was proved a couple of months later). Furthermore, they conjectured that this bound may be reduced to
5
.
If true, this would be tight, since
\chi_{o}(C_{5})=5
.
Recently there has been considerable attention in odd colourings of planar graphs
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
17
]
.

Caro, Petruševski, and Škrekovski
[
2
]
also studied various properties of the odd chromatic number of general graphs; in particular, they proved the following facts: every graph of maximum degree three has an odd 4-colouring; every graph, except for
C_{5}
, of maximum degree
\Delta
has an odd
2\Delta
-colouring. Moreover, they proposed a conjecture for general graphs, which is a weaker form of Conjecture
1.1
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Caro, Petruševski, Škrekovski
[
2
, Conjecture 5.5]
)
.

If
G
is a connected graph of maximum degree
\Delta\geq 3
, then
\chi_{o}(G)\leq\Delta+1.

Our main result states that Conjecture
1.2
holds asymptotically as
\Delta\to\infty
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For every graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
,

\chi_{o}(G)\leq\Delta+\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)\right\rceil.

For small values of
\Delta
, we provide another bound on
\chi_{o}(G)
that is derived from a relatively simple (deterministic) colouring procedure.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every graph
G
of maximum degree
\Delta
,

\chi_{o}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\right\rfloor+2.

1.3.
General hypergraphs

The proofs of our results stated in Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
rely highly on the structure of neighbourhood hypergraphs. In a more general setting, we could wonder how the odd colouring problem behaves on any graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
. We were able to extend Theorem
1.3
to that more general setting, at the cost of requiring a lower bound on the minimum hyper-edge size
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})
in
\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that, for every graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, if
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})\geq C\log\Delta(\mathcal{H})
then there is an odd
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
, where

k\leq\Delta(G)+C\log\Delta(\mathcal{H}).

With that extra condition on
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})
, we can actually derive an upper bound on
\chi_{o}(G,\mathcal{H})
that mainly depends on
\chi(G)
rather than
\Delta(G)
. Moreover, we show with a construction that this bound is tight up to the precise value of the constant
C
.

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that, for every graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, if
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})\geq C\log\Delta(\mathcal{H})
then there is an odd
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
, where

k\leq\chi(G)\cdot C\log\Delta(\mathcal{H}).

When
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})
gets closer to
\Delta(G)
, we show that the difference
\chi_{o}(G,\mathcal{H})-\chi(G)
gets relatively small.

Theorem 1.7
.

There exists a universal constant
C
such that, for every graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, there is an odd
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
, where

k\leq\chi(G)+C\,\frac{\Delta(G)\log\Delta(\mathcal{H})}{\epsilon(\mathcal{H})}.

A direct consequence of Theorem
1.7
is that for quasi-regular graphs
G
(that is, the ratio
\Delta(G)/\delta(G)
is bounded by a uniform constant), the difference
\chi_{o}(G)-\chi(G)
is small, namely
O(\log\Delta(G))
. This contrasts with the general case where that difference can be much larger: if
G
is the
1
-subdivision of the complete graph on
\Delta+1\geq 5
vertices, then
\chi(G)=2
while
\chi_{o}(G)=\Delta+1
.

2.
Probabilistic tools.

The first probabilistic result that we need is the following lopsided version of the Symmetric Lovász Local Lemma (LLL for short) (see e.g.
[
1
]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Lopsided Lovász Local Lemma)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}
be a finite set of random (bad) events, and let
d
be a fixed integer. Suppose that, for every
i\in[n]
, there is a set
\Gamma(i)\subseteq[n]
of size at most
d
such that, for every
Z\subseteq[n]\setminus\Gamma(i)
,

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{i}\;\middle|\;\bigcap_{j\in Z}\overline{B_{j}}\right]\leq p.

If
epd\leq 1
, then
\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{i\in[n]}\limits\overline{B_{i}}\right]>0
.

Many random variables we analyse in this paper are highly concentrated around their mean. This is a consequence of Chernoff’s bounds as stated hereafter.

Lemma 2.2

(Chernoff’s bounds)
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n}
be i.i.d.
(0,1)
-valued random variables, and let
\mathbf{S}_{n}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{X}_{n}
. Let us write
\mu\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\right]
. Then

(i)

for every
0\leq\eta<\mu
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\leq\mu-\eta\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\mu}}\mbox{, and}

(ii)

for every
\eta>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\geq\mu+\eta\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2(\mu+\eta)}}.

Finally, we will need to analyse a specific Markovian process as described in the following lemma.
First we observe that, using the well-known Stirling bounds

\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}\leq n!\leq\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}}

for every integer
n\geq 1
, it is straightforward to derive that

(1)

\frac{n!}{\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)!}\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{2n}{e}\right)^{n/2}

for every even integer
n\geq 2
. We will also use the classical upper bound
\binom{n}{k}\leq\left(\frac{ne}{k}\right)^{k}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{X}_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of binary random variables with values in
\{-1,1\}
, let
S_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
be a non-negative integer, and let
\mathbf{S}_{i}\coloneqq S_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathbf{X}_{j}
for every integer
i\geq 0
. If there exists a real number
\tau>0
such that, for every
i\geq 0
, it holds that
\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=-1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{S}_{i-1}=s\right]\leq s/\tau
, then

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\leq k\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\,\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{2n-2k}{e\tau}\right)^{\frac{n-k}{2}},

for every integers
0\leq k\leq n
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.3
.

Given a possible outcome
(X_{1},\ldots X_{n})
of
(\mathbf{X}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n})
that yields that
\mathbf{S}_{n}\leq k
, let
I\coloneqq\{i:X_{i}=-1\}
. Noting that
\mathbf{S}_{n}=S_{0}+n-2|I|
conditioned on
\mathbf{X}_{1}=X_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n}=X_{n}
, we infer that
|I|\geq\frac{S_{0}+n-k}{2}\geq\frac{n-k}{2}
.
Moreover, if
i_{j}
is the
j
-th last element in
I
, then it deterministically holds that
\mathbf{S}_{i_{j}-1}\leq k+j
, and hence

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i_{j}}=-1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{1}=X_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{i_{j}-1}=X_{i_{j}-1}\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i_{j}}=-1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{S}_{i_{j}-1}\leq k+j\right]\leq\frac{k+j}{\tau}.

We therefore have the following (crude) upper-bound:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{1}=X_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{n}=X_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{i\in I}\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i}=-1\;\middle|\;\mathbf{X}_{1}=X_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{X}_{i-1}=X_{i-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{j=1}^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\frac{k+j}{\tau}=\frac{\left(\frac{n+k}{2}\right)!}{k!\,\tau^{\frac{n-k}{2}}}.

Since there are at most
\binom{n}{\frac{n-k}{2}}
possibles choices for the
\frac{n-k}{2}
last elements of
I
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{S}_{n}\leq k\right]

\displaystyle\leq\binom{n}{\frac{n-k}{2}}\cdot\frac{\left(\frac{n+k}{2}\right)!}{k!\,\tau^{\frac{n-k}{2}}}=\frac{n!}{k!\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)!\,\tau^{\frac{n-k}{2}}}=\binom{n}{k}\frac{(n-k)!}{\left(\frac{n-k}{2}\right)!\,\tau^{\frac{n-k}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\binom{n}{k}\left(\frac{2n-2k}{e\tau}\right)^{\frac{n-k}{2}}

by (
1
)

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{ne}{k}\right)^{k}\left(\frac{2n-2k}{e\tau}\right)^{\frac{n-k}{2}}.

Let
G
be a graph, and
\mathscr{C}(G)\subseteq[k]^{V(G)}
a prescribed set of
k
-colourings of
G
. Given a colouring
\sigma\in\mathscr{C}(G)
and a vertex

, we let
L_{\sigma}(v)
be the set of colours

such that, if we redefine
\sigma(v)\leftarrow x
, we still have
\sigma\in\mathscr{C}(G)
. For instance, if
\mathscr{C}(G)
is the set of proper
k
-colourings of

, then

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph, and
\mathscr{C}(G)
a set of colourings of
G
.
Let
{\bm{\sigma}}
be drawn uniformly at random from
\mathscr{C}(G)
.
For a given subset of vertices
X\subseteq V(G)
, let
B_{\bm{\sigma}}
be the bad event that
X
has no odd colour in
{\bm{\sigma}}
, and let
M\subseteq X
be a subset of size
m\leq|X|
.
If there exists an integer
\tau
such that we deterministically have
|L_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)|\geq\tau
for every vertex
v\in M
, then for every possible realisation
\sigma_{0}
of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M\right.}
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\bm{\sigma}}~\middle|~{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M\right.}=\sigma_{0}\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{2m}{e\tau}\right)^{m/2}.

Proof.

Let
M=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
be a fixed subset of
X
. Let
\sigma_{0}
be a possible realisation of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M\right.}
.
Let
{\bm{\sigma}}_{0}
be drawn uniformly at random from the extensions of
\sigma_{0}
to
\mathscr{C}(G)
.
For every
1\leq i\leq m
, we let
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(G)
be obtained from
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}
by resampling the colour of
u_{i}
uniformly at random from

.
For every

, let
\mathbf{S}_{i}
be the number of odd colours of

in
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
.
For every

, we have
\mathbf{S}_{i}=\mathbf{S}_{i-1}-1
if
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}(u_{i})
is one of the
\mathbf{S}_{i-1}

{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}

\tau

. So the sequence

3.
A greedy bound

Given a proper
k
-colourings
\sigma\colon V(G)\to[k]
, and a vertex
u\in V(G)
, we denote
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}(u)
the set of odd colours of
N_{G}(u)
in
\sigma
. So
\sigma
is an odd
k
-colouring if
|\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}(u)|\geq 1
for every vertex
u\in V(G)
.
If
\mathcal{U}_{\sigma}(u)=\{x\}
, we say that
u
is
\sigma
-critical
, and that
x
is its
witness colour
; we denote it
w_{\sigma}(u)\coloneqq x
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
be an arbitrary ordering of the vertices of
G
. We let
H_{i}\coloneqq G[\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}]
for every
i\in[n]
.
Let
k\coloneqq\left\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\right\rfloor+2
, and let
\mathscr{C}(H)
denote the set of odd
k
-colourings of each induced subgraph
H\subseteq G
.
We construct an odd
k
-colouring of
G
greedily by constructing a sequence of partial colourings
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in[n]}
that satisfies the following induction hypothesis.

(IH)

\sigma_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(H_{i})\mbox{ and }|\mathcal{U}_{\sigma_{i}}(u)|\geq 1\mbox{ for every vertex $u\in N_{G}(V(H_{i}))$.}

For the base case, we may begin with the empty colouring
\sigma_{0}
.
Let us now assume that we have constructed
\sigma_{i}
that satisfies (
IH
).
In order to maintain (
IH
), we forbid that
\sigma_{i+1}(v_{i+1})
is one of
\left\{\sigma_{i}(u)\,:\,u\in N_{H_{i}}(v_{i+1})\right\}\cup\left\{w_{\sigma_{i}}(u)\,:\,u\in N_{G}(v_{i+1})\mbox{ and $u$ is $\sigma_{i}$-critical}\right\}
.
If at most
k-1
colours are forbidden for
v_{i+1}
, then there remains at least one possible choice for
\sigma_{i+1}(v_{i+1})
, and letting
\sigma_{i+1}(u)=\sigma_{i}(u)
for every
u\in V(H_{i})
we have that
\sigma_{i+1}
satisfies (
IH
).

We may now assume that all
k
colours are forbidden for
v_{i+1}
. Let
X\subseteq N_{G}(v_{i+1})
be the set of neighbours of
v_{i+1}
that forbid exactly one colour for
v_{i+1}
, and
Y\subseteq N_{G}(v_{i+1})\setminus X
be the set of neighbours of
v_{i+1}
that forbid exactly two colours for
v_{i+1}
(so
Y\subseteq V(H_{i})
and every vertex
y\in Y
is
\sigma_{i}
-critical). We claim that there is a vertex
y\in Y
such that
\sigma_{i}(y)
is forbidden only by
y
for
v_{i+1}
. Indeed, otherwise the number of forbidden colours for
v_{i+1}
would be at most
|X|+\frac{3}{2}|Y|\leq\frac{3}{2}\Delta<k
, a contradiction.
We also claim that
\sigma_{i}(y)
is not a witness colour of
v_{i+1}
in
\sigma_{i}
. Indeed, otherwise every colour of
N(v_{i+1})\setminus\{y\}
appears at least twice, hence
v_{i+1}
has at most
\left\lfloor\frac{\Delta-1}{2}\right\rfloor+1
adjacent colours in
\sigma_{i}
. Since there are at most
\Delta
witness colours in
N_{G}(v_{i+1})
, there are at most
\left\lfloor\frac{3\Delta+1}{2}\right\rfloor\leq k-1
forbidden colours for
v_{i+1}
, a contradiction.
Let us set
\sigma_{i+1}(v_{i+1})\coloneqq\sigma_{i}(y)
, and
\sigma_{i+1}(u)\coloneqq\sigma_{i}(u)
for every vertex
u\in V(H_{i})\setminus\{y\}
.

There remains to define
\sigma_{i+1}(y)
. Since
|\mathcal{U}_{\sigma_{i}}(y)|=1
, it means that every colour in
N_{G}(y)\cap V(H_{i+1})
appears at least twice in
\sigma_{i+1}
except
w_{\sigma_{i}}(y)
and
\sigma_{i+1}(v_{i+1})=\sigma_{i}(y)
. So
y
has at most
\lfloor\frac{\Delta-2}{2}\rfloor+2=\lfloor\frac{\Delta}{2}\rfloor+1
adjacent colours in
\sigma_{i+1}
. Since
N_{G}(y)
contains at most
\Delta
witness colours in
\sigma_{i+1}
, there are at most
\lfloor\frac{3\Delta}{2}\rfloor+1=k-1
forbidden colours for
y
in
\sigma_{i+1}
, and so there remains at least one possible choice for
\sigma_{i+1}(y)
.
This ends the proof of the induction.

We conclude that
\sigma_{n}
is an odd
k
-colouring of
G
, which proves that
\chi_{o}(G)\leq k
, as desired.
∎

4.
An asymptotic version of the Odd Colouring Conjecture

In this section we prove Theorem
1.3
.

4.1.
Set-up

Let
G
be a connected graph of maximum degree
\Delta
.
Given a vertex-colouring
\sigma\colon V(H)\to[k]
of some induced subgraph
H
of
G
, for each
v\in V(H)
a colour
x
is said to be an
odd colour
of
v
if
x
is an odd colour of
N_{H}(v)
in
\sigma
. Let
w_{\sigma}(v)
denote the unique odd colour of
v
in
\sigma
if such a colour exists; otherwise
w_{\sigma}(v)
is undefined. When it exists, we say that
w_{\sigma}(v)
is the
witness colour
of
v
in
\sigma
.

Let
k>\Delta
be some integer. Let
V^{-}
be the subset of
V(G)
consisting of all vertices of degree less than
k/2
, and
V^{+}\coloneqq V(G)\setminus V^{+}
be the set of vertices of degree at least
k/2
.
We denote
G^{+}\coloneqq G[V^{+}]
.
For every
X\subseteq V^{+}
, we say that a proper partial
k
-colouring
\sigma\colon X\to[k]
of
G^{+}
is
admissible
if every vertex
v\in V^{-}
having
N_{G}(v)\subseteq X
has an odd colour in
\sigma
.
Finally, we let
V^{++}
be the set of vertices
v\in V^{+}
having
N_{G}(v)\subseteq V^{+}
.

Since the bound on
\chi_{o}(G)
that we want to prove is weaker than that of Corollary
1.4
if
\Delta\leq 65
, we may assume that
\Delta\geq 66
.

4.2.
Colouring vertices of large degree.

Let
{\bm{\sigma}}\colon V^{+}\to[k]
be a uniformly random admissible colouring of
G^{+}
.
For every
v\in V^{++}
, we let
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)
be the random event that
N_{G}(v)
has no odd colour in
{\bm{\sigma}}
. The goal of this subsection will be to show that, with non-zero probability, no event
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)
occurs.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
\Delta\geq 49
and
k\geq\Delta+4(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)
, then there exists an admissible colouring
\sigma\colon V^{+}\to[k]
of
G^{+}
such that every vertex
v\in V^{++}
has an odd colour in
\sigma
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Fix
k\coloneqq\Delta+\eta
, for some integer
\eta\geq 1
whose precise value will be determined later in the proof, and let
{\bm{\sigma}}
be a uniformly random admissible
k
-colouring of
G^{+}
. Such a colouring exists, since each vertex
v
has at most
\Delta
constraints (at most
\deg_{V^{+}}(v)
constraints because of the adjacent colours, and at most
\deg_{V^{-}}(v)
constraints because of the adjacent witness colours). In particular, we have
|L_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)|\geq\eta
for every
v\in V^{+}
.
We want to show that, with non-zero probability, no bad event
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)
occurs for
v\in V^{++}
.

Let
m\leq k/2
be some integer whose explicit value will be determined later in the proof.
For every
v\in V^{++}
, we pick an arbitrary subset
M(v)\subseteq N(v)
of size
m
.
Then we let
\Gamma(v)\coloneqq N[M(v)]
.
For a vertex
u\in V^{++}
, the outcome of
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(u)
is entirely determined by the colours assigned to vertices in
N(u)
. So if we fix the realisation of
{\bm{\sigma}}
outside of
M(v)
, we in particular fix the outcomes of all events
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(u)
such that
M(v)\cap N[u]=\emptyset
. This holds for every
u\notin\Gamma(v)
.
We wish to apply Lemma
2.1
to those bad events, with that definition of
\Gamma(v)
.
To that end, let
\Sigma_{0}
be the set of possible realisations of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V^{+}\setminus M(v)\right.}
such that no event
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(u)
occurs for
u\notin\Gamma(v)
. For every
Z\subseteq V^{++}\setminus\Gamma(v)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)\;\middle|\;\bigcap_{u\in Z}\overline{B_{\bm{\sigma}}(u)}\right]\leq\sup_{\sigma_{0}\in\Sigma_{0}}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)\;\middle|\;{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V^{+}\setminus M(v)\right.}=\sigma_{0}\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{2m}{e\eta}\right)^{m/2},

by Lemma
2.4
applied to the graph
G^{+}
with
\mathscr{C}(G^{+})
being the set of admissible
k
-colourings of
G^{+}
.

Let us fix
\eta\coloneqq 2m
, so that this is at most
\sqrt{2}e^{-m/2}
. Since
v\in V^{++}
, we know that
\deg_{G^{+}}(v)\geq k/2\geq\eta/2\geq m
, so this lets us pick any value for
m
. To apply Lemma
2.1
, we need an upper bound of
\frac{1}{em\Delta}
for that probability, which holds precisely when
m\geq-2W_{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}e\Delta}\right)
. We may therefore pick
m\coloneqq\left\lceil-2W_{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}e\Delta}\right)\right\rceil
; a careful analysis of that value yields that
2m\leq\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)\right\rceil
when
\Delta\geq 49
.
∎

4.3.
Colouring vertices of small degree.

By
Lemma

4.1
,
G^{+}
has an admissible
k
-colouring
\sigma\colon V^{+}\to[k]
with
k=\Delta+\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)\right\rceil
, such that every
v\in V^{++}
has an odd colour. Then we colour the vertices of
V^{-}
greedily. Each time we colour
v\in V^{-}
, each neighbour
u
of
v
yields at most
2
forbidden colours (its colour
\sigma(u)
, and its witness colour if it exists), so there are less than
k
forbidden colours for
v
. This ensures that the greedy colouring terminates, and ends the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

5.
Odd colourings of hypergraphs with constrained edge sizes

We recall that, given a graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, an
odd
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
is a mapping
\sigma\colon V\to[k]
that is both a proper colouring of
G
and an odd colouring of
\mathcal{H}
.
Given a set
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, we let
\mathcal{H}[S]=(S,\{e\cap S:e\in E(\mathcal{H})\})
be the sub-hypergraph of
\mathcal{H}
induced by
S
(note that this definition allows the possibility that
\mathcal{H}[S]
contains
\emptyset
as an edge).

5.1.
A bound in terms of the chromatic number for quasi-regular graphs

The proof of Theorem
1.3
relies on the probabilistic method by analysing the behaviour of a uniformly random admissible colouring of a given graph
G
. It turns out that we have exploited the randomness of only a subset of the vertices of
G
: a subset of
m
neighbours of each vertex
v\in V^{++}
, where
m=\Theta(\ln\Delta(G))
. If
G
has a large minimum degree, we may restrict the random choices to a small subset of vertices that should suffice to have an odd colour in every neighbourhood, and colour the other vertices with an optimal proper colouring.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a graph-hypergraph pair. Fix
\eta\coloneqq\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\ln\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+3)\right\rceil
, and assume that
\Delta(\mathcal{H})\geq 49
.
For every subset of vertices
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, if
\epsilon(\mathcal{H}[S])\geq\eta/2
, then
(G,\mathcal{H})
has an odd
k
-colouring, where

k\leq\chi(G\setminus S)+\Delta(G[S])+\eta.

Proof.

Let
k_{0}\coloneqq\chi(G\setminus S)
and let
\sigma_{0}
be a proper
k_{0}
-colouring of
G\setminus S
.
Let
k\coloneqq k_{0}+\Delta(G[S])+\eta
, and let
{\bm{\sigma}}
be a uniformly random proper
k
-colouring of
G
that satisfies
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|G\setminus S\right.}=\sigma_{0}
. For every
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, we let
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e)
be the random event that
e
has no colour appearing at odd times in
{\bm{\sigma}}
. Let us show that, with non-zero probability, no event
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e)
occurs.
Let
m\leq\eta/2
be an integer, and for every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
let
M(e)=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m}\}\subseteq e\cap S
be a subset of
m
vertices in
e
. Let us recolour the vertices in
M(e)
in turn with a uniformly random available colour. Each time we recolour
u_{i}
, the neighbours of
u_{i}
in
S
forbid at most
\deg_{S}(u_{i})\leq\Delta(G[S])
colours, and the neighbours of
u_{i}
not in
S
forbid at most
k_{0}=\chi(G\setminus S)
colours (these colours are fixed by
\sigma_{0}
). So there are at least
\eta
available colours for
u_{i}
. In particular, we have
|L_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(v)|\geq\eta
for each
v\in V(G)
.

We apply Lemma
2.1
with
\Gamma(e)\coloneqq\{e^{\prime}\in E(\mathcal{H}):e^{\prime}\cap M(e)\neq\emptyset\}
for every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, and obtain that, with non-zero probability, none of the events
B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e)
occurs. The size of
\Gamma(e)
is at most
m\Delta(\mathcal{H})
. Let
\Sigma_{1}
be the set of possible realisations of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}
such that no event
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(e^{\prime})
occurs for
e^{\prime}\notin\Gamma(e)
. For every
Z\subseteq E(\mathcal{H})\setminus\Gamma(e)
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e)\;\middle|\;\bigcap_{e^{\prime}\in Z}\overline{B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e^{\prime})}\right]\leq\sup_{\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma_{1}}\mathbb{P}\left[B_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(e)\;\middle|\;{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}=\sigma_{1}\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{2m}{e\eta}\right)^{m/2}=\sqrt{2}\,e^{-m/2},

by Lemma
2.4
applied to the graph
G
with
\mathscr{C}(G)
being the set of proper
k
-colourings of
G
. As in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, by fixing
m\coloneqq\left\lceil-2W_{-1}\left(-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}e\Delta(\mathcal{H})}\right)\right\rceil\leq\eta/2
, the above probability is at most
\frac{1}{e\cdot m\Delta(\mathcal{H})}
. This proves the existence of a proper
k
-colouring
\sigma
of
G
such that every vertex has an odd colour in
\sigma
, as desired.

∎

By taking
S=V(G)
, Theorem
5.1
has the following result as a corollary.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a graph-hypergraph pair, and fix
\eta\coloneqq\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\ln\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+3)\right\rceil
. If
\Delta(\mathcal{H})\geq 49
and
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})\geq\eta/2
, then there exists an odd
(\Delta(G)+\eta)
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
.

We next show how to find a set
S
satisfying the hypothesis of Theorem
5.1
such that
\Delta(G[S])
is as small as possible.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a graph-hypergraph pair.
Let
\Delta\coloneqq\Delta(G)+\Delta(\mathcal{H})
, and
let us assume that the minimum edge size in
\mathcal{H}
is
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})\geq 12\ln\Delta
.
Let
r\coloneqq\min\{\epsilon(\mathcal{H}),\Delta(G)\}
.
Then for every
m
satisfying
4\ln\Delta\leq m\leq r/3
, if
\Delta
is large enough, there is a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
\epsilon(\mathcal{H}[S])\geq m
, and
\Delta(G[S])\leq\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}(m+\sqrt{60\,m\ln\Delta})
.

Proof.

Let us for short denote
D\coloneqq(m+\sqrt{60\,m\ln\Delta})\,\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}
. Let
\mathbf{S}
be a random subset of
V(G)
obtained by taking each vertex independently uniformly at random with probability
p=\frac{m+\sqrt{11\,m\ln\Delta}}{r}
. We note that, since
4\ln\Delta\leq m\leq r/3
by assumption, we have
p<1
and so this probability is well-defined. The result follows if there exists a realization
S
such that every
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
has
|e\cap S|\geq m
and every
v\in V(G)
satisfies
\deg_{S}(v)\leq D
.
Let
V(G)
be ordered arbitrarily. For every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, we let
\widetilde{e}
consist of the first
r
vertices (with respect to that order) of
e
.
For every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, let
\mathbf{X}_{e}\coloneqq|\widetilde{e}\cap\mathbf{S}|
be the random variable that counts the number of vertices of
\widetilde{e}
in
\mathbf{S}
, and we write
\mu_{e}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}_{e}\right]=m+\sqrt{11m\ln\Delta}
. For every vertex
v\in V(G)
, we let
\mathbf{X}_{v}\coloneqq|N_{G}(v)\cap\mathbf{S}|
be the random variable that counts the number of neighbours of
v
in
\mathbf{S}
, and we write
\mu_{v}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{X}_{v}\right]=\frac{m+\sqrt{11m\ln\Delta}}{r}\cdot\deg_{G}(v)\leq\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}(m+\sqrt{11m\ln\Delta})
. For every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, let
B^{\prime}_{e}
be the random event that
\mathbf{X}_{e}<m
, and for every vertex
v\in V(G)
, let
B_{v}
be the random event that
\mathbf{X}_{v}>D
.

Observe that, for every pair of vertices
(u,v)
such that
N(u)\cap N(v)=\emptyset
, the random events
B_{u}
and
B_{v}
are independent. So the bad event
B_{v}
is dependent with at most
\Delta(G)^{2}
bad events
B_{u}
, and at most
\Delta(G)\Delta(\mathcal{H})
bad events
B^{\prime}_{e}
. So the dependency-degree of
B_{v}
is at most
\Delta(G)\Delta\leq\Delta^{2}
.
Moreover, for every pair of edges
(e,f)
, such that
\widetilde{e}\cap\widetilde{f}=\emptyset
, the random events
B^{\prime}_{e}
and
B^{\prime}_{f}
are independent.
So the bad event
B^{\prime}_{e}
is dependent with at most
r\Delta(\mathcal{H})
bad events
B^{\prime}_{f}
, and at most
r\Delta(G)
bad events
B_{v}
. So the dependency-degree of
B^{\prime}_{e}
is at most
r\Delta\leq\Delta^{2}
.
Hence we may apply the LLL in order to show that, with non-zero probability, no event
B_{v}
or
B^{\prime}_{e}
occurs. Regarding that the maximum degree of the dependency-graph of those random events is at most
\Delta^{2}
, it suffices to prove that

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{e}<m\right]\leq\frac{1}{e\Delta^{2}}\quad\et\quad\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{v}>D\right]\leq\frac{1}{e\Delta^{2}}.

We do so by applying Chernoff bounds on the random variable
\mathbf{X}_{e}
of expectancy
\mu_{e}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{e}<m\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{(\mu_{e}-m)^{2}}{2\mu_{e}}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{11m\ln\Delta}{2m+2\sqrt{11m\ln\Delta}}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{11\ln\Delta}{2+2\sqrt{\frac{11\ln\Delta}{m}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-\frac{11}{2+\sqrt{11}}}\leq\frac{1}{e\Delta^{2}}\hskip 60.0pt\mbox{if $\Delta$ is large enough.}

We do the same with the random variable
\mathbf{X}_{v}
of expectancy
\mu_{v}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathbf{X}_{v}>D\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{(D-\mu_{v})^{2}}{2D}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{(\sqrt{60}-\sqrt{11})^{2}m\ln\Delta}{2m+2\sqrt{60m\ln\Delta}}\cdot\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{(\sqrt{60}-\sqrt{11})^{2}\ln\Delta}{2+2\sqrt{\frac{60\ln\Delta}{m}}}\cdot\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{-\frac{\left(\sqrt{60}-\sqrt{11}\right)^{2}}{2+2\sqrt{15}}}\leq\frac{1}{e\Delta^{2}}\hskip 60.0pt\mbox{if $\Delta$ is large enough.}

By Lemma
2.1
, with non-zero probability,
\mathbf{S}
satisfies the conclusion of Lemma
5.3
.
∎

We note that it is possible to drop the condition that
\Delta
is large enough in the statement of Lemma
5.3
if we set
D\coloneqq\frac{\Delta(G)}{r}(m+\sqrt{30m(1+2\ln\Delta)})
and
p\coloneqq\frac{1}{r}(m+\sqrt{5.5m(1+2\ln\Delta)})
instead, and assume that
m\geq 2+4\ln\Delta
and
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})\geq 6+12\ln\Delta
.

We may combine Theorem
5.1
and Lemma
5.3
in order to obtain that, for a quasi-regular graph
G
(that is, a graph
G
where the ratio
\Delta(G)/\delta(G)
is bounded), the odd chromatic number of
G
is not too far from its chromatic number.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
G
be a graph of maximum degree
\Delta
large enough, and minimum degree
\delta\geq 12\ln(2\Delta)
. Then

\chi_{o}(G)\leq\chi(G)+\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta+\ln\ln\Delta+3)\right\rceil+\frac{20\Delta\ln(2\Delta)}{\delta}=\chi(G)+O\left(\frac{\Delta\ln\Delta}{\delta}\right)\quad\mbox{as $\Delta\to\infty$}.

Note that the minimum degree condition in
Corollary
5.4
can be dropped. Indeed, if it is not fulfilled, then the upper bound on
\chi_{o}(G)
is larger than that given by Theorem
1.4
.

5.2.
Graphs of small chromatic number

Given a graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, if we can find a set
S
that satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
5.1
such that
\chi(G[S])
is much smaller than
\Delta(G[S])
, we can use another approach to obtain a better bound.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a graph-hypergraph pair. Fix
\eta\coloneqq\left\lceil 4(\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+\ln\ln\Delta(\mathcal{H})+3)\right\rceil
, and assume that
\Delta(\mathcal{H})\geq 49
.
For every subset of vertices
S\subseteq V(\mathcal{H})
, if
\epsilon(\mathcal{H}[S])\geq\eta/2
, then
(G,\mathcal{H})
has an odd
k
-colouring, where

k\leq\chi(G\setminus S)+\eta\,\chi(G[S]).

Proof.

Let
G_{0}\coloneqq G\setminus S
and
G_{1}\coloneqq G[S]
. For each
i\in\{0,1\}
, we write
k_{i}\coloneqq\chi(G_{i})
, and we let
\sigma_{i}
be a proper
k_{i}
-colouring of
G_{i}
.

We define a random proper
k
-colouring
{\bm{\sigma}}
of
G
as follows, where
k=k_{0}+\eta k_{1}
. For every
v\in S
, draw some random value
\mathbf{x}_{v}
uniformly at random from
[\eta]
, and let
{\bm{\sigma}}(v)\coloneqq(\sigma_{1}(v),\mathbf{x}_{v})
. For every
v\notin S
, let
{\bm{\sigma}}(v)\coloneqq\sigma_{0}(v)
.
Let us order the vertices in
V(G)
arbitrarily.
For every
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
, we let
B_{\bm{\sigma}}(e)
be the random (bad) event that
e
has no odd colour. Let
m\coloneqq\eta/2
, and let
M(e)
contain the smallest
m
vertices of
e\cap S
.
Let
\sigma
be a possible realisation of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}
. By construction, for every
v\in S
, there are
\eta
choices in

6.
The effect of multiplying the constraints

We may now wonder what happens when we seek for an odd colouring of a given graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, such that every edge
e\in E(\mathcal{H})
has
many
odd colours.
For a positive integer

, an
h
-
odd colouring

\sigma
of a hypergraph

satisfies the constraint that every edge

has at least
\min\left\{h,|e|\right\}
odd colours in

For a graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
, an
h
-odd
k
-colouring
of

is a mapping
\sigma\colon V\to[k]
that is both a proper colouring of
G
and an
h
-odd colouring of
\mathcal{H}
. The least

for which

is
h
-odd

-colourable is in turn called the
h
-
odd chromatic number
of

and we denote it by
\chi_{o}^{h}(G,\mathcal{H})
. We say that
\sigma
is an
h
-odd
k
-colouring
of
G
if
\sigma
is an
h
-odd
k
-colouring of
(G,\mathcal{H})
when
\mathcal{H}
is the neighbourhood-hypergraph of
G
. We denote
\chi_{o}^{h}(G)
the
h
-
odd chromatic number
of
G
. By a greedy algorithm, we can immediately get

for any graph

and integer

, or more generally that

. We will show that we can ensure much better upper bounds with an additional reasonable minimum edge size condition in
\mathcal{H}
.

We will rely on an extension of Lemma

to

-odd colourings, that we present hereafter.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(G,\mathcal{H})
be a graph-hypergraph pair, and
\mathscr{C}(G)
a set of colourings of
G
. Let
{\bm{\sigma}}
be drawn uniformly at random from
\mathscr{C}(G)
, and assume that there exists an integer
\tau
such that we deterministically have
|L_{\bm{\sigma}}(v)|\geq\tau
for every integer

and

, and

be the bad event that

has less than
h
odd colours in

. Then, for every subset

of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}

\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\bm{\sigma}}(e)~\middle|~{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}=\sigma_{0}\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\,\binom{m}{t}\left(\frac{2t}{e\tau}\right)^{t/2},

for every
m\leq\epsilon(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

Let

, and let

be a fixed subset of
m
vertices in
e
. Let
\sigma_{0}
be a possible realisation of
{{\bm{\sigma}}}_{\left|V(G)\setminus M(e)\right.}
.
Let
{\bm{\sigma}}_{0}
be drawn uniformly at random from the extensions of
\sigma_{0}
to
\mathscr{C}(G)
.
For every

, we let
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}\in\mathscr{C}(G)
be obtained from
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i-1}
by resampling the colour of

uniformly at random from

.
For every

, let

be the number of odd colours of

in
{\bm{\sigma}}_{i}
.
For every

, we have
\mathbf{S}_{i}=\mathbf{S}_{i-1}-1
if

is one of the
\mathbf{S}_{i-1}

this happens with probability at most

if

. Otherwise, we have

is drawn uniformly at random from

6.1.
Constructions

In the current section, we have derived upper bound for
\chi_{o}^{h}(G,\mathcal{H})
given an integer
h
and a graph-hypergraph pair
(G,\mathcal{H})
that satisfies that
\epsilon(\mathcal{H})-h
is sufficiently large. We now show that, if no such restriction holds, then the bound obtained by a greedy colouring may be close to best possible.

We propose a construction that relies on the existence of Steiner
2
-designs, which was proven in
[
19
]
. Given an integer
q
, a Steiner
2
-design on
[q]
is a collection of sets of uniform size
k
(that we call

), such that every pair of vertices from
[q]
is contained in exactly one set. We denote it

. Among its many properties, it must contain exactly
\binom{q}{2}/\binom{k}{2}
sets, and each vertex is contained in exactly
\frac{q-1}{k-1}
sets.

Proposition 6.5
.

For every integer
h\geq 1
, there exists a family of graphs
G
of increasing maximum degree
\Delta
and of minimum degree
h+1
, such that

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)\geq h\Delta+1.

Proof.

By
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
, we may find an arbitrarily large integer

such that a Steiner
2
-design

exists. We let
H=(X,Y,E)
be its (bipartite) incidence graph. We have
|X|=q
,
|Y|=\binom{q}{2}/\binom{h+1}{2}
, every vertex
y\in Y
has degree
h+1
, every vertex
x\in X
has degree
\frac{q-1}{h}
, and every pair of vertices
x,x^{\prime}\in X
is contained in the neighbourhood of some vertex

. In particular,
\delta(H)=h+1
and
\Delta(H)=\frac{q-1}{h}
.

Let
\sigma
be an
h
-odd
k
-colouring of

, where

. So every neighbourhood of size
h+1
must be rainbow in
\sigma
(i.e. contains no pair of vertices with the same colour), otherwise the number of odd colours in this neighbourhood is at most

. Since every pair of vertices from

is contained in a neighbourhood of size

, we infer that

is rainbow, and so

.

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)=O\left(\frac{\Delta^{2}}{t}\right).

We now show that Corollary
6.6
is tight up to a multiplicative constant.

Proposition 6.7
.

For every even integer
\Delta\geq 2
and
1\leq t\leq\Delta
, there is a
\Delta
-regular graph
G
such that, letting
h\coloneqq\Delta+1-t
, one has

\chi_{o}^{h}(G)>\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\Delta^{2}}{t+1}.

Proof.

Let
n\coloneqq\frac{\Delta}{2}+1
, and let
G\coloneqq L(K_{n,n})
be the line-graph of a complete bipartite graph. Let
k\coloneqq\chi_{o}^{h}(G)
, and let
\sigma
be a
h
-odd
k
-colouring of
G
.

Let
v\in V(G)
. The neighbourhood of
v
can be covered with two cliques, so each colour in
N(v)
has at most
2
occurrences. Let
s_{\sigma}(v)
denote the number of colours with
2
occurrences in
N(v)
; we must have
s_{\sigma}(v)\leq(t-1)/2
. So

(2)

S\coloneqq\sum_{v\in V(G)}s_{\sigma}(v)\leq\frac{t-1}{2}n^{2}.

Let
(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})
be the colour classes of
\sigma
. If
M_{i}
has size
m_{i}
, then it has a contribution of
m_{i}(m_{i}-1)
to
S
. Indeed,
M_{i}

m_{i}
in

, and there are
m_{i}(m_{i}-1)

2
edges from
M_{i}
in

; each of them corresponds to a vertex in
G
with a monochromatic pair of colour

in its neighbourhood. So by convexity we have

Combining this with (
2
), we obtain

k\geq\frac{2n^{2}}{t+1}>\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta^{2}}{t+1}.

∎

We finish this section with the following consequence of Theorem

.

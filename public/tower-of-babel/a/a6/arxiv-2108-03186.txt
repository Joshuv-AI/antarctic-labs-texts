Nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds and their derived categories
Fei Xie

Abstract
. We construct a Kawamata type semiorthogondal decomposition for the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves of nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds, decomposing the bounded derived category into bounded derived categories of finite dimensional algebras. This is achieved by constructing birational maps from nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds to quadric surface fibrations over the projective line.



1.
Introduction

The most interesting smooth Fano threefolds are the ones with Picard rank
1
. Their derived categories are quite well known
[
17
]
. But much less is known about the derived categories of singular Fano threefolds. The first examples are given by Kawamata
[
11
]
for nodal del Pezzo threefolds of degree
6
and
8
(more details below). In both examples the threefolds have only one ordinary double point (or nodal point). It is an interesting and important question to understand the derived categories of other singular Fano threefolds and see what they look like when the Fano threefolds have multiple nodal points. In this paper we study the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
for nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds
X
(terminal Gorenstein Fano threefolds of index
2
and degree
5
) over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
0
and construct a
Kawamata type semiorthogonal decomposition
(KSOD) defined in
[
8
, Definition 4.1]
for Gorenstein projective varieties. This answers the question asked in
[
8
, Example 4.16]
about the existence of KSODs for these threefolds. A KSOD is an
admissible
semiorthogonal decomposition (SOD) of the type

(1.1)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S_{1}),\dots,\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S_{n})\rangle

where
\mathcal{A}
is a subcategory of
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
,
S_{1},\dots,S_{n}
are finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-algebras and
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(S_{i}),1\leqslant i\leqslant n
are bounded derived categories of finitely generated right modules over
S_{i}
. Here admissible means that the components of the SOD are admissible subcategories.

The classification of terminal Gorenstein del Pezzo threefolds of degree
5
states that they have only nodal singularities and the number of nodal points is at most
3
. Moreover, the quintic del Pezzo threefold
X_{m}
with
m
nodes (
0\leqslant m\leqslant 3
) exists and is unique up to isomorphism for each
m
; see
[
21
, Corollary 8.7]
. The SOD of the derived category
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X_{0})
of the smooth quintic del Pezzo threefold is well known; see
[
17
, Theorem 4.2]
. It has a full exceptional collection of four objects. In this paper, we will focus on the singular threefolds
X_{m},1\leqslant m\leqslant 3
and show that two of the exceptional objects in the smooth case are replaced by derived categories of finite dimensional algebras. Furthermore, we will see that the construction of the SODs of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X_{m})
is closely related to derived categories of a chain of
m

\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s. For a chain of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s, there is a SOD as below.

(
[
6
]
)
.

\Gamma
be a chain of
n+1

\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s. Then

The main result of the paper is the construction of the following Kawamata type SODs, which indicates that
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X_{m})
behave in a similar way as
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\Gamma)
.

Theorem
4.8
.

Let
X_{m}
be the quintic del Pezzo threefolds with
m
nodes for
m=1,2,3
. Then there is an admissible semiorthogonal decomposition

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X_{m})=\langle\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(R_{m-1}),\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(R_{1}),\mathcal{O}_{X_{m}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{m}}(1)\rangle

where
R_{0}=\Bbbk
and
R_{1},R_{2}
are the path algebras defined by (
1.2
).

The reason we care about Kawamata type SODs for a singular scheme
X
is because it gives a complete decomposition of the “singular part” of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
in terms of finite dimensional algebras. More precisely, the singularity of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is measured by the Orlov’s triangulated categories
\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{sg}}(X)
of singularities, which is defined as the Verdier quotient of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
by the subcategory
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
of perfect complexes. In this sense Kawamata type SOD would induce a SOD of
\mathbf{D}_{\operatorname{sg}}(X)
by triangulated categories of singularities of finite dimensional algebras.

If a Kawamata type SOD does exist, one can further ask whether there exists a full exceptional collection for the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
in the SOD (
1.1
). In the main result the answer is yes with
\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{O}_{X_{m}},\mathcal{O}_{X_{m}}(1)\rangle
. The known examples where
\mathcal{A}
has a full exceptional collection is provided in
[
8
, §4]
. In dimension
1
, a chain of projective lines studied by Burban (see Proposition
1.1
) is one family of such examples. In dimension
2
, Karmazyn-Kuznetsov-Shinder
[
9
]
proved that a projective Gorenstein toric surface has a SOD of this kind if and only if it has the trivial Brauer group. Moreover, their method also provides some non-toric examples: the du Val sextic del Pezzo surfaces
[
14
]
and the du Val quintic del Pezzo surfaces
[
24
]
. In dimension
3
, Kawamata
[
11
]
provides two such examples, the nodal quadric threefold and the nodal sextic del Pezzo threefold, by investigating the derived category of a threefold with an ordinary double point. More explicitly, Kawamata proved in
[
11
, Theorem 6.1 and 5.1]
that for certain threefolds
X
with an ordinary double point, the right (or left) orthogonal complement to
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is equivalent to
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(R_{1})
(see (
1.2
) for the definition of
R_{1}
). One notes that
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(R_{1})
is also the right orthogonal complement to the structure sheaf in the derived category of a chain of two
\mathbb{P}^{1}
’s by Burban. This phenomena is expected by Knörrer periodicity
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
.

For a threefold
X
with multiple nodal points, one naturally wonders when the SOD (
1.1
) of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
exists and if it does exist, which algebras
S_{i}
will appear. It is already known by
[
8
, Example 3.15 and 4.16]
(see also
[
20
, Corollary 3.7]
) that derived categories of nodal del Pezzo threefolds of degree between
1
and
4

2.1.
Clifford algebras and spinor sheaves

In this section we review the Clifford algebra of a quadric hypersurface and spinor sheaves associated with linear subspaces of a quadric hypersurface, with a focus on quadric surfaces. Spinor sheaves on singular quadrics are studied by Addington in
[
1
]
. He follows the convention that spinor bundles on smooth quadrics are generated by global sections; e.g., the spinor bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is
\mathcal{O}(1)
. Similarly, spinor sheaves of a quadric fibration can be constructed and they are used to study derived categories of nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds in §
4
.

Let
V
be a
\Bbbk
-vector space and let
q
be a quadratic form on
V
. The Clifford algebra of
q
is

B_{q}=T^{\bullet}(V)/\langle v\otimes v-q(v)\cdot 1\rangle_{v\in V}

where
T^{\bullet}(V)
is the free associative algebra generated by
V
. We view
T^{\bullet}(V)
as a graded algebra with elements of
V
having degree
1
. Then
B_{q}
has a natural
\mathbb{Z}/2
-grading: the even part
B_{q0}
is spanned by monomials of even degrees and the odd part
B_{q1}
is spanned by monomials of odd degrees.

Let
W
be an isotropic subspace of
V
, that is,
q|_{W}=0
. Then the subalgebra of
B_{q}
generated by
W
is
\Lambda^{\bullet}W
. Let
I^{W}
be the right ideal
(\Lambda^{\dim W}W)\cdot B_{q}
of
B_{q}
generated by
\Lambda^{\dim W}W
. Let
I^{W}=I^{W}_{0}\oplus I^{W}_{1}
be the decomposition into the even part
I^{W}_{0}
and the odd part
I^{W}_{1}
. Consider the map of vector bundles

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)\otimes I^{W}&\xrightarrow{{\delta^{W}}}&\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\otimes I^{W}\\
v\otimes\xi&\mapsto&1\otimes\xi v\\
\end{array}

where
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)
is regarded as the universal subbundle of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\otimes V
. We use
\delta^{W}(n)
to denote the map obtained by tensoring
\delta^{W}
with
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(n)
. Since
\delta^{W}(n+1)\circ\delta^{W}(n)=q
for every
n
, we have
\delta^{W}
is an isomorphism away from the quadric
Q=\{q=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}(V)
. Because
\ker{(\delta^{W})}
is torsion and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)\otimes I_{W}
is torsion free, we have
\delta^{W}
is injective. Hence, there are short exact sequences

(2.1)

\begin{split}&0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)\otimes I^{W}_{0}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\otimes I^{W}_{1}\to S_{W}\to 0,\\
&0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}(-1)\otimes I^{W}_{1}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{1}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V)}\otimes I^{W}_{0}\to T_{W}\to 0.\\
\end{split}

where
\delta^{W}=\delta^{W}_{0}\oplus\delta^{W}_{1}
and
S_{W}\coloneqq\operatorname{coker}{(\delta^{W}_{0})},T_{W}\coloneqq\operatorname{coker}{(\delta^{W}_{1})}
are supported on
Q
. The map
\delta^{W}_{1}
can be induced from
\delta^{W}_{0}
because

\delta^{W}_{1}\cong\delta^{W}_{0}\otimes_{B_{q0}}B_{q1}.

In particular,
I^{W}_{1}\cong I^{W}_{0}\otimes_{B_{q0}}B_{q1}
. There are resolutions on
Q
for
S_{W}
(and similarly for
T_{W}
):

(2.2)

\begin{split}\cdots\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{0}(-2)}\mathcal{O}_{Q}(-2)\otimes I^{W}_{1}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{1}(-1)}\mathcal{O}_{Q}(-1)\otimes I^{W}_{0}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{0}}\mathcal{O}_{Q}\otimes I^{W}_{1}\to S_{W}\to 0,\\
0\to S_{W}\to\mathcal{O}_{Q}(1)\otimes I^{W}_{0}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{0}(2)}\mathcal{O}_{Q}(2)\otimes I^{W}_{1}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{1}(3)}\mathcal{O}_{Q}(3)\otimes I^{W}_{0}\xrightarrow{\delta^{W}_{0}(4)}\cdots.\\
\end{split}

The sheaves
S_{W},T_{W}
constructed here are called the

of

associated with the linear subspace
\mathbb{P}(W)\subset Q
. They are the generalization of spinor bundles on smooth quadrics. Similarly for a flat quadric fibration
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V})\to S
and an isotropic subbundle
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}
, one can construct spinor sheaves
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{W}},\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{W}}
of
\mathcal{Q}
associated with
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{W})\subset\mathcal{Q}
. For details of this construction, see
[
25
, §2.2]
.

2.2.
Noncommutative projective schemes

In this section we introduce noncommutative projective schemes in the sense of pairs of projective schemes together with sheaves of algebras. We will define the derived push-forward and pull-back functors of a morphism and prove the projection formula (Lemma
2.5
) in this setting. The generalization to noncommutative schemes in the sense of pairs of noetherian schemes together with sheaves of algebras is given in Appendix A of
[
25
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

A pair
(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})
is a
noncommutative projective scheme
over
\Bbbk
if
X
is a projective scheme over
\Bbbk
and
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module and sheaf of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras. A
morphism

\Theta=(\theta,\theta_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
of noncommutative projective schemes over
\Bbbk
consists of a morphism
\theta\colon X\to Y
of schemes and a homomorphism
\theta_{\mathcal{A}}\colon L_{0}\theta^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}\to\mathcal{A}_{X}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras.

Following the same lines after Definition 10.3 in
[
16
]
, there is the derived functor

\Theta_{*}=\theta_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}))\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})).

For every
G\in\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
,

L_{0}\Theta^{*}G\coloneqq\theta^{-1}G\otimes_{\theta^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}\mathcal{A}_{X}\cong L_{0}\theta^{*}G\otimes_{L_{0}\theta^{*}\mathcal{A}_{Y}}\mathcal{A}_{X}\in\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}).

Since
Y
is projective, there is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{V}
(of finite rank if
G
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module) and an
\mathcal{O}_{Y}
-module epimorphism
\mathcal{V}\overset{s}{\twoheadrightarrow}G
. Thus, there is a right
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-module epimorphism

\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{Y}\twoheadrightarrow G,\quad v\otimes a\mapsto s(v)a,\quad v\in\mathcal{V},a\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}

from the locally free
\mathcal{A}_{Y}
-module
\mathcal{V}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{Y}
. This means that
\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
has enough locally free objects. By Lemma 13.29.1 and the proof of Lemma 20.26.12 in
[
22
]
, for every complex
G\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
, there is a locally free resolution
K\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
. We can define the derived functor
\Theta^{*}
of
L_{0}\Theta^{*}
by

\Theta^{*}G\coloneqq L_{0}\Theta^{*}K.

This gives a well-defined functor

\Theta^{*}\colon\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))\to\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})).

It is clear that
\Theta^{*}
is a left adjoint of
\Theta_{*}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Projection formula)
.

Let
\Theta=(\theta,\theta_{\mathcal{A}})\colon(X,\mathcal{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y})
be a morphism between noncommutative projective schemes defined by Definition
2.4
. For every
F\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(X,\mathcal{A}_{X}^{\operatorname{op}}))
and
G\in\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
the natural map

(2.8)

G\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{Y}}^{\mathbb{L}}\Theta_{*}(F)\to\Theta_{*}(\Theta^{*}(G)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}F)

is an isomorphism in
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y))
.

Proof.

There exists a natural map

\Theta^{*}(G\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{Y}}^{\mathbb{L}}\Theta_{*}(F))\cong\Theta^{*}(G)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}\Theta^{*}\Theta_{*}(F)\to\Theta^{*}(G)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}F

because
\Theta^{*}
commutes with derived tensor functors and
(\Theta^{*},\Theta_{*})
are adjoint. Applying the adjointness again gives (
2.8
). Let
T\subset\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
be the full triangulated subcategory of objects
G
such that (
2.8
) is an isomorphism. We will prove that
T=\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
.

Note that
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y))
has a compact generator
U\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(Y)
by
[
5
, Theorem 3.1.1]
and is cocomplete (admits arbitrary direct sums). Then
\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
has a compact generator
U\otimes\mathcal{A}_{Y}
and is also cocomplete. Clearly
U\otimes\mathcal{A}_{Y}\in T
and
T
is closed under arbitrary direct sums because
\Theta^{*},\Theta_{*},\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{Y}}^{\mathbb{L}},\otimes_{\mathcal{A}_{X}}^{\mathbb{L}}
all commute with direct sums. We deduce from
[
18
, Theorem 2.2c]
that
T=\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{QCoh}(Y,\mathcal{A}_{Y}))
.
∎

2.3.
Some facts about derived categories

We first provide a useful lemma that provides an easy criterion for semiorthogonal decompositions of derived categories of Gorenstein projective schemes to be admissible and provides a mutation for such decompositions.

Recall that given a map
f\colon X\to S
, a triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is called
S
-linear if
\mathcal{F}\otimes f^{*}\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{A}
for every
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(S)
. A semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is
S
-linear if each component is
S
-linear, and is admissible if each component is admissible.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f\colon X\to S
be a map between Gorenstein projective schemes. Assume that there is an
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

(2.9)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\rangle

such that all but one components are contained in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
; i.e., there exists some
t
,
1\leqslant t\leqslant n
such that
\mathcal{A}_{j}\subset\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
for every
j\neq t
. Then the relative dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X/S}
is a line bundle and the semiorthogonal decomposition (
2.9
) is admissible. In addition, there is an admissible
S
-linear semiorthogonal decomposition

(2.10)

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X/S},\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\rangle.

Proof.

Since
X,S
are Gorenstein projective, dualizing sheaves
\omega_{X},\omega_{S}
are line bundles. Then
\omega_{X/S}
is a line bundle because
\omega_{X/S}\cong\omega_{X}\otimes f^{*}\omega_{S}^{\vee}
. Set
\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\rangle
. By assumption we have
\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is contained in
\mathbf{D}^{\operatorname{perf}}(X)
. Then by
[
8
, Lemma 2.15]

\mathcal{A}_{n}
is admissible and there is a SOD

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X},\mathcal{A}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X},\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n-1}\rangle,

which by definition is
S
-linear. Since
\omega_{X}\cong\omega_{X/S}\otimes f^{*}\omega_{S}
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is
S
-linear, we have
\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X/S}
. This gives the SOD (
2.10
). Repeating this process, we get
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is admissible for each
i,1\leqslant i\leqslant n
and an
S
-linear SOD

\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{i}\otimes\omega_{X/S},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\otimes\omega_{X/S},\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{i-1}\rangle.

Thus, (
2.9
)(
2.10
) are admissible SODs.
∎

In the next two propositions we present the behavior of the derived push-forward functor of certain proper morphism with fibers of dimension at most
1
and how it induces semiorthogonal decompositions.

Proposition 2.10

(
[
2
, Theorem 7.13]
)
.

Let
\gamma\colon Y\to X
be a proper morphism of normal varieties over
\Bbbk
with fibers of dimension at most
1
such that (the derived push-forward)
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Let
X^{1}
be the locus of
X
where fibers of
\gamma
are one-dimensional and assume that
X^{1}
is a finite set of points. For each closed point
x\in X^{1}
, let
C_{x}\coloneqq\gamma^{-1}(x)_{\text{red}}
be the reduced fiber over
x
and let
C_{x,i}
be its irreducible components. Denote by
l_{x,i}\colon C_{x,i}\hookrightarrow Y
the embeddings. Then each

is a smooth rational curve and
\gamma_{*}\colon\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
is a Verdier quotient with kernel
\ker(\gamma_{*})=\langle l_{x,i*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{x,i}}(-1)\rangle_{x\in X^{1}}
; i.e., we have an equivalence
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)/\ker(\gamma_{*})\cong\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
.

Proof.

The condition
\gamma_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
implies that
H^{1}(C_{x},\mathcal{O}_{C_{x}})=0
for every
x\in X^{1}
. Note that the base field
\Bbbk
is assumed to be algebraically closed. Thus,
C_{x,i}
is a smooth rational curve by
[
3
, Theorem D.1]
.

Now we prove the claims for the derived push-forward functor
\gamma_{*}
. Since the fibers of
\gamma\colon Y\to X
have dimension at most
1
, the spectral sequence
R^{i}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathcal{F})\Rightarrow R^{i+j}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{j}
is the cohomology sheaf in degree
j
, degenerates and we have short exact sequences

(2.11)

0\to R^{1}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{H}^{j-1}(\mathcal{F})\to R^{j}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}\to R^{0}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{H}^{j}(\mathcal{F})\to 0.

This implies that
\ker(\gamma_{*})
is generated by
\ker(\gamma_{*})\cap\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
. Since the one-dimensional fibers of
\gamma
are isolated by assumption, every
\mathcal{F}\in\ker(\gamma_{*})\cap\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
is the direct sum of sheaves supported on
C_{x},x\in X^{1}
. Together with Proposition 7.12 in
[
2
]
this implies that
\ker(\gamma_{*})\cap\operatorname{Coh}(Y)
has finite length. Therefore, Theorem 7.13 in
[
2
]
implies that
\ker(\gamma_{*})=\langle l_{x,i*}\mathcal{O}_{C_{x,i}}(-1)\rangle_{x\in X^{1}}
.

For the equivalence of the induced functor
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)/\ker(\gamma_{*})\to\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
, we use an argument similar to
[
4
, Theorem 2.14]
. We will make use of the canonical truncation functors
\tau^{\leqslant p},\tau^{\geqslant p}
. The sequence (
2.11
) implies that if
\tau^{\leqslant p}\mathcal{G}=0
for
\mathcal{G}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)
, then

(2.12)

\gamma_{*}(\tau^{\leqslant p-1}\gamma^{*}\mathcal{G})=0,\quad\text{i.e.,}\quad\mathcal{G}\cong\gamma_{*}\gamma^{*}\mathcal{G}\cong\gamma_{*}(\tau^{\geqslant p}\gamma^{*}\mathcal{G}).

We deduce from this that
\gamma_{*}
is essentially surjective. Finally, we claim that for
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)
,

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)/\ker(\gamma_{*})}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{F}^{\prime})\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(X)}(\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F},\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}^{\prime})

is a bijection. The inverse map is constructed as follows. Choose

such that
\tau^{\leqslant p}\mathcal{F}=0
and
\tau^{\leqslant p-1}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}=0
. A map

gives a map

Let
K
be the cone of
\gamma^{*}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
, which is bounded above but not necessarily bounded. Taking
\mathcal{G}=\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}
in (
2.12

\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}\cong\gamma_{*}(\tau^{\geqslant p}\gamma^{*}\gamma_{*}\mathcal{F}).

Since

, we get
\gamma_{*}\tau^{\geqslant p}K=0
and thus

. This implies that the map

\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
in the Verdier quotient
\mathbf{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(Y)/\ker(\gamma_{*})
.
∎

3.
Geometry of Nodal Quintic Del Pezzo Threefolds

In this section we will describe the nodal quintic del Pezzo threefolds
X_{m}
in a way that their derived categories can be understood. In short, there is a small resolution

at a nodal point and

Definition 3.1

A quintic del Pezzo threefold is a normal integral projective threefold

-K_{X}
is ample, divisible by
2

The classification indicates that the quintic del Pezzo threefolds have at worst nodal singularities and have at most

. Moreover, they can be realized as codimension

linear sections of
\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)
embedded into
\mathbb{P}^{9}
via Plücker embedding. Let

be a codimension

\operatorname{Gr}(2,5)

Unique Asymptotics of Steady Ricci Solitons with Symmetry
Zilu Ma, Hamidreza Mahmoudian, Nataša Šešum

Abstract
In this paper we study 4d gradient steady Ricci solitons, which are weak κ \kappa -solutions, and admit O ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{O}(3) -symmetry. Under a weak curvature decay condition, we find precise geometric asymptotics of such solitons, which are similar to those for 3d compact κ \kappa -solutions found in [ ABDS22 ] . This is the first step towards the classification of 4d gradient steady Ricci solitons and more general ancient Ricci flows.



1
Introduction

We call a triple
(M^{4},g,f)
a complete gradient steady Ricci soliton,
if
(M^{4},g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold and

\Ric=\nabla^{2}f.

By a classical result due to Hamilton, we may normalize the metric so that

R+|\nabla f|^{2}=1.

If
(\Phi_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denotes the
1
-parameter group of diffeomorphisms generated by
-\nabla f,
then
(M^{4},g_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a Ricci flow, called the canonical form induced by the steady soliton
(M^{4},g,f).

Steady solitons model Type II singularities under Ricci flow (
[
Ham93e
]
,
[
Bre20
]
). Recall that Hamilton (
[
Ham93
]
) distinguished between type I and type II finite time singularities at time
T
, depending on whether
(T-t)\,|{\Rm}|
stays uniformly bounded, or not. By
[
EMT11
]
it is known that type I singularities are modeled on shrinkers, and in general they are easier to analyze than type II singularities. In an attempt to better understand type II singularities, steady Ricci solitons received considerable attention lately, but no classification of steady solitons in general case has been obtained in dimensions
n\geq 4
.

Let us recall the notion of
\kappa
-solutions as introduced by Perelman in
[
Per02
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
\kappa
-solution is an ancient solution of the Ricci flow, which has nonnegative curvature operator, positive scalar curvature, bounded curvature on compact time intervals and is
\kappa
-noncollapsed on all scales.

Note that
\kappa
-solutions arise as blowup limits near cylindrical singularities. They played an important role in Perelman’s proof of the geometrization conjecture in
[
Per03a
]
and
[
Per03b
]
. The complete classification of these solutions in 3d Ricci flow has been recently obtained in
[
Bre20
]
and
[
BDS21
]
, which are, up to scaling and finite quotients, the round
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the round shrinking
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the 3d Bryant soliton, or the 3d Perelman oval.

In this paper, we shall weaken the notion of
\kappa
-solutions and in turn study the following weak
\kappa
-solutions.

Definition 1.2
.

A
weak
\kappa
-solution
is an ancient solution of the Ricci flow, which has nonnegative sectional curvature, positive scalar curvature, bounded curvature on compact time intervals and is
\kappa
-noncollapsed on all scales.

Let us focus now on dimension
n=4
. In
[
Bre14
]
it has been shown that a steady gradient Ricci soliton of dimension
n\geq 4
, which has positive sectional curvature, and is asymptotically cylindrical is the Bryant soliton. Known examples of 4d steady Ricci solitons up to now include the
\mathrm{O}(4)
-symmetric 4d steady soliton constructed by Bryant, the 3d Bryant soliton times a line, and the 1-parameter family of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathrm{O}(3)
symmetric steady solitons constructed by Lai (
[
Lai20
]
). Solutions found by Lai are the analogous of oval bowl translators in the mean curvature flow constructed by Hoffman-Ilmanen-Martin-White. Note that the 4d Bryant soliton has a round cylinder,
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
as its tangent flow, whereas the last two examples have a bubble sheet
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as their tangent flows. Inspired by an analogous classification of noncollapsed translators in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
(
[
CHH23
]
) one conjectures (see also
[
Has23
]
) the following.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Any weak
\kappa
-solution in 4d Ricci flow, that is also a steady Ricci soliton, is up to scaling and finite quotients, given by one of the following: 4d Bryant soliton, the 3d Bryant soliton times a line, or belongs to the 1-parameter family of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathrm{O}(3)
-symmetric steady solitons constructed by Lai (
[
Lai20
]
).

Remark 1.4
.

By the work in
[
BCDMZ21
]
, see Theorem
2.1
below, gradient steady Ricci soliton
(M^{4},g,f)
, that also induces a weak
\kappa
-solution, has a unique tangent flow at infinity. By
[
CMZ23
, Corollary 5.4]
, if
\Ric>0
, the unique tangent flow at infinity is either
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In the former case, by
[
Bre14
, Theorem 1.2]
combined with
[
CMZ23
, Theorem 1.5]
, we know
(M^{4},g,f)
is isometric to the Bryant soliton, c.f.
[
DZ20a
, Theorem 1.5]
and
[
ZZ23
, Corollary 0.3]
. Hence, we can assume from now on that the Ricci gradient steady soliton we consider here has
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the tangent flow at infinity.
We also remark that by the work in
[
CMZ21
]
this tangent flow at infinity (in Bamler’s sense) is identical to Perelman’s asymptotic shrinker, since
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
is smooth.

Remark 1.5
.

As Perelman mentioned in
[
Per02
, §11.1]
which was proved by Zhang in
[
Z20
]
, for an ancient flow with
\Rm\geq 0,

\kappa
-noncollapsedness is equivalent to bounded Nash entropy. See also
[
MZ21
, Theorem 1.13]
, where for steady solitons that have positive sectional curvature,
\kappa
-noncollapsedness is equivalent to bounded Nash entropy. Therefore, in our setting instead of assuming a soliton being
\kappa
-noncollapsed, we may also assume that the canonical Ricci flow induced by
(M^{4},g,f)
is noncollapsed, i.e.,

\mu_{\infty}:=\inf_{\tau>0}\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)>-\infty,

where
\mathcal{N}_{o,0}(\tau)
denotes the pointed Nash entropy based at a space-time point
(o,0)
at scale
\tau
, see, e.g.,
[
Bam20a
]
.

Assumptions:
In what follows we will consider GSRS (gradient steady Ricci soliton),
(M^{4},g,f)
that is as well a weak
\kappa
-solution, and also has

(
A1

.

(

(M^{4},g)

\mathrm{O}(3)
-symmetry, that is,
\mathrm{O}(3)\subset\rm Isom(M^{4},g)
.

(

A soliton potential function

has a critical point.

Next, denote by

\mathcal{M}:=\{\mbox{GSRS}\,\,(M,g,f)\,\,\mbox{that { induces a weak}}\,\,\,\kappa\,\mbox{-solution, and satisfies assumptions {\bf(A1)-(A3)}}\}.

By Remark

we may assume that tangent flow of any solution in
\mathcal{M}
is
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Note that (
A1
) implies a critical point of
f
must be unique, and we denote this point by
o
. (This holds since
\nabla^{2}f=\Ric>0
and
f
is strictly convex.) It is also known that the level sets
\Sigma_{s}:=\{f=s\}
are 3d compact manifolds, which are in fact diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
by (

[
DZ21

.

The analysis is somewhat similar to
[
ABDS22
]
. We describe our approach here in order to make the comparison with
[
ABDS22
]
easier. The main idea is that when we compare different level sets
\Sigma_{s}
for large
s
, the induced metric changes in a way that closely resembles Ricci flow.

Consider the level sets
\Sigma_{s}
, for
s\geq s_{0}
, where
s_{0}
is a large number that will be chosen later. Denote by
\Sigma:=\{f=s_{0}\}
. Let
\chi_{s}
be a family of diffeomorphisms satisfying

\partial_{s}\chi_{s}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\quad{\chi_{s_{0}}={\rm id}_{\Sigma}.}

Clearly,
\Sigma_{s}=\{f=s\}=\chi_{s}(\Sigma)
for
s\geq s_{0}.

We define

\bar{g}_{s}:=\chi_{s}^{*}(g|_{\Sigma_{s}}),

(1.6)

for
s\geq 0
.
Then
(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{s})
is isometric to
(\Sigma_{s},g|_{\Sigma_{s}})
, for
s\geq s_{0}.
So,

\partial_{s}\bar{g}_{s}=\chi_{s}^{*}\left(\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{\nabla f}g\right)|_{\Sigma_{s}}\right)=\frac{2\chi_{s}^{*}(\Ric|_{\Sigma_{s}})}{\chi_{s}^{*}|\nabla f|^{2}}.

Thus, we may write

\partial_{s}\bar{g}_{s}=2\Ric_{\bar{g}_{s}}+2\bar{\mathcal{E}}(s),

(1.7)

where

\chi_{s*}\bar{\mathcal{E}}(s)=\frac{\Ric|_{\Sigma_{s}}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}-\overline{\Ric}.

(1.8)

Here and in the following, we denote by
\overline{\Ric},\bar{R}
the curvatures of the induced metric
g|_{\Sigma_{s}}.

Our goal in this paper is to complete the first step needed in completing Conjecture
1.3
, in the case the tangent flow is the bubble sheet. More precisely, we describe precise asymptotics of
(M^{4},g,f)
. In other words, our result shows that under suitable scalar curvature decay, the level sets
\Sigma_{s}
have similar matched asymptotics as the ones of Perelman’s 3d solution (
[
BDS21
]
). We actually establish unique asymptotics for the profile function of level sets, which in the light of our assumption
(A2)
are
\mathrm{O}(3)
symmetric (see Lemma
2.6
below).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)\in\mathcal{M}
. Assume in addition that

\sup_{\partial B_{r}(o)}R=O(r^{-\eta}),

for some
\eta\in{(\frac{1}{3},1)}
.
Then there exists a reference point
q\in\Sigma
so that the following holds. Let
F(z,s)
denote the radius of the sphere of symmetry in
(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{s})
, which has signed distance
z
from the reference point
q
. Then the profile function
F(z,s)
has the following asymptotic expansions.

(i)

Fix a large number
L
. Then, as
\tau\to-\infty
,

F(z,s)=\sqrt{2s}-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}\log s\sqrt{s}}\,(z^{2}-2s)+o\Big(s^{-1/2}(\log s)^{-1}\Big),

for
|z|\leq L\,\sqrt{s}
.

(ii)

Fix a small number
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, and a large number
M\geq 20
. If
s\geq s(\theta,M)
, then we have

2s-\frac{M^{2}+C(\theta)}{M^{2}-2}\,\frac{z^{2}}{2\log s}\leq F^{2}(z,s)\leq 2s-\frac{M^{2}-C(\theta)}{M^{2}}\,\frac{z^{2}}{2\log s},

whenever
z\geq M\sqrt{s}
and
F(z,s)\geq\theta\,\sqrt{2s}
.

(iii)

The reference point
q
has distance
(2+o(1))\sqrt{s\log s}
from each tip of
(\Sigma,\bar{g}_{s})
. The scalar curvature at each tip is given by
(1+o(1))\,\frac{\log s}{s}
. Finally, if we rescale the solution around one of the tips, then the rescaled solutions converge to the Bryant soliton as
s\to\infty
.

Without the scalar curvature decay assumption asymptotics could look quite differently from what is stated in Theorem
1.9
, and we describe those in Section
6
. Nevertheless, we still expect Theorem
1.9
to hold even without the scalar curvature decay assumption. Based on Brendle’s work in
[
Bre14
]
, Deng and Zhu in
[
DZ20a
]
proved that 4d Bryant is the only positively curved steady soliton with linear curvature decay, i.e.,
\eta=1
. Our work seems to be the first to analyze steady solitons with slower curvature decay.

Organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we prove some geometric facts about our soliton, and also analyze the structure of level sets, which up to an error, turn out to have similar behavior to 3d Perelman’s solution. In Section
3
we study more carefully asymptotics of the level sets in the cylindrical region. We adopt methods from
[
Bre20
]
and
[
ABDS22
]
to show that we have dichotomy in asymptotic behaviors of projections of our soliton onto eigenspaces of linearized Ricci flow operator around the cylinder. In Section
4
we show that under additional curvature decay assumption we have unique asymptotic behavior of our soliton in the cylindrical region. In Section
5
we give precise matched asymtotics of our solution in the case we have curvature decay assumption, and hence neutral mode dominating. In Section
6
we drop the assumption on scalar curvature decay, and instead impose the assumption on reflection symmetry. Without the scalar curvature decay assumption we are not able to rule out the case of positive modes dominating in dichotomy result stated in Proposition
3.41
. In the case of positive modes dominating we give precise asymptotics of our solution in the cylindrical region, hoping that can be used to eventually rule out positive mode dominating at all.

2.1
Coarse estimates

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a steady gradient Ricci soliton inducing a weak
\kappa
-solution. By rescaling

Since

Thanks to technical justifications made in

]

Theorem 2.1

(
[

]

Let

(\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma)\times\mathbb{R},\quad\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad(\mathbb{S}^{2}\times_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}.

As discussed in Section
1

We will use a splitting result for
4

[

]

Definition 2.2

(Dimension Reduction)
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complete Riemannian manifold. We say
(M^{n},g)

dimension reduces
to
(N^{n-1},h)
along some sequence

Theorem 2.3

(
[

, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
(M^{4},g,f)
be a complete noncollapsed steady soliton with
\sec\geq 0,\Ric>0

Suppose

\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
as its tangent flow at infinity.
Then along any sequence

,

dimension reduces to either

, the 3d Bryant’s steady soliton, or

Now assume

, let

denote the radius of the symmetric

passing through

. Using the Theorem above we can show the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

There is a constant

depending on

such that

for any

Proof.

Suppose there exist

such that

By Theorem

,
(M,R(x_{i})g,x_{i})
converges to some limit pointed manifold

, locally smoothly. Here,

is either

or

2.2
Structure of the level sets

The goal of this subsection is to describe some geometric properties of the level sets of the potential function
f
. We show that each level set is rotationally symmetric, asymptotically cylindrical and has exactly two tips resembling the Bryant soliton. Throughout this subsection, we assume
(M^{4},g,f)\in\mathcal{M}
unless otherwise stated. For simplicity, we also assume that the scalar curvature decays uniformly.

In the Lemma that follows we show that under our Assumptions
(A1)-(A3)
, the level sets
\Sigma_{s}=\{f=s\}
of
f
are rotationally symmetric.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
o
be a critical point of the potential
f
. For any
\phi\in{\rm Isom}(g),

\phi(o)=o,\quad\phi^{*}f=f.

As a consequence, for any level set
\Sigma_{s}
with the induced metric,

{\rm Isom}(g)|_{\Sigma_{s}}\subseteq{\rm Isom}(\Sigma_{s}).

Proof.

By Assumptions
(A1)
and
(A3)
, the point
o
is the unique point with
R(o)=1
. Since we have
\phi^{*}R=R
, we must have
\phi(o)=o
. Note also that
\phi^{*}f
is a potential function for
(M,g)
. Hence
\nabla^{2}(\phi^{*}f-f)=0
and
\phi^{*}f=f+C
for some
C
, otherwise
(M,g)
locally splits off a line in the direction of
\nabla(\phi^{*}f-f)
which contradicts (
A1
). Finally we have

C=(\phi^{*}f)(o)-f(o)=0.

∎

In this section we discuss fine asymptotic behaviors of the level sets

\Sigma=\Sigma_{s_{0}}=\{f=s_{0}\}

Consider the family

\partial_{s}\chi_{s}=\frac{\nabla f}{|\nabla f|^{2}},\quad\chi_{s_{0}}={\rm id}.

This family preserves the level sets in the sense that

By the standard Gauss formula, we have (see, for example,

Recall that we have normalized the metric

\displaystyle\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{R_{ij}}{|\nabla f|^{2}}-\overline{R}_{ij}=\frac{1}{|\nabla f|^{2}}RR_{ij}+R_{ij}-\overline{R}_{ij}

\bar{g}_{s}

F(z,s)
in the Proposition below.

Proposition 3.16
.

In commuting variables
z
and
s
, we have

\displaystyle-F_{s}

\displaystyle=F_{zz}-F^{-1}(1+F_{z}^{2})+2F_{z}\left\{\frac{F_{z}}{F}(0,s)-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{F_{z}^{2}}{F^{2}}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-F^{-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}+F_{z}\int_{0}^{z}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Proof.

Fix
p\in\Sigma
and let
B
be the
O(3)
-orbit passing through
p
. Recall that
z(p,s)
is the signed distance form our basepoint
q
to
B
measured with respect to
\bar{g}_{s}
. On one hand,

\left.\partial_{s}\right\rvert_{p=const.}F=\left.\partial_{s}\right\rvert_{z=const.}F+z_{s}F_{z},

and by standard distance distortion,

z_{s}=\partial_{s}z(p,s)=\int_{0}^{z}\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{s}\bar{g}_{s})(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})\,ds=-2\int_{0}^{z}\frac{F_{zz}}{F}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta+\int_{0}^{z(s)}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta.

On the other hand,

\left.\partial_{s}\right\rvert_{p=const.}(F^{2}\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}})=2(-F_{zz}F+1-F_{z}^{2}+\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb})g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}},

and thus

\left.\partial_{s}\right\rvert_{p=const.}F=-F_{zz}+F^{-1}(1-F_{z}^{2})+F^{-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}

Hence, in commuting variables
z
and
s
we have

\displaystyle-F_{s}

\displaystyle=F_{zz}-F^{-1}(1-F_{z}^{2})-2F_{z}\int_{0}^{z}\frac{F_{zz}}{F}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\qquad-F^{-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}+F_{z}\int_{0}^{z}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

The conclusion follows by integration by parts.
∎

We prove the following Lemma that will be implicitly used.

Lemma 3.17
.

For
s\geq\underline{s}
,

\displaystyle|F_{z}|\leq 1,\qquad F_{zz}<0,\qquad F^{m}\partial_{z}^{m+1}F\leq C_{m}(1+F|F_{zz}|)^{m}

for every
m\geq 0
and some constant
C_{m}
.

Proof.

By Gauss formula,
\sec_{\Sigma_{s}}>\sec_{M}\geq 0,
see
[
CMZ23
, Lemma 2.1]
for example.
Recall that the sectional curvature of
\Sigma_{s}
in the radial direction is
-F_{zz}/F
, and the curvature in the orbital direction is
(1-F_{z}^{2})/F^{2}
. So,
F_{zz}<0
and
|F_{z}|\leq 1.

By (
3.7
), (
3.11
) and Perelman’s derivative estimates in Theorem
2.5
,
|\nabla_{k}(R-\overline{R})|=O(R^{2+\frac{k}{2}}).
The last assertion follows verbatim as the three-dimensional case in
[
Bre20
, Proposition 3.4]
.
∎

We need the following rough estimate.

Lemma 3.18
.

Let
r_{\max}(s):=\sup_{z}F(z,s)
denote the maximum radius of the symmetric spheres in
\Sigma_{s}
.

After possibly shifting the
initial time by
s_{0}
, we have

2s\leq r_{\max}^{2}(s+s_{0})\leq(1+o(1))2s,

as
s\to\infty.

Proof.

Since
F(0,s)=(1+o(1))\sqrt{2s}
as
s\to\infty
, we may assume

r_{\max}(s)\geq F(0,s)\geq\sqrt{s}

Let
z_{0}
be the point that achieves the maximum radius on
\Sigma_{s}
. At
(z_{0},s)
,

F=r_{\max}(s),\quad F_{z}=0,\quad F_{zz}\leq 0.

So at
(z_{0},s)
, the evolution equation of
F
and the fact that
\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}\geq 0
(Lemma
3.15
) imply

\displaystyle-r_{\max}^{\prime}(s)

\displaystyle=-F_{s}\leq-F^{-1}-\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}F^{-1}\leq-r_{\max}^{-1},

which can be integrated to

r_{\max}^{2}(s)\geq 2s-C.

for large
s
. On the other hand, since
F(z_{0},s)\geq\sqrt{s}
, we can use Proposition
2.18
. So for any given
\epsilon
, we can assume
z_{0}
to be an
(\epsilon,2)
-center if
s\geq\underline{s}(\epsilon)
. This implies

F_{zz}\geq-\epsilon F^{-1},

and at
(z_{0},s)
, Lemma
3.12
gives

-F_{s}\geq-\epsilon F^{-1}-F^{-1}-CF^{-3}\geq-2\epsilon F^{-1}-F^{-1}.

Thus, for
s\geq\underline{s}

r^{2}_{\max}(s)\leq 2(1+2\epsilon)s.

By considering
\bar{g}_{s_{0}+s}
instead of
\bar{g}_{s}
in the definition (
1.6
) for sufficiently large
s_{0},
we may assume
C=0
while having the same upper bound. More precisely, we have
r^{2}_{\max}(s+s_{0})\geq 2s
.

∎

We are now ready to show the bound on the first derivative of
F
.

Proposition 3.19
.

There exist a universal number
\underline{a}
and a large
D
(depending only on
M^{4}
) such that the following holds:
If
a\geq\underline{a}
is chosen large enough such that

\frac{r_{\max}(s)}{\sqrt{2s}}\leq 1+\tfrac{1}{100}a^{-2}

holds for
s\geq Da^{3}
, then

F_{z}^{2}(z,s)\leq\psi_{a}\left(\frac{F(z,s)}{\sqrt{2s}}\right)

whenever
s\geq Da^{3}
and
F(z,s)\geq r_{*}a^{-1}\sqrt{2s}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of
[
ABDS22
, Proposition 3.8]
, except we have to deal with extra error terms here. we will outline the details below.

Take
\underline{a}
large enough so that the barrier construction in
2.19
works and
s\geq\underline{a}^{3}
implies all the previous estimates, and fix
a\geq\underline{a}
in the statement. Let
\mathcal{I}
be the set of all
s\geq Da^{3}
such that the conclusion holds. Suppose, on the contrary,
\mathcal{I}
is not all of
[Da^{3},\infty)
.
By Proposition
2.18
and the lower bound for
\psi_{a}
in
2.19
,
\mathcal{I}\supseteq[C(a),\infty)
holds for some constant
C(a)
depending on
a
.
Let
\bar{s}=\sup\left([Da^{3},\infty)\setminus\mathcal{I}\right)
. Then there is a point
(\bar{z},\bar{s})
with

F_{z}^{2}=\psi_{a}\left(\frac{F}{\sqrt{2\bar{s}}}\right),\quad F(\bar{z},\bar{s})\geq r_{*}a^{-1}\sqrt{2\bar{s}}

We can argue as in the proof of
[
ABDS22
, Proposition 3.8]
to see that in a neighborhood of
(\bar{z},\bar{s})
, we may find a smooth function
u
such that

F_{z}^{2}(z,s)=u(F(z,s),s).

The function
u
satisfies the equation

\displaystyle-u_{s}

\displaystyle=uu_{rr}-\tfrac{1}{2}u_{r}^{2}+r^{-2}(1-u)(ru_{r}+2u)

\displaystyle\qquad+2u^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}+r^{-1}u_{r}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}-2u(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}/r)_{r}

We postpone deriving this equation to the end of the proof.

We shall carefully estimate the contribution from
\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}
in the equation for
u
. First,
u\leq 1
(Lemma
3.17
) and
\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}
estimates in Lemma
3.12
imply

u|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}|\leq Cr^{-4},\quad|\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}|\leq Cr^{-2},\quad\sqrt{u}|\partial_{r}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}|\leq Cr^{-3}.

(3.20)

Now, (
3.20
) along with
u\leq 1
,
u_{r}<0
(Lemma
3.17
) and
\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}\geq 0
(Lemma
3.9
) imply

2u^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}\leq C\sqrt{u}r^{-4},\qquad r^{-1}u_{r}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}<0.

Using (
3.20
) again we get

\displaystyle-2u(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}/r)_{r}

\displaystyle=2u\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}r^{-2}-2u\partial_{r}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}/r\leq Cur^{-4}+C\sqrt{u}\,r^{-4}\leq 2C\sqrt{u}\,r^{-4}.

Thus,
u
satisfies

-u_{s}\leq uu_{rr}-\tfrac{1}{2}u_{r}^{2}+r^{-2}(1-u)(ru_{r}+2u)+C_{0}\sqrt{u}\,r^{-4},

(3.21)

for some constant
C_{0}
.

On the other hand, by Corollary
2.21

\Psi_{a}(r,s):=\psi_{a}(r/\sqrt{2s})
satisfies

-\Psi_{a,s}>\Psi_{a}\Psi_{a,rr}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Psi_{a,r}^{2}+r^{-2}(1-\Psi_{a})(r\Psi_{a,r}+2\Psi_{a})+Q,

(3.22)

where

Q\geq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tfrac{1}{8}a^{-4}s^{-1}(r/\sqrt{2s})^{-5},&\text{ for }r/\sqrt{2s}\in[Na^{-1},1+\frac{1}{100}a^{-2}]\\
\tfrac{1}{4}as^{-1},&\text{ for }r/\sqrt{2s}\in[r_{*}a^{-1},Na^{-1}]\end{array}\right.

It is enough to show

Q\geq 2C_{0}\sqrt{u}\,r^{-4},

(3.23)

where
C_{0}
is the same constant as in (
3.21
). If (
3.23
) holds, we can use parabolic maximum principle as in Proposition 3.8 of
[
ABDS22
]
and compare (
3.21
) and (
3.22
) to get a contradiction. This would imply that
\bar{s}
as described above does not exist, hence concluding the proof of the Proposition.

Let
\bar{r}=F(\bar{z},\bar{s})
. We first consider the case
\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}}\in[Na^{-1},1+\frac{1}{100}a^{-2}]
. On one hand, by
s\geq Da^{3}
and Proposition
2.19
,

\displaystyle\bar{r}^{4}\,Q\geq\bar{r}^{4}\,\big(\tfrac{1}{8}\bar{s}^{-1}a^{-4}(\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}})^{-5}\big)=\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{s}a^{-4}(\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}})^{-1}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\bar{D}a^{-1}(\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}})^{-1}

On the other hand, at
(\bar{r},\bar{s})
we have

u(\bar{r},\bar{s})=\psi_{a}(\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}})\leq 2a^{-2}(\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}})^{-2}

So we have
Q\geq 2C_{0}\sqrt{u}r^{-4}
if we choose
D>8C_{0}
. So (
3.23
) holds in this case.

We then consider the case where
\bar{r}/\sqrt{2\bar{s}}\in(r_{*}a^{-1},Na^{-1}].

We have

\tfrac{1}{4}a\bar{s}^{-1}\bar{r}^{4}\geq\tfrac{1}{4}a\bar{s}^{-1}(r_{*}a^{-1}\sqrt{2\bar{s}})^{4}=r_{*}^{4}a^{1-4}\bar{s}\geq r_{*}^{4}D>2C_{0},

if we choose
D
large enough. So,
Q\geq 2C_{0}r^{-4}\geq 2C_{0}\sqrt{u}\,r^{-4}
in this case as well.

Thus, (
3.23
) holds for both cases.
This concludes the proof of the Proposition as discussed above.

Finally, we compute the equation for
u
. After the
s
-dependant change of variable
r=F(z,s)
,
\bar{g}_{s}
can be written as

\tilde{g}=u(r,s)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

and for some
V=v(r,s)\partial_{r}

\partial_{s}\tilde{g}=2\Ric_{\tilde{g}}+2\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}+\mathcal{L}_{V}\tilde{g}

Similar to
[
Bre20
]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{V}\tilde{g}=\left(-u^{-2}u_{r}v+2u^{-1}v_{r}\right)\mathrm{d}r^{2}+2rv\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

\displaystyle\Ric_{\tilde{g}}=-\frac{u_{r}}{ru}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\left(1-u-\tfrac{1}{2}ru_{r}\right)g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

and we can write

\tilde{\mathcal{E}}=:\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}\,g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}

Since

\partial_{s}\tilde{g}=-u^{-2}u_{s}\,\mathrm{d}r^{2}

comparing the spherical component gives

v=\frac{u-1}{r}+\frac{u_{r}}{2}-\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}}{r}

and in turn comparing the radial component gives

\displaystyle-u^{-2}u_{s}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2u_{r}}{ru}+2\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}-u^{-2}u_{r}\left(\frac{u-1}{r}+\frac{u_{r}}{2}-\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}}{r}\right)+2u^{-1}\left(\frac{u-1}{r}+\frac{u_{r}}{2}-\frac{\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}}{r}\right)_{r}

After simplification, we get

\displaystyle-u_{s}

\displaystyle=uu_{rr}-\tfrac{1}{2}u_{r}^{2}+r^{-2}(1-u)(ru_{r}+2u)

\displaystyle\qquad+2u^{2}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}+r^{-1}u_{r}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}-2u(\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}/r)_{r}

∎

Let us now perform parabolic rescaling

G(\xi,\tau)=e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}F\left(e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,e^{-\tau}\right)-\sqrt{2}.

with the following change of variable:

z=e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,\quad s=e^{-\tau}.

Note that

G_{\xi}=F_{z},\quad G_{\xi\xi}=e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F_{zz}.

Accordingly, we define

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm rad}(\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle:=e^{-\tau}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}\left(e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,e^{-\tau}\right),

\displaystyle\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}(\xi,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}\left(e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,e^{-\tau}\right),

so that

\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm rad}d\xi^{2}+\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}\mathrm{d}z^{2}+\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}g_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}\left(e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,e^{-\tau}\right).

By convergence to the cylinder (see also Lemma
3.18
) we have

G(\cdot,\tau)\to 0,

as
\tau\to-\infty,
locally smoothly.

We compute the evolution equation for

.

Proposition 3.24
.

The function
G
satisfies

\displaystyle G_{\tau}

\displaystyle=G_{\xi\xi}-\tfrac{\xi}{2}G_{\xi}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+G)-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}(1+G_{\xi}^{2})+2G_{\xi}\left\{\frac{G_{\xi}}{\sqrt{2}+G}(0,\tau)-\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{G_{\xi}^{2}(x,\tau)}{(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}+G_{\xi}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm rad}(x,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

Then

\displaystyle G_{\tau}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+G)-\tfrac{1}{2}\xi F_{z}-e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F_{s}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+G)-\tfrac{\xi}{2}G_{\xi}+e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F_{zz}-e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F^{-1}(1+F_{z}^{2})+2e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F_{z}\left\{\frac{F_{z}}{F}(0,s)-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{F_{z}^{2}}{F^{2}}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F^{-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm orb}+e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}F_{z}\int_{0}^{z}\,\overline{\mathcal{E}}^{\rm rad}(\zeta,s)\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle=G_{\xi\xi}-\tfrac{\xi}{2}G_{\xi}+\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+G)-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}(1+G_{\xi}^{2})+2G_{\xi}\left\{\frac{G_{\xi}}{\sqrt{2}+G}(0,\tau)-\int_{0}^{\xi}\frac{G_{\xi}^{2}}{(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}}(x,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}+G_{\xi}\int_{0}^{\xi}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm rad}(x,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Definition 3.25
.

For each
\tau

By the definitions above and Proposition

As in

Proof.

Lemma 3.36
.

For

\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\tau}\rho_{\max}(\tau)\geq\tfrac{3}{4}\rho_{\max}-C\rho_{\max}^{2}-C\gamma^{\frac{1}{4}}(\tau).

Proof.

First, note that at

\displaystyle\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\tau}\rho_{\max}(\tau)

4
Ruling out the case when the positive mode dominates

Throughout this section, in addition to Assumptions
(A1)-(A3)
we also assume

(4.1)

for some

has to dominate as

, we may assume that the positive term dominates the other two, i.e.,

\Gamma^{0}(\tau)+\Gamma^{-}(\tau)=o(1)\,\Gamma^{+}(\tau),

as

By Lemma

\Gamma^{+}(\tau-1)\leq e^{-1}\Gamma^{+}(\tau)+C\delta^{\frac{1}{200}}(\tau)\Gamma^{+}(\tau).

Iterating this yields

\Gamma^{+}(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right).

and by definition of

\sup_{\xi}G(\xi,\tau)\leq\rho_{\max}(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{16}}\right)

We try to follow the arguments in

, Section 4]

Definition 4.2

Given

Suppose (

α

We first prove the following Claim.

Claim:

Let

Corollary 5.5
.

For
\tau\leq\bar{\tau},

\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)=-2\alpha^{2}(\tau)+o(A^{2}(\tau)).

Proof.

Directly by the previous Proposition, we have

\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)=-2\alpha^{2}(\tau)+o(A^{2}(\tau))+O(e^{\tau}).

It suffices to show that
e^{\tau}\leq o(1)A^{2}(\tau).

Since we are assuming the neutral mode dominates, for
\tau\leq\bar{\tau}
we have

\Gamma^{+}(\tau)+\Gamma^{-}(\tau)\leq o(1)\Gamma^{0}(\tau)\leq o(1)A^{2}(\tau)\Rightarrow\Gamma(\tau)\leq(1+o(1))\Gamma^{0}(\tau).

For any
\beta\in(0,1),
pick
\epsilon>0
satisfying
1-\epsilon=e^{-\beta}
.
By Lemma
3.39
, for
\tau\leq\bar{\tau},

\Gamma^{0}(\tau)\geq(1-\epsilon)\Gamma^{0}(\tau+1)\geq(1-\epsilon)^{2}\Gamma^{0}(\tau+2)\geq\cdots\geq(1-\epsilon)^{k}\Gamma^{0}(\tau_{*}),

where
\tau+k=\tau_{*}\in[\bar{\tau}-1,\bar{\tau}].
Thus, for
\tau\leq 2\bar{\tau},

\Gamma^{0}(\tau)\geq e^{\beta(\tau-\bar{\tau})}\min_{[\bar{\tau}-1,\bar{\tau}]}\Gamma^{0}\geq c\,e^{\beta\tau},

for some constant
c>0.

By the discussions at the beginning of this section,
\Gamma^{0}(\tau)\leq CA^{2}(\tau),
and thus,

A^{2}(\tau)\geq c\,\Gamma^{0}(\tau)\geq c\,e^{\beta\tau}.

It follows that
e^{\tau}\leq o(1)A^{2}(\tau),
and

\alpha^{\prime}(\tau)=-2\alpha^{2}(\tau)+o(A^{2}(\tau)).

∎

Corollary 5.8

Proof.

The proof is similar to the three-dimensional case, c.f.

6
Asymptotics When the Positive Modes Dominate

We now consider the second case in Proposition
3.41
. More specifically, we assume

\Gamma^{0}(\tau)+\Gamma^{-}(\tau)=o(1)\,\Gamma^{+}(\tau),

as
\tau\to-\infty
.

In addition to
(M^{4},g,f)\in\mathcal{M}
, we assume that
(M,g)
has reflexive symmetry, i.e.,
F(\cdot,s)
is an even function.

Under the assumptions above we give precise asymptotics of our solution in the cylindrical region. The hope is that we would be able to use this asymptotics to eventually to exclude the case of positive mode dominating at all.

Theorem 6.1
.

When the positive modes dominate and
(M,g)
is reflexive symmetric,

F(z,s)=\frac{\log s}{\sqrt{2}s}+o\left(\frac{\log s}{s}\right),

uniformly on compact sets of the form
|z|\leq L\sqrt{s}.

By Lemma
3.39
,

\Gamma^{+}(\tau-1)\leq e^{-1}\Gamma^{+}(\tau)+C\delta^{\frac{1}{200}}(\tau)\Gamma^{+}(\tau).

Iterating this yields

\Gamma^{+}(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right).

Thus, as a rough estimate we get
\Gamma^{+}(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\right)
.

We need the following coarse estimate.

Lemma 6.2
.

For
\tau\leq\bar{\tau},

\rho_{\max}(\tau)\geq G(0,\tau)\geq-Ce^{\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right).

As a consequence,

\delta(\tau)\leq Ce^{\frac{\tau}{2}}+C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right).

Proof.

Since
0
is the reference point, we have that
for any
\epsilon>0,
if
\tau\leq\bar{\tau}(\epsilon),
then

|G(0,\tau)|\leq\epsilon.

By the evolution equation of
G
given in Proposition
3.24
, at
(0,\tau),

\displaystyle G_{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}(\sqrt{2}+G)-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}+(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}G_{\xi}^{2}-(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb},

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}-2+C\rho(\tau),

where we applied Lemma
3.9
and Proposition
3.26
.
By integration, if
\tau\leq 2\bar{\tau}(\epsilon),

\displaystyle e^{-\tau}\left((\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}-2\right)(0,\tau)

\displaystyle\geq-10\epsilon e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\int_{\tau}^{\frac{\tau}{2}}e^{-\sigma}\rho(\sigma)\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\geq-e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)(e^{-\tau}-e^{-\frac{\tau}{2}}).

Then

\displaystyle(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}(0,\tau)

\displaystyle\geq 2-e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right),

\displaystyle G(0,\tau)

\displaystyle\geq-\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2-e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right)}\geq-Ce^{\frac{\tau}{2}}-C\rho\left(\tfrac{\tau}{2}\right).

Thus, the conclusion follows.

∎

It follows by Lemma
6.2
that

\rho_{\max}(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{16}}\right),\quad\rho(\tau)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{16}}\right),\quad\delta(\tau)\leq\rho(\tau/2)\leq O\left(e^{\frac{\tau}{32}}\right).

Let
h_{k}
be the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
corresponding to eigenvalues
1-\frac{k}{2},k\in\mathbb{N}.

Write

\widehat{G}(\xi,\tau)=\sum_{k\geq 0}a_{k}(\tau)h_{k}(\xi),

where

a_{k}(\tau)=\langle\widehat{G}(\cdot,\tau),h_{k}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since we assumed reflexive symmetry,

a_{1}(\tau)=\langle\widehat{G}(\cdot,\tau),h_{1}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\equiv 0,\qquad P_{+}\widehat{G}=a_{0}(\tau).

Finally we conclude the following.

Lemma 6.3
.

a_{0}(\tau)=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau e^{\tau}+o(\tau e^{\tau}).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.15
,

\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb}=(\tfrac{1}{2}+o(1))e^{-\tau}(\sqrt{2}+G)^{2}R^{2}.

Then it is not hard to see that, by Lemma
3.33
,

\displaystyle a_{0}^{\prime}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\langle(\partial_{\tau}-\mathcal{L})\widehat{G}+\mathcal{L}\widehat{G},h_{0}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=a_{0}(\tau)-\langle(\sqrt{2}+G)^{-1}\,\mathcal{E}^{\rm orb},h_{0}\rangle+O(e^{\epsilon\tau})a_{0}(\tau)

\displaystyle=a_{0}(\tau)-\left(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}+o(1)\right)e^{\tau}+O(e^{\epsilon\tau})a_{0}(\tau),

where
\epsilon>0
is a small constant.
By integration,

a_{0}(\tau)=-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau e^{\tau}+o(\tau e^{\tau}).

∎

Theorem
6.1
follows by the standard interpolation inequalities.

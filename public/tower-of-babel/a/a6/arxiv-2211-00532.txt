Robust utility maximisation under proportional transaction costs for càdlàg price processes We thank Huy Chau for his comments on an earlier version of the paper.
Christoph Czichowsky Raphael Huwyler

Abstract
We consider robust utility maximisation in continuous-time financial markets with proportional transaction costs under model uncertainty. For this purpose, we work in the framework of Chau and Rásonyi 2019 , where robustness is achieved by maximising the worst-case expected utility over a possibly uncountable class of models that are all given on the same underlying filtered probability space with incomplete filtration. In this setting, we give sufficient conditions for the existence of an optimal trading strategy extending the result for utility functions on the positive half-line of Chau and Rásonyi 2019 from continuous to general strictly positive càdlàg price processes. This result allows us to provide a positive answer to an open question pointed out in Chau and Rásonyi 2019 , and shows that the embedding into a countable product space is not essential.



Key words:
Utility maximisation, proportional transaction costs, model uncertainty, incomplete filtrations.

1
Introduction

Maximising the expected utility from terminal wealth is a classical problem in Mathematical Finance and Financial Economics. Recently, there has been a lot of interest in robust utility maximisation under model uncertainty, where one maximises the worst-case expected utility over a class of models. The motivation for this model-independent view is that the resulting trading strategies are less sensitive to changes of the underlying model and in this sense more robust to model misspecification.

In this paper, we consider robust utility maximisation under proportional transaction costs in the framework of model uncertainty of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
. Here, the worst-case expected utility over a possibly uncountable class of models, that are all given on the same underlying filtered probability space with incomplete filtration, is maximised. In this setting, we extend the existence result of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
for utility functions on the positive half-line from continuous to general strictly positive càdlàg price processes. This work answers an open question in
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
. Without frictions, a rich amount of examples of discrete-time models for this setting of model uncertainty has been proposed in
Rásonyi and Meireles-Rodrigues 2021
. Our results cover the corresponding models under proportional transaction costs and allow to consider both discrete- and continuous-time models in a unified framework.

As already explained in
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
, most of the literature typically parameterises model uncertainty by a family
\mathbb{P}
of probability measures that are given on some underlying canonical probability space (see, e.g.,
Biagini and Pınar 2017
,
Lin and Riedel 2021
and
Neufeld and Nutz 2018
). This means that the dynamics of the risky asset is given by a fixed process and model uncertainty is described by a family of different distributions of this process. The discrete-time case with unbounded utility functions defined on
(0,\infty)
has been solved in
Blanchard and Carassus 2018
. In the context of transaction costs, the discrete-time case with transaction costs and exponential utility preferences has been treated in
Deng et al. 2020
. For diffusion models, drift uncertainty can be modelled by considering a family of absolutely continuous measures that are dominated by a single measure
P^{\ast}
(see e.g.,
Quenez 2004
and
Schied 2006
), while the case of volatility uncertainty requires an uncountable family of singular measures (see e.g.,
Denis and Kervarec 2013
).

In contrast to the approach above,
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
propose a setup of model uncertainty, where different stock price processes are considered. That is, they suggest to work on a fixed filtered probability space, and to use a family of stochastic processes
S^{\theta}
indexed by
\theta
in a non-empty set
\Theta
to describe model uncertainty. From a mathematical point of view, the main advantage of this setup is that no topological or measurability assumptions are need to be put on the set of parameters
\Theta
representing the different models. In contrast, the parametrisation via a family of measures
\mathbb{P}
incorporates technical issues such as the treatment of null events and filtration completion (see e.g.,
Biagini et al. 2017
,
Bouchard and Nutz 2015
and
Nutz 2016
). However, since typical examples consider an uncountable set of models
\Theta
, one cannot complete the filtration with respect to the null sets arising from any price process
S^{\theta}
and has to work with incomplete filtrations. Working with filtrations under “unusual” conditions, that is, without the usual condition of completeness, then brings its own challenges. However, as pointed out in
Jacod 1979
, we may still pass to the usual conditions from time to time, at the cost of working with
P
-indistinguishable equivalence classes. This additional difficulty is in contrast to
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
, who still assume the filtration to be complete. Besides this fact, we refer to the original paper of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
for a more detailed comparison between these two approaches to model uncertainty.

Within their setting of model uncertainty,
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
observe that, similarly as in the case of a single model in
Guasoni 2002
, it is more suitable to optimise directly over trading strategies, and hence stochastic processes, rather than over terminal wealths given by random variables as in the classical case with only one price process. For this purpose, they exploit that, for continuous price processes, it is sufficient to model trading strategies by càdlàg finite variation processes under proportional transaction costs. The key insight is that càdlàg finite variation processes can be identified with their values along the rational numbers and hence objects are taking values in the countable product of complete metric spaces. However, this approach does not avoid the measurability problem arising from working with uncountably many models simultaneously, if one does not assume the filtration to be complete.

While càdlàg strategies are sufficient to obtain the optimal strategies for continuous price processes under proportional transaction costs, this fact is no longer true for price processes with jumps; see Example 4.1 in
Czichowsky and Schachermayer 2016a
. For càdlàg price processes, it matters, whether one is trading immediately before, just at, or immediately after a jump. Therefore, trading strategies have to be modelled by general predictable finite variation processes that can have left and right discontinuities and can no longer be identified with their values along the rationals. To overcome this difficulty, we use a version of Helly’s Theorem of
Campi and Schachermayer 2006
. The latter allows us to obtain a sequence of trading strategies that converges to a càdlàg modification of a suitable limit process at all time points except for the discontinuity points of that modified limit process. The key is now that we can show that the set, where the convergence can fail, is the same for all models. Hence, it can be exhausted by countably many stopping times. The stopping times can be obtained by either passing to the usual conditions or by using a suitable version of the Debut Theorem for filtrations that are not complete. This insight allows us to achieve the convergence at these points as well by a diagonal sequence argument. Mathematically speaking, while
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
work with the topology of
P
-a.s. convergence along all rationales on the set of càdlàg finite variation processes, we work with the topology of convergence in probability at all
[0,T]
-valued stopping times. Somewhat surprisingly our result shows that the embedding into a countable product metric space indexed by the rationals as used in
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
is not essential.

We assume that our utility functions have a reasonable asymptotic elasticity as in the classical single model framework of
Kramkov and Schachermayer 1999
. This assumption allows us to obtain the optimal trading strategy by directly optimising in the primal problem of maximising expected utility from terminal wealth, and we do not need specific properties of the dual problem. Therefore, we only need the existence of (locally) consistent price systems for
one
level of transaction costs
\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,\lambda)
rather than for all
\lambda^{\prime}\in(0,\lambda)
as in
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
; see Remark 4.5 of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
and Lemma
4.3
below.

In the framework of model uncertainty of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
, a super-replication theorem has been recently established in
Chau et al. 2022
. For model uncertainty with uncountably many probability measures on the same probability space,
Bartl et al. 2021
derived a duality result, in the spirit to the one of
Kramkov and Schachermayer 1999
, for the case of a single model, for utility maximisation from terminal wealth without transaction costs.

2
. Our main result is stated and explained in Section
3
. The proof of the main result is covered in Section
4
. For better readability, some proofs and explanations are deferred to Appendix

2
Formulation of the problem

We consider a financial market consisting of one riskless asset and one risky asset. The riskless asset has constant price
1
. The dynamics of the risky asset is uncertain. For this purpose, let
\Theta
be a non-empty set and consider a family of strictly positive adapted càdlàg processes
(S_{t}^{\theta})_{0\leq t\leq T}
, for each
\theta\in\Theta
, on some underlying filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T},P)
. No further conditions are imposed on
\Theta
. By passing to the right-continuous version, we can assume without loss of generality that the filtration
\mathbb{F}\coloneqq(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
is right continuous. However, the filtration does not necessarily need to be complete. We denote by
\mathcal{F}^{P}
the
P
-completion of
\mathcal{F}
, and accordingly
\mathbb{F}^{P}
the usual augmentation of
\mathbb{F}
by adjoining all the
P
-negligible sets to
\mathcal{F}_{0}
. In this framework, model uncertainty is then incorporated by considering simultaneously all models
(S_{t}^{\theta})_{0\leq t\leq T}
, for
\theta\in\Theta
, as possible evolutions for the price process of the risky asset, so that the set
\Theta
provides the parametrisation of model uncertainty.

To illustrate why this particular setting of model uncertainty is useful,
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
provide some interesting examples of well-known models in finance literature (see Examples 2.2, 2.4 and 2.5 therein). For instance, in Example 2.2, they describe a robust version of the Black-Scholes model, where the parametrisation of model uncertainty is a subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which describes potential values for the drift and volatility term of the geometric Brownian motion that models the price process. To see why such a parametrisation of robustness is useful and interesting in our extended setup that includes càdlàg price processes, we provide some more examples.

Example 2.1
.

In the robust Merton jump diffusion model (cf.,
Merton 1976
), the price of the risky asset satisfies the stochastic differential equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}S_{t}^{(\mu,\sigma,\nu)}

\displaystyle=S_{t}^{(\mu,\sigma,\nu)}((\mu-\nu k)\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}W_{t}+\mathrm{d}X_{t}),\qquad S_{0}^{(\mu,\sigma,\nu)}=s_{0}>0,

where
\mu
and
\sigma
are constants,
W
is a standard Brownian motion, and
X
is an independent compound Poisson process with intensity
\nu>0
, whose jump size distribution has expected value
k
. In particular, the process
X=(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

X_{t}\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}}Y_{i},\qquad t\geq 0,

(2.1)

where
N=(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Poisson process with intensity
\nu
, the random variables
Y_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, are independent and identically distributed with mean
\mathrm{E}[Y_{i}]=k
, and which are independent of the process
N
. The uncertainty is then modelled by

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle=\big\{\theta=(\mu,\sigma,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\colon\underline{\mu}\leq\mu\leq\overline{\mu},\,\underline{\sigma}\leq\sigma\leq\overline{\sigma},\,\underline{\nu}\leq\nu\leq\overline{\nu}\big\},

where
\underline{\mu}\leq\overline{\mu}
,
0<\underline{\sigma}\leq\overline{\sigma}
and
0<\underline{\nu}\leq\overline{\nu}
are given constants. The classical, non-robust Merton jump diffusion model corresponds to the case where
\underline{\mu}=\overline{\mu}
,
\underline{\sigma}=\overline{\sigma}
and
\underline{\nu}=\overline{\nu}
. In fact, if we set
\nu=0
, we obtain the non-robust Black-Scholes model as described in Example 2.2 in
[
8
]
.

On the one hand, Example
2.1
shows the importance of considering càdlàg price processes, as very general models may include jump terms to describe the “abnormal” variations in the price, as
Merton 1976
describes it. These variations are due to the arrival of important new information that has more than a marginal effect on the price. On the other hand, we may see from Example
2.1
how this particular way of parameterising model uncertainty can be useful in statistical estimation and model calibration, and therefore relevant for practical applications.

Example 2.2
.

The compound Poisson process can be used to describe more abstract models with jumps. In particular, we may include the parameters of the jump size distribution in the parametrisation of model uncertainty. For instance, we can define the price process as

\displaystyle\log(S_{t}^{(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)}/S_{0}^{(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)})

\displaystyle=X_{t}^{1,(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)}-X_{t}^{2,(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N},

where
X^{1,(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)}
and
X^{2,(\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k},\nu)}
are two independent copies of the compound Poisson process satisfying equation (
2.1
) (see Section 3 in
[
18
]
for details), and where the random variables
Y_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, are distributed with specific parameters
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{k}
, and where the Poisson process
N
has intensity
\nu>0
. For instance, we could take
Y_{i}=|Z_{i}|
, where
Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
, so that
\theta_{1}=\sigma^{2}
. Another example is to take
Y_{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\alpha)
, so that
\theta_{1}=\alpha
.

Example 2.3
.

The previous example is a specific case of a larger class of models (see
Geman et al. 2001
), where the price process is a time-changed Brownian motion. The underlying idea uses the result that every semimartingale can be written as a time-changed Brownian motion on some adequately defined probability space (see, e.g.,
Monroe 1978
). If the time change was continuous, then under fairly general conditions it could be represented as an Itô process. Since time is increasing, this continuity assumption would force us to set the volatility term of the time change process to zero, which means that the time change is locally deterministic. If we relate the time change to the information flow embedded in the price, where there is local uncertainty about these flows, we must assume that the time change is a purely discontinuous process. Hence, the price process itself will be purely discontinuous too.

For instance, in Example
2.2
, if we choose

\displaystyle\log(S_{t}^{(\sigma,\nu)}/S_{0}^{(\sigma,\nu)})

\displaystyle=X_{t}^{1,(\sigma,\nu)}-X_{t}^{2,(\sigma,\nu)},\qquad X_{t}^{j,(\sigma,\nu)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{t}^{j}}Y_{i}^{j},\quad j\in\{1,2\},

where
N^{1}
and
N^{2}
are independent Poisson processes with intensity
\nu>0
, and where
Y_{i}^{j}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\{1,2\}
, are independent random variables with a half-normal distribution (i.e.,
Y_{i}^{j}=|Z_{i}^{j}|
with
Z_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2})
), the time change is given by
T(t)=N_{t}^{1}+N_{t}^{2}
, so that

\displaystyle\log(S_{t}^{(\sigma,\nu)}/S_{0}^{(\sigma,\nu)})

\displaystyle=\sigma W_{T(t)}=\sigma W_{N_{t}^{1}+N_{t}^{2}},\qquad t\geq 0,

where
W=(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a standard Brownian motion. For details of this derivation, we refer to Section 3.2 in
[
18
]
. In such a model, the parametrisation of model uncertainty is of the form
\Theta=\{\theta=(\sigma,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\colon 0<\underline{\sigma}\leq\sigma\leq\overline{\sigma},\,0<\underline{\nu}\leq\nu\leq\overline{\nu}\}
.

Another idea is to use the same price structure as above, where the log-price is the difference of two independent pure jump processes, but instead of using the compound Poisson process, we may use a Gamma process. In particular, we let

\displaystyle\log(S_{t}^{(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})}/S_{0}^{(\nu_{1},\mu_{1},\nu_{2},\mu_{2})})

\displaystyle=U_{t}^{1,(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})}-U_{t}^{2,(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})},\quad U_{t}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}=\frac{\nu_{j}}{\mu_{j}}\gamma_{j}\bigg(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}}{\nu_{j}}t\bigg),\>j\in\{1,2\},

where
\gamma_{1}

\gamma_{2}
are two independent standard Gamma processes, so that
\gamma_{j}(t)\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(t,1)
for

U_{t}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(\frac{\mu_{j}^{2}}{\nu_{j}}t,\frac{\mu_{j}}{\nu_{j}})

U_{t+h}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}-U_{t}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}

\displaystyle f_{U_{t+h}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}-U_{t}^{j,(\mu_{j},\nu_{j})}}(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{\mu_{1}}{\nu_{1}}\bigg)^{\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}h}{\nu_{1}}}\frac{x^{\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}h}{\nu_{1}}-1}\exp\big(-\frac{\mu_{1}}{\nu_{1}}x\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{\mu_{1}^{2}h}{\nu_{1}}\big)},\qquad x>0,

so that

\alpha\gamma_{3}(t)+\beta W_{\gamma_{3}(t)}

\alpha

\beta

\gamma_{3}(t)

\gamma_{1}(t)

\gamma_{2}(t)

[

[
25

\displaystyle\Theta

\displaystyle=\big\{\theta=(\mu_{1},\nu_{1},\mu_{2},\nu_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\colon 0<\underline{\mu_{j}}\leq\mu_{j}\leq\overline{\mu_{j}},\,0<\underline{\nu_{j}}\leq\nu_{j}\leq\overline{\nu_{j}},\,j\in\{1,2\}\big\}.

where equality holds true in (
2.2
). This way we only have to specify one of the holdings, e.g., the number of stocks
H^{1}
. For a fixed model
\theta\in\Theta
and
x>0
, we thus define the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(x)\coloneqq\big\{H^{1}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F},P)\colon(H^{0},H^{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{\theta}(x),\>\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0}=-S_{t}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}+(1-\lambda)S_{t}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}\big\}.

This definition is in line with the set of admissible trading strategies considered in the case of one single model, that is,
\Theta=\{\theta\}
, as discussed in
[
10
]
. We will also refer to this situation as the
non-robust
case. Moreover, by letting
\mathcal{A}(x)\coloneqq\bigcap_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathcal{A}^{\theta}(x)
, we obtain the analogue of the set of model-independent admissible trading strategies given in
[
8
]
.

Note that
H^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
does no longer depend on
\theta
. However, the holdings in the bond
H^{0}
still depend on
\theta
. We will use the notation
H^{0,\theta}
to indicate this dependence. In particular, we define
H_{t}^{0,\theta}\coloneqq x+H_{t}^{0,\theta,\uparrow}-H_{t}^{0,\theta,\downarrow}
with

\displaystyle H_{t}^{0,\theta,\downarrow}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow},\quad\text{and}\quad H_{t}^{0,\theta,\uparrow}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}(1-\lambda)S_{u}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\downarrow}.

(2.7)

Moreover, we write
V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})
to indicate that
H^{0}
in (
2.6
) is defined via (
2.7
). We also notice that the mapping
H^{1}\mapsto V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})
is concave. To see this statement, we first note that
x^{+}=\max(x,0)
and
x^{-}=\max(-x,0)
are convex functions. By definition (see, e.g., equation (5.1) in Section X.5 in
[
17
]
) we have

\displaystyle H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(H_{tk/n}-H_{t(k-1)/n})^{-},

which implies that
(\alpha H_{t}^{1}+(1-\alpha)\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1})^{\downarrow}\leq\alpha H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}+(1-\alpha)\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,\downarrow}
for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
. Now, observe that

\displaystyle V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})=-\lambda\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\downarrow}-\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1}+H_{t}^{1}S_{t}^{\theta}-(H_{t}^{1})^{+}\lambda S_{t}^{\theta},

which together with the fact that
S^{\theta}
is strictly positive implies that
V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})
is concave in
H^{1}
.

Now, we have everything in place to formulate the optimisation problem. For this cause, we consider an investor whose preferences are modelled by a standard utility function
2
2

2

That is a strictly concave, non-decreasing and continuously differentiable function satisfying the Inada conditions
U^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{x\to 0}U^{\prime}(x)=\infty
and
U^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{x\to\infty}U^{\prime}(x)=0
.

U\colon(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
. For a given initial capital
x>0
, the investor wants to maximise the expected utility of terminal wealth with respect to the worst-case scenario of all possible models. This means that the investor wants to find the optimal strategy
\widehat{H}^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
that maximises
\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,H^{1}\big)\big)\big]
. The value function of this
primal optimisation problem
is denoted by

u(x)\coloneqq\sup_{H^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)}\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,H^{1}\big)\big)\big].

(2.8)

In the sequel, we answer the question whether, and under which assumptions, the robust primal problem (
2.8
) admits a solution.

3
Main result

In the frictionless case, the Fundamental Theorem of Asset Pricing states that the no-arbitrage condition is equivalent to the property that the price process admits an equivalent local martingale measure (see
Delbaen and Schachermayer 1994
). In the setting of transaction costs, the notion of
consistent price systems
plays a role analogous to the notion of equivalent martingale measures in the frictionless case.

Definition 3.1
.

Fix
0<\lambda<1
and
\theta\in\Theta
. A strictly positive adapted càdlàg process
S^{\theta}
satisfies the condition
(\mathrm{CPS}^{\lambda})
of having a
\lambda
-consistent price system
, if there exists a pair of processes
Z^{\theta}=(Z_{t}^{0,\theta},Z_{t}^{1,\theta})_{0\leq t\leq T}
, consisting of a density process
Z^{0,\theta}=(Z_{t}^{0,\theta})_{0\leq t\leq T}
of an equivalent local martingale measure
Q^{\theta}\approx P
for a price process
\widetilde{S}^{\theta}=(\widetilde{S}^{\theta}_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
evolving in the bid-ask spread
[(1-\lambda)S^{\theta},S^{\theta}]
, and
Z^{1,\theta}=Z^{0,\theta}\widetilde{S}^{\theta}
. In particular,
\widetilde{S}^{\theta}
satisfies

\displaystyle(1-\lambda)S_{t}^{\theta}\leq\widetilde{S}_{t}^{\theta}\leq S_{t}^{\theta},\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

(3.1)

We further say that
S^{\theta}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CPS}^{\lambda})

locally
, if there exists a strictly positive stochastic process
Z^{\theta}=(Z^{0,\theta},Z^{1,\theta})
and a localising sequence
(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of stopping times, such that
(Z^{\theta})^{\tau_{n}}
is a
\lambda
-consistent prices system for the stopped process
(\widetilde{S}^{\theta})^{\tau_{n}}
for each
n\geq 0
. We denote the space of all such processes by
\mathcal{Z}^{\theta}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\theta}
, respectively.

We impose the existence of consistent price systems for every model
\theta\in\Theta
.

Assumption 3.2
.

For each
\theta\in\Theta
and for some
0<\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
, the price process
S^{\theta}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CPS}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
locally.

In the non-robust setting, i.e., for a fixed model
\theta\in\Theta
and
x>0
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{\theta}(x)\coloneqq\big\{g\in L_{+}^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\colon g\leq V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H),\text{ for some }H\in\mathcal{H}^{\theta}(x)\big\}.

This is the set of terminal positions
g
that one can superreplicate with an admissible trading strategy
H
and initial endowment
x
. Since we are not interested in an analysis of the dual problem on the level of stochastic processes, we can define the dual variables simply on the level of random variables by setting

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)\coloneqq\big\{h\in L_{+}^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)\colon\mathrm{E}[gh]\leq y\text{ for all $g\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta}(1)$}\big\},\quad\text{for all $y>0.$}

(3.2)

Note that
\{yZ^{0}_{T}\colon(Z^{0},Z^{1})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\theta}\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)
so that
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, in the non-robust case it is possible to identify a suitable set of stochastic processes as dual variables, so-called
optional strong supermartingale deflators
, that yields
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)
as terminal values of such processes. We refer to
Czichowsky and Schachermayer 2016a
for the duality theory for portfolio optimisation under transaction costs on the level of stochastic processes in the non-robust setup.

Using the sets
\mathcal{C}^{\theta}(x)
and
\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)
allows us to define the non-robust value functions. In particular, the primal and dual value functions for the
\theta
-model are given by

\displaystyle u^{\theta}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{f\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta}(x)}\mathrm{E}[U(f)],\quad\text{and}\quad j^{\theta}(y)\coloneqq\inf_{h\in\mathcal{D}^{\theta}(y)}\mathrm{E}[J(h)],

where
J(y)\coloneqq\sup_{x>0}(U(x)-xy)
,
y>0
, is the Legendre transform of
-U(-x)
. For our purpose, we need the following assumption.

Assumption 3.3
.

The asymptotic elasticity of
U
is strictly less than one, that is,

\displaystyle\mathrm{AE}(U)\coloneqq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{xU^{\prime}(x)}{U(x)}<1,

and for each model
\theta\in\Theta
, the primal value function
u^{\theta}(x)
is finite for
some

x>0
and hence
all

x>0
by concavity of
u^{\theta}(x)
.

In the original paper of
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
, instead of Assumption
3.3
, they use the assumption that
j^{\theta}(y)
,
y>0
, is finite for all models
\theta\in\Theta
(see Assumption 3.5 in
[
8
]
). However, note that Assumption
3.2
and Assumption
3.3
imply Assumption 3.5 in
[
8
]
(see Theorem 2 and the subsequent Note 2 in

together with Theorem 2.2 in
[

and Theorem 3.2 in

). Moreover, Assumption

is standard and Assumption

Remark 3.4
.

Under Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
, we obtain by Theorem 3.2 in
[

that

\displaystyle(u^{\theta})^{\prime}(\infty)=\lim_{x\to\infty}(u^{\theta})^{\prime}(x)=0,\quad\forall\theta\in\Theta,

which may be restated as

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{u^{\theta}(x)}{x}=0,\quad\forall\theta\in\Theta.

As explained below Theorem 3.2 of
Czichowsky and Schachermayer 2016a
, the condition that

.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper. It extends Theorem 3.6 in
[
8
]
for utility functions defined on the positive half-line from continuous price processes to the general price processes with jumps.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
x>0
. Under Assumptions
3.2
and

\displaystyle u(x)=\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1}\big)\big)\big].

When

Proof of Theorem

.

Let
(H^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}(x)
be a maximising sequence, i.e.,

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,H^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\nearrow u(x),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

By Proposition
4.4
there is a sequence
(\widetilde{H}^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathrm{conv}(H^{1,n},H^{1,n+1},\dots)
and
\widehat{H}^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, such that, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, we have that
\widetilde{H}^{1,n}_{t}
converges for every

to
\widehat{H}^{1}_{t}
. Since the utility function is concave and increasing, and because

is concave, we obtain that
(\widetilde{H}^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is also a maximising sequence, because

\displaystyle\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\geq\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,H^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\to u(x),\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We claim that
\widehat{H}^{1}
is an optimal solution to (
2.8
). For this purpose, we denote by
U^{+}
and
U^{-}
the positive and negative parts of the function
U
. From Fatou’s lemma, we deduce that

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\big[U^{-}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\geq\mathrm{E}\big[U^{-}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1}\big)\big)\big],

for each model
\theta\in\Theta
. The optimality of
\widehat{H}
will follow if we show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]=\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1}\big)\big)\big],

(4.4)

for each model
\theta\in\Theta
. If
U(\infty)\leq 0
, then there is nothing to prove. So we assume that
U(\infty)>0
.

We claim that the sequence
(U^{+}(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n})))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly integrable for each
\theta\in\Theta
. This assertion is equivalent to the validity of (
4.4
). Suppose by contradiction that the sequence is not uniformly integrable for some
\theta
. Then, using the characterization of uniform integrability given in
[
17
]
, Theorem VI.16, and passing if necessary to a subsequence still denoted by
(\widetilde{H}^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we can find a constant
\alpha>0
and disjoint sets
A_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{A_{n}}\big]\geq\alpha\quad\text{for }n\geq 1.

We define the sequence of random variables
(h^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by

\displaystyle h^{n}\coloneqq x_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,k}\big)\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}},

where
x_{0}=\inf\big\{x>0\colon U(x)\geq 0\big\}
. It follows immediately that

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[U(h^{n})]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,k}\big)\big)\mathbbm{1}_{A_{k}}\big]\geq n\alpha.

On the other hand, for any

we have by Proposition
4.2

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[h^{n}f]\leq x_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathrm{E}\big[V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,k}\big)f\big]\leq x_{0}+nx.

Hence, by Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{u^{\theta}(x)}{x}\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{E}[U(h^{n})]}{x_{0}+nx}\geq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{n\alpha}{x_{0}+nx}=\frac{\alpha}{x}>0.

By Remark
3.4
, this strict inequality is a contradiction to our Assumptions
3.2
and
3.3
. As a result,

indeed is a uniformly integrable sequence for each
\theta\in\Theta
.

Since for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, we have that
\widetilde{H}^{1,n}_{t}\to\widehat{H}^{1}_{t}
for every
t\in[0,T]
, we get that
V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n})\to V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1})
almost surely for each
\theta\in\Theta
by Lemma
4.5
. Therefore, Fatou’s lemma and uniform integrability imply

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\Big(\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\Big(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathrm{E}\big[U\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1}\big)\big)\big],

which proves that
\widehat{H}^{1}
is an optimal solution to (
2.8
).

In the case where
U
is bounded from above, we use Fatou’s lemma to get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widetilde{H}^{1,n}\big)\big)\big]\leq\mathrm{E}\big[U^{+}\big(V_{T}^{\mathrm{liq}}\big(\theta,\widehat{H}^{1}\big)\big)\big]

instead of (

\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
so that

satisfies
(\mathrm{CPS}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
locally for some
0<\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
is enough to obtain the optimal strategy and to conclude the proof.

We continue with a result that was originally proven in

in the setting of

Proposition A.1
.

Let
x>0
. For some
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
and for some
0<\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
, assume that
S^{\theta^{\prime}}
satisfies
(\mathrm{CPS}^{\lambda^{\prime}})
locally. Then, for any strategy
H^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
with canonical decomposition
H^{1}=H^{1,\uparrow}-H^{1,\downarrow}
, the elements
H_{T}^{1,\uparrow}
and
H_{T}^{1,\downarrow}
as well as their convex combinations are bounded in
L^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
.

Proof.

Fix
0<\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
. For some
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
there is by assumption a probability measure
Q^{\theta^{\prime}}\approx P
and a local
Q^{\theta^{\prime}}
-martingale
\widetilde{S}^{\theta^{\prime}}=(\widetilde{S}_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}})_{0\leq t\leq T}
satisfying (
3.1
). By stopping, we may assume that
\widetilde{S}^{\theta^{\prime}}
is a true martingale.

We consider a strategy
H^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
, for some
x>0
. Without loss of generality we assume that
H_{T}^{1}=0
, i.e., that the position is liquidated at time
T
. For each
\theta\in\Theta
, using (
2.7
), we obtain the holdings of the bond
H_{t}^{0,\theta}=x+H_{t}^{0,\theta,\uparrow}-H_{t}^{0,\theta,\downarrow}
via
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta,\uparrow}=(1-\lambda)S_{t}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}
and
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta,\downarrow}=S_{t}^{\theta}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}
with
H_{0}^{0,\theta,\uparrow}=H_{0}^{0,\theta,\downarrow}=0
. Now, working with
\theta^{\prime}
as introduced before, we first show that

\mathrm{E}_{Q^{\theta^{\prime}}}\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}\big]\leq\frac{x}{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}.

(A.1)

For this purpose, define the process
H^{\prime}=((H^{0,\theta^{\prime}})^{\prime},(H^{1})^{\prime})
by

\displaystyle H^{\prime}_{t}=((H^{0,\theta^{\prime}})^{\prime}_{t},(H^{1})^{\prime}_{t})=\Big(H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}{1-\lambda}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow},H_{t}^{1}\Big),\qquad 0\leq t\leq T.

This process is a self-financing trading strategy under transaction costs
\lambda^{\prime}
: indeed, whenever we have
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}>0
such that
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}=\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
, the agent sells some units of stock and receives
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}=(1-\lambda)S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}
(resp.,
(1-\lambda^{\prime})S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\downarrow}=\frac{1-\lambda^{\prime}}{1-\lambda}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
) many bonds under transaction costs
\lambda
(resp.,
\lambda^{\prime}
). The difference between these two terms is
\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}{1-\lambda}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
; this difference is the amount by which the
\lambda^{\prime}
-agent does better than the
\lambda
-agent. It is also clear that
((H^{0,\theta^{\prime}})^{\prime},(H^{1})^{\prime})
under transaction costs
\lambda^{\prime}
still is an
x
-admissible strategy for the model
\theta^{\prime}
, i.e.,
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}^{\theta^{\prime}}(x)
.

By Proposition
4.2
, the process
\widetilde{V}(\theta^{\prime},H^{\prime})=(\widetilde{V}_{t}(\theta^{\prime},H^{\prime}))_{0\leq t\leq T}
defined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{t}(\theta^{\prime},H^{\prime})=(H^{0,\theta^{\prime}})^{\prime}_{t}+(H^{1})^{\prime}_{t}\widetilde{S}_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}=H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}{1-\lambda}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}+H_{t}^{1}\widetilde{S}_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}

is an optional strong
Q^{\theta^{\prime}}
-supermartingale. We thus get

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{Q^{\theta^{\prime}}}\big[\widetilde{V}_{T}\big(\theta^{\prime},H^{\prime}\big)\big]=\mathrm{E}_{Q^{\theta^{\prime}}}\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}+H_{T}^{1}\widetilde{S}_{T}^{\theta^{\prime}}\big]+\frac{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}{1-\lambda}\mathrm{E}_{Q^{\theta^{\prime}}}\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}\big]\leq x.

By admissibility of
H^{1}
, we have
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}+H_{T}^{1}\widetilde{S}_{T}^{\theta^{\prime}}\geq 0
, and thus we have shown (
A.1
).

To obtain control on
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}
too, we note that
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\geq-x
, since
H_{T}^{1}=0
. So we have
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\leq x+H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
. Therefore, we obtain the following estimate for the total variation
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}+H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}
of
H^{0,\theta^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{Q^{\theta^{\prime}}}\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}+H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\big]\leq x\Big(1+\frac{2}{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}\Big).

(A.2)

Now we transfer the
L^{1}(Q^{\theta^{\prime}})
-estimate in (
A.2
) to an
L^{0}(P)
-estimate. For
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{\theta^{\prime}}>0
, so that for
A\in\mathcal{F}
with
Q^{\theta^{\prime}}[A]<\delta_{\theta^{\prime}}
, we have
P[A]<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
. Letting
C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{x}{\delta_{\theta^{\prime}}}\big(1+\frac{2}{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}\big)
and applying Markov’s inequality to (
A.2
), we get

\displaystyle P\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}+H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\big]<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

(A.3)

which is the desired
L^{0}(P)
-estimate. At this point we remark that (
A.1
) implies that the convex hull of the functions
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
is bounded in
L^{1}(Q^{\theta^{\prime}})
and (
A.2
) yields the same for
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}
. So by the above reasoning we obtain that also the convex combinations of
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}
and
H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}
remain bounded in
L^{0}(P)
.

As before, it follows from (
2.7
) that
\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}=S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}
, which can be rewritten as

\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}=\frac{\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}}{S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}}.

(A.4)

By assumption, the trajectories of
S^{\theta^{\prime}}
are strictly positive. In fact, we even have for almost all trajectories
(S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\omega))_{0\leq t\leq T}
, that
\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}(\omega)
is strictly positive. Indeed,
\widetilde{S}^{\theta^{\prime}}
being a
Q^{\theta^{\prime}}
-martingale with
\widetilde{S}_{T}^{\theta^{\prime}}>0
almost surely satisfies that

is

-a.s. and therefore

-a.s. strictly positive. In particular, for
\varepsilon>0
we may find
\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}>0
such that

(A.5)

Taking
\eta_{\theta^{\prime}}\coloneqq\min(\delta_{\theta^{\prime}},\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}})
and letting
C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\coloneqq\frac{x}{\eta_{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}}\big(1+\frac{2}{\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}}\big)
, we obtain from (
A.3
), (
A.4
) and (
A.5
) that

P\Big[H_{T}^{1,\uparrow}\geq C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big]\leq P\Big[\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}<\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}\Big]+P\big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\big]<\varepsilon.

(A.6)

The first inequality in (
A.6
) follows from

\displaystyle P\Big[H_{T}^{1,\uparrow}\geq C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq P\Big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq\Big(\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\Big)C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq P\Big[\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}<\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}\Big]+P\Big[\Big\{H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq\Big(\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\Big)C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big\}\cap\Big\{\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\geq\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}\Big\}\Big],

which uses (
A.4
) (note that
H_{0}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}=H_{0}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}=0
by definition) together with

\displaystyle P\Big[\Big\{H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq\Big(\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\Big)C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big\}\cap\Big\{\inf_{0\leq t\leq T}S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\geq\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}\Big\}\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq P\Big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\Big]\leq P\Big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\Big]\leq P\Big[H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\uparrow}+H_{T}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}\geq C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}\Big],

where we use that
\eta_{\theta^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\delta_{\theta^{\prime}}\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}
, which implies
\delta^{\prime}_{\theta^{\prime}}C^{1,\theta^{\prime}}\geq C^{0,\theta^{\prime}}
. The second inequality in (
A.6
) then follows from (
A.3
) and (
A.5
).

To control the term

, we observe that
H_{T}^{1,\uparrow}-H_{T}^{1,\downarrow}=H_{T}^{1}=0
. Therefore, we may use the estimate (
A.6
) of

to also control
H_{T}^{1,\downarrow}

A.4
) also holds for convex combinations of

\widehat{H}^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
and

we have

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}((1-\alpha)H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}+\alpha\widehat{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow})

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha)S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}+\alpha S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\widehat{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}=(1-\alpha)\mathrm{d}H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}+\alpha\mathrm{d}\widehat{H}_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow},

so that dividing by

yields

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}((1-\alpha)H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}+\alpha\widehat{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}((1-\alpha)H_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow}+\alpha\widehat{H}_{t}^{0,\theta^{\prime},\downarrow})}{S_{t}^{\theta^{\prime}}}.

Since (

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Let
\theta\in\Theta
and fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Also fix
\omega\in\Omega
so that
S^{\theta}(\omega)
has càdlàg trajectories and such that
H_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\to H_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Since the function
S^{\theta}_{\cdot}(\omega):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is càdlàg, there can only be finitely many times
0\leq\tau_{1}<\ldots<\tau_{k}\leq T
such that
\big|\Delta S^{\theta}_{\tau_{i}}(\omega)\big|\geq\varepsilon
. Indeed, if there were infinitely many time points with jumps of size larger than
\varepsilon
, the jump times would have a cluster point in the compact set
[0,T]
, leading to a contradiction to the existence of right or left limits of
S^{\theta}_{t}(\omega)
at every
t\in[0,T]
.

Therefore, setting

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\coloneqq S_{t}^{\theta}(\omega)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta S_{\tau_{i}}^{\theta}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau_{i},T]}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

gives a càdlàg function with
\big|\Delta S^{\theta,\varepsilon}_{t}(\omega)\big|\leq\varepsilon
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then, there are finitely many times
\sigma_{0}=0<\sigma_{1}<\ldots<\sigma_{m-1}<T
such that
\big|S_{\sigma_{i+1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{\sigma_{i}}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\big|>\varepsilon
, because
S^{\theta,\varepsilon}_{\cdot}(\omega):[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
is càdlàg. Indeed, if there were infinitely many such time points
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in I}
for some infinite index set
I
, the times
(\sigma_{i})_{i\in I}
would have a cluster point in the compact set
[0,T]
leading to a contradiction to the existence of right or left limits of
S^{\theta,\varepsilon}_{t}(\omega)
at every
t\in[0,T]
. Because the jumps of
S^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)
are bounded by
\varepsilon
, we obtain
\big|S_{t}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{\sigma_{i}}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\big|\leq 2\varepsilon
for all
t\in[\sigma_{i},\sigma_{i+1}]
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
with
\sigma_{m}:=T
. Therefore, the step functions
S^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)
given by

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)

\displaystyle\coloneqq S_{0}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\sigma_{i-1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{(\sigma_{i-1},\sigma_{i}]}(t),\quad t\in[0,T],

satisfy
\big|S_{t}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{t}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\big|\leq 2\varepsilon
for all

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\big(S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\big)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow,n}(\omega)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon H_{T}^{1,\uparrow,n}(\omega),

and

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\big(S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\big)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon H_{T}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega).

Moreover, because of our definition of the stochastic integral at left jumps of the integrator given below the self-financing condition (
2.2
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\sigma_{i-1}+\wedge t}^{\sigma_{i}\wedge t}S_{\sigma_{i-1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\sigma_{i-1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\Delta_{+}H_{\sigma_{i-1}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\sigma_{i-1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)(H_{\sigma_{i}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-H_{\sigma_{i-1}+\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)+\Delta_{+}H_{\sigma_{i-1}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}S_{\sigma_{i-1}}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)(H_{\sigma_{i}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-H_{\sigma_{i-1}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)),

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\to\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon,m}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega),\quad n\to\infty,

as

for all

.
In particular, for

large enough, we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\int_{0}^{t}S_{u}^{\theta,\varepsilon}(\omega)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\bigg|

On the other hand, the finite sum
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta S_{\tau_{i}}^{\theta}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau_{i},T]}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta S_{\tau_{i}}^{\theta}(\omega)\big(H_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-H_{\tau_{i}\wedge t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\big),

(A.7)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta S_{\tau_{i}}^{\theta}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{[\tau_{i},T]}(u)\bigg)\mathrm{d}H_{u}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)

Since

In order to prove that

. This consequence follows from our definition of the stochastic integral and the fact that
H^{1,n,\uparrow}
as well as
H^{1,n,\downarrow}
are elements of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{V}^{+}(\mathbb{F},P)
(see also Remark 1.31 in
[
20
]
). The same argument then holds true for
H^{0,\theta,\uparrow}
and
H^{0,\theta,\downarrow}
. It thus remains to check that
V^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})
satisfies the admissibility condition (
2.6
) for all
\theta\in\Theta
. By assumption, the processes
(H^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
are
x
-admissible for all
\theta\in\Theta
. Hence, by (
4.1
) and (
4.3
), we get for every
t\in[0,T]
and for each
\theta\in\Theta
that
V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1,n})\to V_{t}^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1})
almost surely, by the continuity of the liquidation function (
2.6
) with respect to
(H^{0,\theta}_{t},H^{1}_{t})
, so that admissibility condition (
2.6
) passes to the limit
H^{1}
. We thus have
H^{1}\in\mathcal{A}(x)
and this concludes the proof.
∎

To prove Proposition
4.4
, we also use the following well-known variant of Komlós’ theorem (see
[
12
]
, Lemma A1.1).

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued, random variables on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. There is a sequence
g_{n}\in\mathrm{conv}(f_{n},f_{n+1},\dots)
of convex combinations that converges almost surely to a
[0,\infty]
-valued function
g
. If
\mathrm{conv}(f_{n},f_{n+1},\dots)
is bounded in
L^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
, then
g
is finite almost surely.

We further need the next simple fact about the measurability of limits of measurable functions (see, e.g., Lemma 3.5 in
[
37
]
).

Lemma A.3
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of measurable functions on a measure space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
. Then,
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
are
[-\infty,\infty]
-valued measurable functions and the set
F:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\text{$f_{n}(\omega)$ converges to a limit in $\mathbb{R}$}\right\}
is
\mathcal{F}
-measurable and given by

\displaystyle F={}

\displaystyle\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(\omega)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

Moreover, if
\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
and
\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{n}
are measurable with respect to a sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
, then
F\in\mathcal{G}
.

We are now ready to prove Proposition
4.4
. Here, the main difference to the initial paper by
Chau and Rásonyi 2019
(see Lemma A.1) is the treatment of jump times. For this cause, we revisit the proof of
[
7
]
, Proposition 3.4
3
3

3

Note that, since we are already considering the liquidation value in (
2.8
), we do not need to assume that
H^{1}_{T}=0
. Therefore, we also do not need to assume that
\mathcal{F}_{T}=\mathcal{F}_{T-}
and

7

.
. Our key insight here is that we can show that the set, where the convergence can fail, is the same for all models. It can again be exhausted by countably many stopping times. However, since the filtration
\mathbb{F}
is not assumed to be complete, the treatment of jump times needs special care. In particular, we need to pass from
\mathbb{F}

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Fix
x>0
and let
(H^{1,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}(x)
be a sequence of admissible, self-financing strategies. In particular,
H^{1,n}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{F},P)
is a finite variation process that is
P
-indistinguishable from an
\mathbb{F}
-predictable process, and
V^{\mathrm{liq}}(\theta,H^{1,n})
satisfies (
2.6
) for all
\theta\in\Theta
. As above, we decompose these processes canonically as
H_{t}^{1,n}=H_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}-H_{t}^{1,n,\downarrow}
, with
H^{1,n,\uparrow}
and
H^{1,n,\downarrow}
both being elements of
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{V}^{+}(\mathbb{F},P)
. By Proposition
A.1
we know that
(H_{T}^{1,n,\uparrow})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(H_{T}^{1,n,\downarrow})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as well as their convex combinations are bounded in
L^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
. Hence, let
D\coloneqq\big([0,T]\cap\mathbb{Q}\big)\cup\{T\}
and use Lemma
A.2
together with a diagonalisation procedure to obtain sequences of convex weights
\alpha_{n}^{j}
such that for

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{n}^{j}H_{t}^{1,n+j-1,\uparrow},\quad\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\downarrow}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{n}^{j}H_{t}^{1,n+j-1,\downarrow},\quad t\in D,

there exist
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variables
\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}
and
\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,\downarrow}
, such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}\to\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow},\quad\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\downarrow}\to\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,\downarrow},\quad\forall t\in D,

(A.9)

almost surely. We denote by
\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}
the event where (
A.9
) holds true so that
P[\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}]=1
. Observe now that
q\mapsto\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)
is non-negative and non-decreasing over
D
for all
\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}
. Now, the
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\sigma
, defined as

\displaystyle\sigma\coloneqq\inf\bigg\{r\in\mathbb{Q}\colon\sup_{q\leq r,\>q\in D}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}=\infty\bigg\},

satisfies
P[\sigma=\infty]=1
. Hence, by letting
\bar{\Omega}_{0}=\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}\cap\{\sigma=\infty\}
such that
P[\bar{\Omega}_{0}]=1
, we may define

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega),\quad t\in[0,T),\quad\omega\in\bar{\Omega}_{0},

(A.10)

and
\bar{H}_{T}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)=\widetilde{H}_{T}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)
,
\omega\in\bar{\Omega}_{0}
. On the other hand, if
\omega\notin\bar{\Omega}_{0}
, we set
\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)=0
, for all
t\in[0,T]
. Note that by Lemma
A.3
and the right continuity of the filtration, the process
\bar{H}^{1,\uparrow}
obtained in this way is right continuous and
\mathbb{F}^{P}
-adapted and hence
\mathbb{F}^{P}
-optional. Indeed, for fixed
t\in[0,T)
, we have that
\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}
and
\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}
are
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable by the right-continuity of the filtration. By Lemma
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle F:={}

\displaystyle\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\right\}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\downarrow\downarrow t,\,q\in\mathbb{Q}\end{subarray}}\widetilde{H}_{q}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}\right\}\in\mathcal{F}_{t},

and hence
P[F]=1
, since
\bar{\Omega}_{0}\subseteq F
and
P[\bar{\Omega}_{0}]=1
. However, by letting
\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}=0
outside of
\bar{\Omega}_{0}
, we need to adjoin all the
P
-null sets to
\mathbb{F}
, and thus we only have that
\bar{H}^{1,\uparrow}
is
\mathbb{F}^{P}
-adapted.

We now claim that if
(\omega,t)\in\bar{\Omega}_{0}\times(0,T)
is such that
t
is a continuity point of the function
s\mapsto\bar{H}_{s}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)
, then
\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\to\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)
. Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
let
q_{1}<t<q_{2}
be rational numbers such that
\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)<\varepsilon
. From (
A.9
), there exists
N=N(\omega)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|<\varepsilon,\quad\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|<\varepsilon,\quad\forall n\geq N.

We then estimate, for all
n\geq N
,

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\widetilde{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|+\big|\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|\bar{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)-\widetilde{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle<3\varepsilon.

Therefore, using monotonicity of
\widetilde{H}^{1,n,\uparrow}
, we obtain for all
n\geq N(\omega)
that

\displaystyle\big|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\widetilde{H}_{t}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|+\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{t}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|+\big|\widetilde{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|\bar{H}_{q_{2}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)-\bar{H}_{q_{1}}^{1,\uparrow}(\omega)\big|

\displaystyle<5\varepsilon.

t=T

.

is right continuous and

-adapted, there exists a sequence

[
13
]
and the fact that

. Hence, by passing once more to convex combinations, we may also assume that

\displaystyle\widehat{\Omega}_{0}\coloneqq\Big\{\omega\in\bar{\Omega}_{0}\colon\widetilde{H}_{\tau_{k}(\omega)}^{1,n,\uparrow}(\omega)\text{ converges to a limit in $\mathbb{R}$ for all }k\Big\},

and still have

The case

A theory for the sparsity emerged in the Forward Forward algorithm
Yukun Yang

Abstract
This report explores the theory that explains the high sparsity phenomenon ( Tosato et al. 2023 ) observed in the forward-forward algorithm ( Hinton 2022 ) . The two theorems proposed predict the sparsity changes of a single data point’s activation in two cases: Theorem 1 : Decrease the goodness of the whole batch. Theorem 2 : Apply the complete forward forward algorithm to decrease the goodness for negative data and increase the goodness for positive data. The theory aligns well with the experiments tested on the MNIST dataset.



1
Definition

1.1
Linear layer

Let
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be the input vector to the fully-connected layer with
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
the weight matrix. The output activation of the fully-connected layer with the ReLU activation is given by:

\bm{h}=\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},{\rm~~~where~~~}\text{ReLU}(\bm{v})[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bm{v}[p],\text{~if~}\bm{v}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.,

(1)

where
\bm{v}[p]
is the
p
th element of the vector
\bm{v}
. Further, we define a mask function
m
indicating if each dimension of
\bm{h}
is positive or been masked out by ReLU to zero:

\bm{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{~~~,~~~}\bm{m}[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\text{~if~}\bm{h}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right..

(2)

This proof only discusses the ReLU activation.

1.2
Goodness

For a batch of input vectors
\mathcal{X}=\{\bm{x}_{1},\bm{x}_{2},\cdots,\bm{x}_{N}\}
with batch size
=N
, the corresponding outputs are defined as
\mathcal{H}=\{\bm{h}_{1},\bm{h}_{2},\cdots,\bm{h}_{N}\}
, where
\bm{h}_{i}=\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i})
.
We define the goodness as the summation of all hidden activations’
l_{2}
norm minus a fixed threshold
\theta
, i.e.:

G(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}-\theta.

(3)

1.3
Sparsity

The sparsity
S
of an activation vector
\bm{h}
is defined as
(
Hoyer 2004
)
:

S(\bm{h})=\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}.

(4)

The sparsity measure
S(\bm{h})\in[0,1]
. A higher value of
S(\bm{h})
indicates a sparser vector
\bm{h}
.

1.4
Forward forward algorithm

We define two group of data: A batch of positive data
\mathcal{X}_{+}=\{\bm{x}_{+1},\bm{x}_{+2},\cdots,\bm{x}_{+N}\}
with batch size
=N
, the corresponding outputs are defined as
\mathcal{H}_{+}=\{\bm{h}_{+1},\bm{h}_{+2},\cdots,\bm{h}_{+N}\}
, where
\bm{h}_{+i}=\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{+i})
; and a batch of negative data
\mathcal{X}_{-}=\{\bm{x}_{-1},\bm{x}_{-2},\cdots,\bm{x}_{-N}\}
with batch size
=N
, the corresponding outputs are defined as
\mathcal{H}_{-}=\{\bm{h}_{-1},\bm{h}_{-2},\cdots,\bm{h}_{-N}\}
, where
\bm{h}_{-i}=\text{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{-i})
.

Then, define loss
L
as:

L=G(\mathcal{H}_{-})-G({\mathcal{H}_{+}})

(5)

Minimizing
L
is equivalent to increase positive data’s goodness while decrease negative data’s goodness. In each iteration, we perform the stochastic gradient descent to train the weights
W
with a batch of positive data and a batch of negative data.

2
Results

Assumptions:

1.

The width of the linear layer
n\rightarrow+\infty

2.

Learning rate
\eta=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})

3.

The pre-activation input
\mathbf{W}\bm{x}
does not have zero element:
\forall\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbf{X},\forall p,(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i})[p]\neq 0
. (Note: what I assumed here is neither the input
\bm{x}
nor the output
\bm{h}
which usually has many zero elements. The weighted sum
\mathbf{W}\bm{x}
can rarely equals to exact zero.)

4.

All inputs
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbf{X}
are normalized to have a unit length:
\forall i,~\|\bm{x}_{i}\|_{2}=1

5.

Weights are initialized by a Gaussian distribution
\mathbf{W}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{2}{n})
. The variance
\sigma=\frac{2}{n}
is picked following the Kaiming initialization
(
He et al. 2015
)
, which guarantees the output activation
\bm{h}
has the same scale of
l_{2}
norm as compared to the input
\bm{x}
.

6.

All inputs and all layers at initialization are iid, i.e. independent, identically distributed, and thus, so are the weights and gradients.

7.

The non-linear activation function is ReLU.

It is not always guaranteed that all samples would becomes sparser in the forward forward algorithm. After each weight adjustment, there are always samples’ sparsity that are increased, unchanged, or decreased. This depends on the property of each data point. A property that, if the majority of data points satisfy, leads to a sparser activation for all in general.

This proof begins with a simpler scenario: We wish to know under what property of a data point that
decreasing
the goodness of the whole batch would
increase
the data point’s sparsity. This gives us Theorem
1
.

Theorem 1
.

If a data point
\bm{x}_{i}
with its activation
\bm{h}_{i}
satisfies:

\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}<\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{i},\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})},

(6)

then decreasing the goodness
G(\mathcal{H})
following gradient descent would increase
\bm{h}_{i}
’s sparsity
S(\bm{h}_{i})
.

In above,
\odot
is the elementwise product. Importantly to know, the reverse order of this theorem is also true. If decreasing the goodness
G(\mathcal{H})
following gradient descent would increase
\bm{h}_{i}
’s sparsity
S(\bm{h}_{i})
, then increase the goodness
G(\mathcal{H})
following gradient descent would decrease
S(\bm{h}_{i})
. We cannot always increase sparsity no matter if we decrease or increase the goodness. The phenomenon that both positive and negative samples have their activations sparsity increased in the forward forward algorithm cannot be studied separately. It must be studied as a co-effect of the co-existence of both groups of data samples.

Therefore, under the same assumptions, we derived the following Theorem for the complete setting of the forward forward algorithm, where there exist both positive data
\mathcal{X}_{+}
and negative data
\mathcal{X}_{-}
, and their corresponding activations
\mathcal{H}_{+}
and
\mathcal{H}_{-}
:

Theorem 2
.

If a data point
\bm{x}_{i}
(No matter if
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{-}
or
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}_{+}
) with its activation
\bm{h}_{i}
satisfies :

\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}(A_{+}-A_{-})<\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}(B_{+}-B_{-})

(7)

where

\displaystyle A_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i})

(8)

\displaystyle A_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i})

\displaystyle B_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{i},\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i})

\displaystyle B_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{i},\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i}),

then decreasing the loss
L=G(\mathcal{H}_{-})-G({\mathcal{H}_{+}})
following gradient descent would increase
\bm{h}_{i}
’s sparsity
S(\bm{h}_{i})
.

3
Discussion

The key findings are (Please refer to the experimental results in the later sections for detail):

•

Most of the data points satisfy Theorem
1
at the beginning of training: Decrease the goodness would increase their sparsity, and increase the goodness would decrease their sparsity.

•

When consider the co-effects of both positive and negative data, we observed that there are usually more than half data points satisfy Theorem
2
and are becoming sparser in training. This result holds for both positive and negative data.

To dig out more insights from them, I am considering the following directions like:

•

Interpret the intuitive meaning of the theorems, and explain why they are (Especially Theorem
2
) satisfied by most of the data points in a dataset like MNIST and CIFAR10 in general.

•

Consider principles of designing the negative data based on Theorem
2
.

•

Simplify the theorems by bringing in more details from experiments. For example, consider what if negative samples are generated by substituting positive samples’ labels by randomly generated wrong labels, so that
\bm{x}_{-}i
differs from
\bm{x}_{+}i
by just two pixels.

•

Modularize the theorems to be more general, such that the effects on the sparsity after changing the goodness function, the normalization method, and others can be better predicted/explained.

•

Consider the extended versions like on recurrent neural networks, and on spiking neurons with temporal dynamics to guide neuromorphic applications.

4
Proof

Sparsity comparison

Naming
\bm{h}_{i}
and
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
as the activations before and after an weight update respectively, our strategy is to compare the value of the sparsity measures
S(\bm{h}_{i})
and
S(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime})
:

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)}.

(9)

We aim to demonstrate that the sparsity prior to the update, represented by the left-hand side, is less than or equal to the sparsity following the update, represented by the right-hand side, when
\bm{h}_{i}
fulfills the condition specified in both theorems. We start by simplifying this comparison to its equivalent forms:

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}},

(10)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}},

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}.

Further, when we replace
|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}|_{1}
with its delta changes
\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}+\Delta_{l_{1}}
, and likewise substitute
\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}
with
\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}+\Delta_{l_{2}}
, we obtain the comparison as:

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}+\Delta_{l_{1}})\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}+\Delta_{l_{2}}),

(11)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\Delta_{l_{1}}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\Delta_{l_{2}}.

Such a comparison requires an evaluation on the changes of
\bm{h}_{i}
’s
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
norms:
\Delta{l_{1}}
and
\Delta{l_{2}}
. Next, we will exam how the update on weights
\mathbf{W}
affect these two values.

4.1
Proof of Theorem
1

Weight update:

Recalling the goodness
G(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}-\theta
, we have the partial derivative of
G
w.r.t the weight
\mathbf{W}
as:

\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial G}{\partial\bm{h}_{k}}\frac{\partial\bm{h}_{k}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{k})\bm{x}_{k}^{T}.

(12)

The term
\bm{m}_{k}
comes from the derivative of
ReLU
function, which is defined as:

\bm{m}_{k}[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\text{~if~}\bm{h}_{k}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right..

(13)

We notice that:

(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{k})[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bm{h}_{k}[p],\text{~if~}\bm{h}_{k}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.\text{~~~equals~~to~~~}\bm{h}_{k},

(14)

so
\bm{m}_{k}
can be absorbed into
\bm{h}_{k}
, and (
49
) can be further simplified as:

\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}.

(15)

Applying gradient descent on W with the step size
\eta
, we have the weight change
\Delta\mathbf{W}
as:

\Delta\mathbf{W}=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}.

(16)

Therefore, the activation
\bm{h}_{i}
and its updated activation
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
are:

\bm{h}_{i}=\text{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}\right),~~~\text{and~~~}\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}=\text{ReLU}\left[\left(\mathbf{W}-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\right)\bm{x}_{i}\right].

(17)

Next, we compare the above two vectors, and measure the changes of its
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
norms separately.

Change of the
\bm{l_{1}}
norm:

\Delta_{l_{1}}=\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}-\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}.

(18)

With ReLU activation, all elements in both
\bm{h}_{i}
and
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
are non-negative, so
\forall p,\bm{h}_{i}[p]\geq 0
and
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}[p]\geq 0
, and the
l_{1}
norm is simply the summation of all elements:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{1}}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}-\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}=\sum_{p=1}^{n}\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}[p]-\sum_{p=1}^{n}\bm{h}_{i}[p],

(19)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}^{T}(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}).

In above,
\mathds{1}
is an all-one vector with length
n
. The scalar product between
\mathds{1}
and any other vector
\bm{h}
with the same length results in a summation of all terms of
\bm{h}
:

\mathds{1}^{T}\bm{h}=\sum_{p=1}^{n}h[p].

(20)

Bringing in the value of both
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
and
\bm{h}_{i}
into (
19
), we get:

\Delta_{l_{1}}=\mathds{1}^{T}\left[\text{ReLU}\left((\mathbf{W}-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T})\bm{x}_{i}\right)-\text{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}\right)\right].

(21)

Next, we will simplify the above equation by proving the sign of each element in
ReLU
before and after the weight update is the same.

With assumption
2
, we notice the changes
\Delta
inside the ReLU before and after the weight update is an infinitesimal vector when
n\rightarrow+\infty
:

\Delta=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i},

(22)

From assumption
4
, we have
\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}=cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\leq 1
, which does not enlarge the magnitude of the resulting vector. Since the batch size
N
is a constant, (
n>>N
),
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}
is among the same magnitude as any
\bm{h}_{k}
.

As compared to
\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}
, we can always have a
\Delta
that has a smaller absolute value on every element by increasing
n
. Therefore, with assumption
1
, we can guarantee: For
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
and
\bm{h}_{i}
, the signs of all elements in ReLU does not change after a weight update:

\text{sign}\left[\left(\mathbf{W}-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\right)\bm{x}_{i}\right]=\text{sign}\left(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}\right),

(23)

With this property, the subtraction between two
ReLU
terms can be discussed separately based on the sign inside
ReLU
.

For the
p
-th element, if
(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}[p])>0
, we can remove ReLU in (
19
) and have the change
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}[p]-\bm{h}_{i}[p]
equals to
\Delta[p]
. Whereas if
(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i})[p]<0
, then we have both
\bm{h}_{i}[p]=\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}[p]=0
. So the update has no change on
\bm{h}_{i}[p]
. As a conclusion, we get:

\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}[p]-\bm{h}_{i}[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta[p],\text{~if~}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i})[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~if~}(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i})[p]<0\end{aligned}\right..

(24)

Here we intentionally omit the situation when
(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}[p])=0
by assuming this is not exist (assumption
3
). This assumption is generally true for the weighted sum of an input’s all dimension can hardly be zero if the input
\bm{x}_{i}
is not an all-zero vector. We will further verify this through experiments.

Such an effect can be perfectly captured by masking out the negative part of
\Delta
by
\bm{m}_{i}
:

\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}.

(25)

Bring the above equation into (
19
), we have:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{1}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}^{T}(-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}),

(26)

\displaystyle=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\mathds{1}^{T}(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}).

Since all elements in
(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})
are non-negative with the ReLU activation, the summation of all elements is equivalent to calculating its
l_{1}
norm. We arrive at our final form of the
\Delta_{l_{1}}
as:

\Delta_{l_{1}}=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}.

(27)

The above equation is an accurate measure of the changes on
\bm{h}_{i}
’s
l_{1}
norm before and after a weight update.

Change of the
\bm{l_{2}}
norm:

\Delta_{l_{2}}=\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}-\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}.

(28)

Performing multi-dimension Taylor expansion, we get:

\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}=\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}+(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})^{T}\mathcal{D}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})+\frac{1}{2!}(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})^{T}\mathcal{D}^{2}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})+\cdots,

(29)

where
\mathcal{D}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})
is the gradient of the
l_{2}
norm at
\bm{h}_{i}
, and
\mathcal{D}^{2}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})
is the Hessian matrix. The gradient is:

\mathcal{D}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})=(\frac{\partial\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}{\partial\bm{h}_{i}[1]},\frac{\partial\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}{\partial\bm{h}_{i}[2]},\cdots,\frac{\partial\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}{\partial\bm{h}_{i}[n]})^{T}.

(30)

For each term in the above equation, we have:

\frac{\partial\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}{\partial\bm{h}_{i}[p]}=\frac{\partial\sqrt{\sum_{q=1}^{n}\bm{h}_{i}[q]^{2}}}{\partial\bm{h}_{i}[p]}=\frac{2\bm{h}_{i}[p]}{2\sqrt{\sum_{q=1}^{n}\bm{h}_{i}[q]^{2}}}=\frac{\bm{h}_{i}[p]}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}.

(31)

Bring (
31
) back into (
30
), we have the gradient as:

\mathcal{D}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})=\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\bm{h}_{i}.

(32)

Represent the higher terms by their big-O notation:

\frac{1}{2!}(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})^{T}\mathcal{D}^{2}(\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2})(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})+\cdots=\mathcal{O}(\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}).

(33)

Bring (
25
) into the above equation, we get:

\mathcal{O}(\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}^{2})=\mathcal{O}(\|-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}).

(34)

In the above equation, we know
\eta=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
from assumption
2
; and we know
\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}=cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})<1
from assumption
4
. Based on assumption
5
, the output
\bm{h}
and the input
\bm{x}
shall be in the same magnitude. Considering the dataset’s size
N
as a constant, we have
\|\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Combining them all, we have:

\mathcal{O}(\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}^{2})=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}).

(35)

Bringing (
29
), (
32
), (
33
) and (
35
) into (
28
), we get:

\Delta_{l_{2}}=(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})^{T}\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\bm{h}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}).

(36)

Further bring (
25
) into the above equation to substitute
(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\bm{h}_{k}\bm{x}_{k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\right)^{T}\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\bm{h}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}),

(37)

\displaystyle=-2\eta\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\right)^{T}\bm{h}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}).

To further simplify the above equation, we substitute the scalar product between the two vectors
(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})
and
\bm{h}_{i}
as their cosine similarity multiplies their
l_{2}
norms:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{2}}

\displaystyle=-2\eta\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}),

(38)

\displaystyle=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}).

Now we have the change on both
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
norms. Next, we will bring them back to the sparsity comparison.

Final step

Recall comparing the value of the sparsity measures
S(\bm{h}_{i})
and
S(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime})
:

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)},

(39)

is equivalent to compare

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\Delta_{l_{1}}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\Delta_{l_{2}}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)}.

(40)

Bringing the calculated
\Delta_{l_{1}}
in (
27
) and
\Delta_{l_{1}}
in (
38
) into the above comparison, we get:

\displaystyle-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}

\displaystyle cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}

(41)

\displaystyle\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\left[-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})\right].

Next, we divide both sides by
(-2\eta)
. Since
(-2\eta)<0
, we need to swap the two sides after dividing:

\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right]\text{~~v.s.~~}\sum_{k=1}^{N}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{1}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2},

(42)

where
\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}=\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}
for short. Since
\eta=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
, dividing
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
by
-2\eta
results in
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
. Reformulate the above equation gives us the final form:

\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\text{~~v.s.~~}\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{1}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})}.

(43)

We can omit
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.

In conclusion, an activation
\bm{h}_{i}
becomes sparser after weight update:

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}<\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)},

(44)

is equivalent to requiring:

\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}<\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{1}}{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{k\bm{m}_{i}},\bm{h}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{k},\bm{x}_{i})}.

(45)

[Q.E.D.]

4.2
Proof of Theorem
2

Weight update:

Recalling the goodness
G(\mathcal{H})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}-\theta
, and the loss
L=G(\mathcal{H}_{-})-G({\mathcal{H}_{+}})
we have the partial derivative of
L
w.r.t the weight
\mathbf{W}
as:

\frac{\partial L}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\bm{h}_{-k}}\frac{\partial\bm{h}_{-k}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\bm{h}_{+k}}\frac{\partial\bm{h}_{+k}}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2(\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{-k})\bm{x}_{k}^{T}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}2(\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{+k})\bm{x}_{k}^{T}.

(46)

The term
\bm{m}_{k}
comes from the derivative of
ReLU
function, which is defined as:

\bm{m}_{k}[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1,\text{~if~}\bm{h}_{k}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right..

(47)

We notice that:

(\bm{h}_{k}\odot\bm{m}_{k})[p]=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bm{h}_{k}[p],\text{~if~}\bm{h}_{k}[p]>0\\
&0,\text{~otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.\text{~~~equals~~to~~~}\bm{h}_{k},

(48)

so
\bm{m}_{k}
can be absorbed into
\bm{h}_{k}
, and (
49
) can be further simplified as:

\frac{\partial G}{\partial\mathbf{W}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}-\sum_{k=1}^{N}2\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}.

(49)

Applying gradient descent on W with the step size
\eta
, we have the weight change
\Delta\mathbf{W}
as:

\Delta\mathbf{W}=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}-\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}\right).

(50)

Therefore, the activation
\bm{h}_{i}
and its updated activation
\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}
are:

\bm{h}_{i}=\text{ReLU}\left(\mathbf{W}\bm{x}_{i}\right),~~~\text{and~~~}\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}=\text{ReLU}\left[\left(\mathbf{W}-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}-\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}\right)\right)\bm{x}_{i}\right].

(51)

Next, we compare the above two vectors, and measure the changes of its
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
norms separately.

Change of the
\bm{l_{1}}
norm:

Following the same logic as (
25
) in the previous section, we can get:

\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}-\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\right).

(52)

Bring this into (
19
), we get:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{1}}

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}^{T}(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i}),

(53)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}^{T}(-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}-\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\right)),

\displaystyle=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i})-\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i})\right),

\displaystyle=2\eta(A_{+}-A_{-}),

where

\displaystyle A_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i}),

(54)

\displaystyle A_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i}).

Change of the
\bm{l_{2}}
norm:

Recall the local Taylor expansion gives us (
36
):

\Delta_{l_{2}}=(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})^{T}\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\bm{h}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}).

(55)

Bring the
(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}-\bm{h}_{i})
in (
52
) into the above equation, we get:

\displaystyle\Delta_{l_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\bm{h}_{-k}\bm{x}_{-k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}-\bm{h}_{+k}\bm{x}_{+k}^{T}\bm{x}_{i}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\right)\right)^{T}\frac{1}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}\bm{h}_{i}+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}),

(56)

\displaystyle=-2\eta\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Bigg(\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i})cos(\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i})

\displaystyle-\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i})cos(\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i})\Bigg)+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}),

\displaystyle=2\eta(B_{+}-B_{-})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}}),

where

\displaystyle B_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i})cos(\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i}),

(57)

\displaystyle B_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i})cos(\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i},\bm{h}_{i}).

Now we have the change on both
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
norms. Next, we will bring them back to the sparsity comparison.

Final step

Recall comparing the value of the sparsity measures
S(\bm{h}_{i})
and
S(\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime})
:

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)},

(58)

is equivalent to compare

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\Delta_{l_{1}}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\Delta_{l_{2}}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)}.

(59)

Bringing the calculated
\Delta_{l_{1}}
in (
53
) and
\Delta_{l_{1}}
in (
56
) into the above comparison, we get:

2\eta(A_{+}-A_{-})\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\left(2\eta(B_{+}-B_{-})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})\right).

(60)

Next, we devide both sides by
2\eta
. Since
\eta=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
, dividing
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n^{2}})
by
2\eta
results in
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
:

\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}(A_{+}-A_{-})\text{~~~v.s.~~~}\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}\left((B_{+}-B_{-})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})\right).

(61)

We can omit
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{n})
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
.

In conclusion, when requiring goodness decrease for negative samples and goodness increase for positive samples, asking an activation
\bm{h}_{i}
becomes sparser after weight update:

\text{(Left~hand~side)~~~}\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}<\frac{\sqrt{n}-\frac{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{1}}{\|\bm{h}_{i}^{\prime}\|_{2}}}{\sqrt{n}-1}\text{~~~(Right~hand~side)},

(62)

is equivalent to requiring:

\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{2}(A_{+}-A_{-})<\|\bm{h}_{i}\|_{1}(B_{+}-B_{-}),

(63)

where

\displaystyle A_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i}),

(64)

\displaystyle A_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{1}cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i}),

\displaystyle B_{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{i},\bm{h}_{+k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{+k},\bm{x}_{i}),

\displaystyle B_{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\|\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i}\|_{2}cos(\bm{h}_{i},\bm{h}_{-k}\odot\bm{m}_{i})cos(\bm{x}_{-k},\bm{x}_{i}).

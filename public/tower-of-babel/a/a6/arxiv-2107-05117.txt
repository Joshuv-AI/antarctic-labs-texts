3.
Sparse John–Nirenberg spaces

Let
\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
be the
collection of all dyadic subcubes in
Q_{0}

\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})\subset\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})

\mathbf{Ch}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}(Q)

respect to inclusion) cubes in
\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})
, which are strictly
contained in
Q
.

The concept of sparse family has its roots in the classical Calderón–Zygmund decomposition lemma
[
9

. The related sparse domination principle, which essentially establishes pointwise bounds of general Calderón–Zygmund operators by a supremum of a special collection of dyadic and positive operators (the so-called
sparse operators

[
24
]
. Over the last few years, sparse domination has been further extended and refined to deal with many classical operators in Analysis. In this regard, we only mention
[
25
]
and the references within.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
, the
sparse John–Nirenberg space

SJN_{p}(Q_{0})
is defined as the set of all

\|f\|_{SJN_{p}(Q_{0})}=\sup_{(Q_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\left\|\sum_{i\in I}\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{i}|}\int_{Q_{i}}|f(x)-f_{Q_{i}}|\,dx\right)\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}\backslash\cup_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}(Q_{i})}Q^{\prime}}\right\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}<\infty.

Clearly, if
p=\infty
then

.
Since every sparse family

(i)

.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N},\lambda\in[0,n),p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
. Then

SV^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})=M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}L^{p}(Q_{0}).

More precisely, if
P_{Q_{0}}^{k}f\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{k-1}
denotes a nearly
best polynomial approximation of
f
in
L^{q}(Q_{0})
(i.e.,
E_{k}(f;Q_{0})_{q}\approx(\int_{Q_{0}}|f-P_{Q_{0}}^{k}f|^{q})^{1/q}
) then

(4.5)

\|M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}(f-P^{k}_{Q_{0}}f)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}\leq c_{n}\|M_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})}\|f\|_{SV^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})}

and

\|f\|_{SV^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})}\leq 2\|M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}(f-P^{k}_{Q_{0}}f)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}.

Here,
c_{n}
denotes a purely dimensional constant.

Theorem
3.1
is an immediate consequence of the previous
result with
\lambda=0,k=1
and
q=1
and the Hardy–Littlewood maximal
theorem (cf. (
1.1
)).

Remark 4.3
.

A similar comment as in Remark
3.3
also applies to the previous theorem.

In fact, the estimate (
4.5
) can be sharpened if we replace
the functional
\|f\|_{SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})}

appearing in the right-hand side by a weaker functional involving a fractional
variant of the sparse condition (
3.1
). This will be explained in more detail in the next section.

5.
Fractional Capacities and Sparseness

The following definition is motivated by fractional capacities of sets of
cubes (cf.
[
22
]
). Let
\lambda\in(0,1]
. We say that
\mathcal{S}^{\lambda}(Q_{0})\subset\mathcal{D}(Q_{0})
is
sparse of order
\lambda
if for every
Q\in\mathcal{S}^{\lambda}(Q_{0})
,

(5.1)

\sum_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\mathcal{S}^{\lambda}(Q_{0})}(Q)}\left|Q^{\prime}\right|^{\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2}|Q|^{\lambda}.

Clearly,
\mathcal{S}^{1}(Q_{0})=\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})
and

(5.2)

\mathcal{S}^{\lambda_{0}}(Q_{0})\subset\mathcal{S}^{\lambda_{1}}(Q_{0}),\qquad\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}

where this inclusion must be appropriately interpreted (i.e., if a given
family of cubes satisfies the condition (
5.1
) with

\lambda_{0}
then the corresponding condition with
\lambda_{1}
also holds).
Accordingly, we can now introduce the class of function spaces

\widetilde{SV}_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0}),\,\lambda\in[0,n),

formed by all functions
f\in L^{q}(Q_{0})
such that

\|f\|_{\widetilde{SV}_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})}=\sup_{(Q_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{S}^{1-\frac{\lambda}{n}}(Q_{0})}\bigg\|\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|^{\frac{\lambda}{nq}-\frac{1}{q}}E_{k}(f;Q_{i})_{q}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}\backslash\cup_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}(Q_{i})}Q^{\prime}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}<\infty.

Here a new phenomenon appears, namely, the supremum runs over families of sparse cubes depending on the smoothness parameter
\lambda
. In particular, this will enable us to capture better the smoothness properties of the space and is in sharp contrast with the classical constructions (
4.1
) where the supremum is taken with respect to all possible families of cubes. Clearly

\widetilde{SV}_{p,q}^{k,0}(Q_{0})=SV_{p,q}^{k,0}(Q_{0}),

and for general
\lambda\in[0,n)
, by (
5.2
),

SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})\subset\widetilde{SV}_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|f\|_{\widetilde{SV}_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})}\leq\|f\|_{SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})}.

An improvement of (
4.5
) (cf. (
5.3
)
below) is contained in the following

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N},\lambda\in[0,n),p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty)
. Then

\widetilde{SV}^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})=M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}L^{p}(Q_{0}).

More precisely, if
P_{Q_{0}}^{k}f\in\mathcal{P}^{n}_{k-1}
denotes a nearly
best polynomial approximation of
f
in
L^{q}(Q_{0})
then

(5.3)

\|M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}(f-P^{k}_{Q_{0}}f)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}\leq c_{n}\|M_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})}\|f\|_{\widetilde{SV}^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})}

and

\|f\|_{\widetilde{SV}^{k,\lambda}_{p,q}(Q_{0})}\leq 2\|M_{q,\lambda,Q_{0}}(f-P^{k}_{Q_{0}}f)\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}.

6.
Some applications

In this section we present further selected applications of the spaces

SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(Q_{0})
.

6.1.
A unified theory of commutators.

To simplify our presentation, the results given in this section are only
stated for the Hilbert transform
H
on
\mathbb{R}
, but corresponding
results for smooth Calderón–Zygmund operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
also
hold. An important family of commutators in Complex Analysis, Nonlinear PDE’s,
Operator Theory and Interpolation Theory is given by

[H,b]f=H(bf)-bH(f),\,\qquad b\in L_{\text{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

The mapping properties of this operator between Lebesgue spaces are collected
in the following

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
1<p,q<\infty
. Then

[H,b]:L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R})

if and only if

(i)

p=q
and
b\in\emph{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
,

(ii)

p<q
and
b\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
with
\alpha=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
,

(iii)

p>q
and
b\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
with
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
.

The regime
p\leq q
in the previous theorem is classical. Specifically, the
diagonal case
p=q
corresponds to the celebrated Coifman–Rochberg–Weiss
theorem
[
11
]
(with
[
28
]
as a forerunner), while the case

p<q
is due to Janson
[
20
]
. On the other hand, the case
q<p
was
only achieved recently by Hytönen
[
19
]
and it is intimately
connected with the Jacobian equation. Note that the non-trivial assertion in
this case concerns the necessity of
b\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
and, in
particular, it shows that the cancellation inherited to the commutator does
not play a role. The results of our
paper can now be applied to give a unified treatment of these three cases and, in particular, they show that the appropriate function spaces in commutator theorems are

SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, which are naturally defined
from (
4.2
). For instance, Theorem
6.1
can now be
rewritten as follows.

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume
1<p,q<\infty
. Let
\lambda=-\big(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\big)_{+}
and
\frac{1}{r}=\big(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\big)_{+}
. Then

[H,b]:SV^{1,0}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})\to SV^{1,0}_{q,1}(\mathbb{R})\iff b\in SV^{1,\lambda}_{r,1}(\mathbb{R}).

The previous statement should be adequately interpreted since
SV^{1,0}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R})
coincides with
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
modulo
constants (cf. Theorem
3.1
and (
4.3
)).

The reformulation of the commutator theorem given in Theorem

6.2
paves the way to further lines of research (cf.

[
13
]
). For instance, it is natural to investigate what is the role played
by the parameters
k,\lambda,p
and
q
in
SV_{p,q}^{k,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in the commutator theorem; the pair
(L^{p},L^{q})
can be
replaced by more general pairs of Banach function spaces; the Hilbert
transform can be replaced by another classical operators in Analysis such as
maximal functions (cf.
[
3
]
.)

6.2.
John–Nirenberg inequalities.

We can give an elementary proof of the John–Nirenberg embedding (
2.1
)
via the spaces
SJN_{p}(Q_{0})
. Indeed, given
p>1
, it follows
from Theorem
3.1
(cf. also Theorem

4.2
with
k=1,\lambda=0
and
q=1
) that

\|f-f_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}\leq c_{n}\|M_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})}\|f\|_{SJN_{p}(Q_{0})}.

Furthermore, a well-known interpolation argument yields that

\|M_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})\rightarrow L^{p^{\prime}}(Q_{0})}\leq\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}.

Note that
\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-1}=p
. Therefore

\|f-f_{Q_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(Q_{0})}\lesssim p\,\|f\|_{SJN_{p}(Q_{0})}\leq p|Q_{0}|^{1/p}\|f\|_{\text{BMO}(Q_{0})}

and by classical extrapolation we arrive at (
2.1
).

The above argument can be applied
mutatis mutandis
to derive John–Nirenberg-type inequalities for Morrey spaces. Further details are left to the reader.

6.3.
Sobolev inequalities

Theorem
4.2
can be applied to give an elementary
proof of the local counterpart of the Sobolev inequality (
1.2
).
Indeed, let
\lambda\in(0,n),\,p\in(1,\frac{n}{\lambda})
and
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\lambda}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|M_{\lambda,Q_{0}}(f-f_{Q_{0}})\|_{L^{q}(Q_{0})}

\displaystyle\approx\|f\|_{SV^{1,\lambda}_{q,1}(Q_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(Q_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\bigg\|\sum_{i\in I}|Q_{i}|^{\frac{\lambda}{n}-1}E_{1}(f;Q_{i})_{1}\mathbf{1}_{Q_{i}\backslash\cup_{Q^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Ch}_{\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}(Q_{i})}Q^{\prime}}\bigg\|_{L^{q}(Q_{0})}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(Q_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I}(|Q_{i}|^{\frac{\lambda}{n}-1}E_{1}(f;Q_{i})_{1})^{q}|Q_{i}|\bigg)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(Q_{i})_{i\in I}\in\mathcal{S}(Q_{0})}\bigg(\sum_{i\in I}(|Q_{i}|^{-1}E_{1}(f;Q_{i})_{1})^{p}|Q_{i}|\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{SJN_{p}(Q_{0})}\approx\|f-f_{Q_{0}}\|_{L_{p}(Q_{0})}.

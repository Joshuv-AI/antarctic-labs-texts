On the forward dynamical behavior of nonautonomous lattice dynamical systems This work is supported by NSF of China under Grants 11871368, 11801190.
Chunqiu Li, Jintao Wang

Abstract
In this article, we study the forward dynamical behavior of nonautonomous lattice systems. We first construct a family of sets { 𝒜 ε ​ ( σ ) } σ ∈ Σ \{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma} in arbitrary small neighborhood of a global attractor of the skew-product flow generated by a general nonautonomous lattice system, which is forward invariant and uniformly forward attracts any bounded subset of the phase space. Moreover, under some suitable conditions, we further construct a family of sets { ℬ ε ​ ( σ ) } σ ∈ Σ \{{\mathcal{B}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma} such that it uniformly forward exponentially attracts bounded subsets of the phase space. As an application, we study the discrete Gray-Scott model in detail and illustrate how to apply our abstract results to some concrete lattice system. Keywords : Lattice dynamical system; Pullback attractor; Forward attraction; Exponential attraction; Nonautonomous system MSC2010 : 35B40, 35B41, 35Q92, 37L50.

In this section, we collect some basic concepts on dynamical systems. One can also see

Let
E
be a complete metric space and
\Sigma
a metric space with the metrics

. Define the Hausdorff-semidistance and Hausdorff distance of

{\rm d}_{H}(A,B)=\sup_{x\in A}d(x,B),\hskip 11.38092pt{\rm\delta}_{H}(A,B)=\max\{{\rm d}_{H}(A,B),{\rm d}_{H}(B,A)\}.

The
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of

is defined by the set

N_{\varepsilon}(A)=\{x\in X:d(x,A)<\varepsilon\}.

Let
E
be a Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|
. A function
f(t)\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
is called
translation bounded
in

\|f\|_{L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)}=\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int^{t+1}_{t}\|f\|_{E}^{2}{\rm d}s<+\infty.

Denote
L_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
the sets of translation bounded functions in

.

A function
f(t)\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
is said to be
translation compact
in
L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
, if the set
\{f(\tau+\cdot):\tau\in\mathbb{R}\}
is precompact in
L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
. By
L_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
we denote the union of translation compact functions
in
L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
. Then
L_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)\subset L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R},E)
(see
[
35
]
).

Let
E
be a Banach space. A family of mappings
\{U(t,\tau)\}_{t\geqslant\tau}
is called

in
E
if

(i)

U(t,s)U(s,\tau)=U(t,\tau),\hskip 11.38092ptt\geqslant s\geqslant\tau,\tau\in\mathbb{R};

(ii)

U(\tau,\tau)={\rm Id}_{E},\hskip 11.38092pt\tau\in\mathbb{R}.

\{U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)\}_{t\geqslant\tau},\sigma\in\Xi
is said to be
a family of processes
in
E
, if for any
\sigma\in\Xi
,
\{U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)\}_{t\geqslant\tau}
is a process in
E
, where
\Xi
is called

.

Definition 2.1
.

A set
B_{0}\subset E
is said to be uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Xi
) absorbing for the family of processes
\{U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)\}_{t\geqslant\tau},\sigma\in\Xi
, if for each
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and bounded set

, there exists
t_{0}=t_{0}(\tau,B)\geqslant\tau
such that

\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Xi}U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)B\subset B_{0},\hskip 11.38092ptt\geqslant t_{0}.

Definition 2.2
.

A closed set
{\mathcal{A}}_{\Xi}
is said to be a uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Xi
) attractor for the family of processes
\{U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)\}_{t\geqslant\tau},\sigma\in\Xi
, if

(i)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sup\limits_{\sigma\in\Xi}{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)B,{\mathcal{A}}_{\Xi})=0,
for each fixed
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and each bounded set
B\subset E
;

(ii)

{\mathcal{A}}_{\Xi}
is the minimal set (for inclusion relation) among those satisfying

.

Now we always assume that the following assumptions hold true.

Assumption
(I)
.
Let
\{\theta_{s}:s\geqslant 0\}
be the natural translation semigroup (see
[
11
]
) acting on
\Xi
and satisfy

(a)

\theta_{s}\Xi=\Xi
for all

;

(b)

U_{\sigma}(t+s,\tau+s)=U_{\theta_{s}\sigma}(t,\tau)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. Then for any
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exists unique
\sigma_{1}\in\Sigma
such that

U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)=U_{\sigma_{1}}(t-\tau+\tau_{0},\tau_{0}),\hskip 11.38092ptt\geqslant\tau.

(2.1)

Proof.

Let
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Since

U_{\sigma}(t,\tau)=U_{\sigma}(t-\tau+\tau_{0}+\tau-\tau_{0},\tau-\tau_{0}+\tau_{0}),

we deduce from Assumption (

) that (

) holds true with
\sigma_{1}=\theta_{\tau-\tau_{0}}\sigma
. Note that

is a homeomorphism for each

∎

Remark 2.4
.

If

then the above lemma shows

for each

.

Finally, we recall the definition of a pullback exponential attractor for the family of processes
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma.

Definition 2.5
.

A family of sets
\{{\mathscr{M}}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
is said to be a pullback exponential attractor

, if it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

is a compact subset of

for all
\sigma\in\Sigma
, and its fractal dimension is finite uniformly with respect to

, i.e.

(2)

and any bounded subset

of

, there exists

with

{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(0,-t)B,{\mathscr{M}}(\sigma))\leqslant C_{B}{\rm e}^{-\alpha t},\hskip 11.38092pt\forall t\geqslant T_{\sigma,B},

where

3
Forward dynamical behavior of LDSs

In this section we consider the forward dynamical behavior of general nonautonomous LDSs.

Set

\ell^{2}=\{u=(u_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}:\ u_{m}\in\mathbb{R},\hskip 11.38092pt\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{m}^{2}<+\infty\},

which is equipped with the inner product and norm defined by

(u,v)=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{m}v_{m},\hskip 11.38092pt\|u\|^{2}=(u,u),\hskip 22.76228pt\forall u=(u_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}},\,v=(u_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{2}.

For convenience, we use
E
to denote
\ell^{2}
. Then
E
is a Hilbert space.

Consider the following abstract nonautonomous LDSs in

\displaystyle\dot{u}=f(u)+\sigma(t),\hskip 11.38092ptt>t_{0};

(3.1)

\displaystyle u(t_{0})=u_{0},\hskip 11.38092ptt_{0}\in\mathbb{R},

(3.2)

where

Let the function

as the symbol space. Define the translation group

\theta_{t}\sigma=\sigma(t+\cdot),\hskip 11.38092ptt\in\mathbb{R},\,\sigma\in\Sigma.

Assume that for each fixed

, equations (
3.1
)-(

) has a unique continuous solution

on

in the sense that

u:[t_{0},+\infty)\rightarrow E\,\mbox{is continuous with $u(t_{0})=u_{0}$, and satisfies the equation \eqref{e3.1}.}

Moreover, for

, operators of solutions

generate a family of continuous processes

. Then one can easily check that the assumption (

For convenience in statement, we define a semigroup
\Psi=\{\Psi(t)\}_{t\geqslant 0}
, called a skew-product flow on

corresponding to

by

\Psi(t)(u,\sigma)=(U_{\sigma}(t,0)u,\theta_{t}\sigma),\hskip 11.38092pt(u,\sigma)\in E\times\Sigma.

(3.3)

In the following, we state and prove our main results in this section. We first construct a family of sets
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
in an arbitrary small
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the global attractor of

\Psi
, such that
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Sigma
) forward attracts each bounded subset of
E
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that the family of processes
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma
has a bounded uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Sigma
) absorbing set
B_{0}
. Moreover,

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\lim_{M\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{u\in B_{0}}\sum_{|m|>M}|\big(U_{\sigma}(t,0)u\big)_{m}|^{2}=0,\hskip 22.76228pt\forall\sigma\in\Sigma.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a family of sets
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
with

A(\sigma)\subset{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\subset{\mathcal{N}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma),\hskip 22.76228pt\forall\sigma\in\Sigma,

such that it is forward invariant for
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma
, where
{\mathcal{N}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)
denotes the
\sigma
-section of an
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of
\bigcup\limits_{\sigma\in\Sigma}(A(\sigma)\times\{\sigma\})
. Furthermore,
\{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Sigma
) forward attracts each bounded set

:

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t,0)B,{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\theta_{t}\sigma))=0.

(3.4)

Proof.

Since
B_{0}
is a bounded uniformly (w.r.t.
\sigma\in\Sigma
) absorbing set for the family of processes
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma
in
E
and

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\lim_{M\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{u\in B_{0}}\sum_{|m|>M}|\big(U_{\sigma}(t,0)u\big)_{m}|^{2}=0,\hskip 22.76228pt\forall\sigma\in\Sigma,

one can conclude from
[
54
, Theorem 3.1]
that
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma
has a compact uniform attractor
D=\omega_{\Sigma}(B_{0})
in
E
.

Now let
\Psi
be the semigroup given by (
3.3
). Then we infer from
[
7

11
]
that
\Psi
has a global attractor
{\mathcal{A}}
on

{\mathcal{A}}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\big(A(\sigma)\times\{\sigma\}\big),

(3.5)

where
\{A(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
is the pullback attractor of
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma
and

.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Write
{\mathcal{D}}=D\times\Sigma
. Noticing that the global attractor

\Psi(t){\mathcal{D}}\subset N_{\varepsilon}({\mathcal{A}}),\hskip 22.76228ptt\geqslant T.

Set
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{s\geqslant T}\Psi(s){\mathcal{D}}
. By the definition of semigroups, one can easily verify that the set
{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}
is
\Psi

\sigma
-section

of

satisfies

{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=\bigcup_{s\geqslant T}U_{\sigma}(0,-s)D.

That is

\begin{array}[]{l}\Psi(t)\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\big({\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\times\{\sigma\}\big)\subset\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\big({\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\times\{\sigma\}\big),\hskip 11.38092pt\forall t\geqslant 0.\end{array}

Hence

\begin{array}[]{l}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\big(U_{\sigma}(t,0){\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\times\{\theta_{t}\sigma\}\big)\subset\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\big({\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\theta_{t}\sigma)\times\{\theta_{t}\sigma\}\big)\hskip 11.38092ptt\geqslant 0,\end{array}

\{{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\}_{\sigma\in\Sigma}
is forward invariant for
\{U_{\sigma}(t,0)\}_{t\geqslant 0},\sigma\in\Sigma

.

Write

. Then the set-valued mapping

.
For any
\sigma\in\Sigma

\displaystyle{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t,0)B,{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}(\theta_{t}\sigma))

\displaystyle\leqslant{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t,0)B,{\mathcal{A}}_{\varepsilon}^{T}(\theta_{t}\sigma))

\displaystyle={\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t,0)B,U_{\sigma}(t,t-T)D)

\displaystyle={\rm d}_{H}(U_{\theta_{t-T}\sigma}(T,0)U_{\sigma}(t-T,0)B,U_{\theta_{t-T}\sigma}(T,0)D).

(3.6)

Recalling that

\sup_{\sigma\in\Sigma}{\rm d}_{H}(U_{\sigma}(t-T,0)B,D)\rightarrow 0\hskip 11.38092pt\mbox{as}\hskip 11.38092ptt\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, by the continuity of

Remark 3.2
.

In the proof of the above theorem, one sees that for each

Moreover, the family of sets

may be used to describe the forward dynamical behavior of nonautonomous LDSs.

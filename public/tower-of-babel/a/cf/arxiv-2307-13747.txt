Fully Dynamic Consistent k k -Center Clustering
Jakub Łącki, ⓡ, Bernhard Haeupler, ⓡ, Christoph Grunau, Václav Rozhoň, ⓡ, Rajesh Jayaram

Abstract
We study the consistent k-center clustering problem. In this problem, the goal is to maintain a constant factor approximate k k -center solution during a sequence of n n point insertions and deletions while minimizing the recourse , i.e., the number of changes made to the set of centers after each point insertion or deletion. Previous works by Lattanzi and Vassilvitskii [ICML ’12] and Fichtenberger, Lattanzi, Norouzi-Fard, and Svensson [SODA ’21] showed that in the incremental setting, where deletions are not allowed, one can obtain k ⋅ polylog ⁡ ( n ) / n k\cdot\operatorname{polylog}(n)/n amortized recourse for both k k -center and k k -median, and demonstrated a matching lower bound. However, no algorithm for the fully dynamic setting achieves less than the trivial O ⁡ ( k ) O(k) changes per update, which can be obtained by simply reclustering the full dataset after every update. In this work, we give the first algorithm for consistent k k -center clustering for the fully dynamic setting, i.e., when both point insertions and deletions are allowed, and improves upon a trivial O ⁡ ( k ) O(k) recourse bound. Specifically, our algorithm maintains a constant factor approximate solution while ensuring worst-case constant recourse per update, which is optimal in the fully dynamic setting. Moreover, our algorithm is deterministic and is therefore correct even if an adaptive adversary chooses the insertions and deletions.

Consistency is an important measure from both a theoretical and a practical point of view. From a theoretical perspective, establishing the optimal recourse achievable while maintaining a given approximation is a fundamental combinatorial question about how significantly updates to the data can change the set of all approximately optimal solutions. Problems for which low-recourse algorithms exist, therefore, must possess some smoothness in their set of approximate solutions. Consistency is also closely related, and in some settings equivalent, to the notion of low-recourse algorithms in the online algorithms literature, which has received significant attention in recent years for various problems

For instance, load balancers must make decisions for which machine to place an item on the fly, however, data may later be reshuffled across machines, although this data shuffling is a costly operation. Furthermore, clustering is often employed as a preliminary step as part of a larger ML pipeline, such as for feature engineering and classification tasks; in such settings, changing the clustering can involve costly recomputations of the downstream pipeline.

Our algorithm works by assigning each point a

The interplay between the two rank functions is quite subtle and we capture it by a hierarchical forest structure that we call a

In this section, we describe the intuition behind our algorithm. First, in

2.1
Basic algorithm

We start by describing a
basic algorithm
, a simple online hierarchical algorithm for
k
-center that already achieves a constant approximation ratio but not constant recourse. This algorithm serves as the basis of our more complicated algorithm achieving constant recourse. The main idea behind this basic algorithm is standard in the context of dynamic algorithms for
k
-center: at every step, we assign every point
p
in the current point set
P
its
rank

\xi(p)
, which is a non-negative integer between
0
and
\lceil\log_{2}\Delta\rceil
.
Here,
\Delta
denotes the maximum distance between any two points in
P
throughout the algorithm.
For the purpose of this technical overview, we assume that
\Delta
is known upfront and that the minimum distance between two points in
P
is at least
1
.
We want to maintain that the ranks induce maximal independent sets on all scales. In particular, for every
i
, we maintain that all points of rank at least
i
have the following two properties:

1.

Separation: For every two points
p,q
with
\xi(p),\xi(q)\geq r
, we have have
d(p,q)\geq 2^{r}
.

2.

Maximality: For every point
p
there exists
p^{\prime}
with
\xi(p^{\prime})>\xi(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi(p)+1}
; alternatively
\xi(p)
is strictly larger than
\xi(p^{\prime})
for all
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{p\}
.

Constant approximation

Any rank function that satisfies both the separation and the maximality property has the following property: Any solution formed by
k
points with the largest rank is
O(1)
-approximate.
To see this, first let
r
be the rank of
k+1
-th point with the largest rank. On one hand, notice that the cost of the optimal solution satisfies
OPT\geq 2^{r}/2
since the separation property implies that if we imagine drawing balls of radius strictly less than
2^{r}/2
around the
k+1
points with largest ranks, these balls are disjoint. Hence, any solution with
k
centers has cost at least
2^{r}/2
.

On the other hand, starting at any point
p
with rank
\xi(p)\leq r
, we can construct a chain of points starting at
p
and ending at some point
q
with rank
\xi(q)>r
as follows: The point
p
has a neighbor
p_{1}
with rank
\xi(p_{1})>\xi(p)
such that
d(p,p_{1})\leq 2^{\xi(p)+1}
by the maximality property. The point
p_{1}
has another neighbor
p_{2}
with rank
\xi(p_{2})>\xi(p_{1})
such that
d(p_{1},p_{2})\leq 2^{\xi(p_{1})+1}
etc. Continuing until we reach the first point
q
with
\xi(q)>r
and summing up a geometric sequence, we conclude that the cost of covering
p
is
O(2^{r})=O(OPT)
.

Maintaining the rank function

Next, we describe a natural way of updating the rank function after the insertion/deletion of a point. This way does not quite achieve the constant recourse but it is the basis for our later, more complicated, algorithm.

Insertions:
Whenever a new point
p
is inserted into
P
, we simply assign it the highest possible rank
\xi(p)
such that we maintain the separation property. Notice that doing this maintains the maximality property.

Deletions:
After a point is deleted from our solution, we iterate over all other remaining points in the point set (in arbitrary order) and increase the rank of each point as much as possible to maintain the separation property. Doing this also maintains the maximality property.

The issue with our basic algorithm is deletions: Deletion of one point can imply a change in rank for arbitrarily many points in the point set! As our plan is to keep the top
k
points with the highest rank as our solution, the naive basic algorithm can have an arbitrarily high recourse.

2.2
Leveled forest

Before moving on to a better algorithm, we next explain the crucial property of the basic algorithm. While we noticed that after the deletion of a point, an arbitrary number of other points can have their ranks increased, there is a structural observation to be made.

Fix a rank
r
and assume we delete a point
p
.
Let
\xi
and
\xi^{\prime}
be the rank function before and after the deletion, respectively.
Consider any two points
q_{1},q_{2}
such that
\xi(q_{1})\leq r,\xi(q_{2})\leq r
,
\xi^{\prime}(q_{1})>r
and
\xi^{\prime}(q_{2})>r
.
We observe that the new ranks of
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
cannot both increase by at least
2
, i.e.,
\xi^{\prime}(q_{1}),\xi^{\prime}(q_{2})\geq r+2
is not possible. To see this, first note that we have
d(p,q_{i})<2^{\xi(q_{i})+1}
for
i=1,2
since
p
must have been the point that prevented the ranks of
q_{i}
from being increased before the deletion.
By triangle inequality, we thus have
d(q_{1},q_{2})<2^{\xi(q_{1})+1}+2^{\xi(q_{2})+1}\leq 2^{r+2}
. Hence if it were indeed true that
\xi^{\prime}(q_{1}),\xi^{\prime}(q_{2})\geq r+2
, the distance between
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
would contradict the separation property of the new rank function
\xi^{\prime}
. We can even strengthen this observation: out of all points that increased their rank to at least
r+1
in the same step, at any later point in time, at most one of them can have a rank at least
r+2
.

A clean way to understand this observation is by drawing a diagram that we called a leveled forest (see
Figure

1
). This is a forest such that its leaves correspond to the current points in
P
and are at level
0
(bottom nodes of each subpicture of
Figure

1
). We additionally maintain a decomposition of the forest into edge-disjoint paths going up from the leaves such that the height of each path is equal to the rank of the respective point.

The forest is constructed during the execution of the algorithm as follows. Whenever a new point is inserted and gets rank
r
, we add a new leaf at level
0
and then add a path of length
r
from that leaf up.

Deletion of a point is more complicated and explained in
Figure

1
.
Whenever a point is deleted, we first delete all edges from the corresponding upwards path from the forest; we also delete all vertices from the tree whose degree becomes zero (see the left and the middle picture of
Figure

1
for the forest before and after this operation).
Next, recall that the ranks of some points are increased to preserve the maximality property of the rank function. We split all the increase operations into batches where
r
-th batch corresponds to a group of points whose rank increases from
r
to
r+1
. Starting at
r=0
, we represent each
r
-th batch operation in the leveled forest by adding one additional node to level
r+1
of the leveled forest and connect it with the rank
r
nodes corresponding to the points in that batch (see the right picture in
Figure

1
, thick edges correspond to four different points increasing their rank by one). To be more precise, we perform these updates of the leveled forest sequentially starting at
r=0
; this is because one point can be in several batches if its rank increases by more than one.

We observe that this way of building the forest allows us to keep a natural decomposition of its edges into paths going upwards from the leaves, such that the length of each path equals the rank of the corresponding point.

Importantly, our observation from this section implies that the picture from
Figure

1
is not misleading.
A priori, the way we build the leveled forest could lead to two edges going up from its inner node
w
of rank
r+1
. But whenever this happens, it means that there was some point
p
in the subtree of
w
that increased its rank to at least
r+2
and later, while
p
was not yet deleted, some other point
q
in the subtree of
w
increased its rank to at least
r+2
. Both
p
and
q
raised their rank from
r
to
r+1
in the same step when the node
w
was created. This is exactly the scenario forbidden by our observation.

2.3
Smooth ranks and our algorithm

From now on, we call the rank function from the basic algorithm the
geometric rank
,
\xi_{G}
.
While using this rank function enables us to achieve the constant approximation ratio, its value can change abruptly for many points after every deletion.
3
3

3

We believe that with a certain potential function, one can prove that the basic algorithm achieves constant
amortized
recourse.

As a remedy, in our constant worst-case recourse algorithm, we maintain one more rank function, the
smooth rank

\xi_{S}
, which is supposed to approximate the geometric rank, while only changing a bit in every step.
Our final algorithm is then very similar to the basic algorithm, except for additionally maintaining the smooth ranks and outputting the
k
points with the highest smooth rank as the output at every step.

Defining smooth ranks

How should the smooth rank be defined? The leveled forest leads to a straightforward definition. Recall that we keep a natural decomposition of the forest into
edge
-disjoint paths that correspond to geometric ranks. We additionally maintain a separate decomposition of the forest into
vertex
-disjoint paths going up from the leaves of the leveled forest. The smooth rank of every point is then defined as the length of the respective path (see
Figure

2
). Notice that we are not trying to keep any additional property tying the geometric and the smooth rank (e.g. requiring that the smooth rank of every point is at most its geometric rank etc.); the two rank functions are only tied together by the structure of the leveled forest.

We now explain how we can maintain a decomposition into vertex-disjoint paths under insertions and deletions in a natural way. First, whenever a new point is inserted, we simply define its smooth rank to be equal to the geometric rank, i.e., the whole path corresponding to its geometric rank is colored blue.

Next, consider the case when a point is deleted together with its path in the leveled forest corresponding to the geometric rank. See
Figure

2
that contains the same situation as
Figure

1
from the perspective of smooth ranks.

We iterate over edges deleted from the leveled forest. Whenever such an edge is used in our decomposition into vertex-disjoint paths (i.e., it is blue in
Figure

2
), we check if the higher node of that edge has any other child and if so, we add one such edge to our decomposition. Whenever the node does not have any other child, the node is anyway deleted from the leveled forest. See the left and the middle picture in
Figure

2
to see the smooth ranks before and after such a change.

Recall that after deleting a point, some other points increase their geometric rank which corresponds to adding new nodes to the leveled forest.
Whenever a new internal node is added, we simply pick an arbitrary edge incident to it and add it to the set of vertex-disjoint paths (see
Figure

2
). This finishes the description of how we maintain a decomposition of the leveled forest into vertex-disjoint paths. This decomposition in turn defines smooth ranks and thus the description of our algorithm is finished.

Constant recourse

To argue that our algorithm has constant recourse, we notice that although in one step, many (in fact,
O(\log\Delta)
) points can change their smooth rank, there are only
O(1)
changes “per rank”. That is, for any
r
there are only
O(1)
points such that their smooth rank was at most
r
before the update and more than
r
after the update. Similarly, there are at most
O(1)
points whose smooth rank decreases from more than
r
to at most
r
. This implies that the set of
k
points with the highest smooth rank changes by
O(1)
in every step and we thus get constant recourse (a more detailed analysis shows that the recourse is in fact equal to
2
).

Constant approximation

Consider any point
p
and assume that the geometric rank of the point with
k+1
-th largest geometric rank is equal to
r
. Recall from our discussion of the basic algorithm in
Section

2.1
that
OPT=\Omega(2^{r})
and that we can find a point
p^{\prime}
(possibly
p^{\prime}=p
) such that
\xi_{G}(p^{\prime})\geq r+1
and
d(p,p^{\prime})=O(2^{r})=O(OPT)
.

Next, we show how to find a point
q
such that
d(p^{\prime},q)=O(OPT)
and
q
is included in our solution; this implies our algorithm has a constant approximation ratio.

Locate the leaf corresponding to
p^{\prime}
in the leveled forest and walk up from that leaf
r+1
steps until reaching an internal node
w
at level
r+1
: The fact that
\xi_{G}(p^{\prime})\geq r+1
implies that such a node exists. This internal node is a vertex of one of the vertex-disjoint paths covering the forest; let
q
be the point corresponding to this path (i.e.,
q
is the point corresponding to the bottommost vertex of the path).

On one hand, the point
q
is included as a center in our solution: This is because in any leveled forest, if there are at most
k
edges reaching level
r+1
, then there are at most
k
vertex-disjoint paths reaching that level. On the other hand, the distance between
p^{\prime}
and
q
is
O(2^{r})
: In fact, by the construction of the leveled forest, every pair of points that are in the subtree of
w
in the leveled forest are of pairwise distance
O(2^{r})
.

C_{i}
satisfies

\max_{p\in P_{i}}d(p,C_{i})\leq 24\cdot\min_{C\subseteq\mathcal{X},|C|\leq k}\max_{p\in P_{i}}d(p,C).

Moreover, for every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
, if
P_{i+1}\setminus P_{i}\neq\emptyset
, then
|C_{i}\triangle C_{i+1}|\leq 2
and if
P_{i}\setminus P_{i+1}\neq\emptyset
, then
|C_{i}\triangle C_{i+1}|\leq 4
.

Let us make a few remarks about the theorem. The time complexity of our algorithm in every step is dominated by the need of solving nearest-neighbour problems to determine whether a rank of a point can be increased. Thus, we spend a large polynomial time every step. It would be interesting to have an algorithm that needs only
\operatorname{poly}(k\log(n\Delta))
time in every step.

Second, we don’t know whether the recourse of two after deletion is optimal or can be improved to one. It would be interesting to see an example showing that the recourse cannot be improved or an algorithm with better recourse.

3.1
Basic notation

We use
A\triangle B
to denote the symmetrical difference of two sets
A
and
B
.
We use
d(p,q)
to denote the distance between
p
and
q
in the input metric space
\mathcal{X}
. We use
d(p,C)=\min_{c\in C}d(p,c)
.
For a pointset
P
and its subset
C
, we define

For every

3.2
Basic definitions and invariants

This section lists the invariants that we keep throughout our algorithm. Mostly, we formalize the notions of rank function and the leveled forest that, as we discussed in

2
, are crucial for our algorithm.

Rank functions

We next define the rank function and formalize its properties and implications.

Definition 3.2

(Rank Function
\xi
and Ordered Rank
\xi^{*}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space.
A rank function for a point set
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
assigns each
p\in P
a nonnegative integer, denoted by
\xi(p)
. Additionally, for a given
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,|P|\}
, let
\xi^{*}(i)
denote the
i
-th largest rank assigned by
\xi
.

Definition 3.3

(Separation Property)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\xi\colon P\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
be a rank function. We say that
\xi
satisfies the separation property iff for any two distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
, it holds that
d(p_{1},p_{2})\geq 2^{\min(\xi(p_{1}),\xi(p_{2}))}
.

Lemma 3.4

(Lower Bound on OPT based on Separation)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\xi\colon P\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
be a rank function satisfying the separation property. Then, for every
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,|P|-1\}
, it holds that

OPT_{k}(P)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}.

Proof.

We show that for every set
C\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
|C|\leq k
, there exists a point
p\in P
such that
d(p,C)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
. This then implies that
OPT_{k}(P)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
.

By definition of
\xi^{*}(k+1)
, there exists a set
P^{\prime}\subseteq P
of size
k+1
such that for every
p\in P^{\prime}
,
\xi(p)\geq\xi^{*}(k+1)
. As
\xi
satisfies the separation property, the pairwise distance between any two points in
P^{\prime}
is at least
2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
.

Consider any set
C\subseteq\mathcal{X}
with
|C|\leq k
. By the pigeonhole principle, there must exist two distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}\in P^{\prime}
and a center
c\in C
such that
d(p_{1},c)=d(p_{1},C)
and
d(p_{2},c)=d(p_{2},C)
. As
d(p_{1},p_{2})\geq 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
, the triangle inequality inplies that
d(p_{1},c)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
or
d(p_{2},c)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
must hold. Thus, we have found a point
p\in\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
such that
d(p,C)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
.

Since the set
C
was chosen arbitrarily, we can conclude that
OPT_{k}(P)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(k+1)}
.
∎

Definition 3.5

(Maximality Property)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\xi\colon P\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
be a rank function. We refer to a point
p\in P
as maximal with respect to
\xi
if either
\xi(p)>\xi(p^{\prime})
for every
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{p\}
or if there exists
p^{\prime}\in P
with
\xi(p^{\prime})>\xi(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi(p)+1}
. We say that
\xi
is maximal if any point in
P
is maximal with respect to
\xi
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\xi\colon P\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
be a rank function satisfying the maximality property. For every
p\in P
and
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,|P|\}
, there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi(q)>\xi^{*}(i)
and
d(p,q)\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(i)}
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
j\in\{0,1,\ldots,\xi^{*}(i)\}
that for every point
p
with
\xi(p)\geq\xi^{*}(i)-j
, there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi(q)>\xi^{*}(i)
and
d(p,q)\leq 2\cdot\sum_{\ell=\xi^{*}(i)-j}^{\xi^{*}(i)}2^{\ell}\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(i)}
.

Base case:
For
j=0
, let
p
be a point with
\xi(p)=\xi^{*}(i)
(if
\xi(p)>\xi^{*}(i)
, we can set
q=p
). As
\xi
is maximal and
p
is not the point with the largest rank, there exists a point
q\in P
such that
\xi(q)>\xi(p)=\xi^{*}(i)
and
d(p,q)<2^{\xi(p)+1}=2^{\xi^{*}(i)+1}=2\cdot\sum_{\ell=\xi^{*}(i)}^{\xi^{*}(i)}2^{\ell}\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(i)}
. Therefore, the base case holds.

Inductive step:
Assume the statement holds for some
j\geq 0
. We show it also holds for
j+1
. Let
p
be a point with
\xi(p)=\xi^{*}(i)-(j+1)
. As
\xi
is maximal, there exists a point
q^{\prime}\in P
such that
\xi(q^{\prime})>\xi(p)
and
d(p,q^{\prime})<2^{\xi(p)+1}=2^{\xi^{*}(i)-j}
. By the inductive hypothesis, there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi(q)>\xi^{*}(i)
and
d(q^{\prime},q)\leq 2\cdot\sum_{\ell=\xi^{*}(i)-j}^{\xi^{*}(i)}2^{\ell}
. By the triangle inequality,
d(p,q)\leq d(p,q^{\prime})+d(q^{\prime},q)\leq 2\cdot\sum_{\ell=\xi^{*}(i)-(j+1)}^{\xi^{*}(i)}2^{\ell}\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}(i)}
. This completes the inductive step and hence the proof.
∎

Valid tuples

The following
Definition

3.7
of a valid tuple and
Lemma

3.8
formalize the properties that two rank functions
\xi_{G}
(a geometric rank) and
\xi_{S}
(smooth rank) need to satisfy so as to imply constant approximate ratio.

Definition 3.7

(Valid Tuple)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
\xi_{G},\xi_{S}\colon P\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
be two rank functions for
P
.
We refer to
(\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
as a valid tuple for
P
iff the following properties are satisfied:

1.

The rank function
\xi_{G}
satisfies both the separation and the maximality property.

2.

It holds that
\xi^{*}_{G}(i)\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
for every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,|P|\}
.

3.

For every
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
p\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p)\geq r
, there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi_{S}(q)\geq r
and
d(p,q)\leq 4\cdot 2^{r}
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
(\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
be a valid tuple for
P
.
Let
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,|P|-1\}
and
C\subseteq P
such that each point
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)>\xi_{S}^{*}(k+1)
is contained in
C
. Then,

COST(P,C)\leq 24\cdot OPT_{k}(P).

Proof.

As
\xi_{G}
satisfies the separation property, we can conclude that
OPT_{k}(P)\geq 0.5\cdot 2^{\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)}
using
Lemma

3.4
. It thus suffices to show that
COST(P,C)\leq 12\cdot 2^{\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)}
.
Consider an arbitrary point
p\in P
. As
\xi_{G}
satisfies the maximality property,
Lemma

3.6
implies that there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi_{G}(q)>\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)
and
d(p,q)\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)}
. Using the third property of a valid tuple, there exists a point
q^{\prime}\in P
with
\xi_{S}(q^{\prime})>\xi^{*}_{G}(k+1)
and
d(q,q^{\prime})\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}_{G}(k+1)+1}
. As
\xi_{S}(q^{\prime})>\xi^{*}_{G}(k+1)\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(k+1)
, we get that
q^{\prime}\in C
by the second property. Using triangle inequality, we get

d(p,C)\leq d(p,q^{\prime})\leq d(p,q)+d(q,q^{\prime})\leq 4\cdot 2^{\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)}+4\cdot 2^{\xi^{*}_{G}(k+1)+1}=12\cdot 2^{\xi_{G}^{*}(k+1)}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Definitions related to leveled forest

We next describe the formal definition of the leveled forest. Formally, a leveled forest is a structure that enables us to maintain a valid tuple from
Definition

3.7
.

Definition 3.9

(Leveled Forest)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space and
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
. We refer to
F
as a leveled forest for
P
if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

F
is a rooted forest with edges oriented towards the respective root.

2.

The set of leafs in
F
is equal to
P
.

3.

For each rooted tree
T
in
F
, every leaf in
T
has the same distance to the root of
T
.

For consistency, if
P=\emptyset
, we refer to
F
as a leveled forest for
P
if both the vertex and the edge set is empty.

Definition 3.10

(Height Function
h_{F}
)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
F
be a leveled forest for
P
.
We associate a height function
h_{F}\colon V(F)\mapsto\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
F
, where for each node
v
in
F
,
h_{F}(v)
represents the number of edges on the path from
v
to each leaf in its subtree.

The following definition of a valid triple formalizes that we want to maintain not just the forest itself, but also its two decompositions in edge-disjoint and vertex-disjoint paths that correspond to the geometric and smooth ranks respectively.

Definition 3.11

(Valid Triple)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
,
F
be a leveled forest for
P
and
\xi_{G},\xi_{S}
be two rank functions for
P
. We say that
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
is a valid triple for
P
if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

The rank function
\xi_{G}
satisfies the separation property.

2.

For each point
p
in
P
, the values of
\xi_{G}(p)
and
\xi_{S}(p)
are at most the length of the path from
p
to its root in
F
.

3.

For each node
v
in the forest
F
, there is exactly one leaf
p_{v}
in its subtree such that
\xi_{S}(p_{v})\geq h_{F}(v)
.

4.

For each edge
(u,v)
in
F
, where
v
is the parent of
u
, there is exactly one leaf
p_{uv}
in the subtree of
u
such that
\xi_{G}(p_{uv})\geq h_{F}(v)
.

5.

Let
v
be a node in
F
, and
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
be two distinct leaves in the subtree of
v
with
\min(\xi_{G}(p_{1}),\xi_{G}(p_{2}))\geq h_{F}(v)
. Then,
d(p_{1},p_{2})<2^{h_{F}(v)+1}
.

Valid triples imply valid tuples

We show that maintaining a leveled forest in the sense of
Definition

3.11
implies that the corresponding rank function have the required properties in the sense of
Definition

3.7
. First, we prove that for any node
v
in the leveled forest, all points in its subtree are close to each other.

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
be a valid triple for
P
.
Consider a common ancestor
v
of two leaves
q
and
p
in
F
, where
\xi_{G}(q)\geq h_{F}(v)
. Then, the distance
d(q,p)
between
q
and
p
is at most
\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(v)}2^{i+1}=4\cdot 2^{h_{F}(v)}-4
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
h_{F}(v)
.

Base case:
Assume
h_{F}(v)=1
and let
p
and
q
be two distinct leaves in the subtree of
v
. As
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
is a valid triple, the fourth property implies that both
\xi_{G}(p)\geq 1
and
\xi_{G}(q)\geq 1
. Thus, the fifth property of a valid triple implies that
d(q,p)<2^{h_{F}(v)+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(v)}2^{i+1}
. Therefore, the base case holds.

Inductive step:
Assume the statement holds for some
j
. We show that it also holds for
j+1
. Consider a common ancestor
v
of two leaves
p
and
q
with
h_{F}(v)=j+1
and
\xi_{G}(q)\geq h_{F}(v)
.
Let
u_{p}
and
u_{q}
be the two children of
v
such that
p
is in the subtree of
u_{p}
and
q
is in the subtree of
u_{q}
. If
u_{p}=u_{q}
, which occurs when
v
is not the lowest common ancestor of
p
and
q
, we can directly use induction to conclude that
d(q,p)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(u_{p})}2^{i+1}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(v)}2^{i+1}
.
Thus, it remains to consider the case where
u_{p}\neq u_{q}
. Let
p_{u_{p}v}
be the leaf in the subtree of
u_{p}
satisfying
\xi_{G}(p_{u_{p}v})\geq h_{F}(v)
. Such a node
p_{u_{p}v}
is guaranteed to exist because of the fourth property of a valid triple. Using induction, we have
d(p_{u_{p}v},p)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(u_{p})}2^{i+1}
. By the fifth property, we have
d(q,p_{u_{p}v})<2^{h_{F}(v)+1}
. Therefore, using the triangle inequality, we can conclude that:

d(q,p)\leq d(q,p_{u_{p}v})+d(p_{u_{p}v},p)\leq 2^{h_{F}(v)+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(u_{p})}2^{i+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{h_{F}(v)}2^{i+1}.

This completes the inductive step, and hence the proof.
∎

Next, we prove that valid triples imply valid tuples.
In view of
Lemma

3.8
, this shows that maintaining the leveled forest in the sense of
Definition

3.11
is enough to achieve constant approximation.

Lemma 3.13

(Valid Triple together with Maximality implies Valid Tuple)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space,
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
and
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
be a valid triple for
P
. Assume that
\xi_{G}
additionally satisfies the maximality property (
Definition

3.5
). Then,
(\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
is a valid tuple for
P
.

Proof.

By definition,
\xi_{G}
satisfies both the separation and the maximality property.

To show that the second property of a valid tuple holds, consider an arbitrary
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,|P|\}
. We have to show that
\xi^{*}_{G}(i)\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
. First, note that is suffices to give an injective mapping that maps each point
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
to a point
p^{\prime}\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p^{\prime})\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
. Now, consider some
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
. Let
v
be the ancestor of
p
in
F
with
h_{F}(v)=\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
. The node
v
is well-defined because of the second property of a valid triple.
We now map
p
to an arbitrary descendant
p^{\prime}
of
v
with
\xi_{G}(p^{\prime})\geq h_{F}(v)=\xi^{*}_{S}(i)
. Such a descendant is guaranteed to exist because of the fourth property. The third property, or more concretely that
p
is the only descendant of
v
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq h_{F}(v)
, implies that the mapping is indeed injective.

To show the third property, consider an arbitrary
r\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
p\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p)\geq r
. We have to show that there exists a point
q\in P
with
\xi_{S}(q)\geq r
and
d(p,q)\leq 4\cdot 2^{r}
.
Let
v
be the ancestor of
p
with
h_{F}(v)=r
. Let
q
be the leaf in the subtree of
v
with
\xi_{S}(q)\geq r
, guaranteed by the third property. As
v
is a common ancestor of
p
and
q
and
\xi_{G}(p)\geq h_{F}(v)
, we can use
Lemma

3.12
to conclude that
d(p,q)\leq 4\cdot 2^{h_{F}(v)}-4\leq 4\cdot 2^{r}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

(

,

Lemma 3.14

(Insertion Lemma)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space and
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a point set with minimum pairwise distance at least
1
.
Let
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
be a valid triple for point set
P
and
\xi_{G}
additionally satisfies the maximality property. Let
q\in\mathcal{X}
with
d(q,p)\geq 1
for every
p\in P
. Let
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
be an upper bound on the maximum pairwise distance between any two points in
P\cup\{q\}
. If
P\neq\emptyset
, then we additionally assume that there exists a point
p\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p)=\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
.
Let
(F^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}_{G},\xi^{\prime}_{S})\leftarrow\texttt{INSERT}(q,P,F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S},\Delta)
(
Algorithm

1
). Then,
(F^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}_{G},\xi^{\prime}_{S})
is a valid triple for
P\cup\{q\}
,
\xi^{\prime}_{G}
satisfies the maximality property, there exists
p\in P\cup\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)\geq\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
and
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)=\xi_{S}(p)
for every
p\in P
.

Proof.

First, note that we assume that
d(q,p)\geq 1=2^{\min(0,\xi_{G}(p))}
for every
p\in P
.
Therefore,
i_{q}
is well-defined. We first check that
\xi_{G}^{\prime}
satisfies the separation property.
To check the separation property, consider two distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}\in P\cup\{q\}
.
We have to show that
d(p_{1},p_{2})\geq 2^{\min(\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{1}),\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{2}))}
.
If both
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are in
P
, then this follows from
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)=\xi_{G}(p)
for every
p\in P
and our assumption that
\xi_{G}
satisfies the separation property.
If either
p_{1}=q
or
p_{2}=q
, then this directly follows from how
i_{q}
is defined and that
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(q)=i_{q}
.

We next verify that
\xi^{\prime}_{G}
satisfies the maximality property. Consider an arbitrary
p\in P\cup\{q\}
.
We have to show that either
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)>\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p^{\prime})
for every
p^{\prime}\in(P\cup\{q\})\setminus\{p\}
or there exists some
p^{\prime}\in P\cup\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p^{\prime})>\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)
and

\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{uv})\geq h_{F^{\prime}}(v)
.

Let

be a node in
F^{\prime}
, and
p_{1},p_{2}
be two distinct leaves in the subtree of
v
in
F
such that
\min(\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{1}),\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{2}))\geq h_{F^{\prime}}(v)
. We have to show that this implies
d(p_{1},p_{2})<2^{h_{F^{\prime}}(v)+1}
. First, consider the case

. In that case, both
p_{1},p_{2}\in Q
and one of our assumptions is that
d(p_{1},p_{2})<2^{h+2}=2^{h_{F^{\prime}}(v)+1}
. Thus, it remains to consider the case that
v\neq r
. Then,
\min(\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{1}),\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{2}))\geq h_{F^{\prime}}(v)
implies
\min(\xi_{G}(p_{1}),\xi_{G}(p_{2}))\geq h_{F^{\prime}}(v)=h_{F}(v)
and thus one of our assumptions is that
d(p_{1},p_{2})<2^{h_{F}(v)+1}=2^{h_{F^{\prime}}(v)+1}
, as needed.

Finally, note that the fact that there exists at most one vertex
p^{*}\in P
such that
\xi_{S}(p^{*})=h
,
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p^{*})=h+1

in

is defined.

Lemma 3.18

(Deletion Lemma)
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},d)
be a metric space and
P\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be a point set with minimum pairwise distance at least
1
. Let
(F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S})
be a valid triple for point set
P
and
\xi_{G}
additionally satisfies the maximality property. Let
q\in P
and
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
be an upper bound on the maximum pairwise distance between any two points in
P
.
Let
(F^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}_{G},\xi^{\prime}_{S})\leftarrow\texttt{DELETE}(q,P,F,\xi_{G},\xi_{S},\Delta)
(
Algorithm

5
). Then,
(F^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}_{G},\xi^{\prime}_{S})
is a valid triple for point set
P\setminus\{q\}
and
\xi^{\prime}_{G}
satisfies the maximality property. Moreover, if
P\setminus\{q\}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)\geq\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
.
Moreover, for every
h\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists at most one point
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq h
and either
p=q
or
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)<h
. Also, the number of points
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
satisfying
\xi_{S}(p)<h
and
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)\geq h
is at most
2
.

Proof.

We first show by induction that for every
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil\}
, the following holds:

1.

(F^{(i)},\xi_{G}^{(i)},\xi_{S}^{(i)})
is a valid triple for
P\setminus\{q\}
.

2.

For every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{(i)}_{G}(p)<i
, there exists a
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p^{\prime})>\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)+1}
.

3.

For every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
, it holds that
\xi^{(i)}_{G}(p)\geq\xi_{G}(p)
.

4.

For every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
, it holds that
\xi^{(i)}_{S}(p)\geq\xi^{(0)}_{S}(p)
.

5.

For every
j\geq i
and
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{S}^{(0)}(p)\leq j
, we have
\xi_{S}^{(i)}(p)\leq j
.

6.

For every
j\leq i
, there exists at most one
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{(0)}_{S}(p)<j
and
\xi^{(i)}_{S}(p)\geq j
.

Base case:
First, note that the preconditions for the
DELETE
-operation directly imply that the preconditions are satisfied when we call the
DELETEWITHOUTMAXIMALITY
-operation. Thus, it directly follows from the guarantees of the
DELETEWITHOUTMAXIMALITY
-operation that
(F^{(0)},\xi_{G}^{(0)},\xi_{S}^{(0)})
is a valid triple for
P\setminus\{q\}
and we also get the guarantee that
\xi^{(0)}_{G}(p)=\xi_{G}(p)
for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
. All other conditions are trivially satisfied for
i=0
.

Inductive step:
Now, assume that the conditions hold for some
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil-1\}
. We next show that then the conditions also hold for
i+1
.

We first have to verify that the preconditions are met when we call
GROUPINCREASE
with input
Q_{i},i,P\setminus\{q\},F^{(i)},\xi_{G}^{(i)}
and
\xi_{S}^{(i)}
. The only precondition that does not directly follow from our assumption that
(F^{(i)},\xi_{G}^{(i)},\xi_{S}^{(i)})
is a valid triple and the conditions we impose on
Q_{i}
is the property that
d(q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i})<2^{i+2}
for every
q_{i},q^{\prime}_{i}\in Q_{i}
. By triangle inequality, it suffices to show that
d(q_{i},q)<2^{i+1}
for every
q_{i}\in Q_{i}
. Note that we assume that
\xi_{G}
satisfies the maximality property and that there exists a point
p\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p)\geq\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
. As
q_{i}\in Q_{i}
, we have
\xi_{G}(q_{i})\leq\xi^{(i)}_{G}(q_{i})=i<\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
. Hence, the maximality property implies that there exists
p^{\prime}\in P
with
\xi_{G}(p^{\prime})>\xi_{G}(q_{i})
and
d(q_{i},p^{\prime})<2^{\xi_{G}(q_{i})+1}\leq 2^{\min(i+1,\xi_{G}(p^{\prime}))}
. The second condition of
Q_{i}
implies that
p^{\prime}\notin Q_{i}
and the third condition together with
\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)\geq\xi_{G}(p)
for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
implies that
p^{\prime}\notin P\setminus(\{q\}\cup Q_{i})
. Thus,
p^{\prime}=q
and we indeed get that
d(q_{i},q)<2^{i+1}
. Thus, the preconditions are satisfied when we call
GROUPINCREASE
with input
Q_{i},i,P\setminus\{q\},F^{(i)},\xi_{G}^{(i)}
and
\xi_{S}^{(i)}
.

Thus, we directly get that
(F^{(i+1)},\xi_{G}^{(i+1)},\xi_{S}^{(i+1)})
is a valid triple for
P\setminus\{q\}
.

Next, let
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)<i+1
. We have to show that there exists
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p^{\prime})>\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)+1}
. As
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)<i+1
, we have
p\notin Q_{i}
and therefore
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)=\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)
. First, consider the case that
\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)<i
. Then, the induction hypothesis implies that there exists
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p^{\prime})\geq\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p^{\prime})>\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)=\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)+1}=2^{\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)+1}
. Thus, it remains to consider the case that
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)=\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)=i
. As
Q_{i}
is a
maximal
subset with the specified properties, this implies that there exists
q_{i}\in Q_{i}
with
d(p,q_{i})<2^{i+1}
or there exists some other point
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus(\{q\}\cup Q_{i})
with
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\min(i+1,\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p^{\prime}))}
. In the former case, we have
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(q_{i})=i+1>\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)
and
d(p,q_{i})<2^{\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)+1}
, as needed. Thus, it remains to consider the latter case. As
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}
satisfies the separation property, we have
d(p,p^{\prime})\geq 2^{\min(i,\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p^{\prime}))}
. Together with
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\min(i+1,\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p^{\prime}))}
, this implies that
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p^{\prime})\geq i+1>\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)
, which together with
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\min(i+1,\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p^{\prime}))}\leq 2^{\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)+1}
shows that the second property is satisfied.

The third property, namely that for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
, it holds that
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)\geq\xi_{G}(p)
directly follows from
\xi_{G}^{(i)}(p)\geq\xi_{G}(p)
for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
and
\xi_{G}^{(i+1)}(p)\geq\xi^{(i)}_{G}(p)
for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
.

Note that
GROUPINCREASE
guarantees that there exists at most one
p^{*}\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{(i)}_{S}(p^{*})=i
and
\xi^{(i+1)}_{S}(p^{*})=i+1
and that for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
not equal to
p^{*}
, we have
\xi^{(i+1)}_{S}(p)=\xi^{(i)}_{S}(p)
. Using this guarantee, a simple inductive argument shows that properties
4
,
5
and
6
are preserved. This finishes the induction.

Finishing the proof:

We are now ready to show that
(F^{\prime},\xi^{\prime}_{G},\xi^{\prime}_{S})
satisfies the output guarantees. The fact that
(F^{\prime},\xi_{G}^{\prime},\xi_{S}^{\prime})
is a valid triple for point set
P\setminus\{q\}
directly follows from the fact that
(F^{(\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil)},

\xi_{G}^{(\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil)},\xi_{S}^{(\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil)})
is a valid triple for point set
P\setminus\{q\}
.

The second property for
i=\lceil\log(\Delta+1)\rceil
together with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}=\xi_{G}^{(\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil)}
implies that for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)<\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
, there exists a
p^{\prime}\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi_{G}^{\prime}(p^{\prime})>\xi_{G}^{\prime}(p)
and
d(p,p^{\prime})<2^{\xi_{G}^{\prime}(p)+1}
.
First, this implies that if
P\setminus\{q\}\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)\geq\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
. Moreover, it also implies that
\xi^{\prime}_{G}
satisfies the maximality property, using the fact that there exists at most one point
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
with
\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p)\geq\lceil\log(\Delta)+1\rceil
. This in turn follows as for any two distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}\in P\setminus\{q\}
, we have
2^{\min(\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{1}),\xi^{\prime}_{G}(p_{2}))}\leq d(p_{1},p_{2})\leq\Delta
, where the first inequality follows because
\xi^{\prime}_{G}
satisfies the separation property.

Next, consider an arbitrary
h\in\mathbb{N}
. We first have to show that there exists at most one
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq h
and either
p=q
or
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)<h
.
This directly follows from the guarantee of
DELETEWITHOUTMAXIMALITY
that there exists at most one
p\in P
with
\xi_{S}(p)\geq h
and either
p=q
or
\xi_{S}^{(0)}(p)<h
together with the guarantee that
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)\geq\xi_{S}^{(0)}(p)
for every
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
.

Finally, it remains to show that the number of points
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
satisfying
\xi_{S}(p)<h
and
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)\geq h
is at most
2
. This follows from the guarantee of
DELETEWITHOUTMAXIMALITY
that there exists at most one
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
satisfying
\xi_{S}(p)<h
and
\xi_{S}^{(0)}(p)\geq h
and the guarantee that there exists at most one
p\in P\setminus\{q\}
satisfying
\xi_{S}^{(0)}(p)<h
and
\xi^{\prime}_{S}(p)\geq h
. This finishes the proof.
∎

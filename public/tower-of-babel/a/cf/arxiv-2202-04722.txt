On the stability of unevenly spaced samples for interpolation and quadrature Thanks: Submitted to the editors August 8, 2026.
Annan Yu, Alex Townsend

Abstract
Unevenly spaced samples from a periodic function are common in signal processing and can often be viewed as a perturbed equally spaced grid. In this paper, we analyze how the uneven distribution of the samples impacts the quality of interpolation and quadrature. Starting with equally spaced nodes on [ − π , π ) [-\pi,\pi) with grid spacing h h , suppose the unevenly spaced nodes are obtained by perturbing each uniform node by an arbitrary amount ≤ α ​ h \leq\alpha h , where 0 ≤ α < 1 / 2 0\leq\alpha<1/2 is a fixed constant. We prove a discrete version of the Kadec-1/4 theorem, which states that the nonuniform discrete Fourier transform associated with perturbed nodes has a bounded condition number independent of h h , for any α < 1 / 4 \alpha<1/4 . We go on to show that unevenly spaced quadrature rules converge for all continuous functions and interpolants converge uniformly for all differentiable functions whose derivative has bounded variation when 0 ≤ α < 1 / 4 0\leq\alpha<1/4 . Though, quadrature rules at perturbed nodes can have negative weights for any α > 0 \alpha>0 , we provide a bound on the absolute sum of the quadrature weights. Therefore, we show that perturbed equally spaced grids with small α \alpha can be used without numerical woes. While our proof techniques work primarily when 0 ≤ α < 1 / 4 0\leq\alpha<1/4 , we show that a small amount of oversampling extends our results to the case when 1 / 4 ≤ α < 1 / 2 1/4\leq\alpha<1/2 .

There are several aspects of unevenly spaced samples that we investigate:

•

Conditioning of a nonuniform discrete Fourier transform.

The analogue of the FFT for unevenly spaced nodes is the nonuniform discrete Fourier transform (NUDFT). There are many variants of the NUDFT, but we focus on the one that is closely related to
eq.

1

[
4
]
. Let
N
be an integer and
\underline{c}=\left(c_{-N},\ldots,c_{N}\right)^{\top}
be a vector. The NUDFT task related to
eq.

1
is to compute the vector
\underline{f}=\left(f_{-N},\ldots,f_{N}\right)^{\top}

f_{j}=\sum_{k=-N}^{N}c_{k}e^{-i\tilde{x}_{j}k},\qquad-N\leq j\leq N.

(2)

As

2

2N+1

2N+1

\underline{f}
require
\mathcal{O}(N^{2})
operations; however, there are efficient algorithms that require only
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
operations
[
5
]
. The NUDFT task has an inverse for any perturbed grid with
0\leq\alpha<1/2
, which aims to recover the vector
\underline{c}
from
\underline{f}
. We show that for
\alpha<1/4
, the NUDFT task and its inverse have a condition number that can be bounded independent of
N
when
\alpha<1/4

Trigonometric interpolation.

A trigonometric polynomial

of degree

[-\pi,\pi)

q(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{ikx},\qquad c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}.

(3)

We denote the space of all trigonometric polynomials of degree
\leq n
by
\mathcal{T}_{n}

such that
q
interpolates

at the samples, i.e.,
q(\tilde{x}_{j})=f_{j}
for
-N\leq j\leq N

3

\alpha

theorem

5
and
theorem

Exact quadrature.

A quadrature rule is a method for numerical integration that approximates the integral of

by a weighted sum of the function’s samples, i.e.,

I=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(x)dx\approx\tilde{I}_{N}=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\tilde{w}_{j}f(\tilde{x}_{j}).

(4)

Here,
\tilde{x}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{N}
are called the quadrature nodes and
\tilde{w}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{w}_{N}
the quadrature weights. Given quadrature nodes, it is often desirable to design the quadrature weights so that

4

\mathcal{T}_{n}
for some
n

, the exact condition uniquely defines a quadrature rule for any
\alpha<1/2

\alpha
-perturbed nodes can have negative weights for any

6

and conclude that

for all continuous periodic functions when
\alpha<1/4

The Kadec-
\mathbf{1/4}
theorem and its consequences

In sampling theory, the Kadec-
1/4
theorem shows that the Fourier modes
\left\{e^{i\lambda_{k}x}\right\}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
form a Riesz basis when
|\lambda_{k}-k|\leq\alpha
and
0\leq\alpha<1/4

[
18
]
, which in signal processing means that one can recover a square-integrable function if given its inner-products with
\left\{e^{i\lambda_{k}x}\right\}
. While the standard setting for Kadec’s theorem applies to perturbing an infinite number of Fourier wave numbers from integers
k
to
\lambda_{k}
, we show that there is a discrete analogue when perturbing a finite number of nodes. It has consequences for the condition number of the NUDFT and MZ inequalities.

For an integer
N
and
\alpha
-perturbed nodes
\{\tilde{x}_{j}\}
, let
F
be the DFT matrix given by

F_{jk}=e^{-2\pi ijk/(2N+1)},\qquad-N\leq j,k\leq N,

and
\tilde{F}
be a NUDFT matrix given by

\tilde{F}_{jk}=e^{-i\tilde{x}_{j}k}=e^{-2\pi i(j+\delta_{j})k/(2N+1)},\qquad-N\leq j,k\leq N.

(6)

The matrix
\tilde{F}
is of interest because the sums in
eq.

2
can be neatly written as the matrix-vector product
\tilde{F}\underline{c}=\underline{f}
. Therefore, the action of
\tilde{F}
onto a vector is equivalent to evaluating a trigonometric polynomial of degree
N
at
\tilde{x}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{N}
. This can be performed in
\mathcal{O}(N\log N)
operations
[
27
]
and in Chebfun (a MATLAB package for computing with functions)
[
8
]
can be implemented as follows:

N = 1e4; alpha = 0.1; h = 2*pi/(2*N+1);
tilde_x = (-N:N)’*h + alpha*(2*rand(2*N+1,1)-1)*h; % perturbed nodes
c = randn(2*N+1,1); % trig coeffs
Fc = exp(1i*tilde_x*N).*chebfun.nufft(c,tilde_x/(2*pi)); %\tilde{F}*c

An alternative algorithm that has even faster execution times is available in the fiNUFFT package
[
5
]
.

We now show that
\tilde{F}
and
F
are relatively close in the sense of their distance in the spectral norm. Our proof follows from a similar strategy to the proof of Kadec’s theorem
[
34
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\left|\delta_{j}\right|\leq\alpha<1/4
for all
-N\leq j\leq N
in
eq.

6
, then

\left\|F-\tilde{F}\right\|_{2}\leq\varphi_{\alpha}\|F\|_{2},\qquad\varphi_{\alpha}=1-\cos(\pi\alpha)+\sin(\pi\alpha),

(7)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the spectral norm.

Proof.

Since
\|F-\tilde{F}\|_{2}=\|F^{\top}-\tilde{F}^{\top}\|_{2}
and
\|F\|_{2}=\|F^{\top}\|_{2}
, where superscript ‘
\top
’ denotes the matrix transpose, we prove that
\|F^{\top}-\tilde{F}^{\top}\|_{2}\leq\varphi_{\alpha}\|F^{\top}\|_{2}
. Let
\underline{c}=(c_{-N},\ldots,c_{N})^{\top}
be a vector of unit length so that
\|\underline{c}\|_{2}=1
. We have

(F^{\top}-\tilde{F}^{\top})\underline{c}=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}e^{-ij\underline{t}}\circ\left(\underline{\mathbbm{1}}-e^{-i\delta_{j}\underline{t}}\right)c_{j},

(8)

where
\underline{t}=(t_{-N},\ldots,t_{N})^{\top}
with
t_{k}=2\pi k/(2N+1)
, ‘
\circ
’ is the Hadamard product denoting entry-by-entry multiplication between vectors,
\underline{\mathbbm{1}}
is the column vector of all ones, and the exponential function of a vector is applied entrywise. As in
[
34
]
, for each
-N\leq j\leq N
, we write

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbbm{1}}-e^{-i\delta_{j}\underline{t}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\left(1-\frac{\sin(\pi\delta_{j})}{\pi\delta_{j}}\right)\underline{\mathbbm{1}}}_{A_{j}}+\underbrace{\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2\delta_{j}\sin(\pi\delta_{j})}{\pi(\ell^{2}-\delta_{j}^{2})}\cos(\ell\underline{t})}_{B_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\underbrace{i\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}2\delta_{j}\cos(\pi\delta_{j})}{\pi(\ell-1/2)^{2}-\pi\delta_{j}^{2}}\sin\left(\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)\underline{t}\right)}_{C_{j}},

where
\sin
and
\cos
of a vector is applied entrywise. By substituting this expression into
eq.

8
, we can bound
\|(F^{\top}-\tilde{F}^{\top})\underline{c}\|_{2}
by separately bounding
\sum_{j=-N}^{N}A_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j},\sum_{j=-N}^{N}B_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j},
and
\sum_{j=-N}^{N}C_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}
.

Bounding
\mathbf{\sum_{j=-N}^{N}A_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}}
.
Since
\left|\delta_{j}\right|\leq\alpha<1/4
, from elementary calculus, we have that
\max_{j}\left(1-\sin(\pi\delta_{j})/(\pi\delta_{j})\right)\leq 1-\sin(\pi\alpha)/(\pi\alpha)
. Also, since
\{e^{-ij\underline{t}}\}_{j=-N}^{N}
is an orthogonal set and
A_{j}
is a constant vector,
\{A_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\}_{j=-N}^{N}
is a set of orthogonal vectors. Hence, by the Pythagorean Theorem, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}A_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\!=\!\!\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!\left\lVert A_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\!\left(1-\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}\right)^{2}\!\!\!\!\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!\left\lVert e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

(9)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}\right)^{2}\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left(1-\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}\right)^{2}\!\|F^{\top}\|_{2}^{2}.

Bounding
\mathbf{\sum_{j=-N}^{N}B_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}}
.
Let
D_{\ell,j}=\left((-1)^{\ell}2\delta_{j}\sin(\pi\delta_{j})\right)/\left(\pi(\ell^{2}-\delta_{j}^{2})\right)
. Since
|D_{\ell,j}|=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-2})
and
\left|\cos(\ell t_{k})e^{-ijt_{k}}c_{j}\right|\leq 1
, we have by the Fubini–Tonelli Theorem that

\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!B_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}=\!\!\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}D_{\ell,j}\cos(\ell\underline{t})\right]\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!\left[D_{\ell,j}\cos(\ell\underline{t})\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right].

Define
\underline{v}^{\ell}=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left[D_{\ell,j}\cos(\ell\underline{t})\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right]
. Since
|\cos(\ell t_{k})e^{-ijt_{k}}c_{j}|\leq 1
, there exists
C>0
, independent of
\ell
, such that
\left\lVert\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}\leq C\max_{j}\left|D_{\ell,j}\right|
. Since
\max_{j}\left|D_{\ell,j}\right|=\mathcal{O}(\ell^{-2})
, we have
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}<\infty
. Hence, by Minkowski’s inequality for integrals, we have
\left\lVert\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}
and

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}B_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\underline{v}^{\ell}\right\rVert_{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\cos(\ell\underline{t})\circ\sum_{j=-N}^{N}D_{\ell,j}e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\cos(\ell\underline{t})\right\rVert_{\infty}\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}D_{\ell,j}e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\max_{j}\left|D_{\ell,j}\right|\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\|F^{\top}\|_{2}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\max_{j}\left|D_{\ell,j}\right|,

where (
*
) follows by applying Hölder’s inequality and (
**
) holds due to the orthogonality of
\{e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\}_{j=-N}^{N}
.
Since
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}2\alpha/[\pi(\ell^{2}-\alpha^{2})]
is the partial fraction expansion of
[1/(\pi\alpha)-\cot(\pi\alpha)]
and
|D_{\ell,j}|
is maximized when
|\delta_{j}|=\alpha
, we have

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\max_{j}\left|D_{\ell,j}\right|\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\alpha\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi(\ell^{2}-\alpha^{2})}=\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}-\cos(\pi\alpha).

Hence, we find that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}B_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha)}{\pi\alpha}-\cos(\pi\alpha)\right)\|F^{\top}\|_{2}.

(10)

Bounding
\mathbf{\sum_{j=-N}^{N}C_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}}
.
Let
E_{\ell,j}\!=\!\!\left((-1)^{\ell}2\delta_{j}\!\cos(\pi\delta_{j})\right)\!/\!\!\left(\pi[(\ell-1/2)^{2}\!-\delta_{j}^{2}]\right)
. Then, we have that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!C_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}\!\!\leq\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert\sin\!\left(\!\left(\ell-\frac{1}{2}\right)\!\underline{t}\right)\!\circ\!\!\!\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\!\!E_{\ell,j}e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}\!\!\!\leq\!\|F^{\top}\|_{2}\!\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\!\max_{j}\left|E_{\ell,j}\right|.

Since
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}2\alpha/(\pi[(\ell-1/2)^{2}-\alpha^{2}])
is the partial fraction expansion of
\tan(\pi\alpha)
and
|E_{\ell,j}|
is maximized when
|\delta_{j}|=\alpha
, we find that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\max_{j}\left|E_{\ell,j}\right|\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\alpha\cos(\pi\alpha)}{\pi((\ell-1/2)^{2}-\alpha^{2})}=\sin(\pi\alpha).

Hence, we conclude that

\left\lVert\sum_{j=-N}^{N}C_{j}\circ e^{-ij\underline{t}}c_{j}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sin(\pi\alpha)\|F^{\top}\|_{2}.

(11)

The statement of the theorem follows by combining
eq.

9
,
eq.

10
, and
eq.

11
.

Since
F/\sqrt{2N+1}
is a unitary matrix, we find that
\|F\|_{2}=\sqrt{2N+1}
. Therefore,
theorem

1
shows that
\|\tilde{F}-F\|_{2}\leq\varphi_{\alpha}\sqrt{2N+1}
. It is not immediately obvious that
theorem

1
is a discrete analogue of Kadec’s theorem. We can write
\tilde{F}=F+E
with
\|E\|_{2}\leq\varphi_{\alpha}\|F\|_{2}
, so we have

\|\tilde{F}\|_{2}\leq(1+\varphi_{\alpha})\|F\|_{2}.

(12)

Moreover, since all the singular values of
F
are
\sqrt{2N+1}
, for
0\leq\alpha<1/4
(as this ensures that
\varphi_{\alpha}<1
) we find the following bound on the inverse NUDFT by Weyl’s inequality:

\|\tilde{F}^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{2}-\left\lVert E\right\rVert_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{(1-\varphi_{\alpha})\|F\|_{2}}.

(13)

Hence,
theorem

1
gives the following discrete version of Kadec’s theorem, which is an analogue of the frame bound for Riesz bases in sampling theory.

Corollary 2
.

Under the same assumptions as
theorem

1
, we have

(1-\varphi_{\alpha})^{2}\|\underline{c}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2N+1}\|\tilde{F}\underline{c}\|_{2}^{2}\leq(1+\varphi_{\alpha})^{2}\|\underline{c}\|_{2}^{2},\qquad\underline{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{2N+1},

(14)

where
\varphi_{\alpha}
is given in
eq.

7
.

Proof.

The inequalities follow immediately from
eq.

12
,
eq.

13
, and the definition of the spectral norm.

When
\alpha\geq 1/4
, we believe that there is no constant
C_{1}>0
that is independent of
N
such that
C_{1}\|\underline{c}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2N+1}\|\tilde{F}\underline{c}\|_{2}^{2}
. This is because Levinson has showed that
\left\{e^{i\lambda_{k}x}\right\}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
does not always form a Riesz basis when
|\lambda_{k}-k|\geq 1/4

[
18
]
. Instead, since
\tilde{F}^{-1}
exists for any perturbed nodes with
1/4\leq\alpha<1/2
(as
\tilde{F}
is a Vandermonde matrix), there must be a
C_{1}>0
that depends on
N
such that
C_{1}\rightarrow 0
as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore,
\alpha=1/4
is less of a significant threshold in the discrete setting than in sampling theory. From
[

Interpolation at unevenly spaced samples

At equally spaced nodes, trigonometric interpolation enjoys rapid convergence to functions. Suppose that
f:[-\pi,\pi)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a continuous periodic function and that

is its trigonometric interpolant in

at the equally spaced nodes

One wonders if trigonometric interpolants at perturbed equally spaced nodes enjoy the same kind of rapid convergence. When

in the convergence rate via the MZ inequality in

Theorem 5
.

Let
f:[-\pi,\pi)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a continuous periodic function and
0\leq\alpha<1/4
. For any
\alpha
-perturbed nodes of size
2N+1

\left\lVert f-\tilde{q}_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2N+1}}{\cos(\pi\alpha)-\sin(\pi\alpha)}\right)\min_{q\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

Proof.

First, note that for any trigonometric polynomial
q\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
we have by Young’s inequality and Parseval’s Theorem that

\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\lVert p*q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\lVert p\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{2N+1}{2\pi}}\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

where
(p\ast q)(x)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}p(x-s)q(s)ds
and
p(x)=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}e^{ijx}
. If
\tilde{x}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{N}
are the perturbed nodes and
q
is any polynomial in
\mathcal{T}_{N}
, then, by
eq.

15
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert f-\tilde{q}_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\left\lVert q-\tilde{q}_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\sqrt{\frac{2N+1}{2\pi}}\left\lVert q-\tilde{q}_{N}\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}+\frac{1}{1-\varphi_{\alpha}}\sqrt{\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-f(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{1-\varphi_{\alpha}}\sqrt{2N+1}\right)\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},

where
\varphi_{\alpha}
is given by
eq.

7

q
to be the best fit polynomial in
\mathcal{T}_{N}
to

Using

(18)

respectively. This means that with unevenly spaced samples, one can safely use

as a surrogate for

One can easily do unevenly spaced trigonometric interpolation in Chebfun via the

To analyze the stability of quadrature rules at
\alpha
-perturbed nodes further, we take a look at the absolute sum of the quadrature weights as this is the absolute condition number of computing the quadrature rule. Since we are considering exact quadrature rules, there is a close connection between the NUDFT matrix,
\tilde{F}

6

\underline{\tilde{w}}=(\tilde{w}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{w}_{N})^{\top}

\alpha
-perturbed nodes are simple to compute:

tilde_x = (-N:N)’*h + alpha*(2*rand(2*N+1,1)-1)*h; % perturbed nodes
tF = exp(-1i*tilde_x*(-N:N)); % NUDFT matrix
e0 = zeros(2*N+1,1); e0(N+1)=1; w = tF’\(2*pi*e0); % quad wts

Let

\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|\tilde{w}_{j}\right|\leq\frac{2\pi}{\cos(\pi\alpha)-\sin(\pi\alpha)},

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|\tilde{w}_{j}\right|

6
Oversampling with unevenly spaced samples

Most of the results in the paper so far assumed that
0\leq\alpha<1/4
as MZ inequalities may not hold for all
\alpha
-perturbed nodes when
\alpha\geq 1/4
(see
proposition

8
). In this section, we briefly discuss oversampling, where one assumes that there are more unevenly spaced samples than the number of coefficients in the desired trigonometric approximant. A small amount of oversampling allows us to
use MZ inequalities to
extend our results to the
1/4\leq\alpha<1/2
regime. The oversampling regime is studied in approximation theory
[
15
]
, including the higher dimensional setting
[
22
]
. Here, we restate existing results in terms of our notation and present sharper statements in one dimension.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1/2
and
\varepsilon>0
be an oversampling rate. Suppose one has
\alpha
-perturbed samples of
f
at
\tilde{x}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{N}
. Since we are oversampling, we want to find a trigonometric polynomial of degree
\leq n
(see
eq.

3
) that “fits” the samples of the function as best as possible, where
n
is an integer such that
n\leq\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)N\rfloor
. Since there is typically no hope of a trigonometric polynomial interpolant, a common approach is to compute an approximant by solving a least squares problem. To do this, we first construct a tall-skinny NUDFT matrix given by

\tilde{F}_{jk}=e^{-i\tilde{x}_{j}k},\qquad-N\leq j\leq N,\quad-n\leq k\leq n

(26)

and then solve
\tilde{F}\underline{c}=\underline{f}
for the vector
\underline{c}
, where
\underline{f}=\left(f(\tilde{x}_{-N}),\ldots,f(\tilde{x}_{N})\right)^{\top}
. The least-squares approximant to
f
can be formed as

\tilde{q}_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=-n}^{n}c_{k}e^{-ikx}.

(27)

For notational consistency, we continue to assume that there is an odd number of samples; however, all results in this section hold for an even number of samples too.

6.1
Oversampled Marcinkiewicz–Zygmund inequalities

In
section

2
, we saw the MZ inequality is closely related to the NUDFT condition number via Parseval’s theorem. Below, we state the MZ inequality in the oversampling case and then discuss its consequences for the condition number of
\tilde{F}
in
eq.

26
.

Let
0\leq\alpha<1/2
and
\varepsilon>0
. If
\tilde{x}_{-N},\ldots,\tilde{x}_{N}
are
\alpha
-perturbed nodes and
n=\lfloor(1-\varepsilon)N\rfloor
. Then, it is easy to verify that

\liminf_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left(\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\min_{x\in[-\pi,\pi)}\left|\{\tilde{x}_{j}\}_{j=-N}^{N}\cap(x,x+R/n)\right|}{R}\right)\geq\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon)\pi}.

When this condition is satisfied, Ortega-Cerdà and Saludes
[
23
]
proved that the MZ inequality in
eq.

24
holds for any
1\leq r<\infty
and
q\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
, where the constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are independent of
N,n,
and
\tilde{x}_{j}
, but possibly depend on
\alpha,\varepsilon
, and
r
.

6.2
The condition number of a rectangular NUDFT matrix

Let
\tilde{F}
be the NUDFT matrix given in
eq.

26
. Then, for any vector
\underline{c}\in\mathbb{C}^{2n+1}
, we can rewrite
eq.

24
for
r=2
using Parseval’s theorem to obtain

2\pi C_{1}\left\lVert\underline{c}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2N+1}\left\lVert\tilde{F}\underline{c}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq 2\pi C_{2}\left\lVert\underline{c}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},

where
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants that only depend on
\alpha
and
\varepsilon
. This immediately gives us a bound on the spectral norm of
\tilde{F}
, i.e.,
\|\tilde{F}\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{2\pi C_{2}(2N+1)}
. We also have

\left\lVert\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\underline{f}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi C_{1}(2N+1)}\left\lVert\tilde{F}\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\underline{f}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2\pi C_{1}(2N+1)}\left\lVert\underline{f}\right\rVert_{2}^{2},\qquad\underline{f}\in\mathbb{C}^{2N+1},

where
\tilde{F}^{\dagger}
is the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of
\tilde{F}
. Here, the second inequality follows from the fact that
\|\tilde{F}\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\|_{2}=1
. We find that
\|\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\|_{2}\leq 1/\sqrt{2\pi C_{1}(2N+1)}
and hence, the condition number of
\tilde{F}
is bounded independently of
N
and
n
:

\kappa_{2}(\tilde{F})=\left\lVert\tilde{F}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\tilde{F}^{\dagger}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}}.

We find this a remarkable bound because as soon as there is a small amount of oversampling, the condition number of
\tilde{F}
can be bounded independently of
N
and
n
, even in the
1/4\leq\alpha<1/2
regime. In contrast, we believe that when
N=n
, the condition number of
\tilde{F}
grows slowly with
N
when
1/4\leq\alpha<1/2
.

6.3
Convergence of least-squares approximation at unevenly spaced samples

Let
f:[-\pi,\pi)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a continuous periodic function and let
\tilde{q}_{n}
be the least-squares approximant in
eq.

27
. Then, by definition of
\tilde{q}_{n}
, we have that
\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|f(\tilde{x}_{j})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|f(\tilde{x}_{j})-q(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}
for any

. Hence, we find that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=-N}^{N}(\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-f(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|+\left|f(\tilde{x}_{j})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}(\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-f(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}+\left|f(\tilde{x}_{j})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2}+2\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-f(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|\left|f(\tilde{x}_{j})-\tilde{q}_{n}(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\left|q(\tilde{x}_{j})-f(\tilde{x}_{j})\right|^{2},

where the last inequality follows from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. By a similar argument to the proof of
theorem

5
, we find that

\left\|f-\tilde{q}_{n}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{2(2n+1)}{C_{1}\pi}}\right)\min_{q\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\|f-q\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The reader can now use their favorite bounds on
\min_{q\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\|f-q\|_{L^{\infty}}
(see
eqs.

16
and
17
).

6.4
Nonexact quadrature rules

There also exists a quadrature rule that is exact for all
q\in\mathcal{T}_{n}
such that
[
15

\left|\tilde{I}_{n}(f)-I(f)\right|\leq 2\pi\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}}}\right)\min_{q\in\mathcal{T}_{n}}\left\lVert f-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},\quad\tilde{I}_{n}(f)=\sum_{j=-N}^{N}\tilde{w}_{j}f(\tilde{x}_{j})

are the least-squares solution of the underdetermined system

, where
\underline{e}_{0}
is the zero vector except at its central entry is

.

From numerical experiments, we observer that while oversampling usually avoids negative weights and also reduces the absolute sum of the weights, it is much more unpredictable as

and

We know that oversampling by a minimal amount gives us good rates of convergence for any
\alpha<1/2

for higher dimensions. A translation of their one dimensional technique states that if

d_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{i\tilde{x}_{j}},e^{i\tilde{x}_{k_{i}}})=mh
and
d_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{i\tilde{x}_{j}},e^{i\tilde{x}_{k_{3-i}}})=mh-2\alpha h
for
i=1
or
2
. This gives us

\displaystyle\left|\frac{A}{B}\right|\leq\frac{\sin\left(\!\frac{dh+2\alpha h}{2}\!\right)\prod_{m=1,m\neq d}^{N}\sin^{2}\left(\!\frac{mh+\alpha h}{2}\!\right)}{\prod_{m=1}^{N}\sin\left(\!\frac{mh-2\alpha h}{2}\!\right)\sin\left(\!\frac{mh}{2}\!\right)}\leq\frac{1}{\sin\left(\!\frac{dh}{2}\!\right)}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\!\frac{mh+\alpha h}{2}\!\right)}{\sin\left(\!\frac{mh-2\alpha h}{2}\!\right)\sin\left(\!\frac{mh}{2}\!\right)},

as desired.

It is worth observing that

Lemma 12
.

Using the same notation as
lemma

11
, if
\ell_{j}
is the

\tilde{x}_{j}
associated with
\{\tilde{x}_{k}\}_{k=-N}^{N}

\left|\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})+\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})\right|\leq\frac{\pi\alpha}{d}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{(m+\alpha)^{2}}{(m-2\alpha)m},

(33)

S^{j}_{d}=\{k_{1},k_{2}\}

Proof.

Let
d=d(j,k)
and suppose that
1\leq d\leq N
. For
i=1,2

\displaystyle\ell_{j}(x_{k_{i}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{i}}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{i}}-\tilde{x}_{k_{i}}}{2}\right)\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j,k_{i}}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{i}}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}.

By
lemma

11
, we find that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j,k_{i}}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{i}}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}\right|\leq\frac{\pi}{dh}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{(mh+\alpha h)^{2}}{(mh-2\alpha h)(mh)}=\frac{\pi}{dh}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{(m+\alpha)^{2}}{(m-2\alpha)m},

where we need the fact that
(2/\pi)x\leq\sin x
for all

and the fact that
\sin y/\sin x\leq y/x
for all
0<x\leq y<\pi
. We now prove

by considering the cases
\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})\leq 0

Case 1:
\mathbf{\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})\leq 0}

Since

\displaystyle\left|\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})+\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})\right|\leq\max\{\left|\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})\right|,\left|\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})\right|\}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\max_{i=1,2}\left[\frac{d_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{ix_{k_{i}}},e^{i\tilde{x}_{k_{i}}})}{2}\right]\frac{\pi}{dh}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{(m+\alpha)^{2}}{(m-2\alpha)m}\leq\frac{\pi\alpha}{d}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{(m+\alpha)^{2}}{(m-2\alpha)m},

where we used the fact that
\sin(x)\leq x
for all
0\leq x\leq\pi/2

Case 2:
\mathbf{\ell_{j}(x_{k_{1}})\ell_{j}(x_{k_{2}})>0}

We claim that

\frac{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j,k_{1}}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{1}}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}\frac{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j,k_{2}}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{x_{k_{2}}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}{\prod_{m=-N,m\neq j}^{N}\sin\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{j}-\tilde{x}_{m}}{2}\right)}<0.

The claim follows because if we assume, without loss of generality,

Theorem 13

We have

g(N^{\rm neg}_{\alpha})>\frac{1}{\pi\alpha},\qquad g(N):=\prod_{m=1}^{N}\left[\frac{(m+\alpha)^{2}}{(m-2\alpha)m}\right]\left[\sum_{d=1}^{N}\frac{1}{d}\right]

34

implies that a quadrature rule at
\alpha

\alpha

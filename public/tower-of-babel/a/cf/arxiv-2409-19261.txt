Moduli stabilization and light axion by Siegel modular forms Preprint: EPHOU-24-009 KYUSHU-HET-298 CTPU-PTC-24-21
Shuta Funakoshi 1, Junichiro Kawamura 2, Tatsuo Kobayashi 3, Kaito Nasu 3 , and, Hajime Otsuka 1

Abstract
We discuss the stabilization of multiple moduli by utilizing Siegel modular forms in the framework of S ​ p ​ ( 2 ​ g , ℤ ) Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z}) modular invariant theories. We derive the stationary conditions at CP-conserving fixed points for a generic modular- and CP-invariant scalar potential. The stabilization of multiple moduli is explicitly demonstrated in S ​ p ​ ( 4 , ℤ ) Sp(4,\mathbb{Z}) and S ​ p ​ ( 6 , ℤ ) Sp(6,\mathbb{Z}) modular invariant scalar potentials. Furthermore, it turns out that there exists a light axion when the moduli are stabilized nearby a fixed point.

2.1
Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})
modular symmetry and CP symmetry

We start with the Siegel modular group
\Gamma_{g}:=Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})

whose generators are given by

\displaystyle S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\mathbbm{1}_{g}\\
-\mathbbm{1}_{g}&0\end{array}\right)\,,\qquad T_{m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathbbm{1}_{g}&B_{m}\\
0&\mathbbm{1}_{g}\end{array}\right)\,.

Here,
\{B_{m}\}
with
m=1,2,...,2g-1
is a basis for the
g\times g
integer symmetric matrices
whose explicit form is shown later for
g=2,3
.
The moduli fields transform as

\displaystyle\tau\xrightarrow{T_{m}}\tau+B_{m}\,,\qquad\tau\xrightarrow{S}-\tau^{-1}\,.

(2.5)

Here,
\tau
is
g\times g
matrix-valued, i.e.
\tau_{ij}=s_{ij}+it_{ij}
.
The moduli space of
\tau
is described in the Siegel upper half plane
Siegel:1943
:

\displaystyle{\cal H}_{g}=\{\tau\in GL(g,\mathbb{C})\ |\ \tau^{t}=\tau,\quad{\rm Im}(\tau)>0\},

(2.6)

where
GL(g,\mathbb{C})
is a set of
g\times g
matrices with complex values.
The modular transformation maps any value of
\tau
to the following fundamental domain
Siegel:1943
:

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{g}=\left\{\tau\in{\cal H}_{g}\ \left|\quad\begin{array}[]{l}h^{T}{\rm Im}(\tau)h\geq{\rm Im}(\tau)_{ii}\quad\forall h=(h_{1},...,h_{g})\in\mathbb{Z}^{g}\quad{\rm for}\quad 1\leq i\leq g\\
{\rm Im}(\tau)_{i,i+1}\geq 0\quad{\rm for}\quad 0\leq i\leq g-1\\
|{\rm det}(C\tau+D)|\geq 1\quad{\rm for}\quad\forall\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{g}\\
|{\rm Re}(\tau)_{ij}|\leq 1/2\end{array}\right.\right\},

where
\{h_{1},...,h_{g}\}
are coprime in the first line.
The Siegel modular group has finite modular groups.
By introducing the principal congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{g}(n)
of level
n
with
n
being a positive integer:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{g}(n)=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_{g}~|~\gamma\equiv\mathbbm{1}_{g}\quad{\rm mod}~n\},

(2.11)

one can define the finite Siegel modular group as the quotient group
\Gamma_{g,n}:=\Gamma_{g}/\Gamma_{g}(n)
.

The CP transformation of
\tau
:

\displaystyle\tau\xrightarrow{{\rm CP}}-\bar{\tau}

(2.12)

is regarded as an outer automorphism of the modular symmetry, leading to
the extended modular symmetry
\Gamma_{g}\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{\rm CP}

Ishiguro:2020nuf
;
Ding:2021iqp
.

Next, we examine extrema ensured by

For the

-invariant scalar potential:

\displaystyle V(\tau,\bar{\tau})=V(-\bar{\tau},-\tau)=V(\bar{\tau}^{-1},\tau^{-1}),

we differentiate both sides with respect to
\tau
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})

\displaystyle=+\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial u_{kl}}{\partial\tau_{ij}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(\bar{u},u)=-\sum_{k,l}(\tau^{-1})_{ki}(\tau^{-1})_{jl}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(\bar{u},u),

(2.23)

with

\bar{u}=(\bar{\tau})^{-1}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau^{-1})_{kl}=-\sum_{m,n}(\tau^{-1})_{km}\frac{\partial\tau_{mn}}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau^{-1})_{nl}=-(\tau^{-1})_{ki}(\tau^{-1})_{jl}.

\displaystyle u=\tau^{-1}=\bar{\tau}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\bar{\tau}\tau=\tau\bar{\tau}=\mathbbm{1}_{g},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k,l}(\tau^{-1})_{ki}(\tau^{-1})_{jl}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(\bar{u},u)\biggl|_{u_{kl}=\bar{\tau}_{kl},\bar{u}_{kl}=\tau_{kl}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k,l}\bar{\tau}_{ki}\,\bar{\tau}_{jl}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(\bar{u},u)\biggl|_{u_{kl}=\bar{\tau}_{kl},\bar{u}_{kl}=\tau_{kl}}.

(2.26)

From Eq. (
2.21

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial s_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})\Bigl|_{s_{ij}\equiv 0}=\left[\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})+\frac{\partial V}{\partial\bar{\tau}_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})\right]_{s_{ij}\equiv 0}=0,

(2.27)

with

and
\equiv
is understood as mod
1/2

By combining Eqs. (
2.26
) and (
2.27
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}=\sum_{k,l}\bar{\tau}_{ik}\bar{\tau}_{jl}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{kl}},

(2.28)

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{11}}\\
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{12}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{nn}}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\tau}_{11}\bar{\tau}_{11}&\bar{\tau}_{11}\bar{\tau}_{12}&\cdots&\bar{\tau}_{n1}\bar{\tau}_{1n}\\
\bar{\tau}_{11}\bar{\tau}_{21}&\bar{\tau}_{11}\bar{\tau}_{22}&\cdots&\bar{\tau}_{n1}\bar{\tau}_{2n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\bar{\tau}_{1n}\bar{\tau}_{n1}&\bar{\tau}_{1n}\bar{\tau}_{n2}&\cdots&\bar{\tau}_{nn}\bar{\tau}_{nn}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{11}}\\
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{12}}\\
\vdots\\
\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{nn}}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.29)

Note that the above matrix is a

matrix.

\tau^{-1}=\overline{\tau}

\mathrm{CP}
and modular invariance

\mathrm{CP}
-conserving points satisfying Eq. (

\displaystyle V(\tau,\bar{\tau})=V(-\bar{\tau},-\tau)=V(\gamma\tau,\overline{\gamma\tau}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial u_{kl}}{\partial\tau_{ij}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(u,\bar{u})=\sum_{k,l}\frac{\partial(A\tau+B)_{km}(C\tau+D)^{-1}_{ml}}{\partial\tau_{ij}}\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(u,\bar{u})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k,l}A_{ki}(C\tau+D)^{-1}_{jl}-\sum_{k,l,n}u_{kn}C_{ni}(C\tau+D)^{-1}_{jl}\right)\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(u,\bar{u}),

,

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m,n}((C\tau+D)^{-1})_{km}\frac{\partial(C\tau+D)_{mn}}{\partial\tau_{ij}}((C\tau+D)^{-1})_{nl}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{m,n,p}((C\tau+D)^{-1})_{km}C_{mp}\delta_{pi}\delta_{nj}((C\tau+D)^{-1})_{nl}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V}{\partial\tau_{ij}}(\tau,\bar{\tau})

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k,l}A_{ki}(C\tau+D)^{-1}_{jl}-\sum_{k,l,n}u_{kn}C_{ni}(C\tau+D)^{-1}_{jl}\right)\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{kl}}(u,\bar{u})\biggl|_{u_{kl}=-\bar{\tau}_{kl},\bar{u}_{kl}=-\tau_{kl}}

3.2
Moduli stabilization by single modular form

In this section, we study the supersymmetric effective action in the framework of

S_{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
modular symmetry.
In addition to the moduli fields, we introduce a chiral superfield
X
whose effective Kähler potential and superpotential in units of the reduced Planck mass are described by

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=-h\ln\left(\det(-i\tau+ i \Bar{\tau})\right)+\frac{|X|^{2}}{\det(-i\tau+ i \Bar{\tau})^{k_{X}}}\,,

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{X}^{2}Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}(\tau)X,

where

The explicit forms of the singlet modular forms are shown

The scalar potential is given by

\displaystyle V=e^{K}\left(K^{I\bar{J}}D_{I}WD_{\bar{J}}\bar{W}-3|W|^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{X}W

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{X}^{2}Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}=0,\quad\partial_{\tau_{i}}W=\Lambda_{X}^{2}(\partial_{\tau_{i}}Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)})X=0,\quad W=0.

\tau_{1,3}

\displaystyle\langle Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}(\tau)\rangle=\langle X\rangle=0.

(3.25)

Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}
are minima.
Since the modular forms tend to have zero points at the fixed points,

In general,

\tau_{h^{*}}
of the

\displaystyle Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}(\tau_{h^{*}})=Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}(h_{\ast}\tau_{h^{*}})=\bigl[{\rm det}(C_{h^{*}}\tau_{h^{*}}+D_{h^{*}})]^{k}Y_{\mathbf{1}}^{(k)}(\tau_{h^{*}}),

\displaystyle f_{3}:=M_{p}\sqrt{\frac{h\left\{(\mathrm{Im}\,\tau_{1})^{2}+(\mathrm{Im}\,\tau_{3})^{2}\right\}}{\left\{(\mathrm{Im}\,\tau_{1})^{2}-(\mathrm{Im}\,\tau_{3})^{2}\right\}^{2}}}\simeq M_{p}\sqrt{\frac{4h}{3}}.

\displaystyle m_{\sigma}^{2}=\frac{2\lambda_{X}\Lambda_{X}^{2}}{f_{3}^{2}},\quad m_{a}^{2}=\epsilon_{Q}^{2}(-f_{X}^{\prime\prime}(\phi)+2f_{Q}^{\prime\prime}(\phi))\frac{m_{\sigma}^{2}}{2},

Conclusion

In this paper, we discussed the stabilization of moduli fields by Siegel modular forms
in the
Sp(2g,\mathbb{{Z}})
modular invariant theories.
For a generic modular- and CP-invariant scalar potential,
we derive the stationary conditions at fixed points under the modular and CP symmetries.
We demonstrated that the CP-conserving fixed points of
Sp(4,\mathbb{Z})

are indeed stationary points.
The obtained conditions Eqs. (
2.36
) and (
2.38
)
can be applicable to the fixed points
in a generic
Sp(2g,\mathbb{Z})
modular symmetry.

For concreteness,
we proposed the stabilization mechanism of multi two moduli fields by utilizing
Siegel modular forms under
S^{4}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
of
Sp(4,\mathbb{Z})
in Sec.
3

and
\tilde{\Delta}(96)
of
Sp(6,\mathbb{Z})
in Appendix
B
.
By introducing matter fields in a modular-invariant way,
we obtain the modular-invariant scalar potential.
It turns out that the stabilization of all the moduli fields requires the deviation from fixed points of
\tau
in the Siegel upper half plane.
The coexistence of two Siegel modular forms leads to the stabilization of all the moduli fields in a supersymmetric Minkowski minimum at the classical level, but it requires a specific choice of Siegel modular forms.
When some of the moduli fields are not stabilized at the classical level, quantum corrections from matter fields
may stabilize remaining moduli fields.

We demonstrated a stabilization scenario
in which the tree-level potential is stabilized
at one of the fixed points,
(\tau_{1},\tau_{3})=(\omega,0)

with the massless mode
\sim\tau_{3}
,
and then the radiative correction generates the mass of
\tau_{3}
.
The radiative potential deviates the location of the minimum
due to its negative mass squared at the fixed point.
Interestingly, the phase direction is approximately massless
similarly to the Nambu-Goldstone mode of the Higgs potential in the SM,
but with slight phase dependence at
\order{\abs{\dta_3}^6}
.
There will be phenomenological applications of

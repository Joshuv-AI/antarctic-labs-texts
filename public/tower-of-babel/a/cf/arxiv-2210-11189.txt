L 2 L^{2} -stability near equilibrium for the 4 4 waves kinetic equation
Angeliki Menegaki

Abstract
. We consider the four waves spatial homogeneous kinetic equation arising in wave turbulence theory. We study the long-time behaviour and existence of solutions around the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium solutions. For cut-off’d frequencies, we show that for dispersion relations weakly perturbed around the quadratic case, the linearized operator around the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibria is coercive. We then pass to the fully nonlinear operator, showing an L 2 L^{2} - stability for initial data close to Rayleigh-Jeans.



1.
Introduction

We aim to study the spectral properties of the linearized
kinetic wave equation
or
phonon Boltzmann equation
. According to the theory of wave turbulence, the so-called kinetic wave equation is obtained as a kinetic limit of time-reversible wave dispersive equations where many waves interact weakly non-linearly and it predicts the evolution of the average distribution of energy
in the frequency space.
Put differently, in analogy with the kinetic theory of particles, one can directly compare the kinetic wave equation with the usual Boltzmann equation for dilute gases, obtained in the Boltzmann-Grad limit, describing the evolution of the distribution of particles in the phase space.

This wave kinetic equation has dynamical properties similar to the Boltzmann equation as an
H
-theorem holds and the mass, momentum, and energy are conserved quantities. The system is driven towards the equilibrium solution which is the Rayleigh-Jeans distribution. In addition to this equilibrium stationary state, it was shown by Zakharov
[
Zak66
]
that also non-equilibrium steady states exist, the so-called Kolmogorov–Zakharov solutions. These are characterised by the presence of non-zero fluxes
where energy is transferred through direct cascades from small to large frequencies and mass through inverse cascades from large to small frequencies.
For more details we refer to the books
[
Zak98
,
Naz11
,
NR11
]
.

The research area of wave turbulence theory has now attracted a lot of attention due to the wide range of practical applications. To mention a few the wave kinetic equation appears in the studies of plasma waves, surface capillary and gravity waves
[
Zak99
,
Has62
]
, nonlinear optics
[
DNPZ92
]
and elastic plates
[
DJR17
]
.

1.1.
Kinetic wave equation and properties

The space homogeneous kinetic wave equation reads as follows

\begin{split}\partial_{t}n(t,k)&=\mathcal{L}n(t,k)\\
&:=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\frac{nn_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\end{split}

(1.1)

where

\Sigma=\Sigma(k,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=k_{1}+k_{2}-k_{3}-k\ \text{and}\ \Omega=\Omega(k,k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})=\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}-\omega,

n_{i}=n(k_{i}),\omega_{i}=\omega(k_{i})
and
k\mapsto\omega(k)
is the dispersion relation. Finally
\beta
in the cross-section is a parameter originated from the nonlinearity of the dynamics in the microscopic level and in particular the value
\beta=0
corresponds to the cubic power law in the nonlinear Schrödinger equation.

From the collision operator one can easily see that the mass, momentum and energy are conserved at least formally and that the equilibrium solutions take the form for
(a,b,c)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
:

f_{\infty}(k)=\frac{1}{a+b\cdot k+c\omega},\quad a+b\cdot k+c\omega>0,\ c>0.

as the collision integral vanishes.

1.2.
Linearization around Rayleigh-Jeans equilibria

We linearize around the equilibrium solution
f_{\infty}(k)=\frac{1}{\omega+1\cdot k+1}
:

n(k)=f_{\infty}(k)(1+\varepsilon g(k)).

The new unknown
g
satisfies the following linearized equation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}g(t,k)&=\frac{f_{\infty}^{-1}}{\varepsilon}\partial_{t}n(t,k)\\
&=f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k)f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\times\\
&\times\big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g_{1}+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{3}-f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g\big]\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon).\end{split}

(1.2)

This yields the linearized operator

\begin{split}Lg(k):=&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\times\\
&\times\big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g_{1}+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{3}-f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g\big]\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}.\end{split}

(1.3)

This operator is self-adjoint on
L^{2}
and the Dirichlet form of
-L
satisfies:

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}(g):=-\langle Lg,g&\rangle_{L^{2}}=\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\times\\
&\times\big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g_{1}+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{3}-f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g\big]^{2}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}dk\\
&\geq 0\end{split}

(1.4)

which implies that the spectrum of
L
lies on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
. Its null space is

\operatorname{Ker}(L)=\operatorname{span}\{1,k,\omega\}.

1.3.
Main ideas and assumptions

The goal is (i) to study the linearized around the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium operator
L
and to show the existence of an
L^{2}
spectral gap. For this purpose we split the operator into a sum of two integral operators for which we show that one is coercive while the other is a compact operator. Then we can ensure the existence of a spectral gap due to the coercive part by Weyl’s theorem.
(ii) We control the
L^{2}
norm of the nonlinearities to show through a fixed-point argument that a mild solution of
\mathcal{L}
exists in a ball around the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium whose radius depends on the spectral gap of
L
.
Finally using all the above we show the existence of a basin of attraction of Rayleigh-Jeans, characterized by an
L^{2}
norm.
This argument is reminiscent of an argument of Grad used to study the linearized Boltzmann operator for hard potentials and it is the first time that it is used to obtain stability for the wave kinetic equation.

For these we need the following assumptions:

Cut-off for the frequencies
: Regarding the domain of the frequencies, we restrict to the domain
[0,k_{c}]
for a finite constant
k_{c}
for each component, i.e.
|k|,|k_{i}|\leq k_{c}
. This set-up is not physically motivated as normally we do not expect the frequencies to stay bounded. This cut-off assumption is however needed in order to control the singularities appearing in the collision operator. In particular to ensure the coercivity and the compactness properties of the splitting of the linearised operator. Let us stress that we impose this assumption to all the wavenumbers, so that the symmetries of the collisional operator are conserved. To picture this, see fig.
1
on the plane when the dispersion relation is quadratic and the resonance condition is interpreted as a collision among particles leading to another group of particles.

We will assume for the dispersion relation the following

Assumption 1

(Assumptions on the dispersion relation)
.

We assume the following for the dispersion relation
\omega(k)

(i)

\omega(k)=\Omega(|k|)
,
\Omega(|k|)=|k|^{2}+s(|k|)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

(ii)

There exist
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
positive finite constants with

\|s\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C_{1}\ \text{and }\ 0<C_{2}\leq\frac{\Omega^{\prime}(x)}{x}\leq C_{3},\ x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

(1.5)

(iii)

The Hessian of the perturbation
S(x)=s(|x|)
is a diagonal positive definite so that
\Lambda_{1}|z|^{2}\leq z^{T}\operatorname{Hess}S(x)z\leq\Lambda_{2}|z|^{2}
for all
z,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, for finite positive constants
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
.

That means that we consider a positive dispersion relation under the constraint that it has a quadratic growth while allowing for some oscillations as well.

State of the art

Typically there are two types of such kinetic equations in wave turbulence, namely the
3
-waves interactions and the

-waves interactions. Peierls

[
ZLF92
]
, especially after the discovery of the non-equilibrium Kolmogorov–Zakharov spectra.

Regarding its rigorous derivation, see first a proposed program in
[
Spo06
]
where the starting point is, as also suggested by Peierls, a harmonic lattice with a weakly anharmonic (non-quadratic) on-site potential, otherwise known as the FPUT chain. More recently there many important advances starting from cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation with random initial data, see
[
LS11
]
, then
[
BGHS21
,
CC20
,
DH21
]
up to the kinetic time-scale (up to errors) and a derivation reaching the kinetic time-scale
[
DH23
]
.

Concerning now the well-posedness of (
1.1
), in
[
EV13
]
under velocity-isotropy assumption and for quadratic dispersion relation, without imposing forcing the authors studied the long-time behaviour in a weighted
L^{\infty}
space. In particular in
[
EV13
]
the authors obtained global, measure valued, weak solutions and showed that as time goes to infinity condensation can happen, where all the mass is concentrated at the origin. Furthermore they studied the transfer of energy in higher frequencies and prove a stability result around the Kolmogorov-Zakharov spectrum.
We also mention the article
[
MA16
]
where existence and uniqueness of radial weak solutions to a simplified version of the
4
-waves interactions equation in a broad range of dispersion relations was studied.

More recent works on the local well-posedness for the homogeneous
4
-waves kinetic equation for a general class of dispersion relations and without the isotropy assumption are in
[
GIT20
]
, where the authors work in weighted
L^{\infty}
spaces. There, restricting to the quadratic dispersion relation, they have the estimates in weighted
L^{2}
as well. Moreover a study of space inhomogeneous
4
-waves interaction is done recently in
[
Amp22
]
where global well-posedness and preservation of sign in
L^{\infty}
is proven for a perturbation of the vacuum exploiting the dispersive properties of the transport operator.

Concerning works on the
3
-waves interactions in weak wave turbulence, it has been studied in many works for phonon interactions in anharmonic crystals which as Peierls suggested leads to the kinetic model of a nonlinear Boltzmann equation of interacting phonons and it has the same mathematical formulation as the quantum Boltzmann equation of bosons at very low temperature, see for example
[
ET15
,
CT16
,
EPV11
]
. It is also studied in the context of capillary waves,
[
NT18
]
and for oceanic internal gravity waves
[
GST17
]
. We also mention the recent study in
[
ST20
]
for acoustic waves where the authors study the energy cascades phenomenon and where a comparison with coagulation-fragmentation type of equations is considered.
Finally we mention the article
[
Esc22
]
where the author studied an approximation of the linearized
3
-waves kinetic equation around Rayleigh-Jeans equilibria. Existence, uniqueness and properties of the fundamental solution are obtained and the initial value problem is solved for integrable and locally bounded initial data.

As for the more complicated and physicaly interesting case of stability around the Kolmogorov-Zakharov non-equilibrium solutions, see the preprint
[
CDG22
]
where an

1.5.
Statements of the results

Under the previous assumptions we first prove a coercivity-result for the linearized operator.

Theorem 1
.

Let the hypothesis
1
(i),(ii) hold,
k,k_{i}\in[0,k_{c}]^{3}
for
k_{c}<\infty
and
\beta=0
. Then the linearized operator
L_{k_{c}}
is coercive, i.e.

\forall g\in L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})\cap(\operatorname{Ker}(L_{k_{c}}))^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{D}^{k_{c}}(g)\geq\lambda\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2},

for some positive constant
\lambda=\lambda(k_{c})
, where
\mathcal{D}^{k_{c}}(g)=-\langle L_{k_{c}}g,g\rangle
and
L_{k_{c}}
, defined in (
2.6
), is the operator
L
in (
1.3
) for the cut-off’d frequencies.

Definition 1.1

(Mild solutions)
.

We say that
f=f(t,k)
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a mild solution to (
1.1
) with initial data
f_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
when
f\in C^{1}((0,\infty),L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}))
, for
t>0

f(t,k)=f_{0}(k)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{L}f(s,k)\,{\rm d}s\in L^{2}.

We then pass to the fully nonlinear operator. We deal with the question of existence of mild solutions close to equilibrium and their
L^{2}
- stability.

Theorem 2
.

Let
C>0
,
\beta=0
,
\omega(k)=|k|^{2}+s(|k|)
satisfying assumption
1
(i),(ii), (iii) and
k,k_{i}\in[0,k_{c}]^{3}
. Assuming that the linearized operator has an

spectral gap
\lambda>0

for all

, and the initial data

so that
\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\sqrt{2C}}

satisfying
\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\sqrt{2C}}
.
Also, when
\Pi

\displaystyle\|\Pi^{\perp}g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t}\|\Pi^{\perp}g_{0}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+(1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t})\|\Pi g_{0}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}.

In particular as soon as
\Pi g_{0}=0
,

Remark 1

(On the assumptions)
.

Even though everything is written in
d=3
,
the results hold for all dimensions
d\geq 2
. The further assumption
\beta=0
, is needed to ensure the compactness part in the linearised operator and also for the
L^{2}

|k|,|k_{i}|\leq k_{c}
, cf fig
1
:

(i)

First, on the level of the linearised operator
L
we want
L=
coercive part
+
compact part. The cut-off assumption is used in order to prove the compactness property of the second part.
We are led therefore by technicalities to assume the cut-off to ensure compactness, but we do not expect it to be a necessary assumption for our result. Removal of the cut-off in this functional setting will be an interesting extension of this work.

(ii)

Second, for the fixed-point argument on the nonlinear level, the cut-off is used to ensure an upper bound on the equilibrium
f_{\infty}
.

Remark 2
.

Notice that even though here we show that when we are close to the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium, the dynamics remains close to it in
L^{2}
, this is not in general the case even when there is no external forcing in the system. An example of a different long-time behaviour in the isotropic case under finite mass is in
[
EV13
]
, where weak stationary solutions are characterised as Dirac measures.

Remark 3
.

Here we stress that this stability property holds only for solutions

for all

where

, which due to the conservation of mass and energy also holds for all later times, at least formally. This way we do not allow mass or energy fluctuations which makes our set-up here different than the one studied in
[

.

2.
Method of proofs

First we present the parametrisation of the resonant manifold on which the integral collision operator acts. This parametrisation for more general dispersion relations can be found in
[
GIT20
]
(also in
[
NT19
]
for the quantum Boltzmann equation).

2.1.
Parametrization of the resonant manifold

When the dispersion relation is the quadratic
\omega(k)=|k|^{2}
and the solution
n
is radial, the equation takes the form of the one-dimensional Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}

\displaystyle f(t,|k|^{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}}\frac{|k_{2}||k_{1}|\text{min}(|k|,|k_{1}|,|k_{2}|,|k_{3}|)}{|k|(|k||k_{1}||k_{2}||k_{3}|)^{\beta/2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\Big(f(|k_{2}|^{2})f(|k_{1}|^{2})\big[f(|k|^{2})+f(|k_{3}|^{2})\big]-f(|k|^{2})f(|k_{3}|^{2})\big[f(|k_{2}|^{2})+f(|k_{1}|^{2})\big]\Big)\,{\rm d}|k_{1}|\,{\rm d}|k_{2}|.

where
|k_{3}|^{2}=|k_{1}|^{2}+|k_{2}|^{2}-|k|^{2}
. Since the conservation laws known for Boltzmann hold for this isotropic model as well, the Carleman’s collision parametrization
[
SR09
,
Vil02
,
Cer88
]
can be applied in order to write the collision operator as integrals over spheres. This is helpful as then its decomposed parts have exact and convenient forms making it easier to prove the desired coercivity property for the one part and the compactness property for the rest.

Considering more general dispersion laws for the nonlinear wave kinetic equation (
1.1
), we set
z=k_{2}
and let
S_{k,k_{3}}
be the resonant manifold, i.e. the set of zeros of
\mathcal{C}(z)=\omega(k_{3}-z+k)-\omega(k)-\omega(k_{3})+\omega(z)
.
Then

\delta_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}-\omega}\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}k_{3}=\frac{\,{\rm d}\mu(z)}{|\nabla\mathcal{C}(z)|}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

where
\mu
is the surface measure on
S_{k,k_{3}}
. The derivative of
\mathcal{C}
is given by

\nabla_{z}\mathcal{C}=\frac{z-k-k_{3}}{|z-k-k_{3}|}\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z|)+\frac{z}{|z|}\Omega^{\prime}(|z|)

and the directional derivative in any direction vertical to
k+k_{3}
is positive, i.e.
\mathcal{C}(z)
is strictly increasing in any direction orthogonal to
k+k_{3}
. Indeed if
q
is a vector orthogonal to
k+k_{3}
,

\langle q,\nabla_{z}\mathcal{C}\rangle=|q|^{2}\left(\frac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z|)}{|z|}+\frac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z|)}{|k+k_{3}-z|}\right)>0.

For a parameter
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we write the vector
z
as follows:
z=\alpha(k+k_{3})+q
where
\langle q,(k+k_{3})\rangle=0
. Then since
\mathcal{C}(z)=\mathcal{C}(\alpha(k+k_{3})+q)
is a radial function in
q

we obtain that the intersection of
S_{k,k_{3}}
with the plane

P_{\alpha}:=\{\alpha(k+k_{3})+q:\ \langle q,(k+k_{3})\rangle=0\},

if it is not empty, it is a circle with radius
r_{\alpha}<\infty
and centre at
\alpha(k+k_{3})
.

We then consider a vector
(k+k_{3})^{\perp}
in the plane
P_{0}:=\{q:\langle q,k+k_{3}\rangle=0\}
and
e_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
a unit vector in
P_{0}
so that the angle between
(k+k_{3})^{\perp}
and
e_{\theta}
is
\theta
. We can then parametrise the resonant manifold as

S_{k,k_{3}}=\Big\{z(\alpha,\theta)=\alpha(k+k_{3})+r_{\alpha}e_{\theta}:\theta\in[0,2\pi],\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k,k_{3}}\Big\}

where
\mathcal{A}_{k,k_{3}}
is the set of
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
so that there is a solution to
\mathcal{C}(z)=0.

We note that we can interpret
\mathcal{C}
as a function of
\mathcal{C}(r,\alpha)
where
\partial_{r}\mathcal{C}(r,\alpha)>0
and thus through the implicit function theorem we can write
r=r_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}_{k,k_{3}}
via a smooth function that is vanishing on the boundary. We extend
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
by setting
r_{\alpha}=0
outside of the set
\mathcal{A}_{k,k_{3}}
.

For the computation of the surface area we write

\,{\rm d}\mu(z)=|\partial_{\alpha}z\times\partial_{\theta}z|\ \,{\rm d}\alpha\ \,{\rm d}\theta=|((k+k_{3})+\partial_{\alpha}r_{\alpha}e_{\theta})\times r_{\alpha}\partial_{\theta}e_{\theta}|\ \,{\rm d}\alpha\ \,{\rm d}\theta.

Since
\partial_{\theta}e_{\theta}
is orthogonal to
e_{\theta}
and to
k+k_{3}
, we write

\begin{split}\,{\rm d}\mu(z)&=|r_{\alpha}|\big|((k+k_{3})+\partial_{\alpha}r_{\alpha}e_{\theta})\big|\,{\rm d}\alpha\ \,{\rm d}\theta=\left(r_{\alpha}^{2}|k+k_{3}|^{2}+\frac{|\partial_{\alpha}(r_{\alpha}^{2})|^{2}}{4}\right)^{1/2}\,{\rm d}\alpha\ \,{\rm d}\theta.\end{split}

(2.1)

Now in order to make it explicit we want to compute
\partial_{\alpha}(r_{\alpha}^{2})
. This is written in terms of
\partial_{\alpha}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}
, for
z_{\alpha}=\alpha(k+k_{3})+q
, as
|z_{\alpha}|^{2}=\alpha^{2}|k+k_{3}|^{2}+r_{\alpha}^{2}
which implies
\partial_{\alpha}(r_{\alpha}^{2})=\partial_{\alpha}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}-2\alpha|k+k_{3}|^{2}.

We differentiate the relation
\mathcal{C}(z_{\alpha})=0
and then we take the inner product with
\partial_{\alpha}z_{\alpha}
to have

\displaystyle 0=\left\langle\partial_{\alpha}z_{\alpha},\nabla_{z}\mathcal{C}(z_{\alpha})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{\alpha}z_{\alpha},z_{\alpha}\left(\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\partial_{\alpha}z_{\alpha},\dfrac{k+k_{3}}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)\right\rangle

so that

\begin{split}\partial_{\alpha}|z_{\alpha}|^{2}&=2\frac{|k+k_{3}|^{2}}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}.\end{split}

(2.2)

Then

\begin{split}\partial_{\alpha}(r_{\alpha}^{2})&=2\frac{|k+k_{3}|^{2}}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}-2\alpha|k+k_{3}|^{2}\\
&=2|k+k_{3}|^{2}\left(\frac{(1-\alpha)\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}-\alpha\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\right).\end{split}

(2.3)

Now concerning the
|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{C}(z)|^{2}
:

\begin{split}&|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{C}(z)|^{2}=\left|\frac{z_{\alpha}-k-k_{3}}{|z_{\alpha}-k-k_{3}|}\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)+\frac{z_{\alpha}}{|z_{\alpha}|}\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)\right|^{2}\\
&=|k+k_{3}|^{2}\left|(\alpha-1)\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\alpha\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}\right|^{2}+r_{\alpha}^{2}\left|\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}\right|^{2}.\end{split}

(2.4)

Due to cancellations, using (
2.1
), (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we eventually write

\displaystyle\delta_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}-\omega}\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{\,{\rm d}\mu(z)}{|\nabla\mathcal{C}(z)|}\,{\rm d}k_{3}=\frac{|k+k_{3}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

(2.5)

We notice that since
|z_{\alpha}|^{2}=|\alpha|^{2}|k+k_{3}|^{2}+r_{\alpha}^{2}\leq k_{c}^{2}
,
|\alpha|^{2}=\frac{|z_{\alpha}|^{2}-r_{\alpha}^{2}}{|k+k_{3}|^{2}}
.

Then the nonlinear operator in (
1.1
) restricted to the domain
[0,k_{c}]^{3}
for each wavenumber, is

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}\frac{nn_{1}n_{2}n_{3}}{[|k||k_{1}||k_{2}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}\frac{n(k)n(k+k_{3}-k_{2})n(k_{2})n(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-k_{2}||k_{2}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\left(\frac{1}{n(k+k_{3}-k_{2})}+\frac{1}{n(k_{2})}-\frac{1}{n(k_{3})}-\frac{1}{n(k)}\right)\delta_{\Omega}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\Bigg\{\int_{S_{k,k_{3}}}\frac{n(k)n(k+k_{3}-z)n(z)n(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z||z||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{n(k+k_{3}-z)}+\frac{1}{n(z)}-\frac{1}{n(k_{3})}-\frac{1}{n(k)}\right)\frac{\,{\rm d}\mu(z)}{|\nabla\mathcal{C}(z)|}\Bigg\}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\Bigg\{\int_{-\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}^{2}-r_{\alpha}^{2}}}{|k+k_{3}|}}^{\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}^{2}-r_{\alpha}^{2}}}{|k+k_{3}|}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{n(k)n(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})n(z_{\alpha})n(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{n(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})}+\frac{1}{n(z_{\alpha})}-\frac{1}{n(k_{3})}-\frac{1}{n(k)}\right)\frac{|k+k_{3}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}\alpha\Bigg\}\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

2.2.
Splitting of the operator

The proof of the main results follows techniques already applied for the theory of the Boltzmann equation
[
Gra63
,
SR09
]
. The idea is to decompose the operator between a part satisfying the coercivity estimate and a compact part. We split the operator as follows:

\begin{split}&L_{k_{c}}g(k):=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k)f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\times\\
&\times\big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g_{1}+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{3}-f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g\big]\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\\
&=\int\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g_{1}+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{3}\big]\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\\
&-\int\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}g(k)\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\\
&:=\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}g(k)-\mathcal{A}^{k_{c}}g(k).\end{split}

(2.6)

Concerning the range of values of
\alpha
we see that since by the assumption
|z_{\alpha}|\leq k_{c}
,
\alpha\in\left[-\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}^{2}-r_{\alpha}^{2}}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{\sqrt{k_{c}^{2}-r_{\alpha}^{2}}}{|k+k_{3}|}\right]
.
Then the proof will follow from the next two lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the multiplication operator
\mathcal{A}^{k_{c}}
defined in (
2.6
) is
coercive
when
k,k_{i}\in[0,k_{c}]^{3}
and under hypothesis
1
(i),(ii). In particular

\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}(\mathcal{A}^{k_{c}}g(k))g(k)\,{\rm d}k\geq\frac{\pi k_{c}^{-2\beta+1}}{M^{3}}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k)^{2}\,{\rm d}k

where
M=M(k_{c})
is so that
f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}\leq M
for all
k\in[0,k_{c}]^{3}
.

Proof.

Due to the parametrisation above, we have

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}(\mathcal{A}^{k_{c}}g(k))g(k)\,{\rm d}k=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}g(k)^{2}f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\frac{|k+k_{3}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\right)\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}k_{3}\,{\rm d}k\gtrsim

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{|k|^{\beta/2}|k_{1}|^{\beta/2}|k_{2}|^{\beta/2}|k_{3}|^{\beta/2}}\frac{g(k)^{2}}{f_{\infty}(k)}|k+k_{3}|\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}k_{3}\,{\rm d}k

where we used that
\frac{|k+k_{3}-z|}{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z|)}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(1)
. Also if
M=M(k_{c})
is the upper bound on
f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}
,
1+\omega(k)+1\cdot k\leq M
, we write

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}(\mathcal{A}^{k_{c}}g(k))g(k)\,{\rm d}k\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi k_{c}^{-2\beta}C_{1}}{4M^{3}}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k)^{2}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}^{\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}|k+k_{3}|\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}k_{3}\,{\rm d}k\gtrsim

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi C_{1}}{4M^{3}}2k_{c}^{-2\beta+1}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k)^{2}\,{\rm d}k=\frac{\pi k_{c}^{-2\beta+1}}{M^{3}}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k)^{2}\,{\rm d}k.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

The operator
\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}
defined in (
2.6
) is
compact
when
k,k_{i}\in[0,k_{c}]^{3}
,
\beta=0
and when hypothesis
1
(i),(ii) holds.

Proof.

For the
compactness
, we write

\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}g(k)=\mathcal{K}_{1}^{k_{c}}g-\mathcal{K}_{2}^{k_{c}}g

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}_{2}g

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}^{\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})g(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}k_{3}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}_{1}g(k):=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}^{\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\bigg(\frac{g(k_{3}+k-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}+

\displaystyle+\frac{g(z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\bigg)\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\ \,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

In order to conclude the compactness of both operators
\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}_{1}
,
\mathcal{K}^{k_{c}}_{2}
, we identify their integral kernels and show that their kernels are square integrable. Then as Hilbert–Schmidt integral operators they are compact.

Kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{k_{c}}
: First the kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{2}^{k_{c}}
is directly seen to be

k_{2}^{k_{c}}(k,k_{3})=\int_{\alpha,\theta}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta.

We consider the
L^{2}
-norm of
k_{2}^{k_{c}}
and we write

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}|k_{2}^{k_{c}}(k,k_{3})|^{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\lesssim\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}

\displaystyle\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\left(\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}\right)^{2}}\times

\displaystyle\times\frac{[f_{\infty}(k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})]^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}

Using now that
\frac{\Omega^{\prime}(x)}{x}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(1)
due to Assumption
1
(ii), this is upper bounded by

\displaystyle\|f_{\infty}\|_{\infty}^{4}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}=

\displaystyle\|f_{\infty}\|_{\infty}^{4}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}_{|k+k_{3}|\geq\delta}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}+

\displaystyle\|f_{\infty}\|_{\infty}^{4}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{[-\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|},\frac{k_{c}}{|k+k_{3}|}]}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}_{|k+k_{3}|\leq\delta}\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta}}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}=I^{\delta}_{1}+I^{\delta}_{2}

for some positive
0<\delta
. We see that the first term
I^{\delta}_{1}
is bounded by
4\|f_{\infty}\|_{\infty}^{4}\pi k_{c}k_{c}^{4}\delta^{-4\beta-1}
since we are away from
0
.
For the second term
I^{\delta}_{2}
we notice that since all the frequencies are positive,
|k+k_{3}|\leq\delta
implies that
|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|<\delta
, thus
I^{\delta}_{2}
is finite as
\delta
goes to
0
as long as
\beta\in[0,1/4]
.

Kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{k_{c}}
:
Regarding the kernel of
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{k_{c}}
, after a change of variables we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}^{k_{c}}g(k)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{\alpha,\theta}\frac{f_{\infty}(z_{\alpha})f_{\infty}(k_{3}+k-z_{\alpha})}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}||z_{\alpha}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}g(z_{\alpha})\times

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{|z_{\alpha}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}+\frac{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\right)\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}g(z)\frac{f_{\infty}(z)f_{\infty}(k_{3}+k-z)}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z||z||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\frac{|z|+|k+k_{3}-z|}{|k+k_{3}|}\delta_{\Omega}\,{\rm d}z\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

So the kernel
k_{1}^{k_{c}}
is by inspection

k_{1}^{k_{c}}(k,z)=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\frac{f_{\infty}(z)f_{\infty}(k_{3}+k-z)}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z||z||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\frac{|z|+|k+k_{3}-z|}{|k+k_{3}|}\delta_{\Omega}\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

We then compute

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}|k_{1}^{k_{c}}(k,z)|^{2}\,{\rm d}z\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{\alpha}\int_{\theta}\frac{f_{\infty}(z)^{2}f_{\infty}(k_{3}+k-z)^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z||z||k_{3}|]^{\beta}}\frac{[|z|+|k+k_{3}-z|]^{2}}{|k+k_{3}|}\frac{\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta}{\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|)}{|k+k_{3}-z_{\alpha}|}+\dfrac{\Omega^{\prime}(|z_{\alpha}|)}{|z_{\alpha}|}}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}\int_{\alpha}\int_{\theta}\frac{[|z|+|k+k_{3}-z|]^{2}}{[|k||k+k_{3}-z||z||k_{3}|]^{\beta}|k+k_{3}|}\,{\rm d}\alpha\,{\rm d}\theta\,{\rm d}k_{3}.

and direct calculations show that when
\beta=0
, this is finite, due to the assumptions on the dispersion
\Omega
.
This implies that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{1}^{k_{c}}
is compact (as a Hilbert Schmidt operator).
∎

Remark 4
.

Notice that for the compactness we need to take the exponent of the cross-section term to be
\beta=0
as otherwise we can not control the singularity that appears in the kernel. If we let

, we would have to cut-off further all the frequencies away from
0
.

Proof of Theorem

.

An application then of Weyl’s theorem yields directly that the essential spectrum of the operator
L_{k_{c}}
is the essential spectrum of

. The coercivity of

implies therefore the existence of a spectral gap of

.

3.
From the linearized to the non-linear operator

In this section we aim at proving that the rate of approach to Rayleigh-Jeans equilibria for the nonlinear operator
\mathcal{L}
is governed by the relaxation rate of the linearized operator, when the dispersion relation is weakly perturbed around the quadratic one.

We take
\beta=0
and we first rewrite the evolution problem (
1.1
) as follows, using that
n(t,k)=(\omega+1\cdot k+1)^{-1}(1+g(t,k))
.

\begin{split}\partial_{t}g(t,k)&=-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\left(nn_{2}n_{3}+nn_{1}n_{3}-n_{1}n_{2}n-n_{1}n_{2}n_{3}\right)\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\\
&=-Lg(t,k)+\Gamma(g,g)+Q(g,g,g)\end{split}

(3.1)

where
\Gamma(g,g)
is the bilinear operator

\begin{split}\Gamma(g,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\Big\{(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k))g_{2}g_{3}+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3}))g_{2}g\\
&\qquad+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})+f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2}))gg_{3}+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k))g_{1}g_{3}+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3}))gg_{1}\\
&\qquad\qquad+(-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1}))g_{2}g_{1}\Big\}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\end{split}

(3.2)

and
Q(g,g,g)
is the term with the cubic nonlinearity

\begin{split}Q(g,g,g)&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\big\{f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})gg_{2}g_{3}+f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2})gg_{1}g_{3}\\
&-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3})gg_{1}g_{2}-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k)g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}\big\}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\\
&=:Q_{1}^{+}(g,g,g)+Q_{2}^{+}(g,g,g)-Q_{1}^{-}(g,g,g)-Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g).\end{split}

(3.3)

We rewrite the quadratic term as

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle(g,g)=f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{9}}\big\{(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3}))(gg_{2}-g_{1}g_{3})

\displaystyle+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{1})+f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2}))(gg_{3}-g_{1}g_{2})+(f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{2})-f_{\infty}^{-1}(k_{3}))(gg_{1}-g_{2}g_{3})\big\}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

\displaystyle=\Gamma^{1}(g,g)+\Gamma^{2}(g,g)+\Gamma^{3}(g,g).

We need estimates on the
L^{2}
- norm of the quadratic and cubic nonlinearities on the collision kernel in terms of the
L^{2}
- norm of
g
. Estimates on weighted
L^{2}
spaces have been done already in
[
GIT20
]
without cut-off on the frequencies, thus here we sketch the proof in our notation for the readers convenience. We adapt slightly the proof to include a bounded perturbation around the quadratic dispersion relation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\beta=0
and
\omega(k)=|k|^{2}+s(|k|)
satisfying assumption
1
(i),(ii),(iii). We have

\|\Gamma(g,g)+Q(g,g,g)\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\lesssim C_{1}(\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}+\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{3}).

(3.4)

Proof.

The quadratic nonlinearity is handled easily as in the case of the Boltzmann collision operator.
For the cubic nonlinearity we want to show that
\|Q(g,g,g)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}}^{3}
.

We estimate first the term

Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)=-\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{[|k||k_{1}||k_{2}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}g(k_{1})g(k_{2})g(k_{3})\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

as follows by applying a
TT^{*}
-type argument:

\begin{split}&|Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)|\lesssim\\
&\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}}\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}g(k_{1})g(k_{2})g(k_{3})e^{i\mu\cdot(-k_{1}-k_{2}+k+k_{3})}e^{i\nu(-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+\omega+\omega_{3})}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\right|\lesssim\\
&C\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k_{1})e^{-i\mu\cdot k_{1}-i\nu\omega_{1}}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\right)\left(\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k_{2})e^{-i\mu\cdot k_{2}-i\nu\omega_{2}}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\right)\times\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{3}}g(k_{3})e^{i\mu\cdot k_{3}+i\nu\omega_{3}}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\right)e^{i\mu\cdot k+i\nu\omega}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\Big|\end{split}

(3.5)

so that for a parameter
s>1/2
and denoting by
Tg(\mu,\nu):=\int g(x)e^{i\mu\cdot x+i\nu\omega(x)}\,{\rm d}x
we write:

\displaystyle\|Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\lesssim\|Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\lesssim C\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}}Tg(\mu,\nu)^{2}\ \overline{T\bar{g}}(\mu,\nu)e^{i\mu\cdot k+i\nu\omega}\langle k\rangle^{s}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\right\|_{L^{2}_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{F}^{-1}[Tg(k,\nu)^{2}\ \overline{T\bar{g}}(k,\nu)]e^{i\nu\omega}\langle k\rangle^{s}\,{\rm d}\nu\right\|_{L^{2}_{s}}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)^{2}\ \overline{T\bar{g}}(\cdot,\nu)\|_{H^{s}}\,{\rm d}\nu

where for the last inequality we used that
k
lives in a bounded domain. Then this is upper bounded by

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{H^{s}}\,{\rm d}\nu&\lesssim\sup_{\nu\in\mathbb{R}}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{H^{s}}\int_{\nu}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\nu\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{s}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\nu.\end{split}

(3.6)

It remains therefore to provide the bound

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|Tg(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\,{\rm d}\nu\lesssim\|g\|_{L_{s}^{2}}^{2}.

It is enough to show that
(assuming without loss of generality that
g(k)=\langle k\rangle^{-s}h(k)
for
\|h\|_{L^{2}}=1
) for
F\in L_{\mu}^{1}L_{\nu}^{2}
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Tg(\mu,\nu)F(\mu,\nu)\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\right|\lesssim_{s}1

and since the left-hand side is bounded by

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}Tg(\mu,\nu)F(\mu,\nu)\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\right|\lesssim\left\|\langle k\rangle^{-s}\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(\mu,\nu)e^{-i\mu\cdot k-i\nu\omega(k)}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\right\|_{L^{2}},

equivalently we shall provide the bound

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R})^{2}}\langle k\rangle^{-2s}F(\mu,\nu)F(\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})e^{-ik\cdot\mu-i\nu\omega(k)}e^{ik\cdot\mu^{\prime}+i\nu^{\prime}\omega(k)}\,{\rm d}\mu\,{\rm d}\nu\,{\rm d}\mu^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\nu^{\prime}\,{\rm d}k\right|\lesssim 1.

Now if we were to bound
\int\langle k\rangle^{-2s}e^{-ik\cdot(\mu-\mu^{\prime})-i(\nu-\nu^{\prime})\omega(k)}\,{\rm d}k
uniformly in
\mu-\mu^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and so that it is integrable in
\nu-\nu^{\prime}
, we can write the left-hand side as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}r(\nu-\nu^{\prime})\|F(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L^{1}}\|F(\cdot,\nu^{\prime})\|_{L^{1}}\,{\rm d}\nu\,{\rm d}\nu^{\prime}.

Due to properties of the Schrödinger kernel, when
\omega(k)=|k|^{2}+s(|k|)
, taking
s>1/2
we can find a
\theta=\theta(s)>0
so that
r(\nu-\nu^{\prime})\sim e^{-ic(\nu-\nu^{\prime})}\frac{|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|^{-1+\theta}}{(1+|\nu-\nu^{\prime}|^{2})^{\theta}}
, thus under the assumption that
\|F(\cdot,\nu)\|_{L_{\mu}^{1}}\lesssim 1
, we have the desired bound for
Q_{2}^{-}
:
\|Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}
is upper bounded by
\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{3}
since the weight is bigger than
1
and bounded.

Next, for
Q_{1}^{-}(g,g,g)
we have

\displaystyle|Q_{1}^{-}(g,g,g)|

\displaystyle:=\left|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})}{[|k||k_{1}||k_{2}||k_{3}|]^{\beta/2}}\frac{g(k)g(k_{1})g(k_{2})}{f_{\infty}(k_{3})}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}\frac{f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)}{[|k||k_{1}||k_{2}||k_{1}+k_{2}-k|]^{\beta/2}}\frac{g(k)g(k_{1})g(k_{2})}{f_{\infty}(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)}\delta_{\Omega}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|g(k)\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}g(k_{1})g(k_{2})\delta_{\Omega}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\right|.

We mollify the dirac
\delta_{\Omega}
in the integrand with a smooth function
\delta^{\varepsilon}_{\Omega}\in C_{c}^{\infty}([-1,1])
of mass
1
. So for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}g(k_{1})g(k_{2})\delta_{\omega(k_{1})+\omega(k_{2})-\omega(k)-\omega(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)}^{(\varepsilon)}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\right|\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}.

(3.7)

We Taylor expand and apply mean-value theorem in order to write for
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in(0,1)

\displaystyle[\omega(k)-\omega(k_{1})]

\displaystyle+[\omega(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)-\omega(k_{2})]

\displaystyle=\nabla\omega(k_{1})\cdot(k-k_{1})+(k-k_{1})^{T}\nabla^{2}\omega(\theta k+(1-\theta)k_{1}))(k-k_{1})

\displaystyle+\nabla\omega(k_{2})\cdot(k_{1}-k)+(k_{1}-k)^{T}\nabla^{2}\omega(\theta(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)+(1-\theta)k_{2}))(k_{1}-k)

\displaystyle=(k-k_{1})\cdot[\nabla\omega(k_{1})-\nabla\omega(k_{2})]

\displaystyle+(k-k_{1})^{T}[\nabla^{2}\omega(\theta(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)+(1-\theta)k_{2}))+\nabla^{2}\omega(\theta^{\prime}k+(1-\theta^{\prime})k_{1}))](k-k_{1}).

Assuming that
\omega(x)=|x|^{2}+s(|x|)
, with
s(|x|)=S(x)
, and
\|s\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{1}
this equals to for
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
:

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle(k-k_{1})\cdot(k-k_{2})+(k-k_{1})\cdot\nabla[S(k_{1})-S(k_{2})]+

\displaystyle+(k-k_{1})^{T}[\nabla^{2}S(\theta k+(1-\theta)k_{1})+\nabla^{2}S(\theta^{\prime}(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)+(1-\theta^{\prime})k_{2})](k-k_{1}).

The left-hand side of (

) is written as

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{6}}g(k_{1})g(k_{2})\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(2(k-k_{1})\cdot(k-k_{2})+(k-k_{1})\cdot\nabla[S(k_{1})-S(k_{2})]+

\displaystyle(k-k_{1})^{T}[\nabla^{2}S(\theta k+(1-\theta)k_{1})+\nabla^{2}S(\theta^{\prime}(k_{1}+k_{2}-k)+(1-\theta^{\prime})k_{2})](k-k_{1})\Big)\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg|\int_{[-k_{c},k_{c}]^{6}}\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(2k_{1}\cdot k_{2}+2\Lambda_{i}|k_{1}|^{2}+k_{1}\cdot\nabla[S(k-k_{1})-S(k-k_{2})]\Big)\frac{G(k_{1})}{\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}}\frac{G(k_{2})}{\langle k+k_{2}\rangle^{s}}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{[-k_{c},k_{c}]^{6}}\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(2k_{1}\cdot k_{2}+2\Lambda_{i}|k_{1}|^{2}+k_{1}\cdot\nabla^{2}S(\theta^{\prime\prime}(k-k_{1})+(1-\theta^{\prime\prime})(k-k_{2}))(k_{2}-k_{1})\Big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{G(k_{1})}{\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}}\frac{G(k_{2})}{\langle k+k_{2}\rangle^{s}}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\Bigg|

where we performed a change of variables and where
G(k_{1})=\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}g(k-k_{1})
for some parameter

. Also
\Lambda_{i}=\Lambda_{1}
if we are on the decreasing side of the mollified function and
\Lambda_{2}
if we are on the increasing. Finally we applied again mean-value theorem with interpolation parameter
\theta^{\prime\prime}\in(0,1)
.

Now equivalently we want to prove the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the following linear operator, uniformly in
\varepsilon
and
k
:

\displaystyle L_{2}G(k_{1})

\displaystyle:=\int_{[-k_{c},k_{c}]^{3}}\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(2k_{1}\cdot k_{2}+2\Lambda_{i}|k_{1}|^{2}+k_{1}\cdot\nabla^{2}S(\theta^{\prime\prime}(k-k_{1})+(1-\theta^{\prime\prime})(k-k_{2}))(k_{2}-k_{1})\Big)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\frac{G(k_{2})}{\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}\langle k+k_{2}\rangle^{s}}\,{\rm d}k_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-k_{c},k_{c}]^{3}}\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(k_{1}\cdot(2+\nabla^{2}S(\xi)k_{2}+a\Big)\frac{G(k_{2})}{\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}\langle k+k_{2}\rangle^{s}}\,{\rm d}k_{2},

where

is independent of
k_{2}
.
Since
\nabla^{2}S
is a diagonal matrix with positive eigenvalues, after a change of variables
k_{2}\mapsto(2+\nabla^{2}S(\xi))k_{2}=:Dk_{2}
, up to some constants we need to bound the operator

L_{2}\bar{G}(k_{1})=\int_{k_{2}\in C_{k_{c}}}\delta^{(\varepsilon)}\Big(k_{1}\cdot k_{2}+a\Big)\frac{\bar{G}(k_{2})}{\langle k+k_{1}\rangle^{s}\langle k+D^{-1}k_{2}\rangle^{s}}\,{\rm d}k_{2}

where

is a compact domain and

. As we can now apply
[

, Lemma 4.3]
, which bounds the integral kernel of such operator, we conclude the boundedness of

by a
TT^{*}
argument as in
[
GIT20
, subsection 4.2]
.

Similar calculations work for the remaining two terms:

Q_{1}^{+}(g,g,g):=\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\big\{f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g(k)g(k_{2})g(k_{3})\big\}\delta_{\Omega}\delta_{\Sigma}\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}

3.1.
Existence and uniqueness of solution close to Rayleigh-Jeans

In this section we show the existence of solutions close to the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium. For such solutions we will then show that they are
L^{2}
-stable. For the existence we will proceed via a fixed point argument.
Let
S^{t}
be the semigroup generated by
L_{k_{c}}
. Then Duhamel’s formula reads

g(t,k)=S^{t}g(0,k)+\int_{0}^{t}S^{t-s}\{\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})(k)+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})(k)\}\,{\rm d}s.

Motivated by this, we define the following operator

\mathcal{F}g(k):=S^{t}g(0,k)+\int_{0}^{t}S^{t-s}\{\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})(k)+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})(k)\}\,{\rm d}s.

We also introduce the norm
\|g\|=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|g(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}
.

Next we observe that we can decompose the solution
g
into an element on the subspace of collision invariants and an element vertical to this:
g_{t}=g_{t}^{1}+g_{t}^{2}
with
g_{t}^{1}=\Pi g_{t}
being the component along
\operatorname{Ker}(L_{k_{c}})
which is constant in time as the kinetic wave equation that we consider is the spatial homogeneous one, and
g_{t}^{2}=\Pi^{\perp}g_{t}
. Here with
\Pi
we denote the orthogonal projection on
\operatorname{Ker}(L_{k_{c}})
. Then

g_{t}^{2}(k)=S^{t}g_{0}^{2}(k)+\int_{0}^{t}S^{t-s}\{\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})(k)+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})(k)\}\,{\rm d}s.

Fix a ball
H=\left\{g\in C((0,\infty);L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})):\|g\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq c(\lambda)\right\}
for a constant
c(\lambda)
depending on
\lambda=\lambda(k_{c})
to be determined later. We then want to show that as soon as the initial data satisfy
\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq c(\lambda)/2
:

•

\mathcal{F}H\subset H
and

•

the following contraction property for
\mathcal{F}
: when
g,\tilde{g}\in H
,
\|\mathcal{F}g-\mathcal{F}\tilde{g}\|\leq c\|g-\tilde{g}\|
, for
c<1
.

Let
g\in H
. We calculate

\|\mathcal{F}g_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{-\lambda t}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})\|\,{\rm d}s

where the exponential relaxation of the semigroup of the linearized operator
L_{k_{c}}
with spectral gap
\lambda
is implied by Grönwall’s inequality, using the coercivity property, cf Theorem
1
. This in particular means that we assume
g_{t}\in\operatorname{Ker}(L_{k_{c}})^{\perp}
for all
t\geq 0
.
Then due to Lemma
3.1
the integrand is bounded by

\displaystyle e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\|\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})\|

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\Big(\|g_{s}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}+\|g_{s}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{3}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{1}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}\left(c(\lambda)^{2}+c(\lambda)^{3}\right).

So that

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}g_{2}\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{-\lambda t}+C_{1}(c(\lambda)^{2}+c(\lambda)^{3})\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}ds\\
&\lesssim\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}}e^{-\lambda t}+\frac{c(\lambda)^{2}+c(\lambda)^{3}}{\lambda}C_{1}(1-e^{-\lambda t})\\
&\lesssim\frac{c(\lambda)}{2}e^{-\lambda t}+C_{1}\frac{c(\lambda)^{2}+c(\lambda)^{3}}{\lambda}\lesssim c(\lambda)\end{split}

(3.8)

This is for example satisfied when
c(\lambda)=\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2\sqrt{2C_{1}}}
, where
\lambda=\lambda(k_{c})
.

Contraction of the semigroup in the ball
H
:
Moving now to the contraction property we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{F}(g)-\mathcal{F}(\tilde{g})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|S^{t-s}\{(\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s}))-

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(\Gamma(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})+Q(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s}))\}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda}\|(\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})+Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s}))-(\Gamma(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})+Q(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s}))\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda}[\|\Gamma(g_{s},g_{s})-\Gamma(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\|Q(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})-Q(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}]\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda}\Big[\sum_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\|\Gamma^{i}(g_{s}+\tilde{g}_{s},g_{s}-\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})-Q^{+}(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\|Q^{-}(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})-Q^{-}(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\Big]\,{\rm d}s

where
Q^{+}(g,g,g)=Q_{1}^{+}(g,g,g)+Q_{2}^{+}(g,g,g)
and
Q^{-}(g,g,g)=Q_{1}^{-}(g,g,g)+Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g)

as defined in (
3.3
).
The first term in the bracket in the integrand consisting of quadratic nonlinearities, is bounded by

\sum_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\|\Gamma^{i}(g_{s}+\tilde{g}_{s},g_{s}-\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq\sum_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\|g_{s}+\tilde{g}_{s}\|\|g_{s}-\tilde{g}_{s}\|.

For the second term:

\displaystyle\|Q^{+}(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})-Q^{+}(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}=

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\Big[f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}g(g_{2}-\tilde{g}_{2})g_{3}+f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}(g-\tilde{g})g_{3}\tilde{g}_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+f_{\infty}(k_{1})^{-1}\tilde{g}\tilde{g}_{2}(g_{3}-\tilde{g}_{3})+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}g(g_{1}-\tilde{g}_{1})g_{3}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+f_{\infty}(k_{2})^{-1}(g-\tilde{g})\tilde{g}_{1}g_{3}+\omega_{2}\tilde{g}\tilde{g}_{1}(g_{3}-\tilde{g}_{3})\Big]\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}

\displaystyle:=\|Q_{1}^{+}(g,g-\tilde{g},g)+Q_{1}^{+}(g-\tilde{g},\tilde{g},g)+Q_{1}^{+}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g},g-\tilde{g})+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad Q_{2}^{+}(g,g-\tilde{g},g)+Q_{2}^{+}(g-\tilde{g},\tilde{g},g)+Q_{2}^{+}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g},g-\tilde{g})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g-\tilde{g}\|(\|g\|^{2}+\|g\|\|\tilde{g}\|+\|\tilde{g}\|^{2})

where we applied Lemma
3.1
.
For the third term:

\displaystyle\|Q^{-}(g_{s},g_{s},g_{s})-Q^{-}(\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s},\tilde{g}_{s})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}=

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{[0,k_{c}]^{9}}f_{\infty}(k_{1})f_{\infty}(k_{2})f_{\infty}(k_{3})\Big[f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}(g-\tilde{g})g_{1}g_{2}+f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}g_{2}\tilde{g}(g_{1}-\tilde{g}_{1})+

\displaystyle\qquad f_{\infty}(k_{3})^{-1}\tilde{g}\tilde{g}_{1}(g_{2}-\tilde{g}_{2})+f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g_{1}g_{2}(g_{3}-\tilde{g}_{3})

\displaystyle\qquad+f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}g_{2}(g_{1}-\tilde{g}_{1})\tilde{g}_{3}+f_{\infty}(k)^{-1}\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{3}(g_{2}-\tilde{g}_{2})\Big]\,{\rm d}k_{1}\,{\rm d}k_{2}\,{\rm d}k_{3}\Big\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}

\displaystyle:=\|Q^{-}_{1}(g-\tilde{g},g,g)+Q^{-}_{1}(\tilde{g},g-\tilde{g},g)+Q^{-}_{1}(\tilde{g},\tilde{g},g-\tilde{g})+

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad Q_{2}^{-}(g,g,g-\tilde{g})+Q_{2}^{-}(g-\tilde{g},g,\tilde{g})+Q_{2}^{-}(\tilde{g},g-\tilde{g},\tilde{g})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g-\tilde{g}\|(\|g\|^{2}+\|g\|\|\tilde{g}\|+\|\tilde{g}\|^{2})

due to the estimates in Lemma
3.1
again.
That is, since
g,\tilde{g}\in H
,

\begin{split}\|\mathcal{F}(g_{t})-\mathcal{F}(\tilde{g}_{t})\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\lesssim(c(\lambda)+c(\lambda)^{2})\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda}\|g_{s}-\tilde{g}_{s}\|\,{\rm d}s.\end{split}

(3.9)

Finally we take the supremum in time
t
to have that

\||\mathcal{F}(g)-\mathcal{F}(\tilde{g})|\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|g-\tilde{g}\|.

Thus in this norm
\mathcal{F}
is a contraction mapping. Existence and uniqueness of the solution now immediately follows.

3.2.
Stability around Rayleigh-Jeans in
L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})

In this subsection we show stability for the homogeneous kinetic wave equation for data close to the Rayleigh-Jeans equilibrium.
First we multiply both sides of the equation (
3.1
) by
g
and we integrate over the frequency space. Since
g_{t}=g_{t}^{1}+g_{t}^{2}
and
\partial_{t}g_{t}^{1}=0
,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}=-\langle L_{k_{c}}g_{t}^{2},g_{t}^{2}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\langle(\Gamma(g_{t},g_{t})+Q(g_{t},g_{t},g_{t})),g_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\\
&\lesssim-\lambda\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}+2C\Big\{\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}+\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{3}\Big\}\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}.\end{split}

(3.10)

This implies that as long as
\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}^{2}<\frac{\lambda}{4C}
,

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}&\leq-\lambda\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{t}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\\
&\leq-\lambda\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{0}^{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\\
&=-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g_{0}^{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\end{split}

(3.11)

as
g^{1}
is parallel to the subspace of collision invariants. This yields, by Grönwall’s lemma, exponential relaxation of
g_{t}^{2}:=\Pi^{\perp}g_{t}
as soon as we assume that
\Pi g_{0}=0
(using also conservation of mass).

Notice that without the assumption of having
\Pi g_{0}=0
, one gets

\begin{split}\|g_{t}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}&\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t}\|g_{0}^{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}+(1-e^{-\frac{\lambda}{2}t})\|g_{0}^{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\end{split}

(3.12)

This inequality now means that as long as
\|g_{0}\|_{L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2C}}
, i.e.
g_{0}\in H
where existence of solutions is guaranteed, the perturbation
g
around the equilibrium solution
f_{\infty}(k)
remains small for any time
t
. Thus
n(t,k)=f_{\infty}(k)(1+g(t,k))
remains in
L^{2}([0,k_{c}]^{3})
close to
f_{\infty}(k)
.

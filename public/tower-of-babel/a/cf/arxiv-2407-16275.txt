A higher index theorem on finite-volume locally symmetric spaces
Hao Guo, Peter Hochs, Hang Wang

Abstract
Let G G be a connected, real semisimple Lie group. Let K < G K<G be maximal compact, and let Γ < G \Gamma<G be discrete and such that Γ \ G \Gamma\backslash G has finite volume. If the real rank of G G is 1 1 and Γ \Gamma is torsion-free, then Barbasch and Moscovici obtained an index theorem for Dirac operators on the locally symmetric space Γ \ G / K \Gamma\backslash G/K . We obtain a higher version of this, using an index of Dirac operators on G / K G/K in the K K -theory of an algebra on which the conjugation-invariant terms in Barbasch and Moscovici’s index theorem define continuous traces. The resulting index theorems also apply when Γ \Gamma has torsion. The cases of these index theorems for traces defined by semisimple orbital integrals extend to Song and Tang’s higher orbital integrals, and yield nonzero and computable results even when rank ⁡ ( G ) > rank ⁡ ( K ) \rank(G)>\rank(K) , or the real rank of G G is larger than 1 1 .

Introduction

Consider a connected, real semisimple Lie group
G
, a maximal compact subgroup
K<G
and a discrete subgroup
\Gamma<G
.
Locally symmetric spaces
of the form
X=\Gamma\backslash G/K
come up in many places in mathematics, and include, for example, all hyperbolic manifolds, several moduli spaces, and the space
\SL(2,\mathbb{Z})\backslash\SL(2,\mathbb{R})/\SO(2)
on which modular forms are defined. Spaces
X
that arise in this way are sometimes compact, but in many relevant cases (such as the example mentioned) they only have finite volume. Because of the relevance of these spaces, it is a worthwhile question how to construct and compute topological invariants for them.

Index theory is a useful tool for constructing and computing invariants of manifolds. This works particularly well if
\Gamma
is torsion-free and
\Gamma\backslash G
is compact, so that
X
is a compact, smooth manifold. Then elliptic differential operators on
X
have well-defined Atiyah–Singer indices. And by considering the action by
\Gamma
on
G/K
rather than just its quotient, one can construct a higher index of
\Gamma
-equivariant elliptic operators
[
6
,
21
,
38
]
, with values in
K_{*}(C^{*}(\Gamma))
. Pairings of this index with various cyclic cocycles were computed by Connes and Moscovici
[
11
,
12
]
.

If
X
is smooth and has finite volume, but is not necessarily compact, then Moscovici
[
25
]
constructed an index of elliptic operators on
X
. This was computed for Dirac operators by Barbasch and Moscovici
[
5
]
in the case where the real rank of
G
is
1
. (For groups of higher real rank, index theorems for the signature operator were obtained in
[
27
,
33
,
34
]
.) Barbasch and Moscovici’s computation was based on a version of Selberg’s trace formula for real rank
1
groups due to Osborne and Warner
[
29
,
36
]
. The result is an expression for the index of Dirac operators as a sum of various contributions, all but finitely many of which are given by semisimple orbital integrals. In fact, if
X
is compact, then one only needs these semisimple contributions, so the non-semisimple contributions reflect the noncompactness of
X
. Some of these non-semisimple contributions are defined by conjugation-invariant functionals, which can be expected to define traces on suitable convolution algebras. The sum of the other contributions is conjugation-invariant as well, even though the individual terms are not.

Our goal here is to unify these constructions and computations, and obtain a higher index theorem on finite-volume locally symmetric spaces if
G
has real rank
1
. More precisely, we aim to construct a
K
-theoretic index from which

•

the classical index can be recovered,

•

if
G
has real rank
1
, then the conjugation-invariant parts of Barbasch and Moscovici’s expression for the classical index on
X
can be extracted and computed individually, and

•

the usual
\Gamma
-equivariant index can be recovered if
X
is compact.

To achieve this aim, we show that

1.

every
G
-equivariant, self-adjoint, elliptic, first-order differential operator on
G/K
has a well-defined index in
K_{*}(\mathcal{A}(G))
, where
\mathcal{A}(G)
is an analogue of Casselman’s Schwartz algebra
[
10
]
;

2.

this index can be represented by a standard idempotent involving heat operators
[
12
]
;

3.

the semisimple orbital integrals and the conjugation-invariant non-semisimple components of Barbasch and Moscovici’s index theorem define continuous traces on
\mathcal{A}(G)
.

There are several commonly used convolution algebras on
G
besides Casselmann’s Schwartz algebra on which semisimple orbital integrals define continuous traces. These include
C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
and Harish-Chandra’s
L^{1}
- or
L^{2}
-Schwartz algebras. We use Casselman’s algebra because it is large enough so that the first two points above are true (the second is not true for
C^{\infty}_{c}(G)
), and small enough so that the third point is true (which is not true for some non-semisimple contributions if one uses Harish-Chandra’s Schwartz algebras).
See also Remark
4.3
.

Because of the second point above, applying the traces from the third point to the index in the first point leads to expressions involving heat operators, which were computed in
[
5
]
.
For traces not defined by semisimple orbital integrals, we then simply apply the computations in
[
5
]
. For semisimple orbital integrals, we give an independent index-theoretic computation. This may be of independent interest, and it also has the advantages that

•

it generalises to the higher orbital integrals from
[
32
]
, which yield nonzero results even when
\rank(G)>\rank(K)
, in which case the classical index
vanishes;

•

our computations also apply when
\Gamma
has torsion, so that
X
is an orbifold, and we obtain nonzero contributions from nontrivial elliptic elements;

•

the index theorems for semisimple orbital integrals apply even when
G
does not have real rank
1
.

(We note, however, that the computations for semisimple orbital integrals are more about the action by
G
on
G/K
than about the group
\Gamma
.)

Our higher index on
G/K
is constructed as a special case of a general higher index of elliptic operators equivariant with respect to proper, cocompact group actions, constructed in
[
14
]
. This takes values in the
K
-theory of an algebra of exponentially fast decaying kernels.

be a Lie group (possibly with infinitely many connected components, so

Consider the action by

,

Let

2.2

Let

be an
H
-equivariant, first-order, elliptic, self-adjoint differential operator on

1.

There are

.

(2.7)

Contributions from semisimple elements

To prove Theorem

[

. We hope that this approach and some of the intermediate results may be of independent interest. The index-theoretic arguments also extend to a proof of Theorem
3.19
.

For a locally compact group
H
acting properly and cocompactly on a manifold, we will use
the standard equivariant index

\ind_{H}(D)\quad\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(H))

(5.1)

of
H
-equivariant elliptic operators
D
. There are many equivalent definitions, see e.g.
[
6
,
21
,
38
]
.

5.1
A holomorphic fixed-point formula

The first step is a version of the fixed-point formula in
[
17
]
for Dolbeault–Dirac operators.
Let
X
be a complex manifold. Let
G
be a connected, real semisimple Lie group acting properly and cocompactly on
X
by holomorphic maps. Let
E\to M
be a holomorphic, Hermitian,
G
-equivariant vector bundle. Let
\bar{\partial}_{E}
be the Dolbeault operator on antiholomorphic differential forms twisted by
E
, and let
\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}
be its formal adjoint. Then we have the Dolbeault–Dirac operator
\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}
.

Let
g\in G
be semisimple.
Because
G
acts holomorphically on
X
, the fixed point set
X^{g}\subset X
of
g
is a complex submanifold, and the normal bundle
N\to X^{g}
is a complex vector bundle. (To be precise, the connected components of
X^{g}
are complex submanifolds of possibly different dimensions, so the rank of
N
may jump between these components.)
Its complexification decomposes into holomorphic and antiholomorphic parts as
N\otimes\mathbb{C}=N^{1,0}\oplus N^{0,1}
. Fix a Haar measure
dz
on the centraliser
G_{g}
. Let
\chi^{g}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(X^{g})
be such that for all
x\in X^{g}
,

\int_{G_{g}}\chi^{g}(zx)\,dz=1.

(Here we use compactness of
X^{g}/Z
; see Lemma 2.4 in
[
17
]
.)

The restriction
E^{g}\to X^{g}
of
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle over the complex manifold
X^{g}
, so we have the Dolbeault–Dirac operator
\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}^{*}
on
X^{g}
. For every connected component
X_{j}^{g}
of
X^{g}
, we write
E_{j}^{g}:=E|_{X_{j}^{g}}
. We similarly have the Dolbeault–Dirac operator
\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}_{j}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}_{j}}^{*}
on
X^{g}_{j}
.

Consider the equivariant indices

\begin{split}\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*})&\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G));\\
\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}_{j}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}_{j}}^{*})&\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{g})).\end{split}

The orbital integral trace
\tau_{g}
is a well defined map on
K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G))
; see Section 2.1 of
[
17
]
. The von Neumann trace
\tau_{e}
defines a map on
K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{g}))
. We write
\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}
for the closure of the cyclic subgroup of
G
generated by
g
.

Theorem 5.1
.

(a)

If
g
does not lie in a compact subgroup of
G
, then
\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}))=0
.

(b)

If

g
lies in a compact subgroup of
G
,
then

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}))=\int_{T(X^{g})}\chi^{g}\frac{\ch\left(\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}^{*}}\right](g)\right)\Todd(T(X^{g})\otimes\mathbb{C})}{\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{0,1}\right](g)\right)}.

(c)

Suppose that
g
lies in a compact subgroup of
G
.
Suppose, furthermore, that on each connected component
X_{j}^{g}
of
X^{g}
, the group element
g
acts on every fibre of
E_{j}^{g}
as the same scalar
g|_{E_{j}^{g}}
.
Suppose also that the bundle
N^{0,1}
is trivial as a
\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}
-equivariant vector bundle. Then

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}))=\sum_{j}\frac{g|_{E_{j}^{g}}}{\det_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{N^{0,1}})|_{X_{j}^{g}}}\tau_{e}(\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}^{*})).

Proof.

Part (a) follows from the second statement in Theorem 2.5 in
[
17
]
. (This in fact holds for all semisimple
g\in G
not contained in compact subgroups. The “finite Gaussian orbital integral” condition used there holds for general semisimple group elements, by the equalities from
[
7
]
also mentioned in the proof of Proposition
4.5
.)

To prove part (b), we again apply Theorem 2.5 in
[
17
]
. This now yields

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}))=\int_{T(X^{g})}\chi^{g}\frac{\ch\left(\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}}\right](g)\right)\Todd(T(X^{g})\otimes\mathbb{C})}{\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N\otimes\mathbb{C}\right](g)\right)}.

To be precise, in Theorem 2.5 in
[
17
]
it is stated that
this equality holds for almost all elliptic elements
g
not contained in compact subgroups. The result applies to “almost all” such
g
because of the “finite Gaussian orbital integrals” condition. But this turns out to hold in general, see equations (3.3.36), (3.4.4) and (4.2.9) in
[
7
]
.

We use the decomposition

\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}}|_{X^{g}}\right](g)=\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}(N^{0,1})^{*}\right](g)\otimes\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}^{*}}\right](g)=\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{1,0}\right](g)\otimes\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}^{*}}\right](g)

By multiplicativity of the Chern character,

\frac{\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{1,0}\right](g)\right)}{\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N\otimes\mathbb{C}\right](g)\right)}=\frac{1}{\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{0,1}\right](g)\right)},

so (b) follows.

To deduce (c) from (b), we first note that the assumption on the way
g
acts on
E|_{X^{g}}
implies that

\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E^{g}}^{*}}|_{X_{j}^{g}}\right](g)=g|_{E_{j}^{g}}\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}^{*}}\right],

for every connected component
X_{j}^{g}
of
X^{g}
.
And if
N^{0,1}
is trivial, then for all
j
, we have
N^{0,1}|_{X_{j}^{g}}=X^{g}_{j}\times W_{j}
for some representation
W_{j}
of the compact abelian group
\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}
. (The dimension of
X^{g}_{j}
, and hence of
W_{j}
, may depend on
j
.) Then

[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{0,1}|_{X_{j}^{g}}]=[X_{j}^{g}\times\mathbb{C}]\otimes\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}W_{j}\right]\quad\in K^{0}_{\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}}(X_{j}^{g})\cong K^{0}(X_{j}^{g})\otimes R(\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}).

Here
\textstyle{\bigwedge}W_{j}
should be interpreted as a graded representation, i.e.

\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}W_{j}\right]=\Bigl[\textstyle{\bigwedge}^{\even}W_{j}\Bigr]-\Bigl[\textstyle{\bigwedge}^{\odd}W_{j}\Bigr]\in R(\overline{g^{\mathbb{Z}}}).

So

\ch\left(\left[\textstyle{\bigwedge}N^{0,1}\right](g)\right)=\tr\left(g|_{\bigwedge^{\even}W_{j}}\right)-\tr\left(g|_{\bigwedge^{\odd}W_{j}}\right)={\det}_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{W_{j}})={\det}_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{N^{0,1}})|_{X_{j}^{g}}.

We find that

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*}))=\\
\sum_{j}\left.\frac{\tr(g|_{E})}{{\det}_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{N^{0,1}})}\right|_{X_{j}^{g}}\int_{T(X_{j}^{g})}\chi^{g}|_{X_{j}^{g}}{\ch\left(\left[\sigma_{\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}^{*}}\right]\right)\Todd(T(X_{j}^{g})\otimes\mathbb{C})}.

By Proposition 4.4 and Theorem 6.12 in
[
35
]
, the integral on the right equals
\tau_{e}(\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}+\bar{\partial}_{E_{j}^{g}}^{*}))
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

If
X
and
G
are compact, and
X^{g}
is finite, then Theorem
5.1
(c) reduces to

\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*})(g)=\sum_{x\in X^{g}}\frac{g|_{E_{x}}}{\det_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{T_{x}X^{0,1}})}=\sum_{x\in X^{g}}\frac{g|_{E_{x}}}{\det_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g^{-1}|_{T_{x}X^{1,0}})}.

This is consistent with Atiyah and Bott’s holomorphic fixed-point theorem, Theorem 4.12 in
[
2
]
, because in the notation of that paper,

\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{E}+\bar{\partial}_{E}^{*})(g)=L(g^{-1},g).

5.2
A fixed-point set

Until the end of Subsection
5.4
, we suppose that
T<K
is a compact Cartan subgroup of
G
, so
\rank(G)=\rank(K)
. We fix a positive root system
R^{+}(G,T)
for
(G,T)
. This determines a
G
-invariant complex structure on
G/T
such that
T_{eT}^{1,0}(G/T)=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}
. Let
g\in T
. We will apply Theorem
5.1
to the space
X=G/T
, and we start by analysing the fixed-point set
(G/T)^{g}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For all
x\in G
such that
xgx^{-1}\in T
, there exists
y\in N_{G}(T)
such that
xgx^{-1}=ygy^{-1}
.

Proof.

Because
xTx^{-1}\subset G_{xgx^{-1}}
, we have two maximal tori
T
and
xTx^{-1}
in the connected component
(G_{xgx^{-1}})_{0}
. These are conjugate in
(G_{xgx^{-1}})_{0}
. (See e.g. Propositions 6.59 and 6.61 in
[
23
]
, or use the facts that any two maximal compact subgroups of
(G_{xgx^{-1}})_{0}
are conjugate, and so are any two maximal tori inside such a compact subgroup.) Let
h\in(G_{xgx^{-1}})_{0}
be such that
hxTx^{-1}h^{-1}=T
. Then
y:=hx
has the desired properties.
∎

Recall that we used the Weyl groups
W_{K}=N_{K}(T)/T
and
W_{K_{g}}=N_{K_{g}}(T)/T
. Note that
G_{g}w
is a right
T
-invariant subset of
G
for all
w\in N_{G}(T)
.

Lemma 5.4
.

The fixed-point set
(G/T)^{g}
decomposes as

(G/T)^{g}=\coprod_{w\in W_{K_{g}}\backslash W_{K}}G_{g}w/T,

(5.2)

where the disjoint union is over representatives
w\in N_{K}(T)
of classes in
W_{K_{g}}\backslash W_{K}
.

Proof.

We first show that

(G/T)^{g}=G_{g}N_{G}(T)/T.

(5.3)

It is immediate that the right hand side is contained in the left hand side. Now let
xT\in(G/T)^{g}
. Then
x^{-1}gx\in T
. By Lemma
5.3
, there is a
y\in N_{G}(T)
such that
x^{-1}gx=y^{-1}gy
. So
xy^{-1}\in G_{g}
, and
xT=(xy^{-1})yT
lies in the right hand side of (
5.3
).

Next, we show that

(G/T)^{g}=\coprod_{w\in N_{G_{g}}(T)\backslash N_{G}(T)}G_{g}w/T,

(5.4)

where the disjoint union is over a set of representatives
w\in N_{G}(T)
. Indeed, if
y\in N_{G_{g}}(T)\subset G_{g}
and
w\in N_{G}(T)
, then

G_{g}yw/T=G_{g}w/T
. And if
w,w^{\prime}\in N_{G}(T)
and
G_{g}w/T=G_{g}w^{\prime}/T
, then let
y:=w^{\prime}w^{-1}\in N_{G}(T)
. Then
G_{g}w/T=G_{g}yw/T
, so there are
a\in G_{g}
and
b\in T
such that
awb=yw
. Hence
a^{-1}y=wbw^{-1}\in T\subset G_{g}
. So
y\in G_{g}
, and therefore
y\in N_{G_{g}}(T)
. We find that
G_{g}w/T=G_{g}w^{\prime}/T
if and only if
w^{\prime}w^{-1}\in N_{G_{g}}(T)
, which implies that the right hand side of (
5.3
) equals the right hand side of (
5.4
).

Finally, we have
N_{G}(T)=N_{K}(T)
and
N_{G_{g}}(T)=N_{K_{g}}(T)
; see for example Lemma 6.15 in
[
18
]
.
So the decomposition (
5.2
) follows from (
5.4
) and the equality

N_{K_{g}}(T)\backslash N_{K}(T)=(N_{K_{g}}(T)/T)\backslash(N_{K}(T)/T).

∎

Example 5.5
.

If
g=e
, then Lemma
5.4
reduces to the equality
G/T=G/T
.
At the other extreme, if the powers of
g
are dense in
T
, then Lemma
5.4
becomes
(G/T)^{T}=W_{K}
; this is Lemma 6.15 in
[
18
]
.

5.3
A normal bundle

For
w\in N_{G}(T)
, let
J_{w}
be the
G
-invariant complex structure on
G/T
such that

T_{eT}^{1,0}(G/T)\cong\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{w\alpha}.

Then
J_{e}
is the complex structure we have considered so far.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
w\in N_{G}(T)
.
The map
R_{w}\colon G/T\to G/T
, given by
R_{w}(xT)=xwT
for
x\in G
, is a well-defined,
G
-equivariant diffeomorphism. It is holomorphic as a map

R_{w}\colon(G/T,J_{w})\to(G/T,J_{e}).

(5.5)

Proof.

The first claim follows directly from the definitions. We show that (
5.5
) is holomorphic.

Note that
R_{w}=L_{w}\circ C_{w^{-1}}
, where
L
is the standard action by
G
on
G/T
, and
C_{w^{-1}}=L_{w^{-1}}\circ R_{w}
is induced by conjugation by
w^{-1}
. The map
L_{w}
is holomorphic for any of the complex structures
J_{w^{\prime}}
, because they are
G
-invariant. And

T_{eT}C_{w^{-1}}=\Ad(w^{-1})\colon\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{t}.

Because
\Ad(w^{-1})\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{w^{-1}\alpha}
, this map induces a complex linear map from
(T_{eT}(G/J),J_{w})
to
(T_{eT}(G/J),J_{e})
. For general
x\in G
, we have
C_{w^{-1}}=L_{w^{-1}xw}\circ C_{w^{-1}}\circ L_{x^{-1}}
. So

T_{xT}C_{w^{-1}}=T_{eT}L_{w^{-1}xw}\circ T_{eT}C_{w^{-1}}\circ T_{xT}L_{x^{-1}}.

The three maps on the right hand side are complex-linear, hence so is the map on the left. We find that the map (
5.5
) is indeed holomorphic.
∎

The complex structure
J_{w}
, for
w\in N_{G}(T)
, restricts to the normal bundle
N\subset T(G/T)|_{(G/T)^{g}}
to
(G/T)^{g}
in
G/T
. We denote this restriction by
J_{w}
as well.

Lemma 5.7
.

For every
w\in N_{G}(T)
, the map
R_{w}\colon G_{g}/T\to G_{g}w/T
given by
R_{w}(xT)=xwT
for
x\in G_{g}
, is a well-defined,
G_{g}
-equivariant diffeomorphism. The vector bundles

\begin{split}(N|_{G_{g}/T},J_{w})&\to G_{g}/T;\\
R_{w}^{*}(N|_{G_{g}w/T},J_{e})&\to G_{g}/T\end{split}

(5.6)

are isomorphic as
G_{g}
-equivariant, holomorphic vector bundles.

Proof.

Let
w\in N_{G}(T)
. The first claim follows from the first claim in Lemma
5.6
.

We write
N(S)
for the normal bundle of a submanifold
S\subset G/T
. Because the sets
G_{g}w/T
are connected components of
(G/T)^{g}
, we have

TR_{w}(N|_{G_{g}/T})=TR_{w}(N(G_{g}/T))=N(R_{w}(G_{g}/T))=N(G_{g}w/T)=N|_{G_{g}w/T}.

This fact, together with
G

and the fact that the map (
5.5
) is holomorphic by Lemma

, implies that the bundles (

) are equivariantly isomorphic as holomorphic vector bundles.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

The bundle
N^{0,1}\to(G/T)^{g}
is trivial as a

-vector bundle.

Proof.

Let
w\in N_{K}(T)
.
We first prove that
(N^{0,1}|_{G_{g}/T},J_{w})\to G_{g}/T

N|_{G_{g}/T}=G_{g}\times_{T}\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{g},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{g}
is the Lie algebra of

.
So

(N^{0,1}|_{G_{g}/T},J_{w})=G_{g}\times_{T}((\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{g})^{0,1},J_{w}),

(5.7)

where

((\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{g})^{0,1},J_{w})=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)\setminus R^{+}(G_{g},T)}\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{-w\alpha}.

(5.8)

The bundle (

(N^{0,1}|_{G_{g}/T},J_{w})\to G_{g}/T

and

∎

As in Theorem
3.18
, let
\rho
be half the sum of the positive roots in

, and let

be half the sum of the positive roots in

.

Lemma 5.9
.

For all
w\in W_{K}
,

{\det}_{\mathbb{C}}(1-g|_{N^{0,1}})|_{G_{g}w/T}=e^{w(\rho_{g}-\rho)}(g)\det(w)e^{\rho-\rho_{g}}(g)\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)\setminus R^{+}(G_{g},T)}(1-e^{-\alpha}(g)).

(5.9)

Let

. Then by (

) and (

),

The right hand side with the first exponential factor removed is a ratio of Weyl denominators. This is alternating with respect to

the right hand side of (

5.7

∎

5.4

Lemma 5.10

.

,

\tau_{e}\bigl(\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}+\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}^{*})\bigr)=(-1)^{\dim(G_{g}/K_{g})/2}d^{G_{g}}_{w(\lambda+\rho_{g}-\rho_{n})},

For

.
Then Lemma

And Lemma
5.10
implies that

R_{w}^{*}(L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T})\cong L^{G_{g}/T}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n})}:=G_{g}\times_{T}\mathbb{C}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n})}\to G_{g}/T

as
G_{g}
-equivariant, complex line bundles.
We find that

\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}+\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}^{*})=\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{L^{G_{g}/T}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n})}}+\bar{\partial}_{L^{G_{g}/T}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n})}}^{*}),

(5.11)

where the Dolbeault operator on the right is defined with respect to
J^{G_{g}}_{w}
.

In the proof of Proposition 5.2 in
[
17
]
, it is shown that the right hand side of (
5.11
) equals
(-1)^{\dim(G_{g}/K_{g})/2}\DInd_{K_{g}}^{G_{g}}[W^{K_{g}}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})}]
, where
\DInd_{K_{g}}^{G_{g}}
denotes Dirac induction,
\rho_{n,g}
is half the sum of the noncompact roots in
R^{+}(G_{g},T)
, and
W^{K_{g}}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})}
is the irreducible representation of
K_{g}
with highest weight
w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})
with respect to the positive root system
wR^{+}(K,T)\cap R(K_{g},T)
.
If
\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{g}
is regular for
G_{g}
, then Lemma 5.4 in
[
17
]
implies that

\DInd_{K_{g}}^{G_{g}}[W^{K_{g}}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})}]=[\pi^{G_{g}}_{w(\lambda+\rho_{g}-\rho_{n})}],

the
K
-theory class of the
discrete series representation of
G_{g}
with Harish-Chandra parameter
w(\lambda+\rho_{g}-\rho_{n})
. Hence (
5.10
) follows, because the value of
\tau_{e}
on a
K
-theory class of a discrete series representation is the formal degree of that representation
[
3
,
11
,
24
]
.

If
\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{g}
is singular for
G_{g}
, then
d^{G_{g}}_{w(\lambda+\rho_{g}-\rho_{n})}=0
.
Then the Dirac operator whose
G_{g}
-equivariant index is
\DInd_{K_{g}}^{G_{g}}[W^{K_{g}}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})}]
has trivial kernel by Theorem 9.3 in
[
3
]
. And
\tau_{e}(\DInd_{K_{g}}^{G_{g}}[W^{K_{g}}_{w(\lambda-\rho_{n}+\rho_{n,g})}])
equals the
L^{2}
-index of this Dirac operator (see Proposition 4.4 in
[
35
]
), which is defined in terms of the projection onto its kernel, and hence zero. So now the left hand side of (
5.10
) is zero as well, and (
5.10
) holds.
∎

Theorem 5.12
.

Let
g\in G
be semisimple. Then
\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(D_{W}))=0
if
g
is hyperbolic. If
\rank(G)=\rank(K)
and
g
is elliptic, then

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(D_{W}))=(-1)^{(\dim(G/K)+\dim(G_{g}/K_{g}))/2}\cdot\\
\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{K_{g}}\backslash W_{K}}\det(w)e^{w(\lambda+\rho_{c}-\rho_{g})}(g)d^{G_{g}}_{w(\lambda+\rho_{g}-\rho_{n})}}{e^{\rho-\rho_{g}}(g)\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)\setminus R^{+}(G_{g},T)}(1-e^{\alpha}(g))}.

(5.12)

Proof.

The case for hyperbolic
g
follows from Theorem
5.1
(a).

Suppose that
\rank(G)=\rank(K)
and that
g
is elliptic.
The element
\lambda-\rho_{n}\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
is analytically integral as noted in Lemma
5.11
.
In the proof of Proposition 5.2 in
[
17
]
, it is shown that

\ind_{G}(D_{W})=(-1)^{\dim(G/K)/2}\ind_{G}(\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}}+\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}}^{*}).

(This is a place where we use the assumption that
\rank(G)=\rank(K)
.)
By this equality and Lemmas
5.4
,
5.8
and
5.9
, Theorem
5.1
(c) implies that

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(D_{W}))=(-1)^{\dim(G/K)/2}\cdot\\
\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{K_{g}}\backslash W_{K}}\det(w)e^{w(\rho-\rho_{g})}(g)(\tr(g|_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}})|_{G_{g}w/T})\tau_{e}(\ind_{G_{g}}(\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}+\bar{\partial}_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}|_{G_{g}w/T}}^{*}))}{e^{\rho-\rho_{g}}(g)\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)\setminus R^{+}(G_{g},T)}(1-e^{\alpha}(g))}.

For all
w\in N_{K}(T)
,
Lemma
5.10
implies that

\tr(g|_{L_{\lambda-\rho_{n}}})|_{G_{g}w/T}=e^{w(\lambda-\rho_{n})}(g).

So (
5.12
) follows by Lemma
5.11
.
∎

Example 5.13
.

If

Example 5.14
.

If the powers of

are dense in

G_{g}=K_{g}=T
, and for all

,

So Theorem

\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(D_{W}))=(-1)^{\dim(G/K)/2}\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{K}}\det(w)e^{w(\lambda+\rho_{c})}(g)}{e^{\rho}(g)\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(G,T)}(1-e^{-\alpha}(g))}.

]

Remark 5.15
.

If
\rank(G)>\rank(K)
, then

[

]
implies that the number

equals zero for all hyperbolic

5.5
Higher orbital integrals

We no longer assume that
G
has a compact Cartan subgroup.
We saw in Theorem
5.12
that
\tau_{g}(\ind_{G}(D_{W}))=0
if
\rank(G)>\rank(K)
. However,
\ind_{G}(D_{W})\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G))
is nonzero, and one way to detect its nonvanishing is to pair it with the higher orbital integrals from
[
32
]
. We can compute the resulting pairings using the results in
[
16
]
to generalise Theorem
5.12
. We use notation and assumptions as in Theorem
3.19
.
As in
[
16
]
and in Theorem
3.19
, we assume that the map
(
2.8
) descends to
K
.

Corollary 5.16
.

If
P
is not a maximal cuspidal parabolic subgroup, or
g\in M
is hyperbolic, then

\langle\Phi_{P,g},\ind_{G}(D_{W})\rangle=0.

If
P
is a maximal cuspidal parabolic subgroup and
g\in T\subset M
, then

\langle\Phi_{P,g},\ind_{G}(D_{W})\rangle=(-1)^{(\dim(M/(K\cap M))+\dim(M_{g}/(K\cap M_{g})))/2}\cdot\\
\sum_{U\in\widehat{K\cap M}}m_{U}\frac{\sum_{w\in W_{K\cap M_{g}}\backslash W_{K\cap M}}\det(w)e^{w(\lambda_{U}+\rho^{M}_{c}-\rho^{M}_{g})}(g)d^{M_{g}}_{w(\lambda_{U}+\rho^{M}_{g}-\rho^{M}_{n})}}{e^{\rho^{M}-\rho^{M}_{g}}(g)\prod_{\alpha\in R^{+}(M,T)\setminus R^{+}(M_{g},T)}(1-e^{\alpha}(g))}.

(5.13)

Proof.

The first statement is one conclusion of Theorem 2.1 in
[
16
]
.

Now suppose that
P
is a maximal cuspidal parabolic subgroup and
g\in T
. Let
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{s}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{m}
. For every finite-dimensional representation
Z
of
K\cap M
, consider the Dirac operator

D^{M}_{Z}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}R(X_{j})\otimes c(X_{j})\otimes 1_{Z}

on
(C^{\infty}(M)\otimes\Delta_{\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{m}}\otimes Z)^{K\cap M}
, analogous to (
2.9
).
Let
\tau_{g}^{M}
be the orbital integral with respect to
g
as an element of
M
. Then
(5.4) and (5.5) in
[
16
]
imply that

\langle\Phi_{P,g},\ind_{G}(D_{W})\rangle=\\
\tau^{M}_{g}\bigl(\ind_{M}(D^{M}_{\Delta^{+}_{\mathfrak{k}/(\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{m})}\otimes W|_{K\cap M}})\bigr)-\tau^{M}_{g}\bigl(\ind_{M}(D^{M}_{\Delta^{-}_{\mathfrak{k}/(\mathfrak{k}\cap\mathfrak{m})}\otimes W|_{K\cap M}})\bigr).

Hence (
5.13
) follows by (
3.16
) and Theorem
5.12
.
∎

.

.

If

g\in T
and the powers of

are dense in

5.14
, the equality (

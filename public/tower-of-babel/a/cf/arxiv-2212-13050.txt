Shicheng Wang, Zhongzi Wang

Abstract
. Let F g F_{g} be the closed orientable surface of genus g g . Introduction

In this paper, we will work on the smooth, orientable category unless otherwise clearly stated.
We use
Aut(M)
to denote the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism group of a manifold
M
, and

|A|
to denote the cardinality of a finite set
A
.
Let
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
S^{n}
be the
n
-space and
n
-sphere respectively.
Let
F_{g}
be the closed orientable surface of genus
g>0
.
Let
{\mathcal{M}}(F_{g})
be the mapping
class group of
F_{g}
, i.e., the group of
smooth isotopy classes of orientation preserving
diffeomorphisms on
F_{g}
.

An element
f
in
Aut(F_{g})
is
extendable over
M
with respect to an embedding
e:F_{g}\rightarrow M
if there exists an element
\tilde{f}
in
Aut(M)
such that

\tilde{f}\circ e=e\circ f.

When

is a map of finite order, we say

is periodically extendable over
M
for

\tilde{f}
above is a map of the same order. Call an element
f
in
Aut(F_{g})
is
(periodically) extendable over
M
, if
f
is (periodically) extendable over
M
for some embedding

.

In the definition above, if we allow
\tilde{f}
to be just a homeomorphism on
M
, then we call
f
is
topologically extendable
.

We address the problem when torsion elements of
Aut(F_{g})
are extendable, or periodically extendable over
S^{4}
.
There are two motivations of this study.

One motivation is rather naive: In order to get intuitive feelings of symmetries on surfaces, people try to embed those surfaces symmetries into the symmetries
of our 3-space (3-sphere) in which we live.
Some recent work in this direction include

[
WWZZ
]
,
[
NWW
]
.
The first one tell us that each periodic map on

of order

are not periodically

for any embedding; the second one claims if a periodic map
f
on

is extendable over

for some embedding

, then

is periodically extendable over
S^{3}
for some embedding

. Therefore each map of order

is not
extendable over
S^{3}
for any embeddings.

Another motivation is to extend surface automorphisms over

(or

[

In 2012 it is proved if

Spin structures of a rank
n
vector bundle
\xi
over a CW complex

X
can phrased with trivialization.
\xi
can be
endowed with a spin structure if
\xi\oplus\epsilon^{k}
has a
trivialization over the
1

which may extend over the

2
-skeleton of
X
, (if
n\geq 3
,

; if
n=2
,
k=1
; if
n=1
,

), and a spin structure is a homotopy class of such
trivializations.
A spin structure of a smooth
manifold
M
is a spin structure of its tangent bundle with respect to some (hence any) CW complex structure of
M
, which exists
if and only
M
is orientable and the second Stiefel-Whitney class
w_{2}(M)=0
.
Two spin structures
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{0}
of
\xi
are equivalent
if and only if the trivialization
\sigma_{1}|_{\alpha}\simeq\sigma_{0}|_{\alpha}
for
any closed path
\alpha
on
M
.
Two facts below are basic for us:

(i) The circle
S^{1}
has two spin structures: the bounded
spin structure, namely which bounds a spin structure on a surface
F
, or equivalently, the corresponding element in
\pi_{1}(SO(n))={\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
is trivial for some
n\geq 3
;
and the Lie group spin structure, which is unbounded, or equivalently, the corresponding element in
\pi_{1}(SO(n))
is non-trivial.

(ii) If
\xi=\xi_{1}\oplus\xi_{2}
for bundles over
X
, then
spin structures on two of the bundles determine a spin structure of
the third (cf.
[
Ki
, p. 37]
).

For a spin manifold
M
with boundary
\partial M
,
\partial M
has a natural spin structure induced from the spin structure of
M
and the (inward) normal vector of
\partial M
in
M
by (ii). A spin manifold is said to be spin bounded
if there is a spin manifolds bounded by it, inducing its spin structure.

2.1.
Spin structure on
F_{g}

Let
\mathcal{S}(F_{g})
be the space of spin structures on
F_{g}
. It is known that spin structure on
F_{g}
are 1-1 corresponding to elements in

H^{1}(F_{g},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})

2^{2g}
spin structures on
F_{g}

There is a surjective map:

\mathcal{S}(F_{g})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[.]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega^{\rm Spin}_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathrm{Arf}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},

where
\Omega^{\rm Spin}_{2}
is the second spin cobordism group and
{\mathrm{Arf}}
is the Arf isomorphism. More precisely,
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(F_{g})
, there is an associated nonsingular quadratic function
q_{\sigma}:H_{1}(F_{g};{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
such that
q_{\sigma}(\alpha)=0
(resp.
1
) if the spin
structure on
F_{g}
restricted to the bounding (resp. Lie-group) spin structure on
\alpha
. Note

q_{\sigma}(\alpha+\beta)=q_{\sigma}(\alpha)+q_{\sigma}(\beta)+\alpha\cdot\beta\qquad(S1),

where
\alpha\cdot\beta
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-intersection number, and
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if
q_{\sigma}=q_{\sigma^{\prime}}
.
Thus
{\mathrm{Arf}}([\sigma])
is defined as the Arf invariant of the nonsingular quadratic form
q_{\sigma}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{g}
be the union of bounding (
{\mathrm{Arf}}([\sigma])=0
) spin structures
and
\mathcal{U}_{g}
be the union of unbounding (
{\mathrm{Arf}}([\sigma])=1
) spin structures. Then
\mathcal{S}(F_{g})
is a disjoint union:

\mathcal{S}(F_{g})=\mathcal{B}_{g}\sqcup\mathcal{U}_{g}.

An extending criterion

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
f\in Aut(F_{g})
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1) There is a bounded
f
-invariant spin structure
\sigma
on
F_{g}
.

(2)
f
is topologically extendable over
S^{4}
for some smooth embedding
e:F_{g}\to S^{4}
,

(3)
f
is extendable over
S^{4}
for some trivial embedding
e:F_{g}\to S^{4}
.

Let
q_{\sigma}
be the corresponding quadratic form for the induced spin structure
e^{*}(\varsigma^{4})=\sigma
.
Lemma
3.2
below had appeared in an early version of
[
DLWY
]
, and might be known for experts.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
e:F_{g}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
is a smooth embedding.
The induced spin structure
q_{\sigma}
coincides with the Rohlin form
q_{e}
.

Proof.

To verify this, consider a smoothly embedded surface
P\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
as in the definition of
q_{e}
. Note
\partial P
becomes null-homologous in
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\setminus F
if we push
\partial P
along the normal vector field of
\partial P
in
P
, hence the normal vector field of
\partial P
in
P
is equivalent to
W|_{\partial P}
, where
W
is the vector field in definition of induced spin structure.

There is a trivialization defined by a frame field
(U,V,W,H)|_{\partial P}
such that for any
x\in\partial P
,
U_{x}\in T(\partial P)|_{x}
,
V_{x}\in N_{F_{g}}(\partial P)|_{x}
,
W_{x}\in N_{P}(\partial P)|_{x}
, and
H_{x}
is the restriction of the vector field
H
in the definition of induced spin structure.

Now
(U,V,W,H)\simeq\varsigma^{4}|_{\partial P}
if and only if it extends over
T{\mathbb{R}}^{4}|_{P}
.

Suppose
(U,V,W,H)\simeq\varsigma^{4}|_{\partial P}
. Then by definition, the restriction of the induced spin structure
\sigma=e^{*}(\varsigma^{4})
on
{\partial P}
is given by
(U,V)|_{\partial P}
. Since
(U,V)|_{\partial P}
is the Lie-group spin structure,
q_{\sigma}(\partial P)=1
.

Since
(U,W)|_{\partial P}
is the Lie-group spin structure, it does not (stably) extend over
TP
.
Then
(U,V,W,H)\simeq\varsigma^{4}|_{\partial P}
if and only if
(V,H)|_{\partial P}
fails to extend (stably) over
N_{{\mathbb{R}}^{4}}(P)
, i.e.
|P\cap P^{\prime}|
is odd. That is to say
q_{e}([\partial P])=1
.

We conclude
q_{\sigma}([\partial P])=1
if and only if

q_{e}([\partial P])=1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Suppose
f:F_{g}\to F_{g}
is topologically extendable over
S^{4}
for some smooth embedding
e:F_{g}\to S^{4}
.
By classical fact in manifold topology, we always can assume that the extension
\tilde{f}:S^{4}\to S^{4}
has a fixed point
x

in
S^{4}\setminus e(F_{g})
, therefore
f
is topologically extendable over
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
for the embedding
e:F_{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{4}=S^{4}\setminus x
.
Hence there must be
a bounded spin structure on
F_{g}
, which is
f
-invariant by Theorem
2.5
. So (2) implies (1).

Suppose
\sigma_{1}
is a bounded spin structure which is
f
-invariant, that is

f^{*}(\sigma_{1})=\sigma_{1}.

Let

e:F_{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{4}=S^{4}\setminus x\subset S^{4}

be any trivial embedding, that is
F=e(F_{g})
bounds a 3-dimensional handlebody in
S^{4}
.
This provided a bounded spin structure

\sigma=e^{*}(\varsigma^{4})
on
F_{g}
by Theorem

.

Since the mapping class group acts transitively on the bounded spin structures by Lemma

, there is a diffeomorphism
\phi:F_{g}\to F_{g}

such that

\phi^{*}(\sigma)=\sigma_{1}.

Then

e\circ\phi:F_{g}\to S^{4}

is another trivial embedding with the image

, and

(e\circ\phi)^{*}(\varsigma^{4})=(\phi^{*}\circ e^{*})(\varsigma^{4})=\phi^{*}(e^{*}(\varsigma^{4}))=\phi^{*}(\sigma)=\sigma_{1}.

That is to say, the induced bounded spin structure of the trivial embedding

is the
\sigma_{1}
, which is
f

invariant. Then by Theorem
3.3
,

is extendable as diffeomorphism for the trivial embedding
e\circ\phi:F_{g}\to S^{4}
. So (1) implies (3).

Clearly (3) implies (2).

So we prove the theorem.
∎

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose

are two periodic maps of order

Proof.

We need only to argue that

is (periodically) extendable implies that

is (periodically) extendable.
Let

and
\left<u\right>

Then both of them are cyclic groups of order

.
Since

or

, it is known that those two groups are conjugated

]

that is to say, there is an automorphism

Suppose

is (periodically) extendable for some embedding

(

\in Aut({\mathbb{R}}^{4})

That is to say

is (periodically) extendable for

Let
\tilde{w}=\tilde{u}^{p}
and

Then we have

is (periodically) extendable w.r.t.

.
∎

6.
Extending periodic maps of prime orders

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
f:F_{g}\to F_{g}
be a map of order
p^{m}
, where
p
is an odd prime and
m
is an integer.

(i) If p is not a divisor of
2^{2g-1}+2^{g-1}
, then there is a bounded
f
-invariant spin structure.

(ii) If p is not a divisor of
2^{2g-1}-2^{g-1}
, then there is an unbounded
f
-invariant spin structure.

Proof.

(i) Let
r=p^{m}
and

G={\mathbb{Z}}_{r}=\left<f\right>

acts on
\mathcal{B}_{g}
. For any
g\in\left<f\right>
and
q\in\mathcal{B}_{g}
. Denote by

Orb(q)=\{g^{*}(q)|g\in G\}

and

Stab_{q}=\{g\in G|g^{*}(q)=q\}

Since
p
is prime and
|G|=p^{m}
,
Stab_{q}
is a subgroup of
G
, we have
|Stab_{q}|=p^{u}
for
0\leq u\leq m.

Then

|Orb(q)|=\frac{|G|}{|Stab_{q}|}=p^{s}

for some
0\leq s\leq m
.

Let
Orb(q_{1}),...,Orb(q_{l})
be disjoint orbits such that

\mathcal{B}_{g}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}Orb(q_{i})

Since
|\mathcal{B}_{g}|=2^{2g-1}+2^{g-1}
by Lemma
2.3
, and
2^{2g-1}+2^{g-1}
is not a multiple of
p
,
we have
|Orb(q_{j})|
is not multiple of
p
for some
j\in\{1,...,l\}
.

It follows that
|Orb(q_{j})|=1
. That is
f^{*}(q_{j})=q_{j}
. So
f
has an invariant bounded spin structure.

(ii) The proof is the similar to that of (i).
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

(H. Sun) There are infinitely many prime numbers that are not divisors of any
2^{2g-1}+2^{g-1}
.

Proof.

We first prove the following

Claim:
Each prime number in the form

is not a factor of
2^{g}+1
for any natural number
g
.

Suppose that
p
is a divisor of
2^{g}+1
, then

Since
p

,
2
is a quadratic residue of

[
EW
, Theorem 3.16]
, that is, there exists an integer

So we have

-1=2^{g}=k^{2g}\,\text{mod}\,p.

Thus

[

By the Dirichlet’s theorem

Hearing Exotic Smooth Structures
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, *, Llohann D. Sperança

Abstract
This paper explores the existence and properties of basic eigenvalues and eigenfunctions associated with the Riemannian Laplacian on closed, connected Riemannian manifolds featuring an effective isometric action by a compact Lie group. Our primary focus is on investigating the potential existence of homeomorphic yet not diffeomorphic smooth manifolds that can accommodate invariant metrics sharing common basic spectra. We establish the occurrence of such scenarios for specific homotopy spheres and connected sums. Moreover, the developed theory demonstrates that the ring of invariant admissible scalar curvature functions fails to recover the smooth structure in many examples. We show the existence of homotopy spheres with identical rings of invariant scalar curvature functions, irrespective of the underlying smooth structure.

4
The proof of Theorem
2

In this last section, we prove Theorem
2
. Let
M\leftarrow P\rightarrow M^{\prime}
denote a
\star
-diagram with structure group
G
compact and connected. The manifolds
M,M^{\prime}
, and
P
are assumed to be closed and connected. Assume that
\mathsf{g}_{M},\penalty\ \mathsf{g}_{\omega},\penalty\ \mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}
are as in Lemma
1
. Proposition
1
implies that the basic spectra of
-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}_{M}},\penalty\ -\Delta_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}
coincide. Let
u
be a
G
-invariant eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}})
and consider the general vertical warping metric
[
17
, Chapter 2]

\mathsf{g}_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi^{*}(\mathsf{g}_{M})+e^{2u}Q\circ\omega\otimes\omega
, where
Q
is a bi-invariant metric in
G
and
\omega
is as in Lemma
1
. Let
\mathsf{g}^{\prime}
be the Riemannian metric in
M^{\prime}
making
\pi^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}(P,\mathsf{g}_{u})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},\mathsf{g}^{\prime})
be a Riemannian submersion. It suffices to show that the first positive (invariant) eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}),\penalty\ \lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})
for
-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}
and
-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}
, respectively, are different.

Take
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{G}(M^{\prime})
satisfying
-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\phi=\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})\phi
. Since
\phi
lifts to a function on
P
and
-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\phi=-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\phi-\mathrm{d}\phi(H^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}})
we have

-\Delta_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\phi-\mathrm{d}\phi(H^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}})=\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})\phi.

Therefore,

-\int_{P}\mathrm{d}\phi(H^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}})\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}=\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})\int_{P}\phi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}.

Equation 2.1.4 in
[
17
, Chapter 2, p.46]
ensures that if
\mathrm{dim}G=k
then
H^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}=-k\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{u}}u
. Consequently,

\displaystyle-\int_{P}\mathrm{d}\phi(H^{\pi^{\prime}}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}})\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}

\displaystyle=k\int_{P}\mathsf{g}_{u}(\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\phi,\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{u}}u)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}

\displaystyle=k\int_{P}\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}(\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\phi,\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}

\displaystyle\leq k\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\phi|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}

\displaystyle\leq k\left(\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\phi|^{2}_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the second equality holds because

(TG\pi^{\prime}(p))^{\perp_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}}\cong(T(G\times G)p)^{\perp_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}}\cong(TG\pi(p))^{\perp_{\mathsf{g}_{M}}}\cong(TG\pi^{\prime}(p))^{\perp_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}},\penalty\ \forall p\in P.

Using that
|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}
is
G
-invariant and applying Fubini’s Theorem for Riemannian submersions
[
36
, Satz 1, p.210]
one gets

\displaystyle\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}

\displaystyle=\int_{M^{\prime}}\left(\int_{G}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{Q}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)\int_{M^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}.

The volume
\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)
is computed regarding each orbit’s induced Riemannian metric by
\mathsf{g}_{u}
. Similarly,

\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\phi|^{2}_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}=\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)\int_{M^{\prime}}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\phi|^{2}_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}.

Therefore, the variational characterization for the first eigenvalue (
[
11
, Section 5]
) yields

\displaystyle[\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)]^{-1}k\left(\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}u|_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{P}|\nabla^{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\phi|^{2}_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq k

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus,

\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})^{1/2}\left(\int_{P}\phi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}})^{\frac{1}{2}}k\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}^{\prime})}{\lambda_{1}(\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq k\mathrm{vol}_{u}(G)\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{P}\phi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{u}}\right)^{-1}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=k\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}u^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}_{M^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}\phi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\right)^{-1}\left(\int_{M^{\prime}}\phi^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathsf{g}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Finally, one concludes the desired result as we can indiscriminately scale
u
so that the right-hand side above is strictly lesser than
1
.

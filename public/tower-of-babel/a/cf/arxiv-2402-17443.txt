The classification and representations of positive definite ternary quadratic forms of level 4 ​ N 4N
Yifan Luo, Haigang Zhou

Abstract
Classifications and representations are two main topics in the theory of quadratic forms. In this paper, we consider these topics of ternary quadratic forms. For a given squarefree integer N N , first we give the classification of positive definite ternary quadratic forms of level 4 ​ N 4N explicitly. Second, we give explicit formulas of the weighted sum of representations over each class in every genus of ternary quadratic forms of level 4 ​ N 4N , which are involved with modified Hurwitz class number. In the proof of the main results, we use the relations among ternary quadratic forms, quaternion algebras, and Jacobi forms. As a corollary, we get the formula for the class number of positive ternary quadratic forms of level 4 ​ N 4N . As applications, we derive an explicit base of Eisenstein series space of modular forms of weight 3 / 2 3/2 and level 4 ​ N 4N , and give new proofs of some interesting identities involving representation number of ternary quadratic forms.



Keywords :
ternary quadratic forms, quaternion algebras, Hurwitz class number

Classifications and representations are two main topics in the theory of quadratic forms. Regarding the classifications of ternary quadratic forms, two significant equivalent relations exist, namely equivalence class and semi-equivalence class. \displaystyle=P^{-1}(U^{-1})^{t}PP^{-1}M_{g^{\prime}}P^{-1}PU^{-1}P^{-1}

\displaystyle=(PU^{-1}P^{-1})^{t}P^{-1}M_{g^{\prime}}P^{-1}(PU^{-1}P^{-1})

\displaystyle=(PU^{-1}P^{-1})^{t}M_{f^{\prime}}(PU^{-1}P^{-1}).

Hence
f\sim f^{\prime}
. It follows that this map is injective. For
f\in C(4N,16N^{2}/N_{r})
, it is not hard to check that
\lambda_{4}
is the same as
\phi_{2}
. It is easily to prove that

|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|=|\operatorname{Aut}(\lambda_{4}(f))|.

∎

2.4
Isotropic

Let
f
be a ternary quadratic forms over
\mathbb{Q}
. It can be equivalent over
\mathbb{Q}
to a diagonal form

ax^{2}+by^{2}+cz^{2}.

The Hasse invariant for
f
at
p
is defined to be

S_{p}(f)=(a,-1)_{p}(b,-1)_{p}(c,-1)_{p}(a,b)_{p}(b,c)_{p}(c,a)_{p},

which depends only on the equivalence class of

, and
(a,b)_{p}
is the Hilbert Norm Residue Symbol. We say
f_{p}
is isotropic if
f_{p}(x,y,z)=0
for some non-zero elements

, and it is anisotropic otherwise, where

means the localization of

at

indicates whether

S^{*}_{p}(f)=(-1)^{\delta_{p,2}}S_{p}(f)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $f_{p}$ is isotropic},\\
-1&\text{if $f_{p}$ is anisotropic},\end{cases}

where
\delta_{m,n}=1
if
m=n
and otherwise
0
. Recall that Hilbert’s reciprocity law says
(a,b)_{\infty}\prod_{p}(a,b)_{p}=1
, which futher leads to
S_{\infty}(f)\prod_{p}S_{p}(f)=1
. If
f
is a primitive positive definite ternary quadratic forms, it is not hard to check that

. Hence
f
is anisotropic only at odd number of primes.

It is not hard to check the following proposition.

Proposition 2.8
.

(1)Let
p,q
be primes, and

where
p\nmid d^{\prime}
, then

is isotropic at

if and only if
\phi_{p}(f)
is isotropic at

.

(2)Let
f\in C(8N,64N^{2}/N_{r})
or
f\in C(4N,16N^{2}/N_{r})
, then

is isotropic at

if and only if
\lambda_{4}(f)
is isotropic at

.

Let
f\in C(N^{\prime},p^{h}d^{\prime})
,
p^{g}\parallel N^{\prime}
and

. In terms of Lemma
2.3
, we can assume that

f=(p^{g}a,p^{h-g}b,c,p^{h-g}r,p^{g}s,p^{g}t)

with
a,b,c,r,s
and

integers,
p\nmid ac
. The same process that took some

and constructed automorphs or equivalences involving

can be readily extended to the

f_{q}(x,y,z)=0
for some non-zero elements

For

and

∎

3
Quaternion algebras

In this section we will begin with the basic knowledge about quaternion algebras. For more detailed definition of quaternion algebras one can see
[
13
]
.

For a given filed
F
of characteristic
0
and elements
a,b\in F^{\times}
, we use
Q=\left(\frac{a,b}{F}\right)
for the
F
-algebra which possesses a basis
1,i,j,k=ij=-ji
with
i^{2}=a,j^{2}=b
. In this paper
F
only represents
\mathbb{Q}
or the fields
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of the
p
-adic numbers where
p
is a prime. A quaternion algebra
Q
is a central simple algebra. For the field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
or of real numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
, there are up to isomorphism only two such equivalence classes, the algebras
M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
of
2\times 2
matrices over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, or skew-fields. We use the usual
p
-adic Hilbert symbol
(a,b)_{p}
. The quaternion algebra
Q_{p}=\left(\frac{a,b}{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\right)\simeq M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
if and only if
(a,b)_{p}=1
. In the case of a quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
the algebras
Q=\left(\frac{a,b}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
and
Q^{\prime}=\left(\frac{a^{\prime},b^{\prime}}{\mathbb{Q}}\right)
are isomorphic if and only if
(a,b)_{p}=(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})_{p}
for all
p
and
\infty
. A quaternion algebra
Q
over
\mathbb{Q}
ramifies at
p
if
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes Q
is a skew-field, and splitts at
p
if
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\otimes Q\simeq M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. If it ramifies at
\infty
it is called definite. A quaternion algebra
Q
ramifies only at finitely many primes and this number is always even. Hence given a set
S
of an even number of primes, we can find
a,b
in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
such that
(a,b)_{p}=-1
exactly for
p
in
S
, and then
Q=\left(\frac{a,b}{F}\right)
is a (up to isomorphism) quaternion algebra which ramifies exactly at the primes in
S
.

For any element
\alpha=t+xi+yj+zk
of quaternion algebra
Q
, we define an involution
\overline{\alpha}=t-xi-yj-zk
, which satisfies
\overline{\bar{\alpha}}=\alpha
and
\overline{\alpha\beta}=\bar{\beta}\bar{\alpha}
for
\beta\in Q
. The reduced trace on
Q
is
\mbox{tr}(\alpha)=\alpha+\bar{\alpha}
, and similarly the reduced norm is
\mbox{n}(\alpha)=\alpha\cdot\bar{\alpha}
. If
Q\simeq M_{2}(F)
, and

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in Q,

then

\bar{A}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{pmatrix}.

Hence
\mbox{tr}(A)=a+d,\mbox{n}(A)=ad-bc=\det(A)
. We can see that
a\in Q
is a root of the polynomial

x^{2}-tr(a)x+n(a)=0

which is the reduced characteristic polynomial of
a
.

An ideal of quaternion algebra
Q
is a full
\mathbb{Z}
-lattice of
Q
, that is, a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-submodule of
Q
which contains a basis of
Q
over
\mathbb{Q}
. An order
\mathcal{O}
in a quaternion algebra
Q
is an ideal which is also a ring containing
\mathbb{Z}
. For the rest of this paper
\mathcal{O}
will always denote a quaternion order. If
x\in\mathcal{O}
, then
tr(x),n(x)\in\mathbb{Z}
. An order
\mathcal{O}
is maximal if it is not properly contained in anorher order. An Eichler order
\mathcal{O}
is the intersection of two (not necessarily distinct) maximal orders. For a quaternion algebra over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, Eichler orders can be described more explicitly. A quaternion division algebra
Q_{p}
over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
contains only one maximal order, which is
K+Kj
with
K=\mathbb{Z}_{p}+i\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for odd
p
and
K=\mathbb{Z}_{2}+\frac{i+1}{2}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
p=2
, where
i^{2}=u,j^{2}=p
for some
\left(\frac{u}{p}\right)=-1
. If
Q_{p}
is isomorphic to
M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, there is a characterization of Eichler orders.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
13
, p.372]

For an order
\mathcal{O}\subset Q_{p}\simeq M_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)
\mathcal{O}
is Eichler;

(2)
\mathcal{O}\simeq\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}&\mathbb{Z}_{p}\\
\end{pmatrix};

(3)
\mathcal{O}

The power

\mathcal{O}

The reduced discriminant of

\mbox{disc}(Q)

be the ideal generated by all

is defined to be the square root of the

Theorem 3.2
.

[
13
, p.241,p371]

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset Q
be an order, and

.

(1)
\mathcal{O}
is maximal if and only if
\mbox{discrd}(\mathcal{O})=N
.

(2)
\mathcal{O}

We say

\mathcal{O}

For any pair of relatively prime positive squarefree integers

\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\hskip 9.24994ptm\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8},\\
-1&\text{if}\hskip 9.24994ptm\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8},\\

Set

7.2
Berkovich and Jagy’s genus identity

Berkovich and Jagy
[
1
]
established the following interesting identity connecting the weighted sum of the representation numbers and the sum of three squares

:

r_{3}(p^{2}n)-pr_{3}(n)=48\sum\limits_{f\in TG_{1,p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|}-96\sum\limits_{f\in TG_{2,p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|},

(7.6)

where a sum over forms in a genus should be understood to be the finite sum resulting from taking a single representative from each equivalence class of forms.

We now give a new proof of the identity (
7.6
).
By the level and discriminant of ternary quadratic forms, it becomes evident that
TG_{1,p}
(resp.
TG_{2,p}
) coincides
G_{4p,p^{2},p}
(resp.
G_{4p,16p^{2},p}
).
In terms of the results of Theorem
1.2
, we have

\sum\limits_{f\in TG_{1,p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|}=\sum\limits_{f\in G_{4p,p^{2},p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|}=\frac{1}{4}H^{(p,1)}(4n);

(7.7)

and

\sum\limits_{f\in TG_{2,p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|}=\sum\limits_{f\in G_{4p,16p^{2},p}}\frac{R_{f}(n)}{|\operatorname{Aut}(f)|}=\frac{1}{4}H^{(p,1)}(n).

(7.8)

For a squarefree integer
M
and an odd prime
p
with
(M,p)=1
and a negative discriminant
-D
, one
[
2
]
has

H^{(M,1)}(p^{2}D)-pH^{(M,1)}(D)=H^{(pM,1)}(D).

(7.9)

So we have

(7.10)

and

H^{(p,1)}(16n)-2H^{(p,1)}(4n)=H^{(2p,1)}(4n).

(7.11)

Furthermore, from the above equation we can get

(7.12)

Combining the eqatuions (

In fact, combining Theorem
1.2

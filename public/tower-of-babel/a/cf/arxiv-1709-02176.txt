Representations and Conjugacy Classes of Semisimple Quasitriangular Hopf Algebras
Sebastian BURCIU

Abstract
In this paper we give two general formulae for the Müger centralizers in the category of representations of a semisimple quasitriangular Hopf algebra. The first formula is given in the terms of the Drinfeld map associated to the quasitriangular Hopf algebra. The second formula for the Müger centralizer is given in the terms of the conjugacy classes introduced by Cohen and Westreich in [ J. Algebra 283 (2005), 42–62]. In the case of a factorizable Hopf algebra these formulae extend some particular cases obtained by the author in [ Math. Z. 279 (2015), 227–240].

Introduction

The notion of centralizer in a braided fusion category was introduced by Müger in
[
25
]
. It was shown in
[
12
, Theorem 3.13]
that the centralizer of a nondegenerate fusion subcategory of a braided category is a categorical complement of the nondegenerate subcategory. This principle is the basis of many classification results of braided fusion categories, see for example papers
[
11
,
12
,
15
]
and references therein.

Despite its importance, in the current literature there is no concrete known formula for the Müger centralizer of all fusion subcategories of a given fusion category. Only few particular cases are completely known in the literature. For instance, in the same paper
[
25
]
, Müger described the centralizer of all fusion subcategories of the category of finite-dimensional representations of a Drinfeld double of a finite abelian group. More generally, for the category of representations of a (twisted) Drinfeld double of an arbitrary finite group, not necessarily abelian, a similar formula was then given in
[
26
]
. For the braided center of Tambara–Yamagami categories, in
[
16
]
, the centralizer can be described by computing completely the
S
-matrix of the modular category. In
[
5
]
a different approach gave a partial formula for the centralizer of fusion subcategories of a braided equivariantized fusion category.

Given a fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}
of a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
, the notion of
Müger centralizer of
{\mathcal{D}}
was introduced in
[
12
]
. The centralizer
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is defined as the fusion subcategory
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{C}
generated by all simple objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
satisfying

\displaystyle c_{X,Y}c_{Y,X}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y}

for all objects
Y\in\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{D}})
(see also
[
25
]
). For a fusion category
\mathcal{C}
as usually, we denote by
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
.

If
(A,R)
is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra then the category
\mathrm{Rep}(A)
of finite-dimensional
A
-modules is a braided category with the braiding given by

\displaystyle c_{M,N}\colon\ M\otimes N\rightarrow N\otimes M,\qquad m\otimes n\mapsto R_{21}(n\otimes m)=R^{(2)}n\otimes R^{(1)}m,

for any two objects
M,N\in\mathrm{Rep}(A)
.

Given a quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(A,R)
one can also define the Drinfeld map

\displaystyle\phi_{R}\colon\ A^{*}\rightarrow A,\qquad f\mapsto(f\otimes\id)(R_{21}R)=f(Q_{1})Q_{2},

where
Q=R_{21}R
is the monodromy matrix.

We prove the following theorem which gives a general description for the centralizer of any fusion subcategory of the category of representations of a quasitriangular Hopf algebra:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(A,R)
be a semisimple quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
L
be a left normal coideal subalgebras of
A
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Rep}(A//{L})^{\prime}=\mathrm{Rep}(A//{M}),\qquad\text{where}\quad{M}=\phi_{R}((A//{L})^{*}).

We denote by
F_{0},F_{1},\dots,F_{r}
the central primitive idempotents of the character ring
C(A)
where
F_{0}=t
is the idempotent integral of
A^{*}
. Following
[
7
]
one can define the conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}^{j}
of
A
as
\mathcal{C}^{j}:=\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{j}A^{*}
, where
\Lambda
is an idempotent integral of
A
and
a\leftharpoonup f=\langle f,a_{1}\rangle a_{2}
for all
a\in A
and
f\in A^{*}
. It is well known that these conjugacy classes are the simple
D(A)
-submodules of the induced
D(A)
-module
\Bbbk\uparrow^{D(A)}_{A}\simeq A
, see
[
31
]
.

Let
(A,R)
be a semisimple quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
V_{0}=\Bbbk,\dots,V_{r}
be a complete set of isomorphism classes of irreducible
A
-modules. Let also
\mathrm{Irr}(A)=\{\chi_{0}=\epsilon,\,\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{r}\}
be the set of irreducible characters afforded by these modules and
E_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}(A)
be the associated central primitive idempotent of the irreducible character
\chi_{i}
. Since the Drinfeld map
\phi_{R}\colon C(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}(A)
is an algebra map we may suppose that
\phi_{R}(F_{j})=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}}E_{i}
for some subset
\mathcal{A}_{j}\subseteq\{0,\dots,r\}
. Since
\phi_{R}(1)=1
we obtain a partition for the set of indices of all irreducible representations
\{0,1,\dots,r\}=\bigsqcup\limits_{j\in\mathcal{J}}\mathcal{A}_{j}
. For any
0\leq i\leq r
we denoted by
m(i)
the unique index
j\in\mathcal{J}
such that
i\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
. Therefore in this way we obtain a unique function

\displaystyle m\colon\ \{0,1,\dots,r\}\rightarrow\mathcal{J}

with the property that
E_{i}\phi_{R}(F_{m(i)})\neq 0
for all
i\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}
.

Our second main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
(A,R)
is a semisimple quasitriangular Hopf algebra and
L
is a left normal coideal subalgebra of
A
. With the above notations one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)^{\prime})=\big\{\chi_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{C}^{m(i)}\subseteq L\big\}.

Recall that the quasitriangular Hopf algebra
(A,R)
is called
factorizable
if the Drinfeld map
\phi_{R}\colon A^{*}\rightarrow A
is an isomorphism of algebras. In this case, its restriction
\phi_{R}|_{C(A)}\colon C(A)\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}(A)
is an isomorphism of algebras. For a factorizable semisimple Hopf algebra we can record the primitive central idempotents
F_{j}
of
C(A)
such that
F_{j}:=\phi_{R}^{-1}(E_{j})
any
1\leq j\leq r
. With these notations,
m(i)=i
for any
0\leq i\leq r
and Theorem
1.2
implies the following:

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(A,R)
be a semisimple factorizable Hopf algebra and
L
be a left normal coideal subalgebra of
A
. Then with the above notations one has that

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)^{\prime})=\big\{\chi_{i}\,|\,\mathcal{C}^{i}\subseteq L\big\}.

Shortly, this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall the basic notions of Hopf algebras and fusion categories that are used throughout this paper. In this section we also prove a canonical decomposition of a left normal coideal subalgebra in terms of the decomposition of its integral, see equation (
2.2
). In Section
2.5
we recall the main properties of quasitriangular Hopf algebras and their associated Drinfeld maps. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
and some consequences of it. In particular we apply Theorem
1.1
to the adjoint subcategory of the category of representations of a factorizable Hopf algebra. In this way we obtain a relation, via the Drinfeld map, between the Hopf center and the first commutator of a factorizable Hopf algebra.

In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.2
. Some consequences of this result are also described. In Section
5
we give an example, by considering the semisimple quasitriangular Hopf algebra
H_{8}
of dimension
8
. Based on our results we are able to compute the function
m
in this case and therefore the centralizer of any fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(H_{8})
.

We work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. The comultiplication and antipode of a Hopf algebra are denoted by
\Delta
and
S
respectively. We use Sweedler’s notation for comultiplication with the sigma symbol dropped. All the other Hopf algebra notations are those used in
[
24
]
.

2
Preliminaries

Let
A
be a finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. Then
A
is also cosemisimple and
S^{2}=\id

[
21
]
. The character ring

C(A):=\mathcal{G}_{0}(A)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\Bbbk
is a semisimple subalgebra of
A^{*}
and it has a vector space basis
given by the set
\mathrm{Irr}(A)
of irreducible characters of
A
, see
[
30
]
. Moreover,

C(A)=\mathrm{Cocom}(A^{*})
, the space of cocommutative elements of
A^{*}
. By duality, the
character ring of
A^{*}
is a semisimple subalgebra of
A
and
C(A^{*})=\mathrm{Cocom}(A)
. If

M
is an
A
-representation with character
\chi
then
M^{*}
is also an

A
-representation with character
\chi^{*}=\chi\circ S
. This induces an involution
“
\;{}^{*}\;
”:
C(A)\rightarrow C(A)
on
C(A)
.

Throughout of this paper we denote by
\Lambda
an idempotent integral of
A
and by
t
an idempotent integral of
A^{*}
. Moreover one has that
t(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}
. Recall also
[
20
]
that

\displaystyle\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)\Lambda=\sum_{d\in\mathrm{Irr}(A^{*})}\epsilon(d)d

is the regular character of
A^{*}
. Dually

\displaystyle\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)t=\sum_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(A)}\chi(1)\chi

is the regular character of
A
.

Recall that a
left
coideal subalgebra of
A
is a subalgebra
L\subseteq A
with
\Delta(L)\subset A\otimes L
. Then
L
is called
left normal coideal subalgebra
if
L
is closed under the left adjoint action of
A
, i.e.,
a_{1}lS(a_{2})\in L
for any
l\in L
and any
a\in A
. Recall also from
[
1
]
that given a left coideal subalgebra
L
of
A
there is a unique element
\Lambda_{L}\in L
(called
integral
) such that
l\Lambda_{L}=\epsilon(l)\Lambda_{L}
for all
l\in L
, see also
[
19
]
. Then the coideal subalgebra
L
is normal if and only if
\Lambda_{L}
is central, i.e.,
\Lambda_{L}\in Z(A)
.

For any left normal coideal subalgebra
L
of
A
the augmentation ideal
AL^{+}
is a Hopf ideal and it has the following form
AL^{+}=A(1-\Lambda_{L})=\mathrm{Ann}_{A}(\Lambda_{L})
. Thus one can define the Hopf quotient
A//L:=A/AL^{+}
. It is well known that any fusion subcategories of
\mathrm{Rep}(A)
can be written as
\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)
for some Hopf quotient of
A
.

Remark 2.1
.

Since
A
is free as left
L
-module
[
29
]
it follows that the map

\displaystyle A\otimes_{L}\Bbbk\simeq A\Lambda_{L},\qquad a\otimes_{L}1\mapsto a\Lambda_{L}

is an isomorphism of
A
-modules. Moreover, by
[
2
, Proposition 3.11]
it follows that the regular module of the quotient Hopf algebra
A//L
is isomorphic to the induced module
A\otimes_{L}\Bbbk
.

2.1
Duality between the character ring and the center

Let
A
be a semisimple Hopf algebra over the ground field
\Bbbk
. Let us denote by
\mathrm{Irr}(A)
the set of irreducible characters of
A
. We suppose that
\mathrm{Irr}(A)=\{\chi_{0},\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{r}\}
. Without loss of generality we may suppose that
\chi_{0}=\epsilon
. Let also
E_{0},E_{1},\dots,E_{r}
be the corresponding central primitive central idempotents in
A
.
The evaluation form

\displaystyle C(A)\otimes\mathcal{Z}(A)\rightarrow\Bbbk,\qquad\chi\otimes a\mapsto\chi(a)

is nondegenerate. A pair of dual bases for this form is given by
\big\{\chi_{i},\frac{1}{n_{i}}E_{i}\big\}

since
\big\langle\chi_{i},\frac{1}{n_{j}}E_{j}\big\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq r
.

2.2
Another pair of dual basis in the commutative case

Let
A
be a semisimple Hopf algebra with a commutative character ring. According to
[
7
]
in the case of a commutative ring
C(A)
there is another pair of dual bases corresponding to this nondegenerate form. This pair of dual bases is given in terms of the conjugacy class sums as defined in
[
7
]
.

The
conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}^{j}
of
A
are defined as
\mathcal{C}^{j}=\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{j}A^{*}
, where
\Lambda=\Lambda_{A}
is a two-sided idempotent integral of
A
and
\{F_{j}\}_{j}
is the (complete) set of central primitive idempotents of the semisimple algebra
C(A)
. This notion of conjugacy classes generalizes the usual notion of conjugacy classes in finite groups.

Example 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
A=\Bbbk G
be the associated group algebra. It is easy to see that the conjugacy classes as defined above coincide with the usual notion of conjugacy class in a group. Indeed, let
{\mathcal{C}}_{0},{\mathcal{C}}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{r}
be the usual conjugacy classes of
G
.
Then the set of central primitive idempotents of
C(\Bbbk G)
can be described as
p_{j}=\sum\limits_{h\in\mathcal{C}^{j}}p_{h}
where
p_{h}\in\Bbbk^{G}
is defined as
p_{h}(g)=\delta_{g,h}
. Since
\Lambda=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum\limits_{g\in G}g
it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{j}=\Lambda\leftharpoonup p_{j}\Bbbk^{G}=\Bbbk[\mathcal{C}_{j}]

is the vector sub-space of
\Bbbk G
generated by all group elements of
\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

Recall that the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}\colon A^{*}\rightarrow A
defined by
f\mapsto\Lambda\leftharpoonup f
for any
f\in A^{*}
is a
\Bbbk
-linear isomorphism. Since
A^{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{j=0}^{r}F_{j}A^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}^{j}=\mathcal{F}(F_{j}A^{*})
one has

\displaystyle A=\bigoplus_{j=0}^{r}{\mathcal{C}^{j}}.

One can also define the corresponding
conjugacy class sums

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{j}=\Lambda\leftharpoonup(\dim A)F_{j}.

Note that
{\bf C}_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}(A)
and since
\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{Z}(A)=\dim_{\Bbbk}C(A)
it follows that
{\mathcal{C}^{j}}\cap\mathcal{Z}(A)=\Bbbk{\bf C}_{j}
. Note also that
\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)\Lambda=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}{\bf C}_{j}
. Indeed,
\Lambda=\Lambda\leftharpoonup\epsilon=\Lambda\leftharpoonup\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}F_{j}=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\mathbf{C}_{j}
.

Example 2.3
.

If
A=\Bbbk G
then

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{j}=\bigg(\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}g\bigg)\leftharpoonup|G|p_{j}=\sum_{g\in G}p_{j}(g)g=\sum_{h\in C_{j}}h

is the usual class sum of a conjugacy class
\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

Remark 2.4
.

By the class equation for semisimple Hopf algebras, see
[
22
]
, one has that the value
n_{j}:=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}A^{*}}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A^{*}F_{j})}
is an integer. Moreover as in
[
7
, equation (11)]
one can deduce that
F_{j}(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{n_{j}}
.

Example 2.5
.

If
A=\Bbbk G
then
n_{j}=\frac{|G|}{|C_{j}|}
is the order of the centralizer of any group element
g_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

This implies that a second pair of dual bases for the form of equation (
2.1
) can be given by
\big\{F_{i},\frac{n_{i}}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\bf C}_{i}\big\}
, see also
[
7
, equation (17)]
. This can be written as
\big\langle F_{i},\frac{n_{j}}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\bf C}_{j}\big\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
.

Indeed, one has

\displaystyle\left\langle F_{i},\frac{n_{j}}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\bf C}_{j}\right\rangle=\frac{n_{j}}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}\langle F_{i},\Lambda\leftharpoonup(\dim_{\Bbbk}(A))F_{j}\rangle={n_{j}}\langle F_{j}F_{i},\Lambda\rangle=\delta_{i,j}n_{j}F_{j}(\Lambda)=\delta_{i,j}.

2.3
Decomposition of the integral

Let
L
be a left normal coideal subalgebra of a semisimple Hopf algebra
A
with a commutative character ring
C(A)
. We shall use the notation
\lambda_{L}\in(A//L)^{*}
for the idempotent integral of the Hopf algebra
(A//L)^{*}
. Clearly
\lambda_{L}\in C((A//L)^{*})\subset C(A^{*})
and we may suppose that

\displaystyle\lambda_{L}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}}F_{j}

(2.1)

for some subset of indices
\mathcal{I}_{L}\subseteq\{0,1,\dots,r\}
. Note that by
[
10
, Lemma 1.1]

\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}:=\Lambda\leftharpoonup\lambda_{L}
is a left integral for
L
. It follows from above that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}}\mathbf{C}_{j}.

Then one has

\displaystyle L=\Lambda_{L}\leftharpoonup A^{*}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}}{\bf C}_{j}\leftharpoonup A^{*}=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}}{\mathcal{C}^{j}}.

Note also that

\displaystyle\epsilon(\mathbf{C}_{j})=\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)F_{j}(\Lambda)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{n_{j}}=\dim_{\Bbbk}(F_{j}A^{*})=\dim_{\Bbbk}\big(\mathcal{C}^{j}\big).

Thus

\displaystyle\epsilon\big(\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}\big)=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{L}}\epsilon(\mathbf{C}_{j})\bigg)=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}\bigg(\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{L}}\dim_{\Bbbk}\big(\mathcal{C}^{j}\big)\bigg)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}.

It follows then that

\displaystyle\Lambda_{L}=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}=\frac{1}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}}\mathbf{C}_{j}

(2.2)

is a formula for the idempotent integral of
L
.

Define the functional
p_{{\mathcal{C}^{j}}}\in A^{*}
as the unique linear functional that coincides to
\epsilon
on
{\mathcal{C}^{j}}
and it is equal to zero on the other conjugacy classes
\mathcal{C}_{l}
with
l\neq j
. The following lemma was proven in
[
3
, Theorem 5.13]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Suppose that
A
is a semisimple Hopf algebra with a commutative character ring
C(A)
. Let
\{F_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq r}
be a complete set of central primitive idempotents of
C(A)
. Then
F_{j}=p_{{\mathcal{C}^{j}}}
for all
0\leq j\leq r
.

Equation (
2.2
) and the above lemma implies the following:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
L
be a left normal coideal subalgebra of a semisimple Hopf algebra
A
. With the above notations one has
j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}\iff F_{j}(\Lambda_{L})\neq 0
.

Let
A
be a semisimple Hopf algebra with a commutative character ring
C(A)
. Then
\{F_{j}\}
form a
\Bbbk
-linear basis for
C(A)
and for any character
\chi\in C(A)
one can write
\chi=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{r}\alpha_{\chi,j}F_{j}
with
\alpha_{\chi,j}\in\Bbbk
.
Previous lemma implies the following result, see also
[
8
, Theorem 1.12]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
A
be a semisimple Hopf algebra with a commutative character ring and
\chi\in C(A)
. Then one has

\chi\in C(A//L)\iff\chi F_{j}=\chi(1)F_{j}
for all
j\in\mathcal{I}_{L}.

Proof.

We may suppose that
\chi=\chi_{M}
is the character of an
A
-module
M
. If
\chi\in C(A//L)
then
\chi\lambda_{L}=\chi(1)\lambda_{L}
and equation (
2.1
) implies that
\chi F_{j}=\chi(1)F_{j}
, for any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{L}
. Conversely if
\chi F_{j}=\chi(1)F_{j}
for all
j\in J_{L}
then
\chi\lambda_{L}=\lambda_{L}\chi(1)
. Thus

\displaystyle\chi(\Lambda_{L})=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\chi\big(\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}\big)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\chi(\lambda_{L}\rightharpoonup\Lambda)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\chi\lambda_{L}(\Lambda)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}\chi(1)\lambda_{L}(\Lambda).\!

On the other hand note that
\lambda_{L}(\Lambda)=\epsilon\big(\widetilde{\Lambda_{L}}\big)=\frac{\dim_{\Bbbk}(L)}{\dim_{\Bbbk}(A)}
by equation (
2.2
). Thus
\chi(\Lambda_{L})=\chi(1)
which shows that the restriction of the
A
-module
M
to
L
is trivial. It follows that
M\in\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)
.
∎

Example 2.9
.

If
A=\Bbbk G
then a left normal coideal subalgebra of
A
is of the form
L=\Bbbk N
for some normal subgroup
N\unlhd G
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{L}=\sum_{C_{j}\subseteq N}p_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{I}_{L}=\{j\,|\,C_{j}\subseteq N\}.

2.4
Left kernels and Burnside formula

Let
M
be an
A
-module and let
\mathrm{LKer}_{{}_{A}}(M)
be the
left kernel of
M
. Recall
[
1
]
that
\mathrm{LKer}_{{}_{A}}(M)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{LKer}_{{}_{A}}(M)=\{a\in A\,|\,a_{1}\otimes a_{2}m=a\otimes m,\,\text{for all}\,m\in M\}.

Then by
[
1
]
it follows that
\mathrm{LKer}_{A}(M)
is the largest left coideal subalgebra of
A
that acts trivially on
M
. It is also a left normal coideal subalgebra. For example, if
A=\Bbbk G
is the group algebra of a finite group
G
and
M
is a
\Bbbk G
-module then
\mathrm{LKer}_{A}(M)=\Bbbk\ker_{G}(M)
, where
\ker_{G}(M)=\{g\in G\,|\,g.m=m,\,\text{for all}\,m\in M\}
is the usual kernel of
M
.

Next theorem generalizes a well known result of Brauer in the representation theory of finite groups.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
1
, Theorem 4.2.1]
)
.

Suppose that
M
is a finite-dimensional module over a semisimple Hopf algebra
A
. Then

\displaystyle\langle M\rangle=\mathrm{Rep}(A//\mathrm{LKer}_{A}(M)),

where
\langle M\rangle
is the fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(A)
generated by
M
.

This implies that for any left normal coideal subalgebra
L
of
A
one has that

\displaystyle\bigcap_{M\in\mathrm{Irr}(A//L)}\mathrm{LKer}_{A}(M)=L.

(2.3)

The previous theorem also implies that any fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(A)
is of the type
\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)
for some left normal coideal subalgebra
L
of
A
. Moreover, for any
V\in\mathrm{Rep}(A)
one has

\displaystyle M\in\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)\iff\mathrm{LKer}_{A}(M)\supseteq L.

2.5
Quasitriangular and factorizable Hopf algebras

Recall that a Hopf algebra
A
is called
quasitriangular
if
A
admits an
R
-matrix, i.e., an element
R\in A\otimes A
satisfying the following properties:

1)

R\Delta(x)=\Delta^{\mathrm{cop}}(x)R
for all
x\in A
,

2)

(\Delta\otimes\id)(R)=R^{(1)}\otimes r^{(1)}\otimes R^{(2)}r^{(2)}
,

3)

(\id\otimes\Delta)(R)=R^{(1)}r^{(1)}\otimes r^{(2)}\otimes R^{(2)}
,

4)

Here
R=r=R^{(1)}\otimes R^{(2)}=r^{(1)}\otimes r^{(2)}
. If

is a quasitriangular Hopf algebra then the category of representations is a braided fusion category with the braiding given by

\displaystyle c_{M,N}\colon\ M\otimes N\rightarrow N\otimes M,\qquad m\otimes n\mapsto R_{21}(n\otimes m)=R^{(2)}n\otimes R^{(1)}m

for any two left
A
-modules

(see
[

]
). Recall that

.
Denote

. Then the monodromy of two objects is defined as

\displaystyle c_{M,N}c_{N,M}\colon\ M\otimes N\rightarrow N\otimes M,\qquad(m\otimes n)\mapsto R^{(2)}R^{(1)}m\otimes R^{(2)}R^{(1)}n=Q(m\otimes n).

A quasitriangular Hopf algebra

is called

if and only if the Drinfeld map

\displaystyle\phi_{R}\colon\ A^{*}\rightarrow A,\qquad f\mapsto(f\otimes\id)(R_{21}R)

is an isomorphism of vector spaces. In this situation, following

maps the character ring

onto the center
\mathcal{Z}(A)
of

and the restriction

is an isomorphism of algebras.

Remark 2.11
.

By
[
27
, Lemma 4.1]
one has that

is a left normal coideal for any subcoalgebra

of

.

One can also define the map
{\;{}_{R}\phi}\colon A^{*}\rightarrow A

and

\Bbbk

Remark 2.12

By
[

one has that

In the case of a factorizable Hopf algebra

of a factorizable Hopf algebra
A
. Note that Proposition
4.3
generalizes the above result from normal Hopf subalgebras
K
to left normal coideal subalgebras
L
of
A
. It also drops the factorizability assumption on
A
.

Define
C_{V_{i}}:=C_{\chi_{i}}\subset A^{*}
as the subcoalgebra of
A^{*}
generated by
\chi_{i}
. By
[
9
, Lemma 4.2(i)]
, in the factorizable case one has that
\phi_{R}(C_{V_{i}})=\mathcal{C}_{i}.
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Remark 4.8
.

Let
A
be a semisimple factorizable Hopf algebra and
L
a left normal coideal subalgebra of
A
. If
{\mathcal{D}}:=\mathrm{Rep}(A//L)
then

(A//L)^{*}=\bigoplus\limits_{\chi_{j}\in\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{D}})}C_{V_{j}}

and
L^{\circ}=\phi_{R}((A//L)^{*})=\bigoplus\limits_{\chi_{j}\in\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{D}})}\phi_{R}(C_{V_{j}})
, i.e.,
L^{\circ}=\bigoplus\limits_{\chi_{j}\in\mathcal{O}({\mathcal{D}})}\mathcal{C}^{j}
. This gives another proof for Theorem
1.3
in the case of a factorizable Hopf algebra since in this case
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime\prime}={\mathcal{D}}
.

5
Example
\boldsymbol{H_{8}}

In this section we compute the centralizer of any fusion subcategory of the quasi-triangular Hopf algebra
H_{8}
, the unique semisimple non-trivial Hopf algebra of dimension
8
. We note that the category of representations
\mathrm{Rep}(H_{8})
is a braided Tambara–Yamagami category and therefore
\mathrm{Rep}(H_{8})\subset\mathrm{Rep}(D(H_{8}))
. The
S
-matrix of the center of a Tambara–Yamagami was computed in
[
16
]
. Using this one can describe completely the centralizer of any fusion subcategory of
\mathrm{Rep}(H_{8})
. However, we decided to include this example here to illustrate how Theorem
1.2
can be applied in a concrete example.

The eight-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra (see
[
17
,
23
]
) is generated by
\{x,y,z\}
subject to the relations

\displaystyle x^{2}=y^{2}=z^{2}=1,\qquad xz=zx,\qquad zy=yz,\qquad xyz=yx.

The comultiplication is given by

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=xe_{0}\otimes x+xe_{1}\otimes y,\qquad\Delta(y)=ye_{1}\otimes x+ye_{0}\otimes y,\qquad\Delta(z)=z\otimes z,

(5.1)

and the counit by
\epsilon(x)=\epsilon(y)=\epsilon(z)=1.
The antipode has the formulae

\displaystyle S(x)=xe_{0}+ye_{1},\qquad S(y)=xe_{1}+ye_{0},\qquad S(z)=z.

Based on equation (
5.1
) one can compute that

\displaystyle\Delta(xy)=xye_{0}\otimes xy+xye_{1}\otimes yx,\qquad\Delta(yx)=yxe_{0}\otimes yx+yxe_{1}\otimes xy.

It can also be checked that

\displaystyle\Delta(xz)=xe_{0}\otimes xz-xe_{1}\otimes yz,\qquad\Delta(yz)=ye_{0}\otimes yz-ye_{1}\otimes xz.

Since
z
is a central element in
H_{8}
, there are two central orthogonal idempotents:

\displaystyle e_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(1+z),\qquad e_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-z).

5.1
Dual basis of
\boldsymbol{H^{*}}

H_{8}
has a
\Bbbk
-linear basis given by the set of elements
\{1,x,y,z,{xy},{yx},{xz},{yz}\}
. We consider its linear dual basis on
H_{8}^{*}
given by
\{p_{1},p_{x},p_{y},p_{z},p_{xy},p_{yx},p_{xz},p_{yz}\}
.

It is easy to see that the idempotent integrals of
H_{8}
and
H_{8}^{*}
are given by

\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{e_{0}}{4}(1+x+y+xy)=\frac{1}{8}(1+x+y+xy+z+zx+zy+yx),\qquad\lambda=p_{1}.

5.2
\boldsymbol{H_{8}^{*}}
representations

Since
H_{8}
is a self dual Hopf algebra
[
13
]
it has also four-1-dimensional representations given by the group like elements of
H_{8}
and a 2-dimensional representation.
One has that

\displaystyle G(H_{8})=\{1,g_{1},g_{2},z\},

with
g_{1}=xy(e_{0}+ie_{1})
,
g_{2}=xy(e_{0}-ie_{1})
. It can be easily checked that
g_{1}g_{2}=z
,
zg_{i}=g_{i}
and
g_{i}^{2}=1
. Moreover, the set of central grouplike elements of
H_{8}
is given by
\bar{G}(H_{8})=\{1,z\}
.

5.2.1
On the 2-dimensional comodule

From equation (
5.1
) one can compute that

\displaystyle\Delta(xe_{0})=xe_{0}\otimes xe_{0}+xe_{1}\otimes ye_{1},\qquad

\displaystyle\Delta(xe_{1})=xe_{0}\otimes xe_{1}+xe_{1}\otimes ye_{0},

\displaystyle\Delta(ye_{0})=ye_{0}\otimes ye_{0}+ye_{1}\otimes xe_{1},\qquad

\displaystyle\Delta(ye_{1})=ye_{0}\otimes ye_{1}+ye_{1}\otimes xe_{0}.

This shows that
W=\Bbbk w_{1}\oplus\Bbbk w_{2}
is a left
H_{8}
-comodule with
the comodule structure given by

\displaystyle\rho(w_{1})=xe_{0}\otimes w_{1}+xe_{1}\otimes w_{2},\qquad\rho(w_{2})=ye_{1}\otimes w_{1}+ye_{0}\otimes w_{2}.

Thus

\displaystyle H_{8}=\Bbbk 1\oplus\Bbbk g_{1}\oplus\Bbbk g_{2}\oplus\Bbbk z\oplus(\Bbbk xe_{0}\oplus\Bbbk ye_{1})\oplus(\Bbbk ye_{0}\oplus\Bbbk xe_{1})

(5.2)

is a decomposition of
H_{8}
into simple left
H_{8}
-comodules. We denote
M_{0}=\Bbbk 1
,
M_{1}=\Bbbk g_{1}
,
M_{2}=\Bbbk g_{2}
,
M_{3}=\Bbbk z
the four one-dimensional
H_{8}^{*}
-modules. Moreover,
M_{4}:=(\Bbbk xe_{0}\oplus\Bbbk ye_{1})
is an irreducible
H_{8}^{*}
-module and
M_{4}\simeq M_{5}:=(\Bbbk ye_{0}\oplus\Bbbk xe_{1})
.

5.3
Fourier transform

Based on the comultiplication formulae one can compute the Fourier transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\colon\ H_{8}^{*}\rightarrow H_{8},\qquad f\mapsto\dim_{\Bbbk}(H_{8})f\rightharpoonup\Lambda.

After some computations it follows that under
\mathcal{F}
one has

\displaystyle p_{1}\mapsto 1,\qquad p_{z}\mapsto z,\;p_{x}\mapsto xe_{0}+ye_{1},\qquad p_{xz}\mapsto xe_{0}-ye_{1}

and

\displaystyle p_{y}\mapsto ye_{0}+xe_{1},\qquad p_{yz}\mapsto ye_{0}-xe_{1},\qquad p_{xy}\mapsto yx,\qquad p_{yx}\mapsto xy.

5.4
Irreducible
\boldsymbol{H_{8}}
-modules and their characters

H_{8}
has four-1-dimensional modules
V_{0}
,
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
,
V_{3}
and a 2-dimensional irreducible module
V
. The action of the generators on these modules is given as follows.

For
V_{0}
, the trivial
H
-module the action is given by
xv=yv=zv=v
and therefore the character is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{0}=p_{1}+p_{z}+p_{x}+p_{y}+p_{xy}+p_{yx}+p_{xz}+p_{yz}.

For
V_{1}
the action is given by
xv=-v
,
yv=v
,
zv=v.
The character
\chi_{1}
of
V_{1}
has
\chi_{1}(1)=\chi_{1}(z)=\chi_{1}(y)=\chi_{1}(yz)=1
,
\chi_{1}(x)=\chi_{1}(yx)=\chi_{1}(xy)=\chi_{1}(xz)=-1
. Thus

\displaystyle\chi_{1}=p_{1}+p_{z}-p_{x}+p_{y}-p_{xy}-p_{yx}-p_{xz}+p_{yz}.

For
V_{2}
the action is given by
xv=-v
,
yv=-v
,
zv=v
and the character is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{2}=p_{1}+p_{z}-p_{x}-p_{y}+p_{xy}+p_{yx}-p_{xz}-p_{yz}.

For
V_{3}
one has
xv=v
,
yv=-v
,
zv=v
and the character is given by

\displaystyle\chi_{3}=p_{1}+p_{z}+p_{x}-p_{y}-p_{xy}-p_{yx}+p_{xz}-p_{yz}.

For
V_{4}=V
, the 2-dimensional simple module, if
V=\Bbbk v_{1}\oplus\Bbbk v_{2}
then the action of generators given by

\displaystyle xv_{1}=v_{1},\qquad yv_{1}=v_{2},\qquad zv_{1}=-v_{1},\qquad xv_{2}=-v_{2},\qquad yv_{2}=v_{1},\qquad zv_{2}=-v_{2}.

The character
\chi_{4}
of

has the values
\chi_{4}(1)=2\chi_{4}(z)=-2
,
\chi_{4}(x)=\chi_{4}(y)=\chi_{4}(yx)=\chi_{4}(xy)=\chi_{4}(xz)=\chi_{4}(zx)=0

\displaystyle\chi_{4}=2p_{1}-2p_{z}.

5.4.1
Multiplication of the characters

It can be easily checked that
\chi_{4}^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{3}\chi_{i}
, and
\chi_{i}\chi_{4}=\chi_{4}\chi_{i}=\chi_{i}
for all

5.5
Central primitive idempotent of the character ring

Based on the above multiplication, the central primitive idempotents of

can be computed as follows

\displaystyle F_{0}=\frac{1}{8}(\epsilon+\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\chi_{3}+2\chi_{4})=p_{1}\qquad\text{is the integral of $H_{8}^{*}$},

\displaystyle F_{1}=\frac{1}{4}(\epsilon+\chi_{1}-\chi_{2}-\chi_{3})=p_{y}+p_{yz},\qquad F_{2}=\frac{1}{4}(\epsilon-\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}-\chi_{3})=p_{xy}+p_{yx},

\displaystyle F_{3}=\frac{1}{4}(\epsilon-\chi_{1}-\chi_{2}+\chi_{3})=p_{x}+p_{xz},\qquad F_{4}=\frac{1}{8}(\epsilon+\chi_{1}+\chi_{2}+\chi_{3}-2\chi_{4})=p_{z}.

Note that

is the central primitive idempotent of

attached to the central grouplike element

Using the above formulae for the central primitive idempotents since

\displaystyle{C}_{0}=8(\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{0})=1,\qquad{C}_{1}=8(\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{1})=2ye_{0},

\displaystyle{C}_{2}=8(\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{2})=xy+yx=g_{1}+g_{2},

\displaystyle{C}_{3}=8(\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{3})=2xe_{0},\qquad{C}_{4}=8(\Lambda\leftharpoonup F_{4})=z.

5.7

Using the antipode formulae one can see that the adjoint action of

on itself can be given by

\displaystyle x.a=xax,\qquad y.a=yay,\qquad z.a=a,\qquad\text{for all}\ a\in H_{8}.

Moreover, by the above description of the left adjoint action it can be checked each of the above five subspaces is closed under the adjoint action of

Clearly, the first two subspaces are irreducible

It can be checked directly that the third

Since

Similarly one can check that the simple

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}^{3}=\Bbbk xe_{0}\oplus\Bbbk ye_{1}.

By the same argument

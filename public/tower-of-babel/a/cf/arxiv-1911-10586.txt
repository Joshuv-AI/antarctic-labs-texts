( G ′ / G ) (G^{\prime}/G) -Expansion Method and Weierstrass Elliptic Function Method Applied to Coupled Wave Equation
E.V. Krishnan, M. Al Ghabshi, M. Alquran

Abstract
This paper deals with the exact solutions of a nonlinear coupled coupled wave equation. The ( G ′ / G ) (G^{\prime}/G) -expansion method has been applied to derive kink solutions and singular wave solutions. The restrictions on the coefficients of the governing equations have also been investigated. Solitary wave solutions have also been derived for this system of equations using Weierstrass elliptic function method.

2
The
(G^{\prime}/G)
-Expansion Method

Consider the nonlinear partial differential equation (PDE)

\displaystyle P(u,u_{t},u_{x},u_{tt},u_{xt},u_{xx},...)=0,

(1)

where
u(x,t)
is an unknown function,
P
is a polynomial in
u=u(x,t)
and its various partial derivatives. The traveling wave
variable
\xi=x-ct
reduces the PDE (1) to the ordinary
differential equation (ODE)

\displaystyle P(u,-cu^{\prime},u^{\prime},-c^{2}u^{\prime\prime},-cu^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime\prime},...)=o,

(2)

where
u=u(\xi)
and
′
denotes differentiation with
respect to
\xi
.

We suppose that the solution of equation (2) can be expressed by a
polynomial in
\displaystyle\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)
as
follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{i},\,\,\,a_{m}\neq 0,

(3)

where
a_{i}(i=0,1,2,..)
are constants.

Here,
G
satisfies the second order linear ODE

\displaystyle\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(\xi)+\lambda G^{\prime}(\xi)+\mu G(\xi)=0,

(4)

with
\lambda
and
\mu
being constants. The positive integer
m

can be determined by a balance between the highest order derivative
term and the nonlinear term appearing in equation (2). By
substituting equation (3) into equation (2) and using equation (4),
we get a polynomial in
\displaystyle G^{\prime}/G
. The
coefficients of various powers of
\displaystyle G^{\prime}/G
give
rise to a set of algebraic
equations for
a_{i}\,(i=0,1,2,...,m),\,\lambda
and
\mu
.

The general solution of equation (4) is a linear combination of

{\rm{sinh}}
and
{\rm{\cosh}}
or of sine and cosine functions if

\Delta=\lambda^{2}-4\mu>0
or
\Delta=\lambda^{2}-4\mu<0
, respectively. In this paper we consider only the first case and
so,

\displaystyle G(\xi)=\displaystyle e^{-\lambda\xi/2}\left(C_{1}\,{\rm{sinh}}(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-4\mu}}{2}\,\xi)+C_{2}\,{\rm{cosh}}(\frac{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-4\mu}}{2}\,\xi)\right),

(5)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are arbitrary constants.

3
A Coupled Wave Equation

Consider the system of PDEs

\displaystyle u_{t}+\alpha v^{2}v_{x}+\beta u^{2}u_{x}+\eta uu_{x}+\gamma u_{xxx}=0,

(6)

\displaystyle v_{t}+\sigma(uv)_{x}+\epsilon vv_{x}=0,

(7)

where,
\alpha,\beta,\eta,\gamma,\sigma
and
\epsilon
are
constants.

We seek TWSs of equations (6) and (7) in the form
u=u(\xi),\quad v=v(\xi),\quad\xi=x-ct.

Then equations (6) and (7) give

\displaystyle-cu^{\prime}+\alpha v^{2}v^{\prime}+\beta u^{2}u^{\prime}+\eta uu^{\prime}+\gamma u^{\prime\prime\prime}=0,

(8)

\displaystyle-cv^{\prime}+\sigma(uv)^{\prime}+\epsilon vv^{\prime}=0.

(9)

Integrate equation (9) with respect to
\xi

-cv+\sigma(uv)+\dfrac{\epsilon}{2}v^{2}=k,

(10)

where
k
is the integration constant.

Dividing equation (10) by
v
, we obtain

-c+\sigma u+\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}v=\frac{k}{v}.

(11)

So, for the solutions to be uniformly valid, the integration
constant
k
should be set equal to
0
.

Therefore, equation (11) can be written as

\displaystyle v=\displaystyle\frac{2(c-\sigma u)}{\epsilon}.

(12)

Substituting equation (12) into equation (8), we obtain

-cu^{\prime}-\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma}{\epsilon^{3}}(c^{2}-2c\sigma u+\sigma^{2}u^{2})u^{\prime}+\beta u^{2}u^{\prime}+\eta uu^{\prime}+\gamma u^{\prime\prime\prime}=0.

(13)

Integrating equation (13) with respect to
\xi
and assuming the
boundary conditions
\displaystyle u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0

{\rm{as}}\,\,|\xi|\longrightarrow\infty
,    we have

\gamma u^{\prime\prime}-\bigg\{c+\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma c^{2}}{\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\}u+\bigg\{\dfrac{\eta}{2}+\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma^{2}c}{\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\}u^{2}+\bigg\{\dfrac{\beta}{3}-\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma^{3}}{3\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\}u^{3}=0.

(14)

We rewrite equation (14) as

\gamma u^{\prime\prime}+Au+Bu^{2}+Cu^{3}=0,

(15)

where

\displaystyle A=-\bigg\{c+\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma c^{2}}{\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\},\,\,\,B=\bigg\{\dfrac{\eta}{2}+\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma^{2}c}{\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\},\;C=\bigg\{\dfrac{\beta}{3}-\dfrac{8\alpha\sigma^{3}}{3\epsilon^{3}}\bigg\}.

(16)

With the change of variable
w=u+\delta
, equation (15) can be
reduced to

\gamma w^{\prime\prime}+c_{1}w+c_{2}w^{3}+c_{3}=0,

(17)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\delta=-\frac{B}{3C},\,\,\,\,c_{1}=\frac{3AC-B^{2}}{3C},\,\,\,\,c_{2}=C,\,\,\,\,c_{3}=\frac{2B^{3}-9ABC}{27C^{2}}.

(18)

Assuming the expansion
\displaystyle w(\xi)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}a_{i}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{i},\,\,\,a_{m}\neq 0
in eq. (17)
and balancing the nonlinear term and the derivative term, we get
m+2=3m
so that
m=1
.

So, we assume the solution of equation (17) in the form

\displaystyle w(\xi)=a_{0}+a_{1}\displaystyle\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right),\,\,a_{1}\neq 0.

(19)

So, we can obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle w^{\prime}(\xi)=-a_{1}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{2}-\lambda a_{1}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)-\mu a_{1},

(20)

\displaystyle\displaystyle w^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=2a_{1}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{3}+3a_{1}\lambda\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{2}+(a_{1}\lambda^{2}+2a_{1}\mu)\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)+a_{1}\lambda\mu,

(21)

\displaystyle\displaystyle w^{3}(\xi)=a_{1}^{3}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{3}+3a_{0}a_{1}^{2}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{2}+3a_{0}^{2}a_{1}\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)+a_{0}^{3}.

(22)

Now, substituting equations (19), (21) and (22) into equation (17)
and collecting the coefficients of
\displaystyle\left(\frac{G^{\prime}}{G}\right)^{i},i=0,1,2,3
, we get

\displaystyle\gamma a_{1}\lambda\mu+c_{1}a_{0}+c_{2}a_{0}^{3}+c_{3}=0,

(23)

\displaystyle\gamma a_{1}\lambda^{2}+2\gamma a_{1}\mu+c_{1}a_{1}+3c_{2}a_{0}^{2}a_{1}=0,

(24)

\displaystyle 3a_{1}\lambda\gamma+3a_{0}a_{1}^{2}c_{2}=0,

(25)

\displaystyle 2\gamma a_{1}+c_{2}a_{1}^{3}=0.

(26)

From equation (26), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{1}=\pm\sqrt{-\frac{2\gamma}{c_{2}}}.

(27)

Equation (25) leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle a_{0}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\lambda\sqrt{-\frac{2\gamma}{c_{2}}}.

(28)

When
\mu=0
in equation (24), we get
\lambda=\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}
and when
\lambda=0
,   we get
\displaystyle\mu=-\frac{c_{1}}{2\gamma}.
In both cases,

\displaystyle\Delta=\lambda^{2}-4\mu=\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}.

Equation (23) gives a constraint condition on the coefficients in
the governing equation.

Case 1
:
\displaystyle\mu=0,\,\,\lambda=\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}.

\displaystyle u_{1}(x,t)=\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{-\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}}\left[1+\frac{(C_{1}-C_{2})\left(1-{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)\right)}{C_{1}{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)+C_{2}}\right]+\frac{B}{3C}.

(29)

Case 2
:
\displaystyle\mu=0,\,\,\lambda=-\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}.

\displaystyle u_{2}(x,t)=\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{-\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}}\left[1+\frac{(C_{1}+C_{2})\left(1-{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)\right)}{C_{2}-C_{1}{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)}\right]+\frac{B}{3C}.

(30)

Case 3
:
\displaystyle\lambda=0,\,\,\mu=-\frac{c_{1}}{2\gamma}.

\displaystyle u_{3}(x,t)=\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{-\frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}}}\left[\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)}{C_{1}{\rm{tanh}}\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2c_{1}}{\gamma}}(x-ct)+C_{2}}\right]+\frac{B}{3C}.

(31)

In all three cases,
\gamma
and
c_{1}
should have the same signs
and
c_{2}
should be of opposite sign and
C_{1}\neq\pm C_{2}
.

Figure 1 and Figure 2 represent the solutions given by equation
(29).

Figure 1
:
The solution for
u(x,t)
,
C_{1}=0,C_{2}=1
.

Figure 2
:
The solution for
u(x,t)
,
C_{1}=1,C_{2}=0
.

Using equation (12), we can write down the corresponding solutions

v_{1}(x,t),\,v_{2}(x,t)
and

4
Weierstrass Elliptic Function Solutions of the Coupled Wave Equation

The Weierstrass elliptic function (WEF)
\wp(\xi;g_{2},g_{3})
with
invariants
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\wp^{\prime}}^{2}=4\wp^{3}-g_{2}\wp-g_{3},

(32)

where
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
are related by the inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{2}^{3}-27g_{3}^{2}>0.

(33)

The WEF
\wp(\xi)
is related to the JEFs by the following
relations:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm{sn}}(\xi)=\left[\wp(\xi)-e_{3}\right]^{-1/2},

(34)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm{cn}}(\xi)=\left[\frac{\wp(\xi)-e_{1}}{\wp(\xi)-e_{3}}\right]^{1/2},

(35)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\rm{dn}}(\xi)=\left[\frac{\wp(\xi)-e_{2}}{\wp(\xi)-e_{3}}\right]^{1/2},

(36)

where
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle 4z^{3}-g_{2}z-g_{3}=0

(37)

with

\displaystyle\displaystyle e_{1}=\frac{1}{3}(2-m^{2}),\,\,\,e_{2}=\frac{1}{3}(2m^{2}-1),\,\,\,e_{3}=-\frac{1}{3}(1+m^{2}).

(38)

From equation (38), one can see that the modulus
m
of the JEF and
the
e^{\prime}s
of the WEF are related by

\displaystyle\displaystyle m^{2}=\frac{e_{2}-e_{3}}{e_{1}-e_{3}}.

(39)

We consider the ODE of order
2k
given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{d^{2k}\phi}{d\xi^{2k}}=f(\phi;r+1),

(40)

where
f(\phi;r+1)
is an
(r+1)
degree polynomial in
\phi
.

We assume that

\displaystyle\phi=\gamma Q^{2s}(\xi)+\mu

(41)

is a solution of equation (40), where
\gamma
and
\mu
are
arbitrary constants and
Q^{(2s)}(\xi)
is the
(2s)^{\rm{th}}

derivative of the reciprocal Weierstrass elliptic function (RWEF)

\displaystyle Q(\xi)=\frac{1}{\wp(\xi)},\wp(\xi)
being the WEF.

It can be shown that the
(2s)^{\rm{th}}
derivative of the RWEF

Q(\xi)
is a
(2s+1)
degree polynomial in
Q(\xi)
itself.

Therefore, for
\phi
to be a solution of equation (40), we should
have the relation

\displaystyle 2k-r=2rs.

(42)

So, it is necessary that
2k\geq r
for us to assume a solution in
the form of equation (41). But this is in no way a sufficient
condition for the existence of the PWS in the form of equation (41).

Now, we shall search for the WEF solutions of equation (17). We
introduce a restriction on the coefficients in the form
2B^{2}=9AC
, so that equation (17) reduces to

\gamma w^{\prime\prime}+c_{1}w+c_{2}w^{3}=0,

(43)

where
\displaystyle c_{1}=-\frac{1}{2}\,A,\,\,c_{2}=C
.

For a solution of equation (43) in the form of equation (41), we
should have
r=2
and
k=1
so that
s=0.
So, our solution
will be

\displaystyle u(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{\tau}{\wp(\xi)}+\zeta.

(44)

Substituting equation (44) into equation (43) and equating the
coefficients of like powers of
\wp(\xi)
to zero, we obtain

\displaystyle\wp^{3}(\xi):\,\,\,\,\displaystyle 2\gamma\tau-\frac{1}{2}A\zeta+C\zeta^{3}=0,

(45)

\displaystyle\wp^{2}(\xi):\,\,\,\,\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}A\tau+3C\tau\zeta^{2}=0,

(46)

\displaystyle\wp(\xi):\,\,\,\,\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}\gamma\tau g_{2}+3C\tau^{2}\zeta=0,

(47)

\displaystyle\wp^{0}(\xi):\,\,\,\,\displaystyle-2\gamma\tau g_{3}+C\tau^{3}=0.

(48)

From equations (46) through (48), it can be found that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tau=\pm\sqrt{\frac{2\gamma g_{3}}{C}},

(49)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\zeta=\pm\sqrt{\frac{A}{6C}},

(50)

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{2}=2\sqrt{\frac{Ag_{3}}{3\gamma}}.

(51)

From equations (49) through (51), one can conclude that if
g_{3}>0
,
A,\,C
and
\gamma
should all be of the same signs, whereas
for
g_{3}<0
,
A
and
C
should be of the same signs and
\gamma

should be of opposite sign.

Equation (45) leads us to the value of
g_{3}
given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle g_{3}=\frac{A^{3}}{432\gamma^{3}}.

(52)

The condition
\displaystyle g_{2}^{3}-27g_{3}^{2}>0
gives the
relation

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{8}{9}>\frac{3}{4},

(53)

which is remarkably true for any value of the coefficients in the
governing equation.

The equations (34) through (36) will give rise to the same PWS of
equation (43) which can be obtained using equation (44) with the
help of equation (38). Thus the PWS of equation (43) in terms of
JEFs can be written as

\displaystyle u(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{\tau\,{\rm{sn}}^{2}(\xi)}{1-\frac{1}{3}(1+m^{2}){\rm{sn}}^{2}(\xi)}+\zeta.

(54)

As
m\rightarrow 1
, the SWSs of the coupled wave equation given by
equations (6) and (7) with the restriction
2B^{2}=9AC
are

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{B}{3C}+\frac{\tau\,{\rm{tanh}}^{2}(x-ct)}{1-\frac{2}{3}{\rm{tanh}}^{2}(x-ct)}+\zeta,

(55)

\displaystyle v(x,t)=\displaystyle\frac{2c}{\epsilon}-\frac{2\sigma}{\epsilon}\left[\frac{B}{3C}+\frac{\tau\,{\rm{tanh}}^{2}(x-ct)}{1-\frac{2}{3}{\rm{tanh}}^{2}(x-ct)}+\zeta\right],

(56)

where
\tau
and
\zeta
are given by eqs. (49) and (50).

5
Conclusions

The
(G^{\prime}/G)
-expansion method has been applied to a nonlinear
coupled wave equation. The kink wave solution and the singular wave
solution have been graphically illustrated. It was found that there
are some restrictions on the coefficients in the governing equation
for the solutions in terms of hyperbolic functions to exist. The WEF
method has also been applied to the system of equations to derive
SWSs. The condition
\displaystyle g_{2}^{3}-27g_{3}^{2}>0
was found
to be identically satisfied, which is a remarkable result and has
never been reported in the literature. We intend to apply these
methods for higher order and higher dimensional PDEs of physical
interest.

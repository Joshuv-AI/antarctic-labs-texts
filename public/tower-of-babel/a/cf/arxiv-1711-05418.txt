Fourier-like multipliers and applications for integral operators
Saifallah Ghobber

Abstract
. Timelimited functions and bandlimited functions play a fundamental role in signal and image processing. But by the uncertainty principles, a signal cannot be simultaneously time and bandlimited. A natural assumption is thus that a signal is almost time and almost bandlimited. The aim of this paper is to prove that the set of almost time and almost bandlimited signals is not excluded from the uncertainty principles. The transforms under consideration are integral operators with bounded kernels for which there is a Parseval Theorem. Then we define the wavelet multipliers for this class of operators, and study their boundedness and Schatten class properties. We show that the wavelet multiplier is unitary equivalent to a scalar multiple of the phase space restriction operator. Moreover we prove that a signal which is almost time and almost bandlimited can be approximated by its projection on the span of the first eigenfunctions of the phase space restriction operator, corresponding to the largest eigenvalues which are close to one.

Introduction

Timelimited functions and bandlimited functions are basic tools of signal and image processing. Unfortunately, the simplest form of the
uncertainty principle tells us that a signal cannot be simultaneously time and bandlimited. This leads to the investigation of the set of almost time and almost bandlimited functions, which has been initially carried through Landau, Pollak
[
17
,
18
]
and then by Donoho, Stark
[
9
]
.
In the current paper, we review the uncertainty principles on this set and present and compare different measure of localization. We made use of compositions of time and bandlimiting operators and considered the eigenvalue problem associated with these operators. The resulting operators yield an orthonormal set of eigenfunctions (well-known as prolate spheroidal functions) which satisfy some optimality in concentration in a region in the time-frequency domain.
We prove a characterization of functions that are approximately time and bandlimited in the region of interest, and we obtain approximation inequalities for such functions using a finite linear combination of eigenfunctions.

The aim of this paper is to continue the study of the uncertainty principle to a very general class of integral operators, which has been started in

A version of Hardy’s and Donoho-Stark’s uncertainty principles for integral operators has been proved in

We assume that the measure

Then

One can then define the integral operator

(1)

(2)

Then

to the space of bounded continuous functions

(1.3)

{\left\|{{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{\infty}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}.

Further, if we introduce the dilation operator

{\mathcal{D}}_{\lambda}f(x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{a}}f\left(\frac{x}{\lambda}\right).

Then the homogeneity of

{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\lambda}={\mathcal{D}}_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}{\mathcal{T}}.

The integral operators under consideration will be assumed to satisfy some of

(1)

{\mathcal{T}}

When both

(1.5)

(2)

(1.6)

{\left\langle{{\mathcal{T}}f,{\mathcal{T}}g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}={\left\langle{f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu},

{\left\langle{f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}f(x)\overline{g(x)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x).

In particular,

onto

{\left\|{{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}={\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu},

(1.8)

{\mathcal{T}}^{-1}f(\xi)=\overline{{\mathcal{T}}\bar{f}(\xi)},\quad\xi\in\Omega.

This family of transforms includes for instance the Fourier transform (see

, where the authors instead of counting the number of eigenfunctions with eigenvalue
close to one, they count the maximum number of orthogonal functions that are
\varepsilon
-localized with respect to
L_{S,\Sigma}
. In
[
2
]
, Abreu and Pereira noted that the sharp asymptotic number of these orthogonal functions is
\approx(1-\varepsilon)^{-1}\frac{|S||\Sigma|}{2\pi}
.
On the other hand, we establish the following results, which characterize functions that are in
L^{2}(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)
, and approximate almost time and bandlimited functions.

Theorem D.

Let
f_{\mathrm{ker}}
denote the orthogonal projection of
f
onto the kernel of
L_{S,\Sigma}
.

(1)

A function
f
is in
L^{2}(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)
if and only if,

\ \sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)}(\lambda_{n}+\varepsilon-1){\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|f_{\mathrm{ker}}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\sum_{n=1+n(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)}^{\infty}(1-\varepsilon-\lambda_{n}){\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}.

(2)

If
f
is in
L^{2}(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)
, then

(1.27)

{\left\|{f-\sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon_{0},S,\Sigma)}{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\varphi_{n}}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\;\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

Using the above comparison, it follows that,
if
f\in L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
, then

(1.28)

{\left\|{f-\sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon_{0},S,\Sigma)}{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\varphi_{n}}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\;\|f\|_{2,\mu},

and if
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is
\varepsilon
-localized with respect to
L_{S,\Sigma}
, then

(1.29)

{\left\|{f-\sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon_{0},S,\Sigma)}{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\varphi_{n}}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\;\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Throughout this paper we denote by
{\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle}

the usual Euclidean inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we write for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,

{\left|{x}\right|}=\sqrt{{\left\langle{x,x}\right\rangle}}
and if
A
is a measurable subset in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, we will write
A^{c}
for its complement in
\Omega
.

For
\xi\in\Omega
, we denote by
{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}:\Omega\to\Omega
the kernel defined by
{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}(x)={\mathcal{K}}(x,\xi)
, and for
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}
the closed ball in
\Omega
centred at
0
and of radius
r
.

We will write along this paper
C
for a constant that depends on the parameters
a
,
s
and
c_{\mathcal{K}}

defined above (and may be depends also on some other parameter
\beta
,
\varepsilon
,
\ldots
).
This constant may changes from line to line.

2.2.
Generalities

Let X be a separable and complex Hilbert space (of infinite dimension) in which the inner product
and the norm are denoted by
{\left\langle{\cdot,\cdot}\right\rangle}
and
\|\cdot\|
respectively. Let
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
be a compact operator for which we denote by
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}:X\to X
its adjoint. Then the linear operator
|{\mathcal{A}}|=\sqrt{{\mathcal{A}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}}:X\to X
is positive and compact.
The singular values of
{\mathcal{A}}
are the eigenvalues of the self-adjoint operator
|{\mathcal{A}}|
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, the Schatten class
S_{p}
is the space of all compact operators whose singular values lie in
\ell_{p}
. In particular,
S_{2}
is the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, and
S_{1}
is the space of trace class operators.
Moreover from
[
22
, Section VI.6]
and
[
26
, Proposition 2.6]
, we have the following criterion for a bounded linear operator to be in the trace class.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
be a bounded linear operator such that, for all orthonormal bases
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
X
,

(2.1)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left|{{\left\langle{{\mathcal{A}}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}}\right|}<\infty,

then
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
is in the trace class
S_{1}
with,

(2.2)

{\mathrm{tr}}({\mathcal{A}})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{{\mathcal{A}}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle},

where
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is any orthonormal basis for
X
.

If, in addition
{\mathcal{A}}
is positive,
then (see
[
26
, Proposition 2.7]
),

(2.3)

{\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}}\right\|}_{S_{1}}={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathcal{A}}).

Moreover from
[
26
, Proposition 2.8]
, we have the
following criterion for a bounded linear operator
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
to be in the Hilbert-Schmidt class
S_{2}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
be a bounded linear operator such that, for all orthonormal bases
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
X
,

(2.4)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathcal{A}}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}<\infty,

then
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
is in the Hilbert-Schmidt class
S_{2}
with,

(2.5)

\|{\mathcal{A}}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|{\mathcal{A}}\varphi_{n}\|^{2},

where
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is any orthonormal basis for
X
.

Finally, if the compact operator
{\mathcal{A}}:X\to X
is Hilbert-Schmidt, then the positive operator
{\mathcal{A}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}
is in the space of trace class
S_{1}
and

(2.6)

{\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}}\right\|}_{HS}^{2}:={\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}}\right\|}_{S_{2}}^{2}={\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}}}\right\|}_{S_{1}}={\mathrm{tr}}({\mathcal{A}}^{*}{\mathcal{A}})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}\varphi_{n}}\right\|}^{2},

for any orthonormal basis
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
X
.

For consistency, we define
S_{\infty}:=B\left(X\right)
to be the space of bounded operators from
X
into
X
, equipped with norm,

(2.7)

{\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}}\right\|}_{S_{\infty}}=\sup_{f\ :\ {\left\|{f}\right\|}\leq 1}{\left\|{{\mathcal{A}}f}\right\|}.

It is obvious that
S_{p}\subseteq S_{q}
,
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

2.3.
Fourier-like Multipliers

For
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
, we define the linear operator
F_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
by

(2.8)

F_{\sigma}f={\mathcal{T}}^{-1}\left[\sigma\,{\mathcal{T}}f\right].

In the case of the Fourier transform, this operator is known as the Fourier multiplier. Clearly, if
\sigma=1
, then
F_{\sigma}=I
, where
I
is the identity operator. Moreover, from the formula (
1.7
), it is clair that
F_{\sigma}
is bounded with

(2.9)

\|F_{\sigma}\|_{S_{\infty}}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\infty}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cup L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
and let
\phi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}=1
. We define the linear operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
by

(2.10)

{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}={\left\langle{\sigma{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f),{\mathcal{T}}(\psi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}.

In the case of the Fourier transform, this operator is known as the
wavelet
Fourier multiplier, which can be viewed as a variant of a localization operator with respect to the symbol
\sigma
and the admissible wavelets
\phi
and
\psi
, see the book
[
26
]
and the reference therein.
Notice that, if
\sigma=\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function on the subset
A\subset\Omega
, then we write
F_{\sigma}
as
F_{A}
. In this case, we also write
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
as
P_{A,\phi,\psi}
if
\phi\neq\psi
and
P_{A,\phi}
, if
\phi=\psi
. The linear operator
F_{A}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is a self-adjoint projection, it is known as the
frequency limiting operator
on
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and has many applications in time-frequency analysis. Moreover we will prove in the last section that
P_{A,\phi}
can be viewed as the
phase space
(or
time frequency
)
limiting operator
.

The next proposition shows that
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and
\bar{\psi}F_{\sigma}\phi:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
are unitary equivalent.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cup L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
and let
\phi,\psi\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}=1
. Then

(2.11)

{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}={\left\langle{\bar{\psi}F_{\sigma}\phi,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}.

Proof.

From (
2.8
) and Parseval’s formula (
1.6
), we have

\displaystyle{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\langle{\sigma{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f),{\mathcal{T}}(\psi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\langle{{\mathcal{T}}F_{\sigma}(\phi f),{\mathcal{T}}(\psi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\langle{F_{\sigma}(\phi f),\psi g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\langle{(\bar{\psi}F_{\sigma}\phi)f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.
Uncertainty principles by means of the frequency limiting operator

We will need to introduce the following time limiting operator, defined by

E_{S}f=\chi_{S}f,\quad\quad f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cup L^{2}(\Omega,\mu),

where
S\subset\Omega
. Clearly
E_{S}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is a self-adjoint projection.

3.1.
Uncertainty principles on the space
L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)

The first known result for functions in
L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
is the following Donoho-Stark type uncertainty inequality, see
[
11
, Inequality (3.4)]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}<1
. Then if
f\in L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
we have

(3.1)

\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-2}\left(1-\sqrt{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}^{2}}\right)^{2}.

In the case of the Fourier transform, it goes back to Donoho and Stark
[
9
]
. This inequality implies that the essential support of
f
and
{\mathcal{T}}f
cannot be too small. Moreover, we recall the following local uncertainty principle, see
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

(1)

If
\,0<s<a
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and all measurable subset
\Sigma\subset\Omega
of finite measure
0<\mu(\Sigma)<\infty
,

(3.2)

{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(\Sigma)^{\frac{s}{a}}\,\|{\left|{x}\right|}^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

(2)

If
\,s>a
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and all measurable subset
\Sigma\subset\Omega
of finite measure
0<\mu(\Sigma)<\infty
,

(3.3)

{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(\Sigma)\,\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2-\frac{2a}{s}}\|{\left|{x}\right|}^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{2a}{s}}.

Next, take
s=a
. Then, if we apply the first inequality (
3.2
) with
a(1-\varepsilon)
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, replacing
s
and then apply the following classical inequality

(3.4)

\||x|^{a-a\varepsilon}f\|_{2,\mu}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{\varepsilon}{\left\|{|x|^{a}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{1-\varepsilon},

we obtain for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

(3.5)

{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(\Sigma)^{1-\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2\varepsilon}\,\|{\left|{x}\right|}^{a}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2-2\varepsilon}.

Consequently we conclude the following first corollary comparing the support of
{\mathcal{T}}f
and the generalized time dispersion
\||x|^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}
for function in the range of
F_{\Sigma}
:

\operatorname{Im}(F_{\Sigma})=\{f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\ :\ {\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\subset\Sigma\}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
s>0
. Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in\operatorname{Im}(F_{\Sigma})
,

(3.6)

\mu\left({\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\right)\|{\left|{x}\right|}^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{2a}{s}}\geq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{\frac{2a}{s}}.

Proof.

Let
s>0
and
f\in\operatorname{Im}(F_{\Sigma})
. Then
f=F_{\Sigma}f
, and we apply (
3.2
), (
3.3
), (
3.5
) to obtain the desired result.
∎

Notice that, if
\mu\left({\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\right)
is finite, then
\mu\left({\operatorname{supp}}f\right)
is infinite, because
f
and
{\mathcal{T}}f
cannot be simultaneously supported on subsets of finite measure, see
[
11
, Corollary 3.7]
. This result is known as the Benedicks-Amrein-Berthier uncertainty principle.

Moreover, we can also obtain an inequality comparing the essential support of
{\mathcal{T}}f
and the generalized time dispersion
\||x|^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}
for functions that are
\varepsilon_{2}
-bandlimited on
\Sigma
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
s>0
.

(1)

If
\,0<s<a
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for all function
f
which is
\varepsilon_{2}
-bandlimited on
\Sigma
,

(3.7)

\mu(\Sigma)^{\frac{s}{a}}\,{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq C\left(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right)\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

(2)

If
s>a
, then there exists a constant
C
such that for all function
f
which is
\varepsilon_{2}
-bandlimited on
\Sigma
,

(3.8)

\mu(\Sigma)^{\frac{s}{a}}\,{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq C\left(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{s}{a}}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

(3)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C
such that for all function
f
which is
\varepsilon_{2}
-bandlimited on
\Sigma
,

(3.9)

\mu(\Sigma)\,{\left\|{|x|^{a}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq C(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2})^{\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is
\varepsilon_{2}
-bandlimited on
\Sigma
, then

\|F_{\Sigma}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}-\|F_{\Sigma^{c}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2})\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

For the first result, we use the local inequalities (
3.2
).
Analogously, for the second inequality, we use (
3.3
), and
finally, for the third inequality, we use (
3.5
).
∎

Now, since,
{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}={\left\|{E_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}
, then by interchanging the roles of
f
and
{\mathcal{T}}f

in Theorem
3.2
, Corollary
3.3
and Corollary
5.8
, we obtain the following results involving the time limiting operator instead of the frequency limiting operator, and the frequency dispersion instead of the time dispersion.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\beta>0
.

(1)

If
\,0<\beta<a
, then

(a)

there exists a constant
C
such that for all

and all measurable subset

(3.10)

{\left\|{E_{S}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\mu(S)^{\frac{\beta}{a}}\|{\left|{x}\right|}^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2},

(b)

(2)

If

, then

(a)

(3.12)

{\left\|{E_{S}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(S)\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2-\frac{2a}{\beta}}\|{\left|{\xi}\right|}^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{2a}{\beta}},

there exists a constant
C

\varepsilon_{1}
-concentrated on
S
,

(3.13)

\mu(S)^{\frac{\beta}{a}}\,\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq C\left(1-\varepsilon_{1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{a}}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

(3)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

(a)

there exists a constant
C

and all measurable subset

(3.14)

{\left\|{E_{S}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(S)^{1-\varepsilon}\,\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2\varepsilon}\,\|{\left|{\xi}\right|}^{a}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2-2\varepsilon},

(4)

There exists a constant
C

(3.16)

, with constants that depend on

and

.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
s,\beta>0
. Then for all
f\in L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
:

(1)

if

,

(3.17)

{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{\beta}\,\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{s}\geq C\;\frac{(1-\varepsilon_{1}^{2})^{s/2}(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2})^{\beta/2}}{\left(\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\right)^{\frac{s\beta}{2a}}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{s+\beta},

(2)

if

,

(3.18)

{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{\beta}\,\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{s}\geq C\left(\frac{(1-\varepsilon_{1}^{2})(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2})}{\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)}\right)^{\frac{s\beta}{2a}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{s+\beta},

(3)

for all

,

(3.19)

{\left\|{|x|^{a}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\,{\left\|{|\xi|^{a}{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\geq C\;\frac{\big((1-\varepsilon_{1}^{2})(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2})\big)^{\frac{1}{2-2\varepsilon}}}{\sqrt{\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}.

Remark 3.7
.

(1)

Notice that Corollary

) and (

(2)

On the other hand, from Corollary

3.2.
Uncertainty principles on the space
L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)

The first known result for functions in
L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
is the following Donoho-Stark type uncertainty inequality, see
[
12
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
. Then if
f\in L^{1}\cap L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
we have

(3.22)

\mu(S)\geq\frac{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{2}}{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}}(1-\varepsilon_{1})^{2},\quad\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-2}\frac{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}}{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{2}}(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}),

and then

(3.23)

\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-2}(1-\varepsilon_{1})^{2}(1-\varepsilon_{2}^{2}).

Theorem
3.8
is stronger then Theorem
3.1
, in the sense that the previous theorem give a lower bound of
\mu(S)
and
\mu(\Sigma)
separately, which is not possible in Theorem
3.1
.

Now we will recall the following Carlson-type and Nash-type inequalities, see
[
12
, Proposition 2.2, Proposition 2.3]
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
s,\beta>0
. Then we have:

(1)

A Carlson-type inequality:

there exists a constant
C=C(s,a)
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
,

(3.24)

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{1+\frac{s}{a}}\leq C\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{s}{a}}\;{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}.

(2)

A Nash-type inequality:
there exists a constant
C=C(\beta,a)
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
,

(3.25)

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{1+\frac{\beta}{a}}\leq C\|f\|_{1,\mu}^{\frac{\beta}{a}}\;\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}.

Consequently we obtain a lower bounds for the time and frequency dispersions:

(3.26)

{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}\geq C\left(\frac{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}}{\|f\|_{2,\mu}}\right)^{\frac{s}{a}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}\geq C\left(\frac{{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}}{\|f\|_{1,\mu}}\right)^{\frac{\beta}{a}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
s,\beta>0
. Then

(1)

there exists a constant

such that for all

(3.27)

\||x|^{s}f\|_{1,\mu}^{\beta}\;\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{s}\geq C\,{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{\beta}\,{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{s},

(2)

there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and all measurable subset of
\Sigma
of finite measure,

(3.28)

{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(\Sigma){\left\|{f}\right\|}^{\frac{2s}{a+s}}_{2,\mu}\,{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}^{\frac{2a}{a+s}}_{1,\mu},

(3)

there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and all measurable subset
S
of finite measure,

(3.29)

{\left\|{E_{S}f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{2}\leq C\,\mu(S){\left\|{f}\right\|}^{\frac{2\beta}{a+\beta}}_{1,\mu}\,\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|^{\frac{2a}{a+\beta}}_{2,\mu},

(4)

there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
with
{\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\subset\Sigma
,

(3.30)

\mu\big({\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\big)\,{\left\|{|x|^{s}f}\right\|}^{\frac{2a}{a+s}}_{1,\mu}\geq C\,{\left\|{f}\right\|}^{\frac{2a}{a+s}}_{2,\mu},

(5)

there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
with
{\operatorname{supp}}f\subset S
,

(3.31)

\mu\big({\operatorname{supp}}f\big)\,\||\xi|^{\beta}{\mathcal{T}}f\|^{\frac{2a}{a+\beta}}_{2,\mu}\geq C\,{\left\|{f}\right\|}^{\frac{2a}{a+\beta}}_{1,\mu}.

Proof.

The first inequality follows by combining the Carlson inequality (
3.24
) and the Nash inequality (
3.25
). Next by (
1.7
) and (
1.3
),

{\left\|{F_{\Sigma}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}={\left\|{\chi_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq\mu(\Sigma){\left\|{{\mathcal{T}}f}\right\|}_{\infty}^{2}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\mu(\Sigma){\left\|{f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{2},

and by the Carlson inequality (
3.24
) we obtain (
3.28
).
Now by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we have,

{\left\|{E_{S}f}\right\|}_{1,\mu}^{2}\leq\mu(S){\left\|{f}\right\|}_{2,\mu}^{2},

and by the Nash type inequality (
3.25
) we deduce (
3.29
). Finally (

) by taking

and if we take

in (

3.31
).
∎

4.
The wavelet multiplier

Our motivation here came from the classical setting stated in
[
27
,
28
]
.
In this section let
\phi
and
\psi
will be two functions in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}=1
.

4.1.
Boundedness

The aim of this section is to prove that we can also define
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
for symbol
\sigma\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
.
First, if
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is in
S_{\infty}
and

(4.1)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{\infty}}\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|\sigma\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

|{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}|\leq\|\sigma\|_{\infty}\|{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f)\|_{2,\mu}\|{\mathcal{T}}(\psi g)\|_{2,\mu}.

Then by Plancherel’s formula (
1.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle|{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sigma\|_{\infty}\|\phi f\|_{2,\mu}\|\psi g\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sigma\|_{\infty}\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|f\|_{2,\mu}\|g\|_{2,\mu}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now, if we consider
\sigma\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
, then we obtain the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is in
S_{\infty}
and

(4.2)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{\infty}}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}.

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f)(\xi)={\left\langle{f,\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}
, then by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

\|{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f)\|_{\infty}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}\|f\|_{2,\mu}\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}.

Therefore, since
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}\|{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f)\|_{\infty}\|{\mathcal{T}}(\psi g)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}\|f\|_{2,\mu}\|g\|_{2,\mu}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Thus, by (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and the Riesz-Thorin interpolation argument
[
23
, Theorem 2]
(see also
[
26
, Theorem 12.4]
) we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
. Then the linear operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is bounded and

(4.3)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{\infty}}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{\frac{2}{p}}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\sigma\|_{p,\mu}.

Hence we can define the operator
(\bar{\psi}F_{\sigma}\phi):L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, where
\sigma\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
by

(4.4)

{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}={\left\langle{(\bar{\psi}F_{\sigma}\phi)f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}.

4.2.
Schatten class properties

Let us begin with the following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\sigma
be symbol in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is Hilbert Schmidt and

(4.5)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{P_{\bar{\sigma},\psi,\phi}\,\bar{\psi},\bar{\phi}\,|{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}|^{2}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{1}_{\alpha}}^{2}.

Proof.

First by (
2.10
) it follows immediately that the adjoint of
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is
P_{\bar{\sigma},\psi,\phi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Now, Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then by (
2.10
) and by Fubini’s
theorem we obtain,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{\sigma{\mathcal{T}}(\phi\,\varphi_{n}),{\mathcal{T}}(\psi\,P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n})}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{\varphi_{n},\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\overline{{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},\overline{\psi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{P_{\bar{\sigma},\psi,\phi}\overline{\psi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}{\left\langle{\varphi_{n},\overline{\phi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{P_{\bar{\sigma},\psi,\phi}\overline{\psi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\overline{\phi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi),

where we have used Parseval’s identity in the last line. Therefore from Proposition
4.2
and since
|{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}|\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|P_{\bar{\sigma},\psi,\phi}\|_{S_{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{K}}^{4}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}^{2}.

Thus from Proposition
2.2
, the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is in
S_{2}
and
\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{2}}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}.

∎

Consequently the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is also compact for symbols in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\sigma
be symbol in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Then the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is compact.

Proof.

Let
\{\sigma_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions in
L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
such that
\sigma_{n}\to\sigma
in
L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
as
n\to\infty
. Then by Theorem
4.3
,

(4.6)

\|P_{\sigma_{n},\phi,\psi}-P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{\infty}}\leq\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\sigma_{n}-\sigma\|_{p,\mu}.

Therefore
P_{\sigma_{n},\phi,\psi}\to P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
in
S_{\infty}
as
n\to\infty
. Now, since by Theorem
4.4
, the operators
P_{\sigma_{n},\phi,\psi}
are in
S_{2}
and hence compact, and since the set of compact operators is a closed subspace of
S_{\infty}
,
then the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is also compact.
∎

More precisely we will prove that the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is in fact in the Schatten class
S_{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
. Of particular interest is the Schatten-von Neumann class
S_{1}
(see
[
27
,
28
]
).

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is trace class with

(4.7)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{1}}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu},

and we have the following trace formula

(4.8)

{\mathrm{tr}}\left(P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\right)=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{\overline{\psi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi).

Proof.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\mathcal{T}}(\phi\,\varphi_{n})(\xi)\overline{{\mathcal{T}}(\psi\,\varphi_{n})(\xi)}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{\overline{\psi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}{\left\langle{\varphi_{n},\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi).

Thus by Fubini’s theorem,

(4.9)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{\overline{\psi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}{\left\langle{\varphi_{n},\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi).

Therefore by Parseval’s identity, and the fact that
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=\|\psi\|_{2,\mu}=1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\sigma(\xi)|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2}+\left|{\left\langle{\overline{\psi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\sigma(\xi)|\left(\|\phi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\|\psi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu}.

By Proposition
2.1
, the operator
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}
is in
S_{1}
and with (
4.9
) and Parseval’s identity,

{\mathrm{tr}}(P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left\langle{P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\varphi_{n},\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\sigma(\xi){\left\langle{\overline{\psi{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}},\overline{\phi\,{\mathcal{K}}_{\xi}}\,}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi).

This allows to conclude.
∎

Moreover by (
4.1
), (
4.7
) and by interpolation argument we deduce the following result.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\sigma\in L^{p}(\Omega,\mu)
,
1<p<\infty
. Then
P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}:L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\to L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is in
S_{p}
and

(4.10)

\|P_{\sigma,\phi,\psi}\|_{S_{p}}\leq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2/p}\|\phi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\psi\|_{\infty}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\|\sigma\|_{p,\mu}.

4.3.
An uncertainty relation

In this subsection, we will assume that
\phi
and
\psi
satisfy
\|\phi\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{\infty}=1
.
Now let
\sigma_{1}=\chi_{S}
and
\sigma_{2}=\chi_{\Sigma}
and
let
L_{1}=P_{\sigma_{1},\phi,\psi}
and
L_{2}=P_{\sigma_{2},\phi,\psi}
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}<1
. If
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is
\varepsilon_{1}
-localized with respect to
P_{\sigma_{1},\phi,\psi}
and
\varepsilon_{2}
-localized with respect to
P_{\sigma_{2},\phi,\psi}
then,

(4.11)

\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-4}\left(1-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}\right).

Proof.

From Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle\|f-L_{2}L_{1}f\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f-L_{2}f\|_{2,\mu}+\|L_{2}f-L_{2}L_{1}f\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|L_{2}f-f\|_{2,\mu}+\|L_{2}\|_{S_{\infty}}\|L_{1}f-f\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{1})\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|L_{2}L_{1}f\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2,\mu}-\|f-L_{2}L_{1}f\|_{2,\mu}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2})\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

Thus from Proposition
4.2
it follows that

\displaystyle 1-\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|L_{2}L_{1}\|_{S_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|L_{1}\|_{S_{\infty}}\|L_{2}\|_{S_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{K}}^{4}\,\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma).

This proves the desired result.
∎

Notice that, when
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}=0
in the classical Donoho-Stark uncertainty inequality (
3.1
), we have
\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-2}
. This is a trivial assertion since in this case
S={\operatorname{supp}}f
,
\Sigma={\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f
and then from
[
11
]
, either
\mu({\operatorname{supp}}f)=\infty
or
\mu({\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f)=\infty
.
The case
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}=0
in Theorem
4.8
is not trivial and gives the following result.

Corollary 4.9
.

If
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
is an eigenfunction of
P_{S,\phi,\psi}
and
P_{\Sigma,\phi,\psi}
corresponding to the same eigenvalue
1
, then

(4.12)

\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)\geq c_{\mathcal{K}}^{-4}.

5.
Uncertainty principles for orthonormal sequences

5.1.
The phase space restriction operator

We define the phase space restriction operator by

L_{S,\Sigma}=E_{S}F_{\Sigma}E_{S}=(F_{\Sigma}E_{S})^{*}F_{\Sigma}E_{S}.

We know that the operator
F_{\Sigma}E_{S}
is Hilbert-Schmidt (see
[
11
, Inequality (3.2)]
), and since the pair
(S,\Sigma)
is strongly annihilating, then from
[
11
]
, we have

(5.1)

\|L_{S,\Sigma}\|_{S_{\infty}}=\|E_{S}F_{\Sigma}\|^{2}_{S_{\infty}}=\|F_{\Sigma}E_{S}\|^{2}_{S_{\infty}}<1.

Moreover, the operator
L_{S,\Sigma}
is self-adjoint, positive and from (
2.6
) it is compact and even trace class with

(5.2)

\|L_{S,\Sigma}\|_{S_{1}}=\|F_{\Sigma}E_{S}\|_{S_{2}}^{2},

In the fundamental paper
[
18
]
, Landau and Pollak have considered the eigenvalue problem associated with the
positive self-adjoint operator
E_{S}F_{\Sigma}E_{S}:L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, where
S,\Sigma
are real intervals, for which they proved an asymptotic estimate for the number of eigenvalues.

Motivated by the process in
[
16
]
, we will show that the phase space restriction operator
L_{S,\Sigma}

can be viewed as a wavelet multiplier, and then we will deduce a trace formula.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\phi=\psi
be the function on
\Omega
defined by

\phi=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu(S)}}\chi_{S}

and let
\sigma=\chi_{\Sigma}
. Then

(5.3)

L_{S,\Sigma}=\mu(S)\,P_{\Sigma,\phi}.

Proof.

Clearly, the function
\phi
belongs to
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
, with
\|\phi\|_{2,\mu}=1
.
Then, since
E_{S}
is self-adjoint and by Parseval’s equality (
1.6
), we have for all
f,g\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
,

\displaystyle{\left\langle{L_{S,\Sigma}f,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\left\langle{F_{\Sigma}E_{S}f,\chi_{S}\,g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu(S)}{\left\langle{F_{\Sigma}E_{S}f,\phi g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu(S)}{\left\langle{{\mathcal{T}}F_{\Sigma}E_{S}f,{\mathcal{T}}(\phi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mu(S)}{\left\langle{\chi_{\Sigma}{\mathcal{T}}\chi_{S}f,{\mathcal{T}}(\phi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(S){\left\langle{\sigma{\mathcal{T}}(\phi f),{\mathcal{T}}(\phi g)}\right\rangle}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu(S){\left\langle{P_{\Sigma,\phi},g}\right\rangle}_{\mu}.

This completes the proof.

Corollary 5.2
.

The phase space operator

Theorem 5.4

\ \sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)}(\lambda_{n}+\varepsilon-1){\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|f_{\mathrm{ker}}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\sum_{n=1+n(\varepsilon,S,\Sigma)}^{\infty}(1-\varepsilon-\lambda_{n}){\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}.

Proof.

For a given function

(5.21)

(5.22)

{\left\langle{L_{S,\Sigma}f,f}\right\rangle}_{\mu}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}{\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}.

So the function
f

if and only if

(5.23)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}{\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu_{k}}}\right|}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\left(\|f_{\mathrm{ker}}\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\left|{{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}}\right|}^{2}\right),

and the conclusion follows.

Let

{\mathcal{P}}
the orthogonal projection onto the subspace

Theorem 5.5
.

Let

be a function in

. Then

(5.24)

{\left\|{f-\sum_{n=1}^{n(\varepsilon_{0},S,\Sigma)}{\left\langle{f,\varphi_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\varphi_{n}}\right\|}_{2,\mu}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{0}}}\;\|f\|_{2,\mu}.

Proof.

An easy adaptation of the proof of Proposition 3.3 in
[
25
]
, we can conclue that

(5.25)

\|{\mathcal{P}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq(1-\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0})\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

It follows then,

\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\|{\mathcal{P}}f+(f-{\mathcal{P}}f)\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\|{\mathcal{P}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\|f-{\mathcal{P}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

Thus

\|f-{\mathcal{P}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}-\|{\mathcal{P}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}-(1-\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0})\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{0}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

5.2.
Shapiro–type uncertainty principles

Based on Malinnikova’s ideas
[
20
]
, we will prove in this section a quantitative dispersion inequality for orthonormal sequences and a strong
uncertainty principle for orthonormal bases.
Notice also that the homogeneity of the kernel
{\mathcal{K}}
plays a key role in this section, especially in Lemma

.

5.2.1.
Localization theorem

Our starting point is the following theorem which states that any orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
cannot be infinite.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
2\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}<1
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
be an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},S,\Sigma)
.
Then

(5.28)

N<c_{\mathcal{K}}^{2}\frac{\mu(S)\mu(\Sigma)}{1-2\varepsilon_{1}-\varepsilon_{2}}.

Proof.

The result follows immediately from Inequalities (
5.12
) and (
5.4
).
∎

Consequently, if the generalized dispersions of each element of an orthonormal sequence are uniformly bounded, then this sequence is also finite.

Corollary 5.8
.

Fix
A_{1},A_{2}>0
. Let
s>0
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{N}
be an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{1/s}_{2,\mu}\leq A_{1}
and
\big\||\xi|^{s}\,{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{1/s}_{2,\mu}\leq A_{2}
. Then each
f_{n}
is in
L^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4},B_{4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{1}},B_{4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{2}}\right)
, and

(5.29)

N\leq C\,(A_{1}A_{2})^{2a}.

Proof.

By assumption we have, for all
n\geq 1
,

\int_{|x|>4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{1}}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)=\int_{|x|>4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{1}}|x|^{-2s}|x|^{2s}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\leq\frac{1}{16A_{1}^{2s}}\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\leq\frac{1}{16}.

In the same way we obtain

\int_{|\xi|>4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{2}}|{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(\xi)\leq\frac{1}{16}.

Thus
f_{n}\in L^{2}\left(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4},B_{4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{1}},B_{4^{\frac{1}{s}}A_{2}}\right)
, and from (
5.28
) we conclude the desired result.
∎

5.2.2.
Quantitative dispersion inequality for orthonormal sequences

From Inequality (
1.9
), there exists a constant
C
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
,

(5.30)

\||x|^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}\;\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}\geq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}^{2}_{2,\mu},

and the dilation argument (
1.4
) shows that (
5.30
) is equivalent to

(5.31)

\||x|^{s}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\geq 2C{\left\|{f}\right\|}^{2}_{2,\mu}.

Consequently we obtain immediately the following result.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
s>0
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then there exists
j_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

(5.32)

\forall n\geq 1,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \max\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu},\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}\right)\geq 2^{s(j_{0}-1)}.

This corollary with Corollary
5.8
allows as to prove the following quantitative dispersion inequality.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
s>0
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
.
Then for every
N\geq 1
,

(5.33)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}+\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\right)\geq C\,N^{1+\frac{s}{2a}}.

Proof.

For each
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we define

P_{j}=\left\{n\ :\ \max\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{\frac{1}{s}}_{2,\mu},\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{\frac{1}{s}}_{2,\mu}\right)\in\left[2^{j-1},2^{j}\right)\right\}.

First, by Inequality (
5.32
), we see that
P_{j}
is empty for all
j<j_{0}
.
Moreover, since for each
n\in P_{j}
,
j\geq j_{0}
,

(5.34)

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq 2^{j}\ \ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \ \big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{\frac{1}{s}}\leq 2^{j},

then by Corollary
5.8
,
P_{j}
is finite, for all
j\geq j_{0}
, and if we denote by
N_{j}
the number of elements in
P_{j}
then

N_{j}\leq C\,4^{2aj}.

Therefore, for every
m\geq j_{0}
, the number of elements in
\bigcup_{j=j_{0}}^{m}P_{j}
is less than

C\,4^{2am},

where
C
is a constant that does not depend on
m
.

Now, if
N>2C\,4^{2aj_{0}}
, then we can choose an integer
m>j_{0}
such that

2C\,4^{(m-1)2a}<N\leq 2C\,4^{2am}.

Therefore at least half of
\{1,\ldots,N\}
does
not belong to
\bigcup_{j=j_{0}}^{m-1}P_{j}
and we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}+\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\max\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu},\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}4^{s(m-1)}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{N}{4^{s}}\left(\frac{N}{2C}\right)^{\frac{s}{2a}}.

Finally, if
N\leq 2C\,4^{2aj_{0}}
, then from Corollary
5.9
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}+\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\max\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu},\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|^{2}_{2,\mu}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle N4^{s(j_{0}-1)}\geq\frac{N}{4^{s}}\left(\frac{N}{2C}\right)^{\frac{s}{2a}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

The last Dispersion inequality implies in particular that, there does not exist an infinite sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)

such that the two sequences

and

\left\{\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\|_{2,\mu}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
are bounded. More precisely:

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
s>0
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
.
Then for every
N\geq 1
,

(5.35)

\sup_{1\leq n\leq N}\left\{\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2},\;\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\right\}\geq C\,N^{\frac{s}{2a}}.

In particular

(5.36)

\sup_{n}\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2}+\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\right)=\infty.

5.2.3.
Strong uncertainty principle for orthonormal bases

One wonders if (
5.36
) still valid for the product instead of the sum. We will show that this statement is not true in general for orthonormal sequences, but still valid for orthonormal bases.
Indeed, there is an infinite orthonormal sequence
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
,
with bounded product of dispersions. Fix
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
a radial, real-valued Schwartz function supported in
B(0,2)\backslash B(0,1)
, with
\|f\|_{2,\mu}=1
, and consider
f_{n}(x)=2^{na}f(2^{n}x)
. Then

\|f_{n}\|_{2,\mu}=\|f\|_{2,\mu},\ \ \ \ {\operatorname{supp}}f_{n}\subset B(0,2^{-n+1})\backslash B(0,2^{-n})\ \ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \ {\mathcal{T}}f_{n}(\xi)=2^{-na}{\mathcal{T}}(f)\left(2^{-n}\xi\right).

Therefore
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
form an orthonormal sequence in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and for every
s>0
,

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}=2^{-ns}\big\||x|^{s}f\big\|_{2,\mu},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}=2^{ns}\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f\big\|_{2,\mu}.

Hence for all
n
,

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}\,\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}=\big\||x|^{s}f\big\|_{2,\mu}\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f\big\|_{2,\mu}<\infty.

To prove the main result of this subsection, we will need the following special form of the uncertainty principle for sets of finite measure, see
e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
11
,
21

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
S
and
\Sigma
be measurable subsets of finite measure
0<\mu(S),\,\mu(\Sigma)<\infty
. Then there exists a nonzero function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}f\subset S^{c}
and
{\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\subset\Sigma^{c}
.

Proof.

From
[
11
, Corollary 3.7]
, there exist a positive constant
C(S,\Sigma)
such that
for all functions
f\in\operatorname{Im}(F_{\Sigma})
,

\|f\|_{2,\mu}\leq C(S,\Sigma)\|E_{S^{c}}f\|_{2,\mu}.

Therefore the trace space
\Lambda=\{f|_{S^{c}}:f\in\operatorname{Im}(F_{\Sigma})\}
form a closed subspace in
L^{2}(S^{c},\mu)
which is obviously not the whole space.
Let
g
be a nonzero function in
\Lambda^{c}=L^{2}(S^{c},\mu)\backslash\Lambda
. Since
g=F_{\Sigma}g+F_{\Sigma^{c}}g
, then
f=F_{\Sigma^{c}}g

is a nonzero function in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
such that
f
is supported on
S^{c}
and
{\mathcal{T}}f
is supported on
\Sigma^{c}
.
We extend
f
by zero on
S
in order to get the required function.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
s>0
and let
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Then

(5.37)

\sup_{n}\left(\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}\,\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}\right)=\infty.

Proof.

Assume that there exists an orthonormal basis
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{1/s}\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{1/s}\leq A^{2}.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and let

A_{k}=\left\{f_{n}\ :\ \big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{1/s}\in\left(2^{-k}A,2^{-k+1}A\right]\right\}.

Clearly,
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\bigcup_{k}A_{k}
, and for each
f_{n}\in A_{k}
, we have

\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{1/s}\leq 2^{-k+1}A\ \ \ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \ \ \big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{1/s}\leq A2^{k}.

Then by Corollary
5.8
,

A_{k}
is finite, and if
N_{k}
is the number of elements in
A_{k}

then
N_{k}
is bounded by a constant
C
that does not depend on
k
.

Let
R>0
, then by using Lemma
5.12
, we take a nonzero function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
with
\|f\|_{2,\mu}=1
,
such that

{\operatorname{supp}}f,\,{\operatorname{supp}}{\mathcal{T}}f\subset B_{R}^{c}.

Then for
k\geq 0
and
f_{n}\in A_{k}
we obtain by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality that

(5.38)

\left|{\left\langle{f,f_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2}\leq R^{-2s}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\big\||x|^{s}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq(2AR^{-1})^{2s}4^{-sk}.

Similarly, for
k<0
and
f_{n}\in A_{k}
we obtain by Parseval theorem (
1.6
),

(5.39)

\left|{\left\langle{f,f_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2}=\left|{\left\langle{{\mathcal{T}}f,{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2}\leq R^{-2s}\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\big\||\xi|^{s}{\mathcal{T}}f_{n}\big\|_{2,\mu}^{2}\leq(AR^{-1})^{2s}4^{sk}.

Now, since
\left\{f_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\Omega,\mu)
, then

1=\|f\|_{2,\mu}^{2}=\displaystyle\sum_{k}\sum_{f_{n}\in A_{k}}\left|{\left\langle{f,f_{n}}\right\rangle}_{\mu}\right|^{2},

and by combining Inequalities (
5.38
) and (
5.39
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2AR^{-1})^{2s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}4^{-sk}N_{k}+(AR^{-1})^{2s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}4^{-sk}N_{-k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(2AR^{-1})^{2s}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}4^{-sk}+C(AR^{-1})^{2s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}4^{-sk}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C}{R^{2s}}.

Choosing
R
large enough, we get a contradiction. The theorem is proved.
∎

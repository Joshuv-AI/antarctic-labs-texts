Minimal mass blow-up solutions for the L 2 L^{2} -critical NLS with the Delta potential for radial data in one dimension
Xingdong Tang, Guixiang Xu

Abstract
. We consider the L 2 L^{2} -critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) with the delta potential i ​ ∂ t u + ∂ x 2 u + μ ​ δ ​ u + | u | 4 ​ u = 0 , t ∈ ℝ , x ∈ ℝ , i\partial_{t}u+\partial^{2}_{x}u+\mu\delta u+|u|^{4}u=0,\,\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,x\in\mathbb{R}, where μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in\mathbb{R} , and δ \delta is the Dirac delta distribution at x = 0 x=0 . Local well-posedness theory together with sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality and the conservation laws of mass and energy implies that the solution with mass less than ‖ Q ‖ 2 \|Q\|_{2} is global existence in H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}) , where Q Q is the ground state of the L 2 L^{2} -critical NLS without the delta potential (i.e. μ = 0 \mu=0 ). We are interested in the dynamics of the solution with threshold mass ‖ u 0 ‖ 2 = ‖ Q ‖ 2 \|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2} in H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}) . First, for the case μ = 0 \mu=0 , such blow-up solution exists due to the pseudo-conformal symmetry of the equation, and is unique up to the symmetries of the equation in H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}) from [ 49 ] (see also [ 30 ] ), and recently in L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) from [ 17 ] . Second, for the case μ < 0 \mu<0 , simple variational argument with the conservation laws of mass and energy implies that radial solutions with threshold mass exist globally in H 1 ​ ( ℝ ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}) . Last, for the case μ > 0 \mu>0 , we show the existence of radial threshold solutions with blow-up speed determined by the sign (i.e. μ > 0 \mu>0 ) of the delta potential perturbation since the refined blow-up profile to the rescaled equation is stable in a precise sense. The key ingredients here including the Energy-Morawetz argument and compactness method as well as the modulation analysis are close to the original one in [ 61 ] (see also [ 34 , 39 , 42 , 46 , 55 ] ).

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the
L^{2}
-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the delta potential

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial^{2}_{x}u+\mu\delta u+|u|^{4}u=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),&\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u
is a complex-valued function of
(t,x)
,

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta
is the Dirac delta distribution at the origin.
For
\mu=0
, it is focusing,
L^{2}
-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) in one dimension, here we call it the
L^{2}
-critical NLS since the scaling transformation

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x)

leaves the
L^{2}
norm invariant

\|u_{\lambda}(t,\cdot)\|_{2}=\|u(\lambda^{2}t,\cdot)\|_{2}.

A series of studies dealt with the
L^{2}
-critical NLS, we can refer to
[
7
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
22
,
49
,
62
]
, and references therein. For
\mu\not=0
, it appears in various physical models with a point defect on the line, for instance, quantum mechanics
[
2
]
, nonlinear optics
[
25
,
28
,
29
]
and references therein. We can refer to
[
1
,
2
]
for more details on the
\delta
-perturbation of strength
\mu
of the self-adjoint operator
\partial^{2}_{x}
. The appearance of the delta potential destroys spatial translation, scaling transformation and pseudo-conformal transformation invariances of (
1.1
). For the case
\mu<0
, it corresponds to the repulsive delta potential, while for the case
\mu>0
, it corresponds to the attractive delta potential.

Local well-posedness result for (
1.1
) is well understood in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by many authors, for example, by Cazenave in
[
7
, Theorem 3.7.1]
, Fukuizumi, Ohta and Ozawa in
[
24
]
and Masaki, Murphy and Segata in
[
47
]
. More precisely, we have

Proposition 1.1
.

For any
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, there exists a unique maximal lifespan solution

u\in\mathcal{C}\left(\left(-T_{*},T^{*}\right),H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\right)\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}\left(\left(-T_{*},T^{*}\right),H^{-1}(\mathbb{R})\right)
of (
1.1
)
satisfying the the following blow-up criterion,

T^{*}<+\infty\text{\;implies that\;}\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\nearrow T^{*}}\lVert\partial_{x}u(t)\rVert_{2}=+\infty.

(1.2)

Moreover, the mass and the energy are conserved
under the flow (
1.1
), i.e., for any
t\in\left(-T_{*},T^{*}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle M(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx=M(u_{0}),

(1.3)

\displaystyle E(u(t)):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\frac{1}{2}|\partial_{x}u(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\mu\ \delta(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}-\frac{1}{6}|u(t,x)|^{6}\right]dx=E(u_{0}).

(1.4)

1.1.
The
L^{2}
-critical case
\mu=0
.

Let us firstly recall the well-known results of (
1.1
) for the case
\mu=0
.
From the variational argument in
[
65
]
, the positive, radial ground state solution to

-\partial^{2}_{x}Q+Q-|Q|^{4}Q=0,\ \ Q\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\ \ Q>0,\ \ \mbox{$Q$ radial}

(1.5)

is the extremizer of sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

\|u\|^{6}_{6}\leq C_{\rm GN}\|u\|_{2}^{4}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{2}^{2},

(1.6)

where
C_{\rm GN}=3/\lVert Q\rVert^{4}_{2}
. Moreover, the ground state
Q
is unique up to symmetries of (
1.5
) from
[
5
,
37
]
. Therefore, for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

E(u)=E_{\rm crit}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{x}u\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\lVert u\rVert_{6}^{6}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u\|^{4}_{2}}{\|Q\|^{4}_{2}}\right),

(1.7)

which together with (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and the blow-up criterion (
1.2
) implies that the solutions of (
1.1
) with mass less than
\|Q\|_{2}
globally exist in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Furthermore, it scatters to the linear solutions
e^{it\partial^{2}_{x}}u^{\pm}_{0}
for some
u^{\pm}_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
in both time directions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we can refer to
[
13
]
for more details.

For the critical mass case
\|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2}
. By applying the pseudo-conformal transformation

u(t,x)\longmapsto v(t,x)=\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\bar{u}\left(\frac{1}{t},\frac{x}{t}\right)e^{i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}

(1.8)

to the solitary solution
u(t,x)=Q(x)e^{it}
, we can obtain the explicit blow-up solution with threshold mass

S(t,x)=\frac{1}{|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}}Q\left(\frac{x}{|t|}\right)e^{-i\frac{|x|^{2}}{4|t|}}e^{\frac{i}{|t|}},\quad\|S(t)\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\partial_{x}S(t)\|_{2}\approx\frac{1}{|t|}\,\,\text{as}\,\,t\to 0^{-}.

(1.9)

From
[
49
]
, finite time blow-up solutions with threshold mass
\lVert Q\rVert_{2}
are completely classified by F. Merle in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
in the following sense

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ T^{*}<+\infty\ \ \mbox{imply}\ \ u\equiv S

up to the symmetries of (
1.1
) (see also
[
30
]
for simplified proof). Recently B. Dodson pushes this rigidity result forward in
[
17
,
18
]
, and obtains the classification result of finite time blow-up solutions of the focusing,
L^{2}
-critical NLS with threshold mass in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1\leq d\leq 15
.

For the super-critical mass case
\|u_{0}\|_{2}>\|Q\|_{2}
, there are lots of important blow-up results of (
1.1
) in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can refer to
[
6
,
21
,
50
,
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
58
,
59
,
60
]
,
and references therein.

1.2.
The repulsive potential case
\mu<0

Now we consider the repulsive potential case
\mu<0
. On the one hand, there are no solitary waves of (
1.1
) with the subcritical mass
\|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2}
from classical variational argument in
[
24
,
38
]
. On the other hand, by (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
), we can obtain similar estimate to those in (
1.7
)

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}|u(t,0)|^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{6}^{6}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{4}_{2}}{\|Q\|^{4}_{2}}\right).

This together with the blow-up criterion (
1.2
) implies the global existence of the solutions of (
1.1
) with mass less than
\|Q\|_{2}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Because of the repulsive (
\mu<0
) delta potential perturbation and the lack of the pseudo-conformal symmetry (
1.8
), we have at the threshold mass that

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mu<0
, and radial
u_{0}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\|u_{0}\|_{2}=\|Q\|_{2}
, then the solution of (
1.1
) is global and bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

The result follows from the standard concentration compactness argument with the conservation laws of mass and energy, see proof in Appendix
A
.

In contrast to the subcritical mass, there are solitary waves
Q_{\omega,\mu}(x)e^{i\omega t}
of (
1.1
) with the super-critical mass
\|Q_{\omega,\mu}\|_{2}>\|Q\|_{2}
from standard variational argument in
[
24
,
38
]
, at the same time, these solitary waves are orbitally unstable, and even strongly unstable in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can refer to
[
38
]
, and references therein. Therefore the global existence result in Theorem
1.2
is sharp in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and we also conjecture that for the repulsive delta potential case, the solutions of (
1.1
) with
\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert Q\rVert_{2}
will scatter to the linear solution in both time directions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
due to the result in
[
13
]
. For other long-time asymptotic behavior and scattering results of nonlinear Schrödinger equation with the repulsive delta potential, we can refer to
[
4
,
12
,
31
,
47
]
, and references therein.

1.3.
The attractive potential case
\mu>0

Let us now turn to the attractive potential case
\mu>0
, it is main part for the rest of the paper.
First, by the Sobolev inequality and the Young inequality, we have for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
with
\lVert u\rVert_{2}<\lVert Q\rVert_{2}
that

\frac{\mu}{2}|u(t,0)|^{2}\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\lVert\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}\leq\frac{1}{6}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{4}_{2}}{\|Q\|^{4}_{2}}\right)+C(\mu,\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}),

this together with sharp Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) implies that for any
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2}|u(t,0)|^{2}-\frac{1}{6}\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{6}^{6}\\
\geq\frac{1}{3}\|\partial_{x}u(t,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}\left(1-\frac{\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{4}_{2}}{\|Q\|^{4}_{2}}\right)-C(\mu,\lVert u(t,\cdot)\rVert_{2}),

(1.10)

which once again implies the controllability of the kinetic energy
\lVert\partial_{x}u(t)\rVert^{2}_{2}
of the solution of (
1.1
) by the mass and energy, and deduces global well-posedness of the solution of (
1.1
) with
\|u_{0}\|_{2}<\|Q\|_{2}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
due to (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and the blow-up criterion (
1.2
). However, in contrast to both the case
\mu=0
and the case
\mu<0
, there exist arbitrarily small solitary waves
Q_{\omega,\mu}(x)e^{i\omega t}
as follows,

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mu>0
. For all
M\in(0,\|Q\|_{2})
, there exists
\omega>\mu^{2}/4
and a unique positive, radially symmetric solution of

-\omega Q_{\omega,\mu}+\partial^{2}_{x}Q_{\omega,\mu}+\mu\delta Q_{\omega,\mu}+Q_{\omega,\mu}^{5}=0,\ \ \|Q_{\omega,\mu}\|_{2}=M.

The proof of Proposition
1.3
follows from classical variational argument in
[
23
,
24
]
, we give alternative proof by in Appendix
B
.
Since the solitary waves
Q_{\omega,\mu}(x)e^{i\omega t}
themselves don’t scatter to the linear solution, the solution of (
1.1
) with mass less than
\lVert Q\rVert_{2}
don’t scatter in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
any more in this case. The necessary condition that
\omega>\mu^{2}/4
is related to the eigenvalue of the Schrödigner operator
\partial^{2}_{x}+\mu\delta
in
[
2
,
24
]
. In fact, these solitary waves can be explicitly described as following (see

]

{Q}_{\omega,\mu}(x)=\left[3\omega\sech^{2}\left(2\sqrt{\omega}|x|+\arctanh\left(\frac{\mu}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

(1.11)

By the well-known stability theory in
[

In contrast to the non-existence result of finite time blow-up solutions with threshold mass for the repulsive case

\left(u,v\right)_{2}=\mathrm{Re}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)\bar{v}(x)\,dx\right),\quad\|u\|_{r}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{r}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

We denote the radial functions in

We fix the notation: for

f(u)=|u|^{4}u;\quad g(u)=\mu\,\delta u;\quad F(u)=\frac{1}{6}|u|^{6};\quad G(u)=\frac{1}{2}\mu\,\delta|u|^{2}.

where

is the Dirac delta distribution at the origin and obeys the following scaling property by simple distribution calculation (see
[
26

\forall x\in\mathbb{R},\ \lambda>0,\quad\delta(\lambda x)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\delta(x).

(1.14)

Identifying
\mathbb{C}
with
\mathbb{R}^{2}

by

,

,

and

. Let
\Lambda
be the infinitesimal generator of the

-scaling transformation, i.e.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mu^{2}/4<\omega=1
in the rest of the paper, the linearized operator around

is

L_{+}:=-\partial^{2}_{y}+1-5Q^{4},\qquad L_{-}:=-\partial^{2}_{y}+1-Q^{4}.

and the generalized kernel of

\begin{pmatrix}0&L_{-}\\

(1.18)

For the sake of the localization argument in Section

\phi
and its scaling. Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth even and convex function, nondecreasing on

\phi(r)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2}r^{2}&\text{ for }&r<1,\\

Corollary 3.4

For all

3.3.
The monotonicity formula: the Energy-Morawetz estimate

After we obtain the dynamics of the modulation parameters in last subsection. Now, we turn to introduce the Energy-Morawetz functional in this subsection, use it in next subsection to improve the estimates about the remainder
\varepsilon
and modulation parameters
\lambda
,
b
, and then close the bootstrap argument.

The Energy-Morawetz estimate was firstly introduced to construct minimal mass blow-up solution of the inhomogeneous NLS in
[
61
]
, see also
[
34
,
39
,
46
,
55
]
), and recently, it was also successfully applied by B. Dodson to obtain the global well-posedness and scattering results of the defocusing, nonlinear wave equation in the critical Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for
\frac{1}{2}\leq s<1
in
[
14
,
15
]
.

First, we recall the well-known coercivity property of the linearized operators
L_{\pm}
around
Q
in
H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}+i\varepsilon_{2}\in H^{1}_{\rm rad}(\mathbb{R})
, then there exists constant
C>0
such that

\left\langle L_{+}\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\right\rangle+\left\langle L_{-}\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{2}\right\rangle\geq C\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{C}\left(\left(\varepsilon_{1},Q\right)_{2}^{2}+\left(\varepsilon_{1},|y|^{2}Q\right)_{2}^{2}+\left(\varepsilon_{2},\rho\right)_{2}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Please refer to Lemma
3.2
in
[
61
]
for more details (See also
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
67
]
).
∎

Now, we define the linearized energy functional of the remainder
\varepsilon
according to (
3.16
) as follows

H(s,\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{2}\lVert\partial_{y}\varepsilon\rVert_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon\Big)dy\\
-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(G(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-G(P_{b})-dG(P_{b})\varepsilon\Big)dy.

Note that the equation (
3.16
) can be rewrited as

i\varepsilon_{s}-{D_{\varepsilon}}H(s,\varepsilon)+\ModOp(s)P_{b}-i\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}\Lambda\varepsilon+(1-\gamma_{s})\varepsilon+e^{-ib\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}}\Psi_{K}=0,

(3.43)

where
D_{\varepsilon}H(s,\varepsilon)
denotes the Fréchet derivative of the functional
H(s,\varepsilon)
with respect to
\varepsilon
and

\ModOp(s)P_{b}=-i\left(\frac{\lambda_{s}}{\lambda}+b\right)\Lambda P_{b}+(1-\gamma_{s})P_{b}+\big(b_{s}+b^{2}-\theta\big)\frac{|y|^{2}}{4}P_{b}.

First, as the consequence of Lemma
3.5
, we have the coercivity property for the energy functional
H(s,\varepsilon)
under the orthogonality conditions of
\varepsilon
(see (
3.4
), (
3.42
)) as follows.

Lemma 3.6
.

For all
s\in[s_{*},s_{1}]
, then we have

H(s,\varepsilon)\gtrsim\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+O(s^{-2(K+1)}).

Proof.

Firstly, from (
3.4
), (
3.9
) and (
3.42
), the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,|y|^{2}Q\right)_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon,|y|^{2}P_{b}\right)_{2}+O(|b|\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})+O(\lambda\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})=O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}),

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,i\rho\right)_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon,i\rho_{b}\right)_{2}+O(|b|\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{2})=O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}),

\displaystyle\left(\varepsilon,Q\right)_{2}

\displaystyle=O(s^{-(K+1)}),

where we used the fact that
|P_{b}-Q||\lesssim Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(|b|+\lambda\big)
in the first equality.

Next, If we denote
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}+i\varepsilon_{2}
, then

\left|F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}5Q^{4}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{4}\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right|\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}|y|}|\varepsilon|^{3}+|\varepsilon|^{6}+|\varepsilon|^{2}(|b|+\lambda).

Thus, from (
3.9
), we have

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(F(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-F(P_{b})-dF(P_{b})\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}5Q^{4}\varepsilon_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}Q^{4}\varepsilon_{2}^{2}\right)\,dy\right|\lesssim O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}),

and

\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(G(P_{b}+\varepsilon)-G(P_{b})-dG(P_{b})\varepsilon\Big)dx=O(s^{-2}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}).

Therefore, we have

\left|H(s,\varepsilon)-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle L_{+}\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\right\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\left\langle L_{-}\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{2}\right\rangle\right|\lesssim O(s^{-1}\lVert\varepsilon\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}),

which together with the coercivity properties of
L_{\pm}
in Lemma
3.5
implies the result.
∎

For future reference, we also need the following localized coercivity property.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists
A_{0}>1
such that for any

Irreducible modules of toroidal Lie algebras arising from ϕ ϵ \phi_{\epsilon} -coordinated modules of vertex algebras
Fulin Chen, Huansheng Li, Nina Yu

Abstract
. In this paper, for every ϵ ∈ ℤ \epsilon\in\mathbb{Z} , we introduce an extension of the 2-toroidal Lie algebra by certain derivations. Based on the ϕ ϵ \phi_{\epsilon} -coordinated modules theory for vertex algebras, we give an explicit realization of a class of irreducible highest weight modules for this extended toroidal Lie algebra. When ϵ = 1 \epsilon=1 , this affords a realization of certain irreducible modules for the toroidal extended affine Lie algebras first constructed by Billig.

Introduction

Toroidal Lie algebras are natural multi-variable generalizations of affine Lie
algebras. One starts with a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and
forms the multi-loop algebra

In the literature, various extensions of

For examples, the subalgebra

2.
Toroidal Lie algebras

In this section we introduce the extended toroidal Lie algebras we concern about in this paper.

2.1.
Toroidal Lie algebras
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}

Let
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{C}[t_{0}^{\pm 1},t_{1}^{\pm 1}]
be
the Laurent polynomial ring in the variables
t_{0}
and
t_{1}
. Denote by

\Omega_{\mathcal{R}}^{1}=\mathcal{R}\mathrm{d}t_{0}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mathrm{d}t_{1}=\mathcal{R}\mathrm{k}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mathrm{k}_{1}

the space of
1
-forms on
\mathcal{R}
, where
\mathrm{k}_{0}:=t_{0}^{-1}\mathrm{d}t_{0}
and
\mathrm{k}_{1}:=t_{1}^{-1}\mathrm{d}t_{1}
.
Set

\mathrm{d}({\mathcal{R}})=\{\mathrm{d}(f)\,|\,f\in\mathcal{R}\},

the space of exact
1
-forms, where

\mathrm{d}(f):=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{0}}\mathrm{d}t_{0}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{1}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}=t_{0}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{0}}\mathrm{k}_{0}+t_{1}\frac{\partial f}{\partial t_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{1}\in\Omega_{\mathcal{R}}^{1}

is the differential of
f
.
Form the quotient vector space

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}=\Omega_{\mathcal{R}}^{1}/\mathrm{d}({\mathcal{R}}).

For
m_{0},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

\mathrm{k}_{m_{0},m_{1}}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{m_{1}}t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{0},\ &\textnormal{{if}}\ m_{1}\neq 0\\
-\frac{1}{m_{0}}t_{0}^{m_{0}}\mathrm{k}_{1},\ &\textnormal{{if}}\ m_{1}=0,\ m_{0}\neq 0\\
0,\ &\textnormal{{if}}\ m_{0}=m_{1}=0.\end{cases}

Then the set

(2.1)

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{k}_{0},\mathrm{k}_{1}\}\cup\{\mathrm{k}_{m_{0},m_{1}}\mid m_{0},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\ \textnormal{{with}}\ (m_{0},m_{1})\neq(0,0)\}

is a basis of
\mathcal{K}
,
noting that these elements in
\Omega_{\mathcal{R}}^{1}
should be understood as their images in
\mathcal{K}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra equipped with a symmetric invariant bilinear form
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
.
Form a central extension of the double loop algebra
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{g})=(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathcal{K},

called the
2
-toroidal Lie algebra
, where
\mathcal{K}
is central and

(2.2)

\begin{split}[t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\otimes u,t_{0}^{n_{0}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes v]=t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\otimes[u,v]+{\langle}u,v{\rangle}\sum_{r=0,1}m_{r}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{r}\end{split}

for
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
,
m_{0},n_{0},m_{1},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra and
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
is nondegenerate,

\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{g})
is known as a universal central extension of
\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathfrak{g}
(see
[
MRY
]
).

We denote by

\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R})=\mathcal{R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}\oplus\mathcal{R}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}=\mathcal{R}\mathrm{d}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{R}\mathrm{d}_{1}

the
derivation Lie algebra on
\mathcal{R}
, where

\mathrm{d}_{0}:=t_{0}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}
and
\mathrm{d}_{1}:=t_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}
.
By adding
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R})
to
\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{g})
, we obtain the
full
2
-toroidal Lie algebra
(see
[
B1
]
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu}:=\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R})=(\mathcal{R}\otimes\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathcal{K}\oplus\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R}),

with Lie relations

\displaystyle\left[t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{i},t_{0}^{n_{0}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\otimes x\right]

\displaystyle=n_{i}(t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\otimes x),

\displaystyle\left[t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{i},t_{0}^{n_{0}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=n_{i}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{j}+\delta_{i,j}\sum_{r=0,1}m_{r}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{r},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left[pt_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{i},t_{0}^{n_{0}}t_{1}^{n_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{j}\right]

\displaystyle=n_{i}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{j}-m_{j}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{i}

\displaystyle-\mu m_{j}n_{i}\left(m_{0}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{0}+m_{1}t_{0}^{m_{0}+n_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}+n_{1}}\mathrm{k}_{1}\right)

for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
m_{0},n_{0},m_{1},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, where

\mu
is a fixed complex number throughout this paper.
The algebra
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})
is
\mathbb{Z}
-graded with respect to the adjoint action of
-\mathrm{d}_{0}
,
i.e.,

\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})_{(n)},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})_{(n)}=\{x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})\mid[\mathrm{d}_{0},x]=-nx\}.

For a given integer
\epsilon
, set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon}=\bigg\{f_{0}t_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}+f_{1}\mathrm{d}_{1}\mid f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathcal{R},\ t_{0}^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{0}}(f_{0})+\mathrm{d}_{1}(f_{1})=0\bigg\}\subset\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R}).

For
m_{0},m_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, write

\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{m_{0},m_{1}}^{\epsilon}=(m_{0}-\epsilon+1)t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{1}-m_{1}t_{0}^{m_{0}}t_{1}^{m_{1}}\mathrm{d}_{0},

which is an element of
\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon}
.
A simple fact is that the set

(2.4)

\displaystyle\{t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0},t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{1}\}\cup\{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0},m_{1}}\mid(m_{0},m_{1})\in(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})\backslash\{(\epsilon-1,0)\}\}

is a basis of
\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon}
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon}
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathrm{Der}(\mathcal{R})
with the relations

\displaystyle[t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0},m_{1}}]

\displaystyle=\left(m_{0}-\epsilon+1\right)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0}+\epsilon-1,m_{1}},\quad[t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{1},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0},m_{1}}]=m_{1}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0}+\epsilon-1,m_{1}},

\displaystyle[\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0},m_{1}},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{n_{0},n_{1}}]

\displaystyle=\left((m_{0}-\epsilon+1)n_{1}-m_{1}(n_{0}-\epsilon+1)\right)\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}_{m_{0}+n_{0},m_{1}+n_{1}},

where
m_{0},m_{1},n_{0},n_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Set

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathcal{D}^{\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g}),

which is the Lie algebra we study in this paper.

For any

,

In addition, for

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{n,m}^{\epsilon}:=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{n,m}^{\epsilon}+\mu(1-\epsilon)\left(n+\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon)\right)m^{2}\mathrm{k}_{n,m}.

Let
\mathcal{B}

\{\mathrm{K}_{n},\,\mathrm{D}_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}\}.
Set

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{B}=\left(\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{1}^{-1}]\otimes\mathfrak{g}\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{1}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{d}_{1}\oplus\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}}\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{K}_{n}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{D}_{n}\right).

Define a linear map

\psi_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\epsilon}:\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}[[z,z^{-1}]],\quad a\mapsto a^{\epsilon}(z)

by letting

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}(z)

For

\displaystyle a^{(r)}_{\epsilon}=\prod_{s=0}^{r-1}(a+s(\epsilon-1)).

The following result will be used later:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{N}
,
a,b,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p}\binom{p}{r}\alpha^{p-r}{a_{\epsilon}}^{(p-r)}(-\beta)^{r}{b_{\epsilon}}^{(r)}=\sum_{s=0}^{p}\binom{p}{s}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-s}a^{(p-s)}_{\epsilon}\beta^{s}(-a-b-(p-1)(\epsilon-1))^{(s)}_{\epsilon}.

Proof.

Note that we have the following identity

(2.6)

\displaystyle(a+b)^{(p)}_{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=0}^{p}\binom{p}{i}a^{(i)}_{\epsilon}b^{(p-i)}_{\epsilon},

which can be proved by induction on
p

Using (
2.6
) and the facts

\binom{p-r}{t}\binom{p}{r}=\binom{p}{r+t}\binom{r+t}{r}\quad\left(0\leqslant r\leqslant p,\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant p-r\right),

and

a_{\epsilon}^{(p-r)}=a_{\epsilon}^{(p-s)}\left(a+(p-s)(\epsilon-1)\right)_{\epsilon}^{(s-r)}\quad\left(0\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant p\right),

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p}\binom{p}{r}\alpha^{p-r}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-r)}(-\beta)^{r}b_{\epsilon}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{p-r}\binom{p-r}{t}\binom{p}{r}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-r-t}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-r)}(-\beta)^{r+t}b_{\epsilon}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{p}\sum_{t=0}^{p-r}\binom{p}{r+t}\binom{r+t}{r}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-r-t}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-r)}(-\beta)^{r+t}b_{\epsilon}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{p}\sum_{r=0}^{s}\binom{p}{s}\binom{s}{r}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-s}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-r)}(-\beta)^{s}b_{\epsilon}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{p}\sum_{r=0}^{s}\binom{p}{s}\binom{s}{r}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-s}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-s)}(a+(p-s)(\epsilon-1))_{\epsilon}^{(s-r)}(-\beta)^{s}b_{\epsilon}^{(r)}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{p}\binom{p}{s}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-s}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-s)}(a+b+(p-s)(\epsilon-1))_{\epsilon}^{(s)}(-\beta)^{s}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{p}\binom{p}{s}(\alpha+\beta)^{p-s}a_{\epsilon}^{(p-s)}(-a-b-(p-1)(\epsilon-1))_{\epsilon}^{(s)}\beta^{s}.

∎

3.1.
Highest weight modules for affine-Virasoro algebras

Let

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}=\left(\mathrm{Der}\,\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\right)\ltimes\left(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes\mathfrak{b}\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}},

\mathfrak{b}

\mathrm{k}

(3.1)

\begin{split}[L(m),L(n)]&=(m-n)L(m+n)+\frac{m^{3}-m}{12}\delta_{m+n,0}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}},\\

for
u,v\in\mathfrak{b}

and

Note that
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
contains the Virasoro algebra

\mathsf{Vir}=\mathrm{Der}\,\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}

and the affine algebras

\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}=\left(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes\mathfrak{b}\right)\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k},\quad\widetilde{\mathfrak{b}}=\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\oplus\mathbb{C}t\frac{d}{dt}

For any
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.2)

\displaystyle a^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(a\otimes t^{n}\right)z^{\epsilon-n-1},\quad\forall a\in\mathfrak{b},

(3.3)

\displaystyle L^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}L(n)z^{2\epsilon-n-2}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon}{24}z^{2(\epsilon-1)}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}.

\epsilon=0

.

\mathfrak{b}

where
(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(0)}=\mathfrak{b}\oplus\mathbb{C}L(0)\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}},

and

\displaystyle(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(\pm)}=\oplus_{n>0}(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(\mp n)}={\rm Span}\{u(\pm n),\ L(\pm n)\mid u\in\mathfrak{b},\ n>0\}.

U

\mathfrak{b}
-module and

\mathrm{k}

\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}

\beta
,
\ell

((\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(+)}+(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(0)})
-module

Then we have the induced
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module:

(3.4)

\displaystyle V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,c,U,\beta)=\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}\left((\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(+)}+(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir})_{(0)}\right)}U.

\mathfrak{b}
-module
U

Similarly, we have a
\mathbb{Z}

\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}

\mathfrak{b}_{(n)}=t^{-n}\otimes\mathfrak{b}+\delta_{n,0}\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{(\pm)}=t^{\pm 1}\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]\otimes\mathfrak{b}
.
Extend the
\mathfrak{b}
-module
U
to a
(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{(+)}\oplus\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{(0)})
-module such that
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{(+)}
acts trivially and
\mathrm{k}
acts as the scalar
\ell
.
Then we have the Verma type highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
-module

(3.5)

\displaystyle V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}}(\ell,U)=\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{(+)}+\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}_{(0)})}U

and its irreducible quotient
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}}(\ell,U)
provided that
U
is irreducible.
Note that both
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}}(\ell,U)
and
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}}(\ell,U)
are naturally
\mathbb{N}
-graded with
U
as the degree
0
subspace.

We also have a triangular decomposition
\mathsf{Vir}=\mathsf{Vir}_{(+)}\oplus\mathsf{Vir}_{(0)}\oplus\mathsf{Vir}_{(-)}
of the Virasoro algebra
\mathsf{Vir}
,
where
\mathsf{Vir}_{(\pm)}=\oplus_{m>0}\mathbb{C}L(\pm m)

and
\mathsf{Vir}_{(0)}=\mathbb{C}L(0)+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}
. View
\mathbb{C}
as a
(\mathsf{Vir}_{(+)}\oplus\mathsf{Vir}_{(0)})
-module such that
\mathsf{Vir}_{(+)}
acts trivially,
L(0)
acts as the scalar
\beta
and
\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}
acts
as the scalar
c
.
Then we have the Verma type highest weight module

(3.6)

\displaystyle V_{\mathsf{Vir}}(c,\beta)=\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{Vir})\otimes_{\mathcal{U}(\mathsf{Vir}_{(+)}+\mathsf{Vir}_{(0)})}\mathbb{C}

and its irreducible quotient
L_{\mathsf{Vir}}(c,\beta)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{b}
is finite-dimensional simple,
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
is the normalized form, and
U
is finite dimensional.
By the Sugawara construction (see
[
K1
]
), when
\ell
is not the negative dual Coxeter number, the irreducible
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,c,U,\beta)
can be realized as the tensor product of the
\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}
-module

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{b}}}(\ell,U)
and the
\mathsf{Vir}
-module
L_{\mathsf{Vir}}(c^{\prime},\beta^{\prime})
for some suitable
c^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}
.

3.2.
The main construction

In this subsection we construct a class of irreducible

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-modules.

Let
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{C}{\bf k}+\mathbb{C}{\bf d}
be a vector space equipped with a designated basis
\{{\bf k},{\bf d}\}
and
a symmetric bilinear form
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}
determined by

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\langle}{\bf k},{\bf k}{\rangle}=0={\langle}{\bf d},{\bf d}{\rangle},\quad\quad{\langle}{\bf k},{\bf d}{\rangle}=1.

Set
L=\mathbb{Z}{\bf k}\subset\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{{\bf k}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}L=\mathbb{C}{\bf k}
.
Denote by

\mathbb{C}[L]=\oplus_{\gamma\in L}\mathbb{C}e^{\gamma}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}_{{\bf k}}]=\oplus_{h\in\mathfrak{h}_{{\bf k}}}\mathbb{C}e^{h}

the group algebras of
L
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\bf k}
,
respectively.
We endow
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}_{{\bf k}}]
with an

\mathfrak{h}
-module structure:
he^{h^{\prime}}={\langle}h,h^{\prime}{\rangle}e^{h^{\prime}}
for
h\in\mathfrak{h},h^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\bf k}
.
Note that for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
,
e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L]
is an
\mathfrak{h}
-submodule of
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{h}_{{\bf k}}]
.
Then for any
\ell\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, we have
a Verma type highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
(see (
3.5
)).
Note that we have the following vector spaces identification:

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}\left(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L]\right)=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,0)\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L].

It is known (see
[
LL
]
) that the highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
can be extended to an
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module with

\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}=2
and

(3.8)

\displaystyle L_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)=\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}(z){\bf d}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}.

Let
W
be a vector space and
u,v\in W
. For any two formal series
u(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{n}z^{-n-1}
and
v(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}v_{n}z^{-n-1}
in
W[[z,z^{-1}]]
,
we denote by

{}_{\circ}^{\circ}u(z_{1})v(z_{2})_{\circ}^{\circ}\ =u^{+}(z_{1})v(z_{2})+v(z_{2})u^{-}(z_{1})

the normally ordered product of
u(z_{1})
and
v(z_{2})
, where

u^{+}(z)=\sum_{n\textless 0}u_{n}z^{-n-1}\quad\textnormal{{and}}\quad u^{-}(z)=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}u_{n}z^{-n-1}.

Form the direct sum

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h}

of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
.
Let
U
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
\alpha,\beta,\ell\in\mathbb{C}
with
\ell\neq 0
.
We define an
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module structure on the tensor space

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])

by letting
\mathrm{k}=\ell
,
\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}=24\mu\ell
and

(3.9)

\displaystyle u(z)=u(z)\otimes 1,\quad h(z)=1\otimes h(z),\quad L_{\mathfrak{f}}(z)=L_{\mathfrak{g}}(z)\otimes 1+1\otimes L_{\mathfrak{h}}(z),

for
u\in\mathfrak{g}
and
h\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Let
\mathbb{C}[L]
act on
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
by

e^{\gamma}(v\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}+\gamma^{\prime}})=v\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}+\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma},\quad\forall\text{$\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in L$, $v\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,0)$}.

For
\gamma\in L
, introduce the following operator in
\mathrm{End}\left(V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])\right)[[z,z^{-1}]]
:

(3.10)

\displaystyle E^{\gamma}(z)=\textnormal{{exp}}\left(\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma(-m)}{m}z^{m}\right)\textnormal{{exp}}\left(-\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma(m)}{m}z^{-m}\right)e^{\gamma}.

For convenience, we also set

(3.11)

\displaystyle E^{\gamma}(z)=1\otimes E^{\gamma}(z)\in\mathrm{End}\left(L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])\right)[[z,z^{-1}]].

The following theorem is the first main result of this paper. We will prove this theorem in Section 5.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\ell,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\ell\neq 0
and let
U
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Then there is an irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module structure on the
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L]),

where
\mathrm{k}_{0}=\ell
,
t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0}=-L_{\mathfrak{f}}(\epsilon-1)+\delta_{\epsilon,1}\mu\ell
, and

(3.12)

\begin{split}&(t_{1}^{m}\otimes u)^{\epsilon}(z)=u^{\epsilon}(z)E^{m{\bf k}}(z),\ \mathrm{k}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)={\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z),\ \mathrm{d}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)={\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z),\ \mathrm{K}^{\epsilon}_{n}(z)=\frac{\ell}{n}E^{n{\bf k}}(z),\\
&\mathrm{D}^{\epsilon}_{n}(z)=n_{\circ}^{\circ}L^{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{f}}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)-z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}\\
&\qquad\qquad+n^{2}(\mu-\frac{1}{\ell})\left(z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z)\right)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\end{split}

for
u\in\mathfrak{g},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
.

Remark 3.3
.

When
\epsilon=1
, the
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])

was first constructed by Billig in
[
B2
]
(see also
[
CLiT
]
).
And, when
\epsilon=0
, this
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module was previously constructed in
[
CLiT
]
.

3.3.
Realization of irreducible highest weight
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-modules

In this subsection we give a characterization of the irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-modules
constructed in Theorem
3.2
.

Note that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}=\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(n)}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded subalgebra of
\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})
, where

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(n)}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}\cap\mathcal{T}(\mathfrak{g})_{(n)}
.
Explicitly, we have

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(n)}^{\epsilon}=\textnormal{{Span}}\{t_{0}^{-n}u,\,\mathrm{d}_{-n,m}^{\epsilon},\,\mathrm{k}_{-n,m}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z},u\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\}\oplus\mathbb{C}\delta_{n,1-\epsilon}t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0},

if
n\neq 0
, and

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(0)}^{\epsilon}={\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}}_{1}+\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}}(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{d}_{0,n}^{\epsilon}+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0,n})+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0}+\mathbb{C}\delta_{\epsilon,1}\mathrm{d}_{0}.

This
\mathbb{Z}
-grading induces a natural triangular decomposition of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}=\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(+)}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(-)},

where

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(\pm)}^{\epsilon}=\oplus_{n>0}\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(\mp n)}^{\epsilon}.

As usual, we say that a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module
W
is a
highest weight module
if there exists a vector
w\in W
such that

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(+)}w=0
and
\mathcal{U}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon})w=W
.
Let
T
be an irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module. Then we have the Verma type highest weight
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module

V_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T)=\mathcal{U}\left(\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{U}\left(\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(+)}+\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}\right)}T,

where
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(+)}
acts trivially on
T
.
The
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module
V_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T)
admits an
\mathbb{N}
-grading structure determined by
\deg T=0
.
Denote by
L_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T)
the quotient of
V_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T)
modulo by its maximal graded submodule.
Note that
L_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T)
is an irreducible highest weight
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module, and conversely any
irreducible highest weight
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module is of this form.

Let
U
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module and
\ell,a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.
Associated to these data, we will construct an irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module structure on
the loop space
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U
.
Assume first that
\epsilon\neq 1
. One can check that in this case

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{1}^{-1}]\otimes\mathfrak{g}+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{1}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathbb{C}\mathrm{d}_{0,m}^{\epsilon}+\mathbb{C}t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0}\right),

where
\mathrm{k}_{1}
is central and

\displaystyle[t_{1}^{m}\otimes u,t_{1}^{n}\otimes v]=t_{1}^{m+n}\otimes[u,v]+m\delta_{m+n,0}{\langle}u,v{\rangle}\mathrm{k}_{1},\ [t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0},t_{1}^{n}\mathrm{k}_{0}]=0,\ [t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0},t_{1}^{n}\otimes u]=0,

\displaystyle[\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,m},t_{1}^{n}\mathrm{k}_{0}]=(1-\epsilon)nt_{1}^{m+n}\mathrm{k}_{0}-m^{2}\delta_{m+n,0}\mathrm{k}_{1},\ [\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,m},t_{1}^{n}\otimes u]=(1-\epsilon)nt_{1}^{m+n}\otimes u,

\displaystyle[\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,m},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,n}]=(1-\epsilon)(n-m)\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,m+n}+2\mu m^{3}(\epsilon-1)^{2}\delta_{m+n,0}\mathrm{k}_{1}

for
m,n,k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u,v\in\mathfrak{g}
.
This implies that the assignment (
m\in\mathbb{Z},u\in\mathfrak{g}
)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{k}_{1}\mapsto 0,\quad\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,m}\mapsto(1-\epsilon)\,t^{m+1}\frac{d}{dt},\quad t_{1}^{m}\otimes u\mapsto t^{m}\otimes u,\quad t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0}\mapsto t^{m}\otimes\mathrm{k}_{0}

determines a surjective homomorphism from
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
to the centerless affine-Virasoro algebra

\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{g}}:=(\mathrm{Der}\,\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])\ltimes\left(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes(\mathfrak{g}+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0})\right)

associated to
\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0}
.
Extend
U
to a
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0})
-module such that
\mathrm{k}_{0}
acts as a scalar
\ell
.
Following
[
B3
]
, there is an irreducible
\mathcal{W}_{\mathfrak{g}}
-module structure on the loop space
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U
with

(t^{m}\otimes v)(t^{n}\otimes u)=t^{m+n}\otimes(vu),\quad\left(t^{m+1}\frac{d}{dt}\right)(t^{n}\otimes u)=(n+a+bm)t^{m+n}\otimes u

for
x\in\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{0}
,
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in U
.
Via the homomorphism (
3.13
),
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U
becomes a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module,
which we denote by
T_{U,\ell,a,b}
.

When
\epsilon=1
we have

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{1}_{(0)}=\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{1}^{-1}]\otimes\mathfrak{g}+\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathbb{C}t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{d}_{0}+\mathbb{C}t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0}\right)+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{k}_{1}+\mathbb{C}\mathrm{d}_{1},

which is isomorphic to the affine Kac-Moody algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
associated to
\mathfrak{f}
with

t_{1}^{m}\otimes u\mapsto u(m),\ t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0}\mapsto{\bf k}(m),\ t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{d}_{0}\mapsto{\bf d}(m),\ \mathrm{k}_{1}\mapsto\mathrm{k},\ \mathrm{d}_{1}\mapsto t\frac{d}{dt}

for
m\in\mathbb{Z},u\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Extend
U
to an
\mathfrak{f}
-module such that
{\bf d}
and
{\bf k}
act as the scalars
b
and
\ell
,
respectively. Following
[
CP
]
, we have the loop module
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U
for the affine Lie algebra
\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
with the actions given by

\displaystyle x(m)(t^{n}\otimes u)=t^{m+n}\otimes(xu),\quad t\frac{d}{dt}(t^{n}\otimes u)=(n+a)t^{n}\otimes u,\quad\mathrm{k}=0

for
x\in\mathfrak{f}
,
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
u\in U
.
Since
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(0)}^{1}

is isomorphism to
\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
, the loop
\widetilde{\mathfrak{f}}
-module
\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U

becomes a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{(0)}^{1}
-module, which is also denoted by

T_{U,\ell,a,b}
.

Now, as the second main result of this paper, we give a characterization of the irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-modules constructed in Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\ell,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
\ell\neq 0
and let
U
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Then the
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module

L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
is isomorphic to
the irreducible highest weight module
L_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}}(T_{\ell,U,\alpha,b})
, where
b=\frac{\beta+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell}{1-\epsilon}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}

if
\epsilon\neq 1
and
b=\beta-\mu\ell
if
\epsilon=1
.

Proof.

Write

W=L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L]).

It is clear that
W=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}W_{(k)}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
\widehat{\mathfrak{f}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module such that
W_{(0)}=U\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L]
and
W_{(k)}=0
for
k<0
.
For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

E^{n{\bf k}}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}E^{n{\bf k}}(m)z^{-m}.

Then we have
E^{n{\bf k}}(m)W_{(k)}\subset W_{(m+k)}
for
n,m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
This together with (
3.12
) implies that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(m)}W_{(k)}=W_{(m+k)}
for
m,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
That is,
W
is an irreducible
\mathbb{N}
-graded
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module. Thus,
W_{(0)}
is an irreducible

\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module and we need to prove that

W_{(0)}(=U\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])\quad\text{is isomorphic to}\quad T_{\ell,U,\alpha,b}(=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U)

as
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-modules, where
\ell,U,\alpha,b
are as given in the theorem.

It is straightforward to check that in this case the
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module actions on

T_{\ell,U,\alpha,b}
can be written in the following expressions:

(3.14)

\begin{split}&(t_{1}^{m}\otimes a)(t^{n}\otimes u)=t^{m+n}\otimes au,\quad(t_{1}^{m}\mathrm{k}_{0})(t^{n}\otimes u)=\ell t^{m+n}\otimes u,\\
&\mathrm{d}_{1}(t^{n}\otimes u)=(n+\alpha)t^{n}\otimes u,\quad\mathrm{k}_{1}(t^{n}\otimes u)=0,\\
&\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}_{0,k}(t^{n}\otimes u)=\left(k(\beta+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell)+(1-\epsilon)(\alpha+n+\frac{1}{2}k\epsilon)\right)t^{k+n}\otimes u,\\
&\mathrm{d}_{0}(t^{n}\otimes u)=(\mu\ell-\beta)(t^{n}\otimes u)\quad(\textnormal{{when }}\epsilon=1),\end{split}

for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times},\ a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
u\in U
.

Now we are ready to show that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module actions on
W_{(0)}
coincide with that of
T_{\ell,U,\alpha,b}
given in (
3.14
).
For
u\in U
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

u(r):=u\otimes e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}.

Then we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{k}_{1}(u(r))

\displaystyle={\bf k}(0)(u(r))=u\otimes{\bf k}(0)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}={\langle}{\bf k},(\alpha+r){\bf k}{\rangle}u\otimes e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=0,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{1}(u(r))

\displaystyle={\bf d}(0)(u(r))={\langle}{\bf d},(\alpha+r){\bf k}{\rangle}u(r)=(\alpha+r)u(r).

When
\epsilon=1
, we also have

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{0}(u(r))=

\displaystyle(-L(0)-\sum_{i\textless 1}{\bf k}_{i}{\bf d}_{-i}-\sum_{i\geq 1}{\bf d}_{-i}{\bf k}_{i}+\mu\ell)(u(r))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-L(0)+\mu\ell)(u(r))=(-\beta+\mu\ell)u(r).

On the other hand, for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle E^{m{\bf k}}(0)u(r)=u\otimes e^{m{\bf k}}e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=u(m+r).

In view of this, we have

(3.18)

\displaystyle(t_{1}^{n}\mathrm{k}_{0})(u(r))=

\displaystyle n\mathrm{k}_{0,n}(u(r))=\ell E^{n{\bf k}}(0)(u(r))=\ell(u(n+r)),

(3.19)

\displaystyle(t_{1}^{m}\otimes a)(u(r))=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}a(i)u\otimes E^{m{\bf k}}(-i)E^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}

\displaystyle=\,

\displaystyle a(0)u\otimes E^{m{\bf k}}(0)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=(au)(m+r),

for
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
,
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For the action of
\mathrm{d}_{0,n}^{\epsilon}

(n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times})
on
u(r)
, note
firstly that

\displaystyle[L_{\mathfrak{h}}(i),E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)]e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}{\bf k}(j)E^{n{\bf k}}(-j)\right)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=0\quad(i\in\mathbb{Z}),

as
{\bf k}(i)
commutes with
E^{n{\bf k}}(j)
for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and that

L_{\mathfrak{h}}(i)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}=0
for
i\geq 0.

Using these and (
3.3
) we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\ _{\circ}{}^{\circ}L^{\epsilon}_{\mathfrak{f}}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}\left(\sum_{i\leq 2\epsilon-2}L_{\mathfrak{f}}(i)z^{-i-1}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)+E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\sum_{i>2\epsilon-2}L_{\mathfrak{f}}(i)z^{-i-1}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon}{24}z^{-1}\mathrm{k}_{\mathsf{Vir}}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\right)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i\leq 2\epsilon-2}L_{\mathfrak{f}}(i)E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)+\sum_{i>2\epsilon-2}E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)L_{\mathfrak{f}}(i)+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell(E^{n{\bf k}})(0)\right)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(L_{\mathfrak{g}}(i)u\otimes E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}+u\otimes L_{\mathfrak{h}}(i)E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}\right)+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell(E^{n{\bf k}})(0)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle L_{\mathfrak{g}}(0)u\otimes E^{n{\bf k}}(0)e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}}+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell\,u(n+r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(\beta+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell)\,u(n+r).

Secondly, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\left(z^{2\epsilon-2}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\right)u(r)=(E^{n{\bf k}})(0)u(r)=u(n+r).

Thirdly, from the fact that
{\bf k}(i)
commutes with
E^{n{\bf k}}(j)
for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\left(z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z)\right)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\epsilon-i-1){\bf k}(i)z^{-i-1}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(\epsilon-i-1){\bf k}(i)E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)(u\otimes e^{(\alpha+r){\bf k}})=0.

Fourthly, using the relation
[{\bf d}(i),E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)]=nE^{n{\bf k}}(0)
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\left(z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}\right)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}\ u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}(\epsilon-1)_{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}\ u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(\epsilon-1)\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}\left(\sum_{i\leq\epsilon-1}{\bf d}(i)z^{-i-1}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)+\sum_{i\textgreater\epsilon-1}E^{n{\bf k}}(z){\bf d}(i)z^{-i-1}\right)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(\epsilon-1)\left(\sum_{i\leq\epsilon-1}{\bf d}(i)E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)+\sum_{i\textgreater\epsilon-1}E^{n{\bf k}}(-i){\bf d}(i)\right)u(r)

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(\epsilon-1)\sum_{0\leq i\leq\epsilon-1}{\bf d}(i)E^{n{\bf k}}(-i)u(r),&\textnormal{{if }}\ \epsilon\geq 1\\
(\epsilon-1)\sum_{\epsilon-1\textless i\leq 0}E^{n{\bf k}}(-i){\bf d}(i)u(r),&\textnormal{{if }}\ \epsilon<1\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle(\epsilon-1)(\alpha+r+n\epsilon)\,u(n+r).

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{0,n}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\mathrm{D}_{n}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{1-2\epsilon}\Big(n\ _{\circ}^{\circ}L_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\ _{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)

\displaystyle\ \ \ -z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+n^{2}(\mu-\frac{1}{\ell})z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)\Big).

Then by combining the above identities together, we obtain

(3.20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{0,n}^{\epsilon}(u(r))=(n(\beta+(\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon)\mu\ell)+(1-\epsilon)(\alpha+r+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon))(u(n+r)).

Finally, by comparing (
3.15
)-(
3.20
) with (
3.14
), we conclude that the map

T_{\ell,U,\alpha,b}=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}]\otimes U\rightarrow W_{(0)}=U\otimes e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L],\quad t^{r}\otimes u\mapsto u(r)\quad(r\in\mathbb{Z},u\in U)

is a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}_{(0)}
-module isomorphism.
This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.1
.

-coordinated

\displaystyle(z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z_{2})

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z_{1},z_{2}))),

(4.2)

\displaystyle(\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\left((z_{1}-z_{2})^{k}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z_{2})\right)|_{z_{1}=\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})}.

We denote by
\mathcal{D}
the canonical derivation on

for

.

[

]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For a

\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V
-module

,
we have

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}\left(\mathcal{D}v,z\right)=z\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}\left(v,z\right),\quad\forall v\in V.

The following Borcherds formula and Jacobi-type identity were obtained in
[

]

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon})
be a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated

(4.3)

\displaystyle[Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z),Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,w)]=\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{1}{j!}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{j}v,w)\left(w^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right)^{j}z^{\epsilon-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),

and

(4.4)

\begin{split}&(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{1}-z_{2}}{z_{2}z}\right)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z_{1})Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z_{2})-(z_{2}z)^{-1}\delta\left(\frac{z_{2}-z_{1}}{-z_{2}z}\right)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z_{2})Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z_{1})\\

where

\textnormal{{log}}(1+z),\ &\text{for }\ \epsilon=1.\end{cases}

As in the module case, we have the following result
whose proof is straightforward and is omitted (cf.
[

]

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be two vertex algebras,

(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon})
a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V
-module, and

(W^{\prime},Y_{W^{\prime}}^{\epsilon})

\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V^{\prime}
-module. Then

(W\otimes W^{\prime},Y_{W}^{\epsilon}\otimes Y_{W^{\prime}}^{\epsilon})

is a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated module the tensor product vertex algebra
V\otimes V^{\prime}
.

In what follows, for
u,v\in V
, we consider the expressions of
Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{0}v,z)
and
Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{-1}v,z)
in a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V
-module
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon})
be a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V
-module. Then for
u,v\in V
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{0}v,w)=[\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z),Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,w)].

Proof.

By taking
\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}
in the both sides of (
4.3
), we have

\displaystyle[\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z),Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,w)]=\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{0}v,w)z^{\epsilon-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{0}v,w),

noting that for

\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}\left(w^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\right)^{j}z^{\epsilon-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)=\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}\left(-z^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{j}z^{\epsilon-1}\delta\left(\frac{w}{z}\right)=0.

∎

Set
h_{0}=1
and

h_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\geq 0,\\
r_{1}+2r_{2}+\cdots+nr_{n}=n\end{subarray}}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{m=1}^{n}r_{m}}\left(\sum_{m=1}^{n}r_{m}\right)!\prod_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{m}!}\left(\frac{\epsilon^{(m)}}{(m+1)!}\right)^{r_{m}},\ \forall n\geq 1,

where

.
We have

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon})
be a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V
-module. Then for
u,v\in V
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{-1}v,z)=_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\frac{1}{i!}\epsilon^{(i)}h_{n-i+1}z^{(n+1)(\epsilon-1)}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{n}v,z).

Proof.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})-z_{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n!}z_{0}^{n}\left(z_{2}^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz_{2}}\right)^{n}z_{2}=z_{0}z_{2}^{\epsilon}h(z_{2},z_{0}),

where

Since

h(z_{2},z_{0})^{-1}

\displaystyle h(z_{2},z_{0})^{-1}=\left(1+\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{\epsilon^{(i)}}{(i+1)!}z_{2}^{i(\epsilon-1)}z_{0}^{i}\right)^{-1}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(-\frac{\epsilon^{(i)}}{(i+1)!}z_{2}^{i(\epsilon-1)}z_{0}^{i}\right)^{j}=\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}z_{2}^{n(\epsilon-1)}z_{0}^{n}.

Next we compute the residue
\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-1}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,f_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z))v,z_{2})

By substituting

by

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-1}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,f_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z))v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z_{0}}\left(\frac{\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{2}}-1\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{0}}\left(\frac{\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})}{z_{2}}-1\right)\right)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z_{0}}\left(\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})-z_{2}\right)^{-1}\phi_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z_{0})^{\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z_{0}}z_{0}^{-1}z_{2}^{-\epsilon}h(z_{2},z_{0})^{-1}e^{z_{0}\left(z_{2}^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)}\left(z_{2}^{\epsilon}\right)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y(u,z_{0})v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle\textnormal{{Res}}_{z_{0}}\sum_{n\geqslant-1}z_{0}^{-n-1}z_{0}^{-1}z_{2}^{-\epsilon}h(z_{2},z_{0})^{-1}e^{z_{0}\left(z_{2}^{\epsilon}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}\right)}\left(z_{2}^{\epsilon}\right)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{n}v,z_{2})

\displaystyle=\

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{-1}v,z_{2})+\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\frac{1}{i!}\epsilon^{(i)}h_{n-i+1}z_{2}^{(n+1)(\epsilon-1)}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u_{n}v,z_{2}).

On the other hand, by taking

\displaystyle\text{Res}_{z}z^{-1}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(Y\left(u,f_{\epsilon}(z_{2},z)\right)v,z_{2})

{}_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z_{2})Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,z_{2})_{\circ}^{\circ}.

]

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
is a restricted
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
-module of level
1
(with the action (
5.9
)).
Meanwhile, it is known that (see
[
LL
, (6.5.64)]
for example)

E^{\lambda}(z)E^{\gamma}(z)=E^{\lambda+\gamma}(z),\quad\frac{d}{dz}E^{\lambda}(z)=\lambda(z)E^{\lambda}(z)

for
\gamma,\lambda\in L
.
From this and the fact that
\left(e^{\gamma}\right)^{\epsilon}(z)=E^{\gamma}(z)
, one can conclude that
this
\widehat{\mathfrak{p}}
-module satisfies the conditions in (
5.5
), as desired.

In view of Proposition
5.4
, the above claim implies that the assignment (
5.9
) determines
a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module structure on
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
.
The irreducibility of this
V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module is obvious, and it remains to deduce the action
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
on
W
.

Note that in
V_{\mathfrak{h},L}
we have
{\bf k}_{n}{\bf d}=0
for
n\geq 0
unless
n=1
, in which case
{\bf k}_{1}{\bf d}={\bm{1}}
.
Then from Remark
4.7
we have

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{\mathfrak{h}},z)

\displaystyle=Y^{\epsilon}({\bf k}_{-1}{\bf d},z)

\displaystyle=_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{5\epsilon-4}{12}\epsilon z^{2(\epsilon-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z){\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{5\epsilon-4}{12}\epsilon z^{2(\epsilon-1)}.

Recall from (
3.8
) that
L_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)=\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z){\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}
, and from (
3.3
) that

L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\epsilon}(z)=z^{2\epsilon}L_{\mathfrak{h}}(z)+\frac{\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon}{12}z^{2(\epsilon-1)}=\frac{1}{\ell}z^{2\epsilon}\ _{\circ}{}^{\circ}{\bf k}(z){\bf d}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon}{12}z^{2(\epsilon-1)}.

By the definition of normally ordered products, in the
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z){\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{n<\epsilon}{\bf k}(n)z^{\epsilon-n-1}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)+{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)\sum_{n\geq\epsilon}{\bf k}(n)z^{\epsilon-n-1}

\displaystyle=z^{2\epsilon}\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{n<\epsilon}{\bf k}(n)z^{-n-1}{\bf d}(z)+{\bf d}(z)\sum_{n\geq\epsilon}{\bf k}(n)z^{-n-1}.

This implies that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z){\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-z^{2\epsilon}\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}(z){\bf d}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}\\
=\,&\begin{cases}z^{2\epsilon-2}\cdot\sum_{0\leq n<\epsilon}\frac{1}{\ell}[{\bf k}(n),{\bf d}(-n)],\quad&\textnormal{{if}}\ \ \epsilon>0\\
0,\quad&\textnormal{{if}}\ \ \epsilon=0\\
z^{2\epsilon-2}\cdot\sum_{\epsilon\leq n<0}\frac{1}{\ell}[{\bf d}(-n),{\bf k}(n)],\quad&\textnormal{{if}}\ \ \epsilon<0\end{cases}\\
=\,&z^{2\epsilon-2}\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(\epsilon-1).\end{split}

Summarizing the above relations we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{\mathfrak{h}},z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z){\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{5\epsilon-4}{12}\epsilon z^{2(\epsilon-1)}

\displaystyle=z^{2\epsilon}\frac{1}{\ell}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf k}(z){\bf d}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+z^{2\epsilon-2}\frac{1}{2}\epsilon(\epsilon-1)-\frac{5\epsilon-4}{12}\epsilon z^{2(\epsilon-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}z^{2\epsilon}\ _{\circ}{}^{\circ}{\bf k}(z){\bf d}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{\epsilon^{2}-2\epsilon}{12}z^{2(\epsilon-1)}=L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\epsilon}(z).

This finishes the proof.
∎

5.2.
Proof of theorem
3.2

This section is devoted to a proof of Theorem
3.2
.

By taking the tensor product of the latter two vertex algebras considered in last subsection, for every nonzero complex number
\ell
, we have a conformal vertex algebra

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)},

which has a conformal vector

\omega=\omega_{\mathfrak{g}}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}.

We will often view
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)
and
V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
as subalgebras of

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
in the canonical way.
The following result was obtained in
[
CLiT
, Corollary 5.9]
:

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Then there exists a vertex algebra epimorphism

\Theta:V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}}(\gamma_{\ell})\rightarrow V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)},

which is uniquely determined by

(5.10)

\displaystyle\Theta(t_{1}^{m}\otimes u)=u\otimes e^{m{\bf k}},\quad\Theta(\mathrm{k}_{1})=\ell{\bf k},\quad\Theta(\mathrm{d}_{1})={\bf d},\quad\Theta(\mathrm{K}_{n})=\frac{\ell}{n}e^{n{\bf k}},

(5.11)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\Theta(\mathrm{D}_{n})=n\omega_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}}-\omega_{0}{\bf d}_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}}+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1){\bf k}_{-2}e^{n{\bf k}}

for
u\in\mathfrak{g},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
.

The following result asserts that every (irreducible)
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module admits
an (irreducible)
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module structure.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\cdot,z))
be a

\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module with
\ell\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
.
Then there is a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module structure on
W
with the actions given by

(5.12)

\displaystyle t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z),\quad\mathrm{k}_{0}=\ell,\quad(t_{1}^{m}\otimes u)^{\epsilon}(z)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u\otimes e^{m{\bf k}},z),

(5.13)

\displaystyle\mathrm{k}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\ell Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z),\quad\mathrm{d}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z),\quad\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\epsilon}(z)=\frac{\ell}{n}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z),

(5.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{n}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=n_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1)z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)

for
u\in\mathfrak{g},\ m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}
.
Furthermore, if the
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\cdot,z))
is irreducible, then
W
is also irreducible as a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module.

Proof.

Let
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\cdot,z))
be a

\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module with
\ell\neq 0
.
For every
v\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}}(\gamma_{\ell})
, set

Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[v,z]:=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\Theta(v),z)\in\mathrm{Hom}(W,W((z))).

From Proposition
5.6
, it follows that
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[\cdot,z])

is a
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}}(\gamma_{\ell})
-module.
Thus, by applying Proposition
5.1
,
W
becomes a restricted
\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu,\epsilon}
-module of level
\ell
with

a^{\epsilon}(z)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[a,z],\quad\forall a\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}.

Assume further that
(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\cdot,z))
is irreducible. Then the
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{0}}(\gamma_{\ell})
-module

(W,Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[\cdot,z])
is also irreducible as the map
\Theta
is surjective.
This together with Proposition
5.1
gives that as a
\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module,
W
is still irreducible.

For every
v\in V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
,
it follows from Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.2
that

\displaystyle[\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z),Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,w)]=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{0}v,w)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\mathcal{D}v,w)=w^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dw}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(v,w).

In particular, for
a\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

[-\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z),Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[a,w]]=-w^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dw}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[a,w].

This together with Proposition
2.3
(12) implies that the
\widehat{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module
W
can be extended to a
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module with

t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0}=-\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z).

Thus it remains to prove that the operators
Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[a,z]
,
a\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
have the form as given in theorem.
The case that
a\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}}\mathbb{C}\mathrm{K}_{n}
is obvious, and so we only need to prove that the operators

Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[\mathrm{D}_{n},z]
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}

coincide with the right hand side of (
5.14
).

Indeed, note that for
i\geq 0
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

{\bf d}_{i}e^{n{\bf k}}=n\delta_{i,0}e^{n{\bf k}},\quad{\bf k}_{i}e^{n{\bf k}}=0.

This implies that for
j>0
,
(\omega_{\mathfrak{h}})_{j}e^{n{\bf k}}=({\bf k}_{-1}{\bf d})_{j}e^{n{\bf k}}=0

and hence
\omega_{j}e^{n{\bf k}}=0
. Thus, by Remark
4.7

and Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}z^{\epsilon-1}Y_{W}(\omega_{0}e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}z^{2\epsilon-1}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}(e^{n{\bf k}},z).

Similarly, from Remark
4.7
and Lemma
4.2
,
it follows that

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}\left(\omega_{0}({\bf d}_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}}),z\right)

\displaystyle\ =z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d}_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle\ =z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon z^{\epsilon-1}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(ne^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle\ =z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)-\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon z^{2\epsilon-1}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z),

and that

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k}_{-2}e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}((\omega_{0}{\bf k})_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}},z)=_{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{0}{\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle=z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z),

where we used the fact that
(\omega_{0}{\bf k})_{i}e^{n{\bf k}}
for
i\geq 0

as
{\bf k}_{i}e^{n{\bf k}}=0
.

Finally, by summarizing the above together, we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}[\mathrm{D}_{n},z]

\displaystyle=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\Theta(\mathrm{D}_{n}),z)

\displaystyle=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(n\omega_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}}-\omega_{0}{\bf d}_{-1}e^{n{\bf k}}+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1){\bf k}_{-2}e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=n\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon z^{2\epsilon-1}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle\ -z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon z^{2\epsilon-1}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1)z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=n\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1)z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z),

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.
Now we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
3.2
.
Let
U,\ell,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
be as in Theorem
3.2
.
By Proposition
5.2
, the irreducible highest weight
\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}
-module

W_{1}:=L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)

is naturally an irreducible
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)
-module.
Meanwhile, by Proposition
5.5
, the
\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}
-module

W_{2}:=V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])

is naturally
an irreducible
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated

V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module.
Thus, by taking tensor product (see Lemma
4.4
) we obtain an irreducible
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated

V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module

W:=W_{1}\otimes W_{2}=L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2,U,\beta)\otimes V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\ell,e^{\alpha{\bf k}}\mathbb{C}[L])

with the actions given by

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u,z)=Y_{W_{1}}^{\epsilon}(u,z)\otimes 1=u^{\epsilon}(z)\otimes 1=u^{\epsilon}(z),

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)=1\otimes Y_{W_{2}}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)=1\otimes\frac{1}{\ell}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z)=\frac{1}{\ell}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z),

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)=1\otimes Y_{W_{2}}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)=1\otimes{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)={\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z),

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)=1\otimes Y_{W_{2}}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)=1\otimes E^{n{\bf k}}(z)=E^{n{\bf k}}(z),

\displaystyle Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)=Y_{W_{1}}^{\epsilon}(\omega_{\mathfrak{g}},z)\otimes 1+1\otimes Y^{\epsilon}_{W_{2}}(\omega_{\mathfrak{h}},z)=L_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\epsilon}(z)\otimes 1+1\otimes L_{\mathfrak{h}}^{\epsilon}(z)=L_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\epsilon}(z),

for
u\in\mathfrak{g},\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where we have used the actions
(
5.3
), (
5.7
), the fact (
5.8
), and the conventions (
3.9
), (
3.11
).

According to Theorem
5.7
, the
\phi_{\epsilon}
-coordinated
V_{\widehat{\mathfrak{g}}\rtimes\mathsf{Vir}}(\ell,24\mu\ell-2)\otimes V_{(\mathfrak{h},L)}
-module

W

is also an irreducible
\widetilde{\mathfrak{t}}(\mathfrak{g})^{\epsilon}
-module with
\mathrm{k}_{0}=\ell
and

\displaystyle t_{0}^{\epsilon-1}\mathrm{d}_{0}

\displaystyle=-\textnormal{{Res}}_{z}z^{-\epsilon}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)=-L_{\mathfrak{f}}(\epsilon-1)+\delta_{\epsilon,1}\mu\ell,

\displaystyle(t_{1}^{m}\otimes u)^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(u\otimes e^{m{\bf k}},z)=(Y_{W_{1}}^{\epsilon}(u,z)\otimes 1)(1\otimes Y_{W_{2}}^{\epsilon}(e^{m{\bf k}},z))=u^{\epsilon}(z)E^{m{\bf k}}(z),

\displaystyle\mathrm{k}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\ell Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)={\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z),\quad\mathrm{d}_{1}^{\epsilon}(z)=Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)={\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z),

\displaystyle\mathrm{K}_{n}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell}{n}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)=\frac{\ell}{n}E^{n{\bf k}}(z),

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{n}^{\epsilon}(z)

\displaystyle=n\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(\omega,z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}-z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf d},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)+n^{2}(\mu\ell-1)z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}Y_{W}^{\epsilon}({\bf k},z)Y_{W}^{\epsilon}(e^{n{\bf k}},z)

\displaystyle=n\ _{\circ}^{\circ}L_{\mathfrak{f}}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}+\frac{1}{2}n\epsilon(\epsilon-1)z^{2\epsilon-2}E^{n{\bf k}}(z)-z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}\ _{\circ}^{\circ}{\bf d}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z)_{\circ}^{\circ}

\displaystyle\quad+n^{2}(\mu-\frac{1}{\ell})z^{\epsilon}\frac{d}{dz}{\bf k}^{\epsilon}(z)E^{n{\bf k}}(z).

This finishes the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

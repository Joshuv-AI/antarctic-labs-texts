Euler scheme for approximation of solution of nonlinear ODEs under inexact information
Natalia Czyżewska, Paweł M. Morkisz, Paweł Przybyłowicz

Abstract
. We investigate error of the Euler scheme in the case when the right-hand side function of the underlying ODE satisfies nonstandard assumptions such as local one-sided Lipschitz condition and local Hölder continuity. Moreover, we assume two cases in regards to information availability: exact and noisy with respect to the right-hand side function. Optimality analysis of the Euler scheme is also provided. Finally, we present the results of some numerical experiments. Mathematics Subject Classification: 65L05, 65L70

Introduction

In this paper we investigate error of the Euler scheme for the following initial-value problems

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{\prime}(t)=g(t,z(t)),&t\in[a,b],\\
z(a)=\xi,\end{array}\right.

where
-\infty<a<b<+\infty
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a right-hand side function
g:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Comparing to the classical assumptions such as global Lipschitz condition we assume that the right-hand side function
g
is continuous and of at most linear growth, but it satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition together with the Hölder condition only locally.

The numerical approximation of solutions of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) has drawn the attention of numerous scientists
[

,

]
. Furthermore, solving ODE problems with noisy information was studied in
[

,

,

,

]
. Also, approximation of the solutions of partial differential equations or stochastic differential equations with both exact and inexact information was a subject of extensive study,
[

,

,

]
and
[

respectively.
Nevertheless, when it comes to the nonstandard assumptions about the right-hand side function of the initial-value problem,
only the existence and uniqueness of the solutions were examined, see
[

]
. There is a gap in the literature about numerical methods for such cases under nonstandard assumptions.

If we take a closer look at the computation on a digital computer, we quickly conclude that the computer uses only a finite set of numbers because of the finite memory. So all computer calculations are burdened with errors, e.g., from rounding.
Also, the novel CPUs and GPUs have an option to speed up the computations when there is lower precision of the processed numbers. From the mathematical point of view, lowering the precision can be interpreted as adding noisy information with the control for the relative error of the noise.
Hence, all the applied numerical methods should have a strict error analysis with noisy information setting.

In this paper, we deal with nonstandard assumptions about the right-hand side function
g
, and we allow both exact and inexact information about the initial value
\xi
and values of the right-hand side function
g
. We changed a bit of assumptions from the auxiliary ordinary differential equation in
[
7
]
where we assume locally
Hölder continuity and global one-sided Lipschitz conditions. Now we locally consider
Hölder continuity and one-sided Lipschitz condition only in a certain neighborhood.
This weakening of the assumptions was caused by the necessity that came from a phase change model of metallic materials
[
6
,
7
]
.

According to our best knowledge, there is a lack of literature on numerical methods of initial-value problems under nonstandard assumptions with noisy information. We focus on the most basic explicit Euler scheme. We believe that this paper might be helpful for further research on implicit numerical methods, higher-order methods, or even numerical methods for delay differential equations with noisy information under nonstandard assumptions on the right-hand side function.

To do so, we start with an error analysis for the classical explicit Euler scheme under nonstandard assumptions for the right-hand side function
g
mentioned before, i.e., local Hölder continuity and one-sided Lipschitz condition (Theorem
3.1
). Next, we introduce a corruption for the right-hand side function and then we perform an error analysis for the Euler scheme (Theorem
4.1

5.1
and Proposition
5.2
respectively). Consequently, we can provide a result concerning the optimality of the Euler scheme in a particular case (Theorem
5.3
). The final contribution is performing numerical experiments to confirm our theoretical results.

The paper is organized as follows. The problem posing with the nonstandard assumptions is given in Section

2.
Preliminaries

For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we take
\displaystyle{\langle x,y\rangle=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}y_{k}}
and
\|x\|=\langle x,x\rangle^{1/2}
. For
R\in[0,+\infty]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
B_{x}(R)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\ |\ \|y-x\|\leq R\}
. Of course
B_{x}(+\infty)=\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Moreover, for
H\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
H^{+}=\max\{0,H\}
. Additionally, by
U\in\textrm{comp}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we mean that
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact set.

We assume that the right-hand side function
g:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the following conditions:

(G1)

g\in C([a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(G2)

There exists
K\in(0,+\infty)
such that for all
(t,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\|g(t,y)\|\leq K(1+\|y\|).

(G3)

For every
U\in\textrm{comp}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists
H_{U}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\in[a,b]
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in U

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},g(t,y_{1})-g(t,y_{2})\rangle\leq H_{U}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}.

(G4)

There exist
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
such that for every
U\in\textrm{comp}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
there exists
L_{U}\in(0,\infty)
such that for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[a,b]
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in U

\|g(t_{1},y_{1})-g(t_{2},y_{2})\|\leq L_{U}\Bigl(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}+\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{\beta}\Bigr).

The assumption (G2) is the classical global linear growth condition. However, instead of a global Lipschitz condition, we assume that the right-hand side function
g
locally satisfies one-sided Lipschiz assumption (G3).
It follows from Lemma
7.2
that under the assumptions (G1)-(G3) the equation (
1
) has a unique solution
z\in C^{1}([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The additional assumption (G4), called the
(\alpha,\beta)
-local Hölder condition, is needed to obtain an upper error bound for the Euler scheme that depends on
(\alpha,\beta)
. We stress that under the assumptions (G1)-(G4) the right-hand side function might be highly nonlinear, see, for example, Section
6
.

In the next section, we investigate the error of the classical Euler scheme under such nonstandard assumptions. Furthermore, we consider the cases where exact and inexact information about the right-hand side function
g
is available.

3.
Euler scheme under exact information

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
t_{k}=a+kh
for
k=0,1,\dots,n
where
h=\frac{b-a}{n}
. The Euler scheme is defined as follows

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}y_{0}=\xi,\\
y_{k+1}=y_{k}+h\cdot g(t_{k},y_{k})\quad\hbox{for}&k=0,1,\dots,n-1,\end{array}\right.

The approximation of
z=z(t)
in
[a,b]
is given by a piecewise linear function
l_{n}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined in the following way

l_{n}(t)=l_{k,n}(t)\ \hbox{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\ k=0,1,\ldots,n-1,

(3.4)

where

l_{k,n}(t)=y_{k}+(t-t_{k})\cdot g(t_{k},y_{k}).

(3.5)

The aim of this section is to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let us assume that
g
satisfies (G1)-(G4). Then there exists
\bar{C}_{1}\in[0,\infty)
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-l_{n}(t)\|\leq\bar{C}_{1}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}).

(3.6)

Proof.

By the assumption (G2) we have that for all
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1

\|y_{k+1}\|\leq\|y_{k}\|+h\|g(t_{k},y_{k})\|\leq(1+hK)\|y_{k}\|+hK.

Hence, by the discrete version of Gronwall’s lemma we get that for all
k=0,1,\ldots,n
that

\|y_{k}\|\leq(1+Kh)^{k}\|\xi\|+(1+Kh)^{k}-1\leq e^{K(b-a)}(1+\|\xi\|),

(3.7)

and therefore

\max_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\max_{0\leq k\leq n}\|y_{k}\|\leq C_{1}(1+\|\xi\|),

(3.8)

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(a,b,K)=e^{K(b-a)}
. Note that
C_{1}\leq C_{0}
, where
C_{0}
is defined in Lemma
7.2
. Now for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
we consider the following local ODE

z_{k}^{\prime}(t)=g(t,z_{k}(t)),\quad t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\quad z_{k}(t_{k})=y_{k}.

(3.9)

By Lemma
7.2
there exists a unique solution
z_{k}:[t_{k},t_{k+1}]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
of (
3.9
). From the assumption (G2) and by (
3.8
) we get for all
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
that

\|z_{k}(t)\|\leq e^{K(b-a)}(1+\|\xi\|)+K(b-a)+K\int\limits_{t_{k}}^{t}\|z_{k}(s)\|ds,

(3.10)

and, by the Gronwall’s lemma,

\max_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\max_{k=0,1,\dots,n-1}\sup_{t_{k}\leq t\leq t_{k+1}}\|z_{k}(t)\|\leq C_{2}(\|\xi\|+1),

(3.11)

where
C_{2}=C_{2}(a,b,K)=e^{K(b-a)}[e^{K(b-a)}+K(b-a)]
. We have
C_{0}\leq C_{2}
. Moreover, by (G2) and (
3.11
) we have for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1

\|z_{k}(t)-y_{k}\|\leq hK\Bigl(1+\sup\limits_{t_{k}\leq t\leq t_{k+1}}\|z_{k}(t)\|\Bigr)\leq hC_{3}(1+\|\xi\|),

(3.12)

where
C_{3}=C_{3}(a,b,K)=K(1+C_{2})=K(1+e^{K(b-a)}[e^{K(b-a)}+K(b-a)])
. Therefore, by (
3.8
), (
3.11
), (
7.1
) we have

y_{k},z_{k}(t),z(t)\in B_{0}(C_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))

(3.13)

for all
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This and (G4) imply that for
U=B_{0}(C_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))
there exists
L_{1}\in(0,+\infty)
such that for all
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\|z_{k}(t_{k+1})-y_{k+1}\|\leq\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\|g(s,z_{k}(s))-g(t_{k},y_{k})\|ds

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}\int_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}(|s-t_{k}|^{\alpha}+\|z_{k}(s)-y_{k}\|^{\beta})ds\leq L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)h(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}),

(3.14)

where
C_{4}=C_{4}(a,b,K,\beta)=\max\{1,2C_{3}^{\beta}\}
. (We have also used the fact that
(1+x)^{\beta}\leq 2+x\leq 2(1+x)
for all
x\geq 0
,
\beta\in(0,1]
.) Furthermore, for
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
we have

\|z(t_{k+1})-y_{k+1}\|\leq\|z(t_{k+1})-z_{k}(t_{k+1})\|+\|z_{k}(t_{k+1})-y_{k+1}\|,

(3.15)

and for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
it holds that

\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|^{2}=2\langle z(t)-z_{k}(t),g(t,z(t))-g(t,z_{k}(t))\rangle.

(3.16)

Therefore, by (G3) applied to
U=B_{0}(C_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))
we receive that there exists
H_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{d}{dt}\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|^{2}\leq 2H_{1}\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|^{2}\\
\leq 2H_{1}^{+}\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|^{2}

for all
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
z_{k}(t_{k})=y_{k}
, we get

\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|^{2}\leq\|z(t_{k})-y_{k}\|^{2}+2H^{+}_{1}\int\limits_{t_{k}}^{t}\|z(s)-z_{k}(s)\|^{2}ds,

(3.17)

for
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
and, by the Gronwall’s lemma,

\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|\leq e^{H_{1}^{+}(t-t_{k})}\|z(t_{k})-y_{k}\|.

(3.18)

Combining (
3
), (
3.15
), (
3.18
) we receive for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1

\|z(t_{k+1})-y_{k+1}\|\leq e^{H_{1}^{+}h}\|z(t_{k})-y_{k}\|+L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)h(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}).

(3.19)

Denoting by
e_{k}=z(t_{k})-y_{k}
we arrive at

\|e_{k+1}\|\leq e^{H_{1}^{+}h}\|e_{k}\|+L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)h(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}),

with
e_{0}=z(a)-y_{0}=0
.
If
H_{1}^{+}>0
then by Gronwall’s lemma we obtain

\|e_{k}\|\leq\frac{e^{H_{1}^{+}(b-a)}-1}{H_{1}^{+}}L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}),

(3.20)

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
, where
C_{4}=C_{4}(a,b,K,\beta)
. In the particular case, when
H_{1}^{+}=0
we simply have that for
k=0,1,\ldots,n

\|e_{k}\|\leq kL_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta})\leq(b-a)L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}).

(3.21)

For
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n
we have by (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle\|z(t)-l_{n}(t)\|=\|z(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|\leq\|z(t)-z_{k}(t)\|+\|z_{k}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|

\displaystyle\leq e^{H_{1}^{+}h}\|e_{k}\|+\|z_{k}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|,

(3.22)

where by (
3
)

\|z_{k}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|\leq\int\limits_{t_{k}}^{t_{k+1}}\|g(s,z_{k}(s))-g(t_{k},y_{k})\|ds\leq L_{1}C_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)h(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}).

(3.23)

Combining (
3
), (
3.23
), (
3.20
), (
3.21
) we get the thesis.
∎

4.
Euler scheme under noisy information

In this section we show the upper bound for the error of the Euler scheme in the case of inexact information. Namely, we consider the corrupted right-hand side functions of the following form

\tilde{g}(t,y)=g(t,y)+\delta_{g}(t,y),\quad(t,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(4.1)

where
\delta_{g}:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
corrupting function
that is Borel measurable and

\|\delta_{g}(t,y)\|\leq\delta(1+\|y\|)

(4.2)

for all
(t,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta\in[0,1]
. We refer to
\delta
as to
precision parameter
.

In the case of inexact evaluations of
g
, the Euler scheme has the following form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{y}_{0}=\tilde{\xi},\\
\tilde{y}_{k+1}=\tilde{y}_{k}+h\cdot\tilde{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}),&k=0,1,\dots,n-1,\end{array}\right.

where
\tilde{\xi}\in B_{\xi}(\delta)
. The approximation of
z=z(t)
in
[a,b]
is given by a piecewise linear function
\tilde{l}_{n}:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, defined as

\tilde{l}_{n}(t)=\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)\ \hbox{for}\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\ k=0,1,\ldots,n-1,

(4.6)

where

\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)=\tilde{y}_{k}+(t-t_{k})\cdot\tilde{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}).

(4.7)

Of course when the information is exact (i.e.,
\delta=0
) then
\tilde{y}_{k}=y_{k}
for
k=0,1,\ldots,n
, and
l_{n}\equiv\tilde{l}_{n}
. Moreover, the Euler algorithm
\tilde{l}_{n}
uses
n
(noisy) evaluations of
\tilde{g}
.

In the theorem below we show the upper error bound for the Euler scheme in the case of inexact information about the function
g
. We stress that we have different bounds in the local Lipschitz case (
\beta=1
) and in the local Hölder case (
\beta\in(0,1)
). This is due to the fact that locally Lipschitz continuous function (i.e.
\beta=1
in (G4)) satisfies also locally one-sided Lipschitz condition (G3). This is not the case when
g
is only locally Hölder continuous. We stress that the condition (G3) is crucial for obtaining the upper error bound in the case when
\beta\in(0,1)
, see also Remark
4.2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
g
satisfy (G1)-(G4), and let
\delta_{g}
be a Borel measurable corrupting function satisfying (
4.2
). Then there exists
\bar{C}_{2}\in[0,\infty)
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq\lceil 2(b-a)\rceil+1
,
\delta\in[0,1]
,
\tilde{\xi}\in B_{\xi}(\delta)
the following holds:

•

if
\beta=1
then

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq\bar{C}_{2}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+\delta),

(4.8)

•

if
\beta\in(0,1)
then

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq\bar{C}_{2}(1+\|\xi\|)\Bigl(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}+\mathbf{1}_{(0,1]}(\delta)(h^{1/2}+\delta)\Bigr).

(4.9)

Proof.

By (G2) we have for all
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
that

\|\tilde{y}_{k+1}\|\leq\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|+h(K+1)(1+\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|)\\
\leq(1+h(K+1))\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|+h(K+1).

Therefore, by a discrete version of Gronwall’s lemma
we get for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]
that

\displaystyle\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|\leq(1+h(K+1))^{k}\|\tilde{y}_{0}\|+(1+h(K+1))^{k}-1

\displaystyle\leq e^{(b-a)(K+1)}(\|\tilde{\xi}\|+1)\leq K_{2}(1+\|\xi\|)

(4.10)

where
K_{2}=K_{2}(a,b,K)=2e^{(b-a)(K+1)}
. Since
K_{2}\geq C_{1}
, by (
3.8
),(
4
) we have

y_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}\in B_{0}(K_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))

(4.11)

for all
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]
.
Let us denote
e_{k}=\tilde{y}_{k}-y_{k}
for
k=0,1,\dots,n
. Then we have that
\|e_{0}\|=\|\tilde{y}_{0}-y_{0}\|=\|\tilde{\xi}-\xi\|\leq\delta
and for
k=0,1,\dots,n-1

e_{k+1}=e_{k}+h\left[g(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-g(t_{k},y_{k})\right]+h\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k}).

(4.12)

We consider two cases, when
\beta=1
and
\beta\in(0,1)
.

If
\underline{\beta=1}
then (G4) implies that for
U=B_{0}(K_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))
there exist
L_{2}\in(0,+\infty)
such that for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\|e_{k+1}\|\leq(1+hL_{2})\|e_{k}\|+h\delta(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2}).

(4.13)

By the discrete version of the Gronwall’s lemma we get for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq n}\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}_{k}\|\leq(1+\|\xi\|)\Bigl(e^{L_{2}(b-a)}+\frac{e^{L_{2}(b-a)}-1}{L_{2}}(1+K_{2})\Bigr)\delta.

(4.14)

Hence, by Theorem
3.1
we get that there exists
\tilde{C}_{1}\in[0,+\infty)
such that for all
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}(1+\|\xi\|)h^{\alpha}+\|\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|,

(4.15)

and, by (
4.14
),

\displaystyle\|\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|\leq(1+hL_{2})\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}_{k}\|+h\delta(1+\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{1}(1+\|\xi\|)\delta+h\delta(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2}).

(4.16)

From (
4.15
), (
4.16
) we get (
4.8
).

If
\underline{\beta\in(0,1)}
then we have to proceed in a different way, see also Remark
4.2
. From (
4.12
) we have for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
that

\|e_{k+1}-h\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\|^{2}=\|e_{k}+h\left[g(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-g(t_{k},y_{k})\right]\|^{2}.

(4.17)

We have

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}-h\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\|^{2}=\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}-2h\langle e_{k+1},\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\rangle+h^{2}\|\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}-2h\langle e_{k+1},\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\rangle.

(4.18)

On the other hand, (
4.11
) and (G3), (G4) imply that for
U=B_{0}(K_{2}(1+\|\xi\|))
there exist
L_{2}\in(0,+\infty)
,
H_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
such that for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\|e_{k}+h\left[g(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-g(t_{k},y_{k})\right]\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|e_{k}\|^{2}+2h\left\langle e_{k},g(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-g(t_{k},y_{k})\right\rangle+h^{2}\|g(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})-g(t_{k},y_{k})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|e_{k}\|^{2}+2hH_{2}^{+}\|e_{k}\|^{2}+h^{2}L_{2}^{2}\|e_{k}\|^{2\beta}\leq(1+2hH_{2}^{+})\|e_{k}\|^{2}+h^{2}L_{2}^{2}(1+\|e_{k}\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq(1+K_{3}h)\|e_{k}\|^{2}+h^{2}L^{2},

(4.19)

where
K_{3}=(2H_{2}^{+}+L_{2}^{2})
. (We have also used the fact that
x^{2\beta}=(x^{2})^{\beta}\leq 1+x^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
\beta\in(0,1]
.) Combining (
4.17
), (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) we get

\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(1+K_{3}h)\|e_{k}\|^{2}+h^{2}L_{2}^{2}+2h\langle e_{k+1},\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\rangle.

(4.20)

Moreover,

\langle e_{k+1},\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\rangle\leq\|e_{k+1}\|\cdot\|\delta_{g}(t_{k},\tilde{y}_{k})\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}+\delta^{2}(1+K_{2}^{2})(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}.

(4.21)

Combining (
4.20
) with (
4.21
) we end up with

\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(1+K_{3}h)\|e_{k}\|^{2}+h^{2}L_{2}^{2}+h\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}+2h\delta^{2}(1+K_{2}^{2})(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}.

(4.22)

Since
h\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, we have
0<\frac{1}{1-h}\leq 1+2h\leq 2
and for all
k=0,1,\dots,n-1

\displaystyle\|e_{k+1}\|^{2}\leq(1+2h)(1+K_{3}h)\|e_{k}\|^{2}+K_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}h(h+\delta^{2})

\displaystyle\leq(1+K_{5}h)\|e_{k}\|^{2}+K_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}h(h+\delta^{2}),

(4.23)

where
K_{4}=2\max\{L_{2}^{2},1+K_{2}^{2}\}
,
K_{5}=2+3K_{3}>0
. By the discrete version of the Gronwall’s lemma we obtain for all
k=0,1,\dots,n

\displaystyle\|e_{k}\|^{2}\leq(1+K_{5}h)^{k}\|e_{0}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+K_{5}h)^{k}-1}{K_{5}}K_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}(h+\delta^{2})

\displaystyle\leq e^{K_{5}(b-a)}\delta^{2}+\frac{e^{K_{5}(b-a)}-1}{K_{5}}K_{4}(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}(h+\delta^{2})

\displaystyle\leq K_{6}(1+\|\xi\|)^{2}(h+\delta^{2}),

(4.24)

where
K_{6}=2\max\{e^{K_{5}(b-a)},\frac{e^{K_{5}(b-a)}-1}{K_{5}}K_{4}\}
. Therefore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\max_{k=0,1,\dots,n}\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}_{k}\|\leq K_{7}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta),

(4.25)

with
K_{7}=K_{6}^{1/2}
. Again, by Theorem
3.1
we get that there exists
\tilde{C}_{2},\tilde{C}_{3}\in[0,+\infty)
such that for all
t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,
k=0,1,\ldots,n
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta\in[0,1]

\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq\bar{C}_{1}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta})+\|\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|,

(4.26)

and, by (
4.25
),

\displaystyle\|\tilde{l}_{k,n}(t)-l_{k,n}(t)\|\leq\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}_{k}\|+hL_{2}\|y_{k}-\tilde{y}_{k}\|^{\beta}+h\delta(1+\|\tilde{y}_{k}\|)

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{2}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{1/2}+\delta+h^{1+\frac{\beta}{2}}+h\delta^{\beta})+h\delta(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{3}(1+\|\xi\|)(h^{1/2}+\delta).

(4.27)

Finally, from (
4.26
), (
4
) we get (
4.9
).
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that if
\beta\in(0,1)
then we can only get that

\|e_{k+1}\|\leq\|e_{k}\|+hL_{2}\|e_{k}\|^{\beta}+h\delta(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2})

(4.28)

for
k=0,1,\ldots,n-1
. Since
\|e_{k}\|^{\beta}\leq 1+\|e_{k}\|
, it holds

\|e_{k+1}\|\leq(1+hL_{2})\|e_{k}\|+hL_{2}+h\delta(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2}),

(4.29)

which in turns implies that

\max\limits_{0\leq k\leq n}\|e_{k}\|\leq e^{L_{2}(b-a)}\delta+\frac{e^{L_{2}(b-a)}-1}{L_{2}}(L_{2}+\delta)(1+\|\xi\|)(1+K_{2})=O(1),

(4.30)

as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. This estimate is obviously too rough.

5.
Lower bounds and optimality of the Euler scheme in the worst-case setting

In this section we provide optimality analysis of the Euler algorithm in the case of inexact information about the right-hand side function. We settle our problem in the worst-case model of error (see
[
34
]
), which, in particular, allows us to investigate lower error bounds.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
,
K,L\in(0,+\infty)
,
H\in\mathbb{R}
,
R\in[0,+\infty]
. We consider the class
F_{R}^{\alpha,\beta}=F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R}(a,b,d,K,H,L)
of pairs
(\xi,g)
that satisfy the following conditions:

(A0)

\|\xi\|\leq K
,

(A1)

g\in C([a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(A2)

\|g(t,y)\|\leq K(1+\|y\|)
for all
(t,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(A3)

\langle y_{1}-y_{2},g(t,y_{1})-g(t,y_{2})\rangle\leq H\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2}
for all
t\in[a,b]
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{0}(R)
,

(A4)

\|g(t_{1},y_{1})-g(t_{2},y_{2})\|\leq L\Bigl(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha}+\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{\beta}\Bigr)
for all
t_{1},t_{2}\in[a,b]
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in B_{0}(R)
.

We refer to
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
,
-\infty<a<b<+\infty
,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
K,L\in[0,\infty)
,
H\in\mathbb{R}
,
R\in[0,+\infty]
as to the
parameters of the class
. Except for
a,b,d
the parameters are not known and will not be used by the defined algorithms.

The class
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{\infty}
consists of functions
g
that satisfy conditions (A3), (A4) globally on the whole domain
[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, while functions from
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{0}
might not satisfy the one-sided Lipschitz condition and the
(\alpha,\beta)
-Hölder condition even locally.
We have that
F_{\infty}^{\alpha,\beta}\subset F_{R_{1}}^{\alpha,\beta}\subset F_{R_{2}}^{\alpha,\beta}\subset F_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}
for all
0\leq R_{2}\leq R_{1}\leq+\infty
.

We assume that an algorithm may use exact or noisy evaluations of the function
g
. Consequently, we can have access to the function
g
through its possibly perturbed values
\tilde{g}
at given points
(t,y)
. To be more precise we consider the following set of corrupting functions

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\delta)=\{\tilde{\delta}:[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\ :\ \tilde{\delta}-\hbox{Borel measurable},\ \|\tilde{\delta}(t,y)\|\leq\delta(1+\|y\|)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\hbox{for all}\ t\in[a,b],y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}.

(5.1)

Of course
\mathcal{K}(\delta_{1})\subset\mathcal{K}(\delta_{2})
for all
0\leq\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}\leq 1
. We also set

V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta)=B_{\xi}(\delta)\times V_{g}(\delta),

(5.2)

where

V_{g}(\delta)=\bigcup_{\tilde{\delta}\in\mathcal{K}(\delta)}\{g+\tilde{\delta}\}.

(5.3)

For
0\leq\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{2}\leq 1
we have
V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta_{1})\subset V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta_{2})
and
V_{(\xi,g)}(0)=\{(\xi,g)\}
.

Let
(\xi,g)\in F_{0}^{\alpha,\beta}
and
(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})\in V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta)
.
A vector of noisy information about
(\xi,g)
is of the following form

\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})=[\tilde{g}(t_{0},y_{0}),\tilde{g}(t_{1},y_{1}),\dots,\tilde{g}(t_{i-1},y_{i-1}),\tilde{\xi}]

where
i\in\mathbb{N}
is a total number of noisy evaluations of
g
. The evaluation points
(t_{k},y_{k})
can be chosen in adaptive way. This means that there exist functions
\psi_{j}
such that

(t_{0},y_{0})=\psi_{0}(\tilde{\xi}),

(5.4)

and for
j=1,2,\ldots,i-1

(t_{j},y_{j})=\psi_{j}\Bigl(\tilde{g}(t_{0},y_{0}),\ldots,\tilde{g}(t_{j-1},y_{j-1}),\tilde{\xi}\Bigr).

(5.5)

An algorithm that approximates
z
and uses the vector of (noisy) information
\mathcal{N}
is defined as

\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})=\varphi(\mathcal{N}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})),

(5.6)

for some function

\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{(i+1)d}\to B([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(5.7)

where
B([a,b];\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the linear space of all functions
l:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
bounded in the supremum norm. For a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define
\Phi_{n}
as a class of all algorithms of the form (
5.6
) for which the total number of evaluations
i
is at most
n
. For example,
\tilde{l}_{n}\in\Phi_{n}
.

For a fixed
(\xi,g)\in F^{\alpha,\beta}_{0}
the error of
\mathcal{A}\in\Phi_{n}
is defined as follows

e(\mathcal{A},\xi,g,\delta)=\sup\limits_{(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})\in V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta)}\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(\xi,g)(t)-\mathcal{A}(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})(t)\|,

(5.8)

while the worst-case error of the algorithm
\mathcal{A}
in a subclass
\mathcal{G}\subset F^{\alpha,\beta}_{0}

e(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},\delta)=\sup\limits_{(\xi,g)\in\mathcal{G}}e(\mathcal{A},\xi,g,\delta).

(5.9)

We investigate behavior of the
n
th minimal error defined as below

e_{n}(\mathcal{G},\delta)=\inf_{\mathcal{A}\in\Phi_{n}}e(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{G},\delta).

(5.10)

The following worst-case results in the class
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R}
(with suitable
R
’s) for the Euler algorithm
\tilde{l}_{n}
, follow from the proofs of Theorems
3.1
,
4.1
. Note that the error bounds in the Theorems
3.1
,
4.1
are shown for a fixed
(\xi,g)
, while now we are considering the worst-case error in the class
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R}
. For the clarity we present upper bounds on the error of the Euler algorithm in the case of exact and inexact information separately.

Proposition 5.1
.

(The case of exact information)

Let
R_{1}=e^{K(b-a)}(1+K)\left(e^{K(b-a)}+K(b-a)\right)
. Then there exists
C\in(0,+\infty)
, depending only on the parameters of the class
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R_{1}}
, such for all
(\xi,g)\in F_{R_{1}}^{\alpha,\beta}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-l_{n}(t)\|\leq C(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}).

(5.11)

Proposition 5.2
.

(The case of inexact information)

Let
R_{2}=\max\{R_{1},K_{2}(1+K)\}=e^{K(b-a)}(1+K)\max\{e^{K(b-a)}+K(b-a),2\}
. Then there exists
C\in(0,+\infty)
, depending only on the parameters of the class
F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R_{2}}
, such that for all
n\geq\lceil 2(b-a)\rceil+1
,
\delta\in[0,1]
,
(\xi,g)\in F^{\alpha,\beta}_{R_{2}}
,
(\tilde{\xi},\tilde{g})\in V_{(\xi,g)}(\delta)
the following holds

•

if
\beta=1
then

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq C(h^{\alpha}+\delta).

(5.12)

•

if
\beta\in(0,1)
then

\sup\limits_{a\leq t\leq b}\|z(t)-\tilde{l}_{n}(t)\|\leq C\Bigl(h^{\alpha}+h^{\beta}+\mathbf{1}_{(0,1]}(\delta)\cdot(h^{1/2}+\delta)\Bigr).

(5.13)

In the special case when
\beta=1
we get the following sharp bounds on the
n
th minimal error in the class
F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}}
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
\beta=1
and
R_{2}
defined as in Proposition
5.2
. Then it holds

e_{n}(F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}},\delta)=\Theta(n^{-\alpha}+\delta),

(5.14)

as
n\to+\infty
,
\delta\to 0^{+}
. The Euler algorithm
\tilde{l}_{n}
is the optimal one.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from (
5.12
) in Proposition
5.2
and the fact that
\tilde{l}_{n}\in\Phi_{n}
.

Now we turn to the lower bound. We consider the following class

\mathcal{G}^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}}=\{(0,g)\in F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}}\ |\ g(t,y)=g(t,0)\textrm{ for all }(t,y)\in[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\},

which is a subclass of
F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}}
.
Moreover, for
(\xi,g)\in\mathcal{G}^{\alpha}_{R_{2}}
we have that
\displaystyle{z(\xi,g)(t)=\int\limits_{a}^{t}g(s,0)ds}
,
t\in[a,b]
.
Hence,

e_{n}(F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}},\delta)\geq e_{n}(\mathcal{G}^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}},0)\geq\inf_{\mathcal{A}\in\Phi_{n}}\sup\limits_{(0,g)\in\mathcal{G}^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}}}\|z(0,g)(b)-\mathcal{A}(0,g)(b)\|=\Omega(n^{-\alpha}),

(5.15)

which follows from the result on lower bound for a Riemann
integration problem of Hölder continuous functions under exact information, see Proposition 1.3.9, page 34 in
[
29
]
. Moreover, using analogous argumentation as in the proof of Theorem 3 in
[
2
]
we get, in the case of inexact information, that

e_{n}(F^{\alpha,1}_{R_{2}},\delta)\geq(b-a)\delta.

(5.16)

From (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) we get the lower bound in (
5.14
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

By considering the autonomous problem
z^{\prime}(t)=g(z(t))
,
z(a)=\xi
,
t\in[a,b]
, with globally Lipschitz continuous function
g
we know, from the results of
[
14
]
, that we can only get the lower bound
\Omega(h)
. Hence, under our assumptions there is a gap between the upper bound
O(h^{\beta})
and the lower bound
\Omega(h)
- we conjecture that the optimal error bound is
\Theta(h^{\beta})
.

5.11
). The exact solutions for the analyzed problems are unknown, so we proceeded as follows to estimate the empirical rate of convergence. In parallel, we computed the approximated solution and a numerical reference solution with a smaller step size

The theoretical convergence rate is known. We presented the test results in the log-log plots to test a numerical convergence rate. On the y axis there is

We computed only one approximated solution to get the numerical convergence rate for the case without noise.

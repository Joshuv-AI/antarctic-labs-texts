Abstract
. This paper is concerned with the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for fast diffusion equations posed in bounded domains, where every energy solution vanishes in finite time and a suitably rescaled solution converges to an asymptotic profile. Bonforte and Figalli (CPAM, 2021) first proved an exponential convergence to nondegenerate positive asymptotic profiles for nonnegative rescaled solutions in a weighted L 2 L^{2} norm for smooth bounded domains by developing a nonlinear entropy method . However, the optimality of the rate remains open to question. In the present paper, their result is fully extended to possibly sign-changing asymptotic profiles as well as general bounded domains by improving an energy method along with a quantitative gradient inequality developed by the first author (ARMA, 2023). Moreover, a (quantitative) exponential stability result for least-energy asymptotic profiles follows as a corollary, and it is further employed to prove the optimality of the exponential rate.



1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be any bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. There are a great number of contributions to the study of
nonlinear diffusion equations
posed on bounded domains, that is,

\partial_{t}\rho=\Delta\rho^{m}\ \mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.1)

where
\partial_{t}=\partial/\partial t
,
\rho=\rho(x,t)
denotes the density of a diffusing substance and the diffusion coefficient
D
scales with
\rho^{m-1}
for an exponent
0<m<\infty
. In particular, the case
0<m<1
(respectively,
m>1
) is called a
fast diffusion equation
(respectively,
porous medium equation
) and classified as a
singular diffusion
(respectively,
degenerate diffusion
).

In the present paper, we deal with (possibly sign-changing) solutions to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem for the fast diffusion equation,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(|u|^{q-2}u\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta u\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.2)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.3)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=u_{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\Omega\times\{0\}.

(1.4)

Of course, (
1.2
) is transformed from (
1.1
) by setting
u=\rho^{m}
and
q-1=1/m
, and vice versa. Throughout this paper, we assume that

u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\},\quad 2<q<2^{*}:=\dfrac{2N}{(N-2)_{+}}.

(1.5)

This problem was studied by Berryman and Holland in
[
10
,
11
]
, which were motivated in order to give a theoretical interpretation to the experimental observation of anomalous diffusion of hydrogen plasma across a purely poloidal octupole magnetic field that
after a few milliseconds the density profile always evolves into a fixed shape
(
the “normal mode”
)
which then decays in time
based on the Okuda-Dawson model
D\sim\rho^{-1/2}
(i.e., the case
q=3
) proposed in
[
36
]
.

Let us recall
qualitative
results on asymptotic behavior of (weak) solutions to (
1.2
)–(
1.4
). Due to the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, the diffusion coefficient
D
diverges on the boundary (see (
1.1
)). As a result, every weak solution
u=u(x,t)
of (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) vanishes at a finite time
t_{*}
, which is uniquely determined by the initial datum
u_{0}
(see
[
38
,
13
,
23
,
29
]
); hence, we denote
t_{*}=t_{*}(u_{0})
. Moreover, Berryman and Holland
[
11
]
proved that the extinction rate of the
positive classical
solution
u(\cdot,t)
is just
(t_{*}-t)_{+}^{1/(q-2)}
as
t\nearrow t_{*}
, that is,

c_{1}(t_{*}-t)_{+}^{1/(q-2)}\leq\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}(t_{*}-t)_{+}^{1/(q-2)}

(1.6)

with
c_{1},c_{2}>0
for all
t\geq 0
, provided that
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
; furthermore, this fact is extended to (possibly)
sign-changing weak solutions
by
[
34
,
25
,
39
,
6
]
(see also
[
17
,
18
,
31
,
33
]
). Therefore the
asymptotic profile

\phi(x)
of
u(x,t)
is defined by

\phi(x)=\lim_{t\nearrow t_{*}}(t_{*}-t)^{-1/(q-2)}u(x,t)\not\equiv 0\ \mbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(1.7)

which corresponds to the
fixed shape of the density profile
concerned in
[
10
,
11
]
.
Apply the change of variables,

v(x,s)=(t_{*}-t)^{-1/(q-2)}u(x,t)\ \mbox{ and }\ s=\log(t_{*}/(t_{*}-t))

(1.8)

for
t\in[0,t_{*})
. Then the asymptotic profile
\phi(x)
is reformulated as the limit of
v(x,s)
as
s\to\infty
. Moreover,
v=v(x,s)
turns out to be an energy solution of the following Cauchy-Dirichlet problem:

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\left(|v|^{q-2}v\right)

\displaystyle=\Delta v+\lambda_{q}|v|^{q-2}v\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.9)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),

(1.10)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=v_{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\Omega\times\{0\}

(1.11)

with
\lambda_{q}:=(q-1)/(q-2)>0
and
v_{0}:=t_{*}(u_{0})^{-1/(q-2)}u_{0}
. Here we note that (
1.9
) along with (
1.10
) can also be formulated as a (generalized) gradient flow of the form,

\partial_{s}\left(|v|^{q-2}v\right)(s)=-J^{\prime}(v(s))\ \mbox{ in }H^{-1}(\Omega),\quad s>0,

where
J^{\prime}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
denotes the Fréchet derivative of the energy functional,

J(w):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w(x)|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\int_{\Omega}|w(x)|^{q}\,\text{\rm d}x\quad\mbox{ for }\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Here and henceforth, we may denote
v(s)=v(\cdot,s)
for
s\geq 0
. Moreover, it is also noteworthy that
v_{0}
lies on the set,

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}:=

\displaystyle\ \{t_{*}(u_{0})^{-1/(q-2)}u_{0}\colon u_{0}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}\}

(1.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\colon t_{*}(w)=1\},

which is an invariant set of the dynamical system generated by (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) and plays a role of the phase set in stability analysis of asymptotic profiles (see Definition
2.2
below and
[
6
]
for more details). Moreover, by virtue of (
1.6
), we see that

0<c_{1}\leq\|v(s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}<+\infty\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

(1.13)

Hence the norm
\|v(\cdot,s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
can neither vanish nor grow up to infinity (cf. see
[
6
, Proposition 10]
).

Berryman and Holland
[
11
]
proved that any
positive classical
solution
v(\cdot,s_{n})
of (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) converges strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to a nontrivial solution
\phi=\phi(x)
to the Dirichlet problem,

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\lambda_{q}|\phi|^{q-2}\phi\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(1.14)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,

(1.15)

for some sequence
s_{n}\to+\infty
and, in particular, if
N=1
, then
v(s)\to\phi
as
s\to+\infty
. Such a
quasi-convergence
result was extended to (possibly)
sign-changing weak solutions
in
[
34
,
25
,
39
,
17
,
18
,
6
]
. More precisely, the following theorem holds true:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
11
,
34
,
25
,
39
,
6
]
)
.

Under the assumption (
1.5
), let
u
be a
(
possibly sign-changing
)
energy solution of
(
1.2
)–(
1.4
)
and let
t_{*}\in(0,\infty)
be the extinction time of
u
. Then for any increasing sequence
t_{n}\to t_{*}
, there exist a subsequence
(n^{\prime})
of
(n)
and a nontrivial solution
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
of
(
1.14
), (
1.15
)
such that

\lim_{t_{n^{\prime}}\to t_{*}}\|(t_{*}-t_{n^{\prime}})^{-1/(q-2)}u(t_{n^{\prime}})-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0,

(1.16)

equivalently,

\lim_{s_{n^{\prime}}\to\infty}\|v(s_{n^{\prime}})-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0,

where
v
and
s_{n}
are defined as in (
1.8
) for
u
and
t_{n}
, respectively.

Moreover, Feireisl and Simondon
[
27
]
proved convergence of any
nonnegative
weak solution
v=v(x,s)\geq 0
for (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) to a positive solution
\phi
for (
1.14
), (
1.15
) in
C(\overline{\Omega})
as
s\to+\infty
by developing a Łojasiewicz-Simon gradient inequality. Furthermore, based on this along with the so-called
Global Harnack Principle
(GHP for short), which is valid for bounded
C^{2}
domains and developed in
[
17
]
, that is, for any
\delta>0
, there exist constants
c_{3},c_{4}>0
such that

c_{3}\leq\frac{v(\cdot,s)}{\mathrm{dist}(\cdot,\Omega)}\leq c_{4}\ \mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega}\ \mbox{ for }\ s>\delta,

(1.17)

where
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega):=\inf_{y\in\partial\Omega}|x-y|\asymp\phi(x)>0
, Bonforte, Grillo and Vazquez
[
16
]
proved convergence of the
relative error
,

h(s):=(v(s)-\phi)/\phi\ \mbox{ in }C(\overline{\Omega})\ \mbox{ as }\ s\to+\infty

(1.18)

for positive solutions (see also
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
for a quantitative result, which also gives an alternative proof to the above).

As for
quantitative
results, developing a
nonlinear entropy method
, Bonforte and Figalli
[
14
]
proved a sharp rate of convergence for nonnegative
v=v(x,s)
in the
relative entropy
,

\mathsf{E}(s):=\int_{\Omega}|v(x,s)-\phi(x)|^{2}\phi(x)^{q-2}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}\ \mbox{ for }\ s>0,

(1.19)

where
\lambda_{0}:=2\nu_{k}/(q-1)
and
\nu_{k}
is the least
positive
eigenvalue of the weighted eigenvalue problem

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}e=\nu|\phi|^{q-2}e\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad e=0\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega

(1.20)

for the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}:=-\Delta-\lambda_{q}(q-1)|\phi|^{q-2}
, provided that
\phi
is
positive
and
nondegenerate
(i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}
has no zero eigenvalue) and
\partial\Omega
is smooth (at least of class
C^{2}
). The above rate of convergence seems
sharp
in view of a formal linearization (see
[
14
, §2]
).

Furthermore, an alternative approach based on an energy method along with a quantitative gradient inequality is developed in
[
5
]
to (directly) prove that

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}\ \mbox{ for }\ s>0,

(1.21)

which also immediately yields (
1.19
), for nonnegative solutions
v=v(x,s)
to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) and positive nondegenerate solutions
\phi=\phi(x)
to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) for any bounded
C^{1,1}
domains. Furthermore, in
[
5
]
, it is also proved for (possibly) sign-changing solutions that (
1.21
) is satisfied with
\lambda_{0}
replaced by any

0<\lambda<\frac{2}{q-1}C_{q}^{-2}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{-(q-2)}\frac{\nu_{k}}{\nu_{k}+\lambda_{q}(q-1)},

where
C_{q}
stands for the best constant of a Sobolev-Poincaré inequality and which cannot however reach the sharp exponent
\lambda_{0}
even for least-energy solutions to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) (see Remark 3.2 of
[
5
]
).

On the other hand, the topology of the convergence can be improved with the aid of optimal boundary regularity results developed by Jin and Xiong in
[
31
,
33
]
, which is also motivated from a long-standing open question posed in
[
11
]
. More precisely, Jin and Xiong
[
31
,
33
]
proved the optimal boundary regularity (e.g.,
\partial_{t}^{\ell}u(\cdot,t)\in C^{q+1}(\overline{\Omega})
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
) of nonnegative solutions to (
1.2
)–(
1.4
), which is consistent with the regularity of separable solutions
u=u(x,t)
to (
1.2
), (
1.3
), in smooth bounded domains, by developing Schauder estimates for some linear parabolic equations with degenerate coefficients asymptotic to
\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)^{q-2}
(in front) of the time-derivative, with the aid of the GHP (
1.17
). Moreover, based on the optimal boundary regularity result, they also proved that (
1.19
) can be improved up to

\left\|\frac{v(s)}{\phi}-1\right\|_{C^{q}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}\ \mbox{ for }\ s>1

for nonnegative solutions in
smooth
bounded domains.

Furthermore, Choi, McCann and Seis
[
21
]
proved a dichotomy result on the rate of convergence of
v=v(x,s)\geq 0
to (possibly)
degenerate
positive solutions
\phi=\phi(x)
; more precisely, either of
\mathsf{E}(s)\lesssim{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}
or
\mathsf{E}(s)\gtrsim s^{-1}
always holds (cf. see also
[
32
]
). They observed that the relative error
h(\cdot,s):=(v(\cdot,s)-\phi)/\phi
solves

\partial_{s}h+L_{\phi}(h)=\mathcal{N}(h),

where
L_{\phi}
is a linear elliptic operator including coefficients associated with
\phi
and
\mathcal{N}
is a nonlinear perturbation, which still involves
\partial_{s}h
but can be handled as a small perturbation for
h
small enough, by proving a smoothing estimate for
\partial_{s}h
. Then the dichotomy result follows from an ODE analysis of a reduced system. This dichotomy result also enables us to derive the sharp rate of convergence (
1.19
) for nondegenerate positive asymptotic profiles in smooth bounded domains (see also
[
22
]
). We further refer the reader to the recent article
[
15
]
for a comprehensive survey on this field.

As seen from the above, convergence to
positive
asymptotic profiles in
smooth
bounded domains has been well studied; on the other hand, results for
sign-changing
asymptotic profiles are still limited. In particular, the sharp rate of convergence as in (
1.19
) and (
1.21
) has not yet been proved for sign-changing solutions. Actually, the nonlinear entropy method is deeply based on the GHP, and hence, the positivity of the asymptotic profile may be indispensable. The energy method developed in
[
5
]
is applicable to sign-changing asymptotic profiles; however, the conclusion for sign-changing asymptotic profiles does not reach the sharp rate of convergence.

Another open question in this field is the
optimality of the convergence rate
(see (
1.19
)) even for positive asymptotic profiles; indeed, there seems to be no proof, although it may be expected to be optimal in view of a formal linearlized analysis (see
[
14
, §2]
). On the other hand, as for the porous medium case (i.e.,
m>2
and
1<q<2
), the
optimal
rate of convergence to the (unique) positive asymptotic profile was determined by means of the classical comparison argument in
[
8
]
, and moreover, a finer asymptotics has also been investigated in a recent paper
[
30
]
.

The first purpose of the present paper is to prove (
1.21
) for each (possibly) sign-changing solution
v=v(x,s)
of (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) which converges to a nondegenerate asymptotic profile as
s\to+\infty
. We stress that our method of proof is completely free from both the relative error convergence (
1.18
) and smoothing estimates, which have been developed for nonnegative solutions, but only a few results are known for sign-changing ones. Furthermore, compared to the previous results on nonnegative solutions based on the relative error convergence and smoothing estimates in
[
14
,
31
,
33
,
21
]
as well as results in
[
5
]
, we need no assumption on the smoothness of domains. The second purpose of the present paper is to prove the
optimality
of the convergence rate (see (
1.19
)) to nondegenerate
least-energy asymptotic profiles
(see below for definition) with the aid of the improved convergence result mentioned above.

The main results of the present paper are stated as follows:

Theorem 1.2

(Sharp rate of convergence)
.

Let
\Omega
be any bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Under the assumption (
1.5
), let
v=v(x,s)
be a
(
possibly
)

sign-changing
energy solution to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) and let
\phi=\phi(x)
be a nondegenerate nontrivial solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) such that
v(s_{n})\to\phi
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for some sequence
s_{n}\to+\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle 0\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}\quad\mbox{ for all }\ s\geq 0,

(1.22)

\displaystyle\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s}\quad\mbox{ for all }\ s\geq 0,

(1.23)

where
\lambda_{0}
is defined as in (
1.19
).

In
[
5
]
, some examples of nondegenerate sign-changing asymptotic profiles are exhibited. In particular, for
N\geq 2
, nondegenerate sign-changing asymptotic profiles are constructed in dumbbell domains, and moreover, their exponential stability is also proved under certain symmetry of initial data; actually, sign-changing asymptotic profiles are never asymptotically stable for general initial data (see
[
6
, Theorem 3]
). Furthermore, the exponential convergence (
1.23
) can also be rephrased with the original variables as follows (cf. see (
1.6
), (
1.16
)):

\|(t_{*}-t)^{-1/(q-2)}u(t)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\left(\frac{t_{*}-t}{t_{*}}\right)_{+}^{\lambda_{0}}\quad\mbox{ for }\ t\geq 0.

In what follows, the
least-energy solutions
to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) (or
least-energy asymptotic profiles
) mean nontrivial weak solutions to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) minimizing the energy
J
among all the weak nontrivial solutions to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). The least positive eigenvalue of (
1.20
) for any nondegenerate least-energy asymptotic profile
\phi
is the second one, that is,
k=2
by
[
35
]
. Now we have the following corollary, which improves an exponential stability result in

:

Corollary 1.3

(Quantitative exponential stability of least-energy profiles)
.

Let
\Omega
be any bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Assume (
1.5
) and let
\phi
be a nondegenerate
least-energy
solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). Then there exists constants
\delta_{0},C>0
satisfying the following
:
Let
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
be such that
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<\delta_{0}

be the energy solution to (

\displaystyle 0\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(J(v_{0})-J(\phi)\right){\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s},

(1.24)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(J(v_{0})-J(\phi)\right){\rm e}^{-\lambda_{0}s},

(1.25)

where
\lambda_{0}
is defined as in (
1.19
), for all

.

The corollary mentioned above further enables us to prove the optimality of the rate of convergence provided in Theorem
1.2
(and Corollary

) for

asymptotic profiles.

Theorem 1.4

(Optimality of the convergence rate)
.

Let
\Omega
be any bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Assume (
1.5
) and let
\phi
be a nondegenerate
least-energy
solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and let
\mathbb{P}_{2}
be the spectral projection onto the eigenspace

corresponding to the least positive eigenvalue

). Let

\begin{cases}\|\xi_{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=O(\varepsilon)\ \mbox{ as }\ \varepsilon\to 0_{+},\\

Notation.
Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measurable set and denote by
\mathcal{M}(A)
the set of all Lebesgue measurable functions defined on
A
with values in
\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
C
a generic nonnegative constant which may vary from line to line. We denote by
H^{-1}(\Omega)
the dual space of the Sobolev space
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
equipped with the inner product
(u,v)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\,\text{\rm d}x
for
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
stands for the duality pairing between
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Furthermore, an inner product of
H^{-1}(\Omega)
is naturally defined as

(f,g)_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}=\langle f,(-\Delta)^{-1}g\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{ for }\ f,g\in H^{-1}(\Omega),

(1.28)

which also gives
\|f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}=(f,f)_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}
for
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Then
-\Delta
is a duality mapping (Riesz mapping) between
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, that is,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

Let

3.3.
Eigenvalue problems with possibly degenerate weights

Throughout this subsection, let
s\geq 0
be fixed arbitrarily. Define the operator
A^{s}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by

A^{s}(w):=(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|v(s)|^{q-2}w\right)\quad\mbox{ for }\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

The following argument in this subsection is still valid for any function
\omega\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
instead of
v(s)
in the weight (e.g.,
\omega=\phi
). Then
\mathcal{H}_{s}
is also replaced in an analogous way, and then, it does no longer depend on
s
. This subsection is devoted to discussing eigenvalue problems for the operator
A^{s}
. We shall finally construct a complete orthonormal system of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
by means of eigenfunctions for
A^{s}
.

We first prove that
A^{s}
is self-adjoint and compact. Indeed, let
(w_{n})
be bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and set
u_{n}:=A^{s}(w_{n})
. Then
-\Delta u_{n}=|v(s)|^{q-2}w_{n}
in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Testing both sides by
u_{n}
, we see that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w_{n}u_{n}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}^{2}\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-2}\|w_{n}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

which implies that
(u_{n})
is bounded in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence we have, up to a (not relabeled) subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
and
w_{n}\to w
weakly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega)
(by
q<2^{*}
) for some
u,w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Therefore one can verify that
-\Delta u=|v(s)|^{q-2}w
in
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, and moreover, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w_{n}u_{n}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\to\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}wu\,\text{\rm d}x=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

which along with the uniform convexity of
(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\|\nabla\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
yields

u_{n}\to u\quad\mbox{ strongly in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Thus
A^{s}
turns out to be a compact operator in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Moreover, let
f,g\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. It then follows immediately that

\displaystyle(A^{s}f,g)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle-\Delta(A^{s}f),g\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}fg\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\langle-\Delta(A^{s}g),f\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=(f,A^{s}g)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
stand for the inner product in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and the duality pairing between
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
H^{-1}(\Omega)
, respectively (see Notation). Hence
A^{s}
is symmetric, and therefore, self-adjoint.

Due to the spectral theory for compact self-adjoint operators (see, e.g.,
[
20
, §5]
), all eigenvalues of
A^{s}
are real and bounded. Moreover, since
A^{s}\neq 0
,
\sigma(A^{s})\setminus\{0\}
is either finite or a sequence converging to
0
. Here
\sigma(A^{s})
stands for the spectral set of
A^{s}
. Furthermore,
\sigma(A^{s})\setminus\{0\}
coincides with the set of all nonzero eigenvalues of
A^{s}
. Moreover, we observe that all eigenvalues of
A^{s}
are nonnegative.

Let
\{\lambda_{j}^{s}\}_{j\geq 1}
be the set of all
nonzero
eigenvalues of
A^{s}
. We set

\displaystyle E_{0}^{s}:=N(A^{s}):=

\displaystyle\ \{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\colon A^{s}(w)=0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\colon|v(s)|^{q-2}w=0\ \mbox{ a.e.~in }\Omega\}

and
E_{j}^{s}:=N(A^{s}-\lambda_{j}^{s}I)
. Then we find that

0\leq\dim E_{0}^{s}\leq+\infty,\quad 0<\dim E_{j}^{s}<+\infty.

Here the latter follows from the Fredholm alternative. Then
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is the Hilbert sum of
\{E_{j}^{s}\}_{j\geq 0}
.

We claim that
\mathrm{codim}\,E_{0}^{s}=+\infty
; indeed, since
E^{s}_{0}=N(A^{s})
is closed in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, we find that
\mathrm{codim}\,E^{s}_{0}<+\infty
if and only if
\dim\,(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}<+\infty
(see, e.g.,
[
20
, (b) of Proposition 11.13]
). Recall that

(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}=\{f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)\colon\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0\ \mbox{ for }\ \varphi\in E^{s}_{0}\}.

Since the
N
-dimensional Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{N}(\Omega\setminus Z(s))
of
\Omega\setminus Z(s)
is positive (otherwise,
v(s)\equiv 0
), one can construct a sequence
\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of disjoint Lebesgue measurable sets such that
\mathcal{L}^{N}(M_{j})>0
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}M_{j}=\Omega\setminus Z(s)
(indeed, it is possible, e.g., since
r\mapsto\mathcal{L}^{N}((\Omega\setminus Z(s))\cap B_{r})
is continuous for a ball
B_{r}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of radius
r
). Each characteristic function
\chi_{M_{j}}
supported over
M_{j}
belongs to
H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Moreover, for
j\in\mathbb{N}
, noting that
M_{j}\subset\Omega\setminus Z(s)
and
\varphi=0
a.e. in
\Omega\setminus Z(s)
for
\varphi\in E^{s}_{0}
, we observe that

\langle\chi_{M_{j}},\varphi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{M_{j}}\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x=0\quad\mbox{ for }\ \varphi\in E^{s}_{0}.

Hence
\chi_{M_{j}}
lies on
(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus we obtain
\dim\,(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}=+\infty
.

Hence
\sigma(A^{s})\setminus\{0\}=\{\lambda_{j}^{s}\}_{j\geq 1}
turns out to be a sequence converging to
0
. Here and henceforth, we denote by
\{(\lambda^{s}_{j},e^{s}_{j})\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
the sequence consisting of all eigenpairs of
A^{s}
for nonzero eigenvalues such that
\{\lambda^{s}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is nonincreasing,
\lambda^{s}_{j}\to 0
as
j\to+\infty
and
e^{s}_{j}
is an eigenfunction corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda^{s}_{j}
and normalized in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
(that is,
\|e^{s}_{j}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1
and then
\|e_{j}^{s}/\sqrt{\lambda_{j}^{s}}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}=1
) by rearranging eigenvalues and by repeating the same eigenvalue according to its multiplicity.

Set
\mu^{s}_{j}:=1/\lambda^{s}_{j}>0
(
j\geq 1
). Then for each
j\geq 1
,
(\mu^{s}_{j},e^{s}_{j})
is an eigenpair of the following eigenvalue problem:

-\Delta e=\mu|v(s)|^{q-2}e\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad e=0\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

(3.9)

Then, for any
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists
u_{0}^{s}\in E_{0}^{s}
such that

u=u_{0}^{s}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}_{j}e^{s}_{j}\ \mbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\alpha^{s}_{j}:=(u,e^{s}_{j})_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

(3.10)

which implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}u:=

\displaystyle-\Delta u-\lambda_{q}(q-1)|v(s)|^{q-2}u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{0}^{s}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}_{j}\frac{\mu_{j}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}{\mu_{j}^{s}}(-\Delta)e^{s}_{j}\ \mbox{ in }H^{-1}(\Omega).

(3.11)

Let us also consider the eigenvalue problem,

-\Delta e=\mu|\phi|^{q-2}e\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,\quad e=0\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,

(3.12)

where
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a (possibly sign-changing) solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). Then repeating the argument so far, we can construct eigenpairs
\{(\mu_{j},e_{j})\}_{j\geq 1}
of (
3.12
) for positive eigenvalues. Then
(\nu_{j},e_{j})
with
\nu_{j}:=\mu_{j}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)
becomes an eigenpair of the linearized operator

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}:=-\Delta-\lambda_{q}(q-1)|\phi|^{q-1}

(cf. see
[
14
, §2]
,
[
5
]
). When
\phi
is nondegenerate, all the eigenvalues
\nu_{j}
are nonzero, and therefore,
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\in\mathscr{L}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),H^{-1}(\Omega))
is invertible (i.e., the inverse
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}:H^{-1}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is well defined and bounded linear). Moreover,
\mathcal{H}_{s}
and
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
are replaced by
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}:=L^{2}(\Omega\setminus Z_{\phi};|\phi|^{q-2}\,\text{\rm d}x)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\phi}^{\prime}:=L^{2}(\Omega\setminus Z_{\phi};|\phi|^{2-q}\,\text{\rm d}x)
with the set
Z_{\phi}:=\{x\in\Omega\colon\phi(x)=0\}
.

3.4.
Convergence of eigenvalues

In this subsection, we shall discuss convergence of each eigenvalue
\mu^{s}_{j}
for (
3.9
) as
s\to+\infty
. We first exhibit the following lemma, which may be standard; however, for the completeness, we shall give a proof in Appendix §
A
:

Lemma 3.3

(Variational representation of eigenvalues)
.

For each
s\geq 0
and
j\geq 1
, the eigenvalue
\mu^{s}_{j}>0
of (
3.9
) can be characterized as the following max-min value
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\[0.0pt]
\dim Y=j\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}(A^{s}w,w)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\[0.0pt]
\dim Y=j\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x.

(3.13)

Here
Y
denotes a subspace of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Similarly, each eigenvalue
\mu_{j}>0
of (
3.12
) can be written as

\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\[0.0pt]
\dim Y=j\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x.

(3.14)

Now, we are ready to prove

Lemma 3.4

(Convergence of eigenvalues)
.

There exists a positive constant
C
which depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
and
c(v)
defined by (
3.4
) such that

\left|\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}\right|\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0\ \mbox{ and }\ j\in\mathbb{N},

(3.15)

where
\rho:=\min\{q-2,1\}\in(0,1]
. Moreover, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds that

\mu^{s}_{j}\to\mu_{j}\quad\mbox{ as }\ s\to+\infty.

(3.16)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\left||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right|w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x+C_{q}^{2}\left\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

for
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Taking the sup-inf of both sides as in Lemma
3.3
, we obtain

\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}+C_{q}^{2}\left\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}.

(3.17)

In case
2<q<3
, it follows that

\left||v(x,s)|^{q-2}-|\phi(x)|^{q-2}\right|\leq\left||v(x,s)|-|\phi(x)|\right|^{q-2}\leq\left|v(x,s)-\phi(x)\right|^{q-2},

which yields

\left\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}\leq\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-2}.

In case
q\geq 3
, we observe that

\displaystyle\left||v(x,s)|^{q-2}-|\phi(x)|^{q-2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq(q-2)\left(|v(x,s)|^{q-3}+|\phi(x)|^{q-3}\right)|v(x,s)-\phi(x)|.

Hence

\displaystyle\left\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq(q-2)\left(\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-3}+\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-3}\right)\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}.

Therefore, since
v(s)
is bounded in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for
s\geq 0
, we conclude that

\left\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}\leq c\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho},

(3.18)

where
\rho:=\min\{q-2,1\}\in(0,1]
, for some constant
c\geq 0
which depends on
q
and
c(v)
defined by (
3.4
).

Consequently, it follows from (
3.17
) that

\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}

for any
s\geq 0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Here
C
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
and
c(v)
. One can also prove the inverse inequality,

\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}\geq\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}-C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}

(3.19)

in a similar fashion. Thus we conclude that

\left|\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}-\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}\right|\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0,

which further implies

\left|\mu^{s}_{j}-\mu_{j}\right|\leq C\mu^{s}_{j}\mu_{j}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

(3.20)

From (
3.19
) along with the positivity of
\mu_{j}
and (
3.1
), we observe that
\mu^{s}_{j}\leq 2\mu_{j}
for
s\geq 0
large enough. Hence we obtain

\left|\mu^{s}_{j}-\mu_{j}\right|\leq 2C\mu_{j}^{2}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\gg 1,

which along with (
3.1
) yields (
3.16
). This completes the proof.
∎

As a corollary, we have the following:

Remark 3.5

(Invertibility of
\mathcal{L}_{s}
for
s>0
large enough)
.

(i)

Since
\phi
is nondegenerate, that is,
\nu_{j}=\mu_{j}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\neq 0
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we find from (
3.16
) that
\nu^{s}_{j}=\mu^{s}_{j}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\neq 0
for
s>0
large enough. In particular, we deduce that
\mathcal{L}_{s}\in\mathscr{L}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),H^{-1}(\Omega))
is invertible with its inverse
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\in\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))
for
s>0
large enough.

(ii)

For each
s\geq 0
, let
k(s)\in\mathbb{N}
be the least number such that
\nu^{s}_{k(s)}=\mu^{s}_{k(s)}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)
is positive. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be the least number such that
\nu_{k}=\mu_{k}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)>0
. Since
\nu_{j}\neq 0
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce from (
3.16
) that
k(s)=k
for
s>0
large enough. Hence, in what follows, we shall simply write
k
instead of
k(s)
for
s>0
large enough.

Moreover, we claim that

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists a constant
s_{0}\geq 0
such that

\sup_{s\geq s_{0}}\|\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq 2\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}.

(3.21)

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}w

\displaystyle=-\Delta w-\lambda_{q}(q-1)|v(s)|^{q-2}w

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}w-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\left(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right)w

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\left(w-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\left[\left(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right)w\right]\right)

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{L}_{\phi}\left(T_{s}(w)\right)\quad\mbox{ for }\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

(3.22)

where
T_{s}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is a bounded linear operator given by
T_{s}(w)=w-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}[(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2})w]
for
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Noting that

\displaystyle{\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}[(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2})w]\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2})w\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\||v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\|_{L^{q/(q-2)}(\Omega)}\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

and recalling (
3.18
) along with (
3.1
), we can take
s_{0}\geq 0
large enough so that

\lambda_{q}(q-1)\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}[(|v(s)|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2})w]\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{ for }\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

for all
s\geq s_{0}
. Hence
T_{s}
turns out to be invertible such that
\|T_{s}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\leq 2
for
s\geq s_{0}
. Therefore, thanks to (
3.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\|T_{s}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq s_{0},

which completes the proof.
∎

We close this subsection with the following:

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{X}
be a nondegenerate
least-energy
solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). Then for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
r_{\varepsilon}>0

(
independent of
v_{0}
and
s
)
such that

|\mu^{s}_{j}-\mu_{j}|<\varepsilon\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0\ \mbox{ and }\ j\in\mathbb{N},

(3.23)

provided that
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}

satisfies
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<r_{\varepsilon}
. Moreover, if
\varepsilon>0
is small enough, it holds that
\nu^{s}_{j}\neq 0
and
k(s)=2
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\geq 0
and (
3.21
) holds with
s_{0}=0
under the same assumption for
v_{0}

(
cf. see Remark
3.5
and Lemma
3.6
)
.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. In what follows, in addition to
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, we always assume that
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<1
. Then we can take a constant
M>0
such that
c(v)=\sup_{s\geq 0}\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq M
, where
v
denotes the energy solution to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) with the initial datum
v_{0}
, for any
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
satisfying
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<1
(see (
1.13
) and
[
4
, Lemma 2]
). We emphasize that
M
can be taken uniformly for
v_{0}
and
s
satisfying the assumption above. Moreover, we observe from (
3.15
) that
\mu^{s}_{j}\leq 2\mu_{j}
for any
s\geq 0
, provided that

\sup_{s\geq 0}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}<\frac{1}{2C\mu_{j}},

where
C
can now be taken uniformly for
v_{0}
(instead, it may depend on
M
). It then follows from (
3.20
) that

|\mu^{s}_{j}-\mu_{j}|\leq 2C\mu_{j}^{2}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Thanks to the asymptotic stability result in
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, there exists
r_{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
such that

\sup_{s\geq 0}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho}<\min\left\{\frac{1}{2C\mu_{j}},\frac{\varepsilon}{2C\mu_{j}^{2}}\right\}

and
v(s)\to\phi
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as
s\to+\infty
,
whenever
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<r_{\varepsilon}
. Therefore we then obtain
|\mu^{s}_{j}-\mu_{j}|<\varepsilon
for any
s\geq 0
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

3.5.
Decomposition of the dual space

In this subsection, we shall introduce a complete orthonormal system of
H^{-1}(\Omega)
by means of the eigenfunctions and a Riesz map, and moreover, we shall discuss a spectral decomposition of the inverse
\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}
of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{s}
. Furthermore, it will eventually be proved that eigenfunctions of (
3.9
) for
positive
eigenvalues form a complete orthonormal system of
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
.

Recall that
-\Delta:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to H^{-1}(\Omega)
is a Riesz map and set

F_{0}^{s}=-\Delta E_{0}^{s}:=\left\{-\Delta w\colon w\in E_{0}^{s}\right\}

for
s\geq 0
. Then for any
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
s\geq 0
, one can take
f^{s}_{0}\in F^{s}_{0}
such that

f=f_{0}^{s}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta^{s}_{j}(-\Delta)e^{s}_{j}\ \mbox{ in }H^{-1}(\Omega),\quad\beta^{s}_{j}:=\langle f,e^{s}_{j}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

(3.24)

In what follows, we denote by
P_{F^{s}_{0}}:H^{-1}(\Omega)\to F^{s}_{0}
the orthogonal projection onto
F^{s}_{0}
, that is,
P_{F^{s}_{0}}(f)=f^{s}_{0}
. One can derive from (
3.24
) along with (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}f=(-\Delta)^{-1}f_{0}^{s}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\beta^{s}_{j}\frac{\mu_{j}^{s}}{\mu_{j}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}e^{s}_{j}

(3.25)

for
s>0
large enough (so that
\nu^{s}_{j}=\mu^{s}_{j}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\neq 0
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
; see (i) of Remark
3.5
). Thus we obtain

\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}f\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\|f_{0}^{s}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\beta^{s}_{j})^{2}\frac{\mu_{j}^{s}}{\mu_{j}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}

(3.26)

for
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
represented as (
3.24
) and
s>0
large enough.

We next have

Lemma 3.8
.

For
f\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
and
s\geq 0
, it holds that
P_{F^{s}_{0}}f=0
if and only if
f\in(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}
. In particular, if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
, then
P_{F^{s}_{0}}(\bar{f})=0
, where
\bar{f}
is the zero extension of
f
onto
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
s\geq 0
. Let
f\in(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}\subset H^{-1}(\Omega)
and let
f^{s}_{0}=P_{F^{s}_{0}}(f)
. Then for any
\varphi\in E^{s}_{0}
, we see that

(f,-\Delta\varphi)_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}=\langle f,\varphi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0.

Hence we have
\|f^{s}_{0}\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}=(f,f^{s}_{0})_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}=0
, i.e.,
f^{s}_{0}=0
. The inverse is obvious.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
and let
\bar{f}\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
be the zero extension of
f
onto
\Omega
(see Proposition
3.2
). Then, for every
\varphi\in E^{s}_{0}
, since
\bar{f}=0
a.e. in
Z(s)
and
\varphi=0
a.e. in
\Omega\setminus Z(s)
, we deduce that

\langle\bar{f},\varphi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\Omega\setminus Z(s)}f\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x=0,

that is,
\bar{f}\in(E^{s}_{0})^{\perp}
. Hence we deduce from the above that
P_{F^{s}_{0}}(\bar{f})=0
.
∎

Furthermore, we conclude that

Lemma 3.9
.

For each
s\geq 0
, the set
\{-\Delta e^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{\mu^{s}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
forms a complete orthonormal system of the associate space
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. Hence it holds that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\beta^{s}_{j})^{2}\mu^{s}_{j},\quad\beta^{s}_{j}:=\langle\bar{f},e^{s}_{j}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

(3.27)

for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. Here
\bar{f}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the zero extension of
f
onto
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
s\geq 0
. Note that
-\Delta e^{s}_{j}=\mu^{s}_{j}|v(s)|^{q-2}e^{s}_{j}
vanishes a.e. in
Z(s)
and belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
(see Proposition
3.1
). We see that

\displaystyle(-\Delta e^{s}_{i},-\Delta e^{s}_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus Z(s)}(-\Delta e^{s}_{i})(-\Delta e^{s}_{j})|v(s)|^{2-q}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\mu^{s}_{j}(e^{s}_{i},e^{s}_{j})_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\mu^{s}_{j}\delta_{ij}

for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, that is,
\{-\Delta e^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{\mu^{s}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is an orthonormal system in
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. We next prove that
\{-\Delta e^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{\mu^{s}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
is complete in
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
be such that
(f,-\Delta e^{s}_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}=0
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Due to Proposition
3.2
, the zero extension
\bar{f}
of
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
belongs to
L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)
. Noting that

0=(f,-\Delta e^{s}_{j})_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}=\mu^{s}_{j}\int_{\Omega\setminus Z(s)}fe^{s}_{j}\,\text{\rm d}x=\mu^{s}_{j}\langle\bar{f},e^{s}_{j}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{ for }\ j\in\mathbb{N}

and recalling (
3.24
), we deduce that

\bar{f}=P_{F^{s}_{0}}(\bar{f}).

On the other hand, it follows that
P_{F^{s}_{0}}(\bar{f})=0
from Lemma
3.8
along with
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. Thus
f=0
in
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
. Consequently,
\{-\Delta e^{s}_{j}/\sqrt{\mu^{s}_{j}}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
turns out to be a complete orthonormal system of
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
.
∎

3.6.
Taylor expansion of the energy

This subsection is concerned with a Taylor expansion of the energy functional
J
, which is at least of class
C^{2}
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
but may not be of class
C^{3}
(e.g., for
q\in(2,3)
).

Lemma 3.10

(Taylor expansion of the energy)
.

For each
s\geq 0
, it holds that

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+E(s)

(3.28)

and

J^{\prime}(v(s))=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi)+e(s),

(3.29)

where
s\mapsto E(s)\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\mapsto e(s)\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
denote generic functions satisfying

E(s)\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{2+\rho}\ \mbox{ and }\ \|e(s)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{1+\rho}

(3.30)

with
\rho=\min\{1,q-2\}>0
. Here the constant
C
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
and
c(v)
given by (
3.4
).

Proof.

Fix
s\geq 0
. In case
2<q<3
, by direct computation, we infer that

\displaystyle e(s)

\displaystyle:=J^{\prime}(v(s))-\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi)

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{q}\left[|v(s)|^{q-2}v(s)-|\phi|^{q-2}\phi-(q-1)|\phi|^{q-2}(v(s)-\phi)\right]

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\left[|(1-\theta)v(s)+\theta\phi|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right](v(s)-\phi),

where
\theta\in(0,1)
may depend on
x
and
s
. Hence we observe that, for
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle{|\langle e(s),\varphi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}|}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{q}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}\left||(1-\theta)v(s)+\theta\phi|^{q-2}-|\phi|^{q-2}\right||v(s)-\phi||\varphi|\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{q}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|1-\theta|^{q-2}|v(s)-\phi|^{q-1}|\varphi|\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{q}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|v(s)-\phi|^{q-1}|\varphi|\,\text{\rm d}x,

which along with the arbitrariness of
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
implies

\|e(s)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\lambda_{q}(q-1)C_{q}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-1}.

Moreover, it follows from (
3.28
) that

\displaystyle E(s):=

\displaystyle\ J(v(s))-J(\phi)-\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla(v(s)-\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}(v(s)-\phi)^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\Omega}\nabla(v(s)-\phi)\cdot\nabla\phi\,\text{\rm d}x-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}(v(s)-\phi)^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \lambda_{q}\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}\phi(v(s)-\phi)\,\text{\rm d}x-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|v(s)\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}(v(s)-\phi)^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)\int_{\Omega}\left(-|(1-\theta)v(s)+\theta\phi|^{q-2}+|\phi|^{q-2}\right)(v(s)-\phi)^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

for some constant
\theta\in(0,1)
which may depend on
x
and
s
. Hence one can similarly verify that

\displaystyle|E(s)|\leq\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}\leq\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}(q-1)C_{q}^{q}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Thus (
3.30
) with
\rho=q-2
follows.

In case
q\geq 3
, as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we can also derive (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) along with (
3.30
) and
\rho=1
. Then the constant
C
may further depend on
c(v)
as well.
∎

3.7.
Quantitative gradient inequality

The following lemma provides a quantitative gradient inequality for
J(\cdot)
and will play a crucial role in the proof of Theorem
1.2
:

Lemma 3.11

(Quantitative gradient inequality)
.

There exist constants
s_{1}\geq 0
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}

(3.31)

for all
s\geq s_{1}
. Here
\nu^{s}_{k}
denotes the smallest positive eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{s}

(
see Remark
3.5
)
and
\rho=\min\{1,q-2\}\in(0,1]
. Moreover, the constant
C
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
,
c(v)
given in (
3.4
) and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

(
see Lemma
3.6
)

Proof.

Fix
s\geq s_{0}
large enough in view of Remark
3.5
(see also Lemma
3.6
). Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
be fixed and let
\bar{f}\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
be the zero extension of
f
onto
\Omega
. Then
\bar{f}
can be expanded as in (
3.24
) with
f^{s}_{0}=0
and
\beta^{s}_{j}:=\langle\bar{f},e^{s}_{j}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
(see Lemma
3.8
). Moreover, by virtue of (
3.26
) and Lemma
3.9
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\bar{f},\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\bar{f}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\beta^{s}_{j})^{2}\frac{\mu_{j}^{s}}{\mu_{j}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{k}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}(\beta^{s}_{j})^{2}\mu_{j}^{s}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{k}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2},

(3.32)

where
\mathcal{L}_{s}=-\Delta-\lambda_{q}(q-1)|v(s)|^{q-2}
.

We observe that

\displaystyle J^{\prime}(v(s))

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Tay2}}}{{=}}\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi)+e(s)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{s}(v(s)-\phi)-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\left(|\phi|^{q-2}-|v(s)|^{q-2}\right)\left(v(s)-\phi\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e(s),

(3.33)

which implies that

\displaystyle v(s)-\phi

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{q}(q-1)\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\left(|\phi|^{q-2}-|v(s)|^{q-2}\right)\left(v(s)-\phi\right)\right]

\displaystyle\quad-\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left(e(s)\right).

(3.34)

We can derive from (
3.18
) that

\left\|\left(|\phi|^{q-2}-|v|^{q-2}\right)\left(v(s)-\phi\right)\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq c\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1},

(3.35)

where
\rho=\min\{1,q-2\}\in(0,1]
and
c
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
and
c(v)
given by (
3.4
). Thus by (
3.29
) of Lemma
3.10
and (
3.33
)–(
3.35
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\langle\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(v(s)-\phi),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\langle e(s),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{q}(q-1)\left\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\left[\left(|\phi|^{q-2}-|v(s)|^{q-2}\right)\left(v(s)-\phi\right)\right]\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad-\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}(e(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\langle e(s),v(s)-\phi\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+2}+\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}\right),

where
C
is a constant depending only on
q
,
C_{q}
,
c(v)
given by (
3.4
) and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}
(see Lemma
3.6
).

Therefore combining this and (
3.28
) along with (
3.30
), we deduce that

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)\leq\frac{1}{2}\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\left(\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+2}+\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}\right).

(3.36)

Since
J^{\prime}(v(s))\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
(see §
3.2
), it follows from (
3.32
) that

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)\leq\frac{1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\left(\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+2}+\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}\right).

(3.37)

Moreover, we find from (
3.29
) along with (
3.30
) again that

\displaystyle\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega);H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|e(s)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}.

Since
\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
is small enough, e.g., smaller than the constant
(2C\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))})^{-1/\rho}
, for
s\geq 0
large enough (see (
3.1
)), we get

\displaystyle\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}

(3.38)

for
s>0
large enough. Here we used the fact that
J^{\prime}(v(s))
vanishes on
Z(s)
and
J^{\prime}(v(s))|_{\Omega\setminus Z(s)}
lies on
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
(see (
3.6
)); hence
J^{\prime}(v(s))
coincides with the zero extension of
J^{\prime}(v(s))|_{\Omega\setminus Z(s)}
onto
\Omega
(see also Proposition
3.2
). Combining this with (
3.37
), we can take
s_{1}\geq s_{0}
large enough such that

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}

for
s\geq s_{1}
. Here the constant
C
eventually depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
,
c(v)
given by (
3.4
) and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}
(see Lemma
3.6
). This completes the proof.
∎

In particular, if
\phi
is a nondegenerate least-energy solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
), we can also take
s_{0}=s_{1}=0
whenever
v_{0}
lies on
\mathcal{X}
and is close enough to
\phi
in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Indeed, thanks to the stability result in
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, we can then observe that
\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
is small enough for any
s\geq 0
.

3.8.
Sharp rate of convergence

Combining Lemma
3.11
with (
3.8
), we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)^{-1}\left[J(v(s))-J(\phi)\right]

\displaystyle\leq-\dfrac{\text{\rm d}}{\text{\rm d}s}J(v(s))

(3.39)

for
s\geq s_{1}
. Recalling (
3.15
), we can take a constant
C
such that

\frac{1}{\nu_{k}^{s}}\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{k}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}

for
s\geq s_{1}
large enough (here and henceforth,
s_{1}
is replaced by such a large number). Indeed, by virtue of (
3.15
) and the mean-value theorem, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu^{s}_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1/\mu^{s}_{k}}{1-\lambda_{q}(q-1)/\mu^{s}_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1/\mu_{k}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}}{1-\lambda_{q}(q-1)/\mu_{k}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{k}}+C\left(\frac{\mu_{k}^{2}}{\nu_{k}^{2}}+1\right)\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}

for
s\geq s_{1}
large enough so that the denominator of the second line above is positive (see (
3.1
)).

Set
H(s):=J(v(s))-J(\phi)
for
s\geq 0
. It then follows that

\displaystyle\dfrac{\text{\rm d}H}{\text{\rm d}s}(s)+\frac{2\nu_{k}}{q-1}H(s)\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}H(s)\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq s_{1},

(3.40)

where
C
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
,
c(v)
given by (
3.4
),
\mu_{k}
and

\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}
(see Lemma
3.6
). Thus due to (
3.1
) for any
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
one can take a constant
C_{\lambda}>0
such that

0\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)\leq C_{\lambda}{\rm e}^{-\lambda s}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Here we also used the fact that
J(v(s))\leq J(v_{0})
for
s\geq 0
.

On the other hand, let
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
be fixed. In particular, if
\phi
is a nondegenerate least-energy solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
), we can then assure that
\sup_{s\geq 0}\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}
is small enough and take
s_{0}=s_{1}=0
, whenever
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\ll 1
(see
[
6
, Theorem 2]
); therefore we can obtain

0\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)\leq C_{\lambda}\left(J(v_{0})-J(\phi)\right){\rm e}^{-\lambda s}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Here we stress that
C_{\lambda}
can be chosen as a constant independent of
v_{0}
and
s
(when
\phi
and
v_{0}
fulfill the assumptions mentioned just above).

Now, we prove the following lemma:

Lemma 3.12
.

Assume that

0\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)\leq c{\rm e}^{-\lambda s}\quad\mbox{ for }s\geq 0

(3.41)

for some constants
\lambda>0
and
c>0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\lambda s}\quad\mbox{ for }s\geq 0.

(3.42)

In particular, let
\phi
be a nondegenerate
least-energy
solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). Then there exist constants
\delta>0
and
M\geq 0
such that

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq cM{\rm e}^{-\lambda s}\quad\mbox{ for }s\geq 0,

(3.43)

where
v
is the energy solution to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) with the initial datum
v_{0}
, provided that
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
,
\|v_{0}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}<\delta
and (
3.41
) holds.

To prove this lemma, recall an entropy functional
K:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

K(w)=\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{2}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|w|^{q-2}w\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \mbox{ for }\ w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),

which is another Lyapunov functional, that is,
s\mapsto K(v(s))
is nonincreasing for every energy solution
v=v(x,s)
to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) (see
[
6
, p.567]
). The following lemma provides a coercive estimate for the functional
G:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle G(w)

\displaystyle:=J(w)-\lambda_{q}K(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\lambda_{q}\|w\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{\lambda_{q}^{2}}{2}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|w|^{q-2}w\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. One can directly check that
G(\phi)=0
if
J^{\prime}(\phi)=0
, and
G(w)
will play a crucial role to prove Lemma
3.12
. Moreover, the following lemma may also be of independent interest.

Lemma 3.13

(Coercivity estimate for
G
near
\phi
)
.

For the functional
G
defined above, it holds that

\displaystyle G(w)=\frac{1}{2}\|J^{\prime}(w)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. As a corollary,
G(w)=0
if and only if
J^{\prime}(w)=0
. In addition, if
\phi
is a weak solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
)
(
that is,
J^{\prime}(\phi)=0
)
, and if
\phi
is nondegenerate, then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
there exists a constant
\delta_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle G(w)-G(\phi)\geq\frac{1-\varepsilon}{2}\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}^{-2}\|\nabla w-\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

(3.44)

provided that
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\|w-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}<\delta_{\varepsilon}
. In particular, if
K(w)-K(\phi)\geq-c\|\nabla w-\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
for some constant
c
satisfying
0<c<(2\lambda_{q})^{-1}\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}^{-2}
, then (
3.44
) further gives a strict coercive estimate for
J(w)-J(\phi)
.

Proof.

By direct computation, we have, for
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle G(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{q}^{2}}{2}\left\|\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(|w|^{q-2}w)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\lambda_{q}\int_{\Omega}|w|^{q}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|-\Delta w\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{q}^{2}}{2}\left\||w|^{q-2}w\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}-\lambda_{q}\langle-\Delta w,|w|^{q-2}w\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|-\Delta w-\lambda_{q}|w|^{q-2}w\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\|J^{\prime}(w)\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Next we write
J^{\prime}(w)=\mathcal{L}_{\phi}(w-\phi)-\lambda_{q}\mathcal{R}(w,\phi)
by using
J^{\prime}(\phi)=0
, where the residual term
\mathcal{R}(w,\phi)\in H^{-1}(\Omega)
is given by

\mathcal{R}(w,\phi):=|w|^{q-2}w-|\phi|^{q-2}\phi-(q-1)|\phi|^{q-2}(w-\phi)

(3.45)

and fulfills that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}(w,\phi)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\begin{cases}(q-1)\|w-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q-1}&\mbox{if }\ q\in(2,3),\\

\displaystyle\geq\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}^{-1}\|w-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\lambda_{q}C_{q}\|\mathcal{R}(w,\phi)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}.

one can take

small enough that

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{1-\varepsilon}\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}^{-1}\|\nabla w-\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

provided that

. Thus the latter assertion follows. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
An alternative proof with an
\varepsilon
-regularization

In the last section, in order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we derived (
3.39
) based on the spectral decomposition of
J^{\prime}(v(s))
in the associate space
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
of the weighted
L^{2}
-space
\mathcal{H}_{s}
(see §
3.1
and §
3.5
). To this end, we paid a careful attention to the singularity of the weight function
|v(s)|^{2-q}
of
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
on the set
Z(s)
of zeros of
v(s)
. In this section, instead of using the associate space
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
, we shall introduce an
\varepsilon
-approximation for the singular weight and derive (
3.39
) in another fashion.

4.1.
A modified energy inequality with an
\varepsilon
-regularization

Let us recall the relation used in the last section,

\displaystyle\left\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{4(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2}}\left\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},

(4.1)

the left-hand side of which is now approximated as

\left\langle\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for
\varepsilon>0
(then
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
will no longer appear in what follows). Here we note that

-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2}=(-\Delta)\circ\left(\varepsilon I+A^{s}\right),

which turns out to be a bijective and bounded operator from
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
into
H^{-1}(\Omega)
(see §
3.3
). It also follows that

\displaystyle(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left[(-\Delta)\circ(\varepsilon I+A^{s})\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\varepsilon I+A^{s}\right)^{-1}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}.

(4.2)

We claim that

\displaystyle{\left|\left\langle\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\right|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2}}\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

(4.3)

for
\varepsilon>0
; it will be used below instead of (
4.1
). Indeed, set
f=(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then we see that

-\varepsilon\Delta f+|v(s)|^{q-2}f=\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\ \mbox{ in }H^{-1}(\Omega).

Hence testing it by
f
, we have

\displaystyle{\varepsilon\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}|f|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),f\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\hskip-4.55244pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{time-deri-decomp}}}{{=}}\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\left\langle|v(s)|^{(q-2)/2}\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s),f\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\left(\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s),|v(s)|^{(q-2)/2}f\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\||v(s)|^{(q-2)/2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

whence it follows that

\||v(s)|^{(q-2)/2}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(4.4)

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle{\left|\left\langle\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\right|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{time-deri-decomp}}}{{=}}\frac{2(q-1)}{q}\left|\left(\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s),|v(s)|^{(q-2)/2}f\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right|

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{utestA}}}{{\leq}}\frac{4(q-1)^{2}}{q^{2}}\left\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thus, we have proved (
4.3
). On the other hand, using (
4.2
), we observe that

\displaystyle{\left\langle\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),(-\varepsilon\Delta+|v(s)|^{q-2})^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s),(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\partial_{s}(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2},

(4.5)

which may correspond to the
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}
-norm of
J^{\prime}(v(s))
in the last section. Hence combining (
4.3
) and (
4.5
) along with (
3.5
), we obtain the following modified energy inequality with the
\varepsilon
-regularization:

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{q-1}\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4(q-1)}{q^{2}}\left\|\partial_{s}(|v|^{(q-2)/2}v)(s)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eneq}}}{{\leq}}-\frac{\text{\rm d}}{\text{\rm d}s}J(v(s))

(4.6)

for a.e.
s>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
(cf. see (
3.8
)).

4.2.
Quantitative gradient inequality with the
\varepsilon
-regularization

We next derive a gradient inequality which better fit the present setting.

Lemma 4.1

(Quantitative gradient inequality with the
\varepsilon
-regularization)
.

There exist constants
s_{1}\geq 0
and

such that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{k}^{s}+1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}

(4.7)

for all
s\geq s_{1}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Here
\nu^{s}_{k}
denotes the smallest positive eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{s}
and
\mu^{s}_{k}=\nu^{s}_{k}+\lambda_{q}(q-1)

(
see Remark
3.5
)
and
\rho=\min\{1,q-2\}\in(0,1]
. Moreover, the constant
C
depends only on
q
,
C_{q}
,

given in (
3.4
) and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\phi}^{-1}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{-1}(\Omega),H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}

(

3.6
)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{s}=(-\Delta)\circ[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}]
is invertible for

large enough (see (i) of Remark

and therefore, we observe that

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\left[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right]^{-1}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s)),\left[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right]^{-1}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

\varepsilon I+A^{s}
is positive and self-adjoint in the Hilbert space

Therefore noting that

\displaystyle{\left(I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right)^{-1}}

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(\varepsilon I+A^{s})\circ\left[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right]^{-1}\circ(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2},

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v(s)),\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad(\varepsilon I+A^{s})\circ\left[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right]^{-1}\circ(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\big)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\left(f,(\varepsilon I+A^{s})\circ\left[I-\lambda_{q}(q-1)A^{s}\right]^{-1}f\right)_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon+\lambda_{j}^{s}}{1-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\lambda_{j}^{s}}\alpha_{j}^{s}(f)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon+\lambda_{k}^{s}}{1-\lambda_{q}(q-1)\lambda_{k}^{s}}\sum_{j\geq k}\alpha_{j}^{s}(f)^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{k}^{s}+1}{\mu_{k}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)}\|f\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}.

\displaystyle{\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{1/2}\circ(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{1/2}\|_{\mathscr{L}(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega))}\left\|(\varepsilon I+A^{s})^{-1/2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\circ J^{\prime}(v(s))\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

3.3

for

3.36

3.38

4.7
) from (
4.8
).
∎

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{GIe}}}{{\leq}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon\mu_{k}^{s}+1}{2\nu_{k}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)

5.

21

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\phi
be a nondegenerate

1.15

[
35

where

Lemma 5.1

By subtraction, one derives from (

Define

. Then we find that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{2}(v(s)-\phi)

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}\mathbb{P}_{2}\circ A(v(s)-\phi)

\displaystyle=\mu_{2}\mathbb{P}_{2}\circ(-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|\phi|^{q-2}(v(s)-\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left(w(s)-(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(s),\phi)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\left[w_{2}(s)-\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(s),\phi)\right)\right],

(5.5)

where
\mathcal{R}(\cdot,\phi)
is given by (
3.45
). Thus we infer that

\partial_{s}w_{2}(s)+\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}w_{2}(s)=\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(s),\phi)\right)

(5.6)

in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for a.e.
s>0
. Hence we obtain the formula,

\displaystyle w_{2}(s)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}w_{2}(0)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\int^{s}_{0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(s-\sigma)}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right)\,\text{\rm d}\sigma

(5.7)

in
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for
s\geq 0
. Here we note from (
5.3
) that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|(-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{q}\|\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{R-est}}}{{\leq}}C\|v(\sigma)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{H10-stbl}}}{{\leq}}C\left(J(v_{0,\varepsilon})-J(\phi)\right)^{\frac{\rho+1}{2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)\sigma},

where
\rho:=\min\{q-2,1\}\in(0,1]
. Moreover, we see that

\displaystyle w_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|v_{0,\varepsilon}|^{q-2}v_{0,\varepsilon}-|\phi|^{q-2}\phi\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\left[\mathcal{R}(v_{0,\varepsilon},\phi)+(q-1)|\phi|^{q-2}(v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{proj-rel}}}{{=}}\frac{q-1}{\mu_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{2}(v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi)+\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v_{0,\varepsilon},\phi)\right),

which along with (
5.2
) yields

\displaystyle\|w_{2}(0)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q-1}{\mu_{2}}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-\left\|\mathcal{R}(v_{0,\varepsilon},\phi)\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q-1}{\mu_{2}}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}-C\|v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{opt-hyp}}}{{>}}\frac{q-1}{2\mu_{2}}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Here we used the fact (see (
1.26
)) that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}c_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{-1}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}>0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\|v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}\leq C\varepsilon^{\rho+1}.

Therefore we infer that

\displaystyle\|w_{2}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}\|w_{2}(0)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\int^{s}_{0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(s-\sigma)}\left\|\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right)\right\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\,\text{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q-1}{2\mu_{2}}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}C\left(J(v_{0,\varepsilon})-J(\phi)\right)^{\frac{\rho+1}{2}}\int^{s}_{0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(s-\sigma)}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)\sigma}\,\text{\rm d}\sigma

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}\left[\frac{q-1}{2\mu_{2}}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{\nu_{2}\rho}C\left(J(v_{0,\varepsilon})-J(\phi)\right)^{\frac{\rho+1}{2}}\left({\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}\rho}{q-1}s}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{opt-hyp}}}{{\geq}}\frac{q-1}{4\mu_{2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}c_{\varepsilon}\|\mathbb{P}_{2}(\xi_{\varepsilon})\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

for
\varepsilon>0
small enough. Here we used the fact that

\displaystyle J(v_{0,\varepsilon})-J(\phi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v_{0,\varepsilon}-\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\lambda_{q}\int_{\Omega}|\phi|^{q-2}\phi\left(v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi\right)\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|v_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}+\frac{\lambda_{q}}{q}\|\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla v_{0,\varepsilon}-\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+o\left(\|v_{0,\varepsilon}-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\leq C\varepsilon^{2}.

The last inequality above follows from (
5.2
) and Lemma
5.1
. Thus recalling that

\displaystyle\|w_{2}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|w(s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\left\|(|v|^{q-2}v)(s)-|\phi|^{q-2}\phi\right\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{R}(v(s),\phi)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}+(q-1)\||\phi|^{q-2}(v-\phi)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)s}+C\left(\int_{\Omega}|v(s)-\phi|^{2}|\phi|^{q-2}\,\text{\rm d}x\right)^{1/2},

we conclude that (
1.27
) holds, that is, the rate of convergence (
1.23
) (and (
1.25
)) turns out to be optimal. Thus Theorem
1.4
has been proved.
∎

\displaystyle w_{2}(s)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}w_{2}(0)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\int^{s}_{0}{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(s-\sigma)}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right)\,\text{\rm d}\sigma

{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}s}
as

. We shall find an initial datum

for which the energy solution

\displaystyle w_{2}(0)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\left(|v_{0}|^{q-2}v_{0}-|\phi|^{q-2}\phi\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\left[(q-1)|\phi|^{q-2}(v_{0}-\phi)+\mathcal{R}(v_{0},\phi)\right]\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{proj-rel}}}{{=}}\frac{q-1}{\mu_{2}}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left(v_{0}-\phi\right)+\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v_{0},\phi)\right).

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\eta-\mathbb{P}_{2}(v_{0}-\phi)-\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v_{0},\phi)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\left(\frac{\mu_{2}}{q-1}\right)^{2}\int^{\infty}_{0}{\rm e}^{\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}\sigma}\mathbb{P}_{2}\left((-\Delta)^{-1}\mathcal{R}(v(\sigma),\phi)\right)\,\text{\rm d}\sigma

for

\|w_{2}(s)\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\lesssim{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)s}

(6.7)

\langle\bar{f},\mathcal{L}_{s}^{-1}\bar{f}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\beta^{s}_{j})^{2}\frac{\mu_{j}^{s}}{\mu_{j}^{s}-\lambda_{q}(q-1)},\quad\beta^{s}_{j}=\langle\bar{f},e^{s}_{j}\rangle_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\nu_{m}^{s}}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}+r_{2}(s),

(6.8)

\displaystyle|r_{2}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathbb{P}^{s}_{2})^{*}(J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathbb{P}^{s}_{2})^{*}\circ\mathcal{L}_{s}(v(s)-\phi)\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}^{2}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{\rho+1}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)s}.

Hence as in (

\displaystyle J(v(s))-J(\phi)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{2\nu_{m}^{s}}+C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}\right)\|J^{\prime}(v(s))\|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\prime}}^{2}+C{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)s}

(6.9)

for all

\displaystyle\dfrac{\text{\rm d}H}{\text{\rm d}s}(s)+\frac{2\nu_{m}}{q-1}H(s)

\displaystyle\leq C\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{\rho}H(s)+C{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{2}}{q-1}(\rho+1)s}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{2}}{q-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\right)s},

(6.10)

where
H(s):=J(v(s))-J(\phi)
, for
s\geq s_{1}
. In case
\nu_{2}\left(\rho/2+1\right)>\nu_{m}
, we immediately obtain

H(s)\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{m}}{q-1}s},

which together with Lemma
3.13
implies the desired conclusion. In case
\nu_{2}\left(\rho/2+1\right)<\nu_{m}
, it follows that

H(s)\leq C\left({\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{m}}{q-1}s}+{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu_{2}}{q-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\right)s}\right)\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Due to Lemma
3.13
, we obtain

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\left({\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{m}}{q-1}s}+{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\right)s}\right)\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Using the above improved decay estimate instead of the original one (see (
1.23
)) and repeating the argument so far, we can improve the estimates for
w_{2}
and then obtain

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C\left({\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{m}}{q-1}s}+{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{2}}{q-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{2}+1\right)^{2}s}\right)\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

Hence repeating this procedure (in finite time) and noting that
\rho>0
, we conclude that

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{m}}{q-1}s}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0.

(6.11)

In case
\nu_{2}\left(\rho/2+1\right)=\nu_{m}
, we can derive that

H(s)\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{2\nu}{q-1}s}

for any
\nu_{2}<\nu<\nu_{m}
. Hence we can also obtain (
6.11
) as in the last case. Thus we have obtained

Theorem 6.1

(Faster decay for well-prepared initial data)
.

Let
\Omega
be any bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with boundary
\partial\Omega
. Assume (
1.5
) and let
\phi
be a nondegenerate
least-energy
solution to (
1.14
), (
1.15
). Let
\nu_{m}>\nu_{2}
be the second positive eigenvalue of (
1.20
). Then there exists
v_{0}\in\mathcal{X}\setminus\{\phi\}
such that

\|v(s)-\phi\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{\nu_{m}}{q-1}s}\quad\mbox{ for }\ s\geq 0,

where
v=v(x,s)
is the energy solution to (
1.9
)–(
1.11
) with the initial datum
v_{0}
.

Appendix A
Proof of Lemma
3.3

In this appendix, we shall give a proof of Lemma
3.3
for the convenience of the reader. Due to (
3.10
), for each
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists
w^{s}_{0}\in E^{s}_{0}
such that

w=w^{s}_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}_{j}e^{s}_{j}\ \mbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\alpha^{s}_{j}:=(w,e^{s}_{j})_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}.

Hence, when
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1
, we have

\|w^{s}_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\alpha^{s}_{j})^{2}=1.

Then we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}_{i}\alpha^{s}_{j}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}e^{s}_{i}e^{s}_{j}\,\text{\rm d}x.

Here we used the fact that
|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{s}_{0}=0
a.e. in
\Omega
. Hence we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\alpha^{s}_{i}\alpha^{s}_{j}\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{i}}\int_{\Omega}\nabla e^{s}_{i}\cdot\nabla e^{s}_{j}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha^{s}_{j})^{2}}{\mu^{s}_{j}}.

(A.1)

Set
Y=\mathrm{span}\{e^{s}_{1},e^{s}_{2},\ldots,e^{s}_{j}\}
. Then we find from (
A.1
) that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}},

which implies

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\subset H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\\[0.0pt]
\dim Y=j\end{subarray}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\geq\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}.

(A.2)

We shall prove the inverse inequality. In case
j=1
, thanks to (
A.1
), we note that
\mu^{s}_{1}
can be characterized as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha^{s}_{j})^{2}}{\mu^{s}_{j}}\colon\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\alpha^{s}_{j})^{2}\leq 1\right\}=\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{1}},

which in particular implies the inverse inequality of (
A.2
) with
j=1
. In case
j\geq 2
, for each
j
-dimensional subspace
Y
of
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, one can take
w_{Y}\in Y
such that
\|w_{Y}\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1
and
(w_{Y},e^{s}_{i})_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0
for
i=1,\ldots,j-1
. Hence it holds that

\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-1}w_{Y}^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}};

whence it follows that

\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in Y\\[0.0pt]
\|w\|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}|v(s)|^{q-2}w^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq\frac{1}{\mu^{s}_{j}}

for
j\geq 2
. Thus the inverse inequality of (
A.2
) follows from the arbitrariness of
Y
. Therefore we obtain (
3.13
). The assertion (
3.14
) for
\mu_{j}
can be verified in the same fashion. ∎

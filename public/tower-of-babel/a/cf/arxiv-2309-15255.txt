1.
Introduction

Let
\mathscr{X}
be an arithmetic variety (integral, flat, projective scheme) over
\text{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
with smooth generic fiber. Let
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
be a possibly singular hermitian line bundle on
\mathscr{X}
, and for every integer
k\geq 1
, let
\overline{\mathscr{L}}^{\otimes k}
denote
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}
endowed with the product metric. Let
\|\cdot\|_{k,\infty}
denote the
L^{2}
norm on the global sections
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{C}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\otimes k}):=H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
with respect to a fixed smooth volume form on
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{C})
invariant under complex conjugation. The finite free
\mathbb{Z}
-modules
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})
endowed with
\|\cdot\|_{k,\infty}
are euclidean lattices.

A basic set of invariants of euclidean lattices are their successive minima. Recall that for
i=1,\dots,d_{k}
, where
d_{k}:=\text{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\ H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})
, the
i
th successive minima
,
\mu_{k,i}
, is the smallest real number
\mu
such that there exist vectors
s_{1},\dots,s_{i}\in H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})
that are linearly independent in
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and such that
\|s_{j}\|_{k,\infty}\leq\mu
for all
j
. The study of successive minima has a long history, beginning with Minkowski’s work on the geometry of numbers, to more recent works in Arakelov theory. From the perspective of Arakelov theory, it turns out to be more natural to work with
\lambda_{k,i}:=-\log(\mu_{k,i})
, called the
successive maxima
.

We are interested in the asymptotic distribution of the successive maxima of
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})
as
k\to\infty
. When the metric on
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
is smooth, or even continuous, the work of Chen in
[
Che10
]
shows the existence of a limiting distribution associated to the successive maxima of the sequence of lattices
H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})
. More precisely, if

\nu_{k}:=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\delta_{\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{k,i}},

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac delta function at a real number
x
, then Chen’s results (in particular, theorem 4.1.8 of
[
Che10
]
) show that the sequence of probability measures
(\nu_{k})_{k}
on
\mathbb{R}
converges weakly to a
compactly supported
probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
CGS18
]
, Chinburg, Guignard, and Soulé prove an analogous result when the metric on
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
is
logarithmically singular
(see
[
Küh25
]
definition 3.1, and
[
BGKK20
]
chapter 7). In particular, they consider
\mathscr{X}=\mathscr{X}(1)
an integral model of the modular curve
X(1)_{\mathbb{C}}
for
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
the line bundle of integral modular forms for the group
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weight
12
with the Petersson metric (see §3 of
[
CGS18
]
). As shown by Kühn in proposition 4.9 of
[
Küh25
]
, the Petersson metric on the line bundle of modular forms is logarithmically singular along the cusps and elliptic points. Due to these singularities, the
L^{2}
norms, i.e. the Petersson norms, are no longer defined on the spaces
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(1)_{\mathbb{C}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\otimes k})
of all modular forms of weight
12k
. But they are defined on the subspaces
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(1)_{\mathbb{C}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\otimes k}(-\infty))
of cusp forms (here
\infty\in\mathscr{X}(1)_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the unique cusp). In this setting, theorem 3.2.2 of
[
CGS18
]
describes the asymptotic behavior of the successive maxima of the lattices of integral cusp forms for the group
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weight
12k
with the Petersson norm as
k\to\infty
, in close analogy to Chen’s result mentioned above.

In this paper, we generalize theorem 3.2.2 of
[
CGS18
]
to cusp forms of any finite index subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. We state our results precisely in the following sub-section.

1.1.
Statement of main results

We follow the setup in §4.11 of
[
Küh25
]
. Let
\Gamma(1):=\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, and identify the complex modular curve
X(1)_{\mathbb{C}}
associated to
\Gamma(1)
with
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
via the modular
j
-function (see example
2.1
). Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
be a finite index subgroup. The modular curve
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
and the natural map
\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}:X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
are defined over a number field
E
(see §
2.3
). Let
X(\Gamma)
be a (smooth projective geometrically connected) model of
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
over
E
, let
\pi_{\Gamma,E}:X(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}
be the model of
\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}
over
E
, and let
\pi_{\Gamma}:X(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
denote the composition of
\pi_{\Gamma,E}
with the natural map from
\mathbb{P}_{E}^{1}
to
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
. Let
\infty\in\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
correspond to the unique pole of the
j
-function, and let
D\in\text{Div}(X(\Gamma))
denote the sum of the points in
\pi_{\Gamma}^{-1}(\infty)
. We call
D
the
divisor of cusps of
X(\Gamma)
.

Let
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
be
an arithmetic surface associated to
\Gamma
(see §
2.3
and §4.11 of
[
Küh25
]
). Then
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
is a regular projective arithmetic surface with generic fiber
X(\Gamma)
, and comes with a morphism
\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}}:\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
extending
\pi_{\Gamma}
. Let
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
denote the metrized line bundle on
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
associated to modular forms of level
\Gamma
and weight
12
endowed with the Petersson metric (see §
2.3
and §
2.4
, and §4.12 of
[
Küh25
]
). As mentioned above, this metric has logarithmic singularities at elliptic points and cusps (proposition 4.9 in
[
Küh25
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
be a finite index subgroup. Let
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
be an arithmetic surface associated to
\Gamma
,
X(\Gamma)
its generic fiber, and let
D
denote the divisor of cusps of
X(\Gamma)
. Let
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
be the line bundle on
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
associated to modular forms of level
\Gamma
and weight
12
endowed with the Petersson metric. For every
k\geq 1
, let

\mathscr{S}_{k}:=H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(\Gamma),\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})\cap H^{0}(X(\Gamma),\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}(-D))

denote the euclidean lattice of integral cusp forms of level
\Gamma
and weight
12k
with respect to the Petersson inner product. Let
\mu_{k,i}
denote the successive minima of
\mathscr{S}_{k}
, and let
\lambda_{k,i}:=-\log(\mu_{k,i})
denote the successive maxima. Let
d_{k}:=\emph{rk}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathscr{S}_{k}
, and let

\nu_{k}:=\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}\delta_{\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{k,i}}

denote the probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
associated to the normalized successive maxima of
\mathscr{S}_{k}
. Then the
\nu_{k}
converge weakly to a Borel probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
.
\nu
has support bounded above and unbounded below.

A natural question is: given finite index subgroups
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
of
\Gamma(1)
, how do the successive maxima of their integral cusp forms, and the associated limit measures compare? This is addressed by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
be finite index subgroups of
\Gamma(1)
. Let
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
) be an arithmetic surface associated to
\Gamma^{\prime}
(resp.
\Gamma
) with generic fiber
X(\Gamma^{\prime})
(resp.
X(\Gamma)
). Suppose there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-morphism
\pi_{\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma}:X(\Gamma^{\prime})\to X(\Gamma)
that is a model over
\mathbb{Q}
of the natural map
X(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\mathbb{C}}\to X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
of complex modular curves.

Let
\nu_{k}^{\prime}
(resp.
\nu_{k}
) denote the probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
associated to the normalized successive maxima of the euclidean lattice of integral cusp forms of level
\Gamma^{\prime}
(resp.
\Gamma
) and weight
12k
with respect to
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma^{\prime})
(resp.
\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
) as in theorem
1.1
. Suppose that
\nu_{k}^{\prime}\to\nu^{\prime}
(resp.
\nu_{k}\to\nu
) weakly for a Borel probability measure
\nu^{\prime}
(resp.
\nu
) on
\mathbb{R}
(by theorem
1.1
). Then there is a Borel probability measure
\omega
on
\mathbb{R}
such that

\nu^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\emph{deg}(\pi_{\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma})}\cdot\nu+\left(1-\frac{1}{\emph{deg}(\pi_{\Gamma^{\prime},\Gamma})}\right)\cdot\omega.

1.2.
Remarks

Remark 1.3
.

As mentioned before, theorem
1.1
generalizes theorem 3.2.2 (i), (ii) of
[
CGS18
]
, which addresses the case
\Gamma=\Gamma(1)
for the associated arithmetic surface
\mathscr{X}(1)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
(identification coming from the
j
-function). We remark that our overall approach is similar to that of
[
CGS18
]
, with a couple of key differences. First, the approach in
[
CGS18
]
uses various properties of
q
-expansions of modular forms for
\Gamma(1)
, including integrality of the coefficients of
q
-expansions of modular forms over the integral modular curve
\mathscr{X}(1)
to obtain (lower) bounds on the Petersson norms of integral cusp forms (see for instance, proposition 3.3.1, lemma 3.3.1, theorem 3.4.1, and lemma 3.4.2 in
[
CGS18
]
). For a general (in particular, non-congruence) finite index subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
, we do not have access to such integrality properties for the
q
-expansion coefficients of integral modular forms.

Instead, following
[
Che10
]
, we use results from the intersection theory of logarithmically singular line bundles on arithmetic surfaces as developed by Kühn in
[
Küh25
]
, along with height formulas developed by Bost, Gillet, and Soulé in
[
BGS94
]
to obtain corresponding (lower) bounds for the sup norms of integral cusp forms (see proposition
3.3
, which is the counterpart to lemma 3.4.2 in
[
CGS18
]
). We then appeal to a “Gromov’s lemma” type result in our log-singular setting (see proposition
3.2
, page 1 of
[
FJK13
]
, and theorem 1.7 in
[
AMM16
]
), comparing sup norms to
L^{2}
norms for sections in increasing powers of the line bundle of modular forms, to get analogous (lower) bounds on the
L^{2}
(i.e. Petersson) norms of integral cusp forms for
\Gamma
. The estimates obtained from the intersection theory approach, however, are not as sharp as those obtained from the
q
-expansions, and this ultimately prevents us from showing directly that the support of the limit measure
\nu
in theorem
1.1
is unbounded below. Instead, we deduce unboundedness (in corollary
4.4
) from theorem
1.2
along with the unboundedness in the case
\Gamma=\Gamma(1)
proved in part (ii) of theorem 3.2.2 of
[
CGS18
]
.

Another key difference with
[
CGS18
]
is that, unlike the
\Gamma=\Gamma(1)
case considered there, it is difficult to write down explicit bases for the spaces of cusp forms for general
\Gamma
, which are then used for getting bounds on the successive maxima. In proposition
3.9
, we construct less explicit bases that nevertheless have the same general shape as those in
[
CGS18
]
theorem 3.4.1 and lemma 3.4.4, and that turns out to be enough to yield the desired bounds on the successive maxima in our case.

Remark 1.4
.

An important feature of theorem
1.1
and theorem 3.2.2 of
[
CGS18
]
is the fact that the limit measures have support unbounded below, in stark contrast to Chen’s theorem for continuous metrics mentioned before, where the limit measure has compact support. This is explained, very roughly, as follows. When
\mathscr{L}
has continuous metric, then at least if
\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}
is ample,

\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k})

is a finitely generated quasi-filtered algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
(in the sense of definition 3.2.1 of
[
Che10
]
). The convergence result for the measures then follows from a more general result of Chen (theorem 3.4.3
[
Che10
]
) concerning the distribution of successive maxima for such algebras, which shows, in particular, that the limiting distribution has compact support.

In our setting, and that of
[
CGS18
]
, the Petersson metric on the line bundle
\mathscr{L}
of modular forms is logarithmically singular, and hence to make sense of the norms at the archimedean places, we restricted to the subspace
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}(-D))
of sections vanishing along the cusps. The graded
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra

B:=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}(-D))

is not finitely generated. However, we can “approximate”
B
by a sequence of finite-type quasi-filtered
\mathbb{Q}
algebras
B_{L}
indexed by integers
L
(as in §
3.2
, and §3.6 of
[
CGS18
]
). Then applying theorem 3.4.3 of
[
Che10
]
to each
B_{L}
, we get a sequence of compactly supported measures
\nu_{L,\infty}
, where the support of each
\nu_{L}
is bounded above by a constant independent of
L
, but the lower bound for the support goes to
-\infty
as
L\to\infty
, at least in the case
\Gamma=\Gamma(1)
considered in
[
CGS18
]
. We then show that the
\nu_{L}
converge to
\nu
as
L\to\infty
, which accounts for the support of
\nu
being unbounded below.

Remark 1.5
.

A natural question is whether theorem
1.1
generalizes to automorphic forms for other groups. More generally, we may ask the following general problem in the setting of
[
BM12
]
. Let
\mathscr{X}
be an arithmetic variety with regular generic fiber,
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
a hermitian line bundle on
\mathscr{X}
with logarithmic singularities along a normal crossings divisor
D\in\text{Div}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Fix a smooth volume form on
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{C})
invariant under complex conjugation. For each
k\geq 1
, let
\mathscr{S}_{k}:=H^{0}(\mathscr{X},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k})\cap H^{0}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathscr{L}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{\otimes k}(-D))
denote the euclidean lattice of sections of
\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}
that vanish along
D
equipped with the
L^{2}
norm for the given volume form. Then do the probability measures
\nu_{k}
associated to the (appropriately) normalized successive maxima of
\mathscr{S}_{k}
converge weakly to a probability measure on
\mathbb{R}
?

The case
\dim\mathscr{X}=2
(i.e., an arithmetic surface) is still interesting. One difficulty, even for
\dim\mathscr{X}=2
, is the lack of a Gromov’s lemma type result for general logarithmically singular line bundles, though some results are known with specific hypotheses on
\overline{\mathscr{L}}
(see, for instance,
[
AMM16
]
). We hope to come back to this problem in the future.

2.
Background on integral modular forms

In this section, we start with some background on complex modular forms and modular curves. Then, following
[
Küh25
]
, we define integral models for modular curves and the line bundle of modular forms in §
2.3
, and describe the Petersson metric on this bundle in §
2.4
. We then introduce the notion of adelic vector bundles, as in definition 3.1 of
[
Gau08
]
, their successive maxima, and apply these concepts to the space of rational cusp forms of weight
12k
for finite index subgroups of
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

2.1.
Modular curves and modular forms over
\mathbb{C}

Let
\Gamma
be a finite index subgroup of
\Gamma(1):=\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\Gamma
acts on the (complex) extended upper half plane
\mathfrak{h}^{*}:=\mathfrak{h}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
by linear fractional transformations

\Gamma\ni\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\cdot z=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}.

The quotient

X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}:=\Gamma\backslash(\mathfrak{h}\cup\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})).

is a compact Riemann surface called the
modular curve associated to
\Gamma
. The inclusion
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
induces a holomorphic map
\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}:X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}\to X(\Gamma(1))_{\mathbb{C}}
of Riemann surfaces of degree
[\Gamma(1):\Gamma]
.

For a point
z\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, let

\Gamma_{z}:=\{\tau\in\Gamma:\tau\cdot z=z\}

denote the stabilizer of
z
in
\Gamma
. This group is either trivial, finite, or infinite cyclic, and we call the image of
z
in
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
an ordinary point, elliptic point, or cuspidal point (or just a cusp) in these cases, respectively. For
\Gamma(1)
, the elliptic points are the images of
i
and
e^{2\pi i/3}
and there is a unique cusp corresponding to the image of (any) point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
. For a general
\Gamma
, the elliptic points and cusps of
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
map to elliptic points and cusp of
X(\Gamma(1))_{\mathbb{C}}
, respectively.

Given
\gamma\in\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, the
weight
k
slash operator

(\cdot|_{k}\gamma)
on holomorphic functions on
\mathfrak{h}
is given by

f(z)\mapsto(f|_{k}\gamma)(z):=(cz+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\right)

for
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. For
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
, a
meromorphic modular form of level
\Gamma
and weight
k
is a meromorphic function
f:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(a)

(f|_{k}\gamma)(z)=f(z)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, and

(b)

f
is meromorphic at the cusps of
\Gamma
. This means that at each cusp, if
t
denotes a local parameter, then
f
admits a Fourier expansion of the form
f(t)=\sum_{n}a_{n}t^{n}
, with
a_{n}=0
for sufficiently small
n
.

Meromorphic modular forms of weight
0
are called
modular functions
- these are simply the rational functions of
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
. A
modular form of weight
k
for
\Gamma
is a meromorphic modular form that is holomorphic everywhere, including at the cusps. A
cusp form of weight
k
for
\Gamma
is a modular form that is zero at every cusp (i.e., the
a_{0}
coefficient in the local Fourier expansion at every cusp is zero). The space of modular (resp. cusp) forms of level
\Gamma
and weight
k
is denoted by
M_{k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
(resp.
S_{k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
) - these are finite dimensional vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
.

Example 2.1
.

(i)

The
classical
j
-invariant function
is a modular function for
\Gamma(1)
given by

j(z)=\frac{1}{q}+744+196884q+\dots,

where
q=e^{2\pi iz}
is the local parameter at the unique cusp
S_{\infty}
of
X(1)_{\mathbb{C}}:=X(\Gamma(1))_{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that
j
has a unique pole of order
1
at
S_{\infty}
, so that the induced morphism
j:X(1)_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
is an isomorphism. Henceforth, we identify
X(1)_{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
via
j
.

(ii)

The
classical discriminant function

\Delta
is a cusp form of weight
12
for
\Gamma(1)
given by

\Delta(z)=q\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{24},

where as before,
q=e^{2\pi iz}
.

The functional equations satisfied by meromorphic modular forms show that they are sections of a line bundle
\mathfrak{M}_{k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
on
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
, and the meromorphic modular forms that are zero at every cusp form a sub-bundle
\mathfrak{S}_{k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
. The following is proposition 4.7 in
[
Küh25
]
:

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
be a finite index subgroup, and let
k\geq 1
be a positive integer. Let
S_{1},\dots,S_{t}\in X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the cusps, and let
\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}:X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}
denote the canonical map. Let
D_{\mathbb{C}}:=S_{1}+\dots+S_{t}\in\emph{Div}(X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}})
. Then we have an isomorphism of line bundles:

\mathfrak{M}_{12k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}\to\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}}(\infty)^{\otimes k}:f\mapsto f/\Delta^{k},

which restricts to an isomorphism

\mathfrak{S}_{12k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}\to\left(\pi_{\Gamma,\mathbb{C}}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}^{1}}(\infty)^{\otimes k}\right)(-D_{\mathbb{C}}).

2.2.
Petersson metric on
\mathfrak{M}_{12k}(\Gamma)

The
Petersson metric
on
\mathfrak{M}_{12k}(\Gamma)
is defined as follows. Given a section
f
of
\mathfrak{M}_{12k}(\Gamma)
over the open subset
U\subseteq X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
and a point
z\in\mathfrak{h}
corresponding to a point of
U
, we set

|f|_{\text{Pet}}(z)^{2}:=|f(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{12k}.

This differs from the classical Petersson metric by a factor of
(4\pi)^{12k}
. See
[
Küh25
]
p. 227-228 for the reason for this factor. This metric has
logarithmic singularities
in the sense of definition 3.1 of
[
Küh25
]
at the elliptic points and cusps of
\Gamma
(see proposition 4.9 in
[
Küh25
]
).

Let
d_{\Gamma}:=[\Gamma(1):\Gamma]
, and let
\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma}

2.5.
Adelic vector bundles, heights, and successive maxima

Let
K
be a number field, and let
\Sigma_{K}
denote the set of all places of
K
. For
v\in\Sigma_{K}
, let
\mathbb{C}_{v}
denote the completion of an algebraic closure of
K_{v}
. For
v
finite, let
|\cdot|_{v}
denote the norm on
\mathbb{C}_{v}
that extends the
p
-adic norm on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, where
p
is the rational prime lying under
v
. For
v
an archimedean place, we let
|\cdot|_{v}
denote the usual norm on
\mathbb{C}_{v}=\mathbb{C}
.

A finite dimensional
K
-vector space
V
is called an
adelic vector bundle over
K
if for each
v\in\Sigma_{K}
,
V\otimes_{K}\mathbb{C}_{v}
is equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
subject to the following conditions:

(i)

There exists a
K
-basis
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})
of
V
such that for all but finitely many finite places
v\in\Sigma_{K}
,

\|\alpha_{1}s_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{r}s_{r}\|_{v}=\max(|\alpha_{1}|_{v},\dots,|\alpha_{r}|_{v}).

(ii)

For every
v\in\Sigma_{K}
,
\|\cdot\|_{v}
is invariant under the action of the group
\textrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}_{v}/K_{v})
. Namely, if
(s_{1},\dots,s_{r})
is a
K_{v}
-basis of
E\otimes_{K}K_{v}
, and if
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\mathbb{C}_{v}
, then

\|\tau(\alpha_{1})s_{1}+\dots+\tau(\alpha_{r})s_{r}\|_{v}=\|\alpha_{1}s_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{r}s_{r}\|_{v}

for all
\tau\in\textrm{Gal}(\mathbb{C}_{v}/K_{v})
.

(iii)

For
v\in\Sigma_{K}
finite,
\|\cdot\|_{v}
satisfies
\|s+s^{\prime}\|_{v}\leq\max(\|s\|_{v},\|s^{\prime}\|_{v})
.

Let
V
be an adelic vector bundle over
K
. The
naive adelic height
function on
V
is given by

\displaystyle\lambda:V

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mapsto-\sum_{v\in\Sigma_{K}}k_{v}\log\|s\|_{v},

where
k_{v}:=[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]
for
p
the rational place lying under
v
. Using
\lambda
, we equip
V
with the following filtration: given
a\in\mathbb{R}
, set

V^{a}:=\text{span}_{K}\{s\in V:\lambda(s)\geq a\}.

This is a decreasing filtration on
V
indexed by the real numbers with the property that
V^{a}=V
for
a\ll 0
, and
V^{a}=0
for
a\gg 0
. If
\dim_{K}V\geq 1
, the
naive adelic successive maxima
of
V
are the real numbers
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\dim_{K}V}
, where

\lambda_{i}:=\sup\{a\in\mathbb{R}:\dim_{K}V^{a}\geq i\}.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose
\mathscr{V}
is a finite, free
\mathbb{Z}
-module of rank
d\geq 1
, equipped with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on the
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
. Then
V:=\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
has a natural structure of an adelic vector bundle over
\mathbb{Q}
as follows. For
v=\infty
, we use the given norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
, and for
v=p
finite, define
\|\cdot\|_{p}:V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.1)

\|s\|_{p}:=\inf_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}_{p}}\{|\alpha|_{p}:s\in\alpha\left(\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}R_{p}\right)\},

where
R_{p}
is the closed unit ball of
\mathbb{C}_{p}
. If
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}
denotes any
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathscr{V}
, then if we express
s\in V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}_{p}
as
s=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}s_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{p}
, we have
\|s\|_{p}=\max_{i}(|\alpha_{i}|_{p})
.

If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d}
denote the successive maxima for
V
with respect to the filtration induced by the naive adelic height as above, then

\lambda_{i}=-\log(\mu_{i}),

where
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{d}
are the successive minima for the lattice
\mathscr{V}\subseteq\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
is equipped with the norm induced from that on
\mathscr{V}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}
.

3.
Proof of theorem
1.1

A key ingredient in the proof of theorem
1.1
is theorem 3.4.3 in
[
Che10
]
concerning quasi-filtered graded algebras. We apply this result to the graded algebras
B_{L}
in proposition
3.6
. To do so, we need to get a uniform upper bound on the normalized height
\lambda_{k}(f)/k
of any non-zero
f\in S_{k}
, and show that the algebra
B_{L}
is
quasi-filtered
with respect to an appropriate function (see definition 3.2.1 of
[
Che10
]
).

The uniform upper bound is proved in §
3.1
. The key ingredients for this are formulas for the intersection numbers of logarithmically singular line bundles as in
[
Küh25
]
and heights of cycles with respect to a smoothly metrized line bundle as in
[
BGS94
]
(see proposition
3.3
), along with a version of “Gromov’s lemma” proved in
[
FJK13
]
for cusp forms with respect to the Petersson norm and later improved (and generalized) in
[
AMM16
]
(see proposition
3.2
). Proposition
3.2
is also key to lemma
3.5
, which in turn is used to show that the
B_{L}
are quasi-filtered. Using all of this, we show that the
\nu_{k}
converge vaguely to a sub-probability Borel measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, in §
3.3
, we derive explicit lower bounds on the successive maxima
\lambda_{k,i}
for
k
large by constructing a basis for
S_{k}
of a particular shape, and doing explicit calculations with it. These lower bounds then imply that
\nu
is a probability measure (and hence the vague convergence is also weak convergence).

We follow the general structure of §3.6 and §3.7 of
[
CGS18
]
in §
3.2
and §
3.3
.

Throughout this section, we keep the notation from §
2.6
.

3.1.
Upper bound on heights

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in S_{k}
be a non-zero element. There exists a rational number
\beta
such that
\beta f\in\mathscr{S}_{k}
and
\beta f\not\in B\cdot\mathscr{S}_{k}
for all positive integers
B\geq 2
. Furthermore, for any
f\in\mathscr{S}_{k}
such that
f\not\in B\cdot\mathscr{S}_{k}
for all integers
B>1
, we have
\|f\|_{k,p}=1
for all finite places
p
, and hence,
\lambda_{k}(f)=-\log\|f\|_{k,\infty}
.

Proof.

Take any
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}
of
\mathscr{S}_{k}
, and let
f=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}f_{i}
with
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then it is clear that there is a rational number
\beta
such that
\beta\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
and
\gcd(\beta\alpha_{1},\dots,\beta\alpha_{d})=1
. It is also clear that
\beta f\in\mathscr{S}_{k}
, and
\beta f\not\in B\cdot\mathscr{S}_{k}
for all integers
B>1
.

Finally given any
f\in\mathscr{S}_{k}
such that
f\not\in B\cdot\mathscr{S}_{k}
for all integers
B>1
, writing
f=\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}f_{i}
, we have
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d})=1
. By example
2.4
, we conclude that
\|f\|_{k,p}=\max_{i}(|\alpha_{i}|_{p})=1
for all finite places
p
.
∎

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
be a finite index subgroup, and let
S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the space of complex cusp forms for
\Gamma
of weight
2k
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\text{Pet}}
denote the Petersson inner product on
S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
as in §
2.2
. For
f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma)
, denote by

\|f\|_{\sup}:=\sup_{z\in\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{h}}|f(z)|(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{k}

the sup-norm of
f
with respect to the Petersson metric.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
be a finite index subgroup, and let
S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
denote the space of complex cusp forms for
\Gamma
of weight
2k
. There exist positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, with
c_{2}
independent of
\Gamma
, such that for any
0\neq f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

c_{1}\|f\|_{\emph{Pet}}\leq\|f\|_{\sup}\leq c_{2}k^{3/4}\|f\|_{\emph{Pet}}.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{\Gamma}
be the measure on
X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
induced from the hyperbolic volume form
\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}
on
\mathfrak{h}
with coordinate
z=x+iy
. For the first inequality, note that

\|f\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}=\frac{1}{d_{\Gamma}}\int_{X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}}|f(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}\ d\mu_{\Gamma}(z)\leq\|f\|_{\sup}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{d_{\Gamma}}\int_{X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}}d\mu_{\Gamma}\right),

so we may let
c_{1}=(\frac{1}{d_{\Gamma}}\int_{X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}}d\mu_{\Gamma})^{-1/2}
.

For the other direction, let
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis for
S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
for the Petersson inner product. Note that by our normalization of the Petersson inner product (i.e. the inclusion of the
(4\pi)^{2k}
factor),
\{(4\pi)^{k}f_{1},\dots,(4\pi)^{k}f_{d}\}
is an orthonormal basis for the classical Petersson inner product. Then, for
z\in\mathfrak{h}
, define

B_{k}^{\Gamma}(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|(4\pi)^{k}f_{j}(z)|^{2}\operatorname{im}(z)^{2k}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}

as in
[
AMM16
]
. Note that for any
0\neq f\in S_{2k}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}
, if
f=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}f_{j}
, then
\|f\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}
, and for any
z\in\mathfrak{h}
,

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}f_{j}(z)\right|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}\right)(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}.

Hence,

\frac{|f(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}}{\|f\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}}\leq B_{k}^{\Gamma}(z),

and

\frac{\|f\|_{\sup}^{2}}{\|f\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}}=\sup_{z\in\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{h}}\frac{|f(z)|^{2}(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{2k}}{\|f\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}}\leq\sup_{z\in\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{h}}B_{k}^{\Gamma}(z).

By theorem 1.7 in
[
AMM16
]
,

\sup_{z\in\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{h}}B_{k}^{\Gamma}(z)=\left(\frac{k}{\pi}\right)^{3/2}+O(k).

In particular, there exists a constant
c_{2}
(independent of
k
and
\Gamma
) which gives us the desired bound.
∎

We follow very closely the proof of lemma 4.1.7 in
[
Che10
]
for the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

There is a constant
C
such that for any
0\neq f\in S_{k}
,
\lambda_{k}(f)\leq Ck
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{k}(f)=\lambda_{k}(\alpha f)
for any non-zero
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, we may suppose, after appropriately scaling

, that
f\in\mathscr{S}_{k}
and
f\not\in B\cdot\mathscr{S}_{k}
for any positive integer
B>1
. Then by lemma
3.1
,
\lambda_{k}(f)=-\log\|f\|_{k,\infty}
.

Taking any projective (closed) embedding
\Phi_{\mathbb{Z}}:\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we let
\overline{L}:=\Phi_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(1)}
, where
\overline{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(1)}
refers to
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(1)
endowed with the Fubini-Study metric. Then
\overline{L}
is arithmetically ample with
c_{1}(\overline{L})>0
. Now take any global section
\ell\in H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(\Gamma),L)
such that
\text{div}_{L}(\ell)
and
\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f)
don’t share any common horizontal divisors (i.e. their divisors on the generic fiber

have disjoint support). Then the generalized intersection number
\overline{L}\cdot\overline{\mathscr{L}}^{\otimes k}
in the sense of equation (3.10) in
[
Küh25
]
is given by

\overline{L}\cdot\overline{\mathscr{L}}^{\otimes k}=(\ell\cdot f)_{\text{fin}}+\langle\ell\cdot f\rangle_{\infty},

where
(\ell\cdot f)_{\text{fin}}=(\text{div}_{L}(\ell)\cdot\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f))_{\text{fin}}
is equal to

(\text{div}_{L}(\ell)\cdot\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f))_{\text{fin}}:=\sum_{i,j=0}^{1}(-1)^{i+j}\log\#H^{i}\left(\mathscr{X}(\Gamma),\text{Tor}_{j}^{\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)}}(\mathcal{O}_{\text{div}_{L}(\ell)},\mathcal{O}_{\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f)})\right)

(
\mathcal{O}_{D}
denotes the structure sheaf for an effective divisor
D\subseteq\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)
), and
\langle\ell\cdot f\rangle_{\infty}
is given in our case by

\displaystyle\langle\ell\cdot f\rangle_{\infty}=-\sum_{P\in\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}n_{P}\log|\ell(P)|-\int_{\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}\log|f|_{\text{Pet}}\cdot c_{1}(\overline{L}),

if
\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}_{\mathbb{C}}}(f)=\sum_{P\in\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}n_{P}P
(lemma 3.9 in
[
Küh25
]
). Note that this expression makes sense since
\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}_{\mathbb{C}}}(f)
and
\text{div}_{L_{\mathbb{C}}}(\ell)
have disjoint support. Then, using bilinearity of the intersection pairing, we have

(\ell\cdot f)_{\text{fin}}-\sum_{P\in\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}n_{P}\log|\ell(P)|=k(\overline{L}\cdot\overline{\mathscr{L}})+\int_{\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}\log|f|_{\text{Pet}}\cdot c_{1}(\overline{L}),

where the expression on the left is the height
h_{\overline{L}}(\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f))
of the cycle
\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f)
with respect to
\overline{L}
, as defined in 3.1.1 in
[
BGS94
]
(see also §2.3.4 of
[
BGS94
]
, in particular, equation 2.3.17). Since
\overline{L}
is arithmetically ample and
\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f)
is effective,
h_{\overline{L}}(\text{div}_{\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}}(f))\geq 0
(proposition 3.2.4 in
[
BGS94
]
). Hence

0\leq k(\overline{L}\cdot\overline{\mathscr{L}})+\int_{\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}\log|f|_{\text{Pet}}\cdot c_{1}(\overline{L}),

and since
c_{1}(\overline{L})>0
, we get

\log\sup_{z\in\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}|f|_{\text{Pet}}(z)\geq C^{\prime}k

for
C^{\prime}:=-(\overline{L}\cdot\overline{\mathscr{L}})/\int_{\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}c_{1}(\overline{L})
.

Denote the image of
f
under the map
H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(\Gamma),\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}(-D))\to H^{0}(\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}},\mathscr{L}^{\otimes k}_{\mathbb{C}}(-D_{\mathbb{C}}))
by
(f^{\sigma})_{\sigma}
. Suppose
|f|_{\text{Pet}}(z)
achieves its supremum on the component

, so that

\sup_{z\in\mathscr{X}(\Gamma)(\mathbb{C})}|f|_{\text{Pet}}(z)=\sup_{z\in X(\Gamma_{\sigma^{\prime}})_{\mathbb{C}}}|(f^{\sigma^{\prime}}\Delta^{k})(z)|(4\pi\operatorname{im}(z))^{6k}=\|f^{\sigma^{\prime}}\Delta^{k}\|_{\sup}.

Then

\|f\|_{k,\infty}^{2}=\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{\sigma}\|f^{\sigma}\Delta^{k}\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\|f^{\sigma^{\prime}}\Delta^{k}\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}c_{2}^{-2}k^{-3/2}\|f^{\sigma^{\prime}}\Delta^{k}\|_{\sup}^{2},

where the last inequality uses proposition
3.2
. We conclude that

\lambda_{k}(f)=-\log\|f\|_{k,\infty}\leq Ck

for a constant

independent of
k
.
∎

3.2.

For an integer

). For

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\psi(k):=\frac{3}{4}\log(k)+\log(c_{2})-\log(c_{1})/2
where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are the constants from proposition
3.2
for
\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma(1)
. For any collection of elements
f_{i}\in S_{k_{i}}
(
i=1,\dots,n
, with
n\geq 2
), we have

\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{1}+\dots+k_{n}}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{i}}(f_{i})-\psi(k_{i})\right).

Proof.

Note that
c_{1}
was set to be
(\int_{X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{C}}}d\mu_{\Gamma})^{-1}
, which only depends on the index of
\Gamma
in
\Gamma(1)
. Since
[\Gamma(1):\Gamma]=[\Gamma(1):\Gamma_{\sigma}]
for all
\sigma
, we may use the same
c_{1}
for all the
\Gamma_{\sigma}
. Hence for any
f\in S_{k}
, proposition
3.2
gives

c_{1}^{2}\|f\|_{k,\infty}^{2}\leq\sum_{\sigma}\|f^{\sigma}\Delta^{k}\|_{\sup}^{2}\leq c_{2}^{2}k^{3/2}\|f\|_{k,\infty}^{2}.

Then for
f_{i}\in S_{k_{i}}
(
i=1,\dots,n
), letting
K:=k_{1}+\dots,k_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}\|f_{1}\dots,f_{n}\|_{K,\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma}\|(f_{1}\dots f_{n})^{\sigma}\Delta^{K}\|_{\sup}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma}\|f_{1}^{\sigma}\Delta^{k_{1}}\|_{\sup}^{2}\cdots\|f_{n}^{\sigma}\Delta^{k_{n}}\|_{\sup}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\sigma}\|f_{1}^{\sigma}\Delta^{k_{1}}\|_{\sup}^{2}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{\sigma}\|f_{n}^{\sigma}\Delta^{k_{n}}\|_{\sup}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{2n}k_{1}^{3/2}\cdots k_{n}^{3/2}\|f_{1}\|_{k_{1},\infty}^{2}\dots\|f_{n}\|_{k_{n},\infty}^{2},

and consequently,

\|f_{1}\dots f_{n}\|_{K,\infty}\leq e^{\psi(k_{1})+\dots+\psi(k_{n})}\|f_{1}\|_{k_{1},\infty}\cdots\|f_{n}\|_{k_{n},\infty}.

(We use the fact that
c_{1}<1
here.)

Pick any
\varepsilon>0
. By definition,
f_{i}\in S_{k_{i}}^{\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{i}}(f_{i})-\varepsilon/n}
, and hence
f_{i}=\sum g_{i,j}
for
g_{i,j}\in S_{k_{i}}
with
\lambda_{k_{i}}(g_{i,j})\geq\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{i}}(f_{i})-\varepsilon/n
. It is easy to see that for any finite place

,

\|g_{1,j_{1}}\cdots g_{n,j_{n}}\|_{K,p}\leq\|g_{1,j_{1}}\|_{k_{1},p}\cdots\|g_{n,j_{n}}\|_{k_{n},p}.

This combined with the previous paragraph yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{K}(g_{1,j_{1}}\cdots g_{n,j_{n}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\lambda_{k_{i}}(g_{i,j_{i}})-\psi(k_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{i}}(f_{i})-\psi(k_{i})\right)-\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

\widetilde{\lambda}_{K}(f_{1}\cdots f_{n})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\widetilde{\lambda}_{k_{i}}(f_{i})-\psi(k_{i})\right),

as required.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

With the notation above, the sequence of probability measures

Lemma 3.7
.

For every positive real number
\varepsilon
and bounded Lipschitz function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, there is a constant
L_{0}=L_{0}(\varepsilon,h)
such that for all
L\geq L_{0}
, and for all
k\geq 1
, we have

|\nu_{k}(h)-\nu_{L,k}(h)|\leq\epsilon.

Proof.

Let
|h|_{\text{Lip}}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h(x)|+\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R},x\neq y}\frac{|h(x)-h(y)|}{|x-y|}=M
. Recall that since
B_{L,k}
is equipped with the subspace filtration coming from
S_{k}
, the multi-set
\{\lambda_{L,k,i}\}_{i=1}^{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}B_{L,k}}
is a sub multi-set of
\{\lambda_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}S_{k}}
. Let
d_{L,k}:=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}B_{L,k}
and
d_{k}:=\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}S_{k}
. Then

\displaystyle|\nu_{k}(h)-\nu_{L,k}(h)|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{d_{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}h\left(\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{k,i}\right)-\frac{1}{d_{L,k}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{L,k}}h\left(\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{L,k,i}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{k}}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d_{k}}h\left(\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{i,k}\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{d_{L,k}}h\left(\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{i,L,k}\right)\right|+\left(\frac{1}{d_{L,k}}-\frac{1}{d_{k}}\right)\left|\sum_{i=1}^{d_{L,k}}h\left(\frac{1}{k}\lambda_{i,L,k}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{k}}(d_{k}-d_{L,k})M+\frac{d_{k}-d_{L,k}}{d_{k}d_{L,k}}d_{L,k}M

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{4}\left(\lceil k/L\rceil-1\right)}{d_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{4}}{L},

for some constant

Corollary 3.8
.

Proposition 3.10
.

The sequence of measures
(\nu_{k})_{k}
is uniformly tight, and hence
\nu
is a probability measure and the vague convergence
\nu_{k}\to\nu

Proof.

By proposition
3.3
, there is a constant
C
(which we may take to be positive) such that
\lambda_{k,i}/k\leq C
for all
k
and all
i
, meaning that the supports of the measures
\nu_{k}
are all contained in

. Hence for any positive real number

,
\nu_{k}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-a,C])=\nu_{k}((-\infty,-a))+\nu_{k}((C,\infty))=\nu_{k}((-\infty,-a))
. Thus, to show uniform tightness, it suffices to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a positive real number
a_{1}
and a positive integer
k_{1}
such that for all reals
a\geq a_{1}
and all integers
k\geq k_{1}
,
\nu_{k}((-\infty,-a))<\varepsilon
. (For each
i<k_{1}
, there is some compact set
K_{i}
such that
\nu_{i}(\mathbb{R}\setminus K_{i})<\varepsilon
. Letting
K^{\prime}=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{k_{1}-1}\cup[-a,C]
, we note that
\nu_{k}(\mathbb{R}\setminus K^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon
for all
k
.)

We keep the conventions from proposition
3.9
. Take any
k>k_{0}
. Given a positive real number
a
,

\nu_{k}((-\infty,-a))=\frac{\#\{i:\lambda_{k,i}/k<-a\}}{\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}S_{k}}.

We first restrict to counting only those
i
with
d_{0}+1\leq i\leq d_{0}+(k-k_{0}-1)d_{\Gamma}[E:\mathbb{Q}]
, noting that the remaining
i
’s contribute at most
d_{0}+d_{\Gamma}[E:\mathbb{Q}]
to the count (which is a constant independent of
k
). Then for
i
in the above range, proposition
3.9
gives

which implies that

for

. Hence,

Noting that

. This concludes the proof of uniform tightness.

To see that
\nu
is a probability measure, we note that by Prohorov’s theorem, uniform tightness implies that

admits a weakly convergent subsequence

. If

. Since

is a probability measure, we conclude that

is one as well. It is a standard result that if the limit measure is also a probability measure, then vague convergence is equivalent to weak convergence, and hence
\nu_{k}
converges weakly to
\nu
. Finally, it is clear that the support of

is contained in

∎

\textrm{Aut}(X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{Z},F}/X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{Z}})
. We denote by
(\cdot)^{\alpha_{j}}
the pullback map on sections induced by
\alpha_{j}^{*}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}\trianglelefteq\Gamma
be defined over
E
and suppose that
\pi:X(\Gamma^{\prime})\to X(\Gamma)
is Galois over
E
. Let
F/E
be a Galois extension of number fields. Then with the conventions of §
4.1
, we have

(a)

S_{k}^{a}\subseteq S_{k}^{\prime a}\cap S_{k}\subseteq S_{k}^{a-\log[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]}
, and

(b)

S_{k}^{a}\subseteq S_{F,k}^{a}\cap S_{k}\subseteq S_{k}^{a-\log[F:E]}

for all
a\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The first containment for both (a) and (b) is the content of lemma
4.2
. For the second containment, take any
f\in S_{k}^{\prime a}\cap S_{k}
(resp.
s\in S_{F,k}^{a}\cap S_{k}
). Then
f=\sum_{i}g_{i}
for
g_{i}\in S_{k}^{\prime}
with
\lambda_{k}^{\prime}(g_{i})\geq a
(resp.
s=\sum_{i}t_{i}
for
t_{i}\in S_{F,k}
with
\lambda_{F,k}(t_{i})\geq a
). Let
h_{i}:=\sum_{j}g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}}
(resp.
u_{i}:=\sum_{j}t_{i}^{\alpha_{j}}
). Then we claim that
h_{i}\in S_{k}
with
\lambda_{k}(h_{i})\geq a-\log[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma]
(resp.
u_{i}\in S_{k}
with
\lambda_{k}(u_{i})\geq a-\log[F:E]
) for all
i
, from which the conclusion follows.

First suppose
v=p
is a finite place. Given any
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\{b_{k}\}
of
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{k}
, if
g_{i}=\sum_{k}\alpha_{k}b_{k}
with
\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then
\|g_{i}\|_{k,p}^{\prime}=\max\{|\alpha_{k}|\}_{k}
. Now since
\tau_{j}
extends uniquely to
\textrm{Aut}(X(\Gamma^{\prime})_{\mathbb{Z}}/X(\Gamma)_{\mathbb{Z}})
, the pullback map
(\cdot)|_{\tau_{j}}:M^{\prime}_{k}\to M^{\prime}_{k}
restricts to an automorphism of the integral sections
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{k}
(see the remarks in §
2.3
). Hence
\{b_{k}|_{\tau_{j}}\}
is also a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{k}
, from which we conclude that
\|g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}}\|^{\prime}_{k,p}=\|g_{i}\|^{\prime}_{k,p}
for all
\tau_{j}
. Hence,

\|h_{i}\|_{k,p}=\|h_{i}\|^{\prime}_{k,p}=\left\|\sum_{j}g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}}\right\|^{\prime}_{k,p}\leq\max_{j}\|g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}}\|^{\prime}_{k,p}=\|g_{i}\|^{\prime}_{k,p}.

A similar argument shows that
\|u_{i}\|_{k,p}\leq\|t_{i}\|_{F,k,p}
.

Next, suppose
v=\infty
. First, we address (a). We have the base change diagram by
\sigma:E\to\mathbb{C}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X(\Gamma^{\prime})}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{j}}

where the top horizontal map
\gamma_{j}\in\textrm{Aut}(X(\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{C}}/X(\Gamma_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{C}})
corresponds to the automorphism of the modular curve
X(\Gamma^{\prime}_{\sigma})_{\mathbb{C}}
induced by the coset
\gamma_{j}\Gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}

(g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}})^{\sigma}=g_{i}^{\sigma}|_{\gamma_{j}}
(where

denotes the usual slash operator for modular forms, which in this case is simply pre-composition by
\gamma_{j}
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|h_{i}\|_{k,\infty}^{2}=\|h_{i}\|_{k,\infty}^{\prime 2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\sum_{\sigma:E\to\mathbb{C}}\|h_{i}^{\sigma}\Delta^{k}\|_{\text{Pet}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\frac{1}{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}\frac{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}{d_{\Gamma}}\sum_{\sigma}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}\sum_{j}\left|((g_{i}|_{\tau_{j}})^{\sigma}\Delta^{k})(z)\right|^{2}(4\pi y)^{12k}\ \frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{[E:\mathbb{Q}]}\frac{1}{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}\frac{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}{d_{\Gamma}}\sum_{\sigma}\int_{\mathcal{F}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}\sum_{j}\left|((g_{i}^{\sigma}|_{\gamma_{j}})\Delta^{k})(z)\right|^{2}(4\pi y)^{12k}\ \frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}{d_{\Gamma}}\right)^{2}\|g_{i}\|_{k,\infty}^{\prime 2}.

where we use Cauchy-Schwartz inequality in the second line, and the invariance of the Petersson inner product with respect to the slash operator for modular forms in the fourth line. We conclude that

\lambda_{k}(h_{i})=-\sum_{v}\log\|h_{i}\|_{k,v}\geq-\sum_{v}\log\|g_{i}\|_{k,v}^{\prime}-\log\left(\frac{d_{\Gamma^{\prime}}}{d_{\Gamma}}\right)=\lambda^{\prime}_{k}(g_{i})-\log[\Gamma^{\prime}:\Gamma].

Finally, we address (b). For each

Abstract
We study the one-loop effective action of the higher-spin gauge theory induced by the IKKT matrix model on a ℳ 1 , 3 × 𝒦 \mathcal{M}^{1,3}\times\mathcal{K} background, where ℳ 1 , 3 \mathcal{M}^{1,3} is an FLRW cosmological spacetime brane and 𝒦 \mathcal{K} are compact fuzzy extra dimensions. In particular, we show that all non-abelian ( 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} -valued) gauge fields in this model acquire mass via quantum effects, thus avoiding no-go theorems. This leads to a massive non-abelian quantum 𝔥 ​ 𝔰 \mathfrak{hs} -Yang-Mills theory, whose detailed structure depends on 𝒦 \mathcal{K} . The stabilization of 𝒦 \mathcal{K} at one loop is understood as a result of the coupling between 𝒦 \mathcal{K} and the U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) -flux bundle on space-time. This flux stabilization induces the KK scale into the 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM sector of the model, which break superconformal symmetry.

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(4)}[\alpha]=\text{Tr}_{V_{(4)}}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}\,\alpha\Sigma^{(4)}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},-]}\Big)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(2,\mp)}[\alpha]=\text{Tr}_{\Psi_{(2,\mp)}}\Big(e^{\mathrm{i}\,\alpha\Sigma^{(2,\mp)}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},-]}\Big)\,.

(4.9)

The bosonic and fermionic contributions (

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes V}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}-\Sigma^{(V)}_{\boldsymbol{I}\boldsymbol{J}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{I}\boldsymbol{J}},-])}\Big)

\displaystyle=\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes V_{(6)}}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}-\Sigma^{(6)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\Big)+\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(4)}[\alpha]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha\Box_{6}}\Big)\,,

(4.10a)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Psi}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}-\Sigma^{(\Psi)}_{\boldsymbol{I}\boldsymbol{J}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{I}\boldsymbol{J}},-])}\Big)

\displaystyle=+\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Psi_{(4+)}}\Big(\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(2+)}[\alpha]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}-\Sigma^{(4+)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\,+\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Psi_{(4-)}}\Big(\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(2-)}[\alpha]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}-\Sigma^{(4-)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\Big)\,.

(4.10b)

For the specific background
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}\times\mathcal{K}
in the last section, we can evaluate these traces in the basis of open-string modes on the branes, cf.
[

,
6

.
It is convenient to first carry out the trace over
\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})
by using (

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\mathcal{O}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!d^{4}y\sqrt{G}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{\sqrt{G}}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}\otimes V}(\mathcal{O})\,,

(4.11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{1,3}+m_{s}^{2}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)}f(y,\hat{Y})

(4.12)

is some
\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}
-valued operator on
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}
.

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{M}})\otimes\mathfrak{hs}}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{1,3}+m_{s}^{2}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon)}f(y,\hat{Y})\Big)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{4}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\sqrt{G}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{\sqrt{G}}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{hs}}\Big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(k\cdot k+m_{s}^{2})}f(y,\hat{Y})\Big)

(4.13)

where

\displaystyle\Box_{1,3}=k\cdot k\,,\hskip 20.00003ptk\cdot k=k_{\mu}k_{\nu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}=\rho^{2}k_{\mu}k_{\nu}G^{\mu\nu}\,

(4.14)

arises from approximate plane waves on
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}

As a result, the 1-loop effective action can be written as follows

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{1-loop}}[T]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\alpha}{\alpha}\kappa[\alpha]\,\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{hs}}\Bigg[\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes V_{(6)}}\big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2}-\Sigma^{(6)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\pm}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Psi_{(4\pm)}}\big(\chi_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}^{(2\pm)}[\alpha]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2}-\Sigma^{(4\pm)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\big)-2\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\big(e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})}\big)\Bigg]\,

(4.15)

where
\kappa[\alpha]
, cf. (
A.10
), is obtained from carrying out the integral over
k
using (
A.9
).
This formula is exact under the stated assumptions;
note that the terms at leading order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{0})
under the bracket cancel due to maximal SUSY. Furthermore,
the

5
Quantum
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM at one loop

We compute in this section the
effective coupling of the
\mathfrak{hs}
-Yang-Mills gauge theory at one loop. We also establish some requirements for obtaining a near-realistic gauge theory from the IKKT model on the type of background under consideration.

As we will focus on the Yang-Mills term, which is quadratic in
\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}
, we can expand the
SO(1,3)
characters (
4.9
)
using the following trace formulas for the
\mathfrak{so}(1,3)
generators

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{V_{(4)}}\Big(\Sigma^{V_{(4)}}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}\Sigma^{V_{(4)}}_{\dot{\rho}\dot{\sigma}}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\big(\eta_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\rho}}\eta_{\dot{\nu}\dot{\sigma}}-\eta_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\sigma}}\eta_{\dot{\nu}\dot{\rho}}\big)\,,

(5.1a)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{V_{(6)}}\Big(\Sigma^{V_{(6})}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}\Sigma^{V_{(6)}}_{\mathtt{M}\mathtt{N}}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\big(\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\mathtt{M}}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}\mathtt{N}}-\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\mathtt{N}}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}\mathtt{M}}\big)\,,

(5.1b)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\Psi_{(2,\mp)}}\Big(\Sigma^{\Psi_{(2,\mp)}}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}\Sigma^{\Psi_{(2,\mp)}}_{\dot{\rho}\dot{\sigma}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\eta_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\rho}}\eta_{\dot{\nu}\dot{\sigma}}-\eta_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\sigma}}\eta_{\dot{\nu}\dot{\rho}})\,,

(5.1c)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\Psi_{(4,\pm)}}\Big(\Sigma^{\Psi_{(4,\pm)}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}\Sigma^{\Psi_{(4,\pm)}}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{K}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{L}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{K}}}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{L}}}-\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{L}}}\delta_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{K}}}\,,

(5.1d)

where we have also included the
\mathfrak{so}(6)
case for later use. This gives

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{M}}^{(4)}[\alpha]

\displaystyle=4-2\alpha^{2}[\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},]]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})\,,

(5.2a)

\displaystyle\chi_{\mathcal{M}}^{(2,\mp)}[\alpha]

\displaystyle=2-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}[\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},]]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})\,.

(5.2b)

Then the contribution to the Yang-Mills action obtained from

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{1-loop}}^{\text{YM}}[T]:=\Gamma_{\text{1-loop}}[T]\Big|_{\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}^{2}}

(5.3)

reads

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{1-loop}}^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}[T]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2(2\pi)^{4}}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha\,\alpha\,\kappa[\alpha]\,\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{hs}}\Big[-2\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big([\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},-]]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\pm}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\Psi_{(4\pm)}}\Big([\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},-]]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2}-\Sigma^{(4\pm)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}\Big)\Big]\,.

(5.4)

To proceed, we shall assume that the background
\mathcal{K}
is slowly varying so that
[\Box_{6},\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}]=0
. Expanding

\displaystyle\text{Tr}_{\Psi_{(4\pm)}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2}-\Sigma^{(4\pm)}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-])}=e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})}\Big(4-\alpha^{2}[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-]]+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})\Big)\,

(5.5)

in the UV regime and plugging this back to (
5.4
), we observe that the leading contributions at order
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{0})
and
\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})

cancel, thanks to maximal SUSY. The first non-trivial contribution starts at order
\alpha^{4}
, given by

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}

\displaystyle=+\frac{1}{4(2\pi)^{4}}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha\,\alpha^{3}\kappa[\alpha]\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}}\Big([\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},]]\,[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})}\Big)

(5.6)

dropping higher order contributions in
\mathcal{F}
.
Using (
A.10
), we learn that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\alpha\,\alpha e^{-\mathrm{i}\alpha(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})}=-\frac{1}{(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\,.

(5.7)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\!\!\int_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}}\Bigg([\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},.]]\,[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]\frac{1}{(\Box_{6}+m_{s}^{2})^{2}}\Bigg)\,.

The above can be simplified to

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{hs}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Box_{6}^{2}}[\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}},.]][\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]\Big)\,

(5.8)

under the assumption that
\Box_{6}\gg m_{s}^{2}
.
Here, the trace over
\mathfrak{hs}
-modes in the loop can be performed trivially. In particular, since
\hat{Y}
are effectively commutative at late times, the trace
\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{hs}}
over the higher spin modes simply gives a factor
\mathtt{J}^{2}

for large
\mathtt{J}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}\approx-\frac{\mathtt{J}^{2}}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\sum_{s,m}\!\!\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\!\!\sqrt{G}\rho^{-4}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\Box_{6}^{2}}[\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu};sm},[\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu};sm},.]][\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]\Bigg)\,.

(5.9)

Given that
\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu};sm}=\mathcal{F}_{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu};sm\,\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha}
, we can use (
3.7
) to write the above as

\displaystyle\boxed{\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}=-\frac{\mathtt{J}^{2}}{4(4\pi)^{2}}\sum_{s,m}\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\sqrt{G}\,G^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}G^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\langle F_{\mu\nu;sm},F_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}{}^{sm}\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}}\,

(5.10)

where

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}:=\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Box_{6}^{2}}[\lambda,[\mu,.]]\,[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]\Big)

(5.11)

is a modified inner product for any
\lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{g}
. This inner product is non-negative due to the positivity of the operator
[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]
, and, in general, not
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant for non-trivial/reducible
\mathcal{K}
.

On separable branes.

Before working out some explicit examples, it is important to recall that if
\mathcal{K}
is non-trivial, there will be spontaneous breaking of
\mathfrak{g}
symmetry (or
\mathfrak{g}
-invariance). In particular, consider
a configuration given by a set of separable branes
\mathcal{K}_{i}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}\,.

(5.12)

Figure 2
:
Open string modes
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i}
start and end on the same brane
\mathcal{K}_{i}
, while
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i,j}
stretch between
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}
branes.

In this case, the associated matrix algebra decomposes as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}})\oplus\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}^{*}\big)\equiv\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\neq j}\big(\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}^{*}\big)\,.

(5.13)

As a result, the potentials between different pair of branes can be computed by considering the appropriate Hilbert spaces. The decomposition (
5.13
) leads to two distinct types of
fluctuations. On the one hand, when the fluctuations
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i}
taking values in
\mathfrak{g}_{i}
, they can be interpreted as open strings starting and ending on the same brane
\mathcal{K}_{i}
. On the other hand,
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i,j}
taking values in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}^{*}
can be interpreted as open strings stretching between two different branes
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}
. In that case, the fluctuation
\mathcal{A}^{\boldsymbol{I}}_{i,j}
takes values in the bi-fundamental representation
(d_{\mathcal{K}_{i}},\bar{d}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}})
of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}^{*}
.

Using the decomposition (
5.13
), the
inner product
\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
acquires various contributions from diagonal and off-diagonal blocks.
This can be evaluated quite concretely for
\mathcal{F}^{\dot{\mu}\dot{\nu}}
taking values in the unbroken gauge sector

\mathfrak{y}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, i.e. the commutant of
\mathcal{K}
, with generators
\lambda
.

\symknight

Scenario A: Point branes.
Consider the case where
\mathcal{K}
consists of
k
coinciding point branes.
Then the unbroken gauge sector gets enhanced to

\displaystyle\mathfrak{y}=\mathfrak{su}(k)\supset\mathfrak{u}(1)_{1}\times\ldots\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{k}

(5.14)

leading to an unbroken
\mathfrak{su}(k)
Yang-Mills gauge sector, which may be interpreted as color.

Figure 3
:
Coincident point branes, connected by open strings.

Furthermore,
\Box_{6}=0
and
[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},-]]=0
,
so that
\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop}}(\text{point branes})=0\,.

(5.15)

This is to be expected, since there is no one-loop renormalization of the Yang-Mills coupling in the ordinary
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, i.e. when
\mathcal{K}
is trivial.
Thus, the associated coupling of
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM is given by the bare coupling (
3.14
).

\symknight

Scenario B: A stack of point branes and a bulk brane.
Now consider a stack of (coinciding) point branes denoted as

\mathcal{K}_{k}=\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
and a large brane
\mathcal{K}_{j}
. Generalizing the previous case, the unbroken gauge algebra is now
\mathfrak{y}=\mathfrak{u}(k)
. We can then evaluate
\langle.,.\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
as

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda\mu)\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for}\ \ \lambda,\mu\in\mathfrak{y}\cong\mathfrak{su}(k)\,,

(5.16)

which is proportional to the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
up to some constant

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}=\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Box_{6}^{2}}[\lambda,[\lambda,.]]\,[\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},[\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}},.]]\Big)>0\,,

(5.17)

We will use the normalization
\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda\lambda)=2
and choose an adapted Gell-Mann basis
\lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}
which include the generators in
\mathfrak{y}\cong\mathfrak{su}(k)
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{\alpha},\lambda^{\beta}\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}\delta^{\alpha\beta}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{for}\ \ \lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{y}\,.

(5.18)

Due to string modes linking point branes with
\mathcal{K}_{j}
,
there is a non-trivial contribution to
\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}
.
To compute this, we take some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{y}
as above, and
evaluate the contribution from string modes

\Upsilon=|i\rangle\langle z|
connecting the point
i\in\mathcal{K}_{k}
with

\mathcal{K}_{j}
through a coherent state
|z\rangle
on
\mathcal{K}_{j}
. Since
\lambda
act only on
|i\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle[\lambda,[\lambda,\Upsilon]]=\lambda\lambda\Upsilon\ .

(5.19)

The integral over the string modes
\Upsilon

in the geometric trace formula (
2.36
) amounts to an integral over points
z\in\mathcal{K}_{j}
and a sum over discrete points
i\in\mathcal{K}_{k}
. The latter can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\langle i|[\lambda,[\lambda,.]]|i\rangle=\sum_{i}\langle i|\lambda\lambda|i\rangle=\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda\lambda)=2\,.

(5.20)

:
A stack of
k
point branes interact with a bulk brane via open strings.

Now the trace over the large brane
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}
can be evaluated using (
2.36
) if the geometry of
\mathcal{K}_{j}
is known explicitly, which gives

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}\

\displaystyle\sim\ 2\,\int_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}\frac{\Omega_{z_{j}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{|\mathcal{K}_{j}|}{2}}}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{j})\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{j})}{(m^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}z_{j}^{2}+\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{K}})^{2}}\,.

(5.21)

Note that we integrate over
dimensionless
variables, and all scales
(\Delta_{\mathcal{K}},m_{\mathcal{K}})
are exhibited explicitly. It is also useful recalling that
z^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
and
m_{\mathcal{K}}=d_{\mathcal{K}}^{1/|\mathcal{K}|}\Delta_{\mathcal{K}}
is the curvature radius of the branes, and
\int\Omega_{\mathcal{K}}=d_{\mathcal{K}}
from the Bohr-Sommerfeld quantization condition (
2.27
).

Putting this together, the unbroken
\mathfrak{su}(k)
-sector contributes an amount

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathfrak{hs}-\text{YM}}_{\text{1-loop},\mathfrak{y}}=-\frac{\mathtt{J}^{2}\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}}{2(4\pi)^{2}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{M}^{1,3}}\sqrt{G}\,G^{\mu\mu^{\prime}}G^{\nu\nu^{\prime}}F_{\mu\nu;\alpha},F_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}{}^{\alpha}\,

(5.22)

to the total one-loop effective action of the
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM theory. We can compute
\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}
quite explicitly
using (
5.21
) and assuming
\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}\approx const
of order
\mathcal{O}(\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{K}})
. In particular,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{j}|=2

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{d_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}}{2(1+d_{\mathcal{K}_{j}})}\approx\frac{1}{2}\,,

(5.23a)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{j}|=4

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}

\displaystyle\approx\log(d_{\mathcal{K}_{j}})>0\,,

(5.23b)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}_{j}|=6

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{su}(k)}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{d_{\mathcal{K}}^{1/3}}{2}-\log(d_{\mathcal{K}_{j}})>0\,

(5.23c)

for large
d_{\mathcal{K}_{j}}
.

\symknight

Scenario C: Large (extended) branes.
Now consider the case where
\mathcal{K}
consists of a number of large branes
\mathcal{K}_{i}
. For simplicity, we restrict ourselves to the case of two large branes
\mathcal{K}_{1},\mathcal{K}_{2}
with associated Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}
cf. (
5.12
).
This sector receives significant contributions due to the long/heavy open string modes
\Upsilon=|z_{1}\rangle\langle z_{2}|\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}^{*}
linking
\mathcal{K}_{1}
with
\mathcal{K}_{2}
for
|z_{i}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}
being coherent states on each brane.

The unbroken gauge group in this case is generated by
\mathfrak{y}=\mathfrak{u}(1)_{1}\times\mathfrak{u}(1)_{2}
,
where the subscripts indicate the associated branes. Note that the inner product
\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}
cf.
(
5.11
) for
\lambda=\,\mathrm{diag}(\mbox{1 \kern-5.89996pt{\rm l}}_{1},-\mbox{1 \kern-5.89996pt{\rm l}}_{2})\in\mathfrak{y}
can be evaluated as

\displaystyle[\lambda,|z_{1}\rangle\langle z_{2}|]=2|z_{1}\rangle\langle z_{2}|\,.

(5.24)

Furthermore, unlike scenario B), the normalization for the
\lambda
generators in this case is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda\lambda)=d_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}+d_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}\,.

(5.25)

Analogously to (
5.17
), we write

\displaystyle\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle_{\mathcal{K}}=\frac{1}{2}\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{2}}\mathrm{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\lambda\lambda)\,.

(5.26)

Then the geometric trace formula yields

\displaystyle\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}^{\mathcal{K}_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{2}}\sim\frac{8}{d_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}+d_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}}\int\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{1}\times\mathcal{K}_{2}}\frac{\Omega_{z_{1}}\Omega_{z_{2}}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{|\mathcal{K}_{1}|+|\mathcal{K}_{2}|}{2}}}\frac{(\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{1})-\mathcal{F}_{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{2}))(\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{1})-\mathcal{F}^{\boldsymbol{\mathtt{I}}\boldsymbol{\mathtt{J}}}(z_{2}))}{\big(m^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}+\Delta^{2}_{\mathcal{K}}\big)^{2}}\,.

(5.27)

Since
\vartheta_{\mathfrak{y}}^{\mathcal{K}_{i}-\mathcal{K}_{j}}
will be large as
\mathcal{K}_{i}
and
\mathcal{K}_{j}
are large, the effective couplings (
5.31
) associated with this sector will typically be suppressed in the low-energy regime.

\symknight

Scenario D: Self-intersecting branes.
The formula (

) can essentially be used also for self-intersecting branes such as squashed
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{*}
cf.
[
53
]
,
where the intersecting sheets of these type of branes play (approximately) the role of different

7.3

\mathcal{K}

Given the above results, we can try to address the important problem of how to stabilize
\mathcal{K}

\displaystyle V_{\rm eff}[\mathcal{K}]

\displaystyle\approx\,V_{0}[\mathcal{K}]+V^{\text{1-loop}}_{\mathcal{K}}[\mathcal{K}]+V^{\text{1-loop}}_{S^{2}}+V^{\text{1-loop}}_{S^{2}-\mathcal{K}}

8
Discussion

This work contains several significant new results, which should provide the basis for further investigations of the physics in the matrix model framework.
The first important (but rather disappointing) result is the mechanism described in Section
6
, which induces mass for all (unbroken)
\mathfrak{hs}
-valued gauge fields from quantum effects in the presence of
\mathcal{K}
. While this mechanism allows
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM to avoid no-go theorems
[
50
,
51
]
, it does not lead to near-realistic physics
33
33

33

One possibility to circumvent this issue is to consider the minimal quantum space with
\mathtt{J}=0
. This will be elaborated elsewhere.
. Note, however, that the time-dependent effective couplings of
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM at one loop do show reasonable behavior (cf. Fig.
6
).

A further important result is the scalar potential at one loop, which allows for the stabilization of
\mathcal{K}
. This is also essential for obtaining an induced Einstein-Hilbert term and gravity in the present framework, along the lines of
[
5
,
6
,
9
]
.
It turns out that the scalar potential indeed acquires
a non-trivial minimum for a non-vanishing radius of suitable
\mathcal{K}
, with scale inherited from the non-commutativity scale of the
cosmological space-time
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}
.
The methods developed here provide the basis to determine the dynamically preferred structure of the compact extra dimensions
\mathcal{K}
, which should be elaborated in future work.
The crucial point we want to emphasize here is that the superconformal symmetry of the
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM sector in
\mathfrak{hs}
-YM is broken by
the quantum spacetime. This provides an essential ingredient for obtaining interesting physics.

Finally, we have computed the 1-loop corrections to the Yang-Mills coupling constants. All of these calculations rely crucially on a basic trace formula based on coherent states, which provides a suitable way to perform loop computations, despite the complexity of the model under consideration.
These techniques will certainly be useful also in other contexts, and provide an important step towards identifying more realistic backgrounds in the matrix model.

Some further comments are in order, to clarify the relation of the present work with other approaches. The UV finiteness of the higher-spin gauge theory induced by the IKKT matrix model
on the background brane
\mathcal{M}^{1,3}\times S^{2}_{\mathtt{J}}\times\mathcal{K}
is essential for the loop computations, based on to maximally supersymmetry and the fuzzy nature of
\mathcal{K}
. This is quite different with standard massless higher-spin gravities, see e.g.
[
55
,
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
where one typically needs to provide some regularization scheme. The present matrix model setting also differs from the conventional approach to string theory, where
target space is compactified; this is
not
the case here.
Furthermore, the mechanism of induced masses found in this work should allow obtaining a complete effective theory for massive higher-spin fields where all vertices can be derived by averaging over
S^{2}_{\mathtt{J}}
. It would also be interesting to compare interacting vertices of
\mathfrak{hs}
-IKKT with recent attempts of constructing Lorentz-invariant vertices of massive fields in e.g.
[
62
,
63
,
64
]
.

Uniform estimates for transmission problems in electromagnetism with high contrast in magnetic permeabilities
Victor Péron

Abstract
. We consider the time-harmonic Maxwell equations set on a domain made up of two subdomains that represent a magnetic conductor and a non-magnetic material, and we assume that the relative magnetic permeability μ r \mu_{r} between the two materials is high. We prove uniform a priori estimates for Maxwell solutions when the interface between the two subdomains is supposed to be Lipschitz. Assuming smoothness for the interface between the subdomains, we prove also that the magnetic field possesses a multiscale expansion in powers of ε = 1 / μ r \mathsf{\varepsilon}=1/\sqrt{\mu_{r}} with profiles rapidly decaying inside the magnetic conductor.



Keywords
:
Maxwell equations, High relative permeability, Lipschitz domains, Asymptotic expansions

Many works in the literature are related to issues of uniform estimates for stiff transmission problems in electromagnetism. In this work we investigate a transmission problem in materials presenting high contrast of magnetic permeabilities.
The domain of interest is made up of two subdomains that represent a magnetic conductor and a non-magnetic material, and the relative magnetic permeability
\mu_{r}
between the materials can be very high. Our interest is the solvability of the time-harmonic Maxwell equations together with uniform energy estimates in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
for the electromagnetic field when
\mu_{r}
tends to infinity. This issue of uniform estimates is not obvious, and this is especially true since we do not assume that the interface between the subdomains is smooth.

The main result of this paper is uniform a priori estimates for Maxwell solutions when the interface between the two subdomains is supposed to be Lipschitz, cf. Theorem
2.1
in Sec.
2
. This new result shows in particular that the magnetic field tends to
0
in energy norm inside the magnetic conductor as
\mu_{r}
tends to infinity. Our result is valid for a right-hand side in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
. This is notably a difference with the uniform
\mathbf{L}^{2}
estimates at high conductivity
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, which is valid for data in
\mathbf{L}^{2}
with divergence in
{\mathrm{L}}^{2}
.

The proof of our main result is based on an appropriate decomposition of the magnetic field into a vector field in the Sobolev space
\mathbf{H}^{1}
and a gradient field which is piecewise
\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
(does not belong to the energy space), and the gradient part is estimated thanks to uniform estimates for scalar transmission problems with constant coefficients on two subdomains. Note that the uniform estimates for these scalar transmission problems (cf. Lemma
3.4
in Sec.
3.1
) are a consequence of a more general result
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
, and also that the decomposition of the magnetic field based on a vector potential technique is rather classical
[
5
]
. However, the proof of the main result on uniform estimates for the electromagnetic field (Theorem
2.1
) is new.
In particular, the proof of uniform
\mathbf{L}^{2}
estimates for the electric field at high conductivity
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
does not seem to be easily
adaptable to our problem because we consider that the relative permeability can be unbounded.

As an application of our uniform estimates, we develop also an argument for the convergence of an asymptotic expansion. Assuming
smoothness for the interface
between the subdomains, we prove rigorously that the magnetic field possesses a multiscale expansion in powers of
\mathsf{\varepsilon}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{r}}}
with
profile
terms rapidly decaying inside the magnetic conductor, cf. Sec.

We also refer the reader to several works which are devoted to asymptotic expansions of the electromagnetic field at high relative magnetic permeability (

Let
\Omega

We denote by

\Omega
, and by
\Sigma
the boundary of
\Omega_{-}
.
Finally, we denote by

We consider the time-harmonic Maxwell equations given by Faraday’s and Ampère’s laws in
\Omega

(2.1)

\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\ \boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}-i\omega\underline{\mu}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\ \boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}+(i\omega\varepsilon_{0}-\underline{\sigma})\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,.

is the angular frequency (

is made of two connected subdomains
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}
in which the coefficients
\underline{\sigma}
and

take different values
(\sigma_{+}>0,\sigma_{-}>0)
and
(\mu_{+}>0,\mu_{-}>0)
, respectively, and we denote by
\mu_{r}
the relative magnetic permeability between the subdomains
\Omega_{-}
and
\Omega_{+}
, and which is defined as:

(2.2)

\mu_{r}=\mu_{-}/\mu_{+}\ .

To complement the Maxwell harmonic equations (

2.1.
Main result: uniform estimates in Lipschitz domains

Hereafter, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{0,\mathcal{O}}
the norm in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\mathcal{O})={\mathrm{L}}^{2}(\mathcal{O})^{3}
. In the framework above it is possible to prove uniform a priori estimates for the Maxwell transmission problem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}>0
, and
\mu_{+}>0
. If the interface
\Sigma
is Lipschitz, there are constants
\mu_{\star}
and
C>0
such that for all
\mu_{r}\geqslant\mu_{\star}
the Maxwell problem (
2.1
) with boundary conditions (
2.3
) and data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
has a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
, which satisfies:

(2.5)

\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\sqrt{\mu_{r}}\,\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega_{-}}\leqslant C\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\|_{0,\Omega}.

A similar result holds for boundary conditions (
2.4
).

This result is proved in Section
3
. The proof is based on an appropriate decomposition of the magnetic field into a regular field in
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
and a gradient field. The gradient part is estimated thanks to piecewise
\mathrm{H}^{3/2}
uniform estimates for a scalar transmission problem with constant coefficients on two subdomains (cf. Lemma
3.4
in Section
3.1
).

In that follows, we introduce for convenience the following parameter in the Maxwell equations (
2.1
)

(2.6)

\mathsf{\varepsilon}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{r}}}\,.

Hence,
\mathsf{\varepsilon}
tends to
0
as
\mu_{r}
tends to infinity. Then for
\mu_{r}\geqslant\mu_{\star}
, where the constant
\mu_{\star}
is given by Theorem
2.1
,
we denote by
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})})
the solution of the system (
2.1
) –(
2.3
). As an application of uniform estimates (
2.5
) we will develop an argument for the convergence of an asymptotic expansion of
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})})
as
\mathsf{\varepsilon}\to 0
, see Section
4.2
.

2.2.
Magnetic formulation

We deduce from the Maxwell system (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) the following variational formulation for the magnetic field
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}
. The variational space is the Hilbert space
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}},\Omega)
:

(2.7)

\mathbf{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}},\Omega)=\{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\;|\ \operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega),\,\boldsymbol{\mathsf{u}}\times\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}=0\ \mbox{on}\ \Gamma\}\ ,

endowed with its graph norm, and the variational problem writes

Find
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}},\Omega)
such that for all
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}},\Omega)

(2.8)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\left(1+i\frac{\underline{\sigma}}{\omega\varepsilon_{0}}\right)^{-1}\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}}-\kappa_{+}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\mu_{r}}}(\mathsf{\varepsilon})\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{(\mathsf{\varepsilon})}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}}\right){\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=\\
\int_{\Omega}\left(1+i\frac{\underline{\sigma}}{\omega\varepsilon_{0}}\right)^{-1}{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{K}}}\,{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}\ .

Here we have set

(2.9)

{\boldsymbol{\mu_{r}}}(\mathsf{\varepsilon})={\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\sf+}}}+{\frac{1}{\mathsf{\varepsilon}^{2}}}\,{\mathbf{1}_{\Omega_{\sf-}}}\ ,\quad\quad\mbox{and}\quad\kappa_{+}:=\omega\sqrt{\varepsilon_{0}\mu_{+}}\ .

3.
Proof of uniform estimates for Maxwell solutions at high relative magnetic permeability

We consider the harmonic Maxwell system (
2.1
)-(
2.3
). We are going to prove the following statement:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}>0
, and
\mu_{+}>0
. There are constants
\mu_{\star}
and
C_{0}>0
such that if
\mu_{r}\geqslant\mu_{\star}
any solution
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
of problem (
2.1
) with boundary condition (
2.3
) and data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

satisfies the estimate

(3.1)

\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega}\leqslant C_{0}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\|_{0,\Omega}.

A similar statement holds for boundary conditions (
2.4
).

This lemma is the key for the proof of Theorem
2.1
and is going to be proved in the next subsection. As a consequence of this lemma, we will obtain estimates (
2.5
):

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}>0
, and
\mu_{\pm}>0
. Let
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
be solution of problem (
2.1
) with boundary condition (
2.3
) and data
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. Then we have
\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}(\underline{\mu}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})=0
in
\Omega
,
and if
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}
satisfies estimate (
3.1
), then setting
\mathrm{m}=\min(\sqrt{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{-}},\sqrt{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{+}})
,

C_{1}=\frac{\max\left(\frac{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{-}}{\sigma_{-}},\frac{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{+}}{\sigma_{+}}\right)}{\mathrm{m}}\,,\quad\mbox{and}\quad C_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{0}C^{2}_{1}}{\mathrm{m}^{2}}+\frac{C_{1}}{\omega\,\mathrm{m}}}\,,

there holds

(3.2)

\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\sqrt{\mu_{-}}\,\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}\|_{0,\Omega_{-}}\leqslant(C_{0}+C_{1}+C_{2})\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\|_{0,\Omega}\ .

A similar estimate holds for boundary conditions (
2.4
).

This result is going to be proved in Section
3.3
. Finally, estimate (
3.1
) implies existence and uniqueness of solutions.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\sigma_{\pm}>0
, and
\mu_{\pm}>0
. We assume that estimate (
3.1
) holds for any solution
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
of problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) with
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.
Then for any
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
of problem (
2.1
)-(
2.3
).
A similar result holds for boundary conditions (
2.4
).

This result is going to be proved in Section
3.4
. Finally we deduce Theorem
2.1
from the previous three statements.

3.1.
Preliminary lemma

In this section we provide a uniform piecewise estimate for the solution of a scalar transmission problem with constant coefficients on two subdomains. We will use this result in the proof of Lemma
3.1
(cf. Section
3.2
) for obtaining a uniform
\mathbf{L}^{2}
estimate for the magnetic field.

For any given function
\underline{a}=(a_{+},a_{-})
determined by the two constants
a_{\pm}
on
\Omega_{\pm}
, we consider the following variational problem : Find
\varphi\in{\mathrm{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(3.3)

\forall\psi\in{\mathrm{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega),\quad\int_{\Omega_{+}}a_{+}\nabla\varphi^{+}\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}{}^{+}\,{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}+\int_{\Omega_{-}}a_{-}\nabla\varphi^{-}\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}{}^{-}\,{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=\\[-4.30554pt]
(a_{-}-a_{+})\!\int_{\Sigma}g\;\overline{\psi}\,{\rm d}s\,,

where the right-hand side
g
satisfies the regularity assumption

(3.4)

g\in{\mathrm{L}}^{2}(\Sigma)

and the extra compatibility condition

(3.5)

\int_{\Sigma}g\,{\rm d}s=0\,.

In the framework of Section
2
(we recall that the surface
\Sigma
is Lipschitz), we have the following uniform piecewise estimate for the solution of the problem (
3.3
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let us assume that
a_{+}\neq 0
.
There exist a constant
\rho_{0}>0
independent of
a_{+}
such that for all
a_{-}\in\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|z|\geqslant\rho_{0}|a_{+}|\}
, the problem (
3.3
) with a right-hand side
g
satisfying (
3.4
)-(
3.5
) has a unique solution
\varphi\in{\mathrm{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
which moreover is piecewise
{\mathrm{H}}^{3/2}
and satisfies the uniform estimate

(3.6)

\|\varphi^{+}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{+}}+\|\varphi^{-}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{-}}\leqslant C_{\rho_{0}}\|g\|_{0,\Sigma}\ ,

with a constant
C_{\rho_{0}}>0
, independent of
a_{+}
,
a_{-}
, and
g
.

This result is obtained as a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Remark 3.5
.

The assumption on the interface
\Sigma
, that is a Lipschitz surface, is necessary in Lemma
3.4
. Indeed, there exists non-Lipschitz surfaces such that estimate (
3.6
) does not hold, for instance in the case of a checkerboard configuration in a two dimensional domain, cf.
[
12
, Theorem 8.1]
and
[
10
, Rem 2.3]
.

3.2.
Proof of Lemma
3.1
: Uniform
\mathbf{L}^{2}
estimate of the magnetic field

Reductio ad absurdum: We assume that there is a sequence
(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{m},\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m})\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of solutions of the Maxwell system (
2.1
)-(
2.3
) associated with a magnetic permeability
\underline{\mu}_{m}=(\mu_{+},\mu_{m}\,\mu_{+})
and a right hand side
{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}_{m}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
:

(3.7a)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{m}-i\omega\underline{\mu}_{m}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}+(i\omega\varepsilon_{0}-\underline{\sigma})\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{m}={\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}_{m}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,

(3.7c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\times\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,,

satisfying the following conditions

(3.8a)

\displaystyle\mu_{m}\to\infty\quad

as
m\to\infty
,

(3.8b)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}=1\quad

\forall m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.8c)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}\to 0\quad

as
m\to\infty
.

First, we particularize the magnetic variational formulation (
2.8
) for the sequence
\{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\}
: For all
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}(\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}},\Omega)
:

(3.9)

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{i\omega\varepsilon_{0}-\underline{\sigma}}\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}^{\prime}}+i\omega\underline{\mu}_{m}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}^{\prime}}\right)\;{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=\int_{\Omega}\frac{{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}_{m}}{i\omega\varepsilon_{0}-\underline{\sigma}}\cdot\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\overline{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}}^{\prime}\,{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}\,.

Choosing
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}
in (
3.9
) and taking the real part, and then using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain the following uniform bound on the curl of the magnetic field in
\Omega
:

(3.10)

\|\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}\leqslant\frac{\max\left(\frac{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{-}}{\sigma_{-}},\frac{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{+}}{\sigma_{+}}\right)}{\min(\sqrt{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{-}},\sqrt{\omega^{2}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}+\sigma^{2}_{+}})}\|{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{j}}}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}\,.

3.2.1.
Decomposition of the magnetic field and bound in
{\mathrm{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

We recall that we have assumed that the domain
\Omega
is simply connected and has a smooth connected boundary. Relying to Theorem 2.12 and Theorem 3.17 in
[
5
]
, for all
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
there exists a unique
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(3.11)

\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}=\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m},\quad\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}=0\ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega\ ,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\times\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}=0\ \mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega\,.

Moreover, we have the estimate

(3.12)

\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\|_{1,\Omega}\leqslant C\|\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\|_{0,\Omega}\,,

where
C
is independent of
m
. As a consequence of the equality
\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}=\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}
and the simple connectedness of
\Omega
, we obtain that there exists
\varphi_{m}\in{\mathrm{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(3.13)

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}+\nabla\varphi_{m}\,.

Then using (
3.13
) we write equation (
3.7a
) as

\operatorname{\mathrm{curl}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{E}}_{m}-i\omega\underline{\mu}_{m}(\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}+\nabla\varphi_{m})=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega.

Let
\psi\in{\mathrm{H}}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be a test function. Multiplying the above equality by
\nabla\overline{\psi}
and integrating over
\Omega
, we obtain, using that
\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}=0
:

(3.14)

\int_{\Omega}\underline{\mu}_{m}\;\nabla\varphi_{m}\cdot\nabla\overline{\psi}\;{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=(\mu_{m}-1)\mu_{+}\int_{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\!\left.\vphantom{|_{|}}\right|_{\Sigma}\;\overline{\psi}\;{\rm d}s\,,

where
\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}
denotes the unit normal vector on
\Sigma
, inwardly oriented to
\Omega_{-}
. Note that the boundary value
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
has been used in (
3.14
).

Thus
\varphi_{m}
is solution of the Dirichlet problem defined by the variational equation (
3.14
). Since

\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\in{\mathrm{L}}^{2}(\Sigma)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\!\left.\vphantom{|_{|}}\right|_{\Sigma}\;{\rm d}s=\int_{\Omega_{-}}\operatorname{\mathrm{div}}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\,{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\mathsf{x}}=0,

the Dirichlet problem defined by (
3.14
) satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.4
(cf. Section
3.1
) with
a_{-}=\mu_{m}\mu_{+}
and
a_{+}=\mu_{+}
, and
g=\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\!\left.\vphantom{|_{|}}\right|_{\Sigma}
.
Therefore we obtain the following uniform estimate for
\mu_{m}
large enough (i.e. for
m
large enough, cf. (
3.8a
))

\|\varphi^{+}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{+}}+\|\varphi^{-}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{-}}\leqslant C_{0}\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\|_{0,\Sigma}.

Since
\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathsf{n}}\|_{0,\Sigma}
is bounded by
\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\|_{1,\Omega}
, the last inequality implies

(3.15)

\|\varphi^{+}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{+}}+\|\varphi^{-}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{-}}\leqslant C_{0}\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\|_{1,\Omega}.

Finally (
3.10
),
(
3.12
), and (
3.15
) implies that

(3.16)

\|\varphi^{+}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{+}}+\|\varphi^{-}_{m}\|_{\frac{3}{2},\Omega_{-}}+\|\boldsymbol{\mathsf{v}}_{m}\|_{1,\Omega}\leqslant B

for a constant
B>0
independent of
m
. With (
3.13
), (
3.16
) gives that the sequence
\{\boldsymbol{\mathsf{H}}_{m}\}
is bounded in

5.
Conclusion and perspectives

In this work we tackled a transmission problem in materials with high contrast in magnetic permeabilities. We proved uniform a priori estimates for the electromagnetic field solution of the time-harmonic Maxwell equations as the relative magnetic permeability
\mu_{r}
between a magnetic conductor and a non-magnetic material tends to infinity, and when the interface between the subdomains is Lipschitz. Assuming smoothness for the interface between the subdomains, we derived a multiscale expansion for the magnetic field in powers of
\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{r}}}
with profiles inside the magnetic conductor, and we proved error estimates at any order.

One perspective would be to carry out numerical experiments in axisymmetric domains
[
8
,
16
,
24
,
10
]
. This would make it possible in particular to illustrate our theoretical results. The multiscale expansion provides also an a priori knowledge on the magnetic field useful for the design of meshes in view of a quality finite element approximation. Thus, the mesh should be matched to the boundary of the magnetic conductor and can be coarse far from its boundary in the magnetic conductor, as with the
skin effect
in non-magnetic conductive materials. It would be useful also to derive impedance boundary conditions (see,
e.g.
,
[
7
,
20
,
15
]
for scattering
problems from absorbing obstacles) on the interface (magnetic conductor / non-magnetic material) in order to reduce the computational costs.

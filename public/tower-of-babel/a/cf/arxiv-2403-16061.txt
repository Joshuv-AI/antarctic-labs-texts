Nonstandard interactions of neutrinos with dense matter Preprint: PKNU-NuHaTh-2024
Parada T. P. Hutauruk ID

Abstract
Nonstandard interaction is expected to be a crucial hint in explaining the experimental data on neutrino scattering off electrons. In this context, the nonstandard interaction vector and axial-vector couplings are needed to be extracted from recent experiments and a few of them are now available in the literature. With these coupling bounds, in this paper, I explore their impacts on the neutrinos interacting with the free electron gas in dense matter. To this end, I compute and predict the neutrino differential cross section and mean free path in dense matter for those existing experimental bounds. Interesting behavior in the neutrino cross sections and mean free path is found for the different nonstandard interaction couplings from different experiment constraints. I also found that the neutrino cross-section and mean free path in the dense matter are very sensitive to values and signs of the nonstandard interaction couplings, leading to different prediction results to the Standard Model cross-section and mean free path as well as their totals, which is given by a sum of the Standard Model and nonstandard interaction.

Theoretical Formalism

II.1
NSI Lagrangians and cross section

In this section, I briefly introduce the NSI effective Lagrangian for the elastic
\nu_{e}-
e scattering in dense matter. Similar to the SM Lagrangian expression for the weak interaction part in Ref.
Kalempouw-Williams:2005zbp
;
Horowitz:1990it
;
Reddy:1998hb
, the NC NSI effective four-fermion Lagrangian for the elastic electron neutrino scattering off the free electron gas reaction process can be written as

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rm{NSI}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big[\bar{\nu}_{e}(k^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})\nu_{e}(k)\Big]\Big[\bar{e}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\mu}\left(\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eV}-\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eA}\gamma_{5}\right)e(p)\Big],

(1)

where the subscript of
e
is the electron neutrino flavors.
G_{F}=1.023\times 10^{-5}/M^{2}
is the Fermi constant where
M=
939 MeV as the nucleon free mass. The symbols of the
\nu_{e}(k^{\prime})
and
\bar{\nu}_{e}(k)
stand for the final and initial neutrino spinors, respectively, and
\bar{e}(p^{\prime})
and
e(p)
are respectively the final and initial lepton spinors, where
k=(k_{0},\vec{\mathbf{k}})
and
p=(p_{0},\vec{\mathbf{p}})
are respectively the initial four-momentum of the neutrinos and electrons, while
k^{\prime}=(k^{\prime}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{k^{\prime}}})
and
p^{\prime}=(p^{\prime}_{0},\vec{\mathbf{p^{\prime}}})
are the final four-momentum of the neutrinos and electrons, respectively.
The parameters
\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eV}=\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eL}+\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eR}
and
\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eA}=\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eL}-\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eR}
denote respectively the strength of the NSI vector and axial-vector couplings. In this work, I will concentrate on the NC NSI of the elastic
\nu_{e}
-e scattering in dense matter.

With the help of the NSI effective Lagrangian in Eq. (
1
), I derive the neutrino double differential cross section per unit volume
\mathscr{V}
for NSI of
\nu_{e}-
e scattering in terms of the electron polarization and lepton tensors (See Appendix for details derivation), and it gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathscr{V}}\frac{d^{3}\sigma}{d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}dE_{\nu}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{G_{F}}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{E_{\nu}^{\prime}}{E_{\nu}}\imaginary[L_{\mu\nu}\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{[e]}\Big],

(2)

where
E_{\nu}
and
E_{\nu}^{\prime}
stand for the initial and final neutrino energies, respectively. Following the SM lepton tensor of Ref.
Kalempouw-Williams:2005zbp
;
Horowitz:1990it
;
Reddy:1998hb
, the neutrino tensor
L_{\mu\nu}
for NSI is defined by

\displaystyle L_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\left[2k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+\left(k\cdot q\right)g_{\mu\nu}-\left(k_{\mu}q_{\nu}+q_{\mu}k_{\nu}\right)\mp i\epsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}k^{\alpha}q^{\beta}\right],

(3)

where
-
and
+
signs in the last term in Eq. (
3
) are respectively for neutrinos and antineutrinos.
k
and
q=(q_{0},\mathbf{q})
are four-momentum of the initial neutrinos and transfer, respectively.

In a similar way to the SM polarization tensor for the electrons in dense matter
Kalempouw-Williams:2005zbp
;
Horowitz:1990it
, the expression of the electron polarization tensor
\Pi^{\mu\nu}
for NSI in Eq. (
3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu\nu}_{[e]}(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\Tr[G^{(e)}(p)\gamma^{\mu}\big(\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{\alpha V}-\mathcal{E}_{ee}{\alpha A}\gamma_{5}\big)G^{(e)}(p+q)\gamma^{\nu}\big(\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{\alpha V}-\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{\alpha A}\gamma_{5}\big)\Big],

(4)

where
p=(p_{0},\mathbf{p})
is the initial electron four momentum and
G^{(e)}(p)
is the electron propagator in matter. The values of the vector and axial-vector NSI couplings for different neutrino flavors obtained from solar, atmospheric, and reactor experiments are summarized in Table
1
.
The explicit form of the electron propagator at a given electron Fermi momentum
p_{F}^{(e)}
is given by

\displaystyle G^{(e)}(p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(p\!\!\!/+m_{e}\right)\Big[\frac{1}{p^{2}-m_{e}^{2}+i\epsilon}+\frac{i\pi}{E_{p}}\delta\left(p_{0}-E_{p}\right)\theta\left(p_{F}^{e}-|\mathbf{p}|\right)\Big],

(5)

where
E_{p}=\sqrt{\mathbf{p}^{2}+m_{e}^{2}}
is the electron energy with
m_{e}=0.511
MeV is the electron free mass. It is worth noting that the free space electron mass is the same as in dense matter.

After evaluating the contractions of the electron polarization and neutrino tensors of Eq. (
2
) by considering the current conservation and translational invariance, the final expression of the NSI NDCS is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathscr{V}}\frac{d^{3}\sigma}{d^{2}\Omega^{\prime}dE_{\nu}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{G_{F}^{2}E_{\nu}^{\prime}q_{\mu}^{2}}{4\pi^{3}E_{\nu}}\Big[A\mathscr{R}_{1}+\mathscr{R}_{2}+B\mathscr{R}_{3}\Big],

(6)

where variables of
A=2k_{0}\left(k_{0}-q_{0}\right)+0.5q_{\mu}^{2}/|\mathbf{q}|^{2}
and
B=2k_{0}-q_{0}
are kinematic factors and the uncorrelated response functions are respectively defined by

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{1}=\mathscr{R}_{1}^{V}+\mathscr{R}_{1}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{{eV}2}\Big[\imaginary\Pi_{T}^{(e)}+\imaginary\Pi_{L}^{(e)}\Big]+\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{{\alpha A}2}\Big[\imaginary\Pi_{T}^{(e)}+\imaginary\Pi_{L}^{(e)}\Big],

(7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{2}=\mathscr{R}_{2}^{V}+\mathscr{R}_{2}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{{eV}2}\imaginary\Pi_{T}^{(e)}+\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{{\alpha A}2}\Big[\imaginary\Pi_{T}^{(e)}-\imaginary\Pi_{A}^{(e)}\Big],

(8)

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm 2\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{\alpha V}\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{{\alpha A}}\imaginary\Pi_{VA}^{(e)},

(9)

where the signs of
+
and
-
in the
\mathscr{R}_{3}
are for neutrinos and antineutrinos, respectively. The transverse (T), longitudinal (L), axial (A), and mixed vector-axial (VA) electron polarizations are respectively given by

\displaystyle\imaginary\Pi_{T}^{(e)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi|\mathbf{q}|}\Bigg[\left(m_{e}^{2}+\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{2}+\frac{q_{\mu}^{4}}{4|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}\right)\left(E_{F}-E^{*}\right)+q_{0}\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{2|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}\left(E_{F}^{2}-E^{*2}\right)+\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{3|\mathbf{q}|^{2}}\left(E_{F}^{3}-E^{*3}\right)\Bigg],

(10)

\displaystyle\imaginary\Pi_{L}^{(e)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{2\pi|\mathbf{q}|^{3}}\Bigg[\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{4}\left(E_{F}-E^{*}\right)+\frac{q_{0}}{2}\left(E_{F}^{2}-E^{*2}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(E_{F}^{3}-E^{*3}\right)\Bigg],

(11)

\displaystyle\imaginary\Pi_{A}^{(e)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m_{e}^{2}}{2\pi|\mathbf{q}|}\left(E_{F}-E^{*}\right),

(12)

\displaystyle\imaginary\Pi_{VA}^{(e)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{\mu}^{2}}{8\pi|\mathbf{q}|^{3}}\Bigg[q_{0}\left(E_{F}-E^{*}\right)+\left(E_{F}^{2}-E^{*2}\right)\Bigg],

(13)

where
E^{*}=\min\left[E_{F},E_{\rm{max}}\right]
with
E_{F}=\sqrt{p_{F}^{(e)2}+m_{e}^{2}}
and
E_{\rm{max}}=\max\left[E_{F}-q_{0},\frac{1}{2}\left(|\mathbf{q}|\sqrt{1-4m_{e}^{2}/q_{\mu}^{2}}-q_{0}\right)\right]
. Next, with this NDCS formulation, I will numerically compute the NSI NMFP of the process NC
\nu-
e scattering in Sec.
II.2
.

(14)

where the initial and final neutrino energy are related as

IV
Summary and future perspectives

In this paper, I have explored the effects of the vector and axial-vector NSI couplings extracted from various recent experiments in the NSI neutrino scattering off the free electron gas in dense matter. I compute the NSI NDCS and NMFP in dense matter for various vector and axial-vector NSI couplings. It is worth noting that the vector and axial-vector NSI couplings from different experiments are unique, meaning the values of the NSI couplings are almost different for various experiments, which may lead to new physics.

For the Boroxino Phase I
Agarwalla:2012wf
, I found the NCDS of all NSI couplings is lower than that of the SM. In contrast with the Boroxino Phase I, for the NSI coupling extracted from the TEXONO experiment
TEXONO:2010tnr

-1.53,-0.07
] and [
-1.53,0.008
]. Similar to the TEXONO experiment, the NDCS for all the couplings extracted from the LSNO experiment
Davidson:2003ha
;
LSND:2001akn
is relatively larger than that found in the SM, but different in magnitude. For the SuperKamLAND experiment
Bolanos:2008km
, comparing with the SM NDCS I found that the NDCS for [
-0.021,0.51
] and [
0.052,0.51
] are larger than that of the SM, while the NDCS for [
-0.21,-0.18
] and [
0.052,-0.18
] is lower than that for the SM. I also found that the NDCSs for the Boroxino Phase II
Coloma:2022umy
(first region of
\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eL}
) are larger than that of the SM, while the NDCS for the Boroxino Phase II
Coloma:2022umy
(second region of
\mathcal{E}_{ee}^{eL}
) are smaller than that of the SM. In most cases, I found that the NDCS increases as the nuclear density and neutrino energy increase. Increasing the DCRS leads to a decrease in the NMFP.

In the NMFP results, for the Boroxino Phase I, I found the NMFPs for [
-0.046,0.16

The results of the present work provide useful information for the neutrino-NSI interaction in neutron stars, supernovas, and other astrophysical objects as well as supernova simulation
Janka:2012wk
. It was expected that the neutrino oscillation dominated by matter so the result of this study is also very relevant to such a topic. Additionally, the prediction of this current work on the NSI NMFP and NDCS for different NSI couplings in dense matter gives an intuition of the NSI NMFP’s appearance in dense matter. In the future, I will extend the study to neutrino oscillation in matter through the neutrino interaction. The related works are still in progress and will appear elsewhere.

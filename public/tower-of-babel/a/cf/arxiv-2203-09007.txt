A categorification of the Lusztig–Vogan module
Scott Larson and Anna Romanov

Abstract
. We construct two categorifications of the Lusztig–Vogan module associated to a real reductive algebraic group. The first categorification is given by semisimple complexes in an equivariant derived category, and the second is constructed as a module category over Soergel bimodules. Our categorifications are related by taking equivariant hypercohomology.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Let
G
be a connected complex reductive algebraic group,
\theta
a holomorphic involution of
G
, and
K
a finite-index subgroup of the fixed point group
G^{\theta}
. From this data, Lusztig–Vogan constructed a module over the Hecke algebra of
G
and a collection of polynomials that arise as entries in a change-of-basis matrix for this module
[
Vog82
,
LV83
]
. These Kazhdan–Lusztig–Vogan (KLV) polynomials have both geometric and representation theoretic significance: geometrically, they capture subtle information about the singularity of closures of
K
-orbits in the flag variety of
G
, and representation theoretically, they give character formulas for irreducible representations of the associated real reductive group
G_{\mathbb{R}}
(cf., e.g.,
[
AvLTV20
]
).

In this paper, we take a Soergel bimodule approach to the Lusztig–Vogan module. In classic work
[
Soe90
,
Soe00
]
, Soergel explained that the hypercohomology functor applied to certain categories of constructible sheaves appearing in Lie theory is fully faithful. The upshot is that one can understand categories of constructible sheaves algebraically, as certain modules or bimodules. In this paper, we explain that a piece of the Lusztig–Vogan module can also be understood in this way after taking hypercohomology of a category of constructible sheaves.

A major motivation for Soergel’s work was to establish certain Koszul duality equivalences on the derived category of category
\mathcal{O}
. Similarly, one motivation for giving a bimodule theoretic interpretation of the Lusztig–Vogan module might be to establish Soergel’s conjectures
[
Soe01
]
, which generalize Koszul duality to real reductive groups. It is this point of view that has been taken in recent work of Bezrukavnikov–Vilonen
[
BV21
]
, which has results close to those of this paper
1
1

1

We comment on these results in more detail in Section
1.5
.

Another motivation for our work is to understand as explicitly as possible the Lusztig–Vogan module from a Soergel bimodule point of view, as a rich new source of module categories for Soergel bimodules.

1.2.
Geometry of the Lusztig–Vogan module

The remainder of the introduction describes our results in more detail. Fix a Borel subgroup
B
of
G
. The group
K
acts on the flag variety
X=G/B
with finitely many orbits, and the Lusztig–Vogan module is constructed using the geometry of these orbits. Denote by
\mathscr{D}
the set of pairs
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})
consisting of a
K
-orbit
\mathcal{O}
and an irreducible
K
-equivariant local system on
\mathcal{O}
, and set
M_{LV}
to be the free
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-module with basis
\mathscr{D}
. Let
(W,S)
be the Coxeter system associated to
(G,B)
and
\bm{H}=\bm{H}(W,S)
the corresponding Hecke algebra. For each
s\in S
, one can define endomorphisms

T_{s}:M_{LV}\rightarrow M_{LV}

using push-pull functors to partial flag varieties or via a case-by-case analysis of root types (Definition
3.2.1
). The operators
T_{s}
give
M_{LV}
the structure of an
\bm{H}
-module.

The geometric construction of
M_{LV}
leads naturally to a categorification within
D^{b}_{K}(X)
, the
K
-equivariant derived category of sheaves of
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces

\mathbb{Q}

M_{LV}^{0}

M_{LV}

\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}:=\langle\bm{IC}(\mathcal{Q},\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{Q}})*\mathcal{H}\mid\mathcal{Q}\text{ a closed $K$-orbit}\rangle_{\oplus,\ominus,[1]}

\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{ss}

\bm{IC}

\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{Q}}

.

(Theorem

\bm{H}

The categories
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{ss}

\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
provide geometric categorifications of the Lusztig–Vogan module
M_{LV}
and its trivial block
M_{LV}^{0}
. The existence of these categorifications is not surprising—they are just reformulations of the construction in
[
LV83
]
and classical theorems in
[
Vog81
]
using different geometric language. Our interest in formulating the Lusztig–Vogan module in these geometric terms is twofold: (1) we believe that the equivariant derived category provides the most natural language to describe Lusztig–Vogan’s construction, and (2) this formulation allows us to give a bimodule description of the Lusztig–Vogan module which relates it to the monoidal category of Soergel bimodules.

1.3.
A Soergel bimodule approach

Our tool for moving from the
K
-equivariant derived category to a category of bimodules is the hypercohomology functor (Definition
3.16
):

\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(-):=\Hom_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{Q}_{X},-):D^{b}_{K}(X)\rightarrow H^{*}_{K}(X)\mathrm{-gmod}.

To make this approach work, we add the assumption
K
is the
identity component
of
G^{\theta}
. We suspect that the main results hold for disconnected
K
as well, but different arguments would be needed. Under this connectedness assumption on
K
, a standard computation (Lemma
7.0.1
) proves that the
K
-equivariant cohomology of the flag variety is

H^{*}_{K}(X)=P^{W_{K}}\otimes_{R^{W}}R,

where
P=S(X(T_{K})_{\mathbb{Q}})
is the symmetric algebra on the
\mathbb{Q}
-span of the cocharacter lattice of a maximal torus
T_{K}
in
K
,
R=S(X(T)_{\mathbb{Q}})
is the analogous ring for a maximal
\theta
-stable torus
T
in
G
, and
W_{K}
is the Weyl group of
K
. This allows us to view vector spaces in the image of
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{K}
as graded
(P^{W_{K}},R)
-bimodules.

Our algebraic Lusztig–Vogan category is defined to be the essential image of the functor
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
when restricted to
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
. It can be described in an explicit combinatorial way by computing the hypercohomology of
\bm{IC}
sheaves on closed orbits (Lemma
7.1.2
). For a closed
K
-orbit
\mathcal{Q}
, there exists an element
x\in W
such that

\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\bm{IC}(\mathcal{Q},\mathbb{Q}_{\mathcal{Q}}))=P_{x},

where
P_{x}
is the standard
(P^{W_{K}},R)
-bimodule (Definition
6.2.1
) corresponding to
x
. We define a category

\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}:=\langle P_{x}\otimes_{R}\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}\mid x\in W^{\theta}\rangle_{\oplus,\ominus,(1)}

of
(P^{W_{K}},R)
-bimodules which is generated by standard modules under the natural right action of the category of Soergel bimodules (denoted
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
, Definition
3.3.2
). Here
W^{\theta}
is the set of elements of
W
fixed under the involution induced from
\theta
. These are precisely the elements of
W
corresponding to
T
-fixed points in
X
which are found in closed
K
-orbits.

Though motivated by geometry, this construction can be done completely combinatorially with only the input data of the group
G
and involution
\theta
. From the perspective of Soergel bimodules, this provides an interesting class of module categories for
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
coming from real groups. It also potentially leads to “exotic” Lusztig–Vogan modules attached to “exotic” involutions (e.g. of non-crystallographic Weyl groups).

The main result of our paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3.1
.

(Theorem
7.1.1
, Theorem
7.2.1
)
The hypercohomology functor

\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}:\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}

is an equivalence of categories.

Remark 1.3.2
.

This result can also be deduced from Theorem 3.12 in
[
BV21
]
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3.1
relies on two main ingredients: the theory of parity sheaves developed in
[
JMW14
]
, and localization to torus fixed points as in
[
GKM98
]
. We find it interesting that this appears to be the first application of parity sheaf techniques to
K
-orbits on flag varieties, as they provide a powerful tool which has been used in many other settings in geometric representation theory. It is also of note that when specialized to the case of a complex group (i.e., the involution of
G\times G
which switches the factors), our argument provides an alternate proof of
[
Soe90
, Erweiterungssatz 17]
which does not rely on known facts about dimensions of modules in category
\mathcal{O}
.

Theorem
1.3.1
immediately implies that
\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
categorifies the trivial block of the Lusztig–Vogan module.

Corollary 1.3.3
.

(Corollary
7.2.17
) As right
\bm{H}
-modules,
[\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}]_{\oplus}\simeq M_{LV}^{0}
.

A conjecture of Soergel
[
Soe01
]
predicts that associated to the category
\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
(or
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
) is a Koszul dual category of Harish-Chandra modules for a real form of the Langlands dual group of
G
. The duality on the level of categories should provide a categorification of Vogan’s character duality
[
Vog83
]
, which is a duality on the level of
\bm{H}
-modules. A version of this relationship is established in
[
BV21
]
.

One might hope that by applying the functor
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
to the semisimple category
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{ss}
, we could establish a bimodule description of the entire Lusztig–Vogan module. Unfortunately,
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
vanishes outside of
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
(see Example
7.1.6
), so this strategy does not work.

1.4.
Application: Resolutions of singularities

We can view the results in this paper as studying continuous actions of real reductive algebraic groups.
Gelfand’s program asserts that one should first linearize such actions by considering vector spaces related to functions on the original space.
This philosophy lead to the definition of
\mathscr{D}
through work of Harish-Chandra and Vogan
[
Vog82
]
.
In particular, continuous actions of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
are studied by considering the easier problem of an algebraic group
K
acting on an algebraic variety
X
.
It is interesting to observe that in the case of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
acting on the flag variety, Matsuki duality gives a perfect relationship between orbit structures (cf.,
[
Wol69
]
,
[
Mat79
]
).
Moreover, singularities of
K
-orbit closures in
X
are related to representation theory of
G_{\mathbb{R}}
through KLV polynomials and characteristic cycles of
D
-modules.

Closures of
K
-orbits in
X
have complicated singularities.
In
[
BE94
]
, equivariant resolutions of singularities were constructed to study the geometry of these varieties, and in
[
Lar20
]
equivariant proper maps were further analyzed.
As an application of our geometric categorification of the Lusztig–Vogan module, we give a formula in Theorem
4.4.1
to compute the cohomology of fibers of the resolutions introduced in
[
Lar20
]
using the character map

(1.1)

m\,\colon D_{K}^{b}(X)\to M_{LV}

appearing in the proof of Theorem
4.1.3
.
This result can be viewed through the paradigm of Gelfand and Harish-Chandra as studying equivariant resolutions of singularities first via linearization, then through algebraic methods using our algebraic categorification.

1.5.
Relation to the results of Bezrukavnikov–Vilonen

Recent results of Bezrukavnikov–Vilonen
[
BV21
]
establish an important special case of Soergel’s conjecture on a categorification of Vogan duality (see
1.1
). One side of their equivalence takes place in the equivariant derived category, and a key part of their argument involves the fully-faithfullness of a global sections functor. This part of their argument involves a theorem equivalent to Theorem
1.3.1
above. Our work on this project began before we were aware of their results, and our proofs differ. (In particular, their proof involves a reduction to low ranks, and ours does not, see
1.5.1
below). For the benefit of the reader, we comment here on the relation between our results.

The fully-faithfullness result alluded to above is
[
BV21
, Thm. 3.2]
. They prove that (in their notation)

R\Gamma_{\check{K}}:\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{gr}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Coh}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(\mathfrak{B})

is fully faithful. The relation between this result and Theorem
1.3.1
is as follows.

(1)

Their category
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{gr}}
is equivalent to our category
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{LV}}^{0}
. Indeed, both categories consist of certain
K
-equivariant semisimple complexes on
G/B
, and
[
BV21
, Prop. 3.6]
establishes that
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{gr}}
is generated by
\bm{IC}
-sheaves corresponding to trivial local systems on closed orbits.

(2)

There is an equivalence

(1.2)

\mathrm{Coh}^{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}(\mathfrak{B})\simeq P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R\mathrm{-gmod},

where we are using notation from
[
BV21
]
on the left and our notation on the right. This is because
\mathbb{C}[\mathfrak{B}]=P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R
by
[
BV21
, Lem. 3.1]
. The grading on the right hand side of (
1.2
) is due to the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on the left hand side of (
1.2
). The weights agree by the definition of the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action in
[
BV21
, Thm. 3.2]
.

(3)

In
[
BV21
]
, the category
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{gr}}
is defined using
K
-equivariant
D
-modules on
G/B
. It is then implicitly identified with a subcategory of the (constructible) equivariant derived category
D^{b}_{K}(G/B)
. In contrast, we work exclusively within the equivariant derived category.

We complete this section with some further remarks.

Remark 1.5.1
.

(i)

Bezrukavnikov–Vilonen’s proof uses reduction to small rank and a computation, see
[
BV21
, §3.8]
. Our proof avoids this reduction.

(ii)

Our theorem describes the image in terms of Soergel bimodules. One can deduce a similar description from
[
BV21
]
. Note that our Soergel bimodule action corresponds to their convolution with
\alpha
-lines, see
[
BV21
, §3.3]
.

(iii)

For the purposes of Bezrukavnikov–Vilonen, it is necessary to consider all finite-index subgroups of
K
, included disconnected ones. They overcome this obstacle by imposing equivariance under a finite abelian group.

3.
Preliminaries

We begin by establishing our notation and conventions. We encourage readers who are familiar with this material to skip to Section
4
and refer back to this section only when needed.

3.1.
The Hecke algebra

Let
(W,S)
be the Coxeter system associated to the pair
(G,B)
. We denote by
\ell:W\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
the length function on
W
and
\leq
the Bruhat order. A sequence
\underline{w}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{m})
of simple reflections
s_{i}\in S
is called an
expression
for the group element
w
if
w=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{m}\in W
. Notationally we distinguish between expressions and group elements by underlining. We say an expression
\underline{w}=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{m})
is
reduced
if
m=\ell(w)
.

The
Hecke algebra

\bm{H}=\bm{H}(W,S)
of the Coxeter system
(W,S)
is the unital associative
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
-algebra generated by symbols
\{\delta_{s}\mid s\in S\}
subject to relations

(i)

(quadratic)
\delta_{s}^{2}=1+(v^{-1}-v)\delta_{s}
for all
s\in S
, and

(ii)

(braid)
\delta_{s}\delta_{t}\delta_{s}\ldots=\delta_{t}\delta_{s}\delta_{t}\ldots
for all
s\neq t\in S
,

where the products in (ii) consist of
m_{st}
elements on each side of the equality.

Remark 3.1.1
.

Alternatively, the Hecke algebra can be presented as the unital
\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}]
-algebra generated by symbols
\{T_{s}\mid s\in S\}
subject to relations

(i)

T_{s}^{2}=(q-1)T_{s}+q
for all
s\in S
, and

(ii)

T_{s}T_{t}T_{s}\ldots=T_{t}T_{s}T_{t}\ldots
for all
s\neq t\in S
,

where each product in (ii) consists of
m_{st}
elements. Our initial presentation can be obtained from this one by setting
v=q^{-1/2}
and
\delta_{s}=vT_{s}
.

The elements
\{\delta_{s}\mid s\in S\}
are invertible, with inverses given by
\delta_{s}^{-1}=\delta_{s}+(v-v^{-1})
. For
x\neq e\in W
, define an element
\delta_{x}\in\bm{H}
by choosing a reduced expression
\underline{x}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
of
x
and setting
\delta_{x}:=\delta_{s_{1}}\cdots\delta_{s_{m}}

3.6.
From the geometric Hecke category to Soergel bimodules

By Theorem
3.3.4
and Theorem

, both

and

categorify

. In fact, their relationship can be made more explicit using an alternate characterization of the geometric Hecke category.

The category

is Krull-Schmidt, meaning that every object decomposes into a finite direct sum of indecomposable objects, which are characterized by having local endomorphism rings. Hence, to understand the category

, we should start by understanding the indecomposible objects. By definition, objects in

are direct summands of finite direct sums of shifts of objects of the form

for some expression

of an element

. The objects

can be described in terms of Bott-Samelson resolutions of the Schubert varieties

. If

is an expression for

, then the Bott-Samelson space

is a smooth algebraic variety. There is a natural projective morphism

\xi:BS_{\underline{w}}\rightarrow\overline{X}_{w}

induced by multiplication in
G
. For any expression
\underline{w}
, there is a canonical isomorphism
[
Soe00
, Lem. 3.2.1]

Remark 3.6.1
.

If
\underline{w}
is a reduced expression for
w\in W
, then
\xi
is a resolution of singularities of
\overline{X}_{w}
. Such resolutions are sometimes called

.

Let

-equivariant intersection cohomology complex on

, extended by zero, so it is perverse on

. The morphism

decomposes into a finite direct sum of shifts of

:

Using the characterization (

and

becomes clear by applying hypercohomology. Let

where

The identity element in the LHS of (

), one coordinate for each element of

The RHS of (
4.27
) has global sections equal to

(4.30)

\Hom_{D(Z)}(\mathbb{Q}_{Z},\bigoplus_{n\in N}\mathbb{Q}_{Z}[2d-n])=\bigoplus_{n\in N}H^{2d-n}(Z).

For every

, where we have

The element

corresponds on the LHS of (

since it restricts to isomorphisms over trivializable open sets by Lemma

In particular, every possible nonzero term on the diagonal has even degree.

The claim follows by shifting degree of (

5.
A categorification of the trivial block

In Section
4
, we provide a geometric categorification of the entire Lusztig–Vogan module
M_{LV}
. By considering the subcategory of
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{ss}
generated by trivial
K

5.1.
Blocks

There is a natural equivalence relation on the set
\mathscr{D}
in (

into

[
Vog81

[

Let

, and

Definition 5.1.1

(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{L})\sim(\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\mathcal{L}^{\prime})\text{ if }[\mathcal{L}^{\prime}:\mathcal{H}^{i}(\bm{IC}(\overline{\mathcal{O}},\mathcal{L})]\neq 0

Remark 5.1.2

-modules
[

is the block that governs birational resolutions of

The following standard (but difficult) fact is vital to our arguments. This follows from

6.
An algebraic categorification

In this section, we introduce a category of bimodules which plays the role of Soergel bimodules in the
K
-equivariant setting. Before introducing our category, we recall some facts about torus fixed points in
K
-orbits which will be essential in the arguments of Section
7
.

6.1.
Torus fixed points and closed
K
-orbits

For the remainder of the paper, we impose an additional connectedness assumption on the group
K
. Let
G
be as in Section
1
, and set
K
to be the
identity component
of the fixed point group
G^{\theta}
. With this assumption, the group
K
has the structure of a connected reductive complex algebraic group. Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{k}
the corresponding Lie algebras. The involution
\theta
induces an involution on
\mathfrak{g}
which we refer to by the same symbol. Let

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}

be the decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
into eigenspaces of
\theta
. Here
\mathfrak{k}
is the
+1
-eigenspace and
\mathfrak{p}
is the
-1
-eigenspace.

Remark 6.1.1
.

The assumption that
K
is connected is necessary for several of the arguments in Section
7
. We suspect that the main results of this paper remain true for disconnected
K
, but to prove them one would need to alter the present arguments. We intend to explore this further in future work.

Choose a maximal torus
T_{K}
in
K
, and let
W_{K}:=N_{K}(T_{K})/T_{K}
be the Weyl group of
(K,T_{K})
. Set

(6.1)

T:=Z_{G}(T_{K}).

Lemma 6.1.2
.

The group
T
is the unique
\theta
-stable maximal torus containing
T_{K}
.

Proof.

Clearly
T
is
\theta
-stable: for
z\in T
and
t\in T_{K}
,

\theta(z)t=\theta(zt)=\theta(tz)=t\theta(z),

so
\theta(z)\in T
.

As
T_{K}
is a
\theta
-fixed maximal torus in
K
, it is contained in some
\theta
-stable maximal torus
T^{\prime}
of
G
. Let
\Sigma=\Sigma(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}^{\prime})
be the root system of
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}:=\Lie T^{\prime})
, and

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}

the corresponding root space decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
decomposes into a direct sum
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{t}_{K}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
, where
\mathfrak{t}_{K}=\Lie T_{K}
and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{p}
. Recall that a root
\alpha\in\Sigma
is said to be
real
if
\alpha|_{\mathfrak{t}_{K}}=0
. If
X\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha
real, then
[t,x]=\alpha(t)x=0
for all
t\in\mathfrak{t}_{K}
. Hence we have a decomposition

(6.2)

Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t}_{K})=\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma\atop{\alpha\text{ real}}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

Because
T_{K}
is a maximal torus in
K
, the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}
is
maximally compact
, meaning that the compact dimension (i.e., the dimension of the
+1

\theta
-eigenspace) is maximal among
\theta
-stable Cartan subalgebras. The maximally compact Cartan subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
are precisely those with no real roots
[
Kna94
, Prop. 6.70]
, so in our case, (
6.2
) becomes

Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{t}_{K})=\mathfrak{t}^{\prime}.

This implies the group-theoretic statement.
∎

Let
W:=N_{G}(T)/T
be the Weyl group of
(G,T)
. The following lemma shows that
W
contains
W_{K}
as a subgroup.

Lemma 6.1.3
.

There is an embedding
W_{K}\hookrightarrow W
.

Proof.

To begin, we claim that

(6.3)

N_{K}(T)=N_{K}(T_{K}).

Indeed, let
k\in N_{K}(T)
and
t\in T_{K}
. Then there exists
t^{\prime}\in T
such that
kt=t^{\prime}k
. As both elements
kt
and
k
are fixed by
\theta
, we have

t^{\prime}k=\theta(t^{\prime}k)=\theta(t^{\prime})k,

so
t^{\prime}
is also fixed by
\theta
. Hence
N_{K}(T)\subseteq N_{K}(T_{K})
.

Conversely, consider
kTk^{-1}
for
k\in N_{K}(T_{K})
. This is a
\theta
-stable maximal torus in
G
containing
T_{K}
, so by Lemma
6.1.2
,
kTk^{-1}=T
. Hence
N_{K}(T_{K})\subseteq N_{K}(T)
.

Hence there is a natural injection

N_{K}(T_{K})/T_{K}\xhookrightarrow{i}N_{G}(T)/T_{K}.

By composing
i
with the quotient map

N_{G}(T)/T_{K}\xrightarrow{q}N_{G}(T)/T,

we obtain a map

(6.4)

W_{K}=N_{K}(T_{K})/T_{K}\xrightarrow{i\circ q}N_{G}(T)/T=W.

As
\ker q=T/T_{K}
is not contained in the
\im i
, the map (
6.4
) is injective.
∎

Because the torus
T
is
\theta
-stable,
\theta
induces an involution on
W
. Let
W^{\theta}
be the group of elements fixed by this involution. After identifying
W_{K}
with its image under the injection in Lemma
6.1.3
, we have

(6.5)

W_{K}\subseteq W^{\theta}\subseteq W.

Choose a Borel subgroup
B\subseteq G
containing
T
, and set
B_{K}=B\cap K
. The group
B_{K}
is a Borel subgroup in
K
containing
T_{K}
. Note that
B
is
\theta
-stable by construction. Let
S\subseteq W
be the set of simple reflections determined by the choice of
B
and
\Sigma^{+}\subseteq\Sigma
be the corresponding set of positive roots.

It is well-known that the
T
-fixed points in the flag variety
X=G/B
are in bijection with
W
, and each Bruhat cell contains exactly one
T
-fixed point. Specifically, we have

(6.6)

X^{T}=\{x_{w}:=wB/B\}_{w\in W},\text{ and }x_{w}\in X_{w}=B\cdot wB/B.

Lemma 6.1.4
.

All
T_{K}
-fixed points in
X
are fixed by
T
. In particular,
X^{T_{K}}=X^{T}
.

Proof.

Each
T
-fixed point
x_{w}
lies in an affine chart
U_{w}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{|\Sigma^{+}|}
on which
T
acts linearly by roots. In particular, we can identify

x_{w}=0\in U_{w}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-w(\Sigma^{+})}\mathbb{C}_{\alpha},

where
T
acts on
\mathbb{C}_{\alpha}
by
t\cdot z=\exp\alpha(t)z
for
t\in T
,
z\in\mathbb{C}_{\alpha}
. The charts
\{U_{w}\}_{w\in W}
cover

. Clearly
x_{w}
is the only
T
-fixed point in
U_{w}
.

By restriction we obtain a linear action of
T_{K}
on

, with weights

\{\widetilde{\alpha}:=\alpha|_{\mathfrak{t}_{K}}\}_{\alpha\in-w(\Sigma^{+})}.

The point
x_{w}\in U_{w}
is fixed by

. Moreover,
x_{w}
is the only
T_{K}
-fixed point in

. To see this, recall from the proof of Lemma

is maximally compact, so there are no real roots in

. Hence the restriction map

is non-zero on all roots

As the charts

cover

-fixed point which is also fixed by

∎

Lemma 6.1.5
.

Each closed
K
-orbit
\mathcal{Q}
contains
|W_{K}|

T
-fixed points. Moreover, if
x_{w}\in\mathcal{Q}
, then
x_{vw}\in\mathcal{Q}
for all

, and all representatives of the coset

Proof.

Fix a closed
K
-orbit
\mathcal{Q}
. For any

, the stabilizer

is solvable (as it is the intersection of the solvable group

with

is a closed subvariety of a projective variety), and connected (as it is a parabolic subgroup of a connected group), hence it is a Borel subgroup of

is isomorphic to the flag variety of

, so it contains
|W_{K}|

T_{K}
-fixed points. By Lemma

.

There is a natural action of

on

coming from the conjugation action of

. For

. Similarly, as

is isomorphic to the flag variety of

on

with a subgroup of

), the action of

To see that the

with the variety of Borel subalgebras of

. Under this identification, the

The involution

The closed

\displaystyle\theta(\mathfrak{b}^{w})

\displaystyle=\theta(\mathfrak{t})\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\theta(\mathfrak{g}_{w\alpha})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\theta(w)\theta(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{t}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\theta(w)\alpha}.

Here the third equality follows from the fact that

the natural shift functor, as defined in Section
3.3
. There is a right action of
\mathbb{S}\text{Bim}
on
(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}
given by the tensor product: for
M\in(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}
and

,

(6.9)

M\otimes_{R}S\in(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}

To define our category, we imitate the geometric construction in Section
5.2
. First we need a class of generating objects to play the role of trivial local systems on closed orbits.

Definition 6.2.1
.

For every
x\in W
, define the corresponding
standard bimodule

P_{x}\in(P^{K},R)
-gbim as follows. As a vector space,
P_{x}=P
. The left action of
f\in P^{K}
is given by left multiplication in
P
:

f\cdot p:=fp\text{ for $p\in P_{x}$}.

The right action of
g\in R
is given by right multiplication by
\phi(xg)
, where
\phi
is the algebra homomorphism (
6.8
):

p\cdot_{x}g:=p\phi(xg)\text{ for $p\in P_{x}$}.

Notation 6.2.2
.

We write the action of
W
on
R
(or
W_{K}
on
P
) with no symbol. It is implied from this notation that an element
x\in W
acts only on the element immediately following it. If we wish for
x\in W
to act on a product of elements, we use parentheses. For example,
wrs
denotes the product in
R
of the elements
wr
and
s
, whereas
w(rs)
denotes the image of the element
rs
under action by
w
. Note that because the action of
W
on
R=S(\mathfrak{t}^{*})
(and that of
W_{K}
on
P=S(\mathfrak{t}^{*}_{K})
) is obtained by extending the natural reflection action of
W
on
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
(resp.
W_{K}
on
\mathfrak{t}_{K}^{*}
) multiplicatively, we have
w(rs)=wrws
.

Lemma 6.2.3
.

If
w\in W_{K}
,

P_{wx}\simeq P_{x}

as
(P^{K},R)
-bimodules.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:P_{wx}\rightarrow P_{x}
be the linear map sending
p\mapsto w^{-1}p
. We claim that
\varphi
is an isomorphism of
(P^{K},R)
-bimodules.

Clearly
\varphi
is a bijection of vector spaces. Let
f\in P^{K}
,
p\in P_{wx}
. The following computation shows that
\varphi
is a left
P^{K}
-module morphism:

f\cdot\varphi(p)=f\cdot w^{-1}p=fw^{-1}p=w^{-1}fw^{-1}p=w^{-1}(fp)=\varphi(fp)=\varphi(f\cdot p).

Let
g\in R
,
p\in P_{wx}
. The following computation shows that
\varphi
is a morphism of right
R
-modules:

\displaystyle\varphi(p)\cdot_{x}g=w^{-1}p\cdot_{x}g=w^{-1}p\phi(xg)

\displaystyle=w^{-1}(pw\phi(xg))

\displaystyle=\varphi(pw\phi(xg))=\varphi(p\phi(wxg))=\varphi(p\cdot_{wx}g).

This completes the proof.
∎

Recall from Section
6.1
that
W_{K}
is contained in

, the subgroup of

-fixed elements of

(

and Lemma

-fixed point

Remark 6.2.4
.

If

is equal rank, then the subgroup

Definition 6.2.5

Let

7.
The hypercohomology functor

The bridge between the geometric category
\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
and the algebraic category
\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
is the
K
-equivariant hypercohomology functor. As in (
3.16
), we have a functor

\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}:D^{b}_{K}(X)\rightarrow H^{*}_{K}(X)\mathrm{-gmod},

where
H^{*}_{K}(X)\mathrm{-gmod}
is the category of graded modules over the graded ring
H^{*}_{K}(X)
.

Lemma 7.0.1
.

There is an isomorphism of rings

H^{*}_{K}(X)\simeq P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R,

where
P^{K}
is a
R^{W}
-module via the map
\phi
in (
6.8
).

Proof.

We use standard facts about equivariant cohomology, proofs of which can be found in
[
Bri98
]
in the case of a compact group. After a straightforward reduction from complex reductive algebraic groups to compact groups, these facts also hold in our setting. See also
[
Wys02
, Prop 1.2.1]
.

Using three applications of the induction isomorphism in equivariant cohomology
[
Bri98
, §1, Rmk. 3]
along with
[
Bri98
, Prop. 1]
, we compute:

\displaystyle H^{*}_{K}(G/B)

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{G}(G/B\times G/K)

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{G}(G\times_{B}G/K)

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{B}(G/K)

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{T}(G/K)

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{G}(G/K)\otimes_{R^{W}}R

\displaystyle=H^{*}_{K}(\mathrm{pt})\otimes_{R^{W}}R

\displaystyle=(H^{*}_{T_{K}}(\mathrm{pt}))^{W_{K}}\otimes_{R^{W}}R

\displaystyle=P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R.

∎

As in Section
3.6
, the quotient map
P^{K}\otimes R\rightarrow P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R
induces a fully faithful functor

(7.1)

P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R\mathrm{-gmod}\rightarrow(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim},

and we identify
P^{K}\otimes_{R^{W}}R\mathrm{-gmod}
with its image in
(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}
. Hence we have a functor

(7.2)

\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{K}:D^{b}_{K}(X)\rightarrow(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}.

7.1.
The essential image of the hypercohomology functor

Theorem 7.1.1
.

The essential image of the functor

\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{K}:\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}\rightarrow(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}

defined in (
7.2
) is
\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
.

By construction,
\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
is generated by objects of the form

P_{x}\otimes_{R}B_{s_{1}}\otimes_{R}B_{s_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes_{R}B_{s_{n}}

and

\mathcal{Q}
a closed
K

7.2.
Hypercohomology is fully faithful

Theorem 7.2.1
.

The equivariant hypercohomology functor

\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{K}:\mathcal{M}^{0}_{LV}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}

is fully faithful.

To prove Theorem
7.2.1
, we must show that for any
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
, the map

(7.10)

\Hom_{K}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}}\Hom_{(P^{K},R)\mathrm{-gbim}}(\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}),\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}))

is bijective. We will do so in three steps:

(1)

First, we establish that
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{K}
is compatible with certain adjunctions, allowing us to reduce to the case where
\mathcal{F}=\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. This is Lemma
7.2.4
and the preceding material.

(2)

Second, we establish that
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
is faithful by showing that the map (
7.10
) is injective. This is Theorem
7.2.5
.

(3)

Third, we establish that
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
is full by showing that the map (
7.10
) is surjective. This is Theorem
7.2.12
.

Lemma 7.2.2
.

For any
s\in S
, the functor
\Theta_{s}:\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
and the functor
-\otimes_{R}B_{s}:\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{LV}^{0}
are self-adjoint.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\in D^{b}_{K}(X)
,

\displaystyle\Hom(\mathcal{F},\pi_{s}^{*}\pi_{s*}\mathcal{G}[1])

\displaystyle=\Hom(\mathcal{F},\pi_{s}^{!}\pi_{s*}\mathcal{G}[-1])

\displaystyle=\Hom(\pi_{s!}\mathcal{F},\pi_{s*}\mathcal{G}[-1])

\displaystyle=\Hom(\pi_{s}^{*}\pi_{s*}\mathcal{F}[1],\mathcal{G}).

This establishes the first claim. To see that

is self-adjoint, note that the ring inclusion
R^{s}\hookrightarrow R
is a degree

Frobenius extension. (See
[
EMTW20
, Ch. 8]
for a detailed discussion of Frobenius extensions.) Hence we have two adjoint pairs:

(\Ind_{R^{s}}^{R},\Res_{R^{s}}^{R})\text{ and }(\Res_{R^{s}}^{R}(2),\Ind_{R^{s}}^{R}).

Each functor can be realized as tensor product:

\Ind_{R^{s}}^{R}(-)=-\otimes_{R^{s}}R\text{ and }\Res_{R^{s}}^{R}(-)=-\otimes_{R}R.

It follows that for

\displaystyle\Hom(M,N\otimes_{R}B_{s})

\displaystyle=\Hom(M,N\otimes_{R}R\otimes_{R^{s}}R(1))

\displaystyle=\Hom(M\otimes_{R}R,N\otimes_{R}R(-1))

\displaystyle=\Hom(M\otimes_{R}R\otimes_{R^{s}}R(1),N)

\displaystyle=\Hom(M\otimes_{R}B_{s},N).

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\mathrm{bim}}(\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}),\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}))\hskip 28.45274pt}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}

given by
F=c\circ b\circ a
and
G=d\circ\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}
in the pentagon diagram (
7.17
). Moreover, because
\mathcal{G}
is parity (Corollary

), the natural restriction map (
7.25
)

\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\rres}\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})

and the localization map

(7.24)

\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{i_{T_{K}}^{*}}\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G}),

where
i_{T_{K}}:X^{T_{K}}\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion of
T_{K}
-fixed points, are also injective. This is shown in Lemma
7.2.13
and Lemma
7.2.14
. In Lemma
7.2.16
we show that the image of
\Hom_{K}(\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}},\mathcal{G})
and the image of
\Hom_{\mathrm{bim}}(\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}),\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}))
in
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
agree under this series of injections. Because the diagram (
7.17
) in Lemma
7.2.6
commutes and (
7.11
) is injective (Theorem
7.2.5
), this implies the surjectivity of (
7.11
).

Let
\rres:D^{b}_{K}(X)\rightarrow D^{b}_{T_{K}}(X)
be the restriction functor sending
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{X},\overline{\mathcal{G}},\beta)
to
\rres\mathcal{G}:=(\mathcal{G}_{X},f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}},\beta)
, where
f
is the projection

X\times_{T_{K}}EK\xrightarrow{f}X\times_{K}EK.

Denote by
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\rres}\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})
the induced map on hypercohomology:

(7.25)

\varphi=(\varphi_{X},\overline{\varphi})\xmapsto{\rres}\rres\varphi:=(\varphi_{X},f^{*}\overline{\varphi}).

Lemma 7.2.13
.

If
\mathcal{G}\in D^{b}_{K}(X)
is parity, then
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{\rres}\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{T_{K}}(\rres\mathcal{G})
is injective.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{X},\overline{\mathcal{G}},\beta)\in D^{b}_{K}(X)
. The Leray spectral sequence for
\rres\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{G}_{X},f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}},\beta)\in D^{b}_{T_{K}}(X)
associated to the fibration

K/{T_{K}}\rightarrow X\times_{T_{K}}EK\xrightarrow{f}X\times_{K}EK

is

E^{pq}_{2}=H^{p}(X\times_{K}EK;R^{q}f_{*}f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}})\implies H^{p+q}(X\times_{T_{K}}EK;f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}}).

Because
f
is a fibration with fiber
K/{T_{K}}
, we have

R^{q}f_{*}f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}}\simeq\overline{\mathcal{G}}\otimes H^{q}(K/{T_{K}}).

Hence
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{T_{K}}(\rres\mathcal{G})=H^{*}(X\times_{T}EK;f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}})
can be computed with the spectral sequence

(7.26)

H^{p}(X\times_{K}EK;\overline{\mathcal{G}})\otimes H^{q}(K/{T_{K}})\implies H^{p+q}(X\times_{T_{K}}EK;f^{*}\overline{\mathcal{G}}).

Let
B_{K}=K\cap B
, so
K/B_{K}
is the flag variety of
K
. We have a fibration

B_{K}/{T_{K}}\rightarrow K/{T_{K}}\rightarrow K/B_{K}

with affine fibres, so

H^{*}(K/{T_{K}})=H^{*}(K/B_{K})\simeq P/P_{+}^{K},

where
P_{+}\subseteq P
is the augmentation ideal and
P_{+}^{K}:=P_{+}^{W_{K}}
is the
W_{K}
invariants in
P_{+}
. In particular,
H^{*}(K/{T_{K}})
vanishes in odd degrees.

By Lemma
7.2.9
, if
\mathcal{G}
is
!
-even (resp.
!
-odd), then
\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})=H^{*}(X\times_{K}EK;\overline{\mathcal{G}})
vanishes in odd (resp. even) degree. Hence if
\mathcal{G}
is
!
-parity (i.e., either
!
-even or
!
-odd), the spectral sequence (
7.26
) has a chessboard pattern, hence degenerates on the
E_{2}
page. In this case,

(7.27)

\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})\simeq P\otimes_{P^{K}}\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}).

If
\mathcal{G}
is parity, then
\mathcal{G}
is the direct sum of a
!
-even and a
!
-odd complex, hence (
7.27
) also holds. This implies the result.
∎

Next we will show that the localization map (
7.24
) is also injective.

Lemma 7.2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
and
i_{T_{K}}:X^{T_{K}}\hookrightarrow X
be the inclusion of
T_{K}
-fixed points. The localization map
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})\xrightarrow{i_{T_{K}}^{*}}\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
is injective.

Proof.

Let
\For:D^{b}_{K}(X)\rightarrow D^{b}(X)
be the forgetful functor, as in (
3.13
). Clearly
\For
factors through
D^{b}_{K}(X)\xrightarrow{\rres}D^{b}_{T_{K}}(X)
.

By the localization theorem in equivariant hypercohomology
[
GKM98
, Thm. 1.6.2]
,
i_{T_{K}}^{*}
is injective if
\rres\mathcal{G}
is
equivariantly formal
; that is, if the spectral sequence for its equivariant cohomology

(7.28)

E_{2}^{pq}=H^{p}_{T_{K}}(\mathrm{pt})\otimes\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})\implies\mathbb{H}^{p+q}_{T_{K}}(\rres\mathcal{G})

degenerates at the
E_{2}
page. Here
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})=\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{b}_{c}(X)}(\mathbb{Q}_{X},\For\mathcal{G})
as in (
3.13
). Because
H^{*}_{T_{K}}(\mathrm{pt})
vanishes in odd degree, to show that
\rres\mathcal{G}
is equivariantly formal, it is enough to show that
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})
vanishes in all degrees of one parity (as this implies the spectral sequence (
7.28
) has a checkerboard pattern, hence degenerates at
E_{2}
).

Let
C:=H^{*}(X)=R/R_{+}^{W}
be the cohomology ring of
X
. Note that
C
is a graded ring that vanishes in odd degree. Since
\mathcal{G}\in\mathcal{M}_{LV}^{0}
,
\mathcal{G}
is a direct summand of a finite direct sum of shifts of objects of the form
\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}*\bm{IC}_{s_{1}}*\cdots*\bm{IC}_{s_{n}}
for some closed orbit
\mathcal{Q}
and sequence of simple reflections
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
. If
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}*\bm{IC}_{s_{1}}*\cdots*\bm{IC}_{s_{n}})
vanishes in odd degree for all closed orbits
\mathcal{Q}
and all subsets of simple roots, then
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})
does as well, so without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{G}=\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}}*\bm{IC}_{s_{1}}*\cdots*\bm{IC}_{s_{n}}
. By
[
Soe90
, Lem. 13, Kor. 2]
, we have

\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})=C\otimes_{C^{s_{n}}}\cdots\otimes_{C^{s_{2}}}C\otimes_{C^{s_{1}}}H^{*}(\mathcal{Q})[\dim Q].

As
\mathcal{Q}\simeq K/B_{K}
is a flag variety,
H^{*}(\mathcal{Q})
vanishes in odd degrees, hence
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G})
vanishes in one parity.
∎

Our final step is to compare the images of
\Hom_{\mathrm{bim}}(H^{*}_{K}(\mathcal{Q}),\mathbb{H}_{K}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}))
and
\Hom_{K}(\bm{IC}_{\mathcal{Q}},\mathcal{G})
in
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
. To do so, we will use the
R
-bimodule structure of
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{T_{K}}(i^{*}_{T_{K}}\rres\mathcal{G})
.

The space
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})
is naturally a module over
H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X)=P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
, so it also has the structure of an
(P,R)
-bimodule. Via
i_{T_{K}}^{*}
,
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
inherits a
P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
-module structure from
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\rres\mathcal{G})
: for
\varphi\in\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
and
f\otimes g\in P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
,

f\otimes g\cdot\varphi=\varphi\circ i_{T_{K}}^{*}(f\otimes g).

With respect to this action,
i_{T_{K}}^{*}
is a homomorphism of
P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
-modules.

Recall that the
T_{K}
-fixed points in
X
align with the
T
fixed points, so they are isolated and in bijection with
W
(Lemma
6.1.4
). Denote the skyscraper sheaf on
x_{w}=wB/B\in X^{T_{K}}
by
\mathbb{Q}_{w}
. Analogously to the notation defined in (
3.10
) for raising
R
-bimodules to polynomial powers, we set the following notation for objects in derived categories: for every
q=\sum q_{i}v^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
and
\mathcal{G}
a complex in a derived category, we denote by

\mathcal{G}^{\oplus q}:=\bigoplus\mathcal{G}[i]^{\oplus q_{i}}.

Lemma 7.2.15
.

As graded
(P,R)
-bimodules,

\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})\simeq\bigoplus_{w\in W}(P_{w})^{\oplus q_{w}}

where
q_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]
are given by the decomposition
i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}(\mathbb{Q}_{w})^{\oplus q_{w}}
and
P_{w}\in(P,R)\mathrm{-gbim}
is the standard module corresponding to
w\in W
(given by the obvious adaptation to Definition
6.2.1
).

Proof.

To begin, note that since
\mathbb{Q}_{X^{T_{K}}}=\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathbb{Q}_{w}
, we clearly have

\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})=\Hom_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(\bigoplus_{w\in W}\mathbb{Q}_{w},\bigoplus_{w\in W}(\mathbb{Q}_{w})^{\oplus q_{w}})=\bigoplus_{w\in W}(P_{w})^{\oplus q_{w}}

as vector spaces, so the content of the lemma is that the
R
-bimodule structures align.

The
(P,R)
-bimodule structure on
\mathbb{H}_{T_{K}}^{\bullet}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})
comes from the algebra homomorphism

(7.29)

P\otimes_{R^{W}}R=H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X)\xrightarrow{i_{T_{K}}^{*}}H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X^{T_{K}})=\bigoplus_{w\in W}P_{w},

so to prove the lemma, we need to determine the image of an element
f\otimes g\in P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
under
i_{T_{K}}^{*}
. The algebra
P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
is generated by
P\otimes 1
and
1\otimes X(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
, so it suffices to determine the image of
p\otimes 1
and
1\otimes\chi
for
p\in R
and
\chi\in X(T)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

The map (
7.29
) is compatible with the left
P=H^{*}_{T_{K}}(\mathrm{pt})
-module structures on either side because the commutativity of the the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{T_{K}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{pt}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{T_{K}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{T_{K}}}

implies the commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X^{T_{K}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{T_{K}}(\mathrm{pt})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{T_{K}}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{T_{K}}^{*}}

Hence

i_{T_{K}}^{*}(p\otimes 1)=p\cdot i_{T_{K}}^{*}(1\otimes 1)=p\cdot(1,\ldots,1)=(p,\ldots,p).

It remains to determine the image of
1\otimes\chi
. First, note that we have a natural ring homomorphism
P\rightarrow H_{T_{K}}^{*}(X^{T_{K}})
given by the Borel homomorphism. The construction is similar to that in the proof of Lemma
7.1.2
: for
\chi\in X(T_{K})
and

, construct
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundles

\displaystyle ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}G\times_{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{\chi}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mu^{\chi}}ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}G\times_{B}\mathrm{pt}\simeq ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}X

\displaystyle ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}wB\times_{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{\chi}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\mu_{w}^{\chi}}ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}wB\times_{B}\mathrm{pt}\simeq BT_{K},

where
\chi
is inflated trivially to a character of
B
. By taking first Chern classses, we obtain ring homomorphisms

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\varphi}H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X);\chi\mapsto c_{1}(\mu^{\chi}),\text{ and }

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\varphi_{w}}H^{*}_{T_{K}}(x_{w});\chi\mapsto c_{1}(\mu_{w}^{\chi}).

Because
\mu^{\chi}
and
\mu^{\chi}_{w}
fit into the Cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}G\times_{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{\chi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}G\times_{B}\mathrm{pt}\simeq ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}wB\times_{B}\mathbb{C}^{\times}_{\chi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ET_{K}\times_{T_{K}}wB\times_{B}\mathrm{pt}\simeq BT_{K},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu^{\chi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu_{w}^{\chi}}

the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{w\in W}H^{*}_{T_{K}}(x_{w})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{*}_{T_{K}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{w\in W}\varphi_{w}}}

commutes. This gives a natural ring homomorphism
\bigoplus_{w\in W}\varphi_{w}:P\rightarrow H^{*}_{T_{K}}(X^{T_{K}})
. The image of

is then given by pre-composing this ring homomorphism with
\phi:R\rightarrow P
:

1\otimes\chi\mapsto\bigoplus_{w\in W}1\otimes\varphi_{w}\circ\phi(\chi).

In the proof of Lemma
7.1.2
, we showed that the right action of
g\in R
on
p\in P\simeq H^{*}_{T_{K}}(x_{w})
obtained from
\varphi_{w}\circ\phi
is given by

p\cdot g=p\varphi(wg),

so (
7.29
) sends

1\otimes\chi\mapsto(\phi(wx))_{w\in W}.

Hence for
f\otimes g\in P\otimes_{R^{W}}R
,

i_{T_{K}}^{*}:f\otimes g\mapsto(f\phi(wg))_{w\in W}.

This completes the proof. ∎

-bimodule structure of
\mathbb{H}^{\bullet}_{T_{K}}(i_{T_{K}}^{*}\rres\mathcal{G})

Pell and Pell-Lucas Numbers as difference of two repdigits
Bilizimbéyé EDJEOU and Bernadette Faye

Abstract
. Let { P n } n ≥ 0 \{P_{n}\}_{n\geq 0} be the sequence of Pell numbers defined by P 0 = 0 P_{0}=0 , P 1 = 1 P_{1}=1 and P n + 2 = 2 ​ P n + 1 + P n P_{n+2}=2P_{n+1}+P_{n} for all n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 and let { Q n } n ≥ 0 \{Q_{n}\}_{n\geq 0} be its companion sequence, the Pell-Lucas numbers defined by Q 0 = Q 1 = 2 Q_{0}=Q_{1}=2 and Q n + 2 = 2 ​ Q n + 1 + Q n Q_{n+2}=2Q_{n+1}+Q_{n} for all n ≥ 0 n\geq 0 . In this paper, we find all Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers which can be written as difference of two repdigits. It is shown that the largest Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers which can be written as difference of two repdigits are P 6 = 70 = 77 − 7 and Q 7 = 478 = 555 − 77 . P_{6}=70=77-7\quad\quad\hbox{and}\quad\quad Q_{7}=478=555-77.

1.
Introduction

Let
(P_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of Pell numbers given by
P_{0}=0
,
P_{1}=1
and

P_{n+2}=2P_{n+1}+P_{n}\quad{\text{\rm for all}}\quad n\geq 0.

The Pell-Lucas sequence
(Q_{n})_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the same recurrence as the sequence of Pell numbers
with initial conditions
Q_{0}=Q_{1}=2.
If
(\alpha,\beta)=(1+{\sqrt{2}},1-{\sqrt{2}})
is the pair of roots of the characteristic equation
x^{2}-2x-1=0

of both the Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers, then the Binet formulas for their general terms are:

P_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}\quad{\text{\rm and}}\quad Q_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n}\quad{\text{\rm for~all}}\quad n\geq 0.

This implies easily that the inequalities

(1)

\alpha^{n-2}\leq P_{n}\leq\alpha^{n-1}\quad\hbox{for $n\geq 2$}

and

(2)

\alpha^{n-1}\leq Q_{n}\leq\alpha^{n+1}\quad\hbox{for $n\geq 1$}

hold.

Given an integer
g>1
, a base
g
-repdigit is a number of the form

N=a\cdot\frac{g^{m}-1}{g-1}\quad\quad\hbox{for some $m\geq 1$ and $a\in\{1,\ldots,(g-1)\}$}.

When
g=10
, such number are better know as a
repdigit
.
Investigation of the repdigits in the second-order linear recurrence sequences has been of interest to mathematicians. All Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers which are repdigits have been found in
[
10
]
. The largest repdigits in Pell and Pell-Lucas sequences are
P_{3}=5
and
Q_{2}=6
, respectively. Subsequently, Fibonacci, Lucas, Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers which are expressible as sum of two repdigits have been studied in [
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
].

Then, it is natural to replace in the above problems the sums by diferences. Recently, Erduwan at al. found in
[
9
]
all Fibonacci and Lucas numbers which are difference of two repdigits.

In this paper, we study the same question and ask which
Pell and Pell-Lucas numbers can be written as a difference of two repdigits. That is, we study the Diophantine equations

(3)

\displaystyle P_{k}=a_{1}\cdot\frac{10^{n}-1}{9}-a_{2}\cdot\frac{10^{m}-1}{9}

and

(4)

\displaystyle Q_{k}=a_{1}\cdot\frac{10^{n}-1}{9}-a_{2}\cdot\frac{10^{m}-1}{9}

where
k
,
n
,
m
are some positive integers with
n\geq 2
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,9\}
.
We have the following results.

Theorem 1
.

If
P_{k}
is expressible as a difference of two repdigits, then

P_{k}\in\{2,5,12,29,70\},

with

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2=11-9,\quad P_{3}=5=11-6,

\displaystyle P_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 12=111-99,\quad P_{5}=29=33-4,

\displaystyle P_{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 70=77-7.

Theorem 2
.

If
Q_{k}
is expressible as a difference of two repdigits, then

Q_{k}\in\{2,5,6,14,34,82,478\},

with

\displaystyle Q_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2=11-9,\quad Q_{1}=5=11-6,

\displaystyle Q_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6=11-5,\quad Q_{3}=14=22-8,

\displaystyle Q_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 34=111-77,\quad Q_{5}=82=88-6,

\displaystyle Q_{7}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 478=555-77.

2.1.
Linear forms in logarithms

To prove our main result Theorem

[

, which is one of our main tools. We start
with recalling some basic definitions and results from algebraic number theory

Let
\gamma
be an algebraic number of degree

with minimal primitive polynomial over the integers

a_{0}x^{d}+a_{1}x^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{d}=a_{0}\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\gamma^{(i)}),

where the leading coefficient

is positive and the
\eta^{(i)}

\gamma
. Then the

of

h(\gamma):=\dfrac{1}{d}\left(\log a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\log\left(\max\{|\gamma^{(i)}|,1\}\right)\right).

In particular, if

is a rational number with
\gcd(p,q)=1
and

, then
h(\gamma)=\log\max\{|p|,q\}
. The following are some of the properties of the logarithmic height function
h(\cdot)
, which will be used in the next sections of this paper without reference:

\displaystyle h(\eta\pm\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\eta)+h(\gamma)+\log 2,

(5)

\displaystyle h(\eta\gamma^{\pm 1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle h(\eta)+h(\gamma),

\displaystyle h(\eta^{s})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|s|h(\eta)~~~~~~(s\in\mathbb{Z}).

Theorem 3

(Matveev)
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t}
be positive real algebraic numbers in a real algebraic number field

\mathbb{K}
of degree

be nonzero integers, and assume that

2.2.
Baker-Davenport reduction lemma

During the calculations, we get upper bounds on our variables which are too large, thus we need to reduce them. To do so, we use some results from the theory of continued fractions. Specifically, for a nonhomogeneous linear form in two integer variables, we use a slight variation of a result due to Dujella and Pethő (see
[
8

[

Lemma 1

(Dujella, Pethő)

Let

in positive integers

u\leq M\quad{\text{and}}\quad w\geq\dfrac{\log(Aq/\varepsilon)}{\log B}.

Finally, the following lemma is also useful. It it can be found in

Lemma 2

\Big|1-{(a_{1}-a_{2}10^{m-n})\cdot 10^{n}\cdot\alpha^{-k}}\cdot 9^{-1}\Big|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2k}}+\frac{16}{9\alpha^{k}}<\frac{4}{\alpha^{k}}.

Now, we can apply again Theorem
3
to the above inequality with

(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3})=(\alpha,10,(a_{1}-a_{2}10^{m-n})/9)

and

(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(-k,n,1).

Note that
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
are positive real numbers and elements of the field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2})
. Therefore the degree of the field
\mathbb{K}
is equal to
D=2
. Let

\Lambda_{2}=1-\frac{(a_{1}-a_{2}10^{m-n})\cdot 10^{n}\cdot\alpha^{-k}}{9}.

If
\Lambda=0
, then get that
\alpha^{2k}\in\mathbb{Q}
, which is false for
k>0
. Using the properties of the logarithmic height in (
2.1
), we have that

h(\gamma_{1})=h(\alpha)=\frac{\log\alpha}{2}\leq\frac{0.882}{2},\quad h(\gamma_{2})=\log 10

and

\displaystyle h(\gamma_{3})

\displaystyle=h((a_{1}-a_{2}10^{m-n})/9)

\displaystyle\leq h(9)+h(a_{1})+h(a_{2})+(n-m)h(10)+\log 2

\displaystyle\leq 8.33+(n-m)\log 10.

By Equation (
20
), we get:

h(\gamma_{3})\leq 35\cdot 10^{13}\cdot(1+\log(k+2)).

So we can take
A_{1}=\log(\alpha)=0.9,A_{2}=4.7
and
A_{3}=35\cdot 10^{13}\cdot(1+\log(k+2))
. Since
n<k
and
B\geq\max(n,k,1)
, we can take
B=k
. Thus, we obtain by inequality (
23
) and Theorem
3
that

4\cdot\alpha^{-k}>\Lambda_{2}>\exp(C\cdot(1+\log 2)\cdot(1+\log(k+2))(0.9)(4.6)(35\cdot 10^{13}\cdot(1+\log(k+2)))),

where
C=-1.4\cdot 30^{6}\cdot 3^{4.5}\cdot 2^{2}.
By a simple computation, it follows that

(24)

k<1.7\cdot 10^{27}\cdot(1+\log(k+2))^{2}.

Now using the nequalities (
24
) and (
20
), a computer search with SageMath gives us that
k<9\cdot 10^{30}
and

n-m<13\cdot 10^{14}
.

3.2.
Reducing the Bounding of
k

Now let us reduce the upper bound on
k
. Firstly, we determine a suitable upper bound on
n-m
, and later we use Lemma
1
to conclude that
n
must be smaller than
150
. Let

z_{1}:=k\log\alpha-n\log 10+\log(9/a_{1}).

From the inequality (
19
), we have that

|x|=|e^{z_{1}}-1|\leq\frac{21}{10^{n-m}}<\frac{21}{10^{2}}

for
n-m\geq 2.
Choosing
a:=0.1
, we get the inequality

z_{1}=|\log(x+1)|<\frac{-\log(1-21/10^{2})}{21/10^{2}}\cdot\frac{21}{10^{n-m}}<21\cdot 10^{m-n}

by Lemma
2
. Thus it follows that

0<|k\log\alpha-n\log 10+\log(9/a_{1})|<21\cdot 10^{m-n}.

Dividing this inequality by
\log 10
, we obtain

(25)

\Big|k\left(\frac{\log\alpha}{\log 10}\right)-n+\left(\frac{\log(9/a_{1})}{\log 10}\right)\Big|<10^{m-n+1}.

We can take
\gamma:=\frac{\log\alpha}{\log 10}\not\in\mathbb{Q}
and
M=13\cdot 10^{14}.
Then we found that
q_{70}
, the denominator of the

-th convergent of
\gamma
is greater than
6M
. Now take

In this case, considering the fact that

\epsilon(\mu):=||\mu q_{38}||-M||\gamma q_{38}||>0.02249.

Let
A=10
,

and

in Lemma
1
. Thus, using Sagemath, we can say that the inequality (

So

When
a_{1}=9
, the parameter
\mu=0
; and the resulting inequality from (
25
) has the shape

0<|a\gamma-b|<10\cdot 10^{m-n}
,

with
\gamma
being an irrational number and

. So, one can appeal to the known properties of the convergents of the continued fractions to obtain a nontrivial lower bound for

|a\gamma-b|
.

Let us see.
When
a_{1}=9
, from (
25
), we get that

(26)

0<k\gamma-n<\frac{10}{10^{n-m}}.

Let
[a_{0},a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4},a_{5},a_{6},a_{7},...]=[0;2,1,1,2,2\ldots]
be the continued fraction expansion of

, and let

p_{n}/q_{n}
its

) and (

Now let

z_{2}:=n\log 10-k\log\alpha+\log((a_{1}-a_{2}10^{m-n})/9).

23

Put
\gamma:=\frac{\log 10}{\log\alpha}\not\in\mathbb{Q}

q_{68}

Stable equivalence relations on 4-manifolds
Daniel Kasprowski, John Nicholson, Simona Veselá

Abstract
. Kreck’s modified surgery gives an approach to classifying smooth 2 ​ n 2n -manifolds up to stable diffeomorphism, i.e. up to connected sum with copies of S n × S n S^{n}\times S^{n} . In dimension 4, we use a combination of modified and classical surgery to study various stable equivalence relations which we compare to stable diffeomorphism. Most importantly, we consider homotopy equivalence up to stabilisation with copies of S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} . As an application, we show that closed oriented homotopy equivalent 4-manifolds with abelian fundamental group are stably diffeomorphic. We give analogues of the cancellation theorems of Hambleton–Kreck for homotopy equivalence up to stabilisations. Finally, we give a complete algebraic obstruction to the existence of closed smooth 4-manifolds which are homotopy equivalent but not simple homotopy equivalent up to connected sum with S 2 × S 2 S^{2}\times S^{2} .

Introduction

Surgery theory, as developed by Browder, Novikov, Sullivan, Wall and others, gives a method for determining when two closed
n

but work in the topological category over

Kreck’s modified surgery
[

and a diffeomorphism

The aim of this article is to introduce a wider range of stable equivalence relations on 4-manifolds which can be studied using modified surgery.
By

1.1.
Comparison between stable equivalence relations

Stable diffeomorphism has for example been studied in
[
62
,
28
,
38
]

\xi

,

,

For the other equivalence relations coming from

\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize stable diffeomorphism ($\cong^{\st}$)}\\
\text{\normalsize($\Leftrightarrow$ stable homeomorphism ($\cong_{\text{top}}^{\st}$))}\end{subarray}\,\Rightarrow\,\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize simple homotopy equivalence}\\
\text{\normalsize up to stabilisations ($\simeq_{s}^{\st}$)}\end{subarray}\,\Rightarrow\,\begin{subarray}{c}\text{\normalsize homotopy equivalence up}\\
\text{\normalsize to stabilisations ($\simeq^{\st}$)}\end{subarray}

for closed oriented smooth
4

We will now consider the extent to which these equivalence relations coincide.
In order to study the difference between homotopy equivalence up to stabilisation and stable homeomorphism, we will make the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

A group
\pi
satisfies

if any two closed oriented smooth homotopy equivalent
4
-manifolds
M,M^{\prime}
with fundamental group
\pi
are stably diffeomorphic.

Remark 1.3
.

This is equivalent to the same definition with ‘homotopy equivalent’ replaced by ‘homotopy equivalent after stabilisations’. In particular,
\pi
satisfies stable rigidity if and only if the three stable equivalence relations coincide for 4-manifolds with fundamental group
\pi

It follows from

A
that if

is injective for a group

[

]

is not injective and Teichner gave examples of pairs of smooth manifolds with quaternionic
[

that are homotopy equivalent but not stably diffeomorphic. While

is not injective for abelian groups in general, we use

Theorem B
.

If
\pi

satisfies stable rigidity. That is, if

are two closed oriented smooth homotopy equivalent
4
-manifolds with abelian fundamental groups, then

are stably diffeomorphic.

Equivalently, homotopy equivalence up to stabilisations coincides with stable diffeomorphism for closed oriented smooth
4

[

stated above.

We will also consider the case where

There exist closed oriented smooth

If

1.2.
Comparison between stable and unstable equivalence relations

Kreck’s modified surgery makes it possible to classify closed oriented smooth
4
-manifolds up to stable diffeomorphism (or equivalently stable homeomorphism) over special choices of fundamental group

In order to complete the classification up to homeomorphism (resp. (simple) homotopy equivalence), it remains to solve the corresponding

In the case of homeomorphism, which we denote by

M\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})\cong_{\text{top}}N\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})\quad\Rightarrow\quad M\cong_{\text{top}}N.

This was studied by Hambleton-Kreck
[

and Crowley-Sixt
[

The following allows us study the cancellation problem up to (simple) homotopy equivalence and compare it to the homeomorphism case.

Theorem D
.

Let
M
,
N
be closed oriented topological
4
-manifolds with good fundamental groups. If
M
,
N
are (simple) homotopy equivalent up to stabilisations, then there exists a topological
4
-manifold
N^{\prime}
which is (simple) homotopy equivalent to
N
and stably homeomorphic to
M
.

Remark 1.7
.

Homotopy equivalence up to stabilisations coincides with the equivalence relation generated by homotopy equivalence and stable homeomorphism.
Theorem

D
shows that, on the class of closed topological
4
-manifolds with good fundamental group, only a single homotopy equivalence and a single stable homeomorphism are needed.

The proof is based on the observation that, if
M
,
N
are closed oriented topological
4
-manifolds with good fundamental groups such that
M\simeq N\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
, then there exists a topological
4
-manifold
N^{\prime}
such that
M\cong_{\text{top}}N^{\prime}\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})
and

(see

6.1
).
This is shown to be a consequence of Freedman’s disc embedding theorem
[
25
]
(see also
[

It was shown by Hambleton-Kreck that, if two closed oriented topological 4-manifolds

,

M\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})\cong_{\text{top}}N\#(S^{2}\times S^{2})

Combining this theorem with

Corollary 1.8

Let

In fact, we prove a much more general statement about the relationship between the cancellation problems for homeomorphism and (simple) homotopy equivalence (see

Remark 2.1

Since it is not currently known whether topological
4
-manifolds are homeomorphic to CW-complexes, the usual definition of simple homotopy equivalence for CW-complexes may not apply. A more general notion of simple homotopy equivalence, which applies to topological

2.1.
Kreck’s modified surgery

We start this section by reviewing Kreck’s stable classification result. While

]

Let

4.
Stable rigidity

Recall from
Definition

1.2
that a finitely presented group
\pi
satisfies
stable rigidity
if any two closed, oriented, smooth, homotopy equivalent
4
-manifolds with fundamental group
\pi
are stably diffeomorphic (or equivalently stably homeomorphic).
This terminology is inspired by the Borel conjecture which states that aspherical closed manifolds (in any dimension) are determined up to homeomorphism by their homotopy type, i.e. by the fundamental group.

We will start by studying the analogous notion in the topological category, which we refer to as
strong stable rigidity
(
Definition

4.1
), before establishing
Proposition

4.4
and
Corollaries

4.5
and
4.6
which are the basis for our analyses of whether given groups satisfy stable rigidity.

For the remainder of this section, we will then determine when stable rigidity holds for a range of groups
\pi
. In
Section

4.2
, we relate stable rigidity for
\pi
to that of its odd index subgroups. In
Sections

4.3
,
4.4
,
4.5
and
4.6
, we then determine stable rigidity for groups which are abelian, quaternion, dihedral, semi-dihedral, or modular maximal-cyclic.

4.1.
General results for (strong) stable rigidity

We begin by making the following key definition.
This can be viewed as the analogue of stable rigidity in the topological category.

Definition 4.1
.

A group
\pi
satisfies
strong stable rigidity
if any two closed, oriented, almost spin, homotopy equivalent manifolds
M
and
M^{\prime}
with fundamental group
\pi
are stably homeomorphic.

Remark 4.2
.

(a)

For any finitely presented group
\pi
there exist closed, oriented, homotopy equivalent manifolds with fundamental group
\pi
that are not stably homeomorphic. A pair of such manifolds can be constructed by starting with any closed, oriented manifold
M
with fundamental group
\pi
and considering
M\#\mathbb{CP}^{2}
and

, where
{*\mathbb{CP}^{2}}
is the Chern manifold constructed by Freedman in
[
24
, p. 370]
. For this reason

4.1
is restricted to almost spin manifolds.

(b)

In contrast, two closed, oriented, smooth, totally non-spin manifolds

and
M^{\prime}
are stably diffeomorphic if and only if they have the same signature and the images of their fundamental classes in
H_{4}(\pi;\mathbb{Z})/\Out(\pi)
agree. This follows from
[
45
, Theorem C]
, see also
[
42
, Lemma 2.1]
. In particular, they are stably diffeomorphic if they are homotopy equivalent.

(c)

A group
\pi
that satisfies strong stable rigidity also satisfies stable rigidity. This can be seen as follows. Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be a pair of closed, oriented, smooth, homotopy equivalent manifolds with fundamental group
\pi
. If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are totally non-spin, then they are stably diffeomorphic by (

). If
M
and
M^{\prime}
are almost spin, they are stably diffeomorphic if
\pi
satisfies stable rigidity.

(d)

We could define (strong)

stable rigidity by changing homotopy equivalence to
simple
homotopy equivalence in

1.2
and
4.1
. Then the following results about (strong) stable rigidity also hold for (strong) simple stable rigidity if

is substituted by
\ker(\kappa_{2}^{s})
.

By definition of strong stable rigidity, we only consider manifolds that are almost spin.

is injective for every torsion free group as observed by Davis

See

Proposition 4.4
.

Let a group

.

Proof.

Let

and let

be the image of

in

. Then there exists a 4-manifold

with normal

-type
\xi
and a normal

-smoothing

and a manifold

with normal

\xi
and
\xi

-smoothing

. By

3.2
,

and

are stably homotopy equivalent since

is in the image of

by assumption. Since

and

Conversely, assume that

The second statement follows from the fact that

Corollary 4.5
.

Let

be a group. Then

Corollary 4.6

4.2.
Stable rigidity for subgroups of odd index

We now consider some results relating stable rigidity of
\pi
to its
2
-Sylow subgroup.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\colon G\to\pi
be a group homomorphism inducing a surjection on
\ker(\kappa_{2}^{h})
. If
G
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity, so does
\pi
.

Proof.

If
G
satisfies strong stable rigidity, then
\kappa^{h}_{2}
is injective for
G
and hence also for
\pi
by assumption. Thus
\pi
satisfies strong stable rigidity.

Let
x\in H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
be in
\ker(\kappa_{2}^{h})
and
w\in H^{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
such that
w\frown x=0
. Then there exists a preimage
x^{\prime}\in H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
that also is in the kernel of
\kappa^{h}_{2}
. Since
G
satisfies stable rigidity and
f^{*}w\frown x^{\prime}=w\frown x=0
,
0=[x^{\prime}]\in E_{2,2}^{\infty}(\xi(G,f^{*}w))
. By naturality also
0=[x]\in E_{2,2}^{\infty}(\xi(\pi,w))
. It follows from
Proposition

4.4
that
\pi
satisfies stable rigidity.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
G\leq\pi
be a subgroup of odd index.
If
G
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity, so does
\pi
.

Proof.

Let
i\colon G\to\pi
be the inclusion. Let
x\in H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
be in the kernel of
\kappa^{h}_{2}
. Let
x^{\prime}:=\tr(x)\in H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
, where
\tr
is the transfer map. Since
G
has odd index,
\tr\circ i_{*}
is the identity and thus
x^{\prime}
is a preimage of
x
. By
Proposition

2.16
,
\kappa^{h}_{2}(x^{\prime})=p^{*}(\kappa^{h}_{2}(x))=0
. Hence
i
induces a surjection on the kernel of
\kappa^{h}_{2}
. The proposition now follows from
Lemma

4.7
.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
1\to P\to\pi\to G\to 1
be a short exact sequence with
P
a finite group of odd order.
If
G
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity, then
\pi
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity.

Moreover, if the above sequence splits, then the converse holds. That is,
G
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity if and only if
\pi
satisfies (strong) stable rigidity.

Proof.

If
\pi
does not satisfy stable rigidity, then
Proposition

4.4
implies that there exists
w\in H^{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
and
x\in\ker(\kappa_{2}^{h})\leq H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
such that the image of
x
is non-trivial in
F_{2,2}\leq\Omega_{4}(\xi(\pi,w))
.
Similarly, if
\pi
does not satisfy strong stable rigidity, then there exists
w\in H^{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
and
x\in\ker(\kappa_{2}^{h})\cap\ker(-\frown w)\leq H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)
with the corresponding properties.

Since
\widetilde{H}_{*}(P;\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
, the Lyndon-Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence gives us the isomorphism of the reduced homology
p_{*}\colon\widetilde{H}_{*}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\widetilde{H}_{*}(G,\mathbb{Z}/2)
induced by the projection
p\colon\pi\to G
. Dually,
p^{*}
is an isomorphism and there exists
w^{\prime}\in H^{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
with
p^{*}w^{\prime}=w
.

We now compare the spectral sequences for
\Omega_{4}(\xi(\pi,w))
and
\Omega_{4}(\xi(G,w^{\prime}))
.
Recall that the map

d_{2}\colon H_{4}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)

is given by
\Sq_{2}+-\frown w
. We have

\Sq_{2}(p_{*}y)+p_{*}y\frown w^{\prime}=p_{*}(\Sq_{2}y+y\frown p^{*}w^{\prime})=p_{*}(\Sq_{2}y+y\frown w).

Since
p_{*}\colon H_{4}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)\to H_{4}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)
is an isomorphism, we have an isomorphism

H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{Im}d_{2}\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{Im}d_{2}.

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(d_{2}\colon H_{5}(\pi;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{3}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ker(d_{2}\colon H_{5}(G;\mathbb{Z})\to H_{3}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{Im}d_{2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{Im}d_{2}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{3}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

We get
p_{*}\operatorname{Im}(d_{3})\subset\operatorname{Im}(d_{3})\subset H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)/\operatorname{Im}d_{2}
. This shows that
p
induces an injection

H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)/d_{2}d_{3}\xrightarrow{p_{*}}H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)/d_{2}d_{3}.

It follows the image of
p_{*}(x)\in\ker(\kappa_{2}^{h}(G))
in

is non-trivial. If
x\frown w=0
, then
p_{*}x\frown w^{\prime}=p_{*}(x\frown p^{*}w^{\prime})=p_{*}(x\frown w)=0
. Hence

does not satisfy (strong) stable rigidity by

4.4
. This proves the first part of the corollary.

We now assume that the short exact sequence splits. In this case

is surjective and it follows from the commutative diagram above, that

H_{2}(\pi;\mathbb{Z}/2)/d_{2}d_{3}\xrightarrow{p_{*}}H_{2}(G;\mathbb{Z}/2)/d_{2}d_{3}

is an isomorphism. Moreover, it follows from the naturality of
\kappa_{2}^{h}
that
p_{*}

. Thus the second part of the corollary again follows from
Proposition

4.4
.
∎

4.3.

In this subsection we prove

Lemma 4.10

Let

or

Also note that

(1)

If

for all
i,j
such that
4

n_{i}
or

.

(2)

If there exist

such that
4
divides
n_{i}
or

with

, then

By

We now show (2). Under the projection

Lemma 4.12

Let

4.5.
Dihedral and semi-dihedral groups

For a finite group
\pi
, let
O(\pi)
denote the odd order normal subgroup of
\pi
of maximal order (see, for example,
[
7
, Section 1.2]
). This is unique since, if
N_{1},N_{2}\leq\pi
are odd order normal subgroups, then
N_{1}\cdot N_{2}\leq\pi
is an odd order normal subgroup which contains
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
.
We can therefore always view
\pi
as sitting in an extension

1\to O(\pi)\to\pi\to\pi/O(\pi)\to 1.

By the Schur-Zassenhaus theorem, extensions of groups of coprime order split as semidirect products. Let
S
denote the Sylow
2
-subgroup of
\pi
. It follows that
\pi\cong P\rtimes S
for some group
P
of odd order if and only if
\pi/O(\pi)\cong S
, and in this case we necessarily have that
P\cong O(\pi)
.

We will now discuss the group cohomology and extension properties of groups whose Sylow
2
-subgroup is dihedral or semi-dihedral. Different cases arise according to the number of conjugacy classes of elements of order two (i.e. involutions) and order four there are in
\pi
.

4.5.1.
Dihedral groups

For
n\geq 3
, let
D_{2^{n}}=\langle x,y\mid x^{2},y^{2},(xy)^{2^{n-1}}\rangle
be the dihedral group.
By
[
1
, IV. Theorem 2.7]
, we have

H^{*}(D_{2^{n}};\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2[x,y,u]/(xy=0),

where
x,y
are the
1
-dimensional classes dual to the generators in the above presentation and
u
is
2
-dimensional with
\Sq^{1}(u)=(x+y)u
.

From
[
35
, Theorem 5.2]
we get the calculation of the integral cohomology group of the dihedral group
D_{2^{n}}
for
n\geq 3

H^{*}(D_{2^{n}};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}[a_{2},b_{2},c_{3},d_{4}]/(2a_{2},2b_{2},2c_{3},2^{k-1}d_{4},b_{2}^{2}+a_{2}b_{2},c_{3}^{2}+a_{2}d_{4}).

Lemma 4.17
.

With respect to the dual basis of
\{x^{5},y^{5},x^{3}u,y^{3}u,xu^{2},yu^{2}\}
, the image of
H_{5}(D_{2^{n}},\mathbb{Z})
in

We will now use

We will begin with the following definition.

The

Remark 6.6

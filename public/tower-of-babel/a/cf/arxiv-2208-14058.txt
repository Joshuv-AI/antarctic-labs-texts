Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties with finite Coxeter parts
Xuhua He, Sian Nie, Qingchao Yu

Abstract
. In this paper, we study affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties X w ​ ( b ) X_{w}(b) when the finite part of the element w w in the Iwahori–Weyl group is a partial σ \sigma -Coxeter element. We show that such w w is a cordial element and X w ​ ( b ) ≠ ∅ X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset if and only if b b satisfies a certain Hodge–Newton indecomposability condition. The main result of this paper is that for such w w and b b , X w ​ ( b ) X_{w}(b) has a simple geometric structure: the σ \sigma -centralizer of b b acts transitively on the set of irreducible components of X w ​ ( b ) X_{w}(b) ; and each irreducible component is an iterated fibration over a classical Deligne–Lusztig variety of Coxeter type, and the iterated fibers are either 𝔸 1 \mathbb{A}^{1} or 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Classical/affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties

The classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties were introduced by Deligne and Lusztig in
[
3
]
. They play a crucial role in the representation theory of finite reductive groups. They are defined for a connected reductive group
\mathbf{G}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For any element
w
in the (finite) Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q})
, the corresponding Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}
is a certain locally closed subvariety of the flag variety of
\mathbf{G}(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q})
, and it admits a natural action of the finite reductive group
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. It is known that

(a)
The classical Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}
is smooth and of dimension equal to the length of
w
, and the finite reductive group
\mathbf{G}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
acts transitively on the set of irreducible components of
X_{w}
.

Affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties were introduced by Rapoport in
[
27
]
as the affine analog of classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties. They serve as group-theoretic models for Shimura varieties and shtukas. They are defined for a connected reductive group
\mathbf{G}
over a non-archimedean local field
F
. Let
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. For any element
w
in the Iwahori–Weyl group
\tilde{W}
of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
and any element
b\in\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
, the corresponding affine Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
is a certain locally closed subscheme of finite type in the affine flag variety of
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
, and it admits a natural action of the
\sigma
-centralizer
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
of
b
. Unlike classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties, which have the nice geometric structure described in (a), the geometric structures of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties are very complicated:

•

For many pairs
(w,b)
,
X_{w}(b)
are empty.

•

Even if
X_{w}(b)
is nonempty, it is not equi-dimensional in general, and it is very difficult to determine its dimension.

•

In general, the group
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
does not acts transitively on the set of irreducible components of
X_{w}(b)
, and very little is known about this
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-action.

•

The irreducible components of
X_{w}(b)
, in general, have a very complicated geometric structure.

We refer to the survey article
[
16
]
and the paper
[
17
]
for recent developments regarding the nonemptiness pattern and the dimension formula for
X_{w}(b)
.

1.2.
Main result

In
[
25
]
, Milićević and Viehmann introduced the notion of cordial elements. The geometry of the affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties associated with cordial elements is “well-behaved” in the following sense. If
w
is a cordial element, then the elements
b
with
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
form a saturated set in the sense of
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
, and for any such
b
, there is a simple dimension formula for
X_{w}(b)
. Moreover,
X_{w}(b)
is equi-dimensional. Schremmer gave a classification of the cordial elements in his recent work
[
28
]
.

However, even for a cordial element
w
, very little is known about the
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-orbits on the set of irreducible components of
X_{w}(b)
or about the geometric structure of the irreducible components of
X_{w}(b)
.

On the other hand, by
[
13
]
, there is a family of elements in the Iwahori–Weyl group whose associated affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties have very simple geometric structures. We denote by
\sigma
the Frobenius morphism on
\mathbf{G}(\breve{F})
and the induced group automorphism on the Iwahori–Weyl group
\tilde{W}
. Suppose that
w
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
; then there exists a unique
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
with
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
. In this case, there exist a parahoric subgroup
P
of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
and a classical Deligne–Lusztig variety
X
(associated with the reductive quotient of
P
) such that
X_{w}(b)\cong\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}X
. Such a simple geometric structure has been used in the study of certain Shimura varieties with simple geometric structure (see
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
9
]
). However, these minimal length elements
w
form only a tiny fraction of the whole Iwahori–Weyl group, and such a simple geometric structure only occurs in a few cases.

In this paper, we will focus on another family of elements in
\tilde{W}
. For any
w\in\tilde{W}
, we define its finite part to be the image of
w
under the map
\eta_{\sigma}:\tilde{W}W
(see §
2.5
). The main result of this paper is that if the finite part of
w
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
, then the associated affine Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
for any
b
has a simple geometric structure.

Theorem 1.1

(See Theorem
2.7
)
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
such that
\eta_{\sigma}(w)
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
. Then

(1)

w
is a cordial element;

(2)

X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
b
satisfies a certain Hodge–Newton indecomposability condition;

(3)

For any
b
with
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
, there exists a parahoric subgroup
P
of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
and a classical Deligne–Lusztig variety
X
of Coxeter type, and an iterated fibration
YX
whose iterated fibers are either
\mathbb{A}^{1}
or
\mathbb{G}_{m}
such that
X_{w}(b)\cong\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}Y
.

We refer to §
2
for the precise statement and the definition of the notions used here. The special case of Part (3) where
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}
,
b
is basic and
w
is certain element with finite Coxeter part was studied by Shimada
[
29
]
.

1.3.
Strategy

One major tool used in the study of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties is the Deligne–Lusztig reduction method
[
13
]
. Based on the Deligne–Lusztig reduction, a close relationship between affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties and the class polynomials of affine Hecke algebras was established in
[
13
]
. One remarkable property of these class polynomials is that they are polynomials in
(\mathbf{q}-1)
with non-negative integral coefficients. With each element
w\in\tilde{W}
, we may associate a reduction tree, which encodes the information on the reduction steps and determines the class polynomials associated with
w
. However, obtaining an explicit description of the reduction trees is quite challenging.

Another key ingredient in this paper is the Chen–Zhu conjecture. This conjecture predicts the
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-action on the top-dimensional irreducible components of affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties in the affine Grassmannian. This conjecture was verified recently in
[
26
]
,
[
32
]
, and
[
21
]
. Part of the Chen–Zhu conjecture predicts the isotropy group for the
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-action, which gives some information about the end points of the reduction trees.

Combining the above two ingredients, in §
5
, we show that the end points of each reduction tree for
w
with finite Coxeter part must be certain
\sigma
-Coxeter elements, and
each path, which corresponds to a
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
of
b
, in a reduction tree provides a
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-orbit of irreducible components of
X_{w}(b)
. It remains to show that for any
b
, there is at most one path in the reduction tree that corresponds to
[b]
(i.e., the “multiplicity one” result). For the (unique) maximal
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
with
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
, this “multiplicity one” result is obvious. For the basic
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
, one may deduce the “multiplicity one” result by showing that any path corresponding to
[b]
is of a unique type. See §
5.6
.

It is more challenging to determine the numbers of reduction paths for other
\sigma
-conjugacy classes in a reduction tree. In this paper, we use the following indirect approach to establish the “multiplicity one” result. We first interpret the class polynomials as the number of rational points for certain admissible subsets. We then use the positivity property of the class polynomials to show that the “multiplicity one” result for all
b
is equivalent to the following single combinatorial identity:

\sum_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{?}\mathbf{q}^{-??}=1.

Here
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
is the set of all Hodge–Newton indecomposable
\sigma
-conjugacy classes, and the powers “?” and “??” are certain non-negative integers determined by
w
and
b
. We refer to §
2.3
and §
5.2
for the precise definitions.

Verifying the combinatorial identity (*) is the most technical part of this paper and is done in §
6
. We first establish natural bijections between the sets
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
for various pairs
(\mathbf{G},\mu)
, which is of independent interest. In combination with other techniques, we reduce the verification of (*) to the case for simply laced,
\breve{F}
-simple and split groups and for fundamental coweights.

For classical groups, we may further reduce to the case where
\mu
is minuscule. In this case, the “multiplicity one” result follows from the Chen–Zhu conjecture. For exceptional groups, we use a computer to verify (*). The most complicated case for the exceptional group is
(E_{8},\omega^{\vee}_{4})
. In this case, the left-hand side of (*) involves a summation of
729
terms. It is also worth mentioning that in the case
(A_{n-1},\omega^{\vee}_{i})
, we may write the identity (*) explicitly as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 1,1>\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}>\cdots>\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}>0;\\
a_{i}+\cdots+a_{k}=i,b_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}=n\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{k-1}\mathbf{q}^{k-1-\frac{\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant k}(a_{l_{1}}b_{l_{2}}-a_{l_{2}}b_{l_{1}})+\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant k}\gcd(a_{l},b_{l})}{2}}\\
=\mathbf{q}^{\frac{i(n-i)-n}{2}}.

We do not know if there is a purely combinatorial proof of this identity.

\Sigma^{\mathrm{top}}(X_{w}(b))
. We denote by
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\backslash\Sigma^{\mathrm{top}}(X_{w}(b))
the set of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-orbits on
\Sigma^{\mathrm{top}}(X_{w}(b))
.

Note that if

and
b^{\prime}
are
\sigma
-conjugate in
\breve{G}
, then
X_{w}(b)
and
X_{w}(b^{\prime})
are isomorphic. Thus the affine Deligne–Lusztig variety
X_{w}(b)
(up to isomorphism) depends only on the element
w
in the Iwahori–Weyl group
\tilde{W}
and the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]
in
B(\mathbf{G})
. We set

B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=\{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G});X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset\}.

There is a unique maximal
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
, which we denote by

. By
[

, Lemma 3.2]
,
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=\ell(w)-\langle{\nu}_{b_{w}},2\rho\rangle
. Here

is the half sum of the positive roots in

.

2.5.
Cordial elements

For
b\in\breve{G}
, the
defect
of
b
is defined to be

{\mathrm{def}}(b)=\rank_{F}\mathbf{G}-\rank_{F}\mathbf{J}_{b},

where
\rank_{F}
denotes the
F
-rank of a reductive group over
F
.

By
[
31
, Theorem 3.4]
, we have the following length formula:

(a) For
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
,
{\rm length}([b],[t^{\mu}])=\langle\mu-\nu_{b},\rho\rangle+\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{def}}(b).

Let
{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
be the set of minimal length representatives for the cosets in
W\backslash\tilde{W}
. Note that any element
w\in\tilde{W}
can be written in a unique way as
w=xt^{\mu}y
with
\mu
dominant,
x,y\in W
such that
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
. We have
\ell(w)=\ell(x)+\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle-\ell(y)
. In this case, we set
\eta_{\sigma}(w)=\sigma^{-1}(y)x
. The
virtual dimension
is defined to be

d_{w}(b)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\ell(w)+\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(w))-{\mathrm{def}}(b)-\langle\nu_{b},2\rho\rangle).

For
w=t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
, it is easy to see that
d_{w}(b)=\langle\mu-\nu_{b},\rho\rangle-\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{def}}(b)
.

By
[
13
, Theorem 10.3]
and
[
15
, Theorem 2.30]
, we have

(b) For
w\in\tilde{W}
and
b\in\breve{G}
,
\dim X_{w}(b)\leq d_{w}(b)
.

In the special case
[b]=[b_{w}]
, (b) implies that

\ell(w)-\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(w))\leq\langle\nu_{b_{w}},2\rho\rangle-{\mathrm{def}}(b_{w}).

Cordial elements were introduced by Milićević and Viehmann in
[
25
]
. By definition, an element
w\in\tilde{W}
is
cordial
if
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=d_{w}(b_{w})
. This condition is equivalent to the condition that
\ell(w)-\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(w))=\langle\nu_{b_{w}},2\rho\rangle-{\mathrm{def}}(b_{w})
. The following nice properties of the cordial elements are established in
[
25
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
be a cordial element. Then

(1)

B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
is saturated, that is, if
[b_{1}]\leqslant[b_{2}]\leqslant[b_{3}]
in
B(\mathbf{G})
and
[b_{1}],[b_{3}]\in B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
, then
[b_{2}]\in B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
;

(2)

for each

,

is equi-dimensional of dimension equal to

.

We consider the
\sigma
-conjugation action on

and

such that for each

and

The following result is proved in
[

, Theorem 2.10]
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let

be a
\sigma
-conjugacy class in

. Then the following hold:

For each element
w\in\mathcal{O}
, there exists

such that

.

(2)

Let

2.8.
Classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field and let
\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q}
an algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathbf{H}
be a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
H=\mathbf{H}(\bar{}\mathbb{F}_{q})
. Let
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}
be the Frobenius morphism on

. Let
B
be a
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}
-stable Borel subgroup of

. Let
W_{H}
be the Weyl group of

and let
\mathbb{S}_{H}
be the set of simple reflections. Then

induces a group automorphism on

preserving

(Classical) Deligne–Lusztig varieties were introduced by Deligne and Lusztig in
[
3
]
. They are defined as follows. For

, we set

X^{\mathbf{H}}_{x}=\{hB\in H/B;h^{-1}\sigma(h)\in B\dot{x}B\}.

If

is a
\sigma_{\mathbf{H}}
-Coxeter element of

, then we say that

is a

for
\mathbf{H}
. It is well known that classical Deligne–Lusztig varieties of Coxeter type are irreducible.

It is proved in
[
13
, Theorem 4.8]
that if
w\in\tilde{W}
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class, then
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
b
and
\dot{w}
are in the same
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\breve{G}
. In this case,

X_{w}(b)\cong\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}X.

Here
\cong
means a
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-equivariant universal homeomorphism,
P
is a parahoric subgroup of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
, and

is (the perfection of) a classical Deligne–Lusztig variety for some connected reductive group
\mathbf{H}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
isomorphic to the reductive quotient of

. The group

acts on
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times X
by

, and
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}X
is the quotient space.

By Theorem

that

Theorem 3.1
.

is a free

.

Moreover, by

The polynomials

3.2.
Class polynomials and affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties

The class polynomials encode a lot of information about affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties.

Let
\tilde{W}B(\mathbf{G})
be the map sending
w\in\tilde{W}
to the
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[\dot{w}]

\breve{G}

of

By
[

Let

and

F_{w,[b]}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma);\Psi(\mathcal{O})=[b]}\mathbf{q}^{\ell(\mathcal{O})}F_{w,\mathcal{O}}\in\mathbb{N}[\mathbf{q}-1].

Here

for any

The following “dimension = degree” theorem is established in
[

.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

and

. Then

.

Remark 3.3
.

Here, by convention,
\dim\emptyset=\deg 0=-\infty
.

Moreover, we have the following “leading coefficients = irreducible components” theorem. This is established in

.

Let

equals the leading coefficient of

.

Although not needed in this paper, it is also worth mentioning that in the superbasic case, the class polynomial gives the number of rational points of an affine Deligne–Lusztig variety. This is established in

3.3.
An identity on the class polynomials

We have the following identity on the class polynomials. We first give a proof using representations of Hecke algebras. Then we provide a geometric interpretation of this identity.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. Then

\mathbf{q}^{\ell(w)}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell(\mathcal{O})}F_{w,\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})}F_{w,[b]}.

Proof.

We prove the first equality. The second follows from the definition.

Let
\pi:H\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}^{\pm 1}]
be the homomorphism of
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}^{\pm 1}]
-algebras sending
T_{s_{i}}
to
\mathbf{q}
for any
i\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
. As
\pi\circ\sigma=\pi
,
\pi([H,H]_{\sigma})=0
and thus
\pi
induces a homomorphism of the
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}^{\pm 1}]
-modules
\bar{H}_{\sigma}\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}^{\pm 1}]
, which we still denote by
\pi
.

We have
T_{w}+[H,H]_{\sigma}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)}F_{w,\mathcal{O}}T_{\mathcal{O}}
. Applying
\pi
to both sides, we obtain
\mathbf{q}^{\ell(w)}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in B(\tilde{W},\sigma)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell(\mathcal{O})}F_{w,\mathcal{O}}
.
∎

In the rest of this subsection, we assume furthermore that
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}((\epsilon))
and that
\mathbf{G}
is split over
F
. We give a geometric interpretation of the above identity.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\breve{I}_{n}
be the
n
th congruence subgroup of
\breve{I}
. Following
[
4
, §2.10]
, we call a subset
X
of
\breve{G}

admissible
if for any
w\in\tilde{W}
, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
is stable under right multiplication of
\breve{I}_{n}
. In this case, the action of
\breve{I}_{n}
on
X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
is free, and
\frac{\sharp\bigl((X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})/\breve{I}_{n}\bigr)^{\sigma}}{\sharp\breve{I}_{n}^{\sigma}}
is independent of the choice of such
n
. We set

\breve{\sharp}(X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})=\frac{\sharp\bigl((X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})/\breve{I}_{n}\bigr)^{\sigma}}{\sharp\breve{I}_{n}^{\sigma}}.

An admissible subset
X
is called
bounded
if
X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}=\emptyset
for all but finitely many
w\in\tilde{W}
. For any bounded admissible subset
X
, we set

\breve{\sharp}X=\sum_{w\in\tilde{W}}\breve{\sharp}(X\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})

and call it the
normalized cardinality of the rational points
of
X
.
By
[
14
, Theorem A.1]
, each
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\breve{G}
is admissible. We have the following geometric interpretation of the class polynomials.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})
. Then

\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})=F_{w,[b]}|_{\mathbf{q}=q}.

Proof.

We argue by induction on
\ell(w)
.

If
w
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}
, then

[b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}=\begin{cases}\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}&\text{if }[b]=[\dot{w}],\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

On the other hand, by
[
15
, §2.8.2]
,

F_{w,[b]}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{q}^{\ell(w)}&\text{if }[b]=[\dot{w}],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus the proposition holds in this case.

Now we assume that
w
is not a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class. By Theorem
2.4
(1), there exist
w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
and
i\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
such that
w\approx_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
and
s_{i}w^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})<w^{\prime}
. Set
w_{1}=s_{i}w^{\prime}
and
w_{2}=s_{i}w^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})
. Then
\ell(w_{1}),\ell(w_{2})<\ell(w)
. By the proof of
[
15
, Theorem 2.16]
,

\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})=\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}^{\prime}\breve{I})=(q-1)\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}_{1}\breve{I})+q\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}_{2}\breve{I}).

On the other hand, by
[
15
, §2.8.2]
,
F_{w,[b]}=(\mathbf{q}-1)F_{w_{1},[b]}+\mathbf{q}F_{w_{2},[b]}
. Now the statement for
w
follows from the inductive hypothesis on
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
.
∎

We have the decomposition
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}=\sqcup_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})}[b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
. Thus

\mathbf{q}^{\ell(w)}=\breve{\sharp}(\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})=\sum_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})}\breve{\sharp}([b]\cap\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I})=\sum_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})}F_{w,[b]}.

This gives an alternative proof of Proposition
3.6
and a geometric interpretation of Proposition
3.6
in the case where the
\sigma
-action on
\tilde{W}
is trivial via counting (the normalized cardinality of) the rational points of
\breve{I}\dot{w}\breve{I}
.

3.4.
Reduction trees

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
. We construct the reduction tree for
w
, which encodes the Deligne–Lusztig reduction for the affine Deligne–Lusztig varieties associated with
w
(and with all
b\in\breve{G}
).

The vertices of the graphs are the elements of
\tilde{W}
, and the (oriented) edges are of the form
x\rightharpoonup y
, where
x,y\in\tilde{W}
, and there exists
x^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
and
i\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
with
x\approx_{\sigma}x^{\prime}
,
s_{i}x^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})<x^{\prime}
and
y\in\{s_{i}x^{\prime},s_{i}x^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})\}
. Some elements of
\tilde{W}
may occur more than once in a reduction tree.

The reduction trees are constructed inductively.

Suppose that
w
is of minimal length in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
. Then the reduction tree of
w
consists of a single vertex
w
and no edges.

Suppose that
w
is not of minimal length in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}
and that a reduction tree is given for any
z\in\tilde{W}
with
\ell(z)<\ell(w)
. By Theorem
2.4
(1), there exist
w^{\prime}\in\tilde{W}
and
i\in\tilde{}\mathbb{S}
with
w\approx_{\sigma}w^{\prime}
and
s_{i}w^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})<w^{\prime}
. The reduction tree of
w
is the graph containing the given reduction tree for
s_{i}w^{\prime}
and the reduction tree for
s_{i}w^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})
, and the edges
w\rightharpoonup s_{i}w^{\prime}
and
w\rightharpoonup s_{i}w^{\prime}\sigma(s_{i})
.

Note that the reduction trees of
w
are not unique. They depend on the choices of
w^{\prime}
and
s_{i}
in the construction. We will see in the rest of this section that the reduction trees encode more information than the class polynomials.

3.5.
Reduction path

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. An
end point
of the tree
\mathcal{T}
is a vertex
x
of
\mathcal{T}
such that there is no edge of the form
x\rightharpoonup x^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{T}
. By Theorem
2.4
, each end point is of minimal length in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class. A
reduction path
in
\mathcal{T}
is a path
\underline{p}:w\rightharpoonup w_{1}\rightharpoonup\cdots\rightharpoonup w_{n}
, where
w_{n}
is an end point of
\mathcal{T}
. The
length

\ell(\underline{p})
of the reduction path
\underline{p}
is the number of edges in
\underline{p}
. We also write
\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})=w_{n}
and
[b]_{\underline{p}}=\Psi(\operatorname{end}(\underline{p}))\in B(\mathbf{G})
.

Note that if
x\rightharpoonup y
, then
\ell(x)-\ell(y)\in\{1,2\}
. We say that the edge
x\rightharpoonup y
is of type I if
\ell(x)-\ell(y)=1
and of type II if
\ell(x)-\ell(y)=2
. For any reduction path
\underline{p}
, we denote by
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})
the number of type I edges in
\underline{p}
and by
\ell_{II}(\underline{p})
the number of type II edges in
\underline{p}
. Then
\ell(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(\underline{p})+\ell_{II}(\underline{p})
.

The following relation between class polynomials and reduction trees follows easily from the inductive construction, and we omit the details of its proof.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. Then, for any
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}
of
\tilde{W}
, we have

F_{w,\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{\underline{p}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})},

where
\underline{p}
runs over all the reduction paths in
\mathcal{T}
with
\Psi(\operatorname{end}(\underline{p}))=\mathcal{O}
.

Combining Proposition
2.5
with the construction of the reduction trees, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
and
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. Then, for any
b\in\breve{G}
, there exists a decomposition

X_{w}(b)=\bigsqcup_{\underline{p}\mathrm{\;is\;a\;reduction\;path\;of\;}\mathcal{T};[b]_{\underline{p}}=[b]}X_{\underline{p}},

where
X_{\underline{p}}
is a locally closed subscheme of
X_{w}(b)
, and is
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
-equivariant universally homeomorphic to an iterated fibration of type
(\ell_{I}(\underline{p}),\ell_{II}(\underline{p}))
over
X_{\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})}(b)
.

Remark 3.10
.

Since
\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class, by §
2.8
(a) we have
X_{\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})}(b)\cong\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}X
, where
P
is a parahoric subgroup of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
and
X
is (the perfection of) an irreducible component of a classical Deligne–Lusztig variety. Thus each irreducible component
Y
of
X_{\underline{p}}
is universally homeomorphic to an iterated fibration of type
(\ell_{I}(\underline{p}),\ell_{II}(\underline{p}))
over
X
. We have a natural action of
\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)
on
X_{w}(b)
, and
X_{\underline{p}}
is stable under this action. In this case,
X_{\underline{p}}\cong\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\times^{P}Y
.

4.
Cordiality and the set
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}

4.1.
Maximal Hodge–Newton irreducible elements

Recall that
[b_{t^{\mu}}]
is the unique maximal element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. We have the following result on
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The set
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
contains a unique maximal element.

Remark 4.2
.

We denote this element by
[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]
.

Proof.

Following
[
2
]
, for any subset
E\subseteq(V^{+})^{\sigma}
, we set

C_{\geqslant E}=\{v\in(V^{+})^{\sigma};v\geqslant v^{\prime},\text{ for any }v^{\prime}\in E\}.

For any
i\in\mathbb{S}
, denote by
\mathcal{O}_{i}
the
\sigma
-orbit of
i
. Set
\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{O}_{i}}\omega_{j}
. Define
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\omega_{i}^{\vee}
with

\langle e_{i},\omega_{i}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sharp\mathcal{O}_{i}}\max\{0,\langle\mu,\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\rangle-1\}.

Set
E=\{e_{i};i\in\mathbb{S}\}
. By
[
2
, Theorem 6.5]
, there exists a unique
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
such that
\nu_{b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}}=\min C_{\geqslant E}
and
\langle\mu-\nu_{b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}},\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{k}}\rangle=1
for any
k\in\mathbb{S}-I(\nu)
. Set
\nu=\nu_{b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}}
.

Suppose that
(\mu,[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}])
is Hodge–Newton decomposable with respect to some standard Levi subgroup
\mathbf{M}_{J}
with
J=\sigma(J)\subsetneqq\mathbb{S}
. Let
j\in\mathbb{S}-J
. By definition,
\langle\mu-\nu,\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\rangle=0
. On the other hand, as
I(\nu)\subseteq J
we have
\langle\nu,\alpha_{j}\rangle\neq 0
. Thus
\langle\nu,\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{j}}\rangle=1
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
.

On the other hand, let
[b^{\prime}]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
. Set
\nu^{\prime}=\nu_{b^{\prime}}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{S}-I(v^{\prime})
, we denote by
pr_{(i)}:V=\mathbb{R}\omega^{\vee}_{i}\oplus\sum_{j\neq i}\mathbb{R}\alpha_{j}^{\vee}\mathbb{R}\omega_{i}^{\vee}
the natural projection. Set
e^{\prime}_{i}=pr_{(i)}(v^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\omega_{i}^{\vee}
. Let
E^{\prime}=\{e^{\prime}_{i};i\in\mathbb{S}-I(v^{\prime})\}
. Again by
[
2
, Theorem 6.5]
, we have
v^{\prime}=\min C_{\geqslant E^{\prime}}
. By §
2.2
(a), we have
\langle\mu-\nu^{\prime},\omega_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
for
i\in\mathbb{S}-I(v^{\prime})
. This means that
e_{i}^{\prime}\leqslant e_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{S}-I(v^{\prime})
. So
v\in C_{\geqslant E^{\prime}}
and
v\geqslant\min C_{\geqslant E^{\prime}}=v^{\prime}
. Hence
[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]
is the unique maximal element of
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
.
∎

Combining the description of
[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]
in the proof above with §
2.2
(a), we have the following result.

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that
\mu
is essentially non-central. Then

{{\rm length}}([b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}],[t^{\mu}])=\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle).

4.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.7
(1) and (2) for
w=t^{\mu}c

Now we prove Theorem
2.7
(1) and (2) in the case
w=t^{\mu}c\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
, where
c
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element. In this case, it is easy to see that
\mu
is essentially non-central in
\supp_{\sigma}(c)
. Set
J=\supp_{\sigma}(c)
. We have
J(w)=J(w)_{\mu}^{\prime}=J_{0}(w)=J
. We need to show that
w
is cordial and
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
.

By §
2.3
, we have a natural bijection
B(\mathbf{M}_{J},\mu)_{\mathrm{irr}}\cong B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 3.3.1]
, we have
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=B(\mathbf{M}_{J})_{w}
. Note that
\mu^{\diamond}-\nu_{b_{w}}\in\sum_{j\in J}\mathbb{R}\alpha_{j}^{\vee}
. Hence
\langle\mu-\nu_{b_{w}},\rho\rangle=\langle\mu-\nu_{b_{w}},\rho_{J}\rangle
, where
\rho_{J}
is the half sum of positive roots of
\mathbf{M}_{J}
. By definition,
w
is cordial in
\mathbf{G}
if and only if it is cordial in
\mathbf{M}_{J}
. Hence we may assume that
J=\mathbb{S}
and that
c
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element.

We first show that

(a)
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}\subseteq B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
.

Suppose that
X_{w}(b)\neq\emptyset
and
(\mu,b)
is Hodge–Newton decomposable with respect to some proper standard Levi subgroup
\mathbf{M}
. By
[
8
, Theorem 1.11]
, there is some
u\in W
such that
u^{-1}w\sigma(u)
lies in
\tilde{W}_{\mathbf{M}}
, which contradicts the fact that
c
is
\sigma-
Coxeter. Thus (a) is proved.

Next we show that

(b)
[b_{w}]=[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]
and
w
is a cordial element.

By §
2.5
(b), we have

\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle-2\ell(c)=\ell(w)-\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(w))\leqslant\langle\nu_{b_{w}},2\rho\rangle-{\mathrm{def}}(b_{w}).

Combined with §
2.5
(a), we get
{\rm length}([b_{w}],[t^{\mu}])\leqslant\ell(c)=\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle)
. However, by Corollary
4.3
, we have
{\rm length}([b_{w}],[b_{t^{\mu}}])\geqslant{\rm length}([b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}],[b_{t^{\mu}}])=\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle)
. Thus we must have
[b_{w}]=[b_{\mu,\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}]
and
\dim X_{w}(b_{w})=d_{w}(b_{w})
. Thus (b) is proved.

Finally, we show that

(c)
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}=B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{irr}}
.

Let
[b_{\min}]
be the unique basic
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
B(\mathbf{G})
with
\kappa(b_{\min})=\kappa(\mu)
. Then
[b_{\min}]
is the unique minimal element in
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
. Note that
c
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
. Thus
\nu_{\dot{w}}
is central and
[\dot{w}]=[b_{\min}]
. In particular,
[b_{\min}]\in B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
. By Theorem
2.3
,
B(\mathbf{G})_{w}
is saturated and hence must be equal to

This completes the proof of the

case.

4.3.
Partial conjugation

To handle the general case, we use the partial conjugation method introduced in
[
12
]
.

By partial conjugation, we mean conjugating by elements in the finite Weyl group
W
. For any
x\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W},
set

I(x)=\max\{J\subseteq\mathbb{S};{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\sigma(J)=J\}.

This is well-defined. Indeed, if
{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\sigma(J_{i})=J_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\sigma(J_{1}\cup J_{2})=J_{1}\cup J_{2}
. Let
W_{I(x)}
be the subgroup of
W
generated by the simple reflections in
I(x)
. Then
{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma
gives a length-preserving group automorphism on
W_{I(x)}
. By
[
12
, Proposition 2.4]
, we have

\tilde{W}=\bigsqcup_{x\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}}W\cdot_{\sigma}(W_{I(x)}x)=\bigsqcup_{x\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}}W\cdot_{\sigma}(xW_{\sigma(I(x))}).

Moreover, by
[
12
, Proposition 3.4]
, we have the following:

(a)
For any
w\in\tilde{W}
, there exists
x\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
and
u\in W_{I(x)}
such that
w_{\sigma}ux
and all the simple reflections involved in the conjugations are in
\mathbb{S}
.

By
[
13
, Proposition 4.9]
, we have the following:

(b)
Let
x\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
and
u\in W_{I(x)}
. Then
B(G)_{ux}
=
B(G)_{x}
and
\dim X_{ux}(b)=\dim X_{x}(b)+\ell(u)
for any
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G})_{x}
.

Similar to §
3.4
, we may consider partial reductions. By partial reduction, we mean reduction
w\rightharpoonup s_{i}w
or
w\rightharpoonup s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})
with
i\in\mathbb{S}
. We shows that partial reduction preserves elements with finite Coxeter parts.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
w\in\tilde{W}
with
\eta_{\sigma}(w)
a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
. Let
i\in\mathbb{S}
with
s_{i}w<w
. Then

(1)

\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i}))
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
and
\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})))=\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(w))
.

(2)

If, moreover,
s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})<w
, then
\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w)
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
and
\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w))=\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(w))-\{\sigma^{l}(i^{\prime});l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
for some
i^{\prime}\in\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(w))
.

Proof.

We prove part (1). The proof of part (2) is similar, and we skip the details.

Write
w=xt^{\mu}y
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
. Set
c=\eta_{\sigma}(w)=\sigma^{-1}(y)x
. If
y\sigma(s_{i})\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
, then
\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i}))=\eta_{\sigma}(w)
, and the statement is obvious. Now assume that
y\sigma(s_{i})=s_{i^{\prime}}y
for some
i^{\prime}\in I(\mu)
. Then we have
x^{-1}(\alpha_{i})<0
and
\sigma^{-1}(y)(\alpha_{i})>0
. Thus
(\sigma^{-1}(y)x)(-x^{-1}(\alpha_{i}))<0
. It follows that
\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}x=(\sigma^{-1}(y)x)(x^{-1}s_{i}x)<\sigma^{-1}(y)x
. By the cancellation property of Coxeter groups, we conclude that
\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}x
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element and
\ell(\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}x)=\ell(\sigma^{-1}(y)x)-1
. Write
c^{\prime}=\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}x
. Notice that
\sigma^{-1}(s_{i^{\prime}})c=\sigma^{-1}(s_{i^{\prime}}y)x=\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}x=c^{\prime}
. It follows that
\sigma^{-1}(i^{\prime})
is not in the
\sigma
-support of
c^{\prime}
. Hence
\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i}))=\sigma^{-1}(y)s_{i}xs_{i^{\prime}}=c^{\prime}s_{i^{\prime}}
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element, and

\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})))=\supp_{\sigma}(\eta_{\sigma}(w)).

The statement is proved.
∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
2.7
(1) and (2): general case

Let
w=xt^{\mu}y\in\tilde{W}
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
. We assume that
\eta_{\sigma}(w)
a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element. We prove Theorem
2.7
(1) and (2) by induction on
\ell(x)
.

The case
\ell(x)=0
has already been proved in §
4.2
. Assume that
\ell(x)>0
. Let
i\in\mathbb{S}
such that
s_{i}x<x
. There are three different cases.

Case (1):
\ell(s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i}))<\ell(w)
. Write
w_{1}=s_{i}w
and
w_{2}=s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\eta_{\sigma}(w_{1})
and
\eta_{\sigma}(w_{2})
are both partial
\sigma
-Coxeter elements. The inductive hypothesis applies for

and
w_{2}
. Since

, we have
[b_{w}]=\max([b_{w_{1}}],[b_{w_{2}}])=[b_{w_{1}}]
. Then

and
\dim X_{w_{1}}(b_{w})+1=\dim X_{w}(b_{w})

Observe that

B(\mathbf{G})_{w}=B(\mathbf{G})_{w_{1}}\cup B(\mathbf{G})_{w_{2}}=\sqcup_{J\in[J_{0}(w_{1}),J(w_{1})]_{\mu}\cup[J_{0}(w_{2}),J(w_{2})]_{\mu}}B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}.

Note that
J(w_{2})=J(w)
,
J_{0}(w_{1})=J_{0}(w)
and

This proves part (2) of Theorem

Case (2):
y\sigma(s_{i})<y
. Write

. The inductive hypothesis applies for

, in particular,

. Note also that

.

Case (3):

for some

. Write

5.3.
Estimate
\ell_{I}

Let
\operatorname{Aff}(V)
be the group of affine transformations on

. For any

V^{g}=\{v\in V\mid g(v)=v\}
. We have a natural projection map
p:\tilde{W}\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle\operatorname{Aff}(V)\mathrm{GL}(V)

\dim V_{w}=\dim V^{p(w\sigma)}
.

It is easy to see that for any
g\in\mathrm{GL}(V)
and any reflection

|\dim V^{rg}-\dim V^{g}|\leqslant 1.

In particular, for any
w\in\tilde{W}
and

, we have

|\dim V_{s_{i}w}-\dim V_{w}|\leqslant 1.

Now let
w=t^{\mu}c
be as in Theorem
5.1
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
and let
\underline{p}
be a reduction path in
\mathcal{T}
. Set
e=\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})
and
[b]=\Psi(e)\in B(\mathbf{G})
. Let
(J,x,\breve{K},C)
be a standard quadruple associated with the
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
e
.

Consider the variation of
\dim V_{-}
along the reduction path
\underline{p}
. Note that type II edges do not change
\dim V_{-}
, and type I edges change
\dim V_{-}
by at most 1. Therefore
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})\geqslant|\dim V_{e}-\dim V_{w}|
. Since
p(w)=c
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element,
\dim V^{p(w\sigma)}=0
. Since
C
is
{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma
-elliptic in
\breve{K}
, we have
\dim V_{e}=\dim V_{x}-\sharp(\breve{K}/\langle{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma\rangle)
. Hence

\ell_{I}(\underline{p})\geqslant\dim V_{x}-\sharp(\breve{K}/\langle{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma\rangle).

By
[
24
, §1.9]
,
{\mathrm{def}}(b)=\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle)-\dim V_{x}
. Note that
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})+2\ell_{II}(\underline{p})=\ell(t^{\mu}c)-\ell(e)
. Moreover,

\ell(e)\geqslant\langle\nu_{b},2\rho\rangle+\sharp(\breve{K}/\langle{\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma\rangle),

with equality holding if and only if

is an
({\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma)
-Coxeter conjugacy class in

. We have

\displaystyle\dim X_{\underline{p}}

\displaystyle=\ell_{I}(\underline{p})+\ell_{II}(\underline{p})+\ell(e)-\langle\nu_{b},2\rho\rangle

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\ell_{I}(\underline{p})+\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle-\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle)+\ell(e))-\langle\nu_{b},2\rho\rangle

\displaystyle\geqslant\langle\mu-\nu_{b},\rho\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}(\dim V_{x}-\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle))

\displaystyle=\langle\mu-\nu_{b},\rho\rangle-\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathrm{def}}(b)=d_{w}(b).

By §
2.5
(b), we have
\dim X_{\underline{p}}\leqslant\dim X_{w}(b)\leqslant\dim d_{w}(b)

is an
({\mathrm{Ad}}(x)\circ\sigma)
-Coxeter conjugacy class in
\breve{K}
.

F

6.4.
Reduction to
\breve{F}
-simple groups

We have
\Phi=\Phi_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup\Phi_{l}
, where
\Phi_{1}\cong\cdots\cong\Phi_{l}
are irreducible root systems and
\sigma
induces an isomorphism from
\Phi_{i}
to
\Phi_{i+1}
. Here, by convention, we set
\Phi_{l+1}=\Phi_{1}
. Then the map

\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{l})|\nu|=\nu_{l}+\sigma(\nu_{l-1})+\cdots+\sigma^{l-1}(\nu_{1})

induces an isomorphism of posets

B(\Phi,\sigma,\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}\overset{\sim}{}B(\Phi_{l},\sigma^{l},|\mu|)_{\mathrm{indec}}.

Moreover, there is a natural bijection
\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle\cong\mathbb{S}_{l}/\langle\sigma^{l}\rangle
, where
\mathbb{S}_{l}
is the set of simple reflections for
\Phi_{l}
. Thus
f_{\Phi,\sigma,\mu}=f_{\Phi_{l},\sigma^{l},|\mu|}
. In the rest of this section, we assume that
\mathbf{G}
is an
\breve{F}
-simple unramified adjoint group.

6.5.
Reduction to split groups

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
\sigma
-orbit of
\mathbb{S}
. If all the simple roots in
\mathcal{O}
commute with each other, we define
\alpha_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\alpha_{i}
. If
\mathcal{O}=\{i_{0},j_{0}\}
with
\langle\alpha_{j_{0}}^{\vee},\alpha_{i_{0}}\rangle=\langle\alpha_{i_{0}}^{\vee},\alpha_{j_{0}}\rangle=-1
, we define
\alpha_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}=2(\alpha_{i_{0}}+\alpha_{j_{0}})
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{\prime}=\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle
and let
\Phi^{\prime}
be the root system generated by
\alpha_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}
for all
\mathcal{O}\in\mathbb{S}^{\prime}
. The coroot corresponding to
\mathcal{O}
is given by
\alpha_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime\vee}=\frac{1}{\sharp\mathcal{O}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\alpha_{i}^{\vee}
, and the fundamental weight corresponding to
\mathcal{O}
is given by
\omega_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\omega_{i}
. For any
\nu\in(V^{+})^{\sigma}
,
\nu\in B(\Phi,\sigma,\mu)
if and only if
\langle\mu^{\diamond}-\nu,\omega_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
for any
\mathcal{O}\in\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle
such that
\langle\nu,\alpha_{\mathcal{O}}^{\prime}\rangle\neq 0
, which is also equivalent to
\nu\in B(\Phi^{\prime},\mathrm{id},\mu^{\diamond})
. Hence we have the following:

(a)
The natural identification
(\mathbb{R}\Phi^{\vee})^{\sigma}=\mathbb{R}{\Phi^{\prime}}^{\vee}
induces an bijection of posets

B(\Phi,\sigma,\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}\overset{\sim}{}B(\Phi^{\prime},\mathrm{id},\mu^{\diamond})_{\mathrm{indec}}.

It follows from (a) that
f_{\Phi,\sigma,\mu}=f_{\Phi^{\prime},\mathrm{id},\mu^{\diamond}}
. Therefore, it suffices to prove (
{\spadesuit}^{\prime\prime}
) for the triples
(\Phi,\sigma,\mu)
arising from split groups. In the rest of this section, we assume that
\mathbf{G}
is a split
\breve{F}
-simple adjoint group. We identify
B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
with
B(\Phi,\sigma,\mu)
and write
f_{\mathbf{G},\mu}(\nu)=f_{\Phi,\sigma,\mu}(\nu)
.

By §

, there exists only one reduction path whose end point is associated with a given

There exists an irreducible, simply laced, extended affine Weyl group

•

•

\tilde{F}_{4}

\tilde{E}_{6}

•

if

and

Let

\iota(s_{i})=s_{i_{1}^{\prime}}\cdots s_{i_{k}^{\prime}}

6.7.
A combinatorial identity on graphs

Let
X
be a finite graph and
Y\subseteq X
. Denote by
\mathcal{A}(Y,X)
the set of subsets
J\subseteq X
such that none of the connected components of
X-J
lies in
Y
. Define

f_{Y,X}=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}(Y,X)}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp(Y\cap J^{\circ})}\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{q}],

where
J^{\circ}=\{i\in J;\text{$i$ has no neighbors in $X-J$}\}
is the interior of
J
.

Lemma 6.1
.

We have
f_{Y,X}=\mathbf{q}^{\sharp X}
for any
Y\subseteq X
.

Proof.

Define

\alpha:\{(J,K);J\in\mathcal{A}(Y,X),K\subseteq Y\cap J^{\circ}\}\{\text{subsets of $X$}\},~(J,K)J-K.

We construct the inverse map
\beta
of
\alpha
as follows. Let
H\subseteq X
. Let
C
be the union of connected components of
X-H
that are contained in
Y
. Then we define
\beta(H)=(H\sqcup C,C)
. By definition,
\alpha\circ\beta=\mathrm{id}
. On the other hand, for any
J\in\mathcal{A}(Y,X)
and
K\subseteq Y\cap J^{\circ}
,
\alpha((J,K))=J-K
. Moreover,
X-(J-K)=(X-J)\sqcup K
. As
K\subseteq Y\cap J^{\circ}
,
K
and
X-J
are not connected with each other. Hence
K
is the union of connected components of
X-(J-K)
contained in
Y
. Therefore
\beta\circ\alpha((J,K))=\beta(J-K)=((J-K)\sqcup K,K)=(J,K)
and hence
\beta\circ\alpha=\mathrm{id}
. Therefore
\alpha
is a bijection. Using the binomial expansion, we get

\displaystyle f_{Y,X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}(Y,X)}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp(Y\cap J^{\circ})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}(Y,X)}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\sum_{K\subseteq Y\cap J^{\circ}}(q-1)^{\sharp(Y\cap J^{\circ}-K)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}(X,Y)}\sum_{K\subseteq Y\cap J^{\circ}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-K)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{H\subseteq X}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp H}=\mathbf{q}^{\sharp X}.

The lemma is proved.
∎

6.8.
Reduction to fundamental coweights

Assume that
\mu
is not a fundamental coweight. Then there exist
i,j\in\mathbb{S}
(here
i
and
j
are not necessarily distinct) such that
\mu-\omega_{i}^{\vee}-\omega_{j}^{\vee}
is also dominant. Let
X
be the (unique) path in the Dynkin diagram of
\mathbb{S}
with end points
i,j
.

Let
\lambda=\mu-\sum_{k\in X}\alpha_{k}^{\vee}
and let
\mathcal{A}
be the set of subsets
J\subseteq X
such that
\lambda
is non-central on each connected component of
\mathbb{S}-J
(equivalently,
B(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\lambda)_{\mathrm{irr}}\neq\emptyset
). Note that
\lambda
is non-central on each connected component of
\mathbb{S}-X
and that the connected components of
\mathbb{S}-J
that do not intersect
\mathbb{S}-X
are the connected components of
\{i\in X-J;i\text{ has no neighbors in }\mathbb{S}-X\}
.

Set
Y=\{i\in X\cap I(\lambda);i\text{ has no neighbors in }\mathbb{S}-X\}
. Then it is easy to see that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(Y,X)
defined in §
6.7
.

Lemma 6.2
.

We have the following:

(1)

\lambda
is dominant;

(2)

B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}=\sqcup_{J\in\mathcal{A}}B(\mathbf{G},\lambda)_{(\mathbb{S}-J)\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
.

Proof.

Let
l\in\mathbb{S}
. If
l\in\mathbb{S}-X
; then
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle\geqslant-\langle\sum_{k\in X}\alpha_{k}^{\vee},\alpha_{l}\rangle\geqslant 0
. If
l\in X-\{i,j\}
, then
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle=-\langle\sum_{k\in X}\alpha_{k}^{\vee},\alpha_{l}\rangle=-\langle\alpha_{k}^{\vee}+\alpha_{l}^{\vee}+\alpha_{k^{\prime}}^{\vee},\alpha_{l}\rangle\geqslant-(-1+2-1)=0
, where
k
and
k^{\prime}
are the neighbors of
l
in
X
. If
l\in\{i,j\}
and
i\neq j
, then
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle=\langle\mu,\alpha_{l}\rangle-\langle\sum_{k\in X}\alpha_{k}^{\vee},\alpha_{l}\rangle\geqslant 1-1=0
. If
l=i=j
, then
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle=\langle\mu,\alpha_{l}\rangle-\langle\sum_{k\in X}\alpha_{k}^{\vee},\alpha_{l}\rangle\geqslant 2-2=0
. This proves part (1).

By definition,
B(\mathbf{G},\lambda)_{(\mathbb{S}-J)\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}\subseteq B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
for any
J\subseteq X
. Now we prove the other direction. Let
E=\{e_{i};i\in\mathbb{S}\}
be as in the proof of Proposition
4.1
. Then, for each
i\in\mathbb{S}
, by the definition of
\lambda
, we have

\langle\lambda,\omega_{i}\rangle\geqslant\max\{0,\langle\mu,\omega_{i}\rangle-1\}=\langle e_{i},\omega_{i}\rangle.

By
[
2
, Theorem 6.5]
, this implies that
\lambda\in C_{\geqslant E}
and hence
\lambda\geqslant\min C_{\geqslant E}=[b]_{\mathbf{G}\text{-}\mathrm{indec}}.

For any
\nu\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}
, there exists a unique subset
J\subseteq X
such that
\lambda-\nu\in\sum_{l\in\mathbb{S}-J}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\alpha_{l}^{\vee}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\nu\in B(\mathbf{G},\lambda)_{(\mathbb{S}-J)\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
. Part (2) is proved.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose that the equality (
{\spadesuit}^{\prime\prime}
) holds for all fundamental coweights. Then it holds for all dominant coweights.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the semisimple rank of
\mathbf{G}
and the number
\langle\rho,\mu\rangle
.

Suppose
\mu
is not fundamental. Let the notation be as in §
6.8
. By Corollary
2.2
, we identify
B(\mathbf{G},\lambda)_{(\mathbb{S}-J)\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
with
B(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\mu)_{\mathrm{irr}}
for
J\in\mathcal{A}
. We show that

(a)
For any
J\in\mathcal{A}
and
\nu\in B(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\mu)_{\mathrm{irr}}
, we have

f_{\mathbf{G},\mu}(\nu)=\mathbf{q}^{-\sharp X}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp Y\cap J^{\circ}}f_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\lambda}(\nu).

By definition,
f_{\mathbf{M}_{K},\mu}(\nu)=(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(K-K\cap I(\nu))}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp K\cap I(\nu)-{\rm length}_{\mathbf{M}_{K}}(\mu,\nu)}
for any
K\subseteq\mathbb{S}
.

Note that
{\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda,\mu)=\langle\mu-\lambda,\rho\rangle=\sharp X
, and hence

{\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\nu,\mu)={\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\nu,\lambda)+{\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\lambda,\mu)={\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\nu,\lambda)+\sharp X.

By Lemma
2.1
, we have
{\rm length}_{\mathbf{G}}(\nu,\mu)={\rm length}_{\mathbf{M}}(\nu,\mu)
. To show (a), it remains to show that
I(\nu)-(\mathbb{S}-J)\cap I(\nu)=J\cap I(\nu)=Y\cap J^{\circ}
. Let
l\in J
. Write
\nu=\lambda-\delta
for some
\delta\in\sum_{k\in\mathbb{S}-J}\mathbb{R}_{>0}\alpha_{k}^{\vee}
. Then
l\in J\cap I(\nu)
if and only if
\langle\nu,\alpha\rangle=\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle-\langle\delta,\alpha_{l}\rangle=0
, which is also equivalent to
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l}\rangle=\langle\delta,\alpha_{l}\rangle=0
, that is,
l\in Y\cap J^{\circ}
as desired. (a) is proved.

Now, by Lemma
6.1
and the inductive hypothesis on
\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J}
and
\lambda
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}}f_{\mathbf{G},\mu}(\nu)=\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\nu\in B(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\lambda)_{\mathrm{irr}}}f_{\mathbf{G},\mu}(\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.0pt=\mathbf{q}^{-\sharp X}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}}\sum_{\nu\in B(\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\lambda)_{\mathrm{irr}}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp Y\cap J^{\circ}}f_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-J},\lambda}(\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.0pt=\mathbf{q}^{-\sharp X}\sum_{J\in\mathcal{A}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\sharp(J-Y\cap J^{\circ})}\mathbf{q}^{\sharp Y\cap J^{\circ}}=1.

The proof is finished.
∎

Now it is sufficient to deal with the fundamental coweights of
\mathbf{G}
.

6.9.
Proof for the minuscule coweights

Let
\mu
a be (non-zero) minuscule coweight. Then
\dim V_{\mu}(\lambda_{b})=1
for any
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. By Theorem
5.3
, we conclude that
\sharp(\mathbf{J}_{b}(F)\backslash\Sigma^{\mathrm{top}}(X_{\mu}(b)))=1
for any
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)
. As in §
6.1
, we have

\mathbf{q}^{\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle-\sharp(\mathbb{S}/\langle\sigma\rangle)}=\sum_{[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{indec}}}n_{[b]}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\mu,[b])}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\mu,[b])+\ell_{[b]}},

where
n_{[b]}
is the number of reduction paths
\underline{p}
in a given reduction tree
\mathcal{T}
of
t^{\mu}c
with
[b]_{\underline{p}}=[b]
. Note also that at the end of §
5.3
, we showed that
\dim X_{\underline{p}}=\dim X_{t^{\mu}c}([b]_{\underline{p}})=\dim X_{\mu}([b]_{\underline{p}})
for any reduction path
\underline{p}
. Using Lemma
5.4
, we conclude that all
n_{[b]}=1
. This implies the combinatorial identity
({\spadesuit})
, and then
({\spadesuit}^{\prime\prime})
follows.

In particular, the combinatorial identity (
{\spadesuit}^{\prime\prime}
) holds for type
A
, since all the fundamental coweights are minuscule. For
(A_{n-1},\omega^{\vee}_{i})
, we may write the identity

explicitly as

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geqslant 1,1>\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}>\cdots>\frac{a_{k}}{b_{k}}>0;\\
a_{i}+\cdots+a_{k}=i,b_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}=n\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{k-1}\mathbf{q}^{k-1-\frac{\sum_{1\leqslant l_{1}<l_{2}\leqslant k}(a_{l_{1}}b_{l_{2}}-a_{l_{2}}b_{l_{1}})+\sum_{1\leqslant l\leqslant k}\gcd(a_{l},b_{l})}{2}}\\
=\mathbf{q}^{\frac{i(n-i)-n}{2}}.

We do not know if there is a purely combinatorial proof of this identity.

6.10.

In this subsection, we assume that

For any integer

\displaystyle=\bigsqcup_{i-2\leqslant k\leqslant n-3}B(\mathbf{G},\omega^{\vee}_{i-2})_{[1,k]\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}\cong\bigsqcup_{i-2\leqslant k\leqslant n-3}B(\mathbf{M}_{[1,k]},\omega^{\vee}_{i-2})_{\mathrm{irr}},

For

A_{1}\times A_{1}

\mu_{k}=(1^{i-1},0^{k-i+1},1,0^{n-k-1})

\sum_{\nu\in B_{I}\sqcup B_{II}\sqcup B_{III}}f_{\mathbf{G},\omega^{\vee}_{i}}(\nu)=1.

and

(b)

\displaystyle n_{0}

\displaystyle=\langle\omega^{\vee}_{i}-\nu,\rho\rangle-\langle\mu_{k}-\nu,\rho_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-\{k\}}}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left({\mathrm{def}}_{D_{n}}(\nu)-{\mathrm{def}}_{\mathbf{M}_{\mathbb{S}-\{k\}}}(\nu)\right)

\displaystyle=\langle\omega^{\vee}_{i}-\omega_{i-1}^{\vee},\rho\rangle+1=n-i+1=\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle-n+i.

l<n
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu\in B_{I}\sqcup B_{II}\sqcup B_{III}}f_{D_{n},\omega^{\vee}_{i}}(\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 64.0pt=\mathbf{q}^{-\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle}\cdot\left(\mathbf{q}^{2}+(\mathbf{q}-1)\!\left(\sum_{k=i-2}^{n-3}\mathbf{q}^{n-k-1}+\sum_{k=i}^{n-2}\mathbf{q}^{2n-k-i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 64.0pt=\mathbf{q}^{-\langle\alpha^{\vee},\rho\rangle}\cdot\mathbf{q}^{2n-2i+1}=1.

This completes the proof of (**).

7.1.
Description of the reduction trees

Let
w\in Wt^{\mu}W
with finite partial
\sigma
-Coxeter part, that is,
\eta_{\sigma}(w)
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element. For any
J\in[J_{0}(w),J(w)]_{\mu}
and
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
, we set

\displaystyle J^{\flat,w}

\displaystyle=\{i\in I(\mu^{\diamond})\cap(J(w)-J)\mid i\text{ commutes with }J\},

\displaystyle\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)

\displaystyle=\sharp(J(w)/\langle\sigma\rangle)-\sharp(J^{\flat,w}/\langle\sigma\rangle)-\sharp(I(\nu_{b})\cap J/\langle\sigma\rangle),

\displaystyle\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)

\displaystyle={\rm length}([b],[t^{\mu}])-\sharp(J_{0}(w)/\langle\sigma\rangle).

By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a unique
\sigma
-conjugacy class
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J}}\in B(\mathbf{M}_{J},\mu)
such that
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J}}\subseteq[b]
. We similarly define
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J^{\flat,w}\cup J}}\in B(\mathbf{M}_{J^{\flat,w}\cup J},\mu)
. In this case, a
\sigma
-Coxeter element associated with
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J^{\flat,w}\cup J}}
is equal to the product of a
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W_{J^{\flat,w}}
and a
\sigma
-Coxeter element associated with
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J}}
.

The main result of this section is the following description of the reduction tree of
w
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
w\in Wt^{\mu}W
with
\eta_{\sigma}(w)
a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. Then, for any
J\in[J_{0}(w),J(w)]_{\mu}
and
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
, there exists a unique reduction path
\underline{p}
in
\mathcal{T}
with
[b]_{\underline{p}}=[b]
. Moreover,

(1)

\ell_{I}(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)
and
\ell_{II}(\underline{p})=\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)
;

(2)

\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element associated with
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J^{\flat,w}\cup J}}
.

Combining Theorem
7.1
with Proposition
3.9
and Remark
3.10
, we obtain Theorem
2.7
(3) for
w
.

7.2.
Strategy

The strategy for proving Theorem
7.1
is very different from that adopted for the proof of Theorem
5.1
. In the latter case, we used the Chen–Zhu conjecture and the dimension formula to determine the end points of the reduction trees of
t^{\mu}c
. However, such a method, when applied to general
w
, cannot determine the end points.

The approach we use here is as follows. We first apply the partial reduction method and the class polynomials for
t^{\mu}c
to calculate the class polynomials for
w
. As we mentioned earlier, class polynomials, in general, contains less information than reduction trees. Fortunately, for the elements
w
we consider here, by combining the information on the class polynomials and the estimates on the type I-edges, we obtain the required information for
any
reduction tree.

The information about the class polynomials we need is contained in the following equality on the
\sigma
-cocenter of the Iwahori–Hecke algebra
H
:

T_{w}+[H,H]_{\sigma}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}J\in[J_{0}(w),J(w)]_{\mu},\\
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}T_{\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}}+[H,H]_{\sigma},

where
{\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}}
is the
\sigma
-conjugacy class containing a
\sigma-
Coxeter element associated with
[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J^{\flat,w}\cup J}}
.

7.3.
Proof of the identity
(\diamondsuit)

7.3.1.

We consider the case where
w=t^{\mu}c\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
for some partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element
c
of
W
. Let
J=\supp_{\sigma}(c)
. In this case,
J_{0}(w)=J(w)=J
.

If
J=\mathbb{S}
, that is,
c
is a (full)
\sigma
-Coxeter element, then
(\diamondsuit)
follows from Lemma
3.8
and the description of the reduction tree in Theorem
5.1
. In other words, the class polynomial
F_{w,\mathcal{O}}
is given by

F_{w,\mathcal{O}}=\begin{cases}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}&\text{if }\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}\text{ for some }[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{\mathrm{irr}},\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Assume
J\subsetneq\mathbb{S}
. It follows from
[
19
, Theorem 7.3]
that
F_{w,\mathcal{O}}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}^{J}\subseteq\mathcal{O}}F_{w,\mathcal{O}^{J}}^{J}
, where
\mathcal{O}^{J}
denotes a
\sigma
-conjugacy class of
\tilde{W}_{J}
and
F_{w,\mathcal{O}^{J}}^{J}
denotes the corresponding class polynomial for
\mathbf{M}_{J}
. For any
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
, we have
\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}\supset\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J}}}
. Hence
F_{w,\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}}=F^{J}_{w,\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]_{\mathbf{M}_{J}}}}=(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}
. And
F_{w,\mathcal{O}}=0
if
\mathcal{O}\neq\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}
for any

[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
. This proves
(\diamondsuit)
.

7.3.2.

We consider the case where
w=c_{1}t^{\mu}c_{2}
for some partial
\sigma
-Coxeter elements
c_{1},c_{2}
of
W
such that
t^{\mu}c_{2}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
and
c_{1}\in W_{I(t^{\mu}c_{2})}
.

Set
J_{1}=\supp_{\sigma}(c_{1})
and
J_{2}=\supp_{\sigma}(c_{2})
. One can construct a reduction tree
\mathcal{T}
of
t^{\mu}c_{2}
in
\tilde{W}_{J_{2}}
such that
c_{1}
commutes with all the vertices in
\mathcal{T}
. In particular, if
w_{1}\rightharpoonup w_{2}
is one edge in
\mathcal{T}
, then we have
c_{1}w_{1}\rightharpoonup c_{1}w_{2}
in
\tilde{W}_{J}
. Note that
c_{1}
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}_{J_{1}}
, and the simple reflections in
\tilde{W}_{J_{1}}
commute with the simple reflections in
\tilde{W}_{J_{2}}
. It is easy to see that if
w_{1}
is an end point in
\mathcal{T}
(and hence is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}_{J_{2}}
and commutes with
c_{1}
), then
c_{1}w_{1}
is a minimal length element in its
\sigma
-conjugacy class in
\tilde{W}_{J}
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
be the tree with vertices
c_{1}w_{1}
for all vertices
w_{1}\in\mathcal{T}
and the edges
c_{1}w_{1}\rightharpoonup c_{1}w_{2}
for all edges
w_{1}\rightharpoonup w_{2}
in
\mathcal{T}
. Then, from the above discussion,
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is a reduction tree of
c_{1}t^{\mu}c_{2}
in
\tilde{W}_{J}
. Note that for
J,[b]
as in Theorem
7.1
, we have
\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)=\ell_{I}(t^{\mu}c_{2},[b],J)
and
\ell_{II}(w,[b])=\ell_{II}(t^{\mu}c_{2},[b])
. Hence the identity
(\diamondsuit)
for
w
follows from the identity for
t^{\mu}c_{2}
established in §
7.3.1
.

7.3.3.

We consider the case where
w=c_{1}t^{\mu}c_{2}
for partial
\sigma
-Coxeter elements
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
in
W
such that
t^{\mu}c_{2}\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
and
\supp_{\sigma}(c_{1})\cap\supp_{\sigma}(c_{2})=\emptyset
.

We prove the identity
(\diamondsuit)
by induction on
\ell(c_{1})
. The case
\ell(c_{1})=0
is proved in §
7.3.1
.

Assume that
\ell(c_{1})>0
. Let
i\in\mathbb{S}
such that
s_{i}c_{1}<c_{1}
. There are two cases as follows.

Case (1):
c_{2}\sigma(s_{i})\in{}^{I(\mu)}W
. Write
w_{1}=s_{i}w
and
w_{2}=s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})=s_{i}c_{1}t^{\mu}c_{2}\sigma(s_{i})
. Then
T_{w}+[H,H]_{\sigma}=(\mathbf{q}-1)T_{w_{1}}+\mathbf{q}T_{w_{2}}+[H,H]_{\sigma}
. Note that
J(w_{2})=J(w)
,
J_{0}(w_{1})=J_{0}(w)
,
J(w_{1})=J(w)-\{\sigma^{\ell}(s_{i})\mid\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, and
J_{0}(w_{2})=J_{0}(w)\sqcup\{\sigma^{\ell}(s_{i})\mid\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Then

[J_{0}(w),J(w)]_{\mu}=[J_{0}(w_{1}),J(w_{1})]_{\mu}\sqcup[J_{0}(w_{2}),J(w_{2})]_{\mu}.

By the induction hypothesis, it suffices to prove that

(a)

if
J\in[J_{0}(w_{1}),J(w_{1})]_{\mu}
, then
J^{\flat,w}=J^{\flat,w_{1}}
; and

(b)

if
J\in[J_{0}(w_{2}),J(w_{2})]_{\mu}
, then
J^{\flat,w}=J^{\flat,w_{2}}
.

Statement (b) is obvious since
J(w_{2})=J(w)
. Let us prove (a). Suppose
J^{\flat,w}\neq J^{\flat,w_{1}}
; then
i\in I(\mu^{\diamond})
and
s_{i}
commutes with
J
. Since
J\supseteq J_{0}(w)
,
s_{i}
also commutes with
J_{0}(w)
. Then
\mu
is not essentially non-central over
J_{0}(w_{2})=J_{0}(w)\sqcup\{\sigma^{l}(i)\mid l\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, which is a contradiction. This completes the proof.

Case (2):
\sigma(i)\in I(\mu)
, and
\sigma(s_{i})
commutes with
c_{2}
. Then the equality
(\diamondsuit)
holds for
w
if and only if it holds for
s_{i}w\sigma(s_{i})
. We continue with the procedure until Case (1) happens. If Case (2) happens all the time and the procedure does not stop, then
c_{1}\in W_{I(t^{\mu}c_{2})}
, and the equality
(\diamondsuit)
follows from §
7.3.2
.

7.4.
Proof of Theorem
7.1

7.4.1.

We consider the case
w=ct^{\mu}
, where
c
is a partial
\sigma
-Coxeter element of
W
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. By Lemma
3.8
and the identity
(\diamondsuit)
for
w
, we have that for any
\sigma
-conjugacy class
\mathcal{O}
of
\tilde{W}
,

\sum_{\underline{p};\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})\in\mathcal{O}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})}\\
=\begin{cases}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}&\text{if }\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}\text{ for some }[b],\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let
\underline{p}
be a path in
\mathcal{T}
. Set
e=\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})
and
[b]=[b]_{\underline{p}}
. Assume that
[b]\in B(\mathbf{G},\mu)_{J\text{-}\mathrm{irr}}
for some
J
. As
(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})}\in\mathbb{N}[\mathbf{q}-1]
, there is no cancellation involved in the left-hand side of the above equality. Therefore,
e
must be contained in
\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}
.

As in §
5.3
, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{I}(\underline{p})

\displaystyle\geqslant\dim V_{e}-\dim V_{w}=\sharp(J(w)/\langle\sigma\rangle)-\sharp(J^{\flat,w}/\langle\sigma\rangle)-\sharp(I(\nu_{b})\cap J/\langle\sigma\rangle)

\displaystyle=\ell_{I}(w,[b],J).

Note that
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})+2\ell_{II}(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)+2\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)
. Thus
\deg_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})}\geqslant\deg_{\mathbf{q}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}
, with equality holding if and only if
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)
. Again, since there is no cancellation involved in
\sum_{\underline{p};\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})\in\mathcal{O}}(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})}
, we must have
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)
. In this case,
(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(\underline{p})}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(\underline{p})}=(\mathbf{q}-1)^{\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)}\mathbf{q}^{\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)}
. This also shows that for each
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{w,[b]}
, there is a unique reduction path
\underline{p}
with
\operatorname{end}(\underline{p})\in\mathcal{O}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
7.1
for
w=ct^{\mu}
.

7.4.2.

Now we consider the general case. Let
w=xt^{\mu}y
with
t^{\mu}y\in{}^{\mathbb{S}}\tilde{W}
. Set
c=\sigma^{-1}(y)x
and
w^{\prime}=ct^{\mu}
. We relate
w
and
w^{\prime}
as in the proof of
[
13
, Theorem 10.3]
. Let
y=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{r}
be a reduced expression. Let
w^{(0)}=w^{\prime}
,
w^{(1)}=s_{1}w^{(0)}\sigma(s_{1})
,
w^{(2)}=s_{2}w^{(1)}\sigma(s_{2}),\ldots,w^{(r)}=w
. We have
w^{(0)}\rightarrow_{\sigma}w^{(1)}\rightarrow_{\sigma}\cdots\rightarrow_{\sigma}w^{(r)}
. This give a path
w^{\prime}\rightharpoonup w
, consisting of
\frac{1}{2}(\ell(w^{\prime})-\ell(w))
type II edges. We denote this path by
\underline{p}_{0}
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a reduction tree of
w
. One may construct a reduction tree
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
of
w^{\prime}
containing the concatenation
\underline{p}_{0}\circ\mathcal{T}
as a subgraph. In particular, for any reduction path
\underline{p}
in
\mathcal{T}
, the concatenation
\underline{p}^{\prime}:=\underline{p}_{0}\circ\underline{p}
is a reduction path in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
. By definition,
\ell_{I}(\underline{p})=\ell_{I}(\underline{p}^{\prime})
and
\ell_{II}(\underline{p})+\frac{1}{2}(\ell(w^{\prime})-\ell(w))=\ell_{II}(\underline{p}^{\prime})
. It is obvious that
J(w)=J(w^{\prime})
and
J_{0}(w^{\prime})=\emptyset
. Hence
\ell_{I}(w,[b],J)=\ell_{I}(w^{\prime},[b],J)
. On the other hand, we have
\sharp(J_{0}(w)/\langle\sigma\rangle)={\rm length}([b_{w}],[t^{\mu}])=\frac{1}{2}(\ell(\eta_{\sigma}(w)+\ell(y)-\ell(x)))=\frac{1}{2}(\ell(w^{\prime})-\ell(w))
, where the second equality follows from the definition of cordial elements. Hence
\ell_{II}(w,[b],J)+\frac{1}{2}(\ell(w^{\prime})-\ell(w))=\ell_{II}(w^{\prime},[b],J)
. The statements for
\mathcal{T}
can now be deduced from the statements for
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.

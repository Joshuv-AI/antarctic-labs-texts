f ⁡ ( 𝖱 ) f({\sf R}) Gravity Wormholes sourced by a Phantom Scalar Field
Thanasis Karakasis, Eleftherios Papantonopoulos, Christoforos Vlachos

Abstract
We derive an exact wormhole spacetime supported by a phantom scalar field in the context of f ⁡ ( 𝖱 ) f({\sf R}) gravity theory. Without specifying the form of the f ⁡ ( 𝖱 ) f({\sf R}) function, the scalar field self-interacts with a mass term potential which is derived from the scalar equation and in the resulting f ⁡ ( 𝖱 ) f({\sf R}) model the scalar curvature is modified by the presence of the scalar field and it is free of ghosts and avoids the tachyonic instability.

II
The setup-derivation of the field equations

We consider the following action

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{f(R)}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\phi\partial_{\mu}\phi-V(\phi)\right)~,

(1)

which consists of an arbitrary differentiable function of the Ricci Scalar
f(R)
, a scalar field with negative kinetic energy and a self-interacting potential.

The field equations that arise by variation of this action are

\displaystyle R_{\mu\nu}f_{R}(R)-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f(R)+\Big(g_{\mu\nu}\Box-\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\Big)f_{R}(R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}~,

(2)

\displaystyle\Box\phi+V_{\phi}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0~,

(3)

where
\Box=\nabla^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}
is the D’Alambert operator with respect to the metric,
f_{R}(R)=df(R)/dR
and the energy momentum tensor is given by

T_{\mu\nu}^{\phi}=-\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\alpha}\phi\partial_{\alpha}\phi-g_{\mu\nu}V(\phi)~.

(4)

We consider the following metric ansatz firstly used by Morris and Thorne
Morris
in spherical coordinates

ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi(r)}dt^{2}+\left(1-\frac{b(r)}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega~,

(5)

where
\Phi(r)
is the redshift function and
b(r)
is the shape function and
d\Omega=d\theta^{2}+\sin(\theta)^{2}d\varphi^{2}
. In order to obtain a wormhole geometry, these functions have to satisfy the following conditions
Morris
;
Visser:1995cc
, namely:

1.

\frac{b(r)}{r}\leq 1
for every
[r_{th},+\infty)
, where
r_{th}
is the radius of the throat. This condition ensures that the proper radial distance defined by
l(r)=\pm\int_{r_{th}}^{r}\left(1-\frac{b(r)}{r}\right)^{-1}dr
is finite everywhere in spacetime.
Note that in the coordinates
(t,l,\theta,\phi)
the line element (
5
) can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi(l)}dt^{2}+dl^{2}+r^{2}(l)(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi)\,.

(6)

In this case the throat radius would be given by
r_{th}=\min\{r(l)\}
.

2.

\frac{b(r_{th})}{r_{th}}=1
at the throat. This relation comes from requiring the throat to be a stationary point of
r(l)
. Equivalently, one may arrive at this equation by demanding the embedded surface of the wormhole to be vertical at the throat.

3.

b^{\prime}(r)<\frac{b(r)}{r}
which reduces to
b^{\prime}(r_{th})\leq 1
at the throat. This is known as the flare-out condition since it guarantees
r_{th}
to be a minimum and not any other stationary point.

Moreover, to simplify the calculations and to ensure the absence of horizons we will consider the case
\Phi(r)=C
as in
Lobo:2009ip
, where
C
is a constant. Computing the
tt,rr,\theta\theta
and the Klein-Gordon equations we find

\displaystyle rb^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{R}+3bf^{\prime}_{R}+2r(b-r)f^{\prime\prime}_{R}-rb\phi^{\prime 2}-4rf^{\prime}_{R}+r^{2}f-2r^{2}V+r^{2}\phi^{\prime 2}=0~,

(7)

\displaystyle 2f_{R}\left(b-rb^{\prime}\right)+4r(b-r)f^{\prime}_{R}+r^{2}(b-r)\phi^{\prime 2}+r^{3}(f-2V)=0~,

(8)

\displaystyle r\left(r\left(\left(b^{\prime}-2\right)f^{\prime}_{R}+r\left(-2f^{\prime\prime}_{R}+f-2V+\phi^{\prime 2}\right)\right)-f_{R}b^{\prime}\right)-b\left(r\left(-f^{\prime}_{R}-2rf^{\prime\prime}_{R}+r\phi^{\prime 2}\right)+f_{R}\right)=0

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{\left(r\left(b^{\prime}-4\right)+3b\right)\phi^{\prime}+2r(b-r)\phi^{\prime\prime}}{2r^{2}}-\frac{V^{\prime}}{\phi^{\prime}}=0~,

(10)

where primes denote derivative with respect to the argument, the radial co-ordinate
r
and
f=f(R)=f(r)
, the gravitational model as a function of the radial co-ordinate
r
. Eliminating
f
from equation (
7

f=2V-\frac{\left(r\left(b^{\prime}-4\right)+3b\right)f^{\prime}_{R}+r(r-b)\left(\phi^{\prime 2}-2f^{\prime\prime}_{R}\right)}{r^{2}}~.

(11)

Now substituting
f
back to equations (
8
) and (
9
) we can obtain the following equations

\displaystyle r\left(r\left(b^{\prime}f^{\prime}_{R}-2rf^{\prime\prime}_{R}+2r\phi^{\prime 2}\right)+2f_{R}b^{\prime}\right)-b\left(r\left(f^{\prime}_{R}-2rf^{\prime\prime}_{R}+2r\phi^{\prime 2}\right)+2f_{R}\right)=0~,

(12)

\displaystyle r\left(f_{R}b^{\prime}-2rf^{\prime}_{R}\right)+b\left(2rf^{\prime}_{R}+f_{R}\right)=0~.

(13)

As can be seen in equation (

), there is is a direct relation between the geometry and the
f(R)
gravity, while the scalar field will affect both the
f(R)
function and the geometry as we can see in equation (

The above results indicate that the presence of the phantom

Now we will discuss the violation of the energy conditions
Rahaman:2006vg
. The Weak Energy Condition (WEC) implies that any time like vector
x^{\mu}
satisfies the condition

T_{\mu\nu}^{total}x^{\mu}x^{\nu}\geq 0~.

(47)

In the afforementioned reference frame this inequality takes the form

\rho>0~.

(48)

In our case we have

\rho=\frac{2}{r^{2}}-3m^{2}>0~.

(49)

The WEC is satisfied for any
m\neq 0
and
0<r<\sqrt{\frac{2}{3m^{2}}}<\frac{1}{m}=r_{\text{th}}
. Therefore WEC is violated for any
r>r_{\text{th}}
.

The Null Energy Condition (NEC) states that

T_{\mu\nu}^{total}k^{\mu}k^{\nu}\geq 0~,

(50)

for any null vector
k^{\mu}
. By continuity we expect that the WEC imples NEC. In the orthonormal frame the NEC reads

\rho+p_{r}\geq 0~,

(51)

In our case NEC gives

\frac{2-3m^{2}r^{2}}{r^{2}}+m^{2}-\frac{2}{r^{2}}=-2m^{2}<0~.

(52)

Therefore NEC is always violated.

To have a better understanding on how the energy conditions are violated, we will discuss the energy conditions of the scalar field and the modified gravity part independently. For the scalar field we will only consider the
f_{R}^{-1}T^{\phi}_{\mu\nu}
part of the energy momentum tensor in equation (

\displaystyle\rho^{\phi}=\frac{3}{r^{2}}~,

\displaystyle p_{r}^{\phi}=\frac{3}{r^{2}}-6m^{2}~.

(54)

The WEC energy condition is satisfied for the scalar field while for the NEC we have that

\rho^{\phi}+p_{r}^{\phi}=\frac{6}{r^{2}}-6m^{2}>0~,

(55)

which is satisfied for
r<\frac{1}{m}=r_{th}
(outside of the wormhole region) and is violated outside and on the wormhole throat.

Now for the gravitational part of the energy momentum tensor we will only examine the

\displaystyle\rho^{grav}=-3m^{2}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}~,

(56)

\displaystyle p_{r}^{grav}=7m^{2}-\frac{5}{r^{2}}~.

(57)

The WEC is violated by the higher order gravity terms, while NEC yields

\rho^{grav}+p_{r}^{grav}=4m^{2}-\frac{6}{r^{2}}>0~,

(58)

which holds for

It is clear that if we add the energy densities of the matter and gravity part we obtain the total energy density (

IV
Conclusions

We studied wormhole solutions in a modified gravity theory in which the scalar curvature is generalized to a
f({\sf R})
function. In this theory we considered a scalar field with negative kinetic energy, a phantom scalar field, with a self-interacting potential. Solving the field equations, choosing a
function for the phantom scalar field, we find a wormhole solution without specifying the form of the
f({\sf R})
function. The basic properties of this solution are that the presence of the phantom scalar field influences the scalar curvature and the size of the throat, and by increasing the strength of the scalar field we obtain wormholes with larger throat radii which in turn decreases the curvature in the vicinity of the throat.

Our obtained gravitational model satisfies the Dolgov-Kawasaki stability criterion, is tachyonically stable and it is free of ghosts. We calculated the NEC and the WEC and we showed that they are violated. We also investigated the matter and gravity part of the effective energy-momentum tensor independently to see their effects on the energy conditions. We found that the scalar filed respects WEC and violated NEC, while the gravity part of the effective energy-momentum tensor violates both WEC and NEC. We also note that the
f(\sf R)
model we obtained resembles the Starobinsky inflation model
Starobinsky:1980te
.

Our solution cannot be reduced to GR. To do that we have to consider a more general metric ansatz such the one in
Bronnikov:2018vbs
and try to find a solution to the
f({\sf R})
field equations that reduces to the known solutions of GR
Bronnikov:2018vbs
;
Huang:2020qmn
;
Huang:2019arj
. In our work, the scalar field is minimally coupled to gravity via the volume element, so one could consider a more general ansatz like a regular non-minimally coupled scalar field that will violate the energy conditions and try to find wormhole solutions. It would also be interesting to check the stability of the obtained wormhole solution.

One can fix the
f({\sf R})
function from the beginning and then look for wormhole solutions. For example in
Karakasis:2021rpn
a non-linear correction term in the scalar curvature was introduced
f({\sf R})=\sf R-2\alpha\sqrt{\sf R}
where
\alpha
has the dimensions of
|L|^{-1}
. In this modified gravity theory a new scale is introduced. Therefore to find a wormhole solution that resembles the original Ellis wormhole
Ellis:1973yv
;
Canate:2019spb
(or the
d
-dimensional generalization
Rahaman:2008tq
) another scale has to be introduced to the theory to counterbalance the introduced gravitational scale. The easiest way to do that is to introduce a new scale via a self-interacting potential for the scalar field. So, one can consider our metric ansatz (
5
) and the Ellis scalar field (
16
) to find, at least in principle, a wormhole geometry, which will be reduced to the Ellis one, after parameterizing the constants of the solution appropriately. Wormholes with dynamical redshift function supported by a phantom scalar field might also be interesting to be studied.

An Introduction to PMU-Defect-Robust Power Domination: Bounds, Bipartites, and Block Graphs
Beth Bjorkman, Esther Conrad, Mary Flagg

Abstract
Sensors called phasor measurement units (PMUs) are used to monitor the electric power network. The power domination problem seeks to minimize the number of PMUs needed to monitor the network. We extend the power domination problem and consider the minimum number of sensors and appropriate placement to ensure monitoring when k k sensors are allowed to fail with multiple sensors allowed to be placed in one location. That is, what is the minimum multiset of the vertices, S S , such that for every F ⊆ S F\subseteq S with | F | = k |F|=k , S ∖ F S\setminus F is a power dominating set. Such a set of PMUs is called a k k -PMU-defect-robust power domination set . This paper generalizes the work done by Pai, Chang and Wang in 2010 on fault-tolerant power domination, which did not allow for multiple sensors to be placed at the same vertex. We provide general bounds and determine the k k -PMU-defect-robust power domination number of some graph families.



Keywords—
Power Domination, Robust, Fault-Tolerant

1
Introduction

The
power domination problem
seeks to minimize the number of sensors called phasor measurement units (PMUs) used to monitor the electric power network, defined by Haynes et al. in
[
4
]
. The electrical power grid is represented as a graph in which each vertex represents an electrical node and each edge represents a transmission line joining two electrical nodes. The PMUs are placed on the vertices and observation rules are applied to both the vertices and the edges of the graph: A PMU monitors the vertex where it is placed as well as the adjacent vertices and the edges incident to the vertex. Ohm’s law and Kirchoff’s law are used to solve a system of equations to monitor one unknown edge and one unknown vertex at a time. This process, as defined in
[
4
]
, consisted of five rules focusing on the observation of both the edges and the vertices. In
[
2
]
, Brueni and Heath simplified the process to two rules that focus solely on the observation of the vertices. The vertex-only observation process was shown to be equivalent to the original definition.

Pai, Chang, and Wang
[
5
]
generalized power domination to create
fault-tolerant power domination
in 2010 to model the possibility of sensor failure. The
k
-
fault-tolerant power domination problem
seeks to find the minimum number of PMUs needed to monitor a power network (and their placements) given that any
k
of the PMUs will fail. The vertex containing the failed PMU remains in the graph, as do its edges; it is only the PMU that fails. This generalization allows for the placement of only one PMU per vertex.

We consider the related problem of the minimum number of PMUs needed to monitor a power network given that
k
PMUs will fail
but also allow for multiple PMUs to be placed at a given vertex
. We call this
PMU-defect-robust power domination
, as it is not the vertices that cause a problem with monitoring the network, but the individual PMUs themselves. This models potential synchronization issues, sensor errors, or malicious interference with the sensor outputs.

To demonstrate the difference between fault-tolerant power domination and PMU-defect-robust power domination and how drastic the difference between these two parameters can be, consider the star on
16
vertices with
k=1
, shown in Figure
1
. Notice that in fault-tolerant power domination, if one PMU is placed in the center of the star and this PMU fails, then all but one of the leaves must have PMUs in order to still form a power dominating set. However, with PMU-defect-robust power domination, placing two PMUs in the center is sufficient to ensure that even if one PMU fails, the power domination process will still observe all of the vertices.

Figure 1:
A minimum fault-tolerant power dominating set and a minimum PMU-defect-robust power dominating set shown for a star graph when
k=1

This paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
we give formal definitions of basic graph theory terminology, and of the power domination and PMU-defect-robust power domination processes. Section
3
presents basic bounds for the
k
-PMU-defect-robust power domination number and improve these bounds in the case that minimum (or almost minimum) power dominating sets overlap sufficiently. Complete bipartite graphs are covered in Section
4
and block graphs in Section
5
. We conclude in Section
6
with ideas for future work.

A

G=(V,E)

,
V=V(G)
, and

, consisting of unordered pairs of distinct vertices, usually denoted as
uv

. The

. The

of a vertex

, denoted by
\deg{v}{}
, is the cardinality of

. The

of

is defined as
\delta(G)=\min\{\deg{v}{}:v\in V(G)\}
. The
maximum degree
of
G
is defined as
\Delta(G)=\max\{\deg{v}{}:v\in V(G)\}
.

A

between vertices

and

is a sequence of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell}
such that

for all

. A

v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\ell},v_{1}
such that
v_{i}v_{i+1}\in E(G)
for all

and

. A

is a graph in which there is a path between any two vertices. A

An

has vertex set

and edge set

The

proceeds as follows. Given a set

of initial PMU locations, define the set

of observed vertices recursively by

1.

(Domination Step)
PDS=N[S]

2.

(Zero Forcing Step) While there exists
v\in PDS
such that
v
has exactly one neighbor
w
not in
PDS
,
PDS=PDS\cup\{w\}
.

If
PDS=V(G)
at the end of this process,

is called a
power dominating set
. The
power domination number
of

, denoted by
\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
, is the minimum cardinality of a power dominating set.

A

is a power dominating set
S
in which the removal of any single vertex from
S
results in a set that is not a power dominating set. Minimum power dominating sets are minimal, but minimal power dominating sets are not necessarily minimum.

Observation 1
.

Every power dominating set
S
contains a minimal power dominating set as a subset.

In
[
5
]
, Pai, Chang, and Wang define the following variant of power domination. For a graph
G
and an integer
k
with
0\leq k\leq|V|
, a set

is called a
k
-fault-tolerant power dominating set of

if

is still a power dominating set of

for any subset
F\subseteq V
with
|F|\leq k
. The
k
-fault-tolerant power domination number
, denoted by
{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
, is the minimum cardinality of a
k
-fault-tolerant power dominating set of

.

While

-fault-tolerant power domination allows us to examine what occurs when a previously chosen PMU location is no longer usable (yet the vertex remains in the graph), PMU-defect-robust power domination allows for multiple PMUs to be placed at the same location and consider if a subset of the PMUs fail. This also avoids issues with poorly connected graphs, such as in Figure
1
. Instead of beginning with a set of vertices to be PMU locations, a

of PMU locations is used.

Definition 1
.

Let
G
be a graph,
k\geq 0
be an integer and let
S
be a multiset of PMUs with underlying set a subset of
V(G)
.
The multiset
S
is a
k
-PMU-defect-robust power dominating set
(

-rPDS) if for any submultiset

with
|F|=k
,

contains a power dominating set of vertices. The minimum cardinality of a

-rPDS is denoted
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
. A
k
-rPDS of minimum cardinality is called a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\text{-set}
.

Notice that a set
S\subseteq V(G)
on which PMUs are placed is a multiset in which the multiplicity of
v\in S
represents the number of PMUs placed on the vertex
v
. Denote the multiplicity of
v
in the multiset
S
of PMUs by
\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S}\left(v\right)
, or when
S
is clear by
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(v\right)
. Given a subset

, define

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)=\sum_{v\in A}\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(v\right).

Given a multiset
S
of vertices of the graph

, the ability of

to monitor the graph depends only on whether the underlying set of vertices is a power dominating set, not the multiplicity of each vertex in

. If
S
is a
k
-rPDS, removing any

PMUs must leave an underlying set that contains a minimal (but not necessarily minimum) power dominating set. It will be useful to translate the concept of a minimal power dominating set to a multiset without a minimal condition on multiplicity.

Definition 2
.

Given a graph
G
, a multiset
M
of vertices of
G
is called an
essential multiset
if the underlying set of
M
is a minimal power dominating set.

There are several observations that one can quickly make.

Observation 2
.

Every
k
-rPDS of a graph
G
must contain an essential multiset.

Observation 3
.

Let
G
be a graph and

. Then

1.

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{0}\left(G\right)={\gamma}_{P}^{0}\left(G\right)=\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
,

2.

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
,

3.

\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)=1
if and only if
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)=k+1
.

Observation 4
.

For any graph

, if

is a

-rPDS of

, then any multiset

of vertices of

such that

is also a

-rPDS. To see this, note that removing

removes at most

.

Observation 5
.

If

is a
(k+1)
-rPDS, and

, then

Bounds

3.1
General Bounds

A useful property of PMU-defect-robust power domination is the subadditivity of the parameter with respect to
k
. This idea is established in the next three statements.

Proposition 1
.

Let
k\geq 0
. For any graph
G
,
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)+1\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k+1}\left(G\right)
.

Proof.

Consider a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k+1}\text{-set}
,
S
, of
G
. Let
v\in S
. Create
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{v\}
, that is,
S^{\prime}
is
S
with one fewer PMU at
v
. Observe that for any
F^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}
with
|F^{\prime}|=k
, we have
F^{\prime}\cup\{v\}\subseteq S
and
|F^{\prime}\cup\{v\}|=k+1
. Hence
S\setminus\left(F^{\prime}\cup\{v\}\right)
is a power dominating set of
G
. Thus, for any such
F^{\prime}
, we have
\left(S\setminus\{v\}\right)\setminus F^{\prime}=S^{\prime}\setminus F^{\prime}
is a power dominating set of
G
. Therefore,
S^{\prime}
is a
k
-rPDS of
G
of size
|S|-1
.
∎

Proposition
1
can be applied repeatedly to obtain the next result.

Corollary 1
.

Let
k\geq 0
and
j\geq 1
. For any graph
G
,

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)+j\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k+j}\left(G\right).

Corollary
1
implies the lower bound in the next proposition. The upper bound follows from taking
k+1
copies of any minimum power dominating set for
G
to form a
k
-rPDS.

Proposition 2
.

Let
k\geq 0
. For any graph
G
,

\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)+k\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right).

In a similar vein, we can add a power dominating set to a
k
-rPDS in order to create a
(k+1)
-rPDS.

Lemma 1
.

For a graph
G
and a positive integer
k
,
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k+1}\left(G\right)\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)+\gamma_{P}\left(G\right).

Proof.

For the given graph
G
, let
S
be a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\text{-set}
and let
D
be a set of PMUs consisting of one PMU on each vertex of a minimum power dominating set of vertices. Let
C=S\cup D
, and note that
S
and
D
need not be disjoint since
C
is a multiset of PMUs. Removing
k+1
PMUs from the multiset
C
either removes all
k+1
PMUs from
S
, leaving one PMU on each vertex in
D
, or removes at most
k
PMUs from
S
and at least one PMU from
D
, which leaves a power dominating set since
S
is a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\text{-set}
.
∎

We note that for any graph
G
with
\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)=1
, Observation
3
, Proposition
2
, and Lemma
1
each demonstrate that
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)=k+1.

Haynes et al. observed in
[
4

that in a graph with maximum degree at least 3, a minimum power dominating set can be chosen in which each vertex has degree at least
3
. We observe that this is the same for PMU-defect-robust power domination.

Observation 6
.

Let
k\geq 0
. If
G
is a connected graph with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
, then
G
contains a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\text{-set}
in which every vertex has degree at least 3.

A
terminal path
from a vertex
v
in
G
is a path from
v
to a vertex

such that
\deg{u}{}=1
and every internal vertex on the path has degree 2. A
terminal cycle
from a vertex
v
in
G
is a cycle
v,u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{\ell},v
in which
\deg{u_{i}}{G}=2
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
k\geq 0
and let
G
be a connected graph with
\Delta(G)\geq 3
. Let
S
be a
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\text{-set}
in which every vertex has degree at least 3. Any vertex
v\in S
that has at least two terminal paths from
v
must have
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(v\right)=k+1
. Any vertex
v\in S
that has at least one terminal cycle must have
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(v\right)=k+1
.

Proof.

Let
v
be a vertex in
S
and suppose that
v
has two terminal paths or a terminal cycle. All of the vertices in the terminal paths or terminal cycle have degree 1 or 2 and so are not in
S
. Thus, there are at least two neighbors of

which can only be observed via

. As

can only observe both of these neighbors via the domination step, it must be the case that
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(v\right)=k+1
.
∎

Zhao, Kang, and Chang
[
7
]
defined the family of graphs
\mathcal{T}
to be those graphs obtained by taking a connected graph

and for each vertex

adding two vertices,
v^{\prime}
and

and

, with the edge

optional. The complete bipartite graph

is the graph with vertex set

and edge set

.

Theorem 1
.

[
7

If

is a connected graph on

\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)\leq\frac{n}{3}
with equality if and only if

.

This gives an upper bound for
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
in terms of the size of the vertex set and equality conditions, as demonstrated in the next corollary.

Corollary 2
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with
n\geq 3
vertices. Then
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq(k+1)\frac{n}{3}
for

. When

, this is an equality if and only if
G\in\mathcal{T}\cup\{K_{3,3}\}
. When

, this is an equality if and only if

.

Proof.

The upper bound is given by Proposition
2
and Theorem

. From these results, we need only consider
\mathcal{T}\cup\{K_{3,3}\}
for equality. The

, equality for graphs in

follows from Proposition

. For the case of

and we will show in Corollary

that
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{3,3}\right)=k+\left\lfloor\frac{k}{5}\right\rfloor+2<2(k+1)

.

3.2
The overlapping power dominating sets bound

It is often the case in PMU-defect-robust power domination that we would like to overlap power dominating sets in such a way that the combined set is PMU-defect-robust. Therefore, the following parameter is introduced.

Definition 3
.

Let
G
be a graph and choose an integer
j\geq\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
. Let
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)
be the maximum cardinality of a set
B\subseteq V(G)
such that if
A\subseteq B
and
|A|=j
, then
A
is a power dominating set. If no such set
B
exists,
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)
is undefined. When
j=\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
, we denote
\mathsf{s}_{\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)}\left(G\right)
by
\mathsf{s}\left(G\right)
.

For examples of
\mathsf{s}\left(G\right)
, see Figure
2
.

Figure 2:
Examples of the parameter
\mathsf{s}\left(G\right)
, with the set shaded

Observation 7
.

Note that a set
B
which satisfies the conditions of Definition
3
is a
k
-rPDS and also a
k
-fault-tolerant set for
k=\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j
.

The connection between
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)
, fault-tolerance, and robustness when

, is shown in the next proposition.

Proposition 4
.

For any graph
G
and
k\geq 0
, if
\mathsf{s}\left(G\right)\geq k+\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
then
{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)=\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)=k+\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
.

Proof.

If
\mathsf{s}\left(G\right)\geq k+\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
, then there exists a set
S
of size at least
k+\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
so that any
\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
elements of
S
form a power dominating set of
G
. Thus, any
\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)+k
elements of
S
form a
k
-rPDS and also a
k
–fault-tolerant power dominating set of
G
of size
\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)+k
and so
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)+k
. By the lower bound in Proposition
2
,
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\geq\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)+k
.
∎

Note that in Definition
3
, if
j>\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
, it is possible that some subsets of
B
of size
j
will not be minimal, however, these subsets will still be power dominating sets. Thus, we use the parameter
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)
to create a new upper bound.

Theorem 2
.

Given a graph
G
, integers
k\geq 1
and
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)>j\geq\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
. Using the division algorithm, determine non-negative integers
q
and
r
such that
k+1=q(\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1)+r
and
0\leq r<\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1
. Then,

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)\left(\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right)&\text{if $r=0$}\\
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right\rfloor+r+(j-1)&\text{if $r>0$}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
B=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)}\}\subseteq V(G)
such that every subset of
B
of order
j
is a power dominating set. Use the division algorithm to find nonnegative integers
q
and
r
such that
k+1=q(\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1)+r
and
0\leq r<\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1
.

If
r=0
, then place
q=\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}
PMUs on each vertex in
B
. Note that we need at least

\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1

k+1

\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j
. This leaves at least
j

k
-rPDS of
G
.

If

, let
q=\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right\rfloor

Place

B
. Place

PMUs on the remaining
(\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1)-r

\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1

\displaystyle q((\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1)-r)+(q+1)r

\displaystyle=q(\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1)+r

\displaystyle=k+1

PMUs. This means that in the worst case, we have
j-1
vertices with
q+1
PMUs and
1
vertex with
1
PMU, and so we have constructed a
k
-rPDS of
G
.
∎

Proposition 5

Proof.

Using the division algorithm, determine non-negative integers

(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-\frac{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)(k+1)}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}=(k+1)\left(\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-\frac{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right).

\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)>\frac{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}.

When

is not evenly divisible by
\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1

\displaystyle(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-

\displaystyle\left(\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)\left\lfloor\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right\rfloor+r+(j-1)\right)

\displaystyle>(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)\left(\frac{k+1}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right)-r-j+1

\displaystyle=(k+1)\left(\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-\frac{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right)-(r+j-1)

\displaystyle>(k+1)\left(\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)-\frac{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)}{\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right)-j+1}\right)-\mathsf{s}_{j}\left(G\right).

Observe that the first term above is a multiple of

Proposition 6

Proof.

By Theorem

4
Complete Bipartite Graphs

The complete bipartite graph
G=K_{a,b}
is the graph with vertex set
V(G)=A\cup B
with
A=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{a}\}
and
B=\{y_{1},y_{2},\dots,y_{b}\}
and edge set
E(G)=\{\{x_{i},y_{j}\}:x_{i}\in A,y_{j}\in B\}
. If one of the partite sides contains one or two vertices, then
\gamma_{P}\left(K_{a,b}\right)=1
and so the bounds in Proposition
2
agree, giving
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{a,b}\right)=k+1
. When
3\leq a\leq b
, the standard approach to finding a minimum set with a specific property is to fix
a
and look for a pattern for
K_{a,b}
for all
b\geq a
. Preliminary computation showed that no clear pattern emerged because the modular arithmetic depends on the size of both
a
and
b
. Therefore, our approach is to find the values of
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
for
k\geq 0
recursively for an arbitrary
K_{a,b}
with fixed
3\leq a\leq b
. At the end of this section, we give examples of how to derive
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{a,b}\right)
for specific values of
a
and
b
using this method.

4.1
Essential multisets and the uncover number

It is well known that
\gamma_{P}\left(K_{a,b}\right)=2
with minimum power dominating sets consisting of any one vertex from each side of the partition. A minimal, but not minimum, power dominating set is either a set of any
a-1
vertices in
A
or a set of any
b-1
vertices in
B
.
This means that for the complete bipartite graphs with
3\leq a\leq b
, essential multisets have 3 distinct structures based on the structure of the underlying minimal power dominating sets.

Definition 4
.

For complete bipartite graphs
K_{a,b}
with
3\leq a\leq b
, we distinguish the types of essential multisets contained in
S
using the following notation:

1.

P
-essential multiset
: There is exactly one vertex in
A
and one vertex in
B
that have PMUs, that is, there exists
x_{i}\in A
and
y_{j}\in B
such that
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{i}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)\geq 1
and
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{j}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)\geq 1
.

2.

A
-essential multiset
: No vertices in
B
have a PMU and all except one vertex in
A
has a PMU, that is,
|\{x_{i}\in A:\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{i}\right)>0\}|=a-1
.

3.

B
-essential multiset
: No vertices in
A
have a PMU, and all except one vertex in
B
has a PMU, that is,
|\{y_{i}\in B:\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{i}\right)>0\}|=b-1
.

Throughout this section, it will be useful to order the vertices by the number of PMUs they contain. For the graph
G=K_{a,b}
with vertex set

where

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)\leq\dots\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{a}\right)

and

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{1}\right)\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{2}\right)\leq\dots\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{b}\right).

A
k
-rPDS
S
contains at least
k+2
PMUs by Proposition
2
, so removing
k

k
PMUs leaves one side without a PMU, there must be only one unobserved vertex on the other side to power dominate. Thus, it is useful to define a parameter that quantifies the maximum number of PMUs that may be removed from side A (side B) and still remain an

Definition 5
.

Let
G=K_{a,b}
with
3\leq a\leq b
and assume
V(G)=A\cup B
with
|A|=a
and
|B|=b
. Let
S
be a multiset of the vertices. Let the vertices of

be labelled so that
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)\leq\dots\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{a}\right)
and similarly label the vertices of

so that
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{1}\right)\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{2}\right)\leq\dots\leq\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{b}\right)
. The

, denoted

, is defined to be

u_{a}=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)-1.

Similarly, define the
uncover number for side
B
, denoted
u_{b}
, to be

u_{b}=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{1}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{2}\right)-1.

We make the following observation about negative uncover numbers.

Observation 9
.

It is possible that the uncover number is negative, since
u_{a}=-1
when
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)=0
. This represents the case that the original multiset

does not contain an
A
-essential multiset. Similarly,
u_{b}=-1
when
S
does not contain a

-essential multiset.

The uncover number is useful in determining how many PMUs can be removed from each partite set, as demonstrated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3
.

Given a multiset
S
with
u_{a}\geq 0
(
u_{b}\geq 0
), then
u_{a}
(
u_{b}
) is the maximum number of PMUs that can be removed from side A (side B) with the resulting multiset being an
A
-essential (
B
-essential) multiset.

Proof.

Assume first that
u_{a}=0
. This implies
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)=1
, and
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{j}\right)\geq 1
for all
2\leq j\leq a
. However, the removal of one PMU from

results in a multiset that is not

-essential. Therefore, the maximum number of PMUs that can be removed from side

-essential is 0.

If

,
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{1}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{2}\right)=u_{a}+1
, so the removal of

and

have no PMUs, and so the resulting multiset is not

-essential. On the other hand, since
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{i}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x_{j}\right)\geq u_{a}
for all

, the removal of
u_{a}

A
-essential. Therefore, the maximum number of PMUs that can be removed from side
A
to ensure that the resulting multiset is
A
-essential is
u_{a}
.

The same argument holds for side B.
∎

Theorem 3
.

The multiset

For the sufficiency, assume

When the uncover numbers are both nonnegative, choose a multiset

and

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)
.

Definition 6
.

A multiset
S

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)=y

,

\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(y_{j}\right)\in\{q_{b},q_{b}+1\}

.

with the same
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)
and
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)
such that
S^{e}
is also a
k
-rPDS.

Proof.

Let
G=K_{a,b}
with vertices partitioned into sets

and

with
|A|=a
and
|B|=b
,
S
with
|S|=t
,
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)=x
and
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)=y
with
x+y=t
. Without loss of generality, label the vertices on each side of the graph in increasing order of the number of PMUs on each vertex in
S
. Define
S^{e}
to be the evenly distributed multiset with
x
PMUs on side
A
and
y

B
.

If
x\geq k+1
and
y\geq k+1
the distribution of the PMUs does not matter, since removing any multiset of size
k
will result in a multiset containing a

-essential multiset, so

is also a
k
-rPDS.

If
x\leq k
or

,

must contain an

-essential or a

-essential multiset. (If both sides contain

or fewer PMUs,

must contain both an

-essential and a
B
-essential multiset.)

There exist nonnegative integers
q_{a}
,
r_{a}
,
q_{b}
and
r_{b}
such that

,
0\leq r_{a}<a
and
y=q_{b}b+r_{b}
,
0\leq r_{b}<b
. In the evenly distributed multiset
S^{e}
, the maximum number of PMUs that may be removed from

so that the resultant set still contains an

u_{a}^{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2q_{a}&\text{if $r_{a}=a-1$}\\
2q_{a}-1&\text{if $r_{a}<a-1$}\end{array}\right.

Similarly,

u_{b}^{e}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}2q_{b}&\text{if $r_{b}=b-1$}\\
2q_{b}-1&\text{if $r_{b}<b-1$}\end{array}\right.

We will now show that

and

. First consider the case that

\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S}\left(x_{1}\right)+\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S}\left(x_{2}\right)-1=u_{a}>u_{a}^{e}=2q_{a}-1

. Then by the Pigeonhole Principle and the ordering of the vertices,

and so each of the other vertices

a contradiction. A similar argument holds for the case that

, and a symmetric argument also shows

. Therefore,

and

. Thus

is a

-rPDS by Theorem

.
∎

4.2
The Parameter Sequence

Let
G=K_{a,b}
for a specific
3\leq a\leq b
. For all
k\geq 0
, define
(p_{k})
to be the sequence of positive integers so that
p_{k}=\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
. In this section, we will construct this sequence recursively from the base case that
p_{0}=\gamma_{P}\left(K_{a,b}\right)=2
for any complete bipartite graph with
3\leq a\leq b
. Proposition
1
and Lemma
1
combine to yield the following proposition.

Proposition 8
.

Given the complete graph
K_{a,b}
with
3\leq a\leq b
and an integer

,

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{a,b}\right)+1\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k+1}\left(K_{a,b}\right)\leq\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{a,b}\right)+2.

By Proposition
8
,
p_{k+1}=p_{k}+1
or
p_{k+1}=p_{k}+2
. This leads to the main focus of this section:

Question 1
.

For a complete bipartite graph
K_{a,b}
with
3\leq a\leq b
, when is the addition of two PMUs necessary when moving from a
k
-rPDS to a
k+1
-rPDS?

In order to answer Question
1
, we will need the following definition.

Definition 7
.

Given integers
3\leq a\leq b
,
k\geq 0
, and
p_{k}=\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{a,b}\right)
, the pair
(k,p_{k})
is said to satisfy the Jump Condition for
K_{a,b}
if there exists nonnegative integers
q_{a}
,
r_{a}
,
q_{b}
and
r_{b}
for which
p_{k}=q_{a}a+r_{a}+q_{b}b+r_{b}
such that the following relationships hold:

1.

q_{a}a+r_{a}+2q_{b}-1=k

2.

q_{b}b+r_{b}+2q_{a}-1=k

3.

r_{a}\leq a-3

4.

r_{b}\leq b-3

Observe that the requirement that
q_{a}a+r_{a}+q_{b}b+r_{b}=p_{k}
defines a minimum evenly distributed
k
-rPDS of
K_{a,b}
, denoted
S^{e}
, with
q_{a}a+r_{a}
PMUs on side A, and
q_{b}b+r_{b}
PMUs on side B. We examine this set in Lemma
5
.

Lemma 5
.

If the pair
(k,p_{k})
satisfies the Jump Condition, then the evenly distributed multiset
S^{e}
with
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)=q_{a}a+r_{a}
and
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)=q_{b}b+r_{b}
is the unique evenly distributed minimum
k
-rPDS.

Proof.

The multiset
S^{e}
has
x=q_{a}a+r_{a}
PMUs on side A, and
y=q_{b}b+r_{b}
PMUs on side B. To show that
S^{e}
is the unique evenly distributed minimum
k
-rPDS, it necessary to show that no other multiset
C
with
|C|=p_{k}
and a different number of PMUs on side A and side B is a
k
-rPDS. Let
C^{w}
be an evenly distributed multiset with
x-w=q_{a}a+r_{a}-w
PMUs on side A, and
y+w=q_{b}b+r_{b}+w
PMUs on side B, and let
u_{a}^{w}
and
u_{b}^{w}
be the respective uncover numbers for
C^{w}
.

Consider the case when
w=1
, that is, the multiset
C^{1}
with 1 fewer PMU on side
A
and 1 more PMU on side
B
. Then the condition that
r_{b}\leq b-3
implies that there are at least 3 vertices so that
\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S^{e}}\left(y_{1}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S^{e}}\left(y_{2}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S^{e}}\left(y_{3}\right)=q_{b}
, and so by moving only 1 PMU to side
B
,
\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S^{e}}\left(y_{1}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}_{S^{e}}\left(y_{2}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}_{C^{1}}\left(y_{1}\right)=\operatorname{\#PMU}_{C^{1}}\left(y_{2}\right)
and so
u_{b}^{1}=u_{b}
. However,
\operatorname{\#PMU}_{C^{1}}\left(A\right)+u_{b}^{1}=(x-1)+u_{b}=k-1
, so
C^{1}
is not a
k
-rPDS by Theorem
3
.

The maximum number of PMUs that can be removed from
C^{w}
and retain a
B
-essential multiset is
x-w+u_{b}^{w}
, and this function is a nonincreasing function of
w
. To see this, note that if
x-w+u_{b}^{w}=m_{w}
, then removing one more PMU from side A and adding it to side B can only increase
u_{b}
by at most one. Thus
m_{w+1}=x-(w+1)+u_{b}^{w+1}\leq m_{w}
. Since
m_{1}=k-1
, shifting more PMUs to side B results in a multiset that is not a
k
-rPDS. A symmetric argument shows that shifting PMUs to side A results in a multiset

that is not a
k
-rPDS. Thus,
S^{e}
is the unique evenly distributed multiset which is a minimum
k
-rPDS.
∎

As an implication of Lemma
5
, any multiset
S
that is a minimum
k
-PDS for

when
(k,p_{k})
satisfies the Jump Condition must have
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(A\right)=q_{a}a+r_{a}
,
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(B\right)=q_{b}b+r_{b}
,
u_{a}=2q_{a}-1
, and
u_{b}=2q_{b}-1

even if the PMUs are not distributed evenly
.

We are now able to answer Question

in Theorem

Theorem 4
.

Given
G=K_{a,b}
with
3\leq a\leq b
,
k\geq 0
and
p_{k}=\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)
, then

p_{k+1}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{k}+2&\text{if $(k,p_{k})$ satisfies the Jump Condition}\\
p_{k}+1&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

First assume that
(k,p_{k})
does not satisfy the Jump Condition. Let
S
be a minimum evenly distributed
k
-rPDS with
x=q_{a}a+r_{a}
vertices on side A, and
y=q_{b}b+r_{b}
vertices on side B.

In the case that the Jump Condition fails due to part 1 or 2, without loss of generality, assume
y+u_{a}\neq k
. Since
S
is a
k
-rPDS, Theorem
3
implies
y+u_{a}\geq k
and
x+b_{b}\geq k
. Then we obtain
y+u_{a}\geq k+1
. Add one PMU to side A to create the multiset

. Then

. By Theorem

, this implies
S^{+1}
is
(k+1)
-robust, and thus

.

In the case that the Jump Condition fails due to part 3 or 4, without loss of generality, assume
a-2\leq r_{a}<a
. Create the multiset
S^{+1}
by adding one PMU to a vertex in

with
q_{a}
PMUs. Then
u_{a}^{+1}=u_{a}+1
and so

. Also,
(x+1)+u_{b}\geq k+1
, implying
S^{+1}
is a
(k+1)
-rPDS of order
p_{k}+1
.

If
(k,p_{k})
satisfies the Jump Condition, let
S^{e}
be the unique evenly distributed minimum
k
-rPDS defined by the Jump Condition as shown in Lemma
5
. Suppose for eventual contradiction that
T
is a minimum
(k+1)
-rPDS of order
p_{k}+1
with
x
PMUs on side A and
y
PMUs on side B. By Lemma

, there exists an evenly distributed
(k+1)
-rPDS,

, with the same number of PMUs on each side. Remove one PMU from

in such a way as the resulting multiset

remains evenly distributed. Since

is a
k+1
-rPDS,

must be a

-rPDS by Observation

. Then as

is evenly distributed,

is evenly distributed, and

satisfies the Jump Condition, by Lemma

=

. Adding one vertex to either side of

does not change the uncover numbers since parts 3 and 4 of the Jump Condition imply there are at least 3 vertices with

or

PMUs. If the extra vertex is added to side A, then

and

, and so the multiset

is not

-robust, contradicting the assumption. A similar result holds if the extra PMU is added to side B. Therefore, a
(k+1)
-rPDS of order

is impossible, and
p_{k+1}=p_{k}+2
.
∎

4.3.1

The complete bipartite graph

is called

Theorem 5
.

Given the complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
with

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(K_{n,n}\right)=\begin{cases}2(k+1)-4\left\lfloor\frac{k}{n+2}\right\rfloor&k\equiv 0,1,\dots,n-3\pmod{n+2}\\

Proof.

Given an integer

, the Jump Condition for the graph

requires that integers
q_{a}
,
r_{a}
,
q_{b}
and
r_{b}
be found such that

1.

q_{a}\cdot n+r_{a}+2q_{b}-1=k

2.

q_{b}\cdot n+r_{b}+2q_{a}-1=k

3.

0\leq r_{a}\leq n-3
and
0\leq r_{b}\leq n-3
.

Combining Part 1 and Part 2 and simplifying yields

(n-2)(q_{a}-q_{b})=r_{b}-r_{a}.

Since

is a factor of

, this implies

and so

. Substituting

and

, the Jump Condition requires

For

, increasing from a

-rPDS to a
(k+1)
-rPDS requires 2 extra PMUs. In other words, an additional PMU is needed for

. The formula follows from counting the number of times an extra PMU are not needed in the first case, or counting how many extra PMUs are needed in the second case.
∎

Block Graphs

For a connected graph
G
, a vertex
x\in V(G)
is called a
cut–vertex
if when we remove
x
and its corresponding edges the resultant graph, denoted by
G-x
, is disconnected. A maximal connected induced subgraph of
G
without a cut–vertex is called a
block
. The graph
G
is called a
block graph
if every block is complete. Block graphs are a superclass of trees.

A
rooted tree
is a tree in which one vertex designated as the
root
. Suppose two vertices
u
and
w
are in a rooted tree with root
r
. If
u
is on the
r-w
path, we say that
w
is a
descendant
of
u
and
u
is an
ancestor
of
w
. If
u
and
v
are also neighbors, we say that
v
is a
child
of
u
.

Let
G
be a block graph with
t

BK_{1},BK_{2}\dots BK_{t}
and
p

x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{p}
. For each block

of

, define

. In
[
6
]
, Xu et al. defined the

to be the tree with vertex set

where
w_{i}^{B}
is a vertex representing the set
B_{i}
, called a
block–vertex
, and the edge set is
E^{B}=\{(w_{i}^{B},x_{j}):x_{j}\in BK_{i}\}
.
Block–vertices will be referred to by their cardinality.

1.

w_{i}^{B}
is called an

if

.

2.

w_{i}^{B}
is called a

if

.

3.

w_{i}^{B}
is called a

if

.

In
[
6
]
, Xu et. al. defined a liner–time algorithm on the refined cut–tree to find a minimum power dominating set of a block graph. Their algorithm processes the vertices in the refined cut–tree one at a time from the leaves to the root, but it does not necessarily finish the zero forcing steps once a branch has been processed. In this section power domination rules on the refined cut–tree are formally defined that are equivalent to power domination in the block graph using cut–vertices and clarify the arguments in
[
6
]
.

In block graphs, a cut–vertex efficiently dominates the blocks in which it is contained, which leads to the following result.

Lemma 6

(
[
6
]
, Lemma 2.1)
.

Let
G
be a block graph. Then there exists a minimum power dominating set of
G
in which every vertex is a cut–vertex of
G
.

This can be generalized to PMU-defect-robust power domination as follows.

Corollary 5
.

For a block graph
G
, there exists a minimum
k
-rPDS of
G
in which every vertex is a cut–vertex of
G
.

The refined cut–tree of
G
captures the essential connections between the blocks of
G
, and the following definition explains how to translate power domination with cut–vertices in
G
to the refined cut–tree.

Definition 8
.

Given a block graph
G
and its refined cut–tree
T^{B}=(V^{B},E^{B})
, let
S\subseteq V^{B}
be an initial set of cut–vertices. Define
CTS
to be the set of observed vertices of
T^{B}
, and initially set
CTS=S
. Observe the vertices of
T^{B}
according to the following observation rule:

1.

Domination: For every
x_{j}\in S
, and for all
w_{i}^{B}\in N(x_{j})
,
CTS=CTS\cup N[w_{i}^{B}]
.

2.

Zero forcing through empty–blocks: If there exists an observed cut–vertex
x_{j}\in CTS
such that an empty–block
w_{i}^{B}
is the only unobserved neighbor of
x_{j}
, and
w_{i}^{B}
has at most one unobserved neighbor, then
CTS=CTS\cup N[w_{i}^{B}]
.

3.

Zero forcing through one–blocks: If there exists an observed cut–vertex
x_{j}
such that a one–block
w_{i}^{B}
is the only unobserved neighbor of
x_{j}
and every
v\in N(w_{i}^{B})
is observed,
CTS=CTS\cup\{w_{i}^{B}\}
.

Repeat the zero forcing steps until no further changes are possible. If
CTS=V^{B}
at the end of the process, the set
S
is said to be a
power dominating set for the cut–tree
T^{B}
.

Note that power domination on the refined–cut tree is not the same as power domination on a tree. For example, for the refined cut–tree in Figure
9
, a minimum power dominating set for the cut–tree is
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
because the multi–block
w^{B}_{1}
is only observed if its cut–vertex neighbor
x_{1}
is in the power dominating set. However, a minimum power dominating set for the graph is
\{x_{2}\}
, which shows the difference between power domination and power domination for refined cut–trees.

Lemma 7
.

Given a block graph
G=(V,E)
and its corresponding refined cut–tree
T^{B}=(V^{B},E^{B})
, a set
S\subseteq V(G)
in which every vertex is a cut–vertex of
G
is a power dominating set of
G
if and only if the corresponding set
S\subseteq V^{B}(T^{B})
is a power dominating set for the refined cut–tree
T^{B}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in S
and note that
x
is a cut-vertex of
G
. Recall that
w^{B}_{i}\in N_{T^{B}}(x)
implies
x\in BK_{i}
and
N_{T^{B}}[w^{B}_{i}]=\{v\in V(G):v\in BK_{i}\}
. In the refined cut–tree,
x
observes
N_{T^{B}}[w^{B}_{i}]
for all
w^{B}_{i}\in N_{T^{B}}(x)
. In other words,
x
observes the vertices in
T^{B}
corresponding to the neighbors of
x
in
G
. Therefore, a vertex is observed by the domination step for
S
in
G
if and only if its corresponding vertex is observed in the domination step for
S
on
T^{B}
. To show that the power domination process is equivalent on the block graph and the refined cut–tree, it is sufficient to show that if a vertex is observed by a zero forcing step in
G
if and only if its corresponding vertex is observed by a zero forcing step in
T^{B}
.

It is necessary to first show that if the initial set
S
contains only cut–vertices in
G
and
v\in V(G)
is not a cut–vertex, then
v
cannot perform a zero forcing step. If
v
is observed and
v\in BK_{i}
, then
v
had to be observed by one of its neighbors
y\in BK_{i}
. If
y\in S
, all of
BK_{i}
is observed and
v
has no unobserved neighbors. If
y
is not in
S
and
v
has an unobserved neighbor
z
, then
z
is also adjacent to
y
and
y
cannot force

while

cannot be observed and also have a unique unobserved neighbor. Therefore

cannot perform a zero forcing step. Moreover,if

not a cut–vertex and is the only unobserved neighbor of a cut–vertex

containing

Next we consider how zero forcing on

. Assume that

is an observed cut–vertex and has exactly one unobserved neighbor

,

is observed. By the argument above, this implies

is observed and all the block vertex neighbors of

Conversely, if

Theorem 6
.

Given a block graph
G
,
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)=(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a block graph. By Proposition
2
,
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)\leq(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
. By way of contradiction, assume that
\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(G\right)<(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(G\right)
. By Corollary
5
, there exists a minimum
k
-rPDS of
G
in which every vertex is a cut–vertex. Choose such a minimum
k
-rPDS,
S
, such that the number of vertices
x\in S
with
\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x\right)\leq k
is minimum. Define
A=\{x\in S:\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(x\right)\leq k\}
. Choose a vertex
r\in A
and create the refined cut–tree
T^{B}(G)
rooted at
r
.

Let
z
be a vertex in
A
at the maximum distance from the root of
T^{B}
, that is,
d(z,r)=\max\{d(x,r):x\in A\}
. Since
S
is a
k
-rPDS multiset of
T^{B}
, all of the PMUs on

may be removed and the resulting set is still a power dominating set. This implies that observing the descendants of
z
needs at most one block–vertex child of
z
to be observed by zero forcing from

. Create the multiset
S^{\prime}
in which all of the PMUs on
z
are moved to its nearest cut–vertex ancestor

,
S^{\prime}=(S\setminus\{z^{\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(z\right)}\})\cup\{y^{\operatorname{\#PMU}\left(z\right)}\}
. Let
w_{i}^{B}
be the block–vertex adjacent to both
z
and
y
. By the domination rule of power domination on the refined cut–tree, a PMU on
y
observes
N[w_{i}^{B}]
and thus
z
is observed and has at most one unobserved neighbor so

is able to perform a zero forcing step. Therefore,

is a power dominating set of the refined cut–tree. If

is not a minimum

-rPDS, contradicting the fact that

-rPDS of

∎

Every tree is a block graph, so the result for trees is immediate.

Corollary 7

Given a tree

\ddot{\gamma}_{P}^{k}\left(T\right)=(k+1)\gamma_{P}\left(T\right)

Future Work

We have defined the

-PMU-defect-robust power domination number, determining an upper bound and exact results for some families of graphs. There are still interesting questions to explore.

First, as we see in Example

*A.J. Pan-Collantes, C. Muriel, A. Ruiz, J.L. Romero

Abstract
For a system of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) or, more generally, an involutive distribution of vector fields, the problem of its integration is considered. Among the many approaches to this problem, solvable structures provide a systematic procedure of integration via Pfaffian equations that are integrable by quadratures. In this paper structures more general than solvable structures (named 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -structures) are considered. The symmetry condition in the concept of solvable structure is weakened for 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -structures by requiring their vector fields be just 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -symmetries. For 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -structures there is also an integration procedure, but the corresponding Pfaffian equations, although completely integrable, are not necessarily integrable by quadratures. The well-known result on the relationship between integrating factors and Lie point symmetries for first-order ODEs is generalized for 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -structures and involutive distributions of arbitrary corank by introducing symmetrizing factors. The role of these symmetrizing factors on the integrability by quadratures of the Pfaffian equations associated with the 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} -structure is also established. Some examples that show how these objects and results can be applied in practice are also presented.

2
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetries of distributions and symmetrizing factors

Throughout the paper we will work on a star shaped open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
By
\mathfrak{X}(U)
and
{\Omega}^{k}(U)

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(U)

k

will denote the exterior algebra of all the differential forms on

[
27

(respectively,

\mathcal{S}(B)
the submodule of
\mathfrak{X}(U)
(respectively,
\Omega^{1}(U)
) generated by
B
.
We say that a set of vector fields (or 1-forms) is independent on

if they are pointwise linearly independent at every point of

We will call a rank
r

distribution
on

to a submodule of
\mathfrak{X}(U)
of the form
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
, where

are
r
independent vector fields on

. In a similar way, a rank
s

Pfaffian system
will be a submodule of
\Omega^{1}(U)
generated by
s
independent 1-forms. It is well known
[

29
]
(see
[
26
]
and the references therein) that for a distribution
\mathcal{Z}
of rank

there exists a rank

Pfaffian system, which will be denoted by

whose elements
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}
are characterized by the conditions

Z_{i}\lrcorner\,\omega=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r.

(7)

Conversely, given a rank

{\Lambda}=\mathcal{S}(\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\})
there exists a rank

\Lambda^{\perp},
whose elements

are characterized by the conditions

Z\lrcorner\,\omega_{i}=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq n-r.

(8)

We refer to the distribution
\Lambda^{\perp}
as the kernel or the characteristic space of

Let us recall that a distribution
\mathcal{Z}
is said to be

if it is closed under the Lie bracket. The distribution

is involutive if and only if

is closed under exterior differentiation (see, for instance,

]

Two important notions concerning the search for integral manifolds of involutive distributions are the concepts of symmetry of a distribution and solvable structure

We first recall the concept of

Definition 2.1

Let

A vector field

[X,Z_{i}]=\lambda_{i}X+\sum_{k=1}^{r}c_{ik}Z_{k}.

(9)

Remark 2.1
.

1.

The standard notion of symmetry of a distribution
[
30

corresponds to the particular case of a

-symmetry where

2.

The original concept of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of a

th-oder ODE

corresponds to a

-symmetry of the distribution of rank 1 spanned by the vector field associated to the equation.

3.

When

is a

-symmetry of the involutive distribution

because (

Let us recall that for ODEs the product of a given

-symmetry by a non-vanishing smooth function is also a

-symmetry (see

-symmetries of distributions:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution of vector fields on an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},

r<n,
and consider a non-vanishing smooth function

Then:

1.

If
X
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
then

is also a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}.

2.

In particular, if

is a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
and

is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}.

Proof.

Since

is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
and

is a non-vanishing function on

then the set

By using (

\begin{array}[]{lll}[hX,Z_{i}]&=&-Z_{i}(h)X+h[X,Z_{i}]=\left(h\lambda_{i}-Z_{i}(h)\right)X+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}hc_{ik}Z_{k}\\
&=&\left(\lambda_{i}-\dfrac{Z_{i}(h)}{h}\right)hX+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}hc_{ik}Z_{k}.\end{array}

This proves that

is also a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}.

The second part is a direct consequence of the previous one.
∎

The following result follows immediately from (

Corollary 2.1
.

Let

-symmetry of the involutive distribution

2.1

Theorem 2.2

Proof.

Since

∎

Previous theorem guarantees the compatibility of system (

Conversely, if a non-vanishing smooth function

of
\mathcal{Z}
such that

We set

From

This proves that

Theorem 2.3

Let

-symmetry w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}.
A non-vanishing smooth function
f
is a symmetrizing factor for
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
if and only if
\mu=1/(fX\,\lrcorner\,\omega)
is an integrating factor for
\omega
.

Proof.

By Remark 3.4 (a) in
[
26
]
, the smooth function
h=(X\,\lrcorner\,\omega)^{-1}
is well defined on
U,
and by Proposition
2.1
,
hX
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}.
It is clear that
f
is a symmetrizing factor for
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
if and only
f/h
is a symmetrizing factor for
hX
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}.
Since the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry
hX
satisfies the hypothesis in Lemma
2.1
, it follows that
f/h
is a symmetrizing factor for
hX
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
if and only if
h/f=(fX\,\lrcorner\,\omega)^{-1}
is an integrating factor of
\omega,
which concludes the proof.
∎

2.2
Symmetrizing factors for distributions of arbitrary rank

The results presented in Section
2.1
for distributions of corank 1 show that the existence of a symmetrizing factor for a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry provides an integrating factor for the Pfaffian equation
\omega=0,
and hence ensures its integratibility by quadrature. Our next goal is to extend these results to study the integrability of Pfaffian systems with more than one equation.

With this aim, we first extend the result in Theorem
2.2
for distributions of arbitrary corank:

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
r<n,
and let
X
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry for
\mathcal{Z}
. There exists, at least locally, a
symmetrizing factor

f
for
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{Z}
is involutive, by Frobenius Theorem
[
28
]
there exists (locally) a set of functionally independent smooth functions
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{n-r}\}
such that

\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}(\{{dF_{1}},\ldots,{dF_{n-r}}\}).

Since by Definition
2.1
the set
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r},X\}
is pointwise linearly independent, there must exist some
i_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,n-r\}
such that, locally,
X(F_{i_{0}})={dF_{i_{0}}}(X)\neq 0
and therefore we can define the non-vanishing function

f=\frac{1}{X(F_{i_{0}})}.

Let us prove that
fX
is a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
. Since
X
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z},
by Definition
2.1
, for
i=1,\ldots,r,
we can write

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left[fX,Z_{i}\right]&=&\left[\dfrac{1}{X(F_{i_{0}})}X,Z_{i}\right]=-Z_{i}\left(\dfrac{1}{X(F_{i_{0}})}\right)X+\dfrac{1}{X(F_{i_{0}})}\left[X,Z_{i}\right]=\\
&=&\dfrac{Z_{i}(X(F_{i_{0}}))}{X(F_{i_{0}})^{2}}X+\dfrac{1}{X(F_{i_{0}})}\left(\lambda_{i}X+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}c_{ik}Z_{k}\right)=\\
&=&-\dfrac{[X,Z_{i}](F_{i_{0}})}{X(F_{i_{0}})^{2}}X+\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{X(F_{i_{0}})}X+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\dfrac{c_{ik}}{X(F_{i_{0}})}Z_{k}=\\
&=&\dfrac{\lambda_{i}X(F_{i_{0}})-[X,Z_{i}](F_{i_{0}})}{X(F_{i_{0}})^{2}}X+\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{r}\dfrac{c_{ik}}{X(F_{i_{0}})}Z_{k},\end{array}

(12)

where the functions
c_{ik}
are given in (
9
).

Since

[X,Z_{i}](F_{i_{0}})=\left(\lambda_{i}X+\sum_{k=1}^{r}c_{ik}Z_{k}\right)(F_{i_{0}})=\lambda_{i}X(F_{i_{0}}),

it follows from (
12
) that

\left[fX,Z_{i}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\dfrac{c_{ik}}{X(F_{i_{0}})}Z_{k},

which proves that
fX
is a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
.
∎

We conclude this section with the following statements concerning
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetries and symmetrizing factors, which will be useful for further reference:

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1)

If
X
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
then there exists a symmetry
Y
of
\mathcal{Z}
and a function
h
such that
X=hY
.

(2)

For a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry
X
of
\mathcal{Z},
the corresponding system (
11
) is compatible.

(3)

Let
X
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
with a given symmetrizing factor
f.
A function
\widetilde{f}
is a symmetrizing factor of
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
if and only if
\widetilde{f}=fg
for some first integral
g
of the distribution
\mathcal{Z}
.

Proof.

(1)

Let
f
be a non-vanishing symmetrizing factor of
X
w.r.t
\mathcal{Z}
provided by Theorem
2.4
. The result follows by taking
h=1/f.

(2)

It is direct a consequence of Corollary
2.1
and Theorem
2.4
.

(3)

If
f
and
\widetilde{f}
are two symmetrizing factors of
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
then by (
11
) the function
g=\widetilde{f}/{f}
satisfies

Z_{i}(g)=Z_{i}\left(\frac{\widetilde{f}}{f}\right)=\frac{Z_{i}(\widetilde{f})f-\widetilde{f}Z_{i}(f)}{f^{2}}=\frac{\widetilde{f}}{f}\left(\frac{Z_{i}(\widetilde{f})}{\widetilde{f}}-\frac{Z_{i}(f)}{f}\right)=0\text{, for }i=1,\ldots,r.

Conversely, if
f
is a symmetrizing factor of
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}
and a function
g
satisfies
Z_{i}(g)=0
for
1\leq i\leq r,
then by (
11
) we can write

\lambda_{i}-\frac{Z_{i}(gf)}{gf}=\lambda_{i}-\frac{gZ_{i}(f)}{gf}=\lambda_{i}-\frac{Z_{i}(f)}{f}=0\text{, for }i=1,\ldots,r,

which proves that
\widetilde{f}=gf
is a symmetrizing factor of
X
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}.

∎

3
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures and integrability of distributions

The existence of symmetrizing factors of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry
X,
w.r.t. an involutive distribution of arbitrary corank, has been proved in Theorem
2.4
. In particular, when the corank is 1, it has been shown that the knowledge of a symmetrizing factor for a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry
X
allows to integrate the distribution by quadrature (Lemma
2.1
and Theorem
2.3
). In the following section we aim to study the role of symmetrizing factors in the integrability by quadratures of a distribution of arbitrary corank
n-r>1.
In order to do that, we need not only a single
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
but an ordered sequence of
n-r
vector fields
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\}
that form a specific structure (introduced in
[
26
]
with the name of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure). For the sake of completeness, in this section we present the main notions and results on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures that are relevant for a better understanding of what follows.

3.1
The notion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure

A natural extension of the concept of solvable structure
[
10
,
30
]
can be attained when we consider
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetries instead of standard symmetries:

Definition 3.1

(Definition 3.3 in
[
26
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
be an ordered collection of vector fields on
U
and let us define the following sets:
\mathcal{X}_{0}:=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\}
and

\mathcal{X}_{k}:=\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r},X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}\},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq n-r.

We will say that
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z}
if, for
1\leq k\leq n-r
, the vector field
X_{k}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of the rank
r+k-1
distribution
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{k-1}).

Remark 3.1
.

1.

A solvable structure a particular case of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure in which every
X_{k}
is a symmetry of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{k-1}),
for
1\leq k\leq n-r

[
10
,
30
,
3
,
2
,
31
,
32
]
.

2.

As a direct consequence of Proposition
2.1
, any of the elements
X_{i}
of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure can be multiplied by a non-vanishing function
h
in such a way that
\langle X_{1},\ldots,hX_{i},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
is also a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z}.

From a dual point of view, the notion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure can be expressed in terms of 1-forms as follows:

Definition 3.2

(Definition 3.4 in
[
26
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
be an ordered collection of linearly independent 1-forms on
U
and define the sets

\Lambda_{k}:=\{\omega_{k+1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\}\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-r-1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Lambda_{n-r}=\emptyset.

(13)

We will say that
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
for
\mathcal{Z}
if the following two conditions hold:

(a)

\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{0})=\mathcal{Z}^{\circ}
.

(b)

\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{k})
is a differential ideal for
k=1,\ldots,n-r-1
.

In
[
26
, Proposition 3.3]
it was established a correspondence between the notions introduced in definitions
3.1
and
3.2
: given a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
, there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
such that

\mathcal{S}\left(\mathcal{X}_{k}\right)^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}\left(\Lambda_{k}\right),\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-r-1.

(14)

Conversely, given a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
there exists a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
such that

\mathcal{S}\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)^{{\perp}}=\mathcal{S}\left(\mathcal{X}_{k}\right),\quad\mbox{for}\quad 0\leq k\leq n-r-1.

(15)

If any of the two equivalent conditions (
14
) or (
15
) holds, we will say that
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
and
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
are
\mathcal{Z}
-related
.

3.2
Construction of
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures

In practice, a procedure to construct
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures can be carried out as follows. Let
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be a local system of coordinates on
U
and set the volume form
\boldsymbol{\Omega}=dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge dx_{n}.

Given a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
, we consider the 1-forms

\omega_{i}:={X_{n-r}\,\lrcorner\,\ldots\,\lrcorner\,\widehat{X_{i}}\,\lrcorner\,\ldots\,\lrcorner\,X_{1}\,\lrcorner\,Z_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\ldots\,\lrcorner\,Z_{1}\,\lrcorner\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq n-r,

(16)

where
\widehat{X_{i}}
indicates omission of
X_{i}.

Then it can be checked that
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms.

Remark 3.2
.

We observe that by the independence of the set
\mathcal{X}_{n-r},
the determinant

\Delta={X_{n-r}\,\lrcorner\,\ldots\,\lrcorner\,X_{1}\,\lrcorner\,Z_{r}\,\lrcorner\,\ldots\,\lrcorner\,Z_{1}\,\lrcorner\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}}=\left|\begin{array}[]{ccc}Z_{1}^{1}&\ldots&Z_{1}^{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
Z_{r}^{1}&\ldots&Z_{r}^{n}\\
X_{1}^{1}&\ldots&X_{1}^{n}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
X_{n-r}^{1}&\ldots&X_{n-r}^{n}\\
\end{array}\right|

(17)

does not vanish on
U.
Therefore the 1-forms

\sigma_{i}:=\dfrac{(-1)^{n-r-i}}{\Delta}\omega_{i}

(18)

where the
\omega_{i}
are defined in (
16
), are well defined on
U
and they define a new
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms satisfying the conditions

X_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma_{j}=\delta_{ij},\quad i,j=1,\ldots,n-r.

(19)

Any pair of
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
and
\langle\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n-r}\rangle
satisfying (
19
) will be called
dually
\mathcal{Z}
-related
.

3.3
Procedure of integration by
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures

The notion of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure introduced in the previous sections plays a fundamental role in the determination of the integral manifolds of the distribution
\mathcal{Z}.
The elements of a known
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms for
\mathcal{Z}
permit the stepwise construction of such integral manifolds through the integration of
n-r
completely integrable Pfaffian equations
[
26
, Theorem 3.5]
.

The main ideas of such constructive process can be organized through the following procedure:

1.

Given a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z}
, construct the
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
defined by (
16
).

For
1\leq i\leq n-r
and
1\leq j\leq n,
let
\Delta_{r+i,j}
denote the determinant formed by deleting the
(r+i)
th row and the
j
th column in (
17
). Then it can be checked that the expression of the 1-form
\omega_{i}
defined in (
16
) becomes

\omega_{i}=\omega_{i}^{1}dx_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{i}^{n}dx_{n},
where

\omega_{i}^{j}=(-1)^{n+j}\Delta_{r+i,j}
for
1\leq j\leq n.

2.

Solve the completely integrable Pfaffian equation
\omega_{n-r}\equiv 0,
obtaining a smooth function
I_{n-r}
on an open set
U_{n-r}\subseteq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n},
which defines the corresponding integral manifolds in the form
\Sigma_{(C_{n-r})}=\{x\in U_{n-r}:I_{n-r}(x)=C_{n-r}\},
where
C_{n-r}\in\mathbb{R}.

3.

Let
\iota_{n-r}:N_{n-r-1}\to\Sigma_{(C_{n-r})},
where
N_{n-r-1}
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n-1},
be a local parametrization of
\Sigma_{(C_{n-r})}.
For
1\leq i\leq n-r,
set
\widetilde{\omega}_{i}:=\iota_{n-r}^{*}(\omega_{i})
and denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}
the restriction of
{\mathcal{Z}}
to
N_{n-r-1},
i.e.,
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathcal{S}(\{{(\iota_{n-r}})_{*}(Z_{1}),\ldots,({\iota_{n-r}})_{*}(Z_{r})\}).

4.

Since
\langle\widetilde{\omega}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{\omega}_{n-r-1}\rangle

is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms w.r.t.
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}

we can move back to step
2
, and solve the completely integrable Pfaffian equation
\widetilde{\omega}_{n-r-1}\equiv 0.
Observe that this Pfaffian equation is defined on
N_{n-r-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1},
i.e., the dimension has been lowered by one, although the equation depends on the constant
C_{n-r}.

5.

This process can be continued and at any intermediate step we have a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms defined on an open set
N_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r+k}.
Such
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure has
k
elements that will be simply denoted by
\langle\widehat{\omega}_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\omega}_{k}\rangle,
in order to not complicate the notation.
Now the integration of the completely integrable Pfaffian equation

\widehat{\omega}_{k}\equiv 0

provides a smooth function

I_{k}
on some open set
U_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{r+k}
which defines the corresponding integral manifolds:

\Sigma_{(C_{k},C_{k+1},\ldots,C_{n-r})}=\{x\in U_{k}:I_{k}(x)=C_{k}\}.

As before, we consider a local parametrization
\iota_{k}:N_{k-1}\to\Sigma_{(C_{k},C_{k+1},\ldots,C_{n-r})},
defined on an open set
N_{k-1}
of
\mathbb{R}^{r+k-1},
and the procedure can be continued.

6.

After
n-r
steps, we get a local parametrization
\iota_{1}:N_{0}\to\Sigma_{(C_{1},C_{2},\ldots,C_{n-r})},
defined on some open set
N_{0}
of
\mathbb{R}^{r},
of the corresponding integral manifold of the Pfaffian equation
\widehat{\omega}_{1}=0.
Then the integral manifolds of
\mathcal{Z}
are given by

\iota_{n-r}\circ\ldots\circ\iota_{2}\circ\iota_{1}(N_{0}),

which depend on
(n-r)
constants
C_{1},\ldots,C_{n-r}
.

Remark 3.3
.

A relevant observation is that, in the previous procedure, only the function
I_{n-r}
is in fact a first integral of
\mathcal{Z}
but, for
1\leq k\leq n-r-1,
the functions
I_{k},
defined on a space of dimension
r+k,
are not.

3.4
Example

In this subsection we include an illustrative example to show how the previous procedure can be used to integrate an involutive distribution of two vectors field on
\mathbb{R}^{4}.

Example 3.1
.

Let us consider the distribution
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\})
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
generated by the vector fields

\begin{array}[]{l}Z_{1}=(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})\partial_{x_{2}}-x_{3}\partial_{x_{3}}+x_{4}\partial_{x_{4}},\quad Z_{2}=\partial_{x_{1}}-x_{3}^{2}\partial_{x_{3}}+(2x_{3}x_{4}-x_{2})\partial_{x_{4}}.\end{array}

(20)

The distribution
\mathcal{Z}
is involutive because
[Z_{1},Z_{2}]=-x_{3}Z_{1}
. In this example we firstly determine a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z},
which will be used to integrate completely the given distribution.

As a first element
X_{1}
of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z}

we can choose the vector field
X_{1}=x_{4}\partial_{x_{2}}+\partial_{x_{3}},
because the commutation relationships of
X_{1}
with
Z_{1}
and
Z_{2}
become

\begin{array}[]{l}[X_{1},Z_{1}]=-X_{1},\quad\left[X_{1},Z_{2}\right]=Z_{1}-x_{3}X_{1}.\end{array}

According to
[
26
, Remark 3.2 (d)]
, the second element
X_{2}
of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z}
in some open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{4}
can be any vector field such that the set
\{Z_{1},Z_{2},X_{1},X_{2}\}
is linearly independent on
U
. If we choose, for instance,
X_{2}=\partial_{x_{4}},
then

\Delta={X_{2}\,\lrcorner\,X_{1}\,\lrcorner\,Z_{2}\,\lrcorner\,Z_{1}\,\lrcorner\,\boldsymbol{\Omega}}=-x_{2},

(21)

and hence the ordered set
\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle
where

X_{1}=x_{4}\partial_{x_{2}}+\partial_{x_{3}},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{x_{4}},

(22)

is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure for
\mathcal{Z}
on the open set
U=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:x_{2}\neq 0\}.

We can integrate
\mathcal{Z}
by following the steps described at the beginning of this Section
3.3
:

1.

We use
\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle
to construct the
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\rangle
defined by (
16
). The coordinates of such 1-forms can be calculated from the corresponding determinant in (
17
) as it was explained in the first step of the integration procedure:

\omega_{1}=x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})dx_{1}+{x_{3}}dx_{2}+(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})dx_{3}.

(23)

Similarly,

\omega_{2}=-(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}dx_{1}+{x_{4}}dx_{2}-{x_{4}^{2}}dx_{3}-x_{2}dx_{4}.

(24)

2.

In the second step we have to solve the completely integrable Pfaffian equation

\omega_{2}\equiv 0.
The corresponding integral manifolds are implicitly defined in terms of a smooth function
I_{2}=I_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
satisfying the condition
dI_{2}\wedge\omega_{2}=0.
It can be checked that this condition leads to the following system of functionally independent determining equations for
I_{2}:

\begin{array}[]{lll}x_{2}(I_{2})_{x_{2}}+x_{4}(I_{2})_{x_{4}}&=&0,\\
x_{2}(I_{2})_{x_{3}}-x_{4}^{2}(I_{2})_{x_{4}}&=&0,\\
x_{2}(I_{2})_{x_{1}}-(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}(I_{2})_{x_{4}}&=&0.\\
\end{array}

(25)

The general solution of the first equation in (
25
) becomes

I_{2}=F_{0}\left(x_{1},x_{3},\dfrac{x_{4}}{x_{2}}\right).

(26)

From (
25
) we obtain that the smooth function
F_{0}=F_{0}(a,b,c)
must satisfy the system

\begin{array}[]{lll}(F_{0})_{b}-c^{2}(F_{0})_{c}&=&0,\\
(F_{0})_{a}-(1-bc)^{2}(F_{0})_{c}&=&0.\end{array}

(27)

From the first equation in (
27
) we get

F_{0}=F_{1}\left(a,\dfrac{1}{c}-b\right),

where
F_{1}=F_{1}(r,t)
is any solution to the equation

(F_{1})_{r}+t^{2}(F_{1})_{t}=0.

A particular solution becomes
F_{1}(r,t)=r+1/t,
which leads to the particular solution
F_{0}(a,b,c)=a+\dfrac{c}{1-bc}
of system (
27
). By (
26
), the corresponding particular solution for system (
25
) becomes:

I_{2}=x_{1}+\dfrac{x_{4}}{x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4}},

(28)

which is defined on the open set
U_{2}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in U:x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4}\neq 0\}.

The function given in (
28
) is a first integral for the involutive distribution
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{1})=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2},X_{1}\}).

In consequence, the integral manifolds of
\omega_{2}\equiv 0
can be locally defined as

\Sigma_{(C_{2})}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{4},x_{4})\in U_{2}:x_{1}+\dfrac{x_{4}}{x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4}}=C_{2}\},\quad C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

3.

A local parametrization of
\Sigma_{(C_{2})}
is given, for instance, by the function
\iota_{2}:N_{1}\to\Sigma_{(C_{2})}
defined by

\iota_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\left(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dfrac{x_{2}(C_{2}-x_{1})}{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}\right)

on the open set

N_{1}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})\neq 0\}.

The pullback by
\iota_{2}
of the 1-form
\omega_{1}
given in (
23
) becomes

\widetilde{\omega}_{1}:=\iota_{2}^{*}(\omega_{1})=\frac{x_{2}x_{3}^{2}}{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}dx_{1}+{x_{3}}dx_{2}+\frac{{x_{2}}}{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}dx_{3},

(29)

and defines a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\rangle
for the restriction of
\mathcal{Z}
to
N_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}.

4.

The Pfaffian equation
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\equiv 0
is completely integrable. From the condition
dI_{1}\wedge\widetilde{\omega}_{1}=0
we get the following system of determining equations for
I_{1}=I_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3};C_{2}):

\begin{array}[]{lll}(I_{1})_{x_{1}}+x_{3}(I_{1})_{x_{3}}&=&0,\\
x_{2}(I_{1})_{x_{2}}-x_{3}(1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1}))(I_{1})_{x_{3}}&=&0.\\
\end{array}

(30)

From the first equation in (
30
) we get

I_{1}=G_{0}\left(x_{2},\dfrac{1}{x_{3}}-x_{1}\right),

where, by the second equation in (
30
), the function
G_{0}=G_{0}(a,b)
must satisfy the equation

aG_{a}+(b+C_{2})G_{b}=0.

A particular solution for this last equation becomes
G(a,b)=\dfrac{C_{2}+b}{a},
giving rise to the following particular solution for system (
30
):

I_{1}=\dfrac{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}{x_{2}x_{3}},

(31)

defined on the open set

U_{1}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in N_{1}:x_{2}x_{3}\neq 0\}.
In consequence, the integral manifolds of
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\equiv 0
become

\Sigma_{(C_{1},C_{2})}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in U_{1}:\dfrac{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}{x_{2}x_{3}}=C_{1}\},\quad C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

5.

For
\Sigma_{(C_{1},C_{2})}
we can choose the local parametrization
\iota_{1}:N_{0}\to\Sigma_{(C_{1},C_{2})}
given by the function

\iota_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\frac{1}{x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2}}\right)

defined on the open set
N_{0}=\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2}\neq 0\}.

6.

In order to obtain the 2-dimensional integral manifolds of
\mathcal{Z}
we first calculate
\iota_{2}\circ\iota_{1}.
For
(x_{1},x_{2})\in N_{0}
and
C_{1}\neq 0,
we have

\iota_{2}\circ\iota_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=\left(x_{1},x_{2},\frac{1}{x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2}},\dfrac{(C_{2}-x_{1})(x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2})}{C_{1}}\right).

Therefore the integral manifolds for the distribution
\mathcal{Z}
are locally parametrized by
\iota_{2}\circ\iota_{1}
on
N_{0}.
Alternatively, such integral manifolds can be implicitly described by the equations:

x_{3}=\frac{1}{x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2}},\quad x_{4}=\dfrac{(C_{2}-x_{1})(x_{1}+C_{1}x_{2}-C_{2})}{C_{1}}.

(32)

Remark 3.4
.

A possible variant of the previous procedure is the following.
We first observe that only
\omega_{2}
is necessary to carry out the step 2. The 1-form
\omega_{1}
calculated in (
23
) has been used in the step 3 to determine the 1-form (
29
) whose Pfaffian equation is integrated in the fourth step. An alternative way to construct such Pfaffian equation proceeds along the next lines:

1.

Step 1: Calculate only
\omega_{2},
as in (
24
), from a known
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry
X_{1}
of the distribution
\mathcal{Z}
(the knowledge of the last element
X_{2}
of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure is not necessary).

2.

Step 2: Integrate the Paffian equation
\omega_{2}\equiv 0,
as in the previous procedure.

3.

Step 3: Restrict the distribution
\mathcal{Z}
to
N_{2}
through
({\iota_{2}})_{*}:

\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}=\mathcal{S}(\{{(\iota_{2}})_{*}(Z_{1}),({\iota_{2}})_{*}(Z_{2})\}).
Then the Pfaffian equation
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\equiv 0
arises from any
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms
\langle\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\rangle
for
\widetilde{\mathcal{Z}}.

4
Relative symmetrizing factors and integrating factors

When for an involutive distribution
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1}\ldots,Z_{r}\})
we know not only a single
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry, but a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle,
then Theorem
2.4
demonstrates, for
1\leq i\leq n-r,
the existence of a symmetrizing factor for
X_{i}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i-1})
(or a
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i-1})
-symmetryzing factor for short). In what follows we investigate the role of these symmetrizing factors in the integrability of the Pfaffian equations arising from the application of the procedure explained in Section
3.3
.

First we extend the result given in Lemma
2.1
to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures of distributions of arbitrary corank. With this aim we first need the following definition:

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
be a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure of 1-forms for an involutive distribution
\mathcal{Z}
. Given
{i_{0}}\in\{1,\ldots,n-r\}
we will say that a non-vanishing smooth function
\mu
on
U
is a
integrating factor of
\omega_{i_{0}}
with respect to
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
(or a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
-integrating factor for short) if
\mu\omega_{i_{0}}
is closed module
\Lambda_{i_{0}}
, i.e., if
d(\mu\omega_{i_{0}})\in\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{i_{0}}).

Remark 4.1
.

1.

Since
\Lambda_{n-r}=\emptyset,
the condition
d(\mu\omega_{n-r})\in\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{n-r})
must be understood as
d(\mu\omega_{n-r})=0,
i.e., in this case
\mu
is in fact an integrating factor for
\omega_{n-r}.

2.

Although in the previous definition the term
integrating factor
has been used, a relevant observation is that for
i_{0}<n-r
, the 1-form
\omega_{i_{0}}
is not, in general, Frobenius integrable, and therefore an integrating factor for
\omega_{i_{0}}
does not exist. However, if
\mu
is an integrating factor of
\omega_{i_{0}}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}}),
then the restriction of
d(\mu\omega_{i_{0}})
to the integral manifolds of the involutive distribution
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}})
must vanish, because
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}})^{\circ}=\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}}).
In consequence, the knowledge of the function
\mu
yields the integration by quadrature of the corresponding Pfaffian equation
\widehat{\omega}_{i_{0}}\equiv 0
stated in the fifth step of the procedure described in Section
3.3
. On the other hand, for
i_{0}\neq n-r,
an integrating factor of
\widehat{\omega}_{i_{0}}
is a smooth function defined on a space of dimension
r+i_{0}<n,
whereas a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
-integrating factor must be a smooth function defined on some open set of
\mathbb{R}^{n}.

3.

Given a pair of
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
and
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z},
an integrating factor of
\omega_{i_{0}}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}}),
for
1\leq i_{0}\leq n-r-1,
will be called a
relative integrating factor
, in order to emphasize that such factor
does not
convert the corresponding 1-form
\omega_{i_{0}}
into an exact differential form.

4.

Given a pair of
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
and
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
for
\mathcal{Z},
a symmetrizing factor for
X_{i_{0}}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{{i_{0}}-1}),
for
2\leq i_{0}\leq n-r,
will be called a
relative symmetrizing factor
, in order to emphasize that such factor
does not
convert the corresponding vector field
X_{i_{0}}
into a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
. Only when
i_{0}=1,
a symmetrizing factor for
X_{1}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{0})=\mathcal{Z}
converts
X_{1}
into a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}.

The following result generalizes, for distributions of arbitrary corank, the relationship between integrating factors and symmetrizing factors established in Lemma
2.1
for distributions of corank 1:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-r}\rangle
and
\langle\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-r}\rangle
is a pair of
\mathcal{Z}
-related
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures. Suppose that there exists
{i_{0}}\in\{1,\ldots,n-r\}
such that
X_{i_{0}}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=1
. A non-vanishing smooth function
f
is a symmetrizing factor for
X_{i_{0}}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})
if and only if the function
\mu=1/f
is a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
-integrating factor of
\omega_{i_{0}}
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.1
, the vector field
X_{i_{0}}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetry of
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})
and therefore we can write, for
1\leq i\leq r
and
1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1:

[X_{i_{0}},Z_{i}]=\lambda_{i}X_{i_{0}}+A_{i},\qquad[X_{i_{0}},X_{j}]=\gamma_{j}X_{i_{0}}+B_{j}

(33)

for certain smooth functions
\lambda_{i},\gamma_{j}
and vector fields
A_{i},B_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1}).

According to Corollary
2.1
a non-vanishing smooth function
f_{i_{0}}
is a symmetrizing factor for
X_{i_{0}}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})
if and only if
f_{i_{0}}
satisfies the system

\lambda_{i}-\dfrac{Z_{i}(f)}{f}=0,\quad\gamma_{j}-\dfrac{X_{j}(f)}{f}=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1.

(34)

On the other hand, by Definition
3.2
,
\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{i_{0}-1})
is a differential ideal
and hence there exist 1-forms
\sigma,\sigma_{k}
such that

d\omega_{i_{0}}=\sigma\wedge\omega_{i_{0}}+\sum_{k=i_{0}+1}^{n-r}\sigma_{k}\wedge\omega_{k}.

(35)

Therefore for any non-vanishing smooth function
\mu
we have

d(\mu\omega_{i_{0}})=d\mu\wedge\omega_{i_{0}}+\mu d\omega_{i_{0}}=(d\mu+\mu\sigma)\wedge\omega_{i_{0}}+\displaystyle\sum_{k=i_{0}+1}^{n-r}\mu\sigma_{k}\wedge\omega_{k}.

(36)

It follows that
the function
\mu
satisfies
d(\mu\omega_{i_{0}})\in\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
if and only if
d\mu+\mu\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}-1})=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})^{\circ},
i.e., if and only if

Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,(d\mu+\mu\sigma)=X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,(d\mu+\mu\sigma)=0,\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1.

These conditions can be written in the form

\dfrac{Z_{i}(\mu)}{\mu}+Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma=0,\quad\dfrac{X_{j}(\mu)}{\mu}+X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1.

(37)

In order to link the solutions of systems (
34
) and (
37
) we need to calculate
Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma
and
X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma.

First observe that since
\omega_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}-1})=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})^{\circ}
and
A_{i},B_{j}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})
equations (
33
) imply that

[X_{i_{0}},Z_{i}]\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=\lambda_{i},\qquad[X_{i_{0}},X_{j}]\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=\gamma_{j}.

(38)

Since
X_{i_{0}}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=1
and, for
1\leq i\leq r,
we have that
Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=0,
by (
38
) we can write

d\omega_{i_{0}}(Z_{i},X_{i_{0}})=Z_{i}(X_{i_{0}}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}})-X_{i_{0}}(Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}})-[Z_{i},X_{i_{0}}]\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=\lambda_{i}.

(39)

Similarly, for
1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1,

d\omega_{i_{0}}(X_{j},X_{i_{0}})=X_{j}(X_{i_{0}}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}})-X_{i_{0}}(X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}})-[X_{j},X_{i_{0}}]\,\lrcorner\,\omega_{i_{0}}=\gamma_{j}.

(40)

On the other hand, since
Z_{i},X_{j},X_{i_{0}}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}})=\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})^{\perp},
it follows from (
35
) that

\begin{array}[]{lll}d\omega_{i_{0}}(Z_{i},X_{i_{0}})&=&\sigma\wedge\omega_{i_{0}}(Z_{i},X_{i_{0}})=Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma,\\
d\omega_{i_{0}}(X_{j},X_{i_{0}})&=&\sigma\wedge\omega_{i_{0}}(X_{j},X_{i_{0}})=X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma.\end{array}

(41)

Equations (
39
), (
40
) and (
41
) show that

Z_{i}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma=\lambda_{i},\quad X_{j}\,\lrcorner\,\sigma=\gamma_{j},\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1.

(42)

In consequence, the conditions given in (
37
) can be written in the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{Z_{i}(\mu)}{\mu}+\lambda_{i}=0,\quad\dfrac{X_{j}(\mu)}{\mu}+\gamma_{j}=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\,1\leq j\leq i_{0}-1.\end{array}

(43)

It is clear that
f
satisfies (
34
) if and only if
\mu=1/f
satisfies system (
43
), which concludes the proof.
∎

.

: in this case

and

.

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
be an involutive distribution on an open set

r<n.
Assume that

and

-related

X_{i_{0}}

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i_{0}-1})

is a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i_{0}})
-integrating factor for
\omega_{i_{0}}
.

Remark 4.3
.

In the search of integral manifolds of an involutive distribution by using a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure, a very convenient case appears when some of the
X_{i}
in the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure is in fact a symmetry of the distribution
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{i-1}).
In this case, according to Theorem
4.1
, the function

is a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{i})
-integrating factor. The corresponding Pfaffian equation obtained by the restriction of
\omega_{i}
can be solved by a quadrature (see Remark

). In particular, the integrability by quadratures in terms of solvable structures
[

,
30
]
follows as a corollary of Theorem
4.1
.

5
Examples

In this section we present two examples to show the relationships between relative integrating factors and relative symmetrizing factors for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures that have been theoretically obtained in the previous section.

Example 5.1
.

In this example we consider again the distribution that has been integrated in Example
3.1
by using the procedure based on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures described in Section
3.3
. Once the Pfaffian equations
\omega_{2}\equiv 0
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}\equiv 0,
respectively defined by (
24
) and (
29
), have been integrated, we can obtain, as a consequence, the relative integrating factors from the corresponding first integrals (
28
) and (
31
). Then we will use Theorem
4.1
to construct the relative symmetrizing factors. Finally, we will show how these relative symmetrizing factors could be used to construct a solvable structure for the distribution (
20
).

In Example
3.1
we have obtained, for the distribution
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\})
spanned by the vector fields given in (
20
), a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure
\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle
formed by the vector fields

X_{1}=x_{4}\partial_{x_{2}}+\partial_{x_{3}},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{x_{4}}.

(44)

1.

\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{2})
-integrating factor and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{1})
-symmetrizing factor:

In the second step of the integration procedure associated to the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure, we have calculated the function (
28
):

I_{2}=x_{1}+\dfrac{x_{4}}{x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4}},

(45)

which is a first integral for the involutive distribution
\mathcal{X}_{1}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2},X_{1}\}).
In particular,
I_{2}
is a true first integral for
\mathcal{Z}.
Recall that such first integral has been obtained by integrating the Pfaffian equation
\omega_{2}\equiv 0,
and therefore, there exists a function
\mu_{2}=\mu_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
such that

\mu_{2}\omega_{2}=dI_{2}.

It can be checked that this function
\mu_{2},
which is in fact an integrating factor for the Frobenius integrable 1-form
\omega_{2},
becomes

\mu_{2}=-(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{-2}.

(46)

Therefore, according to Theorem
4.1
, the function

f_{2}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{2}X_{2}\lrcorner\,\omega_{2}}=\dfrac{1}{\mu_{2}\Delta}=\dfrac{(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}}{x_{2}}

(47)

is a symmetrizing factor for
X_{2}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{1})=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2},X_{1}\}).
In other words, the vector field

Y_{2}:=f_{2}X_{2}=\dfrac{(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}}{x_{2}}\partial_{x_{4}}

(48)

is a symmetry for the involutive distribution
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{1})=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2},X_{1}\}).

Observe, however, that
Y_{2}

is not a symmetry
for
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}),
because

[Y_{2},Z_{1}]=-\left(\dfrac{x_{3}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})}{x_{2}}\right)^{2}\left(Z_{1}+x_{3}X_{1}\right)\notin\mathcal{Z}.

2.

\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{1})
-integrating factor and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{0})
-symmetrizing factor:

In the fourth step of the integration procedure performed in Example
3.1
, we have obtained the function (
31
)

I_{1}=\dfrac{1+x_{3}(C_{2}-x_{1})}{x_{2}x_{3}},

by integrating the 1-form
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}
given in (
29
), which is Frobenius integrable on
N_{1}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}.
As before, a corresponding integrating factor
\widetilde{\mu}_{1}=\widetilde{\mu}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3};C_{2})
for
\widetilde{\omega}_{1}
arises from the condition
dI_{1}=\widetilde{\mu}_{1}\widetilde{\omega}_{1},
giving rise to the function

\widetilde{\mu}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3};C_{2})=-\dfrac{1+(C_{2}-x_{1})x_{3}}{x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}}.

(49)

In order to obtain a
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{1})
-integrating factor for the 1-form
\omega_{1}
given in (
23
), we consider the function
\mu_{1}={\mu}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
obtained from (
49
) by replacing
C_{2}
by the right-hand side of (
45
), i.e.,

\mu_{1}=\widetilde{\mu}_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3};I_{2}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}))=-\dfrac{1}{x_{2}x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})}.

(50)

It can be checked that the 1-form

\mu_{1}\omega_{1}=-\dfrac{1}{x_{2}}dx_{1}-\dfrac{1}{x_{2}x_{3}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})}dx_{2}-\dfrac{1}{x_{2}x_{3}^{2}}dx_{3}

satisfies

d(\mu_{1}\omega_{1})=\left(-\dfrac{1}{x_{2}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}}dx_{2}\right)\wedge\omega_{2}\in\mathcal{I}(\Lambda_{1}),

which shows that, although (
50
)
is not an integrating factor
for
\omega_{1},
the 1-form
\mu_{1}\omega_{1}
is closed module
\Lambda_{1},
i.e., the function
\mu_{1}
in (

) is an integrating factor for
\omega_{1}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\Lambda_{1})=\mathcal{S}(\{\omega_{2}\}).

Now we use the relative integrating factor (
50
) to construct, by using Theorem
4.1
, a symmetrizing factor for
X_{1}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X}_{0})=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\})=\mathcal{Z}.
In this case the function

(51)

is a symmetrizing factor for
X_{1}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{Z}.
In other words, the vector field

Y_{1}:=f_{1}X_{1}=-x_{4}x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})\partial_{x_{2}}-x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})\partial_{x_{3}}

is a symmetry for
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},Z_{2}\}).
In fact, it can be checked that

[Y_{1},Z_{1}]=0,\quad[Y_{1},Z_{2}]=f_{1}Z_{1}.

3.

A solvable structure for
\mathcal{Z}:

As a consequence of the previous calculations, we observe that the vector fields
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2},
given in (

provide a solvable structure

for
\mathcal{Z},
because the following commutations relationships hold:

\left[Y_{1},Z_{1}\right]=0,\quad\left[Y_{1},Z_{2}\right]=-x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})Z_{1}

and

\begin{array}[]{l}\left[Y_{2},Z_{1}\right]=-\dfrac{x_{3}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{2}}{x_{2}^{2}}Z_{1}-\dfrac{(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})}{x_{2}^{2}}Y_{1},\\

\left[Y_{2},Y_{1}\right]=-\dfrac{x_{3}^{2}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})^{3}}{x_{2}^{2}}Z_{1}-\dfrac{x_{3}^{2}x_{4}(x_{2}-x_{3}x_{4})}{x_{2}^{2}}Y_{1}.\end{array}

It should be emphasized that the solvable structure

\mathcal{Z},
because this has already been done in Example

by using the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure integration method. Such solvable structure has been constructed to explain better the following considerations that emerge from a comparative analysis of the integration procedures based on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures and solvable structures:

•

The vector fields (
53

44
) that define the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure and the corresponding relative symmetrizing factors (
47
) and (

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure, performed in Example

, has been completed.

•

The Pfaffian equations which appear in the integration procedure associated to a solvable structure are integrable by quadratures. However, a solvable structure is more difficult to calculate than a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure, because their elements must satisfy stronger conditions than in the case of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures. In this example it is clear that the infinitesimals of the vector fields (

) that form a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure are
simpler than the infinitesimals of the vector fields (
53
) that define a solvable structure.

In the next example we calculate and use a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

As in the previous example, relative symmetrizing factors will also be used to construct a solvable structure. \mathcal{C}^{\infty}

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}

6
Final remarks

The integrability of systems of ODEs can be formulated as the geometrical problem of integrating the distribution generated by the vector field associated to the system.
More generally, the integrability of a general involutive distribution
\mathcal{Z}=\mathcal{S}(\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r}\})
is guaranteed by Frobenius theorem. However this theorem does not provide a method to construct the corresponding integral manifolds. One of the most fruitful methods of integration is provided by solvable structures
[
10
,
2
,
30
,
32
,
33
,
35
,
34
]
.
The first element
X_{1}
in a solvable structure must be a symmetry of the distribution
\mathcal{Z}
. The second element
X_{2}
must be a symmetry of the distribution generated by
\{Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{r},X_{1}\}
, and so on. Here
X_{2}
is not necessarily a symmetry of
\mathcal{Z}
. The interest on solvable structures is that they allow the integration by quadratures of the distribution. The main drawback of the method is that, in general, it is very difficult to find the corresponding vector fields in practice. This has motivated several studies that can be useful for that aim. For instance, for ODEs, prior knowledge of some symmetry generators can help to build a solvable structure, as observed by P. Morando and D. Catalano-Ferraioli in
[
36
,
37
]
. However, many times the equations under study lack Lie point symmetries, or the generalized symmetries necessary for the construction of the solvable structure are so intricate that they are very difficult to determine in practice.

In the recent paper
[
26
]
, the concept of solvable structure has been generalized to
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures, by requiring the vector fields
X_{1},X_{2},\ldots,X_{n-r}
be
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-symmetries of the previous distributions.
These new structures are quite more easy to be found than solvable structures, because weaker conditions are required for their elements. A procedure for the stepwise integration of
\mathcal{Z}
via
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures is included in the paper.
Such procedure, as in the case of solvable structures, is based on successive integrations of Pfaffian equations. For
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures, such Pfaffian equations are also completely integrable, although not necessarily integrable by quadratures, as in the case of solvable structures. However, they can still be integrated, even without prior knowledge of an integrating factor, as illustrated by the examples presented in this paper (see also the examples given in
[
26
]
). Furthermore, since
each Pfaffian equation is defined in a space with one less dimension than in the previous stage, its integration (with or without integrating factors) becomes more and more easy.

A remarkable feature of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures procedure is that once the Pfaffian equation at any intermediate step, defined on a
j
th-dimensional space, has been integrated, then it is possible to construct a corresponding integrating factor, which depends on
n-j
constants of integration. As a consequence, a relative integrating factor (depending on the
n
variables) can be constructed by replacing such constants by the corresponding
n-j
first integrals calculated in the previous stages. This procedure considerably simplifies the calculation of such relative integrating factors.

The well-known relationship between symmetries and integrating factors for first-order ODEs has been generalized in this paper for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structures and involutive distributions of arbitrary corank in Theorem
4.1
. This result establishes
a kind of reciprocal relationship between the relative symmetrizing factors of the elements of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure and the relative integrating factors.

The examples presented in this paper clearly illustrate that the relative integrating factors, and hence the relative symmetrizing factors, can be very intricate. Therefore, if only
solvable structures were used to integrate the distribution then such factors would extremely complicate the expressions of the infinitesimals of the vector fields (see, for instance, the expressions (
69
) in Example
5.2
). Since this is not necessary, because the integration procedure can be carried out by using the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure, this makes clear the advantage of the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-structure method.

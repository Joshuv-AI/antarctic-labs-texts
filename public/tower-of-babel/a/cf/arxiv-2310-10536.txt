On explicit embeddings of Q {\mdmathbb Q} into finitely presented groups
V. H. Mikaelian

Abstract
. Explicit embeddings of the group Q {\mdmathbb Q} into a finitely presented group 𝒬 \mathcal{Q} and into a 2 2 -generator finitely presented group T 𝒬 T_{\!\mathcal{Q}} are suggested. The constructed embeddings reflect questions mentioned by Johnson, Bridson, de la Harpe in the literature in late 1990s about possibility of such embeddings for Q {\mdmathbb Q} . Technique used here is based on the methods with integer-valued sequences sets used by Higman, and with specific free constructions of groups, including free product with amalgamation, HNN-extension, an auxiliary technical structure of ✻ -construction, etc.

20F05, 20E06, 20E07.

1.
Introduction

Our objective is to construct explicit embeddings of the additive group of rational numbers
{\mdmathbb Q}
into certain finitely presented groups.
This subject belongs to the context of the celebrated Higman Embedding Theorem
[
9
]
stating that a finitely generated group can be embedded into a finitely presented group if and only if it is recursive. Under a
recursive
group we understand a group
with at most countable (effectively enumerable) generators and with a recursively enumerable set of defining relations.
A newer term for recursive is
computable
, but we are going to stick to the traditional notation here. We namely prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an explicit embedding of the additive group of rational numbers
{\mdmathbb Q}
into a finitely presented group explicitly given by its generators and defining relations.

We are going to build two instances of the finitely presented group promised in Theorem
1.1
. The first is the group
\mathcal{Q}
given by
98
generators and a large number of defining relations listed in point
9.1
.
The explicit embedding
\varphi:{\mdmathbb Q}\to\mathcal{Q}
is given in (
8.15
) as the composition of
\alpha
from (
3.2
) and of
\beta
from (
8.14
).

And the second instance is the group
T_{\!\mathcal{Q}}
with only
2
generators and again a large number of defining relations, see point
9.2
and the presentation (
9.2
).
The explicit embedding
\psi:{\mdmathbb Q}\to T_{\!\mathcal{Q}}
is the composition of the above
\varphi
and of
\gamma
from (
9.1
). In fact,
T_{\!\mathcal{Q}}
contains the first group
\mathcal{Q}
also.

1.1.
The problem on explicit embedding of
{\mdmathbb Q}

As the group

2.
Preliminary notation, constructions and references

2.1.
Integer-valued functions
f

Following
[
9
]
denote by
\mathcal{E}
the set of all functions
f:{\mdmathbb Z}\to{\mdmathbb Z}
with finite supports.
When for a certain
m=1,2,\ldots
we have

f(i)=0
for
all

i<0
and
i\geq m
, then it is comfortable to record
f
as a sequence
f=(j_{0},\ldots,j_{m-1})
assuming
f(i)=j_{i}
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1

f=(0,5,9,8)
sending the integers

to
0,5,9,8
(and all other integers to

).
Denote the set of all such functions written as sequences for a fixed

{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
.
Clearly,

may not be uniquely defined for a given

​, and where necessary we may append extra zeros at the end of a sequence, e.g., the previous function can well be recorded as
f=(0,5,9,8,0,0)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{6}
(the last two zeros change nothing in the way
f
acts on
{\mdmathbb Z}
). Also, the constant zero function can be written as
f=(0)
or, say, as
f=(0,0,0)
where necessary.
See more on such functions in point 2.2 in
[
22
]
.

For any
f\in\mathcal{E}
and
k\in{\mdmathbb Z}
define the function
f_{k}^{+}
​ as follows:

f_{k}^{+}(i)=f(i)
for all
i\!\neq\!k
, and

f_{k}^{+}(k)=f(k)\!+\!1
.
When
f\in{\mathcal{E}}_{m}
(with
m
given by the context), we shorten
f_{m-1}^{+}=f^{+}
​. Say, for the above
f\!=\!(0,5,9,8)\in{\mathcal{E}}_{4}
we have
f_{1}^{+}\!\!=(0,6,9,8)
and

f^{+}\!\!=(0,5,9,9)
, i.e., we just add
1
to the last coordinate of

to get

​.

2.3.

Higman calls a subgroup

with a finitely generated subgroup

. If we wish to stress the correlation of

For detailed information on benign subgroups we refer to Sections 3, 4 in

]

]

Remark 2.2
.

From definition of benign subgroups it is very easy to see that arbitrary finitely generated subgroup

in any finitely presented group

2.5.
The
✻
-construction

Although the constructions in
[

Let

be an arbitrary system of groups such that
K_{i}\cap K_{j}=M

Choosing in each

(2.1)

\Big(\cdots\Big(\big((K_{1}*_{L_{1}}t_{1})*_{M}(K_{2}*_{L_{2}}t_{2})\big)*_{M}(K_{3}*_{L_{3}}t_{3})\Big)\cdots\Big)*_{M}(K_{r}*_{L_{r}}t_{r})\,.

To avoid this very bulky notation, let us for the sake of briefness denote the above by:

(2.2)

\textstyle\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}.

Denote
G\cap\,L_{i}=A_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,r
. If for each
i
we are limited to
K_{i}=G
,
L_{i}=A_{i}
,

, then

\textrm{\footnotesize✻}_{i=1}^{r}(G,A_{i},t_{i})_{G}
is noting but the usual HNN-extension
G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}\!(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
.

All the facts in the rest of the current point are proved in
[
23
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
G\leq M\leq K_{1},\ldots,K_{r}
are groups mentioned above, then in
{\textrm{\footnotesize✻}}_{i=1}^{r}(K_{i},L_{i},t_{i})_{M}

the following equality holds:

\langle G,t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}\rangle=G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}\!(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}).

For any group
G
and its subgroup
A

the well known equality
G\cap G^{t}\!=A
in the HNN-extension
G*_{A}t
can be generalized to the following:

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}
be any subgroups in a group
G
with the intersection

I=\bigcap_{\,i=1}^{\,r}\,A_{i}
.
Then in
G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
we have:

(2.3)

\textstyle G\cap G^{t_{1}\cdots\,t_{r}}=I.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}
be any subgroups in a group
G
with the join

J=\big\langle\bigcup_{\,i=1}^{\,r}\,A_{i}\big\rangle
.
Then in
G*_{A_{1},\ldots,\,A_{r}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{r})
we have:

(2.4)

\textstyle G\cap\big\langle\bigcup_{\,i=1}^{\,r}\,G^{t_{i}}\big\rangle=J.

Now the
✻
-construction of (
2.2
) together with
Lemma
2.3
,
Lemma
2.4
and
Lemma
2.5

provide a corollary which will be extensively used below.

Corollary 2.6
.

If the subgroups

are benign in a finitely generated group

, then:

(1)

their intersection

(2)

Moreover, if the finitely presented groups

The following corollary lets us discover free products inside

Let

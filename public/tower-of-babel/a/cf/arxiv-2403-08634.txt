Bispectrum non-Gaussian Covariance in Redshift Space
Pierluigi Monaco

Abstract
We provide an analytical description of the galaxy bispectrum covariance and the power spectrum-bispectrum cross-covariance in redshift space that captures the dominant non-Gaussian contributions. The Gaussian prediction for the variance of the halo bispectrum monopole significantly underestimates numerical estimates particularly for squeezed triangles, that is bispectrum triangular configurations where one side is much smaller than the other two, whereas the effect is relatively less important when considering the quadrupole. We propose an expression for the missing non-Gaussian contribution valid in the squeezed limit that requires an accurate modeling of the bispectrum alone. We validate our model against the numerical covariance estimated from a large suite of mock catalogs and find that it accurately predicts the variance as well as the dominant off-diagonal terms. We also present an expression for the cross-covariance between power spectrum and bispectrum multipoles and likewise find it to provide a good description of the numerical results.

2
Power spectrum and bispectrum covariance in redshift space

In this section, we review the theoretical modeling of the covariance of power spectrum and bispectrum multipoles in redshift space along with their cross-covariance. We will not consider any super-sample contribution to these quantities, expected in actual galaxy surveys due to finite-volume effects. While our models will correspond to contributions also present in real-survey observations, they only constitute a complete description of the power spectrum and bispectrum covariance properties as measured in simulation boxes with periodic boundary conditions.

For this reason, we adopt the following definition for a generic
N
-point correlation function in Fourier space,
P_{N}
, accounting explicitly for the discrete nature of the wavenumbers
\mathbf{k}
,

\displaystyle\langle\delta(\mathbf{k}_{1})\cdots\delta(\mathbf{k}_{N})\rangle\equiv\frac{\delta_{K}(\mathbf{k}_{1\dots N})}{k_{f}^{3}}P_{N}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{k}_{N})\,.

(2.1)

Here we adopt the notation
\mathbf{k}_{1\dots N}=\mathbf{k}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{k}_{N}
and assume a finite cubic volume leading to the wavenumbers values
\mathbf{k}=\mathbf{n}\,k_{f}
, multiples of the fundamental frequency
k_{f}\equiv 2\pi/L
, with
L
being the side of the box. We also introduce the the adimensional Kronecker symbol
\delta_{K}(\mathbf{k})=1
for
\mathbf{k}=0
, vanishing otherwise. We work in the plane-parallel approximation throughout.

2.1
Power spectrum estimator and covariance

The covariance of the power spectrum has already been studied, in its Gaussian and non-Gaussian components, in several works both for measurements in simulation boxes with periodic boundary conditions
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
30
,
26
,
27
,
28
]
and in the more general case of a realistic galaxy survey including finite volume effects (see
[
25
]
and references therein).

We assume the standard estimator for the power spectrum multipoles given by (see e.g.
[
50
]
)

\hat{P}_{\ell}(k)=(2\ell+1)\frac{k_{f}^{3}}{N_{k}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in k}\delta_{s}(\mathbf{q})\,\delta_{s}(-\mathbf{q})\,\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}})\,,

(2.2)

where
\delta_{s}
is the redshift-space galaxy (or halo) number density in Fourier space,
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
a Legendre polynomial with
\mu_{\mathbf{q}}=\hat{q}\cdot\hat{n}
being the cosine of the angle between
\mathbf{q}
and the line of sight
\hat{n}
, while

N_{k}=\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in k}\simeq\frac{1}{k_{f}^{3}}\int_{k}\mathrm{d}^{3}q=\frac{4\pi k^{2}\Delta k}{k_{f}^{3}}\,

(2.3)

is the number of modes inside the
k
-bin of radial size
\Delta k
, defined as
k-\Delta k/2<|\mathbf{q}|\leq k+\Delta k/2
. The result in the above equation holds in the continuum limit approximation, valid as long as many grid modes are present in each shell.

The covariance associated with the power spectrum multiple estimator is defined as

\mathbf{C}^{P}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},k_{j})\equiv\langle[\hat{P}_{\ell_{1}}(k_{i})-\langle\hat{P}_{\ell_{1}}(k_{i})\rangle][\hat{P}_{\ell_{2}}(k_{j})-\langle\hat{P}_{\ell_{2}}(k_{j})\rangle]\rangle\,,

(2.4)

and, as is well known, it comprises a Gaussian contribution
\mathbf{C}^{P\,(PP)}
, dominant at large scales (see e.g.
[
30
]
) and only depending on the power spectrum, plus an additional non-Gaussian component
\mathbf{C}^{P\,(T)}
sourced by a non-vanishing trispectrum
[
51
]
.

An explicit expression for the Gaussian component is given by
[
30
]

\mathbf{C}^{P\,(PP)}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},k_{j})=\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{k_{i}}N_{k_{j}}}\delta^{K}_{ij}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in k_{i}}P_{{\rm tot}}^{2}(\mathbf{q})\,\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}})\left[\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}})+\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}})\right]\,,

(2.5)

where
P_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{k})
is the anisotropic halo power spectrum
including
the shot noise contribution equal, in the Poisson limit, to
1/[(2\pi)^{3}\bar{n}]
, with
\bar{n}
the galaxy number density. Expanding the power spectrum in multipoles as
P_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q})=\sum_{\ell}P_{{\rm tot},\ell}(q)\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu)
, this last equation can be written in the continuum limit as

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}^{P\,(PP)}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},k_{j})=

\displaystyle\,\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{2N_{k_{i}}}\delta^{K}_{ij}\sum_{\ell_{3}=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell_{4}=0}^{\infty}P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{3}}(k_{i})P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{4}}(k_{i})

\displaystyle\times\int^{1}_{-1}\,d\mu\,\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu)\left[\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu)+\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu)\right]\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu)\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu)\,.

(2.6)

where we used the thin-shell approximation assuming
P(q)\sim P(k)
. Since higher-order multipoles are suppressed, it is typically a good approximation to include only the first few terms in this sum.

In this work, we will ignore the non-Gaussian contribution to the power spectrum covariance as it is beyond its scope. Furthermore, while in some cases relevant, it is subdominant w.r.t. to the Gaussian component at large scales, (see, e.g.
[
25
]
), unlike what happens in the case of the bispectrum.

2.2
Bispectrum estimator and covariance

A theoretical expression for the full bispectrum covariance excluding finite-volume effects is given in
[
7
]
with specific expressions for the redshift-space multipoles presented in
[
42
,
27
,
6

]
. In particular,
[
34
]

The expansion in spherical harmonics of the anisotropic bispectrum adopts the convention of
[
49
,
50
]
:

B_{s}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3},\hat{n})\equiv\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\,B_{\ell}^{m}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,Y_{\ell}^{m}(\mu_{\mathbf{k}_{1}},\xi_{\mathbf{k}_{12}})\,,

(2.7)

where
\mu_{\mathbf{k}_{1}}=\hat{k}_{1}\cdot\hat{n}
and

\xi_{\mathbf{k}_{12}}
is the azimuthal angle of
\mathbf{k}_{2}
around
\mathbf{k}_{1}
with respect to the plane formed by
\mathbf{k}_{1}
and
\hat{n}
as shown in Figure
1
. Furthermore we define

B_{\ell}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\equiv\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}}B_{\ell}^{0}(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\,,

(2.8)

and we will refer to these quantities as bispectrum multipoles in the rest of the paper. We will ignore the case
m\neq 0
thereby restricting ourselves to averages over the azimuthal angle
\xi
(see
[
55
]
for an estimate of the additional information in higher-
m
multipoles). We will also assume, with no (further) loss of generality, the vector
\mathbf{k}_{1}
to be the largest of the triplet.

In the plane-parallel approximation, the bispectrum multipoles estimator is then given by

\hat{B}_{\ell}(t)=(2\ell+1)\frac{k_{f}^{3}}{N_{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{1}\in k_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{2}\in k_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{3}\in k_{3}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\delta_{s}(\mathbf{q}_{1})\delta_{s}(\mathbf{q}_{2})\delta_{s}(\mathbf{q}_{3})\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\,,

(2.9)

where
t
represents the wavenumbers triplet
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
. We will refer to it as the “triangle bin”, with the “fundamental” (i.e. located on a
k_{f}
-spaced grid) wavenumbers
\mathbf{q}_{i}
satisfying the relations
k_{i}-\Delta k/2\leq|\mathbf{q}_{i}|<k_{i}+\Delta k/2
for all
i=1
, 2 and 3 and ensuring the closed triangle condition as
\mathbf{q}_{123}=0
. The normalization factor corresponds to the number of fundamental triplets
(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{q}_{3})
falling in the triangle bin
(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
, given by

N_{t}=\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{1}\in k_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{2}\in k_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{3}\in k_{3}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\,.

(2.10)

The bispectrum multipoles covariance is defined as

\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(t_{i},t_{j})\equiv\langle[\hat{B}_{\ell_{1}}(t_{i})-\langle\hat{B}_{\ell_{1}}(t_{i})\rangle][\hat{B}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})-\langle\hat{B}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})\rangle]\rangle\,.

(2.11)

It can be split into a Gaussian and three, distinct non-Gaussian contributions
[
7
,
27
]

\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(t_{i},t_{j})=\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PPP)}(t_{i},t_{j})+\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(BB)}(t_{i},t_{j})+\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PT)}(t_{i},t_{j})+\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(P_{6})}(t_{i},t_{j})\,,

(2.12)

where
\mathbf{C}^{B\,(PPP)}
refers to the Gaussian covariance,
\mathbf{C}^{B\,(BB)}
and
\mathbf{C}^{B\,(PT)}
refer to the disconnected non-Gaussian contributions containing two bispectra and a trispectrum respectively, and
\mathbf{C}^{B\,(P_{6})}
refers to the connected six-point function contribution.

Gaussian contribution

The Gaussian term in the bispectrum covariance can be written as
[
34
,
6
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PPP)}(t_{i},t_{j})=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{t_{i}}N_{t_{j}}k_{f}^{3}}\delta^{K}_{ij}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{1}\in k_{1,i}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{2}\in k_{2,i}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{3}\in k_{3,i}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})P_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{q}_{1})P_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{q}_{2})P_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{q}_{3})

\displaystyle\times\left[\,(1+\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\;\;+(\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}+\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\;\;+(\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{3,i}}+\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{3}})\,\right]\,,

(2.13)

for
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=0
the terms in the squared parenthesis reduce to a factor equal to
6
, 2 and 1 for equilateral, isosceles and scalene triangles, respectively. Expanding the anisotropic power spectrum in multipoles and using the thin-shell approximation, that is evaluating functions of the momenta at the bin centers, the Gaussian covariance becomes

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PPP)}(t_{i},t_{j})=

\displaystyle\;\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{t_{i}}k_{f}^{3}}\delta^{K}_{ij}\sum_{\ell_{3},\ell_{4},\ell_{5}}P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{3}}(k_{1,i})P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{4}}(k_{2,i})P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{5}}(k_{3,i})

\displaystyle\times R_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4},\ell_{5}}(k_{1,i},k_{2,i},k_{3,i})\,,

(2.14)

where

\displaystyle R_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4},\ell_{5}}(t)=

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{N_{t}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{1}\in k_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{2}\in k_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{3}\in k_{3}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{5}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{3}})

\displaystyle\times\left[\,(1+\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\;\;+(\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}+\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}})\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\;\;+(\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{3,i}}+\delta^{K}_{k_{1,i},k_{2,i}}\delta^{K}_{k_{2,i},k_{3,i}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{3}})\,\right]\,.

(2.15)

As long as there are many grid modes in each shell, this expression can be simplified in the continuum limit by replacing the sum with integrals, yielding an approximated analytical formula for the Gaussian covariance. The procedure and formula are detailed in equation (
A.7
) and Appendix
A
. For this procedure to work the triangles need to be closed of flattened, i.e. to satisfy
k_{1}>k_{2}+k_{3}
or
k_{1}=k_{2}+k_{3}
respectively. Note that in the latter case, the continuum assumption may not be justified as flattened shells contain less modes than their closed counterpart, especially if one or more sides are small. As per the case of the power spectrum, we disregard the contribution of high-
\ell
multipoles. In fact, we only consider
\ell_{3,4,5}=0,2
and the results in §
3.2
will show that no higher mixing is needed to accurately model the covariance of both the monopole and quadrupole.

Non-Gaussian contributions

We begin by considering the non-Gaussian contribution depending on the product of two bispectra, that is

\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(BB)}(t_{i},t_{j})=\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{t_{i}}\,N_{t_{j}}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t_{i}}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t_{j}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{p}_{123})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{3}+\mathbf{p}_{12})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{p}_{3}+\mathbf{q}_{12})\\
\times B_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{3})B_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{q}_{3})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})+8\ \text{perm.}\,,

(2.16)

where we used the short-hand notation

\sum_{\mathbf{q}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t}\equiv\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{1}\in k_{1}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{2}\in k_{2}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}_{3}\in k_{3}}\,,

(2.17)

for the sum over all values for the vectors
\mathbf{q}_{i}
in the triangle bin
t=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
.
B_{\rm tot}(\mathbf{k}_{1},\mathbf{k}_{2},\mathbf{k}_{3})
is the anisotropic bispectrum including the shot noise contribution.
It should be noted that the first term on the r.h.s. is non-vanishing only if
k_{3,i}=k_{3,j}
while the other eight permutations correspond to all other possible equalities between one element of the triplet
t_{i}
and one of the triplet
t_{j}
.

Using the harmonic decomposition of the anisotropic bispectra, as well as the thin-shell approximation we can write

\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(BB)}(t_{i},t_{j})=(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)\sum_{\ell_{3},m_{3}}\sum_{\ell_{4},m_{4}}\,B_{{{\rm tot}},\ell_{3}}^{m_{3}}(k_{1,i},k_{2,i},k_{3,j})\,B_{{\rm tot},\ell_{4}}^{m_{4}}(k_{1,j},k_{2,j},k_{3,i})\\
\times S^{(3,3)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4};m_{3},m_{4}}(t_{i},t_{j})+8\,\text{perm.}\,,

(2.18)

where the mode-counting factor
S^{(a,b)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4};m_{3},m_{4}}
is defined as

S^{(a,b)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4};m_{3},m_{4}}(t_{i},t_{j})=\frac{1}{N_{t_{i}}N_{t_{j}}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t_{i}}\sum_{\mathbf{p}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t_{j}}\,\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\,\delta_{K}(\mathbf{p}_{123})\,\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{a}-\mathbf{p}_{b})\\
\times\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})Y^{m_{3}}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}},\xi_{\mathbf{q}_{12}})Y^{m_{4}}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}},\xi_{\mathbf{p}_{12}})\,.

(2.19)

As pointed out in
[
37
,
38
]
, the leading contribution to the non-Gaussian covariance comes from squeezed triangles that share the smallest momentum (long mode). In our prediction, we focus on this contribution and ignore all other permutations in equation (
2.18
). We ignore the contribution coming from
m\neq 0
multipoles as it is suppressed both by the amplitude of the multipoles of the bispectrum and by the angular integrals. The final expression for the covariance is

\mathbf{C}^{B\,(BB)}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(t_{i},t_{j})=(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+2)\delta^{K}_{k_{3,i},k_{3,j}}\Sigma^{33}_{ij}\,\sum_{\ell_{3},\ell_{4}}B_{{\rm tot},\ell_{3}}(t_{i})B_{{\rm tot},\ell_{4}}(t_{j})\,I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}(t_{i},t_{j})\,,

(2.20)

where

I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}(t_{i},t_{j})=4\pi\frac{S^{(3,3)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\ell_{4};0,0}(t_{i},t_{j})}{\Sigma_{ij}^{33}\sqrt{(2\ell_{3}+1)(2\ell_{4}+1)}}

(2.21)

and the quantity
\Sigma_{ij}^{33}\equiv S^{(3,3)}_{0,0,0,0;0,0}(t_{i},t_{j})
was first defined in
[
38
]
. For closed triangles, it is equal in the continuum limit to

\Sigma^{33}_{ij}=\frac{16\pi^{3}}{k_{f}^{9}N_{t_{i}}N_{t_{j}}}k_{1,i}k_{2,i}k_{1,j}k_{2,j}\Delta k^{5}\,,

(2.22)

and expressions for non-closed triangles can be found in Appendix A of
[
38
]
. The expression for
I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}
in the continuum limit is presented in equation (
A.10
) of Appendix
A
. In the evaluation of equation (
2.20
) for
\ell_{1,2}=0,2
we only consider contributions up to the quadrupole, that is we keep only
\ell_{3,4}=0,2
.

Regarding the
\mathbf{C}^{PT}
contribution to the covariance from equation (
2.12
), it is

\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PT)}(t_{i},t_{j})=\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{t_{i}}N_{t_{j}}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}s\in\mathbf{k}^{i}s}\sum_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}s\in\mathbf{k}^{j}s}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{p}_{123})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{3}+\mathbf{p}_{12})\delta_{K}(\mathbf{p}_{3}+\mathbf{q}_{12})\\
P_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{3})T_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\hat{q}_{1}\cdot\hat{n})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\hat{p}_{1}\cdot\hat{n})+8\ \text{perm.}\,.

(2.23)

Again, we keep only the leading term, which is the permutation with the power spectrum evaluated on the long mode (i.e. the one written explicitly in this expression). For that permutation,
it is argued in
[
38
]
that the products
P_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{3})\,T_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2})
and
B_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{q}_{3})\,B_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{p}_{1},\mathbf{p}_{2},\mathbf{q}_{3})
have the same limit for squeezed triangles (taking
q_{3}=|\mathbf{q}_{1}+\mathbf{q}_{2}|
much smaller than all other momenta).
The same argument holds in redshift space (see for example
[
56
,
57
]
). Since this contribution is expected to be important only for squeezed triangles, we account for it by writing
\mathbf{C}^{B\,(PT)}\simeq\mathbf{C}^{B\,(BB)}
. We disregard the contribution coming from the connected six-point function, as argued in
[
38
]
. Thus, our non-Gaussian covariance is approximated by

\mathbf{C}^{B}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\simeq\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(PPP)}+2\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(BB)}

(2.24)

where
\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{B\,(BB)}
is given by equation (
2.20
).

2.3
Power spectrum-bispectrum cross-covariance

Moving to the cross-covariance among power spectrum and bispectrum, this is defined as

\mathbf{C}^{PB}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})\equiv\langle[\hat{P}_{\ell_{1}}(k_{i})-\langle\hat{P}_{\ell_{1}}(k_{i})\rangle][\hat{B}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})-\langle\hat{B}_{\ell_{2}}(t_{j})\rangle]\rangle\,,

(2.25)

and it comprises two terms

\mathbf{C}^{PB}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})=\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(PB)}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})+\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(P_{5})}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})\,,

(2.26)

where
\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(PB)}
refers to the disconnected contribution, and
\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(P_{5})}
refers to the fully-connected 5-point function contribution. Ignoring the latter, as it is subleading at large scales (see
[
38
]
and our numerical results later), the relevant term is

\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(PB)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})=\frac{(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)}{N_{k_{i}}N_{t_{j}}}\,2\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in k_{i}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}^{\prime}s\in t_{j}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})P_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q})B_{{\rm tot}}(\mathbf{q}_{1},\mathbf{q}_{2},\mathbf{q}_{3})\\
\times\left[\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}_{1})+\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}_{2})+\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}+\mathbf{q}_{3})\right]\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\,.

(2.27)

Similarly to what was done earlier, we decompose
P_{\rm tot}
in Legendre polynomials and
B_{\rm tot}
in spherical harmonics, disregarding
m\neq 0
. We apply the thin-shell approximation to get:

\mathbf{C}^{PB\,(PB)}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}(k_{i},t_{j})=(2\ell_{1}+1)(2\ell_{2}+1)\sum_{\ell_{3}}\sum_{\ell_{4}}P_{{\rm tot},\ell_{3}}(k_{i})B_{{\rm tot},\ell_{4}}(t_{j})\,\tilde{I}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}(k_{i},t_{j})\,,

(2.28)

where the quantity
\tilde{I}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}
is defined as

\tilde{I}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}(k_{i},t_{j})=\frac{2}{N_{k_{i}}N_{t_{j}}}\sum_{\mathbf{q}\mathrm{{}^{\prime}s}\in t_{j}}\delta_{K}(\mathbf{q}_{123})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\\
\times\left[\delta^{K}_{k_{i},k_{1,j}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})+\delta^{K}_{k_{i},k_{2,j}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{2}})+\delta^{K}_{k_{i},k_{3,j}}\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{3}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{3}})\right]\,.

(2.29)

Its expression in the continuum limit can be found in Appendix
A
, see equation (
A
).

To sum up, our approximate model for the cross-covariance of power spectrum and bispectrum is

\mathbf{C}^{PB}_{\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}\simeq\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{PB\,(PB)}\,

(2.30)

with
\mathbf{C}_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}}^{PB\,(PB)}
is given by the expression above.

3.4
Tests on the inverse covariance

Approximate theoretical models for the covariance of complex data vectors such as the combination of power spectrum and bispectrum multipoles are not guaranteed to provide invertible covariance matrices. Indeed, imposing such (necessary) condition in general terms is not a simple problem. In practice we could expect that some level of consistency among the different components can help obtaining a well defined matrix.

In this respect, we notice that using the expression for
\mathbf{C}^{PB}(k_{i},t_{j})
in equation (
2.28
) and (
2.29
) returns a matrix with negative eigenvalues. This is likely related to the fact that these formulae consider all possible permutations when sharing the mode of the power spectrum with the bispectrum triangle, i.e. they account for
k_{i}=k_{1,j},k_{2,j},k_{3,j}
, whereas in the covariance of the bispectrum,
\mathbf{C}^{BB}(t_{i},t_{j})
, we only account for the leading contribution corresponding to the triangles sharing the long mode. We find that discarding the keeping only the term with
k_{i}=k_{3,j}
and discard the other two in the cross-covariance lead to an invertible matrix. Thus, for the results in this section we employ this simplified version of the cross-covariance model.

In order to assess the quality of our analytical covariance matrix and its inverse, we perform two tests, following
[
38
]
. For the first, we compute, for each realization

, the quantity

\chi^{2}_{\mathrm{th},i}=(\mathbf{d}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{d}})\,\mathbf{C}_{\mathrm{th}}^{-1}\,(\mathbf{d}_{i}-\bar{\mathbf{d}})^{T}\,,

(3.5)

where
\mathbf{d}_{i}
is the data vector containing the measured power spectrum and bispectrum monopole and quadrupole. If the model is a good description of the covariance, this quantity should follow a
\chi^{2}
distribution with the number of degrees of freedom equal to the dimension of
\mathbf{d}_{i}

In figure

We see that the non-Gaussian model consistently improves upon the Gaussian approximation. The effect is more pronounced for the monopole alone since, as discussed above, non-Gaussian contributions are less relevant for the quadrupole covariance. Furthermore, the last panel confirms that our model for non-Gaussian terms grows more accurate when looking at squeezed configurations.
Notice also that the improvement is less dramatic than in
[

because we are considering less squeezed triangles in our mock measurements.

4
Conclusions

In this work we provide a test for the approximate model for the bispectrum covariance proposed in
[
38
]
, here extended to redshift-space bispectrum multipoles. This model accounts for the leading non-Gaussian contributions to the covariance expected for squeezed triangular configurations. For these triangles, the non-Gaussian term depending on the product of the power spectrum and trispectrum can be approximated by the product of two bispectra. We consider as well a model for the power spectrum-bispectrum cross-covariance, including all elements where the power spectrum bin coincides with a side of the bispectrum configuration. In this treatment, the main non-Gaussian contributions to the full covariance are written as a function of power spectrum and bispectrum multipoles alone, simplifying their evaluation. Our expressions, obtained in the thin-shell approximation, include as well accurate estimates of the mode-counting factors defined as sums over the Fourier-space grid as integrals in the continuum limit.

We verify the accuracy of our model by comparing it with the numerical covariance obtained from a large suite of 10000 mock halo catalogs. We find that the bispectrum monopole covariance shows significant non-Gaussian contributions for squeezed configurations, of the order of
\sim 10\%
, and that our model correctly recovers these features. We also see that non-Gaussian terms are relatively less important for the covariance of the quadrupole and for the cross covariance between monopole and quadrupole. The same considerations apply for the cross-covariance among power spectrum and bispectrum. The most relevant result, is that our model, despite the approximations, captures all the leading non-Gaussian effects responsible for the off-diagonal structure of the power spectrum and bispectrum covariance matrix. As such it provides a simple analytical expression that can be improved by fitting a few nuisance parameters to a small set of measurements of the data-vector when these are not sufficient for a robust purely numerical estimate of the covariance (see for instance
[
63
]
).

There are two main applications of our results. In the first place, the model can replace the simple Gaussian approximation in the analysis of power spectrum and bispectrum measurements from numerical simulations with periodic boundary conditions. It provide an approximate but essentially complete description of the statistical errors involved, including the correlation between the two statistics. In the second place it constitutes one of the ingredients in a more general analytical model for the power spectrum and bispectrum covariance as measured in galaxy redshift surveys, where additional contributions from finite-volume effects should be taken into account. In this case it is possible that even our treatment of squeezed triangular configurations will be affected, due to the characteristic long mode, by window effects. We leave these investigations for a future work.

\displaystyle\times\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})\,.

(A.8)

We will proceed by solving the Dirac delta argument for
\xi_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}
. There are two roots in the
[0,2\pi)
interval,
\xi_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{\pm}
:

\cos\xi_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{\pm}=\frac{(k_{1,i}+k_{2,i}\,\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{12}})\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}-(k_{1,j}+k_{2,j}\,\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{12}})\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}-k_{2,i}\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}^{2}}\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{12}}^{2}}\cos\xi_{\mathbf{q}_{12}}}{k_{2,j}\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}^{2}}\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{2}}}\equiv\zeta_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}\,.

(A.9)

After integrating out the delta we get

I_{\ell_{1}\ell_{2}\ell_{3}\ell_{4}}(t_{i},t_{j})=\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}}\mathrm{d}\xi_{\mathbf{q}_{12}}\int_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}\frac{k_{3,j}\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{1}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{2}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{3}}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}})\mathcal{L}_{\ell_{4}}(\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})}{k_{2,j}\sqrt{1-\nu_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{\star\,2}}\,\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}^{2}}\,\sqrt{1-\zeta_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{2}(\mu_{\mathbf{q}_{1}},\xi_{\mathbf{q}_{12}},\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}})}}\,,

(A.10)

where
\mathcal{D}
is the set of values of
\mu_{\mathbf{p}_{1}}
where the integral is well-defined, i.e. where
\zeta_{\mathbf{p}_{12}}^{2}<1
. A Python function to solve equation (
A.10
) numerically can be found in the module

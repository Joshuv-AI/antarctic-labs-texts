Global solutions of the one-dimensional compressible Euler equations with nonlocal interactions via the inviscid limit
Jos E ´ \acute{\mathrm{E}} A. Carrillo, Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Difan Yuan, Ewelina Zatorska

Abstract
. We are concerned with the global existence of finite-energy entropy solutions of the one-dimensional compressible Euler equations with (possibly) damping, alignment forces, and nonlocal interactions: Newtonian repulsion and quadratic confinement. Both the polytropic gas law and the general gas law are analyzed. This is achieved by constructing a sequence of solutions of the one-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes-type equations with density-dependent viscosity under the stress-free boundary condition and then taking the vanishing viscosity limit. The main difficulties in this paper arise from the appearance of the nonlocal terms. In particular, some uniform higher moment estimates for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations on expanding intervals with stress-free boundary conditions are obtained by careful design of the approximate initial data.

1.
Introduction

Hydrodynamic models of collective behavior provide a comprehensive framework for characterizing
the behavior of vast assemblies of interacting individuals.
In most of the interesting cases, these models can only be formally derived from the particle-type
systems capturing precise interactions between the individuals.
In cases where full mathematical rigor is attainable,
it is usually established through
the mean-field limit techniques or the BBGKY hierarchies;
see
[
6
,
27
,
4
]

and the classical references therein on the matter.
In this paper, we are interested in a specific
one-dimensional (1-D) example of such models, which captures
local repulsion, and nonlocal attraction and repulsion forces, as well as nonlocal alignment.
This model is described by the compressible Euler equations (CEEs) with the corresponding interaction forces:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+m_{x}=0,\\[2.84526pt]
m_{t}+(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+P(\rho))_{x}=\lambda m-\rho\partial_{x}W\ast\rho+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi(x-y)\big(\rho(x)m(y)-\rho(y)m(x)\big)\,{\rm d}y,\end{cases}

(1.1)

for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
where
\rho\geq 0
is the density,
m
is the momentum,
P
is the pressure.

The nonlocal attraction-repulsion interaction forces are described by potential
W
of the form:

W(x)=-|x|+\frac{x^{2}}{2}.

The long-range attraction between the individuals is captured by the quadratic confinement part
\frac{1}{2}x^{2},
while the short-range repulsion is described by the Newtonian part of the potential
-|x|.

The consensus in velocities (the alignment) among individuals is described by the weight
\varpi
that satisfies:

\varpi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\varpi(x)=\varpi(-x),\quad\varpi\geq 0.

Finally, the linear term
\lambda m
in our system stands for damping, if the coefficient
\lambda
is non-positive.

The equation of state

We consider a general pressure law

P(\rho)
satisfying hypotheses (
\mathcal{H}
) formulated below:

(
\mathcal{H}.1
)

The pressure function
P(\rho)\in C^{1}([0,\infty))\cap C^{4}((0,\infty))
satisfies the hyperbolic and genuinely nonlinear conditions:

P^{\prime}(\rho)>0,\quad 2P^{\prime}(\rho)+\rho P^{\prime\prime}(\rho)>0\qquad\,\,\text{for }\rho>0.

(1.2)

(
\mathcal{H}.2
)

There exist constants
\rho_{*}>0
,
\gamma_{1}\in(1,3)
, and
\kappa_{1}>0
such that

P(\rho)=\kappa_{1}\rho^{\gamma_{1}}\big(1+\mathcal{P}_{1}(\rho)\big)\qquad\text{ for }\rho\in[0,\rho_{*}),

(1.3)

for some function
\mathcal{P}_{1}(\rho)\in C^{4}((0,\infty))

satisfying

|\mathcal{P}_{1}^{(j)}(\rho)|\leq C_{*}\rho^{\gamma_{1}-1-j}\qquad\text{ for }\rho\in(0,\rho_{*}),\ \text{ and }j=0,\cdots,4,

where
C_{*}>0
depends only on
\rho_{*}
.

(
\mathcal{H}.3
)

There exist constants
\rho^{*}>\rho_{*}>0
,
\gamma_{2}\in(1,\gamma_{1}]
, and
\kappa_{2}>0
, such that

P(\rho)=\kappa_{2}\rho^{\gamma_{2}}\big(1+\mathcal{P}_{2}(\rho)\big)\qquad\text{ for }\rho\in[\rho^{*},\infty),

(1.4)

for some function
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\rho)\in C^{4}((0,\infty))
satisfying

|\mathcal{P}_{2}^{(j)}(\rho)|\leq C^{*}\rho^{-\epsilon-j}\qquad\text{ for }\rho\in[\rho^{*},\infty),\ \epsilon>0,\ \text{ and }j=0,\cdots,4,

where
C^{*}>0
depends only on
\rho^{*}
.

Without loss of generality, by scaling,
\kappa_{1}
may be chosen to be equal to
\kappa_{1}=\frac{(\gamma_{1}-1)^{2}}{4\gamma_{1}}
.

A special example of such an equation of state is the pressure-density relation for the
polytropic gases
:

P(\rho)=\kappa\rho^{\gamma}\qquad\text{ for $\gamma\in(1,\infty)$},

(1.5)

where
\gamma
is the adiabatic exponent. In this case, we can choose
\kappa=\frac{(\gamma-1)^{2}}{4\gamma}
.

Initial data

We consider the Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) with the initial data:

(\rho,m)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x)

(1.6)

such that
\rho_{0}\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
and
\frac{m_{0}}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

with finite initial total mass and initial second moment:

M:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x<\infty,\qquad M_{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x<\infty.

(1.7)

We further assume that the initial data are of finite energy:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big|\frac{m_{0}}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}}}\Big|^{2}+\rho_{0}e(\rho_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}W\ast\rho_{0}\Big)(x)\,{\rm d}x<\infty,

(1.8)

where
e(\rho)
is the internal energy:

e(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\rho}\frac{P(\sigma)}{\sigma^{2}}\,{\rm d}\sigma.

In particular, for the polytropic case (
1.5
),

e(\rho):=\frac{\kappa}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma-1}.

Main objectives of the paper

The goal of the present paper is to establish the global-in-time existence of finite-energy entropy solutions
of system (
1.1
) without restriction on the size of initial data. Our method of choice is
the vanishing viscosity limit for the strong solutions of the compressible
Navier-Stokes equations (CNSEs). More precisely, we construct the solution by means of a sequence of approximate problems:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}_{t}+(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})_{x}=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\displaystyle(\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})_{t}+\big(\rho^{\varepsilon}(u^{\varepsilon})^{2}+P(\rho^{\varepsilon})\big)_{x}=\varepsilon(\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}_{x})_{x}+\lambda\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}-\rho^{\varepsilon}\partial_{x}W\ast\rho^{\varepsilon}\\[2.84526pt]
\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\displaystyle+\rho^{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi(x-y)(u^{\varepsilon}(y)-u^{\varepsilon}(x))\rho^{\varepsilon}(y)\,{\rm d}y,\end{cases}

(1.9)

on bounded intervals:

\Omega_{T}^{\varepsilon}=\big\{(t,x)\ :\,b^{-}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq r\leq b^{+}_{\varepsilon}(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\big\},

(1.10)

with the free boundaries
b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)
, expanding to infinity as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.

In the above system, the viscosity coefficient is equal to

\varepsilon\mu(\rho)=\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha},

\alpha\geq 0
,
and the parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
denotes the inverse of the Reynolds number.
A derivation of CNSEs (
1.9
) from the Boltzmann equations (without the nonlocal term)
may be found in
Liu-Xin-Yang
[
36
]
, in which
the viscosity is not constant but depends on the temperature. This dependence
can be translated into the dependence of the viscosity on the density for the isentropic flow.

The state of the art

There is a huge literature devoted to the study of the existence of solutions to CEEs
either via the analysis of the vanishing artificial viscosity limit (see
[
1
,
23
,
29
]
for instance),
or by constructing the finite difference scheme.
The global existence of solutions with large initial data in
L^{\infty}

was first established by DiPerna
[
26
]

for
\gamma=1+\frac{2}{2n+1}
with
n\geq 2
integer
by using the artificial viscosity method for the density equation.
For the general interval
1<\gamma\leq\frac{5}{3},

the global existence problem was solved by Ding-Chen-Luo
[
24
,
25
]
and Chen
[
11
]
by approximation via the Lax-Friedrichs scheme. The case of adiabatic exponent
\gamma>\frac{5}{3}

was covered by Lions-Perthame-Tadmor
[
35
]
and
Lions-Perthame-Souganidis
[
34
]
via the vanishing viscosity method.
We also refer to
[
33
]
for blow-up results on CEEs and
to Chen-LeFloch
[
14
,
15
]

for relevant results on CEEs with general pressure law.

Concerning approximation by the CNSEs, the
L^{\infty}
estimates for the approximate solutions
are not expected when the initial data only are of finite energy.
Therefore, we work with the finite-energy framework, which was first considered
by LeFloch-Westdickenberg
[
32
]
for
1<\gamma<\frac{5}{3}
, and was late
developed
by Chen-Perepelitsa
[
16
]

to the whole physical range of adiabatic exponents
\gamma>1.

In particular, they established
a compensated compactness framework (based on the earlier work by Tartar
[
42
]
and Murat
[
38
]
)
and proved the vanishing viscosity limit of the solutions of the 1-D CNSEs
to the corresponding finite-energy solutions
of CEEs for large initial data for all
\gamma>1
in
[
16
]
.
In this result, the initial density at the far-field is allowed to be positive, and the viscosity
is independent of the density,
i.e.,

\alpha=0.

This framework has been subsequently extended by Chen-Schrecker
[
18
]
and
Chen-Wang
[
19
]
to study spherically symmetric Euler equations.
More recently, Chen-He-Wang-Yuan
[
12
]
established the global existence of finite-energy solutions
of the multi-dimensional Euler-Poisson equations for both compressible gaseous stars and plasmas
with large spherically symmetric initial data.
Adapting the approach developed in
[
12
]
, He-Wang
[
28
]
proved the vanishing viscosity limit
for the 1-D CNSEs.
For CEEs with general pressure law, Shrecker-Schulz
[
40
,
41
]

proved the vanishing viscosity limit for the 1-D CNSEs under asymptotically isothermal assumptions.
More recently, Chen-Huang-Li-Wang-Wang
[
13
]
proved the vanishing physical viscosity
limit for CNSPEs with general pressure law with large spherically symmetric initial data,
in which an
L^{p}
compensated compactness framework for general pressure law was also established.
Finally, the existence of solutions for a general class of density-degenarate viscous models has been
studied in
[
21
,
22
]
.

The main challenges tackled in this paper in comparison to the previous results
are due to the presence of the nonlocal terms. The existence of global-in-time solutions to the nonlocal Euler system (
1.1
) on the whole line has never been proven before. In contrast, it is known that the classical solutions of (
1.1
) without pressure and alignment,
i.e.,

P(\rho)=\varpi=0
, may blow up in finite time
[
5
]
and that they can be approximated by a degenerate vanishing viscosity method
[
8
]
. More recently, Carrillo-Galtung
[
9
]
showed that for such pressureless Euler systems in 1-D, the Lagrangian and entropy solutions are equivalent, which was then used to explore the long-time-asymptotics of the solutions. Moreover, Chaudhuri
et al.

[
10
]
studied the two-velocity reformulation of system (
1.9
) and derived an energy-type inequality, in the spirit of the Bresch-Desjardins estimate
[
2
,
3
]
. It was then used to construct the weak solutions and to study their long-time behaviour leading to the same density profiles as in
[
5
]
or
[
9
]
.
The weak solutions to CNSEs with nonlocal attraction-repulsion forces in three dimensions were recently constructed in
[
37
]
, see also
[
7
]
for the constant viscosity case, where the long-time behavior of the solutions was considered.
Finally, global-in-time well-posedness theory for the Euler equations with Riesz interactions and linear damping was recently established in
[
20
]
for the initial data near the equilibrium state and on the torus. We construct global entropy solutions to (
1.1
) via global weak solutions to the nonlocal Navier-Stokes system (
1.9
) with stress-free boundary conditions.
The proof that these solutions are uniformly bounded, in terms of
\varepsilon
,
despite the presence of nonlocal terms is another novelty. A new procedure for approximating the initial data, with the bounded second moment of the density, is implemented to obtain the propagation in time of the second moment of the density so that the confinement is controlled; see Lemma
3.2
.

The paper has the following structure. In §
2
, we first introduce the definitions of finite-energy entropy solutions for CEEs with nonlocal interactions
and present the main result – Theorem
2.2
. We then describe the construction of the approximate solutions for CNSEs and state the inviscid limit theorem, Theorem
2.6
. The rest of the paper is then devoted to the proof of this result for two types of equations of state: the polytropic equation of state (
1.5
), and the general pressure satisfying hypotheses (
\mathcal{H}
).
In §
3
, we derive the basic estimates for both the density and velocity, and prove the
H^{-1}_{\rm loc}
–compactness of entropy dissipation measures for the approximate solutions for the polytropic case.
In §
4
, we prove the global existence of finite-energy entropy weak solutions for this case,
\it{i.e.},
we prove Theorem
2.6
.
In §
5
, we develop the arguments from §
3
and §
4
to prove the global existence of finite-energy entropy solutions for CEEs for the general pressure law case. In this case, we prove the inviscid limit by proving the
W^{-1,p}_{\rm loc}
compactness of entropy dissipation measures for the approximate solutions for
1\leq p<2
. Finally, in Appendix
A
, we explain how the approximate initial data sequences
(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x),\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x))
can be constructed with desired estimates, regularity, and boundary compatibility for the polytropic equation of state.

2.
Definitions and Main Theorems

In this section, we define the notion of entropy solutions of system (
1.1
)
and then formulate our main results of this paper.

2.1.
Entropy and entropy flux pairs for CEEs

Recall that a pair

(\eta(\rho,m),q(\rho,m))

is called an entropy-entropy flux pair (entropy pair, for short) of the Euler system (
1.1
)
if they satisfy

\nabla q(\rho,m)=\nabla\eta(\rho,m)\nabla\Big(\begin{matrix}m\\
\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+P(\rho)\end{matrix}\Big);

(2.1)

see Lax
[
31
]
.
Furthermore,
\eta(\rho,m)
is called a weak entropy if

\displaystyle\eta|_{\rho=0}=0\qquad\,\,\text{for any fixed $u=\frac{m}{\rho}$}.

From
[
35
]
,
it is well known (
cf
.
[
16
,
17
,
35
]
)
that, for the polytropic case, any weak entropy
(\eta,q)
can be represented by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\eta^{\psi}(\rho,m)=\eta(\rho,\rho u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\rho;s-u)\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle q^{\psi}(\rho,m)=q(\rho,\rho u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\theta s+(1-\theta)u)\chi(\rho;s-u)\psi(s)\,{\rm d}s,\end{cases}

(2.2)

where
\chi(\rho;s-u)=[\rho^{2\theta}-(s-u)^{2}]_{+}^{\mathfrak{b}}

with
\mathfrak{b}=\frac{3-\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}>-\frac{1}{2}

and
\theta=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}
is the weak entropy kernel.
In particular, when
\psi(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{2},

the entropy pair is the pair of the mechanical energy and the associated flux:

\displaystyle\eta^{*}(\rho,m)=\frac{m^{2}}{2\rho}+\rho e(\rho),\quad q^{*}(\rho,m)=\frac{m^{3}}{2\rho^{2}}+m(\rho e(\rho))^{\prime},

From (
2.1
), any entropy satisfies

\eta_{\rho\rho}-\frac{P^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\eta_{uu}=0

(2.3)

with
u=\frac{m}{\rho}
. It has been proved in
[
14
,
15
,
34
,
35
]

that any regular weak entropy can be generated by the convolution of a smooth function
\psi(x)

with a fundamental solution
\chi(\rho,u,s)
of the entropy equation (
2.3
),
i.e.
,

\eta^{\psi}(\rho,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(\rho,u,s)\psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.4)

The corresponding entropy flux is generated from the flux kernel
\sigma(\rho,u,s)
(see (
2.7
)),
i.e.
,

\displaystyle q^{\psi}(\rho,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sigma(\rho,u,s)\psi(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.5)

The entropy kernel
\chi=\chi(\rho,u,s)
is a fundamental solution of the entropy equation (
2.3
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\chi_{\rho\rho}-\frac{P^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\chi_{uu}=0,\\
\displaystyle&\chi|_{\rho=0}=0,\quad\chi_{\rho}|_{\rho=0}=\delta_{u=s}.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.6)

As pointed out in
[
14
]
that equation (
2.6
)
is invariant under the Galilean transformation, which implies that

\chi(\rho,u,s)=\chi(\rho,u-s,0)=\chi(\rho,0,s-u)
.
For simplicity, we write it as
\chi(\rho,u,s)=\chi(\rho,u-s)
below
when no confusion arises.

The corresponding entropy flux kernel
\sigma(\rho,u,s)
satisfies the Cauchy problem
for
\sigma-u\chi
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&(\sigma-u\chi)_{\rho\rho}-\frac{P^{\prime}(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}(\sigma-u\chi)_{uu}=\frac{P^{\prime\prime}(\rho)}{\rho}\chi_{u},\\
\displaystyle&(\sigma-u\chi)|_{\rho=0}=0,\quad(\sigma-u\chi)_{\rho}|_{\rho=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.7)

It can be checked that
P(\rho)
, described by (
1.2
)–(
1.4
),
satisfies all the conditions in
[
14
,
15
]
. In particular,
\sigma-u\chi

is Galilean invariant; see
[
14
]
.

2.2.
Existence of solutions to CEEs

We are now ready to define the notion of solutions to system (
1.1
) and to formulate our main result.

Definition 2.1
.

A pair of functions
(\rho,m)(t,x)
with
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}))

and
\frac{m}{\sqrt{\rho}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+};L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
for
\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty)

is a finite-energy entropy solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) and (
1.6
), with
P(\rho)
satisfying hypotheses
(
\mathcal{H}
)
, if the following conditions hold
:

(i)

The total mass is conserved
:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=:M\qquad\mbox{ a.e. $t\geq 0$},

and
(\frac{m}{\sqrt{\rho}})(t,x)=0
a.e. on
the vacuum states
\{(t,x)\,:\,\rho(t,x)=0\}
.

(ii)

For a.e.
t>0
, the total energy is not increasing
:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big|\frac{m}{\sqrt{\rho}}\Big|^{2}+\rho e(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\Big)(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\leq\mathcal{E}_{0}.

(iii)

For any
\Psi(t,x)\in C^{1}_{0}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\big(\rho\Psi_{t}+m\Psi_{x}\big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x)\Psi(0,x)\,{\rm d}x=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}\Big(m\Psi_{t}+\big(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+P(\rho)\big)\Psi_{x}\Big)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{0}(x)\Psi(0,x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}}\Big(-\lambda m+\rho\partial_{x}W\ast\rho-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi(x-y)\big(\rho(x)m(y)-\rho(y)m(x)\big)\,{\rm d}y\Big)\Psi\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}t.

(2.9)

(iv)

For any convex function
\psi(s)
with subquadratic growth at infinity and any entropy pair
(\eta^{\psi},q^{\psi})
defined in (
2.4
)–(
3.1
),

\displaystyle\eta^{\psi}(\rho,m)_{t}+q^{\psi}(\rho,m)_{x}

\displaystyle-\eta^{\psi}_{m}\Big(\lambda m+\rho\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi(x-y)\big(\rho(x)m(y)-\rho(y)m(x)\big)\,{\rm d}y-\rho\partial_{x}W\ast\rho\Big)\leq 0

is satisfied in the sense of distributions.

We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.2

(Existence of solutions of CEEs with nonlocal interactions)
.

Consider problem (
1.1
) with initial data (
1.6
) satisfying (
1.7
)–(
1.8
). Let
P(\rho)
satisfy hypotheses
(
\mathcal{H}
)
.

Then there exists a global-in-time finite-energy entropy solution
(\rho,m)(t,x)
of problem (
1.1
) and (
1.6
),
in the sense of
Definition
2.1
.
In particular, there exists a global-in-time finite-energy entropy solution for
problem (
1.1
) and (
1.6
) with polytropic gases equation of state (
1.5
).

Remark 2.3
.

The interaction potential can also be extended to the more general form:

W(x)=-|x|+\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\tilde{W}(x),
with

|\tilde{W}(x)|\leq Cx^{2}
at infinity and smooth.
Even more, we can consider

W(x)=-|x|+\frac{x^{2}}{2}+\frac{|x|^{\nu}}{\nu}
for
1<\nu<2.

Remark 2.4
.

We can allow the communication kernel
\varpi(x)\in C(\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\})
, and

\varpi(x)\mathbf{I}_{B(0,R)}\in L^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}}(\mathbb{R}),\quad\varpi(x)\mathbf{I}_{\mathbb{R}\backslash B(0,R)}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\qquad\text{for any }R>0.

For example, we can choose the singular communication weight
\varpi(x)
as
\varpi(x)=\frac{1}{|x|^{a}}
for
a<\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}.

Remark 2.5
.

Our results also hold for an asymptotically isothermal pressure law
(\gamma_{2}=1)
, that is,
P(\rho)/\rho=O(1)
in the limit
\rho\rightarrow\infty
; see
[
40
,
41
]
.

2.3.
Existence of solutions to CNSEs and their inviscid limit

In this paper, we do not prove Theorem
2.2
directly. Instead, the solution from Definition
2.1
is obtained as a limit of the regular solutions of CNSEs (
1.9
)
on a truncated domain
\Omega_{T}^{\varepsilon}
with the moving boundary. We define

\Omega_{T}^{\varepsilon}=\big\{(t,x)\ :\,b^{-}_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq r\leq b^{+}_{\varepsilon}(t),\,0\leq t\leq T\big\},

(2.10)

where
\{x=b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)\,:\,0<t\leq T\}
are the free boundaries determined by

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)=u^{\varepsilon}(t,b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)),\quad b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(0)=\pm b_{\varepsilon}\qquad\quad\mbox{for $t>0$}.

(2.11)

On the free boundaries
x=b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t)
,
the stress-free boundary condition is imposed:

\big(P(\rho^{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon})u^{\varepsilon}_{x}\big)(t,b^{\pm}_{\varepsilon}(t))=0\qquad\mbox{for $t>0$}.

(2.12)

The initial intervals
[-b_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon}]
approximate the whole line when
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. More specifically, we assume explicitly that

b_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-p}\quad\text{with}\quad p>\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}&\quad\text{for polytropic gases,}\\
\frac{\gamma_{1}}{\gamma_{1}-\alpha}&\quad\text{for general pressure law.}\end{array}\right.

Let
(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x)
satisfy (
1.7
)–(
1.8
).
The initial data for the approximate system are given by

(\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})(0,x)=(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}u_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x)\qquad\,\mbox{for $x\in[-b_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon}]$},

(2.13)

where
(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},u_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x)
are obtained by the smooth approximation of
the original data
(\rho_{0},m_{0})
, constructed in Appendix A. In particular, they satisfy Lemmas
A.1
–
A.7
.

We further define

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\varepsilon}:

\displaystyle=\int_{-b_{\varepsilon}}^{b_{\varepsilon}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}e(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}W\ast\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}\Big)\,{\rm d}x,\,\,\,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\varepsilon}:

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{-b_{\varepsilon}}^{b_{\varepsilon}}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{2\alpha-3}|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0x}|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

(2.15)

where the convolution in (
2.14
) is understood as
\int^{b_{\varepsilon}}_{-b_{\varepsilon}}W(x-y)\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(y)\,{\rm d}y
.

We are now ready to state the main theorem of our paper.

Theorem 2.6

(Inviscid limit for CNSEs with nonlocal interactions)
.

Let the hypotheses of

Theorem
2.2
be satisfied.
Let
\gamma_{1}\geq\gamma_{2}>1
and
\frac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1
, and let

\{(\rho^{\varepsilon},\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
be a sequence of the strong solutions to (
1.9
) with initial and boundary data specified in (
2.10
)–(
2.13
) and satisfying:

\displaystyle 0<C^{-1}_{\varepsilon}\leq\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\leq C_{\varepsilon},\quad(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(b))^{\gamma_{1}}b_{\varepsilon}=(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(-b_{\varepsilon}))^{\gamma_{1}}b_{\varepsilon}\leq C_{0}\qquad\mbox{ for $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$},

\displaystyle(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0},\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}_{0})(x)\rightarrow(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x)\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$ in $L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})$}\mbox{ for $q\in\{1,\gamma_{2}\}$},

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(\mathcal{E}_{0},0)\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$},

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M,\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\varepsilon}\leq C_{0},\quad

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x\,\rightarrow\,\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x^{2}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M_{2}\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$},

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
which may depend on
(\mathcal{E}_{0},M,M_{2},\gamma,\alpha)
, while

C_{\varepsilon}>0
is a constant depending on
\varepsilon>0
.

Then there exist both a subsequence
(
still denoted
)

(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})(t,x)
with
m^{\varepsilon}=\rho^{\varepsilon}u^{\varepsilon}
, and a vector-valued function
(\rho,m)(t,x)

such that, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})(t,x)\rightarrow(\rho,m)(t,x)\qquad\mbox{a.e. $(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}$},\\
&(\rho^{\varepsilon},m^{\varepsilon})(t,x)\rightarrow(\rho,m)(t,x)\qquad\mbox{in $L^{q_{1}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})\times L^{q_{2}}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+})$ with $q_{1}\in[1,\gamma_{2}+1)$ and $q_{2}\in[1,\frac{3(\gamma_{2}+1)}{\gamma_{2}+3})$}.\end{split}

Moreover,
(\rho,m)(t,x)

is a global finite-energy entropy
solution of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) and (
1.6
)
in the sense of
Definition
2.1
.
In particular, the result is true for the polytropic case (
1.5
).

.

In
Theorem
2.6
, we need
\frac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1
; see (

). However, note that the condition

;

–

and

–

.
In particular, our results cover the Saint-Venant model for shallow water (
\alpha=1
and
\gamma=2
),
even with the nonlocal terms.

Remark 2.8
.

The regular solutions for the compressible Navier-Stokes problem (
1.9
) with the new nonlocal terms and with stress-free boundary conditions (

)–(

[
30
,

]
; see also

]

3.
Uniform Estimates of Approximate Solutions

This section is dedicated to several uniform estimates with respect to
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

for the regular solutions of (
1.9
) on the bounded time-dependent domain (
2.10
) with corresponding boundary conditions. Although some of the estimates hold also for general
P(\rho)
specified through hypotheses
(\mathcal{H})
, for the purposes of this section, we restrict ourselves to the polytropic equation of state (
1.5
). The general pressure case is discussed in §
5
.

We drop the
\varepsilon
-superscript of approximate solutions when no confusion arises.
In all of the estimates from now on,

C>0
is a universal constant independent of
\varepsilon
, which may depend on
\mathcal{E}_{0},M,M_{2},C_{0},T,\gamma
, and
\alpha
, and can be different at each occurrence. For abbreviation, we also denote

V=V(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi(x-y)\Big(m(y)-\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}\rho(y)\Big)\,{\rm d}y=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\varpi(x-y)\Big(m(y)-\frac{m(x)}{\rho(x)}\rho(y)\Big)\,{\rm d}y

(3.1)

for
x\in(b^{-}(t),b^{+}(t))
.

3.1.
Conservation of mass and Lagrangian coordinates

We present the reformulation of IBVP (
1.9
) and (
2.10
)–(
2.13
)
in the so-called Lagrangian coordinates,
which is equivalent to the Eulerian formulation for sufficiently regular solutions.
It follows from
\eqref{1.8}_{1}
and (
2.11
) that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int_{b^{-}(t)}^{b^{+}(t)}\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=(\rho u)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho u)(t,b^{-}(t))-\int_{b^{-}(t)}^{b^{+}(t)}(\rho u)_{x}(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=0,

which, due to (
A.21
), yields that

\int_{b^{-}(t)}^{b^{+}(t)}\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x=\int_{b^{-}(t)}^{b^{+}(t)}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M\qquad\,\,\mbox{for any $t\geq 0$}.

(3.2)

For
x\in[b^{-}(t),b^{+}(t)]
and
t\in[0,T]
, we define the Lagrangian
coordinates
(\tau,\xi)
as

\xi=\int_{b^{-}(t)}^{x}\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y,\qquad\tau=t,

which transform the domain with the free boundary:
[0,T]\times[b^{-}(t),b^{+}(t)]

into the fixed domain:
[0,T]\times[0,M]
.
A direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial t}=-\rho u,\quad\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial x}=\rho,\quad\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial t}=1,\quad\frac{\partial\tau}{\partial x}=0,\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\partial x}{\partial\tau}=u,\quad\frac{\partial x}{\partial\xi}=\frac{1}{\rho},\quad\frac{\partial t}{\partial\tau}=1,\quad\frac{\partial t}{\partial\xi}=0.\end{cases}

Applying the Euler-Lagrange transformation, IBVP (
1.9
) and (
2.10
)–(
2.13
)
becomes

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}+\rho^{2}u_{\xi}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle u_{\tau}+P(\rho)_{\xi}-\varepsilon(\mu(\rho)\rho u_{\xi})_{\xi}-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}=0,\end{cases}

(3.3)

for
(\tau,\xi)\in[0,T]\times[0,M]
, and

\big(P(\rho)-\varepsilon\mu(\rho)\rho u_{\xi}\big)(\tau,0)=0,\quad\big(P(\rho)-\varepsilon\mu(\rho)\rho u_{\xi}\big)(\tau,M)=0\qquad\,\,\mbox{ for $\tau\in[0,T]$}.

(3.4)

With a slight abuse of notation, we denote by
V
a function defined in (
3.1
), taken as a function of
\xi
.
The fixed boundaries
\xi=0,M
correspond to the free boundaries
x=b^{\pm}(t)
in the Eulerian coordinates.

3.2.
Basic energy estimate

To begin with, we obtain the basic energy estimate for problem (
1.9
)
and (
2.10
)–(
2.13
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For a smooth solution of problem (
1.9
) and (
2.10
)–(
2.13
), the following estimate holds
:

\displaystyle\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+\rho e(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\Big)(t,x)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{b^{-}(s)}^{b^{+}(s)}\left(\varepsilon\rho^{\alpha}|u_{x}|^{2}-\lambda\rho u^{2}\right)(s,x)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int^{t}_{0}\int^{b^{+}(s)}_{b^{-}(s)}\int^{b^{+}(s)}_{b^{-}(s)}\varpi(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x{\rm d}s=\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{0}\leq C_{0}.

(3.5)

Here and hereafter,
C_{0}>0
is the constant from the statement of
Theorem
2.6
, independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

It follows from
\eqref{3.8}_{1}
that

\displaystyle-P(\rho)u_{\xi}=\frac{P(\rho)}{\rho^{2}}\rho_{\tau}=\kappa\rho^{\gamma-2}\rho_{\tau}=e(\rho)_{\tau}.

(3.6)

Multiplying
\eqref{3.8}_{2}
by
u,
we have

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{u^{2}}{2}+e(\rho)\Big)_{\tau}+\varepsilon\rho^{1+\alpha}(u_{\xi})^{2}+\big((P(\rho)-\varepsilon\mu(\rho)\rho u_{\xi})u\big)_{\xi}-\lambda u^{2}-uV+\rho u(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}=0.

(3.7)

Notice that

\displaystyle-\int^{M}_{0}uV\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=-\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(x)u(x)\Big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\varpi(x-y)\big(u(y)-u(x)\big)\rho(y)\,{\rm d}y\Big){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\varpi(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)\big(|u(x)|^{2}-u(x)u(y)\big)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\varpi(x-y)\rho(x)\rho(y)|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x,

where we have used the symmetry of
\varpi(x)
.

For the other nonlocal term, our aim is to prove that

\int^{M}_{0}\rho u(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\,{\rm d}\xi=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x.

(3.8)

First we rewrite the right-hand-side (RHS) to obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\,\,=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\Big)_{t}\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}(\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))\frac{{\rm d}b^{+}(t)}{{\rm d}t}-\frac{1}{2}(\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))\frac{{\rm d}b^{-}(t)}{{\rm d}t}

\displaystyle\,\,=\frac{1}{2}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\left(\rho_{t}W\ast\rho+\rho W\ast\rho_{t}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}(u\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-\frac{1}{2}(u\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t)).

(3.9)

Using the change of the order of integration
and the explicit form of
W(x)=-|x|+\frac{|x|^{2}}{2}
(in particular
W(x-y)=W(y-x)
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho W\ast\rho_{t}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(t,x)\big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}W(x-y)\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y\big)_{t}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}(t,y)\big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}W(x-y)\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\big)\,{\rm d}y+(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}(t,y)\big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}W(y-x)\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\big)\,{\rm d}y+(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}(t,x)\big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}W(x-y)\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y\big)\,{\rm d}x+(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x+(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t)).

Thus, it follows that the first term on the RHS of (
3.2
) is equal to

\frac{1}{2}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\big(\rho_{t}W\ast\rho+\rho W\ast\rho_{t}\big)\,{\rm d}x=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x+\frac{1}{2}(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-\frac{1}{2}(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t)).

Therefore, we have proven that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x+(u\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(u\rho W\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t)).

(3.10)

On the other hand, it follows directly from
\eqref{1.8}_{1}
that

\displaystyle\int^{M}_{0}\rho u(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho u(W\ast\rho)_{x}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))-\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}(\rho u)_{x}W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{+}(t))-(\rho uW\ast\rho)(t,b^{-}(t))+\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho_{t}W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x.

(3.11)

Comparing (
3.10
) with (
3.11
), we deduce (
3.8
).

Since
W=-|x|+\frac{|x|^{2}}{2},

we have that
W+\frac{1}{2}\geq 0.
and so, using (
3.2
), we obtain

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x=\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\frac{1}{2}\rho\Big(W+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\ast\rho\,{\rm d}x.

Integrating (
3.7
) over
[0,\tau]\times[0,M]
, using the Grönwall inequality
and the stress-free boundary conditions (
3.4
), pulling the resultant equation
back to the Eulerian coordinates, we obtain (
3.5
).
This completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

3.3.
Second moment estimate

We now derive the second moment estimate for the density.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
C=C>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\displaystyle\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x^{2}\rho(t,x)\,{\rm d}x\leq C.

(3.12)

Proof.

Using
\eqref{1.8}_{1},
we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x^{2}\rho\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=(b^{+}(t))^{2}(\rho u)(t,b^{+}(t))-(b^{-}(t))^{2}(\rho u)(t,b^{-}(t))+\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x^{2}\rho_{t}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=2\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x\rho u\,{\rm d}x,

such that

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x^{2}\rho\,{\rm d}x\leq\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}x^{2}\rho\,{\rm d}x+\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho u^{2}\,{\rm d}x.

Then we conclude (
3.12
) by using the Grönwall inequality.

\hfill\Box

3.4.
Higher-order estimates of the density and the pressure

In this section, we derive several higher-order estimates
for the density and the pressure.
To start, we analyze the behavior of density
\rho
on the free boundary.
It follows from
\eqref{3.8}_{1}
and (
3.4
) that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\tau}(\rho^{\alpha-\gamma}(\tau,M))=-\frac{\kappa(\alpha-\gamma)}{\varepsilon}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\rho(\tau,M)=\rho_{0}(M)\,\Big(1+\frac{\kappa(\gamma-\alpha)}{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}(M))^{\gamma-\alpha}\tau\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-\alpha}},

so that

\displaystyle\rho(t,b^{+}(t))=\rho_{0}(b)\,\Big(1+\frac{\kappa(\gamma-\alpha)}{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}(b))^{\gamma-\alpha}t\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-\alpha}}.

We obtain

\displaystyle\rho(t,b^{+}(t))=\rho(t,b^{-}(t))=\rho_{0}(b)\Big(1+\frac{\kappa(\gamma-\alpha)}{\varepsilon}(\rho_{0}(b))^{\gamma-\alpha}t\Big)^{-\frac{1}{\gamma-\alpha}}\leq\rho_{0}(b),

(3.13)

where we have used that
\rho_{0}(b)=\rho_{0}(-b).

From (
3.13
), it follows in particular that the third and fourth term
on the left-hand side (LHS) of (
3.3
) below is nonnegative.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\alpha\geq 0
and
\rho^{\gamma}_{0}(\pm b)b\leq C_{0}
.
Then, for any given
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}(\rho^{2\alpha-3}\rho^{2}_{x})(t,x)\,{\rm d}x+\varepsilon\kappa\gamma\int^{t}_{0}\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}(\rho^{\alpha+\gamma-3}\rho^{2}_{x})(s,x)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\varepsilon}\int^{t}_{0}\Big(\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(s,b^{+}(s))b^{+}(s)-\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(s,b^{-}(s))b^{-}(s)\Big)\,{\rm d}s

\displaystyle+\kappa\Big(\rho^{\gamma}(t,b^{+}(t))b^{+}(t)-\rho^{\gamma}(t,b^{-}(t))b^{-}(t)\Big)\leq C.

(3.14)

Proof.

It is direct to calculate from
\eqref{3.8}_{2}
that

\displaystyle\Big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\Big)_{\tau}+P(\rho)_{\xi}-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}=0,

(3.15)

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\mu(\rho)\rho u_{\xi}=\rho^{1+\alpha}u_{\xi}=-\rho^{\alpha-1}\rho_{\tau}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\tau}.

Multiplying (
3.15
) by
u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi},
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi})^{2}}{2}+\frac{\kappa\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho W\ast\rho\Big)_{\tau}+\big(p(\rho)u\big)_{\xi}+\varepsilon\kappa\gamma\rho^{\alpha+\gamma-2}(\rho_{\xi})^{2}

\displaystyle+\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)\big(-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\big)=0.

(3.16)

Using
\eqref{3.8}_{1}
and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\tau}(\tau,M)=-\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}\rho^{\gamma+1-\alpha}(\tau,M),\qquad\rho_{\tau}(\tau,0)=-\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}\rho^{\gamma+1-\alpha}(\tau,0).

Then we obtain

\displaystyle\big(P(\rho)u\big)(\tau,M)-\big(P(\rho)u\big)(\tau,0)

\displaystyle=\kappa\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,M)\frac{{\rm d}b^{+}(\tau)}{{\rm d}\tau}-\kappa\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,0)\frac{{\rm d}b^{-}(\tau)}{{\rm d}\tau}

\displaystyle=\kappa\big(\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)\big)_{\tau}-\kappa\big(\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)\big)_{\tau}

\displaystyle\quad+\kappa\gamma\Big(\rho^{\gamma-1}(\tau,0)\rho_{\tau}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)-\rho^{\gamma-1}(\tau,M)\rho_{\tau}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)\Big)

\displaystyle=\kappa\big(\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)\big)_{\tau}-\kappa\big(\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)\big)_{\tau}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\varepsilon}\Big(\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)-\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)\Big).

Integrating (
3.4
) over
[0,M]
yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\tau}\int^{M}_{0}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)^{2}+\frac{\kappa\rho^{\gamma-1}}{\gamma-1}\Big)\,{\rm d}\xi+\varepsilon\kappa\gamma\int^{M}_{0}\rho^{\alpha+\gamma-2}(\rho_{\xi})^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\,\,\,+\kappa\Big(\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)-\rho^{\gamma}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)\Big)_{\tau}+\frac{\kappa\gamma}{\varepsilon}\Big(\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(\tau,M)b^{+}(\tau)-\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}(\tau,0)b^{-}(\tau)\Big)

\displaystyle=\int^{M}_{0}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)\big(-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\big)\,{\rm d}\xi.

(3.17)

For the last term in (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\int^{M}_{0}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)\big(-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\big)\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int^{M}_{0}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi+\int^{M}_{0}\big(-\lambda u-V+\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int^{M}_{0}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\int^{M}_{0}u^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\int^{M}_{0}V^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\int^{M}_{0}\big(\rho(W\ast\rho)_{\xi}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int^{M}_{0}\big(u+\frac{\varepsilon}{\alpha}(\rho^{\alpha})_{\xi}\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi+C\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho\big(\partial_{x}W\ast\rho\big)^{2}\,{\rm d}x+C,

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\int^{M}_{0}V^{2}\,{\rm d}\xi=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho V^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(t,x)\Big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\varpi(x-y)\big(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\big)\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y\Big)^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\bigg(\rho(t,x)\Big(\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\big(\rho(t,y)+(\rho u^{2})(t,y)\big)\,{\rm d}y\Big)^{2}+u^{2}(t,x)\bigg)\,{\rm d}x\leq C.

Notice that

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}W\ast\rho)(t,x)=\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\big(1-2H(x-y)+x-y\big)\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y,

(3.18)

where
H(\cdot)
is the Heaviside function.
We now rewrite the RHS of (
3.18
) using

\displaystyle\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y=M,\qquad\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}H(x-y)\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y=\int^{x}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y.

We obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}W\ast\rho)(t,x)=M-2\int^{x}_{b^{-}(t)}\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y+xM-\int^{b^{+}(t)}_{b^{-}(t)}y\rho(t,y)\,{\rm d}y.

It follows from (
3.2
) and (
3.12

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\rho\mathbf{I}_{[-b+1,b-1]})^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}(x-y)J^{\frac{1}{\beta}}_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)J^{\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}}_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\Big)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x-y)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\Big)^{\frac{\beta}{\beta^{\prime}}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x-y)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y,

where we have used
\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\beta^{\prime}}=1,
and the property of mollifier:
\int_{\mathbb{R}}J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y=1.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x-y)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}x{\rm d}y=M,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x-y)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{2}\rho_{0}(x-y)\,{\rm d}x\Big)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y+\bar{x})^{2}\rho_{0}(\bar{x})\,{\rm d}\bar{x}\Big)J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{\mathbb{R}}(y^{2}M+M_{2})J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\leq C,

where we have used
\int_{\mathbb{R}}y^{2}J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y\leq\varepsilon.

Now, we estimate (
A.1
) term by term.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)\hat{\rho}_{0}(y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq CM\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)(x^{2}+1)\,{\rm d}x+C\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)M_{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq C(M,M_{2}).

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)e^{-\beta y^{2}}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\rho}_{0}(x)(x^{2}+1)\,{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\beta x^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)\hat{\rho}_{0}(y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\beta x^{2}}(x^{2}M+M_{2}+M)\,{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{4}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\beta x^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(x^{2}+y^{2}+1)e^{-\beta x^{2}}\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x\leq C\varepsilon^{2\beta}.

Combining these estimates together, we conclude (
A.9
).

\hfill\Box

Now we define
\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)
by

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)

\displaystyle:=\tilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\,\mathbf{I}_{[-b,b]}(x).

(A.20)

Remark A.2
.

We have multiplied by a cut-off function
\mathbf{I}_{[-b,b]}(x)
in (
A.20
)
such that
(W\ast\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x)
is meaningful.
Otherwise, when
|y|>b
,
\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(y)=b^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}

so that
(W\ast\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon})(x)\sim\int_{|y|>b}W(x-y)\,{\rm d}y\,b^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}}=\infty
.

Then we can obtain the following lemma.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
b=\varepsilon^{-p},p>\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha},
and
q\in\{1,\gamma,2\alpha-1\}.

The smooth function
\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)
defined in (
A.20
) satisfies the
following properties: There exists
C_{0}>0
independent of
\varepsilon
but may depend on
(\mathcal{E}_{0},M,M_{2},\gamma,\alpha)
such that

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(b)=\bar{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(-b)\leq C_{0}b^{-\frac{1}{\gamma}},

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}|\tilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon,b}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{q}\,{\rm d}x+\int^{b}_{-b}\Big|(\tilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}-(\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|^{\frac{2q}{2\alpha-1}}\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$},

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}\bar{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M\qquad\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int^{b}_{-b}\Big|\big((\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\big)_{x}\Big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq C_{0}\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}x^{2}|\tilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\,\,\mbox{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},$}

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}|\bar{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)W\ast\bar{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)-\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)W\ast\tilde{\rho}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)|\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\,\,\text{ as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$}.

Since
\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}\tilde{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\,{\rm d}x\neq M
in general, we define

\displaystyle\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{M}{\int^{b}_{-b}\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\,{\rm d}x}\bar{\rho}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x).

(A.21)

Combining (
A.21
) with Lemmas
A.1
and
A.3
, we have

Lemma A.4
.

Let
q\in\{1,\gamma,2\alpha-1\}
and
\frac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1.

The smooth function
\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)
defined in (
A.21
) satisfies the following properties: There exists
C_{0}>0

independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
but may depend on
(\mathcal{E}_{0},M,M_{2},\gamma,\alpha)
such that

\displaystyle(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(b))^{\gamma}\,b=(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(-b))^{\gamma}\,b\leq C_{0},

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}\big|\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho_{0}(x)\big|^{q}\,{\rm d}x+\int^{b}_{-b}\Big|(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}-(\rho_{0}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|^{\frac{2q}{2\alpha-1}}\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$},

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int^{b}_{-b}\Big|\big((\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x))^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\big)_{x}\Big|^{2}{\rm d}x\leq C_{0}\varepsilon,

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}x^{2}|\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\rho_{0}(x)|\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\mbox{as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},$}

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)(W\ast\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0})(x)-\rho_{0}(x)(W\ast\rho_{0})(x)|\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{ as $\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}$}.

A.2.
The initial velocity

Next, we construct the approximate initial data for the velocity. We first define

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big(\frac{m_{0}\mathbf{I}_{[-b+2,b-2]}}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}}}\Big)(x-y)\,J_{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(y)\,{\rm d}y,

(A.22)

for which the following properties are valid.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
\frac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1
. Then
\bar{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)
defined in (
A.22
) satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm supp}\,\bar{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,-b+1\leq x\leq b-1\},

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)|\bar{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|m_{0}(x)|^{2}}{\rho_{0}(x)}\,{\rm d}x\qquad\,\,\,\mbox{for any $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$},

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}\bar{u}^{\varepsilon}_{0}-m_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=0.

The proof of Lemma
A.5
is similar to that in
[
13
, Lemma A.8]
and
[
28
, Lemma 5.4]
,
so we omit it for brevity.

We still need to modify
\bar{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)
so that it satisfies the stress-free boundary condition (
2.8
)
at
x=\pm b
. To this end, we define a cut-off function
S=S(z)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
that satisfies

\displaystyle S(z)=\begin{cases}0\quad&\mbox{if $z\in(-\infty,0)$},\\
1\quad&\mbox{if $z\in(2,\infty)$},\\
\mbox{monotonically increasing}&\mbox{if $z\in[0,2]$}.\end{cases}

(A.23)

Then we define

u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x):=\bar{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,S(4(x-(b-\frac{1}{2})))\int^{b}_{x}\frac{P(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))}{\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))}\,{\rm d}z-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\,S(-4(x-(b-\frac{1}{2})))\int^{-b}_{x}\frac{P(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))}{\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))}\,{\rm d}z.

(A.24)

It is direct to check that
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\in C^{\infty}([-b,b])
satisfies
the following boundary conditions:

\displaystyle u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(\pm b)=0,\qquad\big(P((\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)))-\varepsilon\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x))(u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x))_{x}\big)\big|_{x=\pm b}=0.

A direct calculation following (
A.24
) implies
that

\displaystyle\int^{b}_{-b}\Big|\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)}u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)-\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)}\bar{u}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\Big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int^{-b+\frac{1}{2}}_{-b}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\big|S(-4x-4b+2)\int^{x}_{-b}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))^{\gamma-\alpha}\,{\rm d}z\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int^{b}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\big|S(4x-4b+2)\int^{b}_{x}(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(z))^{\gamma-\alpha}\,{\rm d}z\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big(\int^{b}_{b-\frac{1}{2}}b^{-\frac{1}{{}^{\gamma}}}\big|\int^{b}_{x}b^{-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{\gamma}}\,{\rm d}z\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x+\int^{-b+\frac{1}{2}}_{-b}b^{-\frac{1}{{}^{\gamma}}}\big|\int^{x}_{-b}b^{-\frac{\gamma-\alpha}{\gamma}}\,{\rm d}z\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{2}}b^{-\frac{2(\gamma-\alpha)+1}{\gamma}}\leq C\varepsilon^{{\frac{2(\gamma-\alpha)+1}{\gamma}p}-2}\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

(A.25)

for
p>\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha},
where we have used (
A.23
) and
b=\varepsilon^{-p}.

Therefore, similarly to those as in
[
12
,
28
]
, using Lemma
A.5
and (
A.2
),
we conclude

Lemma A.6
.

For fixed
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int^{b}_{-b}\big|\sqrt{\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)}u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|\frac{m_{0}(x)}{\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x)}}\big|^{2}\,{\rm d}x,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int^{b}_{-b}|\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)-m_{0}(x)|\,{\rm d}x=0.

In the previous argument, we have already defined the cut-off for the density. Now,
with
\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)
and
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)
defined respectively in (
A.21
) and (
A.24
),
we also need to define the cut-off for the velocity function. For
b\gg 1
, define

\displaystyle\big(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\big)(x):=\big(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x),u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\big)\mathbf{I}_{[-b,b]}(x).

(A.26)

Then, collecting all the above estimates, we have the following results.

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\frac{2}{3}<\alpha\leq 1,

b=\varepsilon^{-p},

p>\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-\alpha}
,

q\in\{1,\gamma\}
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1].

Let
\big(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\big)(x)
be the functions defined in (
A.26
)
so that
(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0},u^{\varepsilon}_{0})\in C^{\infty}([-b,b]).

Then

(i)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

\displaystyle\,\,\,b^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(-b)=b^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(b)\leq C_{0},\quad u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(\pm b)=0,\quad\big(P(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)-\varepsilon\mu(\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)))(u^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)))_{x}\big)\big|_{x=\pm b}=0,

\displaystyle\,\,\,\int^{b}_{-b}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M,\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}\mathcal{E}^{\varepsilon}_{1}\leq C_{0},

where
C_{0}>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
but may depend on
(\mathcal{E}_{0},M,M_{2},\gamma,\alpha)
.

(ii)

As
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(\mathcal{E}_{0},0),

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x^{2}\rho^{\varepsilon}_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x\rightarrow\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}x^{2}\rho_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x=M_{2},

\displaystyle(\rho_{0}^{\varepsilon},m_{0}^{\varepsilon})\rightarrow(\rho_{0},m_{0})(x)\qquad\,\mbox{\rm in $L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})$},

where
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
are defined in (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), respectively.

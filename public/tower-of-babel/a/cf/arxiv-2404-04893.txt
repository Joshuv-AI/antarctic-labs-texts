On the unification problem for GLP
L.D. Beklemishev

Abstract
We show that the polymodal provability logic GLP \mathrm{GLP} , in a language with at least two modalities and one variable, has nullary unification type. More specifically, we show that the formula [ 1 ] ​ p [1]p does not have maximal unifiers, and exhibit an infinite complete set of unifiers for it. Further, we discuss the algorithmic problem of whether a given formula is unifiable in GLP \mathrm{GLP} and remark that this problem has a positive solution. Finally, we state the arithmetical analogues of the unification and admissibility problems for GLP \mathrm{GLP} and formulate a number of open questions.



1
Introduction

We work in the language
{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)
of propositional polymodal logic with modalities traditionally denoted
[0],[1],\dots,[n-1]
and variables
\{p_{0},\dots,p_{m-1}\}
. Let
\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
denote the set of all formulas of
{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)
. A
substitution
is a map
\sigma:\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)\to\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
preserving all logical connectives and modalities. A
variable-free substitution
is a map
\sigma:\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)\to\mathrm{Fm}(n,0)
.

By a
logic
here we mean a set of formulas closed under the axioms and rules of classical propositional logic and the substitution rule: from
\varphi
infer
\sigma(\varphi)
.
Normal logics
are those containing the axioms of basic modal logic K for each modality and closed under modus ponens and necessitation rules: from
\varphi
infer
[i]\varphi
. If
\Gamma
is a finite set of formulas, we write
\Gamma\vdash_{L}\varphi
if there is a proof of
\varphi
from the axioms of
L
and
\Gamma
using modus ponens and necessitation rules.

Definition 1

A
unifier
for
\varphi
in a logic
L
is a substitution
\sigma
such that
L\vdash\sigma(\varphi)
.

A unifier
\sigma
is
more general
than
\tau
in
L
(denoted
\tau\leqslant\sigma
) if there is a substitution
\theta
such that
\tau=\theta\sigma
in
L
, that is, for all variables
p
,

L\vdash\tau(p)\leftrightarrow\theta(\sigma(p)).

Let
U_{L}(\varphi)
denote the set of all unifiers of a formula
\varphi
in
L
.
\varphi
is called
unifiable
if
U(\varphi)
is not empty. A unifier
\sigma\in U(\varphi)
is called
maximal
for
\varphi
if there is no
\tau\in U(\varphi)
such that
\sigma<\tau
. Here, as usual,
\sigma<\tau
means
\sigma\leqslant\tau
and not
\tau\leqslant\sigma
.

Definition 2

A subset

\Sigma\subseteq U_{L}(\varphi)
is a
complete set of unifiers for
\varphi
if

\forall\tau\in U_{L}(\varphi)\>\exists\sigma\in\Sigma\>\tau\leqslant\sigma.

Definition 3

A logic
L
has
finitary/infinitary unification type
if, for each unifiable formula
\varphi
, there is a finite/infinite complete set of maximal unifiers for
\varphi
.

L
has
nullary unification type
if it is neither finitary nor infinitary. In this case, there is a unifiable formula
\varphi
without a complete set of maximal unifiers.

Silvio Ghilardi in the 1990s (see
[
13
,
14
]
) pioneered the study of unification in propositional logics; his methods were based on the notion of projective formula and projective substitution. He showed that many standard logics, including the intuitionistic logic IPC and the modal logics K4, S4, GL, Grz, have finitary unification type, and complete sets of maximal unifiers can be constructed effectively. As a corollary, this yielded another solution of the problem of
admissibility of inference rules
for these logics previously obtained by Vladimir Rybakov by different methods (see
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
). Explicit axiomatizations of admissible rules based on (extensions of) Ghilardi’s methods for various logics have been obtained by R. Iemhoff, E. Jeřábek, G. Metcalfe, V. Rybakov, and many others (see
[
16
,
20
,
17
,
11
]
).

Ghilardi’s methods essentially rely on
transitivity
and
finite model property
(FMP) of the considered logics. For the basic (intransitive) modal logic K the decidability of the unification problem and of the admissibility of rules problem are still open. E. Jeřábek
[
21
]
has shown that K has nullary unification type.

For polymodal logics the situation is even more complicated. For example, Wolter and Zakharyaschev
[
29
]
show that for the bimodal logics extending K and K4 by the universal modality the unification problem and the admissibility of rules problem are undecidable. In contrast, the derivability problem for these logics is well-known to be decidable.

In this paper, we deal with the polymodal provability logic
\mathrm{GLP}
that is transitive, but does not have FMP (nor is Kripke complete).
The logic GLP introduced by G. Japaridze
[
19
]
plays an important role in proof-theoretic applications of provability logic. It has also been the subject of rather detailed modal-logical studies over the last years. In particular, we know that
\mathrm{GLP}
satisfies Craig’s interpolation property
[
18
]
, as well as uniform interpolation
[
28
]
. Its variable-free fragment has effective normal forms
[
18
,
4
]
, and the elementary theory of the Lindenbaum algebra of the variable-free fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}
(in the language with
n
modalities) is decidable
[
24
]
.

The unification and the admissibility of rules problems for
\mathrm{GLP}
have been stated as open questions in
[
3
]
. The first steps in the study of the unification problem for
\mathrm{GLP}
were undertaken by N. Lukashov in his Bachelor’s Thesis
[
23
]
. He considered the subsystem
\mathrm{J}_{2}
of
\mathrm{GLP}_{2}
and proved that
\mathrm{J}_{2}
has finitary unification type. It is known that the derivability problem for
\mathrm{GLP}_{2}
is reducible to
\mathrm{J}_{2}
, however, as we show here, the reduction does not preserve the unification type.

We show that the fragment
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
of
\mathrm{GLP}
in the language with
n
modalities has nullary unification type.
More specifically, the formula
[1]p
does not have maximal unifiers, and we exhibit an infinite complete set of unifiers for it. Furthermore, we discuss the algorithmic problem of whether a given formula is unifiable in
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
and remark that this problem has a positive solution, which can be inferred from the results of F. Pakhomov and J. Aguilera. In the last section of the paper we point out a few interesting connections of the unification problem for
\mathrm{GLP}
with some recent results in the proof-theory of arithmetic.

2
Japaridze’s provability logic GLP

Logics
\mathrm{GLP}
and
\mathrm{J}
.

The polymodal provability logic
\mathrm{GLP}
is formulated in the language
of the propositional calculus enriched by new unary connectives

[0]
,
[1]
,
[2]
, …, called
modalities
. The dual
connectives
\langle n\rangle
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are treated as
abbreviations:
\langle n\rangle\varphi
means
\neg[n]\neg\varphi
.

The logic
\mathrm{GLP}
is given by the following axiom schemata and
inference rules.

Axiom schemata:

\mathrm{(i)}

Tautologies of classical propositional calculus;

\mathrm{(ii)}

[n](\varphi\rightarrow\psi)\rightarrow([n]\varphi\rightarrow[n]\psi)
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

[n]([n]\varphi\rightarrow\varphi)\rightarrow[n]\varphi

(Löb’s axiom);

\mathrm{(iv)}

\langle m\rangle\varphi\rightarrow[n]\langle m\rangle\varphi
, for
m<n
;

\mathrm{(v)}

[m]\varphi\rightarrow[n]\varphi
, for

m\leqslant n
(monotonicity axiom);

Inference rules:

modus ponens,
\vdash\varphi\ \Rightarrow\ \vdash[n]\varphi
.

Thus, for each modality
[n]

\mathrm{GLP}
contains the axioms
(i)–(iii) and the inference rules of Gödel–Löb provability
logic
\mathrm{GL}
, whereas the schemata (iv) and (v) tie together
different modalities. A well-known consequence of axioms
(i)–(iii) is the
transitivity schema

[n]\varphi\to[n][n]\varphi.

Logic
\mathrm{J}
is obtained from
\mathrm{GLP}
by replacing the monotonicity
axiom (v) by the following two axiom schemata easily derivable in

\mathrm{GLP}
using the transitivity schema:

\mathrm{(vi)}

[m]\varphi\to[n][m]\varphi
, for
m<n
;

\mathrm{(vii)}

[m]\varphi\to[m][n]\varphi
, for
m<n
.

When the language is restricted to
n
modalities
[0],\dots,[n-1]
, the systems axiomatized by the rules of

\mathrm{GLP}
and
\mathrm{J}
are denoted
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
and
\mathrm{J}_{n}
, respectively. Thus,
\mathrm{GLP}_{1}
is the Gödel-Löb logic
\mathrm{GL}
.
It is known that
\mathrm{GLP}
is conservative over
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
,
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is conservative over
\mathrm{GLP}_{m}
, for
m<n
, and similar relations hold for
\mathrm{J}
and
\mathrm{J}_{n}
.

Unlike
\mathrm{GLP}
, the logic
\mathrm{J}
is complete w.r.t. a natural class of
finite Kripke frames described below.

Kripke frames.

A
Kripke frame
for the language of polymodal logic is a
structure
(W;R_{0},R_{1},\ldots)
, where
W
is a nonempty set, and

R_{k}
are binary relations on
W
. The elements of
W
are usually
called the
worlds
. A frame is called finite if
W
is finite
and
R_{k}=\varnothing
, for all but finitely many
k\geqslant 0
.

A
valuation

v
on a frame
W
maps every propositional
variable
p
to a subset
v(p)\subseteq W
called the
truth
set of
p
. A
Kripke model
is a Kripke frame together with a
valuation on it.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}=(W,v)
be a Kripke model. By induction on the build-up of

\varphi
we define a relation
\varphi
is true at
x
in
{\mathcal{W}}
(denoted
{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash\varphi
).

1.

{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash p\iff x\in v(p)
, if
p
is a variable;

2.

{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash(\varphi\land\psi)\iff({\mathcal{W}},x\vDash\varphi\text{ and }{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash\psi)
,

{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash\neg\varphi\iff{\mathcal{W}},x\nvDash\varphi
,

and similarly for the other boolean connectives;

3.

{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash[n]\varphi\iff\forall y\in W\>(xR_{n}y\Rightarrow{\mathcal{W}},y\vDash\varphi)
.

Write
{\mathcal{W}}\vDash\varphi
if
\forall x\in W\>{\mathcal{W}},x\vDash\varphi
.

For the axioms of
\mathrm{J}
to be valid in a given Kripke frame for any
valuation of variables, we impose some restrictions on the relations

R_{k}
. A transitive binary relation
R
on a set
W
is called

noetherian
, if there is no infinite chain of elements of
W

of the form
a_{0}Ra_{1}Ra_{2}\dots
Note that if
W
if finite the
condition of
R
being noetherian on

is equivalent to its
irreflexivity.

A frame
(W;R_{0},R_{1},\ldots)
is called a
\mathrm{J}
-frame
if

1.

R_{k}
is transitive and noetherian, for all
k\geqslant 0
;

2.

\forall x,y\>(xR_{n}y\Rightarrow\forall z\>(xR_{m}z\Leftrightarrow yR_{m}z))
, for
m<n
;

3.

\forall x,y,z\>(xR_{m}y\text{ and }yR_{n}z\Rightarrow xR_{m}z)
, for
m<n
.

A
\mathrm{J}
-model
is any Kripke model based on a
\mathrm{J}
-frame. By
induction on the derivation length one can easily prove the
following lemma.

Lemma 1

For any formula
\varphi
, if
\mathrm{J}\vdash\varphi
then

{\mathcal{W}}\vDash\varphi
, for any
\mathrm{J}
-model
{\mathcal{W}}
.

The converse statement, that is, the completeness theorem for
\mathrm{J}

with respect to the class of all (finite)
\mathrm{J}
-models, is proved in
ref.
[
7
]
. Let us call a
root
of a
\mathrm{J}
-frame
(W;R_{0},R_{1},\dots)
an
element

for which

\forall x\in W\>\exists k\geqslant 0\>rR_{k}x.

Proposition 2

For any formula
\varphi
, if
\mathrm{J}\nvdash\varphi
then
there is a finite
\mathrm{J}
-model
{\mathcal{W}}
with a root
r
such that

{\mathcal{W}},r\nvDash\varphi
.

Define

M^{+}(\varphi)=M(\varphi)\land\bigwedge_{i\leqslant n}[i]M(\varphi),

where

is the maximum modality number occurring in

, and

is the conjunction of all formulas of the form
[i]\psi\to[j]\psi
with
[i]\psi\in\mathit{Sub}(\varphi)
and

.
A relationship between
\mathrm{J}
and
\mathrm{GLP}
is stated in the following proposition (see

).

Proposition 3

\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\varphi
iff
\mathrm{J}\vdash M^{+}(\varphi)\to\varphi
.

In particular, this means that the derivability problem for
\mathrm{GLP}
is reducible to the one for
\mathrm{J}
.

Unifiers of
[1]p

Firstly, we notice that the formula
[1]p

Lemma 4

\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]([0]q\to q)

Proof

\boxtimes

Definition 4

\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash R(\varphi)\to\varphi

Define a sequence of formulas

Therefore, by (
1
) we obtain
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle 0\rangle^{n+1}\top\to\langle 0\rangle^{n+2}\top
whence by Löb’s axiom
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[0]^{n+1}\bot
, which is not the case.

\boxtimes

Corollary 10

For all
i,j>0
,
Q^{i}<Q^{j}
whenever
i<j
.

Proof
. If
i<j
then
Q^{i}\leqslant Q^{j}
, since
Q^{i}
follows from
i<j
instances of reflection. For the same reason,
\langle 0\rangle^{j}\top\leqslant Q^{j}
, yet by Lemma
9

\langle 0\rangle^{j}\top\nleq Q^{i}
. Otherwise,
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle 0\rangle^{j}\top\leftrightarrow Q^{i}(\varphi)
, for some
\varphi
, and

\langle 0\rangle^{j}\top
would follow from
i
instances of reflection
Q^{i}(\varphi)
contradicting the lemma.
\boxtimes

Corollary 11

For any
n>1
, the formula
[1]p
has no maximal unifiers in
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
. The unification type of
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is nullary.

The same corollary also holds for the full language of
\mathrm{GLP}
(with
\omega
-many modalities): a unifier of
[1]p
in
\mathrm{GLP}
is a unifier in
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
, for some
n
, and less general than some
Q^{k}
. On the other hand, since
\mathrm{GLP}_{1}
is just a notational variant of
\mathrm{GL}
, its unification type is finitary.

4
Unifiers of
[1]p
and the reduction property

A sightly simpler characterization of the set of unifiers of
[1]p
in
\mathrm{GLP}
can be obtained via the so-called
reduction property
.
The reduction property for free
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebras is established in
[
9
]
, its arithmetical analogues play a significant role in the applications of
\mathrm{GLP}
. We state here without proof a somewhat less general proposition that is a particular case of Lemma 6 of
[
9
]
.

Define a sequence of formulas
Q^{*}_{n}(p)
as follows:
Q^{*}_{1}(p):=p
,
Q^{*}_{n+1}(p):=p\land\langle 0\rangle Q^{*}_{n}(p)
. Clearly, for each
n
,
Q^{*}_{n}(p)
is equivalent to
\neg Q_{n}(\neg p)
in
\mathrm{GLP}
.

Proposition 12

Suppose
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle 1\rangle\varphi\to\langle 0\rangle\psi
. Then there is a
k
such that
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q^{*}_{k}(\varphi)\to\langle 0\rangle\psi
.

From this proposition we infer

Lemma 13

\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]\varphi
iff
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q_{k}(\varphi)
, for some
k
.

Proof
. Assume
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]\varphi
, then
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle 1\rangle\neg\varphi\to\bot
. Since
\bot
is equivalent to
\langle 0\rangle\bot
, Proposition
12
is applicable and we obtain
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q^{*}_{k}(\neg\varphi)\to\bot
, that is,
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q_{k}(\varphi)
, for some
k
.

In the other direction, assume
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q_{k}(\varphi)
, for some
k
. We prove
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]Q_{i}(\varphi)
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
, by downwards induction on
i
. For
i=k
the claim is obvious, assume
i<k
and the claim holds for
i+1
. Recall that
Q_{i+1}(\varphi)=\varphi\lor[0]Q_{i}(\varphi)
. Assume
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1](\varphi\lor[0]Q_{i}(\varphi))
. Then, since
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\varphi\to Q_{i}(\varphi)
, we have
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1](Q_{i}(\varphi)\lor[0]Q_{i}(\varphi))
.
By Lemma
4
we have
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]([0]Q_{i}(\varphi)\to Q_{i}(\varphi))
, therefore
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]Q_{i}(\varphi)
. Putting
i=1
we obtain
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\varphi
.

\boxtimes

A useful form of this lemma is

Corollary 14

\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[1]\varphi
iff
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow(Q_{k}(\varphi)\to\varphi)
, for some
k
.

Proof
. Clearly,
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash Q_{k}(\varphi)
holds iff
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\varphi\leftrightarrow(Q_{k}(\varphi)\to\varphi)
.
\boxtimes

Corollary 15

A substitution
\sigma
is a unifier of
[1]p
in
\mathrm{GLP}
iff, for some
k
,
\sigma\leqslant(Q_{k}(p)\to p)
.

Proof
. (only if) Putting
\varphi:=\sigma(p)
in the above corollary we obtain that
\sigma
is a unifier of
[1]p
iff
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\sigma(p)\leftrightarrow\sigma(Q_{k}(p)\to p)
, for some
k
. The latter implies
\sigma\leqslant(Q_{k}(p)\to p)
.

(if) By induction on
k
we show that all formulas
Q_{k}(p)\to p
are unifiers of
[1]p
. The statement clearly holds for
k=1
. To show
[1](Q_{k+1}(p)\to p)
it is sufficient to derive
[1]([0]Q_{k}(p)\to p)
. By Lemma
4
we have
[1]([0]Q_{k}(p)\to Q_{k}(p))
, and by the induction hypothesis
[1](Q_{k}(p)\to p)
. Using the syllogism rule under
[1]
we conclude
[1]([0]Q_{k}(p)\to p)
, as required.
\boxtimes

To round off the discussion, we mention without proof that the formulas
Q^{n}(p)
are, in fact, equivalent to
Q_{n+1}(p)\to p
in
\mathrm{GLP}
. Therefore, the two characterizations of the set of unifiers of
[1]p
are essentially the same.

5
The unification problem for
\mathrm{GLP}

The
unification problem
for a logic
L
is to determine, given a formula
\varphi
, whether it is unifiable in
L
or not. We show, in this section, that the unification problem for
\mathrm{GLP}
and its fragments
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is decidable.

First, observe that if
\varphi
is unifiable, then there is a unifier
\sigma
for
\varphi
in
L
such that
\sigma(p)
is a variable-free formula, for each variable
p
(for example, take any unifier
\theta
and consider
\sigma:=\tau\theta
where
\tau
substitutes
\top
for each variable). Therefore, unifiability of a formula can be tested on the variable-free fragment of
L
.

The variable-free fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}
and the associated Lindenbaum algebra are well-studied and play a prominent role in the proof-theoretic applications of
\mathrm{GLP}
, see
[
18
,
10
,
15
]
. Especially interesting for our purposes are two recent papers.

Pakhomov
[
24
]
proves that the first order theory of the Lindenbaum algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
of the variable-free fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is decidable. Unifiability of
\varphi(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})
(by a variable-free substitution) is equivalent to the truth of the existential statement

\exists x_{1}\>\dots\exists x_{m}\>T_{\varphi}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=1

in
{\mathcal{A}}_{n}
, where
T_{\varphi}
denotes the term, corresponding to
\varphi
. Thus, Pakhomov’s theorem entails

Theorem 2

The unification problem for
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is decidable.

The paper
[
24
]
is rather complicated and proves a much stronger result than what is really needed here. A simpler proof can be obtained on the basis of a recent paper by Aguilera and Pakhomov
[
1
]
. They show that the logic of variable-free substitutions of
\mathrm{GLP}
can be axiomatized by a system
\mathrm{GLP.3}
, a polymodal analogue of the logic
\mathrm{GL.3}
of finite irreflexive linear frames. In other words,
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\sigma(\varphi)
, for all variable-free substitutions
\sigma
, if and only if
\mathrm{GLP.3}\vdash\varphi
. They also show the logic
\mathrm{GLP.3}
to be decidable. We do not state an explicit axiomatization of
\mathrm{GLP.3}
here, it is sufficient to use its characterization as the logic of variable-free substitutions in
\mathrm{GLP}
.

Our characterization of unifiable formulas in
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
is the following result.

Theorem 3

Let
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)
. Then
\mathrm{GLP.3}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
iff
there is a variable-free substitution
\sigma:{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)\to{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
such that
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash\sigma(\varphi)
.

Notice that this reduces the unifiability problem for
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
(in the language
{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)
) to the derivability problem for
\mathrm{GLP.3}
in the language
{\mathcal{L}}(n+1,m)
. Our proof is based on certain common techniques for the variable-free fragment of
\mathrm{GLP}
.

Recall that
words
or
worms
are variable-free formulas obtained from
\top
by operations
\langle n\rangle
, for any
n<\omega
. It is well-known that the set of worms is closed under conjunction modulo provability in
\mathrm{GLP}

[
6
, Lemma 9]
.

Lemma 16

If
\varphi
in
{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
is variable-free, then
\langle 0\rangle\varphi
is
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
-equivalent to either
\bot
or a worm of the form
\langle 0\rangle A
, for some
A
in
{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
.

Proof
. We will infer this claim from some results in
[
6
]
(other ways of proving it are surely possible). Bring
\varphi
to a disjunctive normal form. Since
\langle 0\rangle
commutes with disjunction,
\langle 0\rangle\varphi
is equivalent to a disjunction of formulas of the form
\langle 0\rangle\psi
where
\psi
is a conjunction of worms and negated worms. Since conjunction of worms is equivalent to a worm,
[
6
, Lemma 10]
is applicable and yields that each of the formulas
\langle 0\rangle\psi
is equivalent to
\bot
or to a worm of the form
\langle 0\rangle A
. One can delete the disjuncts of the form
\bot
as long as the disjunction is not empty. By
[
6
, Corollary 6]
worms of the form
\langle 0\rangle A
are linearly ordered by implication in
\mathrm{GLP}
. So, the disjunction is equivalent to the weakest one of them.
\boxtimes

Lemma 17

For any variable-free formula
\varphi
in
{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
,

\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash\varphi\iff\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi.

Proof
. It is well-known that
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash\varphi
iff
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash[0]\varphi
iff
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash\neg[0]\varphi\leftrightarrow\bot
. By Lemma
16
, the latter holds if and only if
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash\neg[0]\varphi\leftrightarrow A
, for some worm
A\in{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
. However, for any such
A
we have
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to A
, which is readily seen by induction on the length of
A
. Thus, if
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash\varphi
then
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
.

In the other direction, if
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
then
\neg[0]\varphi
cannot be equivalent to
\bot
(since
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash[n]\bot
), therefore
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\nvdash\varphi
.
\boxtimes

Suppose
\varphi
is a variable-free formula of
{\mathcal{L}}(n+1,0)
. Let
\overline{\varphi}
denote the result of replacing every subformula of
\varphi
of the form
[n]\psi
by
\top
. Obviously,
\overline{\varphi}\in{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
.

Lemma 18

Suppose
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
, then
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\overline{\varphi}
.

Proof
. If
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
, then there is a node
\alpha
in the Ignatiev model
\mathcal{I}
such that
\mathcal{I},\alpha\vDash\langle n\rangle\top,\ [0]\varphi
. Let
\beta
be the uppermost node in which the formula
\langle n\rangle\top
is true, that is, the sequence of ordinals
(\omega_{n},\omega_{n-1},\dots,\omega,1,0,\dots)
where
\omega_{0}=1
and
\omega_{i+1}=\omega^{\omega_{i}}
for
i\geqslant 0
. We have
\alpha R_{0}\beta
or
\alpha=\beta
. By transitivity,
\mathcal{I},\beta\vDash[0]\varphi
. Moreover, for each variable-free
\psi\in{\mathcal{L}}(n+1,0)
and each
\gamma
such that
\beta R_{0}\gamma
, we have
\mathcal{I},\gamma\vDash\psi
iff
\mathcal{I},\gamma\vDash\overline{\psi}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{I},\beta\vDash[0]\overline{\varphi}
which implies the claim.
\boxtimes

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Suppose
\sigma
is a variable-free substitution such that
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash\sigma(\varphi).
Then
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash[0]\sigma(\varphi)
and
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to[0]\sigma(\varphi).
If
\mathrm{GLP.3}\vdash\langle n+1\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
then
\mathrm{GLP.3}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\sigma(\varphi)
. Therefore, we obtain
\mathrm{GLP.3}\vdash[n]\bot
, which is not the case.

In the other direction, suppose
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)
. If
\mathrm{GLP.3}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi
then by Aguilera and Pakhomov there is a variable-free substitution
\sigma:{\mathcal{L}}(n+1,m)\to{\mathcal{L}}(n+1,0)
such that
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\sigma(\varphi)
. By Lemma
18
this entails
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\overline{\sigma(\varphi)}
. However, since
[n]
does not occur in
\varphi
, the formula
\overline{\sigma(\varphi)}
has the form
\sigma^{\prime}(\varphi)
for some substitution
\sigma^{\prime}:{\mathcal{L}}(n,m)\to{\mathcal{L}}(n,0)
. It follows that
\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\sigma^{\prime}(\varphi)
and by Lemma
17

\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash\sigma^{\prime}(\varphi)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
3
.

6
Arithmetical unification and admissibility

In this section we make a few observations on the arithmetical analogues of the results of this paper. They are based on the standard interpretation of polymodal provability logic, according to which propositional formulas are interpreted as arithmetical sentences, and modalities
[k]
are interpreted as
k
-provability assertions over
\mathrm{PA}
.

We call an arithmetical formula
\varphi

k
-provable
, if
\varphi
is provable from the axioms of Peano arithmetic
\mathrm{PA}
and all
\Pi_{k}
-sentences true in the standard model of arithmetic. Let
\mathrm{Pr}_{k}(x)
denote a natural formalization of the statement that the formula with the Gödel number
x
is
k
-provable (see
[
18
,
5
]
for the details).

Let
\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{PA}}
denote the set of sentences in the language of
\mathrm{PA}
.

Definition 5

An
arithmetical realization
is a map
v:\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)\to\mathrm{St}_{\mathrm{PA}}
such that
v
commutes with boolean connectives and, for all
\varphi
and
k<n
,

v([k]\varphi)=\mathrm{Pr}_{k}(\ulcorner v(\varphi)\urcorner).

A formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
is called
arithmetically unifiable
if
\mathrm{PA}\vdash v(\varphi)
, for some arithmetical realization
v
. Such a
v
is then called an
arithmetical unifier
of
\varphi
.

An inference rule in the language of
\mathrm{GLP}
is called
arithmetically admissible
, if every arithmetical unifier of the premisses of the rule unifies its conclusion.

The analogy with the propositional unification is clear. Notice that both types of unification amount to the same notion applied to two different modal algebras: free GLP-algebras in case of propositional unification, and the provability GLP-algebra of
\mathrm{PA}
in case of arithmetical unification. Our goal here is to characterize arithmetically unifiable formulas and admissible rules. The key result here is the second arithmetical completeness theorem for
\mathrm{GLP}
due to G. Japaridze and K. Ignatiev.

Let
\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}
denote the closure under the modus ponens and substitution rules of the set of theorems of
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}
and all sentences of the form
[k]q\to q
, for
k<n
, that is,
\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}:=\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\{[k]q\to q:k<n\}
.

The following theorem is due to G. Japaridze
[
19
]
(for a somewhat different arithmetical interpretation) and K. Ignatiev
[
18
]
(for a more general class of interpretations including this one). See also
[
8
]
for a simplified proof.

Theorem 4

For any
n\leqslant\omega
and any modal formula
\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
,

\mathrm{(i)}

\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash\varphi
iff
\mathrm{PA}\vdash v(\varphi)
, for all arithmetical realizations
v
;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}\vdash\varphi
iff
\mathbb{N}\vDash v(\varphi)
, for all arithmetical realizations
v
.

Let
H(\varphi)
denote the formula

\bigwedge_{i<s}([n_{i}]\varphi_{i}\to\varphi_{i})
, where the formulas

[n_{i}]\varphi_{i}
, for
i<s
, enumerate all the subformulas of
\varphi
of
the form
[k]\psi
. Japaridze showed that
\mathrm{GLPS}\vdash\varphi
iff
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash H(\varphi)\to\varphi
, therefore the derivability problem for
\mathrm{GLPS}
is decidable (see
[
8
, Theorem 4]
).

As a corollary of Theorem
4
(ii) we obtain the following characterization.

Theorem 5

The following statements are equivalent, for any
n<\omega
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
,

\mathrm{(i)}

\varphi
is arithmetically unifiable;

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}\nvdash\neg[0]\varphi
;

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathrm{GLP}\nvdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi.

Proof
. It is more convenient to prove the equivalence of the negations of the three statements.
Assume (i) does not hold, then, for all arithmetical realizations
v
,
\mathrm{PA}\nvdash v(\varphi)
, hence
\mathbb{N}\vDash v(\neg[0]\varphi)
. Therefore, by Theorem
4
(ii),
\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}\vdash\neg[0]\varphi
. Hence, (ii) does not hold.

Assume (ii) does not hold, that is,
\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}\vdash\neg[0]\varphi
. We prove that
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash\langle n\rangle\top\to\neg[0]\varphi.

First, we remark that
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[n]([k]\psi\to\psi)
, for all
k<n
, by the same argument as in Lemma
4
. Since
\varphi\in\mathrm{Fm}(n,m)
, it follows that
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[n]H(\varphi)
. Therefore, if
\mathrm{GLPS}_{n}\vdash\neg[0]\varphi
, then
\mathrm{GLP}_{n}\vdash H(\varphi)\to\neg[0]\varphi
and
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[n]\neg[0]\varphi
. Then from
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[0]\varphi\to[n][0]\varphi
we obtain
\mathrm{GLP}\vdash[0]\varphi\to[n]\bot
, which is a contraposition of the claim.

Assume (iii) does not hold. Let
v
be any arithmetical realization. Then, since
\mathrm{GLP}
is arithmetically sound and
v(\langle n\rangle\top)
is true,
\mathbb{N}\vDash v(\neg[0]\varphi)
. Hence,
\mathrm{PA}\nvdash v(\varphi)
, that is,
\varphi
is not arithmetically unifiable.
\boxtimes

Theorem 6

A rule
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}/\varphi
in the language of
\mathrm{GLP}
is arithmetically admissible iff
\mathrm{GLPS}\vdash[0]\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land[0]\varphi_{n}\to[0]\varphi
.

Proof
. (if) Assume
v
is an arithmetical unifier of
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n}
, that is,
\mathrm{PA}\vdash v(\varphi_{i})
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. Then

\mathbb{N}\vDash v([0]\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land[0]\varphi_{n}).

By the soundness part of Theorem
4
(ii) we conclude that
\mathbb{N}\vDash v([0]\varphi)
and
\mathrm{PA}\vdash v(\varphi)
, as required.

(only if) Assume
\mathrm{GLPS}\nvdash[0]\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land[0]\varphi_{n}\to[0]\varphi
. Then by the completeness part of Theorem
4
(ii) there is an arithmetical realization
v
such that
\mathbb{N}\vDash v([0]\varphi_{1}\land\dots\land[0]\varphi_{n})
and
\mathbb{N}\nvDash v([0]\varphi)
. This means that
\mathrm{PA}\vdash v(\varphi_{i})
, for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, and
\mathrm{PA}\nvdash v(\varphi)
, that is,
v
unifies the premisses of the rule, but not the conclusion.
\boxtimes

Corollary 19

Arithmetical unification and admissibility problems for
\mathrm{GLP}
are decidable.

Finally, we make some remarks on the arithmetical analogues of the results in Section
3
. An
arithmetical unifier
of a formula
[1]p
can be identified with a sentence
A
such that
\mathrm{PA}\vdash\mathrm{Pr}_{1}(\ulcorner A\urcorner)
. Such formulas were studied in
[
22
]
and called
provably 1-provable
. One of the main results in
[
22
]
is a characterization of provably 1-provable formulas as theorems of the extension of
\mathrm{PA}
by
\varepsilon_{0}
-times iterated local reflection schema.

In this result, the ordinal
\varepsilon_{0}
reflects the fact that we deal with arithmetical interpretation over
\mathrm{PA}
. A weaker theory
\mathrm{EA}
as a base theory would yield essentially the same result as in Lemma
6
: Arithmetical unifiers of
[1]p
in
\mathrm{EA}
are theorems of the extension of
\mathrm{EA}
by the local reflection schema
\mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{EA}}(\ulcorner A\urcorner)\to A
, for all sentences
A
.

7
Open questions

A number of questions related to unification and admissibility in
\mathrm{GLP}
remain open. We gather some of the most natural ones for the record here.

We note that Ghilardi’s notion of
projective formula
makes sense in the context of
\mathrm{GLP}
. Similarly, de Jongh and Visser’s notion of
exact formula
equally makes sense. Exact formulas can be defined as the relators of finitely generated subalgebras of a free
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebra. The uniform Craig’s interpolation property for
\mathrm{GLP}

[
28
]
implies that every such finitely generated subalgebra is finitely presented, that is, exact formulas do exist (see a similar proof for
\mathrm{GL}
in
[
2
]
). Projective formulas are the relators of retracts of finitely generated free
\mathrm{GLP}
-algebras, hence every projective formula is exact.

Problem 1.

Are projective formulas the same as exact formulas in
\mathrm{GLP}
? For
\mathrm{GL}
both notions are known to be equivalent (though we do not know a published reference).

Problem 2.

Is there a characterization of projective formulas in
\mathrm{GLP}
in terms of operations on Kripke models or topological models?

For
\mathrm{GL}
it is well-known that a formula is projective iff it is preserved under the extension of any Kripke model downwards by a new root point (with a suitable evaluation at this point). N. Lukashov established a similar characterization for the polymodal logic J.

Problem 3.

Is there a reasonable description of the set of all unifiers of a given formula in
\mathrm{GLP}
?

Obviously, we cannot give a description in terms of finitely many maximal unifiers. However, we have a hope for some more general kind of characterization.

Problem 4.

Is the admissibility problem for
\mathrm{GLP}
decidable?

Perturbative Symmetry Approach for Differential-Difference Equations.
Alexander V. Mikhailov, Vladimir S. Novikov, Jing Ping Wang + + School of Mathematics, University of Leeds, UK ∗ * P. G. Demidov Yaroslavl State University, RF † \dagger School of Mathematics, Loughborough University, UK & \& School of Mathematics, Statistics and Actuarial Science, University of Kent, UK

Abstract
We propose a new method for solution of the integrability problem for evolutionary differential-difference equations of arbitrary order. It enables us to produce necessary integrability conditions, to determine whether a given equation is integrable or not, and to advance in classification of integrable equations. In this paper we define and develop symbolic representation for the difference polynomial ring, difference operators and formal series. In order to formulate necessary integrability conditions, we introduce a novel quasi-local extension of the difference ring. It enables us to progress in classification of integrable differential-difference evolutionary equations of arbitrary order. In particular, we solve the problem of classification of integrable equations of order ( − 3 , 3 ) (-3,3) for the important subclass of quasi-linear equations and produce a list of 17 equations satisfying the necessary integrability conditions. For every equation from the list we present an infinite family of integrable higher order relatives. Some of the equations obtained are new.

The problem to determine whether a given equation is integrable (testing for integrability) and much more difficult problem to give an exhaustive description of all integrable cases for a certain type of equations up to invertible transformations (the classification problem) are central in the theory of integrable systems. 2
Derivations, difference operators and formal series

The main objects of our study are evolutionary differential-difference equations and algebras of their symmetries. Although the phase space of such systems is infinite dimensional, each equation of the system relates a finite number of dynamical variables which we will treat as independent. In order to develop a rigorous theory we will use elements of the theory of difference rings, difference operators and formal series, including their symbolic representation. It will also enable us to introduce quasi-local extensions of the difference rings and formulate verifiable integrability conditions for differential-difference equations.

2.1
Difference ring and its evolutionary derivations

We define the polynomial ring
{\mathcal{R}}=\mathbb{C}[{\bf u}]
and the corresponding field of fractions
{\mathcal{F}}=\mathbb{C}({\bf u})
of the infinite set of (commutative) variables
{\bf u}=\{u_{n}\,|\,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. We will often omit index zero at
u_{0}
. The set of all monomials
{\mathcal{M}on({\mathcal{R}})}=\{u_{n_{1}}^{m_{1}}u_{n_{2}}^{m_{2}}\cdots u_{n_{k}}^{m_{k}}\,|\,n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\ m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\}
is the additive basis in
{\mathcal{R}}
.

There is a natural automorphism
{\mathcal{S}}
of the field
{\mathcal{F}}
,
which we call the
shift operator
, defined as

{\mathcal{S}}:a(u_{k},\ldots,u_{r})\mapsto a(u_{k+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}),\quad{\mathcal{S}}:\alpha\mapsto\alpha,\qquad a(u_{k},\ldots,u_{r})\in{\mathcal{F}},\ \ \alpha\in\mathbb{C}.

The field
{\mathcal{F}}
equipped with the automorphism
{\mathcal{S}}
is a
difference field.

The reflection

{\mathcal{T}}
of the lattice
\mathbb{Z}
defined by

{\mathcal{T}}:a(u_{k},\ldots,u_{r})\mapsto a(u_{-k},\ldots,u_{-r}),\quad{\mathcal{T}}:\alpha\mapsto\alpha,\qquad a(u_{k},\ldots,u_{r})\in{\mathcal{F}},\ \ \alpha\in\mathbb{C},

is another automorphism of
{\mathcal{F}}
. The
composition
{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{T}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathcal{T}}=\mbox{Id}
is the identity map. Thus the automorphisms

{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{T}}
generate the infinite dihedral group
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
and the infinite cyclic subgroup
generated by
{\mathcal{S}}
is normal in
\mathbb{D}_{\infty}
.
The automorphism
{\mathcal{T}}
defines a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
grading of the difference field
{\mathcal{F}}
:

{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\qquad{\mathcal{F}}_{0}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}_{0}={\mathcal{F}}_{0},\ \ {\mathcal{F}}_{0}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}_{1}={\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ \ {\mathcal{F}}_{1}\cdot{\mathcal{F}}_{1}={\mathcal{F}}_{0},

(7)

where
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}=\{a\in{\mathcal{F}}\,|\,{\mathcal{T}}(a)=(-1)^{k}a\}
. The ring
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{F}}
inherits the same grading
{\mathcal{R}}={\mathcal{R}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{R}}_{1}
.

A derivation
\partial
in the field
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
\mathbb{C}
–linear map
\partial\,:\,{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}
satisfying Leibniz’s law

\partial(a\cdot b)=\partial(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\partial(b),\qquad a,b\in{\mathcal{F}}.

A set of all derivations in
{\mathcal{F}}
is denoted
{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
.
Let
\partial,\partial^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
be two derivations in
{\mathcal{F}}
then their commutator
[\partial,\partial^{\prime}]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\partial\circ\partial^{\prime}-\partial^{\prime}\circ\partial\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
is also a derivation. Any three derivations
\partial,\partial^{\prime},\partial^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
satisfy the Jacobi identity

[\partial,[\partial^{\prime},\partial^{\prime\prime}]]+[\partial^{\prime},[\partial^{\prime\prime},\partial]]+[\partial^{\prime\prime},[\partial,\partial^{\prime}]]=0,

and therefore
{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
is a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{C}
. A formal sum

X=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{(n)}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{n}},\qquad f^{(n)}\in{\mathcal{F}}

(8)

is a derivation in
{\mathcal{F}}
. Its action on
X:{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}
is well defined, since any element
a\in{\mathcal{F}}
depends on a finite subset of variables, and thus the sum
X(a)
contains only a finite number of non-vanishing terms.

Partial derivatives
\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
are
commuting derivations satisfying the conditions

{\mathcal{S}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i+1}}{\mathcal{S}},\qquad{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{-i}}{\mathcal{T}}.

(9)

A derivation
X\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
is said to be
evolutionary
if it commutes with the shift operator
{\mathcal{S}}
. For an evolutionary derivation it follows from the condition
X\circ{\mathcal{S}}={\mathcal{S}}\circ X
and (
9
) that all coefficients
f^{(n)}
in (
8
) can be expressed
f^{(n)}={\mathcal{S}}^{n}(f)
in terms of one element
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
, which is called the
characteristic
of the evolutionary derivation. We will use notation

X_{f}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(f)\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}.

(10)

for the evolutionary derivation corresponding to the characteristic
f
.

Evolutionary derivations form a Lie subalgebra of
{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
. Indeed,

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha X_{f}+\beta X_{g}=X_{\alpha f+\beta g},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},\\
\phantom{}[X_{f},X_{g}]=X_{[f,g]},\end{array}

where
[f,g]\in{\mathcal{F}}
denotes the Lie bracket

[f,g]=X_{f}(g)-X_{g}(f),

(11)

which is bi-linear, skew-symmetric and satisfying the Jacobi identity. Thus
{\mathcal{F}}
is a Lie algebra with Lie bracket defined by (
11
). Evolutionary derivations with characteristics belonging to the ring
{\mathcal{R}}
form a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{R}})}
.

The reflection
{\mathcal{T}}
acts naturally on evolutionary derivations

{\mathcal{T}}:\,X_{f}\mapsto X_{{\mathcal{T}}(f)}={\mathcal{T}}\cdot X_{f}\cdot{\mathcal{T}}\,

The polynomial ring
{\mathcal{R}}
is degree graded

{\mathcal{R}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{R}}^{n},

(12)

where
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}
is the set of all homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
. The degree grading (
12
) and
{\mathcal{T}}
–grading (
7
) are compatible and thus
{\mathcal{R}}
is a bi-graded ring

{\mathcal{R}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{1},\quad{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{k}={\mathcal{R}}^{n}\cap{\mathcal{R}}_{k}\,.

(13)

The homogeneous parts
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{k}
are additive groups and
{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{k}{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{\ell}={\mathcal{R}}^{n+m}_{(k+\ell)\mod 2}
.
It follows from the definition of the Lie bracket (
11
) that the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{R}}
is also degree graded and bi-graded

[{\mathcal{R}}^{n},{\mathcal{R}}^{m}]\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{n+m-1}\,,\quad[{\mathcal{R}}^{n}_{p},{\mathcal{R}}^{m}_{q}]\subset{\mathcal{R}}^{n+m-1}_{(p+q)\mod 2}.

(14)

Every element
f
of the polynomial ring
{\mathcal{R}}
can be uniquely represented as a sum of homogeneous components
f=\sum_{k\geqslant 0}f^{(k)},\ f^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{k}
(some of the components may be equal to zero). The selection of the
k
–th homogeneous component
f^{(k)}
is a projection
\pi_{k}\,:\,{\mathcal{R}}\mapsto{\mathcal{R}}^{k}
defined by

\pi_{k}(f)=\pi_{k}\left(\sum_{i\geqslant 0}f^{(i)}\right)=f^{(k)}.

(15)

Obviously
\pi_{k}\pi_{s}=\delta_{s,k}\pi_{k}
. Similarly one can define projectors to bi-graded homogeneous components.

2.2
Difference, pseudo-difference operators and formal series

Let
a(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})
be a non-constant element of
{\mathcal{F}}
, and we assume that
n\leqslant m
are the minimal and the maximal index respectively in the sequence of its arguments. Then
X_{f}(a)
can be represented by a finite sum

X_{f}(a)=\sum_{i=n}^{m}\frac{\partial a}{\partial u_{i}}{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(f)=a_{*}[f],

where

a_{*}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{i=n}^{m}\frac{\partial a}{\partial u_{i}}{\mathcal{S}}^{i}

(16)

is the
Fréchet derivative
of
a(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})
and
a_{*}[f]
is the Fréchet derivative of
a
in the direction
f
.
Using the Fréchet derivative we can represent the Lie bracket (
11
) in the form:

[f\,,\,g]=g_{*}[f]-f_{*}[g].

(17)

It is obvious that

({\mathcal{S}}a)_{*}={\mathcal{S}}\cdot a_{*}=\sum_{i=n}^{m}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{\partial a}{\partial u_{i}}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{i+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\mathcal{T}}a)_{*}={\mathcal{T}}\cdot a_{*}=\sum_{i=n}^{m}{\mathcal{T}}\left(\frac{\partial a}{\partial u_{i}}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{-i}.

Definition 1
.

A difference operator
B
of order
{\rm ord}\,B:=(n,m)
with
coefficients in

{\mathcal{F}}
is a finite sum of the form

B=b^{(m)}{\mathcal{S}}^{m}+b^{(m-1)}{\mathcal{S}}^{m-1}+\cdots+b^{(n)}{\mathcal{S}}^{n},\quad b^{(m)}b^{(n)}\neq 0,\ b^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{F}},\ \ n\leq m,\ n,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(18)

The total order of
B
is defined as
{\rm Ord}B=m-n
. The total order of
the zero
operator is minus infinity
{\rm Ord}\,\,0:=-\infty
by definition.

The Fréchet derivative (
16
) is an example of a difference operator
of order
(n,m)
and total order
{\rm Ord}\,a_{*}=m-n
. For a non-constant element

f\in{\mathcal{F}}
the order and total
order are defined as
{\rm ord}\,f_{*}
and
{\rm Ord}\,f_{*}
respectively.

Difference operators form a unital ring
{{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}]}
of
Laurent
polynomials in
{\mathcal{S}}
with coefficients in
{\mathcal{F}}
,
where multiplication is defined by

a{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\cdot b{\mathcal{S}}^{m}=a{\mathcal{S}}^{n}(b){\mathcal{S}}^{n+m}=ab_{n}{\mathcal{S}}^{n+m}.

(19)

This multiplication is associative, but non-commutative.

From the above definition it follows that if
A
is a difference operator of
order
{\rm ord}\,A=(p,q)
, then
{\rm ord}\,({\mathcal{S}}^{n}\cdot A\cdot{\mathcal{S}}^{m})=(p+n+m,q+n+m)
and the total order
{\rm Ord}\,({\mathcal{S}}^{n}\cdot A\cdot{\mathcal{S}}^{m})={\rm Ord}\,A=q-p
. For any
A,B\in{{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}]}
we have
{\rm Ord}\,(AB)={\rm Ord}\,A+{\rm Ord}\,B
.

For a difference operator
B
given by (
18
), we define its adjoint operator
B^{\dagger}
as

B^{\dagger}={\mathcal{S}}^{-m}\cdot b^{(m)}+{\mathcal{S}}^{-m+1}\cdot b^{(m-1)}+\cdots+{\mathcal{S}}^{-n}\cdot b^{(n)}.

Note that
{\rm ord}\ B^{\dagger}=(-m,-n)
and
{\rm Ord}\ B^{\dagger}={\rm Ord}\ B
.

Definition 2
.

A rational (pseudo–difference) operator

is defined as
M=AB^{-1}

for some

and

. The set of all

{\mathfrak{F}}=\{AB^{-1}\,|\,A,B\in{{\mathcal{F}}[{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}]},\ B\neq 0\}.

Definition 3
.

The sets

of Laurent and
{\mathfrak{F}}_{M}
of Maclaurin formal difference series with coefficients in

are

{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}=\left\{\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}a^{(-n)}{\mathcal{S}}^{-n}\ |\ a^{(m)}\in{\mathcal{F}},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\},\quad{\mathfrak{F}}_{M}=\left\{\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}a^{(n)}{\mathcal{S}}^{n}\ |\ a^{(m)}\in{\mathcal{F}},\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\right\}.

These sets equipped with addition and composition rules for difference operators have structure of skew fields

]
.
The skew field
{\mathfrak{F}}
is a minimal subfield of the skew fields

and

,
containing

A rational operator can be expanded in the Maclaurin or Laurent series and thus represented by an element of

or

respectively.
The skew fields

and

are isomorphic. The

.

For

3
Perturbative symmetry approach

With an evolutionary differential-difference equation

u_{t}=F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m}),\qquad F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})\in{\mathcal{F}}

(20)

we associate the evolutionary derivation
X_{F}\in{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
(the vector field corresponding to the dynamical system (
20
)). Thus, there is a bijection between evolutionary derivations of
{\mathcal{F}}
and differential-difference equations.

Derivation
X_{F}
enables us to differentiate any element
a\in{\mathcal{F}}
in the direction of
F
. It follows from the chain rule that
(
20
)

a_{t}=a_{*}[F]=X_{F}(a).

(21)

In what follow we will study
generators of infinitesimal symmetries
of evolutionary equations and for brevity will call them symmetries.

Definition 4
.

We say that
G(u_{p},\ldots,u_{q})\in{\mathcal{F}}
is a symmetry of
(
20
) if
[G,F]=0.

If
G
is a symmetry of (
20
), equation (
20
) is invariant
{\rm mod}\,\epsilon^{2}
under the near identity transformation
\hat{u}=u+\epsilon G
. The evolution equation associated to this symmetry is

u_{\tau}=G(u_{p},\ldots,u_{q}),

(22)

which is compatible with (
20
). These can be used as equivalent definitions for symmetry.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be any two symmetries of equation (
20
). It follows immediately from the Definition
4
and the Jacobi identity that the Lie bracket
G_{3}=[G_{1},G_{2}]
is also a symmetry of (
20
). Thus, symmetries of equation (
20
) form a subalgebra of the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{D}er({\mathcal{F}})}
which will be denoted
{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}

{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{G\in{\mathcal{F}}\;|\;[F,G]=0\}.

The existence of an infinite dimensional commutative Lie algebra of symmetries is a characteristic property of integrable systems.

Definition 5
.

A differential-difference equation
u_{t}=F
(
20
) is called integrable if its Lie algebra of symmetries
{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}
is infinite dimensional and contains symmetries of arbitrary high total order.

An infinite hierarchy of commuting symmetries of an integrable differential-difference equation (
20
) can be constructed using a recursion operator
\Lambda
(if
\Lambda
is known), namely,
G_{k}=\Lambda(G_{k-1})
, where
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{F}}
is a rational pseudo-difference operator with coefficients in
{\mathcal{F}}
satisfying the equation

X_{F}(\Lambda)-[F_{*},\Lambda]=0.

(23)

Example 1
.

The Volterra equation

u_{t}=F,\ \ F=u_{1}u-uu_{-1},\ \ F_{*}=u{\mathcal{S}}+u_{1}-u_{-1}-u{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}

has order
{\rm ord}(F)=(-1,1)
and total order
{\rm Ord}(F)=2

\Lambda=AB^{-1},\qquad A=u({\mathcal{S}}+1)(u{\mathcal{S}}-{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}u),\ B=u({\mathcal{S}}-1)

generates the infinite hierarchy of symmetries
G_{k}=\Lambda^{k}(F)

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda(u_{1}u-uu_{-1})=A(1-{\mathcal{S}})^{-1}(u_{1}-u_{-1})=A(u+u_{-1}+\gamma_{1})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(u_{1}u_{2}+u_{1}^{2}+uu_{1}-uu_{-1}-u_{-1}^{2}-u_{-2}u_{-1})+\gamma_{1}F,

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}(u_{1}u-uu_{-1})=u(u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}+u_{1}u_{2}^{2}+2u_{1}^{2}u_{2}+uu_{1}u_{2}+u_{1}^{3}+2uu_{1}^{2}+u^{2}u_{1}

(25)

\displaystyle-u^{2}u_{-1}-2uu_{-1}^{2}-u_{-1}^{3}-uu_{-1}u_{-2}-2u_{-1}^{2}u_{-2}-u_{-1}u_{-2}^{2}-u_{-1}u_{-2}u_{-3})+\gamma_{1}G_{1}+\gamma_{2}F,\ldots

where

To find a recursion operator for a given equation is a difficult problem. There is a regular way to solve it if the equation has a Lax representation. A discussion of this problem and many explicit examples of pseudo-difference recursion operators can be found in

3.1
Testing for integrability: symmetry approach

The goal of the symmetry approach is to find necessary conditions for the existence of an infinite dimensional Lie algebra of symmetries for a given equation. Originally, it was proposed and developed by A.B. Shabat and his team, and applied to study of partial differential equations
[
1
]
-
[
3
]
. The formalism enables them to develop an explicit test for integrability and solve a number of classification problems by producing complete lists of integrable partial differential equations. Later on the symmetry approach has been extended to some non-evolutionary PDEs
[
10
]
, integro-differential equations
[
8
,
9
]
and recently to partial-difference and differential-difference equations
[
17
,
16
]
. The method is inspired by the observation that the existence of an infinite algebra of symmetries implies existence of a formal solution
\Lambda
of equation (
23
) in terms of a formal series. The conditions of solvability of the equation can be explicitly formulated and provide us with necessary conditions of integrability.

In the case of differential difference equations, the only result in this aspect so far is obtained by Adler
[
16
]
.

Theorem 1

(Adler
[
16
]
)
.

If an evolutionary differential-difference equation

u_{t}=F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m}),\qquad F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})\in{\mathcal{F}},

(26)

admits symmetries

u_{\tau}=G(u_{p},\ldots,u_{q})

(27)

with
q
arbitrarily large then equation (
23
) admits a solution
\Lambda_{L}\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{L}
of the
form

\Lambda_{L}=F_{*}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a^{(-k)}{\mathcal{S}}^{-k},\qquad\ a^{(-k)}\in{\mathcal{F}}.

If equation (
26
) admits symmetries (
27
) with

\ -p

arbitrarily large then equation (
23
) admits a solution
\Lambda_{M}\in{\mathfrak{F}}_{M}
of the
form

\Lambda_{M}=F_{*}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b^{(k)}{\mathcal{S}}^{k},\qquad\ b^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{F}}.

We call the solutions
\Lambda_{L}
and
\Lambda_{M}
of (
23
) formal recursion operators (formal symmetries in
[
16
]
) for the equation.
Let us consider the implications from the first part of the above statement. We try to find a formal series
\Lambda_{L}
satisfying equation (
23
) for a function
F=F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})
. The substitution of
\Lambda_{L},F,F_{*}
in (
23
) results in a formal Laurent series, each coefficient of which should vanish. Collecting the coefficients at the powers
{\mathcal{S}}^{m-k},\ k=0,1,\ldots
we obtain a triangular system of difference equations to determine the coefficients
a^{(-k)}
of
\Lambda_{L}
. This system is of the form:

{\mathcal{S}}^{m-k}:\ \ {\mathcal{M}}_{m,k}(a^{(-k)})=C_{m,k},\qquad\qquad k=0,1,\ldots

(28)

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{m,k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{m}}{\mathcal{S}}^{m}-{\mathcal{S}}^{-k}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{m}}\right)

and the terms
C_{m,k}
depend on the function
F(u_{n},\ldots,u_{m})
, its partial derivatives and the coefficients
a^{(0)},a^{(-1)},\ldots,a^{(1-k)}
only. For example

\displaystyle C_{m,0}=X_{F}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{m}}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=n}^{m}{\mathcal{S}}^{i}(F)\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial u_{i}\partial u_{m}},

(29)

\displaystyle C_{m,1}=X_{F}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{m-1}}+a^{(0)}\delta_{m,1}\right)+\frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{m-1}}\left(a^{(0)}-{\mathcal{S}}^{m-1}(a^{(0)})\right).

(30)

According to Theorem
1
, for integrable equation (
20
) a solution of the triangular system (
28
) exists and
a^{(-k)}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Thus the necessary integrability conditions for equation (
20
) are conditions of solvability in
{\mathcal{F}}
of the system (
28
) with respect to the coefficients
a^{(-k)}
. The latter can be formulated as the conditions that
C_{m,k}
belong to the image spaces of the linear difference operators
{\mathcal{M}}_{m,k}
, i.e.,

C_{m,k}\in\mbox{Im}{\mathcal{M}}_{m,k},\ k=0,1,\ldots
.
In the case
k=0
it reduces to the problem of membership in the space
\mbox{Im}\,({\mathcal{S}}-1)
, which has a well known solution. Namely, if
a\in\mathbb{C}\bigoplus\mbox{Im}\,({\mathcal{S}}-1)
then
\delta_{u}(a)=0
. Here
\delta_{u}
is the variational derivative

\delta_{u}(a)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{S}}^{-n}\left(\frac{\partial a}{\partial u_{n}}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{0}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathcal{S}}^{n}(a).

There is an algorithmic way to solve the membership problem for the spaces
\mbox{Im}{\mathcal{M}}_{m,k}
and if
C_{m,k}\in\mbox{Im}{\mathcal{M}}_{m,k},\ k=0,1,\ldots
to find the coefficients
a^{(-k)}\in{\mathcal{F}}
recursively.

Example 2
.

Let us consider equation (
20
) with
F=f(u)(u_{1}-u_{-1})
, where
f(u)\in{\mathcal{F}}
. In this case
m=1,\ \frac{\partial F}{\partial u_{1}}=f(u)
and

C_{1,0}=X_{F}(f(u))=f^{\prime}(u)f(u)(u_{1}-u_{-1}),\quad{\mathcal{M}}_{1,0}=f(u)({\mathcal{S}}-1).

The first necessary integrability condition
C_{1,0}\in\mbox{Im}\,{\mathcal{M}}_{1,0}
leads to

f^{\prime}(u)(u_{1}-u_{-1})\in\mbox{Im}\,({\mathcal{S}}-1).

Thus

\delta_{u}\left(f^{\prime}(u)(u_{1}-u_{-1})\right)=f^{\prime\prime}(u)u_{1}+{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}(f^{\prime}(u))-f^{\prime\prime}(u)u_{-1}-{\mathcal{S}}(f^{\prime}(u))=0

Taking the partial derivative with respect to
u_{1}
we get
f^{\prime\prime}(u)={\mathcal{S}}(f^{\prime\prime}(u))
, therefore
f^{\prime\prime}(u)
is a constant (an element of
\mathbb{C}
) and thus

f(u)=\alpha u^{2}+\beta u+\gamma
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
are arbitrary constants. The resulting equation

u_{t}=(\alpha u^{2}+\beta u+\gamma)(u_{1}-u_{-1})

is known to be integrable.

In this example the first integrability condition enables us to give a complete description of all integrable differential-difference equations of the form
u_{t}=f(u)(u_{1}-u_{-1})
.

In Theorem
1
the order of local formal recursion operators depends on the given equation and there is no simple way to find expressions for the coefficients
a^{(-k)},b^{(k)}
explicitly. In Section
5
using symbolic representation we will proof the existence of the universal (canonical) quasi-local formal recursion operators and present explicit recursive formulae for its coefficients.

3.2
Approximate symmetries and integrability

Approximate symmetries proved to be successful in the case of partial differential and integro-differential equations
[
8
,
9
]
. Our definition of approximate symmetries is based on the degree grading of the polynomial ring
{\mathcal{R}}
defined by (
12
).

In order to define approximate symmetries let us consider an evolutionary differential-difference equations

u_{t}=F,\qquad F\in{\mathcal{R}}.

(31)

Then
F
can be uniquely represented as a sum of its homogeneous components

F=F^{(0)}+\ldots+F^{(N)},

where
F^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{k}
or zero,
N
is the degree of the polynomial
F
. It follows from Definition
4
that a polynomial
G=G^{(0)}+\ldots+G^{(M)}
is a symmetry if the Lie bracket
[F,G]=0
vanishes. Due to the degree grading structure of the Lie algebra (
14
), the latter is equivalent to a sequence of homogeneous equations

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{p}[F^{(k)},G^{(p-k)}]=0,\qquad p=0,1,2,\ldots,N+M\,.

(32)

Here we assume that
F^{(k)}=0
if
k\not\in\{0,\ldots N\}
and
G^{(k)}=0
if
k\not\in\{0,\ldots M\}
. If all
N+M+1
equations are satisfied, then
G
is a symmetry. If in the (
32
) the first
s+2
equations with
0\leqslant p\leqslant s+1
are satisfied, then

G
is called
s
–approximate symmetry
(or
approximate symmetry of degree
s
) of the equation (we ignore the rest equations). It follows from the Jacobi identity that the Lie bracket of two
s
–approximate symmetries is also a
s
–approximate symmetry and therefore approximate symmetries of degree
s
form a closed Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}^{s}

{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}^{s}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{G\in{\mathcal{R}}\;|\;\pi_{k}([F,G])=0\ \mbox{for}\ k=0,1,\ldots,s\}.

In other words, the move from symmetries to approximate symmetries of degree
s
is the transition from definitions and computations made in terms of the ring
{\mathcal{R}}
to the quotient ring
{\mathcal{R}}\diagup{\mathcal{I}}^{s+1}
, where

{\mathcal{I}}=\langle{\bf u}\rangle\subset{\mathcal{R}}

(33)

is the maximal ideal generated by the variables
{\bf u}
. Namely, we say that
G\in{\mathcal{R}}
is

s
–approximate symmetry of equation (
31
) if
[F,G]\in{\mathcal{I}}^{s+1}
. We will also need difference operators and formal series with coefficients in the quotient rings
{\mathcal{R}}\diagup{\mathcal{I}}^{s+1}
, but their definitions will be more natural in symbolic representation which will be introduced in the next section.

Remark 1
.

There are no obstructions to replace the polynomial ring
{\mathcal{R}}=\mathbb{C}[{\bf u}]
by the ring
\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}
of formal series

\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}=\mathbb{C}[[{\bf u}]]=\{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f^{(k)}\,|\,f^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{k}\}

(34)

in variables
{\bf u}
and consider equations and their symmetries given by the Maclaurin expansion or just formal series. The above definition of
s
–approximate symmetries remains correct since only a finite number of equations (
32
)

\pi_{k}([F,G])=0,\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,s,\quad F,G\in\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}

has to be verified.

Definition 6
.

A differential-difference equation
u_{t}=F,\ F\in\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}
is called
s
–approximate integrable if its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}_{F}^{s}
is infinite dimensional and contains
s
–approximate symmetries of arbitrary high total order. The equation is called formally integrable, if it is
s
–approximate integrable for arbitrary large
s
.

Compare Definitions
5
and
6
we conclude that integrabe equations are formally integrable. The converse may not be true since we do not assume any convergence of formal series for the equation and its formal symmetries. Conditions of
s
–approximate integrability can be explicitly written and verified. They are strong necessary conditions for integrability and proved to be suitable for classification of integrable equation.
In next section we will formulate criteria of
s
–approximate integrability using symbolic representation, which enable us to test for integrability of a given equation as well as to progress in solution of classification problem for integrable differential-difference equations.

In what follows, we will restrict ourselves by equations
u_{t}=F
and symmetries
G
without a constant term (
\pi_{0}(F)=0,\ \pi_{0}(G)=0
). Let
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}
denote a subspace of formal series without a constant term

{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(1-\pi_{0})\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}=\bigoplus\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\mathcal{R}}^{n}\,.

(35)

Having
N
linearly independent symmetries containing a constant term we can always construct
N-1
linear combinations without constant term. A constant term in
F
can often be removed by a simple invertible transformation of variables. Thus the restriction to the linear space
{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}
(
35
) of formal series with no constant terms does not really affect the generality, but considerably reduces unnecessary stipulations in each case.

Symbolic representation is widely used in theory of pseudo-differential operators. It has been
first applied in the area of integrable systems by Gel’fand and Dickey
[
4
]
and
further developed in works of Beukers, Sanders & Wang
[
5

6
]
and Mikhailov & Novikov
[
8

9

to the ring of difference polynomials, difference operators and formal series.
Similar to the differential case, the symbolic representation can be viewed as a simplified notation for a Fourier transform (

4.1
Symbolic representation of difference polynomials

To define the symbolic representation
\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}=\oplus\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}^{n}
of the degree graded difference
ring (and Lie algebra)
\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}=\oplus{\mathcal{R}}^{n}
of formal series, we first define
an isomorphism of the
\mathbb{C}
–linear spaces
\varphi:{\mathcal{R}}^{n}\mapsto\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}^{n}
and then extend it to
the difference ring and Lie algebra isomorphism equipping
\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}
with the
multiplication, derivation and Lie bracket. The
isomorphism of the
\mathbb{C}
–linear spaces
\varphi:{\mathcal{R}}^{n}\mapsto\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}^{n}
is uniquely defined by its action
on monomials.

Definition 7
.

The symbolic form of a difference monomial terms is defined as

\varphi\,:\,\alpha\mapsto\alpha,\quad\varphi\,:\,\alpha u_{i_{1}}u_{i_{2}}\cdots u_{i_{n}}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{n}\ \mapsto\ \hat{u}^{n}\alpha\langle\xi_{1}^{i_{1}}\xi_{2}^{i_{2}}\cdots\xi_{n}^{i_{n}}\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{n}}\in{\hat{\cal R}}^{n},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{C},

where
\langle\cdot\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{n}}
denotes the average over the permutation group

{{\Sigma}_{n}}
of
n
elements
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}
:

\langle a(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{n})\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{n}}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in{\Sigma}_{n}}a(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,\xi_{\sigma(n)}).

Notations
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots
are reserved for the variables in symbolic representation. We will omit
{{\Sigma}_{n}}
in the group average
\langle\cdot\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{n}}
, assuming that the average is taken over the permutation group of
n
variables
\xi_{k}
where
n
is shown as the degree of
\hat{u}^{n}
.

A few examples:

\displaystyle u_{k}{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\hat{u}\xi_{1}^{k},\ \ \ u^{n}{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\hat{u}^{n},\ \ \ u_{1}u_{2}{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\hat{u}^{2}\langle\xi_{1}\xi_{2}^{2}\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{2}}=\frac{\hat{u}^{2}}{2}(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}\xi_{2}),

\displaystyle\alpha uu_{p}^{2}+\beta u_{q}^{3}{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\hat{u}^{3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{3}(\xi_{2}^{p}\xi_{3}^{p}+\xi_{1}^{p}\xi_{2}^{p}+\xi_{1}^{p}\xi_{3}^{p})+\beta\xi_{1}^{q}\xi_{2}^{q}\xi_{3}^{q}\right),\ \ \alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}.

With this isomorphism a homogeneous polynomial
f\in{\mathcal{R}}^{n}
is in one-to-one correspondence with a term
\hat{f}=\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in{\hat{\cal R}}^{n}
in which the coefficient function
a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
is
n
-variable symmetric Laurent polynomials, i.e.,

a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Xi_{n},
where
\Xi_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\xi_{1}^{-1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n}^{-1}]^{\Sigma_{n}}
.

The projector
\pi_{k}
(
15
) selects the
k
-th homogeneous component of an element
f\in\bar{{\mathcal{R}}},\ \pi_{k}(f)\in{\mathcal{R}}^{k}
. Its symbolic representation
\hat{\pi}_{k}
is induced by the condition
\hat{\pi}_{k}\varphi=\varphi\pi_{k}
. Let

f=\sum_{n\geqslant 0}f^{(n)}\in\bar{{\mathcal{R}}},\quad\pi_{k}(f)=f^{(k)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{k},\quad\varphi(f^{(n)})=\hat{u}^{n}a_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),

then

\hat{\pi}_{k}\left(\sum_{n\geqslant 0}\hat{u}^{n}a_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\right)=\hat{u}^{k}a_{k}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}).

The action of automorphisms
{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{T}}
in symbolic representation is given by

{\mathcal{S}}(f)\quad{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\quad\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})\,,\quad{\mathcal{T}}(f)\quad{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\quad\hat{u}^{n}a(1/\xi_{1},\ldots,1/\xi_{n})\,,

and thus
\varphi\,:\,{\mathcal{S}}^{k}(f)\ \mapsto\ \hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{n})^{k}
.

Definition 8
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{R}}^{n},\ \varphi(f)=\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
and
g\in{\mathcal{R}}^{m},\ \varphi(g=\hat{u}^{m}b(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
, then

\varphi(fg)=\varphi(f)\star\varphi(g)
where

\varphi(f)\star\varphi(g)=\hat{u}^{n+m}\langle a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})b(\xi_{n+1},\ldots,\xi_{n+m})\rangle_{{\Sigma}_{n+m}}.

(36)

If
f\in{\mathcal{R}}^{0}=\mathbb{C}
, then
\varphi(fg)=f\varphi(g)
.
The representation of
difference monomials (Definition
7
) can be deduced from
\varphi:u_{k}\mapsto\hat{u}\xi_{1}^{k}
and this multiplication rule (
36
).

The linear space
\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}=\oplus\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}^{n}
equipped with the

\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}
.

4.2
Difference operators in the symbolic representation

We assign the symbol
\eta
corresponding to the shift operator
{\mathcal{S}}
in the symbolic representation with the action

\eta(\hat{u}^{p}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}))=\hat{u}^{p}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\cdots\xi_{p}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p}).

and the composition rule (corresponding to
{\mathcal{S}}\circ f={\mathcal{S}}(f){\mathcal{S}}
):

\eta\circ\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\hat{u}^{n}(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j})a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\eta\,,

and thus
\eta^{k}\circ\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\hat{u}^{n}(\prod_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j})^{k}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\eta^{k}
.

Now we can extend the symbolic representation to difference operators with coefficients in
\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}

\varphi\,:\,A=\sum\limits_{k=p}^{q}f_{(k)}{\mathcal{S}}^{k}\ \mapsto\hat{A}=\sum\limits_{k=p}^{q}\varphi(f_{(k)})\eta^{k},\qquad f_{(k)}\in\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}.

(37)

Here the coefficients
f_{(k)}
are formal series

f_{(k)}=\alpha_{k}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{(k)}^{(n)},\qquad\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{C},\quad f_{(k)}^{(n)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{n},

and thus

\varphi(f_{(k)})=\alpha_{k}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{u}^{n}A_{k,n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}),\quad\ \ A_{k,n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\Xi_{n}.

After substitution of
\varphi(f_{(k)})
in (
37
) and the change of the order of summation, the
symbolic representation operator
\hat{A}=\varphi(A)
can also be written in the form

\hat{A}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{u}^{n}A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta),\qquad A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta)=\sum\limits_{k=p}^{q}A_{k,n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\eta^{k}\,.

(38)

In the non-commutative ring of difference operators in the symbolic representation there is a natural degree grading (in powers
\hat{u}^{n}
). The projector
\hat{\pi}_{s}
on the homogeneous component can be extended to operators (
38
)

\hat{\pi}_{s}(\hat{A})=\hat{u}^{s}A_{s}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s},\eta).

Symbolic representation enables us to solve immediately the problem to find the pre-image of the Fréchet derivative. For example, let
F=u(u_{1}-u_{-1})
, then

F_{*}=u{\mathcal{S}}+(u_{1}-u_{-1})-u{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}
and

\varphi(F)=\hat{u}^{2}\frac{1}{2}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}^{-1}-\xi_{2}^{-1}),\quad\varphi(F_{*})=\hat{u}(\xi_{1}+\eta-\xi_{1}^{-1}-\eta^{-1})

In general for
f\in{\mathcal{R}}^{n}
we have a symbol
\varphi(f)=\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})
, where
a
is a symmetric Laurent polynomial, and

f_{*}{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\left(\hat{u}^{n}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\right)_{*}=n\hat{u}^{n-1}a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1},\eta)\,.

Similar to the differential polynomial case
[
8
]
, the symbol of the Fréchet derivative is always
symmetric with respect to all permutations of its variables, including the variable

\eta
.

Proposition 1
.

An operator (
38
) is a symbolic representation of the Fréchet derivative of an element
of
\bar{{\mathcal{R}}}
if and only if each term
\hat{u}^{n}A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta)
is a Laurent polynomial in its variables and
is invariant with respect to
all permutations of its variables, including the variable
\eta
.

Given the Fréchet derivative
\hat{f_{*}}
in the symbolic representation, we can immediately reconstruct
\varphi(f)+\alpha
by replacing
\hat{u}^{n-1}\rightarrow\hat{u}^{n}/n
and
\eta\rightarrow\xi_{n}
in each term, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
is an arbitrary constant.

The composition rule for difference operators in symbolic representation (
38
) follows from

\hat{u}^{n}A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta)\circ\hat{u}^{m}B_{m}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m},\eta)=\hat{u}^{n+m}\langle A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i+n})B_{m}(\xi_{n+1},\ldots,\xi_{n+m},\eta)\rangle

(39)

and the linearity. In particular, the action of difference operators on elements of
\hat{{\mathcal{R}}}
follows from

\hat{u}^{n}A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\eta)(\hat{u}^{m}b_{m}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m}))=\hat{u}^{n+m}\langle A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}\xi_{i+n})b_{m}(\xi_{n+1},\ldots,\xi_{n+m})\rangle.

(40)

Now we are going to define the action of evolutionary derivations on elements of the ring and difference operators in symbolic representation.
A differential-difference equation

u_{t}=f,\qquad f=\sum_{m\geqslant 0}f^{(m)},\qquad f^{(m)}\in{\mathcal{R}}^{m}

defines the evolutionary derivation
\partial_{t}=X_{f}=\sum_{m\geqslant 0}X_{f^{(m)}}
. Let
\varphi(f^{(m)})=\hat{u}^{m}b_{m}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
and for an element

. Then

\hat{g}_{t}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varphi(X_{f}(g))=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\hat{u}^{n+m-1}n\langle a(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1},\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\xi_{n+i})b_{m}(\xi_{n},\ldots,\xi_{n+m-1})\rangle_{\Sigma_{n+m-1}}\,.

Similarly for a difference operator
\hat{A}=\varphi(A)
(
38
) we obtain

\hat{A}_{t}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varphi(X_{f}(A))=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\hat{u}^{n+m-1}n\langle A_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-1},\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}\xi_{n+i},\eta)b_{m}(\xi_{n},\ldots,\xi_{n+m-1})\rangle_{\Sigma_{n+m-1}}\right)\,.

4.3
Symmetries in the symbolic representation.

Symbolic representation enables us to reduce the problem of description of symmetries for a given differential-difference equation

u_{t}=F,\qquad F{\,\xmapsto{\varphi}\,}\hat{u}\omega(\xi_{1})+\hat{u}^{2}a_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})+\hat{u}^{3}a_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3})+\cdots,\quad\omega(\xi_{1})\neq 0

Theorem 2

Let

\displaystyle A_{m}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})=\frac{1}{G^{\omega}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})}\bigg(G^{\Omega}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})a_{m}(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{m})

Proof.

Theorem

Remark 2

Example 3

Consider the Narita-Itoh-Bogoyavlensky equation

Example 4

Let us consider the Volterra equation

\displaystyle u_{\tau}=u_{2}-u_{-2}+uu_{2}+uu_{1}+u_{1}^{2}+u_{1}u_{2}-u_{-1}u_{-2}-uu_{-2}-u_{-1}^{2}-uu_{-1}

Most interesting integrable systems possess an infinite hierarchy of local conservation laws. Let us recall that a difference polynomial (or a formal series)

Proposition 5

Suppose equation

(

Proof.

Let the conserved density

5

In the case of partial differential equations universal integrability conditions can be formulated in terms of a formal recursion operator

]
-

Namely, the existence of an infinite hierarchy of symmetries implies the existence of a first order formal pseudo-differential series with the coefficients in the corresponding differential field, satisfying the same equations as the recursion operator. Universality means that this fact does not depend on unknown a priory possible gaps in the hierarchy of symmetries. Later on this theory has been reformulated in the symbolic representation

5.1

In Section

5.2
Canonical formal recursion operator

We now proceed to the construction of the universal form of necessary integrability conditions for finite order evolutionary differential-difference equations, i.e., equations whose right hand side is either a polynomial or a formal series in a finite number of variables
u_{-n},\ldots,u_{n}
:

u_{t}=f=f(u_{-n},\ldots,u_{n}),\qquad f=\sum_{i\geq 1}f^{(i)},\qquad\ \ f^{(1)}\neq 0,\ \ f^{(i)}\in{\cal R}^{i}\cap\mathbb{C}[u_{-n},\ldots,u_{n}].\

(62)

In symbolic representation equation (
62

\hat{u}_{t}=\hat{f}=\hat{u}\omega(\xi_{1})+\sum_{i\geq 2}\hat{u}^{i}a_{i}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i}),\qquad a_{i}\in\Xi_{i}.

(63)

We shall assume that

(see Remark

Definition 11
.

A quasi-local formal series

and for any rational function

Theorem 3

Let

Then, for any choice of a rational function

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\frac{(\phi(\xi_{1}\eta)-\phi(\eta))}{G^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\eta)}a_{2}(\xi_{1},\eta),

(66)

\displaystyle\phi_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p+1)\frac{(\phi(\xi_{1}\cdots\xi_{p}\eta)-\phi(\eta))a_{p+1}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)}{G^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)}+\frac{1}{G^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)}\times

(67)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[\sum_{s=1}^{p-1}s\langle\phi_{s}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s-1},\xi_{s}\cdots\xi_{p},\eta)a_{p-s+1}(\xi_{s},\ldots,\xi_{p})\rangle+

\displaystyle+\sum_{s=2}^{p}s\big(\langle\phi_{p-s+1}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p-s+1},\xi_{p-s+2}\cdots\xi_{p}\eta)a_{s}(\xi_{p-s+2},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)\rangle-

\displaystyle-\langle a_{s}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s-1},\xi_{s}\cdots\xi_{p}\eta)\phi_{p-s+1}(\xi_{s},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)\rangle\big)\bigg],\quad p=2,3\ldots.

Proof.

The proof follows immediately from the observation that equations

can be solved recursively with respect to

∎

6
Classification of integrable differential-difference equations

Here we apply the previous section results to the problem of classification of anti-symmetric quasi-linear integrable differential-difference equations of order
(-n,n)

u_{t}=u_{n}f(u_{n-1},\ldots,u_{1-n})-u_{-n}f(u_{1-n},\ldots,u_{n-1})+g(u_{n-1},\ldots,u_{1-n})-g(u_{1-n},\ldots,u_{n-1}),

(74)

where

are polynomial functions or formal series. Quasi-linear equations are called equivalent if they are related by invertible transformations

u_{k}\mapsto\alpha u_{k}+\beta,\ t\mapsto\gamma t,\ \alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}^{*},\ \beta\in\mathbb{C}
. The equivalence classes may contain equations with
f(0,\ldots,0)=0
(see Example
4
). In the classification list it is sufficient to present a single representative from each equivalence class. Integrable equations are members of infinite hierarchy of symmetries. Each hierarchy has a seed which is hierarchy member of a minimal possible order. Thus instead of presenting all integrable equations of a certain fixed order, we only present the seeds of integrable hierarchies removing the ones possessing lower order symmetries.

In this section we give a complete list for equations of the form (
74
) when
n=3
satisfying necessary integrability conditions - the quasi-locality conditions for the canonical formal recursion operator. The integrability for each equation from the list is proved by either using difference substitutions or presenting Lax representations. Moreover, we investigate the higher order integrable analogue for each equation from the list. Similar to the Narita-Itoh-Bogoyavlensky lattice (
50
), this is a family of integrable equations for any specific
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and for any two different
n
, the corresponding flows do not commute
(cf. Section
6.2
). Finally we present a Lax representation for a new integrable differential-difference hierarchy.

6.1

In this session we present the exhaustive list of integrable differential-difference equations of the form

u_{t}=u_{3}f(u_{2},u_{1},u)-u_{-3}f(u_{-2},u_{-1},u)+g(u_{2},u_{1},u)-g(u_{-2},u_{-1},u),\qquad f(0,0,0)=1,

(75)

where
f,g
are polynomial functions or formal power series. We omit equations admitting a symmetry of order
(1,-1)
, which have been studied in detail in

Proposition 8
.

Let

u_{t}=\sum_{p\geq 1}f_{p},\quad f_{i}\in{\cal R}^{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad u_{t}=\sum_{p\geq 1}\tilde{f}_{p},\quad\tilde{f}_{i}\in{\cal R}^{i}

be two integrable formal differential-difference equations of form (
75
), such that

•

The linear terms are
f_{1}=\tilde{f}_{1}=u_{3}+c_{2}u_{2}+c_{1}u_{1}-c_{1}u_{-1}-c_{2}u_{-2}-u_{-3}
;

•

The quadratic and cubic terms coincide:
f_{2}=\tilde{f}_{2},\quad f_{3}=\tilde{f}_{3}
.

Then the formal differential-difference equations coincide, i.e.

f_{p}=\tilde{f}_{p},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Since the formal differential-difference equations are integrable, they possess the canonical formal recursion operators

\Lambda=\eta+\sum_{p>0}\hat{u}^{p}\phi_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta),\quad\tilde{\Lambda}=\eta+\sum_{p>0}\hat{u}^{p}\tilde{\phi}_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta),

such that all their terms are quasi-local. Moreover, since the linear, quadratic and cubic terms of the formal differential-difference equations coincide, the linear and quadratic terms of the formal recursion operators also coincide, i.e.

\phi_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta)=\tilde{\phi}_{1}(\xi_{1},\eta),\quad\phi_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta)=\tilde{\phi}_{2}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\eta).

Consider cubic elements of
\Lambda
and
\tilde{\Lambda}
:
\hat{u}^{3}\phi_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)
and
\hat{u}^{3}\tilde{\phi}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)
, and let
\hat{u}^{4}a_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})
and
\hat{u}^{4}\tilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\xi_{4})
be the symbolic representations of
f_{4}
and
\tilde{f}_{4}
. Then from (

\phi_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)-\tilde{\phi}_{3}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)=4\frac{\eta(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}-1)(a_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)-\tilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta))}{\omega(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\xi_{3}\eta)-\omega(\xi_{1})-\omega(\xi_{2})-\omega(\xi_{3})-\omega(\eta)}

\displaystyle\hat{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{3}\xi_{2}^{3}\xi_{3}^{3}\eta^{3}\left(a_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)-\tilde{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)\right),

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{3}\xi_{2}^{3}\xi_{3}^{3}\eta^{3}G^{\omega}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta).

Here both
\hat{a}_{4}(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3},\eta)

are polynomials in their arguments. One can show

Theorem 6
.

with a rational (pseudo-difference) operator

, where

\displaystyle Q=u-wu_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1},\quad P=(uw_{1}{\mathcal{S}}-u_{1}){\mathcal{S}}^{n-1},\qquad w=\sqrt{1+u^{2}},

Proof

\displaystyle L_{+}=a^{(n)}{\mathcal{S}}^{n}+a^{(n-1)}{\mathcal{S}}^{n-1}\cdots+a^{(1)}{\mathcal{S}},

\displaystyle a^{(n)}=w_{1},\ a^{(n-1)}=uu_{1},\ a^{(n-l)}=ww_{-1}\cdots w_{2-l}u_{1}u_{1-l},\ 2\leq l\leq n-1.

These are the coefficients of positive shifts in
A
. For the negative parts, we have

a^{(-l)}=-{\mathcal{S}}^{-l}a^{(l)}=-a^{(l)}_{-l}.

The Lax equation
L_{t}=[A,L]
implies that there exists a difference operator
B
such that

\displaystyle P_{t}=BP-PA,\qquad Q_{t}=BQ-QA,

(108)

where the operator

can be determined by

,
P
and
Q
. To do so, we write down the equivalent equations for (

) according to the orders of
{\mathcal{S}}
. Using the equation for the operator
Q

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{n}:

\displaystyle b^{(n)}u_{n}=ua^{(n)};

(109)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{l}:

\displaystyle b^{(l+1)}u_{l+2}w_{l+1}-b^{(l)}u_{l}-u_{1}wa_{-1}^{(l+1)}+ua^{(l)}=0,\ 1\leq l\leq n-1\ \mbox{or}\ -n\leq l\leq-2;

(110)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{0}:

\displaystyle u_{t}=-b^{(1)}u_{2}w_{1}+b^{(0)}u+u_{1}wa_{-1}^{(1)};

(111)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}:

\displaystyle u_{1t}w+u_{1}w_{t}=b^{(0)}u_{1}w-b^{(-1)}u_{-1}+ua^{(-1)};

(112)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}^{-n-1}:

\displaystyle b^{(-n)}u_{1-n}w_{-n}=u_{1}wa_{-1}^{(-n)}=-u_{1}ww_{-n}.

(113)

From (

\displaystyle b^{(n-1)}=\frac{1}{u_{n-1}}\left(b^{(n)}u_{n+1}w_{n}-u_{1}wa_{-1}^{(n)}+ua^{(n-1)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{u_{n-1}}\left(\frac{uw_{1}}{u_{n}}u_{n+1}w_{n}-u_{1}w2+u^{2}u_{1}\right)=\frac{uu_{n+1}w_{1}w_{n}}{u_{n}u_{n-1}}-\frac{u_{1}}{u_{n-1}}.

When

notice that

-u_{1}wa_{-1}^{(l+1)}+ua^{(l)}=0.

Thus in this case (

) becomes

b^{(l)}u_{l}=b^{(l+1)}u_{l+2}w_{l+1},

which leads to

\displaystyle b^{(l)}=\frac{u_{n-1}u_{n}}{u_{l}u_{l+1}}w_{l+1}\cdots w_{n-1}b^{(n-1)}=\frac{1}{u_{l}u_{l+1}}\left(\prod_{j=l+1}^{n-1}w_{j}\right)\left(uu_{n+1}w_{1}w_{n}-u_{1}u_{n}\right).

), we get
b^{(-n)}=-\frac{u_{1}w}{u_{1-n}}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{u_{2-n}w_{1-n}}\left(-\frac{u_{1}w}{u_{1-n}}u_{-n}-u_{1}wu_{-n}u_{1-n}+uw_{1-n}\right)=\frac{uu_{1-n}-u_{1}u_{-n}ww_{1-n}}{u_{1-n}u_{2-n}}\ .

(114)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\left(\prod_{k=0}^{n+l-2}w_{l-k}\right)u_{l+1}(uu_{1-n}-u_{1}u_{-n}ww_{1-n}).

Thus combining (

) and (

We now eliminate
b^{(0)}
from (

\displaystyle\qquad u_{1t}w+u_{1}w_{t}-\frac{u_{1}w}{u}u_{t}=wu_{1t}-\frac{u_{1}}{uw}u_{t}

Remark 5
.

In
[
28
]
, Garifullin and Yamilov established the integrability properties of the equation

\displaystyle u_{t}=(u^{2}-1)\left(\sqrt{u_{1}^{2}-1}u_{2}-\sqrt{u_{-1}^{2}-1}u_{-2}\right),

which is the equation (
107
) when
n=2
under the scaling transformation.
They provided its Lax pair as
L\psi=0
and
\psi_{t}=A\psi
with

\displaystyle L=uw^{\prime}_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{2}+u_{1}{\mathcal{S}}-\lambda\left(u_{1}w^{\prime}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-u\right);\qquad w^{\prime}=\sqrt{u^{2}-1},

\displaystyle A=\frac{w^{\prime}}{u}\left(w^{\prime}(u_{1}{\mathcal{S}}+u_{-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1})-\lambda^{-1}u_{-1}{\mathcal{S}}+\lambda u_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\right).

This is the same as the Lax representation given in the theorem (after rescaling and simple gauge).
Let
P=uw^{\prime}_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{2}+u_{1}{\mathcal{S}}
and
Q=u_{1}w^{\prime}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-u
.
If we eliminate
\lambda
in
A
, we get

\displaystyle A=\frac{w^{\prime 2}u_{1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{w^{\prime 2}u_{-1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-\frac{w^{\prime}u_{-1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}P^{-1}Q+\frac{w^{\prime}u_{1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}Q^{-1}P

\displaystyle=\frac{w^{\prime 2}u_{1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}+\frac{w^{\prime 2}u_{-1}}{u}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-\frac{1}{u}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}(u_{1}w^{\prime}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-u)+P^{-1}Q+\frac{1}{u}(uw^{\prime}_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{2}+u_{1}{\mathcal{S}})+Q^{-1}P

\displaystyle=w^{\prime}_{1}{\mathcal{S}}^{2}+uu_{1}{\mathcal{S}}+uu_{-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}-w^{\prime}_{-1}{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}+P^{-1}Q+Q^{-1}P,

where the nonlocal part commutes with the fractional operator
Q^{-1}P
and thus we can discard it.

We prove that the hierarchy of commuting symmetries is well defined for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. To do so, we use the
r
-matrix approach
[
29
]
. We consider the Lie algebra denoted by
{\mathfrak{g}}
of the formal Laurent series of the shift operator with
the commutator
[A,B]=AB-BA
, where
A,B\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. It is easy to see that any element

B=b^{(m)}{\mathcal{S}}^{m}+b^{(m-1)}{\mathcal{S}}^{m-1}+\cdots\in{\mathfrak{g}}

admits a unique decomposition of the form

B=B_{+}-(B_{+})^{\dagger}+H,

where
B_{+}
is a difference operator with positive powers of
{\mathcal{S}}
. We denote the antisymmetric part
B_{+}-(B_{+})^{\dagger}

of any element
B
as
{\mathfrak{g}}_{+}
and the other part
H
as
{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}
. It is obvious that these both parts form Lie subalgebras.
Thus we have the decomposition of the Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{+}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{-}

into the direct sum of two Lie subalgebras. We define the projections

\pi_{\pm}:{\mathfrak{g}}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{g}}_{\pm}

and the
r
-matrix
r=\frac{1}{2}(\pi_{+}-\pi_{-}).
We now formulate the statement generating commuting symmetries, which can be proved in the standard way in the
r
-matrix approach
[
29
]
.

Corollary
. The flows defined by the Lax equations

\partial_{t_{p}}L=[\pi_{+}(L^{p}),\ L]

commute with each other.

Proof
. Let
A^{(p)}=\pi_{+}(L^{p})
. Using Lax equations, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{q}}\partial_{t_{p}}L-\partial_{t_{p}}\partial_{t_{q}}L=[\partial_{t_{q}}A^{(p)}-\partial_{t_{p}}A^{(q)}-[A^{(q)},A^{(p)}],L].

So it is sufficient to show that

\partial_{t_{q}}A^{(p)}-\partial_{t_{p}}A^{(q)}-[A^{(q)},A^{(p)}]=0.

For
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\partial_{t_{p}}L^{l}=[A^{(p)},L^{l}]
since

\displaystyle[\partial_{t_{p}}L^{l},\ L]=-[L^{l},\partial_{t_{p}}L]=-[L^{l},[A^{(p)},L]]=[[A^{(p)},L^{l}],L]\ .

Thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t_{q}}A^{(p)}-\partial_{t_{p}}A^{(q)}-[A^{(q)},A^{(p)}]

\displaystyle=\pi_{+}(\partial_{t_{q}}L^{p})-\pi_{+}(\partial_{t_{p}}L^{q})-[A^{(q)},A^{(p)}]=\pi_{+}([A^{(q)},L^{p}])-\pi_{+}([A^{(p)},L^{q}])-[A^{(q)},A^{(p)}]

\displaystyle=\pi_{+}([-\pi_{-}(L^{q}),L^{p}])-\pi_{+}([-\pi_{-}(L^{p}),L^{q}])-[L^{q}-\pi_{-}(L^{q}),L^{p}-\pi_{-}(L^{p})]

\displaystyle=\pi_{+}(-[\pi_{-}(L^{q}),\pi_{-}(L^{p})])=0.

This leads to

\partial_{t_{q}}\partial_{t_{p}}L=\partial_{t_{p}}\partial_{t_{q}}L
as required.

\diamond

7
Summary and Discussion

In order to give an exhaustive description of all integrable differential-difference equations of certain type one needs to find strong and verifiable necessary integrability conditions. All previous attempts to tackle this problem were based on the integrability conditions dependent of the symmetry structure of equations which may not be known in advance.
In this paper, we developed an approach to establish the universal integrability conditions by introducing the notion of quasi-locality in the context of symbolic representation. We proved that if an equation of the form

u_{t}=f=f(u_{-n},\ldots,u_{n}),\qquad f=\sum_{i\geq 1}f^{(i)},\qquad f^{(i)}\in{\cal R}^{i},\qquad\ \ f^{(1)}\neq 0,

possesses an infinite dimensional algebra of its symmetries, then there exists a unique quasi-local formal recursion operator with
symbolic representation

\eta+\sum_{p\geq 1}\hat{u}^{p}\phi_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta).

Moreover, the recursive formulae for all terms
\phi_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)
are explicitly given. This is achieved by developing symbolic representation for the difference polynomial ring, difference operators and formal series.
The requirement of quasi-locality for all terms
\phi_{p}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{p},\eta)
leads to the necessary integrability conditions, which are independent on the structure of the symmetry algebra.

We applied our new approach to classification of integrable equations of the form (
74
).
We reproduced the integrable equations of such type in
[
15
]
for
n=2
, and obtained a complete list of integrable equations for
n=3
, which includes 17 equations which are complimentary to the lower order hierarchies.
For each equation in the list we found an infinite family of integrable equations of arbitrary high order. Integrability of the families obtained we proved in each case either by providing a transformations to a known integrable family, or by presenting a Lax representation.

Here, for completeness, we would like present a fractional Lax representation

L_{t}=[A,L],\ L=Q^{-1}P,\ \Leftrightarrow\ P_{t}=BP-PA,\quad Q_{t}=BQ-QA

for the new family of equations (
106
) found by Adler
[
18
]
in which

\displaystyle P=\left(\prod_{l=0}^{n-1}u_{l}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{2n}+{\mathcal{S}};\ \ A=-\left(\prod_{l=-n}^{n-2}u_{l}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{2n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{2n-2}\left((1-u_{j-n})\prod_{l=n+1}^{2n-1}u_{j-l}\right)+{\mathcal{S}}^{1-2n};

\displaystyle Q=\left(\prod_{l=0}^{n-2}u_{l}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{2n-1}+1;\ \ B=-\left(\prod_{l=0}^{2n-2}u_{l}\right){\mathcal{S}}^{2n-1}+\sum_{j=0}^{2n-2}\left((1-u_{j-2n+1})\prod_{l=n}^{2n-2}u_{j-l}\right)+{\mathcal{S}}^{1-2n}.

The global classification problem of integrable differential-difference equations still remains a very challenging problem. In the case of scalar polynomial
evolutionary equations, we believe that there are new integrable hierarchies of high orders starting from any order. Thus it is impossible to achieve the global classification result as it was done for scalar evolution PDEs
[
6
,
7
]
. The higher the order
n
is in the equation, the more integrable equations there are even after we remove the ones being symmetries of lower order ones. Thus it is important to develop an approach to identify the transformations between integrable equations and to develop the concept of integrable families which have been discussed, but not yet defined in this paper.

Integrable differential-difference equations are generalised symmetries for discrete integrable equations. The authors of
[
25
]
constructed the autonomous quad-equations which admit as symmetries five-point differential-difference equations belonging to classification lists in
[
15
]
. Recently it was shown that there exist multi-points integrable discrete equations on a plain lattice related to Bäcklund-Darboux transformations for the Narita-Itoh-Bogoyavlensky equation (
50
)
[
27
,
30
]
.
It would be interesting to construct discrete integrable equations for families discussed in Section
6.2
,
in particular, for the new integrable families (
106
) and (
107
).

1/c deformations of AdS 3 boundary conditions and the Dym hierarchy
Kristiansen Lara, Miguel Pino, Francisco Reyes

Abstract
This work introduces a novel family of boundary conditions for AdS 3 General Relativity, constructed through a polynomial expansion in negative integer powers of the Brown-Henneaux central charge. The associated dynamics is governed by the Dym hierarchy of integrable equations. It is shown that the infinite set of Dym conserved charges generates an abelian asymptotic symmetry group. Additionally, these boundary conditions encompass black hole solutions, whose thermodynamic properties are examined.

As shown by the previous examples, there is further ground for exploration when choosing state-dependent Lagrange multipliers and their holographic implications. However, such a choice is not arbitrary since it must meet the necessary requirements of well-defined boundary conditions. Integrable systems provide the necessary tools to explore the landscape of possibilities and might serve as a guideline to explore nonstandard holography. In this sense, as a first step toward this objective, this paper aims to enlarge the aforementioned landscape of boundary conditions for AdS

2
Constructing the family of boundary conditions

The aim of this section is to construct an infinite family of possible choices for the functions
\mu^{\pm}
in (
1.4
) as local functions of
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
and its derivatives. The starting point is the spatial behavior of the connection (
1.2
), supplemented with the temporal component given by (
1.4
). The first-order Einstein equations translates in the Chern-Simons formulation as two independent copies of the zero-curvature condition
\partial_{t}a^{\pm}_{\phi}-\partial_{\phi}a^{\pm}_{t}+\left[a^{\pm}_{t},a^{\pm}_{\phi}\right]~=~0
, yielding the evolution equations

\pm\ell\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=2\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\partial_{\phi}\mu^{\pm}+\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}-\frac{c_{\mathrm{BH}}}{24\pi}\partial^{3}_{\phi}\mu^{\pm}\,.

(2.1)

These dynamical equations will serve as a guide for constructing the sought functions
\mu^{\pm}
. In this regard, the above is conveniently expressed as follows

\pm\ell\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=\left(\mathcal{D}^{\pm}-c\mathcal{E}\right)\mu^{\pm},

(2.2)

where the operators
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{E}
are defined as

\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\equiv 2\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\partial_{\phi}+\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{\pm},\quad\quad\quad\mathcal{E}\equiv\partial_{\phi}^{3}.

(2.3)

These two differential operators are Hamiltonian, in the sense that they are linear, anti-symmetric and fulfill Jacobi identity
[
95
]
. As shown below, these form the bi-Hamiltonian structure of the Harry Dym hierarchy. The central charge in (
2.2
) is rescaled
c\equiv c_{\mathrm{BH}}/24\pi
to avoid
24\pi
terms in the forthcoming expressions.

As mentioned in the introduction, the main assumption is that the functions
\mu^{\pm}
can be expanded in negative powers of the central charge
c
up to some fixed integer power
N\geq 1

\mu^{\pm}=\mu^{\pm}_{0}+\sum^{N}_{n=1}\mu^{\pm}_{n}\frac{1}{c^{N-n+1}}\,.

(2.4)

The positive integer
N
is arbitrary and can be chosen freely. It plays the role of labeling the family of possible choices for
\mu^{\pm}
; different values for
N
corresponds to inequivalent boundary conditions. Although the integer
N
can have different values on both sectors
\pm
, here are chosen the same for simplicity.

When substituted in the equation of motion (
2.2
), the polynomial ansatz (
2.4
) allows for grouping coefficients with the same power of
c
, resulting in relations among the coefficients
\mu^{\pm}_{n}
. Firstly, the terms linear in
c
renders the condition

\mathcal{E}\mu_{0}^{\pm}=0\,,

(2.5)

which fixes the first coefficient
\mu_{0}^{\pm}
. The solution to (
2.5
), respecting periodic boundary conditions, is given by a constant
\mu_{0}^{\pm}=\alpha_{0}^{\pm}
.

The
c^{0}
terms, involving the time derivative of
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
, yield the evolution equation

\pm\ell\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=\left(\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\mu_{0}^{\pm}-\mathcal{E}\mu_{N}^{\pm}\right)\,.

(2.6)

Since
\mu^{\pm}_{0}=\alpha_{0}^{\pm}
, the first term on the right-hand side of the equation becomes
\alpha_{0}^{\pm}\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
. Therefore, the dynamical equation corresponds to the chiral boson deformed by the second term, which depends on
\mu_{N}^{\pm}
. To determine the form of
\mu_{N}^{\pm}
, we can use the remaining
c^{-1}
to
c^{-N}
terms, as they imply the following relations

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}_{1}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.7a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\mu^{\pm}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}\mu^{\pm}_{n}\,,\quad n\geq 1\,.

(2.7b)

The former differential equation governs
\mu_{1}^{\pm}
, while the latter establishes a recursive relation for deriving
\mu^{\pm}_{n+1}
from its predecessor. These relations allows to iteratively construct all coefficients
\mu^{\pm}_{n}
as functions of
{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}
and its spatial derivatives. Once the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{\pm}
are determined, the function
\mu^{\pm}
is fully specified by the summation in equation (
2.4
), thereby completely defining the boundary conditions.

It is important to remark that solving the recursion relation leads to integration constants. These arise due to the linearity of the differential equation (
2.7b
); the solution for
\mu_{n+1}^{\pm}
consists of a homogeneous component with an integration constant, as well as a particular solution that depends on the previous coefficient
\mu_{n}^{\pm}
. To shed light on this observation, lets examine the role of the differential operators in the recursion relation. Consider the inverse of the operator
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
, given by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}^{\pm}}^{-1}[X(\phi)]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\partial_{\phi}^{-1}\left(\frac{X(\phi)}{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\right)\,.

(2.8)

It allows to define the recursion operator
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}\equiv{\mathcal{D}^{\pm}}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{E}
, yielding

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{\pm}[X(\phi)]=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\partial_{\phi}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}}\partial_{\phi}^{3}X(\phi)\right)\,.

(2.9)

Accordingly, the recursion relation (
2.7b
) can then be expressed as

\displaystyle\mu_{n+1}^{\pm}=\mathcal{R}^{\pm}[\mu_{n}^{\pm}]+\mathcal{R}^{\pm}[0]\,.

(2.10)

The first term on the right-hand side produces the particular solution, whereas the subsequent term corresponds to the homogeneous one, i.e.,
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}[0]=\frac{\alpha}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}
, where
\alpha
is an integration constant. Then, the complete solution to the differential equation (
2.7b
) is given by

\displaystyle\mu_{n+1}^{\pm}=\mathcal{R}^{\pm}[\mu_{n}^{\pm}]+\frac{\alpha^{\pm}_{n+1}}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\,.

(2.11)

This result can be successively used to derive an explicit expression for any of the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{\pm}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{\pm}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{\pm}{\mathcal{R}^{\pm}}^{n-i}\left[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\right]\,,\quad n\geq 1\,.

(2.12)

At this point, it is important to highlight a remarkable property of the recursion operator
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
; the successive action of
\mathcal{R}^{\pm}
on the expression
\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}
consistently yields functional derivatives of quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}
, according to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}^{\pm}}^{n}\left[\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\right]=\frac{\delta H_{n+1}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}}\,.

(2.13)

The reason behind this lies in the properties of the operators
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{E}
; these are not just Hamiltonian operators but are
compatible
among each other, meaning that any linear combination of them also renders a Hamiltonian operator. This compatibility ensures (
2.13
), as demonstrated in
[
95
]
. Such a property is common in bi-Hamiltonian integrable systems, proving useful as it allows the construction of an infinite tower of conserved quantities of the system. As illustrated below, the quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}
found in our construction are conserved charges and serve as generators for the asymptotic symmetry algebra. An explicit list of the first
H_{n}^{\pm}
along with its functional derivatives can be found in Appendix
B
.

According to the previous discussion, the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{\pm}
take the form

\displaystyle\mu_{n}^{\pm}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{n-i+1}^{\pm}\functionalderivative{H_i^\pm}{\LLpm}\,,\quad n\geq 1\,,

(2.14)

rendering the functions
\mu^{\pm}
as

\displaystyle\mu^{\pm}=\alpha_{0}^{\pm}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{c^{N-n+1}}\alpha_{n-i+1}^{\pm}\functionalderivative{H_i^\pm}{\LLpm}\,.

(2.15)

While the above expression is sufficient to specify the boundary conditions, for the sake of simplicity, we aim to make specific choices for the arbitrary constants
\alpha_{n}^{\pm}
. As mentioned earlier, opting for
\alpha^{\pm}_{i}=\delta_{i,0},\,i\geq 0,
yields the Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions, since all the
1/c
terms in the
\mu^{\pm}
functions disappear. Another viable option is
\alpha_{0}^{\pm}=0
and
\alpha_{i}^{\pm}=\delta_{i,1},\,i\geq 1
. This selection has the virtue of extending the asymptotic symmetries to include Lifshitz scaling. However, this possibility will be explored in a separate study.

For the current analysis, we set
\alpha_{0}^{\pm}=1
and
\alpha_{i}^{\pm}=\delta_{i,1},\,i\geq 1
. With these choices, the coefficients
\mu_{n}^{\pm}
take on the following form

\displaystyle\mu^{\pm}_{n}=\functionalderivative{\HHpm_n}{\LLpm}\,.

(2.16)

(Note that within this choice, the same expression also holds for
\mu_{0}^{\pm}
due to the relation
\mu_{0}^{\pm}=\frac{\delta H_{0}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}
, where
H_{0}^{\pm}\equiv\int\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\,d\phi
). The functions
\mu^{\pm}
result in

\displaystyle\mu^{\pm}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c^{N-n+1}}\functionalderivative{\HHpm_n}{\LLpm}\,.

(2.17)

The latter expression reinforces the title of this work: these boundary conditions can be interpreted as the standard Brown-Henneaux case, denoted by the “1” in the latter expression, deformed by suitable terms in negative powers of the central charge, symbolized by the sum. It is worth highlighting that this construction is valid for any value of the central charge, and hence, it is not a perturbative result.

To conclude this section, let us briefly delve into the dynamical equation (
2.6
). Given the choices outlined above for the arbitrary constants
\alpha_{n}^{\pm}
, the simplest scenario arises for
N=1
, reducing (
2.6
) to the well-known Dym’s equation

\displaystyle\pm\ell\partial_{t}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\phi}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}-\partial_{\phi}^{3}\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}}\right).

(2.18)

This non-linear integrable evolution equation was initially discovered in an unpublished manuscript by Harry Dym and later revisited in
[
92
]
. It manifests in various physical scenarios, including the classical string
[
96
,
97
]
and problems in fluid mechanics, specifically governing the dynamics at the interface between two fluids with different viscosities, known as the Saffman-Taylor problem
[
98
]
. Dym’s equation admits a diverse set of traveling solitons
[
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
103
,
104
,
105
,
106
,
107
,
108
,
109
,
110
,
111
]
. An extension to higher dimensions was later uncovered in
[
112
]
. The resulting higher-order differential equations obtained for
N>1
, collectively referred to as the Dym hierarchy, are given by

\pm\ell\partial_{t}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}=\partial_{\phi}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}-\partial_{\phi}^{3}\left(\frac{\delta H_{N}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right).

(2.19)

Concerning the integrability of Dym’s hierarchy, it is important to highlight that each equation within the hierarchy shares an identical infinite tower of conserved quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}

[
113
,
114
]
. To demonstrate this, consider equations (
2.7b
) and (
2.14
), which give rise to the recursion relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\left(\functionalderivative{\HHpm_{n+1}}{\LLpm}\right)=\mathcal{E}\left(\functionalderivative{\HHpm_n}{\LLpm}\right),\quad n\geq 0.

(2.20)

By virtue of the latter, we can express the
N
-th member of the hierarchy (
2.6
) in two distinct Hamiltonian forms

\displaystyle\pm\ell\partial_{t}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}=\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\left(\functionalderivative{H_0^\pm}{\LLpm}-\functionalderivative{\HHpm_{N+1}}{\LLpm}\right)=\mathcal{E}\left(\functionalderivative{H_{-1}^\pm}{\LLpm}-\functionalderivative{H_N^\pm}{\LLpm}\right),

(2.21)

(where
H^{\pm}_{-1}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d\phi\,\left(\partial_{\phi}^{-1}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right)^{2}
), thereby establishing each element of Dym’s hierarchy as a bi-Hamiltonian system. Furthermore, the differential operators
\mathcal{D}^{\pm}
and
\mathcal{E}
enable the construction of two nonequivalent Poisson structures

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{F}{G}_{\mathcal{D}}=\int d\phi\,\functionalderivative{F}{\LLpm}\mathcal{D}^{\pm}\left(\functionalderivative{G}{\LLpm}\right),\quad\poissonbracket{F}{G}_{\mathcal{E}}=\int d\phi\,\functionalderivative{F}{\LLpm}\mathcal{E}\left(\functionalderivative{G}{\LLpm}\right).

(2.22)

A well-established result follows from the recursion relation (
2.20
), asserting that all quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}
Poisson commute with each other, utilizing either of the two defined Poisson brackets (see
[
95
]
),

\displaystyle\poissonbracket{H_n^\pm}{H_m^\pm}_{\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}}=0,\quad\forall\,n,m\geq 0.

(2.23)

The involution of the quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}
demonstrates that each of them serves as a conserved charge for the evolution equation, conclusively establishing the integrability of each equation within the hierarchy.

3
Consistency of the boundary conditions

Well-defined boundary conditions must meet three criteria
[
115
]
. First, they must yield a differentiable action principle. Second, they should permit an asymptotic symmetry group with finite generators. Lastly, it is desirable for them to encompass appealing gravitational configurations, such as black holes.

The objective of this section is to demonstrate that the chosen boundary conditions (
1.2
)(
1.4
) with (
2.17
) fulfill all three aforementioned criteria.

3.1
Action principle

The Hamiltonian Chern-Simons action is given by

\displaystyle I_{H}^{\pm}=-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int\,dt\,d^{2}x\,\epsilon^{ij}\left\langle{A}_{i}^{\pm}\dot{A}_{j}^{\pm}-A_{t}^{\pm}F_{ij}^{\pm}\right\rangle+B^{\pm}\,,

(3.1)

where the boundary term
B^{\pm}
fulfills

\displaystyle\delta B^{\pm}=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\rho\to\infty}dt\,d\phi\,\left\langle A_{t}^{\pm}\delta A_{\phi}^{\pm}\right\rangle\,,

(3.2)

By using the asymptotic behavior of the fields (
1.2
) and (
1.4
), the variation of this surface integral reads

\displaystyle\delta B^{\pm}=\mp\frac{k}{4\pi c\ell}\int_{\rho\to\infty}dt\,d\phi\,\mu^{\pm}\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\,.

(3.3)

By means of equation (
2.17
), the boundary term becomes integrable, rendering

\displaystyle B^{\pm}_{N}=\mp\int\frac{dt}{\ell}\left(H^{\pm}_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c^{N-n+1}}H^{\pm}_{n}\right)\,.

(3.4)

The subscript
N
is included as a reminder that the boundary term is different for distinct choices of
N
.

Since the full gravitational action is the difference of two Chern-Simons functionals
I=I_{H}^{+}-I_{H}^{-}
, then the full boundary term
B_{N}=B_{N}^{+}-B_{N}^{-}
becomes

\displaystyle B_{N}=-\int\frac{dt}{\ell}\left[H_{0}^{+}+H_{0}^{-}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{c^{N-n+1}}\left(H_{n}^{+}+H_{n}^{-}\right)\right]\,.

(3.5)

Therefore, the action principle is differentiable.

3.2
Asymptotic symmetries

Here, we show that the previously identified conserved quantities
H_{n}^{\pm}
serve as canonical generators for the asymptotic symmetry group associated with the given boundary conditions.

To begin, let us examine the asymptotic behavior of the angular component of the connection
a_{\phi}
, shown in (
1.2a
). As mentioned in the introduction, the most general gauge transformation that preserves such behavior has a parameter of the form (
1.3
)
[
78
]
. The corresponding transformation of the dynamical fields
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\pm\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=\left(\mathcal{D}^{\pm}-c\mathcal{E}\right)\eta^{\pm}\,.

(3.6)

At this point, the typical approach involves exploring the preservation of the temporal component of the connection
a_{t}^{\pm}
, often leading to additional conditions on the functions
\eta^{\pm}
. However, we opt for an alternative approach. First, by following the methodology outlined in the previous section, we construct a family of possible choices for
\eta^{\pm}
. Subsequently, we determine the canonical charges and their algebra. Towards the end of the section, we demonstrate that these transformations also respect the form of
a_{t}^{\pm}
.

The construction employs the same methodology as in section
2
, where
\eta^{\pm}
is treated as an expansion in negative powers of the central charge up to some arbitrary integer
M
. It is then inserted into (
3.6
), and the coefficients of the expansion are determined by solving order by order. Following the selection of arbitrary constants in a similar fashion to the previous section, this construction yields the following result

\displaystyle\eta^{\pm}=\epsilon^{\pm}_{0}+\epsilon^{\pm}_{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{1}{c^{M-m+1}}\functionalderivative{\HHpm_m}{\LLpm}\,,

(3.7)

where
\epsilon_{0}
and
\epsilon_{M}
are arbitrary infinitesimal constants.

From a canonical perspective, the variation of the generators for these transformations can be expressed as

\displaystyle\delta Q^{\pm}[\eta^{\pm}]=\mp\frac{1}{\ell}\int d\phi\,\eta^{\pm}\delta{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\,.

(3.8)

This expression is integrable due to (
3.7
). Consequently, we designate the generators as
Q_{0}^{\pm}\equiv Q^{\pm}[\epsilon^{\pm}_{0}=1,\epsilon^{\pm}_{M}=0]
and
Q_{M}^{\pm}\equiv Q^{\pm}[\epsilon^{\pm}_{0}=0,\epsilon^{\pm}_{M}=1]
, leading to the following identifications

\displaystyle Q_{0}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{\ell}H_{0}^{\pm}\,,

(3.9a)

\displaystyle Q_{M}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mp\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{1}{c^{M-m+1}}H^{\pm}_{m}\,.

(3.9b)

It is important to emphasize that
M
can take any positive integer value. Therefore, the generator
Q_{M}^{\pm}
corresponds to an infinite family of generators, each labeled by a distinct value of
M
. According to (
3.6
), the generators induce the following transformations for the dynamical fields

\displaystyle\pm\delta_{0}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\,,

(3.10a)

\displaystyle\pm\delta_{M}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{\delta H_{M}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right)\,.

(3.10b)

With the generators now identified, we can proceed to establish their algebra. For this purpose, the following relation proves to be useful
[
4
]

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{\pm}\left[\eta_{1}^{\pm}\right],Q^{\pm}\left[\eta_{2}^{\pm}\right]\right\}=\delta_{1}Q^{\pm}_{2}\,.

(3.11)

It is important to distinguish the above Poisson bracket from those defined in (
2.22
). The former corresponds to the gravitational phase space, while the latter relates to the bi-Hamiltonian structure of the Dym hierarchy. These are not necessarily equivalent. However, through the utilization of relation (
3.11
) and considering the transformations in (
3.10
), it is straightforward to show that

\displaystyle\left\{Q^{\pm}_{M},Q^{\pm}_{\bar{M}}\right\}=\sum_{m=1}^{\bar{M}}\frac{1}{c^{\bar{M}-m+1}}\int d\phi\,\frac{\delta H^{\pm}_{m}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\mathcal{E}\left(\frac{\delta H_{M}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right)\,.

(3.12)

The right-hand side of the latter equation corresponds to
\poissonbracket{H_m^\pm}{H_M^\pm}_{\mathcal{E}}
, which vanishes due to (
2.23
). Consequently, the generators satisfy an abelian algebra, as expected from the integrability of the dynamical equations. A similar procedure can be executed with
Q_{0}^{\pm}
, leading to the following generator algebra

\displaystyle\left\{Q_{M}^{\pm},Q_{\bar{M}}^{\pm}\right\}=0\quad\forall\,M,\bar{M}\geq 0.

(3.13)

To finalize this section, we show that the aforementioned asymptotic gauge transformations also preserve the form of the temporal component
a_{t}^{\pm}
. The corresponding gauge transformation is given by

\displaystyle\delta a_{t}^{\pm}=\partial_{t}\Lambda^{\pm}+\commutator{a_t^\pm}{\Lambda^\pm}\,.

(3.14)

As mentioned previously, due to the temporal derivative acting on the gauge parameter, it is expected that the latter equation will further impose constraints on the form of
\eta^{\pm}
. For instance, in the case of Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions, a consecuence of (
3.14
) is that the functions obey a chiral boson equation. In the case at hand, it turns out that the equation (
3.14
) implies no further conditions on the functions
\eta^{\pm}
but rather reduces to a combination of the equation of motion (
2.6
) and the transformation of the field (
3.10
), thereby becoming an identity. To prove this statement, we follow
[
91
]
to establish a general relation applicable to any gauge transformation of the form (
1.3
) along with (
3.7
).

First, consider two gauge transformations of parameters
\Lambda^{\pm}
and
\bar{\Lambda}^{\pm}
, whose action on an auxiliary connection
a
is given by

\displaystyle\delta a=

\displaystyle d\Lambda+\left[a,\Lambda\right]\,,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\bar{\delta}a=

\displaystyle d\bar{\Lambda}+\left[a,\bar{\Lambda}\right]\,.

(3.16)

A straightforward calculation reveals that the commutator of two such gauge transformations results in another gauge transformation of parameter
\bar{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\pm}
,

\displaystyle\commutator{\delta}{\bar{\delta}}a^{\pm}=\bar{\bar{\delta}}a^{\pm}\,,

(3.17)

where
\bar{\bar{\Lambda}}
is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\pm}=\delta\bar{\Lambda}^{\pm}-\bar{\delta}\Lambda^{\pm}+\commutator{\Lambda^\pm}{\bar{\Lambda}^\pm}\,.

(3.18)

Now, let us assume that
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
belong to the class of transformations generated by (
3.9
). Each transformation (
3.15
) is canonically generated by
\delta a=\{Q[\Lambda],a\}
and
\bar{\delta}a=\{Q[\bar{\Lambda}],a\}
, where
Q[\Lambda]
and
Q[\bar{\Lambda}]
correspond to some linear combinations of (
3.9
). It follows from the Jacobi identity that the commutator
\commutator{\delta}{\bar{\delta}}a^{\pm}
is generated by

\displaystyle\commutator{\delta}{\bar{\delta}}a^{\pm}=\left\{\left\{Q^{\pm}[\Lambda],{Q[\bar{\Lambda}]}^{\pm}\right\},a^{\pm}\right\}\,.

(3.19)

As the generators satisfy the involution condition (
3.13
), the right-hand side of the latter equation vanishes, indicating that
\bar{\bar{\delta}}a=0
, or equivalently

\displaystyle d\bar{\bar{\Lambda}}^{\pm}+\commutator{a^\pm}{\bar{\bar{\Lambda}}^\pm}=0\,.

(3.20)

Since this relation holds for arbitrary connections
a^{\pm}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\delta\bar{\Lambda}^{\pm}-\bar{\delta}\Lambda^{\pm}+\commutator{\Lambda^\pm}{\bar{\Lambda}^\pm}=0\,.

(3.21)

In summary, considering the transformations (
3.15
), the preceding identity remains valid for any pair of gauge transformations whose generators Poisson-commute. Since the temporal component of the connection,
a_{t}
, belongs to the class of allowable gauge parameters (
1.4
), the above identity encompasses (
3.14
) as a specific case, as anticipated.

3.3
Stationary black hole solution and its thermodynamics

The third requirement for a well-defined boundary condition is to encompass physically sensible gravitational configurations, such as black holes.

Solutions to the equations of the Dym hierarchy (
2.6
) have been extensively explored in the literature
[
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
103
,
104
,
105
,
106
,
107
,
108
,
109
,
110
,
111
]
. A prerequisite for these solutions to represent stationary black holes is that they must be independent of time and exhibit periodicity in the angular coordinate. For instance, solitons typically do not meet such criteria. Therefore, our focus lies in the periodic solutions to the equation

0=\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}-\partial^{3}_{\phi}\left(\frac{\delta H_{N}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right)\,.

(3.22)

To solve the latter, we need to delve into the toolkit provided by the literature on integrable systems, work which will be pursued elsewhere. For our current objectives, we will concentrate on a much simpler family of solutions, given by constant functions

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}(t,\phi)=\mathcal{L}^{\pm}\,.

(3.23)

Note that this solution satisfies the stationary equation for any choice of
N
. These solutions correspond to black hole configurations, as shown in this section using the metric formulation.

Before proceeding, a few comments are in order. Firstly, note that
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=0
is a singularity of the dynamical equation for any
N\geq 1
(see
B
); hence, it is not encompassed in the set of allowed solutions. As shown below, this implies that extremal black holes are not part of this class of solutions. Secondly, due to the presence of square roots in the functions
\mu^{\pm}
, negative values for
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
must be avoided to prevent complex configurations. As a result, global AdS
3
spacetime is not a member of this family of solutions. Finally, it is worth noting that in this class of solutions, the only non-vanishing conserved quantities are
H_{0}^{\pm}
and
H_{1}^{\pm}
. As a result of this, the functions
\mu^{\pm}
become constants

\displaystyle\mu^{\pm}=1+\frac{1}{2c^{N}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}}\,.

(3.24)

We initiate the analysis of the geometry by constructing the metric, in accordance to the relation

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\left\langle\left(A_{\mu}^{+}-A_{\mu}^{-}\right)\left(A_{\nu}^{+}-A_{\nu}^{-}\right)\right\rangle\,,

(3.25)

where
A^{\pm}=b^{-1}_{\pm}\left(d+a^{\pm}\right)b_{\pm}
and the radial group element is chosen as
b_{\pm}(\rho)=\exp\left[\pm\log\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\right)L_{0}\right]
. Thus, the stationary line element reads

\displaystyle\begin{split}ds^{2}&=-\left(\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mu^{-}\mathcal{L}^{-}-\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{+}\right)\left(\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mu^{+}\mathcal{L}^{+}-\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{-}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{\ell^{2}d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt+\left\{\bigg[\frac{\rho^{2}}{\ell}+\frac{\ell}{c}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4c\rho^{2}}\mathcal{L}^{-}\right)\mathcal{L}^{+}\bigg]\mu^{+}-\bigg[\frac{\rho^{2}}{\ell}+\frac{\ell}{c}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{2}}{4c\rho^{2}}\mathcal{L}^{+}\right)\mathcal{L}^{-}\bigg]\mu^{-}\right\}dtd\phi\\
&\hskip 199.16928pt+\left(\rho+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{+}\right)\left(\rho+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{-}\right)d\phi^{2}\,.\end{split}

(3.26)

It is convenient to rewrite the latter metric using Schwarzschild-like coordinates. For this purpose, it is helpful to redefine the radial coordinate according to

\displaystyle r^{2}=\left(\rho+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{+}\right)\left(\rho+\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{-}\right)\,,

(3.27)

through which it can be expressed as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-N^{2}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f^{2}(r)}+r^{2}\left(N^{\phi}(r)dt+d\phi\right)^{2}\,.

(3.28)

The lapse and shift functions
N(r)
and
N^{\phi}(r)
are given by

\displaystyle N(r)=\left(\frac{{\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}}{2}\right)f(r)\,,\quad N^{\phi}(r)=\frac{{\mu^{+}}-{\mu^{-}}}{2\ell}+\frac{\ell\left({\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}\right)\left({\mathcal{L}}^{+}-{\mathcal{L}}^{-}\right)}{4cr^{2}}\,,

(3.29)

while the function
f(r)
corresponds to

\displaystyle f^{2}(r)=\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{{\mathcal{L}}^{+}+{\mathcal{L}}^{-}}{c}+\frac{\ell^{2}\left({\mathcal{L}}^{+}-{\mathcal{L}}^{-}\right)^{2}}{4c^{2}r^{2}}\,.

(3.30)

The horizon is determined by the condition
N(r)=0
, indicating the positions of both the inner and outer horizons

\displaystyle r_{\pm}^{2}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{2c}\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}+\mathcal{L}^{-}\pm 2\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}}^{+}{\mathcal{L}}^{-}}\right)\,.

(3.31)

Extremality occurs when either
\mathcal{L}^{+}=0
or
\mathcal{L}^{-}=0
, a condition not supported in the solution spectrum. Consequently, this family of solutions does not include extremal black holes.

Before adressing the black hole conserved charges and thermodynamics, it’s important to note that global AdS
3
cannot be encompassed within this set of solutions due to the square roots in
\mu^{\pm}
, which prohibit negative values for
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
. This is in line with the boundary conditions, which permit the geometry to fluctuate at the leading order near the boundary. As seen from (
3.28
), the spatial part of the metric takes the form
g_{ij}=g_{ij}^{AdS}+...
, where the ellipsis denotes subleading terms near the boundary, a consequence of the choice (
1.2
) for the gauge fields. However, the temporal components fluctuate at the leading order due to the choice of the Lagrange multiplier (
1.4
). Consequently, it is not expected for the three-dimensional AdS spacetime to be part of this family of solutions.

The black hole’s mass and angular momentum are conserved quantities associated with the Killing vectors
\partial_{t}
and
-\partial_{\phi}
, respectively. In the Chern-Simons formulation, these are expressed as

\displaystyle\delta Q^{\pm}\left[\xi\right]=\frac{k}{2\pi}\oint_{\rho\to\infty}d\phi\,{\xi}^{\mu}\bigg\langle a^{\pm}_{\mu}\delta a^{\pm}_{\phi}\bigg\rangle\,,

(3.32)

where
\xi
represents the Killing vector. Considering both
\pm
sectors, the total charge is
Q=Q^{+}-Q^{-}
. Consequently, the mass and angular momentum correspond to

\displaystyle M=\frac{2\pi}{\ell}\left[\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}+\mathcal{L}^{-}\right)+\frac{1}{c^{N}}\left(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{+}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{-}}\right)\right]\,,\quad J=-2\pi\left(\mathcal{L}^{+}-\mathcal{L}^{-}\right)\,.

(3.33)

and

The thermal properties of the black hole can be examined within the Euclidean counterpart of the metric (
3.28
). The Hawking temperature
\beta
and horizon angular velocity
\Omega_{h}
are selected to regularize the Euclidean version of metric (
3.28
) in the near-horizon sector (see appendix

for details). This yields

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi\ell^{2}r_{+}}{\left({\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}\right)\left(r_{+}^{2}-r_{-}^{2}\right)}=\frac{\sqrt{2c}\ell\pi(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{+}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{-}})}{\left({\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}\right)\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{-}}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{+}}}\,,

(3.34a)

\displaystyle\Omega_{h}

\displaystyle=-N^{\phi}(r_{+})=\frac{{\mu^{-}}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{-}}-{\mu^{+}}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{+}}}{\ell(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{+}}+\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{-}})}\,.

(3.34b)

The black hole entropy can be deduced from the boundary term

\displaystyle\delta B=i\beta\left(\delta M-\frac{1}{\beta}\delta S-\Omega_{h}\delta J\right)\,,

(3.36)

as expected by the universality of the Bekenstein-Hawking result.

3.4
More general solutions

Having introduced the physically sensible black hole solution of the previous section, it is worth exploring more general solutions. Although a difficult task due to the nonlinearity of the equations of motion, we briefly mention some avenues worth exploring.

A simple but enlightening route can be pursued by considering linearized solutions around the black hole background (
3.26
). Consider functions
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(t,\phi)
of the form

\mathcal{L}^{\pm}(t,\phi)=\mathcal{L}^{\pm}+h^{\pm}(t,\phi),

(3.37)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\pm}
are constants as in (
3.23
), while the perturbations
h^{\pm}
are small
h^{\pm}\ll 1
. With this choice, the functions
\mu^{\pm}(t,\phi)
are given to first order in
h^{\pm}
by

\mu^{\pm}(t,\phi)=\mu^{\pm}-\Xi^{\pm}(t,\phi)\,.

(3.38)

The constants
\mu^{\pm}
are those found in (
3.24
), while the small functions
\Xi^{\pm}
correspond to

\Xi^{\pm}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\sigma_{n}^{\pm}}{c^{N-n+1}}\partial_{\phi}^{2n-2}h^{\pm}\,,

(3.39)

where
\sigma^{\pm}_{n}\equiv\left(2^{n+1}{\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}^{n+1/2}\right)^{-1}
. This induces a perturbation of the black hole geometry
g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{bh}}
given in (
3.26
) of the form
g_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{bh}}+h_{\mu\nu}
, where the non vanishing coefficients of

are

\displaystyle\begin{split}\hfil\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{tt}=&\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{-}-\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{+}\mu^{+}\right)\left[\frac{\rho}{\ell}\Xi^{+}+\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\left(c\partial_{\phi}^{2}\Xi^{-}-\mathcal{L}^{-}\Xi^{-}+h^{-}\mu^{-}\right)\right]\\
+&\left(\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{+}-\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{-}\mu^{-}\right)\left[\frac{\rho}{\ell}\Xi^{-}+\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\left(c\partial_{\phi}^{2}\Xi^{+}-\mathcal{L}^{+}\Xi^{+}+h^{+}\mu^{+}\right)\right]\,,\end{split}\\

\hfil\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{t\phi}=&\frac{\ell^{2}}{4c\rho}\left[\left(\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{+}\mu^{+}-\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{-}\right)h^{-}-\left(\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{-}\mu^{-}-\frac{\rho}{\ell}\mu^{+}\right)h^{+}\right]\\
&+\left(\frac{\ell^{2}}{4c\rho}\mathcal{L}^{-}+\frac{\rho}{2}\right)\left[\frac{\rho}{\ell}\Xi^{-}+\frac{\ell}{2c\rho}\left(c\partial_{\phi}^{2}\Xi^{+}-\mathcal{L}^{+}\Xi^{+}+h^{+}\mu^{+}\right)\right]\\

(3.40)

The equations of motion, i.e., the
N

2.19
), reduce to the linear equations

\pm\ell\partial_{t}h^{\pm}=\partial_{\phi}h^{\pm}+\sigma_{N}^{\pm}\partial_{\phi}^{2N+1}h^{\pm}\,.

(3.41)

These correspond to combinations of anisotropic chiral bosons, studied in
[
116

. The general solution to (
3.41
) with periodic boundary conditions is a linear combination of chiral movers

h_{k}^{\pm}=\exp\left[i\left(k\phi+\omega_{k}^{\pm}t\right)\right]\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

(3.42)

obeying the dispersion relation

\pm\ell\omega_{k}^{\pm}=k+(-1)^{N}\sigma^{\pm}k^{2N+1}\,.

(3.43)

The phase velocity is given by

. Since
2N
is an even integer, all Fourier modes (
3.42

\pm

\sigma_{N}^{\pm}
. Although the presented solution is valid only at the linear level, the dispersion relation does codify the nonlinearities of the Dym equation. These perturbations are reminiscent of the Bañados geometries
[

in the case of Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions. These have been studied in the context of holography (see, for example,

(3.45)

4
Final comments

We have shown the construction of Dym boundary conditions for AdS
3
pure gravity and established their well-defined nature. Notably, these conditions give rise to an infinite and abelian asymptotic symmetry group, where the generators correspond to the Dym conserved charges, and encompass black hole solutions within their spectrum.

We conclude this work with a discussion of avenues worth exploring.

It is important to note that the choice of the integration constants
\alpha_{n}^{\pm}
is arbitrary and was inspired by the goal of constructing deformations of Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions. However, as mentioned earlier, alternative possibilities exist. In particular, selecting
\alpha_{0}^{\pm}=0
leads to the dynamical equation

\pm\ell\partial_{t}\mathcal{L}^{\pm}=-\partial^{3}_{\phi}\left(\frac{\delta H_{N}^{\pm}}{\delta\mathcal{L}^{\pm}}\right)\,,

(4.1)

which enhances symmetry to include Lifshitz scaling. This might allow to explore Lifshitz holography within this context
[
122
,
123
,
124
,
125
]
.

It is worth mentioning that the whole solution spectrum of the Dym boundary conditions cannot be reached by applying the allowed gauge transformations (
3.10
) to the zero-mode solutions studied in section
3.3
, unlike the case of Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions. In the latter, solutions within the spectrum manifest as chiral modes oscillating across an identified global AdS
3
spacetime. Such structure is rooted in the asymptotic symmetries, namely the 2D conformal group, and the linearity of the boundary dynamics, governed by the chiral boson equation. However, these considerations manifest differently in the Dym case. Due to the involution of
H_{n}
(
3.13
), the set of conserved charges of two solutions related by a large gauge transformation must coincide. Hence, black holes with nontrivial Dym charges lie on a different orbit than the zero-mode solution and must be found by solving the differential equation (
2.6
), which is challenging due to the equation’s nonlinearity. A quick review of the literature reveals that finding solutions to the Dym equation (or any integrable system) requires powerful methods such as Darboux and Backlund transformations, consistent Tanh expansion, among others, often yielding particular cases that obscure the systematic construction of the solution spectrum
[
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
103
,
104
,
105
,
106
,
107
,
108
,
109
,
110
,
111
]
. Therefore, in this work, we focus solely on studying the properties of the zero-mode solution, as presented in section
3.3
, while the structure of the solution spectrum and its relation to large gauge transformations will be addressed elsewhere.

The holographic implications of this family of boundary conditions will be further explored in subsequent work. The recursive procedure used to construct the Dym boundary conditions is exact and valid for any value of the central charge, allowing for exploration of limiting cases. By noting that the expansion in (
2.4
) involves negative integer powers of
c
, it is tempting to speculate that the holographic properties are somehow linked to a boundary conformal theory at large central charge, which has been extensively studied in the context of holography
[
126
,
127
,
128
,
129
,
130
,
131
,
132
,
133
]
. Such a connection will be pursued elsewhere.

Finally, an extension of this work can be pursued for higher-order algebras, such as
sl(N,R)
, potentially establishing connections between higher spin gravity and a broader hierarchy of integrable equations that includes the Dym hierarchy as a particular case.

Appendix A
Chern-Simons formulation of AdS
3
gravity

We provide a concise overview of the Chern-Simons formulation of AdS
3
gravity, focusing solely on essential details to ensure the self-contained nature of the preceding presentation.

(2+1)
dimensional General Relativity with a negative cosmological constant can be written as the difference of two Chern-Simons action
[
80
,
81
]

\displaystyle I=I_{H}[A^{+}]-I_{H}[A^{-}]\,,\quad I_{H}[A]=-\frac{k}{4\pi}\int dt\,d^{2}x\,\epsilon^{ij}\left\langle A_{i}\dot{A}_{j}-A_{t}F_{ij}\right\rangle+B\,,

(A.1)

where
F_{ij}^{\pm}=\partial_{i}A^{\pm}_{j}-\partial_{j}A_{i}^{\pm}+\commutator{A_i^\pm}{A_j^\pm}
. The Chern-Simons level is given by
k=\ell/4G
, where
\ell
stands for the AdS
3
radius while
G
is the three-dimensional Newton constant. The fields
A^{\pm}
are
sl(2,\mathbb{R})
connections. The three algebra generators
L_{n}
, where
n\in\left\{0,\pm 1\right\}
, fulfill the algebra

\displaystyle\commutator{L_m}{L_n}=(m-n)L_{m+n}\,.

(A.2)

The nonvanishing components of the bilinear product
\left\langle\,,\right\rangle
are

\displaystyle\left\langle L_{0},L_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\,,\quad\left\langle L_{1},L_{-1}\right\rangle=-1\,.

(A.3)

The boundary term
B
in (
A.1
) is constructed to assure the differentiability of the action, yielding

\displaystyle\delta B=-\frac{k}{2\pi}\int_{\rho\to\infty}dt\,d\phi\,\left\langle A_{t}\delta A_{\phi}\right\rangle\,.

(A.4)

Therefore, adequate boundary conditions must be inforced in order for
\delta B
to be integrable.

The infinitesimal gauge transformation

\displaystyle\delta A=d\Lambda+\commutator{A}{\Lambda}\,,

(A.5)

is generated by the smeared integral

decay fast enough towards the boundary such that

proper gauge transform
. On the other hand, if the boundary behavior of the gauge parameter is such that
Q

large gauge transformation

[

]
. More details and discussion on this subject can be found in the review
[
135
]
and references therein.

Finally, the link between Chern-Simons and the metric fields is established through
A^{\pm}=\omega\pm\frac{e}{\ell}

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=\frac{\ell^{2}}{2}\left\langle\left(A_{\mu}^{+}-A_{\mu}^{-}\right),\left(A_{\nu}^{+}-A_{\nu}^{-}\right)\right\rangle\,.

(A.8)

Appendix C
Euclidean analysis of black hole solution

In this analysis, the Euclidean version of the metric (
3.28
) is examined to extract the thermodynamic properties of the black hole solution.

Consider first the Wick rotation

\displaystyle t\to i\tau\,.

(C.1)

Upon implementing, the metric takes on the Euclidean form

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\mathrm{E}}=N^{2}(r)d\tau^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f^{2}(r)}+r^{2}\left(N^{\phi}_{\mathrm{E}}(r)d\tau+d\phi\right)^{2}\,,

(C.2)

where
N^{j}_{\mathrm{E}}=iN^{j}
.

To determine the temperature and velocity of the horizon, the Euclidean metric near the surface
r=r_{+}
is addressed. It results in

\displaystyle ds^{2}_{\mathrm{E}}\simeq\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{{\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}}{2}\right)^{2}\left(\left.{f^{2}(r)}^{\prime}\right|_{r_{+}}\right)^{2}R^{2}d\tau^{2}+dR^{2}+r_{+}^{2}\left(iN^{\phi}(r_{+})d\tau+d\phi\right)^{2}\,,

(C.3)

Here, the radial coordinate has been redefined as
R=2\sqrt{\frac{(r-r_{+})}{\left.{f^{2}(r)}^{\prime}\right|_{r_{+}}}}
. In order for the latter to represent a cylinder of the form
ds^{2}_{\mathrm{E}}=R^{2}d\theta^{2}+dR^{2}+dz^{2}
, the following identification of coordinates is performed

\displaystyle\theta=\left(\frac{{\mu^{+}}+{\mu^{-}}}{4}\right)\left(\left.{f^{2}(r)}^{\prime}\right|_{r_{+}}\right)\tau\,,\quad dz=r_{+}\left(iN^{\phi}(r_{+})d\tau+d\phi\right)\,.

(C.4)

The angular periodicity
\theta\sim\theta+2\pi
implies that the temporal coordinate is identified as
\tau\sim\tau+\beta
, while the absence of periodicity in
z
(the transverse section) results in
\phi\sim\phi+i\beta\Omega_{h}
. Consequently, the inverse of the Hawking temperature
\beta
and the velocity of the horizon
\Omega_{h}
are in agreement with (
3.34a
) and (
3.34b
), respectively.

Now, details about the derivation of (
3.35
) are presented. To determine the variation of the boundary term
\delta B
, the Hamiltonian formulation is employed. The Euclidean Hamiltonian action of General Relativity is expressed as

\displaystyle I_{E}=I_{H}+B\,.

(C.5)

Focusing our attention solely on the boundary term, its variation is given by
[
137
]

\displaystyle\delta B=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int dS^{2}_{l}\,\left[G^{ijkl}\left(N_{|k}\delta\gamma_{ij}-N\delta\gamma_{ij|k}\right)-2N_{\mathrm{E},k}\delta\pi^{kl}_{\mathrm{E}}-\left(2\pi^{il}_{\mathrm{E}}N^{j}_{\mathrm{E}}-\pi^{ij}_{\mathrm{E}}N^{l}_{\mathrm{E}}\right)\delta\gamma_{ij}\right]\,.

(C.6)

Here,
\gamma_{ij}
represents the spatial metric, and
\pi^{ij}
is its conjugate momentum. Additionally,
N
and
N_{E}^{i}
denote the lapse and shift vectors, respectively.

Considering the stationary and axisymmetric symmetry of the solution (
3.28
), the variation of
B
simplifies to

\displaystyle\delta B

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{16\pi G}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\phi\left.\left(G^{ijkr}N\delta\gamma_{ij|k}+2N_{\mathrm{E},j}\delta\pi^{rj}_{\mathrm{E}}\right)\right\rvert_{r=r_{+}}^{r=\infty}\,.

(C.7)

Expressed in terms of Dym’s fields, the above can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta B&=\frac{i}{16\pi G}\beta\left(2\pi\right)\left[\frac{1}{c}\left({\mu^{+}}\delta{\mathcal{L}}^{+}+{\mu^{-}}\delta{\mathcal{L}}^{-}\right)-\frac{4\pi}{\beta}\delta r_{+}\right.\\
&\hskip 142.26378pt\left.-\frac{1}{c}\frac{\left({\mu^{+}}\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}}^{+}}-{\mu^{-}}\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}}^{-}}\right)}{\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}}^{+}}+\sqrt{{\mathcal{L}}^{-}}}\left(\delta{\mathcal{L}}^{+}-\delta{\mathcal{L}}^{-}\right)\right]\,.\end{split}

(C.8)

Recognizing the mass and angular momentum (
3.33
), we arrive at (
3.35
), as promised.

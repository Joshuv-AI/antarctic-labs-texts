Equation of state at neutron-star densities and beyond from perturbative QCD Preprint: LA-UR-23-32625
Oleg Komoltsev, Rahul Somasundaram, Tyler Gorda, Aleksi Kurkela, Jérôme Margueron, Ingo Tews

Abstract
We explore the consequences of imposing robust thermodynamic constraints arising from perturbative Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) when inferring the dense-matter equation-of-state (EOS). We find that the termination density, up to which the EOS modeling is performed in an inference setup, strongly affects the constraining power of the QCD input. This sensitivity in the constraining power arises from EOSs that have a specific form, with drastic softening immediately above the termination density followed by a strong stiffening. We also perform explicit modeling of the EOS down from perturbative-QCD densities to construct a new QCD likelihood function that incorporates additional perturbative-QCD calculations of the sound speed and is insensitive to the termination density, which we make publicly available.



I
Introduction

Studying the impact of perturbative quantum chromodynamics (pQCD) calculations on the inference of the equation of state (EOS) of neutron stars (NSs) has recently become a promising area of research.
The fundamental theory of the strong interaction, quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is non-perturbative at low energies but becomes perturbative at asymptotically high energies, which are reached either at high temperatures or at high densities, at about
20-40n_{\mathrm{sat}}
in baryon number density
Kurkela et al. 2010
;
Kurkela and Vuorinen 2016
;
Gorda et al. 2018
;
Gorda et al. 2021
;
Gorda et al. 2023a
.
Here,
n_{\mathrm{sat}}\approx 0.16
fm
-3
is the nuclear saturation density.

The cores of stable NSs explore densities that are most likely limited to about
5-8n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, depending on the EOS which is realized in their cores
Schaffner-Bielich 2020
.
This implies that the theoretical description of dense matter in stable NSs requires the solution of QCD in a non-perturbative regime.
At low densities, up to
1-2n_{\rm sat}
, effective-field-theory (EFT) approaches, such as chiral EFT
Epelbaum et al. 2009
;
Machleidt and Entem 2011
, are commonly employed to study the NS EOS
Hebeler et al. 2013
;
Tews et al. 2018a
;
Drischler et al. 2019
.
Such calculations are typically extended to higher densities using model-agnostic extrapolations
Read et al. 2009
;
Lindblom 2010
;
Lindblom and Indik 2012
;
Hebeler et al. 2013
;
Lindblom and Indik 2014
;
Kurkela et al. 2014
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Most et al. 2018
;
Tews et al. 2018b
;
Landry and Essick 2019
;
Greif et al. 2019
;
Annala et al. 2020
;
Raaijmakers et al. 2020
;
Essick et al. 2020
;
Annala et al. 2022
;
Altiparmak et al. 2022
;
Somasundaram et al. 2023a
;
Jiang et al. 2023
;
Gorda et al. 2023b
;
Annala et al. 2023
, conditioned with various astrophysical inputs.
Some of these analyses have also included input from the fundamental QCD Lagrangian in the perturbative regime (henceforth referred to as “QCD input”) in the form of pQCD calculations at high densities
Kurkela et al. 2014
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Most et al. 2018
;
Annala et al. 2020
;
Somasundaram et al. 2023a
;
Annala et al. 2022
;
Han et al. 2023
;
Jiang et al. 2023
;
Gorda et al. 2023b
;
Annala et al. 2023
.
These works have reported results that differ from those that do not account for this QCD input.
In particular, the works including the QCD input have reported softening of the EOS at energy densities above
750
MeV/fm
3
, for which there seems to be no evidence without this input.

The connection between the perturbative regime of QCD and NSs was first investigated in Ref.
Kurkela et al. 2014
, by extending model-agnostic extrapolation functions of the EOS up to the densities where pQCD calculations become reliable and imposing the pQCD values as boundary conditions for these extrapolations.
More recently, Komoltsev and Kurkela
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
have suggested a new method to link both density regimes, allowing them to ‘integrate backwards’, i.e., to propagate the pQCD constraints to lower densities in a completely general, analytical, and model-independent manner using only the thermodynamic potential and the conditions of causality and mechanical stability.
In practice, the method provides a necessary condition for an extrapolated EOS that has to be fulfilled at all densities below the validity range of pQCD calculations.
This condition asserts that all the points of the EOS can be connected to the pQCD values by some causal and stable interpolation.
The QCD input was shown to constrain the EOS down to densities of
\sim 2.2-2.5n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, if otherwise only causality and mechanical stability are considered beyond chiral EFT.
Within this construction, the only uncertainty in the constraints arises from the truncation error of the pQCD computation of the EOS at perturbative densities (for a comprehensive study on the pQCD truncation errors, see Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023c
).
However, at these densities, astrophysical observations of NSs are naturally more informative.

While the construction suggested in Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
fully bypasses the need for modeling NSs (as the input to these constraints derives from high-energy particle physics, not from NSs), in order to interface the QCD input with these astrophysical observations several assumptions must be made.
These include extrapolations involved in nuclear theory, assumptions involved when extracting NS properties from observational data, the prior assumptions of how EOS models are constructed, and up to what terminating number density
n_{\mathrm{term}}
such modeling is performed.
These assumptions and their interplay with the pQCD calculations are an important subject in discussions about the constraining power of astrophysical and QCD inputs.

The constraining power of pQCD calculations was studied in detail in two recent publications
Gorda et al. 2023b
;
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
, and was later also investigated by Refs.
Ecker and Rezzolla 2022
;
Annala et al. 2023
;
Essick et al. 2023
;
Brandes et al. 2023
;
Musolino et al. 2023
;
Mroczek et al. 2023
;
Fan et al. 2023
;
Tang et al. 2023
.
These works have employed astrophysical input in the form of gravitational-wave data constraining the tidal deformability of NSs in binary mergers
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2018
;
Abbott et al. 2019
, radio measurements of NS masses
Antoniadis et al. 2013
;
Cromartie et al. 2019
;
Fonseca et al. 2021
, as well as simultaneous mass-radius measurements using X-ray observations
Steiner et al. 2018
;
Nättilä et al. 2017
;
Shaw et al. 2018
;
Miller et al. 2019a
;
Riley et al. 2019
;
Miller et al. 2021
;
Riley et al. 2021
.
They discussed whether the QCD input reduces the uncertainties of the inferred EOS when imposed on top of astrophysical inputs or whether the QCD and astrophysical inputs disfavor similar behaviors.
In the latter case, the two inputs would corroborate one another, strengthening the conclusion that would have been obtained using only one.

In this work, we compare the analyses conducted in Refs.
Gorda et al. 2023b
and
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
.
These studies arrived at different conclusions but used the same pQCD calculations and the same way of constraining the EOS
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022

1
.
While both of these studies agreed that the QCD input impacted the EOS at the densities reached in massive NSs,
Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
found that QCD input provides considerable additional information on top of other sources of data whereas Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
found this additional impact to be marginal.
The conclusions of these two studies seem to be in contradiction, and the aim of this work is to investigate the origin of this apparent discrepancy.

that is required in order for the EOS to remain consistent with the QCD input to higher densities.
We do so by generating ensembles of extensions of a given EOS beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, which we condition with the QCD input at
15n_{\rm sat}
.
In this context, we also introduce a concept of a pQCD tension index, which quantifies the degree to which a given EOS is in tension with the high-density pQCD constraint within the construction of Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022

Finally, we also introduce a method for modifying the QCD input itself by marginalizing over a prior model of high density EOSs constructed by extrapolating down from

.

;

Essick et al. 2023

Importantly, such a construction also permits us to condition the model with further pQCD information not used in the original construction.
In particular, the speed of sound exhibits very small perturbative corrections to the free result down to densities as low as
\approx 25n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
By conditioning the high-density prior with the speed of sound gives us a novel, more theory-informed QCD input at the price of increased model dependence.

II
Review of the QCD input

Following Refs.
Gorda et al. 2023b
and
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
, we implement the QCD input as follows.
Given the EOS at two different points, at a low density
\beta_{L}=(n_{L},p_{L},\mu_{L})
and at a high density
\beta_{H}=(n_{H},p_{H},\mu_{H})
, we may ask whether these two points can be connected by
any
stable and causal EOS.
Here
n
is the baryon density,
p
is the pressure, and
\mu
is the baryon chemical potential.
This question can be answered by exploring the bounds for EOS behavior between
\beta_{L}
and
\beta_{H}
.

The range of possible pressure differences between
(\mu_{L},n_{L})
and
(\mu_{H},n_{H})
are limited by the following two EOSs in this interval

\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{pmin}}(\mu)

\displaystyle=n_{L}\frac{\mu}{\mu_{L}},\quad\mu_{L}\leq\mu<\mu_{H}

(1)

\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{pmax}}(\mu)

\displaystyle=n_{H}\frac{\mu}{\mu_{H}},\quad\mu_{L}<\mu\leq\mu_{H}

(2)

namely those which have one segment of
c_{s}^{2}=1
followed by a phase transition or vice versa, respectively.
These EOSs bound the range of possible pressure differences to be

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\min}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{L}}{2}\left(\frac{\mu_{H}^{2}}{\mu_{L}}-\mu_{L}\right),

(3)

\displaystyle\Delta p_{\max}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{H}}{2}\left(\mu_{H}-\frac{\mu_{L}^{2}}{\mu_{H}}\right).

(4)

Given values for
\beta_{L}
and
\beta_{H}
, the pressure difference between these two points is fixed.
Therefore the simple check (for detailed derivation see Refs.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
;
Gorda et al. 2023b
)

\displaystyle\Delta p=p_{H}-p_{L}

\displaystyle\in[\Delta p_{\min},\Delta p_{\max}]

(5)

determines whether the two points can be connected by a causal and stable EOS.
This condition does not make use of any specific form of the EOS between the low- and high-density points but rather implies the existence of at least one EOS connecting the two limits.
The condition on the pressure difference can be rewritten as

\displaystyle 0\leq I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}\equiv\frac{\Delta p-\Delta p_{\min}}{\Delta p_{\max}-\Delta p_{\min}}\leq 1\,,

(6)

where we introduce a new quantity
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
, the pQCD tension index.
If the index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}\notin[0,1]
the EOS is excluded because there is no possible causal and stable interpolation.
If the index obtains the values
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0\,(I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=1)
, then the EOS is forced to follow
n_{\mathrm{pmin}}\,(n_{\mathrm{pmax}})
, respectively.
In particular, if
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=1
, the EOS must contain a phase transition of size

\Delta n=n_{H}\frac{\mu_{L}}{\mu_{H}}-\mu_{L}\,.

(7)

For typical values of
n_{L}=n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, we obtain that the strength of phase transition reaches
\Delta n\sim 20n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, followed by a
c^{2}_{s}
= 1 segment of
\sim 10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
Note that if
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
is not exactly
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0
or
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=1
, then both extreme EOSs of Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) are excluded.
For
0<I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}<1
, the EOS is bounded in the
n
–
\mu
–
p
-space by a non-trivial envelope whose projection in the plane of energy-density
\varepsilon
and pressure
p
is shown in Fig.
1
(for an explicit expression, see Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
).

Following Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
, we adopt the scale-averaging interpretation of the pQCD uncertainties for
\beta_{H}
.
We use a log-uniform distribution for the dimensionless renormalization scale
X=3\bar{\Lambda}/2\mu_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
in the range [1/2, 2], which can be written as
w(\log X)={\mathbf{1}}_{[\log(1/2),\,\log(2)]}(\log X)
.
Here
\bar{\Lambda}
is the renormalization scale in the modified minimal subtraction scheme
Kurkela et al. 2010
.
The QCD likelihood function then reads

\displaystyle P({\rm QCD|EOS})=\int

\displaystyle d(\log X)w(\log X)\times

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}_{[0,1]}\bigl(I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(X,\rm EOS)\bigr)

(8)

where
\mathbb{1}_{[0,1]}(I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(X,\rm EOS))
is an indicator function discarding EOSs with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}\notin[0,1]
for a fixed value of
X
.
In general, the index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
depends on all six quantities
(n_{L},p_{L},\mu_{L})
and
(n_{H},p_{H},\mu_{H})
, which for fixed values of
\mu_{H}=\mu_{\rm QCD}
reduces to
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(X,\mathrm{EOS})=I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(X,n_{L},p_{L},\mu_{L})
.

We note that the convergence of the pQCD series depends on the unphysical renormalization scale.
In particular, the convergence is slowest for the smaller values of
X\approx 1/2
.
This slow convergence renders the
X=1/2
results, which differ quantitatively from the higher values of
X
, less trustworthy; see Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023c
for a detailed analysis of the convergence of the pQCD results.

In the following sections we apply the QCD likelihood function at different termination densities, meaning that
(n_{L},p_{L},\mu_{L})
corresponds to
(n_{\mathrm{term}},p_{\mathrm{term}},\mu_{\mathrm{term}})
.
For the high density input,
\beta_{H}=\beta_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
, we use the partial next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order pQCD computation of Ref.
Gorda et al. 2021
.

Note that in terms of the sound speed, the QCD input constrains the density
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, which is given by a (logarithmic) integral of
c_{s}^{-2}(\mu)
and the pressure
p_{\mathrm{term}}
, which is the integral of this integral
n(\mu)
.
Hence for a given NS EOS, the input is more sensitive to individual features in the speed of sound at lower densities and is less sensitive to those near
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.

III
Computational Setup

In order to study the dependence of our results on the differences in the analyses between Refs.
Gorda et al. 2023b
;
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
, we use two different models to generate our EOS sets: the parametric sound-speed model of Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
and the Gaussian-process (GP) model of the speed of sound of Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
.
Note that similar models have also been employed by Refs.
Somasundaram et al. 2023a
;
Tews et al. 2018a
;
Landry and Essick 2019
;
Mroczek et al. 2023
.

III.1
Prior Set 1: The sound-speed model

To generate the CSM set, we follow Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
.
At the lowest densities, we fix the EOS to the crust model of Ref.
Douchin and Haensel 2001
and do not explore uncertainties in the crust modeling.
The crust EOS is then matched with the EOS of the core via a cubic spline in the
c^{2}_{s}
versus
n
plane.
For the core EOS, below a certain breakdown density
n_{b}
, we assume that the EOS can be accounted for by nucleonic degrees of freedom, which in this work is described by the meta-model of Refs.
Margueron et al. 2018a
;
Margueron et al. 2018b
.
The meta-model is a density-functional approach, similar to the Skyrme approach, that allows one to incorporate nuclear-physics knowledge directly encoded in terms of the Nuclear Empirical Parameters (NEPs).
We vary the NEPs in a rather broad range in order to explore a wide range of nuclear density functionals that describe matter up to
n_{b}
.
The parameter
n_{b}
is varied uniformly in the range
1-2n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
Furthermore, we discard all models that do not satisfy constraints provided by the chiral EFT calculations of Ref.
Lynn et al. 2016
up to nuclear saturation density.

Above
n_{b}
, we use the CSM and create a non-uniform grid in density between
n_{b}
and
25n_{\textrm{sat}}
with
9
grid points, where the 9th point is fixed at
25n_{\textrm{sat}}
and the other 8 points are drawn from a uniform probability distribution in density.
At each grid point, the squared sound-speed is uniformly sampled between
0
and
1
in units of the speed of light.
Then, the sound speeds at each grid point are connected by linear segments, generating a sound-speed profile
c^{2}_{s}(n)
for the EOS.
The density-dependent speed of sound
c^{2}_{s}(n)
can then be integrated to give the EOS, i.e., the pressure
p(n)
, the energy density
\varepsilon(n)
, and the baryon chemical potential
\mu(n)
, see Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023a
for more details.
The CSM set used here consists of
\approx 16,000
EOSs generated this way.

III.2
Prior Set 2: Gaussian-Process model

In the GP model, for densities below
n=0.57n_{\mathrm{sat}}
we use the crust model by Baym, Pethick, and Sutherland
Baym et al. 1971
.
Above the crust-core transition density, we perform a GP regression in an auxiliary variable
\phi(n)~\equiv~-\ln(1/c_s^2(n) - 1)
, choosing the prior on this variable from the multivariate Gaussian distribution ,

\phi(n)\sim\mathcal{N}\bigl(-\ln(1/\bar{c}_s^2 - 1),K(n,n^{\prime})\bigr)\,,

(9)

with a Gaussian kernel
K(n,n^{\prime})=\eta\exp\bigl(-(n-n^{\prime})^{2}/2\ell^{2}\bigr)

Gorda et al. 2023b
.
In our hierarchical model the hyperparameters of the GP
\eta
,
\ell
, and
\bar{c}_{s}^{2}
are themselves drawn from normal distributions,

\begin{split}\eta&\sim\mathcal{N}(1.25,0.2^{2})\,,\\
\ell&\sim\mathcal{N}(1.0n_{\mathrm{sat}},(0.25n_{\mathrm{sat}})^{2})\,,\\
\bar{c}_{s}^{2}&\sim\mathcal{N}(0.5,0.25^{2})\,.\end{split}

(10)

For
n\leq 1.1n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, this prior is then conditioned on the chiral EFT results from Ref.
Hebeler et al. 2013
, with the average and difference between the “soft” and “stiff” EOSs in that work taken to be the mean and 90% credible interval for the conditioning
Gorda et al. 2023b
.
The final EOSs are then once again obtained by integrating
c_{s}^{2}(n)
.
The GP used in this work consists of 120,000 EOSs generated in this manner.

III.3
Astrophysical input

The astrophysical inputs that we consider in our analysis are the following:

1.

Radio-astronomy constraints:
We consider the mass-measurements of PSR J0348+0432 with
M=2.01\pm 0.04M_{\odot}

Antoniadis et al. 2013
and PSR J1624
-
2230 with
M=1.928\pm 0.017M_{\odot}

Fonseca et al. 2016
.
We approximate these uncertainties as normal distributions, which is valid to good accuracy.

2.

Gravitational-wave data from binary NS mergers: We consider the two-dimensional PDF for the tidal deformability and mass ratio at fixed chirp mass extracted from GW170817 by the LIGO-Virgo collaboration
Abbott et al. 2019
, using the PhenomPNRT waveform model with low-spin priors (see Figure 12 of
Abbott et al. 2019
).

3.

NICER observations: We consider the simultaneous mass and radius measurement of PSR J0740
+
6620 using the NICER + XMM-Newton data.
We have employed here the PDF from Ref.
Miller et al. 2021
(see their Fig. 1, right panel).

After conditioning our priors with the astrophysical input, we obtain a posterior sample of 4,000 EOSs for CSM and 46,000 EOSs for the GP model.
These inputs are identical to those used in Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
(referred to as “Pulsars +
\tilde{\Lambda}
”) with one exception.
We choose not to use the additional “Occam” factor related to the prior mass distribution of NSs,
{(m_{\mathrm{TOV}}-m_{\min})^{-4}}
(one power per mass integral), which penalizes high-mass EOSs that are completely consistent with astrophysical data; see Ref.
Miller et al. 2019b
;
Landry et al. 2020
for a discussion of this point.
These inputs are the same as used in Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
with the exception of the NICER measurement of PSR J0030+0451.
We have not included the latter in the present analysis as we found its impact on estimating the pQCD constraining power to be small.

Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
analyzed the astrophysical as well as the QCD inputs using a Bayesian approach whereas Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
performed a hard cut analysis.
We have checked that, as far as the treatment of the astrophysical data is concerned, this difference does not alter the conclusions of our paper regarding the impact of the QCD input.
The role played by the different treatments of the QCD input is discussed in the next section.

IV
Results

Next, we focus on the impact of the differences in methodology between Refs.
Gorda et al. 2023b
and
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
.
The three major differences between the setups in these works can be summarized as follows:

D1:

Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
used a fully Bayesian approach and the predictions are analyzed in terms of posterior probability distributions whereas Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
performed a hard cut analysis and focused on the envelope associated with all EOS models for all studied quantities.

D2:

The functional basis of the EOS modeling in the two works was different: Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
employed the non-parametric GP model (Prior set 2), while Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
employed the parametric CSM (Prior set 1).

D3:

The two works made different choices for
n_{\mathrm{term}}
:
Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
chose
n_{\mathrm{term}}=10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
whereas Ref.
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
chose
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}\approx 5-8n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, which is different for each EOS.
Hence, the works inferred the EOS in different density ranges.

To investigate point D3, we will discuss results for varying
n_{\mathrm{term}}
expressed either in terms of multiples of the saturation density
n_{\mathrm{sat}}
or in terms of the EOS-dependent central density of the maximum-mass neutron star,
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.

IV.1
Impact of the QCD input on the EOS

We show the QCD likelihood of Eq. (
II
) for the two EOS models, GP and CSM, in the upper panels of Fig.

, where both models terminate at
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
In particular, we show the sorted QCD likelihood for a large sample of EOSs drawn from the posterior distributions of the CSM and GP models after they have been conditioned on the astrophysical data.
EOSs for which the likelihood is
1
are accepted for all values of the pQCD renormalization scale
X
whereas EOSs that have a likelihood of
0
cannot be connected to pQCD with a causal and stable interpolation for any value of
X\in[1/2,2]
.
EOSs with likelihoods between the two limits can be connected to pQCD for some but not all values of

, see Fig.

for more details.

clearly shows that the choice of EOS model has a weak impact on the QCD likelihood, illustrating that the difference D2 plays a minor role in the following analysis.

We observe that for
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
and
\mu_{H}=2.6
GeV (
\mu_{H}=2.4
GeV) roughly 20% (
\sim 40\%
) of the EOSs cannot be connected to pQCD for all values of

and are therefore affected by the QCD input.
For a very small fraction of EOS, the QCD likelihood drops below

but no EOS has a vanishing QCD likelihood for this choice of

.
In contrast, the results are very different for
n_{\mathrm{term}}=10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, which was chosen in Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
.
Now, roughly
50-60\%
of EOS have a vanishing QCD likelihood.
This shows the strong impact of the choice of
n_{\mathrm{term}}
on the pQCD constraining power and explains a large part of the difference in the conclusions of Refs.
Gorda et al. 2023b
;
Somasundaram et al. 2023b
.
The strong differences between the two cases arise from the behavior the EOS must adopt beyond
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
to remain consistent with the QCD input at
10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
This is discussed further in Section
IV.3
.

We quantify the impact of the QCD likelihood function as a function of
n_{\mathrm{term}}
by computing the fraction of the marginalized likelihood (evidence) excluded by the QCD input

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\sum_{i}w^{\rm astro}_{i}\cdot w^{\rm QCD}_{i}}{\sum_{i}w^{\rm astro}_{i}}\,,

(11)

where the index
i
denotes the EOS, and the summation is performed over all EOSs in the ensemble.
The symbols
w^{\rm astro}
and
w^{\rm QCD}
correspond to the likelihood arising from the astrophysical and QCD inputs, respectively.
In Ref.
Pang et al. 2023
this quantity was called the average consistency (see also Eq. (25) of Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
), and corresponds to one minus the average QCD likelihood in the posterior ensemble conditioned with only the astrophysical input, which is indicated by the hatched area.

IV.3
Implications for the EOS beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}

So far, we have discussed the impact the QCD input has on the EOS for
n<n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
In this subsection, we discuss how the QCD constraint affects the behavior of the EOS at densities
n>n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
As shown in Fig.
1
, the QCD input affects the available parameter space for the EOS in the
p
–
\varepsilon
plane beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}
,
n_{\mathrm{term}}<n<n_{H}
.
Some EOS have a large available parameter space while others are tightly constrained.
One possible measure to gauge this freedom is the pQCD tension index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
defined before.
The left panel of Fig.
6
shows the allowed space in the
\varepsilon
–
p
plane beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}
for three EOSs with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})=0.75,0.85
, and
0.98
.
The EOS with the highest
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
must undergo a rapid phase-transition-like softening with
\Delta n\sim 20n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, followed by a segment of
c_{s}^{2}\approx 1
to remain consistent with pQCD calculations.
For
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=1
, this discontinuous behavior would be be completely determined – see Fig.
7
.
Note that this phase transition is not responsible for the onset of the unstable NS branch, which instead is determined by general relativity within the density range modeled by the EOS.
Instead, this phase transition takes place right above
n>n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
Therefore, the onset of this phase transition coinciding with
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is accidental.
If one wants to study the possibility of a phase transition determining the onset of the unstable branch, phase transitions must be included in the EOS models to a significant fraction, as in Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023d
.
For
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.75
a qualitatively similar softening is required but there is more freedom in the EOSs for
n>n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
By contrast, an EOS with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.5
is largely unconstrained above
n>n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.

In order to visualize the behavior above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
explicitly, we have chosen representative EOSs that fulfill the QCD constraint at
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
but not necessarily above.
For each of these EOSs, we generate 5000 extensions between
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}<n<15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
using the GP approach.
The extensions are then conditioned with the QCD constraint at
15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
so that the behavior between
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}<n<15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
remains consistent with the QCD input.
These extensions show how the EOS must evolve beyond
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
given its behavior below
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
Figure
8
shows the extensions for representative EOSs with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.5,0.75
and
0.85
(for
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.98
, our prior does not contain any valid samples).
We have checked that these EOSs are indeed representative of EOSs with these pQCD tension indices.
We find a difference in behavior above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
based on the value of the pQCD tension index at the TOV point
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})
.
For the highest index of
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})=0.85
shown in Fig.
8
, we observe that the EOS must soften drastically above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, with
c_{s}^{2}(n)\leq 0.1
all the way to
n=15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
Furthermore, for this EOS, the value of the index at
15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
remains in the range
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(15n_{\mathrm{sat}})\in[0.9,1]
, indicating that the EOSs needs to remain very soft.
For the smaller value of the pQCD tension index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})=0.75
, we similarly observe that the index at
15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
remains close to its value at
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
and the EOS must continue to remain soft.
Finally, for the lowest
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})=0.5
shown, the EOS extensions can obtain significantly different values of the pQCD tension index at
15n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.

We quantify the degree of softening as a function of pQCD tension index in Fig.
9
, where for 100 EOSs in our posterior with fixed values of the pQCD tension index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})
, we show the distribution of the average speed of sound of the extensions on the interval
n\in[8,15]n_{\mathrm{sat}}
.
From this figure we see that the average
c_{s}^{2}
for the extensions of EOSs with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.85,0.75
are strongly skewed to small values, below

and
c_{s}^{2}\lesssim 0.11

By contrast, the extensions of EOSs with
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=0.5
have a very large spread in the average
c_{s}^{2}
, with a shape that follows the prior distribution, indicating that there is no forced behavior in this case.

IV.4
Marginalization over EOS extensions beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}

The termination of the explicit EOS models at
n_{\mathrm{term}}
puts the EOS below and above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
in an asymmetric role; below the termination density our prior contains a large number of non-extreme EOSs by construction.
Above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, even the most extreme EOS extension of Fig.
7
is realized without any penalty.
This has been criticized in Ref.
Essick et al. 2023
, in which the authors point out that the construction of Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
is similar to performing a maximization, rather than a marginalization over the likelihoods of different possible extensions.
As our final point in this work, we address the marginalization over a set of extensions of the EOS beyond
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
We do so by constructing a model of possible high-density EOSs down from the pQCD EOS at
\mu_{\mathrm{pQCD}}=2.6
GeV, distinct from the prior model of NS EOSs below
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
Having an explicit model for EOSs also above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
allows us to incorporate further theoretical inputs.
Since the pQCD speed of sound is well converged at high densities, see Fig.
7
, we can further condition this model with the pQCD speed of sound.
We may do so either only at the pQCD matching point
n_{\mathrm{pQCD}}\approx 40n_{\mathrm{sat}}
(referred to below as “prior”), or over a larger range of densities where the speed of sound is under good theoretical control,
[25,40]n_{\mathrm{sat}}
(“conditioned”).

For modeling the EOS above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, we define another hierarchical GP model anchored to the (scale-averaged) pQCD EOS defined with the following hyperparameters:

\begin{split}\ell&\sim\mathcal{U}(1n_{\mathrm{sat}},20n_{\mathrm{sat}})\,,\\
\eta&\sim\mathcal{N}(1.25,0.25^{2})\,,\\
\bar{c}_{s}^{2}&\sim\mathcal{N}(0.3,0.3^{2})\,,\end{split}

(12)

see Fig.
10
(left).
Because of the large interval in density between the typical
n_{\mathrm{term}}
and
n_{\mathrm{QCD}}\approx 40n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, we choose a more general prior for the correlation length
\ell
here than was done in Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
.
We also choose the prior on
\bar{c}_{s}^{2}
to have a mean centered near the conformal value of
1/3
, but allow it to vary broadly within the model.
To be conservative with the conditioned GP, we take the standard deviation of the GP at a given
n\in[25,40]n_{\mathrm{sat}}
to equal twice of that of the scale-averaged pQCD calculation.

The procedure for applying this high-density prior as a model for EOS extensions is as follows.
First, we use either the low-density GP or CSM to model the NS EOS up to a specified termination density
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
Then, for this
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, we calculate a QCD likelihood function by constructing the posterior distribution for different
\varepsilon
–
p
values that arise from marginalizing over the new high-density GP model above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
Concretely, we calculate the distribution by performing a kernel-density estimation of the
\varepsilon
–
p
values of the high-density GP model at
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
This posterior distribution is then reinterpreted as a new QCD likelihood function within the NS EOS inference at
n_{\mathrm{term}}
that marginalizes over the hierarchical model above, as opposed to the construction from Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
, which instead returns a weight of 1 for any
\varepsilon
–
p
point within the allowed region at
n_{\mathrm{term}}
for a fixed
X
.
More details of this procedure are given in Appendix
A
.

In Fig.
10
we show in the left panel a small sample of EOSs drawn from these models of EOS extensions, showing a broad range of possible behaviors.
The conditioning of the magenta GP is clearly visible.
In the middle panel we show the QCD likelihood function resulting from the conditioned GP as applied at
n_{\mathrm{term}}=5.5n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, compared to the constraints arising from Ref.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
for the chiral EFT and pQCD constraints.
This value of
n_{\mathrm{term}}
approximately corresponds to the mean of the
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
for our ensembles using only the astrophysical input (see Fig.
2
).
We see that the likelihood function is mostly peaked in the interior of the
X=1
pQCD region.
As has been pointed out already in Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023d
, the impact of the QCD input within the inference of the NS EOS occurs primarily along the upper boundary of the pQCD region in Fig.
10
, since the astrophysical constraints already reduce the allowed
\varepsilon
–
p
region in the neighborhood of the other extreme boundaries (cf. also the discussion of the upper edge of the
\varepsilon
–
p
regions in Fig.
5
).

Turning to the rightmost panel of Fig.
10
, we see the effect of the marginalization over the GP model of EOS extensions directly as the change in the allowed
\varepsilon
–
p
region at
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
As expected from our observations of the central panel, we see a shift towards smaller
p
values in this figure.
The prior GP shows a similar shift to the effect of using a termination density that is about 20-40% larger without the marginalization (cf. the middle and right columns of Fig.
5
), while the conditioned GP shows a much more pronounced softening, more comparable to the effect of the QCD input applied at
n_{\mathrm{term}}=10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
(cf. Fig.
5
).
In both cases, the quantitative effect is to shift the posterior pressure distribution within the most massive NSs to smaller values, with the conditioned GP shifted more strongly.
That the upper limit for the conditioned GP is significantly below that of the prior demonstrates that the additional constraint is indeed due to the conditioning and not the assumed prior.
This decreased upper bound is natural, as the pQCD sound speed used in the conditioning is inconsistent with the stiff high-density segment seen in Fig.
7
, thus affecting the EOS at lower densities as well.

Lastly, we observe that the credible regions of
\varepsilon
–
p
are almost indistinguishable between
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
and
10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, demonstrating independence of this parameter when using the conditioned GP extension.

In this paper, we have studied the impact of the high-density QCD input on the modeling of the dense-matter EOS, and explained the different findings presented in the literature.
In particular, using the most general QCD input we found that the constraining power of the QCD input depends sensitively on the chosen termination density
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
For the conservative choice of
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, the impact of the QCD input is largely removed, while the constraining power rises fast with
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
This difference in constraining power just beyond
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
arises from EOSs that undergo a change in behavior that is qualitatively different from the assumed priors.
In particular, we have shown that most EOSs that are removed for
n_{\mathrm{term}}>n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
have a large pQCD tension index, leading to an abrupt softening above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
that is disfavored when the EOS priors employed here are constructed to higher densities.
Hence, to remain in agreement with the QCD input, the EOS needs either to soften below
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
or it has to exhibit a drastic softening immediately above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
that persists for an extended density range before stiffening again dramatically, see also Ref.
Annala et al. 2020
.
This behavior is seen in the EOS extensions depicted in Fig.
8
.
We reiterate that the strong softening coinciding with
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is accidental, as
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is already set by the behavior of the EOS at lower densities.
While it cannot be excluded by the generic thermodynamic considerations we employ, it would be interesting to see what microphysical behavior would be necessary to realize such behavior within dense matter.
Note, however, that high speeds of sound above
n\gtrsim 25n_{\mathrm{sat}}
are inconsistent with pQCD calculations.

We note that different choices for
n_{\mathrm{term}}
,
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
and
n_{\mathrm{term}}>n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, have their merits and answer slightly different questions: the choice of the termination density eventually reflects the phenomenological question at hand.
On the one hand, the EOS is a fundamental prediction of QCD and exists independently of NSs at densities above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
The EOS above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is required in modeling the post-merger phase of binary NS mergers (see Refs.
Ujevic et al. 2023
;
Tootle et al. 2022
1
1

1

Note that Ref.
Ujevic et al. 2023
found that multi-messenger signals from binary NS merges are not effective at constraining the EOS above
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
, rendering the QCD input the only source of information in this density range in the near future.
)
and sets a prediction for the quantity that may be verified in the future using, e.g., lattice field theory
2
2

2

On this point, see in particular two recent works in Refs.
Moore and Gorda 2023
;
Fujimoto and Reddy 2023
.
.
In order to address these questions, the EOS must be modeled beyond the densities reached in stable NSs, and
n_{\mathrm{term}}>n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
is required.
On the other hand, one can be more conservative and use
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
if one chooses to infer only the EOS along the stable NS branch.
This choice avoids prior-dependent modeling of the unstable branch, allowing for more robust conclusions.
Similarly, for an even more conservative modeling of NSs, one could imagine terminating the EOS model at
n_{\mathrm{term}}=n(2.1M_{\odot})
which is the mass of the heaviest observed neutron star.
Whatever application one has in mind, it is crucial that the QCD input in the form of Ref.
Gorda et al. 2023b
be applied at the highest density that will be studied.
It is inconsistent to apply the QCD input at
n_{\mathrm{term}}
and then consider the model for even higher densities
n>n_{\mathrm{term}}
.

Since the pQCD speed of sound is well converged to even lower densities than the pressure
Gorda et al. 2021
;
Gorda et al. 2023a
, we have additionally constructed a QCD likelihood function that incorporates this additional information.
We have done so by constructing a further GP model down from the pQCD EOS calculation and conditioning it with the pQCD calculation of
c_{s}^{2}
.
This also addresses the points raised in Ref.
Essick et al. 2023
, as this new likelihood function involves marginalizing over the new GP above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
.
We find that the conditioned GP gives results that are independent of
n_{\mathrm{term}}\geq n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
(see Fig.
10
) and agrees with results obtained for
n_{\mathrm{term}}=10n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, see Fig.
11
.
The conditioned likelihood function is made publicly available in Ref.
Komoltsev et al. 2024
.
We also see in Fig.
11
that the unconditioned GP leads to similar results as choosing
n_{\mathrm{term}}=1.2n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
or by restricting the pQCD tension index
I_{\mathrm{pQCD}}(n_{\mathrm{TOV}})<0.75
.

Finally, we discuss the limitations of our study.
An important finding of this work is that the QCD input removes EOS that are very stiff around
n_{\mathrm{TOV}}
.
However, we allow for extreme stiffening of the EOS at low densities, around
1-2n_{\mathrm{sat}}
, where EFT approaches can otherwise provide constraints on the EOS and typically suggest the EOS to be soft
Tews et al. 2018a
;
Drischler et al. 2020
.
In this work, we have not varied the maximal density up to which we consider EFT input, which might bias our EOS sets to be stiffer than if we marginalized over the maximal EFT density
Capano et al. 2019
;
Essick et al. 2020
.
We also have not marginalized over the scale
\mu_{\mathrm{pQCD}}
where we evaluate the pQCD values; marginalizing over lower values than used here would provide a more restrictive QCD input
Gorda et al. 2023c
.
Furthermore, we only considered two EOS priors that model the EOS in terms of the speed of sound and are, hence, rather similar.
We have also not considered astrophysical data from other X-ray sources such as quiescent low-mass X-ray binaries or photospheric-radius–expansion burst, which decrease the upper bound on the radii with respect to the NICER X-ray data
Al-Mamun et al. 2021
.
Finally, for the purpose of marginalizing over EOS extensions above
n_{\mathrm{term}}
, it would be interesting to investigate other prior choices for the EOS model, such as GP models with a density dependent correlation length.

Introduction

In this paper, we prove some sharp estimates for the sublevel set and superlevel set of certain mappings having a log-subharmonic modulus. Sharp estimates for these sets have been substantial for the development of optimal estimates in the Faber-Krahn inequality in the works of various authors
[
9
,

,

,

,

. In most of those results, functions with compact support at the domain have been considered. In this paper, we consider a different case, where the functions do not have compact support.

1.1.
Quasiregular mappings

Assume that
\Omega,D
are domains in the complex plane.
For a smooth mapping
f:\Omega\to D
, we say that
f
is
K
-quasiregular for
K\geq 1
if

(1.1)

|f_{\bar{z}}|\leq\frac{K-1}{K+1}|f_{z}|

for
z\in\Omega
. If
f
is an injection, then we call it
K
-quasi-conformal. If
f
is quasiregular, then its Jacobian

J(z,f)=|f_{z}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}\geq\frac{4K}{(1+K)^{2}}|f_{z}|^{2}\geq 0.

So
f
is sense-preserving. We say that a smooth mapping of the unit disk onto itself is
K
-quasiregular at the boundary if
f
has a differentiable extension up to the boundary and the inequality (
1.1
) is true for
z=e^{is}
for almost every
s\in[0,2\pi]
. In that case, for almost every
s
,

(1.2)

|\partial_{r}f(z)|\leq K|\partial_{t}f(z)|,

where
z=e^{is}
.

1.2.
Index of a curve

The integer
d
is called the winding number of a closed piecewise smooth curve
\gamma:[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\{w\}
around
w
, representing the total number of times that the curve travels counterclockwise around
w
. That number is denoted by
\mathrm{Ind}_{\gamma}(w)
and can be calculated by the formula

\mathrm{Ind}_{\gamma}(w)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\gamma^{\prime}(s)}{\gamma(s)-w}\,ds.

Assume that
\mathbb{D}
is the unit disk and
\mathbf{T}
is the unit circle.
If
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a smooth mapping having a smooth extension up to the boundary
\mathbf{T}=\partial\mathbb{D}
, then the curve
\gamma(s)=f(e^{is})
,
s\in[0,2\pi]
, has a finite index around 0 or any other point
w\in\mathbb{D}
.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a real analytic mapping of the unit disk onto itself which is
K
-quasiregular at the boundary having a log-subharmonic modulus. For
t\in[0,1]
, let
A_{t}=\{z:|f(z)|\geq t\}
. Then

\mu(t)=|A_{t}|\geq\pi-\pi t^{2/(Kd)},

where
d=\mathrm{Ind}_{f(\mathbf{T})}(0)
is the winding number of
f(\mathbf{T})
around the origin. The equality is attained if and only if
f(z)=e^{ids}|z|^{Kd}
,
z=|z|e^{is}
.

We need the following simple lemma:

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f
be a sense-preserving smooth mapping of the unit disk onto itself having a smooth extension up to the boundary so that
\lim_{|z|\to 1}|f(z)|=1
. Then,

\int_{|z|=1}|\partial_{s}f(z)|\,|dz|=2\pi\mathrm{Ind}_{f(\mathbf{T})}(0)=2\pi d.

Moreover,
f(e^{is})=e^{i\phi(s)}
, where
\phi:[0,2\pi]\to[\tau,\tau+2\pi d]
is a non-decreasing surjection.

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.

First of all, if
z=re^{is}
, then
J(z,f)=\frac{1}{r}\Im(f_{s}(z)\overline{f_{r}(z)})\geq 0
. Thus, for
z=e^{is}
,
f(e^{is})=e^{i\phi(s)}
for some smooth function
\phi:[0,2\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
so that
\phi(2\pi)=\phi(0)+2k\pi
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and thus

(1.3)

J(z,f)=\phi^{\prime}(s)\Im(if(z)\overline{f_{r}(z)})=\phi^{\prime}(s)\Re(f(z)\overline{f_{r}(z)}).

Since

\lim_{r\to 1}\frac{1-|f(z)|^{2}}{1-r}=2\Re[f_{r}(e^{is})\overline{f(e^{is})}]\geq 0,

in view of (
1.3
), we obtain that
\phi^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
.

Now,

|z|=1\Rightarrow|f(z)|=1

implies that

|z|=1\Rightarrow\langle\partial_{s}f(z),f(z)\rangle=\Re(\partial_{s}f(z)\overline{f(z)})=0.

Thus,

|z|=1\Rightarrow i\phi^{\prime}(s)=\partial_{s}f(z)\overline{f(z)}=\pm i|\partial_{s}f(z)|.

Since
\phi^{\prime}(s)\geq 0
, we have

|z|=1\Rightarrow\frac{\partial_{s}f(z)}{f(z)}=i|\partial_{s}f(z)|.

Thus,

2\pi di=i\int_{|z|=1}\phi^{\prime}(s)\,dz=i\int_{|z|=1}|\partial_{s}f(z)|\,|dz|,

or what is the same,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\phi^{\prime}(s)\,ds=2\pi d=\phi(2\pi)-\phi(0).

We conclude that
\phi:[0,2\pi]\to[\tau,\tau+2\pi d]
is a non-decreasing function.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
u(z)=|f(z)|
and
A_{t}=\{z:u(z)>t\}
,
0<t<1
.
We start with the co-area formula

\mu(t)=|A_{t}|=\int_{A_{t}}dx=\int_{t}^{\max u}\int_{|u(z)|=\kappa}|\diff z|\,d\kappa.

Then we get

(1.4)

-\mu^{\prime}(t)=\int_{\{z:u(z)=t\}}|\nabla u(z)|^{-1}|\diff z|

along with the claim that
\{z:u(z)=t\}=\partial A_{t}\cap\mathbb{D}
and that this set is a smooth curve for almost all
t\in(0,t_{\circ})
.
These assertions follow the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 3.2]
. We point out that, since
u
is real analytic, it is a well-known fact from measure theory that the level set
\{z:u(z)=t\}
has zero measure
[
8
]
, and this is equivalent to the fact that
\mu
is continuous.

Following the approach from
[
9
]
, our next step is to apply the Hölder inequality with
p=q=2
.

Then

|\partial_{\circ}A_{t}|^{2}=\left(\int_{\partial_{\circ}A_{t}}|\diff z|\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\partial_{\circ}A_{t}}|\nabla u|^{-1}|\diff z|\int_{\partial_{\circ}A_{t}}|\nabla u||\diff z|,

where
\partial_{\circ}A_{t}
is part of the boundary
A_{t}
inside of the unit disk which coincides with the level set
\{z:u(z)=t\}
, shortly written as
u=t
.
Further, let
\mu(t)=|A_{t}|
. Then

|\partial_{\circ}A_{t}|^{2}\leq\mu^{\prime}(t)(-\int_{u=t}|\nabla u|)|\diff z|.

Further by isoperimetric inequality we have

(1.5)

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq|\partial_{\circ}A_{t}|^{2}.

The equality in (
1.5
) is attained if and only if
\mathbb{D}\setminus A_{t}
is a disk. Thus

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq\mu^{\prime}(t)(-\int_{u=t}|\nabla u(z)||\diff z|).

Now
|u(z)||\nabla u(z)|=-\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>
, where
\mathbf{n}
is the outer normal at
\partial_{\circ}A_{t}
. Therefore

\int_{u=t}|\nabla u||\diff z|=-t\int_{u=t}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|.

Hence

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq-t\mu^{\prime}(t)(-\int_{u=t}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|),

where
\mathbf{n}
is the outer normal at
\partial A_{t}
.
Now we state the Green formula

(1.6)

\int_{|z|=1}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|+\int_{u=t}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|=\int_{A_{t}}\Delta\log u\diff x\diff y.

By using (
1.6
) we have

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq-t\mu^{\prime}(t)\left(\int_{|z|=1}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|-\int_{A_{t}}\Delta\log u\diff x\diff y\right).

Since
\Delta\log u\geq 0
, then

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq-t\mu^{\prime}(t)\int_{|z|=1}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|.

For
|z|=1
we have
u(z)=1
, and then in view of (
1.2
) and Lemma
1.2

\begin{split}\int_{|z|=1}\left<\nabla\log u,\mathbf{n}\right>&=\int_{|z|=1}\frac{\left<\nabla u(z),\mathbf{n}\right>}{u(z)}|\diff z|\\
&=\int_{|z|=1}\left<\nabla u(z),\mathbf{n}\right>|\diff z|\\
&=\int_{|z|=1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}|\diff z|\\
&\leq\int_{|z|=1}\left|\frac{\partial f(z)}{\partial r}\right||\diff z|\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{2\pi}K\left|\frac{\partial f(e^{is})}{\partial s}\right|\diff s=2\pi Kd.\end{split}

Hence

4\pi(\pi-\mu(t))\leq-2t\mu^{\prime}(t)\pi Kd

i.e.

(1.7)

2(\pi-\mu(t))\leq-t\mu^{\prime}(t)Kd.

Let
\lambda(s)=\mu^{-1}(s)
. Then

2(\pi-s)\leq-\frac{Kd\lambda(s)}{\lambda^{\prime}(s)}

or what is the same

-\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(s)}{\lambda(s)}\leq\frac{Kd}{2(\pi-s)}.

By integrating in the interval
[0,s]
, the previous inequality for
s<\pi
, having in mind the condition
\mu(1)=0
, i.e.
\lambda(0)=1
we obtain

\log\frac{1}{\lambda(s)}\leq\frac{Kd}{2}\log\frac{\pi}{\pi-s}.

Therefore

\frac{1}{\lambda(s)^{2/(Kd)}}\leq\frac{\pi}{\pi-s}.

Hence

\pi-s\leq\pi\lambda^{2/(Kd)}(s).

Thus

\mu(t)\geq\pi(1-t^{2/(Kd)}).

Since the distribution of
f(z)=e^{ids}|z|^{Kd}
is
\mu_{K}(t)=\pi-\pi t^{2/(Kd)}
,
we get

\mu(t)\geq\mu_{K}(t).

∎

Corollary 1.3
.

Under conditions of Theorem
1.1
, let
\nu(t)=|\{z:|f(z)|<t\}|
. Then
\nu(t)\leq\pi t^{2/(Kd)}
, with an equality if and only if
f(z)=e^{ids}|z|^{Kd}
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
f(z)=\frac{z+a}{1+z\bar{a}}.
Then for
0<t<1
define
A_{t}=\{z:|f(z)|>t\}.
Let
\mu_{a}(t)=|A_{t}|
.
Then by taking the change of variables
w=f(z)=re^{i\tau}
, having in mind the equations

g(w)=f^{-1}(w)=\frac{w-a}{1-w\bar{a}},\ \ \ |g^{\prime}(w)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{2}}{|1-w\bar{a}|^{4}},

for
z=x+iy
, we obtain

\begin{split}\mu_{a}(t)&=\int_{|f(z)|>t}\diff x\diff y\\
&=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{t}^{1}r\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{2}}{|1-w\bar{a}|^{4}}\diff r\diff\tau.\end{split}

Now we use the Parseval’s formula to obtain that

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{t}^{1}r\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{2}}{|1-w\bar{a}|^{4}}\diff r\diff\tau&=2\pi(1-|a|^{2})^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{t}^{1}(1+m)^{2}|a|^{2m}r^{2m+1}\diff r\\
&=\frac{\pi\left(1-t^{2}\right)\left(1-|a|^{4}t^{2}\right)}{\left(1-|a|^{2}t^{2}\right)^{2}}.\end{split}

If we denote
r=|a|
, and
\phi(r)=\frac{\pi\left(1-t^{2}\right)\left(1-r^{4}t^{2}\right)}{\left(1-r^{2}t^{2}\right)^{2}}
, then we obtain

\phi^{\prime}(r)=\frac{4\pi r\left(1-r^{2}\right)t^{2}\left(1-t^{2}\right)}{\left(1-r^{2}t^{2}\right)^{3}}

which is clearly a non-negative function. In particular
\phi
is increasing and thus
\mu_{0}(t)\leq\mu_{a}(t)
which confirms our theorem.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
f
be a real analytic mapping of the unit disk onto itself which is
K-
quasi-regular at the boundary having a log-subharmonic modulus. Assume also that
d=\mathrm{Ind}_{f(\mathbf{T})}(0)
. Then for
p\geq 1
,

(1.8)

\|f\|_{p}\geq\left(\frac{2\pi}{2+Kdp}\right)^{1/p}.

The equality in (
1.8
) is attained for
f_{K}(z)=e^{ids}|z|^{Kd}
.

Proof.

Let
\mu(t)=\{z:|f(z)|\geq t\}
.
We first have

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{1}\mu(t)t^{p-1}\diff t.

Then from Theorem
1.1
,
\mu(t)\geq\mu_{K}(t)=\pi(1-t^{2/(Kd)})
, which implies the inequality

\|f\|^{p}_{p}\geq\|f_{K}\|^{p}_{p}=p\int_{0}^{1}\mu_{K}(t)t^{p-1}\diff t=\frac{2\pi}{2+Kdp}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Example 1.7
.

Let
f(z)=g(r)e^{is}
, where
z=re^{is}
and

g(s)=\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{1}\left(\frac{a}{u}-\frac{\int_{u}^{1}\frac{1}{4}h(v)\diff v}{u}\right)\,\diff u\right).

Then

\Delta\log|f(z)|=h(r).

Then
g(1)=1
. Moreover
f
is
K-
quasiconformal, where

K\geq\max_{s}\frac{sg^{\prime}(s)}{g(s)}=\max_{s}\left(a-\int_{s}^{1}\frac{h(v)}{4}\diff v\right)=a.

Moreover
f
maps the unit disk
\mathbb{U}
onto itself if

{a}-{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{4}h(v)\diff v}>0.

So for
0<a\leq K
, and assuming that
h
is an integrable positive function satisfying the condition

\int_{0}^{1}h(s)\diff s<4a,

f
is a

quasiconformal mapping so that

is subharmonic and satisfies the conditions of our theorem.

Corollary 1.8
.

Assume that
f
is a holomorphic mapping of the unit disk into itself, so that
\lim_{|z|\to 1}|f(z)|=1
. For
t\in[0,1]
let
\nu(t)=|\{z:|f(z)|<t\}|.
Then
\nu(t)\leq\pi t^{2/d}
, where
d
is the number of zeros of
f
inside
\mathbb{D}
including multiplicity. The equality in the last inequality is attained for some number
t\in(0,1)
if and only if
f(z)=e^{i\tau}z^{d}
and in that case the equality is attained for every
t\in[0,1]
.

In other words

(1.9)

|\{z:|B(z)|<t\}|\leq\pi t^{2/d},

where

B(z)=e^{is}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{z-a_{k}}{1-z\overline{a_{k}}},

with an equality in (
1.9
) for a single
t\in(0,1)
if and only if
a_{k}=0
for every
k
.

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.

First of all, if
f
is a holomorphic function mapping the unit disk onto itself such that
\lim_{|z|\to 1}|f(z)|=1
, then, by a result of Fatou
[
2
]
,
f
is a finite Blaschke product. Moreover, (
1.9
) is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
. If, for some
t\neq 0,1
, we have
|\{z:|B(z)|<t\}|=\pi t^{2/d}
, then equality holds in the isoperimetric inequality (
1.5
). In other words,
\{z:|B(z)|=t\}
is a circle
|z-z_{0}|=\rho=t^{1/d}
. Therefore,

|e^{is}\prod_{k=1}^{d}\frac{\rho e^{i\tau}-a_{k}}{1-\rho e^{i\tau}\overline{a_{k}}}|=t,\quad\tau\in[0,2\pi].

Thus, the harmonic function

u(z)=\log\left|B(z)\cdot\left(\frac{1-z\overline{z_{0}}}{z-z_{0}}\right)^{d}\right|

is well-defined in the set
A_{t}=\{z:t<|B(z)|<1\}
because
z_{k}\not\in A_{t}
for every
k=0,\dots,d
. Recall that
B(a_{k})=0
for
k=1,\dots,d
and
z_{0}
is a point that is not in
A_{t}
, because if this were the case,
A_{t}
would coincide with the unit disk, which is a contradiction. Thus,
u
is equal to zero on the boundary
\partial A_{t}=\mathbf{T}\cup\partial_{\circ}A_{t}
. By the maximum principle applied to
u
and
-u
, we conclude that
u\equiv 0
. In particular,

B(z)=c\left(\frac{z-z_{0}}{1-z\overline{z_{0}}}\right)^{d},\quad|c|=1.

Since
|B(z)|=t
if and only if
|z-z_{0}|=t^{1/d}
, we conclude that
z_{0}=0
.
∎

Density properties for fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces
EL-Houcine OUALI*, Azeddine BAALAL, Mohamed BERGHOUT

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new fractional Musielak-Sobolev space W s , Φ x , y ​ ( Ω ) W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega) where Ω \Omega is an open subset in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} and we show some density properties of smooth and compactly supported functions in this space.

1.
Introduction

In recent years there has been an increasing attention on problems involving the theory of fractional modular spaces, in particular the fractional order Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
W^{s,\Phi}(\Omega)
(see
[
16
,
9
,
10
,
8
]
), and the fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponents
W^{s,q(.),p(.,.)}(\Omega)
(see
[
7
,
6
,
20
,
14
,
19
,
5
]
), which are two natural extensions of classical fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
(see
[
15
,
12
]
), and they are two special kinds of fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces (see
[
2
,
3
,
13
]
). Particularly, one of this problems is the density of smooth and compactly supported functions in these spaces, see
[
6
,
16
,
4
,
17
]
.

The objective of this paper is to provide some density properties of smooth and compactly supported functions in a new fractional Musielak-Sobolev space.

We suppose that
\Omega
is an open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geqslant 1
and
\Phi:\Omega\times\Omega\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow

[0,\infty)
is a Carathéodory function defined by

\Phi_{x,y}(t):=\Phi(x,y,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(x,y,s)ds,

(1.1)

where
\varphi:\Omega\times\Omega\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a function satisfying the following assumptions:

(i)

\varphi(x,y,0)=0
and
\varphi(x,y,t)>0
for any
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,t>0
;

(ii)

t\mapsto\varphi(x,y,t)
is increasing on
[0,\infty)
;

(iii)

t\mapsto\varphi(x,y,t)
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
,
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\varphi(x,y,t)=0
and
\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\varphi(x,y,t)=\infty
, for all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
.

It is not hard to show that hypotheses
(i)-(iii)
imply that
\Phi_{x,y}(t)
is continuous, strictly increasing and convex in
t\geq 0
for any
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
. Moreover,
\Phi_{x,y}(0)=0
,
\Phi_{x,y}(t)>0
for all
t>0
and
\Phi_{x,y}(t)
has a sublinear decay at zero and a superlinear growth near infinity in the variable
t
:

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\Phi_{x,y}(t)}{t}=0,\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi_{x,y}(t)}{t}=\infty.

A function
\Phi
defined as before is known as generalized N-function.

We also consider the function
\widehat{\varphi}_{x}(t):=\widehat{\varphi}(x,t)=\varphi(x,x,t)
for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times[0,+\infty)
. This function is increasing homeomorphism from
\mathbb{R^{+}}
onto itself. Let us set

\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(t):=\widehat{\Phi}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\varphi}_{x}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\text{ for all }t\geqslant 0.

(1.2)

Then,
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}
is also a generalized N-function.

Remark 1.1
.

We know that for all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
,
\Phi_{x,y}:\mathbb{R^{+}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R^{+}}
is a strictly increasing continuous function. It follows that for each
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
, the function
t\mapsto

\Phi_{x,y}(t)
has an inverse function denoted by
t\mapsto\Phi_{x,y}^{-1}(t)
.

For technical reasons, we will assume, throughout this paper, that :

(a)

\delta K(x,y)\in L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega\times\Omega)
where
\displaystyle K(x,y)=\frac{1}{|x-y|^{s}\Phi_{x,y}^{-1}\left(|x-y|^{N}\right)}
and

\displaystyle\delta=\min\left\{1,|x-y|\right\}
.

(b)

\Phi_{x,y}
and
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}
are locally integrables, that is for any constant number
c>0
and for every compact set
A\subset\Omega
we have :

\int_{A\times A}\Phi_{x,y}(c)dxdy<\infty\textsl{ and }\int_{A}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(c)dx<\infty.

(1.3)

(c)

\Phi_{x,y}
and
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}
satisfy the following boundedness conditions :

\sup_{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega}\Phi_{x,y}(1)<\infty\textsl{ and }\sup_{x\in\Omega}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(1)<\infty.

(1.4)

(d)

\Phi(x-z,y-z,t)=\Phi(x,y,t)\quad\quad\forall(x,y),(z,z)\in\Omega\times\Omega\textit{, }\forall t\geqslant 0.

(1.5)

(e)

There exist two positive constants
\varphi^{+}
and
\varphi^{-}
such that

1<\varphi^{-}\leqslant\frac{t\varphi_{x,y}(t)}{\Phi_{x,y}(t)}\leqslant\varphi^{+}<+\infty\quad\forall(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

(1.6)

This relation implies that

1<\varphi^{-}\leqslant\frac{t\widehat{\varphi}_{x}(t)}{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(t)}\leqslant\varphi^{+}<+\infty,\quad\forall x\in\Omega,\quad\forall t\geqslant 0.

(1.7)

In light of assumption (
1.6
),
\Phi_{x,y}
and
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}
satisfy the so-called
\Delta_{2}
-condition (see
[
21
]
), written
\Phi_{x,y}\in\Delta_{2}
and
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\in\Delta_{2}
, that is there exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\Phi_{x,y}(2t)\leqslant C_{1}\Phi_{x,y}(t)\quad\text{ for all }(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega,\quad\text{ and all }t\geqslant 0\text{, }

(1.8)

and

\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(2t)\leqslant C_{2}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(t)\quad\text{ for all }x\in\Omega,\quad\text{ and all }t\geqslant 0.

(1.9)

For the function
\widehat{\Phi}_{x}
given in (
1.2
), the Musielak class is defined by

K_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ mesurable : }\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(|u(x)|)\mathrm{d}x<\infty\right\}\text{, }

and the Musielak space
L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)
is defined by

L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ mesurable : }\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(\lambda|u(x)|)\mathrm{d}x<\infty\text{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

The space
L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)
is a Banach space endowed with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leqslant 1\right\}.

The inequality (
1.9
) implies that
L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)=K_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)
(see
[
22
]
).

Let
s\in(0,1)
, for the function
\Phi_{x,y}
given in (
1.1
) we introduce the fractional Musielak-Sobolev space
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
as follows

\displaystyle W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\lambda|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{~d}y<\infty\textsl{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

This space is endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)}:=\|u\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)}+[u]_{s,\Phi_{x,y}},

(1.10)

where
[.]_{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
is the Gagliardo seminorm defined by

[u]_{s,\Phi_{x,y}}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)}{\lambda}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{~d}y\leqslant 1\right\}.

Remark 1.2
.

(a)

The fractional Musielak-Sobolev space was introduced in
[
2
]
as follows

\displaystyle W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L_{\hat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\lambda|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{~d}y}{|x-y|^{N}}<\infty\textsl{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

We observe that this definition creates problems in the calculus and in the embedding results, for example, the Borel measure defined as
d\mu=\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}
is not finish in the neighbourhood of the origin.

(b)

For the case:
\Phi_{x,y}(t)=\Phi(t)
, i.e., when
\Phi
is independent of the variables
x
and
y
, we have that
L_{\Phi}
and
W^{s,\Phi}
are Orlicz spaces and fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces respectively (see
[
4
]
) such that

\displaystyle W^{s,\Phi}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L_{\Phi}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Phi\left(\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{\lambda|x-y|^{s}\Phi^{-1}\left(|x-y|^{N}\right)}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{~d}y<\infty\textsl{ for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

(c)

For the case:
\Phi_{x,y}(t)=|t|^{p(x,y)}
for all
(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
, where
p:\Omega\times\Omega\longrightarrow(1,+\infty)
is a continuous bounded function such that

1<p^{-}=\min_{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega}p(x,y)\leqslant p(x,y)\leqslant p^{+}=\max_{(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega}p(x,y)<+\infty,

(1.11)

and
p
is symmetric, that is,
p(x,y)=p(y,x)\text{ for all }(x,y)\in\Omega\times\Omega
.

If we set
\overline{p}(x)=p(x,x)
for all
x\in\Omega
, then, we replace
L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}
by
L^{\overline{p}(x)}
, and
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
by
W^{s,p(x,y)}
and we refer them as variable exponent Lebesgue spaces, and fractional Sobolev spaces with variable exponent respectively, (see
[
5
,
7
,
6
,
1
,
14
,
19
]
), defined by

L^{\overline{p}(x)}(\Omega)=\left\{u:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\text{ measurable }:\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{\overline{p}(x)}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty\right\},

and

\displaystyle W^{s,p(x,y)}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{\overline{p}(x)}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{\lambda^{p(x,y)}|x-y|^{sp(x,y)+N}}\mathrm{~d}x\mathrm{~d}y<+\infty\text{, for some }\lambda>0\right\}.

with the norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p(x,y)}(\Omega)}=\|u\|_{L^{\overline{p}(x)}(\Omega)}+[u]_{W^{s,p(x,y)}(\Omega)},

where
[.]_{W^{s,p(x,y)}(\Omega)}
is the Gagliardo seminorm with variable exponent given by

[u]_{W^{s,p(x,y)}(\Omega)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p(x,y)}}{\lambda^{p(x,y)}|x-y|^{N+sp(x,y)}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{~d}y\leqslant 1\right\}.

By the same way in
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
it is easy to prove that
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
is a separable and reflexive Banach space.

Let
s\in(0,1)
, we define the space
W^{s}K_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
as follows

W^{s}K_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in K_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega):|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\in K_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega\times\Omega)\right\}.

Remark 1.3
.

(a)

We have
W^{s}K_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
.

(b)

W^{s}K_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)=W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
if and only if
\Phi_{x,y}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-condition.

Let
C(\overline{\Omega})
denote the space of uniformly continuous functions equipped with the supremum norm
\|u\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}|u(x)|
. By
C^{k}(\overline{\Omega}),k\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the space of all functions
u
such that
\partial_{\alpha}u:=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}u}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\partial^{\alpha_{n}}x_{n}}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
for all multi-indices
\alpha=\left(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right),|\alpha|:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\leq k
. This space is equipped with the norm
\sup_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\|\partial_{\alpha}f\right\|_{\infty}
. Let
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})=\bigcap^{\infty}_{k=0}C^{k}(\overline{\Omega})
and let
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
denote the set of all infinitely differentiable functions in
\Omega
. The subspace
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
consist of all those functions in
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
that have compact support in
\Omega
. We also denote by C a constant whose value may change from line to line.

We say that an open domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
-extension domain if there exists a continuous linear extension operator

\mathcal{E}:W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)\longrightarrow W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)

such that
\left.\mathcal{E}u\right|_{\Omega}=u
for each
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
. For example domains with Lipschitz boundary are
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
-extension domains (the proof is similar to the one of Theorem 2.4 in
[
3
]
).

This paper is organized as follows. In section
2
, we state technical and
elementary lemmas useful along the paper. The proof of the main results is given in Section
3
.
The first main result is an approximation with a continuous and compactly
supported function, see Theorem
3.1
. The second main result is an approximation with smooth and compactly supported
functions, see Theorem
3.2
. The third result of our paper is the density of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
in
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is a
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
-extension domain, see Theorem
3.3
.

Some preliminaries results

From
[
2

we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that (
1.6
) is satisfied. Then the following inequalities hold true

\Phi_{x,y}(\sigma t)\geqslant\sigma^{\varphi^{-}}\Phi_{x,y}(t)\quad\quad\forall t>0,\quad\forall\sigma>1,

(2.1)

\Phi_{x,y}(\sigma t)\geqslant\sigma^{\varphi^{+}}\Phi_{x,y}(t),\quad\forall t>0,\quad\forall\sigma\in(0,1),

(2.2)

\Phi_{x,y}(\sigma t)\leqslant\sigma^{\varphi^{+}}\Phi_{x,y}(t)\quad\forall t>0,\quad\forall\sigma>1,

(2.3)

\Phi_{x,y}(t)\leqslant\sigma^{\varphi^{-}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\frac{t}{\sigma}\right)\quad\forall t>0,\quad\forall\sigma\in(0,1).

(2.4)

From
[
23

we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
u,u_{n}\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the following statements are equivalent

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\Omega)}=0.

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\longrightarrow+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}(|u_{n}-u|)dx=0.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
24
, Remark 2.1]
Let
G
be a generalized N-function satisfying (
1.3
) and the
\Delta_{2}
-condition. Then
C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is dense in
(L_{G}(\Omega),\|.\|_{L_{G}(\Omega)})

In the sequel, let
\mathcal{B}_{c}(\Omega)
be the set of bounded functions compactly supported in
\Omega
, let
B_{R}
be the ball with the center
0
and the radius
R>0
and let
|B_{R}|
be the Lebesgue measure
of the ball
B_{R}
.

The proofs of Theorem
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
are mainly based on a basic technique of convolution (which makes functions infinitely differentiable), joined with a cut-off (which makes their support compact). In the remainder of this article, we describe properties of these operations with respect to the norm in fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces.

Let
J
stand for the Friedrichs mollifier kernel defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by

J(x)=\begin{cases}k\mathrm{e}^{-1/\left(1-|x|^{2}\right)}&\text{ if }|x|<1,\\
0&\text{ if }|x|\geqslant 1,\end{cases}

where
k>0
is such that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}J(x)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{B_{1}}J(x)\mathrm{d}x=1
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, we define
J_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-N}J\left(x\varepsilon^{-1}\right)
and for any
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, let us denote by
u_{\varepsilon}
the function defined as the convolution between
u
and
J_{\varepsilon}
; that is,

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=(u*J_{\varepsilon})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}J_{\varepsilon}(x-y)u(y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{B_{1}}u(x-\varepsilon y)J(y)\mathrm{d}y.

Note that, by construction, we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\leq C.

Proof.

Let
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
with
\operatorname{supp}u\subseteq B_{R}
. Therefore,
u
vanishes outside
B_{R}
. Thus

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

Now, we have

|u(x)-u(y)|\leqslant||\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}|x-y|\text{ and }|u(x)-u(y)|\leqslant 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}.

Then

|u(x)-u(y)|\leqslant 2\|u\|_{C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\min\{1,|x-y|\}:=\alpha\delta(x,y).

with
\alpha=2\|u\|_{C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}
. Since
\delta K\in L_{\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\alpha\delta(x,y)K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\delta(x,y)K(x,y)\right)dxdy<\infty.

where the constant
C
depends on
\alpha
,
\varphi^{+}
and
\varphi^{-}
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Note that by Proposition

and Lemma

, we have

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
u\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then there exists a sequence of functions
u_{m}\in

L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that

\left\|u-u_{m}\right\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\text{ as }m\longrightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

We set

u_{m}(x):=\begin{cases}m&\text{ if }u(x)\geq m,\\
u(x)&\text{ if }u(x)\in(-m,m),\\
-m&\text{ if }u(x)\leq-m.\end{cases}

We have

u_{m}\longrightarrow u\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}

and

\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u_{m}(x)\right|\right)\leq\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)|\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Then by the dominated convergence theorem and Proposition
2.2
, the proof follows.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in L_{\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then there exists a sequence of functions
u_{M}\in L_{\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that

\left\|u-u_{M}\right\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }M\longrightarrow+\infty\text{. }

Proof.

We set

u_{M}(x,y):=\begin{cases}M&\text{ if }u(x,y)\geq M\\
u(x,y)&\text{ if }u(x,y)\in(-M,M),\\
-M&\text{ if }u(x,y)\leq-M.\end{cases}

We have

u_{M}\longrightarrow u\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}

and

\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|u_{M}(x,y)\right|\right)\leq\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x,y)|\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Then the result follows from the dominated convergence theorem and Proposition
2.2
.
∎

For small
\varepsilon
, convolutions do not change too much the norm (
1.10
), according to the following result :

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. We suppose that (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold. Then
\left\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Since
u\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
then according to Lemma
2.5
, we can assume that
u
is bounded and compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. So that by Lemma
2.4
, we have

\left\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

(2.5)

Hence, by Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to prove that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0,

(2.6)

as
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
. Using the definition of
u_{\varepsilon}
, the Jensen’s inequality, Tonelli’s and Fubini’s theorems, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-u(x)-u_{\varepsilon}(y)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

(2.7)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(x-z)-u(y-z))J_{\varepsilon}(z)dz-u(x)+u(y)\right]K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left[\int_{B_{1}}(u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y))K(x,y)J(z)dz\right]\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\left[\int_{B_{1}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y)|K(x,y)J(z)\right)dz\right]dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times B_{1}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(J(z)^{\varphi^{+}}+J(z)^{\varphi^{-}}\right)dxdydz.

Now, let us show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0,

(2.8)

as
\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0
.

Fix
z\in B_{1}
and put
w=(z,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We define the function
v:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

v(x,y)=(u(x)-u(y))K(x,y),\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then
v\in L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
. If
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
, by proposition
2.3
, there exists
\mathrm{g}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\displaystyle\|v-g\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}
, so

\displaystyle\|v(.-\varepsilon w)-v\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|v(.-\varepsilon w)-g(.-\varepsilon w)\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|g(.-\varepsilon w)-g\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle+\|v-g\|_{L_{\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}=\varepsilon^{\prime},

with
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. Then (
2.8
) follows.
Moreover, for a.e.
z\in B_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(J(z)^{\varphi^{+}}+J(z)^{\varphi^{-}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\left(J(z)^{\varphi^{+}}+J(z)^{\varphi^{-}}\right)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}}\left(J(z)^{\varphi^{+}}+J(z)^{\varphi^{-}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\in L^{\infty}\left(B_{1}\right),

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, by using (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x-\varepsilon z)-u(y-\varepsilon z)-u(x)+u(y)|K(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle\left(J(z)^{\varphi^{+}}+J(z)^{\varphi^{-}}\right)dxdydz\longrightarrow 0,\textsl{ as }\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0.

From the above assertion and (
2.7
), we obtain (
2.6
). Using this fact in combination with (
2.5
), we conclude our proof.
∎

Now, we will discuss the cut-off technique needed for the density argument. For any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\tau_{j}\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be such that

\begin{gathered}0\leq\tau_{j}(x)\leq 1,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\tau_{j}(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B_{j},\\
0&\text{ if }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{j+1},\end{cases}\end{gathered}

(2.10)

where
B_{j}
denotes the ball centered at zero with radius
j
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then
\tau_{j}u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. It is clear that
\tau_{j}u\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
since
\left|\tau_{j}\right|\leq 1
. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-\tau_{j}(y)u(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)(u(x)-u(y))\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle+2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|u(y)\left(\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle+2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|u(y)\left(\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy,

where the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

is finite since
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.
According to Lemma
2.7
, we can assume that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|u(y)\left(\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\left(\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy,

where the constant
C
depends on
\varphi^{+},\varphi^{-}
, and
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}
. Finally, since
\tau_{j}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then by Lemma
2.6
and the inequality (
2.11
), we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|u(y)\left(\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy<\infty.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Then
\operatorname{supp}\left(\tau_{j}u\right)\subseteq\overline{B}_{j+1}\cap\operatorname{supp}u
, and

\left\|\tau_{j}u-u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow+\infty.

Proof.

By (
2.10
) and
[
17
, Lemma 9 ]
, we get

\operatorname{supp}\left(\tau_{j}u\right)\subseteq\overline{B}_{j+1}\cap\operatorname{supp}u.

Now, let us show that

\left\|\tau_{j}u-u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\text{ as }j\longrightarrow+\infty.

From Proposition
2.2
, it suffices to prove that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)\right|\right)dx\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow+\infty

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)-\tau_{j}(y)u(y)+u(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0,

as
j\longrightarrow+\infty
. We observe that

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(2|u(x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)|\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Moreover, by (
2.10
) we have

\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)\right|\right)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow+\infty\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Then, by using the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)\right|\right)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow+\infty.

Now, let us show that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)-\tau_{j}(y)u(y)+u(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0,

as
j\longrightarrow+\infty
. We set
\eta_{j}=1-\tau_{j}
. Then
\eta_{j}u=u-\tau_{j}u
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)-\tau_{j}(y)u(y)+u(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\eta_{j}(x)(u(x)-u(y))-\left(\tau_{j}(y)-\tau_{j}(x)\right)u(y)\right|.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)u(x)-u(x)-\tau_{j}(y)u(y)+u(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right|K(x,y)|u(y)|\right)dxdy

\displaystyle+2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\eta_{j}(x)\right)dxdy.

Note that by Lemma
2.7
, we can assume that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right|K(x,y)|u(y)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)},\varphi^{+},\varphi^{-}\right)\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right|K(x,y)\right).

By Lemma
2.6
, we have

\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Furthermore, we have

\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right|K(x,y)|u(y)|\right)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Hence, by using the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\tau_{j}(x)-\tau_{j}(y)\right||u(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty.

Also, we have

\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|\eta_{j}(x)K(x,y)\right)\ \leq\eta_{j}(x)^{\varphi^{-}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)

and

\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Again by (
2.10
), we have

\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\eta_{j}(x)\right)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\eta_{j}(x)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty.

This concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Approximation by smooth and compactly supported functions

This section is aimed at proving some density properties of smooth and compactly supported functions in the new fractional Musielak-Sobolev spaces.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that (
1.4
) and (
1.6
) hold. Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, then for any fixed
\delta>0
, there exists a continuous and compactly supported function
u_{\delta}
such that

\left\|u-u_{\delta}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\delta\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, then
u\in L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Thus according to Lemma
2.7
, we can assume that
u\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

Let
\tau_{j}\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be as in Section
2
, with
\tau_{j}(P)=1
if
|P|\leq j
and
\tau_{j}(P)=0
if
|P|\geq j+1
. Let
u_{j}=\tau_{j}u
. Then

u_{j}\longrightarrow u\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty

and

\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u(x)-u_{j}(x)\right|\right)\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)|\right)\in L^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Therefore, by using the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u(x)-u_{j}(x)\right|\right)dx\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty.

Hence, for any fixed
\delta>0
, there exists
j_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u(x)-u_{j_{\delta}}(x)\right|\right)dx\leq\delta.

(3.1)

Since
u_{j_{\delta}}
is supported in
\overline{B}_{j+1}
and
\displaystyle\mu(A)=\int_{A}dx
is finite over compact sets, then by using Lusin’s theorem (see
[
18
, Theorem 7.10]
for the definition of the uniform norm), we obtain that there exist a closed set
E_{\delta}\subset

\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a continuous and compactly supported function
u_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

u_{\delta}=u_{j_{\delta}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash E_{\delta},\quad\mu\left(E_{\delta}\right)\leq\delta\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\|u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq\|u_{j_{\delta}}\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}.

In particular, since
0\leq\tau_{j_{\delta}}(x)\leq 1
, we have

\left\|u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}<\infty.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
and the assumption (
1.4
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)-u_{\delta}(x)\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{\delta}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u_{j_{\delta}(x)}-u_{\delta}(x)\right|\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\left(\int_{E_{\delta}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)|\right)dx+\int_{E_{\delta}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u_{\delta}(x)|\right)dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\varphi^{+},\varphi^{-},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\right)\mu\left(E_{\delta}\right).

Hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(\left|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)-u_{\delta}(x)\right|\right)dx\leq C\delta.

(3.2)

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)-u_{\delta}(x)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\left(\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)-u_{j_{\delta}}(x)|\right)+\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)-u_{\delta}(x)|\right)\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)-u_{\delta}(x)|\right)dx\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)-u_{j_{\delta}}(x)|\right)dx

\displaystyle+2^{\varphi^{+}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)-u_{\delta}(x)|\right)dx.

Then, from (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we deduce that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\widehat{\Phi}_{x}\left(|u(x)-u_{\delta}(x)|\right)dx\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\delta\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore, by Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

\left\|u-u_{\delta}\right\|_{L_{\widehat{\Phi}_{x}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\text{ as }\delta\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|\left(u-u_{j}\right)(x)-\left(u-u_{j}\right)(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }j\longrightarrow\infty.

Hence, for any fixed
\delta>0
, there exists
j_{\delta}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(|\left(u-u_{j_{\delta}}\right)(x)-\left(u-u_{j_{\delta}}\right)(y)|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\leq\delta.

(3.3)

Notice that

v_{j_{\delta}}(x,y)=\left|u_{j_{\delta}}(x)-u_{j_{\delta}}(y)\right|K(x,y)

is supported in
\left\{P\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N};|P|\leq j_{\delta}+1\right\}
and
\displaystyle\mu(A)=\int_{A}\int_{A}dxdy
is finite over compact sets. Therefore, by using Lusin’s theorem, we get that there exist a closed set
E_{\delta}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a continuous and compactly supported function
u_{\delta}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle u_{\delta}

\displaystyle=v_{j_{\delta}}\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash E_{\delta},\quad\mu\left(E_{\delta}\right)\leq\delta\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\left\|u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|v_{j_{\delta}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}.

In particular, since
0\leq\tau_{j_{\delta}}(x)\leq 1
, we have

\left\|u_{\delta}\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq\left\||u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}.

Now, by putting

v(x,y):=|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)

in Lemma
2.8
, we can assume that

|u(x)-u(y)|K(x,y)\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

Therefor

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{x,y}\left(\left|\left(u-u_{\delta}\right)(x)-\left(u-u_{\delta}\right)(y)\right|K(x,y)\right)dxdy\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\delta\longrightarrow 0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

We assume that (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold. Then the space
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
is dense in
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
.

Proof.

We show that for any
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, there exists a sequence
\rho_{\varepsilon}\in

C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
such that

\left\|\rho_{\varepsilon}-u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{ as }\varepsilon\longrightarrow 0.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
, and let us fix
\delta>0
. Let
\tau_{j}
be as in Section
2
. From Lemma
2.11
, we have

\left\|u-\tau_{j}u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq\frac{\delta}{2},

for
j
large enough.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, let us consider

\rho_{\varepsilon}:=\tau_{j}u*J_{\varepsilon},

where
J_{\varepsilon}
is the mollifier function defined in Section
2
. By construction,
\rho_{\varepsilon}\in

C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
. Moreover, by
[
11
, Proposition IV.18]
, we have

\operatorname{supp}\rho_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\operatorname{supp}\left(\tau_{j}u\right)+\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}.

Also, by Lemma
2.11
, we have

\operatorname{supp}\left(\tau_{j}u\right)\subseteq\overline{B}_{j+1}\cap\operatorname{supp}u.

Thus,

\operatorname{supp}\rho_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\left(\overline{B}_{j+1}\cap\operatorname{supp}u\right)+\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}.

Hence,

\rho_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right),

for
\varepsilon
small enough. Furthermore, by Lemma
2.9
, we have

\left\|\rho_{\varepsilon}-\tau_{j}u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}

for
\varepsilon
small enough. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|u-\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|u-\tau_{j}u\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}+\left\|\tau_{j}u-\rho_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\delta}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta

Since
\delta
can be taken arbitrarily small, then the proof follows.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

We assume that (
1.3
), (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold and suppose that
\Omega
is a
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
-extension domain. Then
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is dense in
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
u\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)
. Since
\Omega
is a
W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}
-extension domain, then there exist
\widetilde{u}\in W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\widetilde{u}(x)=u(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
and

\|\widetilde{u}\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\leq C\|u\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)}.

Thus, according to Theorem
3.2
, we can choose
\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
with

\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\longrightarrow\widetilde{u}\quad\text{ in }W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right).

We set
u_{\varepsilon}:=\left.\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\right|_{\Omega}
. Thus,

\left\|u-u_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\widetilde{u}-\widetilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{W^{s,\Phi_{x,y}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)}\longrightarrow 0.

Hence
u_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
are the required approximating functions.
∎

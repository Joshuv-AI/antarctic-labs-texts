Bounded-Depth Frege Lower Bounds for Random 3-CNFs via Deterministic Restrictions
Svyatoslav Gryaznov, Navid Talebanfard

Abstract
A major open problem in proof complexity is to demonstrate that random 3-CNFs with a linear number of clauses require super-polynomial size refutations in bounded-depth Frege systems. We take the first step towards addressing this question by establishing a super-linear lower bound: for every k k , there exists ϵ k > 0 \epsilon_{k}>0 such that any depth- k k Frege refutation of a random n n -variable 3-CNF with Θ ⁡ ( n ) \Theta(n) clauses has Ω ⁡ ( n 1 + ϵ k ) \Omega(n^{1+\epsilon_{k}}) steps w.h.p. Our proof involves a novel adaptation of the deterministic restriction technique introduced by Chaudhuri and Radhakrishnan (STOC’96). For a given formula, this technique provides a method to fix a small number of variables in a bottom-up manner, ensuring that every surviving gate has small fan-in. Consequently, the resulting formula depends on a limited number of variables and can be simplified to a constant by a small variable assignment. Adapting this approach to proof complexity requires addressing the usual challenges associated with maintaining the hardness of a given instance. To this end, we introduce the following generalizations of standard proof complexity tools: • Weak expanders : These bipartite graphs relax the classical notion of expansion by only requiring that small sets have a non-empty boundary, while intermediate-sized sets have a large boundary. This property is sufficient to preserve hardness (e.g., for resolution width) and is easier to maintain as we remove vertices from the graph. • Formula assignments : To simplify a Frege proof, we consider a generalization of partial restrictions that assign values to formulas instead of just variables. We treat these assignments as new axioms added to our formula, as they generally cannot be expressed as variable substitutions.

Introduction

Propositional proof complexity
systematically studies the difficulty of proving tautologies (or equivalently, refuting unsatisfiable formulas). The primary goal is to demonstrate the weakness of a given proof system by showing that there exists a formula that does not admit short refutations. Assuming
\np\neq\conp
, any
reasonable
proof system fails to refute some formulas in polynomial size. Consequently, the pursuit of lower bounds for increasingly strong proof systems can be seen as an attempt to substantiate this assumption. However, even when a hard formula for a proof system is identified, finding additional hard formulas remains highly relevant. The inherent weakness of a proof system is truly exposed when we can show a lower bound for almost all or
random
formulas. Moreover, lower bounds for random formulas support
Feige’s hypothesis

[
10
]
, which posits that there is no polynomial-time algorithm that accepts all satisfiable 3-CNF formulas and rejects almost all unsatisfiable 3-CNF formulas with sufficient density.

Exponential lower bounds for random formulas are known for various proof systems, including resolution
[
8
,
5
]
, resolution operating on
k
-DNF formulas (
\Res(k)
)
[
1
,
28
]
, Polynomial Calculus
[
2
,
4
]
, and Cutting Planes
[
15
,
11
]
. For
\ACz
-Frege systems, which operate with constant-depth, or
\ACz
circuits, lower bounds for random formulas are still largely unknown, despite being extensively studied with several exponential lower bounds. In this paper, we focus on
\ACz
-Frege and make the first non-trivial progress in this direction.

It is of significant interest to prove lower bounds on random formulas for the smallest possible initial width, namely for 3-CNFs. For instance, the Cutting Planes lower bounds for random formulas are only known for
O(\log n)
-CNFs, and reducing this width remains an intriguing open problem. For many proof systems, the lower bounds on random formulas are based on the
expansion properties
of their underlying graphs and employ techniques based on restrictions, which fix only a small number of variables. However, preserving the expansion of the graph of a random 3-CNF under such restrictions is impossible. This issue can usually be addressed by considering wider CNFs (e.g., 12-CNFs provide strong enough expansion, as shown in
[
24
,
28
]
). In such cases, it is possible to show that the graph remains an expander under restrictions of appropriate size, albeit with weaker parameters.

In the current work, we also adopt the restriction-based technique but demonstrate that it is sufficient to work with 3-CNFs. We observe that the restricted graph satisfies a relaxed notion of expansion, and call such graphs
weak expanders
, that still retains the essential properties required to achieve the desired lower bound on the complexity of the restricted formula, specifically in terms of resolution width. This adjustment is highly technical and requires re-establishing some standard properties from expander theory.
Nonetheless, we believe this is valuable, and the notion of weak expanders can be applied to other proof systems to obtain lower bounds for random 3-CNFs. Furthermore, our technique requires working with a more general notion of restrictions, which cannot be expressed via simple variable assignments, and instead operates with formulas directly. We call such restrictions
formula assignments
and show that (syntactic) Frege proofs retain their structure under such restrictions.

Proof systems and circuit classes.

Proof lines in every propositional proof system belong to some circuit class. Intuitively, the deductive power of a proof system should correlate with the computational power of the underlying circuit class. This intuition is supported by many proof complexity lower bounds, such as feasible interpolation, which directly translates circuit lower bounds into proof size lower bounds

A typical application of a switching lemma employs

In the circuit complexity setting, a small
\ACz

Adapting the methods based on switching lemmas to proof complexity is more involved and requires careful consideration of the proof’s structure to avoid trivializing it. This is achieved by using carefully constructed distributions of random restrictions tailored to the specific instance. An instance-specific switching lemma is then used to show that, under these restrictions, each proof line has a simple structure (e.g., it can be computed by a small-depth decision tree), while still preserving the hardness of the restricted instance.

Deterministic restrictions.

In the context of circuit complexity, the switching lemma requires setting

Narrow resolution proofs.

Deterministic restrictions were applied to simplify resolution lower bounds.
The seminal work by Ben-Sasson and Wigderson
[
5
]
employs a similar concept to eliminate all clauses of large width from a resolution proof. We provide an outline of their proof and compare it to the argument above.

Let
\Pi
be a resolution refutation of a CNF
F
with an initial width
w_{0}
that requires resolution width
w
. We can view the clauses in
\Pi
as depth-1 formulas to fit the exposition of the technique above, and let

be the threshold parameter. Lemma 3.2 of

implies that for every variable

1.

such that the resolution width of

2.

The first case captures the notion of forced gates: the values of such variables are determined by the formula and do not simplify the proof. The second case corresponds to live gates, where the width reduces by only one when the variable is fixed. We will refer to these variables as
‘forced’
and
‘live’

d_{1}
live variables and fix the forced variables to their corresponding constant values. This process is repeated until there are no clauses of width greater than

Our contributions

We generalize and combine the methods of Chaudhuri and Radhakrishnan

Comparison with the circuit complexity setting and limitations.

The technique introduced by Chaudhuri and Radhakrishnan
[
6
]
is optimal within its context due to the corresponding upper bound on approximate majority. This implies that if we consider an
\ACz
-Frege proof merely as a collection of formulas (or circuits) disregarding the syntactic nature of the proof, the lower bound obtained is the best possible.

Adapting this technique to proof complexity poses a challenge. The main obstacle is that proof lines are not independent, and the restriction must preserve the proof structure while maintaining the hardness of the instance during the greedy procedure. To address this, we introduce the notion of
formula assignments
, which allows us to carefully maintain
live
and
forced
subformulas.
In the circuit complexity setting, the situation is much simpler. Forced gates are defined as gates that compute constant functions under the current restriction, and thus they can be easily replaced with constants. However, in our context, this is not sufficient. A formula can be complex and non-constant as a Boolean function, but its value might be easily derived by a simple proof. Informally, we call a formula
forced
if its value is easy to prove, and
live
otherwise. This adds a layer of complexity to the analysis that is not present in the circuit complexity setting.

This complexity is further highlighted by the fact that the technique cannot be applied to the semantic variant of
\ACz
-Frege, as maintaining the proof structure becomes impossible in this case. This is supported by the observation that any
t
-CNF, where
t
is constant, can be refuted by a linear-length semantic
\ACz
-Frege proof. Consequently, our analysis heavily relies on the syntactic structure of the proof system. It is plausible that this syntactic nature can be further leveraged to improve the greedy procedure and establish better lower bounds for
\ACz
-Frege.

Future directions and other proof systems.

There is also evidence suggesting that the technique could be applicable to other, more powerful proof systems. As previously mentioned, a variant of the technique can be applied to
\ACz\circ\mathsf{MOD}_{2}

[
7
]
and even
\ACz[2]
circuits
[
16
]
. This suggests that the technique could potentially be adapted to
\ACz[2]
-Frege to establish superlinear lower bounds on random 3-CNFs. This is particularly important because
\ACz[2]
-Frege represents the current frontier of proof complexity, and no non-trivial lower bounds on any formula are known for this system.

3.1
Formula assignments

Many proof complexity lower bounds employ techniques based on partial restrictions of variables. At a high level, these variable assignments simplify proofs of
‘low-complexity’
(e.g., small or narrow) while preserving the hardness of the restricted instance, ultimately leading to a contradiction. Our approach is based on a similar idea, but we introduce a more general notion of
formula assignments
to simplify the proof structure. These restrictions impose additional constraints on the proofs that cannot be captured by standard variable assignments.

A simple example of a formula assignment is a restriction that asserts that the disjunction
x\lor y
(where
x
and
y
are variables) is true. Clearly, this cannot be expressed by a simple variable assignment since we do not specify the values of
x
and
y
individually. Due to the more complex structure of these restrictions, additional care is required to define them appropriately and maintain the proof structure. We begin by formally defining formula assignments and then demonstrate how
syntactic
Frege proofs change under such restrictions.

As noted earlier, for simplicity, we only consider Frege proofs in the
\{\neg,\lor\}
basis, but the results can also be extended to the
\{\neg,\lor,\land\}
basis. For this reason, the definitions in this chapter are given in a general form that can be easily extended to support conjunction.

(Formula assignment)
.

Let
\sigma
be a set of pairs
(C,\alpha)
, where
C

\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}

\dom(\sigma)

\dom(\sigma)\coloneqq\{C:(C,\alpha)\in\sigma\}

C

the value
\alpha
such that
(C,\alpha)\in\sigma
.
We say that
\sigma
is a

if the following conditions hold:

1.

for every

is either a variable or the top gate of

if

Such

weakening
of a disjunction
D=\bigvee_{j\in J}D_{j}
, if
{\{D_{j}\}}_{j\in J}\subseteq{\{C_{i}\}}_{i\in I}
.

Definition 8

(Restricted formula)
.

Let
C
be a Boolean formula and
\sigma
a formula assignment.
Then the restriction of
C
by
\sigma
, denoted
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, is defined inductively on the structure of
C
as follows.

1.

C=x
for some variable
x
: if
x\in\dom(\sigma)
, then
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\coloneqq\sigma(x)
; otherwise we leave
C
unchanged and define
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\coloneqq x
.

2.

{\left.\kern-1.2pt(\neg C)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\coloneqq\neg({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}})
. Note that constant values are propagated, i.e., if
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is a constant
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
, then
{\left.\kern-1.2pt(\neg C)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is defined as
1-\alpha
.

3.

C
is a disjunction
\bigvee_{i\in I}C_{i}
. Applying
\sigma
inductively to the children of
C
, we obtain
C^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigvee_{i\in I}({\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}})
. If there exists a disjunction
D
in the domain of
\sigma
such that
C^{\prime}
is a weakening of
D
, then by the definition of formula assignments, it must be that
(D,1)\in\sigma
. In this case, we define
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\coloneqq\sigma(D)=1
. Otherwise, we set
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\coloneqq C^{\prime}
. Note that
C^{\prime}
is not necessarily a disjunction due to possible simplifications and constant propagation.

For a set or sequence
\mathcal{C}
of formula,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathcal{C}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is obtained by applying
\sigma
to every formula in
\mathcal{C}
. Observe that this is equivalent to considering
\mathcal{C}
as a (disjoint) multi-output Boolean circuit and applying
\sigma
to it.

Note that if
\dom(\sigma)
contains only variables, this definition coincides with the conventional notion of restriction or (partial) variable assignment.

Both conditions we impose on formula assignments are quite natural. If
C=\neg D
for some formula
D
, then the value of
C
is uniquely determined by the value of
D
. Thus, it is redundant to assign a value to
C
separately due to the bottom-up nature of the restriction. Similarly, for the second condition is similar, if
C=\bigvee_{i}D_{i}
, then
C=0
if and only if all
D_{i}=0
. Therefore, it is also redundant to assign a value to
C
separately.

Given a sequence of variable or formula assignments
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
, we denote the successive application of these assignments to
C
as
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}}}
, defined as:

{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(\ldots{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma_{1}}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma_{2}}}\ldots\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma_{m}}}.

Our main result regarding formula assignments is the following theorem, which demonstrates how formula assignments alter Frege proofs. For a given formula
D
, we write
D^{1}
to denote
D
and
D^{0}
to denote
\neg D
. Given a formula assignment
\sigma
, we define its
axiom set
as
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}\coloneqq\{D^{\alpha}:(D,\alpha)\in\sigma\}
.

Lemma 9

(restate=theoremflarestrictions,name=)
.

Let
\Pi=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{s})
be a depth-
k
Frege proof of a formula
C
from a set of formula
\mathcal{F}
and
\sigma
a formula assignment.
Then for every
i\in[s]
, the formula
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is semantically implied by constantly many formulas from the set
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\{T_{1},\ldots,T_{i-1}\}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
.
Consequently, there exists a
semantic
depth-
k
Frege proof
\Pi^{\prime}
of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
from
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathcal{F}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
of width at most
\mathcal{O}\left(\max(w(\Pi),w(\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}))\right)
.

3.2
Weak expanders and unsatisfiable systems over
\mathbb{F}_{2}

We will only provide an overview of the main ideas we require for our application here since the results are mostly adaptations of similar results from standard expander theory. Full details can be found in
Appendix

A
.

Definition 10

(Weak expander)
.

G
is an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weak (boundary) expander
, if the degrees of all vertices in
L
are bounded by
\Delta
and

1.

for every
I\subseteq L
with
{\left|I\right|}\leq r/2
,
{\left|\partial(I)\right|}>0
;

2.

for every
I\subseteq L
with
r/2<{\left|I\right|}\leq r
,
{\left|\partial(I)\right|}\geq c{\left|I\right|}
.

For brevity, we omit the word
‘boundary’
and simply refer to such graphs as
weak expanders
.

This definition differs from the standard definition of expanders in that we require the expansion to hold only for sets of size
r/2<{\left|I\right|}\leq r
. For smaller sets, we only require the boundary to be non-empty. A weaker variant of
Theorem

6
holds for weak expanders. Specifically, the first item provides a weaker upper bound, but it remains sufficient for the subsequent items. This suffices for our purposes to establish the desired lower bounds on the resolution width. We do not require this theorem directly in our main proof, but state it here for comparison with the standard expander theory.

Lemma 11

(restate=weakclosureproperties,name=cf.
[
2
]
)
.

Let
G
be an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weak expander.

1.

If
I
is a closure of
J
, then
{\left|I\right|}\leq\max(\frac{{\left|J\right|}}{c},\frac{r}{2})
.

2.

If
{\left|J\right|}\leq cr/2
, then the closure of
J
is unique.

3.

If
{\left|J\right|}\leq cr/2
and
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
, then
\Cl_{G}(J^{\prime})\subseteq\Cl_{G}(J)
.

The takeaway from this theorem is that for weak expanders, the closure operation is unique and monotone for sufficiently small sets
J
, and the size of the closure does not exceed
r/2
.

The following lemmas illustrate the motivation behind weak expanders. If we simply remove a set
J
from a boundary expander, the resulting graph does not necessarily satisfy even the weak expansion properties. To address this, we further remove all vertices that appear in the closure of
J
. We adopt a similar notation from
[
28
]
and define the
extension
of
J
as
\Ext_{G}(J)\coloneqq J\cup N(\Cl_{G}(J))
. The lemma below demonstrates that for small enough sets
J
, removing
\Ext_{G}(J)
from a boundary expander
G
makes the graph a weak expander. This allows us to establish lower bounds on the resolution width in subsequent steps.

Lemma 12

(restate=weakboundaryfromclosure,name=)
.

Let
G=(L\sqcup R,E)
be an
(r,\Delta,c)
-boundary expander. Then for every
J\subseteq R
such that
{\left|\Ext_{G}(J)\right|}\leq cr/4
, the graph
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G\setminus\Ext_{G}(J)
is an
(r,\Delta,c/2)
-weak expander.

Moreover, since in our application we will be removing such sets iteratively, we need to show that the graph remains a weak expander after each step. This is captured by the next lemma, which shows that this process can be viewed as a one-off operation, guaranteeing the weak expansion property throughout the iterative process.

Lemma 13

(restate=closurestepbystep,name=)
.

Let
G=(L\sqcup R,E)
be an
(r,\Delta,c)
-boundary expander. Let
J\subseteq R
with
{\left|\Ext_{G}(J)\right|}\leq cr/4
and define
G^{\prime}=G\setminus\Ext_{G}(J)
. Then for every
J^{\prime}\subseteq R\setminus\Ext_{G}(J)
with
{\left|\Ext_{G}(J\cup J^{\prime})\right|}\leq cr/4
, we have
\Cl_{G}(J\cup J^{\prime})=\Cl_{G}(J)\cup\Cl_{G^{\prime}}(J^{\prime})
. Consequently,
G^{\prime}\setminus\Ext_{G^{\prime}}(J^{\prime})=G\setminus\Ext_{G}(J\cup J^{\prime})
.

Given a linear system
Ax=b
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, we can associate it with a bipartite graph
G
such that
A
is its incidence matrix. In this graph, the left part corresponds to the equations in
Ax=b
, and the right part consists of the variables. We refer to
G
as the
incidence graph of
Ax=b
. We say that a linear system
Ax=b
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system
if its incidence graph is an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weak expander.
It is well-known that an unsatisfiable linear system requires large resolution width when its incidence graph is a boundary expander. However, it is easy to see that the same holds even when it is a weak expander. This observation is formalized in the following lemma.

Lemma 14
.

Let
Ax=b
be an unsatisfiable
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system. Then the canonical CNF encoding of the system requires resolution width of at least
cr/2
.

The proof of this lemma is identical to the standard argument for expanders, observing that every subsystem of size at most
r
is satisfiable since every set of
r
rows has a non-empty boundary.

3.3
Live and forced formulas

The idea behind our proof of the main result is to iteratively restrict the proof lines by fixing the values of live and forced subformulas. Here we give the definitions of these concepts for
\ACz
-Frege refutations and provide an overview of how they are used in the proof.

Let
L
be a linear system over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
m
equations
\ell_{i}(x)=b_{i}
for
i\in[m]
. For a subset of indices
I\subseteq[m]
, we denote by
L^{I}
the subsystem of
L
consisting of the equations indexed by
I
, i.e., the linear system
\bigwedge_{i\in I}(\ell_{i}(x)=b_{i})
. Given a variable assignment
\rho
to the variables of
L
that does not falsify any equation in
L
, we denote by
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
the linear system obtained by applying
\rho
to
L
, with the trivial equations
0=0
removed.

We will define the notions of live and forced formulas using the closure operation applied to the variables of such formulas. Given a formula
C
, we denote by
\vars(C)
the set of variables in
C
. For brevity, we will write
\Cl(C)
instead of
\Cl(\vars(C))
to represent the closure of the variables of
C
.

Definition 15

(Live and forced formulas)
.

Given an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system
L
and a formula
C
with width
w(C)\leq cr/2
, we say that
C
is
live w.r.t.
L
if
C
is non-constant on the solution set of the linear system
L^{\Cl(C)}
. Otherwise, if
C
becomes a constant
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
on this set, then
C
is
forced to
\alpha
w.r.t.
L
.

Intuitively, while forced formulas do not necessarily compute constant functions, they are
‘provably’
forced to specific values. This intuition is formalized in the following proposition, which directly follows from the definition of forced formulas and completeness.

Proposition 16
.

Let
C
be a formula that is forced to some value
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
w.r.t. a linear system
L
. Then
C^{\alpha}
can be obtained from
L
by a semantic derivation of width at most
{\left|\Ext(\vars(C))\right|}
.

Our definitions of live and forced formulas extends the notion of local consistency from
[
1
]
. In particular, the following generalization of Lemma 3.4 from
[
1
]
holds. This lemma demonstrates that if a formula
C
is consistent with a linear subsystem restricted to the equations from
\Cl(C)
, then it remains consistent with any linear subsystem of sufficiently small size.

Lemma 17

(restate=satremainssat,name=)
.

Let
L
be an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system with
m
equations and
C
a formula of width at most
cr/2
. If
(C=\alpha)\land L^{\Cl(C)}
is satisfiable for some
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
, then for every index set
I\subseteq[m]
of size at most
r/2
,
(C=\alpha)\land L^{I}
is also satisfiable.

The following lemma ensures that the property of being forced to a specific value is robust under both variable and formula assignments, meaning that forced formulas remain forced to the same value under these restrictions.

Lemma 18

(restate=forcedremainsforced,name=)
.

Let
L
be an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system and
C
a formula forced to some value
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
w.r.t.
L
.

1.

If
\rho
is a variable assignment such that
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
is also
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding, then
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
is forced to
\alpha
w.r.t.
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
.

2.

If
D
is a subformula of
C
that is forced to some value
\beta\in{\{0,1\}}
w.r.t.
L
such that
\{(D,\beta)\}
is a formula assignment, then the formula
C^{\prime}\coloneqq{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\{(D,\beta)\}}}
is forced to
\alpha
w.r.t.
L
.

The next lemma shows that the property of being forced propagates through the structure of a formula.

Lemma 19

(restate=howforcednesspropagates,name=)
.

Let
C
be a formula forced to a constant
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}
w.r.t. an
(r,\Delta,c)
-weakly expanding linear system
L
.

1.

If
C=\neg D
for some formula
D
, then
D
is forced to
1-\alpha
w.r.t.
L
.

2.

If
C=\bigvee_{j\in J}C_{j}
and
\alpha=0
, then each
C_{j}
is forced to
0
w.r.t.
L
.

3.4
Proof regularization

We now have all the necessary tools to describe our main result in more detail.

Given a formula
C
, the
in-degree of
C
is defined as the number of children of the top gate of
C
in its merged form.

Definition 20

(
d
-regular proofs)
.

Let
\Pi
be an
F_{k}
-derivation and
d=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
a vector of threshold parameters with
d_{0}=1
. We say that
\Pi
is
d
-regular
if, for every
i\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
and every subformula
C
of
\Pi
at level
i
, the in-degree of
C
is at most
d_{i}
.

The main technical result is the following lemma, which shows how to regularize a proof by iteratively restricting the proof lines based on the concepts of live and forced subformulas. It utilizes the greedy technique that is similar to deterministic restrictions
[
6
]
and the size-width trade-off for resolution
[
5
]
.

Lemma 21

(restate=mainlemma,name=Regularization Lemma)
.

Let
L
be an unsatisfiable
(r,\Delta,c)
-expanding linear system in
n
variables with
r=\Theta(n)
and
k
a positive integer.
Let
m=n^{1/(2^{k}+1)}
and define the vector of threshold parameters
d=(d_{0},d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
with
d_{0}=1
and
d_{i}=m^{2^{i-1}}
for
i\geq 1
.

If there exists an
F_{k}
-refutation
\Pi
of
L
with
\psz(\Pi)=O(n^{1+\epsilon_{k}})
, where
\epsilon_{k}=1/2^{k+1}
, then there exists a variable assignment
\rho
and a formula assignment
\sigma
satisfying the following properties:

1.

the domains of
\sigma
and
\rho
are disjoint;

2.

the linear system
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
is
(r,\Delta,c/2)
-weakly expanding;

3.

for every pair
(D,\alpha)\in\sigma
, the formula
D
is forced to
\alpha
w.r.t.
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
and has a fan-in of at most
d_{1}\cdots d_{k}
;

4.

\Pi|_{\rho}|_{\sigma}
is
d
-regular.

These two restrictions are responsible for different types of subformulas. The variable assignment
\rho
handles live subformulas with high in-degree, while the formula assignment
\sigma
eliminates forced subformulas. The resulting collection of formulas
\Pi|_{\rho}|_{\sigma}
is not necessarily a proof by itself, but it can be transformed into one using
Lemma

9
. By applying this theorem to
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\Pi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
, which is a refutation of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
, and
\sigma
, we obtain a
semantic
refutation of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
with small width. This leads to a contradiction, as the width of such a refutation is lower bounded by
cr/2
due to
Lemma

14
.

We now outline the proof of this lemma, which will be detailed in the next section. The proof is processed layer-by-layer in
k
phases. After processing each layer
i
, we obtain a variable assignment
\rho_{i}
and a formula assignment
\sigma_{i}
such that these restrictions satisfy the properties of the lemma up to level
i
. By the inductive hypothesis, the concepts of live and forced subformulas are well-defined for subformulas up to level
i-1
since they depend on at most
d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1}<cr/2
variables. When processing level
i
, we perform the following steps:

1.

Reducing the in-degree of live subformulas.
We consider all live subformulas on level
i
with more than
d_{i}
children and greedily satisfy them by fixing the values of their children. To preserve the hardness of the instance, we ensure that the graph of the restricted system
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i}}}
remains a weak expander. This is achieved by
Lemmas

12
and
13
. The restriction
\rho_{i}
extends the restriction
\rho_{i-1}
constructed in the previous phase.

2.

Eliminating forced subformulas.
We consider all forced subformulas up to level
i-1
and fix their values. The formula assignment
\sigma_{i-1}
from the previous phase, which eliminates forced subformulas from
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\Pi\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i-1}}}
, cannot be directly extended to obtain
\sigma_{i}
. This is because the notion of forced subformulas was defined w.r.t. the linear system
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i-1}}}
, and may change since we are now considering a different linear system
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i}}}
. Instead, we construct
\sigma_{i}
directly in a top-down manner and then show that it remains consistent with
\sigma_{i-1}
. Specifically, we show that for every formula
D
,

{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i-1},\sigma_{i-1},\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}}}={\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}}}.

This is achieved by
Lemma

18
.

4
Proofs of the main results

We now present the proofs of our main results. First, we demonstrate how the Regularization Lemma (
Lemma

21
) leads to our main result,
Theorem

1
. Following that, we provide the proof of
Lemma

21
itself.

\maintheorem

*

Proof.

It is well-known
[
12
,

]
that for every
C\geq 8\ln(2)
, with high probability, a random 3-CNF with

-boundary expander, where

and

.

Let

be a random 3-CNF with
Cn
clauses, and assume that its graph is a boundary expander. Following
[

, we prove a stronger lower bound on random 3-XOR instances obtained from random 3-CNFs by replacing each clause with a linear equation over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Specifically, for each clause

, we replace it with the equation
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=\delta_{1}+\delta_{2}+\delta_{3}\pmod{2}
. Let
L
be the linear system obtained from

in this way.

Consider an
F_{k}
-refutation
\Pi
of the CNF encoding of

. According to

3
, it suffices to give a lower bound on
\psz(\Pi)
. Assume, for contradiction, that
\psz(\Pi)
is

\rho
and a formula assignment

that satisfy the properties of the lemma.

Since
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
is an

implies that the resolution width of the CNF encoding of

is at least

. By

, there exists a semantic refutation of
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
with width at most

that can also use axioms from the set

. However, by the construction of

and

, all axioms from

can be derived from
{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho}}
with width at most

. This leads to a contradiction and completes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the Regularization Lemma (
Lemma

).

\mainlemma
*

Proof.

Denote the proof size of
\Pi
by

. As described earlier, we will prove the lemma by constructing a sequence of variable assignments

and formula assignments
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}
through

phases
, one for each level of
\Pi
.

Since the depth of formulas in
\Pi
might change during the process, we need to clarify what we mean by the level of a formula. We say that a subformula

in
\Pi|_{\rho_{i}}|_{\sigma_{i}}

appears on level

if there exists a subformula

in
\Pi
such that

and

has depth

. Note that the same formula might appear on different levels in a restricted proof. The level of a formula provides an upper bound on its depth.

The
i
th phase of the proof consists of

. Let

be the incidence graph of the linear system

and

We remove all subformulas of

(a)

Let

that appear on level

(b)

Pick the most frequent pair

, where

and

(c)

Let

(d)

Increase

by

and repeat the process.

2.

Let

be the union of

and all variable assignments constructed during this phase, i.e.,

{\left.\kern-1.2ptL\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i}}}

Claim 22
.

The number of variables in

is at most

.

Proof.

The properties of
\sigma_{i-1}
imply that every subformula of

∎

Claim 23
.

The set of high-in-degree subformulas

\mathcal{H}_{i,q}\subseteq{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathcal{H}_{i,q-1}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\tau_{i,q}}}

Proof.

We need to demonstrate that no new live gates appear during each phase. This is achieved by

, which shows that forced gates remain forced under variable assignments.
∎

Claim 24
.

The number of steps in the
i
th phase,

, is at most
\frac{2s}{d_{i}}\log s
.

Proof.

This is shown by the standard double counting argument. Let us count the sum
\sum_{C\in\mathcal{H}_{i,q}}(\text{number of live children of $C$})
in two ways. Since the number of live children of each formula is at most

, we have a lower bound
d_{i}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{i,q}\right|}
. On the other hand, we can count the number of formulas in
\mathcal{H}_{i,q}
that become constant after replacing a live child

with a constant

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D\text{ is a live child of some formula }C\in\mathcal{H}_{i,q}\\
\alpha\in{\{0,1\}}\end{subarray}}(\text{number of formulas in $\mathcal{H}_{i,q}$ that disappear under $D\coloneqq\alpha$}).

There are at most
s
formulas among the live children of formulas in

, so the most frequent pair

appears at least

can only shrink in each step, the number of steps is at most

is the smallest integer satisfying

\left(1-\frac{d_{i}}{2s}\right)^{q^{\prime}}{\left|\mathcal{H}_{i,1}\right|}<1.

The claim follows by solving this inequality.
∎

Before proving the remaining properties, we need to establish some auxiliary results. We start by giving an upper bound on the size of the extension of a set

Fact 25
.

Given

{\left|\Ext_{G}(J)\right|}\leq\frac{1+\Delta/c}{2}{\left|J\right|}

Next, we show that the extensions of all sets we consider, specifically

Claim 26
.

For every

and

{\left|\Ext_{G}(\mathcal{J}_{i,q})\right|}\leq cr/4

Proof.

Recall the definition of

\mathcal{J}_{i,q}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}\bigcup_{t=1}^{q_{i}}J_{i,t}\cup\bigcup_{t=1}^{q}J_{k,t}.

We have established that each

{\left|\mathcal{J}_{i,q}\right|}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{s}{d_{j}}\log{s}\cdot d_{1}\cdots d_{j}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(s\log{n}\left(\frac{1}{d_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{d_{1}\cdots d_{i-1}}{d_{i}}\right)\right)=o(n).

In particular, since

, we have
{\left|\mathcal{J}_{i,q}\right|}\leq\frac{cr}{2(1+\Delta/c)}

∎

The properties of the graph

now follow easily.

Claim 27
.

The graph

coincides with the graph

and is an

-weak expander.

Proof.

Let

at the end of the previous phase. Otherwise, it is the graph

at the end of the previous step of the same phase. The analysis does not depend on which of these two cases we consider. By the inductive hypothesis,

By definition,
\mathcal{J}_{i,q}=\mathcal{J}_{i^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\cup J_{i,q}
, and the graph

is obtained from

by removing the extension of

, i.e.,
G_{i,q}=G_{i^{\prime},q^{\prime}}\setminus\Ext_{G_{i^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}(J_{i,q})

{\left|\Ext_{G_{i^{\prime},q^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{J}_{i,q})\right|}\leq cr/4
, and thus we can apply

∎

We now proceed with the construction of the formula assignment

. For brevity, we will simply use the terms

and

Given a forced formula

\mathcal{B}_{0}\coloneqq\left\{({\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i}}},\alpha):(D,\alpha)\in\sigma_{i-1}\right\}.

We start by observing that for every pair

in

. We then construct formula assignments

We note that every

Let

is consistent with

.

For every formula

{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i-1},\sigma_{i-1},\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}}}={\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\rho_{i},\sigma_{i}}}

The same reasoning shows that the fan-in of every subformula of

5
Formula assignments

In this section, we provide the proof of
Lemma

9
, which shows how to transform a syntactic Frege proof into a semantic one by applying formula assignments. We begin by proving auxiliary results that are used in the proof of the main theorem.

We start by showing that our definition of formula assignments is idempotent, i.e., applying an assignment twice does not change the result.

Lemma 30

(restate=restrictionidempotent,name=)
.

Let
C
be a Boolean formula and
\sigma
a formula assignment.
The application of
\sigma
is idempotent, i.e.,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}={\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on the structure of
C
. If
C
is a variable, then either this variable is in the domain of
\sigma
and becomes a constant, or it is not and remains unchanged. If
C
is a negation, then since the top gate of any formula in the domain of
\sigma
cannot be a negation, the lemma holds immediately by the inductive hypothesis. Finally, if
C
is a disjunction
\bigvee_{j\in J}C_{j}
, we consider two cases. If
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is a constant, then a second application of
\sigma
clearly does not change it. Otherwise, by definition,
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}=\bigvee_{j\in J}({\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}})
and
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is not a weakening of any circuit in the domain of
\sigma
. By the inductive hypothesis,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}={\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
for all
j\in J
. Therefore, the lemma holds by definition since
\bigvee_{j\in J}{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
coincides with
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
and thus is not a weakening of any circuit in the domain of
\sigma
. This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the restriction operation is distributive with respect to disjunctions.

Lemma 31

(restate=restrictiondistributive,name=)
.

Let
C
and
D
be Boolean formulas and
\sigma
a formula assignment. Then the application of
\sigma
is distributive over
\lor
, i.e.,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}={\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
.
Furthermore, if
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is not a constant,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}={\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
.

Proof.

The disjunction
C\lor D
can be expressed as an unbounded fan-in disjunction
\Big(\bigvee_{j\in J}C_{j}\Big)\lor\Big(\bigvee_{k\in K}D_{k}\Big)
, where
C=\bigvee_{j\in J}C_{j}
if
C
is a disjunction, or
J=\{1\}
and
C_{1}=C
otherwise. The same applies to
D
. Let
E
be the circuit obtained by applying
\sigma
to the children of
C\lor D
, i.e.,

E\coloneqq\Big(\bigvee_{j\in J}{\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\Big)\lor\Big(\bigvee_{k\in K}{\left.\kern-1.2ptD_{k}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\Big).

By definition,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is defined in terms of
E
. If
\left(\bigvee_{j\in J}{\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)
is a weakening of some
F
in the domain of
\sigma
, then both
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
and
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
turn into
1
and the lemma holds. The same reasoning applies when
\left(\bigvee_{k\in K}{\left.\kern-1.2ptD_{k}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)
is a weakening of some
F^{\prime}\in\dom(\sigma)
.
Otherwise, both
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
and
{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
are defined as
\bigvee_{j\in J}{\left.\kern-1.2ptC_{j}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
and
\bigvee_{k\in K}{\left.\kern-1.2ptD_{k}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, respectively. Thus,
{\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
coincides with
E
. Due to the idempotence of the restriction operation (
Lemma

30
),
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is defined in terms of
E
in the same way as
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, and thus they are equal.

To conclude the proof, observe that if
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left(C\lor D\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is not a constant,
{\left.\kern-1.2pt\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is also not a constant and coincides with
\left({\left.\kern-1.2ptC\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2ptD\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\right)
.
∎

Due to
Proposition

4
, we can focus on any Frege system that uses the primitive connectives
\neg
and
\lor
without loss of generality. We will consider Shoenfield’s system
[
27
]
, which consists of the following rules:

Excluded middle:

\vdash p\vee\neg p
;

Weakening (expansion) rule:

p\vdash q\vee p
;

Cut rule:

p\vee q,\neg p\vee r\vdash q\vee r
;

Contraction rule:

p\vee p\vdash p
;

Associative rule:

(p\vee q)\vee r\vdash p\vee(q\vee r)
.

\theoremflarestrictions

*

Proof.

Recall that we consider the proof
\Pi
as a sequence of formulas in merged form. We will prove the theorem by induction on
i
. Observe that if
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}=1
, the result holds trivially. If
T_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
, then clearly
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\in{\left.\kern-1.2pt\mathcal{F}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, and the base case holds. Otherwise,
T_{i}
is derived using one of the rules of Shoenfield’s system. We will show that the result holds for each of these rules.

Contraction, and associative rule:

These rules does not affect the merged form of
T_{i}
, and thus the result holds trivially.

Weakening rule:

The resulting formula can also be obtained by applying the weakening rule to the restricted formulas.

Excluded middle:

The formula
T_{i}
is
A\lor\neg A
for some formula
A
. If
{\left.\kern-1.2ptA\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is a constant, then by definition,
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}=1
. Otherwise,
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
is defined in terms of the formula
B\coloneqq{\left.\kern-1.2ptA\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}\lor{\left.\kern-1.2pt\neg A\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, which is a tautology. Thus, either
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}=B
and can be obtained by applying the excluded middle rule to
{\left.\kern-1.2ptA\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}
, or
{\left.\kern-1.2ptT_{i}\mathchoice{\vphantom{\big|}}{}{}{}\right|_{\sigma}}=1
.

Cut rule:

We adapted the technique of deterministic restrictions to the proof complexity setting and demonstrated its use in proving super-linear lower bounds on the size of

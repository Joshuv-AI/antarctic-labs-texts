On the solutions to p p -Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions when p p goes to + ∞ +\infty
Vincenzo Amato, Alba Lia Masiello, Carlo Nitsch, Cristina Trombetti

Abstract
We study the behaviour, when p → + ∞ p\to+\infty , of the first p p -Laplacian eigenvalues with Robin boundary conditions and the limit of the associated eigenfunctions. We prove that the limit of the eigenfunctions is a viscosity solution to an eigenvalue problem for the so-called ∞ \infty -Laplacian. Moreover, in the second part of the paper, we focus our attention on the p p -Poisson equation when the datum f f belongs to L ∞ ​ ( Ω ) L^{\infty}(\Omega) and we study the behaviour of solutions when p → ∞ p\to\infty . MSC 2020: 35J92, 35J94, 35P15. Keywords: p − p- Laplacian, Robin boundary conditions, eigenvalues problem, infinity Laplacian.



1
Introduction

Let
\beta
be a positive parameter and let
\Omega
be a bounded and open set of
\R^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, with Lipschitz boundary.

We study the
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem with Robin
boundary conditions

\begin{cases}\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\Lambda u,-\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,-\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
\Delta_{\infty}
, the so-called
\infty
-Laplacian, is defined by

\Delta_{\infty}u=\left\langle D^{2}u\cdot\nabla u,\nabla u\right\rangle.

We refer to problem (
1
) as the
\infty
-Laplacian eigenvalue problem because it can be seen as limit, in some sense, of the
p
-Laplacian eigenvalues problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\Lambda_{p}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla u\right|}^{p-2}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}+\beta^{p}{\left|u\right|}^{p-2}u=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(2)

A function
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
2
) if it satisfies

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla u_{p}\right|}^{p-2}\nabla u_{p}\nabla\varphi\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p-2}u_{p}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\Lambda_{p}\int_{\Omega}{\left|u_{p}\right|}^{p-2}u_{p}\varphi\,dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega),

where
\beta
and
\Lambda_{p}
are both positive.

It is well known that the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplacian is the minimum of the following Rayleigh quotient

\Lambda_{p}=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\\
{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\left\{\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla w\right|}^{p}\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|w\right|}^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right\}.

(3)

By classical arguments, one can show that the infimum in (
3
) is achieved and in what follows we will denote by
u_{p}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
the eigenfunction corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\Lambda_{p}
.

In this paper, we firstly prove that

\lim_{p\rightarrow+\infty}(\Lambda_{p})^{1/p}=\Lambda_{\infty}=:\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)\\
{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\end{subarray}}\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\},

(4)

and we give a geometric characterization of this quantity, precisely:

\Lambda_{\infty}=\frac{1}{1/\beta+R_{\Omega}},

(5)

where
R_{\Omega}
denotes the inradius of
\Omega
, i.e. the radius of the l argest ball contained in
\Omega
. Thereafter, we prove that
\Lambda_{\infty}
is the first eigenvalue of the infinite Laplacian, in the sense that equation (
1
) admits non-trivial solutions only if
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{\infty}
.

Similar results, in the case of Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions were obtained in
[
JLM99
,
JL05
,
BDM89
,
EKNT15
,
RS16
]
.

More specifically, in
[
JLM99
,
JL05
]
, Juutinen, Lindqvist and Manfredi have studied the Dirichlet case as
p\rightarrow+\infty
. They provided a complete characterization of the limiting solutions in terms of geometric quantities.
Indeed, the first eigenvalue of the
p
-Laplace operator
\{\lambda_{p}^{D}\}
happens to satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda_{p}^{D}\right)^{{1}/{p}}=\lambda^{D}_{\infty}:=\frac{1}{R_{\Omega}}.

The related eigenfunctions
v^{D}_{p}
also converge (up to a subsequence) to some Lipschitz function
v^{D}_{\infty}
.
Most important, the Authors show that there exists a natural viscosity formulation of the eigenvalue problem for the
\infty
-Laplacian, for which
\lambda^{D}_{\infty}
and
v^{D}_{\infty}
turn out to be the first eigenvalue and first eigenfunction, respectively.

The Neumann case seems to be more subtle. It was investigated in
[
EKNT15
,
RS16
]
and similarly to the Dirichlet case, the Authors established that the first non-trivial eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplacian
\{\lambda^{N}_{p}\}
satisfy

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\lambda^{N}_{p}\right)^{1/p}=\lambda_{\infty}^{N}:=\frac{2}{\diam(\Omega)},

where
\diam(\Omega)
is the intrinsic diameter of
\Omega
, i.e. the supremum of the geodetic distance between two points of
\Omega
.

However, while both first eigenvalues and first eigenfunctions converge (as
p\to\infty
) and are solutions to some appropriate eigenvalue problem for the
\infty
-Laplacian, in
[
EKNT15
]
, the Authors are able to prove that they actually converge to the
first
eigenvalue e
first
eigenfunction only if the domain
\Omega
is convex. Whether or not the same holds true in the general case, it is still an open problem.

In the second part of the paper, we focus our attention on the study of the limit of the
p
-Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v=f&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}}+\beta^{p}{\left|v\right|}^{p-2}v=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(6)

when
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is a non-negative function.

We prove that there exists (up to a subsequence) a limiting solution
v_{\infty}
as
p\to\infty

and we establish conditions on
f
which are equivalent to the uniqueness of
v_{\infty}
.

The
\infty
-Poisson problem for Dirichlet boundary conditions
was already studied in
[
BDM89
]
by Bhattacharya, DiBenedetto and Manfredi, while, to the best of our knowledge, similar results have not been addressed in the case of Neumann boundary conditions.

2.1
Viscosity solutions

Before going on, we recall the definition of viscosity solutions to a boundary value problem, see
[
CIL92
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

We consider the following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}F(x,u,\nabla u,D^{2}u)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
B(x,u,\nabla u)=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(9)

where
F:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\times\R^{n\times n}\to\R
and
B:\R^{n}\times\R\times\R^{n}\to\R
are two continuous functions.

Viscosity supersolution

A lower semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity supersolution to (
9
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
x_{0}
is a strict minimum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\geq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\max\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),B\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\geq 0

Viscosity subsolution

An upper semi-continuous function
u
is a viscosity subsolution to (
9
) if, whenever we fix
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
x_{0}
is a strict maximum in
\Omega
for
u-\phi
, then

•

if
x_{0}\in\Omega
, the following holds

F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right)\leq 0

•

if
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
, the following holds

\min\Set{F\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0}),D^{2}\phi(x_{0})\right),B\left(x_{0},\phi(x_{0}),\nabla\phi(x_{0})\right)}\leq 0

Viscosity solution

A continuous function
u
is a viscosity solution to (
9
) if it is both a super and subsolution.

Remark 2.1
.

The condition
u-\phi
has a strict maximum or minimum can be relaxed: it is sufficient to ask that
u-\phi
has a local maximum or minimum in a ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
for some positive
R
.

3
The
\infty
-eigenvalue problem

Let us start this section observing that Lemma
2.1
brings to the following estimate

\limsup_{p\to\infty}\Lambda_{p}^{1/p}\leq\Lambda_{\infty}.

(10)

We can say something more

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Set{\Lambda_{p}}_{p>1}
be the sequence of the first eigenvalues of the
p
-Laplacian operator with Robin boundary condition. Then,

\lim_{p\to\infty}\left(\Lambda_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\Lambda_{\infty},

(11)

where
\Lambda_{\infty}
is defined in (
4
).

Moreover, if
\Set{u_{p}}_{p>1}
is the sequence of eigenfunctions associated to
\{\Lambda_{p}\}_{p>1}
, then there exists a function
u_{\infty}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

\displaystyle u_{p}\to u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ uniformly in }\,\Omega

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p}\to\nabla u_{\infty}

\displaystyle\text{ weakly in }\,L^{q}(\Omega),\forall q.

Proof.

As a consequence of (
10
), the sequence
\Set{u_{p}}_{p>1}
of eigenfunctions associated to
\Lambda_{p}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,q}(\Omega)
: indeed, if
q<p
, by Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Lambda_{p}^{1/p}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C,

(12)

\displaystyle{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C,

(13)

where the constant
C
is independent of
p
.

By a classical argument of diagonalization, see for instance
[
BDM89
]
, we can extract a subsequence
u_{p_{j}}
such that

\displaystyle u_{p_{j}}\to u_{\infty}\,\text{ uniformly}\implies\|u_{p_{j}}\|_{L^{p_{j}}}\to{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\nabla u_{p_{j}}\to\nabla u_{\infty}\,\text{ weakly in }\,L^{q}(\Omega),\,\forall q>1.

Moreover, from (
12
) and (
13
), the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}{{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}{{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\frac{{\left\|\nabla u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}}{{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\left|\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}}}{{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}}\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\Lambda_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Letting
q\to\infty
we obtain

{\left\|\nabla u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\Lambda_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Similarly

\beta{\left\|u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\beta{\left\|\ u_{p}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Lambda_{p}^{1/p}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\leq C,

gives us

\beta{\left\|u_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\left(\Lambda_{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

hence

\Lambda_{\infty}\leq\liminf_{p\to\infty}\Lambda_{p}^{1/p}.

∎

We are also able to give a geometric characterization of

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Lambda_{\infty}
be the quantity defined in (
4
) and let

\Lambda_{\infty}=\min_{x_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+R_{\Omega}}.

Proof.

The function
\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
, moreover

{\left\|\nabla\left({1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1\quad\text{ and }\quad\beta{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}=1.

Then

\Lambda_{\infty}\leq\min_{x_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}.

In order to prove the reverse inequality, we take

such that
{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
.

The following facts can occur

Case 1

If
\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, then

\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}={\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

We choose

{\left|x-y\right|}=d(x,\partial\Omega)
. So, we have

\displaystyle{\left|w(x)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|}+{\left|w(y)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\left|x-y\right|}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}d(x,\partial\Omega)+\frac{1}{\beta}{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle={\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right)

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

and then

\frac{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}

Case 2

If
\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}>{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, then

\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}=\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}.

With the same choice of
x
and
y
, we have

\displaystyle{\left|w(x)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left|w(x)-w(y)\right|}+{\left|w(y)\right|}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}{\left|x-y\right|}+{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}d(x,\partial\Omega)+{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\left(d(x,\partial\Omega)+\frac{1}{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Hence,

\frac{\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}}{{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}\geq\frac{1}{{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}.

Finally we get
\forall w\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega):{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1,

\max\left\{{\left\|\nabla w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\beta{\left\|w\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\right\}\geq\frac{1}{{\left\|{1}/{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}

and then the desired inequality

\Lambda_{\infty}\geq\min_{x_{0}\in\Omega}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)}.

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Lambda_{\infty}
be the quantity defined in (
4

\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)\geq\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega^{\sharp}),

where
\Omega^{\sharp}
is the ball centered at the origin with the same measure of

.

Proof.

The Faber-Krahn inequality for the first eigenvalue of the

-Laplacian with Robin boundary condition (for instance see
[

]
) states that

Letting

\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega)\geq\Lambda_{\infty}(\Omega^{\sharp}).

This can follow also from the geometric characterization in Lemma

\Lambda_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\beta}+R_{\Omega}}

as the ball maximizes the inradius among sets of given volume.
∎

3.1
The first Robin
\infty
-eigenvalue

Now we want to show that
\Lambda_{\infty}
is the first eigenvalue of (
1
), that is the smallest
\Lambda
such that

\begin{cases}\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\Lambda u,-\Delta_{\infty}u}=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
-\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-\beta u,-\displaystyle{\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}}}=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

admits a non-trivial solution.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded and open set of class
C^{2}
in
\R^{n}
. If for some
\Lambda
, problem (
1
) admits a non-trivial eigenfunction
u
, then
\Lambda\geq\Lambda_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be an eigenvalue to (
1
), let
u
be a corresponding eigenfunction. We normalize it in this way

\max_{x\in\Omega}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}.

Then
u
is viscosity subsolution to

\min\Set{{\left|\nabla u\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}u}=0\text{ in }\Omega.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
\gamma>0
, let us consider the function

g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}=\frac{1}{\beta}+(1+\varepsilon)d(x,\partial\Omega)-\gamma d(x,\partial\Omega)^{2}.

It is well known (see
[
GT01
]
) that in a small tubular neighbourhood
\Gamma_{\mu}
of
\partial\Omega
, the boundary
\partial\Omega
and the distance function
d(x,\partial\Omega)
share the same regularity: so both
d(x,\partial\Omega)
and
g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
are
C^{2}(\Gamma_{\mu})
.

Moreover, by a direct computation, one can check that if

\gamma<\frac{\varepsilon}{2R_{\Omega}},

then
g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}
is a viscosity supersolution to

\min\Set{{\left|\nabla g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}}=0\text{ in }\Omega.

Hence, Theorem
2.1
in
[
Jen93
]
ensures that

m_{\varepsilon}=\inf_{x\in\Omega}(g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)-u(x))=\inf_{x\in\partial\Omega}(g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)-u(x)).

Assume by contradiction that
m_{\varepsilon}<-\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta}
, and set
v=g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}-m_{\varepsilon}
. We observe that
v\geq u
in
\Omega
and
v(x_{0})=u(x_{0})
, where
x_{0}
is the point which achieves the minimum on the boundary, so we can use it as test function in the definition of viscosity subsolution for
u
.

Assuming
\gamma<\frac{\varepsilon}{2R_{\Omega}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla v(x)=\left[1+\varepsilon-2\gamma d(x,\partial\Omega)\right]\nabla d(x,\partial\Omega)

\displaystyle{\left|\nabla v(x_{0})\right|}=1+\varepsilon-2\gamma d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)>1

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}(x_{0})=-\left[1+\varepsilon-2\gamma d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\right]\nabla d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\cdot\nu>0

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\infty}v(x_{0})=2\gamma\left[1+\varepsilon-2\gamma d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\right]^{2}{\left|\nabla d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\right|}^{4}>0.

The fact that
m_{\varepsilon}<-\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta}
implies

{\left|\nabla v(x_{0})\right|}-\beta v(x_{0})=\varepsilon+\beta m_{\varepsilon}<0.

Therefore

-\min\left\{{\left|\nabla v\right|}-\beta v,-\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\right\}>0\quad\text{ and }\quad\min\left\{{\left|\nabla v\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}v\right\}>0

against the fact that

\min\left\{\min\left\{{\left|\nabla v\right|}-1,-\Delta_{\infty}v\right\},-\min\left\{{\left|\nabla v\right|}-\beta v,-\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}\right\}\right\}\leq 0.

So we have

g_{\varepsilon,\gamma}(x)-u(x)\geq m_{\varepsilon}\geq-\frac{\varepsilon}{\beta},

letting
\varepsilon
and
\gamma
go to zero, it follows

\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\geq u(x)\qquad\forall x\in\Omega.

Hence

\frac{1}{\Lambda_{\infty}}=\max_{x\in\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)\right)\geq\max_{x\in\Omega}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Lambda},

which concludes the proof.
∎

4
The
p
-Poisson equation

Let
f
be a function in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and let
\beta>0
. We consider the following
p
-Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}v=f&\text{ in }\Omega\\
{\left|\nabla v\right|}^{p-2}\displaystyle{\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}}+\beta^{p}{\left|v\right|}^{p-2}v=0&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(18)

A function
v_{p}
is a weak solution to (
18
) if it satisfies

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p-2}\nabla v_{p}\nabla\varphi\,dx+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|v_{p}\right|}^{p-2}v_{p}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in W^{1,p}(\Omega).

(19)

It is well known that the solution to this equation is the unique minimum of the functional

J_{p}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla\varphi\right|}^{p}\,dx+\frac{\beta^{p}}{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}{\left|\varphi\right|}^{p}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)-\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx.

(20)

Indeed, it is possible to prove the existence and the uniqueness of the minimum of the functional thanks to the so-called direct method of calculus of variation, see for instance
[
Dac08
,
Giu94
,
Lin06
,
AGM21
]
.

If we let
formally

p
go to
\infty
in (
20
), we obtain the functional

\varphi\longrightarrow\min\int_{\Omega}-f\varphi\,dx\quad\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega).

(21)

The limit procedure imposes two extra constraints to (
21
), namely

{\left\|\nabla\varphi\right\|}_{\infty}\leq 1,\quad\beta{\left\|\varphi\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 1.

The following proposition holds true.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
v_{p}
be the solution to (
18
). Then there exists a subsequence
\Set{v_{p_{j}}}_{j}
such that

v_{p_{j}}\to v_{\infty}\,\text{ uniformly,}\qquad\nabla v_{p_{j}}\to\nabla v_{\infty}\,\text{ weakly in }\,L^{m}(\Omega),\,\forall m>1.

Moreover

{\left\|\nabla v_{\infty}\right\|}_{\infty}\leq 1\qquad\beta{\left\|v_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 1.

Proof.

Choosing
\varphi=v_{p}
in (
19
), we have

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}=\int_{\Omega}fv_{p},

and Young inequality gives

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{p}^{p}p}\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{p}^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}.

(22)

Taking into account (
3
), we get

\begin{multlined}\left(1-\frac{1}{p\Lambda_{p}\varepsilon_{p}^{p}}\right)\left[\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\right]\leq\qquad\qquad\qquad\\
\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{p}^{p}p}\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{p}^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}.\end{multlined}

Choosing
\varepsilon_{p}
such that
\displaystyle{1-\frac{1}{p\Lambda_{p}\varepsilon_{p}^{p}}=\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}+\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\leq 2\frac{\varepsilon_{p}^{p^{\prime}}}{p^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}\leq C\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}

(23)

where the constant
C
is independent of
p
, thanks to Lemma
2.1
.

Hence

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(C\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(C{\left|\Omega\right|}\,{\left\|f\right\|}^{p^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/p},

\displaystyle\left(\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(C\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p}\leq\left(C{\left|\Omega\right|}\,{\left\|f\right\|}^{p^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/p}.

Analogously

\left(\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}f^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p}.

(24)

If
p>m
, Hölder inequality gives

\begin{gathered}\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{m}\right)^{1/m}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}{\left|\nabla v_{p}\right|}^{p}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m-1/p}\leq\left(C{\left\|f\right\|}^{p^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m},\\
\beta\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{m}\right)^{1/m}\leq\left(\beta^{p}\int_{\partial\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{1/m-1/p}\leq\left(C{\left\|f\right\|}^{p^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{1/m},\\
\left(\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{m}\right)^{1/m}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}v_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m-1/p}\leq C\left({\left\|f\right\|}_{\infty}^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{1/p}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m},\end{gathered}

(25)

Then there exists
v_{p_{j}}
such that

\displaystyle v_{p_{j}}\to v_{\infty}\,

\displaystyle\text{ uniformly,}\qquad\nabla v_{p_{j}}\to\nabla v_{\infty}\,\text{ weakly in }\,L^{m}(\Omega),\,\forall m>1,

moreover

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla v_{\infty}\right\|}_{m}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}{\left\|\nabla v_{p_{j}}\right\|}_{m}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(C{\left\|f\right\|}^{p_{j}^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/{p_{j}}}{\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m}={\left|\Omega\right|}^{1/m}

\displaystyle\beta{\left\|v_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{m}(\partial\Omega)}=\beta\lim_{j\to\infty}{\left\|v_{p_{j}}\right\|}_{L^{m}(\partial\Omega)}\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(C{\left\|f\right\|}^{p_{j}^{\prime}}_{\infty}\right)^{1/p_{j}}{\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{1/m}={\left|\partial\Omega\right|}^{1/m}

and then

{\left\|\nabla v_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1\quad\quad\beta{\left\|v_{\infty}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}\leq 1.

∎

Assume now that
\displaystyle{w(x)=\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)}
is the unique extremal of (
26
), and assume by contradiction that
\mathcal{R}\not\subset\Supp f
. Then, thanks to the Aronsson theorem (see
[
Aro67
]
), we can construct a function
\varphi
different from
w
which coincide with
w
in
\Supp f
and such that it is admissible for (
21
). Then
\varphi
is a minimum too.

This contradicts the fact that
w
is the unique minimum of (
21
), so
\mathcal{R}\subset\text{Supp}f
.

∎

We have to prove Lemma
4.6
to complete the proof.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
x\in\Omega\setminus\mathcal{R}
and set
\eta=\nabla(d(x,\partial\Omega))
. Let us consider
y_{t}=x+t\eta
, then there exists
T
such that
y_{T}\in\mathcal{R}
and
y_{t}\notin\mathcal{R}
for all
t<T
. Moreover,

\nabla d(x+t\eta,\partial\Omega)=\eta\qquad\forall t\in[0,T).

Proof.

Consider the following Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\dot{\gamma}(t)=\nabla d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega),\\
\gamma(0)=x\end{cases}

(30)

in a maximal interval
[0,T)
. We have that

•

\displaystyle{L(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{T}{\left|\dot{\gamma}(t)\right|}\,ds=T}
;

•

\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)=\nabla d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)\dot{\gamma}(t)=1,}

then

T=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{d}{dt}d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)\,dt=d(\gamma(T),\partial\Omega)-d(x,\partial\Omega).

These considerations give us the following:

•

T<\infty
, otherwise
d
is unbounded, and this is a contradiction as
\Omega
is bounded;

•

\gamma(T)\in\mathcal{R}
, otherwise one can extend the solution for
t>T
, in contradiction with the fact that
[0,T)
is the maximal interval.

In the end, if
y=\gamma(T)
, we have

d(y,\partial\Omega)=d(x,\partial\Omega)+T=d(x,\partial\Omega)+L(\gamma),

L(\gamma)\geq{\left|y-x\right|}
, and they are equal if and only if
\gamma
is a segment.

If
L(\gamma)>{\left|y-x\right|}
then

d(y,\partial\Omega)=d(x,\partial\Omega)+L(\gamma)>d(x,\partial\Omega)+{\left|y-x\right|}\geq{\left|y-z\right|}

with
z\in\partial\Omega
such that
d(x,\partial\Omega)={\left|x-z\right|}
, and this is a contradiction, because
d(y,\partial\Omega)
is the infimum.
Then
L(\gamma)={\left|y-x\right|}
and, remembering the fact
\dot{\gamma}(t)=\nabla d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)
, whose norm is 1, then
\gamma
is a segment and

\nabla d(\gamma(t),\partial\Omega)=\eta\qquad\forall t\in[0,T).

This conclude the proof.
∎

4.1
The limit PDE

We have proved that any limit
v_{\infty}
of subsequence
\Set{v_{p}}
is a minimum of the functional (
26
) defined in
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. Now we want to understand if such limits are solutions to a certain PDE, which, in some sense,
is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional (
26
).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
be a non-negative function. Then any limit
v_{\infty}
of a subsequence
\Set{v_{p}}
satisfies

{\left|\nabla v_{\infty}\right|}\leq 1\quad\qquad\qquad\text{in the viscosity sense}.

(31)

Proof.

The proof follows the techniques contained in
[
BDM89
,
JLM99
,
Yu07
,
LW08
]
.

If
x_{0}\in\Omega
, let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be such that
v_{\infty}-\varphi
has a local maximum at
x_{0}

(v_{\infty}-\varphi)(x_{0})\geq(v_{\infty}-\varphi)(x),\,\,\forall x\in B_{R}(x_{0}),

then

{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}\leq 1.

(32)

Indeed, let

C=\sup_{q>1}\max{\Set{{\left\|v_{q}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\right)};{\left\|\varphi\right\|}_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\right)}}},

we consider the following sequence

f_{q}(x)=v_{q}(x)-\varphi(x)-k{\left|x-x_{0}\right|}^{a},\quad k=\frac{4C}{R^{a}},\quad a>2.

So
f_{q}(x)\leq-3C
for
x\in\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
, and
f_{q}(x_{0})\geq-2C
. Then
f_{q}(x)
attains its maximum at some point
x_{q}
in the interior of
B_{R}(x_{0})
.
and it holds

\nabla v_{q}(x_{q})=\nabla\varphi(x_{q})+ka(x_{q}-x_{0}){\left|x_{q}-x_{0}\right|}^{a-2}.

Moreover, the sequence
\Set{x_{q}}
must converge to
x_{0}
.

Assume by contradiction that
{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}>1
, so there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}\geq 1+\delta
. Choosing
\overline{q}
large enough, we have

{\left|\nabla v_{q}(x_{q})\right|}>1+\frac{\delta}{2}-ka{\left|x_{q}-x_{0}\right|}^{a-1}\geq 1+\frac{\delta}{4},\quad\forall q\geq\overline{q}.

By Lemma 1.1 of Part III in
[
BDM89
]
,

{\left|\nabla v_{q}(x)\right|}\leq\left(\frac{\gamma}{R^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}(x_{0})}}\left(1+{\left|\nabla v_{q}\right|}\right)^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\qquad\forall x\in B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})

(33)

where
\gamma
is a constant independent of
q
.

For
q
sufficiently large, this contradict
{\left|\nabla v_{q}(x_{q})\right|}>1+\frac{\delta}{4}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
be a non-negative function, then a continuous weak solution to (
18
) is a viscosity solution.

Proof.

The proof follows the same techniques of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
be a non-negative function. Then any
v_{\infty}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\left|\nabla v_{\infty}\right|}=1\quad\qquad\qquad\,\text{on}\,\Set{f>0}

in the viscosity sense

(34)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\infty}v_{\infty}=0\quad\qquad\qquad\text{on}\,(\overline{\Set{f>0}})^{c}

in the viscosity sense

(35)

Proof.

We first prove (
34
). Let
x_{0}\in\Omega\cap\Set{f>0}
and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
v_{\infty}-\varphi
has a strict minimum in
x_{0}
. We want to show

{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}\geq 1

Let us denote by
x_{p}
the minimum of
v_{p}-\varphi
, we have that
x_{p}\to x_{0}
, then
x_{p}\in B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Set{f>0}
for
p
large enough. Setting
\varphi_{p}(x)=\varphi(x)+c_{p}
, then
c_{p}=v_{p}(x_{p})-\varphi(x_{p})\to 0
when
p
goes to infinity. We notice that
v_{p}(x_{p})=\varphi_{p}(x_{p})
and
v_{p}-\varphi_{p}
has a minimum in
x_{p}
, so by Proposition
4.8
,

-{\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-2}\Delta\varphi_{p}(x_{p})-(p-2){\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-4}\Delta_{\infty}\varphi(x_{p})\geq f(x_{p})>0.

Dividing by
(p-2){\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-4}
, we obtain

-\Delta_{\infty}\varphi_{p}(x_{p})-\frac{{\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{2}\Delta\varphi_{p}(x_{p})}{p-2}\geq\frac{f(x_{p})}{(p-2){\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-4}}

(36)

This gives us

{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}\geq 1
, otherwise the right-hand side would go to infinity, in contradiction with the fact that
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
.

We stress that, if we let
p\to\infty
in (
36
), we get
-\Delta_{\infty}\varphi(x_{0})\geq 0
, so
v_{\infty}
is a supersolution to
-\Delta_{\infty}u=0
.

Now, we only have to prove that
v_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution to
\Delta_{\infty}\varphi=0
in
(\overline{\Set{f>0}})^{c}
.

If we fix
x_{0}\in(\overline{\Set{f>0}})^{c}
, and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
v_{\infty}-\varphi
has a strict maximum in
x_{0}
, we can choose
R
such that
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset(\overline{\Set{f>0}})^{c}
. The function
v_{p}-\varphi
has a maximum
x_{p}\to x_{0}
, and
x_{p}\in B_{R}(x_{0})
for
p
large enough.

The definition of viscosity subsolution implies

-{\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-2}\Delta\varphi_{p}(x_{p})-(p-2){\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-4}\Delta_{\infty}\varphi(x_{p})\leq f(x_{p})=0.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
{\left|\nabla\varphi(x_{0})\right|}\neq 0
. Dividing both side of the last equation by
(p-2){\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{p-4}
, we obtain

-\Delta_{\infty}\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\leq\frac{{\left|\nabla\varphi_{p}(x_{p})\right|}^{2}\Delta\varphi_{p}(x_{p})}{p-2}.

Letting
p\to\infty
, we get

-\Delta_{\infty}\varphi(x_{0})\leq 0.

Analogously if
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is such that
v_{\infty}-\varphi
has a minimum at
x_{0}
, a symmetric argument shows that
-\Delta_{\infty}\varphi(x_{0})\geq 0
.
∎

Some examples

Example 4.3
.

We consider the case
\Omega=B_{1}(0)
and
f=1
.
The uniqueness of the solution and the invariance under rotation of the
p
-Laplacian ensure that the solution
v
is radially symmetric
and

r^{n-1}\Delta_{p}v_{p}=\frac{d}{dr}\left(r^{n-1}\left\lvert v_{p}^{\prime}\right\rvert^{p-2}v_{p}^{\prime}\right).

Setting
\alpha=1/(p-1)
, we have

v_{p}(x)=-\frac{p-1}{n^{\alpha}p}{\left|x\right|}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}+\frac{1}{(n\beta^{p})^{\alpha}}+\frac{p-1}{n^{\alpha}p}

Then

v_{\infty}(x)=-{\left|x\right|}+\frac{1}{\beta}+1=\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega)

Example 4.4
.

We fix
0<\varepsilon<1
and we consider

\left.f=\right.\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\\
0&\text{ if }x\in B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0).\end{cases}

In this case,
v_{p}
is radially symmetric, and

\left.v_{p}=\right.\begin{cases}\frac{p-1}{n^{\alpha}p}\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{p}{p-1}}-{\left|x\right|}^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{n\alpha}(p-1)}{n^{\alpha}(p-n)}\left(1-\varepsilon^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{n\alpha}}{(n\beta^{p})^{\alpha}}&\text{ if }x\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\\
\frac{\varepsilon^{n\alpha}(p-1)}{n^{\alpha}(p-n)}\left(1-{\left|x\right|}^{\frac{p-n}{p-1}}\right)+\frac{\varepsilon^{n\alpha}}{(n\beta^{p})^{\alpha}}&\text{ if }x\in B_{1}(0)\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(0)\end{cases}

Letting
p
go to infinity, we obtain

v_{\infty}=\frac{1}{\beta}+d(x,\partial\Omega).

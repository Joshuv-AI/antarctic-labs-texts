Global well-posedness of the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel system near a straight line
Bowei Tu

Abstract




Abstract.

We consider the Cauchy problem of the three-dimensional parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel chemotactic model. The initial data is almost a Dirac measure supported on a straight line with mass less than
8\pi
. We prove that if the data is sufficiently close to the straight line, then global well-posedness holds. This result is parallel to the work on vortex filament solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations by Bedrossian, Germain and Harrop-Griffiths
[
1
]
.

1
Introduction

We consider the following parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel chemotactic system in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\nabla\cdot\left(u\nabla c\right)=\Delta u,\\
-\Delta c=u,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\geq 0.\par\end{cases}

(1)

where
u
is cell density and
c
denotes concentration of chemo-attractant. This system is generally considered the fundamental model for the study of aggregation by chemotaxis of certain microorganisms
[
18
,
19
,
22
,
23
,
26
]
. The first equation describes the motion of the microorganism along the gradient of the chemo-attractant. The second equation describes the production and diffusion of the chemo-attractant. From now on, we will refer to (
1
) as Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS).

An important property of PKS in two dimensions is that it is
L^{1}
-critical. That is, if
u(t,x)
is a solution to (
1
), then for all
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
,

u_{\lambda}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}u\left(\frac{t}{\lambda^{2}},\frac{x}{\lambda}\right)

(2)

also solves (
1
) and preserves the
L^{1}
norm. Since the first equation is of divergence form, the mass

M:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u_{0}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}u(x,t)dx

is a conserved quantity. In fact, the mass serves as a dichotomy between global existence and finite-time blow-up: if
M<8\pi
, then there exists a global in time weak solution
[
11
,
14
]
; if
M>8\pi
, then all solutions blow up in finite time
[
11
,
20
,
24
]
; the case
M=8\pi
is delicate and global well-posedness results for radially symmetric solutions are obtained in
[
4
]
. We also mention the work by Bedrossian and Masmoudi
[
2
]
that proves local well-posedness in the space of finite Borel measures and establishes significant linear estimates for 2D PKS. There is a remarkable free energy structure for (
1
), but we will not discuss it here. Interested readers may refer to
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and references therein for its origin, properties and applications.

Another important property of 2D PKS is the existence of self-similar solutions for mass
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
. These are known to be global attractors for PKS
[
11
]
. We switch to self-similarity variables:

\xi=\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\ \ \ \tau=\log t.

and
w(\tau,\xi):=tu(x,t)
. In these variables, (
1
) becomes the following

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}w+\nabla\cdot(w\nabla c)=\Delta w+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\xi w),\\
-\Delta c=w.\end{cases}

(3)

Biler showed in
[
3
]
that there exists a radially symmetric self-similar solution if and only if the mass
\alpha\in[0,8\pi)
. Naito and Suzuki obtained in
[
25
]
that the self-similar solution must be radially symmetric by the method of moving planes. Uniqueness is obtained by Biler et al. in
[
4
]
. We list important properties of self-similar solutions below, which show that in many ways they are qualitatively similar to Oseen vortices of the Navier-Stokes equations. One can see Appendix B in
[
11
]
for proof of the asymptotic expansion and other interesting properties.

Proposition 1.1.
Let
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
.

(i) There exists a unique radially symmetric stationary solution to (
3
), denoted by
G_{\alpha}
, which is smooth, strictly positive, satisfies
||G_{\alpha}||_{1}=\alpha
and denoting
c_{\alpha}=-\Delta^{-1}G_{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle G_{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle\sim\dfrac{\alpha}{\int e^{c_{\alpha}(\zeta)-|\zeta|^{2}/4}d\zeta}|\xi|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/4}\ \ \ \text{as}\ \xi\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle\nabla G_{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle\sim-\dfrac{\alpha}{2\int e^{c_{\alpha}(\zeta)-|\zeta|^{2}/4}d\zeta}\left(\frac{\alpha\xi}{\pi|\xi|^{2}}+\xi\right)|\xi|^{-\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}}e^{-|\xi|^{2}/4}\ \ \ \text{as}\ \xi\rightarrow\infty.

in the sense of asymptotic expansion. Moreover, for all
p\in[1,\infty]
and
m\geq 0
we have

||G_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(m)}+||\nabla G_{\alpha}||_{L^{p}(m)}\lesssim_{m,p,\alpha}1.

(ii) In physical variables,
\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)
is the unique mild solution with initial data
\alpha\delta
, where
\delta
denotes the Dirac delta mass. In particular,
\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)\rightharpoonup^{*}\alpha\delta
as
t\searrow 0
.

When studied in higher dimensions
d\geq 3
, the dynamics for PKS are quite different. The system becomes mass-supercritical and the scaling transformation in (
2
) preserves the
L^{d/2}
norm. The authors in
[
12
]
showed that for initial data
||u_{0}||_{L^{d/2}}<C(d)
, where
C(d)
is related to Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, there exists a global in time weak solution. See also references in
[
12
]
for earlier results and technique concerning the global existence for (
1
). For certain size of initial data, there exists finite-time blow-up solutions of (
1
), see for example
[
13
]
. It is worth noting that there still exists a gap between the available criteria ensuring blow-up and global existence for general solutions of PKS. Partial results about radially symmetric case in terms of Morrey norms are recently obtained in
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

In this paper, we want to understand well-posedness theory for 3D PKS from another viewpoint. Taking
\mathbb{R}^{3}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
with coordinates
(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, an explicit example of solution evolved from the straight line is given by the 2D self-similar solution
\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)
with mass less than
8\pi
. Our work, to our knowledge, is the first to prove global well-posedness for 3D PKS near large self-similar solutions. To obtain this result, we apply the fluid dynamics regime to the chemotaxis model. This connection may be partially supported by the similarity of works between
[
2
]
and
[
15
]
. In addition, recently
[
1
]
analyzed the dynamics near the vortex filament in 3D Navier-Stokes regime and we expect similar results can hold for 3D PKS model. However, there is a new difficulty in our work: the perturbation term for core part in NSE case has zero integral. This no longer holds for PKS, hence the exponential decay estimate for 2D semigroups cannot be directly applied to control the perturbation term. In order to overcome this difficulty, we have to further decompose the perturbation term into two parts: one is of divergence form and the other has fewer nonlocal operators. By controlling the two parts separately, we can obtain the exponential-decay linear estimate for the core part, which is essential for the construction of contraction mapping.

It is natural to expect that the 2D self-similar solution provides the microscopic structure for the evolution of
any
smooth, non-self-interacting curve. For 3D NSE, this expectation is, in some sense, confirmed by
[
1
]
, where local coordinates are introduced near the filament that straighten out the curve and local well-posedness holds even for large perturbations of filament. For 3D PKS, however, this is still an open problem, and we hope to establish similar results in future papers.

i
, column index
j
.

•

If
a,b
are vectors, then
a\otimes b=\left(a^{i}b^{j}\right)_{ij}
.

•

For vectors, we denote
|v|
to be the usual norm induced by the Euclidean metric.

We denote
g\lesssim f
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
g\leq Cf
and we use
g\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta,...}f
to emphasize dependence of
C
on parameters
\alpha,\beta,...
. We similarly write
g\approx f
if we have both
g\lesssim f
and
f\lesssim g

.

2
Statement of Results and Outline of the Proof

2.1
Function Spaces

In order to state our results, we first define several function spaces.

To handle the self-similar part of the solution, for
1\leq p<\infty
and
m\geq 0
we define the weighted Lebesgue space
L_{\xi}^{p}(m)
with norm

||f||_{L_{\xi}^{p}(m)}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{pm}|f(\xi)|^{p}d\xi.

In order to get exponential decay estimates for semigroups, we also introduce the following function spaces:

L_{0}^{2}(m)=\left\{f\in L_{\xi}^{2}(m)\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}fd\xi=0\right\},

The following normalization is used for the Fourier transform in the
z
-direction throughout the paper:

\hat{f}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int f(z)e^{-iz\zeta}dz.

To control the regularity in the translation-invariant
z
-direction, for a Banach space X of functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define
B_{z}X
as the space of functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
with norm

||f||_{B_{z}X}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\hat{f}(\cdot,\zeta)||_{X}d\zeta,\ \ (x,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.

Finally, given a space of functions
X
continuously embedded in the space of tempered distributions
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
, we say that
u\in C_{w}([0,T],X)
if
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T],X)
and for all
t_{0}\in[0,T]
and test functions
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
we have

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow t_{0}}\left\langle u(t),\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle u(t_{0}),\phi\right\rangle.

2.2
Mild Solution

Formally, we write the solution of (
1
) with initial data
u(t=0)=u_{0}
using the Duhamel formula as

u(t)=e^{t\Delta}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}\text{div}(u(s)\nabla c(s))ds.

(4)

Giga and Miyakawa
[
17
]
considered solution of 3D NSE with vorticity in Morrey type space defined as the set of signed measures satisfying
\sup_{r>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\mu|(B(x,r))/r<+\infty
, where
|\mu|
is the total variation of
\mu
, and they observed that vortex rings, i,e., measures supported on a closed curve, fall into precisely this class. In fact, this setting can be applied to the chemotaxis model and we give a rigorous definition of what we mean by a mild solution of (
1
):

Definition 2.1.
Let
M^{\frac{3}{2}}
be the space of regular Borel measures such that

||\mu||_{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}:=\sup_{r>0,y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left\{r^{-1}|\mu(B(y,r))|\right\}<\infty.

Given a
T>0
, we call a function
u\in C_{w}([0,T];M^{\frac{3}{2}})
a mild solution to (
1
) with initial data
u_{0}\in M^{\frac{3}{2}}
provided

(i) the initial data is attained
u(0)=u_{0}
(hence
u(t)\rightharpoonup^{*}u(0)
as
t\searrow 0
);

(ii) the equations are satisfied in the sense of Duhamel’s formula (
4
) (and in particular, the Duhamel integral is well-defined).

2.3
Main Results

Now we state our main results for small perturbations of the straight line:

Theorem 2.2.
For any
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
and any
m>2
, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that if
\mu^{b}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies
||\mu^{b}||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\leq\epsilon
, then the following holds:

(i) (Existence) There exists a global mild solution of (
1
) with initial data

u(t=0)=\alpha\delta_{x=0}+\mu^{b}.

(5)

which can be decomposed into

u(t,x,z)=\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)+\frac{1}{t}U^{c}\left(\log t,\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},z\right)+u^{b}(t,x,z),

where the core part
U^{c}
and the background part
u^{b}
satisfy the estimates

\sup_{-\infty<\tau<\infty}||U^{c}(\tau)||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}+\sup_{0<t<\infty}t^{\frac{1}{4}}||u^{b}(t)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4/3}}\lesssim\epsilon.

(6)

(ii) (Uniqueness) If
u^{\prime}
is another mild solution with initial data (
5
) admitting the decomposition

u^{\prime}(t,x,z)=\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)+\frac{1}{t}(U^{c})^{\prime}\left(\log t,\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},z\right)+(u^{b})^{\prime}(t,x,z),

where
(U^{c})^{\prime}
and
(u^{b})^{\prime}
satisfy the bounds (
6
), then
u=u^{\prime}
.

(iii) (Lipschitz dependence) The solution map from the data to solution

\mu^{b}\mapsto(u^{b},U^{c})

is Lipschitz continuous if one endows the data space with norm on
B_{z}L_{x}^{1}
and the solution space with the norm (
6
).

The proof of Theorem 2.2 follows from applying the contraction mapping principle to the equations satisfied by the core and background pieces. In order to obtain bounds for these pieces we first introduce the self-similar coordinates

\tau=\log t,\ \ \ \xi=\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}},\ \ \ z=z.

where we note that as
G_{\alpha}
is translation-invariant in
z
, we do not rescale the
z
-coordinate. We then define

U(\tau,\xi,z)=e^{\tau}u(e^{\tau},e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,z),\ \ \ V(\tau,\xi,z)=e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}v(e^{\tau},e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,z).

where
v=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}u
. We may write the equation (
1
) as

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}U+\overline{\nabla}\cdot(UV)=(\mathcal{L}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz})U,\\
-\overline{\Delta}c=U.\end{cases}

where the rescaled gradient
\overline{\nabla}
, rescaled Laplacian
\overline{\Delta}
, and the 2D Fokker-Planck operator
\mathcal{L}
are defined by

\overline{\nabla}=\begin{bmatrix}\nabla_{\xi}\\
e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\partial_{z}\end{bmatrix},\ \ \ \overline{\Delta}=\Delta_{\xi}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz},\ \ \ \mathcal{L}=\Delta_{\xi}+\frac{1}{2}\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}+1.\ \ \

Finally denote

U^{g}=G_{\alpha}(\xi),\ \ \ V^{g}=\begin{bmatrix}V^{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

where
V^{G_{\alpha}}=\nabla_{\xi}(\Delta_{\xi})^{-1}G_{\alpha}
.

The core piece,
U^{c}
, is taken to satisfy the equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}U^{c}+\overline{\nabla}\cdot((U^{g}+U^{c})\cdot V)=(\mathcal{L}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz})(U^{g}+U^{c}),\\
\lim\limits_{\tau\rightarrow-\infty}U^{c}(\tau)=0.\end{cases}

In order to solve this equation, we need to prove estimates for the solution operator
U(\tau)=S(\tau,\sigma)U(\sigma)
of the corresponding linearized equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}U+\nabla_{\xi}\cdot(UV^{G_{\alpha}})+\overline{\nabla}\cdot(G_{\alpha}V)=(\mathcal{L}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz})U,\\
V=\overline{\nabla}\left(-\overline{\Delta}\right)^{-1}U.\end{cases}

The background piece,
u^{b}
, is taken to satisfy the equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{b}+\nabla\cdot(u^{b}v)=\Delta u^{b},\\
u^{b}|_{t=0}=\mu^{b}.\end{cases}

Solutions are constructed by establishing estimates for the solution operator
u(t)=\mathbb{S}(t,s)u(s)
for the corresponding linearized equation

\partial_{t}u+\nabla\cdot(uv^{g})=\Delta u

where
v^{g}
is
V^{g}
in physical coordinates.

The bulk of this paper is to obtain estimates for the linear propagators
S(\tau,\sigma),\mathbb{S}(t,s)
. Given these bounds, the proof of Theorem 2.1 follows from an elementary application of the contraction mapping principle that we carry out in Section 5.

3
Linear Estimates for the Core Part

3.1
Statement of the Estimates

Consider the following linearized equation

\partial_{\tau}U+\nabla_{\xi}\cdot(UV^{G_{\alpha}})+\overline{\nabla}\cdot(G_{\alpha}V)=(\mathcal{L}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz})U.

(7)

For
\tau\geq\sigma
we define the solution operator
S(\tau,\sigma)
for the equation (
7
) by

U(\tau)=S(\tau,\sigma)U(\sigma).

In this section we prove the following result:

Theorem 3.1.
Let
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
and
m>2
. Then for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
the map
\tau\mapsto S(\tau,\sigma)
is continuous as a map from
[\sigma,\infty)
to the space of bounded operators on
B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)
. For any scalar
F
we have the estimate

||S(\tau,\sigma)F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim||F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(8)

Further, there exists
\nu=\nu(\alpha)\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
such that, if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}Fd\xi=0
, we have the estimate

||S(\tau,\sigma)F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}||F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(9)

Finally, for
1<p\leq 2
and all 3-vectors
F\in B_{z}L_{\xi}^{p}(m)
, we have the estimate

||S(\tau,\sigma)\overline{div}F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{a(\tau-\sigma)^{\frac{1}{p}}}||F||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{p}(m)}.

(10)

where
a(\tau)=1-e^{-\tau}
,
\overline{div}F=\overline{\nabla}\cdot F
.

3.2
Long Time Estimates

In this subsection we prove that the solution operator
S(\tau,\sigma)
is well-defined, and satisfies the estimates (
8
), (
9
).

We start by taking the Fourier transform in
z
of the equation (
7
) and setting
W(\tau,\xi,\zeta)=\widehat{U}(\tau,\xi,\zeta)
to obtain the equation

(\partial_{\tau}+e^{\tau}|\zeta|^{2}-\mathcal{L}+\Lambda_{\alpha})W=\tilde{Z}(W),

(11)

where the linear operator is denoted

\Lambda_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\xi}\cdot\left(V^{G_{\alpha}}\cdot\right)+\nabla_{\xi}\cdot V^{G_{\alpha}}+\nabla_{\xi}G_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left(-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}\cdot\right),

and the perturbation term is given by

Z(W)=-\nabla_{\xi}G_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left(-\Delta_{\xi}+e^{\tau}|\zeta|^{2}\right)^{-1}W\right)+\nabla_{\xi}G_{\alpha}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\left(\left(-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}W\right).

The existence of the solution operator
S(\tau,\sigma)
and the estimates (
8
), (
9
) are given in the following proposition:

Proposition 3.2.
Let
m>2
,
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
, and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed. Then, for all
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and all
w_{\sigma}\in L_{\xi}^{2}(m)
, there exists a unique mild solution
W\in C\left([\sigma,\infty);L_{\xi}^{2}(m)\right)
satisfying
W(\sigma)=w_{\sigma}
and the estimate

||W(\tau)||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim||w_{\sigma}||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(12)

Furthermore, there exists some
\nu=\nu(\alpha)\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
so that, for all
w_{\sigma}\in L_{0}^{2}(m)
, we have the improved estimate

||W(\tau)||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}||w_{\sigma}||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(13)

In both estimates, the implicit constant is independent of
\zeta
.

We first recall some known properties of the semigroup
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}
:

Lemma 3.3

[
16
]
. Let
m\geq 0
.

(i)
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L^{2}(m)
and satisfies

||e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim||f||_{L^{2}(m)},\ \ \ ||\nabla e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{a(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}}||f||_{L^{2}(m)}.

(14)

(ii) If
m>2
, then for all
f\in L_{0}^{2}(m)
,

||e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}\tau}||f||_{L^{2}(m)}.

(15)

(iii) Furthermore, for all
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

||\partial^{\alpha}e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}f||_{L^{p}(m)}\lesssim\dfrac{1}{a(\tau)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}+\frac{|\alpha|}{2}}}||f||_{L^{q}(m)}.

(16)

As a compact perturbation of
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}
,
e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}
has similar semigroup estimates:

Lemma 3.4

[
2
]
. Let
\alpha\in(0,8\pi)
and
m>2
.

(i)
e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
L_{\xi}^{2}(m)
and satisfies

||e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim_{\alpha}||f||_{L^{2}(m)},\ \ \ ||\nabla e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim_{\alpha}\dfrac{1}{a(\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}}||f||_{L^{2}(m)}

(17)

(ii) There exists
\nu=\nu(\alpha)\in(0,1/2)
such that for all
f\in L_{0}^{2}(m)
,

||e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim_{\alpha}e^{-\nu\tau}||f||_{L^{2}(m)}.

(18)

(iii) If
q\in(1,2]
then
e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}\nabla
is a bounded operator from
L^{q}(m)
to
L_{0}^{2}(m)
and there exists a
\nu\in(0,1/2)
(the same
\nu
as in (ii)) such that

||e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Lambda_{\alpha})}\nabla f||_{L^{2}(m)}\lesssim_{\alpha}\dfrac{e^{-\nu\tau}}{a(\tau)^{1/q}}||f||_{L^{q}(m)}.

(19)

In addition, we prove several Biot-Savart type estimates that are crucial for the control of perturbation terms:

Lemma 3.5.
Let
m>1
and
\lambda>0
.

(i) If
1<r\leq\infty
and
0<\delta\leq\min\{\frac{1}{2},1-\frac{1}{r}\}
, then

||\left(\lambda^{2}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f||_{L_{\xi}^{r}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\left(\frac{2}{r}+2\delta\right)}||f||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(20)

(ii) If
2\leq r<\infty
, then

||\nabla_{\xi}\left(-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f||_{L_{\xi}^{r}}\lesssim||f||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

(21)

(iii)

||\nabla\left(-\Delta\right)^{-1}f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim||f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4/3}}

(22)

Proof.

(i) It is known that

\left(\lambda^{2}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K(\lambda(\xi-\eta))f(\eta)d\eta.

where
K
is the 2D Bessel potential and for all
1\leq p<\infty
,
K\in L_{\xi}^{p}
. If
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1+\frac{1}{r}
, then applying Young’s inequality, we have

||\left(\lambda^{2}-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f||_{L_{\xi}^{r}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{2}{p}}||K||_{L_{\xi}^{p}}||f||_{L_{\xi}^{q}}.

For
1\leq q\leq 2
and
\frac{1}{q}<\frac{m+1}{2}
we have the embedding

||f||_{L_{\xi}^{q}}\lesssim||f||_{L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}.

Taking
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{r}+\delta
and
\frac{1}{q}=1-\delta
, we obtain the estimate in (i).

(ii) Note that
\nabla_{\xi}\left(-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f=k\ast f
, where

k(\xi)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\xi}{|\xi|^{2}}.

Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev’s inequality tells us that

||\nabla_{\xi}\left(-\Delta_{\xi}\right)^{-1}f||_{L_{\xi}^{r}}\lesssim||f||_{L^{\frac{2r}{2+r}}}.

Using the embedding
L_{\xi}^{2}(m)\subset L^{\frac{2r}{2+r}}

(iii) By scaling it suffices to prove that

\mathbb{S}(t,s)div
. Note that

\mathbb{S}(t,s)div=divK(t,s)

(34)

where
K(t,s)
is the evolution operator of the following equation:

\partial_{t}F+v^{g}divF=\Delta F

(35)

We need to estimate
K(t,s)
:

Proposition 4.2.
For all
s\in(0,t)
, we have

||K(t,s)f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim_{\alpha}||f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

(36)

Proof.

Taking the
z
-direction Fourier transform in (
35
), we obtain the equation

\partial_{t}\hat{F}+v^{g}\left(div_{x}\hat{F^{x}}+i\zeta\hat{F^{z}}\right)=\left(\Delta_{x}-|\zeta|^{2}\right)\hat{F}.

We may then decompose this into a system of two equations:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\hat{F}^{x}+\left(v^{g}\right)^{x}\left(div_{x}\hat{F}^{x}+i\zeta\hat{F}^{z}\right)=\left(\Delta_{x}-|\zeta|^{2}\right)\hat{F}^{x},\\
\partial_{t}\hat{F}^{z}=\left(\Delta_{x}-|\zeta|^{2}\right)\hat{F}^{z}.\end{cases}

Next we switch to self-similar variables, letting

H(\tau,\xi,\zeta)=e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\hat{F}\left(e^{\tau},e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}\xi,\zeta\right).

to obtain the system

\begin{cases}\partial_{\tau}H^{x}+V^{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)div_{\xi}H^{x}+i\zeta e^{\frac{\tau}{2}}V^{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)H^{z}=\left(\mathcal{L}-\frac{1}{2}-e^{\tau}|\zeta|^{2}\right)H^{x},\\
\partial_{\tau}H^{z}=\left(\mathcal{L}-\frac{1}{2}-e^{\tau}|\zeta|^{2}\right)H^{z}.\end{cases}

•

H^{z}
part: Using the semigroup estimate of
e^{\tau\mathcal{L}}
on
L^{1}
,

||H^{z}(\tau)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{z}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}.

•

H^{x}
part: Denote

\Xi_{\alpha}:=V^{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)div_{\xi}.

A contraction mapping argument similar to Proposition 3.7 shows that there exists
0<\delta=\delta(\alpha)<<1
such that for all
\tau\in[0,\delta]
,

||e^{\tau(\mathcal{L}-\Xi_{\alpha})}||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}\lesssim_{\alpha}||f||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}.

Obviously this estimate also holds for all
\tau>0
. Now write the integral equation for
H^{x}
:

H^{x}(\tau)=e^{-(e^{\tau}-e^{\sigma})|\zeta|^{2}+(\tau-\sigma)(\mathcal{L}-\Xi_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2})}H^{x}(\sigma)-\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}e^{-(e^{\tau}-e^{s})|\zeta|^{2}+(\tau-s)(\mathcal{L}-\Xi_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{2})}i\zeta e^{\frac{s}{2}}V^{G_{\alpha}}(\xi)H^{z}(s)ds.

Taking the
L_{\xi}^{1}
norm on both sides and using the estimate for
H^{z}
part, we obtain

\displaystyle||H^{x}(\tau)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{x}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}e^{-(e^{\tau}-e^{s})|\zeta|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-s)}|\zeta|e^{\frac{s}{2}}||H^{z}(s)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{x}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}e^{-(e^{\tau}-e^{s})|\zeta|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-s)}|\zeta|ds\cdot e^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}||H^{z}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{x}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}e^{-(e^{\tau}-e^{s})|\zeta|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-s)}|\zeta|\sqrt{e^{\tau}-e^{s}}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e^{\tau}-e^{s}}}ds\cdot e^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}||H^{z}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{x}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+\int_{\sigma}^{\tau}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{e^{\tau-s}-1}}ds\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}||H^{z}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim e^{-\frac{1}{2}(\tau-\sigma)}(||H^{x}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}}+||H^{z}(\sigma)||_{L_{\xi}^{1}})

Combining the above estimates and returning to the physical variables, we obtain the desired estimate for
K(t,s)
.
∎

We also need short time estimates for
\mathbb{S}(t,s)div
.

Proposition 4.3.
There exists
0<\delta=\delta(\alpha)<<1
so that for all
0<s<t\leq(1+\delta)s
and all 3-vector
F\in B_{z}L_{x}^{1}
, we have the estimate

||\mathbb{S}(t,s)divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim\left(t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

(37)

Proof.

The proof is similar to the short time estimates in Section 3. Write (
35
) in integral form

F(t)=e^{(t-s)\Delta}F(s)-\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-\sigma)\Delta}v^{g}divF(\sigma)d\sigma.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}F=e^{(t-s)\Delta}F(s)-\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-\sigma)\Delta}v^{g}divF(\sigma)d\sigma,

\displaystyle Z=\{F\in B_{z}L_{x}^{1}:||F||_{Z}<+\infty\},

\displaystyle||F||_{Z}=\sup_{t\in[s,(1+\delta)s]}\left(||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}||divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\right).

Using the heat kernel estimates

\displaystyle||e^{t\Delta}f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim||f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}},

\displaystyle||e^{t\Delta}divf||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}||f||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}},

We obtain

\displaystyle||TF(t)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\int_{s}^{t}\left(t-\sigma\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F\otimes v^{g}||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}d\sigma+\int_{s}^{t}||\left(F\cdot\nabla\right)v^{g}||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\int_{s}^{t}\left(t-\sigma\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F||_{Z}d\sigma+\log(1+\delta)||F||_{Z}

\displaystyle\lesssim||F||_{Z}\left(1+\sqrt{\delta}+\log(1+\delta)\right),

\displaystyle\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}||divTF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\left(t-\sigma\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||v^{g}divF(\sigma)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\left(t-\sigma\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sigma^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sigma-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}d\sigma||F||_{Z}

\displaystyle\lesssim||F||_{Z}\left(1+\sqrt{\delta}\right).

Hence
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction on
Z
. Banach’s fixed point theorem tells us that there exists a unique solution of (
35
) in
Z
satisfying

\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}||divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\sqrt{\delta}||F||_{Z}.

Taking the supremum of
t
, we get

\left(1-\sqrt{\delta}\right)||F||_{Z}\lesssim||F(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

Therefore

||\mathbb{S}(t,s)divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\lesssim\left(t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

∎

Finally we establish the long time estimate for
\mathbb{S}(t,s)div
:

Theorem 4.4.
Let
0<s<t\leq T
,
F
is a 3-vector in
B_{z}L_{x}^{1}
, then for all
p\in[1,\infty]
we have the estimate

||\mathbb{S}(t,s)divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{p}}\lesssim\left(t-s\right)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}},

(38)

The implicit constant depends on
p,\alpha
.

Proof.

First notice that if
p=1
case holds, then for general
p
we have

\displaystyle||\mathbb{S}(t,s)divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{p}}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|\mathbb{S}\left(t,\frac{t+s}{2}\right)\mathbb{S}\left(\frac{t+s}{2},s\right)divF\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left|\left|\mathbb{S}\left(\frac{t+s}{2},s\right)divF\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(t-s\right)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

Hence it suffices to prove the case for
p=1
. Take the
\delta
in Proposition 4.3.

Case 1:

s<t\leq(1+\delta)s
. The estimate can be derived directly from Proposition 4.3.

Case 2:

(1+\delta)s<t\leq T
. In this case we apply (
34
) near the end point
T
:

\displaystyle||\mathbb{S}(t,s)divF||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left|\mathbb{S}\left(t,\frac{2}{2+\delta}t\right)divK\left(\frac{2}{2+\delta}t,s\right)F\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left|\left|K\left(\frac{2}{2+\delta}t,s\right)F\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(t-s\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}.

∎

5
Nonlinear Estimates

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem 2.2. We start by splitting the cell density

u(t,x,z)=\frac{1}{t}G_{\alpha}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t}}\right)+u^{c}(t,x,z)+u^{b}(t,x,z)

with the corresponding velocity field given by the Biot-Savart law

v(t,x,z)=v^{g}(t,x,z)+v^{c}(t,x,z)+v^{b}(t,x,z)

As usual, we capitalize
u
and
v
in self-similar variables: for
*=b\ \text{or}\ c
,

U^{*}(\tau,\xi,z)=tu^{*}(t,x,z),\ \ \ V^{*}(\tau,\xi,z)=\sqrt{t}v^{*}(t,x,z).

Recall that
S
is the semigroup associated to
\partial_{\tau}U+\nabla_{\xi}\cdot(UV^{G_{\alpha}})+\overline{\nabla}\cdot(G_{\alpha}V)=(\mathcal{L}+e^{\tau}\partial_{zz})U
, while
\mathbb{S}
is the semigroup associated to
\partial_{t}u+\nabla\cdot(uv^{g})=\Delta u
.

Duhamel’s formula then formally gives

u^{b}(t)=\mathbb{S}(t,0)\mu^{b}-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}(t,s)\nabla\cdot\left(u^{b}{v}^{c}\right)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}(t,s)\nabla\cdot\left(u^{b}v^{b}\right)ds,

U^{c}(\tau)=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}S(\tau,\sigma)\overline{\nabla}\cdot\left(G_{\alpha}V^{b}\right)d\sigma-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}S(\tau,\sigma)\overline{\nabla}\cdot\left(U^{c}{V}^{b}\right)d\sigma-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}S(\tau,\sigma)\overline{\nabla}\cdot\left(U^{c}V^{c}\right)d\sigma.

Writing
\mathcal{Q}
for the above right-hand side, we are looking for a solution of the equation

(u^{b},U^{c})=\left(\mathcal{Q}^{b}\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right),\mathcal{Q}^{c}\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right)\right)=\mathcal{Q}\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right).

Taking
m>2
, we will solve this fixed point problem by applying the contraction mapping principle in the following ball, for constants
M,D\geq 1
determined by the proof below,

B_{\epsilon,T}=\left\{(u^{b},U^{c})\bigg|\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}:=M\sup_{t\in(0,T)}t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\left|u^{b}(t)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}+\sup_{\tau\in(-\infty,\log T)}\left|\left|U^{c}(\tau)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\leq D\epsilon\right\},

To prove Theorem 2.2 we will verify that, whenever the data satisfies
||\mu^{b}||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\leq\epsilon
, the map
\mathcal{Q}:B_{\epsilon,T}\rightarrow B_{\epsilon,T}
is a contraction for any
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
, any
T>0
and an appropriate choice of the constants
M,D
and
\epsilon_{0}
.

Bound for the core
. We abbreviate the three summands in the definition of
\mathcal{Q}^{c}
by

Q^{c}(u^{b},U^{c})=L^{c}+N_{0}^{c}+N_{1}^{c}.

By (
10
) and the rapid decay of
G_{\alpha}
,

\left|\left|L^{c}(\tau)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{\left(a(\tau-\sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\left|G_{\alpha}V^{b}(\sigma)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}d\sigma\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{\left(a(\tau-\sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\left|V^{b}(\sigma)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{4}}d\sigma

By scaling and the Biot-Savart bounds in Lemma 3.5 (iii)

\left|\left|V^{b}(\tau)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{4}}=e^{\frac{\tau}{4}}\left|\left|v^{b}(e^{\tau})\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\left|u^{b}(t)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}.

Hence, with an implicit constant independent of
T
and
\epsilon
,

||L^{c}(\tau)||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{\left(a(\tau-\sigma)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}d\sigma\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}.

Using again (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\left|N_{0}^{c}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{(a(\tau-\sigma))^{\frac{3}{4}}}\left|\left|U^{c}{V}^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{4/3}(m)}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{(a(\tau-\sigma))^{\frac{3}{4}}}\left|\left|U^{c}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\left|\left|{V}^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{4}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\dfrac{e^{-\nu(\tau-\sigma)}}{(a(\tau-\sigma))^{\frac{3}{4}}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}^{2}.

The same proof applies to the nonlinear term
N_{1}^{c}
:

\left|\left|N_{1}^{c}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}^{2}.

Bound for the background
. We abbreviate the three summands in the definition of
\mathcal{Q}^{b}
by

Q^{b}\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right)=L^{b}+N_{0}^{b}+N_{1}^{b}.

By (
38
),

\displaystyle t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\left|N_{0}^{b}(t)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\left|\left|u^{b}{v}^{c}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\left|\left|u^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}\left|\left|{v}^{c}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{\frac{1}{4}}\frac{1}{M}\left|\left|\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right)\right|\right|_{X}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{\left(t-s\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}s^{\frac{1}{2}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\left|\left|\left(u^{b},U^{c}\right)\right|\right|_{X}^{2}.

where we used scaling and Lemma 3.5 (iii)

||v^{c}(t)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim||u^{c}(t)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}=t^{-\frac{1}{4}}||U^{c}(\tau)||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}||U^{c}(\tau)||_{B_{z}L_{\xi}^{2}(m)}\lesssim t^{-\frac{1}{4}}||(u^{b},U^{c})||_{X}.

Similarly,

t^{\frac{1}{4}}\left|\left|N_{1}^{b}(t)\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{M^{2}}\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}^{2}.

Combining Proposition 4.1 and the above estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left|Q(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}

\displaystyle\lesssim M\left|\left|\mu^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{M}\right)+\left|\left|(u^{b},U^{c})\right|\right|_{X}\frac{1}{M}

\displaystyle\lesssim M\left|\left|\mu^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}+\frac{D\epsilon}{M}+\frac{M+1}{M}D^{2}\epsilon^{2}.

Let

\left|\left|\mu^{b}\right|\right|_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}\leq\epsilon,\ \ \ M+\frac{D}{M}<D,\ \ \ \epsilon\leq\dfrac{D-M-\frac{D}{M}}{\frac{M+1}{M}D^{2}},\ \ \ \epsilon\leq\frac{\beta-\frac{1}{M}}{(1+\frac{1}{M})D}\ \ \left(\beta\in(0,1)\right).

Then
\mathcal{Q}:B_{\epsilon,T}\rightarrow B_{\epsilon,T}
is a contraction, which proves the main theorem.

Mild solution of PKS
. Finally, we verify that the solution constructed by the contraction mapping argument above does indeed satisfy Definition 2.1 (the proof is identical to that in
[
1
]
). For this we need two things:
(a)\ u\in C_{w}([0,T];M^{\frac{3}{2}})
, and in particular
\lim_{t\searrow 0}u(t)=\alpha\delta_{x=0}+\mu^{b}
in the sense of
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
;
(b)
that the mild form of the equations (
4
) is satisfied in the sense of tempered distributions.

Using Proposition 4.1, the contraction mapping argument above and the embedding
B_{z}L_{x}^{1}\subset M^{\frac{3}{2}}
, one can obtain the a priori estimate

\sup_{0<t<T}||u(t)||_{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}\lesssim 1

and classical parabolic regularity ensures that
u\in C((0,T],M^{\frac{3}{2}})
.

It remains to verify that
\lim_{t\searrow 0}u(t)=\alpha\delta_{x=0}+\mu^{b}
in the sense of tempered distributions. The term involving
G_{\alpha}
converges to
\alpha\delta_{x=0}
weak* (hence in the sense of tempered distributions). By an inspection of the contraction mapping argument above, we see that the solution constructed satisfies

\lim\limits_{t\searrow 0}||u^{c}(t)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{1}}=0.

Appealing to the Duhamel formula for
u^{b}
, for any
\phi\in\mathscr{S}
, we may apply (
34
) to yield

\int\phi\cdot\mathbb{S}(t,s)\nabla\cdot(u^{b}v^{b})dx=-\int\nabla\phi\cdot K(t,s)(u^{b}v^{b})dx\lesssim||u^{b}(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{4}}||\omega^{b}(s)||_{B_{z}L_{x}^{\frac{4}{3}}}\lesssim\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

and similarly for the other nonlinear term in the
u^{b}
equation. Hence, by the weak* continuity of
\mathbb{S}(t,s)
,
u^{b}(s)
converges to
\mu^{b}
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
and we have proved that
u\in C_{w}([0,T];M^{\frac{3}{2}})
.

Next we verify that
u(t)
satisfies (
4
). By parabolic regularity,
u
is a classical solution of the 3D Patlak-Keller-Segel system for
t>0
. Hence, for all
0<s<t
,

u(t)=e^{(t-s)\Delta}u(s)-\int_{s}^{t}e^{(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\text{div}(u(t^{\prime})\nabla c(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}.

Due to the self-adjointness of the heat semigroup in
L^{2}
, we can pass to the limit in the first term due to the continuity of
u
in
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
:
\lim_{s\searrow 0}e^{(t-s)\Delta}u(s)=e^{t\Delta}u(0)
. Furthermore, we may pass
s\searrow 0
also in the nonlinear term: indeed, using heat semigroup estimates on Morrey spaces (see
[
17
]
Proposition 3.2), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left|\left|e^{(t-t^{\prime})\Delta}\text{div}(u(t^{\prime})\nabla c(t^{\prime}))\right|\right|_{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(t-t^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}}||u(t^{\prime})\nabla c(t^{\prime})||_{M^{\frac{3}{2}}}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+||(u^{b},U^{c})||_{X}+||(u^{b},U^{c})||^{2}_{X}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{1}{(t-t^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}}dt^{\prime}.

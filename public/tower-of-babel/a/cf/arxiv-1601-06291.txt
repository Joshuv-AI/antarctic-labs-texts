A Characterization for the Existence of Connected f f -Factors of Large Minimum Degree Note: Supported by the Indo-German Max Planck Center for Computer Science grant for the year 2013-2014 in the area of Algorithms and Complexity
N. S. Narayanaswamy, C. S. Rahul

Abstract
It is well known that when f ⁡ ( v ) f(v) is a constant for each vertex v v , the connected f f -factor problem is NP-Complete. In this note we consider the case when f ⁡ ( v ) ≥ ⌈ n 2.5 ⌉ f(v)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2.5}\rceil for each vertex v v , where n n is the number of vertices. We present a diameter based characterization of graphs having a connected f f -factor (for such f f ). We show that if a graph G G has a connected f f -factor and an f f -factor with 2 connected components, then it has a connected f f -factor of diameter at least 3. This result yields a polynomial time algorithm which first executes the Tutte’s f f -factor algorithm, and if the output has 2 connected components, our algorithm searches for a connected f f -factor of diameter at least 3.

Introduction

Given a simple undirected graph
G=(V,E)
, (see
West 2001
), on
n
vertices, an
f
-factor of
G
is a spanning subgraph
H
such that
d_{H}(v)=f(v)
for every
v
in
V
. The problem of deciding whether a given graph

has an
f
-factor is a well studied problem over many years by
Anstee 1985
,
Tutte 1952
,
Cornuéjols 1988
,
Liu and Zhang 2008
and
Plummer 2007
.
Tutte 1954
has shown the problem to be polynomial time reducible to perfect matching. Since perfect matching is polynomial time solvable it follows that testing for an
f
-factor is polynomial time solvable, and we refer to this algorithm as the
Tutte’s
f
-factor algorithm
.
We consider the problem of finding a connected subgraph which is an
f
-factor.
For the case where
f(v)=2
for every
v
in
V
, a connected
f
-factor is a Hamiltonian cycle. It is well known that the Hamiltonian cycle problem is NP-Complete. In fact,
Cheah and Corneil 1990
have shown that for each constant
d
, when
f(v)=d
for every
v
, the connected
f
-factor problem is NP-Complete. We refer this connected regular spanning subgraph as a connected
d
-factor. Further, recently
Cornelissen et al. 2014
showed that for every
0<\epsilon<1
, for
f(v)\geq n^{1-\epsilon}
the connected
f
-factor problem is NP-Complete.

For the case when
f(v)\geq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1
,
Cornelissen et al. 2014
have also shown a simple test for the existence of a connected
f
-factor.
We observe that when
f(v)\geq\lceil n/2\rceil
for every
v
in
V
, any
f
-factor of a given graph
G
is connected and has diameter at most 2. From the work by
Cornelissen et al. 2014
when
f(v)=\lceil n/2\rceil-1
, if the input graph has a
f
-factor with two components and
also
a connected
f
-factor, then there exists a connected
f
-factor of diameter 3. Similarly, for the case where
f(v)\geq\frac{n}{c}
for each
v
in
V
and a constant
c
, the diameter of a connected
f
-factor is upper bounded by
3c-1
. This is because if we consider a diametric path
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{3c}
, the sets
N(u_{i})
and
N(u_{i+j})
have to be disjoint for each
j\geq 3
. Further, each such set
|N(u_{i})|\geq\frac{n}{c}
and it is impossible to have such
c
pairwise disjoint vertex sets. Thus for
f(v)\geq\frac{n}{c}
for each
v
in
V
, there seems to be a concrete correspondence between the diameter of connected
f
-factors and the value
c
. We use this correspondence to come up with a polynomial time algorithm for
c=2.5
. It may be possible to extend the idea to solve the problem for any constant
c
.

Our Work.

We present a diameter based characterization of graphs having connected

If an instance

Equitably Colored Graphs and Alternating Circuits

On the Diameter of Connected
f
-factors

In this section we present our diameter based characterization for graphs which have a connected
f
-factor.
The following two facts are simple graph theoretic facts and the proofs are straightforward.

Fact 2
.

Let
G
be an undirected graph such that the minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
, then the diameter of
G
is at most 2.

Fact 3
.

Let
G
be a graph in which each edge is assigned a color from the set
\{red,blue\}
.
G
is a set of vertex disjoint alternating-circuits if and only if
d_{R}(v)=d_{B}(v)
for all
v
in
G^{\prime}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
T
be a minimal alternating-circuit. For each vertex
v
, both
d_{R}(v)=d_{B}(v)\leq 2
.

Proof.

The proof is by contradiction. Let
v
be a vertex in
T
which has at least 3 incident red edges. We show the existence of an alternating circuit
T^{\prime}
whose edges are a proper subset of the edges of
T
as follows: Consider the sequence of edges obtained from
T
by starting at the first occurence of
v
in
T
, and returning to
v
for the
the second time
. Let
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
be the edges incident on
v
in the sequence, in the same order in which they were present in the sequence.
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
are consecutive in the sequence and are of different colors. If
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are of different colors, then the sequence of edges from
e_{1}
to
e_{2}
is an alternating circuit, thus contradicting the minimality of
T
. If
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are of the same color, and if
e_{3}
and
e_{4}
are of the same color, all the edges in the sequence form an alternating circuit, and is proper subset of the edges in
T
. The proper containment follows because at least one red edge incident of
v
is not present in this sequence. This contradicts the minimality of
T
. If
e_{3}
and
e_{4}
are of different colors, then the sequence of edges from
e_{3}
to
e_{4}
is an alternating circuit, again contradicting the minimality of
T
. Hence the lemma.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Let
G
be a graph of diameter 2 and let
\{X,V\setminus X\}
be a partition of
V(G)
. There exists a set of edges
E^{\prime}\subseteq[X,V\setminus X]
that covers
\{X,V\setminus X\}
.

Proof.

We obtain a contradiction to the fact that the graph has diameter 3 by assuming that the lemma is false.
Let
v_{1}\in X,v_{2}\in V\setminus X
such that
N(v_{1})\subseteq X
and
N(v_{2})\subseteq V\setminus X
.
Then the shortest path between
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
is of length at least 3, and this contradicts the premise
diam~G=2
. Hence the claim in the lemma is true.
∎

In the following, given an
f
-factor of
G^{\prime}
of
G
we consider the coloring of
G
in which
E(G^{\prime})
is colored red, and
E(G)\setminus E(G^{\prime})
is colored blue. The alternating circuits we consider are with respect to this coloring.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least 12 vertices.
Let
X
and
V\setminus X
be two components of an
f
-factor
G^{\prime}
of
G
. Let
T
be a minimal alternating-circuit which is a switch on
G^{\prime}
and is incident on
\{X,V\setminus X\}
.
Then Switching(
G^{\prime}
,
T
) is a connected
f
-factor of
G
.

Proof.

Since
X
and
V\setminus X
have at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2.5}\rceil+1
vertices, it follows that both
X
and
V\setminus X
are of size at most
\lfloor{\frac{1.5n}{2.5}}\rfloor-1
. Let
G^{\prime\prime}
denote the graph Switching(
G^{\prime}
,
T
). Without loss of generality, let us consider
X
. We know that the minimum degree in
G^{\prime}[X]
is at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2.5}\rceil
. Since
T
is a minimal alternating circuit, from Lemma
1
, it follows that the degree of each vertex in
G^{\prime\prime}[X]
is at least
\lceil\frac{n}{2.5}\rceil-2
. Further, the number of vertices in
X
is at most
\lfloor{\frac{1.5n}{2.5}}\rfloor-1
. For
n\geq 12
,
\lfloor{\frac{1.5n}{2.5}}\rfloor-1<2\times(\lceil\frac{n}{2.5}\rceil-2)
. In other words the minimum degree in
G^{\prime\prime}[X]
is more than half the number of vertices in
X
. Consequently, by Fact
2
,
G^{\prime\prime}[X]
is of diameter at most 2. Similarly,
G^{\prime\prime}[V\setminus X]
is of diameter at most 2. Further, since
T
is incident on
\{X,V\setminus X\}
, there is an edge with one end point in
X
and the other in
V\setminus X
. Therefore
G^{\prime\prime}
is connected.
∎

The next lemma plays a critical role in the polynomial time computability of
Con-
f
-F
. It implies that if Tutte’s
f
-factor algorithm returns a solution with 2 components and if the graph has a connected
f
-factor, then there is one of diameter at least 3.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be an
f
-factor of
G
with two components
\{X,V\setminus X\}
. If
G
has a connected
f
-factor, then there exists a minimal alternating-circuit
T
incident on
\{X,V\setminus X\}
and is a switch on
G^{\prime}
. Further, Switching(
G^{\prime}
,
T
) is an
f
-factor of diameter at least 3.

Proof.

We prove by contradiction on the diameter of the
f
-factor computed by the switching operation. Let
H
be a connected
f
-factor of
G
. Color the edges in
G^{\prime}
with color red and those in
H
with color blue. By Fact
3
, each component in
E(G^{\prime})\bigtriangleup E(H)

is an alternating-circuit. Since
H
is connected, there exists a minimal alternating-circuit
T
in
E(G^{\prime})\bigtriangleup E(H)
, which is a switch on
G^{\prime}
and is incident on
\{X,V\setminus X\}
. From Lemma
3
it follows that Switching(
G^{\prime}
,
T
), denoted by
G^{\prime\prime}
, is a connected
f
-factor. Our proof analyzes two cases on the structure of such a
T
:

Case 1: There exists a
T
with exactly four edges:
In this case we prove that
G^{\prime\prime}
is of diameter at least 3. Let
u\in X
and
v\in V\setminus X
be vertices not in
V(T)
. Clearly, these are at a distance of atleast 3 in
G^{\prime\prime}
.

Case 2: Every
T
is of length more than 4:
Let us assume that
G^{\prime\prime}
is of diameter at most 2. Consider an edge
(u_{1},u_{2})
in
E(G^{\prime\prime})
such that
u_{1}\in X
and
u_{2}\in V\setminus X
. Now, the number of vertices
in
N_{G^{\prime}}(\{u_{1},u_{2}\})
is at least
\lceil\frac{2n}{2.5}\rceil
, since
G^{\prime}
is an
f
-factor. Therefore the number of vertices in
V\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[\{u_{1},u_{2}\}]
is at most
\lfloor\frac{n}{5}\rfloor-2
. Since we have assumed that
G^{\prime\prime}
is of diameter at most 2, from Lemma
2
we know that one of the two sets from
\{X,V\setminus X\}
has the property that each vertex in the set is incident on an edge in
[X,V\setminus X]\cap E(T)
. Without loss of generality, let
X
be this set and let
E^{\prime}=[X,V\setminus X]\cap E(T)
. Therefore, each vertex in
N_{G^{\prime}}(u_{1})
is incident on an edge in
[X,V\setminus X]\cap E(T)
. Further, each edge incident on a vertex in
N_{G^{\prime}}(u_{1})
is not incident on a vertex in
N_{G^{\prime}}(u_{2})
- the reason for this is that the existence of such an edge will result in an alternating circuit consisting of 4 edges, and we are in the case where such an alternating circuit does not exist. Therefore, the number of blue edges in
E^{\prime}
incident on vertices in
(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}(u_{2})
is at least
\lceil{\frac{n}{2.5}}\rceil
. Since
T
is a minimal alternating circuit,
u_{2}
has at most one more blue edge incident on it other than
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
. Therefore, it follows that the number of edges of
E^{\prime}
incident on vertices in
(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]
is at least
\lceil{\frac{n}{2.5}}\rceil-1
. On the other hand, since
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are in different components in
G^{\prime}
,
|(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]|\leq|V\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[\{u_{1},u_{2}\}]|
. Since
|V\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[\{u_{1},u_{2}\}]|\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{5}\rfloor-2
, it follows that
|(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]|\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{5}\rfloor-2
.
Since
T
is a minimal alternating-circuit, by Lemma
1
the blue degree at each vertex is at most 2.
Therefore, the number of edges in
E^{\prime}
incident on the vertex set
(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]
is at most twice the size of the set. Thus the number of edges in
E^{\prime}
incident on
(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]
is at most
\lfloor\frac{n}{2.5}\rfloor-4
.
We have derived a contradictory set of inequalities involving the number of edges in
E^{\prime}
incident on
(V\setminus X)\setminus N_{G^{\prime}}[u_{2}]
.
Therefore our assumption on the diameter of
G^{\prime\prime}
being at most 2 is wrong. Hence the lemma.
∎

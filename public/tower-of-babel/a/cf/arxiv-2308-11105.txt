Abelian varieties with Real multiplication : classification and isogeny classes over finite fields
Tejasi Bhatnagar, Yu Fu

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide a classification of certain points on Hilbert modular varieties over finite fields under a mild assumption on Newton polygon. Furthermore, we use this characterization to prove estimates for the size of isogeny classes.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
will denote the finite field with
q
elements where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
. We denote by
k
the algebraic closure of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. In
[
4
]
, Deligne provides a classification of ordinary abelian varieties over finite fields in terms of certain
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules along with an action of a “Frobenius map”. A crucial step in this classification requires the existence of the “Serre-Tate canonical lift” of an ordinary abelian variety to characteristic zero. Using the “canonical lift” of an ordinary abelian variety, Deligne provides a characterisation of such abelian varieties by associating to them the integral homology of their lift. A similar classification for simple almost ordinary abelian varieties over finite fields is proved in
[
10
]
. In order to extend Deligne’s work, Oswal and Shankar construct canonical lift(s) of almost ordinary abelian varieties using Grothendieck-Messing theory. Moreover, the lifts are characterized by the property that
all
of the endomorphisms lift to characteristic zero. Using these canonical lift(s), they classify simple almost ordinary abelian varieties, and as an application, they give a lower bound of the size of almost-ordinary isogeny classes. This paper aims to generalise their work to certain abelian varieties, (not necessarily simple) with
real multiplication
over finite fields with some assumptions on
p
, as described below. (See Theorem
1.4
)

1.0.1.
The Hilbert moduli space

Let
L
be a totally real number field of degree
g
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
be its ring of integers. We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}
the coarse Hilbert moduli space over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
associated to
L
. This moduli space parametrises abelian varieties with real multiplication with additional data. More precisely, we describe the moduli problem as follows.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
L
. Define
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}
to be the functor such that for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-scheme
S
,
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}(S)
is the set of isomorphism classes of triples,
(A,\iota,\lambda)
where:

(1)

A\to S
is a
g
-dimensional abelian scheme over
S
;

(2)

\iota:{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\hookrightarrow\End A
is an embedding into the endomorphism ring;

(3)

\lambda:\mathfrak{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L},S}^{\operatorname{Sym}}(A,A^{\vee})
is an isomorphism of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
modules. Here
\lambda
identifies the set of polarizations with the set of totally positive element
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{a}
. For every
a\in\mathfrak{a}^{+}
,
\lambda(a)
is an
\mathcal{O}_{L}
-linear polarization of
A
, and the homomorphism
A\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}\mathfrak{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\smash{\longrightarrow}\rule{0.0pt}{1.72218pt}}}A^{\vee}
induced by
\lambda
is an isomorphism of abelian schemes.

See
[
6
, Chapter 3.6.1]
for a complete description of the moduli problem. For the purpose of this paper, we will work with the coarse moduli space and classify
\mathfrak{a}
-polarised abelian varieties with RM, for a fixed fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
L.
In particular, when the polarisations are principal, the corresponding fractional ideal class is the inverse different of
L
, denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{L/{\mathbb{Q}}}^{-1}
. We restrict to this case while counting the size of isogeny classes.

Definition 1.2
.

Two abelian varieties
(A,\lambda_{A},\iota_{A})
and
(B,\lambda_{B},\iota_{B})
in
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
are said to be

if there is an isogeny
\varphi:A\rightarrow B
over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
that is compatible with the action of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
, that is
\varphi\circ\iota_{A}=\iota_{B}\circ\varphi
. Moreover, the isogeny preserves polarization up to scaling by
L^{\times}
.

Remark 1.1
.

From this section onwards we fix a totally real field
L
of degree
g
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
L
. When we say that an abelian variety
A
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
has RM, we mean that
A
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is
\mathfrak{a}
-polarised and has RM by
\mathcal{O}_{L}.
That is,
A
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-point of
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}.

Let

be a geometrically simple

-point of
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}
. We assume that the
p
-rank

Let

(1)

1.1.
Strategy to prove the classification theorem.

We first prove that
A
lifts to characteristic zero along with all its endomorphism algebra. More precisely, when
A
is totally ramified, it has exactly
2^{a}
lifts to characteristic zero. When
A
is inert, then it has one canonical lift, and finally, in the case when
A
is ramified, its number of lifts depend on the number of ramified quadratic extensions in the direct sum
K_{\sups}
. Fix a lift
\widetilde{A}
of
A
, we associate it with the integral homology
H_{1}(\widetilde{A}\otimes_{i}{\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{Z}})
. The Frobenius map on the homology lattice comes from the lift of the Frobenius of
A.
This gives us a functor from the category of RM-abelian varieties to the category of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules with a “Frobenius” map.
When all is said and done, however, there is no unique choice of a functor between the categories
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and
\mathcal{L}_{h}
. As in
[
10
]
, this ambiguity comes from possible CM-types on
K.
Suppose
\sigma_{1},\sigma^{\prime}_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma^{\prime}_{2},\dots\sigma_{a},\sigma^{\prime}_{a}
are the embeddings corresponding to the slope
1/2
part of
a,
then to give a possible CM type on
K
, we could choose either one of
\sigma_{i}
or
\sigma_{i}^{\prime}
for
1\leq i\leq a
together with the embeddings corresponding to the slope
0
part.
In each of the cases, this relates to the functoriality of the association between the two categories. For example, in the totally ramified case, we get a functorial map between
\mathcal{C}_{h}
and

\mathcal{L}_{h}
once we choose a particular lift of
A
out of
2^{a}
possibilities. On the other hand, in the inert case, even though we have one canonical lift of
A,
the association of
A
with the integral homology of its lift is not functorial. Once we restrict to one of
2^{a}
subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{h}
described in section
4
, we get a functorial association.

1.2.
Application to the size of isogeny classes.

The classification point of view of abelian varieties over finite fields has been utilised in many directions, many computational in nature. For example, Howe in
[
7
]
studies polarisation building on Deligne’s work to show that there exists a principally polarised abelian variety in certain isogeny classes. In
[
12
]
Shankar and Tsimerman
formulate and give evidence of certain conjectures regarding intersections of irreducible varieties and isogeny classes in
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, the moduli space of principally polarised abelian varieties. One of the applications of classification results to answer such questions is to estimate size of isogeny classes.

In this section, using the characterization in Theorem
1.4
, we estimate the size of isogeny classes of a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
point,
A\in\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q})
of the Hilbert modular variety. We define
I(A,q^{n})
to be the set of abelian varieties in
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})
such that there exists an isogeny to
A
over
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
. Denote by
N(A,q^{n})
the size of the set
I(A,q^{n})
. We prove the following estimate for
N(A,q^{n})
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For all but finitely many
n
, we have

N(A,q^{n})=q^{\frac{n}{2}(g-a+o(1))}
.

We note that we get the leading term for all but finitely many
n
, while the error is an asymptotic as
n
goes to infinity.

In
[
1
]
, Achter and Cunningham prove a precise formula for the size of isogeny classes of ordinary abelian varieties with RM. Their paper uses a different technique of orbital integrals. The estimate in
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
agrees with our Theorem
1.5
, when the
p
-rank of the abelian variety is
g
, that is, for ordinary abelian varieties with RM.

1.3.
Strategy to prove the bounds for the size of the isogeny classes.

In order to find the lower bound for
N(A,q^{n})
, we count the number of points in
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})
with endomorphism by the smallest order
R_{n}\subset\mathcal{O}_{K}
that occurs as an endomorphism ring in the isogeny class of
A.
We characterize this ring in proposition
3.2
. The classification theorem gives us a bijection between
\mathcal{C}\ell(R_{n})
and the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
points of
\mathcal{H}_{L,\mathfrak{a}}
with endomorphism ring
R_{n}
. Finding a lower bound therefore reduces to estimating the size of the class group. Furthermore, in section
5.2
, we prove that this bound is sharp. We compute the upper bound by estimating size of the class group of
all
over orders of
R_{n}.
We note that
R_{n}
and all its over orders are Gorenstein, a property that gives a unique characterization of its over-orders in terms of the subgroups of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R_{n}
, a cyclic group in our case (see Proposition
5.10
).

1.4.
Organization of the paper

Our first step is to construct canonical lift(s) of abelian varieties with RM in section
2
. We then give the main theorem of classification in section
3
, and finally, section
4
provides estimates for the size of isogeny classes.

3.
Canonical lift

3.1.
The simple case.

For this section, we fix a geometrically simple
g
-dimensional abelian variety
A
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that has RM by
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}.
Let
g-a
be the
p
-rank of
A
where
1\leq a\leq g-1.
We assume that
p
is totally split in
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}.
Let
A[p^{\infty}]
be the

-divisible group of

Let
\End^{0}(A)
be the endomorphism algebra of

defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let

be the field generated by the relative Frobenius of

over

, the ring of Witt vectors over

Our first step is to prove that the endomorphism algebra of

Lemma 3.1
.

[
2

Let

Corresponding to each prime

Let

and

be the endomorphism rings of

We know by Tate’s theorem that

The endomorphism ring

Proof.

We show that

-divisible group and hence of its Dieuodonné module over

into rank two modules. By Lemma

, we get a decomposition into ordinary and supersingular factors
\mathbb{D}_{\ord}\oplus\mathbb{D}_{\sups}
where

is a product of

supersingular rank. Since

is completely split in

respects the direct sum. Let

denote the characteristic polynomial of

over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Since

. We have a decomposition of

corresponding to the decomposition of the Dieudonné module. We note that

is product of factors

Suppose that

Finally, in order to show that

Suppose

Let

denote the rings of integers of a

The following proposition in

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\mathscr{G}}_{\sups}
denote a one dimensional

-divisible group over

Suppose

is an integrally closed rank two

(1)

If
{\mathcal{O}}
is unramified, there exists a unique lift
{\mathscr{G}}_{\sups}
to
W
such that the action of
{\mathcal{O}}
lifts.

(2)

If
{\mathcal{O}}
is ramified, then there exists two lifts of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\sups}
to
W^{\prime}
such that the action of
{\mathcal{O}}
lifts.

The proof of this proposition is given in

10
]
. Oswal and Shankar construct lifts of

to

by lifting its

-divisible group. More precisely, Grothendieck-Messing theory gives a bijection between the lifts of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\sups}
to

with rank-one filtrations of the Dieudonné module

that reduce to the kernel of the Frobenius on
{\mathbb{D}}
modulo

Moreover, in order to lift the endomorphisms, we need the filtration to be invariant under the action of the endomorphism algebra
{\mathcal{O}}
of
{\mathscr{G}}_{\sups}
. We can explicitly compute such filtrations depending on whether

to

Therefore, in the geometrically simple case, Lemma

Theorem 3.4
.

We assume the notation described at the beginning of this section. The super-singular part

of the

-divisible group admits at-most

lifts to

Moreover, the lifts are characterized by the property that all of the endomorphisms of

lift as well.

3.2.
The non-simple case

In this section, we extend the lifting theorem to the non-simple case. As in the simple case, it is enough to establish the result for the supersingular part of the
p
-divisible group.

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose
A
is a non-simple abelian variety of dimension
2g
with RM by
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then
A
is isogenous to
B^{m}
, an
m
-fold product of a simple RM abelian variety
B
defined over a possible field extension of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proof.

We show that if
A=B_{1}\times B_{2}
, a product of two geometrically simple RM abelian varieties then
B_{1}
is isogenous to
B_{2}
. Let
g_{1}
be the dimension of
B_{1}
and
g_{2}
be the dimension of
B_{2}
. Without any loss of generality, we assume
g_{1}\geq g_{2}.
Then by the definition of RM abelian varieties,
B_{1}
has RM by a totally real field, say
L_{1}
, of degree
g_{1}
, while
B_{2}
has RM by a totally real field
L_{2}
of degree
g_{2}.
Now, if
B_{1}
were not isogenous to
B_{2}
, then
\End^{0}A=\End^{0}(B_{1}\times B_{2})=\End^{0}(B_{1})\times\End^{0}(B_{2})
. We note that
L
is a degree
g
field in
\End^{0}A
. However,
\End^{0}(B_{1})\times\End^{0}(B_{2})
, a direct sum of CM fields can at most have dimension
g_{1}
totally real field embedded in it, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
A
be a non-simple abelian variety with RM by
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}.
Then the supersingular part of its
p
-divisible group
A[p^{\infty}]_{\sups}
lifts to characteristic zero with all of its endomorphisms.

Proof.

We work over the algebraic closure
k
. By the previous lemma, we know that
A
is isogenous to
B^{m}
for some simple abelian variety
B
that is RM by
\mathcal{O}_{L^{\prime}}
over
k
. Let
\varphi:B^{m}\rightarrow A
be an isogeny and let

4.
Classification

Analogous to the work of Deligne for ordinary abelian varieties in

(4)

Since all the endomorphisms of

(5)

We note that

Furthermore by the argument in

(6)

The map

We note that for an abelian variety

Proposition 4.3
.

Every Deligne module

with RM by

in

Proof.

We first prove the result for a simple Deligne module, and the non-simple case follows by taking the product of the corresponding simple abelian varieties. So assume

Since

satisfies a Weil-

\pi_{A}
is the same as that of

We note that up to isogeny, we can always assume that

has endomorphism by the maximal order in

; hence, it has RM by
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
. Therefore,

We wish to find an abelian variety

,that is

-isogenous to

such that

Let

be the subgroup corresponding to

and

If

is of order prime-to-

\widetilde{H}
is a lift of

H
modulo

In the case when

4.1.

Let

be its totally real field. Let

inside the integral closure of

Proof.

We know that a ring

is a free
D

\{1,\alpha\}
such that

. Let

be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
such that

then the complementary module

is invertible. Therefore,

implies that

is a Gorenstein ring.
∎

Corollary 5.2

R_{n}

Proof.

This comes directly from Lemma

One can also get proof by checking that

Let
I\subset K
be an

with

. We say two fractional ideals,

and

are equivalent if and only if there exists some

such that

We define the equivalence classes of all such fractional ideals of

5.1.
The lower bound

We now have a bijection between
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
-isomorphism classes of abelian varieties in
\mathcal{C}_{h}
having endomorphism ring exactly
R_{n}
and the ideal class group
\mathcal{C}\ell(R_{n})
. The class group
\mathcal{C}\ell(R_{n})
is well approximated by the square root of its discriminant as
n
approaches infinity. Therefore, it suffices to compute the (square root) of the discriminant of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]
and the index of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]
inside
R_{n}.

The discriminant of
R_{n}
is then approximated by dividing the two estimates. We recall that
\pi_{A}
is the Weil
q
-integer of
A
. Let
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{g},\overline{\alpha_{1}},\cdots,\overline{\alpha_{g}}
be the image of
\pi_{A}
under the
2g
complex embeddings of
L
. Let
\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{g}
denote the arguments of
\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{g}
respectively. Note that for each
i
,
\overline{\alpha_{i}}=q/\alpha_{i}
is the complex conjugate of
\alpha_{i}
. We have the following lemma which points out the fact that for all
1\leq i\leq g
and for all but finitely many
n
,
n\theta_{i}
(hence
\sin n\theta_{i}
) cannot be too small.

Lemma 5.3
.

For every
\epsilon>0,
and
1\leq i\leq g
, let
\omega_{n,i}\in[0,2\pi)
be the unique real number such that
n\theta_{i}\equiv\omega_{n,i}\pmod{2\pi}
. We have
|\omega_{n,i}|>\frac{1}{q^{n\epsilon}}
for all but finitely many
n
.

Proof.

For each
1\leq i\leq g
, the argument
\theta_{i}
can be viewed as the logarithm of
\beta_{i}:=\alpha_{i}/\overline{\alpha_{i}}
, which is same as
\log(\alpha_{i})-\log(\overline{\alpha}_{i})
.
We first prove that
\log(\alpha_{i})
and
\log(\overline{\alpha}_{i})
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Suppose we have
a,b\neq 0
such that

a\log(\alpha_{i})+b\log(\overline{\alpha}_{i})=0.

This is,

\alpha_{i}^{a}=\overline{\alpha}_{i}^{-b}.

Comparing the
q
-power we see that
a=-b
. This only happens when
b\theta_{i}=k\pi
for some integer
k
. Indeed,
\theta_{i}/\pi\in{\mathbb{Q}}
if and only if
\beta_{i}=\alpha_{i}/\overline{\alpha_{i}}
is a root of unity for each
i
if and only if the Frobenius angle rank of
A
equals zero. This happens and only if
A
is supersingular. Since we have assumed that
A
is geometrically simple, this is true only when
g=1
and
A
is a supersingular elliptic curve. See section 3 of
[
5
]
for details.

Using Baker’s theorem for the absolute value of linear combinations of logarithms of algebraic numbers, we see that there exists some absolute constant
c
such that for every
\epsilon>0
,

|\omega_{n,i}|=|n\log(\alpha_{i})-n(\log q/\alpha_{i})|>\frac{1}{n^{c}}>\frac{1}{q^{n\epsilon}}

for all but finitely many
n
.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

We have
\operatorname{disc}({\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}])=q^{n(g+o(1))}
as
n\to\infty.

Proof.

Consider the tower of extensions of rings
{\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]
. We know that

\operatorname{disc}({\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}])=\operatorname{disc}({\mathcal{O}}_{L})^{2}N_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}/{\mathbb{Z}}}(\operatorname{disc}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}}({\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]))

See, for example,
[
11
, Prop. III.8]
.
Consider a basis
\{1,\pi_{A}^{n}\}
of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
.
We compute,

\displaystyle N_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}/{\mathbb{Z}}}(\operatorname{Disc}_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}}({\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\pi_{A}^{n}]))

\displaystyle=N_{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}/{\mathbb{Z}}}((\pi_{A}^{n}-q^{n}/\pi_{A}^{n})^{2})

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{g}(\alpha_{j}^{n}-q^{n}/\alpha_{j}^{n})^{2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{g}4q^{n}(i\sin n\theta_{j})^{2}

This along with the Lemma
5.3
, completes the proof.

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For all but finitely many
n
, we have
\mathcal{C}\ell(R_{n})=q^{\frac{n}{2}(g-a+o(1))}
.

Proof.

In order to estimate the index of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\alpha^{n}]
inside
R_{n}
, we compute the corresponding index locally at
p
. Without loss of any generality, we do the computation for
n=1
. Let
p(x)
denote the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
which we know is degree
2.

Let
\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{g}
denote the restrictions of
g
embeddings
L\hookrightarrow\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{p}
to
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}.
Then we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[\alpha^{n}]\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[x]}{h(x)}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]}{\sigma_{1}(p(x))}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}[x]}{\sigma_{g}(p(x))}

since
p
splits completely in
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
. Let
f(x)
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha
over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
. We know that
f(x)=\sigma_{1}(p(x))\cdots\sigma_{g}(p(x))
gives the decomposition of
p(x)
over
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}.
Without loss of any generality, we take
\sigma_{1}(p(x)),\cdots,\sigma_{a}(p(x))
to be the
a
supersingular factors of
p(x)
. Let
\delta_{1,1},\delta_{1,2},\cdots,\delta_{a,1},\delta_{a,2}
to be the corresponding roots of the polynomials. Let
g_{i}(x)
denote the polynomial with roots
\delta_{i,1}/q^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\delta_{i,2}/q^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The index of
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}[\alpha]\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
inside
R_{n}\otimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
is approximated by dividing the product of the discriminants of
\sigma_{i}(p(x)
and
g_{i}(x)

which gives the following expression.

\prod_{i=1}^{a}\frac{{(\delta_{i,1}-\delta_{i,2})}}{(\delta_{i,1}/q^{\frac{1}{2}}-\delta_{i,2}/q^{\frac{1}{2}})}=q^{\frac{a}{2}}

The lemma now follows by putting together the above calculation with Lemma
5.4
.
∎

5.2.
The upper bound

In this section, we show that the lower bound computed in the above section is indeed sharp. We will do this by approximating the class groups of all over orders of
R_{n}
in order to count the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
-isomorphism classes of abelian varieties in
\mathcal{C}_{h}
with endomorphism ring
{\mathcal{O}}
where
{\mathcal{O}}
is any arbitrary over-order of
R_{n}
. We recall some standard notation first.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
R
be an order in a number field
K
. Let
\mathcal{I}(R)
be the set of all fractional ideals of
R
The ideal class monoid of
R
, denoted by ICM(R) is defined to be

\operatorname{ICM}(R)=\mathcal{I}(R)/\simeq

where
I\simeq J
if and only if there exists an
\alpha\in K^{*}
such that
I=\alpha J
.

Definition 5.7
.

For all
invertible
factional ideals, we analogously define the Picard group of
R
to be

\mathcal{C}\ell(R)=\{\text{invertible fractional ideal }I\in\mathcal{I}(R)\}/\mathcal{P}(R)

where
\mathcal{P}(R)
is the group of of principal fractional
R
-ideals.

If two fractional
R
-ideals are isomorphic, then they have the same multiplicator ring. It follows that

\bigsqcup_{R\subset{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\mathcal{C}\ell(\mathcal{O})\subseteq\operatorname{ICM}(R)

where the disjoint union is taken over all over-orders
{\mathcal{O}}
of
R
.

Definition 5.8
.

An order
R
is called a
Bass order
if every over-order of
R
is Gorenstein, or equivalently, if
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R
is cyclic, which is to say
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}=R+xR
for some
x\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
.

We have the following lemma, which gives another useful characterization for Bass orders.

Lemma 5.9
.

The following statements are equivalent:

a.

R
is a Bass order,

b.

\bigsqcup_{R\subset{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\mathcal{C}\ell(\mathcal{O})=\operatorname{ICM}(R).

For proof of this proposition, see
[
9
, Proposition 3.7]
. Finally, we have the following proposition that gives us an upper bound on the size of the isogeny classes.

Proposition 5.10
.

For any positive integer
n
, we have

N(A,q^{n})\leq(q^{\frac{n}{2}})^{(g-a+o(1))}

Proof.

From Lemma
5.1
we deduce that every order between
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}[\alpha^{n}]
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
is Gorenstein, hence every element of
\operatorname{ICM}(R_{n})
lies in
\mathcal{C}\ell({\mathcal{O}})
for some unique order
{\mathcal{O}}
over
R_{n}
. Therefore by Lemma
5.9
we have

\bigsqcup_{R_{n}\subset{\mathcal{O}}\subset{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}\mathcal{C}\ell(\mathcal{O})=\operatorname{ICM}(R_{n}).

Let
h({\mathcal{O}})
be the class number of
{\mathcal{O}}
. We note that the class group of the smallest order
R_{n}
has the largest size. By the lattice isomorphism theorem, subgroups of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
containing
R_{n}
are in bijection with subgroups of
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R_{n}
. Since
R_{n}
is Bass, we have that
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R_{n}
is cyclic. Therefore its subgroups are indexed by
d\mid i_{n}
where
i_{n}=|{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R_{n}|.
Putting everything together, we see that

N(A,q^{n})=\sum_{d\mid i_{n}}h({\mathcal{O}}_{d})\leq i_{n}^{o(1)}h(R_{n})=(q^{\frac{n}{2}})^{(g-a+o(1))}

where
{\mathcal{O}}_{d}
is the order corresponding to the subgroup of order
d
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{K}/R_{n}
.
∎

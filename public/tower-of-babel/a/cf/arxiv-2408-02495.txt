Preservation of Topological Properties by Strongly Proper Forcings
Thomas Gilton, Jared Holshouser

Abstract
. In this paper we show that forcings which are strongly proper for stationarily many countable elementary submodels preserve each of the following properties of topological spaces: countably tight; Lindelöf; Rothberger; Menger; and a strategic version of Rothberger. This extends results from Dow’s papers [ 5 ] and [ 6 ] , as well as results of Iwasa ( [ 14 ] ) and Kada ( [ 16 ] ).

1.
Introduction

Our motivation for this paper is to understand classes of forcings that preserve topological properties of interest, such as countable tightness and the Lindelöf property. We first make some remarks about narrower classes of posets, and then we take a step back to motivate the investigation of broader classes of posets. It is known that, in general, countably closed posets needn’t preserve these properties (
[
32
]
). However, Dow has shown that posets of the form
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})
is the poset to add
\omega_{1}
-many Cohen reals and
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
is a name for a countably closed poset, do in fact preserve countable tightness (Lemma 5.6 of
[
5
]
) and do preserve the Lindelöf property (Lemma 3.3 of
[
6
]
).

Taking a step back, one particularly useful class of posets is the class of Proper posets, invented by Shelah (
[
33
]
,
[
34
]
). The class of proper forcings subsumes both the class of countably closed and the class of ccc posets. Moreover, this class is iterable with countable supports, as Shelah proved, thereby generalizing the celebrated result of Solovay and Tennenbaum (
[
35
]
). Since countably closed forcings are proper, and since countably closed forcings do not in general preserve (for example) countable tightness and the Lindelöf property, we can’t hope to prove that proper forcings in general preserve them. We remark that there are known results about the preservation of strengthenings of the Lindelöf property (
[
17
]
) by posets satisfying natural game-theoretic properties.

However, posets of the form
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
(where
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
is forced to be countably closed), which do preserve these properties, are not just proper but satisfy the more stringent condition of being strongly proper (see Lemma 1.12 of
[
13
]
for a proof). Mitchell first explicitly isolated the idea of a strongly proper forcing (
[
21
]
). This class contains Cohen forcing to add any number of subsets of
\omega
; however no countably closed forcing (nor countably distributive forcing, for that matter) is strongly proper, since every (non-trivial) strongly proper forcing adds a Cohen real.

In light of these observations, we study preservation properties of topological spaces under strongly proper forcings, showing that this class is powerful enough to preserve countable tightness, the Lindelöf property, and many other covering properties. These generalize results from Dow, mentioned above, as well as results from Iwasa (
[
14
]
) and Kada (
[
16
]
), among others. We also note that Marun proved in his thesis, earlier and independently of us, that forcings which are strongly proper for stationarily many models preserve the Lindelöf property (
[
20
]
).

We note, though, that we haven’t exactly “carved at the joints” here: there are non-strongly proper posets which do preserve many of these properties. For example, the forcing to add a random real is not strongly proper: this forcing does not add any unbounded reals, and hence does not add any Cohen real (as Cohen reals are unbounded). Nevertheless, the random real forcing does preserve many of the properties that we study here (see
[
16
]
).

A remark on notation: ours is standard. One item that requires remark concerns names for ground model objects. When
x
is a specific set in the ground model
V
and when we are referring to
x
in statements in the forcing language, we will either leave the symbol “
x
” as is, or we will put a stylish hat on it, for example “
\check{x}
.” The use of the hat is to emphasize that this is a canonical name for the set
x
in contexts in which some disambiguation could be helpful.

Finally, a remark on exposition: given that the material in this paper concerns set theory and topology, we have added expository background material about both set-theoretic as well as topological topics. The hope is that a reader who is more rooted in one of those fields, but who is interested in both, can profit from the material in the paper without having to consult too many other sources for background.

2.
Topological Definitions

In this section, we present the relevant topological definitions, particularly for the reader who is more familiar with the set-theoretic side of things.

Some of the main themes of topology are compactness (as witnessed by open covers); convergence and clustering; and density. The study of selection principles takes these topics and expands on them with a particular focus on diagonalization processes. This section of preliminary definitions is broken up by these three themes and builds from standard definitions up to the selection principle versions.

First we discuss the variations on compactness, known as covering properties.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space.

•

X
is
compact
if for every open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
, there is a finite subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
so that
X=\bigcup\mathcal{F}
.

•

X
is
Lindelöf
if for every open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
, there is a countable subset
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
so that
X=\bigcup\mathcal{V}
.

•

X
is
Rothberger
if for every sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of open covers of
X
, there are open sets
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
so that
X=\bigcup_{n}U_{n}
.

•

X
is
Menger
if for every sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of open covers of
X
, there are finite subsets
\mathcal{F}_{n}\subset\mathcal{U}_{n}
so that
X=\bigcup_{n}\bigcup\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

Conveniently, the notion of Rothberger is slightly stronger than it looks:

Lemma 2.2
.

A space
(X,\tau)
is Rothberger if and only if for every sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of open covers of
X
, there are open sets
U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
so that
for every
x\in X
, there are infinitely many
n\in\omega
so that
x\in U_{n}
.

Likewise, the Menger property is stronger than it initially appears:

Lemma 2.3
.

A space
(X,\tau)
is Menger if and only if for every sequence
\langle\mathcal{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle
of open covers of
X
, there exists a sequence
\left<\cal{F}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
so that

•

each
\cal{F}_{n}
is a finite subset of
\cal{U}_{n}
; and

•

for every

, there are infinitely many
n\in\omega
so that for some

,

.

There are variations of these covering properties that naturally arise in

-theory, the study of the space of continuous real-valued functions on
X

For the sake of the reader who may not be familar with these definitions, we now take a moment to show how to separate many of them. Note first that Rothberger implies Menger which in turn implies Lindelöf and also that compact implies Lindelöf. Similarly,

•

The Sorgenfrey line is Lindelöf, but it is not Menger
[
31
]
. Neither is it
\omega
-Lindelöf.

•

The real line with the standard topology is Menger, and in fact,
\omega
-Menger. But is not Rothberger (and hence not

-Rothberger). The fact that

is Menger comes from writing

for each

•

Any countable space is Rothberger. Uncountable examples include the Fortissimo space of an uncountable discrete space: Let

[

]
.

•

The irrational numbers are
\omega
-Lindelöf, but they are not Menger (and perforce not

-Menger). For a reference, see the entry at the Pi-Base [
S28

•

It is hard to find examples of spaces that are Menger but not

-Menger. In fact, in

, it is shown that it is consistent with ZFC that for all metrizable spaces, the Menger and

-Menger properties are equivalent.

•

Under CH, there are Luzin subsets of the real line that are Rothberger but not

-Rothberger. On the other hand, in the Laver model, every Rothberger subset of the real line is countable, and thus

-Rothberger

Finally, we have a few remarks about density.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space.

•

X
is
separable
if there is a countable
D\subseteq X
so that
X=\mbox{cl}(D)
.

•

X
is

if for every sequence
\langle D_{n}:n\in\omega\rangle

so that
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}
is dense.

We take another moment here to discuss how these properties relate to each other. The first chain of implications is that strong countable fan tightness implies countable fan tightness which in turn implies countable tightness. Note also that selectively separable implies separable. It is also true that the strong Fréchet-Urysohn property implies the Fréchet-Urysohn property which implies countable tightness. We can separate the properties out with some counter examples.

•

The Arens-Fort space is countably tight but not Fréchet-Urysohn. This space is defined to be
\omega^{2}
where a set is open if it either does not contain
(0,0)
or it contains all but a finite number of points in all but a finite number of columns. It is countably tight because it is countable, but it fails to be Fréchet-Urysohn because no sequence converges to
(0,0)
.

•

The space
C_{p}(\mathbb{R})
of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with the topology of point-wise convergence, is countably tight at all points (because the real line is
\omega
-Lindelöf), but it does not have strong countable fan tightness at any point (because the real line is not
\omega
-Rothberger, see
[
4
]
).

•

If
X
is the irrational numbers, then
C_{p}(X)
is separable, but as the irrationals are not
\omega
-Menger,

is not selectively separable (see Theorem 35 of
[

The unified way in which these definitions developed is summarized by the concept of a selection principle.

Definition 2.8
.

Let

so that

. This is called a

.

A Brief Overview of Strongly Proper Forcings

In this section, we will present the basics of strongly proper forcings. The reader who is already familiar with this material need only consult Definition
3.4
for some notation that we will use throughout the paper.

For the next three definitions, fix a large enough regular cardinal
\theta\geq\omega_{2}
, a countable elementary submodel
M\prec H(\theta)
, and a poset
\mathbb{P}\in M
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
q\in\mathbb{P}
be a condition. A condition
\bar{q}\in M\cap\mathbb{P}
is a
residue
of
q
to
M
if for all
s\leq\bar{q}
with
s\in M
,
s
is compatible with
q
.

In other words, every extension of
\bar{q}
within
M
is compatible with
q
.

Definition 3.2
.

A condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
is an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-
strongly generic condition
if every
q\leq p
has a residue
\bar{q}
to
M
.

The next layer in the definitions specifies when the poset
\mathbb{P}
is strongly generic for the model
M
; the definition roughly asserts that there are plenty of
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic conditions.

Definition 3.3
.

\mathbb{P}
is
strongly proper
for
M
if every
s\in M\cap\mathbb{P}
can be extended to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition.

It will be helpful to have a function which, for each
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition
q
, chooses a residue of
q
to
M
. The next definition addresses this.

Definition 3.4
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for a countable
M\prec H(\theta)
. An
(M,\mathbb{P})
-
residue function
is a function
\operatorname{res}_{M}
whose domain is the set of
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic conditions and which satisfies that for any such condition
p
,
\operatorname{res}_{M}(p)
is a residue of
p
to
M
.

Finally, we specify when
\mathbb{P}
is strongly generic for a set of models:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
\cal{S}\subseteq[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
be a set of countable elementary submodels of
H(\theta)
.
\mathbb{P}
is
strongly generic
for
\cal{S}
if for all
M\in\cal{S}
,
\mathbb{P}
is strongly generic for
M
.

In practice, the set
\cal{S}
is large in the sense of being
at least
stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Rather than reviewing the concepts of generalized stationarity, we provide the following lemma giving the consequence of stationarity that we use in practice:

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
\cal{S}\subseteq[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
is a stationary set of countable elementary submodels of
H(\theta)
. Then for any
x\in[H(\theta)]^{\leq\aleph_{0}}
, there is an
M\in\cal{S}
so that
x\in M
.

Note in the conclusion of the previous lemma that since
x\in M
and
x
is countable, we in fact have
x\subset M
.

Remark 3.7
.

Throughout this paper we will be working with an arbitrary poset
\mathbb{P}
which is strongly proper for a stationary
\cal{S}\subseteq[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. This is weaker than the usual definition of strongly proper. The usual definition says that
\mathbb{P}
is a strongly proper poset if it is strongly proper for a
club
of countable elementary submodels of
H(\theta)
for all large enough regular
\theta
.

The next item shows that the value of
\theta
doesn’t play a significant role; the proof of the lemma relies on standard facts about generalized stationarity.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
\theta<\theta^{*}
are regular and
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
a poset. Then
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for a stationary
\cal{S}\subseteq[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
iff
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for a stationary
\cal{S}^{*}\subseteq[H(\theta^{*})]^{\aleph_{0}}
.

We now give one example of a strongly proper forcing, for the sake of exposition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal.
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\kappa)
is the poset to add
\kappa
-many Cohen reals. Conditions are finite partial functions
p:\kappa\times\omega\rightharpoonup 2
.
q
extends
p
, denoted

, if
p\subseteq q
.

Example 3.10
.

Let
\theta>\kappa
both be regular. Then
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\kappa)
is strongly proper for any countable
M\prec H(\theta)
with
\kappa\in M
.

Indeed, let
M\prec H(\theta)
be given so that
\kappa\in M
, from which we conclude that the poset
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\kappa)
is in

. We claim that every condition
p\in\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\kappa)
is a strongly
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic condition. Fix any condition

\bar{p}
be the restriction of

to
\operatorname{dom}(p)\cap M
. Then
\bar{p}
is a finite subset of

Strongly proper forcings form a much wider class than Cohen forcing to add subsets of

]

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
be an
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})

\mathbb{P}:=\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}

However, the class of strongly proper posets does

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose that
\theta

\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for a stationary

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be arbitrary. Let
M\in\cal{S}
so that

to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition

\dot{G}\cap(M\cap\mathbb{P})
is

-generic over
M\cap\mathbb{P}
. But

is a non-trivial, countably infinite poset. Hence
M\cap\mathbb{P}
contains a dense subset isomorphic to
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,1)
. From this it follows that a

-generic for
M\cap\mathbb{P}
adds a
V
-generic over
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,1)
.

4.
Preservation of Countably Tight spaces and of Lindelöf spaces

In this section, we introduce our first round of preservation theorems. We show that forcings which are strongly proper for stationarily-many models preserve the countable tightness of a space; we also show that such forcings preserve when a space is Lindelöf. In the next section, we will address the preservation of Menger and Rothberger spaces, and we will need to introduce some additional technology for those arguments. We begin by clarifying what it means for a forcing to preserve a topological property.

Fix a topological space
(X,\tau)
and a poset
\mathbb{P}
in the ground model
V
. In the extension of
V
by a
V
-generic
G
, we consider the set
X
with the topology
\tau^{G}
which has
\tau
as a basis. Intuitively speaking, the information from the generic filter allows us to combine old things (open sets in
\tau
) in new ways to create additional open sets.

We say that
\mathbb{P}
preserves some topological property
\Phi
of
(X,\tau)
if every condition in
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau^{\dot{G}})
has property
\Phi
.

Example 4.1
.

For a simple (counter)example, consider the unit interval
[0,1]
from the ground model, and let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset that adds a new subset of
\omega
, and hence a new real. Note that
\mathbb{P}
then adds a new real between 0 and 1. Then
[0,1]^{V}
is no longer compact in
V[G]
. Since
\mathbb{P}
adds a new real between 0 and 1, we can use rational open intervals from
V
to define, in
V[G]
, an open cover of
[0,1]^{V}
with no finite subcover.

Remark 4.2
.

When checking that a property
\Phi
of a space holds, we often need only check the property with respect to basic open sets. In the context of proving preservation theorems, this allows us to simplify the discussion by working with names for open sets in the ground model, since the ground model topology
\tau
is a basis for the topology on
X
in
V[G]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset,
(X,\tau)
a topological space and
\dot{\cal{U}}
a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open cover of
(X,\tau)
forced to be a subset of
\tau
. Fix
p\in\mathbb{P}
. We define the
trace of
\dot{\cal{U}}
below
p
, denoted
\cal{U}_{p}
, to be the following set:

\cal{U}_{p}:=\left\{W\in\tau:(\exists q\leq p)\;[q\Vdash\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}}]\right\}.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
\dot{\cal{U}}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open cover of
(X,\tau)
forced to be a subset of
\tau
. Let
p\in\mathbb{P}
be arbitrary. Then
\cal{U}_{p}
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
. Let
G
be an arbitrary
V
-generic filter over
\mathbb{P}
with
p\in G
. Then
\cal{U}:=\dot{\cal{U}}[G]
is an open cover of
(X,\tau^{G})
consisting of sets in
\tau
. Let
W\in\cal{U}
so that
x\in W
. Since
W\in\tau
is a set in the ground model, we may find a condition
q\leq p
with
q\in G
so that

q\Vdash\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}}.

Then
q
witnesses that
W\in\cal{U}_{p}
. Since
x\in W
, this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Suppose that
(X,\tau)
is a Lindelöf space. Let
\mathbb{P}
be a poset,
\theta
a large enough regular cardinal, and
M\prec H(\theta)
countable containing
\mathbb{P}
and
(X,\tau)
as elements. Let
\cal{\dot{\mathcal{U}}}
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open cover of
X
with
\cal{\dot{\mathcal{U}}}\in M
so that
\,\dot{\cal{U}}
is forced to be a subset of
\tau
.

Then for all
x\in X
and all
s\in M\cap\mathbb{P}
, there exist
t\leq_{M\cap\mathbb{P}}s
and
W\in M\cap\tau
so that

t\Vdash x\in\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}}.

Proof.

Fix
s\in M\cap\mathbb{P}
. Let
\cal{U}_{s}
be the trace of the name
\dot{\cal{U}}
below
s
, as defined in Definition
4.3
. By Lemma
4.4
,
\cal{U}_{s}
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
. Additionally,
\cal{U}_{s}
is a member of
M
since it is definable in
H(\theta)
by parameters in
M
. By applying the fact that
(X,\tau)
is Lindelöf and the elementarity of
M
, we may find a countable
\cal{B}_{s}\subseteq\cal{U}_{s}
so that

•

\cal{B}_{s}
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
and

•

\cal{B}_{s}\in M
.

Since
\cal{B}_{s}
is countable and a member of
M
, it is a subset of
M
. Since
\cal{B}_{s}
is an open cover, we may find
W\in\cal{B}_{s}
with
x\in W
. However,
W\in\cal{U}_{s}
and
W\in M
. Therefore
M
contains a witness that
W\in\cal{U}_{s}
; that is, there is a
t\leq_{M\cap\mathbb{P}}s
so that
t\Vdash W\in\dot{\cal{U}}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

For the reader who may not be familiar with strongly proper forcings, it is worth making a remark here about the structure of the following proofs. When showing that a certain property of
(X,\tau)
is preserved by
\mathbb{P}
, we will begin with an arbitrary condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
with the goal of extending
p
to a condition which forces the desired property (hence
\mathbb{P}
itself forces the preservation of the desired property). We will find the condition
q
by first placing
p
, as well as other parameters of interest, inside of some countable elementary submodel
M
. An
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition
q
extending
p
is then the desired condition. To see this, we proceed to argue that no extension, say
r
, of
q
can force a specific counterexample. This will involve taking a residue
\operatorname{res}_{M}(r)
of
r
to
M
and working with the elementarity of
M
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\theta
be a large enough regular cardinal,
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
a Lindelöf space, and
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
a poset. Suppose that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau)
is Lindelöf.

Proof.

Fix a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
and a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{\cal{U}}
for an open cover of
X
consisting of elements of
\tau
. We will find a condition
q\leq p
and a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}
so that

q\Vdash\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}\in\left[\dot{\cal{U}}\right]^{\aleph_{0}}\text{ and }\;\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}\text{ covers }X.

By the stationarity assumed in the statement of the theorem, we may find a countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\left\{(X,\tau),\mathbb{P},p\right\}\subset M
and so that every condition in
M\cap\mathbb{P}
has an extension to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition. In particular, we may find
q\leq p
so that
q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic. Let
\operatorname{res}_{M}
be a residue function for
M
(see Definition
3.4
).

Define
\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}
by taking an arbitrary
V
-generic
G
over
\mathbb{P}
and setting
\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}[G]=M\cap\dot{\cal{U}}[G]
. Note that
\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}
is forced to be countable since it is forced to be a subset of
M
, which itself is countable. We claim that

q\Vdash\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}\text{ covers }X.

To prove this claim, it suffices to show that no
r\leq q
can force a specific counterexample. In other words, it suffices to argue that for all
r\leq q
and all
x\in X
, there exist an open set
W\in M\cap\tau
and a condition
r^{*}\leq r
so that

r^{*}\Vdash x\in\check{W}\wedge\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}};

note that such an
r^{*}
then forces
\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}}_{0}
.
Thus fix such
r
and
x
. Since
q
is strongly
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic and
r\leq q
, we know that
\bar{r}:=\operatorname{res}_{M}(r)
is defined. By Lemma
4.5
, we may find a
t\leq_{M\cap\mathbb{P}}\bar{r}
and an open
W\in M\cap\tau
so that

t\Vdash x\in\check{W}\in\dot{\cal{U}}.

Since
t\in M
and
t
extends
\bar{r}
, we know that
t
and
r
are compatible. Let
r^{*}
be a condition extending both, completing the proof.
∎

We obtain the preservation of the
\omega
-Lindelöf property as a corollary of this theorem.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
\theta
be a large enough regular cardinal,
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
an
\omega
-Lindelöf space, and
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
a poset. Suppose that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau)
is
\omega
-Lindelöf.

Proof.

Recall that a space is
\omega
-Lindelöf if and only if
X^{n}
is Lindelöf for all
n
. Assuming
X
is
\omega
-Lindelöf, Theorem
4.6
implies that
X^{n}
is forced to be Lindelöf for all
n
. This is turn suffices to show that
X
is forced to be
\omega
-Lindelöf.
∎

Now we address the preservation of countable tightness of spaces by posets which are strongly proper for stationarily many models. Our theorem generalizes Lemma 5.6 of
[
5
]
, in which Dow showed that any poset of the form
\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_{1})\ast\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
, where
\dot{\mathbb{Q}}
is forced to be countably closed, preserves countable tightness. Such posets, as stated in Lemma
3.11
, are strongly proper. We also recall that a countably closed poset needn’t preserve the countable tightness of a space.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\theta
be a large enough regular cardinal,
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
a countably tight space, and
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
a poset. Suppose that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau)
is countably tight.

Proof.

Fix a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
, a point
x^{*}\in X
, and a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{A}
so that

\Vdash_{\mathbb{P}}x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(\dot{A}),

i.e., so that this is forced by the whole poset. We will find a condition
q\leq p
and a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{B}
so that

q\Vdash\dot{B}\in[\dot{A}]^{\aleph_{0}}\wedge x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(\dot{B}).

By the stationarity assumed in the statement of the theorem, we may find a countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\left\{(X,\tau),\mathbb{P},p,x^{*},\dot{A}\right\}\subset M
and so that every condition in
M\cap\mathbb{P}
has an extension to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition. In particular, we may find
q\leq p
so that
q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic. Let
\operatorname{res}_{M}
be a residue function for
M
.

Since
M
is countable,
M\cap\dot{A}
is forced to be a countable subset of
\dot{A}
. Taking
M\cap\dot{A}
to be the name
\dot{B}
above, it suffices to show that
q\Vdash x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(M\cap\dot{A})
. Since
\tau
(the topology in the ground model) is a basis for the topology in the extension, it suffices to verify the definition of
x^{*}
being in the closure of
M\cap\dot{A}
when applied to open sets from the ground model.

In light of this, fix a condition
r\leq q
and an open
U\in\tau
with
x^{*}\in U
. We will find an extension
r^{*}\leq r
so that

r^{*}\Vdash U\cap(M\cap\dot{A})\neq\emptyset.

Since
r\leq q
and
q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic, let
\bar{r}:=\operatorname{res}_{M}(r)
. Let
A_{\bar{r}}
be the trace of
\dot{A}
below
\bar{r}
, namely

A_{\bar{r}}:=\left\{a\in X:(\exists t\leq_{\mathbb{P}}\bar{r})\;t\Vdash\check{a}\in\dot{A}\right\};

we emphasize that a witness to
a\in A_{\bar{r}}
needn’t be a condition in
M
, only a condition below
\bar{r}
.

Arguing in the ground model
V
, we claim that
x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(A_{\bar{r}})
. Thus fix
E\in\tau
with
x^{*}\in E
. Let
t\leq\bar{r}
(not necessarily with
t\in M
) be any condition which forces that
\check{E}\cap\dot{A}\neq\emptyset
. By further extending
t
if necessary, we may find a specific
a\in X
so that

t\Vdash\check{a}\in\check{E}\cap\dot{A}.

Then
a\in A_{\bar{r}}
, since
t
is a witness, and moreover,
a\in E
. This completes the proof that
x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(A_{\bar{r}})
.

Continuing, we observe that
A_{\bar{r}}\in M
, since
A_{\bar{r}}
is definable in
H(\theta)
by parameters in
M
. Since
(X,\tau)
is countably tight in
V
, we may apply the elementarity of
M
to find a
B\in\left[A_{\bar{r}}\right]^{\aleph_{0}}
with
B\in M
so that
x^{*}\in\operatorname{cl}(B)
. Since
B\in M
and
B
is countable, we in fact have that
B\subseteq M
.

Now recall the open set
U
, fixed above. Since
x^{*}\in U\cap\operatorname{cl}(B)
, we may find a point
a\in B\cap U
. But
B\subseteq M
, so
a\in M
. Moreover,
B\subseteq A_{\bar{r}}
, so
a\in A_{\bar{r}}
as well. By applying the elementarity of
M
once again, we may find an extension
t\leq\bar{r}
with
t\in M
so that
t\Vdash\check{a}\in\dot{A}
. Hence

t\Vdash\check{a}\in U\cap(M\cap\dot{A}).

However, since
t\leq\bar{r}
and since
t
is in
M
, we know that
t
is compatible with
r
. Hence we may fix an extension
r^{*}
of
t
and
r
. This
r^{*}
then finishes the proof.
∎

5.
Preservation of some Covering Properties

In this section we will prove that forcings which are strongly proper for stationarily-many countable elementary submodels preserve the Rothberger and Menger properties of a space. To do so, we will need to translate the idea of an “endowment” (originally from
[
7
]
, and later used in
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
, and
[
16
]
) to this context. The first subsection will handle this task, and in the second subsection, we will prove the preservation theorems.

5.1.
Endowments, Approximations, and Strongly Proper Forcings

For the whole of this subsection, we fix the following objects:

•

a poset
\mathbb{P}
;

•

a Lindelöf space
(X,\tau)
;

•

a
\mathbb{P}
-name
\dot{\cal{U}}
for an open cover of
(X,\tau)
forced to consist of elements of
\tau
;

•

a large enough regular cardinal
\theta
;

•

a countable
M\prec H(\theta)
containing
\mathbb{P},(X,\tau)
, and
\dot{\cal{U}}
as parameters; and

•

an enumeration
\varphi_{M}:\omega\to M\cap\mathbb{P}
of
M\cap\mathbb{P}
.

We will (slightly) generalize the notion of an endowment to the present context in order to establish the existence of useful approximations to generic open covers.

Before launching into the precise definitions, we take a moment to get the reader acquainted with the idea. Recall that a maximal antichain
A
in
M\cap\mathbb{P}
is an antichain so that

\text{for all }p\in M\cap\mathbb{P},\;\text{there is a }q\in A\text{ s.t. }q\text{ is compatible with }p.

For
n\in\omega
, an element
A_{0}
of an “
n
-endowment”
\cal{L}^{M}_{n}
will be a
finite
antichain in
M\cap\mathbb{P}
which contains witnesses to the above statement, but only applied to the first
(n+1)
-many conditions in
M
. Namely,
A_{0}
will satisfy

\text{for all }k\leq n,\;\text{there is a }q\in A_{0}\text{ s.t. }q\text{ is compatible with }\varphi_{M}(k).

The point of these finite antichains is that each condition
q
in such an
A_{0}
will come paired with an open set which
q
forces into
\dot{\cal{U}}
, and (wishing to hedge our bets) we intend to intersect these finitely-many open sets.

Now for the more precise definitions. Let
\cal{A}_{M}
denote the set of all maximal antichains of the poset
M\cap\mathbb{P}
. For each
A\in\cal{A}_{M}
, let
A(n)
be a finite subset of
A
satisfying the following: for all
k\leq n
, there exists
q\in A(n)
so that
\varphi_{M}(k)
is compatible with
q
.

Definition 5.1
.

We define the
n
-th endowment of
M
and
\mathbb{P}
, denoted
\cal{L}^{M}_{n}
, to be the set

\cal{L}^{M}_{n}:=\left\{A(n):A\in\cal{A}_{M}\right\}.

The
endowment of
M
and
\mathbb{P}
, denoted
\cal{L}^{M}
, is the sequence
\left<\cal{L}^{M}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
.

Now we are ready to use
\cal{L}^{M}
to define a sequence
\left<\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}):n\in\omega\right>
of approximations to
\dot{\cal{U}}
.

Remark 5.2
.

We emphasize here that we only define the sequence
\left<\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}):n\in\omega\right>
of approximations to
\dot{\cal{U}}
when
(X,\tau)
is Lindelöf and
\dot{\cal{U}}
a member of
M
.

Fix
n\in\omega
. For each
x\in X
, we may apply Lemma
4.5
to find a maximal antichain
A_{x}\subseteq M\cap\mathbb{P}
so that for all
p\in A_{x}
there is an open
W_{x,p}\in\tau\cap M
so that

p\Vdash x\in\check{W}_{x,p}\in\dot{\cal{U}}.

Let

V_{x,n}:=\bigcap\left\{W_{x,p}:p\in A_{x}(n)\right\}.

Since
A_{x}(n)
is finite,
V_{x,n}
is open; also,
x\in V_{x,n}
. Finally,
V_{x,n}\in M
since each
W_{x,p}
is in
M
and since
A_{x}(n)
is finite.

Definition 5.3
.

The
n
-th
M
-approximation to
\dot{\cal{U}}
, denoted
\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})
, is defined as

\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}):=\left\{V_{x,n}:x\in X\right\}.

Remark 5.4
.

Note that
\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
, since for each
x\in X
,
x
is a member of
V_{x,n}
.

The next lemma, though simple, will be quite useful:

Lemma 5.5
.

For each
n\in\omega
, each
E\in\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})
, and each
k\leq n
, there is a
d\leq_{M\cap\mathbb{P}}\varphi_{M}(k)
so that

d\Vdash(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}})\;\left[\check{E}\subseteq U\right].

Proof.

Fix
n,k
, and
E
. Let
x\in X
so that
E=V_{x,n}
. As
k\leq n
, we may find
p\in A_{x}(n)
so that
p
is compatible with
\varphi_{M}(k)
. By the elementarity of
M
, we may let
d
be an extension of
p
and
\varphi_{M}(k)
with
d\in M
.

Then
E=V_{x,n}\subseteq W_{x,p}
. Moreover,
p
forces that
x\in\check{W}_{x,p}\in\dot{\cal{U}}
. Since
d
extends
p
and
\varphi_{M}(k)
,
d
is the desired witness.
∎

5.2.
More Preservation Theorems

We now show that posets which are strongly proper for stationarily many models preserve the Rothberger and Menger properties. The proofs are nearly identical. Accordingly, we will prove the preservation of the Rothberger property in detail, and then we will carry out the slight modifications sufficient to prove the preservation of the Menger property. We close with some corollaries concerning the preservation of
\omega
-Rothberger and
\omega
-Menger spaces.

Theorem 5.6
.

Suppose that
\theta
is a large enough regular cardinal, that
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
is a Rothberger space, and that
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
is a poset. Suppose further that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau)
is Rothberger.

Proof.

Fix a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
and a sequence
\left<\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of names for open covers of
(X,\tau)
. We may assume that each
\,\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
is forced to consist of basic open sets, and hence is forced to be a subset of
\tau
. Our goal is to find an extension
q\leq p
and sequence
\left<\dot{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of names so that

(1)

for each
n\in\omega
,
q\Vdash\dot{U}_{n}\in\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
and

(2)

q
forces
\left\{\dot{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\right\}
is an open cover of
X
.

Using the stationarity assumed in the statement of the theorem, let
M\prec H(\theta)
be countable so that both of the following are true:

•

M
contains the parameters
p
,
\mathbb{P}
,
(X,\tau)
, and
\left<\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
;

•

every condition in
M
extends to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition.

In particular, we may extend
p
to a condition
q
which is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic.

Let
\varphi_{M}:\omega\to M\cap\mathbb{P}
be the enumeration of
M\cap\mathbb{P}
relative to which we construct the approximations as in Subsection
5.1
.

Since each
\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
is a member of
M
and since
(X,\tau)
is Lindelöf, we know that
\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n})
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
for each
n\in\omega
(recall Remark
5.2
). We now apply the fact that
(X,\tau)
is Rothberger in
V
. By Lemma
2.2
applied to the sequence
\left<\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}):n\in\omega\right>
, we may find a sequence
\left<W_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
so that
W_{n}\in\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n})
for each
n
and so that for each
x\in X
, there are
infinitely
-many
n
with
x\in W_{n}
.

Now we will define a sequence
\left<\dot{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of names and show that
q
forces that this sequence witnesses the definition of Rothberger in the extension. We apply the maximality principle for names by showing, given an arbitrary
V
-generic filter
G
over
\mathbb{P}
, how to define
\left<\dot{U}_{n}[G]:n\in\omega\right>
. So fix
G
, and fix
n
. If there is some
U\in\cal{U}_{n}
so that
W_{n}\subseteq U
, then we let
\dot{U}_{n}[G]
select some such
U
. Otherwise, we let
\dot{U}_{n}[G]\in\cal{U}_{n}
be arbitrary.

We show that

forces that
\left<\dot{U}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
is an open cover of

by showing that no extension of

can force a specific counterexample. So fix
r\leq q
and

, and we will find an

and an

so that

.

Consider
\bar{r}
, a residue of

to
M\cap\mathbb{P}
. Choose

so that
\varphi_{M}(k)=\bar{r}
, where
\varphi_{M}:\omega\to M\cap\mathbb{P}
is the enumeration that we fixed earlier in the proof. Since
\left<W_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
satisfies Lemma
2.2
, we may find an

so that

. We now apply Lemma
5.5
to get a condition

with

so that

Since

and
\bar{r}
is a residue of

to
M
, we may find a condition

. Then since

extends

,

r^{*}\Vdash(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}}_{m})\;[\check{W}_{m}\subseteq U].

By definition of the name

, we then have

This completes the proof.
∎

As mentioned at the beginning of this subsection, the following proof is nearly identical to the proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose that
\theta
is a large enough regular cardinal, that
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
is a Menger space, and that
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
is a poset. Suppose further that the set of countable

so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for

is stationary in

. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau)
is Menger.

Proof.

Fix a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
and a sequence
\left<\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of names for open covers of
(X,\tau)
. We may assume that each
\,\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
is forced to consist of basic open sets, and hence is forced to be a subset of
\tau
. Our goal is to find an extension
q\leq p
and sequence
\left<\dot{\cal{F}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of
\mathbb{P}
-names so that

(1)

for each
n\in\omega
,
q\Vdash\dot{\cal{F}}_{n}\text{ is a finite subset of }\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
; and

(2)

q\Vdash\bigcup\left\{\dot{\cal{F}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right\}
is an open cover of
X
.

Using the stationarity assumed in the statement of the theorem, let
M\prec H(\theta)
be countable so that both of the following are true:

•

M
contains the parameters
p
,
\mathbb{P}
,
(X,\tau)
, and
\left<\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
;

•

every condition in
M
extends to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition.

In particular, we may extend
p
to a condition
q
which is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic.

Let
\varphi_{M}:\omega\to M\cap\mathbb{P}
be the enumeration of
M\cap\mathbb{P}
relative to which we construct the approximations as in Subsection
5.1
.

Apply Lemma
2.3
to the sequence
\left<\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}):n\in\omega\right>
to find a sequence
\left<\cal{F}^{M}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
so that each
\cal{F}^{M}_{n}
is a finite subset of
\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n})
, so that
\bigcup\left\{\cal{F}^{M}_{n}:n\in\omega\right\}
is an open cover of
(X,\tau)
, and so that for each
x\in X
, there are infinitely-many

so that
x\in\bigcup\cal{F}^{M}_{k}
.

We now define the sequence
\left<\dot{\cal{F}}_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of
\mathbb{P}
-names which will witness the desired statement. Accordingly, let
G
be an arbitrary
V
-generic filter over
\mathbb{P}
, and fix
n\in\omega
. We have two cases. First suppose that no
F\in\cal{F}^{M}_{n}
is a subset of any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{n}
. In this degenerate case, we let

be an arbitrary finite subset of
\cal{U}_{n}
. Now suppose that at least one

is a subset of an element of
\mathcal{U}_{n}
. In this case, for each such

, choose

with

, and let

be the set of these

The proof that

forces that

is an open cover of

is nearly identical to the corresponding proof in Theorem

.
∎

6.
Selection Games

Selection principles have naturally corresponding selection games, which include types of topological games.
Topological games have a long history, much of which can be gathered from Telgársky’s survey
[
36
]
.
In this paper, we consider the traditional selection games for two players,
\rm{I}
and
\rm{II}
, of countably infinite length.

Definition 6.1
.

Given sets
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, we define the
finite-selection game

\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
for
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
as follows.
In round
n\in\omega
,
\rm{I}
plays
A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\rm{II}
responds with
\mathcal{F}_{n}\in[A_{n}]^{<\omega}
.
We declare
\rm{II}
the winner if
\bigcup\{\mathcal{F}_{n}:n\in\omega\}\in\mathcal{B}
.
Otherwise,
\rm{I}
wins.

Definition 6.2
.

Given sets
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
, we analogously define the
single-selection game

\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
for
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
as follows.
In round
n\in\omega
,
\rm{I}
plays
A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and
\rm{II}
responds with
x_{n}\in A_{n}
.
We declare
\rm{II}
the winner if
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}\in\mathcal{B}
.
Otherwise,
\rm{I}
wins.

Definition 6.3
.

By
selection games
, we mean the class consisting of
\mathsf{G}_{\square}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})

where
\square\in\{1,\mathrm{fin}\}
, and
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are sets.
So, when we say
\mathcal{G}
is a selection game, we mean that there exist
\square\in\{1,\mathrm{fin}\}

and sets
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
so that
\mathcal{G}=\mathsf{G}_{\square}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

The study of games naturally inspires questions about the existence of various kinds of strategies.
Infinite games and corresponding full-information strategies were both introduced in
[
9
]
.
Some forms of limited-information strategies came shortly after, like positional (also known as stationary) strategies.
For more on stationary and Markov strategies, see
[
10
]
.

Definition 6.4
.

We define strategies of various strengths below.

•

A
strategy for
\rm{I}
in
\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a function

\sigma:(\bigcup\mathcal{A})^{<\omega}\to\mathcal{A}
.
A strategy
\sigma
for
\rm{I}
is called
winning
if whenever
x_{n}\in\sigma\langle x_{k}:k<n\rangle

for all
n\in\omega
,
\{x_{n}:n\in\omega\}\not\in\mathcal{B}
.
If
\rm{I}
has a winning strategy, we write
\mathrm{I}\uparrow\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

A
strategy for
\rm{II}
in
\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is a function

\sigma:\mathcal{A}^{<\omega}\to\bigcup\mathcal{A}
.
A strategy
\sigma
for
\rm{II}
is
winning
if whenever
A_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
for all
n\in\omega
,

\{\sigma(A_{0},\ldots,A_{n}):n\in\omega\}\in\mathcal{B}
.
If
\rm{II}
has a winning strategy, we write
\mathrm{II}\uparrow\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

A
predetermined strategy
for
\rm{I}
is a strategy which only considers the current turn number.
Formally it is a function
\sigma:\omega\to\mathcal{A}
.
If
\rm{I}
has a winning predetermined strategy, we write
\mathrm{I}\underset{\mathrm{pre}}{\uparrow}\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

A
Markov strategy
for
\rm{II}
is a strategy which only considers the most recent move of
\rm{I}
and the current turn number.
Formally it is a function
\sigma:\mathcal{A}\times\omega\to\bigcup\mathcal{A}
.
If
\rm{II}
has a winning Markov strategy, we write

\mathrm{II}\underset{\mathrm{mark}}{\uparrow}\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

•

If there is a single element
A_{0}\in\mathcal{A}
so that the constant function with value
A_{0}
is a winning strategy for
\rm{I}
, we say that
\rm{I}
has a
constant winning strategy
, denoted by
\mathrm{I}\underset{\mathrm{cnst}}{\uparrow}\mathsf{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.

These definitions can be extended to
\mathsf{G}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
in the obvious way.

Note that, for any selection game
\mathcal{G}
,

\mathrm{II}\underset{\mathrm{mark}}{\uparrow}\mathcal{G}\implies\mathrm{II}\uparrow\mathcal{G}\implies\mathrm{I}\not\uparrow\mathcal{G}\implies\mathrm{I}\underset{\mathrm{pre}}{\not\uparrow}\mathcal{G}\implies\mathrm{I}\underset{\mathrm{cnst}}{\not\uparrow}\mathcal{G}.

Strategies for selection games are connected to selection principles. Notably,
\mathrm{I}\underset{\mathrm{pre}}{\not\uparrow}\mathcal{G}_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
if and only if
S_{1}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is true. As another example,
\rm{I}
fails to have a constant winning strategy in the Rothberger game (
G_{1}(\mathcal{O}(X),\mathcal{O}(X))
) if and only if the space is Lindelöf.

For the Rothberger game, some of the levels of strategies are equivalent. Pawlikowski showed that
\rm{I}
has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game if and only if
\rm{I}
has a pre-determined winning strategy
[
22
]
. Scheepers (
[
28
]
) later extended this result to the
\omega
-Rothberger game,
G_{1}(\Omega(X),\Omega(X))
.

In
[
3
]
it is shown that
\rm{II}
has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game if and only if
\rm{II}
has a winning strategy in the
\omega
-Rothberger game.

Finally, its straightforward to check that the following are equivalent:

•

\rm{II}
has a winning Markov strategy in the Rothberger game on
X
,

•

\rm{II}
has a winning Markov strategy in the
\omega
-Rothberger game on
X
, and

•

X
is topologically countable (if
X
is
T_{1}
this is the same as saying
X
is countable; for the full definition of topologically countable, see
[
3
]
).

The following counterexamples distinguish the rest of the properties from each other.

•

\rm{II}
has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game on the Fortissimo space of the reals, but player two does not have a winning Markov strategy, (see the following stack exchange post
[
2
]
).

•

Neither
\rm{I}
nor
\rm{II}
has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game on Luzin subsets of the real line
[
23
]
.

7.
A Preservation Theorem for the Rothberger Game

In this section we prove that strongly proper forcings preserve spaces in which player
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game. We begin by proving an ad hoc density lemma, and then we prove the preservation theorem.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\theta
be a large enough regular cardinal,
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
a poset, and
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
a space so that
\rm{II}
has a winning strategy, say
\sigma
, in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau)
. Assume that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
.

Suppose that
\cal{V}_{1},\dots,\cal{V}_{k}
are a sequence of open covers of
(X,\tau)
played by
\rm{I}
with
W_{1},\dots,W_{k}
the responses by
\rm{II}
via
\sigma
. Finally, let
\,\dot{\cal{U}}\in H(\theta)
be a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open cover of
X
forced to consist of sets in
\tau
. Then the set of
q\in\mathbb{P}
with the following property
(*)
is dense:
(*)
asserts of
q
that there exists a countable
M\prec H(\theta)
with
\left\{\dot{\cal{U}},\mathbb{P},(X,\tau)\right\}\subset M
so that

(1)

q
is a strongly
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic condition and

(2)

there exists an
n\geq 1
so that

q\Vdash(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}})\;\left[\sigma\left(\cal{V}_{1},\dots,\cal{V}_{k},\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})\right)\subseteq U\right].

Proof.

Let
p_{0}
be a given condition in
\mathbb{P}
. Using the stationarity assumed for the lemma, let
M\prec H(\theta)
be countable containing
p_{0}
,
\dot{\cal{U}}
,
(X,\tau)
, and
\mathbb{P}
as elements. Let
\varphi_{M}:\omega\to M\cap\mathbb{P}
be the enumeration of
M\cap\mathbb{P}
relative to which we construct the endowments. Extend
p_{0}
to an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition
p
, and let
n\in\omega
so that

\varphi_{M}(n)=\operatorname{res}_{M}(p).

Let
W_{k+1}
be
\rm{II}
’s reply via
\sigma
where
\rm{I}
plays
\cal{V}_{1},\dots,\cal{V}_{k},\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})
. Among other things,
W_{k+1}\in\cal{V}^{M}_{n}(\dot{\cal{U}})
. Apply Lemma
5.5
to find a condition
t\leq_{M\cap\mathbb{P}}\operatorname{res}_{M}(p)
so that

t\Vdash\left(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}}\right)\left[\check{W}_{k+1}\subseteq U\right].

Since
t\in M
and
t\leq\operatorname{res}_{M}(p)
, we can find a condition
q
extending
p
and
t
. Then
q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic since it extends
p
, and
q
forces the desired statement since it extends
t
.
∎

Now we are ready for the next preservation theorem.

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose that
\theta
is a large enough regular cardinal, that
(X,\tau)\in H(\theta)
is a space for which II has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game on
X
, and that
\mathbb{P}\in H(\theta)
is a poset. Suppose further that the set of countable
M\prec H(\theta)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for
M
is stationary in
[H(\theta)]^{\aleph_{0}}
. Then
\mathbb{P}
forces that II has a winning strategy in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau)
.

Proof.

Fix the space
(X,\tau)
and a winning strategy
\sigma
for player
{\rm II}
in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau)
.

Let
G
be an arbitrary
V
-generic filter over
\mathbb{P}
, and in
V[G]
, we will recursively construct a strategy for player
{\rm II}
in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau_{G})
. This strategy will be denoted
\sigma^{*}
.

The main idea of the proof is this: in
V[G]
, we will simulate a run of the ground model game for
(X,\tau)
where
{\rm II}
plays via
\sigma
.
{\rm I}
’s plays in this game will come from approximations of the form
\cal{V}^{M_{n}}_{\ell(n)}(\dot{\cal{U}}_{n})
for models
M_{n}
, and where
\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
is a name for
{\rm I}
’s play in the game for
(X,\tau_{G})
. We emphasize here that we will be picking objects from
V
while working in the extension
V[G]
. To see that this approach succeeds, we will step back to
V
and work with names for the relevant objects. We will close out the argument by simulating a run of a game in
V
and catching our tail.

To begin, suppose that in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau_{G})
,
{\rm I}
has played
\cal{U}_{1},\dots,\cal{U}_{m}
and that
{\rm II}
has responded via
\sigma^{*}
with
U_{1},\dots,U_{m}
. Let
{\rm I}
now play
\cal{U}_{m+1}
. The situation looks like this:

(X,\tau_{G})\text{-game}:\begin{array}[]{c|cccccccc}{\rm I}&\cal{U}_{1}&&\dots&&\cal{U}_{m}&&\cal{U}_{m+1}\\
\hline\cr{\rm II}&&U_{1}&&\dots&&U_{m}&\end{array}

We will work to define
{\rm II}
’s reply via
\sigma^{*}
, denoted
U_{m+1}
. To do so, we first articulate a recursive hypothesis, and then we will construct
U_{m+1}
by applying Lemma
7.1
.

We suppose as a recursive hypothesis that we have defined a sequence of four-tuples
\left<M_{k},\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{k},p_{k},\ell(k)\right>_{1\leq k\leq m}
consisting of elements of
V
that satisfy six assumptions; we will state the first five of these, make a comment, and then state the sixth. The first five are:

(1)

M_{k}
is a countable elementary submodel of
H^{V}(\theta)
;

(2)

\dot{\cal{U}}_{k}\in M_{k}
is a
\mathbb{P}
-name for an open cover of
(X,\tau)
consisting of elements of
\tau
;

(3)

\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{k}[G]=\cal{U}_{k}
;

(4)

p_{k}\in G
and
p_{k}
is an
(M_{k},\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic condition;

(5)

\operatorname{res}_{M_{k}}(p_{k})
is the
\ell(k)
-th condition in the enumeration of
M_{k}\cap\mathbb{P}
, i.e.,
\varphi_{M_{k}}(\ell(k))=\operatorname{res}_{M_{k}}(p_{k})
.

Next, let
W_{1},\dots,W_{m}
be
{\rm II}
’s replies via
\sigma
in the partial run of the game in
V
, where
{\rm I}
plays
\left<\cal{V}^{M_{k}}_{\ell(k)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{k}):1\leq k\leq m\right>
. So the situation, for the ground model game being played in
V[G]
, looks like this:

(X,\tau)\text{-game}:\begin{array}[]{c|ccccccc}{\rm I}&\cal{V}^{M_{1}}_{\ell(1)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{1})&&\dots&&\cal{V}^{M_{m}}_{\ell(m)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{m})&\\
\hline\cr{\rm II}&&W_{1}&&\dots&&W_{m}\end{array}

Our sixth and final recursive assumption is

(6)

for each
1\leq k\leq m
,

p_{k}\Vdash^{V}_{\mathbb{P}}\,\left(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{k}\right)\left[\check{W}_{k}\subseteq U\right].

Now we apply Lemma
7.1
to the sequence
\left<\cal{V}^{M_{k}}_{\ell(k)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{k}):1\leq k\leq m\right>
of
{\rm I}
’s plays and to a name
\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{m+1}
for
\cal{U}_{m+1}
to find a condition
p_{m+1}\in G
and a countable elementary submodel
M_{m+1}\prec H^{V}(\theta)
(with
M_{m+1}
in the ground model) so that

•

p_{m+1}
is
(M_{m+1},\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic;

•

M_{m+1}
contains
\mathbb{P}
,
(X,\tau)
, and
\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{m+1}
;

•

p_{m+1}
forces over
V
that
\left(\exists U\in\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{m+1}\right)\left[\check{W}_{m+1}\subseteq U\right]
,

where
W_{m+1}
is
{\rm II}
’s response via
\sigma
. We may finally let
{\rm II}
’s reply via
\sigma^{*}
in the
(X,\tau_{G})
-game be some
U\in\cal{U}_{m+1}
with
W_{m+1}\subseteq U
.

To complete the definition of
\sigma^{*}
, we pay our debt to the recursion hypothesis by letting the next four-tuple be equal to
\left<M_{m+1},\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{m+1},p_{m+1},\ell(m+1)\right>
, where

\varphi_{M_{m+1}}(\ell(m+1))=\operatorname{res}_{M_{m+1}}(p_{m+1}).

This completes the definition of
\sigma^{*}
in an arbitrary
V
-generic extension via
\mathbb{P}
and hence completes the definition of the name
\dot{\sigma}^{*}
.

Now, working over
V
, we show that
\dot{\sigma}^{*}
is forced to be a winning strategy for
{\rm II}
in the extension. Towards that end, fix sequences
\left<\dot{\cal{U}}_{k}:k\geq 1\right>
and
\left<\dot{U}_{k}:k\geq 1\right>
which are forced to be a run of the game in which
{\rm II}
plays via
\dot{\sigma}^{*}
. By definition of
\dot{\sigma}^{*}
we may also find a sequence
\left<\dot{W}_{k}:k\geq 1\right>
and a sequence
\left<\dot{M}_{k},\ddot{\cal{U}}_{k},\dot{p}_{k},\dot{\ell}(k)\right>_{k\geq 1}
of
\mathbb{P}
-names for elements of
V
forced to satisfy items (1)-(6) above. Among other things, each
\dot{p}_{k}
is forced to be in
\dot{G}
and
\ddot{\cal{U}}_{k}
(a name for a name) is forced to be some element
z_{k}
of
\dot{M}_{k}
so that the interpretation of
z_{k}
equals the interpretation of
\dot{\cal{U}}_{k}
.

We will show that
\mathbb{P}
forces that
\left\{\dot{U}_{k}:k\geq 1\right\}
is an open cover and hence that
{\rm II}
wins. To do so, fix a condition
p\in\mathbb{P}
and a point

, and we will show that some extension of

forces that

is in
\bigcup\left\{\dot{U}_{k}:k\geq 1\right\}
.

To find the desired extension of

, we create a run of the Rothberger game in
V
where
{\rm II}
plays according to
\sigma
. This will involve constructing an
\omega
-length decreasing sequence
\left<r_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
of conditions starting from
p
, each of which reveals more about the sequence of names.

More precisely, define
\left<r_{n}:n\in\omega\right>
with
r_{0}=p
so that

(a)

for all
n\geq 1

r_{n}
decides the value of
\left<\dot{M}_{n},\ddot{\cal{U}}_{n},\dot{p}_{n},\dot{\ell}(n)\right>

\left<M_{n},\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{n},p_{n},\ell(n)\right>;

(b)

r_{n}\leq p_{n}
(this can be done since
r_{n}\Vdash\check{p}_{n}=\dot{p}_{n}\in\dot{G}

(c)

r_{n}\Vdash\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{n}=\dot{\cal{U}}_{n}
.

Since
r_{n}
forces that the value of the tuple
\left<\dot{M}_{n},\ddot{\cal{U}}_{n},\dot{p}_{n},\dot{\ell}(n)\right>
is equal to

\left<M_{n},\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{n},p_{n},\ell(n)\right>,

we know that
\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{n}\in M_{n}

in the Rothberger game on
(X,\tau)
in the ground model. Let
\left<B_{n}:n\geq 1\right>

, we may find an

r_{i}

decides the values of the previous tuples
\left<\dot{M}_{k},\ddot{\cal{U}}_{k},\dot{p}_{k},\dot{\ell}(k)\right>_{1\leq k<n}

\dot{W}_{n}=\sigma\left(\cal{V}^{M_{1}}_{\ell(1)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{1}),\dots,\cal{V}^{M_{n}}_{\ell(n)}(\dot{\cal{U}}^{\prime}_{n})\right).

Therefore
r_{n}\Vdash\dot{W}_{n}=\check{B}_{n}
. Since
r_{n}\leq p_{n}

\dot{W}_{n}\subseteq U

r_{n}

r_{n}

x\in B_{n}

8.
Open Questions

We have shown that strongly proper forcing preserves countable tightness and the Menger property, two selection principles, but in contrast to our results about the Rothberger game, we do not yet have a proof that strongly proper forcing preserves strategic information in the countable fan tightness or Menger games.

Question 8.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for stationarily many models and that
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the strong countable fan tightness game on a space
(X,\tau)
. Does
\mathbb{P}
preserve this?

Question 8.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for stationarily many models and that
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the Menger game on a space
(X,\tau)
. Does
\mathbb{P}
preserve this?

Even answering the following more limited question about the Menger game would be interesting.

Question 8.3
.

Suppose that
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the Menger game on a space
(X,\tau)
. Does Cohen forcing preserve this?

In the situation that the questions regarding the countable fan tightness game prove intractable, there is an easier question we can pose that relies on the connection between
\omega
-covers on
X
and countable fan tightness for
C_{p}(X)
.

Question 8.4
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for stationarily many models,
X
is a topological space, and that
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the strong countable fan tightness game on
(C_{p}(X),\tau)
. Does
\mathbb{P}
preserve this?

It is also common to study covering properties involving
k
-covers:
\mathcal{U}

Question 8.5
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{P}
is strongly proper for stationarily many models and that
{\rm II}
has a winning strategy in the
k
-Rothberger/Menger game on a space
(X,\tau)
. Does
\mathbb{P}
preserve this?

Abstract
In large scale machine learning, random sampling is a popular way to approximate datasets by a small representative subset of examples. In particular, sensitivity sampling is an intensely studied technique which provides provable guarantees on the quality of approximation, while reducing the number of examples to the product of the VC dimension d d and the total sensitivity 𝔖 \mathfrak{S} in remarkably general settings. However, guarantees going beyond this general bound of 𝔖 ​ d \mathfrak{S}d are known in perhaps only one setting, for ℓ 2 \ell_{2} subspace embeddings , despite intense study of sensitivity sampling in prior work. In this work, we show the first bounds for sensitivity sampling for ℓ p \ell_{p} subspace embeddings for p > 2 p>2 that improve over the general 𝔖 ​ d \mathfrak{S}d bound, achieving a bound of roughly 𝔖 2 − 2 / p \mathfrak{S}^{2-2/p} for 2 < p < ∞ 2<p<\infty . In typical large scale machine learning problems, one encounters a dataset represented by an

Uniform sampling is perhaps the simplest instance of this idea that is often used in practice. However, uniform sampling can lead to significant information loss when there are a small number of important training examples that must be kept under sampling. Thus, recent work, both in theory

In this work, we focus on the use of importance sampling techniques for approximating objective functions for empirical risk minimization problems. Consider an objective function

subspace embeddings. Such perturbations may arise due to roundoff error or finite precision on a computer, and no prior bounds beating Lewis weight sampling or the naïve
\mathfrak{S}d
bound for sensitivity sampling were known for the applications above.

1.2
Other Related Work

The problem of designing sampling algorithms for
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings has a long and rich history, dating back to works in the functional analysis literature and culminating in the Lewis weight sampling result
[
Lew78
,
Sch87
,
BLM89
,
Tal90
,
LT91
,
Tal95
,
SZ01
]
. More recently, the theoretical computer science community has studied this problem for its applications to
\ell_{p}
linear regression and other empirical risk minimization problems. The early works of
[
Cla05
,
DDH+09
]
obtained sampling algorithms for
\ell_{p}
regression based on sensitivity score upper bounds given by various constructions of
\ell_{p}
well-conditioned bases
for
\mathbf{A}
. The theory of Lewis weight sampling was brought to the theoretical computer science literature by
[
CP15
]
, and has been improved both in the computation of the weights
[
Lee16
,
FLPS22
,
JLS22
]
and sampling guarantees
[
WY22
,
WY23
]
.

2
Sampling Error Bounds

We now give a more detailed discussion of our techniques and results. While our discussion in this section will present most of the major ideas necessary to prove our new results, all of the full proofs will be deferred to the appendix due to space considerations.

2.1
Prior Approaches

We start by describing the standard proof of the
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}d)
sample complexity bound for sensitivity sampling
[
Sch87
]
. Using Bernstein bounds, it can be shown that sampling
O(\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}\log\frac{1}{\delta})
rows preserves the
\ell_{p}
norm of a fixed vector
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
up to a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
factor with probability at least
1-\delta
. One can then consider an
\varepsilon
-net

N
, which is a set of size roughly
1/\varepsilon^{d}
such that any
\ell_{p}
unit vector
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
is
\varepsilon
-close to some
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in N
. By a union bound, the norm preservation guarantee holds simultaneously for every
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in N
with constant probability, if we set
\delta=\varepsilon^{d}
. Now for an arbitrary
\ell_{p}
unit vector
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
, the norm preservation guarantee holds for an
\varepsilon
-close point
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in N
, which implies the norm preservation guarantee for
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
itself by a standard argument. Finally, scale invariance ensures that the same conclusion holds for all vectors
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
, rather than just unit vectors.

To improve over this argument, the
\ell_{p}
Lewis weight sampling technique was developed in a line of work from the functional analysis literature
[
Lew78
,
BLM89
,
Tal90
,
LT91
,
Tal95
]
, which incorporates
chaining arguments
. Chaining arguments are a way of improving
\varepsilon
-net arguments by using a
sequence
of
\varepsilon
-nets at different “scales”, rather than using a single scale of
\varepsilon
, and using tighter bounds for net constructions (i.e., smaller cardinality nets) at larger scales (see, e.g.,
[
Nel16
]
for a survey of chaining applications in computer science).

We now delve into a discussion of the overall strategy towards bounding the
sampling error
of our
\ell_{p}
sampling results, which is a random variable
\Lambda
depending on
\mathbf{S}
given by

\Lambda\coloneqq\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert.

(3)

Note that if
\Lambda\leq\varepsilon
, then
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In our discussions in this section, we will focus on bounding
\Lambda
in expectation, although our full proofs in the appendix will bound higher moments of
\Lambda
to obtain high probability bounds.

2.2
Generalized Chaining Bounds for
\ell_{p}
Subspace Embeddings

Our main technical lemma towards bounding (
3
) is a generalization of the chaining argument framework for Lewis weight sampling, which bounds the sampling error of
\ell_{p}
sampling algorithms by the leverage scores and
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of the sampled matrix
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
, rather than by the Lewis weights of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
.

First, we introduce our sampling bounds obtained by generalizing the chaining arguments of
[
BLM89
,
LT91
]
. In this result, we obtain the following bound on a certain Rademacher process, which can be interpreted as the sampling error of a uniform sampling process, as we describe in
Section

2.4
.

Lemma 2.1

(Rademacher Process Bound, Simplified)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\tau\geq\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})
and
\sigma\geq\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
for every
i\in[n]
. Let

E\coloneqq\E_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert.

Then,

E\leq\begin{cases}O(\tau^{1/2})(\log n)^{3/2}&p<2\\
O(\tau^{1/2})(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{3/2}&p>2\end{cases}

This result follows from a Gaussianization argument (
Lemma

7.1
), followed by an application of Dudley’s entropy integral theorem (
Theorem

6.2
), and then bounding the entropy integral in
Lemma

6.6
and
Lemma

6.7
.

As we discuss in
Section

2.4
,
Lemma

2.1
will be a key ingredient in bounding the sampling error in our sensitivity sampling analysis.

2.3
Leverage Score and Sensitivity Bounds

In our final argument,
Lemma

2.1
will be applied with the matrix
\mathbf{A}
set to be (a modified version of) the sampled matrix
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
. Thus, we require a bound on the leverage scores and sensitivities
\tau
and
\sigma
of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
. In fact,
\sigma
is naturally bounded by the
\ell_{p}
sensitivity sampling algorithm: indeed, if we a priori assume that
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq(1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})
is at most

\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{2\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{p_{i}}

(4)

which is at most
2\alpha
if
p_{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha
. While this is only an informal argument, this intuition can be formalized, as we discuss later in this section.

Next, we require a bound on the leverage scores
\tau
of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
, which is more challenging, as sensitivity sampling does not directly bound this quantity. To address this problem, we show how to bound the leverage scores of an arbitrary matrix by the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of the matrix. In particular, we show that for
q\geq p
, the largest
\ell_{q}
sensitivity bounds the largest
\ell_{p}
sensitivity.

Lemma 2.2

(Monotonicity of Max
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity)
.

Let
q\geq p>0
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\frac{\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{\infty}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}}.

We also use an “approximate converse” of the above result:

Lemma 2.3

(Reverse Monotonicity of Max
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity)
.

Let
q\geq p>0
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then,

\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}}\leq\left\lparen\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\right\rparen^{q/p}n^{q/p-1}.

While these lemmas only apply to the max
\ell_{p}
sensitivity and are quite loose when the max
\ell_{p}
sensitivity can be arbitrary, we use the crucial fact that the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
are essentially “flat”, that is, the maximum
\ell_{p}
sensitivity will be within a small factor of the
average

\ell_{p}
sensitivity
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n
. Thus,
Lemma

2.2
and
Lemma

2.3
allow us to bound the leverage scores by the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities for
p>2
and
p<2
, respectively. This idea also allows us to prove
Theorem

1.7
.

2.4
Gaussianization Reduction for Sampling Algorithms

In the works of
[
BLM89
,
LT91
]
, a version of
Lemma

2.1
tailored to
\ell_{p}
Lewis weight sampling is used as a part of a recursive sampling algorithm, where the Rademacher process represents the sampling error of a process that samples each row
i\in[n]
with probability
1/2
and scales the result by
2
. Indeed, if
\mathbf{S}_{i,i}
takes the value
0
or
2^{1/p}
with probability
1/2
each and
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=1
, then
(\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}-1)
is a Rademacher variable and

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}-1)\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p},

and thus
Lemma

2.1
bounds (
3
). While this only reduces the number of rows by a factor of
2
, this process can be applied recursively for
O(\log n)
rounds to reduce the number of rows to
\poly(d)
.

However, we instead primarily use
Lemma

2.1
in a reduction based on
[
CP15
]
for an algorithm with
one
round of sampling. In this reduction, we bound the sampling error (
3
) by introducing an independent copy
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{S}
and estimate

\Lambda\leq\E\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\right\rvert

Because
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
are identically distributed, multiplying each coordinate
i\in[n]
by a Rademacher variable
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\sim\{\pm 1\}
does not change the distribution. Then by applying the triangle inequality, it follows that

\Lambda\leq 2\E_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\{\pm 1\}^{n}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert,

(5)

which closely resembles
Lemma

2.1
.

At this point, for each fixing of
\mathbf{S}
, we wish to apply
Lemma

2.1
with
\mathbf{A}
replaced by
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
, with sensitivities bounded using the idea of (
4
). However, we cannot a priori assume that
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\geq(1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}
. To fix this, the idea of
[
CP15
]
is to introduce an
auxiliary subspace embedding

\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
such that
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}
also has
\ell_{p}
sensitivities bounded by
\alpha
, and
does
satisfy
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\Theta(1)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, we can apply the result of
Lemma

2.1
to the concatenated matrix

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\\
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}\end{pmatrix}.

It can then be shown that the quantity
E
bounded in
Lemma

2.1
indeed bounds the quantity in (
5
), and furthermore, we can fix the argument of (
4
) by bounding

\sup_{\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{1}{p_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{O(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}))}{p_{i}}.

Importantly, we only need to use the existence of
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
for the analysis and thus
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
can be constructed in any way, rather than just by sensitivity sampling.

2.5
Construction of Auxiliary
\ell_{p}
Subspace Embeddings

We now delve into further detail about the other key piece of our analysis, which is the construction of auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
which are compatible with the reduction argument in
Section

2.4
.

2.5.1
Sensitivity Sampling,
p<2

For our sensitivity sampling result for
p<2
in
Theorem

1.5
, we aim to sample roughly
m=\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p}
rows. We first briefly sketch the intuition behind this bound. If we sample each row
i\in[n]
with probability roughly
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha
for an oversampling parameter
\alpha>0
, then we expect the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
to be bounded by
\alpha
according to the informal reasoning of (
4
). We then wish to bound the sampling error using
Lemma

2.1
, which, combined with the bound on the leverage scores using the sensitivities in
Lemma

2.3
, gives a bound of

\tau\leq\alpha^{2/p}m^{2/p-1}=\alpha\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}))^{2/p-1}

by using that
m
is
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha
in expectation. Since we want this to be at most
\varepsilon^{2}
, we set
\alpha=\varepsilon^{2}/\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p-1}
, which gives a bound of
m=\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p}
as claimed.

Now coming back to the formal argument using the auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
, we wish to construct a subspace embedding
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
that preserves
\ell_{p}
norms of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
up to a constant factor, but also has
\ell_{p}
sensitivities at most
\alpha
, since this is the bound that
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
will satisfy. To construct this
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
, we proceed by first using Lewis weight sampling (
Theorem

1.8
) to construct a
\Theta(1)
-approximate
\ell_{p}
subspace sampling with
\tilde{O}(d)
rows. Importantly, we show that this sampling procedure can only increase the total
\ell_{p}
sensitivity by a constant factor (see
Lemma

4.1
). We then apply a flattening procedure (see
Lemma

4.4
), which yields an
\ell_{p}
isometry with at most
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha
rows such that the sensitivity of each row is bounded by
\alpha
. Furthermore, because the number of rows
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha
of this auxiliary subspace embedding
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}
is at most the bound
m
that we seek, we can apply the same reasoning as before using
Lemma

2.3
to bound the leverage scores of
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}
to recover the same bound as that for
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
. Thus, we obtain our desired construction of the auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding, allowing the reduction argument as described in
Section

2.4
to go through.

2.5.2
Sensitivity Sampling,
p>2

For our result on sensitivity sampling when
p>2
, the intuition and reasoning roughly follows the case of
p<2
. However, we must be more careful with the construction of the auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding, since we cannot use Lewis weight sampling to construct it; this is because the sample complexity of
\tilde{O}(d^{p/2})
for Lewis weight sampling is larger than our sample complexity bound of
m=\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-2/p}
that we aim for. Thus, in order to obtain our auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding, we must essentially achieve the same results that we claim in
Theorem

1.5
, using an
alternate construction
that does not directly use one-shot sensitivity sampling. For this, we instead revisit the
recursive sampling
process mentioned earlier in
Section

2.4
, which originates from the functional analysis literature. Here, we first flatten our input matrix using
Lemma

4.4
to a matrix with at most
(4/3)n
rows and
\ell_{p}
sensitivities at most
O(\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n)
, and then use
Lemma

2.1
directly as the sampling error bound for a uniform sampling algorithm which samples each row with probability
1/2
. Note then that overall, we retain
(4/3)(1/2)n=(2/3)n
rows altogether in expectation after this result, and furthermore, we have a sampling error of at most
\varepsilon
. Then, because we sample only a constant fraction of rows after each application of the procedure, it can be shown that recursively applying this result for
O(\log n)
iterations accumulates a total sampling error of
O(\varepsilon\log n)
, while reducing the number of rows down to
\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-2/p}
, which matches the number of rows that we claim for sensitivity sampling in
Theorem

1.5
. By rescaling
\varepsilon
by an
O(\log n)
factor, we can obtain a total error of
\varepsilon
while only losing polylogarithmic factors in
n
. This is carried out in
Lemma

7.3
. Thus, we may again carry out the reduction argument as described in
Section

2.4
.

2.5.3
Root Leverage Score Sampling,
p<2

For our root leverage score sampling theorem
Theorem

1.9
, we take a conceptually different approach for obtaining the leverage score bound required in
Lemma

2.1
. For sensitivity sampling, our idea was to bound the leverage scores by relating them to the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities, which were controlled by the sensitivity sampling process. For root leverage score sampling, however, the idea is that by sampling by the root leverage scores, we directly control the
leverage scores
of the sampled matrix
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
, rather than the sensitivity scores. Indeed, one can show that if the sampling probabilities
p_{i}
satisfy
p_{i}\geq\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})^{p/2}/\alpha
, and if
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq(1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the leverage scores of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
are bounded by

\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq 2\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{1}{p_{i}^{2/p}}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq 2\frac{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})}{p_{i}^{2/p}}

(6)

which is at most
2\alpha^{2/p}
. However, it is not clear that
\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\geq(1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
should hold at all, since
\mathbf{S}
is only a subspace embedding for
\ell_{p}
and not
\ell_{2}
. For this intuition to go through, we require an auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding that preserves both
\ell_{p}
norms and
\ell_{2}
norms, with flat
\ell_{2}
leverage scores.

To make this argument work, we crucially use the fact that the bound in
Lemma

2.1
does not depend polynomially on the number of rows of the input matrix. Thus, we can afford to construct
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}
to have many rows, as long as its leverage scores are controlled. Thus, in
Lemma

7.5
, we take the approach of constructing an
\ell_{p}
isometry of
\mathbf{A}
by splitting every row
\mathbf{a}_{i}
into
k
copies of
\mathbf{a}_{i}/k^{1/p}
, where we will set
k=1/\alpha
. Note then that after splitting, every row contains at most a
1/k
fraction of the
\ell_{2}
mass, so the
\ell_{2}
leverage scores are all at most
\alpha
(in fact, all
\ell_{q}
sensitivities for any
q
are at most
\alpha
). Furthermore, crucially, the
\ell_{2}
norm will be preserved up to a factor of
\alpha^{1/p-1/q}
. Then altogether, we can fix (
6
) and instead bound

\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq 2\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{1}{p_{i}^{2/p}}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\alpha^{2/p-1}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}

which is at most
2\alpha
, where
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
is the concatenation of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{S}^{\prime}\mathbf{A}
. These ideas lead to our
Theorem

1.9
.

2.5.4
Leverage Score + Sensitivity Sampling,
p>2

Finally, for our
Theorem

1.10
, we take essentially the same approach as the recursive form of our sensitivity sampling result for
p>2
in
Theorem

1.5
, except that we improve our flattening approach by flattening
leverage scores
as well, which is formalized in
Lemma

7.9
. Note that when we only flatten
\ell_{p}
sensitivities using
Lemma

4.4
, then the resulting bound on the leverage scores is just roughly the average
\ell_{p}
sensitivity, which is
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n
. We show that by also flattening the leverage scores, we can improve this bound to

\left\lparen\frac{d}{n}\right\rparen^{2/p}\left\lparen\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}\right\rparen^{1-2/p}.

Because
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
is always at least
d/2
for
p>2
due to our
Theorem

1.7
, this is always better than the previous bound of
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n
, which ultimately leads to our improved sampling algorithm
Theorem

1.10
.

We note that we do not obtain a corresponding one-shot sampling algorithm, where the main difficulty is in constructing an auxiliary
\ell_{p}
subspace embedding that has few rows, flat leverage scores, does not increase
\ell_{p}
norms, and does not decrease
\ell_{2}
norms. Nonetheless, the recursive sampling procedure still leads to an efficient algorithm.

3
Conclusion and Future Directions

Our work introduces a new analysis for sensitivity sampling for
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings, which breaks a previous general sampling barrier of
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})d)
samples via a simple union bound argument, to obtain an improved bound of
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p})
samples for
p<2
and
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-2/p})
samples for
p>2
. We also present other novel results for sampling algorithms for
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings based on our techniques, showing that the popular root leverage score sampling algorithm yields a bound of
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-4/p}d)
for
p<2
, as well as an improved
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}d^{2/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-4/p})
bound for
p>2
using a recursive sampling algorithm that combines
\ell_{p}
sensitivity flattening with leverage score flattening. Our improved analyses of sensitivity sampling as well as our novel leverage score and sensitivity flattening algorithm give the best known sampling guarantees for a number of structured regression problems with small arbitrary noise.

We conclude with several open questions. Perhaps the most natural is to completely resolve Question
1.4
by characterizing the sample complexity of sensitivity sampling. We conjecture that a sample complexity of
\tilde{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}(\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})+d))
is possible for
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings, and perhaps for more broad settings where sensitivity sampling applies as well. Furthermore, for
p>2
, we believe it is of interest to obtain this bound even without the use of sensitivity sampling via other methods. Finally, we raise the question of obtaining sampling algorithms for subspace embeddings for the Huber loss with nearly optimal sample complexity, for which our results may be useful.

4
Properties of
\ell_{p}
Sensitivities

4.1
Monotonicity of Max
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity

We first provide proofs of
Lemma

2.2
and
Lemma

2.3
. The results are similar to results used in
[
BDM+20
,
MMWY22
]
. In particular, it generalizes Lemma 4.6 of
[
BDM+20
]
and is a simplification of a specific instance of Lemma C.3 of
[
MMWY22
]
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.2
.

We have that

\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{q}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q-p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q-p}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{p},

so

\frac{\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{\infty}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{\infty}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}/\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q-p}}=\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}}.

∎

Proof of
Lemma

2.3
.

Since
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}n^{1/p-1/q}
, we have that

\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{q}}\leq\frac{\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{q}\cdot n^{1-q/p}}\leq\frac{\lVert\mathbf{y}\rVert_{\infty}^{q}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{q}\cdot n^{1-q/p}}=\left\lparen\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\right\rparen^{q/p}n^{q/p-1}.

∎

4.2
Total Sensitivity

We now derive bounds on the total
\ell_{p}
sensitivity.

4.2.1
Sampling Preserves Total Sensitivity

Lemma 4.1

(Sampling Preserves Total Sensitivity)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathbf{S}
be a random
\ell_{p}
sampling matrix such that with probability at least
3/4
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=(1\pm 1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

simultaneously for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, with probability at least
1/2
,

\Pr\left\{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})\leq 8\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

We have that

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}.

We are guaranteed that

\Pr\left\{\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2\right\}\geq\frac{3}{4}.

On the other hand, we have that

\E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}]\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})=\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})

so by Markov’s inequality,

\Pr\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\leq 4\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq\frac{3}{4}.

By a union bound,

\Pr\left\{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})\leq 8\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

∎

We also prove a high probability and high accuracy version of
Lemma

4.1
.

Lemma 4.2

(Sensitivity Sampling Preserves Total Sensitivity: High Probability and Accuracy)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
0<\varepsilon,\delta<1
. Let
\mathbf{S}
be a random
\ell_{p}
sampling matrix such that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

simultaneously for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, suppose that

\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}}{q_{i}}\leq M\coloneqq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{3\log\frac{2}{\delta}}

for every
i\in[n]
. Then, with probability at least
1-2\delta
,

\Pr\left\{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})=(1\pm O(\varepsilon))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq 1-2\delta.

Proof.

The proof follows
Lemma

4.1
. Just as in
Lemma

4.1
, we have that

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\sup_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}.

Similarly,

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\inf_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}.

Furthermore, since
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}/q_{i}\leq M
,
\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/M
is a random variable bounded by
1
, with

\E\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{M}\right]=\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{M}\geq\frac{3}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log\frac{2}{\delta}.

Thus by Chernoff bounds, we have that

\Pr\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{S}_{i,i}^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})=(1\pm\varepsilon)\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq 1-\delta.

We conclude by a union bound as in
Lemma

4.1
.
∎

4.2.2
Total Sensitivity Lower Bounds

We start with the classical result that the total
\ell_{2}
sensitivity is exactly
d
:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and let
\mathbf{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be an orthnormal basis for the column space of
\mathbf{A}
. Then,

\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})=\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}

and

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})=\left\lVert\mathbf{U}\right\rVert_{F}^{2}=d.

Proof.

We have that

\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}=\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{U}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}=\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}.

∎

We now use
Lemma

4.3
together with
Lemma

2.2
and
Lemma

2.3
to derive lower bounds on
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

By using a simple argument based on “splitting rows” (see, e.g.,
[
LT91
,
CP15
,
CD21
,
MMWY22
]
), it is possible to assume without loss of generality that the maximum
\ell_{p}
sensitivity is related to the average
\ell_{p}
sensitivity, up to a factor of
2
:

Lemma 4.4

(
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity Flattening)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
C\geq 1
. Then, there exists a
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
for
m=(1+1/C)n
such that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})=\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})
, and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i^{\prime}}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n
for every
i^{\prime}\in[m]
.

Proof of
Lemma

4.4
.

Suppose that for any row
\mathbf{a}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
\mathbf{A}
for
i\in[n]
with
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\geq C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n
, we replace the row with
k\coloneqq\left\lceil\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/(C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n)\right\rceil
copies of
\mathbf{a}_{i}/k^{1/p}
to form a new matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. Then, we add at most

\sum_{i:\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\geq\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}\left\lceil\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}\right\rceil-1\leq\sum_{i:\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\geq\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}=\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}=\frac{n}{C}

rows. Furthermore, we clearly have that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and also for any row
i^{\prime}\in[m]
that comes from row
i\in[n]
in the original matrix,

\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}](i^{\prime})\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/n}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq\frac{C\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}.

Finally, it is also clear that the sum of the sensitivities is also preserved, since the sum of the sensitivities of the
k
copies of each row
i\in[n]
in the original matrix is
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
.
∎

We can now prove
Theorem

1.7
:

Proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n\times d}
be the matrix given by
Lemma

4.4
applied with
C=1
. Then for
p>2
, we have by
Lemma

2.2
that

\frac{d}{n}\leq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{2}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq\frac{2\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}

and for
p<2
, we have by
Lemma

2.3
that

\frac{d}{n}\leq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{2}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq\left\lparen\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\right\rparen^{2/p}n^{2/p-1}\leq\left\lparen\frac{2\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}\right\rparen^{2/p}n^{2/p-1}=\frac{2^{2/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p}}{n}

which yield the claimed results.
∎

4.2.3
Random Matrices Have Small Total Sensitivity

We show that the above lower bounds can be tight, up to logarithmic factors. We will use Dvoretzky’s theorem, which can be found in, e.g., Fact 15 in
[
SW18
]
:

Theorem 4.5

(Dvoretzky’s Theorem)
.

Let
1\leq p<2
. Let
n=(d/\varepsilon)^{O(p)}
be sufficiently large, and let
\mathbf{A}
be a suitably scaled random
n\times d
Gaussian matrix. Then, with probability at least
99/100
, we have for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}.

This gives a proof of
Theorem

1.6
:

Proof of
Theorem

1.6
.

By applying
Theorem

4.5
with
\varepsilon=1/2
, we have that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=\Theta(n)\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}
with probability at least
99/100
. Note also that

\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}\leq O(\sqrt{d\log n})=O(\sqrt{d\log d})

which also happens with probability at least
99/100
. By a union bound, both events happen with probability at least
98/100
.

Now for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with unit
\ell_{2}
norm, we have that

\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}\leq O(d\log d)^{p/2}.

Thus,

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\leq n\cdot\max_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}=1}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq n\cdot\max_{i=1}^{n}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}=1}\frac{O(d\log d)^{p/2}}{\Theta(n)}=O(d\log d)^{p/2}.

∎

4.3
Structured Matrices with Small Sensitivity,
p>2

Proof of
Lemma

1.11
.

Let
r
be an integer such that
2^{r}\leq p<2^{r+1}
. Then, for each
i\in[n]
, we may write

\mathbf{a}_{i}=\mathbf{k}_{i}+\mathbf{s}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{i,j}\mathbf{v}_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\beta_{i,j}\mathbf{e}_{i_{j}}

where
\mathbf{v}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
j\in[k]
. Then, the tensor product
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
of
\mathbf{a}_{i}
with itself
2^{r}
times can be written as a linear combination of tensor products
\mathbf{y}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbf{y}_{2^{r}}
, where each
\mathbf{y}_{q}
for
q\in[2^{r}]
is one of
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k},\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{e}_{i_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{i_{s}}\}
. Thus,
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\otimes r}
lies in the span of at most
(k+s)^{2^{r}}
vectors, for a fixed choice of
\mathbf{e}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{e}_{i_{2}},\dots,\mathbf{e}_{i_{s}}
. Since there are at most
d^{s}
possible choices of the sparsity pattern, every
\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
for
i\in[n]
lies in the span of at most
d^{\prime}\coloneqq d^{s}(k+s)^{2^{r}}
vectors. That is, if
\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
is the Khatri-Rao
2^{r}
th power of
\mathbf{A}
, then
\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
is a rank
d^{\prime}
matrix. Then, we have that

\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}=(\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2^{r}})^{p/2^{r}}=(\left\langle\mathbf{a}_{i},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle^{2^{r}})^{p/2^{r}}=\left\lvert\left\langle\mathbf{a}_{i}^{\otimes 2^{r}},\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\right\rangle\right\rvert^{p/2^{r}}

so

\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}=\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2^{r}}](i)\right\rvert^{p/2^{r}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\right\rVert_{p/2^{r}}^{p/2^{r}}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p/2^{r}}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p/2^{r}}^{p/2^{r}}}

that is, the
\ell_{p/2^{r}}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
upper bound the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}
. Since
p/2^{r}\leq 2
, the total
\ell_{p/2^{r}}
sensitivity of
\mathbf{A}^{\otimes 2^{r}}
is bounded by its rank, which is
d^{\prime}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
1.14
.

Let
r
be an integer such that
2^{r}\leq p<2^{r+1}
. Fix some
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k(q+1)}
. Now consider the vector
\left\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle
, where
\mathbf{a}
is a
k(q+1)
-dimensional vector of monomials of degree
0
through
q
of the indeterminate variables
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
, that is,

\mathbf{a}=(1,a_{1},a_{1}^{2},\dots,a_{1}^{q},\quad 1,a_{2},a_{2}^{2},\dots,a_{2}^{q},\quad\dots,\quad 1,a_{k},a_{k}^{2},\dots,a_{k}^{q}).

Then,
\left\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle
is a degree
q
polynomial in the indeterminates
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
with coefficients specified by
\mathbf{x}
, so
\left\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle^{2^{r}}
is a polynomial in the indeterminates
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
, such that every monomial term is at most degree
2^{r}q
in each variable. Note that there are at most
k
variables, so there can be at most
(2^{r}q+1)^{k}
possible monomials, by choosing the degree of each of the monomials. Let
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
denote the coefficients of this polynomial in the monomial basis, for a given set of original coefficients
\mathbf{x}
.

Now consider the matrix
V^{q}(\mathbf{A})
. Then, for a fixed
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{k(q+1)}
,
[V^{q}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}](i)^{2^{r}}
is the evaluation of
\left\langle\mathbf{a},\mathbf{x}\right\rangle^{2^{r}}
at the
i
th row
\mathbf{a}_{i}
of
\mathbf{A}
for the indeterminates
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
, so it can be written as the linear combination of at most
(2^{r}q+1)^{k}
monomials evaluated at
\mathbf{a}_{i}
, with coefficients
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
. Thus,
[V^{q}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}](i)^{2^{r}}=\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
for some
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
with rank at most
(2^{r}q+1)^{k}
.

Finally, note that

\left\lvert[V^{q}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}=(\left\lvert[V^{q}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2^{r}})^{p/2^{r}}=\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}^{\prime}](i)\right\rvert^{p/2^{r}}.

Thus, the total
\ell_{p}
sensitivity of
V^{q}(\mathbf{A})
is bounded by the total
\ell_{p/2^{r}}
sensitivity of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
, which is at most
(2^{r}q+1)^{k}\leq(pq+1)^{k}
.
∎

4.4
Total
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity Under Perturbations

Proof of
Lemma

1.15
.

For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have that

\displaystyle\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\pm\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\pm\sqrt{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\pm\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sqrt{n}}\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\pm\frac{\sigma_{\min}}{\sqrt{n}}\frac{1}{\sigma_{\min}}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\pm\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

\displaystyle=(1\pm 1/2)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}

so

\frac{\left\lvert[(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p-1}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}+2^{p-1}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{E})\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}+2^{p}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}.

The first term is clearly bounded by
2^{p}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
for any
\mathbf{x}
. On the other hand, the second term is bounded by

2^{p}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}n^{p/2}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{E}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\sigma_{\min}^{p}}{n^{p/2+1}}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}\frac{1}{n^{p/2+1}}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2^{p}\frac{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}{n\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}=\frac{2^{p}}{n}.

Thus, the total sensitivity is bounded by

2^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})+\frac{1}{n}=2^{p}(\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})+1).

∎

5
Entropy Estimates

In this section, we collect our results on estimates on various metric entropies, which are needed for our chaining arguments. Our results here are based on similar results given by
[
BLM89
]
. However, we modify their arguments to only depend on leverage scores and
\ell_{p}
sensitivities, rather than using Lewis weights
[
Lew78
,
BLM89
]
.

5.1
Preliminaries

We first recall general definitions from convex geometry that are relevant to this section.

Definition 5.1

(
d_{X}
-balls)
.

Let
d_{X}
be a metric on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, for
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
t\geq 0
, we define the
d_{X}
-ball of radius
t

B_{d}(\mathbf{x},t)
to be

B_{X}(\mathbf{x},t)\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:d_{X}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\leq t\right\}.

Definition 5.2

(Covering numbers and metric entropy)
.

Let
K,T\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be two convex bodies. Then, the
covering number

E(K,T)
is defined as

E(K,T)\coloneqq\min\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\exists\{\mathbf{x}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k},K\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+T)\right\}.

If
d_{X}
is a metric and
t>0
a radius, then
E(K,d_{X},t)
is defined as

E(K,d_{X},t)\coloneqq E(K,B_{X}(0,t))

(see
Definition

5.1
). The
metric entropy
is the logarithm of the covering number.

Next, we introduce some notation that is specific to our setting of
\ell_{p}
subspace embeddings.

Definition 5.3
.

For a matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
p\geq 1
, we define the ball

B^{p}(\mathbf{A})\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq 1\right\}.

We simply write
B^{p}
if
\mathbf{A}
is clear from context.

5.2
Dual Sudakov Minoration

One powerful tool for bounding covering numbers for covers of the Euclidean ball is the
dual Sudakov minoration
theorem, which bounds covering numbers in terms of the so-called
Levy mean
:

Definition 5.4

(Levy mean)
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
be a norm. Then, the
Levy mean of
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
is defined to be

M_{X}\coloneqq\frac{\E_{\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}\right\rVert_{X}}{\E_{\mathbf{g}\in\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})}\left\lVert\mathbf{g}\right\rVert_{2}}.

Bounds on the Levy mean imply bounds for covering the Euclidean ball by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
-balls via the following result:

Theorem 5.5

(Dual Sudakov minoration, Proposition 4.2 of
[
BLM89
]
)
.

Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X}
be a norm, and let
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
denote the Euclidean ball in
d
dimensions. Then,

\log E(B,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X},t)\leq O(d)\frac{M_{X}^{2}}{t^{2}}

5.3
Entropy Estimates for
p>2

We now use the preceding results to obtain the entropy estimates necessary to prove our main result for
p>2
. We start by bounding the Levy mean for the norm defined by
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{q}
for some matrix
\mathbf{A}
.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
q\geq 2
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Then,

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})}\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}\rVert_{q}\leq n^{1/q}\sqrt{q\cdot\tau}.

Proof.

We have for every
i\in[n]
that

\E\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}](i)\rvert^{q}=\frac{2^{q/2}\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}}\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\rVert_{2}^{q}\leq q^{q/2}\cdot\tau^{q/2}

since
[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}](i)
is distributed as a Gaussian random variable. Then by Jensen’s inequality and linearity of expectation,

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})}\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}\rVert_{q}\leq\left\lparen\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{d})}\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{g}\rVert_{q}^{q}\right\rparen^{1/q}=\left\lparen n\cdot q^{q/2}\cdot\tau^{q/2}\right\rparen^{1/q}=n^{1/q}\sqrt{q\cdot\tau}

∎

By combining the above calculation with
Theorem

5.5
, we obtain the following:

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Then,

\log E(B^{2},B^{q},t)\leq O(1)\frac{n^{2/q}q\cdot\tau}{t^{2}}

Proof.

For
\mathbf{A}
orthonormal,
B^{2}(\mathbf{A})=B^{2}
is isometric to the Euclidean ball in
d
dimensions, and thus
Theorem

5.5
applies.
∎

We also get a similar result for
q=\infty
, by applying
Corollary

5.7
with
q=O(\log n)
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Then,

\log E(B^{2},B^{\infty},t)\leq O(1)\frac{(\log n)\cdot\tau}{t^{2}}

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}\leq n^{1/q}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}=O(1)\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}

for
q=O(\log n)
.
∎

5.4
Entropy Estimates for
p<2

By interpolation, we can improve the bound in
Corollary

5.7
, which is needed for our results for
p<2
:

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
2<r<\infty
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Let
1\leq t\leq\poly(d)
. Then,

\log E(B^{2},B^{r},t)\leq O(1)\frac{1}{(t/2)^{2r/(r-2)}}\cdot\left\lparen\frac{r}{r-2}\log d+\log n\right\rparen\tau

Proof.

Let
q>r
, and let
0<\theta<1
satisfy

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1-\theta}{2}+\frac{\theta}{q}

Then by Hölder’s inequality, we have for any
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
that

\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{r}=\left\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{r(1-\theta)}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{r\theta}\right\rparen^{1/r}\leq\left\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{2}\right\rparen^{(1-\theta)/2}\left\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{q}\right\rparen^{\theta/q}=\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{q}^{\theta}

Then for any
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in B^{2}
, we have

\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{r}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{1-\theta}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{q}^{\theta}\leq 2\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{q}^{\theta}

so

\log E(B^{2},B^{r},t)\leq\log E(B^{2},B^{q},(t/2)^{1/\theta})\leq O(1)\frac{n^{2/q}q\cdot\tau}{(t/2)^{2/\theta}}

by
Corollary

5.7
. Now, we have

\frac{2}{\theta}=2\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q}}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}}=\frac{q-2}{q}\frac{2r}{r-2}

so by taking
q=O(\frac{r}{r-2}\log d+\log n)
, we have that
n^{2/q}=O(1)
and
(t/2)^{1/\theta}=\Theta(1)(t/2)^{2r/(r-2)}
, so we conclude as claimed.
∎

Using
Lemma

5.9
, we obtain the following analogue of
Corollary

5.7
for
p<2
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
. Then,

\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t)\leq O(1)\frac{1}{t^{p}}\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n\right\rparen\tau.

Proof.

In order to bound a covering of
B^{p}
by
B^{\infty}
, we first cover
B^{p}
by
B^{2}
, and then use
Corollary

5.8
to cover
B^{2}
by
B^{\infty}
.

We will first bound
E(B^{p},B^{2},t)
using
Lemma

5.9
. For each
k\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{E}_{k}\subseteq B^{p}
be a maximal subset of
B^{p}
such that for each distinct
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
,
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}>8^{k}t
, with
\mathcal{E}_{k}\coloneqq\{0\}
for

. Note then that

\lvert\mathcal{E}_{k}\rvert\geq E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k}t).

By averaging, for each
k
, there exists
\mathbf{y}^{(k)}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}
such that if

\mathcal{F}_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{E}_{k}:\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{(k)}\rVert_{2}\leq 8^{k+1}t\right\},

then

\lvert\mathcal{F}_{k}\rvert\geq\frac{\lvert\mathcal{E}_{k}\rvert}{E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k+1}t)}\geq\frac{E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k}t)}{E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k+1}t)}

We now use this observation to construct an
\ell_{p^{\prime}}
-packing of
B^{2}
, where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Let

\mathcal{G}_{k}\coloneqq\left\{\frac{1}{8^{k+1}t}(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{(k)}):\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{F}_{k}\right\}.

Then,
\mathcal{G}_{k}\subseteq B^{2}
and
\mathcal{G}_{k}\subseteq B^{p}\cdot 2/8^{k+1}t
, and
\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\rVert_{2}>1/8
for every distinct
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
. Then by Hölder’s inequality,

\frac{1}{8^{2}}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\leq\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\leq\frac{4}{8^{k+1}t}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}

so
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{p^{\prime}}\geq 2\cdot 8^{k-2}t
. Thus,
\mathcal{G}_{k}
is an
\ell_{p^{\prime}}
-packing of
B^{2}
, so

\log E(B^{2},B^{p^{\prime}},8^{k-2}t)\geq\log\lvert\mathcal{G}_{k}\rvert=\log\lvert\mathcal{F}_{k}\rvert\geq\log E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k}t)-\log E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k+1}t).

(7)

Summing over
k
gives

\displaystyle\log E(B^{p},B^{2},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\log E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k}t)-\log E(B^{p},B^{2},8^{k+1}t)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\log E(B^{2},B^{p^{\prime}},8^{k-2}t)

(
7
)

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\frac{1}{(t/2)^{2p^{\prime}/(p^{\prime}-2)}}\cdot\left\lparen\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}-2}\log d+\log n\right\rparen\tau

Lemma

5.9
and
Corollary

5.8

\displaystyle=O(1)\frac{1}{(t/2)^{2p/(2-p)}}\cdot\left\lparen\frac{p}{2-p}\log d+\log n\right\rparen\tau

where we take
p^{\prime}/(p^{\prime}-2)=1
for

. Using this and
Corollary

5.8
, we now bound

\displaystyle\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t)

\displaystyle\leq\log E(B^{p},B^{2},\lambda)+\log E(B^{2},B^{\infty},t/\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\frac{1}{(\lambda/2)^{2p/(2-p)}}\cdot\left\lparen\frac{p}{2-p}\log d+\log n\right\rparen\tau+O(1)\frac{(\log n)\cdot\tau}{(t/\lambda)^{2}}

for any
\lambda\in[1,t]

\frac{1}{(\lambda/2)^{2p/(2-p)}}=\frac{(\lambda/2)^{2}}{t^{2}},

which gives

(\lambda/2)^{2p/(2-p)}=\left\lparen t^{2}\right\rparen^{\frac{2p/(2-p)}{2+2p/(2-p)}}=t^{p}

O(1)\frac{1}{t^{p}}\left\lparen\frac{1}{2-p}\log d+\log n\right\rparen\tau.

∎

6
Bounding a Gaussian Process

Using our estimates from
Section

5
, we study estimates on the Gaussian process given by

X:\mathbf{y}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{g}_{i}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p},\qquad\mathbf{y}\in B^{p}(\mathbf{A})

for
\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})
, and in particular, tail bounds and moment bounds on the quantity

\sup_{\mathbf{y}\in B^{p}(\mathbf{A})}\lvert X(\mathbf{y})\rvert=\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq 1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{g}_{i}\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\rvert^{p}\right\rvert

As we show later, this Gaussian process bounds the error of the sensitivity sampling estimate. Our techniques here are based on similar results obtained by
[
LT91
]
.

The main tool is Dudley’s tail inequality for Gaussian processes:

Theorem 6.1

(Theorem 8.1.6,
[
Ver18
]
)
.

Let
(X(t))_{t\in T}
be a Gaussian process with pseudo-metric
d_{X}(s,t)\coloneqq\left\lVert X(s)-X(t)\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\E(X(s)-X(t))^{2}}
and let

\diam(T)\coloneqq\sup\left\{d_{X}(s,t):s,t\in T\right\}

Then, there is a constant
C=O(1)
such that for every
z\geq 0
,

\Pr\left\{\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\geq C\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(T,d_{X},u)}~du+z\cdot\diam(T)\right]\right\}\leq 2\exp(-z^{2})

The expectation bound version of the theorem will also be useful:

Theorem 6.2

(Theorem 8.1.3,
[
Ver18
]
)
.

Let
(X(t))_{t\in T}
be a Gaussian process with pseudo-metric
d_{X}(s,t)\coloneqq\left\lVert X(s)-X(t)\right\rVert_{2}=\sqrt{\E(X(s)-X(t))^{2}}
. Then, there is a constant
C=O(1)
such that

\E\sup_{t\in T}X_{t}\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(T,d_{X},u)}~du.

6.1
Bounds on
d_{X}

We first bound
d_{X}
as well as the
d_{X}
-diameter of
B^{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Define the pseudo-metric

d_{X}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\coloneqq\left\lparen\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{g}_{i}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{g}_{i}\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{2}\right\rparen^{1/2}

Let
\sigma\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
. Then, for any
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\in B_{p}(\mathbf{A})
,

d_{X}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\leq\begin{cases}2\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p/2}&p<2\\
2p\cdot\sigma^{1/2-1/p}\cdot\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}&p>2\end{cases}

Proof.

Note first that by expanding out the square and noting that
\E[\mathbf{g}_{i}\mathbf{g}_{j}]=\mathbbm{1}(i=j)
, we have

d_{X}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})=\left\lparen\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}-\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p})^{2}\right\rparen^{1/2}

For
p<2
, we bound this as

\displaystyle d_{X}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}-\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p/2}-\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p/2})^{2}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p/2}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p/2})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p/2})^{2}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p/2}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p/2})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p}.

For
p>2
, we have by convexity that

\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}-\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p}\leq p\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p-1})

and that
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sigma^{1/p}
, so we have

\displaystyle d_{X}(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}-\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq p^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{2}(\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p-1})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{2}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{2p-2}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{2p-2}

\displaystyle\leq 2p^{2}\max\{\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty},\lVert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\rVert_{\infty}\}^{p-2}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert\mathbf{y}(i)\rvert^{p}+\lvert\mathbf{y}^{\prime}(i)\rvert^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 4p^{2}\sigma^{1-2/p}\left\lVert\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}^{2}

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
. Let
\sigma\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
. Then, the diameter of
B^{p}(\mathbf{A})
with respect to
d_{X}
is bounded by

\diam(B^{p}(\mathbf{A}))\leq\begin{cases}4\cdot\sigma^{1/2}&p<2\\
4p\cdot\sigma^{1/2}&p>2\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
\mathbf{y}\in B^{p}(\mathbf{A})
, we have that
\left\lVert\mathbf{y}\right\rVert_{\infty}\leq\sigma^{1/p}
, so combining the triangle inequality and
Lemma

6.3
yields the result.
∎

6.2
Computing the Entropy Integral

We may now evaluate the entropy integral required in
Theorem

6.1
. We use the following calculus lemma:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
0<\lambda\leq 1
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{t}}~dt=\lambda\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\erfc(\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)})\leq\lambda\left\lparen\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\right\rparen

Proof.

We calculate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{t}}~dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}}^{\infty}x^{2}\exp(-x^{2})~dx

x=\sqrt{\log(1/t)}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}}^{\infty}x\cdot-2x\exp(-x^{2})~dx

\displaystyle=-\left\lparen x\exp(-x^{2})\Big|^{\infty}_{\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}}-\int_{\sqrt{\log(1/\lambda)}}^{\infty}\exp(-x^{2})~dx\right\rparen

integration by parts

\displaystyle=\lambda\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\lambda}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\erfc\left\lparen\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\lambda}}\right\rparen

∎

Lemma 6.6

(Entropy integral bound for
p<2
)
.

Let
1\leq p<2
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
and let
\sigma\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(B^{p},d_{X},t)}~dt\leq O(\tau^{1/2})\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n\right\rparen^{1/2}\log\frac{d\sigma}{\tau}

Proof.

Note that it suffices to integrate the entropy integral to
\diam(B^{p}(\mathbf{A}))
rather than
\infty
, which is at most
4\sigma^{1/2}
for
p<2
and
4p\sigma^{1/2}
for
p>2
by
Lemma

6.4
.

By
Lemma

6.3
, we have that

\log E(B^{p},d_{X},t)\leq\log E(B^{p},2\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p/2},t)=\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},(t/2)^{2/p})

For small radii less than
\lambda
for a parameter
\lambda
to be chosen, we use a standard volume argument, which shows that

\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t)\leq O(d)\log\frac{n}{t}

so

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t)}~dt=\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{d\log\frac{n}{t}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sqrt{d\log n}+\sqrt{d}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{t}}~dt

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sqrt{d\log n}+\sqrt{d}\left\lparen\lambda\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\lambda}}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\lambda\right\rparen

\displaystyle\leq O(\lambda)\sqrt{d\log\frac{n}{\lambda}}

On the other hand, for large radii larger than
\lambda
, we use the bounds of
Lemma

5.10
, which gives

\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},(t/2)^{2/p})\leq O(1)\frac{1}{t^{2}}\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n\right\rparen\tau

so the entropy integral gives a bound of

O(1)\left[\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n\right\rparen\tau\right]^{1/2}\int_{\lambda}^{4p\sigma^{1/2}}\frac{1}{t}~dt=O(1)\left[\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n\right\rparen\tau\right]^{1/2}\log\frac{4p\sigma^{1/2}}{\lambda}.

We choose
\lambda=\sqrt{\tau/d}
, which yields the claimed conclusion.
∎

An analogous result and proof holds for
p>2
.

Lemma 6.7

(Entropy integral bound for
p>2
)
.

Let
2<p<\infty
and let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be orthonormal. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\left\lVert\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}
and let
\sigma\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
. Then,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(B^{p},d_{X},t)}~dt\leq O(p\tau^{1/2})\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\log\frac{p^{2}d\sigma}{\tau}

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case of
p<2
. We again introduce a parameter
\lambda
. For radii below
\lambda
, the bound is the same as
Lemma

6.6
. For radii above
\lambda
, we use
Lemma

6.3
to bound

\log E(B^{p},d_{X},t)\leq\log E(B^{p},2p\cdot\sigma^{1/2-1/p}\cdot\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty},t)\leq\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t/2p\cdot\sigma^{1/2-1/p})

Then by
Corollary

5.8
,

\displaystyle\log E(B^{p},B^{\infty},t/2p\cdot\sigma^{1/2-1/p})

\displaystyle\leq\log E(B^{2},B^{\infty},t/2p\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p})

\displaystyle\leq O(p^{2})\frac{(\log n)\cdot\tau}{t^{2}}\cdot(\sigma n)^{1-2/p}

so the entropy integral gives a bound of

O(p\tau^{1/2})\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\int_{\lambda}^{\diam(B^{p}(\mathbf{A}))}\frac{1}{t}~dt\leq O(p\tau^{1/2})\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\log\frac{p\sigma^{1/2}}{\lambda}

Choosing
\lambda=\sqrt{\tau/d}
yields the claimed conclusion.
∎

6.3
Moment Bounds

Finally, using our tail bound from combining
Theorem

6.1
with the entropy bounds of
Section

6.2
and
Lemma

6.4
, we obtain the following moment bounds:

Lemma 6.8
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\tau\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})
and let
\sigma\geq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
. Let

\Lambda\coloneqq\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq 1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{g}_{i}\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\rvert^{p}\right\rvert

Let
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(B^{p}(\mathbf{A}),d_{X},u)}~du
and
\mathcal{D}=\diam(B^{p}(\mathbf{A}))
. Then,

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})}[\lvert\Lambda\rvert^{l}]\leq(2\mathcal{E})^{l}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})+O(\sqrt{l}\mathcal{D})^{l}

Proof.

By
Theorem

6.1
, we have for
T=B^{p}(\mathbf{A})
that

\Pr\left\{\Lambda\geq C\left[\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(T,d_{X},u)}~du+z\cdot\diam(T)\right]\right\}\leq 2\exp(-z^{2})

for a constant
C=O(1)
. Then,

\displaystyle\E[(\Lambda/\mathcal{D})^{l}]

\displaystyle=l\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{l}\Pr\{\Lambda\geq z\mathcal{D}\}~dz

\displaystyle\leq(2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})^{l+1}+l\int_{2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}z^{l}\Pr\{\Lambda\geq z\mathcal{D}\}~dz

\displaystyle\leq(2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})^{l+1}+l\int_{2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D}}^{\infty}z^{l}\Pr\{\Lambda\geq\mathcal{E}+(z/2)\mathcal{D}\}~dz

\displaystyle\leq(2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})^{l+1}+2l\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{l}\exp(-z^{2}/4)~dz

\displaystyle\leq(2\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})^{l+1}+O(l)^{l/2}

so

\E[\Lambda^{l}]\leq(2\mathcal{E})^{l}(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{D})+O(\sqrt{l}\mathcal{D})^{l}.

∎

7
Sampling Guarantees

We first reduce our proof of sampling guarantees to the problem of bounding a Gaussian process:

Lemma 7.1

(Reduction to Gaussian processes)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
\mathbf{S}
be a random
\ell_{p}
sampling matrix (
Definition

1.1
). Then,

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}\leq(2\pi)^{l/2}\E_{\mathbf{S}}\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l},

where
S\subseteq[n]
is the set of rows with sampling probability
q_{i}<1
.

Proof.

By a standard symmetrization argument
[
CP15
,
CD21
]
, we have that

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}\leq 2^{l}\E_{\mathbf{S},\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq 1}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in S}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l},

where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}\sim\{\pm 1\}^{n}
are independent Rademacher variables. In turn, the right hand side is bounded by

2^{l}(\pi/2)^{l/2}\E_{\mathbf{S},\mathbf{g}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}\leq 1}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l}

via a Rademacher-Gaussian comparison theorem (see, e.g., Equation 4.8 of
[
LT91
]
).
∎

7.1
Sensitivity Sampling,
p<2

Our first result is a sensitivity sampling guarantee for
p<2
.

Theorem 7.2

(Sensitivity Sampling for
p<2
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
1\leq p<2
. Let
\mathbf{S}
be a random
\ell_{p}
sampling matrix with sampling probabilities
q_{i}=\min\{1,1/n+\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})/\alpha\}
for an oversampling parameter
\alpha
set to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p-1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left[O(l\log n)^{2/p-1}\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log\frac{l\log n}{\varepsilon}\right\rparen(\log d)^{2}+l\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p-1}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen

for

l=O\left\lparen\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\log\log n+\log\frac{1}{2-p}+\log\frac{d}{\varepsilon}\right\rparen.

Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
, simultaneously for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}.

Furthermore, with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\mathbf{S}
samples

\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\log\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen

rows.

Proof.

Our approach is to bound

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}

for a large even integer
l
. Using
Lemma

7.1
, we first bound

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}\leq(2\pi)^{l/2}\E_{\mathbf{S}}\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l}

where
S=\{i\in[n]:q_{i}<1\}
. For simplicity of presentation, we assume
S=[n]
, which will not affect our proof.

By
Theorem

1.8
, there exists a matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times d}
with
m_{1}=O(d(\log d)^{3})
such that

\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm 1/2)\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\rVert_{p}^{p}

for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, because
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
in
Theorem

1.8
is constructed by random sampling,
Lemma

4.1
shows that
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq 8\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
(note that we only need existence of this matrix). We then construct a matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{2}\times d}
with
m_{2}=O(\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})+d(\log d)^{3})=O(\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}))
such that

\sigma\coloneqq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime})\leq\alpha,

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime})
, and
\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\rVert_{p}=\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}\rVert_{p}
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by viewing
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
as an
(m_{1}+\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}))\times d
matrix with all zeros except for the first
m_{1}
rows and then applying
Lemma

4.4
.

Now let

\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}\\
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\end{pmatrix}

be the
(m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}})\times d
matrix formed by the vertical concatenation of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}
with
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
, where
n_{\mathbf{S}}
is the number of rows sampled by
\mathbf{S}
.

Sensitivity Bounds for
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}
.

We will first bound the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}
. For any row
i
corresponding to a row of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime}
, the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities are already bounded by
\alpha
, and furthermore,
\ell_{p}
sensitivities can clearly only decrease with row additions. For any row
i
corresponding to a row of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}
that is sampled with probability
q_{i}<1
, we have that

\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2\frac{1}{q_{i}}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}}\leq 2\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{q_{i}}=2\alpha.

Thus, we have that
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime})\leq 2\alpha
for every row
i
of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}
.

With a bound on the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}
in hand, we may then convert this into a bound on the leverage scores of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}
using
Lemma

2.3
, which gives

\tau\coloneqq\max_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime})\leq(2\alpha)^{2/p}(m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}})^{2/p-1}

where
n_{\mathbf{S}}
is the number of nonzero entries of
\mathbf{S}
.

Moment Bounds on the Sampling Error.

We now fix a choice of
\mathbf{S}
, and define

F_{\mathbf{S}}\coloneqq\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert.

Note that the event that
n_{\mathbf{S}}
is at least

n_{\mathrm{thresh}}\coloneqq O(l\log n)\E[n_{S}]=O(l\log n)\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}),

occurs with probability at most
\poly(n)^{-l}
by Chernoff bounds over the randomness of
\mathbf{S}
, and

F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\leq\left[1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right]^{l}\leq(n+1)^{2l},

and thus this event contributes at most
\poly(n)^{-l}
to the moment bound
\E F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}
. Thus, we focus on bounding
\E F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}
conditioned on
n_{\mathbf{S}}\leq n_{\mathrm{thresh}}
. Define

G_{\mathbf{S}}\coloneqq\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert

for
\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})
. Then,

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq(1+2+F_{\mathbf{S}})\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

so

\displaystyle F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{l}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l}

(8)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{l}(1+2+F_{\mathbf{S}})^{l}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime\prime\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2l-1}(3^{l}+F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l})G_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}.

We then take expectations on both sides with respect to
\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})
, and bound the right hand side using
Lemma

6.8
, which gives

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})}G_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\leq\left\lparen 2\mathcal{E}\right\rparen^{l}\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\mathcal{D}}+O(\sqrt{l}\mathcal{D})^{l}

where
\mathcal{E}
is the entropy integral and
\mathcal{D}=4\sigma^{1/2}
is the diameter by
Lemma

6.4
. We have by
Lemma

6.6
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\leq O(\tau^{1/2})\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log(m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}})\right\rparen^{1/2}\log\frac{d\sigma}{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq O(\alpha^{1/p}(m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}})^{1/p-1/2})\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log(m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}})\right\rparen^{1/2}\log\frac{d\sigma}{\tau}

Thus, conditioned on
n_{\mathbf{S}}\leq n_{\mathrm{thresh}}
, we have that

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})}G_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\leq\left[O(\alpha^{1/p}n_{\mathrm{thresh}}^{1/p-1/2})\left\lparen\frac{\log d}{2-p}+\log n_{\mathrm{thresh}}\right\rparen^{1/2}\log d\right]^{l}+O(\sqrt{l}\sqrt{\alpha})^{l}.

Note that

\alpha^{1/p}n_{\mathrm{thresh}}^{1/p-1/2}=O(l\log n)^{1/p-1/2}\alpha^{1/p}(\alpha^{-1}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}))^{1/p-1/2}=O(l\log n)^{1/p-1/2}\alpha^{1/2}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{1/p-1/2},

which shows that

\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})}G_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\leq\varepsilon^{l}\delta

due to our choice of
\alpha
and
l
.

Now if we take conditional expectations on both sides of (
8
) conditioned on the event
\mathcal{F}
that
n_{\mathbf{S}}\leq n_{\mathrm{thresh}}
, then we have

\E[F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\mid\mathcal{F}]\leq 2^{2l-1}(3^{l}+\E[F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\mid\mathcal{F}])\varepsilon^{l}\delta\leq(3^{l}+\E[F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\mid\mathcal{F}])(4\varepsilon)^{l}\delta

which means

\E[F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\mid\mathcal{F}]\leq\frac{(12\varepsilon)^{l}\delta}{1-(4\varepsilon)^{l}\delta}\leq 2(12\varepsilon)^{l}\delta

for
(4\varepsilon)^{l}\delta\leq 1/2
. We thus have

\E[F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}]\leq\frac{(12\varepsilon)^{l}\delta}{1-(4\varepsilon)^{l}\delta}\leq 2(12\varepsilon)^{l}\delta+\poly(n)^{-l}

altogether. Finally, we have by a Markov bound that

F_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}\leq 2(12\varepsilon)^{l}+\frac{1}{\delta}\poly(n)^{l}\leq 3(12\varepsilon)^{l}

with probability at least
1-\delta
, which means that

F_{\mathbf{S}}\leq 3\cdot 12\varepsilon=36\varepsilon

with probability at least
1-\delta
. Rescaling
\varepsilon
by constant factors yields the claimed result.
∎

7.2
Sensitivity Sampling,
p>2

For
p>2
, we first need a construction of a matrix with a small number of rows and small sensitivity. While this construction can be made to be a randomized algorithm succeeding with high probability, it uses a sophisticated recursive sampling strategy. While this is necessary for our results later in Theorem
7.10
, such a complicated algorithm may be undesirable. In
Theorem

7.4
, we use this result to show that a more direct one-shot sensitivity sampling can in fact achieve a similar guarantee.

Lemma 7.3

(Recursive Sensitivity Sampling)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
2<p<\infty
. Let
0<\varepsilon<1
. Then, there exists a matrix
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
for

m=O(p^{2})\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-2/p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log(pd)^{2}\log\frac{pd}{\varepsilon}

such that

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
be the flattened isometric matrix given by
Lemma

4.4
with
C=4
, where
m\leq(5/4)n
. Then for all
i\in[m]
, we have that

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq 4\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}\leq 5\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})}{m}.

Now consider the random sampling matrix
\mathbf{S}
with sampling probabilities
q_{i}=1/2
. Note then that sampling with probability
q_{i}=1/2
and scaling by
1/q_{i}=2
corresponds to muliplying by the random variable
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}+1
, where
\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}
is a Rademacher variable. Thus,

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert=\E_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}+1)\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}-\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert=\E_{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert.

By
Lemma

7.1
and
Theorem

6.2
, this is bounded by

O(1)\int_{0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\log E(T,d_{X},u)}~du\leq O(p\tau^{1/2})\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\log\frac{p^{2}d\sigma}{\tau}

where
\tau
is an upper bound on the leverage scores of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
and
\sigma
is an upper bound on the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. By
Lemma

2.2
, we have that
\tau\leq\sigma
, and furthermore, we can take
\sigma=5\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})/m
. Thus, the resulting bound on the expected sampling error is at most

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}}\coloneqq O(p)\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{1-1/p}}{\sqrt{n}}(\log n)^{1/2}\log(pd)

so with probability at least
99/100
, the same bound holds up to a factor of
100
. Furthermore,
\mathbf{S}
samples
m/2\leq(5/8)n
rows in expectation, so by Markov’s inequality, it samples at most
(3/2)m/2\leq(15/16)n
rows with probability at least
1/3
. We also have that

\frac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{q_{i}}=2\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq 10\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})}{m}

so by
Lemma

4.2
, we have that

\Pr\left\{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=(1\pm O(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}}))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})\right\}\geq\frac{99}{100}.

By a union bound,
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
samples at most
(15/16)n
rows, has sampling error at most
\varepsilon_{n}
, and has
\ell_{p}
total sensitivity at most
(1+O(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}}))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
with probability at least
1/3-1/100-1/100>0
. Thus, such an instantiation of
\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
exists.

We now recursively apply our reasoning, by repeatedly applying the flattening and sampling operation. Note that each time we repeat this procedure, the number of rows goes down by a factor of
15/16
, while the total sensitivity and total sampling error accumulates. Let
\mathbf{A}_{l}
denote the matrix obtained after
l
recursive applications of this procedure and let
n_{l}
denote the number of rows of
\mathbf{A}_{l}

∎

Theorem 7.4

(Sensitivity Sampling for
p>2
)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}

\mathbf{S}

\ell_{p}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}

\displaystyle=O(p^{2})\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{1-2/p}(l\log n)^{1-2/p}\log(pd)\log\frac{l\log n}{\varepsilon}+O(p^{2})l

Furthermore, with probability at least

\mathbf{S}

\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-2/p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},p\right\rparen

Proof.

Our approach is to bound

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}

7.1

\E_{\mathbf{S}}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert^{l}\leq(2\pi)^{l/2}\E_{\mathbf{S}}\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{n})}\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\sum_{i\in S}\mathbf{g}_{i}\left\lvert[\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{p}\right\rvert^{l}

By

7.3

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}\times d}

for every

By

Thus, conditioned on

\displaystyle\E_{\mathbf{g}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{m_{2}+n_{\mathbf{S}}})}G_{\mathbf{S}}^{l}

\displaystyle\leq\left[O(p\alpha^{1/2})(\alpha(m_{2}+n_{\mathrm{thresh}}))^{1/2-1/p}\left\lparen\log(m_{2}+n_{\mathrm{thresh}})\right\rparen^{1/2}\log(pd)\right]^{l}+O(\sqrt{l}p\sqrt{\alpha})^{l}

and

Now if we take conditional expectations on both sides of (

Lemma 7.5

(Flattening All Sensitivities)

Let

Proof.

Let
k\coloneqq\left\lceil 1/\alpha\right\rceil

\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
for

th row

for every

∎

\delta/\Theta(\log\log n)
and accuracy
\varepsilon/\Theta(\log\log n)
recursively until the number of rows is at most

m=\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{4/p}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\log\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen

Then, with probability at least
1-\delta
, simultaneously for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}.

Proof.

We apply
Theorem

7.6
with failure probability
\delta/\Theta(\log\log n)
and accuracy
\varepsilon/\Theta(\log\log n)
recursively for at most
R=O(\log\log n)
rounds, until the number of rows is at most the claimed bound. By a union bound, we succeed at achieving
\varepsilon/\Theta(\log\log n)
sampling error and sampling bound on all
R
rounds, that is, for any number of rows
m
, we reduce the number of rows to at most

\frac{m^{1-p/2}d^{p/2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen.

We then apply the recurrence lemma
Lemma

7.7
on the logarithm of the above bound, so
\lambda=(1-p/2)
and

b=\log\left\lparen\frac{d^{p/2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen\right\rparen.

Then, after
i=O(\log\log n)
iterations, our log row count upper bound is

a_{i}=\log m_{i}=\frac{2}{p}\left\lparen b-(1-p/2)^{i}(b-(p/2)\log n)\right\rparen\leq\frac{2}{p}\left\lparen b+(1-p/2)^{i}(p/2)\log n\right\rparen\leq\frac{2}{p}(b+1)

or

m_{i}\leq O\left\lparen\frac{d^{p/2}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen\right\rparen^{2/p}=\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{4/p}}\poly\left\lparen\log n,\log\frac{1}{\delta},\frac{1}{2-p}\right\rparen

∎

7.4
Leverage Score +
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity Sampling,
p>2

We start with a flattening lemma for
p>2
, which shows how to slightly flatten leverage scores while preserving
\ell_{p}
norms, and with only a small blow up in the number of rows.

Lemma 7.9

(Flattening
\ell_{p}
Sensitivities and Leverage Scores)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and let
2<p<\infty
. Let
C\geq 1
. Then, there exists
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
for
m\leq(1+1/C)n
such that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}
and
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[m]}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in[n]}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A})

\displaystyle\max_{i\in[m]}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq(Cd/n)^{2/p}\max_{i\in[m]}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})^{1-2/p}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})

Proof.

The idea roughly follows
Lemma

4.4
, except that we split rows that have large leverage score, rather than rows that have large
\ell_{p}
sensitivity. For each
i\in[n]
, let
k_{i}=\left\lceil\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})/(Cd/n)\right\rceil
. Then for each
i\in[n]
such that
k_{i}>1
, we replace
i
th row
\mathbf{a}_{i}
of
\mathbf{A}
by
k_{i}
copies of
\mathbf{a}_{i}/k^{1/p}
. Clearly, we have that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}
for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore, the number of rows added is at most

\sum_{i:\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})\geq Cd/n}\left\lceil\frac{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})}{Cd/n}\right\rceil-1\leq\sum_{i:\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})\geq Cd/n}\frac{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})}{Cd/n}=\frac{d}{Cd/n}=\frac{n}{C}.

Next, note that for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

k_{i}\cdot\left\lvert k_{i}^{-2/p}[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}\geq\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}

since
k_{i}\geq 1
, so we have that
\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}\geq\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}
. Then, for any row
j\in[m]
that is a copy of row
i\in[n]
of
\mathbf{A}
, we have that

\boldsymbol{\tau}_{j}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})=\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq\sup_{\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\neq 0}\frac{k_{i}^{-2/p}\left\lvert[\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}](i)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}}\leq\frac{(Cd/n)^{2/p}}{\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})^{2/p}}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})=(Cd/n)^{2/p}\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A})^{1-2/p}.

Finally, it is clear that the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities can only decrease and that the total
\ell_{p}
sensitivity is preserved.
∎

Now using
Lemma

7.9
, we first obtain a construction of a small
\ell_{p}
approximate isometry in a way analogous to
Lemma

7.3
.

Theorem 7.10

(Recursive Leverage Score +
\ell_{p}
Sensitivity Sampling)
.

Let
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
and
2<p<\infty
. Let
0<\varepsilon,\delta<1
. Then, there exists an efficient algorithm producing a matrix
\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
for

m=O(p^{2})\frac{d^{2/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-4/p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\lparen\log\frac{pd}{\delta}\right\rparen^{2}\log\frac{pd}{\varepsilon}.

such that

\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})
.

Proof.

Our proof is almost identical to
Lemma

7.3
, but we will use high probability versions of the results, as we will directly use the recursive sampling procedure algorithmically. A similar algorithmic recursive sampling procedure is considered in
[
MMWY22
]
. We first replace
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
in
Lemma

7.3
with the matrix formed by first applying
Lemma

4.4
with
C=4
, and then applying
Lemma

7.9
with
C=4
. The resulting
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
has at most
(5/4)^{2}n=(25/16)n
rows, preserves
\ell_{p}
norms and
\ell_{p}
total sensitivity, and has

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}

(11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\tau}_{i}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq O(1)\left\lparen\frac{d}{n}\right\rparen^{2/p}\left\lparen\frac{\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})}{n}\right\rparen^{1-2/p}=O(1)\frac{d^{2/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{1-2/p}}{n}

We then again consider sampling half of the rows of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
via an
\ell_{p}
sampling matrix
\mathbf{S}
with
q_{i}=1/2
. By
Lemma

7.1
and
Theorem

6.1
, we then have that

\Pr\left\{\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert\leq O(p\tau^{1/2})\cdot(\sigma n)^{1/2-1/p}(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\log\frac{p^{2}d\sigma}{\tau}+4p\sigma^{1/2}\cdot z\right\}\geq 1-2\exp(z^{2})

where
\tau
is an upper bound on the leverage scores of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
and
\sigma
is an upper bound on the
\ell_{p}
sensitivities of
\mathbf{A}^{\prime}
. These are bounded by (
11
), and thus applying these bounds and setting
z=O(\log(n/\delta))
gives

\Pr\left\{\sup_{\left\lVert\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}=1}\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{S}\mathbf{A}^{\prime}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}-1\right\rvert\leq O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}^{\prime})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}}\left\lparen(\log n)^{1/2}\cdot\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n}{\delta}\right\rparen\right\}\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{\poly\log n}.

By a union bound, the same bound holds for the first
O(\log n)
recursive calls to our recursive sampling algorithm, up to a different
\poly\log n
factor in the denominator of the failure rate bound. Furthermore, by Chernoff bounds, we have that the number
n_{\mathbf{S}}
of rows sampled by
\mathbf{S}

\Pr\left\{n_{\mathbf{S}}\leq(25/32)(5/4)n<0.98n\right\}\geq 1-\exp\left\lparen-\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{16}\cdot\frac{25}{32}n\right\rparen\geq 1-\frac{\delta}{\poly\log n}

as long as
n\geq C\log\frac{1}{\delta}
for a sufficiently large constant
C
. By a union bound, the same bound also holds for the first
O(\log n)
recursive calls to our sampling algorithm. In this case, our sampling process terminates in at most
O(\log n)
rounds, so both the bound on
n_{\mathbf{S}}
and the sampling error bound hold for all
O(\log n)
rounds.

We now apply the same reasoning as
Lemma

7.3
to bound the total sampling error. Let
\mathbf{A}_{l}
denote the
n_{l}\times d
sampled matrix after
l
rounds of the recursive sampling procedure, and let

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}=O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}_{l})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}_{l}}\left\lparen(\log n_{l})^{1/2}\cdot\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n_{l}}{\delta}\right\rparen.

Then,

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l+1}}

\displaystyle=O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}_{l+1})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}_{l+1}}\left\lparen(\log n_{l+1})^{1/2}\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n_{l+1}}{\delta}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\geq(1-O(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}))O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}_{l})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}_{l+1}}\left\lparen(\log n_{l+1})^{1/2}\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n_{l+1}}{\delta}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{0.98}}(1-O(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}))O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}_{l})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}_{l}}\left\lparen(\log n_{l})^{1/2}\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n_{l}}{\delta}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\geq\frac{101}{100}\cdot\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}

so the sum of the
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}
are dominated by the last
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}
, up to a constant factor. Now let
L
be the smallest integer
l
such that
\varepsilon_{\mathbf{A}_{l}}\leq\varepsilon
. Then, we have that

\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A}_{L})\leq(1+O(\varepsilon))\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})

and thus

\left\lVert\mathbf{A}_{L}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}=(1\pm O(\varepsilon))\left\lVert\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\right\rVert_{p}^{p}

for every
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore,
n_{L}
satisfies

\varepsilon=O(p)\frac{d^{1/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{1-2/p}}{\sqrt{n}_{L}}\left\lparen(\log n_{L})^{1/2}\log(pd)+\log\frac{\log n_{L}}{\delta}\right\rparen

or

n_{L}=O(p^{2})\frac{d^{2/p}\mathfrak{S}^{p}(\mathbf{A})^{2-4/p}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left\lparen\log\frac{pd}{\delta}\right\rparen^{2}\log\frac{pd}{\varepsilon}.

∎

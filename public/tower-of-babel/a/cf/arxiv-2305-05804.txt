A step towards the tensorization of Sobolev spaces
Silvia Ghinassi, Vikram Giri, Elisa Negrini

Abstract
We prove that Sobolev spaces on Cartesian and warped products of metric spaces tensorize, only requiring that one of the factors is a doubling space supporting a Poincaré inequality.



Keywords:
Sobolev space, metric space, doubling, cartesian product, warped product. MSC2020 53C23, 46E35, 51F30.

1
Introduction

The theory of first order Sobolev spaces in abstract metric measure spaces became an independent field of study the late 90’s. Hajłasz
[
Ha96
]
defined the concept of Hajłasz gradient and subsequently the so-called Sobolev-Hajłasz spaces.
Heinonen and Koskela soon introduced the concept of upper gradient to replace the distributional gradient in more abstract spaces,
[
HK95
,
HK96
,
HK98
]
. Their definition requires an inequality to hold true for every rectifiable curve in the underlying space. Shanmungalingam
[
Sha00
]
introduced the concept of weak upper gradients, and the relative Newton-Sobolev spaces by allowing the requirement to fail for a null set of curves (in the sense of modulus of a curve family). Cheeger
[
Che99
]
used a relaxation procedure of weak upper gradients to define relaxed upper gradients.

More recently, in the interest of studying spaces that satisfy a lower Ricci curvature bound, Lott and Villani
[
LV07
]
and (independently) Sturm
[
Stu06b
,
Stu06a
]
introduced the celebrated curvature-dimension condition. In order to further study these spaces, Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savarè
[
AGS14b
]
introduced the so-called RCD spaces and a new notion of first order Sobolev spaces, suitable for a larger class of metric measure spaces. There has been a flurry of activity in the last few years, due to the interest in studying these general metric spaces with a lower curvature bound. See the brief surveys
[
Hon20
,
Nab20
]
for more information.
Under sufficient geometric assumptions, the notions of weak upper gradient due to Cheeger, Shanmungalingam, and Ambrosio-Gigli-Savarè are equivalent
[
AGS14a
]
(see also
[
AGS13
]
for a more focused exposition), while Hajłasz gradients are not; one should think of them as the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function of upper gradients.

The tensorization problem asks what is the relation between the Sobolev functions on two metric measure spaces
X
and
Y
and the ones on their product space (equipped with the product measure, and Cartesian product metric). The problem, while basic, seems to not have been explored until recently; in fact it is still open for the general case. Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savarè
[
AGS14b
]
first proved the result under some restrictive condition on the curvature of
both
spaces (namely the
\mathsf{RCD}(K,\infty)
condition). Ambrosio, Pinamonti, and Speight
[
APS15
]
proved it for spaces that are doubling and support a Poincaré inequality, or under certain quadracity assumptions. Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savarè
[
AGS14b
]
proved that, for a general metric space, it is always true that we can control the Euclidean product of the weak upper gradients in each factor by the weak upper gradient on the product.

In this paper we prove that the conjecture is true, provided
one
of the factors is doubling and has a Poincaré inequality. We also prove that the same is true if one considers warped products of metric measure spaces. Both results (and their proofs) generalize the work of Gigli and Han
[
GH18
]
, who proved the conjecture when one of the factors is an interval, both for Cartesian and warped products. We remark that the proof we present is almost self contained, relying only on earlier results concerning the density of Lipschitz functions by Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savarè
[
AGS14a
]
.

Independently, Eriksson-Bique, Soultanis, and Rajala
[
EBRS22a
]
proved similar results to ours, but only in the Cartesian case. Their proof relies on techniques developed by the first and third author
[
EBS21
]
.
The same authors
[
EBRS22b
]
then also proved tensorization where both factors are quasi-Hilbertian metric spaces.

Preliminaries

For basic definitions on metric spaces, we refer the interested reader to

Definition 1

(Absolutely continuous curves)
.

Let
p\in[1,+\infty]

\gamma\in C([0,1],X)

\gamma_{t}=\gamma(t)

(1)

For

the space
AC^{1}([0,1],X)
is denoted
AC([0,1],X)
and it is the space of absolutely continuous curves.

Theorem 1

(Theorem 1.1.2 in
[
AGS05
]
)
.

For
\gamma\in AC([0,1],X)
there exists an a.e. minimal function
G
which satisfies (
1
) called the metric derivative which can be computed for a.e.

as

|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|:=\lim_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{d(\gamma_{t},\gamma_{s})}{|s-t|}.

Proposition 2

(
[
BBI22
]
)
.

The length of a curve
\gamma\in AC([0,1],X)
is given by:

l[\gamma]:=\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,dt.

If
(X,d)
is a length space then for any

\dist(x,y)=\inf\left\{\;\int_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,dt;\quad\gamma\in AC([0,1],X)\text{ connecting }x\text{ and }y\;\right\}.

Definition 2

(Metric doubling)
.

Given a measure space
(X,d,\mu)
, we say that

is
metric doubling
if there exists a constant
C_{X}>1
such that, for all
r>0
every ball of radius
r
can be covered by
C_{X}
balls of radius
r/2
.

Definition 3

(Measure doubling)
.

A measure space
(X,d,\mu)
is said to be

if there exists a constant
D_{X}>1
such that, for all
x\in X
and

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq D_{X}\mu(B(x,r))
.

Remark 1
.

Every metric space
X
that carries a doubling measure with constant
D_{X}
is automatically metric doubling with constant at most
D_{X}^{4}
.

In fact, let
R>0
and
x\in X
.
Consider the ball
B(x,R)
.
If for some
N
,
B(x,R)
cannot be covered by
N
balls of radius
\frac{R}{2}
, then we can construct a sequence of points
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}
in
B(x,R)
which are pairwise at distance
\geq\frac{R}{2}
. This implies that the balls
B(x_{i},\frac{R}{4})
are pairwise disjoint, and they are all contained in
B(x,2R)
. Without loss of generality, assume
m(B(x_{1},\frac{R}{4}))
is the smallest of all the
m(B(x_{i},\frac{R}{4}))
.
Then
m(B(x_{1},4R))\geq m(B(x,2R))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}m(B(x_{i},\frac{R}{4}))\geq NB(x_{1},\frac{N}{4})
, which implies
N\leq D_{X}^{4}
, and so
C_{X}\leq D_{X}^{4}
.

Definition 4

(Local and global Lipschitz constants)
.

Given
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
, the local Lipschitz constant of
f
is the function
\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f):X\rightarrow[0,+\infty]
defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f)(x)=\begin{cases}\limsup_{y\to x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\dist_{X}(x,y)}&\quad\text{if }x\text{ is not isolated},\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2)

Analogously, the global Lipschitz constant is defined as

\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\limsup_{y\neq x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\dist_{X}(x,y)},

(3)

and if
X
is a length space,
\operatorname{Lip}(f)=\sup_{x}\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f)(x)
.
We denote by
\operatorname{Lip}(X)
the space of all Lipschitz functions
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 5

(Test plan)
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be a metric measure space and
\pi
a probability measure on
C([0,1],X)
. The measure
\pi
is said to have bounded compression if there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
t\in[0,1]

(e_{t})_{\#}\pi\leq Cm.

\pi
is said to be a test plan if it has bounded compression, it is concentrated on
AC^{2}([0,1],X)
and

\int_{0}^{1}\;\int\;|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|^{2}\;d\pi(\gamma)\;dt<+\infty.

Definition 6

(Sobolev class)
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be a metric measure space. A Borel function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belongs to the Sobolev class
S^{2}(X,d,m)
(respectively
S_{loc}^{2}(X,d,m)
) if there exists a non-negative function
G\in L^{2}(X,m)
(respectively
G\in L_{loc}^{2}(X,m)
) such that for all test plans
\pi

\int\;|f(\gamma_{1})-f(\gamma_{0})|\;d\pi(\gamma)\leq\int\;\int_{0}^{1}G(\gamma_{s})|\dot{\gamma}_{s}|\;ds\;d\pi(\gamma).

In this case the function
G
is called a 2-weak upper gradient of
f
, or simply a weak upper gradient of
f
.

Remark 2
.

Among all weak upper gradients of
f
there exists a minimal function
G
in the
m
-a.e. sense. Such minimal function is called minimal weak upper gradient and we denote it by
|Df|
. Notice that if
f
is Lipschitz, then
|Df|\leq\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f)

m
-a.e. since
\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f)
is a weak upper gradient of
f
.

We will use that minimal weak upper gradients are lower semicontinuous in the following sense: if
f_{n}\in S^{2}(X,d,m)
is a sequence converging
m
-a.e. to some
f
such that the sequence given by the functions
|Df_{n}|
is bounded in
L^{2}(X,m)
, then
f\in S^{2}(X,d,m)
and for all
G
that are the
L^{2}
-weak limit of some subsequence of
|Df_{n}|
we have

|Df|\leq G.

(4)

Finally, we will later use the fact that space
S^{2}_{loc}(X,d,m)\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}(X,d,m)
is an algebra and the following inequalities hold:

|D(fg)|\leq|f||Dg|+|g||Df|\quad m\text{-a.e., for all $f,g\in S^{2}_{loc}(X,d,m)\cap L^{\infty}_{loc}(X,d,m)$}.

(5)

|D(\alpha f+\beta g)|\leq|\alpha||Df|+|\beta||Dg|\quad m\text{-a.e., for all $f,g\in S^{2}_{loc}(X,d,m)$ and $\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(6)

For additional details on the properties of minimal weak upper gradients, see
[
AGS14a
]
.

Definition 7

(Sobolev space)
.

The Sobolev space
W^{1,2}(X,d,m)
is defined as

W^{1,2}(X,d,m):=S^{2}(X,d,m)\cap L^{2}(X,m).

The space
W^{1,2}(X,d,m)
endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{1,2}(X,d,m)}:=\sqrt{\|f\|_{L^{2}(X,m)}^{2}+\||Df|\|_{L^{2}(X,m)}^{2}}

is a Banach space.

3
Cartesian products

The product space
X\times Y
is given the Euclidean product metric

\dist_{X\times Y}((x,t),(y,s)):=\sqrt{\dist_{X}(x,y)^{2}+\dist_{Y}(t,s)^{2}},

(7)

and the measure on it is the usual product measure and is denoted simply by
m
.

Definition 8

(Beppo Levi space)
.

The Beppo-Levi space
{\mathsf{BL}}(X,Y)
is the space of functions
f\in L^{2}(X\times Y;\mathbb{R})
such that

1.

f(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}(Y)
for
m_{X}\text{ - a.e. }x

2.

f(\cdot,t)\in W^{1,2}(X)
for
m_{Y}\text{ - a.e. }t

3.

the function

|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}(x,t):=\sqrt{|Df(x,\cdot)|_{Y}(t)^{2}+|Df(\cdot,t)|_{X}(x)^{2}}

(8)

belongs to
L^{2}(X\times Y;\mathbb{R})

with the Beppo-Levi norm

\|f\|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}:=\sqrt{\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\,.

(9)

We will use the following property of minimal weak upper gradients on Cartesian products. If
f\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
, then for any
F\subset Y
,

\left|D\left(\int_{F}f(x,y)\,dy\right)\right|_{X}\leq\int_{F}\left|Df(x,y)\right|_{X}\,dy.

(10)

In fact, this follows from Definition
6
and Remark
2
, since for any test plan
\pi
on
C([0,1],X)
we have

\displaystyle\int\left|\int_{F}f(\gamma_{1},y)\,dy-\int_{F}f(\gamma_{0},y)\,dy\right|\,d\pi(\gamma)=\int\left|\int_{F}f(\gamma_{1},y)-f(\gamma_{0},y)\,dy\right|\,d\pi(\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\int\int_{F}\left|f(\gamma_{1},y)-f(\gamma_{0},y)\right|\,dy\,d\pi(\gamma)\leq\int_{F}\int\int_{0}^{1}|Df(\gamma_{s},y)|_{X}|\dot{\gamma}_{s}|\,ds\,d\pi(\gamma)\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\int\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{F}|Df(\gamma_{s},y)|_{X}\,dy\right)|\dot{\gamma}_{s}|\,ds\,d\pi(\gamma)\,,

so
\int_{F}|Df(x,y)|_{X}\,dy
is a weak upper gradient for
\int_{F}f(x,y)\,dy
and (

) consequently follows.

In light of the proposition, it is enough to show that

Lemma 2
.

Let
N>0
be a fixed natural number. Let
f:X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}
be of the form
f(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(t)g_{i}(x)
where

and
h_{i}\in\operatorname{Lip}(Y)
for all

. Then

\operatorname{lip}_{X\times Y}(f)^{2}(x,t)\leq 4\left(\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f(\cdot,t))^{2}(x)+\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f(x,\cdot))^{2}(t)\right).

(13)

for every
(x,t)\in X\times Y

This lemma replaces Lemma 3.3 in
[
GH18
]

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{X\times Y}(f)^{2}(x,t)=\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{|f(y,s)-f(x,t)|^{2}}{\dist_{X\times Y}((y,s),(x,t))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{1}{\dist_{X\times Y}((y,s),(x,t))^{2}}\left(\dist_{X}(y,x)^{2}\frac{|f(y,s)-f(x,s)|^{2}}{\dist_{X}(y,x)^{2}}+\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}\frac{|f(x,s)-f(x,t)|^{2}}{\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(s)(g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x))\right|^{2}}{\dist_{X}(y,x)^{2}}+2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t))g_{i}(x)\right|^{2}}{\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(t)(g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x))+(h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t))(g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x))\right|^{2}}{\dist_{X}(x,y)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t))g_{i}(x)\right|^{2}}{\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(t)(g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x))\right|}{\dist_{X}(x,y)}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t)\right|\frac{\left|g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x)\right|}{\dist_{X}(y,x)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t))g_{i}(x)\right|^{2}}{\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}h_{i}(t)(g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x))\right|^{2}}{\dist_{X}(y,x)^{2}}+4\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left|h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t)\right|^{2}\frac{\left|g_{i}(y)-g_{i}(x)\right|^{2}}{\dist_{X}(x,y)^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+2\limsup_{(y,s)\to(x,t)}\frac{\left|\sum_{i=1}^{N}(h_{i}(s)-h_{i}(t))g_{i}(x)\right|^{2}}{\dist_{Y}(s,t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=4\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f(\cdot,t))^{2}(x)+2\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f(x,\cdot))^{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f(\cdot,t))^{2}(x)+\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f(x,\cdot))^{2}(t)\right),

where in the last step we used that
h
is continuous.

\max\{\min\{f,\lambda\},-\lambda\}
for
\lambda>0
, we can assume that
f
is bounded. Moreover, by multiplying with a sequence of Lipschitz functions supported on an exhausting sequence of balls in
X\times Y
, and employing (
5
) to ensure convergence in the Beppo Levi space, we can also assume that
f
has bounded support contained in
B(y,R)
, for some
y\in Y
and
R>1
. Given
f\in{\mathsf{BL}}(X,Y)
, we define a sequence of functions
F_{k}
as

F_{k}(x,t):=\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}(x),

(14)

where
f_{k,i}(x):=\fint_{Q_{i,k}}f(x,t)\,dt
.
For a fixed
(x,t)
at most
C_{Y}^{3}
terms in the sum are non-zero since, by Lemma
3
, the support of
\chi_{i,k}
can intersect only the support of the neighbours of the
Q_{i,k}
and, by Remark
5
, each
Q_{i,k}
has at most
C_{Y}^{3}
neighbors. Moreover, it is enough to consider cubes that intersect
\supp f
, and since they are bounded sets,
\supp f
and all cubes that intersect it are contained in
B(y,2R)
, for some
y\in Y
.

Moreover, by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle\|f_{k,i}\|_{L^{2}(X)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\left|\fint_{Q_{ik}}f(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\right|^{2}\,dm_{X}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\fint_{Q_{ik}}|f(x,t)|^{2}\,dtdx=\fint_{Q_{ik}}\int_{X}|f(x,t)|^{2}\,dxdt\leq\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}^{2}}{m_{Y}(Q_{ik})}\,

and so

\displaystyle\|F_{k}\|_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}(x)\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}m_{Y}\left(B\left(t_{i,k},\frac{5}{4}\frac{1}{k}\right)\right)^{2}\left\|f_{k,i}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},D_{Y}}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}m_{Y}(Q_{ik})

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},D_{Y}}\;m_{Y}({B(y,2R)})\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)},

where the last inequality is due to the fact that we only consider the cubes
Q_{i,k}
that intersect the bounded support of
f
.

Thus, we see that the linear map
T_{k}\colon L^{2}(X\times Y)\to L^{2}(X\times Y)
that takes
f
with bounded support to
F_{k}
is Lipschitz, uniformly in
k
. Note now that, if
g\in\operatorname{Lip}(X\times Y)
with bounded support then
T_{k}(g)\to g
in
L^{2}(X\times Y)
, since then

\displaystyle\|T_{k}(g)-g\|_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\left\|\chi_{i,k}(t)\left|\fint_{Q_{ik}}\left(g(x,s)-g(x,t)\right)\,dm_{Y}(s)\right|\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
Q_{i,k}\cap\supp g\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Lip}_{Y}(g)\left\|\frac{\chi_{i,k}(t)}{k}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}\to 0\,.

Thus, because
\operatorname{Lip}(X\times Y)
is dense in
L^{2}(X\times Y)
, see Proposition 4.3 in
[
AGS13
]
, we get

F_{k}\to f\qquad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(X\times Y).

(15)

Note now that
f_{k,i}\in W^{1,2}(X)
, since by convexity and (
10
),

\displaystyle|\partial f_{k,i}/\partial x|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\fint_{Q_{ik}}f(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\right)\right|^{2}{\leq}\left(\fint_{Q_{ik}}\left|\partial f(x,t)/\partial x\right|\,dm_{Y}(t)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\fint_{Q_{ik}}|\partial f(x,t)/\partial x|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)\leq\frac{\||Df|_{BL}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(X\times Y)}}{m_{Y}(Q_{ik})}

Thus, by Lemma
1
, we have a sequence of Lipschitz functions
f_{k,i}^{n}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f_{k,i}^{n}\to f_{k,i}
in
L^{2}(X,m_{X})
and
\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f_{k,i}^{n})\to|Df_{k,i}|_{X}
in
L^{2}(X,m_{X})
. Now define

F_{k}^{n}(x,t):=\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}^{n}(x).

(16)

Note that
F_{k}^{n}\in\operatorname{Lip}(X\times Y)
and so, for every fixed
t\in Y
,
\operatorname{lip}_{X}(F_{k}^{n})(t)\to|\partial f_{k}/\partial x|(t)
in
L^{2}(X)
. Finally, as before, we deduce that
\operatorname{lip}_{X}(F_{k}^{n})\to|\partial f_{k}/\partial x|
in
L^{2}(X\times Y)
.

Let
n(k)
be the least number
n
such that, for all
i
, we have

\|f^{n(k)}_{k,i}-f_{k,i}\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\operatorname{lip}_{X}f^{n(k)}_{k,i}-\partial f_{k,i}/\partial x\|_{L^{2}(X)}\leq\frac{1}{k^{3}m_{X\times Y}(B(y,2R))}=:\epsilon\,.

(17)

Now, we define
f_{k}:=F_{k}^{n(k)}
. Observe that
f_{k}\in\operatorname{Lip}(X\times Y)\cap{\mathsf{BL}}(X,Y)
and note also that we get

f_{k}\to f\qquad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(X\times Y).

(18)

It remains to show
|Df_{k}|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}\to|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}
. By lower semicontinuity (
4
), to show this it suffices to show that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{X\times Y}|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,t)\,dm(x,t)\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\int_{X\times Y}|{{\partial}f}/{{\partial}x}|^{2}(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x).

(19)

and that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\int_{X\times Y}|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}t|^{2}(x,t)\,dm(x,t)\lesssim_{C_{Y},C_{P}}\int_{X\times Y}|{{\partial}f}/{{\partial}t}|^{2}(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x).

(20)

To prove (
19
), we first notice that by convexity and (
10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|{\partial}f_{i,k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x)dm_{X}(x)\leq\int_{X}\fint_{Q_{i,k}}|{\partial}f/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,s)dm_{Y}(s)dm_{X}(x).

Also, by sublinearity (
6
),

\displaystyle|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,t)

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\chi_{i,k}(t)f^{n(k)}_{i,k}(x)\right)\right|^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\chi_{i,k}(t)\left|\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x}f^{n(k)}_{i,k}\right|(x)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y}}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\chi_{i,k}(t)|{\partial}f^{n(k)}_{i,k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\chi_{i,k}(t)|{\partial}f_{i,k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x)+\epsilon\,,

where in the last inequality we have used (
17
).
Thus, using sublinearity again, we have

\displaystyle\int_{X\times Y}|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,t)\,dm(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}\int_{Y}|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\int_{Y}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\chi_{i,k}(t)|{\partial}f_{i,k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x)\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{D_{Y}}\int_{X}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}|Q_{i,k}||{\partial}f_{i,k}/{\partial}x|^{2}(x)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{X}\sum_{i\in I_{k}}|Q_{i,k}|\frac{1}{|Q_{i,k}|}\int_{Q_{i,k}}|{\partial}f/{\partial}x|^{2}(x,s)\,dm_{Y}(s)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k^{3}}

\displaystyle=\int_{X\times Y}|{{\partial}f}/{{\partial}x}|^{2}(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k^{3}}\,.

It remains to show (
20
). First, recall that
\chi_{j,k}(t)=1-\sum_{i\neq j}\chi_{i,k}(t)
for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and so we have that, for any
t,s\in Y
, and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle F_{k}^{n}(x,t)-F_{k}^{n}(x,s)=\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-\sum_{i\in I_{k}}\chi_{i,k}(s)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)+\chi_{j,k}(t)f_{k,j}^{n}(x)-\chi_{j,k}(s)f_{k,j}^{n}(x)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(s)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(t)f_{k,j}^{n}(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(s)f_{k,j}^{n}(x)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\chi_{i,k}(s)f_{k,i}^{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}\left(\chi_{i,k}(t)-\chi_{i,k}(s)\right)(f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-f_{k,j}^{n}(x)).

Note that the sum above is finite, as only finitely many of the
\chi_{i,k}
are nonzero.
Dividing this by
t-s
, and then taking a limit we obtain

\displaystyle\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(F_{k}^{n}(x))(t)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{s\to t}\left|\sum_{i\neq j}\frac{\chi_{i,k}(t)-\chi_{i,k}(s)}{t-s}(f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-f_{k,j}^{n}(x))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Lip}_{Y}(\chi_{i,k})\left|f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-f_{k,j}^{n}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{c_{1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in I_{k}\\
i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}k\left|f_{k,i}^{n}(x)-f_{k,j}^{n}(x)\right|\,.

Set
B_{jk}:=B(t_{j,k},\frac{6}{k})
, and note that if
Q_{i,k}
and
Q_{j,k}
are neighbors, then they are both contained in
B_{j,k}
. Defining
f_{B_{jk}}:=\fint_{B_{jk}}f(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\fint_{Q_{i,k}}f(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)-\fint_{Q_{j,k}}f(x,t)\,dm_{Y}(t)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{i,k}|}\int_{Q_{i,k}}\left|f(x,t)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)+\frac{1}{|Q_{j,k}|}\int_{Q_{j,k}}\left|f(x,t)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{i,k}|}\int_{B_{jk}}\left|f(x,t)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)+\frac{1}{|Q_{j,k}|}\int_{B_{jk}}\left|f(x,t)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{D_{Y}}\fint_{B_{jk}}\left|f(x,t)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t).

Hence, using Poincaré inequality, putting the above estimates together, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{X\times Y}|{\partial}f_{k}/{\partial}t|^{2}(x,t)\,dm(x,t){\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1}}}\int_{X}\sum_{j}\int_{Q_{j,k}}k^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}|f^{n(k)}_{k,j}(x)-f^{n(k)}_{k,i}(x)|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1}}\int_{X}\sum_{j}\int_{Q_{j,k}}\,dm_{Y}(t)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}k^{2}\left|\fint_{Q_{i,k}}f(x,s)\,dm_{Y}(s)-\fint_{Q_{j,k}}f(x,s)\,dm_{Y}(s)\right|^{2}\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1},D_{Y}}\int_{X}\sum_{j}|Q_{j,k}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}k^{2}\fint_{B_{jk}}\left|f(x,s)-f_{B_{jk}}\right|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(s)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1},D_{Y},C_{P}}\int_{X}\sum_{j}|Q_{j,k}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
Q_{i,k}\sim Q_{j,k}\end{subarray}}k^{2}\operatorname{rad}({B_{jk}})^{2}\fint_{\lambda{B_{jk}}}|\partial f/\partial s|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(s)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k}

(21)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1},D_{Y},C_{P}}\int_{X}\sum_{j}|Q_{j,k}|\fint_{\lambda{B_{jk}}}|\partial f/\partial s|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(s)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1},D_{Y},C_{P},\lambda}\int_{X}\sum_{j}\int_{\lambda{B_{jk}}}|\partial f/\partial t|^{2}\,dm_{Y}(t)\,dm_{X}(x)+\frac{1}{k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{C_{Y},c_{1},D_{Y},C_{P},\lambda}\int_{X\times Y}|\partial f/\partial t|^{2}\,dm(x,t)+\frac{1}{k}\,,

which, by taking the
\limsup
for
k\to\infty
, concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We already observed that
W^{1,2}(X\times Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times Y)
. Let
f\in{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times Y)
and let
\{f_{k}\}\subset{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times Y)\cap\operatorname{Lip}(X\times Y)
be as in Lemma
4
. Lemma
2
says that

\operatorname{lip}_{X\times Y}(f_{k})^{2}\leq 4\left(\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f_{k})^{2}+\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f_{k})^{2}\right)

By Lemma
4
, the right hand side converges to
4|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}}
in
L^{2}(X\times Y)
and because
f_{k}\to f
in
L^{2}
, the lower semicontinuity of weak upper gradients (
4
) implies that
f\in W^{1,2}(X\times Y)
and

|Df|_{X\times Y}\leq G

where
G
is any weak limit of
\operatorname{lip}_{X\times Y}(f_{k})
. So,

|Df|_{X\times Y}\leq 4|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}},

which together with Proposition
4
concludes the proof.
∎

4
Warped products

Definition 10
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be length spaces, and
w_{d}\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
a continuous function. Let
\gamma=(\gamma^{X},\gamma^{Y})
be a curve such that
\gamma^{X}
and
\gamma^{Y}
are absolutely continuous. Then the
w_{d}
-length of
\gamma
is defined as

\ell_{w}(\gamma)=\lim_{\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{d_{Y}^{2}(\gamma^{Y}_{t_{i-1}},\gamma^{Y}_{t_{i}})+w_{d}^{2}(\gamma^{Y}_{t_{i-1}}d_{X}^{2}(\gamma^{X}_{t_{i-1}},\gamma^{X}_{t_{i}})},

where
\tau
is a partition of
[0,1]
and the limit is taken over refinement ordering of partitions.

The limit exists and

\ell_{w}(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{|\dot{\gamma}^{Y}_{t}|^{2}+w_{d}^{2}(\gamma_{t}^{Y})|\dot{\gamma}^{X}_{t}|^{2}}\,dt.

Definition 11
.

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be length spaces, and
w_{d}\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
a continuous function. We define a pseudo-metric
\dist_{w}
on on the space
X\times Y
by

\dist_{w}(p,q)=\inf\{\ell_{w}(\gamma)\mid\text{$\gamma^{X}\in AC([0,1],X)$, $\gamma^{Y}\in AC([0,1],Y)$, and $\gamma_{0}=p,\gamma_{1}=q$}\},

for any
p,q\in X\times Y
.

The pseudo-metric induces an equivalent relation on
X\times Y
given by
(x,y)\sim(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
if
d_{w}((x,y),(x^{\prime}y^{\prime}))=0
and hence a metric on the quotient. We denote the completion of such quotient by
(X\times_{w}Y,d_{w})
. If both
X
and
Y
are separable, so is
X\times_{w}Y
. Let
\pi\colon X\times Y\to X\times_{w}Y
be the quotient map.

Definition 12
.

Let
(X,d_{X},m_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y},m_{Y})
be complete separable and length metric spaces equipped with non-negative Radon measures. Assume that
m_{X}(X)<\infty
and let
w_{d},w_{m}\colon Y\to[0,\infty)
be continuous functions. Then the warped product
(X\times_{w}Y,d_{w})
is defined as above and the Radon measure
m_{w}
is defined as

m_{w}=\pi_{*}((w_{m}m_{Y})\times m_{X}).

Note that the assumption that
m_{X}
is a finite measure is needed to ensure that
m_{w}
is Radon (it is always Borel). See
[
GH18
]
after Definition 2.9 for more details. Following the notation conventions in
[
GH18
]
, with a slight abuse, we will denote an element of
X\times_{w}Y
by
(x,y)
.

Definition 13
.

As a set, the Beppo Levi space
{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times_{w}Y)
is the subset of
L^{2}(X\times_{w}Y,m_{w})
of all functions
f
such that

•

for
m_{X}
-a.e.
x\in X
, we have
f^{(x)}=f(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}(Y,w_{m}m_{Y})
;

•

for
w_{m}m_{Y}
-a.e.
t\in Y
, we have
f^{(t)}=f(\cdot,t)\in W^{1,2}(X)
;

•

the function

|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}=\sqrt{w_{d}^{-2}|Df^{(t)}|_{X}^{2}(x)+|Df^{(x)}|_{Y}^{2}(t)}

belongs to
L^{2}(X\times_{w}Y,m_{w})
.

On
{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times_{w}Y)
we put the norm

\|f\|_{{\mathsf{BL}}(X\times_{w}Y)}=\sqrt{\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\||Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}.

To handle the warped case we need to introduce an auxiliary space:

Definition 14
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
be the space of functions
f
which are identically
0
on
X\times\Omega
, where
\Omega
is an open set that satisfies
\{w_{m}=0\}\subset\Omega\subset Y
.
{\mathsf{BL}}_{0,w}(X,Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
is defined as the closure of
\mathcal{V}
in
{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
.

We want to compare the Beppo-Levi space with the Sobolev space on the warped product, and the respective notions of minimal upper gradients. The main result of this section is the following.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X
and
Y
satisfy Assumption
1
and
2
, and let
w_{d},w_{m}\colon Y\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be continuous functions such that
\{w_{d}=0\}\subset\{w_{m}=0\}
. Then

{\mathsf{BL}}_{0,w}(X,Y)\subset W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y),

and, for every
f\in W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
, the inequalities

|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}\leq|Df|_{X\times_{w}Y}\leq C_{0}|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}

hold
m_{w}
-a.e., with
C_{0}>0
is as in Theorem
3
.

We will continue denoting the minimal weak upper gradient on the Cartesian product
X\times Y
simply by
|Df|
, while we will denote the minimal weak upper gradient on the warped product by
|Df|_{X\times_{w}Y}
.

Proposition 6
.

We have
W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)
. Then by Lemma
1
, we can find a sequence
f_{n}
of Lipschitz functions on
X\times_{w}Y
such that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
and
\operatorname{lip}_{X\times_{w}Y}(f_{n})\rightarrow|Df|_{X\times_{w}Y}
in
L^{2}(X\times_{w}Y)
. Passing to a subsequence, which we do not relabel, we can assume that
\|f_{n}-f\|_{L^{2}(X\times_{w}Y,m_{w})}<n^{-4}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|f_{n}^{(t)}-f^{(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(X,m_{X})}\right\|_{L^{2}(Y,w_{m}m_{Y})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{Y}\;\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n}{n}\left\|f_{n}^{(t)}-f^{(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(X,m_{X})}\right)^{2}\,w_{m}(t)dm_{Y}(t)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\text{(Cauchy-Schwarz)}\left(\int_{Y}\;\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}\left\|f_{n}^{(t)}-f^{(t)}\right\|_{L^{2}(X,m_{X})}^{2}\right)\,w_{m}(t)dm_{Y}(t)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\left(\int_{Y}\;\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}\int_{X}|f_{n}^{(t)}-f^{(t)}|^{2}\,d_{m}X(x)\,w_{m}(t)dm_{Y}(t)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}\int_{Y}\;\int_{X}|f_{n}^{(t)}(x)-f^{(t)}(x)|^{2}\,d_{m}X(x)\,w_{m}(t)dm_{Y}(t)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{2}\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{L^{2}(X\times_{w}Y,m_{w})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}<\infty

This shows that for
w_{m}m_{Y}
-a.e.
t\in Y
we have
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\|f_{n}^{(t)}-f^{(t)}\|_{L^{2}(X,m_{X})}<\infty
and so in particular
f_{n}^{(t)}\rightarrow f^{(t)}
in
L^{2}(X,m_{X})
. Similarly for
m_{X}
-a.e.
x\in X
, we have
f_{n}^{(x)}\rightarrow f^{(x)}
in
L^{2}(Y,w_{m}m_{Y})
.

Now observe that for
(x,t)\in X\times_{w}Y
we have:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{lip}_{X\times_{w}Y}(f_{n})(x,t)&=\limsup_{(y,s)\rightarrow(x,t)}\frac{|f_{n}(y,s)-f_{n}(x,t)|}{d_{w}((y,s),(x,t))}\\
&\geq\limsup_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{|f_{n}(x,s)-f_{n}(x,t)|}{d_{w}((x,s),(x,t))}\\
&=\limsup_{s\rightarrow t}\frac{|f_{n}^{(x)}(s)-f_{n}^{(x)}(t)|}{d_{Y}(s,t)}=\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f_{n}^{(x)})(t).\end{split}

(22)

Then, by Fatou’s lemma:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{X}\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{Y}\,\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f_{n}^{(x)})^{2}(t)w_{m}(t)dm_{Y}(t)dm_{X}(x)\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X\times_{w}Y}\operatorname{lip}_{Y}(f_{n}^{(x)})^{2}(t)dm_{w}(x,t)\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{X\times_{w}Y}\operatorname{lip}_{X\times_{w}Y}(f_{n})^{2}(x,t)dm_{w}(x,t)\\
&=\int_{X\times_{w}Y}|Df|^{2}_{X\times_{w}Y}dm_{w}(x,t)<\infty.\end{split}

(23)

Since
f_{n}^{(x)}\rightarrow f^{(x)}
in
L^{2}(Y,w_{m}m_{Y})
for
m_{x}
-a.e.
x\in X
, the last inequality together with the lower semicontinuity of minimal weak upper gradients gives that
f^{(x)}\in W^{1,2}(Y,w_{m}m_{Y})
for
m_{x}
-a.e.
x\in X
and

\int_{X_{w}}|Df^{(x)}|_{Y}^{2}(t)dm_{w}(x,t)\;\leq\int_{X_{w}}|Df|^{2}_{X\times_{w}Y}dm_{w}(x,t).

(24)

With an analogous argument we can get conditions on
f^{(t)}
.
Starting from the bound:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{lip}_{X\times_{w}Y}(f_{n})(x,t)&=\limsup_{(y,s)\rightarrow(x,t)}\frac{|f_{n}(y,s)-f_{n}(x,t)|}{d_{w}((y,s),(x,t))}\\
&\geq\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f_{n}(y,t)-f_{n}(x,t)|}{d_{w}((y,t),(x,t))}\\
&=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f_{n}^{(t)}(y)-f_{n}^{(t)}(x)|}{w_{d}(t)d_{X}(x,y)}=\frac{1}{w_{d}(t)}\operatorname{lip}_{X}(f_{n}^{(t)})(x).\end{split}

(25)

This inequality, valid for every
t\in Y
such that
w_{d}(t)>0
, grants that
f^{(t)}\in W^{1,2}(X)
for
w_{m}m_{Y}
-a.e.
t\in Y
(here we are using the assumption that
\{w_{d}=0\}\subset\{w_{m}=0\}
) and that

\int_{X\times_{w}Y}\frac{|Df^{(t)}|_{X}^{2}(x)}{w_{d}^{2}(t)}dm_{w}(x,t)\;\leq\int_{X\times_{w}Y}|Df|^{2}_{X\times_{w}Y}dm_{w}(x,t).

(26)

The bounds (
24
) and (
26
) ensure that
f\in{\mathsf{BL}}(X_{w})
so that the desired inclusion is proved.
∎

Proposition 7
.

Let
f\in W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)\subset{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)

m_{w}
-a.e., where

3

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
t_{0}
be such that
w_{m}(t_{0})>0
, and hence
w_{d}(t_{0})>0
. By continuity we can find
\delta>0
such that

\left|\frac{w_{d}(t)}{w_{d}(s)}\right|\leq 1+\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }t,s\in\overline{B(t_{0},3\delta)}.

(27)

Let
\chi\colon Y\to[0,1]
be a Lipschitz function such that

\overline{B(t_{0},\delta)}

\chi\equiv 0
outside of
\overline{B(t_{0},3\delta)}

\overline{w_{d}}(t)=\begin{cases}w_{d}(t)&\text{if }\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})\leq 2\delta,\\
\frac{\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})-2\delta}{\delta}w_{d}(t_{0})+\frac{3\delta-\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})}{\delta}w_{d}(t)&\text{if }2\delta<\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})<3\delta,\\

(28)

\overline{w_{m}}(t)=\begin{cases}w_{m}(t)&\text{if }\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})\leq 2\delta,\\
\frac{\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})-2\delta}{\delta}w_{m}(t_{0})+\frac{3\delta-\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})}{\delta}w_{m}(t)&\text{if }2\delta<\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})<3\delta,\\
w_{m}(t_{0})&\text{if }\dist_{Y}(t,t_{0})\geq 3\delta.\end{cases}

(29)

and let
(X\times_{\overline{w}}Y,\dist_{\overline{w}},m_{\overline{w}})
be the corresponding product space. Consider the function
\overline{f}\colon X\times_{w}Y\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\overline{f}(x,t)=\chi(t)f(t,x)
. Clearly
\overline{f}\in W^{1,2}(X\times_{w}Y)
, and hence to
{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}(X,Y)
. Since minimal upper gradients are local we know that

|Df|_{X\times_{w}Y}=|D\overline{f}|_{X\times_{w}Y}\quad\text{and}\quad|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}=|D\overline{f}|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}\quad\text{ $m_{w}$-a.e. on $X\times\overline{B(t_{0},\delta)}$}.

Because
\overline{f}
is supported on

, where

is positive we can think of
\overline{f}
as a function on

. With this identification we have

|D\overline{f}|_{X\times_{w}Y}=|D\overline{f}|_{X\times_{\overline{w}}Y}\quad\text{and}\quad|D\overline{f}|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{w}}=|D\overline{f}|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{\overline{w}}}\quad\text{ $m_{w}$-a.e. on $X\times\overline{B(t_{0},2\delta)}$}.

We now want to use this “localized warped space” to utilize the results for the Cartesian product we have obtained in the previous section.

Consider first the space
\overline{X}:=(X,w_{d}(t_{0})\dist_{X},w_{m}(t_{0})m_{X})
. This is simply our original metric measure space
(X,\dist_{X},m_{X})
which has been re-scaled. Now consider
(\overline{X}\times Y,\dist,m)
where with a slight abuse of notation we denote by
\dist
and
m
are the appropriate Cartesian metric and measure, respectively. Set
c=\min_{\overline{B(t_{0},3\delta)}}w_{m}
and
C=\max_{\overline{B(t_{0},3\delta)}}w_{m}
. From the definition of
m_{w}
we immediately have

cm\leq m_{\overline{w}}\leq Cm,

and, recalling (
27
), we have

(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\dist\leq\dist_{\overline{w}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\dist.

(30)

Let us denote by
\overline{{\mathsf{BL}}}:={\mathsf{BL}}(\overline{X},Y)
. Using Lemma
5
, recalling that
X\times_{\overline{w}}Y
and
\overline{X}\times Y
coincide as sets, we obtain

(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{X}\times Y}\leq|D\overline{f}|_{X\times_{\overline{w}}Y}\leq(1+\varepsilon)|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{X}\times Y},

and

(1+\varepsilon)^{-1}|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{{\mathsf{BL}}}}\leq|D\overline{f}|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{\overline{w}}}\leq(1+\varepsilon)|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{{\mathsf{BL}}}}.

We now exploit the Cartesian results applied to the pair
(\overline{X},Y)
: by Theorem
3
, we know that

|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{{\mathsf{BL}}}}\leq|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{X}\times Y}\leq C_{0}|D\overline{f}|_{\overline{{\mathsf{BL}}}}\quad m\text{-a.e.}.

Putting everything together we obtain

(1+\varepsilon)^{-2}|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{\overline{w}}}\leq|Df|_{X\times_{\overline{w}}Y}\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}C_{0}|Df|_{{\mathsf{BL}}_{\overline{w}}}

m_{w}
-a.e. on
X\times B(t_{0},\delta)
. Because
t_{0}
was arbitrary, and because every cover of
\{w_{m}>0\}\subset Y
has a countable subcover, the conclusion holds by letting
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Proposition 9

\displaystyle\{w_{m}=0\}\subset Y\text{ is discrete;}

w_{m}

The Adams spectral sequence for 3-local tmf \mathrm{tmf}
D. Culver

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is to record the computation of the homotopy groups of 3-local tmf \mathrm{tmf} via the Adams spectral sequence.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we will carry out a computation of the homotopy groups of
\mathrm{tmf}
. The homotopy groups of
\mathrm{tmf}
have been known for quite some time. For example, the computation of
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
was explicitly written up in
[
1
]
, though it was known even earlier to Hopkins and Mahowald (cf.
[
9
]
). The usual approach to calculating the homotopy of
\mathrm{tmf}
and its variants is via the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence (also referred to as the descent spectral sequence in this context). One advantage of this approach is that the Adams-Novikov
E_{2}
-term can be computed using the theory of elliptic curves.

However, there are occasions where one wants to know the Adams spectral sequence for computing the homotopy groups of a spectrum. That is, one may want to know the Adams
E_{2}
-term, all differentials, and all hidden extensions. The purpose of this paper is to record the Adams spectral sequence for 3-local topological modular forms.

We should mention that the analogous calculation at the prime 2 is being carried out by Rognes and Bruner (
[
2
]
). It is the author’s understanding that their interest in that spectral sequence stemmed from their work on the topological Hochschild homology of
\mathrm{tmf}
. We speculate knowing the Adams spectral sequence at the prime 3 might be useful for similar reasons.

Conventions

In this article, we will implicitly assume that all spectra are 3-complete. Thus
\mathrm{tmf}
refers, from here on out, to the 3-completion of the spectrum of topological modular forms. We will always denote the mod 3 Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum by
H
. Given a Hopf algebra
\Gamma
and a comodule
C
over
\Gamma
, we will abbreviate
\Ext_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3},C)
by
\Ext_{\Gamma}(C)
. In the case when
\Gamma=A_{*}
, we will write
\Ext(C)
. If
C=H_{*}X
for a spectrum
X
, then we will abbreviate further to
\Ext(X)
. We will always employ Adams indexing unless specifically stated otherwise. We let
\zeta_{n}
denote
\chi\xi_{n}
and
\overline{\tau}_{n}
denote
\chi\tau_{n}
in the dual Steenrod algebra. We will use the symbol
\dot{=}
to indicate equality up to a multiplicative unit. Finally, for a spectrum
X
, we let
E_{r}(X)
denote the
r
th page of the mod 3 Adams spectral sequence for
X
.

1.1.
Outline of the paper

Recall that the Adams spectral sequence is a convergent spectral sequence of the form

\Ext_{A_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3},H_{*}\mathrm{tmf})\implies\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}^{\wedge}_{3}.

Thus, a necessary input is
H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
. This was determined, for example, in
[
13
]
, where Rezk shows there is a short exact sequence of comodules

0\to\Sigma^{8}\scr{B}\to H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}\to\scr{B}\to 0

where
\scr{B}
is a certain subalgebra of the dual Steenrod algebra
A_{*}
. This is the starting point of our calculation. We view this short exact sequence as giving a multiplicative filtration of
H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
by comodules, yielding an algebraic spectral sequence

E_{1}^{*,*,*}=\Ext_{A_{*}}(E_{0}H_{*}\mathrm{tmf})\cong\Ext_{A_{*}}(\scr{B})\otimes E(b_{4})\implies\Ext_{A_{*}}(H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}).

In §
2
we recall these details and establish a change-of-rings formula for
\Ext_{A_{*}}(\scr{B})
. In §
3
we use a Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence to compute
\Ext_{A_{*}}(\scr{B})
. An expert in these affairs can safely ignore this section. In §
4
, we determine the Adams
E_{2}
-term. In section §
5
we discuss the rational homotopy of
\mathrm{tmf}
and its relationship to modular forms. Finally, in §
6
, we establish the Adams differentials and derive
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
.

2.
The mod 3 homology of
\mathrm{tmf}

In this section we recall necessary facts about the mod 3 homology of
\mathrm{tmf}
. In
[

]

is given by

H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}\cong E(b_{4})\otimes\scr{B}

where

\scr{B}:=\mathbb{F}_{3}[\zeta_{1}^{3},\zeta_{n}\mid n\geq 2]\otimes E(\overline{\tau}_{n}\mid n\geq 3)

where the generators have the degrees

\displaystyle|\xi_{n}|

\displaystyle=2(3^{n}-1)

\displaystyle|\overline{\tau}_{n}|

\displaystyle=2\cdot 3^{n}-1

Here, the

is a comodule algebra over

. Note also that for degree reasons, the class

is an

-comodule primitive. Indeed, if

then the degrees of

is the lowest positive degree element of

Furthermore, Rezk shows that there is nontrivial extension of comodules

(2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Sigma^{8}\scr{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scr{B}\to 0.}

Applying

to this short exact sequence of comodules yields a long exact sequence in Ext. We regard this as a convergent spectral sequence

\Ext(\Sigma^{8}\scr{B})\oplus\Ext(\scr{B})\implies\Ext(H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}).

The fact that

is a nontrivial extension implies that this spectral sequence does not immediately collapse. Determining the differentials in this spectral sequence is the subject of section
4
. Thus, it is apparent that we need to compute the Ext groups of
\scr{B}
. We will simplify this by establishing a change-of-rings formula.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be the Hopf algebra

\Gamma:=A_{*}/(\zeta_{1}^{3},\zeta_{n},\overline{\tau}_{m}\mid n\geq 2,m\geq 3)\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}[\zeta_{1}]/(\zeta_{1}^{3})\otimes E(\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1},\overline{\tau}_{2})

with the induced coproduct from the dual Steenrod algebra.

Example 2.3
.

In the dual Steenrod algebra, the coproduct on
\overline{\tau}_{2}
is given by

\psi(\overline{\tau}_{2})=\overline{\tau}_{2}\otimes 1+\overline{\tau}_{0}\otimes\zeta_{2}+\overline{\tau}_{1}\otimes\zeta_{1}^{3}+1\otimes\overline{\tau}_{2}.

Thus, in
\Gamma
,
\overline{\tau}_{2}
is a Hopf algebra primitive. On the other hand,

\psi(\overline{\tau}_{1})=\overline{\tau}_{1}\otimes 1+\overline{\tau}_{0}\otimes\zeta_{1}+1\otimes\overline{\tau}_{1}.

Thus this Hopf algebra is not primitively generated.

The proof of the following proposition is standard.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is an isomorphism

\scr{B}\cong A_{*}\boxempty_{\Gamma}\mathbb{F}_{3}.

We derive the following corollary from Theorem A1.3.12 of
[
12
]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

There is a change-of-rings isomorphism

\Ext(\scr{B})\cong\Ext_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3}).

Thus we must compute the cohomology of the Hopf algebra
\Gamma
. This is done in the next section.

3.
Computing the cohomology of
\Gamma

In the last section we showed that the Ext groups of
\scr{B}
are
\Ext_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
. Since
\Gamma
is a finite Hopf algebra, there is hope of computing its cohomology. Recall that
A(1)
is the subalgebra of the Steenrod algebra generated by the Bockstein
\beta

\mathcal{P}^{1}

A(1)_{*}\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}[\zeta_{1}]/(\zeta_{1}^{3})\otimes E(\overline{\tau}_{0},\overline{\tau}_{1}).

In paticular,
A(1)_{*}
is a sub-Hopf algebra of
\Gamma
. The following proposition relies on the material in the first appendix of
[
12
]
. We recommend the reader look at Definition A1.1.15. The following lemma is easily checked.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following

A(1)_{*}\to\Gamma\to E(\overline{\tau}_{2})

is a cocentral extension of Hopf algebras over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
.

When one has an extension of Hopf algebras, one can consider the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence. In general, if

(D,\Phi)\to(A,\Gamma)\to(A,\Sigma)

is an extension of Hopf algebroids,
N
is a left comodule over
\Gamma
, then there is a natural convergent spectral sequence of the form

E_{2}^{f,s,t}=\Ext^{f,t}_{\Phi}(D,\Ext^{s}_{\Sigma}(A,N))\implies\Ext^{f+s,t}_{\Gamma}(A,N).

Here,
f
denotes the filtration degree,
s
is the cohomological degree, and
t
is the internal degree. The differentials are of the form

d_{r}:E_{r}^{f,s,t}\to E_{r}^{f+r,s-r+1,t}.

See A1.3.14 and A1.3.15 of
[
12
]
for details on this spectral sequence. Applied to our extension of Hopf algebras with
N=\mathbb{F}_{3}
, this spectral sequence takes on the form

(3.2)

E_{2}^{f,s,t}=\Ext^{f,t}_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3},\Ext^{s}_{E(\overline{\tau}_{2})}(\mathbb{F}_{3},\mathbb{F}_{3}))\implies\Ext^{f+s,t}_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3}).

Since
E(\overline{\tau}_{2})
is a primitively generated exterior Hopf algebra, we have that

\Ext_{E(\overline{\tau}_{2})}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}[v_{2}]

where the
(s,t)
-bidegree of
v_{2}
is
(1,17)
. Note that since
\mathbb{F}_{3}
is a comodule algebra, the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence is multiplicative.

In order to determine the
E_{2}
-page of this spectral sequence, we need to understand the coaction of
A(1)_{*}
on
\mathbb{F}_{3}[v_{2}]
. As
\mathbb{F}_{3}[v_{2}]
is a comodule algebra over
A(1)_{*}
, it is enough to determine the coaction on
v_{2}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Under the canonical
A(1)_{*}
-coaction on
\Ext_{E(\overline{\tau}_{2})}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
, the element
v_{2}
is a comodule primitive.

Proof.

Observe that the largest degree element of
A(1)_{*}
is
\zeta_{1}^{2}\overline{\tau}_{0}\overline{\tau}_{1}
, which has degree 14. Since

\Ext_{E(\overline{\tau}_{2})}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cong\mathbb{F}_{3}[v_{2}],\,\,\,\,|v_{2}|=(1,17)

the coaction on
v_{2}
must be
1\otimes v_{2}
for degree reasons.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The
E_{2}

E_{2}\cong\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\otimes\mathbb{F}_{3}[v_{2}]

(f,s,t)
-degree

Thus we must determine the cohomology of

This filtration is defined by assigning the generators of

Corollary 3.6

The cohomology of the Hopf algebra

Proof.

We have already shown that this is the
E_{\infty}
-page. All that remains to be shown is that there are no hidden extensions. First, note that in (

and

are two classes, then a hidden extension from

to

implies that the filtration of
z
is larger than that of

. Note also that there are no hidden extensions in the

-submodule generated by 1. This is seen, for example, by noticing that the map

induces a map in

\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\to\Ext_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3}).

So, the only possible hidden extensions would involve products of classes in the ideal generated by

. Suppose

and

are classes with

v_{2}^{i}x\cdot v_{2}^{j}y=v_{2}^{i+j}xy=0

with

•

|x|=(f_{0},0,t_{0})

•

|y|=(f_{1},0,t_{1})

•

.

(f_{0}+f_{1},i+j,t_{0}+t_{1}+17(i+k))

whereas the tri-degree for

4.
Determining the Adams
E_{2}
-term

In this section we will determine the
E_{2}
-term of the Adams spectral sequence converging to
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
. The way this will be achieved is by applying the functor
\Ext(-)
to the short exact sequence (
2.1
) to obtain a long exact sequence. Regarding this as a spectral sequence provides us with

E_{1}=\Ext(\Sigma^{8}\scr{B})\oplus\Ext(\scr{B})\implies\Ext(\mathrm{tmf}).

For the purposes of this paper, we will refer to this spectral sequence as the
algebraic spectral sequence
.

4.1.
Algebraic differentials

The short exact sequence (
2.1
) gives a multiplicative filtration of
H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
by
A_{*}
-comodules. More precisely, we filter
H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
by setting
F_{0}H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}=H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
and
F_{1}H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}:=(b_{4})
, the ideal generated by
b_{4}
. Since
b_{4}
is a comodule primitive, this is a filtration by comodules. The algebraic spectral sequence is then the spectral sequence associated to this filtration. Since the filtration is multiplicative, the spectral sequence is as well. Moreover, there is an isomorphism of
A_{*}
-comodule algebras

E_{0}H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}\cong\scr{B}\otimes E(b_{4})

with
\scr{B}
concentrated in filtration degree 0 and
b_{4}
a comodule primitive in filtration degree 1. Thus

E_{1}^{s,t,f}\cong\Ext_{\Gamma}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\otimes E(b_{4})

as a graded ring. Note that the bidegree of
b_{4}
is
(0,8,1)
. Since this spectral sequence arises from a long exact sequence in
\Ext
, there is only a
d_{1}
-differential which has the form

d_{1}:E_{1}^{s,t,0}\to E_{1}^{s+1,t,1}.

In depicting charts we will always use the Adams indexing convention and use the axes
(t-s,s)
and suppress the filtration degree.

Lemma 4.1
.

For degree reasons, the classes
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},b_{4},\beta
, and

are permanent cycles of the algebraic spectral sequence.

This observation and the multiplicativity of the spectral sequence eliminate many possible differentials.

From the known computation of

(cf.

), we see that
\pi_{15}\mathrm{tmf}=0
. In the
E_{1}
-term of the algebraic spectral sequence, there are two classes in stem 15; the class
b_{4}\alpha_{2}
and the class
c_{6}\alpha_{1}
. Both of these classes must die, but for degree reasons the only possibility is the following differential
1
1

1

The class

will be dealt with by an Adams differential.

d_{1}(v_{2})\,\dot{=}\,b_{4}\alpha_{2}.

Multiplicativity of the spectral sequence and the previous lemma yields the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

The algebraic spectral sequence has the following
d_{1}
-differentials

\displaystyle d_{1}(v_{2}^{i}v_{0}^{j}c_{6}^{k}\beta^{\ell}\alpha_{1}^{\epsilon})

\displaystyle\,\dot{=}\,v_{2}^{i-1}v_{0}^{j}c_{6}^{k}\beta^{\ell}\alpha_{1}^{\epsilon}b_{4}\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle i\not\equiv 0\mod 3

for natural numbers

and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
. There are no other differentials.

Consequently, this spectral sequence is periodic on the element
v_{2}^{3}
.

Remark 4.3
.

It would be nice to have an argument for this differential from first principles, but the author is not currently aware of one. He suspects this implies the existence of an interesting coproduct on

4.2.
Algebraic
E_{\infty}
-term

We will now describe a few patterns which make up the
E_{\infty}
-page of the algebraic spectral sequence. We will describe these patterns as certain modules over
\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
along with the monomial of the algebraic spectral sequence which generates it.

(Pattern 1)

Since
v_{2}^{3}
is a permanent cycle, we have the free
\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
modules on the powers of
v_{2}^{3}
and the
v_{2}^{3}
-multiples of
v_{2}^{2}b_{4}
, i.e. for all

\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cdot\{v_{2}^{3j},v_{2}^{3j+2}b_{4}\};

(Pattern 2)

For

\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})/(\alpha_{2})\cdot\{v_{2}^{j}b_{4}\}

(Pattern 3)

For

\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})/(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta)\cdot\{v_{0}v_{2}^{j}\}\oplus\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})/(\alpha_{2},v_{0})\cdot\{v_{2}^{j}\alpha_{2}\}.

The way we obtained these patterns was by noting that, as a module over

-page of the algebraic spectral sequence is freely generated by the monomials

E_{1}\cong\bigoplus_{j\geq 0,\,\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}}\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\cdot\{v_{2}^{j}b_{4}^{\varepsilon}\}.

The three patterns arise by partitioning the free modules

4.3.
Algebraic hidden extensions

As with any spectral sequence, there is the possibility of extension problems. We will show that there is a crucial hidden
v_{0}
-extension which will play an important role in the next section. Namely,

Proposition 4.5
.

In the algebraic spectral sequence, there is a hidden multiplicative extension

v_{0}\cdot(v_{2}^{2}\alpha_{2})\,\dot{=}\,v_{2}b_{4}c_{6}\alpha_{1},

consequently for every natural number
j
and

, we have the hidden extension

v_{0}\cdot v_{2}^{2}c_{6}^{j}\beta^{k}\alpha_{2}\,\dot{=}\,v_{2}c_{6}^{j+1}\beta^{k}b_{4}\alpha_{1}.

Remark 4.6
.

One might protest that this is not a hidden extension since

is an element in the correct Adams filtration. However, from the perspective of the algebraic spectral sequence,
v_{2}^{2}\alpha_{2}
has filtration 0 and
v_{2}b_{4}c_{6}\alpha_{2}
has filtration 1. Since
v_{0}
has filtration 0, this is in fact a hidden extension.

Before proving this, we will need to show the following.

Lemma 4.7
.

In
\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
, there is the Massey product

c_{6}\alpha_{1}\in\langle v_{0},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}\rangle.

and there is zero indeterminacy.

To prove this, we need to recall May’s Convergence theorem. The proof of this fact can be found as Theorem 4.1 of
[
10
]
, but we will only be interested in the case of a three-fold Massey product. The variant we will use is Theorem 2.2.2 of
[
3
]
. However, since we are working at an odd prime, we have to keep track of signs. In the following, if

is a class in degree

for

(see

Theorem 4.8

(May’s Convergence Theorem)
.

Let
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be elements of
\Ext
such that the Massey product
\langle\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
is defined. For each

, let

be a permanent cycle on the May
E_{r}
-page which detects
\alpha_{i}
. Suppose further that

(1)

The Massey product
\langle a_{0},a_{1},a_{2}\rangle
is defined on the
E_{r+1}
-page: there are
a_{01}
and
a_{12}
such that
d_{r}(a_{01})=\overline{a_{0}}a_{1}
and
d_{r}(a_{12})=\overline{a_{1}}a_{2}
.

(2)

If
(m,s,t)
is the tri-degree of either
a_{01}
or
a_{12}

and

\overline{a_{01}}a_{3}+\overline{a_{0}}a_{12}
is a permanent cycle and detects an element of
\langle\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\rangle
.

Remark 4.9
.

The second condition in May’s Convergence Theorem is often expressed by saying there are no “crossing differentials”.

We will use May’s convergence theorem to give a proof for Lemma

,
\alpha_{2}:=v_{1}\alpha_{1}
is a non-zero permanent cycle. One easily shows that

.

In
\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})

\alpha_{2}=\langle v_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}\rangle.

Proof of Lemma

.

Since

is exterior on the May

-page, we get the following defining system

-page we have

Since

Remark 4.11
.

Keep in mind that May’s convergence theorem is actually very general (cf. the discussion preceeding

.

In

We will use this corollary to derive the hidden multiplicative extension.

Proof of Proposition

.

One can check, using the May Convergence Theorem applied to the algebraic spectral sequence, that one has the Massey product

∎

We will also have occasion to use the following hidden extension.

5.
Rational Homotopy of
\mathrm{tmf}
and modular forms

In this section, we examine the
v_{0}
-inverted Adams spectral sequence for
\mathrm{tmf}
. In this case, the
v_{0}
-inverted Adams spectral sequence converges to the rational homotopy groups of

v_{0}^{-1}\Ext_{A_{*}}(H_{*}\mathrm{tmf})\implies\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}_{\mathbb{Q}}.

To determine the
v_{0}
-localized Adams

-term, we could take the decomposition from §
4.2

v_{0}^{-1}\Ext_{A(1)_{*}}(\mathbb{F}_{3})\otimes E(b_{4})\implies v_{0}^{-1}\Ext_{A_{*}}(H_{*}\mathrm{tmf}).

-localized algebraic

The spectrum
\mathrm{tmf}
has a close connection to classical modular forms. The ring of integral modular forms,

, has been known for quite some time.

Theorem 5.1

(cf.
[
4
]
)
.

The ring of integral modular forms is given by

MF_{*}=\mathbb{Z}[c_{4},c_{6},\Delta]/(c_{4}^{3}-c_{6}^{2}-12^{3}\Delta).

Here,

and

are the normalized Eisenstein series of weight 4 and 6 respectively. Topologically, these have degree 8 and 12 respectively. The modular form
\Delta
is often referred to as the

, and is a modular form of weight 12. The precise relationship between
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
and integral modular forms is made by examining the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence. We need to make use of the following.

Theorem 5.2

(cf.
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
)
.

The edge homomorphism for the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence for
\mathrm{tmf}
is a map

\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}\to MF_{*}(\mathbb{Z}_{(3)}):=MF_{*}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{(3)}

where
MF_{*}
is the ring of classical integral modular forms. The image of this map contains
c_{4},c_{6},3\Delta,3\Delta^{2}
and
\Delta^{3}
. Moreover, this map is a rational isomorphism.

Remark 5.3
.

Since the edge homomorphism is a map of rings, the theorem determines the image entirely.

Corollary 5.4
.

The rational homotopy groups of
\mathrm{tmf}
are given by

\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}_{\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbb{Q}[c_{4},c_{6}].

Theorem
5.2

-term detect in

The class

in the Adams

It follows for degree reasons that

d_{4}(v_{2}^{7}b_{4})\,\dot{=}\,\langle\beta^{2}\alpha_{1},v_{2}^{4}b_{4},\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}\rangle\,\dot{=}\,v_{2}^{6}\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}.

Proof.

A defining system for the first Massey product arises from the differential
d_{3}(v_{2}^{6})=\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}v_{2}^{4}b_{4}
. There is no indeterminacy, hence we have an equality. Since
\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}
and
v_{2}^{4}b_{4}
are permanent cycles, the differential is an immediate consequence of
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

We would like to derive a
d_{6}
-differential on
v_{2}^{7}b_{4}
to
\beta^{5}v_{2}^{4}b_{4}
. The argument will be similar to the one found in Corollary
6.31
.

Proposition 6.34
.

The class
b^{\prime}:=v_{2}^{6}\alpha_{1}
is given on the
E_{4}
-page by the Massey product

b^{\prime}=\langle v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\beta^{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}\rangle.

By Moss’ convergence theorem this class survives to a class
b^{\prime}
in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
given by the corresponding Toda bracket. Consequently, there is the hidden extension

b^{\prime}\cdot\alpha_{1}\,\dot{=}\,\beta^{3}v_{2}^{4}b_{4}

in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
.

Proof.

The argument is completely analogous to the one in Lemma
6.29
. Alternatively, we can derive this from Lemma
6.32
below by using a juggling theorem for Toda brackets

v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\cdot b=v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\cdot\langle\beta^{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}\rangle\subseteq\langle v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\beta^{2},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}\rangle.

Keep in mind these are Toda brackets in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
. We can do this since
v_{2}^{4}b_{4}
is a permanent cycle. The Toda bracket on the right hand side has indeterminacy in (see

v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\beta^{2}\cdot\pi_{7}\mathrm{tmf}+\pi_{83}\mathrm{tmf}\cdot\alpha_{1}.

It is seen on the

∎

Proposition 6.36
.

The class
v_{2}^{9}
is a permanent cycle.

Proof.

Note that
v_{2}^{9}
is in stem 144. Thus, any higher Adams differential supported by
v_{2}^{9}
must live in odd degree. The only odd degree elements in the
E_{3}
-term live in Pattern 1’ in Proposition
6.17
. In fact, since the generators of these patterns,
v_{2}^{3j}
or
v_{2}^{3j+1}b_{4}

v_{2}^{9}
are of the form
v_{2}^{3j}\beta^{k}\alpha_{1}
or
v_{2}^{3j+1}b_{4}\beta^{k}\alpha_{1}
. By examining the degrees of these elements, we see that the only ones in degree 143 are
v_{2}^{7}b_{4}\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}
or
\beta^{14}\alpha_{1}
.

The first option could be the target of a
d_{3}
-differential, but it follows from Proposition
6.24
, Lemma
6.32

d_{6}

v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\beta^{7}

v_{2}^{4}b_{4}\beta^{7}
where hit by a

3\leq r<6
, then we would not be able to exclude
v_{2}^{7}b_{4}\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}
as a target of a
d_{3}
-differential. However, this cannot happen since the only elements in stem 143 on the
E_{3}
-page are
v_{2}^{7}b_{4}\beta^{2}\alpha_{1}
and
\beta^{1}4\alpha_{1}
.

The other option,
\beta^{14}\alpha_{1}
could be the target of a
d_{20}
-differential, but the target is hit by an earlier
d_{3}
-differential supported by
\beta^{12}v_{2}b_{4}
. Thus
v_{2}^{9}
is a permanent cycle.

∎

6.4.
Hidden extensions

In the previous subsection we showed that the Adams spectral sequence for
\mathrm{tmf}
collapses at
E_{7}
and we completely computed this page via (
6.37
). We’ve already established one hidden extension in Proposition
6.30
, which corresponds to the single hidden extension occurring in the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence for
\mathrm{tmf}
.

However, there are several relations in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
which are apparent on the Adams-Novikov
E_{\infty}
-page appearing in the 0-line, but which are hidden from the perspective of the Adams spectral sequence. We make several observations.

Proposition 6.39
.

The class
v_{0}b_{4}
in
E_{\infty}
detects the class
c_{4}
in
\pi_{8}\mathrm{tmf}
up to a unit.

Proof.

From Theorem
5.2
, the modular form
c_{4}
is in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
. Since
c_{4}
is of degree 8 and a torsion free class, it must be detected by a class in stem 8 in the
E_{\infty}
-page which supports an entire
v_{0}
-tower. The only such class is
v_{0}b_{4}
.
∎

Looking at our chart for
E_{\infty}
, we find that there is a single
v_{0}
-tower in the
16
-stem which is generated by
v_{0}^{2}v_{2}
. This implies the following,

Proposition 6.40
.

The class
v_{0}^{2}v_{2}
detects the class
c_{4}^{2}
in
\pi_{16}\mathrm{tmf}
and we have the hidden extension
(v_{0}b_{4})\cdot(v_{0}b_{4})\,\dot{=}\,v_{0}^{2}v_{2}
in the
E_{\infty}
-term.

Remark 6.41
.

In light of Proposition
6.39
and Proposition
5.5
, we will abuse notation and write
v_{0}b_{4}
as
c_{4}
and
v_{1}^{3}
as
c_{6}
.

We can also say which classes are detecting the various classes involving
\Delta
in
\pi_{*}\mathrm{tmf}
. We will rename some classes in order to give more streamlined expressions. We will rename
b_{4}v_{2}
by
v_{2}^{3/2}
. Thus, for example, the class

refers to
b_{4}v_{2}^{4}

this expression stands for

is 0. However, this notation is motivated by a certain hidden extension which will appear shortly.

Note that from the results of the previous section, we have

Lemma 6.42
.

When

, the classes
(v_{2}^{3/2})^{j}
support a differential, and in this case the classes

.

For
j\equiv 1,2\mod 3

v_{0}(v_{2}^{3/2})^{j}

3\Delta^{j}
up to a unit. For

(v_{2}^{3/2})^{j}

Because these correspond to multiples of powers of

In

,

(v_{0}v_{2}^{3/2})\cdot v_{0}(v_{2}^{3/2})^{2\ell+1}\dot{=}v_{0}^{2}v_{2}^{3\ell+3}

We have the hidden extensions for odd

(v_{2}^{3/2})^{3}\cdot(v_{2}^{3/2})^{3j}\dot{=}(v_{2}^{3/2})^{3(j+1)}.

This corollary justifies our choice of notation. Finally, Theorem
5.2
and the famous relation of modular forms

c_{4}^{3}-c_{6}^{2}=1728\Delta=2^{3}3^{3}\Delta

Proposition 6.45

There is a hidden extension in

c_{4}\cdot(v_{0}^{2}v_{2})\,\dot{=}\,v_{0}^{3}b_{4}v_{2}+c_{6}^{2}

It is rather unsatisfying that these hidden extensions were determined by using the known multiplicative structure in

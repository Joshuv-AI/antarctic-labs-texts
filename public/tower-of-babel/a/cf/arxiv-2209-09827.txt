Abstract
. We introduce a general class of mean-field-like spin systems with random couplings that comprises both the Ising model on inhomogeneous dense random graphs and the randomly diluted Hopfield model. We are interested in quantitative estimates of metastability in large volumes at fixed temperatures when these systems evolve according to a Glauber dynamics, i.e. where spins flip with Metropolis transition probabilities at inverse temperature β \beta . We identify conditions ensuring that with high probability the system behaves like the corresponding system where the random couplings are replaced by their averages. More precisely, we prove that the metastability of the former system is implied with high probability by the metastability of the latter. Moreover, we consider relevant metastable hitting times of the two systems and find the asymptotic tail behaviour and the moments of their ratio. This work provides an extension of the results known for the Ising model on the Erdős–Rényi random graph. The proofs use the potential-theoretic approach to metastability in combination with concentration inequalities.

1.
Introduction

Over the last decade there has been increasing interest in metastability under Glauber dynamics of the Ising model with
random interactions
, in particular, of the Ising model on random graphs. Dommers
[
15
]
considered the case of random regular graphs, Dommers, den Hollander, Jovanovski, and Nardi
[
16
]
the configuration model, in both cases in finite volume and at low temperature. Mossel and Sly
[
20
,
21
]
computed mixing times on sparse Erdős-Rényi random graphs and on random regular graphs, in both cases in finite volume and at high temperature. Recently, Can, van der Hofstad, and Kumagai
[
10
]
analysed mixing times on random regular graphs, in large volumes and at fixed temperature.

Metastability under Glauber dynamics of the Ising model on dense random graphs has so far only been studied for the Erdős-Rényi random graph with fixed edge retention probability, by den Hollander and Jovanovski
[
14
]
and by Bovier, Marello, and Pulvirenti
[
9
]
. In both papers, mean metastable exit times of the random model are compared to those of the standard Curie-Weiss model, in large volumes and at fixed temperature. In
[
14
]
the pathwise approach to metastability (see Olivieri and Vares
[
22
]
) was used to prove that mean metastable exit times are asymptotically equal to those of the Curie-Weiss model, multiplied by a random prefactor of polynomial order in the size of the system. The prefactor estimate was improved in
[
9
]
by using the potential-theoretic approach to metastability (see Bovier and den Hollander
[
6
]
), at the expense of losing generality in the initial distribution. Recently, Bovier, den Hollander and Marello
[
7
]
studied metastability under Glauber dynamics of the Ising model on the complete graph with random independent couplings in large volumes and at fixed temperature. In that model, the product structure of the couplings allows for lumping of states, and for combining the potential-theoretic approach with coarse-graining techniques, to obtain sharp estimates on mean metastable exit times.

The present paper extends the results for the Erdős–Rényi random graph to inhomogeneous dense random graphs and to more general random interactions. We compare the metastable behaviour of a class of spin systems whose Hamiltonian has random and conditionally independent coupling coefficients, called
quenched model
, with the corresponding
annealed model
in which the coupling coefficients are replaced by their conditional mean. More precisely, we prove that metastability of the annealed model implies, in large volumes and at fixed temperature, almost sure metastability of the quenched model with respect to the metastable sets of the annealed model. Moreover, assuming metastability of the annealed model, we consider the ratio between the mean hitting times of the quenched model and the annealed model, and estimate both its tail behaviour and its moments, again in large volumes and at fixed temperature.

As in
[
9
]
, we follow the potential-theoretic approach to metastability, whichallows us to estimate mean metastable exit times by estimating
capacities
andweighted sums of the equilibrium potential called
harmonic sums
. Estimates on the former can be obtained with the help of well-known variational principles, while estimates on the latter are more involved. See, for instance, Bianchi, Bovier and Ioffe
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
, where long and model-dependent computations were needed to prove that the relevant contribution of the harmonic sum is localised around the starting metastable set. Schlichting and Slowik in
[
23
]
, using an alternative definition of metastable sets, prove that localisation of the harmonic sum around the starting metastable set holds in large generality. Their work allows us to derive results for a large class of models. A second novelty of the present paper compared to
[
9
]
concerns the techniques that are used to prove concentration results. In
[
9
]
, Talagrand’s concentration inequality was used, while here we use McDiarmid’s concentration inequality.

Model, results and methods

This section is structured as follows. In Section
2.1
, we introduce the model. In Section
2.2
, we define metastability, introduce relevant quantities, and state our main results. In Section
2.3

2.1.
The model

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\prob)
be an abstract probability space. Let
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
be a sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}
and
J=(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
be a triangular array of real random variables that are
conditionally independent
given
\mathcal{G}
and
uniformly bounded
, i.e., there exists a
k_{J}\in(0,\infty)
such that
|J_{ij}|\leq k_{J}

\prob
-a.s. for all
1\leq i<j<\infty
. We write
\prob_{\mathcal{G}}[\,\cdot\,]
to denote a regular conditional distribution for
J
given
\mathcal{G}
(which exists because
J
is a sequence of real random variables; see Chow and Teicher
[
11
, p. 218]
). Write
\mean
to denote the expectation with respect to
\prob
, and
\mean_{\mathcal{G}}
and
\Var_{\mathcal{G}}
to denote expectation and variance with respect to
\prob_{\mathcal{G}}
.

Given
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the Ising Hamiltonian with random couplings of the form

(2.1)

\displaystyle H_{N}(\sigma)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{N}J_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}-h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N},

with
h\in\mathbb{R}
the magnetic field and
\mathcal{S}_{N}\coloneqq\{-1,1\}^{N}
the set of spin configurations. The corresponding Gibbs measure on
\mathcal{S}_{N}
is denoted by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{N}(\sigma)\coloneqq Z_{N}^{-1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta H_{N}(\sigma)},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N},

with
\beta\in(0,\infty)
the inverse temperature and
Z_{N}
the normalizing partition function. The spin configurations evolve in time as a discrete-time Markov chain
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with state space
\mathcal{S}_{N}
and Glauber-Metropolis transition probabilities given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle p_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{N}\exp\bigl(-\beta\bigl[H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})-H_{N}(\sigma)\bigr]_{+}\bigr),\quad&\text{if }\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime},\\
1-\sum_{\eta\neq\sigma}p_{N}(\sigma,\eta),&\text{if }\sigma=\sigma^{\prime},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N},

where
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
means that
\sigma^{\prime}
is obtained from
\sigma
by a flip of a single spin. The associated (discrete) generator
L_{N}
acts on bounded functions
f:\mathcal{S}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\bigl(L_{N}f\bigr)(\sigma)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}p_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\bigl(f(\sigma^{\prime})-f(\sigma)\bigr),\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}.

Note that the stochastic process
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is irreducible and reversible with respect to the Gibbs measure
\mu_{N}
. We denote by
\Prob_{\nu}^{N}
the law of the Markov chain
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with initial distribution
\nu
. The corresponding expectation is denoted by
\Mean_{\nu}^{N}
. If the initial distribution is concentrated on a single configuration
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
, then we write
\Prob_{\sigma}^{N}
and
\Mean_{\sigma}^{N}
, respectively. For a non-empty subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
, let
\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}
be the first return time to
\mathcal{A}
, i.e.,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\equiv\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigl((\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\bigr)\coloneqq\inf\bigl\{t\in\mathbb{N}:\Sigma_{N}(t)\in\mathcal{A}\bigr\}.

Our main objective is to compare the evolution of the model with Hamiltonian
H_{N}
and the model with Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{N}
defined by

(2.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\coloneqq\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[H_{N}(\sigma)\bigr]=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{N}\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[J_{ij}\bigr]\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}-h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i},\quad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N},\quad\prob\text{-a.s.}

Throughout the paper, we use the superscript
\sim
to denote quantities that refer to the model defined in terms of
\widetilde{H}_{N}
. For instance,
((\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}},\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\sigma}^{N}\colon\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N})
denotes the discrete-time Markov chain with transitions probabilities
(\tilde{p}_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}))_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}
, which is reversible with respect to
\tilde{\mu}_{N}
, where both the transition probabilities and the Gibbs measure are defined as in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), but with
H_{N}
replaced by
\widetilde{H}_{N}
. For lack of better names and with abuse of terminology, we refer to the models defined in terms of
H_{N}
and
\widetilde{H}_{N}
as the
quenched model
and the
annealed model
, respectively.

In the sequel, we provide a list of motivating examples for which the results stated later hold. For this purpose, consider two sequences

(2.7)

(A_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty},\qquad(P_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty},

of triangular arrays with
|A_{ij}|\leq k_{J}
and
P_{ij}\in(0,1)
for
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i<j
, and let
\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\sigma(A_{ij},P_{ij}\colon 1\leq i<j\leq\infty)
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by these sequences. Moreover, let

(2.8)

(U_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}

be a triangular array of i.i.d. random variables distributed uniformly in
(0,1)
. Define

(2.9)

\displaystyle J_{ij}\coloneqq A_{ij}B_{ij},\qquad B_{ij}\coloneqq\mathbbm{1}_{\{U_{ij}\leq P_{ij}\}},\qquad 1\leq i<j<\infty.

Note that
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
and
(B_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
are triangular arrays of
conditionally independent
random variables given
\mathcal{G}
. In particular,
B_{ij}
are Bernoulli random variables with mean
P_{ij}
.

Example 2.1

(Ising model on the Erdős–Rényi random graph)
.

By choosing
A_{ij}\coloneqq 1
and
P_{ij}\coloneqq p\in(0,1]
for
1\leq i<j<\infty
,
\mathcal{G}
becomes the set
\{\emptyset,\Omega\}
and
H_{N}
in (
2.1
) becomes the Hamiltonian of the Ising model on the Erdős–Rényi random graph with edge retention probability
p
, known as the
randomly diluted Curie-Weiss model
. Its metastable behaviour was studied in
[
14
]
and
[
9
]
. In this case the annealed model is the Curie-Weiss model.

Example 2.2

(Ising model on inhomogeneous random graphs)
.

By taking
A_{ij}\coloneqq 1
for
1\leq i<j<\infty
,
H_{N}
in (
2.1
) becomes the Hamiltonian of the Ising model on an inhomogeneous random graph, in which an edge
(ij)
is present with probability
P_{ij}
. Of particular interest is the case
P_{ij}=V_{i}V_{j}
with
(V_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with support in
(0,1)
, known as the Ising model on the
Chung-Lu random graph

[
12
]
. The metastable behaviour of the corresponding annealed model was studied in
[
7
]
for the case where the random variables
V_{i}
have finite support.

Example 2.3

(Randomly diluted Hopfield model)
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
random patterns
\xi^{1},\dots,\xi^{n}
, with
\xi^{k}=(\xi_{i}^{k})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\xi_{i}^{k}\in{[-1,1]}
for
1\leq k\leq n
, set
A_{ij}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\xi^{k}_{i}\xi^{k}_{j}
. By taking
P_{ij}\equiv p\in(0,1)
for
1\leq i<j<\infty
,
H_{N}
in (
2.1
) becomes the Hamiltonian of a
Hopfield model
in which the interaction coefficients are
randomly diluted
by i.i.d. Bernoulli random variables with mean
p
. See Bovier and Gayrard
[
8
]
for a review on the Hopfield model. The metastable behaviour of the annealed model, i.e., the undiluted Hopfield model, was studied by an der Heiden in
[
1
]
in a restricted
(\beta,h)
-regime. We plan to address the metastable behaviour in a more general
(\beta,h)
-regime in a future paper.

2.2.
Metastability and main results

Before stating our main results, we recall the definition of metastable Markov chains and metastable sets put forward in Schlichting and Slowik
[
23
, Definition 1.1]
.

Definition 2.4

(
\rho_{N}
-metastability and metastable sets)
.

For
\rho_{N}>0
and
2\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\dots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
be a set of subsets of
\mathcal{S}_{N}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{i,N}\cap\mathcal{M}_{j,N}=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\neq j\leq K
. The Markov process
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is called
\rho_{N}
-metastable
with respect to
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
when

(2.10)

\displaystyle K\,\frac{\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}\Bigr]}{\min_{\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{N}}\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\Bigr]}\leq\rho_{N}\ll 1,

where
\mathcal{M}_{N}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{i,N}
and, for a non-empty set
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{N}
,
\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}
denotes the Gibbs measure
\mu_{N}
conditioned on the set
\mathcal{X}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The advantage of this definition compared to the one given in Bovier and den Hollander
[
6
, Definition 8.2]
is twofold: it allows for direct control of
\ell^{p}(\mu_{N})
-norms of functions, and does not depend on the cardinality of the state space. For a more detailed comparison of the two definitions of metastability we refer to
[
23
, Remark 1.2]
.

For fixed
2\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
and
k_{1}>0
, define

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)\coloneqq\Bigl\{\omega\colon\exists\,\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}(\omega)\text{ non-empty disjoint subsets of }\mathcal{S}_{N}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\text{ s.t. }(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\omega)\text{ is $\mathrm{e}^{-k_{1}N}$-metastable}\text{ w.r.t. }\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}(\omega)\Bigr\},

i.e., the event that the Markov chain
(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is
\tilde{\rho}_{N}
-metastable with respect to some
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
, where we abbreviate

(2.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}_{N}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-k_{1}N}.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that both
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
and
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
– playing the role of
metastable sets
– are
\mathcal{G}
-measurable, because they are defined in terms of the annealed Hamiltonian
\widetilde{H}_{N}
.

In our main results we impose the following assumption on the annealed model.

Assumption 2.7

(Metastability of the annealed model)
.

For some
(\beta,h)

\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, the following holds for the Markov chain
(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of the annealed model. There exist
2\leq K\in\mathbb{N}
and
k_{1}>0
such that,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\prob\biggl[\liminf_{N\to\infty}\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)\biggr]=1,

where
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
is defined in (
2.11
) and depends on
K
and
k_{1}
.

Remark 2.8
.

Assertion of Assumption
2.7
can be rephrased as follows:
\prob
-a.s., there exists a finite random variable
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
(\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\})_{N\geq N_{0}}
of
K
non-empty mutually disjoint subsets of
\mathcal{S}_{N}
(possibly depending on
\omega
) such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
the process
(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is
\tilde{\rho}_{N}\coloneqq\mathrm{e}^{-k_{1}N}
-metastable with respect to
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
.

Remark 2.9
.

Let us illustrate in the case of Example
2.1
how to identify candidates of metastable sets. It is well know (cf.
[
5
, Section 3.5]
) that, for any
\beta\in(0,\infty)
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\widetilde{Z}_{N}=-\inf_{x\in[-1,1]}\widetilde{F}_{\beta,h}(x),

where
\widetilde{F}_{\beta,h}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the
free energy
per vertex of the annealed model given by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}_{\beta,h}(x)\coloneqq-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-hx+\frac{1}{\beta}\biggl(\frac{1-x}{2}\log\frac{1-x}{2}+\frac{1+x}{2}\log\frac{1+x}{2}\biggr).

In particular, for any
\beta>\beta_{c}\coloneqq 1
and
h\in(-h_{c}(\beta),h_{c}(\beta))
, where the critical strength of the magnetic field is given by

\displaystyle h_{c}(\beta)\coloneqq\sqrt{1-\beta^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\beta}\log\bigl(\beta(1+\sqrt{1-1/\beta})^{2}\bigr),

the free energy
\widetilde{F}_{\beta,h}
admits two local minima
m_{1},m_{2}\in(-1,1)
. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
m_{1,N}
and
m_{2,N}
be the closest point in
\{-1,-1+2/N,\ldots,1-2/N,1\}
to
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, respectively. Define the sets
\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\mathcal{M}_{2,N}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
as the (set-valued) pre-image of the empirical magnetization
\mathcal{S}_{N}\ni\sigma\mapsto m_{N}(\sigma)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}
, i.e.,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1,N}\coloneqq m_{N}^{-1}(m_{1,N})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{2,N}\coloneqq m_{N}^{-1}(m_{2,N}).

By using arguments similar to those given in
[
23
, Lemma 4.1]
, it follows that
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\mathcal{M}_{2,N}\}
forms a pair of metastable sets in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

For fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
, given the metastable sets
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
, we can decompose the state space
\mathcal{S}_{N}
into the domains of attraction with to respect the dynamics of the annealed model. More precisely, by following
[
23
, Definition 1.4]
, within the event
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
the metastable sets
\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}
give rise to a
metastable partition

\{\mathcal{S}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{S}_{K,N}\}
of the state space
\mathcal{S}_{N}
such that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i,N}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{i,N}\subset\mathcal{V}_{i,N},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.

The
local valley

\mathcal{V}_{i,N}
around the metastable set
\mathcal{M}_{i,N}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{i,N}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{i,N}\cup

\displaystyle\biggl\{\sigma\in\mathcal{M}_{N}^{c}\colon

(2.18)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\sigma}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{i,N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{i,N}}^{N}\Bigr]\geq\max_{j\neq i}\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\sigma}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}\Bigr]\biggr\},

where we recall that
\mathcal{M}_{N}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{i,N}
.

Our first theorem says that, subject to Assumption
2.7
,
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
also exhibits metastable behaviour in the sense of Definition
2.4
.

Theorem 2.10

(Metastability)
.

Suppose that
(\beta,h)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies Assumption
2.7
. Then, for any
c_{0}\in(0,k_{1})
, the event

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)\coloneqq\Bigl\{\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)\colon(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}(\omega)\text{ is $\mathrm{e}^{-c_{0}N}$-metastable w.r.t.}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\}(\omega)\Bigr\}

satisfies

(2.20)

\displaystyle\prob\biggl[\liminf_{N\to\infty}\Omega_{\textnormal{meta}}(N)\biggr]=1.

Label the metastable sets
\mathcal{M}_{1,N},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{K,N}
in such a way that they are ordered decreasingly according to their weights under the Gibbs measure of the annealed model, i.e., for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, on the event
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
,

(2.21)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{M}_{1,N}\bigr]\geq\tilde{\mu}_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{M}_{2,N}\bigr]\geq\ldots\geq\tilde{\mu}_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{M}_{K,N}\bigr],\qquad\prob\text{-a.s.}

Fix
i\in\{2,\ldots,K\}
such that there exists a
k_{2}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

(2.22)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{N}[\mathcal{S}_{j,N}]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-k_{2}N}\tilde{\mu}_{N}[\mathcal{S}_{i,N}],\qquad\prob\text{-a.s., }\forall j\in\{i+1,\ldots,K\},\,N\in\mathbb{N},

and set, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
\prob
-a.s. on the event
\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
,

(2.23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{i,N},\qquad\mathcal{B}_{N}\coloneqq\bigcup_{j=1}^{i-1}\mathcal{M}_{j,N}.

Note that
\mathcal{B}_{N}
is the union of all metastable sets with weight not smaller than the weight of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
. Furthermore, note that, since for
i=K
the condition in (
2.22
), to which we will also refer as the
non-degeneracy
condition, is void, the existence of such an index
i
is always guaranteed. However, if the condition holds for further
i
, one has more freedom in the choice of the set
\mathcal{A}_{N}
, making our results more general.

Remark 2.11
.

The non-degeneracy condition in (
2.22
) can be relaxed by replacing
\mathrm{e}^{-k_{2}N}
with some
\tilde{\delta}_{N}
satisfying
\mathrm{e}^{-k_{2}N}\leq\tilde{\delta}_{N}<\mathrm{e}^{-c\sqrt{N}}
for some sufficiently large
c\in(0,\infty)
. The technical reasons can be found in the proofs in Section
5
.

Before proceeding, we define, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and non-empty disjoint sets
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
, the so-called
last-exit biased distribution
on
\mathcal{A}
for the transition from
\mathcal{A}
to
\mathcal{B}
by

(2.24)

\displaystyle\nu_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}(\sigma)\equiv\nu_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}(\sigma)=\frac{\mu_{N}(\sigma)\Prob_{\sigma}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigr]}{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}}\mu_{N}(\sigma)\Prob_{\sigma}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigr]},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{A}.

This distribution plays an essential role in the potential-theoretic approach to metastability, as can be seen in (
2.29
) below.

We are now ready to state our second theorem, in which we compare the mean hitting time of
\mathcal{B}_{N}
for the Markov chain
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
starting from the set
\mathcal{A}_{N}
according to the distribution
\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}
with the corresponding quantity for the Markov chain
(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Under the regular conditional distribution
\prob_{\mathcal{G}}
, we obtain for the ratio of these metastable hitting times estimates both on its tail behaviour and on its moments.

Theorem 2.12
.

Suppose that
(\beta,h)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
satisfies Assumption
2.7
. Set

(2.25)

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}\coloneqq\frac{\beta^{2}}{2N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{N}\Var_{\mathcal{G}}[J_{ij}].

(i)

For
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\prob
-a.s.,

(2.26)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\Biggl[\mathrm{e}^{-t-\alpha_{N}}\leq\frac{\Mean_{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]}{\tilde{\Mean}\mspace{-12mu}\phantom{\Mean}_{\tilde{\nu}_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]}\leq\mathrm{e}^{+t+2\alpha_{N}}\Biggr]\geq 1-4\,\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/(2\beta k_{J})^{2}}.

(ii)

For any
q\geq 1
and

, let

\displaystyle\Omega_{q,c}(N)\coloneqq

(2.27)

\displaystyle\Biggl\{\omega\colon\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1-\tfrac{c}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)\leq\frac{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\Mean_{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]^{q}\Bigr]^{1/q}(\omega)}{\tilde{\Mean}\mspace{-12mu}\phantom{\Mean}_{\tilde{\nu}_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr](\omega)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{4q\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1+\tfrac{c}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)\Biggr\}.

Then, for any
q\geq 1
there exists

such that

(2.28)

\displaystyle\prob\Biggl[\liminf_{N\to\infty}\Omega_{q,c_{1}}(N)\Biggr]=1.

Remark 2.13
.

(a)

Since the random variables
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
are assumed to be uniformly bounded, it follows that
\alpha_{N}=O(1)
.

(b)

The non-degeneracy condition (
2.22
) embedded in the definition (
2.23
) of
\mathcal{A}_{N}
ensures that, if
i<K
, the metastable sets
\mathcal{M}_{j,N}
,
j\in\{i+1,\dots,K\}
, are not relevant for the analysis of the crossing times from
\mathcal{A}_{N}
to
\mathcal{B}_{N}
.

(c)

The choice of the initial configuration being drawn from the (quenched) last-exit biased distribution plays an important role in the potential-theoretic approach to metastability. Indeed, under this particular initial distribution supported on (the inner boundary of) the set
\mathcal{A}_{N}
the mean hitting time,
\Mean_{\sigma}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]
, of the set
\mathcal{B}_{N}
can be represented in terms of two analytic quantities: the harmonic function,
h_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}
, and the capacity,
\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N})
, see (
2.29
). If
\mathcal{A}_{N}
and
\mathcal{B}_{N}
are metastable sets, one expects that the function
\sigma\mapsto\Mean_{\sigma}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]
is almost constant on the set
\mathcal{A}_{N}
. Such regularity statement has been proven for certain spin systems by means of coupling methods, cf.
[
3
,
18
,
19
]
. The construction of a suitable coupling in the present setting, while expected to be possible, requires some highly non-trivial adaptation of the previously used strategies, due to the random interaction, that goes beyond the scope of the present paper.

Remark 2.14
.

(a)

The results still hold true for different reversible, single spin update dynamics. However, the Glauber dynamics with Metropolis transition rate is a standard choice.

(b)

The assertions of both Theorem
2.10
and
2.12
are still valid beyond the regime of uniformly bounded random variables. The forthcoming paper
[
17
]
studies the Potts model with random interactions assuming that
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
are i.i.d. unbounded random variables with finite exponential moments.

2.3.
Methods and outline

2.3.1.
Key notions from the potential-theoretic approach to metastability

To prove Theorems
2.10
and
2.12
, we crucially rely on potential theory, which allows us to express probabilistic objects of interest in terms of solutions of certain boundary value problems. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
6
, Corollary 7.11]
) that, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and any non-empty disjoint
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
, the mean hitting time of
\mathcal{B}
starting from the last-exit biased distribution
\nu_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}
on
\mathcal{A}
is given by

(2.29)

\displaystyle\Mean_{\nu_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}\bigr]=\frac{\lVert h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\rVert_{\mu_{N}}}{\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})},

where
\lVert h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\rVert_{\mu_{N}}
denotes the
\ell^{1}(\mu_{N})
-norm of the
equilibrium potential

h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}
of the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
, i.e., the function
h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}:\mathcal{S}_{N}\to[0,1]
that is the unique solution of the boundary value problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}\big(L_{N}f\big)(\sigma)&\mspace{-5mu}=\mspace{-5mu}&0,&\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}\setminus(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}),\\
f(\sigma)&\mspace{-5mu}=\mspace{-5mu}&\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(\sigma),&\sigma\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}.\end{array}\right.

Note that the equilibrium potential has a natural interpretation in terms of
hitting probabilities
, namely,
h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}(\sigma)=\Prob_{\sigma}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}\bigr]
, for all
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}\setminus(\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B})
. The
capacity

\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
of the pair
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
is defined by

(2.32)

\displaystyle\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}}\mu_{N}(\sigma)\Prob_{\sigma}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigr]=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}}\mu_{N}(\sigma)\bigl(-L_{N}h_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\bigr)(\sigma).

From this definition it is clear that

(2.33)

\displaystyle\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{A}}^{N}\bigr]=\frac{\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})}{\mu_{N}[\mathcal{A}]},

where
\mu_{N}|\mathcal{A}
denotes the Gibbs measure
\mu_{N}
conditioned on the set
\mathcal{A}
.

Capacities can be expressed in terms of variational principles that are very useful to obtain upper und lower bounds (see
[
6
, Section 7.3]
for more details). Upper bounds are obtained by using the
Dirichlet principle
, which states that

(2.34)

\displaystyle\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\inf\bigl\{\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)\colon f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\bigr\}.

Here,
\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}
denotes the set of all functions from
\mathcal{S}_{N}
to
\mathbb{R}
that are equal to
1
on
\mathcal{A}
and
0
on
\mathcal{B}
, and

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)\coloneqq\langle f,-L_{N}f\rangle_{\mu_{N}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\mu_{N}(\sigma)p_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})\bigl(f(\sigma)-f(\sigma^{\prime})\bigr)^{2}

is the
Dirichlet form
. We recall that the transition probabilities
p_{N}
are defined in (
2.3
).

Lower bounds are obtained via the
Thomson principle
, which states that

(2.36)

\displaystyle\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\sup\Bigl\{\frac{1}{\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)}\colon\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\Bigr\}=\Bigl(\inf\bigl\{\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\colon\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}\bigr\}\Bigr)^{-1},

where
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}}^{N}
is the space of all unit antisymmetric
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{B}
-flows
\varphi:\mathcal{S}_{N}\times\mathcal{S}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, and

(2.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}\\
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})^{2}}{\mu_{N}(\sigma)\,p_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})}.

2.3.2.
Strategy of proofs

The proof of Theorem
2.10
relies on Definition
2.4
and on (
2.33
), together with an application of the Dirichlet principle in combination with a comparison of the quenched Hamiltonian
H_{N}
and the annealed Hamiltonian
\widetilde{H}_{N}
on a particular event of high probability.

We prove Theorem
2.12
(i) by combining concentration inequalities for the logarithm of the mean hitting time
\Mean_{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]
of the quenched model with bounds on the distance between the (conditional on
\mathcal{G}
) mean of that logarithm and the logarithm of the mean hitting time
\tilde{\Mean}\mspace{-12mu}\phantom{\Mean}_{\tilde{\nu}_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}\bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\bigr]
of the annealed model. Estimates of the latter type, comparing conditional means with average means, will be called
annealed estimates
. The results in Theorem
2.12
(ii) are annealed estimates as well. In view of (
2.29
), estimates on the mean hitting time
\Mean_{\nu_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}}^{N}[\tau_{\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}]
, or on its logarithm, will follow once we have separately proven corresponding estimates for both
Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N})
and
Z_{N}\lVert h_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\rVert_{\mu_{N}}
.

To prove concentration inequalities for the quantities
\log[Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N})]
and
\log[Z_{N}\lVert h_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\rVert_{\mu_{N}}]
, we use a conditional version of McDiarmid’s bounded differences inequality (see Proposition
A.1
below). This strategy for proving concentration is different from the one used in
[
9
]
, where Talagrand’s concentration inequality was used. The advantage of McDiarmid over Talagrand is twofold. First, McDiarmid’s inequality provides exact constants. Second, it does not require convexity of the map
J\mapsto\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N})\bigr)
, which is crucial because we do not know how to prove convexity.

Estimates on capacities for Theorem
2.12
are proven by using the Dirichlet principle and the Thomson principle, and do not require any assumption on metastability. Finding estimates on the equilibrium potential, however, is more involved. We use a result that is similar to
[
23
, Theorem 1.7]
(Proposition
5.2
below), for which the non-degeneracy assumption in (
2.22
) is required, together with the same comparison of the Hamiltonians
H_{N}
and
\widetilde{H}_{N}
that is used in the proof of Theorem
2.10
, both holding with high probability. We emphasise that the constants appearing in our statements may depend on the parameters of the model.

2.3.3.
Outline

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
3
we provide the proof of Theorem
2.10
on metastability of the quenched model. In Section
4
we provide estimates on capacities. Section
5
is devoted to stating and proving estimates on weighted sums of the equilibrium potential, called harmonic sums. In Section
6
we prove Theorem
2.12
by using the results of the previous sections. Appendix
A
contains the conditional version of the McDiarmid’s inequality that is used in the paper.

3.
Metastability

Before proving Theorem
2.10
, we provide in Lemma
3.1
a comparison of the quenched Hamiltonian
H_{N}
and the annealed Hamiltonian
\widetilde{H}_{N}
. This lemma will be used both in the proof of Theorem
2.10
below and in Section
5
, where we deal with estimates on the equilibrium potential.

Given a positive real sequence
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, let

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Xi(a_{N})\coloneqq\Bigl\{\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\bigl|H_{N}(\sigma)-\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\bigr|<a_{N}\Bigr\}\subset\Omega,\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\},

denote the event that, uniformly in
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,
H_{N}
differs from
\widetilde{H}_{N}
by at most
a_{N}
. On the event
\Xi(a_{N})
we have control on the difference between the quantities determining
(\Sigma_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and
(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{N}(t))_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Moreover, for suitably chosen sequences
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, the event
\Xi(a_{N})^{c}
turns out to be negligible in the limit as
N\to\infty
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a positive real sequence
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
, set
b_{N}\coloneqq a_{N}^{2}/k_{J}^{2}-(N+1)\log 2
. Then,
\prob
-a.s.,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\Xi(a_{N})^{c}\bigr]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-b_{N}}\wedge 1,\qquad\forall N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.

Proof.

Fix
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly, it suffices to prove (
3.2
) in case
b_{N}>0
.
Notice that the map
J\longmapsto H_{N}(\sigma)
satisfies a bounded difference as (
A.1
) with constant
2k_{J}/N
uniformly in
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
, because
H_{N}(\sigma)
depends linearly on the random coupling
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
and we assumed in Section
2.1
that the
|J_{ij}|\leq k_{J}

\prob
-a.s. for all
1\leq i<j<\infty
.
Since in addition the triangular array
J=(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
is assumed to be conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
, we can apply McDiarmid’s concentration inequality (Proposition
A.1
) with
v=k_{J}^{2}(N-1)/(2N)
, together with a union bound, to get that,
\prob
-a.s.,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\max_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\bigl|H_{N}(\sigma)-\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\bigr|\geq a_{N}\Bigr]&\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}}\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl|H_{N}(\sigma)-\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\bigr|\geq a_{N}\Bigr]\\
&\leq 2^{N+1}\exp\biggl(-\frac{a_{N}^{2}N}{k_{J}^{2}(N-1)}\biggr),\end{split}

where the additional factor
2
comes from the absolute value.
Since
(N-1)/N\leq 1
, (
3.2
) follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

We will prove that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\prob\biggl[\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Omega_{\textnormal{meta}}(N)^{c}\biggr]=0,

which is equivalent to (
2.20
).
First note that, by the choice of
\mu_{N}
and
p_{N}
in (

) and (

),

(3.5)

\displaystyle Z_{N}\mu_{N}(\sigma)\,p_{N}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{e}^{-\beta(H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime}))},\qquad\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}.

An elementary computation yields that, on the event
\Xi(a_{N})
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\vee\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})-a_{N}\leq H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})\leq\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\vee\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime})+a_{N},\quad\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}.

Thus, by a comparison of Dirichlet forms it follows that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a_{N}}\leq Z_{N}\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)\leq\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{\beta a_{N}},

for any

. In view of the Dirichlet principle (

), we deduce that, on the event

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a_{N}}\leq\frac{Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\beta a_{N}},\qquad\emptyset\neq\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}\text{ disjoint}.

Moreover, for any
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, on the event
\Xi(a_{N})
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\beta a_{N}}\leq\frac{Z_{N}\mu_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{X}\bigr]}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}\tilde{\mu}_{N}\bigl[\mathcal{X}\bigr]}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\beta a_{N}},\qquad\emptyset\neq\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}.

It follows from (
2.33
), (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) that, on the event
\Xi(a_{N})
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta a_{N}}\leq\frac{\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigr]}{\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}<\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigr]}\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\beta a_{N}},\qquad\emptyset\neq\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}\text{ disjoint}.

Thus, on the event
\Xi(a_{N})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
,

\displaystyle\max_{j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}

\displaystyle\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{j,N}}^{N}\Bigr]

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\rho}_{N}}{K}\,\mathrm{e}^{4\beta a_{N}}\min_{\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{N}}\Prob_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}}^{N}\Bigl[\tau_{\mathcal{M}_{N}}^{N}<\tau_{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\Bigr].

Now set
a_{N}=k_{J}\sqrt{Nk_{1}+(N+1)\log 2}
for
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, and note that, with this choice of

\displaystyle\prob\bigl[\Xi(a_{N})^{c}\bigr]=\mean\bigl[\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\Xi(a_{N})^{c}\bigr]\bigr]\leq\mathrm{e}^{-b_{N}}=\mathrm{e}^{-k_{1}N},\qquad N\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{1\}.

. By choosing

\displaystyle\prob\biggl[\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Omega_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)^{c}\biggr]\;

4.
Capacity estimates

In this section we provide general estimates on the capacity of the quenched model compared to the annealed model. These estimates are general because we do not require any assumption on metastability, and the sets involved in the estimates are general disjoint subsets of the configuration space.

In Section
4.1
we prove concentration for the logarithm of the capacities by using the Dirichlet principle and McDiarmid’s concentration inequality. In Section
4.2
we first estimate the conditional mean of
Z_{N}\mu_{N}
and
p_{N}
in terms of the corresponding quantities of the annealed model
\widetilde{Z}_{N}\tilde{\mu}_{N}
and
\tilde{p}_{N}
in Lemma
4.2
, and afterwards prove annealed capacity estimates by using both the Dirichlet and the Thomson principle, together with Lemma
4.2
. The latter is crucial also in the proof of annealed estimates of
\lVert h_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}\rVert_{\mu_{N}}
in Section
5.3
.

All formulas in this Section
4
are intended to hold
\prob
-a.s. In order to lighten notation, we refrain from repeating that.

4.1.
Concentration of quenched capacities

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider two non-empty disjoint subsets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
. Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\prob_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl|\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)-\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)\bigr]\bigr|>t\Bigr]\leq 2\,\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}/(\beta k_{J})^{2}}.

Proof.

First, recall that the triangular array
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
is assumed to be conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
. Hence, in view of McDiarmid’s concentration inequality (Proposition
A.1
), the assertion in (
4.1
) is immediate once we show that, for any
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, the mapping

(4.2)

\displaystyle(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\longmapsto F_{N}\bigl((J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigr)\coloneqq\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)

satisfies a bounded difference estimate. More precisely, it is sufficient to show that, for any
1\leq k<l\leq N
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\bigl|F_{N}\bigl((J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigr)-F_{N}\bigl((J_{ij}^{\prime})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\bigr)\bigr|\leq\frac{2\beta k_{J}}{N},

where
J_{ij}^{\prime}\coloneqq J_{ij}
for all
1\leq i<j\leq N
such that
(i,j)\neq(k,l)
, and
J_{kl}^{\prime}
is a conditionally independent copy of
J_{kl}
given
\mathcal{G}
. In the sequel, we write
H_{N}^{J}
,
Z_{N}^{J}
,
\mathcal{E}_{N}^{J}
and
\capa_{N}^{J}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
to emphasise the dependence on the random coupling
J=(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
.

We proceed by following the same line of argument that led to (
3.8
) in the proof of Theorem
2.10
. By

(4.4)

\displaystyle\bigl|H_{N}^{J}(\sigma)-H_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\sigma)\bigr|=\frac{\bigl|J_{kl}-J_{kl}^{\prime}\bigr|}{N}\leq\frac{2k_{J}}{N},\qquad\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N},

an elementary computation yields that, for any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle H_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime})-\frac{2k_{J}}{N}\leq H_{N}^{J}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}^{J}(\sigma^{\prime})\leq H_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\sigma^{\prime})+\frac{2k_{J}}{N}.

Thus, by a comparison of Dirichlet forms, we obtain, for any
f:\mathcal{S}_{N}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(4.6)

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{J^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta k_{J}/N}\leq Z_{N}^{J}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{J}(f)\leq Z_{N}^{J^{\prime}}\mathcal{E}_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{2\beta k_{J}/N}.

In view of the Dirichlet principle (
2.34
), we deduce that

(4.7)

\displaystyle Z_{N}^{J^{\prime}}\capa_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\beta k_{J}/N}\leq Z_{N}^{J}\capa_{N}^{J}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq Z_{N}^{J^{\prime}}\capa_{N}^{J^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\,\mathrm{e}^{2\beta k_{J}/N},

which yields (
4.3
).
∎

4.2.
Annealed capacity estimates

Notation 1
.

For any three sequences
(a_{N})_{N\geq 0},(b_{N})_{N\geq 0}
,
(c_{N})_{N\geq 0}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the notation
a_{N}=b_{N}+O(c_{N})
means that there exists a
C\in(0,\infty)
independent of
\omega
and
N
such that

(4.8)

\displaystyle-Cc_{N}\leq a_{N}-b_{N}\leq Cc_{N}.

Before proving annealed capacity estimates, we prove the following lemma which is used both in the current section and for proving further annealed estimates in Section
5.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
the following hold:

(i)

For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta\Delta_{N}(\sigma)}\Bigr]=\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1})\bigr),

(ii)

For any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
with
\sigma\sim\sigma^{\prime}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta(H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime}))}\Bigr]=\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta(\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\vee\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime}))}\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1/2})\bigr),

where
\alpha_{N}
is defined in (
2.25
) and
\Delta_{N}(\sigma)\coloneqq H_{N}(\sigma)-\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
.

Proof.

(i)
Denote by
\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\Lambda_{ij}(t)\coloneqq\log\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\exp\bigl(t(J_{ij}-\mean_{\mathcal{G}}[J_{ij}])\bigr)\bigr]
the conditional log-moment generating function given
\mathcal{G}
. By a Taylor expansion up to the third order, we get that, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{ij}(t)=\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Var_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[J_{ij}\bigr]+\frac{t^{3}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)^{2}\Lambda_{ij}^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta t)\mathrm{d}\theta.

Since the random variables are assumed to be uniformly bounded, i.e.,
|J_{ij}|\leq k_{J}
, an elementary computation exploiting Cramér’s measure yields that,
|\Lambda_{ij}^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 6k_{J}^{3}
. Hence

(4.12)

\displaystyle\bigl|\Lambda_{ij}(t)-\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Var_{\mathcal{G}}[J_{ij}]\bigr|\leq k_{J}^{3}\left\lvert t\right\rvert^{3}.

Since the triangular array
(J_{ij})_{1\leq i<j<\infty}
is conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
, we have

(4.13)

\displaystyle\Bigl|\log\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta\Delta_{N}(\sigma)}\Bigr]-\alpha_{N}\Bigr|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i<j\end{subarray}}^{N}\Bigl|\Lambda_{ij}\bigl(\tfrac{\mp\beta}{N}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\bigr)-\frac{(\mp\beta\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j})^{2}}{2N^{2}}\Var_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[J_{ij}\bigr]\Bigr|\leq\frac{(\beta k_{J})^{3}}{2N},

which concludes the proof of (
4.9
). In particular, for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,

(4.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)+\alpha_{N}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-(\beta k_{J})^{3}/2N}\leq\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta H_{N}(\sigma)}\Bigr]\leq\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)+\alpha_{N}}\,\mathrm{e}^{(\beta k_{J})^{3}/2N}.

(ii)
Because the proofs of (
4.10
) for
\pm\beta
are similar, we give a detailed proof for
+\beta
only. Since the conditional expectation of the maximum of two random variables is bounded from below by the maximum of their conditional expectations, it is immediate from (
4.14
) that, for any
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta(H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{\prime}))}\Bigr]\geq\mathrm{e}^{\beta(\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\vee\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma^{\prime}))}\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1-\frac{(\beta k_{J})^{3}}{2N}\Bigr).

Thus, we are left with proving the desired upper bound. For this purpose, we define, for
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,

(4.16)

\displaystyle H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)\coloneqq-\sigma_{k}\biggl(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j>k\end{subarray}}^{N}J_{kj}\sigma_{j}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i<k\end{subarray}}^{N}J_{ik}\sigma_{i}+h\biggr),

and set
H_{N}^{\neq k}(\sigma)\coloneqq H_{N}(\sigma)-H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)
. Denoting by
\sigma^{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
the configuration that is obtained from
\sigma
by flipping the spin at site
k\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
, we get
H_{N}^{\neq k}(\sigma)=H_{N}^{\neq k}(\sigma^{k})
and
H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)=-H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})
. Since for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
the random variables
H_{N}^{\neq k}(\sigma)
and
H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)
are conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
, it follows that

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta(H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{k}))}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{\neq k}(\sigma)}\Bigr]\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\vee\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr].

In order to estimate the second term, note that for any two non-negative random variables
X,Y
with finite second moment, we have

\displaystyle\mean[X\vee Y]

\displaystyle=\mean[X]\vee\mean[Y]+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\mean\bigl[|X-Y|\bigr]-\bigl|\mean[X]-\mean[Y]\bigr|\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\mean[X]\vee\mean[Y]+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\mean\bigl[|X-\mean[X]|\bigr]+\mean\bigl[|Y-\mean[Y]|\bigr]\Bigr)

(4.18)

\displaystyle\leq\mean[X]\vee\mean[Y]+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\sqrt{\Var[X]}+\sqrt{\Var[Y]}\Bigr),

where we use the triangular inequality and Jensen’s inequality. Hence,

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\vee\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr]

(4.19)

\displaystyle\mspace{12mu}\leq\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\Bigr]\vee\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr]+\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\sqrt{\Var_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\Bigr]}+\sqrt{\Var_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr]}\biggr).

Since,

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\Var_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\bigr]}{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\bigr]^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm 2\beta(H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)-\mean_{\mathcal{G}}[H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)])}\Bigr]}{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\pm\beta(H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)-\mean_{\mathcal{G}}[H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)])}\Bigr]^{2}}

(4.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{\exp\biggl(\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}\Lambda_{kj}(\tfrac{\mp 2\beta}{N}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\Lambda_{ik}(\tfrac{\mp 2\beta}{N}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k})\biggr)}{\exp\biggl(2\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}\Lambda_{kj}(\tfrac{\mp\beta}{N}\sigma_{k}\sigma_{j})+2\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\Lambda_{ik}(\tfrac{\mp\beta}{N}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{k})\biggr)},

and
|\Lambda_{ij}(t)|\leq k_{J}^{2}t^{2}
, which follows from a Taylor expansion of
\Lambda_{ij}(t)
up to second order together with the estimate
|\Lambda_{ij}^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 2k_{J}^{2}
, we obtain that

(4.21)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\vee\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr]\leq\biggl(\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma)}\Bigr]\vee\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta H_{N}^{k}(\sigma^{k})}\Bigr]\biggr)\Bigl(1+\sqrt{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{6(\beta k_{J})^{2}}{N}}-1}\Bigr).

Combining (
4.21
), (
4.17
) and (
4.14
), we see that there exists a
c\equiv c(\beta,k_{J})
such that for all
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\mathrm{e}^{\beta(H_{N}(\sigma)\vee H_{N}(\sigma^{k}))}\Bigr]\leq\mathrm{e}^{\beta(\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma)\vee\widetilde{H}_{N}(\sigma^{k}))}\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\,\Bigl(1+\frac{c}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr).

This concludes the proof of (
4.10
).
∎

We are ready to prove the annealed capacity estimates.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
2\leq N\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
be two non-empty and disjoint.

(i)

Then,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\Bigl|\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)\bigr]-\log\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)\Bigr|=\alpha_{N}+O\Bigl(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr).

(ii)

For any
q\in[1,\infty)
there exists a
c_{3}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.24)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1-\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]^{1/q}}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\leq\mathrm{e}^{q\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1+\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr),

(4.25)

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1-\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{-q}\Bigr]^{1/q}}{\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{{-1}}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{q\alpha_{N}}\Bigl(1+\frac{c_{3}}{\sqrt{N}}\Bigr),

where
\alpha_{N}
is defined in (
2.25
).

Proof.

Fix
\mathbb{N}\ni N\geq 2
and consider two non-empty disjoint subsets
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\subset\mathcal{S}_{N}
. Recall from Section
2.3
the definition of the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)
for functions
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}
and the Dirichlet form
\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)
for unit flows
\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}
. In view of Lemma
4.2
(ii) we have that

(4.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)\bigr]&=\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(f)\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1/2})\bigr)\mspace{72mu}\forall\,f\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}},\\
\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\bigr]&=\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{N}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{\alpha_{N}}\bigl(1+O(N^{-1/2})\bigr)\mspace{59mu}\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}.\end{split}

(i)
The claim in (
4.23
) is an immediate consequence of the Dirichlet principle and the Thomson principle combined with Jensen’s inequality. Indeed, in view of (
4.26
) there exists a
c\equiv c(\beta,k_{J})
such that

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}}}\log\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}\mathcal{E}_{N}(f)\bigr]

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{f\in\mathcal{H}_{{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}}}\log\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{N}(f)\bigr)+\alpha_{N}+cN^{-1/2}

(4.27)

\displaystyle=\log\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}\,\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)+\alpha_{N}+cN^{-1/2}.

Likewise, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[\log\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)\bigr]

\displaystyle\geq-\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}}\log\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\bigr]

\displaystyle\geq-\inf_{\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}}\log\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{N}(\varphi)\bigr)-\alpha_{N}-c\,N^{-1/2}

(4.28)

\displaystyle=\log\bigl(\widetilde{Z}_{N}\,\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)-\alpha_{N}-cN^{-1/2}.

(ii)
Since the proofs of (
4.24
) and (
4.25
) are similar, we present the proof for (
4.25
) only. To get the lower bound, note that by Jensen’s inequality it is immediate that

(4.29)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{-q}\Bigr]^{1/q}\geq\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{-1}\Bigr]\geq\frac{1}{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr]}.

Hence, analogously to (
4.27
), by applying the Dirichlet principle and (
4.26
) we obtain that there exists a
c\equiv c(\beta,k_{J})
such that

(4.30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\bigl[Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr]}\geq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha_{N}}}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}\,\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\bigl(1-cN^{-1/2}\bigr).

To get the upper bound, note that, analogously to (
4.28
), by the Thomson principle we have that

(4.31)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{-q}\Bigr]^{1/q}\leq\inf\nolimits_{\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}}\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]^{1/q}.

By Minkowski’s inequality and an application of (
4.10
) with
\beta
replaced by
\beta q
, we find that for any
q\in[1,\infty)
there exists a
c^{\prime}\equiv c^{\prime}(q,\beta,k_{J})
such that, for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}
,

(4.32)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{D}_{N}(\varphi)\bigr)^{q}\Bigr]^{1/q}\leq\widetilde{Z}_{N}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{D}}_{N}(\varphi)\,\mathrm{e}^{q\alpha_{N}}\,\bigl(1+c^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\bigr).

Therefore, again applying the Thomson principle, we obtain

(4.33)

\displaystyle\mean_{\mathcal{G}}\Bigl[\bigl(Z_{N}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\bigr)^{-q}\Bigr]^{1/q}\leq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{q\alpha_{N}}}{\widetilde{Z}_{N}\,\widetilde{\capa}_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}\bigl(1+c^{\prime}N^{-1/2}\bigr),

and by setting
c_{3}\coloneqq c\vee c^{\prime}
we conclude the proof.
∎

5.

This section contains all our results concerning the
\ell^{1}(\mu_{N})
-norm of the equilibrium potential
h_{\mathcal{A}_{N},\mathcal{B}_{N}}^{N}
, which we call the
harmonic sum
. Before proving concentration estimates in Section
5.2
and annealed estimates in Section

. We emphasise that throughout this section, contrary to Section

5.1.
Preliminary estimates

As mentioned above, for estimates on the harmonic sum we restrict to the event
\Xi(a_{N})
, which is defined in (
3.1

3
to prove Theorem

Notation 2

For any two random variables

\prob
-a.s. on the event

and

Remark 5.1

By

Remark 5.4

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

The proof is inspired by that of
[

and consists of two steps.

Step 1.

Let
(a_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
be any non-negative real sequence and fix

. We start by showing that the following is true
\prob
-a.s. on the event
\Xi(a_{N})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
, for any two

and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{k,N}}\frac{\mu_{N}(\sigma)}{\mu_{N}[\mathcal{S}_{k,N}]}\,h_{\mathcal{M}_{j,N},\mathcal{M}_{k,N}}^{N}(\sigma)\leq\varepsilon+\tilde{\rho}_{N}\,\mathrm{e}^{4\beta a_{N}}\log(1/\varepsilon)\min\biggl\{1,\frac{\mu_{N}[\mathcal{S}_{j,N}]}{\mu_{N}[\mathcal{S}_{k,N}]}\biggr\}.

Recall that, for
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
,
\mathcal{V}_{\ell,N}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,N}
are, respectively, the local valley around the metastable set
\mathcal{M}_{\ell,N}
and a set of the metastable partition of the

. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}_{N}=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{\ell,N}
. By applying (
2.33
) and (
3.10
), we have that, for any
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{V}_{k,N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{k,N}
,
\prob
-a.s. on the event
\Xi(a_{N})\cap\widetilde{\Omega}_{\mathrm{meta}}(N)
,

\displaystyle\mu_{N}

\displaystyle\bigl[\mathcal{X}\bigr]\overset{\mspace{-15mu}\eqref{eq:comparison:exit_prob}\mspace{-15mu}}{\leq}\mathrm{e}^{2\beta a_{N}}\frac{\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{M}_{k,N})}{\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}_{k,N}}^{N}<\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{X}}^{N}\bigr]}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{2\beta a_{N}}\tilde{\rho}_{N}\biggl(\max_{\ell\in\{1,\ldots,K\}}\widetilde{\Prob}\mspace{-12.5mu}\phantom{\Prob}_{\mu_{N}|\mathcal{M}_{\ell,N}}^{N}\Bigl[\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}_{N}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{\ell,N}}^{N}<\tilde{\tau}_{\mathcal{M}_{\ell,N}}^{N}\Bigr]\biggr)^{-1}\capa_{N}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{M}_{k,N})

Appendix A
Concentration inequality

We present a concentration inequality for functionals of conditionally independent random variables that is a slight extension of the classical McDiarmid concentration inequality for functionals of independent random variables satisfying a bounded difference estimate (cf.

, Theorem 6.2]

[
13
, Section 2.4.1]
).

Proposition A.1
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\prob)
be a probability space,
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{F}
a sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
\mathcal{F}
,

and
\mathcal{X}
a Polish space. Consider a vector

of
\mathcal{X}
-valued random variables on

, and let

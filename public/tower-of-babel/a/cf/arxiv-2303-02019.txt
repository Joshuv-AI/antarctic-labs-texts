Abstract
. Given a simple graph, consider the polynomial ring with coefficients in a field and variables identified with the edges of the graph. Given a non-empty even cardinality Eulerian subgraph and a choice of half of its edges, consider the homogeneous binomial obtained by taking the product of these edges minus the product of the remaining edges of the subgraph. We define a homogeneous ideal by taking as generators all binomials obtained in this way, varying the Eulerian subgraph and the choice of half of its edges, together with the squares of the variables of the ring. This ideal is related to the Eulerian ideal, introduced by Neves, Vaz Pinto and Villarreal. We call the corresponding quotient the Eulerian Artinian algebra associated to the graph. The goal of the present work is to study the socle of these algebras through the lens of graph theory. Our main results include a combinatorial characterization of a monomial basis of the socle, a characterization of Gorenstein Eulerian Artinian algebras in the case of bipartite graphs and the computation of the h h -vector and socle degrees in the cases of a complete graph and a complete bipartite graph.

1.
Introduction

Let
R=K[t_{1},\dots,t_{s}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring over a field
K
.
A standard Artinian graded algebra,
A=R/I
, is a
0
-dimensional quotient of
R
by a homogeneous ideal

I\subseteq R
.
These graded rings have been studied from many points of view; one example
is the study of their Lefschetz properties, especially in the Gorenstein and in the level case
(
cf.
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
6
,
9
]
,

for a small sample of recent works and
[
10
]
, for a survey).
In this article, we study the socle of standard Artinian graded algebras associated to simple graphs.
By identifying the set of edges of a simple graph with the set of variables of
R
,
we consider
I
of the form:

I=(\mathscr{E},t_{1}^{2},\dots,t_{s}^{2}),

where
\mathscr{E}
is a set of binomials obtained from the even cardinality Eulerian subsets of edges of the graph.
These standard Artinian graded algebras, which we will call
Eulerian Artinian algebras
,
are canonically related to the Eulerian ideal of the graph, introduced
in
[
13
]
.
Our aim is to give a combinatorial characterization of the socle of an
Eulerian Artinian algebra.

In section
2
, building upon the results of
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
for the Eulerian ideal,
we obtain a combinatorial characterization of a monomial basis for an Eulerian Artinian algebra, in terms
of the graph (
cf
. Proposition
2.7
).
The monomials of this basis are square-free monomials that identify sets of edges of the graph
containing no more than half the edges of any even cardinality Eulerian subgraph,
subject to an additional condition coming from an
ordering of the edge set. These distinguished sets of edges are called
reduced parity joins

(
cf
. Definition
2.6
) and they form a set in bijection with the set
of pairs
(T,p)
, where
T
, a set of vertices of the graph, and
p
, an element
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, are
such that there exists a
T
-join of cardinality of parity
p

(
cf
. Proposition
2.12
and
Corollary
2.13
).
In Sections
4
and
5
,
using these characterizations, we compute the
h
-vector
of an
Eulerian Artinian algebra for complete and complete bipartite graphs.
In section
3
, we study the socle of an Eulerian Artinian algebra.
We start by showing that it is generated over
K
by monomial residues
(
cf
. Proposition
3.1
) and then give a combinatorial
characterization of the sets of edges that the monomials in the socle basis identify
(
cf
. Theorem
3.4
).
As an application, we show that if the graph is bipartite,
the only Gorenstein Eulerian Artinian algebras are the complete intersection ones,
that are obtained when
\mathscr{E}
, above, is empty (
cf
. Theorem
3.6
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Setup

Throughout,
G
will denote a simple, undirected graph.
The vertex set of
G
will be denoted by
V_{G}
and the edge set, which we regard as a non-empty set
of subsets of
V_{G}
, will be denoted by
E_{G}
. We associate to
G
the polynomial ring

K[E_{G}]=K[t_{e}:e\in E_{G}],

where
K
is any field. If
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
, the monomial given by the product
of
t_{e}^{\alpha(e)}
, when
e
varies in
E_{G}
, will be denoted by
\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}
.
If
J\subseteq E_{G}
is any set of edges, we will also use

\mathbf{t}_{J}
for the product of all variables identifying edges in
J
.

Let
C\subseteq E_{G}
and
v\in V_{G}
. The degree of
v
in
C
is defined by

\textstyle\deg_{C}(v)=\sum\limits_{e\in C}|\left\{v\right\}\cap e\,|.

A subset,
C
, is called Eulerian if
\deg_{C}(v)
is even, for every
v\in V_{G}
.
Let
J,L\subseteq E_{G}
be non-empty. The binomial
\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{L}
is called
Eulerian

if
J\cap L=\emptyset
,
|J|=|L|
and
C=J\cup L
is Eulerian.
Let us denote the set of Eulerian binomials by
\mathscr{E}
.

Definition 2.1
.

If
G
is any simple graph, define
I(G)=(\mathscr{E}\cup\left\{t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}\right\})

and define the
Eulerian Artinian algebra
associated to
G
by
A(G)=K[E_{G}]/I(G)
.

If the only Eulerian subset of edges of even cardinality is the empty set, which
is the case when
G
is a forest or a graph with a unique odd cycle, then

A(G)=K[E_{G}]/(t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}),

which is a well-known complete intersection.

2.2.
Eulerian ideal

Consider

K[V_{G}]=K[x_{i}:i\in V_{G}]
and let
\varphi\colon K[E_{G}]\to K[V_{G}]
be the ring homomorphism
defined by
\varphi(t_{e})=x_{i}x_{j}
for every
e=\left\{i,j\right\}\in E_{G}
.
The Eulerian ideal of
G
was defined in
[
13
, Definition 1.1]
as

I(X_{G})=\varphi^{-1}(x_{i}^{2}-x_{j}^{2}:i,j\in V_{G}).

It follows from
[
13
, Proposition 2.5]
that

I(X_{G})=(\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}-t_{f}^{2}:e,f\in E_{G}\})
. In other words,

I(G)
and
I(X_{G})
are related by

I(G)=I(X_{G})+(t_{e}^{2})
, for any choice of
e\in E_{G}
.

2.3.
A Gröbner basis

In the approach of
[
11
,
12
]
, the first step in the study of the Eulerian ideal

I(X_{G})
was to describe a Gröbner basis. As we explain next,
a Gröbner basis for
I(G)
can be easily derived from
the results of
[
11
,
12
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Given an ordering of the edge set
E_{G}=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{s}\}
, define
\succeq
, the
associated graded reversed lexicographic order on
K[E_{G}]
, by postulating that

\mathbf{t}^{\alpha}\succ\mathbf{t}^{\beta}
, where
\alpha\not=\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{E_{G}}
, if and only if
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})>\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\beta})

or
\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\alpha})=\deg(\mathbf{t}^{\beta})
and the last nonzero coordinate of

(\alpha(e_{1})-\beta(e_{1}),\dots,\alpha(e_{s})-\beta(e_{s}))
is negative.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given an ordering of
E_{G}
, the set

\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
I(G)
with respect
to the associated grevlex order.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
the set
\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}-t^{2}_{f}:e,f\in E_{G}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
the Eulerian ideal
I(X_{G})
, with respect to the grevlex order.
Consider
\ell\in E_{G}
, the last edge. Then, as no initial term of an element of this Gröbner basis
is divisible by
t_{\ell}
, by Buchberger’s criterion, we deduce that

(1)

\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}-t^{2}_{f}:e,f\in E_{G}\}\cup\left\{t_{\ell}^{2}\right\}

is a Gröbner basis for
I(G)=I(X_{G})+(t_{\ell}^{2})
. Since
the initial terms of members of the set
\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}\}
generate the same
ideal as those of (
1
), this
set is also Gröbner basis for
I(G)
.
∎

Let us denote the symmetric difference of sets by
\triangle
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
M\subseteq E_{G}
be any set of edges. Then
\mathbf{t}_{M}\in I(G)
if and only if
there exists an even cardinality Eulerian subset
C\subseteq E_{G}
such that

|M\triangle C|<|M|
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathbf{t}_{M}
is divisible by the leading term
of an element of
\mathscr{E}
. Without loss of generality,
let this be
\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{L}
, with
J\cap L=\emptyset
,
|J|=|L|
and

C=J\cup L
an Eulerian subset, with last edge in
L
.
Then
\mathbf{t}_{J}\mid\mathbf{t}_{M}\iff J\subseteq M
and

(2)

\mathbf{t}_{M}=\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}(\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{L})+\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}\mathbf{t}_{L}.

If
\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}\mathbf{t}_{L}
is a square-free monomial then

M\cap C=J\iff(M\setminus J)\cup L=M\triangle C,

so that
\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}\mathbf{t}_{L}=\mathbf{t}_{M\triangle C}
and

|M|=|M\triangle C|
. If
\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}\mathbf{t}_{L}
is not square-free then

|M\cap C|>|J|=|C|/2\iff|M\triangle C|<|M|.

If there exists an even cardinality Eulerian subset
C\subseteq E_{G}
with

|M\triangle C|<|M|\iff|M\cap C|>|C|/2,

we may choose
J\subseteq M\cap C
, a set of cardinality
|C|/2
that
does not include the last edge of
C
. Then
\mathbf{t}_{J}-\mathbf{t}_{L}
is an Eulerian binomial as above and in (
2
)
the monomial
\mathbf{t}_{M\setminus J}\mathbf{t}_{L}
is divisible by a square of a variable. Therefore
\mathbf{t}_{M}\in I(G)
.
Conversely, if
\mathbf{t}_{M}\in I(G)
, then
\mathbf{t}_{M}
has remainder zero in its standard expression with respect
to the Gröbner basis of Proposition
2.3
. Let us compute
this remainder by first dividing only by the elements of
\mathscr{E}
until we get a monomial that is not square-free.
Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{m}
, with
m\geq 1
be the corresponding even cardinality Eulerian subsets. By our initial argument,

\textstyle|(M\triangle C_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle C_{m-1})\triangle C_{m}|<|M\triangle C_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle C_{m-1}|=|M|.

Let
C=C_{1}\triangle C_{2}\triangle\cdots\triangle C_{m}
, which is also an even cardinality Eulerian subset. Then

|M\triangle C|<|M|
.
∎

Subsets
M\subseteq E_{G}
for which the condition in Corollary
2.4

is not satisfied are called parity joins.

Definition 2.5

(
cf
.
[
11
, Definition 4.11]
)
.

A subset
J\subseteq E_{G}
is called a parity join if,
for every Eulerian subset
C\subseteq E_{G}
of even cardinality,

\textstyle|J\cap C|\leq\frac{|C|}{2}\iff|J\triangle C|\geq|J|.

It follows from Corollary
2.4

that the maximum of
d
, for which
A(G)_{d}
is nonzero, which is called the

maximum socle degree
of
A(G)
, is the maximum cardinality of a parity join of the graph.

2.4.
A monomial basis

If
I
is an ideal in a polynomial ring endowed with a monomial order then,
by Macaulay’s theorem (
cf
.
[
2
, Theorem 2.6]
),
the set of residues of monomials that do not belong to the initial ideal of
I
is a
K
-basis
for the quotient of the polynomial ring by the ideal. In our case, fixing any ordering of
E_{G}
,
a monomial not in the initial ideal of
I(G)
is necessarily of the form
\mathbf{t}_{J}

for some parity join
J\subseteq E_{G}
, but not conversely.
The following notion was introduced in
[
12
, Definition 14]
.

Definition 2.6
.

Given an ordering of
E_{G}
, a parity join
J\subseteq E_{G}
is said
reduced

if, whenever
C\subseteq E_{G}
is a non-empty, even cardinality Eulerian subset and

|J\triangle C|=|J|\iff|J\cap C|=|C|/2,

then
J
contains the last edge in
C
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Given any ordering of
E_{G}
,
the set of monomial residues given by
\mathbf{t}_{J}+I(G)
, with
J
in the set of reduced parity joins of
G
,
is a
K
-basis of
A(G)
.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Theorem 15]
.
∎

One can give a second characterization this
K
-basis of
A(G)

using the related notion of
T
-join of a graph.
Let
T\subseteq V_{G}
and
J\subseteq E_{G}
. We say that
J
is a
T
-join if
and only if

T=\{v\in V_{G}:\deg_{J}(v)\text{ is odd}\}.

Accordingly, a subset
C\subseteq E_{G}
is Eulerian if and only if
C
is
an
\emptyset
-join.
Given
T\subseteq V_{G}
,
one can show that there exists a
T
-join if and only if the cardinality of the intersection
of
T
with the vertex set of every connected component of
G
is even
(
cf
.
[
8
, Proposition 12.7]
).

Notation 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{G}
denote the set of
T\subseteq V_{G}
for which there exists a
T
-join.

Fix
T\in\mathcal{T}_{G}
and let
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
be two
T
-joins. Then

J_{1}\triangle J_{2}
, is an
\emptyset
-join (
cf
., for example,
[
8
, Proposition 12.6]
);
in other words,
J_{1}\triangle J_{2}
is an Eulerian subset. Since

|J_{1}\triangle J_{2}|\equiv_{2}|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|,

if there exist
T
-joins of cardinalities of opposite parity, then the graph contains an odd cycle,
i.e., it is non-bipartite. The converse is also true (
cf
., for example,
[
11
, Lemma 4.6]
).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
T\subseteq V_{G}
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
J\subseteq E_{G}
.

(i)

J
is called a
(T,p)
-join if
J
is a
T
-join and
|J|+2\mathbb{Z}=p
.

(ii)

Let
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
denote the set of
(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{G}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}

admitting a
(T,p)
-join.

(iii)

If
(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
, denote
\tau(T,p)=\min\{|J|:\text{$J$ is a $(T,p)$-join}\}
.

(iv)

Define
\theta(J)=(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
, by setting

T=\{v\in V_{G}:\deg_{J}(v)\text{ is odd}\}
and
p=|J|+2\mathbb{Z}
.

If
G
is non-bipartite, then
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}=\mathcal{T}_{G}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, while
if
G
is bipartite, then, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{G}
, the set
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
contains exactly one of the pairs

(T,0)
or
(T,1)
and thus the cardinality of a
T
-join is then determined by
T
.
For a bipartite
G
, we abbreviate
\tau(T,p)
to
\tau(T)
.

The following lemma follows from the elementary property
of
T
-joins used above (
cf
.
[
8
, Proposition 12.6]
)
and the formula for the cardinality of a symmetric difference.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
J_{1}
is a
(T_{1},p_{1})
-join and
J_{2}
is a
(T_{2},p_{2})
-join, then

J_{1}\triangle J_{2}
is a
(T_{1}\triangle T_{2},p_{1}+p_{2})
-join. In particular, if
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are
two
(T,p)
joins, then there exists and even cardinality Eulerian subset
C\subseteq E_{G}

such that
J_{2}=J_{1}\triangle C
.

Remark 2.11
.

Let
J\subseteq E_{G}
and let
(T,p)=\theta(J)
.
The lemma above can be used to show that

J
is a parity join if and only if
\tau(T,p)=|J|
, i.e.,
parity joins are the same as minimum cardinality
(T,p)
-joins.

Finally, the next result provides a second combinatorial characterization of the monomial basis of
A(G)

of Proposition
2.7
, in terms of
T
-joins.

Proposition 2.12
.

Fix an ordering of
E_{G}
. For every
(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
,
there exists a unique reduced parity join among all
(T,p)
-joins.

Proof.

See
[
12
, Proposition 22]
.
∎

It follows that the unique reduced parity join in the set of
(T,p)
-joins has
cardinality equal to
\tau(T,p)
and the restriction of
\theta
to the set
reduced parity joins of
G
is a bijection with
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
. Using this and
Proposition
2.7
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.13
.

The Hilbert series of
A(G)
, in the variable
z
, is the sum of all
z^{\tau(T,p)}

as
(T,p)
varies in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
and, fixing any ordering of the set of edges, it
is the sum of all
z^{|J|}
where
J
varies in the set of reduced parity joins of
G
.

Let us end this section by using Corollary
2.13

to compute the
h
-vector of
A(G)
for graphs admitting a single non-empty, even cardinality
Eulerian subset of edges. We will also use this result, in Sections
4

and
5
, to compute the
h
-vector of
A(G)
for
a complete graph and a complete bipartite graph, respectively.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
E_{G}
possesses a single non-empty, even cardinality Eulerian subset of edges,
C\subseteq E_{G}
.
Let us denote
s=|E_{G}|
,
c=\nicefrac{{|C|}}{{2}}
and let
(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
denote the
h
-vector of
A(G)
.
Then

h_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\binom{s}{i},\quad\text{if}\quad 0\leq i\leq c-1,\\
\binom{2c-1}{c-1}\binom{s-2c}{i-c}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{2c}{c-k}\binom{s-2c}{i-c+k},\quad\text{if}\quad i\geq c.\\
\end{array}\right.

In particular,
r
, the socle degree of
A(G)
, is equal to
s-c
.

Proof.

If
J\subseteq E_{G}
is such that
|J|\leq c-1
then, necessarily,

|J\cap C|<c
and therefore, under our assumption on the graph,
J
is a reduced parity join. Hence,

h_{i}=\binom{s}{i}
, for all
0\leq i\leq c-1
. If
i\geq c
, then any reduced parity join
must have at most
c
edges in
C
and if this number is maximum, then it must include the last edge
in
C
. Hence, for
i\geq c
,

\textstyle h_{i}=\binom{2c-1}{c-1}\binom{s-2c}{i-c}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{2c}{c-k}\binom{s-2c}{i-c+k}.\qed

3.
The socle of
A(G)

Let
R
be a polynomial ring with coefficients in a field
K
, standard graded, and
denote by
\mathfrak{m}
its irrelevant ideal. If
M
is a graded module over
R
, the socle of

M
is the graded submodule
\operatorname{Soc}M=\{u\in M:\mathfrak{m}u=(0)\}
. The
degrees of the minimal generators of the socle of a standard Artinian graded algebra determine the last
term of the minimal graded free resolution of the algebra over the polynomial ring
(
cf
.
[
14
, Lemma 5.3.3]
).
In particular, a standard Artinian graded algebra is
Gorenstein if and only if its socle is
1
-dimensional over
K
.
The degrees of the minimal generators of the socle module are called the

socle degrees
; the dimension of the socle, is called the
type
. A standard Artinian
graded algebra is called
level
if its socle is generated in a single degree.

Theorem 3.1
.

The socle of
A(G)
is generated by residues of monomials.

Proof.

Choose an ordering of
E_{G}
and consider the associated
grevlex order on
K[E_{G}]
. Let
0\not=u\in\operatorname{Soc}A(G)
.
By Proposition
2.7
, we may write

u=(c_{1}\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}+\cdots+c_{m}\mathbf{t}_{J_{m}})+I(G)

for some
J_{1},\dots,J_{m}
, reduced parity joins,
c_{i}\in K\setminus\{0\}
and
m>0
.
By assumption, for every
e\in E_{G}
,

(3)

c_{1}\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}t_{e}+\cdots+c_{m}\mathbf{t}_{J_{m}}t_{e}\in I(G).

Fix
e\in E_{G}
and, to ease notation, assume that there exists
0\leq k\leq m
such that

c_{i}\mathbf{t}_{J_{i}}t_{e}\not\in I(G)
, for all
1\leq i\leq k
and

c_{i}\mathbf{t}_{J_{i}}t_{e}\in I(G)
, for all
k+1\leq i\leq m
. We want to show that
k=0
.
Given that
\mathscr{E}
consists of binomials, the reduction of a monomial modulo this set
is also a monomial. Accordingly, let
d_{i}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{i}}
, with
d_{i}\not=0
,
denote the reduction of
c_{i}\mathbf{t}_{J_{i}}t_{e}
modulo
\mathscr{E}
.
Since
\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
I(G)
,
by our assumptions, none of
d_{1}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{1}},\dots,d_{k}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{k}}

is divisible by a square, while all of
d_{k+1}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{k+1}},\dots,d_{m}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{m}}
are.
Hence, the remainder of
c_{1}\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}t_{e}+\cdots+c_{m}\mathbf{t}_{J_{m}}t_{e}
modulo

\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G}\}
is
d_{1}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+d_{k}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{k}}
.
From (
3
) it follows that

(4)

d_{1}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{1}}+\cdots+d_{k}\mathbf{t}^{\beta_{k}}=0.

By
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
,
\mathscr{E}\cup\{t_{e}^{2}-t^{2}_{f}:e,f\in E_{G}\}
is a Gröbner basis for
the Eulerian ideal,
I(X_{G})\subseteq K[E_{G}]
, and hence, using (
4
), we deduce that

(c_{1}\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}+\cdots+c_{k}\mathbf{t}_{J_{k}})t_{e}\in I(X_{G}).

Since
t_{e}
is regular on
K[E_{G}]/I(X_{G})

(
cf
.
[
12
, Proposition 2.1]
), we get

c_{1}\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}+\cdots+c_{k}\mathbf{t}_{J_{k}}\in I(X_{G})\subseteq I(G),

which is a contradiction. We conclude that
k=0
. Varying
e\in E_{G}
, we
deduce that
\mathbf{t}_{J_{i}}+I(G)
belongs to
\operatorname{Soc}A(G)
,
for every
i=1,\dots,r
.
∎

Let us now address the problem of giving a combinatorial characterization of
reduced parity joins yielding monomial residues in the socle of
A(G)
.
Fix an ordering of
E_{G}
. If
J
is a reduced parity join and
\mathbf{t}_{J}+I(G)
belongs to the socle
of
A(G)
, then
\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}\in I(G)
, for all
e\in E_{G}
.
Using Corollary
2.4
,
what this means is that
J\cup\left\{e\right\}
is no longer a parity join, for every

e\not\in J
.
Hence a monomial socle basis for
A(G)
is in bijection with the set of reduced
parity joins that are maximal, under inclusion, in the set of
all

parity joins. Note that, as Example
4.4
shows,
not all reduced parity joins that are maximal in the set of reduced parity joins
are maximal in the set of all parity joins.

This combinatorial characterization of the socle depends on the choice of ordering of
E_{G}
.
Next, we shall give another combinatorial characterization
in terms of the invariants
\tau(T,p)

of the graph, which, in particular, is independent of an ordering of
E_{G}
.
Before, we need to state an elementary result, concerning these numbers.
Recall that we are regarding an edge of
G
as a subset of
V_{G}
.
Hence, if
e\in E_{G}
and
T\subseteq V_{G}
it makes sense to take the symmetric difference
T\triangle e
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
and
e\in E_{G}
. Then
(T\triangle e,p+1)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
and

\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)=\tau(T,p)\pm 1.

Proof.

If
J\subseteq E_{G}
is a
(T,p)
-join, then, by Lemma
2.10
,

J\triangle\left\{e\right\}
is a
(T\triangle e,p+1)
-join. Hence,
\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)\leq\tau(J,p)+1
.
Repeating this argument with
(T\triangle e,p+1)
, we deduce that

\tau(T,p)-1\leq\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)\leq\tau(J,p)+1

so that
\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)=\tau(T,p)\pm 1
.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

If
(T,p)\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{p},G}
satisfies
\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)=\tau(T,p)-1
, for every
e\in E_{G}
, then
we will refer to
(T,p)
as a
socle pair
of
G
. To ease notation, if
G
is bipartite, we omit the parity,
and we will refer to
T\in\mathcal{T}_{G}
as a
socle set
if and only if
\tau(T\triangle e)=\tau(T)-1
, for every

e\in E_{G}
.

We are now ready to state our second main result.

Theorem 3.4
.

The socle degrees of
A(G)
are given by
\tau(T,p)
, for
(T,p)
varying
in the set of socle pairs. Alternatively, fixing an ordering of
E_{G}
, the socle degrees
are the cardinalities of reduced parity joins
J\subseteq E_{G}
such that

\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}\in I(G)
, for all
e\in E_{G}
, or, equivalently, the cardinalities of reduced parity joins that
are maximal in the set of all parity joins.

Proof.

Let us fix an ordering of
E_{G}
. Let
\left\{J_{1},\dots,J_{t}\right\}
be the set of reduced parity joins
J\subseteq E_{G}

such that
\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}\in I(G)
, for every
e\in E_{G}
. By Proposition
2.7

and Theorem
3.1
,

\left\{\mathbf{t}_{J_{1}}+I(G),\dots,\mathbf{t}_{J_{t}}+I(G)\right\}
is a
K
-basis for the socle of
A(G)
and hence
the socle degrees are given by the cardinalities of
J_{1},\dots,J_{t}
.
Now, let
J
be
any
reduced parity join and denote
\theta(J)=(T,p)
.
Since
\tau(T,p)=|J|
(
cf
. Remark
2.11
), using
Lemma
3.2
, to prove the first assertion,
it suffices to show that for any
e\in E_{G}
,

(5)

\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}\in I(G)\iff\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)<|J|+1.

If
e\in J
then
\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}
is divisible by a square, hence belongs to
I(G)
, and,
on the other hand,
J\triangle\{e\}
is a
(T\triangle e,p+1)
-join of cardinality
|J|-1
.
If
e\not\in J
then, using Corollary
2.4
and
Lemma
2.10
,

\mathbf{t}_{J}t_{e}\in I(G)
if and only if there exists a even cardinality Eulerian subset
C\subseteq E_{G}

such that

\textstyle|C\triangle(J\cup\left\{e\right\})|<|J\cup\left\{e\right\}|=|J|+1,

which is equivalent to
\tau(T\triangle e,p+1)<|J|+1
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

If
E_{G}
possesses a single non-empty, even cardinality Eulerian subset,
C\subseteq E_{G}
,
then
A(G)
is level of socle degree
s-c
, and type
\binom{2c-1}{c-1}
,
where
s=|E_{G}|
and
c=\nicefrac{{|C|}}{{2}}
.

Proof.

If
J\subseteq E_{G}
is a reduced parity join which is maximal in the set of parity joins then

J\cap C=\frac{|C|}{2}
, for otherwise the last edge of
C\setminus J
could be added to
J

to form another reduced parity join. Similarly,
J
must include all edges of
G\setminus C
.
Hence the cardinality of any such reduced parity join is
c+(s-2c)=s-c
, hence

A(G)
is level, and their number is
\binom{2c-1}{c-1}
.
∎

Let us finish this section by giving an application Theorem
3.4
.

Theorem 3.6
.

If
G
is bipartite, then
A(G)
is Gorenstein if and only if

E_{G}
does not possess any non-empty even cardinality Eulerian subset.
If this is the case, then

I(G)=(t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G})

and therefore
A(G)
is a complete intersection.

Proof.

If
E_{G}
does not contain any non-empty, even cardinality Eulerian subset then

\mathscr{E}=\emptyset
and, accordingly,
I(G)=(t_{e}^{2}:e\in E_{G})
. We deduce that
A(G)

is then a complete intersection and therefore that

A(G)
is Gorenstein (
cf
.
[
7
, Corollary A.6.4]
).
Let us assume that there exists a non-empty, even cardinality Eulerian subset of
E_{G}
.
Choose
C\subseteq E_{G}
, one such set of minimum cardinality, and let us show that the socle of
A(G)
is not
1
-dimensional.
Since
G
is bipartite,
C
is the set of edges of a smallest
length even cycle. Let us denote
2c=|C|\geq 4
and let us fix an ordering of
E_{G}
such that the last edge of
G
belongs to
C
.
Let
v
and
v^{\prime}
be the vertices of the last edge.
Let
J_{1}
denote the set of edges of the path, contained in
C
,
that starts at
v
, goes through
v^{\prime}
and has length
c
.
Let
w
denote the second end-point of this path.
Similarly, let
J_{2}
denote the set of edges of the path,
contained in
C
, that starts at
v^{\prime}
, goes
through
v
and has length
c
, and let us denote by
w^{\prime}
the second
end-point of this path.
Given that
c
is the minimum cardinality of an Eulerian subset,
any subset of
c
elements of
C
that includes the last edge
is a reduced parity join in
G
. Hence both
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are
reduced parity joins. In particular,
if
T_{1}=\{v,w\}
and
T_{2}=\{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\}
,
then
\tau(T_{1})=\tau(T_{2})=c
(
cf
. Remark
2.11
,
note also that we are omitting the parity in the notation, as

G
is bipartite). If
T_{1}
is not a socle set then
there exists
e\in E_{G}
such that
\tau(T_{1}\triangle e)=c+1
. Clearly,

e
cannot belong to
C
. The same applies to
T_{2}
.
Iterating this procedure, let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
and
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\}

be sets of edges (possibly empty) of cardinality
k
and

r
, respectively, such that both
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
and
\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\}
have empty intersection with
C
, both

T_{1}\triangle e_{1}\triangle\dots\triangle e_{k}
and
T_{2}\triangle f_{1}\triangle\dots\triangle f_{r}

are socle sets and such that

\tau(T_{1}\triangle e_{1}\triangle\dots\triangle e_{k})=c+k,\quad\tau(T_{2}\triangle f_{1}\triangle\dots\triangle f_{r})=c+r.

Note that since
J_{1}\cup\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\right\}
is a
(T_{1}\triangle e_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle e_{k})
-join of cardinality

c+k=\tau(T_{1}\triangle e_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle e_{k}),

we deduce that
J_{1}\cup\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\right\}

is a parity join (
cf
. Remark
2.11
).
The same applies to
J_{2}\cup\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\right\}
, however either of them may not be reduced.
If
k\not=r
then these socle sets yield two socle monomials
of different degrees, hence
A(G)
is not level and therefore the socle
of
A(G)
is not
1
-dimensional. If
k=r
and the socle
of
A(G)
is
1
-dimensional then we must have

(6)

\begin{array}[]{c}T_{1}\triangle e_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle e_{k}=T_{2}\triangle f_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle f_{k}\\
\iff T_{1}\triangle T_{2}=\{v,v^{\prime},w,w^{\prime}\}=e_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle e_{k}\triangle f_{1}\triangle\cdots\triangle f_{k}.\end{array}

With a view to a contradiction, assume this holds and,
from the equality in (
6
), let us draw a contradiction.
Without loss of generality, we may assume

\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}\cap\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}=\emptyset.

If

\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}\cup\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}

contains the edge set of any cycle, then, because
G
is bipartite,
such cycle must be even. Let
2m
be its length.
Since, as we argued above,
J_{1}\cup\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\right\}

and
J_{2}\cup\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{r}\right\}

are parity joins, such cycle must consist of
m
edges in

\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\right\}
, say
e_{i_{1}},\dots,e_{i_{m}}
, and
m
edges in
\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\right\}
, say

f_{j_{1}},\dots,f_{j_{m}}
, in which event,

e_{i_{1}}\triangle\cdots\triangle e_{i_{m}}=f_{j_{1}}\triangle\cdots\triangle f_{j_{m}}.

Thus, we may assume that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}\cup\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}

does not contain the edge set of any cycle. Then (
6
) implies that

\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}\cup\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}=P\sqcup Q,

where
P\sqcup Q
denotes the disjoint union of the edge sets of two paths,
which have empty intersection, and whose end-points form the set
\{v,w,v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\}
.
Let us denote

P_{e}=P\cap\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\},\quad P_{f}=P\cap\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\}

and, similarly, let us use the notation
Q_{e}
and
Q_{f}
for the corresponding subsets of
Q
.
Without loss of generality,
we may assume that
v
in an end-point of
P
.
There are three cases to consider.

First, let us consider the case when the end-points of
P
are
v
and
w
.
Consider the Eulerian subset given by
J_{1}\triangle P
. Then, because

P\cap C=\emptyset
and
J_{1}\subseteq C
, it follows that

J_{1}\triangle P=J_{1}\sqcup P
and hence
|J_{1}\triangle P|=c+|P|>0
. Also, as
G
is bipartite, we deduce that
c+|P|
is even.
Since both
J_{1}\cup P_{e}\subseteq J_{1}\cup\left\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\right\}
and
J_{2}\cup P_{f}\subseteq J_{2}\cup\left\{f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\right\}

are parity joins. Using the condition of the definition (
cf
. Definition
2.5
) with

C=J_{1}\triangle P
we get

\textstyle c+|P_{e}|\leq\frac{c+|P|}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad 1+|P_{f}|\leq\frac{c+|P|}{2}

which implies
\textstyle c+1+|P_{e}|+|P_{f}|\leq c+|P|\iff 1\leq 0
, a contradiction.

Second, let the end-points of
P
be
v
and
w^{\prime}
.
Arguing as before, we deduce that

J_{1}\triangle\{\{w,w^{\prime}\}\}\triangle P
is an Eulerian subset of
even cardinality, equal to
c+1+|P|
.
Since

J_{1}\cup P_{e}
is a parity join, we get:

\textstyle c+|P_{e}|\leq\frac{c+1+|P|}{2}.

Similarly, since
J_{2}\triangle\{\{v,v^{\prime}\}\}\triangle P
, which has cardinality
c-1+|P|
, is also an Eulerian subset of even cardinality
and
J_{2}\cup P_{f}
is a parity join, we get

\textstyle c-1+|P_{f}|\leq\frac{c-1+|P|}{2}.

Adding the two inequalities above, we conclude that
c\leq 1
, which is a contradiction.

Third and last, assume that the end-points of
P
be
v
and
v^{\prime}
, so that

Q
has end-points
w
and
w^{\prime}
. Consider the non-empty, even cardinality
Eulerian subset given by
C\triangle\{\{w,w^{\prime}\}\}\triangle Q
. Using the parity joins

J_{1}\cup Q_{e}
and
J_{2}\cup Q_{f}
, in the same way as before, we deduce that

2c+|Q|\leq 2c-1+|Q|\iff 0\leq-1
.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If

is bipartite, then

, the quotient of

by the Eulerian ideal, is Gorenstein if and only if

does not possess any non-empty
even cardinality subset.

Proof.

Choose
e\in E_{G}
. Since, by
[
13
, Proposition 2.1]
,
K[E_{G}]/I(X_{G})
is Cohen–Macaulay
and
t_{e}^{2}
is regular in

and since

, we deduce that

is Gorenstein if and only if

is (
cf
.
[
7
, Proposition A.6.2]
).
∎

4.
The case

-vector
(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{r})

Lemma 4.1
.

If

, with

, and

\textstyle\{\tau(T,0),\tau(T,1)\}=\{\frac{|T|}{2},\frac{|T|}{2}+1\}.

\tau(\emptyset,1)=3

If

. Additionally,

-join of odd cardinality. Since

-joins of smaller cardinality,

. If

with

, then the edge

-join and, taking any vertex

-join of minimum cardinality.

and
\tau(T,1)=\nicefrac{{|T|}}{{2}}=1
.

Let us now assume that
|T|\geq 4
and let us take

, any matching of the vertices in

.
Then
|J|
is equal to

and

-join of minimum cardinality.
Without loss of generality, assume that

is even,

-joins, any

-join of cardinality

.

.

, with

.

(i)

,

, by Lemma

,

.

, it is clear that

\textstyle\{\tau(T,0),\tau(T,1)\}=\{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}\}.

, is level.

Example 4.4
.

Let us use
G=\mathcal{K}_{6}
to give an example of a reduced parity join that is maximal
(under the inclusion order) in the set of reduced
parity joins but not in the set of parity joins. Fix an ordering of
E_{G}
. By Theorems
4.2

and
3.4
the only reduced parity joins that are maximal in the set
of all parity joins have cardinalities
\tau(\emptyset,1)=3
,
\tau(V_{G},0)=4
and
\tau(V_{G},1)=3
.
The union of these
3
reduced parity joins has cardinality at most
3+4+3=10
. Let

5.
The case
G=\mathcal{K}_{a,b}

Let us denote by
\mathcal{K}_{a,b}
a complete bipartite graph, with a partition,

V_{G}=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
, of its vertex set into parts of cardinalities
a
and

b
, respectively. We will compute
the
h
-vector
(h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{r})
and the socle degrees of
A(G)
, when
G=\mathcal{K}_{a,b}
by
following a similar path as in previous section, except that,
because
G
is bipartite, we may omit the parity in the notation
\tau(T,p)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
G=\mathcal{K}_{a,b}
, with
a,b\geq 2
, and if
T\subseteq V_{G}
has even cardinality, then,
setting
T_{1}=T\cap V_{1}
and
T_{2}=T\cap V_{2}
,
\textstyle\tau(T)=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let
us assume that
|T_{1}|\leq|T_{2}|

and let us take any a subset of
T_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq T_{2}

of cardinality
|T_{1}|
. Then, as
|T|
is even and
|T|=2|T_{1}|+|T_{2}\setminus T_{2}^{\prime}|
,
we must have
|T_{2}\setminus T_{2}^{\prime}|
even.
If
T_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, let
J=\emptyset\subseteq E_{G}
;
otherwise, let us take
J\subseteq E_{G}
, a matching of the vertices in
T_{1}\cup T^{\prime}_{2}
.
If
T_{2}\setminus T^{\prime}_{2}=\emptyset
, let us take
L=\emptyset\subseteq E_{G}
;
otherwise take any vertex
v\in V_{1}
and let
L
be the set of edges incident to

v
and to a vertex of
T_{2}\setminus T_{2}^{\prime}
.
Then,
J
is a
(T_{1}\cup T^{\prime}_{2})
-join and
L
is a
(T_{2}\setminus T^{\prime}_{2})
-join.
By Lemma
2.10
,

J\triangle L=J\cup L
is a
T
-join. Since
J\cup L
has cardinality

|T_{2}^{\prime}|+|T_{2}\setminus T_{2}^{\prime}|=|T_{2}|

and any
T
-join has at least
|T_{2}|
edges, we conclude that
\tau(T)=|T_{2}|
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
G=\mathcal{K}_{a,b}
be a complete bipartite graph.

(i)

\textstyle h_{i}=\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr]-\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr]
.

(ii)

T\subseteq V_{G}
, of even cardinality,
is a socle set if and only if

|T_{i}|=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}\implies T_{i}=V_{i}.

In particular, if
a<b
, the socle degrees are

\underbrace{a,\cdots,a}_{\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\binom{b}{a-2i}},\underbrace{b,\cdots,b}_{\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}\binom{a}{b-2i}}

and if
a=b
, then
A(G)
is level, of socle degree
a
and type
2^{a}-1
.

Proof.

(i) By Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
2.13
,

h_{i}
is the number of even cardinality subsets
T\subseteq V_{G}

such that, if
T_{1}=T\cap V_{1}
and
T_{2}=T\cap V_{2}
,
i=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}
.
The number of even cardinality
T\subset V_{G}
such that
i=|T_{1}|>|T_{2}|
is:

\textstyle\binom{a}{i}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}.

Using symmetry and adding the case
|T_{1}|=|T_{2}|
we deduce that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}h_{i}&=&\binom{a}{i}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}+\binom{b}{i}\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{a}{i-2k}+\binom{a}{i}\binom{b}{i}\\
&=&\bigl[\binom{a}{i}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\binom{b}{i}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr]-\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr]\\
&=&\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 0}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr]-\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{a}{i-2k}\bigr]\bigl[\sum_{k\geq 1}\binom{b}{i-2k}\bigr].\end{array}

(ii) Let us start by showing that
T
is not a socle set if and only if there exists
i\in\{1,2\}
such that

(8)

|T_{i}|=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{i}\not=V_{i}.

If
T
is not a socle set, then
there exists
e\in E_{G}
such that

(9)

\tau(T\triangle e)=\tau(T)+1=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}+1.

(
cf
. Lemmas
3.2
and
5.1
).
Hence, for some
i\in\{1,2\}
we have
(V_{i}\cap e)\cap T_{i}=\emptyset
and

|T_{i}|=\max\{|T_{1}|,|T_{2}|\}
, which gives (
8
).
Conversely, suppose that (
8
) holds, for some
i\in\{1,2\}
.
Choose
v\in V_{i}\setminus T_{i}
and
e\in E_{G}
, an edge incident to
v
. Then
(
9
) holds and therefore
T
is not a socle set.

Now, if
a\not=b
, then, by the above characterization,

\textstyle\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{b}{a-2i}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{a}{b-2i}

are, respectively, the number of socle sets yielding socle degrees equal to
a

and the number of those yielding socle degrees equal to
b
.
If
a=b
, the case of
T_{1}=V_{1}
,
T_{2}=V_{2}
is not included in the previous
counting, hence, in this case, the type of
A(G)
is then

\textstyle 2\sum_{i\geq 1}\binom{a}{a-2i}+1=2(2^{a-1}-1)+1=2^{a}-1.\qed

Gegenbauer kernel filtration on the unit hypersphere
Louis Omenyi, McSylvester Omaba

Abstract
Filtration of quantifiable objects by smoothing kernels on Riemannian manifolds for visualisation is an ongoing research. However, using common filters created for linear domains on manifolds with non-Euclidean topologies can yield misleading results. While there is a lot of ongoing research on convolution of quantifiable functions with smoothing kernels on the lower dimensional manifolds, higher-dimensional problems particularly pose a challenge. One important generalization of lower dimensional compact Riemannian manifolds is the unit hypersphere. In this paper, we derive explicit forms of convolution formulae for Gegenbauer kernel filtration on the surface of unit hypersphere. We prove that the Gegebauer filtration is the limit of a sequence of finite linear combinations of the hyperspherical Legendre harmonics, among other results.



Keywords:
Spherical Laplacian; hypersphere;
Gegenbauer kernel; convolution; rotations; special orthogonal group
SO(n).

11M06; 11M35; 11M36; 35P05.

Introduction

Spherical harmonic analysis is basically the spectral theory of a differential operator such as
the spherical Laplacian
\Delta_{S^{n}}
on a compact Riemannian manifold e.g.
the hypersphere,
(S^{n},g).
In this analysis, spherical harmonics play salient roles.
Spherical harmonics analysis is a process of decomposing a function on a sphere into
components of various wavelengths using surface spherical harmonics as base functions,
[
5
]
.
Spherical synthesis combines components of various wavelengths to generate function values
on a sphere and is the reverse process of harmonic analysis. Spherical harmonic analysis
and synthesis have many real-life and scientific applications, see e.g.
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

Harmonics analysis on Euclidean spaces and in particular the theory of
spherical harmonics provide an elegant and instructive application of group
theoretical concepts to various questions of classical function theory, see e.g.
[
4
]
.
Eigenspaces of spherical Laplacian with irreducible components with respect to the natural action
of the group
SO(n)
within the
L^{2}
space on the sphere is an interesting occurrence.
The role of classical orthogonal polynomials such as the Gegenbauer polynomials as reproducing
kernels for the spaces of spherical harmonics of a given degree, or more generally,
as providing an explicit construction of symmetry adapted basis functions for those
spaces has been studied extensively, see e.g.
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
.
Also
[
14
]
studied the connection of the Fourier transform on the Euclidean space to the
Hankel transform obtained via restriction to
SO(n)
-finite functions and various integral
identities of the Hecke–Bochner type resulting there.
The generalized concept of convolution on groups is intimately related to the concept of

]

[

The goal of the present paper is to present a novel form of the Gegenbauer kernel filtration
of harmonic functions on the hypersphere. This puts in limelight signal processing methods
on non-Euclidean spaces and in particular on the hypersphere. The most basic is the notion
of Fourier transform, that on the sphere corresponds to the expansion of functions into series
of familiar spherical harmonics. Vast amount of literature is available on such expansions,
mostly from quantum mechanics and mathematical physics, see e.g.

It is known from group representation theory that Fourier transform can be defined on

2
Notations and preliminaries

By a hypersphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
we refer to a set of points whose
Euclidean distance from the origin is equal to unity, that is

S^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{T}x=1\}

(2.1)

where
n>3.
When
n=2,

S^{1}
is the unit circle and for
n=3
we have the unit

2
-sphere,
S^{2}.
The superscript
n-1
in
S^{n-1}

is due to the fact that a sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as defined by
(
2.1
) is a submanifold of dimension
n-1;
see e.g.
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
for details.
The hypersphere
S^{n-1}
may be parameterized by a set of hyperspherical polar coordinates.
If
(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})
are Cartesian
coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n},
then we define the angles
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots\theta_{n-1}
with
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{n-2}\in[0,\pi]
and

\theta_{n-1}\in[0,2\pi]
such that

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{rcl}x_{1}&=&\cos\theta_{1}\\
x_{2}&=&\sin\theta_{1}\cos\theta_{2}\\
x_{3}&=&\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\cos\theta_{3}\\
x_{4}&=&\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\sin\theta_{3}\\
\vdots\\
x_{n-1}&=&\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\cdots\sin\theta_{n-2}\cos\theta_{n-1}\\
x_{n}&=&\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\cdots\sin\theta_{n-2}\sin\theta_{n-1}.\end{array}\right\}

This choice of coordinates is not unique, but it is a natural generalization of
spherical polar coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{3}.
In the familiar case of

S^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3},

\theta_{1}

corresponds to the elevation and
\theta_{2}
corresponds to the azimuth,
often denoted
(\theta,\psi)=(\theta_{2},\theta_{1}).

Basic concepts related to function theory on the hypersphere used throughout this paper
include the Euler Gamma function, volume element, surface element and some functional spaces
over the hypersphere. We briefly explain them here. As it is well known, for any
x\in\mathbb{R},

we denote the smallest integer that is greater or equal to
x
by
[x].
Also, the
Euler Gamma function,
\Gamma,
in argument
s\in\mathbb{C}
is

\displaystyle{\Gamma(s):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{s-1}dt,~~\text{\rm Re}\,(s)>0}.

It can be shown that
\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\pi}
and

\displaystyle{\Gamma(n+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{(2n)}{2^{2n}n!}\sqrt{\pi}}.

On the hypersphere
S^{n},
we use
dV_{n}
for
n
-dimensional volume element and

dS_{n}
for
n
-dimensional surface element over the unit sphere
S^{n}.

We also use
d\xi
for surface element over a general domain.

A point on the hypersphere is specified as follows. For
n\geq 3,
let

\xi=\xi_{(n)}\in S^{n}
then
\xi_{(n)}=te_{n}+\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\xi_{(n-1)}

with
t\in[-1,1],~\xi_{(n-1)}\in S^{n-1}
and

e_{n}=(0,0,\cdots,0,1)^{T}
is basis vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
When we write

\xi_{(n-1)}\in S^{n}
we mean that

\xi=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n-1},0)\in S^{n}.

It can be shown that for
n\geq 3,

\Big(te_{n}+\sqrt{1-t^{2}}\xi_{(\xi_{(n-1)})}\Big)dS_{n-1}=(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dtdS_{n-2}(\xi_{(n-1)})=(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dtdS_{n-2}.

(2.10)

The surface area of the hypersphere is by definition given by

|S^{n-1}|:=\int_{S^{n-1}}dS_{n-1}

and so from (
2.10
) we have

|S^{n-1}|=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt\int_{S^{n-2}}dS_{n-2}=|S^{n-2}|\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt.

In the integral,

\displaystyle{\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt},
let
s=t^{2}
so that

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=\int_{0}^{1}s^{-1/2}(1-s)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}ds=\beta(\frac{1}{2},\frac{n-1}{2})=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}.

Thus we have the recursive relation

|S^{n-1}|=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}|S^{n-2}|=\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(n/2)};~~n\geq 3.

(2.11)

If
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is orthogonal, then there exists
\xi\in S^{n-1}
and

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

dS_{n-1}(A\xi)=dS_{n-1}(\xi)~~\text{and}~~dV_{n}(Ax)=dV_{n}(x).

In polar coordinates, we relate
x_{(n)}
with
\xi_{(n)}
using

x_{(n)}=|x|\xi_{(n)},~~\xi_{(n)}=\xi\in S^{n-1}.

Thus,

dV_{n}(|x|\xi)=|x|^{n-1}d|x|dS_{n-1}(\xi).

(2.12)

On the hypersphere,
(S^{n},g),
where
g
is a Riemannian metric,
we define the Riemannian (volume) measure
dV_{g}
in terms of the metric
g

in standard hyperspherical coordinates (
2
) as

dV_{g}=\sin^{n-1}d\theta d\omega:=\sin^{n-1}d\theta\sin^{n-2}\sin^{n-3}\cdots\sin\theta_{2}d\theta_{2}\cdots d\theta_{n-1}.

(2.13)

The space of real or complex-valued continuous functions
C(S^{n-1})
over the unit
hypersphere
S^{n-1}
is a Banach space with the canonical norm

||f||_{\infty}=\sup_{\xi\in S^{n-1}}|f(\xi)|.

Similarly, the space of real or complex-valued
square integrable functions
L^{2}(S^{n-1})
over
S^{n-1}
is an Hilbert space
with canonical inner product

\langle f,g\rangle:=\int_{S^{n-1}}f\bar{g}dS_{n-1}

(2.14)

with the induced norm

||f||_{2}:=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}.

(2.15)

The space
L^{2}(S^{n-1})
is the closure of
C(S^{n-1})
with respect to the norm
(
2.15
). We study a function
f
on
S^{n-1}
through its extension

f^{\ast}(x):=f(\frac{x}{|x|})=f(\xi)~~\text{for}~~x\in\Omega_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|\in[1-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\}.

3
Gegenbauer Polynomial

Gegenbauer polynomials enables one express Gegenbauer filtration kernels. Thus,
following
[
3
,
2
,
16
,
12
]
and
[
11
]
, we briefly explore them in this section.

The system of Gegenbauer polynomials, which we denote by
C_{l}^{\alpha}

for degree
l
and index
\alpha,
are ultraspherical polynomials expressible
through hyperspherical Legendre polynomials
\{P_{l,n}(x):l=0,1,2,\cdots\}.

Hyperspherical Legendre polynomials are a classical family of polynomials orthogonal with
respect to the weight function
w_{\alpha}=(1-x^{2})^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
on
the support interval
[-1,1].
We make these definitions formal in what follows.

Definition 3.1
.

The function
P_{l,n}
is hyperspherical Legendre polynomial of degree
l
in
n
dimension
and it is given by

P_{l,n}(t):=l!\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sum_{j=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}(-1)^{j}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{j}t^{l-2j}}{4^{j}j!(l-2j)!\Gamma(j+\frac{n-1}{2})}

(3.1)

for
t\in[-1,1].

Specifically,
P_{l,n}(1)=1,
for example.

There are other forms of definition of
P_{l,n}.
Popular among those definitions
is the Rodrigues formulation.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Rodrigues representation).
The hyperspherical Legendre polynomial of degree
l
in
n
dimension (
3.1
)
in Rodrigues form is given by

P_{l,n}(t)=(-1)^{l}R_{l,n}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{3-n}{2}}\frac{d^{l}}{dt^{l}}(1-t^{2})^{l+\frac{n-3}{2}}~~\mathrm{with}~~n\geq 2,

(3.2)

where the Rodrigues constant
R_{l,n}
is given by

R_{l,n}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{2^{l}\Gamma(l+\frac{n-1}{2})}.

(3.3)

For proof, see e.g.
[
11
,
2
]
and
[
13
]
.

We remark that for
n=2,
we have the relation

\Gamma(l+\frac{1}{2})=\frac{(2l)!}{2^{2l}l!}\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})

and so

P_{l,2}(t)=(-1)^{l}\frac{2^{l}l!}{(2l)!}(1-t^{2})^{1/2}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{l}(1-t^{2})^{l-1/2},~~~l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

which can be simplified to

P_{l,2}(t)=\cos(l\arccos t);~~t\in[-1,1].

For
n=3,
one recovers from (
3.2
) the standard Rodrigues representation
formula for the standard Legendre polynomials as

P_{l,3}(t)=\frac{1}{2^{l}l!}\Big(\frac{d}{dt}\Big)^{l}(t^{2}-1)^{l},~~~l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.4)

Moreover,
[
11
]
proved an integral representation of the hyperspherical harmonics
as the theorem (
3.3
) that follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

For
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

n\geq 3
and for any
t\in[-1,1],

P_{l,n}(t)=\frac{|S^{n-3}|}{|S^{n-2}|}\int_{-1}^{1}\Big[t+i(1-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}})s\Big]^{l}(1-s^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}ds.

(3.5)

Now, let
\mathcal{H}_{l,n}
denote the space homogeneous Legendre polynomials of degree
l

in dimension
n.
We call function
f\in\mathcal{H}_{l,n}
such that
\Delta f=0

hyperspherical harmonic. We denote the space of hyperspherical harmonic polynomials
restricted to the unit hypersphere,
S^{n},
by
\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}.

So any
Y_{l}\in\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}
is related to a homogeneous
harmonic
h_{l}\in\mathcal{H}_{l,n}
as follows
h_{l}(r\xi)=r^{l}Y_{l}(\xi)

where
r=|h_{l}|.
So, they have the same dimension.

The hyperspherical harmonic polynomials have the following properties.

Theorem 3.4
.

(Addition theorem).

Let
\{\psi_{l,j}:1\leq j\leq d_{l}(n)\}
be an orthonormal basis of the
space of
n
-dimensional spherical harmonics
\mathcal{H}_{l}(S^{n}),
i.e:

\int_{S^{n}}\psi_{l,j}(x)\bar{\psi}_{l,m}(x)dV_{g}(x)=\delta_{jm};~~1\leq j,m\leq d_{l}(n).

(3.6)

Then

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\psi_{l,j}(x)\bar{\psi}_{l,m}(y)=\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n}|}P_{l,\frac{(n-1)}{2}}(x\cdot y)

(3.7)

where
P_{l,n}(t)
are the hyperspherical Legendre polynomials of degree

l
in
n
dimensions. The dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{l}(S^{n})
is
d_{l}(n)

given by the formula

d_{l}(n)=\begin{pmatrix}l+n\\
n\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}l+n-2\\
n\end{pmatrix}=\frac{(2l+n-1)(l+n-2)!}{l!(n-1)!},~~\text{for}~~l\in\mathbb{N}.

For proof, one may see
[
12
]
and
[
11
]
. This means in particular
that
P_{l,(n-1)/2}(x\cdot y)
is a harmonic function on
S^{n}
with eigenvalue

\lambda_{l}=l(l+n-1)
for the eigenvalue problem

\Delta_{S^{n}}P_{l,(n-1)/2}(\theta)=\lambda_{l}P_{l,(n-1)/2}(\theta)

where
\Delta_{S^{n}}
is the usual Laplacian on
S^{n}
defined by

\Delta_{S^{n}}:=\frac{1}{\sin^{n-1}\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\big(\sin^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\big)+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\Delta_{S^{n-1}}.

(3.8)

We note that (
3.7
) can also be expressed in terms of Jacobian
polynomials
P_{l}^{\alpha,\alpha}
as follows:

\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\psi_{l,j}(x)\bar{\psi}_{l,m}(y)=\frac{1}{P_{l}^{\big(\frac{(n-2)}{2},\frac{(n-2)}{2}\big)}(1)}P_{l}^{(\frac{(n-2)}{2},\frac{(n-2)}{2})}(\cos\theta)~~\text{for}~~x\cdot y=\cos\theta.

(3.9)

A special case of the hyperspherical Legendre polynomials are the familiar spherical harmonics
on
S^{2}
usually given by

Y_{l,m}(\theta,\psi)=\tilde{N}_{lm}P_{l,m}(\cos\theta)\exp(jm\psi)

(3.10)

with normalisation constant

\tilde{N}_{lm}=\sqrt{(2l+1)\frac{(l-m)!}{(l+m)!}}

(3.11)

chosen so that the norm of each harmonic would be unity.

We have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

The hyperspherical harmonic polynomials are orthogonal:

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n}}P_{l,n}(x\cdot y)P_{j,n}(x\cdot y)dV_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{|S^{n}|}{d_{l}(n)}&\text{if}~~l=j.\\
0&\text{if}~~l\neq j.\end{cases}

(3.12)

Proof.

That

\int_{S^{n}}P_{l,n}(x\cdot y)P_{j,n}(x\cdot y)dV_{n}(x)=0~~\text{for}~~l\neq j

follows directly from (
3.6
).
When
l=j
we observe from (
3.7
) that

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n}}|P_{l,n}(x\cdot y)|^{2}dV_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\frac{|S^{n}|}{d_{l}(n)}\big)^{2}\int_{S^{n}}\big|\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\psi_{l,j}(x)\bar{\psi}_{l,m}(y)\big|^{2}dV_{g}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\frac{|S^{n}|}{d_{l}(n)}\big)^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\big|\psi_{l,j}(x)\big|^{2}=\big(\frac{|S^{n}|}{d_{l}(n)}\big)^{2}\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n}|}.

∎

We also have

\int_{-1}^{1}P_{l,n}(t)P_{j,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=\frac{|S^{n-1}|}{d_{l}(n)|S^{n-2}|}\delta_{lj}.

(3.13)

Besides, that

\int_{S^{n-1}}P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)P_{j,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)dS_{n-1}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&=&\text{if}~~l\neq j\\
\frac{|S^{n-1}|}{d_{l}(n)}&=&\text{if}~~l=j\end{array}\right.

follows from the following. Take
t=\xi\cdot\eta
and observe that

\int_{-1}^{1}|P_{l,n}(t)|^{2}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=\frac{|S^{n-1}|}{d_{l}(n)|S^{n-2}|}.

So,

\int_{S^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{-1}^{1}P_{l,n}(t)P_{j,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt\Big)dS_{n-2}=|S^{n-2}|\int_{-1}^{1}P_{l,n}(t)P_{j,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt

where

\int_{-1}^{1}P_{l,n}(t)P_{j,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=0~~\text{for}~~l\neq j.

Another interesting property is the projection of integrable function onto the space
of spherical harmonics on the hypersphere. We make the following definition.

Definition 3.6
.

The projection of
f\in L^{1}(S^{n-1})
into
\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}
is

(F_{l,n}f)(\xi):=\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)f(\eta)dS_{n-1}(\eta),~~\eta\in S^{n-1}.

(3.14)

Let us now consider the problem of finding the best approximation for

f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1}).
That is,

\inf_{Y_{n}\in\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}}||f-Y_{l}||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

(3.15)

In terms of the orthonormal
\{Y_{l,j}:1\leq j\leq d_{l}(n)\}

of
\mathcal{Y}_{l,n},
the solution to (
3.15
) is

(F_{l,n}f)(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\langle f,Y_{l,j}\rangle Y_{l,j}(\xi).

(3.16)

The operator
F_{l,n}
is the projection of any
f
into
\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}
and it is defined
for
f\in L^{1}(S^{n-1}).
One can apply this formula without explicit knowledge
of the orthonormal basis by applying the addition lemma (
3.5
).

Lemma 3.7
.

If
f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
then for any
\xi\in S^{n-1}
we have

||(F_{l,n}f)||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}\leq||f||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
and
\xi\in S^{n-1}
given, we have

\displaystyle|(F_{l,n}f)(\xi)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}|P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)|^{2}dS_{n-1}(\eta)\cdot\int_{S^{n-1}}|f(\eta)|^{2}dS_{n-1}(\eta)

\displaystyle\implies||(F_{l,n}f)||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{d_{l}(n)}||f||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

Similarly,

||(F_{l,n}f)||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}}||f||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

These imply that

||(F_{l,n}f)||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}\leq||f||_{L^{2}(S^{n-1})}.

∎

All these give rise to the following theorem.

Theorem 3.8
.

We have the orthogonal decomposition

L^{2}(S^{n-1})=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}.

This means that any
f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
can be uniquely represented as

f(\xi)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}f_{l}(\xi);~~\text{with}~~f_{l}\in\mathcal{Y}_{l,n};~l\geq 0.

(3.17)

We call
f_{l}\in\mathcal{Y}_{l,n}
hyperspherical component of
f
given by

f_{l}(\xi)=\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}f(\eta)P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)dS_{n-1}(\eta);~~~\eta\geq 0.

(3.18)

We now define another generalisation of the hyperspherical harmonics.

Definition 3.9
.

For
\alpha>0,

l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
l
with index

\alpha
is defined by

C_{l}^{\alpha}(t):=\binom{l+2\alpha-1}{l}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{-1}^{1}(t+i(1-t^{2})^{l/2}s)^{l}(1-s^{2})^{\alpha-1}ds.

(3.19)

Theorem 3.10
.

The Gegenbauer function
C_{l}^{\alpha}
is indeed a polynomial and has a representation in
terms of the hyperspherical harmonics as

C_{l}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(t):=\binom{l+n-3}{l}P_{l,n}(t);~~~\text{for}~~~n\geq 3.

(3.20)

Proof.

From the definition of
C_{l}^{\alpha}
in (
3.19
), we observe that by Binomial expansion

[t+i(1-t^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}s]^{l}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}t^{l-j}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{j}{2}}(is)^{j}.

So for
j=2k+1
odd, the integral corresponding term is

\int_{-1}^{1}s^{2k+1}(1-s^{2})^{\alpha-1}ds=0.

Thus,
C_{l}^{\alpha}
is real valued and has the form

C_{l}^{\alpha}(t)=\binom{l+2\alpha-1}{l}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}\binom{l}{2k}t^{l-2k}(-1)^{k}(1-t^{2})^{k}\int_{-1}^{1}s^{2k}(1-s^{2})^{\alpha-1}ds

which is a polynomial of degree less or equal to
l.

Besides, the coefficient of
t^{l}
in
C_{l}^{\alpha}(t)
is

\binom{l+2\alpha-1}{l}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha)}\sum_{k=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}\binom{l}{2k}\int_{-1}^{1}s^{2k}(1-s^{2})^{\alpha-1}ds.

Therefore,
C_{l}^{\alpha}(t)
is a polynomial.

Moreover, from (
3.5
), it follows that
C_{l}^{\alpha}(t)
has the form (
3.19
).
∎

We remark the following standard result.

Lemma 3.11
.

The Gegenbauer polynomials
C_{l}^{(n-1)/2}(\xi)
are eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami
operator on
S^{n-1}
which satisfy the following eigenvalue problem:

(1-\xi^{2})(C_{l}^{(n-1)/2})^{\prime\prime}(\xi)-n\xi(C_{l}^{(n-1)/2})^{\prime}(\xi)+l(l+n-1)C_{l}^{(n-1)/2}(\xi)=0.

(3.21)

Proof.

Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}(S^{n}).
Then,

\Delta_{S^{n}}\psi=\frac{1}{\sin^{n-1}\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\big(\sin^{n-1}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta}\big)+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\psi.

For a fixed
\xi\in S^{n},
the function
\psi
given by

C_{l}^{n}(\xi\cdot\eta)=C_{l}^{{(n-1)/2}}(\cos\theta)
belongs to

\mathcal{H}_{l}(S^{n})
where
\theta
is the geodesic angle between

\xi
and
\eta\in S^{n}.

A direct computation gives

\Delta_{S^{n}}\psi=l(l+n-1)\psi.

Since
\psi
only depends on
\xi,
so,

\Delta_{S^{n-1}}\psi=0
and thus

l(l+n-1)C_{l}^{n}(\xi)=\Delta_{S^{n}}C_{l}^{n}(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})\frac{\partial^{2}C_{l}^{n}(\xi)}{\partial t^{2}}-n\xi\frac{\partial C_{l}^{n}(\xi)}{\partial\xi}

(3.22)

proving the theorem.
∎

4
Spherical harmonic analysis and filtration

Consider the following generating function of the Gegenbauer harmonic (
3.19
) for a fixed
index
\alpha
:

\Phi(r)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}C_{l}^{\alpha}(t)r^{l}=\frac{1}{(1+r^{2}-2rt)^{(\frac{n-2}{2})}}~~~~\text{for}~~|r|<1,~~|t|\leq 1.

(4.1)

Consequently from (
3.20
),

\Phi(r)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{l+n-3}{n-3}P_{l,n}(t)r^{l}=\frac{1}{(1+r^{2}-2rt)^{(\frac{n-2}{2})}}~~~~\text{for}~~|r|<1,~~|t|\leq 1.

(4.2)

Definition 4.1
.

A Gegenbauer filtration kernel is

G_{n}(r,t):=\frac{|S^{n-2}|}{|S^{n-1}|}\frac{1-r^{2}}{(1+r^{2}-2rt)^{(\frac{n}{2})}}~~~~\text{for}~~|r|<1,~~|t|\leq 1~~~\text{and}~~~n\geq 3.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.2
.

The function (
4.3
) satisfies the following properties

\int_{-1}^{1}G_{n}(r,t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=1

(4.4)

and

\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}G_{n}(r,t)=0.

(4.5)

Proof.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}G_{n}(r,t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{n-2}|}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{-1}^{1}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l}(n)r^{l}P_{l,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{n-2}|}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt=1

showing (
4.4
).

Besides for
n\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big(\frac{1-r^{2}}{(1+r^{2}-2rt)^{\tfrac{n}{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big(\frac{1-r^{2}}{[(1-r)^{2}+2r(1-t)]^{\tfrac{n}{2}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow 1^{-}}\Big(\frac{1-r^{2}}{[2r(1-t_{0})]^{\tfrac{n}{2}}}\Big)=0

for
t\in[-1,t_{0}]
arbitrary, showing (
4.5
).
∎

Now we define the Gegenbauer filtration operator
\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)
to be

(\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)f)(\xi):=\frac{1}{|S^{n-2}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}G_{n}(r,\xi\cdot\eta)f(\eta)dS_{n-1}.

(4.6)

We immediately have the following result.

Theorem 4.3
.

The Gegebauer filtration
\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)
is the limit of a sequence of finite linear
combinations of the hyperspherical Legendre harmonics.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1}).
From (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle(\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S^{n-2}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}G_{n}(r,\xi\cdot\eta)f(\eta)dS_{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S^{n-2}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{-1}^{1}G_{n}(r,t)P_{l,n}(t)(1-t^{2})^{\tfrac{n-3}{2}}dt\Big)f(\eta)dS_{n-1}\Big|_{t=\xi\cdot\eta}

\displaystyle\implies(\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)f)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|S^{n-2}|}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}r^{l}d_{l}(n)\int_{S^{n-2}}C_{l}^{\alpha}(\xi\cdot\eta)f(\eta)dS_{n-1}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}r^{l}(F_{l,n}f)(\xi)

and for
|r|<1,

(\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)f)(\xi)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}d_{l}(r)P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)r^{l}(F_{l,n}f)(\xi)=(F_{l,n}f)(\xi)

(4.7)

which completes the proof.
∎

The inner product of two complex valued functions on the surface of the hypersphere is

\langle f,h\rangle_{S^{n-1}}=\int_{S^{n-1}}f(\xi,\eta)h(\xi,\eta)^{\ast}dS_{n-1}(\eta)

(4.8)

where
\ast
denotes complex conjugation. Using the fact that the Gegenbauer filtration
is rotation invariant over
SO(n),
one can move
h
to any point

(\xi_{0},\eta_{0})\in S^{n-1}.
Then we define a generalised convolution
as a function on the rotation group
SO(n)
to be

(h\ast f)(R)=\int_{S^{n-1}}f(\xi,\eta)h_{R}(\xi,\eta)^{\ast}dS_{n-1}(\eta)

(4.9)

for
R\in SO(n)
and where
h_{R}
is
h
rotated by
R
defined as

h_{R}(A)=h(R^{-1}A).

(4.10)

Thus, every well-behaved function
f\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
admits the expansion

f(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l,m}\hat{f}_{l,m}(\xi,\eta)C_{l}^{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)

(4.11)

where the generalised Fourier transform on the surface of the hypersphere

\hat{f}_{l,m}
is defined to be

\hat{f}_{l,m}(\xi)=\int_{S^{n-1}\ni\eta\in SO(n)}f(R)\rho_{l,m}(R)dS_{n-1}(\eta).

(4.12)

Here,
\rho(R)
is a function on
SO(n)
containing fixed matrix valued functions called
the irreducible representations of
SO(n).

As a consequence of (
3.16
), we observe that for a suitable
\psi
, we have

\displaystyle(F_{l,n}\psi)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}\int_{S^{n-1}}P_{l,n}(\xi\cdot\eta)\psi(\eta)dS_{n-1}(\eta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d_{l}(n)}{|S^{n-1}|}\frac{|S^{n-1}|}{d_{l}(n)}\psi_{l}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\langle\psi_{l},\psi_{l,j}\rangle_{S^{n-1}}\psi_{l,j}(\xi)

\displaystyle\implies(F_{l,n}\psi)(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d_{l}(n)}\hat{\psi}_{l}\psi_{l,j}(\xi).

(4.13)

Another interesting result of this work is that Gegenbauer filtration on
S^{n-1}

coincides with convolution on
S^{n-1}.
Particularly, convolution of two
functions on the hypersphere equals the multiplication of their Fourier coefficients.
This is the next theorem (
4.4
) below.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f,h\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
and
\mathcal{G}(r)
the Gegenbauer filtration operator.
Then the spectrum of the Gegenbauer kernel convolution is given by

\widehat{f\ast h}(l,\alpha)=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(l+n-2)(2l+n-2)}{l!\Gamma(n-1)}}\hat{f}(l,\alpha)\hat{h}(l,0).

(4.14)

A special case of this property has been proved for the special case of convolutions
on
S^{2}
, see e.g.
[
8
,
6
]
and
[
4
]
. We get a proof
for this generalisation on
S^{n-1}.

Proof.

Since
f,h\in L^{2}(S^{n-1})
and by definition of the Gegenbauer filtration,

\displaystyle\widehat{f\ast h}(l,\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}f\ast h(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)(\xi)}dS_{n-1}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\Big(\int_{SO(n)}f(R\eta)h(R^{-1}\xi)dR\Big)\overline{\mathcal{G}_{n}(r)(\xi)}dS_{n-1}(\xi).

After rearranging the integrals and using the (
3.16
) we get

\widehat{f\ast h}(l,\alpha)=\int_{SO(n)}\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}h(R^{-1}\xi)\overline{(F_{l,n})(F_{j,n})C_{l}^{\alpha}C_{j}^{\alpha}(\xi)}dS_{n-1}(\xi)\Big)f(RN)dR

where
N
is the north pole. This implies that from (
4
) and the addition theorem
(
3.4
),

\widehat{f\ast h}(l,\alpha)=\int_{SO(n)}f(RN)\Big(\int_{S^{n-1}}h(R^{-1}\xi)\sum_{|j|\leq d_{l}(n)}d_{l}(n)C_{j}^{\alpha}(R\xi)F_{j,n}(R^{-1}\xi)dS_{n-1}(\xi)\Big)C_{l}^{\alpha}(R^{-1}\xi)dR

which is
0
unless
\alpha=0.

This follows since the measure on
SO(n)
is rotation-invariant.

Therefore,

\widehat{f\ast h}(l,\alpha)=\int_{SO(n)}f(RN)C_{l}^{\alpha}(R^{-1}\xi)dR\hat{h}(l,0).

Finally using the relationship between
C_{l}^{\alpha}
and the hyperspherical harmonics

P_{l,n}
(
3.20
), we conclude that (
4.14
) holds.
∎

5
Conclusion

We have derived formulae for Gegebauer kernel filtration of different
functions on the unit hypersphere. Particularly, we derived a Gegebauer filtration operator
via hyperspherical Legendre harmonics. We related the filtration to convolution on the
hypersphere and proved that Gegenbauer filtration on
S^{n-1}

coincides with convolution on
S^{n-1}.
We showed that convolution of two
functions on the hypersphere equals the multiplication of their Fourier coefficients.

The results obtained suggest that filtrations and convolutions on the Euclidean space
can be successfully extended to spaces with non-Euclidean topologies such as the hypersphere
using the example of Gegenbauer filters. The formulae and approach are readily
extendable to other non-Euclidean geometries such as the flat torus and cylinder in higher
dimensions. These can be done as further extension of this study.

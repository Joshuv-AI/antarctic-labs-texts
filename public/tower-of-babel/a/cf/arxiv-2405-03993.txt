Capillary Surfaces in Manifolds with Nonnegative Scalar Curvature and Strictly Mean Convex Boundary
Yujie Wu

Abstract
. In this paper we use stable capillary surfaces (analogous to the μ \mu -bubble construction) to study manifolds with strictly mean convex boundary and nonnegative scalar curvature. We give an obstruction to filling 2-manifolds by such 3-manifolds based on the Urysohn width. We also obtain a bandwidth estimate and establish other geometric properties of such manifolds.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Gro18a
]
, Gromov asked the question of finding sufficient conditions to allow or disallow fill-in of a given Riemannian manifold
Y^{n}
as the boundary of a Riemannian manifold
X^{n+1}
with nonnegative scalar curvature. Can the mean curvature of
Y=\partial X
prove enough influence so that the we cannot prescribe certain geometry properties on
X
?

In this paper we are interested in the case when
n=2
. If
Y
is a connected orientable closed surface with positive Gaussian curvature, then there is an isometric embedding of
Y
into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
as a strictly convex surface, we denote the mean curvature of this embedding as
H_{0}
(such embedding is unique up to isometry of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
). Using this, Shi-Tam
[
ST02
]
proved that if there is some
(X,g)
with nonnegative scalar curvature filling such a
Y
with positive mean curvature
H
, then

\int_{Y}Hd\sigma\leq\int_{Y}H_{0}d\sigma,

where
d\sigma
is the volume form induced from the metric
g
. Moreover, equality holds if and only if
X
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. This result gives a positive answer to the question in the first paragraph.

In this paper, we give another answer in the following theorem using capillary surfaces. As an example, if a 3-manifold fills
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
H\geq 2
and has nonnegative scalar curvature, then the first Urysohn width of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in the induced metric is no more than
4.5\pi
(take
a_{0}=\frac{2}{3},d_{0}=\frac{3\pi}{4}
). Recent results in upper bound of Urysohn width was also obtained in
[
WZ23
]
,
[
LW23
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
(N^{3},\partial N,g)
is a compact manifold with simply-connected boundary such that
R_{g}\geq 0
and
H_{\partial N}\geq\frac{\pi}{d_{0}}+a_{0}
, then the first Urysohn width of
\partial N
(with respect to the induced metric
g
) is bounded:
U_{1}(\partial N)\leq 4d_{0}+\frac{\pi}{a_{0}}
.

In another direction, Gromov proved the following bandwidth estimate, here we denote
T^{n-1}
as the
n-1

On the other hand, for manifolds with boundary, to constrain a minimizer of a generalized area functional, we can prescribe both mean curvature and the angle of intersection along the boundary. In particular, mean convexity assumption is helpful to constrain capillary surfaces. This allows us to relax the assumption of PSC when studying manifolds with boundary.

In

]
, Ros and Souam studied constant mean curvature surfaces which intersect the ambient boundary at a fixed angle, called capillary surfaces.
In the second variation formula for capillary surfaces, instead of fixed angle, we allow the prescribed angle to vary.
For manifolds with nonnegative scalar curvature and strictly mean convex boundary,
we are able to gain rigidity results for 2-manifolds (Theorem
1.3
), and exhaustion of the boundary for 3-manifolds (Theorem
1.4

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider a connected Riemannian manifold
\Sigma^{2}
with boundary, such that
R_{\Sigma}\geq 0,k_{\partial\Sigma}\geq 1
, then
\partial\Sigma
is connected with length no more than
2\pi
; for any
x\in\Sigma
,
d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq 1,
so
\Sigma
is compact.
\Sigma
is a topological disk. If
|\partial\Sigma|=2\pi
, then
\Sigma
is isometric to a flat unit disk in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The fact that every point in this manifold must be at most distance 1 away from the boundary follows from Li-Nguyen
[
LN14
]
and Li
[
Li14
]
. Furthermore, Li
[
Li14
]
conjectured the following: A complete Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
with nonnegative Ricci curvature and
A_{\partial M}\geq 1
must be compact (
A_{\partial M}
is the second fundamental form of the boundary).
This theorem solves the 2-dimensional case of this conjecture. We note in Theorem
1.3
we do not assume orientability or embeddedness. In the case of
M^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, a strictly convex hypersurface bounding a region with pinched second fundamental form, Hamilton
[
Ham94
]
showed that
M
must be compact.

Contrary to the 2-dimensional case, for a connected 3-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and
H_{\partial M}\geq 2
(
H_{\partial M}
is mean curvature of the boundary), its boundary might not be connected or compact. But we can obtain an exhaustion of the boundary as below, and show that the boundary has linear volume growth under further topological assumptions (see Corollary

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M^{3},\partial M)
be a complete connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature, and
\partial M
is connected non-compact with
H_{\partial M}\geq 2
.

We can find an exhaustion of the boundary
\partial M=\cup_{k}Z_{k}
, with
\partial Z_{k}=\partial\Sigma^{\prime}_{k}
, where
\Sigma^{\prime}_{k}
is a union of finitely many capillary surface of disk type in
M
. Each component
\Sigma_{k}
of
\Sigma^{\prime}_{k}
has bounded boundary length:
|\partial\Sigma_{k}|\leq 2\pi
, and
d_{\Sigma_{k}}(x,\partial\Sigma_{k})\leq 2
for any
x\in\Sigma_{k}
.

Furthermore, if
\pi_{1}(\partial M)=0
, let
E_{k}
be an unbounded component of
\partial M\setminus Z_{k}
such that
E_{k+1}\subset E_{k}
(so
(E_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is an end of
\partial M
), then
\overline{E_{k}}\setminus E_{k+1}
is connected and
\sup_{k}\text{diam}_{\partial M}(\overline{E_{k}}\setminus E_{k+1})\leq 5\pi
.

Using Theorem
1.4
when
\pi_{1}(\partial M)=0
and
\partial M
has weakly bounded geometry we can apply section 3 in
[
Wu23
]
to get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
M^{3}
be a complete connected Riemannian manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature, whose non-compact boundary
\partial M
is simply connected, uniformly mean convex and has weakly bounded geometry, then each end of
\partial M
has linear volume growth. In particular, if
\partial M
has finitely many ends, then
\partial M
has linear volume growth.

2.
2D results

Throughout this paper, if
(N^{k+1},\partial N,g),k\geq 1,
is a Riemannian manifold with boundary, we denote the second fundamental form of
\partial N\subset N
with respect to the outward pointing unit normal
\nu
as
\mathrm{I\!I}(X,Y)=-\langle\nabla_{X}Y,\nu\rangle
for vector fields
X,Y
tangent to
\partial N
. Then the scalar-valued mean curvature is written as
H=\sum_{i=1}^{k}-\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i},\nu\rangle
for an orthonormal basis
(e_{i})_{i}^{k}
of
T_{p}\partial N
at some point
p\in\partial N
. In this convention, the boundary of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{3}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
has positive mean curvature,
H_{\partial\mathbb{B}^{3}}=2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The
d_{\Sigma}(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq 1
part follows directly from
[
LN14
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
Li14
, Theorem 1.1]
. Note that from this result we know that
\Sigma
is compact if and only if
\partial\Sigma
is compact.

Step 1.
If we know that
\Sigma
is compact, we can show that
\partial\Sigma
is connected and the length of
\partial\Sigma
is no more than
2\pi
.

We apply Gauss Bonnet theorem to get, denoting
\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}=K_{\Sigma}
:

(2.1)

2\pi\geq 2\pi\chi=\int_{\Sigma}K_{\Sigma}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k_{\partial\Sigma}\geq|\partial\Sigma|>0.

Here we used
\Sigma
has at least one boundary component, so
\chi_{\Sigma}\leq 1
. And if
\partial\Sigma
has more than 1 boundary component then we would get
\chi_{\Sigma}\leq 0
, a contradiction.
So
\partial\Sigma
is connected with
|\partial\Sigma|\leq 2\pi
, and
\chi(\Sigma)>0
, so
\chi_{\Sigma}=1
, and
\Sigma
is a topological disk.

Step 2.

We now look at the rigidity statement under the assumption that
\Sigma
is compact (and
\partial\Sigma
is connected by Step 1).

If
K\geq 0,k\geq 1

|\partial\Sigma|=2\pi

We denote the unit speed loop of

as
\gamma:[0,2\pi]\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}
, and write
\gamma(0)=\gamma(2\pi)=p
. Then by choosing
\epsilon

\Psi_{t}:B_{\epsilon}(\gamma(t))\rightarrow\mathbb{D}

\Psi_{t}

around origin, we can patch up these isometry

to get a global isometry
\Psi_{0}

to a neighborhood of
\partial\mathbb{D}

\mathbb{D}

We now define a global isometry

\Sigma

as follows, for

\Psi(x)=\Psi_{0}(x)

\mathbb{D}

Step 3.

We now show

If not, then there are at least two components
\partial_{1}\Sigma,\partial_{2}\Sigma

\partial\Sigma

\alpha=\inf\left\{d(x,y),x\in\partial_{1}\Sigma,y\in\partial_{2}\Sigma\right\}

\beta_{0}(t)

T

\beta_{0}

\partial\Sigma

\partial\Sigma

Now we look at the case

Step 4.
We assume
\Sigma

\displaystyle A^{\prime\prime}(\gamma)=

\int_{\partial\Sigma\cap\Omega_{i}}w
is uniformly bounded for a minimizing sequence, so
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\gamma_{i})
is also uniformly bounded. We get that the minimizing sequence must be contained in a bounded set from the point
x
.
We can continue the minimization with standard BV compactness and regularity theory (see
[
PM15
]
).

We now check that the smooth minimizer
\gamma=\partial\Omega
must have nonempty boundary. Note
B^{\prime}_{2}(x)\subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, and
\Omega
is disjoint from the set
W_{+}
. So if
\gamma
is a minimizer with empty boundary, then
\Omega\cap\partial\Sigma
and
\partial\Sigma\setminus{\Omega}
is two disjoint nonempty open sets, whose union is
\partial\Sigma
, a contradiction to
\partial\Sigma
being connected.
∎

3.
3D results

Contrary to the 2-dimensional case, a 3-manifold with nonnegative scalar curvature and uniformly mean convex boundary, might not have connected boundary. We also might not have that any point is at bounded distance away from the boundary (using the same method would require non-negative Ricci curvature).

Before we start the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.1
.

If there is a smooth function
u>0
over a compact surface
(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
with nonempty boundary, such that,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Sigma}u\leq\frac{R_{\Sigma}}{2}u+\frac{|\nabla_{\Sigma}u|^{2}}{2u}\quad

\displaystyle\text{over }\Sigma,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla_{\nu}u}{u}\geq a_{0}-k_{\partial\Sigma}\quad

\displaystyle\text{over }\partial\Sigma,

then
d(x,\partial\Sigma)\leq\frac{2}{a_{0}}
for any
x\in\Sigma
; the outward pointing unit normal along
\partial\Sigma
is denoted as
\nu
,
R_{\Sigma}
is the scalar curvature of
\Sigma
and
k_{\partial\Sigma}
is the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Sigma
.

Proof.

If not, assume
d(z,\partial\Sigma)\geq\frac{2}{a_{0}}+2\delta
for some
z\in\Sigma
and
0<\delta<1

\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
for sets with finite perimeters
\Gamma\subset\Sigma
containing a neighborhood
U_{0}
of
z
(will be chosen later) and disjoint from
\partial\Sigma
. Denote
\partial\Gamma=\gamma
, and
\nu_{\gamma}
the outward unit normal of
\gamma\subset\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)=\int_{\gamma}u-\int_{\Sigma}hu(\chi_{\Gamma}-\chi_{\Gamma_{0}}),

here
h(y)
is a mollification of the function
\hat{h}(y)=\frac{2}{\alpha-d_{\Sigma}(y,\partial\Sigma)}>0
when
x\in U
; here
\alpha=\frac{2}{a_{0}}+\delta
and
U:=\{y\in\Sigma,d(y,\partial\Sigma)<\alpha\}
. We require
h\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}<a_{0},h\rvert_{\Sigma\setminus U}=\infty
, and
\frac{1}{2}h^{2}-|\nabla h|>0
everywhere on
U
. We pick
\Gamma_{0}
to be an open neighborhood of
z
with smooth boundary and
h\rvert_{\Sigma\setminus\Gamma_{0}}\in L^{\infty}
. So for any smooth open set
\Gamma
,
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)>-\infty
.

By the proof of Proposition 12 in
[
CL24
]
, there is a smooth open neighborhood
U_{0}
around

such that

for any
\Gamma
with smooth boundary and
h\rvert_{\Sigma\setminus U_{0}}\in L^{\infty}
. This implies that any minimizing sequence must contain
U_{0}
and
\inf\mathcal{F}>-\infty
.

We now check that any minimizing sequence must be disjoint from a fixed open neighborhood of
\partial\Sigma
. So by interior regularity we have that a smooth minimizer exists
[
Tam84
]

[
CL24
]
.

By first variation, if a smooth minimizer
\Gamma
exists then its boundary
\gamma
has geodesic curvature equal to

. If
\gamma
touches the boundary

at some point

\displaystyle k_{\gamma}(x)

\displaystyle=h\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}(x)-\frac{\nabla_{\nu}u}{u}(x)

\displaystyle\leq h\rvert_{\partial\Sigma}(x)-a_{0}+k_{\partial\Sigma}(x)<k_{\partial\Sigma},

where we used (
3.2
) and

.
Using this observation, similar to Step 5 in Theorem
1.3
, we use a foliation along
\partial\Sigma
to modify our minimizing sequence so that it is disjoint from a fixed open neighborhood of
\partial\Sigma
.

To be precise, given any smooth minimizing sequence
\gamma_{i}=\partial\Gamma_{i}
, we show that
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma_{i}\setminus T^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{F}(\Gamma_{i})
, where
T^{\prime}=\cup_{t\in[0,\epsilon],z\in\partial\Sigma}\exp_{z}(-t\nu)
for some small
\epsilon
to be decided. If we fix

, then we get

a smooth curve with unit normal
\nu_{t}
(pointing in the direction as

increases), and that as

. We denote the outward pointing unit normal of
\partial T^{\prime}\subset T^{\prime}
and
\partial\Gamma_{i}\subset\Gamma_{i}
as
\nu_{\partial T^{\prime}}
and
\nu_{\partial\Gamma_{i}}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\Gamma_{i}\setminus T^{\prime})-\mathcal{F}(\Gamma_{i})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}}u+\int_{\partial\Gamma_{i}\cap T^{\prime}}u\cdot(-1)+\int_{\Gamma_{i}\cap T^{\prime}}hu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}}u(-\nu_{t})\cdot\nu_{\partial T^{\prime}}+\int_{\partial\Gamma_{i}\cap T^{\prime}}u\nu_{t}\cdot\nu_{\partial\Gamma_{i}}+\int_{\Gamma_{i}\cap T^{\prime}}hu

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}}\text{div}^{\Sigma}(u\nu_{t})+hu

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}}u\text{div}^{\Sigma}(\nu_{t})+\nabla_{\nu_{t}}u+hu

\displaystyle=\int_{T^{\prime}\cap\Gamma_{i}}u(\text{div}^{\Sigma}(\nu_{t})+\frac{\nabla_{\nu_{t}}u}{u}+h)\leq 0

in the final inequality we used (

\nu_{t}\rightarrow-\nu

h-\frac{\nabla_{-\nu_{t}}u}{u}+\text{div}(\nu_{t})<0.

So any minimizing sequence must be disjoint from

provided we choose

We now write out the second variation formula for a minimizer of
\mathcal{F}(\Gamma)
. This was derived in Theorem 6.3 of
[

]
(see also
[

]
Lemma 16),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma}|\nabla_{\gamma}\psi|^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}R_{\Sigma}\psi^{2}u-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}_{\gamma}\psi^{2}u+(\Delta_{\Sigma}u-\Delta_{\gamma}u)\psi^{2}-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma}u,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\psi^{2}h-\langle\nabla_{\Sigma}h,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\psi^{2}u

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\gamma}|\nabla_{\gamma}\psi|^{2}u-(\Delta_{\gamma}u)\psi^{2}-(\frac{h^{2}}{2}+\nabla_{\nu_{\gamma}}h)\psi^{2}u+\frac{\psi^{2}(\nabla_{\gamma}u)^{2}}{2u}

\displaystyle<\int_{\gamma}|\nabla_{\gamma}\psi|^{2}u+\nabla_{\gamma}\psi^{2}\nabla_{\gamma}u+\frac{\psi^{2}(\nabla_{\gamma}u)^{2}}{2u}\quad(\star)

where we used (

Then we can plug

there’s some

. Now

implies

, adding up the length proves the claim.

∎

Remark 3.4
.

As a concrete example, take any surface

, i.e. with nonnegative scalar curvature and strictly convex boundary, then Theorem

applies to

. For example if

has nonnegative Ricci curvature, and strictly positive second fundamental form, then

Remark 3.5

In the proof above the bound

Remark 3.6
.

Theorem
1.4
only describes the behavior of
\partial M
, and has no control of the interior of
M
. As an example, if
B=\mathbb{B}^{3}_{r}(z)
is a small interior ball in

with

, then one can concatenate

along

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

There is some

so that

We consider a minimizer of the following functional among open sets
\Omega

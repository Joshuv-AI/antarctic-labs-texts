Characterizations of o-polynomials by the Walsh transform
Claude Carlet and Sihem Mesnager

Abstract
The notion of o-polynomial comes from finite projective geometry. In 2011 and later, it has been shown that those objects play an important role in symmetric cryptography and coding theory to design bent Boolean functions, bent vectorial Boolean functions, semi-bent functions and to construct good linear codes. In this note, we characterize o-polynomials by the Walsh transform of the associated vectorial functions.



Keywords:
o-polynomial, vectorial function, Walsh–Hadamard transform.

1
Introduction

In all this paper,
n
is a positive integer.
The projective space
PG(2,{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}})
is a point-line incidence
structure whose points are the one-dimensional vector subspaces of

{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{3}
, whose lines are the two-dimensional vector
subspaces of
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{3}
, and where a point is incident with a line
if and only if the one-dimensional vector subspace corresponding to
the point is a subspace of the two-dimensional vector subspace
corresponding to the line. A set of
k
points such that no three of them lie
in a common line is called a
k
-arc. The maximum cardinality
k
of an
arc in
PG(2,2^{n})
is
2^{n}+2
. An
oval
of
PG(2,2^{n})
is an
arc of cardinality
2^{n}+1
. A
hyperoval
of
PG(2,2^{n})
is an
arc of maximum cardinality
2^{n}+2
. A hyperoval
\mathcal{O}
of

PG(2,2^{n})
containing the fundamental quadrangle (that is, the set of points
(1,0,0)
,
(0,1,0)
,
(0,0,1)
and
(1,1,1)
) can be described as

\mathcal{O}=\{(1,t,G(t)),t\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{m}}\}\cup\{(0,1,0),(0,0,1)\}
for some polynomial
G
that is called an

o-polynomial
. Those polynomials can be characterized as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
]
)
A polynomial
G
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
is an
o
-polynomial if and only

G
is a permutation and each of the polynomials
G_{s}
,
s\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
,
is a permutation where

G_{s}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{G(t+s)+G(s)}{t}&\mbox{if $t\not=0$}\\
0&\mbox{if $t=0$.}\end{array}\right.

Recently, in
[
1
]
, has been discovered a
relation between Niho bent functions and o-polynomials when
n
is
even. Niho bent functions are those Boolean functions over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
whose
restrictions to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{\frac{n}{2}}}
are linear. It has
been shown that each Niho bent function corresponds to an

o
-polynomial and that each
o
-polynomial gives rise to Niho bent
functions. Further, it has been shown in
[
4
]
, that
o-polynomials also give rise to infinite families of semi-bent
functions in even dimension. Very recently, new connections between bent vectorial functions and the hyperovals of the
projective plane (extending the link provided in
[
1
]
between bent Boolean functions and the hyperovals) as well as a connection between
o-polynomials in even characteristic and
2^{r}
-ary simplex codes have
been established in
[
5
]
. Furthermore, Ding has provided a nice article
[
2
]

where o-polynomials are employed to construct linear codes.

In this note, we present a new characterization of
o
-polynomials by
means of their Walsh transform.

2
Preliminaries

Given a finite set
E
,
|E|
denotes the cardinality of
E
.
Recall that for any positive integers
k
, and
r

dividing
k
, the trace function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{r}}
, denoted
by
Tr_{r}^{k}
, is the mapping defined for every
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
as:

Tr_{r}^{k}(x):=\sum_{i=0}^{\frac{k}{r}-1}x^{2^{ir}}=x+x^{2^{r}}+x^{2^{2r}}+\cdots+x^{2^{k-r}}.

In particular, the
absolute trace
occurs for
r=1
. We shall
denote
tr_{n}
the absolute trace function
Tr_{1}^{n}
. Let
m
be a positive integer and

F:{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
be a vectorial Boolean
function. The Walsh transform of
F
at

(u,v)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\times{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{m}}
equals by definition the Walsh transform
of the so-called component function
tr_{m}(v(F(x))
at
u
, that is:

W_{F}(u,v):=\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{tr_{m}(v(F(x))+tr_{n}(ux)}.

3
A characterization of o-polynomials

Consider a polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and its associated
function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. We shall denote
the polynomial by
F(X)
and the function by
F(x)
. It is well-known (see e.g.
[
1
]
)
that
F(X)
is an
o
-polynomial if and only if, for every
a
and

b\neq 0
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, the equation
F(x)+bx=a
has 0 or 2
solutions (and we know that
F
is then a permutation). Consider the
polynomial over
{\mathbb{R}}
equal to
X(X-2)^{2}=X^{3}-4X^{2}+4X
. It takes
value 0 when
X
equals 0 or 2 and takes strictly positive value when

X
is in
{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\{0,2\}
. We have then that

\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{3}-4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{2}

+4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|\geq 0,

and

F(X)
is an o-polynomial if and only if this inequality is an
equality for every nonzero
b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and every
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. Equivalently, we have

\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\neq 0}{\Big(}\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{3}-4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{2}

+4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|{\Big)}\geq 0,

and
F(X)
is an o-polynomial
if and only if this inequality is an equality.

We shall now characterize this condition by means of the Walsh transform.
We have:

\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|=2^{-n}\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{tr_{n}(v(F(x)+bx+a))},

and therefore, for
j\geq 1
:

\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\neq 0}\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{j}=

2^{-jn}\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\neq 0}\sum_{x_{1},\dots,x_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\atop v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{j}tr_{n}(v_{i}(F(x_{i})+bx_{i}+a))},

and using that
\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{tr_{n}(va)}
is nonzero for
v=0
only and takes then value
2^{n}
, we deduce that

\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\neq 0}\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{j}=

2^{-(j-1)n}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v_{1},\dots,v_{j}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\atop\sum_{i=1}^{j}v_{i}=0}\prod_{i=1}^{j}W_{F}(bv_{i},v_{i})

and that

\sum_{a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}},b\neq 0}{\Big(}\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{3}-4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|^{2}

+4\left|\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}};F(x)+bx+a=0\}\right|{\Big)}=

2^{-2n}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}(bv_{1},v_{1})W_{F}(bv_{2},v_{2})W_{F}(b(v_{1}+v_{2}),v_{1}+v_{2})

-2^{-n+2}\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}^{2}(bv,v)+(2^{n}-1)2^{n+2}.

We have
\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}^{2}(bv,v)=(2^{n}-1)W_{F}^{2}(0,0)+\sum_{u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*},v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}W_{F}^{2}(u,v)=(2^{n}-1)2^{2n+1}-\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}W_{F}^{2}(0,v)
.
We deduce:

Theorem 3.1

Let
F
be any
(n,n)
-function and
F(X)
be the associated polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. Then:

\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}(bv_{1},v_{1})W_{F}(bv_{2},v_{2})W_{F}(b(v_{1}+v_{2}),v_{1}+v_{2})

+2^{n+2}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}W_{F}^{2}(0,v)-2^{4n+2}+2^{3n+2}\geq 0,

(1)

and this inequality is an equality if and only if
F
is an o-polynomial.

The sum
\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}(bv_{1},v_{1})W_{F}(bv_{2},v_{2})W_{F}(b(v_{1}+v_{2}),v_{1}+v_{2})
is equal to

2^{n}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{3}\atop v_{1}x_{1}+v_{2}x_{2}+(v_{1}+v_{2})x_{3}=0}(-1)^{v_{1}F(x_{1})+v_{2}F(x_{2})+(v_{1}+v_{2})F(x_{3})}.

We deduce:

Corollary 3.2

Let
F
be any
(n,n)
-function and
F(X)
be the associated polynomial over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
. Then:

2^{n}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}\sum_{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{3}\atop v_{1}x_{1}+v_{2}x_{2}+(v_{1}+v_{2})x_{3}=0}(-1)^{v_{1}F(x_{1})+v_{2}F(x_{2})+(v_{1}+v_{2})F(x_{3})}

-\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}(0,v_{1})W_{F}(0,v_{2})W_{F}(0,v_{1}+v_{2})

+2^{n+2}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}W_{F}^{2}(0,v)-2^{4n+2}+2^{3n+2}\geq 0,

(2)

and this inequality is an equality if and only if
F
is an o-polynomial.

Remark 3.3

Observe that, when
F
is an
o
-polynomial, we have:

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W^{2}_{F}(bv,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}\sum_{x_{1},x_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{tr_{n}(v(F(x_{1})+F(x_{2})+b(x_{1}+x_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}\sum_{\gamma,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}(-1)^{tr_{n}(v(F(z+\gamma)+F(\gamma)+bz)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}|\{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\mid F(z+\gamma)+F(\gamma)+bz=0\}|.

Now,

|\{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\mid F(z+\gamma)+F(\gamma)+bz=0\}|=2

for every
\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
according to Theorem

1.1
. And thus, if
F
is an
o
-polynomial,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W^{2}_{F}(bv,v)=2^{2n+1}.

One can then deduce from Theorem
3.1
that
A polynomial
F(X)
is an
o
-polynomial if and only if

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{\star}}\sum_{v\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W^{2}_{F}(bv,v)=(2^{n}-1)2^{2n+1}.

and

\displaystyle\sum_{b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}W_{F}(bv_{1},v_{1})W_{F}(bv_{2},v_{2})W_{F}(b(v_{1}+v_{2}),v_{1}+v_{2})=(2^{n}-1)2^{3n+2}.

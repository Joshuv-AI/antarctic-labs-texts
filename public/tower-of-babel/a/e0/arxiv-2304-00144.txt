Non-Archimedean Green’s functions and Zariski decompositions
Sébastien Boucksom and Mattias Jonsson

Abstract
. We study the non-Archimedean Monge–Ampère equation on a smooth variety over a discretely or trivially valued field. First, we give an example of a Green’s function, associated to a divisorial valuation, which is not ℚ \mathbb{Q} -PL (i.e. not a model function in the discretely valued case). Second, we produce an example of a function whose Monge–Ampère measure is a finite atomic measure supported in a dual complex, but which is not invariant under the retraction associated to any snc model. This answers a question by Burgos Gil et al in the negative. Our examples are based on geometric constructions by Cutkosky and Lesieutre, and arise via base change from Green’s functions over a trivially valued field; this theory allows us to efficiently encode the Zariski decomposition of a pseudoeffective numerical class.



Introduction

In the seminal paper
[
Yau78
]
, Yau studied the Monge–Ampère equation

(MA)

(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}=\mu

on a compact
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold
(X,\omega)
, where
\mu
is a smooth, strictly positive measure on
X
of mass
\int\omega^{n}
, and
\varphi
a smooth function on

X
such that the
(1,1)
-form
\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi
is positive. Yau proved that there exists a smooth solution
\varphi
, unique up to a constant. If
\omega
is rational class, say
\omega=c_{1}(L)
for an ample line bundle
L
, then
\varphi
can be viewed as a positive metric on
L
, and
(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}
its the curvature measure.

As observed by Kontsevich, Soibelman, and Tschinkel
[
KS06
,
KT
]
, when studying degenerating 1-parameter families of Kähler manifolds, it can be fruitful to use non-Archimedean geometry in the sense of Berkovich over the field
\mathbb{C}(\!(\varpi)\!)
of complex Laurent series. In this context, a
Monge–Ampère operator was introduced by Chambert–Loir
[
CL06
]
, and a version of (MA) was solved by the authors and Favre
[
BFJ15
]
; see below. Uniqueness of solutions was proved earlier by Yuan and Zhang
[
YZ17
]
.

Now, the method in
[
BFJ15
]
is variational in nature, inspired by
[
BBGZ13
]
in the complex case. It has the advantage of being able to deal with more general measures
\mu
, but the drawback of providing less regularity information on the solution. In fact,
[
BFJ15
]
only gives a continuous solution, and is thus closer in spirit to
[
Koł98
]
than to
[
Yau78
]
.

It is therefore interesting to ask whether we can say more about the regularity of
\varphi
in (MA), at least for special measures
\mu
. In the non-Archimedean setting, there are many possible regularity notions; to describe the one we are focusing on, we first need to make the non-Archimedean version of (MA) more precise, following
[
BFJ15
,
BFJ16
]
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(\varpi)\!)
of dimension
n
. Consider a simple normal crossing (snc) model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
, over the valuation ring
K^{\circ}=\mathbb{C}[\![\varpi]\!]
. The dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
embeds in the Berkovich analytification
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, and there is a continuous retraction
p_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.

A semipositive closed (1,1)-form on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
in the sense of
loc. cit.
is represented by a nef relative numerical class
\omega\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(\mathcal{X}/\Spec K^{\circ})
for some snc model
\mathcal{X}
. We assume that the image
[\omega]
of
\omega
in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
is ample. In this case, there is a natural space
\CPSH(\omega)=\CPSH(X,\omega)
of continuous
\omega
-plurisubharmonic (psh) functions, and a Monge–Ampère operator taking a function
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)

to a positive Radon measure
\varphi\to(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
of mass
[\omega]^{n}
; see also
[
CLD12
]
for a local theory. When
[\omega]
is rational, so that
[\omega]=c_{1}(L)
for an ample (
\mathbb{Q}
-)line bundle
L
on
X
, we can view any
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
as a semipositive metric on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
, with curvature measure
(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}
.

As in
[
BFJ15
]
, let us normalize the Monge–Ampère operator and write

\MA_{\omega}(\varphi):=\tfrac{1}{[\omega]^{n}}(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}.

The main result in
[
BFJ15
]
is that if
\mu
is a Radon probability measure on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
supported in some dual complex, then there exists
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
, unique up to an additive real constant, such that
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mu
. More precisely, this was proved assuming that
X
is defined over an algebraic curve, an assumption that was later removed in
[
BGJKM20
]
. Here we want to study whether for special measures
\mu
, the solution is regular in some sense.

We first consider the class of
piecewise linear
(PL) functions. A function
\varphi\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is (
\mathbb{Q}
-)PL if it is associated to a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on some snc model, and PL functions are also known as
model functions
. The set
\PL(X)
of PL functions is a dense
\mathbb{Q}
-linear subspace of
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
, and it is closed under taking finite maxima and minima.

If
\varphi\in\PL(X)\cap\CPSH(\omega)
, then the measure
\mu=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
is a rational divisorial measure, i.e. a rational convex combination of Dirac masses at divisorial valuations. For example, when
[\omega]=c_{1}(L)
is rational, then the space
\PL(X)\cap\CPSH(\omega)
can be identified with the space of semipositive
model metrics
on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
, represented by a nef model
\mathcal{L}
of
L
, and
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
can be computed in terms of intersection numbers of
\mathcal{L}
.

Assuming
\omega
rational, one may ask whether, conversely, the solution to
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mu
, with
\mu
a rational divisorial measure, is necessarily PL.
Here we focus on the case when
\mu=\delta_{w}
is a Dirac measure, where
w\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
is a divisorial valuation. In this case, it was proved in
[
BFJ15
]
that the solution
\varphi_{w}\in\CPSH(\omega)
to the Monge–Ampère equation

(
\star
)

\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{w})=\delta_{w},\quad\varphi_{w}(w)=0

is the
Green’s function
of
w
, given by
\varphi_{w}=\sup\{\psi\in\CPSH(\omega)\mid\psi(w)\leq 0\}
.

Theorem A
.

Assume that
\omega
is a rational semipositive closed (1,1)-form with
[\omega]
ample, and that
w\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
is a divisorial valuation.
Let
\varphi_{w}\in\CPSH(\omega)
be the Green’s function satisfying (
\star
) above. Then:

(i)

in dimension 1,
\varphi_{w}\in\PL(X)
;

(ii)

in dimension
\geq 2
, it may happen that
\varphi_{w}\not\in\PL(X)
.

Writing
[\omega]=c_{1}(L)
, Theorem A says that the metric on
L^{\mathrm{an}}
corresponding to
\varphi_{w}
is a model metric in dimension 1, but not necessarily in dimension 2 and higher. This answers a question in
[
BFJ15
]
, see Remark 8.8 in
loc. cit.

Here (i) is well known, for example from the work of Thuillier
[
Thu05
]
; we give a proof in §
8.5
. As for (ii), we present one example where
X
is an abelian surface, and another one where
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
. See Examples
9.11
and
9.12
.

We will discuss the structure of these examples shortly, but mention here that they are both
\mathbb{R}
-PL
, i.e. they belong to the space the smallest
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace
\RPL(X)
of
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
containing
\PL(X)
and stable under max and min.
The question then arises whether
also in higher dimension, the solution
\varphi_{w}
to (
\star
) is
\mathbb{R}
-PL for any divisorial valuation
v
. While we don’t have a counterexample to this exact question (with
\omega
rational, but see Example
7.6
), we prove that the situation can be quite complicated in dimension three and higher.

Namely, let us say that a function
\varphi\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is
invariant under retraction
if
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
for some (and hence any sufficiently high) snc model
\mathcal{X}
. For example, a function in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is
\mathbb{R}
-PL iff it is invariant under retraction and its restriction to any dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is
\mathbb{R}
-PL in the sense that it is affine on the cells of some subdivision of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
into real simplices.

If
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
is invariant under retraction, say
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
, then the Monge–Ampère measure
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
is supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.
However, if
\mu
is supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, then the solution
\varphi
to
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)=\mu
may not satisfy
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
, see
[
GJKM19
, Appendix A]
. Still, one may ask whether
\varphi
is invariant under retraction, that is,
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
for any sufficiently high snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, see Question 2 in
loc. cit.
. A version of this question (see Remark
8.8
) in the context of Calabi–Yau varieties plays a key role in the recent work of Yang Li
[
Li20
]
, see also
[
HJMM22
,
Li23
,
AH23
]
. Our next result provides a negative answer in general.

Theorem B
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{K}
, with
K=\mathbb{C}(\!(\varpi)\!)
, and let
\omega
be the closed (1,1)-form associated to the numerical class of
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{K^{\circ}}
. Then there exists
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
such that
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
has finite support in some dual complex, but
\varphi
is not invariant under retraction. In particular,
\varphi\not\in\RPL(X)
.

Let us now say more about the examples underlying Theorem B and Theorem A (ii). They all arise in the
isotrivial case
, when the variety
X
over
K
is the base change of a smooth projective variety
Y
over
\mathbb{C}
, and the
(1,1)
-form is defined by the pullback of an ample numerical class
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(Y)
to the trivial (snc) model
Y_{K^{\circ}}
of
X=Y_{K}
. In this case, we can draw on the global pluripotential theory over a trivially valued field developed in
[
BoJ22b
]
, a theory which interacts well with algebro-geometric notions such as diminished base loci and Zariski decompositions of pseudoeffective classes.

Specifically, given a smooth projective complex variety
Y
, and an ample numerical class
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(Y)
, we have a convex set
\CPSH(\theta)=\CPSH(Y,\theta)\subset\Cz(Y^{\mathrm{an}})
of continuous
\theta
-psh functions, where
Y^{\mathrm{an}}
now denotes the Berkovich analytification of
Y
with respect to the
trivial
absolute value on
\mathbb{C}
. A
divisorial valuation
on
Y
is of the form
v=t\ord_{E}
, where
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and
E\subset Y^{\prime}
is a prime divisor on a smooth projective variety
Y^{\prime}
with a proper birational morphism
Y^{\prime}\to Y
. When instead
t\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, we say that
v
is a
real divisorial valuation
.
If
\Sigma\subset Y^{\mathrm{an}}
is a finite set of real divisorial valuations, then we consider the Green’s function of
\Sigma
, defined as

\varphi_{\Sigma}:=\sup\{\varphi\in\CPSH(Y,\theta)\mid\varphi|_{\Sigma}\leq 0\}.

By
[
BoJ22b
]
,
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\CPSH(Y,\theta)
, and the Monge–Ampère measure of
\varphi_{\Sigma}
is supported in
\Sigma
.

The base change
X=Y_{\mathbb{C}(\!(\varpi)\!)}\to Y
induces a surjective map
\pi\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to Y^{\mathrm{an}}
, and this map admits a canonical section
\sigma\colon Y^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
, called
Gauss extension
, and whose image consists of all
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-invariant points in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
. For any
\varphi\in\CPSH(Y,\theta)
we have
\pi^{\star}\varphi\in\CPSH(X,\omega)
, and

\MA_{\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)=\sigma_{\star}\MA_{\theta}(\varphi).

In particular, if
v\in Y^{\mathrm{div}}
, then
\pi^{\star}\varphi_{\{v\}}
is the Green function for
w:=\sigma(v)\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. As both
\pi^{\star}
and
\sigma^{\star}
preserve the classes of
\mathbb{Q}
-PL and
\mathbb{R}
-PL functions, we see that in order to prove Theorem A (ii), it suffices to find a surface
Y
and
v\in Y^{\mathrm{div}}
, such that
\varphi_{v}:=\varphi_{\{v\}}
is not
\mathbb{Q}
-PL.

Further, to prove Theorem B, it suffices to find a finite set
\Sigma
of real divisorial valuations on
Y
such that
\pi^{\star}\varphi_{\Sigma}
fails to be invariant under retraction. Indeed, the Gauss extension map
\sigma
takes real divisorial valuations to Abhyankar valuations, and these are exactly the ones that lie in a dual complex. We then use the following criterion. Define the
center
of any function
\varphi\in\PSH(Y,\theta)
by

Z_{Y}(\varphi):=c_{Y}\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\},

where
c_{Y}\colon Y^{\mathrm{an}}\to Y
is the center map, see §
3
. We show that if
\pi^{\star}\varphi
is invariant under retraction, then
Z_{Y}(\varphi)\subset Y
is a strict Zariski closed subset, see Corollary
9.9
.
It therefore suffices to find a Green’s function
\varphi_{\Sigma}
whose center is Zariski dense.

Our analysis of the Green’s functions
\varphi_{\Sigma}
is based on a relation between
\theta
-psh functions and families of b-divisors. Namely, we can pick a strict birational morphism
\rho\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
, with
Y^{\prime}
smooth, prime divisors
E_{i}\subset Y^{\prime}
, and
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\Sigma=\{c_{i}^{-1}\ord_{E_{i}}\}
. If we set
D:=\sum_{i}c_{i}^{-1}E_{i}
, then we can express
\varphi_{\Sigma}
in terms of the
b
-divisorial Zariski decomposition
of the numerical class
\rho^{\star}\theta-\lambda[D]
, for
\lambda\in(-\infty,\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}]
, where
\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\in\mathbb{R}
is the largest
\lambda
such that this class is pseudoeffective (psef), see Theorem
6.7
. The analysis of the Zariski decomposition of a psef class
\theta
in terms of
\theta
-psh functions is of independent interest.

Let us first consider the case of dimension two. The Zariski decomposition of
\rho^{\star}\theta-\lambda D
then depends in an
\mathbb{R}
-PL way on
\lambda
, and this implies that the Green’s function
\varphi_{\Sigma}
is
\mathbb{R}
-PL. On the other hand,
\varphi_{\Sigma}
need not be
\mathbb{Q}
-PL. In fact, we prove in Theorem
6.10
that
\varphi_{\Sigma}
is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL iff the quantity

\te(\Sigma):=\sup\varphi_{\Sigma}

is a rational number.
To prove Theorem A (ii), it therefore suffices to find a divisorial valuation
v
on a surface
Y
such that
\te(v)
is irrational, and such examples can be found with
Y
an abelian surface, and
v=\ord_{E}
for a prime divisor
E
on
Y
.

Using a geometric construction by Cutkosky
[
Cut00
]
, we also give an example of a divisorial valuation
v
on
Y=\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
\varphi_{v}
is
\mathbb{R}
-PL but not
\mathbb{Q}
-PL for
\theta=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))
, see Example
7.4
. Being
\mathbb{R}
-PL, this example is invariant under retraction. As explained above, in order to prove Theorem B, it suffices to find
\Sigma
such that
c_{Y}(\varphi_{\Sigma})
is a Zariski dense subset of
Y
. Using the notation above, we show that the center contains the image on
Y
of the diminished base locus of the pseudoeffective class
\rho^{\star}\theta-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}[D]
on
Y^{\prime}
. We can then use a construction of Lesieutre
[
Les14
]
, who showed that if
Y=\mathbb{P}^{3}
,
\theta=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))
, and
\rho\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
is the blowup at nine very general points, then there exists an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
Y^{\prime}
supported on the exceptional locus on
\rho
, such that the diminished base locus of
\rho^{\star}\theta-D
is Zariski dense. If we write
D=\sum_{i=1}^{9}c_{i}E_{i}
, then we can take
\Sigma=\{c_{i}^{-1}\ord_{E_{i}}\}
.

Structure of the paper

The article is organized as follows. In §
1
we recall some facts from birational geometry and pluripotential theory over a trivially valued field. This is used in §
2
to relate
\theta
-psh functions and suitable families of
b
-divisors, after which we study the center of a
\theta
-psh function in §
3
. In §
4
we define the extremal function
V_{\theta}\in\PSH(\theta)
associated to a psef class: by evaluating this function at divisorial valuations we recover the minimal vanishing order of
\theta
along a valuation. The extremal function is also closely related to various notions of Zariski decomposition of a psef class, as explored in §
5
. After all this, we are finally ready to study Green’s functions in §
6
and §
7
. Finally, in §
8
and §
9
we turn to the discretely valued case and prove Theorems A and B.

Notation and conventions

A
variety
over a field
F
is a geometrically integral
F
-scheme of finite type.
We use the abbreviations

usc
for ‘upper semicontinuous’,

lsc
for ‘lower semicontinuous’,
and
iff
for ‘if and only if’.

1.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper—except in §
8
—
X
denotes a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
.

1.1.
Positivity of numerical classes and base loci

We denote by
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
the (finite dimensional) vector space of numerical equivalence classes
\theta=[D]
of
\mathbb{R}
-divisors
D
on
X
. It contains the following convex cones, corresponding to various positivity notions for numerical classes:

•

the
pseudoeffective cone

\Psef(X)
, defined as the closed closed cone generated by all classes of effective divisors;

•

the
big cone

\Bg(X)
, the interior of
\Psef(X)
;

•

the
nef cone

\Nef(X)
, equal to the closed convex cone generated by all classes of basepoint free line bundles;

•

the
ample cone

\Amp(X)
, the interior of
\Nef(X)
;

•

the
movable cone

\Mov(X)
, the closed convex cone generated by all classes of line bundles whose base locus have codimension
2
.

These cones satisfy

\Nef(X)\subset\Mov(X)\subset\Psef(X),

where the first (resp. second) inclusion is an equality when
\dim X\leq 2
(resp.
\dim X\leq 1
), but is in general strict for
\dim X>2
(resp.
\dim X>1
). We will make use of the following simple property:

Lemma 1.1
.

If
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
is movable, then
\theta|_{E}\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(E)
is pseudoeffective for any prime divisor
E\subset X
.

The
asymptotic base locus

\mathbb{B}(D)\subset X
of a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
is defined as the base locus of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(mD)
for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
sufficiently divisible. The
diminished
(or
restricted
)
base locus
and the
augmented base locus
of an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
are respectively defined as

\mathbb{B}_{-}(D):=\bigcup_{A}\mathbb{B}(D+A)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{B}_{+}(D):=\bigcap_{A}\mathbb{B}(D-A),

where
A
ranges over all ample
\mathbb{R}
-divisors such that
D-A
(resp.
D+A
) is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor. Since ampleness is a numerical property, these loci only depend on the numerical class
\theta=[D]\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, and will be denoted by

\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)\subset\mathbb{B}_{+}(\theta)
.

The augmented base locus
\mathbb{B}_{+}(\theta)
is Zariski closed, and satisfies

\theta\in\Bg(X)\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{+}(\theta)\neq X\quad\text{and}\quad\theta\in\Amp(X)\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{+}(\theta)=\emptyset.

The diminished base locus satisfies

(1.1)

\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}}\mathbb{B}_{+}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)

for any
\omega\in\Amp(X)
. It is thus an at most countable union of subvarieties, which is not Zariski closed in general, and can even be Zariski dense (see
[
Les14
]
). We further have

\displaystyle\theta\in\Psef(X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)\neq X;

\displaystyle\theta\in\Nef(X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)=\emptyset;

\displaystyle\theta\in\Mov(X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\codim\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)\geq 2.

1.2.
The Berkovich space

We use
[
BoJ22b
, §1]
as a reference. The
Berkovich space

X^{\mathrm{an}}
is defined as the Berkovich analytification of
X
with respect to the trivial absolute value on
k

[
Berk90
]
. We view it as a compact (Hausdorff) topological space, whose points are
semivaluations
, i.e. valuations
v\colon k(Y)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
for some subvariety
Y\subset X
. We denote by
v_{Y,\mathrm{triv}}\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
the trivial valuation on
k(Y)
, and set
v_{\mathrm{triv}}=v_{X,\mathrm{triv}}
. These trivial semivaluations are precisely the fixed points of the scaling action
\mathbb{R}_{>0}\times X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
given by
(t,v)\mapsto tv
.

We denote
X^{\mathrm{div}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
the (dense) subset of
divisorial valuations
, of the form
v=t\ord_{E}
with
t\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and
E
a prime divisor on a birational model
\pi\colon Y\to X
(the case
t=0
corresponding to
v=v_{\mathrm{triv}}
, by convention). In the present work, where
\mathbb{R}
-divisors arise naturally, it will be convenient to allow
t
to be real, in which case we will say that
v=t\ord_{E}
is a
real divisorial valuation
. We denote by

X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}_{>0}X^{\mathrm{div}}

the set of real divisorial valuations. It is contained in the space
X^{\mathrm{lin}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
of
valuations of linear growth
(see
[
BKMS15
]
and
[
BoJ22b
, §1.5]
).

1.3.
Rational and real piecewise linear functions

In
[
BoJ22b
]
, various classes of
\mathbb{Q}
-PL functions on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
were introduced, and the purpose of what follows is to discuss their
\mathbb{R}
-PL counterparts. First, any ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
defines a homogeneous function

\log|\mathfrak{b}|\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to[-\infty,0]

such that
\log|\mathfrak{b}|(v):=-v(\mathfrak{b})
for
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
.

Second, any
flag ideal

\mathfrak{a}
, i.e. a coherent fractional ideal sheaf on
X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
invariant under the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
and trivial on
X\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
, defines a continuous function

\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}

given by

\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}(v)=-\sigma(v)(\mathfrak{a})
, where
\sigma\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to(X\times\mathbb{A}^{1})^{\mathrm{an}}
is the
Gauss extension
. Concretely, we can write
\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\varpi^{-\lambda}
for a decreasing sequence of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
such that
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
for
\lambda\ll 0
and
\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}=0
for
\lambda\gg 0
, and then
\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}=\max_{\lambda}(\log|\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}|+\lambda)
.

We then denote by:

•

\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
the set of
\mathbb{Q}_{+}
-linear combinations of functions of the form
\log|\mathfrak{b}|
with
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
a nonzero ideal;

•

\PL^{+}(X)
the set of functions
\varphi\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
of the form
\varphi=\max_{i}\{\psi_{i}+\lambda_{i}\}
for a finite family
\psi_{i}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
; equivalently, functions of the form
\varphi=\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}
for a flag ideal
\mathfrak{a}
and

;

•

\PL(X)
the set of differences of functions in
\PL^{+}(X)
, called
rational piecewise linear functions
(
\mathbb{Q}
-PL functions
for short).

The sets
\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
are stable under addition and max, while
\PL(X)
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, stable under max, and is dense in
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
.

As in
[
BoJ22b
, §3.1]
, we denote by
\PL(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by
\PL(X)
. It is not stable under max anymore; to remedy this, we further introduce:

•

the set
\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-linear combinations of functions in
\PL^{+}(X)
;

•

the set
\RPL^{+}(X)
of finite maxima of functions in
\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
;
e

•

the set
\RPL(X)
of differences of functions in
\RPL^{+}(X)
; we call its elements
real piecewise linear functions
(
\mathbb{R}
-PL functions
for short).

As one immediately sees, the sets
\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\RPL^{+}(X)
are convex cones in
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
, and
\RPL(X)
is thus an
\mathbb{R}
-vector space. Further,
\RPL^{+}(X)
, and hence
\RPL(X)
, are clearly stable under max. Thus
\RPL(X)
is the smallest
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace of
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
that is stable under max and contains
\PL(X)
.

Finally, introduce the convex cone
\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-linear combinations of functions in
\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
(this is again not stable under max anymore). We then have:

Lemma 1.2
.

A function
\varphi\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
lies in
\RPL^{+}(X)
iff
\varphi=\max_{i}\{\psi_{i}+\lambda_{i}\}
for a finite family
\psi_{i}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Since any function in
\RPL^{+}(X)
is a finite max of functions
\varphi\in\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, it suffices to show that
\varphi
is of the desired form. Write
\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\varphi_{i}
with
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\varphi_{i}\in\PL^{+}(X)
, i.e.
\varphi_{i}=\max_{j}\{\psi_{ij}+\lambda_{ij}\}
with
\psi_{ij}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
and
\lambda_{ij}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then

\varphi=\max_{j_{1},\dots,j_{r}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}\left(\psi_{ij_{i}}+\lambda_{ij_{i}}\right).

Since each
\sum_{i}t_{i}\psi_{ij_{i}}
lies in
\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, this shows that
\varphi
is of the desired form.

Conversely, assume
\varphi=\max_{i}\{\psi_{i}+\lambda_{i}\}
for a finite family
\psi_{i}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. For each
i
, write
\psi_{i}=\sum_{j}t_{ij}\psi_{ij}
with
\psi_{ij}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)\leq 0
. Pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
and
i
such that
\varphi(v)=\psi_{i}(v)+\lambda_{i}
. Since
\varphi
is bounded, we can find
c\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
\psi_{ij}(v)\geq c
for all
j
. This shows that
\varphi=\max_{i}\varphi_{i}
with
\varphi_{i}:=\sum_{j}t_{ij}\max\{\psi_{ij},c\}+\lambda_{i}
. For all
i,j
,
\max\{\psi_{ij},c\}
lies in
\PL^{+}(X)
, thus
\varphi_{i}\in\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, and hence
\varphi\in\RPL^{+}(X)
.
∎

-divisors

We use
[

, §1]
and
[
BoJ22b
, §6.4]

•

(real)

\mathbb{R}

are

a
b

of

for all higher birational models

Example 1.3
.

Any
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

-Cartier
b
-divisor
\overline{D}\in\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}

-Cartier

For any

and

as follows: pick a prime divisor

This is independent of the choices made, and the function

is homogeneous (with respect to the scaling action of

Definition 1.4
.

We say that a homogeneous function

is of
divisorial type

\psi(\ord_{E})=0
for all but finitely many prime divisors

The next result is straightforward:

Lemma 1.5
.

The map
B\mapsto\psi_{B}
sets up a vector space isomorphism between
\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and the space of homogeneous functions of divisorial type on
X^{\mathrm{div}}
. Moreover,
B\in\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is effective iff
\psi_{B}\geq 0
.

We endow
\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
with the topology of pointwise convergence on
X^{\mathrm{div}}
. If
\Omega
is a topological space, then a map
f\colon\Omega\to\Zunb(X)
is thus continuous iff
v\circ f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous for all
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
.
We will also say that
f\colon\Omega\to\Zunb(X)
is lsc (resp. usc) iff
v\circ f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is lsc (resp. usc) for all
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
.

If
\Omega
is a convex subset of a real vector space, then we say that
f\colon\Omega\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is convex if
v\circ f
is
is convex for all
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
.
This amounts to
f((1-t)x_{0}+tx_{1})\leq(1-t)f(x_{0})+tf(x_{1})
for
x_{0},x_{1}\in\Omega
,
0\leq t\leq 1
.
We say that
f
is concave if
-f
is convex.

Finally, if
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
is an interval, then
f\colon\Omega\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is increasing (resp. decreasing) if
v\circ f
is increasing (resp. decreasing) for each
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
.

Next we will generalize
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 6.32]
to real coefficients.

Definition 1.6
.

We denote by
\Carbp(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
the convex cone of divisors
B\in\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
that are antieffective and relatively semiample over
X
.

Proposition 1.7
.

The map
B\mapsto\psi_{B}
induces an isomorphism between
\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space generated by (the restrictions to

of) all functions
\log|\mathfrak{b}|
with
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
a nonzero ideal. This isomorphism restricts to a bijection

\Carbp(X)_{\mathbb{R}}\overset{\sim}{\to}\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

The first point is a consequence of
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 6.32]
, which also yields

\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}\overset{\sim}{\to}\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X).

Since the right-hand side generates the convex cone
\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, it suffices to show that the convex cone of antieffective and relatively semiample divisors in
\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is generated by antieffective and semiample divisors in
\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. By definition of a relatively semiample
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier
b
-divisor, we have
B=\sum_{i}t_{i}B_{i}
with
t_{i}>0
and

relatively semiample. By the Negativity Lemma (see
[
BdFF12
, Proposition 2.12]
),

is antieffective, and still relatively semiample. Denoting by

the irreducible decomposition of the antieffective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

, we infer

where

∎

The space of

Any

One should be wary of the fact this map

Lemma 1.8

For any finite set
\mathcal{E}
of prime divisors on

For any model

We work with a fixed numerical class

Recall that a function

is uniquely determined by its values on

\psi
is of divisorial type if its restriction to

for all but finitely many prime divisors

Slightly generalizing
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 6.40]
, we show:

Proposition 2.1
.

The map
B\mapsto\psi_{B}
in §
1.4
sets up a 1–1 correspondence between:

(i)

the set of

-divisors

such that

and

(ii)

the set of
\theta

Proof.

Pick
B
as in (i). On the one hand,
\psi_{\overline{B_{X}}}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(-B_{X})
, see Example
1.13
. On the other hand, since
\overline{\theta}+[B]=\overline{(\theta+[B_{X}])}+([B]-\overline{[B_{X}]})

[

, Theorem 6.40]
that
\psi_{B-\overline{B_{X}}}=\psi_{B}-\psi_{\overline{B_{X}}}

\psi_{B}\in\PSH(\theta+B_{X})+\PSH(-B_{X})\subset\PSH(\theta).

Conversely, pick
\psi
as in (ii), so that

with

. By
[
BoJ22b
, Corollary 6.17]
, we can write

as the pointwise limit of a decreasing net

such that

with

. Then

for a Cartier

-divisor

such that

is semiample (see
[

, Lemma 6.34]
), and hence

0\geq-\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}([D])
.

Proof.

If
\psi+\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta-D)
, then
\psi\leq-\psi_{D}
, and hence
\max_{\Gamma}\psi\leq-1
, since
\psi_{D}\equiv 1
on
\Gamma_{D}
. Conversely, assume
\max_{\Gamma_{D}}\psi\leq-1
. Consider first the case where
\theta=c_{1}(L)
for a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L)
. For any
m
sufficiently divisible we thus have
\psi=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|
for a finite set of nonzero section
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X,mL)
. Using the notation of Definition
2.2
, we get for all
i
and all
\alpha

c_{\alpha}^{-1}\ord_{E_{\alpha}}(s_{i})=-\log|s_{i}|(v_{\alpha})\geq m,

and hence
\ord_{E_{\alpha}}(s_{i})\geq\lceil mc_{\alpha}\rceil
. This implies
s_{i}=s_{i}^{\prime}s_{D_{m}}
for some
s_{i}^{\prime}\in\Hnot(X,m(L-D_{m}))
, where

D_{m}:=m^{-1}\lceil mD\rceil=\sum_{\alpha}m^{-1}\lceil mc_{\alpha}\rceil E_{\alpha}

and
s_{D_{m}}\in\Hnot(X,D_{m})
is the canonical section. Since
\psi_{D_{m}}=-\log|s_{D_{m}}|
, we infer

\psi+\psi_{D_{m}}=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\log|s^{\prime}_{i}|\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L-D_{m})\subset\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(L-D_{m}).

When
m\to\infty
,
\psi_{D_{m}}
decreases to
\psi_{D}
, and
[D_{m}]\to[D]
in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, and we infer
\psi+\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(L-D)
.

In the general case,
\psi
can be written as the pointwise limit of a decreasing net
\psi_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L_{i})
, where
L_{i}\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
satisfies that
c_{1}(L_{i})-\theta
is nef and tends to
0
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Corollary 6.17]
). Pick
t\in(0,1)
. For all
i
large enough and all
\alpha
, we then have
c_{\alpha}^{-1}\psi_{i}(\ord_{E_{\alpha}})\leq-t
, and hence

\psi_{i}+t\psi_{D}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L_{i}-tD)\subset\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(L_{i}-tD)

by the previous step of the proof. Since
\psi_{i}+t\psi_{D}
decreases to
\psi+t\psi_{D}
and
L_{i}-tD\to\theta-tD
in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, we infer
\psi+t\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta-tD)
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 4.5]
). Pick any
\omega\in\Amp(X)
. Then
\psi+t\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta-D+\omega)
for all
t\in(0,1)
close to
1
, so by the envelope property (see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 5.11]
), we get
\psi+\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta-D+\omega)
. As this is true for all
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, we conclude
\psi+\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta-D)
(again see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 4.5]
).
∎

2.3.
Psh functions and families of b-divisors

We now extend Proposition
2.1
to general
\theta
-psh functions. We say that
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
is of divisorial type if the homogeneous psh function
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
is of divisorial type, see §
1.7
. By (
1.5
), this is equivalent to
\varphi(\ord_{E})=\sup\varphi
for all but finitely many prime divisors
E\subset X
.

Theorem 2.4
.

There is a 1–1 correspondence between:

(i)

the set of
\theta
-psh functions
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
of divisorial type;

(ii)

the set of continuous, concave, decreasing families
(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}
of
b
-divisors, for some
\lambda_{\max}\in\mathbb{R}
, such that
B_{\lambda}\leq 0
and
\overline{\theta}+[B_{\lambda}]\in\Numb(X)
is nef for all
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}
.

The correspondence is given by

(2.1)

\varphi=\sup_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}\{\psi_{B_{\lambda}}+\lambda\},\quad\psi_{B_{\lambda}}=\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}.

In particular, we have
\lambda_{\max}=\sup\varphi
and
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}=B_{\lambda_{\max}}
.

Proof.

Pick a family
(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}
as in (ii). By Proposition
2.1
, setting
\psi_{\lambda}:=\psi_{B_{\lambda}}
defines a continuous, concave and decreasing family
(\psi_{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}
in
\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
. Since
\theta
has the envelope property, the usc upper envelope
\varphi:=\mathrm{sup}^{\star}_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}(\psi_{\lambda}+\lambda)
lies in
\PSH(\theta)
. On
X^{\mathrm{div}}
,
\varphi
coincides with
\sup_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\max}}(\psi_{\lambda}+\lambda)
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 5.6]
). By Legendre duality, we further have
\psi_{\lambda}=\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}
for
\lambda<\lambda_{\max}
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 6.24]
, and hence also for
\lambda=\lambda_{\max}
, by continuity of both sides on
(-\infty,\lambda_{\max}]
.

Conversely, pick
\varphi
as in (i), so that
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
is of divisorial type. For each
\lambda\leq\sup\varphi
we then have
0\geq\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}\geq\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}
, which shows that
\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
is also of divisorial type. By Proposition
2.1
, we thus have
\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}=\psi_{B_{\lambda}}
for a
b
-divisor
B_{\lambda}\leq 0
such that
\overline{\theta}+[B_{\lambda}]
is nef, and the family
(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\sup\varphi}
is concave, decreasing and continuous, since so is
(\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\sup\varphi}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Not every
\theta
-psh function is of divisorial type. For example, assume
\dim X=1
, and pick a sequence
(p_{j})
of closed points on
X
, with corresponding ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{j}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and a sequence
\varepsilon_{j}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that
\sum_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\leq\deg\theta
. Then
\varphi:=\sum_{j}\varepsilon_{j}\log|\mathfrak{m}_{j}|\in\PSH(\theta)
, and
-c_{j}=\varphi(\ord_{p_{j}})<\sup\varphi=0
for all
j
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Example 4.13]
).

3.
The center of a
\theta
-psh function

In this section we introduce the notion of the center of a
\theta
-psh function. This is a subset of
X
defined in terms of the locus on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
where
\varphi
is smaller than its maximum.

3.1.
The center map

For any
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
, we denote by
c_{X}(v)\in X
its center, and by

Z_{X}(v):=\overline{\{c_{X}(v)\}}\subset X

the corresponding subvariety.
The center map
c_{X}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X
is surjective and anticontinuous, i.e. the preimage of a closed subset is open. In particular, any subvariety
Z\subset X
is of the form
Z=Z_{X}(v)
for some
v
; we can simply take
v=\ord_{Z}
.

More generally, for any subset
S\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
we set

(3.1)

Z_{X}(S):=\bigcup_{v\in S}Z_{X}(v).

This is smallest subset of
X
that contains
c_{X}(S)
and is closed under specialization.

3.2.
The center of a
\theta
-psh function

We can now introduce

Definition 3.1
.

We define the
center
on
X
of any
\theta
-psh function
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
as

Z_{X}(\varphi):=Z_{X}(\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}).

Example 3.2
.

For any nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, the function
\psi=\log|\mathfrak{b}|
is
\theta
-psh if
\theta
is sufficiently ample, and then
Z_{X}(\varphi)=V(\mathfrak{b})
. More generally, if
\varphi=\sum_{i}t_{i}\log|\mathfrak{b}_{i}|
with
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{i}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
a nonzero ideal, then
Z_{X}(\varphi)=\bigcup_{i}V(\mathfrak{b}_{i})
.

Recall that to any
\theta
-psh function
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
we can associate a homogeneous
\theta
-psh function
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
, see §
1.7
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
we have
\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}=\{\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}<0\}
. As a consequence,
Z_{X}(\varphi)=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})
. Moreover, the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is of divisorial type;

(ii)

\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}
is of divisorial type;

(iii)

Z_{X}(\varphi)=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})
contains at most finitely many prime divisors
E\subset X
.

Proof.

Pick any
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
. By (
1.5
) and the fact that
t\mapsto\varphi(tv)
is decreasing and convex, it follows that
\varphi(v)<\sup\varphi
iff
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}(v)<0
. Thus
Z_{X}(\varphi)=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})
since
\sup\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}=0
.

Now the equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) is definitional, and (ii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii) is clear since a prime divisor
E\subset X
belongs to
Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})
iff
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}(\ord_{E})<0
.
∎

Together with Example
1.14
, Lemma
3.3
implies

Corollary 3.4
.

If
\varphi=\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}
for a flag ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\varpi^{-\lambda}
on
X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
, then
Z_{X}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}})=V(\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda_{\max}})
, where
\lambda_{\max}:=\max\{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mid\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

For any
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
, the center
Z_{X}(\varphi)
is a strict subset of
X
, and an at most countable union of (strict) subvarieties. Moreover, we have

c_{X}^{-1}(Z_{X}(\varphi))=\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}
.

Proof.

Note that
Z_{X}(\varphi)
does not contain the generic point of
X
, so
Z_{X}(\varphi)\neq X
. Also note that by Lemma
3.3
we may assume that
\varphi
is homogeneous.

If
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L)
for a
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
L
, so that
\varphi=\tfrac{1}{m}\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|
for a finite set of nonzero sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X,mL)
, then
Z_{X}(\varphi)=\bigcap_{i}(s_{i}=0)
, which is Zariski closed. In general,
\varphi
can be written as the limit of a decreasing sequence
\varphi_{m}\in\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{hom}}(L_{m})
with
L_{m}\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
c_{1}(L_{m})\to\theta
(see
[
BoJ22b
, Remark 6.18]
). For any
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
we then have

c_{X}(v)\in Z_{X}(\varphi)\Leftrightarrow\varphi(v)<0\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{m}(v)<0\ \text{for some $m$},

i.e.
Z_{X}(\varphi)=\bigcup_{m}Z_{X}(\varphi_{m})
, an at most countable union of strict subvarieties.

Next pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
, and set
Z=Z_{X}(v)
. By
[
BoJ22b
, Proposition 4.12]
,
\varphi(tv)=t\varphi(v)
converges to
\varphi(v_{Z,\mathrm{triv}})=\sup_{Z^{\mathrm{an}}}\varphi
as
t\to+\infty
, and hence
\varphi(v)<0\Leftrightarrow\varphi\equiv-\infty
on
Z^{\mathrm{an}}
. By definition of the center, if
c_{X}(v)
lies in
Z_{X}(\varphi)
, then we can find
w\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
\varphi(w)<0
and
c_{X}(v)\in Z_{X}(w)
, i.e.
Z\subset Z_{X}(w)
. Then
\varphi\equiv-\infty
on
Z_{X}(w)\supset Z
, which yields
\varphi(v)<0
. Conversely, assume
\varphi(v)<0
, and hence
\varphi\equiv-\infty
on
Z^{\mathrm{an}}
. We can find
w\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
such that
Z=Z_{X}(w)
. Since
\varphi\equiv-\infty
on
Z^{\mathrm{an}}=Z_{X}(w)^{\mathrm{an}}
, we get
\varphi(w)<0
, and hence
c_{X}(v)\in Z_{X}(w)\subset Z_{X}(\varphi)
.
∎

For later use we record

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\varphi_{i}\in\PSH(\theta_{i})
,
i=1,2
, then
Z_{X}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})=Z_{X}(\varphi_{1})\cup Z_{X}(\varphi_{2})
.

3.3.
Centers of PL functions

The following result will play a crucial role in what follows.

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)
lies in
\RPL^{+}(X)
(resp.
\RPL(X)
), then
Z_{X}(\varphi)
is Zariski closed (resp. not Zariski dense) in
X
.

Proof.

Assume first
\varphi\in\RPL^{+}(X)
, and write
\varphi=\max_{i}\{\psi_{i}+\lambda_{i}\}
for a finite set
\psi_{i}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. As in Example
1.14
, we then have
\max_{i}\lambda_{i}=\sup\varphi
, and
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}=\max_{\lambda_{i}=\sup\varphi}\psi_{i}
. This shows that

Z_{X}(\varphi)=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})=\bigcap_{\lambda_{i}=\sup\varphi}Z_{X}(\psi_{i})

is Zariski closed (see Example
3.2
). Assume next
\varphi\in\RPL(X)
and write
\varphi=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
with
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. After replacing
\theta
with a sufficiently ample class, we may assume that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are
\theta
-psh. By (
1.5
) we have
\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}^{\max}-\widehat{\varphi}_{2}^{\max}
, and hence

Z_{X}(\varphi)=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max})\subset Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}_{1})\cup Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}_{2})=Z_{X}(\varphi_{1})\cup Z_{X}(\varphi_{2}),

which cannot be Zariski dense, since
Z_{X}(\varphi_{1})
and
Z_{X}(\varphi_{2})
are both Zariski closed strict subsets by the first part of the proof.
∎

4.
Extremal functions and minimal vanishing orders

Next we define a trivially valued analogue of an important construction in the complex analytic case.

4.1.
Extremal functions

For any
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, we define the
extremal function

V_{\theta}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to[-\infty,0]
as the pointwise envelope

(4.1)

V_{\theta}:=\sup\left\{\varphi\in\PSH(\theta)\mid\varphi\leq 0\right\}.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
we have

\displaystyle\theta\in\Psef(X)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow V_{\theta}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta);

\displaystyle\theta\notin\Psef(X)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow V_{\theta}\equiv-\infty;

\displaystyle\theta\in\Nef(X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow V_{\theta}\equiv 0.

In particular,
\PSH(\theta)
is nonempty iff
\theta
is pseudoeffective. For any
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, we further have

(4.2)

V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}\searrow V_{\theta}\text{ as }\varepsilon\searrow 0.

Proof.

Since the action
(t,\varphi)\mapsto t\cdot\varphi
of
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
preserves the set of candidate functions
\varphi
in (
4.1
),
V_{\theta}
is necessarily fixed by the action, and hence homogeneous. If
\theta
is not psef, then
\PSH(\theta)
is empty (see Lemma
1.12
), and hence
V_{\theta}\equiv-\infty
. By Lemma
1.12
, we also have
V_{\theta}\equiv 0
iff
\theta
is nef.

Next, assume
\theta\in\Bg(X)
. Then
\PSH(\theta)
is non-empty (see Lemma
1.12
), and the envelope property implies that
V_{\theta}^{\star}
is
\theta
-psh and nonpositive. It is thus a candidate in (
4.1
), and hence
V_{\theta}^{\star}\leq V_{\theta}
, which shows that
V_{\theta}^{\star}=V_{\theta}
is
\theta
-psh.

Assume now
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, and pick
\omega\in\Amp(X)
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, the previous step yields
V_{\varepsilon}:=V_{\theta+\varepsilon\omega}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)
. For
0<\varepsilon<\delta
we further have
\PSH(\theta)\subset\PSH(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)\subset\PSH(\theta+\delta\omega)
, and hence
V_{\delta}\geq V_{\varepsilon}\geq V_{\theta}
. Set
V:=\lim_{\varepsilon}V_{\varepsilon}
. For any
\delta>0
fixed, we have
V_{\varepsilon}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta+\delta\omega)
for
\varepsilon\leq\delta
, and
V_{\varepsilon}\searrow V
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Thus
V\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta+\delta\omega)
for all
\delta>0
, and hence
V\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
. Since
V
is a candidate in (
4.1
), we get
V\leq V_{\theta}
, and hence
V_{\theta}=V=\lim_{\varepsilon}V_{\varepsilon}
. This proves that
V_{\theta}
is
\theta
-psh, as well as (
4.2
).
∎

4.2.
Minimal vanishing orders

For
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the function
V_{\theta}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
is uniquely determined by its restriction to
X^{\mathrm{div}}
, where it is furthermore finite valued. For any
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
we set

(4.3)

v(\theta):=-V_{\theta}(v)=\inf\{-\varphi(v)\mid\varphi\in\PSH(\theta),\,\varphi\leq 0\}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Note that

(4.4)

v(\theta)=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}v(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)

for any
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, by (
4.2
). As we next show, these invariants coincide with the minimal/asymptotic vanishing orders studied in
[
Nak04
,
Bou04
,
ELMNP06
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. Then:

(i)

the function
\theta\mapsto v(\theta)
is homogeneous, convex and lsc on
\Psef(X)
; in particular, it is continuous on
\Bg(X)
;

(ii)

for any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
we have

(4.5)

v(\theta)\leq\inf\left\{v(D)\mid D\equiv\theta\text{ effective }\mathbb{R}\text{-divisor}\right\},

and equality holds when
\theta
is big.

Note that equality in (
4.5
) fails in general for
\theta
is not big, as there might not even exist any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
in the class of
\theta
.

Proof.

Using
\PSH(\theta)+\PSH(\theta^{\prime})\subset\PSH(\theta+\theta^{\prime})
and
\PSH(t\theta)=t\PSH(\theta)
for
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\Psef(X)
and
t>0
, it is straightforward to see that
\theta\mapsto v(\theta)
is convex and homogeneous on
\Psef(X)
. Being also finite valued, it is automatically continuous on the interior
\Bg(X)
. For any
\omega\in\Amp(X)
and
\varepsilon>0
,
\theta\mapsto v(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)
is thus continuous on
\Psef(X)
, and (
4.4
) thus shows that
\theta\mapsto v(\theta)
is lsc, which proves (i).

Next pick
\theta\in\Psef(X)
. For each effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D\equiv\theta
, the function
-\psi_{D}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
, see Example
1.13
, is a competitor in (
4.1
). Thus
-v(D)=\psi_{D}(v)\leq V_{\theta}(v)=-v(\theta)
, which proves the first half of (ii). Now assume
\theta
is big, and denote by
v^{\prime}(\theta)
the right-hand side of (
4.5
). Both
v(\theta)
and
v^{\prime}(\theta)
are (finite valued) convex function of
\theta\in\Bg(X)
. They are therefore continuous, and it is thus enough to prove the equality
v(\theta)=v^{\prime}(\theta)
when
\theta=c_{1}(L)
with
L\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
big. To this end, pick an ample
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
A
, and set
\omega:=c_{1}(A)
. By
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 4.15]
, for any
\varepsilon>0
we can find
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}^{\mathrm{gf}}(L+A)
such that
\varphi\geq V_{\theta}
and
\varphi(v_{\mathrm{triv}})=\sup\varphi\leq\varepsilon
. By definition, we have
\varphi=m^{-1}\max_{i}\{\log|s_{i}|+\lambda_{i}\}
with
m
sufficiently divisible and a finite family of nonzero sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(X,m(L+A))
and constants
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then
\max_{i}\lambda_{i}=m\sup\varphi\leq m\varepsilon
, and
m^{-1}v(s_{i})=v(D_{i})
with
D_{i}:=m^{-1}\mathrm{div}(s_{i})\equiv\theta+\omega
, and hence
m^{-1}v(s_{i})\geq v^{\prime}(\theta+\omega)
. Thus

-v(\theta)=V_{\theta}(v)\leq\varphi(v)=m^{-1}\max_{i}\{v(s_{i})+\lambda_{i}\}\leq-v^{\prime}(\theta+\omega)+\varepsilon.

This shows
v^{\prime}(\theta)\geq v(\theta)\geq v^{\prime}(\theta+\omega)
, and hence
v^{\prime}(\theta)=v(\theta)
, since
\lim_{\omega\to 0}v^{\prime}(\theta+\omega)=v^{\prime}(\theta)
by continuity on the big cone.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If
L\in\Pic(X)
is big, then
[
ELMNP06
, Corollary 2.7]
(or, alternatively, a small variant of the above argument) shows that
v(c_{1}(L))
is also equal to the asymptotic vanishing order

\displaystyle v(\|L\|):

\displaystyle=\lim_{m\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{m}\min\left\{v(s)\mid s\in\Hnot(X,mL)\setminus\{0\}\right\}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{v(D)\mid D\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}L\text{ effective }\mathbb{Q}{-divisor}\right\}.

Remark 4.4
.

Continuity of minimal vanishing orders beyond the big cone is a subtle issue. For any
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
, the function
\theta\mapsto v(\theta)
, being convex and lsc on
\Psef(X)
, is automatically continuous on any polyhedral subcone (cf.
[
How88
]
). When
\dim X=2
, it is in fact continuous on the whole of
\Psef(X)
, but this fails in general when
\dim X\geq 3
(see respectively Proposition III.1.19 and Example IV.2.8 in
[
Nak04
]
).

4.3.
The center of an extremal function

The following fact plays a key role in what follows.

Theorem 4.5
.

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the function
V_{\theta}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
is of divisorial type (see Definition
1.4
). Further, its center
Z_{X}(V_{\theta})
coincides with the diminished base locus
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)
(see §
1.1
).

The proof relies on the next result, which corresponds to
[
Nak04
, Corollary III.1.11]
(see also
[
Bou04
, Theorem 3.12]
in the analytic context).

Lemma 4.6
.

Pick
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, and assume
E_{1},\dots,E_{r}\subset X
are prime divisors such that
\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)>0
for all
i
. Then
[E_{1}],\dots,[E_{r}]
are linearly independent in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
. In particular,
r\leq\rho(X)=\dim\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
.

Proof.

We reproduce the simple argument of
[
BDPP13
, Theorem 3.5 (v)]
for the convenience of the reader.
By (
4.4
), after adding to
\theta
a small enough ample class we assume that
\theta
is big. Suppose
\sum_{i}c_{i}[E_{i}]=0
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, so that
G:=\sum_{i}c_{i}E_{i}
is numerically equivalent to
0
, and choose
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
such that
\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)+\varepsilon c_{i}>0
for all
i
.
Pick any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D\equiv\theta
and set
D^{\prime}:=D+\varepsilon G
. Then
D^{\prime}
is effective, since

\ord_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime})=\ord_{E_{i}}(D)+\varepsilon c_{i}\geq\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)+\varepsilon c_{i}>0

for all
i
. Since
G\equiv 0
, we also have
D^{\prime}\equiv\theta
, and (
4.5
) thus yields for each
i

\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)\leq\ord_{E_{i}}(D^{\prime})=\ord_{E_{i}}(D)+\varepsilon c_{i}.

Taking the infimum over
D
we get
\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)\leq\ord_{E_{i}}(\theta)+\varepsilon c_{i}
(see Proposition
4.2
(ii)), i.e.
c_{i}\geq 0
for all
i
. Thus
G\geq 0
, and hence
G=0
, since
G\equiv 0
. This proves
c_{i}=0
for all
i
which shows, as desired, that the
[E_{i}]
are linearly independent.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

By (
4.3
), the first assertion means that there are only finitely many prime divisors
E\subset X
such that
\ord_{E}(\theta)>0
, and is thus a direct consequence of Lemma
4.6
. Pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. The second point is equivalent to
v(\theta)>0\Leftrightarrow c_{X}(v)\in\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)
. When
\theta
is big, this is the content of
[
ELMNP06
, Theorem B]
. In the general case, pick
\omega\in\Amp(X)
. Then
v(\theta)>0
iff
v(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)>0
for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, by (
4.4
), while
c_{X}(v)\in\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)
iff
c_{X}(v)\in\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta+\varepsilon\omega)
for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, by (
1.1
). The result follows.
∎

For later use, we also note:

Lemma 4.7
.

For any polyhedral subcone
C\subset\Psef(X)
, we have:

(i)

\theta\mapsto v(\theta)
is continuous on
C
for all
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
;

(ii)

the set of prime divisors
E\subset X
such that
\ord_{E}(\theta)>0
for some
\theta\in C
is finite.

Proof.

As mentioned in Remark
4.4
, any convex, lsc function on a polyhedral cone is continuous (see
[
How88
]
), and (i) follows. To see (ii), pick a finite set of generators
(\theta_{i})
of
C
. Each
\theta\in C
can be written as
\theta=\sum_{i}t_{i}\theta_{i}
with
t_{i}\geq 0
. By convexity and homogeneity of minimal vanishing orders, this implies
\ord_{E}(\theta)\leq\sum_{i}t_{i}\ord_{E}(\theta_{i})
, so that
\ord_{E}(\theta)>0
implies
\ord_{E}(\theta_{i})>0
for some
i
. The result now follows from Lemma
4.6
.
∎

5.
Zariski decompositions

Next we study the close relationship between the extremal function in §
4
, and the various versions of the Zariski decomposition of a psef numerical class.

5.1.
The
b
-divisorial Zariski decomposition

Pick
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
a psef class.
By Theorem
4.5
, the function
X^{\mathrm{div}}\ni v\mapsto v(\theta)=-V_{\theta}(v)
is of divisorial type. We denote by

\Nz(\theta)\in\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}

the corresponding effective
b
-divisor, which thus satisfies

\psi_{\Nz(\theta)}(v)=v(\Nz(\theta))=v(\theta)=-V_{\theta}(v)

for all
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. This construction is birationally invariant:

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the
b
-divisor class

\env(\theta):=\overline{\theta}-[\Nz(\theta)]\in\Numb(X)

is nef, and
\Nz(\theta)
is the smallest effective
b
-divisor with this property. Moreover,

(5.1)

\Nz(\theta)\geq\overline{\Nz(\theta)_{Y}}

for all birational models
Y\to X
.

We call
\overline{\theta}=\env(\theta)+[\Nz(\theta)]
the
b
-divisorial Zariski decomposition
of
\theta
. At least when
\theta
is big, this construction is basically equivalent to
[
KM13
, Theorem D]
, and to the case
p=1
of
[
BFJ09
, §2.2]
.

Note that the
b
-divisorial Zariski decomposition is birationally invariant:

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
and any birational model
\pi\colon Y\to X
, we have

\Nz(\pi^{\star}\theta)=\Nz(\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad\env(\pi^{\star}\theta)=\env(\theta)

in
\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}=\Zunb(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\Numb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}=\Numb(Y)_{\mathbb{R}}
, respectively.

Proof.

Since
\PSH(\pi^{\star}\theta)=\pi^{\star}\PSH(\theta)
, see (
1.2
), we have
V_{\pi^{\star}\theta}=\pi^{\star}V_{\theta}
, and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Since
\psi_{-\Nz(\theta)}=V_{\theta}
is
\theta
-psh, Proposition
2.1
shows that
\overline{\theta}-[\Nz(\theta)]
is nef, which yields the last point, by the Negativity Lemma (see Lemma
1.11
). Conversely, if
E\in\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is effective with
\overline{\theta}-[E]
nef, then
-\psi_{E}\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\theta)
, again by Proposition
2.1
. Thus
-\psi_{E}\leq V_{\theta}=-\psi_{\Nz(\theta)}
, and hence
E\geq\Nz(\theta)
.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
4.2
, we get

Corollary 5.3
.

The map
\Psef(X)\ni\theta\mapsto\Nz(\theta)\in\Zunb(X)
is homogeneous, lsc, and convex.

5.2.
The divisorial Zariski decomposition

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, we denote by
\Nz_{X}(\theta):=\Nz(\theta)_{X}
the incarnation of
\Nz(\theta)\in\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
on
X
, which thus satisfies

(5.2)

\Nz_{X}(\theta)=\sum_{E\subset X}\ord_{E}(\theta)E

with
E
ranging over all prime divisors of
X
, and
\ord_{E}(\theta)=0
for all but finitely many
E
.

For any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
with numerical class
[D]\in\Psef(X)
, (
4.5
) yields

(5.3)

\Nz_{X}(D):=\Nz_{X}([D])\leq D.

More generally, the following variational characterization holds.

Theorem 5.4
.

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the class

\env_{X}(\theta):=\theta-[\Nz_{X}(\theta)]\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)

is movable, and
\Nz_{X}(\theta)
is the smallest effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
with this property.

Following
[
Bou04
]
, we call the decomposition

\theta=\env_{X}(\theta)+[\Nz_{X}(\theta)]

the
divisorial Zariski decomposition
of
\theta
. It coincides with the
\sigma
-decomposition
of
[
Nak04
]
.

Proof of Theorem
5.4
.

By definition,
\env_{X}(\theta)
is the incarnation on
X
of
\overline{\theta}-[\Nz(\theta)]
. By Theorem
5.1
, the latter class is nef, and
\env_{X}(\theta)
is thus movable, by Lemma
1.10
.

To prove the converse, assume first that
\theta
is movable. We then need to show
\Nz_{X}(\theta)=0
, i.e.
\ord_{E}(\theta)=0
for each
E\subset X
prime (see (
5.2
)). By (
4.5
), this is clear if
\theta=c_{1}(L)
for a big line bundle
L
with base locus of codimension at least
2
. Since the movable cone
\Mov(X)
is generated by the classes of such line bundles, the continuity of
\theta\mapsto\ord_{E}(\theta)
on the big cone yields the result when
\theta
is further big, and the case of an arbitrary movable class follows by (
4.4
).

Finally, consider any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
and any effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
such that
\theta-[D]
is movable. For any
E\subset X
prime we then have
\ord_{E}(\theta-[D])=0
by the previous step, and
\ord_{E}([D])\leq\ord_{E}(D)
by (
5.3
)). Thus

\ord_{E}(\theta)\leq\ord_{E}(\theta-[D])+\ord_{E}(D)=\ord_{E}(D).

This shows
\Nz_{X}(\theta)\leq D
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Theorem
5.4
implies the following converse of Lemma
1.10
: a class
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
is movable iff
\theta=\alpha_{X}
for a nef
b
-divisor class
\alpha\in\Nefb(X)
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Pick
\theta\in\Psef(X)
and a prime divisor
E\subset X
. Then
(\theta-\ord_{E}(\theta)E)|_{E}\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(E)
is pseudoeffective.

Proof.

We have
\theta-\ord_{E}(\theta)[E]=\env_{X}(\theta)+\sum_{F\neq E}\ord_{F}(\theta)[F]
, where
F
ranges over all prime divisors of
X
distinct from
E
. Since
\env_{X}(\theta)
is movable,
\env_{X}(\theta)|_{E}
is psef. On the other hand,
[F]|_{E}
is psef for any
F\neq E
, and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

For any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
and any birational model
\pi\colon Y\to X
, the incarnation of
\Nz(\theta)
on
Y
coincides with
\Nz_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)
. Further, the following are equivalent:

(i)

the
b
-divisor
\Nz(\theta)
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier, and determined on
Y
;

(ii)

\env_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)
is nef.

Proof.

The first point follows from Lemma
5.2
. If (i) holds then the nef
b
-divisor
\overline{\theta}-\Nz(\theta)
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier and determined on
Y
. Thus
(\overline{\theta}-\Nz(\theta))_{Y}=\pi^{\star}\theta-\Nz_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)=\env_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)
is nef, and hence (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii).

Conversely, assume (ii). Then
\overline{\Nz(\theta)_{Y}}=\overline{\Nz_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)}
is an effective
b
-divisor, and the
b
-divisor class
\overline{\theta}-[\overline{\Nz(\theta)_{Y}}]=\overline{\env_{Y}(\pi^{\star}\theta)}
is nef. By Theorem
5.1
this implies
\Nz(\theta)\leq\overline{\Nz(\theta)_{Y}}
, while
\Nz(\theta)\geq\overline{\Nz(\theta)_{Y}}
always holds (see (
5.1
)). This proves (ii)
\Rightarrow
(i).
∎

Since any movable class on a surface is nef, we get:

Corollary 5.8
.

If
\dim X=2
then
\Nz(\theta)=\overline{\Nz_{X}(\theta)}
for all
\theta\in\Psef(X)
.

In contrast, see
[
Nak04
, Theorem IV.2.10]
for an example of a big line bundle
L
on a
4
-fold
X
such that the
b
-divisor
\Nz(L)
is not
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier, i.e.
\env_{Y}(\pi^{\star}L)
is not nef for any model
\pi\colon Y\to X
.

5.3.
Zariski exceptional divisors and faces

This section revisits
[
Bou04
, §3.3]
.

Definition 5.9
.

We say that:

(i)

an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
on
X
is
Zariski exceptional
if
\Nz_{X}(D)=D
, or equivalently,
\env_{X}([D])=0
;

(ii)

a finite family
(E_{i})
of prime divisors
E_{i}\subset X
is
Zariski exceptional
if every effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor supported in the
E_{i}
’s is Zariski exceptional.

We also define a
Zariski exceptional face

F
of
\Psef(X)
as an extremal subcone such that
\env_{X}|_{F}\equiv 0
.

Here a closed subcone
C\subset\Psef(X)
is extremal iff
\alpha,\beta\in\Psef(X)
,
\alpha+\beta\in C
implies
\alpha,\beta\in C
.

We first note:

Lemma 5.10
.

An effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
is Zariski exceptional iff
\Nz(D)=\overline{D}
.

Proof.

Assume
\Nz_{X}(D)=D
. Then
\Nz(D)\leq\overline{D}
, by Theorem
5.1
, and
\Nz(D)\geq\overline{\Nz_{X}(D)}=\overline{D}
(see (
5.1
)). The result follows.
∎

The above notions are related as follows:

Theorem 5.11
.

The properties following hold:

(i)

if
E\subset X
is a prime divisor, then
E
is either movable (in which case
E|_{E}
is psef), or it is Zariski exceptional;

(ii)

the set of Zariski exceptional families of prime divisors on
X
is at most countable;

(iii)

for any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the irreducible components of
\Nz_{X}(\theta)
form a Zariski exceptional family; in particular,
\Nz_{X}(\theta)
is Zariski exceptional;

(iv)

each Zariski exceptional family
(E_{i})
is linearly independent in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, and generates a Zariski exceptional face
F:=\sum_{i}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E_{i}]
of
\Psef(X)
;

(v)

conversely, each Zariski exceptional face
F
of
\Psef(X)
arises as in (iv).

Proof.

Assume
E\subset X
is prime. Then
\Nz_{X}(E)\leq E
(see (
5.3
)), and hence
\Nz_{X}(E)=cE
with
c\in[0,1]
. If
c=1
, then
E
is Zariski exceptional. Otherwise,

E=(1-c)^{-1}(E-\Nz_{X}(E))\equiv(1-c)^{-1}\env_{X}(E)

is movable. This proves (i).

To see (ii), note that a Zariski exceptional prime divisor satisfies
E=\Nz_{X}(E):=\Nz_{X}([E])
, and hence is uniquely determined by its numerical class
[E]\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
. As a consequence, the set of Zariski exceptional primes is at most countable, and hence so is the set of Zariski exceptional families.

Pick
\theta\in\Psef(X)
. We first claim that
D:=\Nz_{X}(\theta)
is Zariski exceptional. Since
\env_{X}(\theta)=\theta-[D]
and
\env_{X}(D)=[D-\Nz_{X}(D)]
are both movable,
\theta-[\Nz_{X}(D)]
is movable as well. Theorem
5.4
thus yields
\Nz_{X}(D)\geq\Nz_{X}(\theta)=D
, which proves the claim in view of (
5.3
). Denote by
D=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}E_{i}
the irreducible decomposition of
D
, and set
f_{i}(x):=\ord_{E_{i}}(\sum_{j}x_{j}E_{j})
for
1\leq i\leq r
. This defines a convex function
f_{i}\colon\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
which satisfies
f_{i}(x)\leq x_{i}
for all
x
, by (
5.3
). Since equality holds at the interior point
x=c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{r}
, we necessarily have
f_{i}(x)=x_{i}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{r}
, which proves (iii).

Next pick a Zariski exceptional family
(E_{i})
. By Lemma
4.6
, the
[E_{i}]
are linearly independent in
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
. By definition, we have
\env_{X}\equiv 0
on
F:=\sum_{i}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E_{i}]
. To see that
F
is an extremal face of
\Psef(X)
, pick
D:=\sum_{i}c_{i}E_{i}
with
c_{i}\geq 0
, and assume
[D]=\alpha+\beta
with
\alpha,\beta\in\Psef(X)
. We need to show that both
\alpha
and
\beta
lie in
F
. By Definition
5.9
we have
D=\Nz_{X}(D)\leq\Nz_{X}(\alpha)+\Nz_{X}(\beta)
, and hence

(5.4)

[\Nz_{X}(\alpha)]+[\Nz_{X}(\beta)]\leq\env_{X}(\alpha)+\env_{X}(\beta)+[\Nz_{X}(\alpha)]+[\Nz_{X}(\beta)]\\
=\alpha+\beta=[D]\leq[\Nz_{X}(\alpha)]+\Nz_{X}(\beta)],

with respect to the psef order on
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
. Since
\Psef(X)
is strict, we infer
\env_{X}(\alpha)=\env_{X}(\beta)=0
and
[D]=[\Nz_{X}(\alpha)]+[\Nz_{X}(\beta)]
. Since
\Nz_{X}(\alpha)+\Nz_{X}(\beta)-D
is effective, it follows that
\Nz_{X}(\alpha)+\Nz_{X}(\beta)=D
. This implies that
\Nz_{X}(\alpha)
and
\Nz_{X}(\beta)
are supported in the
E_{i}
’s, which proves, as desired, that
\alpha=[\Nz_{X}(\alpha)]
and
\beta=[\Nz_{X}(\beta)]
both lie in
F
. Thus (iv) holds.

Conversely, assume that
F\subset\Psef(X)
is a Zariski exceptional face, and pick a class
\theta
in its relative interior
\mathring{F}
. By (iii), the components
(E_{i})
of
\Nz_{X}(\theta)
form a Zariski exceptional family, which thus generates a Zariski exceptional face
F^{\prime}:=\sum_{i}\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[E_{i}]
. Since
F
and
F^{\prime}
are both extremal faces containing
\theta
in their relative interior, we conclude
F=F^{\prime}
, which proves (v).
∎

As a result, Zariski exceptional families are in 1–1 correspondence with Zariski exceptional faces, which are rational simplicial cones generated by Zariski exceptional primes.

For surfaces, we recover the classical picture (see e.g.
[
Bou04
, §4]
):

Theorem 5.12
.

Assume
\dim X=2
. Then:

(i)

a finite family
(E_{i})
of prime divisors on
X
is Zariski exceptional iff the intersection matrix
(E_{i}\cdot E_{j})
is negative definite;

(ii)

for any
\theta\in\Psef(X)
,
\theta=\env_{X}(\theta)+[\Nz_{X}(\theta)]
coincides with the classical Zariski decomposition,
i.e.
\env_{X}(\theta)
is nef,
\Nz_{X}(\theta)
is Zariski exceptional, and
\env_{X}(\theta)\cdot\Nz_{X}(\theta)=0
.

5.4.
Piecewise linear Zariski decompositions

We introduce the following terminology:

Definition 5.13
.

Given a convex subcone
C\subset\Psef(X)
, we say that
the Zariski decomposition is piecewise linear
(
PL
for short) on
C
if the map
\Nz\colon C\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
extends to a PL map
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
,
i.e.
a map that is linear on each cone of some finite fan decomposition of
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
. If the fan and the linear maps on its cones can further be chosen rational, then we say that
the Zariski decomposition is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL
on
C
.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
C\subset\Psef(X)
be a convex cone, and assume that
C
is written as the union of finitely many convex subcones
C_{i}
. Then the Zariski decomposition is PL (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-PL) on
C
iff it is PL (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-PL) on each
C_{i}
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ part is clear. Conversely, assume the Zariski decomposition is PL (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-PL) on each
C_{i}
. After further subdividing each
C_{i}
according to a fan decomposition of
\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, we may assume that there exists a linear (resp. rational linear) map
L_{i}\colon\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
that coincides with
\Nz
on
C_{i}
. If
C_{i}
has nonempty interior in
C
, then
L_{i}|_{\Vect C}
is uniquely determined as the derivative of
\Nz
at any interior point of
C_{i}
, and we have
\Nz\geq L_{i}
on
C
by convexity of
\Nz
, see Corollary
5.3
. Set
F:=\max_{i}L_{i}
, where the maximum is over all
C_{i}
with nonempty interior in
C
. Then
F\colon\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)\to\Zunb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
is PL (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-PL),
\Nz\geq F
on
C
, and equality holds outside the union
A
of all
C_{i}
with empty interior in
C
. Since
A
has zero measure, its complement is dense in
C
. Since
\Nz-F
is lsc, see Corollary
5.3
, we infer
\Nz\leq F
on
C
, which proves the ‘if’ part.
∎

As a consequence of
[
ELMNP06
, Theorem 4.1]
, we have:

Example 5.15
.

If
X
is a Mori dream space (
e.g.
of log Fano type), then:

•

for each
\theta\in\Psef(X)
, the
b
-divisor
\Nz(\theta)
is
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier;

•

\Psef(X)
is a rational polyhedral cone;

•

the Zariski decomposition is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL on
\Psef(X)
.

The next result is closely related to the theory of Zariski chambers studied in
[
BKS04
]
.

Proposition 5.16
.

If
\dim X=2
, then the Zariski decomposition is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL on any convex cone
C\subset\Psef(X)
with the property that the set of prime divisors
E\subset X
with
\ord_{E}(\theta)>0
for some
\theta\in C
is finite.

By Lemma
4.7
(ii), the finiteness condition on
C
is satisfied as soon as
C
is polyhedral.

Proof.

For each Zariski exceptional face
F
of
\Psef(X)
with relative interior
\mathring{F}
, set
Z_{F}:=\Nz_{X}^{-1}(\mathring{F})
. Thus
\theta\in\Psef(X)
lies in
Z_{F}
iff the irreducible decomposition of
\Nz_{X}(\alpha)
are precisely the generators of
F
. By Theorem
5.12
(ii),
Z_{F}
is a convex subcone of
\Psef(X)
(whose intersection with
\Bg(X)
is a Zariski chamber in the sense of
[
BKS04
]
); further,
\Nz_{X}|_{Z_{F}}:Z_{F}\to\mathring{F}
is the restriction of the orthogonal projection onto
\Vect F
, which is a rational linear map. By Corollary
5.8
, the Zariski decomposition is thus
\mathbb{Q}
-PL on
Z_{F}
. Finally, the finiteness assumption guarantees that
C
meets only finitely many
Z_{F}
’s, and the result is thus a consequence of Lemma
5.14
.
∎

We conclude this section with a higher-dimensional situation in which Zariski decompositions can be analyzed. Assuming again that
\dim X
is arbitrary, consider next a
2
-dimensional cone
C\subset\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
generated by two classes
\theta,\alpha\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
such that
\theta\in\Nef(X)
and
\alpha\notin\Psef(X)
. Set

C_{\mathrm{nef}}:=C\cap\Nef(X)\subset C_{\mathrm{psef}}:=C\cap\Psef(X)\subset C,

and introduce the thresholds

\lambda_{\mathrm{nef}}:=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\theta+\lambda\alpha\in\Nef(X)\},\quad\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}:=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\theta+\lambda\alpha\in\Psef(X)\},

so that
C_{\mathrm{nef}}
(resp.
C_{\mathrm{psef}}
) is generated by
\theta
and
\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}:=\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{nef}}\alpha
(resp
\theta_{\mathrm{psef}}:=\theta+\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\alpha
).

The next result is basically contained in
[
Per21
, §6.5]
.

Proposition 5.17
.

With the above notation, suppose that
C
contains the class of a prime divisor
S\subset X
such that
\Nef(S)=\Psef(S)
and
S|_{S}
is not nef. Then:

(i)

\theta_{\mathrm{psef}}=t[S]
with
t>0
;

(ii)

\lambda_{\mathrm{nef}}=\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}:=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\mid(\theta+\lambda\alpha)|_{S}\in\Nef(S)\right\}
;

(iii)

the Zariski decomposition is PL on
C_{\mathrm{psef}}
, with

\Nz\equiv 0\text{ on }C_{\mathrm{nef}},\quad\Nz(a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])=b\overline{S}\text{ for all }a,b\geq 0.

Proof.

The assumptions imply that
S|_{S}
is not psef. By Theorem
5.11
(i),
S
is thus Zariski exceptional, and
[S]
generates an extremal ray of
\Psef(X)
. This ray is also extremal in
C_{\mathrm{psef}}
, which proves (i).

Next, note that
\lambda_{\mathrm{nef}}\leq\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
, by (i). Pick a curve
\gamma\subset X
. We need to show
(\theta+\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\alpha)\cdot\gamma\geq 0
. This is clear if
\gamma\subset S
(since
(\theta+\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\alpha)|_{S}
is nef), or if
\alpha\cdot\gamma\geq 0
(since
\theta\cdot\gamma\geq 0
and
\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\geq 0
). Otherwise, we have
S\cdot\gamma\geq 0
and
\alpha\cdot\gamma\leq 0
, and we get again
(\theta+\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\alpha)\cdot\gamma\geq 0
since

\theta+\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\alpha\equiv\theta_{\mathrm{psef}}+(\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}})\alpha=t[S]+(\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}})\alpha

with
\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\leq 0
. This proves (ii).

For (iii), note that
\Nz\equiv 0
on
\Nef(X)\supset C_{\mathrm{nef}}
(see Theorem
5.1
). Further,
\Nz([S])=\overline{S}
(see Lemma
5.10
), and hence
\Nz(a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])\leq b\overline{S}
for
a,b\geq 0
. In particular,
c:=\ord_{S}(a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])\leq b
. On the other hand, (
5.1
) yields

\Nz(a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])\geq\overline{\Nz(a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])}\geq c\overline{S},

and it thus remains to see
c=b
. By Corollary
5.6
,

\left((a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+b[S])-c[S]\right)|_{S}
lies in
\Psef(S)=\Nef(S)
. By (b), we infer
a\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+(b-c)[S]\in C_{\mathrm{nef}}
, and hence
b-c=0
, since
C_{\mathrm{nef}}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}
intersects
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[S]
only along
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\theta_{\mathrm{nef}}
.
∎

6.
Green’s functions and Zariski decompositions

In this section we fix an ample class
\omega\in\Amp(X)
.

6.1.
Green’s functions and equilibrium measures

A subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
is
pluripolar
if
\Sigma\subset\{\varphi=-\infty\}
for some
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
. By
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 4.5]
,
\Sigma
is nonpluripolar iff

\te(\Sigma):=\sup_{\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)}(\sup\varphi-\sup_{\Sigma}\varphi)\in[0,+\infty]

is finite. The invariant
\te(\Sigma)
, which plays an important role in
[
BlJ20
,
BoJ21
]
, is modeled in the Alexander-Taylor capacity (which corresponds to
e^{-\te(\Sigma)}
)
in complex analysis.

Definition 6.1
.

For any subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
we set

(6.1)

\varphi_{\Sigma}=\varphi_{\omega,\Sigma}:=\sup\{\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)\mid\varphi|_{\Sigma}\leq 0\}.

Note that
\varphi_{\Sigma}(v_{\mathrm{triv}})=\sup\varphi_{\Sigma}=\te(\Sigma)
, and hence

(6.2)

\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL(X)\Longrightarrow\te(\Sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}.

Theorem 6.2
.

For any compact subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
, the following holds:

(i)

\varphi_{\Sigma}=\sup\{\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)\mid\varphi|_{\Sigma}\leq 0\}
; in particular,
\varphi_{\Sigma}
is lsc;

(ii)

if
\Sigma
is pluripolar then
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\equiv+\infty
;

(iii)

if
\Sigma
is nonpluripolar, then
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}
is
\omega
-psh and nonnegative; further,
\mu_{\Sigma}:=\MA(\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star})
is supported in
\Sigma
,
\int\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\,\mu_{\Sigma}=0
, and
\mu_{\Sigma}
is characterized as the unique minimizer of the energy
\|\mu\|
over all Radon probability measures
\mu
with support in
\Sigma
.

Since the energy of a Radon probability measure
\mu
only appears in this statement, we simply recall here that it is defined as

(6.3)

\|\mu\|=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(\omega)}\left(\en(\varphi)-\int\varphi\,\mu\right)\in[0,+\infty],

and refer to
[
BoJ22b
, §9.1]
for more details.

Definition 6.3
.

Assuming
\Sigma
is nonpluripolar, we call
\mu_{\Sigma}
its
equilibrium measure
, and
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}
its
Green’s function
.

The latter is characterized as the normalized potential of
\mu_{\Sigma}
(in the terminology of
[
BoJ22a
, §1.6]
), i.e. the unique
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(\omega)
such that
\MA(\varphi)=\mu_{\Sigma}
and
\int\varphi\,\mu_{\Sigma}=0
.

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

Denote by
\varphi^{\prime}_{\Sigma}
the right-hand side in (i), which obviously satisfies
\varphi^{\prime}_{\Sigma}\leq\varphi_{\Sigma}
. Pick
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
with
\varphi|_{\Sigma}\leq 0
, and write
\varphi
as the limit of a decreasing net
(\varphi_{i})
in
\CPSH(\omega)
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, a Dini type argument shows that
\varphi_{i}<\varepsilon
on
\Sigma
for
i
large enough. Thus
\varphi_{i}\leq\varphi^{\prime}_{\Sigma}+\varepsilon
, and hence
\varphi\leq\varphi^{\prime}_{\Sigma}+\varepsilon
. This shows
\varphi_{\Sigma}\leq\varphi^{\prime}_{\Sigma}
, which proves (i).

Next, (ii) and the first half of (iii) follow from
[
BoJ22b
, Lemma 13.15]
. Since the negligible set
\{\varphi_{\Sigma}<\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\}
is pluripolar (see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 13.17]
), it has zero measure for any measure
\mu
of finite energy
[
BoJ22b
, Lemma 9.2]
. If
\mu
has support in
\Sigma
, this yields
\int\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\,\mu=\int\varphi_{\Sigma}\,\mu=0
. By (
6.3
) we infer
\|\mu\|\geq\en(\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star})=\|\mu_{\Sigma}\|
. This proves that
\mu_{\Sigma}
minimizes the energy, while uniqueness follows from the strict convexity of the energy
[
BoJ22b
, Proposition 10.10]
.
∎

Further mimicking classical terminology in the complex analytic setting, we introduce:

Definition 6.4
.

We say that a compact subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
is
regular
if
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\CPSH(\omega)
.

In particular,
\Sigma
is nonpluripolar (see Theorem
6.2
).

Lemma 6.5
.

For any compact subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
, the following hold:

(i)

\Sigma
is regular iff
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\leq 0
on
\Sigma
;

(ii)

the regularity of
\Sigma
is independent of
\omega\in\Amp(X)
;

(iii)

if
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{lin}}
then
\Sigma
is regular.

Proof.

If
\Sigma
is regular, then
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}=\varphi_{\Sigma}
vanishes on
\Sigma
. Conversely, assume
\varphi^{\star}_{\Sigma}\leq 0
on
\Sigma
. By (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
6.2
,
\Sigma
is necessarily nonpluripolar, and
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}
is
\omega
-psh. It is thus a competitor in (
6.1
), which implies that
\varphi_{\Sigma}=\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}
is
\omega
-psh, and also continuous by Theorem
6.2
(i).

Assume
\Sigma
is regular for
\omega
, and pick
\omega^{\prime}\in\Amp(X)
. Then
t\omega-\omega^{\prime}
is nef for
t\gg 1
, and hence
\PSH(\omega^{\prime})\subset t\PSH(\omega)
. This implies
\varphi_{\omega^{\prime},\Sigma}\leq t\varphi_{\omega,\Sigma}
, and hence
\varphi^{\star}_{\omega^{\prime},\Sigma}\leq t\varphi_{\omega,\Sigma}
. In particular,
\varphi^{\star}_{\omega^{\prime},\Sigma}|_{\Sigma}\leq 0
, which proves that
\Sigma
is regular for
\omega^{\prime}
, by (i).

Finally, assume
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{lin}}
. Since
\{\varphi_{\Sigma}<\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\}
is pluripolar (see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 13.17]
), it is disjoint from
X^{\mathrm{lin}}
. As a result,
\varphi_{\Sigma}^{\star}\in\PSH(\omega)
vanishes on
\Sigma
, and it again follows from (i) that
\Sigma
is regular.
∎

6.2.
The Green’s function of a real divisorial set

In what follows, we consider a
real divisorial set
, by which we mean a finite set
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}
of real divisorial valuations. By Lemma
6.5
(iii),
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{lin}}
is regular, i.e.
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\CPSH(\omega)
. When
\Sigma=\{v\}
for a single
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we simply write
\varphi_{v}:=\varphi_{\Sigma}
.

Example 6.6
.

Assume
\omega=c_{1}(L)
with
L\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
ample and
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. Then
v
is
dreamy
(with respect to
L
) in the sense of K.Fujita iff
\varphi_{v}\in\mathcal{H}(L)
; see
[
BoJ21
, §1.7,Appendix A]
.

If
v_{\mathrm{triv}}\in\Sigma
, then
\varphi_{\Sigma}\equiv 0
, and we henceforth assume
v_{\mathrm{triv}}\notin\Sigma
. Pick a smooth birational model
\pi\colon Y\to X
which extracts each
v\in\Sigma
, i.e.
v=t_{v}\ord_{E_{v}}
for a prime divisor
E_{v}\subset Y
and
t_{v}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. We then introduce the effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
Y

D:=\sum_{\alpha}t_{\alpha}^{-1}E_{\alpha},

whose set of Rees valuations
\Gamma_{D}
coincides with
\Sigma
(see Definition
2.2
).

Theorem 6.7
.

With the above notation, the following holds:

(i)

\sup\varphi_{\Sigma}=\te(\Sigma)
coincides with the pseudoeffective threshold

\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}:=\max\left\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda D\in\Psef(Y)\right\};

(ii)

\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\CPSH(\omega)
is of divisorial type, and the associated family of
b
-divisors
(B_{\lambda})_{\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}}
(see Theorem
2.4
) is given by

-B_{\lambda}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Nz(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda D)+\lambda\overline{D}&\text{for }\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}]\\
0&\text{for }\lambda\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Pick
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. For any
\psi\in\PSH(\omega)
, we have
\psi+\lambda\leq\varphi\Leftrightarrow\psi|_{\Sigma}\leq-\lambda
, and hence

\widehat{\varphi}_{\Sigma}^{\lambda}=\sup\{\psi\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\omega)\mid\psi|_{\Sigma}\leq-\lambda\}.

When
\lambda\leq 0
this yields
\widehat{\varphi}_{\Sigma}^{\lambda}=0
. Assume now
\lambda>0
. Using Proposition
2.3
and
\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\pi^{\star}\omega)=\pi^{\star}\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\omega)
, we get

(6.4)

\pi^{\star}\widehat{\varphi}^{\lambda}_{\Sigma}=\sup\{\tau\in\PSH_{\mathrm{hom}}(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda D)\}-\lambda\psi_{D}=V_{\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda D}-\lambda\psi_{D}.

Now the left-hand side is not identically
-\infty
iff
\lambda\leq\sup\varphi
, while for the right-hand side this holds iff
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
, by Proposition
4.1
. This proves (i), and also (ii), by Theorem
4.5
.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

The center of
\varphi_{\Sigma}
satisfies

Z_{X}(\varphi_{\Sigma})=\pi\left(\mathbb{B}_{-}(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}D)\right)\cup Z_{X}(\Sigma).

In particular,
Z_{X}(\varphi_{\Sigma})
is Zariski dense in
X
iff
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}D)
is Zariski dense in
Y
.

Proof.

By Lemma (
3.3
), we have

Z_{X}(\varphi_{\Sigma})=Z_{X}(\widehat{\varphi}_{\Sigma}^{\max})=\pi(Z_{Y}(\pi^{*}\widehat{\varphi}_{\Sigma}^{\max})).

It follows from Theorem
6.7
and its proof that

\pi^{\star}\widehat{\varphi}^{\max}_{\Sigma}=V_{\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}D}-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\psi_{D}.

Now
Z_{Y}(V_{\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}D})=\mathbb{B}_{-}(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}D)
by
Theorem
4.5
, whereas we see from Example
3.2
that
Z_{Y}(-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\psi_{D})=Z_{Y}(\Sigma)
, so we conclude using Lemma
3.6
.
∎

6.3.
Dimension one and two

In this section we consider the case
\dim X\leq 2
.

Proposition 6.9
.

If
\dim X=1
, then for any real divisorial set
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. If
\omega
is rational and
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, then we further have
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)
.

Proof.

We may assume
v_{\mathrm{triv}}\not\in\Sigma
, or else
\varphi_{\Sigma}\equiv 0
. Thus assume

\Sigma=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, where
v_{i}=t_{i}\ord_{p_{i}}
,
t_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, and
p_{i}\in X
is a closed point. We may assume
p_{i}\neq p_{j}
for
i\neq j
, or else
\varphi_{\Sigma}=\varphi_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
for
\Sigma^{\prime}=\{v_{i}\}_{i\in I^{\prime}}
, where
I^{\prime}\subset I
is defined by
i\in I^{\prime}
iff for all
j\neq i
, either
p_{j}\neq p_{i}
or
t_{j}>t_{i}
.
Under these assumptions,

\varphi_{\Sigma}=A\max\{1+\sum_{i}t_{i}^{-1}\log|\mathfrak{m}_{p_{i}}|,0\},

where
A>0
satisfies
A\sum_{i}t_{i}^{-1}=\deg\omega
, see
[
BoJ22b
, Example 3.19]
. Thus
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. Further, if
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, then
t_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
for all
i
, so if
\omega
is rational, then
A\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
, and hence
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)
.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

If
\dim X=2
, then for any real divisorial set
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, we have
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. If
\omega
is rational and
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, then we further have

(6.5)

\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL(X)\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)\Leftrightarrow\te(\Sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}.

We will see in Example
7.2
that
\te(\Sigma)
can be irrational.

Lemma 6.11
.

Assume
\dim X\leq 2
, and pick
B\in\Carb(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
B
is relatively nef iff it is relatively semiample.

Proof.

Assume
B
is relative nef, and pick a determination
\pi\colon Y\to X
of
B
. The relatively nef cone of
\operatorname{N^{1}}(Y/X)
is dual to the cone generated by the (finite) set of
\pi
-exceptional prime divisors, and is thus a rational polyhedral cone. As a consequence, we can write
B_{Y}=\sum_{i}t_{i}D_{i}
with
t_{i}>0
and
D_{i}\in\Div(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}
relatively nef. By
[
Lip69
, Theorem 12.1 (ii)]
, each
D_{i}
is relatively semiample, and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.10
.

Use the notation of Theorem
6.7
. By Proposition
5.16
, the Zariski decomposition is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL on the cone

C=(\mathbb{R}_{+}\pi^{\star}\omega+\mathbb{R}_{+}[-D])\cap\Psef(Y)=\mathbb{R}_{+}\pi^{\star}\omega+\mathbb{R}_{+}(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}[D]).

We can thus find
0=\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\dots<\lambda_{N}=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
such that

\lambda\mapsto B_{\lambda}=-(\Nz(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda[D])+\lambda\overline{D})

is affine linear on
[\lambda_{i},\lambda_{i+1}]
for
1\leq i<N
. Setting
B_{i}:=B_{\lambda_{i}}
, it follows that

\varphi_{\Sigma}=\sup_{\lambda\in[0,\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}]}\{\psi_{B_{\lambda}}+\lambda\}=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\{\psi_{B_{i}}+\lambda_{i}\}.

Since
\overline{\omega}+[B_{i}]
is nef, the antieffective divisor
B_{i}
is relatively nef, and hence relatively semiample (see Lemma
6.11
). By Proposition
1.7
, we infer
\psi_{B_{i}}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, and hence
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
.

Now assume
\omega
and
\te(\Sigma)=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
are both rational, and that
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
. Then
D
is rational as well, and
C
is thus a rational polyhedral cone. Since the Zariski decomposition on
C
is the restriction of a
\mathbb{Q}
-PL map on
\operatorname{N^{1}}(Y)
, this implies that the
\lambda_{i}
above can be chosen rational. Using again that the Zariski decomposition is
\mathbb{Q}
-PL on
C
, we infer that
B_{i}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, hence
\psi_{B_{i}}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
, which shows
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)
. The rest follows from (
6.2
).
∎

7.
Examples of Green’s functions

We now exhibit examples of Green’s functions with various types of behavior. These examples serve as the underpinnings of Theorems A and B of the introduction.

7.1.
Divisors on abelian varieties

As a direct application of Theorem
6.7
, we show:

Proposition 7.1
.

Assume
\Nef(X)=\Psef(X)
. Consider a real divisorial set
\Sigma=\{v_{\alpha}\}\subset
with
v_{\alpha}=t_{\alpha}\ord_{E_{\alpha}}
for
E_{\alpha}\subset X
prime, and set
D:=\sum_{\alpha}t_{\alpha}^{-1}E_{\alpha}
. Then

\te(\Sigma)=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}=\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\omega-\lambda D\in\Psef(X)\right\}

and

\varphi_{\Sigma}=\te(\Sigma)\max\left\{0,1-\psi_{D}\right\}.

In particular,
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. If we further assume
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, then

(7.1)

\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL(X)\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)\Leftrightarrow\te(\Sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

Using the notation of Theorem
6.7
, we have
\Nz(\omega-\lambda D)=0
for
\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}=\te(\Sigma)
. Thus
\widehat{\varphi}_{\Sigma}^{\lambda}=-\lambda\psi_{D}
, and hence

\varphi_{\Sigma}=\sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}}\{\lambda-\lambda\psi_{D}\}=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}\max\left\{0,1-\psi_{D}\right\}.

Since
-\psi_{D}=\sum_{\alpha}t_{\alpha}^{-1}\log|\mathcal{O}_{X}(-E_{\alpha})|
lies in
\PL^{+}(X)_{\mathbb{R}}
, it follows that
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
. If
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, then
D
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, and hence
-\psi_{D}\in\PL^{+}_{\mathrm{hom}}(X)
. If we further assume
\te(\Sigma)\in\mathbb{Q}
, we get
\varphi_{\Sigma}\in\PL^{+}(X)
, and the remaining implication follows from (
6.2
).
∎

Example 7.2
.

Suppose
X
is an abelian surface,
\omega=c_{1}(L)
with
L\in\Pic(X)_{\mathbb{Q}}
ample, and
v=\ord_{E}
with
E\subset X
a prime divisor. Then
\Nef(X)=\Psef(X)
, and
\te(v)=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
is the smallest root of the quadratic equation
(L-\lambda E)^{2}=0
. If
X
has Picard number
\rho(X)\geq 2
, then
\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}
is irrational for a typical choice of
L
and
E
, and hence
\varphi_{v}\notin\PL(X)
. In particular,
v
is not dreamy (with respect to
L
) in the sense of Fujita, see Example
6.6
.

7.2.
The Cutkosky example

Building on a construction of Cutkosky
[
Cut00
]
and Proposition
5.17
(itself based on
[
Per21
, §6.5]
), we provide an example of a divisorial valuation on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
for which (
6.5
) fails. This relies on the following general result.

Proposition 7.3
.

Consider a flag of smooth subvarieties
Z\subset S\subset X
with
\codim S=1
,
\codim Z=2
and ideals
\mathfrak{b}_{S}\subset\mathfrak{b}_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and assume that

(i)

S\equiv\omega
;

(ii)

\Nef(S)=\Psef(S)
;

(iii)

\omega|_{S}-Z
is not nef on
S
,
i.e.
\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}:=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\omega|_{S}-\lambda[Z]\in\Nef(S)\}<1
.

The Green’s function of
v:=\ord_{Z}\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
is then given by

\varphi_{v}=\max\left\{0,\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}(\log|\mathfrak{b}_{Z}|+1),\log|\mathfrak{b}_{S}|+1\right\}.

In particular,
\te(v)=1
,
\varphi_{v}\in\RPL^{+}(X)
, and

\varphi_{v}\in\PL(X)\Leftrightarrow\varphi_{v}\in\PL^{+}(X)\Leftrightarrow\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be the blowup along
Z
, with exceptional divisor
E
, and denote by
S^{\prime}=\pi^{\star}S-E
the strict transform of
S
. Since

has codimension
1
on

,
\pi

S^{\prime}
isomorphically onto

, and takes

to
S|_{S}-Z\equiv\omega|_{S}-[Z]
. By (ii) and (iii), we thus have
\Nef(S^{\prime})=\Psef(S^{\prime})
, and
S^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
is not nef.

Consider the cone

generated by
\theta:=\pi^{\star}\omega\in\Nef(Y)
and
\alpha:=-[E]\notin\Psef(Y)
. Since

contains the class of
S^{\prime}
, it follows from Proposition

that

1=\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}:=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda[E]\in\Psef(Y)\}

and
\lambda\mapsto\Nz(\pi^{\star}\omega-\lambda E)
vanishes on

, with value

at

By Theorem

, the concave family

of

is affine linear on

and

at

,
\lambda^{S}_{\mathrm{nef}}
and

.
∎

7.3.
The Lesieutre example

Based on an example by Lesieutre
[
Les14
]
, we now exhibit a Green’s function that is not
\mathbb{R}
-PL. This forms the basis for Theorem B in the introduction.

Proposition 7.5
.

Suppose that
X
admits a class
\theta\in\Psef(X)
whose diminished base locus
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)
is Zariski dense. Then there exist
\omega\in\Amp(X)
and
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
such that
Z_{X}(\varphi_{\omega,v})
is Zariski dense in
X
. In particular,
\varphi_{\omega,v}\notin\RPL(X)
.

Proof.

Note first that
\theta
cannot be big. Otherwise, there would exist an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D\equiv\theta
, and hence
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\theta)
would be contained in
\supp D
. Pick an ample prime divisor
E
on
X
, choose
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
large enough such that
\omega:=\theta+c[E]
is ample, and set
v:=c^{-1}\ord_{E}\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
.
Since
\omega
is ample and
\omega-c[E]=\theta
lies on the boundary of
\Psef(X)
, the threshold
\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}=\sup\{\lambda\geq 0\mid\omega-\lambda[E]\in\Psef(X)\}
is equal to
c
. Thus
\mathbb{B}_{-}(\omega-\lambda_{\mathrm{psef}}[E])
is Zariski dense, and hence so is
Z_{X}(\varphi_{\omega,v})
, by Corollary
6.8
. The last point follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

8.
The non-trivially valued case

In this section, we work over the non-Archimedean field
K=k(\!(\varpi)\!)
of formal Laurent series, with valuation ring
K^{\circ}:=k[\![\varpi]\!]
. We use
[
BFJ16
]
as our main reference.

Thus
X
now denotes a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
over
K
. (In §
9
, it will be obtained as the base change of a smooth projective
k
-variety.)
Working ‘additively’, we view the elements of the analytification
X^{\mathrm{an}}
as valuations
w\colon K(Y)^{\times}\to\mathbb{R}
for subvarieties
Y\subset X
, restricting to the given valuation on
K
.

8.1.
Models

We define a
model
of
X
to be a normal, flat, projective
K^{\circ}
-scheme
\mathcal{X}
together with the data of an isomorphism
\mathcal{X}_{K}\simeq X
. The
special fiber
of
\mathcal{X}
is the projective
k
-scheme
\mathcal{X}_{0}:=\mathcal{X}\times_{\Spec K}\Spec k
. Each
w\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
can be viewed as a semivaluation on
\mathcal{X}
, whose center is denoted by
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. This defines a surjective, continuous
reduction map

\redu_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
. For each
w\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
we also set

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w):=\overline{\{\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\}}\subset\mathcal{X}_{0}.

The preimage under
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}
of the set of generic points of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is finite. We denote it by
\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
, and call its elements the
Shilov points
of
\mathcal{X}
. As
\mathcal{X}
is normal, each irreducible component
E
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
defines a
divisorial valuation

w_{E}\in X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
given by

w_{E}:=b_{E}^{-1}\ord_{E},\,b_{E}:=\ord_{E}(\varpi);

it is the unique preimage under
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}
of the generic point of
E
, and the Shilov points of
\mathcal{X}
are exactly these valuations
w_{E}
.

One says that another model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}

dominates

\mathcal{X}
if the canonical birational map
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{X}
extends to a morphism (necessarily unique, by separatedness). In that case,
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}
is the composition of
\redu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
with the induced projective morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
. The set of models forms a filtered poset with respect to domination. The set

X^{\mathrm{div}}=\bigcup_{\mathcal{X}}\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}

of all divisorial valuations is a dense subset of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
.

8.2.
Piecewise linear functions

A
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
D
on a model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
is
vertical
if it is supported in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
; it then defines a continuous function on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
called a
model function
.
The
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\PL(X)
of such functions is stable under max, and dense in
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
.

Definition 8.1
.

We define the space
\RPL(X)
of
real piecewise linear functions
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
(
\mathbb{R}
-PL functions
for short) as the smallest
\mathbb{R}
-linear subspace of
\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
that is stable under max (and hence also min) and contains
\PL(X)
.

Fix a model
\mathcal{X}
. An ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
is
vertical
if its zero locus
V(\mathfrak{a})
is contained in
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. This defines a nonpositive function
\log|\mathfrak{a}|\in\PL(X)
, determined by minus the exceptional divisor of the blowup of
\mathcal{X}
along
\mathfrak{a}
, and such that

(8.1)

\log|\mathfrak{a}|(w)<0\Longleftrightarrow Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\subset V(\mathfrak{a}).

Functions of the form
\log|\mathfrak{a}|
for a vertical ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
span the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\PL(X)
(see
[
BFJ16
, Proposition 2.2]
). As in §
1.3
, it follows that any function in
\RPL(X)
can be written as a difference of finite maxima of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-linear combinations of functions of the form
\log|\mathfrak{a}|
.

8.3.
Dual complexes and retractions

We use
[
MN15
,
BFJ16
]
as references.

An
snc model

\mathcal{X}
is a regular model
\mathcal{X}
such that the Cartier divisor
\mathcal{X}_{0}
has simple normal crossing support. Denote by
\mathcal{X}_{0}=\sum_{i\in I}b_{i}E_{i}
its irreducible decomposition. A
stratum
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is defined as a non-empty irreducible component of
E_{J}:=\bigcap_{j\in J}E_{j}
for some
J\subset I
. By resolution of singularities, the set of snc models is cofinal in the poset of all models.

The
dual complex

\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
of an snc model
\mathcal{X}
is defined as the dual intersection complex of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Its faces are in 1–1 correspondence with the strata of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and further come with a natural integral affine structure. In particular, the vertices of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
are in 1–1 correspondence with the
E_{i}
’s, and admit a natural realization in
X^{\mathrm{an}}
as the set
\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}
of Shilov points
x_{E_{i}}
.

This extends to a canonical embedding
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\hookrightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}}
onto the set of monomial points with respect to
\sum_{i}E_{i}
. The reduction
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
of a point
w\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
is the generic point of the stratum of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
associated with the unique simplex of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
containing
x
in its relative interior. In particular,
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
is a stratum of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
. This embedding is further compatible with the PL structures, in the sense that the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space
\PL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
of piecewise rational affine functions on
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is precisely the image of
\PL(X)
under restriction.

If another snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
dominates
\mathcal{X}
, then
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}
, and
\PL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})
restricts to
\PL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
. Furthermore, the set

X^{\mathrm{qm}}:=\bigcup_{\mathcal{X}}\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}

of
quasimonomial valuations
coincides with the set of Abhyankar points of
X
, see
[
BFJ16
, Remark 3.8]
and
[
JM12
, Proposition 3.7]
, while the subset of rational points
\bigcup_{\mathcal{X}}\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Q})
coincides with the set
X^{\mathrm{div}}
of divisorial valuations. For later use, we also note:

Lemma 8.2
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is an snc model, then the image
\redu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}
of the dual complex of
\mathcal{X}
under the reduction map of any other model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is finite.

Proof.

Pick an snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
that dominates both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. Then
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is contained in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}}
, and
\redu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
is thus contained in the image of
\redu_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}}(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}})
under the induced morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
. After replacing both
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
, we may thus assume without loss that
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. For any
w\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
,
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
is then the generic point of some stratum of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
, and
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
is thus a finite set.
∎

Dually, each snc model
\mathcal{X}
comes with a canonical
retraction

p_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
that takes
w\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
to the unique monomial valuation
w^{\prime}=p_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
such that

•

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w^{\prime})
is the minimal stratum containing
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
;

•

w
and
w^{\prime}
take the sames values on the
E_{i}
’s.

This induces a homeomorphism
X^{\mathrm{an}}\overset{\sim}{\to}\varprojlim_{\mathcal{X}}\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, which is compatible with the PL structures in the sense that

(8.2)

\PL(X)=\bigcup_{\mathcal{X}}p_{\mathcal{X}}^{\star}\PL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}).

This implies

(8.3)

\RPL(X)=\bigcup_{\mathcal{X}}p_{\mathcal{X}}^{\star}\RPL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}),

where
\RPL(\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
is the space
\mathbb{R}
-PL functions on
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e. functions that are real affine linear on a sufficiently fine decomposition of each face into real simplices.

8.4.
Psh functions and Monge–Ampère measures

We use
[
BFJ16
,
BFJ15
,
GM19
]
as references.

A
closed
(1,1)
-form

\theta\in\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X)
in the sense of
[
BFJ16
, §4.2]
is represented by a relative numerical equivalence class on some model
\mathcal{X}
, called a
determination
of
\theta
. It induces a numerical class
[\theta]\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
. We say that
\theta
is
semipositive
, written
\theta\geq 0
, if
\theta
is determined by a nef numerical class on some model. In that case,
[\theta]
is nef as well.

To each tuple
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
in
\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X)
is associated a signed Radon measure
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
of total mass
[\theta_{1}]\cdot\ldots\cdot[\theta_{n}]
, with finite support in
X^{\mathrm{div}}
. More precisely, if all
\theta_{i}
are determined by a normal model
\mathcal{X}
, then
\theta_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\theta_{n}
has support in
\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}
(see
[
BFJ15
, §2.7]
).

Each
\varphi\in\PL(X)
is determined by a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on some model
\mathcal{X}
, whose numerical class defines a closed
(1,1)
-form
\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi\in\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X)
. We say that
\varphi
is
\theta
-psh
for a given
\theta\in\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X)
if
\theta+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi\geq 0
.

From now on, we fix a semipositive form
\omega\in\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X)
such that
[\omega]
is ample. A function
\varphi\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is
\omega
-plurisubharmonic
(
\omega
-psh
for short) if
\varphi\not\equiv-\infty
and
\varphi
can be written as the pointwise limit of a decreasing net of
\omega
-psh PL functions. The space
\PSH(\omega)
is closed under max and under decreasing limits.

By Dini’s lemma, the space
\CPSH(\omega)
of continuous
\omega
-psh functions coincides with the closure in
\Cz(X)
(with respect to uniform convergence) of the space of
\omega
-psh PL functions.

Each
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
satisfies the ‘maximum principle’

(8.4)

\sup_{X}\varphi=\max_{\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}}\varphi

for any model
\mathcal{X}
determining
\omega
(see
[
GM19
, Proposition 4.22]
). For snc models,
[
BFJ16
, §7.1]
more precisely yields:

Lemma 8.3
.

Pick
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
and an snc model
\mathcal{X}
on which
\omega
is determined. Then:

(i)

the restriction of
\varphi
to any face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
is continuous and convex;

(iii)

the net
(\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}})_{\mathcal{X}}
is decreasing and converges pointwise to
\varphi
.

Remark 8.4
.

The definition of
\PSH(\omega)
given here differs from the one in
[
BFJ16
]
, but Theorem 8.7 in loc. cit. implies that the two definitions are equivalent.

To each continuous
\omega
-psh function
\varphi
(or, more generally, any
\omega
-psh function of finite energy) is associated its
Monge–Ampère measure

\MA(\varphi)=\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
, a Radon probability measure on
X
uniquely determined by the following properties:

•

\varphi\mapsto\MA(\varphi)
is continuous along decreasing nets;

•

if
\varphi
is PL, then
\MA(\varphi)=V^{-1}(\omega+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}
with
V:=[\omega]^{n}
.

By the main result of
[
BFJ15
]
, any Radon probability measure
\mu
with support in the dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
of some snc model can be written as
\mu=\MA(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
, unique up to an additive constant.

Green’s functions

As in the trivially valued case, we can consider the Green’s function associated to a nonpluripolar set
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
. Here we will only consider the following case. Suppose

is a divisorial point, and define

\varphi_{w}:=\varphi_{\omega,w}:=\sup\{\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)\mid\varphi(w)\leq 0\}.

It follows from
[

that

satisfies
\MA(\varphi_{w})=\delta_{w}
and
\varphi_{w}(w)=0
.

Proposition 8.5
.

If
\dim X=1
and
[\omega]
is a rational class, then
\varphi_{w}\in\PL(X)
.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition 3.3.7 in
[

]
, and can also be deduced from properties of the intersection form on

for any snc model
\mathcal{X}
, as in

, Theorem 7.17]

∎

This proves part (i) of Theorem A in the introduction. We will prove (ii) in §

8.6.
Invariance under retraction

It will be convenient to introduce the following terminology:

Definition 8.6
.

We say that a function
\varphi
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
is
invariant under retraction
if
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
for some (and hence any sufficiently high) snc model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
.

Example 8.7
.

By (
8.2
) and (
8.3
), a function
\varphi\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
lies in
\PL(X)
(resp.
\RPL(X)
) iff
\varphi
is invariant under retraction and restricts to a
\mathbb{Q}
-PL (resp.
\mathbb{R}
-PL) function on the dual complex associated to any (equivalently, any sufficiently high) snc model.

Remark 8.8
.

The condition
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
in Definition
8.6
is stronger than the ‘comparison property’ of
[
Li20
, Definition 3.11]
, which merely requires
\varphi=\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
to hold on the preimage under
p_{\mathcal{X}}
of the
n
-dimensional open faces of some dual complex
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
,
i.e.
the preimage of the
0
-dimensional strata of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
under the reduction map.

Proposition 8.9
.

If
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
is invariant under retraction, then
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
, and
\MA(\varphi)
is supported in some dual complex.

The first point is a direct consequence of Lemma
8.3
, while the second one is a special case of the following more precise result. Recall first that the
\omega
-psh envelope
of
f\in\Cz(X^{\mathrm{an}})
is defined as

\env(f)=\env_{\omega}(f):=\sup\{\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)\mid\varphi\leq f\}.

By
[
BFJ16
]
, it lies in
\CPSH(\omega)
.

Theorem 8.10
.

For any
\varphi\in\CPSH(\omega)
and any snc model
\mathcal{X}
on which
\omega
is determined, the following properties are equivalent:

(i)

\MA(\varphi)
is supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
;

(ii)

\varphi=\env(\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}})
.

Proof.

For any
\psi\in\PSH(\omega)
, we have
\psi\leq\psi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}}
(see Lemma
8.3
(iii)), and hence

(8.5)

\env(\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}})=\sup\left\{\psi\in\PSH(\omega)\mid\psi\leq\varphi\text{ on }\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}\right\}.

Assume (i). By the domination principle (see
[
BFJ15
, Lemma 8.4]
), any
\psi\in\PSH(\omega)
such that
\psi\leq\varphi
on
\supp\MA(\varphi)\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
satisfies
\psi\leq\varphi
on
X
. In view of (
8.5
) this yields (ii). Conversely, assume (ii). For any finite set of rational points
\Sigma\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Q})\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}
, consider the envelope

\varphi_{\Sigma}:=\sup\{\psi\in\PSH(\omega)\mid\psi\leq\varphi\text{ on }\Sigma\}.

Then
\varphi_{\Sigma}
lies in
\CPSH(\omega)
, and
\MA(\varphi_{\Sigma})
is supported in
\Sigma
(see
[
BFJ15
, Lemma 8.5]
). The net
(\varphi_{\Sigma})
, indexed by the filtered poset of finite subsets
\Sigma\subset\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Q})
, is clearly decreasing, and bounded below by
\varphi
. Its limit
\psi:=\lim_{\Sigma}\varphi_{\Sigma}
is thus
\omega
-psh, and we claim that it coincides with
\varphi
. Indeed, we have
\psi\leq f
on
\bigcup_{\Sigma}\Sigma=\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}(\mathbb{Q})
, and hence on
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
, where both
\psi
and
\varphi
are continuous. By (
8.5
), this yields
\psi\leq\env(\varphi\circ p_{\mathcal{X}})=\varphi
. By continuity of the Monge–Ampère operator along decreasing nets, we infer
\MA(\varphi_{\Sigma})\to\MA(\varphi)
weakly on
X
, which yields (i) since each
\MA(\varphi_{\Sigma})
is supported in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
.
∎

8.7.
The center of a plurisubharmonic function

We end this section by a version of Theorem
3.5
. In analogy with (
3.1
), for any subset
S\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
and any model
\mathcal{X}
we set

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S):=\bigcup_{w\in S}Z_{\mathcal{X}}(y).

This is thus the smallest subset of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
that is invariant under specialization and contains the image
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(S)
of
S
under the reduction map
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
. For any higher model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, the induced proper morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
maps
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(S)
onto
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S)
.

We say that
S\subset X
is
invariant under retraction
if
p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(S)=S
for some (and hence any sufficiently high) snc model
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 8.15
.

If
S\subset X^{\mathrm{an}}
is invariant under retraction, then
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S)
is Zariski closed for any model
\mathcal{X}
.

Proof.

Pick an snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
dominating
\mathcal{X}
such that
S=p_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}^{-1}(S)
. Since
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S)
is the image of
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(S)
under the proper morphism
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
, we may replace
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and assume without loss that
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
. The set
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S)
obviously contains
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
, which is Zariski closed since
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
is a stratum of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
for any
w\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. Conversely, pick
w\in S
, and set
y:=p_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. Then
y\in p_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(S)=S
, and
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\subset Z_{\mathcal{X}}(y)
since it follows from the definition of
p_{\mathcal{X}}
that
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
is a specialization of
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(y)
. This shows, as desired, that
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S)=Z_{\mathcal{X}}(S\cap\Delta_{\mathcal{X}})
is Zariski closed.
∎

Definition 8.16
.

Given
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
and a model
\mathcal{X}
, we define the
center of
\varphi
on
\mathcal{X}
as

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi):=Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\})=\bigcup\{Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)\mid w\in X,\,\varphi(w)<\sup\varphi\}.

Example 8.17
.

If
\varphi=\log|\mathfrak{a}|
for a vertical ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}}
, then
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)=V(\mathfrak{a})
.

Theorem 8.18
.

For any
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
and any model
\mathcal{X}
, the following holds:

(i)

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)
is an at most countable union of subvarieties of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
;

(ii)

if
\varphi
is invariant under retraction, then
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)
is Zariski closed;

(iii)

Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)=\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\})
;

(iv)

if
\mathcal{X}
determines
\omega
, then
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)
is a strict subset of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.

Question 8.19
.

Is it true that
\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}=\redu_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi))
as in Theorem
3.5
?

Proof.

By
[
BFJ15
, Proposition 4.7]
,
\varphi
can be be written as the pointwise limit of a decreasing sequence
(\varphi_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\omega
-psh PL functions. Since each
\varphi_{m}
is in particular invariant under retraction (see Example
8.7
), Lemma
8.15
implies that
Z_{\mathcal{X}}\{(\varphi_{m}<\sup\varphi\})
is Zariski closed for each
m
. On the other hand, since
\varphi_{m}\searrow\varphi
pointwise on
X
, we have
\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}=\bigcup_{m}\{\varphi_{m}<\sup\varphi\}
, and hence
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)=\bigcup_{m}Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\{\varphi_{m}<\sup\varphi\})
. This proves (i), while (ii) is a direct consequence of Lemma
8.15
.

Pick
w\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
\varphi(w)<\sup\varphi
. To prove (iii), we need to show that any
\xi\in Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
lies in
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\})
. By Lemma
8.3
, we can find a high enough snc model
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
such that
x^{\prime}:=p_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(w)
satisfies
\varphi(x^{\prime})<\sup\varphi
. By properness of
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{0}\to\mathcal{X}_{0}
,
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
is the image of
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(w)
, which is itself contained in
Z_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}(x^{\prime})
. After replacing
\mathcal{X}
with
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and
x
with
x^{\prime}
, we may thus assume without loss that
\mathcal{X}
is snc and
x
lies in
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
. Pick
y\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
with
\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(y)=\xi
(which exists by surjectivity of the reduction map, see
[
Gub13
, Lemma 4.12]
). Set
y^{\prime}:=p_{\mathcal{X}}(y)
, and denote by
\sigma
the unique face of
\Delta_{\mathcal{X}}
that contains
y^{\prime}
in its relative interior, the corresponding stratum of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
being the smallest one containing
\xi
. Since the latter lies on the stratum
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(w)
, it follows that
\sigma
contains
x
(possibly on its boundary). Since
\varphi
is convex and continuous on
\sigma
(see Lemma
8.3
), it can only achieve its supremum at the interior point
y^{\prime}
if it is constant on
\sigma
. As
w\in\sigma
satisfies
\varphi(w)<\sup\varphi
, it follows that
\varphi(y^{\prime})<\sup\varphi
as well. Since
y^{\prime}=p_{\mathcal{X}}(y)
, this implies
\varphi(y)\leq\varphi(y^{\prime})<\sup\varphi
(again by Lemma
8.3
). Thus
\xi=\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(y)\in\redu_{\mathcal{X}}(\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\})
, which proves (iii).

Finally, assume that
\mathcal{X}
is normal and determines
\omega
. By (
8.4
), we can find an irreducible component
E
of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
whose corresponding Shilov point
w_{E}\in\Gamma_{\mathcal{X}}
satisfies
\varphi(w_{E})=\sup\varphi
. Since
w_{E}
is the only point of
X^{\mathrm{an}}
whose reduction on
\mathcal{X}_{0}
is the generic point of
E
, it follows that the latter does not belong to
Z_{\mathcal{X}}(\varphi)
, which is thus a strict subset of
\mathcal{X}_{0}
.
∎

9.
The isotrivial case

We now consider the
isotrivial
case, in which the variety over
K=k(\!(\varpi)\!)
is the base change
X_{K}
of a smooth projective variety
X
over the (trivially valued) field
k
.

9.1.
Ground field extension

We have a natural projection

\pi\colon X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}},

while Gauss extension provides a continuous section

\sigma\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\hookrightarrow X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}

onto the set of
k^{\times}
-invariant points (see
[
BoJ18a
, Proposition 1.6]
). By
[
BoJ18a
, Corollary 1.5]
, we further have:

Lemma 9.1
.

If
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
is divisorial (resp real divisorial) then
\sigma(v)\in X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
is divisorial (resp. quasimonomial).

The base change of
X
to
K^{\circ}
defines the
trivial model

\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}:=X_{K^{\circ}}

of
X_{K}
, whose central fiber will be identified with
X
. More generally, each
test configuration

\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
for
X
induces via base change
\Spec K^{\circ}=\Spec k[\![\varpi]\!]\to\mathbb{A}^{1}=\Spec k[\varpi]
a
k^{\times}
-invariant model of
X_{K}
, that shares the same vertical ideals and vertical divisors as
\mathcal{X}
, and will be simply be denoted by
\mathcal{X}
, for simplicity.

9.2.
Psh functions

For any
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, we denote by
\pi^{\star}\theta\in\mathcal{Z}^{1,1}(X_{K})
the induced closed
(1,1)
-form, determined by
\theta
on the trivial model. If
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, then
[\pi^{\star}\omega]\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X_{K})
coincides with the base change of
\omega
, and hence is ample.

Theorem 9.2
.

Pick
\omega\in\Amp(X)
and
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
. Then:

(i)

\pi^{\star}\varphi\in\PSH(\pi^{\star}\omega)
;

(ii)

if
\varphi
is further continuous, then
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)=\sigma_{\star}\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
.

Lemma 9.3
.

For any
\varphi\in\PL(X)
and
\theta\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
, the following holds:

(i)

\pi^{\star}\varphi\in\PL(X_{K})
;

(ii)

(\pi^{\star}\theta+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\pi^{\star}\varphi)^{n}=\sigma_{\star}(\theta+\operatorname{dd^{c}}\!\varphi)^{n}
;

(iii)

\varphi
is
\theta
-psh iff
\pi^{\star}\varphi
is
\pi^{\star}\theta
-psh.

Proof.

The function
\varphi
is determined by a vertical
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on a test configuration
\mathcal{X}
, that may be taken to dominate the trivial one (see
[
BoJ22b
, Theorem 2.7]
). The induced vertical divisor on the induced model of
X_{K}
then determines
\pi^{\star}\varphi
. This proves (i), and also (ii), by comparing
[
BFJ15
, (2.2)]
and
[
BoJ22b
, (3.6)]
. Finally, denote by
\theta_{\mathcal{X}}
the pullback of
\theta
to
\operatorname{N^{1}}(\mathcal{X}/\mathbb{A}^{1})
. Then
\varphi
is
\theta
-psh iff
(\theta_{\mathcal{X}}+[D])|_{\mathcal{X}_{0}}
is nef, which is also equivalent to
\pi^{\star}\varphi
being
\pi^{\star}\theta
-psh. This proves (iii).
∎

Proof of Theorem
9.2
.

Write
\varphi
as the limit on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
of a decreasing net of
\omega
-psh PL functions
\varphi_{i}
. By Lemma
9.3
,
\pi^{\star}\varphi_{i}
is PL and
\pi^{\star}\omega
-psh. Since it decreases pointwise on
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
to
\pi^{\star}\varphi
, the latter is
\pi^{\star}\omega
-psh, which proves (i). For each
i
, Lemma
9.3
(ii) further implies
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi_{i})=\sigma_{\star}\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})
. If
\varphi
is continuous, then
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi)
and
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)
are both defined, and are the limits of
\MA_{\omega}(\varphi_{i})
and
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi_{i})
, respectively. This proves (ii).
∎

9.3.
PL structures

As a direct consequence of Lemma
9.3
, the projection
\pi\colon X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}\to X^{\mathrm{an}}
is compatible with the PL structures:

Corollary 9.4
.

We have
\pi^{\star}\PL(X)\subset\PL(X_{K})
and
\pi^{\star}\RPL(X)\subset\RPL(X_{K})
.

As we next show, this is also the case for Gauss extension.

Theorem 9.5
.

We have
\sigma^{\star}\PL(X_{K})=\PL(X)
and
\sigma^{\star}\RPL(X_{K})=\RPL(X)
.

Any vertical ideal
\mathfrak{a}
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}
, being trivial outside the central fiber, can be viewed as a vertical ideal on
X\times\mathbb{A}^{1}
, and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\cdot\mathfrak{a}
is then the smallest flag ideal containing
\mathfrak{a}
.

Lemma 9.6
.

With the above notation we have
\sigma^{\star}\log|\mathfrak{a}|=\varphi_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}}
.

Proof.

Pick an ample line bundle
L
on
X
, and denote by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{triv}}
the trivial model of
L_{K}
, i.e. the pullback of
L
to the trivial model
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}=X_{K^{\circ}}
. After replacing
L
with a large enough multiple, we may assume
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{triv}}\otimes\mathfrak{a}
is generated by finitely many sections
s_{i}\in\Hnot(\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}},\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{triv}})
. Then
\log|\mathfrak{a}|=\max_{i}\log|s_{i}|
, where
|s_{i}|
denotes the pointwise length of
s_{i}
in the model metric induced by
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{triv}}
. For each
i
write

s_{i}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}s_{i,\lambda}\varpi^{\lambda}
where
s_{i,\lambda}\in\Hnot(X,L)
, and denote by
\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
the ideal locally generated by
(s_{i,\lambda})_{i}
. Then
\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}\varpi^{\lambda}
. By definition of Gauss extension, we have for any
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}

\log|s_{i}|(\sigma(v))=\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\log|s_{i,\lambda}|+\lambda\}.

Thus
\sigma^{\star}\log|\mathfrak{a}|=\max_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\{\psi_{\lambda}-\lambda\}
with
\psi_{\lambda}:=\max_{i}\log|s_{i,\lambda}|=\log|\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}|
, and hence
\sigma^{\star}\log|\mathfrak{a}|=\max_{\lambda}\{\log|\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}|-\lambda\}=\varphi_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{a}}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
9.5
.

By Corollary
9.4
we have
\pi^{\star}\PL(X)\subset\PL(X_{K})
.
Since
\PL(X_{K})
is generated by functions of the form
\log|\mathfrak{a}|
for a vertical ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}
, Lemma
9.6
yields
\sigma^{\star}\PL(X_{K})\subset\PL(X)
, and hence also
\sigma^{\star}\RPL(X_{K})\subset\RPL(X)
. This completes the proof, since
\sigma^{\star}\pi^{\star}=\id
.
∎

9.4.
Centers

Next we study the relationships between the two center maps
Z_{X}\colon X^{\mathrm{an}}\to X
and
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}\colon X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}\to\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv},0}=X
.

Lemma 9.7
.

For all
w\in X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
and
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
we have

Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(w)\subset Z_{X}(\pi(w)),\quad Z_{X}(v)=Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\sigma(v)).

Proof.

Denote by
\mathfrak{b}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
the ideal of the subvariety
Z_{X}(\pi(w))
. Then
\mathfrak{a}:=\mathfrak{b}+(\varpi)
is a vertical ideal on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}
such that
V(\mathfrak{a})=V(\mathfrak{b})=Z_{X}(\pi(w))
under the identification
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv},0}=X
. Further,

\log|\mathfrak{a}|(w)=\max\{\log|\mathfrak{b}|(\pi(w)),-1\}<0,

and hence
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(w)\subset V(\mathfrak{a})=Z_{X}(\pi(w))
, see (
8.1
).

In particular,
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\sigma(v))\subset Z_{X}(v)
.
Conversely, denote by
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}
the ideal of
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\sigma(v))
. Since
\sigma(v)
is
k^{\times}
-invariant,
\mathfrak{a}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{a}_{\lambda}\varpi^{-\lambda}
is (induced by) a flag ideal. Further,
\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}}(v)=\log|\mathfrak{a}|(\sigma(v))<0
, and hence
Z_{X}(v)\subset Z_{X}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}})
. By Example
1.14
we have
Z_{X}(\varphi_{\mathfrak{a}})=V(\mathfrak{a}_{0})
. The latter is also equal to the zero locus of
\mathfrak{a}_{0}+(\varpi)
on
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}
, which is contained in
V(\mathfrak{a})=Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\sigma(v))
since
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{a}_{0}+(\varpi)
. Thus
Z_{X}(v)\subset Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\sigma(v))
, which concludes the proof.
∎

Proposition 9.8
.

If
\omega\in\Amp(X)
and
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
, then

Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)=Z_{X}(\varphi)
.

Proof.

Pick
v\in X^{\mathrm{an}}
such that
\varphi(v)<\sup\varphi
, and set
w:=\sigma(v)
. Then
\pi^{\star}\varphi(w)=\varphi(v)
and
\sup\pi^{\star}\varphi=\sup\varphi
, so
w
lies in
\{\pi^{\star}\varphi<\sup\pi^{\star}\varphi\}
, and hence
Z_{X}(v)=Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(w)\subset Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)
by Lemma
9.7
. This implies
Z_{X}(\varphi)\subset Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)
. Conversely, assume
w\in X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
satisfies
\pi^{\star}\varphi(w)<\sup\pi^{\star}\varphi
. Then
v:=\pi(w)
lies in
\{\varphi<\sup\varphi\}
, and hence
Z_{X}(v)\subset Z_{X}(\varphi)
. In view of Lemma
9.7
, this implies
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(w)\subset Z_{X}(\varphi)
, and hence
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\pi^{\star}\varphi)\subset Z_{X}(\varphi)
. ∎

Combining Proposition
9.8
and Theorem
8.18
, we obtain

Corollary 9.9
.

Let
\varphi\in\PSH(\omega)
, where
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, and suppose that
\pi^{\star}\varphi\in\PSH(\pi^{\star}\omega)
is invariant under retraction. Then
Z_{X}(\varphi)\subset X
is a Zariski closed proper subset of
X
.

9.5.
Examples

We are now ready to prove Theorems A and B in the introduction, and also provide additional examples. As in the previous section,
X
denotes a smooth projective variety over
k
. Pick a class
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, a
k^{\times}
-invariant divisorial point
w\in X_{K}^{\mathrm{div}}
, and denote as in §
8.5
by
\varphi_{w}\in\CPSH(\pi^{\star}\omega)
the Green’s function associated to
w
; this is the unique solution to the Monge–Ampère equation

\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\varphi_{w})=\delta_{w}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{w}(w)=0.

By Lemma
9.1
, we have
w=\sigma(v)
with
v:=\pi(w)\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
. If
\varphi_{v}\in\CPSH(\omega)
denotes the Green’s function of
\{v\}
, see §
6.1
, then we have

\varphi_{w}=\pi^{\star}\varphi_{v}.

Indeed,
\pi^{\star}\varphi_{v}(w)=\varphi_{v}(v)=0
, and by Theorem
9.2
, we have
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\pi^{\star}\varphi_{v})=\sigma_{\star}\delta_{v}=\delta_{w}
.

Our goal is to investigate the regularity of
\varphi_{w}
.

Corollary 9.10
.

If
\dim X=1
, then
\varphi_{w}\in\PL(X_{K})
. If
\dim X=2
, then
\varphi_{w}\in\RPL(X_{K})
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from Proposition
8.5
. Now suppose
\dim X=2
.
By Theorem
6.10
,
\varphi_{v}\in\RPL(X)
, so that
\varphi_{w}\in\RPL(X_{K})
, see Corollary
9.4
.
∎

However, even when
\omega
is rational,
\varphi_{w}
is in general not
\mathbb{Q}
-PL:

Example 9.11
.

Example
7.2
gives an example of an abelian surface
X
, a rational class
\omega\in\Amp(X)
, and a divisorial valuation
v\in X^{\mathrm{div}}
such that
\varphi_{v}\in\RPL(X)\setminus\PL(X)
. If
w=\sigma(v)
, then
\varphi_{w}:=\pi^{\star}\varphi_{v}\in\RPL(X_{K})\setminus\PL(X_{K})
, by Theorem
9.5
.

Example 9.12
.

Similarly, Example
7.4
gives an example of a divisorial valuation
v\in\mathbb{P}^{3,\mathrm{div}}
such that if we set
\omega=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(4))
, then
\varphi_{v}:=\varphi_{\omega,v}\in\RPL(X)\setminus\PL(X)
. If
w=\sigma(v)
, then
\varphi_{w}:=\pi^{\star}\varphi_{v}\in\RPL(X_{K})\setminus\PL(X_{K})

.

Examples
9.11
and
9.12
establish Theorem A (ii). They also provide a negative answer to Question
8.12
(ii). Indeed, a function
\varphi\in\Cz(X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}})
lies in
\RPL(X_{K})
(resp.
\PL(X_{K})

\varphi
is invariant under retraction and restricts to an
\mathbb{R}
-PL (resp.
\mathbb{Q}
-PL) function on each dual complex, see Example
8.7
.

As the next example shows, if
\dim X=3
, then
\varphi_{w}
need not be
\mathbb{R}
-PL. In fact, it may not even be invariant under retraction.

Example 9.13
.

Example
7.6
shows that we may have
\dim X=3
and
Z_{X}(\varphi_{v})
Zariski dense in
X
. It follows that
Z_{\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv}}}(\varphi_{w})
is Zariski dense in
\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{triv},0}=X
, see
Theorem
9.2
(ii). Thus Theorem
8.18
(ii) shows that
\varphi_{w}
cannot be invariant under retraction.

It could, however, a priori be the case that the restriction
\varphi_{w}
to any dual complex is
\mathbb{R}
-PL, see Question
8.12
(i).

In Example
9.13
, based on Lesieutre’s work, the class
\omega
is irrational. We do not know of an example for which the class
\omega
is rational. However, the following example provides a proof of Theorem B in the introduction.

Example 9.14
.

Set
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
and
\omega:=c_{1}(\mathcal{O}(1))\in\operatorname{N^{1}}(X)
.
By Proposition
7.7
, there exists
\psi\in\CPSH(\omega)
such that
\MA_{\omega}(\psi)
is supported in a finite subset
\Sigma\subset X^{\mathrm{div}}_{\mathbb{R}}
, and
Z_{X}(\psi)
is Zariski dense in
X
.
Theorem
9.2
then shows that
\varphi:=\pi^{\star}\psi
lies in
\CPSH(\pi^{\star}\omega)
,
\MA_{\pi^{\star}\omega}(\varphi)=\sigma_{\star}\MA_{\omega}(\psi)
has finite support in some dual complex (see Lemma
9.1
), and the center of
\varphi
on the trivial model of
X_{K}^{\mathrm{an}}
is Zariski dense. By Theorem
8.18
, it follows that
\varphi
cannot be invariant under retraction.

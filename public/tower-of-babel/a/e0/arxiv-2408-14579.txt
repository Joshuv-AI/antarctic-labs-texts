Galilei covariance of the theory of Thouless pumps
Tilman Esslinger, Gian Michele Graf, Filippo Santi

Abstract
The Thouless theory of quantum pumps establishes the conditions for quantized particle transport per cycle, and determines its value. When describing the pump from a moving reference frame, transported and existing charges transform, though not independently. This transformation is inherent to Galilean space and time, but it is underpinned by a transformation of vector bundles. Different formalisms can be used to describe this transformation, including one based on Bloch theory. Depending on the chosen formalism, the two types of charges will be realized as indices of either the same or different kinds. Finally, we apply the bulk-edge correspondence principle, so as to implement the transformation law within Büttiker’s scattering theory of quantum pumps.



1
Introduction

A quantum pump can be modeled in terms of a 1-dimensional Fermi gas moving in a periodic potential that is periodic in time and, quite conveniently but not necessarily, in space as well. Thouless showed
[
19
]
that charge transport is quantized, provided the Fermi level remains in a gap throughout the pumping cycle. The system is supposed to depend parametrically on physical time
t
, albeit slowly, so that it can be described in the adiabatic approximation. Very controlled Thouless pumping has recently been realized in experiments with ultracold atoms using dynamically controlled superlattice potentials from interfering laser beams, where the drift of the atomic cloud as a result of the pumping was observed and the topological invariance could be extracted
[
15
,
12
,
4
]
. Beyond this, the robustness against disorder
[
14
]
, the effects of atom-atom interactions
[
20
]
, as well as a drift reversal in the presence of an external trapping potential
[
21
]
have been observed.

Realizations of Thouless pumps that are based on solid state devices are naturally described in their rest frame. By contrast, potentials that are shaped by laser beams through their changing interference patterns do not single out any particular frame of reference. To illustrate the idea, consider two superimposed optical lattices,
creating the potential

V(x,t)=\sum_{i=1,2}V_{i}\sin^{2}(k_{i}x-\omega_{i}t)\,,

where
k_{1}
,
k_{2}
are commensurate. In a reference frame having
relative velocity
v
, the two
frequencies are
\hat{\omega}_{i}=\omega_{i}-k_{i}v
. By suitable choice
of
v
, one can make either frequency vanish, but not both, as a rule. Such conditions call for the covariance of the theory of Thouless pumps w.r.t. Galilei transformations. Quite intuitively though, time periodicity could be lost in a generic moving frame and is preserved only for specific velocities between frames.

Let us consider the family of Hamiltonians on the real line

H(s)=p^{2}+V(x,s)\,,

(1.1)

smoothly parametrized by the adiabatic time
s=\varepsilon t
, (
\varepsilon\ll 1
), and where the potential
V(x,s)=V(x,s)^{*}
has the stated periodicities:

\displaystyle V(x,s+T)

\displaystyle=V(x,s)\,,

(1.2)

\displaystyle V(x+L,s)

\displaystyle=V(x,s)\,.

(1.3)

There is room in (

The charge
Q
transported during a period
T
across a fixed fiducial point can be expressed in topological terms. Indeed
[
19
]
, it is equal to the Chern number,

Q=\operatorname{ch}P\,,\qquad\operatorname{ch}P\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\oint ds\oint dk\operatorname{tr}(P[\partial_{s}P,\partial_{k}P])\,,

(1.4)

where
s
and
k
run over a time period and the Brillouin zone (a circle), respectively; and

is the projection onto the states below the Fermi level, inducing projections
P(s,k)
. More conveniently,

should be viewed as a vector bundle over a 2-torus (the Fermi bundle) arising as a subbundle
P\subset E

, as will be explained in

2

refers to the Bloch trivialization of

. Another expression for

, not relying on any trivialization, is

\operatorname{ch}P=\frac{1}{2\pi}\oint ds\oint dk\operatorname{tr}(P[[P,x],\partial_{s}P])\,,

(1.5)

where all operators under the trace, except for
x
, but including
[P,x]
, act fiberwise on the fibers of
P
.

Less surprisingly, another physical quantity that is expressed in topological terms, besides

, is the charge residing in a spatial period
L
, i.e. the number of occupied bands. It equals the rank of
P
,

N=\operatorname{rk}P\,,\qquad\operatorname{rk}P=\dim P_{s,k}\,,\qquad(\text{any }s,k)\,.

(1.6)

Let us point out similarities and differences between the
transported
and the
existing charges
,
Q
and
N
. For one thing,
Q
is based on a temporal period and on a fixed fiducial point in space. Likewise,
N
is based on a spatial period, viewed at a fixed time. Any displacement of the temporal or spatial period or of the reference points in space or time does not affect either
Q
or
N
. Still, the two corresponding indices,
\operatorname{ch}P
and
\operatorname{rk}P
, are of a different kind, in that only the latter can be determined from a single fiber of the bundle. They are referred to as
strong
and
weak
indices of the problem at hand, following the general terminology, cf. e.g.
[
7
]
. More generally, restricting the base space, e.g. the 2-torus, to one of smaller dimension
d
induces a restriction of the vector bundle, thereby giving rise to weak indices. Here,
\operatorname{rk}P
arises in
d=0
.

The main result, to be presented in the next section, is that the indices mix under Galilei transformations in a way that establishes the covariance of the theory.

The structure of the article is as follows. In
Sect.

2
we will address Galilean relativity for various purposes, such as the transformation of transported and existing charges as it is brought about by classical considerations, but also and foremost about how those transformations are embodied in the Thouless theory of pumps; in particular, how the above bundles transform. We will moreover give two independent proofs of the main result, based on
Eqs.

1.4
and
1.5
, respectively. In
Sect.

3

and
N
will be realized by indices of the same kind, and indeed as Chern numbers of two 2-dimensional subtori of the same 3-dimensional torus. In
Sect.

4
, we will consider a truncated system and relate some invariants pertaining to the scattering problem at the edge of the pump to the indices defined in
Sect.

3
. This correspondence will provide a new proof of Sturm’s oscillation theorem. Appendices are devoted to technical results needed in
Sect.

3
.

2
Galilei covariance

The Galilei transformation (boost)

\hat{s}=s\,,\qquad\hat{x}=x-vs

(2.1)

relates a frame

\hat{F}
which moves at constant velocity

\hat{V}(\hat{x},s)=V(x,s)

iff
(v\hat{T},\hat{T})

and

.
∎

In conclusion,

\hat{T}=nT\,,\qquad\hat{L}=mL

The covariance can be addressed from two points of view. The first one is (non-relativistic) electrodynamics. The charge and current densities transform as

\hat{\rho}=\rho\,,\qquad\hat{\jmath}=j-\rho v\,,

(2.6)

where

\hat{\jmath}

They will be commented upon later, including their transformation law, which for
\rho
and

\hat{N}=mN\,,\qquad\hat{Q}=nQ-mN

(2.7)

in the general case (
2.3

\hat{N}=N\,,\qquad\hat{Q}=Q-N

in the simpler but not more restrictive case (
2.5

\mathcal{T}_{a}:E_{s,k}\rightarrow E_{s,k}\,,\qquad(a\in\mathbb{R})\,;

with the typical fiber

\mathrm{i}[P,x]=\partial_{k}P\,.

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi x}:E_{s,k}\equiv F\rightarrow E_{s,k+\varphi}\equiv F

First of all, the Fermi projection

Remark

(Gauss-Codazzi)
.

Let
E\rightarrow M
be a Hermitian vector bundle; let

denote a subbundle, as well as the corresponding orthogonal projection; let
\nabla
be a Hermitian connection on

\nabla^{(P)}=P\nabla P
be the induced connection; let

\nabla=\nabla^{(P)}+A
, i.e.

A=P^{\perp}\nabla P\,;

let finally

and

be the curvatures of
\nabla

\nabla^{(P)}
, respectively. They are related by the Gauss-Codazzi equation, cf.
[
11
, Eq. 1.6.12]

PRP=R^{(P)}-A^{*}\wedge A\,.

(2.29)

A similar construction, but devoid of base space

\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and

\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}\,,\qquad\psi\mapsto e^{Xt}\psi\,,\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

be the “parallel transport” generated by the anti-Hermitian operator

. Thus

\nabla_{X}\psi=X\psi

\begin{gathered}\nabla_{X}=\nabla_{X}^{(P)}+A(X)\,,\\

4
The scattering perspective

In this section we shall review the bulk-edge correspondence
[
2
]
for Thouless pumps, which relates their topological data
\operatorname{ch}P
and
\operatorname{rk}P
with scattering data. The latter data are at the core of the Büttiker theory
[
3
,
4
]
of quantum pumps, the scope of which is wider, in that pumps may be non-topological. The setup of that theory is that of a pump proper connected to any number of (semi-infinite) 1D channels, labelled
\alpha
and sharing a common Fermi level
\mu
. The pump is configured in terms of some external parameter
X
, that determine a scattering matrix
S=(S_{\alpha\beta})=S(X)
at energy
\mu
. As
X
changes slowly in time, a (net) current flows through the channels. Upon completing a cycle in
X
(or just in
S
), a net charge
Q_{\alpha}=(\mathrm{i}/2\pi)\oint((dS)S^{*})_{\alpha\alpha}
has left the pump through channel
\alpha
.

If the pump proper has a gap at the Fermi level, then
S
becomes block-diagonal w.r.t. groups
A
of
channels
\alpha
docking at the same point, provided different ones are widely separated. The block of any group stands for its (unitary) reflection matrix
R_{A}(X)
. Despite transmission being suppressed at fixed parameter
X
, transport remains possible when
X
is varied. Indeed,
Q_{A}=\sum_{\alpha\in A}Q_{\alpha}
equals the winding number of

Appendix A

Let us at first consider a trivial Hermitian vector bundle
E=M\times V
with base space
M
and finite-dimensional fiber
V
. Let
P:M\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(V)
be a smooth map that takes values in the orthogonal projections on
V
and let
P
also denote the subbundle
P\rightarrow M
of
E
having fibers
\operatorname{im}P\subset V
. Then
P
may be non-trivial. For
\operatorname{dim}M=2
its Chern number is

\operatorname{ch}P=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{M}\text{tr}(P(dP\wedge dP))\,.

(A.1)

The purpose of this appendix is (a) to extend the result to bundles that are not Hermitian and (b) to express their Chern numbers in terms of frames that are not orthogonal. Actually, (a) is evident, since the notion of trace rests on duality and not on an inner product, which can though be used to compute
Eq.

A.1
in terms of orthogonal frames.

We thus recall dual systems
(\widetilde{V},V,b)
, where
\widetilde{V}
and
V
are finite-dimensional vector spaces and
b:\widetilde{V}\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a non-degenerate bilinear form, meaning

\displaystyle b(\widetilde{v},v)=0\,,\enskip(v\in V)

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\enskip\widetilde{v}=0\,,

\displaystyle b(\widetilde{v},v)=0\,,\enskip(\widetilde{v}\in\widetilde{V})

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow\enskip v=0\,.

Thus,
b
induces an isomorphism

V^{*}\cong\widetilde{V}\,,

(A.2)

where
V^{*}
is the dual space of
V
. In particular,
\operatorname{dim}\widetilde{V}=\operatorname{dim}V
. Moreover, any subspace
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{V}
defines a subspace
\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}\subset V
by

\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}=\{v\in V\mid b(\widetilde{v}^{\prime},v)=0,\,(\widetilde{v}^{\prime}\in\widetilde{V}^{\prime})\}\,.

(A.3)

It has
\operatorname{dim}\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}=\operatorname{codim}\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
. Let
(\widetilde{V}^{\prime},V^{\prime},b)
be a dual subsystem of
(\widetilde{V},V,b)
, i.e.
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}\subset\widetilde{V}
and
V^{\prime}\subset V
be subspaces such that the restriction of
b
to
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}\times V^{\prime}
is non-degenerate. The dual subspace is uniquely characterized by a projection
P:V\rightarrow V
such that

\operatorname{im}P=V^{\prime}\,,\qquad\operatorname{ker}P=\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}\,.

The claim is a consequence of (i)
V^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}=\{0\}
, (ii)
V^{\prime}+\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}=V
, which are in turn seen as follows: (i) Any
v\in V^{\prime}\cap\widetilde{V}^{\prime\perp}
is
v=0
by (
A.3
) and the non-degeneracy of the restriction of
b
; (ii) then follows on grounds of dimension. As a map
P:V\rightarrow V
, the projection may be seen as a map
P\in V\otimes V^{*}\cong V\otimes\widetilde{V}
, where the isomorphism is by (
A.2
). In those terms,
P
can be constructed by means of a (non-unique) biorthogonal frame, i.e. bases

\widetilde{F}=(\widetilde{v}_{1},\dotsc,\widetilde{v}_{n}),\qquad F=(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})

(A.4)

of
\widetilde{V}^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}
, respectively, such that

b(\widetilde{v}_{i},v_{j})=\delta_{ij}\,.

(A.5)

Then
P
is given by

P=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\otimes\widetilde{v}_{i}\,.

Let next
\widetilde{E}=M\times\widetilde{V}
and
E=M\times V
be two trivial bundles and
(\widetilde{V},V,b)
be a dual system. Let
\widetilde{P}\rightarrow M
and
P\rightarrow M
be (possibly non-trivial) subbundles of
\widetilde{E}
and
E
, respectively, such that
b
remains fiberwise non-degenerate.

Let bases
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F}(\widetilde{p})
and

F=F(p)
of fibers
\widetilde{P}_{\widetilde{p}}
and
P_{p}
be given as in (
A.4
,
A.5
), locally in
\widetilde{p},p\in M
. Let then
b(\widetilde{F},F)
be a matrix of order
n
, defined (likewise locally) by

b(\widetilde{F}(\widetilde{p}),F(p))_{ij}=b(\widetilde{v}_{i}(\widetilde{p}),v_{j}(p))\,,\qquad(i,j=1,\dotsc,n;\enskip\widetilde{p},p\in M)\,.

We then have:

Proposition A.1
.

Let
\operatorname{dim}M=2
. The Chern number of

is given by
Eq.

A.1
as well as by

\operatorname{ch}P=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\pi}\int_{M}\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\widetilde{d}\wedge d\bigr)b(\widetilde{F},F)\Big|_{\widetilde{p}=p}\,.

(A.6)

The integrand is independent of bi-orthogonal frames on the intersection of patches. Hence it is globally defined.

It pays to spell out the integrand. We consider functions
f:M\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and their restriction to the diagonal submanifold
\{(\widetilde{p},p)\mid\widetilde{p}=p\}
. Also, given a vector field

on

and

Given a section

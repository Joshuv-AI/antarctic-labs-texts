Abstract
In this paper, we study the linear and nonlinear Schrödinger equations with a time-decaying harmonic oscillator and inverse-square potential. This model retains a form of scale invariance, and using this property, we demonstrate the asymptotic completeness of wave operators and Strichartz estimates for linear propagators.

2.1
Existence of propagators

We first demonstrate the existence of wave propagators. Given that
\sigma(t)>0
for any fixed
t
, we define

\displaystyle Q_{H_{0}(t)}(u,v):=(H_{0}(t)u,v),\quad u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n}),\quad H_{0}(t)=\frac{p^{2}}{2}+\frac{\sigma(t)x^{2}}{2}.

By the Friedrichs extension (see, for example, Theorem X.23. in
[
14
]
), the closure of
Q_{H_{0}(t)}
corresponds to the quadratic form of the self-adjoint operators
\hat{H}_{0}(t)
, each of which represents a unique positive extension of
H_{0}(t)
. Consequently we obtain a unique self-adjoint operator
\hat{H}_{0}(t)
with form domain
{\mathscr{D}}(Q_{\hat{H}_{0}(t)})=D_{+1}
. Next we consider the quadratic form

\displaystyle Q_{H(t)}(u,v)=\left(H(t)u,v\right),\quad u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n}).

(10)

By the Hardy inequality, we have

\displaystyle Q_{H(t)}(u,u)\leq\frac{\sigma(t)}{2}\|xu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and

\displaystyle Q_{H(t)}(u,u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma(t)}{2}\|xu\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\Lambda\||x|^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4\Lambda}{(n-2)^{2}}\right)\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma(t)}{2}\left\|xu\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\lambda_{0}}{(n-2)^{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma(t)}{2}\left\|xu\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

This implies that, for any fixed
t
, there exists
0<a_{0}<A_{0}
such that

\displaystyle a_{0}Q_{H_{0}(t)}(u,u)\leq Q_{H(t)}(u,u)\leq A_{0}Q_{H_{0}(t)}(u,u).

Because
C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
is dense in
D_{+1}\subset L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
, the closure of
Q_{H(t)}
corresponds to the quadratic form of the self-adjoint operator
\hat{H}(t)
, which represents a unique positive extension of
H(t)
. Consequently, we obtain a unique self-adjoint operator
\hat{H}(t)
with form domain

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}(Q_{\hat{H}(t)})

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,;\,\mbox{There exists}\ \{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\bf N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\mbox{such that}\ Q_{H(t)}(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m})\to 0\ \mbox{and}\ u_{n}\to u\ \mbox{as}\ n,m\to\infty\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,;\,\mbox{There exists}\ \{u_{n}\}_{n\in{\bf N}}\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\mbox{such that}\ Q_{H_{0}(t)}(u_{n}-u_{m},u_{n}-u_{m})\to 0\ \mbox{and}\ u_{n}\to u\ \mbox{as}\ n,m\to\infty\right\}

\displaystyle={\mathscr{D}}(Q_{\hat{H}_{0}(t)})=D_{+1}.

Therefore, we have the following lemma whenever
\lambda_{0}>0

Lemma 2.1

We have

(11)

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{{\cal Y}_{t}}/\|u\|_{{\cal Y}_{s}}\leq e^{c|t-s|},\quad\|u\|_{{\cal X}_{t}}/\|u\|_{{\cal X}_{s}}\leq e^{c|t-s|},\quad u\neq 0.

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\frac{\sigma(t)-\sigma(s)}{\sigma(s)(t-s)}\right|\left|\left((t-s)\sigma(s)|x|^{2}u,u\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\sigma(t)-\sigma(s)}{\sigma(s)(t-s)}\right||t-s|\left|Q_{H(s)}(u,u)\right|,

holds. This, together with Assumption

\displaystyle\left|Q_{H(t)}(u,u)\right|\leq\left(1+C|t-s|\right)|Q_{H(s)}(u,u)|\leq e^{C|t-s|}|Q_{H(s)}(u,u)|.

\displaystyle\left|Q_{H(t)^{-1}}(u,u)\right|\leq\left(1+C|t-s|\right)\left|Q_{H(s)^{-1}}(u,u)\right|\leq e^{C|t-s|}\left|Q_{H(s)^{-1}}(u,u)\right|.

∎

Reduction of propagators

\displaystyle\tilde{H}:=\frac{p^{2}}{2}-\Lambda|x|^{-2},\quad Q_{\tilde{H}}(u,v)=(\tilde{H}u,v),\quad u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n}).

Using the same arguments in the case of
H(t)

\tilde{H}

{\mathscr{D}}(Q_{H})=H^{1}:=\left\{u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\,|\,\|u\|_{H^{1}}:=\left\|(1+p^{2})^{1/2}u\right\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\right\}

-r_{0}>s\geq t
),

\displaystyle\tilde{U}(t,s):={\mathscr{J}}(t){\cal U}(t,s){\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(s),\quad{\mathscr{J}}(t):=e^{i\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{2\zeta(t)}x^{2}}e^{-i\left(\log\zeta(t)\right)A},\quad{\cal U}(t,s)=e^{-i\left(\int_{s}^{t}\zeta(\tau)^{-2}d\tau\right)H},

where
A=(x\cdot p+p\cdot x)/2
. We remark that

\displaystyle\left(e^{-i\left(\log\zeta(t)\right)A}\phi\right)(x)=\frac{1}{(\zeta(t))^{n/2}}\phi(x/\zeta(t))

and that
{\mathscr{J}}(t)C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})\subset C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
, which leads us to find on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{J}}(t)H{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(t)

\displaystyle=e^{i\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{2\zeta(t)}x^{2}}\zeta(t)^{2}\left(\frac{p^{2}}{2}-\frac{\Lambda}{|x|^{2}}\right)e^{-i\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{2\zeta(t)}x^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta(t)^{2}}{2}\left(p-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{\zeta(t)}x\right)^{2}-{\Lambda}{\zeta(t)^{2}}|x|^{-2}.

This result together with

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}{\mathscr{J}}(t)=\left(-\frac{\zeta^{\prime\prime}(t)\zeta(t)-(\zeta^{\prime}(t))^{2}}{2\zeta(t)^{2}}x^{2}+\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{2\zeta(t)}\left(x\cdot\left(p-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{\zeta(t)}x\right)+\left(p-\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{\zeta(t)}x\right)\cdot x\right)\right){\mathscr{J}}(t)

and

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}{\cal U}(t,s)=\frac{H}{\zeta(t)^{2}}{\cal U}(t,s),

we find that
i\partial_{t}\tilde{U}(t,s)=H(t)\tilde{U}(t,s)
holds on
C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
.
Unique existence of the propagator leads us to the following proposition:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
|t|\geq|s|\geq r_{0}
. Then the factorization of the propagator

\displaystyle U(t,s)={\mathscr{J}}(t)e^{-i\left(\int_{s}^{t}\zeta(\tau)^{-2}d\tau\right)H}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(s),\quad{\mathscr{J}}(t):=e^{i\frac{\zeta^{\prime}(t)}{2\zeta(t)}x^{2}}e^{-i\left(\log\zeta(t)\right)A}

holds. Moreover
U(t,s)D_{+1}\subset D_{+1}
holds by Theorem
1.4
.

We first establish the global-in-time Strichartz estimates for

, where (
7

Then it follows from Proposition

that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{r_{0}}^{t}U(t,s)F(s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}([r_{0},\infty);L^{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{r_{0}}^{t}{\mathscr{J}}(t)e^{-iH\int_{s}^{t}\zeta(\tau)^{-2}d\tau}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(s)F(s)ds\right\|_{L^{q}([r_{0},\infty);L^{r})}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{0}}^{\infty}\left\|\int_{r_{0}}^{t}{\mathscr{J}}(t)e^{-iH\int_{s}^{t}\zeta(\tau)^{-2}d\tau}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(s)F(s)ds\right\|_{r}^{q}dt

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\zeta(t)^{-qn(1/2-1/r)}\left\|\int_{0}^{a}e^{-i(a-b)H}{\mathscr{J}}^{\ast}(f^{-1}(b))F(f^{-1}(b))\zeta(s)^{2}db\right\|_{r}^{q}\zeta(t)^{2}da

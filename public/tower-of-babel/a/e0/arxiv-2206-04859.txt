Abstract
. We explore the behavior of the sectional genera of certain primary ideals in Noetherian local rings. In this paper, we provide characterizations of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring in terms of the sectional genera, the Cohen-Macaulay type, and the second Hilbert coefficients for certain primary ideals. We also characterize Gorenstein rings and quasi-Buchsbaum rings in terms of the sectional genera for certain primary ideals.



1.
Introduction

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a commutative Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal.
Let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of
R

of

with respect to

\displaystyle\ell_{R}(R/{I^{n+1}})=\mathrm{e}_{0}(I,R)\binom{n+d}{d}-\mathrm{e}_{1}(I,R)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}\mathrm{e}_{d}(I,R)

denotes the length of an
R
-module

. The leading coefficient

is called

of

with respect to

is named by W. V. Vasconcelos (
[

of

with respect to

In 1987, A. Ooishi (
[
19

for

controls the depth of the associated graded ring of

, and determines the Hilbert-Samuel function of

. Indeed, the results along this line of investigation can be found in, for example,
[
12
]
,
[
21
]
,
[

]
,
[
14
]
,
[
2
]
.
On the other hand, for non-Cohen-Macaulay local rings, not so much is known about the sectional genera. One progress is that S. Goto and K. Ozeki (
[
10
]
) gave a criterion for a certain equality of the sectional genera of parameter ideals for modules in 2016. On the other hand, even how the sectional genera characterizes the ring itself has not yet been investigated.

The purpose of our paper is to study the sectional genera of
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideals, provided
R
is unmixed and a homomorphic image of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. We especially focus our attention on

-parameter ideals. We note here only that the notion of

-parameter ideals is a special kind of parameter ideals of

, and
C
-parameter ideals always exist in our assumption. See Section

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem

,
3.5

Suppose that

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal of

\mathrm{e}_{j}(I,M)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}_{j}(I,M)&\text{if $0\leq j\leq s-2$,}\\

(see Proposition 22.6 in

(see Lemma 3.1 in
[

]
)

We summarize a way to compute the invariants introduced above.
Let

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that

is generalized Cohen-Macaulay with

The purpose of this paper is to provide characterizations of properties of rings (e.g., Cohen-Macaulay, Gorenstein, and quasi-Buchsbaum) via the invariants introduced in this section. 3.
The bound of the sectional genera

In this section, we study the bound of
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)
and
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)
, where
\mathfrak{q}
runs over all
C
-parameter ideals of
R
. We maintain the assumption of the beginning of Section
2
. The goal of this section is to provide a characterization of a Cohen–Macaulay ring in terms of its sectional genera.
We begin with the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
and
\mathfrak{q}
is a
C
-parameter ideal of
R
. Then we have

\displaystyle{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)-\mathcal{N}(R).

Proof.

Put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Since
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
, it follows from by Proposition 2.3 in
[
9
]
and Proposition 11.2.1 in
[
25
]
that
\mathrm{e}_{0}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)
. Since
\mathfrak{q}
is a
C
-parameter ideal, by the definition of the stable value, we have

\displaystyle{\rm sg}(J,R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell(R/J)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(J,R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)-\ell(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)-\mathcal{N}(R),

as required.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
\dim R=1
and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq 0.

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. This immediately follows from the assumption that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay by applying Lemma
3.1
.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
. Since
\dim R=1
,
R
is generalized Cohen-Macaulay. It follows from Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
that we have

-r_{0}(R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq 0.

Thus
r_{0}(R)=0
. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, as required.
∎

Note that in the case where
\dim R=1
, we have
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)=0
for every
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
by Lemma 3.1 in
[
11
]
. Therefore, we have
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)
for every
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
.
In the case where
\dim R\geq 2
, the following result shows that the inequality
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)
still holds true for every
C
-parameter ideal of
R
, provided
R
is unmixed.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
such that

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R).

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a
C
-parameter ideal of
R
and put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Since
R
is to be regular if
R
is unmixed and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)=1
(see
[
16
, Theorem 40.6]
), we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. This immediately follows from the assumption that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay by applying Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
3.1
.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
. Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
. Put
R_{i}=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,d-2
. By Lemma
2.8
, for all
i=1,\dots,d-2
, we have

(a)

JR_{i}=\mathfrak{q}R_{i}:\mathfrak{m}R_{i}
,

(b)

x_{i}
is a superficial element of
R_{i-1}
with respect to
\mathfrak{q}R_{i-1}
and
JR_{i-1}
,

(c)

\mathcal{N}_{R}(R_{i})=\mathcal{N}_{R}(R_{i-1}).

Now, we put
A=R_{d-2}
. By Lemma
2.1
we have
\mathrm{e}_{i}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{i}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)
and
\mathrm{e}_{i}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{i}(JA,A)=\mathrm{e}_{i}(\mathfrak{q}A:\mathfrak{m}A,A)
, for all
i=0,1
. Therefore
we have

(5)

\displaystyle{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\ell(A/\mathfrak{q}A)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)

and

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm sg}(J,R)&=\ell(R/J)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(J,R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)\\
&=\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)-\ell(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{q})\\
&=\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(J,R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\\
&=\ell(A/\mathfrak{q}A)-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(JA,A)-\mathcal{N}(A).\end{split}

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma
2.8

ii)
that
A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is unmixed with
\dim A=2
. Thus by Lemma
2.9

i)
,
A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay and so
A
is. Moreover, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m}A,A)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
. By Lemma
2.2
, (
5
), and (
6
), we obtain that

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm sg}(J,R)&=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(JA,A)-\mathcal{N}(A)=h_{0}(A)-r_{0}(A)-r_{1}(A)\quad\text{and}\\
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)&=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)=h_{0}(A).\end{split}

Since
{\rm sg}(J,R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R),
we have
r_{0}(A)=r_{1}(A)=0
. Therefore,
A
is Cohen–Macaulay. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay by applying Lemma
2.9
.
∎

For each integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
{\underline{x}}^{n}
the sequence
x^{n}_{1},x^{n}_{2},\ldots,x^{n}_{d}
. Let
K^{\bullet}(x^{n})
be the
Koszul complex of
R
generated by the sequence
{\underline{x}}^{n}
and let

H^{\bullet}({\underline{x}}^{n};R)=H^{\bullet}(\operatorname{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(K^{\bullet}({\underline{x}}^{n}),R))

be the Koszul cohomology module of
R
. Then for every
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, the family
\{H^{p}({\underline{x}}^{n};R)\}_{n\geq 1}

naturally forms an inductive system of
R
, whose limit

H^{p}_{\mathfrak{q}}(R)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}H^{p}({\underline{x}}^{n};R)

is isomorphic to the local cohomology module

H^{p}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}{\rm Ext}_{R}^{p}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{n},R).

For each
n\geq 1
and
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\phi^{p,n}_{{\underline{x}},R}:H^{p}({\underline{x}}^{n};R)\to H^{p}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
denote the canonical
homomorphism into the limit.

Definition 3.4

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.12]
)
.

There exists an integer
n_{0}

such that for all systems of parameters
{\underline{x}}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
for
R
contained in
\mathfrak{m}^{n_{0}}
and for all
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, the canonical homomorphisms

\phi^{p,1}_{{\underline{x}},R}:H^{p}({\underline{x}},R)\to H^{p}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)

into the inductive limit are surjective on the socles.
The least integer
n_{0}
with this property is called a
g-invariant
of
R
and denote by
\mathrm{g}(R)
.

The following result shows that
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)
and
{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)
are bounded below by the same finite value, where
\mathfrak{q}
runs over all
C
-parameters ideals of
R
contained in
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R).

iii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R).

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, by Lemma
2.2

ii
), we have

\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)=\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathrm{r}_{d}(R)=0

for all
C
-parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
.

ii)\Rightarrow iii)
follows from Theorem
3.3
.

iii)\Rightarrow i)
.
Since
R
is to be regular if
R
is unmixed and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)=1
(see
[
16
, Theorem 40.6]
), we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.
Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
and put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Now, we put
A=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})
. Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
(see (
7
)), one can show that

{\rm sg}(J,R)=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(JA,A)-\mathcal{N}(A)=h_{0}(A)-r_{0}(A)-r_{1}(A).

On the other hand, it follows from Lemma 3.5 in
[
20
]
and Lemma
2.8
that

\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\leq r_{2}(A)-\mathcal{N}(A)=-2r_{1}(A)-r_{0}(A).

Since
\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\geq{\rm sg}(J,R)
by hypothesis, we have
h_{0}(A)=r_{1}(A)=0
. Therefore,
A
is Cohen–Macaulay. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay because of Lemma
2.9
. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring with
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then for all
C
-parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
, we have

\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R).

Furthermore, each equality occurs if and only if
R
is Cohen-Macaulay.

Proof.

This is now immediately seen from Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.3
.
∎

The following consequence of Theorem
3.5
provides a characterization of Gorenstein rings.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Gorenstein.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq 1-\mathcal{N}(R).

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. Since
R
is Gorenstein, we get by Theorem
3.5
that

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R)=1-\mathcal{N}(R)

for all
C
-parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
of

.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
. We have

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq 1-\mathcal{N}(R)\leq\mathrm{r}(R)-\mathcal{N}(R).

Thus

is Cohen-Macaulay because of Theorem
3.5
. Moreover, we have
\mathrm{r}(R)=1
. Therefore,

is Gorenstein, and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
On the sectional genera and the second Hilbert coefficient

In this section, we will explore the relationship between the sectional genera and the second Hilbert coefficient.

Theorem 4.1
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R).

Proof.

Note that we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
since
R
is to be regular if
R
is unmixed and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)=1
(see
[
16
, Theorem 40.6]
).

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, by Lemma
2.2
, we get that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)=0.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
.
Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
and put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Note that
JR_{i}=\mathfrak{q}R_{i}:_{R_{i}}\mathfrak{m}R_{i}
, where
R_{i}=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,d-2
. Now, we put
A=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})
. We get
\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)
and
\mathrm{e}_{2}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(JA,A)
by Lemma
2.1
. Note that by Lemma
2.8

ii)
,
A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is unmixed of dimension
2
. Thus
A
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay because of Lemma
2.9
. Therefore, by Lemma
2.1
and
2.2
, we have

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2})=h_{0}(A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2})

and

\mathrm{e}_{2}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(JA,A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2})=h_{0}(A)-r_{1}(A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2}).

Since
\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)
, we have
r_{1}(A)=0
. Therefore,
A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is Cohen–Macaulay. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay by Lemma
2.9
. The proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R).

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R).

iv)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}
of
R
such that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R).

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
and
i)\Rightarrow iv)
are immediately seen from the assumption that
R
is Cohen-Macaulay and Lemma
2.2
.
Moreover
ii)\Rightarrow iii)
and
iv)\Rightarrow iii)
was established in Theorem
4.1
.
Now we show
iii)\Rightarrow i)
. Since
R
is to be regular if
R
is unmixed and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)=1
(see
[
16
, Theorem 40.6]
), we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.

Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
and put

. Set

. Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem

, one can show that

{\rm sg}(J,R)=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(JA,A)-\mathcal{N}(A),

On the other hand, by Lemma 3.5 in

, we have

. Since

Since

2.8
. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that

\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{\mathrm{g}(R)}

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\leq\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R)\leq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R).

Proof.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume that
R
is a non-regular unmixed local ring of dimension
d=\dim R\geq 2
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

i)

R
is Cohen–Macaulay.

ii)

There exists a
C
-parameter ideal
\mathfrak{q}
such that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R).

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. Since
R
is Cohen-Macaulay, by Lemma
2.2
we have
\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)=0.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
. Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, we obtain that
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.
Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
.
We put
A=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})
. By Lemma
2.8

ii)
,
A/H_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(A)
is unmixed with
\dim A=2
. Thus
A
is generalized Cohen–Macaulay because of Lemma
2.9
. Moreover, it is follows from Lemma
2.1
that
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m}A,A)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
. Note that
JR_{i}=\mathfrak{q}R_{i}:_{R_{i}}\mathfrak{m}R_{i}
, where
R_{i}=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})
for all
i=1,\ldots,d-2
. By Lemma
2.1
and
2.2
, we get that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(JA,A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2})=h_{0}(R)-r_{1}(A)-\ell((0):_{R_{d-3}}x_{d-2})

and

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)=h_{0}(R).

Since
\mathrm{e}_{2}(J,R)\geq{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)
, we have

h_{0}(R)=r_{1}(A)=0
. Therefore
A
is Cohen–Macaulay. Hence
R
is Cohen-Macaulay because of Lemma
2.8
. The proof is complete.
∎

For the reader’s convenience, we recall the notion of quasi-Buchsbaum modules. We say that an
R
-module
M
is said to be a
quasi-Buchsbaum module
if
\mathfrak{m}H^{i}_{\mathfrak{m}}(M)=0
for all
i<\dim M
.
With this notation, we are now in a position to prove the second main theorem in this study.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Since
R
is to be regular if
R
is unmixed and
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)=1
, we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{m},R)>1
.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
. Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a
C
-parameter ideal of
R
. By lemma
3.1
, we have

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)-\mathcal{N}(R).

Since
R
is quasi-Buchsbaum,
r_{i}(R)=h_{i}(R)
for all
i<d
. By Lemma
2.2
, we get that

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=-\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\binom{d-2}{j-1}h_{j}(R)=\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R).

ii)\Rightarrow i)
.
Let
\underline{x}=x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}
be a
C
-system of parameters of
R
such that
\mathfrak{q}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
and put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Now, we put
A=R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d-2})
.
Using similar arguments as in the proof of Theorem
3.3
, one can show that

{\rm sg}(J,R)=\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(JA,A)-\mathcal{N}(A)=-r_{1}(A)+h_{0}(R)-r_{0}(A)

and

\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}A,A)=-h_{1}(R).

Since
{\rm sg}(J,R)=\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R)
, we have

r_{1}(A)=h_{1}(R)
and so
h_{0}(R)=r_{0}(A)
. Thus
A
is quasi-Buchsbaum. It follows from a
C
-system of parameters forms a
d
-sequence of
R
and Theorem 3.6 in
[
24
]
that
R
is quasi-Buchsbaum, as required. ∎

We close this paper with the following example, which shows that Theorem
4.1
,
4.2
,
and
4.4
are not true without the assumption that
R
is unmixed.

Example 4.6
.

Let
S=k[[X,Y,Z,W]]
be the
formal power series ring over a field
k
. Put

R=S/[(X,Y,Z)\cap(W)]
. Then

i)

\dim R=3
and
R
is not unmixed. Hence,
R
is not a Cohen-Macaulay ring.

ii)

We have

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)

for all
C
-parameter ideals
\mathfrak{q}
in
R
.

Proof.

It is standard to check that
\dim R=3
and
R
is not unmixed. It follows that
R
is not a Cohen-Macaulay ring. We put
A=S/(W)
and
B=S/(X,Y,Z)
. Note that
A
and
B
are Gorenstein rings.

Let
\mathfrak{q}
be a
C
-parameter ideal of
R
and put
J=\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m}
. Then
J^{n}=\mathfrak{q}^{n}:\mathfrak{m}
for all
n\geq 0
(see
[
20
, Lemma 2.6]
). From the exact sequence
0\to B\to R\to A\to 0
and
A
is Cohen-Macaulay, we observe that

0\to B/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}B\to R/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}\to A/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}A\to 0.

By applying the functor

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m},-)
to the above exact sequence, by Lemma 2.6 in
[
20
]
, we obtain the following:

0\to[\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{B}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}B\to[\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}\to[\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{A}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}A\to 0

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\ell_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}(A/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}A)+\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}B)

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}(A/\mathfrak{q}A)\binom{n+3}{3}+\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}B)\binom{n+1}{1}

and

\displaystyle\ell_{R}([\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\ell_{R}([\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{A}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})+\ell_{R}([\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{B}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\binom{n+1}{2}+1

for all integers
n\geq 0
. Since
\ell_{R}(R/I^{n+1})=\ell_{R}(R/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})-\ell_{R}([\mathfrak{q}^{n+1}:_{R}\mathfrak{m}]/\mathfrak{q}^{n+1})
, we have
\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\ell_{R}(A/\mathfrak{q}A),\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R)=0,\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=1
and
\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}B)
. Thus we have

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}B).

On the other hand, it is easily observed from
0\to B\to R\to A\to 0
that
r_{0}(R)=r_{2}(R)=0
and
r_{1}(R)=r_{3}(R)=1
.
Since
R
is sequentially Cohen-Macaulay, it follows from Theorem 1.1 in
[
28
]
that
\mathcal{N}(R)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{3}r_{i}(R)=2
. Hence, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

{\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\ell(R/\mathfrak{q})-\mathrm{e}_{0}(\mathfrak{q},R)+\mathrm{e}_{1}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)-\mathcal{N}(R)=\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}B)-1.

Consequently, we obtain that

\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)=\mathrm{e}_{2}(\mathfrak{q},R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q}:\mathfrak{m},R)+\mathcal{N}(R)-\mathrm{r}(R)={\rm sg}(\mathfrak{q},R)=\ell_{R}(B/\mathfrak{q}B).

The proof is complete.
∎

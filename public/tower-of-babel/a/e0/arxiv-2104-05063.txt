Abstract
. In this paper we consider traces at initial times for functions with mixed time-space smoothness. Such results are often needed in the theory of evolution equations. Our result extends and unifies many previous results. Our main improvement is that we can allow general interpolation couples. The abstract results are applied to regularity problems for fractional evolution equations and stochastic evolution equation, where uniform trace estimates on the half-line are shown.

1.
Introduction

In the theory of evolution equations one often needs to describe the precise regularity of the trace of
u
at
t=0
, where
u
is a function which has mixed fractional smoothness in time and space. Parabolic regularity usually implies that
u
is in

(1.1)

\mathcal{A}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X_{0})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X_{1}),

where
\mathcal{A}^{s,p}=W^{s,p}
is the Sobolev–Slobodetskii space, or
\mathcal{A}=H^{s,p}
is the Bessel potential space, or
\mathcal{A}^{s,p}=F^{s}_{p,q}
is the Triebel-Lizorkin space, and
s>1/p
. Typically,
X_{0}=L^{q}
and
X_{1}
is some Sobolev–Slobodetskii space as well. In the case
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
is an integer, and
\mathcal{A}
is a Sobolev space, then the precise regularity is well-understood and related to the so-called trace method for real interpolation due to J.L. Lions (see
[
53
]
for a detailed account and references).

For
s\in(0,\infty)\setminus{\mathbb{N}}
, the situation is more delicate and much less is known. This case occurs naturally in:

(a)

Evolution equations of Volterra type;

(b)

Stochastic evolution equations driven by Brownian noise.

In (
a
) the smoothness in time is fractional because of the fractional order time derivative in the equations. In (
b
) the smoothness in time is fractional because Brownian motion is only
C^{\alpha}
-regular for all
\alpha\in[0,1/2)
.
These two examples will be discussed in more detail in Section
6
.

Fractional smoothness also occurs when dealing with inhomogeneous boundary value problems. We illustrate this with a simple but nontrivial heat equation. On the time intervals
J=(0,T)
and the half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}=(0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
consider

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=0,&\text{on $J\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}$;}\\
u=g,&\text{on $J\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}$;}\\
u=0,&\hbox{on $\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}$.}\end{array}\right.

Typically one wants to characterize when

(1.2)

u\in W^{1,p}(J;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}))\cap L^{p}(J;W^{2,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+})).

By classical parabolic regularity theory (see
[
11
,
32
]
), the latter holds if and only if

\displaystyle g\in\mathbb{B}:=W^{1-\frac{1}{2p},p}(J;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}))\cap L^{p}(J;W^{2-\frac{1}{p},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})),

and the compatibility conditions
g=0
holds at
\{0\}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d-1}
if
p>3/2
and no condition is required if
p<3/2
(we avoid the case
p=3/2
). The latter space fits exactly in the setting (
1.1
).
In order to prove the above characterization it is important to characterize the trace space of
\mathbb{B}
at
t=0
, which is known to be
W^{2-\frac{3}{p},p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d-1})
. In case one wants
L^{q}
-integrability in space in (
1.2
), then the space for
g
becomes more involved and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces are needed (see
[
11
]
).

Without any structured theory, the complexity of the spaces involved easily gets out of hand for more difficult boundary value problems. We refer to
[
40
]
for the application to the inhomogeneous Stefan problem with Gibbs-Thomson correction, where many traces spaces need to be characterized for spaces of the form (
1.1
), where often even three spaces are intersected. The same technique can be used to include inhomogeneities in other equations of so called
mixed order type
. Details on equations of mixed order type can be found in
[
12
,
13
]
.

After these motivations, we return to the general setting of the trace problem for (
1.1
). There are actually two problems to be solved if one wants to characterize the unknown trace space
X_{\mathtt{Tr}_{0}}
. In the following
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}u:=u(0)
and
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
.

Problem 1.1

(Trace problem)
.

Let
s>0
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\geq 0
. Find
X_{\mathtt{Tr}_{0}}
such that

(1)

\mathtt{Tr}_{0}:\mathcal{A}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{0})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{1})\to X_{\mathtt{Tr}_{0}}
is well-defined and bounded;

(2)

X_{\mathtt{Tr}_{0}}
is the smallest space for which (
1
) holds.

A sufficient condition for (
2
) is

(2)’

There exists a right inverse to
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}
.

In the Sobolev–Slobodetskii scale (
\mathcal{A}=W
) results about traces of spaces with mixed order (fractional) smoothness can be found in
[
15
]
and extensions to weighted spaces in
[
37
, Theorem 4.2]
. The case of Bessel-potential spaces (
\mathcal{A}=H
) was considered in special cases in
[
58
, Theorem 3.6]
under geometric restrictions on
X_{0}
and under the condition that
X_{1}
is the domain of an
R
-sectorial operator
T
. In the paper
[
40
]
by Meyries and the third named author, it was shown that
[
15
,
37
,
58
]
can be unified and geometric and
R
-sectoriality conditions can be omitted. However, in
[
40
]
it is still assumed that
X_{1}
is domain of some sectorial operator
T
, and there is an angle condition on the sectoriality of
T
, which is in terms of the smoothness
s
.

In the current paper we will show that one can actually consider an arbitrary interpolation couple
(X_{0},X_{1})
, and this extends the boundedness part of
[
40
]
, which is an important step in the solution to Problem
1.1
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
, and
s>0
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{s,p}\in\{H^{s,p},W^{s,p},F^{s}_{p,q}\}
.
Then for
s>\frac{1+\gamma}{p}
the following mapping is bounded

\displaystyle\mathtt{Tr}_{0}:\mathcal{A}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{0})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{1})

\displaystyle\to(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\frac{1+\gamma}{sp},p},\ \ \mathtt{Tr}_{0}u=u(0).

Moreover, if
\gamma\in[0,p-1)
, then
\mathcal{A}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{0})\cap L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},t^{\gamma}dt;X_{1})
continuously embeds into

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{b}}\big([0,\infty);(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\frac{1+\gamma}{sp},p}\big)\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{if $s>\frac{1+\gamma}{p}$,}

\displaystyle C_{{\rm{b}},\gamma/p}\big((0,\infty);(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\frac{1}{sp},p}\big)\ \ \ \ \ \text{if $s>\frac{1}{p}$.}

Here
C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);X)
and
C_{\rm{b},\eta}((0,\infty);X)
denote the spaces of
X
-valued continuous functions on
[0,\infty)
and
(0,\infty)
for which
\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|u(t)\|_{X}<\infty
and
\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}t^{\eta}\|u(t)\|_{X}<\infty
, respectively.

The above result extends and unifies many of the previous mentioned results.
Moreover, consequences with homogeneous norms are derived in Sections
4.3
and
5
. Optimality holds in many cases (see
[
40
, Theorem 1.1]
), but not in general. For instance in the trivial case where
X_{0}\hookrightarrow X_{1}
, the trace mapping is actually bounded with values in the smallest space
X_{0}
, which shows that optimality is a delicate problem. Further results on this will be given in a subsequent paper.

Both cases
H^{s,p}
and
W^{s,p}
of Theorem
1.2
follow from the case
F^{s}_{p,q}
. Indeed, these spaces are sandwiched between the smallest case
q=1
, and largest case
q=\infty
of
F^{s}_{p,q}
(see (
2.8
) and (
3.9
) below), and the image space
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
is independent of
q
.
The result on the weighted continuous functions with values in
(X_{0},X_{1})_{1-\frac{1}{sp},p}
does not appear elsewhere as far as we know. If
\gamma>0
this result provides extra information on the regularity in space for
t>0
.
Theorem
1.2
is proved in Sections
4
and
5
where we even allow different
p
,
q
, and
\gamma
for both spaces in the intersection.

Theorem
1.2
shows that the angle conditions of
[
40
, Corollary 4.7]
are not needed for the boundedness of the trace mapping. This angle condition led to restrictions in
[
44
, Section 5]
which can now be omitted as will be explained in detail in Section
6
. Another important aspect is that, unlike in the previous theory, we can allow
X_{1}
to be the homogeneous domain of a sectorial operator. For instance our setting allows to take
X_{0}=L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and the homogeneous space
X_{1}=\dot{W}^{m,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
which is important for certain optimal regularity results for (non)linear evolution equation (cf.
[
10
]
and references therein). In particular, we will state global estimates on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
by using homogeneous function spaces, which would be impossible if one is limited to the inhomogeneous setting. These things are demonstrated in Section
6
.

X

X

2.1.
Real interpolation and the mean method

In this subsection we collect basic facts on interpolation theory which will be needed in the paper. For an exposition of the general theory see
[
6
]
. As usual, we say that
(X_{0},X_{1})
is an interpolation couple of Banach spaces if
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
are Banach spaces and there exists a topological Hausdorff space
V
, such that
X_{i}\hookrightarrow V
continuously for
i\in\{0,1\}
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. For the definition and the basic properties of the real interpolation spaces
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
for
\theta\in(0,1)
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we refer to
[
6
,
25
,
53
]
. Recall that
X_{0}\cap X_{1}\hookrightarrow(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}\hookrightarrow X_{0}+X_{1}
, where the first embedding is dense if
p<\infty
. We will frequently use the following reiteration result:

(2.1)

((X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta_{0},p_{0}},(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta_{1},p_{1}})_{\alpha,p}=(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p},

where
p_{0},p_{1},p\in[1,\infty]
,
\alpha,\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in(0,1)
and
\theta=(1-\alpha)\theta_{0}+\alpha\theta_{1}
.

In this paper we will use the following alternative characterization of real interpolation spaces, which is a variant of the first mean method and can be found in
[
53
, Remark 2, p. 35]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\xi_{0},\xi_{1}
be real numbers such that
\xi_{0}\xi_{1}<0
and
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
. Moreover, set

\theta:=\frac{\xi_{0}}{\xi_{0}-\xi_{1}},\qquad\frac{1}{p}:=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

Then
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
coincides with the set of all
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
for which there exist measurable functions
u_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to X_{j}
(for
j\in\{0,1\}
) such that the maps
t\mapsto t^{\xi_{j}}u_{j}(t)
belong to
L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dt}{t};X_{j})
and

(2.2)

x=u_{0}(t)+u_{1}(t),\qquad\text{for almost all}\;t>0.

Furthermore,

\|x\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\eqsim_{\theta,p,\xi_{0},\xi_{1}}\inf_{u_{0},u_{1}}\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}\|t\mapsto t^{\xi_{j}}u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dt}{t};X_{j})},

where the infimum is taken over all
u_{0},u_{1}
such that (
2.2
) holds.

Proof.

The proof is a simple modification of
[
53
, Remark 2, p. 35]
. For convenience of the reader we provide the details. To begin, we recall that
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
can be characterized as the set of all
x\in X_{0}+X_{1}
such that there exist measurable functions
u_{j}:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to X_{j}
(for
j\in\{0,1\}
) such that the maps
t\mapsto t^{j-\theta}u_{j}(t)
(
j\in\{0,1\}
) belong to
L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dt}{t};X_{j})
and
x=u_{0}(t)+u_{1}(t)
for almost all
t>0
. Furthermore,

\|x\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\eqsim_{\theta,p}\inf_{u}\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}\|t\mapsto t^{j-\theta}u_{j}(t)\|_{L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dt}{t};X_{j})};

where the infimum is taken over all
u
such that (
2.2
) holds. See for instance,
[
53
, Theorem 1.5.2 a) p. 33]
or
[
25
, Proposition C.3.7]
.

To prove the equivalence, it is enough to note that for any measurable maps
u_{j}
(
j\in\{0,1\}
) such that
t\mapsto t^{\xi_{j}}u_{j}(t)\in L^{p_{j}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{dt}{t};X_{j})
, the functions
v_{j}(t):=u_{j}(t^{\frac{1}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{0}}})
satisfy

\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{\xi_{j}}\|u(t)\|_{X_{j}})^{p_{j}}\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{1}{|\xi_{1}-\xi_{0}|}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\tau^{j-\theta}\|v_{j}(t)\|_{X_{j}})^{p_{j}}\frac{dt}{t},\quad j\in\{0,1\};

here we have used the transformation
t=\tau^{\frac{1}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{0}}}
and that
\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{1}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{0}}\frac{d\tau}{\tau}
. Moreover,

x=\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}u_{j}(t^{\frac{1}{\xi_{1}-\xi_{0}}})=\sum_{j\in\{0,1\}}v_{j}(t),\qquad\text{for almost all}\;\;t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Thus, the maps
u_{j}\mapsto v_{j}
are isomorphisms and this concludes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Weighted function spaces

In this subsection
X
denotes a Banach space.
Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be an open set. A weight
w:\Omega\to[0,\infty)
is a measurable function which is nonzero a.e. The norm of
L^{p}(\Omega,w;X)
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w;X)}=\Big(\int_{\Omega}\|f(x)\|_{X}^{p}w(x)\,dx\Big)^{1/p}.

In case
X={\mathbb{C}}
we write
L^{p}(\Omega,w)=L^{p}(\Omega,w;{\mathbb{C}})
, and in case
w=1
we write
L^{p}(\Omega;X)=L^{p}(\Omega,1;X)
. We say
w\in A_{p}
(Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
class) if

\sup_{Q\text{ cubes in }{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w(x)\,dx\Big)\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{Q}w(x)^{-1/(p-1)}\,dx\Big)^{p-1}<\infty.

One further sets
A_{\infty}=\bigcup_{p>1}A_{p}
. For details on
A_{p}
-weights we refer to
[
20
, Chapter 9]
and
[
52
, Chapter V]
.
We will mostly be using
d=1
and the weight

w_{\gamma}(t)=|t|^{\gamma}
which is in
A_{p}
if and only if
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
(see
[
20
, Example 9.1.7]
).

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
p\in[1,\infty]
and a weight
w:\Omega\to X
such that
w^{-1/(p-1)}\in L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
. Note that, by Hölder’s inequality, the latter implies
L^{p}(\Omega,w;X)\hookrightarrow L^{1}_{{\rm loc}}(\Omega;X)
. Denote by
W^{m,p}(\Omega,w;X)
the space of all
f\in L^{p}(\Omega,w;X)
for which the distributional derivative
\partial^{\alpha}f
exists in
L^{p}(\Omega;X)
for all
|\alpha|\leq m
, and set

(2.3)

\|f\|_{W^{m,p}(\Omega,w;X)}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,w;X)}.

2.3.
Besov, Triebel-Lizorkin, and Bessel potential spaces

We recall the definitions of weighted spaces of smooth functions. For details in the unweighted case see
[
4
,
49
,
53
,
54
]
and in the weighted case
[
7
,
39
]
.

Let
\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be the set of all sequences
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 0}\subseteq\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}_{0}=\widehat{\varphi},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}(\xi/2)-\widehat{\varphi}(\xi),\qquad\widehat{\varphi}_{k}(\xi)=\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(2^{-k+1}\xi),\quad k\geq 2,\qquad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where the Fourier transform
\widehat{\varphi}
of the generating function
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
satisfies

(2.5)

0\leq\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\leq 1,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=1\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\leq 1,\qquad\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)=0\ \text{ if }\ |\xi|\geq\frac{3}{2}.

For
(\varphi_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
f\in{\mathscr{S}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
we let

(2.6)

S_{k}f=\varphi_{k}*f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\widehat{f}).

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{\infty}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, for
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
we set

\displaystyle\|f\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\big(2^{sk}S_{k}f\big)_{k\geq 0}\Big\|_{\ell^{q}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X))},

\displaystyle\|f\|_{F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\big(2^{sk}S_{k}f\big)_{k\geq 0}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;\ell^{q}(X))}.

The Besov space
B_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)
and the Triebel-Lizorkin space
F_{p,q}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)
are those spaces on which the respective extended norms are finite. They are all Banach spaces. Any other
(\psi_{k})_{k\geq 0}\in\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
leads to an equivalent norm on the
B
- and
F
-spaces. Note that
B_{p,p}^{s}=F_{p,p}^{s}
.
It is well known that the following continuous embeddings hold for
{\mathcal{A}}\in\{B,F\}
:

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\hookrightarrow}}{\mathscr{S}^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X).

The first embedding is also dense if
q<\infty
(see also Lemma
2.2
below).
For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we define the Sobolev-Slobodetskii space by

W^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)=B_{p,p}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X).

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For
f\in{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};X)
we set

\|f\|_{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)}=\Big\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}[(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}(f)]\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)}

and we define the Bessel-potential space
H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)
as the space on which this extended norm is finite. Recall the following crucial embedding results for
\mathcal{A}\in\{H,W\}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

(2.8)

\displaystyle F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X)\hookrightarrow F^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},w;X).

The following simple density result will be needed.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. Let
p_{0},p_{1}\in[1,\infty)
,
q_{0},q_{1}\in[1,\infty)
,
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
w_{0},w_{1}\in A_{\infty}
and
{\mathcal{A}}\in\{F,B\}
. Then

Proof.

2.4.
Sectorial operators and functional calculus

For a detailed discussion on the theory below we refer to
[
26
,
22
,
31
,
46
]
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence such that
\varepsilon_{j}
’s are independent random variable on a probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{A}},{\mathbb{P}})
such that
{\mathbb{P}}(\varepsilon_{j}=1)={\mathbb{P}}(\varepsilon_{j}=-1)=1/2
for all
j\geq 1
. Usually, such sequence is called a Rademacher sequence. A family
\mathfrak{T}\subseteq{\mathscr{L}}(X)
is called
R
-bounded if there exists
C>0
such that for all
N\geq 1
,
x_{1},\dots,x_{N}\in X
and
T_{1},\dots,T_{N}\in\mathfrak{T}
,

\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}T_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\Omega};X)}\leq C\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{j}x_{j}\Big\|_{L^{2}({\Omega};X)}.

The infimum of all such
C>0
will be denoted by
R(\mathfrak{T})
. With a slight abuse of notation, we write
R(\mathfrak{T})<\infty
if
\mathfrak{T}
is
R
-bounded.

A closed linear operator
A:{\mathsf{D}}(A)\subseteq X\to X
is said to be sectorial (resp.
R
-sectorial) if
\overline{{\mathsf{R}}(A)}=\overline{{\mathsf{D}}(A)}=X
and there exists
\phi\in(0,\pi)
such that
\sigma(A)\subseteq\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,:\,|\arg z|<\phi\}=:\Sigma_{\phi}
and
\sup_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{\phi}}\|\lambda(\lambda-A)^{-1}\|_{{\mathscr{L}}(X)}<\infty
(resp.
R(\lambda(\lambda-A)^{-1}\,:\,\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\Sigma_{\phi})<\infty
). Moreover, we denote
\omega(A)
(resp.
\omega_{R}(A)
) the angle of sectoriality (resp.
R
-sectoriality) and it is the infimum over all
\phi\in(0,\pi)
as above.

Next, we define the
H^{\infty}
-calculus. For
\varphi\in(0,\pi)
, we denote by
H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\varphi})
the set of all holomorphic function
f:\Sigma_{\varphi}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
such that
|f(z)|\leq C|z|^{\varepsilon}/(1+|z|^{2\varepsilon})
for some
C,\varepsilon>0
independent of
z\in\Sigma_{\varphi}
. Let
A
be a sectorial operator of angle
\omega(A)<\nu<\varphi
. Then for
f\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\varphi})
we set

(2.9)

f(A):=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\partial\Sigma_{\nu}}f(z)R(z,A)\,dz;

where the orientation of
\partial\Sigma_{\nu}
is such that
\sigma(A)
is on the right. By
[
26
, Section 10.2]
,
f(A)
is well-defined in
{\mathscr{L}}(X)
and it is independent of
\nu\in(\omega(A),\varphi)
.

Furthermore, the operator
A
is said to have a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\varphi})
-calculus if there exists
C>0
such that for all
f\in H^{\infty}_{0}(\Sigma_{\varphi})
,

\|f(A)\|_{{\mathscr{L}}(X)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\varphi})}\,,

where
\|f\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\varphi})}=\sup_{z\in\Sigma_{\varphi}}|f(z)|
. Finally,
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)
denotes the infimum of all
\varphi\in(\omega(A),\pi)
such that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\varphi})
-calculus.

The homogeneous fractional scale
(\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\sigma}):\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}})

Let
A
be a sectorial operator on
X
. For each
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
A^{\sigma}
defines a closed injective linear operator on
X
with domain
{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\sigma})
(see e.g.
[
22
, Chapter 3]
or
[
46
, Subsection 3.3]
). Furthermore, we may define the following spaces (see
[
31
, Appendix]
)

(2.10)

\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\sigma}):=({\mathsf{D}}(A^{\sigma}),\|A^{\sigma}\cdot\|_{X})^{\sim},\qquad\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\sim
denotes the completion. Note that, if
0\not\in\rho(A)
, then
\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\alpha})\not\hookrightarrow\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\beta})
even if
\alpha>\beta
. Moreover, for
\varphi\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
{\mathbb{N}}\ni k>|\varphi|
we set

(2.11)

\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(\varphi,p):=(\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{-k}),\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{k}))_{\theta,p},\ \ \ \theta:=1/2+\varphi/(2k).

By (
2.1
), for
\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\phi\in(0,1)
and

with

(2.12)

\displaystyle(\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\theta_{0}}),\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\theta_{1}}))_{\phi,q}=(\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}({\theta_{0}},q_{0}),\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}({\theta_{1}},q_{1}))_{\phi,q}=\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(\theta,q).

Characterizations by local means on half-lines

Let

be a measurable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
. We say that

satisfies the

of order

if
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}x^{k}g(x)dx=0
for all
k\leq N
.

Let
I=(a,\infty)
with
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(I;X)
and
\chi\in\mathscr{D}({\mathbb{R}}_{-})
we set

for each

, which is well-defined because
\chi(x-\,\cdot\,)\in\mathscr{D}(I)
for every such

. Then

is an
X
-valued
C^{\infty}
-function on
I
.

Furthermore, we define
{\mathscr{S}^{\prime}}(I;X)
as the subspace of
{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(I;X)
consisting of all
f\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\prime}(I;X)
having finite order and at most polynomial growth at infinity, that is, for which there exist
C\in[0,\infty)
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that

\|f(\phi)\|_{X}\leq C\sup_{k\leq N}\sup_{t\in I}(1+|t|)^{N}|\partial^{k}_{t}\phi(t)|,\qquad\phi\in{\mathscr{D}}(I).

The following theorem is an extension of
[
48
, Theorem 3.2]
to the weighted Banach space-valued setting. It will be needed in one of the estimates in the difference characterization in Proposition
3.5
below.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
I=(a,\infty)
with
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
w\in A_{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\phi_{0},\phi\in\mathscr{D}({\mathbb{R}}_{-})
satisfy
\phi(x)=\phi_{0}(x)-\frac{1}{2}\phi(\frac{x}{2})
. Set
\phi_{j}(x)=2^{j}\phi(2^{j}x)

.

and that

. Then

\|f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w;X)}\eqsim_{p,q,w,s}\|(2^{js}\phi_{j}*f)_{j\geq 0}\|_{L^{p}(I,w;\ell^{q}(X))},\ \ \ f\in{\mathscr{S}^{\prime}}(I;X).

3.2.
Characterization by differences

For
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
I\in\{(a,\infty),{\mathbb{R}}\}
and
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
We define

\displaystyle V^{m}_{I}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\{h\in{\mathbb{R}}:|h|<t,x+mh\in I\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}(-t,t),&I={\mathbb{R}},\\
(-t,t)\cap(\frac{a}{m}-\frac{x}{m},\infty),&I={\mathbb{R}}_{+},\end{array}\right.

for each
x\in I
and
t>0
.
For a strongly measurable function
f:I\to X
we define the
m
-th order difference of
f
as

\Delta_{h}^{m}f(x):=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom{m}{j}(-1)^{j}f(x+(m-j)h),\qquad x\in I,h\in{\mathbb{R}},x+m\in I,

and we define the means of the
m
-th order difference of

as

d^{m}_{t}f(x):=t^{-1}\int_{V^{m}_{I}(x,t)}\|\Delta_{h}^{m}f(x)\|_{X}dh,\qquad x\in I,t>0.

Let
s>0
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{R}})
.
For
f\in L^{p}(I,w;X)
we set

(3.2)

\displaystyle[f]_{F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w;X)}^{(m)}

\displaystyle:=\Big\|\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big(t^{-s}d^{m}_{t}f(\,\cdot\,)\Big)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}\Big\|_{L^{p}(I,w)},

with the usual modification for
q=\infty
, and

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|f\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w;X)}^{(m)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(I,w;X)}+[f]_{F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w;X)}^{(m)}.

By a discretization argument, for any
\kappa>0
,

(3.3)

\displaystyle[f]_{F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w;X)}^{(m)}\eqsim_{s,\kappa}\|(2^{js}d^{m}_{2^{-j}\kappa}f)_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|_{L^{p}(I,w;\ell^{q}(X))}.

One can obtain the following extension to the weighted setting of a well-known result on the characterization by means of differences for
F
-spaces (see
[
50
, Proposition 6]
,
[
54
, Section 2.5.11]

[

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,

,

and

be an integer such that

where
c_{s}>0
depends only on
s
. Therefore, by (
3.8
) it is immediate that
u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})
and

\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|\lesssim[f]_{F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}^{(m)}
. This implies
F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})
.

Next we show that one can obtain a Sandwich lemma of similar nature as (
2.8
). If one assumes
X
has UMD (see e.g.
[
25
, Chapter 4]
), then the embedding in the next result actually follows from (
2.8
) and
[
34
, Theorem 6.8(2)]
.

Lemma 3.7

(Sandwich lemma)
.

Let
s>0
and let
m>s
be an integer. Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in[0,p-1)
. Then
F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})\hookrightarrow F^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
, Moreover, for all
u\in F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle[u]_{F^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim[u]_{F^{s}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},

Proof.

The second embedding follows from the above discussion, but one could also use a variation of the argument below. For the first one, we will first show that

(3.10)

\|u\|_{F^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\leq C\|(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}})^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},

or equivalently, that
\|(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}})^{-s}v\|_{F_{p,\infty}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}
for all
v\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
. Let
Ev
denote the zero extension of
v
to
{\mathbb{R}}
, let
R
denote the restriction to
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, and let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}u=-u^{\prime}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
for
u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})=W^{1,p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
. Then from
[
34
, the proof of Theorem 6.8(2)]
,
(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}})^{-s}v=R(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{-s}Ev
holds for
s=1
, and therefore, by definition of the fractional power the same holds for
s\in(0,1)
. This implies

\displaystyle\|(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}})^{-s}v\|_{F_{p,\infty}^{s}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{-s}Ev\|_{F_{p,\infty}^{s}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\eqsim}}\|J_{s}(1+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{-s}Ev\|_{F_{p,\infty}^{0}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\lesssim}}\|Ev\|_{F_{p,\infty}^{0}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Ev\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)}\leq\|v\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},

where
J_{s}:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}((1+|\cdot|^{2})^{s/2}\mathcal{F}(\cdot))
. Here
(i)
follows from
[
39
, Proposition 3.9]
and
(ii)
follows from Fourier multiplier theory in Triebel–Lizorkin spaces (see
[
56
, 15.6]
and the comments in
[
39
, Proposition 3.10]
).

It remains to prove the first inequality in (
3.9
). For this we use the shorthand notation
L^{p}
and
F^{s}_{p,q}
. By (
3.10
) and
[
22
, Proposition 3.1.9]
we have

[u]_{F^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}}+\|u\|_{L^{p}}.

Applying this to
u_{\lambda}(x)=u(\lambda x)
and using the scaling properties implied by the difference norms and the expression (
3.7
), after rewriting we obtain

[u]_{F^{s}_{p,\infty}}+\lambda^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}}+\lambda^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{p}}.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
this gives the first inequality in (
3.9
).
∎

Remark 3.8
.

One can actually show that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is sectorial on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
for any
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
. In this case
-\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
no longer generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup for
\gamma\in(-1,0)
. However, the first part of Lemma
3.7
on the embedding result
{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})\hookrightarrow F^{s}_{p,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
extends to this setting if one uses the Fourier multiplier argument of that proof. For this we only need to check that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is sectorial. For this consider
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}u=-u^{\prime}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w;X)
for

. This is sectorial for
w=1
. One can check that
\lambda(\lambda+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{-1}
is an integral operator with kernel
k_{\lambda}(t)=\lambda e^{t\lambda}{{\bf 1}}_{(-\infty,0)}
for
\lambda>0
. Now it remains to extrapolate in the weight
w_{\gamma}
with
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
as in
[
29
,
51
]
(also see
[
45
, Proposition 2.3]
).
Therefore,
\lambda(\lambda+\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{-1}
is uniformly bounded on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
for
\lambda>0
and hence
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is sectorial. Arguing as in
[
34
, Theorem 6.8]
one sees that
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
is also sectorial. The above for instance implies that the first part of Corollary
5.3
can be extended to
\gamma_{i}\in(-1,0)
(see the comments before the statement of Corollary
5.3
).

Using the more advanced extrapolation theory from
[
23
, Corollary 2.10]
one can even obtain the above results for
w\in A_{p}
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
we set
\prescript{}{0}{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};X)}^{H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}
.
If
k<s-\tfrac{1+\gamma}{p}<k+1
with
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cup\{-1\}
, then (see
[
34
, Proposotion 6.4]
)

\prescript{}{0}{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)=\{u\in H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X):\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}u=0\},

where we define
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{-1}u=0
. In the next result we compare the fractional domain spaces of
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
and
\mathfrak{D}
under the assumption that
X
is a UMD space (see
[
25
]
for details).

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
X
be a UMD space,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
and
s>0
. Then the operators
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
and
\mathfrak{D}
have a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\pi/2
, and

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{s})\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})

\displaystyle=H^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X).

Moreover, if
s\notin{\mathbb{N}}_{0}+(1+\gamma)/p
, then

(3.11)

{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{s})=\prescript{}{0}{H}^{s,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),\ \text{and}\ \|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\eqsim\|\mathfrak{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

for all
u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{s})
.

Proof.

The identities for the fractional domain spaces are immediate from
[
34
, Proposition 5.6 and Theorem 6.8]
. The latter paper also gives that

\mathfrak{D}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
have a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\pi/2
, and in particular bounded imaginary powers.
To prove the embedding
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{s})\hookrightarrow{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s})
we use the previous identities with an integer
n>s
, and
[
22
, Theorem 6.6.9]
to find

\displaystyle{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{s})

\displaystyle=[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{n})]_{\frac{s}{n}}

\displaystyle=[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),\prescript{}{0}{H}^{n,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)]_{\frac{s}{n}}

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),H^{n,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)]_{\frac{s}{n}}

\displaystyle=[L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X),{\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{n})]_{\frac{s}{n}}={\mathsf{D}}(\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}).

For the final part of (
3.11
), it suffices to consider
s\in(0,1)
, and by density
u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D})
. Note that the first part implies that

\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\eqsim\|\mathfrak{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

Applying the latter to
u_{\lambda}=u(\lambda x)
, and using
[
22
, Proposition 3.1.12]

one obtains

\|\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\lambda^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\eqsim\|\mathfrak{D}^{s}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\lambda^{-s}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, the desired estimate follows.
∎

It would be interesting to know whether (
3.11
) holds without the condition that
X
is a UMD space.

Finally, we collect some standard properties of
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
. Here for
T\in(0,\infty]
,
p\in(1,\infty)
, and
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
we set
\mathfrak{D}_{T}u=u^{\prime}
with
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}_{T})=\prescript{}{0}{W}^{1,p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)
, where the boundary condition only applies to the left endpoint (see below (
3.6
)). By setting
\mathfrak{D}_{\infty}=\mathfrak{D}
, we will treat both cases at the same time. We claim that for all
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
with
\hbox{\rm Re}\,(\lambda)>0
,

(3.12)

(\lambda+\mathfrak{D}_{T})^{-1}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}f(s)ds,\ \ f\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X).

It is standard to check (
3.12
) for
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T];X)
.
Therefore, it remains to check the boundedness of the operator on the RHS of (
3.12
). The uniform boundedness of
\hbox{\rm Re}\,(\lambda)(\lambda+\mathfrak{D}_{T})^{-1}
for
\gamma=0
is clear from Young’s inequality. The boundedness for
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
can be deduced from the case
\gamma=0
by the same extrapolation argument as referred to in Remark
3.8
. Moreover, if
T<\infty
, one can additionally check that
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
is invertible and the inverse is given by the RHS of (
3.12
) for
\lambda=0
, which can be checked via Hardy–Young’s inequality (see (
4.11
) below).

The above implies that
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
is a sectorial operator and since

(3.13)

\displaystyle(\lambda+\mathfrak{D}_{T})^{-1}f=((\lambda+\mathfrak{D})^{-1}E_{T}f)|_{(0,T)},

where
E_{T}
denotes the extension by zero on
(0,\infty)
, it also follows that
\mathfrak{D}_{T}
inherits the bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\pi/2
of
\mathfrak{D}
(see Proposition
3.9
) with uniform estimates in
T
. Moreover, by (
3.13
) and the extended calculus (see
[
22
, Section 3.1]
)

(3.14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{\alpha}f=\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}f|_{(0,T)},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},\ f\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}),

and this extends to
f\in\dot{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha})
by density and closedness.

Next we claim that

(3.15)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{-\alpha}f=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*E_{T}f,\qquad\alpha>0,\ f\in{\mathsf{R}}(\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{\alpha}),

where
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(t):=\frac{|t|^{\alpha-1}{{\bf 1}}_{(0,\infty)}(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
. Indeed, the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
follows from
[
22
, Corollary 3.1.14 and Proposition 3.2.1]
and elementary calculations.
Now by analyticity in
\alpha
, (
3.15
) extends to all
\alpha>0
. Since
0\in\rho(\mathfrak{D}_{T})
for all
T<\infty
, we have
{\mathsf{R}}(\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{\alpha})=L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)
. In the case
T=\infty
and
\alpha>0
one has

(3.16)

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*f\in\dot{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*f=f,\ \ \ f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X).

To see this, by density we may assume
f\in{\mathsf{R}}({\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}))
and by (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) we get, for all
T<\infty
,

(\mathfrak{D}^{-\alpha}f)|_{(0,T)}=\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{-\alpha}f=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*E_{T}f=(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*f)|_{(0,T)}\ \ \text{on $(0,T)$.}

The arbitrariness of
T<\infty
readily yields (
3.16
).

3.4.
Sobolev embedding with power weights

The following embedding result is the half line version of one of the main results of
[
39
]
by Meyries and the third named author.

Theorem 3.10

(Sobolev embedding)
.

Let
I\in\{{\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}\}
,
X
a Banach space,
1<p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty
,
q_{0},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
,
s_{0}>s_{1}
and
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}>-1
. Assume

(3.17)

\frac{\gamma_{0}}{p_{0}}\geq\frac{\gamma_{1}}{p_{1}},\qquad s_{0}-\frac{1+\gamma_{0}}{p_{0}}=s_{1}-\frac{1+\gamma_{1}}{p_{1}}.

Then

(3.18)

F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)\hookrightarrow F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X).

If in addition
s_{0},s_{1}>0
and
\gamma_{i}<p_{i}-1
for
i\in\{0,1\}
and we are given
{\mathbb{N}}\ni m_{i}>s_{i}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
, then for all
f\in F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)
one has

(3.19)

[f]_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X)}^{(m_{1})}\lesssim[f]_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}^{(m_{0})}.

Proof.

The embedding (
3.18
) for
I={\mathbb{R}}
is contained in
[
39
]
, from which the corresponding embedding for
I={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
follows.
To prove the last claim, fix
f\in F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)
.
Let
\lambda>0
and set
f_{\lambda}:=f(\lambda\cdot)
. By applying the norm inequality associated with the embedding (
3.18
) to
f_{\lambda}
, we obtain

\displaystyle[f_{\lambda}]_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X)}^{(m_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{\lambda}\|_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f_{\lambda}\|_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}

\displaystyle=\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma};X)}+[f_{\lambda}]_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}^{(m_{1})}.

By standard computations,
[f_{\lambda}]_{F^{s_{i}}_{p_{i},q_{i}}(I,w_{\gamma_{i}};X)}^{(m_{i})}=\lambda^{\delta_{i}}[f]_{F^{s_{i}}_{p_{i},q_{i}}(I,w_{\gamma_{i}};X)}^{(m_{i})}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
where
\delta_{i}:=s_{i}-\frac{1+\gamma_{i}}{p_{i}}
. Then, by assumption we have
\delta_{0}=\delta_{1}
, and we find that

\displaystyle[f]_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X)}^{(m_{1})}\lesssim\lambda^{-s_{0}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}+[f]_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}^{(m_{0})}.

The claim follows by letting
\lambda\to\infty
.
∎

Corollary 3.11

(Sobolev embedding)
.

Let
1<p_{0}\leq p_{1}<\infty
,
q_{0}\in[1,\infty]
,
s_{0}>0
,
-1<\gamma_{0}<p_{0}-1
,
-1<\gamma_{0}<p_{1}-1
and
{\mathbb{N}}\ni m_{0}>s_{0}
. Assume (
3.17
).
Then

(3.20)

\displaystyle F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)

\displaystyle\hookrightarrow L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X).

and for all
f\in F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)
,

(3.21)

\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X)}\lesssim[f]_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X)}^{(m_{0})}.

Proof.

The embedding (
3.20
) follows from the Sobolev embedding (
3.18
) with
s_{1}=0
and
q_{1}=1
and (
2.8
).
The inequality (
3.21

.
∎

4.
Trace Embeddings for Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

In this section we study trace embedding results for anisotropic function spaces.

4.1.
Traces at the origin

We start with a lemma on the well-definedness of higher order trace operators in the isotropic setting.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
I\in\{{\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}\}
,
X
a Banach space,
p\in(1,\infty)
,
q\in[1,\infty]
,
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)
,
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Let
s>k+\frac{1+\gamma}{p}
. Then
the
k
-th order trace operator
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X)\cap C^{k}(\bar{I};X)\to X
given by
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}u=\partial^{k}_{t}u\,(0)
has a unique extension to a bounded linear operator
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X)\to X
that is also bounded from
\big(F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X),\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F^{\sigma}_{p,1}(I,w_{\gamma};X)}\big)
to
X
for every
\sigma\in(k+\frac{1+\gamma}{p},s)
.

The same holds with
F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X)
replaced by
H^{s,p}(I,w_{\gamma};X)
.

The formulation of the lemma can be slightly confusing at first reading. The final part on
F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X)
with the artificial norm
\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F^{\sigma}_{p,1}(I,w_{\gamma};X)}
is in order to obtain uniqueness of the extension in the case
q=\infty
. Below we will use the simple trace estimate obtained in
[
34
, Proposition 6.3]
and from the proof of the latter it is clear that the following scaling property holds

(4.1)

\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}(u(\lambda\cdot))=\lambda^{k}(\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}u)(\lambda\cdot),\ \ u\in F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X),\ \lambda>0.

Proof.

Let us first consider the case
I={\mathbb{R}}
.
Pick
\widetilde{\gamma}\in(-1,p-1)
with
\widetilde{\gamma}<\gamma
and set
\widetilde{s}:=s+\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}-\gamma}{p}
. Then, by the Sobolev embedding Theorem
3.10
and the elementary embedding (
2.8
),

F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow F^{\widetilde{s}}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\widetilde{\gamma}};X)\hookrightarrow H^{\widetilde{s},p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\widetilde{\gamma}};X).

Combining this with
[
34
, Proposition 6.3]
, we obtain that

\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)\cap C^{k}({\mathbb{R}};X)\to X,\qquad u\mapsto\partial^{k}_{t}u\,(0),

has an extension to a bounded linear operator
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)\to X
.
Given
\sigma\in(k+\frac{1+\gamma}{p},s)
, we have
F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow F^{\sigma}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
and in the same way as above it can be seen that
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}
is bounded from
\big(F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X),\|\,\cdot\,\|_{F^{\sigma}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)}\big)
to
X
.
The uniqueness statement thus follows from the density of
F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
in
F^{\sigma}_{p,1}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\gamma};X)
.

It remains to treat the case
I={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
.
To this end, pick
\widetilde{\gamma}\in(-1,p-1)
with
\widetilde{\gamma}<\gamma
and set
\widetilde{s}:=s+\frac{\widetilde{\gamma}-\gamma}{p}
.
Then, by the Sobolev embedding Theorem
3.10
,

(4.2)

F^{s}_{p,q}(I,w_{\gamma};X)\hookrightarrow F^{\widetilde{s}}_{p,p}(I,w_{\widetilde{\gamma}};X).

By Proposition
3.2
there exists an extension operator

(4.3)

\mathsf{E}_{I}:F^{\widetilde{s}}_{p,p}(I,w_{\widetilde{\gamma}};X)\to F^{\widetilde{s}}_{p,p}({\mathbb{R}},w_{\widetilde{\gamma}};X),\quad\text{with}\quad\mathsf{E}_{I}C^{k}(\bar{I};X)\subseteq C^{k}({\mathbb{R}};X).

Combining (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), the desired result follows from the case
I={\mathbb{R}}
.

The case of Bessel potential spaces follows from (
2.8
).
∎

Our first main result is the following trace embedding.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
I\in\{{\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}\}
and let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. Let
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
q_{0},q_{1}\in[1,\infty]
,
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\in(-1,\infty)
and
s_{0},s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
Assume that
s_{0}-\frac{1+\gamma_{0}}{p_{0}}>k
and
s_{1}-\frac{1+\gamma_{1}}{p_{1}}<k
for some
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Set

(4.4)

\delta_{i}=s_{i}-\frac{1+\gamma_{i}}{p_{i}},\quad i\in\{0,1\},\quad\theta=\frac{\delta_{0}-k}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}},\quad\text{and }\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

Set

(4.5)

{\mathbb{F}}:=F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})\cap F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1}).

Then the
k
-th order trace operator
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})\to X_{0}
(see Lemma
4.1
) acts a bounded linear operator

(4.6)

\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}:{\mathbb{F}}\to(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p},

and there is a constant
C
such that for all

,

(4.7)

\|\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{k}u\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\leq C\|u\|_{F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}^{\theta}.

Moreover, if
s_{1}=0
and
\gamma_{1}\in(-1,p_{1}-1)

the same holds when
{\mathbb{F}}
is replaced by

(4.8)

{\mathbb{F}}_{L}:=F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},q_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})\cap L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})

and
\|u\|_{F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},q_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}
is replaced by
\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}
in (
4.7
).

The proof of Theorem
4.2
will be given at the end of this subsection. Before embarking into its proof, let us point out some useful facts.

Remark 4.3
.

(i)

(iv)

The proof of Theorem

.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
I\in\{{\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\mathbb{R}}\}
and let
(X_{0},X_{1})
be an interpolation couple of Banach spaces. Let
s_{0}\in(0,1)
,
p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
,
\gamma_{0}\in(-1,p_{0}-1)
and
\gamma_{1}\in(-1,p_{1}-1)
.
Set

(4.9)

\theta=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}},\quad\delta_{0}=s_{0}-\frac{1+\gamma_{0}}{p_{0}},\quad\delta_{1}=-\frac{1+\gamma_{1}}{p_{1}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}.

Assume that
\delta_{0}>0
. Then for all
u\in F^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})\cap L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})
,

(4.10)

\|\mathtt{Tr}_{0}u\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\lesssim\Big([u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}\Big)^{1-\theta}\Big(\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}\Big)^{\theta},

where
[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}(I,w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}
is as in (
3.2
) and
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}:=\mathtt{Tr}_{0}^{0}
(see Lemma
4.1
).

By Lemma
4.1
,
\mathtt{Tr}_{0}u
exists in
X_{0}
, and coincides with
u(0)
if
u
is continuous.

Proof.

Let us note that
\delta_{1}\in(-1,0)
as
\gamma_{1}\in(-1,p_{1}-1)
, which will be important in the computations below.
Below we will use the Hardy-Young inequality, which says that for each measurable function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, and
\beta>0
,

(4.11)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-\beta p-1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\sigma}f(s)ds\Big)^{p}d\sigma\leq\beta^{-p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-\beta p-1+p}(f(\sigma))^{p}d\sigma.

To prove (
4.10
) it suffices to consider
I={\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. On the other hand, by an extension argument (see Proposition
3.2
), and density (see Lemma
2.2
), we may assume
u\in\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}},X_{0}\cap X_{1})
.

In order to prove (
4.10
), by a scaling argument it suffices to prove that

(4.12)

\|u(0)\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}\lesssim[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}+\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}.

Indeed, if
u
is zero this is obvious. If
u
is nonzero, then
[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}>0
since otherwise
u
would be a constant function, which contradicts
u\in L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})
.
Applying (
4.12
) to
u_{\lambda}=u(\lambda\,\cdot\,)
with
\lambda>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(0)\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}

\displaystyle=\|u_{\lambda}(0)\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim[u_{\lambda}]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}+\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\delta_{0}}[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}+\lambda^{\delta_{1}}\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}.

As
\theta=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}}
and
1-\theta=-\frac{\delta_{1}}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}}
, the choice

\lambda=\left(\frac{\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}}{[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1)}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}}}

then leads to (
4.10
).

To prove (
4.12
)
we use the standard identity

(4.13)

u(0)=\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\sigma}t^{-2}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(u(\tau)-u(t))d\tau\Big)dt}_{=:T_{0}(\sigma)}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{\sigma}u(\tau)d\tau}_{=:T_{1}(\sigma)},\ \text{ for each }\sigma>0.

To see (
4.13
) note that by elementary calculations one has

\displaystyle T_{0}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\tau}^{t}-\frac{u^{\prime}(s)}{t^{2}}dsd\tau dt=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}-\frac{su^{\prime}(s)}{t^{2}}dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\int_{s}^{\sigma}-\frac{su^{\prime}(s)}{t^{2}}dtds=\int_{0}^{\sigma}\Big(\frac{s}{\sigma}-1\Big)u^{\prime}(s)ds=u(0)-T_{1}(\sigma).

To prove (
4.12
) set
\xi_{0}:=-\delta_{0}<0
,
\xi_{1}:=-\delta_{1}>0
and observe that

\frac{\xi_{0}}{\xi_{0}-\xi_{1}}=\frac{\delta_{0}}{\delta_{0}-\delta_{1}}=\theta.

By (
4.13
) and Lemma
2.1
it suffices to prove

(4.14)

\displaystyle\|\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{\xi_{0}}T_{0}(\sigma)\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma};X_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim[u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1),+},

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{\xi_{1}}T_{1}(\sigma)\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma};X_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})},

where the implicit constants do not depend on
u
. Let us begin by proving (
4.14
):

\displaystyle\Big\|\sigma

\displaystyle\mapsto\sigma^{\xi_{0}}T_{0}(\sigma)\Big\|_{L^{p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma};X_{0})}^{p_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-\delta_{0}p_{0}-1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\sigma}t^{-2}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X_{0}}d\tau\Big)dt\Big)^{p_{0}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:HardyYoung}}}{{\lesssim}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-\delta_{0}p_{0}+p_{0}-1}\sigma^{-2p_{0}}\left(\int_{0}^{\sigma}\|u(\sigma)-u(\tau)\|_{X_{0}}d\tau\right)^{p_{0}}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-s_{0}p_{0}+\gamma_{0}-p_{0}}\Big(\int_{-\sigma}^{0}\|u(\sigma)-u(\sigma+h)\|_{X_{0}}dh\Big)^{p_{0}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{\gamma_{0}}\Big[\sup_{t>0}t^{-s_{0}p_{0}-p_{0}}\Big(\int_{\max\{-\sigma,-t\}}^{0}\|u(\sigma)-u(\sigma+h)\|_{X_{0}}dh\Big)^{p_{0}}\Big]d\sigma

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:defFmnorm}}}{{\leq}}\Big([u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1),+}\Big)^{p_{0}}\lesssim\Big([u]_{{F}^{s_{0}}_{p_{0},p_{0}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{0}};X_{0})}^{(1),+}\Big)^{p_{0}},

where in the last step we used (
3.3
) and
\ell^{p_{0}}\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}
. It remains to prove (
4.15
):

\displaystyle\|\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{\xi_{1}}T_{1}(\sigma)\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\frac{d\sigma}{\sigma};X_{1})}^{p_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-(\delta_{1}+1)p_{1}-1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\sigma}\|u(\tau)\|_{X_{1}}d\tau\Big)^{p_{1}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:HardyYoung}}}{{\lesssim}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma^{-\delta_{1}p_{1}-1}\|u(\sigma)\|_{X_{1}}^{p_{1}}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{p_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})}^{p_{1}}.

∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

The idea is to reduce the claim to the one proven in Lemma

by mixed-derivative and Sobolev embeddings. For the sake of clarity we divide the proof into several steps. In Steps 1-4 we will prove (

Step 1
.
Proof of (
4.7
) in the case
s_{0}<1
,
s_{1}>0
,

,
q_{0}=p_{0}
,

By Corollary

(4.16)

F^{s_{1}}_{p_{1},p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}};X_{1})\hookrightarrow L^{p_{1}}(I,w_{\gamma_{1}-s_{1}p_{1}};X_{1}).

Since

\displaystyle\|\mathtt{Tr}_{0}u\|_{(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}}

.
Proof of (
4.7
) in the case
s_{0}<k+1
,
s_{1}>k
,
q_{0}=p_{0}
,
q_{1}=p_{1}
,

Step 3
.
Proof of (
4.7
) in the case
q_{0}=p_{0}

\delta_{1}<k

\|u\|_{F^{\widetilde{s}_{i}}_{\widetilde{p}_{i},\widetilde{p}_{i}}(I,w_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{i}};(X_{0},X_{1})_{\vartheta_{i},1})}\lesssim\\

4.2.
Embedding into Spaces of Continuous Functions

Next we prove that under the condition
\gamma_{0},\gamma_{1}\geq 0
, the functions
u\in{\mathbb{F}}
are actually continuous with values in
(X_{0},X_{1})_{\theta,p}
. The argument is by a translation argument, which however is not completely standard in Triebel–Lizorkin spaces. For a Banach space
X
,
C_{\rm b}([0,\infty);X)
denotes the space of all bounded and continuous maps
u:[0,\infty)\to X
endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{C_{\rm b}([0,\infty);X)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\|u(t)\|_{X}.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

,

a Banach space,

,

it is important that

is defined in terms of the iterated Bochner space

Proof.

As the case

follows easily from the case

It will be convenient to write

,

\displaystyle\|T(t)f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}};X)}

\displaystyle=\|(S_{n}T(t)f)_{n}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};X))}=\|T(t)(S_{n}f)_{n}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};X))}

as

is an isometry on

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\|T(t)f-f\|_{F^{s}_{p,q}({\mathbb{R}};X)}=\|(T(t)-I)(S_{n}f)_{n}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}};\ell^{q}_{s}({\mathbb{N}}_{0};X))}\to 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\searrow 0

whereas, these were only available on
(0,T)
before. In a follow-up paper we plan to use these estimates to derive new a priori estimates for quasi-linear (stochastic) PDEs considered on homogenous function spaces with critical scaling (see
[
1
,
47
]
).

6.1.
Fractional evolution equations

Equations of fractional type
arise in many physical applications and it is the basic model for anomalous diffusion, and we refer the reader to
[
8
,
19
,
24
,
27
,
43
,
58
]
for more details.
Evolution equations of fractional type fit in the framework of Volterra integral equations. Introductions into Volterra equations can be found in
[
5
,
21
,
43
,
58
]
.

In this section we will focus on a regularity problem for the fractional evolution equation on a Banach space
X
:

(6.1)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u+Au=f\ \ \text{on ${\mathbb{R}}_{+}$},

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
and
A
is a sectorial operator on
X
. For simplicity we only consider Dirichlet boundary conditions:
u(0)=0
, and
u^{\prime}(0)=0
if
\alpha>1
.

Eq. (
6.1
) is extensively studied in the literature
[
43
,
44
,
58
]
, where actually
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is replaced by a more general operator. We will focus on the latter case and only consider zero initial values for simplicity.

In
[
44
]
weighted
L^{p}
-regularity was studied for (
6.1
) under the assumption that
0\in\rho(A)
. Moreover, trace estimates for the solution were provided under the assumption that
A^{1/\alpha}
is sectorial. In this section we will use our results to remove the conditions that
0\in\rho(A)
and that
A^{1/\alpha}
is sectorial.

Motivated by (
3.15
) we say that
u
is a
strong solution
to (
6.1
) with initial value zero if

(6.2)

u(t)+\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*Au(t)=\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}*f(t),\quad\text{\text{a.e.} \ $t\geq 0$},

where
*
denotes the convolution on
{\mathbb{R}}
, and where we use the zero extensions of
Au
and
f
on
(-\infty,0)
and
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}(t):=\frac{|t|^{\alpha-1}{{\bf 1}}_{(0,\infty)}(t)}{\Gamma(\alpha)}
(see (
3.15
)).

Below we use the more suggestive notation
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{D}
is as in (
3.5
). Our main result concerning (
6.1
) or (
6.2
) is:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a UMD space. Let
\alpha\in(0,2)
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in[0,p-1)
. Suppose that
A
is
R
-sectorial on
X
of angle

\omega_{R}(A)<{\pi}(1-\frac{\alpha}{2})
.
Then for each
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
there exists a unique strong solution
u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty),w_{\gamma};{{\mathsf{D}}}(A))
to (
6.2
) which satisfies

(6.3)

\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\|Au\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}.

Moreover, if
j\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha>j+\frac{1+\gamma}{p}
, then

\|\partial_{t}^{j}u\|_{C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(1-\frac{1+\gamma}{\alpha p}-\frac{j}{\alpha},p))}+\|\partial_{t}^{j}u\|_{C_{{\rm{b}},\frac{\gamma}{p}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(1-\frac{1}{\alpha p}-\frac{j}{\alpha},p))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)},

where
C_{{\rm{b}},\gamma/p}
is the weighted space defined in (
4.23
).

Proof.

Step 1: Existence and uniqueness on
(0,T)
with
T\in(0,\infty)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}([0,T];{\mathsf{D}}(A))
. By
[
43
, Proposition 1.2]
there exists a unique strong solution
u\in C([0,T];{\mathsf{D}}(A))
to (
6.2
) on
[0,T]
. Then by (
3.15
)

u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{\alpha})
and (
6.1
) holds on
(0,T)
.
We will first show that the following estimate holds uniformly in
T>0
:

(6.4)

\|Au\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)}.

It follows from the text below Proposition
3.9
that
\mathfrak{D}_{T}^{\alpha}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha
with uniform estimates in
T
.
Since
\omega_{R}(A)+\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha<\pi
, the Kalton–Weis theorem (see
[
46
, Corollary 4.5.9]
) gives (
6.4
).

From (
6.2
), Young’s inequality and (
6.4
), we additionally obtain that

(6.5)

\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)}\lesssim_{T}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)}.

Via a standard density argument using (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) we obtain existence and uniqueness of a strong solution to (
6.2
) for general
f\in L^{p}(0,T,w_{\gamma};X)
. Moreover, the estimates (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) hold as well.

Step 2. Existence and uniqueness on
(0,\infty)
and the proof of (
6.3
):

Let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
, and for each integer
n\geq 1
let
u^{n}
be the solution to (
6.2
) on
[0,n]
. Then by uniqueness, for all
n\geq m
,
u^{n}=u^{m}
on
[0,m]
. Therefore, defining
u:[0,\infty)\to X
by
u(t)=u^{n}(t)
for
t\in[0,n]
, we obtain a strong solution to (
6.2
). Uniqueness is clear from the uniqueness on finite time intervals. From Step 1 we see that (
6.4
) holds with
T
-independent constants. Letting
T\to\infty
, we obtain (
6.4
) for
T=\infty
.
From (
6.2
) and (
3.16
) we deduce that
u\in\dot{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha})
and
\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}u=f-Au
. This also implies the estimate (
6.3
).

Step 3. Proof of the trace estimate:

As before, by density it suffices to consider
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathsf{D}}(A)\cap{\mathsf{R}}(A))
. Since
A^{-1}f\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};{\mathsf{D}}(A))
, the above estimates also hold with
(u,f)
replaced by
(A^{-1}u,A^{-1}f)
. By (
6.3
) this implies
u\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
for these special functions
f
. Together with (
6.3
) and Proposition
3.9
this implies
u\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha})\hookrightarrow H^{\alpha,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)
.
Now if
\alpha>\frac{1+\gamma}{p}+j
with
j\in\{0,1\}
, then by Corollary
5.3
with
\theta=1-\frac{1+\gamma}{\alpha p}-\frac{j}{\alpha}
, (
6.3
) and Proposition
3.9
,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{j}u\|_{C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(\theta,p))}

\displaystyle\lesssim[\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}^{\alpha}u]_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}^{1-\theta}\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A))}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=[\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}u]_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}^{1-\theta}\|Au\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

The
C_{{\rm{b}},\gamma/p}
-term is estimated similarly.
∎

Note that if
0\in\rho(A)
in Theorem
6.1
, then one obtains

\|u\|_{H^{\alpha,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};{\mathsf{D}}(A))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};X)}

and therefore
\dot{{\mathsf{D}}}_{A}
can be replaced by
{\mathsf{D}}_{A}
in the final estimate of Theorem
6.1
. The same holds if
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is replaced by
(0,T)
with
T\in(0,\infty)
.

We conclude this section by analysing a double non-local diffusion equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
(see
[
28
]
for the case
\alpha\in(0,1)
). Setting
\beta=1
, one obtains the fractional heat equation.

Example 6.2

(Double fractional diffusion equation)
.

Let
\alpha,\beta>0
,
q,p\in(1,\infty)
and
\gamma\in[0,p-1)
be such that
\alpha\in(\frac{1+\gamma}{p},2)
. On
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
consider:

(6.6)

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)+(-\Delta)^{\beta}u(t)=f(t),\quad t>0,

with Dirichlet initial condition(s). To recast the above problem in the form (
6.2
) we introduce the fractional Laplacian.
To begin, let us regard the Laplace operator as a map

A_{L}:=-\Delta:W^{2,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\subseteq L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

By
[
26
, Theorem 10.2.25]
,
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A_{L})=0
. Therefore,
A^{\beta}_{L}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
0
and
{\mathsf{D}}(A_{L}^{\beta})=H^{2\beta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
In particular, by
[
26
, Theorem 10.3.4(2)]
,
A^{\beta}_{L}
is
R
-sectorial and
\omega_{R}(A_{L}^{\beta})=0
.
As one may expect, the operator just defined satisfies

(6.7)

A_{L}^{\beta}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\cdot|^{2\beta}\mathcal{F}(f)),

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Fourier transform and
f\in H^{2\beta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(see e.g.
[
22
, Subsection 8.3]
).

Combining the description of the scale
\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\beta}_{L})
in
[
22
, p. 234]
and the interpolation results in
[
6
, Theorem 6.3.1]
we get, for all
\eta\geq 0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
,

(6.8)

\dot{\mathsf{D}}((A_{L}^{\beta})^{\eta})=\dot{H}^{2\beta\eta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A^{\beta}_{L}}(\eta,p)=\dot{B}^{2\eta\beta}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

where
\dot{H}
and
\dot{B}
denote the
homogeneous
Bessel potential and Besov spaces (see e.g.
[
6
, Section 6.3]
), respectively. Now we have rewritten (
6.6
) in the form (
6.2
) with
A=A^{\beta}_{L}
. Therefore, Theorem
6.1
ensures that for each

f\in L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))

there exists a unique strong solution
u\in L^{p}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty),w_{\gamma};H^{2\beta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
to (
6.6
), and

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+\|u\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};\dot{H}^{2\beta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}.

Moreover, if
\alpha>j+\frac{1+\gamma}{p}
with
j\in\{0,1\}
, then

\displaystyle\|\partial^{j}_{t}u\|_{C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);\dot{B}^{\delta}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}+

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{{\rm{b}},\gamma/p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{B}^{\varepsilon}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\gamma};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}

where
\delta=2\beta(1-\frac{1+\gamma}{\alpha p}-\frac{j}{\alpha})
and
\varepsilon=2\beta(1-\frac{1}{\alpha p}-\frac{j}{\alpha})
.

Remark 6.3
.

Example
6.2
can easily be extended to the case where
\Delta
is replaced by an elliptic operator with
x
-dependent coefficients under continuity conditions on the coefficients. Moreover, the abstract setting of Theorem
6.1
is flexible enough to cover boundary value problems as well.

Remark 6.4
.

A special class of Volterra type equations in the case of coefficients which are
(t,x)
-dependent is treated in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
assuming only measurability in
t
and very weak conditions in space. They prove maximal regularity results, and their approach is completely different than the one considered here. They did not consider trace estimates, but they can easily be obtained by combining their results with ours.

6.2.
Stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity and homogeneous spaces

In this section we prove some new regularity estimates for stochastic convolutions using the trace estimates of the previous section, which extend some of the stochastic maximal regularity results in
[
3
,
42
]
to the case where
0\notin\rho(A)
. Similar extensions can be done for the stochastic Volterra equations considered in
[
14
]
, but we will not consider those here.

Stochastic maximal regularity has been recently used to obtain well-posedness for quasilinear stochastic PDE in
[
1
,
2
]
. For unexplained notations and in particular stochastic integration theory and the theory of
\gamma
-radonifying operators we refer the reader to the latter two papers and
[
26
, Chapter 9]
. For a general overview on stochastic evolution equations we refer to
[
9
]
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and let
W_{H}
an
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathfrak{P},({\mathscr{F}}_{t})_{t\geq 0},{\mathbb{P}})
.
Let
X
be a Banach space with UMD and type
2
(see e.g.
[
26
, Chapter 7]
), and let
\gamma(H,X)
denote the space of
\gamma
-radonifying operators (see e.g.
[
26
, Chapter 9]
). Given a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
with generator
-A
, we consider the stochastic evolution equation:

(6.9)

du(t)+Au(t)dt=G(t)dW_{H}(t),\qquad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\ \ \ u(0)=0.

Here
G\in L^{0}(\Omega;L^{2}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\gamma(H,X)))
is strongly progressively measurable.
The
mild solution
to (
6.9
) is given by the
stochastic convolution

S\diamond G:[0,\infty)\to X
defined as

(6.10)

u(t)=S\diamond G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)G(s)dW_{H}(s),\quad t\geq 0.

Below we use the more suggestive notation
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}=\mathfrak{D}^{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{D}
is as in (
3.5
). The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
,
\kappa\in[0,\frac{p}{2}-1)
and
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
.
Let
X
be isomorphic to a closed subspace of an
L^{q}
-space over a sigma-finite measure space with
q\in[2,\infty)
. Assume that
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus on
X
and
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)<\frac{\pi}{2}
. Let
(S(t))_{t\geq 0}
be the semigroup generated by
-A
. Let
G\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega,w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))
be strongly progressive measurable. Then for all
\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})

the mild solution
u
to (
6.9
) satisfies

(6.11)

{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{t}^{\theta}A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}u\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}^{p}.

Moreover,

{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1+\kappa}{p},p}))}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{C_{{\rm{b}},\frac{\kappa}{p}}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}({\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p},p}))}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}^{p},

where
C_{{\rm{b}},\kappa/p}
is the weighted space defined in (
4.23
).

Proof.

We first prove (
6.11
). By an approximation argument it suffices to prove the estimates for a uniformly bounded progressively measurable step process
G:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\to\gamma(H,{\mathsf{D}}(A))
. The method is similar to
[
42
, Theorem 1.2]
(and
[
3
, Theorem 7.16]
in the weighted case). However, since we do not assume
0\in\rho(A)
, some modifications are required.

Step 1: Convergence of approximating problem.
In some part of the proof below, we need invertibility of the involved operators. Therefore, we consider
A_{\varepsilon}=A+\varepsilon
and
S_{\varepsilon}(t)=e^{-\varepsilon t}S(t)
, where
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Let
u_{\varepsilon}=S_{\varepsilon}\diamond G
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}(t)\in{\mathsf{D}}(A)
a.s. for all
\varepsilon,t\geq 0
. Next, we prove that

(6.12)

A^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\to A^{\delta}u\ \text{in}\ Y_{T}:=L^{p}((0,T)\times\Omega;{\mathsf{D}}(A)),\ \ \text{for all}\ \delta\in[0,1],\,T\in(0,\infty).

Using
[
41
, Corollary 3.10]
one can check that (
6.12
) holds for
\delta=1
.
For
\delta\in[0,1)
, note that

(6.13)

\displaystyle\|A^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}-A^{\delta}u\|_{Y_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{Y_{T}}+\|A^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}u-A^{\delta}u\|_{Y_{T}}.

For the first term in (
6.13
), by the moment inequality (see
[
22
, Proposition 6.6.4]
) and Hölder’s inequality we find that

\|A^{\delta}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{Y_{T}}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}-u\|_{Y_{T}}^{1-\delta}\|(A+\varepsilon)(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{Y_{T}}^{\delta}\to 0.

The second term in (
6.13
) tends to zero by
[
35
, Lemma 4.1.11]
and dominated convergence.

Step 2: A priori estimates uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.

Since
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A)<\frac{\pi}{2}
, the same holds for
A+\varepsilon
with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
. Since we assumed
X
has a special structure, by
[
42
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
3
, Lemma 7.11]
, for all
\theta\in[0,1/2)
,

(6.14)

{\mathbb{E}}\|A_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}S_{\varepsilon,\theta}\diamond G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}^{p},

where
S_{\varepsilon,\theta}(t)=\frac{t^{-\theta}}{\Gamma(1-\theta)}S_{\varepsilon}(t)
and the implicit constant does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Next we prove (
6.11
) for
\theta\in(0,1/2)
with
(u,A)
replaced by
(u_{\varepsilon},A_{\varepsilon})
with uniform estimates for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. Let
\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon}
be the closed linear densely defined operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)
with domain
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon})=L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};{\mathsf{D}}(A))
given by
\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon}v(t):=A_{\varepsilon}v(t)
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
. One can check that
\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon}
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\omega_{H^{\infty}}(\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon})=\omega_{H^{\infty}}(A_{\varepsilon})<\pi/2
with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon\geq 0
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.9
, the derivative
\mathfrak{D}
defined in (
3.5
) has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
\pi/2
.

From now one let
\varepsilon>0
. By
[
46
, Corollary 4.5.9]
,
\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon}:=\mathfrak{D}+\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon}
with
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{C}_{\varepsilon})={\mathsf{D}}(\mathfrak{D})\cap{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon})
is a sectorial operator on
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)
, and as in
[
42
]
,

\mathscr{C}^{-\theta}_{\varepsilon}f(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\theta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\theta-1}S_{\varepsilon}(t-s)f(s)ds,\ \ \ f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X).

Moreover, arguing as in the proof of
[
42
, Theorem 1.2]
, one obtains that

\mathscr{C}^{-\theta}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon,\theta}\diamond G)(t)=A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(t),\ \ \text{ a.s.\ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

Combining this with
A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon,\theta}\diamond G,A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)
a.s., it follows that
A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathsf{D}}(\mathscr{C}^{\theta})
a.s. and

(6.15)

A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon,\theta}\diamond G(t)=\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\varepsilon}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})(t),\ \ \text{ a.s.\ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+}.

To proceed further, one can check that,
\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon}
is
R
-sectorial of angle
<\pi/2
with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
(see
[
26
, Theorem 10.3.4(2)]
). Thus, by
[
30
, Lemma 10]
, the set
\{\lambda^{\theta}(\lambda+\mathscr{A}_{\varepsilon})^{-\theta}:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\phi}\}
is
R
-bounded for some
\phi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
. The Kalton-Weis theorem
[
46
, Theorem 4.5.6]
ensures that

(6.16)

\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}\mathscr{C}^{-\theta}_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathscr{L}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X))

with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:boundedness_Co_partial}}}{{\lesssim}}{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathscr{C}^{\theta}_{\varepsilon}A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:stochastic_proof_equality}}}{{=}}{\mathbb{E}}\|A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}S_{\varepsilon,\theta}\diamond G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estimate_SMR_homongeneous}}}{{\lesssim}}{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}

again with uniform estimates in
\varepsilon
.

Step 3: Weak convergence argument.

It follows from the previous step that
A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\dot{{\mathsf{D}}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}))
, and hence there exists a sequence such that
A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to v
weakly in
\dot{{\mathsf{D}}}(\mathfrak{D}^{\theta})
,
and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{t}^{\theta}v\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}\|\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon_{n}}u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}^{p},

where we used our uniform estimate in the last step.
To complete the proof of (
6.11
) it remains to show
v=A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}u
. Fix
T\in(0,\infty)
. By taking restrictions (see (

)) it follows that

A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon_{n}}u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to v
weakly in

. Since
\mathfrak{D}^{\theta}_{T}

A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}_{\varepsilon_{n}}u_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to v
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T,w_{\kappa};X))
. By Step 1,
v=A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}u
on
(0,T)
as required.

Step 3: The trace estimate.

By density it suffices to consider
G
to be a uniformly bounded progressively measurable step process
G:{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\times\Omega\to\gamma(H,{\mathsf{D}}(A)\cap{\mathsf{R}}(A))
. Note that by the previous step
A^{1/2}u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X))
. Moreover, due to the fact that
G
also takes value in
{\mathsf{D}}(A^{-\sigma})
for all
\sigma\in[0,1/2)
(see
[
31

), we additionally have

(6.17)

A^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}u=A^{\frac{1}{2}}A^{-\sigma}u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};X)).

Fix
\sigma\in(\frac{1+\kappa}{p},\frac{1}{2})
.
By (
6.11
) we have

(6.18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\sigma}u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma})))\ \ \text{and}\ \ u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}})))

with corresponding estimates in terms of
\|G\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X)))}
. Moreover, combining the latter with (
6.17
) and Proposition
3.9
, we obtain that

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in L^{p}(\Omega;\prescript{}{0}{H}^{\sigma,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma})))\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\Omega;H^{\sigma,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}))).

Applying, Corollary
5.3
pointwise in
\Omega
, and using (
6.18
) and

(\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}),\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}}))_{1-\frac{1+\kappa}{p\sigma},p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Dd_A_reiteration}}}{{=}}\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1+\kappa}{p},p),

one gets the required estimates for the
C_{\rm{b}}
-norm. The estimate for the
C_{{\rm{b}},\kappa/p}
-norm follows similarly.
∎

If
0\in\rho(A)
in Theorem

{\mathbb{E}}\|S\diamond G\|_{H^{\theta,p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\frac{1}{2}-\theta}))}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|G\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\gamma(H,X))}^{p},

As an application we derive a
new
maximal estimate for the stochastic heat equation using homogenous spaces.
In the following,
(w_{t}^{n}:t\geq 0)_{n\geq 1}
denotes a sequence of independent standard Brownian motions. This determines a cylindrical Brownian motion on
\ell^{2}
. We will use that
\gamma(\ell^{2};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))=L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\ell^{2})
(see
[
41
, Proposition 2.6]
).

Example 6.6

(Stochastic heat equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
)
.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
,
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\kappa\in[0,\frac{p}{2}-1)
. The stochastic heat equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
can be formally written as

(6.19)

\begin{cases}du-\Delta udt=\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{n}dw^{n}_{t},\qquad t>0,\\
u(0)=0.\end{cases}

Using the notation introduced in Example
6.2
, we recast (
6.19
) in the form (
6.9
) with
A=-\Delta
on
L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then
A
has a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus of angle
0
and
\dot{\mathsf{D}}(A^{\eta})
and
\dot{\mathsf{D}}_{A}(\eta,q)
are a special case of (
6.8
) for
\beta=1
.
Thus, Theorem
6.5
ensures that for any progressively measurable
(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\ell^{2})))

there exists a unique mild solution
u
to (
6.19
).
Moreover, for all
\theta\in[0,1/2)
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\|\partial_{t}^{\theta}u

\displaystyle\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};\dot{H}^{1-2\theta,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p}+{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{C_{\rm{b}}([0,\infty);\dot{B}^{\alpha}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p}

\displaystyle+{\mathbb{E}}\|u\|_{C_{{\rm{b}},\kappa/p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+};\dot{B}^{\beta}_{q,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}^{p}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}\|(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}_{+},w_{\kappa};L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{d};\ell^{2}))}^{p},

where
\alpha=1-2\frac{1+\kappa}{p}

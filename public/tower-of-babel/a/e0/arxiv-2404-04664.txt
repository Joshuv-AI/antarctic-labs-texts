Global well-posedness and numerical justification of an effective micro-macro model for reactive transport in elastic perforated media
Jonas Knoch, Markus Gahn, Maria Neuss-Radu

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate an effective model for reactive transport in elastically deformable perforated media. This model was derived by formal asymptotic expansions in [ 25 ] , starting from a microscopic model consisting of a linear elasticity problem on a fixed domain, i.e. in the Lagrangian framework, and a problem for reactive transport on the current deformed domain, i.e. in the Eulerian framework. The effective model is of micro-macro type and features strong non-linear couplings. Here, we prove global existence in time and uniqueness for the effective micro-macro model under a smallness assumption for the data of the macroscopic elasticity subproblem. Moreover, we show numerically the convergence of microscopic solutions towards the solution of the effective model when the scale parameter ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 becomes smaller and smaller, and also compute the approximation error. The numerical justification of the formally derived effective micro-macro model is particularly important, as rigorous analytical convergence proofs or error estimates are not available so far. Finally, we compare the effective micro-macro model with alternative, simpler effective descriptions of transport in elastic perforated media.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the analysis and numerical investigation of an effective micro-macro model for reactive transport in elastically deformable perforated media. This model was derived previously in
[
25
]
by formal two-scale asymptotic expansions, starting from a microscopic model, and is motivated, for example, by biomedical applications concerning the transport of nutrients or drugs through deformable biological tissues, like pulmonary or cardiac tissue. The microscopic description consists of a linear quasi-static elasticity system within the
Lagrangian
framework on a periodically perforated reference domain representing the solid part of a porous medium and a system for reactive transport of multiple species in the current deformed solid domain, i.e. within the
Eulerian
framework. In preparation of the formal upscaling, the problem was transformed into a
unified Lagrangian
description by pulling back the transport problem onto the fixed, periodically perforated reference domain. The formally derived effective model is of micro-macro type and consists of effective (or macro) equations for the effective displacement and the effective concentrations, coupled with (micro) equations on the reference periodicity cell which characterizes the microstructure. In addition, the two
subsystems for elasticity and transport are coupled via non-linear coefficients. Let us mention that a rigorous convergence proof of the microscopic solutions to the solution of the effective model is not available so far.

The aim of this paper is to prove the well-posedness of the effective model and to study its approximation properties by means of numerical simulations. More specifically, we prove global existence and uniqueness for the effective micro-macro problem.
The main difficulty in our analysis is to establish the properties of the effective coefficients of the macroscopic transport problem which are nonlinearly coupled to the macroscopic displacement and the elasticity cell solutions. To overcome this difficulty, we first show higher regularity of the elasticity cell solutions and of the macroscopic displacement, which are independent of the transport subsystem, based on suitable regularity of the data. Here we benefit from the fact that due to scale separation, the mixed boundary conditions that occur in the microscopic problems are now split into inhomogeneous
Dirichlet
boundary conditions for the macroscopic displacement and homogeneous
Neumann
boudary conditions for the cell-problems. Then, the well-posedness of the diffusion cell problems and of the effective reactive transport problem is proved based on a smallness assumption on the data of the macroscopic elasticity problem. Finally, we study the sensitivity of the smallness assumption with respect to microscopic geometry and the elastic properties of the perforated material by means of numerical simulations, and identify critical settings that allow greater flexibility in the choice of data for the macroscopic elasticity problem.

Next, we investigate numerically the convergence of the solutions to the microscopic problems towards the solution of the effective micro-macro problem for smaller and smaller values of the scale parameter
\varepsilon>0
. Our convergence results, yielding estimated convergence orders of approximately
1
and
\frac{1}{2}
in the
L^{2}
and
H^{1}
-norm, respectively, numerically justify the formally derived effective micro-macro model. To further illustrate the approximation quality of the effective model, we present numerical simulations of the approximation errors, in particular when the first order corrector terms are added to the macroscopic solutions. Finally, we compare our effective micro-macro model with two simpler effective descriptions of transport in elastic perforated media and see, that those alternative approaches cannot capture all effects of the deformation on the transport which are observed for our effective micro-macro model. Let us mention, that the numerical investigations in this paper are using the computational framework developed in
[
25
]
based on the open source finite element library
deal.II

[
3
,
4
]
.

Regularity of the displacement

We start by summarizing known results about the elasticity cell solutions and the effective elasticity tensor.

To show higher regularity results for the effective displacement

Proof of Theorem

We first show that the homogenized elasticity tensor

\exists\lambda>0

With
\textbf{B}:=\boldsymbol{\eta}\otimes\boldsymbol{\xi}

\textbf{B}^{S},\textbf{B}^{A}

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}A^{*}_{ijkl}\eta_{k}\xi_{l}\eta_{i}\xi_{j}

Noting that

Now, from

3.2
Existence and uniqueness for the effective diffusion problem and the associated cell problems

The main difficulty in the study of the transport subsystem is to establish the properties of the effective coefficients
J^{*}
and
\textbf{D}^{*}
, see (
5
)-(
6
), among which e.g. the positivity of
J^{*}
is crucial for the well-posedness of the effective transport equation. To overcome this difficulty, a smallness condition on the data
\textbf{F}_{e}
and
\textbf{u}_{D}
of the macroscopic elasticity problem is assumed.

We start by analyzing the coefficients
J_{0}
and
\textbf{D}_{0}
, see (
9
)-(
10
), which enter the definition of the effective coefficients and depend nonlinearly on the macroscopic displacement

and the elasticity cell problems
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose assumptions (
A1
)-(
A5
) are satisfied. Let
C_{\textbf{u}}>0
be given as in (
16
), let
C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}>0
be given as in (
18
)
and suppose that

\|\textbf{F}_{e}(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega})^{n\times n}}+\|\textbf{u}_{D}(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega})^{n}}<\frac{1}{(n+n^{3}C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}})C_{\textbf{u}}}

(28)

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Then

i)

J_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(\widebar{\omega})))
with
0<J_{0}(t,x,y)\leq\Gamma
, where
\Gamma=n^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}
, for all
(t,x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}\times\widebar{Y}^{s}
.

ii)

\textbf{D}_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega};\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\text{per}}(\widebar{\omega})^{n\times n}))
is well-defined, symmetric and elliptic.

Proof.

i)

Remember that according to (
9
) we have

J_{0}(t,x,y)=\det\left(\textbf{E}_{n}+\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\textbf{e}(\textbf{u})_{ij}(t,x)\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}(y)\right).

Due to the regularity of
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}
and
u
established in Theorem
3.1
, we have

\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\widebar{\omega})^{n\times n}\text{ for $i,j=1,...,n$ and }\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T],\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega})^{n\times n}).

The regularity of
J_{0}
follows since
\det\colon\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth mapping. To show the positivity of
J_{0}
, we use Lemma
3.5
below. To this end, let us show that

M(t):=\sup_{(x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}\times\widebar{Y^{s}}}\left\lVert\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\textbf{e}(\textbf{u})_{ij}(t,x)\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}(y)\right\rVert_{2}<1

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle\leq n\sup_{x\in\widebar{\Omega}}\|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)\|_{\infty}\bigl(1+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\underbrace{\sup_{y\in\widebar{Y^{s}}}\|\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}(y)\|_{\infty}}_{=C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}}\bigr)

(29)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}C_{\textbf{u}}(n+n^{3}C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}})\left(\|\textbf{F}_{e}(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\Omega)^{n\times n}}+\|\textbf{u}_{D}(t)\|_{\mathcal{C}^{2,\sigma}(\Omega)^{n}}\right)<1

for all
t\in[0,T]
. Here,
(*)
follows from (
24
) and the last inequality follows from the smallness assumption (
17
). Then, by Lemma
3.5
, we conclude
J_{0}(t,x,y)>0
for all
(t,x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}\times\widebar{Y}^{s}
. Finally, to estimate
J_{0}
from above, we compute by virtue of (
24
) and (
17
):

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sup_{(t,x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}\times\widebar{Y^{s}}}&\|\textbf{F}_{0}(t,x,y)\|_{\infty}\\
&=\sup_{(t,x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}\times\widebar{Y^{s}}}\|\textbf{E}_{n}+\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\textbf{e}(\textbf{u})_{ij}(t,x)\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}(y)\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq 1+\sup_{(t,x,y)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}\times\widebar{Y^{s}}}\|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\textbf{e}(\textbf{u})_{ij}(t,x)\nabla_{y}\boldsymbol{\chi}_{ij}(y)\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq 1+\sup_{(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}}\|\nabla_{x}\textbf{u}(t,x)\|_{\infty}(1+n^{2}C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}})\\
&\leq 1+\frac{1+n^{2}C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}}{n+n^{3}C_{\boldsymbol{\chi}}}=1+\frac{1}{n}.\end{split}

Then, by
Hadamard
’s inequality
[
21
, Corollary 7.8.2]
, we have

J_{0}(t,x,y)=\det(\textbf{F}_{0}(t,x,y))\leq n^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}=:\Gamma.

ii)

Due to i), it holds that
J_{0}(t,x,y)=\det(\textbf{F}_{0}(t,x,y))>0
for all
(t,x,y)\in[0,T]\times\widebar{\Omega}\times\widebar{Y^{s}}
, which is equivalent to the invertibility of
\textbf{F}_{0}
; hence
\textbf{D}_{0}
is well-defined. The regularity follows since the inverse of a matrix with positive determinant can be computed in terms of the determinant and the cofactor matrix which involves only smooth mappings. The symmetry of
\textbf{D}_{0}
follows from the symmetry of
\widehat{\textbf{D}}
:

D_{0,ij}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}J_{0}F_{0,ik}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{kl}F_{0,lj}^{-T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:symmetry_of_D_hat}}}{{=}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}J_{0}F_{0,jl}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{lk}F_{0,ki}^{-T}=D_{0,ji},

for
i,j=1,...,n
and for all
(t,x,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y^{s}
. In order to prove the ellipticity of
\textbf{D}_{0}
, let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, by using the ellipticity of
\widehat{\textbf{D}}
, we get

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{0}\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=J_{0}\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}F_{0,ik}^{-1}\widehat{D}_{kl}F_{0,lj}^{-T}\xi_{j}\xi_{i}=J_{0}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\widehat{D}_{kl}\Biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{0,lj}^{-1}\xi_{j}\Biggr)\Biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}F_{0,ki}^{-1}\xi_{i}\Biggr)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:ellipticity_of_D_hat}}}{{\geq}}J_{0}\delta|\textbf{F}_{0}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}\geq 0,

for all
(t,x,y)\in(0,T)\times\Omega\times Y^{s}
. Equality in the last step is only achieved for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\equiv 0
since
\textbf{F}_{0}^{-1}
is a regular matrix. Thus we have shown that
\textbf{D}_{0}
is symmetric positive definite and we can conclude

\textbf{D}_{0}\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}\geq\lambda_{\text{min}}|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}

with
\lambda_{\text{min}}>0
the smallest eigenvalue of
\textbf{D}_{0}
.

∎

For the sake of a self-contained presentation, we state the following result about the invertibility of a perturbation of the identity matrix
\textbf{E}_{n}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\textbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with
\|\textbf{B}\|_{2}<1

Proof.

The proof is adopted from
[

. Let
\tau\in[0,1]
. Then
\textbf{E}_{n}+\tau\textbf{B}
is invertible, see e.g.
[

(30)

Now, let us define

\delta\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\;\delta(\tau)=\det(\textbf{E}_{n}+\tau\textbf{B}).

We have that
\delta
is continuous with
\delta(0)=1>0
. If we assume that
\delta(1)<0
, then, by

’s intermediate zero theorem, there exists
\tau_{0}\in(0,1)
with
\delta(\tau_{0})=0
. This contradicts (

\det(\textbf{E}_{n}+\textbf{B})=\delta(1)>0

∎

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert(\textbf{D}_{0}(t+h,x,\cdot_{y})-\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,\cdot_{y}))(\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(t+h,x,\cdot_{y})+\textbf{e}_{i})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(Y^{s})^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{y\in\widebar{Y^{s}}}\left\lVert\textbf{D}_{0}(t+h,x,\cdot_{y})-\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,\cdot_{y})\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(t+h,x,\cdot_{y})+\textbf{e}_{i}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(Y^{s})^{n}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{h\rightarrow 0}0,

due to the continuity of
\textbf{D}_{0}
with respect to time and (
31
).

For the differentiability of
\eta_{i}
with respect to
t
, let us subtract (
32
) from (
33
), divide by
h>0
, subtract (
34
) and add suitable terms. We obtain

\displaystyle\int_{Y^{s}}

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,y)\nabla_{y}\left(\frac{\eta_{i}(t+h,x,y)-\eta_{i}(t,x,\cdot_{y})}{h}-\theta_{i}(t,x,y)\right)\cdot\nabla_{y}\zeta(y)\textrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{Y^{s}}\left(\frac{\textbf{D}_{0}(t+h,x,y)-\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,y)}{h}-\partial_{t}\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,y)\right)\left(\textbf{e}_{i}-\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(t+h,x,y)\right)\cdot\nabla_{y}\zeta(y)\textrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{Y^{s}}\partial_{t}\textbf{D}_{0}(t,x,y)\nabla_{y}\left(\eta_{i}(t+h,x,y)-\eta_{i}(t,x,y)\right)\cdot\nabla_{y}\zeta(y)\textrm{d}y

for all
\zeta\in W_{\text{per}}(\omega)
,
i=1,...,n
and
(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\widebar{\Omega}
. Then, by virtue of (
31
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\lVert\frac{\eta_{i}(t+h,x,\cdot_{y})-\eta_{i}(t,x,\cdot_{y})}{h}-\theta_{i}(t,x,\cdot_{y})\Big\rVert_{H^{1}(Y^{s})}\xrightarrow{h\rightarrow 0}0,

by similar estimates as above, using the regularity of
\textbf{D}_{0}
and
\eta_{i}
as well as (
31
). Consequently, we infer
\partial_{t}\eta_{i}=\theta_{i}
. The continuity of the derivative
\theta_{i}
follows with similar steps as before from the continuity of
\partial_{t}\textbf{D}_{0}
. In particular, we can extend
\eta_{i}
and
\theta_{i}
continuously to the boundary of the time interval.

The regularity of
\eta_{i}
with respect to
x
can be shown analogously. Altogether this yields the claimed regularity
\eta_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega};W_{\text{per}}(\omega))
for all
i=1,...,n
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose assumptions (
A1
)-(
A5
) as well as the smallness assumption (
17
) are satisfied. Then we have

i)

J^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega}))
with
0<\gamma^{*}\leq J^{*}(t,x)\leq\Gamma^{*}
,
\gamma^{*},\Gamma^{*}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, for all
(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}
,

ii)

\textbf{D}^{*}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}(\widebar{\Omega})^{n\times n})
is symmetric and elliptic.

Proof.

i)

The
\mathcal{C}^{1}
-
\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}
-regularity follows from the regularity of
J_{0}
. Due to the continuity and positivity of
J_{0}
, there exists
\gamma^{*}>0
such that

J^{*}(t,x)\geq\gamma^{*}\quad\forall(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}.

On the other hand

\sup_{(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}}J^{*}(t,x)=\sup_{(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}}\int_{Y^{s}}J_{0}(t,x,y)\textrm{d}y\leq\Gamma|Y^{s}|:=\Gamma^{*}

with
\Gamma>0
from Proposition
3.4
.

ii)

\mathcal{C}^{1}
-
\mathcal{C}^{1,\sigma}
-regularity and symmetry follow from the regularity and symmetry of
\textbf{D}_{0}
and the regularity of
\eta_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
. To prove the ellipticity, we follow
[
12
, Proposition 6.12]
. Let
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define
w(t,x,y):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}(y_{i}+\eta_{i}(t,x,y))
. We compute

\displaystyle\textbf{D}^{*}(t,x)\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}\int_{Y^{s}}D_{0,kl}(t,x,y)\xi_{j}\partial_{l}(y_{j}+\eta_{j}(t,x,y)\xi_{i}\partial_{k}(y_{i}+\eta_{i}(t,x,y))\textrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{\text{min}}\|\nabla_{y}w(t,x,\cdot_{y})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(Y^{s})^{n}}\geq 0

for all
(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}
using the ellipticity of
\textbf{D}_{0}
shown in Proposition
3.4
. It remains to show that equality in the last step is achieved only for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\equiv 0
. To this end, assume equality to hold for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\neq 0
. Then,
w
is independent of
y
and thus there exists
c=c(t,x)
such that

c(t,x)=w(t,x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}(y_{i}+\eta_{i}(t,x,y))

which is equivalent to

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\eta_{i}(t,x,y)=c(t,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{3}\xi_{i}y_{i}.

This is a contradiction since the right-hand side is not periodic with respect to
y
for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\neq 0
. Thus we have shown that
\textbf{D}^{*}(t,x)
is symmetric positive definite for all
(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}
and therefore

\textbf{D}^{*}(t,x)\boldsymbol{\xi}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}\geq\lambda^{*}_{\text{min}}(t,x)|\boldsymbol{\xi}|^{2}

for all
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and all
(t,x)\in\widebar{\Omega}_{T}
with
\lambda^{*}_{\text{min}}(t,x)>0
the smallest eigenvalue of
\textbf{D}^{*}(t,x)
.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

The global existence for the model investigated within this chapter critically depends on the smallness assumption (
17
) for the data of the macroscopic elasticity problem. The smallness assumption is given in terms of the constants
C_{\textbf{u}}=C_{\textbf{u}}(\Omega,\sigma,\textbf{A}^{*})

and
\textbf{u}_{D}

with respect to

and

by means of numerical simulations and identify critical settings which lead to small values for

.

Comparison of the effective micro-macro model with alternative models for transport problems in elastic perforated media

Figure 9
:
Current mass over time according to different descriptions of transport processes in elastically deformable perforated media.

The effective micro-macro problem
(
3
)-(
11
) was derived in
[
25
]
from a microscopic problem in mixed
Eulerian
/
Lagrangian
formulation by first transforming the microscopic transport problem onto the fixed reference domain and then formally homogenizing the problem on the fixed domain. As a result, the effective model has a strongly coupled micro-macro structure, requiring a high numerical effort for the simulations.

In this section, we discuss two alternative approaches for the derivation of effective models for transport processes in elastic perforated media, which lead to ”simpler” models with a reduced numerical complexity. Then, we compare their solutions with the solution of the effective micro-macro model for the simulation scenario presented at the beginning of the previous section.

The first approach, referred to as
alternative approach A
, is to formulate the microscopic elasticity-transport problem directly in the unified
Lagrangian
framework, i.e. to formulate the microscopic transport problem on the reference domain
\Omega^{\text{s}}_{\varepsilon}
. Previously, this approach has been used, e.g., in
[
23
]
, where transport inside elastically deformable tissue cells has been coupled to transport in the extracellular space. In the microscopic model, the fluid-solid-interface has been linearized, resulting in transport equations on the reference fluid and solid domain respectively. In
[
10
]
, transport processes in elastically deformable domains have been considered in the reference domain and then, for the visualization, a push forward of the concentration onto the deformed domain was considered. Thus, following
approach A
, i.e., starting from the transport equation in the reference domain, the method of asymptotic expansion directly leads to the classical effective model for diffusion problems in fixed domains

|Y^{s}|\partial_{t}c_{A}-\nabla\cdot\left(\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}\nabla c_{A}\right)=|Y^{s}|f_{d}\quad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega.

(41)

Here, the entries of the constant effective diffusion tensor
\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
are given by

\textbf{D}^{*}_{A,ij}=\int_{Y^{s}}\widehat{\textbf{D}}\left(\textbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(y)\right)\cdot\left(\textbf{e}_{j}+\nabla_{y}\eta_{j}(y)\right)\textrm{d}y.

The cell problems are independent of
t
and
x
:

\displaystyle-\nabla_{y}\cdot\left[\widehat{\textbf{D}}\left(\textbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(y)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }Y^{s},

\displaystyle-\widehat{\textbf{D}}\left(\textbf{e}_{i}+\nabla_{y}\eta_{i}(y)\right)\cdot\textbf{n}_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\Gamma,

\displaystyle\eta_{i}\mbox{ is }Y^{s}\mbox{-periodic in $y$, }\,\int_{Y^{s}}\eta_{i}\textrm{d}y=0.

In this case, the computation of the effective diffusion coefficient
\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}
can be done once and for all at the beginning of the simulation, analogously to the computation of the effective elasticity tensor
\textbf{A}^{*}
in Algorithm
1
As a result, the computational effort is significantly reduced in comparison to the effective micro-macro model (
3
)-(
11
). For the graphical presentation of the simulation results the concentration is transformed to the current deformed domain via the push-forward

\widehat{c}_{A}(t,\widehat{x}):=c_{A}(t,\textbf{S}^{-1}(t,\widehat{x})),

using the macroscopic deformation
\textbf{S}(t,x):=x+\textbf{u}(t,x)
.

A second approach, referred to as
alternative approach B
, consists of using the constant effective coefficients from the previous approach A (computed on the reference domain), including the effective diffusion tensor
\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}
, to formulate a homogenized transport problem in the
Eulerian
framework:

|Y^{s}|\partial_{t}\widehat{c}_{B}-\widehat{\nabla}\cdot\left(\widehat{\textbf{v}}_{B}\widehat{c}_{B}-\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}\widehat{\nabla}\widehat{c}_{B}\right)=|Y^{s}|\widehat{f}_{d}\quad\mbox{ in }Q^{T}_{B},

(42)

where
Q^{T}_{B}:=\bigcup_{t\in(0,T)}\{t\}\times\Omega(t)
,
\Omega(t):=\{\widehat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\widehat{x}=\textbf{S}(t,x),\;x\in\Omega\}
and
\widehat{\textbf{v}}_{B}(t,\widehat{x}):=\partial_{t}\textbf{S}(t,\textbf{S}^{-1}(t,\widehat{x}))
. Then, in order to perform the simulations by using our numerical framework, the problem (
42
) is transformed to the fixed reference domain
\Omega
. The transformed problem reads:

|Y^{s}|\partial_{t}(J_{B}c_{B})-\nabla\cdot\left(\textbf{D}^{*}_{B}\nabla c_{B}\right)=|Y^{s}|J_{B}f_{d}\quad\mbox{ in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

(43)

with

\displaystyle c_{B}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\widehat{c}_{B}(t,\textbf{S}(t,x)),

\displaystyle\textbf{D}^{*}_{B}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\left[J_{B}\textbf{F}_{B}^{-1}\textbf{D}^{*}_{A}\textbf{F}^{-T}_{B}\right](t,x),

\displaystyle\textbf{F}_{B}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\nabla\textbf{S}(t,x),

\displaystyle J_{B}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\det(\textbf{F}_{B}(t,x)).

Finally,
the solution on the time-dependent domain is given by

\widehat{c}_{B}(t,\widehat{x})=c_{B}(t,\textbf{S}^{-1}(t,\widehat{x})).

This second approach is highly phenomenological, however it also has a reduced complexity compared to the effective micro-macro model.

Now, we compare the solutions of the two alternative approaches described above with the solution of the effective micro-macro model numerically. We perform simulations, using the simulation scenario from Section
4
, and visualize the mass over time on the deformed domain, see Figure
9
. We observe that all curves exhibit in some way the time-dependent oscillations coming from the stretching-relaxing cycles of the domain. However, neither of the solutions to the alternative approaches match the results obtained from the effective micro-macro model (
3
)-(
11
) which is depicted in blue (solid line). In the case of
alternative approach A
(orange, dashed line), the time-oscillations are shifted and the mass lies well below the mass in of the other approaches. The latter observation can be attributed to the constant source term
|Y^{s}|f_{d}
on the right-hand side of Equation (
41
), see also (
38
), which does not take into account the change of source due to the deformation. In the case of
alternative approach B
(green, dash-dotted line) the time-oscillations are still too damped and slightly shifted in comparison to the blue (solid) curve. However, the mass is significantly increased in comparison to the orange (dashed) curve, since the right-hand side of Equation (
43
) does account for changes in the source term due to deformations. Altoghether, we see from Figure
9
that is necessary to consider the effective micro-macro problem (
3
)-(
11
) in order to capture important features of the transport in elastic heterogeneous media, like the effect of the deforming microstructure on the amplitude of the time-oscillations of the mass in our numerical experiment with cyclic stretching and relaxing.

6
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper, we proved global existence in time and uniqueness of solutions to an effective micro-macro problem for reactive transport in elastically derformable porous media, which was formally derived starting from a microscopic model in mixed
Lagrangian/Eulerian
formulation. This result thus generalizes existing results on global well-posedness for elasticity-transport models of micro-macro type to situations for which no additional assumptions (such as linearization) are made about the evolution of the free boundary at the microscale.
The numerical validation of the effective model underpins its use for the description and investigation of various applications in deformable perforated media.

At the same time, the analytical and numerical investigations in this paper are a starting point for future research on transport processes in elastically deformable porous media.
For this purpose, processes in the fluid part of the porous medium have to be considered. As an application, we mention the transport of bio-chemical compounds, like glucose or lactate, in biological tissue which takes place in both, the intracellular and the extracellular space.
Such models, however, involve transport equations coupled to nonlinear fluid-structure interactions in complex microscopic geometries. The analysis of the resulting micro-macro models is an open problem so far. It is particularly interesting to find and interpret assumptions that guarantee the well-posedness of such models. Furthermore, their numerical treatment require the extension of the computational tools used in this paper to micro-macro models for fluid-elasticity-transport systems.

Type-II heavy Fermi liquids and the magnetic memory of 4Hb-TaS 2
Elio J. König

Abstract
The interplay of quantum spin liquids with itinerant conduction electrons is of crucial interest for understanding layered structures composed of frustrated magnet and metal monolayers. Using parton-mean-field theory, we here demonstrate that a type-II heavy Fermi liquid, which is characterized by a vortex lattice in the slave boson condensate, can occur in the vicinity of the quantum phase transition separating fractionalized and heavy Fermi liquid phases. The magnetic flux threading each such vortex is about v f / 137 ​ c v_{f}/137c times smaller than the magnetic flux threading vortices in type-II superconductors, where v f v_{f} is the speed of magnetic excitations and c c the speed of light. This makes a magnetic observation of this effect challenging. We propose scanning tunneling spectroscopy instead and investigate its signatures. If a type-II heavy Fermi liquid is cooled into a type-II superconductor, vortices in the slave boson condensate and in the superconducting condensate mutually attract. We argue that the type-II heavy Fermi liquid thereby provides a compelling explanation for the magnetic memory observed recently [Persky et al. , Nature 609 , 692 (2022)] in thermal cycles of 4Hb-TaS 2 .

Discussion
.
In summary, we studied the type-II heavy Fermi liquid phase, in which external magnetic fields induce vortex lattices in the slave boson condensate, or equivalently the Kondo hybridization
V\propto\langle b\rangle
. Within mean-field slave-boson calculations this phenomenon is demonstrated to occur in the vicinity of the QPT between fractionalized Fermi liquid and heavy Fermi liquid, provided ring-exchange terms are strong enough. While the magnetic signal of
V
-vortices is strongly suppressed, we propose tunneling microscopy as a means to experimentally access the effect and present the spectrum of subgap states trapped inside the vortex core. We argue that the type-II heavy Fermi liquid provides a compelling scenario for the magnetic memory effect in 4Hb-TaS
2
, by means of mutual attraction of superconducting vortices and
V
-vortices combined with pinning effects of
V
-vortices.

for the mean-field Kondo temperature

\displaystyle T_{K}

\displaystyle=\frac{T_{K}^{(0)}}{\sqrt{1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2}}}-\mu_{f}(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})

\displaystyle\ll T_{K}^{(0)}\equiv\mu e^{-2\pi/J_{K}^{\rm eff}a^{2}m}.

(S41)

Note that the mean-field QPT occurs at
\mu_{f}=T_{K}^{(0)}/(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})^{3/2}
and
\mu_{f}\sim J_{H}
.

The fourth order term can be written as

\displaystyle\beta_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{a_{c}}T\sum_{\epsilon_{n}}\int_{\mathbf{p}}\left(\frac{1}{i\epsilon_{n}-\xi(\mathbf{p})}\frac{1}{i\epsilon-\xi_{f}(\mathbf{p})}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{a_{c}}\int_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\text{sgn}(\xi(\mathbf{p})\theta(-\xi(\mathbf{p})\xi_{f}(\mathbf{p}))}{[\xi(\mathbf{p})-\xi_{f}(\mathbf{p})]^{3}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{Nm}{\pi a_{c}}\frac{1}{\mu_{f}^{2}\left(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2}\right)}.

(S42)

S2.2
Stiffness

Next we determine the stiffness below
T_{K}
and by these means the parameter
m_{K}
of the Ginzburg-Landau functional. The stiffness, as obtained by second order gradient expansion of the free energy in
\nabla\phi
is

\displaystyle K_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\frac{2NV^{2}}{a_{c}}T\sum_{\epsilon_{n}}\int_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\partial_{p_{i}}\xi\partial_{p_{j}}\xi_{f}}{[V^{2}+(\epsilon_{n}+i\xi)(\epsilon_{n}+i\xi_{f})]}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{ij}NV^{2}}{a_{c}}\int_{\mathbf{p}}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{mm_{f}}\frac{\theta(V^{2}-\xi\xi_{f})}{[4V^{2}-(\xi-\xi_{f})]^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\simeq\delta_{ij}\frac{NV^{2}}{2\pi a_{c}\mu_{f}}\frac{m}{m_{f}(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})}.

(S43)

The last line is not only an expansion in small
m/m_{f}
,
\mu_{f}/\mu
.
This implies for the parameter of the kinetic term in the Ginzburg-Landau functional that

\frac{1}{2m_{K}}=\frac{N}{2\pi a_{c}\mu_{f}}\frac{m}{m_{f}}\frac{1}{(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})}=\frac{Nm}{\pi a_{c}}\frac{a^{2}}{(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})}.

(S44)

This leads to the coherence length

\xi_{K}=a\sqrt{\frac{\mu_{f}}{2(T_{K}-T)}}\frac{1}{(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})^{1/4}},

(S45)

or in, in units of
V=\sqrt{|\alpha_{K}|/\beta_{K}}

\displaystyle\xi_{K}

\displaystyle=1/\sqrt{2m_{K}\beta_{K}V^{2}}

\displaystyle=a\frac{\mu_{f}}{V}.

(S46)

Note that the Kondo coherence length is thus given by the smaller of the two velocities divided by the order parameter.

S2.3
Ring exchange terms

Finally, we estimate the effect of ring-exchange terms on the mean-field level following Ref.
Motrunich 2006
. These additional magnetic interaction terms stemming from ring exchange have the form

\displaystyle H_{\rm ring}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mathbf{r}_{1,2,3}\in\triangle}\frac{J_{3}}{N^{2}}f_{ABC}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\alpha_{1}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\beta_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\alpha_{2}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\beta_{2}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\alpha_{3}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\beta_{3}}\tau^{A}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tau^{B}_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\tau^{C}_{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{\mathbf{r}_{1,2,3,4}\in\lozenge}\frac{J_{4}}{N^{3}}\chi_{ABCD}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\alpha_{1}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\beta_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\alpha_{2}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\beta_{2}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\alpha_{3}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\beta_{3}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{4},\alpha_{4}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{4},\beta_{4}}\tau^{A}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tau^{B}_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\tau^{C}_{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}}\tau^{D}_{\alpha_{4}\beta_{4}}.

(S47)

Here,
f_{ABC}=\tr[\tau^{A}\tau^{B}\tau^{C}]
(fully antisymmetrized with respect to all indices) and
\chi_{ABC}=\tr[\tau^{A}\tau^{B}\tau^{C}\tau^{D}]
(fully symmetrized for all indices), and
\tau^{A}
are the generators of SU(N) (traceless, hermitian, N
\times
N matrices). For
N>2
, there is also a term

\delta H_{\rm ring}=-\sum_{\mathbf{r}_{1,2,3}\in\triangle}\frac{J_{3}^{\prime}}{N^{2}}d_{ABC}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\alpha_{1}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{1},\beta_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\alpha_{2}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{2},\beta_{2}}f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\alpha_{3}}f_{\mathbf{r}_{3},\beta_{3}}\tau^{A}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}}\tau^{B}_{\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}\tau^{C}_{\alpha_{3}\beta_{3}},

(S48)

where
d_{ABC}=\tr[\tau^{A}\tau^{B}\tau^{C}]
fully symmetrized over indices. We can use for SU(N) generators
f_{ABC}f_{ABC}=N(N^{2}-1)\sim N^{3}
,
d_{ABC}d_{ABC}=(N^{2}-4)(N^{2}-1)/N\sim N^{3}
and
\chi_{ABCD}\chi_{ABCD}=[2(N^{4}-1)+(N^{2}-4)(N^{2}+2)(N^{2}-1)]/24N^{2}\sim N^{4}
, as well as
\langle f^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{x}}f_{\mathbf{x}+\hat{e}}\rangle=t_{f}/2J_{H}\simeq 1
, so that at the mean-field level and at large
N
Motrunich 2006
;
König et al. 2021

F_{\rm ring}/N\sim J_{3}\sin(\phi)-\left[J_{3}^{\prime}+J_{4}/24\right]\cos(\phi).

(S49)

Here,
\phi=\oint d\vec{l}\cdot d\tilde{\mathbf{a}}(\mathbf{x})
is the flux enclosed in a unit cell in a gauge in which we write the spinon hopping term
f^{\dagger}(-i\nabla-\tilde{\mathbf{a}})f
. Identification
e_{*}\mathbf{a}=\tilde{\mathbf{a}}
and
\phi=e_{*}a^{2}{\mathbf{b}}_{z}
leads to the free energy Eq. (
1
) as in the main text with

\frac{1}{e_{*}^{2}}\sim N\frac{a^{2}}{a_{c}}J_{4}

(S50)

and
|\mathbf{h}|\sim NJ_{3}e_{*}/a_{c}
. For simplicity we have dropped
J_{3}^{\prime}
terms vanishing in the SU(2) limit (note that
J_{3}
and
J_{4}
terms survive the
N=2
limit). We also remark that in the limit of large
J_{4}\sim J_{H}
one may estimate
e_{*}^{-2}\sim v_{f}
(the speed of spinons), corresponding to a dimensionless coupling of order unity, as expected for U(1) QSL with spinon Fermi surface.

S2.4
Order of magnitude of important scales

This concludes the estimate of the ratio of London penetration and coherence lengths as quoted in the main text.
We can estimate their ratio as

\displaystyle\kappa_{K}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{m_{K}^{2}\beta_{K}}{e_{*}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\frac{J_{4}t_{c}}{\mu_{f}^{2}}(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})}.

(S51)

Note that within our calculation one may replace
\mu_{f}\sim J_{H}
(and near the Mott transition
J_{4}\sim J_{H}
) and
p_{f}\sim 1/a
. Note that a
T_{K}^{(0)}/J_{H}
larger than the QPT value, additional factors of
t/J_{H}
, Eq. (
S35
), enter and reduce
\kappa_{K}
such that it eventually drops below unity.

Using the coherence length, Eq. (
S45
), we obtain for the Kondo-London penetration depth

\lambda_{K}=a\sqrt{\frac{J_{4}t_{c}}{2\mu_{f}(T_{K}-T)}}(1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2})^{1/4},

(S52)

which for
J_{4}\sim\mu_{F}\sim J_{H}
leads to the rough estimate
\lambda_{K}\sim a\sqrt{t_{c}/(T-T_{K})}\sim.1\mu\text{m}/(1-T/T_{K})^{1/2}
.
We here use
a\sim 1
nm in the CDW phase,
T_{K}\sim 1
K (if we identify the maximal temperature for the magnetic memory with the mean-field Kondo temperature) and
t_{c}\sim 10000
K.

For the estimate of the magnetic field in the vortex core, which relies on Eq. (
S24
), we additionally employ (using
a_{c}\sim a
)

\frac{e^{2}}{e_{*}^{2}}=\alpha_{\rm QED}\frac{J_{4}a}{c},

(S53)

so that

\displaystyle B_{\rm max}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{0}\alpha_{\rm QED}\frac{1}{c}\frac{\ln(\kappa_K)}{4\pi a}\frac{2\mu_{f}(T_{K}-T)}{t_{c}\sqrt{1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\frac{\Phi_{0}}{a^{2}}}_{\sim 10^{3}\text{T}}\underbrace{\frac{a\alpha_{\rm QED}T_{K}}{2\pi c}}_{\sim 10^{-9}}\frac{m}{m_{f}}\frac{\ln(\kappa_K)(1-T/T_{K})}{\sqrt{1-p_{c}^{2}/p_{f}^{2}}}.

(S54)

In the main text, we drop the last term, assuming
p_{c}\lesssim p_{f},T\lesssim T_{K}
and
\ln(\kappa_K)=\mathcal{O}(1)
. This concludes the estimate presented below Eq. (
2
) of the main text.

S3
Tunneling in the vortex core

In this section of the supplement, we discuss the tunneling density of states above the superconducting transition temperature.
We concentrate on the center of the vortex and, to be concrete, employ the same model of parabolic bands, Eq. (
S36
), as in the microscopic derivation of the Ginzburg-Landau free energy, except that we now fix
V(\mathbf{r})=V(r)e^{i\phi}
to contain a vortex.

S3.1
Subgap states

At the mean-field level, the first quantized fermionic Hamiltonian is thus

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m}-\mu&V(\mathbf{r})\\
V^{*}(\mathbf{r})&-[\frac{\mathbf{p}^{2}}{2m_{f}}-\mu_{f}]\end{array}\right).

(S55)

Here, the first and second row/column correspond to
c
and
f
electrons, respectively.
The interesting regime is
V\ll t_{f}\sim 1/m_{f}a^{2}\ll t_{c}\sim 1/ma^{2}
and energies close to the gap opening, yet our calculations are generic with respect to the ratio
t_{c}/t_{f}
(the
t_{c}=t_{f}
,
\mu=\mu_{f}
is equivalent to a superconductor).
We concentrate on the energy of the band opening, measure all energies with respect to this energy and use that at this energy
k_{c}=\sqrt{2m\mu}
and
k_{f}=\sqrt{2m_{f}\mu_{f}}
are equal (denoted by
k_{\rm gap}
).
The Schrödinger equation in polar coordinates is

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{1}{2m}[\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\bullet)+\frac{\partial_{\phi}^{2}}{r^{2}}]-\mu&V(r)e^{i\phi}\\
V(r)e^{-i\phi}&\frac{1}{2m_{f}}[\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\bullet)+\frac{\partial_{\phi}^{2}}{r^{2}}]+\mu_{f}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{c}(r,\phi)\\
\psi_{f}(r,\phi)\end{array}\right)=E\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{c}(r,\phi)\\
\psi_{f}(r,\phi)\end{array}\right).

(S56)

In the following, we closely follow the classical work
Caroli et al. 1964
by Caroli, deGennes, and Matricon (but generalize it to
m\neq m_{f},\mu\neq\mu_{f}
) and use Pauli matrices
\sigma_{x,y,z}
for
c/f
space. We parametrize

\left(\psi_{c}(r,\phi),\psi_{f}(r,\phi)\right)^{T}=e^{il\phi-i\phi\sigma_{z}/2}\hat{\phi}_{l}(r)

leading to

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2m}[-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\bullet)+\frac{(l-1/2)^{2}}{r^{2}}]-\mu&V(r)\\
V(r)&-\frac{1}{2m_{f}}[-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\bullet)+\frac{(l+1/2)^{2}}{r^{2}}]+\mu_{f}\end{array}\right)\hat{\phi}_{l}=E\hat{\phi}_{l}.

(S57)

Note that
l
is quantized to half-integers to make
\psi_{c,f}(r,\phi)
single-valued.
Following Caroli, de Gennes and Matricon, we define a radius
r_{c}\in[l/k_{\rm gap},\xi_{K}]
, where
\xi_{K}
is the radius of the vortex (determined by the Kondo coherence length, Eq. (
S46
)).

S3.1.1
Solution deep inside the core:
r<r_{c}

We first find the solution in the regime
r\ll r_{c}
deep inside the vortex core. Clearly
V(r)\simeq 0
and positive
l
solutions are

\hat{\phi}_{l}(r)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{c}J_{l-1/2}([k_{c}+E/v_{c}]r)\\
A_{f}J_{l+1/2}([k_{f}-E/v_{f}]r)\end{array}\right),

(S58)

while negative
l
solutions are by symmetry

\hat{\phi}_{l}(r)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{c}J_{|l|+1/2}([k_{c}+E/v_{c}]r)\\
A_{f}J_{|l|-1/2}([k_{f}-E/v_{f}]r)\end{array}\right).

(S59)

We remind the reader that only
J_{0}(1)=1
, all other Bessel functions vanish at the origin. Thus, only the
l=1/2
state has support of
c-
electrons at the origin.

S3.1.2
Solution for
r>r_{c}

Next we search for the solution outside for
r>r_{c}
, note that this still includes part of the outer vortex core. The Ansatz

\hat{\phi}_{l}(r)=\hat{g}_{l}(r)H_{m}(k_{\rm gap}r)+c.c.,

(S60)

where
\hat{g}_{l}(r)
is slow on the scale
1/k_{\rm gap}
and
m=\sqrt{l^{2}+1/4}
. We obtain

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-iv_{c}\partial_{r}&V(r)\\
V(r)&iv_{f}\partial_{r}\end{array}\right)\hat{g}_{l}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}E+\frac{l}{2mr^{2}}&0\\
0&E+\frac{l}{2m_{f}r^{2}}\end{array}\right)\hat{g}_{l}.

(S61)

Here
v_{c}=k_{\rm gap}/m
and
v_{f}=k_{\rm gap}/m_{f}
and their difference and geometric average are called
\Delta v
,
\bar{v}=\sqrt{v_{c}v_{f}}
.

We generalize the Ansatz of Caroli, deGennes and Matricon and write

\hat{g}_{l}(r)=Ae^{-K(r)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{i\psi_{c}/2\bar{v}}&0\\
0&e^{-i\psi_{f}/2\bar{v}}\end{array}\right){\hat{g}}^{(0)}.

(S62)

Here,
K(r)=\int_{0}^{r}dr^{\prime}V(r^{\prime})/\bar{v}
,
A
is a normalization constant and

{\hat{g}}^{(0)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{v_{c}+v_{f}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sqrt{v_{f}}\\
-i\sqrt{v_{c}}\end{array}\right),

(S63)

is the zero mode of the operator on the left of the equation sign in Eq. (
S61
).

Using the result for
{\hat{g}}^{(0)}
we obtain two equations

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{v_{c}}{v_{f}}}[iV(r)+\psi_{c}^{\prime}-ie^{-i(\psi_{c}+\psi_{f})/2\bar{v}}V(r)]

\displaystyle=E+\frac{l}{2mr^{2}},

(S64a)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{v_{f}}{v_{c}}}[-iV(r)+\psi_{f}^{\prime}+ie^{i(\psi_{c}+\psi_{f})/2\bar{v}}V(r)]

\displaystyle=E+\frac{l}{2m_{f}r^{2}}.

(S64b)

Next we use the notation
\psi_{\pm}=\frac{\psi_{c}\pm\psi_{f}}{2}
to and expand in
\psi_{+}
to obtain

\displaystyle[\partial_{r}-V(r)/\bar{v}]\psi_{+}

\displaystyle=\bar{v}\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2\bar{v}^{2}}+\frac{l}{2k_{\rm gap}r^{2}}\right],

(S65a)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\psi_{-}

\displaystyle=E\frac{v_{f}-v_{c}}{\bar{v}},

(S65b)

which has solutions

\displaystyle\psi_{+}

\displaystyle=-\bar{v}\int_{r}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{K(r)-K(r^{\prime})}\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2\bar{v}^{2}}+\frac{l}{2k_{\rm gap}{r^{\prime}}^{2}}\right],

(S66a)

\displaystyle\psi_{-}

\displaystyle=E(v_{f}-v_{c})r/\bar{v}+\text{const.}.

(S66b)

We briefly check that
\psi_{+}/\bar{v}
is small. For large
r\gg\xi\sim v_{c}/V_{\infty}>\bar{v}/V_{\infty}
we can use

\displaystyle\psi_{+}(r)/\bar{v}

\displaystyle\simeq-\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2V_{\infty}\bar{v}}+\frac{l}{2k_{\rm gap}r}\right],

(S67)

which is small so long as
rk_{\rm gap}\gg 1
.
For
r\ll\xi_{K}
we can simplify

\displaystyle K(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}dr^{\prime}V(r^{\prime})/\bar{v}\sim r^{2}/\xi_{K}^{2}

(S68a)

\displaystyle\psi_{+}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})r}{2\bar{v}}

\displaystyle-\bar{v}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{-K(r^{\prime})}\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2\bar{v}^{2}}-\frac{lV(r^{\prime})}{2k_{\rm gap}\bar{v}r^{\prime}}\right].

(S68b)

The first term is much smaller than
\bar{v}
by means of
E\ll\mu_{f}
, while the second term turns out to be fixed to zero by the quantization condition (see below).

In total we find for
r\geq r_{c}

\hat{\psi}_{l}=AH_{m}(k_{\rm gap}r)\frac{e^{-K(r)}}{\sqrt{v_{c}+v_{f}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{i\frac{\psi_{+}+\psi_{-}}{2\bar{v}}}\sqrt{v_{f}}\\
-ie^{i\frac{\psi_{+}-\psi_{-}}{2\bar{v}}}\sqrt{v_{c}}\\
\end{array}\right)+c.c..

(S69)

S3.1.3
Matching solutions

Finally, we match the solution obtained at small and large
r
. The
Expansion of Bessel functions for positive
l
for the solution deep in the core leads to

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{l}(r_{c})

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k_{\rm gap}r}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{c}\cos\left([k_{\rm gap}+E/v_{c}]r_{c}-\frac{l\pi}{2}\right)\\
A_{f}\sin\left([k_{\rm gap}-E/v_{f}]r_{c}-\frac{l\pi}{2}\right)\end{array}\right)

At the same time, the asymptotic form of Hankel functions implies for Eq. (
S69
)

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{l}(r_{c})

\displaystyle\simeq A\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi k_{\rm gap}r_{c}}}e^{i\left(k_{\rm gap}r_{c}-\frac{m\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}\frac{e^{-i\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{-K(r^{\prime})}\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2\bar{v}^{2}}-\frac{lV(r^{\prime})}{2k_{\rm gap}\bar{v}r^{\prime}}\right]}}{\sqrt{v_{c}+v_{f}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{i\frac{Er_{c}}{v_{c}}}\sqrt{v_{f}}\\
-ie^{-i\frac{Er_{c}}{v_{f}}}\sqrt{v_{c}}\end{array}\right)+c.c.

By comparison of exponents we obtain the quantization condition becomes

\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{-K(r^{\prime})}\left[\frac{E(v_{f}+v_{c})}{2\bar{v}}-\frac{lV(r^{\prime})}{2k_{\rm gap}r^{\prime}}\right]=0,

(S74)

or simply

{E_{l}=\frac{2\bar{v}^{2}}{v_{f}+v_{c}}\frac{l}{2k_{\rm gap}\bar{v}}\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{-K(r^{\prime})}\frac{V(r^{\prime})}{r^{\prime}}}{\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}e^{-K(r^{\prime})}}}.

(S75)

Using the minigap energy
E_{\rm mg}=\bar{v}V^{\prime}(0)/2k_{\rm gap}(v_{f}+v_{c})\simeq V_{\infty}^{2}/\sqrt{v_{c}v_{f}k_{\rm gap}^{2}}
we thereby find the result quoted in the main text. We highlight that the (indirect) spectral gap is
V_{\rm gap}=2\sqrt{v_{f}/v_{c}}V_{\infty}
(see below), hence

E_{\rm mg}=\frac{V_{\rm gap}^{2}}{\mu_{f}}\sqrt{\frac{v_{c}}{v_{f}}}.

(S76)

Note that the comparison of prefactors
|A_{c}/A_{f}|\propto\sqrt{v_{f}/v_{c}}=\sqrt{m/m_{f}}
implies that the c-electron component of the subgap states is strongly suppressed.

S3.2
Tunneling spectroscopy

Microscopically, the tunneling Hamiltonian is

H_{\rm tun}=\sum_{\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}}p^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})[t_{pc}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})c_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})+t_{pd}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})d_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})]+H.c.

(S77)

For the spatially resolved tunneling density of states near the center of the vortex this implies in the slave-boson framework

\displaystyle\frac{d\text{I}}{d\text{V}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\propto\int d^{2}x^{\prime}\sum_{l}\delta(e\text{V}-E_{l})

\displaystyle\times\left|(t_{pc}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),V(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\frac{t_{pd}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})}{t_{cd}})\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\psi_{c}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\\
\psi_{f}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\end{array}\right)\right|^{2}.

We consider the limit of a relatively sharp tip,
t_{pc,pd}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\simeq 0
for
|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|>r_{\rm tip}
and
r_{\rm tip}\ll\xi_{K}
. We thus find at the center of the vortex

\displaystyle\frac{d\text{I}}{d\text{V}}

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{l>0}\delta(e\text{V}-lE_{\rm mg})\int_{0}^{k_{\rm gap}r_{\rm tip}}d\rho\rho

\displaystyle\times\left|t_{pc}\sqrt{v_{f}}J_{l-1/2}(\rho)+\frac{V_{\infty}}{t_{cd}}t_{pd}\frac{\rho}{k_{\rm gap}\xi_{K}}\sqrt{v_{c}}J_{l+1/2}(\rho)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{l<0}\delta(e\text{V}-lE_{\rm mg})\int_{0}^{k_{\rm gap}r_{\rm tip}}d\rho\rho

\displaystyle\times\left|t_{pc}\sqrt{v_{f}}J_{|l|+1/2}(\rho)+\frac{V_{\infty}}{t_{cd}}t_{pd}\frac{\rho}{k_{\rm gap}\xi_{K}}\sqrt{v_{c}}J_{|l|-1/2}(\rho)\right|^{2}.

(S80)

We define

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{V_{\infty}t_{pd}}{t_{cd}t_{pc}}\sqrt{\frac{v_{c}}{v_{f}}}\frac{1}{k_{\rm gap}\xi_{K}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{V_{\infty}^{2}t_{pd}}{t_{cd}t_{pc}t_{f}}\sqrt{\frac{\mu}{t_{f}}}\sim\frac{T_{K}^{2}}{t_{cd}^{1/2}t_{f}^{3/2}}\frac{t_{pd}}{t_{pc}},

(S81)

where in the last line we assumed
t_{cd}\sim\mu
. At least on the 1H surface where
t_{pd}<t_{pc}
it is valid to assume that
R\ll 1
, and it appears plausible that this assumption also holds on the 1T surface if at least some electrons tunnel through the 1T layer into the 1H layers.

Assuming that
k_{\rm gap}r_{\rm tip}\sim 1
, the relative strength of
c
-tunneling, mixed tunneling and
f
-tunneling is thus

\displaystyle 1:R:R^{2},

(S82)

and by consequence

\displaystyle\frac{d\text{I}}{d\text{V}}

\displaystyle\propto\sum_{l}\delta(eV-lE_{\rm mg})\int_{0}^{k_{\rm gap}r_{\rm tip}}d\rho\rho J_{|l|-\text{sign}(l)1/2}(\rho).

(S83)

For the plot in the main text, Fig.
2
b), c), we use
k_{\rm gap}r_{\rm tip}=3
, use a Lorentzian broadening of the delta function

\delta(e\text{V}-lE_{\rm mg})\rightarrow\frac{\Gamma}{(e\text{V}-lE_{\rm mg})^{2}+\Gamma^{2}},

(S84)

where
\Gamma=0.2E_{\rm mg}
.

S3.3
Tunneling outside the vortex core

For clarity of presentation, we overlay the density of states in the vortex core with the same observable outside the vortex core using the homogeneous Hamiltonian

h=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}v_{c}p&V\\
V&-v_{f}p\end{array}\right).

(S85)

Note the spectral gap is
V\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{v_{c}+v_{f}}{v_{c}-v_{f}}\right)^{2}}\simeq 2\sqrt{v_{f}/v_{c}}V
. The tunneling density of states is

A Note on the Formal Groups of Weighted Delsarte Threefolds This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Modular Forms and String Theory in honor of Noriko Yui. The full collection is available at http://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/modular-forms.html
Yasuhiro GOTO

Abstract
One-dimensional formal groups over an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic are classified by their height. In the case of K ​ 3 K3 surfaces, the height of their formal groups takes integer values between 1 1 and 10 10 , or ∞ \infty . For Calabi–Yau threefolds, the height is bounded by h 1 , 2 + 1 h^{1,2}+1 if it is finite, where h 1 , 2 h^{1,2} is a Hodge number. At present, there are only a limited number of concrete examples for explicit values or the distribution of the height. In this paper, we consider Calabi–Yau threefolds arising from weighted Delsarte threefolds in positive characteristic. We describe an algorithm for computing the height of their formal groups and carry out calculations with various Calabi–Yau threefolds of Delsarte type.

1
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. Let
X
be a Calabi–Yau threefold over
k
, by which we mean a smooth projective variety over
k
of dimension
3
with a trivial canonical sheaf and
\dim H^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=\dim H^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
. In
[
2
]
, Artin and Mazur defined a functor
\Phi_{X}
for
X
on the category of finite local
k
-algebras
A
with residue field
k
by

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}(A)=\ker\big(H_{\rm et}^{3}(X_{A},{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\longrightarrow H_{\rm et}^{3}(X,{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\big),

where
X_{A}=X\times\operatorname{Spec}A
and
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is the sheaf of multiplicative groups. It is proved in
[
2
]
that this functor (more generally, those for Calabi–Yau varieties of any dimension) is representable by a smooth formal group of dimension equal to the geometric genus of
X
, which is one in our case. By abuse of notation, we also use
\Phi_{X}
for this formal group and called it the
(
Artin–Mazur
)
formal group
of
X
.

A formal group in positive characteristic
p
is endowed with the multiplication-by-
p
map. The
p
-rank of its kernel is called the
height
of
\Phi_{X}
and denoted by
h:=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{X}
, namely

\displaystyle p^{h}=\#\ker([p]\colon\Phi_{X}\longrightarrow\Phi_{X}).

It is known that one-dimensional formal groups in positive characteristic are determined up to isomorphism by the height. In the case of
K3
surfaces, the height takes integer values between
1
and
10
, or
\infty
(cf.
[
1
,
8
,
19
]
). In the case of Calabi–Yau threefolds, it is proved in
[
18
]
that
h
is bounded above by
h^{1,2}+1
if
h\neq\infty
, where
h^{1,2}
is a Hodge number of
X
. Note that it is still a conjecture that
h^{1,2}
is bounded for Calabi–Yau threefolds.

In this paper, we consider Calabi–Yau threefolds arising from weighted projective hypersurfaces of Delsarte type and give an algorithm to compute the height of their Artin–Mazur formal groups. The algorithm involves only combinatorial argument so that one can actually do calculations to find numerical data for the height of Calabi–Yau threefolds. Among others, we find a Calabi–Yau threefold with height
42
or
82
in some characteristic.

2
Quotient maps and the height of formal groups

In this section, we show that the formal groups are invariant under the quotient map by a group of
symplectic
automorphisms (by which we mean the automorphisms that preserve the dualizing sheaf of a variety). The Calabi–Yau threefolds we consider in Sections
4
and
5
often have large groups of symplectic automorphisms. In this section, we focus our attention to a very special case, namely, orbifold Calabi–Yau threefolds
(X,Y)
which form a mirror pair (i.e.,
h^{1,1}(X)=h^{2,1}(Y)
and
h^{2,1}(X)=h^{1,1}(Y)
), and discuss their formal groups briefly. More details about relationships between quotient maps and formal groups will be discussed elsewhere.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a Calabi–Yau threefold over
k
and
G
be a finite group acting on
X
symplectically
(
i.e., preserving the dualizing sheaf of
X)
. Write
Y:=X/G
. Assume that
p
is coprime to the order of
G
and that there exists a crepant resolution
\widetilde{Y}
of
Y
. Then
\widetilde{Y}
is a Calabi–Yau threefold with
\Phi_{X}\cong\Phi_{\widetilde{Y}}
. In particular, the formal groups of
X
and
\widetilde{Y}
have the same height:
\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{X}=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{Y}}
.

Proof.

Since
p
is coprime to the order of
G
,
Y
has at most rational singularities and by Theorem 3.1 (and the paragraph 3.10) of
[
16
]
, we have
\Phi_{Y}\cong\Phi_{\widetilde{Y}}
for a crepant resolution
\widetilde{Y}
of
Y
. Write
f\colon X\longrightarrow Y
for the quotient map. As
G
acts on
X
symplectically,
g^{*}\omega_{X}\cong\omega_{X}
for every
g\in G
and thus
\omega_{X}^{G}\cong\omega_{X}
for the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X}
of
X
. Since
X
is Calabi–Yau, we find
\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}^{G}\cong f_{*}\omega_{X}^{G}\cong f_{*}\omega_{X}\cong f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Again by Theorem 3.1 of
[
16
]
, we see
\Phi_{X}\cong\Phi_{Y}
. Hence
\Phi_{X}\cong\Phi_{\widetilde{Y}}
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a threefold over
k
with
\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\omega_{X}
and
G
be a finite group acting on
X
symplectically. Write
\widetilde{X}
and
\widetilde{Y}
for crepant resolutions of
X
and
Y
, respectively, constructed as in the following diagram:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{@{}clclc}X&&\longleftarrow&\widetilde{X}\\
\downarrow&&&&\\
X/G&=Y&\longleftarrow&\widetilde{Y}.\end{array}

Write
h:=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{X}}
. Assume that
\big(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}\big)
is a mirror pair of Calabi–Yau threefolds and
h
is finite. Then

\displaystyle h\leq\min\big\{h^{1,1}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1,\,h^{1,2}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1\big\}.

Proof.

Note that
h=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{X}}=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{Y}}
by Lemma
2.1
. Since
h
is finite, it follows from Corollary 2.3 of
[
18
]
that

\displaystyle h\leq h^{1,2}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1\qquad\text{and}\qquad h\leq h^{1,2}\big(\widetilde{Y}\big)+1.

As
\big(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}\big)
is a mirror pair of Calabi–Yau threefolds, we find
h^{1,2}\big(\widetilde{Y}\big)+1=h^{1,1}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1
. Combining these relations, we obtain the asserted inequality.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

It is still an open problem whether or not
h^{1,1}
or
h^{1,2}
is bounded for Calabi–Yau threefolds.

In later sections of this paper, we see examples of Calabi–Yau threefolds with many symplectic automorphisms. One may then expect to construct their mirror partners by using some group actions
G
as in Proposition
2.2
. In some cases, however, we do not find such
G
as described in the following statement; a concrete example for this can be found in Example
6.3
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\widetilde{X}
be a Calabi–Yau threefold defined as a crepant resolution of
X
and write
h=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{X}}
. Assume that
h>\min\big\{h^{1,1}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1,\,h^{1,2}\big(\widetilde{X}\big)+1\big\}
. Then either
h=\infty
or there exists no group
G
of symplectic automorphisms on
X
such that
\big(\widetilde{X},\widetilde{Y}\big)
of Proposition
2.2
forms a mirror pair.

Proof.

Take the contrapositive of Proposition
2.2
.
∎

3
Weighted Delsarte varieties

In order to compute the cohomology groups of weighted Delsarte varieties, we explain some geometric properties of them (see also
[
7
]
).

Let
Q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{n})
be an
(n+1)
-tuple of positive integers such that
p\nmid q_{i}
,
0\leq i\leq n
, and
\gcd(q_{0},\dots,\hat{q_{i}},\dots,q_{n})=1
for every
0\leq i\leq n
, where
\hat{q_{i}}
means that
q_{i}
is omitted. The weighted projective
n
-space over
k
of type
Q
, denoted by
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
, is the projective variety
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q):=\operatorname{Proj}k[x_{0},\dots,x_{n}]
, where the polynomial algebra is graded by the condition
\deg(x_{i})=q_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n
(cf.
[
5
]
).

Let
m
be a positive integer such that
p\nmid m
. Let
A=(a_{ij})
be an
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix of integer entries having the properties

(i)

a_{ij}\geq 0
and
p\nmid a_{ij}
for every
(i,j)
with
a_{ij}\neq 0
,

(ii)

p\nmid\det A
,

(iii)

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}q_{j}a_{ij}=m
for
0\leq i\leq n
,

(iv)

given
j
,
a_{ij}=0
for some
i
.

We define an
(n-1)
-dimensional
weighted Delsarte variety in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
of degree
m
with matrix
A
(cf.
[
3
,
7
,
14
]
) to be the weighted projective hypersurface,
X_{A}
, defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}x_{0}^{a_{i0}}x_{1}^{a_{i1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{in}}=0\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q).

When
A
is a diagonal matrix, the equation has the form

\displaystyle x_{0}^{d_{0}}+x_{1}^{d_{1}}+\cdots+x_{n}^{d_{n}}=0

and we call it a
weighted Fermat variety of degree
d
.

Weighted Delsarte varieties are birational to finite quotients of Fermat varieties and many properties of their cohomology groups can be extracted from those of Fermat varieties (cf.
[
7
,
15
,
20
]
). For instance, write
d=|\det A|
and let
F_{d}
be the
(n-1)
-dimensional Fermat variety of degree
d
in the usual projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
:

\displaystyle F_{d}\colon\ y_{0}^{d}+y_{1}^{d}+\cdots+y_{n}^{d}=0.

Write
\mu_{d}
for the group of
d
-th roots of unity in
k^{\times}
, which consists of
d
elements as
p\nmid d
. Set
\Gamma=(\mu_{d}\times\cdots\times\mu_{d})/(\text{diagonal elements})
for the product of
n+1
copies of
\mu_{d}
modulo diagonal elements and define

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{A}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\gamma}=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{0j}},\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{1j}},\dots,\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{nj}}\right)\in\Gamma\,\Bigg|\,(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\mu_{d}^{n+1}\right\}.

(3.1)

Then
{\Gamma}_{A}
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
and it acts on
F_{d}
by

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\gamma}\cdot(y_{0}:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{n})=\left(\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{0j}}\right)y_{0}:\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{1j}}\right)y_{1}:\cdots:\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{a_{nj}}\right)y_{n}\right)

for
\boldsymbol{\gamma}\in{\Gamma}_{A}
and
(y_{0}:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{n})\in F_{d}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X_{A}
be a weighted Delsarte variety in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
with matrix
A
. Then
X_{A}
is birational to the quotient
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
.

Proof.

Write
B=(b_{ij})
for the cofactor matrix of
A
. Then there is a dominant rational map
f\colon F_{d}\cdots\rightarrow X_{A}
defined by

\displaystyle f((y_{0}:y_{1}:\cdots:y_{n}))=\left(\prod_{j=0}^{n}y_{j}^{b_{0j}}:\prod_{j=0}^{n}y_{j}^{b_{1j}}:\cdots:\prod_{j=0}^{n}y_{j}^{b_{nj}}\right).

Hence
X_{A}
is birational to
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
.
∎

We describe the
\ell
-adic étale cohomology of the varieties involved, where
\ell
is a prime different from
p=\operatorname{char}k
. It is known that the cohomology of Fermat variety
F_{d}
is decomposed into one-dimensional pieces parameterized by the characters of
\Gamma
. In fact, note first that
F_{d}
is of dimension
n-1
and

\displaystyle H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})\cong\begin{cases}H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})&\text{if $n$ is even},\\
V(0)\oplus H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})&\text{if $n$ is odd},\end{cases}

where
V(0)
denotes the subspace corresponding to the hyperplane section and
H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
is the primitive part of
H^{n-1}(F_{d},\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
(cf.
[
11
,
13
]
). Define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(F_{d})=\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\,\Bigg|\,\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z},\,\alpha_{i}\neq 0,\ 0\leq i\leq n,\ \sum_{i=0}^{n}\alpha_{i}=0\right\}

and

\displaystyle V(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\big\{v\in H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\,|\,\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{*}(v)=\gamma_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\gamma_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\cdot v,\ \forall\,\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_{0},\dots,\gamma_{n})\in\Gamma\big\},

where
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{*}
is the endomorphism of
H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
induced from
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
. Then the primitive part
H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
of the cohomology for
F_{d}
is given as

\displaystyle H^{n-1}_{\rm prim}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})=\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(F_{d})}V(\boldsymbol{\alpha}).

A similar property holds for
X_{A}
since it is birational to the quotient variety
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
. Here we describe the cohomology of
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X_{A}
be a weighted Delsarte variety in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
with matrix
A
. Let
\Gamma_{A}
be the group defined in (
3.1
) and put

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})=\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})\in\mathfrak{A}(F_{d})\,\Bigg|\,\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}=0,\ 0\leq j\leq n\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d}/\Gamma_{A},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\cong\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})}V(\boldsymbol{\alpha})&\text{if $n$ is even},\\
V(0)\oplus\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})}V(\boldsymbol{\alpha})&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is similar to the case of weighted Delsarte (or diagonal) surfaces; see, for instance,
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
for details. The assertion follows from the isomorphism

\displaystyle H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d}/\Gamma_{A},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\cong H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})^{\Gamma_{A}}

as
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
-vector spaces and by considering the
\Gamma_{A}
-action on
H^{n-1}_{\rm et}(F_{d},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
.
∎

It is a bit lengthy to describe the set
\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
etc. here. The reader may refer, for instance, to
[
6
]
or
[
19
]
for some concrete examples of
\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
.

4
Calabi–Yau threefolds of Delsarte type

In this section, we discuss Calabi–Yau threefolds arising from weighted Delsarte threefolds. Using their cohomology groups, we describe an algorithm for computing the height of their formal groups.

Let
X
be a weighted projective variety in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
with
Q=(q_{0},\dots,q_{n})
.
X
is said to be
quasi-smooth
(cf.
[
5
]
) if its affine quasi-cone is smooth outside the origin. For instance, weighted Fermat varieties are quasi-smooth. As a special case of weighted quasi-smooth varieties, we observe the following property for quasi-smooth weighted Delsarte varieties.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X_{A}
be a quasi-smooth weighted Delsarte variety in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(Q)
of degree
m
with matrix
A
. Write
d=|\det A|
. Let
\Gamma_{A}
be the group defined in
(\ref{ga})
acting on the
Fermat variety
F_{d}
. Then the following assertions hold:

(1)

The quotient variety
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
has at most rational abelian quotient singularities.

(2)

X_{A}
has at most rational cyclic quotient singularities.

Proof.

(1) Since
F_{d}
is a smooth variety and
\Gamma_{A}
is an abelian group,
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
has at most abelian quotient singularities. In characteristic 0, quotient singularities are known to be rational. Hence we only need to show that
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
is liftable to characteristic 0 and this follows from conditions (i) and (ii) on matrix
A
.

(2) A quasi-smooth variety is locally isomorphic to the quotient of a smooth variety by some cyclic group action and this cyclic group is a subgroup of
\mu_{q_{i}}
for some weight
q_{i}
(cf.
[
5
]
). Since
p\nmid q_{i}
for every
i
, the group action by a subgroup of
\mu_{q_{i}}
can be lifted to characteristic 0. Hence
X_{A}
has at most cyclic quotient singularities and they are rational.
∎

Now we consider weighted Delsarte threefolds.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
X_{A}
be a weighted Delsarte threefold in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
. Assume that
X_{A}
is quasi-smooth and
m=q_{0}+q_{1}+q_{2}+q_{3}+q_{4}
. Then the dualizing sheaf of
X_{A}
is trivial and there exists a crepant resolution for
X_{A}
.

Proof.

Since
X_{A}
is a quasi-smooth hypersurface and of dimension 3, it is known (cf.
[
4
, Proposition 6]
) to be in general position relative to
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)_{\rm sing}
(i.e.,
\operatorname{codim}_{X_{A}}\big(X_{A}\cap\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)_{\rm sing}\big)\geq 2
, where
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)_{\rm sing}
is the singular locus of
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
). Hence the dualizing sheaf of
X_{A}
is computed as
\omega_{X_{A}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{A}}(m-q_{0}-q_{1}-q_{2}-q_{3}-q_{4})\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{A}}
(see
[
5
]
). The existence of a crepant resolution for
X_{A}
is proved in
[
10
]
.
∎

Definition 4.3
.

If
X_{A}
is a quasi-smooth weighted Delsarte threefold with matrix
A
of degree
m
with
m=q_{0}+\cdots+q_{4}
, then a crepant resolution
\widetilde{X}_{A}
of
X_{A}
is called a
Calabi–Yau threefold of
(
weighted
)
Delsarte type in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
. When
A
is a diagonal matrix,
X_{A}
is also called a
Calabi–Yau threefold of
(
weighted
)
Fermat type
.

Since the quotient
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
is birational to
X_{A}
, it is also birational to
\widetilde{X}_{A}
. Using the cohomological information of
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
, we write several birational properties of
\widetilde{X}_{A}
. Recall that
A=(a_{ij})
,
d=|\det A|
and that
F_{d}
is the Fermat threefold of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. We have

\displaystyle H^{3}_{\rm et}(F_{d}/\Gamma_{A},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})\cong\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})}V(\boldsymbol{\alpha}),

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})=\left\{(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{4})\in(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{5}\left|\begin{matrix}\alpha_{i}\neq 0,\ 0\leq i\leq 4,\ \sum\limits_{i=0}^{4}\alpha_{i}=0\\
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{4}a_{ij}\alpha_{i}=0\ \text{for }0\leq j\leq 4\end{matrix}\right.\right\}.

For each
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{4})\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
,
define an integer

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|=\sum_{i=0}^{4}\left\langle\frac{\alpha_{i}}{d}\right\rangle-1,

where
<\alpha_{i}/d>
denotes the fractional part of
\alpha_{i}/d
. It takes values
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|=0,1,2
or
3
. If
\widetilde{X}_{A}
is a Calabi–Yau threefold of Delsarte type in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
, then there exists a unique element

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4})\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})

with
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=0
(cf.
[
17
,
19
]
). Note that
\|-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=(4-1)-\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=3-0=3
.

Recall that
p\ (=\operatorname{char}k)
is relatively prime to
d
. Let
f
be the order of
p
modulo
d
. Put

\displaystyle H=\big\{p^{i}\ (\operatorname{mod}d)\,|\,0\leq i<f\big\},

which is a subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{4})\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
, we define a non-negative integer

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})=\sum_{t\in H}\|t\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|.

It is known that the part (line segments) of the Newton polygon of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
with slope less than
1
corresponds to the height of the formal group of
\widetilde{X}_{A}
; if there is no such part, then
\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}=\infty
(cf.
[
2
,
18
]
). In our situation,
X_{A}
,
\widetilde{X}_{A}
and
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
are birational to each other (by resolving rational singularities) and the formal groups are invariant under resolution of rational singularities; see Lemma
2.1
. Hence the height of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
can be computed from the Newton polygon of
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
. The slopes of this polygon is given by
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})/f
and the length of the part with slope less than
1
is equal to the number of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
satisfying
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})/f<1
. In summary, the height of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
can be calculated by evaluating
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})
for
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{A}
be a Calabi–Yau threefold of Delsarte type in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
. If there exists
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime})<f
, then
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime})=\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})
; furthermore, the height of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
is finite and equal to the length
(
cardinality
)
of the
H
-orbit of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\|t\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}\|\geq 1
for all
t\in H
. Then as
\#H=f
, we have
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime})\geq f
; but, this is against the assumption. Hence
\|t\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}\|=0
for some
t\in H
and by the uniqueness of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
, we find
t\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
. Since
H
is a subgroup of
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, we find
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{\prime})=\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})
and this holds for every element in the
H
-orbit of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
. Therefore the number of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
’s with
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})<f
is equal to the length of the
H
-orbit of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
and so is the height of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
.
∎

We are going to calculate the length of the
H
-orbit of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
, namely the number of distinct elements in
\{t\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\,|\,t\in H\}
. Given
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}=(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4})
with
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=0
, we may choose every
\alpha_{i}
as
0<\alpha_{i}<d
so that
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{4}=d
; then we set

\displaystyle e=\gcd(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4},d)\qquad\text{and}\qquad d_{A}=\frac{d}{e}.

(4.1)

We immediately see
e<d
and
d_{A}\geq 2
. Further, suppose
d_{A}=2
. Then it follows from
0<\alpha_{i}<d
that
\alpha_{i}=e
for all
i
, which implies
5e=d=2e
. Since this is absurd, we find
d_{A}\geq 3
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{A}
be a Calabi–Yau threefold of Delsarte type in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
. Let
d_{A}
be the integer defined in (
4.1
). Write
f

(
resp.
f_{A})
for the order of
p
modulo
d

(
resp. modulo
d_{A})
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})=\frac{f}{f_{A}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{f_{A}-1}\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})<f
if and only if
\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|\leq 1
for
0\leq i<f_{A}
.

Proof.

(1) Since
e=\gcd(\gcd(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4}),d)
, write
\gcd(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4})=eg
for some
g
with
\gcd(g,d_{A})=1
. Then

\displaystyle p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p^{i}\alpha_{j}\equiv\alpha_{j}\pmod{d}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow d\,|\,\big(p^{i}-1\big)\gcd(\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{4})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow d_{A}\,|\,\big(p^{i}-1\big)g

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p^{i}\equiv 1\pmod{d_{A}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})=\frac{f}{f_{A}}\big(\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|+\|p\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|+\cdots+\big\|p^{f_{A}-1}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|\big)=\frac{f}{f_{A}}\sum_{i=0}^{f_{A}-1}\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|.

(2) Let
H^{\prime}=\{p^{i}\ (\operatorname{mod}d)\,|\,0\leq i<f_{A}\}
and write
a
(resp.
b
) for the multiplicity of
1
(resp.
2
) among the
\|t\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|
’s for
t\in H^{\prime}
. Depending on whether
H^{\prime}\ni-1
or not, we divide the proof into two cases:

(i) If
H^{\prime}\ni-1
, then
a+b+2=f_{A}
and we find

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{f_{A}-1}\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|=a+2b+3=f_{A}+b+1>f_{A}.

Hence by (1),
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})>f
.

(ii) If
H\not\ni-1
, then
a+b+1=f_{A}
and one sees

\displaystyle\frac{f}{f_{A}}\sum_{i=0}^{f_{A}-1}\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|=\frac{f}{f_{A}}(0+1+\cdots+1+2+\cdots+2)=\frac{f(f_{A}+b-1)}{f_{A}}.

Hence
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})<f
is equivalent to
b-1<0
(i.e.,
b=0
).

Therefore the inequality
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})<f
holds if and only if the case (ii) occurs with
b=0
; this is the case where
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=0
and
\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=1
for all
i
with
1\leq i<f_{A}
.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\widetilde{X}_{A}
be a Calabi–Yau threefold of Delsarte type in
\mathbb{P}^{4}(Q)
with matrix
A
. Let
d_{A}
be the integer defined in (
4.1
) and
f_{A}
be the order of
p
modulo
d_{A}
. Write
h:=\operatorname{ht}\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
. Then the following assertions hold.

(1)

h
is finite if and only if
\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|\leq 1
for
0\leq i<f_{A}
.

(2)

If
h
is finite, then
h=f_{A}
.

Proof.

(1) Recall first that the height of
\Phi_{\widetilde{X}_{A}}
can be computed from the Newton polygon of
F_{d}/\Gamma_{A}
and its slope-less-than-
1
part has length equal to the number of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})
satisfying
\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})<f
. Write
K
for the quotient field of the ring
W(k)
of Witt vectors over
k
. Then by
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle h<\infty

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\dim_{K}\big(H^{3}_{\rm cris}\big(\widetilde{X}_{A}\big)\otimes K_{[0,1[}\big)\geq 1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\#\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})\,|\,\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})<f\}\geq 1

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})<f

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|\leq 1\quad\text{for all $i$ with $0\leq i<f_{A}$.}

The last equivalence follows from Lemma
4.5
.

(2) Let
H
be the orbit of
p
in
(\mathbb{Z}/d\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. From the proof of Lemma
4.5
, one sees that the length of the
H
-orbit of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}
is equal to
f_{A}
. Hence together with Lemma
4.4
, we have the following:

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=\dim_{K}\big(H^{3}_{\rm cris}\big(\widetilde{X}_{A}\big)\otimes K_{[0,1[}\big)=\#\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{A}(X_{A})\,|\,\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha})<f\}

\displaystyle=\text{the length of the $H$-orbit of $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}$}=f_{A}.

∎

For actual calculations, it is simpler to work modulo
d_{A}
rather than modulo
d
. We thus reformulate Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
4.6
in terms of modulo
d_{A}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

With the assumptions as in Theorem
4.6
, define

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}=\frac{1}{e}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}=\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{e},\dots,\frac{\alpha_{4}}{e}\right).

Let
H_{A}=\{p^{i}\pmod{d_{A}}\,|\,0\leq i<f_{A}\}
, and for
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{4})\in(\mathbb{Z}/d_{A}\mathbb{Z})^{5}
, write

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{d_{A}}=\sum_{i=0}^{4}\left\langle\frac{\beta_{i}}{d_{A}}\right\rangle-1,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{H_{A}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\sum_{t\in H_{A}}\|t\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{d_{A}}.

Then we may regard
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}
as an element of
(\mathbb{Z}/d_{A}\mathbb{Z})^{5}
and have the following.

(1)

\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\|=\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}
.

(2)

\mathcal{A}_{H}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0})=\frac{f}{f_{A}}\mathcal{A}_{H_{A}}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A})
.

(3)

h
is finite if and only if
\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}\leq 1
for
0\leq i<f_{A}
.

Proof.

(1) We write
\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}=(\beta_{0},\dots,\beta_{4})
. Then

\displaystyle\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{0}\big\|=\sum_{j=0}^{4}\left\langle\frac{p^{i}\alpha_{j}}{d}\right\rangle-1=\sum_{j=0}^{4}\left\langle\frac{p^{i}e\beta_{j}}{ed_{A}}\right\rangle-1=\sum_{j=0}^{4}\left\langle\frac{p^{i}\beta_{j}}{d_{A}}\right\rangle-1=\big\|p^{i}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\big\|_{d_{A}}.

(2) This follows from Lemma
4.5
.

(3) This follows from (1) and Theorem
4.6
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

With the assumptions and notation as in Theorem
4.6
and Corollary
4.7
, we have the following.

(1)

If
p\equiv 1\pmod{d_{A}}
, then
h=1
.

(2)

If there exists
\mu
such that
p^{\mu}\equiv-1\pmod{d_{A}}
, then
h
is infinite.

Proof.

(1) If
p\equiv 1\pmod{d_{A}}
, then
f_{A}=1
and
\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}=0\leq 1
. By Theorem
4.6
,
h=1
.

(2)
p^{\mu}
is in
H_{A}
and we find
\|p^{\mu}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}=\|-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}=3-\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{A}\|_{d_{A}}=3
. Hence by Theorem
4.6
,
h
is infinite.
∎

Compared with the formal groups of
K3
surfaces of Delsarte type in
[
8
]
, it seems rather restrictive to have

for all

. This may be a reason why the infinite height occurs more often than finite height for Calabi–Yau threefolds
\widetilde{X}_{A}
; see examples in Section

.

Remark 4.9
.

For
K3
surfaces of Delsarte type in

holds, where the converse to (2) is also true (cf.

]
). But, for a threefold

is infinite.

Generalized Gibbs ensemble of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice, Circular β \beta -ensemble and double confluent Heun equation
T. Grava, G. Mazzuca

Abstract
We consider the discrete defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation in its integrable version, which is called defocusing Ablowitz-Ladik lattice. We consider periodic boundary conditions with period N N and initial data sample according to the Generalized Gibbs ensemble. In this setting, the Lax matrix of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice is a random CMV-periodic matrix and it is related to the Killip-Nenciu Circular β \beta -ensemble at high-temperature. We obtain the generalized free energy of the Ablowitz-Ladik lattice and the density of states of the random Lax matrix by establishing a mapping to the one-dimensional log-gas. For the Gibbs measure related to the Hamiltonian of the Ablowitz-Ladik flow, we obtain the density of states via a particular solution of the double-confluent Heun equation.



1
Introduction

The defocusing Ablowitz–Ladik (AL) lattice for the complex functions
\alpha_{j}(t)
,
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
, is the system of nonlinear equations

i\dot{\alpha}_{j}=-(\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_{j-1}-2\alpha_{j})+|\alpha_{j}|^{2}(\alpha_{j-1}+\alpha_{j+1})\,,

(1.1)

where
\dot{\alpha}_{j}=\dfrac{d\alpha_{j}}{dt}
. We assume
N
-periodic boundary conditions
\alpha_{j+N}=\alpha_{j}
, for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The AL lattice was introduced by Ablowitz and Ladik
[
1
,
2
]
as the spatial integrable discretization of the defocusing cubic nonlinear Schrödinger Equation (NLS) for the complex function

\psi(x,t)
,
x\in S^{1}
and
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
:

i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=-\partial^{2}_{x}\psi(x,t)+2\lvert\psi(x,t)\rvert^{2}\psi(x,t).

(1.2)

The cubic NLS equation was proved to be integrable by Zakharov and Shabat
[
70
]
.

It is straightforward to verify that the quantity

K^{(0)}:=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)\,,

(1.3)

is a constant of motion for the AL lattice, namely
\dfrac{d}{dt}K^{(0)}=0
. This implies that if
|\alpha_{j}(0)|<1
for all
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
|\alpha_{j}(t)|<1
for all
t>0
.
We chose the
N
-dimensional disc
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
as the phase space of the AL lattice, here
{\mathbb{D}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,|z|<1\}
.
On
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
we introduce the symplectic form
[
23
,
30
]

\omega=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\rho_{j}^{2}}{\rm d}\alpha_{j}\wedge{\rm d}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}\,,\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}.

(1.4)

The corresponding Poisson bracket is defined for functions
f,g\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{D}}^{N})
as

\begin{split}\{f,g\}&=i\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rho_{j}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial\alpha_{j}}-\frac{\partial f}{\partial\alpha_{j}}\frac{\partial g}{\partial{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}}\right)\,.\end{split}

(1.5)

The phase shift
\alpha_{j}(t)\to e^{-2it}\alpha_{j}(t)
transforms the AL lattice
into the equation

i\dot{\alpha}_{j}=-\rho_{j}^{2}(\alpha_{j+1}+\alpha_{j-1}),\quad\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}},

(1.6)

which we call the
reduced
AL equation.
We remark that the quantity
-2\ln(K^{(0)})
is the generator of the shift
\alpha_{j}(t)\to e^{-2it}\alpha_{j}(t)
, while
H_{1}=K^{(1)}+{\overline{K^{(1)}}}
with

K^{(1)}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j+1},

(1.7)

generates the flow (
1.6
).
The AL equations (
1.1
) have the Hamiltonian structure

\dot{\alpha}_{j}=\{{\alpha}_{j},H_{AL}\},\quad H_{AL}(\alpha_{j},\overline{{\alpha}}_{j})=-2\ln(K^{(0)})+K^{(1)}+\overline{K^{(1)}}.

(1.8)

Integrability.

As we have already said, the AL lattice was discovered by Ablowitz and Ladik by discretizing the
2\times 2
Zakharov-Shabat Lax pair
[
1
]
of the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For a comprehensive review see
[
3
]
. The integrability
of the Ablowitz–Ladik system has also been proved by constructing a bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
23
,
9
]
. A techniques to calculate the
\tau
-function correlators has been introduced in
[
15
]
.

Using the connection between orthogonal polynomials on the unit circle and the AL lattice, Nenciu and Simon
[
54
,
59
]
constructed a new Lax pair for the AL lattice that sometimes is referred to as the big Lax pair and which put the AL equation on the same foot as the Toda lattice.
The link between orthogonal and biorthogonal polynomials on the unit circle and the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy (see also
[
4
]
,
[
43
]
) is
the analogue of the celebrated link between the Toda hierarchy and orthogonal polynomials on
the real line (see e.g.
[
19
]
). This link was also generalized to the non-commutative case
[
14
]
(see also
[
16
]
).
Generalization of this construction to other integrable equations has been considered in
[
55
]
.

To construct the big Lax pair, we follow
[
54
,
59
]
and we double the size of the chain according to the periodic boundary conditions, thus we consider a chain of
2N
particles
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{2N}
such that
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j+N}
for
j=1,\ldots,N
.
Define the
2\times 2
unitary matrix
\Xi_{j}

\Xi_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}&\rho_{j}\\
\rho_{j}&-\alpha_{j}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad j=1,\dots,2N\,,

(1.9)

and the
2N\times 2N
matrices

{\mathcal{M}}=\begin{pmatrix}-\alpha_{2N}&&&&&\rho_{2N}\\
&\Xi_{2}\\
&&\Xi_{4}\\
&&&\ddots\\
&&&&\Xi_{2N-2}\\
\rho_{2N}&&&&&{\overline{\alpha}}_{2N}\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad{\mathcal{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Xi_{1}\\
&\Xi_{3}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&\Xi_{2N-1}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(1.10)

Now let us define the unitary Lax matrix

{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{L}}{\mathcal{M}}\,,

(1.11)

that has the structure of a
5
-band diagonal matrix

\begin{pmatrix}*&*&*&&&&&&&*\\
*&*&*&&&&&&&*\\
&*&*&*&*&&&&&\\
&*&*&*&*&&&&&\\
&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&&&\\
&&&&&&*&*&*&*\\
&&&&&&*&*&*&*\\
*&&&&&&&*&*&*\\
*&&&&&&&*&*&*\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

The matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
is a periodic CMV matrix
(after Cantero Morales and Velsquez
[
17
]
).
The
N
-periodic reduced AL equation (
1.6
) is equivalent to the following Lax equation for the matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
:

\dot{\mathcal{E}}=i\left[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{+}+({\mathcal{E}}^{+})^{\dagger}\right]\,,

(1.12)

where
†
stands for hermitian conjugate and

{\mathcal{E}}^{+}_{j,k}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{E}}_{j,j}\quad j=k\\
{\mathcal{E}}_{j,k}\quad k=j+1\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,2N\,\mbox{or}\,k=j+2\,{\,{\rm mod}\,}\,2N\\
0\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(1.13)

We observe that
({\mathcal{E}}^{+})^{\dagger}+({\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger})^{+}={\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}
and
[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger}]=0
because
{\mathcal{E}}
is unitary. Therefore, one can write the equation (
1.12
)
in the equivalent form

\dot{\mathcal{E}}=i\left[{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{E}}^{+}-({\mathcal{E}}^{\dagger})^{+}\right]\,.

(1.14)

The formulation (
1.12
) implies that the quantities

K^{(\ell)}=\frac{\mbox{Tr}\left({\mathcal{E}}^{\ell}\right)}{2},\quad\ell=1,\dots,N-1,

(1.15)

are constants of motion for the defocusing AL system. For example

K^{(1)}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j+1},\quad K^{(2)}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}[(\alpha_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j+1})^{2}-2\alpha_{j}\overline{\alpha}_{j+2}\rho_{j+1}^{2}]\,.

Furthermore,
K^{(0)},K^{(1)},\dots,K^{(N-1)}
are functionally independent and in involution, showing that the
N
-periodic AL system is integrable
[
54
,
1
,
3
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

The quantity
2\Re(K^{(1)})
generates the reduces AL equation (
1.6
), while the quantity
-2\Im(K^{(1)})

generates the flow

\dot{\alpha}_{j}=(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})(\alpha_{j+1}-\alpha_{j}),

which is called Schur flow. The Schur flow emerges in
[
2
]
as a spatial discretization of the
defocusing modified Korteweg–de Vries equation

\partial_{t}f-6f\partial_{x}f+\partial_{x}^{3}f=0.

For the integration of the Schur flow and its relation to orthogonal polynomial on the unit circle see
[
32
,
60
]
.

Generalized Gibbs Ensemble for the Ablowitz–Ladik Lattice.

The symplectic form
\omega
in (
1.4
) induces on
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
the volume form
{\rm d}\mbox{vol}=\dfrac{1}{K^{(0)}}{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, with
{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}(i{\rm d}\alpha_{j}\wedge{\rm d}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}\,)
. We observe that
\int_{\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{N}}{\rm d}\mbox{vol}=\infty
, however, we can define the Gibbs measure with respect to the Hamiltonian
H_{AL}
in (
1.8
):

\frac{1}{Z_{\beta}}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}H_{AL}}{\rm d}\mbox{vol}=\frac{1}{Z_{\beta}}e^{\beta\Re(K^{(1)})}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\beta-1}{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}},\quad\beta>0,

(1.16)

where
Z_{\beta}=\int_{\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{N}}e^{\beta\Re(K^{(1)})}\prod_{j=1}^{N}(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2})^{\beta-1}{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}<\infty
is the normalizing constant.
The above probability measure is clearly invariant under the Hamiltonian flow
{\alpha}_{j}(0)\to{\alpha}_{j}(t)
associated to the Ablowitz–Ladik equation (
1.1
).

Since the Ablowitz–Ladik lattice posses several conserved quantities (
1.15
), one can introduce a Generalized Gibbs Ensemble on the phase space
{\mathbb{D}}^{N}
in the following way. Fix
{\mathbb{N}}\ni\kappa\leq N-1
and let us define

V(z)=\sum_{m=1}^{\kappa}\eta_{m}\Re(z^{m})\,,

(1.17)

where
\eta_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}
are called chemical potentials. Then

\mbox{Tr}\left(V\left({\mathcal{E}}\right)\right)=\sum_{m=1}^{\kappa}\eta_{m}(K^{(m)}+\overline{K^{(m)}}),

where
K^{(m)}
are the AL conserved quantities (
1.15
).
The finite volume Generalized Gibbs measure can be written as:

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{AL}({\alpha}_{1},\ldots,{\alpha}_{N})=\frac{1}{Z^{AL}_{N}(V,\beta)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta-1}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(V\left({\mathcal{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,,

(1.18)

where
Z^{AL}_{N}(V,\beta)
is the partition function of the system:

Z^{AL}_{N}(V,\beta)=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta-1}\exp\left(-\mbox{Tr}\left(V\left({\mathcal{E}}\right)\right)\right){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,.

(1.19)

Choosing the initial data of the Ablowitz–Ladik lattice according to the Generalized Gibbs measure (
1.18
), the Lax matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
turns into a random matrix.
In
[
51
]
Mendl and Sphon study the dynamic of the Ablowitz–Ladik lattice at non-zero temperature. They study numerically correlation functions and in particular,
introducing the density
\delta_{j}=\Re(\alpha_{j+1}\overline{\alpha}_{j})
, they study the density-density correlation function

{\bf E}\left[\delta_{j}(t)\delta_{1}(0)\right]-{\bf E}\left[\delta_{j}(t)\right]{\bf E}\left[\delta_{1}(0)\right]\,,

where
{\bf E}\left[\cdot\right]
is the expectation with respect to Gibbs measure (
1.16
). They showed numerically that density-density time correlations in thermal equilibrium have symmetrically located peaks, which travel in opposite directions at constant speed, broaden ballistically and decay as
1/t^{\gamma}
when
t\to\infty
, where the scaling exponent
\gamma
is approximately equal to one. This behaviour is believed to be typical of integrable nonlinear systems.

More quantitative results have been obtained for linear (integrable) systems and for the Toda lattice. It was shown in
[
34
]
that the fastest peaks of the correlation functions of harmonic oscillators with short range interactions have a Airy type scaling.
Regarding nonlinear integrable systems in
[
61
]
Spohn was able to connect the Gibbs ensemble of the Toda lattice to the Dumitriu-Edelman
\beta
-ensemble
[
21
]
. In this way, the generalized Gibbs free energy of the Toda chain turns out to be related to the
\beta
-ensembles of random matrix theory in the mean-field regime
[
22
,
7
]
. The behaviour of the correlation functions of the Toda chains has been derived by applying the theory of generalized hydrodynamic
[
62
,
20
]
. We mention also the recent work
[
36
]
, where the authors derive a large deviation principle for the mean density of states for the Generalized Gibbs measure of the Toda lattice.

2
Statement of the results

In this manuscript we derive the mean density of states
\mu^{\beta}_{AL}
of the random Lax matrix
\mathcal{E}
sampled according to generalized Gibbs measure (
1.18
) and we determine the free energy of the AL generalized Gibbs ensemble

F_{AL}(V,\beta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log Z^{AL}_{N}(V,\beta).

This is achieved
by connecting the generalized Gibbs ensemble of the Ablowitz–Ladik lattice to the Killip-Nenciu
[
42
]
matrix Circular
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature investigated by Hardy and Lambert
[
37
]
.
Further connections between discrete integrable systems with Gibbs measure initial data and classes of random matrices has been explored in
[
33
]
. For connections between integrable PDEs and random objects see
[
6
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
be the space of probability measures on the torus
\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]
and for
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
let us consider the functional

\begin{split}{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}(\mu)&=2\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta-\beta\int\int\limits_{\hskip-5.69046pt\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\phi|}{2}\right)\mu(\theta)\mu(\phi){\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\phi\\
&+\int\limits_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\mu(\theta)\right)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta+\ln(2\pi)\,.\end{split}

(2.1)

Remark 2.1
.

Here and below, we make an abuse of notation by denoting the potential
V(z)=V(e^{i\theta})
simply by
V(\theta)
.

For sufficiently regular potential
V(\theta)
, the functional (
2.1
) has a unique minimizer
\mu_{HT}^{\beta}({\rm d}\theta)=\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta){\rm d}\theta
,
[
58
]
, that describes the density of states of the Circular
\beta
-ensemble at high-temperature
[
37
]
. For finite
\beta
and smooth potentials
V(\theta)
, it has been shown
by Hardy and Lambert in
[
37
]
that the minimizer
\mu_{HT}^{\beta}({\rm d}\theta)
has a smooth density and its support is the whole torus
\mathbb{T}
.

Theorem 2.2

(First Main theorem)
.

Consider
\beta>0
and a smooth potential
V
as in (
1.17
) on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. The mean density of states
\mu_{AL}^{\beta}({\rm d}\theta):=\mu_{AL}^{\beta}(\theta){\rm d}\theta
of the Ablowitz–Ladik Lax matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
in (
1.11
) endowed with the probability (
1.18
) is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and takes the form

\mu_{AL}^{\beta}(\theta)=\partial_{\beta}\left(\beta\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)\right)\;a.e.,

(2.2)

where
\mu_{HT}^{\beta}
is the unique minimizer of the functional (
2.1
).

To prove the above theorem we derive a relation (see Proposition
4.2
)
between the free energy of the
\beta
-ensembles at high-temperature, namely the minimum value of the minimizer (
2.1
)

F_{HT}(V,\beta):={\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}(\mu^{\beta}_{HT}),

and the free energy
F_{AL}(V,\beta)
of the AL lattice:

F_{AL}(V,\beta)=\partial_{\beta}\left(\beta F_{HT}(V,\beta)\right)+\ln(2).

Such relation is obtained via transfer operator techniques.

The particular case
V(\theta)=2\eta\cos\theta
corresponds to the free energy associated to the AL equation (
1.1
), and we show that the minimizer of the functional (
2.1
) is obtained via a particular solution of the Double Confluent Heun (DCH) equation.

Theorem 2.3

(Second main theorem)
.

Fix
\beta>0
and let
V(\theta)=\eta\cos\theta
, where
\eta
is a real parameter. There exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
\eta\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, the minimizer

\mu_{HT}^{\beta}({\rm d}\theta)=\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta){\rm d}\theta
of the functional (
2.1
) takes the form

\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}+\frac{1}{\pi\beta}\Re\left(\frac{zv^{\prime}(z)}{v(z)}\right)_{\big|z=e^{i\theta}}\,,

(2.3)

where
v(z)
is the unique solution (up to a multiplicative non-zero constant) of the Double Confluent Heun (DCH) equation

z^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(z)+\left(-\eta+z(\beta+1)+\eta z^{2}\right)v^{\prime}(z)+\eta\beta(z+\lambda)v(z)=0\,

(2.4)

analytic for
|z|\leq r
with
r\geq 1
. Such solution is differentiable in the parameter
\eta
and
\beta
. The parameter
\lambda=\lambda(\eta,\beta)
in (
2.4
) is determined for
\eta\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
by the solution of the equation

\lambda(R_{1})_{11}+\frac{\eta}{\beta+1}(R_{1})_{21}=0,\quad

(2.5)

with the condition
\lambda(\eta=0,\beta)=0
. In the above expression
(R_{1})_{jk}
is the
jk
entry of the matrix
R_{1}
which is defined by the infinite product

R_{1}=M_{1}M_{2}\dots M_{k}\dots,\quad M_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{\lambda\beta\eta}{k(k+\beta)}&\frac{\eta^{2}}{k(k+\beta+1)}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

We remark that the solution of the double confluent Heun equation has generically an essential singularity at
z=0
and
z=\infty
,
and one needs to tune the
accessory parameter

\lambda
to obtain an analytic solution, for a review see
[
57
]
. In our derivation of (
2.4
) the parameter
\lambda
coincides with the first moment of the measure
\mu(\theta)
, namely
\lambda=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu(\theta)e^{i\theta}d\theta
. It is a transcendental function of
\beta
and
\eta
and it is related to the Painlevé III equation
[
24
,
47
]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Under the change of variable

v(z)=\exp\left(-\frac{\eta}{2}\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right)\right)z^{-\frac{\beta+1}{2}}f(z)\,,

the DCH equation (
2.4
) takes the form of a Schrödinger equation

f^{\prime\prime}(z)+q(z)f(z)=0,

with potential
q(z)
singular at the origin

q(z)=\frac{1}{z^{2}}\left(\eta\frac{\beta-1}{2}\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right)-\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}\frac{(z^{2}-1)^{2}}{z^{2}}-\frac{1}{4}(\beta^{2}+4\beta+3)+\eta\beta\lambda\right).

Remark 2.5
.

For the case
V=0
it was shown in
[
37
]
that the minimizer of the functional (
3.18
) is the uniform measure on the circle, while for the case
V(\theta)=\beta V(\theta)

and
\beta\to\infty
the minimizer of (
2.1
) was considered in
[
49
]
. The particular case
V(\theta)\to\beta\eta\cos\theta
and
\beta\to\infty
has first been considered by Gross–Witten
[
35
]
and Baik–Deift–Johansson
[
11
]
.
The measure (
2.3
) in Theorem
2.3
generalizes the result of Gross and Witten
[
35
]
and Baik–Deift–Johansson
[
11
]
to the high-temperature regime (see Remark
5.6
).

3
Circular
\beta
Ensemble at high-temperature

The Circular Ensemble at temperature
\tilde{\beta}^{-1}
is a system of
N
identical particles on the one-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}=[-\pi,\pi]
with distribution

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{\beta}}(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{N})=\frac{1}{Z^{C\tilde{\beta}E}_{N}}\prod_{j<\ell}|e^{i\theta_{\ell}}-e^{i\theta_{j}}|^{{\widetilde{\beta}}}{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\theta},\quad{\rm d}\boldsymbol{\theta}={\rm d}\theta_{1}\dots{\rm d}\theta_{N}\,,

(3.1)

where
Z_{N}^{C\tilde{\beta}E}>0
is the norming constant, or partition function of the system.
For
\tilde{\beta}=1,2,4
Dyson observed that the above measure corresponds to the eigenvalue distribution of circular orthogonal ensemble (COE), circular unitary ensemble (CUE) and circular symplectic ensemble (CSE) of random matrix ensembles (see e.g.
[
50
,
25
]
).
For general
\tilde{\beta}>0
, Killip and Nenciu proved that the Circular
\beta
Ensemble can be associated to the eigenvalue distribution of a random
sparse matrix, the so-called CMV matrix, after Cantero, Moral, Velázquez
[
17
]
. To state their result, we need the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

A complex random variable
X
with values on the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
is
\Theta_{\nu}
-distributed (
\nu>1
) if

{\bf E}\left[f(X)\right]=\frac{\nu-1}{2\pi}\int_{\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}}f(z)(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{\nu-3}{2}}{\rm d}^{2}z\,.

(3.2)

for any measurable function
f\,:\,{\mathbb{D}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
. When
\nu=1
,
\Theta_{1}
is the uniform distribution on the unit circle
S^{1}
.

We recall that for
{\mathbb{N}}\ni\nu\geq 2
, such measure has the following geometrical interpretation: if
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{\nu+1})
is chosen at random according to the surface measure on the unit sphere
S^{\nu}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\nu+1}
, then
u_{1}+iu_{2}
is
\Theta_{\nu}-
distributed.
We can now state the result of Killip-Nenciu.

Theorem 3.2

(cf.
[
42
]
Theorem 1)
.

Consider the block diagonal
N\times N
matrices

M=\mbox{diag}\left(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{3},\Xi_{5}\ldots,\right)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad L=\mbox{diag}\left(\Xi_{0},\Xi_{2},\Xi_{4},\ldots\right)\,,

(3.3)

where the block
\Xi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N-1
, takes the form

\Xi_{j}=\begin{pmatrix}{\overline{\alpha}}_{j}&\rho_{j}\\
\rho_{j}&-\alpha_{j}\end{pmatrix}\,,\;\;\rho_{j}=\sqrt{1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}},

(3.4)

while
\Xi_{0}=(1)
and
\Xi_{N}=({\overline{\alpha}}_{N})
are
1\times 1
matrices.
Define the
N\times N
sparse matrix

E=LM,

(3.5)

and suppose that the entries
\alpha_{j}
are independent complex random variables with
\alpha_{j}\sim\Theta_{{\widetilde{\beta}}(N-j)+1}
for
1\leq j\leq N-1
and
\alpha_{N}
is uniformly distributed on the unit circle.
Then the eigenvalues of
E
are distributed according to the Circular Ensemble (
3.1
) at temperature
\tilde{\beta}^{-1}
.

We observe that each of the matrices
\Xi_{j}
is unitary, and so are the matrices
L
and
M
. As a result, the eigenvalues of
E
clearly lie on the unit circle.
The matrix
E
is a
5
-diagonal unitary matrix that takes the form

Lemma 3.3

Let

Then the eigenvalues of
{\widetilde{E}}
are all double, they lie on the unit circle and are distributed according to (

Moreover

where

Proof.

First, we notice that the eigenvalues of
{\widetilde{E}}
are all double, since it is a block diagonal matrix with two identical blocks.

We consider the change of variables

{\rm d}\mathbb{P}({\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N-1},\varphi)=\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}e^{-\mbox{Tr}(V({\widetilde{E}}))}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}{\rm d}^{2}\alpha_{j}{\rm d}\varphi}{Z^{HT}_{N}(V,\beta)}\,.

Now, let
e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\theta_{N}}

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N-1},\varphi\right)}{\partial(\boldsymbol{\theta},{\boldsymbol{\gamma}})}\right|=2^{1-N}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\gamma_{j}}\,,

(3.12)

here
{\boldsymbol{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{N-1})

\Delta(e^{i\boldsymbol{\theta}})=\prod_{j<\ell}\left(e^{i\theta_{j}}-e^{i\theta_{\ell}}\right)

\begin{split}&{\rm d}\mathbb{P}(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{N-1},\varphi))=\frac{e^{-\mbox{Tr}(V({\widetilde{E}}))}}{Z^{HT}_{N}(V,\beta)}{\rm d}\varphi\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}{\rm d}\alpha_{j}{\rm d}\overline{\alpha}_{j}\\

in (

The mean density of state

is defined as the non-random probability measure such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}({\rm d}\theta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}{\bf E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(\theta)\mu_{N}({\rm d}\theta)\right]\,,

, and the expected value is taken

Let

be the set of probability measures on the one-dimensional torus

When

\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mu_{0}
and we can write
\mu({\rm d}\theta)=\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta

Theorem 3.4
.

(cf.
[

Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})
be the set of probability measures on the one-dimensional torus and

V\,:\,\mathbb{T}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable and bounded function. For any
\beta>0
consider the functional
{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}:\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})\to[0,\infty]

{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}(\mu)=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta+\beta\mathit{E}(\mu)+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\mu(\theta)\right)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta+\ln(2\pi)\,.

(3.18)

Then

(a)

the functional
{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}(\mu)

\mu^{\beta}_{HT}({\rm d}\theta)=\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta){\rm d}\theta

\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{T})

(b)

(c)

(d)

;

(d)

the empirical measure

as

From the above theorem when the potential

is at least

the minimizer of the functional

\dfrac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}}{\delta\mu}=2V(\theta)-2\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\phi|}{2}\right)\mu(\phi){\rm d}\phi+\ln\mu(\theta)+1=C(V,\beta)\,

Lemma 3.5
.

For any

Remark 3.6

For a real valued function

Combining (

The moments of the density of states

Since the proof of this proposition is rather technical, we postpone it to Appendix

Proof of Theorem

First, we define

Proof of Theorem

The proof of Theorem
2.3
consists of mainly two parts: we first derive from the variational equations with respect to the functional
{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}
, the double confluent Heun equation
(
2.4
). Then we show that such equation admits an analytic solution in any compact sets of the complex plane containing the origin.
From Theorem
3.4
we know that the density
\mu^{\beta}_{HT}
is characterized as the unique minimizer of the functional (
3.18
).
We follow the ideas developed in
[
7
,
8
,
27
,
18
]
to find this minimizer explicitly.
We consider the Euler-Lagrange equation of the functional (
3.18
), namely

\dfrac{\delta{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}}{\delta\mu}=2V(\theta)-2\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\phi|}{2}\right)\mu(\phi){\rm d}\phi+\ln\mu(\theta)=C(V,\beta)\,,\;\;a.e.

(5.1)

where the equation holds almost everywhere,
C(V,\beta)
is a constant depending on the potential and
\beta
, but not on the variable
\theta
.
Differentiating the Euler-Lagrange equation (
5.1
) at the minimizer
\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)
with respect to
\theta
we obtain the following integral equation (see
[
37
, Proposition 2.5]
):

\partial_{\theta}\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)+\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)[2\partial_{\theta}(V(\theta))+\beta\mathcal{H}\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)]=0\,,

(5.2)

where
\mathcal{H}
is the Hilbert transform defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
as

\mathcal{H}\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\theta)=-\mbox{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\phi}{2}\right)\mu_{HT}^{\beta}(\phi){\rm d}\phi

(5.3)

and
p.v.
is the Cauchy principal value, that is the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the integral on the torus
\mathbb{T}
restricted to the domain
|e^{i\theta}-e^{i\phi}|>\varepsilon
. We notice that the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
is diagonal on the bases of exponential
\{e^{in\theta}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
, meaning that

{\mathit{H}}e^{in\theta}=2\pi i\mbox{sgn}(n)e^{in\theta}\,,

(5.4)

where
\mbox{sgn}(\cdot)
is the sign function with the convention that
\mbox{sgn}(0)=0
.

Setting
e^{i\theta}=z
and
e^{i\phi}=w
, we recognize the Riesz–Herglotz kernel
\dfrac{z+w}{z-w}
expressed as

\dfrac{z+w}{z-w}=-i\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\phi}{2}\right)\,.

Therefore

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\phi}{2}\right)\mu(\phi){\rm d}\phi=i+2\int_{S^{1}}\dfrac{\mu(\phi)_{|e^{i\phi}=w}dw}{z-w},

where
S^{1}
is the anticlockwise oriented circle, and we used the normalization condition
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\mu(\phi){\rm d}\phi=1
. In the following, in order to simplify the notation, we indicate
\mu(\phi)_{|e^{i\phi}=w}
just as
\mu(w)
.
We can recast (
5.2
) in the form

z\partial_{z}\mu(z)+\mu(z)\left[2z\partial_{z}V(z)-\beta+2i\beta\mbox{p.v.}\int_{S^{1}}\mu(w)\dfrac{{\rm d}w}{z-w}\right]=0\,.

(5.5)

For
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\backslash S^{1}
let us define

G(z):=\int_{S^{1}}\mu(w)\dfrac{{\rm d}w}{w-z}=\frac{i}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cot\left(\frac{\theta-\phi}{2}\right)\mu(\phi){\rm d}\phi\,,

(5.6)

and for
z\in S^{1}
let
G_{\pm}(z)=\lim_{{\widetilde{z}}\to z}G({\widetilde{z}})
for
{\widetilde{z}}
inside and outside the unit circle respectively.
Then by (
5.5
)

\begin{split}G_{\pm}(z)&=\pm\pi i\mu(z)+\mbox{p.v.}\int_{S^{1}}\mu(w)\dfrac{{\rm d}w}{w-z}\\
&=\pm\pi i\mu(z)+\frac{i}{2}-\frac{2iz\partial_{z}V(z)}{2\beta}-\frac{iz\partial_{z}\mu(z)}{2\beta\mu(z)}\,.\end{split}

(5.7)

This implies that for
z\in S^{1}
one has

\displaystyle G_{+}(z)+G_{-}(z)=i-\frac{2iz\partial_{z}V(z)}{\beta}-\frac{iz\partial_{z}\mu(z)}{\beta\mu(z)}\,,

(5.8)

\displaystyle G_{+}(z)-G_{-}(z)=2\pi i\mu(z)\,.

(5.9)

Multiplying the two previous expressions, one obtains:

G_{+}(z)^{2}-G_{-}(z)^{2}=2\pi i\mu(z)\left(i-\frac{2iz\partial_{z}V(z)}{\beta}-\frac{zi\partial_{z}\mu(z)}{\beta\mu(z)}\right)\,.

(5.10)

In order to proceed we have to specify the potential
V(z)
, in our case we will consider

V(z)=\frac{\eta}{2}\left(z+\frac{1}{z}\right).

(5.11)

Applying the Sokhtoski-Plemelj formula
[
28
]
to the above boundary value problem, one obtains

G^{2}(z)=i\int_{S^{1}}\frac{\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w-\frac{i\eta}{\beta}\int_{S^{1}}\frac{\left(w-{\overline{w}}\right)\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w-\frac{i}{\beta}\int_{S^{1}}\frac{w\partial_{w}\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w\,.

(5.12)

The second term in the r.h.s. of the above expression gives

\begin{split}\int_{S^{1}}\frac{(w-{\overline{w}})\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w&=\int_{S^{1}}\frac{w\pm z}{w-z}\mu(w){\rm d}w+\frac{1}{z}\int_{S^{1}}\mu(w)\left(-\frac{1}{w-z}+\frac{1}{w}\right){\rm d}w\\
&=\left(zG(z)+i\lambda-\frac{G(z)}{z}+\frac{i}{z}\right)\,,\end{split}

(5.13)

where we have defined

\lambda:=-i\int_{S^{1}}\mu(w){\rm d}w,\quad\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(5.14)

The third term in the r.h.s. of (
5.12
) gives

\begin{split}\int_{S^{1}}\frac{w\partial_{w}\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w&=\int_{S^{1}}\partial_{w}\mu(w){\rm d}w+z\int_{S^{1}}\frac{\partial_{w}\mu(w)}{w-z}{\rm d}w\\
&=z\int_{S^{1}}\frac{\mu(w)}{(w-z)^{2}}{\rm d}w=z\partial_{z}G(z),\end{split}

(5.15)

where in these last relations we use the results of Theorem
3.4
about the regularity of
\mu
.
Now we can rewrite (
5.12
) as

G^{2}(z)=iG(z)-\frac{i\eta}{\beta}\left(zG(z)+i\lambda-\frac{G(z)}{z}+\frac{i}{z}\right)-\frac{iz\partial_{z}G(z)}{\beta}\,.

(5.16)

Remark 5.1
.

In the above ODE, the parameter
\lambda=\lambda(\eta,\beta)
depends via (
5.14
) implicitly on the function
G(z)
.
Our strategy to solve the above equation is to consider
\lambda
as a free parameter that is uniquely fixed by the analytic properties of the function
G(z)
.

We can now turn the non-linear first order ODE (
5.16
) into a linear second order ODE through the substitution

G(z)=i+\frac{izv^{\prime}(z)}{\beta v(z)},

(5.17)

getting:

z^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(z)+\left(-\eta+z(\beta+1)+\eta z^{2}\right)v^{\prime}(z)+\eta\beta(z+\lambda)v(z)=0\,,

(5.18)

which is the DCH equation in (
2.4

The solutions to this equation have generically essential singularities at
z=0
and

and the local description near the singularities depends on the parameter
\eta
and
\beta
.
Indeed we have that the two fundamental solutions near

\displaystyle v^{(0)}_{1}(z)=e^{\eta(z+\frac{1}{z})}z^{1-\beta}\kappa_{1}(\eta,\beta,\lambda;z),\quad-\frac{3\pi}{2}<\arg(\eta z)<\frac{3\pi}{2}\,,

(5.19)

\displaystyle v^{(0)}_{2}(z)=\kappa_{2}(\eta,\beta,\lambda,z),\quad-\frac{\pi}{2}<\arg(\eta z)<\frac{5\pi}{2}\,,

(5.20)

where
\kappa_{j}(\eta,\beta,\lambda;z)

.
The quantity
\lambda
is usually referred to as
accessory parameter
.
Since
G(z)
is analytic in the unit disk, continuous up to the boundary, and
G(0)=i

v(z)=v_{0}\exp\left[-i\int_{0}^{z}\beta\frac{G(s)-i}{s}ds\right],\quad v_{0}\neq 0,

has to be analytic in the unit disk.
For this reason we seek for a solution

of the DCH equation that is analytic in the unit disk and such that
v(z)\xrightarrow[z\to 0]{}v_{0}

is a nonzero constant.

Construction of the analytic solution of equation (

Of the fundamental solutions (

This implies the following recurrence relations for the coefficients

\displaystyle\eta(a_{0}\lambda\beta-a_{1})=0\,,

\displaystyle a_{k}(k^{2}+k\beta+\lambda\beta\eta)+\eta(k-1+\beta)a_{k-1}-\eta(k+1)a_{k+1}=0\,,\quad k>0\,,

We start by considering the

Let

Since the proof of this lemma is rather technical, we defer it to appendix

\begin{split}\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})&\leq{\widetilde{C}}_{1}(V,\beta)\int\limits_{{\mathbb{D}}^{(M-2)K}}\left[\prod_{j=K+1}^{(M-1)K}{\rm d}^{2}\alpha_{j}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}\right]\\
&\times\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=2}^{M-2}W({\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell+1})\right)\,,\end{split}

(A.30)

\begin{split}\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})&\geq{\widetilde{c}}_{1}(V,\beta)\int\limits_{{\mathbb{D}}^{(M-2)K}}\left[\prod_{j=K+1}^{(M-1)K}{\rm d}^{2}\alpha_{j}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta\left(1-\frac{j}{N}\right)-1}\right]\\
&\times\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=2}^{M-2}W({\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell+1})\right)\,,\end{split}

(A.31)

where
{\widetilde{C}}_{1}(V,\beta)
, and
{\widetilde{c}}_{1}(V,\beta)
are positive constants depending on
V,\,\beta,K
and
L
but not on
N
.
Combining (
A.25
)-(
A.26
)-(
A.30
)-(
A.31
) we deduce (
A.20
).
∎

Applying the previous proposition, we can express the Free energy of the Circular
\beta
ensemble in the high-temperature regime in terms of
\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M})
:

\begin{split}F_{HT}(V,\beta)&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(Z_{N}^{HT}\right)\\
&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\left(\ln\left(\frac{Z_{N}^{HT}}{\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})}\right)+\ln(\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}))\right)\\
&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\ln(\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}))}{N}\,,\end{split}

(A.32)

where in the last equality we used Proposition
A.2
.

As a final step, we have to understand the behaviour of
\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})
, and for this purpose we need to carefully analyse the compact operators
{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}}
.

Let
\{\psi_{n}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\}_{n\geq 1}
be the eigenfunctions of
{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}}
with corresponding eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\}_{n\geq 1}
and
|\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})|\geq|\lambda_{2}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})|\geq\dots
. From a generalized version of Jentzsch’s Theorem (see
[
69
, Theorem 137.4]
), we deduce that
|\lambda_{n}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})|<\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
for all
n\geq 2
.

We are now in the position to prove the following proposition.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\{\psi_{n}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be the eigenfunctions of the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}}
in (
A.7
) with corresponding eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Consider the operator
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}
in (
A.19
), then there are constants
{\mathtt{d}},a_{j},c_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,M-1
uniformly bounded in
N
, and so in
M
, such that :

\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{a_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\,,

(A.33)

\left|\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\left(\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\right|\leq{\mathtt{d}}\prod_{j=3}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)

(A.34)

Proof.

To simplify the notation, we will drop the
V
dependence of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
, and of the eigenfunctions
\psi_{n}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
.

We will prove (
A.33
) by induction on
M
.
For
M=2
, we have that
{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}={\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}
, so we have to compute:

\begin{split}&\bigg(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\bigg)=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\mathcal{M}}_{K}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\\
&\qquad=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),\prod_{j=1}^{K}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})^{-\frac{K\beta}{2N}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\\
&\qquad=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),\left(1+\frac{{\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\\
&\qquad=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(1+\frac{a_{1}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right),\end{split}

(A.35)

where the function
{\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})
is the first term of the expansion of

\prod_{j=1}^{K}(1-|z_{j}|^{2})^{-\frac{K\beta}{2N}}
in powers of
1/N
and the constant

a_{1}=\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)
is uniformly bounded in
N
.
So the first inductive step is proved.

For general
M
,
we define the vector
\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}=(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)},\dots,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})
so that

{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}={\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}.

Using the above relation we obtain

\begin{split}&\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)=\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\\
&=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\,.\end{split}

(A.36)

Thanks to
[
40
, Chapter VII, Theorem 1.8]
, we know that the eigenfunctions
\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
and the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
are analytic functions of the parameter
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}
, so, for
N
big enough, there exists a function
\xi_{1}({\bf z})\in L^{2}({\mathbb{D}}^{K})
independent of
N
such that:

\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})=\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\left(1+\frac{\xi_{1}({\bf z})}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\,

(A.37)

and a constant
c_{j}
such that

\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j+1)})=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right).

(A.38)

Using (
A.37
) and the expansion of the function defining the operator
{\mathcal{M}}_{K}
we can expand (
A.36
) as:

\begin{split}&\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\\
&=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}),{\mathcal{T}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\right)\\
&+\frac{\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})}{N}\Bigg(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\left({\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})+\xi_{1}({\bf z})+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\Bigg)\\
&+\frac{\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})}{N}\Bigg(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\left(\xi_{1}({\bf z})+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M-1,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M-1}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\Bigg)\,.\end{split}

(A.39)

To bound the last two terms in the above relation, we use (
A.18
) and (
A.38
) so that

\left|\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}}\right|\right|=\left|\left|{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j+1)}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}^{-1}\right|\right|\leq\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j+1)})=\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\;\,

(A.40)

for
j=2,\ldots,M-1
, here in the first inequality we use the fact that
||{\mathcal{M}}_{K}^{-1}||=1
.

Using (
A.40
) we can bound the second term in the r.h.s of (
A.39
) by

\begin{split}&\left|\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}),{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}\ldots{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\left({\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})+\xi_{1}({\bf z})+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\right)\right|\\
&\leq\left|\left|\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\right|\right|_{2}\left|\left|\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)})\left({\widetilde{a}}_{1}({\bf z})+\xi_{1}({\bf z})+O\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)\right)\right|\right|_{2}\\
&\times\left|\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}\ldots{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}}\right|\right|\\
&\leq{\mathtt{c}}\prod_{j=2}^{M-1}\left|\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}}\right|\right|\leq{\mathtt{c}}\prod_{j=3}^{M}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\leq{\mathtt{c}}\prod_{j=2}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\,,\\
\end{split}

(A.41)

for some constant
{\mathtt{c}}
uniformly bounded in
N
. An analogous inequality can be obtained
for the second term in (
A.39
). Thus, applying the induction to the first term in the r.h.s. of (
A.39
), we deduce (
A.33
).

We move to the proof of (
A.34
). Applying (
A.33
), we can estimate (
A.34
) as

\begin{split}\bigg|\sum_{\ell\geq 2}&\bigg(\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\bigg)\bigg|\\
&=\left|\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\left(\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\pm\left(\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{1}({\bf z},\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\right|\\
&\leq\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{a_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)+|\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})|\,.\end{split}

(A.42)

Regarding the second term in the r.h.s of the above expression we claim that there exists a constant
c
such that

|\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})|\leq c\prod_{j=3}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\,.

(A.43)

To derive the above inequality first, we consider the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}={\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}
, it is a compact operator and it is trace class since it is the composition of two different Hilbert–Schmidt operators. Let
\{{\widetilde{\lambda}}_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
be its eigenvalues numbered in such a way that
\{|{\widetilde{\lambda}}_{n}|\}_{n\geq 1}
is a non increasing sequence and let
\{\varphi_{n}({\bf z})\}_{n\geq 1}
be the corresponding eigenfunctions. Then

\begin{split}|\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})|&=\left|\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\varphi_{n},{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)}}\ldots{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(3)}}{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}\varphi_{n}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}|{\widetilde{\lambda}}_{n}|\,\left|\left(\varphi_{n},{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)}}\ldots{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(3)}}\varphi_{n}\right)\right|\\
&\leq\left|\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(M-1)}}{\mathcal{M}}_{K}\ldots{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(3)}}\right|\right|\sum_{n\geq 1}|{\widetilde{\lambda}}_{n}|\,.\end{split}

(A.44)

Since
{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}
is trace class, it is a classical result that
[
31
]

\sum_{n\geq 1}|{\widetilde{\lambda}}_{n}|\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}s_{n}:=||{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}||_{\textrm{Trace}}\,,

(A.45)

where
\{s_{n}\}_{n\geq 1}
are the singular values of the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}
. Furthermore, since
{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}
is the composition of two Hilbert–Schmidt operators, we have the following inequality

||{\mathcal{T}}_{2,1}||_{\textrm{Trace}}\leq||{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(2)}}||_{\textrm{HS}}||{\mathcal{M}}_{K}{\mathcal{T}}_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}}||_{\textrm{HS}}\leq{\widetilde{c}}\,,

(A.46)

where
||\cdot||_{\textrm{HS}}
is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm and
{\widetilde{c}}
is a positive constant uniformly bounded in
N
.
Thus, applying the previous chain of inequalities and the same argument as in (
A.41
), we deduce that

|\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})|\leq c\prod_{j=3}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{c_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\,,

(A.47)

so we conclude our proof.

∎

Applying Proposition (
A.3
) to (
A.32
) we obtain that:

\begin{split}F_{HT}(V,\beta)&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\mbox{Tr}({\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}})\right)=\\
&-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\psi_{n}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{n}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)\right)\\
&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\Bigg[\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{a_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(1+\frac{\sum_{\ell\geq 2}\left(\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)}),{\widetilde{{\mathcal{T}}}}_{M,\boldsymbol{\zeta}_{M}}\psi_{\ell}({\bf z},V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(1)})\right)}{\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\left(1+\frac{a_{j}}{N}+O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right)\right)}\right)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(A.48)

applying the inequality (
A.34
) of Proposition (
A.3
), we deduce that the last term in the above relation goes to zero as
N\to\infty
and we obtain that

F_{HT}(V,\beta)=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\ln\left(\prod_{j=1}^{M-1}\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})\right)\,.

(A.49)

Since
\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})
is positive and an analytic function of the parameter
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}
,
we approximate the vector
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)}
with the vector
(1-\frac{jK}{N})\overbrace{(\beta,\beta,\dots\beta)}^{2K}=(1-\frac{jK}{N})\boldsymbol{\beta}
and deduce that

\lambda_{1}(V,\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(j)})=\lambda_{1}\left(V,\boldsymbol{\beta}\left(1-\frac{jK}{N}\right)\right)+O(N^{-1})
. Therefore, we can rewrite (
A.48
) as

\begin{split}F_{HT}(V,\beta)&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{M-1}\ln\left(\lambda_{1}\left(V,\boldsymbol{\beta}\left(1-\frac{jK}{N}\right)\right)\right)=\\
&-\frac{1}{K}\int_{0}^{1}\ln\left(\lambda_{1}\left(V,\boldsymbol{\beta}x\right)\right){\rm d}x\,.\end{split}

(A.50)

This, combined with (
A.14
), leads to (
A.1
). Moreover, as a consequence of the last relation, we deduce that
F_{HT}(V,\beta)
is analytic in
\beta
for
\beta>0
.

We notice that the proof of Proposition
4.2
is heavily based on the assumption that the potential
V
that we are considering is of finite range, otherwise our approach would not work.

We now prove the moments relations (
4.4
). For this purpose we have to prove the relations

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(\theta m)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}({\rm d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}F_{HT}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)_{|_{t=0}}\,,

(A.51)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(\theta m)\mu^{\beta}_{AL}({\rm d}\theta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}F_{AL}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)_{|_{t=0}}\,.

(A.52)

Analogous relation can be written for the imaginary part of the moments.
We focus on (
A.51
). From Remark
3.7
, we know that
F_{HT}(V,\beta)={\mathcal{F}}^{(V;\beta)}(\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta))
, where the functional
{\mathcal{F}}^{(V,\beta)}
is defined in (
3.18
) and
\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)
is the density of states of the Circular
\beta
ensemble at high-temperature.
We write the Euler-Lagrange equation for this functional, getting that
\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)
satisfies:

2V(\theta)-2\beta\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\gamma|}{2}\right)\right)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\gamma){\rm d}\gamma+\ln(\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta))+C(V,\beta)=0\,,

(A.53)

where
C(V,\beta)
is a constant not depending on
\theta
.

Now let us consider the functional corresponding to the potential
{\widetilde{V}}(\theta)=V(\theta)+\frac{t}{2}\cos(m\theta)
:

\begin{split}{\mathcal{F}}^{\left(V(\theta)+\frac{t}{2}\cos(m\theta),\beta\right)}(\mu)&=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta+t\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(m\theta)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\mu(\theta)\right)\mu(\theta){\rm d}\theta\\
&-\beta\int\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\gamma|}{2}\right)\mu(\theta)\mu(\gamma){\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\gamma+\ln(2\pi)\,.\end{split}

(A.54)

Also this functional has a unique minimizer that we denote by
\mu^{(t)}(\theta)
, with
\mu^{(0)}(\theta)=\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)
. Evaluating the above functional at
\mu^{(t)}(\theta)
, and computing its derivative at

, we deduce the following relation:

\begin{split}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}^{\left(V(\theta)+\frac{t}{2}\cos(m\theta),\beta\right)}&(\mu^{(t)})_{|_{t=0}}=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(m\theta)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta){\rm d}\theta\\
&\quad-2\beta\int\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\gamma|}{2}\right)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\gamma)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\gamma\\
&\quad+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)\right)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta\,.\end{split}

(A.55)

Testing (
A.53
) against
\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}
we obtain

\begin{split}&2\int_{\mathbb{T}}V(\theta)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta-2\beta\int\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{T}}\ln\sin\left(\frac{|\theta-\gamma|}{2}\right)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\gamma)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta{\rm d}\gamma\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\ln\left(\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta)\right)\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta)_{|_{t=0}}{\rm d}\theta=0\,,\end{split}

(A.56)

where we have used
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\partial_{t}\mu^{(t)}(\theta){\rm d}\theta=0
.
Thus, we can simplify (

) as :

\partial_{t}{\mathcal{F}}^{\left(V(\theta)+\frac{t}{2}\cos(m\theta),\beta\right)}(\mu)_{|_{t=0}}=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(m\theta)\mu^{\beta}_{HT}(\theta){\rm d}\theta\,,

(A.57)

which is equivalent to (
A.51
).

To complete the proof of Proposition
4.2
we have to show that (
A.52
) holds.
From the definition of mean density of states (
3.17
) we obtain that:

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\cos(m\theta)\mu^{\beta}_{AL}({\rm d}\theta)&=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{{\bf E}\left[\Re(\mbox{Tr}({\mathcal{E}}^{m}))\right]}{2N}\\
&=-\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\partial_{t}\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}}{NZ_{N}^{(AL)}(V,\beta)}\,,\end{split}

(A.58)

where the expected value is taken with respect to the generalized Gibbs ensemble of the Ablowitz–Ladik lattice. A similar equation holds for the imaginary part of the moment.

Let’s focus on the numerator, first we notice that we can assume that
\Re(z^{m})
and
V
to have the same range
K
.
The more general case can be treated in the same way. Differentiating the partition function we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}\Re(\mathrm{Tr}({\mathcal{E}}^{m}))\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta-1}\\
&\qquad\times\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}W({\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell+1}){-W_{1}(\alpha_{KM+1},\ldots,\alpha_{KM+L},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{1})}\right){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,.\end{split}

(A.59)

Due to the structure of the measure and of the Lax matrix
{\mathcal{E}}
, we deduce that there exist two smooth functions
g:{\mathbb{D}}^{K}\times{\mathbb{D}}^{K}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
g_{1}\,:\,\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{L}\times\overline{{\mathbb{D}}}^{K}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\begin{split}\partial_{t}&\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}=\int_{{\mathbb{D}}^{N}}{\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(1-|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\beta-1}\\
&\qquad\times\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}g({\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell+1})+g_{1}(\alpha_{KM+1},\ldots,\alpha_{KM+L},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{1})\right]\\
&\qquad\times\exp\left(-\sum_{\ell=1}^{M}W({\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{\ell+1}){-W_{1}(\alpha_{KM+1},\ldots,\alpha_{KM+L},{\widetilde{{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}}}_{1})}\right){\rm d}^{2}{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\,.\end{split}

(A.60)

Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
A.60
, defining the operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{(t)}
as

{\mathcal{T}}_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{(t)}={\mathcal{T}}_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}e^{tg({\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{M-1},{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}_{M})}\,,

(A.61)

for

big enough, (
A.60
) is asymptotic to

\partial_{t}\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}\sim M\mathrm{Tr}\left(\partial_{t}({\mathcal{T}}_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{(t)})_{|_{t=0}}{\mathcal{T}}_{{\boldsymbol{\beta}}}^{M-2}\right)\,.

(A.62)

Following the same reasoning as in the previous proof, in view of the analyticity of
\lambda_{1}(V,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})
, we deduce that the previous equation is asymptotic to

\partial_{t}\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}\sim M\lambda_{1}(V,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})^{M-2}\partial_{t}\lambda_{1}(V+t/2\Re(z^{m}),{\boldsymbol{\beta}})_{|_{t=0}}\,.

(A.63)

Exploiting (
A.12
)-(
A.14
) and (
A.63
), we can rewrite (
A.58
) as:

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\partial_{t}\left(Z_{N}^{(AL)}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}}{NZ_{N}^{(AL)}(V,\beta)}\\
&\qquad=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{M\lambda_{1}(V,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})^{M-2}\partial_{t}\lambda_{1}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\right)_{|_{t=0}}}{N\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\lambda^{M-1}_{\ell}(V,\beta)}\\
&\qquad=\frac{\partial_{t}\lambda_{1}\left(\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}}{K\lambda_{1}(V,\beta)}\\
&\qquad=-\partial_{t}\left(F_{AL}\left(V+\frac{t}{2}\Re(z^{m}),\beta\right)\right)_{|_{t=0}}\,.\end{split}

(A.64)

Thus, we have completed the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of lemma
5.5

We prove (
5.25
) for
k=1
and the cases
k>1
easily follow. For convenience we consider the more general case of
(\lambda,\eta,\beta)\in{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\times\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}|\Re\,z>0\}
.

Let us define
R_{1}^{(s)}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{s}&h_{s}\\
p_{s}&q_{s}\end{pmatrix}
where
s\geq 1
. If follows from (
5.24
) that

\begin{pmatrix}f_{s}&h_{s}\\
p_{s}&q_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}f_{s-1}&h_{s-1}\\
p_{s-1}&q_{s-1}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1+\frac{\lambda\beta\eta}{s(s+\beta)}&\frac{\eta^{2}}{s(s+\beta+1)}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad s>1.

(B.1)

Note that in the case
\eta=0
the lemma is trivially satisfied.
We will show that all the sequences
\{f_{s},h_{s},p_{s},q_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
converge as
s\to\infty
, moreover
h_{s},q_{s}\xrightarrow{s\to\infty}0
.
First of all, we notice that
h_{s}=\frac{\eta^{2}f_{s-1}}{s(s+\beta+1)}
and
q_{s}=\frac{\eta^{2}p_{s-1}}{s(s+\beta+1)}
, thus the convergence to zero of these two sequences follows from the convergence of
p_{s}
and
f_{s}
as
s\to\infty
. Moreover, the terms of the sequences
\{f_{s},p_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
obey to the 3-terms recurrence:

f_{s}=\left(1+\frac{\lambda\beta\eta}{s(s+\beta)}\right)f_{s-1}+\frac{\eta^{2}}{(s-1)(s+\beta)}f_{s-2}\,,

(B.2)

and the same holds for
p_{s}
in place of
f_{s}
. Thus, we have just to prove that the sequence
\{f_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
converges.
We assume that
(\lambda,\eta,\beta)\in\Omega
where
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathbb{C}}\times\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}|\Re\,z>0\}
is a compact set. With this assumption we can give a bound to
|f_{s}|
from above as:

|f_{s}|\leq\left(1+\frac{2\eta^{2}+|\lambda\beta\eta|}{s(s+\beta)}\right)\max\left(|f_{s-1}|,|f_{s-2}|\right)\,.

(B.3)

Inductively, we deduce that there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega)
such that:

|f_{s}|\leq C\prod_{\ell=1}^{s}\left(1+\frac{2\eta^{2}+|\lambda\beta\eta|}{\ell(\ell+\beta)}\right)\leq C\prod_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\left(1+\frac{2\eta^{2}+|\lambda\beta\eta|}{\ell(\ell+\beta)}\right)\,.

(B.4)

Furthermore, the infinite product on the right-hand side of (
B.4
) is convergent by a classical result, see for example
[
46
, Chapter XIII, Lemma 1]
, this implies that the sequence
\{f_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
is uniformly bounded. Moreover, we have that:

|f_{s+1}-f_{s}|\leq\frac{|f_{s}\lambda\beta\eta|}{(s+1)(s+1+\beta)}+\frac{\eta^{2}|f_{s-1}|}{s(s-1+\beta)}\leq{\widetilde{C}}\frac{\eta^{2}+|\lambda\beta\eta|}{s(s-1+\beta)}\,,

(B.5)

for some constant
\tilde{C}>0
that depends on the compact set
\Omega
.
This last equation implies that the sequence
\{f_{s}\}_{s\geq 1}
is a Cauchy sequence, thus it is convergent. So we get the claim (
5.24
). The claim (
5.26
) easily follows from
(
5.24
).

Regarding the differentiability in the parameters
\lambda
,
\eta
and
\beta
, it follows from (
B.2
) that
f_{s}=f_{s}(\lambda,\eta,\beta)

is analytic in
\Omega
. Since
f_{s}(\lambda,\eta,\beta)\to f(\lambda,\eta,\beta)
as
s\to\infty
uniformly, then by Weierstrasse convergence theorem,
f(\lambda,\eta,\beta)

is analytic in
\Omega
.

∎

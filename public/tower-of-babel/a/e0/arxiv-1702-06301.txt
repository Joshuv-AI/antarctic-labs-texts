Marginals with finite repulsive cost
U. Bindini

Abstract
We consider a multimarginal transport problem with repulsive cost, where the marginals are all equal to a fixed probability ρ ∈ 𝒫 ⁡ ( ℝ d ) \rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) . We prove that, if the concentration of ρ \rho is less than 1 / N 1/N , then the problem has a solution of finite cost. The result is sharp, in the sense that there exists ρ \rho with concentration 1 / N 1/N for which the cost is infinite.

Introduction

Consider a system of
N
unitary-charged particles of negligible mass under the effect of the Coulomb force. We may describe the stationary states using a wave-function
\psi(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})
, where
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
; via the Born interpretation,
\left|\psi(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\right|^{2}
may be viewed as the density of the probability that the particles occupy the positions
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}
, and it is symmetric, since the particles are indistinguishable.

When the semi-classical limit is considered, as already proved in
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
16
]
, the stationary states reach the minimum of potential energy,
i.e.
,

V_{0}=\min_{\psi}V(\psi)=\min\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3N}}c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\left|\psi(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dotsm\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{N},

(1)

where
c
is the Coulomb (potential) cost function
c\colon(\mathbb{R}^{3})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as

c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{1}{\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|}.

This can also be viewed as the exchange correlation functional linking the Kohn-Sham to the Hohenberg-Kohn approach, see for instance
[
13
]
.

Given any wave-function
\psi
, define its single-particle density as

\rho^{\psi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3(N-1)}}\left|\psi(x,x_{2},\dotsc,x_{N})\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2}\dotsm\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{N},

which is quite natural from the physical point of view, since the charge density is a fundamental quantum-mechanical observable.

It is a well-known result by Lieb
[
17
]
(see also Levy
[
15
]
) that the set of all possible marginal densities is

\mathcal{R}=\left\{\rho\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\left|\right.\rho\geq 0,\sqrt{\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(x)dx=1\right\}.

One may thus consider

C(\rho)=\min\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3N}}c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\left|\psi(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\right|^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\dotsm\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{N}\left|\right.\rho^{\psi}=\rho\right\},

and factorize the original minimum problem (
1
) as

V_{0}=\min_{\rho\in\mathcal{R}}\min_{\rho^{\psi}=\rho}V(\psi)=\min_{\rho}C(\rho).

This is a well known approach, which dates back to Thomas and Fermi, and was later revised by Hohenberg and Kohn
[
14
]
, Levy
[
15
]
and Lieb
[
17
]
, whose questions are still sources of ideas for this field.

In this paper, firstly we generalize the physical dimension
d=3
to any
d\geq 1
. Moreover, we adopt a measure-theoretic approach: instead of considering wave-functions, we set the problem for every probability over
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
and formulate the corresponding relaxed minimum problem

\mathcal{C}(P)=\min\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}),

where
P\in\mathcal{P}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N})
is a probability measure. In this fashion, the single-particle density constraint gives rise to a multi-marginal optimal transport problem of the form

\displaystyle\begin{split}C(\rho)=\inf\bigg\{&\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\colon\\
&P\in\mathcal{P}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}),\ \pi_{\#}^{i}P=\rho,i=1,\dotsc,N\bigg\},\end{split}

(2)

where
\rho
is a fixed probability measure over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\pi^{i}
is the projection over the
i
-th factor of
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
. It is a simple and well known observation that the infimum (
2
) is equal to

\displaystyle\begin{split}C(\rho)=\inf\bigg\{&\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}}c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\colon\\
&P\in\mathcal{P}((\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}),P\text{ symmetric },\pi_{\#}^{i}P=\rho,i=1,\dotsc,N\bigg\}.\end{split}

(3)

In order to give even a stronger result, we take as a cost function a general repulsive potential, as in the following

Definition 1
.

A function
c\colon(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
repulsive cost function
if it is of the form

c(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|)}

where
\omega\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is continuous, strictly increasing, differentiable on
(0,+\infty)
, with
\omega(0)=0
.

Although there are many works about this formulation, and the multi-marginal transport problem in general (see for instance
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
]
), none of them gives a condition on
\rho
which assures that the infimum in (
3
) is finite. We found that the correct quantity to consider is the one given by the following

Definition 2
.

If
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
concentration
of
\rho
is

\mu(\rho)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\left\{x\right\}).

This allows us to state the main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
c
be a repulsive cost function, and
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

\mu(\rho)<\frac{1}{N}.

(4)

Then the infimum in (
3
) is finite.

After this paper was already submitted, the author became aware of an independent work in preparation by F. Stra, S. Di Marino and M. Colombo about the same problem. The techniques are different and the second result, although not yet available in preprint form, seems to be closer in the approach to some arguments in
[
3
]
.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
we give some notation, and regroup some definitions, constructions and results to be used later. In particular, we state and prove a simple but useful result about partitioning
\mathbb{R}^{d}
into measurable sets with prescribed mass.

We then show in Section
3
that the condition (
4
) is sharp,
i.e.
, given any repulsive cost function, there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mu(\rho)=1/N
, and
C(\rho)=\infty
. The construction of this counterexample is explicit, but it is important to note that the marginal
\rho
depends on the given cost function.

Finally we devote Sections
4
to
6
to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The construction is universal, in the following sense: given
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that (
4
) holds, we exhibit a symmetric transport plan
P
which has support outside the region

D_{\alpha}=\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\left|\right.\exists i\neq j\text{ with }\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|<\alpha\right\}

for some
\alpha>0
. This implies that
C(P)
is finite for any repulsive cost function.

2.1
Partitions of non-atomic measures

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a finite non-atomic measure, and
b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k}
real positive numbers such that
b_{1}+\dotsb+b_{k}=\left|\sigma\right|
. We may want to write

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}E_{j},

where the
E_{j}
’s are disjoint measurable sets with
\sigma(E_{j})=b_{j}
. This is trivial if
d=1
, since the cumulative distribution function
\phi_{\sigma}(t)=\sigma((-\infty,t))
is continuous, and one may find the
E_{j}
’s as intervals. However, in higher dimension, the measure
\sigma
might concentrate over
(d-1)
-dimensional surfaces, which makes the problem slightly more difficult. Therefore we present the following

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a finite non-atomic measure. Then there exists a direction
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
such that
\sigma(H)=0
for all the affine hyperplanes
H
such that
H\perp y
.

In order to prove Proposition
2.2
, it is useful to present the following

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(X,\mu)
be a measure space, with
\mu(X)<\infty
, and
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a collection of measurable sets such that

1.

\mu(E_{i})>0
for every
i\in I
;

2.

\mu(E_{i}\cap E_{j})=0
for every
i\neq j
.

Then
I
is countable.

Proof.

Let
i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n}
be a finite set of indices. Then using the monotonicity of
\mu
and the fact that
\mu(E_{i}\cap E_{j})=0
if
i\neq j
,

\mu(X)\geq\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}E_{i_{k}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu(E_{i_{k}}).

Hence we have that

\sup\left\{\sum_{j\in J}\mu(E_{j})\left|\right.J\subset I,J\text{ finite}\right\}\leq\mu(X)<\infty.

Since all the
\mu(E_{i})
are strictly positive numbers, this is possible only if
I
is countable.
∎

Now we present the proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Proof.

For

we recall the definitions of the Grassmannian

\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{R}^{d})=\left\{v\text{ linear subspace of }\mathbb{R}^{d}\left|\right.\dim v=k\right\}

and the affine Grassmannian

Given

the unique element of
\mathrm{Gr}(k,\mathbb{R}^{d})

. If

, we say that

is

For every

The goal is to prove that

The following key Lemma leads to the proof in a finite number of steps:

∎

3
The condition (
4
) is sharp

In this section we prove that the condition (
4
) is the best possible,
i.e.
, given any repulsive cost function there exists
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mu(\rho)=1/N
such that
C(\rho)=\infty
.

Fix
\omega
as in Definition
1
, and set

k=\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(\left|y\right|)}{\left|y\right|^{d-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Note that
k
is a positive finite constant, depending only on
\omega
and the dimension
d
. In fact, integrating in spherical coordinates,

k=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{r^{d-1}}\alpha_{d}r^{d-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=\alpha_{d}\omega(1),

where
\alpha_{d}
is the
d
-dimensional volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Now define a probability measure
\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho:=\frac{1}{N}f(0)+\frac{N-1}{N}\int_{B(0,1)}f(x)\frac{\omega^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)}{k\left|x\right|^{d-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x\hskip 14.22636pt\forall f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(6)

This measure has an atom of mass
1/N
in the origin, and is absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Hence the concentration of
\rho
is equal to
1/N
, even if for every ball
B
around the origin one has
\rho(B)>1/N
.

We want to prove that any symmetric transport plan with marginals
\rho
has infinite cost. Let us consider, by contradiction, a symmetric plan
P
, with
\pi(P)=\rho
, such that

\int\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x_{i}-x_{j}\right|)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P(X)<\infty

Then one would have the following geometric properties.

Lemma 3.1
.

(i)

P(\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}):\exists i\neq j,x_{i}=x_{j}\right\})=0;

(ii)

P
is concentrated over the
N
coordinate hyperplanes
\left\{x_{j}=0\right\}
,
j=1,\dotsc,N
,
i.e.
,

\supp(P)\subseteq E:=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}\left\{x_{j}=0\right\}.

Proof.

(i) Since
\omega(0)=0
, recalling Definition
1
, the cost function is identically equal to
+\infty
in the region
\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}):\exists i\neq j,x_{i}=x_{j}\right\}
. Therefore, since by assumption the cost of
P
is finite, it must be

P(\left\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{N}):\exists i\neq j,x_{i}=x_{j}\right\})=0.

(ii) Define

\displaystyle p_{1}

\displaystyle=P(\left\{x_{1}=0\right\})

\displaystyle p_{2}

\displaystyle=P(\left\{x_{1}=0\right\}\cap\left\{x_{2}=0\right\})

\displaystyle p_{N}

\displaystyle=P((0,\dotsc,0)).

Note that
p_{1}=P(\left\{x_{1}=0\right\})=\pi(P)(\left\{0\right\})=\rho(\left\{0\right\})=1/N
. We claim that
p_{2}=\dotsb=p_{N}=0
. It suffices to prove that
p_{2}=0
, since by monotonicity of the measure
P
we have
p_{j}\geq p_{j+1}
. Since
P
has finite cost,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{Nd}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P}{\omega(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|)}

must be finite. However,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{Nd}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P}{\omega(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|)}\geq\int_{\left\{x_{1}=0\right\}\cap\left\{x_{2}=0\right\}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P}{\omega(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|)}=p_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\frac{\delta_{0}(x_{1})\delta_{0}(x_{2})}{\omega(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{2},

and hence
p_{2}
must be zero.

By inclusion-exclusion we have

P(E)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-1)^{j+1}\binom{N}{j}p_{j}=Np_{1}=1,

and hence
P
is concentrated over
E
.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.1
, letting
H_{j}=\{x_{j}=0\}
for

,

For every

\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)d}
such that, recalling equation (

, with

. Since
P
is symmetric, considering a permutation

with

, it follows that

is symmetric; then, considering any permutation in
S_{N}
we see that there exists a symmetric probability

over
\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)d}
such that
Q_{j}=\frac{1}{N}Q
for every

,
i.e.
,

P=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Q\otimes_{j}\delta_{0}.

Projecting

\pi(Q)
is absolutely continuous w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure, with

\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(Q)}{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{d}}=\frac{\chi_{B(0,1)}(x)\omega^{\prime}(x)}{k\left|x\right|^{d-1}}.

Here we get the contradiction, because

\displaystyle\int c(X)dP(X)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{N}\int\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|)}\delta_{0}(x_{1})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x_{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q(x_{2},\dotsc,x_{N})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x_{2}\right|)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}Q(x_{2},\dotsc,x_{N})=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x\right|)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\pi(Q)(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(\left|x\right|)}{\omega(\left|x\right|)}\frac{1}{k\left|x\right|^{d-1}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{N}\frac{\alpha_{d}}{k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\omega^{\prime}(r)}{\omega(r)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}r=+\infty.

4
Non-atomic marginals

This short section deals with the case where
\rho
is non atomic,
i.e.
,
\mu(\rho)=0
. In this case the transport plan is given by an optimal transport map in Monge’s fashion, which we proceed to construct.

Using Corollary
2.5
, let
E_{1},\dotsc,E_{2N}
be a partition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\rho(E_{j})=\frac{1}{2N}\hskip 14.22636pt\forall j=1,\dotsc,2N.

Next we take a measurable function
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, preserving the measure
\rho
and defined locally such that

\displaystyle\phi(E_{j})

\displaystyle=E_{j+2}\hskip 14.22636pt\forall j=1,\dotsc,N-2

\displaystyle\phi(E_{2N-1})

\displaystyle=E_{1}

\displaystyle\phi(E_{2N})

\displaystyle=E_{2}.

The behaviour of
\phi
on the hyperplanes which separate the
E_{j}
’s is arbitrary, since they form a
\rho
-null set. Note that
\left|x-\phi(x)\right|
is uniformy bounded from below by some constant
\gamma>0
, as is clear by the construction of the
E_{j}
’s (see the proof of Corollary
2.5
). A transport plan
P
of finite cost is now defined for every
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{Nd})
by

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{Nd}}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{Nd}}f(x,\phi(x),\dotsc,\phi^{N-1}(x))\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(x),

since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{Nd}}c\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}P=\binom{N}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{\omega(\left|x-\phi(x)\right|)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\rho(x)\leq\binom{N}{2}\frac{1}{\omega(\gamma)}.

5
Marginals with a finite number of atoms

This section constitutes the core of the proof, as we deal with measures of general form with an arbitrary (but finite) number of atoms. Throughout this and the next Section we assume that the marginal
\rho
fulfills the condition (
4
).

5.1
The number of atoms is less than or equal to
N

Note that, if the number of atoms is at most
N
, then
\rho
must have a non-atomic part
\sigma
, due to the condition (
4
). From here on we consider

\rho=\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},

where
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}>0
.

We begin with the following

Definition 3
.

A
partition
of
\sigma
of level
k\leq N
subordinate to
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};

b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
is

\sigma=\tau+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{h=i+1}^{N}\sigma^{i}_{h},

where:

(i)

\tau,\sigma^{i}_{h}
are non-atomic measures;

(ii)

for every
i
and every
h\neq k
, the distance between
\supp\sigma^{i}_{h}
and
\supp\sigma^{i}_{k}
is strictly positive;

(iii)

for every
i,h
, if
j\leq i
then
x_{j}
has a strictly positive distance from
\supp{\sigma^{i}_{h}}
;

(iv)

for every
i,h
,
\left|\sigma^{i}_{h}\right|=b_{i}
, and
\left|\tau\right|>0
.

Note that such a partition may only exists if

\left|\sigma\right|>\sum_{i=1}^{k}(N-i)b_{i}.

(7)

On the other hand, the following Lemma proves that the condition (
7
) is also sufficient to get a partition of
\sigma
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
with
k\leq N
, and

\left|\sigma\right|>\sum_{i=1}^{k}(N-i)b_{i}.

Then there exists a partition of
\sigma
subordinate to
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
.

Proof.

Fix
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})
and for every
\varepsilon>0
define

A_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}B(x_{j},\varepsilon).

and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\sigma\chi_{A_{\varepsilon}}
. Then take
\varepsilon
small enough such that

\left|\sigma-\sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|>\sum_{i=1}^{k}(N-i)b_{i},

(8)

which is possibile because
\mu(\sigma)=0
(
\sigma
has concentration zero), and hence
\left|\sigma_{\varepsilon}\right|\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Due to Corollary
2.6
, the set
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus A_{\varepsilon}
may be partitioned as

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus A_{\varepsilon}=\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\bigcup_{h=i+1}^{N}E^{i}_{h}\right)\cup E,

with
\sigma(E^{i}_{h})=b_{i}
, and
\mathrm{dist}(E^{i}_{h},E^{i}_{k})
is uniformly bounded from below.

Finally define
\sigma^{i}_{h}=\sigma\chi_{E^{i}_{h}}
,
\tau=\sigma_{\varepsilon}+\sigma\chi_{E}
.

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
k\leq N
and
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
are such that

\left|\sigma\right|>Nb_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}.

(9)

Then there exists a transport plan of finite cost with marginals

\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}.

Proof.

In order to simplify the notation, set
b_{k+1}=0
. First of all we shall fix a partition of
\sigma
subordinate to
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};b_{1}-b_{2},\dotsc,b_{k-1}-b_{k},b_{k})
. To do this we apply Lemma
7
, since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}(N-i)(b_{i}-b_{i+1})+(N-k)b_{k}

\displaystyle=(N-1)b_{1}-\sum_{i=2}^{k}b_{i}<\left|\sigma\right|.

Next we define the measures
\lambda_{i}=\delta_{x_{1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{i}}\otimes\sigma^{i}_{i+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\sigma^{i}_{N}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{Nd})
. Let us calculate the marginals of
\lambda_{i}
: since
\left|\sigma^{i}_{h}\right|=b_{i}-b_{i+1}
for all
h=i+1,\dotsc,N
, we get

\pi^{j}_{\#}\lambda_{i}=\begin{cases}(b_{i}-b_{i+1})^{N-i}\delta_{x_{j}}&\text{if $0\leq j\leq i$}\\
(b_{i}-b_{i+1})^{N-i-1}\sigma^{i}_{j}&\text{if $i+1\leq j\leq N$.}\end{cases}

Let us define, for
i=1,\dotsc,k
, the measure

P_{i}=\frac{N}{(b_{i}-b_{i+1})^{N-i-1}}(\lambda_{i})_{sym},

where
P_{i}=0
if
b_{i}=b_{i+1}
. By Lemma
2.1
, the marginals of
P_{i}
are equal to

\pi(P_{i})=\frac{1}{(b_{i}-b_{i+1})^{N-i-1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\pi^{j}_{\#}\lambda_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}(b_{i}-b_{i+1})\delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{h=i+1}^{N}\sigma^{i}_{h},

so that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\pi(P_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{h=i+1}^{N}\sigma^{i}_{h}

It suffices now to take any symmetric transport plan
P_{\tau}
of finite cost with marginals
\tau
, given by the result of Section
4
, and finally set

P=P_{\tau}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{i}.

∎

As a corollary we obtain

Theorem 5.3
.

If
\rho
has
k\leq N
atoms, then there exists a transport plan of finite cost.

Proof.

Let

\rho=\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}.

Note that, since
b_{1}<1/N
,

\left|\sigma\right|=1-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}>Nb_{1}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j},

hence we may apply Proposition
5.2
to conclude.

∎

5.2
The number of atoms is greater than
N

Here we deal with the much more difficult situation in which
\rho
has
N+1
or more atoms,
i.e.
,

\rho=\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}

with
k\geq N+1
and as before
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}>0
. Note that in this case it might happen that
\sigma=0
.

The main point is to use a double induction on the dimension
N
and the number of atoms
k
, as will be clear in Proposition
5.5
. The following lemma is a simple numerical trick needed for the inductive step in Proposition
5.5
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
with
k\geq N+2
and

(N-1)b_{1}\leq\sum_{j=2}^{k}b_{j}.

(10)

Then there exist
t_{2},\dotsc,t_{k}
such that

(i)

t_{2}+\dotsb+t_{k}=(N-1)b_{1}
;

(ii)

for every
j=2,\dotsc,k
,
0\leq t_{j}\leq b_{j}
, and moreover

t_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq t_{k}.

b_{2}-t_{2}\geq b_{3}-t_{3}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}-t_{k},

(iii)

(N-2)t_{2}\leq\sum_{j=3}^{k}t_{j};

(iv)

(N-1)(b_{2}-t_{2})\leq\sum_{j=3}^{k}(b_{j}-t_{j}).

Proof.

For
j=2,\dotsc,k
define

p_{j}=\sum_{h=j}^{k}b_{j},

and let
\bar{\jmath}
be the least
j\geq 2
such that
(N-j+2)b_{j}\leq p_{j}
; note that
j=N+2
works — hence
\bar{\jmath}\leq N+2
. Define

\displaystyle t_{j}

\displaystyle=b_{j}-\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}

for
j=2,\dotsc,\bar{\jmath}-1
,

\displaystyle t_{j}

\displaystyle=b_{j}-\frac{b_{j}}{p_{\bar{\jmath}}}\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)

for
j=\bar{\jmath},\dotsc,k
.

Next we prove that this choice fulfills the conditions (i)-(iv).

Proof of (i)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}

\displaystyle=p_{2}-\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}(\bar{\jmath}-2)-\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)

\displaystyle=p_{2}\left(1-\frac{\bar{\jmath}-2}{N}-\frac{N-\bar{\jmath}+2}{N}\right)+(N-1)b_{1}\left(\frac{\bar{\jmath}-2}{N}+\frac{N-\bar{\jmath}+2}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=(N-1)b_{1}.

Proof of (ii)

In view of the fact that
(N-1)b_{1}\leq p_{2}
and
\bar{\jmath}\leq N+2
, it is clear that
t_{j}\leq b_{j}
. If
j<\bar{\jmath}
we have
(N-j+2)b_{j}>p_{j}
, and hence

p_{2}=b_{2}+\dotsb+b_{j-1}+p_{j}<(j-2)b_{1}+(N-j+2)b_{j}.

Thus, since
2\leq j\leq N+1
,

\displaystyle t_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{Nb_{j}-p_{2}+(N-1)b_{1}}{N}>\frac{Nb_{j}-(N-j+2)b_{j}-(j-2)b_{1}+(N-1)b_{1}}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{(j-2)b_{j}+(N-j+1)b_{1}}{N}\geq 0.

To show that
t_{j}\geq 0
for
j\geq\bar{\jmath}
, we must prove
[p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}](N-\bar{\jmath}+2)\leq Np_{\bar{\jmath}}
, which is trivial if
\bar{\jmath}=N-2
. Otherwise, it is equivalent to

-(\bar{\jmath}-2)[p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}]+N[b_{2}+\dotsb+b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-(N-1)b_{1}]\leq 0.

Since
2\leq\bar{\jmath}\leq N+1
, the first term is negative and
b_{2}+\dotsb+b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-(N-1)b_{1}\leq-(N-\bar{\jmath}+1)b_{1}\leq 0
.

Using the fact that
b_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}
, it is easy to see that
t_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq\dotsb t_{\bar{\jmath}-1}
and
t_{\bar{\jmath}}\geq\dotsb\geq t_{k}
— note that for
j\geq\bar{\jmath}
we have
t_{j}=\alpha b_{j}
, for some
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
. As for the remaining inequality,

t_{\bar{\jmath}-1}\geq t_{\bar{\jmath}}\iff b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-b_{\bar{\jmath}}\geq\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{Np_{\bar{\jmath}}}[p_{\bar{\jmath}}-(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)b_{\bar{\jmath}}],

we already proved

\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{Np_{\bar{\jmath}}}\leq\frac{1}{N-\bar{\jmath}+2};

moreover, by definition of
\bar{\jmath}
, we have
(N-\bar{\jmath}+3)b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}>p_{\bar{\jmath}-1}
, or equivalently
(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}>p_{\bar{\jmath}}
. Thus

\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{Np_{\bar{\jmath}}}[p_{\bar{\jmath}}-(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)b_{\bar{\jmath}}]\leq\frac{p_{\bar{\jmath}}}{N-\bar{\jmath}+2}-b_{\bar{\jmath}}<b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-b_{\bar{\jmath}},

as wanted.

It is left to show that
b_{2}-t_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}-t_{k}
. It is trivial to check that
b_{2}-t_{2}=\dotsb=b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-t_{\bar{\jmath}-1}
, and
b_{\bar{\jmath}}-t_{\bar{\jmath}}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}-t_{k}
using
b_{\bar{\jmath}}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{k}
as before. Finally,

b_{\bar{\jmath}-1}-t_{\bar{\jmath}-1}\geq b_{\bar{\jmath}}-t_{\bar{\jmath}}\iff\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}\geq\frac{b_{\bar{\jmath}}}{p_{\bar{\jmath}}}\frac{p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}}{N}(N-\bar{\jmath}+2),

which is true since
(N-\bar{\jmath}+2)b_{\bar{\jmath}}\leq p_{\bar{\jmath}}
and
p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1}\geq 0
.

Proof of (iii)

The thesis is equivalent to

(N-1)t_{2}\leq\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}\iff(N-1)t_{2}\leq(N-1)b_{1},

and this is implied by
t_{2}\leq b_{2}\leq b_{1}
.

Proof of (iv)

The thesis is equivalent to

N(b_{2}-t_{2})\leq p_{2}-(N-1)b_{1},

which is in fact an equality (see the definition of
t_{2}
).

∎

We are ready to present the main result of this Section, which provides a transport plan of finite cost under an additional hypothesis on the tuple
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
. The result is peculiar for the fact that it does not involve the non-atomic part of the measure – it is in fact a general discrete construction to get a purely atomic symmetric measure having fixed purely atomic marginals.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
k>N
and
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
with

(N-1)b_{1}\leq b_{2}+\dotsb+b_{k}.

(11)

Then for every
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
distinct, there exists a symmetric transport plan of finite cost with marginals
\rho=b_{1}\delta_{x_{1}}+\dotsb+b_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}
.

Proof.

For every pair of positive integers
(N,k)
, with
k>N
, let
\mathfrak{P}(N,k)
be the following proposition:

Let
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k};b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
with
(N-1)b_{1}\leq b_{2}+\dotsb+b_{k}
. Then for every
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{k})
there exists a symmetric
N
-transport plan of finite cost with marginals
b_{1}\delta_{x_{1}}+\dotsb+b_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}
.

We will prove
\mathfrak{P}(N,k)
by double induction, in the following way: first we prove
\mathfrak{P}(1,k)
for every
k
and
\mathfrak{P}(N,N+1)
for every
N
. Then we prove

\mathfrak{P}(N-1,k)\wedge\mathfrak{P}(N,k-1)\implies\mathfrak{P}(N,k).

Proof of
\mathfrak{P}(1,k)

This is trivial: simply take
b_{1}\delta_{x_{1}}+\dotsb+b_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}
as a “transport plan”.

Proof of
\mathfrak{P}(N,N+1)

Let us denote by
A_{N}
the
(N+1)\times(N+1)
matrix

A_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&\cdots&1\\
1&0&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
1&\cdots&1&0\end{pmatrix},

whose inverse is

A_{N}^{-1}=\frac{1}{N}\begin{pmatrix}-(N-1)&1&\cdots&1\\
1&-(N-1)&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
1&\cdots&1&-(N-1)\end{pmatrix}

Define also the following
(N+1)\times N
matrix, with elements in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(x_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}x_{2}&x_{3}&\cdots&x_{N+1}\\
x_{1}&x_{3}&\cdots&x_{N+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&\cdots&x_{N}\end{pmatrix},

where the
i
-th row is
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\dotsc,x_{N+1})
.
We want to construct a transport plan of the form

P=N\sum_{i=1}^{N+1}a_{i}(\delta_{x_{i1}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{iN}})_{sym},

where
a_{i}\geq 0
. Note that, by Lemma
2.1
, the marginals of
P
are equal to

\pi(P)=\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N+1}a_{i}\right)\delta_{x_{j}}.

Thus, the condition on the
a_{i}
’s to have
\pi(P)=\rho
is

A_{N}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{N+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{N+1}\end{pmatrix},

i.e.
,

\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}\\
\vdots\\
a_{N+1}\end{pmatrix}=A_{N}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}\\
\vdots\\
b_{N+1}\end{pmatrix}.

Finally, observe that the condition (
10
) implies that
a_{1}\geq 0
, while the fact that
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\dotsb\geq b_{N+1}
leads to
a_{1}\leq a_{2}\leq\dotsb\leq a_{N+1}
, and hence
a_{i}\geq 0
for every
i
and we are done.

Inductive step

Let
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
satisfying (
10
), with
k\geq N+2
(otherwise we are in the case
\mathfrak{P}(N,N+1)
, already proved). Take
t_{2},\dotsc,t_{k}
given by Lemma
5.4
, and apply the inductive hypotheses to find

•

a symmetric transport plan
Q_{1}
of finite cost in
(N-1)
variables, with marginals

\pi(Q_{1})=\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}\delta_{x_{j}};

•

a symmetric transport plan
R
of finite cost in
N
variables, with marginals

\pi(R)=\sum_{j=2}^{k}(b_{j}-t_{j})\delta_{x_{j}}.

Define

Q=\frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{j=1}^{N}(Q_{1}\otimes_{j}\delta_{x_{1}}).

Since
Q_{1}
is symmetric,
Q
is symmetric. Moreover, using Lemma
5.4
(i),

\pi(Q)=\frac{1}{N-1}\delta_{x_{1}}\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}=b_{1}\delta_{x_{1}}+\sum_{j=2}^{k}t_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}.

The transport plan
P=Q+R
is symmetric, with marginals
\pi(P)=b_{1}\delta_{x_{1}}+\dotsb+b_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}
.

∎

In order to conclude the proof of this Section, we must now deal not only with the non-atomic part of
\rho
, but also with the additional hypothesis of Proposition
5.5
. Indeed, the presence of a non-atomic part will fix the atomic mass exceeding the inequality (
11
), as will be seen soon.

Definition 4
.

Given
N
, we say that the tuple
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell})
is
fast decreasing
if

(N-j)b_{j}>\sum_{i>j}b_{i}\hskip 14.22636pt\forall j=1,\dotsc,\ell-1.

Remark 1
.

Note that if
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell})
is fast decreasing, then necessarily
\ell<N
. As a consequence, given any sequence
(b_{1},b_{2},\dotsc)
, even infinite, we may select its maximal fast decreasing initial tuple
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell})
(which might be empty,
i.e.
,
\ell=0
).

Theorem 5.6
.

If
\rho
is such that

\rho=\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}

with
k>N
atoms, then there exists a transport plan of finite cost.

Proof.

Consider
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{k})
and use the Remark
1
to select its maximal fast decreasing initial tuple
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell})
,
\ell<N
. Thanks to Proposition
5.5
, we may construct a transport plan
P_{\ell+1}
over
\mathbb{R}^{(N-\ell)d}
with marginals
b_{\ell+1}\delta_{x_{\ell+1}}+\dotsb+b_{k}\delta_{x_{k}}
, since

(N-\ell-1)b_{\ell+1}\leq\sum_{j=\ell+2}^{k}b_{j}

by maximality of
(b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell})
— and this is condition (
10
) in this case. We extend step by step
P_{\ell+1}
to an
N
-transport plan, letting

P_{j}=\frac{1}{N-j}\sum_{i=j}^{N}(P_{j+1}\otimes_{i}\delta_{x_{j}}),

for
j=\ell,\ell-1,\dotsc,1
.

Let
p_{\ell}=b_{\ell+1}+\dotsb+b_{k}
, and
q_{\ell}=\tfrac{p_{\ell}}{N-\ell}
. We claim that
\left|P_{j}\right|=(N-j+1)q_{\ell}
. In fact, by construction
\left|P_{\ell+1}\right|=p_{\ell}
, and inductively

\left|P_{j}\right|=\frac{1}{N-j}\sum_{i=j-1}^{N}\left|P_{j+1}\right|=\frac{N-j+1}{N-j}(N-j)q_{\ell}=(N-j+1)q_{\ell}.

Moreover,

\pi(P_{j})=\sum_{i=j}^{k}q_{\ell}\delta_{x_{i}}+\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

This is true by construction in the case
j=\ell+1
, and inductively

\pi(P_{j})=\frac{1}{N-j}\delta_{x_{j}}\left|P_{j+1}\right|+\frac{N-j}{N-j}\pi(P_{j+1})=\sum_{i=j}^{\ell}q_{\ell}\delta_{x_{i}}+\sum_{i=\ell+1}^{k}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

Note that, for every
i=1,\dotsc,\ell
,
b_{i}\geq b_{\ell}>q_{\ell}
. We shall find, using Proposition
5.2
, a transport plan of finite cost with marginals

\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(b_{i}-q_{\ell})\delta_{x_{i}},

since the condition (
9
) reads

N(b_{1}-q_{\ell})-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(b_{i}-q_{\ell})=Nb_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}b_{i}-(N-\ell)q_{\ell}<1-\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}=\left|\sigma\right|.

∎

6
Marginals with countably many atoms

In this Section we finally deal with the case of an infinite number of atoms,
i.e.
,

\rho=\sigma+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}

with
b_{j}>0
,
b_{j+1}\leq b_{j}
for every
j\geq 1
.

The main issue is of topological nature: if the atoms
x_{j}
are too close each other (for example, if they form a dense subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and the growth of
b_{j}
for
j\to\infty
is too slow, the cost might diverge. With this in mind, we begin with an elementary topological result, in order to separate the atoms in
N
groups, with controlled minimal distance from each other.

Lemma 6.1
.

There exists a partition
\mathbb{R}^{d}=E_{2}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup E_{N+1}
such that:

(i)

for every
j=2,\dotsc,N+1
,
x_{j}\in\mathring{E}_{j}
;

(ii)

for every
j=2,\dotsc,N+1
,
\partial E_{j}
does not contain any
x_{i}
.

Proof.

For
j=3,\dotsc,N+1
let
r_{j}>0
small enough such that

x_{i}\notin B(x_{j},r_{j})\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for every $i=1,\dotsc,N$, $i\neq j$.}

Fixed any
j=3,\dotsc,N+1
, by a cardinality argument there must be a positive real
t_{j}
with
0<t_{j}<r_{j}
and
\partial B(x_{j},t_{j})
not containing any
x_{i}
,
i\geq 1
. We take
E_{j}=B(x_{j},t_{j})
for
j=3,\dotsc,N+1
. Note that this choice fullfills the conditions (i), (ii) for
j=3,\dotsc,N+1
. Finally, we take

E_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left(\bigcup_{j=3}^{N+1}E_{j}\right)

Clearly
x_{2}\in\mathring{E_{2}}
, and moreover the condition (ii) is satisfied, since

\partial E_{2}=\bigcup_{j=3}^{N+1}\partial E_{j}.

∎

Consider the partition given by Lemma
6.1
, and define the corresponding partition of
\mathbb{N}
given by
\mathbb{N}=A_{2}\cup\dotsb\cup A_{N+1}
, where

A_{j}=\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}\left|\right.x_{i}\in E_{j}\right\}.

Next we consider, for every
j=2,\dotsc,{N+1}
a threshold
n_{j}\geq 2
large enough such that, defining

\epsilon_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq n_{j}\\
i\in A_{j}\end{subarray}}b_{i},

then

\epsilon_{2}+\dotsb+\epsilon_{N+1}<\min\left\{b_{N+1},\frac{1}{N}-b_{1}\right\}.

(12)

This may be done since the series
\sum b_{i}
converges, and hence for every
j=2,\dotsc,{N+1}
the series

\sum_{i\in A_{j}}b_{i}

is convergent.

For every
j=2,\dotsc,N+1
define the following transport plan:

P_{j}=N\left[\left(\sum_{i\in A_{j},i\geq n_{j}}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}\right)\otimes\delta_{x_{2}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\hat{\delta}_{x_{j}}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\delta_{x_{N+1}}\right]_{sym},

and note that, by Lemma
2.1
,

\pi(P_{j})=\epsilon_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=2\\
h\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N+1}\delta_{x_{h}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq n_{j}\\
i\in A_{j}\end{subarray}}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

Then let

P_{\infty}=\sum_{j=2}^{N+1}P_{j},

and observe that

\pi(P_{\infty})=\sum_{j=2}^{N+1}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{N+1}\epsilon_{i}\right)\delta_{x_{j}}+\sum_{j=2}^{N+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq n_{j}\\
i\in A_{j}\end{subarray}}b_{i}\delta_{x_{i}}.

Let now

\tilde{b}_{i}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle b_{i}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}h=2\\
h\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N+1}\epsilon_{h}&\text{if $2\leq i\leq N+1$}\\
0&\text{if $i\geq n_{j}$ and $i\in A_{j}$ for some $j=2,\dotsc,N+1$}\\
b_{i}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We are left to find a transport plan of finite cost with marginals

\sigma+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{i}\delta_{x_{i}},

which has indeed a finite number of atoms. Note that
\tilde{b}_{i}\geq 0
for every
i
, thanks to condition (
12
). Moreover, since
\tilde{b}_{1}=b_{1}
and
\tilde{b}_{j}\leq b_{j}
, then
\tilde{b}_{1}\geq\tilde{b}_{j}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, as is used in what follows. If

(N-1)\tilde{b}_{1}\leq\sum_{i=2}^{\infty}\tilde{b}_{i}

we may conclude using Proposition
5.5
. Otherwise, we proceed like in the proof of Theorem
5.6
, with
\{\tilde{b}_{j}\}
replacing
\left\{b_{j}\right\}
. At the final stage, it is left to check that

N(\tilde{b}_{1}-\tilde{q}_{k+1})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\tilde{b}_{i}-\tilde{q}_{k+1})<1-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}=\left|\sigma\right|.

Indeed this is true, since using the condition (
12
) one gets

N(\tilde{b}_{1}-\tilde{q}_{k+1})-\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\tilde{b}_{i}-\tilde{q}_{k+1})=Nb_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}+N(\epsilon_{2}+\dotsc+\epsilon_{N+1})<1-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}b_{i}.

Comparison of different definitions of pseudocharacters
Kathleen Emerson and Sophie Morel

Abstract
. We prove that the definitions of a d d -dimensional pseudocharacter (or pseudorepresentation) given by Chenevier and V. Lafforgue agree over any ring. We also compare the scheme of Lafforgue’s G G -pseudocharacters of a group with its G G -character variety.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary
14M35, 13A50; Secondary
14L24, 14R20

Pseudocharacters (also known as pseudorepresentations) were first
introduced by Wiles (
[
19
]
) and Taylor (
[
17
]
) to
study congruences modulo power of prime numbers
between representations of Galois groups.
They defined congruence between representations as congruence between
their characters, and this led them to the introduction of
pseudocharacters, which are functions on a group whose properties
mimick those of the character of a finite-dimensional representation.
More precisely, let
\Gamma
be a group,
A
be a commutative unital
ring and
d
be a positive integer. A map
T:\Gamma\rightarrow A
is a

d
-dimensional pseudocharacter
if
d!\in A^{\times}
,

T(1)=d
,
T(\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2})=T(\gamma_{2}\gamma_{1})
for all
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Gamma
and
T

satisfies the
d
-dimensional
pseudo-character identity
:
for all
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1}\in\Gamma
,

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d+1}}\sgn(\sigma)T^{\sigma}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1})=0,

where, if the decomposition into cycles of
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{d+1}

(including trivial cycles) is

\sigma=(i_{1,1}i_{1,2}\ldots i_{1,n_{1}})\ldots(i_{k,1}i_{k,2}\ldots i_{k,n_{k}}),

then

T^{\sigma}(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d+1})=T(\gamma_{i_{1,1}}\gamma_{i_{1,2}}\ldots\gamma_{i_{1,n_{1}}})\ldots T(\gamma_{i_{k,1}}\gamma_{i_{k,2}}\ldots\gamma_{i_{k,n_{n}}}).

We denote by
\PChar_{\Gamma,d}(A)
the set of
d
-dimensional pseudo-characters
on
\Gamma
with values in
A
. This is a contravariant functor from
the category of commutative
\mathbb{Z}[1/d!]
-algebras to that of sets,
and it is not hard to see that it is representable, unlike the functor
sending
A
to the set
of equivalence classes of
d
-dimensional
representations
\Gamma\rightarrow{\bf{GL}}_{d}(A)
. Moreover, if
A
is an algebraically
closed field (in which
d!
is invertible), then Taylor has shown
in
[
17
, Theorem 1]
that the trace
induces a bijection from the set of equivalence classes of
d
-dimensional

semi-simple
representations of
\Gamma
over
A
and

\PChar_{\Gamma,d}(A)
and, if

k
is a field of characteristic
0
, then Chenevier has shown in

[
5
, Proposition 2.3]

that
\PChar_{\Gamma,d,\Spec k}
is naturally
isomorphic to the
{\bf{GL}}_{d}
-character variety of
\Gamma
over
\Spec k
.

The original definition of a
d
-dimensional
pseudocharacter does not work well in characteristic
p
dividing
d!
,
because a semi-simple
d
-dimensional representation is no longer determined
by its trace. However, it is determined if we know all the coefficients of
its characteristic polynomial, and Chenevier (
[
4
]
) came up
with a compact way of packaging all that data in his theory of
determinants. Determinants make sense over an arbitrary ring
A
without
the requirement that
d!\in A^{\times}
, and the functor
\Det_{\Gamma,d}
that
they define is representable. Moreover, if
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow{\bf{GL}}_{d}(A)
is a
morphism, then it defines a determinant
D_{\rho}\in\Det_{\Gamma,d}(A)
.
Chenevier shows that, if

k
is an algebraically closed field, then
\rho\mapsto D_{\rho}
is a bijection
from the set of
d
-dimensional semi-simple representations
of
\Gamma
over
k
to
\Det_{\Gamma,k}(k)
.
However, it is not known whether the determinant
functor
\Det_{\Gamma,d}
is isomorphic to the
{\bf{GL}}_{d}
-character variety of

\Gamma
.

Vincent Lafforgue also gave a very general definition of pseudocharacters,
that this time makes sense for representations with values in any
affine group scheme
G
over a base ring
C
.
The definition of his pseudocharacters is
less compact, as it requires data for every invariant function on any
power
G
. Again, we obtain a contravariant
functor
\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
on the category of commutative
C
-algebras,
which turns out to be representable by an affine scheme.
Also, if
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow G(A)
is a morphism, then
it defines a Lafforgue pseudocharacter
\Theta_{\rho}\in\LPC_{\Gamma,G}(A)
.
Lafforgues proves in
[
8
, Proposition 11.7]

(see also
[
9
, Proposition 8.2]
)
that, if
A=k
is an algebraically closed field,
then
\rho\mapsto\Theta_{\rho}
is a bijection
from the set of
G(k)
-conjugacy classes of
G
-completely
reducible morphisms
\Gamma\rightarrow G(k)
to
\LPC_{\Gamma,G}(k)
;
see also Theorem 4.5 of the
paper
[
2
]
of Böckle, Harris, Khare and Thorne
for the details of the proof in positive characteristic, where they also
give the more general definition of a Lafforgue pseudocharacter
(Lafforgue himself was working over a field).

In Section 2, we construct a is morphism of schemes from

\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
to the
G
-character variety of
\Gamma
compatible with
the bijections of the previous paragraph. We prove that, for

G
a geometrically reductive group scheme with connected
geometric fibers over a Dedekind domain or a field, this morphism is
an
adequate homeomorphism
in the sense of Alper
(see
[
1
, Definition 3.3.1]
), that is, an integral
universal homeomorphism which is
a local isomorphism at all points with residue characteristic
0
;
see Proposition
2.11
.

We come back to the case
G={\bf{GL}}_{d}
. The main result
of this note is that, over any ring, Lafforgue pseudocharacters of

\Gamma
are in bijection with
d
-dimensional determinants. More precisely,
we prove the following theorem:

Theorem
.

(See Theorem
4.1
.)
There exists an isomorphism
\alpha:\LPC_{\Gamma,{\bf{GL}}_{d}}\rightarrow\Det_{\Gamma,d}
such
that, for any commutative ring
A
and any morphism
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow{\bf{GL}}_{d}(A)
,

\alpha
sends
\Theta_{\rho}
to
D_{\rho}
.

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section
1
, we recall
Chenevier’s definition of determinants and the properties of determinants
that we will need; in particular, we give Vaccarino’s
formula for the universal determinant ring of a free monoid (see

[
18
]
and
[
5
, Theorem 1.15]
).
In Section
2
, we define Lafforgue pseudocharacters
and give some of their basic properties, in particular the (easy) fact
that they define a representable functor and the relationship with the
character variety (Proposition
2.11
).
We construct the morphism

\alpha
in Section
3
and, in Section
4
, we prove that
this morphism is invertible. The main ingredients are
Donkin’s (
[
6
]
) description of
generators of
\mathcal{O}({\bf{GL}}_{d,\mathbb{Z}}^{n})^{{\bf{GL}}_{d,\mathbb{Z}}}
and the result of Vaccarino
recalled in Section
1
.

1.
Determinants of algebras

In this section, we recall the definition of determinants by
Chenevier (
[
5
]
) and some basic properties of the
functor of determinants. Throughout this section,
A
denotes a commutative
unital ring, and
A
-algebras are always supposed to be associative and
unital.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{A}
be the category of commutative

A
-algebras and
\mathbf{Set}
be the category of sets.
We first recall Roby’s definition of a polynomial law (
[
14
]
, I.2),
a homogeneous polynomial law (
[
14
]
, I.8) and a multiplicative
polynomial law (
[
15
]
, III.4).

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
and
N
be
A
-modules. A
polynomial law

f:M\rightarrow N
is
a morphism of functors from
\mathcal{C}_{A}
to
\mathbf{Set}
between the functors

B\mapsto M\otimes_{A}B
and
B\mapsto N\otimes_{A}B
. For every object

B
of
\mathcal{C}_{A}
, we denote the resulting map
M\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow N\otimes_{A}B

by
f_{B}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
M
and
N
be
A
-modules,
and let
f:M\rightarrow N
be a polynomial law. Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

We say that
f
is
homogeneous of degree
d
if,
for all
B\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
,
x\in M\otimes_{A}B
and
b\in B
, we have

f_{B}(bx)=b^{d}f_{B}(x).

(2)

Suppose that
M
and
N
are
A
-algebras. We say that
f

is
multiplicative
if, for every
B\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, we have
f_{B}(1)=1
and

f_{B}(xy)=f_{B}(x)f_{B}(y)
if
x,y\in M\otimes_{A}B
.

If
M
and
N
are
A
-algebras and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we write

\mathcal{M}_{A}^{d}(M,N)
for the set of multiplicative polynomial laws from
M
to
N

that are homogeneous of degree
d
.

The following definition is due to Chenevier (see
[
5
]
, 1.5).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
R
be an
A
-algebra. An
A
-valued
d
-dimensional determinant
on
R
is an element of
\mathcal{M}_{A}(R,A)
. When
R=A[\Gamma]
for some group

\Gamma
, we also talk about

A
-valued
d
-dimensional determinants on
\Gamma
.

We write
\Det_{R,d}(A)
(resp.
\Det_{\Gamma,d}(A)
) for the set of

A
-valued
d
-dimensional determinants on
R
(resp.
\Gamma
);
so
\Det_{\Gamma,d}(A)=\Det_{A[\Gamma],d}(A)
.

Example 1.4
.

(1)

Let
X
be a set, and write
A\{X\}
for the free unital associative

A
-algebra on

. Any map
\rho:X\rightarrow M_{d}(A)
gives rise to an
A
-valued

on

\mathcal{C}_{A}

\rho

\rho_{B}:B\{X\}\rightarrow M_{d}(B)
, where

is the scheme
of
d\times d
matrices; moreover, if

\textstyle{B\{X\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{B}}
\textstyle{M_{d}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B^{\prime}\{X\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{B^{\prime}}}

(2)

Let

Example 1.5
.

Let
R
be an
A
-algebra and
D\in\mathcal{M}_{A}^{d}(R,A)
. Following
Chenevier (
[
5
]
, 1.10), we can define

\Lambda_{i}:R\rightarrow A

For any commutative

-algebra

Note that

If

Definition 1.6
.

Let
R
be an
A
-algebra and

.
The

\Det_{R,d}:\mathcal{C}_{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
is defined by

\Det_{R,d}(B)=\mathcal{M}_{B}^{d}(R\otimes_{A}B,B)=\mathcal{M}_{A}^{d}(R,B)

If

Remark 1.7
.

If

D_{2}\in\Det_{R,d_{2}}

If

Example 1.8

Let

Remark 1.10
.

The scheme

is a contravariant functor of the

Remark 1.11

For every commutative
A
-algebra

Let

be a set.
We recall Vaccarino’s construction of the universal ring of

\rho^{\mathrm{univ}}:\mathbb{Z}\{X\}\rightarrow M_{d}(F_{X}(d))

Theorem 1.12

(Vaccarino, see Theorem 1.15 of

The ring

Corollary 1.13

2.
Lafforgue’s definition of pseudocharacters

Let
A
be a commutative unital ring and
G
be an affine group scheme
over
A
.

As in Section
1
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}_{A}
the category
of commutative unital
A
-algebras. If
B
is a commutative
A
-algebra and

X
is a set, we write
\mathcal{C}(X,B)
for the
B
-algebra of functions
X\rightarrow B
.

For every set
X
(resp. integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
), we make
G
act on the scheme

G^{X}
(resp.
G^{n}
) by diagonal conjugation, and we denote by
\mathcal{O}(G^{X})^{G}

(resp.
\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}
) the
A
-algebra of

G
-invariant regular functions on
G^{X}
(resp.
G^{n}
).

Definition 2.1

(See
[
8
]
Proposition 11.7 and
[
2
]

Definition 4.1)
.

Let
X
be a set and
B
be an object of
\mathcal{C}_{A}
. A

B
-valued

G
-pseudocharacter for the set
X
is a family of

A
-algebra morphisms
\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}(X,B)
, for
n\geq 1
, satisfying
the following condition:

(LPC1)

For all
n,m\geq 1
, every map
\zeta:\{1,2,\ldots,m\}\rightarrow\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

and every
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{m})^{G}
, if we define
f^{\zeta}\in\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}
by

f^{\zeta}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=f(g_{\zeta(1)},\ldots,g_{\zeta(m)})
, then we have

\Theta_{m}(f)(x_{\zeta(1)},\ldots,x_{\zeta(m)})=\Theta_{n}(f^{\zeta})(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})

for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.

Suppose that
X
is the underlying set of a group
\Gamma
.
Then a
B
-valued

G
-pseudocharacter for the group
\Gamma
is
a
B
-valued

G
-pseudocharacter for the set
X
satisfying the following
additional condition:

(LPC2)

For all
n\geq 1
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}
, if we define
\widehat{f}\in\mathcal{O}(G^{n+1})^{G}
by
\widehat{f}(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n+1})=f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1},g_{n}g_{n+1})
, then we have

\Theta_{n+1}(\widehat{f})(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+1})=\Theta_{n}(f)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n-1},\gamma_{n}\gamma_{n+1})

for all
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+1}\in\Gamma
.

We denote by
\LPC^{1}_{X,G}
(resp.
\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
) the functor

\mathcal{C}_{A}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
sending
B
to the set of
B
-valued
G
-pseudocharacters
for the set
X
(resp. for the group
\Gamma
).
If
X
is the underlying set of a group
\Gamma
, then

\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
is a subfunctor of
\LPC^{1}_{X,G}
.

Example 2.2
.

(1)

If
X
is a set and
\rho:X\rightarrow G(A)
is a map, then we define
an
A
-valued
G
-pseudocharacter
\Theta^{1}_{\rho}
for the set

by

\Theta^{1}_{\rho,n}(f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=f(\rho(x_{1}),\ldots,\rho(x_{n})),

for every
n\geq 1
, every
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}
and all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.
Note that
\Theta^{1}_{\rho}
only depends on the conjugacy class of
\rho
.

(2)

Similarly,
if
\Gamma
is a group and
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow G(A)
is a morphism of groups,
then we define an
A
-valued
G
-pseudocharacter
\Theta_{\rho}

for the group
\Gamma
by

\Theta_{\rho,n}(f)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n})=f(\rho(\gamma_{1}),\ldots,\rho(\gamma_{n})),

for every
n\geq 1
, every
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{n})^{G}
and all
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
.
Again,
\Theta_{\rho}
only depends on the conjugacy class of
\rho
.

Remark 2.3
.

The functor
\LPC^{1}_{X,G}
depends contravariantly on the set
X
. Indeed, if

u:X\rightarrow Y
is a map, then, for every commutative
A
-algebra
B
and every

\Theta=(\Theta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\LPC^{1}_{Y,G}(B)
, the family

u^{*}(\Theta)=(u^{*}(\Theta)_{n})_{n\geq 1}
defined by

u^{*}(\Theta)_{n}(f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\Theta_{n}(f)(u(x_{1}),\ldots,u(x_{n}))

for
n\geq 1
,

.

If

Proposition 2.4
.

Let

, every

and all

Then

represents the functor

Proof.

The morphism of functors

corresponding
to
\Theta^{\mathrm{univ}}
sends a morphism of rings

for

,

and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
.
We check that it is an isomorphism.
The central point is that, by definition of the product

Let

This proves that

Let

For all

We have a morphism of

induced
by the projection of

on its second factor, and this

The map

becomes
\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})
-linear for this action
of
\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})
on

\varphi_{\gamma,\delta}(\mathcal{O}(G\times G^{\Gamma}))

(resp.

Proposition 2.5
.

(i)

Let

be the sum of the images
of the
\varphi_{\gamma,\delta}

.

sends any commutative

-algebra

to the set of maps

that are morphisms of groups.

(ii)

Let

\varphi_{\gamma,\delta}(\mathcal{O}(G\times G^{\Gamma})^{G})
,
for all
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
. Then

is an ideal of

, and, if we set

, then the isomorphism

\Spec(R^{1}_{\Gamma,G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\LPC^{1}_{\Gamma,G}
of Proposition

\Spec(R_{\Gamma,G})
of

\Spec(R^{1}_{\Gamma,G})
and
\LPC_{\Gamma,G}\subset\LPC^{1}_{\Gamma,G}
.

Proof.

We already know that
J_{\Gamma,G}
and

are ideals, because
they are sums of ideals.

Let
B
be a commutative
A
-algebra and
\rho:\Gamma\rightarrow G(B)
be a map;
this corresponds to a morphism of
A
-algebras
u:\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})\rightarrow B
, and
we have

u(f)=f((\rho(\gamma))_{\gamma\in\Gamma})

for every
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})
.
We want to prove that
\rho
is a morphism of groups if and only if

u(J_{\Gamma,G})=0
. Suppose that
\rho
is a morphism of groups. Let

\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
and

. Then

\displaystyle u(\varphi_{\gamma,\delta}(f))=f(\rho(\gamma\delta),(\rho(\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Gamma}))-f(\rho(\gamma)\rho(\delta),(\rho(\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Gamma}))=0

as

is a morphism.
This shows that
J_{\Gamma,G}\subset\Ker u
. Conversely, suppose that

. Let
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
. Then, for every

, we have

0=u(\varphi_{1,(\gamma,\delta)}(f))=f(\rho(\gamma\delta))-f(\rho(\gamma)\rho(\delta)),

hence
\rho(\gamma\delta)=\rho(\gamma)\rho(\delta)
.
This finishes the proof of (i).

We now prove the second statement of (ii).
Let
B
be a commutative
A
-algebra, let

\Theta\in\LPC^{1}_{\Gamma,G}(B)
, and let

be the morphism
of

-algebras corresponding to
\Theta
.
Suppose that
\Theta
satisfies condition
(LPC2)
. Let
\gamma,\delta\in\Gamma
and

. Choose a finite subset

\{\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\}
of

and

such

Then we have

so

\displaystyle u(\varphi_{\gamma,\delta}(f))=\Theta_{n+1}(h)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n},\gamma\delta)-\Theta_{n+2}(\widehat{h})(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n},\gamma,\delta)=0.

This proves that
I_{\Gamma,G}\subset\Ker u
.

Conversely, suppose that

.
Let

,

and
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+1}\in\Gamma
.
Define

This implies that

(LPC2)

Next we discuss the behavior of the functors
\LPC^{1}_{X,G}
and

under change of the base ring

.

We will use the notion of

defined by

[
1

): a morphism of schemes
is an adequate homeomorphism if it is integral, a universal homeomorphism and
a local isomorphism at all points whose residue field is of characteristic

, see Definition 9.1.1 of
[
1
]
), which generalizes
that of reductive group schemes. In particular, if

is an affine
smooth algebraic group over a field

, then it is geometrically
reductive if and only if it is reductive (Lemma 9.2.8 of
[
1
]
), and,
if

is a smooth group scheme with connected fibers, then it
is a geometrically reductive group scheme if and only if it is reductive
(Theorem 9.7.5 of
[
1
]
).

Fix a commutative ring
A
and a flat affine group scheme
G
over
A
.
Let
B
be a commutative
A
-algebra. For every

-module

with an action
of

, we have

. As

\mathcal{O}(G^{X})\otimes_{A}B=\mathcal{O}(G_{B}^{X})
for every set

,
we deduce that
J_{\Gamma,G_{B}}=J_{\Gamma,G}\otimes_{A}B
and

I_{\Gamma,G_{B}}\supset I_{\Gamma,G}\otimes_{A}B
.
So we get morphisms of
B
-algebras, for

a set and
\Gamma
a group,

R^{1}_{X,G}\otimes_{A}B=\mathcal{O}(G^{X})^{G}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(G_{B}^{X})^{G_{B}}=R^{1}_{X,G_{B}}

and

R_{\Gamma,G}\otimes_{A}B=(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}\otimes_{A}B)/(I_{\Gamma,G}\otimes_{A}B)\rightarrow R_{\Gamma,G_{B}}

and corresponding morphisms of schemes

\beta_{X}:\LPC^{1}_{X,G_{B}}\rightarrow\LPC^{1}_{X,G}\otimes_{A}B

and

\beta_{\Gamma}:\LPC_{\Gamma,G_{B}}\rightarrow\LPC_{\Gamma,G}\otimes_{A}B.

Proposition 2.6
.

(i)

The morphisms
\beta_{X}
and
\beta_{\Gamma}
are isomorphisms
in the following cases:

(a)

is a flat
A
-algebra;

(b)

is a Dedekind domain and

is geometrically reductive over

(ii)

If

is geometrically reductive over

Proof.

Point (ii) follows from Proposition 5.2.9(3) of
[
1
]
.
We prove (i). Suppose first that

is flat over

. Then,
by Lemma 2 of Seshadri’s paper
[

, for every

with an action of

is an isomorphism. Applying

is an isomorphism.
As the functor

is right exact, Seshadri’s lemma

We finally assume that

the morphism

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain and
G
be a geometrically reductive
group scheme with connected geometric fibers over

\mathcal{O}(G^{Y})^{G}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(G_{B}^{Y})^{G_{B}}
is an isomorphism.

Remark 2.8
.

Lemma
2.7
and its application to the functor
\LPC
are
probably well-known to many people in some form. We found version of it
in a note by Chen (see
[
3
]
), as well as in a preprint by
Quast (see Section 1.3 of
[
12
]
).

Proof of Lemma
2.7
.

We will use a number of results about the cohomology of algebraic groups
that are gathered in Jantzen’s book
[
7
]
; the particular
results that we need are due to Donkin and Mathieu,
see
[
7
]
for the original references.

First, as the tensor product and the functor of invariants commute
with direct limits and as
\mathcal{O}(G^{Y})
is the direct limit of the

\mathcal{O}(G^{Z})
for
Z\subset Y
finite, we may assume that

Y
is finite.
By the universal coefficients theorem (Proposition 4.18 in Chapter I
of
[
7
]
), we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(G^{Y})^{G}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(G_{B}^{Y})^{G_{B}}\rightarrow\Tor_{1}^{A}(\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(G^{Y})),B)\rightarrow 0.

So it suffices to show that
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(G^{Y}))=0
.
For this, it suffices to proves that the localization of

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathcal{O}(G^{Y}))
at every ideal of
A
is zero; as localizations are flat,
by the universal coefficient theorem again, we may replace
A
by one of
its localizations, hence assume that
A
is a field or a discrete valuation
ring. Now, by Lemma B.9
of
[
7
]
, it suffices to prove that, for every maximal
ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
A
, if
k=A/\mathfrak{m}
, then
\mathcal{O}(G_{k}^{Y})
has a good
filtration (in the sense of II.4.16 of
[
7
]
) as a
G_{k}
-module.
This follows immediately from Propositions 4.20 and 4.21 of
Chapter II of
[
7
]
.

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that
G
is a geometrically reductive group scheme with connected geometric fibers over
A

and that

is a Dedekind domain or a field.
Let
\iota
be the morphism
\Char_{\Gamma,G}\rightarrow\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
induced by

\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/I_{\Gamma,G}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}\hookrightarrow(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})/J_{\Gamma,G})^{G}
.

(i)

If

is a field of characteristic
0
, then
\iota
is an
isomorphism.

(ii)

In general,
\iota
is an adequate homeomorphism.

The statement of the proposition is very close to that of
Proposition 11.7 of
[
8
]
and Theorem 4.5 of
[
2
]
, and
its proof uses the same kind of ideas. We still include it here because
it is not very hard.

Proof.

We prove (i). As
A
is a field, the group
G
is reductive over
A
. So,
for every algebraic representation
V
of
G
over
A
(not
necessarily finite-dimensional), we have the Reynolds operator

E=E_{V}:V\rightarrow V
(see
[
10
]
Definition 1.5),
which is a
G
-equivariant projection with image
V^{G}
, compatible with
any morphism of representations
V\rightarrow W
; also, if
V
is a
A
-algebra
and the action of
G
preserves its multiplication, then

E_{V}
is
V^{G}
-linear.
We claim that
I_{\Gamma,G}=J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}
. We already know that
I_{\Gamma,G}\subset J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}
.
Conversely, let
h\in J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}
. Write
h=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{\gamma_{i},\delta_{i}}(f_{i})
,
with
\gamma_{i},\delta_{i}\in\Gamma
and
f_{i}\in\mathcal{O}(G\times G^{\Gamma})
. As the

\varphi_{\gamma_{i},\delta_{i}}
are
G
-equivariant morphisms, they are
compatible with the Reynolds operators, so

h=E(h)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{\gamma_{i},\delta_{i}}(E(f_{i}))\in I_{\Gamma,G}.

It remains to prove that the injective morphism
\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})/J_{\Gamma,G})^{G}
is also surjective. Let
f\in\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})
, and
suppose that the class of
f
modulo
J_{\Gamma,G}
is
G
-invariant.
As the canonical surjection
\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})/J_{\Gamma,G}
is

G
-equivariant, it is compatible with the Reynolds operators, so we
deduce that
f-E(f)\in J_{\Gamma,G}
, which means that
f+J_{\Gamma,G}

is in the image of
\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/J_{\Gamma,G}^{G}
.

We prove (ii). We know that
\Char_{\Gamma,G}\rightarrow\Spec(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/J_{\Gamma,G}^{G})
is an adequate homeomorphism by
Lemma 5.2.12 of
[
1
]
(see Remark 5.2.14 of
loc. cit.
).
So it remains to prove that
\iota^{\prime}:\Spec(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/J_{\Gamma,G}^{G})\rightarrow\Spec(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/I_{\Gamma,G})
is an adequate homeomorphism.
This morphism is a closed embedding, hence it is integral, universally
injective and universally closed.
If
\Frac(A)
is of characteristic
0
, then
\iota^{\prime}
becomes an
isomorphism after we tensor it by
\Frac(A)
by
Proposition
2.6
(ii); otherwise, its source
and target have no point with residue field of characteristic

.
So it remains to show that
\iota^{\prime}
is surjective, which is equivalent
to the fact that
\iota
is surjective.

Let
x
be a point of
\Spec(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/I_{\Gamma,G})
,
which corresponds to a morphism of
A
-algebras
u:\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}/I(\Gamma,G)\rightarrow K
, with
K
a field. We want to find an extension
L
of
K
and
a morphism of
A
-algebras
v:(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})/J_{\Gamma,G})^{G}\rightarrow L
such that

v\circ{\iota}^{*}=u
. We may always enlarge
K
and
L
, so
we may assume that they are algebraically closed. Then, by
Proposition
2.6
(ii) and Lemma
2.10
(ii),

\Char_{\Gamma,G}
and
\Char_{\Gamma,G_{K}}
(resp.
\LPC_{\Gamma,G}
and

\LPC_{\Gamma,G_{K}}
) have the same points over any algebraically closed

, so
we may assume that
A=K
(and so that

is reductive over

).
By Theorem 5.13 of
[
11
]
(or Lemma 5.2.1 and Remark 5.2.2
of
[
1
]
), there exists an extension
L
of

K
and a morphism of
K
-algebras
w:\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})\rightarrow L
such that

is induced by
w_{\mid\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G}}
.
The morphism
w
corresponds to an element
(g_{\gamma})\in G^{\Gamma}(L)
, and
we denote by

the closed subgroup of
G_{L}
generated by the
set
\{g_{\gamma},\ \gamma\in\Gamma\}
. We choose

such that
the dimension of the maximal tori in

is maximal.
We claim that
H
is then strongly reductive
in
G
in the sense of Definition 16.1 of Richardson’s paper
[

]
,
that is,
H
is not contained in any proper parabolic subgroup of

, where

is a maximal torus of

.
Indeed, let
\lambda
be a cocharacter of
Z_{G}(S)
, and suppose that

H
is contained in
P(\lambda):=\{g\in Z_{G}(S)\mid\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}\ \mathrm{exists}\}
. For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, let

g^{\prime}_{\gamma}=\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g_{\gamma}\lambda(t)^{-1}\in G(L)
. Then

(g_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
and
(g^{\prime}_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
have the same
image in
\Spec(\mathcal{O}(G^{\Gamma})^{G})(L)
, so the morphism

corresponding to

satisfies

. On the other hand,

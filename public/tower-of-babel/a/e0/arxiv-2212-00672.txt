A quantum mechanics for magnetic horizons
Francesco Benini

Abstract




SISSA 19/2022/FISI

We construct an
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauged quantum mechanics, by starting from the 3d Chern-Simons-matter theory holographically dual to massive Type IIA string theory on AdS
{}_{4}\times S^{6}
, and Kaluza-Klein reducing on
S^{2}
with a background that is dual to the asymptotics of static dyonic BPS black holes in AdS
4
. The background involves a choice of gauge fluxes, that we fix via a saddle-point analysis of the 3d topologically twisted index at large
N
. The ground-state degeneracy of the effective quantum mechanics reproduces the entropy of BPS black holes, and we expect its low-lying spectrum to contain information about near-extremal horizons. Interestingly, the model has a large number of statistically-distributed couplings, reminiscent of SYK models.

as well as to the sum over fluxes, treated as a continuous variable
\mathfrak{m}
. The idea to compute a supersymmetric index in this way was put forward, for instance, in

2.1
The basic idea

We are interested in the topologically twisted index
[
19
]
of the theory, because this quantity is known to reproduce the entropy of a class of BPS AdS
4
dyonic black holes
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
. The localization formula for the index can be written schematically as

\mathcal{I}_{S^{2}\times S^{1}}=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\,\sum_{\mathfrak{m}\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{h}}}\,\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\,\frac{du^{i}}{2\pi}\;e^{\mathfrak{m}V^{\prime}(u)\,+\,\Omega(u)}\;.

(2.1)

Here
\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert
is the order of the Weyl group,
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{h}}
is the co-root lattice,
N
is the rank of the gauge group, and
\mathcal{C}
is an appropriate integration contour for the complexified Cartan-subalgebra-valued holonomies
\{u^{i}\}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}/2\pi\Gamma_{\mathfrak{h}}
. Let us outline three different approaches to this expression at large
N
.

1.

The approach developed in
[
19
]
was to resum over
\mathfrak{m}
, schematically

\mathcal{I}_{S^{2}\times S^{1}}=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{du^{i}}{2\pi}\,\frac{e^{\Omega(u)}}{1-e^{V^{\prime}(u)}}\;,

(2.2)

then determine the positions
\bar{u}
of the poles by solving the “Bethe Ansatz Equations” (BAEs)

e^{V^{\prime}(\bar{u})}=1\;,

(2.3)

and finally take the residues

\mathcal{I}_{S^{2}\times S^{1}}^{\text{BAE}}=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\sum_{\bar{u}\in\text{BAE}}\,\frac{e^{\Omega(\bar{u})}}{i^{N}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})}\;.

(2.4)

2.

Alternatively, we can evaluate both the sum over
\mathfrak{m}
and the integral over
u
in (
2.1
) in the saddle-point approximation, treating
\mathfrak{m}
as a continuous variable. The simultaneous saddle-point equations for
\mathfrak{m}
and
u
are, schematically:

\left\{\begin{aligned} 0&=V^{\prime}(\bar{u})\\
0&=\bar{\mathfrak{m}}V^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})+\Omega^{\prime}(\bar{u})\;.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

Taking into account that
V^{\prime}(u)
in (
2.1
) is defined up to integer shifts by
2\pi i
, the first set of equations is exactly the set of BAEs (
2.3
), while the second set of equations uniquely fixes
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}
in terms of
\bar{u}
. The Jacobian at the saddle point is

J^{\text{3d}}(\mathfrak{m},u)=\det\begin{pmatrix}0&V^{\prime\prime}(u)\\
V^{\prime\prime}(u)&\mathfrak{m}V^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)+\Omega^{\prime\prime}(u)\end{pmatrix}=-\bigl(V^{\prime\prime}(u)\bigr)^{2}\;.

(2.6)

Therefore, in the saddle-point approximation:

\mathcal{I}^{\text{saddle}}_{S^{2}\times S^{1}}\,\simeq\,\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\sum_{\bar{u}\in\text{saddles}}\frac{e^{\Omega(\bar{u})}}{\sqrt{J^{\text{3d}}}}=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\sum_{\bar{u}\in\text{BAEs}}\frac{e^{\Omega(\bar{u})}}{i^{N}\,V^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})}\;.

(2.7)

This method gives exactly the same answer as the previous method.

3.

A more rough approximation is to fix
\mathfrak{m}
in (
2.1
) to the value determined by the equations (
2.5
),

\mathcal{I}^{\text{fix $\bar{\mathfrak{m}}$}}_{S^{2}\times S^{1}}\,\simeq\,\mathcal{I}_{S^{1}}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\,\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{du^{i}}{2\pi}\;e^{\bar{\mathfrak{m}}V^{\prime}(u)\,+\,\Omega(u)}\;,

(2.8)

and then solve the integral in
u
in the saddle-point approximation. The saddle-point equations are
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}V^{\prime\prime}(u)+\Omega^{\prime}(u)=0
, therefore all solutions
\bar{u}
of (
2.5
) are also saddle points of (
2.8
). Assuming that there are no other solutions, we find

\mathcal{I}_{S^{1}}\,\simeq\,\frac{1}{\lvert\mathsf{W}\rvert}\sum_{\bar{u}\in\text{BAEs}}\frac{e^{\Omega(\bar{u})}}{\sqrt{J^{\text{1d}}}}\;.

(2.9)

The Jacobian in this case is
J^{\text{1d}}=\bar{\mathfrak{m}}V^{\prime\prime\prime}(\bar{u})+\Omega^{\prime\prime}(\bar{u})=V^{\prime\prime}\bigl(\frac{\Omega^{\prime}}{V^{\prime\prime}}\bigr)^{\prime}(\bar{u})
and is different from before, however as long as the Jacobian is subleading with respect to the exponential contribution, this approach captures the leading behavior.

In our setup we will find a series of saddle points
(\bar{u},\bar{\mathfrak{m}})
, and the expression
\mathcal{I}_{S^{1}}
in (
2.8
) evaluated on the dominant one will turn out to be the Witten index of an effective quantum mechanics that we will construct. In order to do so, we will first have to find the saddle-point flux
\bar{\mathfrak{m}}
, and then reduce the 3d theory on
S^{2}
in the presence of such a flux.

u(t)=N^{\alpha}\,\bigl(it+v(t)\bigr)\;.

This introduces the density

\rho(t)\equiv\frac{1}{N}\,\frac{di}{dt}\;,

(2.21)

\sum_{i}\to N\int\!dt\,\rho(t)

3
KK reduction on a flux background

The next step is to perform a Kaluza-Klein (KK) reduction of the 3d
\mathcal{N}=2
gauge theory on the sphere
S^{2}
, in the presence of the flux background
\mathfrak{m}
(
2.30

Here we will first show how the full twisted theory can be seen as a gauged
\mathcal{N}=2
quantum mechanics. Afterwards, we will introduce the background of the reduction and review the standard procedure to fix the 3d gauge group down to the 1d gauge group. We will then explain why complications arise when computing the KK spectrum of the vector multiplet, and how they can be resolved by a further modification of the gauge-fixing Lagrangian.
Lastly, we will exhibit the KK spectra of the vector and chiral multiplets.

3.1
Decomposing 3d multiplets into 1d multiplets

After the topological twist, the theory exactly fits into the framework of a gauged
\mathcal{N}=2
quantum mechanics, and we perform various changes of variables in this section to make it explicit. A similar discussion can be found in
[
44
]
. We give a brief review of 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetry in Appendix

, adapted from
[
45

, but in

We shall write the supersymmetry transformations in terms of anticommuting generators
Q
and
\overline{Q}
, with the understanding that generators should be multiplied by a complex anti-commuting parameter to produce a generic supersymmetry transformation. With
\epsilon=(1,0)^{\sf{T}}

Q
is obtained from
\widetilde{Q}_{\text{3d}}
while
\overline{Q}
is obtained from
Q_{\text{3d}}
in (

) and (

Q
and
\overline{Q}
are related by Hermitian conjugation, that is
\overline{(QX)}=(-1)^{F}\,\overline{Q}\,\overline{X}\,
. The supersymmetry algebra is

Q^{2}=\overline{Q}^{2}=0\;,\qquad\qquad\{Q,\overline{Q}\}=i\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr]\;,

(3.1)

where
\delta_{\text{gauge}}(\alpha)
is a gauge transformation with parameter
\alpha
.
We will use frame fields

,
e^{\bar{1}}_{\mu}
on

, which we introduce in Appendix
C

with flat indices

. From now on,
\overline{X}
will denote the Hermitian conjugate of
X
(since Dirac conjugates are no longer present anyway). After this rewriting, the supersymmetry variations and supersymmetric Lagrangians are as described below.

Vector multiplet.

In Wess-Zumino gauge, the 3d vector multiplet consists of the gauge field
A_{\mu}
, a real scalar
\sigma
, a real auxiliary scalar
D
, and a Dirac spinor
\lambda
. The bosonic components are R-neutral while
\lambda
has R-charge
-1
. We decompose
\lambda
in components as

\lambda=\biggl(\begin{matrix}-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\\
\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\end{matrix}\biggr)\;,

(3.2)

and redefine
D
with a shift

D^{\prime}=D-2iF_{1\bar{1}}\;.

(3.3)

Now,
\Lambda_{\bar{1}}
has R-charge
-1
whereas
\Lambda_{t}
has R-charge
+1
. These field redefinitions have trivial Jacobian. Under the supercharges preserved by the twist, the supersymmetry variations of the vector multiplet split into 2 sets of variations. The first set (Hermitian conjugate relations being implied) is:

\displaystyle QA_{t}

\displaystyle=-Q\sigma=-\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Q\Lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=-D_{t}\sigma-iD\;,

(3.4)

\displaystyle QD

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\,(D_{t}-i\sigma)\,\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}\Lambda_{t}

\displaystyle=0\;.

These coincide with the supersymmetry variations (
D.32
) of a 1d
\mathrm{U}(N)
vector multiplet in Wess-Zumino gauge. Note that here the fields and gauge transformations are also functions on
S^{2}

QA_{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\;,\qquad\overline{Q}A_{\bar{1}}=0\;,\qquad Q\Lambda_{\bar{1}}=0\;,\qquad\overline{Q}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}=2i\bigl(\partial_{t}A_{\bar{1}}-D_{\bar{1}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr)\;.

(3.5)

These coincide with the supersymmetry variations (
D.34
) of a chiral multiplet
\bigl(A_{\bar{1}},\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\bigr)
in Wess-Zumino gauge, provided that the corresponding superfields

\Xi_{\bar{1},h}=A_{\bar{1}}+\frac{\theta}{2}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}-\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}A_{\bar{1}}\;,\qquad\qquad\Xi_{1,\bar{h}}\,\equiv\,\overline{\Xi_{\bar{1},h}}=A_{1}-\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2}\,\overline{\Lambda}_{1}+\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}A_{1}

(3.6)

satisfying
\overline{D}\,\Xi_{\bar{1},h}=D\,\Xi_{1,\bar{h}}=0

\Xi_{\bar{1},h}\,\rightarrow\,h\,\bigl(\Xi_{\bar{1},h}+i\partial_{\bar{1}}\bigr)\,h^{-1}\;,\qquad\Xi_{1,\bar{h}}\,\rightarrow\,\overline{h}^{\,-1}\bigl(\Xi_{1,\bar{h}}+i\partial_{1}\bigr)\,\overline{h}\;,

(3.7)

with

and
\overline{D}\chi=0
. We indicated as
\overline{\Lambda}_{1}
the complex conjugate to
\Lambda_{\bar{1}}
.

The Yang-Mills Lagrangian is composed of two pieces, independently supersymmetric:

\displaystyle 2e_{\text{3d}}^{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{YM}}

\displaystyle=\;\,\Tr\biggl[4\bigl\lvert F_{t\bar{1}}\bigr\rvert^{2}+4iDF_{1\bar{1}}-4\bigl\lvert D_{\bar{1}}\sigma\bigr\rvert^{2}+i\overline{\Lambda}_{1}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\Lambda_{\bar{1}}+2\Lambda_{t}D_{1}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}-2\overline{\Lambda}_{1}D_{\bar{1}}\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\biggr]

\displaystyle\;\;+\Tr\biggl[(D_{t}\sigma)^{2}+D^{2}+i\overline{\Lambda}_{t}(D_{t}-i\sigma)\Lambda_{t}\biggr]\;.

(3.8)

Note that
2e_{\text{3d}}^{2}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{YM}}=Q\overline{Q}\Tr\bigl[-4iA_{1}\partial_{t}A_{\bar{1}}+4i(A_{t}-\sigma)F_{1\bar{1}}\bigr]+Q\overline{Q}\Tr\bigl[-\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\Lambda_{t}\bigr]

D.51

D.42
). Likewise, the Chern-Simons Lagrangian splits into two pieces which are separately supersymmetric:

\frac{4\pi}{k}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{CS}}=\Tr\Bigl[4iA_{1}\partial_{t}A_{\bar{1}}-4i(A_{t}+\sigma)F_{1\bar{1}}+\overline{\Lambda}_{1}\,\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\Bigr]\;+\;\Tr\Bigl[\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\Lambda_{t}-2D\sigma\Bigr]\;.

(3.9)

The superspace expression of the first piece is given in (
D.59

D.45

Chiral multiplet.

A 3d chiral multiplet consists of a complex scalar
\phi
and a Dirac spinor
\Psi
. We split
\Psi
into components as

\Psi=-i\biggl(\begin{matrix}\psi\\
\eta\end{matrix}\biggr)\;.

(3.10)

Their R-charges are
R(\psi)=R(\eta)=R(\phi)-1
. Under the supercharges preserved by the twist, the supersymmetry variations of the 3d chiral multiplet can also be organized into two sets. The first set (Hermitian conjugate relations are again implicit) is:

Q\phi=\psi\;,\qquad\overline{Q}\phi=0\;,\qquad Q\psi=0\;,\qquad\overline{Q}\psi=i(D_{t}-i\sigma)\phi\;.

(3.11)

They coincide with the supersymmetry variations (
D.34
) of a 1d chiral multiplet
(\phi,\psi)
in Wess-Zumino gauge, with corresponding superfield
\Phi_{h}=\phi+\theta\psi-\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\phi
.
The second is:

Q\eta=-f\;,\quad\;\overline{Q}\eta=-2D_{\bar{1}}\phi\;,\quad\;Qf=0\;,\quad\;\overline{Q}f=-i(D_{t}-i\sigma)\eta-2D_{\bar{1}}\psi+i\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\phi\;.

(3.12)

They match the variations (
D.36
) of a 1d Fermi multiplet
(\eta,f)
in Wess-Zumino gauge, whose corresponding superfield

\mathcal{Y}_{h}=\eta-\theta f+2\bar{\theta}D_{\bar{1}}\phi+\theta\bar{\theta}\,\Bigl(-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial_{t}\eta-2D_{\bar{1}}\psi+i\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\phi\Bigr)

(3.13)

satisfies

\overline{D}\,\mathcal{Y}_{h}=E\bigl(\Phi_{h},\Xi_{\bar{1},h}\bigr)=-2\bigl(\partial_{\bar{1}}-i\Xi_{\bar{1},h}\bigr)\Phi_{h}\;.

(3.14)

Here
\partial_{\bar{1}}
contains the background
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
connection. In the language of 1d supersymmetry, there is an E-term superpotential for
\mathcal{Y}_{h}
. After the shift (
3.3
), the kinetic term of a 3d chiral multiplet also splits into two separately supersymmetric pieces,
i.e.
, the kinetic terms of the 1d chiral (
D.46
) and of the 1d Fermi (
D.49

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{chiral}}

\displaystyle=\;\,\Bigl[\lvert D_{t}\phi\rvert^{2}-\lvert\sigma\phi\rvert^{2}-\overline{\phi}D\phi+i\overline{\psi}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\psi-i\overline{\psi}\,\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\phi+i\overline{\phi}\Lambda_{t}\psi\Bigr]

(3.15)

\displaystyle\;\;+\Bigl[i\overline{\eta}(D_{t}-i\sigma)\eta+\overline{f}f-|2D_{\bar{1}}\phi|^{2}-2\overline{\psi}D_{1}\eta+2\overline{\eta}D_{\bar{1}}\psi-i\overline{\eta}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\phi+i\overline{\phi}\,\overline{\Lambda}_{1}\eta\Bigr]\;.

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\text{chiral}}=Q\overline{Q}\bigl(-i\overline{\phi}(D_{t}+i\sigma)\phi\bigr)+Q\overline{Q}\bigl(-\overline{\eta}\eta\bigr)
, so both terms are exact.

The superpotential terms can be written as
\mathcal{L}_{\text{W}}=-Q\bigl(\eta_{a}\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{a}}\bigr)+\overline{Q}\bigl(\overline{\eta}_{a}\frac{\partial\overline{W}}{\partial\overline{\phi}_{a}}\bigr)
, which in the language of 1d supersymmetry are J-terms for the Fermi multiplets
\eta_{a}
with
J_{a}=-\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{a}}
. Supersymmetry of the first term under
Q

\overline{Q}

\overline{Q}
acts on the first term we get, up to a total time derivative,

Q\overline{Q}\biggl(\eta_{a}\,\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{a}}\biggr)=-2Q\biggl(D_{\bar{1}}\phi_{a}\,\frac{\partial W}{\partial\phi_{a}}\biggr)=-2Q(\partial_{\bar{1}}W)=-2\partial_{\bar{1}}QW\;,

(3.16)

which is another total derivative. Thus the superpotential terms are
\bigl(Q+\overline{Q}\bigr)
-exact. The supersymmetric Chern-Simons Lagrangian is the only piece that is not exact under any supercharge.

3.2
Reduction background

As mentioned at the beginning of this section, we want to reduce the theory in the presence of background fluxes for the global symmetries. In particular, we turn on a (negative) unit flux for the R-symmetry
q_{R}
. Since it is a background for a non-dynamical field, it can be off-shell without any consequences. The presence of this background, under which the chiral multiplets are differently charged, generically breaks the
\mathrm{SU}(3)
flavor symmetry down to its diagonal subgroup
\mathrm{U}(1)^{2}_{F}

F_{1\bar{1}}=\frac{i\,\mathfrak{m}}{4R^{2}}\;,\qquad\text{where $\mathfrak{m}$ is a constant in the Cartan subalgebra.}

(3.17)

The choice of
\mathfrak{m}
will eventually be the one dictated by the saddle-point approximation to the topologically twisted index, discussed in Section
2

Since
F_{1\bar{1}}
couples to the auxiliary field
D

3.8

\frac{2i}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\,F_{1\bar{1}}+\sum_{a}\,[\overline{\phi}_{a},\phi_{a}]-\frac{k}{2\pi}\sigma=0\;.

(3.18)

The background should satisfy the D-term equation in order to be supersymmetric, and it is simplest to turn on a background for
\sigma
to cancel the background flux. This falls into the class of “topological” vacua discussed in
[
46

. Moreover, since
A_{t}+\sigma
appears in the algebra (

A_{t}

\sigma

A_{t}+\sigma

F_{1\bar{1}}=\frac{i\mathfrak{m}}{4R^{2}}\;,\qquad\sigma=-\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{2m_{k}R^{2}}\;,\qquad A_{t}=\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{2m_{k}R^{2}}\;,\qquad\text{where}\quad m_{k}\equiv\frac{k\,e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}{2\pi}\;.

(3.19)

One can check that all the equations of motion are satisfied on this background, except for that of
A_{t}+\sigma

\mathfrak{m}=0

\Tr\biggl(\frac{k\mathfrak{m}}{4\pi R^{2}}\,(A_{t}+\sigma)\biggr)\;.

(3.20)

In other words, background fluxes produce a background electric charge in the presence of Chern-Simons terms. As we will discuss later, the presence of this linear term is crucial and it is the main source of complications when computing the vector multiplet spectrum.

3.3
Partial gauge fixing

In order to reduce to a gauged quantum mechanics, we need to fix the 3d gauge group to the unbroken 1d gauge group, consisting of time-dependent transformations that are constant on
S^{2}
. A systematic procedure to achieve that is presented in Appendix
E
and we refer the reader to
[
47
]
for more details. We choose the Coulomb gauge with gauge-fixing function

G_{\text{gf}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\xi}}\,\Bigl(D^{B}_{1}A_{\bar{1}}+D^{B}_{\bar{1}}A_{1}\Bigr)\;.

(3.25)

One can check that it leaves the 1d gauge group unfixed. The covariant derivatives above only contain the spin connection and monopole background. In general, for any
G_{\text{gf}}
, the gauge-fixing procedure adds the following terms to the Lagrangian:

\frac{1}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\Tr\biggl[\frac{b^{2}}{2}+b\,\Bigl(G_{\text{gf}}-\{\widetilde{c},c\}\Bigr)+i\,\widetilde{c}\,\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)\,G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{1}{2}\,\{\widetilde{c},c\}^{2}\biggr]\;.

(3.26)

Here
c
and
\widetilde{c}
are independent Grassmann scalars, while
b
is a bosonic auxiliary field. Importantly, all of them are valued in the part of the gauge algebra that is broken by
G_{\text{gf}}
, and do not contain modes in the residual gauge algebra. In the following, a subscript
\mathfrak{r}
will indicate a restriction to the residual gauge algebra, and a subscript
\mathfrak{f}
a restriction to the complement containing fixed (or broken) gauge generators.
11
11

11

In the Coulomb gauge (
3.25
),
\mathfrak{r}
contains diagonal transformations with
l=0
, while
\mathfrak{f}
contains diagonal transformations with
l>0
as well as all off-diagonal transformations.

We define a BRST supercharge
s
as:

sX=\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)\,X\;,\quad sc=\frac{i}{2}\{c,c\}_{\mathfrak{f}}\;,\quad s\widetilde{c}=ib\;,\quad sb=\delta_{\text{gauge}}(R)\,\widetilde{c}\;,\quad R\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\{c,c\}_{\mathfrak{r}}\;.

(3.27)

One can check that

s^{2}=i\,\delta_{\text{gauge}}(R)\;,\qquad\qquad sR=0\;.

(3.28)

This allows us to define an
s
-cohomology on invariants of the residual gauge group. The terms produced by gauge fixing can then be written in a BRST-exact form:

\text{(\ref{FP gf})}=\frac{1}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\,s\Tr\widetilde{c}\,\biggl(-i\,G_{\text{gf}}-\frac{i}{2}\,b+\frac{i}{2}\,\{\widetilde{c},c\}\biggr)\,\equiv\,s\Psi_{\text{gf}}\;.

(3.29)

We defined
\Psi_{\text{gf}}
as the function in parentheses.
We note that there is still complete freedom in specifying the inner product in the ghost sector,
i.e.
, the Hermiticity properties of
c
and
\widetilde{c}
. In order for the theory to be unitary and have a consistent Hamiltonian formulation
[
48
]
, one needs that
c
and
\widetilde{c}
are Hermitian, so that
s
is a real supercharge and (
3.26
) is real. With this choice, (
3.26
) is invariant under a ghost-number symmetry valued in
\mathbb{R}^{*}
, which acts as:

c\,\mapsto\,e^{\alpha}\,c\;,\qquad\widetilde{c}\,\mapsto\,e^{-\alpha}\,\widetilde{c}\;,\qquad s\,\mapsto\,e^{\alpha}\,s\;,

(3.30)

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We say that
c
has ghost number
n_{g}=1
and
\widetilde{c}
has
n_{g}=-1
. Physical observables are identified with the
s
-cohomology at
n_{g}=0
, since external states must be gauge invariant and cannot contain ghosts. Since
c
,
\widetilde{c}
, and
b
are Hermitian, they are neutral under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
, and (
3.26
) is invariant under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
, since
G_{\text{gf}}
is R-neutral.

3.4
Supersymmetrized gauge fixing

As anticipated, the linear term (
3.20
) causes complications in the computation of the KK spectrum of the vector multiplet, and the following discussion aims to explain why. The standard Faddeev-Popov gauge-fixing procedure we just reviewed generically breaks the supersymmetries that were defined on the original action because of the presence of the BRST-exact term
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
, which might not be supersymmetric.
Considering a supercharge
Q
, and assuming that it does not act on the fields in the gauge-fixing complex, the transformation of
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
is
-sQ\Psi_{\text{gf}}
. When computing
s
-closed (
i.e.
, gauge-invariant) quantities, this is harmless because the potentially violating term is
s
-exact, and it does not affect the result. For example, supersymmetric Ward identities can be derived for any observable in the theory, since their correlators do not depend on
s
-exact terms.

However, the spectrum of the Chern-Simons-matter theory around a monopole background is not gauge invariant, because the quadratic action is not invariant under linearized BRST transformations.
12
12

12

Although the BRST transformations are non-linear in the fields, to have a gauge-invariant spectrum, it would be enough that the quadratic action be invariant under the
linearized
transformations.

This can be seen from the presence of the linear term (
3.20
). Its BRST variation is

\frac{1}{4\pi R^{2}}\Tr\bigl(ik\mathfrak{m}\,[c,A_{t}+\sigma]\bigr)
,
and it must cancel with the linearized BRST variation of the quadratic action, which is then nonzero. Consequently, there is no guarantee that the spectrum will be supersymmetric, because it is computed from a quadratic action that is not
s
-closed, and therefore
s
-exact terms violating supersymmetry cannot be neglected.

A way to resolve this issue takes inspiration from
[
49
]
. In addition to adding
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
to gauge fix our path integral, we can further add
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
. The real supercharge
\mathcal{Q}
acts as
\mathcal{Q}=Q+\overline{Q}
on physical fields, and we choose its action on the gauge-fixing complex such that
\delta\equiv(s+\mathcal{Q})
closes on symmetries and unfixed gauge transformations. We will show that the further addition of
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
does not change the expectation value of any (possibly non-supersymmetric) operator
\mathcal{O}
with ghost number
n_{g}\leq 0
. In particular, physical observables with
n_{g}=0
are not affected. At this point, we have added
\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}
to the original action. The real supercharge
\delta
is explicitly preserved because our choice that
\delta^{2}
contains symmetries and unfixed gauge transformations implies
\delta^{2}\Psi_{\text{gf}}=0
. With this procedure, the number of preserved supercharges has not changed; while the gauge-fixed action with
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
is invariant under
s
, the gauge-fixed action with
\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}
is invariant under
\delta
. Its usefulness for computing the spectrum lies in the fact that
A_{t}+\sigma
can be redefined by shifting with a quadratic combination of ghosts such that
\delta(A_{t}^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime})=0
, making the linear term (
3.20
)
\delta
-closed. By extension, the quadratic action which is modified by the shift is also
\delta
-closed, and its spectrum is supersymmetric.

In order for
\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}=(s+\mathcal{Q})\Psi_{\text{gf}}
to be invariant under
\delta
,
\delta^{2}
should only contain residual gauge transformations and possibly other symmetries of
\Psi_{\text{gf}}
. This condition constrains how
\mathcal{Q}
can act on fields in the gauge-fixing complex. The supersymmetry transformations of the physical fields

under
\mathcal{Q}
are given in (

)-(
3.5
) and (
3.11
)-(
3.12

\mathcal{Q}
acts on the fields
Y
in the gauge-fixing complex, we find:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{2}X

\displaystyle=\{Q,\overline{Q}\}X=i\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr]\,X\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{Q},s\}X=\delta_{\text{gauge}}\bigl(\mathcal{Q}c\bigr)\,X\;,

(3.31)

\displaystyle\delta^{2}X

\displaystyle=i\Bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}\bigl(A_{t}+\sigma+i\mathcal{Q}c-R\bigr)\Bigr]\,X\;.

If we want
\delta
to close on time translations and residual gauge transformations, the only possibility is to set
\mathcal{Q}c=i(A_{t}+\sigma)_{\mathfrak{f}}
. Hence, physical fields satisfy the algebra:

\delta^{2}X=i\Bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}\bigl(A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}}-R\bigr)\Bigr]\,X\;.

(3.32)

Having fixed
\mathcal{Q}c

c

\mathcal{Q}^{2}c=0\;,\qquad\qquad\{\mathcal{Q},s\}c=i\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}})\bigr]\,c\;,

(3.33)

which imply (
3.32

3.33

Y
in the gauge-fixing complex.
Setting
\mathcal{Q}\,\widetilde{c}=0
for simplicity, we find that this fixes
\mathcal{Q}b
and, altogether,
\mathcal{Q}
acts on the fields in the gauge-fixing complex as:

\mathcal{Q}\,c=i(A_{t}+\sigma)_{\mathfrak{f}}\;,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{Q}\,\widetilde{c}=0\;,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{Q}\,b=\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}})\bigr]\,\widetilde{c}\;.

(3.34)

Given
\Psi_{\text{gf}}
that we defined in (
3.29

\mathcal{Q}\,\Psi_{\text{gf}}=\frac{1}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\Tr\biggl[i\,\widetilde{c}\,\mathcal{Q}G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{c}\bigl(D_{t}-i\sigma\bigr)\widetilde{c}\,\biggr]\;,

(3.35)

where
\sigma
acts in the adjoint representation (namely,
\sigma\widetilde{c}
stands for
[\sigma,\widetilde{c}\,]
in matrix notation). Hence, collecting the contributions from (
3.26

), the supersymmetrized gauge-fixing procedure requires us to add the following terms to the original Lagrangian:

\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}=\frac{1}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\Tr\biggl[\frac{b^{2}}{2}+b\,\Bigl(G_{\text{gf}}-\{\widetilde{c},c\}\Bigr)+i\,\widetilde{c}\,\Bigl(\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)+\mathcal{Q}\Bigr)\,G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{1}{2}\{\widetilde{c},c\}^{2}+\frac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{c}\,\bigl(D_{t}-i\sigma\bigr)\,\widetilde{c}\,\biggr]\,.

(3.36)

With the choice that
c
and
\widetilde{c}
are Hermitian,
\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}
is real.

It is important to note (following
[
49
]
) that adding
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
to
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
does not change the expectation values of operators with
n_{g}\leq 0
, even if they are
not
invariant under
\mathcal{Q}
. In particular, it does not change physical observables. This can be shown explicitly for the thermal partition function. We first integrate in an adjoint-valued auxiliary field
a
to rewrite the quartic ghost interactions, after which the gauge-fixing action becomes:

\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}=\frac{1}{e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\Tr\biggl[\frac{b^{2}-a^{2}}{2}+b\,G_{\text{gf}}+\widetilde{c}\,\bigl[a+b,c\bigr]+i\widetilde{c}\Bigl(\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)+\mathcal{Q}\Bigr)G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{i}{2}\,\widetilde{c}\bigl(D_{t}-i\sigma\bigr)\widetilde{c}\biggr].

(3.37)

Note that
a
has both gauge-fixed and residual components. Since the full action is quadratic in the Grassmann fields
\{F_{\text{phys}},c,\widetilde{c}\,\}
, where
F_{\text{phys}}
is the set of physical fermions, we can formally perform the path integral over them, obtaining:

\det\begin{pmatrix}S_{0}|_{F,F}&0&\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{F,\widetilde{c}}\\
0&0&s\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{c,\widetilde{c}}\\
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{\widetilde{c},F}&s\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{\widetilde{c},c}&\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{\widetilde{c},\widetilde{c}}\end{pmatrix}\,\sim\,\det\Bigl(s\Psi_{\text{gf}}|_{c,\widetilde{c}}\Bigr)\,\det\Bigl(S_{0}|_{F,F}\Bigr)\;.

(3.38)

All entries of the matrix on the LHS are (possibly differential) operators involving the bosons. This proves that the thermal partition function does not depend on the term
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
.

More generally, we prove that the expectation value of any operator
\mathcal{O}
with ghost number
n_{g}\leq 0
is unchanged by the addition of
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
to the Lagrangian. The key property is that
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gh}}
is the sum of two terms, of ghost number
-1
and
-2
, respectively.
Let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{s}
be the path integral with
s\Psi_{\text{gf}}
as gauge fixing, and let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\delta}
be the path integral with
\delta\Psi_{\text{gf}}
as gauge fixing. We have

\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{\delta}=\bigl\langle\mathcal{O}\,e^{i\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}}\bigr\rangle_{s}=\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{s}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(i)^{n}}{n!}\,\bigl\langle\mathcal{O}\,(\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}})^{n}\bigr\rangle_{s}=\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{s}\;.

(3.39)

The last equality holds because ghost number is a symmetry of
\langle\cdot\rangle_{s}
, implying null expectation value for any correlator that has
n_{g}\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{O}\,(\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}})^{n}
has
n_{g}<0
, one concludes that
\langle\mathcal{O}\,(\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}})^{n}\rangle_{s}=0
for every
n
. For the restricted set of operators
\mathcal{O}
with
n_{g}\leq 0
, one can constrain
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\delta}
using the symmetries of
\langle\cdot\rangle_{s}
. In particular, although both supersymmetry and
\mathrm{U}(1)_{R}
are not symmetries of
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\delta}
because
\mathcal{Q}\Psi_{\text{gf}}
breaks them, their Ward identities can still be used to constrain the correlators
\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{\delta}
. This result will play a crucial role in Section
5
.

We can now show how the linear Lagrangian term containing
A_{t}+\sigma
can be made
\delta
-invariant
using a field redefinition. This is crucial in order to have a reliable and supersymmetric spectrum. The linear term (
3.20
) only contains modes
(A_{t}+\sigma)_{\mathfrak{r}}
which are constant on
S^{2}
, due to the integral over
S^{2}
. Since

appears in (

) as a central charge,
\delta(A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}}-R)=0
. Therefore, by redefining

A^{\prime}_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{r}}=A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}}+\frac{1}{2}\{c,c\}_{\mathfrak{r}}\;,

(3.40)

the linear term (
3.20
) becomes (dropping the

\frac{k}{4\pi R^{2}}\Tr\Bigl(\mathfrak{m}\,(A_{t}+\sigma)\Bigr)+\frac{m_{k}}{4R^{2}e^{2}_{\text{3d}}}\Tr\Bigl(c\,[\mathfrak{m},c]\Bigr)\;,

(3.41)

where
\mathfrak{m}
is diagonal and
m_{k}
was defined in (
3.19
). The first term is invariant under
\delta
, therefore after adding the second term to the quadratic action, the latter becomes invariant under
\delta
as well, and the spectrum has to be supersymmetric (
i.e.
,
\delta

A_{t,\mathfrak{r}}+\sigma_{\mathfrak{r}}
is still Hermitian because
c
is Hermitian.

Having taken care of

A.2
Polylogarithms

The polylogarithms are defined through their Taylor series around
z=0
:

\text{Li}_{k}(z)=\sum\nolimits_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{\ell}}{\ell^{k}}\;,

(A.27)

which is absolutely convergent for
|z|<1
. This definition can be analytically continued to the whole complex plane, with a branch cut on the real axis from
z=1
to
z=\infty
. In particular
\Li_{1}(z)=-\log(1-z)
, where the principal sheet defined by (
A.27
) is such that
\im\log\in(-\pi,\pi)
.
The functions
\Li_{k\geq 2}
have an absolutely convergent series (
A.27
) on the unit circle and are thus continuous at
z=1
, while the functions
\Li_{k\leq 0}
have a pole at
z=1
but no branch cut (in particular
\Li_{0}(z)=\frac{z}{1-z}
). One can define the single-valued analytic functions

F_{k}(u)=\Li_{k}\bigl(1-e^{-iu}\bigr)

(A.28)

defined by (
A.27
) in the domain
\bigl\lvert 1-e^{-iu}\bigr\rvert<1
with
\re u\in\bigl(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\bigr)
(implying that
F_{k}(0)=0
) and by analytic continuation elsewhere. For instance
F_{0}(u)=e^{iu}-1
whereas
F_{1}(u)=iu
.

Whenever the function is differentiable, we have

z\,\partial_{z}\text{Li}_{k}(z)=\text{Li}_{k-1}(z)

(A.29)

or alternatively

-i\,\partial_{u}\text{Li}_{k}(e^{iu})=\text{Li}_{k-1}(e^{iu})\qquad\text{or}\qquad\partial_{u}F_{k}(u)=\frac{i}{e^{iu}-1}\,F_{k-1}(u)\;.

(A.30)

The last relation allows one to define
F_{k}(u)=\int_{0}^{u}\frac{i}{e^{iw}-1}\,F_{k-1}(w)
which is single-valued because the integrand is analytic with no poles. The polylogarithms satisfy the following identities:

\displaystyle\Li_{0}(e^{iu})+\Li_{0}(e^{-iu})

\displaystyle=-g^{\prime\prime\prime}_{+}(u)=-1

(A.31)

\displaystyle\Li_{1}(e^{iu})-\Li_{1}(e^{-iu})

\displaystyle=-ig^{\prime\prime}_{+}(u)

\displaystyle\Li_{2}(e^{iu})+\Li_{2}(e^{-iu})

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}_{+}(u)

\displaystyle\Li_{3}(e^{iu})-\Li_{3}(e^{-iu})

\displaystyle=ig_{+}(u)\;,

where

g_{+}(u)=\frac{1}{6}u^{3}-\frac{\pi}{2}u^{2}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}u

(A.32)

is the same function defined in (
2.24
).
These relations are valid for
\re u\in(0,2\pi)
and the polylogarithms in their principal determination, and can then be extended to the whole complex plane by analytic continuation (notice that the functions on the RHS are polynomials with no branch cuts).

(\omega^{a}{}_{b})_{\mu}=e^{a}{}_{\nu}\bigl(\partial_{\mu}e^{\nu}{}_{b}+\Gamma^{\nu}_{\mu\rho}e^{\rho}{}_{b}\bigr)
, has non-zero components

(\omega^{1}{}_{1})_{z}=-(\omega^{\bar{1}}{}_{\bar{1}})_{z}=-\frac{\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}\;,\qquad\qquad(\omega^{1}{}_{1})_{\bar{z}}=-(\omega^{\bar{1}}{}_{\bar{1}})_{\bar{z}}=\frac{z}{1+z\bar{z}}\;.

(C.5)

The spinor covariant derivative (without gauge connections)
D_{\mu}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\psi_{+}\\
\psi_{-}\end{smallmatrix}\big)\equiv(D_{\mu}\psi_{+},D_{\mu}\psi_{-})^{\sf{T}}
can be written as

D=d-is\omega\qquad\text{with}\qquad\omega=i\,\frac{\bar{z}\,dz-z\,d\bar{z}}{1+z\bar{z}}=(\cos\theta-1)\,d\varphi

(C.6)

and
s=\pm\frac{1}{2}
is the spin.
Note that
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}d\omega=-2
.
The components
\psi_{\pm}
are sections of the
\mathrm{U}(1)
bundles associated to the line bundles
\mathcal{K}^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\cong\mathcal{O}(\mp 1)
, where
\mathcal{K}
is the canonical bundle. A generic
\mathrm{U}(1)
bundle is labeled by a half-integer monopole charge
q
, and has covariant derivative
D=d-iqa
. To conform with the conventions of
[
54
]
for the monopole harmonics, we write the connection as a half-integer multiple of
a=-\omega
.

Similarly, the Levi-Civita connection on 1-forms is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
connection when projected onto the frame fields:

e^{z}_{1}\,\nabla_{\mu}A_{z}=(\partial_{\mu}-i\omega_{\mu})e^{z}_{1}A_{z}\equiv D_{\mu}A_{1}\;,\qquad e^{\bar{z}}_{\bar{1}}\,\nabla_{\mu}A_{\bar{z}}=(\partial_{\mu}+i\omega_{\mu})e^{\bar{z}}_{\bar{1}}A_{\bar{z}}\equiv D_{\mu}A_{\bar{1}}\;.

(C.7)

Thus
A_{1}=e^{z}_{1}A_{z}
and
A_{\bar{1}}=e^{\bar{z}}_{\bar{1}}A_{\bar{z}}
are sections with
q=-1
and
q=+1
, respectively. On the other hand,
D_{\mu}A_{3}=\partial_{\mu}A_{3}
and thus
A_{3}
is a section of the trivial bundle, like a scalar. Defining
D_{a}=e^{\mu}_{a}D_{\mu}
, one finds
(dA)_{ab}=e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\nu}_{b}(\nabla_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}A_{\mu})=D_{a}A_{b}-D_{b}A_{a}
.
If, in addition, the fields are in the adjoint representation of the gauge group and there is a background gauge field with fluxes,

A=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}_{i}H^{i}\,a\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{2}}dA=\mathfrak{m}_{i}H^{i}\;,

(C.8)

then including this background in the covariant derivatives
D_{\mu}
shifts the spin
s\rightarrow s-\frac{\alpha(\mathfrak{m})}{2}
, or equivalently
q\rightarrow q+\frac{\alpha(\mathfrak{m})}{2}
, where
\alpha
are the roots.

The derivatives
D_{1}
and
D_{\bar{1}}
raise and lower the spin by
1
, respectively. This is opposite in terms of the charge
q
.
Their explicit expressions are

D^{(q)}_{1}=\frac{1}{2R}\Bigl((1+z\bar{z})\,\partial_{z}-q\bar{z}\Bigr)\;,\qquad\qquad D^{(q)}_{\bar{1}}=\frac{1}{2R}\Bigl((1+z\bar{z})\,\partial_{\bar{z}}+qz\Bigr)\;,

(C.9)

where the superscript indicates the charge of the section they act on, whereas under complex conjugation
D_{1}^{(q)\,*}=D^{(-q)}_{\bar{1}}
and
D_{\bar{1}}^{(q)\,*}=D^{(-q)}_{1}
.
We define the operators

L_{+}=z^{2}\partial_{z}+\partial_{\bar{z}}-qz\;,\qquad L_{-}=-\bar{z}^{2}\partial_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{z}-q\bar{z}\;,\qquad L_{z}=z\partial_{z}-\bar{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-q\;,

(C.10)

satisfying the
\mathfrak{su}{(2)}
algebra
[L_{z},L_{\pm}]=\pm L_{\pm}
and
[L_{+},L_{-}]=2L_{z}
. The covariant Laplacian is

\displaystyle-D^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\,L^{2}-q^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\{L_{+},L_{-}\}+L_{z}^{2}-q^{2}=-\bigl(1+z\bar{z}\bigr)^{2}\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}-q(1+z\bar{z})L_{z}-q^{2}

(C.11)

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\partial_{\theta}\,\bigl(\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\bigr)+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\bigl(-i\partial_{\varphi}-q+q\cos\theta\bigr)^{2}\;,

which can be diagonalized simultaneously with
L^{2}
and
L_{z}
. Its eigenfunctions are the monopole spherical harmonics
Y_{q,l,m}
with
|m|\leq l
, that we choose to be orthonormal on an
S^{2}
of radius
1

\int_{S^{2}}\!\sqrt{g}\;\;\overline{Y_{q,l,m}}\;Y_{q,l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}=\delta_{l,l^{\prime}}\,\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\;.

(C.12)

The highest harmonic with
m=l
, annihilated by
L_{+}
, is

Y_{q,l,l}(z,\bar{z})\,\propto\,\frac{z^{l+q}}{(1+z\bar{z})^{l}}\;.

(C.13)

Regularity at the poles implies
l+q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
l\geq|q|
.

The Laplacian can be written in terms of the derivatives as

{-D}^{2}=-4R^{2}D_{1}D_{\bar{1}}+q=-4R^{2}D_{\bar{1}}D_{1}-q=-2R^{2}\{D_{1},D_{\bar{1}}\}\;.

(C.14)

Besides, one can verify that

[D_{1},L_{z}]=[D_{1},L_{\pm}]=[D_{\bar{1}},L_{z}]=[D_{\bar{1}},L_{\pm}]=0\;.

(C.15)

Therefore the derivatives act as bundle-changing operators mapping
Y_{q,m,l}
to
Y_{q\pm 1,m,l}
. The exact relations can be derived integrating by parts the orthonormality conditions. For a suitable choice of phases one finds
[
54
,
55
]
:

\displaystyle D^{(q)}_{1}\,Y_{q,l,m}

\displaystyle=-\frac{s_{-}(q,l)}{2R}\,Y_{q-1,l,m}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{with }\quad s_{-}(q,l)

\displaystyle=\bigl[l(l+1)-q(q-1)\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\;,

(C.16)

\displaystyle D^{(q)}_{\bar{1}}\,Y_{q,l,m}

\displaystyle=\;\;\frac{s_{+}(q,l)}{2R}\,Y_{q+1,l,m}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{with }\quad s_{+}(q,l)

\displaystyle=\bigl[l(l+1)-q(q+1)\bigr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\;.

Following the same conventions as in
[
55
]
, the monopole harmonics satisfy

\overline{Y_{q,l,m}}=(-1)^{q+m}\;Y_{-q,l,-m}

(C.17)

under complex conjugation.

Finally, the triple overlap of harmonics is given in terms of Wigner 3
j
-symbols:

\int\!d\Omega\;Y_{q,l,m}Y_{q^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}Y_{q^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime},m^{\prime\prime}}={}\\
{}=(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}+l^{\prime\prime}}\biggl[\frac{(2l+1)(2l^{\prime}+1)(2l^{\prime\prime}+1)}{4\pi}\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
q&q^{\prime}&q^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(C.18)

or equivalently

\displaystyle Y_{q,l,m}\,Y_{q^{\prime},l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}={}

(C.19)

\displaystyle\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}}(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}+l^{\prime\prime}+q^{\prime\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}}\biggl[\frac{(2l+1)(2l^{\prime}+1)(2l^{\prime\prime}+1)}{4\pi}\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
q&q^{\prime}&q^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}Y_{-q^{\prime\prime},l^{\prime\prime},-m^{\prime\prime}}

The
3j
-symbols are directly related to Clebsch-Gordan coefficients that decompose the angular momentum state
\lvert l^{\prime\prime}\,m^{\prime\prime}\rangle
in terms of
\lvert l\,m\,l^{\prime}\,m^{\prime}\rangle=\lvert l\,m\rangle\otimes\lvert l^{\prime}\,m^{\prime}\rangle
:

C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\,\equiv\,\langle l\,m\,l^{\prime}\,m^{\prime}|\,l^{\prime\prime}\,m^{\prime\prime}\rangle=(-1)^{l-l^{\prime}+m^{\prime\prime}}\sqrt{2l^{\prime\prime}+1}\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&-m^{\prime\prime}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(C.20)

In particular, the Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are zero unless
m+m^{\prime}=m^{\prime\prime}
,
\bigl\lvert m^{(i)}\bigr\rvert\leq l^{(i)}
with
m^{(i)}=l^{(i)}\text{ mod }1
, and
l^{(i)}\leq l^{(j)}+l^{(k)}
. The
3j
-symbol is symmetric under even permutations of its columns, and gains a sign
(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}+l^{\prime\prime}}
under odd permutations. It also gains a sign
(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}+l^{\prime\prime}}
when one changes sign to
m
,
m^{\prime}
and
m^{\prime\prime}
simultaneously.
This implies the following relations among Clebsch-Gordan coefficients:

\displaystyle C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}&l\\
m^{\prime}&-m^{\prime\prime}&-m\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l-l^{\prime\prime}+m^{\prime}}\left[\resizebox{52429}{}{$\dfrac{2l+1}{2l^{\prime\prime}+1}$}\right]^{1/2}\,C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\;,

(C.21)

\displaystyle C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l^{\prime\prime}&l&l^{\prime}\\
-m^{\prime\prime}&m&-m^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l^{\prime\prime}-l^{\prime}+m}\left[\resizebox{52429}{}{$\dfrac{2l^{\prime}+1}{2l^{\prime\prime}+1}$}\right]^{1/2}\,C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\;,

\displaystyle C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l^{\prime}&l&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m^{\prime}&m&m^{\prime\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l+l^{\prime}-l^{\prime\prime}}C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&l^{\prime\prime}\\
m&m^{\prime}&m^{\prime\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\;.

In the special case that

(and

as in the general case):

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&L\\
m&m^{\prime}&-M\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l-l^{\prime}+M}\biggl[\frac{1}{2L+1}\binom{2L}{L+M}^{-1}\binom{2l}{l+m}\binom{2l^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\;,

\displaystyle C\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}l&l^{\prime}&L\\

\displaystyle=\biggl[\binom{2L}{L+M}^{-1}\binom{2l}{l+m}\binom{2l^{\prime}}{l^{\prime}+m^{\prime}}\biggr]^{\frac{1}{2}}\;.

Appendix D
1d
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{N}=2}
superspace

We review here the 1d
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace formalism, drawing from Appendix A of
[
45
]
. The
\mathcal{N}=2
superspace in quantum mechanics, which we denote as
\mathbb{R}^{1|2}
, has coordinates
(t,\theta,\bar{\theta})
, where
\theta
is a complex fermionic coordinate. A supersymmetry transformation is

\delta=-\epsilon\,Q+\overline{\epsilon}\,\overline{Q}
,
where
\epsilon
,
\overline{\epsilon}
are anticommuting parameters, and
Q
,
\overline{Q}
are anticommuting generators so that
\delta
is commuting. Here
Q
and
\overline{Q}
are defined as differential operators acting on superfields:

Q\equiv\partial_{\theta}+\frac{i}{2}\,\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\;,\qquad\overline{Q}\equiv-\partial_{\bar{\theta}}-\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\,\partial_{t}\;.

(D.1)

They satisfy the algebra
Q^{2}=\overline{Q}^{2}=0
and
\{Q,\overline{Q}\}=-i\partial_{t}
. Moreover,
Q
and
\overline{Q}
anticommute with another set of differential operators

D\equiv\partial_{\theta}-\frac{i}{2}\,\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\;,\qquad\overline{D}\equiv-\partial_{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\,\partial_{t}\;,

(D.2)

which satisfy the algebra
D^{2}=\overline{D}^{2}=0
and
\{D,\overline{D}\}=i\partial_{t}
.
One has
\overline{(DX)}=(-1)^{F}\overline{D}\,\overline{X}
and
\overline{(\overline{D}X)}=(-1)^{F}D\overline{X}
.

D.1
Matter multiplets

A chiral superfield
\Phi_{h}
is defined by
\overline{D}\Phi_{h}=0
. Gauge transformations act as

\Phi_{h}\rightarrow h\,\Phi_{h}\;,\qquad h=e^{\chi}\;,\qquad\chi:\mathbb{R}^{1|2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\otimes r\;,\qquad\overline{D}\chi=0\;,

(D.3)

where
r
is some representation of the gauge group.
\overline{D}\Phi_{h}=0
implies that
\Phi_{h}
and its complex conjugate anti-chiral superfield
\overline{\Phi}_{h}
have expansion:

\Phi_{h}=\phi+\theta\psi-\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\phi\;,\qquad\qquad\overline{\Phi}_{h}=\overline{\phi}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{\psi}+\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\overline{\phi}\;.

(D.4)

Acting with (
D.1
) on
\Phi_{h}
and
\overline{\Phi}_{h}
, we find the following supersymmetry variations:

\displaystyle Q\phi

\displaystyle=\psi\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Q\psi

\displaystyle=0\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}\phi

\displaystyle=0\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}\psi

\displaystyle=i\partial_{t}\phi\;.

(D.5)

Suppose that
\Phi_{a,h}
are a collection of bosonic chiral superfields. We can also have fermionic Fermi superfields
\mathcal{Y}_{h}
, satisfying
\overline{D}\mathcal{Y}_{h}=E(\Phi_{h})
for some holomorphic function
E(\Phi_{h})
, and transforming as
\mathcal{Y}_{h}\rightarrow h\mathcal{Y}_{h}
under some representation of the gauge group.
\overline{D}\mathcal{Y}_{h}=E(\Phi_{h})
implies that
\mathcal{Y}_{h}
and its conjugate
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{h}
have expansion:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{h}

\displaystyle=\eta-\theta f-\bar{\theta}E(\phi)+\theta\bar{\theta}\Bigl(\partial_{a}E(\phi)\psi_{a}-\tfrac{i}{2}\partial_{t}\eta\Bigr)=\eta-\theta f-\bar{\theta}E(\Phi)-\tfrac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\partial_{t}\eta\;,

(D.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\overline{\eta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{f}-\theta\overline{E}(\overline{\phi})+\theta\bar{\theta}\Bigl(\overline{\psi}_{a}\overline{\partial}_{a}\overline{E}(\overline{\phi})+\tfrac{i}{2}\partial_{t}\overline{\eta}\Bigr)=\overline{\eta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{f}-\theta\overline{E}(\overline{\Phi})+\tfrac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\partial_{t}\overline{\eta}\;.

Acting with (
D.1
) gives the supersymmetry variations:

\displaystyle Q\eta

\displaystyle=-f\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Qf

\displaystyle=0\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}\eta

\displaystyle=E(\phi)\;,\qquad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}f

\displaystyle=-i\partial_{t}\eta+\partial_{a}E(\phi)\,\psi_{a}\;.

(D.7)

D.2
Vector multiplet

We assume that the gauge group
G
is semi-simple (inclusion of
\mathrm{U}(1)
factors is trivial) with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote the complexified algebra as
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{\mathbb{R}}i\mathfrak{g}
, with Killing form given by the trace operation
\Tr
. It admits a root space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}L_{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}
is a Cartan subalgebra and
\Phi
is the set of all roots. We can use the Chevalley basis
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\{H^{i=1,\ldots,\rk G},E^{\alpha}\;|\;\alpha\in\Phi\}
, where
i
indexes a set of simple roots
\alpha^{i}
and
H^{i}
is defined in the following way:

\exists!\;H^{i}\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}\;\big|\;\alpha^{i}(h)=\Tr(H^{i}h)\,,\;\forall\;h\in\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}\;.

(D.8)

The element
E^{\alpha}
is also normalized so that
\Tr E^{\alpha}E^{-\alpha}=1
. The compact real form is

\mathfrak{g}=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl\{iH^{i},E^{\alpha}-E^{-\alpha},i(E^{\alpha}+E^{-\alpha})\,\bigm|\,\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\bigr\}\;,

(D.9)

where
\Phi^{+}
is the set of positive roots. Using the fact that
\Tr
splits between each summand in
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(L_{\alpha}\oplus L_{-\alpha})
, and that
\Tr
is positive definite on
H^{i}
, it quickly follows that
\Tr
is negative (positive) definite on
\mathfrak{g}
(
i\mathfrak{g}
). Any
\Lambda\in i\mathfrak{g}
can be expressed with
\Lambda^{i}
,
\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha}
,
\Lambda_{2}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}\Lambda^{i}H^{i}+\sum\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\Bigl[\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha}(E^{\alpha}+E^{-\alpha})+\Lambda_{2}^{\alpha}i(E^{\alpha}-E^{-\alpha})\Bigr]

(D.10)

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i}\Lambda^{i}H^{i}+\sum\nolimits_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\bigl(\Lambda^{\alpha}E^{\alpha}+\overline{\Lambda^{\alpha}}E^{-\alpha}\bigr)\,,\qquad\Lambda^{\alpha}\equiv\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha}+i\Lambda_{2}^{\alpha}\;.

Therefore, defining a formal Hermitian conjugation on elements of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
as
\overline{H^{i}}\equiv H^{i}
,
\overline{E^{\alpha}}\equiv E^{-\alpha}
, we can alternatively define
i\mathfrak{g}
as
i\mathfrak{g}=\bigl\{\Lambda\in\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\,\big|\,\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda\bigr\}
. A generic group element
k=e^{i\Lambda}
then satisfies
\overline{k}=e^{-i\Lambda}=k^{-1}
. If
G=\mathrm{U}(N)
, this formal Hermitian conjugation becomes the actual conjugate transpose on
N\times N
matrices.

To build gauge interactions, we introduce the independent superfields
\Omega
and
V^{-}
.
\Omega
is valued in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
, while
V^{-}
is valued in
i\mathfrak{g}
,
i.e.
,
\overline{V^{-}}=V^{-}
. One can either use
\Omega
alone, or include
both

\Omega
and
V^{-}
in the theory. The crucial role played by
\Omega
is to allow for gauge-covariant chiral and Fermi conditions. Under gauge transformations, they transform as:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle e^{\Omega}\rightarrow k\,e^{\Omega}\,h^{-1}\;,\quad

\displaystyle V^{-}\rightarrow kV^{-}k^{-1}+ik(\partial_{t}k^{-1})\;,

(D.11)

\displaystyle h=e^{\chi}\;,

\displaystyle\chi:\mathbb{R}^{1|2}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}\;,\qquad\overline{D}\chi=0\;,

\displaystyle k=e^{i\Lambda}\;,

\displaystyle\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{1|2}\rightarrow i\mathfrak{g}\;,\qquad\quad\overline{\Lambda}=\Lambda\;.

Without loss of generality,
V^{-}
can be expanded as

V^{-}=A_{t}-\sigma-i\theta\overline{\lambda}-i\bar{\theta}\lambda+\theta\bar{\theta}D\;,

(D.12)

where
(A_{t}-\sigma,\,D)
are valued in
i\mathfrak{g}
and
\lambda
is valued in
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We now define the various ingredients used to construct supersymmetric actions. The gauge-covariant superspace derivatives are defined as

\mathcal{D}\,\equiv\,e^{-\overline{\Omega}}\,D\,e^{\overline{\Omega}}\;,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{D}}\,\equiv\,e^{\Omega}\,\overline{D}\,e^{-\Omega}\;,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}\,\equiv\,\partial_{t}-iV^{-}\;,

(D.13)

which, according to (
D.11
) and using
\overline{D}h=D\overline{h}=0
, transform as

\mathcal{D}\,\rightarrow\,k\mathcal{D}k^{-1}\;,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{D}}\,\rightarrow\,k\overline{\mathcal{D}}k^{-1}\;,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}\,\rightarrow\,k\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}k^{-1}\;.

(D.14)

They satisfy the algebra

\mathcal{D}^{2}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}=0\;,\qquad\{\mathcal{D},\overline{\mathcal{D}}\}=i(\partial_{t}-iV^{+})\,\equiv\,i\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\;,

(D.15)

where
V^{+}
is an
i\mathfrak{g}
-valued superfield constructed out of
\Omega
only:

V^{+}\equiv D\bigl[e^{\Omega}\bigl(\overline{D}e^{-\Omega}\bigr)\bigr]+\overline{D}\bigl[e^{-\overline{\Omega}}\bigl(De^{\overline{\Omega}}\bigr)\bigr]+\bigl\{e^{\Omega}\bigl(\overline{D}e^{-\Omega}\bigr),e^{-\overline{\Omega}}\bigl(De^{\overline{\Omega}}\bigr)\bigr\}\;.

(D.16)

If the gauge group is Abelian this simplifies to
V^{+}=-[D,\overline{D}]\,\Omega
. As it was for
D
and
\overline{D}
, one has
\overline{(\mathcal{D}X)}=(-1)^{F}\,\overline{\mathcal{D}}\,\overline{X}
and
\overline{(\overline{\mathcal{D}}X)}=(-1)^{F}\,\mathcal{D}\overline{X}
. One can check that the gauge transformation of
V^{+}
is identical to that of
V^{-}
:

V^{+}\,\rightarrow\,kV^{+}k^{-1}+ik(\partial_{t}k^{-1})\;,

(D.17)

which is consistent with (
D.14
) and (
D.15
). We will also have occasion to use the field strength superfield

\Upsilon\equiv[\overline{\mathcal{D}},\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}]=-i\overline{D}V^{-}-\partial_{t}\bigl[e^{\Omega}\bigr(\overline{D}e^{-\Omega}\bigr)\bigr]-i\bigl[e^{\Omega}\bigl(\overline{D}e^{-\Omega}\bigr),V^{-}\bigr]\;,

(D.18)

which also transforms covariantly as
\Upsilon\rightarrow k\Upsilon k^{-1}
. From the definition, it follows directly that
\overline{\mathcal{D}}\Upsilon=0
.

Instead of
\Omega
and
V^{-}
, we can equivalently use two other superfields
V
and
V^{-}_{h}
defined as

e^{V}\equiv e^{\overline{\Omega}}e^{\Omega}\;,\qquad V^{-}_{h}\,\equiv\,e^{\overline{\Omega}}\,V^{-}e^{\Omega}+\frac{i}{2}e^{\overline{\Omega}}\partial_{t}e^{\Omega}-\frac{i}{2}\bigl(\partial_{t}e^{\overline{\Omega}}\,\bigr)e^{\Omega}\;,\qquad\overline{V^{-}_{h}}=V^{-}_{h}\;,

(D.19)

which only transform under the complexified gauge transformations as:

e^{V}\,\rightarrow\,\overline{h}^{-1}e^{V}h^{-1}\;,\qquad V^{-}_{h}\,\rightarrow\,\overline{h}^{-1}V^{-}_{h}h^{-1}+\frac{i}{2}\overline{h}^{-1}e^{V}\partial_{t}h^{-1}-\frac{i}{2}\bigl(\partial_{t}\overline{h}^{-1}\bigr)e^{V}h^{-1}\;.

(D.20)

Note that
V
is constructed solely out of
\Omega
, while
V^{-}_{h}
is built out of both
V^{-}
and
\Omega
. In this formulation, the theory might contain
V
only, or both
V^{-}_{h}
and
V
. Analogously to the above, out of
V
and
V^{-}_{h}
we can construct

\displaystyle V^{+}_{h}

\displaystyle\equiv\,\frac{1}{2}e^{V}\overline{D}\bigl(e^{-V}De^{V}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}D\bigl(e^{V}\overline{D}e^{-V}\bigr)e^{V}=e^{\overline{\Omega}}V^{+}e^{\Omega}+\frac{i}{2}e^{\overline{\Omega}}\partial_{t}e^{\Omega}-\frac{i}{2}\bigl(\partial_{t}e^{\overline{\Omega}}\,\bigr)e^{\Omega}\;,

(D.21)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{h}

\displaystyle\equiv\,-i\,e^{V}\overline{D}\Bigl[e^{-V}\Bigl(V^{-}_{h}+\frac{i}{2}\partial_{t}e^{V}\Bigr)\Bigr]=e^{\overline{\Omega}}\Upsilon e^{\Omega}\;.

One can check that
V^{+}_{h}
transforms in the same way as
V^{-}_{h}
, and
\Upsilon_{h}
transforms in the same way as
e^{V}
. In an Abelian theory,

V^{+}_{h}=\frac{1}{2}e^{V}\bigl(\overline{D}D-D\overline{D}\bigr)V\;.

(D.22)

When writing matter Lagrangians in terms of
\Phi_{h}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{h}
which transform with chiral gauge transformations
h
, it will be convenient to use
V
and
V^{-}_{h}
.

Given any chiral or Fermi superfield, one can define covariantly-chiral counterparts

\Phi_{k}\,\equiv\,e^{\Omega}\,\Phi_{h}\;,\qquad\mathcal{Y}_{k}\,\equiv\,e^{\Omega}\mathcal{Y}_{h}\;,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{D}}\Phi_{k}=0\;,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{D}}\mathcal{Y}_{k}=E(\Phi_{k})\;,

(D.23)

which transform under the gauge group as
\Phi_{k}\rightarrow k\,\Phi_{k}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{k}\rightarrow k\,\mathcal{Y}_{k}
. These fields are useful when one is using
\Omega
and
V^{-}
to describe the vector multiplet.

D.3
Wess-Zumino gauge

We can expand
\Omega
and the gauge transformation parameters
\chi
,
\Lambda
as:

\Omega=\Omega_{0}+\theta\Omega_{\theta}+\bar{\theta}\Omega_{\bar{\theta}}+\theta\bar{\theta}\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}\,,\quad\;\;\chi=\chi_{0}+\theta\chi_{\theta}-\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\partial_{t}\chi_{0}\,,\quad\;\;\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}+\theta\Lambda_{\theta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{\Lambda}_{\theta}+\theta\bar{\theta}\Lambda_{\theta\bar{\theta}}\,.

(D.24)

We show that, using gauge transformations, every component of
\Omega
can be canceled except for
\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}
, and we can further set
\overline{\Omega}_{\theta\bar{\theta}}=\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}
,
i.e.
,
\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}
is valued in
i\mathfrak{g}
. We shall call this component
-\frac{1}{2}(A_{t}+\sigma)
, where both
A_{t}
and
\sigma
are valued in
i\mathfrak{g}
. Due to the relative sign, this is independent of
(A_{t}-\sigma)
in
V^{-}
. In other words, we can bring
\Omega
to the form

\Omega=-\frac{1}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,(A_{t}+\sigma)\;,

(D.25)

that we dub the Wess-Zumino gauge. First, we use the transformation
\chi=\Omega_{0}-\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\partial_{t}\Omega_{0}
,
\Lambda=0
to set
\Omega_{0}\rightarrow 0
, after which only transformations with
\chi_{0}=i\Lambda_{0}
preserve
\Omega_{0}=0
and are allowed. Next, performing the transformation
\chi=\theta(\Omega_{\theta}+\overline{\Omega}_{\bar{\theta}})
,
\Lambda=i\theta\overline{\Omega}_{\bar{\theta}}+i\bar{\theta}\Omega_{\bar{\theta}}
sets
\Omega_{\theta}
,
\Omega_{\bar{\theta}}\rightarrow 0
. Further transformation parameters cannot have
\theta
or
\bar{\theta}
components since otherwise a nonzero
\Omega_{\bar{\theta}}
would be generated. Lastly, we perform
\chi=0
,
\Lambda=\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}(\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}-\overline{\Omega}_{\theta\bar{\theta}})
, after which
\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}(\Omega_{\theta\bar{\theta}}+\overline{\Omega}_{\theta\bar{\theta}})
is valued in
i\mathfrak{g}
. The residual gauge transformations are
\chi=i\Lambda_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}\partial_{t}\Lambda_{0}
,
\Lambda=\Lambda_{0}
, under which

A_{t}+\sigma\;\rightarrow\;e^{i\Lambda_{0}}(A_{t}+\sigma)e^{-i\Lambda_{0}}+i\,e^{i\Lambda_{0}}\partial_{t}e^{-i\Lambda_{0}}\;.

(D.26)

These are purely time-dependent gauge transformations, as expected. In this gauge, the gauge-covariant superspace derivatives simplify to

\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}=D_{t}^{+}=\partial_{t}-i(A_{t}+\sigma)\;,\qquad\mathcal{D}=\partial_{\theta}-\frac{i}{2}\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\;,\qquad\overline{\mathcal{D}}=-\partial_{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{i}{2}\theta D_{t}^{+}\;,

(D.27)

and

V^{+}=A_{t}+\sigma\;,\qquad\qquad\Upsilon=\lambda-\theta\bigl(D_{t}\sigma+iD\bigr)-\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\lambda\;.

(D.28)

The action of supersymmetry on
\Omega
, using (
D.1
), is
\delta\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\bar{\theta}(A_{t}+\sigma)-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\epsilon}\theta(A_{t}+\sigma)
and the Wess-Zumino gauge is not preserved. This can be compensated by an infinitesimal gauge transformation with parameters

\Lambda=\frac{i}{2}\,\epsilon\bar{\theta}(A_{t}+\sigma)+\frac{i}{2}\bar{\epsilon}\theta(A_{t}+\sigma)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\;,\qquad\chi=-\bar{\epsilon}\theta(A_{t}+\sigma)+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\;.

(D.29)

The supersymmetry transformations that preserve Wess-Zumino gauge are computed using
\delta
with the addition of the compensating gauge transformation above. For
\Omega
, its variation under the combined supersymmetry and gauge transformation is
\delta\Omega+i\Lambda-\chi=0
by construction. The superfields
\Phi_{k}
,
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
are only sensitive to the gauge transformations generated by
\Lambda
, and not to those generated by
\chi
. The addition of the
\Lambda
-transformation (
D.29
) to
\delta
can be directly absorbed into the supercharges:

Q_{\text{WZ}}\equiv\partial_{\theta}+\frac{i}{2}\,\bar{\theta}\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr]\;,\qquad\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\equiv-\partial_{\bar{\theta}}-\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr]\;.

(D.30)

Note that
\delta_{\text{gauge}}(\Lambda)
acts according to the gauge representation of each superfield, except for
V^{\pm}
, on which
\delta_{\text{gauge}}(\Lambda)\,V^{\pm}=\partial_{t}\Lambda-i[V^{\pm},\Lambda]
. The modified supercharges satisfy the algebra

Q_{\text{WZ}}^{2}=\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}^{2}=0\;,\qquad\{Q_{\text{WZ}},\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\}=-i\bigl[\partial_{t}-\delta_{\text{gauge}}(A_{t}+\sigma)\bigr]\;.

(D.31)

D.4
Transformations in Wess-Zumino gauge

Acting with (
D.30
) on
V^{\pm}
and reading off the variations of each component, we find the following supersymmetry variations (and their complex conjugate) for the vector multiplet:

\displaystyle Q_{\text{WZ}}\,A_{t}

\displaystyle=-Q_{\text{WZ}}\,\sigma=-\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\lambda}\;,\qquad

\displaystyle Q_{\text{WZ}}\,\lambda

\displaystyle=-D_{t}\sigma-iD\;,

(D.32)

\displaystyle Q_{\text{WZ}}\,D

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}D_{t}^{+}\overline{\lambda}\;,

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\,\lambda

\displaystyle=0\;.

Note that
Q_{\text{WZ}}(A_{t}+\sigma)=\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}(A_{t}+\sigma)=0
, consistently with (
D.31
). In Wess-Zumino gauge,
\Phi_{k}
and its conjugate
\overline{\Phi}_{k}
have expansion:

\Phi_{k}=\phi+\theta\psi-\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\phi\;,\qquad\qquad\overline{\Phi}_{k}=\overline{\phi}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{\psi}+\frac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\overline{\phi}\;.

(D.33)

Acting with (
D.30
) on
\Phi_{k}
we find the following supersymmetry variations:

Q_{\text{WZ}}\,\phi=\psi\;,\qquad Q_{\text{WZ}}\,\psi=0\;,\qquad\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\,\phi=0\;,\qquad\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\,\psi=iD_{t}^{+}\phi\;.

(D.34)

Alternatively, we can obtain the same variations by acting with
\delta+\chi=-\epsilon\,Q_{\text{WZ}}+\bar{\epsilon}\,\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}
on
\Phi_{h}
, with
\chi
given in (
D.29
). Analogously,
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
and its conjugate
\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{k}
have the expansions

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{k}

\displaystyle=\eta-\theta f-\bar{\theta}E(\phi)+\theta\bar{\theta}\Bigl(\partial_{a}E(\phi)\psi_{a}-\tfrac{i}{2}D_{t}^{+}\eta\Bigr)=\eta-\theta f-\bar{\theta}E(\Phi)-\tfrac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\eta

(D.35)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\overline{\eta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{f}-\theta\overline{E}(\overline{\phi})+\theta\bar{\theta}\Bigl(\overline{\psi}_{a}\overline{\partial}_{a}\overline{E}(\overline{\phi})+\tfrac{i}{2}D_{t}^{+}\overline{\eta}\Bigr)=\overline{\eta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{f}-\theta\overline{E}(\overline{\Phi})+\tfrac{i}{2}\theta\bar{\theta}D_{t}^{+}\overline{\eta}\;,

and acting with (
D.30
) gives the supersymmetry variations:

\displaystyle Q_{\text{WZ}}\,\eta

\displaystyle=-f\;,\quad

\displaystyle Q_{\text{WZ}}\,f

\displaystyle=0\;,\quad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\,\eta

\displaystyle=E(\phi)\;,\quad

\displaystyle\overline{Q}_{\text{WZ}}\,f

\displaystyle=-iD_{t}^{+}\eta+\partial_{a}E(\phi)\,\psi_{a}\;.

(D.36)

Again, we can obtain the same variations by acting with
\delta+\chi
on
\mathcal{Y}_{h}
.

D.5
Supersymmetric Lagrangians

As with the prototypical
4
d
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry, there are two broad classes of supersymmetric terms: D-terms and F-terms. Let
X
be a bosonic, gauge-invariant, real-valued superfield with expansion

X=X_{0}+\theta X_{\theta}-\bar{\theta}\,\overline{X_{\theta}}+\theta\bar{\theta}X_{\theta\bar{\theta}}\;.

(D.37)

Acting with
Q
and
\overline{Q}
, we find that
QX_{\theta\bar{\theta}}=-\frac{i}{2}\partial_{t}X_{\theta}
and
\overline{Q}X_{\theta\bar{\theta}}=\frac{i}{2}\partial_{t}\overline{X_{\theta}}
are total derivatives. Moreover,
Q\overline{Q}X_{0}=X_{\theta\bar{\theta}}
up to a total derivative. Therefore,

\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,X=-X_{\theta\bar{\theta}}=Q\overline{Q}\,(-X_{0})

(D.38)

is supersymmetric, and we call such terms D-terms. They are always
Q
and
\overline{Q}
exact. Conversely, suppose there is a term in the Lagrangian of the form
Q\overline{Q}(-X_{0})
where
X_{0}
is real and gauge invariant. If there is a real-valued superfield
X
with bottom component
X_{0}
, it must have the same expansion (
D.37
). Therefore (
D.38
) holds and this term can be written as a D-term in superspace.

Let
Y
be a fermionic, gauge-invariant, complex-valued chiral superfield,
\overline{\mathcal{D}}Y=\overline{D}Y=0
. Its complex conjugate
\overline{Y}
is anti-chiral and satisfies
D\overline{Y}=0
. They have expansion:

Y=Y_{0}+\theta Y_{\theta}-\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}Y_{0}\;,\qquad\qquad\overline{Y}=\overline{Y_{0}}+\bar{\theta}\,\overline{Y_{\theta}}+\frac{i}{2}\,\theta\bar{\theta}\,\partial_{t}\overline{Y_{0}}\;.

(D.39)

Acting with
Q
and
\overline{Q}
on
Y
and
\overline{Y}
, one finds that
Y_{\theta}
and
\overline{Y_{\theta}}
are separately supersymmetric up to total derivatives. Moreover,
Y_{\theta}=QY_{0}
and
\overline{Y_{\theta}}=-\overline{Q}\,\overline{Y_{0}}
. Therefore:

\int\!d\theta\,Y+\int\!d\bar{\theta}\,\overline{Y}=Y_{\theta}+\overline{Y_{\theta}}=Q\,Y_{0}-\overline{Q}\,\overline{Y_{0}}=(Q+\overline{Q})(Y_{0}-\overline{Y_{0}})

(D.40)

is supersymmetric, and we call such terms F-terms. They are always
(Q+\overline{Q})
exact.

We can now write the following supersymmetric Lagrangians, with component expressions in Wess-Zumino gauge. In the gauge sector, if the theory only contains
\Omega
or equivalently
V
, the only term we can think of is a Wilson line in
A_{t}+\sigma
. For a
\mathrm{U}(1)
gauge group, the supersymmetric Wilson loop of charge
q
can be written as

\exp\biggl(iq\oint dt\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;V\biggr)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,\exp\biggl(iq\oint dt\,(A_{t}+\sigma)\biggr)\;.

(D.41)

If both
V^{-}
and
\Omega
are present, we can write the following terms. The conventional gauge kinetic term is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2e^{2}_{\text{1d}}}\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\overline{\Upsilon}\Upsilon=\frac{1}{2e^{2}_{\text{1d}}}\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\overline{\Upsilon_{h}}e^{-V}\Upsilon_{h}e^{-V}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2e^{2}_{\text{1d}}}\Tr\Bigl[(D_{t}\sigma)^{2}+D^{2}+i\overline{\lambda}D_{t}^{+}\lambda\Bigr]\;.

(D.42)

Note that the superfield
V^{-}-V^{+}
transforms covariantly,
V^{-}-V^{+}\rightarrow k\,(V^{-}-V^{+})\,k^{-1}
, under gauge transformations. For an adjoint-invariant form
\zeta:i\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the Fayet-Iliopoulos term is:

\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\zeta\bigl(V^{-}-V^{+}\bigr)=\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\zeta\Bigl(\bigl(V^{-}_{h}-V^{+}_{h}\bigr)\,e^{-V}\Bigr)\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,-\zeta(D)\;.

(D.43)

If the gauge group is Abelian,
V^{+}_{h}e^{-V}=\frac{1}{2}(\overline{D}D-D\overline{D})V
becomes a total derivative under the superspace integral. Therefore, FI terms for Abelian gauge groups can be written as

\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\zeta\bigl(V^{-}_{h}e^{-V}\bigr)\;.

(D.44)

We can also write a mass term that gaps
V^{-}
(or equivalently the gaugino and
\sigma
):

-\frac{1}{2}\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\bigl(V^{-}-V^{+}\bigr)^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigl(\bigl(V^{-}_{h}-V^{+}_{h}\bigr)\,e^{-V}\Bigr)^{2}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,\Tr\bigl(\overline{\lambda}\lambda-2\sigma D\bigr)\;.

(D.45)

Moving on to the matter sector, the conventional kinetic term for a chiral multiplet is:

\displaystyle i\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\Phi}_{k}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{-}\Phi_{k}

\displaystyle=\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\biggl(\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,e^{V}\partial_{t}\Phi_{h}-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial_{t}\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,e^{V}\Phi_{h}+\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,V^{-}_{h}\,\Phi_{h}

(D.46)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}-\overline{\phi}\bigl(D_{t}^{2}+\sigma^{2}+D\bigr)\phi+i\overline{\psi}D_{t}^{-}\psi+i\overline{\phi}\lambda\psi-i\overline{\psi}\,\overline{\lambda}\phi\;,

where
D_{t}^{-}\equiv\partial_{t}-i(A_{t}-\sigma)
. It requires the presence of both
V^{-}
and
\Omega
. Alternatively, we can write a kinetic term that couples to
V^{+}
in place of
V^{-}
, in which case only
\Omega
(or
V
) is required:

\displaystyle i\!\int\!\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\Phi}_{k}\mathcal{D}_{t}^{+}\Phi_{k}

\displaystyle=\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\biggl(\frac{i}{2}\,\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,e^{V}\partial_{t}\Phi_{h}-\frac{i}{2}\,\partial_{t}\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,e^{V}\Phi_{h}+\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,V^{+}_{h}\,\Phi_{h}\biggr)

(D.47)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}D_{t}^{+}\overline{\phi}\,D_{t}^{+}\phi+i\overline{\psi}D_{t}^{+}\psi\;.

We can also write a term with a first-order action for
\phi
, and it only requires
\Omega
:

\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\Phi}_{k}\Phi_{k}=\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\Phi_{h}}\,e^{V}\Phi_{h}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,i\,\overline{\phi}D_{t}^{+}\phi+\overline{\psi}\psi\;.

(D.48)

The conventional kinetic term for a Fermi multiplet is

\int\!\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{k}\mathcal{Y}_{k}=\int\!\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{h}}e^{V}\mathcal{Y}_{h}\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,i\overline{\eta}D_{t}^{+}\eta+\overline{f}f-\bigl\lvert E(\phi)\bigr\rvert^{2}-\overline{\eta}\,\partial_{a}E(\phi)\,\psi_{a}-\overline{\psi}_{a}\,\overline{\partial}_{a}\overline{E}(\overline{\phi})\,\eta\,,

(D.49)

and it only requires
\Omega
. If present, terms in
E(\Phi)
that are linear in the chiral superfields
\Phi_{a}
give rise to mass terms which gap out the chiral and Fermi multiplets together. Quadratic or higher-order terms in
E(\Phi)
produce cubic or higher-order interactions. We shall call them E-interactions. Suppose now that we have a collection of Fermi superfields
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
with
\overline{D}\mathcal{Y}_{i}=E_{i}(\Phi)
. In addition to
E_{i}
, we associate another holomorphic function
J_{i}(\Phi)
of the chiral superfields to each Fermi such that
E_{i}J_{i}
(with repeated indices summed) is gauge invariant and
E_{i}J_{i}=0
. Then
\mathcal{Y}_{i}\,J_{i}(\Phi)
is a gauge-invariant fermionic chiral superfield. We can therefore write the F-terms:

\int\!\!d\theta\;\mathcal{Y}_{i}\,J_{i}(\Phi)+\!\int\!\!d\bar{\theta}\;\overline{\mathcal{Y}_{i}}\,\overline{J}_{i}(\overline{\Phi})=-f_{i}J_{i}(\phi)-\eta_{i}\,\partial_{a}J_{i}(\phi)\,\psi_{a}-\overline{f}_{i}\,\overline{J}_{i}(\overline{\phi})-\overline{\psi}_{a}\,\overline{\partial}_{a}\overline{J}_{i}(\overline{\phi})\,\overline{\eta}_{i}\;.

(D.50)

Note that because
\mathcal{Y}_{i}J_{i}
is gauge invariant,
\mathcal{Y}_{i,h}J_{i}(\Phi_{h})=\mathcal{Y}_{i,k}J_{i}(\Phi_{k})
. We will call interactions that are constructed in this way J-interactions.

D.6
Twisted 3d Yang-Mills and Chern-Simons terms

In this subsection, we show how the parts of the topologically twisted 3d Yang-Mills and Chern-Simons Lagrangians containing
\Xi_{\bar{1}}
can be written in 1d superspace. The terms lie slightly beyond the scope of the exposition above, because
\Xi_{\bar{1}}
transforms as a connection on
S^{2}
under gauge transformations, as reported in (
3.7
).

Yang-Mills.

The first line in (
3.8
) can be written in superspace as:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\Tr\Bigl[4\lvert F_{t\bar{1}}\rvert^{2}+4iDF_{1\bar{1}}-4|D_{\bar{1}}\sigma|^{2}+i\overline{\Lambda}_{1}(D_{t}+i\sigma\Lambda_{\bar{1}}+2\Lambda_{t}\,D_{1}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}-2\overline{\Lambda}_{1}\,D_{\bar{1}}\overline{\Lambda}_{t}\Bigr]

(D.51)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}\,4i\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\;\Tr\Bigl(\Xi_{1,k}\,\partial_{t}\Xi_{\bar{1},k}-\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}\,V^{-}\Bigr)\,,

where we defined the superfield

\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}\,\equiv\,\partial_{1}\Xi_{\bar{1},k}-\partial_{\bar{1}}\Xi_{1,k}-i\bigl[\Xi_{1,k},\Xi_{\bar{1},k}\bigr]\;.

(D.52)

Here
\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}
transforms covariantly under super-gauge transformations as
\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}\mapsto k\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}k^{-1}
. Note that the superspace expression has the same form as a Chern-Simons term for superfields, with
V^{-}
playing the role of the connection along
t
. Therefore, under finite gauge transformations:

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{gauge}}\;4i\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigl(\Xi_{1,k}\,\partial_{t}\Xi_{\bar{1},k}-\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},k}\,V^{-}\Bigr)=2i\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr k^{-1}\partial_{t}k\,\bigl[k^{-1}\partial_{1}k,\,k^{-1}\partial_{\bar{1}}k]\;,

\displaystyle\qquad=2i\Tr\partial_{t}\partial_{\theta}\Bigl(k^{-1}\partial_{\bar{\theta}}k\bigl[k^{-1}\partial_{1}k,\,k^{-1}\partial_{\bar{1}}k\bigr]\Bigr)+\text{cyclic}\;.

(D.53)

The omitted terms contain cyclic permutations of
(t,1,\bar{1})
. This gauge variation looks like a winding number for super-gauge transformations. Since we are taking derivatives of the winding number density (albeit with respect to fermionic variables), a total derivative is expected because the winding number is homotopy invariant.

Alternatively, we can use superfields which are only sensitive to complexified gauge transformations. The superspace expression in (
D.51
) can then be written as

\eqref{YM chiral part k transf}=4i\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigl(\Xi_{1,h}\,\partial_{t}\Xi_{\bar{1},h}-\mathcal{F}_{1\bar{1},h}\,e^{-V}V^{-}_{h}\Bigr)\;,

(D.54)

where total derivatives of the kind (
D.53
) have been neglected. One can check that (
D.54
) is real and gauge invariant up to total derivatives.

Chern-Simons.

We now want to write the first piece of (
3.9
) in superspace. To do this, we follow a similar procedure as in
[
56
]
. First, the fields
X
are extended to be functions
\widehat{X}
of an auxiliary coordinate
y\in(0,1)
in an arbitrary way, except that they must fulfil boundary conditions

\widehat{X}(\theta,\varphi,t,y=0)=0\;,\qquad\widehat{X}(\theta,\varphi,t,y=1)=X(\theta,\varphi,t)\;.

(D.55)

Extended quantities will be denoted with a hat. Given (
D.55
), we have:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{CS},\Xi}\Big|_{\text{WZ}}=\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CS},\Xi}(y=1)\Big|_{\text{WZ}}=\int_{0}^{1}\!dy\;\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CS},\Xi}\Big|_{\text{WZ}}\;.

(D.56)

Now,
\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CS},\Xi}
can be written in superspace as:

\displaystyle\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CS},\Xi}\Big|_{\text{WZ}}

\displaystyle=\Tr\Bigl[-4i\partial_{y}(\hat{A}_{t}+\hat{\sigma})\hat{F}_{1\bar{1}}+4\partial_{y}\hat{A}_{1}\bigl(i\partial_{t}\hat{A}_{\bar{1}}-i\hat{D}_{\bar{1}}(\hat{A}_{t}+\hat{\sigma})\bigr)+\partial_{y}\hat{\overline{\Lambda}}_{1}\hat{\Lambda}_{\bar{1}}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+4\partial_{y}\hat{A}_{\bar{1}}\bigl(-i\partial_{t}\hat{A}_{1}+i\hat{D}_{1}(\hat{A}_{t}+\hat{\sigma})\bigr)-\partial_{y}\hat{\Lambda}_{\bar{1}}\hat{\overline{\Lambda}}_{1}\Bigr]

\displaystyle=4\partial_{y}\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigl[\hat{\Xi}_{1,\overline{h}}\,\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}-i\hat{V}\Bigl(\partial_{1}\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}-\partial_{\bar{1}}\hat{\Xi}_{1,\overline{h}}-i\bigl[\hat{\Xi}_{1,\overline{h}},\,\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}\bigr]\Bigr)\Bigr]\;.

(D.57)

This superspace expression is only valid in Wess-Zumino gauge where
V=-\theta\bar{\theta}(A_{t}+\sigma)
, and it is not invariant under super-gauge transformations. Even so, we can take it as a starting point for constructing the gauge-invariant completion. A gauge-invariant expression that reduces to the above in Wess-Zumino gauge is

\partial_{y}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{CS},\Xi}=4\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigr[-i\,e^{-\hat{V}}\partial_{y}\big(e^{\hat{V}}\big)\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{1\bar{1},h}+\hat{\Xi}_{1,h}\,\partial_{y}\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}+\partial_{y}\hat{\Xi}_{1,\overline{h}}\,\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},\overline{h}}\Bigr]\;.

(D.58)

One can check that the first term is Hermitian, while the second and third terms are Hermitian conjugates of each other. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{CS},\Xi}

\displaystyle=\,\Tr\Bigl[4iA_{1}\partial_{t}A_{\bar{1}}-4i(A_{t}+\sigma)F_{1\bar{1}}+\overline{\Lambda}_{1}\Lambda_{\bar{1}}\Bigr]

(D.59)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{WZ}}}{{=}}4\int_{0}^{1}\!dy\,d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\Bigl[-i\,e^{-\hat{V}}\partial_{y}\big(e^{\hat{V}}\big)\hat{\mathcal{F}}_{1\bar{1},h}+\hat{\Xi}_{1,h}\,\partial_{y}\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}+\partial_{y}\hat{\Xi}_{1,\overline{h}}\,\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},\overline{h}}\Bigr]\;.

If the gauge group is Abelian, (
D.58
) is a total derivative in
y
and the auxiliary coordinate
y
can be eliminated to give

\mathcal{L}_{\text{CS},\Xi}=4\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\biggl[\Xi_{1,\overline{h}}\,\Xi_{\bar{1},h}-iV\bigl(\partial_{1}\Xi_{\bar{1},h}-\partial_{\bar{1}}\Xi_{1,\overline{h}}\bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{1}V\,\partial_{\bar{1}}V\biggr]\;.

(D.60)

For non-Abelian gauge groups there is no compact expression for the integral in
y
, but we can expand in powers of
V
. Choosing

\hat{\Xi}_{\bar{1},h}=y\,\Xi_{\bar{1},h}\;,\qquad\qquad\hat{V}=y\,V\;,

(D.61)

one obtains the following expression up to quadratic terms in
V
:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{CS},\Xi}=4\!\int\!d\theta d\bar{\theta}\,\Tr\biggl[\Xi_{1,\overline{h}}\,\Xi_{\bar{1},h}-iV\Bigl(\partial_{1}\Xi_{\bar{1},h}-\partial_{\bar{1}}\Xi_{1,\overline{h}}-i\bigl[\Xi_{1,\overline{h}},\,\Xi_{\bar{1},h}\bigr]\Bigr)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\Bigl(\partial_{1}V-i\bigl[\Xi_{1,\overline{h}},V\bigr]\Bigr)\Bigl(\partial_{\bar{1}}V-i\bigl[\Xi_{\bar{1},h},V\bigr]\Bigr)+\mathcal{O}(V^{3})\biggr]\;.

(D.62)

Appendix E
Partial gauge fixing

In this appendix we follow
[
47
]
and review the general procedure for partial gauge fixing. Let
\mathcal{G}
be the infinite-dimensional group of gauge transformations, and
\{e_{A}\}
a Hermitian basis for its algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote the structure constants of
\mathfrak{g}
as
[e_{A},e_{B}]=if_{ABC}\,e_{C}
. The basis
\{e_{A}\}
is also chosen such that it is orthonormal under the inner product

\int\Tr\,(e_{A}\,e_{B})=\delta_{AB}\;.

(E.1)

Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{G}
be a subgroup, which will be the group of residual gauge transformations after partial gauge fixing. We call its algebra
\mathfrak{r}\subset\mathfrak{g}
(
\mathfrak{r}
stands for residual). We split the basis as
\{e_{A}\}=\{e_{i},e_{a}\}
, where
\{e_{i}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{r}
whereas
\{e_{a}\}
is a basis for
\mathfrak{f}\cong\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{r}
(
\mathfrak{f}
stands for gauge fixed). Since
\mathcal{R}
is a subgroup,
\mathfrak{r}
is a subalgebra and
[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{r}]\subset\mathfrak{r}
, or
f_{ija}=0
. By anti-symmetry of the structure constants this implies
f_{iaj}=0
, or
[\mathfrak{r},\mathfrak{f}]\subset\mathfrak{f}
. In summary, the algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
decomposes as

[e_{i},e_{j}]=if_{ijk}\,e_{k}\;,\qquad[e_{i},e_{a}]=if_{iab}\,e_{b}\;,\qquad[e_{a},e_{b}]=if_{abi}\,e_{i}+if_{abc}\,e_{c}\;.

(E.2)

In particular, this implies that the
e_{a}
’s transform under the adjoint action in a real orthogonal representation of
\mathcal{R}
, which we call
R_{f}
.

In order to fix
\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{R}
, we need to choose as many gauge-fixing conditions as there are generators in
\mathfrak{f}
. In other words, we need to choose gauge-fixing functions
G_{\text{gf}}^{a}(X)
, where
X
collectively denotes the physical fields in chiral and vector multiplets. Notice that
G_{\text{gf}}^{a}(X)
should transform in
R_{f}
under
\mathcal{R}
. This is true for all the gauge-fixing functions we can think of. The first step in the gauge-fixing procedure is to integrate in an adjoint scalar
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{g}
, and add
\int\frac{1}{2}\Tr\Lambda^{2}
to the action. Notice that
\Lambda
will have mass dimension
[\Lambda]=3/2
. Since
\Lambda
is completely decoupled from everything else, introducing it does not change the path integral. We then insert
1
in the path integral, written as

1=\Delta(X,\Lambda)\int_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{D}g\,\prod_{a}\,\delta\bigl(G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X^{g})-(\Lambda^{g})^{a}\bigr)\;,

(E.3)

where superscripts
(\cdot)^{g}
denote a finite gauge transformation by
g
. Suppose that
g_{X,\Lambda}\in\mathcal{G}
satisfies
G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X^{g_{X,\Lambda}})-(\Lambda^{g_{X,\Lambda}})^{a}=0
, then so does
rg_{X,\Lambda}
for any
r\in\mathcal{R}
, due to the covariant transformations of
G^{a}_{\text{gf}}
and
\Lambda^{a}
under
\mathcal{R}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{R}
remains as the residual gauge group. Notice that it is necessary for
\Lambda
to transform under gauge transformations. This is different from the standard Faddeev-Popov procedure, in which
\Lambda
is only integrated over at the very last step. That would have been sufficient if the gauge were completely fixed (
\mathcal{R}=0
). The slightly different procedure described here will produce extra interaction terms in the ghost action. Now, as usual, the invariance of
\mathcal{D}g
ensures that the determinant
\Delta
is gauge invariant, and

\Delta(X,\Lambda)^{-1}=\Delta(X^{g_{X,\Lambda}},\Lambda^{g_{X,\Lambda}})^{-1}=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\mathcal{D}g\,\prod_{a}\,\delta\Bigl(G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X^{g\cdot g_{X,\Lambda}})-(\Lambda^{g\cdot g_{X,\Lambda}})^{a}\Bigr)\;.

(E.4)

Assuming no Gribov copies and writing
g=1+\epsilon^{A}e_{A}
,
\delta_{A}\equiv\delta_{\text{gauge}}(e_{A})
, one can expand the argument of the delta function to linear order in
\epsilon^{A}
and obtain
\epsilon^{b}\,\delta_{b}\bigl[G_{\text{gf}}(X^{g_{X,\Lambda}})-\Lambda^{g_{X,\Lambda}}\bigr]^{a}
.
The fact that the terms with
\epsilon^{i}
disappear ensures that
\text{Vol}(\mathcal{R})
is factorized as an overall factor in the Faddeev-Popov determinant:

\Delta(X,\Lambda)=\det\delta_{b}\Bigl[G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X^{g_{X,\Lambda}})-(\Lambda^{g_{X,\Lambda}})^{a}\Bigr]/\,\text{Vol}(\mathcal{R})\;.

(E.5)

The determinant can be shown to be well-defined on the coset
\mathcal{R}g_{X,\Lambda}
. Having determined
\Delta(X,\Lambda)
, inserting
1
in the path integral gives

\int\!\mathcal{D}X\,\mathcal{D}\Lambda\,\mathcal{D}g\;e^{iS(X)-\frac{i}{2}\!\int\Tr\Lambda^{2}}\;\Delta(X,\Lambda)\,\prod_{a}\,\delta\bigl(G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X^{g})-(\Lambda^{g})^{a}\bigr)\;.

(E.6)

Undoing the gauge transformation in the delta function, the integral over the gauge group factorizes and one gets

\int\!\mathcal{D}X\,\mathcal{D}\Lambda\;e^{iS(X)-\frac{i}{2}\!\int\Tr\Lambda^{2}}\det\bigl(\delta_{b}G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X)-\delta_{b}\Lambda^{a}\bigr)\,\prod_{a}\,\delta\bigl(G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X)-\Lambda^{a}\bigr)\;.

(E.7)

By means of
\delta_{b}\Lambda^{a}=i\Lambda^{A}[e_{b},e_{A}]^{a}=-\Lambda^{A}f_{bAa}=-f_{abi}\Lambda^{i}-f_{abc}\Lambda^{c}
we can explicitly write:

\displaystyle\det\Bigl(\delta_{b}G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X)-\delta_{b}\Lambda^{a}\Bigr)=\int\biggl(\prod_{a}\mathcal{D}\widetilde{c}^{\,a}\,\mathcal{D}c^{a}\biggr)\,\exp\!\Bigl[-\widetilde{c}^{\,a}\Bigl(\delta_{b}G^{a}_{\text{gf}}(X)+f_{abi}\Lambda^{i}+f_{abc}\Lambda^{c}\Bigr)\,c^{b}\Bigr]\;,

(E.8)

where we have introduced the Grassmann scalars
c^{a}
,
\widetilde{c}^{\,a}
. Note that they are valued in
\mathfrak{f}
and not in
\mathfrak{g}
: modes corresponding to residual gauge transformations are not present. Also note that by dimensional analysis,
[\,\widetilde{c}\,]+[c]=[G_{\text{gf}}]=3/2
. Without loss of generality, we can take
[c]=0
,
[\,\widetilde{c}\,]=3/2
. Integrating out
\Lambda^{i}
and imposing the delta functions for
\Lambda^{a}
, one gets the action:

S(X)+\int\Tr\biggl[-\frac{G^{2}_{\text{gf}}}{2}+G_{\text{gf}}\bigl\{\widetilde{c},c\bigr\}+i\,\widetilde{c}\,\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)\,G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{1}{2}\{\widetilde{c},c\}_{\mathfrak{r}}\{\widetilde{c},c\}_{\mathfrak{r}}\biggr]\;.

(E.9)

This is equivalent to the following action with extra scalars
b^{a}
integrated in:

S(X)+\int\Tr\biggl[\frac{b^{2}}{2}+b\Bigl(G_{\text{gf}}-\{\widetilde{c},c\}\Bigr)+i\,\widetilde{c}\,\delta_{\text{gauge}}(c)\,G_{\text{gf}}+\frac{1}{2}\{\widetilde{c},c\}^{2}\biggr]\;.

(E.10)

Notice that
b^{a}
have dimension
[b]=3/2
. One should keep in mind that
c,\widetilde{c},b
only contain modes in
\mathfrak{f}
. We will now rescale

G_{\text{gf}}\,\rightarrow\,e^{-1}_{\text{3d}}\,G_{\text{gf}}\;\qquad b\,\rightarrow\,e^{-1}_{\text{3d}}\,b\;,\qquad c\,\rightarrow\,e^{-1}_{\text{3d}}\,c\;,

(E.11)

after which
[G_{\text{gf}}]=2
,
[c]=\frac{1}{2}
, and
[b]=2
. The gauge-fixing action gains an overall factor of
1/e^{2}_{\text{3d}}
. This is useful because the background Coulomb gauge
G_{\text{gf}}=D_{i}^{B}A^{i}/\sqrt{\xi}
(with
\xi
a positive dimensionless parameter) that we choose in the main text has dimension
[G_{\text{gf}}]=2
. This is true for many other standard gauge-fixing functions, such as the Lorenz gauge
\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu}/\sqrt{\xi}
and the background Lorenz gauge
D_{\mu}^{B}A^{\mu}/\sqrt{\xi}
.

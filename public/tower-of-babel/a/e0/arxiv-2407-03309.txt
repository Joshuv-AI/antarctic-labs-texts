Unimodality and peak location of the characteristic polynomials of two distance matrices of trees
Rakesh Jana, Iswar Mahato, Sivaramakrishnan Sivasubramanian

Abstract
Unimodality of the normalized coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of distance matrices of trees are known and bounds on the location of its peak (the largest coefficient) are also known. Recently, an extension of these results to distance matrices of block graphs was given. In this work, we extend these results to two additional distance-type matrices associated with trees: the Min-4PC matrix and the 2-Steiner distance matrix. We show that the sequences of coefficients of the characteristic polynomials of these matrices are both unimodal and log-concave. Moreover, we find the peak location for the coefficients of the characteristic polynomials of the Min-4PC matrix of any tree on n n vertices. Further, we show that the Min-4PC matrix of any tree on n n vertices is isometrically embeddable in ℝ n − 1 \mathbb{R}^{n-1} equipped with the ℓ 1 \ell_{1} norm.

Introduction

Let
A=a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}
be a sequence of real numbers. The sequence
A
is called

unimodal
if there exists an index
k
with
1\leq k\leq m-1

such that
a_{j-1}\leq a_{j}

when
j\leq k
and
a_{j}\geq a_{j+1}
when
j\geq k
. The sequence
A

is called
log-concave
if

a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
when
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Log-concavity and unimodality are significant properties with application across various areas; for example, algebra (see
[
branden-unimodality_log_concavity
]
by Brändén and
[
stanley1989log
]
by Stanley), probability theory (see
[
prekopa1971logarithmic
]
by Prekopa), combinatorics and geometry
[
stanley1989log
]
. These applications emphasize the importance of understanding and identifying log-concave sequences in different mathematical contexts. For the distance matrix of a tree, Graham and Lovász in
[
graham-lovasz
, page 83]
conjectured unimodality of the normalized coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of the distance matrix of trees and also conjectured the location of the peak(s). The unimodality part was proved by Aalipour et. al. in

[
aalipour-hogben-etal-conjecture-graham-lovasz
]
and the peak location was disproved by Collins in
[
collins-disproof-graham-lovasz-peak
]
.

Two points are noteworthy. Firstly,
for a square matrix
M
, the definition of its characteristic
polynomial used in all earlier papers is
\chi_{M}(x)=\det(M-xI)

and this does not always make

\chi_{M}(x)
monic. We thus change the definition slightly
and define

\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)=\det(xI-M).

(1)

Doing this small change helps us get rid of the need to multiply
its coefficients by a power of
(-1)
with the exponent depending on
n
.
Hence, for the rest of this paper, for square matrices
M
, we define

\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
using (
1
) and make the
needed small changes to results before quoting them
from the literature.

Secondly, normalizing the coefficients of the characteristic
polynomial is not important for unimodality as we get similar
results by scaling the coefficients
c_{k}
of the characteristic polynomial
by
\alpha^{k}
, where
\alpha
is a positive real number. This point is also mentioned by both
Abiad et. al
in
[
Abiad-Brimkov-Hayat-Khramova-Koolen-dist-char-poly
, Section 4]

and by Aalipour et. al in

[
aalipour-hogben-etal-conjecture-graham-lovasz
, Observation 1.3]
.
However, to determine the peak location of a unimodal sequence (or for
bounds on the peak location), it is important to know whether we
take the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial or its
normalized version.
In this paper, for results on the peak location, we consider the sequence
of coefficients of
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
without any normalization.
Abiad et. al in

[
Abiad-Brimkov-Hayat-Khramova-Koolen-dist-char-poly
]
also
give their results for the coefficients of the un-normalized characteristic polynomial.

Let
T
be a tree with
n
vertices and let
D
be its distance
matrix. Let
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{D}(x)=\det(xI-D)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}x^{k}

be

’s characteristic polynomial. By definition, as

\mathrm{CharPoly}_{D}(x)
is a monic polynomial,
c_{n}=1
.
We further have
c_{n-1}=0
as
c_{n-1}=\mathrm{Trace}(D)
which is
zero (as all diagonal entries of distance matrices are zero). In
[
aalipour-hogben-etal-conjecture-graham-lovasz
]
, Aalipour et. al proved the following result.

Theorem 1
(Aalipour et. al)

With the notation above, let
\displaystyle d_{k}=\frac{-1}{2^{n-k-2}}c_{k}
be the normalized coefficients of
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{D}(x)
. Then, the
sequence
d_{k}
as
k
varies from
0
to
n-2
is unimodal and log concave.

The proof of Theorem
1
uses real
rootedness of
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{D}(x)
to show log
concavity. For unimodality, they need the following result
of Edelberg, Garey and Graham (see
[
edelberg-garey-graham
, Theorem 2.3]
)
which states that when

,

is negative (and hence

is positive).

Theorem 2
(Edelberg, Garey and Graham)

With

and

as above, let
\displaystyle\mathrm{CharPoly}_{D}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}x^{k}.

Then, for

.

An extension of these results to distance matrices

2
Unimodality and log-concavity

For unimodality, we will need the idea of
real rootedness
of polynomials
with real coefficients. The following result

[
branden-unimodality_log_concavity
, Lemma 7.1]
is known.

Lemma 6

Let
p(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}
be a real-rooted polynomial with real
coefficients.

1.

Then its coefficient sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is
log-concave.

2.

If a sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is both positive and
log-concave, then it is unimodal.

For any real and symmetric matrix
M
, by the Spectral Theorem,

\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
is real rooted and so the first part of
Lemma
6
is trivially satisfied.
When all eigenvalues of
M
are negative, it is easy to see that all
coefficients of
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
are positive.

When
M=(m_{i,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is an
n\times n
real, symmetric
matrix with
m_{i,i}=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
, and if
M
has
exactly one positive eigenvalue then, the
proof of Theorem
2
can be extended
to show that almost all the coefficients of
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)

are negative. This is the main point of

[
Abiad-Brimkov-Hayat-Khramova-Koolen-dist-char-poly
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Below, we mildly generalize this to include real, symmetric
matrices which have a non negative trace. Recall
the inertia of a real symmetric matrix
M
is the triple

\mathrm{Inertia}(M)=\big(n_{+}(M),n_{-}(M),n_{0}(M)\big)
. Here,
n_{+}(M),

n_{-}(M)
and
n_{0}(M)
denote the number of positive, negative and zero
eigenvalues of
M
respectively.

Theorem 7

Consider a real, symmetric matrix
M
of order
n
with
\mathrm{Trace}(M)\geq 0
and
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}
.
Let
\mathrm{Inertia}(M)=(1,r-1,n-r)
, with
2\leq r\leq n
. If

\mathrm{Trace}(M)=0
, then
a_{k}<0
when
n-r\leq k\leq n-2
and
if
\mathrm{Trace}(M)>0
, then
a_{k}<0
when
n-r\leq k\leq n-1
.

Proof:

Let the non zero eigenvalues of
M
be
\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{3},\ldots,-\lambda_{r}

and let the eigenvalue
0
occur with multiplicity
n-r
. Here, we
assume that
\lambda_{i}>0
when
1\leq i\leq r
and that the

\lambda_{i}
’s need not be distinct. Define
g_{0}=1
and
when
k\geq 1
, define

g_{k}
to be the sum of all
k
-fold products of
\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
. Clearly,
g_{k}>0
when
1\leq k\leq r
. Further

\displaystyle\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{n-r}(x-\lambda_{1})\prod_{i=2}^{r}(x+\lambda_{i})=x^{n-r}(x-\lambda_{1})\Bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}g_{k}x^{r-1-k}\Bigg)

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Bigg(x^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}\big(g_{k}-\lambda_{1}g_{k-1}\big)x^{n-k}-\lambda_{1}g_{n-1}x^{n-r}\Bigg)

Since
\lambda_{1}=g_{1}+t
,
g_{k}-\lambda_{1}g_{k-1}=g_{k}-(g_{1}+t)g_{k-1}=(g_{k}-g_{1}g_{k-1})-tg_{k-1}<0
as we have
t\geq 0
and

-\lambda_{1}g_{n-1}=-(g_{1}+t)g_{n-1}<0
.
Moreover,
c_{n-1}=-\mathrm{Trace}(M)=-t
. Hence,
when
n-r\leq k\leq n-1
and
t>0
, we have
a_{k}<0
.
Likewise, when
n-r\leq k\leq n-2
and
t=0
, we have

a_{k}<0
, completing the proof.

The following corollary of Theorem
7
can be drawn.

Corollary 8

Let
M
be a real and symmetric matrix of order
n
with
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}x^{k}
and
\mathrm{Inertia}(M)=(1,n-1,0)
.

1.

If
\mathrm{Trace}(M)=0
, then the sequence
|a_{0}|,|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{n-2}|

of the absolute values of its coefficients from
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)

is log-concave and unimodal.

2.

If
\mathrm{Trace}(M)>0
, then the sequence
|a_{0}|,|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{n-2}|,|a_{n-1}|
of the absolute values of its coefficients
from
\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
is log-concave and unimodal.

Proof:

Since
M
is a real, symmetric matrix,

\mathrm{CharPoly}_{M}(x)
is real-rooted and hence by Lemma
6
, it
follows that the sequence
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-2},a_{n-1},a_{n}
is log-concave.

1. By Theorem
7
, we get
a_{k}<0
when
0\leq k\leq n-2
. Since
all terms
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-2}
are negative, the sequence comprising their absolute

is log-concave and positive. By Lemma

,
|a_{0}|,|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{n-2}|

2. By Theorem

, we have
a_{k}<0
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. As
all the terms
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1}
are negative, the sequence
comprising their absolute values
(|a_{k}|)_{k=0}^{n-1}
is log-concave and positive.
By Lemma
6
,
|a_{0}|,|a_{1}|,\ldots,|a_{n-1}|
is unimodal.

3
, we

Definition 10

Let

\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
M_{p1}&M_{p2}&\ldots&M_{pp}\\

Here,

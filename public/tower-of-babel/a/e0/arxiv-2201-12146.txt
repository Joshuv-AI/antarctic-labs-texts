Ehrenfest dynamics implemented in the all-electron package exciting Journal: Electronic Structure
Ronaldo Rodrigues Pela, Claudia Draxl

Abstract
Ehrenfest Dynamics combined with real-time time-dependent density functional theory has proven to be a reliable tool to study non-adiabatic molecular dynamics with a reasonable computational cost. Among other possibilities, it allows for assessing in real time electronic excitations generated by ultra-fast laser pulses, as e.g. , in pump-probe spectroscopy, and their coupling to the nuclear vibrations even beyond the linear regime. In this work, we present its implementation in the all-electron full-potential package exciting . Three cases are presented as examples: diamond and cubic BN relaxed after an initial lattice distortion, and cubic BN exposed to a laser pulse. Comparison with the Octopus code exhibits good agreement.



1
Introduction

Molecular dynamics (MD) is one of the most important techniques of computational materials science. In one of the first reports on a computer MD, Alder and Wainwright described it as tracking the movement of atoms which interact by means of a given potential
[
1
,
2
]
. In non-empirical MD, the potential is not modeled
a priori
via some parametrized expression, but rather obtained “on the fly” through
ab initio
calculations
[
2
]
. Quite often then, the Born-Oppenheimer approximation (BOA) is employed. Thereby, each nucleus is treated as a point charge moving under the Coulomb potential due to the electrons and other nuclei, whereas the electrons are treated quantum mechanically with a wavefunction that responds adiabatically to the nuclei movement, remaining in an eigenstate in each time
[
3
]
.

Despite many successful theoretical predictions, BOA is not appropriate for several applications,
e.g.
, those involving the interaction between electromagnetic waves and matter. Processes in which the excited electrons respond to a rapidly varying time-dependent field and then lose their energy by coupling to lattice vibrations, are non-adiabatic and thus have to be treated beyond the BOA
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
. Examples of such phenomena of growing interest are pump-probe experiments, in which an initial laser pulse excites the system, while a second one monitors its time-dependent relaxation towards the ground state (or a stable excited-state configuration).

An efficient method to describe such non-adiabatic processes is the Ehrenfest Dynamics
[
9
,
10
]
that combines a classical treatment of the nuclei with a non-adiabatic evolution of electronic wavefunctions – usually carried out in the framework of real-time time-dependent density-functional theory (RT-TDDFT). This approach has been successfully applied in many recent studies, as diverse as electron injection into BN nanotubes with thousands of atoms
[
11
]
, collisions between oxygen and graphite clusters
[
12
]
, excited carrier dynamics in carbon nanotubes
[
13
]
, ion collisions with carbon nanostructures
[
14
]
, photoinduced dynamics in semiconductor quantum dots
[
15
]
, ultrafast electron injection from a PbSe quantum dot into a TiO
2
surface
[
16
]
, electron and hole transfer at a polymer-carbon nanotube heterojunction
[
17
]
, or chlorine ion colliding to a graphene nanoflake consisting of hundreds of atoms
[
18
]
.

A non-negligible aspect in computational materials science, in particular considering that we have entered the big-data era, is the validation of data. In electronic- structure theory, there are different approaches to solve the Kohn-Sham (KS) equations of DFT. Linearized augmented planewaves (LAPW) combined with local-orbitals (lo) can be regarded as the basis to describe KS wavefunctions with ultimate precision, thus serving as a reference for other implementations or approximations
[
19
,
20
]
. In this sense, the full-potential LAPW+lo
exciting
code
[
21
]
can be considered as reference for high-precision calculations. Moreover, it is open source and user-friendly, with more than 50 tutorials explaining its features and how to explore them
[
22
]
. In this manuscript, we report how we combine Ehrenfest Dynamics with RT-TDDFT, another most recent implementation
[
23
]
. To this extent, we briefly summarize the theory and provide the formalism behind our implementation together with LAPW-specific derivations. Finally, we show three different examples of applications, before drawing our conclusions.

2
Theoretical framework

Within Ehrenfest dynamics, the nuclei behave as classical repelling point charges that move being submitted to an external electric field and the one imposed by themselves. The electrons dynamics, in turn, is typically modeled by RT-TDDFT. Considering the external electric field given in the gauge
\mathbf{E}(t)=-\frac{1}{c}\frac{d\mathbf{A}(t)}{dt}
,
\mathbf{A}(t)
being the vector potential and
c
the speed of light, then the action integral for the total system of nuclei and electrons can be written as

[
24
,
25
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathrm{d}t\sum_{J}\left[\frac{M_{J}\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{J}^{2}}{2}+\frac{Z_{J}}{c}\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{J}\cdot\mathbf{A}(t)\right]+

(1)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\mathrm{d}t\sum_{j}f_{j\mathbf{k}}w_{\mathbf{k}}\left\langle\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)\left|\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\hat{H}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R},t)\right|\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)\right\rangle.

M_{J}
is the mass of a nucleus with index
J
,
Z_{J}
its atomic number, and
\mathbf{R}_{J}(t)
its position.
|\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)\rangle
is the single-particle KS wavefunction with wavevector
\mathbf{k}
, state index
j
, and occupation
f_{j\mathbf{k}}
.
w_{\mathbf{k}}
is the weight of the
k
-point. The time-dependent KS hamiltonian is

\hat{H}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R},t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\mathrm{i}\nabla+\frac{1}{c}\mathbf{A}(t)\right)^{2}+v_{H}(\mathbf{r},t)+v_{XC}(\mathbf{r},t)+v_{nucl}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R}),

(2)

where
v_{H}
and
v_{XC}
are the time-dependent Hartree and exchange-correlation potentials.
v_{nucl}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})
is the electrostatic potential that comprises all the nuclei-nuclei and nuclei-electron interactions

v_{nucl}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{R})=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{I,J}^{I\not=J}\frac{Z_{I}Z_{J}}{|\mathbf{R}_{I}-\mathbf{R}_{J}|}-\sum_{J}\frac{Z_{J}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{J}|}}.

(3)

2.1
Electronic equation of motion

exciting
employs the LAPW+lo basis
[
21
,
26
]
. Around the nuclei, non-overlapping spheres (termed
muffin-tin spheres
or
atomic spheres
) are defined. The space outside the muffin-tin (MT) spheres is called the
interstitial region
. An LAPW function
|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle
with wavevector
\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}
, where
\mathbf{G}
is a reciprocal lattice vector and
\mathbf{k}
a crystal momentum from the first Brillouin zone (BZ), are described by planewaves in the interstitial region, each of them being smoothly augmented into atomic-like wavefunctions inside the atomic spheres. lo’s,
|\phi_{\nu}\rangle
, here indexed by
\nu
, are functions defined only inside one muffin-tin sphere and zero outside. In Ehrenfest dynamics, the basis is implicitly time-dependent, since the muffin-tin spheres are centered around the nuclei whose positions evolve in time. The KS wavefunctions
|\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)\rangle
are expanded in terms of the basis as

|\psi_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)\rangle=\sum_{\mathbf{G}}C_{j\mathbf{k}\mathbf{G}}(t)|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle+\sum_{\nu}C_{j\mathbf{k}\nu}(t)|\phi_{\nu}\rangle=\sum_{\mu}C_{j\mathbf{k}\mu}(t)|\phi_{\mu\mathbf{k}}\rangle.

(4)

Using the more compact notation indicated by the last summation in Eq. (
4
), the time-evolution of the array
C_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)
, obtained by minimizing the integral of action, follows

S_{\mathbf{k}}(t)\dot{C}_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)=-\mathrm{i}H_{\mathbf{k}}(t)C_{j\mathbf{k}}(t)-B_{\mathbf{k}}(t)C_{j\mathbf{k}}(t),

(5)

where
[S_{\mathbf{k}}(t)]_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}=\langle\phi_{\mu\mathbf{k}}|\phi_{\mu^{\prime}\mathbf{k}}\rangle
and
[H_{\mathbf{k}}(t)]_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}=\langle\phi_{\mu\mathbf{k}}|\hat{H}(t)|\phi_{\mu^{\prime}\mathbf{k}}\rangle
are the overlap and hamiltonian matrices, respectively, and
B_{\mathbf{k}}(t)
reflects the time evolution of the basis:

B_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\sum_{J}\dot{\mathbf{R}}_{J}\cdot\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t),\qquad[\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t)]_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\left\langle\phi_{\mu^{\prime}\mathbf{k}}\left|\frac{\partial\phi_{\mu\mathbf{k}}}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}\right.\right\rangle.

(6)

The auxiliary matrix
\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}
is obtained by considering that the changes in the LAPW terms due to the movement of the nuclei are
[
27
]
:

\left|\frac{\partial\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}}}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}\right\rangle=\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G})\left|\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}}\right\rangle_{MT_{J}}-\left|\nabla\phi_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{G}}\right\rangle_{MT_{J}},

(7)

where the subscript
MT_{J}
indicates that the corresponding function is non-zero only inside the MT sphere centered around the nucleus
J
. The analogous expression for the lo’s reads
[
27
]
:

\left|\frac{\partial\phi_{\nu}}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}\right\rangle=-\left|\nabla\phi_{\nu}\right\rangle_{MT_{J}}.

(8)

Inserting Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) into Eq. (
6
), and considering that
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
can be LAPWs or lo’s, leads to:

[\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t)]_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phantom{-}\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k})\langle\phi_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}-\mathrm{i}\langle\phi_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}|\mathbf{p}|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\\
-\mathrm{i}\langle\phi_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}|\mathbf{p}|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{\nu}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\\
\phantom{-}\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k})\langle\phi_{\nu^{\prime}}|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}-\mathrm{i}\langle\phi_{\nu^{\prime}}|\mathbf{p}|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\\
-\mathrm{i}\langle\phi_{\nu^{\prime}}|\mathbf{p}|\phi_{\nu}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\end{array}\right.

(9)

where
\mathbf{p}=-\mathrm{i}\nabla
is the momentum operator. The inner products with subscripts
MT_{J}
indicate that the integrals which they contain are evaluated only inside
MT_{J}
.

2.2
Equation of nuclear motion

Extremizing Eq. (
1
) with respect to
\mathbf{R}_{J}
leads to the following equation of motion for the nuclei:

M_{J}\ddot{\mathbf{R}}_{J}(t)=\mathbf{F}_{ext,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)+\mathbf{F}_{HF,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)+\mathbf{F}_{corr,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t).

(10)

The external force
\mathbf{F}_{ext,J}
arises from the interaction with the applied electric field:

\mathbf{F}_{ext,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)=Z_{J}\mathbf{E}(t)=-\frac{Z_{J}}{c}\frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt}(t).

(11)

The Hellmann-Feynman force,
\mathbf{F}_{HF,J}
, can be expressed as
[
28
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{HF,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\,n(\mathbf{r},t)\frac{\partial v_{nucl}}{\partial\mathbf{R}_{J}}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Z_{J}\lim_{r_{J}\to 0}\frac{1}{r_{J}}\sum_{m=-1}^{1}\nabla[r_{J}v_{C,lm}(r_{J})Y_{lm}(\hat{r}_{J})]_{l=1},

where
\mathbf{r}_{J}=\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{R}_{J}
,
v_{C,lm}(r_{J})
is the radial function that is multiplied by
Y_{lm}
when
v_{C}=v_{nucl}+v_{H}
is expanded in spherical harmonics, and the gradient above is taken with respect to
\mathbf{r}_{J}
.

While the Hellmann-Feynman force is common to implementations of any basis-set type, so-called Pulay corrections need to be considered in the LAPW+lo formalism
[
27
,
28
]
. The additional term,
\mathbf{F}_{corr,J}
, arising due to the basis incompleteness, is a sum of contributions from the valence (
\mathbf{F}_{val,J}
) and core (
\mathbf{F}_{core,J}
) states. Neglecting second-order terms with respect to the displacement, one arrives at:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{core,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{MT_{J}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\,n_{core}(\mathbf{r},t)\nabla v_{KS}(\mathbf{r},t)

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{MT_{J}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\,v_{KS}(\mathbf{r},t)\nabla n_{core}(\mathbf{r},t),

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{val,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{j\mathbf{k}}w_{\mathbf{k}}f_{j\mathbf{k}}(C_{j\mathbf{k}})^{\dagger}(\mathcal{H}_{J\mathbf{k}}-\mathcal{S}_{J\mathbf{k}})C_{j\mathbf{k}}

(14)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{MT_{J}}\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}(\nabla n_{val}(\mathbf{r}))\left(v_{KS}(\mathbf{r})+\frac{\mathbf{A}^{2}}{2c^{2}}\right),

n_{core}
and
n_{val}
are the density of core and valence electrons, respectively. The auxiliary matrices
\mathcal{S}_{J\mathbf{k}}
and
\mathcal{H}_{J\mathbf{k}}
are defined as:

\mathcal{S}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t)=H_{\mathbf{k}}(t)S_{\mathbf{k}}^{-1}(t)\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t)+\mathcal{B}_{J\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}(t)S_{\mathbf{k}}^{-1}(t)H_{\mathbf{k}}(t),

(15)

[\mathcal{H}_{J\mathbf{k}}(t)]_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\phantom{-}\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\prime})\left[\langle\phi_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}|\hat{H}(t)|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}-\lambda_{J}\right]\\
-\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k})\langle\phi_{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}|\hat{H}(t)|\phi_{\nu}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\\
\phantom{-}\mathrm{i}(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k})\langle\phi_{\nu^{\prime}}|\hat{H}(t)|\phi_{\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k}}\rangle_{MT_{J}}\\
\phantom{-}0\mbox{ for the case lo-lo}\end{array}\right.

(16)

The definition of the auxiliary function
\lambda_{J}
is

\lambda_{J}=\left(\frac{\mathbf{G}^{\prime}+\mathbf{k}}{2}+\frac{\mathbf{A}(t)}{c}\right)\cdot(\mathbf{G}+\mathbf{k})\frac{4\pi R_{MT_{J}}^{3}}{\Omega}\frac{j_{1}(|\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\prime}|R_{MT_{J}})}{|\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\prime}|R_{MT_{J}}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{(\mathbf{G}-\mathbf{G}^{\prime})}\cdot\mathbf{R}_{J}}

(17)

where
\Omega
is the unit cell volume.

2.3
Different time steps

As suggested in Refs.
[
10
]
and
[
29
]
, we allow for two different time steps. A smaller one,
\Delta t
, is taken to solve Eq. (
5
), which dictates the dynamics of the electrons. The positions of the nuclei are kept fixed and are only changed in periods of
\alpha\Delta t
, where
\alpha
is a positive integer. This is physically grounded on the larger masses of the nuclei, when compared to electrons. At the same time, this procedure permits faster calculations, since the evaluation of the forces on the ions, specially the correction
\mathbf{F}_{val,J}(\mathbf{R}_{J},t)
, can be slow. In Section
3
, we will address the convergence behavior with respect to
\alpha
.

3
Examples of applications

3.1
BN in a distorted lattice

Our first example is BN in zinc-blend geometry with the experimental lattice constant of
a=6.833
bohr
[
30

. An
8\times 8\times 8

(0.26,0.26,0.26)

For the MD, we employ a time step of 0.01 a.u. for electrons and 0.05 a.u. for the nuclei. The approximated enforced time-reversal symmetry (AETRS) method together with a Taylor expansion up to the 4th order
[
23

are used as propagator for solving Eq. (

3.3
BN exposed to an intense electric field

In our third example, we start from the groundstate of zinc-blend BN and apply an electric field
\mathbf{E}(t)=-\frac{1}{c}\frac{d\mathbf{A}}{dt}
along the [111] direction, where
\mathbf{A}(t)=\mathbf{A}_{0}f(t)\cos(\omega_{0}t).

To be able to observe a non-negligible effect of the electric field on the ions, we choose an angular frequency
\omega_{0}
=0.5 a.u and set the magnitude of
\mathbf{A}_{0}
to 10.0 a.u. in each cartesian direction. For the envelope function
f(t)
, we consider
\sin^{2}(\pi t/T_{p})
until a time
T_{p}=1000
a.u., and zero afterwards. This function mimics a gaussian envelope, quite common in experiments.

4
Conclusions

In this paper, we have reported the implementation of Ehrenfest dynamics combined with RT-TDDFT in the all-electron full-potential package
exciting
that implements the LAPW+lo basis. To illustrate its performance, we have considered three examples: diamond and cubic BN with an initial lattice distortion, and cubic BN excited by an oscillating electric field. Comparison of our results with the Octopus code shows good agreement. The code will be freely available in the next release of
exciting
(fluorine), and all results underlying this publication are available on the NOMAD Laboratory
[
34
,
35
,
36
]
.

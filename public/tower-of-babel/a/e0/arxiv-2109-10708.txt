A Note on the Modeling Power of Different Graph Types
Josephine Thomas, Alice Moallemy-Oureh, Silvia Beddar-Wiesing, Rüdiger Nather

Abstract
Graphs can have different properties that lead to several graph types and may allow for a varying representation of diverse information. In order to clarify the modeling power of graphs, we introduce a partial order on the most common graph types based on an expressivity relation. The expressivity relation quantifies how many properties a graph type can encode compared to another type. Additionally, we show that all attributed graph types are equally expressive and have the same modeling power.

Introduction

Let
g=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
be a simple graph. The attributed version
g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\alpha,\omega)
with attribute mappings
\alpha:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
and
\omega:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
into attribute spaces
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
may intuitively express more information than the unattributed graph. However, there are several different graph types, for example, heterogeneous graphs, multigraphs, hypergraphs, directed or undirected graphs, and the question arises:
which graph type can express how much information?

Irrespective of the graph type, two graphs
g_{1}=(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{E}_{1}),g_{2}=(\mathcal{V}_{2},\mathcal{E}_{2})
were compared in standard literature by checking if they are structurally isomorphic
[
Grohe, Martin and Schweitzer, Pascal(2020)
]
, i.e., finding bijections
f_{1}:\mathcal{V}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}_{2},f_{2}:\mathcal{E}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{2}
between their node and edge sets. For the attributed case, the equality of two graphs requires additional bijections between the attribute sets of nodes and edges, respectively.
In this note, however, two graphs are not distinguished by their structure, but the modeling power of different graph types are analyzed, i.e., the number of graph properties that can be encoded by sets of graphs that differ in their structure by definition. We call the relation
graph type expressivity
and define it as follows.

Definition 1.1

(Graph Type Expressivity)
.

A graph type
\mathcal{G}_{2}
is
at least as expressive
as a graph type
\mathcal{G}_{1}
, if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{2}
encodes at least as many graph properties as
\mathcal{G}_{1}
denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{2}
. In case both types encode the same graph properties, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{1}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{2}\wedge\mathcal{G}_{2}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{1}
, it is denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{1}\approx\mathcal{G}_{2}
.

The fact that the graph type expressivity induces a partial order leads to the following theorems for unattributed graphs
1.2
, partially attributed graphs
1.3
and attributed graphs
1.4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{h},\mathcal{G}_{\bar{h}},\mathcal{G}_{d}
and
\mathcal{G}_{het}
be the sets of hypergraphs, non-hypergraphs, directed and heterogeneous graphs. Then it holds
\mathcal{G}_{h}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{h}}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{d}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{het}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{a},\mathcal{G}_{ia}
be the sets of attributed and integer-attributed graphs, and
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a}},\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},het},\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},m}
be the sets of unattributed graphs, unattributed heterogeneous and unattributed multigraphs. Then it holds
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a}}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{a},\ \mathcal{G}_{a}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},het}
, and
\mathcal{G}_{ia}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},m}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

All attributed graph types are equally expressive.

Definition 2.3

(Graph Properties)
.

An elementary graph
G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is called

1.

undirected
if the directions of the edges are irrelevant, i.e.,

(a)

for directed graphs: if
(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}
whenever
(v,u)\in\mathcal{E}
for
u,v\in\mathcal{V}
.

(b)

for directed hypergraphs: if
f_{i}\mathbin{:}\mathsf{x}\rightarrow 0
for all
(\mathsf{x},f_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{E}
. Abbreviated by
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\{\mathsf{x}\mid\mathsf{x}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\}
.

In what follows
\mathcal{G}_{u}
is the set of all undirected graphs.

2.

multigraph
if it is a multi-edge graph, i.e., the edges
\mathcal{E}
are defined as an (ordered) multiset, denoted by
\{\!|\cdot|\!\}
, a multi-node graph, i.e., the node set
\mathcal{V}

3.

heterogeneous
if the nodes or edges can have different types (node- or edge-heterogeneous). I.e., the node set is determined by
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{S}
with a node type set
\mathcal{S}
and the edges are extended by the edge type set
\mathcal{R}
. The set of all heterogeneous graphs is denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{het}
.

4.

attributed
if the nodes
\mathcal{V}
or edges
\mathcal{E}
are equipped with node- or edge attributes. These attributes are given by a node and edge attribute function, respectively, i.e.,
\alpha:\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
and
\omega:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}

\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{W}
are arbitrary attribute sets.
The set of all attributed graphs is denoted as
\mathcal{G}_{a}
.

5.

dynamic
if the graph structure or the graph properties are time dependent. In the following, the notion
G_{i}=(\mathcal{V}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{i}),\;t_{i}\in T
is used, where
T
is a finite set of (not necessarily equidistant) timestamps to emphasize the time-dependence and therefore the dynamics.

In the literature there are many graph types composed from the above definitions of graph properties, but the resulting graph types are not always named accordingly. Therefore, in the following important composed graphs are listed and named according to the definitions from above.

Lemma 3.1
.

The set of directed hypergraphs and non-hypergraphs are equally expressive, i.e.,

\mathcal{G}_{h}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\overline{h}}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{h}
,
g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}_{\bar{h}}
be an arbitrary hyper- and non-hypergraph of the forms

g=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
with
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{N},\;{\mathcal{E}\subseteq\{(\mathsf{x},f_{i})_{i}\mid\mathsf{x}\subseteq\mathcal{V},\,f_{i}\mathbin{:}\mathsf{x}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}\}}
and
g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
with
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{V}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}.

\preccurlyeq
:

Let
f\mathbin{:}\mathcal{G}_{h}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\bar{h}}
be a mapping with
f(g)=g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{E}^{\star}\cup\mathcal{E}^{\star\star}
, with

(a)

the translation of undirected hyperedges to fully connected subgraphs (cliques):

as illustrated in fig.

(b)

the translation of directed hyperedges to chains of fully connected bipartite subgraphs (bicliques):

{\mathcal{E}^{\star\star}=\{(u,v)\mid\exists(\mathsf{x},f_{i})_{i}\in\mathcal{E}\mathbin{:}u,v\in\mathsf{x}\wedge f_{i}(v)=f_{i}(u)+1\}}
as illustrated in fig.
2

Then

is bijective, while the injectivity is insured by the ordering functions

of the corresponding edges.

\succcurlyeq
:

This direction is straight forward since every non-hypergraph is per definition a hypergraph.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{d}
be the sets of attributed directed graphs and
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{d}}
the set of attributed undirected graphs. Then it holds

\mathcal{G}_{d}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\overline{d}}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{d}
be an arbitrary directed graph of the form

g=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\alpha,\omega)\text{ with }\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{N},

\;{\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{V}},

\;{\alpha\mathbin{:}\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}},\;{\omega\mathbin{:}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}.}

\preccurlyeq
:

Let
f\mathbin{:}\mathcal{G}_{d}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\bar{d}}
be a mapping defined by
f(g)=g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})

,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{\{u,v\}\mid(u,v)\vee(v,u)\in\mathcal{E}\}

\left(\omega((v,u)),-1\right),&\text{if }(v,u)\in\mathcal{E}\wedge(u,v)\notin\mathcal{E},\\
\bigl(\left(\omega((u,v)),1\right),\left(\omega((v,u)),-1\right)\bigr)&\text{if }(u,v),(v,u)\in\mathcal{E}.\\
\end{cases}

Then

is bijective.

\succcurlyeq
:

This direction is straight forward, since every undirected edge formally corresponds to two directed edges.

Lemma 5.2
.

The set of attributed multigraphs and attributed non-multigraphs are equally expressive, i.e.,

\mathcal{G}_{m}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{m}
be an arbitrary multigraph of the form
g=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\alpha,\omega)
with
\mathcal{V}\subset\{\!|v\mid v\in\mathbb{N}|\!\},

\;{\mathcal{E}\subseteq\{\!|(u,v)\mid u,v\in\mathcal{V}|\!\},}\;

{\alpha\mathbin{:}\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},\;\omega\mathbin{:}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}}
.

\preccurlyeq
:

Let
f\mathbin{:}\mathcal{G}_{m}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\bar{m}}
be a mapping with
f(g)=g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\{v\mid v\in\mathcal{V}\}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{e\mid e\in\mathcal{E}\}
,
\alpha^{\prime}(v)\mapsto(\alpha(v)\mid v\in\mathcal{V})
and
\omega^{\prime}(e)\mapsto(\omega(e)\mid e\in\mathcal{E})
.
Since the multisets are ordered the function is injective.

\succcurlyeq
:

This direction is straight forward since every set is a multiset.

Lemma 5.3
.

The set of attributed heterogeneous and attributed homogeneous graphs are equally expressive, i.e.,

\mathcal{G}_{het}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\overline{het}}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{G}_{het}
be an arbitrary heterogeneous graph of the form
g=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E},\alpha,\omega)
with
{\mathcal{V}\subset\mathbb{N}\times\mathcal{S}},

\;{\mathcal{E}\subseteq\{\mathcal{V},\mathcal{V}\}\times\mathcal{R}},

{\alpha\mathbin{:}\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathcal{C},}

\;\omega\mathbin{:}\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
.

\preccurlyeq
:

Let
f\mathbin{:}\mathcal{G}_{het}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\overline{het}}
be a mapping with
f(g)=g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
, where

(a)

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\{v\mid(v,s)\in\mathcal{V}\}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\{(u,v)\mid(u,v,r)\in\mathcal{E}\}
,

(b)

\alpha^{\prime}(v)\mapsto(\alpha((v,s)),s\mid(v,s)\in\mathcal{V})\ \forall v\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, and
\omega^{\prime}(e)\mapsto(\omega((e,r)),r\mid(e,r)\in\mathcal{E})\ \forall e\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.

The concatenation of the node and edge types to the attributes directly constitutes the injectivity of the mapping.

\succcurlyeq
:

This direction is straight forward by introducing one node and one edge type since every homogeneous graph is a heterogeneous graph with just one node and edge type.

The set of attributed dynamic and attributed static graphs are equally expressive, i.e.,

{\mathcal{G}_{dy}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\overline{dy}}}

{\mathcal{V}_{t}\subset\mathbb{N}},

\;{\mathcal{E}_{t}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{t}\times\mathcal{V}_{t},}

\preccurlyeq
:

Let
f\mathbin{:}\mathcal{G}_{dy}\rightarrow\text{Im}(f)\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\overline{dy}}
be a mapping with
f(G)=g^{\prime}=(\mathcal{V}^{\prime},\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
, where

(a)

\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in[T]}\mathcal{V}_{t}

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\bigcup\limits_{t\in[T]}\mathcal{E}_{t}

(b)

\alpha^{\prime}(v)\mapsto((\alpha_{t}(v),t)\mid v\in\mathcal{V}_{t})\ \forall v\in\mathcal{V}^{\prime}

Despite the union of the node and edge sets, the function is injective due to the ordering of the graph attributes in the static graph.

\succcurlyeq
:

This direction is straight forward by introducing one time stamp since every static graph is dynamic with only one time stamp.

7
Conclusion

In order to clarify the modeling power of graphs, we evaluated the power to encode different properties, i.e. the expressivity power, for common graph types.
First, we compared unattributed graph types w.r.t. their expressivity power. We proved that hyper- and non-hypergraphs are equally expressive, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{h}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{h}}
and that heterogeneous graphs are at least as expressive as directed graph types, i.e.,
\mathcal{G}_{d}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{het}
.
For this purpose, we showed the existence of bijective embeddings between the graph types. Next, in the case of one attributed and one unattributed graph type, we could show that it holds
\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a}}\preccurlyeq\mathcal{G}_{a}
for unattributed and attributed graphs.
In contrast, for attributed and unattributed heterogeneous graphs and integer-attributed and unattributed multigraphs, it is
\mathcal{G}_{a}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},het}
, and
{\mathcal{G}_{ia}\approx\mathcal{G}_{\bar{a},m}}
, respectively.
Subsequently, we proved that attributed graphs of any graph type could encode any graph property leading to the equal expressivity power of attributed graph types, cf. thm.
1.4
. Finally, we noted the expressivity relation between composed graphs resulting from the composition of existing embeddings between the attributed and unattributed graph types having just one graph property.

Giant High-order Nonlinear and Nonreciprocal Electrical Transports Induced by Valley Flipping in Bernal Bilayer Graphene
Yuelin Shao, Xi Dai

Abstract
We investigate the electrical transport properties of the mini-valley polarized state proposed recently in slightly doped Bernal Bilayer Graphene (BLG) in large electric displacement fields. By minimizing the Hartree-Fock (HF) energy functional, we first confirm the appearance of mini-valley polarized phase. At the low carrier doping regime, the 1-pocket state will be stabilized where only one of the trigonal-wrapping-induced Fermi pockets near the atomic-valley center is filled. Then we study the electrical transport of the 1-pocket state by solving the Boltzmann equation. We find that the valley polarization could be easily flopped by an in-plane electrical field, which will lead to hysteresis loop in the direct current (DC) I-V curves. Such irreversible current responses in the DC limit will directly induce strong nonlinear and nonreciprocal alternating current (AC) responses, which has been already observed in the recent experiments on BLG.



I
Introduction

In the realm of two-dimensional materials, the valley degree of freedom is a critical factor that influences their unique magnetic, transport, and optical characteristics.
This is in addition to the well-known degrees of freedom in condensed matter, such as spin, orbital, and momentum.
In certain systems, like twisted bilayer graphene (TBG) and transition metal dichalcogenides (TMDs), a variety of valley polarization and hybridization states have been proposed
Liu et al. 2019
;
Kang and Vafek 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Xie and MacDonald 2020
;
Liu and Dai 2021
;
Devakul et al. 2021
and observed
Cao et al. 2018
;
Sharpe et al. 2019
;
Jiang et al. 2019
;
Choi et al. 2019
;
Cao et al. 2020
;
Serlin et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2020
;
Polshyn et al. 2020
;
Tschirhart et al. 2021
;
Choi et al. 2021
;
Pierce et al. 2021
.
These states contribute to the emergence of exotic phenomena, including the quantum anomalous Hall effect, correlated insulators, and orbital magnetism.
Recently, valley-polarized states have also been detected in non-twisted systems such as Rhombohedral trilayer and Bernal bilayer graphene when a displacement field is applied along the
z
-axis
Zhou et al. 2021
;
de la Barrera et al. 2022
;
Seiler et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2022
;
Han et al. 2023
.
In this paper, we focus on the Bernal bilayer graphene (BLG) whose atomic structure and Brillouin zone is shown in Fig.
1
(a).
The displacement field first separates the two Dirac nodes in energy and subsequently leads to the formation of a hybridization gap due to inter-layer coupling
McCann 2006
;
McCann and Fal’ko 2006
;
Oostinga et al. 2008
, resulting in a “Mexican hat” type band dispersion for each atomic valley
\pm K
.
The trigonal warping effect in BLG further creates three local maxima in the valence band (or minima in the conduction band) near each atomic valley center
McCann and Fal’ko 2006
, as indicated by
Q_{1,2,3}
in Figure
1
(b).
As a result, the highest valence band (HVB) of gated BLG hosts six band maxima
\pm Q_{1,2,3}
(the prefactor
\xi=\pm
label the atomic valley, the subscript
\alpha=1,2,3
label the mini-valley around the atomic valleys), which from now on are called all together as the valleys thought out this paper for simplicity.
When the filling factor of the system is just slightly away from the charge neutrality point, quantum oscillation experiments indicate an emergence of the half- and quarter-metal phases
de la Barrera et al. 2022
;
Seiler et al. 2022
;
Zhou et al. 2022
.
Starting from the quarter-metal phase where only one spin and atomic valley is occupied, if the doping concentration is further reduced so that the mini-valley
Q_{1,2,3}
could also be viewed as a degree of freedom, a polarized state to the mini-valley is further predicted
Dong et al. 2023
.
Such kind of valley-polarized states break the threefold rotation symmetry
C_{3}
and has been identified by the
C_{3}
-breaking pattern of angle-resolved second-harmonic nonlinear transport measurements
Lin et al. 2023
.
More recently, anomalous high-order nonlinear and nonreciprocal transport signals have also been detected in the slightly doped BLG
Zan et al. 2024
, challenging traditional transport theories and spurring the need for theoretical investigation from new vantage points.

In this paper, we first establish the presence of mini-valley-polarized phases in gated BLG.
This is deduced from the minimization of the system’s total energy functional under Hartree-Fock (HF) approximation.
The proximity of the three valleys around the
K
points makes the polarized states quite susceptible to the transitions induced by current flow, potentially leading to significant nonlinear and non-reciprocal transport behaviors.
We then analyze the current response of systems exhibiting ”Mexican hat” type band dispersion by employing the Boltzmann equation, incorporating the effects of long-range Coulomb interactions among carriers.
Differentiating between relaxation processes within the same mini-valley and across different valleys allowed us to derive equations that effectively describe the dynamics within these systems.
The resulting phenomena from our analysis include:
(1) The mini-valley polarization can be reversed via electric current, causing first-order hysteresis loops in the direct current (DC) I-V curve, which was also pointed out in a recent study
(
Panigrahi and Levitov 2024
)
.
(2) The emergence of irreversible current responses in the DC spectrum, resulting in significant nonlinear and nonreciprocal alternating current (AC) behaviors at low frequencies.
(3) The presence of a critical field intensity threshold beyond which the system’s long-term current response becomes insensitive to initial polarization directions, yielding nearly symmetric angular responses.

We stress that the first-order hysteretic behaviors in the DC I-V curves and the related phenomena discussed above persist as long as the ground state is mini-valley polarized.
This means that these phenomena are also “smoking gun” for confirming the mini-valley polarized ground state.

Similar current-induced hysteretic phenomena were also observed
(
Serlin et al. 2020
;
Sharpe et al. 2019
)
and studied
(
Su and Lin 2020
;
He et al. 2020
)
in TBG.
Nonetheless, the mechanisms in TBG are quite different from this work.
Firstly, the ground state in TBG is an insulator while the ground state in this study is a metal.
Besides, as will be shown in Sec.
III
, the valley flipping phenomenon in our work results from the competition between the electric field driving effect and the inter-valley relaxation.
However, neither of these two processes could possibly exist in any of the insulator phase, such as the previous theoretical studies in TBG
(
Su and Lin 2020
;
He et al. 2020
)
.
In other words, we present a new mechanism of current-induced valley polarization flipping phenomenon in the valley polarized metal.

Under the above assumption, the intra-valley relaxation process is considered to be much faster than the inter-valley process.
Therefore, we can approximately separate the fast and slow processes by ignoring the inter-valley relaxation process when dealing with the intra-valley dynamics.

V
Discussion

When the spin and atomic valley degeneracy is lifted in the quarter-metal phase, the inversion and time reversal symmetries are both broken, which ensures the existence of the anomalous Hall currents as illustrated by the gray line in Fig.
6
(a).
At this time, the approximated point group is
D_{3d}
which contains the threefold rotations
C_{3}
and mirror symmetries
M_{\theta}
with respect to the mirror lines
\theta=0,\;\pm\pi/3
.
If the system is further condensed to the 1-pocket state, for example the
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
-polarized state, the rotation symmetries are broken and only one mirror symmetry
M_{\pi/3}
survives.
As a consequence of the low symmetry, all the three electrical current components
j_{N,\parallel}
,
j_{N,\perp}
and
j_{H}
usually exist at the same time and they have both even and odd frequency responses in the frequency domain as shown in Fig.
6
(b).
To distinguish the two kind of currents in the transverse direction, the surviving mirror symmetry could be utilized.
According to Eq. (
40
) in Appendix
F
, the normal current
j_{N,\perp}
and Hall current
j_{H}
have odd and even parities respectively under mirror operation.
And these symmetry restrictions are clearly shown in Fig.
9
by the last two columns.

Figure 10:
(a)(b) Polarization direction
\beta_{1}
and polarization strength
j_{1}/j_{3}
of the second order longitudinal current responses.
The black dashed lines mark the critical density between the 1- and 2-pocket phases
n_{c,12}
.
(c-f) Angular patterns of the second order longitudinal current responses.
The corresponding hole densities are marked in (a)(b) by the red triangle symbols.
The labels
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
marks the initial valley polarization direction, i.e., in (c-e) the initial state is a 1-pocket state polarized in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
valley, in (f) the initial state is a 2-pocket state polarized in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{3}
valleys.

In Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
, the symmetry of the angular pattern of the second order longitudinal resistivity is used to determine the ground state.
However, as illustrated in Fig.
9
(c1-c3), when the electrical field strength is strong enough to flip the valley polarization, the angular patterns may not reflect the ground state symmetry faithfully.
As discussed in Appendix
E
, the critical field strength for valley flipping decreases with hole concentration
n_{h}
, suggesting a
C_{3}
-persevering pattern is likely to appear near the phase boundary between the 1- and 2-pocket phases.
Following Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
, the second order longitudinal current responses as function of
\theta
could be fitted by

j_{N,\parallel}^{(2)}(\theta)=j_{1}\cos(\theta-\beta_{1})+j_{3}\cos(3(\theta-\beta_{3})),

(16)

where
j_{1}/j_{3}
measures the strength of polarization and
\beta_{1}
indicates the polarization direction.
At fixed electrical field amplitude
\tilde{E}=0.125E^{*}
, quantities
\beta_{1}
and
j_{1}/j_{3}
are plotted in Fig.
10
(a)(b) as functions of hole density
n_{h}
.
When there is no valley flipping in the low density regime, the angular pattern of
j^{(2)}_{N,\parallel}
does reveal the symmetry of the initial
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
-polarized state as shown in Fig.
10
(c).
However, with the increase of the hole density
n_{h}
, due to the valley flipping, the polarization strength of the current responses
j_{1}/j_{3}
decreases as shown in Fig.
10
(b), and the
C_{3}
symmetry emerges gradually as illustrated by Fig.
10
(d)(e).
The dip of
j_{1}/j_{3}
and a
C_{3}
-preserving pattern near the boundary of 1-pocket phase are both observed in Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
(FIG.2), but might be wrongly interpreted as an unpolarized ground state in their paper.
When the hole density is larger than
n_{c,12}
, the ground state is a 2-pocket state and the emerged
C_{3}
symmetry no longer exists as shown by Fig.
10
(f).
For better comparison, in Appendix
G
, we also study the angular pattern of the longitudinal resistivity which is directly measured in the experiment.
When the gating potential
U
is small, the angular pattern of the second order longitudinal resistivity Fig.
15
(b) agrees quantitatively with the experimental results in Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
(FIG.2.b).

A concerning problem is spatial domains where the holes populate different mini-valleys, which make the system nonuniform.
However, as shown in our paper, the valley polarizations in different domains could be aligned by a large enough electrical field or equivalently current flow.
And the discussion of the uniform case in our paper is sufficient.
Besides, it’s also known that the free carriers are very easily localized in the presence of disorders in 2D.
However, the experimental results in Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
show that the devices are clean enough and there are no evidences signaling the localization behavior at the parameter regime we are interested in.
In such clean devices, one may worry that the system falls into the hydrodynamic transport regime, which requires the mean free path due to electron-electron scattering
l_{e}
and impurity scattering
l_{i}
satisfy
l_{e}\ll l_{i}
, or equivalently, the lifetime due to electron-electron scattering
\tau_{e}
and impurity scattering
\tau_{i}
satisfy
\tau_{e}\ll\tau_{i}
.
For 2D electron gases, the inverse lifetime due to electron-electron scattering scales along with the temperature
T
as
\hbar/(\tau_{e}E_{F})\sim-(k_{B}T/E_{F})^{2}\ln(k_{B}T/E_{F})
(
Qian and Vignale 2005
)
, where
E_{F}
is the Fermi energy measured from the band minimum (for electrons) or maximum (for holes).
In
Lin et al. 2023
, the measurements are conducted as
T=20\;\mathrm{mK}
; and at the hole density
n_{h}=0.1\times 10^{-12}\;\mathrm{cm}^{-2}
, the Fermi energy is about
10\;\mathrm{meV}
as shown in Fig.
1
(e).
Thus, the electron-electron scattering lifetime is estimated as
\tau_{e}\sim 10^{-7}\;\mathrm{s}
, which is much longer than the impurity lifetime
\tau_{i}
in BLG (usually in the order of
10^{-14}\sim 10^{-12}\;\mathrm{s}
(
Monteverde et al. 2010
)
).
This means that at the low temperature, the requirement for hydrodynamic transport couldn’t be satisfied.

The valley polarized phases and the related valley flipping phenomenon also have the potential for practical applications.
In the AC case, when the driving field is strong enough, the power-law decay of the current responses in frequency domain as shown by Fig.
6
(b) might be served as a high harmonic generators.
Additionally, as an ordered state which could be manipulated by electrical field and detected by transport measurements, the mini-valley polarized state holds promise for applications in information storage.

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\bm{k}}[n_{\bm{k}}]=\frac{\delta E_{ex}}{\delta n_{\bm{k}}}=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}U_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}},

(17b)

\displaystyle n_{h}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\Theta(\mu-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}),

(17c)

\displaystyle n_{\bm{k}}=\Theta(\mu-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}).

(17d)

Appendix B
The Exchange Energy Functional

To minimize
E_{tot}(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
in the main text, the HF exchange energy functional is further approximated by its series expansions with respect to
n_{\alpha=1,2,3}
.
For convenience, we define the intra-valley exchange energy
E_{ex}^{i}
and inter-valley exchange
E_{ex}^{x}
as

\displaystyle E_{ex}^{i}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\mathcal{S}^{2}}\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}U_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})n_{\bm{k}}n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}\delta k\textrm{d}^{2}\delta k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;|\langle\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}|\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\times V_{col}(\delta\bm{k}-\delta\bm{k}^{\prime})n^{0}_{\alpha,\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}}n^{0}_{\alpha,\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}}

(19)

\displaystyle E_{ex}^{x}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\mathcal{S}^{2}}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha},\bm{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\beta}}U_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})n_{\bm{k}}n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}\delta k\textrm{d}^{2}\delta k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;|\langle\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}|\bm{Q}_{\beta}+\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\times V_{col}(\delta\bm{k}-\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{Q}_{\alpha}-\bm{Q}_{\beta})n^{0}_{\alpha,\bm{Q}_{\alpha}+\delta\bm{k}}n^{0}_{\beta,\bm{Q}_{\beta}+\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}}

(20)

where
\delta k\ll Q_{\alpha}
is measured from the mini-valley center
\bm{Q}_{\alpha}
.
Due to the
1/q
divergence of the Coulomb interaction
V_{col}(q)
in Eq. (
19
), the lowest order term when expanding with respect to
n_{\alpha}
is
n_{\alpha}^{3/2}
, which is similar to the conventional 2D electron gas
(
Rajagopal and Kimball 1977
)
.
Then up to
n_{h}^{3}
, the intra-valley exchange energy is expanded as

E_{ex}^{i}\approx-\sum_{\alpha}(A_{1}n_{\alpha}^{3/2}+A_{2}n_{\alpha}^{5/2})+\mathcal{O}(n_{h}^{7/2}).

(21)

As for the intra-valley term Eq. (
20
), since
\delta k\ll Q_{\alpha}
is satisfied in the low doping regime, and
\delta\bm{k}-\delta\bm{k}^{\prime}+\bm{Q}_{\alpha}-\bm{Q}_{\beta}
can never be zero, there will be no singularities in the integration and the expansion of
E_{ex}^{x}
with respect to
n_{\alpha}
is the conventional Taylor expansion.
Then up to
n_{h}^{3}
, the inter-valley exchange energy should have the form

E_{ex}^{x}\approx-\sum_{\alpha\neq\beta}(B_{1}n_{\alpha}n_{\beta}+B_{2}n_{\alpha}^{2}n_{\beta})+\mathcal{O}(n_{h}^{4}).

(22)

When the inter-layer gating potential is taken as
U=300\;\mathrm{meV}
in the main text, the expansion coefficients are fitted from the numerical results as
A_{1}=0.429\;\mathrm{eVnm}
,
A_{2}=-16.0\;\mathrm{eVnm^{3}}
,
B_{1}=0.366\;\mathrm{eVnm^{2}}
and
B_{2}=41.1\;\mathrm{eVnm^{4}}
.

The Inter-valley Scattering Term

By integrating over the
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
valley, the changing rate of hole concentrations in area
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
due to impurity scattering
(\frac{\partial n_{\alpha}}{\partial t})_{s}
is obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}}{\hbar}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;|T_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|^{2}\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})(n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-n_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}|T_{i}|^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})(n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-n_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}|T_{x}|^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})(n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-n_{\bm{k}})

(23)

where
\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}
means regions exclude
\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}
.
The two terms describe the intra- and inter-valley scattering processes respectively.
The intra-valley term change sign when switching the indices
\bm{k}
and
\bm{k}^{\prime}
which means the integral is zero.
This reflects the fact that the intra-valley scattering process will not effect the hole concentrations in each valley.
To further simplify the inter-valley term, we approximate the distribution
n_{\bm{k}}
in the integrand by
n^{0}_{\bm{k}}
and the last term becomes

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}|T_{x}|^{2}}{\hbar}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k^{\prime}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}})(n^{0}_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}-n^{0}_{\bm{k}})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}|T_{x}|^{2}g^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}\int\textrm{d}\varepsilon\int\textrm{d}\varepsilon^{\prime}\;\delta(\varepsilon+\Sigma_{\alpha}-\varepsilon^{\prime}-\Sigma_{\beta})

\displaystyle\qquad\times[\Theta(\mu_{\beta}-\varepsilon^{\prime})-\Theta(\mu_{\alpha}-\varepsilon)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi n_{imp}|T_{x}|^{2}g^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left[\Theta(\Sigma_{\beta}+\mu_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha}-\mu_{\alpha})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\times\min(\mu_{\beta},\Sigma_{\beta}+\mu_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha}-\mu_{\alpha})-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right].

(24)

Appendix D
Effect of Coulomb Interaction to the Solution of Boltzmann Equation

When the hole concentrations in each mini-valley are fixed as
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
, the solution of the Boltzmann equation Eq. (
8
) is approximately given by Eq. (
9
) in the main text.
However, once the hole distribution function is deformed by the electrical field, the self-energy

\Sigma_{\bm{k}}[n_{\bm{k}}]=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}U_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}

(25)

and the quasiparticle energy
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}=-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{0}+\Sigma_{\bm{k}}
also changes accordingly.
Thus, to fully include the effect of the interaction, one should solve the Boltzmann equation Eq. (
8
) self-consistently.
And the self-consistent equation reads

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}}=-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{0}-\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}U_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}n_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}

(26a)

\displaystyle n_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\alpha}}\Theta(\mu_{\alpha[\bm{k}]}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}})

(26b)

\displaystyle n_{\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{\tau_{i}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\textrm{d}t^{\prime}\;\textrm{e}^{t^{\prime}/\tau_{i}}\Theta(\mu_{\alpha[\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t/\hbar]}-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t/\hbar})

(26c)

Given distribution function
n_{\bm{k}}
, the quasiparticle energy is determined by Eq. (
26a
).
Then the chemical potentials in each mini-valley
\mu_{\alpha[\bm{k}]}
are determined by solving Eq. (
26b
).
Finally, a new distribution function is got from Eq. (
26c
).
Using the same parameters as in Fig.

, the hysteresis curve of the polarization strength

is recalculated and plotted in Fig.

Appendix E
Dependence of the Critical Field on Hole Concentration and Relaxation Times

In this part, we investigate the dependence of the critical field strength for valley flipping on hole concentration
n_{h}
and relaxation times
\tau_{i,x}
.
Without losing generality, we will assume that the electrical field is applied in the direction
\pi/3<\theta<\pi
and the initial state is
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
-polarized.
Other cases could by easily got by symmetry operations.

we first consider the zero-doping limit
n_{h}\to 0
.
At this time, the dynamics equation could be much simplified and analytically solved.
Define the driving coefficient as

\displaystyle\eta\equiv

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\left(\frac{\partial n_{1}}{\partial t}\right)_{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{k}}n_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{1}{n_{1}}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\int_{-\infty}^{0}\textrm{d}t^{\prime}\;\frac{\textrm{e}^{t^{\prime}/\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\frac{e}{\hbar}\bm{E}\cdot\nabla_{\bm{k}}n^{0}_{\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar}.

(27)

In the last line, the chamber formula Eq. (
9
) is used.
According to the defining Eq. (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}n^{0}_{\bm{k}}=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{Q}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{2}[n_{1}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{Q}_{1})+n_{2}\delta(\bm{k}-\bm{Q}_{2})].

(28)

In Eq. (
28
), we only keep the contributions from the
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
valley.
This is because an electrical field in the direction
\pi/3<\theta<\pi
can only drive holes from the
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
to the
\mathcal{Q}_{2}
valley and the
\mathcal{Q}_{3}
valley is irrelevant, i.e.
n_{3}=0
.
Notice that
e\bm{E}/\hbar\cdot\nabla_{\bm{k}}n^{0}_{\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar}=\partial_{t^{\prime}}n^{0}_{\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar}
, Eq. (
27
) becomes

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\textrm{d}t^{\prime}\;\frac{\textrm{e}^{t^{\prime}/\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\left[\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}n^{0}_{\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\textrm{d}t^{\prime}\;\frac{\textrm{e}^{t^{\prime}/\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}{\textrm{d}^{2}k}\;[n_{1}\delta(\bm{k}+e\bm{E}^{\prime}t/\hbar-\bm{Q}_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+n_{2}\delta(\bm{k}+e\bm{E}^{\prime}t/\hbar-\bm{Q}_{2})].

(29)

Since the electrical field is applied in the direction
\pi/3<\theta<\pi
, we always have
\bm{Q}_{2}-e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar\in\mathcal{Q}_{2}
for
t^{\prime}<0
.
Thus

\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\textrm{d}^{2}k\;\delta(\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar-\bm{Q}_{2})=0.

(30)

Besides, only when
t^{\prime}>-\sqrt{3}\hbar Q/(2eE\sin\theta)
is satisfied, the vector
\bm{Q}_{1}-e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar
is in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
valley.
Therefore

\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}\textrm{d}^{2}k\;\delta(\bm{k}+e\bm{E}t^{\prime}/\hbar-\bm{Q}_{1})=\Theta[t^{\prime}+\sqrt{3}\hbar Q/(2eE\sin\theta)].

(31)

Using Eq. (
31
)(
30
), the driving coefficient Eq. (
29
) becomes

\displaystyle\eta=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{0}\textrm{d}t^{\prime}\;\frac{\textrm{e}^{t^{\prime}/\tau_{i}}}{\tau_{i}}\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Theta[t^{\prime}+\sqrt{3}\hbar Q/(2eE\sin\theta)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{e}^{-\sqrt{3}\hbar Q/(2eE\tau_{i}\sin\theta)}}{\tau_{i}}.

(32)

In the zero-doping limit, the scattering term could also be simplified.
According to Eq. (
21
) and (
22
), we have

\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\Sigma_{\alpha}=\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\partial_{n_{\alpha}}E_{ex}=-3A_{1}n_{\alpha}^{1/2}/2+\mathcal{O}(n_{h}^{3/2}).

(33)

Besides, according to Eq. (
5
), we also have

\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\mu_{\alpha}=n_{\alpha}/g+\mathcal{O}(n_{h}^{2}).

(34)

Thus the inter-valley scattering term becomes

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{g}{\tau_{x}}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left[\Theta(\Sigma_{\beta}+\mu_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha}-\mu_{\alpha})\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\left.\times\min(\mu_{\beta},\Sigma_{\beta}+\mu_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha}-\mu_{\alpha})-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{g}{\tau_{x}}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left[\Theta(\Sigma_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha})\min(\mu_{\beta},\Sigma_{\beta}-\Sigma_{\alpha})-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{n_{h}\to 0}\frac{g}{\tau_{x}}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}[\Theta({n_{\alpha}}-{n_{\beta}})\mu_{\beta}-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{x}}\sum_{\beta\neq\alpha}\left[\Theta(n_{\alpha}-n_{\beta})n_{\beta}-\alpha\leftrightarrow\beta\right].

(35)

In summary, the dynamics equation for
n_{\alpha}
s in the zero-doping limit is finally simplified to

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{1}=-\eta n_{1}+\tau_{x}^{-1}[\Theta(n_{1}-n_{2})n_{2}-1\leftrightarrow 2],

\displaystyle\dot{n}_{2}=\eta n_{1}+\tau_{x}^{-1}[\Theta(n_{2}-n_{1})n_{1}-1\leftrightarrow 2].

Define the valley polarization strength as
p\equiv(n_{2}-n_{1})/n_{h}
, then the dynamics equation of

is

\displaystyle\dot{p}=

\displaystyle\eta(1-p)+\tau_{x}^{-1}\left[\Theta(p)(1-p)-\Theta(-p)(1+p)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\eta+\tau_{x}^{-1}\mathrm{sgn}\;p)-(\eta+\tau_{x}^{-1})p

(36)

The steady polarization is gotten by
\dot{p}^{S}=0
, which gives

\displaystyle p^{S}=\frac{\eta-\tau_{x}^{-1}}{\eta+\tau_{x}^{-1}},\;p^{S}\leq 0\;

(37a)

\displaystyle p^{S}=1,\;p^{S}>0.

(37b)

The solution is discontinuous at
p^{S}=0
, which gives the critical electrical field for valley flipping as,

\frac{E_{c}}{E^{*}}=\frac{1}{\sin\theta\ln(\tau_{x}/\tau_{i})}

(38)

where
E^{*}=\sqrt{3}\hbar Q/(2e\tau_{i})
is the electrical field unit.

At
\theta=\pi/2
, the critical field strength in the zero doping limit Eq. (

) is plotted as a function of
\tau_{x}/\tau_{i}
in Fig.

(a) by the blue line.
Besides, numerically results by solving the Boltzmann equation at finite doping concentrations are also plotted for comparisons.
By taking the relaxation times as
\tau_{x}/\tau_{i}=100
(the gray dashed in Fig.

(a)), the critical field strength is also plotted as a function of hole doping strength
n_{h}
in Fig.

(b).

) and the orange and red lines at finite doping concentrations are numerically solved from the Boltzmann equation.

(the dashed gray line in (a)).

Appendix F
Symmetry Restrictions on the Angular Pattern of Current Responses

Assume the system is
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
-polarized and the surviving mirror symmetry is
M=M_{\pi/3}
with respect to the mirror line
\theta=\pi/3
.
After the mirror operation, the electrical field and steady hole distribution transform to
M\bm{E}
and
n^{S}_{M\bm{k}}
respectively, and the current becomes

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{\hbar}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;[-\nabla_{\bm{k}}{\varepsilon}^{0}_{\bm{k}}+e(M\bm{E})\times\hat{z}\Omega_{\bm{k}}]n^{S}_{M\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e}{\hbar}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;M[-\nabla_{\bm{k}}{\varepsilon}^{0}_{\bm{k}}+e(M\bm{E})\times\hat{z}\Omega_{\bm{k}}]n^{S}_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\bm{j}_{N}-\bm{j}_{H}.

(39)

We can see that the normal current
\bm{j}^{\prime}_{N}=M\bm{j}_{N}
transforms along with the mirror operation while the Hall current
\bm{j}^{\prime}_{H}=-\bm{j}_{H}
only changes sign.
This reflects the fact that the Hall current is a pseudo-vector.
Besides, after the mirror operation, the unit vectors in longitudinal and transverse directions also change to
\hat{r}^{\prime}=M\hat{r}
and
\hat{\theta}^{\prime}=\hat{z}\times(M\hat{r})=-M(\hat{z}\times\hat{r})=-M\hat{\theta}
.
Thus the different current components transform as
j^{\prime}_{N,\parallel}\equiv\hat{r}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{\prime}_{N}=j_{N,\parallel}
,
j^{\prime}_{N,\perp}\equiv\hat{\theta}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{\prime}_{N}=-j_{N,\perp}
and
j^{\prime}_{H}\equiv\hat{\theta}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{\prime}_{H}=j_{H}
respectively.
In other words, the electrical current as a function of
\theta
should satisfy

\displaystyle j_{N,\parallel}(2\pi/3-\theta)=j_{N,\parallel}(\theta)

(40a)

\displaystyle j_{N,\perp}(2\pi/3-\theta)=-j_{N,\perp}(\theta)

(40b)

\displaystyle j_{H}(2\pi/3-\theta)=j_{H}(\theta).

(40c)

In the AC case, since the applied AC electrical field satisfies
\tilde{\bm{E}}(t;\theta+\pi)=\tilde{\bm{E}}(t+T/2;\theta)
, there is an additional tempo-spatial symmetry
\bm{j}(t;\theta+\pi)=\bm{j}(t+T/2;\theta)
which reads

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{(n)}(\theta+\pi)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\textrm{d}t\;\bm{j}(t;\theta+\pi)\textrm{e}^{in\omega t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\textrm{d}t\;\bm{j}(t+T/2;\theta)\textrm{e}^{in\omega t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{-T/2}^{T/2}\textrm{d}t\;\bm{j}(t;\theta)\textrm{e}^{in\omega t}(-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}\bm{j}^{(n)}(\theta)

(41)

in the frequency domain.
When the electrical field reverses in the second half period (
t\to t+T/2
,
\theta\to\theta-\pi
), the unit vectors in the longitudinal and transverse direction also reverse, i.e.,
\hat{r}\to\hat{r}^{\prime\prime}=-\hat{r}
,
\hat{\theta}\to\hat{\theta}^{\prime\prime}=-\hat{\theta}
.
So the different current components should satisfy

\displaystyle j^{(n)}_{N,\parallel}(\theta+\pi)=\hat{r}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{(n)}(\theta+\pi)=(-1)^{n+1}j^{(n)}_{N,\parallel}(\theta)

(42a)

\displaystyle j^{(n)}_{N,\perp}(\theta+\pi)=\hat{\theta}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{(n)}(\theta+\pi)=(-1)^{n+1}j^{(n)}_{N,\perp}(\theta)

(42b)

\displaystyle j^{(n)}_{H}(\theta+\pi)=\hat{\theta}^{\prime\prime}\cdot\bm{j}^{(n)}(\theta+\pi)=(-1)^{n+1}j^{(n)}_{H}(\theta)

(42c)

Appendix G
Angular Pattern of the Second Order Resistivity

In the main text, we calculated the angular pattern of the second order current responses, which is the second order conductivity.
However, the quantities directly measured in the experiments are usually resistivity rather than conductivity.
To better compare with the experimental results, we will study the second order resistivity in this part.
For simplicity, we only focus on the weak field limit where the field driving effect is insignificant and the perturbation theory still works.

For convenience, we will assume the system is polarized in the
\mathcal{Q}_{3}
valley.
Since the Hall current
\bm{j}_{H}
is two orders smaller than the normal current
\bm{j}_{N}
as illustrated in Fig.
6
, we will ignore the Hall current responses in the following discussion.
Then up to second order of
E
, the current is written as

j_{a}=\sum_{b}\sigma^{(1)}_{ab}E_{b}+\sum_{b,c}\sigma^{(2)}_{abc}E_{b}E_{c},

(43)

where
a,b,c=x,y
are spatial indices,
\sigma^{(1)}
and
\sigma^{(2)}
are the first and second order conductivity tensors respectively.
According to the perturbation theory, these conductivity tensors are calculated as

\displaystyle\sigma^{(1)}_{ab}=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{e}{\hbar}\right)^{2}\tau_{i}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;n^{0}_{\bm{k}}\partial_{k_{a}}\partial_{k_{b}}\varepsilon^{0}_{\bm{k}},

(44a)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(2)}_{abc}=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{e}{\hbar}\right)^{3}\tau_{i}^{2}\int\frac{\textrm{d}^{2}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\;n^{0}_{\bm{k}}\partial_{k_{a}}\partial_{k_{b}}\partial_{k_{c}}\varepsilon^{0}_{\bm{k}},

(44b)

which are symmetric with respect to the spatial indices, i.e.,
\sigma^{(1)}_{ab}=\sigma^{(1)}_{ba}
and
\sigma^{(2)}_{abc}=\sigma^{(2)}_{acb}=\sigma^{(2)}_{bac}
.
Besides, due to the restriction of the mirror symmetry with respect to the
x
-axis, we have

\displaystyle j_{x}(E_{x},-E_{y})=j_{x}(E_{x},E_{y}),

(45a)

\displaystyle j_{y}(E_{y},-E_{y})=-j_{y}(E_{x},E_{y}),

(45b)

which requires
\sigma^{(1)}_{xy}=0
and
\sigma^{(2)}_{xxy}=\sigma^{(2)}_{yyy}=0
.
With the only non-zero components of the conductivity tensors, the relation between the current density and electrical field is written as

\displaystyle j_{x}=\sigma_{xx}^{(1)}E_{x}+\sigma_{xxx}^{(2)}E_{x}^{2}+\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}E_{y}^{2}

(46a)

\displaystyle j_{y}=\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}E_{y}+2\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}E_{x}E_{y}.

(46b)

Similarly, the electrical field
\bm{E}
could also be written as a series of
\bm{j}

\displaystyle E_{x}=\rho^{(1)}_{xx}j_{x}+\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}j_{x}^{2}+\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}j_{y}^{2},

(47a)

\displaystyle E_{y}=\rho^{(1)}_{yy}j_{y}+2\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}j_{x}j_{y},

(47b)

where
\rho^{(1)}
,
\rho^{(2)}
are the first and second order resistivity tensors.
Substitute Eq. (
47
) into Eq. (
46
) and keep up to the second order of
j
we have

\displaystyle j_{x}=

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{xx}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}j_{x}+\{\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}+[\rho^{(1)}_{yy}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}\}j_{y}^{2}

\displaystyle+\{\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}+[\rho^{(1)}_{xx}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xxx}\}j_{x}^{2},

(48a)

\displaystyle j_{y}=

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{yy}\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}j_{y}+2[\rho^{(1)}_{xx}\rho^{(1)}_{yy}\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}+\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}]j_{x}j_{y}.

(48b)

Eq. (
48
) holds for any
j_{x},j_{y}
, thus the coefficients of the series expansion satisfy the following system of equation

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{xx}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}=1,

(49a)

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}+[\rho^{(1)}_{yy}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}=0,

(49b)

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}+[\rho^{(1)}_{xx}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xxx}=0,

(49c)

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{yy}\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}=1,

(49d)

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{xx}\rho^{(1)}_{yy}\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}+\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}=0.

(49e)

By solving the previous system of equation, the resistivity tensors are expressed by the conductivity tensors as

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{xx}=

\displaystyle[\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}]^{-1}

(50a)

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}_{yy}=

\displaystyle[\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}]^{-1}

(50b)

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}=

\displaystyle-\sigma^{(2)}_{xxx}[\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}]^{-3}

(50c)

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}=

\displaystyle-\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}[\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}]^{-1}[\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}]^{-2}

(50d)

In Ref.
(
Lin et al. 2023
)
, the angular resolved second order resistivity in the longitudinal direction is measured.
When current
\bm{j}=j(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
is applied in the

\displaystyle E_{\parallel}=

\displaystyle E_{x}\cos\theta+E_{y}\sin\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle j[\rho^{(1)}_{xx}\cos^{2}\theta+\rho^{(1)}_{yy}\sin^{2}\theta]

\displaystyle+j^{2}[\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}\cos^{2}\theta+3\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sin^{2}\theta]\cos\theta.

(51)

Thus the second order longitudinal resistivity as a function of theta is

\rho^{(2)}_{\parallel}=[\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}\cos^{2}\theta+3\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}\sin^{2}\theta]\cos\theta.

(52)

Define a dimensionless parameter

\alpha\equiv\frac{3\rho^{(2)}_{xyy}}{\rho^{(2)}_{xxx}}=\frac{3[\sigma^{(1)}_{xx}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xyy}}{[\sigma^{(1)}_{yy}]^{2}\sigma^{(2)}_{xxx}}.

(53)

Then, according to Eq. (

), the angular pattern of the second order longitudinal resistivity

is totally determined by
\alpha
.
At fixed hold density

in Fig.

(a).

.

is small and

, the resistivity takes its maximum value at the polarization direction and the angular pattern shown in Fig.

(
Lin et al. 2023
)
(FIG.2.b).
With the increase of the interlayer potential

has two maximum directions distributed symmetrically on the two sides of the mirror line (when the system is polarized in the

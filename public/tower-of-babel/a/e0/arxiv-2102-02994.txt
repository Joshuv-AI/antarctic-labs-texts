Bounds of Dirichlet eigenvalues for Hardy-Leray operator


Abstract
The purpose of this paper is to study the eigenvalues { λ μ , i } i \{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i} for the Dirichlet Hardy-Leray operator, i.e. − Δ ​ u + μ ​ | x | − 2 ​ u = λ ​ u in ​ Ω , u = 0 on ∂ Ω , -\Delta u+\mu|x|^{-2}u=\lambda u\ \ {\rm in}\ \,\Omega,\quad\quad u=0\ \ {\rm on}\ \ \partial\Omega, where − Δ + μ | x | 2 -\Delta+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}} is the Hardy-Leray operator with μ ≥ − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 \mu\geq-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4} and Ω \Omega is a smooth bounded domain with 0 ∈ Ω 0\in\Omega . We provide lower bounds of { λ μ , i } i \{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i} , as well as the Li-Yau’s one when μ > − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 \mu>-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4} and Karachalios’s bounds for μ ∈ [ − ( N − 2 ) 2 4 , 0 ) \mu\in[-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4},0) . Secondly, we obtain Cheng-Yang’s type upper bounds for λ μ , k \lambda_{\mu,k} . Addtionally we get the Weyl’s limit of eigenvalues which is independent of the potential’s parameter μ \mu . This interesting phenomena indicates that the inverse-square potential does not play an essential role for the asymptotic behavior of the spectrum of the problem under study.

Introduction and main results

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
,
0\in\Omega
and

\mu_{0}:=-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
the best constant for the standard Hardy inequality. The purpose of this paper is to study
the bounds of eigenvalues for the Dirichlet problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}u=\lambda u&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{L_{\text{\tiny$\Delta\,$}}\!}u=0&{\rm{on}}\ \,\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}:=-\Delta+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}
for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
is the Hardy-Leray operator.

When
\mu=0
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
reduces to the Laplacian. In 1912, Weyl
[
45
]
showed that the
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of the Dirichlet problem for any smooth bounded domain
\Omega
with the Laplacian operator has the following asymptotic behavior

\lim_{k\to+\infty}k^{-\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{k}=c_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}\quad{\rm with}\ \ c_{N}=(2\pi)^{2}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2}{N}},

(1.2)

here
c_{N}
is named as Weyl’s constant,
|\Omega|
is the volume of
\Omega
and
B_{1}
is the unit ball centered at the origin.
Later, Pólya
[
43
]
(in 1960) proved that

\lambda_{k}\geq C|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}}

(1.3)

with
C=c_{N},\,(N=2)
and for any “plane-covering domain” in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, ( his proof also works in dimension
N\geq 3
)
and he also conjectured that (
1.3
) holds with
C=c_{N}
for any bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Later on, Lieb
[
38
]
proved (
1.3
) with some positive constant
C
in a general bounded domain and then Li-Yau
[
37
]
improved this constant in (
1.3
) to

C_{N}:=\frac{N}{N+2}c_{N}=\frac{N}{N+2}(2\pi)^{2}|B_{1}|^{-\frac{2}{N}}.

(1.4)

The estimate (
1.3
) with
C=C_{N}
is also called Berezin-Li-Yau’s inequality because the constant
C_{N}
is achieved thanks to Legendre transform in an earlier result obtained by Berezin
[
4
]
. Here we denote
C_{N}
the Li-Yau’s constant. The Berezin-Li-Yau inequality then is generalized in
[
19
,
38
,
33
,
40
,
16
]
for degenerate elliptic operators.
Upper bounds for the first
k
-eigenvalues obtained in
[
34
]
is controlled by
c_{N}k^{\frac{2}{N}}
together with lower order terms; later on, Cheng-Yang in
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
developed a very interesting upper bound condition

\lambda_{k}\leq\Big(1+\frac{4}{N}\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{1},

which is also named Cheng-Yang’s inequality. More results on minimizing the eigenvalues problems could be found in
[
24
,
33
,
22
]
, subject to the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition, to
[
2
,
8
]
with Neumann boundary condition.
Our motivations of the Dirichlet eigenvalues for Hardy operators are twofold, the one is the Polya’s conjecture associating the zero of the Riemann Zeta function with the eigenvalue of a Hermitian operator and the latter is the role of the critical potential in the analysis of PDEs.

When
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and
\mu\not=0
, the Hardy potential has homogeneity
-2
, which is critical from both
mathematical and physical viewpoints. The Hardy-Leary problems have numerous applications in various
fields as molecular physics
[
35
]
, quantum cosmological models such as the
Wheeler-de-Witt equation (see e.g.
[
3
]
) and combustion models
[
30
]
.
The Hardy inequalities
play a fundamental role in the study of Hardy-Leray problems. When the potential’s singularity
\{0\}
is in
\Omega
,
the Hardy inequality
[
20
, (2)]
(also see
[
7
,
6
]
) reads as

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx+\mu_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}dx\geq c_{0}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}(x)dx,\quad\forall\,u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

(1.5)

for some constant
c_{0}>0
. More Hardy-Lieb-Thirring inequalities inequalities could see
[
23
,
27
,
28
]
.
The operator
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
is then positive definite for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},+\infty)
. More properties of Hardy-Leray operator
can be found in
[
9
,
41
]
. It is worth noting that the inverse square potential plays an essential role
in isolated singularity of elliptic Hardy equations. Indeed, for a given ‘source’
f
defined in
\Omega
, the solutions of the Dirichlet problem

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}u=f\quad\text{in }\ \Omega\setminus\{0\},\qquad u=0\quad\text{on }\;\partial\Omega

with isolated singularities at
\{0\}
are classified fully in
[
13
]
, thanks to a new formulation of distributional identity associated to some specific weight. Extensive treatments of the associated semilinear problems are developed in
[
1
,
14
,
15
]
via an introduction of a notion of very weak solution.

Due to the inverse square potential, the spectral of the Hardy-Leray operator remains widely unexplored. In
[
21
]
the authors investigated the essential spectral for general Hardy-Leray potential, for problem (
1.1
), an increasing sequence of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}(\Omega)\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, simply denoted by
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in the sequel, are obtained in
[
44
]
;
[
10
]
studied the optimal first eigenvalue with respect to the domain, and the lower bounds estimates of eigenvalues for (
1.1
) in
[
32
,
46
]
are derived by using the estimates of the related heat kernel and Sobolev inequalities. Precisely, the lower bounds state as following:

(i)

when
N\geq 3
and
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
,
there holds that for
k\in\mathbb{N}

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\Big)\frac{N(N-2)}{4e}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{\frac{2}{N}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}};

(1.6)

(ii)

when
N\geq 3
and
\mu=\mu_{0}
,
there holds that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{\mu_{0},k}\geq e^{-1}S_{N}(N-2)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}\|\mathbb{X}_{1}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}},

where
\mathbb{X}_{1}(x):=\big(-\log(\frac{|x|}{D_{0}})\big)^{-1},\ \forall\,x\in\Omega
,

D_{0}:=\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|
, and
S_{N}:=2^{2/N}\pi^{1+1/N}\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})

is the best constant of Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\Gamma
is the well-known Gamma function.

We note that the defect of the lower bound (
1.6
) is the coefficient
(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})\frac{N(N-2)}{4e}\to 0
as
\mu\to\mu_{0}^{+}
.

It is natural to ask whether the Hardy-Leray potential plays an essential role in the estimates of the spectral estimates or how it works on
the related eigenvalues. Our main aim in this paper is to answer this question and to establish the lower and upper bounds,
and show the limit the eigenvalues as
k\to+\infty
. Now we state our lower bounds as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
D_{0}=\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|
and
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
).

(i)
If
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
and
N\geq 3
, then we have that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{\mu,i}\geq\max\left\{\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\Big)C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}+\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}k,\ b_{k}e^{-1}\sigma_{\mu}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}\right\},

where
C_{N}
is the Li-Yau’s constant defined in (
1.4
),

b_{k}>\frac{1}{2},\ \lim_{k\to+\infty}b_{k}=\frac{N}{N+2}
and

\sigma_{\mu}=\max\Big\{S_{N}(N-2)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}\|\mathbb{X}_{1}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{N}},\,\big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\big)\frac{N(N-2)}{4}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}^{\frac{2}{N}}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}\Big\};

(1.7)

(ii)
If
\mu>0
and
N\geq 2
, then we have that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{\mu,i}\geq C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}+D_{0}^{-2}\mu\,k.

We remark that the regularity of
\Omega
can be released to ‘bounded’ for the lower bounds, and for
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
, the lower bounds in part
(i)
consists of the Berezin-Li-Yau’s type bounds
and Karachalios’s type bounds. In fact, the Li-Yau’s method can’t be applied for
\mu=\mu_{0}
because of
the factor
\big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\big)
, which vanishes as
\mu\to\mu_{0}^{+}
. This also occurs for the Karachalios’s estimate (
1.6
).
To overcome this decay, we develop the Karacholios’s method and our lower bound appears

e^{-1}\sigma_{\mu}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}
, where
\sigma_{\mu}
has the nondecreasing monotonicity for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
; Particularly

\sigma_{\mu}\geq\sigma_{\mu_{0}}=S_{N}(N-2)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}\|\mathbb{X}_{1}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{N}}>0.

From the monotonicity of
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
).

(i)
If
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
and
N\geq 3
, then for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq\max\left\{\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\Big)C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}}+\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1},\ b_{k}e^{-1}\sigma_{\mu}k^{\frac{2}{N}}\right\}.

(ii)
If
\mu>0
and
N\geq 2
, then for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}}+D_{0}^{-2}\mu.

In order to obtain upper bounds, we extend Cheng-Yang’s type inequality for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and our upper bounds state as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
).

(i)
If
\mu\geq 0
and
N\geq 2
, then

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\Big(1+\frac{4}{N}\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{\mu,1}.

(1.8)

(ii)
If
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
and
N\geq 3
, then

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\Big(1+\frac{4}{N}\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{0,1}+\mu D_{0}^{-2}.

(1.9)

It is worth noting that the crucial inequality for obtaining the Cheng-Yang’s type inequality (
1.8
) is the following

\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}\leq 2a_{\mu}\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})\lambda_{\mu,i},

(1.10)

which holds with
a_{\mu}=\frac{2}{N}
if
\mu>0
.
However, (
1.10
) has the constant
a_{\mu}=\frac{2}{N}\frac{-\mu_{0}}{\mu-\mu_{0}}
if
\mu\in(\mu_{0},0)
for
N\geq 3
, then it will give an equality like

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\Big(1+a_{\mu}\Big)k^{a_{\mu}}\lambda_{\mu,1}
, which is too rough since
a_{\mu}>\frac{2}{N}

and
\displaystyle\lim_{\mu\to\mu_{0}^{+}}a_{\mu}=+\infty
. So we adopt an approach
of comparing with the Laplacian directly.

Combining the estimate of the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{\mu,1}
, we have the following corollary:

Corollary 1.4
.

For
N\geq 3
and
\mu>0
, we have that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\Big(\lambda_{0,1}+\mu\|\phi_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-2}dx\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}.

(1.11)

For
N\geq 2
and
\mu>0
,

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\bigg(\lambda_{0,1}+c_{1}^{-2}\tau_{+}(\mu)\|\phi_{0,1}\|^{2}_{C^{1}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}(x)|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)}dx}\bigg)k^{\frac{2}{N}},

(1.12)

where
c_{0}>0
,
\tau_{+}(\mu)=-\frac{N-2}{2}+\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}
,
(\lambda_{0,1},\phi_{0,1})
is the first eigenvalue and associated eigenfunctions of (
1.1
) for
\mu=0
and
\rho(x)={\rm dist}(x,\partial\Omega)
.

It is remarkable that the upper bound (
1.11
) is much simple, but it can’t be used in the case that
N=2
and the upper bound (
1.12
) is available for
N\geq 2
with the help of the value
\tau_{+}(\mu)>0
for
\mu>0
.

Finally, we provide the Weyl’s limit of eigenvalues for Hardy-Leray operators.

Theorem 1.5
.

Assume that
N\geq 2
,
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
). Then there holds

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{\mu,k}k^{-\frac{2}{N}}=c_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}},

Lower bounds

2.1
Berezin-Li-Yau’s type lower bounds

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
and
\{\lambda_{\mu,i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
).
Then we have

(i)
for
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
there holds

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{\mu,i}\geq\Big(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\Big)C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}+\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}k;

(ii)
for
\mu>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
there holds

\sum^{k}_{i=1}\lambda_{\mu,i}\geq C_{N}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}k^{1+\frac{2}{N}}+D_{0}^{-2}\mu\,k,

where we recall
D_{0}=\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|
.

For
N\geq 2
,
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, we denote

H_{\mu}(\Omega)
as the completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with the norm

\|u\|_{\mu}=\sqrt{\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx}

and it is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla vdx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{uv}{|x|^{2}}dx.

We remark that

H_{\mu}(\Omega)=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
for
\mu>\mu_{0}
and if
\mu_{0}\not=0
,
H_{\mu_{0}}(\Omega)\varsupsetneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

The following lemma is crucial to get the Berezin-Li-Yau’s lower bound whch is appeared in
[
37
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
37
, Lemma 1]
If
f
is a real-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
0\leq f\leq M_{1}
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(z)|z|^{2}dz\leq M_{2},

then we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(z)dz\leq(M_{1}|B_{1}|)^{\frac{2}{N+2}}\Big(M_{2}\frac{N+2}{N}\Big)^{\frac{N}{N+2}}.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Let

(\lambda_{\mu,k},\phi_{k})
be the eigenvalue and eigenfunction pair of (
1.1
)
such that for positive integer
k

\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1.

Then

\lambda_{\mu,k}=\min\Big\{\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}u^{2}\Big)dx\>:\>u\in{\mathbb{H}}_{k}(\Omega)\>\text{with}\>\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1\Big\},

where

{\mathbb{H}}_{1}(\Omega)=H_{\mu}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathbb{H}}_{k}(\Omega)=\{u\in H_{\mu}(\Omega)\>:\>\text{$\int_{\Omega}u\phi_{j}\,dx=0$ for $j=1,\dots,k-1$}\}\quad\text{for $k>1$.}

Moreover,
\{\phi_{k}\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu}(\Omega):\>k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Denote

\Phi_{k}(x,y):=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(x)\phi_{j}(y)

and its Fourier transform is then

\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)=(2\pi)^{-\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{k}(x,y)e^{-{\rm i}\,x\cdot z}dx.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}dzdy=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\Phi_{k}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\int_{\Omega}\phi^{2}_{j}(x)dx=k

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\Omega}\big|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{k}(x,y)e^{-{\rm i}\,x\cdot z}dx\big|^{2}dy=(2\pi)^{-N}\big|\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(x)e^{-{\rm i}\,x\cdot z}dx\big|^{2},

which implies by Bessel’s inequality (see
[
39
, (1.2)]
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\Omega}|e^{{\rm-i}\,x\cdot z}|^{2}dx=(2\pi)^{-N}|\Omega|.

Meanwhile, the Hardy inequality (
1.5
) implies that for
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu_{0}\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx,

thus, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}

\displaystyle|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}|z|^{2}dydz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{k}(x,y)|^{2}dydx=\int_{\Omega}\Big|\sum^{k}_{j=1}\nabla\phi_{j}(x)\Big|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})^{-1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\Big|\nabla\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}(x)\Big|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)}{|x|^{2}}dx-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}^{2}(x)dx\Big)

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})^{-1}\Big(\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{\mu,j}-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}k\Big).

For
\mu>0
, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\geq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu D_{0}^{-2}\int_{\Omega}u^{2}dx,

and then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}|z|^{2}dydz

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\Phi_{k}(x,y)|^{2}dydx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big|\sum^{k}_{j=1}\nabla\phi_{j}\Big|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx-D_{0}^{-2}\mu\int_{\Omega}\Big(\sum^{k}_{j=1}\phi_{j}\Big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{\mu,j}-\mu D_{0}^{-2}k.

Now for
Case:
N\geq 3
,
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
.
We apply Lemma
2.2
to the function
f(z)=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\hat{\Phi}_{k}(z,y)|^{2}dy

with

M_{1}=(2\pi)^{-N}|\Omega|\quad{\rm and}\quad M_{2}=(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})^{-1}\Big(\sum^{k}_{j=1}\lambda_{\mu,j}-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}}\lambda_{\mu_{0},1}k\Big),

2.2
Karachalios’ type lower bound

Recall that
S_{N}=2^{2/N}\pi^{1+1/N}\Gamma(\frac{N+1}{2})
is the best constant of Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. In this subsection, we would use the following notations

\mathbb{X}_{1}(x)=\big(-\log(\frac{|x|}{D_{0}})\big)^{-1}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \mathbb{X}_{2}(x)\equiv 1,\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega,

where we recall
D_{0}=\max_{x\in\partial\Omega}|x|
. Note that

\displaystyle\|\mathbb{X}_{1}^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{D_{0}}}\mathbb{X}_{1}^{1-N}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{{}_{N-1}}\int_{0}^{D_{0}}r^{N-1}(-\log\frac{r}{D_{0}})^{N-1}dr<+\infty

and
\|\mathbb{X}_{2}^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=|\Omega|.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume that
N\geq 3
,
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
and
\{\lambda_{\mu,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the increasing sequence of eigenvalues of problem (
1.1
).
Then for
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq e^{-1}\sigma_{\mu}k^{\frac{2}{N}},

where
\sigma_{\mu}
is defined in (
1.7
).

Proof.
When
\mu=\mu_{0}
, the proof is refereed to
[
46
]
and we focus on the case
\mu_{0}<\mu<0
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
be the Hardy-Leray operator with its domain defined as

D({\mathcal{L}}_{\mu})=C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and its Friedrich’s extension, still denoted by
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
, with its domain defined as

D({\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}):=\big\{u\in H_{\mu}(\Omega):{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\big\},

which is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
and the operator gives rise to the semigroup of operators
e^{-{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}t}
for every
t>0
, possessing an integral kernel
K(x,y,t)>0
for all
(x,y,t)\in\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
. Then
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
has compact resolvent and
K
can be represented as

K(x,y,t)=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}e^{-\lambda_{\mu,i}t}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y),

which solves the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}K+{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}K=0&{\rm in}\ \,\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\,}K(x,y,t)>0&{\rm{in}}\ \,\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\,}K(x,y,t)=0&{\rm{on}}\ \,\partial\Omega\times\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

Since
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it follows that

h(t):=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}e^{-2\lambda_{\mu,i}t}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}K^{2}(x,y,t)dxdy.

By the improved Hardy-Sobolev inequalities,
[
26
, Theorem A]

for
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, (the following inequality is sharp for
\mu=\mu_{0}
)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\geq\sigma_{1}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}\mathbb{X}_{1}(x)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}},\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)

and for
\mu>\mu_{0}

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\geq\sigma_{2}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2^{*}}dx\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}},\quad\forall\,u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
,

\sigma_{1}=S_{N}(N-2)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\sigma_{2}=\frac{N(N-2)}{4}(1-\frac{\mu}{\mu_{0}})\omega_{{}_{N-1}}.

By Hölder inequality, we get that for
j=1,2
,

\displaystyle h(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\Big(\big(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{X}_{j}(y)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}K^{2^{*}}(x,y,t)dy\big)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}-1}}\big(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{X}_{j}(y)^{-\frac{2(N-1)}{(N-2)(2^{*}-2)}}K(x,y,t)dy\big)^{\frac{2^{*}-2}{2^{*}-1}}\Big)dx

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{X}_{j}(y)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}K^{2^{*}}(x,y,t)dy\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}dx\Big]^{\frac{2^{*}}{2(2^{*}-1)}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}Q_{j}^{2}(x,t)dx\Big)^{\frac{2^{*}-2}{2(2^{*}-1)}},

(2.2)

where
\frac{2(N-1)}{(N-2)(2^{*}-2)}=\frac{N-1}{2}
and

Q_{j}(x,t)=\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{X}_{j}(y)^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}K(x,y,t)dy.

We observe that
Q_{j}(x,t)
is the solution of the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}Q_{j}+{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}Q_{j}=0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\,\partial_{t}\ }Q_{j}(x,0)=\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}&{\rm{in}}\ \ \Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\,\partial_{t}\ \,}Q_{j}(x,t)=0&{\rm{on}}\ \ \partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty).\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

Multiplying the above equation (
2.3
) by
Q_{j}
, we get the energy equation

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|Q_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|Q_{j}(t)\|^{2}_{\mu}=0

and then

\|Q_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\|Q_{j}(0)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

With the help of above inequalities, letting

C_{j}:=\|\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{2(2^{*}-1)}{2^{*}}}\quad{\rm for}\ \ j=1,2,

for
\mu>\mu_{0}
, from (
2.2
) we obtain that

\displaystyle h^{\frac{2(2^{*}-1)}{2^{*}}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{j}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\mathbb{X}_{j}(y)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}K^{2^{*}}(x,y,t)dy\Big)^{\frac{2}{2^{*}}}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\Big(|\nabla_{y}K(x,y,t)|^{2}dx+\mu\frac{K^{2}(x,y,t)}{|y|^{2}}\Big)dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\|Q_{j}(t)\|^{2}_{\mu}=-\frac{C_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|Q_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{C_{j}}{\sigma_{j}}\frac{1}{2}\frac{dh(t)}{dt},

which, using
h(0)=+\infty
, implies that

\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}e^{-2\lambda_{\mu,i}t}=h(t)\leq\Big(\frac{2^{*}}{2\sigma_{j}(2^{*}-2)}\Big)^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\|\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}t^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\quad{\rm for}\ \ j=1,2.

Now we choose

t=\frac{2^{*}}{2(2^{*}-2)}\frac{1}{\lambda_{\mu,k}}

and

ke^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\leq\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}e^{-\frac{2^{*}\lambda_{\mu,i}}{\lambda_{\mu,k}(2^{*}-2)}}\leq\sigma_{j}^{-\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\lambda_{\mu,k}^{\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}}\|\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad{\rm for}\ \ j=1,2.

Note that
\frac{2^{*}}{2^{*}-2}=\frac{N}{2}
and we conclude that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq e^{-1}\sigma_{j}\|\mathbb{X}_{j}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{N}}k^{\frac{2}{N}}\quad{\rm for}\ j=1,2.

As a consequence, we obtain that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\geq e^{-1}\sigma_{\mu}k^{\frac{2}{N}},

where

\sigma_{\mu}=\max\Big\{\sigma_{1}\|\mathbb{X}_{1}^{\frac{1-N}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{N}},\,\sigma_{2}|\Omega|^{-\frac{2}{N}}\Big\}.

We complete the proof.
\Box

Proposition 3.2

Assume that

Then

for

(3.2)

(ii)

(3.3)

Proof.
The proof for

\delta_{ii}=1

with

By the orthonormality of
\phi_{i}

\varphi_{i}

i.e.

2b_{ij}=(\lambda_{\mu,i}-\lambda_{\mu,j})a_{ij}.

(3.4)

Note that

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}\varphi_{i}=\lambda_{\mu,i}g\phi_{i}-2\nabla g\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}-\sum^{k}_{j=1}a_{ij}\lambda_{\mu,j}\phi_{j}.

Hence, we infer that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{i}\|_{\mu}^{2}

(3.5)

\lambda_{\mu,k+1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}dx\leq\|\varphi_{i}\|_{\mu}^{2}=\lambda_{\mu,i}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}^{2}dx+1+\sum^{k}_{j=1}(\lambda_{\mu,i}-\lambda_{\mu,j})a_{ij}^{2},

(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}

i
from 1 through
k

\displaystyle-2\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\nabla g\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}dx

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}+\sum^{k}_{i,j=1}(\lambda_{\mu,i}-\lambda_{\mu,j})(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}a_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}-4\sum^{k}_{i,j=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})b_{ij}^{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\theta

by the symmetry of
a_{ij}
and the anti-symmetry of
b_{ij}
.

(to be determined later)

\displaystyle\theta^{2}

\displaystyle=\bigg(-2\sum^{k}_{i=1}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{i}\nabla g\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}dx\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=4\bigg(\sum^{k}_{i=1}\int_{\Omega}(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{2}\varphi_{i}\nabla g\cdot\nabla\phi_{i}dx-(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\lambda_{\mu,i})^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\Omega}\sum^{k}_{j=1}d_{ij}\varphi_{i}\phi_{j}dx\bigg)^{2}

3.7
) implies that

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\frac{2+N}{N}\Lambda_{k}\Big)^{2}\leq\frac{4+N}{N}F_{k}-\frac{4+N}{N}\frac{2+N}{N}\Lambda_{k}^{2}

and then

\displaystyle\frac{2}{4+N}\lambda_{\mu,k+1}^{2}+\frac{4+N}{2+N}\Big(\lambda_{\mu,k+1}-\frac{4+N}{N}\Lambda_{k}\Big)^{2}\leq\frac{4+N}{N}F_{k},

that means

\lambda_{\mu,k+1}^{2}\leq\frac{N}{N+2}(\frac{N+4}{N})^{2}F_{k}\leq(1+\frac{4}{N})^{2}k^{\frac{4}{N}}\lambda_{\mu,1}^{2}.

Thus, we have that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}\lambda_{\mu,1}.

(3.8)

Part
(ii)
: Case of
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
.
In this case, the constant
\frac{4}{N}\frac{-\mu_{0}}{\mu-\mu_{0}}>\frac{4}{N}
, which will produce a higher order for parameter
k
from Proposition
3.1
. So we shall derive the upper bounds by comparing with the eigenvalues of Laplacian Dirichlet problem.

Let

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}={\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}-\mu D_{0}^{-2}

and
\lambda_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu},k}
be the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
. Clearly we have

\lambda_{\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu},k}=\lambda_{\mu,k}-\mu D_{0}^{-2}.

Then for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
,

\langle\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}u,u\rangle<\langle-\Delta u,u\rangle,\quad\forall u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)

It follows by
[
5
, Theorem 10.2.2]
that

\lambda_{\mu,k}-\mu D_{0}^{-2}\leq\lambda_{0,k},\quad k=1,2,\cdots

together with the upper bound in
[
16
, (2.10)]

\lambda_{0,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\lambda_{0,1}k^{\frac{2}{N}},

we imply that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\lambda_{0,1}k^{\frac{2}{N}}+\mu D_{0}^{-2},\quad k=1,2,\cdots.

The proof is complete.
\Box

Proof of Corollary
1.4
:

Recall that
(\lambda_{0,1},\phi_{0,1})
is the first Dirichlet eigenvalue and associated eigenfunctions of Laplacian, then for
\mu>0
and
N\geq 3

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu,1}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\nabla\phi_{0,1}|^{2}dx+\mu\int_{\Omega}\frac{\phi_{0,1}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\leq\lambda_{0,1}+\mu\|\phi_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-2}dx,

which, together with (
3.8
), implies that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\Big(\lambda_{0,1}+\mu\|\phi_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{\Omega}|x|^{-2}dx\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}.

For
\mu>0
and
N\geq 2
, take test function
u=\phi_{0,1}\Gamma_{\mu}
where
\Gamma_{\mu}(x)=|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}\,{\rm with}\ \tau_{+}(\mu)=-\frac{N-2}{2}+\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}
, which satisfying
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}\Gamma_{\mu}=0\,{\rm in}\,\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{\mu,1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\mu\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{0,1}+\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi_{0,1}^{2}\cdot\nabla\Gamma_{\mu}^{2}dx}{2\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi_{0,1}^{2}\Gamma_{\mu}^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{0,1}+c_{1}^{-2}\tau_{+}(\mu)\|\phi_{0,1}\|^{2}_{C^{1}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}(x)|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)}dx},

where
c_{1}>0
such that

\phi_{0,1}(x)\geq c_{1}\rho(x),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega.

Together with (
3.8
), we obtain that

\lambda_{\mu,k}\leq\big(1+\frac{4}{N}\big)\Big(\lambda_{0,1}+c_{1}^{-2}\tau_{+}(\mu)\|\phi_{0,1}\|^{2}_{C^{1}}\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}dx}{\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\rho^{2}(x)|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)}dx}\Big)k^{\frac{2}{N}}.

The proof is complete.
\Box

4
Limit of Eigenvalues

Our proof of Weyl’s limit of eigenvalues relies on estimates of the partition function

Z_{\mu}(t):=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}e^{-\lambda_{\mu,i}t},\quad t>0,

which can be written as

Z_{\mu}(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\beta t}d{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(\beta),

(4.1)

where
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(\beta)=\sum_{\lambda_{\mu,i}\leq\beta}1
is the usual counting function.
Additionally, it has another standard formula for the partition function

Z_{\mu}(t)=\int_{\Omega}p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,x,t)dx,

(4.2)

where
p_{\mu,\Omega}
is the heat kernel of Hardy operators
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}
in domain
\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
. More properties of heat kernel with Hardy potentials could be found in
[
25
,
41
]
. In the whole space
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we denote

p_{\mu}=p_{\mu,\mathbb{R}^{N}}
. Particularly,

p_{0}(x,y,t)=p_{0}(x-y,t)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}\ \ {\rm for}\ \ (x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty)

is the usual heat kernel of the Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The following lemma concerns the estimate of heat kernel, which is the essential part for the Weyl’s limit.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
and

\gamma_{\mu}(x,y,t)=p_{0}(x-y,t)-p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,y,t)\ \ {\rm for}\ \ (x,y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,+\infty).

Then

(i)
For
\mu>0
, there exists
c_{2}>0
independent of
\mu
such that

0\leq\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)\leq c_{2}\mu|x|^{-2}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1},\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\},\ \forall\,t\in(0,1].

(ii)
For
\mu\in(\mu_{0},0)
, there exists
c_{3}>0
such that

|\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)|\leq c_{3}|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1},\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\},\ \forall\,t\in(0,1].

(iii)
For
\mu=\mu_{0}
, there exists
c_{4}>0
such that

|\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)|\leq c_{4}\Big(|x|^{-N+\frac{N+2}{8(N-2)}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}+|x|^{-N+\frac{1}{8}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+\frac{7}{8}}\Big),\quad\forall\,x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\},\ \forall\,t\in(0,1].

Proof.

Case of
\mu>0
.
Note that elementary properties infer directly that

p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,y,t)\leq p_{0,\Omega}(x,y,t)\leq p_{0}(x-y,t)\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\times\Omega\times(0,+\infty)

and for fixed
y\in\Omega
, we have that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\partial_{t}\gamma_{\mu}-\Delta\gamma_{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{|x|^{2}}p_{\mu}&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\gamma_{\mu}-\Delta}\gamma_{\mu}=0&{\rm{on}}\ \,\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{\partial_{t}\gamma_{\mu}-\Delta}\gamma_{\mu}=0&{\rm in}\ \ \Omega\times\{0\}.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

Note that
\gamma_{\mu}
could be expressed by heat kernel, i.e. for
y\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}p_{0,\Omega}(x,z,t-s)\frac{\mu}{|z|^{2}}p_{\mu,\Omega}(z,y,s)dzds

\displaystyle\leq\mu\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}p_{0}(x-z,t-s)\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}p_{0}(z-y,s)dzds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{(4\pi)^{N}}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{4s}}dzds.

For
N\geq 3
and
x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}
, we have that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{(4\pi)^{N}}\Big(\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{|z-y|^{2}}{4s}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ \ +\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4s}}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle<\frac{\mu}{(4\pi)^{N}}\Big(4|x|^{-2}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(\frac{1}{t-s}+\frac{1}{s})|z|^{2}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ \ +\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{16}\frac{t}{(t-s)s}}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(0)}\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{(4\pi)^{N}}\Big(2^{\frac{N}{2}+2}|x|^{2}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|\tilde{z}|^{2}}d\tilde{z}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ \ +c_{5}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N-2}(\frac{|x|}{2})^{N-2}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}(\frac{|x|^{-2}}{16}\frac{t}{(t-s)s})^{-\frac{N}{2}}ds\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{(4\pi)^{N}}|x|^{-2}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}\Big(2^{\frac{N}{2}+2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz+c_{5}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N-2}2^{N+2}\Big),

where
c_{5}>0
such that

e^{-a}\leq c_{5}a^{-\frac{N}{2}}\quad{\rm for\ all}\ \ a\geq 1.

Case of
\mu\in(\mu_{0},0)
.

It is known that for
y\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mu}(x,y,t)

\displaystyle=\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}p_{0,\Omega}(x,z,t-s)\frac{\mu}{|z|^{2}}p_{\mu,\Omega}(z,y,s)dzds.

It follows from
[
41
, Theorem 3.10]
that the corresponding heat kernel verifies that for
t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,y,t)\leq p_{\mu,\mathbb{R}^{N}}(x,y,t)\leq c_{6}(|x||y|)^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}t^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-c_{7}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}},

where
c_{6},c_{7}>0
and
\tau_{+}(\mu)<0
. We see that there is no order for
p_{\mu,\Omega}
and
p_{0}
.
Thus, for
x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and
t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle|\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mu|\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}p_{0,\Omega}(x,z,t-s)\frac{1}{|z|^{2}}p_{\mu,\Omega}(z,x,s)dzds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{6}|\mu|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|z|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{7}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{6}|\mu|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}\Big(\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}|z|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{7}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}(x)}|z|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{7}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle<\frac{c|\mu|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}\Big((\frac{|x|}{2})^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{7}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{16(t-s)}-\frac{c|x|^{2}}{s}}\int_{B_{\frac{|x|}{2}}}|z|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{c_{6}|\mu|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}\Big((\frac{|x|}{2})^{\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz\int_{0}^{t}(c_{7}t+(4-c_{7})s)^{-\frac{N}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ +\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N-2}(\frac{|x|}{2})^{N+\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}c_{5}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\Big(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(t-s)}+c_{7}\frac{|x|^{2}}{s}\Big)^{-\frac{N}{2}}ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{8}|\mu|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\Big(2^{2-\tau_{+}(\mu)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz+c_{9}\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N-2}2^{N-\tau_{+}(\mu)+2}c^{-\frac{N}{2}}\Big)|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1},

where
2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2>-N
for
\mu\in(\mu_{0},\,0)
and
c_{8},c_{9}>0
.

Case of
\mu=\mu_{0}
.
When
\mu=\mu_{0}
, the above inequality holds true, but
the factor
|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu_{0})-2}=|x|^{-N}
is non-integrable in
\Omega
. So we have to modify
the above estimates.

From
[
25
, Theorem 1.1]
we have that

t\in(0,1]

\displaystyle 0\leq p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,y,t)\leq c_{10}(|x||y|)^{\frac{2-N}{2}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}}e^{-c_{11}\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{t}}.

We choose

r=\frac{1}{4}|x|^{1-\frac{1}{4(N-2)}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\theta=\frac{1}{4},

then
we have that

\displaystyle|\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c_{10}|\mu_{0}|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\frac{2-N}{2}}\Big(\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x)}|z|^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{11}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|z|^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{11}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle<\frac{c_{10}|\mu_{0}|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\frac{2-N}{2}}\Big(r^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|x-z|^{2}}{4(t-s)}-\frac{c_{11}|x-z|^{2}}{s}}dzds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{16(t-s)}-\frac{c_{11}|x|^{2}}{s}}\int_{B_{r}(0)}|z|^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}dzds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\ \frac{c_{10}|\mu_{0}|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}|x|^{\frac{2-N}{2}}\Big(r^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|z|^{2}}dz\int_{0}^{t}(c_{11}t+(4-c_{11})s)^{-\frac{N}{2}}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\ +\frac{\omega_{{}_{N-1}}}{N-2}r^{\frac{N-2}{2}}c_{14}\int^{t}_{0}\frac{1}{(t-s)^{\frac{N}{2}}s^{\frac{N}{2}}}\Big(\frac{|x|^{2}}{4(t-s)}+c_{11}\frac{|x|^{2}}{s}\Big)^{-\frac{N-\theta}{2}}ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq c_{12}|x|^{\frac{2-N}{2}}r^{-\frac{N+2}{2}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}+c_{13}|x|^{1+\theta-\frac{3}{2}N}r^{\frac{N-2}{2}}t^{-\frac{N-\theta}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}s^{-\frac{\theta}{2}}ds

\displaystyle=c_{12}|x|^{-N+\frac{N+2}{8(N-2)}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+1}+c_{13}|x|^{-N+\frac{1}{8}}t^{-\frac{N}{2}+\frac{7}{8}},

where

c_{12},c_{13}>0
and
c_{14}>0
verifies that

e^{-a}\leq c_{14}a^{-\frac{N-\frac{1}{4}}{2}}\quad{\rm for\ all}\ \ a\geq 1.

This completes the proof.
\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

From Lemma
4.1
, we see that for
t\in(0,1)
and
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,

\displaystyle t^{\frac{N}{2}}\Big|\int_{\Omega}p_{\mu,\Omega}(x,x,t)dx-\int_{\Omega}p_{0}(0,t)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle t^{\frac{N}{2}}\int_{\Omega}|\gamma_{\mu}(x,x,t)|dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{2}t^{\frac{7}{8}}\int_{\Omega}\max\{|x|^{-2},|x|^{2\tau_{+}(\mu)-2}\}dx

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\ \ \ \text{ as $t\rightarrow 0^{+}$},

then we obtain that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\frac{N}{2}}|Z_{\mu}(t)-Z_{0}(t)|=0,

where
Z_{0}
is the partition function for Laplacian and direct computation shows that

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\frac{N}{2}}Z_{0}(t)=\frac{|\Omega|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}.

So there holds

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\frac{N}{2}}Z_{\mu}(t)=\frac{|\Omega|}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}.

(4.4)

From (
4.4
) and Karamata’s Tauberian theorem
[
42
, Theorem 10.3]
, we get that

{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(\beta)\beta^{-\frac{N}{2}}\longrightarrow\frac{|\Omega|}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2}+1)(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\quad{\rm as}\ \beta\to+\infty,

which, taking
\beta=\lambda_{\mu,k}
and
{\mathcal{N}}(\lambda_{\mu,k})=k
, implies that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lambda_{\mu,k}^{-\frac{N}{2}}k=\frac{|\Omega|}{\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})\frac{N}{2}(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}=\frac{|\Omega||B_{1}|}{(2\pi)^{N}},

(4.5)

where

|B_{1}|=\frac{1}{N}\omega_{{}_{N-1}}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}}{N\Gamma(\frac{N}{2})}.

Note that (
4.5
) is equivalent to (
1.13
) and the proof is completed.
\Box

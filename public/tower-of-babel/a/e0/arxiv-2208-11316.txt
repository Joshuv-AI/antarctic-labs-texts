1.
Introduction

The investigations of the largest prime factors of shifted primes start from a cute article of Goldfeld
[
6
]
, where he proved that

\#\{p\leq x:p\in\mathcal{P},P^{+}(p-1)>p^{1/2}\}\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{x}{\log x}+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right),

where
\mathcal{P}
is the set of all primes and
P^{+}(n)
denotes the largest prime factor of integer
n
. Conventionally, we set
P^{+}(1)=1
. For
0<c<1
, let

T_{c}(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\in\mathcal{P},P^{+}(p-1)\geq p^{c}\}.

Goldfeld further remarked that one can show

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{T_{c}(x)}{\pi(x)}>0

(1.1)

provided that
c<7/12
, where
\pi(x)
is the number of primes up to
x
. Fouvry
[
5
]
improved this by showing that there is some
c_{0}\in(2/3,1)
such that inequality (
1.1
) holds.

In the same direction, Luca et al.
[
9
]
considered the lower bound for
T_{c}(x)
for small values of
c
. They proved that for
1/4\leq c\leq 1/2

T_{c}(x)\geq(1-c)\frac{x}{\log x}+E(x),

where

E(x)\ll\begin{cases}x\log\log x/(\log x)^{2},&\text{for~}1/4<c\leq 1/2,\\
x/(\log x)^{5/3},&\text{for~}c=1/4.\end{cases}

As pointed out by Chen and Chen
[
3
]
, the method of Luca et al. (essentially due to Goldfeld) cannot be applied to
c\in(0,1/4)
. By some refinements of the argument employed by Luca et al., Chen and Chen could extend
c
to the interval
(0,1/2)
with slightly better error terms of the order of magnitude
O(x/(\log x)^{2})
. Moreover, Chen and Chen proved the following interesting result. For any integer
k\geq 2
, there exists at most one
c\in[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k})
such that

T_{c}(x)=(1-c)\frac{x}{\log x}+o\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).

Based on this result, Chen and Chen made the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

For any integer
k\geq 1
and any
c\in[\frac{1}{k+1},\frac{1}{k})
, we have

T_{c}(x)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k+1}\right)\frac{x}{\log x}+o\left(\frac{x}{\log x}\right).

Let
\rho(u)
be the Dickman function, defined as the unique continuous solution of the equation
differential–difference

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho(u)=1,&0\leq u\leq 1,\\
u\rho^{\prime}(u)=-\rho(u-1),&u>1.\end{cases}

Let
\theta_{1}\thickapprox 0.3517
be the unique solution of equation

\theta-4\int_{1/\theta-1}^{1/\theta}\frac{\rho(t)}{t}dt=0.

Feng and Wu
[
4
]
proved that

T_{c}(x)\geq\left(1-4\int_{1/\theta-1}^{1/\theta}\frac{\rho(t)}{t}dt+o(1)\right)\pi(x)

provided
0<c<\theta_{1}
, which confirmed Conjecture
1.1
for
k\geq 3
by numerical values involving Dickman’s function. Later, the lower bound for
T_{c}(x)
was further improved to

T_{c}(x)\geq\left(1-4\rho(1/\theta)+o(1)\right)\pi(x)

by Liu, Wu and Xi
[
8
]
provided that
0<\theta<\theta_{2}\thickapprox 0.3734
, where
\theta_{2}
is the unique solution to equation
\theta-4\rho(1/\theta)=0
.

In the present note, we show that Conjecture
1.1
is actually incorrect for
k=1
. Precisely,

Theorem 1.1
.

There is an absolute constant
c<1
such that

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{T_{c}(x)}{\pi(x)}<1/2.

2.
Proofs

Before presenting the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we first fix some notations and then state a few lemmas to be used later. Let
\Lambda(n)
be the von Mangoldt function. As usual,
\varphi(n)
denotes the Euler totient function. Let
\pi(x;b,a)
be the number of primes
p\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ b)
up to
x
. Following Goldfeld and Luca et al., we define

L(x;u,v)=\sum_{u<m\leq v}\Lambda(m)\pi(x;m,1).

The first two lemmas are included in the proof of Goldfeld (see also
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

[
6
, bottom of page 23]

For sufficiently large
x
, we have

L(x;1,x)=x+O\left(x/\log x\right).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
6
, equations (2) and (3)]

For sufficiently large
x
, we have

L(x;1,x^{1/2})=x/2+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right).

The next lemma is another conjecture of Chen and Chen confirmed by Wu later.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
14
, Theorem 2]

For
0<c<1
, let

T^{\prime}_{c}(x)=\#\{p\leq x:p\in\mathcal{P},P^{+}(p-1)\geq x^{c}\}.

Then for sufficiently large
x
we have

T_{c}(x)=T^{\prime}_{c}(x)+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

The proof of our theorem, among other things, is based on the following deep result which appeared first in the paper of Banks and Shparlinski
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
. Here, we use the version stated by Wu with a slight adjustment from prime modulus
q
to integer modulus
m
.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.2]

There exist two functions
K_{2}(\theta)>K_{1}(\theta)>0
, defined on the interval
(0,17/32)
such that for each fixed real
A>0
, and all sufficiently large
Q=x^{\theta}
, the inequalities

K_{1}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}\leq\pi(x;m,1)\leq K_{2}(\theta)\frac{\pi(x)}{\varphi(m)}

hold for all integers
m\in(Q,2Q]
with at most
O\left(Q(\log Q)^{-A}\right)
exceptions, where the implied constant depends only on
a,A
and
\theta
. Moreover, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, these functions can be chosen to satisfy the following properties:

\bullet

K_{1}(\theta)
is monotonic decreasing, and
K_{2}(\theta)
is monotonic increasing.

\bullet

K_{1}(1/2)=1-\varepsilon
and
K_{2}(1/2)=1+\varepsilon
.

Remark. The above lemma is due to the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem for
0<\theta<1/2
, Baker–Harman
[
1
]
for
1/2\leq\theta\leq 13/25
and Mikawa
[
10
]
for
13/25\leq\theta\leq 17/32
.

Now we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
33/64<c<1
be a fixed constant. Throughout our proof,
p
and
q
will always denote primes unless indicated otherwise. The lower bound on
T_{c}(x)
, as observed by Goldfeld, starts from

T_{c}(x)\geq\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q\leq x\\
q|p-1\end{subarray}}1\geq\frac{1}{\log x}\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q\leq x\\
q|p-1\end{subarray}}\log q.

For an upper bound of
T_{c}(x)
, we begin with the dual observation inspired by Goldfeld with the help of Lemma
2.3

\displaystyle T_{c}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q\leq x\\
q|p-1\end{subarray}}1+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log x}{c}\sum_{p\leq x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q\leq x\\
q|p-1\end{subarray}}\log q+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

Changing the order of summations above, we get

\displaystyle T_{c}(x)\leq\frac{\log x}{c}\sum_{x^{c}\leq q\leq x}\pi(x;q,1)\log q+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

(2.1)

Following Goldfeld, the above estimate can be handled firstly by manipulating the weighted sum
L(x;x^{c},x)
. For
33/64<c<1
, from Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
we clearly have

\displaystyle L(x;x^{c},x)

\displaystyle=L(x;1,x)-L(x;1,x^{1/2})-L(x;x^{1/2},x^{c})+O(\log x)

\displaystyle=x/2-L(x;x^{1/2},x^{c})+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right)

\displaystyle\leq x/2-L(x;x^{1/2},x^{33/64})+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right).

(2.2)

We now employ Lemma
2.4
to give a nontrivial lower bound of
L(x;x^{1/2},x^{33/64})
. For integers
1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor
, let
Q_{j}=2^{j}x^{1/2}
. Then we have
Q_{j}>x^{1/2}
and
2Q_{j}<x^{33/64}
for sufficiently large
x
. For any
1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor
, from Lemma
2.4
(with
A=2
) we have

\displaystyle\pi(x;m,1)>\frac{K_{1}(33/64)}{2}\frac{x}{\varphi(m)\log x}

(2.3)

for all integers
m\in(Q_{j},2Q_{j}]
with at most
O\left(Q_{j}/(\log x)^{2}\right)
exceptions. For
1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor
, let
S_{j}
be the set of exceptions of
m
in the interval
(Q_{j},2Q_{j}]
. Thus, in view of equation (
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle L(x;x^{1/2},x^{33/64})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x^{1/2}<m\leq x^{33/64}}\Lambda(m)\pi(x;m,1)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<m\leq 2Q_{j}\\
m\not\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\Lambda(m)\pi(x;m,1)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{K_{1}(33/64)}{2}\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{j=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<m\leq 2Q_{j}\\
m\not\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}.

(2.4)

For
1\leq j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\log x}{65\log 2}\right\rfloor
, we clearly have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<m\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<p\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log p}{p}\geq\log Q_{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<p\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}.

Chebyshev’s estimate or the prime number theorem gives us

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<p\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2Q_{j}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<p\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}1\geq\frac{1}{2Q_{j}}\frac{Q_{j}}{2\log Q_{j}}=\frac{1}{4\log Q_{j}},

from which we deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<m\leq 2Q_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}\geq\frac{1}{4}.

Moreover, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}\ll\log(2Q_{j})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\log\log m}{m}\ll\log x\log\log x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

since
\varphi(m)\gg\frac{m}{\log\log m}
(see for example
[
11
, Theorem 2.9]
), where the implied constants are all absolute. Noticing that
S_{j}\subset(Q_{j},2Q_{j}]
and

|S_{j}|\ll Q_{j}/(\log x)^{2}
by its definition, we get

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\ll\frac{1}{Q_{j}}\frac{Q_{j}}{(\log x)^{2}}=1/(\log x)^{2}.

It follows that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}\ll\frac{\log\log x}{\log x},

where the implied constant is absolute. Thus, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q_{j}<m\leq 2Q_{j}\\
m\not\in S_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{\Lambda(m)}{\varphi(m)}\geq 1/5

(2.5)

provided that
x
is sufficiently large.
Combining equation (
2
), inequalities (
2
) and (
2.5
) we conclude that there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle L(x;x^{c},x)\leq(1/2-\delta)x+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right).

(2.6)

The transition from
L(x;x^{c},x)
to
\sum_{x^{c}\leq q\leq x}\pi(x;q,1)\log q
is somewhat standard. It is clear that

\displaystyle L(x;x^{c},x)-\sum_{x^{c}\leq q\leq x}\pi(x;q,1)\log q=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q^{k}\leq x\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\pi(x;q^{k},1)\log q+O(\log x).

(2.7)

For simplicity, we now require that
3/4<c<1
. Trivial estimates lead to

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{c}\leq q^{k}\leq x\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\pi(x;q^{k},1)\log q

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{3/4}\leq q^{k}\leq x\\
q\leq x^{1/2}\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\pi(x;q^{k},1)\log q\ll x^{1/4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{3/4}\leq q^{k}\leq x\\
q\leq x^{1/2}\\
k>1\end{subarray}}\log q

\displaystyle\ll x^{1/4}\sum_{q\leq x^{1/2}}\log q\log x\ll x^{3/4}(\log x)^{2}.

(2.8)

From equations (
2.6
), (
2.7
) and (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{c}\leq q\leq x}\pi(x;q,1)\log q\leq(1/2-\delta)x+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{\log x}\right)

(2.9)

provided that
3/4<c<1
. Hence, equation (
2.9
) together with equation (
2.1
) yield

T_{c}(x)\leq\frac{1}{c}(1/2-\delta)x/\log x+O\left(\frac{x\log\log x}{(\log x)^{2}}\right).

Now the theorem follows from taking
c=\max\{1-\delta,4/5\}
.
∎

3.
Final remarks

As early as 1980, Pomerance
[
12
]
conjectured that for any
c\in(0,1)
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
P^{+}(p-1)\leq x^{c}\end{subarray}}1\sim\rho(1/c)\pi(x),\quad\text{as~}x\rightarrow\infty.

This is surely a rather difficult conjecture which is beyond the power of present mathematics. Granville
[
7
]
claimed that it follows from the Eillott–Halberstam conjecture. Recently, Wang
[
13
]
offered an alternative proof of this claim. Therefore, assuming the Eillott–Halberstam conjecture, one can prove

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}T_{c}(x)/\pi(x)\rightarrow 0,\quad\text{as~}c\rightarrow 1

by Lemma
2.3
. This immediately leads to a negative answer of Conjecture
1.1
for
k=1
under the Eillott–Halberstam conjecture. So this note can be viewed as an unconditional proof of this matter. The exceptional result for level greater than
1/2
(see Lemma
2.4
) is crucial for this goal.

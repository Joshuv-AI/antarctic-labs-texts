Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a generalization of the theory of Higgs bundles over a smooth complex projective curve in which the twisting of the Higgs field by the canonical bundle of the curve is replaced by a rank 2 2 vector bundle. We define a Hitchin map and give a spectral correspondence. We also state a Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence for a generalization of the Hitchin’s equations to this situation. In a certain sense, this theory lies halfway between the theories of Higgs bundles on a curve and on a higher dimensional variety.

1.
Introduction

Higgs bundles over a smooth complex projective curve
X
were introduced by Hitchin in
[
15
]
in his study of the
self-duality equations, referred now as Hitchin’s equations. A Higgs bundle over
X
is a pair

(E,\varphi)
consisting of a vector bundle
E
over
X
and a twisted endomorphism
\varphi:E\to E\otimes K_{X}
,
where
K_{X}
is the canonical bundle of
X
. The moduli space of these objects has an extremely rich geometric structure.
In particular, the Hitchin map, given by the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of
\varphi
, defines a fibration
which is an algebraic completely integrable system — the Hitchin system. In
[
16
]
, Hitchin gives a description
of the generic fibres in terms of spectral curves. These are curves defined in the total space of the line bundle
K_{X}
. This
construction has been generalized in
[
3
]
for an arbitrary twisting line bundle
L

with
\deg L\geq\deg K_{X}
. The Hitchin fibration plays a central role in the study of mirror symmetry and Langlands duality

[
19
,
9
]
, and has been a crucial ingredient in the proof of the Fundamental Lemma by Ngô
[
21
]
.
Higgs bundles on a higher dimensional variety were introduced by Simpson
[
24
]
. In this theory the role of the twisting bundle

K_{X}
is taken by the cotangent bundle of the variety, and there is the additional condition that

\varphi\wedge\varphi=0
, which is trivally satisfied in dimension
1
. Simpson
[
27
]
considered also the generalization of the Hitchin map. Very recently, Chen and Ngô

[
5
]
have studied this and obtained a number of results in particular in the case of surfaces.

In this paper, we consider a problem that somehow is halfway between the case of curves and the higher dimensional case. While we still work on a complex projective curve
X
, the twisting line bundle
K_{X}
is replaced by a higher rank vector bundle
V
. As in higher dimensions, in this situation one naturally considers the integrability condition
\varphi\wedge\varphi=0
. For concreteness and to simplify computations, we limit ourselves here to the case in which both the rank of
E
and the rank of
V
are two. This is the simplest non-trivial situation where this problem can be considered.

After introducing the objects and defining stability, we give an algebraic construction of our moduli space modeled on a construction of the moduli space of Higgs bundles on a higher dimensional variety given by Simpson
[
27
]
. We also study deformations generalizing the standard approach followed in
[
4
]
. We then define the Hitchin map in this situation. For simplicity we restrict ourselves to the case in which the determinant of
E
is trivial and the trace of
\varphi
vanishes. In Theorem
3.8
we give a spectral correspondence describing the fibres of the Hitchin map. To do this, we follow a procedure inspired by
Chen and Ngô’s interpretation of the Hitchin morphism in terms of universal
spectral data
[
5
]
. This spectral correspondence allows us to prove that the Hitchin map is proper. We go on to study for what elements of the Hitchin base the associated spectral curve has nice properties,
like being integral or smooth. The main results here are given by Propositions
3.10
and
3.14
and by Corollary
3.12
. The question of what conditions can be imposed on the twisting bundle
V
in order to have generic smooth spectral curves remains open. To complete the circle of ideas relating to the original Higgs bundles introduced by Hitchin, we consider natural gauge-theoretic equations in our situation, generalizing the Hitchin equations and state a Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence. The existence of solutions follows from the general theorem for twisted quiver bundles proved in
[
2
]
.

The kind of pairs that we study in this paper were already mentioned by Simpson
[
26
]
*p.86 and have appeared in the literature sporadically. See, for example the notes
[
20
]
*§2.2 and the papers of Donagi with Markman
[
11
]
*§9.3.4 and Gaitsgory
[
10
]
*§17.10. Very recently, in the work of Alfaya and Oliveira
[
1
]
, these are studied in the context of a more general correspondence between
\Lambda
-modules and integrable Lie algebroid connections given by Tortella
[
28
,
29
]
.

When the twisting vector bundle is a direct sum of two line bundles
V=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
with
L_{1}\otimes L_{2}=K_{X}
, these objects have been considered by Xie and Yonekura in
[
30
]
, where they propose the generalized Hitchin’s equations as a BPS equation for a certain class of
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric gauge
theories in four dimensions

2.
V
-twisted Higgs bundles

2.1.
The main definitions

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over
\mathbb{C}
and
V\rightarrow X
a rank
2
vector bundle.

Definition 2.1
.

A
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
on
X
is a pair
(E,\varphi)
, for
E\rightarrow X
a rank
2
vector bundle and
\varphi:E\rightarrow E\otimes V
a twisted endomorphism satisfying the
commuting condition

\varphi\wedge\varphi=0

as a homomorphism
E\rightarrow E\otimes\wedge^{2}V
.
In the course of the text sometimes we will refer to
V
-twisted Higgs bundles simply as
pairs
.

A
homomorphism
of
V
-twisted Higgs bundles
(E,\varphi)\rightarrow(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
is a vector bundle homomorphism
f:E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
such that the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E^{\prime}\otimes V.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes\id}

Remark 2.2
.

It is clear that with the obvious definitions all the constructions in this section can be generalized to the case where
E
and
V
are vector bundles of arbitrary rank.

Remark 2.3
.

The commuting condition can be better understood in a local frame
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
of
V
, so that
\varphi
can be written as

\varphi=\varphi_{1}\otimes v_{1}+\varphi_{2}\otimes v_{2},

where the
\varphi_{i}
are local sections of
\End E
. Now,

\varphi\wedge\varphi=\varphi_{1}\circ\varphi_{2}\otimes v_{1}\wedge v_{2}+\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}\otimes v_{2}\wedge v_{1}=[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]\otimes v_{1}\wedge v_{2}.

Thus, locally we can regard
\varphi
as a pair of commuting endomorphisms.

This commuting condition is the main aspect where the theory of
V
-twisted Higgs bundles over a curve resembles the theory of usual Higgs bundles in higher dimensions. In that situation, the commuting condition arises naturally as part of the
integrability condition
for the operator
\bar{\partial}_{E}+\varphi
(see section 1 in Simpson’s paper
[
25
]
for more details). The commuting condition is also necessary for the existence of a spectral correspondence, since commuting matrices can be simultaneously diagonalized. In this paper we impose the commuting condition in analogy with the higher dimensional Higgs bundle situation, and for the existence of the spectral correspondence, but by doing this we do not mean to imply that pairs
(E,\varphi)
that do not satisfy the commuting condition might not be worthy of consideration.

Definition 2.4
.

We say that a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
is
stable
(resp.
semistable
) if for every line subbundle
L\subset E
such that
\varphi(L)\subset L\otimes V
, we have

\deg(L)<\tfrac{1}{2}\deg(E)\ \ \text{ (resp. }\deg(L)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\deg(E)).

Remark 2.5
.

Note that if a pair
(E,\varphi)
is (semi)stable and
L
is a line bundle, then the associated pair
(E\otimes L,\varphi)
(where we identify
\varphi
with
\varphi\otimes\id_{L}
) is also (semi)stable.

The following result summarizes some important properties about stable pairs and it can be seen as an analogue of the fact that stable vector bundles are simple. The proof is essentially the same as the one for vector bundles
[
23
]
*p.17, Prop. 6.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(E_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(E_{2},\varphi_{2})
be semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles on
X
. Then

(a)

If
\deg(E_{1})>\deg(E_{2})
, then
\Hom((E_{1},\varphi_{1}),(E_{2},\varphi_{2}))=0
.

(b)

If
E_{1}
is stable and
\deg(E_{1})=\deg(E_{2})=d
, then
\Hom((E_{1},\varphi_{1}),(E_{2},\varphi_{2}))=0
or
(E_{1},\varphi_{1})\cong(E_{2},\varphi_{2})
.

(c)

If
(E,\varphi)
is stable, then
\End((E,\varphi))=\mathbb{C}\cdot\id_{E}
.

2.2.
The moduli space

We will construct a
moduli space

\mathcal{M}(d)
that parametrizes equivalence classes of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
on
X
based on Simpson’s “second construction” of the moduli space of (usual) semistable Higgs bundles over projective varieties
[
27
]
. In particular, there will be an open subvariety
\mathcal{M}^{s}(d)\subset\mathcal{M}(d)
parametrizing isomorphism classes of stable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
.

Remark 2.7
.

Of course, like in the case of usual Higgs bundles
[
27
]
*Lemma 6.5, a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle is the same that a
\Lambda
-module, for
\Lambda=\mathrm{Sym}^{\bullet}(V^{*})
, and thus one can simply consider the moduli space of
\Lambda
-modules, as constructed in
[
26
]
*Thm. 4.7. This is Simpson’s “first construction” and the approach followed in
[
1
]
. In particular, the results of
[
29
]
give a correspondence between
V
-twisted Higgs bundles and a certain type of “twisted connections”. However, like in
[
27
]
, for the purpose of this paper (more precisely, for the proof of Proposition
3.9
), we prefer to use the “second construction”.

2.2.2.
V
-twisted Higgs bundles as sheaves on
V

The key idea for the construction of the moduli space is to identify
V
-twisted Higgs bundles with sheaves on
V
.

Let
Z
denote a projective completion of
V
and
D=Z\setminus V
the divisor at infinity. We can choose
Z
so that the bundle projection
q:V\rightarrow X
extends to a map
q:Z\rightarrow X
.

Proposition 2.8
.

There exists some
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the functor
q_{*}
gives an equivalence of categories between families
(E,\varphi)
of
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
parametrized by
S
and coherent sheaves
{E}
on
Z_{S}
of pure dimension

with
\mathrm{supp}({E})\cap D_{S}=\varnothing
and Hilbert polynomial

.
In particular,
(E,\varphi)
is semistable if and only if
{E}
is.

Proof.

This is essentially the same as Lemma 6.8 and Corollary 6.9 in
[
27
]
.
∎

2.2.3.
Construction of the moduli space

Let
Z
be the projective completion of
V
and
k
as in Proposition
2.8
, and consider the polynomial
P(n)=2kn+d
.

Simpson
[
26
]
*Thm. 1.21 showed that the functor
\mathbf{Sh}_{Z,P}:\mathbf{Sch}/\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbf{Set}
sending a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
S
to the set of isomorphism classes of families of semistable sheaves over
Z
with Hilbert polynomial
P
parametrized by
S
is universally corepresented by a moduli space
M(Z,P)
. Simpson constructs this moduli space by identifying the set of classes of semistable sheaves of pure dimension with a subset
Q_{1}
of a certain Quot scheme and considering a subset
Q_{2}\subset Q_{1}
. The moduli space
M(Z,P)
is obtained as a good quotient of
Q_{2}
by the action of a change of basis.

Let
Q_{3}\subset Q_{2}
the open subset parametrizing quotient sheaves
{E}
whose support does not meet
D
. This set
Q_{3}
is invariant by change of basis and the inverse image of a subset of
M(Z,P)
, so a good quotient of
Q_{3}
exists and it is equal to an open subset
M(V,P)\subset M(Z,P)
. Finally, from the fact that
M(Z,P)
universally corepresents
\mathbf{Sh}_{Z,P}
and from Proposition
2.8
, we conclude that
M(V,P)
universally corepresents the functor
\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}
, so we can define the
moduli space of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
on
X
as

\mathcal{M}(d)=M(V,P).

2.2.4.
S
-equivalence and polystability

We have constructed a moduli space
\mathcal{M}(d)
universally corepresenting
\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}
. However, we still have not said what do the closed points of
\mathcal{M}(d)
parametrize. We answer now this question.

Suppose that
(E,\varphi)
is a semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundle that is not stable. Then there exists a line subbundle
L\subset E
with
\varphi(L)\subset L\otimes V
such that
\deg L=\tfrac{1}{2}\deg E
. Now, consider the line bundle
E/L
and the induced map
\bar{\varphi}:E/L\rightarrow E/L\otimes V
. We define the
graded
V
-twisted Higgs bundle associated to
(E,\varphi)
as the direct sum

\gr(E,\varphi)=(L,\varphi|_{L})\oplus(E/L,\bar{\varphi}).

This object is clearly well defined by
(E,\varphi)
up to isomorphism.

When
(E,\varphi)
is stable, we define
\gr(E,\varphi)=(E,\varphi)
.
We say that two semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles are
S
-equivalent
if their associated graded objects are isomorphic. We denote by
S(d)
the set of
S
-equivalence classes of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
on
X
. Now, it is clear that the subset
S^{s}(d)
formed by stable objects is the set of isomorphism classes of stable pairs of degree
d
.

We say that a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
is
polystable
if it is stable or if it can be expressed as a direct sum
(E,\varphi)=(L_{1},\varphi_{1})\oplus(L_{2},\varphi_{2})
, for
L_{i}
line bundles with
\deg L_{i}=\tfrac{1}{2}\deg E
and
\varphi_{i}:L_{i}\rightarrow L_{i}\otimes V
with
\varphi_{i}\wedge\varphi_{i}=0
. Clearly, a polystable pair is semistable, so the map
[E,\varphi]\mapsto[\gr(E,\varphi)]
defines a bijection between
S(d)
and the set of isomorphism classes of polystable pairs of degree
d
.

The natural transformation
\tau:\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}(d)}
corepresenting
\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}
induces a map
\tau_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}(d)}(\mathbb{C})
, where
\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is the set of isomorphism classes of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
and
\underline{\mathcal{M}(d)}(\mathbb{C})
is the set of closed points of
\mathcal{M}(d)
.

Proposition 2.9
.

The map
\tau_{\mathbb{C}}
factors through the quotient map
\mathbf{Higgs}_{d}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow S(d)
induced by the
S
-equivalence relation. Moreover, the induced map
S(d)\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{M}(d)}(\mathbb{C})
is a bijection.

Proof.

The proof is standard and identic to others in the literature. See, for example,
[
26
]
*Thm. 1.21(3) and
[
22
]
*Prop. 4.5.
∎

As a consequence of this result, we have that the set
S^{s}(d)
of isomorphism classes of stable pairs is in bijection with the set of closed points of the open subvariety
\mathcal{M}^{s}(d)
parametrizing stable bundles. This shows that
\mathcal{M}^{s}(d)
is a

for stable

-twisted Higgs bundles of degree

.

2.3.
Infinitesimal deformations

To finish the section, we give a description of the space of infinitesimal deformations of a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle. Our discussion is a generalization of the results of Biswas and Ramanan

Consider the ring

\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon]=\mathbb{C}[X]/(X^{2})=\left\{a+b\varepsilon:a,b\in\mathbb{C}\right\},

which is an Artinian
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with maximal ideal
(\varepsilon)
. Given a
\mathbb{C}
-scheme

, we denote
S[\varepsilon]=S\times_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon])
and identify

with
S_{(\varepsilon)}
.
Recall that the Zariski tangent space of a variety

at a point

can be identified with the set of maps
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon])\rightarrow M
mapping

to the point

-twisted Higgs bundles at a point

, it makes sense to consider the space
T_{(E,\varphi)}
of isomorphism classes of families
(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})
of

-twisted Higgs bundles parametrized by
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon])
such that
(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})|_{X}\cong(E,\varphi)
. We call this
T_{(E,\varphi)}
the
space of infinitesimal deformations
of
(E,\varphi)
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
(E,\varphi)
be a

and consider the chain complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}_{(E,\varphi)}:\End E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi\wedge-}

.

Notice the similarity between the complex

Remark 2.12
.

The above proposition is a particular case of Theorem 47 in the thesis of Tortella

Proof.

We start by computing

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{0,0}=\sum_{i}\tilde{M}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{0,1}=\sum_{i}\tilde{M}_{i}\otimes H^{0}(U_{i},V)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{1,1}=\sum_{ij}\tilde{M}_{ij}\otimes H^{0}(U_{ij},V)}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi\wedge-}

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{tot}}^{1}

\displaystyle=K^{1,0}\oplus K^{0,1}=\sum_{ij}\tilde{M}_{ij}\oplus\sum_{i}\tilde{M}_{i}\otimes H^{0}(U_{i},V),

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{tot}}^{2}

\displaystyle=K^{2,0}\oplus K^{1,1}\oplus K^{0,2}

\displaystyle K_{\mathrm{tot}}^{1}

For every
i
we can consider the bundle
\tilde{E}_{i}=E|_{U_{i}}[\varepsilon]
, over
U_{i}[\varepsilon]
. Now, consider an element
(s_{ij},t_{i})\in Z
. Then, the restriction
\tilde{E}_{i}|_{U_{j}}
can be identified with
\tilde{E}_{j}|_{U_{i}}
through the isomorphism
\id+s_{ij}\varepsilon
. The first equation above ensures that under these identifications the patches
\tilde{E}_{i}
can be glued to give a vector bundle
\tilde{E}
on
X[\varepsilon]
.

Over
U_{i}[\varepsilon]
we have the section
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon
of
\End(\tilde{E}_{i})\otimes V[\varepsilon]
. Since the transition functions of
\tilde{E}
are given by
\id+s_{ij}\varepsilon
, the associated transition functions on
\End(\tilde{E})
are
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\mapsto\tilde{\varphi}_{i}+s_{ij}\varepsilon\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}
. Therefore, the sections
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}
will glue to give a global section
\tilde{\varphi}
of
\End(\tilde{E})\otimes V[\varepsilon]
if

\tilde{\varphi}_{j}=\tilde{\varphi}_{i}+s_{ij}\varepsilon\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}

but this is true since

\tilde{\varphi}_{i}+s_{ij}\varepsilon\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon+s_{ij}\wedge\varphi\varepsilon=\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon-(t_{i}-t_{j})\varepsilon=\varphi+t_{j}\varepsilon=\tilde{\varphi}_{j}.

Finally, since
\varphi\wedge t_{i}=0
, we have

\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=(\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon)\wedge(\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon)=\varphi\wedge\varphi+(\varphi\wedge t_{i}+t_{i}\wedge\varphi)\varepsilon=0.

Therefore, the section
\tilde{\varphi}\in H^{0}(X,\End(\tilde{E})\otimes V[\varepsilon])
satisfies the commuting condition
\tilde{\varphi}\wedge\tilde{\varphi}=0
.

To sum up, we have defined a map

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T_{(E,\varphi)}

\displaystyle(s_{ij},t_{i})

\displaystyle\longmapsto[(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})].

In order to see that this map descends to a map
Z/B\rightarrow T_{(E,\varphi)}
we have to check that an element of the form
(s_{ij},t_{i})=(s_{i}-s_{j},\varphi\wedge s_{i})
, for
(s_{i})\in\sum_{i}\tilde{M}_{i}
, induces a pair
(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})
isomorphic to the “trivial” pair
(E[\varepsilon],\varphi)
. Indeed, the identification
\tilde{E}_{i}\rightarrow\tilde{E}_{j}
on
U_{ij}
is given by the automorphism
1+(s_{i}-s_{j})\varepsilon
. Therefore, the following diagram commutes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}_{ij}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}_{ij}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}_{ij}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{E}_{ij}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+s_{i}\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+s_{ij}\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1+s_{j}\varepsilon}

This implies that the bundle
\tilde{E}
is isomorphic to the trivial bundle
E[\varepsilon]
. Moreover, the isomorphism
1+s_{i}\varepsilon
sends
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}
to

\tilde{\varphi}_{i}+s_{i}\varepsilon\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon+s_{i}\wedge\varphi\varepsilon=\varphi.

We thus have defined a map
\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow T_{(E,\varphi)}
. We define now an inverse map
T_{(E,\varphi)}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})
. Let us take then a family
(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})
of
V
-twisted Higgs bundles parametrized by
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[\varepsilon])
, such that
(\tilde{E},\tilde{\varphi})|_{X}\cong(E,\varphi)
.
Since the
U_{i}
are affine, the restriction
\tilde{E}|_{U_{i}[\varepsilon]}
must be of the form
E_{i}[\varepsilon]
, with
E_{i}
a vector bundle over
U_{i}
. Clearly,
E_{i}\cong E|_{U_{i}}
.
Therefore,
\tilde{E}
is obtained by gluing the restrictions of
E[\varepsilon]
to
U_{i}[\varepsilon]
via automorphisms over
U_{ij}[\varepsilon]
. These have to be of the form
1+s_{ij}\varepsilon
, with the
s_{ij}
satisfying the cocycle condition:
s_{ij}+s_{jk}=s_{ik}
over the triple intersection
U_{ijk}
.

Moreover, the homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}
over each
U_{i}[\varepsilon]
must be of the form
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\varphi+t_{i}\varepsilon
for sections
t_{i}\in H^{0}(U_{i},\End E\otimes V)
. The global nature of
\tilde{\varphi}
imposes the following compatibility condition

\tilde{\varphi}_{i}+s_{ij}\varepsilon\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=\tilde{\varphi}_{j}.

This implies that
\varphi\wedge s_{ij}=t_{i}-t_{j}
.

Finally, from the fact that
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}\wedge\tilde{\varphi}_{i}=0
we deduce that
\varphi\wedge t_{i}=0
. Putting all this together, we see that
(s_{ij},t_{i})
is an element of
Z
. Hence we have given a map
T_{(E,\varphi)}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})
. It is easy to check that this and the map
\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow T_{(E,\varphi)}
constructed above are inverse of each other.
∎

We can now study the complex
{C}_{(E,\varphi)}
in terms of a simpler complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D}_{(E,\varphi)}:\End E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes V.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi\wedge-}

This allows us to obtain the following.

Proposition 2.13
.

If
(E,\varphi)
is a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle on
X
, we have

\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})+\dim\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})=\dim\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)}).

Proof.

There is an obvious short exact sequence of complexes
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V[2]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}_{(E,\varphi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D}_{(E,\varphi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Here, we use the standard notation in derived categories where
[n]
after a chain complex stands for shifting the complex
n
places.
This induces a long exact sequence in cohomology

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow 0.

Computing the Euler characteristic we get

\displaystyle\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})+\dim\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle=\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})-\chi(\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V).

On the other hand, we have the short exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes V[1]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D}_{(E,\varphi)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}

giving the long exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\End E)\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\End E\otimes V)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{1}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\End E)\rightarrow H^{1}(X,\End E\otimes V)\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow 0.

Again, computing the Euler characteristic we get

\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})=\chi(\End E\otimes V)-\chi(\End E)+\dim\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)}).

Putting this together with the above we have

\displaystyle\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})+\dim\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})

\displaystyle=\chi(\End E\otimes V)-\chi(\End E)-\chi(\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V)+\dim\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)}).

Now, by Riemann–Roch it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\chi(\End E\otimes V)-\chi(\End E)-\chi(\End E\otimes\wedge^{2}V)=0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Notice that the vanishing of the relation between the Euler characteristics is a very special situation that only occurs when
V
has rank
2
.

More can be said about the chain complex
{C}_{(E,\varphi)}
when
(E,\varphi)
is assumed to be stable.

Proposition 2.15
.

If
(E,\varphi)
is a stable
V
-twisted Higgs bundle on
X
, then

\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})\cong\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

It is clear from the relations defining
\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})
in the Čech resolution of
{D}_{(E,\varphi)}
that

\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})=\left\{\xi\in H^{0}(X,\End E):\varphi\wedge\xi=0\right\}=\End(E,\varphi).

Since
(E,\varphi)
is stable, by Proposition
2.6
, elements
\xi\in\mathbb{H}^{0}({D}_{(E,\varphi)})
are of the form
\xi=\lambda\id_{E}
.
∎

This proposition implies that, when
(E,\varphi)
is stable, we have the relation

\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})-\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})+\dim\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})=1.

Finally, assuming some conditions on
V
we obtain:

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
K_{X}
be the canonical line bundle of
X
and denote
L=\wedge^{2}V
. Assume that
\deg L\geq\deg K_{X}
. Then

\dim\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})=\begin{cases}\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}),&\text{if }K_{X}\cong L,\\
\dim\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})+1,&\text{else}.\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, if
V
is isomorphic to
V^{\vee}\otimes K_{X}
, then there exists a perfect pairing

\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\times\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

Consider the dual complex of
{C}_{(E,\varphi)}
,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}:(\End E)^{\vee}\otimes L^{-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\End E)^{\vee}\otimes V^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\End E)^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\varphi\wedge-)^{\vee}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\varphi\wedge-)^{\vee}}

By the pairing induced by the Killing form of
\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, we can identify this complex with the complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}:\End E\otimes L^{-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes V^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-(\varphi\wedge-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-(\varphi\wedge-)}

By Grothendieck–Serre duality for hypercohomology (see
[
7
]
*§3.3 for a comprehensive overview in a similar context), we have

(\mathbb{H}^{3}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}))^{*}\cong\mathbb{H}^{0}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X}),

where
{C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X}
is the chain complex
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X}:\End E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes V^{\vee}\otimes K_{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\End E\otimes K_{X}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-(\varphi\wedge-)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-(\varphi\wedge-)}

Now,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}^{0}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X})

\displaystyle=\left\{\xi\in H^{0}(X,\End E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X}):\varphi\wedge\xi=0\right\}

\displaystyle=\Hom((E,\varphi),(E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X},\varphi)).

From Remark
2.5
we have that, since
(E,\varphi)
is stable, so will be
(E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X},\varphi)
. Now, we assumed that
\deg(K_{X})=2g-2\leq\deg(L)
, so
\deg(E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X})\leq\deg(E)
.

If
L
is not isomorphic to
K_{X}
, Proposition
2.6
implies that

\mathbb{H}^{0}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X})=\Hom((E,\varphi),(E\otimes L^{-1}K_{X},\varphi))=0.

On the other hand, if
L\cong K_{X}
that same proposition asserts that

\dim\mathbb{H}^{0}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X})=1.

Finally, Grothendieck–Serre duality gives a perfect pairing

\mathbb{H}^{1}({C}_{(E,\varphi)})\times\mathbb{H}^{2}({C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}.

Now, notice that if
V\cong V^{\vee}\otimes K_{X}
, we have that
{C}_{(E,\varphi)}^{*}\otimes K_{X}={C}_{(E,\varphi)}
, and thus we obtain the desired pairing.
∎

3.
The Hitchin morphism for
V
-twisted
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles

3.1.
The Hitchin map

In this section we introduce the Hitchin morphism and study its fibres by giving a
spectral correspondence
. For simplicity, we restrict to the case where the “structure group” of the pair is
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
V
-twisted
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle
is a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
such that
\det E={O}_{X}
and
\tr\varphi=0
.

The
moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0}
of semistable
V
-twisted
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles
can be obtained from the moduli space of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
0
as the fibre of the element
({O}_{X},0)
by the map

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(0)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Jac}(X)\times H^{0}(X,V)

\displaystyle(E,\varphi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\det E,\tr(\varphi)).

Analogously, we can obtain the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{0}^{s}
of stable
V
-twisted
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles.

The
Hitchin morphism
is defined as the map

\displaystyle h:\mathcal{M}_{0}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)

\displaystyle(E,\varphi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\tr(\varphi^{2}).

This map can be interpreted as some sort of “characteristic polynomial” since, at least formally

\det(\varphi-\id_{E}\otimes T)=T^{2}-\tr(\varphi^{2}).

This is why the elements in the image of
h
are referred as
spectral data
. We are interested in studying the fibres of this morphism. Note that a good description of these fibres will give a way of constructing semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles.

One of the main differences with the well-known case where the twisting bundle is a line bundle is that in our situation the Hitchin morphism is not surjective. In order to see this, suppose that
V
splits as a direct sum
V=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
(which can always be assumed locally). In that case, we can write
\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
, with
\varphi_{i}:E\rightarrow E\otimes L_{i}
. The commuting condition means now that
[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]=0
. The symmetric product
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V
splits as

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V=L_{1}^{2}\oplus L^{2}_{2}\oplus L_{1}L_{2}

and
h(\varphi)
can be written as

h(\varphi)=\tr(\varphi^{2})=(\tr(\varphi_{1}^{2}),\tr(\varphi_{2}^{2}),\tr(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})).

This implies that an element
b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)
in the image of
h
must satisfy the relation

b_{3}^{2}=b_{1}b_{2}.

We define the
Hitchin base
as the space

\mathcal{B}=\left\{b\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)\text{ such that locally }b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\text{ with }b_{3}^{2}=b_{1}b_{2}\right\}.

As we just explained, the Hitchin morphism
h
factors through the inclusion
\mathcal{B}\hookrightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)
.
In the spirit of
[
5
]
, we conjecture that the image of
h
is precisely the Hitchin base
\mathcal{B}
.

3.2.
Universal spectral data

We follow a procedure inspired by Chen and Ngô’s
[
5
]
interpretation of the Hitchin morphism in terms of
universal spectral data
.

The intuition behind this construction is to consider the “models over a point” of each of the elements involved in the Hitchin morphism. For example, over a point a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle is simply a pair
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
of elements of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
with
[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]=0
. We define the
commuting variety
as

\mathfrak{C}=\left\{(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C}):[\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}]=0\right\}.

The point model of the Hitchin morphism is the map

\displaystyle h:\mathfrak{C}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{3}\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{2}\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\tr(\varphi_{1}^{2}),\tr(\varphi_{2}^{2}),\tr(\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2})).

The image of this map is the cone

B=\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}:z^{2}=xy\right\}.

Now, since an element of
\mathfrak{C}
consists of a pair of traceless commuting endomorphisms
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, there exists a common eigenvector
v\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
. If
\lambda_{i}
denotes the eigenvalue of
\varphi_{i}
associated to
v
, we can define the map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}).

In order to remove the dependence on the choice of the eigenvector
v
, we can consider the quotient
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
by the action
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\mapsto(-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2})
. Composing this quotient with the previous map, we get the
universal spectral data morphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{sd}:\mathfrak{C}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})

\displaystyle\longmapsto[(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})]

Moreover, note that
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is in fact isomorphic to the cone
B
via the map

\displaystyle\iota:\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle[(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})]

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\lambda_{1}^{2},\lambda_{2}^{2},\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}).

Thus, we get a factorization of
h
as
\iota\circ\mathrm{sd}
. We conclude that in this point model of the Hitchin morphism, spectral data will be given by elements of the cone
B
.

Definition 3.2
.

We define the
universal characteristic polynomial
as the map

\displaystyle\chi:\mathbb{C}^{2}\times B

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{3}

\displaystyle\left((x,y),(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(x^{2}-b_{1},y^{2}-b_{2},xy-b_{3}).

The
universal spectral cover
is defined as the fibre
\chi^{-1}(0)
with the natural projection map
p:\chi^{-1}(0)\rightarrow B
.

Proposition 3.3
.

The universal spectral cover
p:\chi^{-1}(0)\rightarrow B
is generically étale of degree
2
over the “multiplicity-free” locus
B\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, and it is not flat in general.

Proof.

If
b\in B\setminus\{0\}
, the corresponding points
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
lying above
b
are
\pm(\sqrt{b_{1}},\sqrt{b_{2}})
. Therefore, the analytification
p^{\mathrm{an}}
defines a local biholomorphism, which implies that
p
is étale of degree
2
in
b
.

On the other hand, the fibre over
0
is the spectrum of the
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/(X^{2},Y^{2},XY),

which is a
\mathbb{C}
-vector space of rank
3
. Therefore,
p
cannot be flat over
0
.
∎

Now, we can give the following generalization of the theorem of Cayley–Hamilton.

Proposition 3.4
.

(Universal Cayley–Hamilton theorem)
For any
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\in\mathfrak{C}
, the
\mathbb{C}[X,Y]
-module induced by the map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathbb{C}[X,Y]

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\End\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle f(X,Y)

\displaystyle\longmapsto f(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}),

is supported in the fibre
p^{-1}(h(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))
.

Proof.

The suport of the module is
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/\ker\Phi)
. Let
[(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})]
be the spectral data of
(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})
and consider the ideal

I=(X-\lambda_{1},Y-\lambda_{2})\cdot(X+\lambda_{1},Y+\lambda_{2})=(X^{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2},Y^{2}-\lambda_{2}^{2},XY-\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}).

Clearly
I\subset\ker\Phi
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/\ker\Phi)
is naturally a subscheme of
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[X,Y]/I)=p^{-1}(h(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}))
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that if
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\neq 0
, then in fact
I=\ker\Phi
. This is clear since there exists some basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
where the associated matrices to
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are
\diag(\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{1})
and
\diag(\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{2})
. Therefore, if
f(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})=0
, in particular we have
f(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=0
and
f(-\lambda_{1},-\lambda_{2})=0
, and thus
f\in I
.

This is also true when just one of the eigenvalues (for example
\lambda_{2}
) vanishes. Indeed, in that case we must have
\varphi_{2}=0
, but we also have
I=(X^{2}-\lambda_{1}^{2},Y,XY)
(notice that
\lambda_{1}^{2}Y=X(XY)
).

However, when
[(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})]=[(0,0)]
, in general we can at most hope for a basis where

\varphi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\ \text{ and }\ \varphi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

for
a\in\mathbb{C}
. In this case
f(X,Y)=aX-Y\in\ker\Phi
, but
f\not\in I=(X^{2},Y^{2},XY)
.

3.3.
The spectral correspondence

We will obtain a spectral curve by twisting all the universal objects constructed in the previous section by the
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-torsor attached to the vector bundle
V\rightarrow X
. More precisely we take the frame bundle
P
of
V
, which is a
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-principal bundle and consider the functor sending any
\mathbb{C}
-scheme
F
with a left action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
to the associated fibre bundle
V(F)=P\times_{\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})}F
and equivariant morphisms to the associated maps between the associated bundles.

The natural action of
\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
induces an action on the cone
\mathbb{C}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and thus on
B
. The sections of the associated fibre bundle
V(B)
form precisely the Hitchin base

\mathcal{B}=\left\{b\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)\text{ such that locally }b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\text{ with }b_{3}^{2}=b_{1}b_{2}\right\}.

Moreover, the universal spectral cover is clearly equivariant by this action, so it induces a fibre bundle morphism
V(p):V(\chi^{-1}(0))\rightarrow V(B)
.

Definition 3.6
.

Given
b\in\mathcal{B}
, we define the
spectral cover associated to
b
as the morphism
\pi:Y_{b}\rightarrow X
given by the Cartesian diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y_{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(\chi^{-1}(0))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(B).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V(p)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}

The
1
-dimensional scheme

is called the

.

It is clear from this construction that there is a closed embedding
Y_{b}\hookrightarrow V(\mathbb{C}^{2})=V
. In fact,
\pi
is obtained as

, where

is the bundle projection. The finiteness of

implies that the spectral cover

is a finite morphism. Moreover, we can consider the subset

consisting of sections mapping the generic point of

to the multiplicity free locus

. If

, then

is generically étale of degree

.

In the case of twisting by a line bundle, studied in
[

, the spectral cover is flat as a consequence of the flatness of the universal spectral cover arising in that situation. However, since
p:\chi^{-1}(0)\rightarrow B
is not flat in general, the spectral cover

will not be necessarily flat. If we assume that
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, then the non-flatness phenomena will show up just in the finite subset of points of

where

vanishes.

More precisely, we consider the sheaf
\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
, which is a coherent sheaf on
X
. Since
X
is a smooth curve, the sheaf
\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
splits as a direct sum
F\oplus T
, where
T
is a torsion sheaf and
F
is a locally free sheaf of rank
2
over
X
. The spectral cover
\pi
is flat if and only if
\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
is locally free or, equivalently, if the torsion sheaf
T
vanishes. If we assume that
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, this torsion sheaf

must be supported at the points of
b^{-1}(0)\subset X
.
We can define the subscheme
\tilde{Y}_{b}\subset Y_{b}
as the relative spectrum
\tilde{Y}_{b}=\underline{\mathrm{Spec}}(F)
and the morphism
\tilde{\pi}=\pi|_{\tilde{Y}_{b}}
. This morphism

is finite locally free, and thus flat.

As a consequence of
miracle flatness
, non-flatness of the spectral cover
\pi
amounts to the curve
Y_{b}
not being Cohen–Macaulay and thus having some
embedded components
, located precisely at the points of
b^{-1}(0)\subset X
. Geometrically, one can understand the torsion removing process explained above as eliminating the embedded components of
Y_{b}
, in order to obtain another
1
-dimensional scheme

which is Cohen–Macaulay. This is an example of a
Cohen–Macaulay modification
or
Cohen–Macaulayfication
, easier, but in the same spirit that the one explained in
[
5
]
. More details about this process, with explicit computations, are given in Section

.

Remark 3.7
.

It is clear that torsion removing does not affect the irreducible components, so
\tilde{Y}_{b}
will be irreducible if and only if
Y_{b}
is. Moreover, since
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{Y}_{b}\rightarrow X
is flat,
X
is irreducible and the generic fibre is reduced, we conclude that the curve
\tilde{Y}_{b}
is reduced. Therefore, we have shown that if
Y_{b}
is irreducible, then
\tilde{Y}_{b}
is integral.

Theorem 3.8

(The spectral correspondence)
.

For any
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
the functor
\pi_{*}
gives an equivalence of categories between the category of coherent sheaves
{L}
on
Y_{b}
of generic rank
1
with
\pi_{*}{L}
locally free and
\det\pi_{*}{L}={O}_{X}
and the category of
V
-twisted Higgs bundles
(E,\varphi)
with
\tr(\varphi^{2})=b
. In particular, this category is nonempty.

Moreover, if
Y_{b}
is irreducible,
\pi_{*}
gives a bijection between

and the set of equivalence classes of torsion free sheaves
{L}
on
\tilde{Y}_{b}
of generic rank
1
with
\det\pi_{*}{L}={O}_{X}
.

Finally, if
\tilde{Y}_{b}
is a smooth curve of genus

, the fibre

is an abelian variety of dimension
g^{\prime}-g
, where

is the genus of
X
.

Proof.

We saw in Proposition

that a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
on
X
is the same that a coherent sheaf
{E}
on
V
with
E=q_{*}{E}
locally free of rank
2
and
\det E={O}_{X}
.

The idea now is to apply the universal Cayley–Hamilton theorem. What this result tells us in this situation is that a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
is supported as an
{O}_{V}
-module on the spectral curve
Y_{b}
, for
b=\tr(\varphi^{2})
. Therefore, for
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
, there is a bijective correspondence between
V
-twisted Higgs bundles with
\tr(\varphi^{2})=b
and isomorphism classes of quasi-coherent sheaves
{L}
on
Y_{b}
with
\pi_{*}{L}
locally free of rank
2
. Now, since
\pi
is finite and generically étale of degree
2
, asking that
\pi_{*}{L}
has rank
2
is the same as asking that
{L}
has generic rank
1
.

Using the functoriality of taking pushforward and the equivalence of categories given by the closed embedding
\tilde{Y}_{b}\hookrightarrow Y_{b}
we have that, if
(E,\varphi)
is a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle with
\tr(\varphi^{2})=b
, then there is an equivalence of categories between quasi-coherent sheaves
{L}
on
Y_{b}
with
\pi_{*}{L}=E
and quasi-coherent sheaves
{L}
on
\tilde{Y}_{b}
with
\tilde{\pi}_{*}{L}=E
.
It suffices then to show that if a sheaf
{L}
on
Y_{b}
satisfies
\pi_{*}{L}=E
, it is supported on
\tilde{Y}_{b}
. This is true since the morphism

\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}\longrightarrow\End E

giving the structure of
\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
-module on

must factor through something which is torsion free, since

is torsion free. In particular, it must factor through

. Therefore,
{L}
is supported on
Y_{b}
.

Since
\tilde{\pi}:\tilde{Y}_{b}\rightarrow X
is a flat morphism, for any line bundle

on
\tilde{Y}_{b}
,
\tilde{\pi}_{*}L
will be locally free and have the structure of a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle. The determinant of
\tilde{\pi}_{*}L
is determined by the formula
[
14
]
*Ex. IV.2.6(a)

\det\tilde{\pi}_{*}L\cong\det\tilde{\pi}_{*}{O}_{\tilde{Y}_{b}}\otimes\mathrm{Nm}_{\tilde{\pi}}L,

where
\mathrm{Nm}_{\tilde{\pi}}
is the norm map of
\tilde{\pi}
.
Thus it suffices to pick

such that

in order to have

.

Now, if

is a

-twisted Higgs bundle obtained as

and

must divide

, and

is not irreducible. Therefore, if

is irreducible

is stable.

Moreover, it is clear that if

is integral and

is locally free then

must be torsion-free. The same goes for
\tilde{Y}_{b}
smooth and

consists on those line bundles

but, as we saw above, the set of these bundles is the preimage of

under the norm map. This is an abelian variety of dimension

(in fact, it can be identified with the

).
∎

One of the main consequences of the spectral correspondence is that it allows us to prove the following.

3.4.
Properties of the spectral curve

In the previous section we have obtained a general correspondence between Higgs bundles with spectral data
b
and sheaves on the corresponding spectral curve
Y_{b}
, or on its modification
\tilde{Y}_{b}
. However, we still need to know under what conditions we can assume that the spectral curve
Y_{b}
has nice properties, like being integral or smooth.

First of all, it will be useful to write down explicitly the local equations describing
Y_{b}
. Given a point
x\in X
, we can take an open neighbourhood
U
of
x
so that
V|_{U}\cong{O}_{X}^{2}|_{U}
and
b|_{U}
can be seen as a section
b=(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
of
{O}_{X}|_{U}^{3}
, with
b_{3}^{2}=b_{1}b_{2}
. Recall now that the spectral curve is obtained as a relative spectrum
Y_{b}=\underline{\mathrm{Spec}}({F})
, for
{F}=\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
a sheaf such that

(1)

{F}|_{U}=\frac{{O}_{X}|_{U}[X,Y]}{(X^{2}-b_{1},Y^{2}-b_{2},XY-b_{3})}.

3.4.1.
Integrality

From this description it is clear that
Y_{b}
will be reducible if and only if there exists a section
a\in H^{0}(X,V)
such that
b=a^{2}
. Locally, this means that
a=(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(a_{1}^{2},a_{2}^{2},a_{1}a_{2})
, so

(X^{2}-b_{1},Y^{2}-b_{2},XY-b_{3})=(X-a_{1},Y-a_{2})\cdot(X+a_{1},Y+a_{2}).

In that case, the spectral curve
Y_{b}
can be written as
\im a\cup\im(-a)
.

The image of the natural map
H^{0}(X,V)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
taking
a
to
a^{2}
is closed. Indeed, we can consider the variety
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})
as the quotient
\mathcal{B}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
by the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action
b\mapsto\lambda b
. This quotient is a variety since it is the subvariety of
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V))
corresponding to
\mathcal{B}
(which is locally defined by homogeneous polynomials). Now we can consider the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,V))

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})

\displaystyle[a]

\displaystyle\longmapsto[a^{2}],

which is well defined since
(\lambda a)^{2}=\lambda^{2}a^{2}
. This is a morphism between projective varieties and as such it has closed image
Z
. Now, the image of the corresponding map
H^{0}(X,V)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is the union of
\left\{0\right\}
with the preimage of
Z
by the natural projection
\mathcal{B}\setminus\left\{0\right\}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B})
, which is also closed, since this projection is continuous.

We conclude that
Y_{b}
is either reducible for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
, or irreducible for a generic element
b\in\mathcal{B}
. Moreover, recall that if
Y_{b}
is irreducible, then
\tilde{Y}_{b}
is integral. Therefore, we have proved:

Proposition 3.10
.

If
V
is such that the map
H^{0}(X,V)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
given by
a\mapsto a^{2}
is not surjective, then for a generic
b\in\mathcal{B}
, the fibre of the Hitchin morphism
h^{-1}(b)
is in bijection with the set of equivalence classes of torsion free sheaves
{L}
on
Y_{b}
of generic rank
1
with
\det\pi_{*}{L}={O}_{X}
.

3.4.2.
Étale spectral curves

We consider now the case where the spectral curves are best behaved, which is when the section
b
does not vanish.

Proposition 3.11
.

If
x\in X
is such that
b(x)\neq 0
, the induced map
\pi^{\mathrm{an}}:Y_{b}^{\mathrm{an}}\rightarrow X^{\mathrm{an}}
in the analytifications is a local biholomorphism near any point
y
with
\pi(y)=x
.

Proof.

This is clear from (
1
), since
y
will be of the form
\pm(\sqrt{b_{1}(x)},\sqrt{b_{2}(x)})
, and we can define the local inverse
x^{\prime}\mapsto\pm(\sqrt{b_{1}(x^{\prime})},\sqrt{b_{2}(x^{\prime})})
(making the choice of sign compatible with the value of
y
).
∎

We say that a section
b\in\mathcal{B}
is
nonvanishing
if
b(x)\neq 0
for every
x\in X
.

Corollary 3.12
.

If
b\in\mathcal{B}
is a nonvanishing section, the spectral cover
\pi:Y_{b}\rightarrow X
is étale. In particular, if
Y_{b}
is irreducible, the spectral curve
Y_{b}
is a smooth projective curve of genus
2g-1
, where
g
is the genus of
X
.

Moreover, in that case the fibre of the Hitchin morphism
h^{-1}(b)
will be an abelian variety of dimension
g-1
.

Proof.

Since
b
does not vanish,
\pi^{\mathrm{an}}
is a local biholomorphism, which is equivalent to say that
\pi
is étale.
If
\pi
is étale and
X
is smooth,
Y_{b}
must also be smooth. If this
Y_{b}
is irreducible, its genus
g^{\prime}
is given by the Riemann–Hurwitz formula

2g^{\prime}-2=\deg\pi(2g-2),

so

g^{\prime}=2g-1,

since
\deg\pi=2
.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

Of course, the existence of nonvanishing sections in
\mathcal{B}
is not guaranteed a priori. We expect that if
V
satisfies certain conditions, nonvanishing sections can be found generically, however the question remains open.

3.4.3.
The general situation

We explore now the properties of the spectral curve
Y_{b}
and of the modification
\tilde{Y}_{b}
in the more general case where
b
vanishes at some points of
X
. If we assume that
b
is in the open subset
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{B}
of sections that do not vanish at the generic point, the set
b^{-1}(0)
of points where
b
vanishes will be a finite subset of
X
.

From the above discussion, it is clear that over a point

outside of

the spectral curve

is smooth and thus the spectral cover
\pi
is flat. Therefore, we are reduced to study the case where

.

From (

), it is clear that the stalk of
{F}=\pi_{*}{O}_{Y_{b}}
at a point

is

{F}_{x}=\frac{{O}_{X,x}[X,Y]}{(X^{2}-b_{1},Y^{2}-b_{2},XY-b_{3})},

where the

are actually their equivalence classes in

. This can in principle be decomposed as

{F}_{x}={O}_{X,x}\oplus X\cdot{O}_{X,x}\oplus Y\cdot{O}_{X,x}.

When
x\in b^{-1}(0)
, this module
{F}_{x}
will not be free, so it will have a decomposition

{F}_{x}=F_{x}\oplus T_{x},

where
F_{x}
is a free module and
T_{x}
is a torsion module. Let us find the explicit form of this decomposition.

Take a small neighbourhood
U
of

and a local coordinate

, with

and inverse

, for

a disk centered at

, and define the polynomial functions

. Since

, we have that

. Therefore, we can write

and

, for

and

. Moreover, by switching

and

, we can assume without loss of generality that

. If we define

, we have

\displaystyle b_{1}

\displaystyle=z^{n}a_{1}

\displaystyle b_{3}

\displaystyle=z^{m}a_{3}.

We have the relation

b_{3}X-b_{1}Y=(XY)X-X^{2}Y=0

in the module

, so

This implies that

is a torsion element of

. Now, the determinant

\begin{vmatrix}a_{3}&-z^{n-m}a_{1}\\

Case
(1)
. If

or

, then this determinant is not

,

, as a direct sum of a free

and

. Now we have given an explicit description of

. If

and a torsion module

In this case we have that the modification

is locally described by the equations

J^{2}(X,Y,Z)
the Jacobian of
(F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},F_{4}^{2})
. The Jacobian criterion asserts that
\tilde{Y}_{b}
(if it is irreducible) is smooth at a point
y=\tilde{\pi}^{-1}(x)
if and only if
J^{1}(0,0,0)
, in case
(1)
, or
J^{2}(0,0,0)
, in case
(2)
, has rank greater or equal than
2
, the codimension of
\tilde{Y}_{b}
inside the total space of
V
. Now,

J^{1}(0)=\left.\begin{pmatrix}2X&0&-f_{1}^{\prime}(Z)\\
0&2Y&-f_{2}^{\prime}(Z)\\
Y&X&-f_{3}^{\prime}(Z)\\
g_{1}(Z)&-Z^{n-m}g_{1}(Z)&(\cdots)Y\end{pmatrix}\right|_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-f_{1}^{\prime}(0)\\
0&0&-f_{2}^{\prime}(0)\\
0&0&-f_{3}^{\prime}(0)\\
g_{1}(0)&0&0\end{pmatrix}

and

J^{2}(0)=\left.\begin{pmatrix}2X&0&-if_{3}^{\prime}(Z)\\
0&2Y&-f_{2}^{\prime}(Z)\\
Y&X&-f_{3}^{\prime}(Z)\\
g_{3}(Z)&-ig_{3}(Z)&g_{3}^{\prime}(Z)(X-iY)\end{pmatrix}\right|_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&-if_{3}^{\prime}(0)\\
0&0&-f_{2}^{\prime}(0)\\
0&0&-f_{3}^{\prime}(0)\\
g_{3}(0)&g_{3}(0)&0\end{pmatrix}

Both of these matrices have rank
\geq 2
if and only if one of the
f_{i}^{\prime}(0)
is nonzero; that is, if
m
equals
1
.

We have proven:

Proposition 3.14
.

If the modification
\tilde{Y}_{b}
is irreducible, then it is smooth if and only if
b
does not have a multiple zero.

4.
Gauge theoretic equations and Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence

4.1.
The generalized Hitchin equations

Higgs bundles twisted by a vector bundle appear as solutions to gauge theoretic equations that are a generalization of the Hitchin equations. These are defined over a compact Riemann surface that we also denote by
X
.

We fix once and for all a volume form
\omega_{X}\in\Omega^{1,1}(X)
with
\int_{X}\omega_{X}=1
.
Consider
V
a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
2
over
X
and fix
h
a Hermitian metric on
V
. If the vector bundle
V
is stable, we can choose the solution to the Hermitian Yang–Mills equations given by the theorem of Narasimhan–Seshadri.

Suppose that
(E,\varphi)
a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle on
X
and denote

\lambda_{E}=-i\pi\deg E\id_{E}\in\Omega^{0}(X,\End E).

If
H
is a Hermitian metric on
E
, the twisted endomorphism
\varphi
has a smooth adjoint morphism
\varphi^{\dagger}:E\otimes V\rightarrow E
with respect to the metric induced by
H
and
h
, so it makes sense to consider the composition
\varphi^{\dagger}\circ\varphi\in\Omega^{0}(X,\End E)
. Moreover, regarding
\varphi^{\dagger}
as a smooth map
E\rightarrow E\otimes V^{\vee}
, we can also consider the composition
\varphi\circ\varphi^{\dagger}
and thus the commutator
[\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger}]\in\Omega^{0}(X,\End E)
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(E,\varphi)
be a
V
-twisted Higgs bundle. We say that a Hermitian metric
H
on
E
is a
solution to the generalized Hitchin equations
if

\Lambda F_{H}+[\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger}]=\lambda_{E},

where
F_{H}
is the curvature of the Chern connection associated to the metric
H
on
E
and
\Lambda
is the adjoint operator of
\omega_{X}\wedge-
.

We have the following
Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence
:

Proposition 4.2
.

A
V
-twisted Higgs bundle
(E,\varphi)
is polystable if and only if it admits a solution to the generalized Hitchin equations.

Proof.

The generalized Hitchin equations are a special case of the
twisted quiver vortex equations
, indroduced in
[
2
]
. More precisely, we can regard
(E,\varphi)
as a holomorphic
V
-twisted
Q
-bundle on
X
, where
Q
is the quiver
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q=\bullet}
.

The Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence for twisted quiver bundles
[
2
]
*Thm. 3.1 guarantees the existence of the solution
H
, given that
(E,\varphi)
is polystable, and viceversa.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Although the generalized Hitchin equations are a particular case of the more general gauge equations studied in
[
2
]
, in that paper the commuting condition is not considered. However, this extra condition does not affect the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence.

4.2.
The moment map

Understanding the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence as an infinite-dimensional analogue of the Kempf–Ness theorem,
V
-twisted Higgs bundles can be studied in terms of a moment map. As above, we fix a rank
2
holomorphic vector bundle
V
on
X
and a Hermitian metric
h
on
V
.

We consider
\mathbb{E}\rightarrow X
a rank
2
smooth complex vector bundle and fix a Hermitian metric
H
on
\mathbb{E}
. We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{H}
the space of
H
-unitary connections on
\mathbb{E}
. Of course, given a connection
A\in\mathcal{A}_{H}
, the associated operator
\bar{\partial}_{A}
endows
\mathbb{E}
with the structure of a holomorphic vector bundle. Finally, we denote

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{A}_{H}\times\Omega^{0}(X,\End\mathbb{E}\otimes V).

The tangent space of
\mathcal{H}
at any point
(A,\varphi)
is the vector space
\Omega^{1}(X,\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E}))\times\Omega^{0}(X,\End\mathbb{E}\otimes V)
, where
\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E})
is the bundle of skew-Hermitian endomorphisms of
\mathbb{E}
for the metric
H
. We can define a symplectic on
\mathcal{H}
given by

\Omega\left((B_{1},\psi_{1}),(B_{2},\psi_{2})\right)=-\int_{X}\tr(B_{1}\wedge B_{2})+2i\mathrm{Im}(\psi_{1}^{\dagger}\psi_{2})\omega_{X}.

The (real)
gauge group
of
\mathbb{E}
is
\mathcal{G}=\Omega^{0}(X,\mathrm{U}_{H}(\mathbb{E}))
the group of sections of the bundle
\mathrm{U}_{H}(\mathbb{E})
given by
H
-unitary endomorphisms of
\mathbb{E}
. The group
\mathcal{G}
acts symplectically by by conjugation on
\mathcal{H}
. The Lie algebra of
\mathcal{G}
is
\Omega^{0}(X,\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E}))
.
Therefore, the moment map for the action of
\mathcal{G}
can be regarded as a map

\displaystyle\mu:\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Omega^{0}(X,\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E})).

The same arguments that those given in
[
15
]
*§4 show that this map is precisely

\mu(A,\varphi)=\Lambda F_{A}+[\varphi,\varphi^{\dagger}].

Now, we can consider the associated symplectic quotient
\mu^{-1}(\lambda_{\mathbb{E}})/\mathcal{G}
. The space we are interested in is the subvariety

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbb{E})=\left\{[(A,\varphi)]\in\mu^{-1}(\lambda_{\mathbb{E}})/\mathcal{G}:\bar{\partial}_{A,V}\varphi=0\text{ and }\varphi\wedge\varphi=0\right\},

where
\bar{\partial}_{A,V}=\bar{\partial}_{A}\otimes\id_{V}+\id_{\End\mathbb{E}}\otimes\bar{\partial}_{V}
is the Dolbeaut operator induced on
\End\mathbb{E}\otimes V
by the connection
A
and the holomorphic structure of
V
.

The decomposition
\End(\mathbb{E})=\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E})\oplus i\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E})
induces an isomorphism between
\Omega^{1}(X,\mathfrak{u}_{H}(\mathbb{E}))
and
\Omega^{0,1}(X,\End\mathbb{E})
. This allows us to define a complex structure on
\mathcal{A}_{H}
by
J(\alpha)=i\alpha
. Together with the natural complex structure on
\Omega^{0}(X,\End\mathbb{E}\otimes V)
, this gives a complex structure on
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, this complex structure is compatible with the symplectic structure
\Omega
and thus they give a Kähler structure. Since the action of the gauge group
\mathcal{G}
preserves these structures, the symplectic quotient is in fact a Kähler quotient. Therefore, in general
\mu^{-1}(\lambda_{\mathbb{E}})/\mathcal{G}
is a Kähler orbifold. Since the conditions defining
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbb{E})
are analytic, the space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbb{E})
is a complex analytic variety.

The Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence given by the above Proposition
4.2
implies that there is a canonical bijection between the set
S(d)
of isomorphism classes of polystable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles on
X
and the space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbb{E})
.

Moreover, suppose that
X
is a smooth complex projective curve,
X^{\mathrm{an}}
denotes the Riemann surface obtained by analytification of
X
and
\mathcal{M}(d)^{\mathrm{an}}
is the analytification of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(d)
of semistable
V
-twisted Higgs bundles of degree
d
, where
d=\deg\mathbb{E}
. We expect that the Hitchin–Kobayashi correspondence gives an analytic isomorphism between
\mathcal{M}(d)^{\mathrm{an}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{gauge}}(\mathbb{E})
. This is in fact well-known folklore in the field, and similar results are obtained by applying the
Kuranishi slice method
. The reader may refer to
[
13
]
for a detailed exposition of these techniques for the moduli space of (usual) Higgs bundles.

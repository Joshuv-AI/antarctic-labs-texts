A least-squares Galerkin gradient recovery method for fully nonlinear elliptic equations
Omar Lakkis and Amireh Mousavi

Abstract
. We propose a least squares Galerkin based gradient recovery to approximate Dirichlet problems for strong solutions of linear elliptic problems in nondivergence form and corresponding a priori and a posteriori error bounds. This approach is used to tackle fully nonlinear elliptic problems, e.g., Monge–Ampère, Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman, using the smooth (vanilla) and the semismooth Newton linearization. We discuss numerical results, including adaptive methods based on the a posteriori error indicators.

2.
A least-squares Galerkin approach to gradient recovery for linear

3.
Linearization of fully nonlinear problems

In this section, we present the Newton differentiability concept
to operators, which can even include non-smooth operators. This concept
is useful to extend the standard Newton linearization to the problems
with non-smooth operator. We state the convergence analysis of a
linearization method which is based on this concept. We then discuss
linearization of two specific fully nonlinear PDEs, namely
Monge–Ampère and Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equations that lead to a
sequence of linear equations in nondivergence form.

3.1.
Definition of Newton differentiable operator,
Ito and Kunisch 2008

Let
\mathscr{{X}}
and
\mathscr{{Z}}
be Banach
spaces and let
\mathcal{U}
be a non-empty open
subset of
\mathscr{{X}}
. An operator
\mathcal{{F}}:\mathcal{U}\subset\mathscr{{X}}\to\mathscr{{Z}}
is called

Newton differentiable
at
x\in\mathcal{U}
if there
exists a set-valued map with non-empty images

\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}:\mathcal{U}\rightrightarrows\operatorname{Lin}\left({{\mathscr{{X}}}\to{\mathscr{{Z}}}}\right)

(where the double arrow signifies values in the power set of the right-hand
side) such that

(21)

\lim_{\left\|{e}\right\|_{\mathscr{{X}}}\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{1}{\left\|{e}\right\|_{\mathscr{{X}}}}\sup_{\mathcal{{D}}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}[x]}\left\|{\mathcal{{F}}[x+e]-\mathcal{{F}}[x]-\mathcal{{D}}e}\right\|_{\mathscr{{Z}}}=0\text{ for each }x\in\mathcal{U}.

The nonlinear operator
\mathcal{{F}}

is called
Newton differentiable on

\mathcal{U}
with Newton
derivative
\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}
if
\mathcal{{F}}
is Newton differentiable at
x
, for
every
x\in\mathcal{U}
.

The set-valued map
\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}[x]
is single-valued at
x
if
and only if
\mathcal{{F}}
is Fréchet differentiable and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}[x]={\left\{{\operatorname{D}\!\mathcal{{F}}[x]}\right\}}
.

3.2.
Theorem (Superlinear convergence)

Suppose that a nonlinear operator
\mathcal{{F}}
is Newton differentiable in an open neighborhood

\mathcal{U}
of

x^{\ast}
, solution of
\mathcal{{F}}[x]=0
.
If for any
x\in U
, the all
D\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}[x]
are non-singular and
\left\|{D^{-1}}\right\|

are bounded, then the Newton iteration

(22)

x_{n+1}=x_{n}-D^{-1}_{n}\mathcal{{F}}[x_{n}],\quad D_{n}\in\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\mathcal{{F}}[x_{n}]

converges superlinearly to
x^{\ast}
provided that
x_{0}
is sufficiently close to
x^{\ast}
.

Definition of The Monge–Ampère equation

Let
\varOmega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2}

Let

\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\operatorname{\mathscr{M}}[v]:=\operatorname{Cof}\operatorname{D}\!^{2}v:\operatorname{D}\!^{2}.

Theorem (superlinear convergence of iterative method to MA equation)

The operator

is Fréchet differentiable and thus Newton differentiable.
Moreover, if the initial guess

Definition of Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman equation

Let
\varOmega

\operatorname{\mathfrak{D}}\operatorname{\mathscr{B}}[v]:=\left\{\mathcal{{L}}^{q}:=(\boldsymbol{{A}}^{q}:\operatorname{D}\!^{2}+\boldsymbol{b}^{q}{}^{\intercal}\nabla-c^{q})\left|\penalty\ q\in\mathcal{N}[v]\right\}\right..

4.

We discuss two numerical tests one for each of Monge–Ampère via
Newton and Hamilton–Jacobi–Bellman problems that demonstrate the
robustness of our method to the fully nonlinear problems. For both

Proof of Renyi QNEC for free fermions
Pratik Roy

Abstract
Quantum null energy condition (QNEC) is usually stated as a bound on the expectation value of null components of the stress energy tensor at a point in terms of second null shape variations of the entanglement entropy at the same point. It can be recast as the statement that the sign of the second null shape variation of the relative entropy of any state with respect to the vacuum is positive. Using instead a Renyi generalization of relative entropy, called sandwiched Renyi divergence (SRD), leads to what is termed the Renyi QNEC: the second null shape variation of SRD of any state with respect to the vacuum is positive. In this work, we prove the Renyi QNEC for free and superrenormalizable fermionic quantum field theories in spacetime dimensions greater than 2 using null quantization, for the case where the Renyi parameter n > 1 n>1 . We end with comments on multiple possible generalizations.

Evidence for validity of QNEC has been accumulating over the years.

gave the first proof of QNEC for free and superrenormalizable bosons based on replica trick calculations.

Relative entropy is a measure of the distinguishability of a state

\sigma
. It is defined to be always positive,
S_{\rm rel}(\rho|\sigma)\geq 0

It is in fact monotonic under positive maps

This monotonicity property is often referred to in the literature as the data processing inequality (DPI). For states that are close to each other, DPI can be thought of as a constraint on the sign of the first derivative of
S_{\rm rel}

One such measure is the sandwiched Renyi divergence
Muller:2013Onqr
;
Wilde:2013bdg
, a Renyi generalization of the relative entropy.
Lashkari:2014yva
showed that some sandwiched Renyi divergences (SRDs) can be written as correlation functions in quantum field theory. Based on the path integral expression for SRD in
Lashkari:2014yva
,
Lashkari:2018nsl
put constraints on correlation functions in QFT based on some known properties of the divergence. It was further conjectured there that the second null variation of the SRD should also be positive, thus providing a Renyi generalization of QNEC, which we will refer to as Renyi QNEC. A few examples where the conjecture holds were also demonstrated. Very recently,
Moosa:2020jwt
gave a proof of the Renyi QNEC for free and superrenormalizable bosons in spacetime dimensions
D>2.

In this work, we provide a proof of Renyi QNEC for free and superrenormalizable fermions in
D>2
spacetime dimensions. Our proof closely follows the one presented in
Moosa:2020jwt
. We use the formalism of null quantization to write an arbitrary state as an expansion around the vacuum, and reduce the Renyi QNEC for arbitrary states to a statement regarding a state perturbatively close to the vacuum. One can then evaluate the relevant SRD variations using expansions known in the literature, and show that the conjecture indeed is true in some cases. We note that the conjecture is not true for some values of the Renyi parameter, as also found in
Moosa:2020jwt
. We do not investigate these.

We now present an outline of the rest of the paper. Section

Finally, we conclude with a discussion of possible generalizations and applications of Renyi QNEC. Two appendices contain details of the fermionic theory that we consider, and the calculation of correlation functions needed for completing the proof.

\mathcal{H}_{A}\equiv\mathcal{H}_{\rm auxiliary}
contains all the remaining degrees of freedom. Consider a density matrix on
\mathcal{H}
, which we deform to obtain a one-parameter family of density matrices
\rho(\lambda)
by tracing out the part of the pencil
P
in the past of affine parameter
\lambda.
For small
\mathscr{A}
, the state of the pencil is near the vacuum, and the global state can be written as

\rho(\lambda)=\sigma_{P}(\lambda)\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)+\cdots.

(19)

This can be seen as follows. Since the probability to have
n
particles on a pencil should scale extensively with the area, it is proportional to
\mathscr{A}^{n}
for each pencil. Then, terms of the form
\left|n\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle m\right|
in the pencil Fock basis scale as
\mathscr{A}^{(n+m)/2}
. Thus the leading contribution to the perturbation,
\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)
, is of order
\mathscr{A}^{1/2}
, with terms of the form
\left|0\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle 1\right|
and
\left|1\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle 0\right|
. Explicitly, the state on
N
must take the form

\displaystyle\rho=\left|0\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle 0\right|\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}+

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\sum_{ij}\big(\left|0\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\psi_{ij}^{1}\right|+\left|\psi_{ij}^{2}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle 0\right|\big)\otimes\left|i\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle j\right|

(20)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\sum_{ij}\big(\left|0\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\psi_{ij}^{2}\right|+\left|\psi_{ij}^{1}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle 0\right|\big)\otimes\left|j\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle i\right|+\cdots,

where
\left|\psi_{ij}\right\rangle
are single particle states on the pencil CFT which can be constructed by inserting single operators in the fermionic path integral; and
\left\{\left|i\right\rangle,\left|j\right\rangle\right\}
together form an arbitrary basis of
\mathcal{H}_{A}
such that
\left\{\left|i\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle j\right|,\left|j\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle i\right|\right\}
form a Grasssmann-odd basis of operators on
\mathcal{H}_{A}
. The basis elements need to be Grassmann-odd since the pencil CFT has one fermion excited in the leading contribution to the perturbation. We also note for later use that physical observables can only be Grassmann-even. Note that this can be achieved, e.g., by taking
\left|i\right\rangle(\left|j\right\rangle)
to be states with an even (odd) number of creation operators acting on the vacuum. We stick to this convention throughout the paper. A resolution of the identity operator for the auxiliary system in this basis is

\mathbf{1}_{a}=\sum_{i}\left|i\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle i\right|+\sum_{j}\left|j\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle j\right|\equiv\sum_{\mu}\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\mu\right|,

(21)

where the sum over
i,
respectively
j,
runs over states with even, respectively odd, numbers of creation operators. We use the convention that indices
\mu,\nu
can correspond to either even (
i
) or odd (
j
) states. Note that the auxiliary system,
\rho_{A}^{(0)},
is not necessarily in the vacuum state. Entanglement between the pencil and the auxiliary system is contained in
\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)
and the further subleading terms.

Since the theory on each pencil is chiral, we can replace translations (and derivatives) along
\lambda
by translations (and derivatives) along the spatial direction. Also, the single particle states,
\left|\psi_{ij}\right\rangle
, of the Fock space can be constructed by a Euclidean path integral over the lower half plane,
\tau<0,
with insertions of a single field.

The affine parameter
\lambda=V_{P}
,
V_{P}
some constant, describes the location of the entangling surface on the pencil or, equivalently, the location
x=\lambda
on the
\tau=0
slice of the Euclidean path integral. Using null translation invariance of the free theory, we choose to fix the entangling surface to be at
x=0
by moving the operator insertions simultaneously. The reduced density matrix
\rho(\lambda)
then corresponds to tracing out the region
x<0.
Deformations of the entangling region now amount to changing the location of the operator insertions, allowing us to evaluate shape derivatives with relative ease. Further, the density matrix
\sigma_{P}^{(0)}
is now independent of
\lambda.
In fact,

\sigma_{P}=e^{-2\pi K_{P}},

(22)

where
K_{P}
is the modular Hamiltonian on the pencil
Unruh:1976db
;
Bisognano:1976za
.

We can now write an arbitrary state as

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda)=\sigma_{P}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}\,+

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\sum_{ij}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\,f_{ij}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\otimes\left|i\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle j\right|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\sum_{ij}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\,f_{ji}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\otimes\left|j\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle i\right|+\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{A}),

(23)

which we write as

\displaystyle\rho(\lambda)=\sigma_{P}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}\,+

\displaystyle\,\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\sum_{\mu\nu}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\,f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\otimes\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu\right|+\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{A}).

(24)

Defining

\displaystyle\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\mu\nu}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\,f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\otimes\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu\right|,

we succinctly write

\rho(\lambda)=\sigma_{P}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)+\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{A}),

(25)

with the factor of
\mathscr{A}^{1/2}
explicit. To ensure that the state is Hermitian, we need to impose

f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)=-if_{\nu\mu}^{*}(r,2\pi-\theta),

(26)

where we emphasize that
\mu,\nu
indices can be either
i
or
j
.

It is necessary to require that
f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)
vanishes at
\theta=0,\pi
so that the state is normalizable. To ensure that SRD is finite for the states we study, it is further necessary to restrict the support of the functions
f_{\mu\nu}
to a wedge of size
2\pi/n
centered at
\theta=\pi
, as discussed in Section
2.3
. We will refer to this requirement as the wedge condition.

3.2
Statement of Renyi QNEC

Recall that for density matrices, sandwiched Renyi divergence (SRD) of
\rho
with respect to
\sigma
is given by

S_{n}^{R}(\rho|\sigma)=\frac{1}{n-1}\log\widehat{Z}_{n}(\rho|\sigma),

(27)

where

\widehat{Z}_{n}(\rho|\sigma)=\text{Tr}\,\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\right)^{n}\right].

(28)

For the Renyi QNEC,
\rho
is the density matrix
\rho(\lambda)
introduced above, defined for the subregion
v>V(\vec{y})
, along the null surface
N
, and
\sigma
is the vacuum density matrix. The deformation relevant for Renyi QNEC is then simply translation along the affine parameter
\lambda
on the pencil
P
near
\lambda=0
, and Renyi QNEC is the statement that, for
1<n\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left.\lim_{\mathscr{A}\to 0}\frac{1}{\mathscr{A}}\frac{d^{2}}{d\lambda^{2}}S_{n}(\rho(\lambda)|\sigma)\right|_{\lambda=0}\geq 0.

(29)

If we expand the state
\rho(\lambda)
as

\rho(\lambda)=\sigma_{P}\otimes\rho_{A}^{(0)}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)+\mathscr{A}\rho^{(2)}(\lambda)+\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{A}^{3/2}),

(30)

\widehat{Z}_{n}
can be expanded as

\widehat{Z}_{n}(\rho(\lambda)|\sigma)=\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(0)}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(1)}(\lambda)+\mathscr{A}\left(\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(2)}(\lambda)+\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)\right)+\mathcal{O}(\mathscr{A}^{3/2}),

(31)

where
\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(i)}
contains contributions from the
i^{\rm th}
term of the
\rho(\lambda)
expansion, and
\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}
contains two powers of
\rho^{(1)}
. Using this is (
29
), one can recast the Renyi QNEC statement as
Moosa:2020jwt

\left.\frac{d^{2}}{d\lambda^{2}}Z_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)\right|_{\lambda=0}\geq 0,

(32)

where

Z_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{n-1}\widehat{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda).

(33)

3.3
Reformulation as a perturbative calculation

As it stands, we need to perform a calculation of SRD between an arbitrary state and the vacuum. As demonstrated in
Moosa:2020jwt
however, we can improve the situation and restate our problem as a calculation of SRD between two perturbatively close states.

Let us first define

\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}=\sigma_{P}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)},\qquad\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}=\left(\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho_{A}^{(0)}\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\right)^{n},

(34)

and choose a basis,
\left\{\left|i\right\rangle,\left|j\right\rangle\right\}
, such that
\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}
acts diagonally,

\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}\left|\mu\right\rangle=e^{-2\pi K_{\mu}}\left|\mu\right\rangle.

(35)

We also define

E_{\mu\nu}\equiv e^{\theta(K_{\mu}-K_{\nu})}\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu\right|.

(36)

Then, inserting a complete set of states, we can write

\displaystyle\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}=\sum_{\mu_{1}\mu\nu\mu_{2}}

\displaystyle\,\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}+1}\left(\int drd\theta\,f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}

\displaystyle\otimes\left|\mu_{1}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\mu_{1}\right|\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu\right|\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left|\mu_{2}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\mu_{2}\right|.

(37)

Note that since
\sigma_{a}
is a Grassmann-even operator, elements such as
\left\langle i\right|\sigma_{a}^{m}\left|j\right\rangle
will vanish.

Now define

\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)=\sum_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}f_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r,\theta)\left\langle\mu\right|\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left|\mu_{1}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu_{1}\right|\sigma_{a}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left|\nu\right\rangle,

(38)

which satisfies the same reality conditions as
f_{\mu\nu}
. This allows us to write

\displaystyle\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\otimes\left|\mu\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu\right|.

(39)

Note that any function that satisfies the same reality and support conditions as
f_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)
is equally valid as a support function for the field. We use this freedom to write
Moosa:2020jwt

\displaystyle\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho^{(1)}(\lambda)\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}=

\displaystyle\ \sum_{\mu\nu}\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\left(\sigma_{P}\int drd\theta\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\right)\sigma_{P}^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\otimes\left((\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}E_{\mu\nu}(\theta)(\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\right).

(40)

Defining

\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\nu}\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\int drd\theta\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\left(\psi(re^{i\theta}-\lambda)\otimes E_{\mu\nu}(\theta)\right),

(41)

we have the result that

\displaystyle Z_{n}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{n-1}\text{Tr}\,\left[\left(\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\rho\sigma^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\right)^{n}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{n-1}\text{Tr}\,\left[\left(\big(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\big)^{1/n}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\big(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\big)^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)\big(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\big)^{\frac{1-n}{2n}}\right)^{n}\right].

(42)

This is equivalent to

Z_{n}(\lambda)=Z_{n}\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}+\mathscr{A}^{1/2}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)|\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\right),

(43)

which is just the SRD between two perturbatively close states. This quantity has been studied previously in
May:2018tir
;
Moosa:2020jwt
, and is much simpler than the original statement (
32
) involving a state arbitrarily far from the vacuum.

4
Proof

We prove Renyi QNEC in two ways. We first deal with the case where the Renyi index
1<n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. This proof is much shorter and neater, following just from reflection positivity, the Euclidean analogue of unitarity. Then we proceed to calculate the second variation of SRD for arbitrary
n
, and calculate correlation functions of the pencil and the auxiliary systems to show that the relevant shape variation is in fact positive.

4.1
For integer
n>1

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+},
it is easy to see from (
43
) that

Z_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\text{Tr}\,\left((\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1+\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right).

(44)

Denoting
\lambda
-derivatives by dots, we are interested in the second derivative

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\bigg[

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\bigg((\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1+\frac{k}{n}}\ddot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)\bigg)

\displaystyle+2\,\text{Tr}\,\left((\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1+\frac{k}{n}}\dot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\dot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right)

(45)

\displaystyle+\text{Tr}\,\left((\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1+\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\ddot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right)\bigg].

Using the explicit form of
\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}
given in (
41
) and setting
\lambda=0
, this becomes

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int d\alpha\bigg[

\displaystyle\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{k}{n}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\right)

\displaystyle\ +2\,\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{k}{n}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\ +\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{k}{n}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\right)\bigg],

where we have defined

\int d\alpha=\sum_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}\sum_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int_{\pi-\pi/n}^{\pi+\pi/n}d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\tilde{f}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}),

(47)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\mu\nu}=\psi(re^{i\theta})\otimes E_{\mu\nu}(\theta),\quad\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial\psi(re^{i\theta})\otimes E_{\mu\nu}(\theta),\quad\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial^{2}\psi(re^{i\theta})\otimes E_{\mu\nu}(\theta).

(48)

Since the conformal weight of
\partial^{m}\psi
is
(\frac{1}{2}+m,0)
, it transforms under conjugation by
\sigma_{P}
as

\sigma_{P}^{-\frac{k}{2n}}\partial^{m}\psi(re^{i\theta})\sigma_{P}^{\frac{k}{2n}}=e^{i\pi\frac{k}{n}(m+\frac{1}{2})}\partial^{m}\psi(re^{i\theta+i\pi\frac{k}{n}}).

(49)

The other factor,
E_{\mu\nu}(\theta)
, tranforms under conjugation by
\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}
as

(\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{2n}}E_{\mu\nu}(\theta)(\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{\frac{k}{2n}}=E_{\mu\nu}(\theta+\pi k/n).

(50)

Combining these, we can conjugate the operators
\mathcal{O}_{\mu\nu}
and their derivatives to get

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int d\alpha\bigg[

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{5\pi k}{2n}}\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right)

\displaystyle+\,2

\displaystyle\,\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right)

(51)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\,e^{i\frac{5\pi k}{2n}}\text{Tr}\,\left(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right)\bigg].

Due to the wedge condition, the opertor insertions are angle-ordered and the terms above can be written as correlation functions,

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int d\alpha\bigg[

\displaystyle e^{-i\frac{5\pi k}{2n}}\left\langle\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\ +2\left\langle\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right\rangle

(52)

\displaystyle\ +e^{i\frac{5\pi k}{2n}}\left\langle\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right\rangle\bigg].

Now we note that since

\langle\partial_{z}^{2}\psi(z)\psi(w)\rangle_{p}=-\langle\partial_{z}\psi(z)\partial_{w}\psi(w)\rangle_{p}=\langle\psi(z)\partial_{w}^{2}\psi(w)\rangle_{p},

(53)

the correlators appearing in
\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}
are in fact all proportional to each other. This allows us to write

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\frac{2n}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\int d\alpha\sin^{2}\left(\frac{5\pi k}{4n}\right)\left\langle\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2}+\pi k/n)\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1}-\pi k/n)\right\rangle.

(54)

Defining the (Grassmann-even) operators

\begin{split}\Psi_{k}=&\ \sum_{\mu\nu}\int dr\int_{\pi-\pi/n}^{\pi+\pi/n}d\theta\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta-\pi k/n),\\
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\Psi\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{k}=&\ \sum_{\mu\nu}\int dr\int_{\pi-\pi/n}^{\pi+\pi/n}d\theta\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta+\pi k/n),\end{split}

(55)

we see that

\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\frac{2n}{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{5\pi k}{4n}\right)\langle\overline{\Psi}_{k}\Psi_{k}\rangle.

(56)

Under the change of variables
\theta\to 2\pi-\theta
, we have
f_{\mu\nu}(r,2\pi-\theta)=-if_{\nu\mu}^{*}(r,\theta)
and

\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu\nu}(r,2\pi-\theta)=i(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\nu\mu}^{\dagger}(r,\theta)\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}.

(57)

Using both of these, we get

\overline{\Psi}_{k}=(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1}\Psi_{k}^{\dagger}\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}.

(58)

The correlators in
\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}
now become

\langle\overline{\Psi}_{k}\Psi_{k}\rangle=\langle(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1}\Psi_{k}^{\dagger}\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\Psi_{k}\rangle=\langle\Psi_{k}\Psi_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle>0,

(59)

where the last inequality is just the statement of reflection positivity
9
9

9

See, e.g.,
Simmons-Duffin:2016gjk
, for an overview of reflection positivity.
. Thus all terms in (
56
) are individually positive, and we have proved Renyi QNEC for integer
n>1.

4.2
Second variation of SRD for arbitrary
n

Let
\left|\kappa\right\rangle
denote a basis in which
\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}
is diagonal, i.e.,

\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}\left|\kappa\right\rangle=e^{-2\pi\kappa}\left|\kappa\right\rangle,

(60)

where
\kappa
can be negative since
\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}
is not a normalized density matrix. Recall from (
44
) that

Z_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\text{Tr}\,\left((\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-1+\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{k}{n}}\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right).

(61)

One can show that, in this basis, we have the following result for arbitrary
n

Moosa:2020jwt

Z_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{2}\int d\kappa\int d\kappa^{\prime}e^{2\pi\kappa^{\prime}}F_{n}(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})\left|\left\langle\kappa\left|\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)\right|\kappa^{\prime}\right\rangle\right|^{2},

(62)

where

F_{n}(x)=-\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{e^{2\pi\left(\frac{n-1}{n}\right)x}-1}{e^{-2\pi x/n}-1}.

(63)

Taking two derivatives with respect to
\lambda
gives us

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\ \int d\kappa d\kappa^{\prime}e^{2\pi\kappa^{\prime}}F_{n}(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})

(64)

\displaystyle\ \qquad\times\left(\langle\kappa|\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}(\lambda)|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\ddot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)|\kappa\rangle+\langle\kappa|\dot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\dot{\tilde{\rho}}^{(1)}(\lambda)|\kappa\rangle\right),

where dots again denote
\lambda
-derivatives. Using the definition of
\tilde{\rho}^{(1)}
in (
41
) and setting
\lambda=0
,

\displaystyle\left.\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}\right|_{\lambda=0}

\displaystyle=\int d\alpha\int d\kappa d\kappa^{\prime}e^{-2\pi\kappa}F_{n}(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})

(65)

\displaystyle\times\Big(

\displaystyle\langle\kappa|\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle+\langle\kappa|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle\Big),

where
\int d\alpha
is defined in (
47
) and
\mathcal{O}_{\mu\nu}
in (
48
).

Making the angular-ordering explicit, this is

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\int_{\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}}d\alpha\int d\kappa d\kappa^{\prime}e^{-2\pi\kappa}F_{n}(\kappa-\kappa^{\prime})

(66)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\langle\kappa|\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle+\langle\kappa|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}}d\alpha\int d\kappa d\kappa^{\prime}e^{-2\pi\kappa^{\prime}}F_{n}(\kappa^{\prime}-\kappa)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\langle\kappa|\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle+\langle\kappa|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})|\kappa^{\prime}\rangle\langle\kappa^{\prime}|\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})|\kappa\rangle\right),

where we have used the fact that
F_{n}(x)=e^{2\pi x}F_{n}(-x).
Decomposing
F_{n}(x)
into Fourier modes as

F_{n}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\,e^{isx}\mathcal{F}_{n}(s),

(67)

one obtains

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{\theta_{1}>\theta_{2}}d\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\mathcal{F}_{n}(s)\bigg\{\text{Tr}\,\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{1-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right]

(68)

\displaystyle\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\text{Tr}\,\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{1-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right]\bigg\}

\displaystyle\ +\int_{\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}}d\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\mathcal{F}_{n}(-s)\bigg\{\text{Tr}\,\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{1+\frac{is}{2\pi}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\right]

\displaystyle\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\text{Tr}\,\left[(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{1+\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\right]\bigg\}.

Since correlation functions are defined to be implicitly ordered in angular time, we can now write

\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\int d\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\mathcal{F}_{n}({\rm sign}(\theta_{12})s)\mathcal{G}(s),

(69)

where we have defined
\theta_{ij}=\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\rangle

(70)

\displaystyle\ \qquad+\left\langle(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\rangle.

Using the Fourier transform of
\mathcal{G}(s)
, defined by

G(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-is\omega}\mathcal{G}(s),

(71)

we obtain

\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\int d\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,F_{n}({\rm sign}(\theta_{12})\omega)G(\omega).

(72)

We write this instead as

\ddot{Z}_{n}^{(1,1)}=\int d\alpha\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)e^{{\rm sign}(\theta_{12})\pi\omega}G(\omega),

(73)

with

\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)=e^{-\pi\omega}F_{n}(\omega)=\frac{n}{n-1}\frac{\sinh\pi\omega\frac{n-1}{n}}{\sinh\pi\omega/n}

(74)

The correlation function
\mathcal{G}(s)
and its Fourier transform
G(\omega)
are calculated explicitly in Appendix
B
. The result we obtain is

\displaystyle G(\omega)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{8}\delta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\delta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{1}}e^{-\pi(K_{\mu_{1}}+K_{\mu_{2}})}e^{-{\rm sign}(\theta_{12})\pi\omega}(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{iK_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}

\displaystyle\ \quad\times\left[Q(z-i)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{1-i\omega}+Q(z)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega}\right],

(75)

where
z=K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}-\omega
and

Q(x)=\frac{4x^{2}+1}{\cosh\pi x}.

(76)

We finally have that

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{8}\int d\tilde{\alpha}\,e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{iK_{\mu\nu}}

(77)

\displaystyle\ \times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)\left[Q(z)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega}+Q(z-i)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{1-i\omega}\right],

where

\int d\tilde{\alpha}=\sum_{\mu\nu}\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1},\theta_{1})\tilde{f}_{\nu\mu}(r_{2},\theta_{2}).

(78)

4.3
Proving Renyi QNEC for
n>1

Note that
Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega)
has no poles in the strip
-1/2\leq{\rm Im}(\omega)\leq 1/2,
and that the poles of
\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)
are at
\omega=inp,p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then, for
n>1
, we can make a contour deformation
\omega\to\omega\pm i/2
without crossing any poles. This leads to the equalities

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)Q(z)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)Q(z+i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega-1/2},

(79)

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega)Q(z-i)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{1-i\omega}=

\displaystyle\ \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)Q(z-i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega+1/2}.

We note that this deformation makes use of the fact that the source functions
\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}
are non-vanishing only inside
|\theta-\pi|<\pi/n
, in which range the integrand vanishes as
{\rm Re}(\omega)\to\pm\infty.
Using these relations, we can write

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{8}\int d\tilde{\alpha}\,e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{iK_{\mu\nu}}

(80)

\displaystyle\ \times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)\left[Q(z+i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega-1/2}+Q(z-i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega+1/2}\right].

Under
\mu\leftrightarrow\nu,(r_{1},\theta_{1})\leftrightarrow(r_{2},\theta_{2}),\omega\to-\omega,
we get

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{8}\int d\tilde{\alpha}\,e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{iK_{\mu\nu}}

(81)

\displaystyle\ \times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega+i/2)\left[Q(z-i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega+1/2}+Q(z+i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega-1/2}\right].

This gives us

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{16}\int d\tilde{\alpha}\,e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}\right)^{iK_{\mu\nu}}

(82)

\displaystyle\times\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega

\displaystyle\left[\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega+i/2)+\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)\right]\left[Q(z+i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega-1/2}+Q(z-i/2)\left(\frac{r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}}}{r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}}\right)^{-i\omega+1/2}\right].

We want to show that this expression is positive definite. It can be written as

\displaystyle\ -\frac{i}{16}\sum_{\mu\nu}e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\left[\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega+i/2)+\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)\right]\

\displaystyle\ \times\bigg[Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega+i/2)\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1},\theta_{1})r_{1}^{iK_{\mu\nu}-i\omega-2}e^{-(2i-\omega)\theta_{1}}\tilde{f}_{\nu\mu}(r_{2},\theta_{2})r_{2}^{-iK_{\mu\nu}+i\omega-1}e^{-(i+\omega)\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle\ +Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega-i/2)\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1},\theta_{1})r_{1}^{iK_{\mu\nu}-i\omega-1}e^{-(i-\omega)\theta_{1}}\tilde{f}_{\nu\mu}(r_{2},\theta_{2})r_{2}^{-iK_{\mu\nu}+i\omega-2}e^{-(2i+\omega)\theta_{2}}\bigg]

One can show that for
n\geq 1,

\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega+i/2)+\widetilde{F}_{n}(\omega-i/2)=-\frac{2n}{n-1}\frac{\cosh\pi\omega\sin\frac{\pi}{n}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{n}-\cosh\frac{2\pi\omega}{n}}\leq 0.

We now make the substitution
\theta_{2}\to 2\pi-\theta_{2}
, and use
\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r,\theta)=-i\tilde{f}_{\nu\mu}^{*}(r,2\pi-\theta)
. The term within the square brackets above then becomes

\displaystyle-ie^{-2\pi\omega}\big[Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega+i/2)\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1},\theta_{1})r_{1}^{iK_{\mu\nu}-i\omega-2}e^{-(2i-\omega)\theta_{1}}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}^{*}(r_{2},\theta_{2})r_{2}^{-iK_{\mu\nu}+i\omega-1}e^{(i+\omega)\theta_{2}}

\displaystyle+Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega-i/2)\int dr_{1}dr_{2}\int d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1},\theta_{1})r_{1}^{iK_{\mu\nu}-i\omega-1}e^{-(i-\omega)\theta_{1}}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}^{*}(r_{2},\theta_{2})r_{2}^{-iK_{\mu\nu}+i\omega-2}e^{(2i+\omega)\theta_{2}}\big]

If we now again perform the substitutions
\omega\to\omega\pm i/2
in the first and second terms respectively, we get

\displaystyle\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}=\frac{n}{4(n-1)}\sum_{\mu\nu}e^{-\pi(K_{\mu}+K_{\nu})}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\,e^{-2\pi\omega}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)\frac{\sinh\pi\omega\coth\frac{\pi\omega}{n}}{\cosh\frac{2\pi\omega}{n}-\cos\frac{2\pi}{n}}

(83)

\displaystyle\times Q(K_{\mu\nu}-\omega)\int dr_{1}d\theta_{1}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}(r_{1}\theta_{1})r_{1}^{iz-3/2}e^{(-\frac{3i}{2}+\omega)\theta_{1}}\times\int dr_{2}d\theta_{2}\tilde{f}_{\mu\nu}^{*}(r_{2},\theta_{2})r_{2}^{-iz-3/2}e^{(\frac{3i}{2}+\omega)\theta_{2}}.

We note that the last two integrals are complex conjugates of each other, and the other terms are all positive for
n\geq 1
. Thus,

\ddot{Z}_{n}^{1,1}\geq 0,

(84)

proving the Renyi QNEC for free fermions for arbitrary
n\geq 1.
We note that the limit
n\to 1^{+}
correctly reproduces the answer in
Malik:2019dpg
.

5
Discussion

Sandwiched Renyi divergence is a new measure of distance between states in Hilbert spaces, which has the desirable properties of being positive and satisfying the data processing inequality.
Previous studies of SRD in the context of QFT and holography include
May:2018tir
;
Casini:2018cxg
;
Ugajin:2018rwd
;
Brehm:2020zri
;
Caginalp:2022uzd
. Motivated by the purely information theoretic formulation of QNEC, a Renyi QNEC was conjectured
Lashkari:2018nsl
, and was soon proven for free bosons
Moosa:2020jwt
. In this work, we have generalized the arguments of
Moosa:2020jwt
to the case of free fermions, and showed that the Renyi QNEC indeed holds for
n\geq 1.

Other related distance measures between quantum states are the Petz divergence
Petz:1985inf
;
Petz:1986fin
;
Lashkari:2018nsl
, the
\alpha
-
z
-Renyi relative entropy
Audenaert:2013ere
, optimized quantum
f
-divergences
Wilde:2017okz
;
Gao:2020gzh
and the refined Renyi divergence defined in
Bao:2019aol
. A visual summary of the interrelations between various entropy measures is found in
entropyzoo
. Another measure is the recently defined multi-state quantum
f
-divergence
Furuya:2021kqx
. A Renyi mutual information in QFT was defined very recently in
Kudler-Flam:2022zgm
. It is natural to expect that some of these measures also satisfy a QNEC like contraint on second null shape derivatives. This was already pointed out for some divergences in
Moosa:2020jwt
. It will be very interesting to see if the techniques used in this work can be used to check such conjectural inequalities, and we hope to report on this front in a future work. An important technical direction to explore is proving Renyi QNEC beyond the free regime using methods of algebraic QFT as in
Ceyhan:2018zfg
.

Inverting the question, it is as important to find and understand examples where such general inequalities fail to hold. The first example to demonstrate the violation of QNEC to the author’s best knowledge is
Ishibashi:2018ern
, which attributed the violation to IR effects. In
Ecker:2019ocp
, the authors tudied the evolution of QNEC after a quench in AdS
3
/CFT
2
.
Kibe:2021qjy
found very interestingly that QNEC can in fact be violated in these situations, and that non-violation of QNEC places bounds on the thermodynamics of the system post-quench. This was further studied in
Banerjee:2022dgv
in the context of inhomogeneous quenches. Performing similar calculations for Renyi QNEC in tractable setups should also lead to non-trivial constraints on the dynamics post-quench.

It seems important to the author to understand the holographic dual of the statement of Renyi QNEC, since it might lead to a generalization of the quantum focussing conjecture. This requires first elucidating the holographic dual of the sandwiched Renyi divergence. The holographic dual of the Renyi entropy was proposed in
Dong:2016fnf
and further studied in
Dong:2016wcf
;
Nakaguchi:2016zqi
;
Bianchi:2016xvf
;
Dong:2017xht
;
Akers:2018fow
, among others. In particular
Bao:2019aol
studied the holographic dual to the refined Renyi relative entropy. We hope that the techniques of these works might be further developed, and progress made towards a holographic statement and proof for Renyi QNEC.

Appendix A
Free fermion field

A Majorana fermion in two-dimensional Minkowski space is described by the action

S=k\int d^{2}x(-i)\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\chi,

(85)

where
\chi^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{1}&\chi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\{\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}\}=2\eta^{\mu\nu},\bar{\chi}=\chi^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}
, and
k
is some normalization factor. We choose

\gamma^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\phantom{i}1\\
1&\phantom{i}0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\gamma^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},

(86)

so that the Majorana condition on
\chi
implies that both
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}
are real. After rotating to Euclidean time,
t\to-i\tau
, writing
S_{E}=-iS
, and defining
z=x-i\tau,\bar{z}=x+i\tau
, we get

S_{E}=k\int d\tau dx(\psi\bar{\partial}\psi+\bar{\psi}\partial\bar{\psi}),

(87)

where we have defined
\psi=\sqrt{-i}\chi_{1},\bar{\psi}=\sqrt{i}\chi_{2}.
We focus only on the left-moving chiral field
\psi
throughout the work, which has the property that

\psi(r)^{\dagger}=i\psi(r).

(88)

Since
\psi
is a conformal primary with weight
(h,\bar{h})=(\frac{1}{2},0)
, we also have

\psi(re^{i\theta})=e^{i\theta/2}e^{\theta K_{P}}\psi(r)e^{-\theta K_{P}},

(89)

where
K_{P}
is the modular Hamiltonian generating
\theta
-rotations for the pencil system.

We choose the normalization factor
k
to be such that the two-point correlation function is given by

\langle\psi(z)\psi(w)\rangle=\frac{1}{z-w}\ .

(90)

Appendix B
Calculating correlation functions

We want to evaluate

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\mathcal{O}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\ddot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\rangle

(91)

\displaystyle\ \qquad+\left\langle(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(r_{1},\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\dot{\mathcal{O}}_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(r_{2},\theta_{2})\right\rangle.

Recalling (
48
) and that
\tilde{\rho}^{(0)}=\sigma_{P}\otimes\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)}
, this correlation function factorizes into separate correlation functions for the pencil and the auxiliary system,

\mathcal{G}(s)=\mathcal{G}_{P}(s)\cdot\mathcal{G}_{A}(s),

(92)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{P}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \left\langle\sigma_{P}^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\psi(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})\sigma_{P}^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\partial^{2}\psi(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})\right\rangle_{P}+\left\langle\sigma_{P}^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}\partial\psi(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}})\sigma_{P}^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}\partial\psi(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})\right\rangle_{P},

(93)

and

\mathcal{G}_{A}(s)=\left\langle(\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{-\frac{is}{2\pi}}E_{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}}(\theta_{1})(\tilde{\rho}_{A}^{(0)})^{\frac{is}{2\pi}}E_{\mu_{2}\nu_{2}}(\theta_{2})\right\rangle_{A}.

(94)

The auxiliary system correlation function can be calculated straightforwardly. For
\theta_{1}>\theta_{2},
we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{A}(s)=

\displaystyle\ e^{-2\pi K_{\mu_{1}}}e^{is(K_{\mu_{1}}-K_{\mu_{2}})}e^{\theta_{1}(K_{\mu_{1}}-K_{\nu_{1}})}e^{\theta_{2}(K_{\mu_{2}}-K_{\nu_{2}})}\text{Tr}\,_{a}\big[\left|\mu_{1}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu_{1}\right|\cdot\left|\mu_{2}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\nu_{2}\right|\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ e^{-2\pi K_{\mu_{1}}}e^{(is+\theta_{12})K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}\delta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\delta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{1}},

(95)

where, as above,
\theta_{ij}=\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}
, and also
K_{\mu_{i}\mu_{j}}=K_{\mu_{i}}-K_{\mu_{j}}
. Similarly, for
\theta_{2}>\theta_{1}
, we have

\mathcal{G}_{A}(s)=-e^{-2\pi K_{\mu_{2}}}e^{(is+\theta_{12})K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}\delta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\delta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{1}}.

(96)

where the minus sign appears due to angle-ordering the
E_{\mu\nu}
, which have fermionic statistics. We can combine the above two equations to write for all
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
,

\mathcal{G}_{A}(s)={\rm sign}(\theta_{12})e^{-\pi(K_{\mu_{1}}+K_{\mu_{2}})}e^{-{\rm sign}(\theta_{12})\pi K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}e^{(is+\theta_{12})K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}\delta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\delta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{1}}.

(97)

Let us now look at
\mathcal{G}_{P}(s)
. Using

\sigma_{P}^{-\alpha}\partial^{m}\psi(re^{i\theta})\sigma_{P}^{\alpha}=e^{i2\pi\alpha(m+\frac{1}{2})}\partial^{m}\psi(re^{i(\theta+2\pi\alpha)}),

(98)

and the fermion two point function (
90
), we get

\mathcal{G}_{P}(s)=\frac{2}{(r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}-s}-r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}})^{3}}(e^{-s/2}-e^{-3s/2}).

(99)

Multiplying
\mathcal{G}_{A}(s)
and
\mathcal{G}_{P}(s)
, we get

\mathcal{G}(s)=-{\rm sign}(\theta_{12})2e^{-\pi(K_{\mu_{1}}+K_{\mu_{2}})}e^{-{\rm sign}(\theta_{12})\pi K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}e^{\theta_{12}K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}\delta_{\mu_{1}\nu_{2}}\delta_{\mu_{2}\nu_{1}}\frac{(e^{-s/2}-e^{-3s/2})e^{isK_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}}{(r_{2}e^{i\theta_{2}}-r_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}-s})^{3}},

(100)

where we have collected the
s
dependent terms.

We now want to take a Fourier transform to calculate
G(\omega)
,

G(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ds\,e^{-is\omega}\mathcal{G}(s).

(101)

Note that
\mathcal{G}(s)\to 0
as
{\rm Re}(s)\to\pm\infty.
Also note that

\mathcal{G}(s+2\pi i)=-e^{-2\pi K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}}\mathcal{G}(s).

(102)

These allow us to write

G(\omega)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{1+e^{2\pi(\omega-K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}})}}\oint_{C}ds\,e^{-is\omega}\mathcal{G}(s),

(103)

where
C
is the closed contour

C:(-\infty,\infty)\cup(\infty,\infty+2\pi i)\cup(\infty+2\pi i,-\infty+2\pi i)\cup(-\infty+2\pi i,-\infty).

(104)

This contour integral can now be evaluated using the residue theorem, noting the fact that
\mathcal{G}(s)
has only one pole inside
C
, given by

s=s_{*}=\log\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}+i(\theta_{12}+\pi(1-{\rm sign}(\theta_{12}))),

(105)

where the extra terms take care of the branches when
\theta_{12}<0.
For
z\in\mathbb{C}
, one can calculate that

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left[\frac{e^{-3s/2}e^{isz}}{(1-re^{i\theta-s})^{3}},s=s_{*}\right]=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{8}{\rm sign}(\theta)e^{-\pi z(1-{\rm sign}(\theta))}(re^{i\theta})^{-\frac{3}{2}+iz}(1+4z^{2}),

(106)

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left[\frac{e^{-s/2}e^{isz}}{(1-re^{i\theta-s})^{3}},s=s_{*}\right]=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{8}{\rm sign}(\theta)e^{-\pi z(1-{\rm sign}(\theta))}(re^{i\theta})^{-\frac{1}{2}+iz}(-3-8iz+4z^{2}).

(107)

Putting everything together, and using
(1+e^{-2x})^{-1}=e^{x}\sech x/2,
we get the following. (Note that we write
z=K_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}-\omega

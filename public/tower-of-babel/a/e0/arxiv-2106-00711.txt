1.
Introduction

Given a positive Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
Tolsa
[
6
]
introduced
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
, the regular BMO space with respect to
\mu
.
This space is suitable for the non-doubling measures
\mu
and
it has genuine properties of the classical space BMO
such as the John–Nirenberg inequality.
Moreover, it is proved in
[
6
]
that a bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
Calderón–Zygmund operator
maps
L^{\infty}(\mu)
into
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.
Motivated by this result, we consider Calderón–Zygmund operators on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
:
we obtain a T1 condition sufficient for the boundedness of Calderón–Zygmund operators
on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
; see Section
1.4
for a precise formulation.

1.1.
Cubes and
n
-dimensional measures

In what follows, a cube is a closed cube in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
with sides parallel to the axes and centered at
a point of
\mathrm{supp\,}\mu
. For a cube
Q
, let
\ell=\ell(Q)
denote its side-length.
Also the notation
Q(x,\ell)
is used to indicate explicitly the center
x
of the cube under consideration.

As in
[
6
]
, we always assume that
\mu
is an
n
-dimensional measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
for a real number
n
,

0<n\leq m
.
By definition, it means that

(1.1)

\mu(Q)\leq C\ell^{n}(Q)\ \textrm{for any cube\ }Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},\ \ell(Q)>0,

with a universal constant
C>0
.

1.2.
Calderón–Zygmund operators

Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the standard distance between points of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
A Calderón–Zygmund kernel associated with an
n
-dimensional measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a measurable function

\mathcal{K}(x,y)
on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{(x,x):x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\}

satisfying the following conditions:

(1.2)

|\mathcal{K}(x,y)|\leq Cd^{-n}(x,y),

(1.3)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{K}(x_{1},y)-\mathcal{K}(x_{2},y)|+|\mathcal{K}(y,x_{1})

\displaystyle-\mathcal{K}(y,x_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{d^{\delta}(x_{1},x_{2})}{d^{n+\delta}(x_{1},y)},\quad 2d(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d(x_{1},y),

and

(1.4)

\left|\int_{Q(x,R)\setminus Q(x,r)}\mathcal{K}(x,y)\,d\mu(x)\right|\leq C,\quad 0<r<R,

where
C>0
is a universal constant and

\delta
,
0<\delta\leq 1
, is a regularity constant
specific to the kernel
\mathcal{K}
.

Remark 1
.

Restrictions (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are standard.
Condition (
1.4
) is a more special cancellation property.

The Calderón-Zygmund operator associated to the kernel
\mathcal{K}(x,y)
and the measure
\mu
is defined as

Tf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\mathcal{K}(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y)

for
x\notin\textrm{supp}(f\mu)
.
So, in the general setting, one introduces the following truncated operators
T_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
:

T_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus Q(x,\varepsilon)}\mathcal{K}(x,y)f(y)\,d\mu(y).

The operator
T
is said to be bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
if the operators
T_{\varepsilon}
are bounded on
L^{p}(\mu)
uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
.

1.3.
Regular BMO space

In this section, we give an equivalent definition of
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
, the regular BMO space
introduced by Tolsa
[
6
]
.

1.3.1.
Coefficients
K(Q,R)

Given two cubes
Q\subset R
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, put

K(Q,R)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{N_{Q,R}}\frac{\mu(2^{j}Q)}{\ell^{n}(2^{j}Q)},

where
N_{Q,R}
is the minimal integer
k
such that
\ell(2^{k}Q)\geq\ell(R)
.
Clearly,
K(Q,R)\geq 1
. On the other hand,
K(Q,R)
is bounded
above by
C\log(\ell(R)/\ell(Q))
because
\mu
is
n
-dimensional.

1.3.2.
Doubling cubes

Definition 1
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
\beta>\alpha^{n}
. A cube
Q
is called
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling if

\mu(\alpha Q)<\beta\mu(Q).

Let
\mu
be a Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\alpha>1
.
As indicated in
[
6
]
, it is known that
for a sufficiently large
\beta=\beta(\alpha,n)
, for
\mu
-almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
there is
a sequence of
(\alpha,\beta)
-doubling cubes
\{Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
centered at
x
and with
\ell(Q_{k})
tending
to
0
as
k\to\infty
.
Let
\beta_{n}
denote two times the infimum of the corresponding constants
\beta(4,n)
.

Definition 2
.

A cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called doubling if
Q
is
(4,\beta_{n})
-doubling.

Remark 2
.

The original definition of a doubling cube
and further results in
[
6
]
are given for
\alpha=2
and under assumption
1\leq n\leq m
.
Nevertheless, it is known that the results of Tolsa
[
6
]

are extendable to larger values of
\alpha
and for
0<n\leq m
;
see, for example,
[
4
]
for a generalization
of this theory for a wide class of measures on appropriate metric spaces.
So, in what follows, we use the above definition with

\alpha=4
and still refer to original results of Tolsa
[
6
]
.

1.3.3.
Definition of
\mathrm{RBMO}

Definition 3
.

The space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
consists of those
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
for which there exists a constant
C_{\mathfrak{E}}>0

and a collection of constants
\{f_{Q}\}
(one constant for each doubling cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
)
such that

(1.5)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|\,d\mu\leq C_{\mathfrak{E}}

and

(1.6)

|f_{Q}-f_{R}|\leq C_{\mathfrak{E}}K(Q,R)

for all doubling cubes
Q
,
R
,
Q\subset R
.
Let
\|f\|=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{E}}
denote the infimum of the corresponding constants
C_{\mathfrak{E}}>0
.

Standard arguments guarantee that
\|\cdot\|
is a norm on the space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
modulo constants.

1.4.
Main theorem

Suppose that a Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Then, as mentioned above,
T
maps
L^{\infty}(\mu)
boundedly into
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.
Moreover,
in the classical situation of homogeneous metric spaces and under additional assumption
T1=0
,
the operator
T
is known to be bounded on

\mathrm{BMO}
type spaces;
see, for example,
[
5
, Ch. 4, Sect. 4]
.
In the present paper, we obtain a
T1
condition sufficient for the boundedness of
T
on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.

Given a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, put

K(Q)=K(Q,2^{k}Q),

where
k
is the smallest positive integer such that
\mu(2^{k}Q)>\frac{1}{2}\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive
n
-dimensional measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
T
be a Calderón–Zygmund operator bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Assume that for each doubling cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there exists a constant
b_{Q}
such that

(1.7)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|T1-b_{Q}|\,d\mu\leq\frac{C}{K(Q)}\quad\textrm{for all doubling cubes}\ Q

and

(1.8)

|b_{Q}-b_{R}|\leq C\frac{K(Q,R)}{K(Q)}\quad\textrm{for all doubling cubes}\ Q,R,\ Q\subset R,

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
Q
and
R
.
Then
T
is bounded on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.

Remark 3
.

We say that
T
is bounded on

RBMO(\mu)
if the operators
T_{\varepsilon}
,
\varepsilon>0
, are uniformly bounded on
RBMO(\mu)
. Also, (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) similarly mean that these estimates hold for
T_{\varepsilon}
uniformly in
\varepsilon>0
.

Remark 4
.

We implicitly assume in Theorem
1.1
that
T1\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
Indeed, this property follows from (
1.4
); see Lemma
2.5
.
If
T1
is a constant, then Theorem
1.1
clearly guarantees that

T
is bounded on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.

Remark 5
.

Property (
1.7
) is, in a sense, similar to the oscillation condition used in
[
1
]
,
where a
T1
theorem for
\textrm{BMO}_{H}
in the Hermite-Calderón-Zygmund setting is obtained.

The final step in the proof of Theorem
1.1
uses
\|\cdot\|
only; however, the following
semi-norm on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
is crucial for certain auxiliary results.

Definition 4
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
. Fix a constant
\rho>1
.
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
denote the infimum of the constants
C_{\mathfrak{A}}=C_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}>0

with the following properties: for each cube
Q
, there exists
f_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{A}},

(1.10)

\displaystyle|f_{Q}-f_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{A}}K(Q,R)\quad\text{for any two cubes\ }Q\subset R.

Remark 6
.

The original semi-norm on
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
from
[
6
]
is different from those introduced
in Definitions
3
and
4
. Nevertheless, all these semi-norms are equivalent;
see Section
2
for further details.

1.5.
Notation

As usual, the symbol
C
denotes an absolute constant whose value can vary from line to line.
Notation
C_{\mathfrak{A}}
,
C_{\mathfrak{B}}
, etc. is used in certain specific situations.

1.6.
Organization of the paper

Auxiliary results are presented in Section
2
.
Section
3
is devoted to estimates related to the main technical decomposition of functions from
\mathrm{RBMO}
;
equivalence of Definitions
3
and
4
is essential on this step.
The proof of Theorem
1.1
is given in Section

.

2.
Auxiliary results

2.1.
Equivalent definitions of
\mathrm{RBMO}

As mentioned in the introduction, Definition
3
is not the original one for
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
in
[
6
]
.
In the present section, we show that Definition
3

and the definitions of the regular BMO from
[
6
]
are equivalent.

Firstly, recall several notions introduced by Tolsa
[
6
]
.
Let
\rho>1
and
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mu)
.
Given a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let
\left<f\right>_{Q}
denote the standard
\mu
-average of
f
over
Q
, that is,

\left<f\right>_{Q}=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}f\,d\mu.

\bullet
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}
denote the infimum of the constants

C_{\mathfrak{B}}=C_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}>0

with the following properties:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f-\langle f\rangle_{\widetilde{Q}}|d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{B}}\ \text{for any cube $Q$ (centered at some point of $\textrm{supp}(\mu)$)},

\displaystyle|\langle f\rangle_{Q}-\langle f\rangle_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{B}}K(Q,R)\ \text{for any two doubling cubes\ }Q\subset R,

where
\widetilde{Q}
denotes the smallest doubling cube in the sequence
Q,4Q,4^{2}Q,\dots
.

Remark 7
.

The original definition of
\widetilde{Q}
in
[
6
]
uses the sequence
Q,2Q,2^{2}Q,\dots
. The present definition
of
\widetilde{Q}
is based on
\alpha=4
; related details are
given in Definition
2
and Remark
2
.
See also
[
4
]
for similar definitions with
\alpha>1
.

\bullet
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{C},\rho}
denote the infimum of the constants
C_{\mathfrak{C}}=C_{\mathfrak{C},\rho}>0

with the following properties: for any cube Q

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|f-\langle f\rangle_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{C}}\mu(\rho Q),

\displaystyle|\langle f\rangle_{Q}-\langle f\rangle_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathfrak{C}}K(Q,R)\left(\frac{\mu(\rho Q)}{\mu(Q)}+\frac{\mu(\rho R)}{\mu(R)}\right)\quad\textrm{for any two cubes\ }Q\subset R.

\bullet
Let
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}
denote the infimum of the constants
C_{\mathfrak{D}}>0

with the following properties:

(2.1)

\int_{Q}|f-\langle f\rangle_{Q}|\,d\mu\leq C_{\mathfrak{D}}\mu(Q)\quad\textrm{for any doubling cube\ }Q

and

(2.2)

|\langle f\rangle_{Q}-\langle f\rangle_{R}|\leq C_{\mathfrak{D}}K(Q,R)\quad\textrm{for any two doubling cubes\ }Q\subset R.

The property
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}<\infty
is used to define the regular BMO space in
[
6
]
.
By
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
, the norms
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
are equivalent for different
\rho>1
;
by
[
6
, Lemma 2.8]
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
are equivalent.
By
[
6
, Lemma 2.10]
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{C},\rho}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{D}}
are equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}
.

Standard arguments show that Definition
3
and the property
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}<\infty
define the same space,
with equivalent norms.
Indeed, if
\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}<\infty
, then trivially
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
with
C_{\mathfrak{E}}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Now, assume that
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
. Property (
1.5
) guarantees that

|\left<f\right>_{Q}-f_{Q}|\leq C_{\mathfrak{E}}

for any doubling
Q
.
Hence, (
1.5
) implies (
2.1
) with
C_{\mathfrak{D}}=2C_{\mathfrak{E}}
;
(
1.6
) implies (
2.2
) with
C_{\mathfrak{D}}=3C_{\mathfrak{E}}
.
Therefore,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{D}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{E}}
are equivalent.

In the arguments related to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we will use
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
.
Thus, for further reference, we separately formulate a particular conclusion from the above arguments
as the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a constant
\rho>1
,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
is an equivalent norm on the space
\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
modulo constants.

2.2.
John–Nirenberg inequality for
\mathrm{RBMO}

Tolsa
[
6
]
proved the following version of the John–Nirenberg inequality for
\mathrm{RBMO}
.

Theorem 2.2

(see
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
,
\rho>1
, and let
\{b_{Q}\}_{Q}
be a collection of numbers
satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-b_{Q}|\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}

\displaystyle|b_{Q}-b_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,R)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}

for any two cubes
Q\subset R
,
with an absolute constant
C>0
.
Then for any cube Q and any
\lambda>0
, we have

\mu\{x\in Q:|f(x)-b_{Q}|>\lambda\}\leq C_{J\!N}\mu(\rho Q)\exp\left(\frac{-c_{J\!N}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B},\rho}}\right)

with
C_{J\!N},c_{J\!N}>0
depending on
\rho
.

We will need the following corollary related to Definition
4
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
,
\rho>1
.
Let
\{f_{Q}\}_{Q}
be such numbers that (
1.9
) and (
1.10
)
hold with
C_{\mathfrak{A}}=2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
.
Then

\left(\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\|f\|

for any cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
C=C(C_{J\!N},c_{J\!N},p,\rho)>0
.

Proof.

A standard argument is applicable.
Indeed, by Theorem
2.2
and Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(\rho Q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\mu\{x:|f(x)-f_{Q}|>\lambda\}\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq C_{J\!N}\int_{0}^{\infty}p\lambda^{p-1}\exp\left(\frac{-c_{J\!N}\lambda}{\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}}\right)\,d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|^{p},

as required.
∎

2.3.
Properties of
\mathrm{RBMO}

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu
be a finite
n
-dimensional measure,
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
,
\rho>1
, and let
\{f_{Q}\}_{Q}

be numbers such that (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) hold with
C_{\mathfrak{A}}=2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}<\infty
. Then

|f_{Q}|\leq C_{f}K(Q)\quad\textrm{for all cubes}\ Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m},

where the number
C_{f}>0
does not depend on
Q
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})=1
.
Fix a cube
Q_{1}
such that
\ell(Q_{1})\geq 1
and
\mu(Q_{1})>0
. Put

C_{1}(f)=|f_{Q_{1}}|.

Firstly, let
Q_{2}
be a cube such that
\mu(Q_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
Select a cube
Q_{3}
such that
Q_{3}\supset Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}
.
By the choice of
f_{Q_{j}}
,
j=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle|f_{Q_{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{Q_{2}}-f_{Q_{3}}|+|f_{Q_{3}}-f_{Q_{1}}|+|f_{Q_{1}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2K(Q_{2},Q_{3})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}+2K(Q_{1},Q_{3})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}+C_{1}(f).

Since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional, we have
\ell(Q_{2})\geq\varkappa>0
.
Hence,

K(Q_{m},Q_{3})\leq 1+\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\mu(2^{j}Q_{m})}{\ell^{n}(2^{j}Q_{m})}\leq 1+\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\ell^{n}(2^{j}Q_{m})}\leq C

for
m=1,2
.
Therefore,

|f_{Q_{2}}|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}+C_{1}(f):=C_{0}(f).

Now, consider a cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\mu(Q)<\frac{1}{2}
.
Let
k
be the smallest positive integer such that
\mu(2^{k}Q)\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Since the cube
2^{k}Q
has the properties of
Q_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|f_{Q}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{Q}-f_{2^{k}Q}|+|f_{2^{k}Q}|

\displaystyle\leq 2K(Q,2^{k}Q)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}+C_{0}(f)

\displaystyle\leq C_{f}K(Q),

as required.
∎

2.4.
Estimates of
T1

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mu
be a finite positive measure on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Let
T
be a Calderón–Zygmund operator.
Then
T1\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

Proof.

Since
\mu
is a finite measure, we have

\displaystyle|T_{\varepsilon}1(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus Q(x,2)}\left|\mathcal{K}(x,y)\right|\,d\mu(y)+\left|\int_{Q(x,2)\setminus Q(x,\varepsilon)}\mathcal{K}(x,y)\,d\mu(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\mu(\mathbb{R}^{m})+C\leq C

by (
1.2
) and (
1.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
(ii) hold.
Then

(2.3)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|T1-\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu\leq\frac{C}{K(Q)}\quad\textrm{for any doubling cube\ }Q

and

(2.4)

|\left<T1\right>_{Q}-\left<T1\right>_{R}|\leq C\frac{K(Q,R)}{K(R)}\quad\textrm{for any two doubling cubes\ }Q\subset R,

where the constant
C>0
does not depend on
Q
and
R
.

Proof.

Put
h=T1
.
Firstly, let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a doubling cube.
The following standard arguments guarantee that (
1.7
) implies (
2.3
):

\displaystyle\int_{Q}|h-\left<h\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Q}|h-b_{Q}|\,d\mu+\int_{Q}\left|b_{Q}-\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}h\,d\mu\right|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\mu(Q)}{K(Q)}+\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}|h-b_{Q}|\,d\mu\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\mu(Q)}{K(Q)}.

Secondly, for any doubling cube
R\supset Q
, we have

|b_{R}-\left<h\right>_{R}|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(R)}\int_{R}|h-b_{R}|\,d\mu\leq\frac{C}{K(R)}\leq\frac{CK(Q,R)}{K(R)}

by (
1.7
). Clearly,
K(R)\leq CK(Q)
for
Q\subset R
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle|\left<h\right>_{Q}-\left<h\right>_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq|\left<h\right>_{Q}-b_{Q}|+|b_{Q}-b_{R}|+|b_{R}-\left<h\right>_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CK(Q,R)}{K(R)}

by (
1.8
). The proof of the lemma is finished.
∎

3.
Main construction

Let
T
be a Calderón-Zygmund operator bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
.
Let
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
,
\rho=2
.
Using Definition
4
, for each cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, select a number
f_{2Q}
such that (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)
hold with
C_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}=2\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},\rho}
and with
2Q
in the place of
Q
.
In particular, the assumptions of Lemma
2.3
are satisfied.
Also, to explain further arguments and estimates,
it is worth mentioning that
2Q
is not necessarily doubling even if
Q
is a doubling cube.

In the present section, we give estimates related to the following functions:

\displaystyle f_{1}=f_{1,Q}

\displaystyle=f_{2Q},

\displaystyle f_{2}=f_{2,Q}

\displaystyle=(f-f_{2Q})\chi_{2Q},

\displaystyle f_{3}=f_{3,Q}

\displaystyle=(f-f_{2Q})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q}.

Observe that

f=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}.

This decomposition ascends to
[
3
]
;
see also
[
2
]
.

Let
b_{2,Q}=0
and

b_{3,Q}=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}Tf_{3,Q}(y)\,d\mu(y).

In the following lemma, we assume that
Q
is a doubling cube.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{k}-b_{k,Q}|\,d\mu\leq C\|f\|,\quad k=2,3,

for any doubling cube
Q
.

Proof.

Put

I_{k}=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{k}-b_{k,Q}|\,d\mu,\quad k=2,3.

For
k=2
, we have

I_{2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{2}|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

by Hölder’s inequality.
Since
T
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mu)
, the definition of
f_{2}

guarantees that

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{2Q}|f-f_{2Q}|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Using the doubling property of
Q
and Lemma
2.3
with
\rho=p=2
and
2Q
in the place of
Q
,
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\mu(4Q)}{\mu(Q)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\mu(4Q)}\int_{2Q}|f-f_{2Q}|^{2}\,d\mu\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|,

as required for
k=2
.

For
k=3
, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\left|\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}(Tf_{3}(x)-Tf_{3}(y))\,d\mu(y)\right|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{Q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q}|\mathcal{K}(x,u)-\mathcal{K}(y,u)||f(u)-f_{2Q}|\,d\mu(u)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(x).

For
x,y\in Q
and
u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q
, the defining property of
\mathcal{K}(\cdot,\cdot)
guarantees that

|\mathcal{K}(x,u)-\mathcal{K}(y,u)|\leq C\frac{d^{\delta}(x,y)}{d^{n+\delta}(x,u)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q}\frac{\ell^{\delta}}{d^{n+\delta}(x,u)}|f(u)-f_{2Q}|\,d\mu(u)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}}{d^{n+\delta}(x,u)}\int_{2^{k+1}Q\setminus 2^{k}Q}|f(u)-f_{2Q}|\,d\mu(u)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}}{(2^{k-1}\ell)^{n+\delta}}\int_{2^{k+1}Q\setminus 2^{k}Q}|f(u)-f_{2Q}|\,d\mu(u)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}}{(2^{k}\ell)^{n+\delta}}\left(\int_{2^{k+1}Q}|f(u)-f_{2^{k+1}Q}|\,d\mu(u)+\mu(2^{k+1}Q)|f_{2Q}-f_{2^{k+1}Q}|\right)

by the triangle inequality.
The choice of the constants
f_{2Q}
and
f_{2^{k+1}Q}
guarantees that

I_{3}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}}{(2^{k}\ell)^{n+\delta}}\left(\mu(2^{k+2}Q)+\mu(2^{k+1}Q)K(2Q,2^{k+1}Q)\right)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}.

Since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional, we have

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}(2^{k+2}\ell)^{n}}{(2^{k}\ell)^{n+\delta}}K(2Q,2^{k+1}Q)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{K(2Q,2^{k+1}Q)}{2^{k\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{-k\delta}\sum_{j=2}^{k+1}\frac{\mu(2^{j}Q)}{(\ell 2^{j})^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|

by Lemma
2.1
.
The proof of the lemma is finished.
∎

In the following lemma, the cubes under consideration are not assumed to be doubling.
However, in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we apply this lemma in the doubling setting.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

|b_{k,Q}-b_{k,R}|\leq C\|f\|K(Q,R),\quad k=2,3.

for any two
cubes
Q\subset R
.

Proof.

For
k=2
, the required estimate is trivial; so assume that
k=3
.
Given cubes
Q\subset R
, we have

|b_{3,Q}-b_{3,R}|\leq|b_{3,Q}-b_{3,Q_{0}}|+|b_{3,Q_{0}}-b_{3,R}|=I+J,

where
Q_{0}=2^{k}Q
and
k
is the minimal integer such that
2^{k}Q\supset R
.

Observe that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}Tf_{3,Q}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(z)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}Tf_{3,Q_{0}}(z)\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\int_{Q}\int_{Q_{0}}Tf_{3}(y)-Tf_{3}(z)\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(y)\right|.

By the definitions of
f_{3,Q}
and
f_{3,Q_{0}}
,

\displaystyle Tf_{3,Q}(y)

\displaystyle-Tf_{3,Q_{0}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q}K(y,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q})d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(z,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q_{0}})d\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{2Q_{0}\setminus 2Q}K(y,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q})\,d\mu+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(y,\cdot)(f_{2Q_{0}}-f_{2Q})\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}(K(y,\cdot)-K(z,\cdot))(f-f_{2Q_{0}})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=D+E+F.

We split
D
into dyadic sums as follows:

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}K(y,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}K(y,\cdot)(f-f_{2^{j+1}Q})\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(f_{2^{j+1}Q}-f_{2Q})\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}K(y,\cdot)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=D_{1}+D_{2}.

To estimate
D_{1}
, we apply (
1.2
) and obtain

|K(y,x)|\leq\frac{C}{d^{n}(x,y)}\leq C\frac{1}{(\ell(Q)2^{j+2})^{n}}\quad\textrm{for}\ y\in Q,\ x\in 2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q.

Hence,

(3.1)

\displaystyle|D_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\mu(2^{j+2}Q)}{(\ell(Q)2^{j+2})^{n}}\frac{1}{\mu(2^{j+2}Q)}\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2^{j}Q}|f-f_{2^{j+1}Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|

by Lemma
2.1
.
Below we repeatedly use the equivalence of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}
and
\|\cdot\|
without
explicit reference to Lemma
2.1
.

Next, applying summation by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{2}=(f_{2^{k+1}Q}-f_{2Q})

\displaystyle\int_{2^{k+1}Q\setminus 2Q}K(y,\cdot)\,d\mu

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}(f_{2^{j+2}Q}-f_{2^{j+1}Q})\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2Q}K(y,\cdot)\,d\mu.

By the cancellation property (
1.4
),

\left|\int_{2^{j+1}Q\setminus 2Q}K(y,\cdot)\,d\mu\right|\leq C,\quad j=1,2,\dots,k.

Thus, the choice of the numbers
f_{2^{j+1}Q}
,
j=0,1,\dots,k
, guarantees that

(3.2)

\displaystyle|D_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq CK(2Q,2Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}+C\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}K(2^{j+1}Q,2^{j+2}Q)\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}+CK(2Q,2Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|.

To estimate
E
, we also use the cancellation property (
1.4
) and
we obtain

(3.3)

\displaystyle|E|

\displaystyle=\left|(f_{2Q}-f_{2Q_{0}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(y,\cdot)\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|f_{2Q}-f_{2Q_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq CK(Q,Q_{0})\|f\|.

Now, consider
F
. We have

|K(y,x)-K(z,x)|\leq C\frac{\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})}{d^{n+\delta}(x,Q_{0})}

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|F|

\displaystyle\leq C\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}\frac{|f-f_{2Q_{0}}|}{d^{n+\delta}(x,Q_{0})}\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})}{(2^{j+2}\ell(Q_{0}))^{n+\delta}}\int_{2^{j+1}Q_{0}\setminus 2^{j}Q_{0}}|f-f_{2Q_{0}}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})}{(2^{j+2}\ell(Q_{0}))^{n+\delta}}\left(\int_{2^{j+1}Q_{0}\setminus 2^{j}Q_{0}}|f-f_{2^{j+1}Q_{0}}|\,d\mu\right.

\displaystyle\left.+|f_{2^{j+1}Q_{0}}-f_{2Q_{0}}|\int_{2^{j+1}Q_{0}\setminus 2^{j}Q_{0}}\,d\mu\right)=F_{1}+F_{2}.

Firstly,

(3.4)

\displaystyle F_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})\mu(2^{j+2}Q_{0})}{(2^{j+2}\ell(Q_{0}))^{n+\delta}}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}}{(2^{j})^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|.

Secondly,

\displaystyle F_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0})\mu(2^{j+2}Q_{0})}{(2^{j+2}\ell(Q_{0}))^{n+\delta}}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}K(2Q_{0},2^{j}Q_{0})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}K(2Q_{0},2^{j}Q_{0})}{(2^{j})^{\delta}}.

Since
\mu
is
n
-dimensional, we have
K(2Q_{0},2^{j}Q_{0})\leq Cj
with a universal constant
C>0
. Thus,

(3.5)

F_{2}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{j}{(2^{j})^{\delta}}\leq C\|f\|.

Combining (
3.1
–
3.5
) and integrating with respect to
z
and
y
,
we obtain the required estimate for
I=|b_{3,Q}-b_{3,Q_{0}}|
.
Therefore, it remains to estimate
J
.

We have

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=|b_{3,Q_{0}}-b_{3,R}|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\mu(R)}\int_{R}\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}Tf_{3,Q_{0}}(y)\,d\mu(y)\,d\mu(w)\right.

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\int_{Q_{0}}\frac{1}{\mu(R)}\int_{R}Tf_{3,R}(w)\,d\mu(w)\,d\mu(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{\mu(Q_{0})}\frac{1}{\mu(R)}\int_{Q_{0}}\int_{R}Tf_{3,Q_{0}}(y)-Tf_{3,R}(w)\,d\mu(w)\,d\mu(y)\right|.

By the definitions of
f_{3,Q_{0}}
and
f_{3,R}
,

\displaystyle Tf_{3,Q_{0}}(z)

\displaystyle-Tf_{3,R}(w)\!=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(z,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q_{0}})d\mu-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2R}K(w,\cdot)(f-f_{2R})d\mu

\displaystyle=-\int_{2Q_{0}\setminus 2R}K(w,\cdot)(f-f_{2R})\,d\mu+(f_{2Q_{0}}-f_{2R})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(w,\cdot)\,d\mu

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}(K(z,\cdot)-K(w,\cdot))(f-f_{2Q_{0}})\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}+\mathcal{E}+\mathcal{F}.

Firstly,
\mathcal{D}
is estimated similarly to
D
and even simpler:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=-\int_{2Q_{0}\setminus 2R}K(w,\cdot)(f-f_{2Q_{0}})\,d\mu+(f_{2R}-f_{2Q_{0}})\int_{2Q_{0}\setminus 2R}K(w,\cdot)\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{1}+\mathcal{D}_{2}.

Since
\ell(R)
and
\ell(Q_{0})
are comparable and
\mu
is
n
-dimensional, we have

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2Q_{0}\setminus 2R}\frac{C}{d^{n}(R,2Q_{0}\setminus 2R)}|f-f_{2Q_{0}}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\ell^{n}(4R)}\frac{\mu(4Q_{0})}{\mu(4Q_{0})}\int_{2Q_{0}}|f-f_{2Q_{0}}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{\mu(4Q_{0})}{\ell^{n}(4Q_{0})}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|.

Using again that

\ell(R)
and
\ell(Q_{0})
are comparable and
\mu
is
n
-dimensional,
we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{D}_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{2Q_{0}}-f_{2R}|\frac{\mu(2Q_{0})}{d^{n}(R,2Q_{0}\setminus 2R)}

\displaystyle\leq C|f_{2Q_{0}}-f_{2R}|\frac{\mu(2Q_{0})}{\ell^{n}(R)}

\displaystyle\leq C|f_{2Q_{0}}-f_{2R}|

\displaystyle\leq CK(2R,2Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|.

To estimate
\mathcal{E}
, we use the cancellation property (
1.4
)
and obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}|

\displaystyle=\left|(f_{2R}-f_{2Q_{0}})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}K(w,\cdot)\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq C|f_{2R}-f_{2Q_{0}}|

\displaystyle\leq CK(2R,2Q_{0})\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|.

Now, we estimate
\mathcal{F}
. Property (
1.3
) guarantees that

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}((K(z,\cdot)-K(w,\cdot))(f-f_{2Q_{0}})\,d\mu\right|

\displaystyle\leq Cd^{\delta}(z,w)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus 2Q_{0}}\frac{|f(x)-f_{2Q_{0}}|}{d^{n+\delta}(x,Q_{0})}\,d\mu(x).

We have

d^{\delta}(z,w)\leq\ell^{\delta}(Q_{0}).

Hence, using dyadic decompositions and summation by parts,
we repeat the arguments applied to estimate
|F|
and we
obtain the following analog of (
3.4
)
and (
3.5
):

(3.9)

|\mathcal{F}|\leq C\|f\|.

Now, combining estimates (
3.6
–
3.9
), we conclude that

J=|b_{3,Q}-b_{3,R}|\leq CK(Q,R)\|f\|

for any two cubes
Q\subset R
.
The proof of Lemma
3.2
is finished.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Given an
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
, we have to prove that
Tf\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
.
Put
g=Tf
. In this section, for every doubling cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
we find a constant
g_{Q}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(4.1)

\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|g-g_{Q}|\,d\mu\leq C

and

(4.2)

|g_{Q}-g_{R}|\leq CK(Q,R)\quad\textrm{for any two doubling cubes\ }Q\subset R,

where
C=C_{f}>0
.

So, given an
f\in\mathrm{RBMO}(\mu)
and a doubling cube
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we apply the construction
described in Section
3
and we obtain

f=f_{1,Q}+f_{2,Q}+f_{3,Q}=f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}.

Also, we have constants
b_{2,Q}
and
b_{3,Q}
.
By Lemma
2.6
, properties (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) hold for the constants
\left<T1\right>_{Q}
and
\left<T1\right>_{R}
.
Put

g_{Q}=f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}+b_{2,Q}+b_{3,Q}.

4.1.
Oscillation condition (
4.1
)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|g-g_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\left(\int_{Q}|Tf_{1}-f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu+\int_{Q}|Tf_{2}-b_{2,Q}|\,d\mu+\int_{Q}|Tf_{3}-b_{3,Q}|\,d\mu\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{1}-f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu+C\|f\|

by Lemma
3.1
. Recall that
Tf_{1}=Tf_{2Q}=f_{2Q}T1
.
Therefore,
applying (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|Tf_{1}-f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{|f_{2Q}|}{\mu(Q)}\int_{Q}|T1-\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{f}K(2Q)}{K(Q)}\leq C_{f}.

Hence, the oscillation condition (
4.1
) is proved.

4.2.
K
-condition (
4.2
)

Let
Q\subset R
be doubling cubes.
Combining the triangle inequality and Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle|g_{Q}-g_{R}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}-f_{2R}\left<T1\right>_{R}|+|b_{3,Q}-b_{3,R}|

\displaystyle\leq|f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}-f_{2R}\left<T1\right>_{R}|+C\|f\|K(Q,R).

Next, the choice of the constants
f_{2Q}
and
f_{2R}
guarantees that

|f_{2Q}-f_{2R}|\leq C\|f\|_{\mathfrak{A},2}K(2Q,2R)\leq C\|f\|K(Q,R).

Also, we have
T1\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
by Lemma
2.5
, hence
|\left<T1\right>_{Q}|\leq C
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|f_{2Q}\left<T1\right>_{Q}-f_{2R}\left<T1\right>_{2R}|

\displaystyle\leq|\left<T1\right>_{Q}||f_{2Q}-f_{2R}|+|f_{2R}|\left|\left<T1\right>_{Q}-\left<T1\right>_{R}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f\|K(Q,R)+|f_{2R}|\left|\left<T1\right>_{Q}-\left<T1\right>_{R}\right|.

Applying Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

|f_{2R}|\left|\left<T1\right>_{Q}-\left<T1\right>_{R}\right|\leq C_{f}K(2R)\frac{K(Q,R)}{K(R)}\leq C_{f}K(Q,R).

Combining the above estimates, we conclude that (
4.2
) holds.
This ends the proof of the theorem.

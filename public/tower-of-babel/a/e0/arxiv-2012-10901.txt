Two remarks on the Poincaré metric on a singular Riemann surface foliation
Sahil Gehlawat and Kaushal Verma

Abstract
. Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a smooth Riemann surface foliation on M ∖ E M\setminus E , where M M is a complex manifold and E ⊂ M E\subset M is a closed set. Fix a hermitian metric g g on M ∖ E M\setminus E and assume that all leaves of ℱ \mathcal{F} are hyperbolic. For each leaf L ⊂ ℱ L\subset\mathcal{F} , the ratio of g | L g|L , the restriction of g g to L L , and the Poincaré metric λ L \lambda_{L} on L L defines a positive function η \eta that is known to be continuous on M ∖ E M\setminus E under suitable conditions on M , E M,E . For a domain U ⊂ M U\subset M , we consider ℱ U \mathcal{F}_{U} , the restriction of ℱ \mathcal{F} to U U and the corresponding positive function η U \eta_{U} by considering the ratio of g g and the Poincaré metric on the leaves of ℱ U \mathcal{F}_{U} . First, we study the variation of η U \eta_{U} as U U varies in the Hausdorff sense motivated by the work of Lins Neto–Martins. Secondly, Minda had shown the existence of a domain Bloch constant for a hyperbolic Riemann surface S S , which in other words shows that every holomorphic map from the unit disc into S S , whose distortion at the origin is bounded below, must be locally injective in some hyperbolic ball of uniform radius. We show how to deduce a version of this Bloch constant for ℱ \mathcal{F} .

1.
Introduction

To provide a context for this note, let
M
be a complex manifold of dimension
n
,
E\subset M
a closed subset and
\mathcal{F}
a smooth lamination of
M\setminus E
by Riemann surfaces. Recall that in this case,
\mathcal{F}
is defined by an atlas of flow-boxes
(U_{\alpha},\phi_{\alpha})
where
\phi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
(
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the open unit disc) and the transition maps

\phi_{\alpha\beta}
are smooth in
(x,y)\in\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
and are of the form

\phi_{\alpha\beta}=\phi_{\alpha\beta}(x,y)=\phi_{\alpha}\circ\phi^{-1}_{\beta}(x,y)=(A(x,y),B(y))

with
A
holomorphic in
x
. The leaves of
\mathcal{F}
, which are locally given by
\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}(\{y=\mbox{constant}\})
, are Riemann surfaces and the one passing through a given
p\in M\setminus E
will be denoted by
L_{p}
. We will assume that each leaf is hyperbolic and refer to
(M,E,\mathcal{F})
as a hyperbolic Riemann surface lamination.
Fix a hermitian metric
g
on
M\setminus E
and denote the length of a tangent vector
v
by
|v|_{g}
. Consider the space
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
of all holomorphic maps
f
from the unit disc
\mathbb{D}\subset\mathbb{C}
with values in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
, i.e.,
f(\mathbb{D})\subset L
for some leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
. Pick
f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
and let
f(0)=z
so that
f(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{z}
. Let
\pi_{z}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{z}
be the universal covering map chosen such that
\pi_{z}(0)=z
. Then
f
admits a unique lift
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
that fixes the origin. The Schwarz lemma implies that
|\tilde{f}^{\prime}(0)|\leq 1
and hence
|f^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq|\pi^{\prime}_{z}(0)|_{g}
. Therefore,
\eta:M\setminus E\rightarrow(0,\infty)
given by

\eta(z)=\sup\left\{|f^{\prime}(0)|_{g}:f\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F}),f(0)=z\right\}

is well-defined. Note that
\eta>0
everywhere by considering the holomorphic map that sends
\mathbb{D}
into a local leaf
\phi^{-1}_{\alpha}(\{y=\mbox{constant}\})
, and that its extremal value is achieved by
\pi_{z}
, which evidently belongs to
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
. Thus,
\eta(z)=|\pi^{\prime}_{z}(0)|_{g}
. Let
\lambda_{L}
denote the Poincaré metric on a leaf
L\subset\mathcal{L}
and for a vector
v
that is tangent to
L
at
z
, let
|v|_{L}
denote its Poincaré length. The extremal property of the Kobayashi (which equals the Poincaré) metric shows that
|v|_{g}=\eta(z)|v|_{L}
. In other words, the restriction of
g
to a leaf
L
differs from
\lambda_{L}
by a multiplicative constant which is captured by the positive function
\eta
. This property will be written symbolically as
g=\eta\cdot\lambda_{L}
and can be understood to mean that the restriction of
g/\eta
to a leaf
L
induces the Poincaré metric
\lambda_{L}
on it.

Now, for a fixed leaf
L
,
\lambda_{L}
is a positive real-analytic function on it while
g
is smooth, and hence
\eta
is also smooth along leaves. However, the regularity of
\eta
along transverse directions is not a priori clear. In fact, it is related to the variation of
\lambda_{L}
along such directions. This question has been studied under various hypotheses on
M,E
and

\mathcal{F}
, and we refer the reader to Verjovsky
[
14
]
, Lins Neto
[
11
,
12
]
, Candel
[
4
]
in which the continuity of
\lambda_{L}
on
M\setminus E
is established. In addition, recent work of Dinh–Nguyên–Sibony
[
6
]
shows that
\lambda_{L}
is in fact Hölder continuous when
M
is compact and
E=\emptyset
; a stronger estimate on the modulus of continuity
of
\lambda_{L}
can be found in
[
7
]
under suitable hypotheses on
M,E
and
\mathcal{F}
. Related work on this theme can be found in
[
3
]
,
[
5
]
, and
[
8
]
.

A different continuity property of
\eta
was proved in
[
13
]
. To describe it, let
\mathcal{F}_{U}
denote the restriction of
\mathcal{F}
to a domain
U\subset M
. For
p\in M\setminus E
, let
L_{p,U}
denote the connected component of
L_{p}\cap U
that contains
p
. If it is non-empty,
L_{p,U}
is then the leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
that contains
p
. Let
\eta_{U}:U\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be the positive function corresponding to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
. Note that
\eta_{U}
is defined using holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{D}
into
L_{p,U}
as
p
varies in
U
. The following facts were established in
[
13
]
. First, that
\eta_{U}
is monotone as a function of
U
, i.e., for domains
U\subset V
,
\eta_{U}(p)\leq\eta_{V}(p)
for all
p\in U
. Second, if
\{U_{n}\}
is an increasing exhaustion of
U
, then
\eta_{U_{n}}\rightarrow\eta_{U}
pointwise on
U\setminus E
, and uniformly on compact subsets of
U\setminus E
if the functions
\eta_{U_{n}}
and

\eta_{U}
are known to be continuous.

To strengthen this, let
d
be the distance on
M
induced by
g
, and for

S\subset M
and
\epsilon>0
, let
S_{\epsilon}
be the
\epsilon
-thickening of
S
with distances being measured using
d
. Recall that the Hausdorff distance
\mathcal{H}(A,B)
between compact sets
A,B\subset M
is the infimum of all
\epsilon>0
such that
A\subset B_{\epsilon}
and
B\subset A_{\epsilon}
. For bounded domains
U,V\subset M
, the prescription
\rho(U,V)=\mathcal{H}(\overline{U},\overline{V})+\mathcal{H}(\partial U,\partial V)
defines a metric (see
[
1
]
) on the collection of all bounded open subsets of
X
with the property that if
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
, then every compact subset of
U
is eventually contained in
U_{n}
, and every neighbourhood of
\overline{U}
contains all the
U_{n}
’s eventually.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,E,\mathcal{F})
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface lamination as above.

(i)

Suppose that
U\subset M
is a bounded taut domain and
\{U_{n}\}
is a sequence of bounded domains in
M
such that
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
. Then,
\eta_{U_{n}}
converges to
\eta_{U}
pointwise on
U\setminus E
.

(ii)

In addition, assume that
E
is discrete,
\eta_{U}
is continuous, and there is a taut domain
V
containing
\overline{U}
. Then,
\eta_{U_{n}}
converges to
\eta_{U}
uniformly on compact subsets of
U\setminus E
.

Since the functions
\eta_{U},\eta_{U_{n}}
are a measure of the uniformizations of the leaves, the crux of the proof of these statements is to study the compactness of a family of uniformizations. In (i), which is a pointwise statement, the leaf
L_{p}
does not change but only the open connected subsets
L_{p,U}
are allowed to vary. On the other hand, the situation considered in (ii) allows even the leaves to vary.

The other notion that we consider is motivated by Minda’s definition of a domain Bloch constant – see
[
9
]
. To recall this briefly, let
S
be a hyperbolic Riemann surface and
\lambda_{S}(z)|dz|
the Poincaré metric on it. For
1\leq m\leq\infty
, consider the family
\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},S)
As examples,
\mathcal{O}_{1}(\mathbb{D},S)
is precisely the family of all non-constant holomorphic maps, while
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}(\mathbb{D},S)
is to be understood as the family of locally injective holomorphic maps
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow S
, for roots with infinite multiplicity can only exist for constant maps.

Let
d_{h}(z,w)
denote the distance between
z,w\in\mathbb{D}
in the Poincaré metric and let
B_{h}(z,\delta)\subset\mathbb{D}
be the open ball of radius
\delta>0
centered at
z
with respect to
d_{h}
. Now given
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},S)
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, let

t(z,f)=\sup\{r\in(0,+\infty):f\;\mbox{is locally injective in}\;B_{h}(z,r)\}

where we set
t(z,f)=0
if
f
is not locally injective in any neighbourhood of
z
. The parameter

t(f)=\sup\{t(z,f):z\in\mathbb{D}\}

is then a measure of the largest hyperbolic ball in
\mathbb{D}
on which
f
is locally injective. The
distortion function
defined by

f^{S}_{\mathbb{D}}(p)=\left.\frac{f^{\ast}(\lambda_{S}(z)|dz|)}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)|dz|}\right|_{z=p}

measures the local distortion induced by
f
at
p
measured with respect to the Poincaré metrics on
S
and
\mathbb{D}
. It is known (see
[
10
]
) that the distortion is at most
1
and equality holds precisely when
f:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow S
is a holomorphic covering. For
\alpha\in(0,1]
,

(1.1)

\mathcal{T}_{m,S}(\alpha)=\inf\{t(f):f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},S)\;\mbox{and}\;f^{S}(0)\geq\alpha\}

is called the
domain Bloch constant
, which if positive, would show that every
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},S)
with distortion at least
\alpha
at the origin is locally injective in
some
hyperbolic ball in
\mathbb{D}
of uniform radius. Note that
\mathcal{T}_{m,S}
is an increasing function of both
m
and
\alpha
and
\mathcal{T}_{\infty,S}(\alpha)=+\infty
for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
. Minda
[
9
]
showed that for
1\leq m<\infty
,

(1.2)

\mathcal{T}_{m,S}(\alpha)\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}(\alpha^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}(\alpha^{1/(m+1)})}\right)

where

\tau_{m}(\alpha)=\alpha\left(\frac{m(m+2)}{2(m+1)^{2}-(m^{2}+2m+2)\alpha^{2}+2(m+1)\left(\alpha^{4}-(m^{2}+2m+2)\alpha^{2}+(m+1)^{2}\right)^{1/2}}\right)^{1/2}

and as can be checked, is an increasing function from the closed interval
[0,1]
to itself that fixes both
0
and
1
.

Since these considerations show the existence of a domain Bloch constant for holomorphic maps into a fixed hyperbolic Riemann surface, it seems natural to ask whether there is a foliated version of this notion. It turns out that there is. To describe it, let
(M,E,\mathcal{F})
be a Riemann surface lamination and recall the notion of an
ultrahyperbolic metric on
\mathcal{F}
of curvature bounded by
-a<0
for some
a>0
. Following
[
13
]
,
[
12
]
, a continuous hermitian metric
g
on
M\setminus E
is such a metric if it satisfies the following conditions:

(i) For a leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
and a local holomorphic coordinate
(z,U)
on it,
g|L
, the restriction of
g
to
L
is of the form
g|L=f(z)|dz|^{2}
in
U
, where
f
is continuous and non-negative on
U
and
f^{-1}(0)
is discrete in
U
, and

(ii) For every
z\in U
for which
f(z)\not=0
, there is a positive
C^{2}
-function
h
near
z
such that
h(z)=f(z)
,
h\leq f
near
z
, and the Gaussian curvature of
h(z)|dz|^{2}
is at most
-a<0
.

Let
g
be such a metric on
M\setminus E
. As before, for
1\leq m\leq\infty
, let
\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
denote the family of all non-constant holomorphic maps from
\mathbb{D}
with values in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
with the property that for any
q\in f(\mathbb{D})
, each root of
f(z)=q
is either simple or else has multiplicity at least
m+1
. For a given
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
, suppose that
f
takes values in
L\subset\cal F
. Then

f^{\mathcal{F},g}_{\mathbb{D}}(p)=\left.\frac{f^{\ast}(g|L)}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)|dz|}\right|_{z=p}

defines a distortion function of such an
f
with respect to
g
. As
f
varies in
\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
, we get a family of such distortion functions. With
t(z,f)
and
t(f)
defined as before, and
\alpha>0
, set

\mathcal{T}^{g}_{m,\cal F}(\alpha)=\inf\{t(f):f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F}),f^{\mathcal{F},g}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)\geq\alpha\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,E,\cal F)
be a Riemann surface foliation and suppose that
g
is an ultrahyperbolic metric on
\cal F
with curvature bounded above by
-a^{2}<0
for some
a>0
. Then

\mathcal{T}^{g}_{m,\cal F}(\alpha)\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}\right)

for
\alpha\in(0,1/a]
.

Thus, every
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
whose distortion at the origin is at least
\alpha\in(0,1/a]
is necessarily locally injective in some hyperbolic ball of uniform radius – a lower bound on this radius is given above. The final section contains miscellaneous remarks and examples pertaining to the theorems presented above.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

For
(i)
, let
p\in U\setminus E
. Then
p\in U_{n}
eventually. Let
\alpha:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,U}
be a uniformizing map with
\alpha(0)=p
so that
\eta_{U}(p)=|\alpha^{\prime}(0)|_{g}
. For

\epsilon>0
, let
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
be defined by
\psi(z)=(1-\epsilon)z
, and note that
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
is compactly contained in
U
. Hence,
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})\subset U_{n}
for all large
n
since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
. Further, as
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
is connected and contained in
L_{p}
, it follows that
\alpha\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
is in fact contained in
L_{p,U_{n}}
. The map
\alpha\circ\psi
is then a candidate in the family that defines
\eta_{U_{n}}
and therefore

\eta_{U_{n}}(p)\geq|(\alpha\circ\psi)^{\prime}(0)|_{g}=(1-\epsilon)|\alpha^{\prime}(0)|_{g}=(1-\epsilon)\eta_{U}(p)

for all large
n
. This shows that
\liminf\eta_{U_{n}}(p)\geq\eta_{U}(p)
.

Now let
\{\eta_{U_{n_{j}}}\}
be a convergent subsequence, and let
\alpha_{n_{j}}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,U_{n_{j}}}
be a family of uniformization maps such that
\alpha_{n_{j}}(0)=p
. Then,

\eta_{U_{n_{j}}}(p)=|\alpha^{\prime}_{n_{j}}(0)|_{g}

and since each
\alpha_{n_{j}}
takes values in the fixed hyperbolic leaf
L_{p}
, the family of uniformizations is normal. A further subsequence, which will still be indexed by
n_{j}
for the sake of brevity, then converges to a holomorphic limit
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p}
with
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p
.

The claim now is that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,U}
. To see this, suppose to the contrary that
q=\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})\notin\overline{U}
. Since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
,
q\notin\overline{U}_{n}
for all large
n
. On the other hand, the fact that
\alpha_{n_{j}}(z_{0})\rightarrow\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})=q
implies that
\alpha_{n_{j}}(z_{0})\notin U_{n_{j}}
for all large
n_{j}
– this is a contradiction since
\alpha_{n_{j}}(\mathbb{D})\subset U_{n_{j}}
. Hence
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset\overline{U}
. But as
U
is taut and
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in U
, it follows that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset U
and hence a subset of
L_{p}
. Thus,
\tilde{\alpha}
is a candidate in the family that defines
\eta_{g,U}
and hence

\eta_{U_{n_{j}}}(p)=|\alpha^{\prime}_{n_{j}}(0)|_{g}\rightarrow|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq\eta_{U}(p).

This shows that
\limsup\eta_{U_{n}}(p)\leq\eta_{U}(p)
– this completes the proof of
(i)
.

For
(ii)
, suppose that there is a compact set
K\subset U\setminus E
and a sequence
\{p_{n}\}\in K
such that

|\eta_{U_{n}}(p_{n})-\eta_{U}(p_{n})|>\epsilon

for some
\epsilon>0
. Assume that
p_{n}\rightarrow p
after passing to a subsequence. By the continuity of
\eta_{U}
,
|\eta_{U}(p_{n})-\eta_{U}(p)|<\epsilon/2

and hence

(2.1)

|\eta_{U_{n}}(p_{n})-\eta_{U}(p)|>\epsilon/2

for large
n
. Let
\alpha_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p_{n},U_{n}}\subset U_{n}
be a uniformization map with
\alpha_{p_{n}}(0)=p_{n}
. Since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
,
V
eventually contains all the
U_{n}
’s and hence the family
\{\alpha_{n}\}
is normal. By passing to a subsequence, let
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{V}
be a holomorphic limit with
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p
. To complete the proof, it suffices to show that
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,U}
is a uniformization. Indeed, if this were the case, then
\eta_{U}(p)=|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}
which would then imply that

\eta_{U_{n}}(p_{n})=|\alpha^{\prime}_{n}(0)|_{g}\rightarrow|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}=\eta_{U}(p)

and this contradicts (2.1). That
\tilde{\alpha}
uniformizes
L_{p,U}
is a consequence of the following observations that are inspired by the proof of Proposition
3
in
[
13
]
:

(a) Since
V
is taut and
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in V
, it follows that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset V
. Now suppose that
\tilde{\alpha}(a)\in V\setminus\overline{U}
for some
a\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
\alpha_{n}(a)\in V\setminus\overline{U}
for all large
n
and since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
, it follows that
\alpha_{n}(0)\notin\overline{U}_{n}
for all large
n
. This is a contradiction and hence
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\overline{U}
. Again, the tautness of
U
implies that
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow U
.

(b) To see that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p,U}\cup E
, note that there are two possibilities at this stage. If
\tilde{\alpha}
is constant, then
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})=\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in L_{p,U}
. If
\tilde{\alpha}
is non-constant, note that

N=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:\tilde{\alpha}(z)\notin E\}

is non-empty, for
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in U\setminus E
shows that the origin is contained in
N
. For
w_{0}\in N
, let

N_{w_{0}}=\{w\in N:L_{\tilde{\alpha}(w)}=L_{\tilde{\alpha}(w_{0})}\}.

To see that
N_{w_{0}}
is open in
\mathbb{D}
, pick
w_{1}\in N_{w_{0}}
and a chart
\phi=(x,y):W\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
around
\tilde{\alpha}(w_{1})=q
such that
(x,y)(q)=0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{W}
are described by
y=\mbox{constant}
. Let
V
be the connected component of
\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(W)\subset\mathbb{D}
containing
w_{1}
. Now, the claim is that
y(\tilde{\alpha}(V))=0
. If this is not the case, then
y(\tilde{\alpha}(V))\not=\{0\}
. Since
y(\tilde{\alpha}(w_{1}))=0
, it follows that
y\circ\tilde{\alpha}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
is a non-constant map which implies that its derivative
d(y\circ\tilde{\alpha})(w_{2})\not=0
for some
w_{2}\in V
near
w_{1}
. Since
\alpha_{n}
converges to
\tilde{\alpha}
,
d(y\circ\alpha_{n})(w_{2})\not=0
for
n
large. This means that
\alpha_{n}(\mathbb{D})
is not contained in a leaf of
\mathcal{F}
. This contradiction shows that
y(\tilde{\alpha}(V))=0
. Hence every
w\in V
has the property that the leaf of
\mathcal{F}_{W}
containing
\tilde{\alpha}(w)
coincides with
y=0
which is also the leaf passing through
w_{1}
. This shows that
N_{w_{0}}
is open.

Since
E
is discrete,
\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)\subset\mathbb{D}
is also discrete since
\tilde{\alpha}
is assumed to be non-constant. Therefore,
\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
is connected. Since
N_{w_{0}}
is open for each
w_{0}\in N
, it follows that
\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
coincides with
N_{\tilde{z}}
for some
\tilde{z}\in N
. Hence,
L_{\tilde{\alpha}(z)}=L_{\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{z})}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
and this implies that
\tilde{\alpha}(z)\in L_{\tilde{\alpha}(z)}=L_{\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{z})}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
. By continuity,
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{z})}\cup E
. But
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p\in L_{p}
since
p\notin E
. This shows that
L_{\tilde{\alpha}(\tilde{z})}=L_{p}
and hence that
\tilde{\alpha}(\mathbb{D})\subset L_{p}\cup E
.

(c) We now show that
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)\rightarrow L_{p}
is a covering map. This will be done in two steps – first, we show that
\tilde{\alpha}
is an immersion on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
and second, that it has the path lifting property on
L_{p}
.

For the first step, pick
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
so that
\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})\notin E
. Let
W
be a flow box around
\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})
small enough so that
W
is compactly contained in
U
. Since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
,
W\subset U_{n}
for large
n
. There is a chart
\phi=(x,y):W\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
with
(x,y)(\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0}))=0\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and the leaves of
\mathcal{F}_{W}
are described by
y=\mbox{constant}
. Since
\alpha_{n}(z_{0})\rightarrow\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})
,
\alpha_{n}(z_{0})\in W
for large
n
. Let
s_{n}=\alpha_{n}(z_{0})
and let
\psi_{n}
be a family of holomorphic automorphisms of
\mathbb{D}
with
\psi_{n}(0)=\phi(s_{n})
; here, we consider the unique local leaf, which is a copy of
\mathbb{D}
, that contains
\phi(s_{n})
. Then,
\phi^{-1}\circ\psi_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow W

\mathbb{D}
into the local leaf containing

. Now,

|(\phi^{-1}\circ\psi_{n})^{\prime}(0)|_{g}=|(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi(s_{n}))\circ\psi^{\prime}_{n}(0)|_{g}=|(\phi^{-1})^{\prime}(\phi(s_{n}))|_{g}(1-|\phi(s_{n})|^{2})\geq c>0

for some uniform

, since
|\phi(s_{n})|\rightarrow 0
and
\phi^{-1}
is a smooth diffeomorphism. By the extremal property of

(2.2)

|\alpha^{\prime}_{n}(z_{0})|_{g}\geq|(\phi^{-1}\circ\psi_{n})^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\geq c>0

|\tilde{\alpha}(z_{0})|_{g}\geq c>0
. Thus,
\tilde{\alpha}
is an immersion near

, and in particular near the origin since
\tilde{\alpha}(0)\notin E
.

In fact, more can be said at this stage. Observe that Rouche’s theorem shows that if
G\subset\mathbb{C}
is a domain with

and

is holomorphic with

, then there is a uniform
\delta>0
such that the euclidean disc
B(f(0),\delta|f^{\prime}(0)|/2)\subset f(B(0,\delta))
. With
\phi
a chart as above near
\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p
, let
\tilde{G}\subset\mathbb{D}
,
0\in\tilde{G}
be such that
\phi\circ\tilde{\alpha}:\tilde{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
. Let
G\subset\tilde{G}
be a compactly contained neighbourhood of

\phi\circ\alpha_{n}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
, since
\phi\circ\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\phi\circ\tilde{\alpha}
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
. Therefore,
there is a uniform
\delta>0
such that

B(\phi\circ\alpha_{n}(0),\delta|(\phi\circ\alpha_{n})^{\prime}(0)|/2)\subset(\phi\circ\alpha_{n})(B(0,\delta))

for all
n
large. By (2.2), there is a uniform positive lower bound on
|(\phi\circ\alpha_{n})^{\prime}(0)|
, and hence the images
(\phi\circ\alpha_{n})(B(0,\delta))
contain a ball of fixed size around
\phi\circ\alpha_{n}(0)
.

For the second step, it suffices to show that for every path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow L_{p},\gamma(0)=p
, there exists a path
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
with
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=0\in\mathbb{D}

and
\tilde{\alpha}\circ\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
. We may assume that
\gamma
is
C^{1}
-smooth. Let
\lambda_{U}=g/\eta_{U}
. Since
\eta_{U}
is assumed to be continuous on
U
,
\lambda_{U}
is continuous on
T\mathcal{F}_{U}
, the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{F}_{U}
. Also recall that
\lambda_{U}
induces the Poincaré metric on the leaves and hence

(2.3)

k=\sup\left\{\lambda_{U}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)):t\in[0,1]\right\}

is finite since
\gamma\subset L_{p}
is compact. By
[
2
]
, there exists a compact neighbourhood
\tilde{V}
of
\gamma([0,1])
in
L_{p}
and a tubular neighbourhood
\pi:\tilde{W}\rightarrow\tilde{V}
such that if

is close to

, then
\gamma
lifts to a
C^{1}
-smooth curve
\gamma_{q}:[0,1]\rightarrow L_{q}
such that
\pi\circ\gamma_{q}=\gamma
and
\gamma_{q}\rightarrow\gamma
as
q\rightarrow p
. Since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
and
\tilde{V}\subset\tilde{W}
are compactly contained in
U
, it follows that
\tilde{W}
is compactly contained in
U_{n}
for
n
large. Therefore,
\gamma\subset L_{p,U_{n}}
for
n
large.

The idea now is to lift
\gamma
using
\pi
to get paths that are contained in different leaves and then lift them to
\mathbb{D}
using the uniformizations
\alpha_{n}
. To make all this precise, note that as
\alpha_{n}(0)\rightarrow\tilde{\alpha}(0)=p
, the argument using Rouche’s theorem given above shows that it is possible to choose a sequence
z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
,
z_{n}\rightarrow 0
such that
q_{n}=\alpha_{n}(z_{n})\in\pi^{-1}(p)
. Note that
q_{n}\in L_{p_{n},U_{n}}
. Then
\gamma
admits a lift using
\pi
starting at
q_{n}
and contained in
L_{q_{n}}
. Call this lift
\gamma_{q_{n}}
and note that
\gamma_{q_{n}}\subset\tilde{W}\subset U_{n}
for large
n
as
\gamma\subset\tilde{W}
and
\gamma_{q_{n}}\rightarrow\gamma
. Therefore, there exists a lift
\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
with
\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}(0)=z_{n}
such that
\alpha_{n}\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}=\gamma_{q_{n}}
.

(d) Let
\mathcal{K}\subset T\mathcal{F}_{U}
be a compact set. Note that the projection of
\mathcal{K}
to
M
is a compact set that does not intersect the singular set
E
. Since
\rho(U,U_{n})\rightarrow 0
,
\mathcal{K}\subset T\mathcal{F}_{U_{n}}
for all large
n
. We will now compare
\lambda_{U}
and
\lambda_{U_{n}}
on
\mathcal{K}
.

Claim:
There exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{K})>0
such that

|\lambda_{U_{n}}(z,v)-\lambda_{U}(z,v)|\leq C

for all
(z,v)\in\mathcal{K}
and
n
large.

If this were false, there would exist a sequence of pairs
(w_{n},v_{n})\in\mathcal{K}
such that

|\lambda_{U_{n}}(w_{n},v_{n})-\lambda_{U}(w_{n},v_{n})|\geq n

after passing to a subsequence. By passing to a further subsequence if needed,
(w_{n},v_{n})\rightarrow(w_{0},v_{0})\in\mathcal{K}
, where
w_{0}\notin E
, and by using the continuity of
\lambda_{U}
, it can be seen that

|\lambda_{U_{n}}(w_{n},v_{n})-\lambda_{U}(w_{0},v_{0})|\geq n-1

for all large
n
. Since
|v_{n}|_{g},|v_{0}|_{g}
are uniformly bounded and
0<\eta_{U}(w_{0})<\infty
, it follows that
\eta_{U_{n}}(w_{n})\rightarrow 0
. Choose uniformizations
\psi_{n}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{w_{n},U_{n}}
such that
\psi_{n}(0)=w_{n}
and
\eta_{U_{n}}(w_{n})=|\psi^{\prime}_{n}(0)|_{g}
. Then
|\psi^{\prime}_{n}(0)|_{g}\rightarrow 0
. But this is a contradiction as the proof of (c) shows. Indeed, since

, there is a uniform positive lower bound on

, and thus the claim holds.

(e) Coming back to the lifts
\gamma_{q_{n}}
which are
C^{1}
-perturbations of
\gamma

such that

\sup\left\{\left|\lambda_{U_{n}}(\gamma_{q_{n}}(t),\gamma^{\prime}_{q_{n}}(t))-\lambda_{U}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t)):t\in[0,1]\right|\right\}\leq C

and hence

for all large

and

. Since unramified holomorphic coverings preserve the infinitesimal hyperbolic metric,

|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{q_{n}}(t)|\leq\frac{|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{q_{n}}(t)|}{1-|\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}(t)|^{2}}\leq C+k

and consequently, the maps

are equicontinuous. In addition, if

, then

, the hyperbolic distance between

and
\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}(t)
is at most

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{q_{n}}(t)|}{1-|\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}(t)|^{2}}\leq C+k

and since

is arbitrary, this means that the traces of all paths
\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}
are uniformly compactly contained in
\mathbb{D}
. It follows that, after passing to a subsequence if needed, there is a path
\tilde{\gamma}=\lim\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}
in
\mathbb{D}
that starts at the origin and satisfies

\tilde{\alpha}\circ\tilde{\gamma}=\lim\alpha_{n}\circ\tilde{\gamma}_{q_{n}}=\lim\gamma_{q_{n}}=\gamma.

Since
\gamma
does not intersect

, it follows that
\tilde{\gamma}
does not intersect
\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
. Thus,
\tilde{\alpha}:\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)\rightarrow L_{p}
is a covering map.

(f) For
\epsilon>0
, note that
\tilde{\alpha}\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
is compactly contained in
U
, where
\psi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
is defined by
\psi(z)=(1-\epsilon)z
. Therefore, the images
\alpha_{n}\circ\psi(\mathbb{D})
are also uniformly compactly contained in
U
for all large

. Hence, the maps
\alpha_{n}\circ\psi
are candidates in the family that defines
\eta_{U}
and so

(1-\epsilon)|\alpha_{n}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq\eta_{U}(p_{n}).

The continuity of
\eta_{U}
and the fact that
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\tilde{\alpha}
uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{D}
shows that
(1-\epsilon)|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq\eta_{U}(p)
. By allowing
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, it follows that
|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq\eta_{U}(p)
. By choosing a uniformization
\beta:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,U}
with
\beta(0)=p
, this inequality can be rephrased as
|\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)|_{g}\leq|\beta^{\prime}(0)|_{g}
.

(g) Let
\pi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)
be a covering with
\pi(0)=0
. As
\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)\not=\emptyset
by assumption, the Schwarz lemma shows that
|\pi^{\prime}(0)|<1
. The following diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}\setminus\tilde{\alpha}^{-1}(E)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{p,U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\pi}}

commutes, where
\tilde{\pi}:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}
is a lift of
\tilde{\alpha}\circ\pi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow L_{p,U}
. Since
\beta

are both uniformizations of

, it must be the case that

. On the other hand,

3.
Proof of Theorem 1.2

Recall that
(M,E,\mathcal{F})
is a Riemann surface foliation that is assumed to admit an ultrahyperbolic metric
g
with curvature bounded above by
-a^{2}<0
. The Ahlfors lemma implies that each leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
is hyperbolic and
g|L\leq a^{-1}\lambda_{L}
, where
\lambda_{L}
is the Poincaré metric on
L
.

For a fixed leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
, consider the subfamily
\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},L)\subset\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
. For each
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},L)
, it follows that
f^{\ast}(g|L)\leq a^{-1}f^{\ast}(\lambda_{L})
. Hence

f^{\mathcal{F},g}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)=\left.\frac{f^{\ast}(g|L)}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)|dz|}\right|_{z=0}\leq a^{-1}\cdot\left.\frac{f^{\ast}(\lambda_{L})}{\lambda_{\mathbb{D}}(z)|dz|}\right|_{z=0}=a^{-1}\cdot f^{L}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)

and this means that if
f^{\mathcal{F},g}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)\geq\alpha
, then
f^{L}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)\geq a\alpha
. It follows that

\inf\left\{t(f):f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},L),f^{\mathcal{F},g}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)\geq\alpha\right\}\geq\mathcal{T}_{m,L}(a\alpha)

where
\mathcal{T}_{m,L}
is as in (1.1). The lower bound on
\mathcal{T}_{m,L}
in (1.2) holds for all hyperbolic Riemann surfaces and does not require any further properties of
L
. Since each
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
belongs to
\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},L)
for some leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
, it follows that

\mathcal{T}^{g}_{m,\cal F}(\alpha)\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}\right)

for all
\alpha\in(0,1/a]
. This completes the proof.

4.
Remarks and Examples

(A) The results in
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
describe a paradigm that can be summarized thus. Let
g
be a hermitian metric on
M\setminus E
whose restriction to each leaf
L\subset\mathcal{F}
has Gaussian curvature bounded from above by a uniform negative constant. Then each leaf is hyperbolic. Furthermore, if
g
is complete, then under suitable hypotheses on the singular locus
E
, the family of uniformizations of the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
is normal, i.e., a family of uniformizations has a subsequence that either converges to a uniformization of the limiting leaf or to a constant map with values in
E
. A direct consequence of this is the continuity of
\eta
. As remarked before the statement of Theorem 1.1, it is possible to consider localized versions of
\eta
with respect to a given neighbourhood in
X
, and Theorem 1.1 shows that even these localized versions of
\eta
possess continuity properties as the neighbourhoods vary in the Hausdorff sense.

Several examples on the growth of
\eta
near the singular set
E
can be found in
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
. They are based on explicit local constructions of metrics that reflect the intrinsic geometry of the vector fields defining the foliation
\mathcal{F}
. It turns out that these examples are also useful in illustrating the various possibilities that can arise in Theorem 1.2 above. We collate them below keeping this connection in mind. In what follows,
X
is a germ of a holomorphic vector field near the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We will work with a representative of
X
that is defined in the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)
of radius
\rho<1
and the standing assumption is that the origin is an isolated singularity for
X
. Any additional hypotheses will be mentioned explicitly in the various examples given below. The resulting foliation will be denoted by
\mathcal{F}
.

Example 1:
Consider the restriction of the smooth metric

g(z)=\frac{2\;|dz|}{1-|z|^{2}}

to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. Let
Z=Z(T)
be the solution of

\dot{Z}=dZ/dT=X(Z(T)),\;Z(0)=z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)\setminus\{0\}

where
Z
is defined in a neighbourhood of the origin in the plane. Since
\dot{Z}=X(Z)
, a computation shows that the curvature of the pull back metric
Z^{\ast}g
at
T=0
is

K_{g}(z)=(|z|^{2}-1)-|X(z)|^{-2}\left|\left\langle z,X(z)\right\rangle\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}|X(z)|^{-6}\left|X(z)\wedge DX(z)(X(z))\right|^{2}

where the angle-brackets denote the standard hermitian inner product on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
|z\wedge w|^{2}=|z|^{2}|w|^{2}-|\langle z,w\rangle|^{2}
. Since
|z|<\rho<1
,

K_{g}(z)\leq|z|^{2}-1<\rho^{2}-1<0

and hence
g
is ultrahyperbolic of curvature bounded by
-a^{2}<0
where
a^{2}=1-\rho^{2}>0
. Theorem 1.2 then shows that

\mathcal{T}^{g}_{m,\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}(\alpha)\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}\right)

for all
\alpha\in(0,1/a]
.

Example 2:
Suppose that the origin is a non-degenerate singularity of
X
in the sense that there exists
C>1
such that

C^{-1}|z|\leq|X(z)|\leq C|z|
for
z
near the origin. Let
h
be a smooth hermitian metric on
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)
and let
u
be a continuous plurisubharmonic function on
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)
which is
C^{\infty}
-smooth away from the origin. Consider the restriction of the metric

g=\frac{e^{u}}{(\log|z|)^{2}}\cdot\frac{h}{h(X)}

which is smooth away from the origin, to the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. If
Z=Z(T)
is a solution of

\dot{Z}=dZ/dT=X(Z(T)),\;Z(0)=z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)\setminus\{0\}

near
T=0
, it can be seen that the pull back metric
Z^{\ast}g=f(T)|dT|^{2}
where

f(T)=\frac{e^{u(Z(T))}}{(\log|Z(T)|)^{2}}.

and its curvature
K_{g}
satisfies

-\frac{1}{2}K_{g}(z)=\frac{1}{f(0)}\partial\overline{\partial}(u(Z(T))+\log(\log^{-2}(|Z(T)|)))\big|_{T=0}.

The plurisubharmonicity of
u
implies that the first term above is non-negative and hence

-\frac{1}{2}K_{g}(z)\geq e^{-u(z)}(\log|z|)^{2}\partial\overline{\partial}\log(\log^{-2}(|Z(T)|))\big|_{T=0}.

Now,

\partial\overline{\partial}\log(\log^{-2}(|Z(T)|))\big|_{T=0}=\frac{1}{|z|^{4}|\log(|z|)|}\left(|z|^{2}|X(z)|^{2}-|\langle X(z),z\rangle|^{2}\right)+\frac{|\langle X(z),z\rangle|^{2}}{2|z|^{4}\log^{2}(|z|)}

as a computation shows and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then implies that
K_{g}(z)<0
away from the origin. Note that

(4.1)

-\frac{1}{2}K_{g}(z)\geq\frac{|\log(|z|)|}{e^{u(z)}}\left(\frac{|X(z)|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}-\frac{|\langle X(z),z\rangle|^{2}}{|z|^{4}}\right)+\frac{|\langle X(z),z\rangle|^{2}}{2|z|^{4}e^{u(z)}}.

It follows that if
|z|<\rho<1/\sqrt{e}
, then
|\log(|z|)|>1/2
and hence

-K_{g}(z)\geq\frac{|X(z)|^{2}}{|z|^{2}e^{u(z)}}

as can be seen by combining the second and third terms in (4.1). Finally, using the assumption that the origin is a non-degenerate singularity, it follows that

\limsup_{z\rightarrow 0}K_{g}(z)\leq-C^{-2}/e^{u(0)}

and hence that
K_{g}(z)
is bounded above by a negative constant, say
-a^{2}
, in a neighbourhood of the origin in case
u(0)>-\infty
. The same conclusion holds even if
u(0)=-\infty
. These calculations can be found in
[
11
]
under the hypotheses that
u
is pluriharmonic near the origin. As indicated above, they hold even when
u
is a continuous plurisubharmonic function.

Example 3:
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
admits
n
smooth invariant hypersurfaces that meet in general position at the origin. In this case, there is a coordinate system in which the invariant hypersurfaces are given by
\{z_{j}=0\}
,
1\leq j\leq n
and

X(z)=z_{1}^{k_{1}}f_{1}(z)\;\partial/\partial z_{1}+z_{2}^{k_{2}}f_{2}(z)\;\partial/\partial z_{2}+\ldots+z_{n}^{k_{n}}f_{n}(z)\;\partial/\partial z_{n}

where the
f_{i}
’s are holomorphic near the origin and
k_{i}\geq 1
are integers. For
z_{j}\not=0
, set

\psi_{j}(z)=\frac{|z_{j}|^{2k_{j}-2}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}}{\log^{2}(|z_{j}|)}

and note that
\psi_{j}
extends to a continuous function on
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\rho)\setminus\{0\}
by setting
\psi_{j}(z)=0
if
z_{j}=0
. Then
\psi(z)=\max\{\psi_{j}(z):1\leq j\leq n\}
is a positive continuous function away from the origin. As shown in
[
13
]
, the metric

g(z)=\frac{\psi(z)}{|X(z)|^{2}}\;|dz|^{2}

is ultrahyperbolic of curvature bounded above by
-1
.

All these examples show that it is possible to define smooth or ultrahyperbolic metrics away from the origin with curvatures bounded above by a negative constant. The uniform negative upper bound for the curvature is determined by the metric which in turn reflects the properties of the underlying holomorphic vector field. Theorem 1.2 shows that in each of these examples,
the foliated domain Bloch constant

\mathcal{T}^{g}_{m,\mathcal{F}_{\rho}}(\alpha)\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}((a\alpha)^{1/(m+1)})}\right)

for all
\alpha\in(0,1/a]
. Here, the superscript
g
refers to the metrics in the examples above, and
a>0
controls the curvature of the leaves near the origin in the corresponding cases.

(B) To propose a minor variation of Minda’s construction of the domain Bloch constant
\mathcal{T}_{m.S}(\alpha)
in (1.1), let
\alpha:S\rightarrow[0,1]
be a continuous function on the hyperbolic Riemann surface
S
. For
x\in S
, define

\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{m,S}(x)=\inf\left\{t(f):f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},S),f(0)=x,f^{S}_{\mathbb{D}}(0)\geq\alpha(x)\right\}

which allows for a variable lower bound for the distortion function. Since the family that defines
\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{m,S}(x)
is a subset of that which defines (1.1), it follows that

\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}_{m,S}(x)\geq\mathcal{T}_{m,S}(\alpha(x))\geq\log\left(\frac{1+\tau_{m}((\alpha(x))^{1/(m+1)})}{1-\tau_{m}((\alpha(x))^{1/(m+1)})}\right).

With this in place, it is straightforward to formulate analogs of Theorem 1.2 in which we consider a continuous function
\alpha:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow[0,1]
and use it to allow variable lower bounds for the distortions of maps
f\in\mathcal{O}_{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{F})
and finally obtain a uniform lower bound for the ball on which every such
f
is injective.

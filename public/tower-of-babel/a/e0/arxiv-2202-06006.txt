Sign-changing bubble tower solutions for a Paneitz-type problem
Wenjing Chen, Xiaomeng Huang

Abstract




Abstract
: This paper is concerned with the following biharmonic problem

(0.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=|u|^{\frac{8}{N-4}}u&\text{ in }\ \Omega\backslash\overline{{B(\xi_{0},\varepsilon)}},\\
u=\Delta u=0&\text{ on }\ \partial(\Omega\backslash\overline{{B(\xi_{0},\varepsilon)}}),\end{cases}

where
\Omega
is an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 5
, and
B(\xi_{0},\varepsilon)
is a ball centered at
\xi_{0}
with radius
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon
is a small positive parameter.
We obtain the existence of solutions for problem (
0.1
), which is an arbitrary large number of sign-changing solutions whose profile is a superposition of bubbles with alternate sign which concentrate at the center of the hole.

Keywords
: Biharmonic equation; critical Sobolev exponent; sign-changing bubble tower solutions; reduction method.

1.
Introduction and statement of main result

In this article, we consider the existence of sign-changing bubble tower solutions to the following problem

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}u=|u|^{p-1}u&\text{ in }\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\\
u=\Delta u=0&\text{ on }\ \partial\Omega_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

where
\Delta^{2}
is the biharmonic operator,
\Omega_{\varepsilon}:=\Omega\backslash\overline{{B(\xi_{0},\varepsilon)}}
with
\Omega
being an open bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 5
,
B(\xi_{0},\varepsilon)
is a ball centered at
\xi_{0}
with radius
\varepsilon
,
\varepsilon>0
small enough, and
p=\frac{N+4}{N-4}
is the critical exponent in the sense that the embedding
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{p+1}(\Omega)
.

Problem (
1.1
) is related to the Paneitz operator, which is conformal operator of the fourth-order. It was first introduced by Paneitz
[
35
]
for the study of smooth four-dimensional Riemannian manifolds, and Branson
[
8
]
generalized the dimension of the Riemannian manifold to
N
dimension. Since this type of equation involving Paneitz operators is similar to geometric equations with Paneitz operators, it has received a lot of attention, we refer to
[
1
,
9
,
3
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
and references therein.

When the biharmonic operator in (
1.1
) is replaced by the Laplacian operator, consider the following problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=|u|^{\frac{4}{N-2}}u&\text{ in }\ \Omega,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\ \partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Solvability for problem (
1.2
) is not a trivial issue, since it strongly depends on the geometry of
\Omega
.
A direct consequence of Pohozaev’s identity
[
37
]
is that problem (
1.2
) has no positive solutions when the domain
\Omega
is strictly star-shaped.
On the other hand, if
\Omega
is an annulus, then Kazdan and Warner
[
29
]
showed that solvability for problem (
1.2
) is restored. Coron showed that symmetry is not really needed to have solvability in
[
17
]
, he obtained the existence of a positive solution to (
1.2
) in the case in which
\Omega
has a small (not necessarily symmetric) hole.

The study of sign-changing solutions for elliptic problems with critical nonlinearity has received the interest of several authors in the last years, see for instance
[
5
,
16
,
27
,
28
]
and references therein.
Here we focus our interest in existence and qualitative properties of sign-changing solutions to (
1.2
) for domains
D
which have a hole, that is in the Coron’s setting.
The first result available in literature is the one contained in
[
33
]
, where a large number of sign changing solutions to (
1.2
) in the presence of a single small hole has been proved. To be more precise, the authors assume that the domain is
\Omega\backslash B(0,\varepsilon)
, where
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain containing the origin, and it is symmetric with respect to the origin, while the hole is given by
B(0,\varepsilon)
, a round ball with radius
\varepsilon
.
Substantial improvement of this result was obtained in
[
25
]
where the assumption of symmetry was removed,
see also
[
33
,
34
]
. Recently, the existence of a sequence of finite-energy, sign-changing solutions with a crown-like shape for problem (
1.2
) was obtained in
[
21
]
.

Consider the following biharmonic equation under the Navier boundary condition

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta^{2}u=K|u|^{q-1}u&\text{ in }\ \Omega,\\
u=\Delta u=0&\text{ on }\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In the subcritical case, namely
q=\frac{N+4}{N-4}-\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
small, when
K
is a constant, the asymptotic behavior of solutions of (
1
) has been studied in
[
4
]
. On the other hand,
Ayed and Ghoudi
[
2
]
proved that the low energy sign-changing solutions to (
1
) that are close to two bubbles with different signs and they have to blow up either at two different points with the same speed or at a critical point of the Robin function. Yessine and Rabeh
[
39
]
constructed a solution with the shape of a tower of sign-changing bubbles as
\varepsilon
goes to zero. When
K\neq 1
, Ghoudi
[
26
]
constructed sign-changing solutions of (
1
) having two bubbles and blowing up either at two different critical points of
K
with the same speed or at the same critical point. See
[
15
,
23
]
and the reference therein for the concentration phenomena of solutions of the subcritical problem (
1
).

Concerning the supercritical case, namely
q=\frac{N+4}{N-4}+\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon>0
, Bouh
[
6
]
showed that there is no sign-changing solution with low energy which blow up at exactly two points for
\varepsilon
small and proved that problem (
1.1
) has no bubble-tower sign-changing solutions. Ayed
et al.

[
4
]
got that the supercritical problem (
1.1
) has no solutions which concentrate around a point of
\Omega
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. The case
K
is a nonconstant function, it was proved
[
7
]
that for
\varepsilon
small, (
1
) has no sign-changing solutions that blow up at two near points and also has no bubble-tower sign-changing solutions.

In the critical case
q=\frac{N+4}{N-4}
. Since the Sobolev embedding is not compact, problem (
1.1
) is the lack of compactness. In fact, some researchers have got some results of the existence of solution. A first result of problem (
1.1
) was obtained by Van Der Vorst
[
38
]
, who showed that when
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a starshaped domain, (
1.1
) has no positive solutions. In
[
22
]
, Ebobisse and Ould Ahmedou investigated the influence of the topology of the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
on the existence of solution, they proved that (
1.1
) has a positive solution when some homology group of
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is nontrivial. Subsequently, in
[
24
]
, Gazzola, Grunau and Squassina showed that this topological condition is sufficient, but not necessary by proving existence of nontrivial solutions in some contractible domains which are perturbations of small capacity of domains having nontrivial topology. When the domain
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
is a bounded domain with a small ball removed, Alarcón and Pistoia
[
1
]
constructed solutions of (
1.1
) blowing up at the center of the hole as the size of the hole goes to zero.

Moreover, as far as we know, there are few results on the sign-changing tower solutions for problem (
1.1
).
Inspired by the above works, especially by
[
1
,
25
,
32
,
34
]
, in the present paper, we construct sign-changing solutions to problem (
1.1
), the shape of this solution is a superposition of bubbles with alternating sign centered at the center of the hole, the point
\xi_{0}
, as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
.

Let us consider the following limit equation

(1.5)

\Delta^{2}u=u^{\frac{N+4}{N-4}},\ \ \ u>0,\ \ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Smooth radial solutions of (
1.5
) are completely classified
[
30
]
and are given by

(1.6)

U_{\mu,\xi}(x)=\alpha_{N}\left(\frac{\mu}{\mu^{2}+|x-\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\alpha_{N}=(N(N-4)(N-2)(N+2))^{\frac{N-4}{8}}
,
\mu
is a positive parameter and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Our main result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
N\geq 5
. Then, given an integer
k\geq 1
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
, there exists a pair of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
and
-u_{\varepsilon}
to problem (
1.1
) such that

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\alpha_{N}\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}\left(\frac{d_{i}\varepsilon^{\frac{2i-1}{2k}}}{d_{i}^{2}\varepsilon^{2\frac{2i-1}{2k}}+|x-\xi_{0}|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{2}}(1+o(1)),

where
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
are positive numbers depending only on
N
and
k
and
o(1)\rightarrow 0
uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega
as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction method, we can refer to
[
13
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
25
,
32
,
34
,
36
]
and reference therein.
Let us point out the difficulties arising in the construction of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
in Theorem
1.1
.
Taking into account that the solution concentrates around the hole and at the same time it must satisfy zero Navier boundary conditions on the boundary of the hole, it turns to be extremely delicate to study the behavior of the solution in the region around the hole, which was given in
[
1
]
. Moreover, we have to do some fine estimates on interaction of different bubbles. The proof will also provide much finer estimates on the expansion of the energy functional, and the
C^{1}
-estimate.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give some preliminary results.
The proof of the main result is given in Section
3
. Section
4
is devoted to perform the finite dimensional reduction. Section
5
contains the asymptotic expansion of the reduced energy. Some technical estimates are given in Appendix.

Preliminaries

\begin{cases}\Delta^{2}P_{\varepsilon}U_{\mu,\xi}=U_{\mu,\xi}^{p}&\text{ in }\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\\

By the comparison principle, we have the following crucial estimates.

Lemma 2.1

Let

The space
H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is equipped with
the scalar product
\langle u,v\rangle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\Delta u\Delta v
, which induces the norm

\|u\|=\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|\Delta u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

If
u\in L^{q}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, we denote by
|u|_{q}=(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}|u|^{q})^{\frac{1}{q}}
the
L^{q}
-norm.
By Sobolev embedding Theorem, there exists
C>0
, depending only on
N
, such that
|u|_{\frac{2N}{N-4}}\leq C\|u\|
for all
u\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.
Consider now the adjoint operator of the embedding
i:H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2N}{N-4}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
, namely the map

i^{*}:L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
is
defined as the (unique) weak solution of

\Delta^{2}u=\omega\ \ \text{in}\ \Omega_{\varepsilon},\qquad u=\Delta u=0\ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Thus

i^{*}(\omega)=u\Leftrightarrow\langle u,\varphi\rangle=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\omega\varphi dx,\quad\forall\varphi\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Moreover, there exists a positive
constant
c
, which depends only on the dimension
N
, such that

(2.8)

\|i^{*}(\omega)\|\leq c|\omega|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\quad\text{for\ all}\ \omega\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}).

Using the above definitions and notations, problem (
1.1
) can be rewritten as follows

(2.9)

u=i^{*}[f(u)],\quad u\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),

where
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
with
p=\frac{N+4}{N-4}
.

We next describe the shape of the solutions we are looking for.
Let
d>0
be a small but fixed number. Given an integer
k
, let
\mu_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,k
, be positive numbers and
\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,2,\cdots,k
, be points in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

(2.10)

d<\mu_{i}<\frac{1}{d}\ \text{and}\ |\sigma_{i}|<\frac{1}{d}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\ldots,k.

We assume that

(2.11)

\mu_{i\varepsilon}=\mu_{i}\varepsilon^{\frac{2i-1}{2k}\theta}\ \text{with}\ \theta=\frac{2k(N-2)}{2k(N-2)-2},\ \ \ \text{and}\ \xi_{i\varepsilon}=\xi_{0}+\mu_{i\varepsilon}\sigma_{i}.

We define

(2.12)

V(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(-1)^{i+1}P_{\varepsilon}U_{\mu_{i\varepsilon},\xi_{i\varepsilon}}(x).

We will use the compact notation
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{k})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}
and
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\cdots,\sigma_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{Nk}

We will look for the solutions of (
1.1

Before giving the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.3

There exists
\hat{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}

Proof.

For simplicity, we replace

\displaystyle H_{1}:=c_{2}H(\xi_{0},\xi_{0}),\ f(\sigma_{k}):=-\frac{3(N-2)}{2}meas(\mathbb{S}^{N-1})\frac{\Delta U_{1,0}(\sigma_{k})U_{1,0}(\sigma_{k})}{\alpha_{N}^{p+1}},\ g(\sigma_{j}):=2\Gamma(\sigma_{j}).

Then we can write

If

is strictly positively definite, then
\Phi(\nu,0)
has a minimum point
(\hat{\nu},0)
. Next, we claim that
(\hat{\nu},0)
is a non degenerate critical point of
\Phi(\nu,\sigma)
.
Since

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}\Phi(\hat{\nu},0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathscr{H}\Phi_{\nu}(\hat{\nu},0)&0\\
0&\mathscr{H}\Phi_{\sigma}(\hat{\nu},0)\\
\end{array}\right)

it remains to prove
det(\mathscr{H}\Phi_{\nu}(\hat{\nu},0))\neq 0
and
det(\mathscr{H}\Phi_{\sigma}(\hat{\nu},0))\neq 0
.

In fact, from
[
25

, we have that
x=0
is a non degenerate critical point of

.
Since

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}(\Delta U_{1,0}(x)U_{1,0}(x))}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\big|_{x=0}=0,\ \ \mbox{for}\ i\neq j;

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}(\Delta U_{1,0}(x)U_{1,0}(x))}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}\big|_{x=0}=\alpha_{N}^{2}(N-4)(2N^{2}-6N-4)\neq 0.

We obtain
x=0
is a non degenerate critical point of
f(x)
, namely,
det\big(\mathscr{H}\Phi_{\sigma}(\hat{\nu},0)\big)\neq 0.

From
\nabla\Phi(\nu,\sigma)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\nu_{1}}=2H_{1}\nu_{1}-g(\sigma_{1})\frac{\nu_{2}}{\nu_{1}^{2}}=0;

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\nu_{i}}=\frac{g(\sigma_{i-1})}{\nu_{i-1}}-g(\sigma_{i})\frac{\nu_{i+1}}{\nu_{i}^{2}}=0\quad\text{for}\ i=2,...,k-1;

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\nu_{k}}=-\frac{2(N-2)}{N-4}\frac{f(\sigma_{k})}{\nu_{k}^{\frac{3N-8}{N-4}}}+\frac{g(\sigma_{k-1})}{\nu_{k-1}}=0.

.
This together with the fact that
\Phi({\nu},0)
has a minimum point

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}\Phi_{{\nu}}(\hat{\nu},0)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}2H_{1}+\frac{2g(0)\hat{\nu}_{2}}{\hat{\nu}_{1}^{3}}&-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{1}^{2}}&\frac{2g(0)\hat{\nu}_{3}}{\hat{\nu}_{2}^{3}}&-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{2}^{2}}&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{2}^{2}}&\frac{2g(0)\hat{\nu}_{4}}{\hat{\nu}_{3}^{3}}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\frac{2g(0)\hat{\nu}_{k}}{\hat{\nu}_{k-1}^{3}}&-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{k-1}^{2}}\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-\frac{g(0)}{\hat{\nu}_{k-1}^{2}}&\frac{2(N-2)(3N-8)f(0)}{(N-4)^{2}\hat{\nu}_{k}^{\frac{4(N-3)}{N-4}}}\\
\end{array}\right)_{k\times k}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left(\begin{array}[]{cccccc}3\lambda&-g(0)&0&\cdots&0&0\\
-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{g(0)}&2\lambda&-g(0)&\cdots&0&0\\
0&-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{g(0)}&2\lambda&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&2\lambda&-g(0)\\
0&0&0&\cdots&-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{g(0)}&\frac{2(N-2)(3N-8)f(0)}{(N-4)^{2}\hat{\nu}_{k}^{\frac{2N-4}{N-4}}}\\
\end{array}\right)_{k\times k}:=Q.

By calculations, we can get
det(Q)=\frac{2f(0)(N-2)(3N-8)}{(N-4)^{2}\hat{\nu}_{k}^{\frac{2N-4}{N-4}}}(2k-1)\lambda^{k-1}-(2k-3)\lambda^{k}=\frac{4Nk-8k-4}{N-4}\lambda^{k}\neq 0
, it follows that

.
That proves our claim.
∎

\displaystyle\begin{cases}O\left((\frac{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}})^{4\beta}\right)\int_{\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l+1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l-1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{|y-\frac{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}\sigma_{l}|^{8\beta}}\frac{1}{(1+|y-\sigma_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}},&\text{ if }l>i,\\
O\left((\frac{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}})^{4\beta}\right)\int_{\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l+1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l-1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{(1+|y-\sigma_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}},&\text{ if }l<i,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{N\theta}{2k}}\right)=O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p\beta}{2}}\right).

Similarly, if
5\leq N<12
, set
x-\xi_{0}=\mu_{l\varepsilon}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}|U_{l}^{p-1}U_{i}|^{\beta}\leq c\int_{\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l+1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l-1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}}\frac{\mu_{l\varepsilon}^{N-4\beta}}{(1+|y-\sigma_{l}|^{2})^{4\beta}}\frac{\mu_{i\varepsilon}^{N-\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}}{(\mu_{i\varepsilon}^{2}+\mu_{l\varepsilon}^{2}|y-\frac{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}\sigma_{i}|^{2})^{\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}O\left((\frac{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}{\mu_{i\varepsilon}})^{\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}\right)\int_{\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l+1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l-1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{(1+|y-\sigma_{l}|^{2})^{4\beta}},&\text{ if }l>i,\\
O\left((\frac{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}})^{\frac{N-4}{2}\beta}\right)\int_{\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l+1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}\leq|y|\leq\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{l\varepsilon}\mu_{l-1\varepsilon}}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}}\frac{1}{|y-\frac{\mu_{i\varepsilon}}{\mu_{l\varepsilon}}\sigma_{i}|^{(N-4)\beta}}\frac{1}{(1+|y-\sigma_{l}|^{2})^{4\beta}},&\text{ if }l<i,\end{cases}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\beta}\right).

If
N=12
, we get that
\int_{A_{l}}|U_{l}^{p-1}U_{i}|^{\beta}=O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\beta}|\ln\varepsilon|^{\beta}\right).

∎

Lemma 6.6
.

The following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=\begin{cases}c\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p}{2}},&\text{ if }N\geq 13,\\
c\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}}|\ln\varepsilon|,&\text{ if }N=12,\\
c\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}},&\text{ if }5\leq N\leq 11.\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
U_{\mu_{i\varepsilon},\xi_{i\varepsilon}}:=U_{i}
. It holds:

(6.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=\left(\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus B(\xi_{0},\rho)}+\int_{A_{i}}+\sum_{l\neq i}^{k}\int_{A_{l}}\right)\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}},

where

(6.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus B(\xi_{0},\rho)}\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\leq c\left|\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j\varepsilon}^{4}\bigg)\mu_{i\varepsilon}^{\frac{N-4}{2}}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{N\theta}{2k}}).

Next, let us consider the case
N\geq 13
. For
l\neq i
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{A_{l}}\Big||(PU_{i}-U_{i})+\sum_{j\neq i}PU_{j}|^{p-1}U_{i}\Big|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{A_{l}}\left||PU_{i}-U_{i}|^{p-1}U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}+c\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{A_{l}}\left|U_{j}^{p-1}U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}.

It holds

(6.9)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}\left||PU_{i}-U_{i}|^{p-1}U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\leq c\int_{A_{l}}U_{i}^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\mu_{i\varepsilon}^{\frac{N-4}{2}(p-1)\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p\beta}{2}}),

where
\beta=\frac{2N}{N+4}
.
As for
\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{A_{l}}\left|U_{j}^{p-1}U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}
.

If
l\neq i
and
j=l
. The estimate of
\int_{A_{l}}|U_{l}^{p-1}U_{i}|^{\beta}
is a direct result of Lemma
6.5
.

If
l\neq i
and
j\neq l
. Using Hölder inequality, we have

(6.10)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}|U_{j}^{p-1}U_{i}|^{\beta}\leq\left(\int_{A_{l}}U_{i}^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{N-4}{N+4}}\left(\int_{A_{l}}U_{j}^{p+1}\right)^{\frac{8}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{N\theta}{2k}})=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p\beta}{2}}).

Thus by Lemma
6.5
, (
6
)-(
6.10
), we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{A_{l}}\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p\beta}{2}})\text{ for }l\neq i.

If
l=i
, we obtain

(6.11)

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}}\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}\leq c\int_{A_{i}}\left||PU_{i}-U_{i}|U_{i}^{p-1}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}+c\sum_{j\neq i}\int_{A_{i}}\Big|U_{i}^{p-1}U_{j}\Big|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}.

As the proof of Lemma
6.4
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i}}\left||PU_{i}-U_{i}|U_{i}^{p-1}\right|^{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p\beta}{2}}).

Therefore, it follows that
\left|[f^{\prime}(U_{i})-f^{\prime}(V)]U_{i}\right|_{\frac{2N}{N+4}}=O(\varepsilon^{\frac{(N-4)\theta}{2k}\frac{p}{2}})
by Lemma
6.5
. Similarly, we can get the results for
5\leq N\leq 11
and
N=12
.
∎

Homological vector fields over differentiable stacks
Daniel Álvarez, Miquel Cueca

Abstract
. In this work we solve the problem of providing a Morita invariant definition of Lie and Courant algebroids over Lie groupoids. By relying on supergeometry, we view these structures as instances of vector fields on graded groupoids which are homological up to homotopy. We describe such vector fields in general from two complementary viewpoints: firstly, as Maurer-Cartan elements in a differential graded Lie algebra of multivector fields and, secondly, we also view them from a categorical approach, in terms of functors and natural transformations. Thereby, we obtain a unifying conceptual framework for studying LA-groupoids, L 2 L_{2} -algebroids (including semistrict Lie 2-algebras and 2-term representations up to homotopy), infinitesimal gerbe prequantizations, higher gauge theory (specifically, 2-connections on 2-bundles), quasi-Poisson groupoids and (twisted) multiplicative Courant algebroids.

1.
Introduction

It is usually desirable in both mathematics and physics to describe geometric structures in a flexible enough manner that they can be transported under certain change of frame of reference operations. More specifically, whenever we want to define a geometric structure over a differentiable stack, understood as a class of
weakly equivalent
Lie groupoids
[
13
]
, we have to make sure it is such that it can be transported under Morita equivalences. It has been realized that the appropriate way to do so is by providing definitions that rely on homotopies or natural isomorphisms instead of equalities. In this context, the program of
categorifying
several fundamental concepts is particularly relevant: see
[
10
,
9
,
8
,
11
,
16
,
96
]

for a discussion of the ideas of Lie group, Lie algebra, principal bundle, parallel transport and Poisson and symplectic manifolds in this spirit.

For Lie algebras, in particular, two generalizations naturally appeared: (1) one can define skew-symmetric brackets that satisfy the Jacobi identity
up to chain homotopy
in a complex of vector spaces, this is the idea behind
L_{\infty}
-algebras; (2) on the other hand, we can think of a categorified Lie algebra as a category itself which is endowed with a Lie bracket that is a functor and that satisfies the Jacobi identity
up to a natural isomorphism
. Both approaches were illustrated and contrasted in
[
9
]
.

In this work we further develop this program by examining the idea of
Lie algebroid
. Defining Lie algebroids up to homotopy as
L_{2}
-algebroids (or split degree 2
Q
-manifolds) is straightforward, see for instance
[
15
,
88
]
. On the other hand, a partial categorification of Lie algebroids was achieved by the introduction of LA-groupoids
[
63
]
; however, the properties of such objects are still characterized by equalities instead of natural isomorphisms and so they are not Morita invariant. Notwithstanding this fact, there have been some attempts to use LA-groupoids to define Lie algebroids over differentiable stacks
[
83
,
94
]
(see also
[
14
]
for a study of Lie algebroids and stacks in the algebraic category).

The goal of this work is to unify the study of those two classes of objects in a way that sheds light on many concrete examples that have appeared in the literature. In doing so we aim to give a framework which is concrete enough to perform explicit computations and yet is sufficiently general to explain and put into a suitable context a number of results and constructions about homotopic or categorified notions in classical differential geometry. Our perspective encompasses and clarifies diverse notions of Lie bialgebroids
[
12
,
24
,
36
,
55
]
, the emergence of Lie 2-algebra structures on the spaces of multiplicative sections of LA-groupoids and quasi Poisson groupoids
[
34
,
57
,
80
]
, the definition of a flat 2-connection on a 2-bundle in higher gauge theory
[
8
,
11
,
18
]
, and Courant algebroids over groupoids
[
2
,
72
,
79
]
(possibly twisted as in
[
45
]
).

Our interpretation of
L_{2}
-algebroids in categorical terms
sheds light on the integration of 2-term representations up to homotopy via 2-functors
[
17
]
(the version of Lie’s second theorem relevant for this interpretation is discussed in
[
97
]
).
We plan to examine these global aspects of the theory further in future work.

1.1.
Summary

Due to the length of this work, we include here a brief description of our contributions. Over the last few decades the language of supergeometry has led to the unified study of many different geometric structures such as Poisson manifolds
[
86
]
, Lie algebroids
[
90
]
, Lie bialgebroids and Courant algebroids
[
85
]
; all these objects can be viewed as
Q
-manifolds
, i.e.
\mathbb{N}
-graded manifolds endowed with a vector field
Q
that squares to zero; in this situation,
Q
is called a
Q
-structure or a homological vector field.

It has long been realized that Lie groupoids are very convenient models for differentiable stacks, see
[
13
]
and compare it with the original algebraic approach of
[
6
]
. In the light of this, we can define a
graded differentiable stack
, i.e. a smooth stack in the category of
\mathbb{N}
-graded manifolds, as the Morita equivalence class of a graded Lie groupoid as in
[
70
,
72
]
.
So the first question that we address is the following

(Q1)

What is a homological vector field on a graded differentiable stack?

We give an answer to this question in §
2
by recalling the general definition of graded groupoids as in
[
70
,
72
]
and introducing degree 1 multiplicative vector fields
Q
over them that are homological
up to homotopy
in the sense that
Q^{2}
is equal to a simplicially exact term. This is in contrast with the
Q
-groupoids introduced in
[
70
,
72
]
where
Q^{2}
is required to be equal to 0. The resulting condition immediately characterizes
Q
as part of a Maurer-Cartan element in the dgla of multiplicative multivector fields on a groupoid (see
\lx@sectionsign\ref{sec:MC}
) and hence we get that it is a structure that can be transported along Morita equivalences, providing thus the appropriate concept of
Q
-structure over a differentiable stack. We call these objects
quasi
Q
-groupoids
(Definition
2.8
).

We also characterize the above Maurer-Cartan elements as natural transformations in §
2.3.1
. This equivalent viewpoint allows us to view these objects as genuine categorifications of
Q
-manifolds, in the same way that Lie 2-algebras are a categorification of Lie algebras
[
9
]
. This categorical viewpoint naturally leads to our definition of morphism between quasi
Q
-groupoids in §
2.5
. Let us just point out that this concept extends the idea of gauge transformation between Maurer-Cartan elements and includes morphisms of semistrict Lie 2-algebras
[
9
]
as particular examples.

Poisson structures have been frequently studied in the presence of a homological vector field, most famously under the guise of
QP
-manifolds

[
1
]
. So another closely related question to (
Q1
) is the following:

(Q2)

What is a
QP
manifold structure on a graded differentiable stack?

We give an answer to this question in Definition
2.19
, drawing inspiration from homotopy and shifted Poisson structures
[
16
,
73
,
81
]
. We pay particular emphasis to non degenerate Poisson structures as that situation is what allows us to consider some of the most interesting examples as we shall see below.

In the rest of the paper we specialize our answers to (
Q1
) and (
Q2
) in many situations. Since Lie algebroid structures are equivalent to degree 1
Q
-manifolds
[
90
]
, for graded groupoids of degree 1, (
Q1
) becomes the following question:

(Q3)

What is a Lie algebroid over a differentiable stack?

Our answer to this question in §
3
specializes, on one hand, to LA-groupoids
[
63
]
viewed as
Q
-groupoids
[
70
]
and, on the other, to
L_{2}
-algebroids, see Theorem
3.15
. We refer to the categorified Lie algebroids thus obtained as
quasi LA-groupoids
. Our viewpoint allows us to consider many examples: in fact, we show that quasi LA-groupoids appear as central extensions determined by closed 1-shifted 2-forms and quasi Poisson groupoids structures, see §
3.6.2
. Furthermore, we explain how 2-term representations up to homotopy §
3.6.4
and flat 2-connections are examples of quasi LA-groupoid morphisms §
3.6.5
.

Lie algebroid structures are in duality with respect to linear Poisson structures. In §
3.2.1
we promote that relationship to a duality between our quasi LA-groupoids and linear quasi Poisson structures
[
50
]
. Theorem
3.6
generalizes the main result of
[
57
]
and allows us to study the categories of multiplicative sections of such objects in a straightforward manner; as an application, we obtain the Morita invariance of such categories in §
3.4
(which was proven for LA-groupoids in
[
80
]
). We also use the duality thus established to develop the Lie theory of quasi LA-groupoids, following the differentiation and integration results for LA-groupoids discussed in
[
23
]
. Moreover, we also treat
L_{2}
-algebroids, generalizing the equivalence proven in
[
9
]
between Lie 2-algebras and 2-term
L_{\infty}
-algebras, see §
3.3
.

Question (
Q3
) naturally leads to a subquestion:

(Q4)

What is a Lie bialgebroid over a differentiable stack?

Since a Lie bialgebroid can be described as a homological vector field together with a compatible Poisson bracket, our answer to (
Q2
) leads naturally to an answer to (
Q4
) and hence to the study of
multiplicative Lie bialgebroids
§
3.5
. As we pointed out before, we obtain then a clear general picture for a number of constructions in
[
12
,
24
,
36
,
55
]
. In particular, when the relevant Poisson bracket is symplectic, our multiplicative Lie bialgebroids
become the cotangent groupoids of quasi Poisson groupoids §
3.6.1
. Cotangents of quasi Poisson groupoids are then categorified
QP
-manifolds of degree 1. As a result, we clarify some of the observations in
[
34
]
about the presence of a Lie 2-algebra structure on the category of multiplicative 1-forms of a quasi Poisson groupoid.

In §
4
we study quasi
Q
-groupoids of degree two, answering the questions

(Q5)

What is a Courant algebroid over a differentiable stack?

(Q6)

What is the double of a multiplicative Lie bialgebroid?

The work in this section then promotes the ideas of
[
61
,
84
]
to the setting of differentiable stacks. Thus our definition of
quasi CA-groupoids
can be regarded as a categorification of the idea of
QP
-manifolds of degree 2 and it includes as examples exact (twisted) CA-groupoids as in
[
2
,
72
,
79
]
and also the doubles of the multiplicative Lie bialgebroids described in §
3.5
.

In Appendix §
A
we include a description of double quasi Poisson groupoids which are some of the global counterparts of the multiplicative Lie bialgebroids of §
3.5
. In this regard, we generalize the correspondence between quasi-Manin triples of Lie 2-algebras and quasi Poisson 2-groups
[
35
]
by showing that quasi Poisson LA-groupoids over a unit manifold are indeed integrable to a double quasi Poisson groupoid under natural conditions.

1.2.
Future outlook

The work presented here motivates a number of questions that mostly have to do with the global aspects of the theory. Now we shall briefly elaborate on this.

Our concept of quasi-LA groupoids should be well suited to formulate the integrability condition of quasi-Nijenhuis tensors over Lie groupoids, see
[
27
, Remark 6.4]
. In particular, one should be able to define quasi-complex structures over Lie groupoids using these objects, thus providing a suitable concept of complex structure on a differentiable stack. In the same spirit, one can use the quasi-CA groupoids that we define here as background for generalized complex structures up to homotopy. We hope to analyse these ideas in particular examples in the future.

The notions introduced here can be significantly generalized. In fact, it is natural to consider homological vector fields over graded differential higher (and derived) stacks. To do so one can work with graded simplicial manifolds and shifted multivector fields as in
[
81
]
. Notably, this would allow us to include
L_{\infty}
-algebroids in full generality instead of their truncated versions. Also,
n
-shifted Poisson and symplectic structures should give other natural examples of these higher Lie algebroids via central extensions as in §
3.6.2
. However, working in such generality makes difficult to obtain explicit examples and formulae which was one of our objectives in this project.

In a different direction, one can try to find the global counterparts of quasi
Q
-groupoids and their morphisms. As conceptual guidance for this integration problem, one can look at three different particular cases. (1) Firstly, we have the integration of representations up to homotopy
[
4
,
17
]
. Since 2-term representations up to homotopy can be viewed as an example of quasi LA-groupoid morphism, there should be a general relationship between the integration of quasi LA-groupoids morphisms and two-dimensional holonomy, as suggested by the results in
[
5
,
17
,
69
]
. We believe that a better understanding of this relationship would allow us to study Wilson surface observables
[
69
]
in greater generality and also in contexts more closely related to (higher) symplectic structures. (2) Secondly, it is well known that double Lie groupoids are the integrations of LA-groupoids
[
63
]
but there has been little progress integrating these objects, see
[
89
]
. We hope that combining our more flexible concept of morphism for (quasi) LA-groupoids with Sullivan’s spatial realization (as applied in
[
88
]
) can shed some light on this problem. (3) Finally, we expect that integrating quasi LA-groupoids over unit groupoids should produce stacky Lie groupoids as in
[
98
]
.

On a related note, one can try to quantize the categorified
QP
-manifolds described here. In other words, it seems interesting to study the AKSZ construction
[
1
]
but adapted to the setting of graded differentiable stacks, in the spirit of
[
29
]
. In particular, we expect that categorifying the usual Poisson sigma model should give us a way of quantizing and integrating quasi Poisson groupoids, in the same way that Poisson manifolds are quantized and integrated in
[
30
,
31
]
. We hope that progress in this direction can also shed light on the classical AKSZ sigma models from a global perspective.

2.
Quasi Q-groupoids

In this section we introduce quasi
Q
-groupoids, a generalization of the
Q
-groupoids introduced in
[
72
]
. We approach these objects from two complementary viewpoints: one in terms of functors and natural transformations, the other one, in terms of Maurer-Cartan elements. This last description allows us to study their compatibility with multiplicative homotopy Poisson structures. We conclude this section by showing that quasi
Q
-groupoids form a strict
2
-category.

As motivation for the general theory of graded manifolds, let us emphasize its remarkable power to express classical differential geometric ideas in a concise and conceptual manner, see §
3.1
and §
4.1
for the respective applications of supergeometry to Lie and Courant algebroids.

2.1.
Graded manifolds

Given a non-negative integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{V}=\oplus_{i=1}^{n}V_{i}
be a graded vector space.
An
\mathbb{N}
-manifold of degree
n
(or simply an
n
-manifold
) is a ringed space
\mathcal{M}=(M,C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}))
, where
M
is a smooth manifold and
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
is a sheaf of graded commutative algebras such that, for all
x\in M
, there exists a neighborhood
U
and an isomorphism

(1)

C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})|_{U}\cong C^{\infty}(U)\otimes\sym\mathbf{V}

of sheaves of graded commutative algebras, where
\sym\mathbf{V}
denotes the graded symmetric algebra of
\mathbf{V}
. The manifold
M
is known as the
body
of
\mathcal{M}
. A global section of
C^{i}(\mathcal{M})
is called a
homogeneous function of degree
i
; we write
|f|=i
for the degree of
f
.

A morphism of
n
-manifolds
\Psi:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is a degree preserving morphism of ringed spaces, given by a pair
\Psi=(\psi,\psi^{\sharp})
, where
\psi:M\to N
is a smooth map and
\psi^{\sharp}:C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{N})\to\psi_{*}C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
is a degree preserving morphism of sheaves of algebras over
N
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n
-manifolds with their morphisms form a category that we denote by
n\text{-}\mathcal{M}an
. Now we collect some basic examples, for more details about
\mathbb{N}
-manifolds see e.g.
[
32
,
70
,
72
,
85
]
.

Example 2.1

(1-manifolds)
.

Given a vector bundle
E\to M
, we can define the
1
-manifold

E[1]=(M,\Gamma(\wedge^{\bullet}E^{*})).

Recall that any
1
-manifold is canonically of this type, see e.g.
[
15
]
.
Two important examples of 1-manifolds are

T[1]M=(M,\Omega^{\bullet}(M))\qquad\text{and}\qquad T^{*}[1]M=(M,\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)),

where
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)
are the sheaves of differential forms and multivector fields, respectively.

Example 2.2

(Shifted tangent bundles of
n
-manifolds)
.

One can generalize the shifted tangent bundles of ordinary manifolds considered in
Example
2.1
to arbitrary
n
-manifolds
\mathcal{M}=(M,C_{\mathcal{M}})
. Rather than introducing
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
(see e.g.

[
70
]
), we will give a description using charts and coordinates as in
[
85
]
.

Let
U
be a chart of
\mathcal{M}
with coordinates
\{x^{\alpha},e^{\beta}\}
,
so that
\{x^{\alpha}\}
are coordinates on
U

and
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})|_{U}=C^{\infty}(U)\otimes\sym\mathbf{V}
with
\mathbf{V}=\langle e^{\beta}\rangle
.
Just as for ordinary smooth manifolds, we can introduce the differentials of the coordinates,
\{dx^{\alpha},de^{\beta}\}
, with the transformation rule

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{x}^{\alpha}=&F^{\alpha}(x),\\
\widehat{e}^{\beta}=&F^{\beta}(x,e)\end{array}\right.\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d\widehat{x}^{\alpha}=&\frac{\partial F^{\alpha}(x)}{\partial x^{\alpha^{\prime}}}dx^{\alpha^{\prime}},\\
d\widehat{e}^{\beta}=&\frac{\partial F^{\beta}(x,e)}{\partial x^{\alpha}}dx^{\alpha}+\frac{\partial F^{\beta}(x,e)}{\partial e^{\beta^{\prime}}}dx^{\beta^{\prime}}.\end{array}\right.

For
k\geq 1
we define the
(n+k)
-manifold
T[k]\mathcal{M}=(M,C_{T[k]\mathcal{M}})
by

C^{\bullet}(T[k]\mathcal{M})|_{U}=C^{\infty}(U)\otimes\sym\mathbf{W}\quad\text{with}\quad\mathbf{W}=\mathbf{V}\oplus\langle dx^{\alpha},de^{\beta}\;;\;|dx^{\alpha}|=k,\ |de^{\beta}|=|e^{\beta}|+k\rangle.

Example 2.3

(Vector and multivector fields)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
n
-manifold. A
vector field of degree
k
on
\mathcal{M}

is a degree
k
derivation
X
of the graded algebra
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
, i.e., an

\mathbb{R}
-linear map
X:C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})\to C^{\bullet+k}(\mathcal{M})
with the property that, for all

f,g\in C_{\mathcal{M}}
with
f
homogeneous,
|X(f)|=|f|+k
and

(2)

X(fg)=X(f)g+(-1)^{|f|k}fX(g).

Vector fields give rise to a sheaf of
C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
-modules over
M
. The sheaf of degree

k
vector fields is denoted by
\mathfrak{X}^{1,k}(\mathcal{M})
. The graded commutator of vector
fields, defined for homogeneous vector fields
X,Y
by

[X,Y]=XY-(-1)^{|X||Y|}YX,

makes
\mathfrak{X}^{1,\bullet}(\mathcal{M})
into a sheaf of graded Lie algebras. A degree
1
vector field on an
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
,
Q\in\mathfrak{X}^{1,1}(\mathcal{M}),
such that

[Q,Q]=2Q^{2}=0

is
called a
homological vector field
.
Note that, in this case,
(C^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}),Q)
is a differential complex, justifying
the terminology. The pair
(\mathcal{M},Q)
will be referred as a
Q
-manifold
.

Till now we just defined vector fields on an
n
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
. However, we also need to introduce
multivector fields
. They are the elements of the graded symmetric algebra

\Big(\bigoplus_{i}\sym^{i}\mathfrak{X}^{1,\bullet}(\mathcal{M})=\mathfrak{X}^{i,\bullet}(\mathcal{M}),\wedge\Big)\quad\text{with}\quad X\wedge Y=(-1)^{1\cdot 1+|X||Y|}Y\wedge X\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}^{1,\bullet}(\mathcal{M}).

If we extend the Lie bracket using the Leibniz rule, this algebra carries a Poisson bracket of bi-degree
(-1,0)
that we denote by
[\cdot,\cdot]
and it is known as the
Schouten bracket
.

\begin{split}(q^{\prime})^{l}-(q^{\prime})^{r}=&Q^{\prime}\circ Q^{\prime}=Q^{\prime}\circ(b^{l}-b^{r}+Q)=Q^{\prime}\circ(b^{l}-b^{r})+(b^{l}-b^{r}+Q)\circ Q\\
=&Q^{\prime}\circ(b^{l}-b^{r})+(b^{l}-b^{r})\circ Q+q^{l}-q^{r}.\end{split}

the horizontal multiplication

of natural transformations corresponds to composition of vector fields while the vertical multiplication

\begin{split}(q^{\prime})^{l}-(q^{\prime})^{r}=&(b^{l}-b^{r}+Q)\circ(b^{l}-b^{r})+(b^{l}-b^{r})\circ Q+q^{l}-q^{r}\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}[b,b]^{l}-[b^{r},b^{l}]+\frac{1}{2}[b,b]^{r}+[Q,b^{l}]-[Q,b^{r}]+q^{l}-q^{r};\end{split}

meaning that

∎

3.6.2.

Recall that
(\mathbb{R}\rightrightarrows 0,*\rightrightarrows*)

-groupoid structures on this product.

Proposition 3.26
.

Let
(H[1]\rightrightarrows E[1],Q,q)
be a degree
1
quasi
Q
-groupoid,
h\in C^{2}_{mul}(H[1])
and
\xi\in C^{3}(E[1])
. If

(37)

Q(h)=\mathtt{s}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi-\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi\quad\text{and}\quad Q(\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi)=\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}Q_{0}(\xi)=q^{r}(h)

then
(H[1]\times\mathbb{R}[1]\rightrightarrows E[1],\widehat{Q},\widehat{q})
is a quasi

-groupoid with

\begin{split}\widehat{Q}(h_{0}+\lambda h_{1})=&Q(h_{0})+h\wedge h_{1}-\lambda Q(h_{1}),\\

where
h_{0},h_{1}\in C^{\bullet}(H[1])
and
\lambda
is the degree
1

\mathbb{R}[1].

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{A}_{H[1]\times\mathbb{R}[1]}=\mathcal{A}_{H[1]}\times\mathbb{R}[1]

\lambda
denote the degree

\mathbb{R}[1]
then

and a degree

Notice that

is a quasi

-structure satisfying

Hence, to show the result we just need to check how

\begin{split}\widehat{Q}^{2}(\lambda)&=Q(h)=\mathtt{s}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi-\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi=(\widehat{q}^{l}-\widehat{q}^{r})(\lambda).\end{split}

Corollary 3.27

(Classification of central extensions)
.

Let

be a degree

(1)

The choice of an splitting

makes the central extension isomorphic to the one constructed in Proposition

and

(2)

The central extensions defined by

and
(h^{\prime},\xi^{\prime})
are equivalent if and only if there is an element

such that
\xi-\xi^{\prime}=Q_{0}(b)
and
h-h^{\prime}=\mathtt{s}^{*}_{H[1]}b-\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}b.

Proof.

Observe that, since
\lambda
is a multiplicative function, then

and

makes sense and so

Q(h)=Q(\sigma^{*}\widehat{Q}(\lambda))=\sigma^{*}\widehat{Q}^{2}(\lambda)=\sigma^{*}(\widehat{q}^{l}-\widehat{q}^{r})(\lambda)=\mathtt{s}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi-\mathtt{t}^{*}_{H[1]}\xi,

4.1.

In this subsection we summarize some well known facts about degree

The most important examples of degree 2 manifolds are arguably the ones induced by Courant algebroids. Recall that a
Courant algebroid
is a vector bundle

equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, a bundle map
\rho:E\to TM
, and a bracket
\llbracket\cdot,\cdot\rrbracket
such that

(1)

\llbracket e_{1},fe_{2}\rrbracket=\rho(e_{1})(f)e_{2}+f\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket

(2)

\rho(e_{1})(\langle e_{2},e_{3}\rangle)=\langle\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket,e_{3}\rangle+\langle e_{2},\llbracket e_{1},e_{3}\rrbracket\rangle
,

(3)

\llbracket e_{1},\llbracket e_{2},e_{3}\rrbracket\rrbracket=\llbracket\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket,e_{3}\rrbracket+\llbracket e_{2},\llbracket e_{1},e_{3}\rrbracket\rrbracket
,

(4)

\llbracket e_{1},e_{2}\rrbracket+\llbracket e_{2},e_{1}\rrbracket=\mathcal{D}\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle
,

for all

and
e_{i}\in\Gamma(E)
, where
\mathcal{D}:C^{\infty}(M)\to\Gamma(E)
is defined by

\langle\mathcal{D}f,e\rangle=\rho(e)(f).

With this definition we can state
[

, Theorem 4.5]
as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

There is a one to one correspondence between:

(1)

equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form

and degree
2
symplectic manifolds.

(2)

-manifolds.

5.
Epilogue: Lie and Courant algebroids through the stacky glasses

In the course of this work we have described several structures on (graded) Lie groupoids and we have shown that they can be transported along (and compared using) Morita equivalences. This allows us to re-interpret our previous results in terms of differentiable stacks.

Reciprocity for Kato-Saito idele class group with modulus
Rahul Gupta, Amalendu Krishna

Abstract




2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 14G15; Secondary 14F35, 19F05

Abstract.

We introduce an étale fundamental group with modulus and construct a
reciprocity homomorphism from the Kato-Saito idele class group with
modulus to this fundamental group. This is the
K
-theoretic
analogue of the reciprocity for the cycle-theoretic
idele class group with modulus due to Kerz-Saito. It plays a central role in
showing the isomorphism between the two idele class groups and in proving
Bloch’s formula for the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The aim of the class field theory in the geometric case is the description of
the abelian étale coverings of a finite type scheme over a finite field
in terms of some arithmetic or geometric invariants (such as the Chow groups of
0-cycles) intrinsically associated to the scheme.
For smooth projective schemes, this was achieved by Kato and Saito

[
20
]
who showed that for such a scheme

X
, there is an injective reciprocity map

{\rm CH}_{0}(X)\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X)
which has dense image.
If
X
is smooth but not projective over a finite field, the
class field theory is a much more intricate problem.

Let
X
be an integral normal projective scheme of dimension

d\geq 1
over a finite field
k
and let
U\subset X
be a regular dense
open subscheme.
In order to establish the class field theory for
U
, Kato and Saito

[
21
]
used a
K
-theoretic
invariant
C(U)
which is the limit of the
Nisnevich cohomology groups
H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})
as
D
runs through
all closed subschemes of
X
which are supported on
X\setminus U
.
The main result of
[
21
]
is that there is a reciprocity
homomorphism
\rho\colon C(U)\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)
which is an isomorphism upon the
profinite completion of
C(U)
. In particular,
\rho
induces an isomorphism between
the degree zero subgroups
C(U)_{0}
and
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)_{0}
(see sections

1.5
and
4.4
for definitions of degree zero subgroups).
The cohomology
H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})
has been known as the
‘Kato-Saito idele class group with modulus’ of the pair
(X,D)
.

Unfortunately,
C(U)
and the Kato-Saito idele class group
are difficult objects to understand and compute as
they are made up of cohomology of
K
-theory sheaves.
Until now, there was no version of the fundamental group with modulus which
could be directly related to the Kato-Saito idele class group.

Few years ago,
Kerz and Saito
[
25
]
made a new breakthrough
in the class field theory for
U
. They
discovered the notion of Chow group with
modulus
{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)
and constructed a reciprocity homomorphism
from this group to a quotient
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
of
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)
which
classifies abelian covers of
U
with ramification bounded by
D
in the
sense of Deligne and Laumon
[
28
]
. The main result of

[
25
]
is that there is an injective reciprocity homomorphism

\rho^{\prime}_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
with dense image
if
{\rm Char}(k)\neq 2
. A much simplified proof
was recently found by Binda-Krishna-Saito
[
4
]
without any
restriction on
{\rm Char}(k)
.
Taking the limit over
D
, this provides a cycle-theoretic (analogous
to
[
20
]
) solution to the class field theory for
U
.

Now, one of the main questions in the ramified class field theory that
remains to be settled is whether the solutions
provided by Kato-Saito
[
21
]
and Kerz-Saito
[
25
]

are equivalent when
X
is regular.
It is worth noting here that the Kato-Saito idele class group
is a much more subtle invariant
than the cycle-theoretic idele class group because its study involves
understanding the ramification theory of Galois
extensions of discrete valuation fields with imperfect residue
fields.

In order to answer the above question,
one needs to address the following precise question.

Question 1.1
.

Given an effective Cartier divisor
D\subset X
, is there an isomorphism

cyc_{X|D}\colon{\rm CH}_{0}(X|D)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})

such that upon taking the limit over
D
supported away from
U
, one has
that
\varprojlim\ \rho^{\prime}_{X|D}=\rho\circ(\varprojlim\ cyc_{X|D})
?

A positive answer to the above question will not only make the
class field theories of Kato-Saito and Kerz-Saito equivalent,
it will simultaneously establish
Bloch’s formula for the Chow group of 0-cycles with modulus. The latter is
an essential
tool for connecting cycles with modulus to algebraic
K
-theory.
The present work is the first of the two papers (see
[
13
]
)
which together positively settle this question.

1.2.
Main result

The main obstacle in solving Question
1.1
is that
unlike
\rho^{\prime}
, the reciprocity map
\rho
does not a priori
descend to a reciprocity map
\rho_{X|D}\colon H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
for a fixed divisor
D
.
In order to get around this problem, we introduce a new étale
fundamental group with modulus called
\pi^{\rm adiv}_{1}(X,D)
in this paper.
The major contribution of this paper is to show that
\rho
descends to a reciprocity homomorphism

\rho_{X|D}\colon H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})\to\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D).

In
[
13
]
, we shall show that
\rho_{X|D}
is injective with
dense image. We shall also show that there is an
isomorphism
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\cong\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
which is compatible
with
\rho_{X|D}
and
\rho^{\prime}_{X|D}
. This solves
Question
1.1
. This also provides a new
interpretation of the already known group
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
, and is
expected to play a key role in ramification theory.

In precise terms, the main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be an integral normal scheme which is finite
type over a finite field and has dimension
d\geq 1
.
Let
D\subset X
be a closed subscheme
of pure codimension one such that
U=X\setminus D
is regular.
Then
there exists a continuous reciprocity homomorphism

\rho_{X|D}\colon H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})\to\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)

whose image is dense.
Moreover, upon taking the limit over
D
supported on
X\setminus U
, we have

\varprojlim\ \rho_{X|D}=\rho\colon C(U)\to\pi_{1}^{\rm ab}(U)
.

1.3.
Comparison of Theorem
1.2
with class field
theory of Kerz-Zhao

In
[
27
]
,
Kerz and Zhao consider a variant of the Kato-Saito idele class group with modulus
(the source of
\rho_{X|D}
in Theorem
1.2
) when
X
is smooth and
the support of
D
is a strict normal crossing divisor. They denote this
variant by
H^{d}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D})
. Their main result is that
there is a canonical isomorphism
{H^{d}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D})}/m\xrightarrow{\cong}{H^{d}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D})}/m
(see
[
27
, Corollary 3.3.4]
).
Kerz and Zhao define their étale fundamental group with modulus
with finite coefficients
{C(X,D)}/m
as
{H^{d}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D})}/m

so that one gets
{H^{d}_{{\rm nis}}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D})}/m\cong{C(X,D)}/m

(see
[
27
, Definition 3.3.7, Corollary 3.3.8]
). This isomorphism
appears similar to Theorem
1.2
, but it is actually different
as we explain below.

First, we should note that the sheaf
{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,X|D}
is different from

{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)}
, is usually much simpler to deal with, and is useful
when
X
is smooth and the support of
D
is a strict normal crossing divisor.
Hence, the class group
with modulus used by Kerz-Zhao is in general different from the Kato-Saito idele
class group. Second, Kerz and Zhao define their
étale fundamental group with modulus as a sheaf cohomology and they do not
explain if it describes finite étale coverings of
X\setminus D
with
certain ramifications along
D
. On the other hand, the group
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)

used in Theorem
1.2
is tailor-made to describe such coverings.
Third, Kerz and Zhao work with finite coefficients unlike Theorem
1.2
, which
is an integral statement. Finally, Kerz and Zhao work under a strong assumption
that
X
is smooth and the support of
D
is a strict normal crossing divisor.
Theorem
1.2
makes none of these assumptions.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

We give a brief outline of this paper.
Since the cohomology group
H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})
is very difficult
to understand, we use a recent result of Kerz
[
24
]
in which he
defines a more explicit idele class group
C(X,D)
and shows that it
coincides with
H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D)})
under the hypothesis of
Theorem
1.2
. That one could use
C(X,D)
instead of
the Kato-Saito idele class group is a key observation in our construction.
Kato’s theory of reciprocity for higher local fields and the technique of Parshin chains
allow one us to define a reciprocity map for
C(X,D)
under a weaker hypothesis than
in Theorem
1.2
. Its construction goes into the
following steps.

In §
2
, we recall the theory of Parshin chains and their
Milnor
K
-theory. In the next two sections, we recall the definition of

C(X,D)
from
[
24
]
and prove several properties such as
functoriality and continuity. In §
5
, we construct the
reciprocity for the limit group
C_{U/X}
using Kato’s reciprocity for
higher local fields. In §
7
, we introduce our
new étale fundamental group with modulus called
\pi^{\rm adiv}_{1}(X,D)
and
prove several properties. In §
8
, we prove our main
result using the ramification theories of Abbes-Saito
[
1
]
,
Kato
[
19
]
and Matsuda
[
29
]
. The required results from
ramification theory are proven in §
6
.

. For most parts, this

be the

for some integer

.
A

will usually
mean a separated and essentially of finite type

We shall denote the category of such schemes by

.
If

.

, we shall let

.

For a morphism

For a point

If

is

(resp.

.

For a field

.
For a field

For a Galois extension (possibly infinite)

.
We shall let

.

For a Noetherian scheme

Parshin chains and their Milnor

As mentioned previously, the key idea for constructing the reciprocity

C(X,D)
.
We shall therefore work with the latter group in most parts of this paper.
In this section, we recall Parshin chains and their Milnor
K
-groups
from
[
21

. We also recall the topologies
on these Milnor
K
-groups which will play a very useful role in this
paper. Ideally, this section should have been shorter as it is
mostly recollection from
[
21
]
. But we noticed that apart from the
new lemmas that we prove in this section, many of the
terms of this section are technical for a general reader
and are very heavily used throughout this paper as
well as in our follow-up papers
[
13
]
,
[
14
]

and
[
15
]
.
We therefore decided to write these down in one section instead of

[

2.1.
The dimension function

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme of Krull dimension
d(X)
.
A dimension function on
X
is an assignment

d_{X}\colon X\to{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
which satisfies the catenary condition:
if
x\in\overline{\{y\}}
is a point of codimension one
(
\text{\rm dim}({\mathcal{O}}_{\overline{\{y\}},x})=1
), then
d_{X}(y)=d_{X}(x)+1
.
We let
d_{m}(X)={\rm min}\{d_{X}(x)|x\in X\}

and
X_{i}=\{x\in X|d_{X}(x)=i+d_{m}(X)\}
for
i\geq 0
.
Note that an excellent scheme is necessarily Noetherian and
a Noetherian scheme with a dimension function is
necessarily catenary.

We let
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{\rm dim}}
be the category of pairs
(X,d_{X})
, where
X
is an
excellent integral scheme endowed with a dimension function
d_{X}
.
A morphism in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\text{\rm dim}}
is a quasi-finite morphism of
schemes
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
such that
d_{X^{\prime}}=d_{X}\circ f
. In this case,
we say that
d_{X^{\prime}}
is a pull-back of (or induced by)
d_{X}
.

Example 2.1
.

We give an example which we shall use very often in this paper.
Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme. We let

d_{X}(x)
be the Krull-dimension of
\overline{\{x\}}
.
Since
X
is catenary (because it is excellent),
it follows that
d_{X}
is a dimension function on
X
.
We call it the canonical dimension function on
X
.
Here,
d_{m}(X)=0
.

We now let
P\in X
be a point of codimension
r\geq 0

and let
X_{P}={\rm Spec\,}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P})
. We let
x_{P}
denote the closed
point of
X_{P}
.
We have the canonical morphisms of schemes

X_{P}\setminus\{x_{P}\}\hookrightarrow X_{P}\to X
. We endow
X_{P}
and

X_{P}\setminus\{x_{P}\}
with the
dimension function induced by that of
X
.
Then we have

d_{m}(X_{P})=d(X)-r=d_{X}(P),\ d(X_{P})=r,

\ d_{m}(X_{P}\setminus\{x_{P}\})=d_{m}(X_{P})+1,\ d(X_{P}\setminus\{x_{P}\})=d(X_{P})-1=r-1.

2.2.
Parshin chains

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme with a dimension
function
d_{X}
.
Let
C\subset X
be a reduced nowhere dense
closed subscheme of
X
and let
U=X\setminus C
. Let
K
denote the function field of
X
.
We recall the following from
[
21
, § 1.6]

(or
[
24
, Definition 3.1]
).

Definition 2.2
.

(1)

An
s
-chain on
X
is a sequence of points
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})

on
X
such that
\overline{\{p_{i}\}}\subset\overline{\{p_{i+1}\}}
for

0\leq i\leq s-1
.

(2)

We say that
P
is a Parshin chain (
P
-chain) if
d_{X}({p_{i}})=i+d_{m}(X)

(equivalently,
p_{i}\in X_{i}
) for
0\leq i\leq s
.

(3)

A Parshin chain on the pair
(U\subset X)
is a Parshin chain

P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
on
X
with
s\geq 0

such that
p_{i}\in C
for
0\leq i<s
and
p_{s}\in U
.

(4)

The dimension of a chain
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
on
X
is
d_{X}(p_{s})
,
and it is denoted by
d_{X}(P)
.

(5)

We say that
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
is a maximal Parshin chain if
it is a Parshin chain such that

p_{s}=\eta
, where
\eta
is the generic point of
X
.

(6)

If
P
is a maximal Parshin chain and
D\subset X

is an effective Weil divisor with support
C
,
we let
m_{P}(D)
(often written only as
{\mathfrak{m}}_{P}
) denote the multiplicity of

\overline{\{p_{s-1}\}}
in
D
. Note that
m_{P}(D)=0
if
p_{s-1}\notin C
.

If
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
is an
s
-chain, we shall write

P^{\prime}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s-1})
. This convention will be followed
throughout the paper.

Note that a Parshin
0
-chain on the pair
(U\subset X)
is a point

p_{0}\in U
such that
d_{X}(p_{0})=d_{m}(X)
.
In particular, no such chain exists if
X
is local.
If
P_{1}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
is an
s
-chain and

P_{2}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s},p_{s+1},\ldots,p_{r})
is an
r
-chain,
then we say that
P_{2}
dominates
P_{1}
and write
P_{1}\leq P_{2}
.

Let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
be an
s
-chain on
X
. We define the
ring
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}
as follows.
We let
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,p_{0}}
be the henselization of
{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p_{0}}
.
Suppose now that
s\geq 1
and we have defined
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P^{\prime}}
.
We let
R={\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P^{\prime}}
and let
T
be the set of all
prime ideals of
R
lying over
p_{s}
under the canonical map

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p_{s-1}}\to R
. We let
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}={\underset{{\mathfrak{q}}\in T}{\prod}}R^{h}_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
. It is easily seen by induction on
s
that
T
is a finite set
(e.g., see
[
9
, Lemme 18.6.9.2]
).
We let
J^{h}_{X,P}
be the Jacobson radical of
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}
and

k(P)={{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}}/{J^{h}_{X,P}}
.
Then
k(P)
is the product of the residue fields of
R^{h}_{{\mathfrak{q}}}

(equivalently, of
R_{{\mathfrak{q}}}
), where

{\mathfrak{q}}
runs through
T
. Note that if
X
is regular at
p_{i}
for some

i\geq 0
, then
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}
is a regular semilocal ring.
We call
k(P)
the residue ring of the chain
P
. We write

X_{P}={\rm Spec\,}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
be an
s
-chain on
X
and let

Y=\overline{\{p_{s}\}}
.
Then
P
is an
s
-chain on
Y
and there are canonical isomorphisms

{{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}}/{J^{h}_{X,P}}\xrightarrow{\cong}{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{Y,P}\xrightarrow{\cong}k(P)
.

Proof.

There is nothing to prove if
s=0
, so we can assume that
s\geq 1
.
We write
P=(P^{\prime},p_{s})
. By replacing
p_{s}
by one of the
points in the inverse image of
p_{s}
under the canonical map
X_{P^{\prime}}\to X
,
we see that it suffices to prove the lemma when
X
is a Henselian
local scheme,
P=(p_{0},p_{1})
, where
p_{0}
is the closed point of
X

and
Y=\overline{\{p_{1}\}}
.

If
Y=X
, there is nothing to prove, so we can assume that

Y
has codimension at least one in
X
. In this case, the lemma
is equivalent to the statement that if
A
is a Henselian local ring
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset A
is a prime ideal of height at least one with

B={A}/{{\mathfrak{p}}}
, then there is an isomorphism

{(A_{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{h}}/{J^{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\cong Q(B)
, where
J^{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the maximal
ideal of
(A_{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{h}
.
However, this is clear using two simple observations.
First, the quotient map

A\twoheadrightarrow B
induces an isomorphism
{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}/{J_{\mathfrak{p}}}\xrightarrow{\cong}Q(B)
,
where
J_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the maximal ideal of
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Second,
the henselization map
A_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\to(A_{\mathfrak{p}})^{h}
induces an isomorphism
on the quotients
{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}/{J_{\mathfrak{p}}}\xrightarrow{\cong}{(A_{{\mathfrak{p}}})^{h}}/{J^{h}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
∎

Suppose that
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
is a maximal Parshin chain
and
R={\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P^{\prime}}
.
Then
R
is a finite product of one-dimensional reduced local rings
such that the canonical injection
R\hookrightarrow R_{n}
between reduced rings
induces a one-to-one correspondence between the minimal primes.
Furthermore,
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}
is the total quotient ring of
R

(and of
R_{n}
). In particular, the map
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}\twoheadrightarrow k(P)
is a
bijection and every factor of
k(P)
has a unique discrete valuation
whose ring of integers is the normalization of the quotient of
R
by
a unique minimal prime.
If

2.3.
Residue fields of Parshin chains via valuation rings

In order to study the reciprocity for the

We recall the machinery of doing this from

Let

whose value

.

Let

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

We now keep the notations of §

There are canonical isomorphisms

2.5.
Topologies on Milnor
K
-groups

Let us first assume that
R
is a one-dimensional
excellent regular semilocal integral domain with
Jacobson radical
{\mathfrak{m}}
and quotient field
F
.
We consider

a
topological group whose topology is the smallest
such that the subgroups
\{K^{M}_{n}(F,r)\}_{r\geq 0}
form a basis of
open neighborhoods of 0. This is called the canonical
topology of
K^{M}_{n}(F)
.

Let
R
now be a one-dimensional semilocal excellent integral domain with
fraction field
F
and normalization
R^{\prime}

(we have digressed from our notation
R_{n}
for normalization to
avoid a conflict).
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
denote the Jacobson radicals of

R
and
R^{\prime}
, respectively. The
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic topology of
R

induces a topology on
R^{\times}
generated by subgroups

U_{r}(R):=(1+{\mathfrak{m}}^{r})
with
r\geq 0
, where we let
1+{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}=R^{\times}
.
The same holds for
(R^{\prime})^{\times}
too.
Let us consider following topologies
\tau_{i}
on
K^{M}_{n}(F)
for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
We let
\tau_{1}
be the smallest topology
for which the fundamental
system of neighborhoods of 0 is given by the subgroups

\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}\subset K^{M}_{n}(F)
, where each
U_{i}

is open in
R^{\times}
.

We let
\tau_{2}
be the smallest topology
for which the fundamental
system of neighborhoods of 0 is given by the subgroups

\{1+I^{r},R^{\times},\ldots,R^{\times}\}\subset K^{M}_{n}(F)
, where
I\subset{\mathfrak{m}}
is an ideal such that
\sqrt{I}={\mathfrak{m}},\ r\geq 0
and

.
If

is either local (see
[
21
, Lemma 1.3.1]
)
or contains a field of cardinality at least three
(see
[

, Lemma 3.2]
), then

\{1+I^{r},R^{\times},\ldots,R^{\times}\}=K^{M}_{n}(R|I^{r})\subset K^{M}_{n}(F)
.
We let
\tau_{3}
be the smallest topology
for which the fundamental
system of neighborhoods of 0 is given by the subgroups

\{U_{r}(R),F^{\times},\ldots,F^{\times}\}\subset K^{M}_{n}(F)
, where

.

Lemma 2.6
.

Each of the topologies
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
and
\tau_{3}
coincides with
the canonical topology of
K^{M}_{n}(F)
.

Proof.

The assertion that
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{3}
coincide
follows from
[
18
, Proposition 2 (2)]
.
It is clear that every fundamental neighborhood of 0 in the
canonical topology (see §
2.4
)
contains a fundamental neighborhood in the

\tau_{1}
-topology. We prove the reverse inclusion.

Let
G
be the subgroup
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}\subset K^{M}_{n}(F)
,
where each
U_{i}
is open in
R^{\times}
. It follows from

[
18
, Proposition 2 (2)]
that

G
contains an open subgroup of the form
G_{1}=\{U_{1},F^{\times},\ldots,F^{\times}\}
, where
U_{1}\subset R^{\times}
is open.
Since the conductor ideal of the normalization map

contains an
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
-primary ideal, it follows that
U_{1}
contains an open
subgroup

of
the form

for some

. We are already done if

.
Otherwise, as
\{U_{2},F^{\times},\ldots,F^{\times}\}
contains

contains

for

\tau_{1}
and the canonical

Note now that
\tau_{2}
does not change if we replace

It is now clear that

and we have already
shown above that

∎

2.6.
The direct sum topology

Recall from
[
26
, § 6]
that if

G={\underset{i\in I}{\bigoplus}}G_{i}
is the direct sum of
abelian groups
\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
such that each
G_{i}
is a topological abelian group,
then the direct sum topology on
G

is the inductive limit of the product topologies on finite direct sums.
It is easy to see that if each
G_{i}
is generated by a family of open subgroups

\{G_{i,\lambda_{i}}\}_{\lambda_{i}\in I_{i}}

(in which case one says that
G_{i}
has the subgroup topology), then
the family of direct sums
{\underset{i\in I}{\bigoplus}}G_{i,\lambda_{i}}
,
where
\lambda_{i}
runs through
I_{i}
for each
i\in I
,
generates the direct sum topology of
G
.
The direct sum topology has another property which we shall use often.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
G
be the direct sum of abelian groups

\{G_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. Assume that each
G_{i}
is
a topological abelian group with subgroup topology.
Then
G
, with its direct sum topology, is
a coproduct in the category of topological abelian groups with
the subgroup topology.

Proof.

Let

\{f_{i}\colon G_{i}\to H\}
be a family of continuous homomorphisms
to a topological abelian group
H
.
Then the lemma asserts that there is a unique
continuous homomorphism
f\colon G\to H
such that
f|_{G_{i}}=f_{i}

for all
i\in I
.

Now, it is clear that a homomorphism of groups
f
exists with
the above uniqueness property as a group homomorphism.
We need to show that
f
is continuous.
Since
G
has the inductive limit topology, it suffices to prove
the case when
I
is a finite set.
We can therefore let
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

We now let
H^{\prime}\subset H
be an open subgroup.
By Lemma
2.8
, it suffices to show that
f^{-1}(H^{\prime})
is open.
We let
B_{i}=f^{-1}_{i}(H^{\prime})
and let
B={\underset{1\leq i\leq n}{\prod}}B_{i}\subset G
. It is clear that
B\subset G
is open.
We are left with showing that
f(B)\subset H^{\prime}
.
But this is clear because
f^{-1}(H^{\prime})
is a subgroup of
G
and

f^{-1}_{i}(H^{\prime})=f^{-1}(H^{\prime})\cap G_{i}
via the inclusions
G_{i}\subset G
.
∎

The following useful result may be well known, but we write down a proof
as we could not find a suitable reference.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
f\colon G\to H
be a group homomorphism between two
topological abelian groups with subgroup topologies. Then
f
is continuous
if and only if the inverse image of every generating open subgroup via
f
is
open.

Proof.

Since
G
and
H
have subgroup topologies,

f
will be continuous if the inverse image of every coset of
a generating open subgroup is open. So we let
a\in H
be an arbitrary
element and
B\subset H
a generating open subgroup.
If
f^{-1}(a+B)
is empty, we are done. Otherwise, we let

a^{\prime}\in G
be such that
f(a^{\prime})=a+b
for some
b\in B
.
By our hypothesis, there is an open subgroup
B^{\prime}\subset f^{-1}(B)
.
If
b^{\prime}\in B^{\prime}
, then
f(a^{\prime}+b^{\prime})=a+(b+b^{\prime})\in a+B
.
This implies that
f(a^{\prime}+B^{\prime})\subset a+B
. Since
a^{\prime}+B^{\prime}
is open
in
G
, the lemma follows.
∎

3.

In the construction of the reciprocity homomorphism,

The goal of this section is to recall the idele class group defined

Let

3.4.
Functoriality of the idele class group

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme of dimension
d=d(X)
endowed with a
dimension function
d_{X}
. Let
C\subset X
be a reduced nowhere dense
closed subscheme of
X
with
U=X\setminus C
.
Let

be a finite morphism from an integral scheme
such that the support of
f^{-1}(C)
is contained in a
nowhere dense reduced closed subscheme
C^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime}
with
complement
U^{\prime}
. We endow
X^{\prime}
with the dimension function induced by
d_{X}
.
If these properties are satisfied, we shall say that

is an admissible morphism.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
X
is essentially of finite type over a field.
Then the map
f
induces a push-forward map

f_{*}\colon C_{{U^{\prime}}/{X^{\prime}}}\to C_{U/X}.

Proof.

Let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
be a Parshin chain on
(U^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime})
.
Since
f
is finite,
f(P)=(f(p_{0}),\ldots,f(p_{s}))

is a Parshin chain on
X
. But it may not be a Parshin chain
on
(U\subset X)
unless
C^{\prime}=f^{-1}(C)
. We let
s^{\prime}\leq s
be the
largest integer such that
P^{\prime\prime}:=(f(p_{0}),\ldots,f(p_{s^{\prime}}))
is a
Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)
. We let
P_{1}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s^{\prime}})
.

Since
P_{1}\leq P
, there is a composition of residue homomorphisms

\partial^{P_{1}\leq P}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P)}(k(P))\to K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))
.
We also have the finite map between products of fields

k(P^{\prime\prime})\to k(P_{1})
and this yields the norm map

N^{P_{1}\to P^{\prime\prime}}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))\to K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))
.
We let
f^{P\to P^{\prime\prime}}=N^{P_{1}\to P^{\prime\prime}}\circ\partial^{P_{1}\leq P}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P)}(k(P))\to K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))
.
Composing this with the canonical inclusion
K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))\hookrightarrow I_{U/X}
, we get a map

f^{P}_{*}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P)}(k(P))\to I_{U/X}
.
Taking the direct sum of these maps over all Parshin chains
on
(U^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime})
, we get our push-forward map

f_{*}\colon I_{U^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\to I_{U/X}.

We now show that
f_{*}
preserves the relations that define the
idele class groups.
If
Q=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s-2},p_{s})
is a
Q
-chain on
(U^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime})
,
then
\partial(K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(Q)}(k(Q)))
is the direct sum of
images under the henselization along the discrete valuations rings
coming from the irreducible components of
C^{\prime}
(if the latter is a
divisor)
and the residue maps along points on
U^{\prime}
(see §
3.3
).
Since
f_{*}
is defined by composing residue and the norm maps,
the assertion that it preserves the relations will follow if we
show that the residue maps commute with henselization and the
norm maps commute with henselization as well as the residue maps.

Since a residue map between the Milnor
K
-groups of fields commutes
with field extensions (see
[
18
]
),
it is clear that it commutes with henselization.
Similarly, since the norm map between Milnor
K
-groups commutes with
field extensions by the standard properties of norm (see
[
3
]
),
it commutes with henselization along discrete valuations.
The only nontrivial thing therefore is to check that the norm
and the residue maps between Milnor
K
-groups of fields commute.
But this follows from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

In

4.
Continuity of the push-forward map

We shall now prove the continuity of the push-forward map
between the idele class groups.
Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme of Krull dimension
d

endowed with a dimension function
d_{X}
. Let
\eta
denote the generic
point of
X
.
Let
C\subset X
be a nowhere dense reduced closed subscheme with
complement
U
.

We first consider the easy case.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\colon(X^{\prime},U^{\prime})\to(X,U)
be a dominant admissible morphism.
Then the map
f_{*}\colon C_{{U^{\prime}}/{X^{\prime}}}\to C_{U/X}
is continuous.
If
U
is regular, then
f_{*}
satisfies the following.

(1)

If
f
is birational, then
f_{*}
is surjective.

(2)

If
f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is an isomorphism, then
f_{*}
is
a topological quotient map.

Proof.

For the first part, it suffices to show that the map

f_{*}\colon I_{{U^{\prime}}/{X^{\prime}}}\to I_{U/X}
is continuous.
By virtue of the direct sum topology of
I_{{U^{\prime}}/{X^{\prime}}}
(see §
3.1
) and
Lemma
2.7
, it suffices to show that
the map
f_{*}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P)}(k(P))\to I_{U/X}
is continuous
for every Parshin chain
P
on
(U^{\prime}\subset X^{\prime})
.
If
P
is not maximal, then
f^{-1}_{*}(B)
is zero for
every proper open subgroup
B
of
I_{U/X}
. Since the
topology of
I_{{U^{\prime}}/{X^{\prime}}}
restricts to the discrete topology on

K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P)}(k(P))
, this case follows.
We shall now assume that
P
is maximal.

Let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{d})
, where
d=d_{X}(\eta)-d_{m}
.
Let
s\leq d
be the largest integer such that
(f(p_{0}),\ldots,f(p_{s}))
is a Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)
. We let

P_{1}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})\leq P
. Then
f_{*}
is the composition of the
residue
map
\partial\colon K^{M}_{d}(k(P))\to K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))
and the norm map

N\colon K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))\to K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))
, where

P^{\prime\prime}=f(P_{1})
.

There are two cases to consider. Suppose first that
s=d
so that

P^{\prime\prime}=f(P)
is a maximal Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)
.
In this case,
f_{*}
is simply the norm map
N\colon K^{M}_{d}(k(P^{\prime}))\to K^{M}_{d}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))
. Since the inclusion

K^{M}_{d}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))\hookrightarrow I_{U/X}
is clearly continuous as
P^{\prime\prime}
is maximal,
we need to show that
N
is continuous. But this follows from

[
21
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Suppose now that
s<d
. Then
f_{*}
has a factorization

K^{M}_{d}(k(P))\xrightarrow{\partial}K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))\xrightarrow{N}K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))\hookrightarrow I_{U/X}.

If we take a fundamental open subgroup
G\subset I_{U/X}
, then its
intersection with
K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))
is zero because
P^{\prime\prime}
is a
non-maximal Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)
.
In particular, we have that
f^{-1}_{*}(G)=\partial^{-1}({\rm Ker}(N))
. Hence, it suffices to show that

\partial^{-1}(H)
is open in
K^{M}_{d}(k(P))
for every subgroup

H\subset K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))
. To show this,
we can argue inductively and reduce to the case that

\text{\rm dim}(P_{1})=d-1
. It is enough to check in this case that

{\rm Ker}(\partial)
is open in
K^{M}_{d}(k(P))
.
We can assume that
k(P)
is a field.
In this case,
k(P_{1})
is the residue field of the discrete valuation
ring
A
which is the normalization of
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X^{\prime},P_{1}}
in
k(P)
.
We are now done because the sequence

K^{M}_{d}(A)\to K^{M}_{d}(k(P))\xrightarrow{\partial}K^{M}_{d-1}(k(P_{1}))\to 0

is exact (e.g., see
[
6
, Proposition 2.7]
)
and the image of
K^{M}_{d}(A)
is clearly open in
K^{M}_{d}(k(P))

(see Lemma
2.6
).

Suppose now that
f
is birational and
U
is regular. It suffices to show the
stronger assertion that
f_{*}\colon I_{U^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\to I_{U/X}
is surjective.
Since
f
is finite
and
U
is regular, it follows that is
f
an isomorphism over
U
.
We can therefore assume that
U^{\prime}\subset U
. Suppose now that

P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
is a Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)
.
Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be the set of Parshin chains

P_{1}=(p^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{s})
on
X^{\prime}
such that
f(P_{1})=P
. Since
f
is
finite and
p^{\prime}_{s}\in U
,
it follows from
[
21
, Lemma 3.3.1]
that
\prod_{P_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}}k(P_{1})\cong k(P)

and therefore
the norm map
\oplus_{P_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}}K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))\to K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))
is identity.

We let
s^{\prime}\geq s
be the smallest integer such that

P^{\prime\prime}=(P_{1},p^{\prime}_{s+1},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}})
is a Parshin chain on

(U^{\prime},X^{\prime})
.
It is then enough to show that the
residue homomorphism
K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))\to K^{M}_{d_{X^{\prime}}(P_{1})}(k(P_{1}))

is surjective.
To prove this, one can again argue inductively. Since
p^{\prime}_{s},\ldots,p^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\in U
and
U
is regular, one can
assume that
k(P^{\prime\prime})
is a discrete valuation field with ring of integers
A

and residue field
k(P_{1})
.
In this case, one knows that
\partial(\{\pi,u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\})=\{\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{n}\}
for
n\geq 1

if
u_{i}\in A^{\times}
for all
i

and
\pi
is a uniformizer of
k(P^{\prime\prime})
.

Suppose now that
f
is an isomorphism and
U
is regular. We show the
stronger assertion that
f_{*}\colon I_{U^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}\to I_{U/X}
is a topological
quotient.
Since we have already shown that
f_{*}
is
continuous and surjective, it suffices to show that it takes
a fundamental open subgroup to an open subgroup.
But this is obvious from the definition of topology on the idele groups
and the fact that
f
preserves maximality of Parshin chains and

f_{*}
is identity on the Milnor
K
-theory of maximal Parshin
chains. This finishes the proof.
∎

4.1.
Local idele class groups

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme endowed with a dimension
function
d_{X}
. Let
U\subset X
be a dense open subscheme with
complement
C
. A lifted chain on
X
is a sequence
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})

such that
p_{0}\in X
and
p_{i+1}\in{\rm Spec\,}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,(p_{0},\ldots,p_{i})})

for
i\geq 0
. We let
X_{P}={\rm Spec\,}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P})
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}_{P}
be the maximal ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P}
and
k(P)
its residue field.
We denote the closed point of
X_{P}
by
x_{P}
.
We remark that a lifted chain is different from an
s
-chain.
If
P
is a lifted chain, then
X_{P}
is always a local (Henselian) scheme
but this may not be the case for an
s
-chain on
X
. In fact,
for any
s
-chain
P_{1}
on
X
, the scheme
X_{P_{1}}
is a disjoint union
of the spectra of finitely many lifted chains.

We need to define a local idele class group of a lifted chain.
Let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
be a lifted chain on
X
and let

D_{P}
(resp.
U_{P}
) be the pull-back of
D
(resp.
U
) under the canonical
map
X_{P}\to X
.
We endow
X_{P}
with the dimension function induced by
d_{X}
.
We let
I(X_{P},D_{P})
be exactly as in (
3.2
). However, there is one
difference in
Q
-chains on
(U_{P}\subset X_{P})
.
We say that
Q=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s-2},p_{s})

is a
Q
-chain on
(U_{P}\subset X_{P})
if it satisfies conditions (1) - (3) in
the beginning of §
3.3
, together with an additional condition
that
p_{0}
is the unique closed point of
X_{P}
.
We let
{\mathcal{Q}}^{{\rm loc}}_{U/X}
denote the set of these special
Q
-chains.
We define the local idele class group of
(X_{P},D_{P})
by

(4.1)

C^{{\rm loc}}(X_{P},D_{P})={\rm Coker}\left({\underset{Q\in{\mathcal{Q}}^{{\rm loc}}_{U/X}}{\bigoplus}}K^{M}_{d_{X}(Q)}(k(Q))\xrightarrow{\partial_{U/X}}I(X_{P},D_{P})\right).

The following is straightforward and shows that one can ignore
the above new definition in higher dimensions.
But this is not the case in dimension one.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\text{\rm dim}(X_{P})\geq 2
.
Let
X^{\prime}_{P}=X_{P}\setminus x_{P}
and
D^{\prime}_{P}=D_{P}\setminus x_{P}
.
Then there is a canonical isomorphism

C^{{\rm loc}}(X_{P},D_{P})\xrightarrow{\cong}C(X^{\prime}_{P},D^{\prime}_{P}).

4.2.
Continuity of push-forward in general

To prove the continuity of more general push-forward maps
between the idele class groups, we need some lemmas.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme equipped with a dimension
function
d_{X}
. Let
U\subset X
be a dense open subscheme with
complement
C
. Let
P
be a Parshin chain on
(U\subset X)

whose codimension in

is at most one. Let
K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))
be
endowed with its canonical topology.
Then the canonical map
K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))\to C_{U/X}
is continuous
if and only if the composite map

K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))\to C_{U/X}\twoheadrightarrow C(X,D)
is
continuous for all closed subschemes
D
with support
C
.

Proof.

One of the implications is obvious. For the nontrivial
implication, we first note that
if
P
is maximal, then the inclusion map

K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))\to I_{U/X}
is already continuous, as is easily seen.
We can therefore assume that
P
has codimension one in
X
.
We let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
and
d^{\prime}=d_{X}(P)
.
Let
\eta
be the generic point of
X
and
d=d_{X}(\eta)
.
Let
G={\underset{P_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{{\operatorname{\rm max}}}_{U/X}}{\bigoplus}}K^{M}_{d}(k(P_{1}),m_{P_{1}}(D_{P_{1}}))

be a basic open subgroup of
I_{U/X}
.
We need to show that there is an open subgroup
H\subset K^{M}_{d^{\prime}}(k(P))

with the canonical topology such that

H\subset G+{\rm Image}(\partial_{U/X})
inside
I_{U/X}
, where

\partial_{U/X}
is described in (
3.3
).

To show the last assertion, we first observe that there is a unique

Q=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s-1},\eta)\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{U/X}
such that

\partial_{U/X}(K^{M}_{d}(k(Q)))
can nontrivially intersect

K^{M}_{d^{\prime}}(k(P))
.
We next observe that there are only finitely many
Parshin chains on
(U\subset X)
of the form

\widetilde{P}_{i}=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s-1},q_{i},\eta)

because
C
has only finitely
many irreducible components. We let
S

be the set of these finitely many Parshin chains.
It then follows that the map

\partial_{U/X}\colon K^{M}_{d}(k(Q))\to I_{U/X}
factors through

(4.2)

K^{M}_{d}(k(Q))\xrightarrow{\partial_{U/X}}\left({\underset{P_{1}\in S}{\oplus}}K^{M}_{d}(k(P_{1}))\right)\bigoplus K^{M}_{d^{\prime}}(k(P))\bigoplus\left({\underset{P^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime\prime}_{U/X}}{\oplus}}K^{M}_{d_{X}(P^{\prime\prime})}(k(P^{\prime\prime}))\right)\hookrightarrow I_{U/X},

where
{\mathcal{P}}^{\prime\prime}_{U/X}
is the set of all Parshin chains of codimension
at least one, excluding
P
.

Since the composite map
K^{M}_{d^{\prime}}(k(P))\hookrightarrow I_{U/X}\to C(X,D)
is
continuous by our hypothesis, and since
C(X,D)
is discrete by
Lemma
3.1
, it follows from (

)
that there is an open

H\subset K^{M}_{d^{\prime}}(k(P))
such that

H\subset\left({\underset{P_{1}\in S}{\oplus}}K^{M}_{d}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,{P}^{\prime}_{1}}|I_{D})\right)+{\rm Image}(\partial_{U/X})
.

Since

{\underset{P_{1}\in S}{\bigoplus}}K^{M}_{d}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P^{\prime}_{1}}|I_{D})\subset\left({\underset{P_{1}\in S}{\oplus}}K^{M}_{d}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,P^{\prime}_{1}}|I_{D})\right)\bigoplus\left({\underset{P_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}^{{\operatorname{\rm max}}}_{U/X}\setminus S}{\oplus}}K^{M}_{d}(k(P_{1}),m_{P_{1}}(D_{P_{1}}))\right),

and since for each

, we have

K^{M}_{d}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{i}}|I_{D})=K^{M}_{d}({\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X,\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{i}}|I_{m_{\widetilde{P}_{i}}D_{\widetilde{P}_{i}}})\subset K^{M}_{d}(k(\widetilde{P}_{i}),m_{\widetilde{P}_{i}}(D_{\widetilde{P}_{i}})),

we conclude that

∎

Proposition 4.5

4.3.
Idele class group of the function field

It is easy to see from the construction of the push-forward map between the
idele class groups that if
X^{\prime\prime}\xrightarrow{f^{\prime}}X^{\prime}\xrightarrow{f}X

are admissible morphisms, then
(f\circ f^{\prime})_{*}=f_{*}\circ f^{\prime}_{*}
, whenever
these maps are defined.
In particular, the groups
C_{U/X}
form a co-filtered system of
abelian groups if we let
U\subset X
run through all dense open subschemes.
It follows from Remark
3.6
that

\{C(X,D)\}
, where
D
runs through all closed subschemes with
support

, is a co-filtered system of abelian groups
whose structure maps are surjective.
All this allows us to define the idele and the idele class groups of the
function field of

as follows.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
X
be an excellent integral scheme of dimension
d
endowed with a
dimension function. Let

denote the function field of

.
We let

I_{K/X}={\underset{U\subset X}{\varprojlim}}I_{U/X},\ \ C_{K/X}={\underset{U\subset X}{\varprojlim}}C_{U/X}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \widetilde{C}_{U/X}={\underset{|D|=X\setminus U}{\varprojlim}}C(X,D).

These groups are equipped with their inverse limit topologies.
There are canonical continuous maps
I_{K/X}\to C_{K/X}

and
C_{U/X}\to\widetilde{C}_{U/X}
. These maps may not be
surjective even though their images are dense.
However, if
d=1
, then

and

coincide with the classical
idele group and the idele class group of

(see
[
24
, Proposition 2.1]
). In particular,

I_{K/X}\to C_{K/X}
is a topological quotient in this case.

For any open dense
U\subset X
and closed subscheme
D\subset X

with support
X\setminus U
, there are canonical continuous homomorphisms

(4.6)

C_{U/X}\to\widetilde{C}_{U/X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\lambda_{D}}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}C(X,D).

4.4.
Idele class group and 0-cycles

For

Proposition 4.8

Let

be an integral scheme which is of

Let

Let

(1)

(3)

We now prove (2).
By Proposition

Let

k

is a field of

We consider the following étale sheaves on

Let
\{W_{r}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\}_{r\geq 1}

Recall that

{\mathcal{R}}\colon W_{r+1}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}\to W_{r}\Omega^{\bullet}_{X}

and the Verschiebung

There is Teichmüller lift
[.]\colon{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to W_{r}{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
, which is
locally given by
[x]=(x,0,\ldots,0)
. This is a multiplicative
homomorphism.

The Frobenius of

descends to give a map

Hence we have a map

which is surjective in the étale topology.
We let
W_{r}\Omega^{n}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}}

We let
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/{p^{r}}(n)=(W_{r}\Omega^{n}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}})[-n]

There is a unique map
\underline{p}\colon W_{r}\Omega^{n}_{X}\to W_{r+1}\Omega^{n}_{X}
such that the composite

is multiplication by

. This induces a map

\underline{p}\colon W_{r}\Omega^{n}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}}\to W_{r+1}\Omega^{n}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}}
.
Hence,
\{{{\mathbb{Z}}}/{p^{r}}(n)\}_{r\geq 1}
is a direct system of

.
Note that
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/{p^{r}}(0)=W_{r}{\mathcal{O}}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}}\cong{{\mathbb{Z}}}/{p^{r}}

If

{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(n)={{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
if

and
(\mu_{m})^{\otimes n}
if

For

{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(n)={\mathcal{H}{om}}({{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(-n),{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m)

We let
H^{p}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(q))={\underset{m\geq 1}{\varinjlim}}\ H^{p}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m(q))

We shall write

Let us now assume that

for any

.
Similarly,
we have the dlog map between étale sheaves on

{{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{n,X}}/{p^{r}}\to W_{r}\Omega^{n}_{X,{\operatorname{log}}};\ \{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}\mapsto d{\operatorname{log}}([a_{1}])\wedge\cdots\wedge d{\operatorname{log}}([a_{n}]).

Using the cup product for étale cohomology and taking the limit over

, we get a pairing

(5.1)

K^{M}_{n}(A)\times H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(A,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\to H^{n+1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(A,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(n)).

Let us now assume that there is a sequence of fields

\underline{K}=\{K_{0},\ldots,K_{n}\}
of characteristic
p
such that

K_{0}
is a finite field and each
K_{i}
with
i\geq 1

is the quotient field of a
Henselian discrete valuation ring whose residue field is
K_{i-1}
.
In this case, we shall say that
K_{n}
is an
n
-dimensional
Henselian local field.
It follows from
[
21
, Theorem 3.5]

that there is a residue isomorphism

(5.2)

{\rm Res}\colon H^{i+1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(K_{i},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(i))\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{i}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(K_{i-1},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(i-1))

for every
i\geq 1
. Since it is easy to check that

H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(K_{0},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\cong{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we conclude that there
is a canonical isomorphism

{\rm Res}\colon H^{n+1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(K_{n},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(n))\xrightarrow{\cong}{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
It follows from this and (
5.1
) that
there is a pairing

(5.3)

K^{M}_{n}(K_{n})\times H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(K_{n},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\xrightarrow{\psi_{K_{n}}}{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}.

On the other hand, for every integer
m\geq 1
and every scheme
X
, there is a
natural isomorphism
H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m)\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(\pi_{1}(X),{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m)\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X),{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m)

which characterizes finite étale covers
Y\to X
together with a
morphism
{\rm Aut}(Y/X)\to{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
.
It follows that there is a natural isomorphism

H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(\pi_{1}(X),{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\cong(\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X))^{\vee}
, where
{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
has discrete topology,

\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X)
has profinite topology and
(\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X))^{\vee}

denotes the Pontryagin dual (see §
7.4
).
By the Pontryagin duality for
profinite groups, we get a continuous isomorphism

{H^{1}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(X,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})}^{\vee}\cong\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X)
.

Combining all of the above, we conclude the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
K
be an
n
-dimensional Henselian local field. Then there is a
canonical reciprocity map

\rho_{K}\colon K^{M}_{n}(K)\to{\rm Gal}(K^{\rm ab}/K).

It is easy to see that a finite extension of
an
n
-dimensional Henselian local field is of the same kind.
Recall also that if
L/K
is a finite abelian extension, and
if
G
(resp.
H
) denotes the abelianized Galois group of
K

(resp.
L
), then
H\subset G
is a normal subgroup of finite index.
Hence, there is a co-restriction map

{\rm Cores}_{H/G}\colon H^{*}(H,M)\to H^{*}(G,M)
for
G
-module
M
.
In particular, we have the co-restriction map

{\rm Cores}_{H/G}\colon H^{1}(H,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\to H^{1}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
.
Taking the Pontryagin duals, we get a ‘transfer map’

{\rm tr}_{L/K}\colon G\to H
.

The following result is due to Kato
[
17
]
.

.

Let

.
Then

For each finite extension

5.2.
Openness of Norm subgroup

We shall show in this subsection that the norm subgroups associated to finite
abelian extensions of a Henselian discrete valuation field
K
are often open.
This can not be deduced from the last part of
Proposition
5.2
(1) even if the Galois theory of
K

coincides with that of its completion
\widehat{K}
. The reason is that the
quotient of the norm subgroup of the Milnor
K
-theory associated to a finite
abelian extension
L/K
may not coincide with the corresponding quotient
associated to
{\widehat{L}}/{\widehat{K}}
.

We begin with the following elementary observations.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
L/K
be a finite abelian extension. Then
it is the inductive limit of a tower of abelian extensions
each of which is cyclic of prime order.

Proof.

It is an easy exercise using Galois theory of fields.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

For any field
K
, there is a functorial isomorphism

{\operatorname{Br}}(K)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{2}(K,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}(1)),

where
{\operatorname{Br}}(K)
is the Brauer group.

Proof.

This is an easy exercise using the fact that
{\operatorname{Br}}(K)
is a torsion group
and then using the Norm-residue homomorphisms.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
L/K
be a finite cyclic extension of a Henselian discrete
valuation field. Then
N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
is
an open subgroup of
K^{\times}
.

Proof.

Let
G
and
\overline{G}
denote the Galois groups of
L/K
and
{\overline{K}}/K
,
respectively so that there is a surjection
\overline{G}\twoheadrightarrow G
.
We consider the diagram

(5.7)

\textstyle{H^{0}(G,L^{\times})\times H^{1}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{H^{0}(G,L^{\times})\times H^{2}(G,{\mathbb{Z}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cup}
\textstyle{H^{2}(G,L^{\times})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{{\operatorname{Br}}(L/K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K^{\times}\times H^{1,0}(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{BK\times id}
\textstyle{H^{1,1}(K)\times H^{1,0}(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cup}
\textstyle{H^{2,1}(K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{{\operatorname{Br}}(K),}

where
BK
is the Norm-residue homomorphism of Bloch-Kato
and the isomorphism on the bottom right is by Lemma
5.4
.
The map

H^{1}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\to H^{1,0}(K)\cong H^{1}(\overline{G},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})\cong{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(\overline{G},{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})

is the canonical inclusion induced by
\overline{G}\twoheadrightarrow G
.
It is easy to check that (
5.7
) is commutative.

Since
G
is cyclic,
{\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})=G^{\vee}
is
also cyclic of the same order. Let
\eta
be a generator of

G^{\vee}
. Then the top cup product in
(
5.7
) induces a
map
K^{\times}\xrightarrow{(-)\cup\eta}{\operatorname{Br}}(L/K)
.
Moreover, one knows that this induces an isomorphism

(5.8)

{K^{\times}}/{N_{L/K}(L^{\times})}\xrightarrow{(-)\cup\eta}{\operatorname{Br}}(L/K)

(see
[
33
, Chapter XIV, Proposition 2, Corollary 1]
).

On the other hand, it follows from
[
19
, Lemma 2.2]

that as an element of
H^{1,0}(K)
, there exists an
integer
n\geq 0
such that
\eta\in{\rm fil}_{n}H^{1,0}(K)
, where

{\rm fil}_{n}H^{1,0}(K)
is a certain subgroup of
H^{1,0}(K)
.
What is important for us is that every element
a\in{\rm fil}_{n}H^{1,0}(K)
has
the property that the map

K^{\times}\xrightarrow{(-)\cup a}{\operatorname{Br}}(K)
under the bottom cup product in
(
5.7
) annihilates
U_{n+1}(K)

(see
[
19
, Definition 2.1]
).
It follows that
U_{n+1}(K)\cup\eta=0
in
{\operatorname{Br}}(L/K)
.
We conclude from (
5.8
) that

U_{n+1}(K)\subset N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
L
be a finite abelian extension of a Henselian discrete valuation
field
K
and
n\geq 0
an integer.
Then
N_{L/K}\colon K^{M}_{n}(L)\to K^{M}_{n}(K)
is continuous.
If
L/K
is cyclic, then the image of
N_{L/K}\colon K^{M}_{n}(L)\to K^{M}_{n}(K)

is an open subgroup of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
.

Proof.

The
n=0
case is obvious, so we assume
n\geq 1
.
The first assertion now follows from Lemma
2.6
and

[
21
, Lemma 4.3]
.
To prove the second assertion, we note that Lemma
2.6
implies
that
U=\{U_{1},{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K},\ldots,{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K}\}
is open
in
K^{M}_{n}(K)
for every open subgroup
U_{1}\subset{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K}
.
Furthermore, the projection formula for the norm map shows that
if
U_{1}\subset N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
, then

U\subset N_{L/K}(K^{M}_{n}(L))
. Hence, it suffices to show that

N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
contains some open subgroup of
K^{\times}
.
But this follows from Lemma
5.5
.
∎

Remark 5.7
.

We expect the statement of Lemma
5.5
to be true
for any finite Galois extension of a Henselian discrete
valuation field
K
but we are not aware of a proof.
If one assumes that the field extension is
unramified, an answer is given by the following stronger assertion.
We shall not need this for proving the main results of this paper.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
L/K
be a finite unramified abelian extension of a Henselian discrete
valuation field. Then
N_{L/K}(U_{n}(L))=U_{n}(K)
for all
n\geq 1
.
In particular,
N_{L/K}(L^{\times})
is
an open subgroup of
K^{\times}
.

Proof.

It follows from
[
33
, Chapter V, Proposition 1]

that
N_{L/K}(U_{n}(L))\subset U_{n}(K)
for every
n\geq 1
.
We only need to show that this inclusion is an equality.
Note that although
K
is assumed to be complete in
ibid.
,
proof of this inclusion does not require the completeness assumption.

Let
{\mathfrak{k}}
and
{\mathfrak{l}}
denote the residue fields of
K
and
L
,
respectively. For any
g(X)\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[X]
and
n\geq 1
, we shall denote
its image in
{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{K}}[X]
by
\overline{g}_{n}(X)
. For any
a\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
,
we shall let
\overline{a}_{n}
be its image in
{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{K}}
.
We shall use similar
notations for elements of
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
and
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[X]
.

Since
L/K
is unramified,
{\mathfrak{l}}/{\mathfrak{k}}
is an abelian extension of
degree
[L:K]
. Let this degree be
d
.
We choose
\alpha^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{l}}
such that
{\mathfrak{l}}={\mathfrak{k}}(\alpha^{\prime})

and let
f(X)\in{\mathfrak{k}}[X]
be the minimal polynomial of
\alpha^{\prime}
over
{\mathfrak{k}}
.
Let
g(X)=X^{d}+a_{d-1}X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{1}X+a_{0}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[X]
be
a polynomial such that
\overline{g}_{1}(X)=f(X)
. By the Hensel lemma
(applied to
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
), there exists
\alpha\in{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
such that

g(\alpha)=0
and
\overline{\alpha}_{1}=\alpha^{\prime}
. Note that
\alpha^{\prime}
is
a simple root of
f(X)
over
{\mathfrak{l}}
.

We now let
n\geq 1
be any integer and
u\in U_{n}(K)
.
We let
h(X)=X^{d}+a_{d-1}X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_{1}X+ua_{0}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{K}[X]
.
It is then clear that
\overline{h}_{n}(X)=\overline{g}_{n}(X)\in{{\mathcal{O}}_{K}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{K}}[X]
.
Hence, this equality holds in
{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{L}}[X]
too.

We write
g(X)=(X-\alpha)g^{\prime}(X)
in
{\mathcal{O}}_{L}[X]
.
We then get

\overline{h}_{n}(X)=\overline{g}_{n}(X)=(X-\overline{\alpha}_{n})\overline{g^{\prime}}_{n}(X)

in
{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{L}}[X]
.
Furthermore, since the images of
X-\overline{\alpha}_{n}
and
\overline{g^{\prime}}_{n}(X)
in

{\mathfrak{l}}[X]
generate the unit ideal, they must generate the unit ideal in

{{\mathcal{O}}_{L}}/{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{L}}[X]
too.

We now use the fact that
({\mathcal{O}}_{L},{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}_{L})
is a Henselian pair
because
({\mathcal{O}}_{L},{\mathfrak{m}}_{L})
is.
It follows that there exists
\beta\in{\mathcal{O}}_{L}
such that

h(\beta)=0
and
\overline{\beta}_{n}=\overline{\alpha}_{n}
.
Since
\overline{h}_{1}(X)=\overline{g}_{1}(X)
is irreducible, it follows
that
h(X)
is also irreducible and

h(X)
is therefore the minimal polynomial of
\beta
over

K
.
In particular,
N_{L/K}(\beta)=(-1)^{d}ua_{0}
.
Similarly, we have
N_{L/K}(\alpha)=(-1)^{d}a_{0}
.
We therefore get
N_{L/K}(\alpha^{-1}\cdot\beta)=u
. Note that
\alpha,\beta\in U_{0}(L)
since their images in

{\mathfrak{l}}
are units. Since
\overline{(\alpha^{-1}\cdot\beta)}_{n}=1
,
it follows that
\alpha^{-1}\cdot\beta\in U_{n}(L)
.
This proves the lemma.
∎

5.3.
Continuity of Kato’s reciprocity

We shall now show that Kato’s reciprocity map is continuous.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
K
be an
n
-dimensional Henselian local field. Then
the reciprocity map
\rho_{K}
is continuous with respect to the canonical
topology on
K^{M}_{n}(K)
and profinite topology on
{\rm Gal}({K^{\rm ab}}/K)
.

Proof.

Using the profinite topology on
{\rm Gal}({K^{\rm ab}}/K)
, the proposition is
equivalent to saying that for every finite abelian extension
L/K
,
the composite map
\rho_{K,L}\colon K^{M}_{n}(K)\to{\rm Gal}(L/K)

annihilates some open subgroup of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
in its canonical topology.
We let
G={\rm Gal}(L/K)
. By Lemma
5.3
, we can then
write
G=G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{r}
,
where each
G_{i}

is a finite cyclic group. Let
p_{i}\colon G\to G_{i}
be the projection.
Since the intersection of finitely many nonempty open subgroups in
a topological group is a nonempty open subgroup,
it suffices to show that
p_{i}\circ\rho_{K,L}\colon K^{M}_{n}(K)\to G_{i}

annihilates some open subgroup of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
for each
i
.

We fix
1\leq i\leq r
. Let
K\subset E_{i}\subset L

denote the intermediate
extension such that
{\rm Gal}(E_{i}/K)=G_{i}
.
We have to then show that the composite map

p_{i}\circ\rho_{K,L}=\rho_{K,E_{i}}
annihilates some open subgroup of

K^{M}_{n}(K)
.
We have therefore reduced the proof of the proposition to showing that
for every finite cyclic extension
L/K
,
the composite map
\rho_{K,L}\colon K^{M}_{n}(K)\to{\rm Gal}(L/K)

annihilates some open subgroup of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
.
Using Proposition
5.2
(1), it suffices to show
that
N_{L/K}(K^{M}_{n}(L))
contains some open subgroup of

K^{M}_{n}(K)
. But this follows from Corollary
5.6
.
∎

5.4.
Reciprocity for
C_{U/X}

Let
k
be a finite field. In this subsection, we shall
assume all schemes to be endowed with their canonical dimension functions.
Let
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
be an integral scheme of
dimension
d
.
Let
C\subset X
be a nowhere dense reduced
closed subscheme with complement
U
. Let
\eta
denote the
generic point of
X
and let
K=k(\eta)
.

We shall define a continuous reciprocity map
from
C_{U/X}
to
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)
.
We begin with a more general construction.
We let
P=(p_{0},\ldots,p_{s})
be any Parshin chain on
X
with
the condition that
p_{s}\in U
.
Let
X(P)=\overline{\{p_{s}\}}
be the integral closed subscheme of
X

U(P)=U\cap X(P)
. We let
X(P)_{n}
denote the normalization of

Let

({X(P)}_{n},{U(P)}_{n})\xrightarrow{f^{P}}(X,U)\ \mbox{and}\ (X(P),U(P))\xrightarrow{\iota^{P}}(X,U),

such that
f^{P}
factors through
\iota^{P}
. Note that

{U(P)}_{n}\neq\emptyset

p_{s}\in U
.

Now,
P

whose generic point lies in
U(P)
.
Moreover, Lemma
2.3
says that there is a factorization

{\rm Spec\,}(k(P))\to X(P)\xrightarrow{\iota^{P}}X
,
where the first map is dominant.
On the other hand, Lemma

{\rm Spec\,}(k(P))\to X(P)

{\rm Spec\,}(k(P))\to X(P)_{n}

We thus have the factorization
{\rm Spec\,}(k(P))\to X(P)_{n}\xrightarrow{f^{P}}X

We can therefore consider the push-forward maps

(5.9)

\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}({\rm Spec\,}(k(P)))\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U(P)_{n})\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U(P))\xrightarrow{\iota^{P}_{*}}\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U),

where the composition of the last two arrows is

We now let
V\subset k(p_{s})

.
We have seen in §

\rho_{Q(V^{h})}\colon K^{M}_{s}(Q(V^{h}))\to{\rm Gal}({{Q(V^{h})}^{\rm ab}}/{Q(V^{h})})\cong\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}({\rm Spec\,}(Q(V^{h}))).

Taking the sum of these maps over
{\mathcal{V}}(P)
and using

, we get a reciprocity map

(5.11)

\rho_{k(P)}\colon K^{M}_{s}(k(P))\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}({\rm Spec\,}(k(P))).

Composing this with (

), we get the following.

Lemma 5.10
.

For every Parshin chain
P
on

whose generic point lies in

(5.12)

\rho_{{k(P)}/U}\colon K^{M}_{d_{X}(P)}(k(P))\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U),

.

We let
\widetilde{I}_{U/X}
denote the direct sum of the

whose generic points lie in

Taking the sum of the maps in (

(5.13)

\widetilde{\rho}_{U/X}\colon\widetilde{I}_{U/X}\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U).

The restriction of

(5.14)

\rho_{U/X}\colon I_{U/X}\to\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U).

We shall now show that

Before this, we prove some lemmas.

Lemma 5.11

6.3.
Filtrations of
H^{1}(K)
via Milnor
K
-theory

Let
K
be a Henselian discrete valuation field as above.
The above filtrations can be described in terms of Milnor
K
-theory
which we shall use. In order to explain this,
we need to recall some filtrations of
K^{M}_{*}(K)
.
For any integers
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
, let
U_{m}K^{M}_{n}(K)
be
the subgroup
\{U_{m}(K),K^{\times},\ldots,K^{\times}\}
of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
.
We let
U^{\prime}_{m}K^{M}_{n}(K)
be the subgroup
\{U_{m}(K),{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K},\ldots,{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K}\}
of
K^{M}_{n}(K)
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For every
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
, there are inclusions

U_{m+1}K^{M}_{n}(K)\subseteq U^{\prime}_{m}K^{M}_{n}(K)\subseteq U_{m}K^{M}_{n}(K).

Proof.

We only need to prove the first inclusion. We shall prove it by induction
on
n
. Since this inclusion is obvious for
n=1
, we can assume that

n\geq 2
. Let
\pi
be a uniformizer of
K
.
We let
\alpha=\{1+u_{1}\pi^{m+1},u_{2}\pi^{m_{2}},\ldots,u_{n}\pi^{m_{n}}\}
,
where
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{K}
and
m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Using the bilinearity and additivity properties of the Milnor
K
-theory,
we can assume that
u_{2}=\cdots=u_{n}=1
. By the same token,
we can also assume
m_{2}=\cdots=m_{n}=1
.
We can therefore write
\alpha=\{1+u_{1}\pi^{m+1},\pi,\ldots,\pi\}
.
By
[
3
, Chapter 1, § 1]
, we have the relation

\{\pi,\pi\}=\{\pi,(-1)(-\pi)\}=\{\pi,-1\}+\{\pi,-\pi\}=\{\pi,-1\}
.
Applying this relation and another relation
\{a,b\}=-\{b,a\}
repeatedly,
we see that
\alpha=\{1+u_{1}\pi^{m+1},\pi\}\cdot\beta
, where

\beta\in K^{M}_{n-2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})
. Since
U^{\prime}_{m}K^{M}_{2}(K)\cdot K^{M}_{n-2}({\mathcal{O}}_{K})\subset U^{\prime}_{m}K^{M}_{n}(K)
, we can assume
n=2
and

\alpha=\{1+u_{1}\pi^{m+1},\pi\}
.

Now, we let
t=-(u_{1}\pi^{m})
,
v^{\prime}=(1+t(-1-\pi))^{-1}
and

v^{\prime\prime}=-1-\pi
. It is then clear that

1+v^{\prime}t,1+v^{\prime\prime}t\in U_{m}(K)
and
(1+v^{\prime}t)(1+v^{\prime\prime}t)=1-\pi t
.
We therefore get

\begin{array}[]{lll}\{1+u_{1}\pi^{m+1},\pi\}&=&\{1+(u_{1}\pi^{m})\pi,\pi\}=\{1+(u_{1}\pi^{m})\pi,-(u_{1}\pi^{m})\}=\{1-\pi t,t\}\\
&=&\{(1+v^{\prime}t)(1+v^{\prime\prime}t),t\}=\{1+v^{\prime}t,t\}+\{1+v^{\prime\prime}t,t\}\\
&=&\{1+v^{\prime}t,-v^{\prime}\}+\{1+v^{\prime\prime}t,-v^{\prime\prime}\}\\
&\in&U^{\prime}_{m}K^{M}_{2}(K).\end{array}

The lemma is now proven.
∎

Let us now assume that

is a

Recall from (

) that there is a cup product pairing

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
\chi\in H^{1}(K)

(1)

For every integer

(2)

for all

Proof.

The first part is due to Kato
[
19
, Proposition 6.5]

If

, the second part follows from
Lemma
6.2
and
[
29
, Proposition 3.2.7]

(see also
[
25
, Lemma 2.6]

in all positive characteristics.

Let

and

be the subgroup of

By using the exact sequence of sheaves given in (

Taking the direct sum of these maps for

It follows from
[
19

coincides with the subgroup
~{}^{\prime}{\rm fil}^{{\rm bk}}_{m}H^{1}(K)

of

consisting of characters

\chi
such that

.
We let
~{}^{\prime}{\rm fil}^{{\rm ms}}_{m}H^{1}(K)
be the subgroup of

consisting of

\chi
such that

. It suffices to show for every

that

{\rm fil}^{\dagger}_{m}H^{1}(K)=~^{\prime}{\rm fil}^{{\rm ms}}_{m}H^{1}(K)={\rm fil}^{{\rm ms}}_{m}H^{1}(K).

is the
smallest integer
m\geq 0
such that
\chi\in{\rm fil}^{{\rm ms}}_{m}H^{1}(K)
.
It is denoted by
{\rm ar}(\chi)
.
It follows from Theorem
6.1
that
{\rm sw}(\chi)\leq{\rm ar}(\chi)
.
They are the same and agree with the classical definition

[
19
, Proposition 6.8]
if the residue field of
K
is perfect.

7.
The fundamental group with modulus

In this section, we shall introduce our key object, the
étale fundamental group with modulus
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
of an
integral normal scheme
X
over a field relative to
an effective Weil divisor
D
. This will be the target of our reciprocity map
for the Kato-Saito idele class group with modulus.
This fundamental group is a priori different from the already known
fundamental group with modulus
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
, introduced by
Deligne and Laumon
[
28
]
and used extensively in the class field
theory of Wiesend and others (see
[
25
]
).

7.1.
Ramification of field extensions

Let
K
be a Henselian discrete valuation field with ring of integers

{\mathcal{O}}_{K}
, maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}_{K}
and residue field
{\mathfrak{f}}
.
Let
G^{(\bullet)}_{K}
denote the Abbes-Saito
ramification filtration of
G_{K}
.

Let
L/K
be a finite separable extension and let
n\geq 0
be an integer.
We shall say that the ramification of
L/K
is bounded by
n
if

G^{(n)}_{K}
is contained in
{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/{L})
under the inclusions

G^{(n)}_{K}\subset G_{K}\supset{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/{L})
. Note that this
definition coincides with
[
1
, Definition 6.3]
.
Note also that since
G^{(n)}_{K}
is normal in
G_{K}
, the above condition is same
as the condition that
G^{(n)}_{K}
is contained in
{\rm Gal}(\overline{K}/{\widetilde{L}})
, where

\widetilde{L}
is the Galois closure of
L
in
\overline{K}
.
The following is straightforward.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}
be two finite separable extensions of
K
whose ramifications
are bounded by
n
. Let
L^{\prime}_{1}\subset L_{1}
be a subextension.
Then
L^{\prime}_{1}
and the compositum
L_{1}L_{2}
have ramifications bounded by
n
.

7.2.
Morphisms with bounded ramification along a
divisor

Let us now assume that
k
is a field and
X\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Sch}}}_{k}
is an
integral normal scheme.
Let
K
denote the function field of
X

and
let
D\subset X
be an effective
Weil divisor with support
|D|=C
. We consider
C
as a reduced
closed subscheme of
X
with complement
U
.

We write
D={\underset{\lambda}{\sum}}\ n_{\lambda}\overline{\{\lambda\}}
,
where
\lambda
runs through the set of generic points of
C
.
We allow
D
to be empty in which case we
write
D=0
.
For any generic point
\lambda
of
C
, we let
K_{\lambda}
denote the
fraction field of the henselization of the discrete valuation ring

{\mathcal{O}}_{X,\lambda}
.
If
D^{\prime\prime}\subset X
is another effective Weil divisor, then we shall say
that
D^{\prime\prime}\geq D^{\prime}
if
D^{\prime\prime}-D^{\prime}
is effective in the free abelian
group of Weil divisors on
X
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(U)
be the category of finite étale covers of
U
.
We denote an object of
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)
by
(U^{\prime},f)
, where
f\colon U^{\prime}\to U
is the underlying finite étale map.
For
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)
, let
X^{\prime}
denote the normalization of

X
in
k(U^{\prime})
, where the latter denotes the ring of total quotients of
U^{\prime}
.
Note that
U^{\prime}
is a disjoint union of integral normal schemes each of whose
components lies in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)
. In particular,
X^{\prime}
is also a disjoint
union of integral normal schemes each of which is finite and surjective over

X
and the induced map
X^{\prime}\to X
extends
f
. We denote this extension by
f

itself. Let
K^{\prime}
denote the ring of total
quotients of
X^{\prime}
. For any
\lambda\in X^{\prime(1)}\cap C
, we let

K^{\prime}_{\lambda}
denote the
quotient field of
{\mathcal{O}}^{h}_{X^{\prime},\lambda}
. Note that the latter is a Henselian
discrete valuation ring.

Definition 7.2
.

We shall say that
(U^{\prime},f)
has ramification bounded by
D
if the map

f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
has the property that for every generic point

\lambda
of
D

and every point
\lambda^{\prime}\in f^{-1}(\lambda)
, the extension of fields

K_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow K^{\prime}_{\lambda^{\prime}}
has ramification bounded by
n_{\lambda}
.

Let
L/K
be a finite separable field extension. Let
X_{L}
denote the
normalization of
X
in
L
and let
f\colon X_{L}\to X
denote the
induced finite surjective map. We shall say that
L/K
has ramification
bounded by
D
if
f
is étale over
U
and the resulting cover

(f^{-1}(U),f)
has ramification bounded by
D
.
We shall say that
(U^{\prime},f)
(resp.
L/K
) is tamely ramified if its
ramification is bounded by
C
.

The following is easily deduced from definitions.

Proposition 7.3
.

If
(U^{\prime},f)
has ramification bounded by
D
and
D^{\prime}\geq D
with
|D^{\prime}|=|D|
, then it has ramification bounded by
D^{\prime}
. If
X
is regular, then

(U^{\prime},f)
has ramification bounded by
D=0
if and only if

f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is étale.
If
X
is regular along
C
and the latter is a normal crossing divisor,
then
(U^{\prime},f)
is tamely ramified if and only
if it is tamely ramified in the sense of

[
11
, Definition 2.2.2]
.

Proof.

The first part is clear. The third part follows from
the third property of the Abbes-Saito filtration.
One way implication of the second part is also clear.
So we only need to show that if
X
is regular and

(U^{\prime},f)
has ramification bounded by
D=0
, then

f\colon X^{\prime}\to X
is étale.

It follows from the second property of the Abbes-Saito filtration and
the fppf descent for unramified morphisms that

f
is unramified over all codimension one points of
X
.
Hence, it must be unramified.
Since a dominant morphism over the spectrum of a discrete valuation ring
is flat, it follows that
f
is flat outside a closed subscheme

B\subset X
of codimension more than one. It follows that

f
is étale outside
B
. The Zariski-Nagata purity theorem then
implies that
B
must be empty.
∎

7.3.
The underlying Galois category

We fix a point
u\in U
together with the canonical map

\overline{u}\to\{u\}={\rm Spec\,}(k(u))
, where
\overline{u}
is the
spectrum of a separable closure of
k(u)
.
Let
F\colon{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)\to{\mathbf{Sets}}
be the functor
which assigns to any
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{{\text{\'{e}t}}}(U)
, the finite set

f_{\overline{u}}:={\rm Hom}_{U}(\overline{u},U^{\prime})
.
Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
be the full subcategory of objects in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)

which have ramification bounded by
D
.
There are inclusions of categories with fiber functors

(7.1)

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X),F)\hookrightarrow({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)\hookrightarrow({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U),F).

It is well known that
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X),F)
and
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U),F)

are Galois categories in the sense of
[
10
, § 4, 5, Exposé V]
.
The following lemma asserts a similar property of
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
.

Lemma 7.4
.

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
is a Galois category.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is routine following the
strategy of the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 2.4.2]
.
Since
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U),F)
is known to be a Galois category and

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
is its full subcategory, the axioms
(G3), (G4) and (G5) of a Galois category from

[
10
, § 4, 5, Exposé V]
are immediately verified.
The axioms (G1) and (G2) follow from Lemma
7.1
and
the fact that the category of normal
varieties admits quotients by finite group actions.
The axiom (G6) follows because it holds for

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U),F)
and a finite birational morphism between normal varieties
is an isomorphism. We leave out the details.
∎

Since a Galois category is equivalent to the category of finite sets with
continuous action of a uniquely determined profinite group (e.g.,
see
[
10
, Exposé V, § 4]
), we are led to the following.

Definition 7.5
.

The co-1-skeleton (or divisorial) étale fundamental group of
X
with
modulus
D
is the profinite group
\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D,\overline{u})
such that

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
is equivalent to the category of finite sets with
continuous action of
\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D,\overline{u})
.
We let
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
denote the abelianization of

\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D,\overline{u})
and call it the (divisorial) abelianized
étale fundamental group of
X
with modulus
D
.
Since
\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D,\overline{u})
varies from
\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D,\overline{u^{\prime}})

only by inner automorphisms, the group
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)

is well defined.

Remark 7.6
.

In
[
25
]
, Kerz and Saito use another notion of abelianized fundamental
group with modulus, which they denote by
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
, and where the bound on the
ramification is measured by pulling back the étale covers to curves.
This notion of ramification was originally introduced by Deligne and Laumon

[
28
]
. We shall see in Corollary
7.20
that

\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
coincides with
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X,D)
if
X
is regular and

C
is a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
.

It follows from
[
10
, Exposé V, Proposition 6.9]
that there are
surjective continuous homomorphisms

(7.2)

{\rm Gal}(K^{\rm ab}/K)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X).

Recall that an object
(U^{\prime},f)
of
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
is called connected if

U^{\prime}
is not empty and for any inclusion
(V^{\prime},g)\hookrightarrow(U^{\prime},f)
,
the scheme
V^{\prime}
is either empty or the inclusion is an isomorphism.
Recall also that if
(U^{\prime},f)
is a connected finite étale cover of
U
,
then it is called a Galois cover if
{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
acts transitively on

F(U^{\prime})
. This is equivalent to saying that
U={U^{\prime}}/{{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})}
and

U^{\prime}\times_{U}U^{\prime}\cong U\times_{{\rm Spec\,}(k)}{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
if we consider

{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
a finite constant group scheme over
{\rm Spec\,}(k)
.

Lemma 7.7
.

For any
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
connected, there exists a
Galois cover
(V^{\prime},g)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
dominating
(U^{\prime},f)
.

Proof.

This is standard in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)
. One constructs
(V^{\prime},g)
in

{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)
as a connected component of the
n
-fold self fiber product

U^{\prime}_{n}:=U^{\prime}\times_{U}\cdots\times_{U}U^{\prime}
, where
n=|F(U^{\prime})|
.
If
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
, then
we have seen in Lemma
7.4
that
U^{\prime}_{n}
has
ramification bounded by
D
. Hence, so does
(V^{\prime},g)
.
It follows that
(V^{\prime},g)
is a Galois cover which
lies in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
and dominates
(U^{\prime},f)
.
∎

Lemma 7.8
.

Let
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
be a Galois cover and let

K^{\prime}
be the function field of
U^{\prime}
. Then
{K^{\prime}}/K
is Galois with
Galois group
{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
and its ramification is bounded by
D
.

Proof.

This is an easy consequence of
the fact that for a Galois cover
(U^{\prime},f)
, the action of

{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
on
F(U^{\prime})
defines a bijection

{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\cong}{\rm Hom}_{U}(\xi,U^{\prime})
for every
geometric point
\xi\to U
. We can take
\xi
to be
{\rm Spec\,}(\overline{K})

and the lemma follows.
∎

We say that
(U^{\prime},f)
is abelian if it is a Galois cover with
Galois group
{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})
abelian.
If
(U^{\prime},f)
is a Galois cover and we let
H=[{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime}),{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})]
,
then
H
acts freely on
U^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime}={U^{\prime}}/H
is a Galois cover of

U
with Galois group
{\rm Aut}_{U}(V^{\prime})={{\rm Aut}_{U}(U^{\prime})}^{\rm ab}
.
We call
V^{\prime}
the abelianization of
U^{\prime}
.
If
(U^{\prime},f)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
, then we have seen in the proof of
Lemma
7.4
that
V^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D)
.

Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U)
denote the category of finite étale covers

f\colon U^{\prime}\to U
such that every connected component of
U^{\prime}

is an abelian cover of
U
.
Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D)
denote the full subcategory of

{\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U)
whose objects have ramification bounded by
D
.
We denote the restrictions of the functor

F\colon{\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(U)\to{\mathbf{Sets}}
to
{\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U)
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D)

by the same notation
F
.

Proposition 7.9
.

({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F)
is a Galois category such that

\pi_{1}(({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F))=\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
.

Proof.

It is well known (and easy to show)
that
({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U),F)
is a Galois category with

\pi_{1}(({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U),F))=\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)
.
Since
({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F)
is a full subcategory of

({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(U),F)
, the proof that the former category is Galois
is same as that of Lemma
7.4
.
We shall prove the second assertion.

Let
({\mathcal{U}}_{{\rm Gal}}(X,D),F)
denote the subcategory of
Galois covers in
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
.
Let
I
be the set indexing the connected objects of

({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
.
Let
I^{{\rm Gal}}
(resp.
I^{\rm ab}
) be the set indexing the objects of

({\mathcal{U}}_{{\rm Gal}}(X,D),F)
(resp.
({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F)
).
If
P_{a},P_{b}\in({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F)
corresponding to
a,b\in I^{\rm ab}
,
then we let
a\leq b
if
P_{b}
dominates
P_{a}
. Then
I^{\rm ab}
is a partially
ordered set. Moreover, it is directed. Indeed, we have seen above that
given
a,b\in I^{\rm ab}
, every connected component
Z
of
P_{a}\times_{U}P_{b}

lies in
({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
. Furthermore, it follows from
Lemma
7.7
that there is an object

(Y,g)\in({\mathcal{U}}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,D),F)
which is Galois and dominates
Z
.

We let
P_{c}
denote the abelianization of
Y
and let
g^{\prime}\colon P_{c}\to X

be the induced map. We have seen above that

(P_{c},g^{\prime})\in({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F)
. To show that

(P_{c},g^{\prime})
dominates
P_{a},P_{b}
, it suffices to show that
the function fields of
P_{a}
and
P_{b}
are dominated by the function field
of
P_{c}
. But this follows because the function field of

Z
dominates those of
P_{a}
and
P_{b}
and Lemma
7.8

says that the Galois groups of the function fields
of
P_{a}
and
P_{b}
are abelian.
We have thus shown that
I^{\rm ab}
is a directed set. An easier argument
shows that the same holds for
I
and
I^{{\rm Gal}}
. Clearly,
there are inclusions of directed sets
I^{\rm ab}\hookrightarrow I^{{\rm Gal}}\hookrightarrow I
.

By definition of the automorphism group of a fiber functor,
we have an isomorphism of profinite groups

\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{a\in I}{\varprojlim}}\ {\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})
. It follows from Lemma
7.7
that

(7.3)

\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}\ {\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}).

Now, we know that the transition maps of the
projective system
\{{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})\}_{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}
are surjective.
This implies that the same holds for the projective system of
commutator subgroups

\{[{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}),{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})]\}_{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}
.
It follows that the sequence

(7.4)

1\to{\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}[{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}),{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})]\to\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)\to{\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}({\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}))^{\rm ab}\to 1

is exact.

Since the projection

[\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D),\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)]\to[{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}),{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})]

is surjective for every
a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}
, this implies by

[
32
, Corollary 1.1.8]
that

\overline{[\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D),\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)]}={\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}[{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}),{\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})],

where the left hand side is the closure taken in

\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)
.
On the other hand, there is an exact sequence of topological groups
(by definition of
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
)

(7.5)

1\to\overline{[\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D),\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)]}\to\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)\to\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\to 1.

Comparing (
7.4
) and (
7.5
),
we get
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}\ ({\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}))^{\rm ab}
.

Finally, we note that the map
I^{{\rm Gal}}\to I^{\rm ab}
induced by

P_{a}\mapsto(P_{a})^{\rm ab}
is cofinal in
I^{\rm ab}
. It follows that

(7.6)

\pi_{1}(({\mathcal{U}}_{\rm ab}(X,D),F))={\underset{a\in I^{\rm ab}}{\varprojlim}}\ {\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a})\cong{\underset{a\in I^{{\rm Gal}}}{\varprojlim}}\ ({\rm Aut}_{U}(P_{a}))^{\rm ab}=\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D).

This proves the second part of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
I^{{\rm Gal}}_{D/K}
(resp.
I^{\rm ab}_{D/K}
) be the directed system of finite
Galois (resp. abelian) extensions
L/K
which have
ramifications bounded by
D
.
Then there are canonical isomorphisms of profinite groups

\pi^{{\rm div}}_{1}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{L\in I^{{\rm Gal}}_{D/K}}{\varprojlim}}{\rm Gal}(L/K);\ \ \pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\underset{L\in I^{\rm ab}_{D/K}}{\varprojlim}}{\rm Gal}(L/K).

Proof.

We follow the notations of Proposition
7.9
.
Using Lemma
7.8
together with
(
7.3
) and (
7.6
), we only need to
show that the correspondence
(U^{\prime},f)\mapsto k(U^{\prime})

defines isomorphisms of directed systems

I^{{\rm Gal}}\cong I^{{\rm Gal}}_{D/K}
and
I^{\rm ab}\cong I^{\rm ab}_{D/K}
.
But this is immediate.
∎

7.4.
Pontryagin duality

Our next goal is to identify the Pontryagin dual of
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)
.
Before doing so, we briefly recall this duality.
Let
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
denote the full subcategory of the category of topological
abelian groups consisting of those objects which are either profinite or
discrete abelian groups.
We shall consider
{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
as a discrete topological abelian group.
For any
G\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
, we let

G^{\vee}={\rm Hom}_{{\rm cont}}(G,{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}})
and consider it as a
topological abelian group with respect to the topology of pointwise
convergence.

Suppose that
G
is either profinite or
discrete torsion. Then
G^{\vee}\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
.
In this case, the Pontryagin duality theorem (see
[
32
, § 2.9]
)
says that
G^{\vee}
is the classical Pontryagin dual of
G
.
Moreover, the evaluation map
{\rm ev}_{G}\colon G\to(G^{\vee})^{\vee}
is an isomorphism of
topological abelian groups.
Note that this duality does not hold otherwise. For example,

{\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee}\cong{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
and
{({\mathbb{Z}}^{\vee})}^{\vee}\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
The evaluation map is the profinite completion map

{\mathbb{Z}}\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
In general, we can however say the following.

Lemma 7.11
.

The functor
G\mapsto G^{\vee}
from
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
to the category of
topological abelian groups is exact and the
map
{\rm ev}_{G}
is injective.

Proof.

Let

0\to G^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha}G\xrightarrow{\beta}G^{\prime\prime}\to 0

be an exact sequence in
{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
.
One can check by an easy inspection that

0\to G^{\prime\prime\vee}\to G^{\vee}\to G^{\prime\vee}

is exact.
We prove that the last arrow is surjective.

If
G
is discrete, then other groups are also discrete
and the assertion follows because
{{\mathbb{Q}}}/{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is injective
as an abelian group.
We suppose therefore that
G
is profinite. Then the other groups
are also profinite.
Since profinite groups are compact Hausdorff, we see that
G^{\prime}
is closed
in
G
. If
H={\rm Coker}(G^{\vee}\to G^{\prime\vee})\neq 0
, then
we have an exact sequence

0\to H^{\vee}\to G^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\alpha}G

using the Pontryagin duality theorem. Since
H\neq 0
, one knows that

H^{\vee}
can not be zero, again by the Pontryagin duality. But this
contradicts the assumption that
\alpha
is injective.

The second part of the lemma follows easily
from the first part and the example before the lemma,
using the fact that every torsion-free discrete
abelian group is a direct limit of finitely generated free abelian groups.
We leave out the details.
∎

Lemma 7.12
.

Let
G\in{\operatorname{\mathbf{Pfd}}}
and
H\subset G
a subgroup. Then
H
is dense
in
G
if and only if every element of
G^{\vee}
which annihilates
H
is
zero.

Proof.

Suppose

Proposition 7.16
.

The inclusion
{\rm fil}_{D}H^{1}(K)\hookrightarrow H^{1}(K)
defines an isomorphism

{\underset{L\in I^{\rm ab}_{D/K}}{\varinjlim}}H^{1}(L/K)\xrightarrow{\cong}{\rm fil}_{D}H^{1}(K).

Recall that
H^{1}(K)
is a torsion abelian group. We consider it a

Let

so that there is an exact
sequence of discrete torsion abelian groups

\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)}

commutative.

Proof.

The factorization follows from the construction of
\rho_{U/X}
,
definition of
\widetilde{C}_{U/X}
, Corollary
7.18
and
Theorem
8.1
.
∎

8.1.
Agreement with the Kato-Saito reciprocity map

For every
L\in I^{\rm ab}_{D/K}
, it was shown by Kato-Saito in

[
21
, § 3.7]
that there exists a closed subscheme

D^{\prime}\subset X
with support
C
such that
D^{\prime}\geq D
, and a reciprocity
map
\rho^{\prime}_{D^{\prime}|L}\colon C_{KS}(X,D^{\prime})\to{\rm Gal}(L/K)
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Assume that
U
is regular. Then the diagram

(8.4)

\textstyle{C(X,D^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{X|D^{\prime}}}
\textstyle{C(X,D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{X|D}}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C_{KS}(X,D^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho^{\prime}_{D^{\prime}|L}}
\textstyle{{\rm Gal}(L/K)}

is commutative. In particular,
\widetilde{\rho}_{U/X}
agrees with
the Kato-Saito reciprocity map given by
[
21
, Theorem 9.1(3)]
.

Proof.

It follows from the construction of the isomorphism
\psi_{X|D^{\prime}}
in
Theorem
3.8
that for every closed point

, the diagram

(8.5)

\textstyle{K^{M}_{0}(k(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C(X,D^{\prime})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{X|D^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{C_{KS}(X,D^{\prime})}

is commutative,
where the top horizontal arrow is the canonical map from the
Milnor
K
-theory of the Parshin chain

and the
diagonal arrow is the forget support map

H^{d}_{x}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D^{\prime})})\to H^{d}_{\rm nis}(X,{\mathcal{K}}^{M}_{d,(X,D^{\prime})})
.
It suffices therefore to show using
[
21
, Theorem 2.5]

that for every closed point
x\in U
,
the diagram (
8.4
) commutes if we replace
both
C(X,D^{\prime})
and

by
K^{M}_{0}(k(x))
.
But both maps are simply the Frobenius substitution by
the construction of
\rho_{U/X}
in Theorem
5.13

and by
[
21
, Proposition 3.8]
.
∎

8.2.
The reciprocity map for degree zero subgroups

Assume that
X
is proper over
k
and
U
is regular.
The structure map
X\to{\rm Spec\,}(k)
defines natural maps

\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X)\to{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)\cong\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
.
The latter map is surjective if
X
is geometrically connected.
Let
\deg^{\prime}
denote the composite map.
We denote the composite
map
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\twoheadrightarrow\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(X)\to{\rm Gal}(\overline{k}/k)

also by
\deg^{\prime}
.
We let
\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)_{0}={\rm Ker}(\deg^{\prime}\colon\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})

and
\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)_{0}={\rm Ker}(\deg^{\prime}\colon\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}})
.
We also have the degree map

\deg\colon C(X,D)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
by Proposition
4.8
.

Lemma 8.4
.

There exists an integer
n\geq 1

depending only on
U
and a commutative diagram of exact

(8.6)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{C(X,D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\deg}/n}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{X|D}}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{\deg^{\prime}}/n}
\textstyle{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

where the right vertical arrow is the profinite completion map.

Proof.

We need to show that the right square commutes.
We first show that this square commutes without dividing the
degree maps by any integer
n
.
Since the map
Z_{0}(U)\to C(X,D)
is surjective
by Theorem
3.8
and
[
21
, Theorem 2.5]
,
it suffices to show that for every closed point
x\in U
,
the diagram

(8.7)

\textstyle{K_{0}(k(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\rho_{k(x)}}
\scriptstyle{\deg}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(k(x))\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{q_{*}}
\textstyle{\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}}

commutes, where
q\colon{\rm Spec\,}(k(x))\to{\rm Spec\,}(k)
is the projection.
But this is classical.

We now let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be the images of the maps

\deg\colon C_{U/X}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\deg^{\prime}\colon\pi^{\rm ab}_{1}(U)\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
respectively.
We must have
M=n{\mathbb{Z}}
for some integer
n\geq 1
.
Moreover,
M
and
M^{\prime}
must also be the images of

\deg\colon C(X,D)\to{\mathbb{Z}}
and

\pi^{{\rm adiv}}_{1}(X,D)\to\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, respectively.
We thus get a commutative diagram of exact sequences

(8.8)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{0.}

M
with
{\mathbb{Z}}
via the map

.
By the functoriality of the profinite completion, there is a
commutative diagram

\textstyle{{\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{n}
\textstyle{{{\mathbb{Z}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where the vertical arrows are profinite completion maps.
Since
{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m\xrightarrow{n}{{\mathbb{Z}}}/m
is continuous for all

,
it follows that the bottom horizontal arrow is
continuous. It follows from the universal property of profinite
completion and (
8.8
)
that the image of this bottom horizontal arrow lies inside

.
Since the image

see that
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}\xrightarrow{n}\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}}
is an embedding into

.

On the other hand, the Chebotarev-Lang density theorem
(see
[
35
, Theorem 5.8.16]
) says that the image of

\rho_{U/X}
is dense. Hence, the image of

C(X,D)
is dense in

.

.
We therefore conclude that

.
It also follows from this that the right vertical arrow of

∎

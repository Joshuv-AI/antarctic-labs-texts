IMPROVING CASAZZA-KALTON-CHRISTENSEN-VAN EIJNDHOVEN PERTURBATION WITH APPLICATIONS
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be an invertible Lipschitz map. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then we prove that
T
is an invertible Lipschitz map. This improves 25 years old Casazza-Kalton-Christensen-van Eijndhoven perturbation. It also improves 28 years old Soderlind-Campanato perturbation and 2 years old Barbagallo-Ernst-Thera perturbation. We give applications to the theory of metric frames. The notion of Lipschitz atomic decomposition for Banach spaces is also introduced.

Keywords
: Paley-Wiener perturbation, Lipschitz map, metric frame, atomic decomposition.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 26A16, 47A55, 42C15.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
I_{\mathcal{X}}
be the identity operator on
\mathcal{X}
. Carl Neumann’s classical result says that if
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is a bounded linear operator such that
\|T-I_{\mathcal{X}}\|<1
, then
T
is invertible
[
39
]
. Following two results are consequences of this result. They are known as
Paley-Wiener theorems
.

(1)

Sequences close to orthonormal bases in Hilbert spaces are Riesz bases
[
52
,
40
]
.

(2)

Sequences close to Schauder bases in Banach spaces are Schauder bases
[
4
,
44
]
.

History of Paley-Wiener theorems are nicely presented in
[
1
,
43
]
. It was in the setting of Hilbert spaces, Paley-Wiener theorem was first generalized by Pollard
[
42
]
, second generalized by Sz. Nagy
[
21
]
and third generalized by Hilding
[
33
]
. Hilding proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
33
]

(
Hilding perturbation
) Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space. If a linear operator
T:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is such that there exists
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right)
with

\displaystyle\|Th-h\|\leq\lambda\|Th\|+\lambda\|h\|,\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H},

then
T
is bounded, invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\|h\|\leq\|Th\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\|h\|,\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H};

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda}{1+\lambda}\|h\|\leq\|T^{-1}h\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda}{1-\lambda}\|h\|,\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

It took around 50 years to improve Theorem
1.1
to the most generality for Banach spaces.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
9
,
49
,
11
]

(
Casazza-Kalton-Christensen-van Eijndhoven perturbation
) Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a bounded invertible operator. If a linear operator
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is such that there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
with

\displaystyle\|Tx-Sx\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

then
T
is bounded, invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|Sx\|\leq\|Tx\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|Sx\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X};

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\|S\|}\|y\|\leq\|T^{-1}y\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|S^{-1}\|\|y\|,\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

There is an improvement of Theorem
1.2
which is due to Guo with an extra assumption that
T
is bounded.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
26
]

Let
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a bounded invertible operator. If a bounded linear operator
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
is such that there exist
\lambda_{1}\in\left[0,1\right)
and
\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right]
with

\displaystyle\|Tx-Sx\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X},

then
T
is invertible. Further, for every
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
1>\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\|TS^{-1}\|<1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon\|TS^{-1}\|}{1+\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon}\|Sx\|\leq\|Tx\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\|TS^{-1}\|}{1-\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon}\|Sx\|,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X};

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon}{1+\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\|TS^{-1}\|}\frac{1}{\|S\|}\|y\|\leq\|T^{-1}y\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon}{1-\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon\|TS^{-1}\|}\|S^{-1}\|\|y\|,\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{Y}.

Theorem
1.2
and its variants are useful in various studies such as stability of frames for Hilbert spaces
[
11
]
, stability of frames and atomic decompositions for Banach spaces
[
47
]
, stability of frames for Hilbert C*-modules
[
27
]
, stability of G-frames
[
48
]
, multipliers for Hilbert spaces
[
46
]
, quantum detection problem
[
5
]
, continuous frames
[
24
]
, fusion frames
[
10
]
, operator representations of frames (dynamics of frames)
[
15
]
, pseudo-inverses of operators
[
22
]
, outer inverses of operators
[
51
]
, shift-invariant spaces
[
35
]
, frame sequences
[
17
]
, sampling
[
53
]
etc.

The main objective of this paper is to generalize Theorem
1.2
for Lipschitz functions between Banach spaces. We do this in Theorem
2.9
. We show that our result generalizes Soderlind-Campanato Perturbation (Theorem
3.1
) and Barbagallo-Ernst-Thera perturbation (Theorem
3.2
). We then give an application to the theory of frames for metric spaces. Further, the notion of Lipschitz atomic decomposition for Banach spaces is introduced and a perturbation result is derived using Theorem
2.9
.

2.
Improving Casazza-Kalton-Christensen-van Eijndhoven perturbation

Let
\mathcal{M}

be a metric space and
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space. Recall that a function
f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is said to be Lipschitz if there exists
b>0
such that

\displaystyle\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq b\,d(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

A Lipschitz function
f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is said to be bi-Lipschitz if there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle a\,d(x,y)\leq\|f(x)-f(y)\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

Definition 2.1
.

[
50
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space.

(i)

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a metric space. The collection
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})

is defined as
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\{f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\operatorname{is~Lipschitz}\}.
For
f\in\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})
, the Lipschitz number
is defined as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Lip}(f)\coloneqq\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{M},x\neq y}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|}{d(x,y)}.

(ii)

Let
(\mathcal{M},0)
be a pointed metric space. The collection
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})

is defined as
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\{f:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\operatorname{is~Lipschitz~and}f(0)=0\}.

For
f\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})
, the Lipschitz norm
is defined as

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}_{0}}\coloneqq\sup_{x,y\in\mathcal{M},x\neq y}\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|}{d(x,y)}.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
50
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space.

(i)

If
\mathcal{M}
is a metric space, then
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})
is a semi-normed vector space w.r.t. the semi-norm
\operatorname{Lip}(\cdot)
.

(ii)

If
(\mathcal{M},0)
is a pointed metric space, then
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{X})
is a Banach space w.r.t. the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\operatorname{Lip}_{0}}
. Further,
\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})\coloneqq\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X})
is a unital Banach algebra. In particular, if
T\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
satisfies
\|T-I_{\mathcal{X}}\|_{\operatorname{Lip}_{0}}<1,

then
T
is invertible and
T^{-1}\in\operatorname{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
.

We now develop perturbation result for Lipschitz functions using various results. Our developments are motivated from the linear version of improvement of Paley-Wiener theorem by van Eijndhoven
[
49
]
. Since
\text{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
is a unital Banach algebra, we can talk about the notion of spectrum and resolvents. In the remaining part of the paper, the spectrum of an element
T\in\text{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
is denoted by
\sigma(T)
and the resolvent by
\rho(T).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
T\in\text{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
. Suppose there are
\alpha_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{0}>0
such that

(1)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|\geq\beta_{0}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},\forall\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}.

Then
(-\infty,\alpha_{0}]\subseteq\rho(T).

Proof.

If
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{R}=\emptyset
, then there is nothing to argue. So let
\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{R}\neq\emptyset
. Since
\sigma(T)
is non empty, define

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\coloneqq\min\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda\in\sigma(T)\cap\mathbb{R}\}.

Claim:
\lambda_{0}\geq\alpha_{0}
. If this is false, we have
\lambda_{0}<\alpha_{0}
. From the assumption we then have

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\lambda_{0}(x-y)\|\geq\beta_{0}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}

which implies

\displaystyle\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x-(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})y\|\geq\beta_{0}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}

which says that
T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}}
is injective. We now try to show that
T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}}
is surjective. Let
y\in\mathcal{X}
. Define

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\coloneqq\lambda_{0}-\frac{1}{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

From the definition of
\lambda_{0}
we then have
T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}}
is invertible for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using Inequality (
1
) we have

\displaystyle\|(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})x-(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})y\|\geq\beta_{0}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

which gives

\displaystyle\|u-v\|\geq\beta_{0}\|(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}u-(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{X},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}

which implies

\displaystyle\|(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\frac{1}{\beta_{0}},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now define

\displaystyle x_{n}\coloneqq(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}y,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Now using resolvent identity in Banach algebra
[
20
]
we get

\displaystyle\|x_{n}-x_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right|\|(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}(T-\alpha_{m}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}y\|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right|\|(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|(T-\alpha_{m}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|y\|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{m}\right|\frac{1}{\beta_{0}^{2}}\|y\|,\quad\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
is Cauchy and it converges to an element, say
x\in\mathcal{X}
. Finally

\displaystyle\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x-y\|

\displaystyle=\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x-(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x-(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x_{n}\|+\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x_{n}-(T-\alpha_{n}I_{\mathcal{X}})x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|x_{n}-x\|+|\alpha_{n}-\lambda_{0}|\|x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|x_{n}-x\|+\frac{1}{n}\|x_{n}\|,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Letting
n\to\infty
, we get
(T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}})x=y
. Thus
T-\lambda_{0}I_{\mathcal{X}}
is invertible. Hence
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma(T)\cap\rho(T)=\emptyset
which is a contradiction which proves the claim as well as the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Note that Lemma
2.3
also holds for real Banach spaces. In fact, if spectrum
\sigma(T)
is empty then the lemma holds trivially and if it is non empty, then it becomes a compact set and proof of Lemma
2.3
can be carried over. Due to this, all coming results in this paper are valid for real Banach spaces also.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
T\in\text{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X})
. Suppose there are
\alpha_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{1}>0
such that

(2)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|\geq\beta_{1}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},\forall\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}.

Then
(-\infty,\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1})\subseteq\rho(T).

Proof.

Let
0<\eta<\beta_{1}
. Define
\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\alpha_{1}+\eta
and
\beta_{0}\coloneqq\beta_{1}-\eta
. Let
\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
.

Case (i):
\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}
. Then from Inequality (
2
),
\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|\geq\beta_{1}\|x-y\|,\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}
.

Case (ii):
Let
\alpha_{1}<\alpha\leq\alpha_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|

\displaystyle\geq\|Tx-Ty-\alpha_{1}(x-y)\|-(\alpha-\alpha_{1})\|x-y\|

\displaystyle\geq(\beta_{1}-\eta)\|x-y\|=\beta_{0}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Lemma
2.3
now gives
(-\infty,\alpha_{0}]\subseteq\rho(T).
Result follows by observing that

\displaystyle(-\infty,\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1})=\bigcup\limits_{0<\eta<\beta_{1}}(-\infty,\alpha_{1}+\eta].

∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space,
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a map,
T0=0
and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

(3)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

We have

\displaystyle\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)\subseteq\rho(T).

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\|x-y\|\leq\|T^{-1}x-T^{-1}y\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\leq\|T\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\leq\|T^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}.

Proof.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{X}.
Then using Inequality (
3
)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle\leq\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|+\|x-y\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|+\|x-y\|

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda_{1})\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle\implies\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|

and

\displaystyle\|x-y\|

\displaystyle\leq\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|+\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|+\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+(1+\lambda_{2})\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle\implies\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|.

Hence
T
is Lipschitz and (i) holds. Let
\alpha\leq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|

\displaystyle=\|(1-\alpha)(x-y)-(x-y-(Tx-Ty))\|

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha)\|x-y\|-\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha)\|x-y\|-\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|-\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha-\lambda_{1})\|x-y\|-\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha-\lambda_{1})\|x-y\|-\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|+\lambda_{2}\alpha\|x-y\|

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\alpha)\|x-y\|-\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|

which implies

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-\alpha(x-y)\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\alpha-\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\alpha}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\lambda_{1}-(1-\lambda_{2})\alpha}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|\geq\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|.

By applying Theorem
2.5
for
\alpha_{1}=0
and
\beta_{1}=\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}
we get
(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}})\subseteq\rho(T).
Since
0\in(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}})
,
T
is invertible. Using Inequality (
4
) we then get

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\|u-v\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{X}

which gives (iii). Finally (iv) follows from (i) and (iii).
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S\in\text{Lip}_{0}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be invertible. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map,
T0=0
and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha S-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}}\|u-v\|\leq\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|S^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\|T\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}}\leq\|T^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|S^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}.

Proof.

Define
R\coloneqq TS^{-1}
. Then Inequality (
5
) gives

\displaystyle\|TS^{-1}u-TS^{-1}v-(SS^{-1}u-SS^{-1}v)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|SS^{-1}u-SS^{-1}v\|+\lambda_{2}\|TS^{-1}u-TS^{-1}v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\|Ru-Rv-(u-v)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|u-v\|+\lambda_{2}\|Ru-Rv\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

By applying Theorem
2.6
to
R
we get the following.

(i)

R
is Lipschitz hence
T
is Lipschitz. Further,

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|R(Sx)-R(Sy)\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

But
\|R(Sx)-R(Sy)\|=\|Tx-Ty\|
,
\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha I_{\mathcal{X}}-R
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
. Since
S
is invertible we then have
\alpha S-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

R
is invertible hence
T
is invertible. Further,

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}}\|u-v\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\|S\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}}\|R^{-1}u-R^{-1}v\|\leq\|S^{-1}(R^{-1}u)-S^{-1}(R^{-1}v)\|

\displaystyle=\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\|S^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|R^{-1}u-R^{-1}v\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|S^{-1}\|_{\text{Lip}_{0}}\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

(iv)

This follows easily from (i) and (iii).

∎

Our next job is to derive the results by removing the condition
T0=0
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space,
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a map and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha I_{\mathcal{X}}-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\|x-y\|\leq\|T^{-1}x-T^{-1}y\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\leq\text{Lip}(T)\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\leq\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle\tilde{T}x\coloneqq Tx-T0,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

Then
\tilde{T}0=0
and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{T}x-\tilde{T}y-(x-y)\|

\displaystyle=\|Tx-Ty-(x-y)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|\tilde{T}x-\tilde{T}y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Applying Theorem
2.6
and using the fact that ‘a map is bijective if and only if its translate is bijective’, proof is complete.
∎

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S\in\text{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be invertible. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha S-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\|u-v\|\leq\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1})\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\leq\text{Lip}(T)\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\leq\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1}).

Proof.

Define

and the proof is similar to proof of Theorem
2.7
.
∎

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S\in\text{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be invertible. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

(6)

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq((\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|)^{p}+(\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha S-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\|u-v\|\leq\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1})\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}}{1+\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\leq\text{Lip}(T)\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}}{1-\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\leq\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1}).

Proof.

Note that if
r,s\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle(r^{p}+s^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq r+s\quad\text{if}\quad p\geq 1.

Hence Inequality (

) gives

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|

\displaystyle\leq((\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|)^{p}+(\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Result follows by applying Theorem
2.9
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
0<p<1
. Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S\in\text{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be invertible. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map and there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,2^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\right)
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq((\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|)^{p}+(\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then

(i)

T
is Lipschitz and

\displaystyle\frac{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha S-T
is invertible for all
\alpha\in\left(-\infty,\frac{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}\right)
.

(iii)

T
is invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\|u-v\|\leq\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\frac{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1})\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\leq\text{Lip}(T)\leq\frac{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}\text{Lip}(S)\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\leq\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\frac{1+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}}{1-2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}}\text{Lip}(S^{-1}).

Proof.

Note that if
r,s\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle(r^{p}+s^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}(r+s)\quad\text{if}\quad p<1.

Hence Inequality (
6
) gives

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|

\displaystyle\leq((\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|)^{p}+(\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|)^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+2^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Result follows by applying Theorem
2.9
. ∎

Next we generalize Corollary 1 in
[

]

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and
S\in\text{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be invertible. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
be a map and there exists
\lambda\in\left[0,1\right)
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq\lambda\|Sx-Sy\|+\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then
T
is Lipschitz, invertible and

\displaystyle\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\frac{2}{1-\lambda}\text{Lip}(S^{-1}).

Proof.

Define
R\coloneqq TS^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\|Ru-Rv-(u-v)\|\leq\lambda\|u-v\|+\|Ru-Rv\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

Note that
\text{Lip}(R)\neq 0
. Let

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{1,\frac{1-\lambda}{\text{Lip}(R)}\right\}.

Define

\lambda_{1}\coloneqq\lambda-\varepsilon\text{Lip}(R)
and
\lambda_{2}\coloneqq 1-\varepsilon
. Then
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right)
and

\displaystyle\|Ru-Rv-(u-v)\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\|u-v\|+\|Ru-Rv\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\|u-v\|+\|Ru-Rv\|+\varepsilon(\text{Lip}(R)\|u-v\|-\|Ru-Rv\|)

\displaystyle=(\lambda+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(R))\|u-v\|+(1-\varepsilon)\|Ru-Rv\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

By applying Theorem
2.8
we get that
R
is Lipschitz, invertible and

\displaystyle\text{Lip}(R^{-1})\leq\frac{2-\varepsilon}{1-(\lambda+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(R))}.

Since
\varepsilon
can be made arbitrarily small, we must have

\displaystyle\text{Lip}(R^{-1})\leq\frac{2}{1-\lambda}.

Substituting the expression of
R
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S^{-1})}\text{Lip}(T^{-1})\leq\text{Lip}(ST^{-1})=\text{Lip}(R^{-1})\leq\frac{2}{1-\lambda}.

∎

Theorem 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
,
\mathcal{Y}
be Banach spaces and

\lambda_{2}\in\left[0,1\right]

\displaystyle\|Tx-Ty-(Sx-Sy)\|\leq\lambda_{1}\|Sx-Sy\|+\lambda_{2}\|Tx-Ty\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Then
T
is Lipschitz invertible. Further, for every
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
1>\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})<1
, we have

(i)

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}{1+\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon}\|Sx-Sy\|\leq\|Tx-Ty\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}{1-\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon}\|Sx-Sy\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

(ii)

\alpha S-T
is invertible for all

.

(iii)

T

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon}{1+\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}\frac{1}{\text{Lip}(S)}\|u-v\|\leq\|T^{-1}u-T^{-1}v\|\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon}{1-\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}\text{Lip}(S^{-1})\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v\in\mathcal{Y}.

(iv)

We have

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{1}-\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}{1+\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon}\text{Lip}(S)\leq\text{Lip}(T)\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{1}+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(TS^{-1})}{1-\lambda_{2}+\varepsilon}\text{Lip}(S)\quad\text{ and }

Proof.

Define
R\coloneqq TS^{-1}
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
satisfying
1>\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\|Ru-Rv-(u-v)\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\|u-v\|+\lambda_{2}\|Ru-Rv\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\|u-v\|+(\lambda_{2}-\varepsilon)\|Ru-Rv\|+\varepsilon\text{Lip}(R)\|u-v\|

Theorem 3.1

Theorem 3.2
.

[

]
(

\mathcal{X}
be a real Banach space,

Remark 3.3

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\|S\{c_{n}\}_{n}-S\{d_{n}\}_{n}\|+\lambda_{2}\|T\{c_{n}\}_{n}-T\{d_{n}\}_{n}\|

(8)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\|\{c_{n}-d_{n}\}_{n}\|,\quad\forall\{c_{n}\}_{n},\{d_{n}\}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}.

Given

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\|x-y\|+\lambda_{2}\|T\theta_{f}x-T\theta_{f}y\|+\mu\|\theta_{f}x-\theta_{f}y\|

now says that the map

(iii)

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}
, for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
,

then we say that
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
is a
Lipschitz atomic decomposition
for
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. We say constant
a
as lower Lipschitz atomic bound
and constant
b
as upper Lipschitz atomic bound.

In
[
8
]
it is proved that not every Banach space admits an atomic decomposition. Motivated from this, we ask the following open problem.

Question 3.8
.

Classify Banach spaces which admit Lipschitz atomic decompositions.

Following proposition gives various examples of Lipschitz atomic decompositions.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
be an atomic decomposition for a Banach space
\mathcal{Y}
w.r.t. BK-space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and let
A:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a bi-Lipschitz map such that there exists a linear map
A:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
satisfying
BA=I_{\mathcal{X}}
. Then
(\{f_{n}\coloneqq g_{n}A\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\coloneqq B\omega_{n}\}_{n})
is a Lipschitz atomic decomposition
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. In particular, if a Banach space admits a Schauder basis, then it admits a Lipschitz atomic decomposition.

Particular case of Proposition
3.9
gives the following example.

Example 3.10
.

Let
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
be an atomic decomposition for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t. a BK-space
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Let
T:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
be any bi-Lipschitz map. Define
A:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto(x,Tx)\in\mathcal{X}\oplus\mathcal{X}
and
B:\mathcal{X}\oplus\mathcal{X}\ni(x,y)\mapsto x\in\mathcal{X}
. Then
A
is bi-Lipschitz,
B
is linear and satisfies
BA=I_{\mathcal{X}}
. Hence
(\{f_{n}\coloneqq g_{n}A\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\coloneqq B\omega_{n}\}_{n})
is a Lipschitz atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.

At this point it seems that it is best to develop some theory of Lipschitz atomic decompositions before giving an application of Theorem
2.9
.

Proposition 2.3 in
[
8
]
shows that under certain conditions, there is a close relationship between Banach frames and atomic decompositions. Following is the non-linear version of that result.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a Banach space and
\mathcal{M}_{d}
be a BK-space. Let

\{f_{n}\}_{n}
be a sequence in
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{K})
and
S:\mathcal{M}_{d}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a bounded linear operator. If the standard unit
vectors
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
form a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{M}_{d}
, then the following are equivalent.

(i)

(\{f_{n}\}_{n},S)
is a metric frame for
\mathcal{X}
.

(ii)

(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{Se_{n}\}_{n})
is a Lipschitz atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}

w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.

Proof.

We set
\tau_{n}=Se_{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
and see that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)Se_{n}=S\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)e_{n}\right)=S\left(\{f_{n}(x)\}_{n}\right),\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

∎

Well established dilation theory of frames for Hilbert spaces says that frames are images of Riesz bases under projections
[
19
,
34
,
29
]
. This result has been extended to frames and atomic decompositions for Banach spaces
[
32
,
30
,
37
,
31
,
8
]
. In the next theorem we derive a dilation result for Lipschitz atomic decompositions. We need a proposition to use in the theorem.

Proposition 3.12
.

[
38
]

A sequence
\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}
in a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
is a Schauder basis for
\mathcal{X}
if and only if the following three conditions hold.

(i)

\tau_{n}\neq 0
for all
n
.

(ii)

There exists
b>0
such that for every sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k}
of scalars and every pair of natural numbers
n<m
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\tau_{k}\right\|\leq b\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{m}a_{k}\tau_{k}\right\|.

(iii)

\overline{\operatorname{span}}\{\tau_{n}\}_{n}=\mathcal{X}
.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
be a Lipschitz atomic decomposition for a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}_{d}
.
Then there is a Banach space
\mathcal{Z}
with a Schauder basis
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n}
, an injective map
\theta:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}
and a
map
P:\mathcal{Z}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
satisfying
P(\mathcal{Z})=\mathcal{X}
,

P^{2}=P
and

P\omega_{n}=\theta\tau_{n},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We generalize the idea of proof of Theorem 2.6 in
[
8
]
(which is motivated from the arguments in
[
41
]
) to non-linear setting. Let
c_{00}
be the vector space of scalar sequences with only finitely many non-zero terms. Let
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
be the standard unit vectors in
c_{00}
.

Case (i):
\tau_{n}\neq 0
, for all
n
. We define a norm on
c_{00}
as follows. Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n}\in c_{00}
. Define

(9)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}\right\|\coloneqq\max_{n}\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\tau_{k}\right\|.

Proposition
3.12
then tells that
\{e_{n}\}_{n}
is a Schauder basis for the completion of
c_{00}
, call as
\mathcal{Z}
w.r.t. just defined norm. Define

\displaystyle\theta:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\theta x\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)e_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}.

From the first condition of the definition of Lipschitz atomic decomposition, from Definition
9
and from the construction of
\mathcal{Z}
, it follows that
\theta
is well-defined. From the third condition of definition of atomic decomposition,
\theta
is injective. We next define

\displaystyle\Gamma:\mathcal{Z}\ni\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}\mapsto\Gamma\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}\right)\coloneqq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{X}.

By verifying
\Gamma
is bounded linear on dense subset
c_{00}
of
\mathcal{Z}
, we see that
\Gamma
is bounded on
\mathcal{Z}
. Then

(10)

\displaystyle\Gamma\theta x=\Gamma\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)e_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\tau_{n}=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

So if we define
P\coloneqq\theta\Gamma
, then
P^{2}=\theta\Gamma\theta\Gamma=\theta\Gamma=P
. Equation (
10
) tells that
P(\mathcal{Z})=\mathcal{X}
. We next see that
Pe_{n}=\theta\Gamma e_{n}=\theta\tau_{n}
,
\forall n
. Thus we can take
\omega_{n}=e_{n}
, for all
n
to get the result.

Case (ii):
\tau_{n}=0
, for some
n
. Let
J=\{n:\tau_{n}\neq 0\}
. We now apply case (i) to the collection
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in J}
. Let
\theta
,
\mathcal{Z}
,
\Gamma
and
P
be as in the case (i). Without affecting the definition of atomic decomposition, we can take
f_{n}=0
for all
n\in J^{c}
. Now consider the space
\mathcal{Z}\oplus\ell^{2}(J^{c})
and let
\{\rho_{n}\}_{n\in J^{c}}
be an orthonormal basis for
\ell^{2}(J^{c})
. Define
Q:\mathcal{Z}\oplus\ell^{2}(J^{c})\ni z\oplus y\mapsto Q(z\oplus y)\coloneqq Pz\oplus 0\in\mathcal{Z}\oplus\ell^{2}(J^{c})
. Now the space
\mathcal{Z}\oplus\ell^{2}(J^{c})
has Schauder basis
\{\tau_{n}\oplus 0,0\oplus\rho_{m}\}_{n\in J,m\in J^{c}}
and
Q
satisfy the conclusions. Thus we can take
\omega_{n}=e_{n}
, for all
n\in J
and
\omega_{n}=\rho_{n}
, for all
n\in J^{c}
to get the result.
∎

We leave the further study of Lipschitz atomic decomposition to future work and end the paper with an application of Theorem
2.9
.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(\{f_{n}\}_{n},\{\tau_{n}\}_{n})
be a Lipschitz atomic decomposition with lower Lipschitz atomic bound
a
and upper Lipschitz atomic bound
b
for
\mathcal{X}
w.r.t.
\mathcal{M}_{d}
. Let
\{\omega_{n}\}_{n}
be a collection in
\mathcal{X}
and suppose that there exist
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\mu\geq 0
such that the following conditions hold.

(i)

For each
x\in\mathcal{X}
, the series
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\omega_{n}
converges in
\mathcal{X}
.

(ii)

\max\{\lambda_{2},\lambda_{1}+\mu b\}<1
and

(iii)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(c_{n}-d_{n})(\tau_{n}-\omega_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(c_{n}-d_{n})\tau_{n}\right\|+\lambda_{2}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(c_{n}-d_{n})\omega_{n}\right\|

(11)

\displaystyle\quad+\mu\|\{c_{n}-d_{n}\}_{n}\|,\quad\forall\{c_{n}\}_{n},\{d_{n}\}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}.

Then there exists a collection
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
in
\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{X},\mathbb{K})
such that
(\{g_{n}\}_{n},\{\omega_{n}\}_{n})
is a Lipschitz atomic decomposition for
\mathcal{X}
with lower and upper Lipschitz atomic bounds

\displaystyle\frac{a(1-\lambda_{2})}{1+\lambda_{1}+\mu b},\quad\frac{b(1+\lambda_{2})}{1-(\lambda_{1}+\mu b)}

respectively.

Proof.

From the first condition we get that the map
T:\mathcal{X}\ni x\mapsto\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\omega_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
is well-defined. Now using Inequality (
3.14
),

\displaystyle\|x-y-(Tx-Ty)\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y))\tau_{n}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y))\omega_{n}\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y))(\tau_{n}-\omega_{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{1}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y))\tau_{n}\right\|+\lambda_{2}\left\|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y))\omega_{n}\right\|+\mu\|\{f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)\}_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\left\|x-y\right\|+\lambda_{2}\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|+\mu\|\{f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)\}_{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq(\lambda_{1}+\mu b)\left\|x-y\right\|+\lambda_{2}\left\|Tx-Ty\right\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Theorem
2.9
then says that
T
is Lipschitz, invertible and

\displaystyle\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}+\mu b}\leq\text{Lip}(T)^{-1}\leq\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-(\lambda_{1}+\mu b)}.

Define
g_{n}\coloneqq f_{n}T^{-1}
,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\{g_{n}(x)\}_{n}\in\mathcal{M}_{d}
, for each
x\in\mathcal{X}
and

\displaystyle\|\{g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)\}_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\{f_{n}(T^{-1}x)-f_{n}(T^{-1}y)\}_{n}\|_{n}\leq b\|T^{-1}x-T^{-1}y\|

\displaystyle\leq b\frac{1+\lambda_{2}}{1-(\lambda_{1}+\mu b)}\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},

\displaystyle a\frac{1-\lambda_{2}}{1+\lambda_{1}+\mu b}\|x-y\|

\displaystyle\leq a\|T^{-1}x-T^{-1}y\|\leq\|\{f_{n}(T^{-1}x)-f_{n}(T^{-1}y)\}_{n}\|

\displaystyle=\|\{g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)\}_{n}\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X}.

Finally

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}(x)\omega_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(T^{-1}x)\omega_{n}=T(T^{-1}x)=x,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

∎

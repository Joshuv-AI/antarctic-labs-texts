Algebras of Generalized Singular Integral Operators with Cauchy kernel
Yuanqi Sang

Abstract
. For bounded Lebesgue measurable functions f , g , ϕ f,g,\phi and ψ \psi on the unit circle, P + ​ f ​ P + + P − ​ g ​ P + + P + ​ ϕ ​ P − + P − ​ ψ ​ P − P_{+}fP_{+}+P_{-}gP_{+}+P_{+}\phi P_{-}+P_{-}\psi P_{-} is called a generalized singular integral operator (GSIO) on L 2 ​ ( 𝕋 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{T}) , where P + P_{+} is the Riesz projection, P − = I − P + . P_{-}=I-P_{+}. In this paper, we relate GSIOs to a number of operators, including Cauchy singular integral operator, (dual) truncated Toeplitz operator, Foguel-Hankel operator, multiplication operator, Toeplitz plus Hankel operator etc. We establish the short exact sequences associated of the C ∗ − C^{*}- algebras generated by GSIOs with bounded or quasi-continuous symbols. As a consequence we obtain the spectra of various classes of GSIOs, the spectral inclusion theorem and comput the Fredholm index of GSIOs. Moreover, we gave the necessary and sufficient conditions for invertibility(Fredholmness) of GSIOs via Winer-Hopf factorization.

2.
Preliminaries

The generalized singular
integral operator

R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}f&\phi\\
g&\psi\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}
can be expressed as an operator matrix with respect to
the decomposition

L^{2}(\mathbb{T})=H^{2}\oplus\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}},
the result
is of the form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&H^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
H_{g}&\tilde{T}_{\psi}\end{pmatrix},

(2.1)

where
T_{f}
denote the Toeplitz operator on
H^{2}
such that

\displaystyle T_{f}x=P_{+}(fx),\quad x\in H^{2};

H_{g}
denote the Hankel operator on
H^{2}
such that

\displaystyle H_{g}x=P_{-}(gx),\quad x\in H^{2};

H^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}
denote the adjoint of Hankel operator such that

\displaystyle H^{*}_{\bar{\beta}}y=P_{+}(\phi y),\quad y\in\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}};

\tilde{T}_{\psi}
denote the dual Toeplitz operator on
\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}}
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{\psi}y=P_{-}(\psi y),\quad y\in\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}}.

Converse, if an operator
T
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
has form (
2.1
), then
T
is a GSIO.
Moreover, the generalized singular
integral operator

R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}f&\phi\\
g&\psi\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}
is unitarily equivalent to an operator matrix on
H_{2}^{2}.

To illustrate this, we need to introduce two useful operators and their properties.
For
x\in L^{2},
define

\displaystyle Vx(z)

\displaystyle=\bar{z}\overline{x(z)};

\displaystyle Jx(z)

\displaystyle=\bar{z}x(\bar{z}).

Note that
V
is an anti-unitary operator and
U
is an unitary operator, and they have the following properties:

(1)

\langle Vx,Vy\rangle=\langle y,x\rangle,

\langle Ux,Uy\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle;

(2)

VM_{f}V=M_{\bar{f}},

UM_{f}U=M_{\tilde{f}},

where

\tilde{f}(z)=f(\bar{z});

(3)

VP_{-}=P_{+}V,

UP_{-}=P_{+}U;

(4)

VH^{2}=\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}},

UH^{2}=\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}};

(5)

Uz^{n}=Vz^{n}=\bar{z}^{n+1}.

Using the operator
U,
for
g\in L^{2},
we can define the Hankel operator on
H^{2}
by

\Gamma_{g}=UH_{g}.

The operator

\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&U\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}=H^{2}\oplus\bar{z}\overline{H^{2}}\rightarrow H^{2}\oplus H^{2}

is unitary.
A simple computation gives

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&U\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&H^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
H_{g}&S_{\psi}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&U\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&H^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}U\\
UH_{g}&US_{\psi}U\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&UP_{-}M_{\psi}P_{-}U\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&P_{+}UM_{\psi}UP_{+}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&P_{+}M_{\tilde{\psi}}P_{+}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma^{*}_{\bar{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&T_{\tilde{\psi}}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma_{\tilde{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&T_{\tilde{\psi}}\end{pmatrix}.

This shows that
the operator
R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}f&\phi\\
g&\psi\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}:L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}
is unitary equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{f}&\Gamma_{\tilde{\phi}}\\
\Gamma_{g}&T_{\tilde{\psi}}\end{pmatrix}:H^{2}\oplus H^{2}\rightarrow H^{2}\oplus H^{2}.

Therefore,
R(\bar{z},0,\phi,\bar{z})
is unitary equivalent to the Foguel-Hankel operator
[
4
]

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T^{*}_{z}&\Gamma_{\tilde{\phi}}\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix}.

Proof.

In particular,
QR_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&z\\
z&z\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}=R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&z\\
z&z\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}Q

and
R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&z\\
z&z\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}
is the bilateral shift. Since the commutant of the
bilateral shift is the set of all multiplications
[
18
, 146]
, it follows that
Q=M_{\chi_{\Delta}},
where
\chi_{\Delta}
is a characteristic function.
Note that

\displaystyle R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}Q=

\displaystyle QR_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}T_{\chi_{\Delta}}&H^{*}_{\chi_{\Delta}}\\
H_{\chi_{\Delta}}&\tilde{T}_{\chi_{\Delta}}\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}T_{\chi_{\Delta}}&H^{*}_{\chi_{\Delta}}\\
H_{\chi_{\Delta}}&\tilde{T}_{\chi_{\Delta}}\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}T_{\chi_{\Delta}}&T_{z}H^{*}_{\chi_{\Delta}}\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}T_{\chi_{\Delta}}T_{z}&0\\
T_{\chi_{\Delta}}H_{\chi_{\Delta}}&0\end{pmatrix},

implies

\displaystyle T_{z}T_{\chi_{\Delta}}=T_{\chi_{\Delta}}T_{z}.

Since the commutant of

is the set of all analytic Toeplitz operators on

[

,
it follows that

\chi_{\Delta}
is
0
or

, and

or
Q=0.
This contradicts our assumption.
Therefore
\mathfrak{R}_{C(\mathbb{T})}
is irreducible.

Applying the formula

yields

\displaystyle R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}-R_{\begin{pmatrix}\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\\

0&0\end{smallmatrix}\end{pmatrix}}=1\otimes 1.

where

By

∎

Definition 5.1

The relation

Theorem 5.3
.

6.
Applications

6.1.
The Spectral Inclusion Theorem

In the theory of Toeplitz operator, the spectrum of
T_{\phi}
always includes
the essential range of
\phi.
Corollary
4.4
shows that

\displaystyle\emph{Ran}_{ess}{f}\cup\emph{Ran}_{ess}{\psi}\subset\sigma(R(f,g,\phi,\psi)).

Hence, for the bounded singular integral operator
R_{\alpha,\beta},
we have

\displaystyle\emph{Ran}_{ess}{\alpha}\cup\emph{Ran}_{ess}{\beta}\subset\sigma(R_{\alpha,\beta}),

for the bounded dual truncated Toeplitz operator
D_{\phi},
we have

\displaystyle\emph{Ran}_{ess}{\phi}\subset\sigma(D_{\phi});

for the bounded Foguel-Hankel operator

\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix},
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}\subset\sigma\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&S\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, for every constant
\lambda,
we have

\displaystyle\lambda I-\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&\lambda I-T_{z}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&-X\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda I-T_{z}^{*}&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}.

Note that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I&-X\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}

is always invertible and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I&-X\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&X\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}.

If both of
\lambda I-T_{z}
and
\lambda I-T_{z}^{*}
are
invertible, then
\lambda I-\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix}
is invertible.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix}\subset\sigma(T_{z})=\overline{\mathbb{D}}.

6.2.
Essential spectrum

The essential spectrum of Toeplitz operator with continous symbol equals the essential range of the symbol.
Corollary
4.9
shows that
If
f,g,\phi,\psi\in C(\mathbb{T}),
then

\sigma_{e}(R(f,g,\phi,\psi))=f(\mathbb{T})\cup\psi(\mathbb{T}).

Hence, for bounded singular integral operator, if
\alpha,\beta\in C(\mathbb{T}),
then

\sigma_{e}(R_{\alpha,\beta})=\alpha(\mathbb{T})\cup\beta(\mathbb{T}).

For bounded dual truncated Toeplitz operator, if
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{T}),
then

\sigma_{e}(D_{\varphi})=\varphi(\mathbb{T}).

For bounded Foguel-Hankel operator, if
X=\Gamma_{\phi}
and
\phi\in H^{\infty}+C(\mathbb{T}),

then

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}\begin{pmatrix}T_{z}^{*}&X\\
0&T_{z}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbb{T}.

We consider one of operators

Suppose that

Abstract
. For n ∈ { 2 t − 3 , 2 t − 2 , 2 t − 1 } n\in\{2^{t}-3,2^{t}-2,2^{t}-1\} ( t ≥ 3 t\geq 3 ) we study the cohomology algebra H ∗ ​ ( G ~ n , 3 , ℤ 2 ) H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2}) of the Grassmann manifold G ~ n , 3 \widetilde{G}_{n,3} of oriented 3 3 -dimensional subspaces of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} . A complete description of H ∗ ​ ( G ~ n , 3 , ℤ 2 ) H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2}) is given in the cases n = 2 t − 3 n=2^{t}-3 and n = 2 t − 2 n=2^{t}-2 , while in the case n = 2 t − 1 n=2^{t}-1 we obtain a description complete up to a coefficient from ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} .

1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Let
G_{n,k}
be the Grassmann manifold of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}
be the Grassmann manifold of
oriented
k
-dimensional subspaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Over both manifolds there are canonical
k
-dimensional vector bundles, denoted by
\gamma_{n,k}
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,k}
respectively. There are several ways to describe the mod
2
cohomology algebra
H^{*}(G_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, one of them being due to Borel’s famous result
[
2
]
, which establishes that every element in
H^{*}(G_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
can be expressed as a polynomial in Stiefel–Whitney classes
w_{i}(\gamma_{n,k})\in H^{i}(G_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
1\leq i\leq k
. On the other hand, the situation in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is more complicated, although
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}
is simply connected, and so
w_{1}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,k})=0
. Namely, besides the Stiefel–Whitney classes
w_{i}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,k})\in H^{i}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, which we from now on abbreviate to
\widetilde{w}_{i}
,
2\leq i\leq k
, there are some additional indecomposable classes (a positive dimensional cohomology class is indecomposable if it cannot be written as a polynomial in classes of smaller dimension). Put in other words, the subalgebra of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
generated by
\widetilde{w}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{w}_{k}
does not coincide with
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. There is a description of this subalgebra as a quotient of the polynomial algebra in
\widetilde{w}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{w}_{k}
(analogous to the Borel description of
H^{*}(G_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
), but the whole algebra
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is not completely described in general.

The case
k=1
is trivial, since
\widetilde{G}_{n,1}
is the
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere
S^{n-1}
. The algebra
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is known in the case
k=2
as well. In
[
5
]
one can find a complete description of this algebra for
k=2
. In the same paper, the additional particular case
(n,k)=(6,3)
was also fully calculated (it suffices to study
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}
with
n\geq 2k
, since
\widetilde{G}_{n,k}\cong\widetilde{G}_{n,n-k}
).

For
k=3
and
n\in\{2^{t}-3,2^{t}-2,2^{t}-1,2^{t}\}
(
t\geq 3
), significant results in this regard were obtained by Basu and Chakraborty in
[
1
]
. In that paper the authors showed that, besides Stiefel–Whitney classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, there is exactly one (up to addition of a polynomial in
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
) indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and they identified the algebra
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
as the quotient of the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a]
by a certain ideal. In the generating set of that ideal some polynomials in
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
remained undetermined. This description was completed in the case
n=2^{t}
(
t\geq 3
) in
[
3
]
. For
n\in\{2^{t}-3,2^{t}-2,2^{t}-1\}
(
t\geq 3
), the description is essentially the following (
[
1
, Theorem B(2)]
):

(1.1)

H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]}{\big(g_{n-2},g_{n-1},g_{n},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}-P_{2}\big)},

where
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are some (unknown) polynomials in
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
, with
P_{2}=0
for
n\in\{2^{t}-3,2^{t}-2\}
. Via the isomorphism (
1.1
), the cosets of
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
from the right-hand side correspond to the Stiefel–Whitney classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}\in H^{2}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}\in H^{3}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
respectively, while the coset of
a_{2^{t}-4}
corresponds to the indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. The polynomials
g_{r}
,
r\geq 0
, appearing in (
1.1
), are well-known polynomials in
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
.

This paper deals with the problem of computing the polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
from (
1.1
). For
n\in\{2^{t}-3,2^{t}-2\}
, there is actually only
P_{1}
to be determined, and we prove that
P_{1}=0
in these cases (more precisely, that
P_{1}
can be chosen to be the zero-polynomial in order for the isomorphism (
1.1
) to hold). This means that
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is isomorphic to the tensor product of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]/(g_{n-2},g_{n-1},g_{n})
and the exterior algebra
\Lambda(a_{2^{t}-4})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[a_{2^{t}-4}]/(a_{2^{t}-4}^{2})
, that is,
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Therefore, we obtain a complete description of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
in these cases. In the case
n=2^{t}-1
we prove that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\neq 0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, and establish that
P_{1}=g_{2^{t}-4}
and
P_{2}=\gamma w_{2}^{2^{t}-4}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. So the main results of this paper are given in the following theorem (it is well known that
g_{2^{t}-3}=0
, and so this polynomial is redundant in (
1.1
)).

Theorem 1.1
.

For all integers
t\geq 3
we have the following isomorphisms of graded algebras (where
|w_{i}|=i
,
i=2,3
, and
|a_{2^{t}-4}|=2^{t}-4
):

(a)

\displaystyle H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]}{\big(g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-g_{2^{t}-4}a_{2^{t}-4}-\gamma w_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\big)}
,

for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
;

(b)

\displaystyle H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]}{(g_{2^{t}-4},g_{2^{t}-2})}\otimes\Lambda(a_{2^{t}-4})
;

(c)

\displaystyle H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-3,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]}{(g_{2^{t}-5},g_{2^{t}-4})}\otimes\Lambda(a_{2^{t}-4})
.

The technique that we use to prove this theorem combines the theory of Gröbner bases with some classical methods of algebraic topology. In Section
2
of the paper we establish a framework for the proof of Theorem
1.1
by recalling some basic facts concerning the cohomology algebras
H^{*}(G_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,k};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
, in particular the Gysin sequence connecting these two cohomologies. In Section
3
we use the Gröbner basis for the ideal
(g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1})\trianglelefteq\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
obtained by Fukaya in
[
4
]
, as well as an additive basis for
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
that it produces, to prove part (a) of the theorem. Finally, Sections
4
and
5
prove parts (b) and (c) of the theorem.

2.
Some preliminaries on cohomology of Grassmann manifolds

We will be working with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
coefficients exclusively, so in the rest of the paper, these coefficients for cohomology are understood.

By Borel’s description
[
2
]
, as a graded algebra

(2.1)

H^{*}(G_{n,k})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{k}]/I_{n,k},

where the coset of
w_{i}
in the quotient on the right-hand side corresponds to the Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{i}(\gamma_{n,k})\in H^{i}(G_{n,k})
on the left-hand side. So the grading on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{k}]
is such that the degree of
w_{i}
is
i

1\leq i\leq k

is the graded ideal in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{k}]

\overline{w}_{n-k+1},\ldots,\overline{w}_{n}

1/(1+w_{1}+w_{2}+\cdots+w_{k})
. Therefore, they satisfy the relation

(2.2)

(1+w_{1}+w_{2}+\cdots+w_{k})(\overline{w}_{0}+\overline{w}_{1}+\overline{w}_{2}+\cdots)=1.

. Therefore,

The following lemma will be used repeatedly in the upcoming sections.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a)
Suppose that
r\geq 0
is an integer with the property that in
H^{r}(G_{n+1,k})
one has
\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}=0
(see (
2.3
)). Then for an arbitrary class
x\in H^{r}(\widetilde{G}_{n+1,k})
the following implication holds:

x\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\widetilde{i}^{*}(x)\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}.

(b)
Similarly, if
r\geq 0
is an integer with the property that
\ker w_{1}\cap\ker j^{*}=0
in
H^{r}(G_{n+1,k+1})
(see (
2.4
)), then for a class
x\in H^{r}(\widetilde{G}_{n+1,k+1})
the following implication holds:

x\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\widetilde{j}^{*}(x)\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma comes down to elementary diagram chasing. For (a) we use diagram (
2.3
). If
x\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}=\ker\varphi
, then
\varphi(x)\neq 0
and
\varphi(x)\in\ker w_{1}
. From
\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}=0
we now conclude that
\varphi(x)\notin\ker i^{*}
, and so

\varphi\big(\,\,\widetilde{i}^{*}(x)\big)=i^{*}\big(\varphi(x)\big)\neq 0,

which means that
\widetilde{i}^{*}(x)\notin\ker\varphi=\operatorname{im}p^{*}
.

The proof of part (b) is identical (one just uses diagram (
2.4
)).
∎

Hence, we will be interested in the kernels of the maps
i^{*}:H^{*}(G_{n+1,k})\rightarrow H^{*}(G_{n,k})
and
j^{*}:H^{*}(G_{n+1,k+1})\rightarrow H^{*}(G_{n,k})
. The total space of canonical vector bundle
\gamma_{n,k}
over
G_{n,k}
consists of pairs
(V,v)
, where
V\in G_{n,k}
and
v\in V
, so it is easy to see that the embeddings
i:G_{n,k}\hookrightarrow G_{n+1,k}
and
j:G_{n,k}\hookrightarrow G_{n+1,k+1}
are covered by bundle maps from
\gamma_{n,k}
to
\gamma_{n+1,k}
and from
\gamma_{n,k}
to
\gamma_{n+1,k+1}
respectively. Therefore, the next lemma is straightforward from the naturality of the Stiefel–Whitney classes and the fact that the vector bundle
\gamma_{n,k}
is
k
-dimensional (see
[
6
, p. 37]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

(a)
For the map
i^{*}:H^{*}(G_{n+1,k})\rightarrow H^{*}(G_{n,k})
we have

i^{*}\big(w_{r}(\gamma_{n+1,k})\big)=w_{r}(\gamma_{n,k}),\quad 1\leq r\leq k.

(b)
For the map
j^{*}:H^{*}(G_{n+1,k+1})\rightarrow H^{*}(G_{n,k})
we have

\displaystyle j^{*}\big(w_{r}(\gamma_{n+1,k+1})\big)

\displaystyle=w_{r}(\gamma_{n,k}),\quad 1\leq r\leq k,

\displaystyle j^{*}\big(w_{k+1}(\gamma_{n+1,k+1})\big)

\displaystyle=w_{k+1}(\gamma_{n,k})=0.

The same arguments work in the oriented case as well, and so for embeddings
\widetilde{i}:\widetilde{G}_{n,k}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{n+1,k}
and
\widetilde{j}:\widetilde{G}_{n,k}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{n+1,k+1}
one has

(2.5)

\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{w}_{r})=\widetilde{w}_{r},\quad 2\leq r\leq k,

and

(2.6)

\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{w}_{r})=\widetilde{w}_{r},\quad 2\leq r\leq k;\qquad\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{w}_{k+1})=0.

In the case
k=3
, equality (
2.2
) leads to the recurrence relation

(2.7)

\overline{w}_{r+3}=w_{1}\overline{w}_{r+2}+w_{2}\overline{w}_{r+1}+w_{3}\overline{w}_{r},\quad r\geq 0,

and also an explicit formula for calculating
\overline{w}_{r}
. Specifically,

(2.8)

\overline{w}_{r}=\sum_{a+2b+3c=r}\binom{a+b+c}{a}\binom{b+c}{b}w_{1}^{a}w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c},\quad r\geq 0,

where the sum is taken over all triples
(a,b,c)
of nonnegative integers such that
a+2b+3c=r
(both binomial coefficients in the sum are considered modulo
2
).

It is well known (see e.g.
[
4
]
or
[
3
]
) that in the case
k=3

(2.9)

\operatorname{im}p^{*}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]/J_{n,3},

where
J_{n,3}
is the graded ideal in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
generated by the polynomials
g_{n-2}
,
g_{n-1}
and
g_{n}
. These polynomials
g_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
,
r\geq 0
, are modulo
w_{1}
reductions of the polynomials
\overline{w}_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
. From (
2.8
) we now get

(2.10)

g_{r}=\sum_{2b+3c=r}{b+c\choose b}\,w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c},\quad r\geq 0,

and (
2.7
) becomes

(2.11)

g_{r+3}=w_{2}g_{r+1}+w_{3}g_{r},\quad r\geq 0.

By definition,
J_{n,3}=(g_{n-2},g_{n-1},g_{n})
, but from (
2.11
) we see that actually

(2.12)

g_{r}\in J_{n,3}\quad\mbox{ for all }r\geq n-2.

For a (homogeneous) polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
, we shall denote by
\widetilde{f}\in\operatorname{im}p^{*}\subset H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3})
the cohomology class corresponding to (the coset of)
f
via the isomorphism (
2.9
). This is in accordance with the notation
\widetilde{w}_{i}
, that we use for the Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{i}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,3})
,
i=2,3
. For instance, the relation (
2.12
) can now be rewritten as

(2.13)

\widetilde{g}_{r}=0\quad\mbox{ in }H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{n,3}),\quad\mbox{ for all }r\geq n-2.

3.
The case
n=2^{t}-1

Let
t\geq 3
be a fixed integer. By
[
1
, Theorem A(b)]
, in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, besides
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, there is one more indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
in cohomological dimension
2^{t}-4
. Indecomposable means that it cannot be expressed as a polynomial in classes of (strictly) smaller dimension. Since all classes of smaller dimension are polynomials in
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, and the subalgebra
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
is generated by these two Stiefel–Whitney classes, this is equivalent to the statement
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. The uniqueness of
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
is only up to addition of an element of
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. Put in other words, if
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
is an indecomposable class in
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, then so is
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\widetilde{w}
for any
\widetilde{w}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\cap\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. This means that for
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
we can choose any class from
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
.

We are going to make this choice in the following way. By
[
1
, Theorem A(a)]
we have an indecomposable class (outside
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
)
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1}\in H^{2^{t}-1}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t},3})
. We will prove that
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})\in H^{2^{t}-1}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
, where
\widetilde{i}:\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{2^{t},3}
is the embedding from the previous section. Since every class in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
is a polynomial in
\widetilde{w}_{2}
,
\widetilde{w}_{3}
and some (indecomposable) class in
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
(by
[
1
, Theorem A(b)]
), we see that an odd-dimensional class must be divisible by
\widetilde{w}_{3}
. Hence,
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})
must be of the form
\widetilde{w}_{3}\sigma
, for some
\sigma\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, and if we show
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}
, we will have
\sigma\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. Then we will be able to take
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}:=\sigma
and thus ensure the property:

(3.1)

\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})=\widetilde{w}_{3}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}.

Therefore, we are left to prove that
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})\notin\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. This will be an immediate consequence of Lemma
2.1
(a) (for
r=n=2^{t}-1
and
k=3
) as soon as we establish the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
H^{2^{t}-1}(G_{2^{t},3})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2^{t}}(G_{2^{t},3})
be the multiplication with the Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{1}(\gamma_{2^{t},3})
and
i^{*}:H^{2^{t}-1}(G_{2^{t},3})\rightarrow H^{2^{t}-1}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
the map induced by the embedding
i:G_{2^{t}-1,3}\hookrightarrow G_{2^{t},3}

\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}=0

2.1

[f]\in\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}

[f]\in\ker w_{1}
, we have
[w_{1}f]=0
in

, so

(3.2)

w_{1}f=(\lambda w_{1}^{2}+\mu w_{2})\overline{w}_{2^{t}-2}+\nu w_{1}\overline{w}_{2^{t}-1}+\varepsilon\overline{w}_{2^{t}},

. By (

We have thus chosen indecomposable classes
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1}\in H^{2^{t}-1}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t},3})
and
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
such that (
3.1
) holds.

It is well known (see e.g.
[
1
, Lemma 2.1(i)]
) that
g_{2^{t}-3}=0
, so
J_{2^{t}-1,3}=(g_{2^{t}-3},g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1})=(g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1})
. Also, according to
[
1
, Theorem B(2)]
we have an isomorphism of graded algebras

(3.5)

H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]}{\big(g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}-P_{2}\big)},

mapping the classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}
,
\widetilde{w}_{3}
and
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
to the cosets of
w_{2}
,
w_{3}
and
a_{2^{t}-4}
respectively, where the (unknown) polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
do not contain the variable
a_{2^{t}-4}
.

In
[
4
]
Fukaya found a Gröbner basis for the ideal
J_{2^{t}-1,3}
, and in
[
3
]
it was shown that
J_{2^{t}-1,3}=J_{2^{t},3}
and that this Gröbner basis consists of polynomials

(3.6)

f_{i}=g_{2^{t}-3+2^{i}}=\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}}{b+c\choose b}w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c},\quad\mbox{ for }0\leq i\leq t-1.

These polynomials, of course, belong to
J_{2^{t}-1,3}
(see (
2.12
)), and so we have that
\widetilde{f}_{i}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
for all
i
. By
[
4
, p. 203]
(or
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
) we know that the leading monomial (with respect to the lexicographic order with
w_{2}>w_{3}
) of
f_{i}
is the following:

(3.7)

\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}.

Moreover,
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
gives us that

(3.8)

f_{t-1}=\mathrm{LM}(f_{t-1})=w_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}.

In the same way as in
[
3
]
we can use this Gröbner basis and the isomorphism (
3.5
) to find an additive basis for the cohomology algebra
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
. First, if we choose the lexicographic order with
a_{2^{t}-4}>w_{2}>w_{3}
for the monomial order in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]
, then the set
\{f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{t-1},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}-P_{2}\}
turns out to be a Gröbner basis for the ideal
(g_{2^{t}-2},g_{2^{t}-1},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}-P_{2})
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]
(whatever the polynomials
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
might be). This is essentially due to the fact that the leading monomial of the polynomial
a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}-P_{2}
is
a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}
(since
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are polynomials in
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
only), and
a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}
is coprime with every
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})
,
0\leq i\leq t-1
(cf.
[
3
, Theorem 3.9]
). On the other hand, if we observe the monomials not divisible by any of the leading monomials from Gröbner basis, it is well known that their cosets form an additive basis for the quotient. Therefore, the isomorphism (
3.5
) leads to the conclusion that the set

(3.9)

B=\left\{\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{r}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\mid r<2,\,\left(\forall i\in\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\}\right)\,b<2^{t-1}-2^{i}\vee c<2^{i}-1\right\}

is an additive basis for
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
(cf.
[
3
, Corollary 3.10]
).

The elements of
B
with
r=0
, i.e., the classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
such that for all
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\}
either
b<2^{t-1}-2^{i}
or
c<2^{i}-1
, form an additive basis for
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
(this could be obtained e.g. from the isomorphism (
2.9
) by an analogous consideration). Now it is not hard to see that the basis element from
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
of the highest cohomological dimension is
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\in H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
. Indeed, for
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}\in B
one must have
c\leq 2^{t-1}-2
(since
c\geq 2^{t-1}-1
would imply
b<0
), and if
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,t-2\}
is such that
2^{i}-1\leq c\leq 2^{i+1}-2
, then
b\leq 2^{t-1}-2^{i}-1
. So

(3.10)

2b+3c\leq 2(2^{t-1}-2^{i}-1)+3(2^{i+1}-2)=2^{t}+2^{i+2}-8\leq 2^{t+1}-8.

This means that the following implication holds

(3.11)

j>2^{t+1}-8\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H^{j}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\cap\operatorname{im}p^{*}=0.

Note also that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
is the only element in
B\cap\operatorname{im}p^{*}
of the cohomological dimension
2^{t+1}-8
. Namely, in order to have equality in (
3.10
) one must have
b=2^{t-1}-2^{i}-1
,
c=2^{i+1}-2
and
i=t-2
. On the other hand, by
[
4
, Corollary 4.12]
we know that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\neq 0
, and so we conclude

(3.12)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\neq 0\quad\mbox{ in }H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3}).

Similarly, the only element of
B
in the top dimension
3\cdot 2^{t}-12
(this is the dimension of the manifold
\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3}
) is
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
, so we have

(3.13)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}\neq 0\quad\mbox{ in }H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-12}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Theorem
1.1
(a) states that in (
3.5
) we can take
P_{1}=g_{2^{t}-4}
and
P_{2}=\gamma w_{2}^{2^{t}-4}
for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In order to prove this it suffices to show that in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
we have

(3.14)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}

(for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
).

We start off by representing
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}
in the basis
B
(see (
3.9
)). Since
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\in H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, we need to list basis elements in this cohomological dimension. As we have already noticed, from
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
we have
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-2}-1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-2}
, and outside of
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
we have
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}
for every
k\geq 0
such that
2^{t-1}-2-3k\geq 0
, i.e.,
k\leq(2^{t-1}-2)/3
. Indeed,
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}\in B
for all such
k
, since
2^{t-1}-2-3k\geq 2^{t-1}-2^{i}
and
2k\geq 2^{i}-1
for some
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\}
would imply
2^{i}\geq 3k+2
and
2^{i}\leq 2k+1
, which is obviously impossible. Having in mind (
3.12
), we thus have

(3.15)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}+\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\lambda_{k}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k},

for some uniquely determined
\gamma,\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\ldots\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In order to obtain (
3.14
), we see that we need to equate the sum on the right-hand side with
\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
. As the first step in that direction, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
\lambda_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq\lfloor(2^{t-1}-2)/3\rfloor
, are the coefficients from (
3.15
), then

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\lambda_{k}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}=\lambda_{0}\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}.

Proof.

The main result of
[
3
]
is the equality
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1}^{2}=0
, where
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1}\in H^{2^{t}-1}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t},3})
is the indecomposable class. Therefore, by (
3.1
) we get

\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=(\widetilde{w}_{3}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})^{2}=\big(\,\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1})\big)^{2}=\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-1}^{2})=0.

So, multiplying (
3.15
) by
\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}
leads to

(3.16)

0=\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\lambda_{k}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+2}

(
\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}=0
due to (
3.11
)). The classes
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+2}
for
k\geq 1
are elements of the basis
B
, since there is no
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,t-1\}
such that
2^{t-1}-2-3k\geq 2^{t-1}-2^{i}
and
2k+2\geq 2^{i}-1
(if
k\geq 1
). However, for
k=0
we have the class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}
, which is not in
B
. We wish to express this class as a sum of basis elements. In order to do so, note that the monomial
a_{2^{t}-4}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}w_{3}^{2}
is divisible by
\mathrm{LM}(f_{1})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}w_{3}
(see (
3.7
)), and by (
3.6
) we have

(3.17)

f_{1}=g_{2^{t}-1}=\sum_{2b+3c=2^{t}-1}\binom{b+c}{b}w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}w_{3}^{2k+1}.

The last equality comes from the assertion that
c
must be odd (since
2b+3c=2^{t}-1
), and then one substitutes
2k+1
for
c
. We know that
\widetilde{f}_{1}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, and so

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}\widetilde{w}_{3}+\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+1}.

Multiplying this equation with
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{3}
we get

(3.18)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+2}

Inserting (
3.18
) in (
3.16
) leads to

0=\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\left(\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{0}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}\right)\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k+2}.

As we have already indicated, the right-hand side is a linear combination of
basis elements, which means that all coefficients must vanish. Therefore,
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}
for all
k
such that
1\leq k\leq\lfloor(2^{t-1}-2)/3\rfloor
. We conclude that

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\lambda_{k}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}=\lambda_{0}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}.

It remains to prove that this sum on the right-hand side is equal to
\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}
. If

S=\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{2^{t-1}-2}{3}\right\rfloor}\binom{2^{t-1}-1-k}{2k+1}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}w_{3}^{2k},

then using (
3.17
) and (
2.11
) we get that in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3}]
the following identity holds

w_{3}S=g_{2^{t}-1}=w_{2}g_{2^{t}-3}+w_{3}g_{2^{t}-4}=w_{3}g_{2^{t}-4}

(since
g_{2^{t}-3}=0
). Canceling out
w_{3}
finishes the proof.
∎

So now (
3.15
) simplifies to

(3.19)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\lambda_{0}\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}

(for some
\lambda_{0},\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
), and in order to obtain (
3.14
) (and thus finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
(a)) we are left to prove that
\lambda_{0}=1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2^{t}-3}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
be the multiplication map and
j^{*}:H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})\rightarrow H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,2})
the map induced by the embedding
j:G_{2^{t}-2,2}\hookrightarrow G_{2^{t}-1,3}
(see Section
2
). Then

\ker w_{1}\cap\ker j^{*}=0
.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
, let
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
be such that for the corresponding cohomology class in
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
one has
[f]\in\ker w_{1}\cap\ker j^{*}
. We want to prove that
[f]=0
in
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
.

By Lemma
2.2
(b),
j^{*}[f]=[\rho(f)]
, where
\rho:\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2}]
is the reduction modulo
w_{3}
. Since
j^{*}[f]=0
, we first get
[\rho(f)]=0
, but actually
\rho(f)=0
, because the degree of
\rho(f)
in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2}]
is
2^{t}-4
, and
H^{*}(G_{2^{t}-2,2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2}]/(\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3},\overline{w}_{2^{t}-2})
. We conclude that
f=w_{3}h
, for some
h\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
.

On the other hand,
[f]\in\ker w_{1}
means that
[w_{1}f]=0
in
H^{2^{t}-3}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
, and since
H^{*}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]/(\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3},\overline{w}_{2^{t}-2},\overline{w}_{2^{t}-1})
, we conclude that
w_{1}f=\alpha\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. When we combine these two results, we get

w_{1}w_{3}h=\alpha\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}.

If
\alpha
were nonzero, then the polynomial
\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}
would be divisible by
w_{3}
. However, it is obvious that the monomial
w_{1}^{2^{t}-3}
appears in
\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}
with nonzero coefficient (see (
2.8
)). Therefore,
\alpha=0
, which implies
w_{1}f=\alpha\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}=0
, and so
f=0
.
∎

We know that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
, and so Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
2.1
(b) (for
r=2^{t}-4
,
n=2^{t}-2
and
k=2
) imply

(3.20)

\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}

(here,
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
is the subalgebra of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})
generated by
\widetilde{w}_{2}
). A complete description of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})
is given in
[
5
, Theorem 2.1(b)]
and reads as follows:

(3.21)

H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},b_{2^{t}-4}]}{\big(w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-1},b_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}b_{2^{t}-4}\big)}.

If
\widetilde{b}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})
is the class corresponding to (the coset of)
b_{2^{t}-4}
via the isomorphism (
3.21
), then (
3.20
) implies

(3.22)

\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})=\widetilde{b}_{2^{t}-4}+\mu\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2},

for some
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Since
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2})
, squaring the equation (
3.22
) leads to

(3.23)

\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2})=\widetilde{b}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\neq 0\quad\mbox{ in }H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,2}).

On the other hand, by (
3.19
) and (
2.6
)

\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2})=\lambda_{0}\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=\lambda_{0}\widetilde{j}^{*}(\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}),

leading to the conclusion
\lambda_{0}=1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
(a).

Note that we have proved that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\neq 0
in
H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
. This follows from (
3.23
), and it also follows from the description of
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
(given in Theorem
1.1
(a)) itself.

We were not able to compute the coefficient
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
from this description, and likewise we could not deduce whether the top dimensional cohomology class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{3}\in H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-12}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
vanishes or not. Nevertheless, in the upcoming Proposition
3.5
we prove that these two problems are equivalent.

We first show that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}
is the only basis element in
\operatorname{im}p^{*}\cap H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
whose square is nonzero. Actually, we already know that
(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2})^{2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\neq 0
(see (
3.12
)), so we now verify that the square of
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}
vanishes if
k>0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
k>0
and
2^{t}-4-6k\geq 0
, then

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k}=0\quad\mbox{ in }H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3}).

Proof.

The proof is by reverse induction on
k
. If
k\geq 2^{t-3}
, i.e.,
4k\geq 2^{t-1}
, then

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k-2^{t-1}+1}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{t-1}-1}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k-2^{t-1}+1}\widetilde{f}_{t-1}=0,

by (
3.8
).

Now let
0<k<2^{t-3}
and suppose that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6j}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4j}=0
for all integers
j>k
(such that
2^{t}-4-6j\geq 0
). There exists a unique integer
i\in\{2,\ldots,t-2\}
with the property
2^{i}\leq 4k\leq 2^{i+1}-4
. Note that then

12k\leq 3\cdot 2^{i+1}-12=2^{i+2}+2^{i+1}-12\leq 2^{t}+2^{i+1}-12,

i.e.,
6k\leq 2^{t-1}+2^{i}-6
, implying
2^{t}-4-6k>2^{t-1}-2^{i}
. This means that we can write

(3.24)

\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k-2^{t-1}+2^{i}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k-2^{i}+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{i}-1}.

On the other hand, according to (
3.7
) and (
3.6
),

w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}=\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=f_{i}+\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}\\
(b,c)\neq(2^{t-1}-2^{i},2^{i}-1)\end{subarray}}{b+c\choose b}w_{2}^{b}w_{3}^{c}.

Since
\widetilde{f}_{i}=0
, by (
3.24
) in cohomology we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{i}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k-2^{i}+1}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2^{i}-1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}+2^{i}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{4k-2^{i}+1}\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}\\
(b,c)\neq(2^{t-1}-2^{i},2^{i}-1)\end{subarray}}{b+c\choose b}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}

\displaystyle=\!\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}\\
(b,c)\neq(2^{t-1}-2^{i},2^{i}-1)\end{subarray}}{b+c\choose b}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b+2^{t-1}+2^{i}-4-6k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c+4k-2^{i}+1}

To complete the induction step, it now suffices to prove that for a summand with
{b+c\choose b}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
one has
c+4k-2^{i}+1=4j
for some
j>k
.

First, since
\mathrm{LM}(f_{i})=w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2^{i}}w_{3}^{2^{i}-1}
, for a nonzero summand in this sum, one must have
b<2^{t-1}-2^{i}
, and consequently
c>2^{i}-1
(because
2b+3c
is the same for all monomials in
f_{i}
). This implies
c+4k-2^{i}+1>4k
, and so we only need to prove that
c+4k-2^{i}+1
is divisible by
4
.

Since
i\geq 2
and
2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}
, we first conclude that
c
is odd, and therefore
c+4k-2^{i}+1
is even, which means that it remains to rule out the possibility
c+4k-2^{i}+1\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, i.e.,
c\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. But
c\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
2b+3c=2^{t}-3+2^{i}
would imply
2b\equiv 2\pmod{4}
, and we would have that
b
is odd, contradicting the fact
{b+c\choose b}\equiv 1\pmod{2}
.
∎

This lemma ensures that

(3.25)

\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}.

Namely, the leading monomial of
g_{2^{t}-4}
is obviously
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2}
, and all other monomials are of the form
w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}w_{3}^{2k}
for some
k>0
.

As we have already indicated, the indecomposable class in
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
is not unique. If
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
is such a class, then so is
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\widetilde{w}
for any
\widetilde{w}\in\operatorname{im}p^{*}\cap H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
(
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
is unique only up to addition of such a
\widetilde{w}
). As another consequence of Lemma
3.4
, we note that taking some other indecomposable class from
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
has no effect on equality (
3.14
). Indeed,

(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\widetilde{w})^{2}=\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}+\widetilde{w}^{2}=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}+\widetilde{w}^{2}=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\widetilde{w})+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4},

where the last equality comes from Lemma
3.4
. Namely, one can fairly easy show that
\widetilde{w}^{2}=\widetilde{w}\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}
using the lemma and the following facts:
\widetilde{w}
is a sum of monomials of the form
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t-1}-2-3k}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{2k}
for some
k\geq 0
;
\widetilde{w}_{3}\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-1}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
(see (
2.13
)); and for every monomial
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{b}\widetilde{w}_{3}^{c}
with nonzero coefficient in
\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}
the exponent
c
is divisible by
4
(see the proof of Proposition
3.2
,
g_{2^{t}-4}=S
in that proof).

Proposition 3.5
.

For an indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
and the coefficient
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
from Theorem
1.1
(a) the following equivalence holds:

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{3}\neq 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma=0.

Proof.

By (
3.14
) and (
3.25
) we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{3}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}+\gamma\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=(1+\gamma)\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4},

since
\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\in H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-12}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})\cap\operatorname{im}p^{*}=0
(by (
3.11
)). On the other hand, by (
3.13
) we know that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}
is the nonzero element in
H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-12}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
, and so this equality proves the proposition.
∎

4.
The case
n=2^{t}-2

By
[
1
, Theorem A(b)]
the cohomology algebra
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
contains an indecomposable class in dimension
2^{t}-4
. Similarly as in the case
n=2^{t}-1
, for this class we can choose any
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. If
\widetilde{i}:\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3}
is the embedding and
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-1,3})
the indecomposable class from the previous section, we will prove that
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
, and then make a choice:

(4.1)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}:=\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}).

Similarly as before, the relation
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
will be a direct consequence of Lemma
2.1
(a) and the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2^{t}-3}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
be the multiplication map and
i^{*}:H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})\rightarrow H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})
the map induced by the embedding
i:G_{2^{t}-2,3}\hookrightarrow G_{2^{t}-1,3}
(see Section
2
). Then

\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}=0
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to those of Propositions
3.1
and
3.3
. Take a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
such that
[f]\in\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}
. By Lemma
2.2
(a),
[f]=i^{*}[f]=0
in
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})
, and so
f=\alpha\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. On the other hand, since
[w_{1}f]=0
in
H^{2^{t}-3}(G_{2^{t}-1,3})
, we have
w_{1}f=\beta\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Combining these two equations, we get

(4.2)

\alpha w_{1}\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}=\beta\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}.

If
\alpha
were nonzero, then the monomial
w_{1}^{3}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3}
would appear on the left-hand side of (
4.2
) with nonzero coefficient (since
w_{1}^{2}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3}
appears in
\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}
with nonzero coefficient, by (
2.8
)). However, the coefficient of
w_{1}^{3}w_{2}^{2^{t-1}-3}
in
\overline{w}_{2^{t}-3}
is
\binom{2^{t-1}}{3}=0
. We conclude that
\alpha=0
, and so
f=\alpha\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}=0
.
∎

So now we have the indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
, chosen by (
4.1
). According to
[
1
, Theorem B(2)]
there exists an algebra isomorphism

(4.3)

H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{2},w_{3},a_{2^{t}-4}]}{\big(g_{2^{t}-4},g_{2^{t}-2},a_{2^{t}-4}^{2}-P_{1}a_{2^{t}-4}\big)},

which maps the classes
\widetilde{w}_{2}
,
\widetilde{w}_{3}
and
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}
to the cosets of
w_{2}
,
w_{3}
and
a_{2^{t}-4}
respectively, and
P_{1}
is some polynomial in variables
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
only. In order to prove part (b) of Theorem
1.1
, we need to show that for
P_{1}
we can take the zero polynomial, which amounts to showing that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
. We shall need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=0
in
H^{2^{t+1}-8}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
.

Proof.

Assume to the contrary that
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\neq 0
. The dimension of the manifold
\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3}
is
3\cdot 2^{t}-15
, and so by the Poincaré duality, there exists a class
\sigma\in H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15-(2^{t+1}-8)}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
such that
\sigma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}\neq 0
in the top dimensional cohomology group
H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
. Since
3\cdot 2^{t}-15-(2^{t+1}-8)=2^{t}-7<2^{t}-4
,
\sigma
is a polynomial in
\widetilde{w}_{2}
and
\widetilde{w}_{3}
, i.e.,
\sigma\in\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. This means that the nonzero top dimensional class
\sigma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}
belongs to
\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. However, the relevant part of the Gysin sequence shows that this is not the case.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

Namely,
H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})\cong H^{3\cdot 2^{t}-15}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,
\varphi
is onto, and hence isomorphism. Therefore,
p^{*}=0
. This contradiction concludes the proof.
∎

Now, according to (
4.1
), (
3.14
) and (
2.5
)

\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2})=\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4})\cdot\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})+\gamma\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4})=\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}+\gamma\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\widetilde{w}_{2}^{2^{t}-4}=0
, and by (
2.13
),
\widetilde{g}_{2^{t}-4}=0
in
H^{*}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
. So,
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=0
, and the proof of Theorem
1.1
(b) is complete.

5.
The case

In this section we prove part (c) of Theorem
1.1
, and the proof goes along the same lines as the proof of part (b) (given in the previous section). We choose the indecomposable class
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-3,3})
to be
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})
, where
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3})
is the indecomposable class chosen in the previous section, and
\widetilde{i}:\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-3,3}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-2,3}
is the embedding from Section

. When we do so, then it is immediate that
\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2}=\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4}^{2})=0
, and Theorem
1.1
(c) follows.

So the only thing to be proved is the claim that we can take
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})
as the indecomposable class in
H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-3,3})
, that is,
\widetilde{i}^{*}(\widetilde{a}_{2^{t}-4})\in H^{2^{t}-4}(\widetilde{G}_{2^{t}-3,3})\setminus\operatorname{im}p^{*}
. As in the previous cases, Lemma
2.1
(a) ensures that it is enough to establish the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

For the multiplication map
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{2^{t}-3}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})
and the map
i^{*}:H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})\rightarrow H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-3,3})
induced by the embedding
i:G_{2^{t}-3,3}\hookrightarrow G_{2^{t}-2,3}
, we have

\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}=0
.

Proof.

For a polynomial
f\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
such that
[f]\in\ker w_{1}\cap\ker i^{*}
we have
[f]=i^{*}[f]=0
in
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-3,3})
, and so
f=\alpha w_{1}\overline{w}_{2^{t}-5}+\beta\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}
for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. But
\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}\in J_{2^{t}-2,3}
, which means that in
H^{2^{t}-4}(G_{2^{t}-2,3})

[f]=[\alpha w_{1}\overline{w}_{2^{t}-5}+\beta\overline{w}_{2^{t}-4}]=[\alpha w_{1}\overline{w}_{2^{t}-5}],

and so we can take
\beta=0
. We wish to prove that
\alpha=0
as well.

We also know that
[w_{1}f]=0

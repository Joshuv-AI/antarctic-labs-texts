Suppression of blow-up in multi-species Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system via the Poiseuille flow in a finite channel
Shikun Cui, Wendong Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the multi-species parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel system coupled with the Navier-Stokes equations near the 2-D Poiseuille flow ( A ⁡ ( 1 − y 2 ) , 0 ) (\ A(1-y^{2}),0\ ) in a finite channel Ω = 𝕋 × 𝕀 \Omega=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I} with 𝕀 = ( − 1 , 1 ) \mathbb{I}=(-1,1) . Furthermore, the Navier-slip boundary condition is imposed on the perturbation of velocity u u . We show that if the Poiseuille flow is sufficiently strong ( A A is large enough), the solutions to the system are global in time without any smallness restriction on the initial cell mass.



Keywords:
Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system;
Poiseuille flow;
Enhanced dissipation;
Blow up;

1.
Introduction

Consider the following multi-species parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) system coupled with the
Navier-Stokes (NS) equations:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n_{1}+v\cdot\nabla n_{1}=\triangle n_{1}-\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(n_{1}\nabla c),\\
\partial_{t}n_{2}+v\cdot\nabla n_{2}=\triangle n_{2}-\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(n_{2}\nabla c),\\
\triangle c+n_{1}+n_{2}-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P=\triangle v+(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot v=0,\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I},~\mathbb{I}=(-1,1)\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

along with initial conditions

(n_{1},n_{2},c,v)\big|_{t=0}=(n_{1,{\rm in}},n_{2,{\rm in}},c_{\rm in},v_{\rm in}),

where
c
denotes the chemoattractant density,

n_{1}
and
n_{2}
denote the cell density of the first and second species, respectively.
The positive constant
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}

represent the sensitivities of two species to the chemoattractant, and
v
denotes the velocity of fluids.
In addition,
P
is the pressure and
\phi
is a given potential function. We assume
\phi=y
as Zeng-Zhang-Zi in
[
25
]
.

If
v=0
and
\phi=0
, the system (
1.1
) becomes the classical multi-species Patlak-Keller-Segel system.
In this system, the cooperative interaction between two species and their response to the chemoattractant are described.
The single-species Patlak-Keller-Segel (PKS) system is a model in mathematical biology,
primarily used to describe the chemotaxis of biological cells, i.e.
how cells react to and move in response to changes in the concentration
of chemical substances in their surrounding environment.
The PKS system was jointly developed by Patlak
[
20
]
, and Keller-Segel
[
16
]
.

In recent years, Patlak-Keller-Segel system and its variants have received great attention in mathematics.
The 2-D parabolic-parabolic PKS model has a critical mass of
8\pi
.
When the cell mass
M:=||n_{\rm in}||_{L^{1}}
is less than
8\pi
, Calvez-Corrias
[
3
]
proved the solutions of the system are global.
When the cell mass is greater than
8\pi
, the solution will blow up in finite time obtained by Schweyer in
[
21
]
.
In the case of the 2D parabolic-elliptic Patlak-Keller-Segel equation, it is globally well-posed if and only if the total mass
M\leq 8\pi
by Wei in
[
22
]
.
Generally, consider a PKS equation with the chemoattractant
\chi
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n=\triangle n-\chi\nabla\cdot(n\nabla c),\\
\triangle c+n=0,\\
n(x,0)=n_{0},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

When
\chi M<8\pi
, Dolbeault-Perthame proved the global existence of
a solution in a weak sense
[
6
]
.
When
\chi M=8\pi
, Blanchet-Carrillo-Masmoudi proved the global existence of free-energy solution
under the assumption
(1+|x|^{2})n_{0}\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

[
2
]
.
They also showed the global classical solutions can not exist and blow up in finite time for
\chi M>8\pi

under the assumption
(1+|x|^{2})n_{0}\in L^{1}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
For the multi-species Patlak-Keller-Segel system (
1.1
), define the total cell mass by

M_{1}:=||n_{1}(t)||_{L^{1}}=||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{1}}\ {\rm and}\ M_{2}:=||n_{2}(t)||_{L^{1}}=||n_{2,\rm in}||_{L^{1}}.

Espejo-Vilches-Conca gave a critical curve on the initial mass
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
planes, where one side of the solution globally exists and the other side explodes
[
8
]
.
He-Tadmor showed that the system (
1.1
) is globally well-posed under the sub-critical condition
\max\{\chi_{1}M_{1},\chi_{2}M_{2}\}<8\pi

[
12
]
.

An interesting question is to consider whether the stabilizing effect of the moving fluid can suppress the finite-time blow-up.
Recently, some progress has been made on this topic.
For the parabolic-elliptic PKS system, Kiselev-Xu suppressed the blow-up by stationary relaxation
enhancing flows and time-dependent Yao-Zlatos flows in
\mathbb{T}^{d}

[
17
]
.
Bedrossian-He have studied the suppression of blow-up by shear flows in

\mathbb{T}^{2}
for the 2-D parabolic-elliptic case,
and for the 3-D parabolic-elliptic case, they investigated the suppression of blow-up by shear flows in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
[
1
]
.
He investigated the suppression of blow-up by a large strictly monotone shear flow for the parabolic-parabolic PKS model in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}

[
10
]
.
Feng-Shi-Wang used the planar helical flows as transport
flow to research the advective Kuramoto-Sivashinsky and
Keller-Segel equations. They proved that when the amplitude of the flow is large enough, the
L^{2}
norm of the solution is uniformly bounded in time
[
9
]
. For the coupled PKS-NS system, Zeng-Zhang-Zi considered the 2-D PKS-NS system near the Couette flow in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, and they
proved that if the Couette flow is sufficiently strong, the solution to the system stays globally regular
[
25
]
.
He considered the blow-up suppression for the parabolic-elliptic PKS-NS system in

\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
with the coupling of buoyancy effects
[
11
]
for a class of small initial data.
Li-Xiang-Xu studied the suppression of blow-up in PKS-NS system via the Poiseuille flow in
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
,
and they showd that if Poiseuille flow is sufficiently strong, the solution is global in a preprint
[
18
]
.
Besides, for the stability effect of buoyancy,
Hu-Kiselev-Yao consider the blow-up suppression for the Patlak-Keller-Segel equation coupled with a fluid flow that obeys Darcy’s law for incompressible porous media via buoyancy force
[
14
]
.
Hu and Kiselev proved that when the coupling is large enough, the Keller-Segel equation coupled with Stokes-Boussinesq flow is globally well-posed
[
15
]
, see also the recent result by Hu
[
13
]
.

As mentioned by Zeng-Zhang-Zi (
Remark 1.3, in
[
25
]
):
“It is very interesting to investigate the corresponding problems with bound-ary effects taken into account.”
Exploring the potential suppression of blow-up when taking boundary effects into account seems to have not been taken into account in this issue yet.
In this paper, we focus on the two-dimensional multi-species Patlak-Keller-Segel-Navier-Stokes system around the Poiseuille flow

(\ A(1-y^{2}),0\ )
, where
(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}
.
Consequently, the Navier-slip boundary condition is imposed on the perturbation
u
.

As is well known, the Poiseuille flow
U(y)=(\ A(1-y^{2}),0\ )
is a stationary solution for the Navier-Stokes equations and the simplest non-trivial shear flow besides the Couette flow.
In the meanwhile,
(\ 0,0,U(y)\ )
is also a stationary solution of system (
1.1
).
We introduce a perturbation
u
around the Poiseuille flow on system (
1.1
),
setting
u(t,x,y)=v(t,x,y)-U(y)
, then we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n_{1}+A(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{1}-\triangle n_{1}=-\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(n_{1}\nabla c)-u\cdot\nabla n_{1},\\
\partial_{t}n_{2}+A(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{2}-\triangle n_{2}=-\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(n_{2}\nabla c)-u\cdot\nabla n_{2},\\
\triangle c+n_{1}+n_{2}-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}u+A(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}u+\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-2Ayu^{2}\\
0\\
\end{array}\right)-\triangle u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla\phi,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

which is assigned the Navier-slip boundary condition:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u^{2}(t,x,\pm 1)=0,\\
\partial_{y}u^{1}(t,x,\pm 1)=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

To deal with the pressure
P
, we introduce the vorticity
\omega
and the stream function
\Phi
satisfying

\omega=\partial_{y}u^{1}-\partial_{x}u^{2}\ {\rm and}\ u=(u^{1},u^{2})=\nabla^{\bot}\Phi,

where
\nabla^{\bot}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{y}\\
-\partial_{x}\\
\end{array}\right)
.
Then the vorticity
\omega
satisfies

\partial_{t}\omega+A(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}\omega-2Au^{2}-\triangle\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega=-(\partial_{x}n_{1}+\partial_{x}n_{2}).

The Navier-slip boundary condition (
1.3
) yields

\omega(t,x,\pm 1)=0.

After the time rescaling
t\mapsto\frac{t}{A}
, we rewrite the system (
1.2
) into

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n_{1}+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{1}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{1}=-\frac{1}{A}\Big(\chi_{1}\nabla\cdot(n_{1}\nabla c)+\nabla\cdot(un_{1})\Big),\\
\partial_{t}n_{2}+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{2}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{2}=-\frac{1}{A}\Big(\chi_{2}\nabla\cdot(n_{2}\nabla c)+\nabla\cdot(un_{2})\Big),\\
\triangle c+n_{1}+n_{2}-c=0,\\
\partial_{t}\omega+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}\omega-\frac{1}{A}\triangle\omega+2\partial_{x}\triangle^{-1}\omega=-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{x}n_{1}+\partial_{x}n_{2}+u\cdot\nabla\omega),\\
u=\nabla^{\bot}\triangle^{-1}\omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the initial data
n_{1,{\rm in}}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})
,

n_{2,{\rm in}}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})
and
u_{1,{\rm in}}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})
. The Dirichlet boundary conditions
n_{1}(t,x,\pm 1)=0
,
n_{2}(t,x,\pm 1)=0
and
c(t,x,\pm 1)=0
are imposed.
Then there exists a positive
A_{2}
depending on
||n_{1,{\rm in}}||_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
,

||n_{2,{\rm in}}||_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
,
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}

and
||u_{\rm in}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
, such that if
A>A_{2}
, the solution of (
1.4
) is global in time.

Remark 1
.

For Dirichlet boundary conditions of (
1.4
) without the nonlinear term
u\cdot\nabla u
, local well-posedness of regular solutions is proved in
[
14
]
. Moreover, they proved the blow-up happens if the density becomes unbounded. Here we show that the density
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
will remain bounded under the suppression of shear flow. When considering the non-slip boundary condition of the velocity, it is still unknown and at this time it is much more involved, see
[
4
]
, for example.

Remark 2
.

Compared with the
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
case, we no longer need to introduce the weighted
L^{2}
norm

||f||_{X}^{2}:=||f||_{L^{2}}^{2}+||yf||_{L^{2}}^{2}

presented by
[
5
]
.

Remark 3
.

In the classical multi-species PKS-NS system, the sensitivity parameters
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
should be positive real numbers. Following our proof line by line, we can find that there is no restriction on the symbol of
\chi_{1}
and
\chi_{2}
.

Remark 4
.

As we all know, the enhanced dissipation effect plays an important role in suppressing the blow-up of the solution and stabilizing the Navier-Stokes equations
[
23
]
.
In
(x,y)\in\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}
, the enhanced dissipation effect still plays an important role in suppressing the blow-up.

The above Dirichlet boundary conditions can be replaced with the usual Neumann boundary conditions.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of
Theorem
1.1
, and the boundary conditions are replaced by
\partial_{y}n_{1}(t,x,\pm 1)=0
,
\partial_{y}n_{2}(t,x,\pm 1)=0
and
\partial_{y}c(t,x,\pm 1)=0
.
There exists a positive
A_{3}
depending on
||n_{1,{\rm in}}||_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
,

||n_{2,{\rm in}}||_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
,
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}

and
||u_{\rm in}||_{H^{1}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I})}
, such that if
A>A_{3}
, the solution of (
1.4
) is global in time.

2.
Methodology

The system (
1.4
) has two components: the
x
-independent part and the
x
-dependent part.
These two components have markedly different properties, as the
x
-independent part remains constant and does not change,
while the
x
-dependent part varies with
x
.
We decompose the system (
1.4
) into
x
-independent part (
2.1
) and
x
-dependent part (
2.2
) as in
[
25
]
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n_{1,0}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{1,0}=-\frac{\chi_{1}}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(n_{1,\neq}\nabla c_{\neq})_{0}+\partial_{y}(n_{1,0}\partial_{y}c_{0})\Big)-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{1,\neq})_{0},\\
\partial_{t}n_{2,0}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{2,0}=-\frac{\chi_{2}}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(n_{2,\neq}\nabla c_{\neq})_{0}+\partial_{y}(n_{2,0}\partial_{y}c_{0})\Big)-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{2,\neq})_{0},\\
-\triangle c_{0}+c_{0}=n_{1,0}+n_{2,0},\\
\partial_{t}\omega_{0}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle\omega_{0}=-\frac{1}{A}\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})_{0},\\
u_{0}=(\partial_{y}(\partial_{yy})^{-1}\omega_{0},0),\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\partial_{t}n_{1,\neq}+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{1,\neq}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{1,\neq}=-\frac{\chi_{1}}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(n_{1,\neq}\nabla c_{\neq})_{\neq}+\nabla\cdot(n_{1,0}\nabla c_{\neq})+\partial_{y}(n_{1,\neq}\partial_{y}c_{0})\Big)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{1,\neq})_{\neq}+\nabla\cdot(u_{0}n_{1,\neq})+\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{1,0})\Big),\\
\partial_{t}n_{2,\neq}+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}n_{2,\neq}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle n_{2,\neq}=-\frac{\chi_{2}}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(n_{2,\neq}\nabla c_{\neq})_{\neq}+\nabla\cdot(n_{2,0}\nabla c_{\neq})+\partial_{y}(n_{2,\neq}\partial_{y}c_{0})\Big)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{2,\neq})_{\neq}+\nabla\cdot(u_{0}n_{2,\neq})+\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}n_{2,0})\Big),\\
-\triangle c_{\neq}+c_{\neq}=n_{1,\neq}+n_{2,\neq},\\
\partial_{t}\omega_{\neq}+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}\omega_{\neq}-\frac{1}{A}\triangle\omega_{\neq}+2\partial_{x}\triangle^{-1}\omega_{\neq}=-\frac{1}{A}\Big(\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}\omega_{\neq})_{\neq}+\nabla\cdot(u_{0}\omega_{\neq})+\nabla\cdot(u_{\neq}\omega_{0})\Big)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{x}n_{1,\neq}+\partial_{x}n_{2,\neq}),\\
u_{\neq}=\nabla^{\bot}\triangle^{-1}\omega_{\neq}.\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

We introduce a energy functional

E(t)=\|n_{1,\neq}\|_{X_{a}}+\|n_{2,\neq}\|_{X_{a}}+\|\omega_{\neq}\|_{X_{a}}.

Let’s designate
T
as the terminal point of the largest range
[0,T]
such that the following
hypothesis hold

\displaystyle E(t)+||n_{1}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+||n_{2}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq 2E_{0},

(2.3)

for any
t\in[0,T]
, where
E_{0}
will be calculated during the calculation.

In the following sections, our objective is to prove the subsequent proposition, which will allow us to refine the previous hypotheses. By achieving this and ensuring the local well-posedness of the system (
1.4
), we can confirm the global existence of the solution.

Proposition 2.1
.

Under the assumption of
Theorem
1.1
,
there exists a positive constant
A_{2}
depending on
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
,
\chi_{1}^{2}
,
\chi_{2}^{2}
,
||u_{\rm in}||_{H^{1}}
,

||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}}
and
||n_{2,\rm in}||_{L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}}
, such that if
A>A_{2}
, there holds

E(t)+||n_{1}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+||n_{2}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq E_{0},

for all
t\in[0,T].

First, we need to prove that
E(t)
satisfies

E(t)\leq C\big(E_{\rm in}+\frac{C_{0}}{A^{\alpha}}E(t)\big),

where
\alpha>0
and

E_{\rm in}=\|(n_{1,{\rm in}})_{\neq}\|_{X_{a}}+\|(n_{2,{\rm in}})_{\neq}\|_{X_{a}}+\|(\omega_{{\rm in}})_{\neq}\|_{X_{a}}.

When
\frac{CC_{0}}{A^{\alpha}}<\frac{1}{2}
, we get

E(t)\leq 2CE_{\rm in}:=E_{0,1}.

Then, using the Moser-Alikakos iteration
[
25
]
,
we can prove that

||n_{1}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+||n_{2}||_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq E_{0,2}

At last, setting
E_{0}=E_{0,1}+E_{0,2},
the
Proposition
2.1
holds.

3.
Time-space estimates

3.1.
Main result of time-space estimates

In finite channel
\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}
, where
\mathbb{I}=(-1,1)
,
the following time-space estimate can be used to bound the non-zero mode of
\omega
.

Lemma 3.1

(Proposition 4.2 in
[
7
]
)
.

Let

\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\frac{1}{A}\triangle+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x}+2\partial_{x}\triangle^{-1},

and
f
satisfies the following equation

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{0})f=\nabla\cdot f_{1},\\
f(t;x,\pm 1)=0,\\
f\big|_{t=0}=f_{\rm in}=f(0),\end{cases}

(3.1)

then there exists a positive constant
a>0
, such that

||f_{\neq}||_{X_{a}}^{2}\leq C\Big(||f_{\neq}(0)||_{L^{2}}^{2}+{A}||{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{2}}t}(f_{1})_{\neq}||_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

In fact,
Lemma
3.1
only can be used to bound the non-zero mode of
\omega
,
and cannot be used to bound the non-zero mode of
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
.
In this section, we will prove the following time-space estimate according to the ideas of
[
7
,
19
]
,
which can be used to bound the non-zero mode of
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{A}\triangle+(1-y^{2})\partial_{x},

and
f
satisfies the following equation

\begin{cases}(\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L})f=\nabla\cdot f_{2},\\
f(t;x,\pm 1)=0,\\
f\big|_{t=0}=f_{\rm in}=f_{0},\end{cases}

(3.2)

then we have

||f_{\neq}||_{X_{a}}^{2}\leq C\Big(||(f_{0})_{\neq}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+{A}||{\rm e}^{aA^{-\frac{1}{2}}t}(f_{2})_{\neq}||_{L^{2}L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

The steps for proving
Lemma
3.2
are as follows:

•

Taking the Fourier transformation in
x
, the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}
becomes

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}=-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{y}^{2}-|k|^{2})+ik(1-y^{2}).

•

Studying the resolvent of

-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{y}^{2}-|k|^{2})f+ik(1-y^{2}-\lambda)f=F,

and establishing the resolvent estimate

A^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}\leq C||F||_{L^{2}}.

•

Using the semigroup method to obtain the enhanced dissipative estimate

\|{\rm e}^{-t\mathcal{L}}f_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C{\rm e}^{-c^{\prime}A^{-\frac{1}{2}}t-A^{-1}t}\|f_{\neq}\|_{L^{2}},

and proving the time-space estimate.

3.2.
Resolvent estimate for the Orr-sommerfeld equation

In this subsection, we will establish the resolvent estimate for the Orr-sommerfeld equation

\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{y}^{2}-|k|^{2})f+ik(1-y^{2}-\lambda)f=F,\\
f(\pm 1)=0.\end{cases}

(3.3)

The main result of the resolvent estimate is as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{I})
be the solution of (
3.3
) with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

and
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{I})
,
then there holds

A^{-\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}\leq C||F||_{L^{2}}.

Remark 5
.

The proof of

is divided into three parts,

\lambda>1

3.2.1.
Case of
\lambda>1

By integration by parts, we get

\big|{\rm Re}\langle F,f\rangle\big|=\big|\langle-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{y}^{2}-|k|^{2})f,f\rangle\big|=\frac{1}{A}(||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+|k|^{2}||f||_{L^{2}}^{2}),

and

\big|{\rm Im}\langle F,f\rangle\big|=|k|\int_{-1}^{1}(\lambda-1+y^{2})|f|^{2}dy,

which yields that

||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}^{2}+||kf||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq A||F||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{2}},

(3.4)

and

\int_{-1}^{1}(\lambda-1+y^{2})|f|^{2}dy\leq\frac{1}{|k|}||F||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}.

When
y\in[-1,1]\setminus(-\delta,\delta)
, we have

\lambda-1+y^{2}\geq\delta^{2}.

(3.5)

Using (
3.4
), (
3.5
), and
||f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim||f||_{L^{2}}||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}
,
we conclude that

\displaystyle||f||^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq||f||^{2}_{L^{2}([-1,1]\setminus(-\delta,\delta))}+2\delta||f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{[-1,1]\setminus(-\delta,\delta)}\frac{1}{\lambda-1+y^{2}}(\lambda-1+y^{2})|f|^{2}dy+\delta||f||_{L^{2}}||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|\delta^{2}}||F||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}+\delta A^{\frac{1}{2}}||F||^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}}||f||^{\frac{3}{2}}_{L^{2}}.

Taking
\delta=(A^{-1}|k|^{-1})^{\frac{1}{4}}
, we get

||f||_{L^{2}}\lesssim A^{\frac{1}{2}}|k|^{-\frac{1}{2}}||F||_{L^{2}}.

3.2.2.
Case of
0\leq\lambda\leq 1

Let
-1\leq y_{1}\leq 0\leq y_{2}\leq 1
, such that
\lambda=1-y_{1}^{2}=1-y_{2}^{2}
. It is obvious that
y_{1}=-y_{2}
.
The
L^{2}
estimate of the interval
(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1},y_{2})
is stated in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\delta\in(0,1]
, there holds

\displaystyle||f||^{2}_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1},y_{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}}||F||_{L^{2}}}{|k|(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}+\frac{||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A|k|(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}

\displaystyle+\frac{||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{\infty}}}{A|k|\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)}+\delta||f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\Big).

Proof.

By integration by parts, we get

\displaystyle\Big|{\rm Im}\langle F,\frac{f}{1-y^{2}-\lambda}\chi_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|{\rm Im}\langle-\frac{1}{A}(\partial_{y}^{2}-|k|^{2})f+ik(1-y^{2}-\lambda)f,\frac{f}{1-y^{2}-\lambda}\chi_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}k|f|^{2}dy+{\rm Im}\langle-\frac{1}{A}\partial_{y}^{2}f,\frac{f}{1-y^{2}-\lambda}\chi_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}\rangle\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}k|f|^{2}dy+{\rm Im}\Big(-\frac{f^{\prime}\bar{f}}{A(1-y^{2}-\lambda)}\big|_{-1}^{y_{1}-\delta}-\frac{f^{\prime}\bar{f}}{A(1-y^{2}-\lambda)}\big|^{1}_{y_{2}+\delta}\Big)

\displaystyle+{\rm Im}\Big(\frac{1}{A}\int_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}\frac{2yf^{\prime}\bar{f}}{(1-y^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}dy\Big)\Big|,

which yields

\displaystyle|k|\int_{(-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta)}|f|^{2}dy

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\frac{f}{1-y^{2}-\lambda}\big\|_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta))}||F||_{L^{2}}+2\frac{||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A|y_{1}^{2}-(y_{1}-\delta)^{2}|}

(3.6)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A}||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\frac{2y}{(1-y^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{||f||_{L^{2}}||F||_{L^{2}}}{(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}+2\frac{||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{A}||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{\infty}}\big\|\frac{2y}{(1-y^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}\big\|_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta))}.

Thanks to

one obtains

\displaystyle||f||^{2}_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1},y_{2}))}

\displaystyle\leq||f||^{2}_{L^{2}((-1,1)\setminus(y_{1}-\delta,y_{2}+\delta))}+2\delta||f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|f\|_{L^{2}}||F||_{L^{2}}}{|k|(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}+\frac{||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A|k|(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)\delta}

\displaystyle+\frac{||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{\infty}}}{A|k|\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}(y_{2}-y_{1}+\delta)}+\delta||f||^{2}_{L^{\infty}}.

∎

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{(y_{1}+\delta,y_{2}-\delta)}k|f|^{2}dy+{\rm Im}\langle-\frac{1}{A}\partial_{y}^{2}f,\frac{f}{1-y^{2}-\lambda}\chi_{(y_{1}+\delta,y_{2}-\delta)}\rangle\Big|

It is obvious that

\displaystyle\big|\frac{1}{(1-y^{2}-\lambda)^{2}}\big|\lesssim\frac{1}{\delta^{2}(y_{2}-y_{1})^{2}},

for

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|\delta(y_{2}-y_{1})}||F||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}+\frac{2||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A|k|\delta(y_{2}-y_{1})}

\displaystyle+\frac{||f^{\prime}||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{\infty}}}{A|k|\delta^{\frac{3}{2}}(y_{2}-y_{1})},

\displaystyle||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(y_{1},y_{2})}

\displaystyle\leq||f||^{2}_{L^{2}(y_{1}+\delta,y_{2}-\delta)}+2\delta||f||_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|\delta(y_{2}-y_{1})}||F||_{L^{2}}||f||_{L^{2}}+\frac{2||f^{\prime}\bar{f}||_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{1},\delta)\cup B(y_{2},\delta))}}{A|k|\delta(y_{2}-y_{1})}

Lemma 3.6

]
, one deduces

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{1}^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\max\Big\{6C^{2}(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)\chi^{4}p^{4}\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{1}^{p}||^{2}_{L_{1}},2||n_{1,\rm in}^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2},1\Big\},

(5.10)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{2}^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\max\Big\{6C^{2}(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)\chi^{4}p^{4}\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{2}^{p}||^{2}_{L_{1}},2||n_{2,\rm in}^{p}||_{L^{2}}^{2},1\Big\}.

Next the Moser-Alikakos iteration is used to determine
E_{0,2}
.
We rewrite (
5.10
) into

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy\leq\max\Big\{6C^{2}(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)\chi^{4}16^{j}\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{1}^{p}||^{2}_{L_{1}},2\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(0)|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy,1\Big\}.

(5.11)

Due to

\sup_{t\geq 0}||n_{1}(t)||_{L^{2}}\leq|T|||n_{1,0}||_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}+||n_{1,\neq}||_{L^{\infty}L^{2}}\leq|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0},

by interpolation, we have

||n_{1}(0)||_{L^{2^{j}}}\leq||n_{1}(0)||^{\theta}_{L^{2}}||n_{1,\rm in}||^{1-\theta}_{L^{\infty}}\leq||n_{1}(0)||_{L^{2}}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}},\ {\rm for}\ j\geq 1,

which yields

2\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(0)|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy\leq 2\Big(\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}\Big)^{2^{j+1}}\leq K^{2^{j+1}},

where
K=2(|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}+1).

We infer from (
5.11
) that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{j+1}}dxdy\leq\max\Big\{C_{1}16^{j}\Big(\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{j}}dxdy\Big)^{2},K^{2^{j+1}}\Big\},

where
C_{1}=6C^{2}(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)\chi^{4}.

When
j=1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{2}}dxdy\leq C_{1}^{a_{1}}16^{b_{1}}K^{2^{2}},

where
a_{1}=1
and
b_{1}=1
.

When
j=2
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{3}}dxdy\leq C_{1}^{a_{2}}16^{b_{2}}K^{2^{3}},

where
a_{2}=1+2a_{1}
and
b_{2}=2+2b_{1}
.

When
j=k
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{k+1}}dxdy\leq C_{1}^{a_{k}}16^{b_{k}}K^{2^{k+1}},

where
a_{k}=1+2a_{k-1}
and
b_{k}=k+2b_{k-1}
.

Generally, one can obtain the following formulas

a_{k}=2^{k}-1,\ {\rm and}\ \ b_{k}=2^{k+1}-k-2.

Therefore, one obtains

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}(\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{I}}|n_{1}(t)|^{2^{k+1}}dxdy)^{\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}}\leq C_{1}^{\frac{2^{k}-1}{2^{k+1}}}16^{\frac{2^{k+1}-k-2}{2^{k+1}}}K.

Letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, there holds

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n_{1}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 32\sqrt{6}C(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi^{2}(|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}+1).

Similarly, we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|n_{2}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 32\sqrt{6}C(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi^{2}(|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+E_{0}+||n_{2,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}+1).

We conclude that

\|n_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}+\|n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}L^{\infty}}\leq 32\sqrt{6}C(T_{1}^{4}+E_{0}^{4}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\chi^{2}(2|\mathbb{T}|T_{1}+2E_{0}+||n_{1,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}+||n_{2,\rm in}||_{L^{\infty}}+2):=E_{0,2}.

The proof is complete.

∎

A_{2}=\max\{A_{0},A_{1}\}

E_{0}=E_{0,1}+E_{0,2},

the proof of

2.1
is complete.

Forbidden subgraphs and complete partitions
John Byrne, Michael Tait, Craig Timmons

Abstract
A graph is called an ( r , k ) (r,k) -graph if its vertex set can be partitioned into r r parts of size at most k k with at least one edge between any two parts. Let f ⁡ ( r , H ) f(r,H) be the minimum k k for which there exists an H H -free ( r , k ) (r,k) -graph. In this paper we build on the work of Axenovich and Martin, obtaining improved bounds on this function when H H is a complete bipartite graph, even cycle, or tree. Some of these bounds are best possible up to a constant factor and confirm a conjecture of Axenovich and Martin in several cases. We also generalize this extremal problem to uniform hypergraphs and prove some initial results in that setting.

Let

,

.
We write

An

Given an

Graph splits were originally mostly studied with respect to topological properties (see for example
[

There are several interesting questions one can ask, such as the largest complete minor in an

[

[

In another direction, if each

If there is no restriction on the

of

\psi(G)
for the

of

Complete and pseudocomplete colorings of graphs and their associated parameters have a fairly large body of work devoted to them. Researchers have studied them for specific graph families

[

38
]

[

In the case of

Assuming that

-graph
and produces an

-free graph with a

minor by adding vertices and edges to connect the

’s. Regarding coloring, if
G
is an

(r,k)
-graph, then
r\leq\psi(G)
and any

r
-coloring
defines an

-partition. Since we will consider only

-free graphs in this paper and removing edges cannot create a copy of

, any graph with partition

that we consider in this paper may be assumed to have each

an independent set.
So bounds for

can imply bounds on achromatic numbers and pseudoachromatic numbers and vice versa.
However, in this paper we want to minimize the maximum size of a color class in a pseudocomplete
r
-coloring rather than maximizing the number of color classes. Because of these differing objectives, we phrase all of our work in the language of
[
2
]
instead of using the terminology from these graph coloring or minor problems.
It would be interesting to explore these connections in more depth, but this is not the focus of the present paper.

A simple observation is that if

is an

-free

-graph, then

\binom{r}{2}\leq e(G)\leq\textup{ex}(rk,H).

The first inequality is true since there is at least

-free with at most

, this inequality gives a lower bound on

which implies a lower bound on

. Any lower bound on
f(r,H)
obtained using

\binom{r}{2}\leq\textup{ex}(rk,H)

(1)

will be called the “trivial (lower) bound”. Observe that the inequality
\binom{r}{2}\leq e(G)
also holds if

is replaced by
\chi_{a}(G)
,
\psi(G)
, and the Hadwiger number of
G
which the largest
r
such that
K_{r}
is a minor of

(see Proposition 1 of
[
31
]

If

is not bipartite, a simple argument given in
[

. For this reason we will assume that

Supposing for a moment that

Axenovich and Martin proved an upper bound that differs from this lower bound by a logarithmic factor under an assumption on the Turán number of

Theorem 1.1
(Axenovich, Martin
[

]

If

They conjectured that the trivial lower bound (

Conjecture 1.2
(Axenovich, Martin
[

]
)

Let

Using the incidence graph of an affine plane, they proved that Conjecture

C_{4}

Theorem 1.3
(Axenovich, Martin
[
2
]
)

The function

satisfies

r^{1/3}-o(r^{1/3})\leq f(r,C_{4})\leq 2r^{1/3}+o(r^{1/3}).

For any

,
C_{4}
is a subgraph of
K_{2,d+1}
so

. This means that

1.3
implies the same upper bound for

. The asymptotic formula

\textup{ex}(N,K_{2,d+1})=\frac{\sqrt{d}}{2}N^{3/2}+o(N^{3/2})
, proved by Füredi
[
18

, together with the trivial bound (
1
) and Theorem

\frac{1}{d^{1/3}}r^{1/3}-o(r^{1/3})\leq f(r,K_{2,d+1})\leq 2r^{1/3}+o(r^{1/3}).

For fixed

, Theorem
1.3
proves Conjecture
1.2
for

. However, this result does not capture the dependence of

.
Using the projective norm graphs, we improve the constant in the upper bound on

, and we confirm Conjecture
1.2
for
K_{t,(t-1)!+1}

Theorem 1.4

(a) For

,

\frac{1}{((t-1)!-t)^{1/(2t-1)}}r^{1/(2t-1)}-o(r^{1/(2t-1)})\leq f(r,K_{t,(t-1)!+1})\leq 2r^{1/(2t-1)}+o(r^{1/(2t-1)}).

(b) For

\frac{1}{d^{1/3}}r^{1/3}-o(r^{1/3})\leq f(r,K_{2,d+1})\leq\frac{2}{d^{1/3}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-1.5d^{-1/2})^{5/3}}r^{1/3}+o(r^{1/3}).

It is important to remark that improvements for small

In particular, for

Details for how to obtain these bounds can easily be read out of the proof of Theorem

cN^{a}\leq\textup{ex}(N,H)\leq CN^{b}~~\mbox{for some $a<b$ with}~~b-a<\frac{(2-b)(b-1)}{5-b}.

(2)

For

Theorem 1.8

Suppose

Theorem 1.9

Suppose that

are integers and

An example showing that Theorem

If

is a graph and

is a hypergraph, then

is a

if there is a bijection

such that for every

. This notion was introduced in

and generalizes Berge paths and Berge cycles.
For integers

, Györi, Katona, and Lemons

proved that

is the family of

. If

is an

-uniform

-hypergraph with no Berge path of length

, then

. This can rewritten as

In the case of graphs, we are unaware of

However, there is a family of 3-uniform hypergraphs

If

General Bounds

For a set
\mathcal{H}
of
m
-uniform hypergraphs, write

\textup{ex}_{m}(n,\mathcal{H})
for the Turán number of
\mathcal{H}
. This is the maximum number of edges in an
m
-uniform
n
-vertex hypergraph that does not contain any graph in
\mathcal{H}
as a subgraph. When
\mathcal{H}
consists of one graph, say
H
, we write

\textup{ex}_{m}(n,H)
for the Turán number
of
H
. The first result of this section is a
generalization of (
1
) to hypergraphs.

Proposition 2.1

Let
H
be an
m
-uniform hypergraph with

\textup{ex}_{m}(N,H)\leq CN^{e}
for some positive constant
C
.
If there is an
H
-free
m
-uniform
(r,k)
-hypergraph, then

\frac{(r-m)^{m}}{m!}\leq C(rk)^{e}.

Proof.
Suppose that there exists an
H
-free
m
-uniform

(r,k)
-hypergraph
G
. The number of edges of
G

is at least
\binom{r}{m}
and is at most
\textup{ex}_{m}(rk,H)
. Therefore,

\binom{r}{m}\leq e(G)\leq\textup{ex}_{m}(rk,H)

which implies
\frac{(r-m)^{m}}{m!}\leq C(rk)^{e}
.

As
r
tends to infinity, Proposition
2.1
gives the asymptotic lower bound

f_{m}(r,H)\geq(1-o_{r}(1))\frac{r^{m/e-1}}{(Cm!)^{1/e}}.

(4)

Next we prove a generalization to hypergraphs of the proposition

f(r,H)\leq 2~\mbox{for all non-bipartite $H$ and
$r\geq 2$}.

(5)

Let
k\geq 2
and
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}=m
be a partition of
m
. Say that a hypergraph

G
has
Property
B_{\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}}
if there is a
k
-coloring of

V(G)
such that each edge has exactly
c_{i}
vertices of color
i
. So for example, a graph having Property
B_{\{1,1\}}
means that the graph is bipartite.

Proposition 2.2

Let
k\geq 2
and
c_{1}+\cdots+c_{k}
be a partition of
m
. If
H
is any hypergraph that does not have Property
B_{\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}}
, then

f_{m}(r,H)\leq k
for all
r\geq m
.

Proof.
Let
V_{1},\dots,V_{r}
be
r
pairwise disjoint sets
with
|V_{i}|=k
for
1\leq i\leq r
. In each
V_{i}
, color the vertices with
1,2,\dots,k
using each color exactly once. For each
\{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\}\in\binom{[r]}{m}
, let
e_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}
be an edge that contains one vertex from each
V_{i_{j}}
and chosen
so that there are exactly
c_{i}
vertices of color
i
. Let
G
be the hypergraph with vertex set
\cup_{j=1}^{r}V_{j}
whose edges are the
e_{i_{1},\dots,i_{m}}
’s. It is clear that

G
is an
m
-uniform
(r,k)
-hypergraph.
Now let
H
be any
m
-uniform hypergraph which does not have Property
B_{\{c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\}}
. By definition,
G
cannot contain
H
giving us an upper bound on
f_{m}(r,H)
.

Since an
m
-uniform hypergraph is
m
-partite if and only if it has Property

,
Proposition

gives the following generalization of (

).

Corollary 2.3

Let
m\geq 2
and let
H
be an
m
-uniform hypergraph. If

is not
m
-partite, then

f_{m}(r,H)\leq m
for all
r\geq m
.

3.1
Complete bipartite graphs

In this subsection we begin by proving the following result
which will be used to prove

.

Theorem 3.1

Suppose that
q
is an even power of an odd prime and
that
d
is a positive integer for which
d|q-1
.
For any
t\geq 2
and positive integers
a\leq h

for which
ha=\frac{q-1}{d}
,

f(q^{t-1}a,K_{t,(t-1)!d^{t-1}+1})\leq a+h+O(h^{2/5}).

(6)

Proof.
Let
q
be an even power of an odd prime and
assume that
d
is a positive integer that divides
q-1
.
Let
t\geq 2
be an integer and
N:\mathbb{F}_{q^{t-1}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}

be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-norm on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t-1}}
defined by

N(x)=x^{1+q+q^{2}+\dots+q^{t-2}}
.
Let
K_{d}
be the subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
d
elements.
Let
\theta
be an element such that
\langle\theta\rangle=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
so
K_{d}=\langle\theta^{(q-1)/d}\rangle
.
Let
\mathcal{B}_{d}(q,t)
be the bipartite graph with parts
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{t-1}}\times(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}/K_{d})

and
\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{t-1}}\times(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}/K_{d})
where

(x,aK_{d})_{\mathcal{P}}

in
\mathcal{P}

is adjacent to

(y,bK_{d})_{\mathcal{L}}
in
\mathcal{L}
if and only if

N(x+y)\in abK_{d}.

Here
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}/K_{d}
is the quotient ring
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
modulo
K_{d}
. This graph comes from the projective norm graph by taking quotients, in a similar spirit to Füredi’s construction of
K_{2,t+1}
free graphs

\mathcal{P}_{v_{1},v_{3,2},v_{4}}=\{(v_{1},v_{2,1}+v_{2,2}\mu,v_{3,1}+v_{3,2}\mu,v_{4,1}+v_{4,2}\mu)_{\mathcal{P}}:v_{2,1},v_{2,2},v_{3,1}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}\}

where
v_{1},v_{4}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
v_{3,2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
. Each of these parts contains
q^{3/2}
vertices.
Likewise, partition
\mathcal{L}
into the sets

\mathcal{L}_{w_{1},w_{2},w_{4,1}}=\{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3,1}+w_{3,2}\mu,w_{4,1}+w_{4,2}\mu)_{\mathcal{L}}:w_{3,1},w_{3,2},w_{4,2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}\}

where
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
w_{4,1}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
.

Fix two parts
\mathcal{P}_{v_{1},v_{3,2},v_{4}}
and

\mathcal{L}_{w_{1},w_{2},w_{4,1}}
. There will be an edge between these two
parts provided that there exists

v_{2,1},v_{2,2},v_{3,1},w_{3,1},w_{3,2},w_{4,2}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
such that

\displaystyle v_{2,1}+v_{2,2}\mu+w_{2,1}+w_{2,2}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}w_{1}

\displaystyle v_{4,1}+v_{4,2}\mu+w_{3,1}+w_{3,2}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}^{2}w_{1}

\displaystyle v_{3,1}+v_{3,2}\mu+w_{4,1}+w_{4,2}\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle v_{1}w_{1}^{2}.

The first two equations are solved by choosing
v_{2,1}+v_{2,2}\mu
to
equal
v_{1}w_{1}-w_{2,1}-w_{2,2}\mu
,
and
w_{3,1}+w_{3,2}\mu
to equal
v_{1}^{2}w_{1}-v_{4,1}-v_{4,2}\mu
.
The last equation can be solved by
choosing
v_{3,1}
and
w_{4,2}
so that

v_{3,1}+w_{4,2}\mu=v_{1}w_{1}^{2}-v_{3,2}\mu-w_{4,1}
.
This gives a unique solution to this system of equations and so we have
an edge between these parts.
Take an arbitrary pairing of the
\mathcal{P}_{v_{1},v_{3,2},v_{4}}
’s
and
\mathcal{L}_{w_{1},w_{2},w_{4,1}}
’s to obtain a
(p^{5},2p^{3})
-graph. Hence,

f(q^{5/2},\theta_{3,4})\leq 2q^{3/2}
for
q
an even power of an odd prime. By a density of
primes argument,

f(r,\theta_{3,4})\leq 2r^{3/5}+o(r^{3/5}).

The trivial bound together with

\textup{ex}(N,\theta_{3,4})=O(N^{5/4})
proved by Faudree and Simonovits
[
17
]
implies that
f(r,\theta_{3,4})=\Omega(r^{3/5})
.

3.3
Longer even cycles

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
. We first prove a general result that allows one to find an upper bound on
f(r,H)
provided a sufficiently pseudorandom
H
-free graph exists.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with adjacency matrix
A
. Then
A
has
n
real eigenvalues
\rho_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\rho_{n}
. We will make use of the expander-mixing lemma which relates the second largest eigenvalue to the distribution of the edges. This result can be traced back to at least as early as a theorem about designs in Haemers’ Ph.D Thesis
[
23
]
. For the non-bipartite and bipartite versions stated below in graph-theoretic terms, we refer to
[
1
]
and
[
8
]
respectively. If
U,W\subseteq V(G)
then we define

e(U,W)=|\{(u,w):u\in U,w\in W,u\sim w\}|
.

Theorem 3.2
(Expander-Mixing Lemma)

Suppose that
G
is a
d
-regular graph with
\rho=\max\{\rho_{2},-\rho_{n}\}
. Then for any
U,W\subseteq V(G)
, we have

\left|e(U,W)-\frac{d}{n}|U||W|\right|\leq\rho\sqrt{|U||W|}.

Theorem 3.3
(Bipartite Expander-Mixing Lemma)

If
G
is a
d
-regular bipartite graph with parts
X
and
Y
where
\rho_{2}=\rho
, then for any
U\subseteq X,W\subseteq Y
, we have

\left|e(U,W)-\frac{2d}{n}|U||W|\right|\leq\rho\sqrt{|U||W|}.

Theorem 3.4

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with no vertices of degree 1. Suppose there exists an
H
-free
d
-regular graph
G
on
n
vertices, where
d=n^{a}
and
a<\frac{1}{3}
. Let
\rho=\max\{\rho_{2},-\rho_{n}\}
if
G
is not bipartite and
\rho=\rho_{2}
if
G
is bipartite and assume that
\rho=O(d^{\frac{1}{2}})
. Then there exists an absolute constant
C
such that if
n
is large enough, we have
f(m,H)\leq Cm^{\frac{2}{1+a}-1}(\log m)^{\frac{1}{1+a}}
, where
m=\frac{1}{4}n^{\frac{1+a}{2}}/(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.
We fix some notation. Let
V=V(G)
, and if
G
is bipartite let
(X,Y)
be a balanced bipartition. For convenience let
A,B
both denote
V
if
G
is not bipartite, and let
A=X
,
B=Y
if
G
is bipartite. For
U,W\subseteq V
, let
N(U)
be the set of vertices not in
U
which are adjacent to a vertex in
U
. Unless otherwise indicated, all vertices and adjacency relations are within the graph
G
. We show that the following algorithm successfully terminates and outputs an
H
-free
(m,k)
-graph with parts
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
.

1.

Initialize
V_{1}=\cdots=V_{m}=\emptyset
.

2.

For each
i
, add
n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
vertices in
G
to
V_{i}
such that each vertex added is not already contained in any other
V_{j}
and is also at distance at least
3
from every other vertex in
V_{i}
. If
G
is bipartite then all added vertices should also belong to
X
.

3.

For
1\leq i\leq m
, define
\mathcal{S}_{i}=\{j:j\neq i,e(V_{i},V_{j})=0\}
and
s_{i}=|\mathcal{S}_{i}|
. If for every
i
we have
s_{i}<n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, proceed to step 4. Otherwise, for each
1\leq i\leq m
, choose the vertex in
B-(N(V_{i})\cup N(N(V_{i})))-(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m})
which is adjacent to the maximum number of vertices in
\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}V_{j}
, and add it to
V_{i}
. Return to the beginning of step 3.

4.

For each
i
, for each
j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}
, create a new vertex in
V_{i}
and make it adjacent to some vertex in
V_{j}
which was added to
V_{j}
in one of the previous steps.

Is it possible to perform step 2, because at any point during step 2 when we want to add a vertex to
V_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle|A-(N(V_{i})\cup N(N(V_{i})))-(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m})|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}-d^{2}|V_{i}|-m\cdot|V_{1}|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2}-n^{2a+\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{4}n^{\frac{1+a}{2}+\frac{1-a}{2}}.

The choice of
a
ensures that both exponents in the right-hand side are at most 1 and the right-hand side is positive for
n
sufficiently large.

To show it is possible to perform step 3, we track how the number
s_{i}
changes each time a vertex is added. Let
s_{i}(t)
be the value of
s_{i}
after
t
iterations of step 3 have been performed. Now during any of the first
2an^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
iterations of step 3 we have

\displaystyle|B-(N(V_{i})\cup N(N(V_{i})))-(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup

\displaystyle V_{m})|\geq\frac{n}{2}-n^{2a}(n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}+2an^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\frac{n^{\frac{1+a}{2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}(n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}+2an^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}).

The choice of
a
ensures that the right-hand side is
\Theta(n)
. Set
W=B-(N(V_{i})\cup N(N(V_{i})))-(V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m})
. To apply the expander mixing lemma we would like

\frac{d\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right||W|}{n}\gg\rho\sqrt{\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right||W|},

in other words
\Theta(n)=|W|\gg\frac{\rho^{2}n^{2}}{d^{2}\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right|}
. Noting that
\rho=\Theta(d^{\frac{1}{2}})
and
\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right|\geq s_{i}(t)n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq n^{1-a}(\log n)
we see that this condition is met. Therefore,

e\left(W,\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{ds_{i}(t)n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}|W|}{n}=\frac{1}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}|W|s_{i}(t)(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, there exists a vertex in
W
with at least
\frac{1}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}s_{i}(t)(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
neighbors in
\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right|
, and so in iteration
t
of step 3, a vertex is added to
V_{i}
which has at least
\frac{1}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}s_{i}(t)(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
neighbors in
\left|\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)}V_{j}\right|
. Now since each of the sets
V_{j}
has all of its vertices at distance at least 3 from each other, each neighbor of the vertex added belongs to a different set
V_{j}
(where
j\in\mathcal{S}_{i}(t)
). It follows that

s_{i}(t+1)\leq s_{i}(t)-\frac{1}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}s_{i}(t).

Using
s_{i}(0)\leq m\leq n^{\frac{1+a}{2}}/(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we then have that as long as
|W|
is large enough as above,

s_{i}(t)\leq\frac{n^{\frac{1+a}{2}}}{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{t}.

Using the estimate
\log(1-x)\leq-x
, we therefore have

\log s_{i}(t)\leq\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\log n-\frac{1}{2}\log\log n\right)-t\frac{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}.

Step 3 terminates when for all
i
,
s_{i}(t)\leq n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Solving for
t
in the inequality

\left(\frac{1+a}{2}\log n-\frac{1}{2}\log\log n\right)-t\frac{(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}}\leq\log\left(n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

we see that this is guaranteed to happen by iteration
t=2a(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}.

In step 4, none of the vertices created creates a copy of
H
, since
H
has no vertex of degree 1.

We calculate the maximum size of a part in the resulting
(m,k)
-graph. For each
V_{i}
,
n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
vertices were added in step 2, at most
2an^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
were added in step 3, and at most
n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
were added in step 4. Therefore we have
f(m,H)\leq 4n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Choose
C
such that
C\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{\frac{2}{1+a}-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+a}}\geq 4
. Using the fact that

\log m=\frac{1+a}{2}\log n-\log 4-\frac{1}{2}\log\log n\geq\frac{1}{2}\log n

for
n
large enough, we find that

Cm^{\frac{2}{1+a}-1}(\log m)^{\frac{1}{1+a}}\geq 4n^{\frac{1-a}{2}}(\log n)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq f(m,H).

We remark that if
G
is optimal in the sense that
\textup{ex}(N,H)=\Theta(N^{1+a})
then this result matches the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
. An advantage of this theorem is that it applies (if one has a suitably pseudorandom graph) even in cases where the known upper and lower bounds on the Turán number are too far apart to apply Theorem
1.1
, illustrated as follows with even cycles.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We use the bipartite Lubotzky-Phillips-Sarnak graphs
[
34
]
. If
p,q
are primes such that
p
is a nonresidue
\pmod{q}
, then
X^{p,q}
is a
(p+1)-
regular graph on
q(q^{2}-1)
vertices with girth at least
4\log_{p}q-\log_{p}4
and its second eigenvalue is
\rho_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{p}
. To satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.4
we require a sequence of such pairs
(p,q)
where
q>p^{\frac{\ell}{2}}
for the girth and
q\in(p^{\frac{\ell}{2}},Cp^{\frac{\ell}{2}})
for some constant
C>1
to ensure
d=\Theta(n^{\frac{2}{3\ell}})
. A construction of a similar sequence occurs in Section 4 of
[
25
]
and we refer to that paper for the details (the only difference is that in our case
p
must be a nonresidue rather than a residue).

4
Trees and Hypergraphs

4.1
Trees

Let
H
be a graph and
k\geq 2
be an integer.
The
multicolor Ramsey number for
H
, denoted

R(H;k)
, is the smallest
n
such that in every

k
coloring of
E(K_{n})
, there is a
copy of
H
whose edges are all the same color.
It is proved in
[
2
]
that
there is an
H
-free
(r,k)
-graph where

r=R(H;k)-1
. Thus, a lower bound on

R(H;k)
implies an upper bound on
f(r,H)
.
For a tree
T
with
t
edges,
Axenovich and Martin use the bound

R(T;k)>(t-1)\lfloor(k+1)/2\rfloor
to prove
that

\frac{n-1}{2(t-1)}\leq f(n,T)\leq\frac{2(n-1)}{t-1}.

They noted that for large
k
congruent to
1
modulo
n
, there is a better lower bound
R(T;k)>(n-1)k+1
due to Erdős and Graham
[
15
]
.
Erdős
and Graham defined an edge-coloring of the complete graph using resolvable balanced incomplete block designs.
Recall that an
m
-
(r,t,\lambda)
design is an incidence structure consisting of
r
points in which every block contains
t
points, and any set of
m
points is contained in exactly
\lambda
blocks. A BIBD is a design with
t=2
and applying this Ramsey bound to the argument in
[
2
]
gives

f(n,T)\leq\frac{n}{t-1}

for infinitely many
n
. Here we present another construction using designs which gives asymptotically equivalent upper bounds on
f(n,T)
and also generalizes to hypergraphs.

Proposition 4.1

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a set of connected hypergraphs each with more than
t
vertices. If there exists an
m
-
(r,t,1)
design then
f_{m}(r,\mathcal{H})\leq\binom{r-1}{m-1}/\binom{t-1}{m-1}
.

Proof.
Suppose there is a
m
-
(r,t,1)
-design and let

p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
be the points and
B_{1}\dots,B_{b}
be the blocks where
b
is the number of blocks in the design.
Let
G
be the
m
-uniform hypergraph consisting of
b
disjoint copies of
K_{t}^{(m)}
, the
m
-uniform complete hypergraph on
t
vertices. Observe that
G
is
\mathcal{H}
-free since each
H\in\mathcal{H}
is connected with more than
t
vertices, and no component of
G
has more than
t
vertices.
We now prove that
G
is an
(r,k)
-hypergraph where

k
is the replication number
k=\binom{r-1}{m-1}/\binom{t-1}{m-1}
of the design.

Label the vertices in the
j
-th copy of

K_{t}^{(m)}
with
(p_{i_{1}},j),\dots,(p_{i_{t}},j)

where
p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{t}}
are the points
contained in the block
B_{j}
. Consider
the partition
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r}
of
V(G)

where

V_{i}=\{(p_{i},j):p_{i}\in B_{j}\}.

For any
m
parts, say
V_{i_{1}},\dots,V_{i_{m}}
,
the corresponding points
p_{i_{1}},\dots,p_{i_{m}}
form an
m
-set and so there is some block
B_{j}
that contains it. The vertices

\{(p_{i_{1}},j),(p_{i_{2}},j),\dots,(p_{i_{m}},j)\}
form an edge in
G
with one vertex in each
of the
m
parts
V_{i_{1}},\dots,V_{i_{m}}
.

We can apply Proposition
4.1
to any value of
m
by taking
r=\alpha t(t-1)\cdots(t-m+1)+m-1
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}
. By Keevash’s result
[
28
]
on the existence of designs, when
\alpha
is large enough an
m
-
(r,t,1)
design exists and the upper bound of Proposition
4.1
holds. Taking
\alpha
to infinity proves Theorems
1.8
and
1.9
.

4.2
Hypergraphs

Using polarity graphs of Desarguesian planes, Lazebnik and Verstraëte
[
33
]
proved the
asymptotic formula

\textup{ex}_{3}(n,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{3}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})=\frac{1}{6}(1+o(1))n^{3/2}.

(12)

Two constructions were given in
[
33
]
that prove the lower bound. We use one of these constructions to prove an upper bound on
f_{3}(r,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})
and
f_{3}(r,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{3}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})
.

We use similar methods to prove Theorem
1.10
.

Proof of Theorem
1.10
.

Let
q
be an even power of an odd prime, and
C_{q}=\{(x_{1},2^{-1}x_{1}^{2}):x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\}
. Let
G_{q}
be the 3-uniform hypergraph with vertex set

\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}\backslash C_{q}
where
\{(a_{1},a_{2}),(b_{1},b_{2}),(c_{1},c_{2})\}
is an edge if and only if

\displaystyle a_{2}+b_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{1}b_{1}

\displaystyle b_{2}+c_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{1}c_{1}

\displaystyle c_{2}+a_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{1}a_{1}.

This 3-uniform hypergraph is
\mathcal{F}:=\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{3}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\}
-free and has
q^{2}-q
vertices.

Partition
\mathcal{P}
into the
q
parts

\mathcal{P}_{x_{1}}=\{(x,y)\in\mathcal{P}:x=x_{1}\},~x_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}.

A part
\mathcal{P}_{x_{1}}
intersects
C_{q}
in exactly
(x_{1},2^{-1}x_{1}^{2})
so that each part has
q-1
vertices.
Let
\mu
be a root of an irreducible
quadratic over
\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
.
We can then write each
x_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
as
x_{j}=x_{1,j}+x_{2,j}\mu
where

x_{1,j},x_{2,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{\sqrt{q}}
.

Fix three distinct parts
\mathcal{P}_{a_{1}}
,
\mathcal{P}_{b_{1}}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{c_{1}}
. There will be an edge with one vertex in each of these parts if and only if there exists an
a_{2},b_{2},c_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

a_{2}\neq 2^{-1}a_{1}^{2}
,
b_{2}\neq 2^{-1}b_{2}^{2}
,
c_{2}\neq 2^{-1}c_{2}^{2}
, and
a_{2},b_{2},c_{2}
solve
the above system of equations. Row reduction gives that the solution is

a_{2}=2^{-1}(a_{1}b_{1}-b_{1}c_{1}+c_{1}a_{1})
,

b_{2}=2^{-1}(b_{1}c_{1}-c_{1}a_{1}+a_{1}b_{1})
,

c_{2}=2^{-1}(c_{1}a_{1}-a_{1}b_{1}+b_{1}c_{1})
.

For contradiction, suppose that the first equation implies
a_{2}=2^{-1}a_{1}^{2}
.
Then
a_{1}^{2}=a_{1}b_{1}-b_{1}c_{1}+c_{1}a_{1}
and after factoring by grouping,

0=(a_{1}-c_{1})(b_{1}-a_{1})
. This is a contradiction, because the three chosen parts are all distinct. The same argument
shows
b_{2}\neq 2^{-1}b_{1}
and
c_{2}\neq 2^{-1}c_{1}

so that the needed
a_{2},b_{2},c_{2}
exist.
As there is a triple between any three parts,
G_{q}
is a 3-uniform
(q,q-1)
-hypergraph that is
\mathcal{F}
-free. Hence, for any
q
that is an even power of an odd prime,
f_{3}(q,\mathcal{F})\leq q-1
. A density of primes argument and the observation that
f_{3}(r,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})\leq f_{3}(r,\mathcal{F})
completes the upper bound. In
[
16
]
, the formula
\textup{ex}_{3}(n,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})=\frac{1}{6}n^{3/2}+o(n^{3/2})
is proved. Using this formula gives

f_{3}(r,\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\})\geq r+o(r).

Remark:
For
\mathcal{F}=\{\mathcal{C}_{2}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{3}^{(3)},\mathcal{C}_{4}^{(3)}\}
, in this proof we showed that for any even power of an odd prime
q
, one has
f_{3}(q,\mathcal{F})\leq q-1
. If we set
k=f_{3}(q,\mathcal{F})
, in this special case the best possible bound is
q-1
or
q-2
. Indeed,
if
k\leq q-3
, then by Theorem 2.1 from
[
33
]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(qk)\sqrt{qk-3/4}+\frac{1}{12}qk

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(qk)(q^{2}-3q+3/4-3/2)+\frac{1}{12}q(q-3)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}(qk)(q-3/2)+\frac{1}{12}q(q-3)<\binom{q}{3}

for all
q\geq 3
. Thus,
k\in\{q-1,q-2\}
.

Concluding Remarks

In the introduction it was mentioned that this upper bound
can be improved for small
d
. For
d\geq 2
, write
c_{d}
for a value such that
f(r,K_{2,d+1})<c_{d}r^{1/3}+o(r^{1/3})
. One can use the proof of Theorem
1.4
to show that
c_{3}\leq c_{2}\leq 1.89
,
c_{5}\leq c_{4}\leq 1.26
,

We proved that

As noted in the discussion before Theorem
1.6
, the densest known construction of
C_{2\ell}
-free graphs are the
CD(k,q)
graphs of Lazebnik, Ustimenko, and Woldar.
This is a
q
-regular bipartite graph on
2q^{k-\lfloor\frac{k+2}{4}\rfloor+1}
vertices with girth at least
k+5
, if
k
is odd (for details, see
[
32
]
). We cannot apply Theorem
3.4
to
CD(k,q)
because the eigenvalues of these graphs are not fully understood. For the background to the following conjecture, see
[
36
]
.

Conjecture 5.1
(Ustimenko)

For all
(k,q)
, the nontrivial eigenvalues of
D(k,q)
(and therefore
CD(k,q)
) are at most
2\sqrt{q}
.

If the conjecture were true, then Theorem
3.4
would apply to
CD(k,q)
. If
\ell
is odd, we take
2\ell=k+3
so that
n=q^{2\ell-3-\frac{2\ell-2}{4}+1}
and
d=q=n^{\frac{2}{3\ell-3}}
. This would give the upper bound

f(m,C_{2\ell})\leq Cm^{\frac{3\ell-5}{3\ell-1}}(\log m)^{\frac{3\ell-3}{3\ell-1}}.

If
\ell
is even, then again with
2\ell=k+3
we have
n=q^{2\ell-3+\frac{2\ell-4}{4}+1}
and
d=q=n^{\frac{2}{3\ell-6}}
, giving the upper bound

f(m,C_{2\ell})\leq Cm^{\frac{3\ell-8}{3\ell-4}}(\log m)^{\frac{3\ell-6}{3\ell-4}}.

The condition
\rho=O(d^{\frac{1}{2}})
in Theorem
3.4
means that our
H
-free graph is asymptotically a best possible pseudorandom graph. In this case we match the upper bound of Theorem
1.1
. If one weakened this assumption to
\rho=O(d^{b})
for some
b>\frac{1}{2}
one could obtain weaker (but still nontrivial) upper bounds on
f(m,H)
.

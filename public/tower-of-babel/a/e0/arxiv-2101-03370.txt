Trace formulas for time periodic complex Hamiltonians on lattice
Evgeny, L. Korotyaev

Abstract
. We consider time periodic Hamiltonians with complex potentials on the lattice and determine trace formulas. As a corollary we estimate eigenvalues of the quasienergy operator in terms of the norm of potentials.

34A55, (34B24, 47E05)

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Introduction

We discuss scattering and trace formulas for the Schrödinger
equation on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
:

\displaystyle{\tfrac{d}{dt}}u(t)=-ih(t)u(t),\hskip 10.00002pth(t)=\Delta+V(t),

(1.1)

where
h(t)
is the Hamiltonian,
\tau
-periodic in time
t
and

\Delta
is the discrete Laplacian given by

\big(\Delta f\big)_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{|x-y|=1}(f_{x}-f_{y}),\hskip 20.00003ptf=(f_{x})_{x\in{{\mathbb{Z}}}^{d}}\in\ell^{2}({{\mathbb{Z}}}^{d}),\hskip 10.00002ptx=(x_{j})_{1}^{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}.

(1.2)

It is known that the spectrum of the Laplacian
\Delta
is absolutely
continuous and satisfies

\sigma(\Delta)=\sigma_{\textup{ac}}(\Delta)=[0,2d].

Here
V(t)
is
\tau
-periodic in time potential:
(V(t)f)_{x}=V_{x}(t)f_{x},
for all
(t,x)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
.
Introduce the space
\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),p\geqslant 1
of
sequences
f=(f_{x})_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
equipped with the norm
given by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}=\|f\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}=\big(\sum_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|f_{x}|^{p}\big)^{1\over p},\hskip 10.00002pt\ p\in[1,\infty),

and let
\|f\|_{\infty}=\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}=\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|f_{x}|
. For a Banach space
\mathfrak{B}
we write
\ell^{r}(\mathfrak{B}),r\geqslant 1

for the space of
\mathfrak{B}
-valued sequences with
p^{th}
power summable
norms, and
L^{r}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\mathfrak{B})
for the
\mathfrak{B}
-valued
L^{r}
-space. In the
case
f(\cdot)\in L^{r}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
we define the norm

\|f\|_{r,p}
by

\|f\|_{p,r}^{r}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}}\|f(t)\|_{\ell^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})}^{r}dt,\hskip 10.00002ptp,r\geqslant 1.

(1.3)

Note that
\|f\|_{p,r}\leqslant\|f\|_{q,r}
for all
p\geqslant q\geqslant 1
. We
assume that potentials can be complex-valued and satisfy

Condition V.

Let
d\geqslant 3
.
The function
V(t)
is
\tau
-periodic, and satisfies

\displaystyle\|V\|_{p,2}<\infty,\hskip 20.00003pt\begin{cases}1\leqslant p<{6\over 5}&if\ \ d=3\\
1\leqslant p<{4\over 3}&if\ \ d\geqslant 4\end{cases}.

(1.4)

We discuss trace formulas for operators with complex potentials.
Recall that, in general, a trace formula is an identity connecting
the integral of the potential and various sums of eigenvalues and
integrals of coefficients of S-matrix of the Schrödinger operator
(or other spectral characteristics). We shortly describe results
about multidimensional trace formulas:

\bullet
Real potentials. The first result was obtained by Buslaev

[
6
]
, see also
[
12
]
,
[
38
]
and references therein.
Trace formulas for Stark operators and magnetic Schrödinger
operators were discussed in
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
.
The trace formulas for Schrödinger operators on the lattice

{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
with real decaying potentials were determined by
Isozaki–Korotyaev
[
15
]
.

\bullet
Complex potentials. Unfortunately, we know only few papers
about the trace formulas for Schrödinger operators with
complex-valued potentials decaying at infinity. Trace formulas for
Schrödinger operators with complex decaying potentials were
determined by Korotyaev
[
25
]
in the continuous case and in
the discrete case by Korotyaev and Laptev
[
28
]
, Korotyaev

[
27
]
and for the specific case
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits V\leqslant 0
by Malamud and
Neidhardt
[
34
]
.

Our main goal is to determined trace formulas for time periodic
Hamiltonian with complex (and for real) potentials on the lattice.
The case of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
is more complicated
[
24
]
. We do not know
any results about it.

For Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathbb{T}}_{\tau}={\mathbb{R}}/(\tau{\mathbb{Z}})
we introduce the space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},{\mathcal{H}})
of functions
f\to f(t)
that are
\tau
–periodic
in time with values in
{\mathcal{H}}
equipped with the norm

\|f\|^{2}_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}={1\over\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\|f(t)\|^{2}_{{\mathcal{H}}}dt.

The space
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
can be realized as
\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})
via the Fourier
transform
\Phi:\tilde{\mathcal{H}}\to\ell^{2}({\mathcal{H}})
defined by

\displaystyle f\to\Phi f=(f_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}},\hskip 10.00002ptf_{n}=(\Phi f)_{n}={1\over\sqrt{\tau}}\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-int\omega}f(t)dt,\hskip 10.00002pt\omega={2\pi\over\tau},\hskip 10.00002ptf\in\tilde{\mathcal{H}}.

Let
\partial=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
be the self-adjoint operator in

L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau})
. We also denote
\partial=-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
the
corresponding operator in
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
with the natural domain

{\mathscr{D}}={\mathscr{D}}(\partial)
. We use the notation
\langle A(t)\rangle
to indicate
multiplication by
A(t)
on the space
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
. Introduce the
operators
\widetilde{h}_{o}
and
\widetilde{h}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}=L^{2}({\mathbb{T}}_{\tau},\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}))

by

\widetilde{h}_{o}=\partial+\Delta,\hskip 20.00003pt\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{h}_{o}+\langle V(t)\rangle.

It is known that the spectrum
\sigma(\Delta)=[0,2d]
. Then the spectrum of

\widetilde{h}_{o}
has the form

\sigma(\widetilde{h}_{o})=\sigma_{ac}(\widetilde{h}_{o})=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\sigma(\Delta+\omega n)=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}[\omega n,\omega n+2d].

(1.5)

Note that if
\omega>2d
, then the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}_{o}
has the band
structure with the bands
\sigma(\Delta+\omega n)=[\omega n,\omega n+2d]
separated by
gaps. Let
{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}_{2}
be the trace and the Hilbert-Schmidt
class equipped with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{1}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{B}}_{2}}
, respectively. Introduce the free resolvent
R_{o}(\lambda)=(\widetilde{h}_{o}-\lambda)^{-1},\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}
. Below we show that if
V

satisfies Condition V, then

VR_{o}(\lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\ \lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}.

(1.6)

This yields
{\mathscr{D}}(\widetilde{h})={\mathscr{D}}(\widetilde{h}_{o})
and

\sigma_{ess}(\widetilde{h})=\sigma_{ess}(\widetilde{h}_{o}).

(1.7)

Thus the operator
\widetilde{h}
has only discrete spectrum in
{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}
.
Define the perturbed resolvent
R(\lambda)=(\widetilde{h}-\lambda)^{-1}
for all

\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}\setminus\sigma_{disc}(\widetilde{h})
. We have the very useful identity

\langle e^{it\omega}\rangle R(\lambda)\langle e^{-it\omega}\rangle=R(\lambda+\omega),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}.

(1.8)

It means that the spectrum of
\widetilde{h}
(and
\widetilde{h}_{o}
) is

\omega
–periodic. Thus it is sufficient to study eigenvalues of
\widetilde{h}
in a strip
\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits\lambda\in[0,\omega)
. We consider the case of the half
strip
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
defined by

\textstyle\Lambda=[0,\omega)\times i{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\subset{\mathbb{C}}_{+},\hskip 20.00003pt\omega={2\pi\over\tau}.

The proof for the lower half strip
\overline{\Lambda}
is similar. The
operator
\widetilde{h}
has
N\leqslant\infty
eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j},j=1,....,N\}

in the strip
\Lambda
. Here and below each eigenvalue is counted
according to its algebraic multiplicity. We have similar
consideration for the case
{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

1.2.
Main results

We assume that a potential
V
satisfy Condition V. We define a
operator-valued function
\mathfrak{F}
(below we show that
\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{2}

\mathfrak{F}(\lambda)=|V|^{1\over 2}R_{0}(\lambda)|V|^{1\over 2}e^{i\mathop{\mathrm{arg}}\nolimits V},\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm},

(1.9)

and

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\mathcal{D}}(\lambda)=\mathop{\mathrm{det}}\nolimits\Big[(I+\mathfrak{F})e^{-\mathfrak{F}}\Big](\lambda),\hskip 20.00003pt\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}.

(1.10)

is the basic function to study trace formulas. We describe
main properties of

.

Let

1) For complex potentials we discuss eigenvalues

B_{n}=\psi_{n}+\mu_{n},\hskip 20.00003ptn=1,2,3,....

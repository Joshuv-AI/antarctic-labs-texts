Introduction

Recently, sharp convergence rate in the Wasserstein distance has been derived in
[
14
]
for empirical measures of symmetric diffusion processes on compact Riemannian manifolds, see
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
for further study of Dirichlet diffusion processes and SDEs/SPDEs, and see

[
1
,
2
,
5
]
and references within for earlier results on i.i.d. random variables and discrete time Markov chains.
In this paper,
we aim to extend the main results of
[
14
]
to jump processes, for which a natural model is the subordination of diffusion processes.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional connected compact Riemannian manifold possibly with a smooth boundary
\partial M
. Let
V\in C^{2}(M)
such that
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x
is a probability measure on
M
, where
\text{\rm{d}}x
is the Riemannian volume measure on
M
. Then the (reflecting, if
\partial M\neq\emptyset
) diffusion process
X_{t}
generated by
L:=\Delta+\nabla V
on
M
is reversible; i.e. the associated diffusion semigroup
\{P_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is symmetric in
L^{2}(\mu)
, where

P_{t}f(x):=\mathbb{E}^{x}f(X_{t}),\ \ t\geq 0,f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M).

Here,
\mathbb{E}^{x}
is the expectation taken for the diffusion process
\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with

X_{0}=x,
and we will use
\mathbb{P}^{x}
to denote the associated probability measure.
In general, for
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
(the set of all probability measures on
M
), let
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\nu}
be the expectation and probability taken for the diffusion process with initial distribution
\nu
. For any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
and
t\geq 0
,
\nu P_{t}:=\mathbb{P}^{\nu}(X_{t}\in\cdot)
is the distribution of
X_{t}
with initial distribution
\nu
.

A function
B\in C^{\infty}((0,\infty);[0,\infty))\cap C([0,\infty);[0,\infty))
is called a Bernstein function if

(-1)^{n-1}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}^{n}}{\text{\rm{d}}r^{n}}B(r)\geq 0,\ \ n\in\mathbb{N},r>0.

We will use the following classes of Bernstein functions:

\displaystyle{\bf B}:=\big\{B:B\text{\ is\ a\ Bernstein\ function\ with }B(0)=0,B^{\prime}(0)>0\big\},

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}:=\bigg\{B\in{\bf B}:\ \int_{1}^{\infty}r^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-tB(r)}\text{\rm{d}}r<\infty\ \text{for}\ t>0\bigg\}.

For each
B\in{\bf B}
, there exists a unique stable process
S_{t}^{B}
on
[0,\infty)
with Laplace transform

(1.1)

\mathbb{E}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda S_{t}^{B}}=\text{\rm{e}}^{-tB(\lambda)},\ \ t,\lambda\geq 0.

Moreover, for any
\alpha\in[0,1]
, let

\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}:=\Big\{B\in\mathbb{B}:\ \liminf_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{-\alpha}B(\lambda)>0\Big\},\ \ \mathbb{B}_{\alpha}:=\Big\{B\in\mathbb{B}:\ \limsup_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}\lambda^{-\alpha}B(\lambda)<\infty\Big\}.

For any
B\in{\bf B}
, let
X_{t}^{B}
be the Markov process on
M
generated by
B(L):=-B(-L)
, which can be constructed as the time change (subordination) of
X_{t}
:

X_{t}^{B}=X_{S^{B}_{t}},\ \ t\geq 0,

where
(S^{B}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the stable process satisfying (
1.1
) independent of
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
We consider the empirical measure

\mu_{t}^{B}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X_{s}^{B}}\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t>0.

Let
\rho
be the Riemiannian distance (i.e. the length of shortest curve linking two points) on
M
.
For any
p>0
, the
L^{p}
-Wasserstein distance
\mathbb{W}_{p}
is defined by

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\bigg(\int_{M\times M}\rho(x,y)^{p}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p\lor 1}},\ \ \mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{P},

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the set of all probability measures on
M\times M
with marginal distributions
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
. A measure
\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is called a coupling of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.

Since
M
is connected and compact,
L
has discrete spectrum and all eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
of
-L
listed in the increasing order counting multiplicities satisfy (see for instance
[
3
]
)

(1.2)

\kappa^{-1}i^{\frac{2}{d}}\leq\lambda_{i}\leq\kappa i^{\frac{2}{d}},\ \ i\geq 0

for some constant
\kappa>1
. Our main results are stated as follows, which cover the corresponding assertions derived in
[
14
]
for
B(\lambda)=\lambda
.

Theorem 1.1

(Lower bound estimates)
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}
.

(1)

There exists a constant
c\in(0,1]
with
c=1
when
\partial M
is empty or convex, such that

\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{x\in M}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}\geq c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}.

(2)

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
.
If
d>2(1+\alpha)
, then for any
p>0
,

\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{x\in M}\Big\{t^{\frac{2}{d-2\alpha}}\big(\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{2}{p\land 1}}\Big\}>0.

(3)

Let
B(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
. If
d=2(1+\alpha)\ (
i.e.
\alpha=1
and
d=4
, or
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
d=3)
,
M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
V=0
, then

\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{x\in M}\Big\{\frac{t}{\log t}\big(\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)]\big)^{2}\Big\}>0.

Theorem 1.2

(Upper bound estimates)
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

(1)

If
d<2(1+\alpha)
, then

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}<\infty.

(2)

If
d>2(1+\alpha)
, then

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}\Big\{t^{\frac{2}{d-2\alpha}}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}<\infty.

(3)

If
d=2(1+\alpha)
, i.e. either
\alpha=1
and
d=4
, or
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
and
d=3
, then

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}\Big\{\frac{t}{\log t}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}<\infty.

The following is a straightforward consequence of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{B}_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
.

(1)

If
\partial M
is empty or convex, then

(1.3)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}

uniformly in
x\in M
, where the limit is finite if and only if
d<2(1+\alpha)
.
In general, there exists a constant
c\in(0,1]
such that

(1.4)

\begin{split}&c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{x\in M}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}\\
&\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\Big\}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}.\end{split}

(2)

If
d>2(1+\alpha)
, then for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha)
there exist constants
c>c(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle c(\varepsilon)t^{-\frac{2}{d-2\alpha}}\leq\inf_{x\in M}\big(\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{t}^{B},\mu)]\big)^{\frac{2}{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{B},\mu)^{2}]\leq\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq ct^{-\frac{2}{d-2\alpha}},\ \ \ t\geq 1.

(3)

Let
d=2(1+\alpha),
i.e. either
d=3
and
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}
, or
\alpha=1
and
d=4
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq ct^{-1}\log t,\ \ t\geq 2.

On the other hand, when
B(\lambda)=\lambda^{\alpha},M=\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
V=0
, then there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

\inf_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu^{B}_{t},\mu)^{2}]\geq c^{\prime}t^{-1}\log t,\ \ t\geq 2.

Finally, we have the following result on the weak convergence of
t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{B},\mu)^{2}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
B\in\mathbb{B}^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in[0,1]
, and let
\partial M
be empty or convex. If
d<2(1+\alpha)
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}|\mathbb{P}^{x}(t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{B},\mu)^{2}<a)-F(a)|=0,\ \ a\geq 0,

where
F(a):=\mathbb{P}(\xi<a)
for

\xi:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\xi_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})}

and i.i.d. random variables
\{\xi_{i}\}
with the standard normal distribution
N(0,1)
.

Following the line of
[
14
]
, we will first study the modified empirical measure
\mu_{t,r}^{B}:=\mu_{t}^{B}P_{r}
for
r>0
in Section 2, present some lemmas in Section 3,
and finally prove Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
in Sections 4, 5 and 6 respectively.

2
Modified empirical measures

In this part, we allow
M
to be non-compact, but assume that the (Neumann) semigroup
P_{t}
generated by
L
is ultracontractive, i.e.

(2.1)

\|P_{t}\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}:=\sup_{\mu(|f|)\leq 1}\|P_{t}f\|_{\infty}<\infty,\ \ t>0.

Consequently,
-L
has discrete spectrum and the heat kernel
p_{t}(x,y)
of
P_{t}
with respect to
\mu
satisfies

(2.2)

p_{t}(x,y)=1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-t\lambda_{i}}\phi_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(y)\leq\|P_{t}\|_{1\rightarrow\infty}<\infty,\ \ t>0,x,y\in M,

where
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
are all eigenvalues of
-L
and

\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is the eigenbasis, i.e.
\phi_{0}\equiv 1
and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mu)
with
L\phi_{i}=-\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
.

For any
p\geq 1
and
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
, let
\|f\|_{p}:=\{\mu(|f|^{p})\}^{\frac{1}{p}}
be the
L^{p}(\mu)
-norm of
f
.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|P_{t}f\|_{p}\leq c\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{1}t}\|f\|_{p},\ \ t\geq 0,p\in[1,\infty],f\in L^{p}_{0}(\mu),

where
L_{0}^{p}(\mu):=\{f\in L^{p}(\mu):\mu(f):=\int_{M}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0\}
.
Consequently, for any
B\in\mathbb{B}
,

(2.3)

\|P_{t}^{B}f\|_{p}=\|\mathbb{E}P_{S_{t}^{B}}f\|_{p}\leq c\|f\|_{p}\mathbb{E}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda_{1}S_{t}^{B}}=c\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{1})t}\|f\|_{p},\ \ t\geq 0,p\in[1,\infty],f\in L^{p}_{0}(\mu).

As in
[
14
]
, we consider the modified empirical measure

\mu_{t,r}^{B}:=\mu_{t}^{B}P_{r},\ \ \ r,t>0.

By (
2.2
), we have

(2.4)

f_{t,r}:=\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\mu_{t,r}^{B}}{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\text{\rm{e}}^{-r\lambda_{i}}\psi_{i}(t)\phi_{i},\ \ \psi_{i}(t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{i}(X_{s}^{B})\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ \ \ r,t>0.

The main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
B\in{\bf B}
,
M
be a
d
-dimensional connected complete Riemannian manifold possibly with a boundary such that
(
2.1
)
holds.

(1)

For any
r>0
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{x\in M}\left|t\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t,r},\mu)^{2}]-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\right|=0.

(2)

For any
C>0
, let

\mathscr{P}(C):=\{\nu\in\mathscr{P}:\ \nu=h_{\nu}\mu,\|h_{\nu}\|_{\infty}\leq C\}.

Then for any
C>1
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}(C)}\left|\mathbb{P}^{\nu}(t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t,r}^{B},\mu)^{2}<a)-F_{r}(a)\right|=0,\quad a\in\mathbb{R},

where for i.i.d. random variables
\xi_{i}
with distribution
N(0,1)
,
F_{r}:=\mathbb{P}(\xi_{r}<\cdot)
is the distribution function of

\xi_{r}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\xi_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2\lambda_{i}r}},\quad r>0.

To prove this result, we first present some lemmas, where the first follows from
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
, which goes back to
[
1
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
B\in{\bf B}
,
\mathscr{M}(a,b):=\frac{a-b}{\log a-\log b}1_{\{a\wedge b>0\}}.
Then

\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu^{B}_{t,r},\mu)^{2}\leq\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla L^{-1}(f_{t,r}-1)|^{2}}{\mathscr{M}(f_{t,r},1)}\,\text{\rm{d}}\mu,\quad t,r>0.

By the ergodicity we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{M}(f_{t,r},1)=1
(see Lemma
2.4
below), so that this lemma implies that
t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t,r}^{B},\mu)^{2}
is asymptotically bounded above by

(2.5)

\Xi_{r}(t):=t\mu\big(|\nabla L^{-1}(f_{t,r}-1)|^{2}\big),\ \ \quad t,r>0,

where
\mu(f):=\int_{M}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu
for
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
.
Thus, we first estimate
\Xi_{r}(t)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
B\in{\bf B}
.
There exists a constant
c>0
such that

(2.6)

\left|\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Xi_{r}(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\right|\leq\frac{c\|h_{\nu}\|_{\infty}}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}},\quad t\geq 1,r>0,

holds for any probability measure
\nu=h_{\nu}\mu.
Consequently,

(2.7)

\sup_{x\in M}\left|\mathbb{E}^{x}\Xi_{r}(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\right|\leq\frac{c\|P_{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{2\rightarrow\infty}^{2}}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{r\lambda_{i}}},\quad t\geq 1,r>0.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
),
L\phi_{i}=-\lambda_{i}\phi_{i}
and
\mu(\phi_{i}\phi_{j})=1_{\{i=j\}}
for
i,j\geq 0
,
we obtain

(2.8)

\Xi_{r}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{|\psi_{i}(t)|^{2}}{\lambda_{i}\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}},\quad t,r>0.

Since
P_{t}^{B}
is the Markov semigroup of
X_{t}^{B}
, the Markov property implies

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(\phi_{i}(X_{t}^{B})|X_{s}^{B})=P_{t-s}^{B}\phi_{i}(X_{s}^{B})=\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{i})(t-s)}\phi_{i}(X_{s}^{B}),\ \ i\geq 0,t\geq s\geq 0.

So,
\psi_{i}(t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{i}(X_{s}^{B})\text{\rm{d}}s
satisfies

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}^{\nu}|\psi_{i}(t)|^{2}=\frac{1}{t}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{i}(X^{B}_{s})\,\text{\rm{d}}s\right|^{2}=\frac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\,\text{\rm{d}}s_{1}\int_{s_{1}}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\phi_{i}(X^{B}_{s_{1}})\phi_{i}(X^{B}_{s_{2}})]\,\text{\rm{d}}s_{2}\\
&=\frac{2}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}|\phi_{i}(X^{B}_{s_{1}})|^{2}\,\text{\rm{d}}s_{1}\int_{s_{1}}^{t}\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{i})(s_{2}-s_{1})}\,\text{\rm{d}}s_{2}\\
&=\frac{2}{B(\lambda_{i})t}\int_{0}^{t}\nu(P_{s}^{B}\phi_{i}^{2})(1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{i})(t-s)})\,\text{\rm{d}}s,\ \ t>0.\end{split}

This together with (
2.8
) imply

(2.10)

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Xi_{r}(t)=\frac{2}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\int_{0}^{t}\nu(P_{s}^{B}\phi_{i}^{2})(1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{i})(t-s)})\,\text{\rm{d}}s=:I_{1}+I_{2},

where

(2.11)

\begin{split}I_{1}:=\frac{2}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-(t-s)B(\lambda_{i})}}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\,\text{\rm{d}}s=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}-\frac{2}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{i})t}}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})^{2}\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}},\end{split}

and due to
\nu(P^{B}_{s}\phi_{i}^{2})=\mu(h_{\nu}P_{s}^{B}\phi_{i}^{2})=\mu(\phi_{i}^{2}P_{s}^{B}h_{\nu})
,

I_{2}:=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Xi_{r}(t)-I_{1}=\frac{2}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1-\text{\rm{e}}^{-(t-s)B(\lambda_{i})}}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\mu(\phi_{i}^{2}P_{s}^{B}h_{\nu}-1)\,\text{\rm{d}}s.

Since
\mu(\phi_{i}^{2})=1,
by (
2.3
), there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

|\mu(\phi_{i}^{2}P_{s}^{B}h_{\nu}-1)|=|\mu((P_{s}^{B}h_{\nu}-1)\phi_{i}^{2})|\leq\|P_{s}^{B}(h_{\nu}-1)\|_{\infty}\leq c_{0}\text{\rm{e}}^{-B(\lambda_{1})s}\|h_{\nu}\|_{\infty},\quad s\geq 0.

Therefore, we find a constant
c_{1}>0
such that

(2.12)

|I_{2}|\leq\frac{c_{1}}{t}\|h_{\nu}\|_{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}<\infty.

Combining (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we find a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\Xi_{r}(t)-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}\right|\leq\frac{c_{2}\|h_{\nu}\|_{\infty}}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}B(\lambda_{i})\text{\rm{e}}^{2r\lambda_{i}}}.

When

g(r_{1},r_{2}):=(\phi_{i}P^{B}_{r_{2}-r_{1}}\phi_{i})(X^{B}_{r_{1}})=\text{\rm{e}}^{-(r_{2}-r_{1})B(\lambda_{i})}\phi_{i}(X^{B}_{r_{1}})^{2}.

On the other hand, by (

Lemma 3.5

Let

and

Next, for any

and
\varepsilon>0

\nu_{x,\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{P}(\alpha_{\varepsilon}),\ \ x\in M,\varepsilon>0.

3.18

Similarly, the last estimate also holds.

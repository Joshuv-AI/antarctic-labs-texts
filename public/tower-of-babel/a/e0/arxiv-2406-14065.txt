Abstract
This paper is the second in a series of works on weak convergence of one-step schemes for solving stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with one-sided Lipschitz conditions. It is known that the super-linear coefficients may lead to a blowup of moments of solutions and numerical solutions and thus affect the convergence of numerical methods. Wang et al. (2023, IMA J. Numer. Anal.) have analyzed weak convergence of one-step numerical schemes when solutions to SDEs have all finite moments . Therein some modified Euler schemes have been discussed about their weak convergence orders. In this work, we explore the effects of limited orders of moments on the weak convergence of a family of explicit schemes. The schemes are based on approximations/modifications of terms in the Ito-Talyor expansion. We provide a systematic but simple way to establish weak convergence orders for these schemes. We present several numerical examples of these schemes and show their weak convergence orders. AMS subject classification: 60H35, 60H15, 65C30. Keywords: Non-globally Lipschitz coefficients; modified Ito-Taylor schemes; weak approximation; sigmoid functions

Introduction

In this work, we investigate weak convergence of first- and second-order explicit schemes for stochastic differential equations (SDEs) with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients of superlinear growth, following discussions in
[
13
,
17
]
.
In
[
17
]
, an approximation theorem is established on the weak convergence orders of one-step numerical schemes with
infinity
moments.
The weak convergence of first-order numerical schemes has also been analyzed for SDEs with global Lipschitz coefficients
[
13
]
and globally monotone coefficients
[
17
,
19
]
.
However, several limitations in
[
17
]

should be addressed. The limitations include but are not limited to
1)
requiring infinitely many moments of exact solutions and numerical solutions, 2) schemes of at most first-order weak convergence, and 3) not structure-preserving from the schemes.

We will address the first two limitations by discussing
1) relaxing requirements of all finite moments, which necessitate the moments of derivatives of solutions with respect to the initial condition;
2) second-order schemes obtained from modifying classical second-order schemes for SDEs with Lipschitz coefficients.
We will not focus on 3) while we discuss some possibilities in Remark
3.11
. For 1), we extend the weak convergence theorem for one-step numerical schemes in
[
17
]
, where the authors assume the solutions to the SDEs have infinitely many moments
before developing a weak error analysis with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients of superlinear growth. We will reduce the requirements of all finite moments of both exact and numerical solutions from the infinite order to a finite order for a
q
-th order numerical scheme (
0<q\leq 2
). The reduction allows the design of numerical schemes of weak convergence when solutions have only a few moments. Also, we show the required moments of finite orders are critical for the desired convergence order.
For 2), since only first-order schemes are considered in
[
17
,
3
]
, we discuss numerical schemes with second-order weak convergence, which haven’t been explored for SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients. We modify schemes of weak order two for SDEs with Lipschitz coefficients and show that the weak convergence order of the modified schemes may degenerate to one for nonlinear SDEs with only limited moments.
See Example
3.16
for a representative example.

We omit a thorough review of schemes of weak convergence for SDEs with non-globally Lipschitz coefficients and refer interested readers to
[
17
]
.

The main novelty and contributions of the work are summarized as follows.

•

Relaxed assumptions
(compared to
[
17
]
).
We relax one of the key assumptions in the fundamental theorem from
[
17
]
, which requires all moments for solutions to SDEs and their numerical approximations. The relaxation leads to wider applications of the fundamental theorem to practical numerical schemes. To establish a similar theorem with the relaxation, we investigate the solutions and their derivatives with respect to the initial data. After analyzing the moments of these derivatives, we provide a simpler proof (see Appendix
A
and
B
)
with a more relaxed assumption than in
[
17
]
, which is inherited from

[
2
,
17
]
.

•

Schemes of weak convergence of high orders and effects of moments of solutions
.
Based on classical second-order schemes, we present several modified schemes (Examples
3.10
-
3.14
) and discuss their weak convergence orders for nonlinear SDEs. We show in Examples

3.16
(theoretically) and

4.2
(numerically) that the convergence order of the schemes of
second-order weak convergence
become one when the moment conditions are not satisfied.

The article is structured as follows. We introduce key notations and assumptions in Section
2
. We also present an approximation theorem for SDEs with weaker assumptions than those in
[
17
]
. The proof for the improvement is found in Appendix
C

We introduce second-order weak convergence schemes in Section

The essential proofs of the main results are presented

2.2
The approximation theorem

We begin by defining
Y(t,x;t+h)
as the one-step approximation for the solution
X(t,x;t+h)
, with an initial value
x
on a uniform mesh on
[0,T]
and a step size given by
h=\frac{T}{N}
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
. On the uniform mesh grid

\{t_{n}=nh,n=0,1,\cdots,N\}
, we recursively construct the numerical approximations
\{Y_{n}\}_{0\leq n\leq N}
using the relation
Y_{n+1}=Y(t_{n},Y_{n};t_{n+1})
. To simplify notation,
C
denotes a positive constant independent of the step size
h
.

We consider the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(Y_{n})]
to
\mathbb{E}[\varphi(X_{t_{n}})]
where
\varphi
and its derivatives have at most a polynomial growth at infinity.

Assumption 2.3
.

Let
\varphi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
(
m\geq 1
).
Assume that
\varphi\in\mathbb{G}^{2q+2}
, i.e., there exist constants

and
\kappa\geq 1
such that

|D^{j}\varphi(x)|\leq L(1+|x|^{\kappa}),\,j\in\{j=0,1,2,...,2q+2\}.

(2.11)

The following regularity condition is needed for our main theorem.

Lemma 2.4
.

(c.f.
[
2
, Theorem 1.3.6]
)

Let Assumption
2.1
and inequality
2.1
be fulfilled.
Then, the solution to SDE (

is

with respect to the initial data

and for any

it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[|DX(t,x;s)|^{\mathds{P}}]

\displaystyle\leq C(T,\mathds{P}),\quad 1\leq\mathds{P}\leq p_{0},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[|D^{j}X(t,x;s)|^{\mathds{P}}]

\displaystyle\leq C(T,\mathds{P},j),\quad\mathds{P}\in\big[1,\frac{p_{0}}{((2r+1-j)\vee 0)+j}\big],\quad j=2,\ldots,2q+2,

(2.13)

where

This lemma is proved in Appendix

has been known if the coefficients are Lipschtiz continuous

[

]

Theorem 2.5

(The approximation theorem, c.f.
[

]
)
.

Let Assumption
2.3
hold.
Suppose

(i)

(Coefficients of SDEs)
Assumptions
2.1
and
2.1
hold;

(ii)

(Local approximation error)
For some constants
C>0
and
\varkappa\geq 0
, and for any
i_{j}\in\{1,2,\cdots,d\}
and

, the one-step approximation

has the following orders of accuracy:

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(\delta_{X,x}\big)^{i_{j}}\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod_{j=1}^{s}\big(\delta_{Y,x}\big)^{i_{j}}\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{\varkappa})h^{q+1},\,\,\,s=1,\ldots,2q+1,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{j=1}^{2q+2}\big(\delta_{X,x}\big)^{i_{j}}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{\varkappa})h^{q+1},

(2.15)

\displaystyle\Big\|\prod_{j=1}^{2q+2}\big(\delta_{Y,x}\big)^{i_{j}}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|x|^{\varkappa})h^{q+1},

(2.16)

where we denote

(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{j}}:=X^{i_{j}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{i_{j}},\quad(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{j}}:=Y^{i_{j}}(t,x;t+h)-x^{i_{j}}.

(Moment bounds)

(2.17)

Then we obtain a global weak convergence order of order

\big|\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(X(t_{0},X_{0};T)\big)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(Y(t_{0},Y_{0};t_{N})\big)\big]\big|\leq C\big(1+|X_{0}|^{\beta(\beta\kappa+\varkappa)}\big)h^{q}.

(2.18)

The proof of this theorem is presented in Appendix

3
Schemes of weak convergence
based on the Itô-Taylor expansion

Here we recall the Milstein–Talay method, a one-step approximation based on the Itô-Taylor expansion
[
15
]
:

\displaystyle X_{n+1}

\displaystyle=Y_{MT}(t_{n},X_{n};t_{n}+h),

\displaystyle Y_{MT}(t,x;t+h)

\displaystyle=x+f(x)h+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int_{t}^{t+h}g^{r}(x)\,\text{d}W_{r}(s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{r_{1}=1}^{m}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{t}^{s}\Lambda_{r_{1}}g^{r}(x)\,\text{d}W_{r_{1}}(s_{1})\text{d}W_{r}(s)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int_{t}^{t+h}\mathcal{L}g^{r}(x)h\,\text{d}W_{r}(s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int_{t}^{t+h}\int_{t}^{s}\big(\Lambda_{r}f(x)-\mathcal{L}g^{r}(x)\big)\,\text{d}W_{r}(s_{1})\text{d}s+\frac{h^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}f(x).

(3.1)

Here
\Lambda_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}g^{i,r}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\text{ and }\mathcal{L}=f^{\top}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{i,j=1}^{d}g^{i,r}g^{j,r}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}.

The method is shown to have weak convergence of order two when

and

[

]

When Lipschitz continuity conditions are violated and coefficients have polynomial growth instead of linear growth, the Milstein–Talay method can explode at a certain time as in the Euler scheme
[
5
]

Inspired by modified Euler schemes in the literature, we propose the following scheme

\displaystyle Y_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Y_{n}+\mathcal{T}_{1}({f}(Y_{n}),h)h+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}_{2}({g^{r}}(Y_{n}),h)\,\Delta W_{r}(n)

(3.2)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{r_{1}=1}^{m}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{t_{n}}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{3}\big(\Lambda_{r_{1}}g^{r}(Y_{n}),h\big)\,\text{d}W_{r_{1}}(s_{1})\text{d}W_{r}(s)+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathcal{T}_{4}\big(\mathcal{L}g^{r}(Y_{n}),h\big)h\,\text{d}W_{r}(s)

\displaystyle+\sum_{r=1}^{m}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\int_{t_{n}}^{s}\mathcal{T}_{5}\big((\Lambda_{r}f(Y_{n})-\mathcal{L}g^{r}(Y_{n})),h\big)\,\text{d}W_{r}(s_{1})\text{d}s+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}\mathcal{T}_{6}\big(\mathcal{L}f(Y_{n}),h\big)\frac{h}{2}\,\text{d}s,

where the maps
\mathcal{T}_{i}(\cdot)
(
i=1,\ldots,6
) are approximations of ‘
\cdot
’ or zero and will be specified in Section
3.1
and

\Delta W_{r}(n)=W_{r}(t_{n+1})-W_{r}(t_{n}),\,n=0,1,2,\ldots,N-1.

Proof of Lemma

3.6
.

To prove the boundedness of moments, we adopt a proof strategy similar to that of Lemma
3.2
. However, we provide only the necessary details to streamline our presentation. The key to proving the boundedness of moments is to estimate the growth of the solution under some events

\Omega_{\mathcal{R},n}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\sup_{0\leq i\leq n}\left|Y_{i}(\omega)\right|\leq\mathcal{R}\right\},\quad n=0,1,\ldots,N,\,\quad N\in\mathbb{N}.

For integer
\bar{p}\geq 1
, We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\mathds{1}_{\Omega_{\mathcal{R},n+1}}|Y_{n+1}|^{\bar{p}}\big]

6
Conclusion and discussion

We have discussed the weak convergence orders of modified Euler and Milstein-Talay schemes. When
solutions and numerical solutions have finite moments, it is crucial to find the highest moments of numerical solutions to determine convergence orders.
We show how the modification maps in the modified schemes determine the highest order of the moments. We investigate the effects of limited moments on weak convergence orders theoretically and numerically.
However, the theoretical orders of weak convergence seem to be lower than the empirically observed orders. Specifically,
the modified Euler schemes seem to be of first order even with limited moments while the modified Milstein-Talay schemes can degenerate in convergence orders - it may be of first order or less.
Further exploration in this direction is needed to understand the mismatch.

To carry out the weak error analysis,

Lemma C.1

Under the same condition of Theorem
2.5
,

fulfills

\sup_{t\in[0,T-h]}\big|\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,X(t,x;t+h))\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,Y(t,x;t+h))\big]\big|\leq C(1+|x|^{\beta\kappa+\varkappa})h^{q+1},

where

,

come from assumption

and
(iii)
in Theorem

Proof.

We recall that

and Lemma

together with chain rule we could show that

(C.2)

By the Taylor expansion, we get

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,X(t,x;t+h))\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[u(t+h,Y(t,x;t+h))\big]

(C.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{2q+1}_{k=1}\sum^{d}_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}=1}\frac{1}{k!}\frac{\partial^{k}u(t+h,x)}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial x_{i_{k}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod^{k}_{j=1}(\delta_{X,x})^{i_{j}}-\prod^{k}_{j=1}(\delta_{Y,x})^{i_{j}}\Big]+\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{R}_{2q+2}],

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{2q+2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=2p+2}\frac{|\alpha|}{\alpha!}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)^{|\alpha|-1}D^{\alpha}u\big(t+h,x+s\delta_{X,x}\big)\text{d}s\,(\delta_{X,x})^{\alpha}

(C.4)

Using the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|=2q+2}C\int_{0}^{1}\|D^{\alpha}u\big(t+h,(1-s)x+sX(t,x;t+h)\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\text{d}s\|(\delta_{X,x})^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

(C.5)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{|\alpha|=2q+2}C\int_{0}^{1}\|D^{\alpha}u\big(t+h,(1-s)x+sY(t,x;t+h)\big)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}\text{d}s\|(\delta_{Y,x})^{\alpha}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle C(1+|x|^{\beta\kappa+\varkappa})h^{p+1},

The proof of the lemma is thus complete.

Proof of Theorem
2.5
.

The proof follows the main ideas from

,

,

]
. Here, we show the key steps of the proof. Fix

and define

. Then, we find that

\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(X(t_{0},X_{0};T)\big)\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\varphi\big(Y(t_{0},Y_{0};t_{N})\big)\big]=\mathbb{E}[u(t_{0},X_{0})]-\mathbb{E}[u(t_{N},Y_{N})],

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\big[u(t_{i+1},X(t_{i},Y_{i};t_{i+1}))\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[u(t_{i+1},Y(t_{i},Y_{i};t_{i+1}))\big]\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big(u(t_{i+1},X(t_{i},Y_{i};t_{i+1}))-u(t_{i+1},Y(t_{i},Y_{i};t_{i+1}))|\sigma(Y_{i})\big)\Big]\Big|.

Strong congruence spaces and dimension in 𝔽 1 {\mathbb{F}}_{1} -geometry
Manoel Jarra

Abstract
. We introduce strong congruence spaces, which are topological spaces that provide a useful concept of dimension for monoid schemes. We study their properties and show that, given a toric monoid scheme over an algebraically closed basis, its strong congruence space and the complex toric variety associated to its fan have the same dimension.



Introduction

Dimension theory for
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}
-geometry has not yet been developed in a satisfactory way. The main problem is that the underlying topological space of a monoid scheme can have too few points, so it fails to reflect the expected dimension. For example, if
{\mathbb{F}}
is a pointed group, the underlying topological space of the
n
-torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{{\mathbb{F}}}
is a singleton, so its Krull dimension (in the sense of
[
Sta23
, Def. 5.10.1]
) is zero, while the dimension of the complex variety
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
is
n
. We address this issue by attaching a new topological space to a monoid scheme
X
over a basis
k
whose Krull dimension behaves as expected if
k
is an algebraically closed pointed group.

Throughout this text all monoids are assumed to be commutative. A
congruence
on a pointed monoid

is an equivalence relation
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq A\times A
satisfying
(\mylambda a,\mylambda b)\in\mathfrak{c}
whenever
(a,b)\in\mathfrak{c}
and
\mylambda\in A
, which implies that the projection
A\twoheadrightarrow A/\mathfrak{c}
induces a natural structure of pointed monoid on the quotient. We say that a congruence
\mathfrak{c}
is
prime
if
A/\mathfrak{c}
is integral. We refer the reader to
Section 1
for basic facts. Spaces of prime congruences were considered in
[

]
for monoids, in
[
Dei13
]
for

and more recently in
[

]
for monoid schemes. The set of prime congruences of a pointed monoid

endowed with the topology generated by the open sets

is the

of

, denoted by

. For a monoid scheme

, the

of

is constructed by gluing the affine pieces

, such that the result

is independent of the choice of affine covering.

We introduce the term

for a pointed monoid that can be embedded as a multiplicative submonoid into a field.

gives an intrinsic characterization of domains, without mention to fields. A prime congruence

on a pointed monoid

is a domain. If

An extension of pointed groups

Definition 2.1

Let
A

such that

The

The quotient set

is a pointed monoid with product

, absorbing element
[0]
and one
[1]

for

4.3.
The topology of

We prove that if

-scheme locally of finite type, then

is a sober space,

, every irreducible closed subset of

has a unique generic point.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let

be a domain and

Proof.

If

Suppose that

Let

. By

,

.

Let

be an irreducible closed subset. Then

is a closed subset of

. As

is a Noetherian ring, there exists

. For each

5.1.

We introduce algebraically closed pointed groups and gather some results about them (cf.

Definition 5.1
.

An

of pointed groups is an injective morphism
f:{\mathbb{G}}_{1}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
. In this case we identify
{\mathbb{G}}_{1}
with its image via

. The extension is

if it is an isomorphism. An element

. We say that

is

if every

is algebraic over

.

Definition 5.2
.

A pointed group
{\mathbb{G}}
is

if for every
\myalpha
in
{\mathbb{G}}^{\times}
and

one has
\#\{x\in{\mathbb{G}}|\,x^{n}=\myalpha\}
equal to
n
.

Remark 5.3
.

By
Section 4.2
, every algebraically closed pointed group is a domain.

\myvarphi:{\mathbb{G}}_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
. Let
\mylambda\in{\mathbb{G}}_{2}
. As
\mylambda
is algebraic over
{\mathbb{G}}
, there exists
m:=\textup{min}\{n\geq 1\mid\mylambda^{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}\}
. Let
L_{i}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{G}}_{i}\mid x^{m}=\mylambda^{m}\}
, for

. As
\myvarphi(L_{1})\subseteq L_{2}
and

, one has
\myvarphi(L_{1})=L_{2}
. As
\mylambda\in L_{2}
, we conclude that
\mylambda\in\textup{im}\,\myvarphi
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
{\mathbb{G}}
be a pointed group that is also a domain. Then
\ {\mathbb{G}}
is algebraically closed if, and only if, there is no non-trivial algebraic extension
{\mathbb{G}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime}
with
{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime}
a pointed group that is also a domain.

Proof.

Assume that
{\mathbb{G}}
is algebraically closed. Let
{\mathbb{G}}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime}
be an algebraic extension of pointed groups with
{\mathbb{G}}^{\prime}
a domain. If
\mylambda\in({\mathbb{G}}^{\prime})^{\times}
, then there exists
n\geq 1
such that
\myalpha:=\mylambda^{n}\in{\mathbb{G}}
. Note that

has
n
elements. As

is contained in

, which has at most

. Thus

, the extension

The opposite direction follows from

.
∎

5.2.
Main theorem

We prove that the strong congruence space of a toric monoid scheme over an algebraically closed pointed group reflects its expected dimension.

We use the following notations from
[
Bra01
]
and
[
Eur15
]
for toric geometry. For a torsion-free abelian group
L
of finite rank, we use
L_{\mathbb{R}}
to denote the vector space
L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
. Throughout this subsection, fix
M
and
N
dual torsion-free abelian groups of finite rank
n
with perfect pairing
\langle\;,\;\rangle:M\times N\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
, which extends to an
{\mathbb{R}}
-linear pairing
\langle\;,\;\rangle:M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. A
rational polyhedral cone
in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
is a set of the form

\mysigma=\text{Cone}(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{\ell}):=\{\mylambda_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+\mylambda_{\ell}z_{\ell}\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\mylambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0},\text{ for all }i\}

for some
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{\ell}\in N
, where we identify
N
with its image in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
via
x\mapstochar\rightarrow x\otimes 1
. The
dimension
of
\mysigma
is the dimension of the linear subspace generated by
\mysigma
. The
dual cone
of
\mysigma
is the set

\mysigma^{\vee}:=\{x\in M_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle x,y\rangle\geq 0,\text{ for all }y\in\mysigma\}.

It is a fact that
\mysigma^{\vee}
is a rational polyhedral cone in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
(\mysigma^{\vee})^{\vee}=\mysigma
. A
face
of
\mysigma
is a set of the form

\mybeta=\mysigma\cap H_{m},\quad\text{where}\quad H_{m}:=\{u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\},

for some
m\in\mysigma^{\vee}
. Any face of
\mysigma
is a rational polyhedral cone. We say that
\mysigma
is
strongly convex
if
\dim\mysigma^{\vee}=\dim M_{\mathbb{R}}=n
. Equivalently,
\mysigma
is strongly convex if it does not contain any non-zero linear subspace.

A
fan
is a finite collection
\Sigma
of rational polyhedral cones in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying:

(i)

Every
\mysigma\in\Sigma
is strongly convex;

(ii)

Every face of a cone in
\Sigma
is also in
\Sigma
;

(iii)

If
\mysigma_{1},\mysigma_{2}\in\Sigma
, then
\mybeta:=\mysigma_{1}\cap\mysigma_{2}
is a face of both
\mysigma_{1}
and
\mysigma_{2}
.

We define the
dimension
of
\Sigma
as
\dim\Sigma:=\dim N_{\mathbb{R}}=n
.

Given a rational polyhedral cone
\mysigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the affine semigroups

S_{\mysigma}:=\mysigma^{\vee}\cap M\quad\text{and}\quad S_{\mysigma}^{\vee}:=\mysigma\cap N,

which are finitely generated monoids. Note that
S_{(\mysigma^{\vee})}=S_{\mysigma}^{\vee}
.

Remark 5.8
.

The notations
\dim\Sigma
and
S_{\mysigma}^{\vee}
are introduced in the present text.

Given a fan
\Sigma
, let
\mathcal{U}
be the category whose objects are the elements of
\Sigma
and the morphisms are the inclusions of faces. Given two cones
\mytau\subseteq\mysigma
in
\Sigma
, the induced map

\MSpec{\mathbb{F}}_{1}[S_{\mytau}]\rightarrow\MSpec{\mathbb{F}}_{1}[S_{\mysigma}]

is an open embedding. Define
X(\Sigma)
as the monoid scheme

\underset{\mysigma\in\mathcal{U}}{\textup{colim}}\MSpec{\mathbb{F}}_{1}[S_{\mysigma}].

Let
A
be a pointed monoid. The monoid scheme

X(\Sigma)\times_{{\mathbb{F}}_{1}}\MSpec A

has an open covering given by
\{\MSpec A[S_{\mysigma}]\}_{\mysigma\in\Sigma}
(
cf.

[
Dei05
, Prop 3.1]
).

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a pointed group that is also a domain,
\mysigma
a rational polyhedral cone and
\mytau
a face of
\mysigma^{\vee}
. Note that the set
\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}:=({\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]\backslash{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau}^{\vee}])\cup\{0\}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-prime of
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
. The next result shows that every prime in
\MSpec_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
has this form.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a pointed group that is also a domain and
\mysigma
a rational polyhedral cone. Then the map
\mytau\mapstochar\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}
is a bijection between the set of faces of
\mysigma^{\vee}
and
\MSpec_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
that reverses inclusions.

Proof.

It is enough to show that the map
\{\textup{faces of }\mysigma^{\vee}\}\rightarrow\MSpec_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
above is surjective.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\MSpec_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
. We use
S_{\mysigma}
to denote the semigroup
{\mathbb{F}}_{1}[S_{\mysigma}]\backslash\{0\}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
and define
\mathfrak{q}:=\mathfrak{p}\cap S_{\mysigma}
. As
S_{\mysigma}\backslash\mathfrak{q}
is a multiplicative subset, by
[
Ogu18
, Prop. 2.2.1]
, there exists a morphism of semigroups
h:S_{\mysigma}\rightarrow{\mathbb{N}}
such that
h^{-1}(0)=S_{\mysigma}\backslash\mathfrak{q}
. We have a unique extension of
h
to a morphism of groups
\tilde{h}:M\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
and to a map of
{\mathbb{R}}
-algebras
f:M_{\mathbb{R}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. One knows that there exists a unique
u\in N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
f(m)=\langle m,u\rangle
for all
m\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Note that
u\in\mysigma
and define
\mytau:=\{m\in\mysigma^{\vee}\mid\langle m,u\rangle=0\}
, which is a face of
\mysigma^{\vee}
. Next we show that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}
.

If
\mylambda.\mychi^{m}\in\mathfrak{p}\backslash\{0\}
, one has
m\in\mathfrak{q}
, which implies
h(m)\neq 0
and, consequently,
\mylambda.\mychi^{m}
not in
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau}^{\vee}]
. Thus
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}
. If
\myalpha.\mychi^{s}\in\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}\backslash\{0\}
, then
s\notin S_{\mytau}^{\vee}=S_{\mysigma}\cap\mytau
, which implies
h(s)\neq 0
and, consequently,
s\in\mathfrak{q}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\mysigma
be a rational polyhedral cone and
\mytau\subseteq\myalpha
faces of
\mysigma^{\vee}
such that
\dim\,\mytau=\dim\,\myalpha
. Then
\mytau=\myalpha
.

Proof.

Let
t\in(\mysigma^{\vee})^{\vee}
such that
\mytau=\mysigma^{\vee}\cap H_{t}
. As
\dim\,\mytau=\dim\,\myalpha
, one has
\textup{span}\,\mytau=\textup{span}\,\myalpha
. As
H_{t}
is a linear subspace, one has
\myalpha\subseteq H_{t}
if, and only if,
\textup{span}\,\myalpha\subseteq H_{t}
. Note that
\mytau\subseteq\myalpha\subseteq\textup{span}\,\myalpha\cap\mysigma^{\vee}=\textup{span}\,\mytau\cap\mysigma^{\vee}\subseteq H_{t}\cap\mysigma^{\vee}=\mytau
. Thus
\mytau=\myalpha
.
∎

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\mysigma
be a rational polyhedral cone and
\mytau\subseteq\myalpha
faces of
\mysigma^{\vee}
. Let
\ell
be an integer such that
\dim\,\mytau\leq\ell\leq\dim\,\myalpha
. Then there exists a face
\mydelta
of
\mysigma^{\vee}
of dimension
\ell
with
\mytau\subseteq\mydelta\subseteq\myalpha
.

Proof.

If
\mybeta
is a face of
\myalpha
and
\mydelta
is a face of
\mybeta
, then
\mydelta
is a face of
\myalpha
. Thus it is enough to prove the result for

, which is done in
[
Ful93
, p. 10]
.
∎

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
G
be a torsion-free abelian group of finite rank and
H_{1},H_{2}
subgroups such that
H_{1}\cap H_{2}=\{0\}
and
G/H_{i}
is torsion-free for
i=1,2
. Then
G/(H_{1}\oplus H_{2})
is torsion free.

Proof.

Let
\mypi_{i}:G\rightarrow G/H_{i}
be the natural projection for
i=1,2
. As
G/H_{1}
is torsion-free, it is also free, thus there exists a morphism
\mytheta:G/H_{1}\rightarrow G
such that
\mypi_{1}\circ\mytheta=\textup{id}_{G/H_{1}}
. For each
g\in G
there exists
h_{g}\in H_{1}
satisfying
\mytheta(g+H_{1})=g+h_{g}
, thus the kernel of
\mypi_{2}\circ\mytheta
is
(H_{1}\oplus H_{2})/H_{1}
.

As
G/H_{2}
is torsion-free and one has the injection
(G/H_{1})/\ker(\mypi_{2}\circ\mytheta)\hookrightarrow G/H_{2}
and the isomorphism

(G/H_{1})/\big((H_{1}\oplus H_{2})/H_{1}\big)\simeq G/(H_{1}\oplus H_{2}),

the result follows.
∎

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraically closed pointed group and
\mysigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
a strongly convex rational polyhedral cone. Then
\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
is a catenary space of Krull dimension
n
(the rank of
N
).

Proof.

Firstly, we define two functions
\mathfrak{N}
and
\mathfrak{T}
from
\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
to
{\mathbb{N}}
whose sum is analogous to the height of a prime ideal in a ring. Let
\mathfrak{c}
be an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence on
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
. By
Section 5.2
, there exists a unique face
\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}
of
\mysigma^{\vee}
such that
\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}}=I_{\mathfrak{c}}
. Define
\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{c})
as
n-\dim\,\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{c}}
be the strong
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence on
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}]\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]/I_{\mathfrak{c}}
induced by
\mathfrak{c}
. Let
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}
be the linear subspace of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
generated by
\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}
. As
I_{\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{c}}}=\{0\}
and
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}]=\textup{Frac}({\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}])
, one has
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{c}}=f^{*}(\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}})
, where
f:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}]
is the inclusion and
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}:=f_{*}(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathfrak{c}})\in\SCong{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}]
. Let
\equiv
be the equivalence relation on
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}
given by
a\equiv b
if
\mychi^{a}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\mylambda\mychi^{b}
for some
\mylambda\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
and define the subgroup
H_{\mathfrak{c}}:=\{a\in S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}\mid a\equiv 0\}
of
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}
. Note that

\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}=\langle\mychi^{a}\sim\mylambda\mid a\in H_{\mathfrak{c}}\text{ and }\mychi^{a}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\mylambda\rangle.

Define
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{c})
as
\textup{rk}(H_{\mathfrak{c}})
and note that
\mathfrak{t}(\mathfrak{c})\leq\textup{rk}(S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee})=\textup{dim}\,\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Next we show that
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}/H_{\mathfrak{c}}
is torsion-free. Let
y\in S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee}
and
r>0
such that
ry\in H_{\mathfrak{c}}
. Then there exists
\mylambda\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
(\mychi^{y})^{r}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\mylambda
. As
{\mathbb{F}}
is algebraically closed and
\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}
is strong, there exists
\myzeta\in{\mathbb{F}}
such that
\myzeta^{r}=\mylambda
and
\mychi^{y}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{c}}}\myzeta
, thus
y\in H_{\mathfrak{c}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{c}_{2}
be
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruences on
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
such that
\mathfrak{c}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{c}_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a maximal chain in
\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
with minimum
\mathfrak{c}_{1}
and maximum
\mathfrak{c}_{2}
. We claim that
\mathcal{C}
is finite. Note that
\mathcal{C}_{I}:=\{I_{\mathfrak{c}}\mid\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}\}
is a chain in
\MSpec_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
, thus
\#\mathcal{C}_{I}\leq n
, and given
J\in\mathcal{C}_{I}
, one has that
\mathcal{H}_{J}:=\{H_{\mathfrak{c}}\mid\mathfrak{c}\in\mathcal{C}\text{ with }I_{\mathfrak{c}}=J\}
is a chain whose length is bounded by
\textup{dim}\,\mytau
, where
\mytau
is the unique face of
\mysigma^{\vee}
satisfying
\mathfrak{p}_{\mytau}=J
. Next we show that the length of
\mathcal{C}
is
\big(\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{c}_{2})+\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{c}_{2})\big)-\big(\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{c}_{1})+\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{c}_{1})\big)
.

It is enough to show that if
\mathfrak{r}\subsetneq\mathfrak{r}^{\prime}
are two consecutive congruences in
\mathcal{C}
, then

\big(\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})-\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})\big)+\big(\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})-\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{r})\big)=1.

Let
\mypi:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]/I_{\mathfrak{r}}
be the projection. If
\mathfrak{c}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
satisfies
I_{\mathfrak{r}}\subseteq I_{\mathfrak{c}}
, then
\mypi_{*}(\mathfrak{c})\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]
,
\mathfrak{N}(\mypi_{*}(\mathfrak{c}))=\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{c})-\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{r})
and
\mathfrak{T}(\mypi_{*}(\mathfrak{c}))=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{c})
, thus we can assume
I_{\mathfrak{r}}=\{0\}
. Therefore
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}=M_{\mathbb{R}}
and we have two cases to analyze:

Case 1:

I_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}=\{0\}
. Then
H_{\mathfrak{r}}\subseteq H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
, which implies

\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})\leq\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
. One has the injection
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}\hookrightarrow S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}
, which shows that
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}
is torsion-free. If
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
, then
H_{\mathfrak{r}}=H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
and we conclude that
\mathfrak{r}=\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}
. Thus
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})<\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
.

If
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})\leq\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})-2
, let
s:=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
,
\ell:=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})
and
\{b_{1},\dotsc,b_{s}\}
a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
such that
\{b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
H_{\mathfrak{r}}
. Then, for each
1\leq i\leq s
, there exists a unique
\mylambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
b_{i}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}\mylambda_{i}
. As
\mathfrak{r}\subseteq\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}
, one has
b_{j}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r}}}\mylambda_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Let
\mathfrak{a}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]
be the congruence
\langle b_{i}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell+1\rangle
and define
\mathfrak{c}:=g^{*}(\mathfrak{a})
, where
g:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]
is the inclusion. Thus
\mathfrak{r}\subsetneq\mathfrak{c}\subsetneq\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r})=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})-1
.

Case 2:

I_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\neq\{0\}
. Suppose that
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\not\subset H_{\mathfrak{r}}
, which is equivalent to
H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\subsetneq H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
. One has that
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
is torsion free, because
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}\subsetneq S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}
and
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\hookrightarrow S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/H_{\mathfrak{r}}
. Let
\ell:=\textup{rk}(H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}})
and
s:=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
. Then there exists a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{b_{1},\dotsc,b_{s}\}
of
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
such that
\{b_{1},\dotsc,b_{\ell}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
. For each
1\leq i\leq s
, let
\mylambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
b_{i}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}\mylambda_{i}
. Let
\mathfrak{b}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]
be the congruence
\langle b_{i}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq\ell\rangle
and define
\mathfrak{c}:=p^{*}(\mathfrak{b})
, where
p
is the composition

{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]/I_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}].

Note that
I_{\mathfrak{c}}=I_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
and
H_{\mathfrak{c}}=H_{\mathfrak{r}}\cap H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
, thus
\mathfrak{r}\subsetneq\mathfrak{c}\subsetneq\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}\subseteq H_{\mathfrak{r}}
.

Let
s:=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
,
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\}
a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}
such that
\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{s}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
G:=H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}
and
\mylambda_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
x_{i}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}\mylambda_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq s
. The map of groups

\begin{array}[]{ccl}S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow&({\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/G])^{\times}\\
&&\\

{}\mychi^{\overline{x_{i}}}&\text{if }s+1\leq i\leq n\end{array}\right.}\end{array}

induces a surjective morphism of
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebras
\myvarphi:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/G]
with congruence kernel
\myiota_{*}(\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}})\subseteq\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{r}}
, where
\myiota:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}]
is the inclusion. As
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}
and
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}/G
are free, one has that
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/G
is free, which implies that
\overline{S_{\mysigma}}:=\big\{\overline{x}\in S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/G\;\big|\;x\in S_{\mysigma}\big\}

\myvarphi({\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}])={\mathbb{F}}[\overline{S_{\mysigma}}]
and define

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\mypsi:&{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}[\overline{S_{\mysigma}}]\\
&a&\longmapsto&\myvarphi(a),\end{array}

\mathfrak{s}:=\mypsi_{*}(\mathfrak{r})
and
\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}:=\mypsi_{*}(\mathfrak{r^{\prime}})
. Let
\widetilde{M}
be the group
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{r}}}^{\vee}/G
and
\widetilde{N}:=\textup{Hom}(\widetilde{M},{\mathbb{Z}})
its dual. Let

S_{\mysigma}
as a monoid and
\myrho:=\textup{Cone}(\overline{y_{1}},\dotsc,\overline{y_{w}})^{\vee}\subseteq\widetilde{N}_{\mathbb{R}}
. Note that
\overline{S_{\mysigma}}=S_{\myrho}
and
\textup{congker}(\mypsi)\subseteq\mathfrak{r}
, which implies
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]
. As
H_{\mathfrak{s}}=H_{\mathfrak{r}}/G
and
H_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}=H_{\mathfrak{r^{\prime}}}/G=\{0\}
, it is enough to show that

\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})-\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})=1.

Define
m:=\textup{rk}(\widetilde{M})
,
d:=\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})
and
e:=m-\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})
. Let
\{u_{1},\dotsc,u_{m}\}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\widetilde{M}
such that
\{u_{1},\dotsc,u_{d}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
H_{\mathfrak{s}}
, and
\mybeta_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
with
\mychi^{u_{i}}\sim_{\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{s}}}\mybeta_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
. Analogous to what is done above, there exists a surjective morphism of
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebras

\begin{array}[]{cccl}\widetilde{\myvarphi}:&{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}]&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}]\\
&&\\
&\mychi^{u_{i}}&\longmapsto&\smash{\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mybeta_{i}&\text{if }1\leq i\leq d\\
{}\mychi^{\overline{u_{i}}}&\text{if }d+1\leq i\leq m\end{array}\right.}\end{array}

noindent such that
\congker(\widetilde{\myvarphi})=\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{s}}
and
\widetilde{\myvarphi}({\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}])={\mathbb{F}}[\overline{S_{\myrho}}]
, where
\overline{S_{\myrho}}
is the affine semigroup
\{\overline{y}\in\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}\mid y\in S_{\myrho}\}
. As the composition

{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]/\mathfrak{s}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]/\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}

is an isomorphism, one has the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mytau_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]/\mathfrak{s}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}]/\mathfrak{a}_{\mathfrak{s}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}],}

which shows that
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}\cap H_{\mathfrak{s}}=\{0\}
. By
Section 5.2
, there exists a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{c_{1},\dotsc,c_{m}\}
of
\widetilde{M}
such that
c_{i}=u_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq d
and
\{c_{d+1},\dotsc,c_{d+e}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}
. Therefore
d+e\leq m
,
i.e.
,
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})\leq\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})
. Next we show that
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})=\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})-1
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})=\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})
. Then the map
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}]
is surjective. Let
Q
be the submonoid of
\widetilde{M}
generated by
S_{\myrho}
and
S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}
. As
{\mathbb{F}}[Q]
is a localization of
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]
by elements not in
I_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}
or
I_{\mathfrak{s}}=\{0\}
, one has that
q_{*}(\mathfrak{s})
and
q_{*}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})
are
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruences on
{\mathbb{F}}[Q]
, where
q:{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[Q]
is the inclusion map. Note that
\textup{congker}(\widetilde{\myvarphi}|_{{\mathbb{F}}[Q]})=q_{*}(\mathfrak{s})
. As the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[Q]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[Q]/q_{*}(\mathfrak{s})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[Q]/q_{*}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}}}^{\vee}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{F}}[Q]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\myvarphi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\myvarphi}}

commutes, one has
q_{*}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})=\textup{congker}(\widetilde{\myvarphi}|_{{\mathbb{F}}[Q]})=q_{*}(\mathfrak{s})
, which is a contradiction.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{s})\leq\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{s^{\prime}})-2
. Let
\widetilde{\mypsi}:=\widetilde{\myvarphi}|_{{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]}
and
\mathfrak{u}:=\langle\overline{c_{m}}\sim 1\rangle
on
{\mathbb{F}}[\widetilde{M}/H_{\mathfrak{s}}]
. As
\mathfrak{u}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence, one has that
\mathfrak{v}:=\widetilde{\mypsi}^{*}(\mathfrak{u})
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence on
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\myrho}]
satisfying
\mathfrak{s}\subsetneq\mathfrak{v}\subsetneq\mathfrak{s^{\prime}}
, which is a contradiction.

The topological dimension of
\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
is
n
because for any maximal congruence
\mathfrak{c}
in
\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]
, one has
{\mathbb{F}}[S_{\mysigma}]/\mathfrak{c}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}
and

\mathfrak{T}(\mathfrak{c})=\textup{rk}(H_{\mathfrak{c}})=\textup{rk}(S_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{c}}}^{\vee})=\dim\mytau_{\mathfrak{c}}=n-\mathfrak{N}(\mathfrak{c}).

∎

Definition 5.14
.

Let
k
be a pointed monoid and
X
a
k
-scheme. We define the

-dimension
of

as the Krull dimension of the topological space

, and denote it by

.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be an algebraically closed pointed group,
\Sigma

the toric

Proof.

By

6.
Examples: The torus and affine space

Throughout this section, we fix an algebraically closed pointed group
{\mathbb{F}}
and give an explicit description of the strong congruence space of the torus and affine space over
{\mathbb{F}}
.

Notation
.

For
y=(y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, we use
X^{y}
to denote the monomial
X_{1}^{y_{1}}\cdots X_{n}^{y_{n}}
.

6.1.
The torus

The
n
-torus over
{\mathbb{F}}
is the affine monoid scheme

{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}:=\MSpec{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}].

Let
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{c}\}
be part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
\mylambda_{1},\dotsc,\mylambda_{c}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
. Define the congruence

\mathfrak{c}:=\langle X^{z_{i}}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle

on
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]
. Let
z_{c+1},\dotsc,z_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{n}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis. The map

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\myvarphi:&{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow&{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]\\
&X_{i}&\longmapsto&X^{z_{i}}\end{array}

defines an isomorphism of
{\mathbb{F}}
-algebras. As

\myvarphi^{*}(\mathfrak{c})=\langle X_{i}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle,

one has
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{c}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]/\myvarphi^{*}(\mathfrak{c})\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[X_{c+1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]
. Therefore
\mathfrak{c}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence.

The next result shows that every
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence on
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]
has this form.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Then there exists an integer
0\leq c\leq n
, constants
\mylambda_{1},\dotsc,\mylambda_{c}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
and
z_{1},\dotsc,z_{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis such that

\mathfrak{c}=\langle X^{z_{i}}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle.

Proof.

As
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]
is a pointed group, one has
I_{\mathfrak{c}}=\{0\}
. Let

H:=\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}\mid X^{z}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\mylambda\text{ for some }\mylambda\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}\}.

Note that
H
is a subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. We show that
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}/H
is torsion-free. Let
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
and
m>0
such that
mz\in H
. Then there exists
\mylambda\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
(X^{z})^{m}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\mylambda
. Let
\myalpha\in{\mathbb{F}}
such that
\myalpha^{m}=\mylambda
and
\myzeta\in{\mathbb{F}}
be a primitive
m^{\textup{th}}
-root of
1
,
i.e.
, a generator of the group
\mymu_{m}:=\{w\in{\mathbb{F}}\mid w^{m}=1\}
. As
\mathfrak{c}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence, the sets

\big\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}]/\mathfrak{c}\;\big|\;x^{m}=\mylambda\big\}\quad\text{and}\quad\big\{[\myzeta^{i}\myalpha]\;\big|\;i=0,\dotsc,m-1\big\}

coincide. As
[X^{z}]^{m}=\mylambda
, there exists
0\leq j\leq m-1
such that
X^{z}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\myzeta^{j}\myalpha
. Thus
z\in H
. Then there exists a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{c}\}
of
H
that can be completed to a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
.

As
\mathfrak{c}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence and
{\mathbb{F}}
is a pointed group, there exist unique
\mylambda_{1},\dotsc,\mylambda_{c}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
such that
X^{z_{i}}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\mylambda_{i}
for
i=1,\dotsc,c
. Next we show that
\mathfrak{c}
is equal to

\mathfrak{d}:=\langle X^{z_{i}}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle.

Let
g,h\in({\mathbb{F}}[X_{1}^{\pm},\dotsc,X_{n}^{\pm}])^{\times}
such that
g\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}h
. Then
gh^{-1}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}1
. Let
\myomega\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
and
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
such that
gh^{-1}=\myomega X^{z}
. As
X^{z}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\myomega^{-1}
, one has
X^{z}\in H
. Thus there exist
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{c}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
z=\sum m_{i}z_{i}
, which implies
\myomega^{-1}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}X^{z}\sim_{\mathfrak{c}}\prod\mylambda_{i}^{m_{i}}
. As
\mathfrak{c}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence, one has
\myomega^{-1}=\prod\mylambda_{i}^{m_{i}}
. Note that
X^{z}\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}\prod\mylambda_{i}^{m_{i}}
, which implies

g\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}gh^{-1}h\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}\myomega X^{z}h\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}\myomega\myomega^{-1}h\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}h.

Therefore
g\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}h
.
∎

6.2.
Affine space

The
affine
n
-space over
{\mathbb{F}}
is the affine monoid scheme

{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}:=\MSpec{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}].

We show the analogue of
Section 6.1
in the case of the affine space. For a subset
L\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, let
\mathfrak{p}_{L}
be the prime ideal
\langle X_{i}\mid i\in L\rangle\in{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Note that

{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]/\mathfrak{p}_{L}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L].

Thus, for an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence
\mathfrak{d}
on
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}^{\pm}\mid i\notin L]=\textup{Frac}({\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L])
, one has that
\myvarphi^{*}(\mathfrak{d})
is an
{\mathbb{F}}
-congruence on
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]
, where
\myvarphi
is the composition

{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]/\mathfrak{p}_{L}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L]\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}^{\pm}\mid i\notin L].

By
Section 6.1
,
\mathfrak{d}=\langle X^{z_{i}}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle
, where
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{c}\}\subset{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-\#L}
is part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis, which implies that

\myvarphi^{*}(\mathfrak{d})=\langle\{X_{i}\sim 0\mid i\in L\}\cup\{a\sim b\mid a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L]\text{ with }a\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}b\}\rangle.

The next result shows that every
\mathfrak{c}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
has this form.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
. Then there exists a subset
L\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, an integer
0\leq c\leq n-\#L
, constants
\mylambda_{1},\dotsc,\mylambda_{c}\in{\mathbb{F}}^{\times}
and
\{z_{1},\dotsc,z_{c}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{Z}}^{n-\#L}
part of a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis such that

\mathfrak{c}=\langle\{X_{i}\sim 0\mid i\in L\}\cup\{a\sim b\mid a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L]\text{ with }a\sim_{\mathfrak{d}}b\}\rangle,

where
\mathfrak{d}
is the congruence
\langle X^{z_{i}}\sim\mylambda_{i}\mid i=1,\dotsc,c\rangle
on
{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}^{\pm}\mid i\notin L]
.

Proof.

As
I_{\mathfrak{c}}\in{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n}
, there exists
L\subseteq\{1,\dotsc,n\}
such that
I_{\mathfrak{c}}=\mathfrak{p}_{L}
. Let

\mypi:{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n}]/\mathfrak{p}_{L}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L]

be the projection,

\myiota:{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}\mid i\notin L]\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow{\mathbb{F}}[X_{i}^{\pm}\mid i\notin L]

the localization map,
\mathfrak{r}:=\mypi_{*}(\mathfrak{c})
and
\mathfrak{d}:=\myiota_{*}(\mypi_{*}(\mathfrak{c}))
. Note that
\mathfrak{r}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-\#L}
and
\mathfrak{c}=\mypi^{*}(\mathfrak{r})
. As
\mathfrak{r}
satisfies
I_{\mathfrak{r}}=\{0\}
, by
Section 3
, one has
\mathfrak{d}\in\SCong_{\mathbb{F}}{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-\#L}
and
\mathfrak{r}=\myiota^{*}(\mathfrak{d})
. Thus the result follows from the discussion above, because
\mathfrak{c}=(\myiota\circ\mypi)^{*}(\mathfrak{d})
.
∎

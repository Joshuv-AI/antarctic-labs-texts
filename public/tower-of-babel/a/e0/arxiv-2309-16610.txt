ℋ \mathcal{H} -Killing Spinors and Spinorial Duality for Homogeneous 3- ( α , δ ) (\alpha,\delta) -Sasaki Manifolds
Ilka Agricola and Jordan Hofmann

Abstract
. We show that 3 - ​ ( α , δ ) ​ -Sasaki {\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}} manifolds admit solutions of a certain new spinorial field equation (the ℋ \mathcal{H} -Killing equation ) generalizing the well-known Killing spinors on 3 ​ -Sasakian 3\text{-Sasakian} manifolds. These ℋ \mathcal{H} -Killing spinors have more desirable geometric properties than the spinors obtained by simply deforming a 3 ​ -Sasakian 3\text{-Sasakian} metric; in particular we obtain a one-to-one correspondence between ℋ \mathcal{H} -Killing spinors on dual pairs of homogeneous 3 - ​ ( α , δ ) ​ -Sasaki {\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}} spaces. Finally, we show that ℋ \mathcal{H} -Killing spinors generalize certain special spinors in dimension 7 7 previously constructed by Agricola-Friedrich and Agricola-Dileo using G 2 \G_{2} -geometry.

1.
Introduction

One of the most consequential mathematical achievements of the past century is the Berger holonomy theorem
[
Ber55
,
Sim62
]
, which classifies the possible holonomy groups of irreducible non-symmetric Riemannian manifolds. The only odd-dimensional geometry appearing in Berger’s list is
\G_{2}
in dimension
7
, which has led over the subsequent decades to the study of
non-integrable
odd-dimensional geometries via connections better adapted to the geometry: so-called
characteristic connections
(see e.g.
[
Agr06
]
). In dimension
3
(mod
4
) in particular, natural and abundant examples of non-integrable geometries are given by
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifolds, which, roughly speaking, are characterized by the existence of certain tensors facilitating the identification of the tangent spaces with
\text{Im}(\mathbb{H})\oplus\mathbb{H}^{n}
. These are related to two of the even-dimensional geometries from Berger’s list; the cone over a
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold is hyperKähler (in fact one can take this as a definition), and, if the
Reeb vector fields
are complete, the space of leaves of the corresponding
3
-dimensional foliation is a quaternionic Kähler orbifold
[
BGM94
]
.

It is well-known that every
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold is spin (as a consequence of the reduction of structure group of the tangent bundle proved by Kuo in
[
Kuo70
]
), and they are highly relevant objects in spin geometry, carrying many examples of interesting spinor fields (see e.g.
[
Bär93
,
AF10
]
). In fact, Bär showed in
[
Bär93
]
that the existence of a compatible 3-Sasakian structure on a Riemannian manifold is completely determined by the number of linearly independent Riemannian Killing spinors carried by the metric, i.e. the number of linearly independent spinors satisfying the
Killing equation

\nabla^{g}_{X}\psi=\pm\frac{1}{2}X\cdot\psi\qquad\text{ for all }X\in TM.

Specifically, Bär proved that
(M^{4n-1},g)
carries a compatible 3-Sasakian structure if and only if there exist at least
n+1
linearly independent Riemannian Killing spinors for the Killing number
\frac{1}{2}
(or
-\frac{1}{2}
, up to a change of orientation). The existence of Killing spinors puts strong constraints on the geometry of the manifold, implying, for example, that the metric is Einstein and that equality is achieved in Friedrich’s estimate for the first eigenvalue of the Dirac operator (see e.g.
[
Fri00
, Chaps. 5.1, 5.2]
). The first authors to realize that spinors apart from Killing spinors can be deformed and carry important information were Friedrich and Kim, who defined in
[
KF00
]
the class of
Sasakian quasi-Killing spinors
and proved that they induce, in certain dimensions, solutions of the coupled Einstein-Dirac system. Other generalizations of Killing spinors have also been studied, and the existence of such spinors is known to be closely related to
G
-structures in low dimensions (see e.g.
[
Iva04
,
CS07
,
ACFH15
]
).

The aim of the present article is to identify spinors satisfying new types of remarkable field equations on
3
-contact manifolds of every dimension
4n-1\geq 7
, outside of the well-studied
3\text{-Sasakian}
case. The setting in which we do this is that of
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structures, using their remarkable geometric properties such as the existence of a
canonical connection
(a metric connection with totally skew-symmetric torsion well-adapted to the
3
-contact structure). The strategy we employ is a modification of Friedrich and Kath’s method in
[
FK90
]
; we identify a certain spinorial connection which acts trivially on a subbundle of the spinor bundle and then extract a spinorial equation by writing this connection as the sum of the Levi-Civita connection and another term. Furthermore, in the homogeneous setting we show that our spinors are compatible with a certain natural notion of duality, observed in
[
ADS21
]
, between positive and negative
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
homogeneous spaces. The study of homogeneous
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
spaces is heavily rooted in Boyer, Galicki, and Mann’s classification of homogeneous
3\text{-Sasakian}
spaces in
[
BGM94
]
, and we note that a new, self-contained, and elegant Lie theoretic proof of this classification has recently appeared in
[
GRS23
]
.

Our results are as follows:

•

In §
3
we describe how the Killing spinor equation on a
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold changes under so-called
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformations
, which transform the
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure into a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure with
\alpha\delta>0
. We obtain Theorem
3.6
, which asserts that a positive
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold of dimension
4n-1
carries at least
n+1
linearly independent solutions of the
deformed Killing equation
(
17
).

•

The so-called
deformed Killing spinors
do not naturally extend to negative
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structures (
\alpha\delta<0
), so in §
4
we prove the existence of spinors satisfying a different generalization of the Killing equation which is valid for any
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold (Theorem
4.1
). We call these
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors
to emphasize that they behave as Killing spinors along the horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
. Along the vertical distribution they satisfy the Killing equation plus a correction term which vanishes in the
3
-
\alpha
-Sasaki case (
\alpha=\delta
).

•

Section
5
is a bit different in flavour, as it concentrates on the homogeneous case, where more structure and a full classification (for
\alpha\delta>0
) are available
[
ADS21
]
. In this section we demonstrate that the
\mathcal{H}
-Killing equation is a natural one to consider by proving that
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors on a compact homogeneous
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
space and its non-compact dual correspond in a one-to-one manner.

•

Finally, in §
6
we use certain algebraic identities in dimension
7
to prove in Theorem
6.4
that the
\mathcal{H}
-Killing equation in this dimension is equivalent to the generalized Killing equation satisfied by the
auxiliary spinors
introduced in
[
AF10
,
AD20
]
. The auxiliary spinors are defined using the natural
\G_{2}
-structure induced by a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure in dimension
7
(see
[
AD20
, Thm. 4.5.1]
), and therefore until now had no obvious generalization to higher dimensions.

2.1.
3-(
\alpha,\delta
)-Sasaki Structures

Here we recall the basic definitions related to
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifolds, following the notation and conventions of
[
AD20
]
. For systematic examination of these structures we refer the reader to
[
AD20
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

(Almost 3-contact, almost 3-contact metric, 3-Sasakian). An almost 3-contact manifold
(M^{4n-1},\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})_{i=1}^{3}
consists of a manifold of dimension
4n-1
together with three almost contact structures satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\xi_{j})

\displaystyle=-\varphi_{j}(\xi_{i})=\xi_{k},\qquad\eta_{i}=\eta_{j}\circ\varphi_{k}=-\eta_{k}\circ\varphi_{j},

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{j}\circ\varphi_{k}-\eta_{k}\otimes\xi_{j}=-\varphi_{k}\circ\varphi_{j}+\eta_{j}\otimes\xi_{k}

for all even permutations
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
. The structure tensors induce a splitting
TM=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}
into
horizontal
and
vertical
spaces, defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\cap_{i=1}^{3}\ker(\eta_{i}),\qquad\mathcal{V}:=\text{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3},

and the horizontal restrictions
\varphi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}:=\varphi_{i}\rvert_{\mathcal{H}}
satisfy the defining relations of the quaternions:

\varphi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}\circ\varphi_{j}^{\mathcal{H}}=\varphi_{k}^{\mathcal{H}}

for all even permutations
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
. In particular this defines a quaternionic contact structure. The action of
\varphi_{1}
,
\varphi_{2}
,
\varphi_{3}
in the vertical directions is determined by

(1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{i}(\xi_{j})=\xi_{k}

for all even permutations
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
. Notably, any almost 3-contact manifold admits a single metric which is compatible with all three almost contact structures in the sense that

\displaystyle g(\varphi_{i}(-),\varphi_{i}(-))=g-\eta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}\quad\text{ for all }i=1,2,3,

and we call the data
(M,g,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
an almost 3-contact metric manifold. If, furthermore, each of the three structures is Sasakian, the manifold is called 3-Sasakian.

With the above definitions in mind,
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structures are defined as almost
3
-contact metric structures satisfying a certain integrability condition:

Definition 2.2
.

A 3-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold is an almost 3-contact metric manifold
(M,g,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
satisfying

d\eta_{i}=2\alpha\Phi_{i}+2(\alpha-\delta)\eta_{j}\wedge\eta_{k},\qquad\alpha,\delta\in\mathbb{R},\ \alpha\neq 0

for all even permutations
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
, where
\Phi_{i}:=g(-,\varphi_{i}(-))
is the metric dual (in the second component) of the skew-symmetric endomorphism
\varphi_{i}
.

This notion contains the 3-Sasakian spaces (
\alpha=\delta=1
) as well as many other interesting classes such as Einstein 3-
\alpha
-Sasakian structures (
\alpha=\delta
), parallel structures (
\delta=2\alpha
), degenerate structures (
\delta=0
), and a second Einstein metric (
\delta=(2n+1)\alpha
, where
\dim M:=4n-1
).

2.2.
Connections on
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
Manifolds

In this subsection we review the canonical connection of a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold, and, in the homogeneous setting, the algebraic description of the canonical and Levi-Civita connections in terms of
Nomizu maps
. To begin, we recall from
[
AD20
]
the formulas for the Levi-Civita derivatives of the structure tensors:

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
AD20
, Prop. 2.3.2, Cor. 2.3.1]
).

If
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold then

(2)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\varphi_{i})X

\displaystyle=\alpha[g(X,Y)\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}(X)Y]-2(\alpha-\delta)[\eta_{k}(Y)\varphi_{j}X-\eta_{j}(Y)\varphi_{k}X]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(\alpha-\delta)[\eta_{j}(Y)\eta_{j}(X)+\eta_{k}(Y)\eta_{k}(X)]\xi_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-(\alpha-\delta)\eta_{i}(X)[\eta_{j}(Y)\xi_{j}+\eta_{k}(Y)\xi_{k}],

(3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}^{g}\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=-\alpha\varphi_{i}(Y)-(\alpha-\delta)[\eta_{k}(Y)\xi_{j}-\eta_{j}(Y)\xi_{k}],

for any even permutation
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
.

Indeed, these formulas become rather unwieldy when
\alpha\neq\delta
, and thus the Levi-Civita connection is not well suited to computations in the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
setting. While it is not possible in general to find a nice connection parallelizing all the structure tensors of a 3-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold (see
[
AD20
, Thm. 4.2.1]
), there is nonetheless a good choice available:

Proposition 2.4
.

(Thms. 4.1.1, 4.4.1, and the discussion in Section 4 of
[
AD20
]
).

Let
(M,g,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
be a 3-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki manifold. Then
M
admits a unique metric connection
\nabla
with skew torsion such that, for some function
\beta\in C^{\infty}(M)
,

\nabla_{X}\varphi_{i}=\beta(\eta_{k}(X)\varphi_{j}-\eta_{j}(X)\varphi_{k})\quad\forall X\in\Gamma(TM).

Furthermore,
\beta
is the constant function
\beta\equiv 2(\delta-2\alpha)
and the torsion of
\nabla
is the 3-form

(4)

\displaystyle T=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}\wedge d\eta_{i}+8(\delta-\alpha)\eta_{1,2,3}=2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}\wedge\Phi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}+2(\delta-4\alpha)\eta_{1,2,3},

where
\eta_{1,2,3}:=\eta_{1}\wedge\eta_{2}\wedge\eta_{3}
. The
\nabla
-derivatives of the structure tensors are given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\varphi_{i}

\displaystyle=\beta(\eta_{k}(X)\varphi_{j}-\eta_{j}(X)\varphi_{k}),\quad\nabla_{X}\xi_{i}=\beta(\eta_{k}(X)\xi_{j}-\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{k}),\quad\nabla_{X}\eta_{i}=\beta(\eta_{k}(X)\eta_{j}-\eta_{j}(X)\eta_{k}),

and, in particular, are parallel in the horizontal directions.

As a matter of notation, throughout the article we shall always denote by
T
the torsion of
\nabla
. The connection
\nabla
is called the
canonical connection
of the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure, and we shall make frequent use of it to simplify the curvature calculations in §
4
. We will also sometimes use the shorthand
\eta_{i,j}:=\eta_{i}\wedge\eta_{j}
,
e_{i,j}:=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}
, etc. for differential forms. To translate curvature identities for the canonical connection back in terms of the Levi-Civita connection, we need the following:

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
ADS23
, Section 1.2]
).
The curvature tensors
R,R^{g}
of the canonical and Levi-Civita connections are related by

(5)

\displaystyle R^{g}(X,Y,Z,V)=R(X,Y,Z,V)-\frac{1}{4}g(T(X,Y),T(Z,V))-\frac{1}{8}dT(X,Y,Z,V),

where
T
is the torsion tensor of
\nabla
.

Finally we recall the description of metric connections in the homogeneous setting via Nomizu maps. Indeed, it is well-known (see e.g.
[
Nom54
,
Wan58
,
KN63
]
) that an invariant metric connection
\nabla^{\prime}
(we use
\nabla^{\prime}
rather than
\nabla
in order to distinguish it from the canonical connection defined above) on a Riemannian homogeneous space
(M=G/H,g)
corresponds to an
H
-equivariant linear map
T_{o}M\to\mathfrak{so}(T_{o}M)
(
o:=eH
). Following the modern exposition in
[
ANT23
, Chap. 6]
, this relationship may be expressed as follows:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(M=G/H,g)
be a Riemannian homogeneous space with reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
, and let
(\pi_{*})_{e}\colon\mathfrak{m}\to T_{o}M
(
o:=eH
) denote the isomorphism induced by the (restriction to
\mathfrak{m}
of the) derivative of the projection
\pi\colon G\to M=G/H
at the identity element.

(i)

There is a one-to-one correspondence between
G
-invariant metric connections
\nabla^{\prime}
and linear maps
\Uplambda^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})
satisfying

\Uplambda^{\prime}(h\cdot X)Y=h\cdot(\Uplambda^{\prime}(X)(h^{-1}\cdot Y))\qquad\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{m},\ h\in H,

where
h\cdot V:=\Ad_{H}(h)V
denotes the isotropy action of
h\in H
on
V\in\mathfrak{m}\cong T_{o}M
.

(ii)

The correspondence in (i) is given explicitly by

(\pi_{*})_{e}(\Uplambda^{\prime}(X)Y)=(\nabla^{\prime}_{\widehat{X}}-\mathcal{L}_{\widehat{X}})_{o}(\widehat{Y}_{o})\qquad\text{ for all }X,Y\in\mathfrak{m},

where
\widehat{X},\widehat{Y}
denote the fundamental vector fields induced by
X,Y
.

(iii)

If
\tau
is a
G
-invariant tensor field or differential form on
M
, then

(\nabla^{\prime}_{\widehat{X}}\tau)_{o}=\Uplambda^{\prime}(X)\tau_{o}\qquad\text{ for all }X\in\mathfrak{m},

where
\widehat{X}
denotes the fundamental vector field induced by
X
and the action of
\Uplambda^{\prime}(X)\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})
on
\tau
is via the extension to the tensor or exterior algebra of the representation of
\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})
on
\mathfrak{m}
.

In particular, statement (iii) in the preceding proposition allows one to compute covariant derivatives of invariant tensors and differential forms in a purely algebraic way. To similarly compute the covariant derivatives of invariant spinors (with respect to the spinorial connection
\widetilde{\nabla^{\prime}}
induced by
\nabla^{\prime}
), one uses the lifted Nomizu map
\widetilde{\Uplambda^{\prime}}\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{spin}(\mathfrak{m})
obtained by composing
\Uplambda
with the Lie algebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})\cong\mathfrak{spin}(\mathfrak{m})
. The relationship between
\widetilde{\nabla^{\prime}}
and
\widetilde{\Uplambda^{\prime}}
is then given by a similar formula as in (iii) above, with the action of
\widetilde{\Uplambda^{\prime}}(X)\in\mathfrak{spin}(\mathfrak{m})
on an invariant spinor
\psi_{o}\in(\Sigma_{\inv})_{o}
via the spin representation. It is well-known that the Nomizu map of the Levi-Civita connection is given by a simple formula in terms of the Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}
:

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
ANT23
,
KN69
,
Nom54
]
).
If
(M=G/H,g)
is a Riemannian homogeneous space then the Nomizu map
\Uplambda^{g}
associated to the Levi-Civita connection is given by

\Uplambda^{g}(X)Y=\frac{1}{2}\text{\emph{proj}}_{\mathfrak{m}}[X,Y]+U(X,Y)

for all
X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}
, where
U
is the symmetric
(2,0)
-tensor specified by

(6)

\displaystyle 2g(U(X,Y),Z)=g(\text{\emph{proj}}_{\mathfrak{m}}[Z,X],Y)+g(X,\text{\emph{proj}}_{\mathfrak{m}}[Z,Y]).

We conclude by recalling from
[
ADS21
]
the explicit formulas for the Nomizu maps of the canonical and Levi-Civita connections of a homogeneous
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
space. We give the formulas only in the case of a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
homogeneous space fibering over a Wolf space (i.e. a symmetric quaternionic Kähler space), which are much simpler than the general case and will be sufficient for our purposes.

Proposition 2.8
.

(
[
ADS21
, Props. 4.2.1, 4.2.2]
).
Let
(M=G/H,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
be a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
homogeneous space fibering over a Wolf space, and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
be a reductive decomposition. Denote by
\mathcal{V},\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
the respective images under the identification
T_{o}M\cong\mathfrak{m}
of the vertical and horizontal spaces at the origin.

(i)

The Nomizu map for the canonical connection is given by

(7)

\displaystyle\Uplambda(V)W=\begin{cases}\frac{\delta-2\alpha}{\delta}[V,W]&V\in\mathcal{V},\\
0&V\in\mathcal{H},\end{cases}

(ii)

The Nomizu map for the Levi-Civita connection is given by

(8)

\displaystyle\Uplambda^{g}(V)W=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\text{\emph{proj}}_{\mathfrak{m}}[V,W]&V,W\in\mathcal{V}\text{\emph{ or }}V,W\in\mathcal{H}\\
\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\right)[V,W]&V\in\mathcal{V},\ W\in\mathcal{H}\\
\frac{\alpha}{\delta}[V,W]&V\in\mathcal{H},\ W\in\mathcal{V}.\\
\end{cases}

Note that the choice of reductive complement
\mathfrak{m}
in the preceding proposition is unique for the following reason. Any
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
homogeneous space fibering over a Wolf space is specified by certain Lie algebraic data, called
generalized
3\text{-Sasakian}
data
, described in
[
ADS21
]
. In particular the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is simple, hence the complement
\mathfrak{m}
is canonically given by the orthogonal complement of the isotropy subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the Killing form. Explicit realizations of the reductive complement
\mathfrak{m}
are given in
[
DOP20
]
in the case of homogeneous
3\text{-Sasakian}
spaces, and this description carries over to the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
setting (see
[
ADS21
]
).

2.3.
Spin Structures and the Spinor Bundle

In this subsection we briefly recall the basic definitions and facts about spinors, following the conventions of
[
AHL23
]
. Let
(M^{m},g)
be an oriented
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
P_{\SO}
its oriented orthonormal frame bundle. Furthermore, let
\lambda\colon\Spin(m)\to\SO(m)
be the double covering homomorphism and by abuse of notation let
R\colon P_{G}\times G\to P_{G}
denote right multiplication map (
G=\SO(m),\Spin(m)
). A
spin structure
on
M
is a principal
\Spin(m)
-bundle
P_{\Spin}
together with a
2
-to-
1
covering map
\Lambda\colon P_{\Spin}\to P_{\SO}
such that the following diagram commutes:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{\Spin}\times\Spin(m)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{\Spin}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{\SO}\times\SO(m)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{P_{\SO}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda\times\lambda}

The
spinor bundle
is the associated vector bundle

\Sigma M:=P_{\Spin}\times_{\Spin(m)}\Sigma

induced by the
spin representation

\Spin(m)\to\text{GL}(\Sigma)
. The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
induces a connection on
\Sigma M
, which we also denote by
\nabla^{g}
(by composing the connection
1
-form with the Lie algebra isomorphism
\mathfrak{so}(m)\cong\mathfrak{spin}(m)
), and this spinorial connection parallelizes a
\Spin(m)
-invariant Hermitian product
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
.

The following is an explicit realization of the spin representation in dimension
m=4n-1
. Letting
e_{1},\dots,e_{4n-1}
be the standard orthonormal basis of
(\mathbb{R}^{4n-1},g_{\text{Euc}})
, define

L^{\prime}:=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{x_{j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2j}-ie_{2j+1})\}_{j=1}^{2n-1},\qquad L^{\prime}:=\Span_{\mathbb{C}}\{y_{j}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_{2j}+ie_{2j+1})\}_{j=1}^{2n-1}

to be respectively the spaces of formal
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
vectors in
T^{\mathbb{C}}M
(we do not assume the existence of a complex/contact structure for this definition). The spin representation of

(9)

\displaystyle\Spin(m):=\{v_{1}\dots v_{2\ell}\colon v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},||v_{i}||=1,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset\langle\mathbb{R}^{n}\ \rvert\ v\cdot w+w\cdot v=-2g_{\text{Euc}}(v,w)\rangle

may be realized on the vector space
\Sigma:=\Lambda^{\bullet}L^{\prime}
by specifying the action of the unit vectors
e_{i}
:

(10)

\displaystyle e_{2j}\cdot\eta

\displaystyle=i(x_{j}\lrcorner\eta+y_{j}\wedge\eta),\quad e_{2j+1}\cdot\eta=(y_{j}\wedge\eta-x_{j}\lrcorner\eta),\quad e_{1}=i\Id\rvert_{\Sigma^{\text{even}}}-i\Id\rvert_{\Sigma^{\text{odd}}},

for all
\eta\in\Sigma
. The algebra on the right hand side of (
9
) is called the
Clifford algebra
, and the action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
\Sigma
by the above formulas is called
Clifford multiplication
. Clifford multiplication extends naturally to an action of
\Lambda^{\bullet}\mathbb{R}^{m}
on
\Sigma
by specifying
e_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{i_{\ell}}\cdot\eta=e_{i_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot e_{i_{\ell}}\cdot\eta
for all
\eta\in\Sigma
, and at the level of bundles this gives an action of vector fields and differential forms on spinors. Furthermore, Clifford multiplication by vector fields is skew-symmetric with respect to the Hermitian form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
.

2.4.
Killing Spinors on
3\text{-Sasakian}
Manifolds

In this subsection we recall previously known results about Killing spinors on
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifolds. Throughout the article we shall always fix the orientation such that all Killing spinors correspond to the Killing number
\frac{1}{2}
(which is possible in dimension 3 (mod 4)). The story begins in the late 80s and early 90s with results obtained independently by several different authors. Focusing specifically on the Sasakian and

Theorem 2.9

(

If

Remark 2.10

For a

3.
\mathcal{H}
-Homothetic Deformations of Killing Spinors on Positive
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
Spaces

In this section we examine how Killing spinors on
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifolds transform under the
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformations
introduced in
[
AD20
, §2.3]
. We begin by recalling the definition of these deformations and their basic properties:

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
AD20
, §2.3]
). Let
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
be real numbers satisfying
a>0
,
a+b>0
,
c\neq 0
,
c^{2}=a+b
, and define
\alpha^{\prime}:=\alpha c/a
,
\delta^{\prime}=\delta/c
. The
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
relative to
a,b,c
is the
3
-
(\alpha^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
-Sasaki manifold
(M,g^{\prime},\xi_{i}^{\prime},\eta_{i}^{\prime},\varphi_{i}^{\prime})
with structure tensors given by

\eta_{i}^{\prime}=c\eta_{i},\qquad\xi_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{c}\xi_{i},\qquad\varphi_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i},\qquad g^{\prime}=ag+b\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}.

In particular, observe that
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformations satisfy
\alpha^{\prime}\delta^{\prime}=\alpha\delta/a
, so it is impossible to change type (positive, degenerate, negative) by such a deformation. It is demonstrated in
[
AD20
, §2.3]
that every positive (
\alpha\delta>0
) structure is an
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of a
3
-
\hat{\alpha}
-Sasakian structure (
\hat{\alpha}=\hat{\delta}
), every negative (
\alpha\delta<0
) structure is an
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of a negative structure with
\hat{\alpha}=-\hat{\delta}
, and any
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation of a degenerate (
\delta=0
) structure is again degenerate.

Starting with a
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure (
\alpha_{0}=\delta_{0}=1
), it is easy to see from the preceding definition that the parameters
a,b,c
needed to obtain a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure with a given
\alpha,\delta
satisfying
\alpha\delta>0
are

(12)

\displaystyle a=\frac{1}{\alpha\delta},\qquad b=\frac{\alpha-\delta}{\alpha\delta^{2}},\qquad c=\frac{1}{\delta}.

In particular, we have

(13)

\displaystyle\eta_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\delta}\eta_{i},\qquad\xi_{i}^{\prime}=\delta\xi_{i},\qquad\varphi_{i}^{\prime}=\varphi_{i},\qquad g^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\alpha\delta}g+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{\alpha\delta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}\otimes\eta_{i}.

Remark 3.2
.

Since we are interested in deforming the
3\text{-Sasakian}
Killing spinors along this family we need the orientation to remain consistent, so we shall only consider deformations resulting in
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structures with
\alpha,\delta>0
.

In what follows, we adapt the argument from
[
BB12
, Chap. 2.2]
to the
3
-contact setting in order to see how the Riemannian Killing spinors on the original Sasakian manifold change under the
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation (
12
). As a matter of notation, we denote by
g
the
3\text{-Sasakian}
metric and by
g^{\prime}
the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
metric, and we consider the isometric identifications
\sigma\colon(TM,g)\to(TM,g^{\prime})
of the tangent spaces given by

(14)

\displaystyle\sigma(X)=\sqrt{\alpha\delta}X+(\delta-\sqrt{\alpha\delta})\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(X)\xi_{p}\qquad\text{ for all }X\in TM.

Concretely,
\sigma
acts as multiplication by
\sqrt{\alpha\delta}
on
\mathcal{H}
and multiplication by
\delta
on
\mathcal{V}
. A tedious but straightforward calculation shows that the Levi-Civita connection of the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
metric is simply the sum of the original Levi-Civita connection and a correction term depending on the original structure tensors:

Lemma 3.3
.

The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g^{\prime}}
of the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure obtained from a
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure by the
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformation (
12
) is given by

\nabla^{g^{\prime}}_{X}Y=\nabla^{g}_{X}Y+\frac{\delta-\alpha}{\delta}\sum_{p=1}^{3}[\eta_{p}(X)\varphi_{p}(Y)+\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)].

Letting
\Sigma
(resp.
\widetilde{\Sigma}
) denote the spinor bundle of
(M,g)
(resp.
(M,g^{\prime})
), there is an identification
\Sigma\to\widetilde{\Sigma}
,
\psi\mapsto\widetilde{\psi}
which relates the Clifford multiplication between the two spinor bundles via
\sigma
:

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{X\cdot\psi}=\sigma(X)\cdot\widetilde{\psi}\qquad\text{ for all }\psi\in\Sigma,X\in TM.

In the following lemma we introduce another metric connection on
(TM,g^{\prime})
whose spinorial lift has a particularly useful property:

Lemma 3.4
.

The connection
\nabla^{\sigma}
on
(TM,g^{\prime})
defined by

\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}Y:=(\sigma\circ\nabla^{g}_{X}\circ\sigma^{-1})Y=\sigma(\nabla^{g}_{X}(\sigma^{-1}(Y))),\qquad\text{ for all }X,Y\in TM

is metric with respect to
g^{\prime}
, and is given explicitly by

\displaystyle\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X}^{g}Y+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\xi_{p}+\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\delta}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{V}},

where superscript
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
denote projection onto the horizontal and vertical spaces respectively. Furthermore, its spinorial lift satisfies

(16)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{\sigma}\widetilde{\psi}=\widetilde{\nabla_{X}^{g}\psi},\qquad\text{ for all }\psi\in\Sigma,X\in TM.

Proof.

We directly calculate:

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{\sigma}Y=\sigma\left(\nabla_{X}^{g}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}Y+\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\xi_{p}\right]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\sigma(\nabla_{X}^{g}Y)+\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\sigma(\nabla_{X}^{g}(\eta_{p}(Y)\xi_{p}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\left[\sqrt{\alpha\delta}\ \nabla_{X}^{g}Y+(\delta-\sqrt{\alpha\delta})\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(\nabla_{X}^{g}Y)\xi_{p}\right]+\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\sigma(X(\eta_{p}(Y))\xi_{p}+\eta_{p}(Y)\nabla_{X}^{g}\xi_{p}),

and hence the difference of
\nabla^{\sigma}
and
\nabla^{g}
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{\sigma}Y-\nabla^{g}_{X}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}-1\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left[X(\eta_{p}(Y))-(\nabla_{X}^{g}\eta_{p})Y\right]\xi_{p}+\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left[\delta X(\eta_{p}(Y))\xi_{p}-\eta_{p}(Y)\sigma(\varphi_{p}(X))\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}-1\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\xi_{p}-\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\left[\sqrt{\alpha\delta}\ \varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{H}}+\delta\varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{V}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\xi_{p}+\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\delta}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{H}}-\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)^{\mathcal{V}}.

The fact that
\nabla^{\sigma}
is metric with respect to
g^{\prime}
is easily seen by noting that

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}Y,Z)+g^{\prime}(Y,\nabla_{X}^{\sigma}Z)

\displaystyle=g(\nabla_{X}^{g}(\sigma^{-1}Y),\sigma^{-1}Z)+g(\sigma^{-1}Y,\nabla_{X}^{g}(\sigma^{-1}Z))

\displaystyle=Xg(\sigma^{-1}Y,\sigma^{-1}Z)=Xg^{\prime}(Y,Z).

Finally, (
16
) follows essentially by definition of the spin lift of a metric connection, using (
15
).
∎

The reason we consider
\nabla^{\sigma}
is that it serves as an intermediate step in determining how
\nabla^{g^{\prime}}
acts on Killing spinors in
\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Indeed, since
\nabla^{\sigma}
is a metric connection we have
\nabla^{g^{\prime}}=\nabla^{\sigma}+\tau
for some difference tensor
\tau\in TM^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{2}TM
, and the right hand side of (
16
) has a very simple form when
\psi
is a Killing spinor on the
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold
(M,g)
. Thus, in order to differentiate such a deformed Killing spinor
\widetilde{\psi}
all that remains is to describe its Clifford product by
\tau_{V}
for all
V\in TM
, as we do in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

The difference tensor
\tau:=\nabla^{g^{\prime}}-\nabla^{\sigma}\in TM^{*}\otimes\Lambda^{2}TM
satisfies

\tau_{X}=(\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha\delta})\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}^{\prime}\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{p}^{\prime}),\qquad\tau_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}=(\alpha-\delta)(\Phi_{i}^{\prime})^{\mathcal{H}}

for all
X\in\mathcal{H}
and all
Y,Z\in TM
.

Proof.

Let
Y,Z\in TM
be arbitrary. For
X\in\mathcal{H}
we compute, using the preceding two lemmas,

\displaystyle\tau_{X}(Y,Z)=g^{\prime}(\nabla^{g^{\prime}}_{X}Y-\nabla^{\sigma}_{X}Y,Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=1}^{3}g^{\prime}\left(\frac{\delta-\alpha}{\delta}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X)-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\xi_{p}-\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\delta}\right)\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(X),Z\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha\delta}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left[\frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\delta}\eta_{p}(Y)\Phi_{p}(X,Z)-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\eta_{p}(Z)\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\alpha-\delta}{\alpha\delta^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Z)\Phi_{p}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\alpha\delta^{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left[\eta_{p}(Y)\Phi_{p}(X,Z)-\eta_{p}(Z)\Phi_{p}(X,Y)\right]=\frac{\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{\alpha\delta^{2}}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\left(\eta_{p}\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{p})\right)(Y,Z)

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha\delta})\sum_{p=1}^{3}(\eta_{p}^{\prime}\wedge(X\lrcorner\Phi_{p}^{\prime}))(Y,Z).

In the vertical directions the difference between
\nabla^{g^{\prime}}
and
\nabla^{\sigma}
is given by

\displaystyle\nabla^{g^{\prime}}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}Y-\nabla^{\sigma}_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}Y=\delta\left(\nabla^{g^{\prime}}_{\xi_{i}}Y-\nabla^{\sigma}_{\xi_{i}}Y\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\delta-\alpha)(\varphi_{i}(Y)+\eta_{k}(Y)\xi_{j}-\eta_{j}(Y)\xi_{k})-\left(\delta-\frac{\delta^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\Phi_{p}(\xi_{i},Y)\xi_{p}+\left(\delta-\frac{\delta\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\varphi_{p}(\xi_{i})

for all
Y\in TM
. Simplifying, using the formula (
1
) describing the action of the endomorphisms
\varphi_{p}
(and the associated
2
-forms
\Phi_{p}
) in the vertical directions and the formula (
13
) for
g^{\prime}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau_{\xi_{i}^{\prime}}(Y,Z)=\frac{1}{\alpha}\left[\frac{\delta-\alpha}{\delta}\left(-\Phi_{i}(Y,Z)-\eta_{j,k}(Y,Z)\right)+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\eta_{j,k}(Y,Z)-\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\eta_{j,k}(Y,Z)\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{\alpha\delta}\left[\left(1-\frac{\delta}{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}\right)\eta_{j,k}(Y,Z)-\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\right)\eta_{j,k}(Y,Z)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-\delta}{\alpha\delta}\left(\Phi_{i}+\eta_{j,k}\right)(Y,Z)=(\alpha-\delta)(\Phi_{i}^{\prime})^{\mathcal{H}}(Y,Z),

where
(i,j,k)
is an even permutation of
(1,2,3)
.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following theorem specifying the differential equations of the deformed Killing spinors on
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifolds with respect to the Levi-Civita connection. Beginning with this theorem and continuing for the remainder of the article, we shall always use
(g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
to denote a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure rather than a
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure.

Theorem 3.6
.

A positive
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold
(M^{4n-1},g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
of dimension
4n-1
admits at least
n+1
linearly independent spinors satisfying

(17)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi_{i}}^{g}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}\xi_{i}\cdot\psi+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\Phi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\psi,\qquad\nabla^{g}_{X}\psi=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{2}X\cdot\psi+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}-\alpha}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\xi_{p}\cdot\varphi_{p}(X)\cdot\psi

for all
X\in\mathcal{H}
. If
(M,g)
is isometric to the round sphere
S^{4n-1}
(with its standard
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure), then the spinor bundle is spanned by solutions of (
17
).

Proof.

The formulas follow from a straightforward calculation using (
16
) and Lemma
3.5
. The lower bound on the number of solutions is a consequence of the well-known fact that a
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold of dimension
4n-1
admits
n+1
linearly independent Killing spinors if it is not isometric to the round sphere
S^{4n-1}
(with its standard
3\text{-Sasakian}
structure) and a full basis of Killing spinors otherwise (see e.g.
[
Bär93
]
).
∎

We introduce a name for spinors satisfying (
17
).

Definition 3.7
.

A
deformed Killing spinor
on a positive
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold is a solution of (
17
).

Remark 3.8
.

Deformed Killing spinors, as per the preceding definition, are not to be confused with the solutions of (
20
) in the next section, which we shall call
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors
. This is an unfortunate clash of terminology with the previous work
[
AHL23
]
, which used the name deformed Killing spinors to describe solutions of (
20
), however we believe that spinors obtained from Killing spinors by
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformations are much more deserving of the name
“deformed”
.

Remark 3.9
.

Conceptually, the existence of spinors satisfying (
17
) emphasizes an important point:
3\text{-Sasakian}
structures exist within the large family of
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structures, and the spinors on these spaces should also vary smoothly along the family. Equation (
17
) says that these spinors are Killing spinors up to a correction term, defined in terms of the structure tensors, which vanishes in the
3
-
\alpha
-Sasakian case (
\alpha=\delta
).

In the following proposition we collect the spinorial equations satisfied by solutions of (
17
) with respect to the other relevant spinorial connections, namely the canonical connection
\nabla
of the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure and the connection
\nabla^{\sigma}
defined in Lemma
3.4
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Deformed Killing spinors are generalized Killing spinors with torsion for the metric connection
\nabla^{\sigma}
, satisfying the equation

\nabla_{X}^{\sigma}\psi=\frac{1}{2}\sigma(X)\cdot\psi=\left(\frac{\delta}{2}X^{\mathcal{V}}+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{2}X^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\cdot\psi

for all
X\in TM
. With respect to the canonical connection,
\nabla
, they satisfy

(18)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{2}\xi_{i}\cdot\psi+\frac{2\alpha-\delta}{2}\Phi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\psi+\frac{\delta-4\alpha}{2}\eta_{j}\cdot\eta_{k}\cdot\psi,

(19)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{2}X\cdot\psi+\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\xi_{p}\cdot\varphi_{p}(X)\cdot\psi

for all
X\in\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

The differential equation with respect to
\nabla^{\sigma}
follows immediately from (
16
). The other equations follow from an easy calculation using the formula (
4
) for the torsion of
\nabla
.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

The preceding proposition lends credence to the notion that
\nabla^{\sigma}
is better suited to the deformed Killing spinors than either the Levi-Civita or canonical connections of the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure. This is not altogether surprising, given that the Levi-Civita connection on a
3\text{-Sasakian}
manifold has good spinorial properties, and
\nabla^{\sigma}
is its natural extension under
\mathcal{H}
-homothetic deformations.

In practice, although deformed Killing spinors behave nicely with respect to
\nabla^{\sigma}
, they also have several unfortunate properties:

(i)

They are not typically Killing spinors for the Levi-Civita connection in the horizontal or vertical directions;

(ii)

They do not generalize well to negative
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifolds due to the appearance of coefficients involving
\sqrt{\alpha\delta}
.

Let us elaborate on point (ii). At present, the main techniques for finding globally defined spinor fields are quite limited: one can look for bundles of spinors preserved by a connection whose restriction to the bundle is flat (as Friedrich and Kath did in
[
FK90
]
), or one can use the existence of a
G
-structure to find trivial subbundles for the (lifted) action of
G
on the spinor module (important examples of this include invariant spinors on homogeneous spaces and the defining spinors of
\SU(3)
- and
\G_{2}
-structures in dimensions
6
and
7
respectively). The first technique is ruled out for our purposes since the natural choice of connection to consider would be
\bar{\nabla}
defined by

\bar{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}}\psi=\nabla^{g}_{\xi_{i}}\psi-\frac{\delta}{2}\xi_{i}\cdot\psi-\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\Phi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\psi,\quad\bar{\nabla}_{X}\psi=\nabla^{g^{\prime}}_{X}\psi-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}}{2}X\cdot\psi-\frac{\sqrt{\alpha\delta}-\alpha}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\xi_{p}\cdot\varphi_{p}(X)\cdot\psi

for
X\in\mathcal{H}
, and this doesn’t generalize well to the negative
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
setting in light of point (ii) above; one would need to consider spinorial equations with complex coefficients, which deviate from the spirit of classical real and imaginary Killing spinors. The second technique is also not straightforward in our situation; while there is a reduction to
\Sp(n-1)\cong\Sp(n-1)\times\{1\}\subset\SO(4n-1)
(
[
Kuo70
]
) and this group is known to stabilize
2n
linearly independent spinors (see e.g.
[
AHL23
]
), the differential equations satisfied by these spinors depend on the intrinsic torsion of the
\Sp(n-1)
-structure, which is not well understood at present. The rest of the paper is devoted to a different generalization of Killing spinors, introduced in Theorem
4.1
, which avoids these problems. We will show that these spinors can be defined for any
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold, including when
\alpha\delta<0
, and that they behave well in the homogeneous setting with respect to a certain notion of duality which we introduce in Section
5
.

4.
\mathcal{H}
-Killing Spinors on
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
Spaces

Let
(M,g,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
be a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{g}
and canonical connection
\nabla
. In this section we will prove that the bundles
E_{i}
recalled in (
11
) are spanned by spinors satisfying the
\mathcal{H}
-Killing equation
(
20
). To construct such spinors we adapt the method of Friedrich and Kath in
[
FK90
]
: we prove that the bundles
E_{i}
are preserved by a certain modified connection
\widehat{\nabla}
, and that the restriction of
\widehat{\nabla}
to
E:=E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}
is flat. The connection
\widehat{\nabla}
will be chosen in an obvious way so that its parallel sections are precisely
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors, and we shall assume that the underlying manifold is simply-connected so that flatness of
\widehat{\nabla}\rvert_{E}
implies the existence of globally defined parallel sections. While the overall strategy of Friedrich and Kath still works in this new setting, the calculations become considerably more complicated than in the
3\text{-Sasakian}
case. We will mitigate this by using the canonical connection–which was only recently introduced in
[
AD20
]
–where possible, in order to take advantage of the curvature identities calculated in
[
ADS23
]
. The main result of this section is the following:

Theorem 4.1
.

On a simply-connected
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
space
(M,g,\varphi_{i},\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
, the bundle
E:=E_{1}+E_{2}+E_{3}
has rank at least
2
and is spanned by solutions of the equation

(20)

\displaystyle\nabla^{g}_{X}\psi

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}X\cdot\psi+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(X)\Phi_{p}\cdot\psi\qquad\text{ for all }X\in TM.

Definition 4.2
.

We call solutions of (
20
)
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors
.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that
\mathcal{H}
-Killing spinors are generalizations of Killing spinors (which may be obtained by setting
\alpha=\delta
), and also avoid several of the problems encountered with deformed Killing spinors in the previous section: they are Killing spinors in the horizontal directions, and are well-defined for all
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifolds (not just the positive ones).

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

4.1.
The Modified Spinorial Connection

In this subsection we introduce a
modified connection

\widehat{\nabla}
on the spinor bundle and show that it preserves the bundles
E_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
. To begin, we prove two technical lemmas which will allow us to adapt the calculation of Friedrich and Kath in
[
FK90
]
to this new setting. The first lemma describes how sections of
E_{i}
behave with respect to Clifford multiplication by the structural
2
-forms
\Phi_{j},\Phi_{k}
, which is necessary to understand due to the second term on the right hand side of the
\mathcal{H}
-Killing equation (
20
).

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
be a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold. If
\psi\in\Gamma(E_{i})
and
(i,j,k)
is an even permutation of
(1,2,3)
, then

(21)

\displaystyle(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\Phi_{j}\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=[8\varphi_{k}(X)-2\xi_{k}X+2X\xi_{k}-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+4\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}]\cdot\psi,

(22)

\displaystyle(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\Phi_{k}\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=[-8\varphi_{j}(X)+2\xi_{j}X-2X\xi_{j}-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{k}+4\eta_{k}(X)\xi_{i}]\cdot\psi,

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
.

Proof.

We calculate in the Clifford algebra,

\displaystyle-2\varphi_{i}(X)\cdot\Phi_{j}

\displaystyle=2(2\varphi_{i}(X)\lrcorner\Phi_{j}-\Phi_{j}\cdot\varphi_{i}(X))=2(-2\varphi_{j}(\varphi_{i}(X))-\Phi_{j}\cdot\varphi_{i}(X))

\displaystyle=-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+4\varphi_{k}(X)-2\Phi_{j}\cdot\varphi_{i}(X),

\displaystyle\xi_{i}\cdot X\cdot\Phi_{j}

\displaystyle=\xi_{i}\cdot(-2X\lrcorner\Phi_{j}+\Phi_{j}\cdot X)=2\xi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}(X)+\xi_{i}\cdot\Phi_{j}\cdot X

\displaystyle=2\xi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}(X)+(-2\xi_{i}\lrcorner\Phi_{j}+\Phi_{j}\cdot\xi_{i})\cdot X

\displaystyle=2\xi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}(X)-2\xi_{k}\cdot X+\Phi_{j}\cdot\xi_{i}\cdot X,

\displaystyle-X\cdot\xi_{i}\cdot\Phi_{j}

\displaystyle=-X\cdot(-2\xi_{i}\lrcorner\Phi_{j}+\Phi_{j}\cdot\xi_{i})=2X\cdot\xi_{k}+(2X\lrcorner\Phi_{j}-\Phi_{j}\cdot X)\cdot\xi_{i}

\displaystyle=2X\cdot\xi_{k}-2\varphi_{j}(X)\cdot\xi_{i}-\Phi_{j}\cdot X\cdot\xi_{i}.

Adding these equations and using the defining relation for
E_{i}
gives

\displaystyle(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\Phi_{j}\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[4\varphi_{k}(X)-2\xi_{k}X+2X\xi_{k}]\cdot\psi+[-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+2\xi_{i}\cdot\varphi_{j}(X)-2\varphi_{j}(X)\cdot\xi_{i}]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[4\varphi_{k}(X)-2\xi_{k}X+2X\xi_{k}]\cdot\psi+[-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+4\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{j}(X))]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[4\varphi_{k}(X)-2\xi_{k}X+2X\xi_{k}]\cdot\psi+[-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+4\varphi_{k}(X)+4\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[8\varphi_{k}(X)-2\xi_{k}X+2X\xi_{k}-4\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+4\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}]\cdot\psi.

Equation (
22
) is proved analogously.
∎

The second technical lemma is an identity which arises from Friedrich and Kath’s calculations for the
3\text{-Sasakian}
case, and also holds for
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifolds:

Lemma 4.5
.

(Based on the proof of
[
FK90
, Thm. 1]
).
If
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold and
\psi\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, then

(23)

\displaystyle[-2(g(X,Y)\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}(X)Y)-\varphi_{i}(Y)X+X\varphi_{i}(Y)]\cdot\psi+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2}Y\cdot\psi\right)=0.

Proof.

This follows by the same calculation as on
[
FK90
, p. 547]
(note that their calculation has a small typo on the second last line; the term
“
+XY\xi
”
should instead say
“
\pm XY\xi
”
).
∎

Using these two lemmas we prove the desired result:

Proposition 4.6
.

If
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold then the modified spinorial connection

(24)

\displaystyle\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}\psi:=\nabla_{Y}^{g}\psi-\frac{\alpha}{2}Y\cdot\psi-\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\Phi_{p}\cdot\psi,\qquad Y\in TM,\ \psi\in\Sigma M

preserves the bundles
E_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
.

Proof.

Let
\psi\in\Gamma(E_{i})
, and take
(i,j,k)
an even permutation of
(1,2,3)
. Differentiating the defining equation

(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\psi=0

with respect to
Y
gives

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[-2(\nabla^{g}_{Y}\varphi_{i})X-2\varphi_{i}(\nabla_{Y}^{g}X)+(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\xi_{i})X+\xi_{i}(\nabla_{Y}^{g}X)-(\nabla_{Y}^{g}X)\xi_{i}-X(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\xi_{i})]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\nabla_{Y}^{g}\psi

(25)

\displaystyle=[-2(\nabla^{g}_{Y}\varphi_{i})X+(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\xi_{i})X-X(\nabla_{Y}^{g}\xi_{i})]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}Y\cdot\psi+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\sum_{p=1}^{3}\eta_{p}(Y)\Phi_{p}\cdot\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}\psi.

(i)

If
Y\in\mathcal{H}
then (
25
) simplifies, using Proposition
2.3
, to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=[-2\alpha(g(X,Y)\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}(X)Y)-\alpha\varphi_{i}(Y)X+\alpha X\varphi_{i}(Y)]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}Y\cdot\psi\right)+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{Y}\psi,

and the fact that the first two terms on the right hand side of the above equation sum to zero follows immediately from (
23
).

(ii)

If
Y=\xi_{i}
then Proposition
2.3
reduces (
25
) to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\xi_{i}\cdot\psi+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\Phi_{i}\cdot\psi\right)+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{\xi_{i}}\psi,

whose first term vanishes due to the fact that
\Phi_{i}
acts on
E_{i}^{-}
as a multiple of
\xi_{i}
, and
\xi_{i}
anti-commutes with the operator defining
E_{i}^{-}
.

(iii)

If
Y=\xi_{j}
then (
25
) reduces to

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=-2[\alpha(\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}-\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j})+2(\alpha-\delta)\varphi_{k}(X)+(\alpha-\delta)\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}-(\alpha-\delta)\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[(-\alpha\xi_{k}+(\alpha-\delta)\xi_{k})X-X(-\alpha\xi_{k}+(\alpha-\delta)\xi_{k})]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\xi_{j}\cdot\psi+\frac{\alpha-\delta}{2}\Phi_{j}\cdot\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{\xi_{j}}\psi

\displaystyle=(\alpha-\delta)[-4\varphi_{k}(X)-2\eta_{j}(X)\xi_{i}+2\eta_{i}(X)\xi_{j}+\xi_{k}X-X\xi_{k}+\frac{1}{2}(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\Phi_{j}]\cdot\psi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+(-2\varphi_{i}(X)+\xi_{i}X-X\xi_{i})\cdot\widehat{\nabla}_{\xi_{j}}\psi

(for the second equality we have again used (
23
) to eliminate some of the terms). The vanishing of the first term then follows immediately by substituting (
21
).

(iv)

For the case
Y=\xi_{k}
, one performs an analogous calculation using (
22
).

∎

4.2.
Curvature and Torsion Identities for the Canonical Connection

In this subsection we derive some useful curvature identities for the canonical connection, and also some torsion identities which will allow us to translate the former into curvature identities for the Levi-Civita connection. These will be used in the next subsection towards our ultimate goal of showing that the restriction of
\widehat{\nabla}
to
E
is flat. We denote by
\nabla^{g}
,
\nabla
,
\widehat{\nabla}
the Levi-Civita, canonical, and modified connections, and by
R^{g}
,
R
,
\widehat{R}
their respective curvature operators, and we recall from Proposition
2.4
the constant
\beta:=2(\delta-2\alpha)
. For the purposes of our calculations, it will be convenient to use a special type of orthonormal frame called an
adapted frame
:

Definition 4.7
.

An orthonormal frame
e_{1},\dots,e_{4n-1}
with
\xi_{i}=e_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
is said to be adapted if the fundamental 2-forms
\Phi_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
take the form

(26)

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{2,3}-\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}(e_{4p,4p+1}+e_{4p+2,4p+3}),

(27)

\displaystyle\Phi_{2}

\displaystyle=\xi_{1,3}-\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}(e_{4p,4p+2}-e_{4p+1,4p+3}),

(28)

\displaystyle\Phi_{3}

\displaystyle=-\xi_{1,2}-\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}(e_{4p,4p+3}+e_{4p+1,4p+2}).

From this point forward, we shall always work with adapted orthonormal frames. We begin by proving a curvature identity for a certain
\varphi_{i}
-twisted trace of the canonical curvature. The reason to consider such a twisting is the same as in the classical argument by Friedrich and Kath; if
\psi\in\Gamma(E_{i})
then the
E_{i}
-projection of the Clifford product of a
2
-form
\tau=\sum_{p<q}\tau_{p,q}e_{p}\wedge e_{q}
with
\psi
is given precisely by considering only the Clifford products with the terms
\tau_{p,q}e_{p}\wedge e_{q}
where
e_{q}=\pm\varphi_{i}e_{p}
(see the proof of
[
FK90
, Thm. 1]
). In particular, in the next subsection we will apply this to the curvature
2
-forms
R^{g}(X,Y)\in\Lambda^{2}TM
associated to pairs of tangent vectors
X,Y\in TM
in the course of proving that the restriction of
\widehat{\nabla}
to
E
is flat. To calculate these curvature
2
-forms we shall first calculate the corresponding
2
-forms for the canonical connection (taking advantage of its nice behaviour with respect to the
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
structure tensors), and then translate these back in terms of the Levi-Civita connection using (
5
). As such, we begin with a proposition giving identities for the
\varphi_{i}
-twisted traces of the canonical curvature operator
R
. The subsequent proposition gives the
\varphi_{i}
-twisted traces of certain operators defined in terms of the canonical torsion
T
and its exterior derivative
dT
; this is precisely the information needed in order to translate the canonical curvature identities into identities for the Levi-Civita curvature using (
5
).

Proposition 4.8
.

If
(M,g,\xi_{i},\eta_{i},\varphi_{i})
is a
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
manifold and
e_{1},\dots,e_{4n-1}
an adapted orthonormal (local) frame, then

\sum_{s=1}^{4n-1}R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))=\begin{cases}4n\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y),&X,Y\in\mathcal{H},\\
0,&X\in\mathcal{H},Y\in\mathcal{V},\\
0,&X\in\mathcal{V},Y\in\mathcal{H},\\
8n\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y),&X,Y\in\mathcal{V}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We consider the cases one at a time:

(i)

Suppose first that
X,Y\in\mathcal{H}
. For
e_{s}\in\mathcal{H}
and any even permutation
(i,j,k)
of
(1,2,3)
, it follows from
[
ADS23
, Eqn. (2.6)]
that

\displaystyle 2\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))+R(X,Y,\varphi_{j}(e_{s}),\varphi_{k}(e_{s}))

\displaystyle=R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))+R(X,Y,\varphi_{j}(e_{s}),\varphi_{i}(\varphi_{j}(e_{s}))),

and taking the sum over the horizontal basis vectors
e_{s}
,
s=4,\dots,4n-1
then gives

(29)

\displaystyle 2\sum_{s=4}^{4n-1}R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))

\displaystyle=2(4n-4)\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y)

(since
\varphi_{j}(e_{s})
runs through the list
\pm e_{4},\dots,\pm e_{4n-1}
as
s
runs through
4,\dots,4n-1
). In the vertical directions, it follows from
[
ADS23
, Eqns. (1.2), (2.5)]
that
R(X,Y,\xi_{j},\xi_{k})=2\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y)
,
and hence

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{3}R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))

\displaystyle=4\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y).

Combining (
29
) and (
30
) then gives the result in this case:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{4n-1}R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))

\displaystyle=[(4n-4)\alpha\beta+4\alpha\beta]\Phi_{i}(X,Y)=4n\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y).

(ii)

For the mixed cases, the first paragraph of
[
ADS23
, Section 2.2]
shows that
R(X,Y)
is the zero operator when
X\in\mathcal{H},Y\in\mathcal{V}
or
X\in\mathcal{V}
,
Y\in\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

Suppose now that
X,Y\in\mathcal{V}
, and without loss of generality write
X=\xi_{p}
,
Y=\xi_{q}
for
(p,q,r)
an even permutation of
(1,2,3)
. Letting
e_{s}\in\mathcal{H}
, it follows from
[
ADS23
, Eqns. (2.4), (2.5)]
respectively that

\displaystyle R(\xi_{p},\xi_{q},\xi_{j},\xi_{k})

\displaystyle=-4\alpha\beta(\delta_{p,j}\delta_{q,k}-\delta_{p,k}\delta_{q,j})=-4\alpha\beta(\eta_{p}\wedge\eta_{q})(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})=4\alpha\beta\Phi_{r}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k}),

\displaystyle R(\xi_{p},\xi_{q},e_{s},\varphi_{i}e_{s})

\displaystyle=2\alpha\beta\Phi_{r}(e_{s},\varphi_{i}e_{s}),

and combining these gives

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{4n-1}R(X,Y,e_{s},\varphi_{i}(e_{s}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{4n-1}R(\xi_{p},\xi_{q},e_{s},\varphi_{i}e_{s})=2[4\alpha\beta\Phi_{r}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})]+2\alpha\beta\sum_{s=4}^{4n-1}\Phi_{r}(e_{s},\varphi_{i}e_{s})

\displaystyle=8\alpha\beta\Phi_{r}(\xi_{j},\xi_{k})-2\alpha\beta(4n-4)\delta_{i,r}=-8\alpha\beta\delta_{i,r}-8(n-1)\alpha\beta\delta_{i,r}

\displaystyle=-8n\alpha\beta\delta_{i,r}=8n\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(\xi_{p},\xi_{q})=8n\alpha\beta\Phi_{i}(X,Y),

where we have calculated using an even permutation
(i,j,k)
of

5.
Compact/Noncompact Dual Pairs of Homogeneous 3-
(\alpha,\delta)
-Sasaki Spaces

Let us begin by briefly recalling the notion of 3-Sasakian data from
[
DOP20
]
, which the authors of that paper use to characterize homogeneous 3-Sasakian manifolds:

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
DOP20
, Def. 4.1]
). A 3-Sasakian data consists of a pair of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
such that,

(i)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is compact and simple;

(ii)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(1)
a sum of commuting subalgebras;

(iii)

There exists an
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
-module
U
of complex dimension
2(n-1)
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes U
as a module for
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{sp}(1)^{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
, where
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the standard representation of
\mathfrak{sp}(1)^{\mathbb{C}}\cong\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.

In
[
ADS21
]
this same data without the compactness assumption is called
generalized 3-Sasakian data
, and the authors note that it specifies families of
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
homogeneous spaces.

5.1.
Duality of Extended Symmetric Data

What follows is a dualization procedure generalizing the homogeneous
{\text{$3$-}(\alpha,\delta)\text{-Sasaki}}
duality described in
[
ADS21
, Remark 3.1.1(c)]
. Generalizing Definition
5.1
, we define:

Definition 5.2
.

Extended symmetric data

(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k},g)
consists of a triple of real Lie algebras with
\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, together with an inner product
g
on
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
, such that the following properties hold:

(i)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple;

(ii)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
such that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
;

(iii)

The inner product
g
is positive definite and takes the form

(34)

\displaystyle g=\lambda_{0}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}\rvert_{\mathfrak{k}\times\mathfrak{k}}+\lambda_{1}\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}\rvert_{\mathfrak{g}_{1}\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}},\qquad\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},

under the natural identification
\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, where
\kappa_{\mathfrak{g}}
denotes the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
.

The idea behind the preceding definition is that the Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
constitute a Riemannian symmetric pair, thus the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
can be viewed as the Lie algebraic data of a homogeneous space fibering over a symmetric base. Indeed, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, together with the fact that
\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{h}
commute, gives the following commutator relations:

(35)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h},\quad[\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{k}]\subseteq\mathfrak{k},\quad[\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k}]=0,\quad[\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}_{1},\quad[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subseteq\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{k}.

The case of most interest for us is that of
3\text{-Sasakian}
data as in Definition
5.2
, though we give a construction which is valid in the slightly more general setting of extended symmetric data.

Remark 5.3
.

Associated to extended symmetric data
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{k},g)
is the vector space

\mathfrak{m}:=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}

serving as a reductive complement to
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
. Conversely, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
may be recovered from
\mathfrak{m}
via
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. For this reason we shall also refer to
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m},g)
as extended symmetric data. We shall also denote by
\mathfrak{m}_{i}:=\mathfrak{m}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{i}
,
i=0,1
the components of
\mathfrak{m}
with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading on
\mathfrak{g}
.

With the preceding remark in mind, we are ready to define our notion of duality at the Lie algebra level:

Definition 5.4
.

Given extended symmetric data
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m},g)
, we define (for the same
\mathfrak{k}
) the dual extended symmetric data
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
by setting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\mathfrak{k}\oplus i\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},

and taking
g^{\prime}
to be the real inner product induced on
\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}
by extending
g
sesquilinearly to
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
and restricting to the real form
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.

The extension of
g
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is done sesquilinearly in the preceding definition in order to ensure that
g^{\prime}
is positive definite–exactly as in the usual duality of symmetric spaces. It is furthermore clear that this dualization procedure is involutive, so it makes sense to consider pairs of dual extended symmetric spaces without specifying in which direction the duality goes.

Remark 5.5
.

Let us briefly compare Definition
5.4
with Kath’s notion of duality in
[
Kat00
]
. Apart from the fact that Kath’s duality is between Riemannian and pseudo-Riemannian spaces, we note that it also depends on a Lie algebra involution
T
, whose
1
-eigenspace (resp.
(-1)
-eigenspace) indicates which tangent directions on the compact side should correspond to tangent directions on the non-compact side with positive norm squared (resp. negative norm squared). Such an involution is not necessary for our purposes, as the decomposition
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
automatically keeps track of which directions are to be modified. Rather, our duality construction is obtained from the duality between compact and non-compact symmetric spaces by dualizing the symmetric pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{0})
.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
K
be a connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
. An
extended symmetric space
(relative to
K
) is a connected Riemannian homogeneous space
(M:=G/H,g)
with Lie algebra decomposition as in Definition
5.2
. Letting
G^{\prime}
be the connected subgroup of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
corresponding to the real Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, we define the dual of
(M,g)
to be
(M^{\prime}:=G^{\prime}/H,g^{\prime})
.

In order to investigate the spinorial properties of the dual we first describe the special orthogonal group
\SO(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
of the Riemannian metric
g^{\prime}
. We define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{0}

\displaystyle:=\{A\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m}):A(\mathfrak{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{k}\text{ and }A(\mathfrak{m}_{1})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{1}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{B\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m}):B(\mathfrak{k})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{1}\text{ and }B(\mathfrak{m}_{1})\subseteq\mathfrak{k}\},

and the non-standard Lie bracket
[[\cdot,\cdot]]
on

given by

[[A_{1},A_{2}]]:=[A_{1},A_{2}]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}},\quad[[A,iB]]:=i[A,B]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}},\quad[[iB_{1},iB_{2}]]:=[B_{1},B_{2}]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}},

where

is constructed so that

has the same commutators as

Lemma 5.7

Let
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m},g)
be extended symmetric data, and
(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime},\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
the dual data. The map
\tau\colon\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{1}\to\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})
given by

\displaystyle\tau(A)(x)=A(x),\quad\tau(A)(iy)=iA(y),\quad\tau(iB)(x)=iB(x),\quad\tau(iB)(iy)=B(y),

for all
A\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{0}
,
B\in\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{1}
,

,
y\in\mathfrak{m}_{1}
is an isomorphism of Lie algebras.

Proof.

Let
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{k}
,

,

\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{0}
,
\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})_{1}

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\tau(A+iB)x_{1},x_{2})+g^{\prime}(x_{1},\tau(A+iB)x_{2})

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(Ax_{1}+iBx_{1},x_{2})+g^{\prime}(x_{1},Ax_{2}+iBx_{2})

\displaystyle=g(Ax_{1},x_{2})+g(x_{1},Ax_{2})=0,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\tau(A+iB)x_{1},iy_{1})+g^{\prime}(x_{1},\tau(A+iB)(iy_{1}))

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(Ax_{1}+iBx_{1},iy_{1})+g^{\prime}(x_{1},iAy_{1}+By_{1})

\displaystyle=g(Bx_{1},y_{1})+g(x_{1},By_{1})=0,

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(\tau(A+iB)(iy_{1}),iy_{2})+g^{\prime}(iy_{1},\tau(A+iB)(iy_{2}))

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(iAy_{1}+By_{1},iy_{2})+g^{\prime}(iy_{1},iAy_{2}+By_{2})

\displaystyle=g(Ay_{1},y_{2})+g(y_{1},Ay_{2})=0,

hence

\displaystyle\tau[[A+iB,C+iD]]

\displaystyle=\tau([A,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+[B,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[B,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[A,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}),

and on the other hand,

\displaystyle[\tau(A+iB),\tau(C+iD)]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})}(x)

\displaystyle=(\tau(A+iB)\circ\tau(C+iD))(x)-(\tau(C+iD)\circ\tau(A+iB))(x)

\displaystyle=\tau(A+iB)(Cx+iDx)-\tau(C+iD)(Ax+iBx)

\displaystyle=ACx+iADx+iBCx+BDx-(CAx+iCBx+iDAx+DBx)

\displaystyle=\tau([A,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[A,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[B,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+[B,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}})(x)

in the

directions, and

\displaystyle[\tau(A+iB),\tau(C+iD)]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m}^{\prime},g^{\prime})}(iy)

\displaystyle=(\tau(A+iB)\circ\tau(C+iD))(iy)-(\tau(C+iD)\circ\tau(A+iB))(iy)

\displaystyle=\tau(A+iB)(iCy+Dy)-\tau(C+iD)(iAy+By)

\displaystyle=iACy+ADy+BCy+iBDy-(iCAy+CBy+DAy+iDBy)

\displaystyle=\tau([A,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[A,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+i[B,C]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}}+[B,D]_{\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{m})^{\mathbb{C}}})(iy)

in the

\displaystyle\omega:=\eta_{1,2,3}+\sum_{i=1}^{3}\eta_{i}\wedge\Phi_{i}^{\mathcal{H}}

In the

Remark 6.7

Finally, we comment on

Asymptotic behavior of 2D wave–Klein-Gordon coupled system under null condition
Shijie Dong, Yue Ma, Xu Yuan

Abstract
. We study the 2D coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems with semilinear null nonlinearities Q 0 Q_{0} and Q α ​ β Q_{\alpha\beta} . The main result states that the solution to the 2D coupled systems exists globally provided that the initial data are small in some weighted Sobolev space, which do not necessarily have compact support, and we also show the optimal time decay of the solution. The major difficulties lie in the slow decay nature of the wave and the Klein-Gordon components in two space dimensions, in addition, extra difficulties arise due to the presence of the null form Q 0 Q_{0} which is not of divergence form and is not compatible with the Klein-Gordon equations. To overcome the difficulties, a new observation for the structure of the null form Q 0 Q_{0} is required.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The model problem

In this article, we consider the 2D coupled wave–Klein-Gordon system under null condition,

\displaystyle-\Box w

\displaystyle=C_{1}Q_{0}(w,v)+C_{1}^{\alpha\beta}Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v),\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

(1.1)

\displaystyle-\Box v+v

\displaystyle=C_{2}Q_{0}(w,v)+C_{2}^{\alpha\beta}Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v),\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

where
C_{1},C_{2},C_{1}^{\alpha\beta},C_{2}^{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q_{0}
,
Q_{\alpha\beta}
are the standard null forms:

\displaystyle Q_{0}(w,v)=\partial_{\alpha}w\partial^{\alpha}v\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)=\partial_{\alpha}w\partial_{\beta}v-\partial_{\alpha}v\partial_{\beta}w.

The initial data are prescribed at
t=0

\big(w,\partial_{t}w,v,\partial_{t}v\big)_{{|t=0}}=(w_{0},w_{1},v_{0},v_{1}).

(1.2)

We use
\Box=\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}=-\partial_{t}^{2}+\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
to denote the d’Alembert operator. We use Latin letters
i,j,\cdots\in\{1,2\}
to represent space indices, and use Greek letters
\alpha,\beta,\cdots\in\{0,1,2\}
to represent spacetime indices, and the Einstein summation convention for repeated upper and lower indices is adopted.

For various kinds of wave-type equations and systems under a suitable restriction of dimension and nonlinearity, the local well-posedness of the Cauchy problem is well-known. Now, one question of basic importance is that whether the Cauchy problem admits a global-in-time solution provided the initial data are sufficiently smooth and small.
In the 3D case, this question was answered in the affirmative in the seminal works of Christodoulou
[
5
]
and Klainerman
[
23
]
on nonlinear wave equations with null nonlinearities, and of Klainerman
[
21
]
and Shatah
[
31
]
on nonlinear Klein-Gordon equations with general quadratic nonlinearities. Shortly after these fundamental results, the study on 3D coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems started in
[
4
,
14
]
.
On the other hand, in the 2D case, such study is somewhat harder due to the slower decay nature of the wave and the Klein-Gordon components compared to the 3D case (see for instance
[
1
,
30
]
and references therein).
In the present article, we aim to demonstrate the global existence for the 2D coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems in some important cases, i.e., the semilinear null nonlinearities.

1.2.
Main result

The goal of the present paper is twofold. The first one is to generalize the classical result
[
14
]
of Georgiev on coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems from 3D to 2D with more types of quadratic nonlinearities and non-compactly supported initial data, which is more challenging. The other one is that we expect to provide more tools and ideas which can be used to tackle other related physical models in 2D. More precisely, the main result of this article is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\geq 14
be an integer. There exists
0<\varepsilon_{0}\ll 1
such that for all initial data
(w_{0},w_{1},v_{0},v_{1})
satisfying the smallness condition

\displaystyle\sum_{k\leq N+1}\left(\left\|\langle|x|\rangle^{k}\nabla^{k}w_{0}\right\|_{L^{1}+L^{2}}+\left\|\langle|x|\rangle^{k+1}\log(2+|x|)\nabla^{k}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)

(1.3)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\leq N}\left(\left\|\langle|x|\rangle^{k+1}\nabla^{k}w_{1}\right\|_{L^{1}+L^{2}}+\left\|\langle|x|\rangle^{k+2}\log(2+|x|)\nabla^{k}v_{1}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right)\leq\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},

the Cauchy problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) admits a global-in-time solution
(w,v)
, which enjoys the following pointwise decay estimates

|w(t,x)|\lesssim\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},\ \ |v(t,x)|\lesssim\varepsilon\langle t\rangle^{-1},\ \ \left|\partial w(t,x)\right|\lesssim\varepsilon\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-\frac{3}{4}}\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(1.4)

Remark 1.2
.

In Theorem
1.1
, we can treat non-compactly supported initial data.
Besides, the pointwise asymptotic behavior (
1.4
) of the solution
(w,v)
to (
1.1

Remark 1.3

Compared with the previous work

Second, the term

The study of the coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems has large physical significance and is mathematically challenging.
In the physical aspect, several fundamental physical models are governed by coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems, and such models include the Dirac–Klein-Gordon model, the Klein-Gordon–Zakharov model, the Maxwell–Klein-Gordon model, the Einstein–Klein-Gordon model, and so on. To show these models are stable, one is required to study the relevant coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems. In the mathematical aspect, the most well-known obstacle to study such coupled equations is that the scaling vector field

1.3.
Comparison between
Q_{0}
and
Q_{\alpha\beta}

The difficulties in studying coupled wave–Klein-Gordon systems include the non-commutativity of the scaling vector field and the Klein-Gordon operator, the different decay properties of the wave and the Klein-Gordon components etc.,
and we lead one to
[
7
, Introduction]
for the discussion. Here we only discuss in detail about the extra difficulties caused by the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
compared with the term
Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)
.

First, the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
is not compatible with the Klein-Gordon equations. We recall the estimates (see for instance
[
32
, Section 3]
)

|Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\big|\Gamma w\big|\,\big|\Gamma v\big|,

in which
\Gamma\in\{\partial_{\alpha},H_{i},\Omega\}
(see the definition in Section
2
) which are compatible with the Klein-Gordon equations. On the other hand, the estimates on the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
demands the scaling vector field
S
, which is not compatible with the Klein-Gordon equations (and that is the reason why we call
Q_{0}
non-compatible), i.e.,

|Q_{0}(w,v)|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\big(\big|\Gamma w\big|+\big|Sw\big|\big)\big|\Gamma v\big|.

Thus in this sense, it is harder to bound the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
by using the vector field method.

Second, we recall that if the nonlinearities in the wave equation take divergence form, then in most cases we can derive better bounds on the wave component. For instance, we consider the following wave equation (we ignore the initial data for simplicity)

-\Box u=\partial_{\alpha}f

where
\alpha=0,1,2
and
f
is a sufficiently regular function.
For the solution
u
, we can decompose it into two parts
u=u_{1}+\partial_{\alpha}u_{2}
, in which
u_{1},u_{2}
are solutions to the following wave equations

-\Box u_{1}=0,\qquad-\Box u_{2}=f.

In this case, we can get very good control of
\partial u
by the relation

\partial u=\partial u_{1}+\partial\partial_{\alpha}u_{2},

as
u_{1}
satisfies the linear homogeneous wave equation (see the estimates in (
2.13
)), and
\partial\partial_{\alpha}u_{2}
is part of the Hessian of
u_{2}
which roughly speaking enjoys an extra
\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1}
decay compared with
\partial u_{2}
(see the estimates in Lemma
2.10
).
This decomposition can be used to treat the term
Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)
, as it is of divergence form, i.e.,

Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)=\partial_{\beta}(v\partial_{\alpha}w)-\partial_{\alpha}(v\partial_{\beta}w).

However, since the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
is not of divergence form, the above argument cannot be directly applied, thus it requires a new decomposition to handle the term
Q_{0}(w,v)
.

1.4.
New ingredients

To prove Theorem
1.1
, one crucial step is to close the highest-order energy estimates for the solution. One natural choice is to apply the ghost weight energy estimates of Alinhac
[
1
]
. We note that it is necessary to first show (see (
2.33
))

|\partial w|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1/2}\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1/2-\delta}

for some
\delta>0
, so that the ghost weight energy estimates can be applied. Unlike the case of
Q_{\alpha\beta}(w,v)
in the wave equation which is of divergence form (note the nonlinearities in the wave equation of
[
7
,
33
]
are also of divergence form), and from which roughly speaking we can gain one more derivative and hence an extra
\langle t-|x|\rangle^{-1}
decay on the wave component, we need to find a new strategy to treat the case of
Q_{0}(w,v)
which is not of divergence form.

The key to treat the nonlinear term
Q_{0}(w,v)
is one simple but useful observation

Q_{0}(w,v)=\partial_{\alpha}w\partial^{\alpha}v=\partial^{\alpha}(v\partial_{\alpha}w)-v\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}w,

and we note that the first term in the right hand side is of divergence form. Then we note that the term
\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}w=\Box w
contains only quadratic terms which makes
v\partial_{\alpha}\partial^{\alpha}w
into cubic terms. Furthermore, this way of decomposing the nonlinear term
Q_{0}(w,v)
can be carried on so that we can express
Q_{0}(w,v)
as the summation of quartic terms and terms of divergence form; see Lemma
2.3
for more details.

Compared with the proof in
[
7
]
, we do not need to rely on an iteration procedure, and the key is to apply the Sobolev inequality (
2.10
) proved in
[
15
]
by Georgiev. This makes the presentation of the proof in the present paper neater.

Organisation

The article is organized as follows.

In this subsection, we state some preliminary estimates related to commutators and null forms

We first recall the well-known relations

\left[\Box,\Gamma_{k}\right]=\left[(\Box-1),\Gamma_{k}\right]=0,\quad\mbox{for}\ k=1,2,\dots,6.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any smooth function
m=m(t,x)

(i)

Estimate on commutators
. For all

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\left|\left[\partial_{\alpha},\Gamma^{I}\right]m\right|+\left|\left[S,\Gamma^{I}\right]m\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|J|<|I|}\sum_{\beta=0}^{2}\left|\partial_{\beta}\Gamma^{J}m\right|.

(2.1)

(ii)

Estimates on

and

.
We have

\langle t-r\rangle\left|\partial m\right|+\langle t+r\rangle\left|G_{i}m\right|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=1}\left(\left|Sm\right|+\left|\Gamma^{I}m\right|\right).

(2.2)

Proof.

Proof of (i). Note that, for
k=1,2,\dots,6
and

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{\alpha},\Gamma_{k}\right]m,\ \left[S,\Gamma_{k}\right]m

which implies (

) for all

Proof of (ii). By an elementary computation, for

\displaystyle\partial_{t}m

\displaystyle=(t^{2}-r^{2})^{-1}\left(tSm-x^{j}H_{j}m\right),

\displaystyle\partial_{i}m

\displaystyle=(t^{2}-r^{2})^{-1}\left(tH_{i}m-x_{i}Sm-(-1)^{i}x_{3-i}\Omega m\right),

\displaystyle G_{i}m

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\left(H_{i}m+(r-t)\partial_{i}m\right)=\frac{1}{t}\left(H_{i}m-\frac{x_{i}}{r}(r-t)\partial_{t}m\right).

2.3.
Global Sobolev inequality

In this subsection, we recall some Sobolev type inequalities associated with the vector field set

Lemma 2.5

(
[

22

Let

(i)

Standard Sobolev inequality

\left|u(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 2}\left\|\Gamma^{I}u(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

(2.8)

(ii)

Estimate inside of a cone.
For

\left|u(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 3}\left\|\Gamma^{I}u(t,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}.

(2.9)

(iii)

Global Sobolev inequality.
We have

\left|u(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 3}\left\|\Gamma^{I}u(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

For (
2.8
) and (

Remark 2.6
.

The inequality (
2.10
) by Georgiev can be used to get the pointwise decay of a nice function with

is required, which is more compatible with the coupled wave–Klein-Gordon equations. However, these two kinds of inequalities, only in the 2D case, yield the same decay rate of a function; and for higher dimensional case, the decay rate derived from Georgiev
[

]
is slower than the rate derived from the Klainerman-Sobolev inequality (see more details in
[

, Proposition 3]
).

Estimates on 2D linear wave equation

In this subsection, we recall several technical estimates for 2D linear wave equation. We start with the
L^{2}
and
L^{\infty}
estimates for 2D homogeneous wave equation. The proofs are similar to
[
28
, Theorem 4.3.1 and Theorem 4.6.1]
, but they are given for the sake of completeness and for the
readers’ convenience.

Lemma 2.7

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
u
be the solution to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Box u(t,x)&=0,\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}&=(u_{0},u_{1}).\end{aligned}\right.

(2.11)

Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

L^{2}
estimate on solution.
We have

\left\|u(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\left(\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\right).

(2.12)

(ii)

L^{\infty}
estimate on solution.
We have

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{W^{2,1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}}\right).

(2.13)

Proof.

Proof of (i). Taking the Fourier transform in the Cauchy problem with respect to the argument
x
, we have

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{2}\hat{u}(t,\xi)+|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}(t,\xi)=0,\\
&\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\quad\partial_{t}\hat{u}(0,\xi)=\hat{u}_{1}(\xi).\end{aligned}\right.

We solve the above second-order ODE in
t
to arrive at the expression of the solution
u
in Fourier space

\hat{u}(t,\xi)=\cos(t|\xi|)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi),\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,\xi)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Note that, from the Plancherel theorem and polar coordinate transformation,

\displaystyle\left\|\cos(t|\xi|)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\left\|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin^{2}r}{r}{\rm{d}}r\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

Therefore, using again the Plancherel theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}=\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\cos(t|\xi|)\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}+\left\|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}},

which implies (
2.12
).

Proof of (ii). From the expression of solutions for 2D linear wave equation, we decompose

u(t,x)=\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)+\mathcal{I}_{2}(t,x)\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

where

\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{u_{1}(y){\rm{d}}y}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}},\quad\mathcal{I}_{2}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{t}\int_{B(x,t)}\frac{u_{0}(y){\rm{d}}y}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x-y|^{2}}}.

Estimate on
\mathcal{I}_{1}
.

We claim that

\left|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}},\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.14)

Indeed, taking the change of variable
y\mapsto(x-y)
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,t)}\frac{u_{1}(x-y)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}{\rm{d}}y,\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.15)

Case I: Let
(t,x)\in[0,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We rewrite (
2.15
) as

\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-t}^{t}\int_{-\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}^{\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}\frac{u_{1}(x-(y_{1},y_{2}))}{\sqrt{t^{2}-y_{1}^{2}-y_{2}^{2}}}{\rm{d}}y_{2}{\rm{d}}y_{1}.

Note that, from the Newton-Leibniz formula and change of variable, we have

\displaystyle\int_{-t}^{t}\left\|u(x-(y_{1},y_{2}))\right\|_{L_{y_{2}}^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}y_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-t}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\partial_{2}u_{1}(x-(y_{1},s))\right|{\rm{d}}s{\rm{d}}y_{1}\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}},

\displaystyle\max_{y_{1}\in[-t,t]}\int_{-\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}^{\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}\frac{{\rm{d}}y_{2}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}-y^{2}_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\max_{y_{1}\in[-t,t]}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{{\rm{d}}s}{\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm{d}}s}{\sqrt{s}\sqrt{2-s}}\lesssim 1.

Combining the above estimates, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)\right|\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{-t}^{t}\left\|u(x-(y_{1},y_{2}))\right\|_{L_{y_{2}}^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}y_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\max_{y_{1}\in[-t,t]}\int_{-\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}^{\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}}}\frac{{\rm{d}}y_{2}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-y^{2}_{1}-y^{2}_{2}}}\right)\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}},

which implies (
2.14
) for this case.

Case II: Let
(t,x)\in(2,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We decompose

\mathcal{I}_{1}(t,x)=\mathcal{I}_{11}(t,x)+\mathcal{I}_{12}(t,x)\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in(2,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{11}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,{t-1})}\frac{u_{1}(x-y){\rm{d}}y}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}},\quad\mathcal{I}_{12}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,t)\setminus B(0,{t-1})}\frac{u_{1}(x-y){\rm{d}}y}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}.

First, we know that

\max_{y\in B(0,t-1)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}=\max_{y\in B(0,t-1)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+|y|}\sqrt{t-|y|}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}},

which implies

\left|\mathcal{I}_{11}(t,x)\right|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u(y)|{\rm{d}}y\left(\max_{y\in B(0,t-1)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}\right)\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

(2.16)

Second, by the polar coordinate transformation
y=\rho\omega
with
{\rm{d}}y=\rho{\rm{d}}\rho{\rm{d}}\omega
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{12}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\int_{t-1}^{t}\frac{\rho u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-\rho^{2}}}{\rm{d}}\rho{\rm{d}}\omega

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\int_{t-1}^{t}\frac{\rho u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t+\rho}}{\rm{d}}\sqrt{t-\rho}{\rm{d}}\omega.

Based on the above identity and integration by parts, we decompose

\mathcal{I}_{12}(t,x)=\mathcal{I}_{12}^{1}(t,x)+\mathcal{I}_{12}^{2}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{12}^{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\frac{(t-1)u_{1}(x-(t-1)\omega)}{\sqrt{2t-1}}{\rm{d}}\omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{12}^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\int_{t-1}^{t}\sqrt{t-\rho}\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\rho u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t+\rho}}\right){\rm{d}}\rho{\rm{d}}\omega.

Using again the Newton-Leibniz formula, we have

(t-1)u_{1}(x-(t-1)\omega)=-\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\left(u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)-\rho\omega\cdot\nabla u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)\right){\rm{d}}\rho,

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{12}^{1}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(2t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\left(|u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)|+|\rho\omega\cdot\nabla u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)|\right){\rm{d}}\rho{\rm{d}}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B(x,t-1)}\left(\frac{|u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|}+|\nabla u_{1}(y)|\right){\rm{d}}y\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}}.

On the other hand, by direct computation, we have

\partial_{\rho}\left(\frac{\rho u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t+\rho}}\right)=\frac{u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t+\rho}}-\rho\omega\cdot\frac{\nabla u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{\sqrt{t+\rho}}-\frac{\rho u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)}{2(t+\rho)^{\frac{3}{2}}},

which implies

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{I}_{12}^{2}(t,x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim(2t-1)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\int_{t-1}^{\infty}\left(|u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)|+\rho|\nabla u_{1}(x-\rho\omega)|\right){\rm{d}}\rho{\rm{d}}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B(x,t-1)}\left(\frac{|u_{1}(y)|}{|x-y|}+|\nabla u_{1}(y)|\right){\rm{d}}y\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}}.

Combining the above two estimates, we obtain

\left|\mathcal{I}_{12}(t,x)\right|\lesssim\left|\mathcal{I}_{12}^{1}(t,x)\right|+\left|\mathcal{I}_{12}^{2}(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{1}\|_{W^{1,1}}.

(2.17)

We see that (
2.14
) for this case follows from (
2.16
) and (
2.17
).

Estimate on
\mathcal{I}_{2}
.

We claim that

\left|\mathcal{I}_{2}(t,x)\right|\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{W^{2,1}},\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.18)

Indeed, taking the change of variable
y\mapsto x-ty
, we have

\mathcal{I}_{2}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\partial_{t}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{tu_{0}(x-ty)}{\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}}{\rm{d}}y=\mathcal{I}_{21}(t,x)+\mathcal{I}_{22}(t,x),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{21}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{u_{0}(x-ty)}{\sqrt{1-|y|^{2}}}{\rm{d}}y,\quad\quad\ \quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{22}(t,x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(0,t)}\frac{y\cdot\nabla u_{0}(x-y)}{t\sqrt{t^{2}-|y|^{2}}}{\rm{d}}y,\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Using a similar argument as in the proof of (
2.14
), one can obtain (
2.18
).

Combining (
2.14
) and (
2.18
), we complete the proof of (
2.13
).
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u
be the solution to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Box u(t,x)&=f(t,x),\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}&=(0,0),\end{aligned}\right.

with
f(t,x)
a sufficiently regular function. Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

L^{2}
estimate on solution.
We have

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{1}}+\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\right){\rm{d}}s.

(2.19)

(ii)

L^{\infty}
estimate on solution.
We have

\displaystyle\left\|u(t,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|Sf(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}{\rm{d}}s

(2.20)

\displaystyle+\langle t\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{|I|\leq 1}\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\Gamma^{I}f(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}}{\rm{d}}s.

Proof.

Proof of (i). Taking the Fourier transform for the Cauchy problem and using the Duhamel’s principle,

\hat{u}(t,\xi)=\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,\xi)+\mathcal{I}_{4}(t,\xi),\quad t\in[0,\infty),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,\xi)=\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{f}(s,\xi){\rm{d}}s,\quad t\in[0,\infty),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{4}(t,\xi)=\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-s)|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\hat{f}(s,\xi){\rm{d}}s,\quad t\in[0,\infty).

Note that, from the Plancherel theorem

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{f}(s,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\left\|\frac{\sin(t-s)|\xi|}{|\xi|}\textbf{1}_{\{|\xi|\geq 1\}}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}{\rm{d}}s\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|{f}(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{I}_{4}(t,\xi)\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|\hat{f}(s,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{\infty}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin^{2}r}{r}{\rm{d}}r\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}s\lesssim\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\int_{0}^{t}\|{f}(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{1}}{\rm{d}}s.

Therefore, using again the Plancherel theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=\|\hat{u}(t,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\mathcal{I}_{3}(t,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}+\|\mathcal{I}_{4}(t,\xi)\|_{L_{\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,x)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}{\rm{d}}s+\log^{\frac{1}{2}}(2+t)\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{1}}{\rm{d}}s,

which implies (
2.19
).

Proof of (ii). Estimate (
2.20
) is due to Hörmander
[
16
]
. We also refer to
[
28
, Theorem 4.6.2]
for details of the proof.
∎

To establish the energy estimates of the wave equation for future reference, we first introduce the standard energy
\mathcal{E}
and the conformal energy
\mathcal{G}
for the 2D wave equation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\partial_{t}u)^{2}+(\partial_{1}u)^{2}+(\partial_{2}u)^{2}\right)(t,x){\rm{d}}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(t,u)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bigg((Su+u)^{2}+(\Omega u)^{2}+\sum_{i=1,2}({H}_{i}u)^{2}\bigg)(t,x){\rm{d}}x.

Now we recall the energy estimates for 2D wave equation from
[
3
,
32
]
.

Lemma 2.9

(
[
3
,
32
]
)
.

Let
u
be the solution to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Box u(t,x)&=f(t,x),\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}&=(u_{0},u_{1}),\end{aligned}\right.

with
f(t,x)
a sufficiently regular function. Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

Standard energy estimate.
We have

\mathcal{E}(t,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s.

(2.21)

(ii)

Conformal energy estimate.
We have

\mathcal{G}(t,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{G}(0,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\langle s+r\rangle f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s.

(2.22)

Proof.

Proof of (i). First, we can rewrite the product
\left(-\Box u\right)\partial_{t}u
as the following divergence form,

\left(-\Box u\right)\partial_{t}u=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{2}\partial_{t}(\partial_{\alpha}u)^{2}-\partial^{i}\left(\partial_{i}u\partial_{t}u\right).

Integrating the above identity on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
for any
t>0
, and using
-\Box u=f

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t,u)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(0,u)+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|\partial_{t}u(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{E}(0,u)+2\int_{0}^{t}\left\|f(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\mathcal{E}(s,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}{\rm{d}}s.

Based on the above inequality, we obtain (
2.21
).

Proof of (ii). Here, we briefly sketch the proof and refer to
[
3
, Theorem 6.11]
for the complete proof.
Consider the nonspacelike multiplier

K_{0}=(r^{2}+t^{2})\partial_{t}+2rt\partial_{r}\quad\mbox{where}\ \partial_{r}=\frac{x^{i}}{r}\partial_{i}.

By an elementary computation, we have

\displaystyle-\Box u\left(K_{0}u+tu\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\bigg((Su+u)^{2}+(\Omega u)^{2}+({H}_{1}u)^{2}+({H}_{2}u)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\partial^{i}\left(tx_{i}\left(-(\partial_{t}u)^{2}+(\partial_{1}u)^{2}+(\partial_{2}u)^{2}\right)-tu\partial_{i}u\right)

\displaystyle-\partial^{i}\left((r^{2}+t^{2})(\partial_{t}u)(\partial_{i}u)+2rt(\partial_{r}u)(\partial_{i}u)+{\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}}(x_{i}u^{2})\right).

Integrating the above identity on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
and then using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we see that

\mathcal{G}(t,u)-\mathcal{G}(0,u)\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\langle s+r\rangle f(s,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|\langle s+r\rangle^{-1}\left(K_{0}u+tu\right)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s.

From the definition of
K_{0}
, we observe that

K_{0}u=tSu+r(t\partial_{r}u+r\partial_{t}u)\Rightarrow\left|K_{0}u+tu\right|\lesssim\langle t+r\rangle\left(|Su+u|+|H_{1}u|+|H_{2}u|\right).

Combining the above inequalities, we obtain (
2.22
).
∎

Last, we recall the extra decay for Hessian of 2D inhomogeneous wave equation.
For the convenience of the reader, we revisit the complete proof in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Extra decay for Hessian)
.

Let
u
be the solution of the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Box u(t,x)&=f(t,x),\\
(u,\partial_{t}u)_{|t=0}&=(u_{0},u_{1}).\end{aligned}\right.

Then we have

\left|\partial\partial u\right|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=0,1}\frac{1}{\langle t-r\rangle}\left|\partial\Gamma^{I}u\right|+\frac{t}{\langle t-r\rangle}|f|,\quad\mbox{for}\ r\leq 2t.

(2.23)

Proof.

Case I: Let
t\in[0,1]
. It is easily seen that

\left|\partial\partial u\right|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=0,1}\left|\partial\Gamma^{I}u\right|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=0,1}\frac{1}{\langle t-r\rangle}\left|\partial\Gamma^{I}u\right|+\frac{t}{\langle t-r\rangle}|f|,\quad\mbox{for}\ r\leq 2t.

(2.24)

Case II: Let
t\in(1,\infty)
. We first express the wave operator
-\Box
by
\partial_{\alpha}
and
H_{i}
to obtain

\displaystyle-\Box=\frac{(t-r)(t+r)}{t^{2}}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}+\frac{x^{i}}{t^{2}}\partial_{t}H_{i}-\frac{1}{t}\partial^{i}H_{i}+\frac{2}{t}\partial_{t}-\frac{x^{i}}{t^{2}}\partial_{i}.

Based on the above identity and
-\Box u=f
, we have

\left|\partial_{t}\partial_{t}u\right|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=0,1}\frac{1}{\langle r-t\rangle}\left|\partial\Gamma^{I}u\right|+\frac{t^{2}}{\langle r-t\rangle\langle r+t\rangle}|f|,\quad\mbox{for}\ r\leq 2t.

(2.25)

On the other hand, for
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, we have the following two identities

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle={-\frac{x_{i}}{t}}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{t}\partial_{t}{H}_{i}-\frac{1}{t}\partial_{i},

\displaystyle\partial_{i}\partial_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{t^{2}}\partial_{t}\partial_{t}-\frac{x_{i}}{t^{2}}\partial_{t}H_{j}+\frac{1}{t}\partial_{j}H_{i}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{t}\partial_{t}+\frac{x_{i}}{t^{2}}\partial_{j}.

Based on the above two identities and (
2.25
), we have

|\partial_{i}\partial_{t}u|+|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}u|\lesssim\sum_{|I|=0,1}\frac{1}{\langle r-t\rangle}\left|\partial\Gamma^{I}u\right|+\frac{t^{2}}{\langle r-t\rangle\langle r+t\rangle}|f|,\quad\mbox{for}\ r\leq 2t.

(2.26)

We see that (
2.23
) follows from (
2.24
), (
2.25
) and (
2.26
).
∎

Remark 2.11
.

The above Lemma states that, for the solution
u
of wave equation, the Hessian form
\partial\partial u
has extra
\langle t-r\rangle^{-1}
decay than
\partial u
in the spacetime region
\left\{(t,x):r\leq 2t\right\}
if the source term
f
has sufficiently fast decay. Note that, this extra decay can be used to obtain the sharp pointwise decay for
\partial w
thanks to the hidden divergence form structure in the wave equation of
w
(see more details in §
3.3
).

\delta_{0}>0

\sum_{|I|\leq 4}\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\langle s\rangle^{\delta_{0}}\|\langle s+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I}f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq C_{f},

then we have

\langle t+r\rangle|u(t,x)|\lesssim\frac{C_{f}}{1-2^{-\delta_{0}}}+\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle|x|\rangle\log(2+|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

(2.29)

Proof.

From
\mbox{supp}\psi_{0}\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,2]
and
\mbox{supp}\psi_{j}=[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]
for
j\geq 1
, we infer

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle|x|\rangle\psi_{j}(|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle x\rangle\log(2+|x|)\psi^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j}(|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\left\|\frac{\psi_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|x|)}{\log(2+|x|)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(j+1)}\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle|x|\rangle\log(2+|x|)\psi^{\frac{1}{2}}_{j}(|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\quad\mbox{for}\ j\geq 0.

Based on the above estimates, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\sum\psi_{j}=1
,

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle|x|\rangle\psi_{j}(|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{|I|\leq 5}\left\|\langle|x|\rangle\log(2+|x|)\Gamma^{I}u(0,x)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}.

(2.30)

On the other hand, using again the definition of
\psi_{j}
and (
2.28
), for
j\geq 0
, we have

\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\psi_{j}(s)\left\|\langle s+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I}f(s,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim C_{f}\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\psi_{j}(s)\langle s\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}\lesssim C_{f}2^{-j\delta_{0}},

which implies

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|I|\leq 4}\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\psi_{j}(s)\left\|\langle s+|x|\rangle\Gamma^{I}f(s,x)\right\|_{L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim C_{f}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{-j\delta_{0}}\lesssim\frac{C_{f}}{1-2^{\delta_{0}}}.

(2.31)

We see that (
2.29
) follows from (
2.30
) and (
2.31
).
∎

Similar to the case of 2D linear wave equation, we introduce the following standard energy

for the 2D linear Klein-Gordon equation,

\mathcal{E}_{1}(t,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left((\partial_{t}u)^{2}+(\partial_{1}u)^{2}+(\partial_{2}u)^{2}+u^{2}\right)(t,x){\rm{d}}x.

Now we introduce the energy estimates for 2D Klein-Gordon equation.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
u
be the solution to the Cauchy problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(-\Box+1\right)u(t,x)&=f(t,x),\\
(u(0,x),\partial_{t}u(0,x))&=(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x)),\end{aligned}\right.

with

a sufficiently regular function. Then the following estimates hold.

(i)

Standard energy estimate.
We have

\mathcal{E}_{1}(t,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{1}(0,u)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}{\rm{d}}s.

(2.32)

(ii)

Ghost weight estimate (see also
[

]

For all
\delta_{0}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,2}\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\frac{u^{2}}{\langle r-s\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{|G_{i}u|^{2}}{\langle r-s\rangle^{\frac{3}{2}}}\right){\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}s

(2.33)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{E}_{1}(0,u)+\int_{0}^{t}\langle s\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}\|f(s,x)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|\partial_{t}u(s,x)\|_{L^{2}_{x}}{\rm{d}}s.

Proof.

Proof of (i). The proof is similar to Lemma
2.9
(i), and we omit it.

Proof of (ii). Set

q(t,r)=\int_{-\infty}^{r-t}\langle s\rangle^{-\frac{3}{2}}{\rm{d}}s\quad\mbox{for}\ (t,r)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We apply the multiplier

and obtain:

\displaystyle\langle t\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}e^{q}\partial_{t}u\left(-\Box u+u\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\bigg(\langle t\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}e^{q}\bigg(\sum_{\alpha=0}^{3}(\partial_{\alpha}u)^{2}+u^{2}\bigg)\bigg)-\partial^{i}\left(\langle t\rangle^{-\delta_{0}}e^{q}\partial_{t}u\partial_{i}u\right)

Integrating the above identity on

∎

Type-II see-saw at μ + \mu^{+} μ − \mu^{-} collider
Siddharth P. Maharathy, Manimala Mitra

Abstract
Doubly-charged Higgs bosons have extensively been searched at the LHC. In this work, we study the sensitivity reach of the doubly-charged scalar ( H ± ⁣ ± H^{\pm\pm} ) in muon collider for the well-known Type-II seesaw scenario. First, we perform a cut-based analysis to predict the discovery prospect in the muon collider operating with 3 TeV center of mass energy. In addition to this, we have also performed a multivariate analysis and compare the cut-based result with the result obtained from the multivariate analysis. We find that the cut-based analysis is more significant as compared to the multivariate analysis in the large doubly-charged scalar mass region. We predict that a doubly-charged scalar mass, M H ± ⁣ ± M_{H^{\pm\pm}} , upto 1450 GeV can be probed with 5 ​ σ 5\sigma significance for center of mass s = 3 \sqrt{s}=3 TeV and integrated luminosity ℒ = 1000 ​ fb − 1 \mathcal{L}=1000\,\textrm{fb}^{-1} .

II
Model

The Type-II seesaw model has an extended scalar sector, where in addition to the SM scalar doublet,
\Phi=(\Phi^{+}\,\,\Phi^{0})^{T}
, a
SU(2)
triplet scalar with hyper-charge Y=1 is also present.

\displaystyle\Delta=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}&\Delta^{++}\\
\Delta^{0}&-\frac{\Delta^{+}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{pmatrix}

(1)

The neutral components of the scalars are parameterised as
\Phi^{0}=(v_{\Phi}+\phi^{0}+iZ_{1})/\sqrt{2}
and
\Delta^{0}=(v_{\Delta}+\delta^{0}+iZ_{2})/\sqrt{2}
. The vacuum expectation values (
vevs
) of the SM doublet and the BSM triplet are
v_{\Phi}
and
v_{\Delta}
respectively, and they satisfy the relation
v=\sqrt{v^{2}_{\Phi}+2v^{2}_{\Delta}}=246\,\,\rm{GeV}
.

The kinetic Lagrangian of the SM scalar doublet
\Phi
and the scalar triplet
\Delta
has the following form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm{kin}}(\Phi,\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\Phi)+\rm{Tr}[(D_{\mu}\Delta)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\Delta)]~.

(2)

In the above, the covariant derivatives are given by,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\mu}+i\frac{g}{2}\tau^{a}W_{\mu}^{a}+i\frac{g^{\prime}}{2}B_{\mu}\right)\Phi~,

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\Delta+i\frac{g}{2}[\tau^{a}W_{\mu}^{a},\Delta]+ig^{\prime}B_{\mu}\Delta~.

(3)

The scalar potential of the model is,

\displaystyle V(\Phi,\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-m_{\Phi}^{2}\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi+\tilde{M}^{2}_{\Delta}\rm{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)

(4)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left(\mu\Phi^{T}i\tau_{2}\Delta^{\dagger}\Phi+\rm{h.c.}\right)+\frac{\lambda}{4}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)\rm{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)+\lambda_{2}\left[\rm{Tr}(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}\rm{Tr}[(\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta)^{2}]+\lambda_{4}\Phi^{\dagger}\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}\Phi~.

After the symmetry breaking the charged scalars and neutral scalars mix resulting several physical mass eigen states. The masses can be obtained by
diagonalising with rotation matrices,
R^{\pm}
,
R^{0}
and
R^{0}_{A}
, respectively:

\displaystyle R^{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{\beta{{}_{\pm}}}&-s_{\beta{{}_{\pm}}}\\
s_{\beta{{}_{\pm}}}&c_{\beta{{}_{\pm}}}\end{array}\right),R^{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{\beta_{0}}&-s_{\beta_{0}}\\
s_{\beta_{0}}&c_{\beta_{0}}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle R^{0}_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{\alpha}&-s_{\alpha}\\
s_{\alpha}&c_{\alpha}\end{array}\right)

where
c_{\beta_{\pm}}
=
\cos\beta_{\pm}
,
c_{\beta_{0}}
=
\cos\beta_{0}
,
c_{\alpha}
=
\cos{\alpha}
. The mixing angles
\beta_{\pm}
,
\beta_{0}
and
\alpha
have the following forms:

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\tan\beta_{\pm}=\tan\beta_{0}=\frac{2v_{\Delta}}{v_{\Phi}},~~~\tan 2\alpha=\frac{2B}{A-C}

(13)

where
A=\frac{\lambda v_{\Phi}^{2}}{2}
,
B=-\sqrt{2}\mu v_{\Phi}+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{4})v_{\Phi}v_{\Delta}
and
C=\frac{\mu v_{\Phi}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}v_{\Delta}}+2(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})v_{\Delta}^{2}
.
In the above

represents the rotation matrix between charged scalar eigenstates,

represent the rotation matrix between the CP-even and CP-odd neutral scalar states, respectively.

In addition to the three Goldstone bosons

,

,

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Z_{1}^{0}\\

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi^{0}\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R^{0}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h\\

A detailed discussion on the mass spectrum of the physical scalars of the model has been presented in
Arhrib et al. 2011

\displaystyle M^{2}_{H^{\pm\pm}}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\tilde{M}^{2}_{\Delta}-\frac{\lambda_{4}}{2}v_{\Phi}^{2},~~~M^{2}_{H^{\pm}}\simeq\tilde{M}^{2}_{\Delta}-\frac{\lambda_{4}}{4}v_{\Phi}^{2},

\displaystyle 2v_{\Phi}^{2}\lambda,~~~~M^{2}_{H}\approx M^{2}_{H}\simeq\tilde{M}^{2}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle M^{2}_{H^{\pm}}-M^{2}_{H^{\pm\pm}}\approx M^{2}_{H,A}-M^{2}_{H^{\pm}}\approx\frac{\lambda_{4}}{4}v_{\Phi}^{2}

III.1.3
Multivariate Analysis (MVA)

In this section we discuss the results of the multivariate analysis (MVA)
Hocker et al. 2007
that we have performed. Fig.
11
shows the kinematic variables used for the MVA analysis assuming the variables are having a good discriminating power between the signal and background, and have low correlations among themselves. A detailed discussion about the variables used has already been presented in the text. The method-unspecific separation is a good measure of the discriminating power of any variable, for a given feature
y

Hocker et al. 2007
. This is defined as,

\displaystyle\left<S^{2}\right>=\frac{1}{2}\int dy\frac{(\hat{y}_{S}(y)-\hat{y}_{B}(y))^{2}}{\hat{y}_{S}(y)+\hat{y}_{B}(y)}

(34)

where
\hat{y}_{S}
and
\hat{y}_{B}
are the probability functions for the signal and background for the particular feature
y
. The quantity is equal to zero for similar signal and background distributions, and 1 for distributions with no overlaps. We have considered those variables having method-unspecific separation value greater than 1%. In Table.
2
, we present the method-unspecific separation for the input variables. In Fig.
9
, we have shown the Pearson’s linear correlation coefficients between the input variables, defined as,

\displaystyle\rho(x,y)=\frac{\left<xy\right>-\left<x\right>\left<y\right>}{\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}}

(35)

where
\left<x\right>
and
\sigma_{x}
are the expectation value and standard deviation respectively for the dataset
x
.

In Table.
3
, we have summarised the relevant BDT hyperparameters. We have optimised the BDT hyperparameters with
adaptive boost
algorithm with a learning rate of 0.1. The so-called
Gini index
has been used for the separation between the nodes in decision trees. In Table.
2

IV
Summary

We have discussed the discovery prospects of the doubly-charged scalar,
H^{\pm\pm}
, present in Type-II seesaw model in
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
collider. We mainly focus our attention to that part of the parameter space where the produced doubly-charged scalar mainly decays into
W^{\pm}W^{\pm}
final state and subsequently we consider the hadronic final states from the
W
decays. All hadronic final states are not favourable channel to probe at the LHC due to the presence of the towering QCD backgrounds. Hence this region is more favorable for linear colliders,
viz

e^{+}e^{-}
and
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
collider. Among these two, the
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
collider has a low energy loss due to synchrotron radiation. Firstly, we have performed the trivial cut-based analysis and predict the statistical significance of the muon collider at
\sqrt{s}=3
TeV. Secondly, we have also performed a multivariate analysis and compared both the results. From the cut-based analysis we have concluded that at 1000
\textrm{fb}^{-1}
luminosity up to 1450 GeV massive doubly-charged scalar can be discovered with
5\sigma
confidence level, see Fig.
8
. The result from the multivariate analysis for different
M_{H}^{\pm\pm}
value has been given in Table.
4
, from which it is evident that, apart from a higher mass of 1.450 TeV, BDT analysis offers a larger statistical significance, greater than
5\sigma
.

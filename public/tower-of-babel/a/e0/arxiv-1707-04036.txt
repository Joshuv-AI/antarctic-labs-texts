Vanishing theorems of Kodaira type for Witt canonical sheaves
Hiromu Tanaka

Abstract
. Given a smooth projective variety over a perfect field of positive characteristic, we prove that the higher cohomologies vanish for the tensor product of the Witt canonical sheaf and the Teichmüller lift of an ample invertible sheaf. We also give a generalisation of this vanishing theorem to one of Kawamata-Viehweg type.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
14F30, 14F17.

1.
Introduction

One of deep results of complex algebraic geometry
is the degeneration of Hodge to de Rham spectral sequence.
Indeed, it is related to several important results such as the Hodge decomposition and
the Kodaira vanishing theorem.
Unfortunately Kodaira vanishing is known to fail in positive characteristic
[
Ray78
]
.
On the other hand,
one of positive-characteristic counterparts of the Hodge decomposition
is the slope decomposition of the Crystalline cohomologies by
the de Rham–Witt complex
[
Ill79
]
.
Thus it is natural to seek a positive-characteristic analogue of the Kodaira vanishing theorem in terms of de Rham–Witt complex.
One of the purposes of this paper is to propose candidates as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(cf. Theorem
4.2
, Theorem
4.17
)
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be an
N
-dimensional smooth projective connected scheme over
k
.
Let
A
be an ample invertible sheaf on
X
and let
\underline{A}
be
the Teichmüller lift of
A
(cf. Definition
3.10
).
Then

(1)

H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A})=0

for any positive integer
i
, and

(2)

H^{j}(X,\underline{A}^{-1})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0
for any integer
j
satisfying
j<N
.

Remark 1.2
.

We actually need to take the tensor product with
\mathbb{Q}
in the statement (2) of Theorem
1.1
, as
H^{j}(X,\underline{A}^{-1})
does not vanish in general
(cf. Proposition
5.4
).

Since we have established an analogue of the Kodaira vanishing theorem,
it is tempting to find analogous results related to the Kodaira vanishing theorem.
In this direction, we shall give a generalisation of Theorem
1.1

that can be considered as a vanishing theorem of Kawamata–Viehweg type.

Theorem 1.3

(cf. Corollary
4.16
, Theorem
4.17
)
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
.
Let
f:X\to Y
be a projective
k
-morphism
from an
N
-dimensional connected scheme
X
smooth over
k

to a scheme
Y
of finite type over
k
.
Let
A
be an
f
-ample
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X

such that
{\operatorname{Supp}}\,\{A\}
is simple normal crossing.
Let
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(-A)
be the Witt divisorial sheaf associated to
-A

(cf. Definition
3.1
).
Then

(1)

R^{i}f_{*}(\mathcal{H}om_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(W\mathcal{O}_{X}(-A),W\Omega_{X}^{N}))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0

for any positive integer
i
, and

(2)

if the induced morphism
Y\to{\operatorname{Spec}}\,k
is an isomorphism, then

H^{j}(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(-A))\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0

for any integer
j
satisfying
j<N
.

Remark 1.4
.

One of the forms of the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem in characteristic zero
is the following vanishing

R^{i}f_{*}(\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(-A),\Omega_{X}^{N}))=0

under the same assumption as in Theorem
1.3
except for the characteristic of
k
.
Indeed, this is the relative version of
[
KMM87
, Corollary 1-2-2]
.
Therefore, Theorem
1.3
can be considered as a Witt analogue of the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem.

Remark 1.5
.

If
A
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
-divisor, then
(1) of Theorem
1.3
holds even without
(-)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
(Theorem
4.15
).
The author does not know whether we may drop
(-)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
in general.

Remark 1.6
.

We now summarise open problems related to Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.3
.
Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be an
N
-dimensional smooth projective connected scheme over
k
.

(1)

If
A
is a nef and big invertible sheaf on
X
, then
does it hold that
H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0
for
i>0
?

(2)

If
A
is an ample invertible sheaf on
X
, then
does it hold that
H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{j}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A})=0
for

?

The original motivation of the author was to prove (1), as its relative version
deduces the Grauert–Riemenschneider vanishing for Witt canonical sheaves.
Contrary to the situation in characteristic zero, Theorem

does not seem to be enough to conclude (1).

It is worth mentioning that
the cohomologies of the Teichmüller lifts of invertible sheaves
can be infinite dimensional over

.
More specifically, we shall prove the following statement.

Theorem 1.7

(cf. Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and set
Q:=W(k)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.
Let

be a projective scheme over

such that
\dim X>0
.
Let

be an ample invertible sheaf on

\underline{A}
be
the Teichmüller lift of

Then the following hold.

(1)

H^{i}(X,\underline{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}=0

.

(2)

\dim_{Q}(H^{0}(X,\underline{A})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})=\infty

(3)

1.1.
Description of proofs

1.1.1.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

Let us overview some of the ideas of the proof of Theorem
1.1
(1).
The argument consists of three steps.
The first step is to twist the finite levels
W_{n}(\bullet)
by Frobenius
in order to achieve the vanishing, i.e. we can find a positive integer
t
such that

H^{i}(X,W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F_{X}^{t})^{*}\underline{A})\simeq H^{i}(X,W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A}^{p^{t}})=0

for any
i>0
and
n>0
, where
F_{X}:X\to X
is the absolute Frobenius morphism.
We can find such a positive integer
t
by the exact sequence (cf. the proof of Theorem
4.2
(2)):

0\to\Omega_{X}^{N}\to W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{N}\to(F_{X})_{*}(W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N})\to 0.

The second step is to go to the infinite level
W(\bullet)
from the finite levels
W_{n}(\bullet)
,
i.e. we take the projective limit:

H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F_{X}^{t})^{*}\underline{A})\simeq\varprojlim_{n}H^{i}(X,W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F_{X}^{t})^{*}\underline{A})=0,

where the above isomorphism holds by the Mittag–Leffler condition.
The final step is to untwist the infinite level
W(\bullet)
by Frobenius, i.e.

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,(F^{t}_{X})_{*}(W\Omega_{X}^{N})\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}\underline{A})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,(F^{t}_{X})_{*}(W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F^{t}_{X})^{*}\underline{A}))

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle H^{i}(X,W\Omega_{X}^{N}\otimes_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(F^{t}_{X})^{*}\underline{A})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

We can apply such an untwisting argument by using the
W\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear isomorphism

W\Omega_{X}^{N}\simeq(F^{t}_{X})_{*}(W\Omega_{X}^{N})
(Theorem
2.9
).

1.1.2.
The relative version of Theorem
1.1

In Theorem
4.2
, we shall establish the relative version of Theorem
1.1
.
The proof is identical to the above argument in Subsubsection
1.1.1

except for the second step, i.e. the step taking the projective limit.
This is because we can not use the Mittag–Leffler criterion any more.
For the details on this argument, see the proof of Theorem
4.2
(3).

1.1.3.
Sketch of the proof of Theorem
1.3

We now overview the outline of the proof of Theorem
1.3
(1).
The main idea is to apply Kawamata’s covering trick,
as in Kawamata’s original proof of the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem.
To this end, we first take a base change
f:X^{\prime}:=X\times_{k}k^{\prime}\to X

for a suitable finite extension
k\subset k^{\prime}
.
Applying Kawamata’s covering trick
[
KMM87
, Theorem 1-1-1]
,
we obtain a finite Galois cover
g:X^{\prime\prime}\to X^{\prime}

such that
[K(X^{\prime\prime}):K(X^{\prime})]
is not divisible by
p
and
the pullback of
A
to
X^{\prime\prime}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor.
Let
\varphi:Z^{\prime}\to Z
be one of
f
and
g
.
For
V\in\{Z,Z^{\prime}\}
, we denote by
A_{V}
the pullback of
A
to
V
.
Roughly speaking, the problem is reduced to prove the following two assertions:

(I)

W\mathcal{O}_{Z}(-A_{Z})\to\varphi_{*}W\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}(-A_{Z^{\prime}})
splits.

(II)

The induced homomorphism

\mathcal{H}om_{W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\varphi_{*}W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}(-A_{Z^{\prime}}),W_{n}\Omega_{Z}^{N})\to\mathcal{H}om_{W\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\varphi_{*}W\mathcal{O}_{Z^{\prime}}(-A_{Z^{\prime}}),W_{n}\Omega_{Z}^{N})

is an isomorphism.

The proof of (I) is rather standard.
More specifically, we shall check that
several standard arguments on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
can be applied for
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
.
For more details, see Subsection
4.2
.

Subsection
4.3
is devoted to prove (II),
which is the most technical part in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
We introduce notion of weakly
\ell
-cyclic morphisms
in order to treat the two cases
\varphi=f
and
\varphi=g
simultaneously (Definition
4.8
).
For the technical details, see the proofs of Lemma
4.10
and Proposition
4.11
.

1.2.
Witt divisorial sheaves

Another novelty of this paper is the notion of Witt divisorial sheaves

W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
and
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D
.
Section
3
is devoted to establishing their fundamental properties.
If
D
is a Cartier divisor, then
the Witt divisorial sheaf
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
coincides with the Teichmüller lift of
the invertible sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
(Proposition
3.12
).
The author introduces this notion to find a Witt analogue of
the Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem (Theorem
1.3
).
It is remarkable that
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, for certain special cases, has already appeared in the study of Swan conductor by Brylinski–Kato (cf. Remark
3.4
).
In this sense, the Witt divisorial sheaves naturally appear in different situations.
The author hopes that
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
will produce further applications in future.

2.2.
Iterated Cartier operators

In this subsection,
we recall definition of
B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and
Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
.
Our definition slightly differs from the one of
[
Ill79
, Ch. 0, Section 2.2]
(Remark
2.1
).

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be a smooth scheme over
k
.
It is easy to check that the Frobenius push-forward of
the de Rham complex:

0\to(F_{X})_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\xrightarrow{d_{0}}(F_{X})_{*}\Omega^{1}_{X}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}(F_{X})_{*}\Omega^{2}_{X}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}\cdots

is a complex of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module homomorphisms,
where
\Omega^{r}_{X}:=\bigwedge^{r}\Omega^{1}_{X/\mathbb{Z}}
.
Since
k
is a perfect field,
it holds that
\bigwedge^{r}\Omega^{1}_{X/\mathbb{Z}}\simeq\bigwedge^{r}\Omega^{1}_{X/k}
.
For any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we set

B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}:={\rm Im}\,(d_{r-1}),\quad Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}:={\operatorname{Ker}}\,(d_{r}).

In particular, both
B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
and
Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}

are coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}
.
It is well known that there is an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module homomorphisms:

0\to B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\to Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\xrightarrow{C}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to 0.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and assume that we have already defined

B_{n}\Omega^{r}_{X}
and
Z_{n}\Omega^{r}_{X}
as coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}
.
We have

0\to(F^{n}_{X})_{*}B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\to(F^{n}_{X})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\xrightarrow{C_{n}}(F^{n}_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to 0,

where
C_{n}:=(F^{n}_{X})_{*}C
. We set

B_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}:=(C_{n})^{-1}(B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}),\quad Z_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}:=(C_{n})^{-1}(Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}).

In particular, both
B_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and
Z_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
are
coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
(F_{X}^{n+1})_{*}\Omega^{r}_{X}
.
For convenience, we set

B_{0}\Omega_{X}^{r}:=0,\quad Z_{0}\Omega_{X}^{r}:=\Omega_{X}^{r}.

Remark 2.1
.

We define
B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and
Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}

in a slightly different way from the one of
[
Ill79
, Ch. 0, Section 2.2]
.
Two definitions are the same up to the base change
induced by the absolute Frobenius morphism of the base field

In other words, we treat the absolute version, whilst
[
Ill79
]
considers the relative one.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be a smooth scheme over
k
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
the sheaves
B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and

Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
are coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

(2)

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
there are exact sequences of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules:

0\to(F^{n}_{X})_{*}B_{1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to B_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to 0,

0\to(F^{n}_{X})_{*}B_{1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to Z_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to 0.

(3)

(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and

(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
are coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

Proof.

The assertion (1) holds by
[
Ill79
, Ch. 0, Proposition 2.2.8(a)]
(cf. Remark
2.1
).
The assertion (2) follows from the construction.

Let us show (3) by induction on
n
.
We first treat the case when
n=1
.
By the exact sequence

0\to Z_{1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}\xrightarrow{d}B_{1}\Omega_{X}^{r+1}\to 0,

it follows from (1) that
(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{1}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
By the exact sequence

0\to B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\to Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}\xrightarrow{C}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to 0,

also
Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}/B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Furthermore, we have the following exact sequence

0\to\frac{Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}}{B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}}\to\frac{(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}}{B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}}\to\frac{(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}}{Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}}\to 0.

Since an extension of coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module,
also
(F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
This completes the proof for the case when
n=1
.

Assume that

(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/B_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
and

(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
are coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.
We have the following commutative diagram in which each horizontal sequence is exact:

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>(F_{X}^{n})_{*}B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}@>{}>{}>(F_{X}^{n})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}@>{C_{n}}>{}>(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}@>{}>{}>0\\
\Big\|@A{}A{\rm inclusion}A@A{}A{\rm inclusion}A\\
0@>{}>{}>(F_{X}^{n})_{*}B_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}@>{}>{}>Z_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}@>{C_{n}}>{}>Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}@>{}>{}>0.\\
\end{CD}

Since
(F_{X}^{n})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module,
the snake lemma implies that
(F_{X}^{n})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}/Z_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}

is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
By the case of
n=1
,

(F_{X}^{n+1})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/(F_{X}^{n})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}=(F_{X}^{n})_{*}((F_{X})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X})

is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Hence, an extension
(F_{X}^{n+1})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/Z_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
of
(F_{X}^{n+1})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/(F_{X}^{n})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
and
(F_{X}^{n})_{*}Z_{1}\Omega^{r}_{X}/Z_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
By a similar argument, we can show that
(F_{X}^{n+1})_{*}\Omega_{X}^{r}/B_{n+1}\Omega^{r}_{X}
is a coherent locally free
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
∎

2.4.
De Rham–Witt complex

In this subsection, we summarise some of results established by
[
Ill79
]
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
.
We have de Rham-Witt sheaves
W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
on
X
,
which are
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.
De Rham-Witt sheaves are equipped with

W\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module homomorphisms
(cf.
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, Définition 1.1(V2’), Théorème 1.3, Théorème 2.17]
):

F:W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to(F_{X})_{*}(W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}),

V:(F_{X})_{*}(W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r})\to W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r},

R:W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}.

Note that the above map
F
is
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear because

F:\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{r}\to\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a homorphism of graded algebras
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, Théorème 2.17]
.
Also
V
is
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear by
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, (1.15.2)]
.
Furthermore,
R
is surjective
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, Théorème 1.3]
.
Then we set
W\Omega_{X}^{r}:=\varprojlim_{n}W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
,
where we consider
\{W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
as a projective system via
R
.
If
X
is an
N
-dimensional smooth variety over
k
,
then it is known that
W_{n}X
is Cohen–Macaulay and
W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N}
is
a dualising sheaf of
W_{n}X

[
Eke84
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be a scheme of finite type over
k
.
Then
W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a coherent
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In particular, also
(F_{X}^{e})_{*}W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a coherent
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
for any
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

By
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, Proposition 1.13.1]
,
each
W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r}
is a quasi-coherent
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Assuming
X={\operatorname{Spec}}\,A
, it suffices to show that

W_{n}\Omega_{A}^{r}=\Gamma(X,W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{r})
is a finitely generated
W_{n}(A)
-module.
By
[
Ill79
, Théorème 1.3]
,
there is a surjecitve
W_{n}(A)
-module homomorphism

\Omega_{W_{n}(A)/\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\to W_{n}\Omega^{r}_{A}.

Hence, it is enough to prove that
\Omega_{W_{n}(A)/\mathbb{Z}}^{1}
is
a finitely generated
W_{n}(A)
-module.
By
[
Mat89
, Theorem 25.1]
,
we have an exact sequence:

\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(k)/\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{W_{n}(k)}W_{n}(A)\to\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/\mathbb{Z}}\to\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/W_{n}(k)}\to 0.

Since
\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(k)/\mathbb{Z}}=0
(cf.
[
Ill79
, Ch. I, Lemma 1.7]
),
we get an isomorphism:

\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/\mathbb{Z}}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/W_{n}(k)}.

Since
W_{n}(A)
is a finitely generated
W_{n}(k)
-algebra (
[
Ill79
, Corollaire 1.5.7]
),

\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/W_{n}(k)}
is a finitely generated
W_{n}(A)
-module,
hence so is
\Omega^{1}_{W_{n}(A)/\mathbb{Z}}
.
∎

Definition 2.6
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic

and

be a smooth variety over

.
Fix

Note that the

Proposition 2.8
.

(2)

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
and
let
X
be an
N
-dimensional smooth variety over

.
Then, for any positive integer

is a

-module isomorphism.

Proof.

The problem is reduced to the case when

.
We may assume that
X
is affine.
By abuse of notation, we denote
\Gamma(X,M)
simply by
M
for a sheaf
M
on
X
.
Recall that
F
can be written as follows:

\displaystyle F:W\Omega_{X}^{N}=\varprojlim_{n}W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{N}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\varprojlim_{n}(F_{X})_{*}W_{n}\Omega_{X}^{N}=(F_{X})_{*}W\Omega_{X}^{N}

\displaystyle(a_{n+1})_{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle(F(a_{n+1}))_{n},

where
a_{n+1}\in W_{n+1}\Omega_{X}^{N}
and

It follows from
[

of

3.
Witt divisorial sheaves

3.1.
Basic properties

In this subsection, we introduce Witt divisorial sheaves:

W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
and
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
(Definition
3.1
).
We also establish some fundamental properties such as
coherence of
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
(Proposition
3.8
) and
invariance under linear equivalence (Lemma
3.9
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be an integral normal noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
Then we define the subpresheaf
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
of the constant sheaf
W(K(X))
on
X

by

\Gamma(U,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)):=\{(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots)\in W(K(X))\,|\,\left({\rm div}(\varphi_{n})+p^{n}D\right)|_{U}\geq 0\}

for any open subset
U
of
X
,
where
{\rm div}(\varphi_{n})
denotes the principal divisor associated to
\varphi_{n}
.
By definition,
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is a subsheaf of
W(K(X))
(cf. Remark
3.2
).
We call
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
the
Witt divisorial sheaf associated to

D
.
We define the subsheaf
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
of the constant sheaf
W_{n}(K(X))

in the same way.
In particular, we have

\Gamma(U,W_{1}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\{\varphi\in K(X)\,|\,\left({\rm div}(\varphi)+D\right)|_{U}\geq 0\}.

Remark 3.2
.

We use notation as in Definition
3.1
. If we identify
W(K(X))
with the infinite direct product
\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}K(X)
as sets, then it follows by definition that as subsets of
W(K(X))=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}K(X)
, we obtain an equation

\Gamma(U,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(p^{n}D)).

Remark 3.3
.

We use notation as in Definition
3.1
.
Fix a positive integer
n
.
By Remark
3.2
, we obtain an equation of subsets of

W_{n}(K(X))=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}K(X)
:

\Gamma(U,W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(p^{m}D))=\prod_{m=0}^{n-1}\Gamma(U,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\llcorner p^{m}D\lrcorner)).

Thus, if
E
is an effective
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
,
then there exists a positive real number
\epsilon
such that
the equation

W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)=W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D^{\prime})

holds as subsheaves of the constant sheaf
W_{n}(K(X))
for any
\mathbb{R}
-divisor
D^{\prime}

satisfying
D\leq D^{\prime}\leq D+\epsilon E
.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
K
be a discrete valuation field.
Brylinski–Kato introduced the following subgroup of
W_{n}(K)

[
Kat89
, Definition 3.1]

(strictly speaking,
[
Kat89
]
assumes
K
to be henselian):

{\rm fil}_{r}(W_{n}(K))=\{(b_{0},...,b_{n-1})\in W_{n}(K)\,|\,{\rm ord}_{K}(b_{i})\geq-\frac{rp^{i}}{p^{n-1}}\text{ for all }i\}.

This subgroup can be considered as a stalk of a Witt divisorial sheaf as follows.

Let
X
be an integral normal noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme and let
D
be a
\mathbb{Z}
-divisor on
X
.
Set
K:=K(X)
.
Fix an irreducible component
D^{\prime}
of
D
.
Let
\xi
be the generic point of
D^{\prime}
and
let
r
be the coefficient of
D^{\prime}
in
D
, i.e.
D=rD^{\prime}+\cdots
.
We consider
K
as a discrete valuation field with respect to the valuation corresponding to
\mathcal{O}_{X,\xi}
.
Then the equation

W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}\left((1/p^{n-1})D\right)_{\xi}={\rm fil}_{r}(W_{n}(K))

holds.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
X
be an integral normal noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Let
D
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X
.
Then the following hold.

(1)

W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
is a sheaf of
W\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
W(K(X))
,
i.e. for any open subset
U
of
X
,

\Gamma(U,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
is a
W\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
-submodule of
W(K(X))
.

(2)

For any positive integer
n
,

is a sheaf of

-submodules of

,
i.e. for any open subset

-submodule of

.
Furthermore,

is a quasi-coherent

-module.

Proof.

Let us show (1).
We may assume that
X=U
and
X
is affine, say

.

Fix

.
Let us prove

.
We can write

\varphi=(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots),\quad\psi=(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\cdots)

for some
\varphi_{n},\psi_{n}\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(p^{n}D))
.
By Lemma
2.3
,
we have that

\varphi+\psi=(S_{0}(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}),S_{1}(\varphi_{0},\psi_{0},\varphi_{1},\psi_{1}),\cdots)

for some polynomials

We equip the polynomial ring

with the structure of graded
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra defined in the statement of Lemma
2.4
,
i.e. we consider
\mathbb{Z}[x_{0},y_{0},x_{1},y_{1},\cdots]
as a weighted polynomial ring
such that

.
Pick a monomial

Since

is homogeneous of degree

(Lemma

),
it holds that

\sum_{i=0}^{n}p^{i}(a_{i}+b_{i})=p^{n}.

By

{\rm div}(\varphi_{i})\geq-p^{i}D\qquad{\rm and}\quad\quad{\rm div}(\psi_{i})\geq-p^{i}D,

we have that

\displaystyle{\rm div}(\varphi_{0}^{a_{0}}\psi_{0}^{b_{0}}\cdots\varphi_{n}^{a_{n}}\psi_{n}^{b_{n}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}(a_{i}{\rm div}(\varphi_{i})+b_{i}{\rm div}(\psi_{i}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{n}p^{i}(a_{i}+b_{i})D

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p^{n}D.

In other words, we obtain

.
Therefore, it holds that
\varphi+\psi\in\Gamma(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
.

Fix

and
\varphi=(\varphi_{0},\varphi_{1},\cdots)\in\Gamma(X,W\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
.
Let us show

\displaystyle V^{m}(\underline{b}\cdot(\varphi_{0}^{p^{m}},\varphi_{1}^{p^{m}},\cdots))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\cdots).

In particular, we get

Since we have

Thus, (1) holds.

Let us show (2).
By the same argument as in (1), we have that

is
a sheaf of
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-submodules of
W_{n}(K(X))
.
What is remaining is to prove that

is
a quasi-coherent
W_{n}\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
We may assume that
X={\operatorname{Spec}}\,A
.
Take

and let

be the Teichmüller lift of

.
It suffices to show that the natural

-module homomorphism

is bijective.

is compatible with the inclusions to

it holds that

Let us prove that

Take

where

Definition 3.10
.

Let

be an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme and let

be an invertible
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module.
Then

•

•

•

The equation

By definition, we obtain

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
X
be an integral normal noetherian
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-scheme.
Let
D
be a Cartier divisor on

.
Then the following hold.

(1)

The Witt divisorial sheaf

is isomorphic to the Teichmüller lift
\underline{\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)

(2)

of

Let us show (1).

for any

Theorem 5.3

Step 1

There exists a positive integer

Step 2
.

There exists a positive integer
s_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

.

of

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>(F_{X})_{*}(\underline{L}^{p}_{\leq n})@>{V}>{}>\underline{L}_{\leq n+1}@>{}>{}>L@>{}>{}>0\\
@V{}V{(F_{X})_{*}F}V@V{}V{F}V@V{}V{F}V\\

, it holds that

,
hence we get a commutative diagram:

implies Step

.
∎

Step 3
.

There exists a positive integer

such that the map

.

.

Set

.
Fix

and an invertible

Step 4
.

.

Step 6

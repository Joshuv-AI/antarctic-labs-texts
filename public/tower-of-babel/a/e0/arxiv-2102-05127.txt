Rahul Kumar

Abstract
. Ramanujan provided several results involving the modified Bessel function K z ​ ( x ) K_{z}(x) in his Lost Notebook. One of them is the famous Ramanujan-Guinand formula, equivalent to the functional equation of the non-holomorphic Eiesenstien series on S ​ L 2 ​ ( z ) SL_{2}(z) . Recently, this formula was generalized by Dixit, Kesarwani, and Moll. In this article, we first obtain a generalization of a theorem of Watson and, as an application of it, give a new proof of the result of Dixit, Kesarwani, and Moll. Watson’s theorem is also generalized in a different direction using K z μ ​ ( x , λ ) {}_{\mu}K_{z}(x,\lambda) which is itself a generalization of K z ​ ( x ) K_{z}(x) . Analytic continuation of all these results are also given.



1.
Introduction

On page 253 of his Lost Notebook
[
11
]
, Ramanujan recorded the following beautiful formula.
Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
. For
\alpha,\ \beta>0
such that
\alpha\beta=\pi^{2}
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-z}(n)n^{\frac{z}{2}}K_{\frac{z}{2}}(2n\alpha)-\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-z}(n)n^{\frac{z}{2}}K_{\frac{z}{2}}(2n\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\zeta(z)\left\{\beta^{\frac{1-z}{2}}-\alpha^{\frac{1-z}{2}}\right\}+\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(-\frac{z}{2}\right)\zeta(-z)\left\{\beta^{\frac{1+z}{2}}-\alpha^{\frac{1+z}{2}}\right\},

(1.1)

where
K_{z}(x)
is the modified Bessel function
[
19
, p. 34]
and
\sigma_{z}(n):=\sum_{d|n}d^{z}
is the generalized divisor function.

Theorem
1
was rediscovered by Guinand
[
8
]
in 1955 which is why sometimes this formula is referred to as Ramanujan-Guinand formula in the literature. Several years after Ramanujan, the special case
z=0
of (
1
) was also rediscovered by Koshliakov
[
13
]
. For a detailed history of the Ramanujan-Guinand formula (
1
) and its implications, see
[
2
]
.

It is well-known that (
1
) is equivalent to the functional equation of the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series. For Re
(s)>1
and
\tau:=x+iy,\ y>0
, the non-holomorphic Eisenstein series
G(\tau,s)
is defined by

\displaystyle G(\tau,s):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\\
{(m,n)\neq(0,0)}\end{subarray}}\frac{y^{s}}{|m\tau+n|^{2s}}.

G(\tau,s)
is a non-holomorphic modular function of weight
0
as it is invariant in
\tau
under the usual action of SL
{}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Moreover,
G(\tau,s)
has analytic continuation in
\mathbb{C}
except a simple pole at
s=1
with residue
\frac{\pi}{2}
. Analytic continuation of this function can be given by its Fourier expansion

\displaystyle G(\tau,s)=\zeta(2s)y^{s}+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\Gamma(s)}\zeta(2s-1)y^{1-s}+\frac{4\sqrt{y}\pi^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{2s-1}(n)n^{\frac{1}{2}-s}K_{s-\frac{1}{2}}(2\pi ny)\cos(2\pi nx).

(1.2)

It is effortless to see that (
1
) follows from (
1.2
) and by using the modular property of
G(\tau,s)
, namely,
G(\tau,s)=G\left(-\frac{1}{\tau},s\right),\ \tau\in\mathbb{H}
(upper half-plane).

Several proofs of (
1
) have been given in the literature, for example, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
8
]
. The main ingredient in the proofs of
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
is the following result of Watson
[
18
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

For Re
(x)>0
and Re
(z)>0
, we have

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{nx}{2}\right)^{z}K_{z}(nx)+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(z)-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{x}\Gamma\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2\sqrt{\pi}x^{2z}\Gamma\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4n^{2}\pi^{2})^{z+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(1.3)

In
[
3
]
, Dixit provided another proof of the Ramanujan-Guinand formula (
1
) without invoking Theorem
1.1
. For the details of the proof, we refer the reader to
[
3
, Theorem 1.4]
.

The main goal of this paper is to accomplish two different generalizations of Theorem
1.1
along with an application of one of the theorem. In the literature, Theorem
1.1
has been generalized in many directions, for example, see
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
. Theorem
1.1
has important application in Number Theory, for example, see
[
2
]
and the references therein. The series on the left-hand side of (
1
) has played an important role in the work of Gupta and the author
[
9
]
. Moreover, very recently, Theorem
1.1
is employed nicely in
[
6
, Section 9]
to obtain a modular relation involving the generalized Hurwitz zeta function
[
6
, Equation (1.3)]

\displaystyle\zeta_{w}(s,a)

\displaystyle:=\frac{4}{w^{2}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+1}{2}\right)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(uv)^{s-1}e^{-(u^{2}+v^{2})}\sin(wv)\sinh(wu)}{\left(n^{2}u^{2}+(a-1)^{2}v^{2}\right)^{s/2}}\,dudv,

where
w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
, Re
(s)>1
and
a\in\mathfrak{B}:=\{\xi:\textup{Re}(\xi)=1,\textup{Im}(\xi)\neq 0\}
. For the theory of
\zeta_{w}(s,a)
, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
.

Recently, Dixit, Kesarwani, and Moll
[
5
]
provided an elegant generalization of Theorem
1
. To state their result, we first need to define their new generalization of the modified Bessel function
K_{z}(x)
. For
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
, and
x\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{x\in\mathbb{R}:x\leq 0\}
, the generalized modified Bessel function is defined by
[
5
, Equation (1.3)]

\displaystyle K_{z,w}(x):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma\left(\frac{s-z}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+z}{2}\right){}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{s-z}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{-w^{2}}{4}\right){}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{s+z}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{-w^{2}}{4}\right)2^{s-2}x^{-s}\ ds,

(1.4)

where
c:=\mathrm{Re}(s)>\left|\mathrm{Re}(z)\right|
and
{}_{1}F_{1}(a;c;z)
is the confluent hypergeometric function defined by
[
15
, p. 172, Equation (7.3)]

\displaystyle{}_{1}F_{1}(a;c;z):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\ |z|<\infty,

with
(a)_{m}:=\Gamma(a+m)/\Gamma(a)
for
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Here, and throughout the paper,
\int_{(c)}
denotes the line integral
\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}
.

It is straightforward to see that for
w=0
,
K_{z,w}(x)
reduced to
K_{z}(x)
by using
[
14
, p. 115, Formula 11.1]

\displaystyle K_{z}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\Gamma\left(\frac{s-z}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{s+z}{2}\right)2^{s-2}x^{-s}\ ds.

For more details on the theory of
K_{z,w}(x)
and its connection in Analytic Number Theory and Physics, we refer the reader to
[

]
and
[
12
]

The aforementioned generalization of the Ramanujan-Guinand formula is given in the following theorem
[

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
z,w\in\mathbb{C}

K_{z,w}(x)
be defined in (
1.4

\alpha,\beta>0

\alpha\beta=\pi^{2}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-z}(n)n^{\frac{z}{2}}e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{4}}K_{\frac{z}{2},iw}(2n\alpha)-\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{-z}(n)n^{\frac{z}{2}}e^{\frac{w^{2}}{4}}K_{\frac{z}{2},w}(2n\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\zeta(z)\left\{\beta^{\frac{1-z}{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-z}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)-\alpha^{\frac{1-z}{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1-z}{2};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\left(-\frac{z}{2}\right)\zeta(-z)\left\{\beta^{\frac{1+z}{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1+z}{2};\frac{1}{2};\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)-\alpha^{\frac{1+z}{2}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1+z}{2};\frac{1}{2};-\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)\right\}.

(1.5)

Here we note that authors of
[

[

]

1.2

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\mathrm{Re}(x^{2})>0
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(n,z,w,x):={}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+z;\frac{1}{2};\frac{w^{2}(2n\pi+ix)}{8n\pi-4ix}\right)+{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+z;\frac{1}{2};\frac{w^{2}(2n\pi-ix)}{8n\pi+4ix}\right).

(1.6)

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}
and
\mathrm{Re}(z)>0

K_{z,w}(x)
be defined in (
1.4

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{nx}{2}\right)^{z}K_{z,w}(nx)+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(z){}_{1}F_{1}\left(z;\frac{1}{2};-\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{x}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{4}}{}_{1}F_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+z;\frac{1}{2};-\frac{w^{2}}{4}\right)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}x^{2z}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{4}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}(n,z,w,x)}{(x^{2}+4n^{2}\pi^{2})^{z+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(1.7)

We now obtain an extended version of Theorem
1.3
in which the restriction on

is removed.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

,
\mathrm{Re}(x^{2})>0
, and
\mathcal{A}(n,z,w,x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}x^{2z}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{4}}\Bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}(n,z,w,x)\Bigg\{\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4n^{2}\pi^{2})^{z+\frac{1}{2}}}-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\binom{-z-\frac{1}{2}}{m}\frac{x^{2m}}{(2n\pi)^{2z+2m+1}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\binom{-z-\frac{1}{2}}{m}\frac{x^{2m}}{(2\pi)^{2z+2m+1}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}(n,z,w,x)}{n^{2z+2m+1}}\Bigg].

(1.8)

\mathrm{Re}(x^{2})>0

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{nx}{2}\right)^{z}K_{z}(nx)+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma(z)-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{x}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\pi}x^{2z}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)\Bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{(x^{2}+4n^{2}\pi^{2})^{z+\frac{1}{2}}}-\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\binom{-z-\frac{1}{2}}{m}\frac{x^{2m}}{(2n\pi)^{2z+2m+1}}\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\binom{-z-\frac{1}{2}}{m}\frac{x^{2m}\zeta(2z+2m+1)}{(2\pi)^{2z+2m+1}}\Bigg].

(1.9)

Next, we present a generalization of Theorem
1.1

1.3

2.
Generalization of Watson’s result with
K_{z,w}(x)

The main ingredient to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.6
is Poisson’s summation formula in the following form
[
16
, p. 60-61]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

If
f(t)
is continuous and of bounded variation on
(0,\infty)
, and if
\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\ dt
exists, then

\displaystyle f(0^{+})+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\ dt+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\cos(2\pi nt)\ dt.

One of the nice properties of
K_{z,w}(t)
, derived in
[
5
]
, is the following integral representation for it
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
.
Let
z,w\in\mathbb{C}
and
|\arg(x)|<\frac{\pi}{4}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{z,w}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-z}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}-\frac{x^{2}}{u^{2}}}\cos(wu)\cos\left(\frac{wx}{u}\right)u^{2z-1}\ du.

(2.1)

To prove Theorem
1.3
, it is imperative to obtain the following new integral evaluation which contain
K_{z,w}(x)
in its integrand.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathrm{Re}(z)>0
, and
|\arg(x)|<\frac{\pi}{4}
. Let
\mathcal{A}\left(n,z,w,x\right)
be defined in (
1.6
). If
a\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{xt}{2}\right)^{z}K_{z,w}(xt)\cos(at)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}+z\right)\frac{x^{2z}e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{4}}}{(x^{2}+a^{2})^{z+\frac{1}{2}}}\mathcal{A}\left(\frac{a}{2\pi},z,w,x\right).

(2.2)

Invoke (
2.1
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{xt}{2}\right)^{z}K_{z,w}(xt)\cos(at)\ dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}-\frac{x^{2}t^{2}}{4u^{2}}}\cos(wu)\cos\left(\frac{wxt}{2u}\right)u^{2z-1}\cos(at)\ dudt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u^{2}}\cos(wu)u^{2z-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}t^{2}}{4u^{2}}}\cos\left(\frac{wxt}{2u}\right)\cos(at)\ dtdu,

(2.3)

where in the last step we interchanged the order of the integration which is justified because of the absolute convergence. Note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}t^{2}}{4u^{2}}}\cos\left(\frac{wxt}{2u}\right)\cos(at)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}t^{2}}{4u^{2}}}\cos\left(at+\frac{wxt}{2u}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{x^{2}t^{2}}{4u^{2}}}\cos\left(at-\frac{wxt}{2u}\right)\ dt

(2.4)

From
[
7
, p. 488, Equation (3.896.4)]
, for Re
(\beta)>0

On Stability and Isoperimetry of Constant Mean Curvature Spheres of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} and 𝕊 n × ℝ . \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
R. F. de Lima, M. F. Elbert and B. Nelli

Abstract
. We approach the one-parameter family of rotational constant mean curvature (CMC) spheres of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} and 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} focusing on their stability and isoperimetry properties. We prove that all rotational CMC spheres of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} are stable, and that the ones in 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} with sufficiently small (resp. large) mean curvature are unstable (resp. stable). We also show that there exists a one-parameter family of stable CMC rotational spheres in 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} which are not isoperimetric (i.e., they do not bound isoperimetric regions). We establish the uniqueness of the regions enclosed by the rotational CMC spheres of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} as solutions to the isoperimetric problem, filling in a gap in the original proof given by Hsiang and Hsiang. We establish, as well, a sharp upper bound for the volume of the spherical regions of 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} which are unique solutions to the isoperimetric problem. In essence, all these results come from the fact that the rotational CMC spheres of ℍ n × ℝ \mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} , and those of 𝕊 n × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R} with sufficiently large mean curvature, are nested. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 53A10 (primary), 53B20 (secondary). Key words and phrases: mean curvature – stability – isoperimetric problem.



1.
Introduction

Compact hypersurfaces of constant mean curvature (CMC)
in Riemannian manifolds are known to be critical points of the area
functional
\mathcal{A}

for volume-preserving variations.
A CMC hypersurface is then called

stable
if it minimizes
\mathcal{A}
up to second order for
any such variation. In simply connected space forms, as established
in
[
3
]
, geodesic spheres are the only compact stable
hypersurfaces.

Souam
[
13
]
considered the rotational CMC spheres of

\mathbb{H}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
, previously obtained by
Hsiang and Hsiang
[
9
]
,
proving that they are all stable, and that those with
(nonnormalized) constant mean curvature
H\geq\sqrt{2}

are unique with respect to this property.
He considered the rotational CMC spheres of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
as well, constructed by
Pedrosa in
[
11
]
, and showed that such a sphere is stable if and only if its
mean curvature is greater than, or equal to, a constant which is approximately
0.36
.
Furthermore, any compact stable CMC (non-minimal) surface of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
is necessarily a rotational sphere.

Stability is a concept closed related to isoperimetry. Recall that
an embedded compact hypersurface
\varSigma
of a Riemannian manifold
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
is called

isoperimetric
if the compact region
\varOmega\subset\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
having
\varSigma
as boundary is
the solution to the isoperimetric problem for prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}:={\rm Vol}(\varOmega).

In this case, we call
\varOmega
an
isoperimetric region
.
This means that, among all regions of
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
of volume
\mathcal{V},

\varOmega
is the one
whose boundary has least area, where by
volume
and
area
we mean
the
(n+1)
-dimensional and
n
-dimensional Riemannian measures, respectively.
A standard result, then, establishes that
any
C^{2}
-smooth isoperimetric hypersurface is necessarily CMC and stable.
However, there exist stable embedded CMC hypersurfaces which are not isoperimetric, such as
the geodesic spheres of large radius in the real projective space (cf.
[
3
, Appendix]
).

In
[
9
]
, Hsiang and Hsiang showed that,
for any
H>n-1\geq 1,
there exists
an embedded rotational sphere
\varSigma_{H}

of constant (nonnormalized) mean curvature
H
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

which is necessarily isoperimetric.
In addition, by means of Alexandrov reflections, they established that
any compact embedded hypersurface in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

of constant mean curvature
H>0
is congruent to
a rotational sphere
\varSigma_{H}.
In particular,
H
must be larger than
n-1.

In fact, by the tangency principle, the mean curvature
of any compact immersed CMC hypersurface of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
must be larger than

n-1
, since there exists an entire vertical graph in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
of constant mean curvature

H
for any
H\in(0,n-1]
(cf.
[
7
,
8
]
).

It is also shown in
[
9
]
that the spherical region
\varOmega_{H}

with boundary
\varSigma_{H}
is the only solution to the isoperimetric problem for
prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}={\rm Vol}(\varOmega_{H}).

However, the proof the authors provide is incorrect (cf. Remark
1

in Section
4
).

Similar results regarding the isoperimetric problem in

\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
were obtained by Pedrosa
[
11
]
.
He proved that there exists an embedded rotational sphere

\varSigma_{H}
of constant mean curvature
H
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
for any given
H>0,

and that a solution to the isoperimetric problem is either the spherical region

\varOmega_{H}
bounded by a rotational sphere

\varSigma_{H}
or a cylindrical section
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[a,b].

In addition, if
H
is sufficiently large,

\varOmega_{H}
is the only solution
to the isoperimetric problem in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

for prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}={\rm Vol}(\varOmega_{H}).

In his work, Pedrosa also obtained a sharp lower bound
(approximately 0.66) for the mean curvature of an isoperimetric
sphere of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.
Notice that, in what concerns stability, the lower bound of
H

obtained by Souam is smaller (approximately 0.36). Therefore, any rotational
CMC sphere of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
having mean curvature between these
two lower bounds is stable and non-isoperimetric.

In this paper, inspired by the aforementioned works,
we establish results on stability and isoperimetry of
CMC spheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.

Our approach brings to light that, in this context,
the fundamental properties of the CMC spheres are
intimately related with the
nesting property
, which can be described as follows.

First, denote by
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}
the simply
connected
n(\geq\hskip-2.84544pt2)
-space form of constant
sectional curvature
\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}
(i.e.,
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{Q}_{-1}^{n}
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\mathbb{Q}_{1}^{n}
).
Then, set
C_{\epsilon}:=n-1
for
\epsilon=-1
and
C_{\epsilon}:=0
for
\epsilon=1,
and
write
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
for the family of all noncongruent
rotational spheres
\varSigma_{H}
in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
of constant
mean curvature
H>C_{\epsilon}.
Assume that all
spheres of
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
have the same rotational axis

\{o\}\times\mathbb{R}\subset\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

and the same point
o\in\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\{0\}

of minimal height. Finally, as above, denote by
\varOmega_{H}
the compact spherical region
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
enclosed by
\varSigma_{H}.

In this setting, for a given
C\geq C_{\epsilon},
we say that the
subfamily
\mathscr{F}_{C}:=\{\varSigma_{H}\,;\,H>C\}\subset\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
is
nested
if

\varOmega_{H}\subset\varOmega_{H^{*}}
whenever
H>H^{*}>C.

Our first result, as stated below, describes the behavior of the
CMC spheres of the family
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
regarding the nesting property.
Its proof will be based on the fact that any sphere
\varSigma_{H}\in\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
has
a horizontal hyperplane
P_{H}
of symmetry, and that a subfamily

\mathscr{F}_{C}\subset\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
is nested if and only if the height of

P_{H}
is a decreasing function of
H
for
H>C
(see Fig.
1
).

Figure 1
.

\varSigma_{H_{i}},

i=1,2,3
,

H_{1}<H_{2}<H_{3}
. As the mean curvature increases from
H_{1}
to
H_{3},

the height of the horizontal hyperplanes of symmetry (represented by the horizontal lines)
decreases in the nested case, and increases in the non-nested case.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}:=\{\varSigma_{H}\,;\,H>C_{\epsilon}\}
be the family of
rotational CMC spheres of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
Then, the following hold:

A)

For
\epsilon=-1
, the whole family
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
is nested.

B)

For
\epsilon=1,
there exists
C_{\min}>C_{\epsilon}:=0
such that
\mathscr{F}_{C}
is
nested if and only if
C\geq C_{\min}.
Furthermore, as
H
decreases to
0
,
the height function of
\varSigma_{H}\in\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}

strictly decreases to
0,
and
\varSigma_{H}
converges uniformly to (a double copy of)
the totally geodesic horizontal hyperplane
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\{0\}.

Theorem
1
, together with
[
9
, Theorems 1 and 2]

and
[
11
, Theorem 2]
, yields the following uniqueness result, which,
in the case of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},
was previously obtained by
Pedrosa with a different proof (cf.
[
11
, Theorem 5]
).

Theorem 2
.

Given
\mathcal{V}_{0}>0
(resp. a sufficiently small
\mathcal{V}_{0}>0
),
there exists a spherical region
\varOmega_{H_{0}}
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
)
which is, up to ambient isometries, the only solution to the isoperimetric problem
for prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}_{0}.

Next, we apply Theorem
1

to establish the following extension of the result by Souam we mentioned before;
namely
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
,
which is set in
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.

We remark that, in what concerns stability and isoperimetry of
rotational CMC spheres of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},
our result
ensures the existence of one-parameter families of
all types: stable, unstable, isoperimetric, and stable non-isoperimetric.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}:=\{\varSigma_{H}\,;\,H>C_{\epsilon}\}
be the family of
rotational CMC spheres of
\mathbb{Q}_{\epsilon}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}.
Then, the following hold:

A)

For
\epsilon=-1,
any sphere
\varSigma_{H}
of
\mathscr{F}_{\epsilon}
is stable.

B)

For
\epsilon=1,
there exist positive numbers
H_{0}<H_{1}
such that:

i)

\varSigma_{H}
is stable if
H\geq H_{0}.

ii)

\varSigma_{H}
is unstable if
H<H_{0}
is sufficiently close to
H_{0}.

iii)

\varSigma_{H}
is stable and non-isoperimetric if
H_{0}\leq H<H_{1}
.

iv)

\varSigma_{H}
is isoperimetric if
H\geq H_{1}
.

The result in Theorem
3
-
A
follows directly from the fact that
the rotational spheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
are all isoperimetric. Nevertheless,
we considered relevant to give it an independent proof, as did Souam for the case
n=2.

Regarding Theorem
3
-
B
, we point out that
the stability of a rotational sphere
\varSigma_{H}
of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
depends on the
growth behavior of its area
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(H)

considered as a function of its mean curvature
H
(this is also true for
the rotational spheres of
\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},
but we will not use it here).

As we shall see, stable spheres are those for which
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(H)\leq 0
,
whereas the unstable ones are those for which
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(H)>0
.
For
n=2,

\mathcal{A}
can be expressed in terms
of elementary functions of
H
(cf.
[
11
, Section 4]
).
Using this expression, Souam concluded that
\mathcal{A}
has only one
critical point
H_{0}
(necessarily a maximum), which is precisely the
sharp lower bound for the mean curvature of stable spheres of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}

he obtained.
For
n>2,
instead,
\mathcal{A}
is defined by means of an integral of elliptic type which
is not explicitly computable, making a complete determination of the growth behavior of
\mathcal{A}

nearly impossible. Yet, we believe that
\mathcal{A}
has only one critical point, just as in the case
n=2

(see Figure
7
in Section
4
, which depicts
an illustrative graph of
\mathcal{A}
as a function
of the “radiuses” of the rotational spheres).

Our final result constitutes a refinement of Theorem
2

in the case
\epsilon=1.
It establishes a sharp upper bound for the volume of
the spherical regions
\varOmega_{H}
which are unique solutions to the isoperimetric
problem.

Theorem 4
.

Given an integer
n\geq 2,
there exists a positive constant
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(n)

which, regarding
the isoperimetric problem for any prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}_{0}>0
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},

has the following properties:

i)

If
\mathcal{V}_{0}<\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, any solution is necessarily a
spherical region
\varOmega_{H}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

ii)

If
\mathcal{V}_{0}>\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, there exists a cylindrical section
\varOmega_{[a,b]}:=\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[a,b]
which is a solution.

iii)

If
\mathcal{V}_{0}=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, there exist a spherical region
and a cylindrical section which are both solutions.

Consequently, if a spherical region
\varOmega_{H}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is a unique solution
to the isoperimetric problem for prescribed volume
\mathcal{V}_{0}:={\rm Vol}(\varOmega_{H}),

then
{\rm Vol}(\varOmega_{H})<\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu.
Moreover, the constant

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is sharp in the following sense:
for any
\delta\in(0,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)

\mathcal{V}_{0}\in(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu-\delta,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, and a spherical region
of volume
\mathcal{V}_{0}
which is the unique solution.

For
n=2,
the lower bound for the mean curvature of isoperimetric spheres obtained by
Pedrosa gives that the upper bound
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
of their volumes is approximately

16.66
. Moreover, any spherical region of volume less than
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathcal{V}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is unique

, we cannot ensure this

\varOmega_{H}
is not constant when

varies in an open interval.

2.
Stability of CMC hypersurfaces

Let
\varSigma
be a compact oriented hypersurface of a Riemannian manifold
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 2,
and recall that
the nonnormalized mean curvature function
H
of
\varSigma

is the trace of its shape operator, that is,
the sum of its principal curvatures. Suppose that
\varSigma=\Phi_{0}(M),
where

\Phi_{0}\colon M\to\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
is an immersion of a compact

n
-dimensional manifold
M
into
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}.
Given a smooth variation
\Phi(t,.)

of
\Phi_{0}=\Phi(0,.),

t\in(-\delta,\delta),

its
area
and
balance of volume
functionals are defined as (see
[
2
,
3
]
)

\mathcal{A}(t):=\int_{M}dM_{t}\quad\text{and}\quad V(t):=\int_{[0,t]\times M}\Phi^{*}d(\mathbb{M}^{n+1}),

where
dM_{t}
is the area element of
M
in the metric induced by the immersion
\Phi(t,.)
, and

\Phi^{*}d(\mathbb{M}^{n+1})
is the pull-back by
\Phi
of the volume element of
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
.
In this setting, one defines the
variational field

\xi
of
\Phi(t,.)
as

\xi:=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(t,.)|_{t=0}.

It is well known that (cf.
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
)

(1)

\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(0)=-\int_{\varSigma}Hf\quad\text{and}\quad V^{\prime}(0)=\int_{\varSigma}f,

where
f:=\langle\xi,N\rangle
and
N
is the unit normal field of
\varSigma.

From these identities, one sees that
compact CMC hypersurfaces are critical points of the functional area for
volume-preserving variations (i.e., those satisfying
V^{\prime}(0)=0
), as we mentioned before.
A compact CMC hypersurface is then called
stable
if
\mathcal{A}^{\prime\prime}(0)\geq 0
for such variations.

The stability of a CMC hypersurface
\varSigma
is equivalent to the
nonnegativeness of the quadratic form
Q
defined by

Q(u,u)=-\int_{\varSigma}uLu

for functions
u\in C^{\infty}(\varSigma)
satisfying
\int_{\varSigma}u=0
(cf.
[
3
, Proposition 2.7]
).
Here,
L
is the (self-adjoint)
Jacobi operator
on
C^{\infty}(\varSigma),
which is defined as

Lu:=\Delta u+(\|\sigma\|^{2}+{\rm Ric}(N))u,

being
\Delta u
the Laplacian of
u
on
\varSigma,

\sigma
the second fundamental form of
\varSigma,

and
{\rm Ric}(N)
the Ricci curvature of
\mathbb{M}^{n+1}
evaluated on the unit normal field

N
of
\varSigma.

We add that the Jacobi operator
L
of the CMC hypersurface
\varSigma
satisfies the following identity:

(2)

\frac{\partial H}{\partial t}(t,.)|_{t=0}=Lf.

In
[
10
]
, Koiso established a fundamental result
on stability of CMC surfaces with boundary in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{3}.
As pointed out by Souam in
[
14
]
,
her result is easily extended to compact oriented CMC hypersurfaces with empty boundary in any Riemannian manifold,
giving the following result (see also
[
6
]
,
where the authors established a broad extension of Koiso’s Theorem).

Generalized Koiso’s Theorem
.

Let
\varSigma
be a smooth compact
immersed oriented CMC hypersurface in a complete
(n+1)
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
\mathbb{M}^{n+1},

and let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}

be the first and second eigenvalues of the Jacobi operator
L
on

\varSigma,
respectively. Under these assumptions, the following hold.

A)

Assume
\lambda_{1}<0=\lambda_{2}.
Then, one has:

i)

If there exists an eigenfunction
u
associated to
\lambda_{2}=0
such that
\int_{\varSigma}u\neq 0
, then

\varSigma
is unstable.

ii)

If
\int_{\varSigma}u=0
for any eigenfunction
u
associated to
\lambda_{2}=0,

then there exists a unique smooth function
v\in(\ker L)^{\perp}
(orthogonal with respect to the
L^{2}
norm)
which satisfies
Lv=1
and determines the stability condition of
\varSigma;
namely,

\varSigma
is stable if and only if
\int_{\varSigma}v\geq 0.

B)

If
\lambda_{2}<0,
then
\varSigma
is unstable.

Again, we have

(0,\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}).

as we wished to prove.

Lemma 2
.

For
\epsilon=1,
the function
\psi\colon(0,\pi)\to\mathbb{R}

\psi(s):=\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s)}{\varphi_{\epsilon}(s)}

s_{*}\in(0,\pi)

global minimum. Furthermore,
\psi
is strictly decreasing in

(0,s_{*})

(s_{*},\pi).

Proof.

Firstly, let us observe that there is no interval

on which
\psi

is constant. Indeed, assuming otherwise, there is a constant
\psi_{0}>0
such that, for all
s\in I,

\psi(s)=\psi_{0},
that is,
\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s)=\psi_{0}\varphi_{\epsilon}(s).

Hence, there is a constant
C>0
such that
\varphi_{\epsilon}(s)=Ce^{\psi_{0}s}
,
s\in I.

However, this function is clearly distinct from the one defined in (
5

Thus,
\psi
is constant in no interval, which implies that
\psi
is increasing (resp. decreasing)

\psi
is strictly increasing (resp. strictly decreasing) in

By items (i) and (ii) of Lemma

, we have that

\lim_{s\to 0}\psi(s)=+\infty.

Also, from equality (
6
),

(11)

\lim_{s\to\pi}\psi(s)=\lim_{s\to\pi}\frac{\varphi_{\epsilon}^{\prime}(s)}{\varphi_{\epsilon}(s)}=\lim_{s\to\pi}\left[-(n-1)\cot(s)+\frac{1}{\varphi_{\epsilon}(s)}\right]=+\infty.

It follows from (
10

\psi
is bounded from below.
In particular,
\psi
has a global minimum
s_{*}\in(0,\mathcal{R}_{\epsilon}).

Now, set

. Then,
\psi=a+1/\varphi_{\epsilon},
which yields

Hence, if

\psi,
we have

\psi
is not strictly
increasing in
(s_{*},\pi).
Under this hypothesis,

\psi
has a critical point

\psi
is strictly increasing in

Hence, if we define the function

\psi
is strictly increasing in the interval

Assume now that
\psi

\psi

•

k_{n}(s,s_{0})=\varrho^{\prime}(s,s_{0}).

But, for all fixed

In particular,

This proves statement

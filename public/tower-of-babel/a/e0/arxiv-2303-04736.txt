Rigidity of harmonic functions on the supercritical percolation cluster
Ahmed Bou-Rabee, William Cooperman, Paul Dario

Abstract
. We use ideas from quantitative homogenization to show that nonconstant harmonic functions on the percolation cluster cannot satisfy certain structural constraints, for example, a Lipschitz bound. These unique-continuation-type results are false on the full lattice and hence the disorder is utilized in an essential way.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

We consider harmonic functions on the supercritical bond percolation cluster of the integer lattice. Much of the study of these functions has been devoted to showing that they share large scale features,
e.g.
, a Harnack inequality
[
Bar04
]
and polynomial approximation
[
AD18
]
, with their counterparts on
\Z^{d}
and
\R^{d}
. Such results are often viewed probabilistically; the convergence of random walk on the cluster to Brownian motion, as established in
[
BB07
,
MP07
]
is closely related to the fact that harmonic functions on the cluster approximate continuum harmonic functions.

In this article, we focus on the differences between the percolation cluster and
\Z^{d}
or
\R^{d}
by proving three unique-continuation-type theorems on the cluster which are false on both the full lattice and the continuum. We are guided by the principle that harmonic functions on the percolation cluster should appear fairly generic at small scales. In Figure
1
, we illustrate the rippling effect of closing one bond on the family of linear harmonic functions. Closing or opening a bond will typically have a small but nonzero effect, preventing many fine properties (for example, linear relations between values of a harmonic function at nearby sites) from holding.

Our work, although self-contained, is motivated by the Abelian sandpile
[
LP10
,
LP17
]
and we discuss applications to the model briefly in Section
1.3
and in detail in Section
6
below.

1.2.
Main results

Let
E\left(\Z^{d}\right)
denote the set of
edges
,
i.e.
, unordered pairs
\{x,y\}\in\Z^{d}
satisfying
|x-y|=1
and associate to each edge
an i.i.d Bernoulli random variable

\mathbf{a}:E\left(\Z^{d}\right)\to\{0,1\},\quad\mbox{with }\mathbb{P}[\mathbf{a}(e)=1]=\mathfrak{p}

where
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1)
, and
\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d)\in(0,1)
is the bond percolation threshold for the lattice
\Z^{d}
.
Consequently, there is a unique infinite connected component denoted by
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
.

A function on the cluster
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
is
harmonic
if

(1.1)

\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}}u=0\quad\mbox{in $\mathscr{C}_{\infty}$}

where the
graph Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
is defined as

(1.2)

\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}}u(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}(u(y)-u(x)),

and the sum
y\sim x
is over the edges
(x,y)\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)
.

For our first theorem, we say that a function
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
is
Lipschitz
if there exists a constant
C
such that

(1.3)

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C\dist_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}(x,y),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty},

where
\dist_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
refers to the graph distance on the cluster.
On the full lattice,
\Z^{d}
, and the continuum,
\R^{d}
, many harmonic functions are Lipschitz: take, for instance,
x\to(x_{1}-x_{2})
. However, harmonic functions on
\Z^{d}
are generally not harmonic
on the cluster; in fact, it is not obvious how to construct any nontrivial harmonic function on the cluster.

It was shown by Benjamini, Duminil-Copin, Kozma, and Yadin in
[
BDCKY15
]
that the space of linearly growing harmonic functions on the cluster has the same dimension as that of
\R^{d}
. Their construction was not explicit and they asked
[
Yad22
]
whether there are nontrivial
Lipschitz
harmonic functions on the cluster. We show that the only Lipschitz harmonic functions on the cluster are constant.

Our proof relies on the large-scale regularity of harmonic functions on the percolation cluster established in
[
AD18
]
. This regularity is used to measure how changing a bond’s value (as in Figure
1
) affects harmonic functions of linear growth.

Theorem 1.1
.

Almost surely, if
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
is Lipschitz and harmonic then

u\equiv c
for some
c\in\R
.

The Lipschitz function
x\to(x_{1}-x_{2})
is also integer-valued. The delicate dependence of harmonic functions on the structure of open and closed bonds suggests that there are also no nontrivial integer-valued harmonic functions
on the cluster. We prove this under the additional assumption of linear growth, a technical condition which we conjecture can be replaced by any deterministic growth, see Problem
1.4
below.

Theorem 1.2
.

Almost surely, if
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
is harmonic, integer-valued, and grows at most linearly, i.e.,

u(x)=O(|x|)
, then
u\equiv a
for some
a\in\Z
.

To motivate our next theorem, recall that any bounded harmonic function on
\Z^{d}
,
\R^{d}
, or
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
is constant. This is, for instance, a consequence
of the elliptic Harnack inequality. However, this Liouville theorem is false on
\Z^{d}
and
\R^{d}
if one relaxes the harmonic constraint
to harmonic outside of a finite set. For example, the discrete derivative of the elliptic Green’s function on
\Z^{d}
decays to zero at infinity.
In fact, by iteratively taking finite differences of the elliptic Green’s function,
[
SV09
,
HJL19
]
,
one can construct a function on
\Z^{d}
that is harmonic outside a finite set and decays faster than any given inverse polynomial,
but is not compactly supported.

Our next theorem states that this construction fails on the cluster. Specifically, we show on the two-dimensional supercritical percolation cluster that any function with an integer-valued Laplacian that decays faster than
|x|^{-1}
is zero outside a finite set.

Theorem 1.3
.

The following holds for
d=2
. Almost surely and for all functions with finite support,
f:\Z^{d}\to\Z
, if
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
satisfies

\displaystyle-\Delta_{{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}}u

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\to\infty}\,R

\displaystyle\left(\sup_{B_{R}\setminus B_{R/2}}|u|\right)=0,

then the support of
u
is finite.

In fact, we characterize the possible decay rates of functions with integer-valued Laplacians on the cluster; see Theorem
5.1
below. We conjecture that Theorem
1.2
holds in all dimensions and discuss a possible route to proving this under Problem
1.5
below.

1.3.
Abelian sandpile

As mentioned earlier, this investigation is partly motivated by the desire to understand the Abelian sandpile model
[
LP10
,
LP17
]

on the supercritical percolation cluster. Significant differences between the model on the full lattice and on the cluster can be observed in numerical experiments — this has been documented in the physics literature by Sadhu and Dhar
[
SD11
]
. Until this article, nothing to this effect had been rigorously demonstrated.

We defer a definition of the model to Section
6
below. For now, we note that Theorem
5.1
gives a complete description of the so-called toppling invariants
[
DRSV95
]
of the model on the cluster
which is considerably different from that of
\Z^{2}
. Moreover, integer-valued harmonic functions on periodic graphs lay the foundations for the fractal patterns appearing in the Abelian sandpile
[
PS13
,
BR21
,
LPS17
,
LPS16
,
BR24a
]
. The absence of such functions, Theorem
1.2
, provides a partial explanation for why such patterns do not appear on the cluster.

In Section
6
we also prove a new ‘slow mixing’ theorem for the sandpile Markov chain on the cluster.

1.4.
Open questions

As mentioned previously, we expect the assumption of linear growth in Theorem
1.2

can be replaced by that of any deterministic growth.

Problem 1.4
.

Show that there are no non-constant integer-valued harmonic functions on the cluster (of any growth).

In fact, we prove a weaker statement for integer-valued functions of quadratic growth; see Section
4.3
below.

Similarly, we expect that Theorem
1.3
is true in all dimensions
d\geq 2

Problem 1.5
.

Prove Theorem
1.3
in dimensions
d>2
.

A large part of the proof of Theorem
1.3
carries over verbatim when the dimension
d
is arbitrary, except for one step which contains arguments that depend crucially on the planarity of the lattice, Proposition
5.11
. We present a more general version of Proposition
5.11
explicitly as a problem, which we believe to be independently interesting. For the statement of this problem and the next,
we say that a set
A\subset\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
on the cluster has
positive density
if

(1.4)

\liminf_{N\to\infty}|B_{N}|^{-1}|A\cap B_{N}|>0,

where
B_{N}
is the ball of radius
N
centered at the origin.

Problem 1.6
.

Consider the cluster in dimensions
d>2
and let
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
be a function which is harmonic outside of a finite set and which decays to zero at infinity,
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0
. Show that if the set of edges
e
such that
\{\nabla u(e)=0\}
has positive density, then
u
must vanish outside of a finite set.

This distinction between two and higher dimensions has appeared previously in the study of singular sets and unique continuation of elliptic equations
[
LM20
,
Han94
]
. For instance, the solution of a divergence-form elliptic equation with Hölder continuous coefficients which vanishes on an open set is identically zero
in two dimensions. However, this property may fail in dimensions higher than two
[
Mil73
]
.

The following question seems to be closely related.

Problem 1.7
.

Let
h
be a harmonic function on
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
. Show that if the set of sites in the cluster where
h
is bounded has positive density, then
h
is a constant.

On
\Z^{2}
, it was shown in a spectacular work by Buhovsky, Logunov, Malinnikova, and Sodin that if the set of sites where
h
is bounded has a sufficiently
high density, then
h
is constant
[
BLMS22
]
. This was then applied to Anderson localization in the breakthrough papers

[
DS20
,
LZ22
]
.

Buhovsky, Logunov, Malinnikova, and Sodin demonstrate via counterexamples that their result fails for a low density on
\Z^{2}
and for any density on
\Z^{d}
for
d\geq 3
. Their arguments utilize the exact symmetries of
\Z^{d}
. In particular, the counterexamples in
[
BLMS22
]
show that a solution to Problem
1.7
must use the randomness of the cluster.

1.5.
Method and paper outline

Each of our results may be thought of as a consequence of the following (intentionally ambiguous) claim: almost surely, the supercritical percolation cluster has no symmetries. Our proof strategy is thus, roughly, to proceed by contradiction. We suppose that a function with some structure on the percolation cluster exists and then identify a specific asymmetry which this contradicts.

An early example of this technique appears in a paper of Chayes, Chayes, Franz, Sethna, and Trugman on the quantum percolation problem
[
CCF+86
]
.
There they used the fact that every finite subgraph of
\Z^{d}
appears in the percolation cluster with positive density to construct finitely supported eigenfunctions of the Laplacian.
Other works which establish qualitative differences between the cluster and the lattice include
[
BGW13
]
and
[
Abe15
]
.

Our main technical innovation in this paper is in the use of quantitative stochastic homogenization. We use these ideas together with ergodicity arguments to identify specific events which preclude harmonic functions with structure from existing on the cluster. The proofs of each of our theorems, while sharing the common thread of quantitative homogenization, can be read independently.

We collect several common preliminary results in Section
2
on percolation, homogenization, and topology.
In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
, in Section
4
, Theorem
1.2
,
and in Section
5
, Theorem
1.3
. The beginning of each section contains an outline of each proof.
This arrangement is roughly in the order of technicality; in particular, the proof of Theorem
1.3
is the most involved and occupies the bulk of this article.

We conclude in Section
6
with a discussion of how our results relate to the Abelian sandpile. This section is mostly expository and contains several open questions which stem from this work. The main contribution of this section is a ‘slow mixing’ result for the sandpile Markov chain stated in Theorem
6.5
below.

2.2.
An elementary property of sets of full measure

We collect the following elementary result which will be used repeatedly in the proofs below. Let us denote by
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
the probability space where
\Omega:=\left\{0,1\right\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
is the set of all the percolation configurations and
\mathcal{F}
is the product
\sigma
-algebra. Given a measurable set
E\in\mathcal{F}
and a finitely supported function
f:E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\{0,1\}
we denote by
E_{f}
the event which is
E
except the values at edges
e
with
f(e)=1

are flipped:

E_{f}:=\left\{\mathbf{a}\in\Omega\,:\,{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}\in E~\mbox{with}~{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}(e)=(1-f(e))\mathbf{a}(e)+f(e)(1-\mathbf{a}(e))\,\,\forall\,e\in E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\right\}.

We denote by
E_{\infty}
the intersection of
E_{f}
over all such finitely supported
f
. The following (trivial) implication holds,
E_{\infty}\subseteq E
. We observe that if
E
is an event of full probability,
i.e.
, satisfying
\mathbb{P}(E)=1
, then
E_{\infty}
is also an event of probability
1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any measurable event
E\in\mathcal{F}
of full probability, one has the identity

\mathbb{P}(E_{\infty})=1.

Proof.

This is immediate from the fact that for every finitely supported
f:E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\to\{0,1\}
, we have that
\mathbb{P}[E_{f}]=1
and the set of such
f
is countable.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The prior result is a consequence of the finite energy of Bernoulli percolation, see, e.g.,
[
LP16
, Section 7.2]
.

2.3.
Connectivity properties of the cluster

Recall the notation for the cluster from Section
2.1
. Given a cube
Q_{R}
, we write
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{R})
for the largest connected component of open edges contained in the cube,
breaking ties in a deterministic fashion. We say that a cube
Q_{R}
is
well-connected
if the following properties hold:

•

R
is large enough so that
R^{1/(10d)^{2}}\leq R/100
.

•

Q_{R}
is
crossing
: each of the
d
pairs of opposite
(d-1)
dimensional faces of
Q_{R}
is joined by a path in
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{R})
.

•

Every cube
Q_{M}
of side length
M
for
M\in[R^{1/(10d)^{2}}/2,R/10]
which intersects
Q_{R}
is crossing. Moreover, for every such cube, every open path of length at least
\frac{M}{100}
in
Q_{M}
is connected, within
Q_{M}
, to
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{M})
.

We remark that the exponent
1/(10d)^{2}
could be made smaller, at the cost of changing the constants and exponents below.
Moreover, for
R
sufficiently large,
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{R})
is comparable to
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap Q_{R}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

There exist constants
c(d,\mathfrak{p})>0,C(d,\mathfrak{p})<\infty

2.4.
Homogenization of harmonic polynomials

In this section we record the preliminary theorems we use from
[
Dar21
,
DG21
,
AD18
]
, introduce the corrector, corrected planes, and spaces of harmonic functions.
Start by denoting by
\mathcal{A}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})
the space of harmonic functions on the cluster. For each
k\in\N
, we let
\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})
denote the subspace
of
\mathcal{A}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})
consisting of functions growing more slowly at infinity than a polynomial of degree
(k+1)
:

\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{A}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}):\limsup_{R\to\infty}R^{-(k+1)}\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R})}=0\right\},

and the space of harmonic polynomials of degree at most

\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k}:=\left\{u:\R^{d}\to\R:\Delta u=0~\mbox{in}~\mathbb{R}^{d}~\mbox{and}~\limsup_{R\to\infty}R^{-(k+1)}\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{R})}=0\right\}.

Recall the classical fact that harmonic functions of polynomial growth are polynomials,
i.e.
, the space
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k}
consists of harmonic polynomials of degree at most
k
. Below we record the analogue of this fact, proved in
[
AD18
]
, for the percolation cluster.

2.4.1.
Large-scale regularity

We recall below the large-scale regularity for harmonic functions on the percolation cluster. The result was originally established in
[
AD18
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 2.4

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
AD18
]
)
.

There exist exponents
s(d,\mathfrak{p})>0
and
\delta(d,\mathfrak{p})>0
, a constant
C:=C(d,\mathfrak{p})<\infty
such that for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, there exists a nonnegative random variable
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)
satisfying the stochastic integrability estimate,

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that the following hold:

(i)

For each

, there exists a constant

such that, for every

,
there exists

such that, for every

,

(2.3)

\|u-p\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq C_{k}r^{-\delta}\|p\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}.

(ii)

2.4.2.
First order corrector

In the case

.

Theorem 2.5

((1.22) in
[

]
and Proposition 2.12 in
[
DG21
]
)
.

Every function

can be uniquely written as

where

,

, and

is called the corrector and is defined up to additive constant.
The corrector satisfies the following properties:

•

Linearity with respect to the variable

is linear.

•

Quantitative sublinearity and Lipschitz bound. For any exponent
\alpha>0
, there exist an exponent

such that for any vertex

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{corr},\alpha}(x)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that for every radius

and every

(2.5)

\osc_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x)}\chi_{p}+\|\nabla\chi_{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq C|p|r^{\alpha}.

In the rest of this article, we write

(2.6)

\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}=\mbox{unique, modulo additive constant harmonic function which grows like $p\cdot x$ at infinity}\\

and refer to this function as the

2.5.
Homogenization of the Green’s function

Given the results of
[
AD18
]
and
[
DG21
]
, the following results are a straightforward adaptation of the proofs of
[
AKM19
, Theorem 8.14 and Theorem 8.20]
.
For the statement, denote by
G(\cdot,\cdot)
the elliptic Green’s function on the percolation cluster and
\bar{G}(\cdot)
its continuum homogenized version. The function
\bar{G}(\cdot)
is a multiple of the standard elliptic Green’s function on
\R^{d}
, and the multiplicative coefficient is explicit in terms of the diffusivity of the random walk on the percolation cluster and of the density of the infinite cluster (see e.g.,
[
BH09
, (1.9)]
or
[
DG21
, (1.9)]
)

\bar{G}(x):=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2\pi\bar{\mathbf{a}}(\mathfrak{p})}\ln|x|\quad\mbox{if~$d=2$}\\
\frac{\Gamma(d/2-1)}{2\pi^{d/2}\bar{\mathbf{a}}(\mathfrak{p})}\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2}}\quad\mbox{if~$d\geq 3$},\end{cases}

where
\bar{\mathbf{a}}(\mathfrak{p})
is a deterministic function of the percolation probability
\mathfrak{p}
(the homogenized coefficient associated with the infinite cluster).

In dimension
d\geq 3
, given a vertex
y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
,
we define
G(\cdot,y)
as the solution to

(2.7)

\begin{cases}&\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}G(\cdot,y)=-\delta_{y}\quad\mbox{in $\mathscr{C}_{\infty}$}\\
&\lim_{|x|\to\infty}G(x,y)=0\end{cases}

and in dimension
d=2
,

(2.8)

\begin{cases}&\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}G(\cdot,y)=-\delta_{y}\quad\mbox{in $\mathscr{C}_{\infty}$}\\
&\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{1}{|x|}G(x,y)=0\\
&G(y,y)=0.\end{cases}

The first result we present is a homogenization theorem for the gradient of the Green’s function. This result can be fairly easily deduced from
[
DG21
, Theorem 2]
and the large-scale regularity on the infinite cluster stated in Theorem
2.4
. The proof is written below for completeness.

Proposition 2.6

(Homogenization of the gradient of the Green’s function)
.

For any
\delta>0
, there exist constants
s(d,\mathfrak{p},\delta)>0
,
C(d,\mathfrak{p},\delta)<\infty
, and, for any
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, there exists a minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{\nabla-\mathrm{Homog},\delta}(y)

satisfying

\mathcal{M}_{\nabla-\mathrm{Homog},\delta}(y)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that, for every
x,y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
with
|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}_{\nabla-\mathrm{Homog}}(y)
,

(2.9)

\left|\nabla_{x}G(x,y)-\nabla\bar{G}(x-y)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\chi_{e_{i}}(x)\partial_{i}\bar{G}(x-y)\right|\leq C|x-y|^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}|x-y|^{1-d}.

Remark 2.7
.

The exponent
1/2
in the right-hand side of (
2.9
) is not optimal but is sufficient for our purposes and can be obtained with a relatively short argument.

The following proposition provides decay estimates on the mixed derivative of the Green’s function. Once again, the proof can be fairly easily obtained by combining the results of
[
DG21
]

and
[
AD18
]
.

Proposition 2.8

(Decay estimate for the mixed derivative of the Green’s function)
.

For any
\delta>0
, there exist constants
s(d,\mathfrak{p},\delta)>0
,
C(d,\mathfrak{p},\delta)<\infty
, and, for any
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, there exists a minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\nabla-\mathrm{Decay}}(y)

satisfying

(2.10)

\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\nabla-\mathrm{Decay},\delta}(y)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that, for every
x,y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
with
|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\nabla-\mathrm{Decay},\delta}(y)
, one has the estimate

|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq\frac{C}{|x-y|^{d-\delta}}.

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

We present the proof in the case
d\geq 3
, the only change for
d=2
is to replace the bound in (
2.11
) below
with the
d=2
bound of
[
DG21
]
. Fix an
x,y\in\Z^{d}
and restrict to the event that
y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
.

Step 1: Preliminary results.

We start by recalling two results. The first one is a main result of
[
DG21
]
and establishes a quantitative homogenization theorem for the Green’s function on the percolation cluster.
Fix a small exponent
\delta>0
.
By
[
DG21
, Theorem 2]
, there exists a nonnegative random variable
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Homog},\delta}(y)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)
such that

(2.11)

|G(z,y)-\bar{G}(z-y)|\leq\frac{1}{|z-y|^{1-\delta}}\frac{C}{|z-y|^{d-2}},\quad\forall z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty},\,|z-y|\geq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Homog},\delta}(y).

The second one is a density estimate for the infinite cluster due to
[
PP96
, Theorem 1]
:
there exists a constant
\mathfrak{c}(\mathfrak{p})>0
and a minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that for all
r\geq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)
any ball of radius
\frac{r}{100}

containing the vertex
x
contains at least
\mathfrak{c}r^{d}
vertices in the cluster.

We next note that Theorem
2.4
and Theorem
2.5
can be combined so as to establish the following statement. For every
R\geq 2\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)
and every function
u\in\mathcal{A}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{2R}(x))
,
there exists a slope
\xi\in\R^{d}
such that

(2.12)

\inf_{a\in\R}\|u-\ell_{\xi}-a\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\|u\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}(x))},\quad\forall r\in\left[\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x),\frac{1}{2}R\right].

Finally, we require the following (standard) deterministic estimates on the homogenized Green’s function,
which hold for all
r\geq 1
:

(2.13)

\|\bar{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}\leq Cr^{2-d},

(2.14)

\|\nabla\bar{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}\leq Cr^{1-d},

(2.15)

\|\nabla^{2}\bar{G}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}\setminus B_{r})}\leq Cr^{-d}.

Step 2: Reduction to a weaker statement.

Let

(2.16)

\mathcal{X}(x,y)=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Homog},\delta}(y)\vee\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)\vee\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{corr},\delta}(x)\vee\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)\vee R_{0}

where
R_{0}
is a deterministic constant depending only on
d
,
\mathfrak{p}
, and
\delta
to be determined in Step 3 below
and write for notational simplicity,

g(\cdot):=G(\cdot,y)\quad\bar{g}(\cdot):=G(\cdot-y)\quad\mbox{and}\quad R=\frac{|x-y|}{2}.

We explain in this step that it suffices to prove the following weaker statement: if
R^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\mathcal{X}(x,y)
, then

(2.17)

\|\nabla g-(\nabla\bar{g}+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)})\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq CR^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\frac{3\delta}{2}},\quad\forall r\in[\mathcal{X}(x,y),R^{\frac{1}{2}}].

where, for any
z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
,

\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)}(z):=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\nabla\chi_{e_{i}}(z)\partial_{i}\bar{g}(z).

First, assume that
x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
and observe that (
2.17
) implies the following (trivial) bound:

\|\nabla g-(\nabla\bar{g}+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)})\|_{{L}^{\infty}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq Cr^{\frac{d}{2}}R^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\frac{3\delta}{2}},\quad\forall r\in[\mathcal{X}(x,y),R^{\frac{1}{2}}]

and therefore we may take
r=\mathcal{X}(x,y)
in the above to get

(2.18)

|\nabla g(x)-(\nabla\bar{g}(x)+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)}(x))|\leq C\mathcal{X}^{\frac{d}{2}}(x,y)R^{-d+\frac{\delta}{2}}.

We then define the random variable

\mathcal{M}(x,y):=2\mathcal{X}^{\frac{d}{\delta}}(x,y).

By shrinking the stochastic integrability exponent
s
and increasing the value of the constant
C
, we have
\mathcal{M}(x,y)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)
. Additionally note that
d/\delta\geq 2
. Thus, if
|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}(x,y)
, then
R^{1/2}\geq\mathcal{X}(x,y)
and
\mathcal{X}^{d/2}(x,y)R^{-\delta/2}\leq 1.
Combining the previous display with (
2.18
), we obtain that: if
|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}(x,y)
, then

|\nabla g(x)-(\nabla\bar{g}(x)+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)}(x))|\leq CR^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\delta}.

We then complete the proof by defining

\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\nabla-\mathrm{Decay},\delta}(y):=\sup\left\{|x-y|\,:\,x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\,\mathcal{M}(x,y)\geq|x-y|\right\}.

The integrability estimate
\mathcal{M}(x,y)\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)
and a union bound implies that
\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\nabla-\mathrm{Decay},\delta}(y)
satisfies the same stochastic integrability estimate.

Step 3: Proof of weaker statement.

It remains to prove (
2.17
). Note that
g
is harmonic in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\setminus\{y\}

and so

\mbox{$g$ is harmonic in $B_{R}(x)\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty}$}.

Assume that
R\geq\mathcal{X}(x,y)
(from (
2.16
)) and fix an
r\in[\mathcal{X}(x,y),R/2]
. By the large scale regularity (
2.12
) and then (
2.11
) and (
2.13
), we have that there exists a slope
\xi\in\R^{d}
such that

(2.19)

\inf_{a\in\R}\|g-\ell_{\xi}-a\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2}\|g\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}(x))}\leq Cr^{2}R^{-d}.

So, by Caccioppoli’s inequality (see,
e.g.
,
[
AD18
, Lemma 3.5]
or
[
Del99
]
),

(2.20)

\|\nabla g-\nabla\ell_{\xi}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq CrR^{-d}.

Also, by (
2.11
),

|g(z)-\bar{g}(z)|\leq\frac{1}{|z-y|^{d-2}}\frac{C}{|z-y|^{1-\delta}},\quad\forall z\in B_{R}(x)

and so

(2.21)

\|g-\bar{g}\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq CR^{1-d+\delta}.

Since
\bar{g}
is harmonic and hence smooth in
B_{R}(x)
, we have by Taylor’s theorem and then (
2.15
) that

(2.22)

\sup_{z\in B_{r}(x)}|\bar{g}(z)-(\bar{g}(x)+(z-x)\cdot\nabla\bar{g}(x))|\leq Cr^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\bar{g}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\leq Cr^{2}R^{-d}.

For the following computation, we introduce two notations for the affine functions

l_{g}(z)=(\bar{g}(x)+(z-x)\cdot\nabla\bar{g}(x))\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptl_{\xi}(z):=\xi\cdot z

and observe that
\nabla\ell_{\xi}=\xi+\nabla\chi_{\xi}=\nabla l_{\xi}+\nabla\chi_{\xi}.

We then write

\displaystyle r|\nabla\bar{g}(x)-\xi|

\displaystyle=r|\nabla(l_{g}-l_{\xi})|

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{a\in\R}\|l_{g}-(l_{\xi}+a)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}

(for
r\geq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{dense}}(x)
the
L^{2}
norm

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{of an affine function in $B_{r}(x)$ majorizes its gradient at $x$})

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{a\in\R}\|\bar{g}-(l_{\xi}+a)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}+Cr^{2}R^{-d}\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{eq:green-taylor-expansion})}

\displaystyle\leq C\inf_{a\in\R}\|g-(\ell_{\xi}+a)\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}+C\osc_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x)}\chi_{\xi}

\displaystyle\qquad+Cr^{2}R^{-d}+CR^{1-d+\delta}

(by the triangle inequality and (
2.21
))

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{2}R^{-d}+Cr^{\delta}|\xi|+CR^{1-d+\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{eq:green-large scale-regularity} and sublinearity of the corrector \eqref{eq:corrector-sublinearity-and-lipschitz})}.

Now, take the constant
R_{0}
in (
2.16
) sufficiently large so that
R_{0}^{1-\delta/2}
is larger than
2C
to see from the triangle inequality that

Cr^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\xi|\leq Cr^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)-\xi|+Cr^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)|\leq\frac{r}{2}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)-\xi|+Cr^{\frac{\delta}{2}}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)|

and so

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)-\xi|

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\delta-1}|\nabla\bar{g}(x)|+CrR^{-d}+Cr^{-1}R^{1-d+\delta}\quad\mbox{(by the above two displays)}

\displaystyle\leq Cr^{\delta-1}R^{1-d}+CrR^{-d}+Cr^{-1}R^{1-d+\delta}\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{eq:green-gradient-estimate})}

\displaystyle=CR^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\frac{\delta}{2}}+CR^{\frac{1}{2}-d}+CR^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\delta}\quad\mbox{(set $r=R^{1/2}$)}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\delta}.

From this and (
2.20
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\|\nabla g-(\nabla\bar{g}(x)+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(x)})\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla g-(\xi+\nabla\chi_{\xi})\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}+\left(|\nabla\bar{g}(x)-\xi|(1+\sup_{e\in B_{1}}\|\nabla\chi_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))})\right)

\displaystyle\leq CrR^{-d}+r^{\delta}R^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\frac{3\delta}{2}}\quad\mbox{(choose $r\leq R^{\frac{1}{2}}$)}.

However, we also have that,

\displaystyle\|(\nabla\bar{g}+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(\cdot)})-(\nabla\bar{g}(x)+\nabla\chi_{\nabla\bar{g}(x)})\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{r}(x))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\osc_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla\bar{g}|\right)\left(1+\sup_{e\in B_{1}}\|\nabla\chi_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq rR^{-d}(1+r^{\delta})\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{eq:green-hessian-estimate} and gradient of corrector bound~\eqref{eq:corrector-sublinearity-and-lipschitz})}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1}{2}-d+\frac{\delta}{2}}\quad\mbox{(choose $r\leq R^{\frac{1}{2}}$)}.

This implies (
2.17
).
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

Before proving Proposition
2.8
, we collect two preliminary results. The first one provides a Lipschitz regularity estimate for harmonic functions on the infinite percolation cluster and can be obtained by applying Theorem
2.4
with the value
k=0
and combining it with the Caccioppoli inequality (the exact statement below can be found in
[

, Proposition 2.14]
). The statement reads as follows. For any
r,R\geq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)
with

, and any harmonic function

,

\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq\frac{C}{R}\left\|u-(u)_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}(x)}\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}(x))}\leq\frac{C}{R}\left\|u\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{R}(x))}.

In particular, choosing
r=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)
yields

(2.23)

|\nabla u(x)|\leq C\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)^{\frac{d}{2}}\left\|\nabla u\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap B_{r}(x))}\leq\frac{C\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{R}\left\|u\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}(B_{R}(x)\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}.

The second result is a direct consequence of

DG21
, Theorem 3]
(or specifically
[
DG21
, Remark 1.3]
) and an integration of the heat kernel over time, and reads as follows: for any
y\in\mathbb{Zd}
, there exists a random variable
\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)
such that, if
y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
then for any
x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
with
|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)
,

(2.24)

\left|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{|x-y|^{d-1}}.

We next combine (
2.23
) and (
2.24
) and obtain that, for any
x,y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
with
|x-y|\geq 2\left(\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)\vee\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)\right)
,

\displaystyle\left|\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{|x-y|}\left\|\nabla_{y}G(\cdot,y)\right\|_{\underline{L}^{2}\left(B_{|x-y|/2}\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{reg}}(x)^{\frac{d}{2}}\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{|x-y|^{d}}.

The previous inequality implies Proposition
2.8
with the definition

The stochastic integrability estimates

and

2.6.
Topology in two dimensions and planarity

A key input into our proof of Theorem
1.3
is planarity. In this subsection we collect several standard results on the topology of the plane, lattice, and cluster. First, we first introduce the stereographic projection.

Definition 2.9

(Stereographic projection)
.

Let
\mathbb{S}^{2}\subseteq\R^{3}
be the Euclidean ball and let
N:=(0,0,1)
and
S:=(0,0,-1)
be the north and south poles of the sphere respectively.
We denote by
SP:\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\left\{N\right\}\to\R^{2}
the stereographic projection.

The stereographic projection is a homeomorphism
\R^{2}\to\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\left\{N\right\}
. Any path
\gamma
is mapped to a continuous path on the sphere through the stereographic projection (with the important remark that any infinite path
\gamma
is mapped to a continuous map
\tilde{\gamma}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{S}^{2}
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(1)=N
). Any bi-infinite path
\gamma

Definition 2.10

(Simply connected domain)

A nonempty open set

and its complement in the sphere

(using the stereographic projection) are connected.

Definition 2.11

(Simple boundary of simply connected sets)

A boundary point

if it has the following property: To

Remark 2.12
.

The boundary of any simply connected Lipschitz and bounded domain

is simple. All the sets we consider in this section can be written as a finite union of boxes of the form

The following lemma shows that if all the points of the boundary of a simply connected open set are simple, then it is a Jordan curve.

Lemma 2.13

(Simply connected sets with simple boundaries, Remark 14.20 of
[

]
)
.

Let

be a simply connected bounded domain whose boundary

is simple, then

is homeomorphic to a circle, i.e., it is a Jordan curve.

We next state the Jordan curve theorem on the sphere

Lemma 2.14

(Jordan curve theorem on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
)
.

Let

•

The sets

•

The sets

The result is obtained by combining Jordan curve theorem with the Riemann mapping theorem (with extension to the boundary in the case of simple boundaries) from

4.

In this section we prove Theorem

We use Lemma

Lemma 4.9
.

If the event that there exists an integer-valued harmonic function of linear growth on the cluster
has positive probability, then there exists a deterministic slope
\underline{p}\in\R^{d}

such that, on an event of probability one, there is an integer-valued harmonic function
of linear growth
\underline{p}
on the cluster.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
assuming Lemma
4.9
.

By Theorem
2.5
, the conclusion of Lemma
4.9
is incompatible with Lemma
4.7
applied with slope
\underline{p}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.9
.

The event that there exists some integer-valued harmonic function of linear growth on
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
is translation invariant and hence occurs with probability zero or one by ergodicity of Bernoulli percolation. Restrict to the event of probability one that it occurs. We first show, using compactness, that the set of integer-valued harmonic functions of linear growth is closed.

Fix a realization of the infinite cluster and consider a sequence of slopes
p_{n}\to\underline{p}
and observe that, there exists an integer-valued harmonic function with asymptotic growth
p_{n}\in\R^{d}
if and only if, for any edge
e\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)
,
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p_{n}}}(e)
is an integer. Since, for any edge
e\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)
, the map
p\mapsto\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e)
is linear, we have that
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p_{n}}}(e)\to\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{\underline{p}}}(e)
as
p_{n}\to\underline{p}
. A combination of the two previous observations shows that, for any edge
e\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)
,
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{\underline{p}}}(e)
is an integer which implies that there exists an integer-valued harmonic function with asymptotic growth
\underline{p}\in\R^{d}
, and completes the proof of closure.

Now, consider the random variable,
\underline{p}
defined as

\displaystyle\underline{p}=\arg\inf

\displaystyle\{|p|\geq 1\mid

\displaystyle\mbox{ there exists an integer-valued
harmonic function of growth $p$ at infinity}\},

where the
\arg\inf
indicates a choice of slope
p
such that
|p|\geq 1
is minimized,
and ties are broken by choosing the lexicographically smallest such
p
(the existence of this slope is guaranteed by the closure of the set of slopes
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that there exists an integer-valued harmonic function with growth
p
). Note that if an integer-valued harmonic function exists, then by multiplying
by a sufficiently large integer, we may produce an integer-valued harmonic function with asymptotic growth
|p|\geq 1
.

The random variable
\underline{p}
is translation invariant and hence, by ergodicity again, is deterministic.
Since the set of integer-valued harmonic functions of linear growth is closed, this implies the existence of an integer-valued harmonic function
on the cluster growing like
\underline{p}
at infinity.
∎

4.3.
Polynomial growth

In this section we indicate some progress towards extending Theorem
1.2
to all polynomial growths. The below is stated for quadratic polynomials,
but the statement and its proof can be extended to arbitrary polynomial growths.

Proposition 4.10
.

For each deterministic harmonic polynomial
\overline{p}\in\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{2}
, almost surely, there is no nonconstant integer-valued harmonic function
u
such that

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{1}{|x|}|u(x)-\overline{p}(x)|=0.

Proof.

Denote by
E
the event that there exists an integer-valued harmonic function
u
with

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{1}{|x|}|u(x)-\overline{p}(x)|=0

and suppose

\overline{p}(x)=x^{T}Mx+b^{T}x.

Suppose for sake of contradiction that
\mathbb{P}[E]>0
.
By the ergodic theorem, we have that almost surely,

(4.15)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B_{N}|}\sum_{x\in B_{N}}1\{\mbox{$E$ occurs in the environment translated by $x$}\}=\mathbb{P}[E]>0.

In particular, almost surely, there is some random
N
, two sites
y^{\pm}\in B_{N}
, and two integer-valued harmonic functions
u^{\pm}

such that,

p^{\pm}(x)=x^{T}Mx+(2My^{\pm}+b)^{T}x,

and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{1}{|x|}|u^{\pm}(x)-\overline{p}^{\pm}(x)|=0.

Note that, by (

), we may assume that

is not in the kernel of

(if

, the result is a direct consequence of Theorem

). Thus, setting

we have
constructed a non-trivial integer-valued harmonic function of linear growth,

contradicting Theorem

.
∎

5.
Integer-valued Laplacian

In this section, we fix the dimension to be
d=2
and prove the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Almost surely, any function
u:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\R
which decays to 0,
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)=0
,
and has integer-valued Laplacian,
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u\in\Z
, satisfies the following dichotomy:

•

Either the function
u
decays at most like
|x|^{-1}
at infinity, i.e.,

(5.1)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{|u(x)|}{|x|}>0;

•

Or
u
is finitely supported, i.e.,

(5.2)

u(x)\equiv 0,\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\setminus B_{R}$ for some $R>0$}.

Remark 5.2
.

This result fails on
\Z^{2}
as the function
u=G^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(\cdot-e_{1})+G^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}(\cdot+e_{1})-2G^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
, where
G^{\mathbb{Z}^{2}}
is the discrete Green’s function on the lattice, is not finitely supported and decays like
|x|^{-2}
– see
[
HJL19
]
or
[
SV09
]
.

The proof of Theorem
5.1
proceeds by successive reduction.

5.1.
Reduction to mean zero deterministic pole functions

We first reduce the proof of Theorem
5.1
to deterministic pole functions
f
on the event
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
using the two following propositions.

Proposition 5.3
.

Fix a deterministic and compactly supported function
f:\Z^{2}\to\Z
and let

(5.3)

u_{f}(x):=\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)G(x,y),

where
G
is the elliptic Green’s function on
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
.
Then, almost surely, either
\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)\neq 0
, or it satisfies (
5.1
), or it satisfies (
5.2
).

Remark 5.4
.

On the event of positive probability where
\mathrm{supp\,}f\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty}=\emptyset
, we have that
u_{f}\equiv 0
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Almost surely, if
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)\neq 0
, then
u_{f}=\Theta(\log|x|)
.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
assuming Proposition
5.3
and Proposition
5.5
.

We start by observing that the set of integer-valued and finitely supported functions
\mathscr{F}
is countable,
thus we may restrict to the event of probability one where Proposition
5.3
holds for all
f\in\mathscr{F}
.

As
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u_{f}(x)=0
and
-\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u_{f}=f\in\Z
, we must have that
f
is compactly supported. Consequently,
by extending
f
to be zero outside of
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
, we have that
f\in\mathscr{F}
. This completes the proof by the assumption that
u_{f}
decays to zero at infinity.
∎

In the remainder of this subsection, we prove Proposition
5.5
.
In subsequent subsections we further reduce the proof of Proposition
5.3

and then prove its reduction.

For the remainder of the proof, we fix a function
f:\Z^{2}\to\Z
as in the statement of Proposition
5.3
and a function
u_{f}
as in (
5.3
). Using the terminology of potential theory, we refer to the function
u_{f}
as the
potential
. We additionally allow all constants and exponents to depend on the function
f
(and the probability
\mathfrak{p}
).

We first observe that under the assumption
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
, the function
f
can be written as the divergence of a compactly supported vector field on the infinite cluster. This then allows us to represent
u_{f}
as a linear combination of gradients of the Green’s function.

Lemma 5.6
.

For almost every realization of the infinite percolation cluster satisfying the condition
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
, there exists a random compactly supported vector field
F:E_{d}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)\to\R

such that

(5.4)

u_{f}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)G(x,y)=\sum_{e\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}F(e)\nabla G(x,e).

The vector field
F
can be chosen so that there exist
C_{f}(\mathfrak{p},f)<\infty
and
s(\mathfrak{p})>0
such that

(5.5)

\left|\mathrm{supp\,}F\right|\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C_{f})~~\mbox{and}~~\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C_{f}).

Proof.

Fix a realization of the percolation cluster and let
\square_{f}
be the smallest box centered at
0
such that
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})
contains
\mathrm{supp\,}f\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
,
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})\subset\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
and such that the Poincaré inequality applies in
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})
in the following form: there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{Poinc}}(\mathfrak{p})<\infty
such that, for any function
v:\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})\to\R
satisfying
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})}v(x)=0
,

\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}))}\leq C_{\mathrm{Poinc}}\mathrm{size}(\square_{f})\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}))}

Such a box exists almost surely and its size satisfies the stochastic integrability estimate

(5.6)

\mathrm{size}(\square_{f})\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C_{f}).

The Neumann problem (with the normalizing condition
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})}v(x)=0
)

(5.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}v=f&~\mbox{in}~\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}),\\
\mathbf{n}\cdot\nabla v=0&~\mbox{on}~\partial_{e}\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}),\end{aligned}\right.

is well-posed because the set
\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})
is connected and
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})}f(x)=0
. We then define
F(e):=\nabla v(e)
if
e\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})\right)
and
F(e)=0
if
e\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)\setminus E\left(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})\right)
. It is a consequence of the definition of
F
that
\nabla\cdot F=f
in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}

and the representation (
5.4
) follows by the discrete divergence theorem.

We finally prove the estimates (
5.5
). The first one is a consequence of (
5.6
) and the observation
\mathrm{supp\,}F\subseteq\square_{f}
. The second one follows from testing the function
v
in the Neumann problem (
5.7
) and applying the Cauchy-Schwarz and Poincaré inequalities. We obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}))}^{2}=\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f})}f(x)v(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{Poinc}}\mathrm{size}(\square_{f})\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\left\|\nabla v\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{*}(\square_{f}))},

which implies, using the identity
F=\nabla v
inside the box
\square_{f}
and that
F
is equal to
0
outside this box,

\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\leq C_{\mathrm{Poinc}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\mathrm{size}(\square_{f}).

The second estimate of (
5.5
) is then a consequence of the bound (
5.6
).
∎

We are now ready to complete the proof of Proposition
5.5
using Lemma
5.6
.

Proof of Proposition
5.5
.

As recalled above in (
2.24
), there exists a random variable

\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)

such that, if
y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
then for any
x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
with

|x-y|\geq\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y),

we have

(5.8)

\left|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)\right|\leq\frac{C\mathcal{M}_{\nabla\mathrm{-Decay}}(y)^{\frac{d}{2}}}{|x-y|}.

Pick a site
z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
, let

so that

has mean zero. Thus, letting

be defined by (
5.3
), we have by (

) and (

) that

. By definition, we have that

and by

∎

5.2.
Reduction to a statement on the corrected plane

In light of the previous proposition, we may restrict our attention to the event that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
.
We then show that Proposition
5.3
can be obtained as a consequence of the two following propositions.

The first one is a result related to the behavior of the corrected plane, its proof is the core of the argument and occupies the rest of Section
5
.

Proposition 5.7
.

For any fixed slope
p\in\R^{2}
, almost surely on the event
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
,

(5.9)

\mbox{either}~\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(y)\neq 0~\mbox{or}~|\mathrm{supp\,}u_{f}|<\infty.

The second proposition is a stochastic homogenization result which identifies the first order-term of the asymptotic behavior of the function
u_{f}
in terms of the corrected plane. Its proof builds upon the results collected and proved in the prior subsection, and is presented in the rest of this subsection.

Proposition 5.8
.

There exist a constant
C:=C(f,\mathfrak{p})<\infty
, an exponent
s:=s(f,\mathfrak{p})<\infty
and a minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{f}
satisfying the stochastic integrability estimate

(5.10)

\mathcal{M}_{f}\leq\mathcal{O}_{s}(C)

such that, if we consider a realization of the infinite cluster
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
such that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
, then, for any
x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
satisfying

,

(5.11)

\left|u_{f}(x)-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{i}}}(y)\right)\partial_{i}\bar{G}(x)\right|\leq C|x|^{-\frac{5}{4}},

where
\bar{G}
is a multiple of the elliptic Green’s function on
\R^{2}
.

Remark 5.9
.

We note that in the right hand side, the exponent
5/4
is strictly larger than
1
and that the two terms in the left-hand side (considered individually) decay like

. Their difference is thus smaller than their typical size.

Remark 5.10
.

Although in this section, we set

,
Proposition
5.8
is valid for any

by replacing

by

in the right-hand side of (

).
Moreover, the exponent

is not optimized but is sufficient for our purposes.

We first show how to complete the proof of Proposition
5.3
(and thus of Theorem
6.5
).

Proof of Proposition
5.3
assuming Proposition
5.7
and Proposition
5.8
.

By Proposition

, we may restrict to the event that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
.

Proof of Proposition
5.8
.

By Lemma
5.6
, we have the identity

u_{f}(x)=\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)G(x,y)=\sum_{e\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}F(e)\nabla_{y}G(x,e),

for a compactly supported vector field

satisfying the estimates (

). Denote by

and define the minimal scale

according to the formula

\mathcal{M}_{f}:=\left(\mathcal{M}_{\nabla-\mathrm{Homog},\frac{1}{8}}(0)\vee\mathrm{size}(\square_{f})\vee\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{corr},\frac{1}{2}}(0)\vee\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{C}_{\infty})}\right)^{16(2+2)}

Since the elliptic Green’s function on

is smooth away from its pole, we have the inequality, for any

\left|\nabla\bar{G}_{x}(\{y,y+e_{i}\})-\partial_{i}\bar{G}(x)\right|\leq\frac{C\left|y\right|}{|x|^{2}}.

5.3.
Further reduction to a statement on the level sets of harmonic functions

In this subsection we reduce, via a martingale sensitivity argument, the proof of Proposition
5.7
to the following statement.

Proposition 5.11
.

Almost surely, on the event that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
and
|\mathrm{supp\,}u_{f}|=\infty
, we have that

(5.13)

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B_{2^{k}}|}\left|\left\{e\in E(B_{2^{k}}):\nabla u_{f}(e)\neq 0\mbox{ and }\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e)\neq 0\right\}\right|>0.

Proof of Proposition
5.7
assuming Proposition
5.11
.

Fix
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and denote by
E
the event

E:=\{\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0\}\cap\{\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(y)=0\}\cap\left\{|\mathrm{supp\,}\,u_{f}|=\infty\right\};

we will show that

has probability zero. We first prove, using an argument similar to the proof of the Efron-Stein inequality, that the occurrence of the event

does not depend too much on the value of a single edge. We then show, using Proposition

has probability zero.

Step 1: The event
E
cannot not depend too much on the value of a single edge.

Order the edges of the lattice following a deterministic procedure and denote the set of ordered edges by
(e_{i})_{i\in\N}
. For each
n\in\N
, introduce the sigma-algebra

\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma\left(\mathbf{a}(e_{i})\,:\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\right),

that is,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
contains the information of the first
n
edges (where
\mathbf{a}(e_{i})=0
or
1
tells us whether or not the edge is open or closed). Then define the martingale

M_{n}:=\E\left[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}|\mathcal{F}_{n}\right],

and note that

(5.14)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\E\left[\left(M_{n+1}-M_{n}\right)^{2}\right]=\mathrm{var}\left[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\right]\leq 1

by the orthogonality of martingale increments and

(5.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\E\left[\left({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}-M_{n}\right)^{2}\right]=0

by the convergence theorem for bounded martingales.

The previous two properties imply that the event
E
is not very dependent on the value of the edge
e_{n}
. To state this precisely, we introduce the notation
\mathbf{a}=(\mathbf{a}(e_{i}))_{i\in\N}
and
\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}:=((\mathbf{a}(e_{i}))_{i<n},1-\mathbf{a}(e_{n}),(\mathbf{a}(e_{i}))_{i>n})
(
i.e.
, the environment
\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}
is equal to the environment
\mathbf{a}
except at the edge
e_{n}
where we have flipped the value).
We claim that for any fixed small
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an integer
n_{0}(\varepsilon)
so that, for all
n\geq n_{0}
,

(5.16)

\E\left[\left({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left(\mathbf{a}\right)-{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}\right)\right)^{2}\right]\leq\varepsilon.

To prove (
5.16
), we first choose
n
large enough so that, by (
5.14
), we have

\sum_{k\leq n-2}\E[(M_{k+1}-M_{k})^{2}]>\mathrm{var}\left[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\right]\left(1-\frac{\min(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p})}{4\max(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p})}\varepsilon\right).

This implies, by (
5.15
), that
\E[({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a})-M_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}))^{2}]<(\varepsilon\min(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p}))/(4\max(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p}))
. Since the value
\mathbf{a}(e_{i})
is sampled according to the Bernoulli distribution of parameter
\mathfrak{p}
independently of the collection
(\mathbf{a}(e_{j}))_{j\neq i}
, we have the inequality

\E[({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}})-M_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}))^{2}]\leq\frac{\max(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p})}{\min(\mathfrak{p},1-\mathfrak{p})}\E[({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a})-M_{n-1}(\mathbf{a}))^{2}]\leq\varepsilon/4.

Combining these inequalities yields (
5.16
).

Step 2: The event
E
is sensitive.

We show that,

(5.17)

{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a})=1,~\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})\neq 0,~\mbox{ and}~\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})\neq 0\implies{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}})=0,

where
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})
and
\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})
denote the difference of the values at the two vertices of the edge
e_{n}

of the corrected plane and
u_{f}
respectively (we are overloading notation here and using
\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})
to refer to this difference when
e_{n}
is closed but both of its endpoints are in the infinite cluster).
When either vertex of the edge
e_{n}
is not present in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
, we define
\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})=\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})=0
.

Lemma
2.1
guarantees that the gradient of the corrected plane in the environment
\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}
exists almost surely. We denote this corrected plane by
{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}
and the infinite cluster after flipping the edge
e_{n}
,
i.e.
, changing
\mathbf{a}(e_{n})
from 0 to 1 or from 1 to 0, by
\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}
.

To prove (
5.17
), we first rule out the case where the set of vertices in
\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}
does not coincide with the set of vertices in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
. If the set becomes larger after flipping the edge, then we have by definition that
\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})=0
(as we have connected a finite isolated component to the infinite cluster). If the set becomes smaller, then
e_{n}
must disconnect an isolated component and hence
\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})=0
.

Now, suppose that the set of vertices of
\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}
and
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
are the same and denote by
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}}
the graph Laplacian on the infinite cluster (with the edge flipped). We first note that, if
\nabla{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(e_{n})=0
, then the map
{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}
is harmonic on the infinite cluster
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
and thus the difference between the two functions
ℓ

p

and
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}^{e_{n}}
is constant (as the difference
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}^{e_{n}}
is a sublinear harmonic function on the infinite cluster). The same argument shows that if
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})=0
then
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}^{e_{n}}
is constant. These observations imply that
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})\neq 0
if and only if
\nabla{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(e_{n})\neq 0
.

Since the set of vertices of the two infinite clusters coincide, we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(y)-{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(y))

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}-\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u_{f}(y)\left(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(y)-{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(y))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u_{f}(y)(\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}-\Delta_{\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}}){\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(y)

(integration by parts and
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}=\Delta_{\mathscr{C}^{e_{n}}_{\infty}}{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}=0
)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}})(e_{n})\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})\nabla{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(e_{n}).

We note that the integration by parts is justified because the difference
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}^{e_{n}}
is sublinear (and in fact one could prove that it converges to
0
at infinity) and the function
u_{f}
and its gradient decay sufficiently fast to zero at infinity.

Since the event
E
includes
\{\sum_{y\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(y)\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(y)=0\}
, the previous display shows

{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a})=1~\mbox{and}~\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})\neq 0~\mbox{and}~\nabla{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(e_{n})\neq 0\implies{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}})=0.

This shows (
5.17
) as (as mentioned above)
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})\neq 0
implies that
\nabla{\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}}^{e_{n}}(e_{n})\neq 0
.

Step 3: Conclusion.

In terms of indicator functions, the property (
5.17
) can be rewritten as

1\{E\cap\left\{\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})\neq 0\right\}\cap\left\{\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})\neq 0\right\}\}\left(\mathbf{a}\right)\leq\left({\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left(\mathbf{a}\right)-{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left(\mathbf{a}^{e_{n}}\right)\right)^{2}.

By taking the expected value of the previous display and using (
5.16
), we obtain, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon)
,

\E\left[1\{E\cap\left\{\nabla u_{f}(e_{n})\neq 0\right\}\cap\left\{\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e_{n})\neq 0\right\}\}\right]\leq\varepsilon.

Summing over the edges in the ball
B_{2^{k}}
, we obtain, for
k
large enough,

\E\left[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left|\left\{e\in E(B_{2^{k}})\,:\,\nabla u_{f}(e)\neq 0~\mbox{and}~\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e)\neq 0\right\}\right|\right]\leq\varepsilon|B_{2^{k}}|.

In particular, we have

(5.18)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{|B_{2^{k}}|}\E\left[{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E}\left|\left\{e\in E(B_{2^{k}})\,:\,\nabla u_{f}(e)\neq 0~\mbox{and}~\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e)\neq 0\right\}\right|\right]=0,

which contradicts (
5.13
) except if
\mathbb{P}(E)=0
. ∎

5.4.
Topological obstructions in the level set of the potential

Our goal for the next two subsections is to prove Proposition
5.11
. We seek to show, for a fixed direction
p\in\R^{2}
, that the set of
sensitive
edges,
i.e.
, edges
e
for which
\nabla u_{f}(e)\neq 0
and
\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(e)\neq 0
, has a density. Our strategy is to identify a ‘good event’
which occurs with positive density in the cluster and map each good event to a sensitive edge.

Specifically, in this subsection, we use Theorem
1.1
and regularity properties of the cluster to
show that we may find a positive density of ‘good’ edges
\tilde{e}
in the cluster for which

\nabla\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{p}}(\tilde{e})
is large and for which the cluster near the edge is sufficiently well-behaved.
In the subsequent subsection we use an exploration process based on the construction of a block-cut tree to show that the level set of the potential around every good edge contains a nearby sensitive edge.

For the entirety of this subsection and the next, we restrict ourselves to the event in the statement of the proposition that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}f(x)=0
, and
|\mathrm{supp\,}u_{f}|=\infty
. Recall that this event implies that
u_{f}
decays to zero at infinity.
Also note that as
|\mathrm{supp\,}u_{f}|=\infty
, we have that
(\mathrm{supp\,}f)\cap\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\neq\emptyset
, (as otherwise
u_{f}\equiv 0
).

In Section
5.4.1
we collect some preliminary definitions on graphs, connectivity,
and the block-cut tree. Given a real number
a\in\R
, we denote by

(5.19)

L_{a}:=\left\{x\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\,:\,u_{f}(x)=a\right\}

the level set of
u_{f}
at the value
a
. In Section
5.4.2
we use planarity
and the fact that
u_{f}
is harmonic outside of a finite set to show that the connected components of
L_{a}
cannot contain many disjoint infinite paths. Then, in Section
5.4.3

we show that the biconnected components of
L_{a}
are not too large. Finally
in Section
5.4.4
we set up the topological obstructions and good event which
we couple with sensitive edges.

5.4.1.
Graph theory definitions and block cut tree

We consider graphs
G:=(V,E)
which are a collection of vertices
V

and set of unordered vertices
E
also called edges. A subgraph induced by
V^{\prime}\subset V
is the graph
(V,^{\prime}E^{\prime})
where
E^{\prime}
are the subset of edges with both ends in
V^{\prime}
.

•

The
degree
of a vertex
x\in V
is the cardinality of the set of edges
e\in E
satisfying
x\in e
.

•

A
(finite) path
in
G
is a function
\gamma:[1,\ldots,\mathrm{end}]\to G
(with
\mathrm{end}\in\N
if
\gamma
is finite) such that
\{\gamma(i+1),\gamma(i)\}\in E
.

•

We say that
G
is
connected
if for every two vertices

x,y\in V
, there is a path
\gamma\subset V
connecting
x
and
y
.

•

The graph
G
is
biconnected
if for every
x\in G
, the induced subgraph
G\setminus\{x\}
is connected.

•

A
connected component
of
G
is an induced subgraph of
G
which is connected
and is maximal for the inclusion.
Biconnected components
are defined analogously.

•

A
cut-vertex

v
of
G
is a vertex which lies in a connected component
such that the component becomes disconnected in the induced subgraph formed by removing the vertex.

We note that the collection of connected components of a graph
G
partitions the set of vertices of
G
, but the collection of biconnected components do not (they in fact partition the set of edges of
G
), and two different biconnected components overlap at at most one vertex which is a cut-vertex of
G
. Reciprocally, any cut-vertex belongs to at least two biconnected components of
G
. Building upon these observations, it is possible to construct the block-cut tree of a graph
G
, and we first introduce a few defintions.

•

A
tree

T=(V,E)
is a graph in which any two vertices are connected by exactly one path.

•

A
rooted tree

(T,v)
is a tree in which a special (labeled) vertex
v\in V
has been singled out. We equip the rooted tree
(T,v)
with a partial order by writing, for
x,y\in V
,
x\preceq y
if the unique path going from the root to
y
passes through
x
. We denote by
\dist(x,y)
the length of the path connecting
x
to
y
.

•

Given a rooted tree
(T,v)
with
T=(V,E)
,
a leaf
is a vertex of
V\setminus\{v\}
whose degree is equal to
1
.

•

A
branch
is a path from the root to a leaf.

•

Given a vertex
y\in V\setminus\{v\}
, we define
the parent
of
y
to be the only vertex
x\in V
such that
x\preceq y
and
\dist(x,v)=\dist(y,v)-1
. Similarly, we define
the children
and
the descendants
of
y
to be respectively the collections of vertices

\{x\in V\,:\,y\preceq x\,\mbox{and}\,\dist(x,v)=\dist(y,v)+1\}~\mbox{and}~\{y\in V\,:\,x\preceq y\}.

•

The
block-cut tree

G_{\mathrm{tree}}
of a connected graph
G:=(V,E)
is the tree
formed in the following way. The vertex set of
G_{\mathrm{tree}}
is the collection of all the biconnected components of
G
and all the cut-vertices of
G
.
There is an edge between a biconnected component
C
and a cut-vertex
x
if and only if
x\in C
(see Figure
12
). Note that with this construction, biconnected components are only connected to cut-vertices and cut-vertices are only connected to biconnected components.

Figure 12.

\Z^{2}
with its biconnected components drawn in alternating blue and red. Multi-colored vertices are cut vertices which belong to multiple biconnected components. On the right
is the block-cut tree, the biconnected components are drawn in the blocks with the same labelling as on the left. The cut points between the biconnected components are displayed as black vertices.

5.4.2.
Unbounded level sets of the potential

In this section, we establish that the connected components of the level sets of the function
u_{f}
cannot contain more than a deterministic number (depending only on the function

) of disjoint infinite paths.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a connected component of
L_{0}
. Then there exists at most
(\left|\mathrm{supp\,}f\right|+2)
disjoint infinite paths in
\mathcal{S}
.

Remark 5.13
.

This statement is only relevant for the level set associated with the value
0
as all the other level sets are bounded and thus finite (since

decays to

at infinity)

Before proving Proposition
5.12
, we collect a few results pertaining to supercritical percolation and the level sets of discrete harmonic functions. They are written in Lemma
5.14
to Lemma
5.18
below. We start with a connectivity lemma on the cluster.

Lemma 5.14
.

There exists a minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{annuli}}
which is finite almost surely such that for all
R\geq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{annuli}}

there is a loop in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap(Q_{2R}\setminus Q_{R})
which has nonzero winding number around the origin.

Remark 5.15
.

We only need to prove that the minimal scale
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{annuli}}
is finite almost surely, but we remark that the proof below is quantitative and shows that its tail decays exponentially fast.

Proof.

By, for example,
[
Kes82
, Theorem 11.1 and (11.5)]
, there exists a constant
c(\mathfrak{p})\in(0,1)
such that

\mathbb{P}[\mbox{there exists an open left-to-right path in $[0,2R]\times[0,R]$}]\geq 1-Ce^{-cR},\quad\mbox{for all $R\geq 1$}.

The claim (which is standard, see,
e.g.
,
[
Gri87
]
for a similar argument) then follows by a union bound and planarity.
∎

In the next lemma we use the following stronger version of the maximum principle which holds only when the Green’s function of the graph is unbounded.
Note in the statement the important omission of the assumption that

at infinity.

.

Suppose

is smooth around the value

), we see that there exists a (universal) constant

Combining the two previous displays completes the proof of (

∎

We finally complete this section by proving a refinement of Proposition

. Specifically, we establish that since the renormalized percolation stochastically dominates a (very) supercritical site Bernoulli percolation, any path must intersect the infinite cluster at least a fraction of the length between the two endpoints of the path. The statement is contained in the following proposition, and can be compared to
[

, Lemma 5.3]
.

Proposition 5.24
.

There exists a constant
R_{1}(\mathfrak{p},f)<\infty
such that for any
R\geq R_{1}
, the following holds. There exist constants
C(R)<\infty
,
c(R)>0
,
c_{1}(R)>0
such that, for any

,

(5.26)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\gamma\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\,\mbox{ with }\gamma(0)=0,~\left|\gamma(\mathrm{end})\right|\geq K~\mbox{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{|\gamma|}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{E_{R}\left(\gamma(i)\right)}\leq c_{1}|\gamma(\mathrm{end})|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\exp\left(-cK\right).

Proof.

We first set
R_{1}:=R_{0}\vee\left(10(C_{f}+1)\right)^{2}
where
R_{0}
is the constant in the proof of Proposition
5.21
which ensures that, for any

, the renormalized percolation process of size

stochastically dominates a i.i.d. Bernoulli site percolation on

with probability

(hence supercritical).

We next fix
R\geq R_{1}
and consider the renormalized percolation process of size
R
. We then introduce the notions of distance and rectangles in the renormalized lattice:

•

Given two sites

and

•

For

N
in the renormalized lattice

2N
and short side length
N
in the renormalized lattice according to the formula

\mathrm{HR}^{R}_{N}:=\left\{x+Q_{R}\,:\,\frac{x}{2R+1}\in[-2N,2N]\times[0,2N]\right\}.

\mathrm{VR}^{R}_{N}:=\left\{x+Q_{R}\,:\,\frac{x}{2R+1}\in[0,2N]\times[-2N,2N]\right\}.

We next make the two observations. First, to each path
\gamma
in the infinite cluster, we can associate a path in the renormalized lattice obtained by listing all the sites of the form
(x+Q_{R})
with

visited by the path
\gamma
and erasing the loops. We denote this path in the renormalized lattice by
[\gamma]
and observe that, for some universal constant

(5.27)

[\gamma](0)=Q_{R}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dist_{R}([\gamma](\mathrm{end}),Q_{R})\leq\frac{\left|\gamma(\mathrm{end})\right|}{CR}.

Next, since the renormalized percolation process of size
R
dominates a supercritical Bernoulli site percolation, it possesses Kesten channels with exponentially high probability.
That is, by
[
Kes82
, Theorem 11.1]
and a union bound, for any

(5.28)

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mbox{for all $N\geq N_{0}$ there exists at least $c_{2}N$}

\displaystyle\mbox{disjoint horizontal crossings of open sites in the rectangle $\mathrm{HR}^{R}_{N}$}

\displaystyle\mbox{for the renormalized percolation process of size $R$})

\displaystyle\geq 1-C\exp\left(-cN_{0}\right).

A similar statement holds for vertical rectangles (with vertical crossings). We remark that the constants

depend only on the probability of the stochastically dominated Bernoulli site percolation, and can thus be chosen universally (at the cost of increasing the value of

).

We then combine the two previous remarks as follows. First, the conditions (
5.27
) imply that we may find a constant
C
(depending only on

•

Either the path

•

Or the path
[\gamma]

In any of each of the two cases, we may apply the inequality (

to depend on

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\exists\gamma\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\,\mbox{ with }\gamma(0)=0,~\left|\gamma(\mathrm{end})\right|\geq K~\mbox{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{|[\gamma]|}{\boldsymbol{1}}_{[\gamma](i)\in\mathcal{C}_{\infty}}\leq c_{1}|\gamma(\mathrm{end})|\right)

Using the implication (

∎

5.4.4.
Building the obstruction

We introduce the good event which we will use in the next subsection, together with the
topological results which we have just established, to prove Proposition
5.11
.

Start by setting
R:=R_{1}(f,\mathfrak{p})<\infty

is the constant which appears in the statement of Proposition

. For a parameter

and

where

is the constant which appears in the statement of Proposition

and

We then define a second good event

), we have the lower bound

\mathbb{P}\left(G_{2}(x,K)\right)\geq 1-C\exp\left(-cK^{s}\right).

We finally fix a constant

and define and consider a third good event

and symmetry arguments ensure that this assumption can be made without loss of generality. Using the previous observations, we see that for any

, we may choose

(depending on

) such that the event

Lemma 5.25
.

For every
C_{\chi}\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant

such that, for any
K\geq K_{0}
and all

, the event

(5.34)

G(x,C_{\chi},K):=G_{1}(x,K)\cap G_{2}(x,K)\cap G_{3}(x,C_{\chi})

\mathbb{P}[G(x,C_{\chi},K)]\geq c(\mathfrak{p},C_{\chi})>0
.

Proof.

By stationarity, it suffices to consider the case
x=0
. Let
C_{\chi}
be given.
By (
5.30
) and (
5.32
), we have that as

goes to infinity,
\mathbb{P}[G_{1}(0,K)\cap G_{2}(0,K)]
goes to one
and by Proposition

,
\mathbb{P}[G_{3}(0,C_{\chi})]\geq c(\mathfrak{p},C_{\chi})>0
.

Thus, by choosing

, we have that
\mathbb{P}[G_{1}(0,K)\cap G_{2}(0,K)]\geq 1-c(C_{\chi})/2
.
Hence, a union bound implies the claim. ∎

This, together with the ergodic theorem, implies the following density result. In the following lemma, we recall the definition of the triangle
T_{L}:=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Z^{2}\,:\,|x|\leq L,x_{1}\geq|x_{2}|\}
.

Lemma 5.26
.

For every
C_{\chi}\in[1,\infty)
, there exists a constant
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p},f,C_{\chi})<\infty
such that, for any

,

\liminf_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sum_{y\in T_{L}}1\{{G(y,C_{\chi},K)}\}=\mathbb{P}[G(0,C_{\chi},K)].

Proof.

By, for example, the proof of

, which combines the ergodic theorem together with translation invariance,
for every pair of Lipschitz domains

with

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{|L\cdot V|}\sum_{y\in(L\cdot W)\cap\Z^{2}}1\{{G(y,C_{\chi},K)}\}=\mathbb{P}(G(y,C_{\chi},K))\frac{|W|}{|V|}>0

denote the scaling of the domain by

. This implies the claim with

and

.
∎

5.5.
Exploring the block-cut tree

Building upon all the preliminary results established in the previous sections, we establish the following result, which implies Proposition

.

Lemma 5.27
.

There exist three constants
C_{\chi}(\mathfrak{p},f)<\infty
,
K_{0}(\mathfrak{p},f)<\infty
, and

(ii)

Proof of Proposition
5.11
assuming Lemma

.

By Lemma

, for almost every realization of the percolation configuration, there exists a mapping

. By Lemma

∎

Proof of Lemma
5.27
.

We split the proof into several steps. In the first step, we collect several preliminary objects
and construct a particular subgraph of the level set of the potential
u_{f}
around a good edge,
\mathcal{G}
. This subgraph
has the important property that every site in
\mathcal{G}
is connected to the good edge along a path
upon which the corrected plane
ℓ

p

is strictly increasing. We use this ordering to construct, in Step 2,
a rooted block-cut tree of
\mathcal{G}
.

We seek to use this tree and regularity properties of the corrected plane to find a nearby sensitive edge.
Our strategy is to show that if the nearest sensitive edge is far away, then either some topological obstruction
is violated or the corrected plane grows too quickly.

To that end, in Step 3, we define the flux of the corrected plane through cut-vertices.
There we establish a basic identity on the flux through cut-vertices of the tree.
In Step 4, we show that the flux bounds the growth of the corrected plane through branches
of the block-cut tree.

We use this lower bound in Step 5 to construct an exploration of the block-cut tree which
maximizes the growth of the corrected-plane. Finally in Step 6, we show that this exploration
must terminate at a nearby sensitive edge.

Step 1: Construction of the set

(linearly) as

.

We select the value of the constant

5.24

i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in[x_{0}]\\
y\sim x_{\mathrm{cut}}\end{subarray}}(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)).

We similarly define the outgoing flux of the corrected plane at the cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
: if we let
[x_{1}],\ldots,[x_{n}]
be the biconnected components which are the children of
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
, then, for any

, we define

i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}},[x_{i}]):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in[x_{i}]\\
y\sim x_{\mathrm{cut}}\end{subarray}}(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})).

In this step we prove the following result: if we consider a biconnected component
[x]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
(which is not the root nor a leaf), denote by
x_{\mathrm{cut},0}
the cut-vertex which is its parent, and by
x_{\mathrm{cut},1},\ldots,x_{\mathrm{cut},n}
the cut-vertices which are its children, then we have the inequalities

(5.38)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i})\geq i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x])

and if we denote by
[x],[x_{1}],\ldots,[x_{m}]
the children of
x_{\mathrm{cut},0}
(since we are in two dimensions, we have that

), then we have

(5.39)

i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x])+\sum_{i=1}^{m}i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x_{i}])\geq i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0}).

To prove (
5.38
), we denote by
X:=[x]\setminus\{x_{\mathrm{cut},n},x_{\mathrm{cut},1},\ldots,x_{\mathrm{cut},n}\}
the biconnected component after removing the cut-vertices. We then use the harmonicity of the corrected plane on the infinite cluster, perform a discrete integration by parts and obtain

(5.40)

0=\sum_{y\in X}\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\setminus X,y\in[x]\\
z\sim y\end{subarray}}\left(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)\right).

The sum on the right-hand side can be decomposed into two terms, depending on whether the vertex
z
belongs to the graph
\mathcal{G}
or not. In the case where the vertex
z
belongs to the graph
\mathcal{G}
, we use the definitions of the incoming flux of a cut-vertex and obtain the identity

(5.41)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{G}\setminus X,y\in X\\
z\sim y\end{subarray}}\left(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i})-i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x]).

For the pair of vertices
z
and
y
satisfying
z\sim y
,
y\in[x]
, and
z\notin\mathcal{G}
, we observe that, since
[x]
is not a leaf of the tree

, we must that
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)\leq\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)
. Indeed, if we had
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)>\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)
then either

and the definition of the graph

would imply

which is a contradiction, or

and in that case the edge

would satisfy (
5.37
), and

would thus be a leaf of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
(by construction), which is also a contradiction. Collecting the previous observations, we see that

(5.42)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{G},y\in[x]\\
z\sim y\end{subarray}}\left(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)\right)\leq 0.

Combining (
5.40

5.41

Similarly, we use the definitions of the incoming and outgoing flux and the inequality (
5.42
) to obtain

\displaystyle 0=\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0})

\displaystyle=i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x])-i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\setminus\mathcal{G}\\
z\sim x_{\mathrm{cut},0}\end{subarray}}\left(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(z)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0})\right)

\displaystyle\leq i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x])-i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0}),

to prove (

), completing this step.

Step 4: Regularity of the block-cut tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
.

In this step, we prove the following properties on the block-cut tree

:

(i)

The number of (potentially infinite) branches of the tree

is bounded by a constant

depending only on the support of

(ii)

If we let

its parent in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}

We defer the proof of (i) to Lemma

below. To prove (ii), fix a biconnected component
[x]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
(which is neither the root nor a leaf), let
x_{\mathrm{cut},0}
be its parent in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
and

one of its children. Using the definition of the incoming flux

and the observation that any vertex has at most

Next using the definition of the graph

Step 5: Exploring a path of large flux in the block-cut tree

We have now established all the preliminary ingredients to construct a short path in the block-cut tree which ends at a sensitive edge. This path is constructed
via an exploration of the block-cut tree, which we show (in the next step) has to end quickly.

Denote by
[x_{\mathrm{root}}]
the root of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
(
i.e.
, the biconnected component of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
containing the edge

). We then let
\mathcal{L}
be the collections of all the leafs of the tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
together with the root
[x_{\mathrm{root}}]
. By point (i) in Step 4, we have the following identity

(5.44)

(\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])-1)+\sum_{v\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}\setminus\mathcal{L}}(\deg(v)-2)\leq C_{\mathrm{branches}}.

Let us denote by
x_{\mathrm{root},1},\ldots,x_{\mathrm{root},\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])}
the cut-vertices which are the children of the root
[x_{\mathrm{root}}]
in the tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
. Using the assumption
\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(e_{1})-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(0)\geq C_{\chi}
, we have that

\sum_{i=1}^{\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])}i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{root},i})\geq C_{\chi}.

The previous inequality implies that there exists a cutpoint, which we may choose without loss of generality to be
x_{\mathrm{root},1}
such that

i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{root},1})\geq\frac{C_{\chi}}{\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])}.

We then construct a path
[\gamma]\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
in the tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
so as to maximize the flux of the corrected plane along the path. It is defined according to the following iterative procedure:

•

The path
[\gamma]
starts from the root
[x_{\mathrm{root}}]
and first visit the cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{root},1}
;

•

If
[\gamma]
visits a cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
, we then select the biconnected component
[x]
which maximises the outgoing flux
i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}},[x])
among all the biconnected components which are the children of
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
(and use an arbitrary criterion to break ties). We then extend the path
[\gamma]
so that it visits this biconnected component.

•

If
[\gamma]
visits a biconnected component
[x]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
, we select the cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
which maximises the incoming flux
i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})
among all the cut-vertices which are the children of
[x]
(and break ties using an arbitrary criterion). We then extend the path
[\gamma]
so that it visits the cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
. If there is no such cutpoint, then the path
[\gamma]
has reached a leaf of the tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
and we stop the iterative construction.

The path
[\gamma]
is constructed so as to satisfy the following inequality: for any biconnected component
[x]\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
which is neither the root nor a leaf, if we denote by
x_{\mathrm{cut},0}
the parent of
[x]
in
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
and
x_{\mathrm{cut},1}
the child of
[x]
which belongs to the path
[\gamma]
, then

i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},1})\geq\frac{i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},0},[x])}{\deg([x])-1}.

Similarly, for any cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
, if we denote by
[x]
the child of
x_{\mathrm{cut}}
which belongs to the path
[\gamma]
, then we have

i_{\mathrm{out}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}},[x])\geq\frac{i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})}{\deg(x_{\mathrm{cut}})-1}.

Combining the two previous inequalities, we obtain the following (crude) lower bound: for any cut-vertex
x_{\mathrm{cut}}\in[\gamma]
,

i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})\geq\frac{C_{\chi}}{\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])\prod_{v\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}\setminus\mathcal{L}}(\deg(v)-1)}.

Using the upper bound
e^{x-1}\geq x
valid for any

together with the lower bound (
5.44
), we deduce that

(5.45)

\displaystyle i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut}})

\displaystyle\geq C_{\chi}\exp\left(-(\deg([x_{\mathrm{root}}])-1)-\sum_{v\in\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}\setminus\mathcal{L}}(\deg(v)-2)\right)

\displaystyle\geq C_{\chi}\exp\left(-C_{\mathrm{branches}}\right).

Step 6: The exploration cannot be too long.

We now conclude by showing the path
[\gamma]
constructed in the previous step must be short. We distinguish between two cases depending on whether the path
[\gamma]
is finite or infinite.

Case 1: the path
[\gamma]
is finite.
We let

be the number of cut-vertices of
[\gamma]
, and denote these cut-vertices by

. We then denote by

the biconnected components of
[\gamma]
(since the cut-vertices and the biconnected components alternate along
[\gamma]
, there must be

can be written as follows
[\gamma]:=([x_{\mathrm{root}}],x_{\mathrm{cut},1},[x_{1}],\ldots,x_{\mathrm{cut},N},[x_{N}])
.

We then observe that the endpoint
[x_{N}]
of
[\gamma]
must be a leaf of
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
(otherwise the iterative construction would continue). As mentioned above and by construction of the tree
\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{tree}}
, there exists an edge
e=(y,z)\in E\left(\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\right)
(with
y\in[x_{N}]
and

) on the boundary of the biconnected component
[x_{N}]
such that the sensitivity condition (
5.37
) is satisfied. We then consider a path
\gamma\subseteq\mathcal{G}
connecting the edge
(x_{0},x_{0}+e_{1})
to the edge

along which the corrected plane is increasing (the existence of this path is guaranteed by the definition of the graph
\mathcal{G}
). Additionally, the path
\gamma
must pass through the cut-vertices
x_{\mathrm{cut},1},\ldots,x_{\mathrm{cut},N}
. We may thus decompose the difference of the corrected plane between the vertices

and

as follows

\displaystyle\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},1})-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{0}))+(\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},N}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i+1})-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i+1})-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i}),

where in the second inequality, we used that the first two terms

\displaystyle\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(y)-\hyperref@@ii[eq:corrected-plane]{\ell_{e_{1}}}(x_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}i_{\mathrm{in}}(x_{\mathrm{cut},i})\quad\mbox{(by ~\eqref{eq:increasevaluefluxcorr})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(N-1)}{4}C_{\chi}\exp\left(-C_{\mathrm{branches}}\right)\quad\mbox{(by ~\eqref{eq:lowerboundiin1410})}.

We next recall the formula for the constant

(5.46)

C_{\chi}:=\frac{100R^{2}\exp\left(C_{\mathrm{branches}}\right)}{c_{1}},

the definition of the constant

and the one of the event

which, together with the previous inequality allows us to conclude. Indeed, by the previous inequality and the definition of the good event

, we obtain that, if

, this implies

which is a contradiction (as

). We have thus obtained that

which is the desired conclusion.

It only remains to prove the lower bound (

\left|\gamma\right|\geq 4R^{2}
and if there exists an index

such that the event

holds, then there exists an index

. To justify this observation, let us assume that the event

\gamma(i)

[\gamma(i)]
the biconnected component containing

is not a cut-vertex). If for any index

Using that the good event

(as

Case 2: the path

6.
Abelian sandpile

A sandpile is a function from the set of vertices of an undirected graph to the nonnegative integers.
The value of the function is to be thought of as the number of chips or grains at
that vertex. A vertex is
unstable
if it has at least as many chips as it has edges,
in which case it
topples
, giving one chip to each of the vertices it shares an edge with.
With appropriate boundary conditions, sandpiles can be
stabilized
, that is, unstable vertices topple until every vertex is stable
and the order in which unstable vertices topple does not affect the final, stable configuration.

In this section, we consider two aspects of the Abelian sandpile model. First, we briefly exposit, in Section
6.1
, the large scale behavior of sandpiles on infinite graphs and explain the connection to Theorem
1.2
. The rest of this section concerns the
Abelian sandpile Markov chain
.
The chain is introduced in Section
6.2
.
We then discuss the connection to Theorem
5.1

and also give an argument which shows, despite this result, that the Abelian sandpile Markov chain mixes (up to constants) as slowly on the supercritical percolation cluster as it does on the full lattice.

This is mostly an expository section and several open questions are presented. The only new material is Theorem
6.5
which is a mixing result for the sandpile on the percolation cluster.

6.1.
Sandpile growth

Condition the origin to be contained in the infinite cluster
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
of
\Z^{d}
. Start with
n
chips at the origin in the cluster and topple
unstable vertices until every vertex is stable. Denote the final collection of chips by
s_{n}:\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\to\{0,\ldots,\deg_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}-1\}
.
Simulations show that when
n
is large the support of
s_{n}
approximates a Euclidean ball intersected with the cluster — this has been documented by Sadhu and Dhar
[
SD11
]
. As we explain further below, Theorem
1.2

and Proposition
4.10
provides some evidence that this indeed occurs.

Conjecture 6.1
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1)
.
Almost surely, as
n\to\infty
, the support of the sequence of rescaled sandpiles

\overline{s}_{n}(x):=s_{n}([n^{1/d}x])
converges in
\R^{d}
to a ball.
Moreover,
\overline{s}_{n}
converges weakly-* to a function which takes on two values;
\bar{s}:\R^{d}\to\{0,s_{\mathfrak{p}}\}

where
s_{\mathfrak{p}}>0
is a constant depending only on
\mathfrak{p}
and
d
.

The significance of the previous conjecture is in its contrast with the
sandpile on
\Z^{d}
(and other periodic lattices)
[
PS13
,
LPS17
,
LPS16
,
BR24a
,
BR24b
]
— see Figure
22
. In this case, one starts with
n
chips at the origin on, say,
\Z^{d}
on a (possibly random) initial
background

\eta:\Z^{d}\to\Z
, which can be thought of as an initial arrangement of chips,
and runs the same toppling dynamics as before.

It is known in this case (under some mild assumptions on
\eta
,
e.g.
, i.i.d and bounded from above by
d
) that the sandpile has a deterministic scaling limit described by the Laplacian of the solution to a fully nonlinear elliptic PDE
[
BR21
]
, the
sandpile PDE
.
The (canonical) example
\eta\equiv 0
was first considered rigorously in
[
PS13
]
.

The sandpile PDE has a delicate dependence on both the lattice and the distribution of the random initial background,
\eta
. In the case when
\eta\equiv 0
and the underlying
lattice,
\mathbb{L}
, is embedded into
\R^{d}
, it is characterized
by a set of
allowed Hessians
, the set of
d\times d
symmetric matrices which have an integer-valued superharmonic representative
with that growth at infinity:

(6.1)

\Gamma_{\mathbb{L}}:=\{M\in\symm^{d}:\exists u:\mathbb{L}\to\Z\mbox{ such that }\mbox{$u(x)\geq\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Mx+o(|x|^{2})$ and $\Delta_{\mathbb{L}}u\leq 0$}\}.

These sets are conjectured to have a rich, fractal description for all periodic graphs and this is known rigorously in two cases,
\Z^{2}

[
LPS17
]
and the
F
-lattice,
[
BR24a
]
.
The set
\Gamma
provides an explanation for the kaleidoscopic patterns which appear
in large sandpiles
[
PS20
,
LPS16
]
.

Both proofs
[
LPS17
,
BR24a
]
proceed recursively
and the ‘base cases’ require the existence of integer-valued harmonic functions of quadratic growth,
e.g.
, on
\Z^{2}
the function
x\to(x_{1}^{2}-x_{2}^{2})
.
The lack of such ‘base cases’ on the cluster, Proposition
4.10
, suggest that
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
is a trivial set.

Conjecture 6.2
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1)
.
There exists a deterministic constant
s_{p}(\mathfrak{p},d)
such that, almost surely

\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}=\{M\in\symm^{d}:\Tr(M)\leq s_{p}\}.

Unlike the lattice, on the percolation cluster we do not yet have a rigorous relationship between
\Gamma_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
and the large scale properties of sandpiles.
In fact, it is not known that the scaling limit of the sandpile on the percolation cluster exists. This is because the programs
in
[
PS13
]
and
[
BR21
]
presuppose
affine-invariance
of the sandpile PDE — that is, that the sandpile PDE is purely second order. This affine-invariance is an easy consequence of the existence of linear growth integer-valued harmonic functions and hence such functions are an important feature of the proofs in
[
PS13
]
and
[
BR21
]
.
Consequently, due to Theorem
1.2
, establishing the following will require new ideas.

Conjecture 6.3
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1)
. There exists a deterministic
\bar{s}:\R^{d}\to\R
such that almost surely, as
n\to\infty
,
\overline{s}_{n}
converges weakly-* to
\bar{s}
.

In the remainder of this section we shift our attention from the patterns appearing in sandpiles on infinite graphs to the statistical properties
of sandpiles on finite graphs.

6.2.
Sandpile Markov chain and toppling invariants

In order to exposit the cases of both the full lattice and the percolation cluster, take
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1]
.
We consider the discrete time sandpile Markov chain on
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})
and restrict to the event that
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})\subset\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap Q_{n}
and
\partial Q_{n}\cap\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})\neq\emptyset
.
Start with a fully saturated sandpile,
s_{0}\equiv(\deg-1)
on
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})
. Each discrete time step,
s_{t}\to s_{t+1}
is as follows:

(1)

Pick a site uniformly at random in
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})
and add a chip.

(2)

Stabilize with dissipating boundary conditions; that is, each time there is a site with at least
\deg_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
chips on it, the site topples,
losing
\deg_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}
chips and giving one chip to each of its neighbors. If the site is on the boundary,
i.e.
, has a neighbor not in
Q_{n}
, chips are lost across that edge and thus eventually this process stops.

The stationary distribution of this Markov chain is uniform over a subset of sandpiles called
recurrent sandpiles
.
Uniform recurrent sandpiles are of interest in the physics literature
[
DRSV95
]

and thus it is important to determine how many steps are needed to run the above Markov chain before the resulting sandpile is close to uniform recurrent.

Techniques from the theory of random walks on finite groups were used in
[
JLP19
]
to
study this problem and it is precisely through this theory that functions with integer-valued Laplacian become relevant. We briefly recall
what was proved by Jerison, Levine, and Pike and refer the interested reader to
[
JLP19
]
for more details.

The set of of recurrent sandpiles has the structure of an Abelian group where the operation is pointwise addition followed by stabilization and the sandpile Markov chain is a random walk on that group. Its eigenvectors are given by a dual group which can be expressed as the additive group of functions

\xi:\overline{\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})}\to\R/\Z
such that
\xi(\partial\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n}))\equiv 0
and
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}\xi\in\Z
.

Functions in the dual group represent quantities which are invariant under toppling and were first considered in
[
DRSV95
]
.
Specifically, if
\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u\in\Z
,
s_{0}
is the initial (possibly unstable) sandpile
and
s_{\infty}
is the final, stable sandpile, then an integration by parts shows that

(6.2)

\sum s_{0}u=\sum s_{\infty}u\mod\Z.

Along with the application to the Markov chain we discuss in the next section,
toppling invariants
were also used in
[
HJL19
, Theorem 1]
to demonstrate a certain non-universality
of random sandpiles on
\Z^{2}
. We expect Theorem
1.3
to also be useful in this regard.

6.3.
Slow mixing

The mixing time of the sandpile Markov chain is controlled by the frequencies with eigenvalues

(6.3)

\lambda_{\xi}=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})|}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})}e^{2\pi i\xi(x)}

which are close to one. This leads us to
multiplicative harmonic
functions, functions
h:\overline{\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})}\to\C
such that
h(\partial\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n}))\equiv 1
and

h(v)^{\deg(v)}=\prod_{u\sim v}h(u),\quad\forall v\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})

and, we see that, by definition,

(6.4)

\mbox{$\Delta_{\mathscr{C}_{\infty}}u\in\Z$ $\iff$ $e^{2\pi iu}$ is multiplicative harmonic}.

By associating a frequency with a multiplicative harmonic function as in (
6.4
),
each eigenvalue may be indexed by a multiplicative harmonic function
h
,

\lambda_{h}=\frac{1}{|\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})|}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})}h(x).

By discrete Fourier analysis, (see,
e.g.
,
[
JLP19
, Lemma 2.9]
), after
t
discrete time steps the
L^{2}
distances from the uniform recurrent distribution
U
are

(6.5)

\|P_{t}-U\|^{2}_{2}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{1\}}|\lambda_{h}|^{2t}

where
\mathcal{H}
is the set of multiplicative harmonic functions on
\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})
,
P^{t}
is the transition matrix for
t
steps of the chain, and for two measures
\mu
and
\nu
on the sandpile group,
\mathcal{G}
,

\|\mu-\nu\|_{2}:=\left(|\mathcal{G}|\sum_{g\in\mathcal{G}}|\mu(g)-\nu(g)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Mixing times of sandpiles on general graphs were first studied rigorously in
[
JLP19
]

and a cut-off theorem was proved for sandpiles on the two-dimensional torus in
[
HJL19
]

which was later extended to general periodic tiling graphs in
[
HS21a
,
HS21b
]
.
In particular, it was shown, via an analysis of multiplicative harmonic functions on growing subsets of
\Z^{d}
that the sandpile Markov chain we consider, when
\mathfrak{p}=1
, has a mixing time of order
n^{d}\log n

[
HS21a
,
HS21b
]
. The extra log factor
leads to ‘slow mixing’.

The sandpile Markov chain on the percolation cluster,
i.e.
, for
\mathfrak{p}\in(\mathfrak{p}_{c}(d),1)
also mixes slowly, but for different reasons which stem from the following.

Proposition 6.4
.

If
u:\Z^{d}\to\R
has an integer-valued Laplacian and is compactly supported, then
u
is integer-valued.

Proof.

We give the proof in two dimensions.
The idea is to induct along diamonds of decreasing size, arguing that at each step, the boundary of the diamond must consist entirely of points where
u
is integer-valued.
Specifically, let
D_{1}
be the smallest diamond containing the support of
u
,
i.e.
, some translation of

D(k):=\{x\in\Z^{2}:|x|\leq k\}.

and let
D_{1}\supset D_{2}\supset\cdots
be a sequence of diamonds each a translation of
D(k-1),D(k-2),\ldots,\{0\}

which exhaust the support of
u
.

We claim, by induction on
m\geq 1
that the function
u
on the outer boundary of each
D_{m}
is integer-valued

(6.6)

u(\partial D_{m})\in\Z.

As
u
is compactly supported, by definition of
D_{1}
,
u(\partial D_{1})=0
and hence the base case is satisfied.
Suppose now, by induction, that (
6.6
) holds for
m
and we seek to show it for
m+1
.

See Figure
23
. The site
a
, at the corner of
D_{m}
has three neighbors in
D_{m+1}^{c}
,
l,u,d
.
By evaluating the graph Laplacian at
a
, we see that

-4a+l+u+d+r\equiv 0\mod 1

and since
a,l,u,d
are integer-valued, we have

r\equiv 0\mod 1.

We now continue to the site
a^{\prime}
, we see that

-4a^{\prime}+d+r+r^{\prime}+d^{\prime}\equiv 0\mod 1

and since
a^{\prime},d^{\prime},d^{\prime},r
are integer-valued, we have

r^{\prime}\equiv 0\mod 1.

We may iterate to see that every site on the boundary of
D_{m+1}
is integer-valued.
∎

The previous proposition shows that on
\Z^{d}
, the only frequencies which can achieve a large non-trivial eigenvalue (
6.3
) are those corresponding to functions which are
not
compactly supported.
However, this fact is false on the percolation cluster and the Markov chain mixes slowly on
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
for
this reason. See Figure
24
.

Theorem 6.5
.

There exists positive constants
c(\mathfrak{p},d),C(\mathfrak{p},d)
such that the following holds almost surely.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, for all
n
sufficiently large,

(6.7)

\|P^{t}-U\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all $t\geq Cn^{d}\log n$}

and

(6.8)

\|P^{t}-U\|_{2}^{2}\geq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all $t\leq cn^{d}\log n$},

where
P^{t}
and
U
are as in (
6.5
).

Proof.

First recall that there exists a constant
\theta(\mathfrak{p})\in(0,1)
such that, almost surely,

(6.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})|}{n^{d}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{|\mathscr{C}_{\infty}\cap Q_{n}|}{n^{d}}=\theta(\mathfrak{p}).

By
[
JLP19
, Theorem 4.3]
, writing
m:=|\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})|
we have

\|P^{t}-U\|_{2}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all $t\geq(5/4)m\log\left(2(m-1)(1+1/\varepsilon)\right)$}

and thus the upper bound (
6.7
) follows from the previous two displays.

The lower bound follows by explicitly constructing eigenvectors which obstruct mixing. Specifically,
consider a square isomorphic to
[0,1]\times[0,1]\times\{0\}^{d-2}
with all edges removed
except those shown in Figure
24
. The multiplicative harmonic function,
h:\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})\to\R
, as illustrated in the right of Figure
24
, has eigenvalue

\frac{1}{m}\sum_{x\in\mathscr{C}_{*}(Q_{n})}h(x)=\frac{1}{m}\left(m-2\right)=(1-\frac{2}{m}).

This prescription of edges appears in the percolation cluster with positive probability
and thus, by the ergodic theorem, has positive density in
\mathscr{C}_{\infty}
. Consequently, the eigenvalue
(1-\frac{2}{m})
has multiplicity of order
n^{d}
. Therefore, by (
6.5
)

\|P_{t}-U\|^{2}_{2}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\{1\}}|\lambda_{h}|^{2t}\geq cm(1-\frac{2}{m})^{2t}

which implies, by (
6.9
), the desired lower bound (
6.8
) with a smaller choice of
c
.
∎

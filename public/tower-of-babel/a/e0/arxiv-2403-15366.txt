Abstract
In the turnstile streaming model, a dynamic vector 𝐱 = ( 𝐱 1 , … , 𝐱 n ) ∈ ℤ n \mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n} is updated by a stream of entry-wise increments/decrements. Let f : ℤ → ℝ + f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}_{+} be a symmetric function with f ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 f(0)=0 . The f f -moment of 𝐱 \mathbf{x} is defined to be f ⁡ ( 𝐱 ) := ∑ v ∈ [ n ] f ⁡ ( 𝐱 v ) . f(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{v\in[n]}f(\mathbf{x}_{v}). We revisit the problem of constructing a universal sketch that can estimate many different f f -moments. Previous constructions of universal sketches rely on the technique of sampling with respect to the L 0 L_{0} -mass (uniform samples) or L 2 L_{2} -mass ( L 2 L_{2} -heavy-hitters), whose universality comes from being able to evaluate the function f f over the samples. To get samples, hash collisions are deliberately detected and avoided (with high probability), e.g. singleton-detectors are used in L 0 L_{0} -sampling and the CountSketch is used in L 2 L_{2} -sampling. Such auxiliary data structures introduce significant overhead in space. Apart from this issue, sampling-based methods are shown to perform poorly for estimating certain “nearly periodic functions” where Ω ⁡ ( poly ⁡ ( n ) ) \Omega(\operatorname{poly}(n)) samples are needed. In this paper, we propose a new universal sketching scheme that is almost “dual” to the sampling-based methods. Instead of evaluating f f on samples, we decompose f f into a linear combination of homomorphisms f 1 , f 2 , … f_{1},f_{2},\ldots from ( ℤ , + ) (\mathbb{Z},+) to ( ℂ , × ) (\mathbb{C},\times) , where the f k f_{k} -moments can be estimated regardless of hash collisions— because each f k f_{k} is a homomorphism! Then we synthesize the estimates of the f k f_{k} -moments to obtain an estimate of the f f -moment. Universality now comes from the fact that we can weight the f k f_{k} -moments arbitrarily, where the correct weighting depends on the harmonic structure of the function f f . In contrast to the sampling-based methods, the new SymmetricPoissonTower sketch takes the harmonic approach. It embraces hash collisions instead of avoiding them, which saves multiple log ⁡ n \log n factors in space, e.g., when estimating all L p L_{p} -moments ( f ⁡ ( z ) = | z | p , p ∈ [ 0 , 2 ] f(z)=|z|^{p},p\in[0,2] ). For many nearly periodic functions, the SymmetricPoissonTower is exponentially more efficient than sampling-based methods. We conjecture that the SymmetricPoissonTower is the universal sketch that can estimate every tractable function f f .

2.3
Formal Statement of New Results

We now formally state the main theorem. We first define the function family

\displaystyle|\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma}-\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma;a,b}|

\displaystyle=\left|(\mathbb{E}(A-B-C))^{3}-(\mathbb{E}A)^{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3(\mathbb{E}|A|)^{2}(\mathbb{E}|B|+\mathbb{E}|C|)+3(\mathbb{E}|A|)(\mathbb{E}|B|+\mathbb{E}|C|)^{2}+(\mathbb{E}|B|+\mathbb{E}|C|)^{3}

According to
Lemma

8
, we know
\mathbb{E}|B|\leq e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
,
\mathbb{E}|C|\leq e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m}\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
, and
\mathbb{E}|A|\leq\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
. Since we assume
a\leq 0\leq b
, we have

\displaystyle\leq(e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}+e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m})\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m),

Now we bound the difference in the second moment.

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}|V_{\gamma}|^{2}-\mathbb{E}|V_{\gamma;a,b}|^{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\mathbb{E}|A-B-C|^{2}\right)^{3}-\left(\mathbb{E}|A|^{2}\right)^{3}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\mathbb{E}(|A|^{2}+|B|^{2}+|C|^{2}-A\overline{B}-A\overline{C}-B\overline{A}+B\overline{C}-C\overline{A}+C\overline{B}))^{3}-(\mathbb{E}|A|^{2}\right)^{3}\right|,

where the dominating term
(\mathbb{E}|A|^{2})^{3}
is canceled and we have

\displaystyle=O((\mathbb{E}|A|^{2})^{2}\cdot(\mathbb{E}|A||B|+\mathbb{E}|A||C|))

Similarly by
Lemma

8
, we know
\mathbb{E}|A|^{2}=\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
,
\mathbb{E}|A||B|=e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
, and
\mathbb{E}|A||C|=e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m}\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
. We thus have

\displaystyle\leq(e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}+e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m})\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m).

We have proved first and second moment have vanishing differences, which implies the difference in variance is also
(e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}+e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m})\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
.
∎

5
Analysis of Combined Estimators

Let
\{V_{\gamma;a,b}\}_{\gamma>0}
be the truncated harmonic estimators in
Theorem

3
. We just showed that the mean of
V_{\gamma;a,b}
can be made arbitrarily close to the mean of
V_{\gamma}
by picking small enough
a
and large enough
b
. Choosing
a=-\Omega(m\log(mn))
and
b=\Omega(m\log(mn))
, the difference
|\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma;a,b}-\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma}|
becomes
O(1/\operatorname{poly}(mn))
. For a function
f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
, to estimate the
f
-moment one may try to combine the individual estimates as discussed in
Section

2.2
. However, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{\gamma;a,b}\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma}+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(mn)))\,\nu(d\gamma)

(
Theorem

3
)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}((1+O(m^{-1}))f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(mn)))\,\nu(d\gamma)

(
Theorem

2
)

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))f(\mathbf{x})+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(mn))\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\nu(d\gamma),

where in general
\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\nu(d\gamma)
can be
infinite
. For example, to estimate the
L_{1}
-moment, we have
f(x)=|x|=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\frac{2}{\pi\gamma^{2}}\,d\gamma
. For this function
f
, we have
\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\pi\gamma^{2}}\,d\gamma=\infty
. Recall that for
\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
to be well-defined, one only needs
\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(\gamma^{2},1)\,\nu(d\gamma)
to be finite. The reason that the attempt above fails is that, when
\gamma
is near 0, the density
\nu(d\gamma)
is unbounded and any non-zero additive bias is too large for the combined estimator. We thus need to split
f
into two parts
f_{<\zeta}(x)=\int_{0}^{\zeta}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
and
f_{\geq\zeta}(x)=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
for estimation, where
\zeta
is to be determined later.

Before we proceed, we first define a quantity that measures the “size” of the function
f
.

Definition 5

(function constant
C_{f}
)
.

For any
f=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
where
\nu
is a positive measure, define
C_{f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(\gamma^{2},1)\,\nu(d\gamma)
. Note that for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
f(x)
is finite if and only if
C_{f}<\infty
.

5.1
Estimation of the
f_{<\zeta}
-Moment

We first show that, when
\zeta
is small enough, the
f_{<\zeta}
-moment can be approximated by the
L_{2}
-moment
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}
.

Lemma 9
.

Recall that
f_{<\zeta}(x)=\int_{0}^{\zeta}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
. The
f_{<\zeta}
-moment of
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be bounded as follows.

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\zeta^{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}}{12}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)\leq f_{<\zeta}(\mathbf{x})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma).

Proof.

We first note the following approximation of
(1-\cos(x))
. For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

x^{2}/2-x^{4}/24\leq 1-\cos(x)\leq x^{2}/2.

On the one hand, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in[n]}\int_{0}^{\zeta}(1-\cos(\gamma\mathbf{x}_{v}))\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\in[n]}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\mathbf{x}_{v}^{2}/2\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in[n]}\int_{0}^{\zeta}(1-\cos(\gamma\mathbf{x}_{v}))\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{v\in[n]}\int_{0}^{\zeta}(\gamma^{2}\mathbf{x}_{v}^{2}/2-\gamma^{4}\mathbf{x}_{v}^{4}/24)\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)-\frac{1}{24}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{4}^{4}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{4}\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)-\frac{1}{24}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{4}^{4}\zeta^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma).

Finally we note that
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{4}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
.
∎

Thus by choosing
\zeta=O(1/\operatorname{poly}(m\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}))
,
\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)
will be a
(1\pm O(m^{-1}))
-approximation of the
f_{<\zeta}
-moment. It suffices now to approximate the
L_{2}
-moment
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}
. The classic
L_{2}
-moment can be estimated with a separate AMS sketch
[
AMS99
]
. Nevertheless, to demonstrate the universality of the
SymmetricPoissonTower

sketch, we will use its cells
X_{0},\ldots,X_{m-1}
to estimate
L_{2}
, which are
not
independent from the harmonic estimators
\{V_{\gamma;a,b}\}
.

Lemma 10

(
L_{2}
-moment estimator)
.

Let
(X_{k})_{a\leq k<b}
be a truncated symmetric Poisson tower with parameter
m
where
a\leq 0
and
b\geq m
. Then we define the
L_{2}
-estimator,

\displaystyle\Psi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}X_{k}^{2}e^{k/m}.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Psi

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\Psi

\displaystyle\leq(3+e)m^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}.

Proof.

Recall that
X_{k}=\sum_{v\in[n]}Z_{v,k}\mathbf{x}_{v}
where
Z_{v,k}\sim\operatorname{SymmetricPoisson}(e^{-k/m})
. By
Lemma

4
, we know
\mathbb{E}Z_{v,k}=0
,
\mathbb{E}Z_{v,k}^{2}=e^{-k/m}
, and
\mathbb{E}Z_{v,k}^{4}=e^{-k/m}+3e^{-2k/m}
. Thus similar to the AMS sketch,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}=\sum_{v\in[n]}\mathbf{x}_{v}^{2}\mathbb{E}Z_{v,k}^{2}=e^{-k/m}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus
\mathbb{E}\Psi=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in[n]}\mathbf{x}_{v}^{4}\mathbb{E}Z_{v,k}^{4}+6\sum_{s<t}\mathbf{x}_{s}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{t}^{2}\mathbb{E}Z_{v,s}^{2}\mathbb{E}Z_{v,t}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(e^{-k/m}+3e^{-2k/m})\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}

Now

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Psi^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{4}e^{2k/m}+(\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{2}e^{k/m})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}}{m^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(e^{k/m}+3)+\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}}{m}(3+e)+\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}.

Thus
\mathbb{V}\Psi\leq\mathbb{E}\Psi^{2}-(\mathbb{E}\Psi)^{2}\leq(3+e)m^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}
.
∎

5.2
Estimation of the
f_{\geq\zeta}
-Moment

We proceed as in
Section

2.2
. Let
\{V_{\gamma;a,b}\}_{\gamma\geq\zeta}
be the set of harmonic estimators generated from the truncated triple
SymmetricPoissonTower
in
Theorem

3
. For
f_{\geq\zeta}(x)=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
, define

\displaystyle\phi_{f_{\geq\zeta};a,b}

\displaystyle=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}V_{\gamma;a,b}\,\nu(d\gamma).

By
Theorem

3
, we know
\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma;a,b}=\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma}+O(\delta)
where
\delta=(e^{\frac{1}{3}a/m}+e^{-\frac{2}{3}b/m})\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}m)
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\phi_{f_{\geq\zeta};a,b}

\displaystyle=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}V_{\gamma;a,b}\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}((1+O(m^{-1}))f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})+O(\delta))\,\nu(d\gamma)

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})\,\nu(d\gamma)+O(\delta)\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\,\nu(d\gamma)

note that since
\zeta<1
, we have
\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\,\nu(d\gamma)\leq\zeta^{-2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(\gamma^{2},1)\,\nu(d\gamma)=\zeta^{-2}C_{f}

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})\,\nu(d\gamma)+O(\delta)\zeta^{-2}C_{f}.

The variance can be bounded similarly and we will leave it in the proof of
Theorem

4
.

5.3
Combination

We now prove the main technical theorem.

Theorem 4

(“frequency-domain” estimator)
.

Let
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be the input frequency vector and
(X_{k}^{(j)})_{a\leq k<b,j=1,2,3}
be a
[a,b)
-truncated triple
SymmetricPoissonTower
with parameter
m
. For any function
f(x)=\sigma^{2}x^{2}/2+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu(d\gamma)
where
\nu
is a positive measure with
C_{f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\min(\gamma^{2},1)\,\nu(d\gamma)
. Denote the estimator for
f
as

\displaystyle\phi_{f;a,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma^{2}+\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)\right)\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(X_{k}^{(j)})^{2}e^{k/m}}{3m}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}(1-e^{i\gamma X_{k}^{(j)}})e^{\frac{1}{3}k/m}\right)}{m^{3}(-\Gamma(-1/3))^{3}}\,\nu(d\gamma).

Set
\zeta=O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
. If
a=-\Omega(m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
and
b=\Omega(m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
, then
\phi_{f;a,b}
is a
(1\pm O(m^{-1}))
-approximate of the
f
-moment with negligible bias.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\phi_{f;a,b}

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))\sum_{v\in[n]}f(\mathbf{x}_{v})+O(\frac{C_{f}}{\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)})

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}\phi_{f;a,b}

\displaystyle\leq O(m^{-1})\cdot\left(\sum_{v\in[n]}f(\mathbf{x}_{v})\right)^{2}+O(\frac{C_{f}^{2}}{\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)}).

Proof.

For simplicity, we write
\phi_{f;a,b}
as
\alpha A+B
where
\alpha=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma^{2}+\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma))
,
A=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(X_{k}^{(j)})^{2}e^{k/m}}{3m}
, and
B=\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{3}(\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}(1-e^{i\gamma X_{k}^{(j)}})e^{\frac{1}{3}k/m})}{m^{3}(-\Gamma(-1/3))^{3}}\,\nu(d\gamma)
. By
Lemma

10
, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}A

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}A

\displaystyle\leq(1+e/3)m^{-1}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{4}.

With
Lemma

9
, we know

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\alpha A

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))f_{<\zeta}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\alpha A)

\displaystyle\leq(1+e/3)m^{-1}\left(f_{<\zeta}(\mathbf{x})\right)^{2}(1+O(m^{-1}))

Since
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we always have
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}\leq\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}
. By the calculation in
Section

5.2
and the assumptions on
\zeta,a,b
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}B

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))\sum_{v\in[n]}f_{\geq\zeta}(\mathbf{x}_{v})+C_{f}\cdot O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))

Similarly, for the variance we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}B

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}V_{\gamma;a,b}}\,\nu(\gamma)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\sqrt{\mathbb{V}V_{\gamma}+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))}\,\nu(\gamma)\right)^{2}

(
Theorem

3
)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\sqrt{(m^{-1}+O(m^{-2}))\cdot C\cdot f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})^{2}+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))}\,\nu(\gamma)\right)^{2}

(
Theorem

2
)

note that
f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})\leq 2\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0}\leq 2\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}
and
\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\,\nu(d\gamma)\leq\zeta^{-2}\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\min(\gamma^{2},1)\,\nu(d\gamma)=\zeta^{-2}C_{f}
.

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}\sqrt{(m^{-1}+O(m^{-2}))\cdot C\cdot f_{\gamma}(\mathbf{x})^{2}}+\sqrt{O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))}\,\nu(\gamma)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{(m^{-1}+O(m^{-2}))\cdot C}f_{\geq\zeta}(\mathbf{x})+\sqrt{O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))}\zeta^{-2}C_{f}\right)^{2}

Note that we have
1+e/3<2
and
C=\frac{3\cdot 2^{2/3}(-\Gamma(-2/3))}{(-\Gamma(-1/3))^{2}}<2
.
We conclude that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\alpha A+B)

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))f(\mathbf{x})+C_{f}\cdot O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\alpha A+B)

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{\mathbb{V}(\alpha A)}+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}B})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sqrt{2(m+O(m^{-2}))}f(\mathbf{x})+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))\cdot C_{f}\right)^{2}

We know
f_{\geq\zeta}(\mathbf{x})\leq\sum_{v\in[n]}\int_{\zeta}^{\infty}2\,\nu(d\gamma)=O(\zeta^{-2}C_{f}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0})
and
f_{<\zeta}(\mathbf{x})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}^{2}C_{f}
(
Lemma

9
). Therefore
f(\mathbf{x})=O(\zeta^{-2}C_{f}\|\mathbf{x}\|_{0})
. We have assumed
\zeta=O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
and thus

\displaystyle\leq O(m^{-1})\left(f(\mathbf{x})\right)^{2}+O(1/\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))\cdot C_{f}^{2}.

∎

The “frequency domain” estimator above might look daunting but in practice
f
can usually be approximated with a few main harmonic moments. In the case where
f
cannot be approximated by sparse harmonics, we provide the following alternative estimator, which can be computed with at most
O(m^{3}\log^{3}(nMm))
evaluations of the function
f_{\geq\zeta}
.

Corollary 1

(“time-domain” estimator)
.

With the setup in
Theorem

4
, define
\psi_{f;a,b}
as

\displaystyle\psi_{f;a,b}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma^{2}+\int_{0}^{\zeta}\gamma^{2}\,\nu(d\gamma)\right)\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{3}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(X_{k}^{(j)})^{2}e^{k/m}}{3m}

\displaystyle\quad+{m^{-3}(-\Gamma(-1/3))^{-3}}\sum_{j=a}^{b-1}\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}\sum_{l=a}^{b-1}e^{\frac{k+j+l}{3m}}\left(f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{j}^{(1)})+f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{k}^{(2)})+f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{l}^{(3)})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.-f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{j}^{(1)}+X_{k}^{(2)})-f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{k}^{(2)}+X_{l}^{(3)})-f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{l}^{(3)}+X_{j}^{(1)})+f_{\geq\zeta}(X_{j}^{(1)}+X_{k}^{(2)}+X_{l}^{(3)})\right).

Then
\psi_{f;a,b}
is a
(1\pm O(m^{-1}))
-approximate of the
f
-moment with negligible bias. In particular,

\displaystyle\psi_{f;a,b}=\mathrm{Re}(\phi_{f;a,b}).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}(1-e^{i\gamma X_{k}^{(j)}})e^{\frac{1}{3}k/m}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=a}^{b-1}\sum_{k=a}^{b-1}\sum_{l=a}^{b-1}(1-e^{i\gamma X_{j}^{(1)}})(1-e^{i\gamma X_{k}^{(2)}})(1-e^{i\gamma X_{l}^{(3)}})e^{\frac{1}{3}(k+j+l)/m}

where for any integers
A_{1},A_{2},A_{3}

\displaystyle(1-e^{i\gamma A_{1}})(1-e^{i\gamma A_{2}})(1-e^{i\gamma A_{3}})

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{j}e^{i\gamma A_{j}}+\sum_{j<k}e^{i\gamma(A_{1}+A_{2})}-e^{i\gamma(A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3})}

\displaystyle=(1-e^{i\gamma(A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3})})+\sum(1-e^{i\gamma A_{j}})-\sum_{j<k}(1-e^{i\gamma(A_{j}+A_{k})}).

Note that
\mathrm{Re}(1-e^{i\gamma k})=1-\cos(\gamma k)
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus by change the order of the triple-summation and integration we have what we want. Finally, note that for any complex random variable
W
,
\mathbb{V}W=\mathbb{V}\mathrm{Re}W+\mathbb{V}\mathrm{Im}W
and thus
\mathbb{V}\psi_{f;a,b}\leq\mathbb{V}\phi_{f;a,b}
.
∎

5.4
Signed Combination

As in
[
Coh17
,
Coh18
]
, the
transportation of the mean and the relative variance of
\{V_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma>0}
in the combined estimator
\int_{0}^{\infty}V_{\gamma}\,\nu(d\gamma)
relies crucially on the fact that
\nu
is a positive measure. In general, when
\nu
is a signed measure,
the combined mean
Eq.

4
still holds but the combined variance
Eq.

5
now depend on
|\nu|
.

Corollary 2

(signed combination)
.

Let
f(x)=\sigma_{1}^{2}x^{2}/2+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu_{1}(d\gamma)
and
g(x)=\sigma_{2}^{2}x^{2}/2+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\cos(\gamma x))\,\nu_{2}(d\gamma)
where
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
are positive measures. Let
\phi_{f;a,b}
and
\phi_{g;a,b}
be generated from a same truncated triple
SymmetricPoissonTower
sketch in
Theorem

4
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\phi_{f;a,b}-\phi_{g;a,b})

\displaystyle=(1+O(m^{-1}))(f(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x}))+O(\frac{C_{f}+C_{g}}{\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)})

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\phi_{f;a,b}-\phi_{g;a,b})

\displaystyle\leq O(m^{-1})\cdot\left(f(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{x})\right)^{2}+O(\frac{C_{f}^{2}+C_{g}^{2}}{\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)}).

Proof.

Both follow from
Theorem

4
.
The mean results from the linearity of expectation. For the variance, note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}(\phi_{f;a,b}-\phi_{g;a,b})\leq(\sqrt{\mathbb{V}\phi_{f;a,b}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{V}\phi_{g;a,b}})^{2}.

∎

Thus for
(\phi_{f;a,b}-\phi_{g;a,b})
to be a multiplicative approximation of the
(f-g)
-moment, one needs
f(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{x})=\Omega(f(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x}))
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

6
Proof of the Main Theorem

The main theorem (
Theorem

1
) almost directly follows from
Theorem

4
and
Corollary

2
. We only need to analyze the space and translate the variance guarantee to
(1\pm\epsilon)
-approximation with Chebyshev’s inequality.

Lemma 11
.

With probability
5/6
, all the cells in the triple
SymmetricPoissonTower
have magnitude at most
O(\operatorname{poly}(Mmn))
.

Proof.

The truncated tower stores
X_{j}
s from
a=-\Omega(m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
to
b=\Omega(m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m))
. Suppose
b-a=C_{1}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)
for some
C_{1}>0
. Each cell
j
stores an integer. Specifically
X_{j}\sim\sum_{v\in[n]}\mathbf{x}_{v}Y_{v}
where
Y_{v}\sim\operatorname{SymmetricPoisson}(e^{-k/m})
. By a simple coupling argument, we have
|X_{j}|\leq MY
where
M
is the frequency upper bound and
Y\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(ne^{-k/m})
. Therefore by Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(|X_{j}|\geq C_{2}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)Mne^{-k/m})\leq\mathbb{P}(Y\geq C_{2}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)ne^{-k/m})\leq\frac{1}{C_{2}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)}.

Choosing
C_{2}=18C_{1}
, by the union bound, with probability
1-\frac{C_{1}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)}{C_{2}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)}=17/18
, that
|X_{j}|<C_{2}m\log(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)Mne^{-k/m}=O(\operatorname{poly}(Mmn))
for all
j\in[a,b)
, where we bound
\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}
by
nM^{2}
. With probability at least 5/6 that this event occurs for all three truncated towers.
∎

Lemma 12
.

With probability 5/6, the triple
SymmetricPoissonTower
with
m=\Omega(\epsilon^{-2})
outputs a correct
(1\pm\epsilon)
-approximation of the
f
-moment.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4
, an estimate of the
f
-moment can be returned with
O(m^{-1})
relative variance and an
O(C_{f}/O(\operatorname{poly}(\|\mathbf{x}\|_{2}m)))
additive bias. Note that for any non-empty stream, the
f
-moment
f(\mathbf{x})
is at least
\xi(f,M)
. Thus the additive bias can be absorbed in the multiplicative term since we have
\xi(f,M)=\Omega(C_{f}/\operatorname{poly}(nMm))
by assumption in
Theorem

1
. Therefore, the estimator is a
(1\pm O(m^{-1/2}))
-approximation with probability
5/6
by Chebyshev’s inequality with a suitable constant in
O(m^{-1/2})
. It suffices to set
m=\Omega(\epsilon^{-2})
for a
(1\pm\epsilon)
-approximation.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

.

By a union bound of the events in

and

, with probability 2/3 the estimate is a correct
(1\pm\epsilon)
-approximation and at the same time

7
Conclusion

The set of tractable
f
-moments are those that can be approximated in polylog space. It is a major open question to fully characterize this class. To date, Braverman, Chestnut, Woodruff, and Yang
[
BCWY16
]
have
a nearly-complete characterization of this class,
but it cannot handle the class of tractable nearly
periodic functions.

In this work, we proposed a new method for estimating
f
-moments based on a harmonic decomposition of
f
. The
SymmetricPoissonTower
is a new

O(\epsilon^{-2}\log^{2}n)
-space data structure that
can estimate all the important functions in Braverman et al.’s class
[
BCWY16
]
, as well as many nearly periodic functions. We conjecture that the
SymmetricPoissonTower
is a universal sketch for the class of tractable functions.

Conjecture 1
.

The
SymmetricPoissonTower
is universal for the class of tractable functions.

There is some subtlety in this conjecture, as the tractable class probably does not have a sharp boundary.
For fixed
f,g\in\mathcal{F}_{*}
(
Section

2.3
), define

\tilde{h}(M)=(\max_{x\in[M]}\frac{f(x)+g(x)}{f(x)-g(x)})^{2}
.
As
\tilde{h}(M)
becomes large, more and more functions that can be written as
f-g
can be included in the range with the cost of having an increasingly large
\tilde{h}(M)
factor in space.
This is analogous to the role that
h(M)
plays in Braverman et al.
[
BCWY16
]
.

Appendix A
Example Functions

A.1
Nearly Periodic
g_{np}

The function
g_{np}
is constructed in
[
BCWY16
]
for the purpose of showing there are tractable functions outside the reach of
L_{2}
-heavy hitters framework. We decompose
g_{np}
into harmonic moments and compute its function constant
C_{g_{np}}
in this section. Recall the following definitions.

•

\mathbb{B}=\{\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}2^{-j}\mid\forall j,a_{j}\in\{0,1\},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is the set of finite-precision binary numbers in
[0,1)
;

•

\tau:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\tau(x)=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N}\mid 2^{j}|x\}
which returns the position of the least significant bit of
x
;

•

\tau_{*}:\mathbb{B}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\tau_{*}(r)=\min\{j\in\mathbb{N}\mid 2^{-j}|r\}
which returns the length of the binary representation of
r
.

Lemma 13
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle g_{np}(x)

\displaystyle=2^{-\tau(x)}=\frac{4}{3}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{B}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))2^{-2\tau_{*}(\gamma)}

Proof.

Partition
\mathbb{B}
as
B_{0},B_{1},B_{2},\ldots
where
B_{k}=\{r\in\mathbb{B}\mid\tau_{*}(r)=k\}
. We have
B_{0}=\{0\}
,
B_{1}=\{1/2\}
,
B_{2}=\{1/4,3/4\}
,
B_{3}=\{1/8,3/8,5/8,7/8\}
, and so on.
By symmetry, for
k\geq 2

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in B_{k}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2^{k-1},&\tau(x)\leq k-2\\
2^{k},&\tau(x)=k-1\\
0,&\tau(x)\geq k\end{cases}

where in the first case
\sum_{\gamma\in B_{k}}\cos(2\pi\gamma x)=0
, in the second case
\cos(2\pi\gamma x)=-1
for any
\gamma\in B_{k}
, and in the last case,
\cos(2\pi\gamma x)=1
for any
\gamma\in B_{k}
.
Thus if
\tau(x)\geq 1
, i.e.,
x
is even, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{B}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))2^{-2\tau_{*}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\gamma\in B_{k}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))2^{-2k}

\displaystyle=2^{\tau(x)+1}2^{-2(\tau(x)+1)}+\sum_{k=\tau(x)+2}^{\infty}2^{k-1}\cdot 2^{-2k}

\displaystyle=2^{-(\tau(x)+1)}+2^{-(\tau(x)+2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}2^{-\tau(x)}.

When
x
is odd, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{B}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))2^{-2\tau_{*}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{\gamma\in B_{k}}(1-\cos(2\pi\gamma x))2^{-2k}

\displaystyle=2\cdot 2^{-2}+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}2^{k-1}\cdot 2^{-2k}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}.

∎

Lemma 14
.

C_{g_{np}}\leq\frac{2}{3}
.

Proof.

\displaystyle C_{g_{np}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{B}}\min(4\pi^{2}\gamma^{2},1)2^{-2\tau_{*}(\gamma)}\leq\frac{4}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in B_{k}}2^{-2k}=\frac{4}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}2^{k-1}2^{-2k}=\frac{2}{3}.

∎

A.2
The Golden Harmonic Moment
g_{gold}

The golden harmonic moment
g_{gold}
is constructed for additional examples that are tractable in the harmonic sketching framework but out of the reach of prior techniques.

With the pigeonhole argument in
Lemma

2
, it is clear that
\xi(g_{gold},M)=O(1/M^{2})
. We now bound
\xi(g_{gold},M)
from below, which is based on the theory of continued fractions.

Lemma 15
.

Let
g_{gold}(x)=1-\cos(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\cdot 2\pi x)
for
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then
\xi(g_{gold},M)=\Omega(1/M^{2})
.

Proof.

The convergents of
\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
are
(F_{n+1}/F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
where
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the Fibonacci sequence. It is known that for any
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
q\leq F_{n}
,
|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-p/q|\geq|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-F_{n+1}/F_{n}|
. Thus for any
x\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}x=w+r
where
w\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in(-1/2,1/2)
. Let
F_{n}\geq x
. Then

\displaystyle|r/x|=|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-\frac{w}{x}|\geq|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-F_{n+1}/F_{n}|\geq|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-F_{n+2}/F_{n+1}|

Thus

\displaystyle 2|r/x|

\displaystyle\geq|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-F_{n+1}/F_{n}|+|\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}-F_{n+2}/F_{n+1}|

\displaystyle\geq|F_{n+1}/F_{n}-F_{n+2}/F_{n+1}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{F_{n}F_{n+1}}

Note that
F_{n}=\Theta((\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2})^{n})
and thus we may choose
F_{n},F_{n+1}
that are
O(x)
. Therefore
|r|=\Omega(1/x)
, which implies
1-\cos(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\cdot 2\pi x)=1-\cos(w2\pi+2\pi r)=1-\cos(2\pi r)\leq 2\pi^{2}r^{2}=\Omega(1/x^{2})
.
∎

Appendix B
Mathematical Lemmas

In this section we estimate the rate that sums like
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}(1-e^{-e^{-k/m}})e^{\frac{1}{3}k/m}
converge to its limit
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1-e^{-e^{-r}})e^{\frac{1}{3}r}\,dr
as
m
increases, supporting our analysis of the smoothed (-1/3)-aggregation.

Lemma 16
.

Let
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a differentiable function. If both
h
and
h^{\prime}
are (Lebesgue) integrable, then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h(k/m)\right|\leq m^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|h^{\prime}(s)|\,ds.

Proof.

We bound the difference

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}h(k/m)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1/m}h(k/m+s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}h(k/m)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\int_{0}^{1/m}h(k/m+s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}h(k/m)\right|.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1/m}h(k/m+s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}h(k/m)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1/m}\int_{0}^{s}h^{\prime}(k/m+t)\,dtds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/m}\int_{0}^{s}|h^{\prime}(k/m+t)|\,dtds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/m}\int_{0}^{1/m}|h^{\prime}(k/m+t)|\,dtds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{1/m}|h^{\prime}(k/m+t)|\,dt.

Thus

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}h(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}h(j/m)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{1/m}|h^{\prime}(k/m+t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|h^{\prime}(t)|\,dt.

∎

Lemma 17
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

\displaystyle\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx},

\displaystyle\eta_{2}(x;a,c)

\displaystyle=ae^{-x}e^{-ae^{-x}}e^{cx}.

If
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1)
, then

1.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)\,dx=(a^{c}-b^{c})\Gamma(-c),
where
\Gamma(\cdot)
here denotes the Gamma function (
Fig.

4
);

2.

\eta_{1}^{\prime}(x;a,b,c)=c\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)+\eta_{2}(x;a,c)-\eta_{2}(x;b,c)
;

3.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)|\,dx\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1}{1-c}\right)|b-a|^{c}
.

4.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\eta_{2}(x;a,c)|\,dx\leq|a|^{c}\Gamma(1-c)
.

5.

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\eta_{1}^{\prime}(x;a,b,c)|\,dx\leq 2\left(1+\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|b-a|^{c}+(|a|^{c}+|b|^{c})\Gamma(1-c)
.

6.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)\,dx-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\eta_{1}(k/m;a,b,c)\right|

\displaystyle{\hskip 113.81102pt}\leq m^{-1}\left(2\left(1+\frac{c}{1-c}\right)|b-a|^{c}+(|a|^{c}+|b|^{c})\Gamma(1-c)\right).

Proof.

Part 1.

Integral by parts.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx}\,dx

\displaystyle=\left.(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})c^{-1}e^{cx}\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(ae^{-x}e^{-ae^{-x}}-be^{-x}e^{-be^{-x}})c^{-1}e^{cx}\,dx

Since
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0
and
c\in(0,1)
,
\left.(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})c^{-1}e^{cx}\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}=0
,

\displaystyle=-c^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ae^{-(1-c)x}e^{-ae^{-x}}\,dx+c^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}be^{-(1-c)x}e^{-be^{-x}}\,dx.

Set
z=ae^{-x}
and we have

\displaystyle c^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}ae^{-(1-c)x}e^{-ae^{-x}}\,dx

\displaystyle=c^{-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}a^{c}z^{-c}e^{-z}\,dz

\displaystyle=a^{c}\frac{\Gamma(1-c)}{c}=a^{c}(-\Gamma(-c)).

(6)

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx}\,dx

\displaystyle=-a^{c}(-\Gamma(-c))+b^{c}(-\Gamma(-c))=(a^{c}-b^{c})\Gamma(-c).

Part 2.

\displaystyle\eta_{1}^{\prime}(x;a,b,c)

\displaystyle=(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})ce^{cx}+(ae^{-x}e^{-ae^{-x}}-be^{-x}e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx}

\displaystyle=c\eta_{1}(x;a,b,c)+\eta_{2}(x;a,c)-\eta_{2}(x;b,c).

Part 3.

Define the path
\phi(t)=(1-t)a+tb
and
g_{x}(r)=e^{-re^{-x}}
. By the path integral,

\displaystyle\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}}\right|

\displaystyle=|g_{x}(a)-g_{x}(b)|=\left|\int_{0}^{1}g_{x}^{\prime}(\phi(t))\phi^{\prime}(t)\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\phi(t)e^{-x}}(-e^{-x})(b-a)\,dt\right|\leq|b-a|e^{-x}\int_{0}^{1}|e^{-\phi(t)e^{-x}}|\,dt

We assumed
\mathrm{Re}(a),\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0
and thus
\mathrm{Re}(\phi(t))\geq 0
for any
t\in[0,1]
, which implies
|e^{-\phi(t)e^{-x}}|<1
. We then have

\displaystyle\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|b-a|e^{-x}.

On the other hand, since
\mathrm{Re}(a),\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0
, we have
\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}\right|,\left|e^{-be^{-x}}\right|\leq 1
and thus

\displaystyle\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}\right|+\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}\right|\leq 2.

We conclude that for any

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{q}\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}}\right|e^{cx}\,dx+\int_{q}^{\infty}\left|e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}}\right|e^{cx}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{q}2e^{cx}\,dx+\int_{q}^{\infty}|b-a|e^{-x}e^{cx}\,dx

\displaystyle=2\frac{e^{cq}}{c}+|b-a|\frac{e^{(c-1)q}}{1-c},

since
c\in(0,1)
. Now set

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|(e^{-ae^{-x}}-e^{-be^{-x}})e^{cx}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{c}+\frac{1}{1-c}\right)|b-a|^{c}.

Part 4.

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|ae^{-x}e^{-ae^{-x}}e^{cx}\right|\,dx

\displaystyle=|a|\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-x}e^{-\mathrm{Re}({a})e^{-x}}e^{cx}\,dx

by

\mathrm{Re}({a})\geq 0

\displaystyle=|a|\frac{1}{\mathrm{Re}(a)}(\mathrm{Re}(a))^{c}\Gamma(1-c)

\displaystyle\leq|a|^{c}\Gamma(1-c).

Part 5.

Follows from 2, 3, and 4.

Poly-meromorphic Itô–Hermite functions associated with a singular potential vector on the punctured complex plane
Hajar Dkhissi, Allal Ghanmi

Abstract
. We provide a theoretical study of a new family of orthogonal functions on the punctured complex plane solving the eigenvalue problems for some magnetic Laplacian perturbed by a singular vector potential with zero magnetic field modeling the Aharonov–Bohm effect. The functions are defined by their β \beta -modified Rodrigues type formula and extend the poly-analytic Itô–Hermite polynomials to the poly-meromorphic setting. Mainly, we derive their different operational representations and give their explicit expressions in terms of special functions. Different generating functions and integral representations are obtained.

1.
Introduction

The real Hermite polynomials

(1.1)

\displaystyle H_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}{\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}}(e^{-x^{2}}).

are first considered by Laplace
[
20
]
and next studied in details by Tchebeychev
[
6
]
. They play a crucial role in the one dimensional quantum harmonic oscillator
[
9
,
21
]

and are used to provide analytic proofs for some combinatorial results
[
7
]
. Moreover, they have been shown to be useful in expanding the probability generating function of a generalized Poisson distribution
[
18
]
. A non-trivial two dimensional analog that is not the tensor product of the real Hermite polynomials is giving by the Itô–Hermite polynomials defined by the Rodrigues formula

(1.2)

\displaystyle H_{m,n}^{\alpha}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m+n}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{m+n}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}\partial z^{n}}\left(e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}\right),\,\,\alpha>0.

Here
{\partial}/{\partial z}
and
{\partial}/{\partial\overline{z}}
denote the Wirtinger derivatives with respect to the variables
z
and
\overline{z}
, respectively.
The polynomials in (
1.2
) have been introduced by Itô within the framework of multiple Wiener integrals
[
17
]
. Since then, they have been extensively studied, namely in connection with various branches of engineering sciences, mathematics and physics
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
16
,
14
,
13
,
17
,
23
,
24
]
.
They
form an orthogonal complete system in the Hilbert space
L^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{C})=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}dxdy)
(see
[
16
,
10
]
). Moreover, they provide a concrete
description of the spectral analysis of the Landau Hamiltonian
[
19
]

(1.3)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}=\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-2\alpha y\right)^{2}+\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+2\alpha x\right)^{2};\,\,z=x+iy.

Different generalized class of these polynomials are suggested by special magnetic Schrödinger operators. The one associated with constant magnetic field acting on mixed planar automorphic functions attached to a given equivariant pair
[
8
]
is given by

(1.4)

\mathfrak{G}^{\nu}_{m,n}(z,\overline{z}|\xi)=\frac{(-1)^{m+n}}{\nu^{m}}e^{\nu|z|^{2}+\frac{\xi}{2}z}\dfrac{\partial^{m+n}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}\partial z^{n}}\left(e^{-\nu|z|^{2}-\frac{\xi}{2}z}\right)

and has been studied in sufficient detail in
[
10
]
.

In the present paper, we identify a special class of orthogonal and poly-meromorphic functions generalized to previous one and associated with the second order differential operator

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}=\Delta_{\alpha}+S_{\alpha,\beta},

which is essentially the Laplacian
\Delta_{\alpha}
in (
1.3
) perturbed by the first order differential operator

S_{\alpha,\beta}
explicitly given by

(1.5)

\displaystyle S_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{z}}}\right)-\beta\left(2\alpha-\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}\right)

and is associated with the closed and singular potential vector

(1.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\theta}_{\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{i\beta}{|z|^{2}}\left(\overline{z}dz-zd\overline{z}\right),

modeling an Aharonov–Bohm effect.
Mainly, we aim to explore the role
played by the injection of this singular potential in generating non-trivial orthogonal eigenstates
within the factorization formalism.
In fact, we provide an accurate study of the special functions

(1.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}):=(-1)^{n}z^{-\beta}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}(z^{\beta+m}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}),

refereed to as poly-meromorphic (or
\beta
-modified complex) Itô-Hermite functions.
Here,
\alpha
and
\beta
are given fixed reals with
\alpha>0
, and
n
and
m
are varying integers such that
n=0,1,2,\cdots
and
m>-\beta-1
. This leads to a special generalization of the complex Itô-Hermite polynomials in (
1.2
). The polynomial case, shown in Section
4
to correspond to
m\geq n
and
\beta\geq 0
, coincides with the polynomials
Z^{\beta}_{m,n}
considered by Ismail and Zeng in
[
15
, Section 3]
.
More precisely, we are concerned with certain basic algebraic, analytic and spectral properties of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
defined by their Rodrigues formula (
1.7
).
In particular, the interrelation of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
with to spacial functions such as the Itô–Hermite polynomials and the confluent hypergeometric functions are considered in Section
2
.
Their spectral realization as eigenfunctions of the magnetic Laplacian
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
as well as their regularity and their exact bi-order as poly-meromorphic functions on the complex plane are also studied in Sections
3
and
4
, respectively.
Associated generating functions are obtained in Section
5
, and next employed to discuss some of their applications such as their integral representations (Section
6
) and spacial attached integral transforms (Section
7
).
The fractional side as well as the associated functional spaces of Segal–Bargmann type will be introduced and studied in details in forthcoming papers.

2.
Preliminary results

It is worth noticing that to avoid multiple-valuedness of the argument
\arg(z)
in
z^{\beta}=e^{\beta\log(z)}
involved in (
1.7
), we choose the principal branch for the logarithm.
Notice also that the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
satisfy
\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\overline{\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\overline{z},z)}
as well as the symmetry relation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\alpha^{m}z^{\beta}\psi_{n+\beta,m-\beta}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\alpha^{n+\beta}\overline{z}^{\beta}\overline{\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})}

valid for
\beta
being integer and for given non-negative integers
m
and
n
such that
n\geq max(0,-\beta)
.
We have in addition

\overline{z}^{\beta}\psi_{m,m}^{1,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z^{\beta}\psi_{m+\beta,m-\beta}^{1,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

and

\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z^{-[\beta]}\psi_{n,m+[\beta]}^{\alpha,\tilde{\beta}}(z,\overline{z}),

valid for any real
\beta
such that
\beta+m>-1
, where
[\beta]
denotes the integer part of
\beta
.
For the particular case of
\beta=1/2
we have

\sqrt{\alpha|z|}2^{2m+1}\psi_{m,m}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}}(z,\overline{z})=H_{2m+1}(\sqrt{\alpha|z|})=2(-4)^{m}m!(\alpha|z|)^{1/2}L_{m}^{\left({{1}/{2}}\right)}(\alpha|z|),

where
H_{k}(x)
are the Hermite polynomials in (
1.1
) and
L^{\alpha}_{k}
denotes the generalized Lageurre polynomials.
For arbitrary non-negative integer
\beta
, the functions
\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
are closely connected to the complex Itô-Hermite polynomials in (
1.2
) on the punctured complex plane
\mathbb{C}^{*}=\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle z^{\beta}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\psi_{n,m+\beta}^{\alpha,0}(z,\overline{z})=\alpha^{\frac{n-m-\beta}{2}}H_{m+\beta,n}(\sqrt{\alpha}z,\sqrt{\alpha}\overline{z}).

The interrelation with these polynomials for arbitrary
\beta
can be obtained by specifying
f
in the Burchnall’s operational formula
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]

\displaystyle(-1)^{n}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}\left(z^{m}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}f\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\alpha^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!(n-k)!}H_{m,n-k}^{\alpha}(z,\overline{z})\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial{z^{k}}}.

Thus, for
f(z)=z^{\beta}
we obtain

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\alpha^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}\Gamma(\beta+1)}{k!(n-k)!\Gamma(\beta-k+1)}z^{-k}H_{m,n-k}^{\alpha}(z,\overline{z}).

For the explicit expression of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
(with
m>-\beta-1
), we claim that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)}c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}z^{m-k}\overline{z}^{n-k},

where the starred minimum
m\wedge^{*}b
is defined by the classical minimum
m\wedge^{*}b=\min(m,b)
when
b
is an integer and by
m\wedge^{*}b=m
otherwise. The involved constants
c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}
stand for

c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}:=\frac{(-1)^{k}n!\Gamma(\beta+m+1)}{k!(n-k)!\Gamma(\beta+m-k+1)}\alpha^{n-k}.

The expression in (
2.3
) can be handled by applying the Leibniz formula to (
1.7
) keeping in mind the fact that

\displaystyle z^{-(\beta+m-k)}\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial{z^{k}}}(z^{\beta+m})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0\,,&\quad\beta=0,1,2,\cdots,k>\beta+m,\\
\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\beta+m+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+m-k+1)}&\quad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Notice also that the monomials
z^{q}\overline{z}^{p}
can be expressed in terms of the considered functions. In fact, by rewriting them as a derivation of the Gaussian function
e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha^{p}z^{q}\overline{z}^{p}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}z^{q}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{p}}{\partial{z^{p}}}(e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}z^{q}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{p}}{\partial{z^{p}}}(z^{-\beta-n+p-q}z^{\beta+n+q-p}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\binom{p}{n}(-1)^{p}\frac{\partial^{p-n}}{\partial{z^{p-n}}}\left(z^{-\beta-n+p-q}\right)z^{q}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}(z^{\beta+n+q-p}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}).

The last equality holds making use of the Leibniz formula. Now, by means of the Rodrigues formulas (
1.7
) with
q-p\geq 0
,
it follows

(2.4)

\displaystyle\alpha^{p}z^{q}\overline{z}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{p}\binom{p}{n}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+q)}{\Gamma(\beta+q+n-p)}\psi_{n,n+q-p}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

for every non-negative integers
p
and
q
with
p\leq q
.

The first few terms of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}

are given by

\psi_{0,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z^{m}
and
\psi_{1,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z^{m-1}\left(\alpha z\overline{z}-(\beta+m)\right)
when
n=0
and
n=1
respectively, while for
n=2
we have

\psi_{2,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z^{m-2}\left(\alpha^{2}z^{2}\overline{z}^{2}-2\alpha(\beta+m)z\overline{z}+(\beta+m)(\beta+m-1)\right).

This reveals in particular that the
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
are no longer polynomials unless
\beta
is a non-positive integer.
This becomes clear from their hypergeometric representation in terms of the hypergeometric functions defined by the series

{{}_{p}F_{q}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{p}\\
c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{q}\end{array}\bigg|x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(a_{1})_{n}(a_{2})_{n}\cdots(a_{p})_{n}}{(c_{1})_{n}(c_{2})_{n}\cdots(c_{q})_{n}}}{\frac{x^{n}}{n!}}

provided that
c_{\ell}\neq 0,-1,-2,\cdots
for
\ell=1,2,\cdots,q
.
Indeed, from (
2.3
) and making use of the classical identities on Gamma function and Pochammer symbol, we get

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}z^{m}\overline{z}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)}(-n)_{k}(-\beta-m)_{k}\frac{(-\alpha z\overline{z})^{-k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}z^{m}\overline{z}^{n}{{}_{2}F_{0}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-n,-\beta-m\\
-\end{array}\bigg|-\frac{1}{\alpha|z|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}\Gamma(\beta+m+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+m-n+1)}z^{m-n}{{}_{1}F_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-n\\
\beta+m-n+1\end{array}\bigg|\alpha|z|^{2}\right).

Subsequently, by means of (
2
) we obtain the expression of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
in terms of the generalized Laguerre polynomials
[
22
, p. 240]
,
to wit

(2.9)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}n!z^{m-n}L_{n}^{(\beta+m-n)}(\alpha|z|^{2}).

This can also be recovered starting from (
1.7
) using the derivation formula

[
22
, p.241]
.
Accordingly, with the use of (
2.9
), it is straightforward to prove
the orthogonality of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
in the Hilbert space

L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},d\mu_{\alpha,\beta})
of square integrable functions with respect to the measure
d\mu_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=|z|^{2\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}d\lambda(z)
,
where

d\lambda(z)=dxdy
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the complex plane with
z=x+iy
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. More explicitly, we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})\overline{\psi_{k,j}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})}|z|^{2\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}d\lambda(z)=\frac{\pi\alpha^{n}n!}{\alpha^{m+\beta+1}}\Gamma(\beta+m+1)\delta_{m,j}\delta_{n,k}.

We conclude these preliminaries by noticing that starting from (
1.7
), it is clear that

z\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta-1}(z,\overline{z}).

Also, by rewriting
\partial^{n+1}
as
\partial^{n}\partial
,
one obtains the
recurrence formula

(2.10)

\displaystyle\alpha\overline{z}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\psi_{n+1,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})+(\beta+m)\psi_{n,m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}).

Additional recurrence formulas can be derived from the different known ones for the generalized Laguerre polynomials. For example from those in
[
22
, p. 241]
one obtains

\displaystyle(1)\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta-1}(z,\overline{z})=\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})+n\psi_{n-1,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle(2)\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{n+1,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\left(\alpha|z|^{2}-[n+\beta+m+1]\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})-n\left(\beta+m\right)\psi_{n-1,m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle(3)\quad

\displaystyle\alpha\overline{z}\psi_{n,m+2}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\left(\alpha|z|^{2}-n\right)\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})-n\left(\beta+m+1\right)\psi_{n-1,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle(4)\quad

\displaystyle\psi_{n+1,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\left(\alpha|z|^{2}-n-1\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})-z\left(\beta+m\right)\psi_{n,m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle(5)\quad

\displaystyle\left(\beta+m-n+\alpha|z|^{2}\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=z\left(\beta+m\right)\psi_{n,m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})+\alpha\overline{z}\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}).

3.
Spectral realization

The result below shows that the functions
\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}
are
L^{2}
-eigenfunctions of the perturbed magnetic Lapalcian defined by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial{\bar{z}}}+\left(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}\right)\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{z}}}

and the second order differential operator

(3.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}:=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}+\alpha z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{\beta}{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}.

Theorem 3.1
.

The functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}

form an orthogonal system in
L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})
that solve the eigenvalue problems
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha n
and
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha m
.

Proof.

Notice first that the second order differential operator in (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}A=AA^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}-\alpha,

where
A={\partial}/{\partial{\overline{z}}}
and
A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}
are the first order differential operators given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}=-[\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)]^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial{z}}\left(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)f(z)\right).

Here
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z):=|z|^{2\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}
.
Thus, using the commutation rule

AA^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}-A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}A=\alpha Id
, one proceeds by induction to get the identity

A(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n+1}=(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n+1}A+\alpha(n+1)(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}
. Subsequently, we have

\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}((A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}(g))=\left(AA^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}-\alpha\right)((A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}(g))=\alpha n((A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}(g)),

for any
g\in\ker(A)
. Thus, by considering the case of the generic elements
g_{m}(z)=z^{m}
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
for
z
in the punctured complex plane, one deduces that the function

(3.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}):=(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}(g_{m})=(-1)^{n}[\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)]^{-1}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}\left(z^{m}\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)\right)

is a eigenfunction of
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}
with
n\alpha
as a corresponding eigenvalue.

Now, to prove that
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha m\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
, we make use of
the partial raising operations

(3.5)

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\alpha\overline{z}+\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\psi_{n+1,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}-\alpha z\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\psi_{n,m+1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}),

which are immediate by straightforward computation.
On the other hand, from (
2.10
) and (
3.5
) one has

(3.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=(\beta+m)\psi_{n,m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}).

Accordingly, by combining (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), it follows

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}-\alpha z\right)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=-\alpha(\beta+m+1)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}).

This completes the proof by observing that the operator
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}
in (
3.2
) can be factorized, up to an additive constant, as

\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\beta}{z}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}-\alpha z\right)-\alpha(\beta+1).

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let
E=z\partial/{\partial z}
be the Euler derivative operator and
\overline{E}=\overline{z}\partial/{\partial\overline{z}}
its complex conjugate. Then, the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
satisfy

(E-\overline{E})\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}=(m-n)\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta},

which readily follows since
\widetilde{\Delta}_{\alpha,\beta}-\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}=\alpha(E-\overline{E})
. This
is the analog for
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
at
arbitrary integer
m
such that
m>-\beta-1
of the one obtained in
[
15
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

The orthogonality of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
in
L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})
can be reproved by observing that the operator
A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}
is in fact
the adjoint of
A
when acting on a densely domain in
L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})
.
In fact, we have

\displaystyle{\left\langle{\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta},\psi_{n+p,q}^{\alpha,\beta}}\right\rangle}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{A(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}(g_{m}),(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n+p-1}(g_{q})}\right\rangle}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n}A(g_{m})+n\alpha(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n-1}(g_{m}),(A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}})^{n+p-1}(g_{q})}\right\rangle}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\alpha n{\left\langle{\psi_{n-1,m}^{\alpha,\beta},\psi_{n+p-1,q}^{\alpha,\beta}}\right\rangle}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{n}n!{\left\langle{\psi_{0,m}^{\alpha,\beta},\psi_{p,q}^{\alpha,\beta}}\right\rangle}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi\alpha^{n}n!}{\alpha^{m+\beta+1}}\Gamma(\beta+m+1)\delta_{0,p}\delta_{m,q}.

Below, we prove that the considered functions are closely connected to the spectral analysis of a specific Schrödinger operator
L=\nabla_{\theta}^{*}\nabla_{\theta}
associated with a specific singular vector potential
\theta
, where
\nabla_{\theta}=d+i\mathbb{ext}_{\theta}
is the co-derivation operator acting on
\Omega^{\infty}_{p,c}(\mathbb{C})
, the space of smooth differential
p
-forms with compact support, and
\mathbb{ext}_{\theta}(\omega:)=\theta\wedge\omega
denotes the exterior multiplication by
\theta
.
The operator
\nabla^{*}_{\theta}
denotes its formal adjoint with respect to the Hermitian scalar product

(3.9)

\displaystyle\Big(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\Big)_{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}\omega_{1}\wedge\star\omega_{2},

for
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in\Omega^{\infty}_{p,c}(\mathbb{C})
.
Here
\star
is the Hodge star operator on differential forms defined to satisfy
\star(f\omega)=\overline{f}(\star\omega)
for scalar functions
f
, and

\star(dz\wedge d\bar{z})=2i
.
This readily follows for the metric
ds^{2}
being conformal to the Euclidean metric
ds^{2}(z)=dz\otimes d\overline{z}
.
Now, by considering the strong extensions of the differential operators
d
,
\nabla
and
L
initially defined on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{C})=\Omega^{\infty}_{0,c}(\mathbb{C})
,
we can extend them to the whole
L^{2}
-Hilbert space as the closure of the
L^{2}
-norm with respect to
{\left\langle{\omega_{1},\omega_{2}}\right\rangle}_{0}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For given reals
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
\alpha>0
, we set
\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}=d+i\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}}
and we let
\theta_{\alpha,\beta}
be the real-valued differential
1
-form given by

\theta_{\alpha,\beta}:=-i(\partial-\overline{\partial})Log(\rho_{\alpha,\beta})
. Then,
\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}

coincides with the second order differential operator given by

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\left\{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial{\bar{z}}}+\left(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}\right)\left(z\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{z}}}\right)\right\}+\left(\alpha-\frac{\beta}{|z|^{2}}\right)^{2}|z|^{2}.

Proof.

Notice first that the differential 1-form
\theta_{\alpha,\beta}
is explicitly given by

\theta_{\alpha,\beta}=ik^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z)\left({\bar{z}}dz-zd\bar{z}\right)
with

k^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z):=\alpha-{\beta}/{|z|^{2}}.
Straightforward computation
making use of the well-known facts
d^{*}=-\star d\star
and
(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}=\star(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})\star

shows that for every smooth differential
1
-form
\omega=Adz+Bd\overline{z}
we have

(3.11)

\displaystyle d^{*}\left(Adz+Bd\overline{z}\right)=-2\left(\frac{\partial A}{\partial\overline{z}}+\frac{\partial B}{\partial z}\right)

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}\left(Adz+Bd\overline{z}\right)=-2ik^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z)\left(zA-\overline{z}B\right).

Therefore, by taking
\omega=df=\partial fdz+\overline{\partial}fd\overline{z}
in (
3.11
) one recovers the explicit expression of the Hodge–de Rham operator

(3.13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{0,0}^{*}\nabla_{0,0}=\frac{1}{4}d^{*}d=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial{\bar{z}}}.

Moreover, the explicit differential expression of the operators

d^{*}(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})
,
(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}d

and
(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})
are given respectively by

(3.14)

\displaystyle\qquad d^{*}(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})f=2ik^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z)(E-\bar{E})f,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\qquad(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}df=-2ik^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z)(E-\bar{E})f,

(3.16)

\displaystyle\qquad(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})f=4k^{\alpha}_{\beta}(z)^{2}|z|^{2}f.

Subsequently, by expanding
\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}
as

(3.17)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}=\frac{1}{4}\left\{d^{*}d+i(d^{*}\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}}-(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}d)+(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})^{*}(\mathbb{ext}_{\theta_{\alpha,\beta}})\right\},

and next making use of (
3.13
)–(
3.16
), we get its explicit expression given through
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
in (
3.10
).
∎

Remark 3.5
.

The second order differential operator
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
in
(
3.10
) is a magnetic Laplacian with a constant
homogeneous magnetic field of magnitude
\alpha
applied perpendicularly on the complex plane. Indeed, we have

d\theta_{\alpha,\beta}=d\theta_{\alpha}=2i\partial\overline{\partial}(Log(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}))=2i\alpha dz\wedge d\overline{z},

where
\theta_{\alpha,\beta}=\theta_{\alpha}+\widetilde{\theta}_{\beta}
with

\widetilde{\theta}_{\alpha}=i\alpha\left({\bar{z}}dz-zd\bar{z}\right)
and
\widetilde{\theta}_{\beta}=-i\beta\left(zdz-zd\bar{z}\right)/|z|^{2}
.
Moreover, the operator
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
is essentially the classical Landau Hamiltonian in (
1.3
)
perturbed by a first order differential operator associated with the potential
1
-form
\widetilde{\theta}_{\beta}

closed (with zero magnetic field), singular (at the origin) and modeling the Aharonov–Bohm effect.

Theorem 3.6
.

The functions
|z|^{2\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}\psi_{n,m}^{2\alpha,2\beta}
are
L^{2}
-eigenfunctions of the magnetic Laplacian
\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}^{*}\nabla_{\alpha,\beta}

with
\alpha(2n+1)
as associated eigenvalue.

Proof.

The proof is immediate using Theorem
3.1
, Lemma
3.4
and observing that the operators
\Delta_{2\alpha}^{2\beta}
in (
3.1
) and the magnetic Laplacian
\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}
in (
3.10
)
are unitary equivalent. More precisely, we have

\rho_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\Delta_{2\alpha}^{2\beta}+\alpha\right)\left((\rho_{\alpha,\beta})^{-1}f\right)=\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta},

which readily follows since

\mathcal{D}_{\alpha,\beta}=B^{*_{-\alpha,-\beta}}\circ A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}+\alpha=A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}\circ B^{*_{-\alpha,-\beta}}-\alpha.

Here
A^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}
is as in (
3.3
) and
B^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}
is the differential operator given by

B^{*_{\alpha,\beta}}f=[\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)]^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial{\overline{z}}}\left(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(z)f\right).

∎

4.
Analytical side (poly-meromorphy)

In this section, we discuss the regularity of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
as poly-meromorphic functions on the complex plane and we determinate the ”bi-order” of its unique pole. Recall first from

[
3
, p 199]
that a
n
-meromorphic function (or poly-meromorphic of order
n
) on an open set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
is a complex-valued function for which there exist some meromorphic functions
\psi_{k}
;
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1
on
U
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle f(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\psi_{0}(z)+\overline{z}\psi_{1}(z)+\cdots+\overline{z}^{n-1}\psi_{n-1}(z).

They are called simply
n
-analytic (
n
-poly-holomorphic) when the component functions are holomorphic in
U
,
\psi_{k}\in Hol(U)
. The latter ones can equivalently be defined as those satisfying the generalized Cauchy–Riemann equation
\partial^{n}/\partial{\overline{z}}^{n}=0
.
In order to give the exact statement of the main result of this section, we need first to precise the notion of bi-order of a zero or a pole of a given poly-meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}
.
Thus, for a given non-constant
n
-analytic function
f
on an open set
U\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, a point
z_{0}\in U
is said to be a zero of bi-order
(r,s)
, for given non-negative integers
r,s
with
0\leq r\leq n-1
and
(r,s)\neq(0,0)
, if the following conditions are met

(a)

f
can be rewritten as
f=\overline{\left(z-z_{0}\right)}^{s}g

for certain
(n-s)
-analytic function

(4.2)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{k=0}^{n-s-1}\overline{(z-z_{0})}^{k}\phi_{k},

with
\phi_{k}\in Hol(U)
and
\phi_{0}
is not identically zero on
U
.

(b)

z_{0}
is a zero of order
r
for the constant component function
\phi_{0}
in (
4.2
).

The first condition
(a)
is to say that
z_{0}
is a zero of order
s
for
f
seen as a polynomial in
\overline{z}
.
Notice also that the suggested definition is equivalent to have

(4.3)

\displaystyle f\left(z,\overline{z}\right)=\overline{\left(z-z_{0}\right)}^{s}\left(\left(z-z_{0}\right)^{r}\varphi_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{n-s-1}\left(\overline{z-z_{0}}\right)^{k}\phi_{k}\right)

for
\varphi_{0},\phi_{j}\in Hol(U)
with
\varphi(z_{0})\neq 0
.
Notice here that
z_{0}
does not need to be a zero of the holomorphic components
\phi_{k}
;
k=1,2,\cdots,n-s-1
.
However, for the particular case of
z_{0}
being a common zero of
\phi_{k}
the expression in (
4.3
) reduces to

f(z,\overline{z})=(z-z_{0})^{r}\overline{(z-z_{0})}^{s}g(z,\overline{z}),

for certain non-vanishing poly-analytic function
g
. This makes
z_{0}
a zero of
f
of bi-order
(r,s)
.

A point
z_{0}\in U
is said to be a pole of order
r
(
r<0
) for given
n
-poly-meromorphic function
f
in (
4.1
)
if
\left(z-z_{0}\right)^{|r|}f
is a
n
-analytic function on
U
and
r
is the smallest negative integer satisfying this property. This is equivalent to
z_{0}
being a pole for certain component meromorphic function
\psi_{j}
with

r=min\{Ord_{p}\left(z_{0},\psi_{j}\right),j=0,1,\cdots,n-1\},

where
Ord_{p}\left(z_{0},\psi_{j}\right)
is exactly the multiplicity of
z_{0}
if it is a pole of
\psi_{j}
and
0
otherwise.
Such pole is said to be of bi-order
(r,s)
, if in addition
(a)
is satisfied.
Accordingly, we denote by
\mbox{bi-Ord}(z_{0};f)
the bi-order of a point
z_{0}
when is a zero or a pole of given
n
-poly-meromorphic function
f
.

Theorem 4.1
.

The functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
are poly-meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
. The origin is either a zero or a pole of bi-order

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{bi-Ord}(0;\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta})=\left(m-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)],n-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]\right).

Proof.

First of all, we point out that in view of (
2.3
), it is clear that the terms
\overline{z}^{n-k}
are always regulars for every
k\leq n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)\leq n
. The singularity of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
then lies in
z^{m-k}
for
k\leq n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)
.
In particular, the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
are poly-holomrphic (since they are polynomials in
z
and
\overline{z}
) if and only if

m\geq n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)
. The latter condition is equivalent to

\beta
being a non-positive integer or
m\geq n
.
In this case the expression of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
reduces to

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=z^{m-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]}\overline{z}^{n-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]}R_{n,m;n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}),

where the involved
R_{n,m;N}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
are the radial polynomials given by

(4.5)

\displaystyle R_{n,m;N}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=0}^{N}c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}|z|^{2([n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-k)}.

Subsequently, since
\beta+m+1>n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)
and then
c_{m,n,n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)}^{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0
, the origin is a zero of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
whenever
\min(m,n)>n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)
. Its bi-order is then

\displaystyle\mbox{bi-Ord}(0;\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(m-n,0),&\mbox{if}\quad\beta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}^{-}\mbox{ or }m\geq n,\\
(-\beta,n-m-\beta),&\mbox{if}\quad\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{-},\beta\neq 0\mbox{ and }n>\beta+m.\end{array}\right.

To achieve the proof, it remains sufficient to discuss the case of

m<n\wedge^{*}(m+\beta)
(i.e.
n>m>-\beta-1
and
\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
). In this case we have

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}z^{m-k}\overline{z}^{n-k}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)}c_{m,n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}z^{m-k}\overline{z}^{n-k}

\displaystyle=\overline{z}^{n-m}R_{n,m;m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})+\overline{z}^{n-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]}\sum_{j=0}^{[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m-1}c_{m,n,m+1+j}^{\alpha,\beta}\frac{\overline{z}^{[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m-1-j}}{z^{j+1}}

\displaystyle=\overline{z}^{n-m}R_{n,m;m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})+\frac{\overline{z}^{n-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]}}{z^{[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m}}S_{n,m;[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m-1}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}),

where
R_{n,m;m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
is as in (
4.5
) with
N=m
, and

S_{n,m;N}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\sum_{j=0}^{[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m-1}c_{m,n,m+1+j}^{\alpha,\beta}|\overline{z}|^{2([n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]-m-j-1)}.

It convenient to mention here that both
R_{n,m;N}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
and

S_{n,m;N}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
are poly-analytic radial polynomials on the whole complex plane for which the origin is not a zero (for again
c_{m,n,n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)}^{\alpha,\beta}\neq 0
).
This proves that the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}

are purely poly-meromorphic functions with
0
as unique pole if and only if
\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}
and
m<n
. The multiplicity of their unique pole is given by

\mbox{Ord}(0;\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta})=m-[n\wedge^{*}(\beta+m)]<0
and then

\displaystyle\mbox{bi-Ord}(0;\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(m-n,0\right)&\quad\beta\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z},n>m,\\
\left(m-n,0\right)&\quad\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+*},\beta+m\geq n>m,\\
\left(-\beta,n-\beta-m\right)&\quad\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{+*},n\geq\beta+m>m,\\
\left(-\beta,n-\beta-m\right)&\quad\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{-*},n>m.\end{array}\right.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The polynomial case, i.e., the restriction to the case of
m\geq n
(with
\beta\geq 0
) leads to the class of polynomials
Z^{\beta}_{m,n}(z,w)
introduced and studied by Ismail and Zeng

5.

Notice first that using the relation of

to the generalized Laguerre polynomials and the generating function for the latter ones

5

\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!j!}z^{j+n}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}z^{k}}{k!(n-k)!(j-(n-k))!}\psi_{k,j}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

for all

.
Moreover, by
[

with
\beta+k>-1

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{t^{n}\psi_{n,n+k}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})\psi_{n,n+k}^{\alpha,\beta}(w,\overline{w})}{n!(1+\beta+k)_{n}}=\frac{(wz)^{k}}{(1-t)^{\beta+k+1}}e^{\frac{\alpha t(|z|^{2}+|w|^{2})}{(1-t)}}{{}_{0}F_{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-\\
\beta+k+1\end{array}\bigg|\frac{|\alpha zw|^{2}}{t(1-t)^{2}}\right).

The next one is an analog of the standard one for the Itô–Hermite polynomials
[
11
, p. 7]

which appears as the special case when
\beta=0
and
\alpha=1
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any real
\beta>-1
,
the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
satisfy

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{m!n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\left(1-\frac{v}{z}\right)^{\beta}e^{zu+\alpha v\overline{z}-uv}.

Proof.

Starting from the left hand-side of (
5.1
) and inserting (
1.7
) and next interchanging the sum in
m
and the
n
-th derivative, one obtains

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{m!n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=z^{-\beta}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-v)^{n}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}\left(\varphi_{\overline{z}}(x)\right)}{d{x^{n}}}|_{x=z}

\displaystyle=z^{-\beta}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\varphi_{\overline{z}}(z-v),

with
\varphi_{\overline{z}}(x):=z^{\beta}e^{-\alpha(\overline{z}+u)x}
. The last equality follows using the translation operator of the Taylor series of the involved function and gives rise to the right hand-side of (
5.1
).
∎

The following results are partial generating functions for
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
(with fixed
n
or
m
).

Proposition 5.2
.

For
\beta>-1
, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\psi_{n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})\frac{v^{n}}{n!}=\frac{(z-v)^{k+\beta}}{z^{\beta}}e^{\alpha v\overline{z}}

as well as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}}{m!}\psi_{n,m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{z^{n}}e^{uz}L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(\alpha|z|^{2}-uz).

Proof.

The first assertion can be handled starting from the Rodrigues formula (
1.7
) and next expanding
e^{(z-v)u}
in the second right hand-side of (
5.1
). Indeed, the identity (
5.2
)
immediately follows from Theorem
5.1
by identifying the obtained series in
u
.

For (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}}{m!}\psi_{n,m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{z^{\beta}}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}(z^{\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}+zu})

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{z^{n}}e^{uz}L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(\alpha|z|^{2}-uz).

The latter expression in terms of the generalized Laguerre polynomials is immediate by making the variable change

.
∎

The exact statement of the next result concerning a special generating function for the
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle\gamma(s,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}t^{s-1}e^{-t}dt,\,\Re(s)>0.

Its expansion series reads

(5.5)

\displaystyle\gamma(s,x)=e^{-x}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{x^{k+s}}{(s)_{k+1}}}.

Theorem 5.3
.

For every
\beta>0
and
|uv|<|uz|
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{(\beta+1)_{m}n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\beta u^{-\beta}z^{-\beta}e^{u(z-v)+\alpha\overline{z}v}\gamma(\beta,u(z-v)).

The left hand-side in (

) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{(\beta+1)_{m}n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\beta z^{-\beta}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-v)^{n}}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}\left(u^{-\beta}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(zu)^{m+\beta}}{(\beta)_{m+1}}\right]\right).

This follows making use of (
1.7
) as well as the expansion (
5.5
). Moreover, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{(\beta+1)_{m}n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{z^{\beta}u^{\beta}}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-v)^{n}}{n!}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial{z^{n}}}\left(e^{z(u-\alpha\overline{z})}\gamma(\beta,zu)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{z^{\beta}u^{\beta}}e^{zu}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-v)^{n}(-\alpha\overline{z})^{n-k}(-u)^{k}}{k!(n-k)!}(1-\beta)_{k}\gamma(\beta-k,zu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta}{(zu)^{\beta}}e^{zu+\alpha\overline{z}v}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{k}v^{k}}{k!}(1-\beta)_{k}\gamma(\beta-k,zu).

The second equality follows using the Leibniz formula combined with the derivative formula given for the lower incomplete Gamma function in
[
5
, p. 21]
.
Finally, by means of the series formula in

valid for all

.

6.
Integral representations

The aim below is to derive some integral representations for the considered poly-meromorphic Itô–Hermite functions
\psi_{n,m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
.
The first one involves the Bessel function of order
\nu>-1
of the first kind defined by
[
5
, p. 675]
,

J_{\nu}(z):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\nu+1)}\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)^{\nu}{{}_{0}F_{1}}\left(\nu+1;-\frac{z^{2}}{4}\right).

More specifically, we assert the following.

Proposition 6.1
.

For fixed real
\beta
and integers
n,m
such that
n=0,1,\cdots
and
\beta+m-n>-1
, we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=(-1)^{n}\frac{z^{m-n}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}}{(\sqrt{\alpha}|z|)^{\beta+m-n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{n+m+\beta+1}J_{\beta+m-n}(2\sqrt{\alpha}|z|x)e^{-x^{2}}dt.

Proof.

Making use of the close connection of
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})
to the Laguerre polynomials combined with their integral representation in terms of the Bessel function
[
22
, p. 243]

L_{n}^{(\mu)}(x)=\frac{x^{{-\mu}/{2}}e^{x}}{n!}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}t^{n+\frac{{}_{\mu}}{2}}J_{\mu}(2\sqrt{tx})dt,

valid for
n=0,1,2,\cdots,
and
n+\mu>-1

(6.2)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=(-1)^{n}\frac{z^{m-n}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}}{(\sqrt{\alpha}|z|)^{\beta+m-n}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-t}t^{\frac{n+m+\beta}{2}}J_{\beta+m-n}(2|z|\sqrt{\alpha t})dt,

Proposition 6.2
.

The integral representation

(6.3)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,m}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}z^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}u^{m}v^{n}\left(z-\overline{v}\right)^{\beta}e^{-|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}+\alpha\overline{v}\overline{z}+\overline{u}z-\overline{u}\overline{v}}d\lambda(u,v)

\beta>-1
. Moreover, we have

(6.4)

\displaystyle\psi_{n,k}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})\frac{|v|^{2j}}{j!}=\frac{1}{z^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}v^{m}(z-\overline{v})^{\beta+k}e^{-(v-\alpha\overline{z})\overline{v}}d\lambda(v).

Proof.

Thanks to
\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\overline{\psi_{m,n}^{\alpha,\beta}(\overline{z},z)}
, we can rewrite the generating function (
5.1
) in the following equivalent form

(6.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\overline{u}^{m}\overline{v}^{n}}{m!n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})=\left(1-\frac{\overline{v}}{z}\right)^{\beta}e^{\overline{u}z+\alpha\overline{v}\overline{z}-\overline{u}\overline{v}}.

Next, by multiplying the both sides by the monomials in
u
and
v

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\overline{v}}{z}\right)^{\beta}u^{m}v^{n}e^{-|u|^{2}-|v|^{2}+\overline{u}z+\alpha\overline{v}\overline{z}-\overline{u}\overline{v}}d\lambda(u,v)=\pi^{2}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}),

which leads to (
6.3

Analogously, one gets (
6.4

5.2

∎

Let

7.1.
New integral formula for the generalized Laguerre polynomials

Using the obtained results one can derive new interesting integral formulas for the generalized Laguerre polynomials.
Thus, we claim the following.

Theorem 7.1
.

The
integral identity

(7.1)

\displaystyle L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(\alpha|z|^{2}-uz)=\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-\beta}}{n!\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{v}^{n}\left(z-v\right)^{\beta}e^{\alpha v\overline{z}-uv}e^{-|v|^{2}}d\lambda(v)

holds for
\beta>-1
.

Proof.

From (
5.1
), one has

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}}{m!}\psi_{k,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}{\left\langle{\sum_{m,n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{u^{m}v^{n}}{m!n!}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}),v^{k}}\right\rangle}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-|v|^{2}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{uz}}{\pi z^{\beta}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{v}^{k}\left(z-v\right)^{\beta}e^{\alpha v\overline{z}-uv}e^{-|v|^{2}}d\lambda(v).

Accordingly, the proof of (
7.1
) readily follows from (
5.3
).
∎

Remark 7.2
.

As particular case we get

(7.2)

\displaystyle L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(z)=\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-\beta}}{n!\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{v}^{n}\left(z-v\right)^{\beta}e^{-v(\overline{v}-1)}d\lambda(v)

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
by specifying
\alpha=0
and
u=-1
. Also, by taking
\alpha=1
and
u=0
, we get

(7.3)

\displaystyle L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(|z|^{2})=\frac{(-1)^{n}z^{n-\beta}}{n!\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\overline{v}^{n}\left(z-v\right)^{\beta}e^{v\overline{z}}e^{-|v|^{2}}d\lambda(v).

7.2.
Associated integral transforms

The orthogonality property of the functions
\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
suggest the consideration of two special functional spaces
\mathcal{F}^{2,\alpha}_{\beta,n}(\mathbb{C})
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{2,\alpha}_{\beta,m}(\mathbb{C})
of poly-meromorphic or anti-poly-meromorphic functions on the punctured complex plane in

L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})
for fixed non-negative integers
m
and
n
with
m+\beta>-1
. These spaces are spanned by
\psi_{n,j}^{\alpha,\beta}
,
j\geq[-\beta]
, and
\psi_{k,m}^{\alpha,\beta}
,
k=0,1,2,\cdots
, respectively.
Moreover, they can be seen as the poly-meromorphic analogs of the true poly-analytic (and anti-poly-analytic) Bargmann spaces
[
1
,
25
]
defined as specific closed subspace in

\ker\left({\partial^{n+1}}/{\partial\overline{z}^{n+1}}\right)\cap L^{2,\alpha}_{g}(\mathbb{C}),

and realized also as
L^{2}
-eigenspace

\mathcal{F}^{2,\alpha}_{n}(\mathbb{C})=\ker(\Delta_{\alpha}-\alpha n)
associated with the
n
-th Landau levels of the self-adjoint magnetic Laplacian

\Delta_{\alpha}=\Delta_{\alpha,0}=-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z\partial\overline{z}}+\alpha\overline{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}}

acting on
L^{2,\alpha}_{g}(\mathbb{C})
(see
[
2
,
24
]
).

Next, we show that
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{2,\alpha}_{\beta,m}(\mathbb{C})=\overline{Span\{\psi_{k,m}^{\alpha,\beta},\,k=0,1,2,\cdots\}}^{L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})}
can be realized as the image of the classical Segal–Bargmann space
\mathcal{F}^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{C})=Hol(\mathbb{C})\cap L^{2,\alpha}_{g}(\mathbb{C})
of holomorphic functions in the Hilbert space of the Gaussian functions,
L^{2,\alpha}_{g}(\mathbb{C}):=L^{2}(\mathbb{C},e^{-\alpha|\xi|^{2}}d\lambda)
, by means of the specific integral transform

(7.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,\beta}f(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\beta+m}}{\Gamma(\beta+m+1)}\right)^{1/2}z^{m}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\left(1-\frac{\overline{w}}{z}\right)^{\beta+m}e^{-\alpha\overline{w}(w-\overline{z})}f(w)d\lambda(w),

provided that the integral exists. Here

m
is a fixed integer such that
m>-\beta-1
for given
\beta>-1
.
In fact, the transform
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,\beta}
is well defined and maps the orthonormal basis
e_{n}^{\alpha}(z)=(\alpha^{n+1}/\pi n!)^{1/2}z^{n}
of
\mathcal{F}^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{C})
to an orthonormal basis of
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{2,\alpha}_{\beta,m}(\mathbb{C})
. More precisely, we have

\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,\beta}(e_{n})(z)=\left(\frac{\alpha^{\beta+m+1}}{\pi\alpha^{n}\Gamma(\beta+m+1)n!}\right)^{1/2}\psi_{n,m}^{\alpha,\beta}(z,\overline{z}).

This follows by observing that the integral kernel of the transform
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,\beta}
in (
7.4
) is the generating function in (
5.2
).
Its inverse is given by

(7.5)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,\beta})^{-1}(f)(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\left(\frac{\alpha^{\beta+m}}{\Gamma(\beta+m+1)}\right)^{1/2}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{(\overline{z}-w)^{\beta+m}}{\overline{z}^{\beta}}e^{-\alpha(\overline{z}-w)z}f(z)d\lambda(z).

It is worth noticing that for the particular case of
\beta=0
, the corresponding transform
\mathcal{B}_{m}^{\alpha,0}
reduces further to the one considered in
[
4

mapping unitarily the Segal-Bargmann space to the true anti-poly-analytic Bargmann spaces
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{2,\alpha}_{m}(\mathbb{C})
.

Similarly, associated with the kernel function on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
given through the partial generating function in (
5.3
),

(7.6)

\displaystyle s_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(u,z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{z^{n}}e^{uz}L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(\alpha|z|^{2}-uz),

we consider the integral transform

(7.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}f(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{z^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}L^{(\beta-n)}_{n}(\alpha|z|^{2}-uz)e^{-u(\overline{u}-z)}f(u)d\lambda(u).

The image of

\mathcal{F}^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{C})
by
\mathcal{S}_{n}^{\alpha,\beta}
for arbitrary
\beta>-1

L^{2,\alpha}_{\beta}(\mathbb{C})
spanned by
\psi_{n,j}^{\alpha,\beta}

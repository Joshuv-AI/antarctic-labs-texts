Prospects for ditauonium discovery at colliders
David d’Enterria, Hua-Sheng Shao

Abstract
The feasibility of observing ditauonium, the bound state of two tau leptons, at e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} colliders (BES III at s = 3.78 \sqrt{s}~=~3.78 GeV, Belle II at s = 10.6 \sqrt{s}~=~10.6 GeV, a future super tau-charm factory (STCF) at s ≈ 2 ​ m τ \sqrt{s}\approx 2m_{\tau} , and the FCC-ee at s = 91.2 \sqrt{s}~=~91.2 GeV) as well as in hadronic and photon-photon collisions at the LHC, is studied. Cross sections and expected yields for spin-0 para- ( 𝒯 0 \mathcal{T}_{0} ) and spin-1 ortho- ( 𝒯 1 \mathcal{T}_{1} ) ditauonium are presented for nine different production and decay processes. Para-ditauonium can be observed at the FCC-ee via photon fusion in its diphoton decay ( γ ​ γ → 𝒯 0 → γ ​ γ \gamma\gamma\to\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\gamma\gamma ). Ortho-ditauonium can be observed at STCF via e + ​ e − → 𝒯 1 → μ + ​ μ − e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-} , where a threshold scan with monochromatized beams can also provide a very precise extraction of the tau lepton mass with at least 𝒪 ⁡ ( 25 CLOSE \mathcal{O}(25 keV) uncertainty. Observing pp → 𝒯 1 ​ ( μ + ​ μ − ) + X \to\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})+X at the LHC is possible by identifying its displaced vertex with a good control of the combinatorial dimuon background. In addition, we compute the rare decay branching fractions of ditauonium into quarkonium plus a photon.



I
Introduction

Leptons of opposite electric charge (
\ell^{\pm}=e^{\pm},\mu^{\pm},\tau^{\pm}
) can form short-lived “onium” bound states under their quantum electrodynamics (QED) interaction. Among the six possible leptonium states —
(e^{+}e^{-})
,
(\mu^{\pm}\mathrm{e}^{\mp})
,
(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})
,
(\tau^{\pm}\mathrm{e}^{\mp})
,
(\tau^{\pm}\mu^{\mp})
, and
(\tau^{+}\tau^{-})
— only the first two, positronium
Deutsch:1951zza
and muonium
Hughes:1960zz
, have been observed to date. The lightest system, positronium, has been thoroughly studied for precision QED tests
Karshenboim:2005iy
, and in searches for violations of the discrete space-time CPT symmetries (where C, P, and T are charge conjugation, parity, and time reversal, respectively) either singly or in various combinations
Bernreuther:1988tt
;
Yamazaki:2009hp
. This work focuses on the heaviest leptonium state, ditauonium, proposed originally in
Moffat:1975uw
;
Avilez:1977ai
;
Avilez:1978sa
, and whose detailed spectroscopic properties
dEnterria:2022alo
and first feasibility studies for its production via two-photon fusion at
e^{+}e^{-}
and hadron colliders
dEnterria:2022ysg
, have been recently presented. Its ground state (principal quantum number
n=1
) has two states with total angular momentum
J=0
and 1, depending on the relative (opposite or parallel) orientation of its constituent tau leptons, known as para- (
\mathcal{T}_{0}
) and ortho- (
\mathcal{T}_{1}
) ditauonium, respectively. These states, labelled also as
1^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}
and
1^{3}\mathrm{S}_{1}
using the spectroscopic
n^{2S+1}L_{J}
notation (for total spin
S=0,1
and
L=0,1,...\equiv\mathrm{S},\mathrm{P},...
orbital angular momentum), have quantum numbers
J^{\mathrm{PC}}=0^{-+}
and
1^{--}
, respectively.

The mass of the ditauonium ground state is
m_{{}_{\mathcal{T}}}=2m_{\tau}+E_{\text{bind}}=3553.6962\pm 0.2400
MeV, with a binding energy of
E_{\text{bind}}=-\alpha^{2}m_{\tau}/(4n^{2})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4})=-23.655
keV at next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) accuracy in the electromagnetic coupling
\alpha=1/137.036
. This value accounts for a
-115
eV downwards Lamb shift, and there is a minuscule
\mathcal{O}(3
eV) hyperfine splitting between the
1^{1}\mathrm{S}_{0}
and
1^{3}\mathrm{S}_{1}
states
dEnterria:2022alo
. The ditauonium mass uncertainty is fully dominated by the current experimental precision of the tau lepton mass,
m_{\tau}=1776.86\pm 0.12
MeV
Zyla:2020zbs
. The total decay widths of para- and ortho-ditauonium are
\Gamma_{\text{tot}}=23.84
and 31.59 meV, corresponding to lifetimes of
\tauup=\hslash c/\Gamma_{\text{tot}}=27.60
and 20.83 fs, respectively
dEnterria:2022alo

\tauup=290.3
fs
Zyla:2020zbs

(\tau^{+}\tau^{-})
bound state. As a matter of fact, and despite being the shortest-lived leptonium, the ditauonium ground states have lifetimes that are 5 to 8 orders-of-magnitude longer than those of similar charmonium states, with masses also in the 3–4 GeV range, that decay much promptly through the strong interaction.

The motivation for the production and study of ditauonium is threefold. First, ditauonium can provide new high-precision information about fundamental properties of the free tau lepton itself such as its mass, as explained below, and associated precision tests of the SM (e.g., lepton flavour universality) and of quantities
(e.g., the CKM
|V_{us}|

\tau

Koide:1983qe
)
that parametrically depend on
m_{\tau}

HFLAV:2022pwe

a_{0}=2/(m_{\tau}\alpha)=30.4
fm is the smallest of all leptonium systems, and its associated minimum “photon ionization” energy (Rydberg constant),
R_{\infty}=\alpha/(4\pi a_{0})=3.76
keV, is the largest. This implies that
\mathcal{T}
is the most strongly bound of all leptonia, and that the investigation of its properties can thereby provide new tests of QED and CPT symmetries at much smaller distances than other exotic atoms. Third, ditauonium features enhanced sensitivity to any physics beyond the standard model (BSM) that is suppressed by powers of
{{\cal{O}}}\left(m_{\ell}/\Lambda_{\text{BSM}}\right)
or affected by uncertainties from hadronic effects, as is the case for, e.g., positronium or muonic-hydrogen states, respectively. Since various hints of violation of lepton flavour universality have appeared in the last years in studies with free leptons
HFLAV:2022pwe
;
Bifani:2018zmi
, it appears relevant to examine if any such effects shows up in the properties of the ditauonium bound states compared to its lighter siblings, positronium and dimuonium.

In the processes
\gamma\gamma\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}
where ditauonium is produced alone at rest, its decay vertex is indistinguishable from the primary collision vertex. This holds even in the presence of longitudinal boosts because displaced
\mathcal{T}
decays along the beam axis fall within the typical collision spot sizes in the
z

\mathcal{O}(350~\mu
m)
z
-width). For these cases, the ditauonium decay products are swamped by much larger prompt diphoton and/or dilepton (plus photon) backgrounds, and its observation requires the largest possible integrated luminosities. The measurement through resonant
s
-channel production,
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
(Fig.
1
(d)), relies on counting the number of dilepton events and observing an excess from the ortho-ditauonium decay above the expectations from background
e^{+}e^{-}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
processes that have orders-of-magnitude larger cross sections. Maximizing the signal counts requires also to run
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions with c.m. energies as close as possible to the pole mass of the very narrow
\mathcal{T}
Breit–Wigner (BW) peak.

For the rest of the processes, the presence of decay charged leptons accompanied by the additional production of a photon or a jet (in the p-p case), may facilitate the measurement of ditauonium by exploiting its finite decay length (leading to an exponential tail of secondary vertices with proper
\left<L\right>=c\tau=6.2,\,8.2~\mu
m decay lengths for
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{0}
, respectively), and the boost given by the extra photon
\gamma
or jet
j
emitted. Both considerations lead to a potentially visible decay away from the
e^{+}e^{-}
or p-p interaction point at an average transverse distance of
\left<L_{xy}\right>=\text{boost}_{\perp}\times c\tau
, free from very large prompt SM physics backgrounds, such as
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}(\gamma),\mu^{+}\mu^{-}(\gamma)
processes.
The experimental signature is similar to that of low-mass long-lived dark photon searches via
A^{\prime}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays at
e^{+}e^{-}
and p-p colliders
BaBar:2014zli
;
BESIII:2017fwv
;
Kou:2018nap
;

\mathcal{T}

(\beta\gamma)_{\perp}=p_{\mathrm{T},\mathcal{T}}/m_{\mathcal{T}}\gtrsim 2
are needed in order to identify the tail of secondary dilepton-decays vertices in the standard silicon trackers with typical vertex position resolutions of the order of
\delta L_{xy}\approx 10~\mu
m. We will therefore provide estimates of the number of ditauonium events with displaced vertices, as a proxy for the signal discovery possibilities.
Although decays with
L_{xy}\gtrsim 30~\mu
m take place
sufficiently separated from the interaction point to expect negligible backgrounds from prompt SM processes, in particular in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, such events will nonetheless be potentially subject to instrumental effects that may difficult its observation. In a real analysis, vertex resolution tails, lepton track misreconstruction, and, in the case of dielectron final states,
\gamma\to e^{+}e^{-}
conversions occurring at larger radii but wrongly extrapolated to a vertex closer to the nominal interaction point, should be taken into account. Resolution effects will also slightly reduce the signal efficiency. All such subtle reconstruction effects must be evaluated with full detector simulations, for both signal efficiency and background rejection, that are beyond the scope of this work.

III.4
Ortho-ditauonium via
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}

The cross section for resonant
\mathcal{T}_{1}
production in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions at a c.m. energy
\sqrt{s}
is theoretically given by the relativistic BW expression:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\text{ideal}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{12\pi\Gamma_{\text{tot}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\,\Gamma_{e^{+}e^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})}{(s-m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2})^{2}+\Gamma_{\text{tot}}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\,m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}.

(8)

For an
e^{+}e^{-}
run exactly at the pole mass,
\sqrt{s}=m_{\mathcal{T}}
, this cross section amounts to
\sigma^{\text{ideal}}=12\pi\mathcal{B}_{e^{+}e^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})/m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}=236.6~\mu
b. However, similar to the case of resonant Higgs boson production at the FCC-ee,
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathrm{H}

dEnterria:2021xij
, two effects reduce substantially this purely theoretical cross section. First, the actual beams are never perfectly monoenergetic but have a distribution of incoming energies that is many orders-of-magnitude wider than the width
\Gamma_{\text{tot}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
of the very narrow ditauonium resonance. Secondly, the occurrence of ISR photon emission from the
e^{\pm}
beams will shift the actual collision energy
\sqrt{s}
below the BW peak. Both effects render the
(s-m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2})^{2}
term much larger than
\Gamma_{\text{tot}}^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{1})\,m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}
in the denominator of Eq. (
8
), and strongly reduce the actual cross section. For a Gaussian
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. energy spread of width
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}
(with
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}\ll\sqrt{s}
), the actual
s
-channel cross section with both effects convolved can be written as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\sigma^{\text{actual}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{12\pi^{2}\Gamma_{e^{+}e^{-}}(\mathcal{T}_{1})}{m_{\mathcal{T}}}\int_{0}^{1}{\!\!dx_{1}\!\!\int_{0}^{1}{\!\!dx_{2}f_{e^{-}/e^{-}}(x_{1},s)f_{e^{+}/e^{+}}(x_{2},s)V_{2}\left(\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}s};m_{\mathcal{T}},\Gamma_{\text{tot}}(\mathcal{T}_{1}),\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}}\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}\right)}},

(9)

where the
f_{e^{-}/e^{-}}
and
f_{e^{+}/e^{+}}
structure functions describe the distribution of energy of each
e^{\pm}
beam reduced by a fraction
x_{1,2}
after collinear initial photon emission, and where the relativistic Voigtian function
V_{2}
reads

\displaystyle V_{2}(E;m,\Gamma,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{d\tilde{E}\frac{m\Gamma}{\pi}\frac{1}{(\tilde{E}^{2}-m^{2})^{2}+\Gamma^{2}m^{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\delta}e^{-\frac{(\tilde{E}-E)^{2}}{2\delta^{2}}}},

(10)

which can be expressed in terms of the complementary error function
Kycia:2017gjn
. In order to carry out the two integrations in Eq. (
9
), we use the leading-logarithmic expressions for
f_{e^{\pm}/e^{\pm}}
, and the parametrization of the fractions of beam energies
x_{1,2}
, of Ref.
Frixione:2021zdp
. With those ingredients at hand, one can compute the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}
cross sections for any
\sqrt{s}
and
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}
values. Figure
4
(left) shows the resonant
\mathcal{T}_{1}
cross section in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=m_{\mathcal{T}}
and
m_{\mathcal{T}}\pm 50~{\rm keV}
as a function of the c.m. spread
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}
. Assuming that one runs exactly at the
m_{\mathcal{T}}
mass, the cross section decreases very rapidly, following a
1/\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}
dependence (top black curve). If the c.m. energy of the
e^{+}e^{-}
collision misses the mass of the resonance by
+50
keV (middle green curve) or by
-50
keV (bottom red curve), the cross section peaks at
\sigma^{\text{actual}}(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1})\approx 20
pb for a
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}\approx 50
keV spread, and decreases to the left and right of it. The drop in the cross section in the region
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}\lesssim 50
keV is much smaller for the
+50
-keV shift, than for the
-50
-keV case, because running above the resonance profits from possible ISR radiative returns that bring the
e^{+}e^{-}
c.m. energy onto the BW peak.

The experimental observation of resonant
\mathcal{T}_{1}
production is a “counting experiment” whereby one measures the number of dilepton events searching for an excess issuing from the ortho-ditauonium decays above the expectations from the background-only
e^{+}e^{-}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
processes. We focus on the
\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decay mode which has smaller continuum backgrounds than the
\mathcal{T}_{1}\to e^{+}e^{-}
channel. As discussed in the direct Higgs boson production in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions at
\sqrt{s}=m_{\mathrm{H}}
at the FCC-ee
dEnterria:2021ljz
, in order to maximize the cross section one needs to: (i) run at a
\sqrt{s}
value as close as possible to the true ditauonium mass
m_{\mathcal{T}}
(known today with a 250-keV uncertainty, from twice the tau-lepton mass value precision), (ii) to be able to measure and calibrate the actual machine
\sqrt{s}
within the same high accuracy, and (iii) to reduce the c.m. energy spread
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}
down to values of the same order. Regarding the first point, the BES III collaboration performed a fine mass scan experiment in 2018 with five points around the tau pair production threshold with a
\sqrt{s}
spread of
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}=1.24
MeV, and a total integrated luminosity of 140 pb
-1

Achasov:2019rdp
. The final expected uncertainty in
m_{\tau}
will be around 50 keV. Similarly, Belle II forecasts to reach tau mass uncertainties below 150 keV (systematic and statistical sources combined) with about 300 fb
-1
of data
Belle-II:2020wbx
. Combining these results, plus all others from the PDG world average
Zyla:2020zbs
, it is not inconceivable to reach a precision of
\mathcal{O}(50~\text{keV})
in the tau mass (i.e., twice this value,
\mathcal{O}(100~\text{keV})
, in the
\mathcal{T}
mass) whenever a dedicated search for the ditauonium is carried out at a future STCF.
Regarding the
\sqrt{s}
calibration, the BES-III experiment has already achieved a high accuracy, at the level of
2\cdot 10^{-5}
, by exploiting the measurement of monochromatic laser photons backscattered from the
e^{\pm}
beams in its beam energy measurement system (BEMS)
Abakumova:2011rp
. The application of the same technique at three different lepton colliders can reach accuracies of the actual c.m. energy not worse than 50 keV
Achasov:2020iou
. Last but not least, on the c.m. energy spread, the possibility of monochromatization of electron/positron beams has been considered several times in the literature
Kirkby:1996qt
;
Bogomyagkov:2017uul
;
Telnov:2020rxp
, with values of
\delta_{\!\sqrt{s}}=50
keV at the tau-pair production threshold theoretically achievable.

III.5
Ortho-ditauonium via
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})
in ISR collisions

The cross section to produce ortho-ditauonium in
e^{+}e^{-}
annihilation at
\sqrt{s}>m_{\mathcal{T}}
after
\gamma
ISR emission (Fig.
1
(e), with a collinear photon) is given by

\displaystyle\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1},\,\text{ISR})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{5}}{n^{3}}\frac{1}{s}\frac{1}{(m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}-m_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2})^{2}}\left[m_{\mathrm{Z}}^{4}+\frac{8c_{w}^{2}-9}{8s_{w}^{2}c_{w}^{2}}m_{\mathrm{Z}}^{2}m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}+\frac{45-84c_{w}^{2}+40c_{w}^{4}}{128s_{w}^{4}c_{w}^{4}}m_{\mathcal{T}}^{4}\right]\,\mathcal{L}_{e^{+}e^{-}}\left(\frac{m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{s},s\right),

(11)

with the same parameters defined in Eq. (
6
), and where the last factor encodes the change in the ditauonium cross section due to the emission of ISR photon(s). This latter factor is defined as an effective
e^{+}e^{-}
luminosity in terms of the electron and positron structure functions,
f_{e^{-}/e^{-}}(x,s)
and
f_{e^{+}/e^{+}}(x,s)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{e^{+}e^{-}}(z,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{z}^{1}{\frac{dx}{x}f_{e^{-}/e^{-}}\left(x,s\right)f_{e^{+}/e^{+}}\left(\frac{z}{x},s\right)},

(12)

where
x
is the fraction of the beam momentum carried out by the
e^{\pm}
after collinear ISR emission.
This effective luminosity can be evaluated either via MC methods
Shao:2014rwa
or in a parton-distribution-function (PDF)-like approach
Kuraev:1985wb
;
Beenakker:1996kt
;
Frixione:2019lga
;
Bertone:2019hks
.
Again, in the infinite Z boson mass limit, a more handy formula is available

\displaystyle\lim_{m_{\mathrm{Z}}\to\infty}{\sigma(e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1},\,\text{ISR})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{2}\alpha^{5}}{n^{3}s}\,\mathcal{L}_{e^{+}e^{-}}\left(\frac{m_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}{s},s\right).

(13)

Here, we use the
\mathcal{L}_{e^{+}e^{-}}(z,s)
effective luminosity at leading-logarithmic accuracy from Eq. (67) of Ref.
Beenakker:1996kt
, with the so-called ‘mixed’ prescription that partially accounts for a few extra non-leading terms in the cross section.

Ortho-ditauonium via

In order to transversely boost the ortho-ditauonium and facilitate the observation of its displaced decay vertex, we consider
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}+\gamma
where the emitted photon has a large scattering angle (i.e., it is not collinear with the beams,
\theta\neq 0,\pi
) in the rest frame of the
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\gamma
system (Figs.
1
(e) and (f) for a non-collinear photon). In the above frame, the differential cross section with respect to the cosine of the relative angle

III.8
Ditauonium decays into quarkonia-plus-photon,
\mathcal{T}_{0,1}\to(c\overline{c})_{1,0}+\gamma

Ditauonium can decay into final states containing a light quark-antiquark pair (
q\overline{q}=u\overline{u},d\overline{d},s\overline{s}
), either directly in the ortho-ditauonium case (
\mathcal{T}_{1}\to q\overline{q}
), or via the Dalitz channel for para-ditauonium where one photon splits into the pair (
\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\gamma q\overline{q}
). In principle, the
c\overline{c}
decay is not kinematically accessible because the lightest charm D meson has a mass that is more than half the ditauonium mass. It is however possible for ditauonium to decay into light charmonium states plus a photon, via
(\tau^{+}\tau^{-})_{0}\to\mathrm{J/\psi}+\gamma
and
(\tau^{+}\tau^{-})_{1}\to\eta_{c}+\gamma
(Fig.
6
).

e^{+}e^{-}
and p-p collisions followed by
(c\overline{c})+\gamma

The corresponding partial widths can be derived from the NNLO expressions for
\mathcal{T}_{0}
Dalitz decays and for
\mathcal{T}_{1}
diquark-plus-photon decays respectively, multiplied by the standard long-distance matrix elements (LDMEs),
\langle\mathcal{O}^{\mathrm{J/\psi},\eta_{c}}(^{2s+1}L_{J}^{[c]})\rangle
. LDMEs describe the formation of each quarkonium system in the non-relativistic QCD (NRQCD) framework, as an expansion in terms of the relatively low velocity
v_{c}
of the bound charm quarks
Bodwin:1994jh
. The partial widths for the quarkonium decay modes, suppressed by a
\mathcal{O}(\alpha v_{c}^{3})
factor and a small phase space compared to the leading decay channels, read

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mathrm{J/\psi}\gamma}(\mathcal{T}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\alpha^{6}\pi\,m_{\tau}}{27\,n^{3}}\left(1-\frac{m_{\mathrm{J/\psi}}^{2}}{4m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)\frac{8\langle O^{\mathrm{J/\psi}}(^{3}S_{1}^{[1]})\rangle}{m_{\mathrm{J/\psi}}^{3}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(v_{c}^{2},\alpha_{s},\alpha)\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\alpha^{4}}{n^{3}}\frac{m_{\tau}}{m_{\mathrm{J/\psi}}}\left(1-\frac{m_{\mathrm{J/\psi}}^{2}}{4m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)\Gamma_{\mathrm{J/\psi}\to e^{+}e^{-}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(\alpha)\right)=6.46\pm 0.12~\mu\text{eV,}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\eta_{c}\gamma}(\mathcal{T}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{16\alpha^{6}\pi\,m_{\tau}}{243\,n^{3}}\left(1-\frac{m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}}{4m_{\tau}^{2}}\right)\frac{m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}}{4m_{\tau}^{2}}\frac{8\langle O^{\eta_{c}}(^{1}S_{0}^{[1]})\rangle}{m_{\eta_{c}}^{3}}\left(1+\mathcal{O}(v_{c}^{2},\alpha_{s},\alpha)\right)=1.53_{-0.19}^{+0.89}~\mu\text{eV,}

(19)

where the numerical results correspond to the
n=1
ground states, the QCD coupling evaluated at the Z scale is taken as
\alpha_{s}(m_{\mathrm{Z}})=0.118
, and

keV is the

. The uncertainty of the
\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\mathrm{J/\psi}\,\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0.0271\pm 0.0005)\%,

\displaystyle\left(0.0048^{+0.0028}_{-0.0006}\right)\%.

\,+\,
photon decay channels appear hopeless. The
\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\chi_{c}\gamma

IV
Summary

We have evaluated the possibility to experimentally produce and observe ditauonium, the bound state of two tau leptons, in high-energy
e^{+}e^{-}
and hadron collisions. Ditauonium is the heaviest and most compact purely leptonic “atomic” system, and remains experimentally unobserved to date. Its experimental study can provide a high-precision extraction of the tau mass, as well as novel QED tests sensitive to physics beyond the standard model, such as e.g., violations of lepton flavour universality, that may not impact its lighter siblings (positronium and dimuonium). Nine production and decay channels have been studied at four different
e^{+}e^{-}
facilities (BES III, Belle II, STCF, and FCC-ee) as well in hadronic and photon-fusion collisions at the LHC. The overall conclusion is that the observation of the para- and/or ortho-ditauonium states is challenging but feasible.

Para-ditauonium can be measured at Belle II and FCC-ee via
\gamma\gamma\to\mathcal{T}_{0}
photon fusion in its dominant diphoton decay with
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\gamma\gamma)=77.72\%
branching fraction, provided the large background from the overlapping
\mathrm{\chi_{c2}}\to\gamma\gamma
decay can be precisely controlled in-situ.
On the other hand, its Dalitz decay with
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma)=2.31\%
, will likely remain unobserved. Ortho-ditauonium can be observed in the
s
-channel in a normal run at a future STCF operating at the
e^{+}e^{-}\to\tau^{+}\tau^{-}
threshold. In addition, running with monochromatized beams, leading to a
\mathcal{O}(100
keV) c.m. energy spread, the ditauonium cross sections are large, and its mass can be determined from a threshold scan within a precision just given by the c.m. energy calibration. For a typical
\mathcal{O}(50
keV) c.m. energy calibration, the mass of the tau lepton can be obtained with an uncertainty half this value,
\mathcal{O}(25
keV). Such a ditauonium-based approach will likely become the most precise and accurate experimental means to determine the
\tau
mass. Ditauonium should thus be incorporated as an integral part of the experimental programme of precision studies of the tau lepton at any future STCF. The ISR production of ortho-ditauonium has too low cross sections at all
e^{+}e^{-}
colliders to be observable over the huge prompt dilepton
e^{+}e^{-}\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}(\gamma)
backgrounds, unless one requires the production of a large-angle photon that boost the resonance decay away from the interaction point. This latter possibility may lead to the observation of a few
e^{+}e^{-}\to\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})+\gamma
events with displaced transverse decays at Belle II.
Finally, the associated production with a jet, p p
\to\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mu^{+}\mu^{-})+j
, can be observed by ATLAS and CMS at the HL-LHC by measuring its displaced dimuon vertex beyond
L_{xy}\approx 100~\mu
m, with a good control of the large instrumental combinatorial dimuon backgrounds. Last but not least, the very rare decay branching fractions of ditauonium into quarkonium plus a photon,
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{0}\to\mathrm{J/\psi}\gamma)=0.0271\%
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1}\to\eta_{c}\gamma)=0.005\%
, have been computed for the first time.

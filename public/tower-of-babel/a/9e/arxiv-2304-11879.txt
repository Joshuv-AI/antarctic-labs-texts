1.
Introduction

In this paper, we investigate the existence, uniqueness,
L_{p}
-regularity, and Hölder regularity of a solution to the following stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE):

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u=a^{ij}u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}u_{x^{i}}+cu-\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}+\xi u^{1+\gamma}\dot{F},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

where
\beta,\gamma>0
and the coefficients
a^{ij},b^{i},c,\bar{b}
and
\xi
are
\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-measurable real-valued maps with Einstein’s summation convention on
i,j=1,...d
. Here
\dot{F}
is a spatially homogeneous colored noise. More precisely,
\dot{F}
is a centered generalized Gaussian random field whose covariance is given by

\mathbb{E}[F(t,x)F(s,y)]=\delta_{0}(t-s)f(x-y),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta distribution and
f
is a
correlation function (or measure)
. In other words,
f
is a nonnegative and nonnegative definite function/measure. We denote that the spectral measure
\mu
of
f
is a nonnegative tempered measure defined by

(1.2)

\mu(\xi):=\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}f(dx)

which is the Fourier transform of
f
.

The SPDE (
1.1
) is also called the stochastic reaction-diffusion equation (SRDE) whose typical form is given by

(1.3)

\partial_{t}u=\mathcal{L}u+g(u)+\sigma(u)\dot{F},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

where
\mathcal{L}
is a second-order eilliptic differential operator. The function
g(u)
denotes the reaction force and the term
\sigma(u)\dot{F}
denotes the random force called the multiplicative noise. There have been successful investigations on the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the SRDE (
1.3
). When
g
and
\sigma
are globally Lipschitz continuous with at most linear growth, the well-posedness of (
1.3
) was studied in
[
DPZ14
,
Dal99
,
Wal86
,
Kry99
]
. For the case where
\sigma
is locally Lipschitz continuous with linear growth and
g
is non-Lipschitz, including the Fisher-KPP type (
g(u)=u-u^{2}
) and the Allen-Cahn type (
g(u)=u-u^{3}
), there have been also fruitful achievements such as
[
Cer03
,
Cer11
,
CDPF13
]
under some monotonicity conditions on
g
with polynomial growths (see also
[
MR10
,
Mar18
]
). We refer to
[
MMQ11
,
KKMS20
]
for interesting topics on equations of this type.
For
\sigma
with super-linear growh, Mueller
[
Mue91
,
Mue00
]
showed that when
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
and
g=0
, the solution does blow-up at finite time with positive probability if
|\sigma(u)|\gtrsim|u|^{1+\gamma}
for
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
and does not if
|\sigma(u)|\lesssim(1+|u|)^{1+\gamma}
for
0\leq\gamma<\frac{1}{2}
(see also
[
CH21
]
for colored noise cases). We refer to
[
DKZ19
,
CH22
]
for the the case where
|g(u)|\lesssim|u|\log|u|
and
|\sigma(u)|\lesssim|u|\log|u|^{\kappa/2}
for some
\kappa>0
satisfying the reinforced Dalang’s condition on
f
(see Assumption
2.3
).

In the present paper, we discuss the well-posedness problem of (
1.3
) when
g
is given by the
strong dissipative term

g(u)=-\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}
and
super-linear multiplicative term

\sigma(u)=\xi u^{1+\gamma}
for
\beta,\gamma>0
satisfying
\gamma<{\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{d+2}}
. We also assume that the
\mathcal{L}
is a second order differential operator with random coefficients. Our purpose is to show that the strong dissipativity of
g
(denoted by
\beta
) push down the solution to the finite value, thus it allows the random force of
\sigma
(denoted by
\gamma
) to be stronger. In other words, given any large
\gamma>0
, we prove that a unique solution exists in a suitable Sobolev space (possibly embedded into a Hölder space) until an arbitrary bounded stopping time provided that
\beta>0
is sufficently large.

In a recent work
[
Sal22
]
, Salins has recently addressed a similar problem on an open bounded domain. He proved that when
g(u)\lesssim-u|u|^{1+\beta}
and
|\sigma(u)|\lesssim(1+|u|)^{1+\gamma}
, a local mild solution is indeed a global solution (see Theorem 2.7 of
[
Sal22
]
) if
\gamma<\frac{(1-\eta)\beta}{2}
, where
\eta<1
is given in (2.9) of
[
Sal22
]
. His proof idea hinges on the estimate of stochasic convolutions with nicely combinating a stopping time argument, which is different from ours (see Remark
3.5
). In Remark
3.4
, we discuss the comparison of our result with Salins’.

We mention a few words on our contribution and novelty. First, employing the
L_{p}
-theory developed by Krylov
[
Kry99
]
, we obtain the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of the solution to (
1.1
) in
L_{p}
spaces. Additionally, our approach allows for random coefficients
a^{ij},b^{i},c,\bar{b}
and
\xi
(see Assumption
3.1
). Moreover, our setting considers
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as the spatial domain, instead of an open bounded domain
D
which was studied in
[
Sal22
]
. Since our proof does not rely on the series representation of the noise using the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
in
L^{2}(D)
with the Dirichlet boundary condition, our results are achieved on the whole spatial domain
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. To the best of our knowledge, our main theorem (Theorem
3.3
) is the first result that establishes the well-posedness of (
1.1
) on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, which is essentially due to the strong dissipativity. Furthermore, we obtain the Hölder regularity of the solution in time and space (see Corollary
3.7
):

(1.4)

u\in C_{t,x}^{\frac{1}{2}\left[\kappa-\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}(d+2)\right]-\varepsilon,\kappa-\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}(d+2)-\varepsilon}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

almost surely, where
\kappa
is the constant related to reinforced Dalang’s condition (see Assumption
2.3
). Our result is new in that (
1.4
) shows the regularizing effect of the strong dissipativity or a large
\beta
.

Now we pose to give proof ideas of Theorem
3.3
. Our first step is to construct a local solution
u_{m}
(see Lemma
4.3
) using the
L_{p}
-theory of SPDEs (Theorem
4.2
) whose reaction term is bounded uniformly (see Lemma
4.5
). We then introduce a auxillary process
v_{m}
, a solution to a SPDE related to the nonlinear noise part of
u_{m}
without the reaction term, which dominates
u_{m}
, i.e.,
u_{m}\leq v_{m}
(see Lemmas
4.6
and
4.7
). We show that
v_{m}
(hence
u_{m}
) is controlled by the reaction term due to the strong dissipativity condition
\gamma<\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{d+2}
(see the choice of
p
and
p_{0}
in the proof of Lemma
4.8
). We finally obtain a uniform bound on
u_{m}
in Lemma
4.9
, which is the essential part of the proof of Theorem
3.3
(see Section
4.3
).

Our paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we collect some preliminiaries for SPDEs with colored noise. Section
3
presents the main result (Theorem
3.3
). Section
4
is devoted to proving Theorem
3.3
.

We finish the introduction with some notations and conventions. Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}
denote the set of natural numbers and real numbers, respectively. We write
:=
to denote definition. For a real-valued function
g
, we set

g_{+}:=\frac{g+|g|}{2},\quad g_{-}=-\frac{g-|g|}{2}

For a normed space
F
, a measure space
(X,\mathcal{M},\mu)
, and
p\in[1,\infty)
, a space
L_{p}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)
is a set of
F
-valued
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}
-measurable function such that

(1.5)

\|u\|_{L_{p}(X,\mathcal{M},\mu;F)}:=\left(\int_{X}\|u(x)\|_{F}^{p}\mu(dx)\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

A set
\mathcal{M}^{\mu}
is the completion of
\mathcal{M}
with respect to the measure
\mu
.
For
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
T>0
, a set
C^{\alpha}([0,T];F)
is the set of
F
-valued continuous functions

such that

|u|_{C^{\alpha}([0,T];F)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|u(t)|_{F}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,t\in[0,T],\\
s\neq t\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(t)-u(s)|_{F}}{|t-s|^{\alpha}}<\infty.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, set

a\wedge b:=\min\{a,b\}
,
a\vee b:=\max\{a,b\}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the set of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The Einstein’s summation convention with respect to
i,j
, and
k
is assumed. A generic constant
N
can be different from line to line. We denote by
N=N(a,b,\ldots)
or
N=N_{a,b,\ldots}
that
N
depends only on
a,b,\ldots
. For functions depending on
\omega
,
t
, and
x
, the argument
\omega\in\Omega
is usually omitted.

2.
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to introducing preliminaries related to (
1.1
). We first review the definition of the spatially homogeneous colored noise
\dot{F}
and the reinforced Dalang’s condition on
\dot{F}
which implies the existence, uniqueness and the Hölder continuity of the solution to (
1.1
) (see
[
SSS02
,
FSS06
]
). We then introduced the definitions and properties of stochastic Banach spaces
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{n+2}(\tau)
and
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{n+2}(\tau)
, where in particular the latter is our solution space (see
[
Kry99
]
).

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
be a complete probability space equipped with a filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The filtration
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is assumed to satisfy the usual conditions. A set
\mathcal{P}
denotes the predictable
\sigma
-field related to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
. First, we provide definitions and assumptions for the noise
\dot{F}
.

Definition 2.1
.

\{F(\phi):\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
is a mean-zero Gaussian process with covariance functional given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\phi)F(\psi)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(dx)\,(\phi(t,\cdot)*\tilde{\psi}(t,\cdot))(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mu(d\xi)\,\hat{\phi}(t,\xi)\,\overline{\hat{\psi}(t,\xi)},

where
\tilde{\psi}(t,x):=\psi(t,-x)
,
\hat{\phi}(t,\xi):=\mathcal{F}(\phi(t,\cdot))(\xi)
is the Fourier transform of
\phi
with respect to
x
,
f
is a nonnegative, and nonnegative definite tempered measure, and
\mu
is the Fourier transform of
f
introduced in (
1.2
). We say that
f
is a correlation function or measure.

Remark 2.2
.

By the framework of Dalang-Walsh theory (see
[
Wal86
,
Dal99
]
), the
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
-indexed Gaussian process
F
can be extended to an
L^{2}(\Omega)
-valued martingale measure
F(ds,dx)
. Additionally, it should be remarked that the integral with
L^{2}(\Omega)
-valued martingale measure
F(ds,dx)
can also be written as an infinite summation of Itô stochastic integral, which we use throughout the paper. For example, if a predictable process
X(t,\cdot)
is given as

X(t,x)=\zeta(x)1_{\text{\,\raise 0.60275pt\hbox{${\scriptstyle|}$}\kern-2.87111pt$($}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\text{$]$\kern-1.26662pt$]$}}(t),

where
\tau_{1}
,
\tau_{2}
are bounded stopping times,
\text{\,\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{${\scriptstyle|}$}\kern-3.4755pt$($}\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\text{$]$\kern-1.53325pt$]$}:=\{(\omega,t):\tau_{1}(\omega)<t\leq\tau_{2}(\omega)\}
, and
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, then we have

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}X(s,x)F(ds,dx)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(f\ast e_{k})(x)X(s,x)dxdw^{k}_{s},

where
\{w_{t}^{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a collection of one-dimensional independent Wiener processes and
\{e_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
is a complete orthonormal basis of a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
induced by
f
; see
[
FSS06
,
CH21
]
. The construction and properties of
\mathcal{H}
and
\{e_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\}
are described in
[
CH21
, Remark 2.6]
.

We assume the reinforced Dalang’s condition for
f
. This assumption is required to obtain the solvability of the stochastic partial differential equation driven by spatially homogeneous colored noise (e.g., see
[
FSS06
, Remark 4]
).

Assumption 2.3
.

The correlation function
f
satisfies the following: For some
\kappa\in(0,1]
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\int_{|x|<1}|x|^{2-2\kappa-d}\,f(dx)<+\infty\quad&\text{if}\quad 0<1-\kappa<\frac{d}{2},\\
\int_{|x|<1}\log\left(\frac{1}{|x|}\right)\,f(dx)<+\infty\quad&\text{if}\quad 1-\kappa=d/2,\\
\text{no conditions on }f&\text{if}\quad 1-\kappa>d/2.\end{cases}

Example 2.4

(Examples of noise)
.

Assumption
2.3
covers a large class of correlation functions. For example,
f(dx)=\delta_{0}(dx)
(space-time white noise) satisfies (
2.2
) for
d=1
and
\kappa\in(0,1/2)
. One may consider the Riesz type kernel
f(dx)=|x|^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,2(1-\kappa)\wedge d)
for
\kappa\in(0,1)
. Moreover, the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck type kernel
f(x)=\exp(-|x|^{\beta})
with
\beta\in(0,2]
and the Brownian motion
f(x)\equiv 1
also satisfy Assumption
2.3
with any
\kappa\in(0,1)
.

Remark 2.5
.

The condition on
f
given in Assumption
2.3
is equivalent to

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\mu(d\xi)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1-\kappa}}<+\infty\quad\text{for some $\kappa\in(0,1]$,}

where
\mu
is the spectral measure of
f
defined in (
1.2
) (see
[
SSS00
, Proposition 5.3]
). The reinforced Dalang’s condition guarantees the existence, uniqueness, and the Hölder continuity of the solution when
g
and
\sigma
in (
1.3
) are globally Lipschitz (see
[
FSS06
, Theorem 6]
and
[
CH21
, Theorem 3.5]
).

Definition 2.6

(Bessel potential space)
.

Let
p>1
and
n\in\mathbb{R}
. The space
H_{p}^{n}=H_{p}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of all tempered distributions
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying

\|u\|_{H_{p}^{n}}:=\left\|(1-\Delta)^{n/2}u\right\|_{L_{p}}=\left\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{n/2}\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)\right]\right\|_{L_{p}}<\infty.

Similarly,
H_{p}^{n}(\ell_{2})=H_{p}^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\ell_{2})
is a space of
\ell_{2}
-valued functions
g=(g^{1},g^{2},\cdots)
satisfying

\|g\|_{H_{p}^{n}(\ell_{2})}:=\left\|\left|\left(1-\Delta\right)^{n/2}g\right|_{l_{2}}\right\|_{L_{p}}=\left\|\left|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\left(1+|\xi|^{2}\right)^{n/2}\mathcal{F}(g)(\xi)\right]\right|_{\ell_{2}}\right\|_{L_{p}}<\infty.

For
n=0
, we set
L_{p}:=H_{p}^{0}
and
L_{p}(\ell_{2}):=H_{p}^{0}(\ell_{2})
.

Remark 2.7
.

It is well-known that for
n\in(0,\infty)
and
u\in\mathcal{S}
, we have

(1-\Delta)^{-n/2}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}R_{n}(x-y)u(y)dy,

where

|R_{n}(x)|\leq N(n,d)\left(e^{-|x|/2}1_{|x|\geq 2}+A_{n}(x)1_{|x|<2}\right)

and

\displaystyle A_{n}(x):=\begin{cases}|x|^{n-d}+1+O(|x|^{n-d+2})\quad&\mbox{if}\quad 0<n<d,\\
\log(2/|x|)+1+O(|x|^{2})\quad&\mbox{if}\quad n=d,\\
1+O(|x|^{n-d})\quad&\mbox{if}\quad n>d.\end{cases}

Additionally, notice that

(R_{n}\ast R_{n})(x)=R_{2n}(x).

For more information, see
[
Gra09
, Proposition 1.2.5.]
.

.

(ii)

Definition 2.12

Let

and

in

for each

. We omit

(iv)

If

3.
Main results

In this section, we present the assumptions on coefficients and the main results of this paper. Assumption
3.1
includes conditions on the measurability, ellipticity, and boundedness of the coefficients of (
1.1
). Theorem
3.3
establishes the existence, uniqueness, and
L_{p}
regularity of the solution to (
1.1
). Furthermore, the Hölder regularity of the solution is obtained using the Hölder embedding theorem for
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)
. Theorem
3.6
presents the inclusion relation of the solution spaces
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)\subset\mathcal{H}_{q}^{\kappa}(\tau)
when
p<q
and initial data satisfy additional conditions on summability. Finally, we combine Theorems
3.3
and
3.6
to derive the maximal Hölder regularity of the solution, which is given in Corollary
3.7
.

Assumption 3.1
.

(i)

The coefficients
a=a(t,x)
,
b=b(t,x)
,
c=c(t,x)
,
\bar{b}=\bar{b}(t,x)
, and
\xi=\xi(t,x)
are
\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-measurable.

(ii)

There exists
K>0
such that

(3.1)

K^{-1}|\eta|^{2}\leq a^{ij}(t,x)\eta^{i}\eta^{j}\leq K|\eta|^{2}\quad\forall(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta=(\eta^{1},\dots,\eta^{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.2)

\bar{b}(t,x)\geq K^{-1}\quad\forall(\omega,t,x)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

and

(3.3)

\sum_{i,j}\left|a^{ij}(t,\cdot)\right|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\sum_{i}\left|b^{i}(t,\cdot)\right|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|c(t,\cdot)|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\bar{b}(t,\cdot)|_{C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+|\xi(t,\cdot)|_{L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq K

for all
(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,\infty)
.

Remark 3.2
.

In order to establish the global existence of a solution to (
1.1
), we need to have a uniform bound of local solutions. Therefore, we first obtain a uniform
L_{p}
bound of local solutions. The lower bound of
\bar{b}
is crucial in obtaining this uniform bound (see Lemma
4.5
and (
4.17
)). Intuitively, the lower bound of
\bar{b}
prevents the dissipative term
u^{1+\beta}
from vanishing, which in turn allows for the long time existence of a solution.

Below we provide the main result of our paper.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose Assumptions
2.3
and
3.1
hold. Let
\beta,\gamma>0
and
p\geq 2
satisfy

(3.4)

\gamma<\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{d+2}\quad\text{and}\quad p\in\left(\frac{d+2}{\kappa},\frac{1+\beta}{\gamma}\right),

where
\kappa
is the constant introduced in Assumption
2.3
. Then, for nonnegative initial data
u_{0}\in L_{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{F}_{0};L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap U_{p}^{\kappa}
, (
1.1
) has a unique nonnegative solution
u\in\mathcal{H}_{p,loc}^{\kappa}
. Furthermore, for any bounded stopping time
\tau\leq T
and
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
satisfying

(3.5)

\frac{1}{p}<\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\frac{1}{2}\left(\kappa-\frac{d}{p}\right),

we have
u\in C^{\alpha_{1}-1/p}([0,\tau];C^{\kappa-2\alpha_{2}-d/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
almost surely and

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}|u|^{p}_{C^{\alpha_{1}-1/p}([0,\tau];C^{\kappa-2\alpha_{2}-d/p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq N(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\kappa,d,p,T)\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}(\tau)}^{p}.

Remark 3.4

(Comparison with Salins’ result
[
Sal22
]
)
.

[
Sal22
, Theorem 2.7]
identifies a condition that the solutions of (
1.3
) with
g(u)\text{sign}(u)\lesssim|u|^{1+\beta}
and
\sigma(u)\lesssim 1+|u|^{1+\gamma}
on a bounded domain
D
never explode when
\gamma<\frac{(1-\eta)\beta}{2}
where
\eta<1
is some constant related to the correlation function
f
(see
[
Sal22
, Assumption 2.2]
). Since
\eta
is associated with the eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}(D)
, it is difficult to compare the range of (
\beta,\gamma
) with ours (
\gamma<\frac{\kappa(1+\beta)}{d+2}
). The only case that can be identified is the (
1+1
)-dimensional space-time white noise (
f=\delta_{0}
) where
\gamma<\frac{\beta}{4}
for
[
Sal22
]
and
\gamma<\frac{(1+\beta)}{6}
for Theorem
3.3
.

Remark 3.5
.

Note that choice of
(\beta,\gamma)
in (
3.4
) ensures that there exists
p\geq 2
such that
p\in\left(\frac{d+2}{\kappa},\frac{1+\beta}{\gamma}\right)
. We briefly explain why this range of
p
in (
3.4
) is necessary for the proof of Theorem
3.3
with a sketch of the proof in the case of the one-dimensional SPDE with the space-time white noise and simple coefficients:

(3.7)

du=u_{xx}-u^{1+\beta}+u^{1+\gamma}e_{k}dw_{t}^{k},\quad t\in(0,T],\,\,x\in\mathbb{R};\quad u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

where
T<\infty
,
\{e_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is the orthonormal
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
basis, and
\{w_{t}^{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a set of one-dimensional independent Wiener process. In order to obtain the global existence of a solution to (
3.7
), we need to have a uniform bound of a nonnegative local solutions
u_{m}
. For this purpose, we separate
u_{m}
into two parts: the noise-dominant part
v_{m}
and the dissipative-dominant part
u_{m}-v_{m}
. Let
v_{m}
satisfy the following SPDE

(3.8)

dv_{m}=v_{mxx}+u_{m}^{1+\gamma}e_{k}dw_{t}^{k},\quad t\in(0,T],\,\,x\in\mathbb{R};\quad v_{m}(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

Note that the stochastic part of (
3.8
) is
u_{m}^{1+\gamma}
, not
v_{m}^{1+\gamma}
. Then, using Duhamel’s principle we can deduce that
u_{m}\leq v_{m}
since
u_{m}-v_{m}
satisfies

u_{m}(t,x)-v_{m}(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-s,x-y)u_{m}^{1+\beta}(s,y)dyds\leq 0,

where
p(t,x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{1/2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4t}|x-y|^{2}}
denotes the heat kernel. Consequently, to control
\sup_{t,x}u_{m}(t,x)
, it suffices to control
\sup_{t,x}v_{m}(t,x)
. Additionally, since
v\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)
, Corollary
2.14
yields that
{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t,x}|v_{m}(t,x)|^{p}
is controlled by
\|v_{m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}
. Thus, we will control
\|v_{m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}
in what follows. As
v_{m}
is a solution to (
3.8
), we get
\|v_{m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}\lesssim\|u_{m}\|^{p(1+\gamma)}_{\mathbb{L}_{p(1+\gamma)}}
(to simplify, we dropped the subscript for the stopping time). On the other hand, we have a uniform bound
\|u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{1+\beta}}^{1+\beta}\lesssim 1
with the help of the conservation law (see Lemma
4.5
). Therefore, if
p^{\prime}:=p(1+\gamma)-(1+\beta)<p
, then we can use Jensen’s inequality and the fact
u_{m}\leq v_{m}
to have

\|v_{m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}\lesssim\|u_{m}\|^{p(1+\gamma)}_{\mathbb{L}_{p(1+\gamma)}}\lesssim\|u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{1+\beta}}^{1+\beta}\cdot\mathbb{E}\sup_{t,x}|u_{m}|^{p^{\prime}}\lesssim\left(\mathbb{E}\sup_{t,x}|v_{m}|^{p}\right)^{p^{\prime}/p}\lesssim\|v_{m}\|^{p^{\prime}/p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}.

We now use Young’s inequality to get a uniform bound of
\|v_{m}\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{p}}
until a suitable stopping time (see Lemma
4.8
). Thus,
p(1+\gamma)-(1+\beta)<p
, (equivalent to
p<\frac{1+\beta}{\gamma}
in (
3.4
)), is required. The lower bound
p>\frac{d+2}{\kappa}
is needed to choose
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
satisfying (
3.5
) so that the Hölder exponents in (
3.6
) are positive.

By optimizing the choice of
(p,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
in (
3.5
), we can obtain the maximal Hölder regularity of the solution. However, it should be noted that the solution
u=u^{(p)}
constructed in Theorem
3.3
depends on
p
. To obtain the maximal Hölder regularity, we let
p=p_{\varepsilon}
depend on a parameter
\varepsilon>0
, which goes to
\frac{1+\beta}{\gamma}
as
\varepsilon\to 0,
and all the conditions of Theorem
3.3
hold for all
p_{\varepsilon}
. To this end, we need to verify that
u^{(p_{\varepsilon})}
for any
\varepsilon>0
coincides with each other, and
u=u^{(p_{\varepsilon})}
is indeed a unique solution in
\cap_{\varepsilon>0}\mathcal{H}_{p_{\varepsilon},loc}^{\kappa}
. Thus, we need the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\beta,\gamma,p
and
u_{0}
satisfy the conditions in Theorem

and

be the solution to (

). Then, if

4.
Proof of Theorem
3.3

This section devoted to proving Theorem
3.3
. The main challenge is to construct a global solution to (
1.1
) as the the uniqueness and regularity of the global solution follow from those of local solutions. In Section
4.1
, we establish the existence, uniqueness, and regularity of local solutions
u_{m}
on some time interval
[0,\tau_{m}]
. The properties of local solutions follow from the
L_{p}
theory for semilinear SPDEs, as described in
[
Kry99
, Theorem 5.1]
. In order to construct a global solution, we need a uniform bound of local solutions
u_{m}
. In Section
4.2
, we decompose the local solution
u_{m}
into two components: the noise-dominant part
v_{m}
and the dissipative-dominant part
u_{m}-v_{m}
. The properties of noise-dominant part
v_{m}
are suggested in Lemma
4.6
. As illustrated in Remark
3.5
, we show that the local solution
u_{m}
is bounded by noise-dominant part
v_{m}
; i.e., the dissipativity-dominant part
u_{m}-v_{m}
is non-positive (see Lemma
4.7
). Then,
\sup_{t,x}|u_{m}(t,x)|
is bounded by
\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)}
because
\sup_{t,x}|v_{m}(t,x)|
is bounded by
\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)}
(see Lemma
4.6
). Additionally, observe that
\|v_{m}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau)}
is controlled by
\|u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{p(1+\gamma)}(\tau)}
(see (
4.37
)) and there is a uniform bound of
\|u_{m}\|_{\mathbb{L}_{1+\beta}(\tau)}
by Itô’s formula (see Lemma
4.5
). In conclusion, the noise-dominant part
v_{m}
can be controlled by the dissipativity term (see Lemma
4.8
). With this bound, we prove that the candidate of a global solution (
u(t,\cdot)=u_{m}(t,\cdot)
for
t\in[0,\tau_{m}]
) does not explode in finite time (see Lemma
4.9
and Section
4.3
). In Section
4.4
we prove Theorem
3.6
.

4.1.
Existence, uniqueness, and regularity of local solutions

Let
\tau\leq T
be a bounded stopping time and

. We review the

-solvability of the SPDE

(4.1)

where
Lu(t,x):=a^{ij}(t,x)u_{x^{i}x^{j}}(t,x)+b^{i}(t,x)u_{x^{i}}(t,x)+c(t,x)u(t,x)
and
w_{t}^{k}
is a one-dimensional Brownian motion. We present some assumptions on
g
and
\sigma
.

Assumption 4.1

(
\tau
)
.

(i)

The functions
g(t,x,u)
and
\sigma^{k}(t,x,u)
are
\mathcal{P}\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
-measurable satisfying

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.3

We are ready to prove Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

(Step 1). (Uniqueness).

Suppose
u,\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p,loc}^{\kappa}
are nonnegative solutions of (
1.1
). By Definition
2.12
(
iii
), there are bounded stopping times
\tau_{n}
,

such that

\tau_{n}\uparrow\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad u,\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau_{n}).

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
p>\frac{3}{\kappa}
, by Corollary
2.14
, we have
u,\bar{u}\in C([0,\tau_{n}];C(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
(a.s.) and

(4.40)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau_{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{p}+\mathbb{E}\sup_{t\leq\tau_{n}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bar{u}(t,x)|^{p}<\infty.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\begin{gathered}\tau_{m,n}^{1}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|>m\right\}\wedge\tau_{n},\quad\tau_{m,n}^{2}:=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\bar{u}(t,x)|>m\right\}\wedge\tau_{n},\end{gathered}

and

(4.41)

\tau_{m,n}:=\tau_{m,n}^{1}\wedge\tau_{m,n}^{2}.

Due to (
4.40
),
\tau_{m,n}^{1}
and
\tau_{m,n}^{2}
are well-defined stopping times, and thus
\tau_{m,n}
is a stopping time.
Observe that
u,\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau_{m,n})
and
\tau_{m,n}\uparrow\tau_{n}
as
m\to\infty
almost surely. Fix
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Notice that both
u,\bar{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau_{m,n})
are solutions to

dv=\left(Lv-\bar{b}v_{+}^{1+\beta}h_{m}(v)\right)\,dt+\xi v_{+}^{1+\gamma}{h_{m}(v)}(f\ast e_{k})dw^{k}_{t},\quad 0<t\leq\tau_{m,n};\quad v(0,\cdot)=u_{0},

where

By the uniqueness result in Lemma

, we conclude that
u=\bar{u}
in
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau_{m,n})
for each

. The monotone convergence theorem yields
u=\bar{u}
in
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{\kappa}(\tau_{n})
. Since

is arbitrary, this implies
u=\bar{u}
in
\mathcal{H}_{p,loc}^{\kappa}
.

(Step 2). (Existence).
The idea of the proof folllows from
[

4.3
, there exists a nonnegative solution

\tau_{R}^{R}=\tau_{m}^{R}\leq\tau_{m}^{m}.

for

and
u_{R}

du=\left(Lu-\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}h_{R}(u)\right)dt+\xi u^{1+\gamma}h_{R}(u)dw_{t}^{k},\quad 0<t\leq\tau_{m}^{R}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{l}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{p}|u(t,x)|^{q-p}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sup_{t\leq\tau_{l}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|\right)^{q-p}\int_{0}^{\tau_{l}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{L_{p}}dt

\displaystyle\leq l^{q-p}\int_{0}^{\tau_{l}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{p}_{H_{p}^{\kappa}}dt<\infty\quad\mbox{(a.s.)}.

Therefore, we can define a bounded stopping time

\tau_{l,l_{0}}:=\tau_{l}\wedge\inf\left\{t>0:\int_{0}^{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{q}_{L_{q}}dt>l_{0}\right\}

such that
\tau_{l,l_{0}}\uparrow\tau_{l}
as
l_{0}\to\infty
and
u\in\mathbb{L}_{q}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
for each
l_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, it suffices to show that
u\in\mathcal{H}_{q}^{\kappa}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
to show
u\in\mathcal{H}_{q,loc}^{\kappa}
.

Observe that a similar computation as in (
4.10
) with
v=0
implies

(4.44)

\displaystyle\left\|\xi u^{1+\gamma}(f\ast\bm{e})\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\kappa-1}_{q}(\tau_{l,l_{0}},\ell_{2})}^{q}\leq N\int_{0}^{\tau_{l,l_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{q(1+\gamma)}dxdt\leq Nl^{q\gamma}\int_{0}^{\tau_{l,l_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{q}dxdt.

Moreover, notice that

(4.45)

\left\|\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{\kappa-2}_{q}(\tau_{l,l_{0}},\ell_{2})}^{q}\leq N\int_{0}^{\tau_{l,l_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{q(1+\beta)}dxdt\leq Nl^{q\beta}\int_{0}^{\tau_{l,l_{0}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{q}dxdt.

Notice that (
4.44
) and (
4.45
) imply
\xi u^{1+\gamma}(f\ast\bm{e})\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{\kappa-1}(\tau_{l,l_{0}},\ell_{2})\subset\mathbb{H}_{q}^{-1}(\tau_{l,l_{0}},\ell_{2})
and
\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{\kappa-2}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})\subset\mathbb{H}_{q}^{-2}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
, respectively. Thus,
u\in\mathcal{H}_{q}^{0}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
since
u_{0}\in U_{q}^{\kappa}\subset U_{q}^{0}
,
a^{ij}u_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}u_{x^{i}}+cu\in\mathbb{H}_{q}^{\kappa-2}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})\subset\mathbb{H}_{q}^{-2}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
. Therefore, Theorem
4.2
yields that there exists a unique solution
\hat{u}\in\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{q}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
to the SPDE

d\hat{u}=\left(a^{ij}\hat{u}_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}\hat{u}_{x^{i}}+c\hat{u}-\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}\right)\,dt+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\xi u^{1+\gamma}(f\ast e_{k})dw^{k}_{t},\quad 0<t\leq\tau_{l,l_{0}};\quad\hat{u}(0,\cdot)=u_{0}.

It should be remarked that the nonlinear terms of the above equation are
-\bar{b}u^{1+\beta}
and
\xi u^{1+\lambda}(f\ast e_{k})
, not
\bar{b}\hat{u}^{1+\beta}
and
\xi\hat{u}^{1+\lambda}(f\ast e_{k})
. Since
\varphi:=u-\hat{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{q}^{0}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
satisfies the deterministic PDE

d\varphi=\left(a^{ij}\varphi_{x^{i}x^{j}}+b^{i}\varphi_{x^{i}}+c\varphi\right)\,dt,\quad 0<t\leq\tau_{l,l_{0}};\quad\varphi(0,\cdot)=0,

the deterministic version of Theorem
4.2
(see
[
LSU88
]
) implies
u=\hat{u}
almost all
(\omega,t,x)
. Therefore,
u\in\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}_{q}(\tau_{l,l_{0}})
. This proves the theorem.

∎

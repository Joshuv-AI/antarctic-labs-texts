ℤ / r ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z} -equivariant covers of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} with moving ramification
Carl Lian, Riccardo Moschetti

Abstract
. Let X → ℙ 1 X\to\mathbb{P}^{1} be a general cyclic cover. We give a simple formula for the number of equivariant meromorphic functions on X X subject to ramification conditions at variable points. This generalizes and gives a new proof of a recent result of the second author and Pirola on hyperelliptic odd covers .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a moduli space of branched covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, and let
\overline{\mathcal{H}}
be its compactification by Harris-Mumford admissible covers, see
[
HM82
]
. Let
\phi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the map remembering the source of a branched cover, possibly with additional marked points (e.g. ramification points).

Pushing forward the fundamental class by
\phi
yields a cycle class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, which, by a theorem of Faber-Pandharipande, lies in the tautological ring, see
[
FP05
]
. While an algorithm is given to compute this class, it is in general quite impractical to implement, and few explicit formulas are known.

In the case where
\dim(\mathcal{H})=\dim(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
, so that
\phi
is generically finite, computing this cycle class amounts to computing the degree of
\phi
, for which formulas were given (assuming the ramification points lie in distinct fibers) in
[
L19
]
using limit linear series and Schubert calculus techniques. By definition, these degrees count maps
h:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
out of a fixed curve with ramification conditions imposed at possibly moving (variable) points.

A variant of this problem has been studied in
[
MP20
]
, in which
X
is assumed to be hyperelliptic and
h
is required to be equivariant for the hyperelliptic action. More precisely, it is proven that:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
MP20
]

be a general hyperelliptic curve of genus
g
with Weierstraß points

2.
Setup

In this section, we introduce the various moduli spaces and the relations between them. We work over
\mathbb{C}

2.1.
Cyclic covers

Let
X
be a smooth, projective, and connected curve of genus
g
, and let
\sigma:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a cyclic cover of degree
r
, ramified over
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Let
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in(\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z})^{m}
be the tuple of monodromy elements over the
Q_{i}
, and let
\mathcal{H}_{g,\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z},\xi}
be the moduli space of such covers, which is non-empty and smooth of dimension
m-3
as long as
\sum_{i}\xi_{i}=0
in
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
and
\gcd(\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m},r\})=1
, see
[
SvZ18
, Theorem 3.7 and Proposition 3.20]
. We will assume throughout that

, but the results still hold in the case

, with minor modifications. Taking
\xi_{i}\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,r-1\}
, we may express

birationally as the curve given by the equation
y^{r}=(x-\lambda_{1})^{\xi_{1}}\cdots(x-\lambda_{m})^{\xi_{m}}
, and
\sigma
as the projection to the

For each

, let

, and denote by

under
\sigma
. Let

be the common ramification index of the

. By Riemann-Hurwitz, we have

(1)

2g-2=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(e_{i}-1)-2r=r(m-2)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}.

We remark that the hyperelliptic locus
\mathcal{H}_{g}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
is the image of the map
\mathcal{H}_{g,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},(1^{2g+2})}\to\mathcal{M}_{g}
remembering the source of the cover, see
[
SvZ18
, §4.4]
.

2.3.
Congruence condition on
a_{i}

In this section, we show that in order for equivariant covers
h
to exist, the vanishing orders
a_{i}
need to satisfy a certain congruence condition. We give two perspectives, one algebraic and one topological.

Consider the map
h^{\prime}
. The fiber over 0 consists of the points
Q_{i}
previously defined for
i\in S_{0}
, with ramification index
a_{i}
, and
t_{0}/r
points of ramification index
r
each. Similarly, the fiber over
\infty
consists of
Q_{i}
for
i\in S_{\infty}
with ramification index
-a_{i}
and
t_{\infty}/r
points of ramification index
r
each. Moreover, it is ramified at
k
additional (unlabelled) points with ramification index
b_{j}
.

We may therefore represent the map
h^{\prime}
by the meromorphic function

h^{\prime}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}(x)}{P_{\infty}(x)}\right)^{r}

where
P_{0},P_{\infty}
are non-zero polynomials of degree
t_{0}/r,t_{\infty}/r
, respectively. We also allow one of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
to have strictly smaller degree, which occurs when
h^{\prime}
has a pole or zero at
x=\infty
.

Lemma 2.1
.

h^{\prime}
lifts to a
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant cover
h:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
if and only if
a_{i}\equiv\xi_{i}\pmod{r}
for all
i
. If this is the case, the lift is unique, up to composition with the
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-action on the target.

Proof.

In order to lift such an

, the meromorphic function
h
must send a general point
(x,y)
on
X
to an
r
-th root of
h^{\prime}(x)
, that is, such an
r
-th root must exist in the function field
\mathbb{C}(X)=\mathbb{C}(x)[\sqrt[r]{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{\xi_{i}}}]
of
X
. The field extension
\mathbb{C}(X)/\mathbb{C}(x)
is Galois with Galois group
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
, corresponding to the cyclic cover
\sigma
. Write
f=(x-\lambda_{i})^{\xi_{i}}
and let
\tau\in G
be a generator such that

\tau(\sqrt[r]{f})=\omega\cdot\sqrt[r]{f},

for some primitive root of unity
\omega\in\mathbb{C}
, so that

\tau((\sqrt[r]{f})^{a})=\omega^{a}\cdot(\sqrt[r]{f})^{a},

for
a=0,1,\ldots,r-1
.

Suppose now that
h^{\prime}(x)
has an
r
-th root
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}(X)
, so that
\alpha^{r}\in\mathbb{C}(x)
. Then,
\tau(\alpha)^{r}=\tau(\alpha^{r})\in\mathbb{C}(x)
, so
\tau(\alpha)^{r}=\alpha^{r}
. Thus,
\tau(\alpha)=\omega\cdot\alpha
for some
r
-th root of unity
\omega
. This immediately implies that

is of the form
f_{0}\cdot(\sqrt[r]{f})^{a}
for some

. In particular, modulo

of exponents appearing in

Now, suppose that

. Then, up to multiplication by an

h(x,y)=y^{a}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{(a_{i}-a\xi_{i})/r}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}(x)}{P_{\infty}(x)}\right)

.
∎

We may also see the necessary condition

2.4.
Reduction to
h^{\prime}

The main idea which allows us to count the number of
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-equivariant covers

, is that we can count maps
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that
h^{\prime}\circ\sigma=g\circ h
instead. In this section we formulate this statement in terms of moduli spaces.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
be the moduli space of covers
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with ramification profiles dictated by
h
upon passing to the scheme-theoretic
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-quotients. That is, the zeroes and poles of
h^{\prime}
are given by the
\lambda_{i}
and the roots of

and have orders exactly
|a_{i}|,r
, respectively, and

is branched over
k

.

The source
\mathbb{P}^{1}
has a fixed choice of coordinate

, whereas the target
\mathbb{P}^{1}
has fixed points

, but the functions
h^{\prime}
are taken only up to a constant factor. That is, two covers are considered isomorphic if they agree up to scaling on the target.

Now, we have a quasi-finite and étale morphism
\delta_{\rat}:\mathcal{H}_{\rat}\to\mathcal{P}_{k}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{k}
is the space of collections of
k
unordered branch points on the target, up to scaling. Thus,
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
is smooth of dimension

.

On the other hand, we have a morphism
\phi_{\rat}:\mathcal{H}_{\rat}\to(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{m-3}
remembering the
\lambda_{i}
for

. The source and target have the same dimension, and by generic smoothness, the general fiber of
\phi_{\rat}
consists of a finite number of reduced points equal to the degree of
\phi_{\rat}
. The above discussion shows that
\deg(\phi_{\rat})=\deg(\phi_{B})
.

by (
5
). Then, in the notation of §
2.3
, we must have simply
h^{\prime}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}
, and indeed Theorem
2.3
gives
\deg(\phi_{B})=1
.

2.6.
Theta characteristics

We comment here on one additional aspect of the case
r=2
which plays an important role in the computation of
[
MP20
]
, namely that of the theta characteristic associated with a hyperelliptic odd cover, and how the situation changes in our more general setting.

Recall that if
h:X\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is any cover for which all ramification indices are odd, one may naturally associate to
h
a theta characteristic on
X
, that is, a square root of the canonical bundle (see
[
ACGH85
, Appendix B]
and
[
Mum71
]
for the basic theory of theta characteristics). Namely, we have

\omega_{X}\cong h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-2)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2R).

where
2R
denotes the ramification divisor of
h
, which by assumption is a square. Thus, the divisor

\Theta_{h}:=h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(R)

is a theta characteristic on
X
.

Now, suppose
X
is hyperelliptic and
h
is equivariant for the hyperelliptic action, that is,
h
is a point of
\mathcal{H}_{g,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},(1^{2g+2}),B}
. Assume further that the
|a_{i}|
are all odd and that all
b_{j}
are equal to 3. This situation generalizes that of
[
MP20
]
in that Weierstraß points are allowed over 0.

In this case, we can write

\displaystyle h^{-1}(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S_{0}}(2n_{i}+1)P_{i}+\frac{t_{0}}{2}H,

\displaystyle h^{-1}(\infty)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in S_{\infty}}(2n_{i}+1)P_{i}+\frac{t_{\infty}}{2}H

where
H
is the hyperelliptic divisor on
X
.

If we denote by
R^{\prime}
the ramification divisor outside
h^{-1}(0)
and
h^{-1}(\infty)
, we have
\deg(R^{\prime})=4k
, where as before,
k
is the number of
\mathbb{Z}/r\mathbb{Z}
-orbits of branch points of outside
0
and
\infty
. In particular,
R^{\prime}=2kH
. Thus, we find, up to linear equivalence,

\displaystyle\Theta_{h}

\displaystyle=kH+\sum_{i\in S_{0},S_{\infty}}n_{i}P_{i}-\sum_{i\in S_{0}}(2n_{i}+1)P_{i}-\frac{t_{0}}{2}H

\displaystyle=\left(k-\frac{t_{0}}{2}\right)H+\sum_{i\in S_{\infty}}n_{i}P_{i}-\sum_{i\in S_{0}}(n_{i}+1)P_{i}.

In the covers of
[
MP20
]
, we have
k=2g
,
t_{0}=4g
,
t_{\infty}=0
, and
S_{0}=\emptyset
, so in fact
\Theta_{h}
is
effective
. In our more general situation in which
S_{0}
may be non-empty, note that even if we retain the conditions
(t_{0},t_{\infty})=(4g,0)
and
k=2g
, any (possibly non-effective) theta characteristic of
X
arises as above. Indeed, if

\Theta=\sum_{i=1}^{2g+2}c_{i}P_{i},

then we may take
S_{\infty}
to be the set of
i
such that
c_{i}\geq 0
, and
n_{i}=c_{i}
for
i\in S_{\infty}
and
n_{i}=-c_{i}-1
for
i\in S_{0}
.

3.
Counting meromorphic functions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}

In this section, we carry out the proof of Theorem
2.3
. We have reduced to counting the possible meromorphic functions

h^{\prime}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}(x)}{P_{\infty}(x)}\right)^{r},

where we impose the condition that, away from the
\lambda_{i}
and the roots of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
, the cover
h^{\prime}
has ramification indices (at distinct branch points) given by the multi-set
B
. We parametrize choices of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
by a product of projective spaces, and impose the ramification conditions from
B
in terms of a combination of Segre and Veronese embeddings.

3.1.
The map
\psi

Let
\mathbb{P}^{t_{0}/r},\mathbb{P}^{t_{\infty}/r}
be the projective spaces parametrizing non-zero polynomials
P_{0},P_{\infty}
of degrees (at most)
t_{0}/r,t_{\infty}/r
, up to scaling, and let
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{t_{0}/r}\times\mathbb{P}^{t_{\infty}/r}
be the open locus consisting of polynomials
P_{0},P_{\infty}
satisfying the following properties:

•

P_{0},P_{\infty}
have no roots in common,

•

P_{0},P_{\infty}
do not have roots at any of the
\lambda_{i}
,

•

P_{0},P_{\infty}
have no double roots.

Here, we regard
x=\infty
as a possible root of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
. Thus, the statement that
P_{0},P_{\infty}
have no roots in common includes the statement that at most one of them has zero leading coefficient, and the statement that they have no double roots includes the statement that the first two coefficients of either polynomial do not both vanish.

If
(P_{0},P_{\infty})\in U
, the ramification divisor for
h^{\prime}
away from the
\lambda_{i}
and the roots of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
is given by the roots of the polynomial

\displaystyle\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x):

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}-1}}\cdot\frac{P_{\infty}^{r+1}}{P_{0}^{r-1}}\cdot\frac{d}{dx}h^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})}{x-\lambda_{i}}\cdot P_{0}\cdot P_{\infty}+r\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})\cdot(P^{\prime}_{0}P_{\infty}-P_{0}P^{\prime}_{\infty})

The degree of
\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
is
b=\sum_{j}(b_{j}-1)
(note that the leading terms of the two summands cancel), unless
\infty
is among the additional ramification points of
h^{\prime}
, in which the ramification index at
\infty
is
b-\deg(\psi_{h^{\prime}})+1
.

Note that the definition of
\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
makes sense even for
(P_{0},P_{\infty})\notin U
, and that the roots of
\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
away from
\lambda_{i}
and the roots of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
, taken with multiplicity, still control the ramification of
h^{\prime}
. In this case,
\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
will in general have extraneous roots among
\lambda_{i}
and the roots of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
.

We have always that
\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
is not identically zero, or else
h^{\prime}
is constant, which is impossible even for
(P_{0},P_{\infty})\in U
because the
a_{i}
are not divisible by
r
. Therefore, we may make the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

We define
\psi:\mathbb{P}^{t_{0}/r}\times\mathbb{P}^{t_{\infty}/r}\to\mathbb{P}^{b}
by
\psi((P_{0},P_{\infty}))=\psi_{h^{\prime}}(x)
.

The map
\psi
is the composition of a Segre embedding followed by a linear projection whose base locus does not meet the image of
\mathbb{P}^{t_{0}/r}\times\mathbb{P}^{t_{\infty}/r}
, so its degree is equal to that of the Segre embedding, which is
\binom{t/r}{t_{0}/r}
.

3.2.
The map
\rho

We want to intersect
\psi
with the locus of polynomials that arise as a product of the form
\prod_{\ell\in L}R_{\ell}(x)^{\ell}
, where
R_{\ell}
has degree
c_{\ell}
. This is the image of the map defined below:

Definition 3.2
.

We define the map

\rho:\prod_{\ell\in L}\mathbb{P}^{c_{\ell}}\to\mathbb{P}^{b}

by sending
(R_{\ell})_{\ell\in L}\mapsto\prod_{\ell\in L}R_{\ell}^{c_{\ell}}
.

The map
\rho
is the composition of the
\ell
-Veronese embedding on the factor corresponding to
\ell\in L
, followed by a Segre embedding, followed by a linear projection. Because
\rho
has no base locus, its degree is the degree of the composition of the first two maps, which is

\prod_{\ell\in L}\ell^{c_{\ell}}\cdot\frac{(\sum_{\ell\in L}c_{\ell})!}{\prod_{\ell\in L}c_{\ell}!}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(b_{j}-1)\cdot\frac{k!}{\prod_{\ell\in L}c_{\ell}!}.

3.3.
Intersection of
\psi
and
\rho

Theorem
2.3
now follows from Bézout’s Theorem, assuming that, for
\lambda_{i}
general, the intersections of
\psi
and
\rho
correspond exactly to points in the corresponding fiber of
\mathcal{H}_{rat}
, and that this intersection is transverse. These statements are verified below, completing the proof of the main result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose the
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
are general and consider the intersection of
\psi,\rho
in
\mathbb{P}^{b}
. Then, any cover
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined by a point of intersection between
\psi,\rho
is a point of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
.

Proof.

Suppose we have
h^{\prime}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}(x)}{P_{\infty}(x)}\right)^{r}
defining a point of intersection of
\psi,\rho
outside of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
. We claim that the corresponding map
h^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, possibly of degree less than
d
, has strictly fewer than
k
branch points away from
0,\infty
, and thus varies in a family of dimension strictly less than that of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
, so is avoided by a general choice of
\lambda_{i}
. (If
k=1
, then we find that
h^{\prime}
may only have two branch points, and hence two ramification points, but we see that this is impossible when
m\geq 3
, as
r\neq a_{i}
.)

To see this, note first that if
h^{\prime}
has
k
distinct ramification points away from
0,\infty
, then the ramification indices must be given exactly by the elements of
B
. Now, we claim that any choices of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
that define a cover
h^{\prime}
outside of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
increases the total amount of ramification of
h^{\prime}
over 0 and
\infty

compared to the change in degree
, which cannot happen if the ramification away from
0,\infty
is unchanged.

On one hand, suppose that the degree of
h^{\prime}
is
d
, so that
P_{0},P_{\infty}
have no roots in common and no roots at the
\lambda_{i}
. Then, the only way
h^{\prime}
can fail to define a point of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
is if some of the ramification imposed by the
\lambda_{i}
and
P_{0},P_{\infty}
coalesces, either by allowing
P_{0},P_{\infty}
to have multiple roots or roots at
\lambda_{i}
. However, this increases the total amount of ramification of
h^{\prime}
.

On the other hand, if
P_{0},P_{\infty}
are chosen to decrease the degree of
h^{\prime}
, any such choice will decrease the degree of
h^{\prime}
strictly more than the amount of ramification lost. For example, if
P_{0},P_{\infty}
have a linear factor in common, the degree of
h^{\prime}
drops by
r
, which must decrease the total ramification by
2r
, but the amount of ramification lost over
0,\infty
is only
2r-2
. Similarly, if
P_{0}
or
P_{\infty}
has a root at
\lambda
canceling a factor of
(x-\lambda)
, we get a similar contradiction.

Therefore, if the
\lambda_{i}
are chosen generally, the ramification for all covers in the intersection of
\psi,\rho
is as expected.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose the
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
are general. Then, the intersection of
\psi,\rho
is transverse.

Proof.

Suppose
\psi,\rho
have a tangent vector. We then obtain a 1-parameter deformation

\widetilde{h^{\prime}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}+\epsilon Q_{0}}{P_{\infty}+\epsilon Q_{\infty}}\right)^{r}

of the meromorphic function
h^{\prime}
, which yields a 1-parameter deformation as a point of
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
. By construction,
\widetilde{h^{\prime}}
keeps the
\lambda_{i}
constant on the target. Because the
\lambda_{i}
are general and
\phi_{\rat}
is generically unramified,
\widetilde{h^{\prime}}
must is in fact be the trivial deformation on
\mathcal{H}_{\rat}
.

This means that, as meromorphic functions on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\Spec\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
, we have

\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}+\epsilon Q_{0}}{P_{\infty}+\epsilon Q_{\infty}}\right)^{r}=\mu\prod_{i=1}^{m}(x-\lambda_{i})^{a_{i}}\cdot\left(\frac{P_{0}}{P_{\infty}}\right)^{r}

for a scalar
\mu\in\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]/\epsilon^{2}
. We find that
Q_{0},Q_{\infty}
must be scalar multiples of
P_{0},P_{\infty}
, corresponding to the trivial tangent vector on
\psi
, and the ramification remains unchanged to first order, corresponding to the trivial tangent vector on
\rho
. This completes the proof.
∎

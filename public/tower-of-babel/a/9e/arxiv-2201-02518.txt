Skew Dyck paths with catastrophes
Helmut Prodinger

Abstract
. Skew Dyck paths are like Dyck paths, but an additional south-west step ( − 1 , − 1 ) (-1,-1) is allowed, provided that the path does not intersect itself. Lattice paths with catastrophes can drop from any level to the origin in just one step. We combine these two ideas. The analysis is strictly based on generating functions, and the kernel method is used.

2.
A warm-up: Dyck paths and catastrophes

To offer a gentle path for our readers, we analyze the paths as in Fig.
2
(which is also contained in the earlier papers
[
2
,
3
]
),
since we will approach the skew Dyck paths with catastrophes in a similar style in the next section.

We introduce generating functions
f_{i}=f_{i}(z)
, where the coefficient of
z^{n}
counts the number of paths starting at the origin (=the big circle)
and end after
n
steps at state
i
(=level
i
). The following recursions are easy to see:

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=1+z(f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}+f_{4}+\cdots),

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=zf_{i-1}+zf_{i+1},\ i\geq 1.

Since
f_{0}
is somewhat special, we leave it out for the moment and compute the other ones,
f_{i}
,
i\geq 1
.
Eventually we will solve the equation for
f_{0}
, which will turn out to be just linear.
Therefore we introduce the bivariate generating function

F(u)=F(u,z)=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}f_{i}

and we treat
f_{0}
as a parameter. Summing the recursions,

F(u)=zf_{0}+zuF(u)+\frac{z}{u}[F(u)-f_{1}],

or

F(u)=\frac{zf_{0}-\frac{z}{u}f_{1}}{1-zu-\frac{z}{u}}=\frac{zf_{1}-zuf_{0}}{zu^{2}-u+z}=\frac{zf_{1}-zuf_{0}}{z(u-r_{1})(u-r_{2})},

with

r_{1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2z},\quad r_{2}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2z}.

An essential step of the
kernel method
is that the ‘bad’ factor
(u-r_{2})
must cancel. The reciprocal of this factor does not
have a power series expansion in
(u,z)
. Dividing this factor out in both, denominator and numerator, leads to

F(u)=\frac{-zf_{0}}{z(u-r_{1})}=\frac{zf_{0}}{zr_{1}(1-u/r_{1})},

and from this

f_{i}=[u^{i-1}]F(u)=\frac{zf_{0}}{zr_{1}^{i}}=f_{0}r_{2}^{i}.

This formula holds for all
i\geq 0
. It is interesting to note that all these functions are just a multiple of the parameter
f_{0}
. Now we can go to the first recursion:

f_{0}=1+z(f_{0}r_{2}^{1}+f_{0}r_{2}^{2}+f_{0}r_{2}^{3}+\cdots)=1+zf_{0}\frac{r_{2}}{1-r_{2}}

and solve the equation,

f_{0}=\frac{2-3z-2z^{2}+z\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2(1-z-2z^{2}-z^{3})}.

It has the power series expansion

f_{0}=1+z^{2}+z^{3}+3z^{4}+5z^{5}+12z^{6}+23z^{7}+52z^{8}+105z^{9}+232z^{10}+480z^{11}+1049z^{12}+2199z^{13}+\cdots,

and the list of coefficients is sequence A224747 in the OEIS
[
6
]
. The papers
[
2
,
3
]
have this already. We can also compute the number of Dyck paths
with catastrophes and open (unspecified) end, via the generating function

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle+f_{1}+f_{2}+f_{3}+f_{4}+\dots

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z+(1+z)\sqrt{1-4z^{2}}}{2(1-z-2z^{2}-z^{3})}

\displaystyle=1+z+2{z}^{2}+4{z}^{3}+8{z}^{4}+17{z}^{5}+35{z}^{6}+75{z}^{7}+157{z}^{8}+337{z}^{9}+712{z}^{10}+\cdots.

This is almost sequence A274115 in the OEIS
[
6
]
;
1+z(f_{0}+f_{1}+f_{2}+\cdots)
has this sequence as coefficients. Thanks go to Michel Marcus to point out the
connection with the sequences in the OEIS.

3.
Skew Dyck paths with catastrophes

Skew Dyck are a variation of Dyck paths, where additionally to steps
(1,1)
and
(1,-1)
a south-west step
(-1,-1)
is also allowed, provided that the path
does not intersect itself.

we replace each step
(-1,-1)
by
(1,-1)
but label it red. Here is the list of the 10 paths again (Figure

.

Three layers of states according to the type of steps leading to them (up, down-black, down-red). Purple arrows all end in the origin.

As in the motivating example from the previous section, we introduce generating functions. But since we need 3 layers to control everything,
we need to introduce
f_{j}
,
g_{j}
,
h_{j}
. The purple edges all end in the origin and represent the catastrophes. If they are ignored, one models
skew Dyck paths in this way, in particular
f_{0}+g_{0}+h_{0}
is the generating function of skew Dyck paths coming back to level 0.
However, now, we will deal with the purple arrows as well.

The following recursions can be read off immediately from the diagram
5
:

\displaystyle f_{0}=1+z\sum_{i\geq 2}f_{i}+z\sum_{i\geq 2}g_{i}+z\sum_{i\geq 2}h_{i},

\displaystyle f_{i+1}=zf_{i}+zg_{i},\quad i\geq 0,

\displaystyle g_{i}=zf_{i+1}+zg_{i+1}+zh_{i+1},\quad i\geq 0,

\displaystyle h_{i}=zh_{i+1}+zg_{i+1},\quad i\geq 0.

As before, we first ignore
f_{0}
and treat is as a parameter. We introduce

F(u)=\sum_{i\geq 1}u^{i-1}f_{i},\quad G(u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i},\quad H(u)=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}h_{i}.

Now we sum the recursions and get

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}f_{i+1}=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zf_{i}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zg_{i}

or

\displaystyle F(u)=f_{0}+uzF(u)+zG(u).

Further,

\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}g_{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zf_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zg_{i+1}+\sum_{i\geq 0}u^{i}zh_{i+1}

which translates into

G(u)=zF(u)+\frac{z}{u}[G(u)-G(0)]+\frac{z}{u}[H(u)-H(0)];

similarly

H(u)=\frac{z}{u}[G(u)-G(0)]+\frac{z}{u}[H(u)-H(0)].

We will eliminate the functions
G
and
H
:

G=-{\frac{z{f_{0}}+zuF-F}{z}}

and

H=G-zF.

Therefore we end up with just one equation for
F

-uzf_{0}-z{u}^{2}F+uF=-{z}^{2}Fu-2{z}^{2}f_{0}+2zF-2g_{0}{z}^{2}-{z}^{3}F+{z}^{3}f_{1}

which can be solved,

F(u)=-{\frac{\left(-2zf_{0}-2zg_{0}+{z}^{2}f_{1}+f_{0}u\right)z}{-{z}^{2}u+2z-{z}^{3}+z{u}^{2}-u}}=-\frac{\left(-2zf_{0}-2zg_{0}+{z}^{2}f_{1}+f_{0}u\right)z}{z(u-r_{1})(u-r_{2})}

with

r_{1}=\frac{1+z^{2}+\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2z},\quad r_{2}=\frac{1+z^{2}-\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2z}.

The fact that
u-r_{2}
must divide the numerator of
F
leads to

g_{0}={\frac{-2zf_{0}+{z}^{2}f_{1}+r_{2}f_{0}}{2z}},

so that
g_{0}
can be eliminated and only
f_{1}
remains as unknown;

F=\frac{zf_{0}}{1-zu+z^{2}-zr_{2}}

plugging in
u=0
allows to compute it:

f_{1}=\frac{zf_{0}}{1+z^{2}-zr_{2}}=\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}}.

Therefore the function
F
is known:

F(u)=\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}-u}

and thus

f_{j}=[u^{j-1}]F(u)=\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j}}.

From this, the other functions can be computed as well:

G(u)=\frac{1-zr_{1}}{z}\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}-u}=(r_{2}-z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}-u}

and

g_{j}=[u^{j}]G(u)=(r_{2}-z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j+1}},

as well as

H(u)=(r_{2}-2z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}-u}

and

h_{j}=[u^{j}]H(u)=(r_{2}-2z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j+1}}.

Now we can finally deal with
f_{0}
. The evaluations that are needed are simple:

\sum_{j\geq 2}f_{j}=\frac{f_{0}/r_{1}^{2}}{1-1/r_{1}},\quad\sum_{j\geq 2}g_{j}=(r_{2}-z)\frac{f_{0}/r_{1}^{3}}{1-1/r_{1}},\quad\sum_{j\geq 2}h_{j}=(r_{2}-2z)\frac{f_{0}/r_{1}^{3}}{1-1/r_{1}};

of course this is better done with a computer.
From this, the equation (just linear) for
f_{0}
can be solved:

f_{0}=\frac{1-9{z}^{3}-5{z}^{4}+3{z}^{5}+2{z}^{6}-(1-z^{2}-z^{3})\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2(1-2{z}^{2}-6{z}^{3}-3{z}^{4}+{z}^{5}+{z}^{6})}.

The series expansion

1+{z}^{3}+{z}^{4}+4{z}^{5}+6{z}^{6}+18{z}^{7}+31{z}^{8}+85{z}^{9}+157{z}^{10}+410{z}^{11}+792{z}^{12}+2004{z}^{13}+\cdots

is not in OEIS
[
6
]
. To compute the number of all skew Dyck paths coming back to the
x
-axis, we need

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle+g_{0}+h_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-z^{2})(3-z)(1-z-2z^{2}-z^{3})-(1-4z-3z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{4})\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2(1-2{z}^{2}-6{z}^{3}-3{z}^{4}+{z}^{5}+{z}^{6})}.

The series expansion is

1+z^{2}+z^{3}+4z^{4}+5z^{5}+17z^{6}+25z^{7}+76z^{8}+125z^{9}+353z^{10}+625z^{11}+1681z^{12}+\cdots.

One can also sum everything to enumerate the open ended paths in this model:

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}

\displaystyle f_{j}+\sum_{j\geq 0}g_{j}+\sum_{j\geq 0}h_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+z)(1-4z+4z^{2}+4z^{3}-z^{5})+(1+5z+3z^{2}-z^{3}-z^{4})\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2(1-2{z}^{2}-6{z}^{3}-3{z}^{4}+{z}^{5}+{z}^{6})};

the series expansion is

1+z+2z^{2}+4z^{3}+9z^{4}+18z^{5}+41z^{6}+85z^{7}+193z^{8}+410z^{9}+929z^{10}+2004z^{11}+\cdots.

Theorem 1
.

The generating functions related to the model of skew Dyck paths with catastrophes are

\displaystyle f_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j}},

\displaystyle g_{j}

\displaystyle=(r_{2}-z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j+1}},

\displaystyle h_{j}

\displaystyle=(r_{2}-2z)\frac{f_{0}}{r_{1}^{j+1}},

with

r_{1}=\frac{1+z^{2}+\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2z},\quad r_{2}=\frac{1+z^{2}-\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2z},

and

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle+g_{0}+h_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-z^{2})(3-z)(1-z-2z^{2}-z^{3})-(1-4z-3z^{2}+z^{3}+z^{4})\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2(1-2{z}^{2}-6{z}^{3}-3{z}^{4}+{z}^{5}+{z}^{6})}.

The enumeration of all paths, regardless of the final level, is

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}

\displaystyle f_{j}+\sum_{j\geq 0}g_{j}+\sum_{j\geq 0}h_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+z)(1-4z+4z^{2}+4z^{3}-z^{5})+(1+5z+3z^{2}-z^{3}-z^{4})\sqrt{1-6z^{2}+5z^{4}}}{2(1-2{z}^{2}-6{z}^{3}-3{z}^{4}+{z}^{5}+{z}^{6})}.

4.
Conclusion

From our analysis, other types of catastrophes (example: only from an even-numbered level one can drop to the ground) can be computed without much further effort. This changes only the equation for
f_{0}
; the other quantities stay as they are.

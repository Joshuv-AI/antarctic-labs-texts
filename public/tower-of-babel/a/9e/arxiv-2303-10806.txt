On Robustness of Double Linear Policy with Time-Varying Weights
Xin-Yu Wang, Chung-Han Hsieh, Member, IEEE

Abstract
In this paper, we extend the existing double linear policy by incorporating time-varying weights instead of constant weights and study a certain robustness property, called robust positive expectation (RPE), in a discrete-time setting. We prove that the RPE property holds by employing a novel elementary symmetric polynomials characterization approach and derive an explicit expression for both the expected cumulative gain-loss function and its variance. To validate our theory, we perform extensive Monte Carlo simulations using various weighting functions. Furthermore, we demonstrate how this policy can be effectively incorporated with standard technical analysis techniques, using the moving average as a trading signal.

Problem Formulation

For stage
k=0,1,2,\dotsc,
let
S(k)>0
be the

at stage

X(k):=\frac{S(k+1)-S(k)}{S(k)}.

Assume that
X(k)\in[X_{\min},X_{\max}]

2

In the sequel, we assume that the trades incur zero transaction costs and that the underlying asset has perfect liquidity. This setting serves as a good starting point for building the model and is closely related to the

II-A

Double Linear Policy with Time-Varying Weights

In
[
14
]
and many SLS literature, the trading policy is proposed with constant weights. This paper extends the constant weights to a time-varying weighting function.
With initial account value
V(0):=V_{0}>0
, we spilt it into two parts: Taking a fraction
\alpha\in[0,1]
, define
V_{L}(0):=\alpha V_{0}
as the initial account value for
long
position and
V_{S}(0):=(1-\alpha)V_{0}
for
short
position.
If
\alpha=1
, we are in a long-only position while
\alpha=0
corresponds to a pure short position.

The trading policy
\pi(\cdot)
is given by
\pi(k):=\pi_{L}(k)+\pi_{S}(k)
, where
\pi_{L}
and
\pi_{S}
are of double linear forms:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\pi_{L}(k)=w(k)V_{L}(k);\\
\pi_{S}(k)=-w(k)V_{S}(k).\end{cases}

(1)

The weighting function
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}:=[0,w_{\max}]
for all
k
with
w_{\max}:=\min\{1,1/X_{\max}\}
and is assumed to be
causal
; i.e., it may depend only on the information up to stage
k-1
.
Any
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
is called
admissible
weight. This condition is closely related to the survival trades; see Section
II-B
. Hence, the account values under the double linear policy
\pi_{L}
and
\pi_{S}
, denoted by
V_{L}(k)
and
V_{S}(k)
, can be described as the following linear time-varying stochastic difference equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}V_{L}(k+1)=V_{L}(k)+X(k)\pi_{L}(k)+(V_{L}(k)-\pi_{L}(k))r_{f};\\
V_{S}(k+1)=V_{S}(k)+X(k)\pi_{S}(k),\end{cases}

where
r_{f}\geq 0
is a
riskless
rate for a bank account or a treasury bond.
3
3

3

In practice, when shorting an asset, the corresponding proceeds are typically held as
collateral
by the broker to cover any potential losses from the short position. These proceeds are generally not available for immediate reinvestment into a riskless asset, such as a bank account or treasury bond.

Note that when
r_{f}>0
, account profit increases. Hence, as seen later in sections to follow, when studying the robustness of the double linear policy, we assume without loss of generality
r_{f}:=0
. Then the account value for long position reduce to
V_{L}(k+1)=V_{L}(k)+X(k)\pi_{L}(k)
.
Therefore, the overall account value for both long and short positions at stage
k
is given by

\displaystyle V(k)

\displaystyle=V_{L}(k)+V_{S}(k)=V_{0}\left(\alpha R_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)R_{-}(k)\right),

where
R_{+}(k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w(j)X(j))
and
R_{-}(k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)X(j))
.

II-B

Survivability Considerations

Fix
V_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we ensure that the trades are survivable for all
k
; i.e., the
w
-value that can potentially lead to
V(k)<0
is disallowed. To see this, for stage
k=0,1,\dotsc
, fix
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
. We observe that for the long position, we have
V_{L}(k)\geq V_{0}\alpha(1+w_{\max}X_{\min})^{k}>0

since

and

On the other hand, for the short position, we also have

V_{S}(k)\geq V_{0}(1-\alpha)(1-w_{\max}X_{\max})^{k}\geq 0

since
w_{\max}\leq 1/X_{\max}
.
Therefore, the overall account value satisfies
V(k)=V_{L}(k)+V_{S}(k)>0
for all
k
with probability one.

II-C

Robust Positive Expectation Problem

The primary objective of this paper is to study the following RPE problem.

Definition 2.1

(Robust Positive Expectation)
.

For stage
k=0,1,\dots
, let
V_{0}>0
be the initial account value, and
V(k)
be the account value at stage
k
. Define the expected cumulative gain-loss function up to stage
k
as
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(k):=\mathbb{E}[V(k)]-V_{0}
. A trading policy is said to have a
robust positive expectation
(RPE) property if it ensures that
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(k)>0
for all
k>1
and under all market conditions.

III
Gain-Loss Analysis

For
k>0

X:=\{X(j)\}_{j=0}^{k-1}
and
{\bm{w}}:=\{w(j)\}_{j=0}^{k-1}

With

\alpha\in(0,1)
and weight

for all

The

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X)

\displaystyle:=V(k)-V_{0}

\displaystyle=V_{0}(\alpha R_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)R_{-}(k)-1),

\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,{\bm{w}},k,\mu):=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X)]

If the weights are constant; i.e.,

]
. However, difficulties arise when the weighting function is time-varying.
To address this, a set of

4

for

.
Note that

for all

,

(Elementary Symmetric Polynomials)

This example illustrates the calculation of the elementary symmetric polynomials

.
Specifically, for

With the aid of the independence of

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
m\geq 1.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and

\overline{R}_{+}(k)

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{-}(k)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{j=0}^{m}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}.

On the other hand, for
k=2m
,

and

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{+}(k)

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j};

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{-}(k)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}.

Proof.

We use a shorthand notation

for
w(j)
in the proof.
Fix

.
Now for the case

, which is an odd number,

\displaystyle\overline{R}_{+}(k)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w_{j}\mu)

\displaystyle=(1+w_{0}\mu)(1+w_{1}\mu)\dotsm(1+w_{k-1}\mu)

\displaystyle\quad=1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}(-1)^{j}e_{j}(k)\mu^{j}

\displaystyle\quad=1+\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{2j+1}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(-1)^{2j}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}

On the other hand, for the even number case

Lemma 3.3
.

For
k>1
,
e_{2}(k)>0
provided that at least two weights
w(i),w(j)>0
for some
i,j\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
and
i\neq j
.

Proof.

Fix
k>1
. Then

e_{2}(k):=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq k-1}w(i_{1})w(i_{2}).

Proceed a proof by induction. If
k=2
, which corresponds to

e_{2}(2)=w(0)w(1).

Since we are assuming that at least two weights are strictly positive, in this case, it corresponds to
w(0),w(1)>0
. Therefore,
e_{2}(2)>0.

Next, assuming that
e_{2}(k)>0
for at least two weights
w(i),w(j)>0
for some
i,j\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
, we must show
e_{2}(k+1)>0.

Note that

\displaystyle e_{2}(k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq k}w(i_{1})w(i_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq k-1}w(i_{1})w(i_{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}w(k-i)w(k)

\displaystyle=e_{2}(k)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}w(k-i)w(k)>0,

where the last inequality holds by inductive hypothesis that
e_{2}(k)>0
for at least two weights, say
w(i),w(j)>0
for some
i,j\in\{0,1,\dots,k-1\}
and the fact that the sum
\sum_{i=1}^{k}w(k-i)w(k)\geq 0
.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(RPE with Time-Varying Weights)
.

Let
V_{0}>0
. Consider a double linear policy with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and weights
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
for all
k
. Then, the expected cumulative gain-loss function is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,{\bm{w}},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\left(\alpha\overline{R}_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)\overline{R}_{-}(k)-1\right).

Moreover, when
\alpha=1/2
and
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
with at least two weights being strictly positive, the RPE property holds; i.e.,
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,{\bm{w}},k,\mu)>0
for
k>1
and all
\mu\neq 0
.

Proof.

To calculate the expected cumulative gain-loss function, we use the fact that per-period returns
X(k)
are independent with common mean
\mathbb{E}[X(k)]=\mu
for all
k
. Thus, it is readily verified that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[V_{0}\left(\alpha R_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)R_{-}(k)-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle=V_{0}\left(\alpha\mathbb{E}[R_{+}(k)]+(1-\alpha)\mathbb{E}[R_{-}(k)]-1\right)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\left(\alpha\overline{R}_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)\overline{R}_{-}(k)-1\right),

which is identical to the desired equality in the statement of the theorem.
To complete the proof, we now show that the RPE property holds. Fix
k>1
. Consider two cases by splitting
k
into odd and even numbers.
We begin by considering
k=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
, corresponding to an odd number. Then, using Lemma
3.2
for the odd case, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\bigg(\alpha\Big(1+\sum_{j=0}^{m}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-\alpha)\Big(1-\sum_{j=0}^{m}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\Big)-1\bigg)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\bigg((2\alpha-1)\sum_{j=0}^{m}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\bigg((2\alpha-1)\mu\sum_{j=0}^{m}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\bigg).

Since
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
, we have

e_{j}(k)\geq 0
for all
j
and
k
.
Hence, it follows that
e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j}\geq 0
and
e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\geq 0
.
In addition, for
\alpha={1}/{2}
, the expected cumulative gain-loss function becomes

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,{\bm{w}},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}

\displaystyle=V_{0}\left(e_{2}(k)\mu^{2}+\sum_{j=2}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\right).

Since
V_{0}>0
,
\mu\neq 0
and at least two weights are strictly positive
w(i),w(j)>0
for some
i,j
with
i\neq j
, Lemma
3.3
indicates that
e_{2}(k)>0
. It follows that
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)>0
.
On the other hand, consider the case
k=2m
, which is an even number.
Using the second part of Lemma
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=V_{0}\bigg((2\alpha-1)\mu\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}e_{2j+1}(k)\mu^{2j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\bigg).

A similar argument can be made for showing that
e_{2j}(k)\mu^{2j}\geq 0
for all
j
and
k
.
Hence, taking
\alpha=1/2
, using the fact that at least two weights are strictly positive, and Lemma
3.3
, we again have
\overline{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)>0
when
\mu\neq 0
, which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.6

(Variance of the Gain-Loss Function)
.

Let
V_{0}>0
. Consider a double linear policy with
\alpha\in(0,1)
and weights
w(k)\in\mathcal{W}
for all
k
then
the variance of the cumulative gain-loss function is given by

\displaystyle{\rm var}(\mathcal{G}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X))

\displaystyle=V_{0}^{2}\Bigg(\alpha^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(w(j)^{2}\sigma^{2}+(1+w(j)\mu)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(w(j)^{2}\sigma^{2}+(1-w(j)\mu)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(1-w(j)^{2}(\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-2\alpha(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)^{2}\mu^{2})

\displaystyle\qquad-\alpha^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w(j)\mu)^{2}-(1-\alpha)^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)\mu)^{2}\Bigg).

Proof.

The proof is based on straightforward calculation on
{\rm var}(\mathcal{G}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X))=\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}^{2}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X)]-\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)
.
We first calculate the second moment of the gain-loss function:
With the aid of the independence of
X(k)
, a lengthy but straightforward calculation leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{G}^{2}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,X)]

\displaystyle=V_{0}^{2}\Bigg(\alpha^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(w(j)^{2}\sigma^{2}+(1+w(j)\mu)^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+(1-\alpha)^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(w(j)^{2}\sigma^{2}+(1-w(j)\mu)^{2}\Big)+1

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\Big(1-w(j)^{2}(\sigma^{2}+\mu^{2})\Big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt-2\alpha\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w(j)\mu)-2(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)\mu)\Bigg).

(2)

Then we calculate the square of the expected cumulative gain-loss function. That is,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{G}}^{2}(\alpha,\bm{w},k,\mu)

\displaystyle=V_{0}^{2}\bigg(\alpha\overline{R}_{+}(k)+(1-\alpha)\overline{R}_{-}(k)-1\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=V_{0}^{2}\bigg(\alpha^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w(j)\mu)^{2}+(1-\alpha)^{2}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)\mu)^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+1+2\alpha(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)^{2}\mu^{2})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt-2\alpha\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+w(j)\mu)-2(1-\alpha)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-w(j)\mu)\bigg).

(3)

In combination with Equations (

) and (

), a lengthy but straightforward calculation again leads to the desired expression for the variance of the gain-loss function.

Example 4.1

(GBM with Jumps)
.

We now collect historical daily prices for Apple Inc. (Ticker: AAPL) over a one-year period from January 2022 to December 2022.
5
5

5

Note that this one-year period provides a good test case since 2022 is often described as a bearish market.

Having estimated the volatility
\sigma^{*}
, we simulate the associated GBM prices with jumps, see
[
17
]
, using Monte Carlo simulations. That is, for
t\in[0,T]
, we generate the price governed by the following stochastic differential equation:

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle=S_{0}\exp\left(\left(\mu^{*}-\frac{1}{2}{\sigma^{*}}^{2}\right)t+\sigma^{*}W_{t}\right)(1-\delta)^{N_{t}},

(4)

where
W_{t}:=\{W(t):t\geq 0\}
is a standard Wiener process,

is the

\sigma^{*}
is the volatility constant,

that is independent with
W_{t}
,
\lambda
is the average rate of the jump that occurs for the process, and
\delta\in[0,1)
is the magnitude of the random jump.

6

6

4

\lambda=0.2

\delta=0.1

,
we consider four admissible weighting functions defined by

\displaystyle w_{0}(k)

\displaystyle:=0.8;

\displaystyle w_{1}(k)

\displaystyle w_{2}(k)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sin\left(\frac{1}{\frac{0.02}{N}k-0.01}\right)+1\right);

\displaystyle w_{3}(k)

\displaystyle:=f(k)\sin\left(\frac{1}{f(k)}\right)\mathbb{1}_{\left\{f(k)\sin\left(\frac{1}{f(k)}\right)\geq 0\right\}}(k),

where

V
Concluding Remarks

This paper extends the double linear policy by incorporating time-varying weights in a discrete-time setting. Using a set of elementary symmetric polynomials, we prove that the RPE property is preserved in the extended policy. In addition, we derive an explicit expression for the expected cumulative gain-loss function and its variance.
We conducted extensive Monte Carlo simulations using various weighting functions to validate our theory. Our results also show that the extended double linear policy with time-varying weights can be integrated with the standard technical analysis technique such as moving average.

In future research, it would be interesting to expand our analysis to a multi-asset case, where the weights can be optimized for a portfolio of assets; see
[
14
]
for an initial approach. Additionally, one valuable direction would be to investigate the impact of serial-correlated returns on the performance of the double linear policy with time-varying weights.
For example, an Auto-Regressive (AR) return model might be worth pursuing.
Finally, the impact of transaction costs could be considered to assess the practicality of the proposed policy in real-world applications; see
[
15
]
.

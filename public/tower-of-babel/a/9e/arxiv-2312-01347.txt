Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves in ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} with index of speciality five and beyond The author was supported in part by National Research Foundation of South Korea (2022R1I1A1A01055306).
Changho Keem

Abstract
. We study the Hilbert scheme of smooth, irreducible, non-degenerate and linearly normal curves of degree d d and genus g g in ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} ( r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 ) whose complete and very ample hyperplane linear series 𝒟 \mathcal{D} have relatively small index of speciality i ⁡ ( 𝒟 ) = g − d + r i(\mathcal{D})=g-d+r . In particular we completely determine the existence as well as the non-existence of Hilbert schemes of linearly normal curves ℋ d , g , r ℒ \mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r} for every possible triples ( d , g , r ) (d,g,r) with i ⁡ ( 𝒟 ) = 5 i(\mathcal{D})=5 and r ≥ 3 r\geq 3 . We also determine the irreducibility of the Hilbert scheme ℋ g + r − 5 , g , r ℒ \mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-5,g,r} when the genus g g is near to the minimal possible value with respect to the dimension of the projective space ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} for which ℋ g + r − 5 , g , r ℒ ≠ ∅ \mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-5,g,r}\neq\emptyset , say r + 9 ≤ g ≤ r + 11 r+9\leq g\leq r+11 . In the course of proofs of key results, we show the existence of linearly normal curves of degree d ≥ g + 1 d\geq g+1 with arbitrarily given index of speciality with some mild restriction on the genus g g .

1.
An overview, motivation and preliminary set up

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
be the Hilbert scheme of smooth, irreducible and non-degenerate curves of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
the union of components of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
whose general element corresponds to a linearly normal curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
By abuse of terminology, we say that a component of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
has index of speciality
\alpha
if
the hyperplane series
\mathcal{D}=g^{r}_{d}
of a general element of the component has index of speciality
\alpha
, i.e.

h^{1}(C,\mathcal{D})=g-d+\dim|\mathcal{D}|=\alpha\geq g-d+r.

It is possible that there may exist a component of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
which has index of speciality strictly greater than
g-d+r
; cf.
[
21
]
. In other words, there may exist a (component of) Hilbert scheme entirely consisting of non-linearly normal curves.
However the index of speciality of any component of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is
g-d+r
, which we define as the
index of speciality of a Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves
.

We recall the following modified assertion of Severi (
[
33
]
), which has been given attention by some authors recently; cf.
[
5
,
25
]
.

Modified Assertion of Severi
.

A nonempty
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is irreducible for any triple
(d,g,r)
in the Brill-Noether range

\rho(d,g,r)=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0.

We note that the modified assertion of Severi makes sense only if the index of speciality of Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
is non-negative.
In other words,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}\neq\emptyset
only when
g-d+r\geq 0
. Through a preliminary attempt to settle down the Modified Assertion of Severi
[
33
,
25
]
, one has a quite extensive knowledge about certain basics of the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves of small index of speciality
\alpha\leq 4
, which can be summarized as follows; cf.
[
22
,
5
,
25
,
23
]
.

Remark 1.1
.

We assume
r\geq 3
.

(i)

\alpha=0
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and is irreducible.

(ii)

\alpha=1
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-1,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and is irreducible for
g\geq r+1
and is empty for
g\leq r
.

(iii)

\alpha=2
:
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-2,g,r}\neq\emptyset
and is irreducible for every
g\geq r+3
and is empty for

g\leq r+2
.

(iv)

\alpha=3
:

(a)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}=\emptyset
for
g\leq r+4
and
r\geq 5
.

(b)

For
g\geq r+5
and

,
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}\neq\emptyset
unless
g=r+6
and
r\geq 10
.

(c)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-3,g,r}
is irreducible for every

and is reducible for almost all

in the range
r+5\leq g\leq 2r+2
.

(d)

Somewhat stronger results hold for curves in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{4}
. We refer
[

for details.

(v)

\alpha=4
:

(a)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}=\emptyset
for

,

.

(b)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}\neq\emptyset

for

,
r\geq 5
.

(c)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
~5\leq r\leq 8
for

,

.

(d)

if and only if

for

,

.

(e)

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-4,g,r}\neq\emptyset

for any
g\geq r+10
,
r\geq 5
.

(f)

Furthermore, the irreducibility has been fully determined in all the peculiar cases in (c) as well as several cases in (d).

(g)

For curves in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{4}
with
\alpha=4
– which are not explicitly mentioned in the above – we refer
[
23
, Remark 2.1]
and references therein for details.

Results mentioned in Remark
1.1
indicate that the Modified Assertion of Severi may continue to hold even beyond the Brill-Noether range
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
espeically when the index of speciality is rather small. For example, in the case
\alpha=3
, the Brill-Noether range
\rho(g+r-3,g,r)\geq 0
is
g\geq 3r+3
. However the irreducibility holds for every
g\geq 2r+3
.

There are a couple of advantages in studying the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves according to a given fixed index of speciality
\alpha
. One notes that the residual series of the very ample and complete hyperplane series
g^{r}_{d}
corresponding to a general element of a component of

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
of index of speciality
\alpha
has the fixed dimension
\alpha-1
, regardless of the values of the genus
g
and the
degree
d
. Therefore one may work more effectively in exploring out several important properties of Hilbert schemes under consideration by studying the family of curves in a fixed projective space
\mathbb{P}^{\alpha-1}
induced by the residual series of the (complete) hyperplane series.

To be more specific, in the cases
\alpha=2,3,4
,
the residual series of hyperplane series of a general element of a component of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
are pencils, nets or webs. Therefore one may expect to squeeze out several basic properties of the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
such as existence, irreducibility, gonality of a general element in each component, as well as the number of moduli by considering the corresponding property of Hurwitz space, Severi variety of plane curves or the Severi variety of curves on a surface of low degree in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

There is another advantage in studying the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
according to its index of speciality. Unless the genus
g
is fairly high with respect to the degree
d
of the projective curve in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, a general curve in a component of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
defined by a very ample complete hyperplane series
\mathcal{D}
is by no means extremal or nearly extremal i.e. the genus
g
is rather far below the maximal possible genus of curves of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. However the residual series
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
of
\mathcal{D}
with respect to the canonical series may induce an extremal or a nearly extremal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{\alpha-1}
. If this occurs, studying (extrinsic) curves defined by the residual series
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
may become relatively easier than directly handling the original curve in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. We will encounter this sort of phenomenon several times in this article.

In this paper we study the Hilbert scheme of linearly normal curves with index of speciality
\alpha=5
. Equivalently we study Hilbert schemes of non-degenerate, smooth and linearly normal curves in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
(
r\geq 3
) of degree
d=g+r-5
. We completely determine the existence (i.e. non-emptyness) as well as the non-existence (if any) of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for every triple

(d,g,r)
with
g-d+r=5
; cf. Theorem
3.2
and Figure
1
in the end of §3. In order to show the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for a given triple
(d,g,r)
, it is sufficient to demonstrate the existence of a complete and very ample linear series
g^{r}_{d}
on a particular curve. We will carry this out on a suitably chosen general
k
-gonal curve of genus
g
.

We also determine the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
when the genus
g
is near to the minimal possible value
r+9
with respect to
r
for which
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}\neq\emptyset
; cf. Theorem
3.2
, Proposition
4.1
, Theorem
5.1
, Proposition
5.4
, Theorem
5.6
, Theorem
6.1
, Proposition
6.2
, Theorem
6.3
and Figure
2
in the end of §6.
Happily, when
g
is equal or near to
r+9
, the curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
corresponding to a general element of a component of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
or its residual curve
C^{\vee}
– which is by definition the curve induced by the residual series of the very ample and complete hyperplane series of
C
– necessarily lies on a surface of relatively small degree. This is a consequence of a classical result which says that an integral projective curve in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
whose (arithmetic) genus
g
is in between
the first and second Castelnuovo genus bound – which we call a nearly extremal curve – necessarily lies on a surface of minimal or near to minimal degree. By analyzing the family of such curves on these special
surfaces, one may determine the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
for some particular triples
(d,g,r)
especially when the genus
g
is near to the minimal possible value with
respect to the dimension of the ambient projective space
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. In carrying out this process, non-trivial results such as the the irreducibility of the Severi
variety of nodal curves on Hirzebruch surfaces plays an important role.
We will be more specific with our road map for the determination of the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
in corresponding sections.

For notation and conventions, we follow those in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
; e.g.
\pi(d,r)
is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible, non-degenerate and reduced curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
which is usually referred as the first Castelnuovo genus bound. We shall refer to curves
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree

whose (arithmetic) genus equals

as
extremal curves
.
\pi_{1}(d,r)
is the so-called the second Castelnuvo genus bound which is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible, non-degenerate and reduced curve of degree

in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
not lying on a surface of minimal degree

; cf.
[
16

,
[
3
, page 123]
.
We shall call curves
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
d
and (arithmetic) genus

\pi_{1}(d,r)<g\leq\pi(d,r)

.

Following classical terminology, a linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on a smooth curve
C
is denoted by
g^{r}_{d}
.
A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
(

) on a smooth curve
C
is called
birationally very ample
when the morphism

C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
induced by the

is generically one-to-one onto (or is birational to) its image curve.
A base-point-free linear series

on

is said to be compounded of an involution (

gives rise to a non-trivial covering map

Throughout we work exclusively over the field of complex numbers.

(2)

(3)

.

For a complete linear series

.
Given an irreducible family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}

consisting of elements corresponding to smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curves is a

We recall the following fundamental fact regarding the scheme

Proposition 1.2
.

For non-negative integers

,
g
and
r
, let

\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)

The dimension of any component of

2.
Glossary of relevant generalities and auxiliary results

In this section we prepare a couple of lemmas and collect several facts which are relevant to our study. Even though almost all the results we quote in this section are quite well known, we list them up for the convenience of readers. We first recall the following generalities regarding the Severi variety of (nodal) curves on a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{e}
, i.e. a geometrically ruled surface over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with invariant
e\geq 0
.

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

Given a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{e}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbb{P}^{1}
where
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e)
, let
C_{0}
be the section with
C_{0}^{2}=-e\leq 0
and
f
be a fibre of
\pi
. Given a very ample linear system
\mathcal{L}=|aC_{0}+bf|
on
\mathbb{F}_{e}
, let

p_{a}(\mathcal{L})=(a-1)(b-1-\frac{1}{2}ae)

be the arithmetic genus of an integral curve belonging to
\mathcal{L}
.

(ii)

Given an integer
0\leq g\leq p_{a}(\mathcal{L})
, we set

\delta=p_{a}(\mathcal{L})-g
. We denote by

\Sigma_{\mathcal{L},\delta}\subset\mathcal{L}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{L}))

the (equi-singular) Severi variety which is the closure of the locus of integral curves in the linear system
\mathcal{L}
whose singular locus consists of exactly
\delta
nodes.

(iii)

Let
\Sigma_{\mathcal{L},g}
be the (equi-generic) Severi variety which is the closure of the locus of integral curves of geometric genus
g
in the linear system
\mathcal{L}
.

We shall make use the following well-known results adopted for our specific situation. Almost all the results quoted below are known to be true in a more general context. Readers are advised to refer
[
11

Remark 2.2
.

(i)

A general member of every irreducible component of the equi-generic Severi variety

is a nodal curve; cf.

Mainly for dimension count of certain families of curves or linear series under our investigation, we make a note of
the following facts regarding a surface

Remark 2.3
.

For an irreducible and non-degenerate surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
of minimal degree

, one of the following holds; cf.
[
7

(i)

S
is a smooth rational normal scroll, in which case we have;

\dim|aH+bL|=\frac{1}{2}a(a+1)n+(a+1)(b+1)-1

(1)

where
H
(resp.
L
) is the class of a hyperplane section (resp. the class a line of the ruling).
For any reduced and irreducible curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
of degree
d
contained in the linear system
|aH+bL|
, we have

d=(aH+bL)\cdot H=na+b

(2)

and by adjunction formula

p_{a}(C)=\frac{1}{2}\,a\left(a-1\right)\cdot n+\left(a-1\right)\left(b-1\right).

(3)

Throughout, whenever we deal with a rational normal surface scroll

(resp.

(ii)

S
is a Veronese surface in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
in which case any reduced irreducible curve in

\frac{(e-1)(e-2)}{2}
.

(iii)

S
is a cone over a rational normal curve in a hyperplane
\mathbb{P}^{n}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
, which is the image of the Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{F}_{n}=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-n))
. We denote by

(resp.
f
) the class in
\textrm{Pic}(\mathbb{F}_{n})
of the tautological bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}(1)
(resp. of a fibre);

. The morphism
\mathbb{F}_{n}\rightarrow S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
given by the complete linear system

is an embedding outside

– the curve with negative self-intersection – and contracts

to the vertex

of the cone

;

C_{0}^{2}=-n,C_{0}\in|h-nf|,K_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}=-2h+(n-2)f=-2C_{0}+(-n-2)f.

Let

Setting

(4)

We further remark that

0\leq\widetilde{C}\cdot C_{0}=\widetilde{C}\cdot(h-nf)=d-nk=m

Proposition 2.4

An epilogue, beyond index of speciality five

In this final section, we would like to discuss and make a couple of statements on families of projective curves with arbitrarily given index of speciality
\alpha
, as an attempt to seek for a reasonable generalization of the results we obtained for
\alpha\leq 5
.
Even though we need to impose certain restrictions on the range of the triples
(d,g,r)
in which the statements work, it may be worthy of coming up with some generalizations for further study along the line of ideas we followed in this paper. For almost all the statements which will be made in this section, the author is reluctant to provide proofs, mainly because this article would get too lengthy otherwise. Verifications for several statements in this section and more extensive relevant results can be found in our forthcoming paper
[
24
]
.

We are interested in studying Hilbert schemes of smooth curves of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
with a given fixed index of speciality. There is a sharp upper bound of the index of speciality of the hyperplane series of a linearly normal projective curve in terms of
(d,g)
. To be precise, we recall the following.

Remark 7.1
.

(i)

By Castelnuovo theory, it is known that the dimension

of a birationally very ample
linear series

on a curve of genus

is bounded by (cf.
[
26
, Lemma1.5]

r\leq\begin{cases}\frac{d+1}{3}&~\mathrm{~if~}d\leq g\\
\frac{1}{3}(2d-g+1)&~\mathrm{~if~}d\geq g.\end{cases}

(26)

(ii)

Setting
g-d+r=\alpha
, the bound (
26

\alpha\leq\begin{cases}\frac{3g-2d+1}{3}&~\mathrm{~if~}d\leq g\\
\frac{1}{3}(2g-d+1)&~\mathrm{~if~}d\geq g.\end{cases}

(27)

We recall Claim (3.2.A) in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, which we quote as the following proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

Given

, we have

\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{g+r-\alpha,g,r}\neq\emptyset

r\geq\alpha+1\mathrm{~~and~~}g\geq r+3\alpha-2.

Remark 7.3
.

(i)

One of numerical condition

in Proposition

is equivalent to

. One may think that imposing the restriction

is a too much sacrifice in view of the fact that many hard but interesting cases occur when

.
On the contrary, our knowledge on

is still on the poor side even when

as far as the author perceives. Therefore it is worthwhile to settle down or at least make a comprehensive treatment under the assumption

. Accordingly, for the rest of this section,

(ii)

be a smooth rational surface
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{s}
embedded by a very ample linear system
\mathcal{Z}=(a;b_{1},\cdots,b_{s})
,
b_{1}\geq b_{2}\geq\cdots\geq b_{s}\geq 1
with
\sum b_{i}<3a
.
Let
\mathcal{L}=(\tilde{a};\tilde{b}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{b}_{s})
be a very ample linear system such that

\tilde{a}\geq a,\tilde{b}_{1}\geq b_{1},\cdots,\tilde{b}_{s}\geq b_{s}
. Assume that the linear system

\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{Z}^{-1}
contains a smooth connected curve
C\subset S
.
Then a general
\tilde{C}\in\mathcal{L}
is smooth, irreducible and linearly normal.

Proof.

Let
Z=S\cap H\in\mathcal{Z}
be a general hyperplane section of
S
which is smooth, irreducible and non-degenerate in
H
. Note that

(i)

\deg Z=\deg S=Z^{2}=a^{2}-\sum b_{i}^{2}

(ii)

p_{a}(Z)=\frac{(a-1)(a-2)}{2}-\sum\frac{b_{i}(b_{i}-1)}{2}
by adjunction

(iii)

h^{0}(H,\mathcal{I}_{Z,H}(1))=0
since
Z
is non-degenerate in
H

(iv)

h^{1}(H,\mathcal{I}_{Z,H}(1))=0
: By
\sum b_{i}<3a
,
2p_{a}(Z)-2<\deg Z
and we have

n=h^{0}(H,\mathcal{O}_{H}(1))=h^{0}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(1))=\deg Z-p_{a}(Z)+1.

Thus
h^{1}(H,\mathcal{I}_{Z,H}(1))=0

The effective divisor
X=C+Z\in\mathcal{L}
satisfiles
h^{j}(\mathbb{P}^{n},\mathcal{I}_{X}(1))=0,j=0,1
by Lemma
B.1
. Hence by semi-continuity, a general element of the very ample
\mathcal{L}
is a linearly normal, smooth and irreducible curve.
∎

Primary 13E15, 13D02; Secondary 05E40, 05C20, 05C22 Thanks: Keywords: Regularity, depth, symbolic powers, ordinary powers, the join of weighted oriented graphs
Yijun Cui 1 , Guangjun Zhu ∗ 2 and Xiaoqi Wei 3

Abstract
. In this paper, we describe primary decomposition of the edge ideal of the join of some graphs in terms of that information of the edge ideal of every weighted oriented graph. Meanwhile, we also study depth and regularity of symbolic powers and ordinary powers of such an edge ideal. We explicitly compute depth and regularity of ordinary powers of the edge ideal of the join of two graphs consisting of isolated vertices, and also provide upper bounds of regularity of symbolic powers of such an edge ideal. For the edge ideal of the join of two graphs with at least an oriented edge for per graph, we give the exact formulas for their depth and regularity, and also provide the upper bounds of regularity of ordinary powers of such an edge ideal. Some examples show that these upper bounds can be obtained, but may be strict.



1.
Introduction

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple (no loops, no multiple edges) undirected graph.
A
weighted oriented graph

D
whose underlying graph is
G

(V(D),E(D),w)

where
V(D)=V(G)
is the vertex set,
E(D)
is a directed edge set and
w
is a function

, where
\mathbb{N}^{+}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
.
Specifically,
E(D)
consists of ordered pairs
(x_{i},x_{j})\in V(D)\times V(D)
where the pair
(x_{i},x_{j})

represents a directed edge from
x_{i}
to
x_{j}
.
Some times for short we denote
V(D)
and
E(D)

by
V
and
E
respectively. For any
x_{i}\in V
, its weight is denoted by
w(x_{i})
or
w_{i}
.
For any
x_{i}\in V(D)
, the sets
N^{+}_{D}(x_{i})=\{x_{j}\,|\,(x_{i},x_{j})\in E(D)\}
and
N^{-}_{D}(x_{i})=\{x_{j}\,|\,(x_{j},x_{i})\in E(D)\}

are called the
out-neighbourhood
and
in-neighbourhood
of
x_{i}
, respectively. Furthermore,
the set
N_{D}(x_{i})=N^{+}_{D}(x_{i})\cup N^{-}_{D}(x_{i})
is called the
neighbourhood
of
x_{i}
.
If
N_{D}(x_{i})=N^{+}_{D}(x_{i})
, then
x_{i}
is called a source of
D
; If
N_{D}(x_{i})=N^{-}_{D}(x_{i})
, then
x_{i}
is called a sink of
D
.
Define
deg_{D}(x_{i})=|N_{D}(x_{i})|
and
N_{D}[x_{i}]=N_{D}(x_{i})\cup\{x_{i}\}
.

Let
D=(V(D),E(D),w)
denote a weighted oriented graph with vertices
V(D)=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
.
By identifying the vertices with the variables in the polynomial ring
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
.
we can associate to each weighted oriented graph
D
a monomial ideal

I(D)=(x_{i}x_{j}^{w(x_{j})}\mid(x_{i},x_{j})\in E(D)).

This ideal is called the
edge ideal
of
D
. The
generators of
I(D)
are independent of the weight assigned to a source vertex. Therefore, to simplify our
formulas, throughout this paper, we shall assume that source vertices always have weight one.

Let
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
and
I
be a nonzero
homogeneous ideal of

. Then for

, where

. Geometrically, the symbolic powers are important since
they capture all the polynomials that vanish with a given multiplicity (see
[
8
]
). It is clear that

for all

but the reverse containment may fail.
We know that the regularity and depth are two central invariants associated to

.
It is well known that
\operatorname{reg}(I^{t})
is asymptotically a linear function for

, i.e., there exist constants

and a positive integer

such that for all

(see
[

,

]
).
It was proved that if

is an edge ideal of a simple graph, then

.
Kumar et al.
[
15
]
studied the regularity of symbolic powers of edge ideals of the join of simple graphs, they show that:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be an integer and
G_{1}*\cdots*G_{r}
be the join of

with pairwise disjoint vertex sets

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(G)^{(t)})=\max\{\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(G_{j})^{(i)})-i+t\mid 1\leq i\leq t,1\leq j\leq r\},\text{ for all $t\geq 1$}.

In
[
19

\mathcal{A}=\{G\mid\operatorname{reg}(S/(I(G)^{t+1}:u))\leq\operatorname{reg}(S/I(G)),u\in\mathcal{G}(I(G)^{t}),t\geq 1\},

where

denotes the minimal monomial generating set of

be two graphs with disjoint vertex sets.Then for all

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(G_{1}*G_{2})^{t})\leq 2t+\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(G_{1}*G_{2}))-2.

Our main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
D:=D_{1}*D_{2}
be the join of two weighted oriented graphs
D_{1}
and
D_{2}

D_{i}
consists of isolated vertices with

Then, for all

(1)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I(D)^{t})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I(D)^{(t)})=1
;

(2)

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{t})=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{(t)})=\sum\limits_{x\in V(D)}{w(x)}-|V(D)|+1+(t-1)(w+1)

where
w=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D)\}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer and let
D:=D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r}
be the join of weighted oriented graphs
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
, where

consists of isolated vertices with

.

The equality holds if

, where

and

.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer, and let
D:=D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r}
be the join of weighted oriented graphs
D_{1},\ldots,D_{r}
, where the vertex set of

(1)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I(D))=1

(2)

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

be the join of two weighted oriented graphs

and

and

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we gather together the needed notations and basic facts, which will be used throughout this paper.
Two important invariants we focus on are depth and regularity,
we define them by means of local cohomology modules.

Let
S
be a positively graded algebra and
{\mathfrak{m}}
its maximal homogeneous ideal. Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module. Let
H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)
, for
i\geq 0
,
denote the

with respect to
{\mathfrak{m}}
. We define

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(M)

\displaystyle=\text{min}\,\{i\,|\,H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(M)

\displaystyle=\text{ max}\,\{i+j\,|\,H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)_{j}\neq 0\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Let
a_{i}(M):=\max\,\{j\,|\,H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)_{j}\neq 0\}
with the convention that

a_{i}(M):=-\infty
if
H_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(M)=0
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(M)

\displaystyle=\min\,\{i\,|\,a_{i}(M)\neq-\infty\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(M)

\displaystyle=\max\,\{a_{i}(M)+i\mid i\geq 0\}.

When
S
is a polynomial ring over a field
{\mathbb{K}}
and
I
is a nonzero proper homogeneous ideal in

,
depth and regularity of

are closely related to the minimal free resolution and graded Betti numbers of

in the following way. Suppose that
I

admits the following minimal free resolution

0\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}(I)}\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{p-1,j}(I)}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\bigoplus\limits_{j}S(-j)^{\beta_{0,j}(I)}\rightarrow I\rightarrow 0,

where
S(-j)
is an
S
-module obtained by shifting
the degrees of
S
by

. The
(i,j)
-th graded Betti number
\beta_{i,j}(I)
is
an invariant of
I
that equals the number of minimal generators of degree

in the

i
-th syzygy module of
I
.

Let
\mbox{pd}\,(I)
denote the

of
I
. Then

\displaystyle\mbox{pd}\,(I)

\displaystyle=\mbox{max}\,\{i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\text{\ for some }j\,\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(I)

\displaystyle=\mbox{max}\,\{j-i\ |\ \beta_{i,j}(I)\neq 0\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(I)

\displaystyle=n-\mbox{pd}\,(I).

Remark 3.3

A monomial ideal is called
irreducible
if it cannot be written as proper intersection of two other monomial ideals.
It is called
reducible
if it is not provide. It is well known that a monomial ideal is irreducible if and only if it is generated
by pure powers of variables, that is, it has the form
(x_{i_{1}}^{a_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{k}}^{a_{k}})
. The following lemma is a fundamental fact.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
11
, Theorem 1.3.1]
)
Let
I\subset S
be a monomial ideal. Then there exists a unique decomposition

I=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{r}

such that none of the
Q_{i}
can be omitted in this intersection and each
Q_{i}
is an irreducible monomial ideal.

This decomposition is called
irredundant presentation
of
I
and each
Q_{i}
is called an
irreducible
component
of
I
.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset S
, we denote by
\mathcal{G}(I)
the unique minimal set of monomial generators of
I
. Let
u\in S
be a monomial, we set
\operatorname{supp}\,(u)=\{x_{i}:x_{i}|u\}
. If
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
, we set
\operatorname{supp}\,(I)=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{supp}\,(u_{i})
.

Now, we determine the irredundant presentation of the edge ideal
I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r})
by using the irredundant presentation of the edge ideal
I(D_{i})

of each weighted oriented graph
D_{i}
.

r\geq 2

I_{i}^{w_{i}}=(x_{ij}^{w_{ij}}\,|\,j\in[n_{i}])
,
\widehat{I}_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{i-1}{I_{j}}+\sum\limits_{j=i+1}^{r}{I_{j}^{w_{j}}}
and
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{0}{I_{j}}=\sum\limits_{j=r+1}^{r}{I_{j}^{w_{j}}}=(0)
by convention. Let
I(D_{i})=Q_{i1}\cap\dots\cap Q_{it_{i}}
be the irredundant presentation of
I(D_{i})
. Then the irredundant presentation of
I(D)
is

I(D)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{t_{i}}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+Q_{ij})}.

Proof.

We first prove that
I(D)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i}))}
by induction on
r
. If
r=2
, then

\displaystyle(\widehat{I}_{1}+I(D_{1}))\cap(\widehat{I}_{2}+I(D_{2}))

\displaystyle=\widehat{I}_{1}\cap\widehat{I}_{2}+\widehat{I}_{1}\cap I(D_{2})+\widehat{I}_{2}\cap I(D_{1})+I(D_{1})\cap I(D_{2})

\displaystyle=\widehat{I}_{1}\widehat{I}_{2}+I(D_{1})+I(D_{2})

\displaystyle=(x_{1i}x_{2j}^{w_{2j}}\mid i\in[n_{1}],j\in[n_{2}])+I(D_{1})+I(D_{2})=I(D)

where the second equality holds because of
\operatorname{supp}\,(\widehat{I}_{1})\cap\operatorname{supp}\,(\widehat{I}_{2})=\emptyset
,
I(D_{1})\subset\widehat{I}_{2}
and
I(D_{2})\subset\widehat{I}_{1}
.

Suppose that
r\geq 3
and that the statement holds for
r-1
, that is

\displaystyle I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})=

\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}(\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i}))=(I_{2}^{w_{2}}+\cdots+I_{r-1}^{w_{r-1}}+I(D_{1}))\cap(I_{1}+I_{3}^{w_{3}}+\cdots

\displaystyle+I_{r-1}^{w_{r-1}}+I(D_{2}))\cap\cdots\cap(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-2}+I(D_{r-1})).

Thus

\displaystyle\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i}))}

\displaystyle=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i}))}\cap(\widehat{I}_{r}+I(D_{r}))

\displaystyle=(I_{2}^{w_{2}}+\cdots+I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I(D_{1}))\cap(I_{1}+I_{3}^{w_{3}}+\cdots+I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I(D_{2}))

\displaystyle\cap\cdots\cap(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-2}+I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I(D_{r-1}))\cap(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-1}+I(D_{r}))

\displaystyle=(I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1}))\cap(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-1}+I(D_{r}))

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{i}\cap I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I_{r}^{w_{r}}\cap I(D_{r})+I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})\cap(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-1})

\displaystyle+I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})\cap I(D_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}I_{i}I_{r}^{w_{r}}+I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})+I(D_{r})=I(D)

where the penultimate equality holds because of
\operatorname{supp}\,(I_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}\,(I_{r}^{w_{r}})=\emptyset
,
I(D_{r})\subset I_{r}^{w_{r}}
and
I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})\subset(I_{1}+\cdots+I_{r-1})
, the last
equality holds because
I(D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r-1})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}I(D_{i})+(x_{ij}x_{k\ell}^{w_{k\ell}}\mid j\in[n_{i}],\ell\in[n_{k}]\text{\ where\ }1\leq i<k\leq r-1)
.

Since each
I(D_{i})=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{t_{i}}Q_{ij}
, one has
\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i})=\widehat{I}_{i}+\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{t_{i}}Q_{ij}=\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{t_{i}}(\widehat{I}_{i}+Q_{ij})
. Note that
\operatorname{supp}\,(\widehat{I}_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}\,(Q_{ij})=\emptyset
, it implies that the ideal
\widehat{I}_{i}+Q_{ij}
is irreducible for any
i\in[r]
,
j\in[n_{i}]
.
It follows that

I(D)=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i}))}=\bigcap\limits_{i=1}^{r}\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{t_{i}}{(\widehat{I}_{i}+Q_{ij})}.

We finish the proof.

∎

Given an ideal
I\subset S
, we set

\operatorname{bight}\,(I)=\sup\{\operatorname{ht}\,(P)\mid P\text{ is a minimal prime ideal of }S\text{ over }I\}.

As a direct consequence of the above theorem, one has the following corollary.

(c)

\operatorname{bight}\,(I(D))=\max\{\sum\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{r}n_{j}+\operatorname{ht}\,(I(D_{i}))\mid i\in[r]\}

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
D:=D_{1}*\cdots*D_{r}

as Theorem

.

In the following, we provide a technical lemma, which is useful to prove our main result of this section.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be a field of
\operatorname{char}\,({\mathbb{K}})=0
, and let
S={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
be a polynomial ring over
{\mathbb{K}}
and
I\subset(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{2}
a monomial ideal in
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
. Then, for all
t\geq 1
, we have

\operatorname{reg}\,((I+(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})}))^{(t)}+(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{(t)})\\
\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)(w+1)

where
w=\max\{w(y_{i})\,|\,i\in[n]\}
.

Proof.

Let
K=(I+(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})}))^{(t)}+(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{(t)}
. Since
x_{i}^{t},y_{j}^{tw(y_{j})}\in K
for all
i\in[m]
and
j\in[n]
, and
K
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary, where
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
is a maximal graded ideal in
S
. Therefore,

\operatorname{reg}\,(K)=\max\{j+1:(S/K)_{j}\neq 0\}.

Let
A=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)(w+1)
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
w=w(y_{n})
. In order to get the desired assertions, it is sufficient to prove that
(S/K)_{j}=0
for all
j\geq A
.
That is, it is enough to prove that
f\in K
for any monomial
f
with
\deg\,(f)\geq A
.

Indeed, let
f=(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}^{a_{i}})(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{b_{j}})
with
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\geq A
. We consider the following two cases:

(1) If
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\geq t
, then
f\in(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{t}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})^{(t)}\subseteq K
.

(2) If
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\leq t-1
, then
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}b_{j}\geq A-t+1=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)w
. In this case, let
f_{1}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{b_{j}}
, then
\deg\,(f_{1})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)w
.

Next, we prove that
f_{1}\in(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t}
by induction on
t
and
n
, thus
f_{1}\in K
, since
(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t}\subseteq(I+(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})}))^{(t)}
.

The case
n=1
is trivial. If
t=1
, then
\deg\,(f_{1})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}{b_{j}}\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1
.
In this case, there exists some
i\in[n]
such that
b_{i}\geq w(y_{i})
, which implies that
f_{1}\in(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})
, as wished.
On the contrary, if
b_{j}\leq w(y_{j})-1
for any
j\in[n]
, then
\deg\,(f_{1})\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(w(y_{i})-1)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}w(y_{i})-n
, contradicting with the assumption that
\deg\,(f_{1})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1
.

Now, we assume that
n,t\geq 2
. By similar arguments as the case
t=1
, we can obtain that
there exists some
i\in[n]
such that
b_{i}\geq w(y_{i})
. In this case, we set
b_{i}=pw(y_{i})+q
with
p\geq 1
,
0\leq q\leq w(y_{i})-1
. We divide into the following two cases:

(i) If
p\geq t
, then
f_{1}\in(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t}
.

(ii) If
p\leq t-1
, then

\displaystyle\deg\,(f_{1}/y_{i}^{b_{i}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)w-b_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+1+(t-1)w-(pw(y_{i})+w(y_{i})-1)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}{w(y_{i})}-n+2+(t-1)w-(p+1)w(y_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}{w(y_{i})}-(n-1)+1+(t-1-p)w

where the last inequality holds because of
w=\max\{w(y_{i})\,|\,i\in[n]\}
and
w=w(y_{n})
.
Hence, by induction, we have
f_{1}/y_{i}^{b_{i}}\in(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,\widehat{y_{i}^{w(y_{i})}},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t-p}
,
where
\widehat{y_{i}^{w(y_{i})}}
denotes the element
y_{i}^{w(y_{i})}
being omitted
from
(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{i}^{w(y_{i})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t-p}
.
It follows that
f_{1}\in(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,\widehat{y_{i}^{w(y_{i})}},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t-p}(y_{i}^{w(y_{i})})^{p}\subseteq(y_{1}^{w(y_{1})},\dots,y_{n}^{w(y_{n})})^{t}
.
And this concludes the proof.

∎

Theorem 4.8
.

and
D_{2}

(2)

where

.

Proof.

(1) follows from Theorem

, since

in this case.

(2) It is clear for the

Now, assume that

4.4

4.1

4.3

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S/(I_{2}^{w_{2}})^{(t)})

Theorem 4.9

(2)

And these equalities hold if

w=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V\}
and

Proof.

We prove the assertion by induction on
r
and

4.5

Now, assume that

.
By similar arguments as the case

Note that

4.3

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{(J+I_{r}^{w_{r}})^{(t)}}\right)=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\in[t-1]\\

\end{subarray}}\left\{{\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{J^{(t-i)}}\right)+\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{(I_{r}^{w_{r}})^{(i)}}\right)+1,\operatorname{reg}\left(\frac{S}{J^{(t-j+1)}}\right)}\right.

\displaystyle=\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[t]\\

\displaystyle\leq\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in[t]\\
\end{subarray}}\{\sum\limits_{x\in V\setminus V_{r}}{w(x)}-|V\setminus V_{r}|+1+(t-j)(w^{\prime\prime}+1)+\sum\limits_{x\in V_{r}}{w(x)}-|V_{r}|+(j-1)w^{\prime}\}

where

and

. And the second equality holds because of

(1) to the following exact sequence

5.
Ordinary powers of the edge ideal of the join of graphs with at least an oriented edge

In this section, we study depth and regularity of ordinary powers of the edge ideal of the join of weighted oriented graphs with at least an oriented edge for per graph.
We give the exact formulas for depth and regularity of the edge ideal of the join of two weighted oriented graphs, and also provide the upper bounds of regularity of ordinary powers when the second graph is a
weighted oriented complete graph.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
D:=D_{1}*D_{2}
be the join of two weighted oriented graphs
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, where the vertex set of
D_{i}
is
V_{i}=\{x_{ij}\,|\,j\in[n_{i}]\}
and
n_{i}=|V_{i}|
for
i\in[2]
.
If each
D_{i}
contains at least an oriented edge.
Then

(1)

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/I(D))=1
;

(2)

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))=\max\{\sum\limits_{x\in V_{2}}{w(x)}-|V_{2}|+\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1})),\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{2}/I(D_{2}))\}
,
where
S_{1}=k[x_{11},\ldots,x_{1n_{1}}]
and
S_{2}=k[x_{21},\ldots,x_{2n_{2}}]
.

Proof.

We use notations of Lemma
4.4
. Obviously, we have
I(D)=(\widehat{I}_{1}+I(D_{1}))\cap(\widehat{I}_{2}+I(D_{2}))

by Theorem
3.5
.
Set
J_{i}=\widehat{I}_{i}+I(D_{i})
for
i\in[2]
, then
I(D)=J_{1}\cap J_{2}
and
\operatorname{supp}\,(\widehat{I}_{i})\cap\operatorname{supp}\,(I(D_{i}))=\emptyset
. Thus,
by Lemma
2.5
(2), one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(S/J_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{2}/\widehat{I}_{1})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1})),

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(S/J_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{2}/I(D_{2}))+\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{1}/\widehat{I}_{2})=\operatorname{depth}\,(S_{2}/I(D_{2})).

By Lemmas
2.2
(1) and
2.5
(1), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S/J_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{2}/\widehat{I}_{1})+\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))=\sum\limits_{x\in V_{2}}{w(x)}-|V_{2}|+\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1})),

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S/J_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/\widehat{I}_{2})+\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{2}/I(D_{2}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{2}/I(D_{2})).

Note that
(J_{1},J_{2})=\widehat{I}_{1}+\widehat{I}_{2}=(x_{11},\ldots,x_{1n_{1}},x_{21}^{w_{21}},\ldots,x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}})
. Hence one has

\operatorname{depth}\,(S/(J_{1},J_{2}))=0\text{ and\ }\operatorname{reg}\,(S/(J_{1},J_{2}))=\sum\limits_{x\in V_{2}}{w(x)}-|V_{2}|.

Applying lemma
2.4
(1), (4) to the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow S/J_{1}\cap J_{2}\longrightarrow S/J_{1}\oplus S/J_{2}\longrightarrow S/(J_{1},J_{2})\longrightarrow 0,

we obtain the desired results.

∎

Theorem 5.2

∎

Theorem 5.5
.

We apply induction on
n
and

(1),

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{matrix}0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{P_{1}}(-w_{n})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{n}^{w_{n}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{I(K_{n})^{t}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{Q_{0}}&\longrightarrow&0&\\

we get the desired results,

.

Let
I(K_{4})=(x_{1}x_{2}^{2},x_{1}x_{3}^{3},x_{1}x_{4}^{6},x_{2}x_{3}^{3},x_{2}x_{4}^{6},x_{3}x_{4}^{6})
be the edge ideal of a weighted oriented complete graph
K_{4}
, its weight function is
w_{1}=1,w_{2}=2,w_{3}=3
and
w_{4}=6
. By using CoCoA, we obtain
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(K_{4})^{2})=15
. However,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{4}{w_{i}}-4+1+(2-1)(w+1)=16
, where
w=6
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
D=D_{1}*D_{2}
be the join of two weighted oriented graphs
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, where
V(D_{i})=\{x_{ij}\,|\,j\in[n_{i}]\}
and
n_{i}=|V(D_{i})|
for
i\in[2]
. If
n_{2}=1
,
then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))\geq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+w_{21}-1.

where
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{11}\ldots,x_{1n_{1}}]
. The equality holds when
D_{1}
contains at least an oriented edge.

Proof.

The case
D_{1}
contains of isolated vertices is obvious by Theorem
4.2
(2).
We can assume that
D_{1}
contains at least an oriented edge. Since
I(D):x_{21}^{w_{21}}=(x_{1i}\,|\,i\in[n_{1}])
and
I(D)+(x_{21}^{w_{21}})=I(D_{1})+(x_{21}^{w_{21}})
, we have

\operatorname{reg}\,((S/(I(D):x_{21}^{w_{21}}))(-w_{21}))=w_{21}
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/(I(D)+(x_{21}^{w_{21}})))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+w_{21}-1
.
We can obtain the desired result by a fact that
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))\geq 1
and by using Lemma
2.4
(1) to the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow(S/I(D):x_{21}^{w_{21}})(-w_{21})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{21}^{w_{21}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}S/I(D)\longrightarrow S/(I(D),x_{21}^{w_{21}})\longrightarrow 0.\hbox to0.0pt{\qquad\qed\hss}

Now we are ready to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
D=D_{1}*D_{2}
be the join of two weighted oriented graphs
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
, where
V(D_{i})=\{x_{ij}\,|\,j\in[n_{i}]\}
and
n_{i}=|V(D_{i})|
for
i\in[2]
. If
D_{2}
is a weighted oriented complete graph as Theorem
5.5
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1})^{t})\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
, where
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1i}:i\in V(D_{1})]
and
w^{\prime}=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D_{1})\}
.
Then, for any integer
t\geq 1
, we have

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{t})\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)

where
w=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D_{1})\cup V(D_{2})\}
. The equality holds when
w^{\prime}=w
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
.

Proof.

We apply induction on
t
and
n_{2}
. The case
t=1
is trivial and the
n_{2}=1
case follows from Lemma
5.10
.
Now, suppose
t\geq 2
and
n_{2}\geq 2
. Let
P_{0}=I(D)^{t}
,
P_{j}=I(D)^{t}:(x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}})^{j}
and
Q_{j-1}=P_{j-1}+(x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}})
for any
j\in[t]
, and set
J=(x_{1i}\,|\,i\in[n_{1}])+(x_{2i}\,|\,i\in[n_{2}-1])
. By some simple calculations, we can get

P_{j}=\sum\limits_{i=j+1}^{t}J^{i}x_{2n_{2}}^{(i-j)w_{2n_{2}}}I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-i}+J^{j}I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j}

with the convention
P_{t}=J^{t}
, and

Q_{j-1}=J^{j-1}I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j+1}+(x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}})
.

By Lemmas
2.5
(1),
5.8
and the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S/Q_{0})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t})+w_{2n_{2}}-1

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime\prime}+1)+w_{2n_{2}}-1

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w^{\prime\prime}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1),

where
w^{\prime\prime}=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})\}
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,((S/P_{t})(-tw_{2n_{2}}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/J^{t})+tw_{2n_{2}}=(t-1)(w_{2n_{2}}+1)+w_{2n_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1).

where the inequality holds since
w\geq w_{2n_{2}}
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))\geq w_{2n_{2}}
.

Note that
\dim\,(S/J^{j})=1
for any
j\in[t-1]
. Then, by Lemmas
2.3
,
2.2
(1),
2.5
(1) and the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,((S/Q_{j})(-jw_{2n_{2}}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(J^{j}I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j}+(x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}}))-1+jw_{2n_{2}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(J^{j}I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j})+w_{2n_{2}}-2+jw_{2n_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(J^{j})+\operatorname{reg}\,(I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j})+w_{2n_{2}}-2+jw_{2n_{2}}

\displaystyle=j+\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}})^{t-j})+w_{2n_{2}}-1+jw_{2n_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D\setminus x_{2n_{2}}))+(t-j-1)(w^{\prime\prime}+1)

\displaystyle+w_{2n_{2}}-1+j(w_{2n_{2}}+1)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-j-1)(w^{\prime\prime}+1)+j(w_{2n_{2}}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)

where the last inequality holds since
w_{2n_{2}},w^{\prime\prime}\leq w
, and the penultimate equality holds because
D\setminus x_{2n_{2}}
contains at least an oriented edge.

In particular, if
w^{\prime}=w
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
, it follows from Lemmas
2.5
(1),
5.8
and the inductive hypothesis that

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/Q_{0})=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)
.

We can confirm the
assertion by applying Lemma
2.4
(1) to the following exact sequences

(1)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\begin{matrix}0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{P_{1}}(-w_{2n_{2}})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{I(D)^{t}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{Q_{0}}&\longrightarrow&0&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{P_{2}}(-w_{2n_{2}})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{{2n_{2}}}^{w_{2n_{2}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{P_{1}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{Q_{1}}&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{P_{t-1}}(-w_{2n_{2}})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{P_{t-2}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{Q_{t-2}}&\longrightarrow&0\\
0&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{P_{t}}(-w_{2n_{2}})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cdot x_{2n_{2}}^{w_{2n_{2}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\frac{S}{P_{t-1}}&\longrightarrow&\frac{S}{Q_{t-1}}&\longrightarrow&0.\end{matrix}

∎

Lemma 5.10
.

With the assumptions and notation of Theorem

5.9
. If
n_{2}=1
, then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{t})\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)

where
w=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V_{1}\cup V_{2}\}
.
These equalities hold when
w^{\prime}=w
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
for all
t\geq 1
.

Proof.

The proof will be essentially the same as that for Theorem
5.9
.
We apply induction on
t
with the
t=1
case being trivial. Now, suppose
t\geq 2
. Let
J=(x_{1i}\,|\,i\in[n_{1}])
,
P_{0}=I(D)^{t}
,
P_{j}=I(D)^{t}:(x_{21}^{w_{21}})^{j}
and
Q_{j-1}=P_{j-1}+(x_{21}^{w_{21}})
for any
j\in[t]
.

By Lemmas
2.5
(1) and
5.8
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,(S/Q_{0})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D\setminus x_{21})^{t})+w_{21}-1

\displaystyle=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t})+w_{21}-1

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)+w_{21}-1

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)

where the first inequality holds by the assumption that
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))+w_{21}-1\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)+w_{21}-1
.

By some similar arguments as the proof of Theorem
5.9
, we can get

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,((S/P_{t})(-tw_{2n_{2}}))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1),

\displaystyle\operatorname{reg}\,((S/Q_{j})(-jw_{21}))

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)\text{\ for\ }j=1,\ldots,t-1.

In particular, if
w^{\prime}=w
and
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
for all
t\geq 1
, then

D_{1}
contains at least an oriented edge, because on the contrary, we have
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=0
. It follows from Lemmas
2.5
(1) and
5.8
that

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/Q_{0})=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(t-1)(w+1)
.
Thus we can obtain the assertion by applying Lemma
2.4
(1) to the exact sequences (

).

Remark 5.11
.

The conditions that
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1})^{t})\leq\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w^{\prime}+1)
in Theorems
5.9
can be obtained for many weighted oriented graphs, such as weighted rooted forests, naturally weighted oriented cycles, the disjoint union of some weighted oriented gapfree bipartite graph and so on
(see
[
21
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 4.5]
,
[
23
, Theorem 4.9]
).

As a consequence of Lemma
5.10
, we have the following:

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
D_{1}
be a naturally weighted oriented cycle with
w(x)\geq 2
for all
x\in V(D_{1})
and let
D_{2}
be a graph composed of a isolated vertex. If
D=D_{1}*D_{2}
, i.e.,

D
is a weighted oriented wheel graph, and
w:=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D)\}=\max\{w(x)\,|\,x\in V(D_{1})\}
.
Then

\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{t})=\sum\limits_{x\in V(D)}{w(x)}-|V(D)|+1+(t-1)(w+1).

Proof.

By
[
20
, Theorem 4.5]
, we have
\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/(I(D_{1})^{t}))=\operatorname{reg}\,(S_{1}/I(D_{1}))+(t-1)(w+1)
for all
t\geq 1
.
The expected formulas follow from Theorem
5.9
.

∎

The following examples show that the upper bounds in Theorems
5.9
can be obtained, but may be strict.

Example 5.13
.

Let
I(D)=(x_{1}x_{2}^{2},x_{2}x_{3}^{2},x_{3}x_{1}^{4},x_{1}y^{3},x_{2}y^{3},x_{3}y^{3})
be the edge ideal of a weighted oriented wheel graph
D
, its weight function is
w(x_{1})=4,w(x_{2})=w(x_{3})=2
and
w(y)=3
. By using CoCoA, we have
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))=7
,
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{2})=12
. Thus
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{2})=\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(w+1)
, where
w=4
.

Example 5.14
.

Let
I(D)=(x_{1}x_{2}^{2},x_{2}x_{3}^{2},x_{3}x_{1}^{2},x_{1}y^{3},x_{2}y^{3},x_{3}y^{3})
be the edge ideal of a weighted oriented wheel graph
D
, its weight function is
w(x_{1})=w(x_{2})=w(x_{3})=2
and
w(y)=3
. By using CoCoA, we have
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))=5
,
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{2})=8
. Thus
\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D)^{2})<\operatorname{reg}\,(S/I(D))+(w+1)
.

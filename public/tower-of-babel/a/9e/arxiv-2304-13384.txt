Rigidity of equilibrium states and unique quasi-ergodicity for horocyclic foliations
Pablo D. Carrasco, Federico Rodriguez-Hertz

Abstract
In this paper we prove that for topologically mixing metric Anosov flows their equilibrium states corresponding to Hölder potentials satisfy a strong rigidity property: they are determined only by their disintegrations on (strong) stable or unstable leaves. As a consequence we deduce: the corresponding horocyclic foliations of such systems are uniquely quasi-ergodic, provided that the corresponding Jacobian is Hölder, without any restriction on the dimension of the invariant distributions. This gives another proof of a result of Babillott-Ledrappier.



1
Introduction and Main Results

1.1
Equilibrium states for metric Anosov flows

Let
\mathtt{f}=(f_{t})_{t}:M\to M
be an Anosov flow with invariant bundle decomposition
TM=E^{s}\oplus E^{c}\oplus E^{u}
. As it is well known, both
E^{s},E^{u}
are integrable to leafwise smooth foliations
\mathcal{W}^{s},\mathcal{W}^{u}
(which in this article we refer as the
s-,u-
horocyclic foliations) that are contracted and expanded respectively under
f_{t},t>0
. More generally, in this work we will be concerned with metric Anosov flows (also called Smale flows), which were introduced by Pollicot (inspired in the corresponding concept for homeomorphisms due to Ruelle) and gained recent interest due to their applicability to geometrical problems. Indeed, for a word hyperbolic group
G
, Gromov constructs a compact metric space
M
which comes together with an action
G\curvearrowright M
by isometries, and a transitive metric Anosov flow
\mathtt{f}:M\to M
, in such a way that
M/x\sim f_{t}(x)
is
G
-equivariantly identified with
\partial^{2}G=\{(g,h)\in\partial G:g\neq h\}
. The orbits of
\mathtt{f}
are a model for the (coarse) behavior of geodesics in
G
. See
[
1
]
. More recently, in the same setting
[
2
]
Bridgeman et al. construct for a (projective) Anosov representation
\rho:G\to\mathtt{Sl}_{d}(\mathbb{R})
a metric Anosov flow (which turns out to be a Hölder reparametrization of the previous flow
\mathtt{f}:M\to M
), and use it among other things to obtain information about the spectrum of the representation. We remark that in these contexts the dimension of the horocyclic laminations is typically larger than one.

In what follows
\mathtt{f}
denotes a transitive metric Anosov flow with invariant laminations
\mathcal{W}^{s},\mathcal{W}^{u}
; the definition is recalled in
Section

2.1
, but if the reader is not acquainted by the theory for this introduction she/he can safely assume that
\mathtt{f}
is a differentiable Anosov flow. Transitive metric Anosov flows come in two flavors: either the horocyclic laminations are minimal (each one of its leaves is dense), or none of its horocyclic leaves are dense. See
[
3
]
(Plante’s arguments also apply to metric Anosov flows). The first case corresponds to the flow
\mathtt{f}
being topologically mixing, while the second is the so called suspension case (in particular, there is a global transversal to the flow).

A central objective here is showing that in the first case the horocyclic laminations are much more rigid that appear at first sight, and relate this with the ergodic properties of the underlying dynamics
\mathtt{f}
, particularly its equilibrium states corresponding to Hölder observables (see
Section

2.4
for a review of the theory). Connecting the properties of equilibrium states of hyperbolic systems with their induced measures along horocyclic leaves is a classical and important topic in (usually smooth) ergodic theory, which typically depends on the fact that the
s-,u-
leaves carry a natural Lebesgue class induced by some Riemannian metric on the manifold. Given an invariant probability measure
\mathrm{m}
for the flow one can detect whether or not it is an equilibrium state for the geometric potential
\varphi(x)=\int_{0}^{1}-\log|\det D_{x}f_{t}|_{E^{u}}|\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0ptt
by checking if the disintegrations of
\mathrm{m}
along
\mathcal{W}^{u}
are in equivalent to the corresponding Lebesgue measures on leaves
[
4
]
. The word disintegration above is used in the sense of Rohklin theory
[
5
]
: given a Borel probability measure
\mathrm{m}
and a countably generated partition
\xi=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
of
M
with respect to
\mathrm{m}
(
i\neq j\Rightarrow\mathrm{m}(A_{i}\cap A_{j})=0
), there is an essentially unique family
\{\mathrm{m}^{\xi}_{x}\}_{x\in M_{0}}
of probability measures on
M
(referred as the conditionals of
\mathrm{m}
with respect to
\xi
) satisfying

S-1

\mathrm{m}(M_{0})=1
,

S-2

x,y\in A_{i}
implies that
\mathrm{m}_{x}^{\xi}=\mathrm{m}_{y}^{\xi}
assigns measure one to
A_{i}
, and

S-3

for a Borel set
A\subset M,\mathrm{m}(A)=\int\mathrm{m}^{\xi}_{x}(A)\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0pt\mathrm{m}(x)
.

There is a subtle but important point is the discussion above: the partition by
u
-leaves is not measurable in the sense of Rohklin, so one has to consider measurable partitions subordinated to
\mathcal{W}^{u}
. Since these depend in general on
\mathrm{m}
, to be able to compare we need our reference measures to be defined everywhere, and not almost everywhere with respect to some fixed particular measure. When considering the geometrical potential this is not an issue, but for general equilibrium states (even for the entropy maximizing, measure which corresponds to the zero potential) the existence of these reference measures is not clear at all.

This is precisely what we elucidate here.

Main Theorem
.

Assume
\mathtt{f}=(f_{t})_{t}:M\to M
to be a topologically mixing metric Anosov flow, and let
\varphi:M\to\mathbb{R}
be a Hölder function. Then there exists a family
\{\nu^{u}_{x}\}_{x\in M}
satisfying:

1.

\nu^{u}_{x}
is a Radon measure on the
W^{u}(x)
.

2.

If
\mathrm{m}
is any probability, invariant or not, and
\xi=\{A_{i}\}_{i\in\Lambda}
is a measurable partition adapted to
\mathcal{W}^{u}
whose conditionals are of the form

x\in A_{i}\Rightarrow\mathrm{m}^{\xi}_{x}=\frac{\nu^{u}_{x}}{\nu^{u}_{x}(A)},

then
\mathrm{m}
is the unique equilibrium state for the system
(\mathtt{f},\varphi)
.

The adapted partitions referred above are measurable partitions with some convenient additional properties: they are measurable partitions whose atoms are subsets of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
that contain relatively open neighborhoods of each point, and are increasing under the discretized action
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(
f_{1}(A_{i})
is union of atoms of
\xi
, for every
A_{i}\in\xi
). These are also universal in the sense that they are partitions with respect to every possible equilibrium state. Partitions of this were introduced to Sinai for uniformly hyperbolic system (see for example
[
6
]
), and generalized to non-uniformly expanding foliations by Ledrappier and Strelcyn
[
7
]
. We remark that these are constructed for invariant measures, but if
\mathrm{m}
does not admit such partitions, then it is a priori discarded as a possible equilibrium state.

1.2
Uniqueness of the transversal measure and rigidity

On first glance the main motif of the
Main Theorem
is to construct the family of measures on the unstable lamination which are can be used to detect equilibrium states. While this may be true, more careful analysis shows that it reveals a very rigid structure of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
. Indeed, the previous theorem does not assume that the measure
\mathrm{m}
is invariant under the flow, which demonstrates that all information is encoded in how this measure disintegrates along
\mathcal{W}^{u}
. This is somewhat surprising: conditions S-2, S-3 for disintegrations show that
\mathrm{m}
can be uniquely determined by its conditionals with respect to
\xi
together with the transverse measure, that is, the induced measure on
M/\xi
. Simple minded examples in product spaces show that these can be chosen fairly independently.

The reason for this behavior is that in fact, for a given (regular) Jacobian
J
there is only one possible quasi-invariant transverse measure for the holonomy pseudo-group of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
associated to
J
. Let us explain this.

Given a lamination
\mathcal{F}
of a metric space
M
, one can use the lamination charts to make sense of what it means for a subset
D\subset M
to be transverse to
\mathcal{F}
. In the case when
M
is a manifold,
D\subset M
is an embedded disc and
\mathcal{F}
has continuous tangent bundle, this notion coincides with transversality in the differential topology sense between
D
and every leaf of
\mathcal{F}
that it intersects. We say that a family
\{\mu_{x}:x\in M\}
is a
transverse measure
if it satisfies:

1.

each
x\in M
is contained in some transversal
D_{x}
, and

2.

\mu_{x}
is a non-trivial Borel measure on
D_{x}
.

Given one of such measures and
y\in\mathcal{F}(x)
, one can use the holonomy of the foliation to compare
\mu_{x}
and
\mu_{y}
, and least for nearby
x,y
: we denote
\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}}_{y,x}:E_{x}\subset D_{x}\to E_{y}\subset D_{y}
, the holonomy transport, where
E_{x},E_{y}
are relatively open.

Definition 1.1
.

The transverse measure
\{\mu_{x}\}_{x}
of
\mathcal{F}
is quasi-invariant if there exist a family of positive functions
\mathrm{Jac}_{\mu}=\{\mathrm{Jac}_{y,x}:E_{x}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}:y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\}
and positive constants
\{C(y,x):y\in\mathcal{F}(x)\}
such that for
y\in\mathcal{F}(x)
,

\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}}_{x,y}\mu_{y}=C(y,x)\mathrm{Jac}_{x,y}\mu_{x}.

The family
\mathrm{Jac}_{\nu}
is the Jacobian of the quasi-invariant measure. If
\mathrm{Jac}_{\nu}\equiv 1
then the measure is said to be an invariant transverse measure.

In the setting that we are working it is convenient to use local center-stable sets as the family of transversals to
\mathcal{W}^{u}
, and we use implicitly this choice in what follows. Next we discuss the possible Jacobians, and remark that necessarily such a family is a
(\mathbb{R}_{>0},\cdot)
-valued cocycle over the holonomy pseudo-group of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
. Here is a natural way to define such a family: let

\mathscr{Coc}(\mathtt{f})=\{h:M\to\mathbb{R}_{+}:h(x)=e^{\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(f_{t}x)dt}\text{ for some H\"{o}lder function }\varphi\},

(1)

and given
h\in\mathscr{Coc}(\mathtt{f})
, define

\mathscr{h}=\{H_{x_{0},y_{0}}:x_{0},y_{0}\in M,y_{0}\in W^{u}(x_{0})\}

(2)

with

H_{x_{0},y_{0}}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{h(f_{-j}\circ\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{W}^{u}}_{y_{0},x_{0}}x)}{h(f_{-j}(x))},\quad x\in W^{cs}(x_{0}),\mathrm{hol}^{\scriptstyle\mathcal{W}^{u}}_{y_{0},x_{0}}x\in W^{cs}(y_{0}).

(3)

It is then direct to verify that
\mathscr{h}
is a multiplicative cocycle, and in fact one can check that for every equilibrium state of
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
of
\mathtt{f}
corresponding to a Hölder potential, the transverse measure of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
is quasi-invariant, with a Jacobian of the previous form. This is discussed in our article
[
8
]
for differentiable Anosov flows, but the proof given extends without any change to the metric case. See
[
9
]
for further discussion.

We are able to conclude the following.

Main Corollary
.

In the hypotheses of the
Main Theorem
, given
h\in\mathscr{Coc}(\mathtt{f})
there exists
\mu^{cs}=\{\mu_{x}^{cs}\}
a transverse measure for
\mathcal{W}^{u}
such that
\mu^{cs}
is the unique quasi-invariant measure with Jacobian given by the family
\mathscr{h}
determined by
h
.

The proof that the
Main Theorem
implies the
Main Corollary
is exactly the same as the one given in
[
9
]
, and thus we will not repeat it here.

1.3
Comparison with other literature

The
Main Theorem
, which improves the classical SRB theorem
[
4
]
, appears in our recent paper
[
9
]
under additional hypotheses: differentiability of the Anosov flow and one-dimensionality of the unstable distribution. Questions due to professor F. Labourie motivated us to reformulate a previous version without assuming any differentiable structure; as mentioned at the beginning of the introduction, this may be relevant for geometrical applications. We remark here that the family of measures
\{\nu_{x}\}_{x\in M}
are not the same as the ones constructed by Haydn
[
10
]
(in particular, if
x,y
are in the same unstable leaf then
\nu_{x}
is typically different from
\nu_{y}
), although they are related. Uniqueness of the Haydn family is proven in
[
11
]
.

The
Main Corollary
was established originally by Babillot-Ledrappier
[
11
]
, and a different proof was given by Schapira
[
12
]
in the context of hyperbolic manifolds (in particular, algebraic). The method of the proof of the original result consist of establishing first the theorem for the symbolic model
[
13
]
of the flow, and then assembling everything back in the manifold. The second part is delicate, and Babillot-Ledrappier refer to the work
[
14
]
for this part, while the same statement is proven for the zero potential. On the other hand, the second proof relies heavily in the geometrical information that constant negative curvature provides. Our method is different and more analytical in flavor: given a Hölder potential on
M
we push everything to the symbolic model
\Sigma
of the dynamics under the flow of
u-
leaves and construct a family of averaging operators
\{R_{n}:\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)\toit\}
. For each
n\geq 0
,
R_{n}
is a modified version of the transfer operator associated to the equilibrium state
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
of the potential restricted to natural
\sigma
-algebra of
\Sigma
that consists of
n
-cylinders. Finally, we prove SOT convergence of these operators to the functional defined by
\mathrm{m}_{\scriptstyle\varphi}
, and show that this can be transferred back to the manifold. The heart of the argument is that we have never used the transverse structure (the lamination
\mathcal{W}^{cs}
), and from this we deduce our results. This type of argument is more similar to Marcus’ proof of the unique ergodicity of
\mathcal{W}^{u}
for codimension-one Anosov flows
[
15
]
, which in turn generalizes the classical theorem of Furstenberg about equi-distribution of the horocycles in surfaces of constant negative curvature
[
16
]
. We remark however that Marcus’ proof explicitly uses that the corresponding conditional measures along
\mathcal{W}^{u}
behave quite tamely under iterations (the Jacobian is constant), which together with one-dimensionality of the leaves makes dealing wih the corresponding averaging operators more manageable than for general potentials. For the proof of the
Main Corollary
we do not make such assumptions, and thus we provide a complete direct proof of Babillot-Ledrappier result without further assumptions.

We finish this introduction by posing a general question. It follows by the results of this article that horocyclic foliations have strong rigidity properties in terms of their admitted quasi-invariant measures. On the other hand, the existence of the renormalizing dynamics (i.e., the associated hyperbolic system) is probably not necessary for this phenomena, as the result of Veech
[
17
]
shows (see also
[
18
]
). We can thus ask:

Question 1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a co-compact lattice in a lie group
G
, and let
U
be a one-parameter unipotent subgroup of
G
. Consider
\alpha
a differentiable multiplicative cocycle over
U\curvearrowright\Gamma/G
. Does there exists a unique quasi-invariant measure for the action with Jacobian given by
\alpha
?.

Veech answers positively the previous question when the cocycle is trivial.

Prerequisites

2.1
Metric Anosov flows

We follow the exposition of
[
2
]
. Let
M
be a metric space. A collection of embeddings
\mathcal{A}=\{\phi_{U}=(\phi_{U}^{1},\phi^{2}_{U}):U\subset M\to N_{U}\times K_{U}\}
, where
N_{U},K_{U}
are topological spaces, is a lamination atlas if

•

\mathcal{U}=\{U\}
is an open covering of
M
;

•

whenever
U\cap V\neq\emptyset
it holds

x,y\in U\cap V\text{ implies }\phi_{U}^{1}(x)=\phi_{U}^{1}(y)\Leftrightarrow\phi_{U}^{2}(x)=\phi_{U}^{2}(y).

Level sets of
\phi_{U}^{2}
(for
\phi_{U}\in\mathcal{A}
) are called plaques, and a plaque chain is a finite collection
P_{1},\ldots,P_{k}
of plaques such that
P_{i}\cap P_{i+1}\neq\emptyset,\forall i=0,\ldots,k-1
. Given such an atlas, it defines an equivalence relation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
, where
x,y
are equivalent if and only if they can be joined by a plaque chain. The partition induced by
\mathcal{F}
(also denoted by
\mathcal{F}
) is referred as the lamination, whereas its atoms are called leaves. The collection of plaques induced by a given lamination atlas is called a plaquation. It is useful to consider as equivalent lamination atlases defining the same structure on
M
: the phrase “lamination atlas of
\mathcal{F}
” is used in this sense. Note that if
U\subset M
is open then
\mathcal{F}
defines naturally a lamination in
U
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{F}|U
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{F}
is a lamination and
\mathtt{f}=(f_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a flow on
M
. The lamination
\mathcal{F}
is invariant under
\mathtt{f}
if each
f_{t}
permutes the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
. An invariant lamination
\mathcal{F}
is transverse to
\mathtt{f}
if there exists an atlas
\mathcal{B}=\{\psi_{U}:N_{U}\times(K_{U}\times(-\epsilon_{U},\epsilon_{U}))\}
of
\mathcal{F}
so that

x\in U,\psi(x)=(a,b,t_{0})\Rightarrow\phi_{t}(x)=\psi_{U}^{-1}(a,b,t_{0}+t)\quad\forall|t+t_{0}|<\epsilon_{U}.

In this case note that the same atlas
\mathcal{B}
serves as a laminated atlas for the partition induced by the orbits of
\mathtt{f}
. Due to invariance, we can use this structure to extend the lamination
\mathcal{F}
by saturating their leaves under
\mathtt{f}
and obtain a new lamination
\mathcal{F}^{c}=\{f_{(-\infty,\infty)}(L):L\in\mathcal{F}\}
. By definition, each leaf of
\mathcal{F}^{c}
is saturated by orbits of
\mathtt{f}
: if it is also saturated by leaves of
\mathcal{F}
we say that
\mathcal{F}
is complete under
\mathtt{f}
.

Given
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
laminations of
M
, we say that they are transvere if there exists a lamination atlas
\mathcal{A}=\{\phi_{U}=(\phi_{U}^{1},\phi^{2}_{U}:U\subset M\to N_{U}\times K_{U}\}
for
\mathcal{F}_{1},\mathcal{F}_{2}
such that each
\phi_{U}
sends
\mathcal{F}_{1}|U
to the horizontal lamination
\{N_{U}\times\{b\}:b\in K_{U}\}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{2}|
to the vertical lamination
\{\{a\}\times K_{U}:a\in N_{U}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,d)
be a compact metric space and
\mathtt{f}=(f_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
a continuous flow on it. We say that
\mathtt{f}
is a metric Anosov flow if there exist laminations
\mathcal{W}^{u},\mathcal{W}^{s}
with some fixed associated plaquations
\mathcal{P}^{u}=\{P^{u}\},\mathcal{P}^{s}=\{P^{s}\}
, and constants
C>0,0<\lambda<1
such that

1.

\mathcal{W}^{u},\mathcal{W}^{s}
are invariant under
\mathtt{f}
and complete.

2.

\mathcal{W}^{cs},\mathcal{W}^{u}
and
\mathcal{W}^{s},\mathcal{W}^{cu}
are transverse.

3.

It holds for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle x,y\in P^{u}\Rightarrow d(f_{-t}(x),f_{-t}(y))\leq C\lambda^{t}

\displaystyle x,y\in P^{s}\Rightarrow d(f_{t}(x),f_{t}(y))\leq C\lambda^{t}.

For the rest of the article
\mathtt{f}=(f_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denotes a metric Anosov flow.

2.2
Symbolic models for hyperbolic systems

Given a matrix
A\in\mathtt{Mat}_{d}(\{0,1\})
one considers the two-sided subshift of finite type that it determines,

\Sigma_{A}=\{\underline{x}=(x_{n})_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}:A_{x_{n}x_{n+1}}=1,\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

For
k,l\in\mathbb{N}
and a word
a_{-k}\cdot a_{-k+1}\cdots a_{l}\in\{0,1\}^{l+k+1}
with
A_{a_{i},a_{i+1}}=1
for all
i
, we denote

C(a_{-k}\cdots a_{l})_{-k}=\{\underline{x}\in\Sigma_{A}:x_{i}=a_{i},\forall-k\leq i\leq l\}.

Note that

C(a_{-k}\cdots a_{l})_{-k}=\bigcap_{i=-k}^{l}\sigma^{-i}(C(a_{i})_{0}).

Definition 2.2
.

Sets of the previous form are called rectangles. If
k=l
we say that the rectangle is symmetric.

The topology in
\Sigma_{A}
is metrizable, where a compatible metric is given as

d(\underline{x},\underline{y})=\frac{1}{2^{N+1}};

above
N
is the size of the largest symmetric rectangle that contains
\underline{x},\underline{y}
.

Similar considerations can be applied to the one-sided shift spaces

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{-}=\{\underline{x}=(x_{n})_{n\leq 0}\in\{0,1\}^{-\mathbb{N}}:A_{x_{n}x_{n+1}}=1,\forall n<0\}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{A}^{+}=\{\underline{x}=(x_{n})_{n>0}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}:A_{x_{n}x_{n+1}}=1,\forall n>0\}

Note that any
\underline{x}\in\Sigma_{A}
can be written uniquely in the form

\underline{x}=\underline{x}^{-}\cdot\underline{x}^{+}\quad\underline{x}^{-}\in\Sigma_{A}^{-},\underline{x}^{+}\in\Sigma_{A}^{+}

where the

denotes concatenation. In both
\Sigma_{A},\Sigma_{A}^{+}
there is a continuous homeomorphism, respectively a

-to-
1

\sigma(\underline{x})=(x_{n+1})_{n},

whereas in
\Sigma_{A}^{-}

.

Definition 2.3
.

A

1

It is easy to verify that

For

Given

If

2.3
Suspension

If
R:\Sigma_{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
is continuous, we consider the space

\{(\underline{x},t)\in\Sigma_{A}\times\mathbb{R}:0\leq t\leq R(\underline{x})\}

and identify

(\underline{x},R(\underline{x}))\sim(\sigma\underline{x},0)

to obtain a compact bundle over the circle,
S(A,R)=\Sigma_{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}/\sim
, together with the natural flow

s_{t}([\underline{x},u])=[\underline{x},u+t].

Definition 2.4
.

The space
S(A,R)
is the suspension of the shift map
\sigma:\Sigma_{A}\to\Sigma_{A}
under the roof function
R

is the suspension flow.

Remark 2.1
.

The sets

D(i)=\{[\underline{x},0]:x_{0}=i\},\quad 1\leq i\leq d

T=\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}D(i)

Write, using

R^{\ast}-R=v^{\ast}-v^{\ast}\circ\sigma\quad\ast\in\{+,-\}

\displaystyle S^{\ast}(A,R)=\{(\underline{x},t)\in\Sigma_{A}\times\mathbb{R}:0\leq t\leq R^{\ast}(\underline{x})\}/\sim

by identifying
(\sigma^{-1}\underline{x},R^{-}(\underline{x}))\sim(\underline{x},0)

Theorem 2.1
.

Given a metric Anosov flow

For a metric Anosov flow

Given a continous function

(4)

Proposition 3.1
.

The family

Proof.

Let
\underline{x},\underline{y},\underline{z}\in C(a)_{0}

•

\underline{y}\in W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{x})
,

•

\underline{z}\in W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{y})

\left|\frac{1}{\nu^{u}_{\underline{y}}(W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{y}))}\int_{W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{y})}\mathfrak{h}\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0pt\nu^{u}_{\underline{y}}-\frac{1}{\nu^{u}_{\underline{z}}(W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{z}))}\int_{W^{u}_{\scriptstyle\mathrm{loc}}(\underline{z})}\mathfrak{h}\operatorname{d}\hskip-2.0pt\nu^{u}_{\underline{z}}\right|

Putting everything together and using the local product structure inside

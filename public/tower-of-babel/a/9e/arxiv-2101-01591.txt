In this paper

Theorem 1.5

For each

Theorem 1.6

For each

.

.

Theorem 1.7

For each

Theorem 1.8

For each

We explain the main ideas in the proofs of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.7
.

i)

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
A
be a nonempty finite subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and
\psi_{d}
be the
d
-Veronese map. For each hyperplane
H
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
, we have that
\psi_{d}^{-1}(H)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
for some
e\in[1,d]
; conversely, for each
C\in\bigcup_{e=1}^{d}{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
, there is a hyperplane
H
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
such that
\psi_{d}^{-1}(H)=C
. Thus the problem of finding curves determined by
A
which contain few points of
A
is (almost) equivalent to the problem of finding hyperplanes generated by
\psi_{d}(A)
which contain few points of
\psi_{d}(A)
.

ii)

It is easier to deal with hyperplanes than to do it with curves. As we mentioned above, there are already results that assure the existence of several hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{e}
which are generated but contain few points of a given set
S
, see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. Nonetheless, these results require that any
e
points of
S
generate a hyperplane. In general, the set
\psi_{d}(A)
wont satisfy this condition in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
. Thus we cannot take advantage of
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
. What we will do is to fix a
\binom{d+2}{2}-4
-dimensional flat
F
and a
2
-dimensional flat
G
such that
F\cap G=\emptyset
. Considering
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
and the homogenization
G^{h}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
of
G
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
, we will project each
\binom{d+2}{2}-3
-dimensional flat
K
containing
F
into
K\cap G^{h}
(which is always a single point in the projective space
G^{h}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
); this will induce a map
\pi_{F}:{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}\setminus F\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
. In this way we take the problem of hyperplanes containing
F
into a problem of lines.

iii)

Nevertheless, we will need very special flats
F
. To be able to say that if a line
L
contains few points of
(\pi_{F}\circ\psi_{d})(A)
, then the curve
(\pi_{F}\circ\psi_{d})^{-1}(L)
contains few points of
A
, we need that
F
satisfies certain conditions. The flats
F
will be the affine hull of
\psi_{d}(B)
where
B
is a subset of
A
which satisfies some technical conditions. The family of subsets
B
of
A
satisfying those assumptions will be denoted by
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
. The longest and most tedious part of this paper (which is Section 3) is to warranty that
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
is not very small, however this is the core of this article. The proof of this fact will depend on the structure of
A
; specifically, it depends on whether there is a low degree curve that contains several points of
A
or not.

iv)

Let
F
be the affine hull of
\psi_{d}(B)
for some
B\in{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
and denote by
\varphi
the restriction of
\pi_{F}\circ\psi_{d}
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\psi_{d}^{-1}(F)
. In Section 4 we will prove that there are
k\leq d^{2}
and a finite collection (bounded in terms of
d
) of Zariski closed subsets
\{E_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that

\bullet

\varphi(E_{i})
is a singleton for each
i\in I
.

\bullet

For each
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(\psi_{d}^{-1}(F)\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i})
, we have that
\varphi({\mathbf{a}})\not\in\{\varphi(E_{i}):i\in I\}
.

\bullet

For each
{\mathbf{a}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus(\psi_{d}^{-1}(F)\cup\bigcup_{i\in I}E_{i})
, we have that
|\varphi^{-1}(\varphi({\mathbf{a}}))|\leq k
.

v)

With the map
\varphi
as in iv), any line
L
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
disjoint from
\{\varphi(E_{i}):i\in I\}
which contains exactly two points of
\varphi\left(A\setminus\psi_{d}^{-1}(F)\right)
satisfies that
\varphi^{-1}(L)
is an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{d}
which contains few points of
A
and is determined by it. A result of T. Boys , C. Valculescu and F. de Zeeuw (see Lemma
2.11
) will warranty that there are several lines

as above, and therefore we will have a number of the desired curves for each
B\in{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
. This can be done for each flat
F
generated by an element of
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
. Thus, since
|{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)|\gg 0
, we will have several curves satisfying the desired conditions.

We think that at least as valuable as the main results of this paper is the method we use since it allows to translate many Sylvester-Gallai problems of curves into Sylvester-Gallai problems of lines where much more tools are available.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we establish some notation and auxiliary results that will be needed in the forthcoming sections. As we already mentioned, the families
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
are fundamental tools in the proofs of the main results. We introduce and state some properties of the families
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
in Section 3. Using the elements of the families
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
, we will take the problem of finding curves with few points into finding ordinary lines which avoid a finite set and this will be done in Section 4. The proofs of the main results of this paper are completed in Section 5.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we state some notation and results that will be needed later.

Let
p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
and
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
. We denote by
{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))
its zero set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and by
\deg(p(x,y))
its degree. We say that
p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
is
irreducible
if
\deg(p(x,y))>0
and for any factorization
p(x,y)=p_{1}(x,y)p_{2}(x,y)
, we get that
p_{i}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
. We say that
{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))
is
irreducible
if
p(x,y)
is irreducible. Write

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{e=1}^{d}{\mathcal{C}}_{e}

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]

\displaystyle:=\{p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]:\;\deg(p(x,y))\in[1,d]\};

also, for technical reasons, we write
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq 0}:=\emptyset
. In
{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
, we define the relation
p(x,y)\sim q(x,y)
if there is
r\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
such that
p(x,y)=r\cdot q(x,y)
, and we denote by
[p(x,y)]
the class of
p(x,y)
and by
{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]/\sim
the set of classes. For any subset
X
of
{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
, we write

X/\sim:=\{[p(x,y)]\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]/\sim:\;[p(x,y)]\cap X\neq\emptyset\}.

Note that for any
p(x,y),q(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
such that
[p(x,y)]=[q(x,y)]
, we have that
\deg(p(x,y))=\deg(q(x,y))
and
{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))={\mathcal{Z}}(q(x,y))
; thus

\sigma_{d}:{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim\longrightarrow{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d},\qquad\sigma_{d}([p(x,y)])={\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y)).

is well defined. Let
p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
be such that
\deg(p(x,y))>0
and consider a factorization
p(x,y)=r\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x,y)^{m_{i}}
with
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
r\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
[p_{i}(x,y)]\neq[p_{j}(x,y)]
for all
i,j\in[1,n]
such that
i\neq j
, and
p_{i}(x,y)
is irreducible for each
i\in[1,n]
. Then the irreducible curves
{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{1}(x,y)),{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{2}(x,y)),\ldots,{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{n}(x,y))
are known as the
irreducible components of
{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))
. The irreducible components satisfy that

{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{i}(x,y))={\mathcal{Z}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}(x,y)\right)

and

\deg(p(x,y))=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\deg(p_{i}(x,y))\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\deg(p_{i}(x,y)).

We will use a weak version of Bezout’s theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
d,e\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
and
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. If
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
do not have an irreducible component in common, then

|C_{1}\cap C_{2}|\leq de.

Proof.

See
[
9
, Ch. I.7]
.
∎

The next facts can be proven easily by the reader.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and
e,f\in[1,d]
.

i)

For any
C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
and
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
, notice that
C_{1}\cup C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e+f}
.

ii)

For any

and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
such that
C_{0}\subseteq C
, there is
C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
such that

.

iii)

For any

such that

, there is
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
such that
A\subseteq C
.

For a curve
C
, it may happen that there exist

such that
[p(x,y)]\neq[q(x,y)]
but
{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))=C={\mathcal{Z}}(q(x,y))
. The next lemma shows that for each

there are at most

classes

such that

.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and

with pairwise distinct irreducible components

. Then

\sigma^{-1}_{d}(C)=\left\{\left[\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{m_{i}}\right]\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim:\;m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}},\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\deg(p_{i})\leq d\right\}.

Since the number of solutions
(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}^{n}
of

is bounded by

, we get in particular that

|\sigma^{-1}_{d}(C)|\leq d^{d}.

Proof.

See
[
10
, Cor.7]
.
∎

Let

{\mathcal{G}}_{e}(S)
.

A fundamental tool in this paper is the Veronese map. Write
I_{d}:=\\
\left\{(n,m)\in{\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}}^{2}:\;n+m\in[1,d]\right\}
so
|I_{d}|=\binom{d+2}{2}-1
. The
d
-Veronese map
is the map

\psi_{d}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\longrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1},\qquad\psi_{d}(a_{1},a_{2})=\left(a_{1}^{n}a_{2}^{m}\right)_{(n,m)\in I_{d}}

To avoid confusion, the ring of polynomials which corresponds to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
will be denoted by
{\mathbb{R}}[x,y]
and the ring of polynomials which corresponds to
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
will be denoted by
{\mathbb{R}}[z_{(n,m)}]_{(n,m)\in I_{d}}
. There is a quite important relation between elements of
{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim
and hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
given by the next map

\displaystyle\tau_{d}:{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}_{\binom{d+2}{2}-2},

\displaystyle\tau_{d}\left(\left[r_{(0,0)}+\sum_{(n,m)\in I_{d}}r_{(n,m)}x^{n}y^{m}\right]\right)={\mathcal{Z}}\left(r_{(0,0)}+\sum_{(n,m)\in I_{d}}r_{(n,m)}z_{(n,m)}\right)

The Veronese map has some well-known properties that we will need later. The proof of the following facts can be found in standard algebraic geometry books, see for instance
[
9
, Ch. I]
,
[
18
, Ch. 1]
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
.

i)

The map
\psi_{d}
is an isomorphism onto its image.

ii)

The map
\tau_{d}
is a bijection. Note that for any
[p(x,y)]\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim
, we have that

\psi_{d}({\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y)))=\tau_{d}([p(x,y)])\cap\psi_{d}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}).

iii)

For all
e\in[1,d]
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)
, we have that

\dim\psi_{e}(A)\leq\dim\psi_{d}(A).

A crucial part of this paper is to take the problem of finding curves determined by
A
containing few points of
A
into the problem of finding hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
generated by
\psi_{d}(A)
which contain few points of
\psi_{d}(A)
. The main tool to do this is the following lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)
be such that there is no element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
which contains
A
. Then

\tau_{d}\left(\sigma_{d}^{-1}({\mathcal{D}}_{d}(A))\right)={\mathcal{G}}_{\binom{d+2}{2}-2}(\psi_{d}(A)).

Proof.

See
[
10
, Lemma 12]
.
∎

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)
be finite. Since
A
is finite, there is always a line
L={\mathcal{Z}}(rx+sy-t)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that
L\cap A=\emptyset
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))\cap A

\displaystyle=\left({\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))\cap A\right)\cup(L\cap A)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Z}}\left(p(x,y)\cdot(rx+sy-t)^{d-\deg(p(x,y))}\right)\cap A.

From this observation, we get the next fact.

Remark 2.6
.

Let

,
p(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
e\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
f\in{\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}}
,
F
be a proper flat in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\binom{f+2}{2}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)
be such that
A
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
.

i)

If
e\geq f
, then
|\psi_{e}(A)\cap F|\leq 1+\dim F
.

ii)

If
e<f
, then
|\psi_{e}(A)\cap F|\leq\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}-\binom{e+2}{2}+2+\dim F
.

iii)

If
e<f
, then
\dim\psi_{d}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))\geq\binom{f-e+2}{2}-1
for all
d\geq f-e
.

Proof.

First we show i). If
f=0
, then
|A|=1
so

|\psi_{e}(A)\cap F|\leq|\psi_{e}(A)|=1\leq 1+\dim F.

Thus, from now on, we assume that
f>0
. Since
A
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
, Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{f}(A)
cannot be contained in a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{f+2}{2}-1}
and therefore

(1)

\binom{f+2}{2}-1=\dim\psi_{f}(A).

In so far as
e\geq f
, Remark
2.4
.iii implies that
\dim\psi_{f}(A)\leq\dim\psi_{e}(A)
and thereby

(2)

\dim\psi_{f}(A)\leq\dim\psi_{e}(A)\leq|\psi_{e}(A)|-1=|A|-1.

Since
|A|=\binom{f+2}{2}
, we get from (
1
) and (
2
) that

(3)

\dim\psi_{e}(A)=|\psi_{e}(A)|-1=|A|-1.

As a consequence of (
3
), for any
S\in{\mathcal{P}}(\psi_{e}(A))
, we get that
\dim S=|S|-1
; in particular,

\dim\psi_{e}(A)\cap F=|\psi_{e}(A)\cap F|-1,

and hence

|\psi_{e}(A)\cap F|=1+\dim\psi_{e}(A)\cap F\leq 1+\dim F,

which completes the proof of i).

Now we prove ii). For any flat
E
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}
, write
\alpha(E):=\binom{e+2}{2}-2-\dim E
. In so far as
F
is a proper flat, note that
\alpha(F)\geq 0
. The proof of ii) will be done by induction on
\alpha(F)
. First suppose that
\alpha(F)=0
so
F
is a hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}
. Then Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Trivially,

(4)

\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A\subseteq\psi_{e}^{-1}(F).

We claim that

(5)

|\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A|\leq\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}.

Indeed, if (
5
) is false, then

|A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)|=|A|-|\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A|<|A|-\binom{f+2}{2}+\binom{f-e+2}{2}=\binom{f-e+2}{2},

and therefore Remark
2.2
.iii implies that there exists
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f-e}
such that

(6)

A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\subseteq C.

However, from (
4
) and (
6
),

A=(\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A)\cup(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))\subseteq\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cup C

with
\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cup C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
by Remark
2.2
.i; this contradicts the assumption so (
5
) is true. Therefore

\displaystyle|F\cap\psi_{e}(A)|

\displaystyle=|\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}

\displaystyle\Big(\text{by }(\ref{E5})\Big)

\displaystyle=\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}-\alpha(F)

\displaystyle\Big(\text{since }\alpha(F)=0\Big),

and the basis of induction is complete. Now assume that the claim holds for all flats
E
such that
0\leq\alpha(E)<\alpha(F)
. Since
A
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
and
e<f
, we get that
A
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Thus Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{e}(A)
is not contained in a hyperplane and therefore
\dim\psi_{e}(A)=\binom{e+2}{2}-1
. In particular, this means that
\psi_{e}(A)\setminus F\neq\emptyset
, and then we can fix
{\mathbf{a}}\in A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)
. Set
E:={\mathrm{Fl}}(F\cup\{\psi_{e}({\mathbf{a}})\})
. Since
\psi_{e}({\mathbf{a}})\not\in F
, we have that
\dim E=1+\dim F
, and therefore

(7)

\alpha(E)+1=\alpha(F).

In so far as
\psi_{e}({\mathbf{a}})\in\psi_{e}(A)\setminus F
, we get that

(8)

|F\cap\psi_{e}(A)|+1\leq|E\cap\psi_{e}(A)|.

Then

\displaystyle|F\cap\psi_{e}(A)|

\displaystyle\leq|E\cap\psi_{e}(A)|-1

\displaystyle\Big(\text{by }(\ref{E8})\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}-\alpha(E)-1

\displaystyle\Big(\text{by induction}\Big)

\displaystyle=\binom{f+2}{2}-\binom{f-e+2}{2}-\alpha(F),

\displaystyle\Big(\text{by }(\ref{E7})\Big)

and this completes the induction and the proof of ii).

Finally, we show iii). Since
F
is a proper flat, there is a hyperplane
H
which contains
F
. Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{e}^{-1}(H)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Since
H\supseteq F
,

(9)

\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A\subseteq\psi_{e}^{-1}(H)\cap A\subseteq\psi_{e}^{-1}(H).

We claim that

(10)

\dim\psi_{f-e}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))\geq\binom{f-e+2}{2}-1.

Indeed, if (
10
) does not hold, then
\dim\psi_{f-e}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))<\binom{f-e+2}{2}-1
, and hence there exists a hyperplane
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{f-e+2}{2}-1}
which contains
\psi_{f-e}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))
with
\psi_{f-e}^{-1}(K)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f-e}
by Remark
2.4
.ii. Then

(11)

A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\subseteq\psi_{f-e}^{-1}(K).

Nonetheless, from (
9
) and (
11
),

A=(\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)\cap A)\cup(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))\subseteq\psi_{e}^{-1}(H)\cup\psi_{f-e}^{-1}(K),

and Remark
2.2
.i yields that
\psi_{e}^{-1}(H)\cup\psi_{f-e}^{-1}(K)\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
; this contradicts the assumption so (
10
) needs to be true. Now, in so far as
d\geq f-e
, Remark
2.4
.iii yields that

(12)

\dim\psi_{d}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F))\geq\dim\psi_{f-e}(A\setminus\psi_{e}^{-1}(F)).

Then iii) is a consequence of (
10
) and (
12
).
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
e\in{\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{+}}
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\right)
be such that
A
is finite and it is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Denote by
{\mathcal{R}}
the family of elements
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\binom{e+2}{2}}(A)
such that
B
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Then

|{\mathcal{R}}|\geq\frac{1}{2^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}}|A|.

Proof.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and
T\in{\mathcal{P}}\left({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right)
be such that
\dim T=d
. Set
{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T):=\\
\left\{R\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d+1}\left(T\right):\;\dim R=d\right\}
. First we show that

(13)

|{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)|\geq\frac{1}{2^{d}}|T|.

We prove (
13
) by induction on
d
. If
d=1
, then

{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(T)=\left\{R\in{\mathcal{P}}_{2}\left(T\right):\;\dim R=1\right\}={\mathcal{P}}_{2}\left(T\right)

so
|{\mathcal{S}}_{1}(T)|=\binom{|T|}{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|T|
, and the basis of induction is complete. Assume that (
13
) holds for
d-1
and we show it for
d
. Since
\dim T=d
, there exists
S\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(T)
such that
\dim S=d-1
; fix
S\in{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(T)
such that
\dim S=d-1
and write
H:={\mathrm{Fl}}(S)
. We have two cases.

\bullet

Assume that
|T\cap H|\geq\frac{1}{2}|T|
. Fix
{\mathbf{t}}\in T\setminus H
. Notice that for all
R\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(T\cap H)
, we have that
|R\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}|=|R|+1=d
and
\dim R\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}=1+\dim R=d
so
R\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)
. This map

{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(T\cap H)\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T),\qquad R\mapsto R\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}

is injective so

(14)

|{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)|\geq|{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(T\cap H)|.

By induction,
|{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(T\cap H)|\geq\frac{1}{2^{d-1}}|T\cap H|
so (
14
) leads to

|{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)|\geq|{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(T\cap H)|\geq\frac{1}{2^{d-1}}|T\cap H|\geq\frac{1}{2^{d}}|T|,

and this completes the induction in this case.

\bullet

Assume that
|T\cap H|<\frac{1}{2}|T|
. Note that for all
{\mathbf{t}}\in T\setminus H
, we have that
|S\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}|=|S|+1=d
and
\dim S\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}=1+\dim S=d
so
S\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)
. The map

T\setminus H\rightarrow{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T),\qquad{\mathbf{t}}\mapsto S\cup\{{\mathbf{t}}\}

is injective so

|{\mathcal{S}}_{d}(T)|\geq|T\setminus H|=|T|-|T\cap H|>\frac{1}{2}|T|\geq\frac{1}{2^{d}}|T|,

and this completes the induction.

If
e=0
, then
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}=\emptyset
so
{\mathcal{R}}=\{\{{\mathbf{a}}\}:\;{\mathbf{a}}\in A\}
which means that
|{\mathcal{R}}|=|A|
. Thus, from now on, assume that
e>0
. Since
A
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
, Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{e}(A)
is not contained in a hyperplane and then
\dim\psi_{e}(A)=\binom{e+2}{2}-1
. Applying (
13
) to
\psi_{e}(A)
and
\binom{e+2}{2}-1
, we get that

(15)

\left|{\mathcal{S}}_{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}(\psi_{e}(A))\right|\geq\frac{1}{2^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}}|\psi_{e}(A)|=\frac{1}{2^{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}}|A|.

For each
R\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}(\psi_{e}(A))
, we have that
\dim R=\binom{e+2}{2}-1
so
R
is not contained in a hyperplane, and hence, by Remark
2.4
.ii,
\psi_{e}^{-1}(R)
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
. Thus the map

{\mathcal{S}}_{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}(\psi_{e}(A))\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}},\qquad R\mapsto\psi_{e}^{-1}(R)

is well defined and injective yielding that

|{\mathcal{R}}|\geq\left|{\mathcal{S}}_{\binom{e+2}{2}-1}(\psi_{e}(A))\right|,

and finally (
15
) implies the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
f\in[0,d-1]
,
C_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{d-f}
and
B_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\binom{f+2}{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus C_{0})
be such that
B_{0}
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
. Take
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\binom{d+2}{2}-3}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
such that
B\supseteq B_{0}
and
B\cap C_{0}=B\setminus B_{0}
. Then, for any
e\in[d-f,d]
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e}
such that
C\supseteq C_{0}
, we have that

(16)

|B\cap C|<\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-2.

Since
C_{0}\subseteq C
, Remark
2.2
.ii implies that there is
C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e+f-d}
such that
C=C_{0}\cup C_{1}
. In so far as
B_{0}\cap C_{0}=\emptyset
, notice that

(17)

B_{0}\cap C=B_{0}\cap(C_{0}\cup C_{1})=B_{0}\cap C_{1}\subseteq C_{1}.

If (
16
) is false, then

(18)

|B_{0}\setminus C|\leq|B\setminus C|=|B|-|B\cap C|\leq\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1.

If
e=d
, then (
18
) implies that
B_{0}\subseteq C=C_{0}\cup C_{1}
; hence, inasmuch as
B_{0}\cap C_{0}=\emptyset
, we get that
B_{0}\subseteq C_{1}
but this contradicts that
B_{0}
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
. If
e<d
, then, by (
18
), Remark
2.2
.iii yields the existence of
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
such that

(19)

B_{0}\setminus C\subseteq C_{2}.

C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq e+f-d}
and
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}

C_{1}\cup C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}

2.2
.i. Notice that

17
) and (
19
); however, this contradicts that
B_{0}
is not contained in an element of
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq f}
and it proves (
16
).
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
e\in[1,d-1]
,
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
and
B\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
.

i)

If
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)=\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1
, then
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)=\binom{d+2}{2}-1
.

ii)

If
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)=\binom{d-e+2}{2}-2
, then
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)=\binom{d+2}{2}-2
.

Proof.

First we show i). Assume that
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)<\binom{d+2}{2}-1
so
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)
is contained in a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
. Then Remark
2.4
.ii implies that there is
C_{1}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
such that
B\cup C\subseteq C_{1}
; in particular,
C\subseteq C_{1}
and then Remark
2.2
.ii yields the existence of
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
such that
C_{1}=C\cup C_{2}
. In so far as
B\cup C\subseteq C_{1}=C\cup C_{2}
, we get that
B\setminus C\subseteq C_{2}
. Inasmuch as
C_{2}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
, Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)
is contained in a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1}
and therefore
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)<\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1
, which proves i).

Now we prove ii). Assume that
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)\neq\binom{d+2}{2}-2
and we will show that

(20)

\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)\neq\binom{d-e+2}{2}-2.

We have to deal with two cases.

\bullet

Suppose that
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)>\binom{d+2}{2}-2
so
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)=\binom{d+2}{2}-1
. We claim that

(21)

\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)=\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1.

Indeed, if (
21
) is false, there is a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1}
which contains
\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)
. Then, by Remark
2.4
.ii, there is a curve
C^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
such that
B\setminus C\subseteq C^{\prime}
. Hence

B\cup C=(B\setminus C)\cup C\subseteq C^{\prime}\cup C

with
C^{\prime}\cup C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
by Remark
2.2
.i. This means that
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)
is contained in a hyperplane by Remark
2.4
.ii, and thus
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)<\binom{d+2}{2}-1
. This contradiction proves (
21
).

\bullet

Suppose that
\dim\psi_{d}(B\cup C)<\binom{d+2}{2}-2
. On the one hand, there are infinitely many hyperplanes in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d+2}{2}-1}
containing
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)
. On the other hand, for each curve
C^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d}
, there are only finitely many
[p(x,y)]\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]/\sim
such that
C^{\prime}={\mathcal{Z}}(p(x,y))
by Lemma
2.3
. Therefore we can choose
p_{1}(x,y),p_{2}(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}_{d}[x,y]
such that
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)\subseteq\tau_{d}([p_{1}(x,y)])
,
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)\subseteq\tau_{d}([p_{2}(x,y)])
and
{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{1}(x,y))\neq{\mathcal{Z}}(p_{2}(x,y))
; write
C_{1}:={\mathcal{Z}}(p_{1}(x,y))
and
C_{2}:={\mathcal{Z}}(p_{2}(x,y))
. Since
\psi_{d}(B\cup C)\subseteq\tau_{d}([p_{1}(x,y)])
, Remark
2.4
.ii implies that
B\cup C\subseteq C_{1}
. Then Remark
2.2
.ii yields the existence of
C_{3}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
such that
C_{1}=C\cup C_{3}
. Since
B\cup C\subseteq C_{1}=C\cup C_{3}
, we get that
B\setminus C\subseteq C_{3}
. In so far as
C_{3}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
, Remark
2.4
.ii implies that there is a hyperplane
H_{3}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1}
such that
\psi_{d-e}^{-1}(H_{3})=C_{3}
and
\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)\subseteq H_{3}
. Proceeding in the same way with
C_{2}
, there exist
C_{4}\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq d-e}
and a hyperplane
H_{4}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1}
such that
C_{2}=C\cup C_{4}
,
\psi_{d-e}^{-1}(H_{4})=C_{4}
and
\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)\subseteq H_{4}
. Since
C_{1}\neq C_{2}
, notice that
C_{3}\neq C_{4}
and therefore
H_{3}\neq H_{4}
. Now, since

\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)\subseteq H_{3}\cap H_{4},

we get that
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)<\binom{d-e+2}{2}-2
.

3.
Families
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)

The purpose of this section is to define and prove some properties of the families
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
which will be fundamental objects in the proof of the main results of this paper.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
and
A\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
. We denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A)
the family of subsets
B\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\binom{d+2}{2}-3}(A)
which have following four properties:

i)

\dim\psi_{d}(B)=\binom{d+2}{2}-4
.

ii)

For all
e\in[1,d-1]
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
, we have that
|B\cap C|<\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}
.

iii)

For all
e\in[1,d-1]
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
such that
|B\cap C|=\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1
, we have that
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)=\binom{d-e+2}{2}-3
.

iv)

For all
e\in[1,d-1]
and
C\in{\mathcal{C}}_{e}
such that
|B\cap C|<\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1
, we have that
\dim\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus C)>\binom{d-e+2}{2}-3
.

We will need more notation. For
d\in{\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
,
e\in[1,d-1]
,
A,B,C\in{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
and
D\in{\mathcal{P}}(B)
, write

\displaystyle V_{e}(D)

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{Fl}}(\psi_{e}(D))

\displaystyle W_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:={\mathrm{Fl}}(\psi_{d-e}(B\setminus\psi^{-1}_{e}(V_{e}(D))))

\displaystyle\alpha_{e}(D)

\displaystyle:=\binom{e+2}{2}-2-\dim V_{e}(D)

\displaystyle\beta_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:=\binom{d-e+2}{2}-3-\dim W_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle\gamma_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:=|V_{e}(D)\cap\psi_{e}(B)|

\displaystyle\mu_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if $\alpha_{e}(D)<0$};\\
&\\
\alpha_{e}(D)+\gamma_{e}(B,D)+\binom{d-e+2}{2}&\text{if $\alpha_{e}(D)\geq 0$}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\tau_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{if $\min\{\alpha_{e}(D),\beta_{e}(B,D)\}<0$ or}\\
&\text{$\gamma_{e}(B,D)>\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1$};\\
&\\
\alpha_{e}(D)+\beta_{e}(B,D)+|B|+2&\text{if $\min\{\alpha_{e}(D),\beta_{e}(B,D)\}\geq 0$ and}\\
&\text{$\gamma_{e}(B,D)=\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1$};\\
&\\
\alpha_{e}(D)+\beta_{e}(B,D)+|B|+3&\text{if $\min\{\alpha_{e}(D),\beta_{e}(B,D)\}\geq 0$ and}\\
&\text{$\gamma_{e}(B,D)<\binom{d+2}{2}-\binom{d-e+2}{2}-1$}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle U_{e}(B,D)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi_{d}^{-1}(V_{d}(B))&\text{if $\alpha_{e}(D)<0$};\\
&\\
\psi_{d}^{-1}(V_{d}(B))\cup\psi_{e}^{-1}(V_{e}(D))&\text{if $\beta_{e}(B,D)<0\leq\alpha_{e}(D)$};\\
&\\
\psi_{d}^{-1}(V_{d}(B))\cup\psi_{e}^{-1}(V_{e}(D))\cup\psi_{d-e}^{-1}(W_{e}(B,D))&\text{if $\beta_{e}(B,D),\alpha_{e}(D)\geq 0$}.\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle I(B,C)

\displaystyle:=\{(f,E)\in[1,d-1]\times{\mathcal{P}}(B):\;C\nsubseteq U_{f}(B,E)\}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A,B,C)

\displaystyle:=\{E\in{\mathcal{N}}_{d}(A):\;B\in{\mathcal{P}}(E)\text{ and }E\cap C=E\setminus B\}.

The following facts are direct consequences of the definitions.

Lemma 3.7
.

,

.

Non-Relativistic Scenario of a Free Particle in an Infinite Cylindrical Potential Well
Pratik Adarsh, Sabyasachi Ghosh

Abstract
There are various types of infinite potential well problems occurring in elementary quantum mechanics formalism. The infinite square well (one dimensional), cubical box and, spherical well are quite common in textbooks. In this paper, we consider a rather uncommon potential well, infinite cylindrical well, and try to find its energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions using Schrödinger equation. These results are also derived in the paper [ 1 ] . But our approach here is to elaborate every step just the way Griffiths did in his book [ 2 ] for hydrogen atom, using spherical potential well. We also plot some radial wavefunctions and density plots.



\justify

1
Introduction

In infinite spherical potential well, we readily solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation to get energy eigenvalues and eigenfunctions. In this problem of infinite cylindrical well, we proceed with the same approach by solving the time-independent Schrödinger equation in cylindrical coordinates.

The motivation is to see how cylindrical symmetry differs from spherical one and to what extend its analytical solutions are possible in the most general way.

2
The Problem

Figure 1:
Infinite cylindrical well.

We have a particle inside a cylindrical-shaped well for which the potential is zero inside the boundaries but infinite at and outside the boundaries.

The dimensional attributes of the well are:

Height of the cylinder =
H

Radius of the cylinder =
a

With these we have to solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation:

{\frac{-{\hbar}^{2}}{2m}}{{\nabla}^{2}}{\psi}+{V\psi}=E\psi

3
The Approach

3.1
Separation of Variables

The Laplacian in cylindrical polar coordinates is:

{\nabla^{2}}={\frac{1}{r}}{\frac{\partial}{\partial r}}\left({r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right)+{\frac{1}{r^{2}}}{\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}}+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}

The wavefunction is zero outside the well, so the Schrödinger equation inside(
V=0
) the well reads:

\frac{-\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial z^{2}}\right]=E\psi

(3.1.1)

We let the solutions separable into products:

\psi(r,\phi,z)=\psi_{r\phi}(r,\phi)\psi_{z}(z)

Substituting this into equation
3.1.1
,we have

\frac{-\hbar^{2}}{2m}\left[\frac{\psi_{z}}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r\frac{\partial\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial r}\right)+\frac{\psi_{z}}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial\phi^{2}}+\psi_{r\phi}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{z}}{\partial z^{2}}\right]=E\psi_{r\phi}\psi_{z}

(3.1.2)

Rearranging terms, we get

\underbrace{\frac{1}{\psi_{r\phi}}\left[\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{r\phi}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial\phi^{2}}\right]}_{\text{depends on }r\text{ and }\phi}+\underbrace{\frac{1}{\psi_{z}}\left[\frac{d^{2}\psi_{z}}{dz^{2}}\right]}_{\text{depends on }z}=-\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}

(3.1.3)

In the L.H.S of equation
3.1.3
, the first term depends only on
r
and
\phi
. Whereas the second term depends only on
z
. So we let the first term equals some factor
A_{1}
(say)and the second term equals to some another factor
A_{2}
. With these, the equation
3.1.3
reads as:

A_{1}+A_{2}=\frac{-2mE}{\hbar^{2}}

(3.1.4)

This equation
3.1.4
is the main equation that would give us the energy eigenvalues for the system. Our aim would be now to find these factors,
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
.

Coming back to equation
3.1.3
, we write the first term on the L.H.S as:

\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial^{2}\Psi_{r\phi}}{\partial r^{2}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi_{r\phi}}{\partial\phi^{2}}=A_{1}\psi_{r\phi}

(3.1.5)

This is just another partial differential equation we have to solve for proceeding in the right direction. We let the solutions separable like previously:

\psi_{r\phi}(r,\phi)=R(r)X(\phi)

Substituting this into equation
3.1.5
, we have

\frac{X}{r}\frac{dR}{dr}+X\frac{d^{2}R}{dr^{2}}+\frac{R}{r^{2}}\frac{d^{2}X}{d\phi^{2}}=A_{1}RX

(3.1.6)

Rearranging terms, we get

\underbrace{\left[\frac{r}{R}\frac{dR}{dr}+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\frac{d^{2}R}{dr^{2}}-A_{1}r^{2}\right]}_{\text{depends on }r}+\underbrace{\left[\frac{1}{X}\frac{d^{2}X}{d\phi^{2}}\right]}_{\text{depends on }\phi}=0

(3.1.7)

The first term on L.H.S of the equation
3.1.7
depends only on
r
and the second term depends only on
\phi
. So we let the first term equals to some factor
B
(say), then obviously the second term equals to
-B
.

With these attributes, we have:

\frac{r}{R}\frac{dR}{dr}+\frac{r^{2}}{R}\frac{d^{2}R}{dr^{2}}-A_{1}r^{2}=B

(3.1.8)

\frac{1}{X}\frac{d^{2}X}{d\phi^{2}}=-B

(3.1.9)

And from equations
3.1.3
and
3.1.4

\frac{1}{\psi_{z}}\left[\frac{d^{2}\psi_{z}}{dz^{2}}\right]=A_{2}

(3.1.10)

Y_{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{437263}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\sqrt{B}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{437263}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\sqrt{B}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{387787}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\sqrt{B}$}}}}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{\resizebox{387786}{327680}{\hbox{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sqrt{B}$}}}}}}\left(0\right)\,\to\,\infty

3.3
The Angular Equation

The equation
3.1.9
is:

\frac{1}{X}\frac{d^{2}X}{d\phi^{2}}=-B

Its solution can be written as:

X(\phi)=C_{3}\exp\left(i\sqrt{B}\phi\right)+C_{4}\exp\left(-i\sqrt{B}\phi\right)

(3.3.1)

For rotational symmetry, we must have:

X(\phi+2\pi)=X(\phi)

\implies C_{3}\exp\left(i\sqrt{B}(\phi+2\pi)\right)+C_{4}\exp\left(-i\sqrt{B}(\phi+2\pi)\right)=C_{3}\exp\left(i\sqrt{B}\phi\right)+C_{4}\exp\left(-i\sqrt{B}\phi\right)

Simplifying and equating real and imaginary parts, we get:

\left(C_{3}+C_{4}\right)\left[\cos(\sqrt{B}\phi+2\pi\sqrt{B})\right]=\left(C_{3}+C_{4}\right)\cos(\sqrt{B}\phi)

and

\left(C_{3}-C_{4}\right)\left[\sin(\sqrt{B}\phi+2\pi\sqrt{B})\right]=\left(C_{3}-C_{4}\right)\sin(\sqrt{B}\phi)

Therefore
\sqrt{B}
is an integer, for
C_{3}+C_{4}\neq 0
and
C_{3}\neq C_{4}
. Hence we have

\sqrt{B}=n_{\phi}=0,1,2,...

(3.3.2)

where
n_{\phi}
denotes a quantum number associated with azimuthal angle.

3.4
The Equation along Z Direction

The equation
3.1.10
is:

\frac{1}{\psi_{z}}\left[\frac{d^{2}\psi_{z}}{dz^{2}}\right]=A_{2}

Its solution can be written as:

\psi_{z}(z)=C_{5}\exp\left(iz\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}\right)+C_{6}\exp\left(-iz\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}\right)

(3.4.1)

Now we have the following boundary conditions:

(
i.
)

\psi_{z}(0)=0

(
ii.
)

\psi_{z}(H)=0

Imposing the first condition on the equation
3.4.1
, we have:

C_{6}=-C_{5}

With this and by second boundary condition, the equation
3.4.1
becomes:

C_{5}\exp\left(iz\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}\right)-C_{5}\exp\left(-iz\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}\right)=0

\implies 2iC_{5}\left[\sin\left(H\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}\right)\right]=0

\implies H\sqrt{\frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+A_{1}}=n_{z}\pi\hskip 5.0pt;n_{z}=1,2,3,...

(3.4.2)

Where
n_{z}
denotes a quantum number associated with z-direction.

Using equations
3.4.2
and
3.1.4
, we have:

A_{2}=\frac{-n_{z}^{2}\pi^{2}}{H^{2}}

(3.4.3)

Therefore we have energy expression redefined as:

E=\frac{\hbar^{2}(n_{z}^{2}\pi^{2}-A_{1}H^{2})}{2mH^{2}}

(3.4.4)

The Normalization Technique

Before finding the wavefunctions, we emphasize that how normalization can be done separately for each of them.

In cylindrical coordinates, we have elementary volume as:

dV=rdrd\phi dz

So normalization of wavefunction in the view of equation
3.6.1
becomes

\iiint_{V}\psi(r,\phi,z)\psi^{*}(r,\phi,z){rdrd\phi dz}=\int_{0}^{a}R(r)R^{*}(r)rdr\int_{0}^{2\pi}X(\phi)X^{*}(\phi)d\phi\int_{0}^{H}\psi_{z}(z)\psi_{z}^{*}(z)dz=1

We can normalize each wavefunctions separately as follows:

(
i.
)

\int_{0}^{a}R(r)R^{*}(r)rdr=1

(3.6.2)

(
ii.
)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}X(\phi)X^{*}(\phi)d\phi=1

(3.6.3)

(
iii.
)

\int_{0}^{H}\psi_{z}(z)\psi_{z}^{*}(z)dz=1

(3.6.4)

5
Conclusions

We can see from energy eigenvalues expression, equation
3.5.3
, that the energy of a free particle in an infinite cylindrical well depends on three quantum numbers associated with three independent directions. But the eigenfunctions, given by equation
3.6.16
, depend on an extra quantum number
p
. For each
n_{\phi}
, there are two values of
p
. Thus all excited states are 2-fold degenerate.

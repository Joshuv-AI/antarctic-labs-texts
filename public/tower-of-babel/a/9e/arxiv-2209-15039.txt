Abstract
. We construct a canonical stabilizer reduction X ~ \widetilde{X} for any derived 1 1 -algebraic stack X X over ℂ \mathbb{C} as a sequence of derived Kirwan blow-ups, under mild natural conditions that include the existence of a good moduli space for the classical truncation X cl X_{\mathrm{cl}} . Our construction has several desired features: it naturally generalizes Kirwan’s classical partial desingularization algorithm to the context of derived algebraic geometry, preserves quasi-smoothness, and is a derived enhancement of the intrinsic stabilizer reduction constructed by Kiem, Li and the third author. Moreover, if X X is ( − 1 ) (-1) -shifted symplectic, we show that the semi-perfect and almost perfect obstruction theory of X ~ cl \widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}} and the associated virtual fundamental cycle and virtual structure sheaf, constructed by the same authors, are naturally induced by X ~ \widetilde{X} and its derived tangent complex. As corollaries, we define virtual classes for moduli stacks of semistable sheaves on surfaces, give a fully derived perspective on generalized Donaldson–Thomas invariants of Calabi–Yau threefolds and define new generalized Vafa–Witten invariants for surfaces via Kirwan blow-ups.



Introduction

Some brief history

It has long been understood that in order to effectively study geometric objects and their families, one needs to enlarge the category of algebraic varieties and consider the more general notion of algebraic stacks. While the theory of algebraic stacks now enjoys a vast literature and features prominently within algebraic geometry and beyond, the presence of automorphisms of objects, also called stabilizers, which is typically a desirable feature, can sometimes be problematic regarding the use of standard techniques that apply to algebraic varieties, e.g., (co)homological methods like integration.

A very useful way to associate an algebraic space to an Artin stack is to consider its moduli space, whenever this makes sense. However, depending on the scope of one’s considerations, this might not be adequate, given that it is not necessarily a well-behaved operation. For example, a smooth stack need not have a smooth moduli space.

Stabilizer reduction refers to the process of resolving the stackiness of an algebraic stack to produce a canonical Deligne–Mumford stack, whose stabilizers are all finite. In classical algebraic geometry, Kirwan
[
Kir85
]
was the first to carry this out for smooth quotient stacks
X=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
obtained by Geometric Invariant Theory
[
MFK94
]
, namely by an action of a reductive group
G
on a smooth projective variety
Q
together with a linearization.

More precisely (assuming that
Q^{s}\neq\emptyset
), Kirwan produced a sequence of GIT quotient stacks

X_{0}=X=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G],\ X_{1}=[Q_{1}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G],\ \dots\ ,\ \widetilde{X}\coloneqq X_{n}=[\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]

by iteratively blowing up
X_{i}
along the (smooth) locus
X_{i}^{{\mathrm{max}}}\subseteq X_{i}
of points with maximal dimensional stabilizer and then deleting unstable points by a careful study of stability on the blow-up. The final result
\widetilde{X}=[\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
is a GIT quotient Deligne–Mumford stack satisfying
\widetilde{Q}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\widetilde{Q}^{s}
. At the level of GIT quotients, each morphism
X_{i+1}\to X_{i}
fits into a natural commutative square

\textstyle{X_{i+1}=[Q_{i+1}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{i}=[Q_{i}^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{i+1}=Q_{i+1}/\!\!/G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{i}=Q_{i}/\!\!/G}

where the lower horizontal arrow is a birational blow-up map. Thus, since
\widetilde{Y}
only has finite quotient singularities and admits a projective, birational map
\widetilde{Y}\to Y
, it is a partial desingularization of
Y
. Kirwan’s construction has had a wealth of applications, see for example
[
Kir86
,
KL04
,
Kie07
]
.

Subsequently, Kirwan’s construction has been independently generalized by Edidin–Rydh
[
ER21
]
, and Kiem–Li and the third author
[
KLS17
,
Sav20
]
, to apply to (possibly singular) Artin stacks
X
with a good moduli space
q\colon X\to Y
in the sense of Alper
[
Alp13
]
. Such stacks are a natural generalization of GIT quotient stacks
X=[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
with good moduli space morphism
q\colon[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]\to Q/\!\!/G
. In fact, by deep results of Alper–Hall–Rydh
[
AHR19
,
AHR20
]
, the GIT picture is the étale local model for this general class of stacks.

The two constructions, while related, differ in the notion of blow-up being applied at each iterated step: Edidin–Rydh introduce a saturated blow-up by first blowing up
X
along
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
and then carefully deleting a closed substack of unstable points, whereas Kiem–Li and the third author use a so-called Kirwan blow-up by first taking an intrinsic blow-up of
X
along
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
and then deleting unstable points. The intrinsic blow-up was constructed using local embeddings into smooth quotient stacks and preserved Kuranishi models. It was thus expected to be the classical shadow of a derived blow-up.

Derived stabilizer reduction: statement of results

In this paper, we generalize Kirwan’s algorithm to the setting of derived algebraic stacks
X
whose classical truncation
X_{\mathrm{cl}}
admits a good moduli space
q\colon X_{\mathrm{cl}}\to Y
. By the hidden smoothness principle, derived stacks should behave in analogy with smooth classical stacks, and hence it is natural to expect that a derived stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{X}
should be the result of repeatedly blowing up
X
along
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
and then deleting unstable points. This is exactly what we do.

Our first result proves the existence of

in the derived setting, under mild finiteness assumptions which we leave implicit here and in the rest of the introduction. Uniqueness refers to uniqueness up to a contractible space of homotopies.

With

-shifted symplectic stacks and obtain associated virtual fundamental classes. These constructions have natural applications towards enumerative invariants of sheaves on surfaces and threefolds.

Quasi-smoothness and invariants of Donaldson-type

Using the properties of derived blow-ups and the definition of derived intrinsic and Kirwan blow-ups, we show that they preserve the property of being quasi-smooth (see Proposition
7.4
). We thus obtain the following theorem.

Theorem

(Theorem
7.1
)
.

The derived stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{X}
of a quasi-smooth derived Artin stack
X
is quasi-smooth. In particular, the intrinsic stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}
admits a natural virtual fundamental class
[\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{\ast}(\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}})
and virtual structure sheaf
[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\in K_{0}(\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}})
.

Quasi-smooth derived Artin stacks naturally appear as derived moduli stacks
{\mathcal{M}}
of semistable sheaves or perfect complexes on smooth, projective surfaces. There are several variations of how one can obtain such stacks, e.g., by considering reduced versions when the genus of the surface is positive, or fixing the determinant. For more details, the interested reader can consult
[
Moc09
]
or
[
Joy21
]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a quasi-smooth moduli stack of this type such that the good moduli space
M_{\mathrm{cl}}
of

is proper. Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack and integrals

may be viewed as Donaldson-type invariants (see Definition

Future directions

Given Kirwan’s original algorithm and its generalization in
[
ER21
]
, it is natural to wonder whether there is a theory of derived good moduli spaces for derived algebraic stacks, such that, in particular, our derived stabilizer reduction procedure gives a sequence of diagrams

\textstyle{X_{i+1}=\widehat{X}_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{i}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{i+1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{i},}

where the vertical maps are derived good moduli space morphisms, and which are thus derived enhancements of the diagrams (
Theorem
). In fact, using the derived étale slice theorem and local-to-global methods, one can indeed construct derived good moduli spaces whose existence is preserved by the operation of derived Kirwan blow-up. The details will appear elsewhere to keep the length of this paper under control.

Another interesting direction of inquiry would be to investigate the properties and degeneracy of the pullback of an
n
-shifted symplectic form to the derived stabilizer reduction and possibly develop a theory of
n
-shifted quasi-symplectic derived stacks, of which derived stabilizer reductions are a particular case.

Other natural extensions of this work would be to generalize the stabilizer reduction algorithm to derived stacks over a base of positive or even mixed characteristic. Since our main arguments ultimately depend on the classical situation and on the formal properties of Weil restrictions, we in fact expect a generalized stabilizer reduction algorithm to exist in any nonconnective algebraic geometry. Such geometries are extensions of derived algebraic geometry—of which derived analytic geometry in the sense of
[
BK17
]
,
[
BM22
]
,
[
BKK
]
is an example—and are proposed by Ben-Bassat and the first author in
[
BH23
]
, together with a generalization of derived blow-ups to this setting.

Finally, there are several purely enumerative potential ramifications for sheaf-theoretic invariants that remain to be explored.

We intend to come back to these questions in the future.

Notation and conventions

Throughout, we work over
\mathbb{C}
. All rings and algebras are assumed to be commutative. We will use both simplicial/derived rings and commutative differential graded algebras interchangeably, assuming their usual equivalence under the Dold–Kan correspondence. The particular model we are working with will be clear from context.

All derived stacks and all good moduli space morphisms are assumed to have affine diagonal. By a derived algebraic stack we mean a derived
1
-algebraic stack.

The classical truncation of

For a morphism

to denote

Quasi-coherent modules and algebras are written as

1.1.
Background on
\infty

Recall that an
\infty

We use similar shorthands for

Write

for the

Let

The functor

induces adjunctions

\displaystyle\pi_{0}:\mathsf{Mod}_{\geq 0}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{Mod}^{\heartsuit}:i

\displaystyle\pi_{0}:\mathsf{Alg}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{Alg}^{\heartsuit}:i

-vector spaces, and

-algebras.

For

1.3.
Cell attachments

Let

. For

Let a sequence

of elements

be given. Then we define the

of

by

, via the pushout
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R[u_{1},\dots,u_{k}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R/(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

where the

, and

A finite quotient of the form

.

1.4.
Standard forms

For the purpose of performing computations, it will be useful to work with certain
1
-categorical models.

Recall that a morphism
A\to B
of algebras (and the corresponding map on spectra) is
locally of finite presentation
if
B
is a compact object in
\mathsf{Alg}_{A}
, i.e., the
\mathsf{Spc}
-valued functor
\mathsf{Alg}_{A}(B,-)
preserves filtered colimits. It is
finitely presented
if

can be obtained from

by a finite number of cell attachments. Then the following are equivalent: (1)
B
is locally of finite presentation over
A
; (2) the map
\Spec B\to\Spec A
is, Zariski-locally on
\Spec B
, finitely presented; and (3)
B
is a retract of a finitely presented
A
-algebra.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A
be an algebra. A cdga model
R
for

is
in standard form
if
R_{0}
is smooth with
\Omega_{R_{0}}
free, and the underlying graded-commutative ring of
R
is freely generated over
R_{0}
by a finite number of generators.

Remark 1.5
.

Observe, if a cdga
R
is in standard form, then it can be obtained from
R_{0}
by a finite number of cell-attachments, say
R(0)\to R(1)\to\dots\to R(n)
, in such a way that when we terminate the process at

, then

is also in standard form. In fact, the underlying graded-commutative ring of
R(k)
is the subring of the underlying graded-commutative ring of
R
generated by elements of homogeneous degree
\leq k
, and
R(k+1)=R(k)/(\sigma)
where
\sigma\colon M_{k+1}[k]\to R(k)
and
M_{k+1}
is a free module of finite rank over
R(k)
. Moreover
R_{k+1}=R(k)_{k+1}\oplus(M_{k+1})_{0}
. We say that
R
is
generated
by the
M_{k+1}
.

If
R
is a model of standard form for
A
, then the cotangent complex of
\Spec A
is given by the Kähler differentials
\Omega_{R}
, see
[
BBJ19
, §2.3]
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
x\in\Spec A
with

be given. Then a model

in standard form for

is
minimal at

if the differentials of

are all zero.

By the following lemma, locally finitely presented derived schemes can Zariski-locally be described by models in standard form.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
A\in\mathsf{Alg}
be locally of finite presentation and
x\in\Spec A
. Then, up to Zariski localizing around

, we can find a model

in standard form for

such that

is a finitely generated polynomial ring, or such that it is minimal at

Proof.

This follows for example from
[
BBJ19
, Theorem 4.1]
.
∎

Remark 1.8
.

In general, the two conditions cannot be met simultaneously, see
[
BBJ19
, Remark 3.2]
.

1.5.
Graded derived rings

Derived blow-ups in arbitrary centers are defined along similar lines as the classical construction—namely, through a derived version of the Rees algebra, of which one then takes the projective spectrum. To explain this, let us first review graded algebras in the derived setting as defined in
[
Hek21
]
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a commutative monoid, considered as a discrete symmetric monoidal category in the obvious way. Let
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}
be the functor
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbb{M},\mathsf{Mod})
, endowed with symmetric monoidal structure via Day convolution and with
t
-structure inherited from
\mathsf{Mod}
. An object
M
of
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}
is called an
\mathbb{M}
-graded module
, written as
\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{M}}M_{d}
, where
M_{d}
is
M
evaluated at
d
.

For
d\in\mathbb{M}
and
N\in\mathsf{Mod}
, we write
N(d)
for the
\mathbb{M}
-graded module which is
N
concentrated in homogeneous degree
-d
. Let then
(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}})^{0}
be the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}
generated by finite coproducts of modules of the form
N(d)
, where
N\in\mathsf{Mod}^{0}
and
d\in\mathbb{M}
. Then
(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}})^{0}
generates
(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}})_{\geq 0}
under sifted colimits.

Define the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{M}
-graded algebras
, written
\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}
, as the
\infty
-category of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras in
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}
. As in the ungraded case, the forgetful functor
\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}\to(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}})_{\geq 0}
has a left adjoint, written
\mathrm{LSym}^{\mathbb{M}}
. Let
\mathsf{Poly}^{\mathbb{M}}
be the essential image of
\mathrm{LSym}^{\mathbb{M}}
evaluated on
(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}})^{0}
. Then the canonical map
\mathsf{P}_{\Sigma}(\mathsf{Poly}^{\mathbb{M}})\to\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}
is an equivalence.

A key result from
[
Hek21
]
is that, as expected, the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is (contravariantly) equivalent to the
\infty
-category of affine derived schemes with
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action. The general theory of
\infty
-actions is reviewed in §
2.1
, and we give an in-depth analysis of
G
actions on derived stacks for reductive algebraic groups
G
in §
3
.

For
B\in\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}
, write
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}_{B}
for the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{M}
-graded
B
-modules
, which by definition are
B
-modules in
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}
. As in the ungraded case,
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}_{B}
is stable and symmetric monoidal, and
(\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}_{B})_{\geq 0}
is generated under sifted colimits by the
\infty
-category of finitely generated, free,
\mathbb{M}
-graded
B
-modules. Once again,
\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}_{B}\coloneqq(\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}})_{B/}
is generated under sifted colimits by the finitely generated,
\mathbb{M}
-graded polynomial
B
-algebras, which are defined similarly as in the ungraded case.

We are primarily interested in
\mathbb{N}
-graded and
\mathbb{Z}
-graded rings. These exhibit familiar behavior: to a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring
B
we can associate an
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring
B_{\geq 0}
by discarding the pieces in negative homogeneous degrees, and an
\mathbb{N}
-graded ring
B^{\prime}
can be promoted to a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring by putting
0
in negative homogeneous degrees. We can also take the homogeneous degree-zero part
B_{0}
of a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded ring, which gives us morphisms
B_{0}\to B_{\geq 0}\to B
of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded rings (where we think of
B_{0},B_{\geq 0}
as
\mathbb{Z}
-graded by the procedure just described, which we suppress from notation). If
B
is
\mathbb{N}
-graded, we also have a morphism
B\to B_{0}
such that the composition
B_{0}\to B\to B_{0}
is homotopic to the identity.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be either
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
, and let
B\in\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{M}}
. As in the ungraded case, we can adjoin a free variable to
B
in homological degree
n\in\mathbb{N}
and homogeneous degree
d\in\mathbb{M}
, which produces an
\mathbb{M}
-graded
B
-algebra

with the universal property that the space of
\mathbb{M}
-graded
B
-algebra maps
B[u]\to C
is equivalent to the space of maps
(S^{n},*)\to(C_{d},0)
, i.e., to
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{M}}(B[n]_{d},C)
, where
B[n]_{d}
is the homogeneous degree-
d
part of the module
B[n]
.

The
\mathbb{M}
-grading on
B
induces an
\mathbb{M}
-grading on each
\pi_{n}(B)
. We can thus define a
finite quotient
of
B
by a sequence
\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{k})
of elements
\sigma_{i}\in\pi_{n_{i}}(B)_{d_{i}}
, with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
d_{i}\in\mathbb{M}
, using the graded free variables construction just described and performing cell attachments in the same way as in the ungraded case. The result is again written as
B/(\underline{\sigma})
.

1.6.
Derived stacks

Let
\mathbf{P}
be one of the following properties: étale, a (Zariski) open immersion, smooth. A morphism
A\to B
of derived rings is called
\mathbf{P}
if
\pi_{0}A\to\pi_{0}B
is
\mathbf{P}
, and the morphism
(\pi_{n}A)\otimes_{\pi_{0}A}(\pi_{0}B)\to\pi_{n}B
is an isomorphism, for any
n\geq 0
. Define the
\infty
-category of
affine derived schemes
as
\mathsf{Aff}\coloneqq\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathrm{op}}
, and write
\Spec(-)\colon\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Aff}
for the obvious functor. Declare morphisms in
\mathsf{Aff}
to be
\mathbf{P}
if the corresponding map in
\mathsf{Alg}
is
\mathbf{P}
. For
U=\Spec A
in
\mathsf{Aff}
, the
underlying classical scheme
is
U_{\mathrm{cl}}\coloneqq\Spec\pi_{0}A
.

We endow
\mathsf{Aff}
with the
étale topology
by declaring a family
\{U_{i}\to U\}_{i}
in
\mathsf{Aff}
to be a cover if all
U_{i}\to U
are étale, and the induced maps on classical schemes
(U_{i})_{\mathrm{cl}}\to U_{\mathrm{cl}}
are jointly surjective.

A
derived prestack
is a functor
X\colon\mathsf{Aff}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Spc}
. A
derived stack
is a derived prestack which is a sheaf (in the
\infty
-categorical sense) for the étale topology. We write
\mathsf{Stk}
for the
\infty
-category of derived stacks. For
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
, we write
\mathsf{Stk}_{X}
for the
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Stk}_{/X}
of derived stacks over
X
. The Yoneda embedding induces an embedding
\Spec(-)\colon\mathsf{Aff}\to\mathsf{Stk}
.

Recall that a space
K
is called
n
-truncated
if
\pi_{m}(K,x)
is trivial for all points
x\in K
and all
m>n
. A stack
X
is called
n
-truncated
if
X(T)
is
n
-truncated for all classical affine schemes
T
. A morphism
X\to Y
of derived stacks is
affine
if
X\times_{Y}(\Spec A)
is affine, for all
\Spec A\to Y
. Then a derived stack
X
has
affine diagonal
if the canonical morphism
\Delta\colon X\to X\times X
is affine. Observe that
X
has affine diagonal if and only if the intersection
U\times_{X}V
is affine, for any pair of affine derived schemes
U,V
over
X
.

Assumption 1.9
.

From here on, assume that all derived stacks are 1-truncated and have affine diagonal, unless otherwise stated.

Let
{\mathrm{cl}\mathsf{Stk}}
be category of classical stacks. Composition with the inclusion
\mathsf{Alg}^{\heartsuit}\to\mathsf{Alg}
gives a functor
(-)_{\mathrm{cl}}\colon\mathsf{Stk}\to{\mathrm{cl}\mathsf{Stk}}
which sends
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
to the
underlying classical stack

X_{\mathrm{cl}}
. This functor has a fully faithful left adjoint
\iota
, and both functors
\iota,(-)_{\mathrm{cl}}
preserve affines. A morphism
X\to Y
of derived stacks is
quasi-compact
/
a closed immersion
if the morphism
X_{\mathrm{cl}}\to Y_{\mathrm{cl}}
is quasi-compact/a closed immersion.

1.7.
Derived schemes

In this and the next paragraph, let
\mathbf{P}
be one of the properties: étale, smooth, an open immersion. Although we could add here any property which is étale-local in an appropriate sense, we restrict ourselves to what we need.

Let
X
be a derived stack. A family
\{U_{i}\to X\}_{i}
of morphisms in
\mathsf{Stk}
is a
cover
if the canonical map
\colim\check{C}(f)\to X
is an equivalence, where
\check{C}(f)
is the Čech nerve of
f\colon\bigsqcup U_{i}\to X
, and the colimit is taken in
\mathsf{Stk}
. A morphism
T\to X
in
\mathsf{Stk}
with affine source
is
\mathbf{P}
if
S\times_{X}T\to S
is
\mathbf{P}
for all affine derived scheme
S
over
X
. The latter is well-defined, since we assume that all derived stacks have affine diagonal.

A derived stack
U
is called a
derived scheme
if there is cover
\{U_{i}\to U\}
of open immersions
U_{i}\to U
by affine derived schemes
U_{i}
. Observe, the adjunction
\iota:{\mathrm{cl}\mathsf{Stk}}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{Stk}:(-)_{\mathrm{cl}}
restricts to an adjunction between classical schemes (which we assume to also have affine diagonal) and derived schemes. The
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Sch}
of derived schemes is closed under fiber products in
\mathsf{Stk}
.

A morphism
U\to X
of derived stacks with
U
a derived scheme
is
\mathbf{P}
if
U\times_{X}V\to V
is
\mathbf{P}
, for any derived scheme
V
over
X
.

1.8.
Derived algebraic spaces and stacks

A
derived algebraic space
is a
0
-truncated derived stack
Y
for which there is an étale cover
U\to Y
by a derived scheme
U
. A morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
of derived stacks is
representable
if for all derived schemes
U
over
X
the fiber product
U\times_{X}X^{\prime}
is a derived algebraic space. Observe that any morphism
Y\to X
from a derived algebraic space to a derived stack is representable. Again, this is because
X
has affine diagonal, and since for any affine morphism
X^{\prime}\to V
in
\mathsf{Stk}
with
V
a derived scheme it holds that
X^{\prime}
is a derived scheme as well.

A morphism
Y^{\prime}\to Y
of algebraic spaces
is
\mathbf{P}
if there is an étale cover
V\to Y^{\prime}
by a derived scheme
V
such that the composition
V\to Y
is
\mathbf{P}
. A representable morphism
X^{\prime}\to X
of derived stacks
is
\mathbf{P}
if for any derived scheme
U
over
X
, the map
U\times_{X}X^{\prime}\to U
is
\mathbf{P}
.

A
derived algebraic stack
is a derived stack
X
such that there is a smooth cover
Y\to X
with
Y
a derived algebraic space. Observe, the adjunction
\iota:{\mathrm{cl}\mathsf{Stk}}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{Stk}:(-)_{\mathrm{cl}}
restricts to an adjunction between classical algebraic stacks and derived algebraic stacks, and also between classical algebraic spaces and derived algebraic spaces.

Let
X^{\prime}\to X
be a morphism of algebraic stacks. Then
X^{\prime}\to X
is
smooth
if there is a smooth cover
Y^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
by a derived algebraic space
Y^{\prime}
such that the composition
Y^{\prime}\to X
is smooth. Likewise,
X^{\prime}\to X
is
étale
if, smooth-locally on
X
, there is an étale cover
Y^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
by a derived algebraic space
Y^{\prime}
, such that the composition
Y^{\prime}\to X
is étale. Finally,
X^{\prime}\to X
is an
open immersion
if it is representable, étale and a monomophism.

1.9.
Quasi-coherent modules and algebras

Let
f\colon\Spec B\to\Spec A
be a morphism of affine derived schemes. Then we have an adjunction
f^{*}:\mathsf{Mod}_{A}\leftrightarrows\mathsf{Mod}_{B}:f_{*}
, where
f^{*}
sends
M\in\mathsf{Mod}_{A}
to
M\otimes_{A}B
. This gives us a functor
\mathsf{Aff}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Cat}
that sends
\Spec R
to
\mathsf{Mod}_{R}
.
Taking the right Kan extension yields the functor

\displaystyle\mathsf{Stk}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Cat}\colon X\mapsto\QCoh(X)

which sends

to the
\infty
-category of

\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules
. Concretely, for
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
, we can write
\QCoh(X)
as the limit of the diagram
\Spec R\mapsto\mathsf{Mod}_{R}
over the comma
\infty
-category
\mathsf{Aff}^{\mathrm{op}}_{/X}
consisting of derived affine schemes over
X
. This description tells us that we can think of a quasi-cohorent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
as a homotopy coherent diagram
\{\mathcal{F}_{A}\}_{\Spec A\to X}
, where each
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is in
\mathsf{Mod}_{A}
. We also write
\mathcal{F}(A)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}(\Spec A)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}_{A}
.

For
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
algebraic, we can restrict the indexing category in the formula
\QCoh(X)\simeq\lim_{\Spec R\to X}\mathsf{Mod}_{R}
to derived affine schemes which are smooth over
X
, and still get the same result. Then
\QCoh(X)
is naturally a stable, presentable, symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category for which the tensor product commutes with colimits in each variable separately, and moreover
\QCoh(X)
carries a
t
-structure such that
\mathcal{F}\in\QCoh(X)
is connective if and only if
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is, for all
\Spec A\to X
. For a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
of derived algebraic stacks, by the adjoint functor theorem we have an adjunction
f^{*}:\QCoh(Y)\leftrightarrows\QCoh(X):f_{*}
, where
f^{*}
is symmetric monoidal and
f_{*}
is right-lax symmetric monoidal.

Following the same procedure, we have a functor
\mathsf{Aff}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Cat}
which sends
\Spec R
to
\mathsf{Alg}_{R}
. Right-Kan extending this yields a functor
\mathsf{Stk}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{Cat}
, which we write as
X\mapsto\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
. Objects in
\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
are called
quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-algebras
. When restricted to derived algebraic stacks, this functor lands in the
\infty
-category of presentable, symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories, and symmetric monoidal colimit preserving functors between them. Since we are in characteristic zero, we can also describe
\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
as the
\infty
-category of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras in
\QCoh(X)_{\geq 0}
. We furthermore have a left adjoint to the forgetful functor
\mathrm{QAlg}(X)\to\QCoh(X)_{\geq 0}
, written
\mathrm{LSym}_{X}
.

Example 1.10
.

For
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
, let
\mathcal{O}_{X}\in\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
be such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(A)=A
, for all
\Spec A\to X
, which is a unit in
\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
. We also write
\mathcal{O}_{X}
for the underlying quasi-coherent module.

Let
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
be algebraic. For
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
, the (relative)
spectrum
of
\mathcal{A}
is the derived algebraic stack
\Spec\mathcal{A}
over
X
such that for

, the space
(\Spec\mathcal{A})(T)
is the space of quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-algebra maps
f^{*}\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{O}_{T}
.

Write
\mathsf{Aff}_{X}
for the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Stk}_{X}
spanned by derived stacks which are affine over

, i.e., for which the structure map is affine. For
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{QAlg}(X)
, it holds that
\Spec\mathcal{A}
is affine over
X
. In fact, the functor
\mathcal{A}\mapsto\Spec\mathcal{A}
yields an equivalence
\mathrm{QAlg}(X)^{\mathrm{op}}\simeq\mathsf{Aff}_{X}
.

Let
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
be algebraic, and let
\mathcal{E}\in\QCoh(X)_{\geq(-1)}
be given. Define
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})
as the derived stack over
X
with the universal property that for

it holds that
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})
is the space of
\mathcal{O}_{T}
-module maps

. If
\mathcal{E}
is connective, then
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{E})\simeq\Spec\mathrm{LSym}_{X}(\mathcal{E})
.

1.10.
The cotangent complex and the normal bundle

Let
X
be a derived algebraic stack. A quasi-coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
\mathcal{F}
is called

if for all
\Spec A\to X
there is some

such that
\mathcal{F}_{A}\in(\mathsf{Mod}_{A})_{\geq n}
.

Let

be an affine derived scheme, and

We now prove and collect a few additional properties of derived blow-ups, which we will use in Section

As a consequence of the functorial description of the derived blow-up, we have the following proposition, which shows that the classical truncation

The classical truncations

∎

3.
Equivariant derived algebraic geometry

A main ingredient in the canonical reduction of stabilizers algorithm for algebraic stacks from
[
ER21
]
is the locus
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
of points with maximal-dimensional stabilizers, for a given algebraic stack
X
. This locus is a closed substack of
X
in the case that
X
admits a good moduli space, as shown in
[
ER21
, Prop. B.4]
.

In the next section, §
4
, we give a derived analogue of

in the case that
X
is a derived algebraic stack for which
X_{\mathrm{cl}}
admits a good moduli space. In order to do this, in this section we give a treatment of several elements of equivariant geometry and study fixed loci in the derived setting. This allows us to obtain equivariant versions of the results of the previous section, including equivariant derived blow-ups. We note that there is some overlap between what we present here and what is presented in
[
AKL+22
, §A.3]
.

The statements of this section are derived analogues of familiar statements from classical equivariant geometry, and the arguments follow similar lines to arguments for derived analogues of ordinary (i.e., non-equivariant) classical geometry.

3.1.

Derived stabilizer reduction of Artin stacks

Let

admits a good moduli space

Under these assumptions, an intrinsic stabilizer reduction procedure was developed in

\displaystyle X^{0}=X,\ X^{1}\coloneqq\widehat{X}^{0},\ \ldots,\ X^{m}\coloneqq\widehat{X}^{m-1}

6.3.
Derived stabilizer reduction of Artin stacks

We are now in position to carry out the derived stabilizer reduction procedure, as described in the introductory part of the section.

By Theorem

, the Kirwan blow-up

by the canonical sequence

\displaystyle X^{0}=X,\ X^{1}\coloneqq\widehat{X}^{0},\ \ldots,\ X^{m}\coloneqq\widehat{X}^{m-1}.

.

The derived Deligne–Mumford stack

is called the

of

.

It is clear by Theorem

that the classical truncation of

is the intrinsic stabilizer reduction of

.

Remark 6.11
.

By Remark

, if

.

6.4.
Connections with other approaches

We conclude with a brief discussion regarding the connections between the following stabilizer reduction approaches in the literature:

(i)

Kirwan’s original desingularization procedure for classical, smooth quotient stacks obtained by Geometric Invariant Theory (GIT)
[
Kir85
]
.

(ii)

Edidin–Rydh’s canonical reduction of stabilizers for classical (possibly singular) Artin stacks with good moduli spaces
[
ER21
]
.

(iii)

The intrinsic stabilizer reduction procedure introduced in
[
KLS17
,
Sav20
]
for (possibly singular) GIT quotient stacks and Artin stacks with good moduli spaces.

(iv)

The derived stabilizer reduction procedure of the present paper.

In general, (ii) is a generalization of (i), while we have shown that (iv) is always a derived enhancement of (iii).

Both (ii) and (iii) are sequences of two-step operations consisting of a blow-up and the deletion of certain unstable points (this two-step operation in (ii) is called a saturated blow-up, while in (iii) a Kirwan blow-up). The main difference between (ii) and (iii) is in the blow-up being used, which can now be succinctly formulated as follows, using Theorem
6.7
. For a classical stack
X
, the classical blow-up
\Bl^{\mathrm{cl}}_{X_{\mathrm{cl}}^{{\mathrm{max}}}}X
along the classical locus
X_{\mathrm{cl}}^{{\mathrm{max}}}
is used in (ii), while the underlying classical stack
(\Bl_{X^{{\mathrm{max}}}}X)_{\mathrm{cl}}
of the derived blow-up of
X
(viewed as a derived stack) along the derived locus
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
is used in (iii). Recall here that
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is both the classically defined locus and the underlying classical stack of the derived locus.

For classical GIT quotient stacks that are smooth, all four constructions coincide: approaches (i)–(iii) are literally identical, while in (iv) it suffices to notice that the derived intrinsic blow-up of a classical smooth Artin stack is the same as the classical intrinsic blow-up (since the derived
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
is smooth and coincides with the classical
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
) so that the derived Kirwan blow-up is classical and equals the saturated blow-up in each step.

However, in the singular case, the derived enhancement
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
of the classical locus
X_{\mathrm{cl}}^{{\mathrm{max}}}
is in general not itself classical, as can be seen in the following example. Hence, the outputs of (ii) and (iv) will typically differ.

Example 6.12
.

Suppose that
G=\mathbb{G}_{m}=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is the one-dimensional torus acting on the affine plane
V=\mathbb{C}^{2}_{x,y}
with weights
1
and
-1
on the coordinates
x
and
y
respectively, and that

is the closed
G
-invariant subscheme cut out by the ideal
I=(xy)
. Let

. Then the classical locus
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is the quotient stack
\Spec\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x,y)\times BG
.

To describe the derived locus
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
, we can find a model
B
for
\mathcal{O}_{U}
in standard form, and then apply Proposition
3.24
. In this case, it is clear that we can take
B(0)=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
and
M_{0}=B(0)\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}W_{0}
, where

is the trivial

-dimensional
G
-representation and

is multiplication by

. But then,
B_{G}
is freely generated by
\mathbb{C}[x,y]/(x,y)
in degree

and

in degree

, therefore the derived

is not classical and differs from its classical counterpart.

To prove the theorem, we make use of the following proposition.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let

be a closed embedding of quasi-smooth derived schemes. Then

Proof.

The statement is local, so we may assume that

. Moreover, since

.

The preceding proposition has also been used in recent work of Zhao

]
.

We may now deduce that quasi-smoothness is preserved by derived intrinsic blow-ups.

Suppose that

Proof.

Both statements are étale local, so by Lemma

Proof of Theorem

.

8.
(-1)
-shifted symplectic stacks

(-1)
-shifted symplectic derived Artin stacks play a fundamental role in enumerative geometry and Donaldson–Thomas (DT) theory, since they naturally arise as derived moduli stacks of sheaves and perfect complexes on Calabi–Yau threefolds. In
[
KLS17
,
Sav20
]
, it is shown that if
X
is such a stack, then the intrinsic stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}
admits a semi-perfect obstruction theory of virtual dimension zero and hence by
[
CL11
]
a virtual fundamental cycle
[\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{\mathrm{\mathrm{vir}}}]\in A_{0}(\widetilde{X}_{\mathrm{cl}})
, whose degree was defined to be the generalized DT invariant via Kirwan blow-ups associated to

. This was possible by rather cumbersome local computations.

In this section, we pursue a more detailed study of the derived stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{X}
to put this result on a much more robust footing. Namely, we will show that, by truncating the derived tangent complex of
\widetilde{X}
, there is a natural way to recover the above semi-perfect obstruction theory. As an application, we construct generalized Vafa–Witten invariants in Section
9
.

8.1.
Brief review of shifted symplectic geometry

Shifted symplectic structures were introduced by Pantev–Toën–Vaquié–Vezzosi in
[
PTVV13
]
. Their definition is given in the affine case first, and then generalized by showing that the local notion satisfies smooth descent. We use the presentation from
[
PV18
]
, but with different grading. See also
[
Rak20
]
.

The local definition is as follows. Let
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]
be the graded algebra, where
\epsilon
is free in homogeneous degree 1 and homological degree 1, hence with
\epsilon^{2}\simeq 0
. An object in
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]}
is then a graded
\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]
-module
\bigoplus_{p}M^{p}
, and multiplication by
\epsilon
induces maps
M^{p}\to M^{p+1}[1]
. Then the functor
\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]}\to\mathsf{Alg}
, which sends an algebra
\bigoplus_{p}B^{p}
to
B^{0}
, has a left adjoint, written

\mathrm{dR}_{(-)}\colon\mathsf{Alg}\to\mathsf{Alg}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]}

The algebra
\mathrm{dR}_{A}
for
A\in\mathsf{Alg}
is called the
derived de Rham
algebra. The underlying graded module of
\mathrm{dR}_{A}
is

\mathrm{dR}_{A}\simeq\bigoplus\nolimits_{p\geq 0}\Lambda^{p}_{A}({\mathbb{L}}_{A})[p]\simeq\mathrm{LSym}_{A}({\mathbb{L}}_{A}[1](-1))

where
(-)(q)
means twisting in homogeneous degree, so that
(M(q))^{p}=M^{p+q}
.

The space of
n
-shifted, closed
p
-forms
on
A
is

\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(A,n)\coloneqq\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathbb{Z}}_{\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]}(\mathbb{C}(-p)[p-n],\mathrm{dR}_{A})

An element
\omega\in\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(A,n)
thus induces an element in

\mathrm{dR}_{A}^{p}[n-p]\simeq\Lambda^{p}{\mathbb{L}}_{A}[n]

In particular, for
p=2
, we obtain a map
\omega\colon\mathbb{C}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{A}\wedge{\mathbb{L}}_{A}[n]
, hence a morphism
\omega^{\flat}\colon{\mathbb{L}}_{A}^{\vee}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{A}[n]
by adjunction. We say that
\omega
is
non-degenerate
if
\omega^{\flat}
is an equivalence.

Definition 8.1
.

Suppose that
A\in\mathsf{Alg}^{G}
. Then the standard
G
-action on
{\mathbb{L}}_{A}
induces a
G
-action on
\mathrm{dR}_{A}
, and
\omega\in\mathrm{dR}_{A}
is called
G
-invariant
if it is invariant with respect to this
G
-action.

The functor
\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(-,n)
is a derived stack. For
X\in\mathsf{Stk}
, the space of
n
-shifted, closed
p
-forms on
X
is then

\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(X,n)\coloneqq\mathsf{Stk}(X,\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(-,n))\simeq\lim\nolimits_{\Spec A\to X}\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(A,n)

An
n
-shifted, closed
p
-form
\omega
on
X
thus induces a homotopy coherent family of
\omega_{A}\in\mathcal{A}^{p,\mathrm{cd}}(A,n)
, indexed over all
\Spec A\to X
. It can be shown that, for
p=2
, this again induces a morpshim
\omega^{\flat}\colon{\mathbb{L}}^{\vee}_{X}\to{\mathbb{L}}_{X}[n]
, and that
\omega
is
non-degenerate
if
\omega^{\flat}
is an equivalence.

Definition 8.2

(
[
PTVV13
, Def. 1.18]
,
[
PV18
, Def. 1.10]
)
.

An
n
-shifted, closed
2
-form
\omega
on
X
is called an
n
-
shifted symplectic structure
if it is non-degenerate. If such
\omega
exists, we say that
X
is
n
-shifted symplectic.

Proof.

9.
Sheaf-theoretic invariants

In this section, we explore immediate applications of our results to the enumerative geometry of sheaves and complexes and Donaldson–Thomas theory in particular.

Namely, we define virtual classes for moduli stacks of semi-stable sheaves and complexes on smooth, projective surfaces.

Moreover, we obtain a fully derived upgrade of the construction of generalized Donaldson–Thomas invariants via Kirwan blow-ups developed in
[
KLS17
,
Sav20
]
. These act as virtual counts of semi-stable sheaves and perfect complexes on smooth, projective Calabi–Yau threefolds.

The robustness of derived geometry also allows us to apply the same method to define new generalized Vafa–Witten invariants, enumerating semi-stable Higgs pairs on projective surfaces.

9.1.
Donaldson-type invariants of surfaces

Let
S
be a smooth, projective surface and
\mathcal{M}
a derived moduli stack that parametrizes semi-stable sheaves or perfect complexes on
S
. Possible choices of stability include Gieseker or slope stability
[
HL10
]
for sheaves and Bridgeland stability
[
Bri07
]
for complexes. It is a standard fact that
\mathcal{M}
is then quasi-smooth.

If the genus
p_{g}=H^{0}(K_{S})
of
S
is positive or the rank of the objects in question is zero, it might be necessary to consider a modification of
\mathcal{M}
, for example, a “reduced” projective linear version as in
[
Joy21
]
, or fix the determinant, in order to remove certain trivial components from the cotangent complex of
\mathcal{M}
. Without going into more detail, we assume that this is the case so that
\mathcal{M}
is a derived moduli stack of semi-stable sheaves or perfect complexes on a smooth, projective surface, which is of finite type with the good moduli space
M_{\mathrm{cl}}
of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cl}}
being proper.

By Theorem
7.1
, the derived stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is a proper quasi-smooth derived Deligne–Mumford stack and we have virtual fundamental classes

[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{\ast}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}),\ [\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\in K_{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}).

Remark 9.1
.

Following up on Remark
7.5
, it would be interesting to compare these classes with the virtual classes of
\mathcal{M}
itself, constructed by Khan
[
Kha19
]
, and also with the homological virtual classes constructed in
[
Joy21
]
.

We may thus define intersection-theoretic and
K
-theoretic Donaldson-type invariants as follows.

Definition 9.2
.

For any
\gamma\in H^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}},\mathbb{Q})
, the intersection-theoretic generalized Donaldson-type invariant associated to
\mathcal{M}
is defined by the formula

\mathrm{DT}(\mathcal{M},\gamma)=\int_{[\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\gamma.

Similarly, for any
\beta\in K^{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}})
, the
\beta
-twisted
K
-theoretic generalized Donaldson-type invariant associated to
\mathcal{M}
is defined by the formula

\mathrm{DT}^{\mathrm{K-th},\beta}(\mathcal{M})=\chi\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}},[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}}\beta\right).

9.2.
Donaldson–Thomas invariants of Calabi–Yau threefolds via Kirwan blow-ups

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a derived moduli stack parametrizing semi-stable sheaves or perfect complexes on a smooth, projective Calabi–Yau threefold
W
, as considered in
[
Sav20
]
. Possible choices of stability here are Gieseker stability
[
HL10
]
for sheaves and PT, polynomial or Bridgeland stability
[
Bri07
,
Bay09
,
PT09
]
for complexes.

As in
[
Sav20
]
,
\mathcal{M}
is
(-1)
-shifted symplectic and its classical truncation
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cl}}
admits a good moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cl}}\to M_{\mathrm{cl}}
with
M_{\mathrm{cl}}
proper.

Let
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cl}}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cl}}\!\!\fatslash\mathbb{G}_{m}
be the rigidification of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cl}}
by the scaling automorphisms of sheaves or complexes. In
[
Sav20
]
, it is shown that the intrinsic stabilizer reduction
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
of
\mathcal{N}
admits a natural semi-perfect obstruction theory of virtual dimension zero. The degree of the induced virtual fundamental cycle
[\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
is defined to be the generalized Donaldson–Thomas invariant via Kirwan blow-ups associated to
\mathcal{M}
. By
[
KS22
]
, the obstruction theory can be upgraded to an almost perfect obstruction theory with induced virtual structure sheaf
[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\in K_{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}})
.

Our results above allow us to obtain a canonical derived enhancement as follows.

Theorem 9.3
.

There exists a derived Artin stack
\mathcal{N}
with classical truncation
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cl}}
. The truncation
E^{\bullet}=\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}
of the tangent complex is perfect, with induced virtual fundamental cycle (using Theorem
8.15
) equal to
[\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}})
and induced virtual structure sheaf equal to
[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\in K_{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}})
.

Proof.

The existence of
\mathcal{N}
follows from
[
Hal20
, Lem. 4.4.9]
, which generalizes the rigidification construction to derived Artin stacks.

Now, observe that
\mathcal{M}
admits a strongly étale cover by quotient stacks
[\Spec A/G]
where
A
satisfies property
(\dagger)
. Since
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\!\!\fatslash\mathbb{G}_{m}
, it follows that
\mathcal{N}
admits a strongly étale cover by the quotient stacks
[\Spec A/(G/\mathbb{G}_{m})]
.

It is then clear that the arguments of Section
6
extend verbatim to show that Kirwan blow-ups preserve the form of these étale covers and, in addition, Deligne–Mumford stacks with such covers have perfect two-term truncation of their tangent complex in amplitude
[0,1]
(with cohomological grading), as desired.

The equality of virtual fundamental cycles and virtual structure sheaves follows identical arguments as for Theorem
8.15
.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

With minor modifications, the above also apply if one fixes the determinant of the sheaves or complexes under consideration.

9.3.
Stacks with a torus action

Let
X\in\mathsf{Stk}^{T}
be a derived Artin stack with a
T
-action, for a torus
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\dots\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
X\in\mathsf{Stk}^{T}
be a derived stack with a
T
-action. Then
X^{{\mathrm{max}}}
admits a natural
T
-action and is a
T
-invariant closed substack of
X
.

Proof.

Observe that the action map
X\times T\to X
is affine, since
T
is affine. The claim then follows from functoriality of
(-)^{d}
for representable and separated morphisms by Proposition
4.19
(ii) and
(X\times T)^{{\mathrm{max}}}=X^{{\mathrm{max}}}\times T
.
∎

9.4.
Vafa–Witten invariants of surfaces

Let
(S,\mathcal{O}_{S}(1))
be a smooth, polarized projective surface and
\pi\colon W=\mathrm{Tot}(K_{S})\to S
the total space of its canonical bundle with polarization
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
. Let
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}
be the one-dimensional torus.

By the results of
[
TT20
,
TT17
]
, there exists a
(-1)
-shifted symplectic derived Artin stack
\mathcal{N}
, which parametrizes Gieseker semi-stable Higgs pairs
(E,\phi\colon E\to E\otimes K_{S})
on
S
with fixed positive rank and Chern classes, fixed determinant
\det E
and zero trace
\mathrm{tr}\ \phi=0
.
\mathcal{N}
is a moduli stack of compactly supported Gieseker semi-stable sheaves on
W
with respect to the polarization
\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{S}(1)
. It admits a natural
T
-action induced by scaling the fibers of the projection map
W\to S
.

By construction, the classical truncation
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is a GIT quotient stack
[Q^{\mathrm{ss}}/G]
, where
Q
is an appropriate Quot scheme on
W
and the
T
-action is induced by a
T
-action on
Q^{\mathrm{ss}}
, which commutes with the
G
-action on
Q^{\mathrm{ss}}
. Moreover, the
T
-fixed locus
\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}
has a good moduli space
N_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}
which is a projective scheme.

We now see that, since
\mathcal{N}^{\max}
is
T
-invariant by Proposition
9.5
,
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
admits a
T
-action such that the projection
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}\to\mathcal{N}
is
T
-equivariant. Since
N_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}
is proper, it follows that
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
is a proper Deligne–Mumford stack. Moreover, Lemma
8.13
, Corollary
8.14
and Theorem
8.15
apply
T
-equivariantly and imply that
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}|_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}
is a
T
-equivariant perfect complex on
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}
and the same is true for the
T
-fixed component of the restriction to the fixed locus
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}
, i.e.,
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}|_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{T}}^{\mathrm{fix}}
, which we know is the same as
\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{T}}|_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{T}}
. Write
N^{\mathrm{vir}}\coloneqq\tau^{[0,1]}\mathbb{T}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}}|_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{T}}^{\mathrm{mv}}
for the moving component.

In particular, by
[
Kie18
,
KS20
]
, the
T
-fixed locus
{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{T}
admits a virtual fundamental cycle
[\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}]^{\mathrm{vir}}
and also a virtual structure sheaf
[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]\in K_{0}^{T}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T})
. Thus, mirroring the virtual torus localization formula
[
GP99
,
Lee04
]
, we can give the following definition.

Definition 9.6
.

The numerical generalized Vafa–Witten invariant via Kirwan blow-ups associated to
\mathcal{N}
is defined by the formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{VWK}(\mathcal{N})=\int_{\left[\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}\right]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\frac{1}{e(N^{\mathrm{vir}})}.

For any
\beta\in K_{T}^{0}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]}\mathbb{Q}(t^{\frac{1}{2}})
, the
\beta
-twisted
K
-theoretic generalized Vafa–Witten invariant via Kirwan blow-ups associated to
\mathcal{N}
is defined by the formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{VWK}^{\mathrm{K-th},\beta}(\mathcal{N})=\chi_{t}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T},\frac{[\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{T}_{\mathrm{cl}}}^{\mathrm{vir}}]}{e(N^{\mathrm{vir}})}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{\mathrm{cl}}^{T}}}\beta\right)\in\mathbb{Q}(t^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}).

Here
t
denotes the torus parameter and
\chi_{t}
has the same meaning as in
[
Tho20
, Subsection 2.4]
.

It is interesting to investigate whether this definition satisfies the wall-crossing formulas of
[
TT17
,
Joy21
]
. Deformation invariance of
\mathrm{VWK}(\mathcal{N})
follows from a
T
-equivariant version of the arguments in
[
Sav20
]
.

Remark 9.7
.

The reason for the localization to
\mathbb{Q}(t^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}})
above is to preserve consistency with the notation used in
[
Tho20
]
, as well as the classical case, where it might be necessary to consider half-integer weights for the torus action. This happens already with the square root of the virtual canonical bundle in the classical case.

Remark 9.8
.

Observe that
\mathcal{N}
does not have connected stabilizers, so we have to perform the derived stabilizer reduction procedure with non-connected stabilizers in this case.

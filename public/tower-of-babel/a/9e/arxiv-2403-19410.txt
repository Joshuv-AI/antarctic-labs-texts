On the Exact Fourier Dimension of Sets of Well-Approximable Matrices
Thomas Cai and Kyle Hambrook

Abstract
. We compute the exact Fourier dimension of the set of Ψ \Psi -well-approximable m × n m\times n matrices (and the set of Ψ \Psi -well-approximable numbers) in the homogeneous and inhomogeneous cases for any approximation function Ψ \Psi satisfying ∑ q ∈ ℤ n Ψ ​ ( q ) m < ∞ \sum_{q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The Fourier dimension of a set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\dim_{F}E=\sup\left\{{\beta\in[0,d]:\exists\mu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\text{ such that }|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\ll|\xi|^{-\beta/2}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\xi\neq 0}\right\}.

Here and elsewhere
\mathcal{P}(E)
is the set of Borel probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose support is contained in
E
.
Fourier dimension is closely related to Hausdorff dimension.
Indeed, as a consequence of Frostman’s lemma, the Hausdorff dimension of a Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\dim_{H}E=\sup\left\{{\alpha\in[0,d]:\exists\mu\in\mathcal{P}(E)\text{ such that }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|^{2}|\xi|^{s-d}<\infty}\right\}.

Thus the Fourier dimension concerns the pointwise Fourier decay of measures supported on a set and the Hausdorff dimension is about the
L^{2}
Fourier decay of such measures.
More concretely, it follows that

\dim_{F}E\leq\dim_{H}E

for every Borel set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Hausdorff and Fourier dimension are generally different.
For
k<d
, every
k
-dimensional hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
has Hausdorff dimension
k
and Fourier dimension 0.
The middle-
1/3
Cantor set has Hausdorff dimension
\log 2/\log 3
and Fourier dimension 0.
In fact, for every
\theta\in(0,1)
, the middle-
\theta
Cantor set
C_{\theta}
has
\dim_{F}C_{\theta}<\dim_{H}C_{\theta}
.
Moreover,
0<\dim_{F}C_{\theta}
for almost every
\theta\in(0,1)
.

A set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a Salem if
\dim_{F}E=\dim_{H}E
.
Every set with Hausdorff dimension 0 is a Salem set of dimension 0.

\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Salem set of dimension
d
, as is any subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
that contains an open ball.
Every sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Salem set of dimension
d-1
.
Salem
[
26
]
proved that for every
\alpha\in[0,1]
there exists a Salem set in
\mathbb{R}
of dimension
\alpha
using random Cantor-type sets.
Kahane
[
16
]
(see also
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
) proved that
for every
\alpha\in[0,d]
there exists a Salem set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of dimension
\alpha

by considering images of stochastic processes.
Other random constructions of Salem sets exist (see, e.g.
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
22
]
).

Kaufman
[
19
]
was the first to find non-random (also called explicit) examples of Salem sets with dimension different from
0,d-1,
and
d
.
Specifically, Kaufman showed that the set

E(\tau)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}:|xq-r|<|q|^{-\tau}\text{ for infinitely many $(r,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}$}\}

is a Salem set of dimension
2/(1+\tau)
for all
0<\tau<1
.

E(\tau)
is called the set of
\tau
-well-approximable numbers.
Jarník
[
15
]
and Besicovitch
[
5
]
proved earlier that the Hausdorff dimension of
E(\tau)
is
2/(1+\tau)
for
0<\tau<1
.
(Note: Dirichlet’s approximation theorem implies
E(\tau)=\mathbb{R}
if
\tau\geq 1
.)

1.2.
Definition of Well-Approximable Matrices

Our main theorem concerns the following generalization of
E(\tau)

that is often studied in metric Diophantine approximation.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
Q
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
Let
\Psi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
. Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Define
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
to be the set of all points

x=(x_{11},\ldots,x_{1n},\ldots,x_{m1},\ldots,x_{mn})\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}

such that

\max_{1\leq i\leq m}|\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{ij}q_{j}-r_{i}-\theta_{i}|<\Psi(q)\text{ for infinitely many $(r,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times Q$}.

By identifying
x
with the
m\times n
matrix with
x_{ij}
in the
i
-th row and
j
-th column, the defining inequality becomes

|xq-r-\theta|<\Psi(q)\text{ for infinitely many $(r,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times Q$}.

Note: In this paper
|{}\cdot{}|
denotes the max norm and
|{}\cdot{}|_{2}
denotes the Euclidean norm.
The set
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
is called a set of
\Psi
-well-approximable matrices (or a set of
\Psi
-well-approximable linear forms).
Note
E(\tau)=E(1,1,\mathbb{Z},|q|^{-\tau},0)
.

The function
\Psi
is called an approximation function.
Note that redefining
\Psi
at finitely many points does not change the set
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
.
Note also that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
and
q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
there is an
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
such that
|xq-r|\leq 1/2
.
So replacing
\Psi
by
\max\left\{{\Psi,1/2}\right\}
does not change the set.
Therefore, in the proofs, we can (and will) always assume
\Psi\leq 1/2
and
\Psi(0)=1/2
.

The set
Q
may be called the set of denominators.
Since
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=E(m,n,\mathbb{Z}^{n},\Psi\mathbf{1}_{Q},\theta)
,
it is not strictly necessary to have
Q
explicitly in the definition,
but it is convenient to do so.

The cases
\theta=0
and
\theta\neq 0
are called the homogeneous case and inhomogeneous case, respectively.

1.3.
Previous Results

Many authors have studied the Hausdorff dimension of
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
at various level of generality
(see
[
12
]
for a short survey).
We state only the results most relevant to our main theorem.
Bovey and Dodson
[
7
]
showed that

\dim_{H}E(m,n,\mathbb{Z},|q|^{-\tau},0)=\min\left\{{\dfrac{m(n-1)}{1+\tau},mn}\right\}.

Also in the homogeneous case, Rynne
[
25
]
proved a completely general result. Let

\eta(Q,\Psi)=\inf\{\eta\geq 0:\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in Q\\
q\neq 0\end{subarray}}|q|^{m}\left({\frac{\Psi(q)}{|q|}}\right)^{\eta}<\infty\}.

Rynne showed that

\dim_{H}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,0)=\min\{m(n-1)+\eta(Q,\Psi),mn\}.

For the inhomogeneous case,
Theorem 1 of Beresnevich and Velani
[
2
]
implies that, if
\Psi(q)\rightarrow 0
as
|q|\rightarrow\infty
,
then

\dim_{H}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=\min\{m(n-1)+\eta(Q,\Psi),mn\}.

For the Fourier dimension of
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
, the major results are the following.
Hambrook
[
12
]
proved

\dim_{F}E(m,n,\mathbb{Z}^{n},|q|^{-\tau},\theta)\geq\min\left\{{\frac{2n}{1+\tau},mn}\right\}.

(In fact,
[
12
]
also proves a lower bound
on the Fourier dimension for more general approximation functions
\Psi

and sets of denominators
Q
.
However, that result is less general and less aesthetically pleasing than the lower bound proved in the present paper.)
Hambrook and Yu
[
13
]
established
the matching upper bound to obtain

\dim_{F}E(m,n,\mathbb{Z}^{n},|q|^{-\tau},0)=\min\left\{{\frac{2n}{1+\tau},mn}\right\}.

By comparing to Bovey and Dodson’s formula for the Hausdorff dimension, this proved
E(m,n,\mathbb{Z},|q|^{-\tau},0)
is not Salem unless
m=n=1
.

1.4.
Main Theorem

The following is our main result, which gives a formula for the Fourier dimension of
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, all
Q\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
and very general functions
\Psi
.
It can be viewed as a Fourier dimension analog of the results of Rynne
[
25
]
and of Beresnevich and Velani
[
2
]
mentioned above.
Define
\Psi_{*}(q)
=
\Psi(q)|q|^{-1}
and

s(Q,\Psi)=\inf\{s\geq 0:\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi_{*}(q)^{s}<\infty\}.

Theorem 1.4.1
.

If
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty
, then

\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=\min\{2s(Q,\Psi),mn\}
.

Remark 1.4.2
.

We do not have a formula for the Fourier dimension of
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
for all
\Psi
when
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty
.
The Fourier dimension does not necessarily equal
\min\{2s(Q,\Psi),mn\}
in that case.
However, we can conclude that
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=mn
for certain classes of functions.
See Section
2
for details.

We prove Theorem
1.4.1
by proving the following two propositions.

Proposition 1.4.3
.

If
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty
, then

\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)\leq 2s(Q,\Psi)
.

Proposition 1.4.4
.

\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)\geq\min\left\{{2s(Q,\Psi),mn}\right\}
.

Proposition
1.4.3
is proved by generalizing the method of
[
13
]
.
In particular, we must prove an inhomogeneous version of the Theorem 2.3 in
[
13
]
,
which is the main techincal result of that paper.
Our result is Lemma
3.1.1
; we call it the Inhomogeneos Lattice Lemma.

Proposition
1.4.3
is proved by modifying the argument of
[
12
]
.
Significant changes are required as the hypothesis
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty

is in a different form and more general than the hypothesis imposed on
\Psi
and
Q
in
[
12
]
.
The key idea is the definitions of the sets
Q(M)
and
Q^{\prime}(M)
(which are subsets of
Q
) in Subsection
4.1
.
The set
Q(M)
is defined significantly differently than the set of the same name in
[
12
]
because
it is adapted to the form of the hypotheses on
\Psi
and
Q
.
The assumption that
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}
converges is critical to lower bounding the size of
Q(M)
,
which is in turn critical to obtaining the necessary Fourier decay estimate on the measure we construct.
The set
Q^{\prime}(M)
has no analog in
[
12
]
.
It is defined by removing from
Q(M)
the vectors
q
that divide small non-zero vectors
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
.
The idea of defining
Q^{\prime}(M)
(and the proof of lower bound on its size) is borrowed from
[
10
]
.
With
Q(M)
and
Q^{\prime}(M)
properly defined, the rest of the proof of Proposition
1.4.4

is similar to the proof in
[
12
]
.

2.
The Divergence Case

When
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty
, the Fourier dimension of
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
no longer depends solely on
s(Q,\Psi)
, as the following example illustrates.

Example 2.0.1
.

Take
Q=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and consider the function

\Psi(q)=\begin{cases}1/2&\text{if }q_{1}=2^{k}\text{ for some }k\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }q_{2}=\cdots=q_{n}=0\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

For every
s>0
,
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi_{*}(q)^{s}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}(1/2)^{s}(2^{-ks})<\infty
, and hence
s(Q,\Psi)=0
.
But
E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=\mathbb{R}^{mn}
because
\Psi(q)=1/2
for infinitely many
q
.

However, the following lemma and its corollary allows us to conclude that
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=mn
under certain circumstances.

Lemma 2.0.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{mn}
. If
\lambda(E^{c})=0
, then
\dim_{F}E=mn
.

Proof.

Let
B=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}:|x|_{2}\leq 1\}
be the closed unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{mn}
.
If
\lambda(E^{c})=0
and
\lambda^{\prime}
is Lebesgue measure restricted to
E\cap B
, then
\mathrm{supp}(\lambda^{\prime})\subseteq E
and

\widehat{\lambda^{\prime}}(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}1_{E\cap B}(x)dx=\int e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\mathbf{1}_{B}(x)dx=\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{B}}(\xi),

since the integrands agree
\lambda
-almost everywhere. But, by a standard calculation (see e.g.,
[
23
, p.34]
),
|\widehat{\mathbf{1}_{B}}(\xi)|\ll|\xi|^{-(mn+1)/2}
.
∎

For brevity,
define

|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)|=\lambda(E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)\cap[0,1]^{mn}).

Since
\lambda(E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta))
is
\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
-periodic,

|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)|=1
if and only if
\lambda(E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)^{c})=0
.
Thus:

Corollary 2.0.3
.

If
|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)|=1
, then
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=mn
.

There are several theorems in metric Diophantine approximation
that conclude
|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)|=1
from the divergence of
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty
(or a similar sum) and some additional hypotheses.
We state a few of these theorems below; a good survey is
[
3
]
.
Combining these theorems with Corollary
2.0.3
gives us cases where
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=mn
.

Khinchine’s theorem
[
20
]
says

|E(1,1,\mathbb{N},\Psi,0)|=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\Psi(q)<\infty\\
1&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\Psi(q)=\infty\text{ and $\Psi$ is monotone}\end{cases}

Monotonicity is required here. Indeed, Duffin and Schaeffer
[
9
]

provided a counterexample where
|E(1,1,\mathbb{N},\Psi,0)|=0
and
\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}\Psi(q)=\infty
.

The Duffin-Schaeffer theorem (conjectured by Duffin and Schaeffer
[
9
]

and proved by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
21
]
) states

|E(1,1,\mathbb{N},\Psi,0)|=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-1}{\phi(q)\Psi(q)}<\infty\\
1&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{-1}{\phi(q)\Psi(q)}=\infty,\end{cases}

where
\phi
is the Euler totient function.

If
\Psi
is symmetric (i.e.,
\Psi(q)=\psi(|q|)
for some function
\psi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,1/2]
), Allen and Ramirez
[
1
]
proved

|E(m,n,\mathbb{Z}^{n},\Psi,\theta)|=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty\\
1&\text{if }\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty\text{ and either $mn>2$ or $\psi$ is monotone}\end{cases}

At present, this is the strongest version of the Inhomogeneous Khinchine-Groshev theorem.
The divergence case for
n\geq 3
, the divergence case for
n=1
,
m\geq 3
, and the convergence case were proved earlier (see
[
1
]
for details on attribution).
Monotonicity is required when
mn=1
, as shown by the Duffin-Schaeffer counterexample mentioned above.
It is unknown if monotonicity is necessary for
mn=2
.

If
\Psi(q)\rightarrow 0
as
|q|\rightarrow\infty
, Schmidt’s theorem
[
27
]
(see also
[
2
]
) is

|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)|=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}<\infty\\
1&\text{if }\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty\text{ and $m+n>2$}.\end{cases}

In the homogeneous case (
\theta=0
),
some stronger results are known.
Beresnevich and Velani
[
4
]
proved

|E(m,n,Q,\Psi,0)|=1\quad\text{if $\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty$ and $m>1$}.

See also Ramirez
[
24
]
for an even stronger statement.

We are not aware of any examples where
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty
and
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)<mn
.
However, it may be possible to modify the Duffin-Schaeffer counterexample to obtain such an example. Thus we propose the following problem.

Problem 2.0.4
.

Prove or Disprove: If
\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}=\infty
, then
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)=mn
.

3.
Proof of Proposition
1.4.3

3.1.
Inhomogeneous Lattice Lemma

The following lemma is an inhomogeneous version of Theorem 2.3 in
[
13
]
.

Let
\theta=(\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
For all non-zero
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\delta\geq 0
, define

L_{\delta,q,\theta_{i}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists r\in\mathbb{Z},|q_{1}x_{1}+\dots+q_{d}x_{n}-r-\theta_{i}|\leq\delta\}

and

L_{\delta,q,\theta}^{m}=L_{\delta,q,\theta_{1}}\times\cdots\times L_{\delta,q,\theta_{m}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{mn}.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
be integers. Let
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
q\neq 0
. Let
0<\delta<1/2
.
Let
\mu
be a Borel probability measure on
[0,1]^{mn}.

Then

\mu(L_{\delta,q,\theta}^{m})\ll\delta^{m}\left(1+O(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},0<|t|\leq 2/\delta}|\hat{\mu}(tq^{T})|)\right),

and, for all
K>0
and
N>0
,

\mu(L_{\delta,q,\theta}^{m})\gg\delta^{m}\left(1+O(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},0<|t|\leq K/\delta}|\hat{\mu}(tq^{T})|)\right)+O(K^{-N}).

Moreover, the implied constant in the
O(K^{-N})
term depends on
N
only, and all other implied constants are absolute.

Note.
According to our convention for identifying
m\times n
matrices with points in
\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,

tq^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}\\
\vdots\\
t_{m}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}q_{1}&\cdots&q_{n}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}q_{1}&\cdots&t_{1}q_{n}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
t_{m}q_{1}&\cdots&t_{m}q_{n}\end{bmatrix}=(t_{1}q_{1},\ldots,t_{1}q_{n},\ldots,t_{m}q_{1},\ldots,t_{m}q_{n})

Preparation for Proof of Lemma
3.1.1

Let
q=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},q\neq 0
.
Let
0<\delta<1/2
.
Fix
1\leq i\leq m
.

For each
r\in\mathbb{R}
, define
the plane

P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}=\left\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:q\cdot x-r-\theta_{i}=0}\right\},

and the
slab

P_{\delta,q,r,\theta_{i}}=\left\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|q\cdot x-r-\theta_{i}|\leq\delta}\right\}.

Let

L_{q,\theta_{i}}=\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{q,r}=\left\{{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists r\in\mathbb{Z}:q\cdot x-r-\theta_{i}=0}\right\}.

Note that
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}=P_{0,q,r,\theta_{i}}
and
L_{q,\theta_{i}}=L_{0,q,\theta_{i}}
.

Define
\delta_{\ast}=\delta/|q|_{2}
. Let
B_{\delta_{\ast}}(0)
be the closed Euclidean metric ball with radius is
\delta_{\ast}
centered at the origin.
The Euclidean distance between any two planes
P_{q,r^{\prime},\theta_{i}}
and
P_{q,r^{\prime\prime},\theta_{i}}
is
|r^{\prime}-r^{\prime\prime}|/|q|_{2}
.
Therefore
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
is a union of planes that are orthogonal to
q
and spaced a distance of
1/|q|_{2}
apart.
Also,

P_{\delta,q,r,\theta_{i}}
is the
\delta_{\ast}
-thickening of the plane
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}
, i.e.,

P_{\delta,q,r,\theta_{i}}=P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}+B_{\delta_{\ast}}(0),

and
L_{\delta,q,\theta_{i}}

is the
\delta_{\ast}
-thickening of
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
, i.e.,

L_{\delta,q,\theta_{i}}=\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{\delta,q,r,\theta_{i}}=L_{q,\theta_{i}}+B_{\delta_{\ast}}(0).

Since
\delta<1/2
, the slabs

P_{\delta,q,r,\theta_{i}}

in the union
are disjoint.

In an abuse of notation, we use
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}
to denote
the surface measure on the plane
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}
.
Likewise,
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
denotes the surface measure on the union of planes
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
. The measures are related by

L_{q,\theta_{i}}=\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}.

Since the measure
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
is (a multiple of)
the restriction of the
(n-1)
-Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the set
L_{q,\theta_{i}}
,
we have the following property:
There are constants
a,b>0
(independent of
q
) such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle a\epsilon^{n-1}\leq L_{q,\theta_{i}}(B_{\epsilon}(x))\leq b\epsilon^{n-1}

for all
0<\epsilon<1
and all
x\in L_{q,\theta_{i}}
.

Assume (without loss of generality) that
q_{1}\neq 0
. Then the measure
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}
is
given by

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dP_{q,r,\theta_{i}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}f(g(x_{2},\dots,x_{n}))\frac{1}{|q_{1}|}|q|_{2}dx_{2}\dots dx_{n},

where

g(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})=(q_{1}^{-1}(r+\theta_{i}-q_{2}x_{2}-\cdots-q_{n}x_{n}),x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})

is the parameterization of the plane
P_{q,r,\theta_{i}}
.

Lemma 3.1.2
.

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\widehat{L_{q,\theta_{i}}}(k)|=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{-2\pi it\theta_{i}}|q|_{2}&\text{if $k=tq$ for some $t\in\mathbb{Z}$}\\
0&\text{if $k\notin\mathbb{Z}q$ }\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We have

\widehat{L_{q,\theta_{i}}}{k}\int_{[0,1)^{n}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x}dL_{q,\theta_{i}}(x)=\int_{[0,1)^{n}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot x}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}}dP_{q,r,\theta_{i}}(x).

Note: If
r_{1}=kq_{1}+r_{2}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in P_{q,r_{1},\theta_{i}}
if and only if

3.2.
Countable Stability Lemma

The following is Lemma 2.5 of

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Fourier dimension is countably stable on closed sets, i.e., for every countable collection
(A_{i})
of closed sets in

\dim_{F}\left({\bigcup_{i}A_{i}}\right)=\sup_{i}\dim_{F}A_{i}.

and every set

We will use the following corollary.

.

Let

.
If

For all

, let

. Note

3.3.
Borel-Cantelli Argument

To prove
\dim_{F}E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)\leq 2s(Q,\Psi)

Lemma 3.3.1

(Borel-Cantelli Lemma - Convergence Case)
.

Suppose
(\Omega,\mathcal{E},P)
is a probability space and (

) is a sequence of sets in
\mathcal{E}
(i.e., a sequence of events). If
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P(E_{i})<\infty
, then

P(\left\{{\omega\in\Omega:\omega\in E_{i}\text{ for infinitely many }i}\right\})=0.

Corollary
3.2.2
means we only need to show

\dim_{F}(E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta))\cap[0,1]^{mn})\leq 2s(Q,\Psi)
.
Seeking a contradiction, suppose the inequality is false.
So there exists a
\mu\in P(E(n,d,Q,\Psi))\cap[0,1]^{mn}
and
s>s(Q,\Psi)
such that

|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|\ll|\xi|^{-s}
.
Lemma
3.1.1
tells us, for
0<\delta<1/2
,

\mu(L_{\delta,q,\theta})\ll\delta^{m}\left({1+O\left({\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},0<|t|\leq 2/\delta}|\widehat{\mu}(tq^{T})|}\right)}\right).

Because of the Fourier decay assumption, the sum over

is

\ll\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},0<|t|\leq 2/\delta}|tq^{T}|^{-s}\leq|q|^{-s}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},0<|t|\leq 2/\delta}|t|^{-s}\ll|q|^{-s}\delta^{s-m}.

Setting

\sum_{q\in Q}\mu(L_{\delta,q,\theta})\ll\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}+\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}|q|^{-s}\Psi(q)^{(s-m)}=\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{m}+\sum_{q\in Q}\Psi(q)^{s}|q|^{-s}<\infty.

So, because of the convergence case of the Borel-Cantelli lemma and because

q\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\epsilon}_{q,\theta}(x)=\Phi^{\epsilon}(qx-\theta)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\widehat{\phi}(\epsilon k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot(xq-\theta)}\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}

and the series on the right converges uniformly to a continuous function.
By uniform convergence,

\displaystyle\widehat{\Phi^{\epsilon}_{q,\theta}}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\int_{[0,1]^{mn}}\widehat{\phi}(\epsilon k)e^{2\pi ik\cdot(xq-\theta)}e^{-2\pi i\ell\cdot x}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\widehat{\phi}(\epsilon k)\int_{[0,1]^{mn}}e^{2\pi i(k\cdot(xq-\theta)-\ell\cdot x)}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}e^{-2\pi ik\cdot\theta}\widehat{\phi}(\epsilon k)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\int_{[0,1]}e^{2\pi ix_{ij}(k_{i}q_{j}-\ell_{ij})}dx_{ij}.

If
k_{i}q_{j}=\ell_{ij}
for every
i,j
, the product of the integrals is
1
.
Otherwise, the product is
0
. Whenever
q_{j}
is non-zero,
k_{i}=q_{j}^{-1}\ell_{ij}
for every
i
, i.e.,
k=q_{j}^{-1}\ell_{j}
.
∎

4.3.
Single-Scale Function

For
M>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
, define

\displaystyle F_{M}(x)=\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)}\Phi^{\Psi(q)}_{q,\theta}(x).

Note
F_{M}

is periodic for the lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

For every
M>0
and
\ell\in Z^{mn}
,
\ell\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)=\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}e^{-2\pi iq_{1}^{-1}\ell_{1}\cdot\theta}\widehat{\phi}(\Psi(q)q_{j_{0}}^{-1}\ell_{j_{0}})\quad\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}.

where
j_{0}
is chosen so that
|\ell|=|\ell_{j_{0}}|
.

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
4.2.1
.
∎

For part (d) of the next lemma, recall the definition of
M_{0}
from Lemma
4.1.4
.

Lemma 4.3.2
.

(1)

\widehat{F_{M}}(0)=1
for every
M>0

(2)

|\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)|\leq 1
for every
M>0
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}

(3)

\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)=0
for every
M\geq 2
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
,
0<|\ell|\leq\left({\dfrac{M}{2\log_{2}^{n+1}(M)}}\right)^{1/2mn}

(4)

|\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)|\leq C_{\zeta}|\ell|^{-s}w_{\zeta}(|\ell|)\log^{n+1}(M)
for every
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
M\geq M_{0}
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
,
|\ell|\geq 3
,
\zeta>\log 2
.

Proof.

(1)

Since
\widehat{\phi}(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(x)dx=1
and
D(0)=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, Lemma
4.2.1
implies (a).

(2)

Since
\phi\geq 0
, we have
F_{M}\geq 0
, which gives
|\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)|\leq\widehat{F_{M}}(0)
. Then (a) implies (b).

(3)

Because of the definition of
Q^{\prime}(M)
,
if
0<|\ell|\leq\left({{M}/{(2\log_{2}^{n+1}(M))}}\right)^{1/2mn}
,
then the sum over
q
in Lemma
4.3.1

is empty; hence,
\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)=0
.

(4)

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
,
|\ell|\geq 3
. Let
M\in\mathcal{M}
,
M\geq M_{0}
. By Lemma
4.3.1
and (
4.1
),

\displaystyle|\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}|\widehat{\phi}(\Psi(q)q_{j_{0}}^{-1}\ell_{j_{0}})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\zeta}}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}(1+\Psi(q)|q|^{-1}|\ell|)^{-K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\zeta}}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}(1+\Psi(q)|q|^{-1}|\ell|)^{-s}

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\zeta}}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}(1+\Psi_{*}(q)|\ell|)^{-s}

\displaystyle\leq|\ell|^{-s}\frac{C_{\zeta}}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)\cap D(\ell)}\frac{1}{\Psi_{*}(q)^{s}}

For all
q\in Q(M)
, we have
1/{\Psi^{s}_{*}(q)}\leq M
, so

|\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)|\leq|\ell|^{-s}\frac{C_{\zeta}}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}|D(\ell)|M

The desired result now follows from Lemma
4.1.3
and Lemma
4.1.4
.

∎

Lemma 4.3.3
.

(1)

For every
M>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(F_{M})\subseteq\bigcup_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)}\bigcup_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}:|xq-r-\theta|\leq\Psi(q)\}.

(2)

For every sequence
(M_{k})
that satisfies
0<2M_{k}\leq M_{k+1}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{supp}(F_{M_{k}})\subseteq E(m,n,Q^{\prime},\Psi,\theta).

Proof.

Observe that, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
,

\displaystyle F_{M}(x)=\frac{1}{|Q^{\prime}(M)|}\sum_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)}\sum_{r\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}}\Psi(q)^{-m}\phi(\Psi(q)^{-1}(xq-r-\theta)).

Since
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subseteq(-1,1)^{m}
, there is some constant
0<c<1
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subseteq[-c,c]^{m}
.
If
F_{M}(x)>0
, then there is some
q\in Q^{\prime}(M)
and some
r\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
such that
\phi(\Psi(q)^{-1}(xq-r-\theta))>0
.
Hence
|xq-r-\theta|\leq c\Psi(q)

and

|r|\leq c\Psi(q)+|\theta|+|x||q|_{1}.

Now suppose
x^{\prime}\in\mathrm{supp}(F_{M})
.
Choose a sequence
(x_{k})
that converges to
x^{\prime}
and satisfies
F_{M}(x_{k})>0
for all
k
.
Since
(x_{k})
converges, there is some constant
C>0
such that
|x_{k}|\leq C
for all
k
. Thus
(x_{k})
is contained in the set

\bigcup_{q\in Q^{\prime}(M)}\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\\
|r|\leq c\Psi(q)+|\theta|+C|q|_{1}\end{subarray}}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}:|xq-r-\theta|\leq c\Psi(q)\}

Since this set is closed, it contains
x^{\prime}
.
This proves (a).
If
(M_{k})
satisfies
2M_{k}\leq M_{k+1}
for all
k
,
then the sets
Q^{\prime}(M_{k})
are pairwise disjoint,
so (b) follows from (a).
∎

4.4.
Stability Lemma

The next lemma will
let us pass the Fourier decay of the function
F_{M}
to the measure
\mu
.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

Define

g(\xi)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }|\xi|\leq 3,\\
|\xi|^{-s}w_{1}(|\xi|)\log^{n+1}(|\xi|)&\text{if }|\xi|>3.\end{cases}

For every
\delta>0
,
M_{0}>0
, and
\chi\in C^{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})
, there is an

M_{\ast}(\delta,M_{0},\chi)\in\mathcal{M}

and
C_{\chi}>0
such that
M_{\ast}\geq M_{0}
and

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi F_{M_{\ast}}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|\leq\delta g(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}.

Note: The proof will show
M_{\ast}
can be taken to be any sufficiently large element of
\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

We start with two facts.
First, since
\chi\in C^{K}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})
, there is a
C_{\chi}>0
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|\leq C_{\chi}(1+|\xi|)^{-K}\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}.

Second, for every
p>mn
, we have

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}}\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-p}<\infty.

Now we expand the expression
\widehat{\chi F_{M_{\ast}}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)
.
Since
F_{M}
is
C^{K}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{mn}
-periodic, we have

F_{M}(x)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}}\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)e^{2\pi i\ell\cdot x}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}

with uniform convergence.
Since
\chi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})
, multiplying by
\chi
and taking the Fourier transform yields

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi F_{M}}(\xi)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}}\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{mn}}\chi(x)e^{2\pi i(\ell-\xi)\cdot x}dx=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}}\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)\widehat{\chi}(\xi-\ell)

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
.
Then by Lemma
4.3.2
(a) and (c) we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi F_{M}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{mn}}\widehat{\chi}(\xi-\ell)\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)=\sum_{|\ell|>h}\widehat{\chi}(\xi-\ell)\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell).

Define
h=\left({{M}/{(2\log_{2}^{n+1}(M))}}\right)^{1/2mn}
. Note
h\rightarrow\infty
as
M\rightarrow\infty
.
Define
\eta=(K-mn-s)/2
. Recall that
K
was chosen to be greater than
mn+s
, so
\eta
is positive.
To estimate
\widehat{\chi F_{M}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)
,
we
consider two cases.

Case 1:
Assume
|\xi|<h/2
. If
|\ell|>h
, then
|\xi-\ell|>h/2>|\xi|
. Hence by Lemma
4.3.2
(b), (
4.2
),(
4.3
), and (
4.4
) we have

\displaystyle|\widehat{\chi F_{M}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}\sum_{|\ell|>h}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-K}=C_{\chi}\sum_{|\ell|>h}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-s-\eta-(mn+\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}(1+|\xi|)^{-s}(1+h/2)^{-\eta}\sum_{|\ell|>h}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-(mn+\eta)}\leq\delta g(\xi)

for all sufficiently large M.

Case 2:
Assume
|\xi|\geq h/2
.
Thus
(2|\xi|)^{2mn}\geq M
.
Using (
4.4
), write

\widehat{\chi F_{M}}(\xi)-\widehat{\chi}(\xi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|\leq|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}\widehat{\chi}(\xi-\ell)\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|>|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}\widehat{\chi}(\xi-\ell)\widehat{F_{M}}(\ell)=S_{1}+S_{2}.

If
|\ell|\leq|\xi|/2
, then
|\xi-\ell|\geq|\xi|/2\geq h/4
.
Hence by Lemma
4.3.2
(b), (
4.2
), and (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle|S_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|\leq|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-K}=C_{\chi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|\leq|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-s-\eta-(mn+\eta)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\chi}(1+|\xi|/2)^{-s}(1+h/4)^{-\eta}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|\leq|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-(mn+\eta)}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}g(\xi)

for all sufficiently large
M
.

Fix
\log 2<\zeta<1.
Note that
|\ell|^{-s}w_{\zeta}(|\ell|)
is an eventually decreasing function.
By
4.3.2
(d), (
4.2
), and (
4.3
),
we have

\displaystyle|S_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\zeta}C_{\chi}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|>|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}|\ell|^{-s}w_{\zeta}(|\ell|)\log^{n+1}(M)(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\zeta}C_{\chi}(|\xi|/2)^{-s}w_{\zeta}(|\xi|/2)\log^{n+1}((2|\xi|)^{2mn})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\ell|>h\\
|\ell|>|\xi|/2\end{subarray}}(1+|\xi-\ell|)^{-K}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{2}(|\xi|)^{-s}w_{1}(|\xi|)\log^{n+1}(|\xi|)=\frac{\delta}{2}g(\xi)

for all sufficiently large
M\in\mathcal{M}
.

∎

4.5.
The Measure
\mu

To complete the proof of Proposition
1.4.4
, the measure
\mu
with the desired Fourier decay and support properties (see Subsection
4.1
)
is constructed the same way as in
[
12
]
.
Arbitrarily fix
f_{0}\in C_{c}^{K}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{mn}}f_{0}(x)dx=1
,
\text{supp}(f_{0})=[-1,1]^{mn}
, and
f_{0}(x)>0
for all
|x|<1
.
Use Lemma
4.4.1
to define numbers

M_{1}=M_{\ast}(2^{-1},1,f_{0}),\quad M_{k}=M_{\ast}(2^{-k-1},2M_{k-1},f_{0}F_{M_{1}}\cdots F_{M_{k-1}})\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},k\neq 1.

and measures

d\mu_{0}=f_{0}dx,\quad d\mu_{k}=f_{0}F_{M_{1}}\cdots F_{M_{k}}dx\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}

such that

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mu_{k}}(\xi)-\widehat{\mu_{k-1}}(\xi)|\leq 2^{-k-1}g(\xi)\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{mn},k\in\mathbb{N}.

Lévy’s continuity theorem implies

(\mu_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
converges weakly
(i.e., in distribution) to a finite non-zero Borel measure
\mu
satisfying

|\widehat{\mu}(\xi)|=o(|\xi|^{-s})
as
\xi\to\infty
.
Multiplying by an appropriate constant, makes
\mu
a probability measure.
Lemma
4.3.3
implies
\mathrm{supp}(\mu)\subseteq E(m,n,Q,\Psi,\theta)
.
Since there is nothing new about the construction, we omit further the details.

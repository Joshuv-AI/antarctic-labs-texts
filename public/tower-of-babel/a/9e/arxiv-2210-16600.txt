Stability and large-time behavior on 3D incompressible MHD equations with partial dissipation near a background magnetic field
Hongxia Lin 1 , Jiahong Wu 2 and Yi Zhu 3

Abstract
. Physical experiments and numerical simulations have observed a remarkable stabilizing phenomenon: a background magnetic field stabilizes and damps electrically conducting fluids. This paper intends to establish this phenomenon as a mathematically rigorous fact on a magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) system with anisotropic dissipation in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . The velocity equation in this system is the 3D Navier-Stokes equation with dissipation only in the x 1 x_{1} -direction while the magnetic field obeys the induction equation with magnetic diffusion in two horizontal directions. We establish that any perturbation near the background magnetic field ( 0 , 1 , 0 ) (0,1,0) is globally stable in the Sobolev setting H 3 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) . In addition, explicit decay rates in H 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) are also obtained. When there is no presence of the magnetic field, the 3D anisotropic Navier-Stokes equation in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} is not well understood and the small data global well-posedness remains an intriguing open problem. This paper reveals the mechanism of how the magnetic field generates enhanced dissipation and helps stabilize the fluid.

Introduction

This paper deals with the stability and large-time behavior problem on a system of 3D anisotropic MHD equations near a background magnetic field. To shed some light on the potential
difficulties of this problem, we briefly review several facts on the behavior of solutions to the Euler and the anisotropic Navier-Stokes equations.

It is well-known that solutions of the incompressible Euler equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla P,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0\end{cases}

can grow rather rapidly in time. In fact, Kiselev and Sverak are able to construct
a vorticity solution of the 2D Euler equations in a disk whose gradient grows double exponentially in time
[
42
]
. In the periodic setting, an example of Zlatos
shows that the vorticity gradient can grow at least exponentially
[
88
]
. Choi and Jeong
obtain linear in time growth for the vorticity gradient for certain smooth and compactly supported initial vorticity in
\mathbb{R}^{2}

[
16
]
.
Classical solutions to the 3D Euler equations could develop finite-time singularities (
[
14
,
28
]
). Many more results in this direction can be found in a review paper by Drivas and Elgindi
[
25
]
.
As a special consequence, perturbations governed by the Euler equations near the trivial solution
are generally not stable. How much dissipation does one really need in order to achieve the
stability? Adding the full Laplacian dissipation is certainly sufficient. As demonstrated by
Schonbek and others (see, e.g.,
[
60
,
61
,
62
,
70
]
), solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla P+\mu\Delta u,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0\end{cases}

are asymptotically stable and decay in time with explicit decay rates. When the dissipation is anisotropic and only in two directions, the Navier-Stokes equations become

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla P+\mu\Delta_{h}u,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0.\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Delta_{h}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\partial_{2}^{2}
is the horizontal Laplacian. Due to its physical applications
and special mathematical properties, (
1.1
) has attracted considerable interests and an array of beautiful small data global well-posedness results have been obtained
(see, e.g.,
[
12
,
13
,
38
,
52
,
54
,
55
,
83
,
84
]
).
New approaches have very recently been developed to tackle the large-time behavior problem and
explicit decay rates have been extracted for (
1.1
) (see
[
40
,
77
]
). If we further reduce the dissipation to be in just one
direction, the resulting 3D anisotropic Navier-Stokes equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla P+\mu\partial_{1}^{2}u,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\,\,t>0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=0\end{cases}

(1.2)

is not well-understood. In particular, the small data global well-posedness problem remains open.
In addition, very little is known on the stability properties and the large-time behavior.

This paper focuses on the following system of the 3D MHD equations with anisotropic dissipation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u=-\nabla P+\mu\partial_{1}^{2}u+(B\cdot\nabla)B,\ \ \ x\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}},\ t>0,\\
\partial_{t}B+(u\cdot\nabla)B=\eta\,\Delta_{h}B+(B\cdot\nabla)u,\\
\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot B=0\end{array}\right.

with the initial data

u(x,0)=u_{0},\,\quad B(x,0)=B_{0}.

Here
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})^{\top},B=(B_{1},B_{2},B_{3})^{\top}
and

represent the velocity field of the fluid, the magnetic field and the scalar pressure, respectively. The constants
\mu>0

are the viscosity coefficient and the magnetic diffusivity. The MHD system (
1

focused here is relevant in the modeling of reconnecting plasmas (see, e.g.,
[

The motivation for studying (

stabilizing phenomenon observed in physical experiments involving electrically conducting fluids.
The experiments exhibit a remarkable phenomenon: a background magnetic field actually stabilizes
and damps turbulent MHD fluids (see, e.g.,

We explain the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Due to the lack of velocity dissipation in two
directions, we take the functional setting to be the Sobolev space
H^{3}
in order to guarantee
the uniqueness. The local existence follows from a standard procedure (see, e.g.,
[
53
]
), so
we focus on the global
a priori
bounds of
(u,b)
. This is accomplished via the bootstrapping
argument (see, e.g.,
[
66
]
). A crucial step is to construct a suitable energy
functional. Naturally it should include the
H^{3}
-norm together with the time integral pieces
from the dissipative terms

E^{(1)}_{0}(t)=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|(u(\tau),b(\tau))\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\partial_{1}u(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}b(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Big)d\tau.

However, due to the lack of velocity dissipation in two directions, the triple product generated by
the nonlinearity, namely
((u\cdot\nabla)u,u)_{H^{3}}
can not be bounded in terms of
E^{(1)}_{0}(t)
. The
most difficult piece is the following triple product

\int\partial_{3}^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx.

Here we have used
\int
to denote the integral in
x
over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. To distinguish the
derivatives in different directions, we further write it as

\displaystyle\int\partial_{3}^{3}(u\cdot\nabla u)\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx

\displaystyle=3\int\partial_{3}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{3}^{2}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx+3\int\partial_{3}^{2}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}\partial_{3}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx+\int\partial_{3}^{3}u_{h}\cdot\nabla_{h}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+3\int\partial_{3}u_{3}\,\partial_{3}^{3}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx+3\int\partial_{3}^{2}u_{3}\,\partial_{3}^{2}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx+\int\partial_{3}^{3}u_{3}\,\partial_{3}u\cdot\partial_{3}^{3}u\,dx.

(1.11)

Clearly we need to seek enhanced dissipation in the
x_{2}
or the
x_{3}
direction to complement the
existing dissipation in the
x_{1}
-direction. The background magnetic field is along the
x_{2}
direction and it is in this direction that the extra regularization is generated. Mathematically
this is reflected in the wave structure. We explain this. To avoid unnecessary complications, we
look at the linearized system of (
1
), namely

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\mu\partial_{2}^{2}u+\partial_{2}b,\\
\partial_{t}b=\eta\Delta_{h}b+\partial_{2}u,\\
\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot b=0.\end{cases}

(1.12)

By differentiating (
1.12
) in
t
and making several substitutions, (
1.12
) can be converted
into the following system of wave equations

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u-(\mu\partial_{2}^{2}+\eta\Delta_{h})\partial_{t}u+\mu\eta\partial_{2}^{2}\Delta_{h}u-\partial_{2}^{2}u=0,\\
\partial_{tt}b-(\mu\partial_{2}^{2}+\eta\Delta_{h})\partial_{t}b+\mu\eta\partial_{2}^{2}\Delta_{h}b-\partial_{2}^{2}b=0,\\
\nabla\cdot u=\nabla\cdot b=0.\end{cases}

(1.13)

(
1.13
) is a system of anisotropic and degenerate wave equations. In comparison with (
1.12
),
(
1.13
) exhibits much more smoothing and stabilizing properties. In particular, the two terms

\partial_{2}^{2}u
and
\partial_{2}^{2}b
in (
1.13
), emerged from the interaction of the velocity and the magnetic
field, generates the dissipation in the
x_{2}
-direction. This confirms the stabilizing effect of the background magnetic field. To include this regularizing property in the energy functional,
we define

E^{(2)}_{0}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{2}u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}d\tau.

We emphasize that the extra dissipative effect in the
x_{2}
-direction is one-derivative lower than
what a standard dissipation term
\partial^{2}_{2}u
provides. This is why this energy functional only allows
the time integrability of
\|\partial_{2}u\|_{H^{2}}^{2}
, not
\|\partial_{2}u\|_{H^{3}}^{2}
.
Combining
E^{(1)}_{0}
and
E^{(2)}_{0}
gives

\displaystyle E_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=E^{(1)}_{0}+E^{(2)}_{0}

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|(u(\tau),b(\tau))\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\partial_{1}u(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\partial_{2}u(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}b(\tau)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Big)d\tau.

However, there are still two terms in (
1
) (the third term and the fourth term) that can
not be bounded in terms of
E_{0}(t)
. After invoking the divergence-free condition

\partial_{3}u_{3}=-\partial_{1}u_{1}-\partial_{2}u_{2}
, these terms are reduced to the difficult term

\displaystyle\int|\partial_{2}u|\,|\partial_{3}^{3}u|\,|\partial_{3}^{3}u|dx.

(1.14)

Due to the aforementioned weaker smoothing effect in the
x_{2}
-direction, (
1.14
) can not be bounded by
E_{0}(t)
. Extra maneuvers are necessary. Our idea
is to include two extra time-weighted energy functionals

\displaystyle E_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}(1+\tau)\|(\nabla_{h}u(\tau),\nabla_{h}b(\tau))\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)\Big(\|\partial_{1}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\partial_{2}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla_{h}^{2}b(\tau)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\Big)d\tau,

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)=

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\Big((1+\tau)\|(u(\tau),b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1+\tau)^{2}\|(\nabla_{h}u(\tau),\nabla_{h}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\tau)^{1-2\varepsilon}\|(\partial_{3}u(\tau),\partial_{3}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{2}(1+\tau)^{\frac{5}{2}-2\varepsilon}\|(\partial_{1}\partial_{j}u(\tau),\partial_{1}\partial_{j}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{3}(1+\tau)^{\frac{4}{3}-2\varepsilon}\|(\partial_{2}\partial_{j}u(\tau),\partial_{2}\partial_{j}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+(1+\tau)^{2-2\varepsilon}\|(\partial_{1}\partial_{3}u(\tau),\partial_{1}\partial_{3}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1+\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{3}^{2}u(\tau),\partial_{3}^{2}b(\tau))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\Big).

We shall show that the inclusion of
E_{1}(t)
and
E_{2}(t)
enables us to bound the term in (
1.14
)
suitably and thus establish a closed energy inequality. The definition of
E_{2}(t)
is certainly
not simple. It takes into account of the precise time decay rate of each norm involved in
E_{2}(t)
.
We will resort to the integral representation of (
1
) and spectral analysis to control the
terms in
E_{2}(t)
. Having obtained the necessary components of the energy functional, we sum them
up to form our total energy functional

E(t)=E_{0}(t)+E_{1}(t)+E_{2}(t).\\

Our main efforts are devoted to proving the following estimate

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C_{1}F(u_{0},b_{0})+C_{2}\Big(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E^{2}(t)\Big),

(1.15)

where
C_{1}
and

are constants, and

\displaystyle F(u_{0},b_{0})

\displaystyle=\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{3}u_{0},\partial_{3}b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{3}^{2}u_{0},\partial_{3}^{2}b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}.

1.15
) is a very lengthy process. For the sake of clarity, we divide

\displaystyle E_{0}(t)\leq CE(0)+CE^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),

(1.16)

\displaystyle E_{1}(t)\leq CE(0)+CE_{0}(t)+CE^{\frac{3}{2}}(t),

(1.17)

\displaystyle E_{2}(t)\leq C\Big(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E^{2}(t)\Big)+C\Big(\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}

(1.18)

and

, as aforementioned. The boundedness of

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C_{1}F(u_{0},b_{0})+C_{2}\Big(E^{\frac{3}{2}}(t)+E^{2}(t)\Big),

(2.1)

\displaystyle F(u_{0},b_{0})

\displaystyle=\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|(u_{0},b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}+\|(\partial_{3}u_{0},\partial_{3}b_{0})\|_{L^{2}_{x_{3}}L^{1}_{x_{1}x_{2}}}^{2}

In order to apply the bootstrapping argument, we make the ansatz that

\displaystyle E(t)\leq M:=\min\Big\{1,\frac{1}{(4C_{2})^{2}}\Big\}.

(2.2)

We then verify that
E(t)
actually admits a smaller bound,

\displaystyle E(t)\leq\frac{M}{2}.

Inserting (
2.2
) in (
4.1
) and recalling the initial assumption (
1.1
), we have

\displaystyle E(t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}F(u_{0},b_{0})+C_{2}\Big(M^{\frac{1}{2}}+M\Big)E(t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\delta^{2}+2C_{2}M^{\frac{1}{2}}E(t)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\delta^{2}+\frac{1}{2}E(t),

or

\displaystyle E(t)\leq 2C_{1}\delta^{2}.

If the initial data is sufficiently small, say

\delta^{2}\leq\frac{M}{4C_{1}},

then we derive

\displaystyle E(t)\leq 2C_{1}\delta^{2}\leq\frac{M}{2}.

The bootstrapping argument then implies
T=\infty
and asserts that for any time
t>0
,

\displaystyle E(t)\leq C\delta^{2},

which, in particular, implies the desired global bound on the solution
(u,b)
. As a
consequence, we obtain the global existence of solutions. The uniqueness is obvious due to the
high regularity of the solution. The global bound on
E_{2}(t)
yields the desired decay rates
stated in Theorem
1.1
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

is a small parameter. Next we show the bound of

5.1.
Integral representation and bounds for the kernels

The subsection derives the integral representation of (

The first one specifies the decay rate of a general heat operator associated with a fractional
Laplacian operator. Here the fractional Laplacian operator can be defined through the Fourier transform

\widehat{\Lambda^{\alpha}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(\xi).

The decay rate is stated in the following lemma, whose proof can be found in many references (see, e.g.,

Lemma 5.2

Assume

The second tool is an elementary inequality providing upper bounds for a convolution type integral. Its proof is straightforward.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
0<s_{1}\leq s_{2}
. Then, for some constant

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-s_{1}}(1+\tau)^{-s_{2}}\ d\tau\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C(1+t)^{-s_{1}},\ \ \ \ \ \text{if}\ \ s_{2}>1,\\
C(1+t)^{-s_{1}}\ln(1+t),\ \text{if}\ \ s_{2}=1,\\
C(1+t)^{1-s_{1}-s_{2}},\ \text{if}\ \ s_{2}<1.\end{array}\right.

Now we derive an integral representation of (

\mathbb{P}=I-\nabla\Delta^{-1}\nabla\cdot

\partial_{t}\bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{u}\cr&\widehat{b}}=A\bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{u}\cr&\widehat{b}}+\bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{N}_{1}\cr&\widehat{N}_{2}},

\bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{u}\cr&\widehat{b}}=e^{At}\ \bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{u_{0}}\cr&\widehat{b_{0}}}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{A(t-\tau)}\ \bordermatrix{\text{}\cr&\widehat{N_{1}}(\tau)\cr&\widehat{N_{2}}(\tau)}d\tau.

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\partial_{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{3}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{3}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{3}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}).

(5.54)

Inserting (
5.2
) in (
5.53
), and using Lemma
5.3
, the first three terms can be bounded by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\|\partial_{3}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|u_{3}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{3}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{3}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\big)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE_{2}(t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big((1+\tau)^{-\frac{13}{12}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}d\tau+(1+\tau)^{-\frac{25}{24}+\frac{3\varepsilon}{2}}\Big)d\tau

\displaystyle\quad+CE_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{3}{4}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)\Big((1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\Big).

The last term needs more subtle estimates. We resort to Hölder’s inequality and the integrability of
\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{h}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}u_{h}(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-1}(1+\tau)^{-1+\varepsilon}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|\partial_{3}^{2}\nabla_{h}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}d\tau\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CE_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Combining all the estimates above, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)(\tau)\|_{L_{x_{3}}^{2}L_{x_{1}x_{2}}^{1}}\,d\tau\leq CE(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle K_{3331}\leq CE(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(5.55)

Finally, applying Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev’s inequality, for
m>1
, we infer

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-m}\|\partial_{3}^{2}(u\cdot\nabla u)(\tau)\|_{L^{2}{({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-m}(\|\nabla^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{4}}+\|u(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\partial_{3}^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-m}(\|\nabla^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|\nabla u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\partial_{3}^{2}u(\tau)\|_{L^{2}})\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq CE_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)E_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-m}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{1}{4}}d\tau+\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-\tau)^{-m}(1+\tau)^{-\frac{3}{8}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\,d\tau\Big)

\displaystyle\leq CE(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle K_{3332}\leq CE(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

which, together with (
5.55
), yields

\displaystyle K_{333}\leq CE(t)(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(5.56)

As a consequence of (
5.2
) and (
5.56
),

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{3}^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq CE(t)+C(\|(\partial_{3}^{2}u_{0},\partial_{3}^{2}b_{0})\|_{L_{x_{3}}^{2}L_{x_{1}x_{2}}^{1}}+\|(\partial_{3}^{2}u_{0},\partial_{3}^{2}b_{0})\|_{L^{2}}).

Combining all the estimates for the four cases above, we derive the desired estimate (
5.7
). This completes the proof of Lemma
5.7
.
∎

Proposition
5.1
then follows from the estimates (
5.11
), (
5.12
), (
5.21
) and (
5.7
). This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

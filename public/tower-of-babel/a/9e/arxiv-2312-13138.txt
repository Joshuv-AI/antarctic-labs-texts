Breakdown of homoclinic orbits to L 3 L_{3} : Nonvanishing of the Stokes constant
Inmaculada Baldomá, Maciej J. Capiński, Mar Giralt, Marcel Guardia

Abstract
The Restricted Planar Circular 3-Body Problem models the motion of a body of negligible mass under the gravitational influence of two massive bodies, called the primaries, which perform circular orbits coplanar with that of the massless body. In rotating coordinates, it can be modelled by a two degrees of freedom Hamiltonian system, which has five critical points called the Lagrange points. Among them, the point L 3 L_{3} is a saddle-center which is collinear with the primaries and beyond the largest of the two. The papers [ 3 , 4 ] provide an asymptotic formula for the distance between the one dimensional stable and unstable manifolds of L 3 L_{3} in a transverse section for small values of the mass ratio 0 < μ ≪ 1 0<\mu\ll 1 . This distance is exponentially small with respect to μ \mu and its first order depends on what is usually called a Stokes constant. The non-vanishing of this constant implies that the distance between the invariant manifolds at the section is not zero. In this paper, we prove that the Stokes constant is non-zero. The proof is computer assisted.

The Restricted Circular
3

If one also assumes that the massless body moves on the same plane as the primaries one has the Restricted Planar Circular

Remark 1.4

A fundamental question in dynamical systems is to prove that a system exhibits chaotic dynamics.
One of the classical ways to prove this it is to show the existence of transverse intersections between the stable and unstable manifolds of a particular invariant object (see

In the case of

The invariant manifolds of
L_{3}
and the inner equation

Theorem
1.1
falls into what is usually called exponentially small splitting of separatrices. That is, on the perturbative analysis of the distance between the stable and unstable manifold of an invariant object when it is exponentially small.
Its proof follows the original approach proposed by Lazutkin in his seminal work on the Standard Map
[
15
,
16
]
. Note that the papers
[
3
,
4
]
are the first ones where such approach has been implemented in a Celestial Mechanics model. We refer to
[
3
]
for a detailed list of references on the exponentially small splitting of separatrices phenomenon.

Let us summarize the main steps of this proof and explain where the Stokes constant arises.

Note first that, in the limit problem
H
in (
1.1
) with
\mu=0
, the five Lagrange points and the associated invariant manifolds “collapse” into the circle of (degenerate) critical points
\left\lVert q\right\rVert=1
and
p=(p_{1},p_{2})=(-q_{2},q_{1})
.
Therefore, to analyze the invariant manifolds, it is convenient to perform a singular change of coordinates to obtain a “new first order” Hamiltonian which has a saddle-center equilibrium point with stable and unstable manifolds that coincide along a separatrix.
This change of coordinates boils down to a suitable (singular with respect to
\mu
) scaling of the classical Poincaré planar elements (see
[
17
]
). These coordinates are explained in full detail in
[
3
]
.

In these coordinates, the proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the following steps.

A.

We perform the aformentioned change of coordinates which captures the slow-fast dynamics of the system.
The new Hamiltonian becomes a (fast) oscillator weakly coupled to a
1
-degree of freedom Hamiltonian with a saddle point and a separatrix associated to it.

B.

We analyze the analytical continuation of a time-parametrization of the separatrix.
In particular, we obtain its maximal strip of analyticity, which is given by
|\mathrm{Im\,}t|<A
where
A
is the constant introduced in (
1.2
) and
t
the time of the parametrization.
We also describe the character and location of the complex singularities at the boundaries of this region.

C.

We derive the inner equation, which gives the first order of the original system close to the singularities of the separatrix described in Step B.
This equation is independent of the perturbative parameter
\mu
.

D.

We study two special solutions of the inner equation which are approximations of the perturbed invariant manifolds near the singularities.
Moreover, we provide an asymptotic formula for the difference between these two solutions of the inner equation.
This difference is given in terms of the Stokes constant
\Theta
introduced in Theorem
1.1
.

E.

We prove the existence of the analytic continuation of suitable parametrizations of
W^{{\mathrm{u}},+}(L_{3})
and
W^{{\mathrm{s}},+}(L_{3})
in appropriate complex domains (and as graphs).
These domains contain a segment of
the real line and intersect a neighborhood sufficiently close to the singularities of the separatrix.

F.

By using complex matching techniques, we compare the solutions of the inner equation with the graph parametrizations of the perturbed invariant manifolds.

G.

Finally, we prove that the dominant term of the difference between manifolds is given by the term obtained from the difference of the solutions of the inner equation.

Steps A, B, C and D are performed in
[

is analogous and leads to exactly the same estimates.

4.1
The fixed point equation and the functional setting

The invariance equation (
2.5
) can be written as
\mathcal{L}Z^{\mathrm{u}}=\mathcal{R}[Z^{\mathrm{u}}]

where
\mathcal{L}
is the linear operator

\mathcal{L}\varphi=(\partial_{U}-\mathcal{A})\varphi.

To construct a fixed point equation from (
2.5
), we consider the following left inverse operator of
\mathcal{L}
,

\mathcal{G}[\varphi](U)=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\varphi_{1}(s+U)ds,\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{-is}\varphi_{2}(s+U)ds,\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{is}\varphi_{3}(s+U)ds\right)^{T},

(4.1)

(see Lemma
4.4
below). We look for a fixed point of the operator

\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{R},

(4.2)

in a suitable Banach space.

Given
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa>0
, we define the norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\nu}=\sup_{U\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\kappa}}\left|U^{\nu}\varphi(U)\right|,

where the domain
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\kappa}
is given in (
2.8
),
and we introduce the Banach space

\begin{split}\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\nu}&=\left\{\varphi:\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\kappa}\to\mathbb{C}\hskip 2.84526pt:\hskip 2.84526pt\varphi\text{ analytic, }\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\nu}<+\infty\right\}.\end{split}

(4.3)

A solution of

Z^{\mathrm{u}}=\mathcal{F}[Z^{\mathrm{u}}]

belonging to
\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}\times\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\nu}\times\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\nu}

with
\eta,\nu>0

satisfies equation (
2.5
) and the asymptotic condition (
2.7
).
Then, to prove Theorem
3.1
, we look for a fixed point of the operator
\mathcal{F}
in the Banach space

\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\times}=\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\frac{8}{3}}\times\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\frac{4}{3}}\times\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\frac{4}{3}},

(4.4)

endowed with the norm

\lVert\varphi\rVert_{\times}=\max\left\{\lVert\varphi_{1}\rVert_{\frac{8}{3}},\lVert\varphi_{2}\rVert_{\frac{4}{3}},\lVert\varphi_{3}\rVert_{\frac{4}{3}}\right\}.

(4.5)

Theorem
3.1
is a direct consequence of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\kappa\geq\kappa^{*}
where
\kappa^{*}
is the constant introduced in (
3.1
), the fixed point equation
Z^{\mathrm{u}}={\mathcal{F}}[Z^{\mathrm{u}}]
has a solution
Z^{\mathrm{u}}\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\times}
.

We devote the rest of this section to prove Proposition
4.1
. Note that our goal is to compute explicit estimates throughout the proof to obtain a “not too large”
\kappa^{*}
.

Let us explain the main steps of the proof, which are carried out in the forthcoming sections.

•

In Section
4.2
, we provide properties of the Banach spaces introduced in (
4.3
) and give explicit bounds for the norm of the linear operator
\mathcal{G}
in (
4.1
).

•

In Section
4.3
, we give estimates for
\mathcal{F}[0]
.

•

In Section
4.4
, we provide estimates for the derivatives of
\mathcal{R}
(see (
2.6
)) in a suitable domain.

•

In Section
4.5
, we provide explicit expressions for the Lipschitz constant of the operator
\mathcal{F}
in a suitable region of the Banach space (
4.4
). Finally, we prove that the Lipschitz constant is smaller than
1
, and therefore
\mathcal{F}
is contractive and has a unique fixed point.

To have accurate estimates in these steps, we rely on explicit formulae for the non-linear operator
\mathcal{R}
in (
2.6
) and its first and second derivatives. They are given in Appendix
A
.

Note that in
[
3
]
, we were not able to prove that
\mathcal{F}
is contractive. Instead, we considered a slightly modified operator which had the same fixed points as
\mathcal{F}
and a smaller Lipschitz constant. Since the estimates in the present paper are more accurate, we are able to prove that the original operator
\mathcal{F}
is contractive.

The proof of this lemma follows the same lines of the proof of
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
. We first state the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\eta>1
. Then,

\int^{0}_{-\infty}\frac{ds}{(1+s^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)}.

Proof.

Considering the change of coordinates
s=\tan x
,

\int^{0}_{-\infty}\frac{ds}{(1+s^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\leq\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{0}\frac{dx}{(1+\tan(x)^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}\cos^{2}(x)}=\int^{\frac{\pi}{2}}_{0}\cos^{\eta-2}(x)dx.

To complete the proof of the lemma, it only remains to recall that

\int^{\frac{\pi}{2}}_{0}\cos^{\eta-2}(x)dx=\frac{1}{2}B\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)},

where

is the classical beta function.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

To prove the first item of the lemma, we consider
\eta>1
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}
.
Then, for
j=1,2,3
and
U\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{u}}_{\kappa}
, one has that

\left|\mathcal{G}_{j}[\varphi](U)\right|\leq\lVert\varphi\rVert^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}\int^{0}_{-\infty}\frac{dS}{\left|s+U\right|^{\eta}}.

Moreover, for
s\in(-\infty,0]
, one has that
\left|s+U\right|^{2}\geq s^{2}+\left|U\right|^{2}
. Then, by Lemma

\left|\mathcal{G}_{j}[\varphi](U)\right|\leq\int^{0}_{-\infty}\frac{\lVert\varphi\rVert^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}}{(s^{2}+\left|U\right|^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}ds=\frac{\lVert\varphi\rVert^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}}{\left|U\right|^{\eta-1}}\int_{-\infty}^{0}\frac{ds}{(1+s^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}}\leq\frac{\lVert\varphi\rVert^{\mathrm{u}}_{\eta}}{\left|U\right|^{\eta-1}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta-1}{2}\right)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\right)}.

Next, we prove the second item.
By the geometry of

and using the Cauchy’s theorem, we can change the path of integration in the integral (

) to
te^{i{\sigma}}
,

Notice that

. Then, the function

has a minimum at

and

.
Notice that, since

Therefore,

The norm for

∎

Formulae for
\mathcal{K}

The Hamiltonian
\mathcal{K}
introduced in (
2.2
) is given by

\mathcal{K}(U,Z)=-\frac{3}{4}U^{\frac{2}{3}}W^{2}-\frac{1}{3U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\mathcal{J}(U,Z)}}-1\right).

Then, its first derivatives are

\begin{split}\partial_{U}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{W^{2}}{2U^{\frac{1}{3}}}+\frac{2}{9U^{\frac{5}{3}}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\mathcal{J}}}-1\right)+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{U}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
&=-\frac{W^{2}}{2U^{\frac{1}{3}}}-\frac{2}{9U^{\frac{5}{3}}}\frac{\mathcal{J}}{\sqrt{1+\mathcal{J}}(1+\sqrt{1+\mathcal{J}})}+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{U}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
\partial_{W}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{3}{2}U^{\frac{2}{3}}W+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{W}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
\partial_{X}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{X}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}},\\
\partial_{Y}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\end{split}

and its second derivatives are

\begin{split}\partial_{UW}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{W}{U^{\frac{1}{3}}}-\frac{1}{9U^{\frac{5}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{W}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{UW}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{U}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{W}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial_{UX}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{1}{9U^{\frac{5}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{X}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{UX}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{U}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{X}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial_{UY}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{1}{9U^{\frac{5}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{UY}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{U}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial^{2}_{W}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=-\frac{3}{2}U^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial^{2}_{W}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{(\partial_{W}\mathcal{J})^{2}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial_{WX}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{WX}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{W}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{X}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial_{WY}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{WY}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{W}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\end{split}

\begin{split}\partial^{2}_{X}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial^{2}_{X}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{(\partial_{X}\mathcal{J})^{2}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial_{XY}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{XY}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial_{X}\mathcal{J}\cdot\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}},\\
\partial^{2}_{Y}\mathcal{K}(U,Z)&=\frac{1}{6U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{\partial^{2}_{Y}\mathcal{J}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{3}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4U^{\frac{2}{3}}}\frac{(\partial_{Y}\mathcal{J})^{2}}{(1+\mathcal{J})^{\frac{5}{2}}}.\end{split}

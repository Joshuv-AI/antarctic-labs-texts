Carrollian superconformal theories and super BMS Preprint: TUW–22–01
Arjun Bagchi, Daniel Grumiller, and Poulami Nandi

Abstract
We investigate the supersymmetric versions of Bondi–Metzner–Sachs or, equivalently, conformal Carroll symmetry in boundary dimensions d > 3 d>3 , with applications of flat space holography in mind. We identify the contraction of the relativistic symmetry relevant for our purposes and construct a finite-dimensional Carrollian superconformal algebra (CSA) before proposing an infinite-dimensional lift. We provide the superspace formulation for 𝒩 = 1 \mathcal{N}=1 CSA and work towards an understanding of the representation theory of the algebra. We conclude with some aspects of 𝒩 > 1 \mathcal{N}>1 CSA.

Introduction

The Holographic Principle
tHooft:1993dmi
;
Susskind:1994vu
is, at present, our most promising tool for understanding quantum gravity. Its best-studied realization is the Anti-de Sitter/Conformal Field Theory (AdS/CFT) correspondence
Maldacena:1997re
;
Gubser:1998bc
;
Witten:1998qj
;
Aharony:1999ti
. The idea of holography,
viz.
understanding a higher-dimensional gravitational theory in terms of a theory without gravity that lives on its boundary, was based on the fact that the entropy of a black hole is proportional to its area and not its volume. This area law is not tied to AdS spacetimes. Therefore, the overwhelming expectation is that holography is true in general and applies also to spacetimes that are not asymptotically AdS. In particular, an outstanding question is how to formulate a holographic duality for asymptotically flat spacetimes, see
Polchinski:1999ry
;
Susskind:1998vk
;
Giddings:1999jq
for some early attempts.

Given the spectacular success of AdS/CFT, a particularly compelling way to attempt building holography in flat space is to take the AdS radius to infinity and the corresponding limit on the CFT side. It has been shown that the infinite radius limit in the bulk manifests itself as a limit where the speed of light in the dual field theory goes to zero
Bagchi:2012cy
. This ultra-relativistic (UR) limit leads to a class of field theories called Carrollian field theories, where the Carroll group replaces the relativistic Poincaré group. The conformal versions of these are Carrollian CFTs. The putative dual theories of flat space are therefore Carrollian CFTs in one lower dimension
Bagchi:2010eg
;
Bagchi:2012cy
;
Bagchi:2016bcd
;
Bagchi:2019xfx
;
Bagchi:2019clu
;
Banerjee:2020qjj
. These field theories live naturally on the null boundary of asymptotically flat spacetime as well as on black hole horizons, see e.g.
Donnay:2019jiz
.

Asymptotic symmetries are generated by boundary condition preserving transformations that do not fall off sufficiently fast near the boundary, see e.g.
Compere:2018aar
;
Harlow:2019yfa
and refs. therein. In many applications, the associated asymptotic symmetry group (ASG) provides an infinite enhancement of the bulk isometries. The most famous examples are asymptotically flat spacetimes in 4d, studied originally by Bondi, van der Burgh, Metzner and Sachs (BMS)
Bondi:1962px
;
Sachs:1962zza
, who found an enhancement of Poincaré symmetries by an infinite set of angle-dependent translations, and asymptotically AdS spacetimes in 3d, where the analysis of Brown and Henneaux
Brown:1986nw
uncovered two copies of the Virasoro algebra generating the asymptotic symmetries. The latter example is seen by many as a precursor to the AdS/CFT correspondence. Therefore, the BMS analysis could be a precursor to a flat space holographic correspondence. This vision is behind many current approaches to flat space holography, including ours.

One version of the BMS group is the semi-direct product of the group of all (local) conformal transformations of the sphere at infinity (also known as super-rotations) with super-translations, the angle-dependent translations at null infinity.

Bosonic story — Carrollian CFTs

We begin our discussions by revisiting the bosonic construction of Carrollian CFTs. We shall quickly remind the reader of the UR limit of a CFT in generic dimensions and the corresponding finite-dimensional CCA emerging from it. We then give this an infinite lift. Finally, mention some rudimentary facts about Carrollian CFT representation theory.

2.2
Representation theory

In
Bagchi:2016bcd
, the representation theory of the CCA based on highest weights was described. The analysis was further extended to fields of different integer and half-integer spins in
Bagchi:2019xfx
. Even though the construction was primarily intended for
d=4
, it was expected to work for higher dimensions as well. For the CCA, the states were labeled with the eigenvalues of dilatation and rotation generators. We briefly describe this construction below.

We label the Carrollian CFT fields with scaling dimension
\Delta
and spin
j
as

[D,\Phi(0,0)]=\Delta\Phi(0,0)\qquad\qquad[J^{2},\Phi(0,0)]=j(j+1)\Phi(0,0)\,.

(10)

The action of Carrollian rotation, space- and time-translation on a generic field is given by

[J_{ij},\Phi(0,0)]=\Sigma_{ij}\Phi(0,0),\quad[H,\Phi(t,x_{i})]=\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x_{i}),\quad[P_{i},\Phi(t,x_{i})]=\partial_{i}\Phi(t,x_{i})\,.

(11)

The Carrollian conformal primaries are defined as

[K_{i},\Phi(0,0)]=0,\;\;[K,\Phi(0,0)]=0,~~[M_{f},\Phi(0,0)]=0~~\text{for polynomial degree}>1\,.

(12)

The fields are not eigenstates of Carrollian boosts. Hence, using the Jacobi identity, the transformation of a generic field under Carrollian boosts can be written as

[B_{k},\Phi(0,0)]=r\varphi_{k}+\,f\sigma_{k}\phi+f^{\prime}\sigma_{k}\chi\,+aA_{t}\delta_{ik}+bA_{k}+\ldots

(13)

Here,
\varphi,\,\{\phi,\chi\},\,\{A_{t},A_{k}\}
denote the primaries involving different spins
(0,\frac{1}{2},1)
. The constants
r,\{f,f^{\prime}\},\{a,b\}
are determined from the dynamics of the corresponding theory. The above expression can be generalised for higher spins as well.

Using the fact that the Carrollian primary
\Phi(t,x_{i})
evolves to a generic spacetime point from the origin as

\Phi(t,x)=U\Phi(0,0)U^{-1}\quad\text{where}~U=e^{-tH-x_{i}P_{i}}

(14)

the action of the finite and infinite sets of generators of CCA on this generic Carrollian primary
\Phi(t,x_{i})
can be written as

\displaystyle[J_{ij},\Phi(t,x_{i})]

\displaystyle=-(x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i})\Phi(t,x)+\Sigma_{ij}\Phi(t,x_{i})

(15a)

\displaystyle[B_{j},\Phi(t,x_{i})]

\displaystyle=-x_{j}\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)+U_{j}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad U_{j}:=U[B_{j},\Phi(0,0)]U^{-1}

(15b)

\displaystyle[D,\Phi(t,x_{i})]

\displaystyle=(-t\partial_{t}-x_{i}\partial_{i}+\Delta)\Phi(t,x_{i})

(15c)

\displaystyle[K_{j},\Phi(t,x_{i})]

\displaystyle=-(2\Delta x_{j}+2x_{j}t\partial_{t}+2x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{i}-2x_{i}\Sigma_{ij}-x_{i}x_{i}\partial_{j})\,\Phi(t,x)+2t\,U_{j}

3.1
Two different SUSY extensions

The BMS algebras arise as the asymptotic symmetries of gravitational theories at the null boundary of asymptotically flat spacetimes. The super BMS algebras, in the same way, are expected to arise as the asymptotic symmetries of flat space supergravity. . The resulting supergravity theory is a twisted version of flat space supergravity, the asymptotic symmetries of which are given by

\displaystyle[L_{m},L_{n}]

\displaystyle=(m-n)\,L_{m+n}

(23a)

For the purposes of this paper, we are interested in constructing higher-dimensional version of the super BMS or equivalently CSAs. We focus on the symmetries of the dual field theory of Poincaré supergravity and hence concentrate entirely on the homogeneous versions of the above-mentioned algebras.

3.2
Constructing the
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{N}=1}
algebra

We aim to construct CSAs for arbitrary dimensions. For the moment, we focus on
d=4
. The strategy that we follow is the following:

•

Take an appropriate UR contraction of the relativistic superalgebra
SU(2,2|\mathcal{N})
, so that we reach the homogeneous version of the contracted algebra. This generates a finite algebra with the same number of generators as
SU(2,2|\mathcal{N})
.

•

Write the super-algebra in a suggestive form and give it an infinite-dimensional lift. For this, we will construct a Carrollian superspace and will write the generators in superspace coordinates.

•

Check the consistency with similar constructions for the lower-dimensional cases to verify the isomorphism with the super BMS algebras.

We begin our analysis with the simplest case of
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetry. For
\mathcal{N}=1
, the number of supercharges in the parent relativistic theory is 8 (4 supercharges
\{Q,\bar{Q}\}
, and 4 superconformal charges
\{S,\bar{S}\}
):

(Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},S_{\alpha},\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}),~\text{where}\>\alpha,\dot{\alpha}=1,2.

We also have the
R
-symmetry group, which is
U(1)
for
\mathcal{N}=1
. There can be numerous ways to perform the UR contraction on the relativistic superconformal algebra, resulting in different possible algebras. As explained above, taking a cue from the two-dimensional algebras, we focus on the homogeneous algebras, where the fermionic generators close to form super-translations.

Possible scalings

We start by considering two ways to contract the fermionic generators of
\mathcal{N}=1
relativistic superconformal algebra in
d=4
, namely the symmetric and asymmetric scaling. Both will lead to the same algebra.

Symmetric scaling

First, we describe the symmetric scaling of the 8 supercharges. We assume

\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(Q_{\alpha})^{\dagger}\qquad\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(S_{\alpha})^{\dagger}\,.

(24)

The symmetric scaling is given by

Q_{\alpha}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}Q_{\alpha}\qquad\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\qquad S_{\alpha}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}S_{\alpha}\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\qquad R\to R\,.

(25)

The above scaling for the fermionic generators along with the bosonic generators in (
6
) results in the following algebra:

Fermionic sector:

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle=2\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}H

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},Q_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=0

(26a)

\displaystyle\{S_{\alpha},\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle=2\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}K

\displaystyle\{S_{\alpha},S_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=0

(26b)

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},S_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=2(\sigma^{0i})_{\alpha}^{~\gamma}\epsilon_{\gamma\beta}B_{i}

\displaystyle\{Q^{A}_{\alpha},\bar{S}^{B}_{\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=0

(26c)

\displaystyle\{\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=-2\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}(\bar{\sigma}^{0i})^{\dot{\gamma}}_{~\dot{\beta}}B_{i}

\displaystyle\{\bar{Q}^{A}_{\dot{\alpha}},S^{B}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=0

(26d)

Mixed sector:

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},P_{i}]

\displaystyle=[Q_{\alpha},H]=0

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},P_{i}]

\displaystyle=i(\sigma^{i})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\bar{Q}^{\dot{\alpha}}

(27a)

\displaystyle[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},P_{i}]

\displaystyle=[\bar{Q}_{\alpha},H]=0

\displaystyle[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},P_{i}]

\displaystyle=i\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}(\bar{\sigma}^{i})^{\dot{\beta}\alpha}Q_{\alpha}

(27b)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},B_{i}]

\displaystyle=[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},B_{i}]=0

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},H]

\displaystyle=[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},H]=0

(27c)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},K]

\displaystyle=[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},K]=0

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},B_{i}]

\displaystyle=[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},B_{i}]=0

(27d)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},J_{ij}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}(\sigma_{ij})^{\>\;\>\beta}_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},K]

\displaystyle=[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},K]=0

(27e)

\displaystyle[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},J_{ij}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}(\bar{\sigma}_{ij})^{\dot{\beta}}_{\>\;\>\dot{\gamma}}\bar{Q}^{\dot{\gamma}}

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},K_{i}]

\displaystyle=[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},K_{i}]=0

(27f)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},D]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},J_{ij}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}(\sigma_{ij})^{\>\;\>\beta}_{\alpha}S_{\beta}

(27g)

\displaystyle[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},D]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},J_{ij}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{2}\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}(\bar{\sigma}_{ij})^{\dot{\beta}}_{\>\;\>\dot{\gamma}}\bar{S}^{\dot{\gamma}}

(27h)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},K_{i}]

\displaystyle=i(\sigma^{i})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\bar{S}^{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},D]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}S_{\alpha}

(27i)

\displaystyle[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},K_{i}]

\displaystyle=i\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}(\bar{\sigma}^{i})^{\dot{\beta}\alpha}S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},D]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

(27j)

R
-symmetry sector:

[R,Q_{\alpha}]=-i\frac{3}{4}Q_{\alpha}\qquad[R,\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}]=i\frac{3}{4}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\qquad[R,S_{\alpha}]=i\frac{3}{4}S_{\alpha}\qquad[R,\bar{S}_{\alpha}]=-i\frac{3}{4}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(28)

The above algebra (
26
)–(
28
) is the finite part of
\mathcal{N}=1
CSA in
d=4
. The generators
\{B_{i},K,H,Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},S_{\alpha},\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}
are analogous to the translational part of finite CSA. In other words, the anti commutators (
26
) between the fermionic generators give rise to the bosonic generators
(H,B_{i},K)
, which constitute the finite super-translations of the bosonic CCA.

With the
R
-symmetry scaling as in (
25
),
R
does not appear on the RHS of the fermionic sector of the algebra and also is not a central term. However, if we choose the scaling such that
R\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}R
, then
R
becomes a central term: it appears on the RHS of the fermionic sector of the algebra. The corresponding brackets are

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle=2\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}H

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},Q_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=0

(29a)

\displaystyle\{S_{\alpha},\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\}

\displaystyle=2\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}K

\displaystyle\{S_{\alpha},S_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\}=0

(29b)

\displaystyle\{Q_{\alpha},S_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=2(\sigma^{0i})_{\alpha}^{~\gamma}\epsilon_{\gamma\beta}B_{i}-4i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}R

\displaystyle\{Q^{A}_{\alpha},\bar{S}^{B}_{\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=0

(29c)

\displaystyle\{\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},\bar{S}_{\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=-2\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\gamma}}(\bar{\sigma}^{0i})^{\dot{\gamma}}_{~\dot{\beta}}B_{i}+4i\epsilon_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}R

\displaystyle\{\bar{Q}^{A}_{\dot{\alpha}},S^{B}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=0

(29d)

\displaystyle[Q_{\alpha},R]

\displaystyle=[\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}},R]=0

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},R]

\displaystyle=[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},R]=0\,.

(29e)

There is no change in the mixed sector.
The scaling (
R\to\epsilon^{n}R,n>0
) will result in the absence of the
R
term on the RHS of the algebra. In this scaling, the LHS of
[Q_{\alpha},R]
scales as
\epsilon^{n-1}
and thus vanishes in the limit
\epsilon\to 0
.

Asymmetric scaling

Now, we explore another type of scaling where we scale the fermionic generators aysmmetrically. Contrary to expectations, we end up with the same algebra as before. The important point here is that the hermiticity conditions change as compared to (
24
). The asymmetric scaling is given by either of the following two possibilities.

either:

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\to R

(30a)

or:

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\to R\,.

(30b)

The above scaling leaves the algebra the same as (
26
) – (
28
).
However, for this case, the hermiticity conditions

\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(S_{\alpha})^{\dagger}\qquad\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(Q_{\alpha})^{\dagger}

(31)

exchange positive and negative scaling weights, analogous to usual Virasoro generators
L_{n}^{\dagger}=L_{-n}
. We elucidate the connection between the symmetric and asymmetric scaling more prominently when addressing the superspace formalism below.

3.3
Carrollian superspace

We have explored the various types of possible scaling for the fermionic generators for
\mathcal{N}=1
finite CSA by contracting their parent relativistic generators. We found that the algebra remains the same for both symmetric and asymmetric scalings. We now build the Carroll equivalent of the superspace formalism and connect the different scalings. In the superspace coordinates
\theta,\bar{\theta}
the relativistic supercharges are given as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}-\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\mu}

(32a)

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\Theta^{\beta}\sigma^{\mu}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\mu}

(32b)

where,
\partial_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta^{\alpha}},\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\Theta^{\dot{\alpha}}}
.
Let us now take the UR scaling on the superspace coordinates,

\Theta^{\alpha}\to\epsilon^{a}\Theta^{\alpha}\qquad\qquad\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\to\epsilon^{b}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(33)

Here,
a,b
are some arbitrary parameters which will be determined later. Now, the LHS of (
32a
) gives

\epsilon^{-a}\partial_{\alpha}-\epsilon^{b-1}\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}-\epsilon^{b}\sigma^{i}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\partial_{i}.

(34)

Requiring
\{Q,\bar{Q}\}\sim H
obtains

b=1-a\,.

(35)

From the above relation, we can consider three simple choices
out of the infinitely many possible choices for
a
. There are two natural choices (assuming
a=b
, so that both sectors scale in the same
way or assuming
a=0
, so
that only one sector scales) and a third one related to one of them by
exchanging unbarred and barred sectors (assuming
a=1
which is the same after exchange in
a=0
), as
evident from (
33
).

Case 1: Symmetric scaling

Here we have
a=\frac{1}{2},\>\Theta^{\alpha}\to\sqrt{\epsilon}\Theta^{\alpha},\>\>\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\to\sqrt{\epsilon}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}
.
We first choose
a=\frac{1}{2}
, yielding

Q_{\alpha}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}Q_{\alpha}\qquad\qquad\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(36)

This reproduces the symmetric scaling (see section
3.2
). Next we write the bosonic and other fermionic generators in terms of the superspace coordinates.

Fermionic generators:

The
Q
supercharges in the contracted superspace are given by

Q_{\alpha}=\partial_{\alpha}-\sigma^{0}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}\qquad\qquad\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}=-\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\Theta^{\beta}\sigma^{0}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{t}\,.

(37)

The relativistic
S
supercharges are given by

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{\mu})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x^{\mu}_{-})\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\beta}}+i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{\mu})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x^{\mu}_{+})\Theta^{\beta}\sigma^{\nu}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\nu}-2i(\Theta\Theta)\partial_{\alpha}

(38a)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{\mu})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}(x^{\mu}_{+})\partial_{\beta}+i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{\mu})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}(x^{\mu}_{-})\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\sigma^{\nu}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\nu}+2i(\bar{\Theta}\bar{\Theta})\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(38b)

Taking the UR limit on the first equation,

S_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}[-i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x_{i})\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\beta}}+i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x_{i})\Theta^{\beta}\sigma^{0}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}]+\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{\epsilon})

(39)

is consistent with our scaling in (
3.2
), which gives

S_{\alpha}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}S_{\alpha}\qquad\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}\to\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

(40)

The same argument holds for the second equation as well. Hence, we can write the contracted
S
supercharges as

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x_{i})\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\beta}}+i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}(x_{i})\Theta^{\beta}\sigma^{0}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}

(41a)

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{i})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}(x_{i})\partial_{\beta}+i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{i})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}(x_{i})\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\sigma^{0}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}\,.

(41b)

Bosonic generators:

The space and time translation generators do not have any fermionic pieces.
Hence, the contracted generators in the superspace are

P_{i}=\partial_{i}\qquad\qquad H=\partial_{t}\,.

(42)

Taking the UR limit for the relativistic dilatation generator,

D=-(x_{k}\partial_{k}+t\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}+\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}))\xrightarrow{\text{UR limit}}-(x_{k}\partial_{k}+t\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}+\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}))

(43)

yields the superspace Carrollian dilation generator,

D=-(x_{k}\partial_{k}+t\partial_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}+\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}}))\,.

(44)

The relativistic SCT generators in superspace are

K_{\mu}=\left(x^{\nu}x_{\nu}+2(\Theta\Theta)(\bar{\Theta}\bar{\Theta})\right)\partial_{\mu}-2\left(x_{\mu}x^{\nu}+(\Theta\sigma_{\mu}\bar{\Theta})(\Theta\sigma^{\nu}\bar{\Theta})\right)\partial_{\nu}-\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma^{\nu}\bar{\sigma}_{\mu})_{\gamma}^{\;\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha}x_{\nu}\partial_{\beta}\,.

(45)

First, consider the temporal part of SCT which we call
K
. Taking the UR limit, the contracted
K
in superspace is given by

K=x_{i}x_{i}\partial_{t}\,.

(46)

The spatial part of SCT
K_{i}
contracts to

K_{i}=-2x_{i}(t\partial_{t}+x_{k}\partial_{k})+x_{k}x_{k}\partial_{i}-\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma^{j}\bar{\sigma}_{i})_{\gamma}^{\;\alpha}\Theta_{\alpha}x_{j}\partial_{\beta}\,.

(47)

The relativistic Lorentz generators are

J_{\mu\nu}=-x_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}+x_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}-\frac{i}{2}\left(\sigma^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}\Theta_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}-\bar{\sigma}^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}_{\mu\nu}\bar{\Theta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\beta}}\right)\,.

(48)

In the UR limit, these give rise to Carrollian boosts and rotations in superspace

B_{i}=-x_{i}\partial_{t}\qquad\qquad J_{ij}=-x_{i}\partial_{j}+x_{j}\partial_{i}-\frac{i}{2}\left(\sigma^{\alpha\beta}_{ij}\Theta_{\alpha}\partial_{\beta}-\bar{\sigma}^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}_{ij}\bar{\Theta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\beta}}\right)\,.

(49)

It is straightforward to check that the above fermionic and bosonic generators in the superspace give back the finite
\mathcal{N}=1
CSA.

\boldsymbol{R}
symmetry generators:

Finally, we consider the superspace formulation of the
R
-symmetry generator. For
\mathcal{N}=1
, the symmetry group is
U(1)
. The relativistic
R
-symmetry generator is given by

R=-\frac{1}{2}(\Theta^{\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}-\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\partial}_{\dot{\alpha}})\,.

(50)

Irrespective of any scaling of the
\Theta
, the contracted generator remains the same, suggesting

R\to R
. Following this scaling, the
R
terms drop from the RHS of the
\{Q,S\}
and
\{\bar{Q},\bar{S}\}
brackets in the algebra of section
3.2
. Also, the brackets with the supercharges become non-vanishing.

[R,Q_{\alpha}]=-\frac{3i}{4}Q_{\alpha}\qquad\;[R,\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}]=\frac{3i}{4}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\qquad\;[R,S_{\alpha}]=\frac{3i}{4}S_{\alpha}\qquad\;[R,\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}]=-\frac{3i}{4}\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

(51)

Case 2. Asymmetric scaling

Here we have
a=0,\;\Theta_{\alpha}\to\Theta_{\alpha},\;\bar{\Theta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\to\epsilon\bar{\Theta}_{\dot{\alpha}}
. Consider the value of
a=0
in (
35
) and repeat the analysis similar to Case 1 (symmetric scaling). This value of

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\to\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle\to R

(52a)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}H

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}B_{i}

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle\to\frac{1}{\epsilon}K

\displaystyle P_{i}

\displaystyle\to P_{i}

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\to D

\displaystyle K_{i}

\displaystyle\to K_{i}

\displaystyle J_{ij}

\displaystyle\to J_{ij}\,.

(52b)

It is the asymmetric scaling as discussed in Sec.

. The superspace formulation of finite-CSA generators also justifies the scaling of the bosonic part in (

). The algebra remains the same as (

). Also, for this case,

\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(S_{\alpha})^{\dagger}\qquad\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=(Q_{\alpha})^{\dagger}\,.

(53)

There is again an identical asymmetric sector with the barred and unbarred generators interchanged.

3.4
Infinite extension of algebra

In this section, we construct an infinite extension of the finite CSA (
26
)–(
28
). We focus on
d=4
, but similar constructions work for
d>4
. We begin with the fermionic generators in the superspace coordinates.

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}-\sigma^{t}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Theta}^{\dot{\beta}}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{i})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}x_{i}Q_{\beta}

(54a)

\displaystyle\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=-{\bar{\partial}}_{\dot{\alpha}}+\sigma^{t}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}}\Theta^{\beta}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=i\epsilon^{{\dot{\beta}}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}x_{i}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}

(54b)

Let us now define the matrix
\Lambda
, which can be used to flip from
Q
to
\bar{S}
.

\Lambda^{\beta}_{\;\;\;\dot{\alpha}}=-i\epsilon^{\beta\gamma}(\sigma_{i})_{\gamma\dot{\alpha}}x_{i}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\Lambda^{\beta}_{\;\>\dot{\alpha}}Q_{\beta}

(55)

Similarly, we define
\bar{\Lambda}
which can be used to go from
\bar{Q}
to
S
.

\bar{\Lambda}^{\;\;\;\dot{\beta}}_{\alpha}=i\epsilon^{\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma}}(\sigma_{i})_{\alpha\dot{\gamma}}x_{i}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\bar{S}_{\alpha}=\bar{\Lambda}^{\;\;\;\dot{\beta}}_{\alpha}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\beta}}

(56)

We deduce the identities

\Lambda^{\alpha}_{\;\>\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Lambda}^{\;\;\dot{\gamma}}_{\alpha}=(x_{i}x_{i})\delta^{\dot{\gamma}}_{\dot{\beta}}\qquad\qquad\Lambda^{\alpha}_{\;\>\dot{\beta}}\bar{\Lambda}^{\;\>\dot{\beta}}_{\gamma}=(x_{i}x_{i})\delta^{\alpha}_{\gamma},\Lambda^{\beta}_{\;\;\;\dot{\alpha}}=[\bar{\Lambda}^{\;\;\;\dot{\beta}}_{\alpha}]^{\dagger}\,.

(57)

Conventions and further identities related to
\Lambda
and
\bar{\Lambda}
are collected in Appendix
C
. Let us now define the following fermionic generators:

\displaystyle G^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}

\displaystyle=x^{r_{1}}y^{r_{2}}z^{r_{3}}Q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle G^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}

\displaystyle=x^{r_{1}}y^{r_{2}}z^{r_{3}}\Lambda_{\;\;\dot{\alpha}}^{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}

(58a)

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}

\displaystyle=x^{r_{1}}y^{r_{2}}z^{r_{3}}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}

\displaystyle=x^{r_{1}}y^{r_{2}}z^{r_{3}}{\bar{\Lambda}}_{\alpha}^{\;\;\dot{\alpha}}\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(58b)

Here,
r_{i}
’s can take any integer value.
Then,

[G^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},P_{x}]=-r_{1}G^{+}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2},r_{3}}\qquad\quad[G^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},P_{x}]=-r_{1}G^{-}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2},r_{3}}-\partial_{x}\Lambda G^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}\\

(59)

and similar expressions for the tilde sector with
\Lambda
replaced by
\bar{\Lambda}
.
Similarly, the fermionic generators give the following brackets with SCT generator
K_{i}
:

\displaystyle[G^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},K_{x}]

\displaystyle=(r_{1}+2r_{2}+2r_{3})G^{+}_{r_{1}+1,r_{2},r_{3}}-r_{1}\big(G^{+}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2}+2,r_{3}}+G^{+}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2},r_{3}+2}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-(\Lambda\cdot\partial_{x}\bar{\Lambda})G^{+}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}}

(60a)

\displaystyle[G^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},K_{x}]

\displaystyle=(r_{1}+2r_{2}+2r_{3})G^{-}_{r_{1}+1,r_{2},r_{3}}-r_{1}\big(G^{-}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2}+2,r_{3}}+G^{-}_{r_{1}-1,r_{2},r_{3}+2}\big)

(60b)

and similar expressions for the tilde sector, now with
\Lambda\cdot\partial_{x}\bar{\Lambda}
replaced by
\partial_{x}\Lambda\cdot\bar{\Lambda}
.
The brackets for the other components of
P_{i}
and
K_{i}
follows similarly. The brackets with the dilatation generator

5
Representation theory of

In this section, we take the first steps towards building a representation theory for the infinite-dimensional algebras we have constructed in our previous sections. We first review relevant aspects of relativistic superconformal algebras and then focus on analogous constructions for the

5.1
Relativistic
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{N}=1}
chiral super multiplet

We begin by revisiting some relevant aspects of the representation theory of the relativistic supersymmetric algebra. We introduce first conventions here and summarise important characteristics of the Wess–Zumino chiral super multiplet structure. These discussions will help prepare the ground for the construction of the representation theory of CSA later in the section.

For a generic relativistic field
\Phi(x_{i},t)
at an arbitrary spacetime point the infinitesimal supersymmetric transformation is given by

\delta_{\alpha}\Phi(x_{i},t)=-i[(\alpha Q+\bar{Q}\bar{\alpha}),\Phi(x_{i},t)]\,.

(85)

Here,
\alpha_{\beta},\bar{\alpha}_{\dot{\beta}}
are relativistic anti-commuting parameters. The finite supersymmetric transformation on the generic field is written as

\Phi(x_{i},t)\to\Phi^{\prime}(x_{i},t)=U^{\dagger}\Phi(x_{i},t)U\qquad\qquad U=\exp\{-i(\alpha Q+\bar{Q}\bar{\alpha})\}

(86)

We quote here the
\mathcal{N}=1
relativistic algebra between the fermionic generators
Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}
.

{[\alpha^{\beta}_{1}Q_{\beta},\bar{Q}_{\beta}\bar{\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}_{2}]=2\alpha^{\beta}_{1}(\sigma^{\mu})_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\bar{\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}_{2}P_{\mu}}\qquad\qquad[P_{\mu},\Phi(x_{i},t)]=-i\partial_{\mu}\Phi(x_{i},t)

(87)

Thus, the two successive infinitesimal SUSY transformations act on a generic field as

[\delta_{\alpha_{1}},\delta_{\alpha_{2}}]\Phi(x_{i},t)=-2i(\alpha_{1}\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\alpha}_{2}-\alpha_{2}\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\alpha}_{1})\partial_{\mu}\Phi(x_{i},t)\,.

(88)

Wess–Zumino multiplet:

Now, we take a complex relativistic scalar field
\phi(x_{i},t)
along with its complex conjugate
\phi^{\star}(x_{i},t)
to review the relativistic representation theory of
\mathcal{N}=1
supersymmetric algebra (
87
) on a complex scalar field. The infinitesimal supersymmetric transformations
Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}
acting on the complex scalar field generate chiral fermions
\psi_{\alpha}
(left chiral) and
\chi_{\dot{\alpha}}
(right chiral). In this process, the mass dimension change by
\pm\frac{1}{2}
resulting in chiral fermion or the derivative of the scalar field. Next, the chiral fermions of mass dimension
\frac{3}{2}
transform into complex auxiliary scalar field
F,F^{\star}
by the action of
Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}
on them. The mass dimension of the auxiliary field is 2. Thus, we get two sectors in the multiplet structure. The left chiral sector is generated by the following transformations

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\phi(x)=\sqrt{2}\alpha\psi(x),

(89a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\psi(x)=\sqrt{2}i(\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\alpha}\partial_{\mu}\phi(x))+\sqrt{2}\alpha\tilde{F}(x),

(89b)

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}F(x)=-\sqrt{2}i(\partial_{\mu}\psi(x)\sigma^{\mu}\bar{\alpha}).

(89c)

The right chiral sector is generated as following

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\phi^{\star}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}\bar{\alpha}\chi(x),

(90a)

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}\chi(x)

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}i({\alpha}\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\phi^{\star}(x))+\sqrt{2}\bar{\alpha}\tilde{F}^{\star}(x),

(90b)

\displaystyle\delta_{\alpha}F^{\star}(x)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}i(\alpha\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\chi(x)).

(90c)

This multiplet is also known as the Wess–Zumino multiplet.

5.2
Carrollian fermions in Weyl representation

In this section, we discuss the Carrollian fermions in Weyl representation. The Carrollian Weyl spinors decouple and the equations of motion do not carry any interaction term. To see this, we start with the relativistic Weyl fermion

\Psi_{W}=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{L}\\
\chi_{R}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(91)

Here,
\psi_{L}
and
\chi_{R}
are left and right chiral spinors. The equations of motion are

i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Psi_{W}=0\qquad\implies\qquad i(\sigma^{0}\partial_{t}\psi_{L}-\sigma^{i}\partial_{i}\psi_{L})=0\qquad\qquad i(\sigma^{0}\partial_{t}\chi_{R}+\sigma^{i}\partial_{i}\chi_{R})=0\,.

(92)

The gamma matrices are in Weyl representation

\gamma^{0}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\sigma^{0}\\
\bar{\sigma}^{0}&0\end{bmatrix}\qquad\qquad\gamma^{i}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\sigma^{i}\\
\bar{\sigma}^{i}&0\end{bmatrix}\,.

(93)

The action of boosts on the chiral fermions at the origin is given by

[B_{i},\Psi_{W}(0,0)]=\Sigma_{i0}\Psi_{W}(0,0)\qquad\qquad\Sigma_{i0}=-\frac{1}{4}[\gamma^{i},\gamma^{0}]=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\sigma^{i}}{2}&0\\
0&\frac{\sigma^{i}}{2}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(94)

Similarly, under the rotation generators the chiral spinors transform as

5.3
Representation theory of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{N}=1}
CSA in
\boldsymbol{d=4}

To discuss the representation theory of
\mathcal{N}=1
CSA in

, first, we consider the action of the finite set of generators on a complex scalar field and then extend our formalism to the infinite set of CSA generators.

We construct the highest-weight representation for
\mathcal{N}=1
CSA on fields with different spins. Similar to its non-supersymmetric cousin (reviewed in Sec.
2
), we label the states with their scaling dimensions
\Delta
and spin
j
. Due to
R
-symmetry transformations we have an additional label
r
on the states. Using the state-operator correspondence, let us write

[D,\Phi(0,0)]=\Delta\Phi(0,0)\qquad\;[J^{2},\Phi(0,0)]=j(j+1)\Phi(0,0)\qquad\;[R,\Phi(0,0)]=r\Phi(0,0)\,.

Here,
\Delta
is the eigenvalue of the dilatation operator
D

is the eigenvalue of the quadratic Casimir associated with
SO(3)
rotations, and

is the corresponding spin eigenvalue. The
R
-symmetry generator commutes with all other bosonic generators (the
R
-symmetry group for
\mathcal{N}=1
CSA is
U(1)
). Hence, the states also carry a label
r

We expect the spectrum, in particular the scaling dimension
\Delta

[K_{i},\Phi(0,0)]=0\qquad\;[K,\Phi(0,0)]=0\qquad\;[S_{\alpha},\Phi(0,0)]=0\qquad\;[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},\Phi(0,0)]=0\,.

(102)

In the finite CSA we have
\{S,\bar{S}\}\sim K
. Hence, the constraint regarding the temporal part of special conformal transformation
K

([K,\Phi(0,0)]=0)
becomes implicit. Incorporating the infinite generators, the CSA primaries are defined as

\displaystyle[K_{i},\Phi(0,0)]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[S_{\alpha},\Phi(0,0)]

\displaystyle=0,\;[\bar{S}_{\dot{\alpha}},\Phi(0,0)]=0

(103a)

\displaystyle[G^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},\Phi(0,0)]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\tilde{G}^{-}_{r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}},\Phi(0,0)]

\displaystyle=0~~\text{for any $r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\geq 0$}

(103b)

\displaystyle[M_{f},\Phi(0,0)]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{for the degree of polynomial $>0$}.

(103c)

The scaling dimensions of the fermionic operators
Q_{\alpha},\bar{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}
are
+\frac{1}{2}
, whereas that of the fermionic superconformal operators

-\frac{1}{2}

and

6
Conclusions

We have explored the algebraic structures associated with Carrollian superconformal symmetry — or equivalently super BMS symmetry — in the context of field theories in dimensions greater than three. We specifically concentrated on boundary dimension
d=4
, but the method outlined is simply generalisable for all higher dimensions. We identified the contraction of the corresponding relativistic algebra that is useful for holography of asymptotically flat spacetimes and then lifted the finite contracted algebra to an infinite-dimensional one. Our constructions generalised to higher
\mathcal{N}
. We checked our proposed infinite lift by reproducing results in the literature for
d=2,3
. Finally, we returned to
\mathcal{N}=1
Carrollian superconformal field theories and worked out details of its representation theory. In particular, we discussed the Carrollian version of the Wess–Zumino multiplet, laying the groundwork for similar constructions for other multiplets to be addressed in the future.

6.1
Discussion and future directions

The algebraic construction in this paper is relevant for understanding Carrollian superconformal field theories. Our final aim is to build a Carrollian version of
\mathcal{N}=4

SU(N)
super Yang–Mills theory so that we have a concrete proposal for the boundary theory in the flat limit of Maldacena’s original AdS/CFT correspondence. Building on the representation theory of the
\mathcal{N}=1
Carrollian superconformal field theories in the present paper, we aim to construct explicit examples of Carrollian superconformal field theories, starting with
\mathcal{N}=1
Carrollian SUSY electrodynamics, before moving to Yang–Mills and higher
\mathcal{N}
. It could be rewarding to find all possible central extensions of our algebras (
66
)-(
68
) and (
82
). Our preliminary analysis for finding central terms by educated guessing was unsuccessful, but we did not attempt a complete analysis of finding non-trivial co-cycles.

The fact that the underlying symmetry structures are infinite-dimensional may indicate some hidden integrability in the Carrollian sector of these SUSY gauge theories.
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM provides a natural playground to investigate integrability
Beisert:2010jr
. A first step would be to understand what exists in the planar limit of the Carrollian
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, which possibly can be obtained directly from the corresponding UR limit. But there could be more structure in the Carrollian sector and perhaps integrability beyond the planar limit. If this were true, we would have stumbled on a new integrable sector of relativistic
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM, viz., the Carrollian sector.

When considering the flat version of the AdS
5
/CFT
4
correspondence, it is necessary to understand the ’tHooft large-
N
limit in the Carrollian context. Here the speed of light goes to zero along with the usual ’tHooft scaling of

N\to\infty
and fixed
\lambda\equiv g_{\text{YM}}^{2}N
.
There is a myriad of possibilities on how to take this modified large
N
limit, generalising the original analysis in relativistic gauge theories. Now that we understand the algebraic structures that govern the symmetries of the Carrollian superconformal theory we are after, the possible large
N
limits have to be consistent with the particular scalings discussed in our paper. It could be useful to first understand this in the
\mathcal{N}=1
context before generalising to the case of interest, viz.,
\mathcal{N}=4
.

After the large
N
limit is established, the next step would be to construct the bulk-boundary dictionary between flat space supergravity (and, ultimately, superstrings) and these Carrollian superconformal theories. For the AdS
5
/CFT
4
correspondence, the curvature radii of the AdS
5
and the accompanying S

are equal and it would seem that a flat limit on the AdS

R

\mathcal{N}=4
in Sec.

. Eventually, we intend to understand the bulk-boundary dictionary both as a limit of the original correspondence and intrinsically. It is likely that the limit only yields a part of the answer, as the contraction of symmetries generates only the Poincaré subalgebra of the BMS algebra and not the entire symmetry structure. Our expectation is that the infinite-dimensional symmetries constructed in our work will make it easier to understand holography in flat space in general dimensions.

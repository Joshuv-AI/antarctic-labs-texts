CONVENIENT TAIL BOUNDS FOR SUMS OF RANDOM TENSORS
Shih Yu Chang

Abstract
This work prepares new probability bounds for sums of random, independent, Hermitian tensors. These probability bounds characterize large-deviation behavior of the extreme eigenvalue of the sums of random tensors. We extend Lapalace transform method and Lieb’s concavity theorem from matrices to tensors, and apply these tools to generalize the classical bounds associated with the names Chernoff, Bennett, and Bernstein from the scalar to the tensor setting. Tail bounds for the norm of a sum of random rectangular tensors are also derived from corollaries of random Hermitian tensors cases. The proof mechanism can also be applied to tensor-valued martingales and tensor-based Azuma, Hoeffding and McDiarmid inequalities are established.

1.2
Technical Results

Given a finite sequence of random Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, the main purpose of this work is to bound the following probability:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right),

(1)

where
\lambda_{\max}
represents the largest eigenvalue of a Hermitian tensor obtained from
eigenvalue decomposition
. The problem posted in Eq. (
1
) are associated to the following problems: (1) the smallest and the largest singular value of a sum of random tensors with square or rectangular shapes; (2) extension random variable probability bounds, e.g., Chernoff and Bernstein bounds, to tensors settings; (3) tensor martingales and other adapted random sequences of tensors.

There are two main technical tools required by this work to build those tensor probability bounds. The first is
Laplace transform method
, which provides a systematic way to give tail bounds for the sum of scalar random variables. In
[
1
]
, the authors apply Laplace transform method to bound Eq. (
1
) with the matrix setting, i.e., the tail probability for the maximum eigenvalue of the sum of Hermitian matrices is controlled by a matrix version of the moment-generating function. They proved following:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i}\mathbf{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i}t\mathbf{X}_{i}\right)\Big\}.

(2)

However, the bound to
\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i}t\mathbf{X}_{i}\right)\Big\}
is far from optimal according to
[
35
]
(see Sections 3.7 and 4.8 in
[
35
]
). In this work, we extend the Laplace transform method to tensors. The other important technique utilized in this work is to extend Lieb’s concavity theorem
[
25
]
to tensors. Tensor Lieb’s theorem is introduced in Section
3.2
, and we illustrate how to combine this result with the tensor Laplace transform method as our main technique to prove a series of random tensors bounds. We utilize this mechanism as our main approach to derive a large family of probability inequalities that are essentially tight in a wide variety of scenarios. Most random matrix inequalities studied in
[
35
]
have same origin as those random tensor inequalities discussed at this work, however, we enhance main tools, i.e., Laplace transform method and Lieb’s concavity theorem, from matrices to tensors, and obtain new random tensor inequalities with tensor orders and dimensions as factors.

This work will derive various inequalities based on Eq. (
1
). We will provide basic notations first before listing main inequalities investigated at this paper. The symbols
\lambda_{\min}
and
\lambda_{\max}
are used to represent the minimum and maximum eigenvalues of a Hermitian tensor. The notation
\succeq
is used to indicate the semidefinite ordering of tensors.
\left\|\mathcal{A}\right\|
is defined as the largest singular value of the tensor
\mathcal{A}
.
The first category of concentration is associated with a sum of real numbers multiplied by independent random variables. If the independent random variables are drawn as normal (Gaussian) distribution or Rademacher distribution, the sum of real numbers multiplied by such random variables has sub-Gaussian tail behavior. In Section
4
, we will provide the proof and its application about the following theorem.

Theorem 1

(Tensor Gaussian and Normal Series)
.

Given a finite sequence
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of fixed Hermitian tensors with dimensions as
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, and let
\{\alpha_{i}\}
be a finite sequence of independent normal variables. We define

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right\|,

(3)

then, for all
\theta\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},

(4)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|\geq\theta\right)\leq 2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

(5)

This theorem is also valid for a finite sequence of independent Rademacher random variables
\{\alpha_{i}\}
.

Chernoff bound provides an estimate on the probability of the concentration results related to the number of successes in a sequence of independent random trials. In the tensor situation, the similar theorem concerns a sum of positive-semidefinite random
tensors subject to a uniform eigenvalue bound. The tensor Chernoff bound indicates that the largest (or smallest) eigenvalues of the tensor series have similar binomial random variable behavior.

Theorem 2

(Tensor Chernoff Bound)
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
of independent, random, Hermitian tensors that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{~~ almost surely.}

(6)

Define following two quantities:

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\mbox{~~and~~}\mu_{\min}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right),

(7)

then, let
\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}=\prod\limits_{m=1}^{M}I_{m}
, we have following two inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq(1+\theta)\mu_{\max}\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\frac{e^{\theta}}{(1+\theta)^{1+\theta}}\right)^{\mu_{\max}/T},\mbox{~~ for $\theta\geq 0$;}

(8)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq(1-\theta)\mu_{\min}\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\frac{e^{-\theta}}{(1-\theta)^{1-\theta}}\right)^{\mu_{\min}/T},\mbox{~~ for $\theta\in[0,1]$.}

(9)

In Section
5
, we will prove Chernoff inequality and discuss its applications.

Bernstein inequality is another inequality to bound the sum of independent, bounded random tensors by restricting the range of the maximum eigenvalue of each random tensors. Bernstein inequality can provide tighter bound compared to Hoeffding inequality.
In Section
6
, following tensor Bernstein bound theorem and its applications are provided.

Theorem 3

(Bounded
\lambda_{\max}
Tensor Bernstein Bounds)
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{~~almost surely.}

(10)

Define the total variance
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\right)\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\right);

(11)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T$};

(12)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta}{8T}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T$}.

(13)

. In probability theory, the Azuma inequality gives a concentration result for the values of martingales that have bounded differences. Tensor Azuma inequality is given below.

Theorem 4

(Tensor Azuma Inequality)

Given a finite adapted sequence of Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}

where
i=1,2,3,\cdots
.

Define the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}.

(15)

17

(Tensor Hoeffding Inequality)
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
and a fixed sequence of Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\mbox{almost surely},

(16)

where
i=1,2,3,\cdots
.

Define the total variance
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}.

(17)

McDiarmid inequality is crucial in determining the stability of machine learning algorithms by applying a simple concept: small change in training set will make small change in hypothesis. In Section

\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
as an input argument as

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{U}\star_{M}g(\Lambda)\star_{M}\mathcal{U}^{H},~~\mbox{where $\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{U}\star_{M}\Lambda\star_{M}\mathcal{U}^{H}$.}

(29)

The
spectral mapping theorem
asserts that each eigenvalue of
g(\mathcal{X})
is equal to
g(\lambda)
for some eigenvalue
\lambda
of
\mathcal{X}
. From the semidefinite ordering of tensors, we also have

\displaystyle f(x)\geq g(x),~~\mbox{for $x\in[a,b]$}~~~\Rightarrow~~~f(\mathcal{X})\succeq g(\mathcal{X}),~~\mbox{for eigenvalues of $\mathcal{X}\in[a,b]$;}

(30)

where
[a,b]
is a real interval.

Definition 12
.

Given a square tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, the
tensor exponential
of the tensor
\mathcal{X}
is defined as

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{X}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{X}^{k}}{k!},

(31)

where
\mathcal{X}^{0}
is defined as the identity tensor
\mathcal{I}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
and

\mathcal{X}^{k}=\underbrace{\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\dots\star_{M}\mathcal{X}}_{\mbox{$k$ terms of $\mathcal{X}$}}
.

Given a tensor
\mathcal{Y}
, the tensor
\mathcal{X}
is said to be a
tensor logarithm
of
\mathcal{Y}
if
e^{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{Y}

Several facts are about tensor exponential. First, the exponential of a Hermitian tensor is always positive-definite due to the spectral mapping theorem. Second, the trace exponential function,
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathrm{Tr}\exp(\mathcal{X})
, is convex. Third, the trace exponential function follows monotone property with respect to semidefinite ordering as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\succeq\mathcal{Y}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\mathrm{Tr}\exp(\mathcal{X})\geq\mathrm{Tr}\exp(\mathcal{Y}).

(32)

However, different from exponential rules for scalers, the tensor exponential does not convert sums into products. The Golden-Thompson inequality for tensors
[
5
]
shows the following relationship

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{X}+\mathcal{Y}}\leq\mathrm{Tr}\left(e^{\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}}\right),

(33)

where
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
are Hermitian tensors.

For the tensor logarithm, we have the following monotone relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\succeq\mathcal{Y}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\log(\mathcal{X})\succeq\log(\mathcal{Y}).

(34)

Moreover, the tensor logarithm is also concave, i.e., we have

\displaystyle t\log(\mathcal{X}_{1})+(1-t)\log(\mathcal{X}_{2})\preceq\log(t\mathcal{X}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{X}_{2}),

(35)

where
\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{X}_{2}
are positive-definite tensors and
t\in[0,1]
. The concavity of tensor logarithm can be derived from Hansen-Pedersen Characterizations, see
[
7
]
.

If a given tensor
\mathcal{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{M}}
is not square, we can perform
Hermitian dialation
, represented as
\mathbb{D}
, to the tensor
\mathcal{Y}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{Y})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{O}&\mathcal{Y}\\
\mathcal{Y}^{H}&\mathcal{O}\\
\end{bmatrix}

(36)

where
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{Y})\in\mathbb{C}^{(I_{1}+J_{1})\times\cdots\times(I_{M}+J_{M})\times(I_{1}+J_{1})\times\cdots\times(I_{M}+J_{M})}
is a Hermitian tensor. Since we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{Y})^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{Y}^{H}&\mathcal{O}\\
\mathcal{O}&\mathcal{Y}^{H}\mathcal{Y}\\
\end{bmatrix},

(37)

then, we have following spectral norm relation

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}(\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{Y}))=\left\|\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{Y})\right\|=\left\|\mathcal{Y}\right\|,

(38)

where
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is the spectral norm for a tensor and this will return the maximum singular value of its argument tensor. Hermitian dilation operation enables us to extend results from Hermitian tensors to other non-square tensors.

2.2
Tensor Moments and Cumulants

Since the expectation of a random tensor can be treated as convex combination, expectation will preserve the semidefinite order as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\succ\mathcal{Y}\mbox{~~almost surely}~~~\Rightarrow~~~\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X})\succ\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{Y}).

(39)

From operator Jensen’s inequality
[
16
]
, we also have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{2})\succeq\left(\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X})\right)^{2}.

(40)

Suppose a random Hermitian tensor
\mathcal{X}
having tensor moments of all orders, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{n})
existing for all
n
, we can define the tensor moment-generating function, denoted as
\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)
, and the tensor cumulant-generating function, denoted as
\mathbb{K}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)
, for the tensor
\mathcal{X}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}},\mbox{~~and~~~}\mathbb{K}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}},

(41)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Both the tensor moment-generating function and the tensor cumulant-generating function can be expressed as power series expansions:

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)=\mathcal{I}+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{n}),\mbox{~~and~~~}\mathbb{K}_{\mathcal{X}}(t)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\psi_{n},

(42)

where
\psi_{n}
is called
tensor cumulant
. The tensor cumulant
\psi_{n}
can be expressed as a polynomial in terms of tensor moments up to the order
n
, for example, the first cumulant is the mean and the second cumulant is the variance:

\displaystyle\psi_{1}=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}),\mbox{~~and~~~}\psi_{2}=\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{2})-(\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}))^{2}.

(43)

Finally, in this work, we also assume that all random variables are sufficiently regular for us to compute their expectations, interchange limits, etc.

The main purpose of this section is to develop two important tools which will be applied
intensively in the proof of probability inequalities for the maximum eigenvalue of a sum
of independent random tensors. The first one is the Laplace transform method for tensors discussed in Section
3.1
, and the second one is the tensor trace concavity which will be presented in Section

3.1
Laplace Transform Method for Tensors

We extend the Laplace transform bound from matrices to tensors based on
[
1
]
. Following lemma is given to establish the Laplace transform bound for tensors.

Lemma 13

(Laplace Transform Method for Tensors)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a random Hermitian tensor. For
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X})\geq\theta)\leq\inf_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-\theta t}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{t\mathcal{X}}\Big\}

(44)

Proof.

Given a fix value
t
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X})\geq\theta)=\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{\max}(t\mathcal{X})\geq t\theta)=\mathbb{P}(e^{\lambda_{\max}(t\mathcal{X})}\geq e^{t\theta})\leq e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{E}e^{\lambda_{\max}(t\mathcal{X})}.

(45)

The first equality uses the homogeneity of the maximum eigenvalue map, the second equality comes from the monotonicity of the scalar exponential function, and the last relation is Markov’s inequality. Because we have

\displaystyle e^{\lambda_{\max}(t\mathcal{Y})}=\lambda_{\max}(e^{t\mathcal{Y}})\leq\mathrm{Tr}e^{t\mathcal{Y}},

(46)

where the first equality used the spectral mapping theorem, and the inequality holds because the exponential of an Hermitian tensor is positive definite and the maximum eigenvalue of a positive definite tensor is dominated by the trace
[
24
]
. From Eqs (
45
) and (
46
), this lemma is established.
∎

The Lemma
13
helps us to control the tail probabilities for the maximum eigenvalue of a random Hermitian tensor by utilizing a bound for the traces of the tensor moment-generating function introduced in Section
2.2
.

3.2
Tensor Trace Concavity

In this section, we will extend Lieb’s concavity theorem to tensors and we begin with the definition about the relative entropy between two tensors.

Definition 14
.

Given two positive definite tensors
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
and tensor
\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. The relative entropy between tensors
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is defined as

\displaystyle D(\mathcal{A}\parallel\mathcal{B})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{A}\star_{M}(\log\mathcal{A}-\log\mathcal{B}).

(47)

Given a continuous function defined over a real interval as
f:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathcal{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
as a Hermitian tensor with spectrum in
[a,b]
and spectrum decomposition as
\mathcal{T}=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{n}}\lambda_{n}\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{n}}
, where
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{n}}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
are mutually orthogonal tensors, then the mapping for the tensor
\mathcal{T}
by
f
can be defined as
f(\mathcal{T})=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{n}}f(\lambda_{n})\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{n}}
. The function
f
is called as a
tensor convex
function if
f(\lambda_{n})
is convex on the Hermitian tenor with spectrum in
[a,b]
. We apply
perspective function

[
12
]
notion for tensor convex and introduce the following lemma about the convexity of a tensor convex function.

Lemma 15
.

Given
f
as a tensor convex function, two commuting tensors
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}\in

\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{Y}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}
, and the existence of the
\mathcal{Y}^{-1}
, then the following map
h

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})=f(\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1})\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}

(48)

is jointly convex in the sense that, given
t\in[0,1]
, if
\mathcal{X}=t\mathcal{X}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{X}_{2}
and
\mathcal{Y}=t\mathcal{Y}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{Y}_{2}
with
\mathcal{X}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}_{1}=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{1}
and
\mathcal{X}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}_{2}=\mathcal{Y}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{2}
, we should have

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})\leq th(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{1})+(1-t)h(\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{Y}_{2}).

(49)

Proof.

Constructing tensors
\mathcal{A}=(t\mathcal{Y}_{1})^{1/2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1/2}

and
\mathcal{B}=((1-t)\mathcal{Y}_{2})^{1/2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1/2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{H}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}^{H}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{I}

(50)

Since we have

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1})\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}

(51)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}\star_{M}f(\mathcal{Y}^{-1/2}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1/2})\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}\star_{M}f(\mathcal{A}^{H}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}^{H}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{B})\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}\star_{M}\left(\mathcal{A}^{H}\star_{M}f(\mathcal{X}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{1})\star_{M}\mathcal{A}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\mathcal{B}^{H}\star_{M}f(\mathcal{X}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{2})\star_{M}\mathcal{B}\right)\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t\mathcal{Y}_{1})^{1/2}f(\mathcal{X}_{1}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{1})(t\mathcal{Y}_{1})^{1/2}+((1-t)\mathcal{Y}_{2})^{1/2}f(\mathcal{X}_{2}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{-1}_{2})((1-t)\mathcal{Y}_{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle th(\mathcal{X}_{1},\mathcal{Y}_{1})+(1-t)h(\mathcal{X}_{2},\mathcal{Y}_{2})

where
\leq_{1}
is based on Hansen-Pedersen-Jensen inequality and the condition provided by Eq. (
50
), see Theorem 2.1 in
[
12
]
.
∎

Following lemma is given to establish the joint convexity property of relative entropy for tensors.

Lemma 16

(Joint Convexity of Relative Entropy for Tensors)
.

The relative entropy function of two positive-definite tensors is a jointly convex function. That is

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}(t\mathcal{A}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{A}_{2}\parallel t\mathcal{B}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{B}_{2})\leq t\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}_{1}\parallel\mathcal{B}_{1})+(1-t)\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}_{2}\parallel\mathcal{B}_{2}),

(52)

where
t\in[0,1]
and all the following four tensors
\mathcal{A}_{1}
,
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
\mathcal{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{B}_{2}
, are positive definite.

Proof.

From the definition
14
, we wish to show the joint convexity of the function
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}\parallel\mathcal{B})
with respect to the tensors
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. Let us define tensor operators
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{A}\star_{M}\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{X}\star_{M}\mathcal{B}
for the variable tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. Then, we have
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X})
and
\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})
commuting on the inner product operation
\langle\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X}),\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X})\rangle
defined by Eq. (
27
), i.e.,
\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{F}^{H}(\mathcal{X})\star_{M}\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{X}))=\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{G}^{H}(\mathcal{X})\star_{M}\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{X}))
. Since the function
f(x)=x\log x
is tensor convex, we apply Lemma
15
to operators
\mathcal{F}(~~),\mathcal{G}(~~)
and the function
h
definition provided by Eq. (
48
) to obtain the following relation:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I},h(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I}),\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I},~~\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I})\star_{M}(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I})\star_{M}\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{I}))\log(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I})\star_{M}\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\mathcal{I}))\rangle

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I},\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I})(\log\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I})-\log\mathcal{G}(\mathcal{I}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{A}\log\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}\log\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}\parallel\mathcal{B}),

is jointly convex with respect to tensors
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
.
∎

Theorem 17

(Lieb’s concavity theorem for tensors)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hermitian tensor. Following map

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{H}+\log\mathcal{A}}

(54)

is concave on the positive-definite cone.

Proof.

From Klein’s inequality for the map
t\rightarrow t\log t
(which is strictly concave for
t>0
) and Hermitian tensors
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y},
we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{Y}\geq\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}-\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\log\mathcal{X}+\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\log\mathcal{Y}.

(55)

If we replace
\mathcal{Y}
by
e^{\mathcal{H}+\log\mathcal{A}}
, we then have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{H}+\log\mathcal{A}}=\max\limits_{\mathcal{X}\succ\mathcal{O}}\Big\{\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\star\mathcal{H}-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X}\parallel\mathcal{A})+\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\Big\}

(56)

where
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X}\parallel\mathcal{A})
is the quantum relative entropy between two tensor operators. For real number
t\in[0,1]
and two positive-definite tensors
\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{H}+\log(t\mathcal{A}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{A}_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{\mathcal{X}\succ\mathcal{O}}\Big\{\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\mathcal{H}-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X}\parallel t\mathcal{A}_{1}+(1-t)\mathcal{A}_{2})+\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\Big\}

(57)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle t\max_{\mathcal{X}\succ\mathcal{O}}\Big\{\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\mathcal{H}-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X}\parallel t\mathcal{A}_{1})+\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\Big\}

\displaystyle+(1-t)\max_{\mathcal{X}\succ\mathcal{O}}\Big\{\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\mathcal{H}-\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{X}\parallel(1-t)\mathcal{A}_{2})+\mathrm{Tr}\mathcal{X}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{H}+\log\mathcal{A}_{1}}+(1-t)\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{H}+\log\mathcal{A}_{2}},

where the first and last equalities are obtained based on the variational formula provided by Eq. (
56
), and the inequality is due to the joint convexity property of the
relative entropy from Leamm
16
.
∎

Based on Lieb’s concavity theorem for tensors, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 18
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a fixed Hermitian tensor, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a random Hermitian tensor, then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}}\leq\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\log\left(\mathbb{E}e^{\mathcal{X}}\right)}.

(58)

Proof.

Define the random tensor
\mathcal{Y}=e^{\mathcal{X}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}}=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\log\mathcal{Y}}\leq\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\log\left(\mathbb{E}\mathcal{Y}\right)}=\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\log\left(\mathbb{E}e^{\mathcal{X}}\right)},

(59)

where the inequality is based on Lieb’s concavity theorem for tensors
17
and Jensen’s inequality .
∎

3.3
Tail Bounds for Independent Sums

This section will present the tail bound for the sum of independent random tensors and several corollaries according to this tail bound for independent sums. We begin with the subadditivity lemma of tensor cumulant-generating functions.

Lemma 19
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian random tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\right),~~\mbox{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$.}

(60)

Proof.

We first define the following term for the tensor cumulant-generating function for
\mathcal{X}_{i}
as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\log(\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}).

(61)

Then, we define the Hermitian tensor
\mathcal{H}_{k}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{k}(t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}t\mathcal{X}_{k}+\sum\limits_{i=k+1}^{n}\mathbb{K}_{i}(t).

(62)

By applying Eq. (
62
) to Theorem
17
repeatedly for
k=1,2,\cdots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{n-1}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+t\mathcal{X}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{n-2}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+\log\left(\mathbb{E}_{n-1}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{n-2}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+t\mathcal{X}_{n-1}+\mathbb{K}_{n}(t).\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{0}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{n-3}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-2}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+\mathbb{K}_{n-1}(t)+\mathbb{K}_{n}(t)\right)

\displaystyle\cdots

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{K}_{i}(t)\right)

(63)

where the equality
=_{1}
is based on the law of total expectation by defining
\mathbb{E}_{i}
as the conditional expectation given
\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{i}
.
∎

We are ready to present the theorem for the tail bound of independent sums.

Theorem 20

(Master Tail Bound for Independent Sum of Random Tensors)
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian random tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i})\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\right)\Big\}.

(64)

Proof.

By substituting the Lemma
19
into the Laplace transform bound provided by the Lemma
13
, this theorem is established.
∎

Several useful corollaries will be provided based on Theorem
20
.

Corollary 21
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian random tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
with dimensions in
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. If there is a function
f:(0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
and a sequence of non-random Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}
with following condition:

\displaystyle f(t)\mathcal{A}_{i}\succeq\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}},~~\mbox{for $t>0$.}

(65)

Then, for all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{\exp\left[-t\theta+f(\theta)\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\right]\Big\}

(66)

Proof.

From the condition provided by Eq. (
65
) and Theorem
20
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-t\theta}\mathrm{Tr}\exp(f(\theta)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i})

(67)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(I_{1}\cdots I_{M})e^{-t\theta}\lambda_{\max}\left(\exp(f(\theta)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-t\theta}\exp\left(f(\theta)\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\right),

where the second inequality holds since we bound the trace of a positive-definite tensor by the dimension size
I_{1}\cdots I_{M}
(the multiplication of
M
positive integers) times the maximum eigenvalue; the last equality is based on the spectral mapping theorem since the function
f
is nonnegative. This theorem is proved by taking the infimum over positive
t
.
∎

Corollary 22
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian random tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
with dimensions in
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. For all
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{\exp\left[-t\theta+n\log\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}}{n}\right)\right]\Big\}

Proof.

From tensor logarithm given by Eq. (
35
), we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}=n\cdot\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\preceq n\log\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\right),

(69)

and from the trace exponential monotone property provided by Eq. (
32
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq e^{-t\theta}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(n\log\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\right)\right)~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle\leq(I_{1}\cdots I_{M})\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{\exp\left[-t\theta+n\log\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}}{n}\right)\right]\Big\},

(70)

where the last inequality holds since we bound the trace of a positive-definite tensor by the dimension size
I_{1}\cdots I_{M}
(the multiplication of
M
positive integers) times the maximum eigenvalue and apply spectral mapping theorem.
∎

4
Tensor with Random Series

A tensor Gaussian series is one of the simplest cases of a sum of independent random tensors. For scalers, a Gaussian series with real coefficients satisfies a normal-type tail bound where the variance is controlled by the sum of squares coefficients. The main purpose of the first Section
4.1
is to extend this scenario to tensors. In Section
4.2
, we will apply results from Section
4.1
to consider Gaussian tensor with nonuniform variances. Finally, we will provide the lower and upper bounds of tensor expectation in Section
4.3
.

4.1
Tensor with Gaussian and Rademacher Random Series

We begin with a lemma about moment-generating functions of Rademacher and Gaussian normal random variables.

Lemma 23
.

Suppose that the tensor
\mathcal{A}
is Hermitian. Given a Gaussian normal random variable
\alpha
and a Rademacher random variable
\beta
, then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\alpha t\mathcal{A}}=e^{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}/2}\mbox{~~and~~}e^{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}/2}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{\beta t\mathcal{A}},

(71)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

For the Gaussian normal random variable, because we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\alpha^{2n})=\frac{(2i)!}{i!2^{i}}\mbox{~~and~~}\mathbb{E}(\alpha^{2i+1})=0,

(72)

where
i=0,1,2,\cdots
; then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\alpha t\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}(\alpha^{2i})(t\mathcal{A})^{2i}}{(2i)!}

(73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{(t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}/2)^{i}}{i!}=e^{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}/2}.

For the Rademacher random variable, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{\beta t\mathcal{A}}=\cosh(t\mathcal{A})\preceq e^{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}/2}.

(74)

Therefore, this Lemma is proved.
∎

We are ready to present the main Theorem of this section about Hermitian tensors with Gaussian and Rademacher series.

Theorem 24

(Hermitian Tensor with Gaussian and Rademacher Series)
.

Given a finite sequence
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of fixed Hermitian tensors with dimensions as
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, and let
\{\alpha_{i}\}
be a finite sequence of independent normal variables. We define

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right\|,

(75)

then, for all
\theta\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},

(76)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|\geq\theta\right)\leq 2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

(77)

This theorem is also valid for a finite sequence of independent Rademacher random variables
\{\alpha_{i}\}
.

Proof.

Given a finite sequence of independent Gaussian or Rademacher random variables
\{\alpha_{i}\}
, from Lemma
23
, we have

\displaystyle e^{\frac{t^{2}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}}{2}}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{\alpha_{i}t\mathcal{A}_{i}}.

(78)

From the definition in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta+\frac{t^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2}}\Big\}=\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

(79)

This establishes Eq. (
76
). For Eq. (
77
), we have to apply following facts:
\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|
for any given Hermitian tensor
\mathcal{X}
. Because Gaussian and Rademacher random variables are symmetric, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-\alpha_{i})\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)=\mathrm{Pr}\left(-\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

(80)

Then, we obtain Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|\geq\theta\right)

∎

From the Hermitian dilation definition provided by Eq. (

Corollary 25

\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle\ \sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|\geq\theta\right)\leq\prod\limits_{m=1}^{M}(I_{m}+J_{m})e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}}.

(83)

\{\alpha_{i}\}

Let
\{\alpha_{i}\}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|

\displaystyle\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathbb{D}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\right)=\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{D}\left(\mathcal{A}_{i}\right)\right).

(84)

Due to the following singular value relation

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum\limits_{i}\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{A}_{i})^{2}\right\|=\left\|\begin{bmatrix}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{H}&\mathcal{O}\\

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{i}\right\|,\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{H}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{A}_{i}\right\|\Bigg\}.

4.2
A Gaussian Tensor with Nonuniform Variances

In this section, we will apply results obtained from the previous section to consider Gaussian tensor with nonuniform variances

Corollary 26
.

Given a tensor
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{M}}
and a random tensor
\mathcal{X}\in

\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{M}}
whose entries are independent standard Gaussian normal random variables. Let
\circ
be used to represent a Hadamard product (entrywise) between two tensors with same dimensions. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{A}\right\|\geq\theta\right)\leq\prod\limits_{m=1}^{M}(I_{m}+J_{m})e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}},

(86)

where

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}=\max\Bigg\{\max_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}\left\|a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},:}\right\|^{2},\max_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left\|a_{:,j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right\|^{2}\Bigg\},

(87)

where
a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},:}
and
a_{:,j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}
represent the row-part of the tensor
\mathcal{A}
and column-part of the tensor
\mathcal{A}
, respectively.

Proof.

Since we can decompose the tensor
\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{A}
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}x_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}},

(88)

where
\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{M}}
is the tensor with all zero entries except unity at the position
i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}
; then, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left(a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right)\left(\overline{a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}\left(\sum\limits_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left\|a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right\|^{2}\right)\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle=\mbox{diag}\left(\left\|a_{1,\cdots,1:}\right\|^{2},\left\|a_{1,\cdots,2:}\right\|^{2},\cdots,\left\|a_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{M}:}\right\|^{2}\right),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(89)

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left(\overline{a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}}\right)\left(a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\mathcal{E}_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left(\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}\left\|a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right\|^{2}\right)\mathcal{E}_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M},j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

\displaystyle=\mbox{diag}\left(\left\|a_{:1,\cdots,1}\right\|^{2},\left\|a_{:,1,\cdots,2}\right\|^{2},\cdots,\left\|a_{:,J_{1},\cdots,J_{M}}\right\|^{2}\right).~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

(90)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{\mbox{diag}\left(\left\|a_{1,\cdots,1:}\right\|^{2},\left\|a_{1,\cdots,2:}\right\|^{2},\cdots,\left\|a_{I_{1},\cdots,I_{M}:}\right\|^{2}\right),

(91)

\displaystyle\mbox{diag}\left(\left\|a_{:1,\cdots,1}\right\|^{2},\left\|a_{:,1,\cdots,2}\right\|^{2},\cdots,\left\|a_{:,J_{1},\cdots,J_{M}}\right\|^{2}\right)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Bigg\{\max_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M}}\left\|a_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{M},:}\right\|^{2},\max_{j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\left\|a_{:,j_{1},\cdots,j_{M}}\right\|^{2}\Bigg\}.

Finally, from Corollary
25
, this Corollary is proved.

4.3
Lower and Upper Bounds of Tensor Expectation

Given a finite sequence
\mathcal{A}_{i}
of fixed Hermitian tensors with dimensions as
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, and let
\{\alpha_{i}\}
be a finite sequence of indepedent normal variables. We define following random tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}.

(92)

From Theorem
24
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|>\sqrt{t}\right)dt\leq 2\sigma^{2}\log(2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})

(93)

\displaystyle+2(I_{1}\cdots I_{M})\int_{2\sigma^{2}\log(2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})}^{\infty}=2\sigma^{2}\log(2e\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}).

On the other hand, from Jesen’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|^{2}\right)=\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}^{2}\right\|\geq\left\|\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{2})\right\|=\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right\|=\sigma^{2}.

(94)

From both Eqs. (
93
) and (
94
),
we have following relation:

\displaystyle c\sigma\leq\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|\leq\sigma\sqrt{2\log(2e\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M})}.

(95)

This shows that the tensor variance parameter
\sigma^{2}
controls the expected norm
\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|
with square root of logarithmic function for the tensor dimensions.

5
Tensor Chernoff Bounds

The traditional Chernoff bounds concern the sum of independent, nonnegative, and uniformly bounded random variables. In this work, we will try to extend such Chernoff bounds under the scenario of random tensors.

5.1
Tensor Chernoff Bounds Derivations

We begin to present a Lemma about the semidefinite relation for the tensor moment-generating function of a random positive semidefinite contraction.

Lemma 27
.

Given a random positive semifefinite tensor with
\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X})\leq 1
, then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+(e^{t}-1)\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}}.

(96)

Proof.

Consider a convex function
g(x)=e^{tx}
, we have

\displaystyle 1+(e^{t}-1)x\geq g(x),

(97)

where
x\in[0,1]
. Since the eigenvalues of the random tensor
\mathcal{X}
lie in the interval
[0,1]
, from Eq. (
30
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+(e^{t}-1)\mathcal{X}\succeq e^{t\mathcal{X}}.

(98)

Then, this Lemma is proved by taking the expectation with respect to the random tensor
\mathcal{X}
.
∎

Given two real values
a,b\in[0,1]
, we define
binary information divergence
of
a
and
b
, expressed by
\mathfrak{D}(a||b)
, as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(a||b)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}a\log\frac{a}{b}+(1-a)\frac{1-a}{1-b}.

(99)

We are ready to present tensor Chernoff inequality.

Theorem 28

(Tensor Chernoff Bound I)
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
of independent, random, Hermitian tensors that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq 1\mbox{~~ almost surely.}

(100)

Define following two quantaties:

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}_{\max}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\mbox{~~and~~}\overline{\mu}_{\min}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right),

(101)

then, we have following two inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-n\mathfrak{D}(\theta||\overline{\mu}_{\max})},\mbox{~~ for $\overline{\mu}_{\max}\leq\theta\leq 1$;}

(102)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-n\mathfrak{D}(\theta||\overline{\mu}_{\min})},\mbox{~~ for $0\leq\theta\leq\overline{\mu}_{\min}$.}

(103)

Proof.

From Lemma
27
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+f(t)\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}},

(104)

where
f(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}e^{t}-1
for
t>0
.
By applying Corollary
22
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\alpha\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\alpha+n\log\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{I}+f(t)\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)\right)

(105)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\alpha+n\log\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathcal{I}+f(t)\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\alpha+n\log\left(1+f(t)\overline{\mu}_{\max}\right)\right).

The last equality follows from the definition of
\overline{\mu}_{\max}
and the eigenvalue map properties. When the value
t
at the right-hand side of Eq. (
105
) is

\displaystyle t=\log\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}-\log\frac{\overline{\mu}_{\max}}{1-\overline{\mu}_{\max}},

(106)

we can achieve the tightest upper bound at Eq. (
105
). By substituting the value
t
in Eq. (
106
) into Eq. (
105
) and change the variable
\alpha\rightarrow n\theta
, Eq. (
102
) is proved. The next goal is to prove Eq. (
103
).

If we apply Lemma
27
to the sequence
\{-\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}-g(t)\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{t(-\mathcal{X}_{i})},

(107)

where
g(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}1-e^{t}
for
t>0
.
By applying Corollary
22
again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq\alpha\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(-\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)\geq\alpha\right)

(108)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(t\alpha+n\log\lambda_{\max}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{I}-g(t)\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(t\alpha+n\log\left(1-f(t)\lambda_{\min}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(t\alpha+n\log\left(1-g(t)\overline{\mu}_{\min}\right)\right),

where we apply the relation
\lambda_{\min}(-\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i})=-\lambda_{\max}(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i})
at the equality
=_{1}
. When the value
t
at the right-hand side of Eq. (
108
) is

\displaystyle t=log\frac{\overline{\mu}_{\max}}{1-\overline{\mu}_{\max}}-\log\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha},

(109)

we can achieve the tightest upper bound at Eq. (
108
). By substituting the value
t
in Eq. (
109
) into Eq. (
108
) and change the variable
\alpha\rightarrow n\theta
, Eq. (
103
) is proved also.
∎

The tensor Chernoff bounds discussed at Theorem
28
is not related to
\mu_{\max}
and
\mu_{\min}
directly. Following theorem is another version of tensor Chernoff bounds to associate the probability range in terms of
\mu_{\max}
and
\mu_{\min}
directly and this format of tensor Chernoff bounds are easier to be applied.

Theorem 29

(Tensor Chernoff Bound II)
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
of independent, random, Hermitian tensors that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{~~ almost surely.}

(110)

Define following two quantaties:

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\mbox{~~and~~}\mu_{\min}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right),

(111)

then, we have following two inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq(1+\theta)\mu_{\max}\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\frac{e^{\theta}}{(1+\theta)^{1+\theta}}\right)^{\mu_{\max}/T},\mbox{~~ for $\theta\geq 0$;}

(112)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq(1-\theta)\mu_{\min}\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\frac{e^{-\theta}}{(1-\theta)^{1-\theta}}\right)^{\mu_{\min}/T},\mbox{~~ for $\theta\in[0,1]$.}

(113)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume
T=1
in our proof. From Eq. (
105
) and the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x>-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq t\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp(-\delta t+f(\delta)\mu_{\max})

(114)

By selecting
\delta=\log(1+\theta)
and
t\rightarrow(1+\theta)\mu_{\max}
, we can establish Eq. (
112
).

From Eq. (
108
) and the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq x
for
x>-1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\min}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq t\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp(-\delta t-f(\delta)\mu_{\min})

(115)

By selecting

and
t\rightarrow(1-\theta)\mu_{\min}
, we can establish Eq. (
113
). Therefore, this theorem is proved.
∎

5.2
Application of Tensor Chernoff Bounds

Consider a general random tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{N}}
, we can express the
\mathcal{X}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{J_{1}\cdots J_{N}}],

(116)

where
x_{i}
is a family of independent random tensors in
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
(vector part in
\mathcal{X}
. The squared norm of
\mathcal{X}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{X}\right\|^{2}=\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}^{H})=\lambda_{\max}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{J_{1}\cdots J_{N}}x_{i}\star_{0}\overline{x_{i}}).

(117)

Similarly, for the minimum singular value of the tensor
\mathcal{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\mbox{Minimum singular value of $\mathcal{X}$}=\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{X}\star_{N}\mathcal{X}^{H})=\lambda_{\min}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{J_{1}\cdots J_{N}}x_{i}\star_{0}x_{i}^{H}).

(118)

From tensor Chernoff bounds provided by Theorem
29
, we can bound both Eqs. (
117
) and (
118
).

Another application of tensor Chernoff bounds is to estimate the expectation of the maximum eigenvalue of independent sum of random tensors.

Corollary 30

(Upper and Lower Bounds for the Maximum Eigenvalue)
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
of independent, random, Hermitian tensors that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{~~ almost surely.}

(119)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}\leq\mathbb{E}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq C\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\mu_{\max}/T},

(120)

where the constant value of
C
is about 10.28.

Proof.

The lower bound at Eq. (
120
) is true from the convexity of the function
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{A})
and the Jensen’s inequality.

For the upper bound, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq t\right)dt

(121)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp(-\delta t+(e^{\delta}-1)\mu_{\max}/T)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{e^{\delta}}}{\delta}\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\mu_{\max}/T}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{e^{e^{\delta_{opt}}}}{\delta_{opt}}\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\mu_{\max}/T}=C\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\mu_{\max}/T},

where the inequality
\leq_{1}
comes from Eq. (
114
) with the scaling factor
T
. If we select
\theta
as the solution of the following relation
e^{\delta_{opt}}=\frac{1}{\delta_{opt}}
to minimize the right-hand side of Eq. (
121
), we have the desired upper bound when
\delta_{opt}\approx=0.56699
. This corollary is proved.
∎

6
Tensor Bernstein Bounds

For random variables, Bernstein inequalities give the upper tail of a sum of
independent, zero-mean random variables that are either bounded or subexponential. In this section, we wish to extend Bernstein bounds for a sum of zero-mean random tensors.

6.1
Tensor Bernstein Bounds Derivation

We will condier bounded Tensor Bernstein bounds first by considering the bounded Bernstein moment-generating function with the following Lemma.

Lemma 31
.

Given a random Hermitian tensor
\mathcal{X}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
that satisfies:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X})\leq 1\mbox{~~almost surely.}

(122)

Then, we have

\displaystyle e^{(e^{t}-t-1)\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{2})}\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}}

(123)

where
t>0
.

Proof.

If we define a real function
g(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\frac{e^{tx}-tx-1}{x^{2}}
, it is easy to see that this function
g(x)
is an increasing function for
0<x\leq 1
. From Eq (
30
), we have

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{X})\preceq g(1)\mathcal{I}.

(124)

Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle e^{t\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+t\mathcal{X}+g(\mathcal{X})\star_{M}\mathcal{X}^{2}

(125)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+t\mathcal{X}+g(1)\mathcal{X}^{2},

where the
\preceq
comes from Eq. (
124
). By taking the expectation for both sides of Eq. (
125
), we then obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+g(1)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}\right)\preceq e^{g(1)\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}\right)}

(126)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{(e^{t}-t-1)\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{2})}.

This lemma is established.
∎

We are ready to present the Tensor Bernstein bounds for random tensors with bounded
\lambda_{\max}
.

Theorem 32

(Bounded
\lambda_{\max}
Tensor Bernstein Bounds)
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{X}_{i})\leq T\mbox{~~almost surely.}

(127)

Define the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\right)\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\right);

(128)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T$};

(129)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-3\theta}{8T}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T$}.

(130)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
T=1
since the summands are 1-homogeneous and the variance is 2-homogeneous. From Lemma
31
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\preceq e^{(e^{t}-t-1)\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}_{i}^{2})}\mbox{~~ for $t>0$.}

(131)

By applying Corollary
21
, we then have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\theta+(e^{t}-t-1)\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\right)\right)\right)

(132)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\theta+\sigma^{2}(e^{t}-t-1)\right).

The right-hand side of Eq. (
132
) can be minimized by setting
t=\log(1+\theta/\sigma^{2})
. Substitute such
t
and simplify the right-hand side of Eq. (
132
), we obtain Eq. (
128
).

For
\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+T(\sigma^{2}/T)/3}=\frac{3}{4\sigma^{2}},

(133)

then, we obtain Eq. (
129
). Correspondingly, for
\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}/T}{\sigma^{2}+T(\sigma^{2}/T)/3}=\frac{3}{4T},

(134)

and, we obtain Eq. (
130
) also.
∎

The following theorem
34
is the extension of the theorem
32
by allowing the moments of the random tensors to grow at a controlled rate. We have to prepare subexponential Bernstein moment-generating function Lemma first for later proof of Theorem
34

Lemma 33
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{X}
is a random Hermitian tensor that satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{p})\preceq\frac{p!\mathcal{A}^{2}}{2}\mbox{for $p=2,3,4,\cdots$.}

(135)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}}{2(1-t)}\right)\succeq\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}},

(136)

where
0<t<1
.

Proof.

From Tayler series of the tensor exponential expansion, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+t\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}+\sum\limits_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{p}\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{p})}{p!}\preceq\mathcal{I}+\sum\limits_{p=2}^{\infty}\frac{t^{p}\mathcal{A}^{2}}{2}

(137)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}+\frac{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}}{2(1-t)}\preceq\exp\left(\frac{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}}{2(1-t)}\right),

therefore, this Lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem 34

(Subexponential Tensor Bernstein Bounds)
.

Given a finite sequence of independent Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{p}_{i})\preceq\frac{p!T^{p-2}}{2}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2},

(138)

where
p=2,3,4,\cdots
.

Define the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta}\right);

(139)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T$};

(140)

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(\frac{-\theta}{4T}\right)~~\mbox{for $\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T$}.

(141)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
T=1
. From Lemma
33
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}e^{t\mathcal{X}_{i}}\preceq e^{\frac{t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}}{2(1-t)}},

(142)

where
0<t<1
.

By applying Corollary
21
, we then have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\theta+\frac{t^{2}}{2(1-t)}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right)\right)

(143)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(-t\theta+\frac{\sigma^{2}t^{2}}{2(1-t)}\right).

The right-hand side of Eq. (
143
) can be minimized by setting
t=\frac{\theta}{\theta+\sigma^{2}}
. Substitute such
t
and simplify the right-hand side of Eq. (
143
), we obtain Eq. (
139
).

For
\theta\leq\sigma^{2}/T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta}\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+T(\sigma^{2}/T)}=\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}},

(144)

then, we obtain Eq. (
140
). Similarly, for
\theta\geq\sigma^{2}/T
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\theta}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta}\geq\frac{\sigma^{2}/T}{\sigma^{2}+T(\sigma^{2}/T)}=\frac{1}{2T},

(145)

,
therefore, we also obtain Eq. (
141
).
∎

6.2
Application of Tensor Bernstein Bounds

The tensor Bernstein bounds can also be extended to rectangular tensors by dilation. Consider a sequence of tensors
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}\}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times J_{1}\times\cdots\times J_{M}}
satisfy following:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{Y}_{i}=\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\left\|\mathcal{Y}_{i}\right\|\leq T\mbox{~~almost surely.}

(146)

If the variance
\sigma^{2}
is expressed as:

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\max\Bigg\{\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{Y}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}^{H}_{i}\right\|,\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{Y}^{H}_{i}\star_{M}\mathcal{Y}_{i}\right\|\Bigg\},

(147)

we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{Y}_{i}\right\|\geq\theta\right)\leq\prod_{m=1}^{M}(I_{m}+J_{m})\exp\left(\frac{-\theta^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+T\theta/3}\right);

(148)

from Theorem
32
.

Another application of tensor Bernstein bounds is to get upper and lower Bounds for the maximum eigenvalue with subexponential tensors. This application can relax the corollary
30
conditions by allowing the moments of the random tensors to grow at a controlled rate.

Corollary 35

(Upper and Lower Bounds for the Maximum Eigenvalue for Subexponential Tensors)
.

Consider a sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
of independent, random, Hermitian tensors that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{i}\succeq\mathcal{O}\mbox{~~and~~}\mathbb{E}(\mathcal{X}^{p}_{i})\preceq\frac{p!T^{p-2}}{2}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2},

(149)

and
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right\|
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{\max}\leq\mathbb{E}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq 2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\sigma\mathfrak{G}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2T}\right)+2Te^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4T^{2}}}\right),

(150)

where
\mathfrak{G}(\frac{\sigma}{2T})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\sigma}{2T}}e^{-s^{2}}ds
.

Proof.

The lower bound at Eq. (
150
) is true from the convexity of the function
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{A})
and the Jensen’s inequality.

For the upper bound, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq t\right)dt

(151)

\displaystyle\leq_{1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\int_{0}^{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{T}}\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}\right)dt+\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\int_{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{T}}^{\infty}\exp\left(-\frac{t}{4T}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\left(\sigma\mathfrak{G}\left(\frac{\sigma}{2T}\right)+2Te^{-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{4T^{2}}}\right),

where the inequality
\leq_{1}
comes from the Eqs. (
140
) and (
141
). This corollary is proved by introducing Gaussian integral function
\mathfrak{G}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{x}e^{-s^{2}}ds
.
∎

7
Martingale Deviation Bounds

In this section, we introduce concepts about tensor martingales in Section
7.1
, and extend Hoeffding, Azuma, and McDiarmid inequalities to tensors context in Section
7.2
.

7.1
Tensor Martingales

Necessary definitions about tensor martingales will be provided here for later tensor martingale deviation bounds derivations. Let
(\Omega,\mathfrak{F},\mathbb{P})
be a master probability space. Consider a filtration
\{\mathfrak{F}_{i}\}
contained in the master sigma algebra as:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{F}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{F}_{2}\subset\cdots\subset\mathfrak{F}_{\infty}\subset\mathfrak{F}.

(152)

Given such a filtration, we define the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}_{i}[\cdot]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}_{i}[\cdot|\mathfrak{F}_{i}]
. A sequence
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}\}
of random tensors is called
adapted
to the filtration when each tensor
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
is measurable with respect to
\mathfrak{F}_{i}
. We can think that an adapted sequence is one where the present depends only on the past.

An adapted sequence
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
of Hermitian tensors is named as a
tensor martingale
when

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\mathcal{X}_{i}=\mathcal{X}_{i-1}\mbox{~~~and~~~}\mathbb{E}\left\|\mathcal{X}_{i}\right\|<\infty,

(153)

where
i=1,2,3,\cdots
. We obtain a scalar martingale if we track any fixed entry of a tensor martingale
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
. Given a tensor martingale
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
, we can construct the following new sequence of tensors

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{X}_{i}-\mathcal{X}_{i-1}\mbox{~~for $i=1,2,3,\cdots$}

(154)

We then have
\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\mathcal{Y}_{i}=\mathcal{O}
.

7.2
Tensor Martingale Deviation Bounds

Two Lemmas should be presented first before presenting tensor martingale deviation bounds and their proofs.

Lemma 36

(Tensor Symmetrization)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a fixed Hermitian tensor, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a random Hermitian tensor with
\mathbb{E}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}}\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+2\beta\mathcal{X}},

(155)

where
\beta
is Rademacher random variable.

Proof.

Build an independent copy random tensor
\mathcal{Y}
from
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}
denote the expectation with respect to the new random tensor
\mathcal{Y}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}}=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}-\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Y}}\leq\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}}=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\beta(\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y})},

(156)

where the first equality uses
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{Y}}\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{O}
; the inequality uses the convexity of the trace exponential with Jensen’s inequality; finally, the last equality comes from that the random tensor
\mathcal{X}-\mathcal{Y}
is a symmetric random tensor and Rademacher is also a symmetric random variable.

This Lemma is established by the following:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\left(e^{\mathcal{A}/2+\beta\mathcal{X}}e^{\mathcal{A}/2-\beta\mathcal{Y}}\right)

(157)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+2\beta\mathcal{X}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}-2\beta\mathcal{Y}}\right)^{1/2}=\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}e^{\mathcal{A}+2\beta\mathcal{X}},

where the first inequality comes from tensor Golden-Thompson inequality
[
6
]
, the second inequality comes from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the last identity follows from that the two factors are identically distributed.
∎

The other Lemma is to provide the tensor cumulant-generating function of a symetrized random tensor.

Lemma 37

(Cumulant-Generating Function of Symetrized Random tensor)
.

Given that
\mathcal{X}
is a random Hermitian tensor and
\mathcal{A}
is a fixed Hermitian tensor that satisfies
\mathcal{X}^{2}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta t\mathcal{X}}|\mathcal{X}\right]\preceq 2t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2},

(158)

where
\beta
is a Rademacher random variable.

Proof.

From Lemma
23
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2\beta t\mathcal{X}}|\mathcal{X}\right]\preceq e^{2t^{2}\mathcal{X}^{2}}.

(159)

And, from the monotone property of logarithm, we also have

\displaystyle\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2t\theta\mathcal{X}}|\mathcal{X}\right]\preceq 2t^{2}\mathcal{X}^{2}\preceq 2t^{2}\mathcal{A}^{2}~~\mbox{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

(160)

Therefore, this Lemma is proved.
∎

At this point, we are ready to present tensor martingale deviation bounds. In probability theory, the Azuma inequality for a scaler martingale gives normal concentration about its mean value, and the deviation is controlled by the total maximum squared of the difference sequence. Below is a tensor extension.

Theorem 38

(Tensor Azuma Inequality)
.

Given a finite adapted sequence of Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}\}
and a fixed sequence of Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\mbox{almost surely},

(161)

where
i=1,2,3,\cdots
.

Define the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequalities:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}.

(162)

Proof.

Define the filtration
\mathfrak{F}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathfrak{F}(\mathcal{X}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{X}_{i})
for the process
\{\mathcal{X}_{i}\}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+t\mathcal{X}_{n}\right)|\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)|\mathfrak{F}_{n-1}\right)

(163)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+2\beta t\mathcal{X}_{n}\right)|\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)|\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+\log\mathbb{E}\left(e^{2\beta t\mathcal{X}_{n}}|\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)\right)|\mathfrak{F}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n-1}t\mathcal{X}_{i}+2t^{2}\mathcal{A}_{n}^{2}\right),

where the first equality comes from the total expectation property of conditional expectation; the first inequality comes from Lemma
36
; the second inequality comes from Corollary
18
and the relaxation the connection to the larger algebra set
\mathfrak{F}_{n}
; finally, the last inequality requires Lemma
37
.

If we continue the iteration procedure based on Eq. (
163
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\leq\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(2t^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right),

(164)

then apply Eq. (
164
) into Lemma
13
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\geq\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}t\mathcal{X}_{i}\right)\Big\}

(165)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{Tr}\exp\left(2t^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\lambda_{\max}\left(\exp\left(2t^{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}\right)\right)\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{t>0}\Big\{e^{-t\theta}\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}\exp\left(2t^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}},

where the third inequality utilizes
\lambda_{\max}
to bound trace, the equality applies the definition of
\sigma^{2}
and spectral mapping theorem, finally, we select
t=\frac{\theta}{4\sigma^{2}}
to minimize the upper bound to obtain this theorem.
∎

If we add extra assumption that the summands are independent, Theorem
38
gives a tensor extension of Hoeffding’s inequality. If we apply Theorem
38
to a Hermitian tensor martingale, we will have following corollary.

Corollary 39
.

Given a Hermitian tensor martingale
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}:i=1,2,\cdots,n\}\in

\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
, and let
\mathcal{X}_{i}
be the difference sequence of
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}\}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{Y}_{i}-\mathcal{Y}_{i-1}
for
i=1,2,3,\cdots
. If the difference sequence satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{i-1}\mathcal{X}_{i}=0\mbox{~~and~~}\mathcal{X}^{2}_{i}\preceq\mathcal{A}_{i}\mbox{almost surely},

(166)

where
i=1,2,3,\cdots
and the total varaince
\sigma^{2}
is defined as as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(\mathcal{Y}_{n}-\mathbb{E}\mathcal{Y}_{n}\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}.

(167)

In the scalar setting, McDiarmid inequality can be treated as a corollary of Azuma’s inequality. McDiarmid inequality states that a function of independent random variables exhibits normal concentration about its mean, and the variance
depends on the function value sensitivity with respect to the input. A version
of the bounded differences inequality is still valid in tensor context.

Theorem 40

(Tensor McDiarmid Inequality)
.

Given a set of
n
independent random variables, i.e.
\{X_{i}:i=1,2,\cdots n\}
, and let

F
be a Hermitian tensor-valued function that maps these
n
random variables to a Hermitian tensor of dimension within
\mathbb{C}^{I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}\times I_{1}\times\cdots\times I_{M}}
. Consider a sequence of Hermitian tensors
\{\mathcal{A}_{i}\}
that satisfy

\displaystyle\left(F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i},\cdots,x_{n})-F(x_{1},\cdots,x^{\prime}_{i},\cdots,x_{n})\right)^{2}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i},

(168)

where
x_{i},x^{\prime}_{i}\in X_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq n
. Define the total variance
\sigma^{2}
as:
\sigma^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\left\|\sum\limits_{i}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{i}^{2}\right\|
.
Then, we have following inequality:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left(\lambda_{\max}\left(F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})-\mathbb{E}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\right)\geq\theta\right)\leq\mathbb{I}_{1}^{M}e^{-\frac{\theta^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}}.

(169)

Proof.

We define following random tensors
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n
as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}\left(F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})|X_{1},\cdots,X_{i}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+2}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),

(170)

where
\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}
is the expectation with respect to the random variable
X_{i+1}
. The constructed sequence
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
forms a martingale. The associated difference sequence with respect to
\mathcal{Y}
, denoted as
\{\mathcal{Z}_{i}\}
, can be stated as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny def}}}{{=}}\mathcal{Y}_{i}-\mathcal{Y}_{i-1}=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+2}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}\left(F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})-\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\right).

(171)

Because
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{i})
forms a filtration with respect to
i
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\mathcal{Y}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+2}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})|X_{i-1}\right)

(172)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\left(\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})|X_{i-1}\right)=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\mathcal{Y}_{i-1},

then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\mathcal{Z}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\mathcal{Y}_{i}-\mathbb{E}_{X_{i-1}}\mathcal{Y}_{i-1}=\mathcal{O}

(173)

Let
X^{\prime}_{i}
be an independent copy of
X_{i}
, and construct the following two random vectors:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1},X^{\prime}_{i},X_{i+1},\cdots,X_{n}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(X_{1},\cdots,X_{i-1},X_{i},X_{i+1},\cdots,X_{n}).

(174)

Since
\mathbb{E}_{X_{i}}F(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{E}_{X^{\prime}_{i}}F(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{i}=\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+2}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{X^{\prime}_{i}}\left(F(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right).

(175)

Then,
\left(F(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right)^{2}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}
from requirement provided by Eq. (
168
). We have following upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+1}}\mathbb{E}_{X_{i+2}}\cdots\mathbb{E}_{X_{n}}\mathbb{E}_{X^{\prime}_{i}}\left(F(\mathbf{x})-F(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\right)^{2}\preceq\mathcal{A}^{2}_{i}.

(176)

Therefore, from conditions provided by Eq. (
173
) and Eq. (
176
), this theorem is proved by applying Corollary
39
to the martingale
\{\mathcal{Y}_{i}\}
.
∎

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we generalize Lapalce transform method and Lieb’s concavity theorem from matrices to tensors, and apply these techniques to extend following classical bounds from the scalar to the tensor situation: Chernoff, Azuma, Hoeffding, Bennett, Bernstein, and McDiarmid. The purpose of these probability inequalities tries to identify large-deviation behavior of the extreme eigenvalue of the sums of random tensors. Tail bounds for the norm of a sum of random rectangular tensors follow as an immediate corollary. Finally, we also apply the proof techniques developed at this work to study tensor-valued martingales.

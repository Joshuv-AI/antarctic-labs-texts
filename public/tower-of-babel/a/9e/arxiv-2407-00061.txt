Probabilistic multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and probabilistic multi-Lah numbers Thanks: * is corresponding author.
Taekyun Kim*, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Assume that the moment generating function of the random variable Y Y exists in a neighborhood of the origin. We introduce the probabilistic multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with Y Y and the probabilistic multi-Lah numbers associated with Y Y , both of indices ( k 1 , k 2 , … , k r ) (k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}) , by means of the multiple logarithm. Those numbers are respectively probabilistic extensions of the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and the multi-Lah numbers which, for ( k 1 , k 2 , … , k r ) = ( 1 , 1 , … , 1 ) (k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})=(1,1,\dots,1) , boil down respectively to the Stirling numbers of the second and the unsigned Lah numbers. The aim of this paper is to study some properties, related identities, recurrence relations and explicit expressions of those probabilistic extension numbers in connection with several other special numbers.

For integers
k_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq r)
, the multiple logarithm of index
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
is given by

(1)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)=\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}},\quad(|t|<1).\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [19,24]).

In particular, for
r=1
, we have

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(t)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m^{k_{1}}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [19,24,28]),

where
\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1}}(t)
is called the polylogarithm.

Recently, the multi-Lah number of index
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
is defined by

(2)

\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-t)^{r}}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [19,24]).

Note that

(3)

\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{1,1,\dots,1}(1-e^{-t})}{(1-t)^{r}}=\frac{1}{r!}\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Here
L(n,r)
are the unsigned Lah numbers given by

(4)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}L(n,k)(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [12,17,18,20-25,27]),

where
\langle x\rangle_{0}=1,\ \langle x\rangle_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1),\quad(n\geq 1)
, and
(-1)^{n}\langle-x\rangle_{n}=(x)_{n}
.

In [19], Kim-Kim introduced the multi-Bernoulli number of index
(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
defined by

(5)

\frac{1}{(1-e^{-t})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(1-e^{-t})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Note that
B_{n}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-times}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{r!}B_{n}^{(r)}
, where
B_{n}^{(r)}
are the Bernoulli numbers of order
r
, given by

\bigg(\frac{t}{e^{t}-1}\bigg)^{r}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}^{(r)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-32]).

The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(6)

\langle x\rangle_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brack k}x^{k},\quad(n,k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [17,18,20]).

The Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(7)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,12,29]).

Throughout this paper, we assume that
Y
is random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
,

(8)

E\big[e^{tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}E\big[Y^{n}\big],\quad(|t|<r),\quad\textrm{exists for some $r>0$.}

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of random variable
Y
, and let

S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1),\quad\mathrm{with}\quad S_{0}=0.

The probabilistic Lah numbers associated with
Y
are given by

(9)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(E\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{Y}\bigg]-1\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}L_{Y}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,11,20,21]).

When
Y=1
,
L_{Y}(n,k)=L(n,k),\ (n\geq k\geq 0)
.

From (
9
), we note that

L_{Y}(n,k)=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}E\big[\langle S_{l}\rangle_{n}\big],\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [27]).

In [24], the unsigned multi-Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(10)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brack k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
10
), we note that

(11)

\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brack\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}(t)=\frac{1}{r!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brack r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
11
), we get

{n\brack\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}={n\brack r},\quad(n\geq r\geq 0).

As the inverse relation of (
11
), the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(12)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-e^{t}}\Big)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [24]).

From (
12
), we note that

(13)

\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\Big(1-e^{1-e^{t}}\Big)=\frac{1}{r!}\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{r}=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace r}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
13
), we get

(14)

{n\brace\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}={n\brace r},\quad(n\geq r\geq 1).

By (
11
) and (
12
), we get for
n\geq 0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(15)

{n+1\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}=\sum_{m=r-1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [24]).

The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
are defined by

(16)

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[S_{j}^{n}\big],\quad(n\geq k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20-22,25,32]).

When
Y=1
,

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}j^{n}={n\brace k}.

2.
Probabilistic multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind and probabilistic multi-Lah numbers

For
k_{i}\geq 1
,
(i=1,2,\dots,r)
, by (
1
), we get

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{t^{m_{r}-1}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r}^{k_{r}-1}}=\frac{1}{t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},k_{r}-1}(t).

Letting
k_{r}=1
in (
17
), we have

(18)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{1}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{m_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}t^{m_{r}-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{t^{m_{r-1}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}=\frac{1}{1-t}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1}}(t).

Thus, by (
18
), we get

\mathrm{Li}_{1,1}(t)=\frac{1}{2!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{2},\quad\mathrm{Li}_{1,1,1}(t)=\frac{1}{3!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{3}.

Continuing this process, we have

(19)

\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}(t)=\frac{1}{r!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{r},\quad(r\geq 1).

Now, we define the
probabilistic multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
as

(20)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
, we have

{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}={n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}.

Note that

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\frac{}{}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yt}\big]-1\Big)^{r}=\frac{1}{r!}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(-1)^{r-j}E\big[e^{S_{j}t}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r!}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(-1)^{r-j}E\big[S_{j}^{n}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
16
) and (
21
), we get

(22)

{n\brace\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\frac{}{}_{Y}={n\brace r}_{Y},\quad(n\geq r\geq 1).

From (
1
), we note that

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}

\displaystyle\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{0<m_{1}<m_{2}<\cdots<m_{r}}\frac{(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]})^{m_{r}}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}m_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}m_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{E[Ye^{Yt}]e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\sum_{n_{r}=m_{r-1}+1}^{\infty}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)^{m_{r}-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<m_{1}<\cdots<m_{r-1}}\frac{E[Ye^{Yt}]e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}}{m_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots m_{r-1}^{k_{r-1}}}\frac{(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]})^{m_{r-1}}}{e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}}

\displaystyle=E\big[Ye^{Yt}\big]\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}E\big[Y^{l+1}\big]\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=r-1}^{\infty}{m\brace k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}}_{Y}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r-1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=r-1}^{n}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1}}_{Y}E\big[Y^{n-m+1}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
20
), we get

(24)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r-1}^{\infty}{n+1\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},1}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 2.1
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]})^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r-1},k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}(-1)^{m-l}{l\brace m}{n\brace l}_{Y}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}=\sum_{l=r}^{n}\sum_{m=r}^{l}(-1)^{m-l}{l\brace m}{n\brace l}_{Y}{m\brack k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}.

Now, we consider the
probabilistic multi-Lah numbers associated with
Y
which are given by

(28)

\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\bigg(1-e^{1-E\big[\big(\frac{1}{1-t}\big)^{Y}\big)\big]}\bigg)=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L_{Y}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By (
19
), we note that

(29)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L_{Y}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-\mathrm{times}}}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Li}_{\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1}_{r-\mathrm{times}}}\bigg(1-e^{1-E\big[\big(\frac{1}{1-t}\big)^{Y}\big]}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r!}\bigg(E\bigg[\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{Y}\bigg]-1\bigg)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L_{Y}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
29
), we get

(30)

L_{Y}^{\overbrace{(1,1,\dots,1)}^{r-\mathrm{times}}}(n,r)=L_{Y}(n,r),\quad(n\geq r>0).

Using (
28
) and (
20
) with
t
replaced by
-\log(1-t)
, we get

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}L_{Y}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E\big[\big(\frac{1}{1-t}\big)^{Y}\big]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\frac{1}{k!}\big(-\log(1-t)\big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brack k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}{n\brack k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

L_{Y}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}{n\brack k}.

Now, from (
5
) and (
20
), we observe that

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}

\displaystyle{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\big)}{\big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\big)^{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}\frac{1}{m!}\Big(E[e^{Yt}]-1\Big)^{m}(-1)^{r}r!\frac{1}{r!}\Big(e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}-1\Big)^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}\frac{1}{m!}\Big(E[e^{Yt}]-1\Big)^{m}(-1)^{r}r!\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}{l\brace r}\frac{1}{l!}\Big(1-E[e^{Yt}]\Big)^{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}r!(-1)^{l-r}{l\brace r}\frac{(m+l)!}{m!l!}\frac{1}{(m+l)!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yt}\big]-1\Big)^{m+l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}r!(-1)^{l-r}{l\brace r}\binom{m+l}{m}\sum_{n=m+l}^{\infty}{n\brace m+l}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{m=0}^{n-r}\sum_{l=r}^{n-m}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}r!(-1)^{l-r}\binom{m+l}{m}{l\brace r}{n\brace m+l}_{Y}\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
32
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
n,r
be positive integers with
n\geq r
. Then we have

{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-r}\sum_{l=r}^{n-m}r!(-1)^{l-r}\binom{m+l}{m}{l\brace r}{n\brace m+l}_{Y}B_{m}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}.

In particular, we have

{n\brace r}_{Y}=\sum_{m=0}^{n-r}\sum_{l=r}^{n-m}(-1)^{m+l-r}\binom{m+l}{m}{l\brace r}{n\brace m+l}_{Y}B_{m}^{(r)}.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the probabilistic Fubini polynomials of order
r
associated with
Y
are defined by

(33)

\frac{1}{\big(1-y(E[e^{Yt}]-1)\big)^{r}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(r,Y)}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [32]).

Replacing
t
by
E[e^{Yt}]-1
in (
2
), on the one hand, we have

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2-E[e^{Yt}])^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(k,r)\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yt}\big]-1\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(k,r)\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(k,r)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
20
) and (
33
), we get

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2-E[e^{Yt}])^{r}}\mathrm{Li}_{k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}\Big(1-e^{1-E[e^{Yt}]}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}F_{l}^{(r,Y)}(1)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=r}^{n}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}\binom{n}{k}F_{n-k}^{(r,Y)}(1)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
34
) and (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n\geq r
, we have

\sum_{k=r}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y}L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(k,r)=\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{k\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}F_{n-k}^{(r,Y)}(1).

3.
Conclusion

Let
Y
be a random variable whose moment generating function exists in a neighborhood of the origin. The Stirling numbers of the second
{n\brace r}
are generalized to the multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}
and further to the probabilistic multi-Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}
. Likewise, the unsigned Lah numbers
L(n,r)
are generalized to the multi-Lah numbers
L^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
and further to the probabilistic multi-Lah numbers associated with
L_{Y}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
. In connection with several other special numbers, we investigated some properties, related identities, recurrence relations and explicit expressions of the numbers
{n\brace k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r}}_{Y}
and
L_{Y}^{(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})}(n,r)
.

In recent years, degenerate versions,
\lambda
-analogues and probabilistic extensions of many special numbers and polynomials have been studied and many remarkable results have been obtained (see [2,3,10,11,16-18,20,21,23,25-28,32] and the references therein). It is one of our future research projects to continue to study degenerate versions,
\lambda
-analogues and probabilistic extensions of various special numbers and polynomials and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

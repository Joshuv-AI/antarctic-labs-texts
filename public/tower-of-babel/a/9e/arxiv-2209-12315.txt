Tree decompositions with bounded independence number: beyond independent sets
Martin Milanič, Paweł Rzążewski

Abstract
We continue the study of graph classes in which the treewidth can only be large due to the presence of a large clique, and, more specifically, of graph classes with bounded tree-independence number. In [Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel, Treewidth versus clique number. II. Tree-independence number, 2022], it was shown that the Maximum Weight Independent Packing problem, which is a common generalization of the Independent Set and Induced Matching problems, can be solved in polynomial time provided that the input graph is given along with a tree decomposition with bounded independence number. We provide further examples of algorithmic problems that can be solved in polynomial time under this assumption. This includes, for all even positive integers d d , the problem of packing subgraphs at distance at least d d (generalizing the Maximum Weight Independent Packing problem) and the problem of finding a large induced sparse subgraph satisfying an arbitrary but fixed property expressible in counting monadic second-order logic. As part of our approach, we generalize some classical results on powers of chordal graphs to the context of general graphs and their tree-independence numbers.



1
Introduction

Structured graph decompositions and the corresponding graph width parameters have become one of the central tools for dealing with algorithmically hard graph problems.
One of the most well-known and well-studied graph width parameters is the
treewidth
of a graph, which, roughly speaking, measures how similar the graph is to a tree.
Since graphs of bounded treewidth are necessarily sparse, several generalizations of treewidth were proposed in the literature that allow for families of dense graphs, including clique-width,
rank-width,
Boolean-width,
mim-width,
and twin-width.
A common feature of all these parameters is that when the parameter of the input graph is bounded by a fixed constant, efficient dynamic programming algorithms can be developed for various graph problems, typically applied along some hierarchical decomposition of the graph into simpler parts (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
18
,
34
,
12
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
20
]
, as well as the algorithmic metatheorems
[
3
,
6
,
13
,
18
]
).

Another generalization of bounded treewidth that allows for families of dense graphs is given by the concept of
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-boundedness
, a property of a graph class meaning that the treewidth of graphs in the class can only be large due to the presence of a large clique, and the same is true for all induced subgraphs of graphs in the class.
While this property is known to imply some good algorithmic properties related to clique and coloring problems (see
[
16
,
15
,
22
,
25
,
23
]
), it is an interesting question whether
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-boundedness has any other algorithmic implications, in particular, for problems related to independent sets.
This question was partially answered by Dallard, Milanič, and Štorgel, who initiated a systematic investigation of
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded graph classes (see
[
25
,
22
]
, as well as
[
23
,
24
]
).
In particular, in
[
23
]
a sufficient condition was identified for
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded graph classes to admit a polynomial-time algorithm for the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem and, as a consequence, for the weighted variants of the
Independent Set
and
Induced Matching
problems.
These results were obtained using the notion of
tree-independence number
, a graph invariant related to tree decompositions, which is defined similarly to treewidth but aims at minimizing the maximum size of an
independent
set of vertices contained in a bag of a tree decomposition—as opposed to an arbitrary set of vertices, which is the case for treewidth.

Ramsey’s theorem implies that every graph class with bounded tree-independence number is
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-bounded.
As shown in
[
24
]
, the converse implication is known to hold in graph classes defined by excluding a single graph with respect to any of six well-known graph containment relations (the subgraph, topological minor, and minor relations, as well as their induced variants).
In general, it is not known whether the two concepts are equivalent; it is conjectured that they are
[
24
]
.
The main algorithmic result from
[
23
]
states that the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem can be solved in polynomial time provided that the input graph is given along with a tree decomposition with bounded independence number.
(The precise definition of the problem and the result statement will be given in
Section

2
.)
Several questions regarding the computation of tree-independence number left open by the work
[
23
]
were resolved in a subsequent work by Dallard et al.
[
21
]
, where the following results were obtained.
First, it was shown that the exact computation of the tree-independence number is
para-NP-hard
, more precisely, that for every
k\geq 4
it is
NP
-complete to determine if a given graph has tree-independence number at most
k
.
Second, a polynomial-time algorithm was developed for computing a tree decomposition with bounded independence number, whenever one exists.
Given an
n
-vertex graph
G
and an integer
k
, in time
2^{\mathcal{O}(k^{2})}n^{\mathcal{O}(k)}
the algorithm either outputs a tree decomposition of
G
with independence number at most
8k
, or determines that the tree-independence number of
G
is larger than
k
.

Combined with the results from
[
23
]
, it follows that the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem is polynomial-time solvable in any class of graphs with bounded tree-independence number.
Thus, boundedness of the tree-independence number is an algorithmically useful refinement of
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-boundedness
that is still general enough to capture a wide variety of families of graph classes, including, among others, graph classes of bounded treewidth, graph classes of bounded independence number, and intersection graphs of connected subgraphs of graphs with bounded treewidth (see
[
23
, Theorem 3.12]
).

In this paper,
we provide further examples for algorithmic usefulness of bounded tree-independence number.

1.1
Our results and relation to previous work

We obtain two main sets of results.

First, we generalize the polynomial-time solvability of the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem in classes of graphs equipped with a tree decomposition with bounded tree-independence number from
[
23
]
to the problem of packing subgraphs at distance
d
, for all even positive integers
d
.
The case
d=2
corresponds to the result from
[
23
]
.
Unless
\textsf{P}=\textsf{NP}
, this result does not generalize to odd values of
d
, since the distance-
3
variant of the
Independent Set
problem is
NP
-hard for chordal graphs, which have tree-independence number at most one.

We obtain this algorithmic result by proving a structural result on tree-independence number that may be of interest on its own.
Given a graph
G
and a positive integer
k
, we denote by
G^{k}
the
k
-th power
of
G
, that is, the graph obtained from the graph
G
by adding to it all edges between pairs of vertices at distance at most
k
.
We show that for any graph
G
and any positive integer
k
, the tree-independence number of the graph
G^{k+2}
cannot exceed that of the graph
G^{k}
.
This is a significant generalization of a result of Duchet
[
26
]
, who proved the same result for the case when
G^{k}
is a chordal graph (that is, when
G^{k}
has tree-independence number at most one).
As a consequence, we obtain that for every positive integer
k
, the class of graphs with tree-independence number at most
k
is closed under taking odd powers, which generalizes the analogous result for the class of chordal graphs proved by Balakrishnan and Paulraja
[
5
]
.
We complement this result by observing that the class of even powers of chordal graphs (for any fixed power) is not contained in any nontrivial hereditary graph class.
This implies in particular that any such class is
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-unbounded and has unbounded tree-independence number.

Our second set of results is an algorithmic metatheorem for graphs equipped with a tree decomposition with bounded independence number.
It deals with the problem of finding a large induced subgraph of bounded chromatic number, satisfying an arbitrary but fixed property expressible in Counting Monadic Second-Order logic (
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
). This far-reaching generalization of
Max Weight Independent Set
goes along the lines of analogous results proven in various settings, e.g., for bounded-treewidth graphs
[
17
]
, for graphs with polynomially many
potential maximal cliques

[
28
]
, or for graphs with no long induced cycles
[
1
,
31
]
.
An astute reader might notice that usually such problems concern finding large
sparse
subgraphs, which typically means bounded treewidth
[
28
,
1
]
or degeneracy
[
31
]
.
However, it is straightforward to observe that in classes of bounded tree-independence number treewidth is bounded by a linear function of the chromatic number. Note that this is far from the truth in general graphs; consider, e.g., large complete bipartite graphs.

Some examples of problems that can be solved in polynomial time with this approach include

•

finding the largest induced forest (which is equivalent to
Min Feedback Vertex Set
),

•

finding the largest induced bipartite subgraph (which is equivalent to
Min Odd Cycle Transversal
),

•

finding the largest induced planar subgraph (which is equivalent to
Planarization
),

•

finding the maximum number of pairwise disjoint and non-adjacent cycles,

•

given a graph whose every vertex
v
is equipped with a list
L(v)\subseteq\{1,\ldots,r\}
for constant
r
,
finding a largest induced subgraph that admits a proper
r
-coloring respecting lists
L
.

We point out that the bound on the chromatic number of the subgraph we are looking for must be constant.
Indeed,
Coloring
is
NP
-hard in graphs with tree-independence number 2, and
List Coloring
is
NP
-hard in graphs with tree-independence number 1 (i.e., chordal graphs).

Organization of the paper

In
Section

2
, we provide the necessary preliminaries.
In
Section

In

2
Preliminaries

For an integer
n
, by
[n]
we denote the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let
G
be a graph.
For a set
X\subseteq V(G)
, by
G[X]
we denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
.
A
clique
in a graph
G
is a set of pairwise adjacent vertices; an
independent set
is a set of pairwise non-adjacent vertices.

The
distance
between two vertices
u
and
v
in
G
is denoted by
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)
and defined as the length of a shortest path between
u
and
v
(or
\infty
if there is no
u
-
v
-path in
G
).
Given a positive integer
k
, the
k
-th power
of
G
is the graph
G^{k}
with vertex set
V
, in which two distinct vertices
u
and
v
are adjacent if and only if
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq k
. Note that
G^{1}=G
.

A graph is
chordal
if it does not contain any induced cycles of length at least four.
A graph is
split
if it admits a partition of the vertex set into a clique and an independent set.
It is known (and easy to see) that every split graph is a chordal graph.

Tree decompositions and tree-independence number.

A
tree decomposition
of a graph
G
is a pair
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
where
T
is a tree and every node
t
of
T
is assigned a vertex subset
X_{t}\subseteq V(G)
called a
bag
such that the following conditions are satisfied: every vertex is in at least one bag, for every edge
uv\in E(G)
there exists a node
t\in V(T)
such that
X_{t}
contains both
u
and
v
, and for every vertex
u\in V(G)
the subgraph of
T
induced by the set
\{t\in V(T):u\in X_{t}\}
is connected (that is, a tree).

Usually we consider tree decompositions to be rooted.
For a node
t
of
T
, by
T_{t}
we denote the subtree of
T
rooted at
t
, and by
G_{t}
we denote the subgraph of
G
induced by
\bigcup_{t^{\prime}\in V(T_{t})}X_{t^{\prime}}
.
A rooted tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X\}_{t\in V(T)})
of a graph
G
is
nice

if each node
t
is of one of the following types:

leaf

a leaf in the decomposition tree such that
|X_{t}|=1
,

introduce

an inner node with a single child
t^{\prime}
, such that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\cup\{v\}
for some
v\in V(G)\setminus X_{t^{\prime}}
,

forget

an inner node with a single child
t^{\prime}
, such that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\setminus\{v\}
for some
v\in X_{t^{\prime}}
,

join

an inner node with exactly two children
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
, such that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t^{\prime\prime}}
.

It is well known that given a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
, one can obtain in polynomial time a nice tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T^{\prime},\{X^{\prime}t\}_{t\in V(T^{\prime})})
of
G
with at most
\max_{t\in V(T)}|X_{t}|\cdot|V(G)|
nodes and whose every bag is contained in some bag of
\mathcal{T}
(see, e.g., the textbook
[
19
]
, or
[
23
]
for a treatment focused on the independence number).

Consider a graph
G
and a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
.
The
independence number
of
\mathcal{T}
, denoted by
\alpha(\mathcal{T})
, is defined as follows:

\alpha(\mathcal{T})=\max_{t\in V(T)}\alpha(G[X_{t}])\,.

Note that if
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
are tree decompositions such that every bag of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is contained in some bag of
\mathcal{T}
(in particular,
if
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is a nice tree decomposition obtained from
\mathcal{T}
), then we have
\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})
.

The
tree-independence number
of
G
, denoted by
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
, is the minimum independence number among all possible tree decompositions of
G
.
A graph
G
is
chordal
if it has no induced subgraphs isomorphic to a cycle of length at least four.
A graph
G
is chordal if and only if
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)\leq 1
(see
[
23
]
).

Independent packing of subgraphs.

Given a graph
G
and a family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of subgraphs of
G
, we denote by
G(\mathcal{H})
the graph with vertex set
J
, in which two distinct elements
i,j\in J
are adjacent if and only if
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
either have a vertex in common or there is an edge in
G
connecting them.
This construction was considered by Cameron and Hell in
[
14
]
, who focused on the particular case when
\mathcal{H}
is the set of all subgraphs of
G
isomorphic to a member of a fixed family
\mathcal{F}
of connected graphs; they showed that if

is a chordal graph, then so is

.
Dallard et al. generalized this result as follows.

Lemma 2.1

(Dallard et al.
[
23
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph, let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
be a tree decomposition of

, and let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a finite family of connected non-null subgraphs of

.
Then
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=\big(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)}\big)
with
X^{\prime}_{t}=\{j\in J:V(H_{j})\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset\}
for all
t\in V(T)
is a tree decomposition of
G(\mathcal{H})
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})
.

Let us now explain the main algorithmic result related to packing subgraphs from
[
23
]
.
Given a graph
G
and a family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of subgraphs of
G

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
of

is said to be an

\mathcal{H}
-packing

if every two graphs in
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
are vertex-disjoint and there is no edge between them, in other words, if

is an independent set in the graph

.
Assume now that the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
are equipped with a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
assigning weight
\mathfrak{w}_{j}
to each subgraph

.
For any set
I\subseteq J
, we define the

of the family
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
as the sum
\sum_{i\in I}\mathfrak{w}_{i}
.
In particular, the weight of any independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing is well-defined.
Given a graph
G
, a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
on the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
, the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem asks to find an independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing in

of maximum weight.

When
\mathcal{H}
is the set of all subgraphs of
G
isomorphic to a member of a fixed family
\mathcal{F}
of connected graphs, the problem coincides with the
Max Weight Independent
\mathcal{F}
-Packing
problem studied in Dallard et al.
[
23
]
.
This latter problem in turn generalizes several problems studied in the literature, including the
Independent
\mathcal{F}
-Packing
problem (see
[
14
]
), which corresponds to the unweighted case, the
Max Weight Independent Set
problem, which corresponds to the case
\mathcal{F}=\{K_{1}\}
, the
Max Weight Induced Matching
problem (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
38
]
), which corresponds to the case
\mathcal{F}=\{K_{2}\}
, and the
Dissociation Set
problem (see, e.g.,
[
40
,
39
,
37
]
), which corresponds to the case when
\mathcal{F}=\{K_{1},K_{2}\}
and the weight function assigns to each subgraph

the weight equal to

, and the

-Separator
problem (see, e.g.,
[
35
,
36
,
7
]
), which corresponds to the case when
\mathcal{F}
contains all connected graphs with at most

is equipped with a vertex weight function
\mathfrak{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}

.

Dallard et al. showed in
[

]
that the

Theorem 2.2

(Dallard et al.
[
23
]
)
.

Fix an integer

.
Then, given a graph
G
and a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected nonnull subgraphs of
G
, the
Max Weight Independent Packing
problem can be solved in time
\mathcal{O}(|J|\cdot((|J|+|V(T)|)\cdot|V(G)|+|E(G)|+|J|^{k}\cdot|V(T)|)
if

is given together with a tree decomposition

with independence number at most

.

Packing subgraphs at even distance

Given a positive integer
d
, a
distance-
d
independent set
in a graph
G
is a set of vertices at pairwise distance at least
d
, that is, an independent set in the graph
G^{d-1}
.
The
Distance-
d
Independent Set
problem is the following problem:
Given a graph
G
and a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
, find a distance-

For

, the problem coincides with the classical

3.1
Tree-independence number and graph powers

Balakrishnan and Paulraja proved in
[
5
]
that the class of chordal graphs is closed under taking odd powers.
Combined with the polynomial-time solvability of the
Max Weight Independent Set
problem in the class of chordal graphs (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
), this implies that the
Distance-
d
Independent Set
problem is solvable in polynomial time in the class of chordal graphs for all even
d\geq 2
.
On the other hand, Eto et al.
[
27
]
showed that the problem is
NP
-complete in the class of chordal graphs for all odd
d\geq 3
.

The result of Balakrishnan and Paulraja was generalized by Duchet as follows.

Theorem 3.1

(Duchet
[
26
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a positive integer such that
G^{k}
is chordal.
Then the graph
G^{k+2}
is also chordal.

In terms of tree-independence number,
Theorem

3.1
can be phrased as follows.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a positive integer such that
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k})\leq 1
.
Then
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k+2})\leq 1
.

We now generalize this result as follows.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a positive integer.
Then
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k+2})\leq\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k})
.

The proof of
Theorem

3.3
will be based on the following lemma, the case
d=1
of which was observed by Duchet in
[
26
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, and
k
and
d
positive integers.
For
v\in V
, let
H_{v}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices at distance at most
d
from
v
, and let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{v}\}_{v\in V}
.
Then
G^{k+2d}=G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
.

Proof.

Let us first observe that for every vertex
v
of
G
, the graph
H_{v}
is also a subgraph of
G^{k}
.
Hence, the graph
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
is well-defined.
The graphs
G^{k+2d}
and
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
both have the same vertex set, namely
V
.

As for the edge set, consider two distinct vertices
u
and
v
in
V
.
Assume first that
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
.
Then the graphs
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
either have a vertex in common, or they are vertex-disjoint but there is an edge between them in
G^{k}
.
If
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
have a vertex in common, say
x\in V(H_{u})\cap V(H_{v})
, then
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,x)+\textsf{dist}_{G}(x,v)\leq 2d\leq k+2d
, and hence
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G^{k+2d}
.
If
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
are vertex-disjoint but there exist vertices
x\in V(H_{u})
and
y\in V(H_{v})
such that
\{x,y\}\in E(G^{k})
, then
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,x)+\textsf{dist}_{G}(x,y)+\textsf{dist}_{G}(y,v)\leq d+k+d=k+2d
, and hence
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G^{k+2d}
.

Assume now that
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G^{k+2d}
.
Then
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq k+2d
.
We want to show that the graphs
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
either have a vertex in common, or they are vertex-disjoint but there is an edge between them in
G^{k}
.
If
u
and
v
at distance at most
2d
in
G
, then there exists a vertex
x\in V
that is at distance in
G
at most
d
from each of
u
and
v
, and hence the graphs
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
have a vertex in common.
We may thus assume that
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\geq 2d+1
or, equivalently, that the graphs
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
are vertex-disjoint.
Since
2d+1\leq\textsf{dist}_{G}(u,v)\leq k+2d
, there exists a
u
-
v
-path
P=(u=u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{q}=v)
in
G
such that
2d+1\leq q\leq k+2d
.
This implies that
1\leq\textsf{dist}_{G}(u_{d},u_{q-d})\leq k
and hence the vertices
u_{d}
and
u_{q-d}
are adjacent in
G^{k}
.
Since
u_{d}
is at distance at most
d
from
u
, it belongs to
H_{u}
.
Similarly,
u_{q-d}
belongs to
H_{v}
.
Thus,
\{u_{d},u_{q-d}\}
is an edge in
G^{k}
between the subgraphs
H_{u}
and
H_{v}
.
This shows that
u
and
v
are adjacent in
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
.

We showed that the graphs
G^{k+2d}
and
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
have the same vertex sets and the same edge sets.
Hence, they are the same.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

3.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and
k
a positive integer.
For
v\in V
, let
H_{v}
be the subgraph of

induced by
v
and its neighbors, and let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{v}\}_{v\in V}
.
Let

and let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
be a tree decomposition of
G^{k}
such that the independence number of
\mathcal{T}
equals

.
Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
with
X^{\prime}_{t}=\{v\in V:V(H_{v})\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset\}
for all
t\in V(T)
.
Since for each
v\in V
, the graph
H_{v}
is a connected non-null subgraph of

,
Lemma

2.1
implies that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is a tree decomposition of
G^{k}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})=q
.
Since

, we conclude that
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k+2})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq q=\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k})
.
∎

Theorem

3.3
has the following consequence.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
an odd positive integer.
Then
\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k})\leq\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G)
.

We will also make use of the following algorithmic version of
Corollary

3.5
.

Corollary 3.6
.

For every positive odd integer
k
, there exists an algorithm that takes as input a graph
G
and a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G
, and computes in time
\mathcal{O}\left((|V(G)|+|V(T)|)\cdot(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)\right)
the graph
G^{k}
and a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G^{k}
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

The statement is trivial for
k=1
, so let us assume that
k\geq 3
.
Let
d=(k-1)/2
.
For
v\in V(G)
, let
H_{v}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices at distance at most
d
from
v
in
G
, and let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{v}\}_{v\in V(G)}
.

Lemma

3.4
implies that
G^{k}=G(\mathcal{H})
.
Let
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
with
X^{\prime}_{t}=\{v\in V(G):V(H_{v})\cap X_{t}\neq\emptyset\}
for all
t\in V(T)
.
Since for each
v\in V(G)
, the graph
H_{v}
is a connected non-null subgraph of
G
,
Lemma

2.1
implies that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
is a tree decomposition of

such that
\alpha(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})\leq\alpha(\mathcal{T})
.
Note that for a vertex

, the condition

is equivalent to the condition that

is at distance at most
d
in

from

.
Thus, each bag

can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)
using a BFS traversal up to distance

from a new vertex adjacent to all vertices of the bag

.
The graph

can be computed using a BFS traversal from each vertex

in time

\mathcal{O}(|V(G)|(|V(G)|+|E(G)|))

The total time complexity of this approach is

∎

Finally, let us remark that a result similar to
Corollary

3.5
does not hold for even powers.

Proposition 3.7
.

Fix an even positive integer
k
.
Then there is no function
f
satisfying

\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G^{k})\leq f(\mathsf{tree}\textnormal{-}\alpha(G))

for all graphs
G
.

Proof.

Since the tree-independence number of chordal graphs is at most one, it suffices to show that the

k
-th powers of chordal graphs have arbitrarily large tree-independence number.
To this end, we show that for every graph
H
there exists a chordal graph
G
such that
G^{k}
contains an induced subgraph isomorphic to
H
.
This will suffice, since there exist graphs with arbitrarily large tree-independence numbers (for example complete bipartite graphs
K_{n,n}
, see
[
23
]
).
First, let
\widehat{H}
be the graph obtained from
H
by subdividing each edge exactly once and adding edges so that the newly introduced vertices form a clique.
Then
V(H)
is an independent set in
\widehat{H}
and
V(\widehat{H})\setminus V(H)
is a clique in
\widehat{H}
.
Hence
\widehat{H}
is a split graph and thus a chordal graph.
We obtain the graph
G
from
\widehat{H}
by appending to each vertex
v\in V(H)
a path
P^{v}
of length
(k-2)/2
such that one endpoint of
P^{v}
is
v
and all the other vertices of
P^{v}
are new.
In particular, for any two different vertices
v,w\in V(H)
, the corresponding paths
P^{v}
and
P^{w}
are vertex-disjoint.
By construction, the graph
G
is chordal.
For each vertex
v\in V(H)
, let us denote the two endpoints of
P^{v}
by
v
and
v^{\prime}
(with
v^{\prime}=v
if and only if
k=2
), and let
X=\{v^{\prime}:v\in V(H)\}
.
For arbitrary two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(H)
, it holds that
u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in E(G^{k})
if and only if
\textsf{dist}_{G}(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\leq k
, which happens if and only if
u
and
v
are adjacent in
H
.
Thus, the subgraph of
G^{k}
induced by
X
is isomorphic to
H
.
This shows that for every graph
H
there exists a chordal graph
G
such that
G^{k}
contains an induced subgraph isomorphic to
H
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

Since the balanced complete bipartite graph
K_{n,n}
has clique number two and treewidth
n
, the above proof shows, in fact, that for any even positive integer
k
, the class of
k
-th powers of chordal graphs is
(\mathrm{tw},\omega)
-unbounded.

3.2
Algorithmic consequences

Given a graph
G
and two sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
\textsf{dist}_{G}(A,B)
the minimum over all distances in
G
between a vertex in
A
and a vertex in
B
.
This definition is naturally extended to subgraphs of
G
, by considering the distance between their vertex sets.

Fix a positive integer
d
.
Given a graph
G
and a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, a
distance-
d

\mathcal{H}
-packing
in
G
is a subfamily
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subgraphs from
\mathcal{H}
(that is,
I\subseteq J
) that are at pairwise distance at least
d
.
If the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
are equipped with a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
assigning weight
\mathfrak{w}_{j}
to each subgraph
H_{j}
, we define the
weight
of a distance-
d
independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
G
as the sum
\sum_{i\in I}\mathfrak{w}_{i}
.
Given a graph
G
, a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and a weight function
w:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
on the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
, the
Maximum Weight Distance-
d
Packing
problem asks to find a distance-
d

\mathcal{H}
-packing in
G
of maximum weight.
When
d=2
and
\mathcal{H}
is the set of all subgraphs of
G
isomorphic to a member of a fixed family
\mathcal{F}
of connected graphs, the problem coincides with the
Maximum Weight Independent
\mathcal{F}
-Packing
problem studied in Dallard et al.
[
23
]
.

Observation 3.9
.

Let
d
be a positive integer, let
G
be a graph, let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a finite family of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and let
I\subseteq J
.
Then, the corresponding subfamily
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a distance-
d

\mathcal{H}
-packing in
G
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is an independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing in the graph
G^{d-1}
.

Proof.

Consider two distinct elements
i,j\in I
.
It suffices to show that
\textsf{dist}_{G}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\geq d
if and only if
\textsf{dist}_{G^{d-1}}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\geq 2
, or, equivalently, that
\textsf{dist}_{G}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq d-1
if and only if
\textsf{dist}_{G^{d-1}}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq 1
.
Assume first that
\textsf{dist}_{G}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq d-1
and let
P=(v_{0},\ldots,v_{r})
be a path in
G
from a vertex in
H_{i}
to a vertex in
H_{j}
such that
r\leq d-1
.
If
r=0
then
\textsf{dist}_{G^{d-1}}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))=0
.
If
r>0
, then in
G^{d-1}
, vertices
v_{0}
and
v_{r}
are adjacent, and hence
\textsf{dist}_{G^{d-1}}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq 1
.
Assume now that
\textsf{dist}_{G^{d-1}}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq 1
.
If this distance is
0
, then the graphs
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
have a vertex in common and hence
\textsf{dist}_{G}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))=0\leq d-1
.
If this distance is
1
, then there is a path
P
in
G
of length at most
d-1
from a vertex in
H_{i}
to a vertex in
H_{j}
.
Thus,
\textsf{dist}_{G}(V(H_{i}),V(H_{j}))\leq d-1
.
∎

We now generalize
Theorem

2.2
.

Theorem 3.10
.

For every even positive integer
d
and every
k\geq 1
, given a graph
G
, a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
on the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
, the
Maximum Weight Distance-
d
Packing
problem is solvable in time
\mathcal{O}\left((|V(G)|+|V(T)|)\cdot|E(G)|+|J|\cdot|V(G)|\cdot((|J|+|V(T)|+|V(G)|)+|J|^{k+1}\cdot|V(T)|)\right)
if
G
is given with a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
with independence number at most
k
.

Proof.

Let the input to the
Maximum Weight Distance-
d
Packing
be given by the graph
G
, a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
on the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
.
The problem asks for a maximum-weight distance-
d

\mathcal{H}
-packing in
G
, that is, a maximum-weight subfamily
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of subgraphs from
\mathcal{H}
that are at pairwise distance at least
d
in
G
.
By
3.9
, for every
I\subseteq J
, the corresponding subfamily
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\{H_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a distance-
d

\mathcal{H}
-packing in
G
if and only if
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is an independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing in the graph
G^{d-1}
.

By
Corollary

3.6
, the graph
G^{d-1}
and a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(T,\{X^{\prime}_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
of
G^{d-1}
with independence number at most
k
can be computed from
G
and
\mathcal{T}
in time
\mathcal{O}\left((|V(G)|+|V(T)|)\cdot(|V(G)|+|E(G)|)\right)
.
By
Theorem

2.2
, a maximum-weight independent
\mathcal{H}
-packing in the graph
G^{d-1}
can be computed in time
\mathcal{O}(|J|\cdot((|J|+|V(T)|)\cdot|V(G^{d-1})|+|E(G^{d-1})|+|J|^{k}\cdot|V(T)|))
.
Using the fact that
|V(G^{d-1})|=|V(G)|
and
|E(G^{d-1})|\leq|V(G)|^{2}
, the time complexity simplifies to

\mathcal{O}(|J|\cdot((|J|+|V(T)|)\cdot|V(G)|+|V(G)|^{2}+|J|^{k}\cdot|V(T)|))
.
Altogether, we obtain the claimed time complexity
\mathcal{O}\left((|V(G)|+|V(T)|)\cdot|E(G)|+|J|\cdot|V(G)|\cdot((|J|+|V(T)|+|V(G)|)+|J|^{k+1}\cdot|V(T)|)\right)
.
∎

Note that the proof of
Theorem

3.10
does not require the distance bound

to be fixed.
The result remains true, and with the stated time complexity, even if
d
is part of input (as long as it is even).
Furthermore, if the input graph is not equipped with a tree decomposition of bounded independence number, we can obtain one in polynomial time using a recent result of Dallard et al.
[
21
]
.
Thus, given a graph
G
, a finite family
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of connected non-null subgraphs of
G
, and a weight function
\mathfrak{w}:J\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
on the subgraphs in
\mathcal{H}
, the

d
Packing
problem is polynomial-time solvable in any class of graphs in which the tree-independence number is bounded by a constant.

On the other hand, the assumption that
d
is even is necessary in
Theorem

3.10
, unless
\textsf{P}=\textsf{NP}
.
Indeed, Eto et al.
[
27
]
showed that for all odd
d\geq 3
, the
Distance-
d
Independent Set
problem is
NP
-complete in the class of chordal graphs, which is exactly the class of graphs with tree-independence number at most
1
.
Note that this

-completeness result is in line with the fact, shown in the proof of
Proposition

3.7
, that even powers of chordal graphs may contain arbitrary graphs as induced subgraphs.

4
Finding large induced sparse subgraphs and
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}

We start this section by recalling some basic notions and properties related to
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
.

4.1
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
: basic notions and properties

\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
logic on graphs

We assume that graphs are encoded as relational structures: each vertex and each edge is represented by a single variable (distinguishable by a unary predicate), and there is a single binary relation
\mathsf{inc}
binding each edge with its endvertices.

\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
(
Monadic Second Order
) logic is a logic on graphs that allows us to use vertex variables, edge variables, vertex sets variables, and edge set variables,
and, furthermore, to quantify over them. An example of an
\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
formula is the following expression checking if the chromatic number of a given graph is at most
r
(for constant
r
).
Here,
S_{i}
’s are vertex set variables,
e
is an edge variable, and
x,y
are vertex variables.

\displaystyle\begin{split}\phi_{\chi\leq r}\;:=\;&\exists_{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{r}}\;(\forall_{x}\;x\in S_{1}\cup S_{2}\ldots\cup S_{r})\;\land\\
&\forall_{e}\forall_{x,y}\;(\mathsf{inc}((e,x)\land\mathsf{inc}(e,y)\land x\neq y)\to\bigwedge_{i=1}^{r}(x\notin S_{i}\lor y\notin S_{i}).\end{split}

(1)

For a positive integer
p
, by
\mathsf{C}_{\leq p}\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
we mean the extension of
\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
that allows us to use atomic formulae of the form
|S|\equiv a\bmod b
, where
S
is a (vertex of edge) set variable, and
a,b\leq p
are integers. We define
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
(
Counting Monadic Second Order
logic) to be
\bigcup_{p>0}
\mathsf{C}_{\leq p}\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
.
The
quantifier rank
of a formula is the maximum number of nested quantifiers in the formula.

Boundaried graphs and
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
types

Let
\ell>0
be an integer.
An
\ell
-boundaried graph
is a pair
(G,\iota)
, where
G
is a graph and
\iota
is a partial injective function from
V(G)
to
[\ell]
. The domain of
\iota
, denoted by
\mathrm{dom}(\iota)
is the
boundary
of
(G,\iota)
. For
v\in\mathrm{dom}(\iota)
, the value of
\iota(v)
is called the
label
of
v
.

Let us define two natural operations on boundaried graphs.
For an
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota)
and
l\in[\ell]
,
the result of
forgetting the label
l
is the
[\ell]
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota_{\neg l})
,
here
\iota_{\neg l}:=\iota|_{\mathrm{dom}(\iota)\setminus\iota^{-1}(l)}
.
In other words, if the boundary of
(G,\iota)
contains vertex
v
with label
l
,
we remove the label from
v
, while keeping
v
in the graph. Note that if
l\notin\mathrm{dom}(\iota)
, then
this operation does not do anything.

For two
\ell
-boundaried graphs
(G_{1},\iota_{1})
and
(G_{2},\iota_{2})
,
the result of
gluing
them is the
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G_{1},\iota_{1})\oplus_{[\ell]}(G_{2},\iota_{2})
obtained from the disjoint union of
(G_{1},\iota_{1})
and
(G_{2},\iota_{2})
by identifying vertices with the same label.
Note that it might happen that some label
l
is used in, e.g.,
\iota_{1}
but not in
\iota_{2}
. Then the vertex with label
l
in
(G_{1},\iota_{1})\oplus_{[\ell]}(G_{2},\iota_{2})
is the copy of the vertex with label
l
from
(G_{1},\iota_{1})
.

It is straightforward to observe that a graph has treewidth less than
\ell

if and only if it can be constructed from
\ell
-boundaried graphs with at most two vertices by a sequence of forgetting and gluing operations (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
).

By
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
in
[\ell]
-boundaried graphs we mean
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
extended with
\ell
unary predicates: for each
l\in[\ell]
we have a predictate that selects a vertex with label
l
(if such a vertex exists).
The following proposition is folklore (see, e.g.,
[
33
, Lemma 6.1]
). The exact formulation we use here comes from Gartland et al.
[
31
, Proposition 8]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every triple of integers
\ell,p,q
, there exists a finite set
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
and a function that assigns to every
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota)
a
type

\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota)\in\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
such that the following holds:

1.

The types of isomorphic graphs are the same.

2.

For every
\mathsf{C}_{\leq p}\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
sentence
\phi
on
\ell
-boundaried graphs, whether
(G,\iota)
satisfies
\phi
depends only on the type
\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota)
, where
q
is the quantifier rank of
\phi
.
More precisely,
there exists a subset
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}[\phi]\subseteq\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}

such that for every
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota)
we have

(G,\iota)\models\phi\qquad\textrm{if and only if}\qquad\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota)\in\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}[\phi].

3.

The types of ingredients determine the type of the result of the gluing operation.
More precisely, for every two types
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}\in\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}

there exists a type
\tau_{1}\oplus_{\ell,p,q}\tau_{2}
such that
for every two
\ell
-boundaried graphs
(G_{1},\iota_{1})
,
(G_{2},\iota_{2})
,
if
\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G_{i},\iota_{i})=\tau_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then

\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}((G_{1},\iota_{1})\oplus_{[\ell]}(G_{2},\iota_{2}))=\tau_{1}\oplus_{\ell,p,q}\tau_{2}.

Also, the operation
\oplus_{\ell,p,q}
is associative and commutative.

4.

The type of the ingredient determines the type of the result of the forget label operation.
More precisely,
for every type
\tau\in\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
and
l\in[\ell]

there exists a type
\tau_{\neg l}
such that
for every
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota)
,
if
\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota)=\tau

and
(G,\iota_{\neg l})
is the result of forgetting
l
in
(G,\iota)
, then

\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota_{\neg l})=\tau_{\neg l}.

Intuitively, for any
\ell
-boundaried graph
(G,\iota)
,
whether
(G,\iota)\models\phi
is fully determined by
\mathsf{type}^{\ell,p,q}(G,\iota)
(where
p
and
q
are as in
Proposition

4.1
).
Furthermore, if
\phi
and
\ell
are fixed, then the sets
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
and
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}[\phi]
are of constant size.
Finally, it is known that there is an algorithm that, given
\ell
and
\phi
, computes
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
and
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}[\phi]

[
10
]
.

4.2
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
-definable subgraphs of graphs with small tree-independence number

Now we are ready to prove our main result concerning sparse induced subgraphs satisfying some
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
-definable property.

Theorem 4.2
.

For every
k,r
and a
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
formula
\psi
there exists a positive integer
f(k,r,\psi)
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be a graph given along with a tree decomposition of independence number at most
k
,
and let
\mathfrak{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
be a weight function.
Then in time
f(k,r,\psi)\cdot|V(G)|^{\mathcal{O}(kr)}
we can find a set
Y\subseteq V(G)
, such that

1.

G[Y]\models\psi
,

2.

\chi(G[Y])\leq r
,

3.

F
is of maximum weight subject to the conditions above,

or conclude that no such set exists.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
be a tree decomposition of
G
with independence number at most
k
.
Recall that without loss of generality we can assume that
\mathcal{T}
is nice.
Let
F
be a fixed (unknown) optimal solution. Note that by removing all vertices from
V(G)\setminus F
from each bag of
\mathcal{T}
, we obtain a tree decomposition of
G[F]
with independence number at most
k
. As
\chi(G[F])\leq r
, we conclude that every bag of
\mathcal{T}
contains at most
kr
vertices of
F
.
In particular, the treewidth of
G[F]
is less than
\ell:=kr
.
This is the only way in which we use that
\chi(G[F])\leq r
.
Thus, in order to make sure that the obtained solution satisfies the bound on the chromatic number,
instead of
\psi
we will use the
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
formula
\phi:=\psi\land\phi_{\chi\leq r}
, where
\phi_{\chi\leq r}
is the formula (
1
).

We will think of
G[F]
as constructed by a sequence of forgetting and gluing operations on
\ell
-boundaried graphs.

Consider the minimum integer
p
such that
\phi
belongs to
\mathsf{C}_{\leq p}\mathsf{MSO}_{2}
, and let
q
be the quantifier rank of
\phi
.
Let
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}
and
\mathsf{Types}^{\ell,p,q}[\phi]
be as in
Proposition

4.1
.
Recall that these sets are of constant size (since
k,r
, and
\phi
are fixed); we assume that they are hard-coded in our algorithm.
Similarly, we assume that the forgetting and gluing operations on types, i.e., functions

(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\mapsto\tau_{1}\oplus_{\ell,p,q}\tau_{2}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad(\tau,l)\mapsto\tau_{\neg l},

are hard-coded. Finally, our algorithm has the list of types of all
\ell
-boundaried graphs with at most
\ell
vertices.
As
\ell,p,q
are fixed throughout the algorithm, we will simplify the notation by dropping subscripts and superscripts, i.e., we will simply write
\mathsf{Types},\mathsf{Types}[\phi]
, and
\oplus
.

Our algorithm is a standard bottom-up dynamic programming on a nice tree decomposition. We will describe how to compute the weight of an optimal solution; adapting our approach to return the solution itself is a standard task.

We introduce the table
\mathsf{Tab}[\cdot,\cdot]
indexed by nodes
t
of
T
and
states
.
Each state is a triple
(S,\iota,\tau)
, where
S\subseteq X_{t}
has at most
\ell
vertices,

\iota
is an injective function from
S
to
[\ell]
, and
\tau\in\mathsf{Types}
.
The value of
\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]
will encode the maximum weight of a subset
F_{t}
of
V(G_{t})
such that

1.

F_{t}\cap X_{t}=S
,

2.

\mathsf{type}((G[F_{t}],\iota))=\tau
.

Intuitively, we think of
S
as the intersection of a fixed optimal solution with
X_{t}
and we look for a maximum-weight induced
\ell
-boundaried subgraph of
G_{t}
with boundary
S
and type
\tau
.
If there is no subset
F_{t}
satisfying the conditions above, we will have
\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]=-\infty
.
Clearly, having computed all entries of
\mathsf{Tab}[\cdot,\cdot]
, the weight of the sought-for optimum solution is

\max\mathsf{Tab}[\mathrm{root}(T),(S,\iota,\tau)]\text{ over all feasible }S,\iota\text{ and }\tau\in\mathsf{Types}[\phi],

where by
\mathrm{root}(T)
we mean the root of
T
.

Let us now describe how we compute the entries
\mathsf{Tab}[t,\cdot]
for a node
t
of
T
.
The exact way depends on the type of
x
.

Case:
t
is a leaf node.

Note that in this case
G_{t}
has only one vertex, and thus the values of
\mathsf{Tab}[t,\cdot]
can be extracted from the information that is hard-coded in the algorithm.

Case:
t
is an introduce node.

Let
t^{\prime}
be the child of
t
in
T
, and let
v
be the unique vertex in
X_{t}\setminus X_{t^{\prime}}
.
Clearly if
v\notin S
, then
\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]=\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]
.

So suppose
v\in S
, let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{v\}
, and let
\iota^{\prime}=\iota|_{S^{\prime}}
.
Let
G_{S}
be the subgraph of
X_{t}
induced by
S
. As
|S|\leq\ell
, the value
\tau_{S}:=\mathsf{type}((G_{S},\iota))
is hard-coded in our algorithm.
Now we set

\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]=\max_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathsf{Tab}[t^{\prime},(S^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})],

where the maximum is taken over all
\tau^{\prime}
such that
\tau_{S}\oplus\tau^{\prime}=\tau
.

Case:
t
is a forget node.

Let
t^{\prime}
be the child of
t
in
\mathcal{T}
, and let
v
be the unique vertex in
X_{t^{\prime}}\setminus X_{t}
.
Intuitively, partial solutions at
t
correspond to partial solutions at
t^{\prime}
with the label of
v
(if any) forgotten.

Thus

\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]=\max\left(\mathsf{Tab}[t^{\prime},(S,\iota,\tau)],\max_{\iota^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}}\mathsf{Tab}[t^{\prime},(S\cup\{v\},\iota^{\prime},\tau^{\prime})]\right),

where in the second term we assume that
|S|<\ell
.
The maximum in the second term is computed over all pairs
\iota^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}
, such that
\iota^{\prime}|_{S}=\iota
and
\tau^{\prime}|_{\neg\iota^{\prime}(v)}=\tau
.

Case:
t
is a join node.

Let
t^{\prime},t^{\prime\prime}
be the children of
t
.
Thus

\mathsf{Tab}[t,(S,\iota,\tau)]=\max_{\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}}\mathsf{Tab}[t^{\prime},(S,\iota,\tau^{\prime})]+\mathsf{Tab}[t^{\prime\prime},(S,\iota,\tau^{\prime\prime})]-\mathfrak{w}(S),

where the maximum is taken over all
\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}
such that
\tau^{\prime}\oplus\tau^{\prime\prime}=\tau
. Note that we subtract the weight of
S
,
as it is counted both for
t^{\prime}
and for
t^{\prime\prime}
.

The correctness of the procedure above follows directly from the properties of tree decompositions and
Proposition

4.1
.

For each node
t
of
T
, the number of states is at most
|X_{t}|^{\ell}\cdot\ell!\cdot|\mathsf{Types}|
.
Each entry of
\mathsf{Tab}[\cdot,\cdot]
can be computed in time proportional to the number of states for each node (here we use that
k,r,\phi
are fixed).
As the number of nodes of
T
is polynomial in
|V(G)|
, we conclude that the running time is
f(k,r,\psi)\cdot|V(G)|^{\mathcal{O}(kr)}
for some function
f
depending on
k,r
, and
\psi
.
∎

4.3
Generalizations

In the first generalization we show that we can maximize the weight of some carefully chosen subset of the solution.
The formula
\psi
uses this set as a free variable.

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
k,r
and a
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
formula
\psi
with one free vertex-set variable, there exists a positive integer
f(k,r,\psi)
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be a graph given along with a tree decomposition of independence number at most
k
,
and let
\mathfrak{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
be a weight function.
Then in time
f(k,r,\psi)\cdot|V(G)|^{\mathcal{O}(kr)}
we can find sets
X\subseteq F\subseteq V(G)
, such that

1.

(G[F],X)\models\psi
,

2.

\chi(G[F])\leq r
,

3.

X
is of maximum possible weight.

Proof sketch.

The proof follows by a simple trick used by Gartland et al.
[
31
, Theorem 38]
.
We just sketch the argument here and refer the reader to
[
31
, Theorem 38]
for more details.

For a graph
G
and a subset
M\subseteq V(G)
, by
\breve{G}
we denote the
forked version of

(G,M)
, which is obtained as follows.
We start the construction with a copy of
G
.
For every vertex
v
of
G
we introduce to
\breve{G}^{M}
three vertices of degree 1, each adjacent only to
v
.
For every vertex
v
of
M
we introduce to
\breve{G}^{M}
a two-edge path, whose one endvertex
is identified with
v
.
If
G
is equipped with weights
\mathfrak{w}
, we set the weights
\breve{\mathfrak{w}}
of all vertices of
\breve{G}^{M}
to 0, with the exception that the value of
\mathfrak{w}(v)
for
v\in M
is transferred to the other endvertex of the two-edge path attached to
v
.

Note that if
F^{\prime}
is a forked version of some
(F,M)
, then
F
and
M
can be uniquely decoded from
F^{\prime}
.
Indeed,
F
is the subgraph of
F^{\prime}
induced by vertices of degree at least four, and vertices of
M
are those that are adjacent (in
F^{\prime}
) to a vertex of degree 2. Furthermore,
\chi(F^{\prime})\leq\max(\chi(F^{\prime}),1)
.

Moreover, observe that if
G
is given with a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}=(T,\{X_{t}\}_{t\in V(T)})
with independence number at most
k
,
then we can easily obtain a tree decomposition
\breve{\mathcal{T}}=(\breve{T},\{\breve{X}_{t}\}_{t\in V(\breve{T})})
of
\breve{G}^{V(G)}
with independence number at most
k
.
The idea is to call the algorithm from
Theorem

4.2
on
\breve{G}^{V(G)}
and the formula
\breve{\psi}
that for a graph
F^{\prime}
ensures that

•

if
F^{\prime}
is a forked version of some
(F,M)
, then
F^{\prime}\models\breve{\psi}
if and only if
F\models\psi
,

•

otherwise
F^{\prime}\not\models\breve{\psi}
.

Such a formula can be easily constructed in
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}

[
31
]
.
We observe that the solution found by the algorithm satisfies the conditions stated in the theorem; again, see
[
31
, Theorem 38]
for details.
∎

The idea introduced in the proof of
Theorem

4.3
allows us to annotate vertices.
In other words, our input is a graph and a constant number of subsets of vertices. The formula
\psi
uses these sets as free variables,
and we assume that the instance graph
G
is given along with a valuation of these free variables.

Theorem 4.4
.

For every
k,r,p
and a
\mathsf{CMSO}_{2}
formula
\psi
with
p+1
free vertex-set variables there exists a positive integer
f(k,r,p,\psi)
such that the following holds.
Let
G
be a graph given along with a tree decomposition of independence number at most
k
,
and let
\mathfrak{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{Q}_{+}
be a weight function.
Furthermore, let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p}\subseteq V(G)
be specified subsets of vertices of
G
.
Then in time
f(k,r,p,\psi)\cdot|V(G)|^{\mathcal{O}(kr)}
we can find sets
X\subseteq F\subseteq V(G)
, such that

1.

(G[F],X,A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{p})\models\psi
,

2.

\chi(G[F])\leq r
,

3.

X
is of maximum possible weight.

Theorem

4.4
can be proven in a way analogous to
Theorem

4.3
, but this time we distinguish the vertices from the sets
A_{i}
by the number of attached leaves. We skip the details, as they do not bring any new insight.

4.4
Algorithmic applications

Using the framework of
Theorem

4.2
we can solve several well-studied problems in graphs given with a tree decomposition of bounded independence number:

•

Max Weight Independent Set
,

•

Max Weight Induced Forest
, which is equivalent by complementation to
Min Weight Feedback Vertex Set
,

•

Max Weight Induced Bipartite Subgraph
, which is equivalent by complementation to
Min Weight Odd Cycle Transversal
,

•

Max Weight Induced Odd Cactus
, which is equivalent by complementation to
Min Weight Even Cycle Transversal
,

•

Max Weight Induced Planar Subgraph
, which is equivalent to
Planarization
.

Using the extension from
Theorem

4.3
we can solve some other problems, for example:

•

find the maximum number of pairwise independent induced cycles.

Note that the difference is that we maximize the
number
of cycles, and not their
total size
(or weight), as we could do using
Theorem

4.2
.
On other other hand, the number of induced cycles in a graph might be very large, so this problem cannot be solved using
Theorem

2.2
(or
Theorem

3.10
).
However, we can solve our problem by calling
Theorem

4.3
with the following parameters:

•

d=3
,

•

\psi
is the formula saying that
G[F]
is 2-regular (i.e., each component of
G[F]
is a cycle) and each vertex from
X
must be in a distinct connected component of
G[F]
, and

•

the weight of each vertex is 1.

Finally, the extension from
Theorem

4.4
allows us to solve problems where we allow annotations on vertices.
An example of such a problem is the following one:

•

given a graph, whose every vertex is equipped with a subset of
[r]
(called a
list
),
find a maximum induced subgraph that admits a proper
r
-coloring respecting the lists.

Indeed, it is sufficient to call
Theorem

4.4
, where each set
A_{i}
contains vertices with a given list (note that there are at most
2^{r}
, i.e., a constant number of distinct lists), and
\psi
is a formula similar to (
1
), but also checking if the colors assigned to vertices respect the lists. Note that the last problem is a generalization of
List
-
r
-
Coloring
(for constant
r
).

Furthermore, let us recall that if the input graph is not equipped with a tree decomposition with bounded independence number, we can again invoke a result from
[
21
]
to infer that all of the above problems are polynomial-time solvable in any class of graphs in which the tree-independence number is bounded by a constant.

In conclusion, let us point out that in
Theorems

4.2
,
4.3
and
4.4
we need to assume that the chromatic number
d
of the solution is constant. Indeed, if the number of colors is a part of the input, already the (
List-
)
Coloring
problem becomes
NP
-hard in graphs of bounded tree-independence number.

Theorem 4.5
.

The following problems are
NP
-hard:

1.

List-Coloring
in graphs of tree-independence number 1,

2.

Coloring
in graphs of tree-independence number 2.

Proof.

The first statement follows from the result of Golovach and Paulusma
[
32
]
, who showed that
List-Coloring
is
NP
-hard in complete split graphs, i.e., graphs whose vertex set can be partitioned into an independent set and a clique, and all edges between these two sets are present. Complete split graphs are in particular chordal
and thus of tree-independence number
1
.

To show the second statement, it suffices to observe that the
Coloring
problem is
NP
-complete in the class of circular-arc graphs, as shown by Garey et al.
[
30
]
.
Furthermore, as we explain next, circular-arc graphs have tree-independence number at most two.
Every circular-arc graph
G
is the intersection graph of a family
\mathcal{F}
of connected subgraphs of a graph
H
such that
H
is a cycle.
Fix a vertex
v\in V(H)
and let
G_{\bar{v}}
denote the intersection graph of the graphs in
\mathcal{F}_{\bar{v}}=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:v\not\in V(F)\}
.
Then
G_{\bar{v}}
is a chordal graph (in fact, an interval graph) and hence admits a tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
in which each bag is a clique.
Finally, denoting by
K
the set of vertices of
G
corresponding to graphs in
\mathcal{F}
containing
v
, a tree decomposition of
G
with independence number at most
2
can be obtained from the tree decomposition
\mathcal{T}
of
G_{\bar{v}}
by adding the vertices of the clique
K
to every bag.
∎

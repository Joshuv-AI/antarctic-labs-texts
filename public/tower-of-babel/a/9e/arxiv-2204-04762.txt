Rockafellian Relaxation and Stochastic Optimization under Perturbations
Johannes O. Royset,    Louis L. Chen,    Eric Eckstrand

Abstract




Abstract
.
In practice, optimization models are often prone to unavoidable inaccuracies due to dubious assumptions and corrupted data. Traditionally, this placed special emphasis on risk-based and robust formulations, and their focus on “conservative” decisions. We develop, in contrast, an “optimistic” framework based on Rockafellian relaxations in which optimization is conducted not only over the original decision space but also jointly with a choice of model perturbation. The framework enables us to address challenging problems with ambiguous probability distributions from the areas of two-stage stochastic optimization without relatively complete recourse, probability functions lacking continuity properties, expectation constraints, and outlier analysis. We are also able to circumvent the fundamental difficulty in stochastic optimization that convergence of distributions fails to guarantee convergence of expectations. The framework centers on the novel concepts of exact and limit-exact Rockafellians, with interpretations of “negative” regularization emerging in certain settings. We illustrate the role of Phi-divergence, examine rates of convergence under changing distributions, and explore extensions to first-order optimality conditions. The main development is free of assumptions about convexity, smoothness, and even continuity of objective functions. Numerical results in the setting of computer vision and text analytics with label noise illustrate the framework.

1
Introduction

Uncertain parameter values in stochastic optimization are modeled using probability distributions, but these can themselves be unsettled due to a lack of data or incorrect information. Even the slightest error in the prescribed distribution could make a truly optimal solution look poor. This vulnerability also underscores the threat of adversarially-caused corruption, which could be strategically crafted in advance to mislead naive decision-makers away from good solutions. In this work, we propose a framework that supports decision-making robust to modeling errors, adversarial perturbations, and other deviations from an actual problem. Decision-makers are oftentimes unaware of the “true” form to a problem so that they cannot detect the presence of deviations, much less determine how such deviations can be corrected. The best that could be hoped for is that their decisions made based on solving slightly deviated problems are also only slightly deviated from those of the unknown actual problem. We aim to arm the decision-maker with a framework that exhibits this kind of continuity properties in the setting of expectation functions.

Risk-based and distributionally robust approaches to minimizing expectation functions furnish performance guarantees across a collection of possible distributions, among which we hope to find the actual one. Thus, it replaces expectation functions by higher, more conservative approximations. It is easily seen via Fatou’s lemma that in some applications the move to “high” approximations is misguided: A weakly converging sequence of nonnegative random variables can have expectations much above that of the limiting random variable. Consequently, even a nearly correct distribution may already produce a value too high. In this paper, we address such situations by constructing “low” approximations via
Rockafellian relaxations
and thereby consider best-case instead of worst-case distributions. The focus on relaxation over restriction of a problem allows us to keep in play decisions that
might
be good under the actual probability distribution.

Undoubtedly, problem relaxations lead to decisions that could be less conservative but may also uncover previously hidden possibilities. This is especially the case for tightly constrained problems, for problems with induced constraints such as in the absence of relatively complete recourse, for problems with discontinuous functions expressing quantiles and probabilities, and for statistical problems with outliers and other abnormalities in the training data. Through examples, we show that even in the case of finite probability spaces an arbitrarily accurate estimate of the actual distribution can cause large errors in optimal solutions. We develop a framework for addressing such situations and examine convergence as distributions become more accurate. In some cases, the framework has the interpretation of inducing a negative regularization to a problem.

In practice, the actual probability distribution

(failure of convergence of solutions).
For

The examples illustrate how a small change to a probability distribution in a problem may cause the values of expectation functions to shift up in a disproportional manner. Since the shift is up, it is futile to attempt to address the situations by passing to a “robust” formulation involving worst-case distributions as this would only cause expectation functions to shift even higher. As an alternative, we aim to anticipate the shift from the actual but unknown distribution

P^{\nu}
.

3
Rockafellian Relaxation

We now abstract the elements of the previous section and formalize the framework. For
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we consider the
actual problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x).

(3.1)

However,
\varphi
is unavailable for the decision-maker due to some unsettled or corrupted quantity. To handle this situation we proceed in two steps. First, we design a perturbation scheme for the actual problem and thus place it within a family of problems that are in some sense better positioned to absorb any deviation from the actual problem. Second, we analyze the effect of deviations caused by the unsettled or corrupted quantities. We accomplish the first step by
designing
a suitable Rockafellian in anticipation of the deviations of the second step. Generally, a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is referred to as a
Rockafellian
for (
3.1
) if

f(0,x)=\varphi(x)~~~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The first argument of
f
, usually denoted by
u
, specifies a perturbation of the actual problem
2

2

[

, Section 5.1]
for a slightly broader definition.
.

We adopt the following notation and terminology: For
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varepsilon\in[0,\infty)
, we define
\inf_{x}\psi(x)=\inf\{\psi(x)~|~x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
,
\dom\psi=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~\psi(x)<\infty\}
, and
\varepsilon\mbox{-}\hskip-1.70709pt\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\psi(x)=\{\bar{x}\in\dom\psi~|~\psi(\bar{x})\leq\inf_{x}\psi(x)+\varepsilon\}
, with
0\mbox{-}\hskip-1.70709pt\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\psi(x)
simply being written as
\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\psi(x)
. For any set
C

indicator function
is given as
\iota_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and
\iota_{C}(x)=\infty
otherwise. We recall that
\psi
is
proper

\dom\psi\neq\emptyset
and
\psi(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

lower semicontinuous
(lsc) if, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\psi(x^{\nu})\geq\psi(x)
whenever
x^{\nu}\to x
. The

\psi^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of

is given by
\psi^{*}(y)=\sup_{x}\langle x,y\rangle-\psi(x)
. For
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we let the
min-value function
be given by

{\cal V}(u)=\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x}f(u,x).

If
f
is a Rockafellian for the actual problem (

), then
{\cal V}(0)
specifies its minimum value.

3.1
Exactness

While the actual problem (
3.1
) can be associated with many Rockafellians, we seek to design one that exhibits an exactness property, which is related to conditions for strong duality
[
51
, Chapter 11]
.

3.1 Definition

(exact Rockafellians).

A Rockafellian
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
for (
3.1
) is
exact, supported by

\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, if

\mathcal{V}(u)\geq\mathcal{V}(0)+\langle\bar{y},u\rangle~~~\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

If the inequality holds strictly for all
u\neq 0
, then
f
is
strictly exact
, supported by
\bar{y}
.

A trivial design (but still useful as seen in the next section) is to let
f(u,x)=\infty
for
u\neq 0
and
f(0,x)=\varphi(x)
, which indeed produces an exact Rockafellian supported by any
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. A characterization of exactness is available via conjugacy. All the proofs in this section appear in the appendix.

3.2 Proposition

(exactness from conjugacy).

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a Rockafellian for (
3.1
). Then
f
is exact, supported by
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, if and only if
\mathcal{V}(0)=-\mathcal{V}^{*}(\bar{y})
.

Since
-\mathcal{V}^{*}(\bar{y})=\inf_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle
and the right-hand side involves joint optimization over
u
and
x
, one has
{\cal V}(0)\geq-\mathcal{V}^{*}(\bar{y})
always and the
relaxed problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle

(3.2)

is indeed a relaxation of the actual problem (
3.1
). This holds for any
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. We refer to the construction of (
3.2
) as
Rockafellian relaxation
. As seen in Proposition
3.2
, exactness amounts to tightness in this relaxation.

Another characterization of exactness emphasizes relations between minimizers.

3.3 Proposition

(minimizer characterization of exactness).
Suppose
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a Rockafellian for the problem (
3.1
) of minimizing
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
.

If
f
is exact, supported by
\bar{y}
, then

x^{\star}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\varphi(x)~~\Longrightarrow~~(0,x^{\star})\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle.

(3.3)

When
\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\varphi(x)\neq\emptyset
, (
3.3
) conversely implies exactness.

If
f
is strictly exact, supported by
\bar{y}
, then

(u^{\star},x^{\star})\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle~~\Longrightarrow~~u^{\star}=0,~x^{\star}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\varphi(x).

(3.4)

When
\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle\neq\emptyset
and
\argmin_{x}f(u,x)\neq\emptyset
for all
u\in\dom{\cal V}
, then (
3.4
) conversely implies strict exactness.

Hence, exactness of a Rockafellian

produces an optimality condition for the actual problem (
3.1
):

x^{\star}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\varphi(x)~~\Longrightarrow~~\exists u^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\mbox{ such that }\,(u^{\star},x^{\star})\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle\bar{y},u\rangle.

(3.5)

The optimality condition helps justify the passing from (
3.1
) to (
3.2
) even without strict exactness. Further discussion of optimality conditions is deferred to Section
5
.

\mathcal{V}
is convex with
\bar{y}
as a subgradient at
0
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is also strictly convex, then the Rockafellian is strictly exact, supported by
\bar{y}
.

Since an inf-projection of a convex Rockafellian is convex
[
59
, Proposition 1.21]
, it is immediately clear that the convexity requirement in the corollary holds for such Rockafellians. In this setting, the dual problem of maximizing
\inf_{u,x}f(u,x)-\langle y,u\rangle
over
y
furnishes
\bar{y}

[
51
, Theorem 11.39]
.

3.5 Proposition

(exactness from calmness).

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a Rockafellian for (
3.1
). Let
\|\cdot\|
be an arbitrary norm on
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For any
\theta\in[0,\infty)
, the function given by
f_{\theta}(u,x)=f(u,x)+\theta\|u\|

is also a Rockafellian for (
3.1
). If there is
\bar{\theta}\in[0,\infty)
such that

{\cal V}(u)\geq{\cal V}(0)-\bar{\theta}\|u\|~~\forall u\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(3.6)

then
f_{\theta}
is exact, supported by
\bar{y}=0
as long as
\theta\geq\bar{\theta}
. The exactness is strict if
\theta>\bar{\theta}
and
{\cal V}(0)\in\mathbb{R}
.

The requirement (
3.6
) holds for sufficiently large
\bar{\theta}
if the min-value function
{\cal V}
is calm from below at
0

[
51
, Equation 8(12)]
and it is also minorized by some affine function. Calmness in the context of right-hand side perturbations of constraint systems is discussed in
[
11
]
.

3.2
Limiting Exactness

The motivation for designing a Rockafellian
f
is to mitigate the effect of any unsettled or corrupted quantities in the actual problem (
3.1
). We formalize the setting by considering the approximating functions
f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. For example, the actual problem and its Rockafellian
f
might be defined in terms of the “true” distribution
P
, while the approximating function
f^{\nu}
involves the corrupted distribution
P^{\nu}
. The approximating functions might also reflect other changes from the Rockafellian, possibly motivated by computational considerations.

These functions lead to the
approximating problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{\nu}(u,x)-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle,

(3.7)

where
y^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
serves as a substitute for some vector
\bar{y}
that in turn supports exactness. In contrast to the actual problem (
3.1
) and a relaxed problem (
3.2
), which are conceptual in the sense that they involve unsettled quantities, an approximating problem is assumed accessible to the decision-maker and eventually will be solved numerically. Under exactness, there is equivalence between the actual problem and a relaxed problem. We next turn to the relationship between the chosen relaxed problem (
3.2
) and its approximations (
3.7
) using the notion of epi-convergence
3
3

3

For background information about epi-convergence; see for example
[
59
, Chapter 4]
.
.

The functions
\psi^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are said to
epi-converge
to
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
when

\displaystyle\forall x^{\nu}\to x,~\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\psi^{\nu}(x^{\nu})\geq\psi(x)

(3.8)

\displaystyle\forall x,~\exists x^{\nu}\to x\mbox{ with }\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\psi^{\nu}(x^{\nu})\leq\psi(x).

(3.9)

In the context of Rockafellians, this leads to the following definition.

3.6 Definition

(limit-exact Rockafellians).

For the problem (
3.1
), the functions
\{f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are
limit-exact Rockafellians
if they epi-converge to an exact Rockafellian
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
of the problem.

The functions
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are
strictly limit-exact Rockafellians
if
f
is strictly exact. The (strict) limiting exactness is said to be supported by
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
when
f
is (strictly) exact, supported by
\bar{y}
.

In the presence of limit-exact Rockafellians, we are on solid ground for passing from the actual problem (
3.1
) to the approximating problem (
3.7
); the latter provides arbitrarily accurate solutions of the former as seen by the following fact.

3.7 Theorem

(convergence under limiting exactness)
.
For the problem (
3.1
) of minimizing
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, suppose that
\dom\varphi\neq\emptyset
and that
\{f^{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians, supported by
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
y^{\nu}\to\bar{y}
,
\varepsilon^{\nu}{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\,\searrow\,$}}0
, and

(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\in\varepsilon^{\nu}\hskip-0.56917pt\mbox{-}\hskip-1.70709pt\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}\big\{f^{\nu}(u,x)-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle\big\}.

Then, every cluster point
(\hat{u},\hat{x})
of
\{(u^{\nu},x^{\nu}),\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfies
\hat{x}\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{x}\varphi(x)
.

If strict limiting exactness is replaced by limiting exactness, then
(\hat{u},\hat{x})
satisfies the necessary optimality condition (
3.5
) with
f:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
being furnished by the limit of
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
.

The theorem establishes that the approximating problems (
3.7
), when defined by strictly limit-exact Rockafellians, are in the limit equivalent to the actual problem (
3.1
). They exhibit the desirable continuity property. An approximating problem becomes an accessible and viable alternative to solve in lieu of the unknown actual problem. Still, the approximating problems could be challenging computationally as discussed in Sections 5 and 6.

4
Applications to Distributional Perturbations

The wide framework of Section
3
offers a pathway to analyzing stochastic optimization problems under changes to underlying probability distributions. This section illustrates some possibilities in the context of the “classical” stochastic optimization problem (
2.1
) with perturbations of the probability vector
p
. We supplement the treatment by also considering changes to the support, expectation constraints, and more general distributions.

4.1
Minimization of Expectations

In this subsection, we consider the actual problem (
2.1
) under the assumption that
p\in\Delta
and each
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is proper and lsc,
i=0,1,\dots,s
. As illustrated in Section
2
, a change to
p
could have outsized effects on solutions. Even if
p^{\nu}\in\Delta
is near
p
, we cannot automatically assume that minimizers of the alternative problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}f_{i}(x)

(4.1)

would be close to those of (
2.1
). It turns out that an approximating problem defined via a Rockafellian is better behaved.

We design the Rockafellian
f:\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

f(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\iota_{\{0\}^{s}}(u).

(4.2)

For
p^{\nu}\in\Delta
and
\theta^{\nu}\in[0,\infty)
, let

f^{\nu}(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u),

(4.3)

which, for any
y^{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, defines an approximating problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{s},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u)-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle.

(4.4)

In the following, we provide justifications for considering (
4.4
) as a substitute for the actual problem (
2.1
). These approximating problems turn out to have better properties than (
4.1
), which can be sensitive to changes in the probability vector.

4.1 Proposition

(exactness in expectation minimization).

In the setting of this subsection, with
p\in\Delta
and proper lsc
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=0,1,\dots,s
, the Rockafellian
f
in (
4.2
) is strictly exact, supported by any
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
. If
\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
and
\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2}\to 0
with
p^{\nu}\in\Delta
, then the functions
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
in (
4.3
) are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians with
f
as their limit.

Proof.
The strict exactness follows immediately from Definition
3.1
. The second claim is a special case of Theorem
4.5
below.

The proposition recommends that
\theta^{\nu}
should grow slower than
\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2}
vanishes. The rate by which
p^{\nu}
tends to
p
depends on the setting. In a conceptual study, rate details may not be needed. In algorithmic developments, however, it becomes more critical as the approximating problem (
4.4
) depends on
\theta^{\nu}
. One would like to know the magnitude of any corruption or modeling error, but the exact value can, importantly, remain unknown. Approximating probability vectors stemming from empirical distributions based on
\nu
sample points are supported by the following fact, which stipulates that
\theta^{\nu}
should grow slightly slower than
\nu
, but this is just one possibility.

4.2 Example

(convergence of empirical distributions).

Suppose that
\mathbf{p}^{\nu}
is the random vector representing the empirical distribution obtained by sampling independently
\nu
times according to
p\in\Delta
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
, one has
\nu^{1/2-\varepsilon}\|\mathbf{p}^{\nu}-p\|_{2}\to 0
almost surely.

Detail.
This fact holds because
p
can be viewed as the probability mass function of a random variable support on
\{1,2,\dots,s\}
. Sampling
\nu
times according to
p
produces another
s
-dimensional vector specifying the frequency of the different outcomes. We view this vector as a random vector and denote it by
{\bf p}^{\nu}
. Recall that
\int_{1}^{\infty}\exp(-2t^{\alpha})dt<\infty
if
\alpha>0
and
\infty
if
\alpha=0
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2)
,
F:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
be the distribution function corresponding to
p
, and
\mathbf{F}^{\nu}
be the (random) distribution function corresponding to
\mathbf{p}^{\nu}
. By the (univariate) Dvoretzky-Kiefer-Wolfowitz inequality,

{\mathop{\rm prob}\nolimits}\left(\mathop{\rm sup}\nolimits_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big|\mathbf{F}^{\nu}(t)-F(t)\big|>\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\nu}}\right)\leq 2\exp(-2\gamma^{2})\;\;\;\forall\gamma\in\mathbb{R}.

Setting
\gamma_{\nu}=\nu^{\varepsilon}
, we conclude that
\sum_{\nu=1}^{\infty}2\exp(-2\gamma_{\nu}^{2})<\infty
by the integral test for series. The Borel-Cantelli lemma yields then, with probability one, that
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|\mathbf{F}^{\nu}(t)-F(t)|\leq\nu^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)}
holds for all but finitely many
\nu
. In particular, for any
i
,
|\mathbf{p}_{i}^{\nu}-p_{i}|\leq 2\nu^{-(1/2-\varepsilon)}
holds for all but finitely many
\nu
, from which we reach the conclusion.

The minimization over
u
in the approximating problem (
4.4
) can largely be carried out “explicitly,” which results in a reformulation of the approximating problem with an interpretation as a regularized version of the alternative problem (
4.1
). Specifically, for fixed
x\in\cap_{i=1}^{s}\dom f_{i}
, let
F(x)=(f_{1}(x),\dots,f_{s}(x))
. Then, the minimization over
u
in (
4.4
) amounts to

\displaystyle\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{s}}\Big\{f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u)-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle\Big\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}f_{i}(x)-\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{s}}\Big\{\big\langle y^{\nu}-F(x),u\big\rangle-{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u)\Big\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}f_{i}(x)-r^{\nu}(x),

where

r^{\nu}(x)=\min_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{s}}\Big\{\max_{i=1,\dots,s}w_{i}-\langle p^{\nu},w\rangle+\frac{1}{2\theta^{\nu}}\big\|y^{\nu}-F(x)-w\big\|_{2}^{2}\Big\}.

The transition from the sup-expression to
r^{\nu}(x)
is achieved by recognizing that the former is the conjugate of the function
u\mapsto{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u)
, evaluated at a certain point. This in turn is expressed by the conjugates of
{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
and
\iota_{\Delta}
; see
[
59
, Example 5.29]
as well as Equation 11(3) and Theorem 11.23(a) in
[
51
]
.

At least when
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
are real-valued, the derivation establishes that the approximating problem (
4.4
) is equivalently stated as

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}f_{i}(x)-r^{\nu}(x),

where the role of
r^{\nu}
as a regularizer is now apparent when comparing to the alternative problem (
4.1
). Since
r^{\nu}(x)\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the additional term amounts to a “negative regularization” that brings down the potentially too high values of the objective function under
p^{\nu}
.

The value
r^{\nu}(x)
equals a Moreau envelope of the real-valued convex function
w\mapsto\max_{i=1,\dots,s}w_{i}-\langle p^{\nu},w\rangle
evaluated at the point
y^{\nu}-F(x)
. Thus, by
[
51
, Theorem 2.26]
,
r^{\nu}
is continuously differentiable with
\nabla r^{\nu}(x)=-\nabla F(x)^{\top}(y^{\nu}-F(x)-\hat{w}(x))/\theta^{\nu}
, where
\hat{w}(x)
is the unique minimizer in the problem defining
r^{\nu}(x)
, provided that
f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
are continuously differentiable. Albeit our motivation stems from problems without such differentiability, this derivation helps us understand the characteristics of the regularizer.

Further justification for the passing from the actual problem (
2.1
) to the approximating problem (
4.4
) is furnished by the next result on rate of convergence as
p^{\nu}
tends to
p
. We adopt the notation
\mathbb{B}(\bar{x},\rho)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~\|x-\bar{x}\|_{2}\leq\rho\}
. The
point-to-set distance

if

and

otherwise.

4.3 Theorem

(rates in expectation minimization).

p,p^{\nu}\in\Delta

In terms of these quantities, (

\dist_{*}\big((u^{\nu},x^{\nu}),~\delta\mbox{-}\hskip-1.70709pt\mathop{\rm argmin}\nolimits_{u,x}f(u,x)\big)\leq d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\epi f,\epi\tilde{f}^{\nu})

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{\tilde{u},\tilde{x}}\big\{\tilde{f}^{\nu}(\tilde{u},\tilde{x})\,\big|\,\|\tilde{u}-u\|_{2}\leq\eta_{1}^{\nu},\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{2}\leq\eta_{1}^{\nu}\big\}\leq\tilde{f}^{\nu}(p-p^{\nu},x)

\displaystyle~~~~=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu})f_{i}(x)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2}-\langle y^{\nu},p-p^{\nu}\rangle

. This results in the following bound:

\displaystyle\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}(p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}-u_{i})f_{i}(x)-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I_{0}}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\kappa,\gamma^{\nu}\}\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}|p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}-u_{i}|+\kappa\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I_{0}}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\eta_{2}^{\nu}=\sqrt{s}\big(\max\{\kappa,\gamma^{\nu}\}+\kappa\big)\big(\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}+\beta/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}}\big).

We leverage this bound to compute

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{\tilde{u},\tilde{x}}\big\{f(\tilde{u},\tilde{x})~\big|~\|\tilde{u}-u\|_{2}\leq\beta/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}},\|\tilde{x}-x\|_{2}\leq\beta/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}}\big\}\leq f(0,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}^{\nu}(u,x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)-{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{f}^{\nu}(u,x)+\eta_{2}^{\nu}+\beta\|y^{\nu}\|_{2}/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}}.

The Kenmochi condition 6.58 in
[
59
]
then produces the bound

d\hat{\kern-1.49994ptl}_{\rho}(\epi f,\epi\tilde{f}^{\nu})\leq\max\big\{\eta_{1}^{\nu},~\beta/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}},~\eta_{2}^{\nu}+\beta\|y^{\nu}\|_{2}/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}}\big\}.

The conclusion follows after slightly relaxing the upper bound to facilitate a simple expression and noting that
\gamma^{\nu}\leq\beta\sqrt{3/(2\alpha)}
.

The trade-offs in the rate expression in the theorem could be addressed, for example, by setting

\theta^{\nu}=\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{-4/3}
. Then,
1/\sqrt{\theta^{\nu}}
and
\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2}
decay at the same rate as
\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2/3}
. We conclude that the error, in the sense of the theorem, by passing from the actual problem (
2.1
) to the approximating problem (
4.4
) vanishes at the rate
\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2/3}
. This holds under mild assumptions;
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
could be nonconvex, nonsmooth, discontinuous, and extended real-valued. In comparison, the alternative problem (
4.1
) may exhibit large errors in the absence of additional assumptions.

4.2
Minimization of Expectations: Refinements

We now discuss three refinements of the previous subsection: (A) approximating functions
f^{\nu}
based on
\Phi
-divergence instead of
\|\cdot\|_{2}^{2}
, (B) perturbation of probability vector
and
corresponding support, and (C) strictly limit-exact Rockafellians without relying on the rate of decay of
\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}
.

Refinement A.
While retaining the Rockafellian
f
from Subsection
4.1
, we consider new approximating functions
f^{\nu}
. We recall that the
\Phi
-divergence of
q\in\Delta
from
\bar{q}\in\Delta
is given by

d_{\Phi}(q|\bar{q})=\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}\bar{q}_{i}\Phi(q_{i}/\bar{q}_{i}),

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex function, real-valued on
[0,\infty)
, with a unique minimizer at
1
and a minimum value of
0
. (While
\Phi
-divergence can be defined for slightly broader classes of convex functions
\Phi
, we limit the scope to such functions here.) In the expression for
\Phi
-divergence, we adopt the usual conventions:
0\cdot\Phi(0/0)=0
, and, for
\beta\in(0,\infty)
,
0\cdot\Phi(\beta/0)=\beta\mathop{\rm lim}\nolimits_{\alpha\to\infty}\Phi(\alpha)/\alpha
. The requirements on
\Phi
are satisfied by the commonly used Kullback-Leibler divergence, Burg-entropy,
J
-divergence,
\chi^{2}
-distance, modified
\chi^{2}
-distance, variational distance, and Hellinger distance.

As an alternative to the path in Subsection
4.1
, we now define

f^{\nu}(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p^{\nu}_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}+u|p^{\nu})+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u).

(4.7)

The resulting approximating problem is also considered in
[
20
]
for a study of out-of-sample performance, but there under the assumptions of strict convexity and twice smoothness of each
f_{i}
. We require only proper lsc functions to confirm that the new approximating functions are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians.

4.4 Theorem

(exactness in expectation minimization;
\Phi
-divergence).

In the setting of Subsection
4.1
with
p,p^{\nu}\in\Delta
and proper lsc
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=0,1,\dots,s
, consider
f
from (
4.2
) and
f^{\nu}
from (
4.7
). The functions
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians with
f
as their limit provided that
\theta^{\nu}\to\infty
and
\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}\to 0
.

Proof.
We show that
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
via the conditions (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).

Let
(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\to(u,x)
. First, suppose that
u\neq 0
. Then,
f(u,x)=\infty
. We consider two cases. (i) Suppose that
p+u\not\in\Delta
. Then,
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\not\in\Delta
for sufficiently large
\nu
because
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\to p+u
. Thus,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})=\infty
. (ii) Suppose that
p+u\in\Delta
. We assume without loss of generality that
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\in\Delta
for all
\nu
because
f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})=\infty
when
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\not\in\Delta
. Next, there is
i^{*}\in\{1,\dots,s\}
such that
p_{i^{*}}>0
and
u_{i^{*}}\neq 0
. This claim holds by the following argument:

For the sake of contradiction, suppose that
u_{i}=0
for all
i
with
p_{i}>0
. Since
p+u\in\Delta
, one has

\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}+u_{i}=\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i|p_{i}>0}p_{i}+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i|p_{i}=0}u_{i}=1.

This implies that
\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i|p_{i}=0}u_{i}=0
. All summands in this sum cannot be zero because
u\neq 0
. In turn, this means that there is a negative summand, say
j
, which makes
p_{j}+u_{j}=0+u_{j}<0
. However, this contradicts the fact that
p+u\in\Delta
.

Returning to the main argument, the claim confirms that
(p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}+u_{i^{*}}^{\nu})/p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}\to 1+u_{i^{*}}/p_{i^{*}}\neq 1
. The continuity of
\Phi
on
(0,\infty)
, the convexity of
\Phi
, and the unique minimizer of
\Phi
at 1 with minimum value of 0 imply that

\Phi\Big(\frac{p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}+u_{i^{*}}^{\nu}}{p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}}\Big)\to\beta>0,

where
\beta=\Phi(1+u_{i^{*}}/p_{i^{*}})
if
1+u_{i^{*}}/p_{i^{*}}>0
and
\beta=\lim_{\alpha{\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\,\searrow\,$}}0}\Phi(\alpha)
otherwise. Thus,

\theta^{\nu}p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}\Phi\Big(\frac{p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}+u_{i^{*}}^{\nu}}{p_{i^{*}}^{\nu}}\Big)\to\infty.

Since
\Phi(\alpha)\geq 0
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}|p^{\nu})\to\infty
. This fact together with the assumption about proper lsc functions
f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{s}
imply that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f_{0}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\big((p^{\nu}_{i}+u_{i}^{\nu})f_{i}(x^{\nu})\big)+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\big(\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}|p^{\nu})\big)+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\big(\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}|p^{\nu})\big)=\infty.

Second, suppose that
u=0
. Similar to above, one obtains

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\big(\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}|p^{\nu})\big)

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)=f(0,x).

We have shown that the liminf-condition (
3.8
) holds for
f^{\nu}
and
f
.

Next, we turn to the limsup-condition (
3.9
). Let
(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. If
u\neq 0
, then
f(u,x)=\infty
and the limsup-condition holds trivially. Thus, we concentrate on the case
u=0
. Set
x^{\nu}=x
and
u^{\nu}=p-p^{\nu}
. Then,

f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})=f^{\nu}(p-p^{\nu},x)=f(0,x)+\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p|p^{\nu}).

Let
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
be arbitrary. We consider two cases: (i) Suppose that
p_{i}>0
. Then, for sufficiently large
\nu
,
p_{i}^{\nu}>0
. Since
\Phi
is convex and real-valued in a neighborhood of 1 and
\Phi(1)=0
, there exist
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
\Phi(\alpha)\leq\delta|\alpha-1|
for all
\alpha\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
. This implies that, for sufficiently large
\nu
, one has

p_{i}^{\nu}\Phi\Big(\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}^{\nu}}\Big)\leq p_{i}^{\nu}\delta\Big|\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}^{\nu}}-1\Big|=\delta|p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}|.

(ii) Suppose that
p_{i}=0
. If
p_{i}^{\nu}>0
, then
p_{i}^{\nu}\Phi(p_{i}/p_{i}^{\nu})=p_{i}^{\nu}\Phi(0)=\Phi(0)|p_{i}-p_{i}^{\nu}|
. If
p_{i}^{\nu}=0
, then, by convention,
p_{i}^{\nu}\Phi(p_{i}/p_{i}^{\nu})=0\cdot\Phi(0/0)=0
. Since
i
is arbitrary and
\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}\to 0
, we conclude that
\theta^{\nu}d_{\Phi}(p^{\nu}|p)\to 0
. This means that
\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq f(u,x)
. We have established that
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
.

Refinement B.
Subsection
4.1
deals with changes to the probability vector
p
in the actual problem (
2.1
). Now, we consider the possibility that the support may also be unsettled. Changes to a support underpin adversarial training of neural nets
[
32
]
, but with a focus on conservativeness. We consider relaxations.

For proper lsc functions
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, support
\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, and corresponding probability vector
p=(p_{1},\dots,p_{s})\in\Delta
, we consider the actual problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}g(\xi_{i},x).

(4.8)

The problem furnishes additional structure to (
2.1
), where one has
f_{i}(x)=g(\xi_{i},x)
. Mimicking the development in Subsection
4.1
, we design the Rockafellian
f:\mathbb{R}^{s+sm}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
given by

f\big((u,v),x\big)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})g(\xi_{i}+v_{i},x)+\iota_{\{0\}^{s}}(u)+\iota_{\{0\}^{sm}}(v).

(4.9)

For
p^{\nu}=(p_{1}^{\nu},\dots,p_{s}^{\nu})\in\Delta
,
\xi^{\nu}=(\xi_{1}^{\nu},\dots,\xi_{s}^{\nu})\in\mathbb{R}^{sm}
, and
\theta^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu}\in[0,\infty)
, we define the approximating functions by

f^{\nu}\big((u,v),x\big)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})g(\xi_{i}^{\nu}+v_{i},x)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\lambda^{\nu}\|v\|_{2}^{2}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u).

(4.10)

These approximating functions turn out to be strictly limit-exact Rockafellians.

4.5 Theorem

(exactness in expectation minimization; support uncertainty).

Suppose that
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
are proper lsc functions and
p,p^{\nu}\in\Delta
. For the actual problem (
4.8
), the Rockafellian
f
in (
4.9
) is strictly exact, supported by any
\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{s+sm}
.

If
\theta^{\nu},\lambda^{\nu}\to\infty
,
\theta^{\nu}\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}^{2}\to 0
, and
\lambda^{\nu}\|\xi^{\nu}-\xi\|_{2}^{2}\to 0
, where
\xi=(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{s})\in\mathbb{R}^{sm}
, then the functions
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
in (
4.10
) are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians with
f
as their limit.

Proof.
The claim about strict exactness holds trivially. We establish that
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
using (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Let
(u^{\nu},v^{\nu},x^{\nu})\to(u,v,x)
. Then,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f_{0}(x^{\nu})\geq f(x)>-\infty
,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\iota_{\{0\}^{s}}(u)
,
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\lambda^{\nu}\|v^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\iota_{\{0\}^{sm}}(v)
, and
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu})\geq\iota_{\Delta}(p+u)
because
\Delta
is closed. Since
f^{\nu}((u^{\nu},v^{\nu}),x^{\nu})=\infty
if
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\not\in\Delta
, we assume without loss of generality that
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\in\Delta
for all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. This in turn implies that

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})g(\xi_{i}^{\nu}+v_{i}^{\nu},x^{\nu})\geq\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})g(\xi_{i}^{\nu}+v_{i}^{\nu},x^{\nu})\geq\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})g(\xi_{i}+v_{i},x).

We combine these facts to conclude that the liminf-condition (
3.8
) holds. For the limsup-condition (
3.9
), fix
(u,v,x)
and construct
x^{\nu}=x
,
u^{\nu}=p-p^{\nu}
, and
v^{\nu}=\xi-\xi^{\nu}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
u=0
and
v=0
because
f((u,v),x)=\infty
otherwise. Since
p\in\Delta
,
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\in\Delta
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}\big((u^{\nu},v^{\nu}),x^{\nu}\big)

\displaystyle\leq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})g(\xi_{i}^{\nu}+v_{i}^{\nu},x)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\lambda^{\nu}\|v^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}g(\xi_{i},x)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|p-p^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\lambda^{\nu}\|\xi-\xi^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}g(\xi_{i},x)=f\big((u,v),x\big).

We conclude that
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
and the assertion follows by Definition
3.6
.

Refinement C.
We return to the actual problem (
2.1
), but now design an alternative Rockafellian with the advantage that in implementation of the resulting approximating problem there is no need to know the rate by which
p^{\nu}
tends to
p
. Specifically, for
\theta\in[0,\infty)
, we define a Rockafellian given by

f(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\theta\|u\|_{1}+\iota_{\Delta}(p+u).

(4.11)

In contrast to earlier Rockafellians defined in terms of indicator functions, it is not immediately clear whether
f
is exact. We also define the approximating functions by setting

f^{\nu}(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\theta\|u\|_{1}+\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u).

(4.12)

We see next that the desired exactness emerges when
\theta
is sufficiently large.

4.6 Theorem

(exactness under alternative Rockafellian).

In the setting of Subsection
4.1
with
p,p^{\nu}\in\Delta
and proper lsc
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=0,1,\dots,s
, consider
f
from (
4.11
) and
f^{\nu}
from (
4.12
). Suppose that there are
\eta\in[0,\infty)
and
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f_{i}(\bar{x})<\infty
and
\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x}f_{i}(x)\geq-\eta
,
i=0,1,\dots,s
.

If
\theta
is sufficiently large, then the Rockafellian
f
is strictly exact, supported by
0\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, and, under the additional assumption that
\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{2}\to 0
, the approximating functions
\{f^{\nu},\nu\in\mathbb{N}\}
are strictly limit-exact Rockafellians with
f
as their limit.

Proof.
First, we consider the strict exactness of
f
. For
u\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, we adopt the notation

\delta(u)=\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\iota_{\Delta}(p+u).

If for some
\kappa\in[0,\infty)
one has
\delta(u)\geq\delta(0)-\kappa\|u\|_{1}
for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
in a neighborhood of
0
and
\delta(0)\in\mathbb{R}
, then one can invoke Proposition
3.5
to conclude that
f
is strictly exact, supported by
0\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
, provided that
\theta
is sufficiently large. Certainly,

-2\eta\leq\delta(0)\leq f_{0}(\bar{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(\bar{x})<\infty.

For the purpose of constructing
\kappa
, we adopt the notation

I_{0}=\{i~|~p_{i}=0\}
,
I^{+}=\{i~|~p_{i}>0\}
, and
\alpha=\min\{p_{i}~|~i\in I^{+}\}
.

Suppose that
\|u\|_{1}\leq\alpha/2
. If
\delta(u)\geq\delta(0)
, then
\kappa=0
applies so we proceed under the assumption that
\delta(u)<\delta(0)
. Since
\delta(0)
is finite, this implies that
p+u\in\Delta
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\delta(0)-\delta(u)]
. Since
-2\eta\leq\delta(u)<\delta(0)<\infty
, there exists
\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

f_{0}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(\hat{x})\leq\delta(u)+\varepsilon\leq\delta(0).

Let
i\in I^{+}
. Then, one has

(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(\hat{x})\leq\delta(0)-f_{0}(\hat{x})-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{j\neq i}(p_{j}+u_{j})f_{j}(\hat{x})\leq\delta(0)+2\eta.

The probability
p_{i}+u_{i}\geq\alpha/2
because
p_{i}\geq\alpha
and
|u_{i}|\leq\alpha/2
. Consequently,

f_{i}(\hat{x})\leq\frac{\delta(0)+2\eta}{p_{i}+u_{i}}\leq\frac{2\delta(0)+4\eta}{\alpha}<\infty.

This bound together with
f_{i}(\hat{x})\geq-\eta
facilitate the following development:

\displaystyle\delta(u)

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(\hat{x})-\varepsilon

\displaystyle=f_{0}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I_{0}}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(\hat{x})-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}p_{i}f_{i}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}u_{i}f_{i}(\hat{x})-\eta\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I_{0}}(0+u_{i})-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(\hat{x})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i\in I^{+}}p_{i}f_{i}(\hat{x})-\max\Big\{\eta,\frac{2\delta(0)+4\eta}{\alpha}\Big\}\|u\|_{1}-\eta\|u\|_{1}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{x}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x)-\bigg(\max\Big\{\eta,\frac{2\delta(0)+4\eta}{\alpha}\Big\}+\eta\bigg)\|u\|_{1}-\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we have shown that
\delta(u)\geq\delta(0)-\kappa\|u\|_{1}
when
\|u\|_{1}\leq\alpha/2
for
\kappa
given inside the parentheses on the previous line.

Second, we consider the approximating functions and establish epi-convergence using the liminf-condition (
3.8
) and the limsup-condition (
3.9
). Let
\theta\in[0,\infty)
. For the liminf-condition, let
(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\to(u,x)
. If
p+u\not\in\Delta
, then
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\not\in\Delta
for sufficiently large
\nu
because
\Delta
is closed. This implies that

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\geq f(u,x)

(4.13)

holds because each side equals infinity. If
p+u\in\Delta
, then, without loss of generality, we assume that
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}\in\Delta
for all
\nu
. We obtain that

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f_{0}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})f_{i}(x^{\nu})+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\theta\|u^{\nu}\|_{1}+\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\iota_{\Delta}(p^{\nu}+u^{\nu})

\displaystyle\geq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})f_{i}(x^{\nu})+\theta\|u\|_{1}.

Since
p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu}\geq 0
, we find that
\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits(p_{i}^{\nu}+u_{i}^{\nu})f_{i}(x^{\nu})\geq(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)
. Thus, (
4.13
) holds again.

For the limsup-condition, let
(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{s}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and construct
x^{\nu}=x
and
u^{\nu}=p-p^{\nu}+u
. We assume without loss of generality that
p+u\in\Delta
. Then,
p^{\nu}+u^{\nu}=p+u\in\Delta
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})

\displaystyle\leq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\theta\|p-p^{\nu}+u\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits\theta\|p^{\nu}-p\|_{1}+\theta\|u\|_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\theta\|u\|_{1}=f(u,x).

We conclude that
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
regardless of
\theta
.

4.7 Example

(bankrupt-prone newsvendor).
In response to a random demand, a newsvendor orders
x
newspaper each morning at a cost of
\gamma>0
dollars per paper. The newsvendor sells a newspaper for
\delta>\gamma
dollars. Unsold papers cannot be returned and are worthless at the end of the day. The goal of the newsvendor is to choose an order quantity that minimizes the expected loss (expense minus income). Classically, the newsvendor can handle a loss of any size (see, e.g.,
[
59
, Section 1.C]
), but we adjust the problem by requiring the loss to be almost surely nonpositive and show that Rockafellian relaxation leads to a decision that remains hidden for an analyst following a more naive approach.

Detail.
When ordering
x
newspapers and
\xi
is the demand, the loss turns out to be

\begin{cases}\gamma x-\delta x&\mbox{ if }x\leq\xi\\
\gamma x-\delta\xi&\mbox{ if }\xi<x\leq\delta\xi/\gamma\\
\infty&\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

The threshold
\delta\xi/\gamma
is the largest order quantity that avoids the expense
\gamma x
to exceed the income
\delta\xi
.

We assume that the demand has a finite distribution with support
\{\xi_{i}\in\mathbb{N},i=1,\dots,s\}
and associated probabilities
\{p_{i},i=1,\dots,s\}
. The problem of minimizing the expected loss can then be written as the following two-stage stochastic optimization problem (cf.
[
59
, Section 1.C]
)

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{N}}\;(\gamma-\delta)x+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}f_{i}(x),

where the recourse function is
f_{i}(x)=\inf\{\delta y^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\;|\;y^{\scriptscriptstyle+}-y^{\scriptscriptstyle-}=x-\xi_{i},\;(\delta-\gamma)x/\delta\geq y^{\scriptscriptstyle+}\geq 0,\;y^{\scriptscriptstyle-}\geq 0\}
. For the specific instance with
\gamma=1
,
\delta=2
,
\{\xi_{i}=i,i=1,2,3\}
, and
p=(0,1/4,3/4)
, we find that the unique minimizer is
x=3
with minimum value
-5/2
. However, if we use the slightly different probabilities
p^{\nu}=(1/\nu,1/4-1/\nu,3/4)
for
\nu\geq 9
in place of
p
, then the unique
minimizer becomes
x=2
with minimum value
-2+2/\nu
. This holds even as
\nu\to\infty
and there is a disconcerting sensitivity to arbitrarily small changes to the probability vector. The minimizer
x=3
of the actual problem remains hidden for an analyst that proceeds naively with the seemingly accurate probability vector
p^{\nu}
.

The Rockafellian given by
f(u,x)=\iota_{\mathbb{N}}(x)+(\gamma-\delta)x+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}(p_{i}+u_{i})f_{i}(x)+\theta\|u\|_{1}+\iota_{\Delta}(p+u)
and its approximation
f^{\nu}
obtained by replacing
p
by
p^{\nu}
are special cases of (
4.11
) and (
4.12
). The resulting approximating problem
has minimizer
(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})=(-1/\nu,0,1/\nu,3)
with minimum value
-5/2+6/\nu
for
\nu\geq 9
and
\theta\geq 2
. Thus, the approximating problem allows us to recover the minimizer of the actual problem despite having incorrect probabilities.

Expectations in Compositions

Expectation functions also arise in constraints due to fairness considers in statistical learning (see Example
2.2
), in the construction of Neyman-Pearson classifiers
[
42
]
, and in reliability constraints involving buffered failure probabilities
[

. Again, small changes to the underlying probabilities may cause large errors in solutions of the corresponding optimization problems. In this subsection, we consider the actual problem

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+h\Big(\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)\Big),

(4.14)

where

,

and

is continuous for each

and
g_{ik}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}

, which
produces the expectation constraints

\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}g_{ik}(x)\leq 0,~~k=1,\dots,m,

but the results below hold for any proper lsc

Since perturbation of a constraint can cause large changes in minimizers of the corresponding problems, it is clear that naively replacing

by

in (

f(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+h\Big(u+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)\Big)+\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}(u).

(4.15)

For
p^{\nu}\in\Delta

, we select the approximating functions given by

f^{\nu}(u,x)=f_{0}(x)+h\Big(u+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}G_{i}(x)\Big)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}.

Again strict limiting exactness holds under mild assumptions.

4.8 Proposition

(exactness in composite optimization).

In the setting of this subsection with
p,p^{\nu}\in\Delta
, proper lsc functions
f_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, and continuous mappings

as their limit.

Proof.
The strict exactness follows directly from Definition

. For the second assertion, we leverage the liminf-condition (

) and the limsup-condition (

. Let
(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\to(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits f_{0}(x^{\nu})\geq f_{0}(x)

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}(u)
, and

Since

and

\mathop{\rm liminf}\nolimits\bigg(f_{0}(x^{\nu})+h\Big(u^{\nu}+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}G_{i}(x^{\nu})\Big)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u^{\nu}\|_{2}^{2}\bigg)\geq f_{0}(x)+h\Big(u+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)\Big)+\iota_{\{0\}^{m}}(u).

For
(u,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we construct
x^{\nu}=x

If

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq f(u,x)
. If

\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits f^{\nu}(u^{\nu},x^{\nu})\leq f_{0}(x)+h\Big(\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)\Big)+\mathop{\rm limsup}\nolimits{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\Big\|\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)-\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}G_{i}(x)\Big\|_{2}^{2}.

. Thus,
f^{\nu}
epi-converges to
f
.

We observe that the result holds without any constraint qualification. Thus, it addresses difficult cases with
h=\iota_{(-\infty,0]^{m}}
and
\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}G_{i}(x)\geq 0
for all

.

If

is not only proper and lsc but also convex, then the minimization over

in the approximating problem can be achieved “explicitly.” (The case

is a prime example.) Specifically, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and

, one has

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm inf}\nolimits_{u}\big\{f_{0}(x)+h(u+v)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle\big\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)-\mathop{\rm sup}\nolimits_{u}\big\{\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle-h(u+v)-{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle=f_{0}(x)+h^{\nu}(v),

where

h^{\nu}(v)=-\mathop{\rm min}\nolimits_{w\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}\Big\{h^{*}(w)-\langle v,w\rangle+\frac{1}{2\theta^{\nu}}\|y^{\nu}-w\|_{2}^{2}\Big\}.

The transition from the sup-expression to

is achieved by recognizing that the former is the conjugate of the function

. This in turn is expressed by the conjugate of

[

]

Thus, for any

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+h\Big(u+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}G_{i}(x)\Big)+{\raisebox{1.0pt}{$\frac{1}{2}$}}\theta^{\nu}\|u\|_{2}^{2}-\langle y^{\nu},u\rangle

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+h^{\nu}\Big(\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{s}p_{i}^{\nu}G_{i}(x)\Big).

The approximating function

in this subsection is closely related to augmented Lagrangians within the general duality framework of

4.4
Countable Supports

As an illustration of possibilities beyond finite probability spaces, we consider countable supports and thus address Example
2.1
. For proper lsc functions
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
,
i=0,1,2,\dots

\mathop{\rm minimize}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f_{0}(x)+\mathop{\sum}\nolimits_{i=1}^{\infty}p_{i}f_{i}(x).

(4.17)

Let
\ell^{1}=\{(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)~|~\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|u_{i}|<\infty\}
and adopt the norm given by
\|u\|_{\ell^{1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|u_{i}|

.

While our original definition of Rockafellians only allows for finite-dimensional perturbations, it trivially extends to perturbations defined on
\ell^{1}

can be viewed as a Rockafellian for the actual problem (
4.17
). Although we do not formally define the framework, it is clear that minimizing the actual problem is equivalent to minimizing this Rockafellian. Thus, we have, in an extended sense, strict exactness.

We know from Example
2.1
that naively replacing
p
by some approximating probabilities

The probability vector

defines the approximating function given by

In view of the strict exactness (in an extended sense) of

hinges on epi-convergence of

to

. The convergence in the liminf- and limsup-conditions (
3.8
) and (

) are now understood in the sense of the norm

would specify a minimizer of the actual problem; see
[
54
]
for details about epi-convergence.

of a sequence of sets

5.1 Theorem

(first-order optimality condition).

Since

0

in the assertion.

In the earlier sections, we passed from a Rockafellian to its approximations

5.2 Theorem

(convergence to first-order optimality condition).

Proof.
Under (

It applies because

We end by examining the requirement (

The claim about (

For other

The computational challenges of solving an approximating problem (

[

and the additional constraint
f_{i}(x)\leq z_{i}

[

[

2

40

are especially promising for solving (

) and (
4.12

u
and
x
. Optimizing
u
amounts to linear or quadratic optimization and optimizing

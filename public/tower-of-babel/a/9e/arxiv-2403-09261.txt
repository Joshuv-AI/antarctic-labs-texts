Dietrich Häfner, Christiane Klein

Abstract
. In [ 7 ] , the Unruh state for massless fermions on a Kerr spacetime was constructed and the authors showed its Hadmard property in the case of very slowly rotating black holes | a | ≪ M {\left|a\right|}\ll M . In this note, we extend this result to the full non extreme case | a | < M {\left|a\right|}<M .



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
81T13, 81T20, 35S05, 35S35

1.
Introduction

When studying quantum fields in black hole spacetimes, one crucial and non-trivial step is the choice of state for the quantum field at hand. Ideally, the state should be motivated by the physical situation. One state which satisfies this condition for a variety of black hole spacetimes is the Unruh state
[
26
]
- it appears as the final state when one considers the collapse of a star to a black hole, see e.g.
[
11
]
,
[
9
]
.

Moreover, the selected state should be physically reasonable, in the sense of satisfying the Hadamard property, see e.g.
[
6
]
for why this is the necessary condition. In this note, we will consider the microlocal formulation of the Hadamard property due to Radzikowski
[
23
]
. The Unruh state is also favourable in this regard. It was first constructed and its Hadamard property shown for the wave equation on the Schwarzschild spacetime by Dappiaggi-Moretti-Pinamonti
[
3
]
, and this proof has since been expanded to other spherically symmetric black hole spacetimes
[
2
,
14
]
.

However, astrophysical black holes are expected to be rotating, and therefore not spherically symmetric. Rotating black holes are described by the Kerr spacetime. Constructing explicit Hadamard states in the case of the Kerr spacetime is more complicated because of the lack of a global timelike Killing vector field in the exterior region. Nonetheless, Gérard, Wrochna and the first author have recently constructed the Unruh state for massless Dirac fields on the Kerr spacetime in
[
7
]
(see
[
9
]
for an earlier construction of the Unruh state on block I also in the massive charged case). They have also shown its Hadamard property, but only under the condition that the angular momentum per unit mass
a
of the black hole is small.
In
[
9
]
,
[
7
]
, to circumvent the lack of a global timelike Killing field, the data are separated in data for solutions going to the black hole horizon and solutions going to infinity by means of asymptotic velocities constructed in the framework of scattering theory, see
[
10
]
. Two different Killing vector fields are then used to construct the state. Later on, the second author constructed the Unruh state for the Klein-Gordon equation on the De Sitter Kerr spacetime in
[
16
]
and showed its Hadamard property, again under the condition that the angular momentum per unit mass of the black hole is small. The scattering construction is there replaced by an argument using the fast decay of the field (see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
12
]
).

The assumption of small angular momentum is an important shortcoming, since most black holes are expected to rotate rapidly, see
[
1
]
and
[
25
]
. The restriction is used in
[
7
]
to show the Hadamard property. For sufficiently small
a
, any geodesic in the black hole exterior
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
(see Section
3
for the definition) either reaches the past black hole horizon or past null infinity or meets a region, where both vector fields which have been used to construct the Unruh state are timelike. For those geodesics who reach either the past black hole horizon or past null infinity, a refinement of a strategy of Moretti
[
19
]
, see also
[
13
]
,
[
8
]
, showing a Hadamard property from characteristic data was used. This argument works the same way in the large
a
case. Yet, for large
a
not all other geodesics meet a region where both relevant vector fields are timelike.

In this note, we extend the result of
[
7
]
to all subextreme values of the angular momentum
{\left|a\right|}<M
. This requires a precise analysis of the situation on the backward/forward trapped set. The argument therefore only concerns the geodesic flow and should in principle apply also to other fields. Note, however, that in the case of the De Sitter Kerr metric other problems arise, in particular in the full range of angular momentum, it is up to today unknown if the wave equation has growing modes or not. We refer to
[
7
]
for a full review of the literature.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
-
4
, we revise the necessary constructions to define the Unruh state in Section
5
, where the main result of this paper is stated. Sections
2
-
4
essentially summarizes the constructions in
[
7
]
, we also refer to
[
7
]
for more details and additional references. In Section
6
, we prove our main result.

2.
The Dirac and Weyl operators

In this section, we summarize some elementary facts about Dirac and Weyl operators. For details of the construction, we refer to
[
7
]
.

2.1.
Notation

Let
(M,g)
be an oriented and time oriented Lorentzian manifold of dimension
4
. We denote by
\Omega_{g}\in\mbox{\Large$\wedge$}^{4}M
the volume form associated to
g
and by
\mathop{}\!d{\rm vol}_{g}=|\Omega_{g}|
the volume density.

If
{\pazocal S}\xrightarrow{\pi}M
is a vector bundle, we denote by
C^{\infty}(M;{\pazocal S})
the space of its smooth sections. In the case when
{\pazocal S}
is a complex vector bundle, we denote its anti-dual bundle by
{\pazocal S}^{*}
. The complex conjugate bundle (obtained by considering the fibers as complex vector spaces with the opposite complex structure) is denoted by
\overline{{\pazocal S}}
.

If
{\pazocal X}
is a complex vector space, we denote by
L_{\rm h}({\pazocal X},{\pazocal X}^{*})
, resp.
L_{\rm a}({\pazocal X},{\pazocal X}^{*})
, the space of Hermitian, resp. anti-Hermitian forms on
{\pazocal X}
. Generally, if
\beta\in L({\pazocal X},{\pazocal X}^{*})
, i.e. if
\beta
is a sesquilinear form, we denote by
\overline{\psi_{1}}\cdot\beta\psi_{2}
its evaluation on
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in{\pazocal X}
. If instead
\beta\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathit{End}({\pazocal S},{\pazocal S}^{*}))
, the same notation is used for the fiberwise evaluation of
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M;{\pazocal S})
.

2.2.
Dirac and Weyl operators

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime of dimension 4, equipped with its unique spin structure. From the spin structure, one obtains in a canonical way a spinor bundle
{\pazocal S}\xrightarrow{\pi}M
of rank
4
, a spin connection
\nabla^{{\pazocal S}}
, and a representation
\gamma
of the Clifford bundle
{\rm Cl}(M,g)
in
End({\pazocal S})
. One also obtains a positive energy Hermitian form
\beta
, and a complex conjugation
\kappa
acting on the fibers of
{\pazocal S}
.
The massless Dirac operator

\not{D}
acting on smooth sections of the canonical spinor bundle
{\pazocal S}
over
M
is the differential operator defined as

\not{D}=g^{\mu\nu}\gamma(e_{\mu})\nabla^{{\pazocal S}}_{e_{\nu}},

where
(e_{0},\dots,e_{3})
is a local frame of
TM
. In the massless case, it is well known that the whole analysis can be reduced to the
Weyl equation

(2.1)

{\mathbb{D}}\phi=0,

which accounts for half of the degrees of freedom. We call
{\mathbb{D}}
the associated Weyl operator.
More precisely, one can identify
{\pazocal S}
with
{\mathbb{S}}^{*}\oplus{\mathbb{S}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\#}}
, where
{\mathbb{S}}^{*}=\overline{{\mathbb{S}}^{{\scriptscriptstyle\#}}}
is the bundle of even Weyl spinors. We have

(2.2)

\Gamma(X)=\beta\gamma(X)\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathit{End}({\mathbb{S}}^{*},{\mathbb{S}})),\ \ X\in C^{\infty}(M;TM)

and

(2.3)

{\mathbb{D}}=g^{\mu\nu}\Gamma(e_{\mu})\nabla_{e_{\nu}}^{{\pazocal S}}.

If
S
is a space-like Cauchy surface, the Cauchy problem

(2.4)

\begin{cases}{\mathbb{D}}\phi=0,\\
r_{S}\phi=\varphi\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(S;{\mathbb{S}}^{*}_{S}),\end{cases}

where
{\mathbb{S}}^{*}_{S}
is the restriction of
{\mathbb{S}}^{*}
to
S
and
r_{S}\phi=\phi_{|S}
, has a unique solution
\phi=\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}\mathbb{U}_{S}\varphi
in the space of smooth space-compact solutions ,
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M)
.
\phi

space-compact
means that the intersection of
\supp\phi
with any space-like Cauchy surface is compact.

The
principal symbol
of
{\mathbb{D}}
is the section
\sigma_{\mathbb{D}}\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}M\setminus{\mskip-4.0mu{\rm\textit{o}}};\mathit{End}({\mathbb{S}}^{*},{\mathbb{S}}))
given by

(2.5)

\sigma_{\mathbb{D}}(x,\xi)=\Gamma(g^{-1}(x)\xi),\ \ (x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\setminus{\mskip-4.0mu{\rm\textit{o}}}.

By
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
, the Weyl operator
{\mathbb{D}}
is pre-normally hyperbolic, i.e., there exists a differential operator
{\mathbb{D}^{\prime}}
such that
(\sigma_{{\mathbb{D}}}\circ\sigma_{{{\mathbb{D}}}^{\prime}})(x,\xi)=(\xi\cdot g^{-1}(x)\xi)\boldsymbol{1}
.
As a consequence, the
characteristic manifold
of
{\mathbb{D}}
defined as

\Char({\mathbb{D}})=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\setminus{\mskip-4.0mu{\rm\textit{o}}}\,:\,\sigma_{\mathbb{D}}(x,\xi)\mbox{ is not invertible}\}.

is given by

\Char({\mathbb{D}})=\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\setminus{\mskip-4.0mu{\rm\textit{o}}}\,:\,\xi\cdot g^{-1}(x)\xi=0\}=\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}{\pazocal N}.

Its two connected components are

(2.6)

{\pazocal N}^{\pm}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}={\pazocal N}\cap\{(x,\xi)\in T^{*}M\setminus{\mskip-4.0mu{\rm\textit{o}}}\,:\,\pm v\!\cdot\!\xi>0\ \forall v\in T_{x}M\hbox{ future directed timelike}\}.

If
v\!\cdot\!\xi>0
for all
v\in T_{x}M
which are future directed timelike, we say that
\xi
is future pointing.

2.3.
L^{2}
solutions

Let
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M)
be the space of smooth space-compact solutions of
{\mathbb{D}}\phi=0
,
\phi\in C^{\infty}(M;{\mathbb{S}}^{*})
.
The
current

J(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\in C^{\infty}(M;T^{*}M)
defined by

(2.7)

J(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\!\cdot\!X\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}=\overline{\phi}_{1}\!\cdot\!\Gamma(X)\phi_{2},\ \ X\in C^{\infty}(M;TM),\ \phi_{i}\in{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M),

satisfies

(2.8)

\nabla^{a}J_{a}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})=0,\ \ \phi_{i}\in{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M).

For a smooth hypersurface
S
, let
i:S\to M
be the canonical injection. Since
\nabla^{a}J_{a}\Omega_{g}=d(g^{-1}J\lrcorner\Omega_{g})
, we deduce from Stokes’ formula that

(2.9)

\int_{\partial{U}}i^{*}(g^{-1}J(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\lrcorner\Omega_{g})=0,\ \phi_{i}\in{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M),

if
U
is any open set whose boundary
\partial U
is a union of smooth hypersurfaces, such that
\supp J(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\cap\partial U
is compact. Let
S\subset M
be a smooth hypersurface. To express

\int_{S}i^{*}(g^{-1}J\lrcorner\Omega_{g})

for
J
a
1
-form on
M
, we choose a vector field
l=l^{a}
transverse to
S
, future pointing and a
1
-form
\nu=\nu_{a}dx^{a}
on
M
such that
TS=\Ker\nu
, normalized so that
\nu\!\cdot\!l=1
. We obtain:

(2.10)

i^{*}(g^{-1}J\lrcorner\Omega_{g})=(\nu\!\cdot\!g^{-1}J)i^{*}(l\lrcorner\Omega_{g}).

Let now
S\subset M
be any smooth Cauchy surface (with the convention that a Cauchy surface does not need to be space-like). We set

(2.11)

\displaystyle(\phi_{1}|\phi_{2})_{{\mathbb{D}}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}={\rm i}\int_{S}i^{*}(g^{-1}J(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})\lrcorner\Omega_{g})

\displaystyle={\rm i}\int_{S}\overline{\phi}_{1}\!\cdot\!\Gamma(g^{-1}\nu)\phi_{2}\mathop{}\,i^{*}_{l}d{\rm vol}_{g},

where
i^{*}_{l}d{\rm vol}_{g}=|i^{*}(l\lrcorner\Omega_{g})|
.

From (
2.9
), we see that the r.h.s. in (
2.11
) is independent on the choice of the Cauchy surface
S
. If
S
is space-like with future pointing normal
n
, we obtain

(2.12)

(\phi_{1}|\phi_{2})_{{\mathbb{D}}}={\rm i}\int_{S}\overline{\phi_{1}}\!\cdot\!\Gamma(n)\phi_{2}\mathop{}\!d{\rm vol}_{h},

where
\mathop{}\!d{\rm vol}_{h}=i^{*}_{n}d{\rm vol}_{g}
. By the properties of
\beta
,
{\rm i}\Gamma(n)
is positive definite, which shows that

(\cdot|\cdot)_{{\mathbb{D}}}
is a Hilbertian scalar product on
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M).
We can reformulate this as being a scalar product on the Cauchy data on a spacelike hypersurface
\Sigma
defined by

(2.13)

\displaystyle\overline{\varphi}_{1}\!\cdot\!\nu_{\Sigma}\varphi_{2}={\rm i}\int_{\Sigma}\overline{\varphi}_{1}\!\cdot\!\Gamma(n)\varphi_{2}\,d{\rm vol}_{h},\ \varphi_{i}\in C_{\rm c}^{\infty}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{S}}^{*}_{\Sigma}).

Definition 2.1
.

The Hilbert space
{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}(M)
, called the
space of
L^{2}
solutions
, is the completion of
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M)
for the scalar product
(\cdot|\cdot)_{{\mathbb{D}}}
.

Note that
{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}(M)\subset L^{2}_{\rm loc}(M;{\mathbb{S}})
continuously, so elements of
{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}(M)
are distributional solutions of
{\mathbb{D}}\phi=0
.

2.4.
Action of Killing vector fields

Let
X
be a complete Killing vector field on
(M,g)
. Let
{\pazocal L}_{X}
be the corresponding Lie derivative on spinors. Then
{\pazocal L}_{X}
preserves
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M)
.
By
[
7
, Proposition 3.5]
, we have

Proposition 2.2
.

The operator
{\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X}
with domain
{\rm Sol}_{\rm sc}(M)
is essentially selfadjoint on the Hilbert space
{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}(M)
.

3.
The Kerr spacetime

3.1.
Boyer-Lindquist blocks

We now recall the relevant facts on Kerr black hole geometry.
We fix
0<{\left|a\right|}<M
, i.e. we consider the
subexetreme
Kerr case.
One sets

\begin{array}[]{l}\Delta=r^{2}-2Mr+a^{2},\ \ \rho^{2}=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta,\\[5.69054pt]
\sigma^{2}=(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}-a^{2}\Delta\sin^{2}\theta=(r^{2}+a^{2})\rho^{2}+2a^{2}Mr\sin^{2}\theta,\end{array}

and
r_{\pm}=M\pm\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}}
for the two roots of
\Delta
as a function of
r
.

The Boyer-Lindquist blocks are the manifolds
({\rm M}_{\rm I},g)
,
({\rm M}_{\rm II},g)
, where

\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm M}_{\rm I}={\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{+},+\infty\right[}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi},\\[5.69054pt]
{\rm M}_{\rm II}={\mathbb{R}}_{t}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{-},r_{+}\right[}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi},\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

\theta\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]0,\pi\right[}
,
\varphi\in{\mathbb{R}}/2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}
are the spherical coordinates on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and

g=-\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\rho^{2}}\right)dt^{2}-\frac{4aMr\sin^{2}\theta}{\rho^{2}}\mathop{}\!dtd\varphi+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\Delta}\mathop{}\!dr^{2}+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\sin^{2}\theta\mathop{}\!d\varphi^{2}.

(We will not need to consider the third Boyer-Lindquist block
{\rm M}_{\rm III}
corresponding to
r\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]-\infty,r_{-}\right[}
). The global coordinates
(t,r,\theta,\varphi)
on
{\rm M}_{\rm I},{\rm M}_{\rm II}
are called
Boyer-Lindquist coordinates
. The vector field
\nabla t=g^{-1}(dt,\cdot)=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\rho^{2}\Delta}\left(\partial_{t}+\frac{2Mar}{\sigma^{2}}\partial_{\varphi}\right)
is timelike on
({\rm M}_{\rm I},g)
and one fixes the time orientation of
({\rm M}_{\rm I},g)
by declaring
-\nabla t
to be future directed. By convention, the time orientation of
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
is the one inherited from its embedding into
{\rm K}^{*}
, see
3.3.2
below.
({\rm M}_{\rm I},g)
is globally hyperbolic.

3.2.
Time reversal

When considering the gluing of various blocks into a larger spacetime, it is useful to introduce the following notation. If
(M,g)
is a spacetime, we denote by
(M^{\prime},g)
the same Lorentzian manifold with the opposite time orientation. So,
{\rm id}:(M,g)\to(M^{\prime},g)
is an isometric involution reversing the time orientation. If
(M,g)
admits a spinor bundle
{\pazocal S}\xrightarrow{\pi}M
, then so does
(M^{\prime},g)
. We will generally decorate with primes the objects associated to
(M^{\prime},g)
.

3.3.
The
{\rm K}^{*}
and
{}^{*}\!{\rm K}
spacetimes

We now recall the
Kerr-star
and
star-Kerr
coordinates, which allow to glue together
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
and
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
along parts of
\{r=r_{+}\}
. As pre-announced, we will remove from
{\rm K}^{*}
and
{}^{*}\!{\rm K}
the parts corresponding to the third Boyer-Lindquist block
\mathrm{M}_{\rm III}
.

3.3.1.
The
{\rm K}^{*}
spacetime

We set

{\rm K}^{*}={\mathbb{R}}_{t^{*}}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{-},+\infty\right[}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi^{*}},

equipped with the metric

g=g_{tt}dt^{*2}+2g_{t\varphi}dt^{*}d\varphi^{*}+g_{\varphi\varphi}d\varphi^{*2}+2dt^{*}dr-2a\sin^{2}\theta d\varphi^{*}dr+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}.

The global coordinates
(t^{*},r,\theta,\varphi^{*})
are called
Kerr-star coordinates
. We will denote by
\partial_{r^{*}}
,
\partial_{\theta^{*}}
, the coordinate vector fields
\partial_{r}
,
\partial_{\theta}
of the Kerr-star coordinates. We time orient
{\rm K}^{*}
by declaring that the null vector
-\partial_{r^{*}}
is future directed.

3.3.2.
Embedding
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
and
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
into
{\rm K}^{*}

Let
x(r)
and
\Lambda(r)
for
r\in\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{-},r_{+}\right[}\cup\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{+},+\infty\right[}
be such that

(3.1)

\frac{dx}{dr}=\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta},\ \ \frac{d\Lambda}{dr}=\frac{a}{\Delta}.

We define the map
j^{*}:{\rm M}_{\rm I}\cup{\rm M}_{\rm II}\to{\rm K}^{*}
by:

t^{*}\circ j^{*}=t+x(r),\ \ r\circ j^{*}=r,\ \ \theta\circ j^{*}=\theta,\ \ \varphi^{*}\circ j^{*}=\varphi+\Lambda(r),

which allows to identify isometrically
({\rm M}_{\rm I},g)
resp.
({\rm M}_{\rm II},g)
with
({\rm M}_{\rm I}^{*},g)
, resp.
({\rm M}_{\rm II}^{*},g)
, where

{\rm M}_{\rm I}^{*}={\rm K}^{*}\cap\{r_{+}<r\},\ \ {\rm M}_{\rm II}^{*}={\rm K}^{*}\cap\{r_{-}<r<r_{+}\}.

Using
j^{*}
, one can glue
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
with
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
inside
{\rm K}^{*}
along the
future horizon
:

\mathscr{H}_{+}={\mathbb{R}}_{t^{*}}\times\{r=r_{+}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi}.

The embedding of
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
and
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
into
{\rm K}^{*}
respects the time orientation. For coherence of notation, we will use the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

We set

{\rm M}_{{\rm I}\cup{\rm II}}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}=(j^{*})^{-1}({\rm K}^{*})={\rm M}_{\rm I}\cup{\rm M}_{\rm II}\cup\mathscr{H}_{+},

with the spacetime structure inherited from
{\rm K}^{*}
.

The spacetime
({\rm M}_{{\rm I}\cup{\rm II}},g)
is globally hyperbolic.

3.3.3.
The
{}^{*}\!{\rm K}
spacetime

Similarly, we set

{}^{*}\!{\rm K}={\mathbb{R}}_{{}^{*}\!t}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]r_{-},+\infty\right[}_{r}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,{}^{*}\!\varphi},

equipped with the metric

g=g_{tt}d{}^{*}\!t^{2}+2g_{t\varphi}d{}^{*}\!td{}^{*}\!\varphi+g_{\varphi\varphi}d{}^{*}\!\varphi^{2}-2d{}^{*}\!tdr+2a\sin^{2}\theta d{}^{*}\!\varphi dr+\rho^{2}d\theta^{2}.

The global coordinates

,

,

3.3.4.
Embedding
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
and
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
into
{}^{*}\!{\rm K}

Again, the map

allows to identify isometrically

Using

, one can glue

with
{\rm M}_{\rm II}
inside

(3.2)

\mathscr{H}_{-}={\mathbb{R}}_{{}^{*}\!t}\times\{r=r_{+}\}\times\mathbb{S}^{2}_{\theta,{}^{*}\!\varphi}.

(resp.

3.4.
Conformal extension of
{\rm M}_{\rm I}

The Penrose conformal extension of
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
is obtained by setting
\hat{g}=w^{2}g
, where

extends as a smooth, non-degenerate metric to

\epsilon_{0}>0

\hat{g}
is Lorentzian for

We define the conformal extension of
{\rm M}_{\rm I}

\hat{{\rm M}}_{\rm I}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.46387pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\cdot$}}={\mathbb{R}}_{t^{*}}\times\mathopen{}\mathclose{\left]-\epsilon_{0},r_{+}^{-1}\right]}_{w}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi^{*}}.

Past null infinity
is then the null hypersurface

(3.3)

\mathscr{I}_{-}={\mathbb{R}}_{t^{*}}\times\{w=0\}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{2}_{\theta,\varphi^{*}}.

In an analogous way, extending
\hat{g}

({}^{*}\!t,w,\theta,{}^{*}\!\varphi)

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X=v_{\mathscr{H}}
or
v_{\mathscr{I}}
and recall that
{\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X}
is the selfadjoint generator of a unitary group on
{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}({\rm M}_{\rm I})

\chi^{\pm}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})

\chi^{\pm}-\boldsymbol{1}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}}\in O(\langle\lambda\rangle^{-\infty})

\sing\supp\chi^{\pm}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}\cup\{0\}
is compact. Then one has:

{\rm WF}(\chi^{\pm}({\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X})\phi)\subset\{(x,\xi);\,X\!\cdot\!\xi>0\}\,\hbox{for all }\,\phi\in{\rm Sol}_{{\rm L}^{2}}({\rm M}_{\rm I}).

Note that similar results have been obtained in
[
15
, Theorem 8.4.8]
,
[
24
, Theorem 2.8]
and
[
22
]

Proof.
The proof is essentially the same as for
[
7
, Lemma 8.6]
. We repeat it here for the convenience of the reader.
We consider the case of
X=v_{\mathscr{I}}=\partial_{t}
, the other case being analogous. Let us denote
\phi^{+}=\chi^{+}({\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X})\phi
. Let
q_{0}=(x^{0},\xi^{0})=(t^{0},r^{0},\varphi^{0},\theta^{0},\xi_{t}^{0},\xi_{r}^{0},\xi_{\varphi}^{0},\xi_{\theta}^{0})
with
\xi^{0}_{t}<0

Let
\vartheta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
be such that
\vartheta(\xi_{t}^{0})=1

\vartheta(\lambda)=1
for
\lambda\ll 0
and
\vartheta(\lambda)=0
for
\lambda>0
and
\lambda\in\sing\supp\chi^{+}

\chi_{\infty}=\vartheta\chi^{+}\in O(\langle\lambda\rangle^{-\infty})

(6.3)

\vartheta({\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X})\phi^{+}=\chi_{\infty}({\rm i}^{-1}{\pazocal L}_{X})\phi.

In coordinates,

for some smooth family of invertible fiber endomorphisms

Let

and microsupported in a small conic neighborhood

of

equals

The proof of the minus sign version of the statement is analogous. ∎

6.2.
Characterisation of
{\pazocal N}^{+}

Let us consider a generic null covector
\xi\in T_{x}^{*}{\rm M}_{\rm I}
for some
x\in{\rm M}_{\rm I}
. Then one has the following general result

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
x\in{\rm M}_{\rm I}
and
\xi\in T^{*}_{x}{\rm M}_{\rm I}
a null covector. Then
\xi
is future pointing iff
\xi\cdot(-\nabla t)>0
.

Proof.
We use abstract index notation.
\xi_{a}
is future directed if and only if
-\xi^{b}=-g^{ab}\xi_{a}
is future directed if and only if
\overline{C}_{x}(-\xi^{b})=\overline{C}_{x}(-\nabla t)
, where
\overline{C}_{x}(u)
is the causal cone at
x
containing
u
. By
[
21
, Lemma 5.29, Exercise 5.3]
, this holds if and only if
\xi_{a}(-\nabla t)^{a}=g_{ab}\xi^{a}(-\nabla t)^{b}>0
.
∎

\xi
must satisfy
g^{-1}(\xi,\xi)=0
, and therefore
G=G_{r}+G_{\theta}=0
, where
G_{r}
and
G_{\theta}
are as given in (
3.9
) and (
3.10
).
It can for example be specified by picking any
(\xi_{r},\xi_{\theta},\xi_{\varphi})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
arbitrary, and setting

(6.4)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=-\frac{2Mar}{\sigma^{2}}\xi_{\varphi}\pm\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\Delta\xi_{r}^{2}+\xi_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{\rho^{4}}{\sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\xi_{\varphi}^{2}\right)}\,.

Note that the root is non-vanishing, unless either
\Delta=0
or
(\xi_{r},\xi_{\theta},\xi_{\varphi})=0
, and hence also
\xi_{t}=0
.

Lemma 6.4
.

If we choose the
+
sign in (
6.4
), then
\xi
is future pointing. If we choose the
-
sign, then
\xi
is past pointing.

Proof.
Let
\xi_{t}
be determined as above.
By Lemma
6.3
, it suffices to check that
\xi\cdot(-\nabla t)>0
. Then

\displaystyle\xi\cdot(-\nabla t)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\rho^{2}\Delta}\xi_{t}+\frac{2Mar}{\rho^{2}\Delta}\xi_{\varphi}=\pm\frac{\sigma^{2}}{\rho^{2}\Delta}\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\Delta\xi_{r}^{2}+\xi_{\theta}^{2}+\frac{\rho^{4}}{\sigma^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}\xi_{\varphi}^{2}\right)}\,.

Therefore,
\pm\xi\cdot(-\nabla t)>0
if one chooses the
\pm
sign in (
6.4
).

Since the coordinates used above do not cover the axis of rotation, we have to consider it separately. Due to the symmetry of the spacetime under reflection along the equatorial plane, it is sufficient to consider the north pole. To do so, we use stereographic coordinates on the sphere, given by

\displaystyle x_{1}=\sin\theta\cos\varphi\quad x_{2}=\sin\theta\sin\varphi\,,\quad\xi_{\theta}=\cot\theta(x_{1}\xi_{1}+x_{2}\xi_{2})\,,\quad\xi_{\varphi}=x_{1}\xi_{2}-x_{2}\xi_{1}\,.

Following the computation in
[
4
]
, one finds that on the axis, and in this new coordinate system, one can express the condition
G=0
as

\displaystyle\Delta\xi_{r}^{2}-\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}{\Delta}\xi_{t}^{2}+\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=\pm\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\Delta\xi_{r}^{2}\right)}\,.

Similarly, one finds that on the axis
-\nabla t=\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta}\partial_{t}
, since
\partial_{\varphi}=x_{1}\partial_{x_{2}}-x_{2}\partial_{x_{1}}
vanishes there. Therefore

\displaystyle\xi\cdot(-\nabla t)=\pm\frac{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\Delta}\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}\left(\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\Delta\xi_{r}^{2}\right)}\,.

This is positive (negative) if one chooses the upper (lower) sign.
∎

It is interesting to note that

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}}{a}-\frac{2Mr}{\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{r^{2}+a^{2}}-\frac{2Mr}{\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})}\left(\rho^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})+2a^{2}Mr\sin^{2}\theta-2Mr(r^{2}+a^{2})\right)

(6.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho^{2}\Delta}{\sigma^{2}(r^{2}+a^{2})}

which is positive if
\Delta>0
.

6.3.
Analysis on the trapped set

Let us now consider the trapped set. Recall that the trapped set is characterized by

\displaystyle K=\{G=\partial_{r}G_{r}=\xi_{r}=0,\,\tilde{\xi}\neq 0\}\subset T^{*}{\rm M}\backslash o.

Assume that we have chosen
\xi
with
\xi_{r}=0
and
\xi_{t}
given by one of the two options in (
6.4
), so that
G=0
is satisfied. Note that
(r^{2}+a^{2})\xi_{t}+a\xi_{\varphi}=0,\,G=0
entails
\tilde{\xi}=0
. The condition
\partial_{r}G_{r}=0,\,\tilde{\xi}\neq 0
can then be rewritten in the following way
[
4
]
:

\displaystyle 4r\Delta\xi_{t}-\partial_{r}\Delta((r^{2}+a^{2})\xi_{t}+a\xi_{\varphi})

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(2r\Delta-(r-M)(r^{2}+a^{2}))\xi_{t}-(r-M)a\xi_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=0

(6.6)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\xi_{t}\left[r^{2}(r-3M)+a^{2}(r+M)\right]-a\xi_{\varphi}(r-M)

\displaystyle=0.

Note that on the trapped set
\xi_{t}=0
entails
\tilde{\xi}=0
. We therefore suppose
\xi_{t}\neq 0
in the following.
Multiplying (
6.3
) by
\xi_{t}
in the first line, one obtains the condition

(6.7)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}(\xi_{t}+\Omega_{0}\xi_{\varphi})>0\,,

where we have set
\Omega_{0}(r)=a/(r^{2}+a^{2})
.

Lemma 6.5
.

\{\xi_{t}<0\}\cap\{\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0\}\cap K=\emptyset.

Proof.
Note that on the axis of rotation, in the coordinate system
(t,r,x_{1},x_{2})
discussed above, one finds
v_{\mathscr{H}}=\partial_{t}
, and therefore on the axis the condition
\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0
reduces to
\xi_{t}>0
. We can thus work away from the axis of rotation.

The only way for
(x,\xi)
to be in the intersection
\{\xi_{t}<0\}\cap\{\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0\}
is by having
-\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}\xi_{\varphi}<\xi_{t}<0
, and therefore
a\xi_{\varphi}>0
, since
a^{-1}\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}>0
. If we also demand that
(x,\xi)\in K
, by (
6.5
) and (
6.7
), we need to take the minus-sign for the root in (
6.4
), with
\xi_{\theta}
chosen small enough so that

(6.8)

\displaystyle\Omega(\xi_{\theta}):={\left|\frac{\xi_{t}}{a\xi_{\varphi}}\right|}=\frac{2Mr}{\sigma^{2}}+\sqrt{\frac{\Delta}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{4}}{\sigma^{2}a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}+\frac{\xi_{\theta}^{2}}{a^{2}\xi_{\varphi}^{2}}\right)}<\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}}{a}\,.

To exclude this possibility, we need to take into account the trapped set condition
\partial_{r}G_{r}=0
in more detail. Starting from (
6.3
) and considering that
\xi_{t}
and
a\xi_{\varphi}
are of opposite sign, one finds

(6.9)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}\left[r^{2}(r-3M)+a^{2}(r+M)\right]-a\xi_{\varphi}(r-M)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle-\Omega(\xi_{\theta})\left[r^{2}(r-3M)+a^{2}(r+M)\right]-(r-M)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle-\left[r^{2}(r-3M)+a^{2}(r+M)\right]=\frac{(r-M)}{\Omega(\xi_{\theta})}.

Since
(r-M)>0
in
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
, and by (
6.8
), one obtains the estimate

(6.10)

\displaystyle-\left[r^{2}(r-3M)+a^{2}(r+M)\right]>\frac{a(r-M)}{\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle P(r):=r^{3}-3Mr^{2}+(r_{+}^{2}+2a^{2})r-Mr_{+}^{2}<0.

Using that
r^{2}-2Mr>-a^{2}
in
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\partial_{r}P(r)=3r^{2}-6Mr+r_{+}^{2}+2a^{2}>r_{+}^{2}-a^{2}=2(M^{2}-a^{2}+M\sqrt{M^{2}-a^{2}})>0\,,

so
P(r)
is strictly monotonically increasing on
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
as long as
|a|<M
. Moreover,

\displaystyle P(r_{+})=r_{+}^{3}-3Mr_{+}^{2}+r_{+}^{3}+2a^{2}r_{+}-Mr_{+}^{2}=2r_{+}\Delta(r_{+})=0\,.

Therefore,
P(r)>0
on
{\rm M}_{\rm I}
, in contradiction to (
6.10
). This concludes the lemma.
∎

6.4.
The backward/forward trapped sets

Recall from Section
3
that the backward/forward trapped sets are described by the condition

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\pm}=\left\{(r,\hat{x};\xi_{r},\hat{\xi}):(\hat{x},\hat{\xi})\in\hat{K};\xi_{r}=\pm{\rm sgn}(r-r^{\prime}_{\hat{x},\hat{\xi}})\sqrt{\Phi_{\hat{x},\hat{\xi}}(r)\Delta_{r}^{-1}}\right\}.

Here
\Phi_{\hat{x},\hat{\xi}}(r)=-G(r,\hat{x},0,\hat{\xi})
,
\hat{K}
is the projection of the trapped set to
(t,\theta,\varphi;\xi_{t},\xi_{\theta},\xi_{\varphi})
and

is the unique solution of
\Phi_{\hat{x},\hat{\xi}}(r)=0
.
\hat{K}
is a submanifold of
T^{*}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{2})
of codimension one.
The condition on
\xi_{r}
ensures that
(r,\hat{x};\xi_{r},\hat{\xi})
in the backward/forward trapped set is null, i.e. satisfies a relation of the form (
6.4
).

Proposition 6.6
.

If
\xi\cdot\partial_{t}>0
or
\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0,\,(x,\xi)\in\Gamma^{\pm}
, then
\xi
is future-pointing.

Proof.
Assume
(x,\xi)=(r,\hat{x},\xi_{r},\hat{x})\in\Gamma^{\pm}
.
Let us start with the case
\xi_{t}<0
, but
\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0
. This amounts to
\xi_{t}>-\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}\xi_{\varphi}
. Since
(\hat{x},\hat{\xi})\in\hat{K}
, there must be a radius
\hat{r}
such that
(\hat{r},\hat{x},0,\hat{\xi})
is in the trapped set. However, by Lemma
6.5
, no such
\hat{r}
exists.

It then remains to consider the case
\xi_{t}>0
. Since
\xi\in T^{*}_{x}{\rm M}_{\rm I}
is null,
\xi_{t}
is given by (
6.4
) evaluated at
r
. Assume that
\xi
is past pointing. Then, by Lemma
6.4
,
\xi_{t}
is given by the lower sign in (
6.4
). Together with
\xi_{t}>0
, this implies that
a\xi_{\varphi}<0
and leads to the upper bound

(6.11)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}<\frac{2Mr}{\sigma^{2}}{\left|a\xi_{\varphi}\right|}<\frac{1}{r^{2}+a^{2}}{\left|a\xi_{\varphi}\right|}<\frac{\Omega_{\mathscr{H}}}{a}{\left|a\xi_{\varphi}\right|}\,,

by an application of (
6.5
).

Next, we focus on the condition for
(\hat{x},\hat{\xi})\in\hat{K}
. Let us assume that
\hat{r}
is the radius so that
(\hat{r},\hat{x},0,\hat{\xi})\in K
. Then we must have
\partial_{\hat{r}}G_{\hat{r}}=0
. However, given the bound (
6.11
) and the fact that
\xi_{t}
and

are of opposite sign, it follows from the proof of Lemma
6.5
that no such
\hat{r}
exists. Therefore, this case is excluded. In other words: any
(x,\xi)\in\Gamma^{\pm}
that satisfies

or
\xi\cdot v_{\mathscr{H}}>0

Proof of Proposition

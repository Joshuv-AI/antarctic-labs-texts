Heisenberg double of the generalized quantum euclidean group and its representations
Limeng Xia

Abstract
. The generalized quantum Euclidean group 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) is a natural generalization of the quantum Euclidean group 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝔟 1 , 1 ) {\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{1,1}) . The Heisenberg double 𝒟 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) of 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) is the smash product of 𝒪 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) with its Hopf dual 𝒰 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) . In this paper, we study the weight modules, the prime spectrum and the automorphism group of the Heisenberg double 𝒟 q ​ ( 𝔟 m , n ) {\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}) .



Keywords:
Heisenberg double, generalized quantum euclidean group, weight module, prime spectrum

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper,
{\mathbb{K}}
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, for example
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
{\mathbb{K}}^{*}={\mathbb{K}}\setminus\{0\}
and
q\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
is not a root of unity.

Assume that
\mathfrak{g}
is an indecomposable Lie algebra of dimension
3
, then it is one of the following:

•

The three dimensional simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
.

•

The three dimensional Heisenberg Lie algebra.

•

\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}={\rm span}\{h,x,y\}
such that
[h,x]=x,[h,y]=\lambda y,[x,y]=0
for some
\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

They are very important in the representation theory of Lie algebras. The simple modules over
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and the simple modules over Heisenberg Lie algebra have been completely classified in
[
B
]
.
If
\lambda
is not rational, the classification of simple
\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
-modules is not yet done. When
\lambda
is rational,
\mathfrak{b}_{\lambda}
is isomorphic to some
\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}
, which is the three dimensional indecomposable Lie algebra with basis
\{h,x,y\}
and brackets

\displaystyle[h,x]=mx,[h,y]=ny,[x,y]=0,

for some nonzero integers
m,n
. The simple modules over
\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}
have been classified, and the classification has a little difference according to
{\rm sgn}(mn)
.

In this paper, we consider the Heisenberg double of quantum groups obtained from the quantized universal enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
is generated by the elements
a,a^{-1},b,c
subject to the following relations

\displaystyle ab=q^{n}ba,\;ac=q^{m}ca,\;bc=cb,\;aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a=1.

Its comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
, antipode
S
are defined by

\displaystyle\Delta(a)=a\otimes a,\;\varepsilon(a)=1,\;S(a)=a^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(b)=b\otimes a^{-n}+a^{n}\otimes b,\;\varepsilon(b)=0,\;S(b)=-q^{-n^{2}}b,

\displaystyle\Delta(c)=c\otimes a^{m}+a^{-m}\otimes c,\;\varepsilon(c)=0,\;S(c)=-q^{m^{2}}c.

Definition 1.2
.

The Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
is generated by the elements
K,K^{-1},E,F
subject to the following relations

\displaystyle KE=q^{2m}EK,\;KF=q^{-2n}FK,\;EF=FE,\;KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1.

Its comultiplication
\Delta
, counit
\varepsilon
, antipode
S
are defined by

\displaystyle\Delta(K)=K\otimes K,\;\varepsilon(K)=1,\;S(K)=K^{-1},

\displaystyle\Delta(E)=E\otimes K^{m}+1\otimes E,\;\varepsilon(E)=0,\;S(E)=-EK^{-m},

\displaystyle\Delta(F)=F\otimes 1+K^{-n}\otimes F,\;\varepsilon(F)=0,\;S(F)=-K^{n}F.

Note that
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
is a quantized analogue of
U(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
, at the same time
{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
is a quantized analogue of
U(\mathfrak{b}_{m,-n})
. In particular,
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{1,1})
is the quantum Euclidean group and
\mathfrak{b}_{1,-1}
is the Euclidean Lie algebra. Thus we call
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
the generalized quantum Euclidean group and call
\mathfrak{b}_{m,-n}
the generalized Euclidean Lie algebra.

Proposition 1.3
.

{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,-n})
is isomorphic to a Hopf subalgebra of
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that the following map defines an embedding of Hopf algebras:

\displaystyle K\mapsto a^{2},\;E\mapsto a^{m}c,\;F\mapsto ba^{n}.

∎

There is a unique non-degenerate dual pair
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})\times{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
determined by non-vanishing pairs:

\displaystyle\langle K,a\rangle={q}^{-1},\langle K,a^{-1}\rangle=q,\langle E,c\rangle=1,\langle F,b\rangle=1.

Note that there are two different non-degenerate dual pairs
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,-n})\times{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})\rightarrow{\mathbb{K}}
.

This dual pair makes
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
to be a left
{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
-module algebra with the action defined by

\displaystyle u\cdot x=\sum_{x}x_{(1)}\langle u,x_{(2)}\rangle,\;\forall u\in{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}),x\in{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}).

Explicitly, the action takes the following form

\displaystyle K\cdot a=q^{-1}a,\;K\cdot b=q^{n}b,\;K\cdot c=q^{-m}c,

\displaystyle E\cdot a=0,\;E\cdot b=0,\;E\cdot c=a^{-m},

\displaystyle F\cdot a=0,\;F\cdot b=a^{n},\;F\cdot c=0.

We define the
Heisenberg double of the generalized quantum Euclidean group
, which is the smash product algebra

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})={\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})\rtimes{\mathcal{U}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n}).

In this paper we are concerned with the Heisenberg double
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
and its representation theory.

Definition 1.4
.

The Heisenberg double
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
of the generalized quantum Euclidean group is the unital algebra generated by the elements
a,a^{-1},b,c,K,E,F
subject to the following relations

\displaystyle ab=q^{n}ba,\;ac=q^{m}ca,\;bc=cb,\;aa^{-1}=a^{-1}a=1,

\displaystyle KE=q^{2m}EK,\;KF=q^{-2n}FK,\;EF=FE,\;KK^{-1}=K^{-1}K=1,

\displaystyle Ka=q^{-1}aK,\;Kb=q^{n}bK,\;Kc=q^{-m}cK,

\displaystyle Ea=aE,\;Eb=bE,\;Ec=cE+a^{-m}K^{m},

\displaystyle Fa=q^{n}aF,\;Fb=q^{-n^{2}}bF+a^{n},\;Fc=q^{mn}cF.

The centrally extended Heisenberg double of
SL_{2}
and the Heisenberg double
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{1,1})
of the quantum Euclidean group were studied by Tao (see
[
T1
,
T2
]
). For the general construction of the Heisenberg double of a Hopf algebra, one is referred to
[
L
]
.

2.
The weight modules

Because the map
\xi_{K}:x\rightarrow KxK^{-1}
(resp.
\xi_{a}:x\rightarrow axa^{-1}
) is diagonal, it is possible to study the
K
-weight (resp.
a
-weight) modules over
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
-module
V
is called a
K
-weight module provided that
V=\oplus_{\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}}V_{\lambda}
where
V_{\lambda}=\{v\in V:Kv=\lambda v\}
. Denote by
{\rm supp}_{K}(V)=\{\lambda\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}|V_{\lambda}\not=0\}
the set of all
K
-weights of
V
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
-module
V
is called an
a
-weight module provided that
V=\oplus_{\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}}V^{\mu}
where
V^{\mu}=\{v\in V:av=\mu v\}
. Denote by
{\rm supp}_{a}(V)=\{\mu\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}|V^{\mu}\not=0\}
the set of all
a
-weights of
V
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{+}
be the polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[E,b]
, let
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{0}
be the quantum torus
{\mathbb{K}}[K^{\pm 1},a^{\pm 1}]
and let
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{-}
be the quantum polynomial algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[F,c]
. Then as algebra,
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
has a triangular decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})={\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{-}{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{0}{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{+}.

Let
b^{\prime}=a^{n}bK^{-n},c^{\prime}=a^{-m}cK^{-m},E^{\prime}=a^{2m}E,F^{\prime}=q^{-n^{2}}a^{-2n}FK^{n}
. Then
\{a^{\pm 1},K^{\pm 1},b^{\prime},c^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime}\}
is also a generating set of
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle Kb^{\prime}=b^{\prime}K,\;Kc^{\prime}=c^{\prime}K,\;KE^{\prime}=E^{\prime}K,\;KF^{\prime}=F^{\prime}K,

\displaystyle ab^{\prime}=b^{\prime}a,\;ac^{\prime}=c^{\prime}a,\;aE^{\prime}=E^{\prime}a,\;aF^{\prime}=F^{\prime}a,

\displaystyle b^{\prime}c^{\prime}=q^{2mn}c^{\prime}b^{\prime},\;E^{\prime}b^{\prime}=q^{2mn}b^{\prime}E^{\prime},\;F^{\prime}b^{\prime}=q^{-2n^{2}}b^{\prime}F^{\prime}+1,

\displaystyle E^{\prime}c^{\prime}=q^{2m^{2}}c^{\prime}E^{\prime}+1,\;F^{\prime}c^{\prime}=q^{-2mn}c^{\prime}F^{\prime},\;E^{\prime}F^{\prime}=q^{-2mn}F^{\prime}E^{\prime}.

Let
S
be the subalgebra
{\mathbb{K}}[b^{\prime},c^{\prime},E^{\prime},F^{\prime}]
, then we have the following isomorphism of algebras

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})^{0}\otimes S.

Lemma 2.3
.

The
S
has no finite dimensional modules.

Proof.

Note that
S
has two subalgebras
S_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[E^{\prime},c^{\prime}]
and
S_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[F^{\prime},b^{\prime}]
, both of them are generalized Weyl algebras. It is known that a generalized Weyl algebra has no finite dimensional modules.
∎

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

(i) Let
V
be a simple
K
-weight
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
-module. Then
{\rm supp}_{K}(V)=\{q^{i}\lambda|i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for some
\lambda
and any
K
-weight space of
V
is a simple
S
-module. In particular, any
K
-weight space of
V
is infinite dimensional.

(ii) Let
W
be a simple
a
-weight
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
-module. Then
{\rm supp}_{a}(W)=\{q^{i}\mu|i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for some
\mu
and any
a
-weight space of
W
is a simple
S
-module. In particular, any
a
-weight space of
W
is infinite dimensional.

Proof.

We only prove part (i), the proof for part (ii) is very similar.

Let
V
be a simple
K
-weight module with a weight
\lambda\in{\rm supp}_{K}(V)
. Because
a,a^{-1}
are invertible and
KaK^{-1}=q^{-1}a
, we have

\displaystyle aV_{\mu}\subseteq V_{q^{-1}\lambda},\;a^{-1}V_{\mu}\subseteq V_{q\lambda}.

By induction, it holds
{\rm supp}_{K}(V)\supseteq\{q^{i}\lambda|i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
. Because
[K,S]=0
, it implies

\displaystyle S\cdot V_{q^{i}\lambda}\subseteq V_{q^{i}\lambda}.

So
{\rm supp}_{K}(V)=\{q^{i}\lambda|i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
and any weight space is an
S
-module. If
V_{q^{i_{0}}\lambda}
contains a proper submodule
V^{\prime}
over
S
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a^{i}V^{\prime}

is a proper
K
-weight submodule over
{\mathcal{D}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
. Thus any weight space is a simple
S
-module. By Lemma
2.3
, any
K
-weight space of
V
is infinite dimensional.
∎

4.
The prime and primitive spectra of
S

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a proper ideal of a ring
R
. Then for all ideals
\mathfrak{i},\mathfrak{j}
, if
\mathfrak{ij}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
then either
\mathfrak{i}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\mathfrak{j}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
. In particular, if
R/\mathfrak{p}
is a domain, the ideal
\mathfrak{p}
must be prime. Let
{\rm Spec}(S)
be the set of all prime ideals of
S
.

Define symbols

\displaystyle\phi_{1}=E^{\prime}c^{\prime}-c^{\prime}E^{\prime},\;\phi_{2}=F^{\prime}b^{\prime}-b^{\prime}F^{\prime}.

Lemma 4.1
.

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}\phi_{1},\;\phi_{1}F^{\prime}=F^{\prime}\phi_{1},\;\phi_{1}b^{\prime}=b^{\prime}\phi_{1},\;E^{\prime}\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}E^{\prime},\;c^{\prime}\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}c^{\prime},

\displaystyle\phi_{1}E^{\prime}=q^{-2m^{2}}E^{\prime}\phi_{1},\;\phi_{1}c^{\prime}=q^{2m^{2}}c^{\prime}\phi_{1},\;F^{\prime}\phi_{2}=q^{-2n^{2}}\phi_{2}F^{\prime},\;b^{\prime}\phi_{2}=q^{2n^{2}}\phi_{2}b^{\prime}.

Proof.

It follows by directly computing.

Assume
d=(m,n)
. Set
(0)=\{0\},I_{1}=S\phi_{1},I_{2}=S\phi_{2},I_{3}=I_{1}+I_{2}
.
If
mn>0
, we set

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)=S(\phi_{1}^{|n|/d}-zb^{\prime|m|/d})+S\phi_{2},\quad J_{2}(z)=S\phi_{1}+S(\phi_{2}^{|m|/d}-zc^{\prime|n|/d}).

If
mn<0
, we set

\displaystyle J_{1}(z)=S(\phi_{1}^{|n|/d}-zF^{\prime|m|/d})+S\phi_{2},\quad J_{2}(z)=S\phi_{1}+S(\phi_{2}^{|m|/d}-zE^{\prime|n|/d}).

Proposition 4.2
.

The following statements hold.

1. Both

and

are domains.

2.
S/I_{3}
is simple.

3. Both

1. In the quotient algebra

\displaystyle S/I_{1}\cong{\mathbb{K}}[E^{\prime},E^{\prime-1}][F^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\cong{\mathbb{K}}[c^{\prime},c^{\prime-1}][F^{\prime},b^{\prime}],

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\Omega=\{zb^{\prime i}c^{\prime j}|z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*},i,j\in\mathbb{N}\}
. If

, then

If

\mathfrak{p}
of
S
(equivalent to consider the prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\Omega^{-1}
of
S\Omega^{-1}
), either
I_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
or
\phi_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}
.

When

is isomorphic to a quantum torus, which is simple, we obtain
\mathfrak{p}=I_{3}
.

Assume
I_{1}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\not=I_{3}
. Because
S\Omega^{-1}/I_{1}\Omega^{-1}\cong{\mathbb{K}}[b^{\prime},b^{\prime-1},c^{\prime},c^{\prime-1},\phi_{2}]
,
there exists an irreducible polynomial

\displaystyle y=\sum_{i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\in\mathbb{N}}z_{i,j,k}b^{\prime i}c^{\prime j}\phi_{2}^{k}\in\mathfrak{p}\Omega^{-1}.

Similar to above approach, by considering the number of nonzero coefficients of

\displaystyle\phi_{2}y-q^{-2n^{2}i_{0}}y\phi_{2},

we may assume
z_{i,j,k}=0
except
i=i_{0}
for some
i_{0}

\displaystyle y_{1}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}},k\in\mathbb{N}}z_{j,k}c^{\prime j}\phi_{2}^{k}\in\mathfrak{p}\Omega^{-1},

and hence some

\displaystyle y_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}f_{j}(c^{\prime})\phi_{2}^{j}\in\mathfrak{p},

with

\displaystyle b^{\prime i}y_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}q^{2n^{2}ij}f_{j}(q^{2mni}c^{\prime})\phi_{2}^{j}b^{\prime i},

it implies

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{r}q^{2n^{2}ij}f_{j}(q^{2mni}c^{\prime})\phi_{2}^{j}\in\mathfrak{p},

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
Therefore, we can assume

\displaystyle y_{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{r}z_{j}c^{\prime i_{j}}\phi_{2}^{j}\in\mathfrak{p},

such that
nj-mi_{j}
is a constant. Such irreducible element of the polynomial ring

has to be

\phi_{2}^{|m|/d}-zc^{\prime|n|/d}
for some nonzero constant

\mathfrak{p}=J_{1}(z)

The proof for
I_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\not=I_{3}
is similar. ∎

Theorem 5.1
.

1. For
m=\pm n
, let
\tau\in{\rm Aut}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
be defined by

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a\mapsto a,b\mapsto c,c\mapsto b,&m=n,\\
a\mapsto a^{-1},b\mapsto c,c\mapsto b,&m=-n.\end{array}\right.

Then
\{{\rm id},\tau\}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
.

2. For any

, let
\xi_{i}\in{\rm Aut}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
be defined by

\displaystyle\xi_{i}:a\mapsto a,b\mapsto a^{\frac{in}{(m,n)}}b,c\mapsto a^{\frac{im}{(m,n)}}c.

Then
\{\xi_{i}|i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
.

3. For all
z,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
, let
\zeta_{z,z_{1},z_{2}}\in{\rm Aut}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
be defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{z,z_{1},z_{2}}:a\mapsto za,b\mapsto z_{1}b,c\mapsto z_{2}c.

Then
\{\zeta_{z,z_{1},z_{2}}|z,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}\}\cong({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{3}

4.

\displaystyle{\rm Aut}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{3}\rtimes({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}),&m=\pm n,\\
({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{3}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}},&m\not=\pm n.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

It is clear that the statements 1-3 hold. Moreover, the subgroup
G
generated by
\tau,\xi_{i}(i\in{\mathbb{Z}}),\zeta_{z,z_{1},z_{2}}(z,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*})
is isomorphic to
({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{3}\rtimes({\mathbb{Z}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2})
and
the subgroup
G_{1}
generated by
\xi_{i}(i\in{\mathbb{Z}}),\zeta_{z,z_{1},z_{2}}(z,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*})
is isomorphic to
({\mathbb{K}}^{*})^{3}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We prove statement 4. Assume
\rho\in{\rm Aut}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
. Obviously, the group
\{za^{i}|z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{+},i\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}={\mathbb{K}}^{*}\times\langle a\rangle
is the set of all invertible elements in
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
, which can be generated by

{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
and
a
. So this group also can be generated by
{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
and
\rho(a)
. This implies that
\rho(a)=za^{\pm 1}
for some
z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
.

(i) If
m=n
,
{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})
has a decomposition of
a
-weight spaces

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})_{q^{im}},

under the action

, where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}_{q}}(\mathfrak{b}_{m,n})_{q^{im}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{r=0}^{i}{\mathbb{K}}[a,a^{-1}]b^{i-r}c^{r}.

So either
\rho(b)=xb,\rho(c)=yc
for some

or
\rho(b)=x^{\prime}c,\rho(c)=y^{\prime}b
for some

,

and

\displaystyle\rho(a)=za,\;\rho(b)=z_{1}a^{i}b,\;\rho(c)=z_{2}a^{i}c,

Then

(ii) If

z,z_{1},z_{2}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
and
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that either

\displaystyle\rho(a)=za,\;\rho(b)=z_{1}a^{i}b,\;\rho(c)=z_{2}a^{-i}c,

\displaystyle\rho(a)=za^{-1},\;\rho(b)=z_{1}a^{-i}c,\;\rho(c)=z_{2}a^{i}b.

Then

(iii) If

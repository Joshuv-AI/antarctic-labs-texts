Abstract
This paper studies the quantitative unique continuation for a semi-linear parabolic-elliptic coupled system on a bounded domain Ω \Omega . This system is a simplified version of the chemotaxis model introduced by Keller and Segel in [ 14 ] . With the aid of priori L ∞ L^{\infty} -estimates (for solutions of the system) built up in this paper, we treat the semi-linear parabolic equation in the system as a linear parabolic equation, and then use the frequency function method and the localization technique to build up two unique continuation inequalities for the system. As a consequence of the above-mentioned two inequalities, we have the following qualitative unique continuation property: if one component of a solution vanishes in a nonempty open subset ω ⊂ Ω \omega\subset\Omega at some time T > 0 T>0 , then the solution is identically zero. Keywords: Unique continuation inequalities; parabolic-elliptic coupled system; frequency function method; localization technique AMS subject classifications: 35K20, 35K55.

Introduction

This paper studies the unique continuation property for the semi-linear parabolic-elliptic coupled system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\triangle u(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\nabla v(x,t))=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
-\triangle v(x,t)+av(x,t)-bu(x,t)=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,t)=0,\>v(x,t)=0,&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.1)

\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\,(n\geq 1)
with a

\partial\Omega

The system (

introduced by Keller and Segel in
[

depicts the change of motion when a population reacts in response to an external chemical stimulus spread in the environment where they reside. In the system (

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=\triangle u(x,t)-\nabla\cdot(u(x,t)\nabla v(x,t)),&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\\
\tau\partial_{t}v(x,t)=\triangle v(x,t)-av(x,t)+bu(x,t),&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,+\infty),\end{cases}

[

[

In the studies of the chemotaxis model, boundary conditions can be either Dirichlet type, Neumann type, or Robin type, particularly, the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition means the zero density on the boundary. (See

This paper aims to show two unique continuation inequalities

Most studies on the unique continuation property for PDEs focus on the linear cases.

Remark 1.1

From the application point of view,

2.1
Preliminary lemmas

We introduce several estimates on a linear elliptic equation:

\begin{cases}-\triangle v(x)+\hat{a}v(x)=\eta(x),&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\\

(2.1)

(Here,

The following

Lemma 2.1

For each

Lemma 2.2

For each

so that for each

\displaystyle\|S_{p}(t)\varphi\|_{p}\leq\|\varphi\|_{p}\>\textrm{ and }\>\|\nabla S_{p}(t)\varphi\|_{p}\leq Ct^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi\|_{p},\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;t\in(0,+\infty).

As a direct consequence of Lemma

For each

Though the divergence operator does not commute with

Lemma 2.3
.

For each
p\in(1,+\infty)
,
there is
C=C(\Omega,p)
so that for each

,

\displaystyle\|S_{p}(t)\nabla\cdot\Phi\|_{p}\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi\|_{[L^{p}(\Omega)]^{n}},\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;t\in(0,+\infty).

(2.2)

Proof.

Let

satisfy

that when

\displaystyle\|S_{p}(t)\nabla\cdot\Phi\|_{p}=\sup_{\|\phi\|_{p^{\prime}}=1}|\langle S_{p}(t)\nabla\cdot\Phi,\phi\rangle_{p,p^{\prime}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\phi\|_{p^{\prime}}=1}|\langle\Phi,\nabla S_{p^{\prime}}(t)\phi\rangle_{[L^{p}(\Omega)]^{n},[L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)]^{n}}|\leq C(\Omega)t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\Phi\|_{[L^{p}(\Omega)]^{n}}.

(Here,

denotes the pair between
L^{p}(\Omega)
and

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{[L^{p}(\Omega)]^{n},[L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)]^{n}}

This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\|v(t_{1})-v(t_{2})\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}=\|bJ_{a}\big(\xi(t_{1})-\xi(t_{2})\big)\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle b\|J_{a}\|\cdot\|\xi(t_{1})-\xi(t_{2})\|_{p},\;\;\;\;\mbox{when}\;\;t_{1},t_{2}\in(0,+\infty).

This, along with the continuity of
\xi
, yields that
(u,v)\in C([0,\bar{T}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,\bar{T}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
.

Hence, we finish the proof.
∎

2.3
Well-posedness and estimates on the system (
1.1
)

In this subsection, we first build up the well-posedness for the system (
1.1
), then we present
some estimates for the solution to the system (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
n<p<+\infty
and
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then the following conclusions are true:

(i)
The system (
1.1
) has a unique solution
(u,v)\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T^{*}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
for some
positive constant
T^{*}:=T^{*}(\|u_{0}\|_{p},\Omega)
.

(ii)
The above solution satisfies
(t^{\frac{n}{2p}}u,v)\in[L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))]^{2}
.

(iii)
If we further assume that
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, then
(u,v)\in[L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))]^{2}
.

Proof.

Arbitrarily fix
n<p<+\infty
and
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. We organize the proof in several steps.

Step 1. We prove
that for some
T^{*}:=T^{*}(\|u_{0}\|_{p},\Omega)>0
, the system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v)\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T^{*}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
satisfying

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{p},T^{*},\Omega).

(2.16)

We will use the contraction mapping theorem to prove it.
To this end, we first set up the following framework: Let
T^{*}>0
, which will be determined later.
Let

\displaystyle K:=\{\xi\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\>\>|\>\>\>\|\xi\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{p}\}.

According to Proposition
2.1
, for each
\xi\in K
,
the system (
2.5
) (with the above
u_{0}
and the zero extension of
\xi
over
(T^{*},+\infty)
)
has a unique solution
(u^{\xi},v^{\xi})\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T^{*}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
.
We define a map
\Psi
from
K
to
C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))
by
setting

\displaystyle\Psi(\xi):=u^{\xi}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;\xi\in K.

We claim that for some
T^{*}>0
,
\Psi
has a unique fixed point
u
in
K
.
When this is done, we have

\displaystyle u(t)=S_{p}(t)u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)ds,\;t\in[0,T^{*}]

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\triangle v+av-bu=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
v=0,&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T^{*}].\end{cases}

From these, we see that
(u,v):=(u,v^{u})\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T^{*}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
is a solution to the system (
1.1
).

We now check conditions ensuring the contraction mapping theorem.
First, it is clear that
K
is a closed subset of
C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))
.
Second, we claim that
\Psi(K)\subseteq K
for some
T^{*}>0
. Indeed, it follows from Lemma
2.2
and
Lemma
2.3
that

\displaystyle\|u^{\xi}(t)\|_{p}\leq\|S_{p}(t)u_{0}\|_{p}+\|\int_{0}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u^{\xi}\nabla v^{\xi})ds\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{p}+C(\Omega)t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla v^{\xi}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\|u^{\xi}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in[0,T^{*}].

This, together with (
2.8
) and the fact
\xi\in K
, yields

\displaystyle\|u^{\xi}(t)\|_{p}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{p}+C(\Omega)t^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\|u^{\xi}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{p}+C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}\|u^{\xi}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\;\;\mbox{for each}\;\;t\in[0,T^{*}],

which leads to

\displaystyle\|u^{\xi}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{1}{1-C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}.

(2.17)

By choosing
T^{*}:=T^{*}(\|u_{0}\|_{p},\Omega)
so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}\leq 2,

(2.18)

we get from (
2.17
) that
\Psi(\xi)\in K
. Therefore,
\Psi(K)\subseteq K
.
Third, we claim that
\Psi
is a strict contraction for some
T^{*}>0
satisfying (
2.18
). In fact, given
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in K
,
write
u_{i}=\Psi(\xi_{i})
and
v_{i}=v^{\xi_{i}}
(
i=1,2
). Let

w:=u_{1}-u_{2}
.
Then one can easily check that

w
belongs to
C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))
and satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}w-\triangle w+\nabla\cdot(w\nabla v_{1})+\nabla\cdot(u_{2}\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2}))=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
w=0,&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
w(x,0)=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Thus, we have

\displaystyle w(t)=-\int_{0}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot[w\nabla v_{1}+u_{2}\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2})]ds,\;\;t\in[0,T^{*}].

This, along with Lemma
2.3
,
yields that when
t\in[0,T^{*}]
,

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{p}\leq C(\Omega)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\|w\|_{p}+\|\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\|u_{2}\|_{p}\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\nabla v_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\|w\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\!+\!\|\nabla(v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\|u_{2}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\big).

The above inequality, together with (
2.8
) and the fact that
\xi_{1},u_{2}\in K
, indicates that when
t\in[0,T^{*}]
,

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{p}\leq C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}\big(\|w\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}+\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\big),

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\xi_{1})-\Psi(\xi_{2})\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}{1-C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}\|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}.

(2.19)

Choosing
T^{*}:=T^{*}(\|u_{0}\|_{p},\Omega)
satisfying (
2.18
) and

\displaystyle\frac{C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}{1-C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}<1,

we see from (
2.19
) that

\Psi
is a strict contraction map from
K
to
K
.
Thus, according to the contraction mapping theorem,
\Psi
has a unique fixed point
u
in
K
.

Finally, we show (
2.16
).
Indeed, one can easily check that
\Psi(0)=S_{p}(t)u_{0}\in K
. Then
by taking
\xi_{1}=u
and
\xi_{2}=0
in (
2.19
), we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}=\|\Psi(u)\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\Psi(0)\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}+\|\Psi(u)-\Psi(0)\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|u_{0}\|_{p}+\frac{C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}{1-C(\Omega)T^{*\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}}\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))},

which leads to (
2.16
).

Step 2. We
show that the solution of the system (
1.1
) is unique.

By contradiction, we suppose that it is not true. Then
we could find another solution
(\tilde{u},\tilde{v})\in

C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T^{*}];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
to the system (
1.1
) (with the initial datum
u_{0}
),
differing from the solution
(u,v)
obtained in Step 1.
Write

\displaystyle\varrho:=\max\{\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))},\|\tilde{u}\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}\},

(2.20)

and

\displaystyle t_{0}:=\inf\{t\in[0,T^{*}]\>|\>u(t)\neq\tilde{u}(t)\}.

(2.21)

It is obvious that
0\leq t_{0}<T^{*}
.
By the continuity of
u
and
\tilde{u}
, we have
u(t_{0})=\tilde{u}(t_{0})
. Now, we have

\displaystyle u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[S_{p}(t-t_{0})u(t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)ds]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle[S_{p}(t-t_{0})\tilde{u}(t_{0})-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(\tilde{u}\nabla\tilde{v})ds],\;\;t\in(t_{0},T^{*}].

After simple computations, we see

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\|_{p}=\|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)ds-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(\tilde{u}\nabla\tilde{v})ds\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot((u-\tilde{u})\nabla v)+S_{p}(t-s)\nabla\cdot(\tilde{u}\nabla(v-\tilde{v}))\|_{p}ds,\;\;t\in(t_{0},T^{*}].

This, along with Lemma
2.3
, yields that for each
t\in(t_{0},T^{*}]
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\|_{p}\leq C(\Omega)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\|(u-\tilde{u})\nabla v\|_{p}+\|\tilde{u}\nabla(v-\tilde{v})\|_{p})ds

(2.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|\nabla v\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{p}+\|\nabla(v-\tilde{v})\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\|\tilde{u}\|_{p}\big)ds.

(2.23)

By the second equation of the system (
1.1
), Lemma
2.1
, and the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have that for each
s\in(0,T^{*}]
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla v(s)\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\leq C(\Omega)\|u(s)\|_{p}\;\;\textrm{ and }\;\;\|\nabla(v-\tilde{v})(s)\|_{[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n}}\leq C(\Omega)\|(u-\tilde{u})(s)\|_{p}.

(2.24)

This, along with (
2.22
) and (
2.20
), implies

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\tilde{u}(t)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(\|u\|_{p}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{p}+\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{p}\|\tilde{u}\|_{p}\big)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\varrho C(\Omega)(t-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{C([t_{0},t];L^{p}(\Omega))},\;\;t\in(t_{0},T^{*}],

which leads to

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{C([t_{0},t];L^{p}(\Omega))}\leq 4\varrho C(\Omega)(t-t_{0})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{C([t_{0},t];L^{p}(\Omega))},\;\;t\in(t_{0},T^{*}].

Let
\epsilon>0
satisfy that

4\varrho C(\Omega)\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}<1
and
t_{0}+\epsilon\leq T^{*}
. Then the above inequality indicates that

\displaystyle u(s)=\tilde{u}(s),\textrm{ when }s\in(t_{0},t_{0}+\epsilon),

which leads to a contradiction to (
2.21
).
Hence, the desired uniqueness holds.

Step 3. We show (ii), with the aid of Lemma
2.4
.

By the same argument in the proof of (
2.24
), we can easily check that the solution
(u,v)
obtained in Step 1 satisfies

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\leq C(\Omega)\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}.

(2.25)

This, along with (
2.16
), implies that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{p},T^{*},\Omega),

which leads to
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

Next, we define a differential operator
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)
by

\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)\varphi:=\triangle\varphi-\nabla v\cdot\nabla\varphi-av\varphi,\;\;\varphi\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega),

and let
U_{1}(t,s)
be the evolution system generated by
\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)

(T^{*}\geq t\geq 0)
.
Since (
1.1
) can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\triangle u+\nabla v\cdot\nabla u+avu=bu^{2},&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
-\triangle v+av-bu=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
u=0,\>v=0,&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T^{*}],\\
u(x,0)=u_{0},&\textrm{ in }\Omega,\end{cases}

we see from the variation of constants formula that

\displaystyle u(t)=U_{1}(t,0)u_{0}+b\int_{0}^{t}U_{1}(t,s)u^{2}ds,\;\;t\in[0,T^{*}].

(2.26)

Then, it follows from (
2.26
) and Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\|U_{1}(t,0)u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|b\int_{0}^{t}U_{1}(t,s)u^{2}(s)ds\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\varpi(1+t)}t^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}+b\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{p}}e^{\varpi[1+(t-s)]}\|u^{2}(s)\|_{\frac{p}{2}}ds,\;\;t\in(0,T^{*}],

where
\varpi
is a positive number depending on
C(\|u_{0}\|_{p},T^{*},n,\Omega)
. After some computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq e^{\varpi(1+t)}t^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}+b\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{p}}e^{\varpi[1+(t-s)]}\|u(s)\|^{2}_{p}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\varpi(1+T^{*})}\left(t^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\|u_{0}\|_{p}+\frac{bp}{p-n}t^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}^{2}\right),\;\;t\in(0,T^{*}].

Since
p>n
, the above inequality leads to
t^{\frac{n}{2p}}u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))
. In summary, we conclude that
(t^{\frac{n}{2p}}u,v)\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))]^{2}
.

Step 4. We show (iii).

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By Step 1 and Step 3, we already have

v\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))
and
u\in C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))

(n<p<+\infty)
.
The remainder is to show
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.
To this end, we see from (
2.26
) and Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq\|U_{1}(t,0)u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|b\int_{0}^{t}U_{1}(t,s)u^{2}(s)ds\|_{\infty}

(2.27)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\varpi(1+t)}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+b\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{p}}e^{\varpi[1+(t-s)]}\|u(s)\|^{2}_{p}ds

(2.28)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\varpi(1+T^{*})}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+\frac{bp}{p-n}t^{1-\frac{n}{p}}(\|u\|_{C([0,T^{*}];L^{p}(\Omega))}^{2}\big)<+\infty,\;\;t\in[0,T^{*}],

(2.29)

where
\varpi
is a positive number depending on
C(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},T^{*},n,\Omega)
.
From (
2.27
) and
p>n
, it follows that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))
.

Hence, we complete the proof of Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

(i)
It follows from (
2.16
) and (
2.27
) that when
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T^{*};L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},T^{*},n,\Omega).

(ii)
Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
(u,v)
is the solution to the system (
1.1
) over
[0,T]
for some
T>0
.
First, by the conclusion
(iii)
in Theorem
2.1
, we have that

(u,v)\in[L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))]^{2}
, and it follows from (
2.25
) that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\leq CM\textrm{ and }\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;[L^{\infty}(\Omega)]^{n})}\leq CM,

(2.30)

where
C>0
depends only on
\Omega
and where

\displaystyle M:=M(\|u_{0}\|_{\infty},T):=\max\{1,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))}\}.

(2.31)

Second, for each
p\in(n,+\infty)
,
the above solution
(u,v)\in C([0,T];L^{p}(\Omega))\times C((0,T];W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega))
.

(iii)
By (
1.1
) and the uniqueness given in Theorem
2.1
, we
can easily verify what follows:
u_{0}=0

over
\Omega
if and only if
u=v=0
over
\Omega\times(0,+\infty)
.

Next, we present some estimates for the system (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
(u,v)
is the solution to the system (
1.1
) over
[0,T]
for some
T>0
.
Then,

\displaystyle\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{2}\leq e^{L_{1}M^{2}t}\|u_{0}\|_{2},\;\;t\in[0,T];

(2.32)

\displaystyle\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{2}\leq\frac{e^{L_{1}M^{2}t}}{\sqrt{t}}\|u_{0}\|_{2},\;\;t\in(0,T],

(2.33)

where
L_{1}>0
is a constant depending only on
\Omega
and where
M
is given by (
2.31
).

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation of (
1.1
) by
e^{-lt}\partial_{t}u
, where
l>0
will be determined later, and then integrating it over
\Omega
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}u-\triangle u+\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)]\cdot e^{-lt}\partial_{t}udx=0,\;t\in[0,T].

From the above, the second equation of the system (
1.1
), (
2.30
), Cauchy’s inequality, and Lemma
2.1
, we find

\displaystyle e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+auv-bu^{2})\cdot\partial_{t}udx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}dx+C(\Omega)M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle C(\Omega)M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{3b^{2}}{2}M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx,\;t\in[0,T],

which yields that when
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx)

(2.34)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!C(\Omega)M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+(C(\Omega)+\frac{3b^{2}}{2})M^{2}e^{-lt}\!\!\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

Meanwhile, multiplying the first equation of (
1.1
) by
e^{-lt}u
, and integrating it over
\Omega
,
we see

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}[\partial_{t}u-\triangle u+\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)]\cdot e^{-lt}udx=0,\;t\in[0,T].

From the above, the second equation of the system (
1.1
), (
2.30
), Cauchy’s inequality, and Lemma
2.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx)+e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+av-bu)udx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+C(\Omega)M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx+\frac{a}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\frac{a}{2}+b)e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx,\>\>t\in[0,T],

from which, it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx)+\frac{1}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx

(2.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(C(\Omega)+a+b)M^{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx-\frac{l}{2}e^{-lt}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx,\;t\in[0,T].

According to (
2.34
), (
2.35
), and Poincaré inequality, there is a constant
l=L_{1}M^{2}>0
,
where
L_{1}>0
is a constant
depending only on
\Omega
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(e^{-L_{1}M^{2}t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dx)+e^{-L_{1}M^{2}t}\int_{\Omega}|\partial_{t}u(x,t)|^{2}dx\leq 0,\;t\in[0,T];

(2.36)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{g}}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx\leq 2e^{\frac{1}{\theta}\frac{R_{g}^{2}}{4\mu k}}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx.

This, along with (
3.6
) and (
3.7

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{g}}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx\leq 2\bigg(e^{L_{4}M^{2}T}e^{L_{5}(1+\frac{1}{T})}\frac{\mathbb{E}}{\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{g}}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx}\bigg)^{\frac{L_{3}R_{g}^{2}}{4\mu k}}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx.

(3.49)

Meanwhile, according to Theorem
2.2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\leq(1+Te^{L_{1}M^{2}T})\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}dx\leq(1+T)e^{L_{1}M^{2}T}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}dx.

This, together with (
3.49
), implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{g}}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx\leq 2\bigg(D\frac{\int_{\Omega}|u(x,0)|^{2}dx}{\int_{\Omega\cap B_{R_{g}}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx}\bigg)^{\frac{L_{3}R_{g}^{2}}{4\mu k}}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x,T)|^{2}dx,

where
D=(1+T)e^{(L_{1}+L_{4})M^{2}T+L_{5}(1+\frac{1}{T})}
.
The above leads to (
3.2
) with

\gamma=\frac{L_{3}R_{g}^{2}}{4\mu k+L_{3}R_{g}^{2}}
(which depends only on
\Omega,r,R_{g},\delta_{g},M
, and
T
).
This completes the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with
n<p\leq+\infty
and let
\omega
be a nonempty open subset of
\Omega
. Suppose that
(u,v)
is the solution to the system (
1.1
) over
[0,T]
for some
T>0
.
Then

\displaystyle u=0\;\mbox{over}\;\Omega\times[0,T]\>\textrm{ and }v=0\;\mbox{over}\;\Omega\times(0,T],

(4.25)

provided that either
u(\cdot,T)=0
over
\omega
or
v(\cdot,T)=0
over
\omega
.

Proof.

We organize the proof in two steps.

Step 1. We show the corollary when
p=+\infty
.

In the first case that

u(\cdot,T)=0
over
\omega
,
we can apply (
1.3
) to see that

u_{0}=0
,
which, along with
(iii)
of Remark
2.3
,
leads to (
4.25
) in this case.

We next consider the second case that
v(\cdot,T)=0
over
\omega
. Multiplying the second equation of (
1.1
) by a test function
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with
\textrm{supp}\>\chi\subset\omega
, we find

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(\cdot,T)\chi dx=\frac{1}{b}\int_{\Omega}[-\triangle v(\cdot,T)+av(\cdot,T)]\chi dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{b}\big[\int_{\Omega}av(\cdot,T)\chi dx+\int_{\Omega}v(\cdot,T)(-\triangle\chi)dx\big]=0,

which yields that
u(\cdot,T)=0
over
\omega
, i.e.,
we return to the first case.
Consequently, (
4.25
) is true for the second case.

Step 2. We show the corollary when
n<p<+\infty
.

Arbitrarily fix
\epsilon\in(0,T)
. We define two functions

u_{\epsilon}
and
v_{\epsilon}

on

\Omega\times[0,T-\epsilon]
by setting
u_{\epsilon}(x,t):=u(x,t+\epsilon)
and
v_{\epsilon}(x,t):=v(x,t+\epsilon)
, respectively. It is
obvious that
u_{\epsilon}
and
v_{\epsilon}
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{\epsilon t}-\triangle u_{\epsilon}(x,t)+\nabla\cdot(u_{\epsilon}(x,t)\nabla v_{\epsilon}(x,t))=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T-\epsilon],\\
-\triangle v_{\epsilon}(x,t)+av_{\epsilon}(x,t)-bu_{\epsilon}(x,t)=0,&\textrm{ in }\Omega\times(0,T-\epsilon],\\
u_{\epsilon}(x,t)=0,\>v_{\epsilon}(x,t)=0,&\textrm{ on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T-\epsilon],\\
u_{\epsilon}(x,0)=u(x,\epsilon),&\textrm{ in }\Omega.\end{cases}

Since
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega)

(n<p<+\infty)
, it follows from the conclusion
(ii)
in Theorem
2.1
that
u_{\epsilon}(\cdot,0)=u(\cdot,\epsilon)\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})\in[L^{\infty}(0,T-\epsilon;L^{\infty}(\Omega))]^{2}
.
Thus we can apply Theorem
1.1
(where
(u,v)
is replaced by
(u_{\epsilon},v_{\epsilon})
)
to see what follows:

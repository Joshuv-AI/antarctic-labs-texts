Abelian p p –groups and their endomorphism rings
Phill Schultz

Abstract
. The lattice of fully invariant subgroups of an abelian p p –group and the lattice of ideals of its endomorphism ring are classified by systems of cardinal invariants.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
20K10, 20K30

1.
Introduction

Finite abelian groups were the first algebraic structures to be studied from the abstract point of view. Every such group is a direct sum of finite
p
–groups for finitely many primes
p
; a finite
p
–group
G
is a direct sum of cyclic
p
–groups, say
m_{n}
summands isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{n})
for finitely many
n
, and the set of such pairs
(n,\,m_{n})
of positive integers is a complete isomorphism invariant for
G
.

This result can be extended a little further: let
G
be a direct sum of cyclic
p
–groups. Let

S=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\colon{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{n})\text{ is isomorphic to a cyclic summand of }G\}

and let
m_{n}
be the number of summands of order
p^{n}
. Both
S
and
m_{n}
may be infinite. Then the set of pairs
\{(n,\,m_{n})\colon n\in S\}
is a complete isomorphism invariant of
G
.

Every abelian
p
–group decomposes as a direct sum of a unique maximal divisible summand and a reduced complement unique up to isomorphism. Divisible
p
–groups have a satisfactory structure theorem: they are direct sums of quasi–cyclic groups
{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{\infty})
and the number of such summands is an invariant. Thus the classification of torsion abelian groups reduces to the problem of classifying unbounded reduced
p
–groups.

In the first half of this paper, I review known results to show that every unbounded reduced abelian
p
–group
G
is determined up to isomorphism by an infinite ordinal
\lambda
and for each limit ordinal
\xi<\lambda
, a
\Sigma
–cyclic group
B^{(\xi)}
and a group
H^{(\xi)}
which lies between
B^{(\xi)}
and its torsion completion in the
p
–adic topology.. The groups
B^{(\xi)}
have complete systems of cardinal invariants, but the extensions
H^{(\xi)}
remain mysterious unless
G
is simply presented.

The second half of the paper is a description of the lattice of fully invariant subgroups of an abelian
p
–group and its application to the classification of the lattice of ideals of the endomorphism ring of the group.

3.
Invariants of
G

The following results are independent of the choice of prime
p
.

3.1.
Basic subgroup

A
basic group

B
is a direct sum of cyclic groups, together with a presentation of the form
B=\bigoplus_{i<s}B_{i}
where

(1)

s\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
or
s=\omega
;

(2)

B_{i}\cong({\mathbb{Z}}(p^{n_{i}}))^{m_{i}},\ n_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
and
m_{i}
is a non–zero cardinal;

(3)

i<j<s
implies
{n_{i}}<n_{j}
.

We denote
B
by
B(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
. where
\mathbf{n}=(n_{i}\colon i<s)
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{i}\colon i<s)
.

Let
G
be a group. By
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 5, §5]
,
G
has a basic subgroup
B
satisfying the following properties:

B1

B
is a basic group, pure in
G
, and for all
i<\omega,\ \bigoplus_{j\leqslant i}B_{j}
is a maximal pure
p^{n_{i}}
–bounded subgroup of
G
;

B2

For all
i<\omega,\ G=\bigoplus_{j\leqslant i}B_{j}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j>i}B_{j}+p^{i}G\right)
;

B3

\operatorname{rank}(B_{i})=m_{i}=\operatorname{rank}(B_{i}[p])
so
\operatorname{rank}(B)=\sum_{i<\omega}m_{i}=\dim(B[p])
.

B4

G/B
is divisible;

B5

If
A
is a pure subgroup of
G
which is a direct sum of cyclic groups, then
A
is a direct summand of some basic subgroup of
G
.

B6

For all
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+},\ \ p^{n}G[p]\cong B_{n+1}[p]\oplus p^{n+1}G[p]
.

B7

All basic subgroups of
G
are isomorphic.

Property B2 implies that
G
is bounded if and only if
G=B
, in which case
G
itself is basic. More precisely, if
G
has exponent
e
, then
p^{n}G=0
for all
n\geqslant e
. Consequently,
G
is unbounded if and only if
B
is unbounded.

Property B4 ensures that every
f\in\mathcal{E}
is determined by its value on
B
i.e., if
f,\ g\in\mathcal{E}
satisfy
g|_{B}=f|_{B}
then
f=g
. In particular, if
f
is zero on
B
, then
f=0
.
Thus by B1 and B4,
G
is a pure extension of
B
by
G/B
, both of which are classified by cardinal invariants.

Property B6 ensures that if
A
is a basis for the
\mathbb{F}
–space
p^{k}G[p]/p^{k+1}G[p]
, then there is an independent set
\{b_{a}\colon a\in A\}
in
B_{k}
such that for all
a\in A,\ a=p^{k-1}b_{a}
.

Property B7 ensures that every basic subgroup of
G
is isomorphic to
B(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
for a unique pair
(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
. To summarise:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group.

(1)

If
G
is bounded, then there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
and finite sequences
\mathbf{n}=(n_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}\colon i\in[k],\ n_{i}<n_{i+1})
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{i}\colon i\in[k],\ m_{i}\text{ a cardinal})
such that
G\cong B(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
;

(2)

If
G
is unbounded and there are infinite sequences
\mathbf{n}=(n_{i}\colon i<\omega,\ n_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+})
and
\mathbf{m}=(m_{i}\colon i<\omega,\ m_{i}\text{ a cardinal })
such that

has basic subgroup
B(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
. ∎

3.2.
Heights and Ulm invariants

The following properties, due to Ulm, are found in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapters 10 and 11]
:

Let
p^{0}G=G
and for all ordinals
\kappa>0
,

p^{\kappa}G=\begin{cases}p(p^{\kappa-1}G)\text{ if $\kappa$ is a successor}\\
\bigcap_{\mu<\kappa}p^{\mu}G\text{ if $\kappa$ is a limit}\end{cases}

Since
(p^{\kappa}G\colon\kappa\geqslant 0)
is a decreasing chain of subgroups, there is a least ordinal
\lambda
, called the
length of
G

for which
p^{\lambda}G=p^{\lambda+1}G
.
If
G
is reduced,
p^{\lambda}G=0
. Otherwise
p^{\lambda}G
is the maximal divisible subgroup of
G
.

For each
a\in G
, the
height of
a
,
0pt(a)
, is the least
\kappa
for which
a\in p^{\kappa}G\setminus p^{\kappa+1}G
if such
\kappa
exists; otherwise
a\in p^{\lambda}G
and
0pt(a)=\infty
.

The subgroups
p^{\kappa}G,\ \kappa<\lambda
, have the following properties:

U1

\left(p^{\kappa}G[p]\colon\kappa\leqslant\lambda\right)
is a decreasing chain of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–spaces which are fully invariant subgroups of
G
;

U2

For all
\kappa<\lambda,\ V_{\kappa}=p^{\kappa}G[p]/p^{\kappa+1}G[p]
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–space such that
p^{\kappa}G[p]\cong V_{\kappa}\oplus p^{\kappa+1}G[p]
. Denote
the dimension
\dim(V_{\kappa})
by
u_{\kappa}
. The cardinals
u_{\kappa}
are called the
Ulm invariants
of
G
.

U3

If
G
and
H
are countable groups with the same Ulm invariants, then
G\cong H
.

U4

The property B2 implies that the finite Ulm invariants
u_{n},\ n<\omega
, of
G
are also the Ulm invariants of any basic subgroup of
G
;

U5

Let
B=B(\mathbf{n},\,\mathbf{m})
be a basic subgroup of
G
. Then for all
j<\omega,\ u_{j}=m_{j}
; in particular,
B
, and hence
G
, has a cyclic summand of exponent
i+1
if and only if
u_{i}\neq 0
.

Notation 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group of length
\lambda
.

(1)

Let
\Omega=(\kappa<\lambda\colon u_{\kappa}\neq 0)
, and let
\mathbf{u}(G)=(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa\in\Omega)
be the sequence of non–zero Ulm invariants of
G
.

(2)

Let
\mathbf{u}=(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa\in\Omega)
be a sequence of non–zero cardinals.
\mathbf{u}
is

\lambda
–admissible
if there is a reduced
p
–group
G
of length
\lambda
such that
\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}(G)
.

(3)

An abelian
p
–group

is
simply presented
(
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 11, §3]
) if it has a presentation
G=\langle X,R\rangle
where every relation in

has the form
px=0
or
px=y
. The simply presented groups have the following properties:

(a)

Basic groups are simply presented;

(b)

Countable simply presented groups are basic;

(c)

A simply presented group of limit length is the direct sum of simply presented groups of smaller length; in particular, a simply presented group of length

is basic;

(d)

The class of simply presented groups is closed under direct sums and summands;

(e)

If

is simply presented, then so are

and
G/p^{\kappa}G
for all ordinals

;

(f)

If

.

Necessary and sufficient conditions for admissibility of Ulm invariants are proved in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 11, Theorem 3.7]
.
We quote them here for future reference.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{u}=(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa\in\Omega)
be a sequence of non–zero cardinals and let
\lambda=\sup\{\kappa\colon\kappa\in\Omega\}
.

(1)

If
\mathbf{u}
is
\lambda
–admissible, then
for all
\rho<\lambda
and
\kappa
with
\kappa+\omega<\lambda
,

\sum_{\rho\geqslant\kappa+\omega}u_{\rho}\leqslant\sum_{n<\omega}u_{\kappa+n}

(2)

Let

satisfy the condition
(*)
. Then there exists a simply–presented group

of length

such that for all

is the
\kappa
–Ulm invariant of

, i.e.

is
\lambda
–admissible.
∎

Condition
(*)
is called the

.

3.3.
The
p^{\kappa}
–adic topology
[
Jacoby and Loth, 2019
]

Let
G
be a group of length
\lambda
and let
\kappa\leqslant\lambda
be a limit ordinal. The subgroups
(p^{\mu}G\colon\mu\leqslant\kappa)
form a basis of neighbourhoods of 0 for a topology on
G
, called the
p^{\kappa}
–adic topology
which make
G
a topological group which is Hausdorff if and only if
\kappa=\lambda
. When
\kappa=\omega
, the topology is simply called the
p
–adic topology
.
Since endomorphisms of
G
do not decrease height, every
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}\nolimits(G)
is continuous in the
p^{\kappa}
–topology for all
\kappa\leqslant\lambda
.

If
G
is Hausdorff in the
p^{\kappa}
–topology,
G
has a completion which in general is not a
p
–group, although its torsion subgroup is.

4.
Separable groups

In this section, we describe the groups of length
\omega
, i.e., unbounded groups with no elements of infinite height, called
separable groups
.

Let
G
be a separable group with basic subgroup
B=\bigoplus_{i<\omega}B_{i}
.
Then the
p
–adic completion of
B
is a subgroup of
\prod_{i<\omega}B_{i}
whose torsion subgroup
\overline{B}
is a
p
–group consisting of all bounded sequences in
\prod_{i<\omega}B_{i}
, called the
torsion completion of
B
. If
|B|=\mu
, then
|\overline{B}|=2^{\mu}
and
\overline{B}/B
is divisible of rank
2^{\mu}
.

Fuchs
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapter 10]
shows that the following properties are equivalent;.

S1

G
is separable;

S2

every finite subset of
G
is contained in a finite direct summand,

S3

B\leqslant G\leqslant\overline{B}
,
G
is pure in
\overline{B}
and
\overline{B}/G
is divisible.

Consequently, a separable
G
with basic subgroup
B
is simply–presented if and only if
G=B
.
The importance of separable groups lies in the fact that, as I show in §5, they are the intrinsic building blocks of all reduced
p
–groups.

Throughout this section, unless noted otherwise,
G
is a separable group with basic subgroup
B=\bigoplus_{i<\omega}B_{i}=B(\mathbf{n}.\mathbf{m})
.

Countable separable groups are basic, but uncountable separable groups are notoriously complicated; as Fuchs says,
[
Fuchs, 2015
, page 316]
, no general structure theorem is available, and none is expected.
In particular, Corner
[
Corner, 1969
]
shows that even in the simplest case when each
B_{n}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}(p^{n})
there exists a family of
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
non–isomorphic pure subgroups of
\overline{B}
containing
B
. Corner’s proof
[
Corner, 1969
, Theorem 2.1]
is non–constructive in the sense that it shows that there are
2^{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
non–isomorphic rings each of which is isomorphic to the endomorphism ring of some group
G
with
B\leqslant G\leqslant\overline{B}
. Hence there are as many non–isomorphic groups
G
in
\overline{B}
.
The result holds for any
|B|\leqslant 2^{\aleph_{0}}
and for larger
|B|
Shelah has shown,
[
Fuchs, 2015
, p. 332]
, that for every infinite cardinal
\kappa
there are
2^{\kappa}
non–isomorphic separable groups of cardinal
\kappa
with the same basic subgroup.

Although a full classification of separable groups is out of reach, it is possible to classify an important subclass.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
B
be an unbounded basic group. A group
G
satisfying
B\leqslant G\leqslant\overline{B}
is an
almost complete extension of
B
if there exists a decomposition
B=C\oplus D
where
C
and
D
are unbounded, no cyclic summand of
C
is isomorphic to a cyclic summand of
D
,
and
G=\overline{C}\oplus D
, where
\overline{C}
is the torsion completion of
C
.

In the following lemma,
B=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{i}
and we represent elements of
\overline{B}
as bounded sequences
\mathbf{b}=(b_{i}\colon i\in{\mathbb{N}})\in\prod_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{i}
where
b_{i}\in B_{i}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
G=\overline{C}\oplus D
and
G^{\prime}=\overline{C^{\prime}}\oplus D^{\prime}
be almost complete extensions of
B
. Then
G
and
G^{\prime}
are pure subgroups of
\overline{B}
and
G\cong G^{\prime}
if and only if
C=C^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\overline{G}=\overline{B}=\overline{G}^{\prime},\ G
and
G^{\prime}
are subgroups of
\overline{B}
containing
B
. Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\overline{B}
such that
p^{n}\mathbf{b}\in G
. Then
p^{n}\mathbf{b}=p^{n}\mathbf{c}+p^{n}\mathbf{d}
with
p^{n}\mathbf{c}\in\overline{C}
and
p^{n}\mathbf{d}\in D
Since both
\overline{C}
and
D
are pure in
\overline{B}
, there exist
\mathbf{e}\in\overline{C}
and
f\in D
such that
p^{n}\mathbf{e}=p^{n}\mathbf{c}
and
p^{n}f=p^{n}\mathbf{d}
. Hence
G
is pure in
\overline{B}
and similarly
G^{\prime}
is pure in
\overline{B}
.

Let
\alpha\colon G\to G^{\prime}
be an isomorphism and let
B_{i}
be a maximal homogeneous summand of
B
contained in
C
and
B_{j}
a mavimal homogeneous summand of
B
contained in
D
. Since
\alpha
preserves exponents and heights,
B_{i}\alpha
is a maximal summand of
G^{\prime}
generated by elements of exponent
n_{i}
and height 0. Hence
B_{i}\alpha=B_{i}
and similarly for
B_{j}\alpha=B_{j}
. Thus
C^{\prime}=C\alpha=C
and
D^{\prime}=D\alpha=D
. Since
\alpha
is continuous in the
p
–adic topology,
\overline{C}\alpha=\overline{C^{\prime}}
, so
C=C^{\prime}
.

The converse is clear, so we have the following classification:
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
B=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{N}}}B_{i}
be an unbounded basic group.
There exist
{2^{\aleph_{0}}}
non–isomorphic almost complete extensions of

parametrized by partitions of
{\mathbb{N}}
into two infinite subsets.

Proof.

Proposition
4.2
shows that almost complete extensions of
B
are pure subgroups of
\overline{B}
and
that non-isomorphic almost complete extensions derive from unequal decompositions of
B
. Hence There is a 1–1 correspondence between almost complete extensions of
B
and partitions of
{\mathbb{N}}
into two infinite sets;

{\mathbb{N}}
has
2^{\aleph_{0}}
subsets of which
\aleph_{0}
are finite, and
\aleph_{0}
have a finite complement. Hence there remain
2^{\aleph_{0}}
which determine partitions of
{\mathbb{N}}
into two infinite subsets.
∎

5.
Ulm subgroups

Throughout this section,
G
is a reduced group of infinite length
\lambda
with basic subgroup
B
;
\mathbf{u}=(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa\in\Omega)
is the sequence of non–zero Ulm invariants of

, and

\Xi
is the sequence of limit ordinals

.

For all
\xi\in\Xi
, let
G^{(\xi)}
denote
p^{\xi}G=\bigcap_{\mu<\xi}p^{\mu}G
. Let
B^{(\xi)}
be a basic subgroup of
G^{(\xi)}
so that
G^{(0)}=G
and
B^{(0)}=B
.
The group

is called the
\xi

of

, (
[
Fuchs, 2015

), and

the
\xi
–derived basic subgroup
of

The sequence

is called the

and
(B^{(\xi)}\colon\xi\in\Xi)

of

.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Omega,\ \Xi,\ (G^{(\xi)})
and
(B^{(\xi)})
be defined as above.

(1)

\Xi\subseteq\Omega
, that is, for all
\xi\in\Xi,\ u_{\xi}\neq 0
;

(2)

The sequence
(G^{(\xi)}\colon\xi\in\Xi)
is strictly decreasing;

(3)

If
\xi+\omega\leqslant\lambda
, then
B^{(\xi)}
is unbounded;

(4)

If
\xi+\omega>\lambda
, then there exists
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\lambda=\xi+k
and
B^{(\xi)}
has exponent
k
;

Proof.

(1) Let
\xi\in\Xi
. Since
\xi<\lambda,\ G^{(\xi)}\neq 0
, so
p^{\xi}G[p]\neq 0
and hence
\xi\in\Omega
.

(2) By definition,
G^{(\xi+1)}
is a proper subgroup of
G^{(\xi)}
and
G^{(\xi+\omega)}
is a proper subgroup of
G^{(\xi+n)}
for all
n<\omega
whenever these goups exist..

(3) Since
G^{(\xi+\omega)}=\bigcap_{\nu<\xi+\omega}p^{\nu}G,\ G^{(\xi+\omega)}\leqslant\bigcap_{n<\omega}p^{n}G^{(\xi)}
. Hence for each

there is a sequence
(n_{i})\in{\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence
(b_{i})\in G^{(\xi)}
such that

where

has height 0 so
\exp(a)\geqslant n_{i}
.

(4) The fact that
\lambda=\xi+k,\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}^{+}
, implies that
B^{(\xi)}
is has exponent
k
.
∎

The connection between Ulm subgroups and the Ulm invariants described in §3.2 is as follows:

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\xi\in\Xi
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

(1)

For all

if and only if

;

(2)

If
\lambda=\xi+n
then
B^{(\xi)}
has exponent

;

(3)

If
\xi+\omega<\lambda
, then

B^{(\xi)}
is unbounded and isomorphic to a basic subgroup of

.

(4)

For all
\xi\in\Xi,\ \operatorname{rank}(B^{(\xi)})\geqslant\sum_{\xi<\rho\in\Xi}\operatorname{rank}(B^{(\rho)})
.

Proof.

(1) The following are equivalent:

(a)

p^{n}a\in G^{(\xi)}
;

(b)

there exists
b\in G^{(\xi)}
of height
\xi
such that
p^{n}b=a
;

(c)

0pt(a)\geqslant\xi+n
.

(2) By definition,
n
is minimal such that
p^{n}G^{(\xi)}=0
.

(3) Since
G^{(\xi+\omega)}\neq 0
,
G^{(\xi+n_{i})}\neq 0
for some sequence
\mathbf{n}=(n_{i})
. Let
m_{i}
be the dimension of
p^{n_{i}}G^{(\xi)}[p]/p^{n_{i+1}}G^{(\xi)}[p],\ \mathbf{m}=(m_{i})
so
B^{(\xi)}=B(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
is unbounded.

Let
\eta\colon G\to G/G^{(\xi+\omega)}
be the natural epimorphism. Then
\eta
maps
B^{(\xi)}
isomorphically onto a basic subgroup of
G^{(\xi)}/G^{(\xi+\omega)}
.

(4) By the Ulm sum criterion (Proposition
3.3
), for all
\xi\in\Xi,\ \operatorname{rank}(B^{(\xi)})=\sum_{n<\omega}u_{\xi+n}\geqslant\sum_{\rho>\xi}u_{\rho}=\sum_{\xi<\rho\in\Xi}\operatorname{rank}(B^{(\rho)})
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

With the notation above, the graded group
\bigoplus_{\xi\in\Xi}G^{(\xi)}/G^{(\xi+\omega)}
is separable. It has a basic subgroup
\bigoplus_{\xi\in\Xi}B^{(\xi)}

6.
Indicators

The following definitions and properties are found in
[
Fuchs, 2015
, Chapters 10 and 11]
.

(1)

An
indicator
is a strictly increasing finite or countable sequence of ordinals followed by the symbol
\infty
, denoted
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i}\colon i\in[n],\infty)
or
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i}\colon i<\omega,\infty)
. When
n=0
, the sequence is denoted
(\infty)
. An indicator
\mathbf{\sigma}
has
length
n
in the former case, and has
finite entries
if each
\sigma_{i}
is finite.

(2)

The set
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
of indicators is ordered pointwise:
\mathbf{\sigma}

precedes

\mathbf{\tau}
, denoted
\mathbf{\sigma}\preceq\mathbf{\tau}
, if both have infinite length and
\sigma_{i}\leqslant\tau_{i}
for all
i<\omega
, or
\mathbf{\sigma}
has length
\geqslant n,\ \mathbf{\tau}
has length
n
and
\sigma_{i}\leqslant\tau_{i}
for all
i\leqslant n
.

In general
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
has infinite descending chains, e.g.,
(1,\infty)\succ(1,2,\infty)\succ\dots
and infinite ascending chains, e.g.,
(\kappa_{1},\infty)\prec(\kappa_{2},\infty)\prec\dots
where
(\kappa_{i}\colon i<\omega)
is a countable increasing chain of ordinals.

(3)

Let
\mathbf{\sigma}
be an indicator of length
\geqslant n
and let
i<n
. We say that
\mathbf{\sigma}

has a gap at
i
if
\sigma_{i}+1<\sigma_{i+1}
.

(4)

Let
G
be a group. An indicator
\mathbf{\sigma}\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d
is
G
–admissible
if for all
i<\omega
, if
\mathbf{\sigma}
has a gap at
i
and
\sigma_{i}=\kappa
, then the Ulm invariant
u_{\kappa}\neq 0
. Let
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
be the set of
G
–admissible indicators.

(5)

A set
\mathcal{S}
of indicators satisfies
Kaplansky’s gap condition for
G
if
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
.

(6)

If
G
is bounded of exponent
n
then
\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)
has a unique minimum, namely
(0,1,\dots,n-1,\infty)
.

(7)

Let
a\in G
with
\exp(a)=n+1
. The
indicator of
a
is the
G
–admissible indicator
\operatorname{ind}(a)=(\sigma_{i}\colon i\in[n],\infty)
, where
\sigma_{i}=0pt(p^{i}a)
, so
\sigma_{0}=0pt(a)
and
\sigma_{n}=0pt(p^{n}a)
.

(8)

Let
a\in G
and
f\in\mathcal{E}
. Then
\operatorname{ind}(a)\preceq\operatorname{ind}(af)
.

(9)

G
is
transitive
if for all
a,\,b\in G,\ \operatorname{ind}(a)\preceq\operatorname{ind}(b)
implies there exists
f\in\mathcal{E}
such that
b=af
.
Simply presented groups are transitive.

7.
Fully invariant subgroups

Let
G
be a group with endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
. A subgroup
H\leqslant G
is

if for all
f\in\mathcal{E},\ Hf\leqslant H
. Denote by
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(G)
the set of fully invariant subgroups of
G
. For example,
0,\,G,\,p^{k}G
and
G[p^{n}]\in\mathcal{H}
for all
\kappa<\operatorname{length}(G)
and
n<\exp(G)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
G
be a group with endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{H}
its set of fully invaraint subgroups. Then
\mathcal{H}
is a complete lattice under inclusion.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq\mathcal{H}
and
f\in\mathcal{E}
. For all
a\in\bigcap S,\ af\in\bigcap S
. Hence
S\in\mathcal{H}
.

Let
b\in\sum S
. Then there exists a finite set
S_{i}\colon i\in[n]
such that
b=\sum b_{i}
with
b_{i}\in S_{i}
. Hence
bf=\sum b_{i}f\in\sum S
, so
\sum S\in\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be a group,
\mathbf{\sigma}=(\sigma_{i},\infty)\in\mathcal{I}\kern-1.0pt\textit{n}d(G)

(1)

(2)

(5)

Proof.

(1) Since

By 4.2 (8),

(2) It is clear that

Let

is homocyclic of exponent

\vdots\\

Lemma 7.9
.

With the notation above,

(1)

Every entry

of

is a distinct fundamental subgroup of
G
;

(2)

(1) Each

8.
The endomorphism ring of
G

Current knowledge of the structure of the endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
of an abelian
p
–group

is presented in
[
Krylov, Mikhalev and Tuganbaev, 2003
, Chapter 4, §20]
which mainly draws on the results presented in
[
Fuchs, 2015
]
and
[
Pierce, 1963
]
.
For the particular case of separable
G
, see
[
Corner, 1969
]
and
[
Stringall, 1967
]
.
These references are mainly concerned with identifying the Jacobson radical
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathcal{E}
and the factor ring
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{J}
. The main structure theorem states that if
G
has non–zero Ulm invariants
(u_{\kappa}\colon\kappa<\lambda)
then
\mathcal{E}
is the split extension of the Jacobson radical
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathcal{E}
by a subring of the direct product
\prod_{i<\lambda}\mathcal{L}(u_{i})
where
\mathcal{L}(u_{i})
is the ring of linear transformations of the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–space of dimension
u_{i}
.
\mathcal{J}
is charatacterised (
[
Pierce, 1963
, Lemma 4.5]
) as the
p
–adic closure of the ideal of endomorphisms which raise finite heights in the socle,

The paper
[
AvS, 2000
, Theorem 4.5]
contains an explicit description, in terms of cardinal invariants, of the ideal lattice of
\mathcal{E}
in the bounded case. In
[
ASZ, 2020
]
the endomorphism ring of a bounded group is represented by a finite digraph whose vertices are the fundamental subgroups and arrows the irreducible endomorphisms. The ideals are represented by particular sets of paths in the digraph.

In contrast, the approach in this paper is to use the lattice
\mathcal{H}
of fully invariant subgroups of
G
to describe certain sublattices of ideals of
\mathcal{E}
. In the unbounded case, this approach fails to identify such important ideals as the various radicals of
\mathcal{E}(G)
.

The functors
(\kappa,\,n)\to p^{\kappa}G[p^{n}]
for ordinals
\kappa
and natural numbers

defined in §3.1 and §3.2 for abelian
p
–groups

, can also be applied to the ring
\mathcal{E}
, even though the additive group of
\mathcal{E}
is not a
p
–group when
G
is unbounded. Namely, we define by induction
p^{\kappa+1}\mathcal{E}=p(p^{\kappa}\mathcal{E})
and
p^{\kappa}\mathcal{E}=\bigcap_{\mu<\kappa}p^{\mu}\mathcal{E}
if
\kappa
is a limit ordinal; and
\mathcal{E}[p^{n}]=\{f\in\mathcal{E}\colon p^{n}f=0\}
. Clearly,
p^{\kappa}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}[p^{n}]
are closed under subtraction. Since endomorphisms do not decrease heights or increase exponents, it is also clear that
p^{\kappa}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}[p^{n}]
and their intersection

are ideals of
\mathcal{E}
.

In the definitions of fundamental subgroups and indicator subgroups in
\mathcal{H}
, the ranks of the homocyclic components of

play no rôle. but
this is not true of the ideals of the endomorphism ring
\mathcal{E}
. For example, let

have infinite rank; an endomorphism has rank

\mathcal{E}
.

This is exemplified for bounded groups in
[
AvS, 2000
]
, where it is shown that ideals of
\mathcal{E}
are parametrised by three variables: height, exponent and rank. More precisely, let
G
have exponent

, homocyclic components
B_{i},\ i\in[k]
and infinite rank
\rho
. Let

and let
\mu
an infinite cardinal
\leqslant\rho
. Let

Theorem 4.5 of
[

states that if

is bounded, then for every ideal

of
\mathcal{E},\ I=I(\kappa,\,n,\,\mu)
for suitable choice of the parameters

. The aim of this paper is to prove a similar result for unbounded groups.

On n n -pre-Lie algebras and dendrification of n n -Lie algebras
Taoufik Chtioui , Atef Hajjaji , Sami Mabrouk and Abdenacer Makhlouf 1. University of Sfax, Faculty of Sciences Sfax, BP 1171, 3038 Sfax, Tunisia2. University of Gafsa, Faculty of Sciences Gafsa, 2112 Gafsa, Tunisia3. IRIMAS - Département de Mathématiques, 6, rue des frères Lumière, F-68093 Mulhouse, France

Abstract
The main purpose of this paper is to introduce the notion of n n -L-dendriform algebra which can be seen as a dendrification of n n -pre-Lie algebras by means of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators. We investigate the representation theory of n n -pre-Lie algebras and provide some related constructions. Furthermore, we introduce the notion of phase space of a n n -Lie algebra and show that a n n -Lie algebra has a phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a n n -pre-Lie algebra. Moreover, we present a procedure to construct ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -pre-Lie algebras from n n -pre-Lie algebras equipped with a generalized trace function.

Introduction

The notion
of an
n
-Lie algebra, or a Filippov algebra was introduced in
[
24
]
. It is the algebraic structure corresponding
to Nambu mechanics
[
36
,
40
]
. Notice that
n
-Lie algebras, or more generally,
n
-Leibniz algebras, have attracted
attention from several fields of mathematics and physics due to their close connection with dynamics,
geometries as well as string theory
[
13
,
14
]
, see also
[
15
,
16
,
25
,
29
,
38
]
. For example, the structure of

n
-Lie algebra is applied to study supersymmetry and gauge symmetry transformations of the
word-volume theory of multiple
M2
-branes and the generalized identity for
n
-Lie algebras can be regarded
as a generalized Plucker relation in the physics literature. See the review article
[
22
]
for more details.

A dendriform algebra is a module equipped with two binary products whose sum is associative. This
concept was introduced by Loday in the late 1990s in the study of periodicity in algebraic K-theory
[
32
]
.
Several years later, Loday and Ronco introduced the concept of tridendriform algebra from their study of
algebraic topology
[
33
]
. It is a module with three binary operations whose sum is associative. Afterward,
quite few similar algebraic structures were introduced, such as the quadri-algebra and ennea algebra
[
1
]
.
The notion of splitting of associativity was introduced by Loday to describe this phenomena in general, for
the associative operation (see
[
23
,
28
,
34
]
, for more details).

Pre-Lie algebras are a class of nonassociative algebras coming from the study of convex homogeneous
cones, affine manifolds and affine structures on Lie groups, and the cohomologies of associative algebras.
See the survey
[
10
]
and the references therein for more details. The main properties of a pre-Lie algebra is that
the commutator gives rise to a Lie algebra and the left multiplication gives rise to a representation of the
commutator Lie algebra. Conversely, a relative Rota-Baxter operator action on a Lie algebra gives rise to a
pre-Lie algebra
[
27
,
31
]
and thus the pre-Lie algebra can be seen as the underlying algebraic structure of
a relative Rota-Baxter operator. In
[
18
]
, the authors introduce the notion of
n
-pre-Lie algebra, which gives
a
n
-Lie algebra naturally and its left multiplication operator gives rise to a representation of this
n
-Lie
algebra. A
n
-pre-Lie algebra can also be obtained through the action of a relative Rota-Baxter operator
on a
n
-Lie algebra. For (
n=3
), see
[
7
]
for more details.

Note that the notion of a symplectic structure on a
n
-Lie algebra was introduced in
[
18
]
and
it is shown that the underlying structure of a symplectic
n
-Lie algebra is a quadratic
n
-pre-Lie
algebra. In this paper, we introduce the notion of a phase space of a
n
-Lie algebra
A
, which is a symplectic
n
-Lie
algebra
A\oplus A^{*}
satisfying some conditions, and show that a
n
-Lie algebra has a phase space if and
only if it is sub-adjacent to a
n
-pre-Lie algebra. We also introduce the notion of Manin triple
of
n
-pre-Lie algebras and show that there is a one-to-one correspondence between Manin triples of

n
-pre-Lie algebras and phase spaces of
n
-Lie algebras.

In
[
5
]
, the authors introduce a realization of the quantum Nambu bracket in terms
of matrices (using the commutator and the trace of matrices). This construction was
generalized in
[
2
]
to the case of any Lie algebra where the commutator is replaced
by the Lie bracket, and the matrix trace is replaced by linear forms having similar
properties, that led to a so called
3
-Lie algebras induced by Lie algebras. This construction
is generalized to the case of
n
-Lie algebras in
[
3
]
, see also
[
4
]
.

The following is an outline of the paper. In Section 2, we summarize some definitions and known results
about
n
-Lie algebras and
n
-pre-Lie algebras which will be useful in the sequel. Section 3 is dedicated to study representations of
n
-pre-Lie algebras. In Section 4,
we introduce the notion of phase space of a
n
-Lie algebra and show that a
n
-Lie algebra has a
phase space if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a
n
-pre-Lie algebra. We also introduce the notion of
Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras and study its relationship with phase spaces of
n
-Lie algebras. Next, we provide a construction
procedure of
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebras starting from an
n
-pre-Lie algebra
and a trace map. In Section 5, we introduce the
notion of
n
-L-dendriform algebra. We establish that it has two associated
n
-pre-Lie algebras (horizontal and
vertical). They have the same sub-adjacent
n
-Lie algebra. In addition, the left multiplication operator of the
first operation (called north-west:
\nwarrow
) and the right multiplication operator of the second operation (called
north-east:
\nearrow
) lead to a bimodule of the associated horizontal
n
-pre-Lie algebra.

Throughout this paper
\mathbb{K}
is a field of characteristic
0
and all vector spaces are over
\mathbb{K}
and finite dimensional.

Preliminaries and Basics

In this section, we give some preliminaries and basic results on

Definition 2.1
.

[
24
]

A
n
-Lie algebra consists of a vector space
A
equipped with a skew-symmetric
n
-linear map
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]:A^{n}\rightarrow A
such that the following Filippov-Jocobi identity holds (for
x_{i},y_{i}\in A,1\leq i\leq{n}
)

\displaystyle[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

(2.1)

Recall that the
n
-linear bracket is said to be skew-symmetric if for any permutation
\sigma
in the permutation group
S_{n}
and
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in A
, we have

[x_{\sigma(1)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)}]=Sgn(\sigma)[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],

where
Sgn(\sigma)
is the signature of
\sigma
.

The Filippov-Jacobi identity can expressed as follows. For
X=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\in A^{n-1}
, the operator

\displaystyle ad(X):A\to A,\quad ad(X)(y):=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y],\quad\forall y\in A,

(2.2)

is a derivation in the sense that

ad(X)([y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}])=\sum_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},ad(X)(y_{i}),y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

(2.3)

A morphism between
n
-Lie algebras is a linear map that preserves the
n
-Lie brackets.

.

[
24

Consider
(n+1)

-Lie algebra

generated by
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n+1})
with the following multiplication

[e_{1},\cdots,\widehat{e}_{i},\cdots,e_{n}]=e_{i},

where
\widehat{e}_{i}
means that the element

is omitted.

Definition 2.2
.

A representation of a

-Lie algebra

\displaystyle\rho([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n})\rho(x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}),

(2.4)

\displaystyle[\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}),\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})]=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1}),

(2.5)

for
x_{i},y_{i}\in A,1\leq i\leq n
.

We refer to the representation by the pair
(V,\rho)
. We say also that

is an
A
-module.
Let
\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=ad(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})
for

. Then

is an

.

, and

Let

be a representation of a

-Lie algebra

Definition 3.1
.

A representation of a
n
-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})

\displaystyle\bullet l(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})=r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\mu(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]^{C},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1})+r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2},\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{n-1}\}),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\bullet r([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]^{C},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}l(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})r(x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-2}),

(3.2)

\displaystyle\bullet r(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},\{y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\})=l(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})r(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},y_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i+1}r(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{i}},\cdots,y_{n})\mu(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},y_{i}),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\bullet r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\mu(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=l(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})r(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}r(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},\{x_{i},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1}\}),

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\Big(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{j}r(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq j<i\leq n}(-1)^{j+1}r(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i})(u_{j})\Big)

(3.7)

4

In this section, we introduce the notion of phase space of a
n
-Lie algebra and show that a
n
-Lie algebra has a phase space
if and only if it is sub-adjacent to a
n
-pre-Lie algebra. Moreover,
we introduce the notion of Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras and show that there is a one-to-one
correspondence between Manin triples of

-pre-Lie algebras and perfect phase spaces of

-Lie algebras. Finally, we give a procedure to construct a

4.1
Symplectic structures and phase spaces of
n
-Lie algebras

Definition 4.1
.

[
18
]

An element
B\in\wedge^{2}A^{*}
is a symplectic structure on a
n
-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
if
B
is a nondegenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form
satisfying the following equality

B([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}B(x_{i},[x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},y]),

(4.1)

for any
x_{i},y\in A
and
1\leq i\leq{n}
.

Definition 4.2
.

[
8
]

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdots])
be a
n
-Lie algebra and let
B:A\times A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be a
non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
A
. If
B
satisfies

B([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n}],x_{n+1})=-B([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n+1}],x_{n}),\;\forall x_{i}\in A,\;1\leq i\leq{n+1}.

(4.2)

Then
B
is called an invariant scalar product on
A
or a metric on
A
, and
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B)
is a
metric
n
-Lie algebra.

If there exists a metric
B
and a symplectic structure
\omega
on a
n
-Lie algebra
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
, then

(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B,\omega)
is called a metric symplectic
n
-Lie algebra.

Let
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B)
be a metric
n
-Lie algebra, we set

Der_{B}(A)=\{D\in Der(A)\;|\;B(Dx,y)+B(x,Dy)=0,\;\forall x,y\in A\}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(A,B)
be a metric
n
-Lie algebra. Then there exists a symplectic structure
on
A
if and only if there exists a skew-symmetric invertible derivation
D\in Der_{B}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
(A,B,\omega)
be a metric symplectic
n
-Lie algebra. Defines
D:A\rightarrow A
by

B(Dx,y)=\omega(x,y),\;\forall x,y\in A.

(4.3)

It is clear that
D
is invertible. Next we will check that
D
is a skew-symmetric invertible
derivation of
(A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B)
. From Eq.(
4.1
), for
x_{i},y\in A,\;1\leq i\leq{n}

\displaystyle\quad\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}B([x_{1},\cdots,x_{i-1},D(x_{i}),x_{i+1}\cdots,x_{n}],y)-B(D([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]),y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}B([x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n},y],Dx_{i})-B(D([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]),y)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\omega(x_{i},[x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x}_{i},\cdots,x_{n},y])-\omega([x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}],y)=0,

and by the definition of
D
, we have

B(Dx,y)+B(x,Dy)=\omega(x,y)+\omega(y,x)=0,

that is,
D\in Der_{B}(A)
. Conversely, if
D\in Der_{B}(A)
is invertible. Defines
\omega:A\times A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
by Eq.(
4.3
). Then by the
above discussion,
\omega
is non-degenerate, and satisfies Eq.(
4.1
). The result follows.
∎

-Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

If
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B)
is a phase space of a
n
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})
is
the associated
n
-pre-Lie algebra. Then both
(\mathfrak{h},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(\mathfrak{h}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\}_{\mathfrak{h}^{*}})
are subalgebras of the

n
-pre-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\rho
be a representation of
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
on
\mathfrak{h}
and
\rho^{*}
is its dual representation. If
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],B)
is a phase space of a
n
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
such that the
n
-Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
is a semi-direct
product
\mathfrak{h}\ltimes_{\rho^{*}}\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, then

\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}=\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})x_{n},\;\forall x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}.

(4.14)

defines a
n
-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}
.

Proof.

For all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{h}
,
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\alpha,\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}\rangle=-B(\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\},\alpha)=B(x_{n},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\alpha]_{\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}})

\displaystyle B(x_{n},\rho^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\alpha)=-\langle\rho^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\alpha,x_{n}\rangle=\langle\alpha,\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})x_{n}\rangle.

Therefore,
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\}=\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})x_{n}
.
∎

Example 4.7
.

Let
(A,\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\}_{A}
be a
n
-pre-Lie algebra. Since there is a semi-direct product
n
-pre-Lie algebra
(A\ltimes_{L^{*},0}A^{*},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\}_{L^{*},0})
on the phase space
T^{*}A^{c}=A^{c}\ltimes_{L^{*}}A^{*}
, one can construct a new phase space
T^{*}A^{c}\ltimes_{L^{*}}(T^{*}A^{c})^{*}
. This process can be continued indefinitely. Hence,
there exist a series of phase spaces
\{A_{(m)}\}_{m\geq 2}:

A_{(1)}=A^{c},\;\;\;A_{(2)}=T^{*}A_{(1)}=A^{c}\ltimes_{L^{*}}A^{*},\cdots,\;\;\;A_{(m)}=T^{*}A_{(m-1)},\cdots.

A_{(m)}\;(m\geq 2)
is called the symplectic double of
A_{(m-1)}
.

At the end of this section, we introduce the notion of Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras.

Definition 4.4
.

A Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras is a triple
(\mathcal{A};A,A^{\prime})
, where

\bullet

(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\},B)
is a quadratic
n
-pre-Lie algebra,

\bullet
both
A
and
A^{\prime}
are isotropic subalgebras of
(\mathcal{A},\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})
,

\bullet

\mathcal{A}=A\oplus A^{\prime}
as vector spaces,

\bullet
for all
x,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in A
and
\alpha,\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\in A^{\prime}
, holds:

\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\alpha\}\in A^{\prime},\;\{\alpha,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\}\in A^{\prime},\;\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1},x\}\in A,\;\{x,\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\}\in A.

(4.15)

In a Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras, since the skew-symmetric bilinear form
B
is nondegenerate,

A^{\prime}
can be identified with
A^{*}
via

\langle\alpha,x\rangle\triangleq B(\alpha,x),\;\forall x\in A,\;\alpha\in A^{\prime}.

Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is isomorphic to
A\oplus A^{*}
naturally and the bilinear form
B
is exactly given by (
4.12
). By
the invariant condition (
4.11
), we can obtain the precise form of the
n
-pre-Lie structure
\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\}
on

A\oplus A^{*}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
(A\oplus A^{*};A,A^{*})
be a Manin triple of
n
-pre-Lie algebras, where the nondegenerate
skew-symmetric bilinear form
B
on the
n
-pre-Lie algebra is given by (
4.12
). Then we
have

\displaystyle\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\alpha\}=\Big(L^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}R^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{i})\Big)\alpha,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\{\alpha,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\}=-R^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\alpha,

(4.17)

\displaystyle\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1},x\}=\Big(\mathcal{L}^{*}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}\mathcal{R}^{*}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\alpha_{i}},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1},\alpha_{i})\Big)x,

(4.18)

\displaystyle\{x,\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1}\}=-\mathcal{R}^{*}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n-1})x.

(4.19)

Proof.

\forall x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in A,\;\alpha\in A^{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad\;\langle\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\alpha\},x_{n}\rangle=B(\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},\alpha\},x_{n})

\displaystyle=-B(\alpha,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]^{C})=-B(\alpha,\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\{x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{i}\})

\displaystyle=-B(\alpha,\Big(L(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}R(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{i})\Big)x_{n})

\displaystyle=-\langle\alpha,\Big(L(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}R(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{i})\Big)x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Big(L^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i}R^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{i})\Big)\alpha,x_{n}\rangle,

which implies that (
4.16
) holds. We have

\displaystyle\quad\;\langle\{\alpha,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\},x_{n}\rangle=B(\{\alpha,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\},x_{n})=-B(x_{n-1},[\alpha,x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},x_{n}]^{C})

\displaystyle=B(x_{n-1},[x_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},\alpha]^{C})=-B(\{x_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\},\alpha)=B(\alpha,\{x_{n},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\})

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha,R(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})x_{n}\rangle=-\langle R^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\alpha,x_{n}\rangle,

which implies that (
4.17
) holds. Similarly, we can deduce that (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) hold.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

-pre-Lie algebras

-Lie algebras. More precisely, if

(

). Conversely, if

(\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*};\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h}^{*})

-pre-Lie algebras, where the
n
-pre-Lie algebra structure on
\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h}^{*}

is given by (
4.10
).

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
)
Let
(A\oplus A^{*};A,A^{*})

and

the

and

respectively. By Proposition
4.8
,

\displaystyle[x_{1}+\alpha_{1},\cdots,x_{n}+\alpha_{n}]^{C}=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{A}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\mathcal{L}^{*}(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\alpha_{i}},\cdots,\alpha_{n})(x_{i})

\displaystyle+[\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}]_{{A}^{*}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}L^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n})(\alpha_{i}).

then we deduce that

is a

(

) Conversely, let

4.2
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebras induced by
n
-pre-Lie algebras

In this subsection we show how to derive
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebras from
n
-pre-Lie algebras and a trace map, extending to pre-Lie structures the construction on Lie structures provided in
[
3
]
.
Let us start
by defining a map which is skew-symmetric on the first
n
variables that will be used to induce the higher order
algebra.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
\phi:\otimes^{n}A\rightarrow A
be a
n
-linear map and let
\tau
be a map from
A

to
\mathbb{K}
.

Define
\phi_{\tau}:\otimes^{n+1}A\rightarrow A
by

\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\tau(x_{k})\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{n+1})

(4.20)

where the hat over
\widehat{x_{k}}
on the right hand side means that
x_{k}
is excluded, that is
\phi

is calculated on
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k-1},x_{k+1},\cdots,x_{n+1})
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\phi:\otimes^{n}A\rightarrow A
be a
n
-linear map. A linear map
\tau:A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is called a
\phi
-trace if it
satisfies for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in A

\tau(\phi(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}))=0,\forall x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in A.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
\phi:(\wedge^{n-1}A)\otimes A\rightarrow A
be a n-linear map and
\tau

a linear map
A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
. Then
\phi_{\tau}
is a
(n+1)
-linear map such that is skew-symmetric on the first
n
variables.
Furthermore, if
\tau
is a
\phi
-trace then
\tau
is a
\phi_{\tau}
-trace.

Proof.

The
(n+1)
-linearity property of
\phi_{\tau}
follows from
n
-linearity of
\phi
and linearity
of
\tau
as it is a linear combination of
(n+1)
-linear maps.
To prove the skew-symmetry on the first
n
variables one simply notes that

\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}Sgn(\sigma)\tau(x_{\sigma(1)})\phi(x_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,x_{\sigma(n)},x_{n+1}),

which is clearly skew-symmetric on the first
n
variables. Since each term in
\phi_{\tau}
is proportional to
\phi
and
since
\tau
is linear and a
\phi
-trace,
\tau
will also be a
\phi_{\tau}
-trace.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Suppose that
(A,\phi)
is a
n
-pre-Lie algebra and
\tau:A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a
\phi
-trace, then
(A,\phi_{\tau})
is a
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebra. We shall say that
(A,\phi_{\tau})
is induced by
(A,\phi)
.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{\tau}
is skew-symmetric on the first
n
variables and multilinear by Lemma
4.10
, one only has to check that
the two identity of
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\phi_{\tau})
are fulfilled. The first identity is written as

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}\Big(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},\phi_{\tau}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n+1})\Big)-\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n},\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{n+1})\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}]^{C},y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big),

where
[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n+1}]^{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{(n+1)-i}\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n+1},x_{i})
.

Let us write the left-hand-side of this equation as
B-C-D
. Furthermore, we expand
B
into terms
B_{kl}
and
C
into terms
C_{lk}
such that

\displaystyle B_{kl}=(-1)^{k+l}\tau(x_{k})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},\phi(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1})\Big),

\displaystyle B=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}B_{kl},

\displaystyle C_{lk}=(-1)^{l+k}\tau(y_{l})\tau(x_{k})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,y_{n},\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{n+1})\Big),

\displaystyle C=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}C_{lk}.

Taking into account that
\tau
is a
\phi-
trace form, we expand
D
as

\displaystyle D=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}]^{C},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i},x_{j})+\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi_{\tau}(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i},x_{j}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle=D^{(1)}+D^{(2)},

where

\displaystyle D^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\tau(x_{k})\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big),

\displaystyle D^{(2)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{n}(-1)^{k+1}\tau(x_{k})\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i},x_{j}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\tau(y_{i})\phi_{\tau}\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{j}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big).

Let us now consider the expansion of
D^{(1)}
via
D^{(1)}_{kl}

\displaystyle D^{(1)}_{kl}=\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(-1)^{k+l}\tau(x_{k})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}),\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}(-1)^{k+l}\tau(x_{k})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle D^{(1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}D^{(1)}_{kl},

and we expand
D^{(2)}
as

\displaystyle D^{(2)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\sum_{k=1,k\neq j}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{n}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(-1)^{k+l}\tau(x_{k})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i},x_{j}),\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}(-1)^{k+l}\tau(x_{k})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{k}},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},y_{i},x_{j}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{(n+1)-j}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}(-1)^{n+l}\tau(y_{i})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{j}),\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}(-1)^{n+l}\tau(y_{i})\tau(y_{l})\phi\Big(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{l}},\cdots,\phi(x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{j}},\cdots,x_{n},x_{j}),\cdots,y_{n},y_{n+1}\Big)\Big).

By a direct simplification and using the two identity of
n
-pre-Lie algebras
(A,\phi)
, we get that the first identity of
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebras is satisfied for
\phi_{\tau}
. Also, by the same way we can prove that
\phi_{\tau}
satisfies the second identity of
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebras.
∎

In particular, one may derive from a pre-Lie algebra a 3-pre-Lie algebra using a trace map.
Recall that a pre-Lie algebra is a pair
(A,\circ)
, where
A
is a vector space and
\circ:A\times A\rightarrow A

is a bilinear multiplication satisfying, for all
x,y,z\in A
,

(x\circ y)\circ z-x\circ(y\circ z)=(y\circ x)\circ z-y\circ(x\circ z).

(4.21)

It is obvious that all associative algebras are pre-Lie
algebras and for a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\circ)
, the commutator
[x,y]^{C}=x\circ y-y\circ x
,
defines a Lie algebra. By Theorem
4.11
,
suppose that
(A,\circ)
is a pre-Lie algebra and
\tau:A\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
is a linear map, such that
\tau
is a
\circ
-trace. Then
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{\tau})
is a
3
-pre-Lie algebra, and we say that it is induced by
(A,\circ)
where

\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}_{\tau}:(\wedge^{2}A)\otimes A\rightarrow A
is defined by

\{x,y,z\}_{\tau}=\tau(x)y\circ z-\tau(y)x\circ z,\;\forall x,y,z\in A.

(4.22)

Example 4.12
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a three-dimensional pre-Lie algebra with a basis

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
whose nonzero brackets are given as follows:

e_{3}\circ e_{2}=e_{2},\quad e_{3}\circ e_{3}=-e_{3}.

We define a trace map as

\tau(e_{1})=a,\;\tau(e_{2})=0,\;\tau(e_{3})=0,

for any
a\in\mathbb{K}
. Then, according to Theorem
4.11
, we obtain a three-dimensional
3
-pre-Lie algebra with a basis

\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
whose nonzero brackets are given as follows:

\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{2}\}=ae_{2},\quad\{e_{1},e_{3},e_{3}\}=-ae_{3}.

Since
\tau
is also a
\phi_{\tau}
-trace, one can repeat the procedure in Theorem
4.11
to induce
a
(n+2)
-pre-Lie algebra from an
n
-pre-Lie algebra. However,
the result is an abelian algebra.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=(A,\phi)
be a
n
-pre-Lie algebra. Let
\mathcal{A}^{{}^{\prime}}

be any
(n+1)
-pre-Lie algebra induced by
\mathcal{A}
via the
\phi
-trace
\tau
. If

\mathcal{A}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
is a
(n+2)
-pre-Lie algebra induced by
\mathcal{A}^{{}^{\prime}}
using the same
\tau
again, then
\mathcal{A}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
is abelian.

Proof.

By the definition of
\phi_{\tau}
, the bracket on the algebra
\mathcal{A}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}
can be written as

Proof.

In addition there exists a

\displaystyle\nwarrow(Tu_{1},\cdots,Tu_{n})=T(l(Tu_{1},\cdots,Tu_{n-1})u_{n}),\quad\nearrow(Tu_{1},\cdots,Tu_{n})=T(r(Tu_{2},\cdots,Tu_{n})u_{1}),\forall u_{i}\in V.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(A,\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})
be a
n
-pre-Lie algebra and
B
be a symmetric bilinear form on
A
. We say that
B
is closed if it satisfies

\displaystyle B(\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\},w)+B(x_{n},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},w]^{C})

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{i+1}B(x_{i},\{w,x_{1},\cdots,\widehat{x_{i}},\cdots,x_{n-1},x_{n}\})=0,

(5.13)

for any
x_{i},w\in A
,
\;1\leq i\leq n
. If in addition
B
is non-degenerate, then
B
is called a pseudo-Hessian structure on
A
.

A
n
-pre-Lie algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\})
is called pseudo-Hessian if it is
equipped with a symmetric, closed and nondegenerate form
B
. It is
denoted by

.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(A,\{\cdot,\cdots,\cdot\},B)
be a pseudo-Hessian
n
-pre-Lie algebra. Then there exists a compatible
n
-L-dendriform algebra structure on
A
given by

\displaystyle B(\nwarrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),w)=-B(x_{n},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},w]^{C}),

\displaystyle B(\nearrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),w)=B(x_{1},\{w,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}),

(5.14)

\forall x_{i},w\in A,\;1\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Define the linear map
T:A^{*}\to A
by
\langle T^{-1}x,y\rangle=B(x,y)
. Using Eq. (
5.2
), we obtain that
T
is an invertible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A
associated to the dual representation
(A^{*},ad^{*},-R^{*})
. By Proposition
5.6
, there exists a compatible
n
-L-dendriform algebra structure given by

\displaystyle\nwarrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=T(ad^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})T^{-1}(x_{n})),\quad\nearrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=-T(R^{*}(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})T^{-1}(x_{1})).

Then we have

\displaystyle B(\nwarrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),w)=B(T(ad^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})T^{-1}(x_{n})),w)=\langle ad^{*}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})T^{-1}(x_{n}),w\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;=-\langle T^{-1}(x_{n}),[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},w]^{C}\rangle=-B(x_{n},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},w]^{C})

\displaystyle B(\nearrow(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}),w)=-B(T(R^{*}(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})T^{-1}(x_{1}))),w)=-\langle R^{*}(x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})T^{-1}(x_{1}),w\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;=\langle T^{-1}(x_{1}),\{w,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}\rangle=B(x_{1},\{w,x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\}).

The proof is finished.
∎
